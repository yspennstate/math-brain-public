    Let P be the transition matrix of an irreducible, reversible Markov chain on a finite state space with stationary distribution pi, and let gamma* = 1 - max{|lambda| : lambda an eigenvalue of P other than 1} be the absolute spectral gap, with relaxation time t_rel = 1/gamma*. Then for every epsilon in (0, 1/2) the total-variation mixing time satisfies (t_rel - 1) log(1/(2 epsilon)) <= t_mix(epsilon) <= t_rel log(1/(epsilon pi_min)), where pi_min is the smallest stationary probability (Levin, Peres, Wilmer, Theorems 12.4 and 12.5). Mixing time is therefore the relaxation time up to a factor between log(1/(2 epsilon)) and log(1/(epsilon pi_min)); on a state space of size N the gap between the two sides can be as large as log N, and neither side can be dropped in general. For a lazy chain (holding probability at least 1/2) all eigenvalues are nonnegative, so the absolute gap equals the ordinary gap 1 - lambda_2; a periodic chain (a simple random walk on a bipartite graph) has an eigenvalue -1, gamma* = 0, and never mixes. the chain is reversible with respect to pi (real eigenvalues); the bounds are stated for the absolute spectral gap aperiodicity (e.g. laziness) so that gamma* > 0 the upper bound carries the factor log(1/pi_min); the lower bound holds for epsilon < 1/2

This uniform statement does not itself give a strict degree-uniform improvement when the degree is not three.
