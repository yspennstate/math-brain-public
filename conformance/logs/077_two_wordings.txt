Whether Chatterjee's own theorem carries a curvature hypothesis was NOT verified by lane P4 - see the flag in the body THIS NODE EXISTS BECAUSE THE RESULT IS ROUTINELY MISQUOTED. The four misquotations, named: (1) 'Chatterjee proved chi = 1/3.' No. The relation is between two unknown exponents. Getting chi = 1/3 out of it needs xi = 2/3, which is exactly as open as chi = 1/3. (2) 'chi = 2 xi - 1 holds for FPP.' Not for the standard exponents. It holds for Chatterjee's strengthened ones, and only if they exist. (3) 'The relation is known for some distribution.' The survey is explicit: 'as of today, there is not a single distribution of passage times where we know that [chi = 2 xi - 1] holds', and it poses finding one as an open question. (4) 'It is universal, hence distribution-free.' The universality is a statement ABOUT the relation (independent of d and of the law within the class), not a licence to drop the nearly-Gamma hypothesis. Auffinger-Damron removed nearly-Gamma for the inequality chi <= 2 xi - 1 only. THE AUTHOR'S OWN SUMMARY: 'This article gives a rigorous proof of this conjecture assuming that the exponents exist in a certain sense' (abstract, arXiv:1105.4566v3). METHOD: break a geodesic into segments and compare the passage time to a sum of nearly i.i.d. variables through an approximation scheme, then trade off approximation error against the variance; the nearly-Gamma hypothesis is what lets that optimisation be carried out. CURVATURE - THE ONE PLACE THIS NODE IS MOST LIKELY TO BE SUBTLY WRONG, FLAGGED ON PURPOSE. The survey says two things: that the relation 'should be valid only under the unproven assumption of uniform positive curvature of the limit shape', and that this 'is one of the main reasons for the introduction of a strong definition of exponents'. It is tempting to read that as 'Chatterjee's theorem therefore has no curvature hypothesis', and an earlier draft of this node did. That is an INFERENCE from the survey's motivation sentence, not a quoted hypothesis list, and lane P4 did not verify it. THE CHECK THAT WOULD SETTLE IT: read the hypothesis list of the main theorem of arXiv:1105.4566v3 and record whether a curvature or limit-shape condition appears in it. Until then, treat the curvature status of this theorem as unknown here. Related, and this part IS quoted: the survey adds that with a different curvature exponent kappa one would expect chi = kappa xi - (kappa - 1), reducing to chi = 2 xi - 1 at kappa = 2.
