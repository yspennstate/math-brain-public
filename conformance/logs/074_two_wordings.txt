[2] Total Variation Distance of Probability Measures  (id: claim_d007b509b76b2574e8db1cb4d99bb511beec50788f3846f5da7d43e190951280; claim; score 21.98)

    For two probability measures mu and nu on a measurable space (S, A), the total variation distance is d_TV(mu, nu) = sup over A in A of |mu(A) - nu(A)|, a number in [0, 1]. If mu and nu have densities f and g with respect to a common reference measure lambda (which always exists, for instance lambda = mu + nu), then d_TV(mu, nu) = (1/2) integral |f - g| d lambda = 1 - integral min(f, g) d lambda, and the supremum is attained at A = { f > g }. Equivalent characterisations: d_TV is half the supremum of |integral h d mu - integral h d nu| over measurable h with |h| <= 1; and by the coupling characterisation, d_TV(mu, nu) = min P(X not equal to Y) over all couplings (X, Y) with X distributed as mu and Y as nu, the minimum being attained by the maximal coupling. Convergence in total variation is strictly stronger than weak convergence (point masses at 1/n converge weakly to the point mass at 0 but stay at total variation distance 1). The normalisation matters: some texts use the un-halved integral |f - g| d lambda, which lies in [0, 2].
