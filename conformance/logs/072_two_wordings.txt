    For bond percolation on the square lattice Z^2, p_c = 1/2, and moreover theta(1/2) = 0: there is no infinite open cluster AT the critical point. The two halves have separate histories and separate proofs. HARRIS (1960) proved theta(1/2) = 0, which gives p_c >= 1/2. KESTEN (1980) proved that theta(p) > 0 for every p > 1/2, giving p_c <= 1/2. The self-duality of Z^2 bond percolation at p = 1/2 - the dual of the open configuration at parameter p is a configuration at parameter 1 - p on an isomorphic lattice - makes 1/2 the ONLY candidate but does not by itself decide the critical behaviour, which is why the theorem is hard. The corresponding value for SITE percolation on Z^2 is not known; for site percolation on the triangular lattice the answer is 1/2 by the same duality mechanism, and that is the case where conformal invariance at criticality is proved. [8] Bernoulli Percolation, theta(p), and the Critical Probability  (id: claim_f03dd267f4dbe993abe345addd53bd73d98a6535068e27ed077b61bcdaa98a66; claim; score 24.22)

    In BOND percolation on a graph G with parameter p in [0,1], each edge is declared open with probability p and closed otherwise, independently; P_p denotes the resulting product measure on {0,1}^{E(G)}. The open cluster C(x) of a vertex x is the set of vertices reachable from x through open edges. Write theta(p) = P_p( |C(0)| = infinity ) for a fixed origin. Coupling all parameters on a common family of independent uniform variables - edge e open at level p exactly when U_e <= p - shows theta is nondecreasing, so the CRITICAL PROBABILITY p_c(G) = sup{ p : theta(p) = 0 } = inf{ p : theta(p) > 0 } is well defined. SITE percolation is defined analogously with vertices; the two have different critical values on the same graph, and p_c^{bond} <= p_c^{site} on any graph. On Z^d with d >= 2 one has 0 < p_c < 1, with the explicit elementary bounds 1/(2d-1) <= p_c^{bond}(Z^d) < 1. The value is known exactly only in special cases, most famously p_c^{bond}(Z^2) = 1/2. Whether theta(p_c) = 0 in every dimension is a celebrated open problem: it is known for d = 2 (Harris and Kesten) and, by lace-expansion methods, in high dimensions - Hara and Slade for large d, later reduced to d >= 11 by Fitzner and van der Hofstad - and it was still open for the intermediate dimensions 3 <= d <= 10 at the time this node was written. [standard; e.g. Grimmett, Percolation, 2nd ed., Chapters 1 and 8]
