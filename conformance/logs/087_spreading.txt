    For bond percolation on the square lattice Z^2, p_c = 1/2, and moreover theta(1/2) = 0: there is no infinite open cluster AT the critical point. The two halves have separate histories and separate proofs. HARRIS (1960) proved theta(1/2) = 0, which gives p_c >= 1/2. KESTEN (1980) proved that theta(p) > 0 for every p > 1/2, giving p_c <= 1/2. The self-duality of Z^2 bond percolation at p = 1/2 - the dual of the open configuration at parameter p is a configuration at parameter 1 - p on an isomorphic lattice - makes 1/2 the ONLY candidate but does not by itself decide the critical behaviour, which is why the theorem is hard. The corresponding value for SITE percolation on Z^2 is not known; for site percolation on the triangular lattice the answer is 1/2 by the same duality mechanism, and that is the case where conformal invariance at criticality is proved.     Publication record. Y. Shmalo, Entropy and singularity of spectral and pivotal sets in planar percolation (arXiv:2609.15388, September 2026) proves that for crossings of critical bond percolation on the square lattice, and of site percolation on the triangular lattice with critical or inhomogeneous near-critical parameters, the Shannon entropies of the spectral sample and of the pivotal set are both comparable to the total influence, of order R^2 alpha_4(R). This answers Question 1 in Section 11 of C. Garban, G. Pete and O. Schramm, The Fourier spectrum of critical percolation (Acta Math. 205, 2010, 19-104), and proves both bounds of Conjecture 8 in G. Kalai, Three puzzles on mathematics, computation, and games (Proc. ICM 2018), for these crossings; the spectral bound is the percolation case of the Friedgut-Kalai Fourier entropy-influence conjecture. For critical triangular-site square crossings the two laws are asymptotically mutually singular.
