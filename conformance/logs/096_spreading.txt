    Let mu be a probability measure on R^n satisfying a logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant C, that is Ent_mu(f^2) <= 2 C E_mu[|grad f|^2] for all locally Lipschitz f. Then for every 1-Lipschitz F with E_mu[F] finite and every real lambda, E_mu[ exp( lambda (F - E F) ) ] <= exp( C lambda^2 / 2 ), and consequently mu( F - E F >= t ) <= exp( - t^2 / (2C) ) for every t > 0, with the same bound for the lower tail. For the standard Gaussian, C = 1 by the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality, giving the sharp Gaussian concentration bound exp(-t^2/2) for 1-Lipschitz functions. The argument converts a functional inequality into an exponential moment bound and is the reason log-Sobolev, rather than Poincare, is the inequality one wants: Poincare alone yields only exponential, not Gaussian, tails. The hypothesis is a Lipschitz bound on F with respect to the EUCLIDEAN metric; changing the metric changes the effective constant. [Herbst, unpublished; see Ledoux, The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon, Chapter 5] mu satisfies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality with constant C F is 1-Lipschitz with respect to the Euclidean metric E_mu[F] finite
