Its Stieltjes transform m(z) = integral (x - z)^{-1} mu_sc(dx) satisfies the self-consistent equation m(z)^2 + z m(z) + 1 = 0 for every z in C^+, equivalently m(z) = -1/( z + m(z) ), and is the UNIQUE solution of that equation with Im m(z) > 0 for z in C^+; explicitly m(z) = ( -z + sqrt(z^2 - 4) )/2 with the branch of the square root chosen so that m(z) is asymptotic to -1/z as |z| tends to infinity.     For the semicircle density (1/(2 pi)) sqrt(4 - x^2) on [-2, 2] (mean 0, variance 1), all odd moments vanish and the 2k-th moment equals the Catalan number C_k = (1/(k+1)) binom(2k, k): 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, ... . A semicircle of radius R has 2k-th moment C_k (R/2)^{2k}. In the moment method for a Wigner matrix W = X / sqrt n, where X is real symmetric with independent entries above the diagonal, mean 0 and variance 1, the normalized traces satisfy E tr(W^{2k}) / n -> C_k: expanding the trace as a sum over closed walks of length 2k on the index set, only walks whose edges are each traversed exactly twice and whose underlying graph is a tree survive as n grows, and such walks are counted by C_k; the Catalan numbers determine the semicircle law uniquely because it has compact support. Wigner's theorem then follows from convergence of moments plus a variance bound (or a concentration bound on the trace) giving weak convergence of the empirical spectral distribution in probability. entries independent up to symmetry with mean 0 and variance 1 before scaling by 1/sqrt n moments bounded uniformly in n (finite fourth moment suffices for the semicircle limit of the empirical spectral distribution; the diagonal entries may have a different variance) the moment method identifies the limit because the semicircle law is determined by its moments (compact support)
