# Review record

Manuscript: "Phi-mixing implies interlaced rho-mixing for Markov chains", Y. Shmalo, dated
18 September 2026. Reviewed by Claude (Anthropic, Fable 5.1) on 18 September 2026 against the
submitted draft, the two primary sources (Bradley, Probability Surveys 2 (2005), and Peyre,
arXiv:1004.1602v2), and independent computation. This is a review record, not refereeing.

## Verdict

The main theorem holds as stated. Every step of the argument was re-derived: the channel lemma
(finite case and the passage to standard Borel spaces), the row-good set from phi, the conditional
support remark, side-separated conditioning, the prefix interface (both the adjacent and the
non-adjacent case, including the block containing the separator), the transcription of Peyre's two
theorems, the path eigenvalue, the arcsine bound, the finite-coordinate approximation, the two
corollaries, the three examples and the appendix. No fatal error and no gap was found. The
dependency on Peyre's tensorization theorems was checked at the source: in Peyre's proofs of
Theorems 3.3.1 and 3.3.10 every use of a decorrelation hypothesis is conditional on the prefix
sigma-field generated by the earlier members of both families (the "N against M" proof applies its
Lemma 3.3.3 conditionally on the earlier Y's, and that lemma conditions on the earlier X's; the
"Z against Z" proof bounds each term S_ij conditionally on G_{j-1} v F_{i-1}), which is the form
stated in his Section 3.4(a) and used in the paper. The theorem numbering (3.3.1, 3.3.10) and the
section reference (3.4(a), the subsection "Minimal Hypotheses") were confirmed by counting the
theorem environments of chapter 3 of the source.

## Findings and how they were handled

1. Bibliography. The draft cited three works. The classical sources of the objects used
   (Renyi; Kolmogorov and Rozanov; Stein and Rosenblatt for the interlaced coefficient; Dobrushin's
   coefficient; the inequality rho <= 2 sqrt(phi) in Ibragimov and Linnik) and Bradley's directly
   related results (the lower psi-mixing criterion of 1997, the rho-mixing but not interlaced
   rho-mixing chain of 2001, the reversible-chain example of 2015, the monograph of 2007) were absent,
   as were the classical source of Lemma 2.1 (Cohen, Iwasa, Rautu, Ruskai, Seneta and Zbaganu,
   1993) and the published version of Polyanskiy and Wu. All added, with the consequence for limit
   theorems under interlaced rho-mixing (Bradley 1992; Peligrad 1998; Utev and Peligrad 2003) noted
   in the introduction.
2. Relation to the known sufficient condition. The reset chain of Section 7 has lower psi
   coefficients psi'(n) = 0 for every n, because P^n(i, j) = 0 for j >= n, j != i + n, while
   pi_i pi_j > 0. So Bradley's criterion (psi'(n) > 0 for some n implies interlaced rho-mixing) does
   not cover it, although the chain is phi-mixing at an exponential rate. Added as Remark 7.2; it
   makes the separation from the 1997 result explicit.
3. Statement-level clarifications. In the proof of the prefix interface the chain
   U_i -> X_s -> X_t -> V_j is used with X_t a coordinate of V_j; a sentence now says why this is a
   Markov chain. In the finite proof of Lemma 2.1 the existence of an eigenvector for the top
   centered eigenvalue is now stated (finite dimension). The notation L^2_0, the essential supremum
   in the definition of the subjective correlation, and the vectors X_S, X_T are now defined where
   first used, and the martingale convergence theorem is named in Lemma 5.1.
4. Peyre's theorems. The paper's Theorem 4.1 is a faithful transcription; the padding by
   deterministic variables is the same reduction Peyre uses (the "cemetery point" in the proof of
   Theorem 3.3.10), which disposes of the tail-field question for Z-indexed families. A sentence was
   added recording how the hypotheses are stated in the source and where the prefix form is recorded.
5. Constants. The abstract, Theorem 1.1, Section 5, Corollary 5.3 (phi < 1/8 gives
   2 sqrt(2 phi) < 1) and Section 9 use the same constants; the strong bound is below one exactly
   when phi < 1/4. All of these are among the Lean-checked statements.
6. References by kind. Every \ref to a lemma, proposition, corollary or theorem label is preceded
   by the matching word; checked by grep.
7. The declarations were rewritten to record which system produced what, and the repository is now
   cited in the paper.

## Checks performed

- Exact values of the six-state example (phi(1) = eps, row diameter 2 eps, rho*(1) = sqrt(2 eps),
  rho*(n) = 0 for n >= 2) reproduced for eps in {0.01, 0.1, 0.25, 0.4} by exhaustive enumeration of
  all two-colourings of four times in a six-step window and by random sampling, with the maximal
  correlation computed as the second singular value of the normalised joint law.
- The inequalities rho(X_S, X_T) <= Psi(sqrt Delta), <= 2 sqrt(Delta) cos(pi/(m+1)) and
  <= min(1, 2 sqrt(2 phi)) tested on random stationary and time-inhomogeneous chains with 2 to 6
  states, windows of 6 to 11 times, lags 1 to 3 and random interlacings of up to 8 times: about
  24,000 tests, zero violations (checks/break_main_theorem.out, checks/break_small_diameter.out).
  The largest observed ratio rho / sqrt(Delta) was 1.22 against the bound 2, and the largest
  rho / sqrt(phi) was 1.57 against 2 sqrt 2.
- The scalar identities and inequalities, the six-state and two-state algebra, the path matrix
  bounds, the finite Dobrushin argument and the reset-chain kernel and invariant law were proved in
  Lean 4 against Mathlib (lean/), each with axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound only.
- Bradley's Question 5, the definition of rho*(n), Theorem 3.3(6)-(7), Question 2 and the
  inequality (1.13) were read in the survey; Peyre's definitions and theorems were read in the LaTeX
  source of arXiv:1004.1602v2.
- Literature: web searches for the implication and for the terms "interlaced rho-mixing",
  "rho*-mixing" and "phi-mixing" with "Markov chain" returned Bradley's 1997, 2001 and 2015 papers,
  his 2022 and 2025 preprints on reversible chains, and papers on INAR and copula-based Markov
  chains; none of them proves or disproves the implication.

## Open doubts

- The journal data for Bradley's paper on reversible Markov chains (New Zealand J. Math. 45
  (2015), 71-87) come from a secondary listing; the arXiv identifier is verified.
- Peyre's monograph is an arXiv preprint. Its "N against M" proof was read in full; of the
  "Z against Z" proof, the reduction to the three relations between the arrays V, W, S and the use
  of the prefix hypothesis were read, the remaining optimisation over "correct arrays" was not.
  The qualitative implication and the bound 2 sqrt(2 phi) rest on the "N against M" theorem alone
  (through the elementary bound ||E|| <= 2 kappa); only the sharper arcsine bound needs the
  "Z against Z" theorem.
- The novelty claim rests on the literature search described above; a claim of an exhaustive
  search is not made.
