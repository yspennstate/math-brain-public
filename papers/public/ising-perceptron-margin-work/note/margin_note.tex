\title{Notes on the Ising perceptron at nonzero margin}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We study the replica-symmetric system behind the Ising perceptron
capacity at general margin $\kappa$.  Two elementary identities make
the general-$\kappa$ system as well behaved as the zero-margin one:
the free energy is stationary in $(q,\psi)$ exactly at the fixed point,
so the threshold $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ is the unique root of a
strictly decreasing function; and $dR/dq$ has a one-term integral
representation with positive integrand, so the fixed-point map is
monotone at every $\kappa$.  We compute the capacity candidate curve
and the numerical content of Huang's conditions on
$\kappa\in[-2,1.5]$, and we certify, in ball arithmetic, the
fixed-point condition together with the AMP and local-concavity
conditions on the strip $\kappa\in[-0.65,0.19]$ (the last
$0.09$ beyond the reach of the rectangle product bound, on Nakajima's
certified premise), with
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ located to $2\cdot10^{-6}$ at five margins
and the local maximality of the first-moment functional at the
distinguished point certified at six margins spanning the strip.  The global part of the
two-variable condition remains open at $\kappa\neq 0$; its numerical
landscape is essentially independent of $\kappa$ on the range
scanned.
\end{abstract}

\section{The system}

Let $\varphi$ and $\Psi$ be the standard Gaussian density and upper
tail, $M=\varphi/\Psi$ the inverse Mills ratio, and $Z\sim N(0,1)$.
Fix $\kappa\in\R$ and set
\[
u(z;q,\kappa)=\frac{\kappa-\sqrt{q}\,z}{\sqrt{1-q}},\qquad
0<q<1 .
\]
Following Ding--Sun and Huang, define
\begin{align}
P(\psi)&=\E\bigl[\tanh^2(\sqrt{\psi}\,Z)\bigr],\qquad
R(q,\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{1-q}\,\E\bigl[M(u(Z))^2\bigr],
\label{eq:PR}\\
G(\alpha,q,\psi)&=-\frac{\psi(1-q)}{2}
+\E\log\bigl(2\cosh(\sqrt{\psi}\,Z)\bigr)
+\alpha\,\E\log\Psi\bigl(u(Z)\bigr).
\label{eq:G}
\end{align}
The replica-symmetric prediction is that the storage threshold is
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$, the zero of
$\alpha\mapsto G_\star(\alpha)=G(\alpha,q_\star(\alpha),\psi_\star(\alpha))$
along the fixed-point branch $q=P(\psi)$, $\psi=R(q,\alpha)$.  At
$\kappa=0$ this is the Krauth--M\'ezard value
$\alpha_\star=0.833078599\ldots$, now verified without numerical
hypotheses; Huang's general-$\kappa$ theorem upper-bounds the capacity
by $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ for every $\kappa$, conditional on four
numerical conditions of which only the $\kappa=0$ instances had been
checked.

\section{Two identities}

\begin{lemma}[Stationarity]\label{lem:stat}
For all $\alpha>0$, $\kappa\in\R$, $\psi>0$ and $0<q<1$,
\[
\frac{\partial G}{\partial\psi}=\frac{q-P(\psi)}{2},
\qquad
\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}=\frac{\psi-R(q,\alpha)}{2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is Gaussian integration by parts on the
$\log\cosh$ term.  For the second, differentiating $u$ in $q$ gives
\[
u_q=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-q}}
\Bigl(-\frac{z}{\sqrt q}+\frac{u}{\sqrt{1-q}}\Bigr),
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\,\E\log\Psi(u)=-\E\bigl[M(u)\,u_q\bigr].
\]
Stein's lemma applied to the $z$ part,
$\E[zM(u)]=-\sqrt{q/(1-q)}\;\E[M'(u)]$, together with the Mills
identity $M'(u)=M(u)^2-uM(u)$, collapses the two pieces:
\[
\E[M(u)\,u_q]
=\frac{\E[M'(u)]+\E[u\,M(u)]}{2(1-q)}
=\frac{\E[M(u)^2]}{2(1-q)}
=\frac{R(q,\alpha)}{2\alpha}.
\]
Hence $\partial_q G=\psi/2-\alpha\cdot R/(2\alpha)=(\psi-R)/2$.
\end{proof}

Both derivatives vanish exactly on the fixed-point branch, so the
envelope theorem gives
$G_\star'(\alpha)=\E\log\Psi(u)<0$ strictly, for every $\kappa$: the
root $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ is unique on the branch.  This is the
general-$\kappa$ form of the monotonicity clause in the fixed-point
condition.

\begin{lemma}[Derivative of $R$]\label{lem:dR}
For all $\alpha>0$, $\kappa\in\R$ and $0<q<1$,
\[
\frac{\partial R}{\partial q}
=\frac{\alpha}{(1-q)^2}\,
\E\Bigl[M'(u)^2+2\,M(u)^2M'(u)\Bigr]\;>\;0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating \eqref{eq:PR} in $q$ produces
$R/(1-q)+\tfrac{\alpha}{1-q}\E[2MM'u_q]$.  Splitting $u_q$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} and applying Stein's lemma to
$\E[z\,MM'(u)]$ (with $(MM')'=M'^2+MM''$ and
$M''=M'(M-u)+MM'-M$) turns the cross term into
\[
\E[2MM'u_q]=\frac{\E[M'^2+2M^2M'-M^2]}{1-q},
\]
and the $-\E[M^2]/(1-q)^2$ piece cancels $R/(1-q)$ exactly.
Positivity is immediate since $M'\in(0,1)$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:dR} replaces Ding--Sun's Lemma~7.2 (monotonicity of
$R$, stated at $\kappa=0$) at every margin, and its one-term form is
what makes a tight interval enclosure of $dR/dq$ possible.  We checked
both identities against high-precision finite differences (agreement
to $10^{-15}$).

\section{The two-variable function at general $\kappa$}

Huang's global condition asks that
$\mathcal S_\star(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)\le0$ everywhere, with equality
at $(1,0)$.  Writing his functional at general $\kappa$ (the margin
enters both as an additive shift in the $\log\Psi$ argument and inside
the planted cavity law $N(z)=M(u(z))/\sqrt{1-q_0}$), the anchor
$\mathcal S_\star(1,0)=0$ holds along the whole computed branch (to
$2\cdot10^{-14}$ in double precision), with optimal tilt
$s=\sqrt{1-q_0}$ to eight digits --- his zero-margin lemma appears to
extend verbatim.  On the grid scanned
($\kappa\in\{0,\pm.05,0.13,\pm.25,\pm.5,\pm.75,\pm1\}$, the interior
point on the second lane) the landscape is
essentially $\kappa$-independent: a single maximum at $(1,0)$,
off-center values below $-3\cdot10^{-3}$, negative far rays, Hessian
eigenvalues near $(-0.22,-0.0045)$, and moment-body clearance near
$0.017$.  On roughly the half-plane $\lambda_1<0$ the $s$-infimum is
$-\infty$: $\alpha\,\E[N^2]=\psi_0$ exactly at the fixed point, so the
quadratic tilt coefficient cancels; these directions satisfy the
condition trivially and correspond to the capped-tilt case in Huang's
treatment.

\section{Certificates on a strip}

For $\kappa$ in sub-slabs of width $5\cdot10^{-4}$ covering
$[-0.65,0.10]$, with per-slab parameter rectangles seeded by the
continuation, we verify in ball arithmetic
(\texttt{python-flint}, 80-bit working precision):

Two implementation points carry the whole computation.  Adaptive
complex quadrature cannot resolve enclosure width that comes from
parameter balls --- subdividing the integration variable does not
shrink it, and the integrator exhausts any budget --- so every
wide-parameter integral is a real-cell Riemann sum.  And the ball
evaluation of $\varphi/\Psi$ NaNs on wide arguments in the steep
region (the enclosure of $\Psi$ crosses zero), while the ratio is
monotone; all Mills-family factors are therefore enclosed by two thin
endpoint evaluations, and the sign-critical sums use the mean-value
form (midpoint value plus a derivative correction, $O(h^2)$ against
the plain hull's $O(h)$).  A third point joined below
$\kappa=-0.35$: expectations that nearly cancel must be evaluated as
one correlated integrand, never as separate sums whose parameter
widths add.  For the local-concavity check this is the identity
(with $t=M'$, $A=1-q_0$, and $b=m(\hat z)-A$ for
$m(\hat z)=\E[(\hat z+\cosh^2(\sqrt{\psi_0}Z))^{-1}]$)
\[
\lambda(\hat z)=\hat z+\alpha\,\E\!\left[\frac{b\,t^2}{A(A+bt)}\right],
\]
whose integrand is monotone in $t$ for fixed $(m,A)$, regular on all
of $t\in[0,1]$, and bounded by $|b|/(Am)$; the separated form's
enclosure crossed zero there while the true value stays near $-0.14$
(the first $[-0.45,-0.35]$ batch, kept as evidence, failed on exactly
this).  With those in place the checks are:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] the contraction
$\sup_q(P\circ R_\alpha)'(q)\le
\sup_\psi P'\cdot\sup_q|dR/dq|<1$ on the slab rectangle, via
Lemma~\ref{lem:dR};
\item[(ii)] existence: the endpoints of the $q$ interval map strictly
inward under $P\circ R_\alpha$ on each of eight $\alpha$
sub-intervals;
\item[(iii)] the crossing: at the two $\alpha$ endpoints the fixed
point is located by certified Picard iteration and the sign of $G$
resolves through a mean-value form that keeps the stationarity
cancellations of Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} explicit
($G(\alpha_{\mathrm{lb}})>0>G(\alpha_{\mathrm{ub}})$);
\item[(iv)] Huang's AMP condition
$\alpha\,\E[\sech^4(\sqrt{\psi_0}Z)]\,
\E[M'(u(\sqrt{q_0}Z))^2]/(1-q_0)^2<1$; and
\item[(v)] local concavity at the witness $\hat z=-0.6693$:
$\lambda(\hat z)<0$.
\end{itemize}

Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:stat} (strict decrease of $G_\star$),
(i)--(iii) discharge the fixed-point condition verbatim on each slab:
$\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ exists, is unique, and lies in the slab's
$\alpha$ interval (of width about $4\cdot10^{-3}$) for every $\kappa$
in the slab.

All fifteen hundred slabs covering $[-0.65,0.10]$ passed every check
(forty independent worker runs, contiguous coverage; two first
attempts failed and are kept as evidence --- one at doubled slab
width, since the $q$ pad must also cover the within-slab drift
$dq_\star/d\kappa$, which reaches $3.4$ per unit $\kappa$ near
$-0.2$, and one on the separated-form $\lambda$ enclosure described
above).  A second lane of one hundred eighty slabs extends the strip
to $0.19$ (Section~\ref{sec:remains}), with uniqueness supplied by
Nakajima's theorem under a certified premise instead of the
rectangle contraction, for sixteen hundred eighty in all.  Certified
point intervals at five margins tighten the slab pads by bisection on
the certified sign of $G$:
$\alpha_\star(0.05)\in[0.780449,0.781325]$,
$\alpha_\star(-0.05)\in[0.889115,0.890013]$,
$\alpha_\star(0.0995)\in[0.732844,0.733711]$,
$\alpha_\star(-0.45)\in[1.557652,1.557655]$, and, past the product
bound's reach on the second lane,
$\alpha_\star(0.13)\in[0.705932,0.705934]$, each containing its
continuation value (endpoints displayed rounded outward).
The certified $\alpha_\star(\kappa)$ intervals have width about
$3.5\cdot10^{-3}$; the slab at $\kappa\in[0,5\cdot10^{-4}]$ contains
the Ding--Sun value $0.833078599\ldots$, and a mid-slab continuation
solve lands inside its interval.  Thus conditions (i)--(v) --- the
fixed-point condition verbatim, the AMP condition, and the
local-concavity condition --- hold for every
$\kappa\in[-0.65,0.19]$ (past $0.10$ through the second lane); of
Huang's four conditions only the global two-variable bound remains
open on the strip.  Across the slabs the certified contraction margins run monotonically
from $0.069$ at $\kappa=-0.05$ through $0.029$ at $\kappa=0.05$ to
$0.0083$ on the last slab before $\kappa=0.1$, placing the saturation
of the product bound near $\kappa\approx0.12$ (a projection past the
certified range, not a finding); on the negative side the margins
grow, and uniqueness there is carried entirely by our own contraction
certificates, since Nakajima's theorem is stated for $\kappa\ge0$.

\section{The center of the two-variable landscape at general
$\kappa$}

The first ingredient of the global condition's certificate at
$\kappa=0$ is local maximality of the fixed-tilt functional at the
distinguished point, via the Hessian
$M=\nabla^2\bar{\mathcal S}_\star(1,0)$.  The second-derivative
expansion before the zero-margin specialization gives, at every
$\kappa$, the same quadratic form
$-\langle u,\mathrm{diag}\,I\,u\rangle+C_1a^2+C_2ab+C_3b^2$ in
$a=I_1u_1+I_2u_2$, $b=I_2u_1+I_3u_2$, with the prior-side integrals
$I_j$ unchanged and the constraint-side constants $C_j$ now given by
integrals at the shifted law (their zero-margin closed forms use
$\alpha_\star\E[N\hat H]=-q_0(1-q_0)\psi_0$, which does not survive;
$\alpha_\star\E[N^2]=\psi_0$ does).  A float transcription of the
general-$\kappa$ constants lands inside every certified interval of
the zero-margin appendix ($I_j$ to their printed digits, $C_1$
through $C_3$, $\det M$), and along the curve it gives a definite
boundary: $M$ stays negative definite up to $\kappa=1.15$ and loses
definiteness between $1.15$ and $1.20$, while the Hessian of the
tilt-optimized functional remains negative out to at least
$\kappa=1.5$.  The same formulas evaluated in ball arithmetic on the
Ding--Sun rectangle certify $M_{1,1}<0$ and $\det M>0$ at $\kappa=0$
--- an independent re-verification of the zero-margin claim through
the general-$\kappa$ route, with the near-cancellations
$1-4q_0+3p_4$ and $q_0-p_4$ evaluated as single integrands and the
constraint-side integrals by mean-value Riemann sums.  At $\kappa=0.05$, deepening the
bisection quadrature locates $\alpha_\star$ to
$[0.781073068,0.781074776]$ (width $1.7\cdot10^{-6}$), and on the
resulting boxes ($3.6\cdot10^{-6}$ in $q_0$, $5.2\cdot10^{-5}$ in
$\psi_0$) the certificate resolves: $M_{1,1}<0$ and $\det M>0$ in
ball arithmetic --- the local maximality at the distinguished point,
the center ingredient of the two-variable condition, certified at a
nonzero margin.  The same chain at $\kappa=-0.05$
($\alpha_\star\in[0.889408032,0.889409783]$), at the strip's
thin-margin edge $\kappa=0.0995$
($\alpha_\star\in[0.733478514,0.733480204]$), and at the
deep-negative edge $\kappa=-0.45$
($\alpha_\star\in[1.557652103,1.557654133]$, where the
constraint-side constants sit far from their zero-margin values,
$C_1=-0.855$ against $-0.718$) also certifies, and so does
$\kappa=0.13$ on the second lane
($\alpha_\star\in[0.705932218,0.705933898]$), so the center
certificate now holds at six margins spanning the two-lane strip,
two of them on the relaxed side where uniqueness has no analytic
backup and one past the product bound's reach.  The two are consistent by the Schur relation --- the
fixed-tilt form dominates the optimized one --- so the condition
itself survives where the fixed-tilt certificate does not: past
$\kappa\approx1.18$ the local argument must re-optimize the tilt,
as the zero-margin verification's ray certificates already do.

\section{What remains}\label{sec:remains}

The one open condition at $\kappa\neq0$ is the global two-variable
bound, whose certified analogue at $\kappa=0$ is the Region I/II
machinery of the capacity verification; its center ingredient (local
maximality at the distinguished point) is now certified at four
margins spanning the strip, and the scanned landscape suggests the
global part transfers with essentially unchanged margins.  Past the
strip's positive edge the rectangle product bound saturates near
$\kappa\approx0.12$, but the pointwise contraction does not, and the
wall is demonstrably a method artifact: at $\kappa=0.13$ the box
product reads $1.32$ while a certified sign bisection of the
displacement $P(R(q))-q$ (no contraction input) locates the fixed
point at both $\alpha$ endpoints to width $2\cdot10^{-8}$, the
contraction on the located intervals reads $0.79$, and the $G$
crossing resolves with margins $2\cdot10^{-3}$
(\texttt{past\_wall\_0p13.py}); Nakajima's hypothesis is certified
alongside ($\alpha_{\mathrm{ub}}<\alpha_c(0.13)=1.0394\ldots$ in ball
arithmetic), so with his theorem supplying uniqueness this is the
fixed-point condition at that margin, and a strip past $0.12$ needs
only the existence sweep, which is already contraction-free.  That
strip is now certified: $180$ slabs on $[0.10,0.19]$, each carrying
the premise $\alpha_{\mathrm{ub}}<\alpha_c(\kappa)$ in ball
arithmetic, the inward sweep, per-sub-ball sign-bisection location
with the contraction consumed only on located intervals (margins
$\ge0.18$), and the $G$ crossing.  The lower bound is a
separate matter entirely: Ding--Sun's conditioned second-moment
reduction is proved at $\kappa=0$ only, so nothing here should be read
as an exact threshold statement at nonzero margin --- the certified
content feeds Huang's upper-bound theorem.  On the relaxed side
($\kappa<0$) the same certificates apply but saddle uniqueness has no
analytic backup, and the approach to replica-symmetry breaking
territory at large negative margin deserves its own study.
