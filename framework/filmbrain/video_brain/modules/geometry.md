# Teach through pictures and geometry

A mathematical picture should carry an inference, with explicit limits on what the picture establishes.

## Give each mathematical slide a geometric carrier

Begin with the object: vectors, a region, transformation, probability mass, trajectory, partition or measured curve. Add the formula that describes the picture, then the inference. Keep labels stable as the argument develops. Aim for the owner-requested 3Blue1Brown-like geometric intuition: picture, transformation, equation, then explanation. ## Explain high-dimensional structure honestly

Use projections, slices, coordinate comparisons, distributions and animations of transformations. Label a projection as a projection. Show the basis or reference directions when they help distinguish what moves and what stays fixed. Blender is a useful candidate for real 3D surfaces, sections and camera views when the spatial relation matters. Build from the mathematical objects and parameters, and inspect a small preview for scale and occlusion before an expensive render.

A 3D rendering is useful only if the viewer understands its axes and what its hidden coordinates represent. A rotating shape without meaning adds work for the viewer.

**Check:** Axes, units, dimension and the relation to the theorem are visible or spoken; occlusion never hides the important comparison.

## Make numerical pictures traceable to real results

Numerics illustrate evidence; they do not prove a theorem. A typed decimal can diverge from the plotted value after a data update.
