# Furstenberg's ×2 ×3 conjecture

## Informal statement

Let \(T_2, T_3 : [0,1) \to [0,1)\) be the maps \(T_2(x) = 2x \bmod 1\) and \(T_3(x) = 3x \bmod 1\).
Let \(\mu\) be a Borel probability measure on \([0,1)\) that is invariant under both \(T_2\) and \(T_3\)
and is **ergodic** under the joint action of the semigroup generated by \(T_2\) and \(T_3\).

**Conjecture.** \(\mu\) is either Lebesgue measure or supported on a finite set of rational numbers
with denominators coprime to \(6\).

A weaker form (proved by Furstenberg himself, 1967): the only **closed** subsets of \([0,1)\) invariant under both
\(T_2\) and \(T_3\) are \([0,1)\) and finite unions of rational orbits.

## Known partial results (the scaffolding ProofSwarm builds on)

| Year | Author | Result |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1967 | Furstenberg | Closed-set version: only invariant closed sets are finite or all of \([0,1)\). |
| 1990 | D. Rudolph | Conjecture holds under the assumption \(h_\mu(T_2) > 0\) (positive entropy). |
| 1995 | B. Host | Alternative proof of Rudolph's theorem; pointwise equidistribution refinements. |
| 1998 | A. Johnson | Generalization to \(\times m \times n\) with \(\log m / \log n \notin \mathbb{Q}\). |
| 2006 | E. Lindenstrauss | Adelic measure rigidity (related semigroup actions). |
| open | — | The **zero-entropy case** (\(h_\mu(T_2) = 0\) and \(\mu\) non-atomic) — full conjecture. |

## Why this problem is the right POC target

1. **Numerical handle.** Candidate non-Lebesgue, non-atomic, jointly invariant measures can be searched for
   computationally (self-similar measures, Bernoulli convolutions, IFS-generated measures, sub-Cantor sets
   invariant under both maps). Each candidate either violates joint invariance, has positive entropy
   (covered by Rudolph), or falls within a known impossibility — and the agents can check which.

2. **Lean handle.** Mathlib4 has measure theory, ergodic theory (`Mathlib.Dynamics.Ergodic.*`), and
   the basic dynamics of \(\times m\) maps. Statements like "Lebesgue is jointly invariant",
   "Dirac on a rational with denom coprime to 6 is jointly invariant", and the closed-set version
   (Furstenberg 1967) are all formalizable. Rudolph's theorem statement is formalizable; its proof
   is too long for POC scope but the statement + a key lemma is realistic.

3. **Sub-conjecture decomposition.** The proof-ticket protocol naturally yields:
   - **L1 (statement):** Formal Lean 4 statement of the conjecture.
   - **L2 (closed-set version):** Formal proof of Furstenberg 1967.
   - **L3 (Rudolph statement):** Formal Lean statement of the positive-entropy theorem.
   - **L4 (ergodic decomposition):** Formal lemma that joint invariance + ergodicity → measure is in the
     extreme-points convex hull.
   - **L5 (numerical certificate):** Cloud Batch search over self-similar measures and Bernoulli
     convolutions, certifying that the searched class contains no counterexamples (or finding one).
   - **L6 (entropy lemma):** \(h_\mu(T_2) > 0 \iff h_\mu(T_3) > 0\) (folklore but needs Lean proof).

4. **Counterexample search has teeth.** Bernoulli convolutions \(\nu_\beta\) (\(\beta \in (0,1)\),
   \(\sum \pm \beta^n\)) are a famous family where for some \(\beta\) the measure is singular (Erdős 1939).
   None are known to be jointly \(T_2, T_3\)-invariant, but the search is concrete: for each rational
   \(\beta = p/q\) we can check joint invariance numerically and rule out.

## Initial subgoal for ProofSwarm POC

**Tier 1 (Lean):** Formalize statement L1 + closed-set theorem L2 (Furstenberg 1967), get a Lean kernel-checked proof of L2.

**Tier 2 (numerical):** Counterexample Hunter sweeps Bernoulli convolutions and self-similar IFS measures
on \([0,1)\), checks joint \(T_2, T_3\)-invariance up to \(10^{-12}\), produces a certificate ruling out
candidates with denominators \(\le 100\).

**Tier 3 (referee):** Referee agent reviews both, flags any unstated assumptions (e.g., "ergodic under
joint action" vs. "ergodic under each separately" — these differ).
