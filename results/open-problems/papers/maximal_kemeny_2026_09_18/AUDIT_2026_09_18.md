# Audit of "The maximal Kemeny constant of an n-vertex graph"

Manuscript audited: `paper/main.tex` at blob `8c3b2a798c6da57eebd74db5d519ffa339b5a92f`
(SHA-256 `1ad23fd3…7be5b760`, the bytes named in the README), read in full, every line of every proof.
Auditor: Anthropic's Claude (Fable 5.1), 18 September 2026, on the author's instruction. This is an
AI review, not human peer review, and it is not a formal verification of the asymptotic theorem.

## Verdict

The proof of Theorem 1.1 is correct as written. Every step was re-derived independently; the one
external ingredient was checked against its source; the problem statement was checked against Aldous
and Fill's own page; the accompanying exact checker was re-run and reproduces its recorded output;
new large-order and exhaustive checks agree with the theorem; and the scalar identity that fixes the
constant 1/54 is machine-checked in Lean 4. No priority conflict was found in a bounded search.

The exponent 19/7 is the output of the proof and depends on the exact shape of the cited decomposition
theorem. That shape is confirmed below, so the exponent stands. The theorem's leading term does not
depend on it: any polynomial choice of the clustering parameter with both error terms o(n³) gives
M_n ∼ n³/54.

## What was checked, and how

### The problem is what the paper says it is

Aldous and Fill, Chapter 6, Section 6.3.2, Open Problem 6.13, read at the source URL: "max τ₀ ∼ n³/54,
max τ₂ ∼ n³/54", over n-vertex graphs, with τ₀ Kemeny's constant. Open Problem 6.14 is the regular-graph
question (max τ₀ ∼ n²/4) and is not claimed. The τ₂ half is the theorem of Aksoy, Chung, Tait and Tobin
(2018); the paper credits it and recovers it as Corollary 5.2 without counting it as new. The scope
stated in Section 8 matches the source exactly.

### The convention

The paper uses K(G) = Σ_{j≥2} 1/(1−λ_j) with E_v T_v = 0, which is Aldous–Fill's τ₀. The resistance form
K = (1/2m) Σ_{u<v} d_u d_v R(u,v) follows from the commute identity E_uT_v + E_vT_u = 2m R(u,v) and
Kemeny's identity; re-derived, and the checker compares it against the fundamental-matrix formula on
158 graphs in exact arithmetic. An eigenvalue −1 contributes 1/2, so bipartite graphs need no separate
treatment.

### Each lemma, re-derived

* Lemma 2.1 (resistance identity, coarse bound). Correct. K ≤ m(n−1) since R ≤ n−1 and Σ_{u<v} d_u d_v ≤ 2m².
* Lemma 2.2 (comparison under deletion). Correct. Expanding (d^H+r)(d^H+r) with R_G ≤ R_H and R_H ≤ n−1:
  the d^H d^H term gives (m_H/m_G)K(H); the cross terms are bounded by (n−1)(2m_H)(2q)/(2m_G); the rr term by
  (n−1)·2q²/(2m_G). With m_H = m_G − q the fraction is (n−1)(2q − q²/m_G). The checker verifies the inequality
  on 1,942 deletions.
* Lemma 3.1 (bridge-tree identity). Correct. A side S of a quotient edge has volume
  2Σ_{i∈S}m_i + 2(|S|−1) + 1 = 2Wp(S) − 1; the product of the two sides is 4W²p(1−p) − 2W + 1; summing
  over bridges with E d_Q(X,Y) = 2Σ_e p_e(1−p_e) gives (3.4). Verified exactly on all 995 connected graphs
  of order ≤ 7.
* Lemma 3.2 (Hölder). Correct: 1 = Σ (p_i²)^{1/3}(√p_i)^{2/3} ≤ Q₂^{1/3}S^{2/3}. Equality forces two entries of 1/2.
* Proposition 3.3 (uniform sharp bridge bound). Correct. Re-derived: small-block mass 1−P ≤ γ/(2κ√n) + 1/(κn);
  the capacity–mass inequality A ≤ γ(s+a_n)²/(2PS²) from √P·S = W^{−1/2}Σ_I √(m_i+1) ≤ W^{−1/2}(√(γ/2)·sn + √n);
  the distance budget n^{−1}E d_Q ≤ P²(1−Q₂)(1−s+n^{−1/2}) + 2(1−P), where a path between two large blocks
  visits at most (1−s)n small blocks and √n large ones; then A·P²(1−Q₂)(…) ≤ (γ/2)P(s+a_n)²(1−s+n^{−1/2})(1−Q₂)/S²
  ≤ (γ/8)(s+a_n)²(1−s+n^{−1/2}), and the O(n^{−1/2}) terms are uniform in s. The scalar step
  1/54 − s²(1−s)/8 = (3s−2)²(3s+1)/216 is machine-checked (below). The capacity bound applies to each bridge
  block because a bridge block's edge set is exactly the induced edge set of its vertex set.
* Theorem 4.1 (Alev–Anari–Lau–Oveis Gharan). Checked against arXiv:1711.06530: for a parameter δ the
  inter-cluster weight is ≲ w(E)/δ and the effective-resistance diameter of each induced cluster G[V_i] is
  ≲ δ³·|V|/w(E), measured inside the induced subgraph. This is the form the paper states, with the
  exponents 1 and 3 on δ that the balance in Remark 4.5 uses.
* Lemma 4.2 (counting nontrivial clusters). Correct: R(u,v) ≥ 1/d(u) ≥ 1/(k−1) by Cauchy–Schwarz on the unit
  flow out of u, so a cluster of resistance diameter ε has ≥ 1 + 1/ε vertices. Verified exactly by the checker.
* Proposition 4.3 (tree reduction). Correct. The retained cross edges are bridges of H, resistances add in
  series through cut vertices, the internal sum along any path is ≤ nε²/(1+ε), and (1/2m_H)Σ_{u<v}d_ud_v ≤ m_H.
* Proof of Theorem 1.2. Correct. With δ = n^{2/7}: q_cross = O(n^{12/7}), ε = O(n^{−1/7}), the two reduction
  errors are O(n^{19/7}), the bridge error O(n^{5/2}); 5/2 < 19/7.
* Lemma 5.1 (lower bound). Correct. The antisymmetric test function has π-mean zero by the block-swapping
  automorphism, variance ≥ (2e+1)/m, Dirichlet energy 2/(mℓ), so τ_rel ≥ (2e+1)ℓ/2 and K ≥ τ_rel.
* Theorem 1.1, lower bound. With q = ⌊n/3⌋ and B = K_q: (e+1/2)ℓ = n³/54 + O(n²). Correct.
* Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 1.4 (stability). Correct. Near-equality in (3.12) forces s → 2/3 by the unique
  maximum, then (1−Q₂)/S² → 1/4, then two masses of 1/2 by the ℓ¹-tightness argument; the two upper bounds
  A ≤ 1/9 + o(1) and n^{−1}E d_Q ≤ 1/6 + o(1) multiply to exactly 1/54, so both are tight; the rest is counting.
* Lemma 7.1 and Theorem 1.3. The Motzkin–Straus proof of Turán is standard; every induced subgraph of a
  K_{r+1}-free graph is K_{r+1}-free, so the capacity bound holds with γ_r; the r-partite barbell is
  r-colorable and gives the matching lower bound.

### Large-order and exhaustive checks, added

`checks/large_order_checks.py` (numpy spectral formula, pinned to an exact rational computation on one
barbell to relative error 2.8·10⁻¹⁴; 2.7 s in total):

| n | 54·K/n³ for the balanced complete barbell | (n³/54 − K)/n² |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 1.15701 | −0.0872 |
| 120 | 1.02901 | −0.0645 |
| 480 | 1.00651 | −0.0578 |
| 1440 | 1.00211 | −0.0563 |

The ratio decreases monotonically to 1. The second column converges to about −0.056: the barbell itself is
n³/54 + 0.056n² + o(n²), so the maximum has a positive second-order term of order n² and it is the upper
bound's n^{19/7} that is loose, which bears on the question raised in Section 8.1.

Among complete barbells of orders 60, 120 and 240 the best block size is exactly n/3 in every case.

Over every connected graph of order 2 to 7 (Graph Atlas, 995 graphs) the exact maximizer of K is a barbell
at every order; at n = 7 it is two triangles joined by a path of length two, with K = 12.2083. The paper does
not classify finite maximizers, and this does not classify them either, but at small orders the family the
paper names is the exact answer.

Two balanced complete bipartite blocks joined by a path give 108·K/n³ = 1.0095 at n = 960, the bipartite
constant 1/108 of Theorem 1.3.

### Machine check of the scalar input

`checks/lean/ScalarGap.lean`, Lean 4.34.0, core only, exit code 0: the identity
1/54 − s²(1−s)/8 = (3s−2)²(3s+1)/216 over the rationals, and the cleared form
27s³ − 27s² + 4 = (3s−2)²(3s+1) over the integers. The inequality s²(1−s)/8 ≤ 1/54 on [0,1] is the one-line
consequence that the right side is a square times 3s+1 ≥ 0. A Mathlib-based file would also certify the
Hölder input; Mathlib is not built on the audit machine and building it is hours of compute, so that is not
claimed.

### Priority

Bounded searches on 18 September 2026 found no proof of the Kemeny half. Aksoy–Chung–Tait–Tobin (2018) is
the relaxation-time half. Li–Sun–Yang (arXiv:2608.08797) prove two conjectures of Kim et al. on diameter-two
graphs and complement pairs, not this one. Abiad et al. (arXiv:2609.17481) prove lower bounds
(K ≥ n − 2 + 1/χ), not an upper bound of order n³. Zhu (arXiv:2609.06818) is the regular-graph relaxation
time. This is not an exhaustive priority search.

## Remarks for the author

1. The barbell data above suggest the true remainder is +cn² with c ≈ 0.056. The upper-bound remainder
   n^{19/7} comes entirely from the decomposition theorem's constants; a sharper second-order statement would
   need a different route to the cluster structure.
2. The exact small-order maximizers being barbells for n ≤ 7 is consistent with the stability theorem but is
   far below the orders at which the asymptotic argument bites; the finite-order classification remains open,
   as the paper says.
3. The paper's statement of Theorem 4.1 is faithful to the source; readers may want the citation to name the
   theorem number in the arXiv version as well as the ITCS version.
