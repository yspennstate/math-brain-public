# 3. Proof of `P25-CYCLIC-MULTIORBIT-SIMPLEX-KHOMOLOGY-45`

Let

\[
K=C_q=\langle g\rangle
\]

and let

\[
\Omega\cong K\times\{1,\ldots,s\}.
\]

The real permutation representation is

\[
\mathbb R[\Omega]\cong s\,\mathbb R[K].
\]

Center the simplex at its barycenter. Its affine tangent representation is

\[
V_{q,s}
=
\mathbb R[\Omega]-\mathbb R
\cong
s\,\mathbb R[K]-\mathbb R.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Radial rescaling from the barycenter is `K`-equivariant, hence

\[
(\Delta(\Omega),\partial\Delta(\Omega))
\cong_K
(D(V_{q,s}),S(V_{q,s})).
\tag{3.2}
\]

We compute the real representation `V_{q,s}` up to a complex summand and at most one real line.

## 3.1 Odd cyclic order

Assume `q` odd. The real regular representation has the form

\[
\mathbb R[K]
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus (U_q)_{\mathbb R},
\tag{3.3}
\]

where

\[
U_q=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{(q-1)/2}\chi^j
\]

is a complex `K`-representation. Therefore

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
(s-1)\mathbb R
\oplus
s(U_q)_{\mathbb R}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If `s` is odd, then `s-1` is even and the trivial real summand is itself the underlying real representation of a complex trivial representation. Thus all of `V_{q,s}` has a `K`-equivariant complex structure. Equivariant complex Bott periodicity gives

\[
K_i^K(D(V_{q,s}),S(V_{q,s}))
\cong K_i^K(\mathrm{pt}).
\]

Hence `(0.2)`.

If `s` is even, write

\[
(s-1)\mathbb R
=\mathbb R\oplus (\text{even-dimensional trivial complex part}).
\]

Thus

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus W_{\mathbb R}
\tag{3.5}
\]

for a complex representation `W`. Bott periodicity on `W` followed by suspension by one trivial real line gives

\[
K_i^K(D(V_{q,s}),S(V_{q,s}))
\cong K_{i-1}^K(\mathrm{pt}).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Since finite-group K-homology of a point is `R(K)` in even degree and zero in odd degree, `(3.6)` is exactly `(0.1)`.

## 3.2 Even cyclic order

Assume

\[
q=2r.
\]

Let `sigma` be the real sign representation, so

\[
\ker\sigma=C_r.
\]

The real regular representation decomposes as

\[
\mathbb R[K]
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus\sigma\oplus(U_q)_{\mathbb R},
\tag{3.7}
\]

where the remaining nonreal characters pair to form the complex representation `U_q`. Hence

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
(s-1)\mathbb R
\oplus s\sigma
\oplus s(U_q)_{\mathbb R}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

### `s` even

Then `s-1` is odd, while `s sigma` is an even number of sign lines. Two copies of the real sign line are the underlying real representation of the one-dimensional complex sign character `chi^r`. Consequently

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus W_{\mathbb R}
\]

for a complex representation `W`. The same argument as `(3.6)` gives

\[
K_0^K(D(V),S(V))=0,
\qquad
K_1^K(D(V),S(V))\cong R(K).
\]

This proves `(0.1)` for even `q`.

### `s` odd

Now `s-1` is even and `s sigma` contains exactly one unpaired real sign line. Thus

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
\sigma\oplus W_{\mathbb R}
\tag{3.9}
\]

for a complex representation `W`. Bott periodicity reduces the calculation to the sign-line pair

\[
(D\sigma,S\sigma).
\]

The sphere `S(sigma)` is the orbit

\[
K/C_r.
\]

The pair exact sequence is

\[
0\to K_1^K(D\sigma,S\sigma)
\to R(C_r)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{C_q}}
R(C_q)
\to K_0^K(D\sigma,S\sigma)
\to0.
\tag{3.10}
\]

If `eta_j` is the `j`th character of `C_r`, direct character induction gives

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{C_q}(\eta_j)
=\chi^j+\chi^{j+r}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

The supports in the standard character basis of `R(C_q)` are disjoint pairs, so the induction map is injective. Therefore

\[
K_1^K(D\sigma,S\sigma)=0
\]

and the even group is the cokernel in `(0.3)`.

This completes the proof.

# 4. Orbit-count parity gate for an actual simplicial filtration

We prove `P25-SIMPLEX-ORBIT-COUNT-PARITY-GATE-45`.

Let `G` act simplicially on `X`, freely on vertices. Let `A` be a `G`-invariant subcomplex containing the full boundary of a simplex `sigma`, and suppose we attach the entire `G`-orbit of the interior of `sigma`.

Put

\[
K=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\sigma).
\]

Because the action on vertices is free, no nonidentity element of `K` fixes a vertex. Hence the vertex set of `sigma` is a disjoint union of regular `K`-orbits:

\[
V(\sigma)\cong K\times\{1,\ldots,s\}
\]

for a unique integer `s`.

Interiors of distinct simplex translates are disjoint. Excision and induction give

\[
K_i^G(A\cup G\sigma,A)
\cong
K_i^K(\sigma,\partial\sigma).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Apply Section 3.

If `s` is odd, the relative `K_1` group vanishes. The pair long exact sequence contains

\[
K_1^G(A\cup G\sigma,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(A\cup G\sigma),
\]

so the second map is injective.

If `s` is even, the relative `K_0` group vanishes and the relative `K_1` group is `R(K)`. The long exact sequence gives

\[
0
\longrightarrow
K_1^G(A)
\longrightarrow
K_1^G(A\cup G\sigma)
\]

on the `K_1` side, while any kernel created on `K_0^G(A)` is exactly the image of

\[
R(K)
\cong
K_1^G(A\cup G\sigma,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(A).
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the claimed gate.

## 4.1 Relation to P18-P45

P18-P45 computes a standard proper cell

\[
G/K\times D^n
\]

whose stabilizer fixes the disk pointwise. Its parity is the ordinary dimension `n`.

An original Rips simplex with setwise stabilizer `K` is generally **not** such a cell: `K` permutes its vertices. Barycentric subdivision resolves the inversion into standard cells, but the resulting cellular differential can be difficult to read globally.

Section 3 computes the unsubdivided simplex orbit in one step. The controlling parity is the number `s` of regular `K`-vertex orbits.

For `K=C_q`, the simplex dimension is

\[
\dim\sigma=sq-1.
\]

When `q` is even, this dimension is odd for **every** `s`, but only even `s` can kill `K_0`. Therefore ordinary dimension of the original simplex does not determine the answer.

This is a refinement, not a contradiction, of P18-P45.

## 5.1 Exact word distance on the cyclic subgroup

For every integer `k`,

\[
|a^k|_{\Gamma_L}=|k|_L,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `|k|_L` is circular distance modulo `L`.

The upper bound uses the word `a^k`. The lower bound is the retained circle action

\[
a:x\mapsto x+1,
\qquad b:x\mapsto-x,
\qquad t:x\mapsto x+1/2
\]

on `R/LZ`, whose orbit map is 1-Lipschitz. Thus the distance from `1` to `a^k` is at least the circular displacement `|k|_L`.

## 5.2 The tetrahedron exists exactly at scale `d`

Fix `j` and set

\[
S_j=\{j,j+d,j+1,j+d+1\}\subset\mathbb Z/L.
\]

The pairwise circular differences are among

\[
1,\ d,\ d-1,\ d+1,
\]

and

\[
|d+1|_L=d-1.
\]

Therefore every pair of vertices of `(0.6)` has group distance at most `d`, while the antipodal pairs have distance exactly `d`. Hence `sigma_j` is a `3`-simplex in `P_d Gamma_L` and is not present in `P_{d-1} Gamma_L`.

## 5.3 Exact stabilizer

Left multiplication by `c=a^d` swaps the two vertices in each of the pairs

\[
\{a^j,a^{j+d}\},
\qquad
\{a^{j+1},a^{j+d+1}\},
\]

so `C_2=<c>` stabilizes `sigma_j`.

If `g sigma_j=sigma_j`, then choosing any vertex `x` of `sigma_j` gives

\[
g=(gx)x^{-1}\in C_L,
\]

because both `x` and `gx` are powers of `a`. Thus the stabilizer is a translation stabilizer of the subset `S_j` in `C_L`.

For `d>=4`, translation by `d` preserves `S_j`, while translation by `1`, `d+1`, or `d-1` does not; checking the four possible images of `j` shows that only `0` and `d` preserve the set. Hence the exact stabilizer is `C_2`.

The vertex set has exactly two regular `C_2`-orbits, so Section 3 with `(q,s)=(2,2)` gives `(0.8)`.

## 5.4 Why this is the first genuine obstruction to the P44 shortcut

For a one-orbit `C_2` edge, `(q,s)=(2,1)` and P25-P44 gives relative `K_1=0`: it cannot kill a degree-zero source class.

\]

Thus passing from one orbit to two changes the relevant degree. The nested geometry is not cosmetic; it creates the first nonzero local module from which a critical `K_0` relation may arise.
