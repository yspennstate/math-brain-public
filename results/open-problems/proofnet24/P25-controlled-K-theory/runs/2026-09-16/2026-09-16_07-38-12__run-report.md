# 3. Quantitative affine-height word bound

We prove `P25-M2-AFFINE-HEIGHT-WORD-LOWER-47`.

## 3.1 The affine circle quotient

Let `S_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z` with its geodesic metric. Define isometries

\[
\rho(a)(x)=x+1,
\qquad
\rho(t)(x)=x+\tfrac12,
\qquad
\rho(b)(x)=-x.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The defining relations are respected:

\[
\rho(a)^L=1,
\qquad
\rho(t)\rho(a)\rho(t)^{-1}=\rho(a),
\]

and

\[
\rho(t)\rho(b)\rho(t)^{-1}(x)
=-x+1
=\rho(a)\rho(b)(x).
\]

Hence `(3.1)` defines a genuine quotient action of `\Gamma_L` by circle isometries.

At the base point `0`, the generator displacements are

\[
d(0,a^{\pm1}0)=1,
\qquad
d(0,t^{\pm1}0)=\tfrac12,
\qquad
d(0,b^{\pm1}0)=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let a word `w` for `g` contain `A` letters `a^{\pm1}`, `T` letters `t^{\pm1}`, and `B_0` letters `b^{\pm1}`. Following the orbit of `0` along the word and using left-invariance of the circle action gives

\[
d_{S_L}(0,\rho(g)0)
\le A+\frac T2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

If

\[
\nu:\Gamma_L\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\nu(t)=1,
\quad\nu(a)=\nu(b)=0,
\]

is the height homomorphism, then necessarily

\[
T\ge|\nu(g)|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since the word length is

\[
\ell=A+T+B_0,
\]

we have

\[
A+\frac T2
=\ell-B_0-\frac T2
\le\ell-\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
\]

Taking an infimum over words gives the general estimate

\[
\boxed{
|g|_{\Gamma_L}
\ge
 d_{S_L}(0,\rho(g)0)
 +\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

For `g=a^rt^n`,

\[
\rho(g)0=r+\frac n2\pmod L,
\qquad \nu(g)=n,
\]

so `(3.5)` is exactly `(0.2)`.

## 3.2 Cross-height diameter for `|n|\ge2`

By inversion it suffices to take `n>0`.

If `n=2m`, choose

\[
r=d-m.
\]

Then

\[
r+\frac n2=d,
\]

so `(0.2)` gives

\[
|a^{d-m}t^{2m}|
\ge d+m
\ge d+1.
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `n=2m+1\ge3`, choose

\[
r=d-m.
\]

Then

\[
r+\frac n2=d+\frac12,
\]

whose circular distance from zero is `d-1/2`. Hence

\[
|a^{d-m}t^{2m+1}|
\ge
d-\frac12+\frac{2m+1}{2}
=d+m
\ge d+1.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus `(0.3)` is proved for `|n|\ge2`.

## 3.3 Borderline height one: equality is impossible

Take `g=a^dt`. Formula `(0.2)` only gives

\[
|g|\ge d
\]

because

\[
\left|d+\frac12\right|_L=d-\frac12.
\]

Suppose for contradiction that `|g|=d`. Let a geodesic word have counts `A,T,B_0` as above. Equality in `(3.3)`–`(3.5)` forces

\[
T=1,
\qquad B_0=0,
\qquad A=d-1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Moreover the circle displacement has magnitude

\[
d-\frac12=A+\frac T2,
\]

strictly below the circle diameter `d`. Equality in the path triangle inequality therefore forces all nonzero generator increments in the unique shortest lift to have the same orientation.

If that orientation is positive, the unique `t` letter is `t` and the endpoint displacement is

\[
(d-1)+\frac12=d-\frac12,
\]

whereas `a^dt` acts on zero by

\[
d+\frac12\equiv-(d-\tfrac12)\pmod L.
\]

If the orientation is negative, the unique `t` letter is `t^{-1}`, contradicting the height `\nu(g)=1`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{|a^dt|\ge d+1.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Applying the same argument to the inverse gives

\[
|a^dt^{-1}|\ge d+1.
\]

This completes `(0.3)` for every nonzero height.

## 3.4 Geometric restatement

For any `h\in\mathbb Z`, the horizontal `C_L`-orbit at height `h` is

\[
\mathcal O_h=\{a^jt^h:0\le j<L\}.
\]

If `h'\ne h`, then `(0.3)` gives vertices `x\in\mathcal O_h`, `y\in\mathcal O_{h'}` with

\[
d_{\Gamma_L}(x,y)\ge d+1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal O_h\cup\mathcal O_{h'})>d
\qquad(h\ne h').
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

This is the exact metric obstruction needed below.
