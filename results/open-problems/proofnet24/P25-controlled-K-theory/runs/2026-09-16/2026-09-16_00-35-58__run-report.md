# 4. Proof of the conjugate cyclic length detector

Let `S={a^{+-1},b^{+-1},t^{+-1}}` be the standard generating set. For the basepoint `0 in S_L`,

\[
d_L(0,A^{\pm1}0)=1,
\qquad
d_L(0,B^{\pm1}0)=0,
\qquad
d_L(0,T^{\pm1}0)=1/2.
\]

Therefore the orbit map

\[
\Phi_L:\Gamma_{2,L}\to S_L,
\qquad
\Phi_L(g)=\rho_L(g)0,
\]

is 1-Lipschitz. Indeed, if `g=s_1...s_n` is a word in the standard generators, then by isometric invariance

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_L(0,\rho_L(g)0)
&\le
\sum_{j=1}^n
d_L(\rho_L(s_1...s_{j-1})0,
\rho_L(s_1...s_j)0)\\
&=
\sum_{j=1}^n d_L(0,\rho_L(s_j)0)
\le n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
|g|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}
\ge d_L(0,\rho_L(g)0).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now let `u in Gamma_{2,L}` and `s in Z`. The isometry `rho_L(u)` is either a rotation or a reflection. Conjugating the rotation `A^s` by any circle isometry changes the rotation exponent at most by sign:

\[
\rho_L(u)^{-1}A^s\rho_L(u)=A^{\varepsilon s},
\qquad
\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
d_L(0,\rho_L(u^{-1}a^su)0)=|s|_L.
\]

Combining with `(4.1)` proves

\[
\boxed{
|u^{-1}a^su|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}
\ge |s|_L.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This proves `P25-M2-DIHEDRAL-CIRCLE-LENGTH-DETECTOR-39`.

### Important distinction

The map `(3.3)` is a group homomorphism into an isometry group used only for a word-metric lower bound. It is **not** a map of reduced group C*-algebras and is not used to assert any reduced-C* functoriality.

# 5. Linear finite-isotropy visibility

We now prove `P25-M2-LINEAR-TORSION-VISIBILITY-39`.

Every finite subgroup of `Gamma_{2,L}` maps trivially to the quotient

\[
\Gamma_{2,L}\to\mathbb Z
\]

and hence lies in

\[
H_{2,L}=C_L*\mathbb Z.
\]

A finite-order element of a free product is conjugate into a factor: a cyclically reduced word of syllable length at least two has powers with strictly increasing reduced syllable length. Since the infinite cyclic factor is torsion-free, every finite subgroup of `H_{2,L}` is conjugate to a subgroup of `C_L`.

Let `F` be a nontrivial finite subgroup of order `q>=2`. After conjugation it is generated by `a^{L/q}`. Choose an element of that subgroup whose circular exponent is

\[
\left\lfloor\frac q2\right\rfloor\frac Lq.
\]

For every `q>=2`,

\[
\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}\ge\frac13,
\]

with the minimum attained at `q=3`. Therefore `F` contains an element `f` conjugate to `a^s` with

\[
|s|_L\ge L/3.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Suppose that `F` stabilizes a simplex of `P_D Gamma_{2,L}`. Choose a vertex `g` of that simplex. Since the simplex is `F`-stable, `fg` is another vertex of the same simplex, so

\[
d(g,fg)=|g^{-1}fg|\le D.
\]

By `(4.2)` and `(5.1)`,

\[
|g^{-1}fg|\ge |s|_L\ge L/3.
\]

Thus no nontrivial finite subgroup stabilizes a simplex when

\[
D<L/3.
\]

Point stabilizers in the geometric Rips complex are finite because a stabilizer permutes the finite support of a point and the kernel of this permutation action fixes a vertex, hence is trivial for left translation. Therefore the action is free.

This proves

\[
\boxed{
P_D\Gamma_{2,L}
\text{ is a free }\Gamma_{2,L}\text{-space whenever }D<L/3.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Comparison with the previous general-depth lower bound

P25-P36 used the abelianized nilpotent triangular quotient and obtained, at `m=2`, freeness only while roughly `D^2<L/3`. The circle-isometry quotient exploits a special depth-two symmetry and upgrades this to the linear condition `(5.2)`.

This improvement is special to the present `m=2` representation. No extension to `m>=3` is asserted.
