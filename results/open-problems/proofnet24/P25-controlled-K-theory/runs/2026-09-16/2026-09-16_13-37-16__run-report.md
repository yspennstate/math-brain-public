# 3. Proof of `P25-FESHBACH-ORDERED-BLOCK-GAP-53`

Let `A=A*` and decompose the Hilbert space as

\[
\mathcal H=p\mathcal H\oplus q\mathcal H.
\]

Relative to this decomposition,

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b\\
b^* & d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Fix a real `lambda` with

\[
\beta<\lambda<\delta.
\]

Then

\[
d_\lambda:=d-\lambda q
\ge(\delta-\lambda)q>0,
\]

so `d_lambda` is invertible on `qH`. The Schur complement is

\[
S_\lambda
=a-\lambda p-bd_\lambda^{-1}b^*.
\]

Since `d_lambda^{-1}>=0`,

\[
bd_\lambda^{-1}b^*\ge0,
\]

and therefore

\[
S_\lambda
\le
(\beta-\lambda)p
=-(\lambda-\beta)p<0.
\]

Hence `S_lambda` is invertible. The exact factorization

\[
A-\lambda
=
\begin{pmatrix}1&bd_\lambda^{-1}\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}S_\lambda&0\\0&d_\lambda\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\d_\lambda^{-1}b^*&1\end{pmatrix}
\]

shows that `A-lambda` is invertible. Thus

\[
\sigma(A)\cap(\beta,\delta)=\varnothing.
\]

Since `A` is self-adjoint,

\[
\operatorname{dist}\!\left(c,\sigma(A)\right)
\ge \frac{\delta-\beta}{2}
\]

for `c=(beta+delta)/2`, and hence

\[
\|(A-c)^{-1}\|
\le\frac{2}{\delta-\beta}.
\]

If `A>=0`, the lower spectral half-line is absent, giving

\[
\sigma(A)\subset[0,\beta]\cup[\delta,\|A\|].
\]

### Important point

The coupling `b` can be large. The ordered diagonal gap is not closed by an off-diagonal self-adjoint coupling; the Schur term has the sign that pushes the two spectral sectors apart.

# 4. Proof of `P25-FESHBACH-LOW-BAND-HOMOTOPY-53`

Define

\[
A_t
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&tb\\tb^*&d
\end{pmatrix}
=a+d+t(b+b^*),
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]

The diagonal blocks are independent of `t`, so the proof of Section 3 applies verbatim to every `A_t`:

\[
\sigma(A_t)\cap(\beta,\delta)=\varnothing
\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].
\]

Choose one continuous scalar function `f` such that

\[
f(x)=1\quad(x\le\beta),
\qquad
f(x)=0\quad(x\ge\delta).
\]

Because every `A_t` has no spectrum in the transition interval, each

\[
P_t=f(A_t)
\]

is an actual projection. Norm continuity of `t -> A_t` and continuous functional calculus imply norm continuity of `t -> P_t`.

At `t=0`,

\[
A_0=a\oplus d.
\]

Since `a<=beta p` and `d>=delta q`,

\[
P_0=p.
\]

At `t=1`, `P_1=P`. Therefore `p` and `P` are homotopic through projections in the same C*-algebra, and

\[
\boxed{[P]=[p]\in K_0.}
\]

This is a **stable** statement. It does not assert `P=p`, finite propagation of `P`, stable rank one, or cancellation. The controlled representative is `p`, not the spectral projection itself.

# 5. Proof of `P25-FESHBACH-PROJECTION-DISTANCE-53`

Let

\[
P=\mathbf1_{(-\infty,\beta]}(A),
\qquad P^\perp=1-P.
\]

Set

\[
A_-:=A|_{P\mathcal H},\qquad
A_+:=A|_{P^\perp\mathcal H}.
\]

The spectral gap gives

\[
A_-\le\beta,
\qquad
A_+\ge\delta.
\]

We use an elementary ordered Sylvester estimate. If `B>=delta`, `C<=beta`, and

\[
BZ-ZC=Y,
\]

then

\[
Z=\int_0^\infty e^{-tB}Ye^{tC}\,dt
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\|Z\|\le\frac{\|Y\|}{\delta-\beta}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

No perturbation theorem is required.

## 5.1 The `qP` corner

Put `Y_1=qP`. Since `P` commutes with `A`,

\[
dY_1-Y_1A_-
=-qApP.
\]

Thus by `(5.1)`,

\[
\|(1-p)P\|
=\|qP\|
\le\frac{\|qAp\|}{\delta-\beta}
\le\frac{\alpha}{\delta-\beta}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 5.2 The `P^perp p` corner

Put `Y_2=P^\perp p`. Again using commutation of `P` with `A`,

\[
A_+Y_2-Y_2a
=P^\perp qAp.
\]

Hence

\[
\|P^\perp p\|
\le\frac{\alpha}{\delta-\beta}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For two orthogonal projections,

\[
\|P-p\|
=
\max\{\|(1-p)P\|,\|(1-P)p\|\}.
\]

One can verify this by observing that `(P-p)^2` is block diagonal relative to `P` with diagonal blocks `P(1-p)P` and `P^perp p P^perp`. Combining `(5.2)` and `(5.3)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\|P-p\|
\le\frac{\alpha}{\delta-\beta}.
}
\]

If `alpha<delta-beta`, then `||P-p||<1`; the element

\[
v=Pp+(1-P)(1-p)
\]

is invertible, and the unitary in its polar decomposition conjugates `p` to `P`. This is an **unstable same-matrix-algebra** equivalence. The stronger numerical target `||P-p||<1/2` follows from

\[
2\alpha<\delta-\beta.
\]

and preserves the collected coefficient norms of `a,b,E`.

The coefficient estimate gives

\[
\|a_H\|
\le\|a\|_{1,op;H}
=\|a\|_{1,op;G}
\le\beta,

hence

\[
0\le a_H\le\beta p_H.

Likewise

\[

For the complement,

=

The transferred residual obeys `||E_H||<=epsilon`; after `p_H=p_H^*` is known, `E_H` is self-adjoint. Therefore

\[
E_H\ge-\varepsilon q_H

after compression, and

\[
\boxed{
q_HA_Hq_H
\ge

Thus

# 7. Adversarial tests, counterexamples, and limitations

## 7.1 Block gap does **not** imply `<1/2` projection closeness

For `0<beta<1`, take

\[
p=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\beta&\sqrt\beta\\
\sqrt\beta&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then `A_beta>=0`,

\[
pA_\beta p=\beta p,
\qquad
qA_\beta q=q,
\]

so the diagonal gap `(beta,1)` is genuine. The matrix has eigenvalues `0` and `1+beta`. Its low spectral projection is the kernel projection, and direct calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\|P_\beta-p\|
=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}}.
}
\]

For `beta>1/3` this is already `>1/2`, and as `beta -> 1^-` it tends to `1/sqrt(2)`. Thus a block gap alone gives stable K-homotopy but not the requested `1/2` norm closeness. Coupling/gap control is genuinely needed for that stronger conclusion.

This also illustrates why stable and unstable/norm-close conclusions must not be conflated.

## 7.2 The strict robust gap threshold is necessary

Let `0<beta<delta` and

\[
p=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A=\beta I_2,
\qquad D_j=0.
\]

Then

\[
a=\beta p,
\qquad b=0,
\]

and

\[
E=qAq-\delta q
=-(\delta-\beta)q.
\]

At the endpoint

\[
\varepsilon=\delta-\beta,
\]

one has `beta=delta-epsilon`, so the robust ordered gap closes. Indeed the low projection at energy `beta` is `1`, not `p`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\beta<\delta-\varepsilon}
\]

cannot be weakened to a non-strict inequality in a theorem guaranteeing separation.

## 7.3 First-order sharpness of the distance constant

For a two-by-two ordered block matrix with fixed diagonal gap `g=delta-beta` and off-diagonal coupling `alpha -> 0`, the low eigenspace rotates by angle `alpha/g+O(alpha^3)`. Thus the coefficient `1/g` in the bound

\[
\|P-p\|\le\alpha/g
\]

is first-order sharp, even though the bound can be very non-sharp for large coupling.

## 7.4 Reduced/full and norm/strong distinctions

All group-algebra spectral statements in Section 6 are in `M_n(C_r^*(H))`. No statement about the full group C*-algebra is inferred. No strong-operator or marked convergence is upgraded to norm convergence. The only transferred norm bounds are coefficient-l1 estimates whose validity is representation-independent.

## 7.5 Finite matrix / von Neumann distinction

The low projection `P_H` belongs to the reduced C*-algebra because the ordered block gap makes its characteristic function continuous on `sigma(A_H)`. This is fundamentally different from a soft zero where the Borel kernel projection may exist only in the group von Neumann algebra.

## 7.6 Stable/unstable distinction

- `[P_H]=[p_H]` is stable K0 equality and needs no small-coupling hypothesis.
- `||P_H-p_H||<1` gives actual unitary equivalence in the same matrix algebra.
- `||P_H-p_H||<1/2` is a stronger quantitative statement requiring `2alpha<g_H` under the present estimate.
- None of these implies stable rank one or cancellation for unrelated projections.
