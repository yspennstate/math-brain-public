# 5. Finite mapping-cone killers map naturally to Tate killers

We now prove `P25-FINITE-TO-TATE-KILLER-OCTAHEDRON-44`.

Let

\[
f:P\to Y
\]

be a `C`-map with `P` free. Choose `s:P->EC` and define

\[
a=(s,f):P\to E:=EC\times Y,
\qquad
\varepsilon:E\to Y.
\]

Then

\[
\varepsilon a=f.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Consider the commutative square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
P&\xrightarrow{f}&Y\\
\downarrow a&&\downarrow\mathrm{id}_Y\\
E&\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}&Y.
\end{array}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Functoriality of mapping cones gives

\[
\Theta_f:\operatorname{Cof}(f)
\to\operatorname{Cof}(\varepsilon).
\tag{5.3}
\]

But

\[
\operatorname{Cof}(\varepsilon)
\simeq_C
\widetilde EC\wedge Y_+,
\tag{5.4}
\]

because `(EC\times Y)_+\cong EC_+\wedge Y_+` and `\varepsilon` is induced by `EC_+->S^0` smashed with `Y_+`.

Naturality of the cofiber long exact sequence gives the boundary identity

\[
\partial_{\rm iso}(\Theta_f)_*
=a_*\partial_f.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Suppose

\[
u\in K_0^C(\operatorname{Cof}(f)),
\qquad
\partial_f u=x.
\]

Then `(5.5)` yields

\[
\partial_{\rm iso}(\Theta_f)_*u
=a_*x
=(s,f)_*x
=\widehat x_f.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\tau=(\Theta_f)_*u
\]

is an isotropy-separation killer. If the free-origin class is nonzero, then `tau` cannot vanish because its boundary is nonzero.

Finally, apply the octahedral axiom to the composable maps `(5.1)`. There is a distinguished cofiber triangle

\[
\operatorname{Cof}(a)
\to
\operatorname{Cof}(f)
\xrightarrow{\Theta_f}
\operatorname{Cof}(\varepsilon)
\to
\Sigma\operatorname{Cof}(a).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Using `(5.4)` gives `(0.9)`.

### Consequence

# 6. Cyclic regular simplex: exact equivariant K-homology

We prove `P25-CYCLIC-REGULAR-SIMPLEX-KHOMOLOGY-44`.

Let

\[
K=C_q=\langle g\rangle
\]

act by the regular permutation action on the vertices of `\Delta^{q-1}`.

## 6.1 Centering converts the simplex to a representation disk

Embed the simplex in the real regular representation

\[
\mathbb R[K]
\]

as the convex hull of the basis vectors. Its barycenter is the invariant vector

\[
\frac1q\sum_{h\in K}e_h.
\]

After translating the barycenter to the origin, the affine span is the reduced real regular representation

\[
V_q=\mathbb R[K]-\mathbb R.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The centered simplex is a `K`-invariant convex body containing `0` in its interior. Radial rescaling from the origin gives a `K`-equivariant homeomorphism of pairs

\[
(\Delta^{q-1},\partial\Delta^{q-1})
\cong
(D(V_q),S(V_q)).
\tag{6.2}
\]

So the computation is the complex equivariant K-homology of the representation sphere `S^{V_q}`.

## 6.2 Odd order

Assume `q` is odd. Let `\chi` be the standard complex character of `C_q`. The nontrivial complex characters occur in conjugate pairs, and the reduced real regular representation is the underlying real representation of

\[
W_q
=
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{(q-1)/2}\chi^j.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus

\[
V_q\cong (W_q)_{\mathbb R}.
\]

Complex equivariant Bott periodicity gives

\[
K_i^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))
\cong
K_{i-2\dim_\mathbb C W_q}^K(\mathrm{pt})
\cong K_i^K(\mathrm{pt}).
\tag{6.4}
\]

For finite `K`,

\[
K_0^K(\mathrm{pt})=R(K),
\qquad K_1^K(\mathrm{pt})=0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
K_0^K(\Delta,\partial\Delta)\cong R(K),
\qquad
K_1^K(\Delta,\partial\Delta)=0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

No primality assumption appears.

## 6.3 Even order: isolate the real sign line

Now assume

\[
q=2r.
\]

The reduced real regular representation contains one nontrivial one-dimensional real character, the sign representation

\[
\sigma(g)=-1.
\]

Its kernel is

\[
H=\ker\sigma=\langle g^2\rangle\cong C_r.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The remaining real summand has a complex structure:

\[
V_q
\cong
\sigma
\oplus
(W_q)_{\mathbb R},
\qquad
W_q=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r-1}\chi^j.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Complex Bott periodicity for `W_q` reduces the relative K-homology to the sign-line pair:

\[
K_i^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))
\cong
K_i^K(D(\sigma),S(\sigma)).
\tag{6.9}
\]

The disk `D(\sigma)` contracts `K`-equivariantly to its fixed center, so

\[
K_0^K(D(\sigma))\cong R(K),
\qquad
K_1^K(D(\sigma))=0.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The sphere `S(\sigma)` consists of the two endpoints exchanged by `K/H`; as a `K`-space it is the orbit `K/H`. Hence Shapiro gives

\[
K_0^K(S(\sigma))\cong R(H),
\qquad
K_1^K(S(\sigma))=0.
\tag{6.11}
\]

The map induced by the inclusion

\[
S(\sigma)=K/H\longrightarrow D(\sigma)\simeq\mathrm{pt}
\]

is the covariant induction homomorphism

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_H^K:R(H)\to R(K).
\tag{6.12}
\]

The relative long exact sequence therefore reduces to

\[
0
\to K_1^K(D(\sigma),S(\sigma))
\to R(H)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}_H^K}
R(K)
\to K_0^K(D(\sigma),S(\sigma))
\to0.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Let `\eta_j`, `0<=j<r`, be the characters of `H`. Restriction of `\chi^j` and `\chi^{j+r}` to `H` is `\eta_j`, so Frobenius reciprocity / direct character calculation gives

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_H^K(\eta_j)
=\chi^j+\chi^{j+r}.
\tag{6.14}
\]

The supports of the `r` elements in `(6.14)` are disjoint pairs in the standard basis of `R(K)`. Therefore the induction map is injective.

It follows from `(6.13)` that

\[
\boxed{K_1^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))=0}
\tag{6.15}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
K_0^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))
\cong
R(K)/\operatorname{Ind}_H^K R(H).
}
\tag{6.16}
\]

This quotient is free abelian of rank `q-r=q/2`.

Equations `(6.6)`, `(6.15)`, and `(6.16)` prove `(0.1)`–`(0.3)`.

# 8. Three-cone separation of intrinsic and ambient death

We prove `P25-TRIANGULAR-THREE-CONE-SEPARATION-44` in a general equivariant generalized-homology setting.

Let

\[
P\xrightarrow{q}A\xrightarrow{j}Y,
\qquad f=jq.
\]

Write

\[
Q=\operatorname{Cof}(q),
\quad
M=\operatorname{Cof}(f),
\quad
B=\operatorname{Cof}(j).
\]

The octahedral axiom gives a cofiber triangle

\[
Q\xrightarrow{\alpha}M
\xrightarrow{\beta}B
\xrightarrow{\gamma}\Sigma Q.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Let

\[
x\in K_1^C(P),
\qquad x_A=q_*x.
\]

The three ordinary cofiber exact sequences give immediately `(0.11)`–`(0.13)`.

The square

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
P&\xrightarrow{f}&Y\\
\downarrow q&&\downarrow\mathrm{id}\\
A&\xrightarrow{j}&Y
\end{array}
\]

induces `\beta:M->B`; boundary naturality gives

\[
\partial_j\beta_*=q_*\partial_f.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Assume ambient death. Choose

\[
u_M\in K_0^C(M),
\qquad
\partial_f u_M=x.
\]

Set

\[
u_B=\beta_*u_M.
\]

Then `(8.2)` gives

\[
\partial_j u_B=x_A.
\]

Exactness of `(8.1)` gives

\[
\gamma_*u_B=0.
\]

This proves necessity of `(0.14)`.

Conversely, suppose `u_B` satisfies `(0.14)`. Since `\gamma_*u_B=0`, exactness of `(8.1)` gives a lift

\[
v_M\in K_0^C(M),
\qquad \beta_*v_M=u_B.
\]

By `(8.2)`,

\[
q_*\partial_f v_M
=\partial_j u_B
=x_A=q_*x.
\]

Hence

\[
\partial_f v_M-x\in\ker q_*.
\]

The cofiber sequence of `q` gives

\[
\ker q_*=\operatorname{im}\partial_q.
\]

Choose `v_Q in K_0^C(Q)` with boundary equal to this difference. Naturality in the octahedron identifies the boundary of `\alpha_*v_Q` with the same class (up to the standard consistent sign convention). Replacing `v_M` by

\[
v_M-\alpha_*v_Q
\]

leaves `\beta_*v_M=u_B` unchanged and makes

\[
\partial_f v_M=x.
\]

Therefore `f_*x=0`. This proves the converse and `(0.14)`.
