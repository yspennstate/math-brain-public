# 3. The free-kernel Bredon reduction

## 3.1 `N=ker(pi)` is torsion-free and free

We first prove the algebraic point needed to make the quotient legitimate.

The group

\[
H=C_L*F_{m-1}
\]

is a free product of a finite cyclic group and a free group. Every finite-order element of a free product is conjugate into a finite-order element of a free factor. Thus if

\[
1\ne n\in N
\]

had finite order, we could write

\[
n=u c u^{-1}
\]

with `1 ne c in C`. Applying `pi`, and using that `C` is abelian,

\[
1=\pi(n)=\pi(u)c\pi(u)^{-1}=c,
\]

contradiction. Therefore `N` is torsion-free.

By the Kurosh subgroup theorem, a subgroup of `C_L*F_{m-1}` is a free product of conjugates of intersections with the factors and a free group. Since `N` has trivial intersection with every conjugate of the finite cyclic factor, all remaining factors are free. Hence

\[
\boxed{N\text{ is free and torsion-free}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

No arithmetic property of `L` is involved.

## 3.2 `N` acts freely on every proper `H`-space

Let `X` be a proper `H`-CW complex. Every point stabilizer `H_x` is finite. If `n in N` fixes `x`, then

\[
n\in N\cap H_x.
\]

The left side is torsion-free and the right factor finite, so

\[
n=e.
\]

Therefore the restricted `N`-action is free. Since `N` is discrete and `X` is proper, the action is proper as well.

Because `N normal H`, the quotient

\[
Y=N\backslash X
\]

carries the residual action of

\[
C=H/N\cong C_L.
\]

## 3.3 Exact stabilizer correspondence

For `x in X`, suppose `hN in C` fixes `Nx`. Then

\[
Nhx=Nx.
\]

Hence there is `n in N` with

\[
nhx=x,
\]

so `nh in H_x` and

\[
hN=(nh)N\in\pi(H_x).
\]

The reverse inclusion is immediate. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_C(Nx)=\pi(H_x).
\]

Moreover

\[
\ker(\pi|_{H_x})=H_x\cap N=\{e\},
\]

so `pi|_{H_x}` is an isomorphism onto the residual stabilizer.

This proves `(0.2)`. In particular `Y` is proper, and every isotropy group is retained with the same abstract finite cyclic subgroup order.

## 3.4 Equivariant K-homology isomorphism

There are two equivalent ways to see `(0.1)`. The transformation groupoids

\[
H\ltimes X
\quad\text{and}\quad
C\ltimes(N\backslash X)
\]

are equivalent because `N` acts freely and properly. To keep the proof fully local and auditable, we instead use cells.

A proper `H`-cell has the form

\[
H/K\times D^q
\]

with `K` finite. Because `N cap K={e}`, its `N`-quotient is

\[
N\backslash(H/K\times D^q)
\cong
C/\pi(K)\times D^q.
\]

Shapiro/induction gives

\[
K_*^H(H/K)
\cong K_*^K(\mathrm{pt}),
\]

while

\[
K_*^C(C/\pi(K))
\cong K_*^{\pi(K)}(\mathrm{pt}).
\]

The stabilizer isomorphism `K cong pi(K)` identifies these groups. The identification is compatible with attaching maps because it is induced by the quotient of the actual `H`-cell, not by a choice of orbit representatives.

Equivariant K-homology is a generalized homology theory on proper CW pairs. Induction on the skeleta, using the long exact sequences for successive cell attachments and the five lemma, therefore gives a natural isomorphism

\[
\mathcal Q_X:K_*^H(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}K_*^C(N\backslash X).
\]

For the finite-overlap complexes used below, only finitely many orbit cells occur at every fixed `D`, so no continuity subtlety is needed.

This proves `P25-TRIANGULAR-FREE-KERNEL-BREDON-REDUCTION-43`.

\qquad h\in H,\ n\in\mathbb Z.

then height forces `n_1=n_2`, and equality of left `N`-cosets is equivalent to

\pi(h_1)=\pi(h_2).

Thus `(0.3)` is a bijection.

## 4.2 Pascal exponents

Let `ab:H->Z/L e_1 direct-sum Z e_2 direct-sum ... direct-sum Z e_m` be abelianization. The automorphism induced by `alpha_m` is the unitriangular operator

\[
T=I+S,
\qquad
S(e_j)=e_{j-1},
\quad
S(e_1)=0.
\]

Since `S^m=0`, for every integer `n` one has the finite generalized-binomial identity

\[
T^n
=
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom nk S^k.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For negative `n`, this is the standard finite expansion of `(I+S)^n`, valid because `S` is nilpotent.

The `x_1`-coordinate of `T^n e_j` is therefore

\[
\binom{n}{j-1}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\pi(\alpha_m^n(x_j))
=x_1^{\binom{n}{j-1}}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now

\[
h t^n x_j
=h\alpha_m^n(x_j)t^n,
\]

so passing to `N`-cosets gives `(0.5)`. Equation `(0.4)` is immediate.

A private exact diagnostic checked `(4.1)` and the first-coordinate formula for `m=2,3,4,5` and `-4<=n<=4`; all checks passed. This computation is only a sanity test. The proof is the nilpotent binomial identity above.
