\[
G=C_2\times\mathbb Z
=\langle s,u\mid s^2=1,\;su=us\rangle

\[
p=\frac{1+s}{2},
\qquad
q=\frac{1-s}{2}.

\[
\alpha=\frac{\sqrt M+\sqrt\delta}{2},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{\sqrt M-\sqrt\delta}{2},
\qquad
C=q(\alpha-\beta u),
\]

\[
A=C^*C
=q\,|\alpha-\beta u|^2.
\tag{0.7}
\]

Fourier transform in the `Z` direction and the two characters of `C_2` give

\[
C_r^*(G)\cong C(\mathbb T)\oplus C(\mathbb T),

\[
A(e^{i\theta})
=|\alpha-\beta e^{i\theta}|^2,
\]

whose range is

\[
[(\alpha-\beta)^2,(\alpha+\beta)^2]=[\delta,M].
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(A)=\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad
\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)=p.
}
\tag{0.8}
\]

The exact kernel projection therefore has propagation `1`, independent of the gap.

The example even has an exact finite SOS gap certificate. Since

\[
|\alpha-\beta u|^2-\delta
=\alpha\beta(1-u)^*(1-u),

\[
B=\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,q(1-u)(\alpha-\beta u).

Commutativity gives

\[
\boxed{
A^2-\delta A=B^*B.

# 3. Proof of `P25-CHEBYSHEV-UNIFORM-CUTOFF-MINIMAX-50`

Fix `0<delta<M` and set

\[
\phi(x)=\frac{M+\delta-2x}{M-\delta}.
\]

Then

\[
\phi:[\delta,M]\to[-1,1]
\]

is an affine bijection and

\[
\phi(0)=c:=\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}>1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a real polynomial `p` of degree at most `n`, define

\[
r(t):=p(\phi^{-1}(t)).
\]

Then `deg r<=n`, `r(c)=p(0)=1`, and

\[
\sup_{x\in[\delta,M]}|p(x)|
=
\sup_{t\in[-1,1]}|r(t)|.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the problem is equivalent to minimizing the sup norm on `[-1,1]` among degree-`n` polynomials taking value `1` at the exterior point `c>1`.

## 3.1 Exterior-point Chebyshev inequality, proved directly

We prove:

> If `s` is a real polynomial of degree at most `n` and `|s(t)|<=1` for `t in [-1,1]`, then for every `c>1`,
> \[
> |s(c)|\le T_n(c).
> \tag{3.3}
> \]

It suffices to treat `s(c)>0`, replacing `s` by `-s` otherwise.

Suppose for contradiction that

\[
s(c)>T_n(c).
\]

Set

\[
\lambda:=\frac{T_n(c)}{s(c)},
\qquad 0<\lambda<1,
\]

and

\[
h(t):=T_n(t)-\lambda s(t).
\]

Then `h(c)=0`.

At the `n+1` Chebyshev extrema

\[
t_k=\cos\frac{k\pi}{n},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,n,
\]

one has

\[
T_n(t_k)=(-1)^k.
\]

Since `|lambda s(t_k)|<1`, the sign of `h(t_k)` is exactly `(-1)^k`. Therefore `h` has at least one root in each interval

\[
(t_{k+1},t_k),
\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.
\]

That gives `n` distinct roots in `(-1,1)`, plus the root `c>1`, for at least `n+1` distinct roots. But `deg h<=n`, contradiction. This proves `(3.3)`.

## 3.2 Exact minimax value

Let

\[
E=\sup_{[-1,1]}|r|.
\]

Since `r(c)=1`, `E>0`. Apply `(3.3)` to `s=r/E`:

\[
\frac1E=|s(c)|\le T_n(c).
\]

Hence

\[
E\ge\frac1{T_n(c)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equality is attained by

\[
r_n(t)=\frac{T_n(t)}{T_n(c)},
\]

because `r_n(c)=1` and `|T_n(t)|<=1` on `[-1,1]`. Undoing the affine change of variables gives exactly `(0.2)`. Therefore

\[
E_n(\delta,M)=\frac1{T_n(c)}.
\]

This proves `(0.1)`.

## 3.3 Exact degree threshold

For `c>1`,

\[
T_n(c)=\cosh\bigl(n\operatorname{arcosh}c\bigr).
\]

The condition `E_n<=eta` is therefore equivalent to

\[
\cosh\bigl(n\operatorname{arcosh}c\bigr)\ge\frac1\eta,
\]

or

\[
n\ge
\frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\eta)}{\operatorname{arcosh}c}.
\]

Taking the least integer proves `(0.3)`.

## 3.4 Closed denominator formula

Put

\[
x=\sqrt{\delta/M}\in(0,1).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}
=\frac{1+x^2}{1-x^2}.
\]

The elementary identity

\[
\cosh(2\operatorname{artanh}x)
=\frac{1+x^2}{1-x^2}
\]

gives `(0.4)` and hence `(0.5)`.

Finally,

\[
\operatorname{artanh}x=x+\frac{x^3}{3}+O(x^5),
\]

which yields `(0.6)`.

This completes the proof.

# 4. Consequence for the P49 controlled representative

P49 chooses `eta=eta_beta` so that

\[
\eta_\beta(1+\eta_\beta)=\beta/2
\]

and then chooses a polynomial with positive-spectrum sup norm at most `eta_beta`.

The new theorem says that **no other uniform polynomial cutoff can asymptotically remove the square-root gap factor**. For any theorem that insists on:

1. one real polynomial `p`;
2. normalization `p(0)=1`;
3. uniform control on the whole interval `[delta,M]`;
4. correction to the kernel by thresholding near `1/2`;

the exact minimal degree is `(0.5)`.

Thus the P49 upper bound

\[
 n_\beta
 =
 \left\lceil
 \frac12\sqrt{M/\delta}
 \log(2/\eta_\beta)
 \right\rceil
\]

is asymptotically optimal up to replacing

\[
\log(2/\eta_\beta)
\]

by the exact slightly smaller constant

\[
\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\eta_\beta).
\]

### Important logical boundary

This is a **degree** lower bound for uniform polynomial functional calculus. It is not yet a propagation lower bound in an arbitrary group algebra, because high polynomial degree can undergo algebraic word cancellations. More importantly, Section 5 shows that an entirely different controlled representative can have constant propagation even when every polynomial cutoff has diverging degree.

No stronger claim is made.

# 5. Proof of `P25-POLYNOMIAL-DEBT-NOT-INTRINSIC-K-CONTROL-50`

Let

\[
G=C_2\times\mathbb Z
\]

with commuting generators `s,u`, where `s^2=1`. In the reduced group algebra set

\[
p=\frac{1+s}{2},
\qquad
q=\frac{1-s}{2}.
\]

Because `s=s^*=s^{-1}` and `s^2=1`,

\[
p^2=p=p^*,
\qquad
q^2=q=q^*,
\qquad
pq=0,
\qquad
p+q=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Both have word propagation `1`.

Fix `0<delta<M` and define `alpha,beta,C,A` as in `(0.7)`.

## 5.1 Exact spectrum and exact kernel projection

The regular representation of the direct product decomposes by the two characters of `C_2` and Fourier transform on `Z`. Equivalently,

\[
C_r^*(C_2\times\mathbb Z)
\cong
C_r^*(C_2)\otimes C_r^*(\mathbb Z)
\cong
(C\oplus C)\otimes C(\mathbb T)
\cong
C(\mathbb T)\oplus C(\mathbb T).
\]

On the `p` summand, `q=0`, hence `A=0`.

On the `q` summand,

\[
A(z)=|\alpha-\beta z|^2
=\alpha^2+\beta^2-\alpha\beta(z+\bar z),
\qquad |z|=1.
\]

Its minimum and maximum are

\[
(\alpha-\beta)^2=\delta,
\qquad
(\alpha+\beta)^2=M.
\]

The range is the full interval `[delta,M]` by continuity. Hence `(0.8)` follows, and the kernel projection is the exact finite-propagation element `p`.

## 5.2 Exact SOS gap certificate

Let

\[
c(u)=\alpha-\beta u.
\]

Then

\[
c^*c-\delta
=\alpha\beta(2-u-u^{-1})
=\alpha\beta(1-u)^*(1-u).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `q` is central and all `u`-polynomials commute,

\[
A=q c^*c.
\]

Define

\[
B=\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\,q(1-u)c.
\]

Then

\[
B^*B
=q c^*\bigl[\alpha\beta(1-u)^*(1-u)\bigr]c
=q c^*c(c^*c-\delta)
=A^2-\delta A.
\]

This proves `(0.9)`.

Thus the example lies exactly in the P49/P59 certified-gap regime; it is not a no-gap loophole.

## 5.3 Polynomial lower bound versus structural projection

For any real polynomial `f` with `f(0)=1`,

\[
f(A)-p
\]

is zero on the `p` summand exactly when `f(0)=1`, while on the `q` summand it is multiplication by `f(x)` over the full interval `x in [delta,M]`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|f(A)-p\|
=
\sup_{x\in[\delta,M]}|f(x)|.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If the norm is at most `eta<1`, Section 3 forces

\[
\deg f\ge n_*(\eta;\delta,M).
\]

For fixed `M,eta` this diverges like `delta^{-1/2}` as `delta -> 0`.

But the exact projection `p` itself has propagation `1` for every `delta`. Hence no universal lower bound of order `delta^{-1/2}` can hold for arbitrary controlled representatives of the same kernel class.

This proves `P25-POLYNOMIAL-DEBT-NOT-INTRINSIC-K-CONTROL-50`.

# 6. Strategic consequence for moving Baum–Connes control

The previous P25 report exposed the sufficient marked-local budget

The current run separates two very different questions.

## 6.1 What is now rigid

If the proof route uses only a **uniform polynomial spectral cutoff in the positive matrix itself**, then the `sqrt(M_L/delta_L)` degree debt cannot be improved asymptotically. Chebyshev is already minimax.

Therefore future work should not spend time trying to replace P49's Chebyshev polynomial by a different polynomial and expect a better gap exponent.

## 6.2 What is not rigid

The group-algebra counterexample shows that a kernel class can have a short algebraic projection even when every polynomial-in-`A` cutoff is expensive. Hence the P49 radius is not an intrinsic lower bound on controlled `K_0`.

A class-sensitive permanence theorem may beat the gap debt if it transports additional algebraic structure, for example:

- a finite-support idempotent `p` with `pA=Ap=0` and a certified inverse/gap on `(1-p)`;
- a block decomposition exposing the kernel summand;
- a finite mapping-cone splitting or partial isometry identifying the kernel class without approximating the discontinuous characteristic function by a polynomial;
- a spectral projection furnished directly by another finite algebraic relation.

This is exactly the kind of substitute still allowed by P01-P60 after the GLSZ functional-calculus route is closed.

## 6.3 Full versus reduced and norm versus strong distinctions

Nothing in this run constructs a homomorphism between reduced group `C*`-algebras of different groups. The example lives in one fixed reduced group algebra.

The distinction remains:

- `A` is a finite group-ring positive element;
- `p=1_{\{0\}}(A)` is an actual reduced-`C*` projection in the structural example;
- in the GLSZ example, the analogous von-Neumann kernel projection is **not** produced in `C_r^*` and cannot be norm-approximated from `C^*(D,1)`;
- strong/tracial convergence does not replace norm control;
- scalar Baum–Connes is not replaced by Baum–Connes with coefficients;
- stable quantitative `K_0` representatives are not silently identified with unstable matrix projections without the usual stabilization map.

## Independent derivation

P01 supplies a positive operator `D` with nonzero von-Neumann kernel projection

\[
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]

and positive spectral atoms

\[
\lambda_n\downarrow0.
\]

Let `f in C(sigma(D))` and write `c=f(0)`.

On `ker D`,

\[
(f(D)-P)|_{\ker D}=(c-1)I,
\]

hence

\[
\|f(D)-P\|\ge|1-c|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

On the nonzero spectral subspace for `lambda_n`, `P=0` and `f(D)=f(lambda_n)`, hence

\[
\|f(D)-P\|\ge|f(\lambda_n)|
\]

for every `n`. Continuity and `lambda_n->0` imply

\[
\|f(D)-P\|\ge|c|.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\|f(D)-P\|
\ge\max\{|c|,|1-c|\}
\ge\frac12.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The constant function `f=1/2` attains `1/2` because `P` is a nontrivial projection. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{dist}(P,C^*(D,1))=\frac12.
\]

If `f(0)=1`, `(7.3)` gives norm at least `1`, attained by `f=1`.

For the threshold-correction statement, if a real polynomial `q` has `q(0)>1/2`, continuity forces `q(lambda_n)>1/2` for all sufficiently large `n`, so thresholding necessarily includes positive spectral subspaces. If `q(0)<1/2`, it misses the kernel. At equality there is no open threshold separating zero from the accumulating positive atoms.
