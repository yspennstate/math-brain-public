## 2. Main new controlled lemma: a finite-propagation Chebyshev representative for an isolated kernel projection

The P01 memo already proves that a uniform positive spectral gap makes a kernel projection stable inside one fixed C*-algebra. The missing controlled ingredient is a **finite-propagation** representative that makes sense before one has a homomorphism between the different reduced C*-algebras.

I now prove such a representative with explicit constants.

### Theorem P25-CSP (controlled spectral projection polynomial)

Let `A=A*>=0` be an element of a unital C*-algebra with

`spec(A) ⊂ {0} ∪ [δ,M]`,  with `0<δ<M`.

Let `P=1_{ {0} }(A)` be the kernel projection. Define

`a=(M+δ)/(M-δ)>1`,

`z(x)=(2x-(M+δ))/(M-δ)`,

and, for `n>=1`,

`p_n(x)=T_n(z(x))/T_n(z(0))`,

where `T_n` is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Then:

1. `p_n(0)=1`;
2. for every `x in [δ,M]`, `|p_n(x)| <= ε_n := 1/T_n(a)`;
3. writing `θ = arcosh(a) = 2 artanh sqrt(δ/M)`, one has `ε_n <= 2 exp(-n θ) <= 2 exp(-2n sqrt(δ/M))`;
4. therefore `||p_n(A)-P|| <= ε_n`;
5. `p_n(A)` is a self-adjoint `ε_n(1+ε_n)`-idempotent: `||p_n(A)^2-p_n(A)|| <= ε_n(1+ε_n)`;
6. whenever `ε_n<1/2`, spectral rounding at `1/2` recovers the exact projection: `1_{(1/2,∞)}(p_n(A)) = P`.

If `A` is a finite-propagation group-ring matrix with propagation/support radius at most `R`, then `p_n(A)` has propagation at most `nR`.

### Proof

The affine map `z` sends `[δ,M]` onto `[-1,1]`, while `z(0)=-(M+δ)/(M-δ)=-a`. Hence `p_n(0)=1`. On `[-1,1]`, `|T_n|<=1`; therefore on `[δ,M]`, `|p_n(x)| <= 1/|T_n(a)|`.

For `a>1`, `T_n(a)=cosh(n arcosh a)`, so `1/T_n(a) <= 2e^{-nθ}`. Putting `r=sqrt(δ/M)`, `cosh(2 artanh r)=(1+r^2)/(1-r^2)=(M+δ)/(M-δ)=a`, hence `θ=2 artanh r >=2r`, which gives the second exponential estimate.

Functional calculus now proves (4). On the kernel, `p_n(A)=1`; on the positive spectral subspace its spectrum lies in `[-ε_n,ε_n]`, so `|t^2-t| <= ε_n(1+ε_n)` there, proving (5). If `ε_n<1/2`, the only spectrum of `p_n(A)` above `1/2` is the eigenvalue `1` carried by `ker A`, proving (6). Finally, a degree-`n` polynomial in a propagation-`R` element has propagation at most `nR`.

**Interpretation.** A uniform gap `δ` produces an explicit controlled representative at scale `nR = O(R sqrt(M/δ) log(1/ε))`. This square-root gap dependence is sharper than the elementary heat/resolvent approximation `O((M/δ)log(1/ε))` obtained from `(1-A/M)^n`.

## 3. Marked-group transport theorem without reduced C*-functoriality

The preceding polynomial gives a direct theorem tailored to P01's missing-functoriality situation.

Let `F_S` be the free group on a fixed finite marking `S`, and let `G_i -> G` in the marked-group topology. Fix a matrix `B in M_k(C[F_S])`, and let `B_i,B_∞` denote its evaluations in the groups. Put `A_i=B_i^*B_i`, `A=B_∞^*B_∞`. Because the group-ring expression is fixed, there is a common finite propagation radius `R` and a common coefficient bound `M` with `||A_i||,||A||<=M` for all `i`.

### Theorem P25-MG (uniform-gap transport)

Assume there is a single `δ>0` such that, for all sufficiently large `i`, `spec(A_i) ⊂ {0} ∪ [δ,M]`. Then:

**(a)** the limit also has the same gap: `spec(A) ⊂ {0} ∪ [δ,M]`;

**(b)** the kernel projections `P_i=1_{0}(A_i) in M_k(C_r^*(G_i))`, `P=1_{0}(A) in M_k(C_r^*(G))` exist in their respective reduced matrix algebras;

**(c)** their canonical von-Neumann traces converge: `Tr_k⊗τ_{G_i}(P_i) -> Tr_k⊗τ_G(P)`;

**(d)** more quantitatively: if the marked relations of `G_i` and `G` agree for every word that can occur in degree at most `n` in the fixed expression for `A`, then `|τ_k(P_i)-τ_k(P)| <= 2k ε_n <= 4k e^{-nθ}`, where `θ=2 artanh sqrt(δ/M)` and `τ_k=Tr_k⊗τ` is the unnormalized matrix canonical trace.

### Proof of the gap passage

For every sufficiently large `i`, functional calculus gives `A_i^2-δA_i >=0`, because `x(x-δ)>=0` on `{0}∪[δ,M]`.

Take any finitely supported vector `ξ` in `l2(G)^k`. Choose word representatives for its finitely many group elements. Marked convergence implies that, for all sufficiently large `i`, every multiplication/equality relation needed to evaluate `< (A^2-δA) ξ, ξ >` is identical in `G_i` and `G`. Thus this quadratic form equals the corresponding nonnegative form for `A_i`. Hence `< (A^2-δA) ξ, ξ > >=0` for every finitely supported `ξ`. Density gives `A^2-δA>=0`. Since `A>=0`, spectral mapping forces `spec(A)⊂{0}∪[δ,∞)`, and the common norm bound cuts this to `{0}∪[δ,M]`.

This is important: **one does not need upper reduced-norm convergence to pass a uniform polynomial spectral inequality.**

### Proof of trace transport

Take the polynomial `p_n` from P25-CSP. For sufficiently deep marked agreement, the identity coefficient of every entry of `p_n(A_i)` is exactly the same as that of `p_n(A)`, because it depends on only finitely many word relations. Therefore `τ_k(p_n(A_i))=τ_k(p_n(A))`.

The Chebyshev estimate gives `||P_i-p_n(A_i)||<=ε_n`, `||P-p_n(A)||<=ε_n`. Since the norm of the unnormalized matrix trace is `k`, `|τ_k(P_i)-τ_k(P)| <= k||P_i-p_n(A_i)|| + k||P-p_n(A)|| <=2kε_n`.

For each fixed `n`, marked convergence eventually supplies the required finite relation agreement; then send `n->∞`.

### Controlled-K-theory interpretation

Although there is generally **no** homomorphism `C_r^*(G_i)->C_r^*(G)`, the polynomial `p_n(A)` supplies a common finite-word, finite-propagation almost-idempotent. At any fixed tolerance, sufficiently late marked groups literally see the same controlled polynomial expression. Rounding it separately inside each reduced C*-algebra gives the kernel projections.

This is weaker than a full quantitative Baum–Connes functoriality theorem, but it is stronger than mere lower-semicontinuity of reduced norm and directly transports one isolated spectral K-class at the level of its controlled representative and canonical trace.

## 4. What this changes for P01

Finn-Sell's withdrawn claim confirms that “norm control in the K-theory component” is a genuine problem. P25-MG shows that, for the **specific kernel-projection route**, the analytic obligation can be weakened:

> It is enough to prove a uniform positive spectral gap for one fixed finite-support positive group-ring matrix along the marked approximants. Full two-sided convergence of reduced norms of arbitrary K-theory representatives is not needed to transport that matrix's kernel trace.

Equivalently, a uniform inequality `A_i^2 >= δ A_i` on all late stages is a complete finite-propagation certificate for this fixed projection route.

This does **not** prove scalar Baum–Connes. It also does not supply a missing finite matrix for the Fournier-Facio–Sauer trace. It only sharpens the proof-side bottleneck from a broad “reduced-norm/resolvent control theorem” to a concrete **uniform-gap certificate** whenever the desired K-class is a kernel projection of a fixed finite group-ring matrix.

A useful contrapositive follows. Suppose a fixed matrix has a gap `δ_∞>0` in the limit and, along a subsequence, the canonical kernel traces stay separated from the limit trace by `Δ>0`. If local relation agreement is deep enough to support degree `n`, then the approximant gaps cannot all be at least a fixed `δ`: otherwise P25-MG would give `Δ <= 4k exp(-2n sqrt(δ/M))`. Thus any such trace discontinuity necessarily forces positive spectrum to collapse toward zero. This gives a precise place to look for failure in lacunary constructions: **small spectrum, not just absence of a reduced quotient map.**

## 5. Independent audit of P18: a concrete theorem in the current source is false as stated

**AUDIT_FOR=P18 — source:** Gmail message `19ed3746ce1eb340`, attachment `master.pdf`, Theorem 12.122, pages 286–287 of the PDF section §12.13.

The theorem claims, for a minimal Cantor system `(X,T)` and Putnam orbit-breaking algebra `A_y`, that `K0(A_y) ≅ C(X,Z)` with pointwise positive cone, and argues that the tower-incidence direct limit is “by construction” all of `C(X,Z)`.

**Verdict: DISPROVED AS STATED.**

### Dyadic odometer counterexample

Let `X = product_{j>=1} {0,1}` and let `T` be addition of `1` with binary carry, the dyadic odometer. Use the standard nested Kakutani–Rokhlin partitions: at level `n` there is one tower of height `2^n`.

For the orbit cut at the distinguished base point `y`, the finite orbit-breaking algebra at level `n` is `A_{y,n} ≅ M_{2^n}`. Under refinement, one tower of height `2^n` becomes the twofold refinement inside the height-`2^{n+1}` tower; on `K0`, with the minimal-projection generator, the connecting map is multiplication by `2`. Therefore `K0(A_y) ≅ lim (Z --×2--> Z --×2--> ...) ≅ Z[1/2]`.

By contrast, `C(X,Z)` contains, for every `m`, the free subgroup generated by the `2^m` disjoint cylinder indicators of length `m`; hence its rational rank is unbounded/infinite. It cannot be isomorphic to the rank-one group `Z[1/2]`.

So the error is exactly in the sentence identifying the **tower incidence dimension group** with raw locally constant functions. The incidence maps impose the dynamical/tower identifications; the limit is a dynamical dimension group, not unquotiented `C(X,Z)`.

This is consistent with Putnam's classical analysis of AF subalgebras for minimal Cantor homeomorphisms and K0 order structure, and with the standard dyadic-odometer dimension group `Z[1/2]`.

### Consequences and repair

The final classical conclusion `K0(C(X)⋊_T Z) ≅ C(X,Z)/(1-T_*)C(X,Z)`, `K1 ≅ Z` is still correct for a minimal Cantor Z-system; it should be obtained from the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence or a correct Putnam/Herman–Putnam–Skau dimension-group computation. The current “self-contained” proof in Theorem 12.122 cannot be retained unchanged.

There is a second internal warning in the same source. Corollary 12.120 claims the borderline criterion `g>=0 iff τ(g)>=0 for every invariant-state trace`. Taken literally for all minimal Cantor systems, this kills every infinitesimal: if a nonzero `g` has every state zero, the criterion makes both `g` and `-g` positive, contradicting antisymmetry. Yet Theorem 12.126 later explicitly discusses systems with nonzero infinitesimal subgroup. Thus the source needs its strict-vs-borderline state-order criterion repaired as well. The **strict** statement (all states uniformly positive implies positivity/order-unit behavior under the usual dimension-group hypotheses) is the safe one; nonnegative trace inequalities do not in general detect infinitesimal directions.

The good news is that Theorem 12.123's factor-map trace functoriality and the abstract gap-labelling conclusion can be salvaged after replacing Theorem 12.122 by the correct PV/Putnam K0 identification. Since the acting group is `Z`, full and reduced crossed products coincide; the factor-map covariant representation really does induce the crossed-product *-homomorphism.

## 6. Independent audit of one P01 lemma

**AUDIT_FOR=P01 — message `1a09edbf662d4195`, reduced quotient functoriality lemma.**

P01 claims that, for a discrete-group quotient `q:G->G/N`, the canonical algebraic quotient extends to `C_r^*(G) -> C_r^*(G/N)` exactly when `N` is amenable.

**Verdict: PROVED.**

The quotient representation is `λ_{G/N}∘q = Ind_N^G(1_N)`. If `N` is amenable, Hulanicki gives `1_N ≺ λ_N`; induction preserves weak containment, and `Ind_N^G λ_N` is a multiple/canonical realization of `λ_G`, so `λ_{G/N}∘q ≺ λ_G`, which is exactly the condition for the reduced quotient map.

Conversely, if the reduced quotient map exists, then `λ_{G/N}∘q ≺ λ_G`. Restricting to `N`, the left side is the trivial representation (with multiplicity), while `λ_G|_N` is a multiple of `λ_N`. Hence `1_N ≺ λ_N`, so `N` is amenable. This confirms P01's use of nonamenability of the filling kernels as a genuine obstruction to naive reduced-C*-functoriality.

## 7. Failed / overstrong routes explicitly ruled out

- **Marked-group convergence alone transports kernel projections:** false. A uniform gap or equivalent spectral inequality is essential; even norm convergence inside one algebra does not preserve the zero projection if a gap collapses (`A` versus `A+εI`).
- **Local group agreement creates a reduced C*-homomorphism:** false. P25-MG deliberately avoids such a map; it compares one finite polynomial expression and its trace.
- **Strong/von-Neumann convergence is enough for K0:** false. The P01 infinite block projection has exactly this failure: strong convergence and finite normal trace do not imply membership in `C_r^*(G)⊗K`.
- **P18's orbit-breaking AF K0 is raw `C(X,Z)`:** false, already contradicted by the dyadic odometer.
- **Trace nonnegativity detects the full positive cone in the presence of infinitesimals:** false without an Archimedean/infinitesimal-free qualification.
