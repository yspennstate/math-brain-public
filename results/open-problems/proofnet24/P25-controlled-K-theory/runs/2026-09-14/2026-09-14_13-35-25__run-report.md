## 2. Previous controlled theorem retained

For later use I keep the exact P25-CSP notation.

Let `A=A*>=0` in a unital C*-algebra and suppose

`spec(A) ⊂ {0} ∪ [δ,M]`, with `0<δ<M`.

For

`a=(M+δ)/(M-δ)`

and

`z(x)=(2x-(M+δ))/(M-δ)`,

define

`p_n(x)=T_n(z(x))/T_n(z(0))`.

Then

`p_n(0)=1`

and

`||p_n(A)-P_ker A|| <= ε_n(δ)`

with

`ε_n(δ)=1/T_n((M+δ)/(M-δ))`

and

`ε_n(δ) <= 2 exp(-2n sqrt(δ/M))`.

If `A` has propagation radius at most `R`, then `p_n(A)` has propagation at most `nR`.

The previous run used this to show: if a fixed positive group-ring matrix has a **uniform** gap on marked approximants, its kernel traces converge without any reduced-C*-homomorphisms.

This run proves the converse quantitative statement in a stronger form: trace mismatch forces not merely one small eigenvalue, but a definite amount of canonical spectral mass near zero.

## 3. New theorem P25-SMC — spectral mass collapse from kernel-trace mismatch

Let `S` be a finite marking and `F_S` the free group. Fix

`A = A* >= 0 in M_k(C[F_S])`.

Write

`A = Σ_g C_g g`

and set the formal support radius

`R = max{|g|_S : C_g != 0}`.

A uniform reduced norm bound valid in every quotient is

`M = Σ_g ||C_g||`,

because every group element acts unitarily and hence every evaluation satisfies `||A_G||<=M`.

Let `G_i` and `G` be `S`-marked groups and let `A_i,A_∞` denote the evaluations of the same formal matrix `A`.

Assume that the marked relations of `G_i` and `G` agree for every reduced free-group word of length at most `nR`. Equivalently for the present purpose,

`τ_k(A_i^j)=τ_k(A_∞^j),  0<=j<=n`,

where

`τ_k = Tr_k ⊗ τ_G`

is the **unnormalized** matrix canonical trace, so `τ_k(1)=k`.

Assume the limit operator has an isolated zero:

`spec(A_∞) ⊂ {0} ∪ [δ_∞,M]`

for some `δ_∞>0`.

Let `μ_i, μ_∞` be the spectral measures of `A_i,A_∞` for `τ_k`; thus each has total mass `k`. Put

`p_i = μ_i({0}) = dim_{N G_i} ker A_i`

and

`p = μ_∞({0}) = dim_{N G} ker A_∞`.

For any `0<η<=δ_∞`, define

`a_η=(M+η)/(M-η)`

and

`ε_{n,η}=1/T_n(a_η)`.

Then:

### P25-SMC(a): one-sided kernel-trace stability

`p_i <= p + 2k ε_{n,η}`.

In particular, for fixed `η>0`, once the relation-agreement degree `n` tends to infinity,

`limsup_i p_i <= p`.

This conclusion requires **no spectral gap at all for the approximants**.

### P25-SMC(b): a kernel-trace deficit forces low positive spectral mass

`μ_i((0,η)) >= [p-p_i-2k ε_{n,η}]_+`.

Thus, if the approximant kernel trace is smaller than the limit kernel trace, the missing trace must reappear as positive spectrum below `η`, up to the explicit Chebyshev error.

### P25-SMC(c): optimized `n^{-2}` collapse scale

Suppose

`p-p_i >= Δ >0`.

Define

`η_{n,Δ}
 = M tanh^2( (1/(2n)) arcosh(4k/Δ) )`.

If `η_{n,Δ}<=δ_∞`, then

`μ_i((0,η_{n,Δ})) >= Δ/2`.

Moreover

`η_{n,Δ}
 <= (M/(4n^2)) arcosh(4k/Δ)^2
 <= (M/(4n^2)) log(8k/Δ)^2`.

So a fixed kernel-trace deficit `Δ` forces at least `Δ/2` of canonical spectral mass into an interval of width `O(M n^{-2} log^2(k/Δ))` above zero.

If the marked relation agreement radius is `L_i` and `n_i=floor(L_i/R)`, then the same statement gives the concrete geometric scale

`η_i
 <= (M R^2/(4(L_i-R)^2)) log(8k/Δ)^2`

whenever `L_i>R`.

This is strictly stronger than the previous P25 gap-collapse statement, which only forced the smallest positive spectral value to go to zero.

## 4. Proof of P25-SMC

Fix `0<η<=δ_∞` and abbreviate

`a=(M+η)/(M-η)`,

`z(x)=(2x-(M+η))/(M-η)`,

`q(x)=T_n(z(x))/T_n(z(0))`,

`ε=1/T_n(a)`.

Because `q` has degree `n`, exact equality of the first `n` canonical moments gives

`∫ q dμ_i = ∫ q dμ_∞`.                                      (4.1)

The key extra observation, beyond the previous run, is the **sign** of the Chebyshev approximant below the cutoff.

For `0<=x<=η`, one has `z(x)∈[-a,-1]`. Hence

`T_n(z(x))
 = (-1)^n cosh(n arcosh(-z(x)))`

and

`T_n(z(0))
 = (-1)^n cosh(n arcosh(a))`.

Therefore

`ε <= q(x) <= 1,  0<=x<=η`.                                 (4.2)

For `η<=x<=M`, `z(x)∈[-1,1]`, so

`|q(x)|<=ε`.                                                  (4.3)

Since the positive spectrum of the limit begins at `δ_∞>=η`, (4.3) gives

`|∫ q dμ_∞ - p| <= k ε`.                                     (4.4)

For the approximant, the contribution from `(0,η)` is nonnegative by (4.2), while the contribution from `[η,M]` is bounded below by `-kε`. Hence

`∫ q dμ_i >= p_i-kε`.                                        (4.5)

Combining (4.1), (4.4) and (4.5),

`p_i-kε <= p+kε`,

which proves

`p_i <= p+2kε`.

This is part (a).

For part (b), use instead the upper estimate

`∫ q dμ_i
 <= p_i + μ_i((0,η)) + kε`,                                 (4.6)

because `q<=1` on `(0,η)` and `|q|<=ε` above `η`. On the limit side, (4.4) also gives

`∫ q dμ_∞ >= p-kε`.                                          (4.7)

Combine (4.1), (4.6), (4.7):

`p-kε
 <= p_i + μ_i((0,η)) + kε`.

Thus

`μ_i((0,η))
 >= p-p_i-2kε`,

and positivity gives the displayed positive-part form.

For part (c), choose `η=η_{n,Δ}`. If

`r=sqrt(η/M)`

then by construction

`2 artanh(r)
 = n^{-1} arcosh(4k/Δ)`.

Also

`a=(1+r^2)/(1-r^2)
   = cosh(2 artanh r)`.

Therefore

`T_n(a)
 = cosh(n arcosh a)
 = 4k/Δ`,

so

`ε=Δ/(4k)`.

Part (b) gives

`μ_i((0,η)) >= Δ-2k(Δ/(4k)) = Δ/2`.

Finally `tanh x<=x` gives

`η <= M arcosh(4k/Δ)^2/(4n^2)`,

and `arcosh x<=log(2x)` for `x>=1` gives the logarithmic bound.

Everything is at the level of the canonical trace and the spectral theorem; no reduced quotient map is used.

## 5. Exact relation-agreement requirement

The theorem is deliberately stated in terms of free-word relations rather than a vague “same local ball.”

If the formal support of `A` lies in words of length at most `R`, then the support of `A^j` lies in words of length at most `jR`. The canonical trace of `A^j` is the matrix trace of the identity coefficient. Therefore agreement of trivial/nontrivial status for all reduced free words of length at most `nR` is enough to guarantee

`τ_k(A_i^j)=τ_k(A_∞^j)`

for every `j<=n`.

If one uses a marked-ball isomorphism convention instead, the conversion between ball radius and relation radius must be stated explicitly; I do not hide a factor of two inside the theorem.

## 6. Consequences for P01

P25-SMC gives a sharper dichotomy for any proposed **fixed finite** group-ring matrix.

Assume the limit operator has isolated zero with kernel trace `p`.

### 6.1 Fixed upward trace mismatch is impossible without any approximant gap hypothesis

If along a subsequence

`p_i >= p+Δ`

for a fixed `Δ>0`, then part (a), with any fixed `η<=δ_∞`, contradicts this once the local relation depth is large enough.

So local moment convergence plus a gapped limit already forces

`limsup p_i <=p`.

This is one-sided and does not require `0` to be isolated in `spec(A_i)`.

### 6.2 Fixed downward trace mismatch creates macroscopic near-zero spectrum

If

`p_i <= p-Δ`,

then at relation degree `n_i` at least `Δ/2` of the canonical spectral mass lies in

`(0, O(n_i^{-2}))`.

This is much stronger than “the smallest positive eigenvalue tends to zero.” A **positive trace fraction** of spectrum must collapse.

For a higher-Kazhdan/Laplacian route, this is the exact obstruction one should now test: any construction trying to change the limiting kernel trace while keeping a finite-support operator locally fixed must pay by producing a macroscopically visible near-zero band.

### 6.3 What this does not solve

The theorem does not construct the missing finite matrix.

It also does not obstruct a sequence with `p_i→p`. Allowed rational trace values can converge to a forbidden real number. In that regime the relevant quantity is the comparison between the trace defect

`Δ_i=|p-p_i|`

and the relation depth `n_i`; the theorem then forces low spectral mass below approximately

`M n_i^{-2} log^2(k/Δ_i)`.

If `Δ_i` is extremely small, that may be too weak to contradict any available spectral estimate.

Thus the next P01 attack should seek either:

1. a group-theoretic lower bound preventing so much spectral mass from entering an `n^{-2}` window, or
2. a concrete finite matrix for which the trace defect from approximating stages is quantitatively large enough relative to relation depth.

## 7. Controlled-K-theory interpretation

P25-SMC is a **trace/spectral-distribution theorem**, not yet an identification of K0 groups across different reduced algebras.

At finite tolerance, the same formal polynomial `q(A)` is a finite-propagation almost-projection in every quotient where the relevant gap estimate holds. But because nonamenable kernels prevent reduced quotient homomorphisms, there is still no canonical map

`K0(C_r^*(G_i)) -> K0(C_r^*(G))`.

The theorem therefore does not smuggle in functoriality. What it supplies is a common finite-word observable whose trace detects exactly where failed kernel-trace transport must go: into low positive spectrum.

This is close to the quantitative-K-theory philosophy of Oyono-Oyono–Yu, but I am not claiming a new categorical comparison theorem until the controlled morphisms and their exact functorial hypotheses are written down.

### Primary-source check

Elizabeth Gillaspy, *K-theory and homotopies of 2-cocycles on transformation groups*, J. Operator Theory 73 (2015), 465–490, DOI `10.7900/jot.2014feb14.2033`, proves that for a transformation group `G⋉X`, if `G` satisfies Baum–Connes with coefficients, then a homotopy of 2-cocycles induces an isomorphism between the K-groups of the endpoint **reduced twisted groupoid C*-algebras**.

The P17 hypotheses fit:

1. `Z^d` is countable discrete and amenable;
2. amenable groups satisfy Baum–Connes with coefficients;
3. `Ω` is compact metrizable, hence the transformation groupoid is second countable locally compact Hausdorff;
4. `σ_t` is a continuous cocycle homotopy;
5. the cocycle is independent of `x∈Ω`, which is a special case of the theorem, not an extra difficulty.

Therefore Gillaspy gives the reduced endpoint K-theory isomorphism. For amenable `Z^d`, full and reduced twisted crossed products agree.

**Audit verdict: PROVED / PASS.**

This audit also confirms a key distinction already made by P17: homotopy invariance of the abstract K-groups does **not** identify the canonical trace pairing. Magnetic gap labels can vary even though `K0,K1` are abstractly unchanged.

### Quantitative P17 corollary

If a finite-range positive magnetic Hamiltonian section `H_t` over the cocycle homotopy has a uniform isolated zero band

`spec(H_t)⊂{0}∪[δ,M]`

for all `t`, then the same Chebyshev polynomial gives uniformly

`||p_n(H_t)-P_t|| <= 2e^{-2n sqrt(δ/M)}`

with propagation at most `nR`.

Thus any verified continuous-field model for the finite-range sections immediately gives quantitative finite-propagation approximation of the spectral-projection section. This is useful for P17 ordered/trace questions, but continuity of the trace pairing must be proved in the continuous-field model; it is not a consequence of K-group invariance alone.

## 9. Counterexamples and failed routes

### 9.1 Norm convergence without a uniform gap does not transport kernel projections

Take

`A=diag(0,1)`

and

`A_ε=diag(ε,1)`.

Then `||A_ε-A||=ε→0`, but

`P_ker(A)=diag(1,0)`

whereas

`P_ker(A_ε)=0`.

So even norm convergence of operators does not preserve the kernel projection when the spectral gap at zero collapses. Any marked-group argument that has only local convergence and no quantitative spectral input is strictly weaker.

### 9.2 Strong/von-Neumann convergence is not stabilized K0 membership

The FFS infinite block projection can have a perfectly legitimate finite normal trace while every nonzero tail has norm one. Strong convergence therefore does not put the projection in `C_r^*(G)⊗K`.

### 9.3 Finite moment matching is not K-theory functoriality

P25-SMC uses equality of finitely many canonical moments. It yields trace inequalities and spectral-mass localization, but not a homomorphism between reduced C*-algebras or an identification of K0 classes.

### 9.4 Cocycle homotopy is not trace invariance

P17-M1 is correct for abstract K-groups. The toral Pfaffian formula already shows that the canonical trace image can vary with magnetic flux. A controlled spectral-projection theorem must therefore track both the class and the trace functional.
