### New theorem — `P25-UE-MESH-TRANSFER-05`

be marked quotients which agree on relations through radius \(L\), and assume both groups lie in one fixed Ozawa class \(\mathcal{UE}(k)\).  Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and put

\[

\[

\]

Assume that every diagnostic element

\[
v_r^*v_r-1,
\qquad
v_rv_r^*-1,
\qquad
v_{r+1}-v_r

has word-support radius at most \(S\) and evaluated support cardinality at most \(Q\) in either quotient.  Replacing \(k\) by a monotone majorant if necessary, set

\[
K=k\!\left(Q+\left\lfloor\frac2\varepsilon\right\rfloor\right).
\]

If

\[
\boxed{L\ge 2(K+1)S,}
\tag{P25-MESH-RADIUS}
\]

then `P01-P20A` applies simultaneously to every diagnostic element, with constants independent of the matrix size \(m\).

Suppose in \(H\)

\[
\max\{\|v_r^*v_r-1\|,\|v_rv_r^*-1\|\}\le \delta
\]

and

\[
\|v_{r+1}-v_r\|\le d_0
\]

for all \(r\).  Then in \(\Gamma\)

\[
\max\{\|v_r^*v_r-1\|,\|v_rv_r^*-1\|\}\le \alpha\delta,
\qquad
\|v_{r+1}-v_r\|\le \alpha d_0.

\[
U_r=v_r(v_r^*v_r)^{-1/2}

with the explicit correction bound

\[
\boxed{
\|U_r-v_r\|
\le
\theta(t)
:=
\sqrt{1+t}\left((1-t)^{-1/2}-1\right).
}
\tag{P25-POLAR-ERR}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|U_{r+1}-U_r\|
\le
\alpha d_0+2\theta(\alpha\delta).
}
\tag{P25-UNITARY-MESH}
\]

If

\[
\boxed{
\alpha d_0+2\theta(\alpha\delta)<2,
}
\tag{P25-K1-MARGIN}
\]

then every adjacent pair \(U_r,U_{r+1}\) is homotopic through unitaries, hence the complete stable \(K_1\) relation represented by the finite mesh transports from \(H\) to \(\Gamma\).  The same statement holds with \(H,\Gamma\) interchanged.

be self-adjoint formal elements whose diagnostics

and \(t=\alpha\delta<1/4\), then the spectral projections

\boxed{

\le

\tag{P25-PROJ-ERR}

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|P_{r+1}-P_r\|

\tag{P25-PROJ-MESH}

### Finite-propagation polar refinement

The polar correction itself need not have finite propagation.  For controlled calculations one can keep a polynomial approximation.  With \(t<1\), write

\[
(v^*v)^{-1/2}
=
\sum_{j\ge0}c_j(1-v^*v)^j,
\qquad
c_j=\binom{2j}{j}4^{-j}\le1.
\]

The truncation

\[
U^{(N)}
=
v\sum_{j=0}^N c_j(1-v^*v)^j
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|U-U^{(N)}\|
\le
\sqrt{1+t}\,\frac{t^{N+1}}{1-t}.
}
\tag{P25-POLAR-TRUNC}
\]

If \(v\) has propagation/support radius \(R\), then \(U^{(N)}\) has radius at most \((2N+1)R\).  Therefore tolerance \(\eta\) is reached with

\[
N+1
\ge
\frac{
\log\!\left(\sqrt{1+t}/((1-t)\eta)\right)
}{|\log t|},
\]

making the propagation blow-up logarithmic in \(1/\eta\) at fixed defect.  This is compatible with the earlier P25 finite-propagation inverse/Neumann technology.

### New counterexample — `P25-MOVING-K1-CE-05`

The complexity restriction is genuinely necessary even for exact unitary relations.  Let

\[
G_L=\langle a,b\mid a^L=1\rangle\cong C_L*\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow F_2
\]

in the marked-group limit and use the formal unitary

\[
u_L=a^L.
\]

In \(C_r^*(G_L)\), \(u_L=1\), so it is trivially null-homotopic.  In \(C_r^*(F_2)\), Pimsner–Voiculescu's computation

\[
K_1(C_r^*(F_2))\cong\mathbb Z^2
\]

identifies the canonical free-generator unitaries with the standard generators; therefore

\[
[u_L]=L[\lambda(a)]\ne0.
\]

The diagnostic endpoint difference \(u_L-1\) has support radius \(L\), exactly the relation scale.  Hence a reverse-K-theory theorem with no strict complexity-vs-relation-depth inequality is false even for exact unitaries and a one-step null-homotopy.

This is the K-theoretic strengthening of the previous norm counterexample `P25-MOVING-SUPPORT-CE-04`.

## 2.1 Reduced-norm comparison of the diagnostics

`P01-P20A` applies to any matrix-valued formal element whose evaluated support has cardinality bounded by \(Q\), whose support radius is bounded by \(S\), and for which

\[
L\ge 2\big(k(Q+\lfloor2/\varepsilon\rfloor)+1\big)S.
\]

The estimate is completely bounded, so its constant does not depend on \(m\).  Applying it separately to

\[
v_r^*v_r-1,
\quad
v_rv_r^*-1,
\quad
v_{r+1}-v_r
\]

gives the claimed defect and mesh-distance bounds in the other quotient.

For a crude complexity bound, if every \(v_r\) has radius at most \(R\) and group support cardinality at most \(q\), then one may take

\[
S=2R,
\qquad
Q\le q^2+2q+1.
\]

This deliberately overcounts collisions and is therefore safe.  Matrix size still does not enter.

## 2.2 Polar correction

Assume

\[
\|v^*v-1\|\le t<1,
\qquad
\|vv^*-1\|\le t<1.
\]

Then both \(v^*v\) and \(vv^*\) are invertible, so

\[
U=v(v^*v)^{-1/2}
\]

is unitary.  Since

\[
\operatorname{spec}(v^*v)\subset[1-t,1+t],
\]

\[
\|v\|\le\sqrt{1+t}
\]

and

\[
\|(v^*v)^{-1/2}-1\|
\le
(1-t)^{-1/2}-1.
\]

Multiplying gives `P25-POLAR-ERR`.

For adjacent mesh points,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U_{r+1}-U_r\|
&\le
\|U_{r+1}-v_{r+1}\|
+
\|v_{r+1}-v_r\|
+
\|v_r-U_r\|\\
&\le
\alpha d_0+2\theta(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

If the last quantity is strictly below two, then

\[
\|1-U_r^*U_{r+1}\|<2.
\]

For a unitary, strict distance below two from the identity excludes \(-1\) from the spectrum.  The continuous principal logarithm of \(U_r^*U_{r+1}\) is therefore defined, and

\[
U_r\exp\big(s\log(U_r^*U_{r+1})\big),
\qquad 0\le s\le1,
\]

is a unitary path from \(U_r\) to \(U_{r+1}\).  Concatenation transports the finite stable \(K_1\) relation.

## 2.3 Projection correction

Let \(p=p^*\) and

\[
\|p^2-p\|\le t<1/4.
\]

For every spectral value \(\lambda\),

\[
|\lambda(\lambda-1)|\le t.
\]

The interval around \(1/2\) is therefore excluded.  The nearest-point error to \(\{0,1\}\) is bounded by

\[
\kappa(t)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t}}2,
\]

which also dominates the smaller outer-root error

\[
(\sqrt{1+4t}-1)/2.
\]

Hence the spectral cutoff

\[
P=1_{[1/2,\infty)}(p)
\]

is defined and

\[
\|P-p\|\le\kappa(t).
\]

The adjacent projection estimate follows from the triangle inequality.  If it is strictly below one, the standard close-projections theorem gives unitary equivalence and a projection homotopy, so the \(K_0\) relation transports.

## 2.4 Finite propagation of the polar approximation

With

\[
x=1-v^*v,
\qquad
\|x\|\le t<1,
\]

the binomial series

\[
(1-x)^{-1/2}
=
\sum_{j\ge0}c_jx^j,
\qquad
0<c_j\le1
\]

converges absolutely.  Therefore

\[
\left\|
(1-x)^{-1/2}
-
\sum_{j=0}^Nc_jx^j
\right\|
\le
\sum_{j>N}t^j
=
\frac{t^{N+1}}{1-t}.
\]

Multiplication by \(v\) gives `P25-POLAR-TRUNC`.  If \(v\) has radius \(R\), then \(x\) has radius at most \(2R\), so the truncated polar word has radius at most \((2N+1)R\).

This controlled refinement is useful when one needs another round of marked-group comparison before passing to the exact ordinary \(K_1\) class.

# 5. Exact categorical distinctions

## Full versus reduced

`P25-UE-MESH-TRANSFER-05` is a theorem about **reduced** group \(C^*\)-norms.  There is no quotient-induced reduced \(*\)-homomorphism between the two marked groups.  The transport occurs only because finite compressions are locally identical and Ozawa controls the reduced norm on each side separately.

The magnetic P17/P18 setting is categorically different: the acting group is \(\mathbb Z^d\), hence amenable, so full and reduced twisted crossed products agree and Gillaspy supplies actual evaluation maps from one homotopy algebra.

## Norm versus strong convergence

The mesh theorem uses operator-norm inequalities with explicit strict margins.  Strong-operator or von Neumann convergence would not suffice to preserve a unitary distance \(<2\), a projection distance \(<1\), or a spectral gap around \(1/2\).

## Finite matrices versus von Neumann projections

All mesh elements live in finite matrix amplifications of reduced group \(C^*\)-algebras.  A von Neumann kernel projection is not substituted for an ordinary \(C^*\)-projection.  P01's trace obstruction still needs an actual reduced spectral gap before a kernel projection belongs to ordinary reduced \(K_0\).

## Scalar versus coefficient Baum–Connes

Nothing here proves Baum–Connes with coefficients for a P01 hard group.  P01's target remains coefficient-free/scalar assembly.  Coefficient Baum–Connes is used in the magnetic homotopy only for amenable \(\mathbb Z^d\), where it is known.

## Stable versus unstable K-theory

The conclusions are stable \(K_0/K_1\) statements.  Matrix size may be enlarged before applying the theorem.  Close corrected projections/unitaries represent the same stable class; no equality or conjugacy in one prescribed unstabilized matrix size is asserted.
