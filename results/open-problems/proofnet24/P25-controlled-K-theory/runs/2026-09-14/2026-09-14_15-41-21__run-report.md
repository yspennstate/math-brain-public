# 3. New theorem P25-LGB — local Green-defect conservation

Let \(S\) be a fixed finite marking set and \(F_S\) the free group.

\[

\[
w=e\text{ in }G_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
w=e\text{ in }G_\infty

for every free word \(w\) with
\[
|w|\le L_i.
\]

Let
\[
A=A^*\in M_k(\mathbb C[F_S])
\]
have
\[
\operatorname{rad}(A)\le R,
\]
and assume its evaluations
\[
A_i,\ A_\infty
\]
are positive.

Let
\[
P_i=1_{\{0\}}(A_i),
\qquad
P_\infty=1_{\{0\}}(A_\infty)
\]
inside the matrix group von Neumann algebras, with unnormalized canonical traces
\[
p_i=\tau_i(P_i),
\qquad
p=\tau_\infty(P_\infty).

For each \(i\), choose an arbitrary formal matrix
\[
B^{(i)}\in M_k(\mathbb C[F_S]),
\qquad
\operatorname{rad}(B^{(i)})\le r_i.
\]
Let
\[
B_i
\]
and
\[
B_{\infty}^{(i)}
\]
be the evaluations of this **same formal matrix** in \(G_i\) and \(G_\infty\).

Assume
\[
\boxed{
2(R+r_i)\le L_i.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define the right Green defects
\[
e_i(B^{(i)})

\|(1-P_i)-B_iA_i\|_{2,\tau_i},

\|(1-P_\infty)-B_\infty^{(i)}A_\infty\|_{2,\tau_\infty}.

Then:

\[
\boxed{
e_i(B^{(i)})^2
=
e_\infty(B^{(i)})^2

\tag{P25-LGB}

The same identity holds for the left defects
\[
\|(1-P_i)-A_iB_i\|_2.

## 4. Proof of P25-LGB

The proof has two exact pieces.

### 4.1 Remove the nonlocal kernel projection from the defect trace

Work first in one finite tracial von Neumann algebra and suppress the group index.

Let
\[
A=A^*\ge0,
\qquad
P=1_{\{0\}}(A).
\]
Then
\[
AP=PA=0.
\]

For arbitrary \(B\), put
\[
D=1-BA.
\]
The Green defect is
\[
(1-P)-BA=D-P.
\]

Because the trace is tracial,
\[
\tau(PBA)
=
\tau(APB)
=
0.
\]
Hence
\[
\tau(PD)=\tau(P)=\tau(D^*P).
\]

Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(1-P)-BA\|_2^2
&=
\|D-P\|_2^2\\
&=
\tau(D^*D)+\tau(P)-2\Re\tau(PD)\\
&=
\tau(D^*D)-\tau(P).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\|(1-P)-BA\|_2^2
=
\tau\!\left[
(1-BA)^*(1-BA)
\right]
-
\tau(P).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This identity is exact even though \(P\) itself is nonlocal.

Likewise, with
\[
D_L=1-AB,
\]
traciality and \(AP=PA=0\) give
\[
\boxed{
\|(1-P)-AB\|_2^2
=
\tau\!\left[
(1-AB)^*(1-AB)
\right]
-
\tau(P).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### 4.2 The remaining trace is local

For the right defect,
\[
D_i
=
1-B_iA_i
\]
is the evaluation of the formal matrix
\[
D^{(i)}=1-B^{(i)}A.
\]

Its support radius is at most
\[
R+r_i.
\]
Therefore
\[
(D^{(i)})^*D^{(i)}
\]
has support radius at most
\[
2(R+r_i).
\]

By (3.1), \(G_i\) and \(G_\infty\) have exactly the same trivial words on the entire support of this formal matrix. Consequently their canonical matrix traces agree:

\[
\boxed{
\tau_i(D_i^*D_i)
=
\tau_\infty((D_\infty^{(i)})^*D_\infty^{(i)}).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Applying (4.1) at the stage and at the limit gives
\[
e_i^2+p_i
=
e_\infty^2+p,
\]
hence
\[
e_i^2=e_\infty^2+p-p_i.
\]

This proves P25-LGB.

# 9. New theorem P25-OPTGREEN — exact optimal bounded-norm Green defect

P25-AGT gave a one-cutoff inequality. The next theorem identifies the exact optimum over *all* Green operators with a given norm bound.

Let
\[
(\mathcal M,\tau)
\]
be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, with \(\tau(1)<\infty\).

Let
\[
A=A^*\ge0\in\mathcal M,
\qquad
P=1_{\{0\}}(A),
\]
and let \(\mu_A\) be its canonical spectral measure:
\[
\tau(f(A))
=
\int f(x)\,d\mu_A(x).
\]

For \(L\ge0\), define
\[
\mathfrak e_A(L)
=
\inf_{\|B\|\le L}
\|(1-P)-BA\|_2.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak e_A(L)^2
=
\int_{(0,\infty)}
(1-Lx)_+^2
\,d\mu_A(x).
}
\tag{P25-OPTGREEN}
\]

The identical formula holds for the left defect
\[
\inf_{\|B\|\le L}
\|(1-P)-AB\|_2.
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
\mathcal N=W^*(A)\subset\mathcal M.
\]

Because \(\mathcal M\) is finite and tracial, there is a unique trace-preserving normal conditional expectation
\[
E_{\mathcal N}:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N.
\]
It is operator-norm contractive and is the orthogonal projection in \(L^2(\mathcal M,\tau)\).

For any \(B\) with
\[
\|B\|\le L,
\]
put
\[
C=E_{\mathcal N}(B).
\]
Then
\[
\|C\|\le L.
\]
Since \(A,P\in\mathcal N\) and the expectation is \(\mathcal N\)-bimodular,
\[
E_{\mathcal N}\big((1-P)-BA\big)
=
(1-P)-CA.
\]

\(L^2\)-contractivity yields
\[
\|(1-P)-BA\|_2
\ge
\|(1-P)-CA\|_2.
\]

Thus noncommuting \(B\)'s cannot beat the abelian algebra generated by \(A\).

Write
\[
C=f(A),
\qquad
|f(x)|\le L.
\]
Then
\[
\|(1-P)-CA\|_2^2
=
\int_{(0,\infty)}
|1-xf(x)|^2\,d\mu_A(x).
\]

Pointwise,
\[
\inf_{|z|\le L}|1-xz|
=
(1-Lx)_+.
\]

The minimum is attained by the bounded Borel function
\[
f_L(x)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&x=0,\\
L,&0<x<1/L,\\
1/x,&x\ge1/L,
\end{cases}
\]
with the evident interpretation at \(L=0\).

Therefore
\[
B_L=f_L(A)
\]
has
\[
\|B_L\|\le L
\]
and attains the displayed integral exactly.

This proves P25-OPTGREEN.

### Primary-source check for the conditional expectation

The only external operator-algebra theorem used here is the trace-preserving conditional expectation onto a von Neumann subalgebra of a finite tracial von Neumann algebra. This is the tracial case of Takesaki's conditional-expectation theorem; the primary source is:

Masamichi Takesaki, **“Conditional expectations in von Neumann algebras,”**
*Journal of Functional Analysis* 9 (1972), 306–321,
DOI `10.1016/0022-1236(72)90004-3`.

The missing ingredient is not “one better Chebyshev polynomial.”
