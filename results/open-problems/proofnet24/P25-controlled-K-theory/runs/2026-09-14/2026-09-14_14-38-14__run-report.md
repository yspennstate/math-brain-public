## 3. New theorem P25-AGT — approximate Green tradeoff

Let \((\mathcal M,\tau)\) be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra; \(\tau(1)\) may be any finite number. Let
\[
A=A^*\ge0\in\mathcal M,
\qquad
P=1_{\{0\}}(A),
\]
and for \(\eta>0\) let
\[
Q_\eta=1_{(0,\eta)}(A).
\]

Let \(B\in\mathcal M\) be arbitrary. Define the **right approximate-Green defect**
\[
E_R=(1-P)-BA,
\]
and write
\[
e_R=\|E_R\|_{2,\tau},
\qquad
L=\|B\|.
\]

### P25-AGT(a)

If
\[
\eta L<1,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\tau(Q_\eta)
\le
\frac{e_R^2}{(1-\eta L)^2}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

There is an identical left-sided form: if
\[
E_L=(1-P)-AB,
\qquad
e_L=\|E_L\|_{2,\tau},
\]
and \(\eta L<1\), then
\[
\boxed{
\tau(Q_\eta)
\le
\frac{e_L^2}{(1-\eta L)^2}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

No commutation between \(A\) and \(B\) is required.

### Proof

Because \(Q_\eta\le1-P\),
\[
E_RQ_\eta
=
Q_\eta-BAQ_\eta.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|E_RQ_\eta\|_2
&\ge
\|Q_\eta\|_2-\|BAQ_\eta\|_2\\
&\ge
(1-\eta\|B\|)\|Q_\eta\|_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second inequality uses
\[
\|BAQ_\eta\|_2
\le
\|B\|\,\|AQ_\eta\|_2
\le
L\eta\|Q_\eta\|_2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\|E_RQ_\eta\|_2\le\|E_R\|_2=e_R.
\]
Since
\[
\|Q_\eta\|_2^2=\tau(Q_\eta),
\]
(3.1) follows.

For the left form,
\[
Q_\eta E_L=Q_\eta-Q_\eta AB
\]
and
\[
\|Q_\eta AB\|_2
\le
\|Q_\eta A\|_2\|B\|
\le
\eta L\|Q_\eta\|_2.
\]
This proves (3.2).

## 4. P25-AGT + P25-SMC: explicit norm-control obstruction for P01

Apply P25-AGT to the stage operator \(A_i\) inside
\[
M_k(\mathcal N G_i)
\]
with its canonical finite trace. Let
\[
P_i=1_{\{0\}}(A_i).
\]
Suppose \(B_i\) is a stagewise approximate Green operator and define
\[
e_i
=
\|(1-P_i)-B_iA_i\|_2,
\qquad
L_i=\|B_i\|.
\]

If \(0<\eta\le\delta_\infty\) and \(\eta L_i<1\), then P25-SMC(b) and P25-AGT give
\[
\boxed{
p-p_i
\le
2k\varepsilon_{n,\eta}
+
\frac{e_i^2}{(1-\eta L_i)^2}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the exact quantitative bridge that was missing in the previous run: a kernel-trace defect is bounded by a finite-moment Chebyshev term plus an approximate-resolvent defect.

### Optimized dichotomy

Assume
\[
p-p_i\ge\Delta>0
\]
and use the optimized cutoff \(\eta_{n,\Delta}\) from (1.3), so that
\[
2k\varepsilon_{n,\eta_{n,\Delta}}=\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]
Then P25-SMC gives
\[
\tau_k(Q_{\eta_{n,\Delta}})\ge\frac{\Delta}{2}.
\]

If
\[
L_i\eta_{n,\Delta}\le\frac12,
\]
P25-AGT gives
\[
\frac{\Delta}{2}
\le
4e_i^2,
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
e_i\ge\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{8}}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Otherwise,
\[
L_i>\frac1{2\eta_{n,\Delta}}.
\]
Using (1.5),
\[
\boxed{
L_i
>
\frac{2n^2}{
M\operatorname{arcosh}(4k/\Delta)^2
}
\ge
\frac{2n^2}{
M\log^2(8k/\Delta)
}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore:

### P25-AGT(b) — quadratic norm blow-up dichotomy

Provided \(\eta_{n,\Delta}\le\delta_\infty\), every stagewise approximate Green operator satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\text{either}\quad
\|(1-P_i)-B_iA_i\|_2
\ge
\sqrt{\Delta/8},
}
\]
or
\[
\boxed{
\|B_i\|
>
\frac{2n^2}{
M\log^2(8k/\Delta)
}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a direct theorem-sized refinement of P01's “missing norm control” diagnosis. A fixed trace mismatch cannot coexist with both:

1. small \(L^2\) inverse defect; and
2. Green-operator norm \(o(n^2/\log^2(1/\Delta))\).

No reduced quotient map is used.

### Sharpness mechanism

The norm term cannot be omitted. In a finite-dimensional algebra take
\[
A_\lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,1),
\qquad
P=0,
\qquad
B=A_\lambda^{-1}.
\]
Then the inverse defect is exactly zero,
\[
1-BA_\lambda=0,
\]
but \(\|B\|=1/\lambda\). For any cutoff \(\eta>\lambda\), the low spectral projection is nonzero and
\[
\eta\|B\|>1.
\]
Thus a perfect inverse can coexist with low positive spectrum only by paying the reciprocal spectral scale in operator norm. P25-AGT captures exactly this tradeoff.

## 5. Consequence for the P01 proof route

P25-AGT turns P01's proof-side problem into a concrete quantitative target.

Suppose an FFS-like marked approximation supplies a **fixed finite** positive group-ring matrix \(A\), relation depth \(n_i\), and stage trace defects
\[
\Delta_i=p-p_i>0.
\]
Then any construction of approximate Green operators \(B_i\) with
\[
\|(1-P_i)-B_iA_i\|_2=o(\sqrt{\Delta_i})
\tag{5.1}
\]
and
\[
\|B_i\|
=
o\!\left(
\frac{n_i^2}{\log^2(1/\Delta_i)}
\right)
\tag{5.2}
\]
would contradict P25-SMC once \(\eta_{n_i,\Delta_i}\le\delta_\infty\).

Conversely, any genuine trace-changing approximation must exhibit one of two visible failures:

- a nonvanishing \(L^2\) Green defect; or
- at least quadratic blow-up of the inverse norm at the marked relation scale.

This is stronger and more operational than asking abstractly for “resolvent convergence.”

It also clarifies what a higher-Kazhdan/expansion input would have to prove: not full reduced \(C^*\)-functoriality, but an approximate inverse with a quantitative norm bound below the \(n^2\) barrier and a small trace-\(L^2\) defect.

## 6. New theorem P25-QK0 — Chebyshev representatives as quantitative K0 classes

The previous P25-CSP supplied a finite-propagation almost-projection but deliberately stopped short of placing it in a formal quantitative-K framework.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be a unital filtered \(C^*\)-algebra with filtration
\[
(\mathcal A_r)_{r>0}.
\]
Let
\[
a=a^*\ge0\in\mathcal A_R
\]
and suppose
\[
\operatorname{spec}(a)
\subset
\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad
0<\delta<M.
\]
Let
\[
P=1_{\{0\}}(a).
\]

Define the same Chebyshev polynomial
\[
p_n(x)
=
\frac{
T_n\!\left(\frac{2x-(M+\delta)}{M-\delta}\right)
}{
T_n\!\left(-\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}\right)
}.
\]
Then
\[
p_n(a)\in\mathcal A_{nR}
\]
and
\[
d_n:=\|p_n(a)-P\|
\le
2e^{-2n\sqrt{\delta/M}}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Fix
\[
0<\varepsilon<\frac14.
\]
If
\[
d_n<\frac{\varepsilon}{4},
\tag{6.2}
\]
then the standard perturbation lemma in Oyono-Oyono–Yu quantitative operator K-theory implies that \(p_n(a)\) is an
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon\text{-}nR\text{ projection}.}
\]
Its quantitative \(K_0\) class
\[
[p_n(a)]_{\varepsilon,nR}
\in
K_0^{\varepsilon,nR}(\mathcal A)
\]
maps under the forget-control/functional-calculus map to
\[
\boxed{[P]\in K_0(\mathcal A).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

In the present special construction one can see the rounded projection explicitly. On
\(\ker a\), \(p_n(a)=1\). On the positive spectral band,
\[
|p_n(a)|\le d_n<1/2.
\]
Therefore the \(1/2\)-spectral cutoff of \(p_n(a)\) is exactly \(P\).

A sufficient explicit degree is
\[
\boxed{
n>
\frac12\sqrt{\frac{M}{\delta}}
\log\frac{8}{\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus P25-CSP is now a genuine controlled \(K_0\) representative **inside each filtered algebra**.

### What P25-QK0 does not do

It does not create a map
\[
K_0^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*(G_i))
\longrightarrow
K_0^{\varepsilon',r'}(C_r^*(G))
\]
between the P01 stages. The nonamenable quotient kernels still prevent the ordinary reduced
\(*\)-homomorphisms needed to induce such maps.

The theorem formalizes the class at finite propagation; it does not manufacture cross-algebra functoriality.

## 7. P17 magnetic consequence: controlled factor-map transport

P17-M4 is in a fundamentally better categorical situation than P01. For a surjective equivariant factor map
\[
p:\Omega\to\Omega'
\]
at the **same** magnetic matrix \(B\), there is an actual injective homomorphism
\[
\Phi_{p,B}:A_B(\Omega')\to A_B(\Omega).
\]

Filter the twisted crossed products by \(\mathbb Z^d\)-word/Fourier propagation. The map \(\Phi_{p,B}\) preserves Fourier group elements and is therefore filtration-preserving.

Let
\[
h'=h'^*\ge0
\]
be a finite-range Hamiltonian in the downstairs algebra, with propagation \(R\), and suppose
\[
\operatorname{spec}(h')
\subset
\{0\}\cup[\delta,M].
\]
Put
\[
h=\Phi_{p,B}(h').
\]
Then spectral permanence gives the same gap upstairs, and polynomial naturality gives
\[
p_n(h)=\Phi_{p,B}(p_n(h')).
\]

Consequently, whenever (6.2) holds, the filtered homomorphism carries the quantitative class
\[
[p_n(h')]_{\varepsilon,nR}
\]
to
\[
[p_n(h)]_{\varepsilon,nR}.
\]
Forgetting control recovers exactly the kernel/Fermi-band \(K_0\) transport already proved in P17-M4(d).

This is a genuine controlled-morphism statement because an actual filtered \(*\)-homomorphism exists. It must **not** be transplanted to P01's nonamenable quotient fillings, nor to a change of magnetic cocycle \(B\to B'\) without an explicit gauge/cohomology identification.

**Verdict:** **PROVED / PASS.**

Let
\[
H=\mathbb Z^{d-1}\subset\mathbb Z^d
\]
be the tangential subgroup and \(e_d\) the normal generator. At fixed \(B\), the tangential algebra is
\[
A_B^H(\Omega)
=
C(\Omega)\rtimes_{\tau|_H,\sigma_B|_H,r}H.
\]

Conjugation by the final magnetic translation defines an automorphism \(\alpha_\Omega\). On
\(f\in C(\Omega)\),
\[
\alpha_\Omega(f)=f\circ\tau_{e_d}.
\]
For the tangential twisted unitary \(U_h\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{e_d}U_hU_{-e_d}
&=
\sigma_B(e_d,h)\,
\sigma_B(e_d+h,-e_d)\,U_h\\
&=
e^{2\pi i e_d^TBh}U_h,
\end{aligned}
\]
where skew-symmetry of \(B\) gives the displayed phase.

For a surjective equivariant factor map
\[
p:\Omega\to\Omega',
\]
the tangential pullback map
\[
\Phi_{p,B}^H:A_B^H(\Omega')\to A_B^H(\Omega)
\]
sends coefficients by \(f\mapsto f\circ p\) and \(U_h'\mapsto U_h\). Equivariance of \(p\) and the fact that the **same** cocycle is used upstairs and downstairs give
\[
\Phi_{p,B}^H\circ\alpha_{\Omega'}
=
\alpha_\Omega\circ\Phi_{p,B}^H.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The full magnetic algebra is the crossed product of the tangential algebra by this final
\(\mathbb Z\)-automorphism; the \(\mathbb Z\)-direction itself has trivial self-cocycle because \(B\) is skew, while the cross phases are exactly encoded in \(\alpha\).

Hence the Pimsner–Voiculescu Toeplitz extensions form a morphism of short exact sequences. Naturality of the six-term exact sequence gives
\[
\boxed{
\partial_\Omega\circ\Phi_{p,B,*}
=
\Phi_{p,B,*}^H\circ\partial_{\Omega'}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The tangential canonical traces obey the same factor-map identity as in the bulk, so the associated boundary trace/index pairings agree.

The caveats in P17 are necessary and correct:

- the same \(B\) is essential;
- no injectivity of the induced K-theory map follows merely from algebraic injectivity;
- the general boundary map need not admit the toral exterior-contraction formula;
- full=reduced here is justified by amenability of \(\mathbb Z^d\), unlike P01.

The original Pimsner–Voiculescu 1980 paper is the primary source for the Toeplitz extension and six-term exact sequence used here.

## 9. Counterexamples / failed-route graveyard

### 9.1 Trace equality does not identify K0

Even within one algebra, trace is not generally a complete K0 invariant. In
\[
\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C
\]
with trace
\[
\tau(a,b)=\frac{a+b}{2},
\]
the projections
\[
(1,0),\qquad(0,1)
\]
have the same trace \(1/2\), but define different classes in
\[
K_0(\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C)\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Therefore P25-SMC's trace/moment information cannot by itself prove cross-stage K0 equality.

### 9.2 Low spectrum is compatible with a perfect inverse only through norm blow-up

The diagonal example in §4 shows that a defect-free Green operator has norm exactly at the reciprocal low-spectral scale. This rules out any attempt to prove P25-AGT with no \(\|B\|\) term.

### 9.3 Norm convergence without a uniform zero gap does not transport kernels

The prior counterexample remains:
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),
\qquad
A_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon,1).
\]
Then \(A_\varepsilon\to A\) in norm but
\[
P_{\ker A}=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),
\qquad
P_{\ker A_\varepsilon}=0.
\]

### 9.4 Strong convergence does not create a stabilized K0 class

The FFS infinite block projection has a legitimate finite von-Neumann trace but every nonzero tail has norm one. Strong convergence is therefore irrelevant to membership in \(C_r^*(G)\otimes\mathcal K\).

### 9.5 Finite propagation does not manufacture reduced functoriality

The same polynomial word can be evaluated in different marked groups, but this gives neither a reduced \(C^*\)-homomorphism nor a controlled K-theory map when the quotient kernel is nonamenable.
