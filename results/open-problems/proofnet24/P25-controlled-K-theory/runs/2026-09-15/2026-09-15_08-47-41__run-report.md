# Categorical distinctions

**Full vs reduced.** The cyclic calibration is amenable, so full and reduced group C*-algebras coincide there only. Nothing is exported to nonamenable fillings. The coefficient lift is not a global C*-homomorphism.

**Norm vs strong.** Controlled K and the nullhomotopy barriers use operator-norm defects. Strong/von Neumann convergence is not substituted.

**Finite matrices vs von Neumann projections.** The controlled assembly representatives are finite matrices in finite propagation pieces. No von Neumann projection is promoted to an ordinary reduced K-class.

**Scalar vs coefficient Baum–Connes.** The classes are coefficient-free. Controlled assembly exists without assuming Baum–Connes surjectivity. No difficult coefficient theorem is imported.

**Stable vs unstable.** All nullhomotopies are stable; matrix size may grow under stabilization. The propagation lower bounds are not artifacts of fixing an unstable matrix dimension.

**Quantitative vs ordinary.** This is the core distinction. The finite stage has ordinary \(K_1=0\), while bounded-control quantitative K contains an infinite cyclic controlled assembly class. Ordinary K forgets the control scale at which the killing relation appears.
