# Main theorem — exact secondary trace index

## `P25-SECONDARY-TRACE-SMITH-10` — PROVED

Fix an even \(n\ge2\). Put

\[
A_{n-1}=K_0^G(X^{(n-1)}),\qquad
A_n=K_0^G(X^{(n)}),
\]

and

\[
\lambda_j=\tau_*\mu_{X^{(j)}}:A_j\to\mathbb Q.
\]

Assume, as in P01-P27,

\[
T_{n-1}=\lambda_{n-1}(A_{n-1})=\frac1D\mathbb Z.
\]

The equivariant cellular exact sequence gives

\[
0\to A_{n-1}\to A_n\to Z_n\to0,
\]

where

\[
Z_n=
\ker\!\left[
C_n\to K_1^G(X^{(n-1)})
\right],
\qquad
C_n=\bigoplus_e R_{\mathbb C}(H_e).
\]

Since \(C_n\) is free abelian, \(Z_n\) is free. Choose an integral basis \(z_1,\ldots,z_k\) and arbitrary integral lifts \(\widetilde z_j\in A_n\). Define

\[
\beta_j=D\,\lambda_n(\widetilde z_j)\in\mathbb Q.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{
s_n=[T_n:T_{n-1}]
=
\operatorname{lcm}_{j}
\operatorname{den}(\beta_j).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{den}(0)=1\).

Equivalently, for every prime \(p\),

\[
\boxed{
v_p(s_n)
=
\max_j[-v_p(\beta_j)]_+.
}
\tag{2}
\]

If a common denominator \(B\) is chosen so that

\[
\beta_j=\frac{v_j}{B},
\qquad v_j\in\mathbb Z,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
s_n=
\frac{B}{\gcd(B,v_1,\ldots,v_k)}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

These formulas are independent of the basis of \(Z_n\), the chosen lifts, and the chosen common denominator.

### Proof

If \(\widetilde z_j'\) is another lift, then

\[
\widetilde z_j'-\widetilde z_j\in A_{n-1}.
\]

Naturality of assembly implies

\[
\lambda_n(\widetilde z_j'-\widetilde z_j)
\in D^{-1}\mathbb Z.
\]

Hence \(\beta_j+\mathbb Z\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) is lift-independent.

Under multiplication by \(D\),

\[
\mathbb Q/(D^{-1}\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,
\]

and P01's secondary trace map sends \(z_j\) to \(\beta_j+\mathbb Z\). The order of \(\beta+\mathbb Z\) is the reduced denominator of \(\beta\). A finite subgroup of \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) is cyclic, and the subgroup generated by elements of orders \(d_j\) has order \(\operatorname{lcm}_j d_j\). This proves (1). Formula (2) is its \(p\)-primary form. Formula (3) follows because the subgroup generated by \(v_j/B+\mathbb Z\) is generated by \(g/B+\mathbb Z\), where \(g=\gcd(B,v_1,\ldots,v_k)\).

QED.
