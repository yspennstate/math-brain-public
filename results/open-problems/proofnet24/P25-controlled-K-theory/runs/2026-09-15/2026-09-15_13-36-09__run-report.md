# 2. `P25-BERNSTEIN-SZEGO-POINTWISE-23` — PROVED

Let

\[
f(\theta)=\sum_{k=-m}^{m}a_ke^{ik\theta}
\]

be a **real-valued** trigonometric polynomial of degree at most `m>=1`, and put

\[
M=\|f\|_\infty.
\]

Then for every real `theta`,

\[
\boxed{
|f'(\theta)|
\le
m\sqrt{M^2-f(\theta)^2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
(f'(\theta))^2+m^2f(\theta)^2
\le
m^2M^2.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is the exact pointwise inequality P18 left conditional.

## Proof

If `M=0`, the result is trivial. Normalize `M=1` and translate the point under consideration to `theta=0`.

Write

\[
a=f(0).
\]

If `|a|=1`, then `0` is a local extremum of the bounded real function `f`, so `f'(0)=0` and `(2.1)` holds.

Assume

\[
|a|<1,
\qquad
\phi=\arccos a\in(0,\pi).
\]

Suppose first, for contradiction, that

\[
f'(0)>m\sin\phi.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define

\[
g(\theta)=\cos(m\theta-\phi),
\qquad
h=f-g.
\]

Then

\[
h(0)=0,
\qquad
h'(0)>0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The extrema of `g` occur at

\[
t_k=\frac{\phi+k\pi}{m}.
\]

Modulo `2pi` there are exactly `2m` such points, and

\[
g(t_k)=(-1)^k.
\]

Because `|f|<=1`,

\[
g(t_k)=1\Longrightarrow h(t_k)\le0,
\]

\[
g(t_k)=-1\Longrightarrow h(t_k)\ge0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus on every cyclic interval between consecutive extrema of `g`, the endpoint values of `h` have opposite weak signs. Such an interval contributes at least one zero of `h`, counted with multiplicity. If an endpoint itself is a zero, then

\[
f(t_k)=g(t_k)=\pm1,
\]

so both `f` and `g` have zero derivative there; hence

\[
h'(t_k)=0,
\]

and that endpoint zero has multiplicity at least two. It can therefore legitimately supply the zero count for both adjacent intervals.

The particular interval

\[
\left[\frac{\phi-\pi}{m},\frac{\phi}{m}\right]
\]

contains `0`. By `(2.4)`, `h` is negative immediately to the left of zero and positive immediately to the right. By `(2.5)`, the left endpoint has `h>=0` and the right endpoint has `h<=0`. Therefore this one interval contains at least three zero units counted with multiplicity: one on the left side, the zero at `0`, and one on the right side.

The other `2m-1` cyclic intervals contribute at least one zero unit each. Thus `h` has more than `2m` zeros on one period, counted with multiplicity.

But a nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degree at most `m` has at most `2m` zeros counted with multiplicity: multiplying by `e^{im\theta}` turns it into an algebraic polynomial in `z=e^{i\theta}` of degree at most `2m`. Since `(2.4)` makes `h` nonzero, this is a contradiction.

Hence

\[
f'(0)\le m\sin\phi
=m\sqrt{1-a^2}.
\]

If instead

\[
f'(0)<-m\sqrt{1-a^2},
\]

compare with

\[
g(\theta)=\cos(m\theta+\phi),
\]

whose derivative at zero is `-m sin(phi)`. The same zero-count argument gives a contradiction.

Therefore

\[
|f'(0)|\le m\sqrt{1-a^2}.
\]

Undo normalization and translation to obtain `(2.1)`.

QED.

# 3. Extremal propagation from a norm-attaining point

The pointwise inequality yields the exact cosine propagation estimate needed for root sampling.

## `P25-COSINE-PROPAGATION-23` — PROVED

Let `f` be real trigonometric of degree at most `m`, with

\[
\|f\|_\infty\le1,
\qquad
f(0)=1.
\]

Then for

\[
|\theta|\le\frac{\pi}{m},
\]

\[
\boxed{
f(\theta)\ge\cos(m|\theta|).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

## Proof

For `0<delta<1`, define

\[
u_\delta(\theta)
=\arccos((1-\delta)f(\theta)).
\]

The argument stays strictly inside `(-1,1)`, so `u_delta` is continuously differentiable. By `(2.1)`,

\[
|u_\delta'|
=
\frac{(1-\delta)|f'|}
{\sqrt{1-(1-\delta)^2f^2}}
\le
m
\frac{(1-\delta)\sqrt{1-f^2}}
{\sqrt{1-(1-\delta)^2f^2}}
\le m.
\]

Hence

\[
u_\delta(\theta)
\le
\arccos(1-\delta)+m|\theta|.
\]

Let `delta -> 0`. Then

\[
\arccos f(\theta)
\le
m|\theta|.
\]

For `m|theta|<=pi`, monotonicity of cosine on `[0,pi]` gives `(3.1)`.

QED.

# 4. `P25-SELFADJOINT-SECANT-NORMING-23` — PROVED

Let

\[
F(e^{i\theta})
=
\sum_{k=-m}^{m}A_ke^{ik\theta}
\]

be an `N x N` matrix trigonometric polynomial satisfying

\[
F(e^{i\theta})^*=F(e^{i\theta})
\]

for every `theta`. Let

\[
S_L(F)
=
\max_{\zeta^L=1}\|F(\zeta)\|.
\]

If

\[
\boxed{m<L/2,}
\tag{4.1}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\|F\|_{C(\mathbb T)}
\le
\sec\!\left(\frac{m\pi}{L}\right)
S_L(F).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The estimate is independent of matrix size.

## Proof

Let

\[
M=\|F\|_{C(\mathbb T)}.
\]

Choose `theta_0` with

\[
\|F(e^{i\theta_0})\|=M.
\]

Because `F(theta_0)` is Hermitian, choose a unit eigenvector `xi` and real eigenvalue `lambda` such that

\[
F(e^{i\theta_0})\xi=\lambda\xi,
\qquad
|\lambda|=M.
\]

Set

\[
q(\theta)
=
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\lambda)}{M}
\langle F(e^{i\theta})\xi,\xi\rangle.
\]

Then `q` is real trigonometric of degree at most `m`,

\[
\|q\|_\infty\le1,
\qquad
q(\theta_0)=1.
\]

Choose an `L`th-root angle `theta_j` with circular distance

\[
|\theta_j-\theta_0|\le\pi/L.
\]

By `(4.1)`,

\[
m\pi/L<\pi/2.
\]

Apply `(3.1)`:

\[
q(\theta_j)
\ge
\cos(m\pi/L)>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
S_L(F)
\ge
\|F(e^{i\theta_j})\|
\ge
|\langle F(e^{i\theta_j})\xi,\xi\rangle|
\ge
M\cos(m\pi/L).
\]

Rearrange to get `(4.2)`.

QED.

## Sharpness certificate

P18's earlier Nyquist counterexample is actually an equality case for `(4.2)`:

\[
L=4,
\qquad
m=1,
\qquad
f(\theta)=\frac65\cos(\theta-\pi/4).
\]

Then

\[
\|f\|_\infty=6/5,
\]

while at the four roots

\[
S_4(f)=\frac{3\sqrt2}{5}.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{\|f\|_\infty}{S_4(f)}
=\sqrt2
=\sec(\pi/4).
\]

So the secant factor cannot be uniformly lowered even in the scalar self-adjoint class at `(L,m)=(4,1)`.

This simultaneously preserves the earlier counterexample:

> Nyquist uniqueness is not contractive norm control.

# 11. Categorical distinctions

## Full versus reduced C*-algebras

For the finite cyclic calibration,

\[
C^*(C_L)=C_r^*(C_L)
\]

because `C_L` is amenable. The coefficient-lift argument uses this Fourier model.

Nothing in this run constructs a quotient-induced homomorphism between reduced C*-algebras of nonamenable marked groups.

## Norm versus strong convergence

Every persistence and relation estimate is in operator norm. The decisive threshold is that the lifted unitary defect norm is strictly below one.

No strong, trace, or von Neumann convergence is substituted.

## Finite matrices versus von Neumann projections

All controlled representatives and homotopies are finite matrices in filtered reduced group algebras. P01-P39's von Neumann-only exotic kernel projections are categorically different objects.

## Scalar versus coefficient Baum–Connes

The controlled assembly maps are coefficient-free here. Their construction and naturality do not assume scalar surjectivity, and no coefficient Baum–Connes theorem is used in the cyclic proof.

## Stable versus unstable K-theory

The obstruction survives arbitrary finite stabilization. Matrix size is unrestricted; the norming theorem is dimension free.

## Endpoint versus relation complexity

The controlled endpoint is born at bounded propagation. The null-relation barrier is linear in `L`. This remains the key warning for P01's nonamenable proof route.
