# 7. Nilpotent quotient and finite-isotropy visibility

The source upper problem suggested looking for a genuinely nonabelian triangular compression. Before trying to construct one, one should know the earliest scale at which torsion can be geometrically visible.

Define the abelianized-base quotient

\[
\mathcal N_{m,L}
=
A_{m,L}\rtimes_T\mathbb Z,
\]

where

\[
A_{m,L}
=(\mathbb Z/L)e_1\oplus
\mathbb Ze_2\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Ze_m,
\]

and

\[
T(e_1)=e_1,
\qquad
T(e_j)=e_{j-1}+e_j.
\]

Abelianizing `H_{m,L}` gives a surjective homomorphism

\[
\pi:\Gamma_{m,L}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal N_{m,L}.
\]

The element `e_1` is central in `N_{m,L}`.

## `P25-TRIANGULAR-COORDINATE-LOWER-36` — **PROVED**

If a word `w` in the standard generators of `Gamma_{m,L}` has length at most `R` and

\[
\pi(w)=e_1^s
\]

in `N_{m,L}`, then, with circular residue

\[
|s|_L:=\min_{q\in\mathbb Z}|s+qL|,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
|s|_L
\le
R\binom{R+m-2}{m-1}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

Write `T=I+N`, where

\[
Ne_j=e_{j-1},
\qquad
N^m=0.
\]

For every integer height `h`, including negative `h`, the finite generalized binomial expansion gives

\[
T^he_j
=
\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}
\binom hr e_{j-r}.
\]

Thus the `e_1` coefficient of `T^he_j` is

\[
\binom h{j-1}.
\]

During normal-form collection of a word of length `R`, every base letter is inserted at a `t`-height `h` with

\[
|h|\le R,
\]

and there are at most `R` base letters.

For `0<=r<=m-1`,

\[
\left|\binom hr\right|
\le
\binom{R+m-2}{m-1}.
\]

For negative `h` this uses

\[
\left|\binom{-n}{r}\right|
=
\binom{n+r-1}{r}.
\]

Hence an integer lift of the final `e_1` coordinate has absolute value at most the right side of `(7.1)`. Taking the minimal representative modulo `L` proves the claim. ∎

## `P25-TRIANGULAR-TORSION-VISIBILITY-36` — **PROVED**

If

\[
D\binom{D+m-2}{m-1}<\frac L3,
\tag{7.2}
\]

then the action of `Gamma_{m,L}` on the `D`-Rips complex is free.

### Proof

Any finite subgroup `F<=Gamma_{m,L}` maps trivially to the quotient `Gamma_{m,L}->Z`, hence lies in

\[
H_{m,L}=C_L*F_{m-1}.
\]

By the Bass–Serre tree for the free product, every finite subgroup of `H_{m,L}` is conjugate to a subgroup of the finite cyclic factor `C_L`.

Let `|F|=q>=2`. Inside that cyclic subgroup choose an element conjugate to `x_1^s` with

\[
|s|_L\ge L/3.
\]

Indeed, for `q=2` take the half-turn, while for `q>=3` a power at least one third of the way around the cyclic subgroup has circular residue at least `L/3`.

Suppose `F` stabilizes a Rips simplex. Pick a vertex `g` in one of the vertex orbits in that simplex. Then for the chosen `f in F`, both `g` and `fg` lie in the simplex support, so

\[
|g^{-1}fg|<D
\]

(up to the harmless strict/non-strict Rips convention; `(7.2)` is stated with the larger integer `D` and is therefore safe for either convention used in the repository).

Because `e_1` is central in `N_{m,L}`,

\[
\pi(g^{-1}fg)=e_1^s.
\]

Applying `(7.1)` with word length `<D` contradicts `(7.2)` and `|s|_L>=L/3`.

Every point stabilizer in a locally finite Rips complex is finite: it permutes the finite support of the point, and the kernel of that permutation action fixes a vertex, hence is trivial for left translation. Therefore there are no nontrivial point stabilizers. ∎

The exact threshold `rho_{m,L}` in `(0.1)` is therefore a rigorous lower bound for the first scale at which nontrivial finite isotropy can appear.

Its odd finite lens-space skeleta are cofinal. For each such finite skeleton,

\[

and all positive even integral homology groups vanish.

In the homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence for complex K-homology,

\[

The generator in `E^2_{1,0}=Z/L` has no outgoing differential for degree reasons. A possible incoming differential `d_r` would originate at `p=1+r`.
