# 3. Equivariant Bott/Thom exact sequence

Write
\[
R=R(H)

\[
K_0^H(D(W))\cong R,
\qquad
K_1^H(D(W))=0,
\]
and
\[
K_0^H(D(W),S(W))\cong R,
\qquad
K_1^H(D(W),S(W))=0.
\]

Under these identifications, the map
\[
K_0^H(D(W))
\longrightarrow

\boxed{

\lambda_{-1}(W^*)

\tag{3.1}

The pair exact sequence is therefore

\boxed{

\longrightarrow
K_0^H(S(W))

\xrightarrow{\partial}

\tag{3.2}

This is the local representation-sphere block which enters at prime isotropy.

# 4. Kernel of the Euler map

Let
\[
N
=
1+t+\cdots+t^{p-1}.
\]

Every factor \(1-t^{-j}\) annihilates \(N\), hence
\[
e_WN=0.
\]

Over \(\mathbb C\), multiplication by \(e_W\) diagonalizes by evaluation at the \(p\)-th roots of unity. If
\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p},
\]

\[
e_W(1)=0,

while for
\[

\[
e_W(\zeta^k)
=
\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\zeta^{-jk})
\ne0.
\]

Thus the rational kernel has dimension one.

The vector \(N\) is primitive in the integral lattice \(R\), because its coefficients have gcd \(1\). Therefore the full integral kernel is
\[
\boxed{
\ker(\cdot e_W)
=
\mathbb Z N.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently
\[
\boxed{
K_0^H(S(W))
\cong
\mathbb Z.

\tag{4.2}
\]

# 5. Cokernel, free generator, and torsion order

The cokernel has rank one.

The augmentation
\[
\epsilon:R(H)\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\epsilon(t)=1,
\]
satisfies
\[
\epsilon(e_W)=0.
\]
Hence it descends to a surjection
\[
\bar\epsilon:
R/e_WR
\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z.
\]

The class of \(1\) maps to \(1\):
\[
\bar\epsilon([1])=1.
\]

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
[1]\in R/e_WR
\text{ is primitive and has infinite order}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, under the pair boundary in `(3.2)`, the relative Bott generator corresponding to
\[
1\in R
\]
maps to a primitive infinite-order element of
\[
K_1^H(S(W)).
\]

This is already enough to reject a universal "local boundary is multiplication by \(p\)" rule.

We can compute the total torsion order exactly.

Let
\[
M_e
\]
be the \(p\times p\) integer circulant matrix for multiplication by \(e_W\) in the basis
\[
1,t,\ldots,t^{p-1}.
\]

It has rank \(p-1\), and both its left and right integral kernels are generated by the all-ones vector.

Its nonzero complex eigenvalues are
\[
e_W(\zeta^k),
\qquad
k=1,\ldots,p-1.
\]

Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}e_W(\zeta^k)
\right|
&=
\prod_{j=1}^{m}
\left|
\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^{-jk})
\right|
\\
&=
p^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Here multiplication by a nonzero \(j\) permutes the nonzero residues modulo \(p\), and
\[
\prod_{k=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^k)=p.
\]

Since \(M_e\) has one-dimensional left and right kernel generated by the all-ones vector,
\[
\operatorname{adj}(M_e)=cJ
\]
for some integer \(c\), where \(J\) is the all-ones matrix.

The sum of the principal cofactors equals the product of the nonzero eigenvalues, so
\[
p|c|
=
p^m.
\]
Hence
\[
|c|=p^{m-1}.
\]

The torsion order of the cokernel of a rank-\((p-1)\) integer matrix is the gcd of its maximal minors. Those signed maximal minors are exactly the entries of the adjugate, so
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\operatorname{Tor}
\operatorname{coker}(\cdot e_W)
\right|
=
p^{m-1}
=
p^{(p-3)/2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus
\[
\boxed{
K_1^H(S(W))
\cong
\mathbb Z\oplus T_p
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
as an abelian group, noncanonically, with
\[
\boxed{
|T_p|
=
p^{(p-3)/2}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The theorem does **not** require or claim a full invariant-factor decomposition of \(T_p\).
