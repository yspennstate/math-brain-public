# 1. Visibility radius

Let \(G\) be a finitely generated discrete group with word length \(|\cdot|\).

For a finite subgroup \(H<G\), define

\[
\boxed{
\rho(H)
=
\min_{g\in G}
\max_{h\in H}|g^{-1}hg|.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is the conjugacy-optimized radius already used in the P01 Rips fixed-point analysis.

Let \(P_d(G)\) denote the Vietoris–Rips complex whose simplices are finite subsets of \(G\) of pairwise word distance at most \(d\).

For a finite subgroup \(K\le G\) satisfying

\[
K\subseteq B_d(e),
\]

every pair \(k_1,k_2\in K\) satisfies

\[
d_G(k_1,k_2)
=
|k_1^{-1}k_2|
\le d
\]

because \(k_1^{-1}k_2\in K\).

Thus \(K\) spans a Rips simplex.

Its barycenter

\[
\boxed{
b_K
=
\frac1{|K|}
\sum_{k\in K}[k]
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

is fixed by \(K\).

Hence there is a \(G\)-equivariant orbit map

\[
\boxed{
j_K:G/K\longrightarrow P_d(G),
\qquad
gK\longmapsto g\,b_K.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Conversely, a \(G\)-map

\[
G/H\to P_d(G)
\]

exists exactly when \(P_d(G)^H\neq\varnothing\), and the standard support-simplex argument gives

\[
P_d(G)^H\neq\varnothing
\iff
\rho(H)\le d.
\]

The theorem below uses this visibility criterion but does not claim that the K-class \([e_H]\) cannot happen to have some unrelated smaller-scale preimage.
