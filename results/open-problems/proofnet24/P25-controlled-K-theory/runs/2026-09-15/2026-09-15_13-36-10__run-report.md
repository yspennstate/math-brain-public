## 2. `P25-BERNSTEIN-SZEGO-POINTWISE-23` — PROVED

Let `f` be a real-valued trigonometric polynomial of degree at most `m>=1` and put `M=||f||_infty`. Then for every angle theta,

`|f'(theta)| <= m sqrt(M^2 - f(theta)^2)`.

Equivalently,

`(f')^2 + m^2 f^2 <= m^2 M^2` pointwise.

### Proof

Normalize `M=1` and translate the test point to `theta=0`. Write `a=f(0)`.

If `|a|=1`, boundedness makes `0` a local extremum, hence `f'(0)=0`.

Assume `|a|<1` and put `phi=arccos(a)`. Suppose first that

`f'(0) > m sin(phi)`.

Set

`g(theta)=cos(m theta - phi)` and `h=f-g`.

Then `h(0)=0` and `h'(0)>0`. The extrema of `g` occur at

`t_k=(phi+k pi)/m`,

and alternate between `+1` and `-1`. Because `|f|<=1`, at a `+1` extremum one has `h<=0`, while at a `-1` extremum one has `h>=0`.

Thus every cyclic interval between consecutive extrema contributes at least one zero of `h`, counted with multiplicity. If an endpoint itself is a zero, then `f=g=+/-1` there; both derivatives vanish, so that endpoint zero has multiplicity at least two and legitimately counts for both adjacent intervals.

The interval `[(phi-pi)/m, phi/m]` contains zero. Since `h'(0)>0`, `h` is negative immediately to the left of zero and positive immediately to the right. Its left endpoint has nonnegative `h` and its right endpoint has nonpositive `h`. Therefore this one interval contributes at least three zero units: one on the left side, the zero at `0`, and one on the right side.

Together with the remaining `2m-1` cyclic intervals, `h` has more than `2m` zeros in one period, counted with multiplicity.

But a nonzero trigonometric polynomial of degree at most `m` has at most `2m` zeros counted with multiplicity: multiply by `e^{im theta}` and view it as an algebraic polynomial of degree at most `2m` in `z=e^{i theta}`. Contradiction.

Therefore

`f'(0) <= m sqrt(1-a^2)`.

If `f'(0)<-m sqrt(1-a^2)`, compare instead with `cos(m theta + phi)` and obtain the symmetric contradiction.

Undo normalization and translation. QED.

## 3. `P25-COSINE-PROPAGATION-23` — PROVED

If `||f||_infty<=1`, `f(0)=1`, and `deg f<=m`, then for `|theta|<=pi/m`,

`f(theta) >= cos(m |theta|)`.

### Proof

For `0<delta<1`, define

`u_delta(theta)=arccos((1-delta) f(theta))`.

The argument stays strictly in `(-1,1)`. By the pointwise theorem,

`|u_delta'| <= m (1-delta) sqrt(1-f^2) / sqrt(1-(1-delta)^2 f^2) <= m`.

Hence

`u_delta(theta) <= arccos(1-delta) + m |theta|`.

Let `delta -> 0`. Then `arccos f(theta) <= m |theta|`, and monotonicity of cosine on `[0,pi]` gives the claim. QED.

## 4. `P25-SELFADJOINT-SECANT-NORMING-23` — PROVED

Let

`F(e^{i theta}) = sum_{k=-m}^m A_k e^{ik theta}`

be an `N x N` self-adjoint matrix trigonometric polynomial and let

`S_L(F)=max_{zeta^L=1} ||F(zeta)||`.

If `m<L/2`, then

`||F||_{C(T)} <= sec(m pi/L) S_L(F)`.

The estimate is independent of matrix size.

### Proof

Choose `theta_0` with `||F(theta_0)||=M=||F||_infty`. Since `F(theta_0)` is Hermitian, choose a unit eigenvector `xi` and real eigenvalue `lambda` with `|lambda|=M`.

The scalar compression

`q(theta)=sgn(lambda) <F(theta) xi,xi>/M`

is real, has degree at most `m`, satisfies `||q||_infty<=1`, and `q(theta_0)=1`.

Choose an `L`th-root angle `theta_j` within distance `pi/L` of `theta_0`. Since `m<L/2`, the cosine-propagation theorem gives

`q(theta_j) >= cos(m pi/L)>0`.

Therefore

`S_L(F) >= ||F(theta_j)|| >= |<F(theta_j)xi,xi>| >= M cos(m pi/L)`.

Rearrange. QED.

### Sharpness certificate

P18's earlier Nyquist counterexample is an equality case:

`L=4`, `m=1`, `f(theta)=(6/5) cos(theta-pi/4)`.

Then

`||f||_infty=6/5`, while `S_4(f)=3 sqrt(2)/5`, so

`||f||_infty/S_4(f)=sqrt(2)=sec(pi/4)`.

Thus the secant factor cannot be uniformly lowered even in the scalar self-adjoint class at `(L,m)=(4,1)`.

## 5. `P25-CYCLIC-SHARP-INFINITE-ORDER-WINDOW-23` — PROVED

Let

`A_L=C_r^*(C_L)=C(Omega_L)`

and fix `0<epsilon<1/4`. Let `y_{L,r}` be the inherited scale-one controlled assembly class whose coefficient lift to `C(T)` has winding one.

Define

`c_epsilon^sharp = arccos(3 epsilon)/(4 pi)`.

Then throughout the admissible birth range,

`r < c_epsilon^sharp L  =>  ord(y_{L,r})=infinity`.

### Proof

Assume `n y_{L,r}=0` for nonzero integer `n`. Published quantitative-K1 bookkeeping supplies, after stabilization, a path through `3 epsilon`-unitaries of propagation at most `2r`, from the `n`-fold block sum of the birth representative to the identity.

The hypothesis gives

`4 pi r/L < arccos(3 epsilon) < pi/2`,

hence `r<L/8`. Coefficient lift is unique. The two unitary defects are self-adjoint Laurent polynomials of degree at most `4r<L/2`, and their norms at every `L`th root are below `3 epsilon`.

The secant theorem gives

`||lifted defect|| < 3 epsilon sec(4 pi r/L)`.

Because `cos(4 pi r/L)>3 epsilon`, this is strictly below one. Every lifted path matrix is invertible. Polar correction gives a genuine unitary path over `C(T)`.

The initial determinant winding is `n`; the identity has winding zero. Contradiction. Hence no nonzero multiple vanishes. QED.

### Improvement

The preceding P25 constant was `(1-3 epsilon)/(4 pi)`. P18 improved it to `sqrt(1-3 epsilon)/(2 sqrt(2) pi)`.

The new constant is `arccos(3 epsilon)/(4 pi)`. Since `arccos x >= sqrt(2(1-x))` for `0<=x<1`, the new window contains P18's window. As `epsilon -> 0`, the constants tend respectively to about `0.07958`, `0.11254`, and exactly `1/8`.

## 7. `P25-CYCLIC-SHARP-DIRECT-PATH-23` — PROVED

Let `0<=delta<1`. Suppose a path of `delta`-unitaries of propagation at most `R` joins an endpoint whose coefficient lift has nonzero winding to a trivial endpoint. Then

`R >= L/(2 pi) arccos(delta)`.

### Proof

If instead `R < L/(2 pi) arccos(delta)`, then `R<L/4`. The coefficient lift is unique, and the two self-adjoint defects have degree at most `2R<L/2`. Their sampled norms are below `delta`.

The secant theorem gives

`||lifted defect|| < delta sec(2 pi R/L) < 1`.

Polar correction produces a genuine circle-unitary homotopy, contradicting winding. QED.

At `delta=0`, this gives exactly

`R>=L/4`,

recovering the inherited exact no-wrap stable-nullhomotopy barrier from one analytic formula.

## 11. Categorical distinctions

**Full versus reduced.** For finite cyclic groups, full and reduced group C*-algebras coincide by amenability. Nothing here constructs a quotient-induced reduced-C* map for arbitrary nonamenable marked groups.

**Norm versus strong.** Every relation estimate is an operator-norm statement. Strong, trace, or von Neumann convergence is not substituted.

**Finite matrices versus von Neumann projections.** The controlled representatives and homotopies are finite matrices in filtered reduced group algebras. P01-P39's exotic kernel projections are von-Neumann objects and are categorically different.

**Scalar versus coefficient Baum–Connes.** The cyclic controlled assembly is coefficient-free. No scalar surjectivity or coefficient theorem is used.

**Stable versus unstable.** The obstruction survives arbitrary finite stabilization. The norming theorem is matrix-size independent.

**Endpoint versus relation complexity.** The endpoint is born at bounded propagation, while its stable relation requires propagation linear in `L`.
