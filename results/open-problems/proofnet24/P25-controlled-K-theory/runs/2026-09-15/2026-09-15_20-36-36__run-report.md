## 3.1 Ricard–Xu reduced-free-product Khintchine theorem

Éric Ricard and Quanhua Xu, *Khintchine type inequalities for reduced free products and Applications*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 599 (2006), 27–59, arXiv `math/0505302`.

Primary record:

`https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505302`

The source proves fixed-word-length Khintchine inequalities for reduced free products at matrix level and completely bounded homogeneous projections. The constants depend on homogeneous word length, not on the identity of the unchanged free factor. This is the operator-space input behind both the forward and reverse local base estimates.

## 3.2 Pimsner–Voiculescu exact sequence

M. Pimsner and D. Voiculescu, *Exact sequences for K-groups and Ext-groups of certain cross-product C*-algebras*, J. Operator Theory 4 (1980), 93–118.

Primary journal record:

`https://jot.theta.ro/jot/archive/1980-004-001/1980-004-001-005.html`

The natural six-term sequence for a crossed product by `Z` is used below for the invariant subgroup generated by `x_1`. The action on that subgroup is the identity, so the boundary of the reduced Bott class is the circle-generator class, up to the conventional sign.

## 3.3 Pimsner–Voiculescu free-group K-theory

M. Pimsner and D. Voiculescu, *K-groups of reduced crossed products by free groups*, J. Operator Theory 8 (1982), 131–156; original 1981 preprint preserved at

`https://imar.ro/~increst/1981/62_1981.pdf`.

The computation gives

\[
K_0(C_r^*(F_m))\cong\mathbb Z[1],
\qquad
K_1(C_r^*(F_m))\cong\mathbb Z^m,

with the canonical free-generator unitary classes as the standard `K_1` basis. Only the nonvanishing of `[x_1]` is needed here.

## 3.4 Functoriality warning

The group quotient

\[
\Gamma_m\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma_{m,L}
\]

has nonamenable kernel in general and is **never** promoted here to a global reduced-group-C*-algebra quotient homomorphism.

There are only three map types below:

1. algebraic coefficient evaluation on finite group-ring support;
2. local finite-propagation cb coefficient lifts whose norms are proved directly;
3. genuine reduced C*-homomorphisms on the **amenable abelian subgroup** `Z^2 -> C_L x Z`, where reduced and full functoriality agree.

This distinction is essential.

# 5. Reverse free-product base theorem with unchanged `F_{m-1}` factor

Put

\[
A_\infty=C_r^*(F_m)
=C_r^*(\mathbb Z)*C_r^*(F_{m-1}),
\]

\[
A_L=C_r^*(C_L*F_{m-1})
=C_r^*(C_L)*C_r^*(F_{m-1}).
\]

Fix an integer `S>=1` with

\[
2S<L.
\]

On the cyclic first factor, every stage Laurent polynomial of degree at most `S` has a unique coefficient lift to the circle. The inherited matrix secant theorem gives, at every matrix size,

\[
\|\widetilde F\|_{C(\mathbb T)}
\le
c_{S,L}\|F_L\|,
\qquad
c_{S,L}:=\sec\frac{\pi S}{L}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

On the row and column GNS Hilbert spaces the same coefficient lift is an exact isometry: the Fourier modes of degrees `|k|<=S` remain orthonormal because `2S<L` prevents aliasing. The second free factor `F_{m-1}` is unchanged.

Ricard–Xu's homogeneous operator-space model `E_d` contains row/column GNS factors and at most one diagonal C*-operator-space factor. Consequently the degree-`d` stage-to-parent lift has norm at most `c_{S,L}` on `E_d`.

Using

\[
\|y_d\|\le(2d+1)\|y_d\|_{E_d}
\]

and the homogeneous projection bound

\[
\|y_{d,L}\|\le4d\|y_L\|
\qquad(d\ge1),
\]

we obtain

\[
\|y_d\|
\le4d(2d+1)c_{S,L}\|y_L\|.
\]

The degree-zero expectation is contractive and unchanged. Summing `d=0,...,S` gives

\[
\boxed{
\|y\|
\le B^-_{S,L}\|y_L\|,
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
B^-_{S,L}
=
1+c_{S,L}(A_S-1),
\]

\[
A_S
=1+\sum_{d=1}^{S}4d(2d+1)
=1+\frac{S(S+1)(8S+10)}3.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `c_{S,L}>=1`, for `S>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
B^-_{S,L}
\le13S^3\sec\frac{\pi S}{L}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Every estimate is completely bounded. This is a genuine extension of the earlier P25 reverse base theorem to an arbitrary unchanged free factor `F_{m-1}`; no dimension-dependent loss appears.

\[

\le
\exp\!\left(
10\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)\|X_L\|.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

This proves `P25-TRIANGULAR-REVERSE-CB-GATE-33`.

### Matrix stability

The cyclic secant lift is completely bounded, the Ricard–Xu estimates are completely bounded, Fourier coefficient extraction is completely contractive, and C*-powers are taken inside matrix algebras. Thus (7.8) is uniform over stabilization size.

# 8. Two-sided triangular local near-isometry

P18 proves the forward estimate

\[
\|X_L\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
8\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)\|X\|
\tag{8.1}
\]

for `R<=q/8`.

\[
\boxed{
 e^{-10\Lambda_m R\log L/(L-1)^{1/m}}\|X\|

\le
 e^{8\Lambda_m R\log L/(L-1)^{1/m}}\|X\|.

\[
\boxed{
R_L\log L=o(L^{1/m}).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This proves `P25-TRIANGULAR-TWO-SIDED-CB-GATE-33`.

The theorem still does **not** imply ordinary K-theory continuity: a K-relation can require much larger propagation than the representative. The next sections turn that warning into an exact theorem.

# 9. A Bott class fixed by the parent but killed by every cyclic filling

## 9.1 The invariant two-torus subgroup

Because `alpha_m(x_1)=x_1`, the elements `x_1` and `t` commute in `Gamma_m`. Thus

\[
A:=\langle x_1,t\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

At the stage,

\[
A_L:=\langle x_1,t\rangle\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z.
\]

Subgroup inclusions always give isometric embeddings of reduced group C*-algebras, so

\[
C_r^*(A)=C(\mathbb T^2)
\hookrightarrow C_r^*(\Gamma_m),
\]

\[
C_r^*(A_L)=C(\mu_L\times\mathbb T)
\hookrightarrow C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}).
\]

Let `P_B` be a Bott projection over `C(T^2)`, let `e` be a constant projection of the same rank, and define

\[
\beta=[P_B]-[e]\in\widetilde K_0(C(\mathbb T^2))\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Let `beta_m` denote its image in `K_0(C_r^*(Gamma_m))`.

## 9.2 Parent nonvanishing by the PV boundary

Put

\[
B=C_r^*(F_m).
\]

The subgroup `C_r^*(\langle x_1\rangle)=C(\mathbb T)` is invariant under `alpha_m`, and `alpha_m` acts as the identity on it. The inclusion

\[
C(\mathbb T)\hookrightarrow B
\]

is equivariant. Naturality of the Pimsner–Voiculescu exact sequence therefore gives a commutative square for the boundary maps

\[
K_0(C(\mathbb T)\rtimes\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow
K_1(C(\mathbb T))
\]

and

\[
K_0(B\rtimes_{\alpha_m}\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow
K_1(B).
\]

For the identity action, the reduced Bott class has boundary, up to sign,

\[
[z]\in K_1(C(\mathbb T)).
\]

Its image in `K_1(B)` is the canonical free-generator class

\[
[x_1].
\]

Pimsner–Voiculescu compute

\[
K_1(C_r^*(F_m))\cong\mathbb Z^m
\]

with `[x_1],...,[x_m]` as a basis. Hence

\[
[x_1]\ne0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\beta_m\ne0.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is an ordinary **stable reduced-`K_0`** statement. It is not an unstable projection noncancellation claim.

## 9.3 Stage vanishing

On the amenable subgroup the group quotient

\[
\mathbb Z^2\twoheadrightarrow C_L\times\mathbb Z
\]

induces the genuine C*-homomorphism

\[
\rho_L:C(\mathbb T^2)	o C(\mu_L\times\mathbb T),
\qquad
f\mapsto f|_{\mu_L\times\mathbb T}.
\]

The target is

\[
\bigoplus_{\zeta^L=1}C(\mathbb T).
\]

On every component, restriction of the Bott line-bundle difference has rank zero. Since

\[
K_0(C(\mathbb T))\cong\mathbb Z
\]

is detected by rank,

\[
(\rho_L)_*(\beta)=0.
\]

After inclusion into the stage group algebra,

\[
\boxed{\beta_{m,L}=0\in K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}))}
\tag{9.2}
\]

for every `L`.

This proves `P25-TRIANGULAR-BOTT-COLLAPSE-33`.

### Important map distinction

Equation (9.2) does **not** use a global map

\[
C_r^*(\Gamma_m)\to C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}).
\]

It uses the genuine abelian subgroup map and then the inclusion of that subgroup into the stage. The finite group-ring representative used below is evaluated algebraically in the full stage group ring.

# 10. A fixed finite-propagation approximate Bott representative

Laurent-polynomial matrices are dense in matrix algebras over `C(T^2)`. Choose a self-adjoint finite Laurent-polynomial matrix

\[
p_B=p_B^*
\]

such that

\[
\|p_B-P_B\|<\frac1{512}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Let `r_B` be its finite word propagation in `A=Z^2`; `r_B` is fixed once and for all.

Writing `h=p_B-P_B`,

\[
p_B^2-p_B
=P_Bh+hP_B+h^2-h,
\]

so

\[
\|p_B^2-p_B\|
\le3\|h\|+\|h\|^2
<
\frac3{512}+\frac1{512^2}
=
\frac{1537}{262144}
<\frac1{128}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Because the abelian stage restriction `rho_L` is contractive, the same defect estimate holds for `p_{B,L}`. Spectral rounding at `1/2` recovers the exact restricted Bott projection class for all stages.

Thus the **representative propagation is constant**, independent of `L`.

# 11. Existence of finite stage null relations, with no uniform radius asserted

Since the rounded stage class is zero in ordinary stable `K_0`, group completion gives a common auxiliary projection after stabilization so that the rounded Bott projection and the matching constant projection are stably Murray–von Neumann equivalent. After a further standard `2x2` rotation this yields an exact norm-continuous projection path in a matrix algebra over `C_r^*(Gamma_{m,L})`.

Concatenate:

1. the short linear segment from `p_{B,L}` to its exact rounded projection;
2. the exact stabilized projection path;
3. the constant auxiliary block at both endpoints.

The first segment has a strict idempotent-defect margin because of (10.2); the rest has zero defect.

The matrix group ring is dense in the reduced group C*-algebra. By compactness of the path, choose finitely many times, approximate the path and the common auxiliary projection uniformly by self-adjoint finite group-ring matrices, and interpolate linearly. Approximation can be made small enough that every matrix along the resulting finite-support path satisfies

\[
\|a_t^2-a_t\|<1/64.
\]

The union of finitely many supports is finite, so the resulting path has some finite propagation radius `R_L`.

This proves:

> For every `L`, a finite-propagation stable `1/64` null relation exists.

No quantitative upper bound on `R_L` is claimed. In fact the next section proves it must diverge on the exact scale of the current norm window.

---

# 12. Stable relation escape: proof of the quantitative lower bound

Let

\[
a_t=a_t^*
\]

be a stable `1/64` null relation of propagation at most `R`, from

\[
p_{B,L}\oplus f_L
\]

to

\[
e\oplus f_L,
\]

where the same auxiliary approximate projection appears at both endpoints.

Assume for contradiction that

\[
R<
\frac{\log16}{20\Lambda_m}
\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\log L}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

For all sufficiently large `L`, this implies

\[
R\le q_{m,L}/8.
\tag{12.2}
\]

Indeed `q=(L-1)^{1/m}/Lambda_m`, whereas the coefficient in (12.1) is `log16/(20)<1/8` after division by `log L` for large `L`.

## 12.1 Local coefficient lift of the path

Lift each base Fourier coefficient of `a_t` through the no-wrap map. The path stays in one finite-dimensional local operator space, and the reverse cb theorem makes the lift bounded; hence the lifted path

\[
\widetilde a_t
\]

is norm continuous.

The idempotent defect has propagation at most `2R`. Under (12.2), the base support involved in multiplying two lifted radius-`R` terms is far below the cyclic relation length, so local multiplication is exact: the coefficient lift of

\[
a_t^2-a_t
\]

is precisely

\[
\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t.
\]

Apply the reverse theorem (7.8) at radius `2R`:

\[
\|\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
20\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)
\|a_t^2-a_t\|.
\tag{12.3}
\]

By (12.1), the exponential factor is strictly less than `16`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t\|<\frac14
}
\tag{12.4}
\]

uniformly in `t`.

## 12.2 Spectral rounding in the parent

For a self-adjoint element with idempotent defect `<1/4`, the spectrum avoids `1/2`. Thus

\[
\widetilde P_t
=
\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(\widetilde a_t)
\]

is a norm-continuous path of genuine projections in the parent reduced group C*-algebra.

At the first endpoint, the fixed block `p_B` rounds to the Bott projection `P_B`; the lifted auxiliary block rounds to some parent projection `F`. At the final endpoint, the constant block rounds to `e` and the **same** lifted auxiliary block rounds to the same `F`.

Therefore the rounded parent homotopy gives

\[
[P_B]+[F]=[e]+[F]
\]

in ordinary stable `K_0(C_r^*(Gamma_m))`. Cancelling in the K-group,

\[
[P_B]-[e]=0,
\]

contradicting (9.1).

Hence (12.1) is impossible. We have proved

\[
\boxed{
R\ge
\frac{\log16}{20\Lambda_m}
\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\log L}
}
\tag{12.5}
\]

for every stable `1/64` relation once `L` is sufficiently large.

This proves `P25-TRIANGULAR-BOTT-RELATION-ESCAPE-33`.

### Stable versus unstable

The proof allows arbitrary finite matrix stabilization and an arbitrary common auxiliary approximate projection. The constant is independent of stabilization size because the reverse theorem is completely bounded. Thus this is a genuinely **stable** relation lower bound, not an unstable connected-component obstruction.

### Ordinary versus controlled K-theory

Ordinary `K_0` says the stage class is zero and therefore some finite relation exists. The theorem says every such controlled path witness at tolerance `1/64` must have propagation at least (12.5). This is exactly the distinction between ordinary stable K-theory and quantitative/controlled K-theory that the P01 approximation program must preserve.

# 13. Consequences for the P01 moving-relation bottleneck

The result gives a clean four-budget calibration.

For the Bott family:

1. **target representative propagation:** fixed, equal to `r_B`;
2. **ordinary target class at every stage:** zero;
3. **target stable relation propagation:** at least
   \[
   c_m\frac{L^{1/m}}{\log L},
   \qquad
   c_m=\frac{\log16}{20\Lambda_m};
   \]
4. **available two-sided local cb window:** asymptotically exactly the regime
   \[
   R\log L=o(L^{1/m}).
   \]

Thus the relation witness is forced to the boundary scale at which the known local near-isometry ceases to be asymptotically trivial.

This is a rigorous falsification of the shortcut

\[
\text{short representative}
\Rightarrow
\text{short stable relation}.
\]

It also gives a more structured version of the earlier free-product relation-escape phenomenon: the lower bound now tracks the polynomial monodromy depth `m`.

The theorem does **not** yet solve P01's assembly problem, because no quantitative **source-side** Rips/localization relation for this Bott class has been constructed here. The source representative/death budget remains OPEN and is the correct next attack.

# 14. Counterexamples and rejected overstatements

## 14.1 `P25-FIXED-REPRESENTATIVE-NOT-FIXED-RELATION-CE-33` — **DISPROVED overstrong principle**

The family `beta_{m,L}` has a representative of fixed propagation but every stable `1/64` null relation has propagation tending to infinity at least as in (12.5).

Therefore fixed representatives do not imply fixed or even sub-`L^{1/m}/log L` relations.

## 14.2 Rejected: two-sided local cb convergence forces ordinary K-theory continuity

False. For every radius sequence satisfying

\[
R_L\log L=o(L^{1/m}),
\]

the local operator spaces are completely near-isometric by (8.2), yet the parent Bott class is nonzero and every finite-stage class is zero.

The missing datum is relation propagation.

## 14.3 Rejected: the group quotient supplies a global reduced-C* map

False and unused. The only global quotient C*-map in the Bott argument is on the amenable subgroup `Z^2 -> C_L x Z`. All nonamenable parent/stage transfer is finite-support and norm-controlled.

## 14.4 Rejected: stage `K_0` zero gives a bounded relation radius

Ordinary `K_0` supplies existence of some stable homotopy after stabilization, but compactness/density only gives a finite radius depending on `L`. The theorem proves this dependence is necessarily large.

## 14.5 Rejected: trace meshes are relevant to this Bott escape proof

The new Bott relation-escape theorem does **not** use the `L^{-m}Z` trace mesh. The Bott class is trace zero. Its nontriviality is detected by the PV boundary into `K_1(C_r^*(F_m))`, not by the canonical trace. This is useful precisely because the earlier fixed-gap rank-sandwich mechanism is trace-sensitive while moving stable relations can hide in trace-zero directions.
