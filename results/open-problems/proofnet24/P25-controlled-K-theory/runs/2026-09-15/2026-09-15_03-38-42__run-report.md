# 6. Diffusive scale is intrinsic to uniform polynomial resolvent calculus

One might hope that a more clever degree-\(N\) polynomial could uniformly resolve the resolvent at a much smaller scale than \(M/N^2\).

That is false.

We use the sharp Markov inequality on \([-1,1]\):

\[
\|q'\|_{\infty,[-1,1]}
\le
N^2
\|q\|_{\infty,[-1,1]}
\]

for every polynomial of degree at most \(N\).

After affine rescaling to \([0,M]\),

\[
\boxed{
\|p'\|_{\infty,[0,M]}
\le
\frac{2N^2}{M}
\|p\|_{\infty,[0,M]}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The exact \(N^2\) constant is the classical Markov inequality.

Assume

\[
0<\varepsilon\le M/3
\]

and a degree-\(N\) polynomial satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|p-f_\varepsilon\|_{\infty,[0,M]}
\le
\frac18.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

At \(x=0\),

\[
p(0)\ge\frac78.
\]

At \(x=3\varepsilon\),

\[
f_\varepsilon(3\varepsilon)=\frac14,
\]

hence

\[
p(3\varepsilon)\le\frac38.
\]

So the mean value theorem gives

\[
\|p'\|_\infty
\ge
\frac{1/2}{3\varepsilon}
=
\frac1{6\varepsilon}.
\]

Also

\[
\|p\|_\infty\le\frac98.
\]

By `(6.1)`,

\[
\frac1{6\varepsilon}
\le
\frac{2N^2}{M}\frac98
=
\frac{9N^2}{4M}.
\]

Therefore:

## `P25-DIFFUSIVE-POLY-BARRIER-12` — PROVED

Any polynomial satisfying `(6.2)` must obey

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon
\ge
\frac{2M}{27N^2}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
N
\ge
\sqrt{\frac{2M}{27\varepsilon}}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Hence the \(N^{-2}\) energy scale in P01-P29 and in `(4.2)` is not merely an artifact of one Chebyshev construction.

It is the correct resolution order for **uniform polynomial approximation of the resolvent**.

### Scope of the barrier

This does **not** prove that every conceivable local method is limited to \(N^{-2}\).

It does not cover, for example:

- geometry-specific inequalities;
- non-polynomial information not determined by finitely many local moments;
- rational approximation implemented through additional inverses;
- a genuine cross-stage reduced-\(C^*\) morphism, if one happens to exist.

It does show that replacing the P29 polynomial by another uniformly accurate degree-\(N\) polynomial resolvent approximation cannot beat the diffusive exponent.

The present result is functional calculus, not a fixed-size representative theorem.
