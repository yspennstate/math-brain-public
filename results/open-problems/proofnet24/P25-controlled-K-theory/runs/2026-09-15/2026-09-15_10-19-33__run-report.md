## Visibility radius

For a finite subgroup `H<G`, define
\[
\rho(H)=\min_{g\in G}\max_{h\in H}|g^{-1}hg|.
\]

If `d >= rho(H)`, choose a conjugate
\[
K=g^{-1}Hg\subseteq B_d(e).
\]
Because `K` is a subgroup, for `k_1,k_2 in K`,
\[
|k_1^{-1}k_2|\le d,
\]
so `K` spans a simplex of `P_d(G)`. Its barycenter
\[
b_K=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{k\in K}[k]
\]
is fixed by `K`, hence
\[
j_K:G/K\to P_d(G),\qquad xK\mapsto x b_K
\]
is `G`-equivariant.

Conversely, a `G`-map `G/H -> P_d(G)` exists iff `P_d(G)^H` is nonempty, and the standard support-simplex argument gives
\[
P_d(G)^H\neq\varnothing\iff \rho(H)\le d.
\]

This is an exact visibility statement for the canonical orbit source. It is not a claim that the target K-class cannot accidentally have some different smaller-scale preimage.

## Exact finite-stabilizer matrix units

Let `K` be finite and let
\[
\pi:K\to U(d_\pi)
\]
be irreducible. Define
\[
E_{ij}^{\pi}
=
\frac{d_\pi}{|K|}
\sum_{k\in K}\pi(k^{-1})_{ji}\,k.
\]
Schur orthogonality gives
\[
E_{ij}^{\pi}E_{\ell m}^{\sigma}
=
\delta_{\pi\sigma}\delta_{j\ell}E_{im}^{\pi},
\qquad
(E_{ij}^{\pi})^*=E_{ji}^{\pi}.
\]
Therefore
\[
p_\pi:=E_{11}^{\pi}
\]
is an exact projection in `C K`. Under
\[
C^*K\cong\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\widehat K}M_{d_\sigma}(\mathbb C),
\]
`p_\pi` is a rank-one projection in the `pi` block, hence its K-class is the generator corresponding to the irreducible representation `pi`.

If `K subset B_d(e)`, then
\[
\operatorname{prop}(p_\pi)\le d.
\]
Its canonical group trace is
\[
\tau_G(p_\pi)=\frac{d_\pi}{|K|}.
\]
For the trivial representation,
\[
p_{\mathbf 1_K}=e_K=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{k\in K}k.
\]

## `P25-ISOTROPY-REPRESENTATION-CONTROL-19` — PROVED

Let `G` be finitely generated, `H<G` finite, and `d >= rho(H)`. Choose `K=g^{-1}Hg subset B_d(e)` and transport an irreducible representation `pi` of `H` to `pi^g` of `K`.

Then the orbit map `j_K` pushes
\[
[\pi^g]\in K_0^G(G/K)\cong R(K)
\]
to a class
\[
\xi_{\pi,d}\in K_0^G(P_d(G))
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\mu_d(\xi_{\pi,d})=[p_{\pi^g}]
\in K_0(C_r^*(G)).
}
\]

The projection `p_{\pi^g}` is exact and has propagation at most `d`.

Moreover
\[
\lambda_g p_{\pi^g}\lambda_g^*=p_\pi,
\]
so
\[
[p_{\pi^g}]=[p_\pi]
\]
in reduced K-theory. Thus the finite-subgroup K-class can be represented by the short conjugate even if the original subgroup sits very far from the identity.

Finally
\[
\boxed{
\tau_*\mu_d(\xi_{\pi,d})
=
\frac{\dim\pi}{|H|}.
}
\]

No scalar Baum–Connes surjectivity, coefficient Baum–Connes theorem, functional calculus, strong convergence, or reduced-norm comparison is used.

### Orbit assembly proof

For finite `K`,
\[
K_0^G(G/K)\cong K_0^K(pt)\cong R(K)\cong K_0(C^*K).
\]
Since `K` is finite,
\[
C^*K=C_r^*K.
\]
The restriction of the left regular representation of `G` to `K` is a direct sum of copies of the left regular representation of `K`, indexed by left cosets. Hence
\[
C_r^*K\hookrightarrow C_r^*G
\]
is isometric.

Under Green/induction identification, assembly for the proper orbit `G/K` is the K-theory map induced by this subgroup inclusion. Therefore the representation-ring generator `pi` maps to `[p_\pi]`. Naturality along `j_K:G/K -> P_d(G)` proves the theorem.

## `P25-FINITE-ISOTROPY-SPAN-19` — PROVED

Define
\[
\mathcal I_d(G)
=
\sum_{\substack{H<G\text{ finite}\\ \rho(H)\le d}}
\operatorname{im}\bigl(R(H)\to K_0(C_r^*G)\bigr).
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_d(G)
\subseteq
\mu_d\bigl(K_0^G(P_d(G))\bigr).
}
\]

Every element of `I_d(G)` can be represented as
\[
[P_+]-[P_-],
\]
where `P_+` and `P_-` are finite block-diagonal exact projections over `C G` and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{prop}(P_\pm)\le d.
}
\]

The matrix size may depend on the integral combination; the propagation does not.

Because these are exact projections, they define quantitative K-theory classes in
\[
K_0^{\varepsilon,d}(C_r^*G)
\]
for every `0<epsilon<1/4`, with no tolerance or spectral-correction cost.

This is a representative theorem, not a universal relation theorem: stable equivalences among these classes can still require large propagation.

## Categorical distinctions

**Full vs reduced.** All target statements are in `C_r^*(G)`. For finite `K`, full and reduced coincide, and the subgroup inclusion into `C_r^*(G)` is reduced-isometric. No quotient map between unrelated reduced group algebras is used.

**Norm vs strong.** The new representatives are exact algebraic projections. No strong convergence, trace convergence, or approximate norm comparison is needed to construct them.

**Finite matrices vs von Neumann projections.** Every finite-isotropy representation generator has an exact finite group-ring projection. This is stronger than a von Neumann projection or a trace-only statement.

**Scalar vs coefficient Baum–Connes.** Everything is coefficient-free.

**Stable vs unstable.** Integral combinations use stabilization. Matrix size can grow; propagation remains bounded by the Rips visibility scale.

**Generator vs relation control.** Visible isotropy generators are locally exact. Relations among them may still require long stable homotopies; this run does not erase the moving controlled-witness obstruction.
