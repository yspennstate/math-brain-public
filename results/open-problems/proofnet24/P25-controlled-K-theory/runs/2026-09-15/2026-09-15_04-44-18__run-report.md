## `P25-QUANTITATIVE-ASSEMBLY-ENDPOINT-13` — PROVED / primary-source specialization

Let \(\Gamma\) be a finitely generated discrete group. Filter \(C_r^*(\Gamma)\) by word propagation. Fix a Rips scale \(d>0\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\).

Quantitative K-theory uses
\[
K_*^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*\Gamma),
\]
represented in degree zero by finite matrices \(p=p^*\) of propagation at most \(r\) with
\[
\|p^2-p\|<\varepsilon,
\]
and in degree one by finite matrices \(u\) of propagation at most \(r\) with
\[
\|u^*u-1\|<\varepsilon,\qquad \|uu^*-1\|<\varepsilon.
\]
There is a comparison map
\[
\iota_*^{\varepsilon,r}:K_*^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*\Gamma)\to K_*(C_r^*\Gamma).
\]

Oyono-Oyono–Yu's quantitative assembly construction, and Dell'Aiera's controlled groupoid version, give for fixed \(d,\varepsilon\) a radius \(r_{d,\varepsilon}<\infty\) and a map
\[
\mu_{\Gamma,*}^{\varepsilon,r_{d,\varepsilon},d}:
KK_*^\Gamma(C_0(P_d\Gamma),\mathbb C)
\longrightarrow
K_*^{\varepsilon,r_{d,\varepsilon}}(C_r^*\Gamma)
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\iota_*^{\varepsilon,r_{d,\varepsilon}}
\circ
\mu_{\Gamma,*}^{\varepsilon,r_{d,\varepsilon},d}
=
\mu_{\Gamma,*}^{d}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

In the standard word-ball controlled structure, Dell'Aiera's universal controlled Kasparov transform uses a control pair \((\alpha_J,k_J)\). Choosing \(\varepsilon'\) with \(\alpha_J\varepsilon'\le\varepsilon\), the target control is obtained by a \(k_J(\varepsilon')\)-fold enlargement of the Rips control set. Since
\[
B_d(e)^k\subset B_{kd}(e),
\]
one obtains a class-independent linear propagation bound
\[
\boxed{
r_{d,\varepsilon}\le \kappa(\varepsilon)d,
}
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\kappa(\varepsilon)\) is the universal integer enlargement coming from the controlled Kasparov transform. I do not invent a numerical value for \(\kappa\); the cited source does not provide one in the form needed here.

Consequently every
\[
x\in K_*^\Gamma(P_d\Gamma)
\]
has a finite-support quantitative endpoint
\[
\widetilde\mu_{d,\varepsilon}(x)
\in
K_*^{\varepsilon,r_{d,\varepsilon}}(C_r^*\Gamma)
\]
whose ordinary comparison class is exactly
\[
\mu_d(x).
\]

For \(K_0\), one may represent the controlled class by a finite-support \(\varepsilon\)-projection \(p_x\); the exact projection
\[
\chi_{[1/2,\infty)}(p_x)
\]
represents the ordinary assembly class after stabilization. For \(K_1\), one uses a finite-support \(\varepsilon\)-unitary and the ordinary polar comparison.

The matrix size may depend on \(x\). The propagation bound does not.

## Matrix size is not propagation

Quantitative K-theory separates matrix size, propagation radius, and idempotence/unitarity defect. Stabilization can increase matrix size without increasing group-word propagation. Therefore long topological complexity can in principle be absorbed into matrix size rather than word support.

This is relevant to the P01/P18 marked-transfer program because their completely bounded norm-comparison mechanism is designed not to charge matrix amplification as additional group support.

## `P25-EXACT-FC-PROPAGATION-CE-13` — overstrong form DISPROVED

It is false that exact spectral correction must preserve finite propagation.

Take
\[
G=\mathbb Z,\qquad C_r^*(G)=C(\mathbb T),
\]
with canonical unitary \(z\), and set
\[
c=\frac{z+z^{-1}}2.
\]
Consider
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
c&1\\
1&-c
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is self-adjoint and has propagation \(1\). Moreover
\[
A^2=(1+c^2)I,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{spec}(A)\subset(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty).
\]
The positive spectral projection is
\[
\boxed{
P_+=\frac12\left(I+A(1+c^2)^{-1/2}\right).
}
\tag{3}
\]
The factor \((1+c^2)^{-1/2}\) is not a Laurent polynomial. If it were a Laurent polynomial \(q(z)\), then
\[
(1+c^2)q(z)^2=1
\]
in \(\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]\), forcing \(1+c^2\) to be a unit. The only Laurent-polynomial units are \(\lambda z^k\), and \(1+c^2\) is not of that form. Hence \(P_+\) has infinite Fourier/group support.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{exact functional calculus can destroy finite propagation even across a fixed spectral gap.}
}
\]

The correct endpoint notion for the controlled program is an \(\varepsilon\)-projection/\(\varepsilon\)-unitary, not an exact finite-support spectral projection.

\[

\]

\[
\alpha=\mu_H((0,\varepsilon)),
\]
we have \(\log\lambda\le\log\varepsilon\) on \((0,\varepsilon)\) and \(\log\lambda\le\log\widehat M\) on \([\varepsilon,M]\). The positive spectral mass is \(m-b\). Therefore
\[
0\le
\int\log\lambda\,d\mu_H
\le
\alpha\log\varepsilon+(m-b-\alpha)\log\widehat M,
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
\alpha\le
\frac{(m-b)\log\widehat M}{\log(\widehat M/\varepsilon)}

\frac{m\log\widehat M}{\log(\widehat M/\varepsilon)}.

\tag{4}

\[

## Categorical distinctions

**Full vs reduced.** The endpoint is constructed in the reduced crossed product/reduced group algebra. No map between reduced algebras of different marked groups is asserted.

**Norm vs strong.** An \(\varepsilon\)-projection/unitary is defined by operator-norm inequalities. Strong/von Neumann convergence is not substituted.

**Finite matrices vs von Neumann projections.** The controlled endpoint is a finite matrix with finite group-word propagation, stronger information than existence of a von Neumann projection. Exact correction may lose finite propagation.

**Scalar vs coefficient Baum–Connes.** This run specializes to trivial coefficients. Controlled assembly exists without assuming Baum–Connes surjectivity. Stronger controlled-isomorphism theorems require additional hypotheses and are not claimed.

**Stable vs unstable.** Matrix size may change under stabilization; propagation control is independent of matrix size. No fixed unstable dimension is asserted.

**Exact vs quantitative representatives.** This distinction is essential. Approximate finite-propagation representatives are what controlled K-theory preserves.

## Bottom line

The fixed-matrix low-energy-cloud route on determinant-class stages is closed by the newest P01 theorem. The P01-side controlled K-theory bottleneck is therefore the moving assembly class.

Primary quantitative K-theory already supplies the crucial endpoint theorem:
\[
\boxed{
K_*^\Gamma(P_d\Gamma)
\longrightarrow
K_*^{\varepsilon,\kappa(\varepsilon)d}(C_r^*\Gamma),
}
\]
with ordinary comparison class equal to the finite-scale Baum–Connes assembly class.

So every finite-scale assembly class has a finite-support approximate projection/unitary endpoint, with propagation bounded independently of the class and independently of matrix size.

The exact finite-support projection version is too strong: exact spectral correction can create infinite group support even from a finite-support uniformly gapped matrix.

The correct next target is therefore not endpoint existence. It is to transport controlled assembly representatives and their controlled homotopies across the marked family before their propagation budget reaches the marked relation radius.
