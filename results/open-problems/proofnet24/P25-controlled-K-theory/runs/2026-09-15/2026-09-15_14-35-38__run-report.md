The remaining logarithm comes from applying an `O(S^3)` Ricard–Xu base constant at a powered radius `S=Theta(L)` and taking a high root.

P18's trace-preserving Fejér free-product channel is much more structured. On a reduced word

`g = a^{k_1} b^{l_1} a^{k_2} ...`,

it acts by

`lambda_g -> w_L(g) lambda_{q_L(g)}`,

where

`w_L(g)=product_i (1-|k_i|/L)`.

For `|g|<=R<cL`, pointwise one has a lower bound on `w_L(g)`, but that does not control the cb norm of the inverse multiplier.

The next theorem should attack the exact operator-space question:

> Is there an absolute C such that, for every fixed `c<1/2`, the inverse attenuation multiplier on the radius-R free-product word space satisfies
> `||M_{w_L^{-1}}||_cb <= exp(CR/L)` uniformly for `R<=cL`?

A positive answer would combine with the ucp Fejér channel to give two-sided near-isometry throughout `R=o(L)`, much closer to the linear relation barriers. A negative answer with an explicit cb lower-bound example would certify that the logarithmic P40 window reflects a real operator-space obstruction rather than only a proof artifact.

Promising tools to check first:

1. Herz–Schur multiplier norms for free-product/tree lengths;
2. Ricard–Xu's Poisson semigroup and truncated multipliers;
3. Hankel-matrix formulas for free-group radial multipliers, while noting that `w_L` depends on `a`-syllable lengths rather than only total word length;
4. free-product Markov dilation / generator Bernstein inequalities on finite spectral subspaces.

Do **not** infer a cb inverse from pointwise control of `w_L(g)^{-1}`.

## Bottom line

P01 proved the forward nonamenable upper-norm gate in the window `R log L=o(L)`. P18 proved a two-sided Fejér gate, but only in a logarithmic radius window because coefficientwise correction pays the ball size.

This run combines P25's sharp cyclic matrix norming with Ricard–Xu and the C*-power trick to prove the missing reverse estimate at the P01 scale:

`||J_{L,R}||_cb ->1` and `||J_{L,R}^{-1}||_cb ->1` whenever `R log L/L ->0`.

The resulting two-sided quantitative-K transfer yields a new genuinely nonamenable relation-escape theorem. Although the stage generator `a` is ordinary-K1 trivial, any fixed-defect controlled nullhomotopy satisfies

`liminf (R_L log L)/L >= (1/12) log(1/delta)`,

and a standard quantitative-K1 zero relation satisfies

`liminf (r_L log L)/L >= (1/24) log(1/(3epsilon))`.

So the free-product family now certifies the exact phenomenon P01 needed to isolate:

**matrix-stable local norm convergence can be excellent while K-relations still escape.**

The next analytic gate is no longer basic upper norm control; it is whether the `L/log L` window can be enlarged to `o(L)` by controlling the inverse Fejér attenuation multiplier in cb norm.
