# 2. Local coefficient transport

Fix a propagation radius

\[
r>0.
\]

Assume the relation-agreement window is large enough that the marked balls through radius \(4r\) are canonically identified.

Write

\[
\iota:B_{4r}(H)\longrightarrow B_{4r}(G)
\]

for the induced marked-ball bijection.

For a finite-propagation matrix

\[
x=
\sum_{h\in B_r(H)} A_h\lambda_H(h)
\in
M_m((C_r^*H)_r),
\]

define its coefficient transport by

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_r(x)
=
\sum_{h\in B_r(H)}
A_h\lambda_G(\iota(h)).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because the group basis in the reduced group algebra is linearly independent, this is well-defined.

Because marked multiplication agrees locally,

\[
\Theta_{2r}(xy)
=
\Theta_r(x)\Theta_r(y)
\]

whenever \(x,y\) both have propagation at most \(r\).

Also

\[
\Theta_r(x^*)=\Theta_r(x)^*.
\]

For continuous paths in the finite-dimensional propagation-\(r\) subspace, coefficient transport is continuous coefficientwise.

This is only a local linear / multiplicative transport on bounded-propagation pieces.

It is **not** a global \(C^*\)-homomorphism.

# 3. Quantitative K-theory conventions

For a unital filtered \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\), write

\[
K_*^{\varepsilon,r}(A)
\]

for Oyono-Oyono–Yu quantitative K-theory.

An \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projection is a self-adjoint element \(p\in A_r\) with

\[
\|p^2-p\|<\varepsilon.
\]

An \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) unitary is \(u\in A_r\) with

\[
\|u^*u-1\|<\varepsilon,
\qquad
\|uu^*-1\|<\varepsilon.
\]

The exact equivalence relations are important.

For \(K_0^{\varepsilon,r}\), stable equivalence is given by a homotopy of \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projections.

For \(K_1^{\varepsilon,r}\), two representatives are equivalent when they are joined, after stabilization as necessary, by a homotopy of

\[
3\varepsilon\text{-}2r
\]

unitaries.

This \(3\varepsilon,2r\) relaxation is built into the definition.

That exact feature is what makes a direct marked-transfer theorem possible without discretizing a homotopy into a mesh.

# 5. Main theorem

## `P25-MARKED-QUANT-K-INTERLEAVING-14` — PROVED

Fix

\[
\beta>0,
\qquad
\alpha=1+\beta,
\]

and

\[
0<\varepsilon<\frac1{4\alpha}.
\]

Assume:

1. \(G,H\in\mathcal{UE}(k)\);
2. their marked relations agree through radius \(L\);
3. the quantitative support budget `(4.2)` holds.

Then coefficient transport induces well-defined group homomorphisms

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak T^{\varepsilon,r}_{H\to G,*}:
K_*^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*H)
\longrightarrow
K_*^{\alpha\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*G).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The map is represented simply by coefficient transport:

\[
[p,\ell]_{\varepsilon,r}
\longmapsto
[\Theta_r(p),\ell]_{\alpha\varepsilon,r},
\]

and

\[
[u]_{\varepsilon,r}
\longmapsto
[\Theta_r(u)]_{\alpha\varepsilon,r}.
\]

The same theorem holds with \(G,H\) interchanged.

### K0 proof

Let \(p\) be an \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projection over \(H\).

Its defect

\[
p^2-p
\]

has propagation at most \(2r\) and group support contained in \(B_{2r}\).

Local multiplication gives

\[
\Theta_r(p)^2-\Theta_r(p)
=
\Theta_{2r}(p^2-p).
\]

P20A gives

\[
\|\Theta_r(p)^2-\Theta_r(p)\|
\le
\alpha\|p^2-p\|
<
\alpha\varepsilon.
\]

Self-adjointness is preserved.

Hence \(\Theta_r(p)\) is an \(\alpha\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projection.

Now suppose

\[
(p,\ell)\sim(q,\ell')
\]

in \(K_0^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*H)\).

By definition, after stabilization there is a continuous path

\[
h(t)
\]

of \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projections joining the stabilized endpoints.

The propagation-\(r\) subspace is finite-dimensional, so \(h(t)\) has continuous coefficient functions.

Transport it coefficientwise:

\[
\widetilde h(t)
=
\Theta_r(h(t)).
\]

For every \(t\),

\[
\|\widetilde h(t)^2-\widetilde h(t)\|
<
\alpha\varepsilon.
\]

Therefore \(\widetilde h(t)\) is an \(\alpha\varepsilon\)-\(r\) projection homotopy.

Thus the K0 equivalence relation is preserved.

Block sums and stabilization are preserved coefficientwise, so `(5.1)` is a group homomorphism in degree zero.

### K1 proof

Let \(u\) be an \(\varepsilon\)-\(r\) unitary over \(H\).

The two defects

\[
u^*u-1,
\qquad
uu^*-1
\]

have propagation at most \(2r\).

Exactly as above,

\[
\|\Theta_r(u)^*\Theta_r(u)-1\|
<
\alpha\varepsilon,
\]

\[
\|\Theta_r(u)\Theta_r(u)^*-1\|
<
\alpha\varepsilon.
\]

Thus \(\Theta_r(u)\) is an \(\alpha\varepsilon\)-\(r\) unitary.

Now suppose

\[
u\sim v
\]

in \(K_1^{\varepsilon,r}(C_r^*H)\).

By the exact quantitative-K definition, there is a continuous path

\[
h(t)
\]

of

\[
3\varepsilon\text{-}2r
\]

unitaries connecting the stabilized representatives.

The defects of \(h(t)\) have propagation at most \(4r\).

By `(4.2)` and P20A,

\[
\|\Theta_{2r}(h(t))^*\Theta_{2r}(h(t))-1\|
<
3\alpha\varepsilon,
\]

and likewise for the other defect.

Hence the transported path is a homotopy of

\[
3\alpha\varepsilon\text{-}2r
\]

unitaries.

But this is **exactly** the equivalence relation defining

\[
K_1^{\alpha\varepsilon,r}.
\]

Therefore the degree-one map is well-defined.

QED.
