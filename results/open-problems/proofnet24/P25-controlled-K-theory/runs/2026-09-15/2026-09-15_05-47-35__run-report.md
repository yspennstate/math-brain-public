# 3. Proof of the isomorphism-naturality theorem

The proof is transport of structure through every ingredient of Dell'Aiera's definition.

## 3.1 Rips spaces

Push a finite probability measure forward by \(\Phi\):

\[
\Phi_P:
P_E(\mathcal G)
\longrightarrow
P_{\Phi(E)}(\mathcal H),
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_g\lambda_g\delta_g
\longmapsto
\sum_g\lambda_g\delta_{\Phi(g)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because \(\Phi\) preserves source, range, multiplication and inverse,

\[
g'g^{-1}\in E
\iff
\Phi(g')\Phi(g)^{-1}\in\Phi(E).
\]

Thus \(\Phi_P\) is an equivariant homeomorphism.

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\Phi(g)}(\Phi_P\eta)
=
\lambda_g(\eta).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 3.2 Cutoff projection

Using `(3.2)` in Dell'Aiera's explicit formula `(1.1)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_*(L_E)
=
L_{\Phi(E)}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

**exactly**, not merely in K-theory.

This is the key finite-propagation point.

The support of the cutoff is carried from \(E\) to \(\Phi(E)\) without enlargement.

## 3.3 Reduced crossed products and filtrations

On compactly supported sections, transport along \(\Phi\) gives a \(*\)-algebra isomorphism

\[
C_c(\mathcal G,B)
\stackrel{\cong}{\longrightarrow}
C_c(\mathcal H,\Phi_*B).
\]

The regular representations correspond under the unit-space bijection, hence this extends to a reduced crossed-product \(*\)-isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{B\rtimes r}:
B\rtimes_r\mathcal G
\stackrel{\cong}{\longrightarrow}
(\Phi_*B)\rtimes_r\mathcal H.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If a section is supported in \(E\), its image is supported in \(\Phi(E)\).

Therefore `(3.4)` is exactly filtration preserving after relabelling the control sets.

## 3.4 Equivariant KK transport

A \(\mathcal G\)-equivariant Kasparov cycle transports to an \(\mathcal H\)-equivariant cycle by relabelling the groupoid action through \(\Phi\).

This gives the canonical equivalence

\[
KK_*^{\mathcal G}(A,B)
\cong
KK_*^{\mathcal H}(\Phi_*A,\Phi_*B).
\]

The construction is exact at the cycle level.

## 3.5 Controlled Kasparov transform

Dell'Aiera's controlled transform is built from:

1. reduced descent of the equivariant cycle;
2. a filtered extension and its controlled boundary map in the odd case;
3. suspension/Bott reduction in the even case;
4. the propagation-preserving matrix-stabilization Morita map.

Under `(3.4)`, the descended cycles and filtered extensions are isomorphic term by term.

Controlled boundary maps are natural for morphisms of filtered extensions.

Dell'Aiera's Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 explicitly record naturality of the controlled Kasparov transform with respect to equivariant \(*\)-morphisms in the source and target coefficient algebras.

After identifying \(\mathcal H\) with \(\mathcal G\) through \(\Phi\), the groupoid-isomorphism case is exactly transport of these constructions.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{B\rtimes r,*}
\circ
J_{\mathcal G}(z)
=
J_{\mathcal H}(\Phi_*z)
\circ
\Phi_{A\rtimes r,*}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

The universal control pair remains unchanged.

## 3.6 Assembly

Dell'Aiera's controlled assembly is

\[
J_{\mathcal G}(z)
\]

evaluated on

\[
[L_E].
\]

Combine `(3.3)` and `(3.5)`.

This gives `(2.1)`.

QED.

# 6. Counterexample: no bounded word-length selector for the full open star

## `P25-FULL-OPEN-WORD-SELECTOR-CE-15` — PROVED

Let

\[
G=\mathbb Z
\]

with its standard word metric, acting by translations on

\[
X=P_1(\mathbb Z),
\]

the geometric realization of the nearest-neighbour Rips graph.

Let

\[
U=\operatorname{st}(0)
\]

be the open star of the identity vertex.

Then \(U\) is full: every \(\mathbb Z\)-orbit in \(X\) meets \(U\).

Nevertheless there is **no finite constant \(S\)** such that every unit \(x\in X\) can be carried into \(U\) by a transformation-groupoid arrow whose group label has word length at most \(S\).

### Proof

Consider the vertex

\[
x=n\in\mathbb Z\subset X.
\]

An arrow in

\[
\mathbb Z\ltimes X
\]

with source \(n\) and label \(g\in\mathbb Z\) has range

\[
g+n.
\]

Since the only vertex of the 0-skeleton lying in the open star of the vertex \(0\) is \(0\) itself, an arrow from the vertex \(n\) into \(U\) must satisfy

\[
g+n=0.
\]

Thus

\[
g=-n
\]

and

\[
|g|=|n|.
\]

As \(n\to\infty\), the required arrow length diverges.

QED.

# Bottom line

The preceding P25 run isolated controlled assembly naturality as the last categorical bridge in fixed-scale marked K-theory transfer.

This run resolves half of that bridge and kills one tempting shortcut.

For an actual control-preserving étale groupoid isomorphism,

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_*\mu_{\mathcal G}^{\varepsilon,E,F}
=
\mu_{\mathcal H}^{\varepsilon,\Phi E,\Phi F}\Phi_*
}
\]

exactly.

Therefore P18's local open-star reduction isomorphism exactly intertwines the controlled assembly maps of the two local reduced groupoids.

However, fullness alone does not give a uniformly word-controlled Morita return map. For

\[
\mathbb Z\ltimes P_1(\mathbb Z),
\]

the vertex \(n\) can be returned to the identity open star only by an arrow of word length \(|n|\).

So the remaining theorem cannot be a generic bounded-Morita statement.

It must exploit the **specific cutoff-plus-descent structure of the Baum–Connes assembly map**.

That is now the precise highest-value P25 bottleneck.
