# 11. Categorical distinctions

## Full versus reduced C*-algebras

For finite cyclic groups,

\[
C^*(C_L)=C_r^*(C_L)
\]

because the group is amenable. The coefficient-lift argument exploits this special Fourier model.

Nothing in this run produces a quotient-induced map between reduced C*-algebras of arbitrary nonamenable marked groups.

## Norm versus strong convergence

All target persistence and null-relation estimates are operator-norm statements about quantitative unitaries. Strong or von Neumann convergence is not substituted.

## Finite matrices versus von Neumann projections

All target representatives and all controlled homotopies are finite matrices in filtered reduced group algebras. No von Neumann projection is promoted to an ordinary K-class.

## Scalar versus coefficient Baum–Connes

Everything is coefficient-free. The quantitative assembly maps used above exist as constructions; no Baum–Connes surjectivity or coefficient theorem is assumed.

## Stable versus unstable K-theory

The lower relation obstruction is stable: matrix size may increase under block sum and stabilization. The propagation lower bound is therefore not an artifact of fixed unstable matrix dimension.

## Representative versus relation complexity

This is the central distinction. The assembly endpoint is born at fixed propagation, but its source torsion relations and target null-relations require linear control in `L`.
