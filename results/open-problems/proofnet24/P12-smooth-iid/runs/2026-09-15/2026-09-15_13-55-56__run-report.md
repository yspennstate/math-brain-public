# 6. New theorem: tunable sparse-kernel avoidance under planar coordinate \(L^2\)

Let
\[
C\in\mathbb C^{N\times n}
\]
have iid entries \(\xi\) satisfying
\[
E|\xi|^2=1,
\qquad
\|f_\xi\|_{L^2(\mathbb C)}\le K_2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

No centering is required in this theorem.

Assume
\[
N\ge c_*n
\tag{6.2}
\]
for a fixed \(c_*>0\).

Let \(q_n\) be an integer sequence such that
\[
\boxed{
q_n\log n=o(n).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a coordinate set \(R\subset[n]\), write
\[
S_R=\operatorname{span}\{e_j:j\in R\}.
\]

## `P12-COMPLEX-TUNABLE-SPARSEKERNEL-L2-22` — NEW / PROVED

For every prescribed
\[
A>0
\]
there are fixed constants
\[
L=L(A,c_*)>1,
\qquad
a=a(A,K_2,c_*)>0
\]
such that, with
\[
\boxed{
\eta_n=\frac{a}{8Ln},
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
one has for all sufficiently large \(n\)
\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\exists x\in\ker C,\ \|x\|_2=1,\ 
\exists R\subset[n],\ |R|\le q_n:
\operatorname{dist}(x,S_R)\le\eta_n
\right\}
\le e^{-An}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The constants are uniform over every triangular family satisfying `(6.1)` with the same \(K_2\).

### Proof

Put
\[
\theta_n=\frac a{\sqrt n}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

## Step 1 — tunable fixed-vector small ball

Fix
\[
y\in S_{\mathbb C}^{n-1}.
\]

Choose \(j\) with
\[
|y_j|\ge n^{-1/2}.
\]

Condition on all coordinates of one fresh iid row \(R\) except \(\xi_j\).

The conditional law of
\[
\langle R,y\rangle
\]
is a translate of \(y_j\xi_j\).

Planar scaling gives
\[
\|f_{y_j\xi_j}\|_2
=
|y_j|^{-1}\|f_\xi\|_2
\le
K_2\sqrt n.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on a disk of radius \(\theta_n\) yields, uniformly in the center \(z\),
\[
\boxed{
P\{|\langle R,y\rangle-z|\le\theta_n\}
\le
\sqrt\pi K_2a
=:
q_0.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

By taking \(a\) sufficiently small, \(q_0\) may be made arbitrarily small.

## Step 2 — majority row-norm event

Since
\[
E\|R\|_2^2=n,
\]
Markov gives
\[
P\{\|R\|_2>L\sqrt n\}\le L^{-2}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For \(N\) independent rows,
\[
P\{\text{at least }N/2\text{ rows violate `(6.8)`}\}
\le
2^N L^{-N}
=
(2/L)^N.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Choose \(L\) large enough that `(6.9)` is
\[
\le e^{-(A+3)n}.
\]

## Step 3 — sparse nets

Set
\[
\delta_n
=
\frac{\theta_n}{2L\sqrt n}
=
\frac a{2Ln},
\qquad
\eta_n=\delta_n/4.
\tag{6.10}
\]

For every support \(R\) of cardinality \(\ell\), a \(\delta_n\)-net of the complex unit sphere in \(S_R\) has cardinality at most
\[
(C/\delta_n)^{2\ell}.
\]

The union over all supports \(\ell\le q_n\) therefore has cardinality
\[
\mathcal N_n
\le
\sum_{\ell\le q_n}
\binom n\ell
(C/\delta_n)^{2\ell}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

By `(6.3)` and
\[
\log(1/\delta_n)=O(\log n),
\]
\[
\boxed{
\log\mathcal N_n=o(n).
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

## Step 4 — kernel vector forces one net point to be small on a majority of rows

Suppose
\[
x\in\ker C,\qquad \|x\|_2=1,
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{dist}(x,S_R)\le\eta_n
\]
for some \(|R|\le q_n\).

Normalize the coordinate projection:
\[
z=\frac{x_R}{\|x_R\|_2}.
\]

For large \(n\),
\[
\|x-z\|_2
\le
2\eta_n.
\]

Choose a net point \(y\in S_R\) with
\[
\|z-y\|_2\le\delta_n.
\]

Then
\[
\|x-y\|_2
\le
\frac32\delta_n.
\tag{6.13}
\]

For every row satisfying
\[
\|R_i\|_2\le L\sqrt n,
\]
using
\[
\langle R_i,x\rangle=0,
\]
we obtain
\[
|\langle R_i,y\rangle|
\le
L\sqrt n\|x-y\|_2
\le
\frac34\theta_n.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Thus, unless the row-norm failure `(6.9)` occurs, at least \(N/2\) independent rows satisfy a disk small-ball event for one fixed net point.

For one fixed \(y\), `(6.7)` and independence imply
\[
P\{\text{at least }N/2\text{ rows satisfy `(6.14)`}\}
\le
2^Nq_0^{N/2}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

Choose \(a\) so small that, using \(N\ge c_*n\),
\[
2^Nq_0^{N/2}
\le
 e^{-(A+3)n}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

Union over the net `(6.11)`.  The factor is \(e^{o(n)}\) by `(6.12)`.

Combining with `(6.9)` proves `(6.5)`.

QED.

# 8. New deterministic theorem: two disjoint coordinate bases with polynomial volume

\ge

\ge

\tag{8.2}

### Proof

\[
\sum_{|S|=k}|\det A_S|^2
=

# 9. Self-contained two-basis \(L^2\) projection density theorem

The theorem was inherited from earlier P12 work, but it is rederived here because it is the analytic core of the new lower extreme.

## `P12-COMPLEX-TWOBASIS-L2-22` — PROVED / REPROVED

Let
\[
X=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\in\mathbb C^n
\]
have independent coordinate densities satisfying
\[
\|f_{\xi_j}\|_{L^2(\mathbb C)}\le K_2.
\]

Let
\[
L:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^k
\]
have full row rank.

Suppose there are disjoint \(k\)-sets \(S,T\) such that
\[
L_S,\qquad L_T
\]
are invertible.

Then
\[
\boxed{
\|f_{LX}\|_\infty
\le
\frac{
K_2^{2k}
}{
|\det_{\mathbb C}L_S|
|\det_{\mathbb C}L_T|
}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
\phi_j(w)=Ee^{i\langle w,\xi_j\rangle_{\mathbb R^2}}.
\]

The characteristic function of \(LX\) is
\[
\Phi(t)
=
\prod_j\phi_j(l_j^*t),
\qquad
t\in\mathbb C^k\simeq\mathbb R^{2k}.
\]

Since
\[
|\phi_j|\le1,
\]
\[
|\Phi(t)|
\le
\left[
\prod_{j\in S}|\phi_j(l_j^*t)|^2
\right]^{1/2}
\left[
\prod_{j\in T}|\phi_j(l_j^*t)|^2
\right]^{1/2}.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\int|\Phi|
\le
(I_SI_T)^{1/2},
\]
where
\[
I_S
=
\int_{\mathbb R^{2k}}
\prod_{j\in S}
|\phi_j(l_j^*t)|^2dt.
\]

The complex-linear change of variables
\[
y=L_S^*t
\]
has real Jacobian
\[
|\det_{\mathbb C}L_S|^2.
\]

Planar Plancherel gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\phi_j(y)|^2dy
=
(2\pi)^2\|f_{\xi_j}\|_2^2
\le
4\pi^2K_2^2.
\]

Hence
\[
I_S
\le
|\det L_S|^{-2}
(4\pi^2K_2^2)^k,
\]
and similarly for \(T\).

Thus
\[
\int|\Phi|
\le
\frac{
(4\pi^2K_2^2)^k
}{
|\det L_S||\det L_T|
}.
\]

Fourier inversion in \(2k\) real dimensions contributes
\[
(2\pi)^{-2k}
=
(4\pi^2)^{-k}.
\]

This proves `(9.1)`.

QED.
