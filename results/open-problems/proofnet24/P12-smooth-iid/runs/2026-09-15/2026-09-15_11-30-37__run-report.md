# 5. New analytic lemma: planar \(L^2\) clean laws are uniformly elliptic after a fixed cutoff

Let \(\eta\in\mathbb C\simeq\mathbb R^2\) be centered.

Assume:

- \(|\eta|\le2T\) almost surely;
- \(\eta\) has planar density \(g\);
- \(\|g\|_2\le K_c\).

## `P12-PLANAR-L2-ELLIPTIC-19` — NEW / PROVED

For every real unit direction
\[
\theta\in S^1,
\]
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\theta\cdot\eta)
\ge
\kappa(T,K_c)
:=
\frac1{2048\,T^2K_c^4}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Also
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\theta\cdot\eta)
\le4T^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence the real \(2\times2\) covariance matrix satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\kappa I_2
\preceq
\Sigma_\eta
\preceq
4T^2I_2.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

For \(r>0\), the strip
\[
\{z\in B(0,2T):|\theta\cdot z|\le r\}
\]
has area at most
\[
8Tr.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
P\{|\theta\cdot\eta|\le r\}
\le
K_c\sqrt{8Tr}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Choose
\[
r_0
=
\frac1{32TK_c^2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Then the right side of `(5.4)` is \(1/2\).

Therefore
\[
P\{|\theta\cdot\eta|>r_0\}\ge\frac12,
\]
and since \(\eta\) is centered,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\theta\cdot\eta)
=
E(\theta\cdot\eta)^2
\ge
\frac{r_0^2}{2}
=
\frac1{2048T^2K_c^4}.
\]

The upper bound follows from
\[
|\theta\cdot\eta|\le|\eta|\le2T.
\]

QED.

# 6. New analytic theorem: a self-contained planar triangular density local CLT

The real P13 \(L^2\)-clean-core theorem uses a one-dimensional density Berry–Esseen theorem after pairing.

For complex entries we need a genuine two-dimensional version that permits non-identical covariance matrices.

## `P12-PLANAR-TRIANG-LOCALCLT-19` — NEW / PROVED

Let
\[
Y_1,\ldots,Y_q\in\mathbb R^2
\]
be independent centered random vectors.

Assume fixed constants
\[
R,M,\lambda,\Lambda>0
\]
such that for every \(\ell\):

\[
|Y_\ell|\le R
\quad\text{a.s.},
\tag{6.1}
\]
\[
\|f_{Y_\ell}\|_\infty\le M,
\tag{6.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\lambda I_2
\preceq
\Sigma_\ell
:=
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_\ell)
\preceq
\Lambda I_2.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Put
\[
Z_q
=
q^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell=1}^qY_\ell,
\]
\[
\Gamma_q
=
q^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^q\Sigma_\ell.
\]

Then \(Z_q\) has a continuous density \(p_q\), and for a constant
\[
C=C(R,M,\lambda,\Lambda)
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{z\in\mathbb R^2}
|p_q(z)-\varphi_{\Gamma_q}(z)|
\le
\frac C{\sqrt q}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Here \(\varphi_{\Gamma_q}\) is the centered Gaussian density with covariance \(\Gamma_q\).

### Primary-source inputs

This proof uses two explicitly verified facts from Bobkov–Götze, *Berry–Esseen bounds in the local limit theorems*, arXiv:2407.20744:

1. Proposition 3.1: for a one-dimensional random variable with standard deviation \(\sigma\) and density maximum \(M_1\),
   \[
   |\phi(t)|
   \le
   1-
   \frac{c}{M_1^2\sigma^2}
   \min\{\sigma^2t^2,1\};
   \tag{6.5}
   \]
2. Proposition 5.3: for a \(d\)-dimensional random vector with bounded density maximum \(M(X)\),
   \[
   \frac1{(2\pi)^d}
   \int|\phi(t)|^{2m}\,dt
   \le
   \left(\frac e{2m}\right)^{d/2}
   M(X).
   \tag{6.6}
   \]

The rest of the proof is included here.

### Step 1 — uniform characteristic-function separation

Let
\[
\psi_\ell(t)=Ee^{it\cdot Y_\ell}.
\]

For a unit vector
\[
\theta\in S^1,
\]
the scalar projection
\[
\theta\cdot Y_\ell
\]
has a one-dimensional density bounded by
\[
2RM,
\tag{6.7}
\]
because every line section of the support disk \(B(0,R)\) has length at most \(2R\).

Its variance lies in
\[
[\lambda,\Lambda].
\]

Apply `(6.5)` to the scalar projection in direction
\[
\theta=t/|t|.
\]

There is a fixed
\[
c_0=c_0(R,M,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
|\psi_\ell(t)|
\le
\exp\{-c_0\min(|t|^2,1)\}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

### Step 2 — low-frequency Gaussian comparison

For
\[
|s|\le\delta
\]
with fixed small \(\delta=\delta(R,\Lambda)\), bounded support gives the Taylor expansion
\[
\psi_\ell(s)
=
1-\frac12s^\top\Sigma_\ell s
+
O_R(|s|^3).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Also
\[
g_\ell(s)
:=
e^{-\frac12s^\top\Sigma_\ell s}
=
1-\frac12s^\top\Sigma_\ell s
+
O_\Lambda(|s|^4).
\]

Hence
\[
|\psi_\ell(s)-g_\ell(s)|
\le
C|s|^3.
\tag{6.10}
\]

For
\[
|t|\le\delta\sqrt q,
\qquad
s=t/\sqrt q,
\]
telescoping the products
\[
\Psi_q(t)
=
\prod_{\ell=1}^q
\psi_\ell(t/\sqrt q)
\]
and
\[
e^{-\frac12t^\top\Gamma_qt}
=
\prod_{\ell=1}^q
g_\ell(t/\sqrt q),
\]
while using `(6.8)` and `(6.3)` on the remaining \(q-1\) factors, gives
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\Psi_q(t)
-
e^{-\frac12t^\top\Gamma_qt}
\right|
\le
\frac C{\sqrt q}
|t|^3e^{-c|t|^2}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore
\[
\int_{|t|\le\delta\sqrt q}
\left|
\Psi_q(t)
-
e^{-\frac12t^\top\Gamma_qt}
\right|dt
\le
\frac C{\sqrt q}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

### Step 3 — far Fourier tail

For \(q\) large, split the indices into two groups
\[
G_1\sqcup G_2=[q]
\]
such that:

- \(|G_1|\ge q/3\);
- \(|G_2|\ge q/3\);
- \(|G_2|\) is even.

For
\[
|t|>\delta\sqrt q,
\]
every factor in \(G_1\) satisfies
\[
|\psi_\ell(t/\sqrt q)|
\le e^{-c_\delta}.
\]

Thus
\[
\prod_{\ell\in G_1}
|\psi_\ell(t/\sqrt q)|
\le
e^{-cq}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

It remains to integrate the \(G_2\) product.

Write
\[
|G_2|=2m_2.
\]

By scaling in two real dimensions,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\prod_{\ell\in G_2}
|\psi_\ell(t/\sqrt q)|dt
=
q
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\prod_{\ell\in G_2}
|\psi_\ell(u)|du.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Hölder with exponent \(|G_2|\), followed by `(6.6)` in dimension two, gives
\[
\int
|\psi_\ell(u)|^{|G_2|}du
\le
4\pi^2
\frac{eM}{|G_2|}.
\]

Hence
\[
\int
\prod_{\ell\in G_2}
|\psi_\ell(u)|du
\le
4\pi^2
\frac{eM}{|G_2|}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

Since
\[
|G_2|\asymp q,
\]
`(6.14)`–`(6.15)` give a uniform constant.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\int_{|t|>\delta\sqrt q}
|\Psi_q(t)|dt
\le
Ce^{-cq}.
}
\tag{6.16}
\]

The Gaussian tail is also
\[
O(e^{-cq})
\]
because
\[
\Gamma_q\succeq\lambda I.
\]

### Step 4 — Fourier inversion

Combining `(6.12)` and `(6.16)`,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\left|
\Psi_q(t)
-
e^{-\frac12t^\top\Gamma_qt}
\right|dt
\le
\frac C{\sqrt q}.
\]

In particular \(\Psi_q\in L^1\), so \(Z_q\) has a continuous density.

Fourier inversion in \(\mathbb R^2\) gives `(6.4)`.

QED.
