### Real
\[
\boxed{
\|f_{LY}\|_\infty
\le

}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Complex
\[
\boxed{

\le

\tag{5.2}

These are the \(q=1\) special cases of the previously proved low-leverage Brascamp--Lieb/Fourier theorems.

The point of the present section is to remove the leverage hypothesis completely.

## 5.2 Real induction

Let
\[
Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\]
have independent real coordinates, each with density at most \(K\), and let
\[
L\in\mathbb R^{k\times n},
\qquad
LL^\top=I_k.
\]

We prove by induction on \(k\) that
\[
\|f_{LY}\|_\infty
\le
(\sqrt{2e}\,K)^k.
\]

The case \(k=0\) is vacuous.

Assume the theorem for output rank \(k-1\).

If every
\[
\lambda_j=\|Le_j\|^2
\le\frac12,
\]
apply `(5.1)`.

Otherwise choose \(j\) with
\[
\lambda_j>\frac12.
\]

There is an orthogonal matrix \(U\in O(k)\) such that
\[
ULe_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,e_1.
\]

Write
\[
UL
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sqrt{\lambda_j} & *\\
0 & B
\end{bmatrix},
\tag{5.3}
\]
after putting column \(j\) first.

Because \(UL(UL)^\top=I_k\), the lower \(k-1\) rows are orthonormal.  Their \(j\)-th column is zero.  Hence
\[
BB^\top=I_{k-1}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Write
\[
ULY
=
\left(
\sqrt{\lambda_j}Y_j+c(Y_{-j}),
\,
BY_{-j}
\right).
\]

Condition on \(Y_{-j}\).  The first coordinate is a translate of
\[
\sqrt{\lambda_j}Y_j,
\]
whose conditional density is at most
\[
K/\sqrt{\lambda_j}
<
\sqrt2\,K.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The lower block \(BY_{-j}\) is independent of \(Y_j\) and, by the induction hypothesis,
\[
\|f_{BY_{-j}}\|_\infty
\le
(\sqrt{2e}\,K)^{k-1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore the joint density satisfies
\[
\|f_{ULY}\|_\infty
\le
\sqrt2\,K
(\sqrt{2e}\,K)^{k-1}
\le
(\sqrt{2e}\,K)^k.
\]

Orthogonal output rotation preserves the density maximum.  This proves:

## `P12-REAL-ARBITRARY-LEVERAGE-DENSITY-36` — NEW / PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\|f_{LY}\|_\infty
\le
(\sqrt{2e}\,K)^k
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
for every real coisometry \(L\), with no leverage restriction.

## 5.3 Complex induction

Now let

# 7. Nonasymptotic least-singular lower tail from scalar concentration

Let
\[

\qquad
k=r+1.
\]

Write its columns as
\[
C_1,\ldots,C_m.
\]

For each \(j\),
\[
H_j=\operatorname{span}\{C_\ell:\ell\ne j\}.
\]

The rectangular negative-second-moment identity implies that
\[
\sigma_m(B)\le\tau
\]
forces, for some \(j\),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)
\le
\sqrt m\,\tau.
\tag{7.1}
\]

If the frozen \(m-1\) columns have rank \(m-1\), then
\[
\dim H_j^\perp=k.
\]

If they are rank deficient, then
\[
\dim H_j^\perp>k;
\]
choose any frozen \(k\)-dimensional subspace
\[
E_j\subset H_j^\perp.
