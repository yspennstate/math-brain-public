# 5. Exact atom cap for every finite iid homogeneous linear form

Let `X` be any real or complex random variable. Let `p_x=P{X=x}` on its countable atom set, and define

\[
p_0=P\{X=0\},\qquad \kappa=P\{X'=X''\}=\sum_xp_x^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For iid copies `X_1,…,X_s` and a nonzero coefficient vector `c`, put `L_c=Σ c_jX_j`.

## `P12-FINITE-LINEAR-ATOM-CAP-32` — NEW / PROVED

For every finite `s`,

\[
\boxed{\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}P\{L_c=0\}\le\max\{p_0,\kappa\}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{\sup_{s\ge1}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}P\{L_c=0\}=\max\{p_0,\kappa\}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

If exactly one coefficient is nonzero, the probability is `p_0`.

If at least two coefficients are nonzero, condition on all copies except two. The remaining event is

\[
aX+bY=t,
\qquad a,b\ne0,
\]

with independent copies `X,Y`. For every target `t`,

\[
P\{aX+bY=t\}=\sum_xp_xp_{(t-ax)/b}.
\]

The affine map `x↦(t-ax)/b` is injective. By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\sum_xp_xp_{(t-ax)/b}
\le
\left(\sum_xp_x^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_xp_{(t-ax)/b}^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\kappa.
\]

Average over the conditioned copies. This proves `(5.2)`.

Sharpness is immediate: `s=1,c_1=1` gives `p_0`, while `s=2,c=(1,-1)/√2` gives `κ`. QED.

This is pure probability and linear algebra; no arithmetic inverse Littlewood--Offord input is used.

\[
E|X|^4\ge\frac{2}{1-\kappa}-3,

# 8. Uniform fixed-sparse anti-concentration below the threshold

Fix

\[
1<\gamma<2,\qquad B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),\qquad S<\infty.
\]

## `P12-FOURTH-FIXEDSPARSE-GAP-32` — NEW / PROVED

There exist `ρ=ρ(B,γ,S)>0` and `δ=δ(B,γ,S)>0` such that every centered variance-one real or complex law with `E|X|^4≤B` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{1\le s\le S}\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}
P\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^s c_jX_j\right|\le\rho\right\}
\le\frac1\gamma-\delta.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proof

Suppose not. Then for each `n` choose a law `X_n`, an integer `s_n≤S`, and a unit vector `c^{(n)}` with

\[
P\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{s_n}c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}\right|\le1/n\right\}>1/\gamma-1/n.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Pass to a subsequence with fixed `s`. Uniform second moments imply tightness; the uniform fourth-moment bound gives uniform integrability of first and second moments. Thus, after a subsequence,

\[
X_n\Rightarrow X,
\qquad EX=0,
\qquad E|X|^2=1,
\qquad E|X|^4\le B,
\]

where the last inequality is lower semicontinuity. Compactness of the coefficient sphere gives `c^{(n)}→c`, `||c||=1`, and product weak convergence yields

\[
L_n:=\sum c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}\Rightarrow L:=\sum c_jX_j.
\]

For fixed `η>0` and large `n`, `(8.2)` implies `P{|L_n|≤η}>1/γ-o(1)`. The closed-set Portmanteau inequality gives

\[
P\{|L|\le\eta\}\ge1/\gamma.
\]

Let `η↓0`; then

\[
P\{L=0\}\ge1/\gamma.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Section 5 gives `max(p_0,κ)≥1/γ`. At this boundary, the direct zero-atom and collision inequalities of Sections 6–7 imply

\[
E|X|^4\ge B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),
\]

contradicting `B<B_exact(γ)`. QED.

This theorem is nonvacuous only for `γ<2`, since `B_exact(γ)=1` for `γ≥2` and every variance-one law has fourth moment at least one.

It is deliberately only a **fixed-sparse, fixed-radius** theorem. It does not supply exponentially microscopic density control or growing-rank projection small balls.

# 9. Smooth bounded-fourth triangular counterexample at the exact-relation threshold

Fix `γ>1`, `B>B_exact(γ)`, and

\[
\delta_m=\delta_0e^{-m^2}
\]

with one sufficiently small fixed `δ_0>0`.

Let `Z` be a symmetric real `C_c^∞` variable supported in `[-1,1]` in the real field, or a circular `C_c^∞(C)` variable supported in the unit disk in the complex field. Let `Y` be a bounded centered variance-one discrete extremizer as below, independent of `Z`, and define

\[
\xi_m=\frac{Y+\delta_mZ}{\sqrt{1+\delta_m^2E|Z|^2}}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Then every `ξ_m` is exactly centered and variance one, has a `C^∞` density (genuinely planar in the complex field), and the family has uniform compact support, uniform subgaussianity and all absolute moments uniformly bounded. Since normalized fourth moments depend continuously on `δ` at zero, `δ_0` may be chosen so that

\[
\sup_mE|\xi_m|^4<B.
\tag{9.2}
\]

### Branch I: `1<γ<3/2`

Choose `p>1/γ` close enough to `1/γ` that `1/(1-p)<B`, and let

\[
Y=0\ \text{with probability }p,
\qquad
Y=\pm(1-p)^{-1/2}\ \text{with probabilities }(1-p)/2.
\]

On the latent event `Y=0`, `|ξ_m|≤Cδ_m`; this occurs independently in each row with probability `p>1/γ`. Since `(m+r_m)/N_m→1/γ`, Chernoff gives at least `m+r_m` such rows with probability `1-e^{-cm}`. Selecting them and testing the column vector `e_1` yields

\[
M_{m,r_m}\le C\sqrt{m+r_m}\,e^{-m^2}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

### Branch II: `γ≥3/2`

Choose a centered standardized two-point `Y` with collision probability `κ>1/γ` and `E|Y|^4<B`. On the latent event `Y=Y'`,

\[
\left|\frac{\xi_m-\xi_m'}{\sqrt2}\right|\le C\delta_m.
\]

The event has row probability `κ>1/γ`. Apply the inherited projective two-column obstruction with coefficient vector `(1,-1)/√2` to obtain the same bound `(9.3)`.

## `P12-SMOOTH-BOUNDED4-EXACTREL-CE-32` — NEW / PROVED

For every `γ>1`, `B>B_exact(γ)`, in both fields,

\[
\boxed{P\{M_{m,r_m}\le C\sqrt m\,e^{-m^2}\}\to1,}
\tag{9.4}
\]

with the analogous complex statement, while all the regularity/tail/moment properties above hold.

Hence

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}\le-k_mm+o(1)
\]

with probability tending to one.

This is a triangular-array counterexample. It does **not** refute the fixed-law smooth-iid conjecture, and it is claimed sharp only for exact finite-sparse relation mechanisms and their narrowing smooth approximations.

# 18. Bottom line

The August 30 question was whether subgaussianity could be weakened toward bounded fourth moment.

The positive answer remains stronger than bounded fourth moment: fixed finite-convolution smoothing + centering + variance normalization gives the complete real and complex source exponents with **no moment above two**.

The negative answer is now sharper. For triangular families, bounded fourth moment plus smooth densities and excellent tails cannot replace uniform microscopic smoothing. The exact infimal fourth-moment budget for every finite-column exact linear-relation mechanism is

\[
\boxed{
B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\gamma}{\gamma-1},&1<\gamma\le3/2,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1},&3/2\le\gamma<2,\\[7pt]
1,&\gamma\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]

For every budget strictly above this value there are smooth, uniformly compactly-supported/subgaussian real and complex triangular counterexamples with catastrophic least-singular decay. For budgets strictly below it, bounded fourth moment forces a fixed-scale anti-concentration gap for every fixed-sparse linear form.

Thus the remaining mathematical gap is

\[
\boxed{\text{fixed-sparse coarse anti-concentration}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\text{high-dimensional exponentially microscopic smoothing}.}
\]

That is the exact point where the current fixed finite-convolution sufficient theorem is still stronger than the sharp sparse obstruction theory.
