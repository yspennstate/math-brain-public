There exists
\[
a=a(A,K_2,\delta,C_H)>0
\]
such that, with
\[
\eta_m=\frac{a}{m},
\]
\[
\boxed{
P\{
\ker C\cap S(H,\eta_m)\ne\varnothing
\}
\le
e^{-Am}
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).

The estimate is uniform over any family of
\[
\exp\{O((\log m)^2)\}

### Step 1 — arbitrary unit linear forms have a tunable \(m^{-1/2}\)-scale upper bound

Let
\[
y\in S_{\mathbb C}^{m-1}.
\]

Choose \(j\) with
\[
|y_j|\ge m^{-1/2}.
\]

Condition on all coordinates of a fresh product row \(R\) except \(\xi_j\).

The conditional density of
\[
\langle R,y\rangle
\]
is a translate of \(y_j\xi_j\).

In two real dimensions,
\[
\|f_{y_j\xi_j}\|_2
=
|y_j|^{-1}\|f_{\xi_j}\|_2
\le
K_2\sqrt m.

\[
\theta_m=\frac a{\sqrt m},

\boxed{

P\{|\langle R,y\rangle-z|\le\theta_m\}
\le
\sqrt\pi K_2a.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Choose \(a\) so small that
\[
q_0:=\sqrt\pi K_2a
\]
has any desired small fixed value.

### Step 2 — row-norm truncation

Since
\[
E\|R\|_2^2=m,
\]
for fixed \(L>1\),
\[
P\{\|R\|_2>L\sqrt m\}
\le
L^{-2}.
\tag{8.3}

Choose \(L\) so large that the probability that at least half of the \(N\) rows violate the norm bound is at most
\[
e^{-2Am}.

\delta_m=\frac{\theta_m}{2L\sqrt m}

\frac a{2Lm},
\qquad
\eta_m=\frac{\delta_m}{4}.
\tag{8.4}

e^{o(m)}.
\tag{8.5}

\[

\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(x,H)\le\eta_m,

\frac34\theta_m.
\tag{8.6}

# 9. New deterministic lemma: sequential transversality makes the entire pair plane robust

Let
\[
u,v\in\mathbb C^m
\]
be unit vectors and put
\[
E=\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}.
\]

For a coordinate set \(R\), let
\[
S_R=\operatorname{span}\{e_j:j\in R\}.
\]

Assume for some
\[
\rho,\eta>0
\]
that for every \(|R|\le L\),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(u,S_R)\ge\rho,
\tag{9.1}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{dist}(v,\operatorname{span}(u)+S_R)\ge\eta.
\tag{9.2}
\]

## `P12-COMPLEX-SEQUENTIAL-PLANE-ROBUST-21` — NEW / PROVED

There is an absolute \(c>0\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\substack{w\in E\\\|w\|=1}}
\operatorname{dist}(w,S_R)
\ge
c\rho\eta
}
\tag{9.3}
\]
for every \(|R|\le L\).

Moreover
\[
\boxed{
1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2
\ge
\eta^2.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

### Proof

Write
\[
v=\alpha u+\sqrt\Delta\,z,
\qquad
z\perp u,\quad \|z\|=1,
\]
where
\[
\Delta=1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2.
\]

Taking \(R=\varnothing\) in `(9.2)` gives
\[
\sqrt\Delta
=
\operatorname{dist}(v,\operatorname{span}u)
\ge\eta,
\]
which proves `(9.4)`.

Also
\[
\operatorname{dist}(v,\operatorname{span}(u)+S_R)
=
\sqrt\Delta\,
\operatorname{dist}(z,\operatorname{span}(u)+S_R)
\ge\eta.
\]

Since \(\sqrt\Delta\le1\),
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,\operatorname{span}(u)+S_R)
\ge\eta.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Now let
\[
w=au+bz,
\qquad
|a|^2+|b|^2=1.
\]

Suppose
\[
\operatorname{dist}(w,S_R)<\tau.
\]

If
\[
|b|<\rho/4
\]
and
\[
\tau<\rho/8,
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{dist}(au,S_R)
\le
\tau+|b|
<
3\rho/8.
\]

Since \(|a|\) is bounded below by an absolute constant, this contradicts `(9.1)` after decreasing the numerical constants.

If
\[
|b|\ge\rho/4,
\]
then
\[
z=\frac{w-au}{b},
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,\operatorname{span}(u)+S_R)
\le
\frac{\tau}{|b|}
\le
\frac{4\tau}{\rho}.
\]

This contradicts `(9.5)` once
\[
\tau<c\rho\eta.
\]

Thus `(9.3)` holds.

QED.
