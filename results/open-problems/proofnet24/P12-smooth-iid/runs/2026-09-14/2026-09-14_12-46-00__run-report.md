**Main conclusion.** The real smooth-iid conjecture has a candidate complete proof under the quantitatively cleaner assumptions
\[
\mathbb E\xi_m=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=1,\qquad
\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,\qquad \mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B,
\]
uniformly in a triangular array, with the stronger moderate-surplus conclusion
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma),

\]
This run closes an omitted detail in the incompressible-direction local-density lemma: the diffuse component must have a density floor on an interval large enough to survive convolution with the large-coordinate component. The proof below supplies that interval explicitly.

Uniform fourth moment is a simple sufficient package for the latter two conditions.

## 1. Exact source statement: Conjecture 8.2

The source is the user's June 28, 2026 manuscript *Extreme least singular values with redundancy, radial ensembles, and quantitative stability of phase retrieval*, Section 8.2.

For an \(N_m\times m\) real matrix \(A_m\),
\[
M_{m,r}(A_m)
=
\min_{T\subset[N_m],\,|T|=m+r}\sigma_m((A_m)_T).
\]

**Conjecture 8.2 (Smooth iid universality).** Let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with mean zero, variance one, a bounded sufficiently smooth density, and sufficiently many finite moments. If
\[
N_m/m\to\gamma>1,
\]
then for every fixed integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1},
\qquad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]
The manuscript predicts in the complex iid case
\[
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{2(r+1)}.
\]

The same section identifies the intended mechanism: delocalize random hyperplane normals; prove a density-level local CLT valid at exponentially small slab widths; obtain a two-normal estimate with the angle determinant; then insert these into the second-moment criterion. It explicitly warns that an ordinary Berry--Esseen CDF error is much too large at scale \(e^{-\Theta(m)}\).

## 3. New fully explicit incompressible local-density lemma

### Lemma P12-LD-02

Let \(\xi\) be real, centered, variance one, with Lebesgue density bounded by \(K\) and
\[
\mathbb E|\xi|^3\le M_3.
\]
Fix incompressibility parameters \(\delta,\rho\in(0,1)\). There are constants
\[
m_0,c,C>0
\]
depending only on \(K,M_3,\delta,\rho\) such that, for every \(m\ge m_0\), every \((\delta,\rho)\)-incompressible \(u\in S^{m-1}\), and \(X=(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_m)\) with independent coordinates,
\[
c\le f_{\langle X,u\rangle}(x)\le C\qquad(|x|\le1),
\]
and consequently
\[
2ct\le
\Pr\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t\}
\le2Ct,\qquad 0<t\le1.
\]

Here incompressible means that \(u\) is at Euclidean distance \(>\rho\) from every unit vector supported on at most \(\delta m\) coordinates.

### Proof

Let
\[
J=\left\{j:|u_j|>\frac1{\sqrt{\delta m}}\right\},
\qquad D=J^c.
\]
Then \(|J|<\delta m\). Put
\[
v=\|u_D\|_2.
\]
If \(v<\rho/2\), normalize \(u_J\) to a unit vector \(z\) supported on \(J\). Since
\[
\|u-z\|_2^2
=
2\left(1-\sqrt{1-v^2}\right)
\le2v^2<\rho^2,
\]
this contradicts incompressibility. Therefore
\[
v\ge\rho/2.
\]

For \(j\in D\), let \(a_j=u_j/v\). Then
\[
\sum_{j\in D}a_j^2=1,
\qquad
\|a\|_\infty
\le\frac{2}{\rho\sqrt{\delta m}}.
\]
Set
\[
Y=\sum_{j\in D}a_j\xi_j,\qquad W=vY,
\qquad L=\sum_{j\in J}u_j\xi_j.
\]
Then
\[
\langle X,u\rangle=W+L,
\]
and \(W,L\) are independent.

Choose a large integer \(q=q(K,M_3,\delta,\rho)\). For sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\max_j a_j^2\le \frac1{4q}.
\]
Partition the nonzero weights \(a_j^2\) greedily into \(r\) blocks \(B_1,\dots,B_r\) so that, after merging the terminal remainder,
\[
\frac1q\le w_\ell:=\sum_{j\in B_\ell}a_j^2\le\frac9{4q},
\qquad
\frac{4q}{9}\le r\le q.
\]
Define
\[
Z_\ell=\sqrt r\sum_{j\in B_\ell}a_j\xi_j.
\]
Then the \(Z_\ell\) are independent and centered,
\[
\frac49\le\operatorname{Var}(Z_\ell)=rw_\ell\le\frac94,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^r\operatorname{Var}(Z_\ell)=r.
\]

For each block, after normalizing its coefficient vector, the sharp one-dimensional projection-density bound gives
\[
\|\operatorname{dens}(Z_\ell)\|_\infty\le C_1K.
\]
Rosenthal's inequality at \(p=3\), together with \(\mathbb E|\xi|^3\le M_3\) and \(rw_\ell\asymp1\), gives
\[
\mathbb E|Z_\ell|^3\le C_2(K,M_3)
\]
uniformly.

The Bobkov--Götze density Berry--Esseen theorem therefore gives
\[
\sup_x|f_Y(x)-\phi(x)|\le\frac{C_3}{\sqrt r}.
\]
We need a larger interval than \([-1,1]\), because the omitted coordinates will be convolved back in. Set
\[
R=\frac6\rho.
\]
Choose \(q\), hence \(r\), so large that
\[
\frac{C_3}{\sqrt r}
\le\frac12\min_{|x|\le R}\phi(x).
\]
Then for a constant \(c_0>0\),
\[
f_Y(x)\ge c_0
\qquad (|x|\le R).
\]

Since \(v\ge\rho/2\), for \(|x|\le3\),
\[
|x/v|\le6/\rho=R,
\]
and therefore
\[
f_W(x)=\frac1v f_Y(x/v)\ge c_0.
\]
Also
\[
\operatorname{Var}(L)=\sum_{j\in J}u_j^2\le1,
\]
so Chebyshev gives
\[
\Pr\{|L|\le2\}\ge\frac34.
\]
For every \(|x|\le1\),
\[
f_{W+L}(x)
=
\mathbb E[f_W(x-L)]
\ge
c_0\Pr\{|L|\le2\}
\ge\frac34c_0.
\]
This proves the uniform density floor.

The uniform upper density follows directly from the sharp one-dimensional marginal-density theorem for product measures:
\[
\|f_{\langle X,u\rangle}\|_\infty\le\sqrt2\,K.
\]
Integrating over \([-t,t]\) yields the claimed two-sided small-ball bound.

### Audit consequence

This proves the local-density ingredient used by P13 without subgaussianity. The finite-third-moment assumption is sufficient for this component; the previous report's informal "convolve back the large-coordinate part" is valid once the diffuse density floor is established on the enlarged interval \([-3,3]\), not merely at the origin.

## 4. Why finite third moment alone is not enough for the current proof

The preceding lemma might suggest replacing fourth moment everywhere by third moment. That shortcut is false for the untrimmed global-norm architecture.

Fix
\[
3<\alpha<4
\]
and take a symmetric bounded density with
\[
f(x)\asymp(1+|x|)^{-(\alpha+1)},
\]
rescaled to variance one. Then
\[
\mathbb E|\xi|^3<\infty,\qquad \mathbb E|\xi|^4=\infty.
\]
For \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\), \(N_m\sim\gamma m\), and any fixed \(M>0\),
\[
p_m:=\Pr\{|\xi|>M\sqrt m\}
\asymp M^{-\alpha}m^{-\alpha/2}.
\]
There are \(\asymp\gamma m^2\) entries, so
\[
N_m m\,p_m
\asymp
m^{2-\alpha/2}\to\infty.
\]
Hence
\[
\Pr\left\{\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}|\le M\sqrt m\right\}
\le
\exp(-c m^{2-\alpha/2})\to0.
\]
Since
\[
\|A_m\|_{\rm op}\ge\max_{i,j}|A_{ij}|,
\]
we get
\[
\frac{\|A_m\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt m}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\infty.
\]

Thus:

**P12-TAIL-01 (PROVED).** Bounded density + finite third moment does not imply the \(O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\) global norm bound used by the staged proof.

This is a **method obstruction**, not a counterexample to Conjecture 8.2 itself. A deletion/trimming proof may still work below fourth moment.

The clean modular package supported by the proof is therefore:

1. uniformly bounded coordinate density;
2. uniformly bounded third absolute moment, for the local density theorem;
3. rectangular operator-norm tightness
   \[
   \|A_m\|_{\rm op}/\sqrt m=O_{\mathbb P}(1).
   \]

Uniform fourth moment is a convenient sufficient hypothesis for (2) and (3), via Hölder and Latała's norm estimate.

## 5. Second-pass audit of the full real moderate-surplus proof

Let \(\xi_m\) be centered variance-one real laws satisfying
\[
\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,
\qquad
\mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B.
\]
Let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with law \(\xi_m\),
\[
N_m/m\to\gamma>1,
\qquad
k_m=r_m+1,
\qquad
k_m\log m=o(m).
\]

The proof decomposes into four inputs.

### (i) Lower estimate

For a fixed \((m+k-1)\times m\) matrix \(B\), the negative second-moment identity plus the \(k\)-dimensional bounded-density projection theorem gives
\[
\Pr\{\sigma_m(B)\le t\}
\le
\exp\{O(k\log m)\}t^k.
\]
Union over
\[
\binom{N_m}{m+k-1}
=
\exp\{h(\gamma)m+o(m)\}
\]
gives
\[
\Pr\left\{
M_{m,r_m}\le
e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k_m}
\right\}\to0.
\]
No tail assumption is used here.

### (ii) Many normals are incompressible

On
\[
E_M=\{\|A_{\rm base}\|_{\rm op}\le M\sqrt m\},
\]
a standard compressible-net argument gives, for each \((m-1)\times m\) row submatrix \(B\),
\[
\Pr\{\ker B\cap\operatorname{Comp}(\delta,\rho)\ne\varnothing,\ E_M\}
\le e^{-cm}.
\]
Averaging over all exponentially many row subsets and using Markov shows that, on \(E_M\), a positive fraction of the candidate hyperplanes have incompressible normals except with exponentially small probability.

### (iii) Nearly parallel low-overlap pairs are exponentially sparse

If \(S,T\) differ in \(q\ge\delta_0m\) rows and
\[
d_{\rm pr}(u_S,u_T)<a,
\]
then on \(E_M\) the \(q\) fresh rows satisfy
\[
\|B_Ju_S\|_2\le Ma\sqrt m.
\]
Therefore at least \(q/2\) fresh scalar projections are \(O(a)\). Uniform bounded-density anti-concentration and row independence yield
\[
\Pr\{d_{\rm pr}(u_S,u_T)<a,\ E_M\mid S\}
\le e^{-Am}
\]
after \(a\) is chosen sufficiently small. The high-overlap pairs are already exponentially sparse by deterministic entropy counting.

### (iv) Staged marked-row amplification

Choose a small reserve fraction, \(\beta<\gamma\), so close to \(\gamma\) that
\[
h(\beta)>h(\gamma)-\varepsilon.
\]
Set
\[
\lambda=h(\gamma)-\varepsilon,\qquad
t=e^{-\lambda m/k},\qquad
s=t/\sqrt k.
\]
Split the reserved rows into \(k\) fresh batches of size \(b\asymp m/k\).

At each successful stage,
\[
M_j\ge\theta sM_{j-1},
\qquad
e_j\le As e_{j-1}.
\]
Because
\[
\log(sM_j)
\ge
(h(\beta)-\lambda)m-\frac{k}{2}\log k-O(k)+o(m)\to+\infty
\]
and
\[
s^{-k}=e^{\lambda m}k^{k/2},
\]
the candidate family remains large and its exceptional-pair fraction remains negligible:
\[
\log\frac{e_j}{sM_j^2}
\le
-4\kappa m+\frac{k}{2}\log k+O(k)
=
-4\kappa m+o(m).
\]
Each batch fails with probability at most \(e^{-cb}\), so
\[
k e^{-cm/k}\to0
\]
because \(k\log m=o(m)\). At the end, the selected \(k\) rows obey
\[
\sum_{j=1}^k|\langle X_j,u\rangle|^2
\le
ks^2=t^2,
\]
producing an \((m+k-1)\)-row submatrix with least singular value at most \(t\).

Uniform fourth moment gives, by Latała,
\[
\|A_m\|_{\rm op}/\sqrt m=O_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
Thus, for any \(\eta>0\), choose \(M\) so that \(\limsup\Pr(E_M^c)\le\eta\), run the preceding proof on \(E_M\), then send \(\eta\downarrow0\).

**P12-MAIN-01 remains CANDIDATE COMPLETE:** under uniform bounded density and uniform fourth moment,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
-h(\gamma)
}
\]
for \(k_m\log m=o(m)\). For fixed \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1}.
}
\]

This run found no new structural gap after making P12-LD-02 explicit.

## 6. Exact variance-normalization statement

The usual "variance one is only normalization" claim needs a scale qualification for triangular arrays.

Let
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=\sigma_m^2>0,\qquad
\widetilde A_m=A_m/\sigma_m.
\]
Then exactly
\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
=
\sigma_m M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m),
\]
so
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
=
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)
+
\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\]

Therefore the exponent is unchanged whenever
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0
}
\]
and the normalized laws retain uniform density/moment constants.

For a fixed positive variance this is automatic. For fixed \(r\), it is enough that
\[
|\log\sigma_m|=o(m).
\]
Conversely, if \(\sigma_m=e^{cm/k_m}\), the limiting exponent is shifted by \(c\). Thus no theorem for a varying-variance triangular array should say "variance is irrelevant" without this scale condition.

## 7. Exact phase-retrieval implication

For a full-spark real
\[
A\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1+r)\times m},
\]
define
\[
\omega(A)
=
\min_{J:\operatorname{rank}(A_J)<m}
\sigma_m(A_{J^c}).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\omega(A)=M_{m,r}(A).}
\]

Indeed, full spark forces every rank-deficient \(J\) to have \(|J|\le m-1\), so \(|J^c|\ge m+r\). Choosing \(T\subset J^c\) with \(|T|=m+r\) and using row-addition monotonicity gives
\[
\sigma_m(A_{J^c})\ge\sigma_m(A_T)\ge M_{m,r}(A).
\]
Conversely, if \(T\) minimizes \(M_{m,r}\), then \(J=T^c\) has \(|J|=m-1\), hence is rank deficient, and
\[
\omega(A)\le\sigma_m(A_T).
\]

Bounded-density iid matrices are full spark almost surely. At
\[
N_m=2m-1+r_m,\qquad \gamma\to2,\qquad h(2)=\log4,
\]
the candidate real theorem gives
\[
\frac{r_m+1}{m}\log\omega(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-\log4,
\]
and for fixed \(r\),
\[
\omega(A_m)
=
4^{-m/(r+1)+o_{\mathbb P}(m)}.
\]

## 10. Highest-value next attack

Do not broaden the theorem yet. The next run should attack the **below-fourth-moment norm bottleneck** in a way compatible with an exponentially small target singular value.

The promising route is not Weyl perturbation. Instead:

1. choose a row-deletion rule based on large coordinates/row norms before constructing the hyperplane family;
2. prove that only \(o(m)\) rows are deleted under a tail assumption weaker than fourth moment, so the aspect ratio and entropy \(h(\gamma)\) are unchanged;
3. prove an \(O(\sqrt m)\) operator norm bound for the retained base matrix;
4. rerun the incompressible-normal and pair-angle lemmas entirely inside the retained rows;
5. quantify whether the weakest possible tail assumption is a truncated second-moment/uniform-integrability condition rather than a literal fourth moment.

Any such proof must preserve the exponential candidate count; deleting a positive fraction of rows changes \(h(\gamma)\) and therefore changes the target exponent.
