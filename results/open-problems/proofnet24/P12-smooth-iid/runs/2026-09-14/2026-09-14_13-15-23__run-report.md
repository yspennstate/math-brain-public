Run timestamp: 2026-09-14 13:15:23 Asia/Jerusalem

## 0. Exact target

The source target remains Conjecture 8.2 (“Smooth iid universality”) from the June 28, 2026 manuscript *Extreme least singular values with redundancy, radial ensembles, and quantitative stability of phase retrieval*, Section 8.2.

For an \(N_m\times m\) real matrix \(A_m\), define
\[
M_{m,r}(A_m)=\min_{T\subset[N_m],\ |T|=m+r}\sigma_m((A_m)_T).
\]
The conjecture states: if the entries are iid, real, mean zero, variance one, with a bounded sufficiently smooth density and sufficiently many finite moments, and if \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\), then for every fixed integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1},
\qquad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]
The same source predicts, for complex iid entries with bounded two-real-dimensional density,
\[
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{2(r+1)}.
\]

The public August 7, 2026 preprint (Yang–Wen–Shmalo, arXiv:2608.07410) proves the real \(r=0\) theorem for centered bounded-density subgaussian entries. The August redundancy draft strengthens this, at draft level, to \(k_m=r_m+1\) with \(k_m\log m=o(m)\). The latest P12/P13 state replaced the subgaussian assumption by bounded fourth moment, using incompressibility plus a density-level local CLT and a bounded-operator-norm event.

**The theorem-sized target of this run was to remove the fourth-moment hypothesis without losing the exponential rate, by deleting only \(o(m)\) rows before constructing the hyperplane family.**

## 2. Previous state that remains valid

The latest P12 report had a candidate-complete real theorem under
\[
\mathbb E\xi_m=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=1,
\qquad \|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,
\qquad \mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B,
\]
uniformly in a triangular array, with
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma),
\qquad k_m=r_m+1,\qquad k_m\log m=o(m).
\]
The proof has four pieces:

1. a lower estimate using the negative-second-moment identity and bounded-density projection estimates;
2. incompressibility of a positive fraction of hyperplane normals on a bounded-norm event;
3. exponential sparsity of nearly parallel low-overlap pairs;
4. staged marked-row amplification, with the exact scalar threshold
   \[
   s=t/\sqrt{k},\qquad t=e^{-(h(\gamma)-\varepsilon)m/k}.
   \]

The key local-density input was already repaired in P12-LD-02: for an incompressible direction \(u\), bounded coordinate density plus finite third moment suffices to give a uniform density floor for \(\langle X,u\rangle\) near zero. The fourth moment was only being used globally to obtain \(\|A\|=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\).

The previous heavy-tail example with tail index \(\alpha\in(3,4)\) correctly shows that finite third moment does **not** give an untrimmed \(O(\sqrt m)\) operator norm. This run shows that this is only an obstruction to the untrimmed proof, not to the theorem.

## 3. New tail condition and clean-row construction

Fix a small absolute constant \(a>0\), chosen below to fit Rebrova’s bounded-entry norm lemma, and define
\[
\tau_m=a\sqrt{\frac{m}{\log m}}.
\]
For a triangular array of centered variance-one laws \(\xi_m\), put
\[
p_m=\mathbb P(|\xi_m|>\tau_m),
\qquad
\eta_m=\mathbb E\big[\xi_m^2\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\}}\big].
\]

The new verified modular assumptions are
\[
\boxed{m p_m\to0,\qquad \eta_m\to0.}
\tag{T}
\]
The first says that a row has a large coordinate with probability \(o(1)\); the second prevents the deleted tail from carrying a macroscopic fraction of the variance.

Call row \(i\) **clean** when
\[
\max_{1\le j\le m}|\xi_{ij}|\le\tau_m.
\]
Because the row coordinates are independent,
\[
q_m:=\mathbb P\{\text{row dirty}\}
=1-(1-p_m)^m\le mp_m=o(1).
\]
For \(N_m\sim\gamma m\), the number of dirty rows is therefore \(o_{\mathbb P}(m)\), and
\[
\frac{N_m^{\rm clean}}m\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\gamma.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Hence deleting dirty rows preserves the aspect ratio and the combinatorial entropy \(h(\gamma)m+o(m)\).

Conditional on a row being clean, its coordinates remain independent and identically distributed, with one-coordinate law
\[
\zeta_m\stackrel d=\xi_m\mid\{|\xi_m|\le\tau_m\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
This product structure is essential: the deletion rule is rowwise but factorizes over coordinates inside each accepted row.

## 4. New lemma P12-TRIM-01: the retained matrix has optimal norm

Let
\[
\widehat\xi_m=\xi_m\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi_m|\le\tau_m\}},
\qquad
\mu_m=\mathbb E\widehat\xi_m.
\]
Since \(\mathbb E\xi_m=0\),
\[
\mu_m=-\mathbb E\big[\xi_m\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\}}\big].
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
|\mu_m|^2\le p_m\eta_m,
\qquad
\sqrt m\,|\mu_m|
\le \sqrt{m p_m}\,\sqrt{\eta_m}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write
\[
s_m=\widehat\xi_m-\mu_m.
\]
Then \(\mathbb Es_m=0\), \(\mathbb Es_m^2\le1\), and eventually
\[
|s_m|\le2\tau_m=2a\sqrt{m/\log m}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Rebrova’s Lemma 4.2 states that an \(n\times n\) iid matrix with mean-zero entries, variance at most one, and entries bounded by a sufficiently small constant times \(\sqrt{n/\log n}\) has operator norm at most \(13r\sqrt n\) with probability at least \(1-n^{-r}\). It does **not** require symmetry in that lemma.

Pad the \(N_m\times m\) centered-truncated matrix by independent entries to an \(n_m\times n_m\) square matrix with
\[
n_m=\max(N_m,m)=\Theta(m).
\]
Choose \(a>0\) small enough that (4.2) satisfies Rebrova’s threshold for all large \(m\). Then, for any fixed \(r\ge2\),
\[
\|S_m\|=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m).
\tag{4.3}
\]
The rank-one mean matrix has norm
\[
\|\mu_m\mathbf 1_{N_m}\mathbf 1_m^\top\|
=|\mu_m|\sqrt{N_m m}
=o(\sqrt m)
\tag{4.4}
\]
by (4.1). Hence the entrywise truncated matrix obeys
\[
\|\widehat A_m\|=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m).
\tag{4.5}
\]
Every clean row of \(A_m\) equals the corresponding row of \(\widehat A_m\), so the clean-row submatrix satisfies
\[
\boxed{\|A_m^{\rm clean}\|=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m).}
\tag{P12-TRIM-01}
\]

This is the missing global input, obtained without a fourth moment.

## 5. Conditional clean law: centering, variance and density

Let
\[
a_m=\mathbb E\zeta_m,
\qquad
\sigma_m^2=\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_m).
\]
Then
\[
a_m=\frac{\mu_m}{1-p_m},
\qquad
\sqrt m\,|a_m|\to0,
\tag{5.1}
\]
and by \(\eta_m\to0\),
\[
\sigma_m^2\to1.
\tag{5.2}
\]
If the original coordinate density satisfies \(\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K\), then for large \(m\)
\[
\|f_{\zeta_m}\|_\infty\le\frac K{1-p_m}\le2K.
\tag{5.3}
\]
After centering and dividing by \(\sigma_m\), the density bound remains uniform.

Thus the accepted-row law is again a product law with uniformly nondegenerate variance and uniformly bounded one-dimensional density. The only apparent loss is that its third moment may grow with \(m\), because \(|\zeta_m|\le\tau_m\).

## 6. New lemma P12-LD-03: local density survives without an original third moment

Define
\[
\eta_{m,j}=\frac{\zeta_{m,j}-a_m}{\sigma_m}.
\]
Then the \(\eta_{m,j}\) are iid, centered, variance one, uniformly bounded-density, and
\[
\mathbb E|\eta_{m,j}|^3\le C\tau_m.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let \(u\in S^{m-1}\) be \((\delta,\rho)\)-incompressible with fixed \(\delta,\rho>0\). As in P12-LD-02, discard the coordinates with
\[
|u_j|>1/\sqrt{\delta m}.
\]
The remaining coordinates carry at least \(\rho/2\) of the \(\ell_2\) norm. After normalizing that diffuse part, its coefficients \(c_j\) satisfy
\[
\sum c_j^2=1,
\qquad
\max_j|c_j|\le \frac{C}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Partition the diffuse coefficients into a fixed large number \(R\) of variance-balanced blocks. For one block define
\[
Z_\ell=\sqrt R\sum_{j\in B_\ell}c_j\eta_{m,j}.
\]
Its variance is bounded above and below by positive constants, and its density maximum is uniformly bounded by the product-measure marginal-density theorem. Rosenthal’s inequality at \(p=3\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|Z_\ell|^3
&\le C_R\left[1+
\mathbb E|\eta_m|^3\max_j|c_j|\right]\\
&\le C_R\left[1+C\frac{\tau_m}{\sqrt m}\right]
=C_R\left[1+O((\log m)^{-1/2})\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Thus the normalized block third moments remain uniformly bounded even though the one-coordinate third moment need not be.

Bobkov–Götze’s density Berry–Esseen theorem applies to the sum of these independent blocks. Since \(R\) is fixed but can be chosen arbitrarily large, choose it once so that the uniform density error is smaller than half the Gaussian density on the fixed interval needed for the convolution step. Exactly as in P12-LD-02, the diffuse component then has a positive density floor on a fixed interval; convolving back the omitted coordinates gives
\[
c\le f_{\langle \eta_m,u\rangle}(x)\le C,
\qquad |x|\le2,
\tag{6.4}
\]
with constants independent of \(m\) and of the incompressible \(u\).

Finally,
\[
\langle \zeta_m,u\rangle
=\sigma_m\langle \eta_m,u\rangle+a_m\sum_{j=1}^m u_j,
\]
and
\[
\left|a_m\sum_j u_j\right|
\le\sqrt m|a_m|\to0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Together with \(\sigma_m\to1\), (6.4) implies, for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\boxed{
ct\le
\mathbb P\{|\langle X^{\rm clean},u\rangle|\le t\}
\le Ct,
\qquad 0<t\le1,
}
\tag{P12-LD-03}
\]
uniformly over all fixed-parameter incompressible \(u\).

This is the crucial point: **the original law need not have a finite third moment.** Truncation makes the coordinate third moment grow only like \(\sqrt{m/\log m}\), while the diffuse coefficients are \(O(m^{-1/2})\); the product tends to zero.

## 7. Candidate theorem P12-MAIN-02

### Triangular-array form

Let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with law \(\xi_m\). Assume uniformly:

1. \(\mathbb E\xi_m=0\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=1\);
2. \(\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K\);
3. for \(\tau_m=a\sqrt{m/\log m}\) with sufficiently small fixed \(a>0\),
   \[
   m\,\mathbb P(|\xi_m|>\tau_m)\to0,
   \qquad
   \mathbb E[\xi_m^2;|\xi_m|>\tau_m]\to0;
   \tag{7.1}
   \]
4. \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\);
5. \(k_m=r_m+1\) and \(k_m\log m=o(m)\).

Then the previous P12 staged proof can be rerun entirely on the clean rows. Indeed:

- by P12-TRIM-01 the clean base matrix has \(O(\sqrt m)\) norm with probability tending to one;
- the compressible-net proof therefore gives fixed-parameter incompressibility for a positive fraction of candidate hyperplane normals;
- P12-LD-03 supplies the uniform one-slab probability \(\asymp s\);
- bounded density supplies the two-slab upper bound and the low-overlap near-parallel estimate exactly as before;
- only \(o(m)\) rows were removed, so for every fixed \(\beta<\gamma\) there are at least \(\beta m\) clean rows with probability tending to one, and
  \[
  \log\binom{N_m^{\rm clean}}{m-1}=h(\gamma)m+o_{\mathbb P}(m).
  \]
  Equivalently, in the staged proof one can fix \(\beta<\gamma\) with \(h(\beta)>h(\gamma)-\varepsilon\), and the random \(o(m)\) deletion does not consume the fixed reserve margin;
- the lower estimate is run on the original matrix and never needed a tail assumption.

Therefore the proof architecture yields
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
}
\tag{P12-MAIN-02}
\]
For fixed \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

I classify P12-MAIN-02 as **CANDIDATE COMPLETE, not portfolio-closed**. The new truncation and local-density lemmas are proved here, but the project requires two independent worker audits before a full theorem is declared closed.

## 8. Fixed-law corollaries: finite \(2+\delta\) moments are more than enough

For one fixed centered variance-one law, the tail-variance condition in (7.1) is automatic from finite variance. A simple sufficient condition for the tail-count part is
\[
\boxed{
t^2\log(e+t)\,\mathbb P(|\xi|>t)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{Tlog}
\]
Indeed, at \(t=\tau_m\),
\[
\tau_m^2\log\tau_m\sim \frac{a^2}{2}m,
\]
so (Tlog) implies \(m\mathbb P(|\xi|>\tau_m)\to0\).

In particular, if for some \(\delta>0\)
\[
\mathbb E|\xi|^{2+\delta}<\infty,
\]
then Markov gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
m\mathbb P(|\xi|>\tau_m)
&\le C_\delta
\frac{(\log m)^{1+\delta/2}}{m^{\delta/2}}	o0,\\
\mathbb E[\xi^2;|\xi|>\tau_m]
&\le C_\delta\tau_m^{-\delta}	o0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Hence:

**P12-COR-2+δ.** For every fixed \(\delta>0\), the real theorem holds under centered variance-one entries with uniformly bounded density and a finite \((2+\delta)\)-moment. No differentiability of the density is used.

This strictly improves the previous bounded-fourth-moment candidate theorem. It also covers the former finite-third-moment obstruction: finite third moment is now sufficient after clean-row deletion even though the full untrimmed matrix can have \(\|A_m\|/\sqrt m\to\infty\).

The condition (Tlog) is weaker than any fixed \((2+\delta)\)-moment. For example, tails of order roughly \(1/(t^2\log^2 t)\) satisfy it while generally having no \((2+\delta)\)-moment.

## 9. Counterexample to an overstrong finite-variance truncation claim

Finite variance alone does **not** imply the tail-count condition needed by this particular row-deletion proof.

Choose rapidly increasing \(t_j=e^{2^j}\). Put symmetric mass
\[
p_j\asymp\frac{1}{t_j^2\log t_j}
\]
in tiny disjoint intervals around \(\pm t_j\), with interval widths chosen so that the resulting density is uniformly bounded, and put the remaining probability in a bounded symmetric density near the origin. Rescale to variance one.

Then
\[
\sum_j p_jt_j^2\asymp\sum_j\frac1{\log t_j}<\infty,
\]
so the law has finite variance. But along dimensions \(m_j\) chosen so that
\[
a\sqrt{m_j/\log m_j}\asymp t_j,
\]
one has \(m_j\asymp C t_j^2\log t_j\) and therefore
\[
m_j\mathbb P(|\xi|>a\sqrt{m_j/\log m_j})\gtrsim c>0.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Thus a row is dirty with probability bounded away from zero along this subsequence; deleting every row containing a coordinate beyond Rebrova’s \(\sqrt{m/\log m}\) threshold removes a positive fraction of rows and changes the entropy exponent.

**Conclusion:** finite variance alone is not justified by the present row-deletion route. This is a method obstruction, not a disproof of Conjecture 8.2 under finite variance.

## 10. Exact variance normalization

The previous normalization calculation remains exact. If
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=\sigma_m^2>0,
\qquad \widetilde A_m=A_m/\sigma_m,
\]
then
\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=\sigma_m M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m),
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
=
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)
+
\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\tag{10.1}
\]
Therefore the same exponent is preserved exactly when
\[
\boxed{\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0}
\tag{10.2}
\]
provided the normalized laws satisfy the uniform density and tail conditions. Fixed positive variance is harmless. An exponentially varying triangular-array variance can shift the exponent and must not be hidden under “normalization.”

## 11. Phase-retrieval implication

For a full-spark real matrix
\[
A\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1+r)\times m},
\]
with Balan–Wang stability parameter
\[
\omega(A)=
\min_{J:\operatorname{rank}(A_J)<m}
\sigma_m(A_{J^c}),
\]
the deterministic identity remains
\[
\boxed{\omega(A)=M_{m,r}(A).}
\tag{11.1}
\]
A bounded-density iid matrix is full spark almost surely. Therefore, at
\[
N_m=2m-1+r_m,
\qquad \gamma\to2,
\qquad h(2)=\log4,
\]
P12-MAIN-02 gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{r_m+1}{m}\log\omega(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-\log4.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]
For fixed \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
\omega(A_m)=4^{-m/(r+1)+o_{\mathbb P}(m)}.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
Thus the phase-retrieval exponential stability law survives under the new logarithmic second-tail condition, in particular under every finite \((2+\delta)\)-moment with bounded density.

No \(Ce^{-c\sqrt m}\) deviation rate is claimed under these weaker tails.

## 12. Real/complex, square/rectangular, centered/noncentered distinctions

- **Real:** P12-MAIN-02 is a real candidate theorem.
- **Complex:** still OPEN. The expected factor \(1/2\) requires a genuinely two-real-dimensional density argument and a complex staged proof; it is not obtained by cosmetic replacement of \(t\) with \(t^2\).
- **Square submatrices \(r=0\):** included as a special case.
- **Fixed positive surplus \(r>0\):** included.
- **Moderate surplus:** included for \(k_m=r_m+1\) with \(k_m\log m=o(m)\).
- **Centered:** required in the present proof. The truncation bias becomes \(o(m^{-1/2})\) only because the original mean is zero.
- **Noncentered:** OPEN. A fixed nonzero coordinate mean creates a rank-one component of norm \(\Theta(m)\), and the one-slab density floor at zero is no longer automatic. It is not legitimate to subtract that rank-one mean by a Weyl argument at an exponentially small singular-value scale.
- **Variance normalization:** fixed positive variance is harmless; triangular-array variance must satisfy (10.2).
- **Density smoothness:** no differentiability is used in the present real argument; a uniform bounded Lebesgue density suffices for all verified small-ball inputs.

## 13. Route comparison and failed approaches

### Distance-to-span / negative second moment
Still the correct route for the lower bound. It uses only bounded density and remains untouched by the heavy-tail weakening.

### Compressible/incompressible decomposition
Still central for the upper bound. The important change is that it is now run on the retained clean matrix, for which P12-TRIM-01 restores the fixed \(O(\sqrt m)\) norm event. This preserves fixed incompressibility parameters instead of allowing them to deteriorate with \(m\).

### Smoothing / local CLT
The local CLT remains essential because the slab width is exponentially small. Ordinary CDF Berry–Esseen errors are still useless. P12-LD-03 shows that the needed density theorem can be applied after truncation even if the original third moment is infinite.

### Global Lindeberg replacement
Still rejected. The observable is a minimum over exponentially many submatrices and is nonsmooth at singular-value collisions; derivative losses are not under control.

### Naive truncation + Weyl perturbation
Still rejected. Entrywise truncation changes the matrix by polynomial scale, vastly larger than the target \(e^{-\Theta(m/k)}\). The successful argument truncates only to *select rows on which the original matrix is unchanged*; it never compares final least singular values by Weyl.

### Untrimmed finite-third-moment norm control
Disproved as a route by the previous \(\alpha\in(3,4)\) heavy-tail example. P12-MAIN-02 bypasses this obstruction rather than contradicting it.

### Finite variance alone via the same coordinate-threshold row deletion
Disproved as a route by the sparse-spike construction in Section 9. A different regularization or no-gaps theorem would be required to go all the way to arbitrary finite variance.

Source/run identifiers:

Claim: under centered variance-one bounded-density subgaussian coordinates, conditional on a shared block \(A\), the two residual null lines \(v,w\in S^{D-1}\) satisfy for \(D\ge3\)
\[
\mathbb P\{1-(v\cdot w)^2\le\varepsilon\mid A\}
\le
\big[C\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(e/\varepsilon)}\big]^{D-1}.
\tag{14.1}
\]

**Verdict: PROVED under the stated assumptions.**

Audit: condition further on the first private block, so \(v\) is deterministic while the second private block \(V=CQ_A\) remains independent. If \(Y=1-(v\cdot w)^2\), then \(Vw=0\) implies
\[
\|Vv\|_2\le\sqrt Y\,\|V\|_{\rm op}.
\]
Conditional on \(A,v\), the coordinates of \(Vv\) are independent scalar forms \(\langle X_i,Q_Av\rangle\). Since \(Q_Av\) is a deterministic unit vector in the original product-coordinate space, the one-dimensional product-measure marginal-density theorem gives a uniform density cap. The joint density bound and Euclidean-ball volume therefore give the correct \((D-1)\)-dimensional small-ball power. The projected rows \(Q_A^TX_i\) have dependent coordinates, but they are independent across rows, isotropic, and uniformly subgaussian; the standard independent-isotropic-subgaussian-row norm theorem applies at that level and yields the logarithmic factor. Thus there is no illicit iid-coordinate step after projection.

This audit does **not** extend P14-EDGE to the new heavy-tailed P12 regime; P14 uses a stronger operator-norm tail for a different singular-edge integral.

## 16. Next attack

The next highest-value attack is **finite variance beyond (Tlog)**, but it should not be attempted by weakening the same threshold blindly. Two concrete routes are worth testing:

1. **Row/column regularization without changing candidate submatrices.** Adapt heavy-tail norm regularization so that the bad set can be covered by \(o(m)\) whole rows (not arbitrary entries or a positive fraction of columns), preserving the row-submatrix combinatorial entropy.
2. **Norm-free no-gaps route.** Prove directly that a positive fraction of \((m-1)\)-row null normals have a fixed diffuse \(\ell_2\) mass under bounded density + finite variance, without conditioning on a global \(O(\sqrt m)\) norm event. Such a theorem would remove the only reason for the logarithmic tail-count assumption.
