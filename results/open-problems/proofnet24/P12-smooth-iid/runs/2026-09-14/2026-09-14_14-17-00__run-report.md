## 1. Exact source target recovered

The exact source is the June 28, 2026 manuscript *Extreme least singular values with redundancy, radial ensembles, and quantitative stability of phase retrieval*, Section 8.2, Conjecture 8.2.

For an \(N_m\times m\) matrix \(A_m\), define
\[
M_{m,r}(A_m)
=
\min_{\substack{T\subset[N_m]\\ |T|=m+r}}
\sigma_m((A_m)_T).
\]

The real conjecture states:

> If the entries are iid real, mean zero, variance one, with a bounded sufficiently smooth density and sufficiently many finite moments, and if \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\), then for every fixed integer \(r\ge0\),
> \[
> \frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)
> \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
> -\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1},
> \qquad
> h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
> \]

The source predicts for complex iid entries with bounded two-real-dimensional density
\[
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{2(r+1)}.
\]

The manuscript itself says the missing inputs are density-level local central limit control for random hyperplane normals and the corresponding two-normal determinant/angle bound; ordinary distribution-function Berry--Esseen errors are much too large at the exponentially small slab scale.

The public August 7, 2026 Yang--Wen--Shmalo preprint proves the real \(r=0\) theorem for centered bounded-density subgaussian entries. The August redundancy work extends the target to
\[
k_m=r_m+1,\qquad k_m\log m=o(m),
\]
with the predicted normalized limit
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)\to_{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
\]

## 2. Inherited strongest full candidate theorem

The latest P12 state proves, modulo the project's required outside audits, the following real triangular-array theorem.

Let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries \(\xi_m\). Assume uniformly

1. \(\mathbb E\xi_m=0\), \(\operatorname{Var}\xi_m=1\);
2. \(\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K\);
3. for
   \[
   \tau_m=a\sqrt{\frac{m}{\log m}}
   \]
   with sufficiently small fixed \(a>0\),
   \[
   m\,\mathbb P(|\xi_m|>\tau_m)\to0,
   \qquad
   \mathbb E[\xi_m^2;|\xi_m|>\tau_m]\to0;
   \tag{T}
   \]
4. \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\);
5. \(k_m=r_m+1\) and \(k_m\log m=o(m)\).

Then the clean-row deletion argument gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\to_{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
}
\tag{P12-MAIN-02}
\]

For a fixed entry law, the sufficient condition
\[
t^2\log(e+t)\,\mathbb P(|\xi|>t)\to0
\tag{Tlog}
\]
implies (T), and every finite \((2+\delta)\)-moment is more than sufficient.

The key inherited facts are:

- only \(o_{\mathbb P}(m)\) dirty rows are deleted;
- the retained matrix has \(O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\) norm by bounded-entry regularization;
- the conditional retained-row law remains a product law;
- density-level one-slab probabilities are \(\asymp t\) for useful incompressible normals;
- the marked-row threshold is exactly
  \[
  s=\frac{t}{\sqrt{k}},
  \]
  not \(\sqrt{t/k}\);
- pair overlaps are controlled by bounded-density anti-concentration plus the fixed norm event.

The result is candidate-complete, not ProofNet-closed: it still needs the required two OTHER-worker complete audits.

## 3. New theorem P12-LD-04 — finite variance suffices for the one-normal density theorem for a fixed law

The old P12-LD-02 used a third moment. P12-LD-03 removed the original third moment after clean-row truncation. For a **fixed** entry law, neither is necessary.

### Statement

Let \(\xi\) be a fixed real random variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=0,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^2=1,
\qquad
\|f_\xi\|_\infty\le K.
\]
No moment above two is assumed.

Fix \(\delta,\rho\in(0,1)\). Then there exist
\[
m_0=m_0(\xi,\delta,\rho),
\qquad
0<c<C<\infty
\]
such that for every \(m\ge m_0\) and every
\((\delta,\rho)\)-incompressible \(u\in S^{m-1}\), the density
\(f_u\) of
\[
S_u=\langle X,u\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^m u_j\xi_j
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
c\le f_u(x)\le C,\qquad |x|\le1.
}
\tag{P12-LD-04}
\]
Consequently, uniformly over all such \(u\),
\[
\boxed{
2ct\le
\mathbb P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t\}
\le2Ct,
\qquad 0<t\le1.
}
\]

The upper constant may be taken universal times \(K\). The lower constant can depend on the fixed law and on \((\delta,\rho)\).

### Step 1: deterministic spread subset

If \(u\) is \((\delta,\rho)\)-incompressible, the standard spread argument gives a set
\(J\subset[m]\) such that
\[
|J|\ge c_0m,
\qquad
\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2m}}
\le |u_j|
\le \frac1{\sqrt{\delta m}}
\quad(j\in J),
\]
and
\[
v:=\sum_{j\in J}u_j^2\ge\frac{\rho^2}{2}.
\]
Put
\[
c_j=\frac{u_j}{\sqrt v},\qquad j\in J.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{j\in J}c_j^2=1,
\qquad
\frac{a_0}{\sqrt m}\le |c_j|\le\frac{A_0}{\sqrt m}
\]
with positive constants depending only on \(\delta,\rho\).

### Step 2: a weighted density local CLT under only finite variance

Let
\[
Z_m=\sum_{j\in J}c_j\xi_j.
\]
I claim
\[
\boxed{
\|f_{Z_m}-\varphi\|_\infty\to0
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
uniformly over all coefficient arrays satisfying the preceding two-sided \(m^{-1/2}\) bounds.

Let
\[
\phi(t)=\mathbb E e^{it\xi}.
\]
Because \(f_\xi\in L^1\cap L^\infty\), we have
\(f_\xi\in L^2\), so Plancherel gives \(\phi\in L^2\).
Absolute continuity implies
\[
|\phi(t)|<1\qquad(t\ne0),
\]
and Riemann--Lebesgue gives \(\phi(t)\to0\).

Finite variance and centering give
\[
\phi(s)=1-\frac{s^2}{2}+o(s^2).
\]
Hence for some \(s_0,c_1>0\),
\[
|\phi(s)|\le e^{-c_1s^2},\qquad |s|\le s_0.
\]

The characteristic function of \(Z_m\) is
\[
\Phi_m(t)=\prod_{j\in J}\phi(c_jt).
\]
For every fixed \(t\),
\[
\Phi_m(t)\to e^{-t^2/2},
\]
uniformly over the admissible coefficient arrays, because
\(\max_j|c_j|=O(m^{-1/2})\) and
\(\sum c_j^2=1\).

Set
\[
T_m=\frac{s_0\sqrt m}{A_0}.
\]
For \(|t|\le T_m\),
\[
|\Phi_m(t)|
\le
\exp\!\left(-c_1t^2\sum_jc_j^2\right)
=e^{-c_1t^2}.
\]
Thus dominated convergence handles the full low-frequency integral up to \(T_m\).

For \(|t|>T_m\), every spread coefficient satisfies
\[
|c_jt|\ge q_0:=\frac{a_0s_0}{A_0}>0.
\]
Since \(\xi\) is absolutely continuous,
\[
\vartheta:=\sup_{|s|\ge q_0}|\phi(s)|<1.
\]
Select two indices \(j_1,j_2\in J\). The remaining factors contribute
\(\vartheta^{|J|-2}\). By Cauchy--Schwarz and Plancherel,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}
|\phi(c_{j_1}t)\phi(c_{j_2}t)|\,dt
&\le
\frac{\|\phi\|_2^2}
{\sqrt{|c_{j_1}c_{j_2}|}}\\
&\le C_\xi\sqrt m.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\int_{|t|>T_m}|\Phi_m(t)|\,dt
\le
C_\xi\sqrt m\,\vartheta^{c_0m-2}
\to0.
\]
The Gaussian Fourier tail beyond \(T_m\) also vanishes. Hence
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
|\Phi_m(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\,dt\to0.
\]
Fourier inversion proves (3.1).

This proof uses no third moment, no differentiability of \(f_\xi\), and no Berry--Esseen rate.

### Step 3: convolve back the omitted coordinates

Write
\[
S_u=\sqrt v\, Z_m+L,
\qquad
L=\sum_{j\notin J}u_j\xi_j.
\]
The two summands are independent,
\[
\mathbb EL=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}L=1-v\le1,
\qquad
v\ge\rho^2/2.
\]
Chebyshev gives
\[
\mathbb P\{|L|\le2\}\ge3/4.
\]
For \(|x|\le1\), on \(|L|\le2\),
\[
\left|\frac{x-L}{\sqrt v}\right|
\le\frac{3\sqrt2}{\rho}.
\]
The Gaussian density has a strictly positive minimum on this fixed interval. Uniform convergence (3.1) therefore yields a uniform positive lower bound for the density of \(S_u\).

For the upper bound, the one-dimensional product-measure marginal-density theorem gives
\[
\|f_u\|_\infty\le C K
\]
for every unit \(u\).

This proves P12-LD-04.

## 4. New theorem P12-LD2-01 — two-normal density-level CLT under finite variance

The same Fourier idea yields the determinant/angle factor required by the source manuscript, provided the two coefficient vectors are genuinely diffuse.

### General two-dimensional form

Let \(a_{j,m}\in\mathbb R^2\), \(1\le j\le m\), satisfy
\[
\Sigma_m:=\sum_{j=1}^m a_{j,m}a_{j,m}^{T},
\]
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_m)\ge\kappa>0,
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_m)\le K_0,
\qquad
\max_j\|a_{j,m}\|\le \frac{A}{\sqrt m}.
\]
For independent copies \(\xi_j\), put
\[
Y_m=\sum_{j=1}^m a_{j,m}\xi_j\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
Then \(Y_m\) has a density \(g_m\), and
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{y\in\mathbb R^2}
|g_m(y)-\varphi_{\Sigma_m}(y)|
\to0.
}
\tag{P12-LD2-01}
\]

### Fourier proof: the only new high-frequency point

The characteristic function is
\[
\Phi_m(t)=
\prod_{j=1}^m\phi(a_{j,m}\cdot t).
\]
For \(|t|\le c\sqrt m\), all scalar arguments are in the quadratic region and
\[
|\Phi_m(t)|
\le e^{-c t^T\Sigma_m t}
\le e^{-c\kappa|t|^2}.
\]

For large \(|t|\), the frame lower bound implies that, in every direction
\(\theta\in S^1\),
\[
\sum_j(a_j\cdot\theta)^2\ge\kappa.
\]
Since every summand is at most \(A^2/m\), a positive fraction of the indices obey
\[
|a_j\cdot\theta|\ge c/\sqrt m.
\]
Thus beyond a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt m\), a positive linear number of characteristic-function factors are bounded by a fixed \(\vartheta<1\).

To make the infinite two-dimensional tail integrable rather than merely exponentially small pointwise, use Cauchy--Binet:
\[
\det\Sigma_m
=
\sum_{i<j}\det(a_i,a_j)^2
\ge\kappa^2.
\]
Hence there is a pair with
\[
|\det(a_i,a_j)|\ge c/m.
\]
Removing two rows changes \(\Sigma_m\) by only \(O(m^{-1})\), so for large \(m\) there is a second disjoint pair with the same property.

For one such pair,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2}
|\phi(a_i\cdot t)\phi(a_j\cdot t)|^2dt
=
\frac{\|\phi\|_2^4}{|\det(a_i,a_j)|}
\le C_\xi m.
\]
Using Cauchy--Schwarz on the two disjoint pairs gives an \(O(m)\) \(L^1\) bound for their four-factor product. The remaining \(cm\) factors supply the exponential \(\vartheta^{cm}\) tail. This proves \(L^1\) convergence of the two-dimensional characteristic functions and hence uniform density convergence.

### Pair-normal corollary

For unit vectors \(u,v\in S^{m-1}\), take
\[
a_j=(u_j,v_j).
\]
Then
\[
\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\rho\\
\rho&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\rho=u\cdot v.
\]
If
\[
\|u\|_\infty+\|v\|_\infty\le C/\sqrt m,
\qquad
|\rho|\le1-\kappa,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
f_{(\langle X,u\rangle,\langle X,v\rangle)}(0,0)
=
\frac{1+o(1)}
{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}},
}
\]
uniformly over such pairs.

In particular, for all sufficiently large \(m\) and every \(0<t\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{
|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t,\,
|\langle X,v\rangle|\le t
\}
\le
\frac{C t^2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus, for a fixed bounded-density finite-variance law, the source manuscript's **density-level one-normal and two-normal analytic steps do not require any moment beyond two** once the normal vectors themselves satisfy the stated diffuse geometry.

The remaining finite-variance obstruction is therefore geometric: prove that the exponentially large family of random normals has enough fixed-parameter diffuseness and enough anti-alignment, without imposing a global \(O(\sqrt m)\) operator-norm event.

## 5. Counterexample P12-TRI-CE-01 — triangular arrays need a Lindeberg-type condition

The preceding fixed-law theorem must not be promoted blindly to arbitrary triangular arrays.

Fix \(a\in(0,1)\) and a small \(\eta>0\). Let
\[
L_m=\sqrt{am}
\]
and define a smooth symmetric mixture
\[
\xi_m\sim
\left(1-\frac1m\right)N(0,v_m)
+
\frac1{2m}N(L_m,\eta^2)
+
\frac1{2m}N(-L_m,\eta^2),
\]
where \(v_m\) is chosen so that \(\operatorname{Var}\xi_m=1\). Then
\[
v_m\to1-a>0.
\]
The laws are centered, variance one, \(C^\infty\), and have densities uniformly bounded in \(m\).

Nevertheless, for
\[
S_m=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{j=1}^m\xi_{m,j},
\]
the characteristic functions satisfy
\[
\mathbb Ee^{itS_m}
\longrightarrow
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{1-a}{2}t^2+\cos(\sqrt a\,t)-1
\right).
\]
The limit is the convolution of \(N(0,1-a)\) with a rate-one compound-Poisson law having jumps \(\pm\sqrt a\). It is not \(N(0,1)\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uniform bounded density + mean zero + variance one alone}
}
\]
does **not** imply a uniform triangular-array density CLT, even for the equal coefficient vector.

This explains why P12-MAIN-02's explicit tail/Lindeberg package is not cosmetic. The fixed-law finite-variance local theorem and the triangular-array theorem are genuinely different statements.

## 6. Random-rounding literature and the remaining geometric bottleneck

Livshyts' random-rounding theorem is directly relevant to the remaining finite-variance route. It was designed precisely to bypass operator-norm bounds in invertibility arguments. For square matrices with independent entries, concentration function separated from one, and
\[
\mathbb E\|A\|_{HS}^2\le Kn^2,
\]
it gives the sharp \(C\varepsilon+e^{-cn}\) least-singular-value form without requiring a fourth moment; with iid rows it covers the full \(\varepsilon\)-range.

For a fixed variance-one bounded-density law:

- bounded density gives a concentration function separated from one;
- finite variance gives the Hilbert--Schmidt second-moment condition.

So the **methodological obstruction identified by P13 -- dependence on a global operator norm in the compressible-net argument -- is not fundamental for one-matrix invertibility**.

However, I do not import Livshyts' one-matrix theorem as if it automatically proved the P12 normal-family statement. P12 needs uniform information over exponentially many overlapping \((m-1)\)-row spans and, for the second moment, control of near-alignment of pairs of their null normals. That is a stronger geometric object.

The new state is therefore:

1. local density under fixed finite variance: solved in P12-LD-04/P12-LD2-01;
2. one-matrix heavy-tail invertibility without operator norm: available in the literature;
3. exponentially many random normals + pair anti-alignment under finite variance: still OPEN.

This is a sharper bottleneck than “need a local CLT.”

## 7. Exact variance normalization

The normalization argument remains exact.

If
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=\sigma_m^2>0,\qquad
\widetilde A_m=A_m/\sigma_m,
\]
then
\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
=
\sigma_m M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m),
\]
hence
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
=
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)
+
\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\]
Thus the normalized and unnormalized triangular arrays have the same exponent exactly under
\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0,
}
\]
provided the normalized laws satisfy the other uniform hypotheses.

A fixed positive variance is harmless. An exponentially varying variance on the \(m/k_m\) scale can shift the exponent and must not be hidden under the phrase “without loss of generality, variance one.”

## 8. Phase-retrieval implication

For a full-spark real
\[
A\in\mathbb R^{(2m-1+r)\times m},
\]
the Balan--Wang obstruction
\[
\omega(A)
=
\min_{J:\operatorname{rank}(A_J)<m}
\sigma_m(A_{J^c})
\]
satisfies the exact deterministic identity
\[
\boxed{\omega(A)=M_{m,r}(A).}
\]
A bounded-density iid matrix is full spark almost surely.

Therefore P12-MAIN-02, at
\[
N_m=2m-1+r_m,
\qquad
\gamma\to2,
\qquad
h(2)=\log4,
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{r_m+1}{m}\log\omega(A_m)
\to_{\mathbb P}-\log4
}
\]
under its verified tail package. For fixed \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
\omega(A_m)
=
4^{-m/(r+1)+o_{\mathbb P}(m)}.
}
\]

P12-LD-04 does **not yet** extend this final phase-retrieval theorem to every bounded-density finite-variance law, because the exponential family of random normals and pair anti-alignment remain unresolved there.

\[
Y_1,\ldots,Y_{D-1}\in\mathbb R^D
\]
be iid centered isotropic projected rows. Let \(C\) be their generalized cross product:
\[
a\cdot C
=
\det(Y_1,\ldots,Y_{D-1},a).

P14 claims
\[
\mathbb E[CC^T\mid A]=(D-1)!\,I_D.
\]

**Verdict: PROVED / PASS.**

\[
(a\cdot C)(b\cdot C)

\[
\mathbb E[Y_{i,\sigma(i)}Y_{i,\tau(i)}]

\delta_{\sigma(i),\tau(i)}.

\[
\sigma(i)=\tau(i),\qquad i=1,\ldots,D-1.

\[
\mathbb E[(a\cdot C)(b\cdot C)]
=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_D}
a_{\sigma(D)}b_{\sigma(D)}
=
(D-1)!\,a\cdot b.
\]
That is exactly the claimed tensor identity.

R=\|C\|,\qquad v=C/R,\qquad W=R^2/D!,

\mathbb E[Wvv^T\mid A]=I_D/D.

\mathbb E[WW'(v\cdot w)^2\mid A]

\operatorname{tr}\big((I_D/D)^2\big)

> **P12-NORMAL-FV.** For an \((m-1)\times m\) iid matrix with one fixed centered variance-one bounded-density law, prove that, among the exponentially many \((m-1)\)-row spans used by the marked-row construction, all but an exponentially negligible fraction of null normals have fixed-parameter diffuse mass, and that low-overlap pairs have an exponentially negligible near-parallel fraction, without conditioning on a global \(O(\sqrt m)\) operator norm.

Random-rounding / regularized Hilbert--Schmidt nets are the leading candidate for the first half. P14-style shared-row conditioning and cofactor-size-bias or random-partition geometry are the leading candidates for the pair half.

If P12-NORMAL-FV is proved, P12-LD-04 plus the existing marked-row amplification would remove the final \(2+\delta\)/(Tlog) tail requirement for **fixed-law real Conjecture 8.2** and would immediately extend the phase-retrieval law
\[
\omega(A_m)=4^{-m/(r+1)+o_{\mathbb P}(m)}
\]
to every centered variance-one bounded-density finite-variance entry law.
