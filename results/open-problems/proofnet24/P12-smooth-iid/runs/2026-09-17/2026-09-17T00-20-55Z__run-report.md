## 4. Move #5 — add a smoothing parameter and differentiate: the deformation is singular

To test whether one could reach a B4 law by a continuous smoothing deformation, restrict first to the real case and let `X` be centered with variance one. For `0<tau<1`, set

\[
X_\tau=\sqrt{1-\tau}\,X+\sqrt\tau\,G,
\qquad G\sim N(0,1)
\tag{4.1}
\]

independently. Then `X_tau` remains centered and variance one. Whatever the original law of `X`, the Gaussian convolution gives a density satisfying

\[
\|f_\tau\|_\infty\le (2\pi\tau)^{-1/2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus every **fixed** `tau>0` lies in the `q=1` finite-convolution class, and the frozen theorem applies with a constant depending on `tau`.

The problem is the endpoint. The natural density envelope has

\[
\log M(\tau)=\frac12\log\frac1\tau+O(1),
\qquad
\frac{d}{d\tau}\log M(\tau)=-\frac1{2\tau},
\tag{4.3}
\]

so the control is singular at `tau=0`. If one tries a triangular smoothing scale

\[
\tau_m=\exp\!\left(-\beta\frac{m}{k_m}\right),
\tag{4.4}
\]

then

\[
k_m\log M(\tau_m)=\frac{\beta}{2}m+O(k_m),
\tag{4.5}
\]

which is an order-`m` quantity, not one of the `o(m)` losses that the frozen proof is allowed to discard.

There is an additional logical gap: proving (2.3) for every fixed `tau>0` does not allow one to send `tau downarrow 0` after `m to infinity`. The statistic is the minimum over exponentially many submatrices at an exponentially small source scale, so ordinary weak convergence of one entry law gives no uniform continuity statement of the required strength.

**Outcome of move #5:** useful negative diagnosis. Gaussian smoothing explains exactly why “prove it after adding a tiny Gaussian and remove the noise” is not a free route from B4 to universality. The derivative of the microscopic regularity constant blows up at the endpoint, and the blow-up reaches the leading exponential budget at natural exponentially small smoothing scales. ## 5. Move #7 — take the limit at the regime boundary: why `k log m=o(m)` is structural for this proof

The theorem's leading extreme scale is

\[
\log M_{m,r_m}\sim -\frac{h(\gamma)}{d_{\mathbb F}}\frac{m}{k_m}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Inside the frozen lower and upper arguments, determinant extraction, polynomial density constants, basis counting, marked-row staging, and pair stratification contribute losses of the form

\[
\exp\{O(k_m\log m)\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Under (2.2), (5.2) is `e^{o(m)}` and cannot perturb the entropy balance at scale `m`.

Now force the boundary

\[
k_m\sim \alpha\frac{m}{\log m},\qquad \alpha>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then the nominal extreme itself becomes polynomial:

\[
M_{m,r_m}\approx
\exp\!\left[-\frac{h(\gamma)}{d_{\mathbb F}\alpha}\log m\right]
=
 m^{-h(\gamma)/(d_{\mathbb F}\alpha)},
\tag{5.4}
\]

while the “lower-order” ledger becomes

\[
k_m\log m\sim \alpha m.
\tag{5.5}
\]

So the same factors that are negligible in the frozen regime can now shift the order-`m` exponent. The current proof architecture therefore has a genuine boundary layer at `m/log m`: below it, the target is smaller than every fixed polynomial scale and polynomial bookkeeping is negligible on the entropy scale; at it, the target is polynomial and the bookkeeping can compete with entropy.

**Outcome of move #7:** this does not refute a possible future theorem beyond (2.2), but it refutes the idea that the existing proof automatically extends there. The condition `k_m log m=o(m)` is not cosmetic in the frozen proof and must remain in the finished rewrite unless a new argument removes those losses.

## 6. Move #9 — randomize the candidate search: why subexponential sampling cannot replace pair geometry

At a source scale parameterized by `lambda>0`, one candidate's microscopic small-ball cost is of the schematic form

\[
p_m(\lambda)=\exp\{-\lambda m+o(m)\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

coming from the `t^{d_{\mathbb F}k_m}` factor after the source-scale substitution. The entire candidate family has exponential entropy `exp(h(gamma)m+o(m))`, which is why the upper extreme becomes visible when `lambda<h(gamma)`.

Suppose instead one samples only

\[
R_m=\exp\{o(m)\}
\tag{6.2}
\]

candidate row sets at random and hopes to find a witness without controlling the full pair structure. Even granting the optimistic marginal (6.1), the union bound gives

\[
\Pr\{\hbox{one sampled candidate succeeds}\}
\le R_m p_m(\lambda)
=
\exp\{-\lambda m+o(m)\}\to0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

To obtain a nonvanishing chance of a hit one needs exponentially many samples, on the same scale as the candidate entropy. But then the candidates share the same random matrix rows and their overlaps/near-parallel normals reappear; one must control pair correlations or build a structured adaptive survivor family. Randomization therefore does not erase the central upper-tail difficulty.

**Outcome of move #9:** brute randomized candidate sampling fails as a simplification. The public-paper/frozen-proof strategy of using the exponential family but controlling pair geometry and then amplifying with genuinely fresh probe rows is not replaceable by a small Monte Carlo search at the relevant scale.

## 8. Required P12 -> P13 independent audit: exact covariance Green representation

I audited a component not previously used for P12's directed P13 duty: `P13-I0051-COV-GREEN-01` in the Aldous--Janson Open Problem 9 clade-count package.

The current DTCS split law is

\[
q(n,i)=\frac{n}{2h_{n-1}i(n-i)},
\qquad 1\le i\le n-1,
\tag{8.1}
\]

where `h_{n-1}=sum_{j=1}^{n-1}1/j`. Let

\[
\mu_r(n)=E N_n(r),\qquad
V_{rs}(n)=\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)),
\qquad
C_{rs}(n)=\frac{V_{rs}(n)}n,
\tag{8.2}
\]

and

\[
g_r(n,i)=\mu_r(i)+\mu_r(n-i),
\qquad
\eta_{rs}(n)=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q(n,\cdot)}
(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)).
\tag{8.3}
\]

### Independent proof of the recursion

Condition on the root split `I=i`. With independent left and right daughter trees,

\[
N_n(r)=\mathbf 1_{\{n=r\}}+N_i^{(L)}(r)+N_{n-i}^{(R)}(r).
\tag{8.4}
\]

The root toll is deterministic after `n` is fixed. By the law of total covariance,

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_{rs}(n)
&=E\big[\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)\mid I)\big]
 +\operatorname{Cov}\big(E[N_n(r)\mid I],E[N_n(s)\mid I]\big)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q(n,i)
\{V_{rs}(i)+V_{rs}(n-i)\}
+n\eta_{rs}(n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Now insert `V_{rs}(i)=iC_{rs}(i)`, divide by `n`, and use the symmetry `q(n,i)=q(n,n-i)`. The two daughter terms become equal, so

\[
C_{rs}(n)
=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q(n,i)iC_{rs}(i)+\eta_{rs}(n).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Equation (8.1) gives the exact identity

\[
\frac{2i}{n}q(n,i)=\frac1{h_{n-1}(n-i)},
\tag{8.7}
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
C_{rs}(n)=
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{C_{rs}(i)}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}
+\eta_{rs}(n).
}
\tag{8.8}
\]

The coefficients in (8.8) sum to one:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac1{h_{n-1}(n-i)}=1,
\tag{8.9}
\]

so they are exactly the transition probabilities of the strictly descending harmonic-descent chain. Since the chain can visit a state at most once, repeated substitution of (8.8), with terminal value at `1`, yields the Green/occupation representation

\[
\boxed{
C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{j=2}^n a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j),
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

where `a(n,j)` is the probability that harmonic descent started at `n` visits `j`. If `j<max(r,s)`, at least one corresponding clade count is identically zero on every daughter tree of total size `j`, so `eta_{rs}(j)=0`; therefore the sum may start at `max(r,s)`.

This independently verifies both the total-covariance forcing and the exact harmonic Green kernel. I record

\[
\boxed{\texttt{P12-AUDIT-P13-I0051-COV-GREEN-62 = PASS / COMPONENT}.}
\tag{8.11}
\]
