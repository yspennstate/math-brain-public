\[
(N_m^{(n)},2\le m\le n)
\tag{4.1}
\]

in a way that allows covariance calculations and deduction of CLTs.

The P13 package claims an exact recursive finite-n joint law, the full normalized covariance kernel, and a moving-vector `ell^2` Gaussian limit. I audited the package against the P13 frozen audit map and the current primary harmonic-descent input of Brandenberger--Chin--Mossel (BCM), arXiv:2505.24821v2.

## 4.1 Exact split law and full PGF recursion — PASS

The DTCS root split law is

\[
q(n,i)=\frac{n}{2h_{n-1}i(n-i)},\qquad 1\le i\le n-1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Conditionally on a split `(i,n-i)`, the two daughter trees are independent and the clade-count vectors add, while the root contributes one size-`n` clade. Therefore the multivariate PGF satisfies exactly

\[
F_n(\mathbf z)
=z_n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q(n,i)F_i(\mathbf z)F_{n-i}(\mathbf z),
\tag{4.3}
\]

with the natural coordinate truncations. This recursion determines the complete finite-n joint distribution recursively.

The counterexample-first gate also survives: P12 previously rederived the exact `n=4` witness independently and obtained

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(N_4(2),N_4(3))=-\frac{24}{121},
\tag{4.4}
\]

using an exact `fractions.Fraction` checker. That artifact remains the finite exact certificate for this audit surface; I did not manufacture a redundant checker this run.

## 4.2 Exact covariance recursion and Green representation — PASS

Let

\[
\mu_r(n)=E N_n(r),\qquad g_r(n,i)=\mu_r(i)+\mu_r(n-i),
\]

\[
\eta_{rs}(n)=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q_n}(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)),
\qquad
C_{rs}(n)=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Total covariance gives

\[
V_{rs}(n)
=\sum_i q(n,i)\{V_{rs}(i)+V_{rs}(n-i)\}+n\eta_{rs}(n).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using symmetry of `q` and

\[
\frac{2i}{n}q(n,i)=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-i)},
\tag{4.7}
\]

one obtains

\[
C_{rs}(n)
=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{C_{rs}(i)}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}+\eta_{rs}(n).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Iteration along the strictly descending harmonic-descent chain yields

\[
\boxed{C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{j=2}^{n}a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j).}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This is exact and gives the complete finite covariance block because `eta_rs(j)=0` below `max(r,s)`.

## 4.3 Uniform mean remainder — PASS

P13 uses

\[
\rho_r=\frac{6h_{r-1}}{\pi^2r(r-1)},
\qquad
e_r(j)=\mu_r(j)-\rho_r j.
\tag{4.10}
\]

First,

\[
\sum_{r\ge2}\rho_r=1,
\tag{4.11}
\]

because

\[
\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{h_{r-1}}{r(r-1)}
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{h_k}{k(k+1)}
=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac1\ell\sum_{k\ge\ell}\frac1{k(k+1)}
=\sum_{\ell\ge1}\frac1{\ell^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

BCM prove monotonicity and a quantitative long-time estimate for harmonic descent. Applied to the clade occupation recursion, their dyadic step estimate gives

\[
0\le e_r(j)\le D:=\frac2{\log2}
\quad (j\ge r),
\tag{4.13}
\]

while for `j<r`, `e_r(j)=-j rho_r` and the same uniform bound is trivial. Hence

\[
\boxed{|e_r(j)|\le 2/\log2}
\tag{4.14}
\]

uniformly in `r,j`.

## 4.4 Uniform covariance forcing and limiting kernel — PASS

At a root split `I`, the linear part `rho_r n` is deterministic, so the forcing covariance depends only on

\[
R_r=e_r(I)+e_r(n-I).
\tag{4.15}
\]

From (4.14), `|R_r|<=2D`, hence

\[
|\eta_{rs}(n)|
\le \frac{4D^2}{n}
=\frac{16}{(\log2)^2}\frac1n.
\tag{4.16}
\]

Therefore

\[
\Sigma_{rs}:=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)\eta_{rs}(j),
\qquad
a(j)=\frac{6h_{j-1}}{\pi^2(j-1)},
\tag{4.17}
\]

is absolutely convergent entrywise.

Let

\[
d=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},\qquad
\alpha=1-d=d^2=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.
\tag{4.18}
\]

For `K=n^d`, split the Green sum at `K` and at the short-time boundary `n-j=j^d`. BCM's long-time estimate gives, for `j<=K`,

\[
0\le a(n,j)-a(j)\lesssim \frac{j}{n-j},
\tag{4.19}
\]

so after the `1/j` forcing weight the total error is `O(K/n)=O(n^{-alpha})`. In the middle region BCM's short-time estimate gives

\[
a(n,j)\lesssim \frac{1}{j^d h_j},
\tag{4.20}
\]

and the weighted sum is `O(K^{-d})=O(n^{-d^2})=O(n^{-alpha})`. The near-diagonal region contributes `O((log n)/n)`, and the limiting tail is faster than `n^{-alpha}`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{r,s\ge2}|C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}|=O(n^{-\alpha}).
}
\tag{4.21}
\]

This independently checks the strongest P13 covariance-kernel rate, not merely pointwise convergence.

## 4.5 Trace forcing and trace-class covariance — PASS

For `r<=s`, (4.13) gives `0<=e_r(s)<=D`. Also

\[
\sum_{r\le s}e_r(s)
=(s-1)-s\sum_{r\le s}\rho_r
=s\sum_{r>s}\rho_r-1
=O(\log s),
\tag{4.22}
\]

because `rho_r=O((log r)/r^2)`. Consequently

\[
\sum_{r\le s}e_r(s)^2\le D\sum_{r\le s}e_r(s)=O(1+\log s).
\tag{4.23}
\]

For `r>s`, `e_r(s)=-s rho_r`, and

\[
s^2\sum_{r>s}\rho_r^2=O((\log s)^2/s)=O(1+\log s).
\tag{4.24}
\]

Hence

\[
\|e(s)\|_{\ell^2}^2=O(1+\log s).
\tag{4.25}
\]

At a split, covariance trace is bounded by the second moment of `e(I)+e(j-I)`, so

\[
T_j:=\sum_{r\ge2}\eta_{rr}(j)
\le \frac1j E\|e(I)+e(j-I)\|_2^2
=O\!\left(\frac{1+\log(j+1)}{j}\right).
\tag{4.26}
\]

Together with `a(j)=O((log j)/j)`, (4.26) is summable against `a(j)` and gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}\Sigma=\sum_r\Sigma_{rr}<\infty.
\tag{4.27}
\]

Thus `Sigma` defines a centered Gaussian law on `ell^2`.

## 4.6 Fixed-dimensional scalar contraction and Cramer--Wold — PASS

For a finitely supported coefficient vector `t=(t_2,...,t_M)`, let

\[
X_n(t)=\sum_{r=2}^M t_r N_n(r).
\tag{4.28}
\]

It obeys the same recursive split structure with a deterministic toll `b_n(t)=t_n 1_{2<=n<=M}`, hence the toll is eventually zero. The mean is

\[
E X_n(t)=\rho_t n+O_t(1).
\tag{4.29}
\]

The normalized variance recursion has forcing `O_t(1/n)`. Applying the same long/short-time decomposition with the boundary `n-j=j^{1/6}` gives

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)}{n}
=\sigma_t^2+O_t(n^{-1/12}).
\tag{4.30}
\]

so the absolute variance error is `O_t(n^{11/12})`.

BCM Proposition 1.3 requires, for some `epsilon>0`, mean error `O(n^{1/2-epsilon})`, variance error `O(n^{1-epsilon})`, and toll third moment at most `n^{3/2-epsilon}`. Here (4.29), (4.30), and eventual vanishing of the toll satisfy those conditions for any fixed `epsilon<1/12`. If `sigma_t^2>0`, BCM therefore gives the scalar CLT. If `sigma_t^2=0`, (4.30) gives convergence to zero in `L^2` after `sqrt n` normalization. Cramer--Wold then yields joint Gaussian convergence for every fixed coordinate block.

This is the step that was not merely assumed from earlier P12 component audits: I checked the exact BCM hypothesis interface against the current v2 proposition.

## 4.7 Uniform Hilbert tail and full moving-vector CLT — PASS

For the zero-padded vector

\[
Z_n(r)=\frac{N_n(r)-E N_n(r)}{\sqrt n},
\qquad r\ge2,
\tag{4.31}
\]

let `Q_R` project onto coordinates `r>R`. By the Green formula and trace forcing,

\[
E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
=\sum_{j>R}a(n,j)T_j.
\tag{4.32}
\]

The BCM short-time estimate actually gives a slightly stronger audit bound than P13 needs. Away from the diagonal, `a(n,j)<=C/(j^d h_j)`, and (4.26) gives a summand `O(j^{-1-d})`; its tail is `O(R^{-d})`. Near the diagonal, writing `m=n-j`, the bound `a(n,n-m)<=C/(m h_{n-m})` gives total `O((log n)/n)` (with the `m=0` endpoint separately also `O((log n)/n)`). Uniformly in `n>=R`,

\[
\sup_n E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
=O(R^{-d}+R^{-1}\log R),
\tag{4.33}
\]

which in particular implies P13's stated

\[
\boxed{
\sup_n E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
=O(R^{-\alpha}\log R)\to0.
}
\tag{4.34}
\]

Fixed-dimensional convergence from section 4.6 plus (4.34) gives tightness in `ell^2` and identifies the unique limit as the centered Gaussian `G` with covariance `Sigma`:

\[
\boxed{Z_n\Longrightarrow G\quad\text{in }\ell^2.}
\tag{4.35}
\]

## 4.8 Exact-covariance Gaussian comparison and singular constraints — PASS

Let

\[
C_n=\operatorname{Cov}(Z_n),\qquad \Gamma_n=N_{\ell^2}(0,C_n).
\tag{4.36}
\]

The uniform covariance convergence and trace-tail control imply `Gamma_n=>G`. Since also `Z_n=>G`, the bounded-Lipschitz triangle inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
d_{BL}^{\ell^2}(\mathcal L(Z_n),\Gamma_n)\to0.
}
\tag{4.37}
\]

The finite-n Gaussian must be singular, and it is. Every rooted full binary `n`-leaf tree has exactly `n-1` internal clades and the root is the unique size-`n` clade, so

\[
N_n(n)=1,\qquad
\sum_{r=2}^{n-1}N_n(r)=n-2
\tag{4.38}
\]

deterministically. Hence

\[
Z_n(n)=0,\qquad
\sum_{r=2}^{n-1}Z_n(r)=0
\tag{4.39}
\]

almost surely. These deterministic zero linear functionals lie in `ker C_n`, so `Gamma_n` satisfies the same constraints almost surely. No artificial nonsingularity assumption is hidden in (4.37).
