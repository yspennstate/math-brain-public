# 6. Hostile self-check 1: lower-side disjoint-basis extraction

A possible audit failure would be that the sparse-kernel event does not actually furnish the `2q` disjoint coordinate bases needed by the finite-convolution projection theorem. I rechecked that implication explicitly.

Let `A` be an orthonormal row frame for the `k`-dimensional fresh projection space. Suppose every unit vector `A^*u` stays at distance at least `tau` from every coordinate subspace supported on at most `(2q-1)k` coordinates. Let `R` be the union of coordinates already used by earlier extracted bases, so `|R|<=(2q-1)k`. Then for every unit `u`,

\[
\|A_{R^c}^*u\|_2
=\operatorname{dist}(A^*u,\mathbb F^R)
\ge\tau.
\]

Therefore

\[
A_{R^c}A_{R^c}^*\succeq\tau^2I_k.
\]

Cauchy–Binet yields

\[
\sum_{S\subset R^c,\ |S|=k}|\det A_S|^2
=
\det(A_{R^c}A_{R^c}^*)
\ge\tau^{2k},
\]

so some fresh coordinate basis satisfies

\[
|\det A_S|
\ge
\frac{\tau^k}{\sqrt{\binom{|R^c|}{k}}}
\ge
\frac{\tau^k}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}.
\]

Iterating `2q` times is legitimate because `q` is fixed. With the canonical `tau` polynomially small in `m`, the accumulated determinant loss is `exp(O_q(k log m))`, hence `e^{o(m)}` under `k log m=o(m)`. I found no missing exponential loss here.
