# 7. Fresh-row fixed-rank small ball by leverage pruning + Bentkus

We now prove `P12-B4-BENTKUS-SMALLBALL-46`.

Fix one admissible residual space `E_A` satisfying `(0.7)` and a `d`-dimensional subspace

\[
V\subset E_A.
\]

Choose an orthonormal basis `e_1,...,e_d` of `V`. For each coordinate `j`, define the coefficient vector

\[
a_j=(e_1(j),...,e_d(j))\in\mathbb R^d.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{j=1}^m a_ja_j^T=I_d,
\qquad
\sum_j\|a_j\|_2^2=d.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Set

\[
L_d=\frac{2d}{\alpha},
\qquad
J=\{j:\|a_j\|_2^2>L_d/m\},
\qquad
I=J^c.
\tag{7.3}
\]

By `(7.2)`,

\[
|J|\frac{L_d}{m}<d,
\]

so

\[
|J|<\alpha m/2.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Let

\[
\Sigma_I=\sum_{j\in I}a_ja_j^T.
\tag{7.5}
\]

For any unit `y in R^d`, the vector

\[
w=\sum_{\ell=1}^dy_\ell e_\ell
\]

is a unit vector in `E_A`. Thus `(0.7)` applied to `J` gives

\[
y^T\Sigma_Iy
=
\|w_I\|_2^2
\ge\theta^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Sigma_I\succeq\theta^2I_d.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For `j in I`, put

\[
b_j=\Sigma_I^{-1/2}a_j.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{j\in I}b_jb_j^T=I_d,
\qquad
\sum_{j\in I}\|b_j\|_2^2=d,
\tag{7.7}
\]

and by `(7.3)`--`(7.6)`,

\[
\max_{j\in I}\|b_j\|_2
\le
\theta^{-1}\sqrt{\frac{2d}{\alpha m}}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Because `E|xi|^2=1` and `E|xi|^4<=B`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
E|\xi|^3\le\sqrt B.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Hence the Lyapunov quantity satisfies

\[
\sum_{j\in I}E\|b_j\xi_j\|_2^3
\le
\sqrt B
\left(\max_{j\in I}\|b_j\|_2\right)
\sum_{j\in I}\|b_j\|_2^2
\le
C_{B,\gamma}\frac{d^{3/2}}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

We now use the current primary theorem of V. Bentkus, *A Lyapunov-type Bound in R^d*, Theory of Probability and Its Applications 49(2) (2005), 311--323, DOI `10.1137/S0040585X97981123`: for independent centered vectors with invertible sum covariance, the supremum error over convex sets is at most an absolute constant times `d^{1/4}` times the whitened third-moment sum.

Applied to

\[
S_I=\sum_{j\in I}a_j\xi_j,
\]

this gives, uniformly over convex `C subset R^d`,

\[
\left|
P\{S_I\in C\}-P\{G_I\in C\}
\right|
\le
C_{B,\gamma}\frac{d^{7/4}}{\sqrt m},
\tag{7.11}
\]

where `G_I` is centered Gaussian with covariance `Sigma_I`.

Now condition on all high-leverage coordinates `xi_j`, `j in J`. They only translate the target ball. The Gaussian density is bounded by

\[
(2\pi)^{-d/2}(\det\Sigma_I)^{-1/2}
\le
(2\pi)^{-d/2}\theta^{-d}.
\tag{7.12}
\]

The `d`-ball of radius `t sqrt(d)` has volume at most

\[
\left(\sqrt{2\pi e}\,t\right)^d.
\]

Therefore the Gaussian ball mass is at most `(C_gamma t)^d`. Combining with `(7.11)` and integrating the conditioned high-leverage coordinates proves `(0.8)`.

### 7.1 One-normal specialization

For `d=1`, the same argument gives

\[
\sup_zP\{|\langle X,v\rangle-z|\le t\mid (A_m)_A\}
\le C_{B,\gamma}(t+m^{-1/2}).
\tag{7.13}
\]

This is an **all-center** microscopic statement for the fresh row, unlike the inherited population input `(5.2)`, which was only a fixed-radius homogeneous gap.

It is, however, useful only down to the CLT error floor `m^{-1/2}`.

# 8. Two-normal projective small ball

We prove `P12-B4-PAIR-PROJECTIVE-BE-46`.

Fix unit `u,v in E_A` with

\[
\rho=\langle u,v\rangle,
\qquad
\Delta=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}>0.
\]

Let

\[
e_1=u,
\qquad
e_2=\frac{v-\rho u}{\Delta}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then `e_1,e_2` are orthonormal and

\[
\langle X,v\rangle=\rho\langle X,e_1\rangle+\Delta\langle X,e_2\rangle.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Repeat the leverage pruning from Section 7 with `d=2`. Conditional on the high-leverage coordinates, the remaining two-dimensional sum is within `C_{B,gamma}/sqrt(m)` in convex-set probability of a Gaussian whose covariance is bounded below by `theta^2 I_2`.

The event

\[
|\langle X,u\rangle-z_1|\le t_1,
\qquad
|\langle X,v\rangle-z_2|\le t_2
\]

becomes, in the `(e_1,e_2)` output coordinates, a translated parallelogram. The linear map

\[
(y_1,y_2)\mapsto(y_1,\rho y_1+\Delta y_2)
\]

has determinant `Delta`, so that parallelogram has exact area

\[
\frac{4t_1t_2}{\Delta}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The Gaussian density ceiling from `(7.12)` at `d=2` is at most

\[
(2\pi\theta^2)^{-1}.
\]

Thus the Gaussian contribution is at most

\[
C_{B,\gamma}\frac{t_1t_2}{\Delta}.
\]

Bentkus contributes `C_{B,gamma}/sqrt(m)`, proving `(0.10)`.

=
(1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2)^{-1/2}.

The weak-moment obstruction is the **additive local-CLT error**, not a missing angle determinant.
