# 6. `P12-SMOOTH-LOWLEVERAGE-LOCALCLT-50`: fixed-dimensional density theorem

This theorem is proved directly by Fourier inversion; no black-box local CLT is imported.

Let `d` be fixed, and assume `(0.5)--(0.7)`.

## 6.1 Small-frequency expansion

Finite third moment gives, uniformly for small `s`,

\[
\phi(s)=1-\frac{s^2}{2}+O(E|\xi|^3|s|^3).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Choose `s_0>0` so small that for `|s|<=s_0`,

\[
|\phi(s)|\le e^{-c_0s^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
\Phi_m(t)
=
E e^{i\langle t,Z_m\rangle}
=
\prod_{i=1}^M\phi(\langle b_i,t\rangle).
\]

Whenever `|t|<=s_0 sqrt(m)/L`, every scalar argument lies in the small zone. A telescoping comparison with

\[
G_m(t)
=
\prod_i e^{-\langle b_i,t\rangle^2/2}
=e^{-|t|^2/2}
\]

gives

\[
|\Phi_m(t)-e^{-|t|^2/2}|
\le
C\sum_i|\langle b_i,t\rangle|^3
\exp\left[-c\sum_{\ell\ne i}\langle b_\ell,t\rangle^2\right].
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because

\[
\sum_i\langle b_i,t\rangle^2=|t|^2
\]

and

\[
\max_i|\langle b_i,t\rangle|
\le L|t|/\sqrt m,
\]

we have

\[
\sum_i|\langle b_i,t\rangle|^3
\le
\frac{L}{\sqrt m}|t|^3.
\tag{6.4}
\]

In `(6.3)`, deleting one summand from the quadratic exponent loses at most the fixed amount `s_0^2`, so

\[
\boxed{
|\Phi_m(t)-e^{-|t|^2/2}|
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt m}|t|^3e^{-c|t|^2}
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

throughout the small-frequency zone. Multiplying by `1+|t|` and integrating costs `O(m^{-1/2})`.

## 6.2 Direction-uniform supply of nontrivial factors

For a unit direction `theta`, put

\[
c_i=\langle b_i,\theta\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i c_i^2=1,
\qquad
|c_i|\le L/\sqrt m.
\]

Let

\[
J_\theta
=
\left\{i:|c_i|\ge(2m)^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]

If `q=|J_theta|`, then

\[
1
\le
\frac{qL^2}{m}+rac{m-q}{2m},
\]
so

\[
\boxed{|J_\theta|\ge\frac{m}{2L^2-1}.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Absolute continuity implied by `(0.6)` gives `|phi(s)|<1` for `s!=0`, and `(0.6)` implies `phi(s)->0`. Therefore, for every fixed `a>0`,

\[
\rho(a):=\sup_{|s|\ge a}|\phi(s)|<1.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For `|t|` above a fixed multiple of `sqrt(m)`, every index in `J_theta` therefore contributes a factor at most one fixed `rho<1`.

## 6.3 Integrable tail: one dimension

When `d=1`, some coefficient satisfies

\[

\]

Removing that factor leaves at least `c_Lm-1` factors bounded by `rho`. Hence

\[
\int_{\text{large }t}(1+|t|)|\Phi_m(t)|dt
\le
\rho^{c_Lm-1}
\int_{\mathbb R}(1+|t|)|\phi(b_{i_0}t)|dt.

By `(0.6)` and `|b_{i_0}|>=m^{-1/2}`, the last integral is polynomial in `m`, while the prefactor is exponentially small.

## 6.4 Integrable tail: arbitrary fixed dimension

For general fixed `d`, Cauchy--Binet gives

\[
1
=
\det\left(\sum_i b_ib_i^T\right)
=
\sum_{I:\,|I|=d}\det(B_I)^2,
\tag{6.8}
\]

where `B_I` is the `d x d` matrix with the selected coefficient columns. Therefore one `d`-tuple `I_0` satisfies

\[
|\det B_{I_0}|
\ge
\binom Md^{-1/2}
\ge c_d m^{-d/2}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Remove those `d` factors. At a large frequency, `(6.6)` leaves at least `c_Lm-d` factors bounded by `rho`. Changing variables

\[
y=B_{I_0}^Tt
\]

in the remaining `d` selected factors costs at most a fixed power of `m` by `(6.9)` and the coefficient upper bound. The weighted integral

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}(1+|t|)
\prod_{i\in I_0}|\phi(\langle b_i,t\rangle)|dt
\]

is therefore at most polynomial in `m`, because `(0.6)` supplies both `||phi||_1` and `|| |s|phi(s)||_1`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\text{large }t}(1+|t|)|\Phi_m(t)|dt
\le \operatorname{poly}_d(m)e^{-c_{\xi,L}m}.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Together with the Gaussian tail and `(6.5)`, this gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^d}(1+|t|)
|\Phi_m(t)-e^{-|t|^2/2}|dt
\le Cm^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Fourier inversion gives `(0.8)`.

### Why the smoothness assumption is declared rather than inferred

For one fixed absolutely continuous law, the Cramér gap `(6.7)` is automatic. For a triangular family, neither a uniform Cramér gap nor a uniform weighted Fourier integral follows merely from saying each density is smooth. The retained narrow-smoothed-Rademacher counterexamples exploit exactly such degeneration. A uniform triangular version of `(0.8)` therefore requires uniform versions of `(0.5)`, `(0.6)`, and `(6.7)`.

# 7. Low-leverage extraction from incompressibility

The Fourier theorem requires `(0.9)`. Classical incompressibility supplies it deterministically for every fixed-dimensional residual subspace.

Let `E subset R^n` be a `d`-dimensional subspace and let `W in R^{d x n}` have orthonormal rows spanning `E`. Write `w_i in R^d` for its `i`th column, so

\[
\sum_iw_iw_i^T=I_d.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Assume there are fixed `alpha,eta>0` such that every unit `x in E` has Euclidean distance greater than `eta` from the unit vectors supported on at most `alpha n` coordinates.

For every coordinate set `J` with `|J|<=alpha n`, this implies

\[
\boxed{
\|x_{J^c}\|_2^2
>\theta^2
:=
\eta^2-\frac{\eta^4}{4}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

for every unit `x in E`. Indeed, if `v=||x_{J^c}||` and `x_J` is normalized, then

\[
\operatorname{dist}(x,x_J/\|x_J\|)^2
=
2-2\sqrt{1-v^2};
\]

this is at most `eta^2` whenever `v^2<=eta^2-eta^4/4`.

Now fix

\[
L_0> d/\alpha
\]

and define the high-leverage set

\[
H=\{i:\|w_i\|_2^2>L_0/n\}.
\]

Since the leverages sum to `d`,

\[
|H|<dn/L_0<\alpha n.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let `I=H^c` and

\[
Q_I=\sum_{i\in I}w_iw_i^T.
\]

For every unit `a in R^d`, `x=W^Ta` is a unit vector in `E`, and `(7.2)` gives

\[
a^TQ_Ia
=\|x_I\|_2^2
\ge\theta^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
Q_I\succeq\theta^2I_d,
\qquad
\max_{i\in I}\|w_i\|_2\le\sqrt{L_0/n}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is exactly `(0.9)`.

The retained `P13-B4-GLOBAL-LEFTCOMP-45` gives the required incompressibility on one shared event for every candidate leave-one-out subspace. It is independent of `B_exact`: any finite uniform fourth-moment ceiling and bounded macroscopic aspect ratio suffice.

# 8. Projective local density module

## 8.1 One residual subspace

Let `E` have fixed dimension `d` and satisfy `(7.4)`. If `X` is a genuinely fresh iid row/column with law `xi`, choose an orthonormal basis `W` of `E`. Then

\[
WX=\sum_iw_i\xi_i.
\]

The subset theorem `(0.10)` gives a uniform density bound. In particular, for every `0<t<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
P\{\|P_EX\|_2\le t\}
\le C_dt^d.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

There is no `m^{-1/2}` additive remainder and no arithmetic floor.

For `d=1`, the lower bound in `(0.10)` also gives

\[
ct\le P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t\}\le Ct
\tag{8.2}
\]

for every candidate normal `u` on the global incompressibility event, uniformly down to exponentially small `t`.

## 8.2 Two vectors and the exact projective determinant

Let `u,v` be real unit vectors with

\[
\rho=\langle u,v\rangle,
\qquad |\rho|<1,
\qquad
\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Suppose the whitened two-dimensional span

\[
W=\Sigma^{-1/2}
\begin{pmatrix}u^T\\v^T\end{pmatrix}
\]

satisfies the common-span incompressibility hypothesis of Section 7. Then `Z=WX` has a `C^1` density satisfying `(0.10)`. For

\[
Y=(\langle X,u\rangle,\langle X,v\rangle)=\Sigma^{1/2}Z,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
\le f_Y(0)
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
\frac{\sqrt{t_1^2+t_2^2}}{\sqrt{1-|\rho|}}
\le c_0,
\tag{8.4}
\]

then the gradient bound in `(0.10)` implies

\[
\boxed{
 c\frac{t_1t_2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
\le
P\left\{
|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t_1,
|\langle X,v\rangle|\le t_2
\right\}
\le
 C\frac{t_1t_2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Indeed, the rectangle in `Y`-coordinates has image under `Sigma^{-1/2}` of area

\[
\frac{4t_1t_2}{\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}
=
\frac{4t_1t_2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}},
\]

and `(8.4)` keeps that image inside one fixed ball where the whitened density is bounded above and below.

This is the source-correct projective singularity. The theorem does **not** assert that an arbitrary pair of adaptive normals automatically has common-span incompressibility; that is a separate overlap-geometry obligation. When two normals lie in one already-controlled residual subspace, the hypothesis is automatic.

# 9. Proof of `P12-SMOOTH-FIXEDR-SOURCE-LOWER-50`

\,\middle|\,
A_{m,-j}
\right\}

\tag{9.1}

for all `0<s<=1`, with one fixed constant independent of `T,j,m`.

\tag{9.2}

Therefore

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
