# 4. Elementary binomial moderate-deviation bracket

This section proves `(0.5)` without importing a moderate-deviation theorem.

Let
\[
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,q),
\qquad
n=N\beta\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
0<q\le\beta,
\]
with
\[
\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\Subset(0,1).
\]

## 4.1 Chernoff upper bound

For every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
P\{X\ge n\}
\le
e^{-\lambda n}
(1-q+qe^\lambda)^N.
\]
Optimizing at
\[
e^\lambda
=
\frac{\beta(1-q)}{q(1-\beta)}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
B_{N,n}(q)
\le
e^{-ND(\beta\|q)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.2 Exact mass at the threshold

The mass at \(n\) satisfies
\[
P_q\{X=n\}
=
\binom Nn q^n(1-q)^{N-n}.
\]
Since
\[
e^{-ND(\beta\|q)}
=
\left(\frac q\beta\right)^n
\left(\frac{1-q}{1-\beta}\right)^{N-n},
\]
we have the exact factorization
\[
P_q\{X=n\}
=
\left[
\binom Nn\beta^n(1-\beta)^{N-n}
\right]
e^{-ND(\beta\|q)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Uniform Stirling on
\(
\beta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]
\)
gives
\[
\boxed{
P_q\{X=n\}
\ge
c_0N^{-1/2}
e^{-ND(\beta\|q)}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.3 Gain from summing the first tail masses

Put
\[
d=\beta-q.
\]

If \(d\ge\beta_-/4\), then
\[
1+\sqrt N d\asymp\sqrt N
\]
and `(4.3)` already gives the desired lower bound.

Assume now
\[
0\le d<\beta_-/4.
\]
Then \(q\) stays in a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,1)\).

Put
\[
\epsilon=d+N^{-1/2},
\qquad
J=\left\lfloor \frac{c_1}{\epsilon}\right\rfloor,
\]
with \(c_1>0\) sufficiently small depending only on
\(\beta_-,\beta_+\).

For
\[
p_j=P\{X=n+j\},
\]
the adjacent-mass ratio is
\[
\frac{p_{j+1}}{p_j}
=
\frac{N-n-j}{n+j+1}
\frac q{1-q}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For \(0\le j\le J\),
\[
\frac jN
\le
C\epsilon,
\]
and all arguments in `(4.4)` stay in one compact subset of \((0,1)\). At \(q=\beta\), \(j=0\), the ratio is \(1+O(N^{-1})\). A uniform first-order bound therefore gives
\[
\log\frac{p_{j+1}}{p_j}
\ge
-C\epsilon.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Consequently, for every \(0\le j\le J\),
\[
p_j
\ge
p_0e^{-C\epsilon j}
\ge
c_2p_0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Summing gives
\[
B_{N,n}(q)
\ge
c_2(J+1)p_0.
\]
Using `(4.3)` and
\[
J+1\asymp\frac1{d+N^{-1/2}},
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
B_{N,n}(q)
\ge
c\,
\frac{
e^{-ND(\beta\|q)}
}{
1+\sqrt N d
}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Together `(4.1)` and `(4.7)` prove `P12-BINOMIAL-MODERATE-BRACKET-40`.
