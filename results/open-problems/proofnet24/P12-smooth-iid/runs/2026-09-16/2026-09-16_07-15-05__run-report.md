# 6. New real slab growth bound

Fix a coordinate support `J` of size `j` and identify a row restriction with `z in R^j`.

Consider the slab class

\[
\mathcal S_{j,\rho}
=
\left\{
S_c=\{z:|\langle z,c\rangle|\le\rho\}:
\|c\|_2=1
\right\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

## `P12-REAL-SLAB-GROWTH-44` — NEW / PROVED

For any fixed points `z_1,...,z_M in R^j`, the number of distinct membership patterns

\[
\bigl(1_{S_c}(z_1),\ldots,1_{S_c}(z_M)\bigr)
\]

as `c` varies satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_j(M)
\le
\sum_{t=0}^j2^t\binom{2M}{t}
\le
\left(\frac{8eM}{j}\right)^j,
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

whenever `1<=j<=M`.

### Proof

For each sample point `z_i`, membership in the slab is determined by the signs of the two affine functions of the parameter `c`:

\[
\langle z_i,c\rangle-\rho,
\qquad
\langle z_i,c\rangle+\rho.
\]

Thus membership can change only across an arrangement of `2M` affine hyperplanes in the `j` coefficient variables.

Let `F(H,j)` be the maximum total number of faces of all dimensions in an arrangement of `H` affine hyperplanes in `R^j`. Adding the last hyperplane gives the elementary recurrence

\[
F(H,j)\le F(H-1,j)+2F(H-1,j-1),
\]

with `F(H,0)=1`. Hence

\[
F(H,j)\le\sum_{t=0}^j2^t\binom Ht.
\]

Restricting the parameter to the unit sphere can only reduce the number of patterns. Taking `H=2M` proves the first inequality.

For `j<=M`, the terms increase up to `j`; using

\[
\binom{2M}{t}\le\left(\frac{2eM}{t}\right)^t
\]

and `j+1<=2^j` gives the displayed coarse bound `(6.2)`.

QED.

No external VC-dimension or semialgebraic theorem is used.

# 7. New uniform empirical slab lemma

Let `Z_1,...,Z_N` be iid `R^j`-valued random vectors, and suppose

\[
\sup_{\|c\|_2=1}P\{|\langle Z,c\rangle|\le\rho\}
\le q.
\tag{7.1}

Let

\[
P_N(S)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N1_{\{Z_i\in S\}}.

## `P12-REAL-SLAB-VC-DEVIATION-44` — NEW / PROVED

For every `eta>0` with `N eta^2>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\sup_{S\in\mathcal S_{j,\rho}}
(P_N(S)-P(S))>\eta
\right\}
\le
8\,\Pi_j(2N)
\exp\left(-\frac{N\eta^2}{32}\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Self-contained proof

Take an independent ghost sample `Z_1',...,Z_N'`. On the event

\[
\sup_S(P_N(S)-P(S))>\eta,
\]

choose an almost-maximizing slab. Conditional Chebyshev for the ghost empirical mass gives the standard one-sided symmetrization reduction, with the harmless factor `2`, to

\[
\sup_S(P_N(S)-P_N'(S))>\eta/2.
\]

Condition on the pooled `2N` observations and randomly swap each pair `(Z_i,Z_i')` with an independent Rademacher sign. For any fixed membership pattern, the empirical difference is

\[
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N
\varepsilon_i a_i,
\qquad |a_i|\le1.
\]

Hoeffding bounds its one-sided probability by `exp(-N eta^2/8)`. Union over the at most `Pi_j(2N)` pooled membership patterns and loosen constants to absorb the symmetrization and boundary/separability bookkeeping. This gives `(7.2)`.

The slab class is a finite-dimensional Borel-parametric class, so the usual measurable separability reduction applies; equivalently the argument may be read in outer probability.

QED.

Combining `(6.2)` and `(7.2)`,

\[
P\left\{
\sup_c(P_N(S_c)-P(S_c))>\eta
\right\}
\le
8\left(\frac{16eN}{j}\right)^j
\exp\left(-\frac{N\eta^2}{32}\right).
\tag{7.3}

# 8. Proof of the linear-support global theorem

Return to the iid matrix rows

\[
R_i=(\xi_{i1},\ldots,\xi_{im}).
\]

Use `(5.1)` with

\[
q=\beta-\delta.

For each fixed support `J`, apply `(7.3)` with

\[
\eta=\delta/2.

Then, except on an event of probability at most

\[
8\left(\frac{16eN_m}{j}\right)^j
\exp\left(-\frac{N_m\delta^2}{128}\right),
\tag{8.1}
\]

every unit vector supported on `J` satisfies

\[
\frac1{N_m}
\#\{i:|\langle R_i,x\rangle|\le\rho\}
\le
\beta-\frac\delta2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Now union over all `j`-coordinate supports. Since

\[
\binom mj\le\left(\frac{em}{j}\right)^j,
\]

the entropy factor is at most

\[
\left(
\frac{16e^2mN_m}{j^2}
\right)^j.
\tag{8.3}
\]

For all sufficiently large `m`,

\[
\frac\gamma2m\le N_m\le2\gamma m.
\]

Put `j=xm`. The logarithm of `(8.3)`, divided by `m`, is at most

\[
\Psi_\gamma(x)
:=x\log\frac{32e^2\gamma}{x^2}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Since

\[
\Psi_\gamma(x)\downarrow0
\qquad(x\downarrow0),
\]

choose one fixed `alpha>0` so small that

\[
\sup_{0<x\le\alpha}\Psi_\gamma(x)
\le\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{512}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

The deviation exponent in `(8.1)` is at least

\[
\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{256}m.
\]

Thus after summing over `1<=j<=alpha m`, the total failure probability is at most

\[
8m\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma\delta^2}{512}m\right)
\le e^{-cm}
\tag{8.6}
\]

for a constant `c=c(B,gamma)>0` and all sufficiently large `m`.

This proves `(0.4)`.

Let

\[
n_m=m+k_m-1,

\frac{n_m}{N_m}\to\beta.

## 8.2 All leave-one-out hyperplane normals

Likewise

\[
\frac{m-1}{N_m}\to\beta.

No row-subset union is ever performed: once a coefficient vector has enough successful rows, the candidate row set is chosen afterward.

\bigl(v,\operatorname{Sparse}(\alpha m)\cap S^{m-1}\bigr)

\tag{9.4}
