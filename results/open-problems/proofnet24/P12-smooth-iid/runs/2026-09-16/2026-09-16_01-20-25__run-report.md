# 4. Proof of `P12-RARECONTAM-TRANSFER-38`

Fix \(\mathbb F\), the base law \(X\), and the bounded smooth centered bump \(Z\).

Let \(I_{ij}\) be iid Bernoulli\((p_m)\) indicators used to couple the full \(N_m\times m\) matrix. On entry \((i,j)\), use \(X_{ij}\) when \(I_{ij}=0\) and \(\delta_mZ_{ij}\) when \(I_{ij}=1\), then divide every entry by the deterministic \(\sigma_m\).

Because \(EX=EZ=0\) and \(E|X|^2=1\),
\[
\sigma_m^2
=(1-p_m)+p_m\delta_m^2v.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Hence \(\sigma_m\to1\) whenever \(p_m\to0\).

## 4.1 Probability exponent survives

Let
\[
G_m=\{I_{ij}=0\ \text{for all }1\le i\le N_m,\ 1\le j\le m\}.
\]
Then
\[
P(G_m^c)
\le N_mm p_m
\to0
\tag{4.2}
\]
by `(0.9)`.

On \(G_m\), the entire matrix is exactly
\[
A_m=\sigma_m^{-1}A_m^{(X)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
-\frac{k_m}{m}\log M(A_m)
=
-\frac{k_m}{m}\log M(A_m^{(X)})
+
\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Since \(\log\sigma_m=O(p_m)\) and \(k_m=o(m)\), the last term tends to zero. Together with `P(G_m^c)\to0`, `(0.7)` implies `(0.10)`.

This transfer uses no candidate union and no perturbative Weyl inequality. With probability \(1-o(1)\), there is literally no contaminated entry anywhere in the matrix.

## 4.2 A predetermined candidate produces the deep logarithmic tail

Eventually \(n_m=m+k_m-1\le N_m\). Let \(T_0\) be the first \(n_m\) rows and consider the first column.

Define
\[
E_m=\{I_{i1}=1\text{ for every }i\in T_0\}.
\]
Independence gives
\[
P(E_m)=p_m^{n_m}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Let \(R\) be a deterministic support radius for \(Z\). On \(E_m\), the first column of \((A_m)_{T_0}\) has Euclidean norm at most
\[
\frac{R\sqrt{n_m}\,\delta_m}{\sigma_m}.
\]
Testing the least singular value on the first coordinate vector gives
\[
M_{m,r_m}^{\mathbb F}(A_m)
\le
\frac{R\sqrt{n_m}\,e^{-L_m}}{\sigma_m}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus on \(E_m\),
\[
W_m
\ge
\frac{k_m}{m}
\left[
L_m-rac12\log n_m+\log\sigma_m-\log R
\right].
\tag{4.7}
\]
Since \(L_m\to\infty\), for the explicit choice `(0.14)` and all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
W_m\ge\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Therefore for every fixed \(s\ge1\),
\[
E(W_m^+)^s
\ge
p_m^{n_m}
\left(\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}\right)^s.
\tag{4.9}
\]
This proves `(0.12)` whenever `(0.11)` holds.

## 4.3 One explicit sequence destroys all finite logarithmic moments

Take `(0.14)`. Because \(k_m\log m=o(m)\), we have \(k_m=o(m)\), hence
\[
n_m=m+o(m).
\tag{4.10}
\]
For fixed \(s\ge1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\left[
p_m^{n_m}
\left(\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}\right)^s\right]
&=-(m+C_0)n_m+3sm^2+s\log\frac{k_m}{2m}\\

# 5. Smooth fourth-moment budgets arbitrarily close to one

The transfer theorem becomes a sharp moment-level counterexample once the base law is chosen with fourth moment below an arbitrary \(B>1\).

## 5.1 Real base

Let \(\varepsilon\) be balanced Rademacher and let \(Z_0\) be independent, symmetric, supported in \([-1,1]\), with a \(C_c^\infty\) density. Put
\[
v=EZ_0^2,
\qquad
w=EZ_0^4,
\]
and
\[
X_\eta=
\frac{\varepsilon+\eta Z_0}{\sqrt{1+\eta^2v}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Then \(EX_\eta=0\), \(EX_\eta^2=1\), \(X_\eta\) has a bounded compactly-supported smooth density, and
\[
EX_\eta^4
=
\frac{1+6\eta^2v+\eta^4w}{(1+\eta^2v)^2}
\longrightarrow1.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus for every \(B>1\) one may fix \(\eta\) so that
\[
\beta:=EX_\eta^4<B.
\]

## 5.2 Complex planar base

Let \(\Omega\) be uniform on
\[
\{1,-1,i,-i\}
\]
and let \(Z_0\) be independent, circularly symmetric, supported in the unit disk, with a planar \(C_c^\infty\) density. Put
\[
v=E|Z_0|^2,
\qquad
w=E|Z_0|^4,
\]
and
\[
X_\eta=
\frac{\Omega+\eta Z_0}{\sqrt{1+\eta^2v}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
EX_\eta=0,
\qquad
E|X_\eta|^2=1,
\qquad
EX_\eta^2=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]
So the real covariance of \(X_\eta\) is exactly \(I_2/2\). The density is a finite mixture of four translated scaled copies of the planar bump density and is therefore \(C_c^\infty\) and bounded.

Circular symmetry gives
\[
E|\Omega+\eta Z_0|^4
=1+4\eta^2v+\eta^4w,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
E|X_\eta|^4
=
\frac{1+4\eta^2v+\eta^4w}{(1+\eta^2v)^2}
\longrightarrow1.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Again every budget \(B>1\) admits one fixed \(\eta\) with base fourth moment \(\beta<B\).

Because these base laws have bounded densities, they satisfy the retained fixed-FC theorem with \(q=1\).

## 5.3 Uniform fourth moment after rare contamination

For either field, let \(X\) be the chosen base with
\[
E|X|^4=\beta<B.
\]
For the raw mixture `(0.8)`, if
\[
w_Z=E|Z|^4,
\]
then
\[
E|\xi_m|^4
=
\frac{(1-p_m)\beta+p_m\delta_m^4w_Z}
{[(1-p_m)+p_m\delta_m^2v]^2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
The right side converges to \(\beta\) uniformly for \(0\le\delta_m\le1\) as \(p_m\to0\). Therefore one fixed sufficiently large \(C_0\) in `(0.14)` guarantees
\[
\boxed{\sup_mE|\xi_m|^4<B.}
\tag{5.7}
\]
The same bounded support gives a common subgaussian norm and uniformly bounded moments of all orders.

This proves the regularity assertions in `P12-COMPLEX-B4-PROB-NOT-LS-38` and the real phase-retrieval corollary.
