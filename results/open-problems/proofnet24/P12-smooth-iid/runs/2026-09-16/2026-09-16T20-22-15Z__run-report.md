## 4. New-method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: `(FC)` as a convolution-semigroup smoothing budget

The finite-convolution hypothesis becomes more transparent if one temporarily stops viewing it as a random-matrix tail condition and views it as an `L^infinity` smoothing statement for the additive convolution semigroup.

### `P12-FC-SEMIGROUP-58` — PROVED

If `f` is a probability density on `R^d` and

\[
\|f^{*q}\|_\infty\le M
\]

for one integer `q>=1`, then for every integer `n>=q`,

\[
\boxed{\|f^{*n}\|_\infty\le M.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Write

\[
f^{*n}=f^{*q}*f^{*(n-q)}.
\]

The second factor is a probability density, and convolution with a probability density contracts `L^infinity`:

\[
\|h*p\|_\infty
\le\|h\|_\infty\|p\|_1
=\|h\|_\infty.
\]

Apply this with `h=f^{*q}`. QED.

This simple category change explains why the proof is permitted to group coordinates into a fixed number of independent blocks. `(FC)` is not asserting that one raw coordinate has a bounded density; it asserts that **a fixed additive block has acquired a bounded density**, and every larger additive block retains that ceiling.

It also gives a clean audit warning: any proof step that replaces `(FC)` by a raw-coordinate linear small-ball estimate is stronger than the hypothesis and must be separately justified.

### Affine normalization

Let `X` have density `f` on `R^d`, let `Y=(X-a)/sigma` with `sigma>0`, and let `g` be the density of `Y`. Then the q-fold sum satisfies

\[
Y_1+\cdots+Y_q
=\frac{(X_1+\cdots+X_q)-qa}{\sigma},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\|g^{*q}\|_\infty
=\sigma^d\|f^{*q}\|_\infty.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the real/complex scalar fields this is exactly `d=d_F`. Thus the finite-convolution ceiling must be checked **after** variance normalization for a triangular family; a raw pre-normalization ceiling is not an invariant statement.

The singular-value statistic itself scales exactly:

\[
M_{m,r}(\sigma A)=\sigma M_{m,r}(A),
\]

so with `k_m=r_m+1` the normalized logarithmic statistic shifts by

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\]

Hence the exact exponent-neutral triangular scaling condition remains

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Centering is separate: a scalar rescaling does not remove a nonzero mean.

## 5. New-method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the scalar `r^{d/q}` anti-concentration exponent is sharp

The frozen package uses the elementary consequences

\[
Q_\xi(r)\lesssim r^{1/q}
\quad\text{in }\mathbb R,
\qquad
Q_\xi(r)\lesssim r^{2/q}
\quad\text{in }\mathbb C.
\]

A potential hidden proof error would be to silently strengthen this to a raw-coordinate density bound, i.e. `Q(r)=O(r^d)`. The following smooth centered variance-one family shows that such a strengthening is false even with compact support and all moments.

Work in `R^d`, where `d=1` or `2`. Fix centered `C_c^infinity` probability densities `u,g` with

\[
\operatorname{supp}(u)\subset B(0,1),
\qquad
E_g|X|^2=1,
\]

and write

\[
U=\|u\|_\infty,
\qquad
G=\|g\|_\infty,
\qquad
v_u=E_u|X|^2<\infty.
\]

For `0<delta<1`, put

\[
u_\delta(x)=\delta^{-d}u(x/\delta),
\qquad
p_\delta=c\,\delta^{d/q},
\]

where `c>0` is fixed small enough that `p_delta<=1/2`, and define

\[
f_\delta
=p_\delta u_\delta+(1-p_\delta)g.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### `P12-FC-SCALAR-SHARPNESS-58` — PROVED

For every fixed `q>=1`, this family satisfies all of the following.

1. `f_delta` is `C_c^infinity` and centered.
2. Its q-fold convolution has a uniform density ceiling:
   \[
   \boxed{
   \sup_{0<\delta<\delta_0}\|f_\delta^{*q}\|_\infty
   \le G+c^qU.
   }
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
3. At the microscopic radius `delta`,
   \[
   \boxed{
   Q_{f_\delta}(\delta)
   \ge p_\delta
   =c\delta^{d/q}.
   }
   \tag{5.3}
   \]
4. After a scalar normalization tending to one, the laws have variance one while preserving a uniform `(FC)` ceiling and the same `delta^{d/q}` lower bound up to constants.

**Proof of (5.2).** Expand the q-fold convolution by the binomial theorem:

\[
f_\delta^{*q}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{q}
{q\choose j}
 p_\delta^j(1-p_\delta)^{q-j}
\,u_\delta^{*j}*g^{*(q-j)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For every `j<q`, at least one `g` factor is present. Since all other factors are probability densities,

\[
\|u_\delta^{*j}*g^{*(q-j)}\|_\infty
\le\|g\|_\infty=G.
\]

The total binomial weight of all `j<q` terms is at most one, so their combined `L^infinity` contribution is at most `G`.

For `j=q`, convolution contracts the `L^infinity` norm of the first factor, giving

\[
\|u_\delta^{*q}\|_\infty
\le\|u_\delta\|_\infty
=U\delta^{-d}.
\]

But

\[
p_\delta^q=c^q\delta^d,
\]

hence the all-spike term is bounded by `c^qU`. This proves (5.2).

**Proof of (5.3).** The entire `u_delta` component lies in `B(0,delta)`, so

\[
P_{f_\delta}\{|X|\le\delta\}
\ge p_\delta.
\]

**Variance normalization.** Because both components are centered,

\[
s_\delta^2:=E_{f_\delta}|X|^2
=(1-p_\delta)+p_\delta\delta^2v_u
\longrightarrow1.
\]

For small `delta`, `s_delta` is bounded above and below by fixed positive constants. Let `Y_delta=X_delta/s_delta`. By (4.2),

\[
\|(f_{Y_\delta})^{*q}\|_\infty
=s_\delta^d\|f_\delta^{*q}\|_\infty
\le C_{u,g,q,c},
\]

while

\[
P\{|Y_\delta|\le\delta/s_\delta\}
\ge c\delta^{d/q}
\asymp (\delta/s_\delta)^{d/q}.
\]

Thus the normalized laws are centered, variance one, smooth, compactly supported, have all moments uniformly bounded, and still attain the `r^{d/q}` scalar concentration order. QED.

### Consequences for the rewrite

- For `q>1`, `(FC)` does **not** imply a uniform bound on the raw density. Indeed the spike height in (5.1) is of order
  \[
  p_\delta\delta^{-d}
  =c\delta^{-d(1-1/q)},
  \]
  which diverges as `delta->0`.
- The scalar anti-concentration exponents `1/q` in the real case and `2/q` in the complex case are optimal under `(FC)` alone, up to constants.
- Therefore the P12/P13 upper proof must genuinely use block smoothing / low-leverage projection density; it may not substitute a fictitious linear raw-coordinate small-ball bound.
- This family is **not** a counterexample to `(T-FC)`: the shrinking spike has mass `p_delta->0`, while the q-fold block is uniformly smoothed exactly as the theorem requires.

This stress test strengthens confidence in the proof architecture by identifying the precise sharp boundary of the scalar input.

## 7. Why the B4 route still does not replace `(FC)`

The new spike/bulk construction above should not be confused with the earlier narrowing-dominant-cluster counterexamples.

- The present family **satisfies** one fixed uniform `(FC)` ceiling and demonstrates the sharp scalar concentration permitted by that hypothesis.
- The earlier near-discrete smooth triangular examples have microscopic packets whose dominant mass remains too concentrated; every fixed convolution power retains an exponentially narrowing packet with too much mass. Those examples can have centered variance one, uniform B4, smooth compact support or subgaussian tails, yet fail the source-scale linear small-ball mechanism needed by a naive B4 proof.

Therefore there is no contradiction. The correct hierarchy is:

\[
\text{uniform bounded density}
\implies
(FC)\text{ with }q=1
\implies
\text{uniform microscopic smoothing},
\]

while

\[
\text{B4 + pointwise smoothness + fixed-scale anti-concentration}
\not\Rightarrow
(FC)\text{ uniformly}.
\]

The first arrow is exactly why Conjecture 8.2's fixed bounded-density class is covered. The failure of the second implication is exactly why the proposed August B4 enlargement is still unsupported.
