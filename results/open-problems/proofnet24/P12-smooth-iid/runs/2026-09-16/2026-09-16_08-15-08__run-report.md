# 3. Exact variance normalization

For every scalar `sigma`,

\[
\boxed{
M_{m,r}(\sigma A)=|\sigma|M_{m,r}(A).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For fixed nonzero variance scale this changes `m^{-1} log M` by `o(1)`.

For triangular scales `sigma_m`, the moderate-surplus statistic changes by

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log|\sigma_m|.
\]

Hence the exact exponent-neutral condition is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0.
}
\tag{3.2}

Scaling does **not** remove a nonzero mean.

If a fourth-moment hypothesis is written before normalization, the relevant normalized budget is

\[
\frac{E|\xi_m-E\xi_m|^4}{(\operatorname{Var}\xi_m)^2},

not the raw fourth moment. The new theorem is stated after centering and variance-one normalization.

# 6. New theorem: exponential fixed-radius compressibility exclusion

We prove `P12-B4-EXP-COMPRESSIBILITY-45`.

Let

\[
s_m=\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor
\]

and define

\[
\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)
=
\{x\in S^{m-1}:\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{Sparse}(s_m)\cap S^{m-1})\le\eta\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

## 6.1 A soft slab

Define `phi:R->[0,1]` by

\[
\phi(t)=
\begin{cases}
1,&|t|\le\rho_0/2,\\
2-2|t|/\rho_0,&\rho_0/2<|t|<\rho_0,\\
0,&|t|\ge\rho_0.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{|t|\le\rho_0/2\}}
\le\phi(t)
\le\mathbf 1_{\{|t|\le\rho_0\}},
\tag{6.3}
\]

and `phi` is Lipschitz with

\[
L_\phi=2/\rho_0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

By `(5.2)`, for every unit `x`,

\[
E\phi(\langle R,x\rangle)
\le\beta-\delta_0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Let

\[
P_Nf=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^Nf(R_i)
\]

and

\[
F_m
=
\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)}
\left[
P_N\phi(\langle\cdot,x\rangle)
-E\phi(\langle R,x\rangle)
\right].
\tag{6.6}
\]

We bound `E F_m` without a covering net.

## 6.2 Symmetrization and contraction

Let `epsilon_i` be independent Rademacher signs, independent of the rows, and set

\[
H
=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\varepsilon_iR_i
\in\mathbb R^m.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Standard ghost-sample symmetrization followed by scalar contraction, applied to

\[
\psi(t)=\phi(t)-\phi(0),
\qquad \psi(0)=0,
\]

gives the explicit safe bound

\[
\boxed{
E F_m
\le
4L_\phi
E\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)}
|\langle H,x\rangle|
+
\frac2{\sqrt N}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

For completeness, the last term comes from

\[
\phi(0)E\left|N^{-1}\sum_i\varepsilon_i\right|
\le N^{-1/2}.
\]

Thus no external VC theorem is imported at this stage.

## 6.3 Rademacher width of the compressible class

For each `x in C_m(alpha,eta)`, choose a unit `s_m`-sparse vector `y` with

\[
\|x-y\|_2\le\eta.
\]

Then

\[
|\langle H,x\rangle|
\le
|\langle H,y\rangle|
+
\eta\|H\|_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)}|\langle H,x\rangle|
\le
\max_{|J|\le s_m}\|H_J\|_2
+
\eta\|H\|_2.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Deterministically,

\[
\max_{|J|\le s_m}\|H_J\|_2
\le
s_m^{1/4}
\left(\sum_{j=1}^mH_j^4\right)^{1/4}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Indeed, for every `J`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_{j\in J}H_j^2
\le
\sqrt{|J|}
\left(\sum_jH_j^4\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Now fix a coordinate `j`. The variables

\[
Y_i=\varepsilon_i\xi_{ij}
\]

are independent, centered, satisfy `EY_i^2=1`, and have fourth moment at most `B`. Hence

\[
E H_j^4
=
\frac{NEY_1^4+3N(N-1)(EY_1^2)^2}{N^4}
\le
\frac{B+3}{N^2}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Consequently, by Jensen,

\[
E\max_{|J|\le s_m}\|H_J\|_2
\le
(B+3)^{1/4}
\frac{(s_mm)^{1/4}}{\sqrt N}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Also

\[
E\|H\|_2
\le
(E\|H\|_2^2)^{1/2}
=
\sqrt{m/N}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Combining `(6.9)`--`(6.13)`,

\[
\boxed{
E\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)}|\langle H,x\rangle|
\le
(B+3)^{1/4}
\frac{(s_mm)^{1/4}}{\sqrt N}
+
\eta\sqrt{m/N}.
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

Since `N/m->gamma`, eventually `N/m>=gamma/2`. With `s_m<=alpha m`,

\[
E\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m}|\langle H,x\rangle|
\le
\sqrt{\frac2\gamma}
\left[
(B+3)^{1/4}\alpha^{1/4}+\eta
\right].
\tag{6.15}
\]

Choose fixed `alpha,eta>0` sufficiently small that

\[
4L_\phi\sqrt{\frac2\gamma}
\left[
(B+3)^{1/4}\alpha^{1/4}+\eta
\right]
\le\frac{\delta_0}{8}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

For all sufficiently large `m`, `(6.8)` then gives

\[
E F_m\le\frac{\delta_0}{4}.
\tag{6.17}
\]

The constants are fixed in `m` and depend only on `(B,gamma)` through `rho_0,delta_0`.

## 6.4 Exponential concentration of the empirical supremum

Changing one row changes every empirical average in `(6.6)` by at most `1/N`, hence changes `F_m` by at most `1/N`.

McDiarmid's bounded-difference inequality therefore yields

\[
P\left\{
F_m-EF_m>\frac{\delta_0}{4}
\right\}
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{N\delta_0^2}{8}\right].
\tag{6.18}
\]

On the complementary event, for every `x in C_m(alpha,eta)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1N
\#\{i:|\langle R_i,x\rangle|\le\rho_0/2\}
&\le
P_N\phi(\langle\cdot,x\rangle)\\
&\le
E\phi(\langle R,x\rangle)+F_m\\
&\le
\beta-\frac{\delta_0}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.19}
\]

Thus with probability at least `1-e^{-cm}`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x\in\mathcal C_m(\alpha,\eta)}
\frac1N
\#\{i:|\langle R_i,x\rangle|\le\rho_0/2\}
\le
\beta-\frac{\delta_0}{2}.
}
\tag{6.20}
\]

This is the core new global theorem.

## 6.5 Every candidate has a macroscopic norm on compressible vectors

Let

\[
n_m=m+k_m-1,
\qquad
k_m=o(m).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{n_m}{N_m}\to\beta.
\]

For all sufficiently large `m`,

\[
\frac{n_m}{N_m}
\ge
\beta-\frac{\delta_0}{4}.
\tag{6.21}
\]

On `(6.20)`, every candidate `T` of size `n_m` and every `x in C_m(alpha,eta)` contains at least

\[
\frac{\delta_0}{4}N_m
\tag{6.22}
\]

rows satisfying

\[
|\langle R_i,x\rangle|>\rho_0/2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|(A_m)_Tx\|_2^2
\ge
\frac{\rho_0^2}{4}\frac{\delta_0N_m}{4}
=
\frac{\rho_0^2\delta_0}{16}N_m.
\tag{6.23}
\]

Since eventually `N_m>=gamma m/2`, one may take

\[
\boxed{
a_*
=
\rho_0\sqrt{\frac{\delta_0\gamma}{32}}.
}
\tag{6.24}
\]

This proves `(0.6)`.

## 6.6 Every adaptive leave-one-out null normal is fixed-radius incompressible

Let `S subset[N_m]` have `|S|=m-1` and suppose

\[
(A_m)_Sv=0,
\qquad
\|v\|_2=1.
\]

If `v in C_m(alpha,eta)`, then all `m-1` rows in `S` satisfy

\[
|\langle R_i,v\rangle|\le\rho_0/2.

But

\[
\frac{m-1}{N_m}\to\beta,
\]

so for all large `m`,

\[
\frac{m-1}{N_m}
>\beta-\frac{\delta_0}{2},
\]

contradicting `(6.20)`.

Hence every adaptive leave-one-out null normal lies outside the fixed compressible neighborhood. This proves `(0.7)`.

# 7. Deterministic spread-set corollary

We now prove `P12-B4-SPREAD-NORMALS-45`.

Let

\[
s=\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor,
\]

and suppose a unit vector `x` satisfies

\[
\operatorname{dist}
\bigl(x,\operatorname{Sparse}(s)\cap S^{m-1}\bigr)>\eta,
\qquad
0<\eta<1.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `J` index the `s` largest coordinates of `x` in absolute value, and write

\[
a=\|x_J\|_2,
\qquad
r=\|x_{J^c}\|_2.
\]

The closest unit vector supported on `J` is `x_J/a`, and

\[
\left\|x-\frac{x_J}{a}\right\|_2^2
=2(1-a).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence `(7.1)` implies

\[
a<1-\frac{\eta^2}{2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
r^2>\eta^2-\frac{\eta^4}{4}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Put

\[
\theta_\eta
=\sqrt{\eta^2-\eta^4/4}>0.
\]

At least one coordinate outside `J` has magnitude at least

\[
\frac r{\sqrt{m-s}}
\ge
\frac{\theta_\eta}{\sqrt m}.
\]

Since `J` consists of the `s` largest coordinates, every coordinate in `J` has magnitude at least this large. Thus

\[
|x_j|\ge\frac{\theta_\eta}{\sqrt m},
\qquad j\in J.
\tag{7.4}
\]

On the other hand, by `||x||_2=1`, at most `(alpha/2)m` coordinates can satisfy

\[
|x_j|>\sqrt{\frac2\alpha}\frac1{\sqrt m}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore, for all large `m`, at least `(alpha/3)m` coordinates satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\theta_\eta}{\sqrt m}
\le |x_j|
\le
\sqrt{\frac2\alpha}\frac1{\sqrt m}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Taking

\[
c_*:=\alpha/3,
\quad
c_1:=\theta_\eta,
