3. there are fixed `q>=1` and `M<infinity` such that

Then the frozen theorem states

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M^{\mathbb F}_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
-\frac{h(\gamma)}{d_{\mathbb F}},
\qquad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).

## 3. Hostile test: smooth triangular B4 + fixed-scale anti-concentration still does not buy microscopic density control

Before trying to streamline the proof, I attacked the most tempting weakening: replace finite-convolution smoothing by smoothness, a uniform fourth moment, and fixed-scale anti-concentration.

Fix `c>0`, let `epsilon` be Rademacher, let `Z~N(0,1)` be independent, and define

\[
\delta_m=e^{-2cm},\qquad
\xi_m=\frac{\epsilon+\delta_m Z}{\sqrt{1+\delta_m^2}}.
\]

Every `xi_m` has a `C^infinity` density, is centered, and has variance one. Moreover

\[
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{1+6\delta_m^2+3\delta_m^4}{(1+\delta_m^2)^2}
\le 3,
\]

because three times the denominator minus the numerator is exactly `2`. At every fixed macroscopic radius smaller than the separation of the two clusters, the concentration function is bounded strictly below one for all sufficiently large `m`; thus this family has perfectly adequate fixed-scale anti-concentration. What fails is uniform microscopic smoothing: its density ceiling is of order `delta_m^{-1}` and every fixed convolution power still contains finitely many narrow Gaussian packets of that width.

For even `m`, take the flat unit vector

\[
u_m=m^{-1/2}(1,\dots,1),\qquad t_m=e^{-cm}.
\]

Conditional on the sign event `sum_i epsilon_i=0`,

\[
\langle (\xi_{m,1},\ldots,\xi_{m,m}),u_m\rangle
\sim
N\!\left(0,\frac{\delta_m^2}{1+\delta_m^2}\right).
\]

Since `t_m/delta_m=e^{cm}`, the conditional probability of absolute value at most `t_m` tends to one. Also

\[
P\{\textstyle\sum_i\epsilon_i=0\}
=2^{-m}{m\choose m/2}
\ge \frac1{m+1},
\]

the last inequality following because the central binomial coefficient is at least the average coefficient of `(1+1)^m`. Hence

\[
P\{|\langle \xi^{(m)},u_m\rangle|\le t_m\}
\ge \frac{1-o(1)}{m+1},
\]

whereas any source-scale density estimate of the form `C t_m` is exponentially smaller.

**Conclusion.** Smoothness at each `m` + centered variance one + a uniform fourth moment + fixed-scale anti-concentration does **not** imply the source-scale linear small-ball control used in the constructive upper proof. This is a rigorous no-go for that proof mechanism, not a proof that every possible B4 universality theorem is false. It is exactly why the rewrite must retain a uniform microscopic hypothesis such as `(FC)` unless a genuinely different arithmetic mechanism is supplied.

## 4. New-method #11 — DUALIZE: sparse-kernel geometry as a coordinate-minor certificate

The lower-side finite-convolution proof becomes easier to audit if the sparse-kernel/Cauchy-Binet step is stated as a dual certificate rather than as a sequence of ad hoc basis extractions.

Let `U` be a `k x n` matrix with orthonormal rows, so `UU^*=I_k`. Suppose that for every coordinate set `R` with

\[
|R|\le (a-1)k
\]

we have the robust deletion bound

\[
\lambda_{\min}(U_{R^c}U_{R^c}^*)\ge \tau^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here `a` is a fixed integer; in the frozen finite-convolution application one takes a fixed number of blocks depending only on `q` (the existing proof uses the corresponding fixed disjoint-basis count).

I claim that one can extract pairwise disjoint coordinate sets `S_1,...,S_a`, each of cardinality `k`, such that

\[
|\det U_{S_l}|
\ge
\frac{\tau^k}{\sqrt{{n\choose k}}}
\qquad (1\le l\le a).
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Suppose `S_1,...,S_{l-1}` have been selected and put `R=union_{t<l} S_t`. Then `|R|=(l-1)k`, so (4.1) applies. Cauchy-Binet gives

\[
\det(U_{R^c}U_{R^c}^*)
=
\sum_{\substack{S\subset R^c\\|S|=k}}
|\det U_S|^2.
\]

Every eigenvalue of the Gram matrix on the left is at least `tau^2`, hence its determinant is at least `tau^{2k}`. There are at most `{n\choose k}` summands, so at least one unused `S` satisfies (4.2). Choose it as `S_l` and iterate. QED.

For fixed `a`, the determinant loss is subexponential on the P12 scale:

\[
\log {n\choose k}
\le k\log\frac{en}{k},
\]

so

\[
\frac a2\log {n\choose k}=O_a(k\log m)=o(m).
\]

This is not a new theorem enlarging P12. It is an **audit normal form** for an existing dependency: robust avoidance of sparse coordinate directions is exactly the dual statement that enough disjoint coordinate minors carry non-negligible determinant mass. It also exposes what P26 needs to check: the proof only needs deletion robustness through a fixed multiple of `k`; it does not need a uniform determinant lower bound for every coordinate minor.

## 5. New-method #19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: the constructive upper branch as a predictable filtration contract

The dangerous point in any survivor/probe construction is adaptivity. Earlier P12 stress tests already showed the maximal failure if one lets the tested normal depend on the same fresh row: for `m>=2`, a measurable unit vector `u(X)` can be chosen perpendicular to `X`, making `|<X,u(X)>|=0` almost surely. Therefore the correct upper proof is not an informal "repeat independent trials" argument. It is a predictable algorithm.

Write `F_j` for the sigma-field after the base pool and the first `j` marked/probe rows have been exposed. At stage `j+1`, every survivor decision, current normal, pair class, and threshold used to test the next row must be `F_j`-measurable. The next original iid row `X_{j+1}` is independent of `F_j`. Only after its test is evaluated may it enter the state update.

The only probabilistic amplification needed at this level is the following elementary adapted Bernoulli lemma.

Let `I_1,...,I_L` be `{0,1}`-valued and adapted, and suppose

\[
P(I_j=1\mid F_{j-1})\ge p_*>0\quad\text{a.s. for every }j.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every `theta>0`,

\[
E[e^{-\theta I_j}\mid F_{j-1}]
=1-(1-e^{-\theta})P(I_j=1\mid F_{j-1})
\le 1-p_*+p_*e^{-\theta}.
\]

Iterating conditional expectations gives

\[
E\exp\!\left(-\theta\sum_{j=1}^L I_j\right)
\le
(1-p_*+p_*e^{-\theta})^L.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
P\left\{\sum_{j=1}^L I_j<k\right\}
\le
 e^{\theta k}(1-p_*+p_*e^{-\theta})^L.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `L` is a fixed positive fraction of `m` and `k=o(m)`, a fixed `theta>0` makes the right side exponentially small. Independence of the success indicators is unnecessary; conditional freshness is sufficient.

This rephrasing is useful because it turns the upper branch into a finite audit contract:

1. state before a probe is measurable with respect to previously revealed data;
2. the tested row is fresh relative to that state;
3. the one-row or paired-row local law yields the required conditional success bound on the retained survivor class;
4. update occurs only after the test;
5. (5.2)-(5.3) perform amplification.

P26 therefore need not certify a fictitious global independence statement. It should certify predictability/freshness at each marked-row insertion and then the adapted amplification above.

## 6. New-method #28 — REPLACE "FOR ALL" WITH "FOR TYPICAL": quantifier polarity of the two extreme tails

The manuscript is easiest to misread if both sides of the extreme-value law are treated as uniform-over-candidates estimates. They are not symmetric.

Because

\[
M_{m,r}(A)=\min_T s_{\min}(A_T),
\]

the lower-extreme statement `M_{m,r} > t` requires ruling out a bad event for **every** candidate. Equivalently,

\[
\{M_{m,r}\le t\}=\bigcup_T\{s_{\min}(A_T)\le t\},
\]

so candidate entropy `exp(h(gamma)m+o(m))` must be paid by a per-candidate source-scale small-ball exponent. This is where projection density, negative second moment, and the candidate union bound belong.

The constructive upper-extreme statement has the opposite quantifier:

\[
\{M_{m,r}\le t\}
\quad\text{only requires the existence of one sufficiently bad candidate.}
\]

Thus an upper proof is allowed to discard almost all candidates, retain a typical/good survivor class, and construct one witness using fresh probes. A uniform source-scale local CLT for every one of exponentially many adaptive hyperplanes would be both unnecessary and, under weak tails, structurally implausible. The public August 7 Yang-Wen-Shmalo square-candidate paper explicitly describes its main new upper-tail ingredient as avoiding uniform control over exponentially many random hyperplanes; the frozen finite-convolution proof uses the same quantifier economy in a more general setting.

This yields a concrete rewrite instruction: the lower half should be presented as **entropy versus per-candidate small-ball**, while the upper half should be presented as **one predictable survivor construction**. Any sentence suggesting that the proof supplies simultaneous microscopic Gaussianity for all candidate normals should be removed.
