# 4. Fourth moment of an arbitrary iid linear form

This is the algebraic core of the new result.

Let `X_1,...,X_s` be iid centered complex random variables with
\[
E|X|^2=1,
\qquad
\mu_4=E|X|^4,
\qquad
\tau=EX^2.
\]
The real case is included by `tau=1`.

For `c in C^s`, `||c||_2=1`, put
\[
S=\sum_jc_jX_j,
\qquad
\kappa=\sum_j|c_j|^4,
\qquad
u=\sum_jc_j^2.
\]
A direct expansion by index pairings gives
\[
\boxed{
E|S|^4
=
2+|\tau|^2|\nu|^2
+(\mu_4-2-|\tau|^2)\kappa.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed the two variance pairings contribute `2(1-kappa)`, the same-index terms contribute `mu_4 kappa`, and the pseudo-covariance pairing contributes
\[
|\tau|^2(|\nu|^2-\kappa).
\]

Since `|tau|<=1`, `|nu|<=1`, and `mu_4<=B`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E|S|^4
&\le
2+(B-2)\kappa+|\tau|^2(|\nu|^2-\kappa)\\
&\le
2+(B-2)\kappa+(1-\kappa)\\
&=
\boxed{3+(B-3)\kappa.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For real coefficients and a real law this is exact:
\[
E S^4=3+(\mu_4-3)\kappa.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Three checks are important.

1. One coordinate: `kappa=1`, giving `ES^4<=B`.
2. Equal real pair: `kappa=1/2`, giving
   \[
   ES^4\le\frac{B+3}{2}.
   \]
3. Flat high-height vectors: `kappa->0`, giving the Gaussian fourth-moment ceiling `3`. This is exactly why fourth moment alone loses force on very spread real linear forms when `gamma>3/2`, where `B_0(gamma)<3`.

No density or tail theorem enters this calculation.

# 5. Robust fourth-moment small-ball conversion

The next inequality is valid over `R` or `C`.

Let `Y` satisfy `E|Y|^2=1` and put
\[
q=P\{|Y|\le\rho\}<1.
\]
On the event `A={|Y|<=rho}`,
\[
E[|Y|^2 1_A]\le q\rho^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
E[|Y|^2 1_{A^c}]\ge1-q\rho^2.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz on `A^c` gives
\[
(1-q\rho^2)^2
\le
(1-q)E[|Y|^4 1_{A^c}]
\le
(1-q)E|Y|^4.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
E|Y|^4\ge\frac{(1-q\rho^2)^2}{1-q}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

At `rho=0`,
\[
P\{Y=0\}\le1-\frac1{E|Y|^4}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now fix `gamma>1`, put
\[
\beta=1/\gamma,
\qquad
B_0=1/(1-\beta),
\]
and suppose `E|Y|^4<=C<B_0`.

Define
\[
q_0=1-\frac1C<\beta,
\qquad
\bar q=\frac{\beta+q_0}{2},
\qquad
delta=\beta-\bar q>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Since `C(1-bar q)<1`, put
\[
theta=\sqrt{C(1-\bar q)}<1
\]
and choose
\[
\boxed{
\rho^2=
\min\left\{
\frac14,
\frac{1-\theta}{2\bar q}
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
For `a=rho^2<=1/4`, the function
\[
F_a(q)=\frac{(1-aq)^2}{1-q}

is increasing on `[0,1)` because

Moreover `(5.4)` gives `F_a(bar q)>C`. Therefore `(5.1)` rules out `q>=bar q`.

# 6. Exact coefficient-height consequences

For every unit coefficient vector,
\[
E|S_c|^4\le C_B(\kappa):=3+(B-3)\kappa.

Suppose now
\[

\qquad
B<3.
\]
Then `B_0(gamma)<3`, so the inequality
\[
C_B(\kappa)<B_0
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\kappa>
\frac{3-B_0}{3-B}
=:\kappa_*(B,\gamma).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Thus for each fixed `eta>0`, all vectors with
\[
\kappa(c)\ge\kappa_*+\eta

have one common coarse anti-concentration gap below `1/gamma`.

Equivalently, if a sequence of linear forms has microscopic mass tending to the critical row fraction, its coefficient height must satisfy
\[
\limsup\kappa(c_m)\le\kappa_*.
\tag{6.2}
\]
This is a quantitative restriction on the actual adaptive leave-one-out normal family.

## 6.2 Support-size form

If `c` is supported on at most `s` coordinates, Cauchy gives
\[
\kappa(c)=\sum|c_j|^4\ge\frac1s.
\tag{6.3}
\]
The largest value of `C_B(kappa)` for `kappa in[1/s,1]` is
\[
\boxed{
C_{B,s}
=
\max\left\{
B,\,3+\frac{B-3}{s}
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Hence `C_{B,s}<B_0` gives a common fixed-radius gap for **all** `s`-sparse directions.

The regimes are explicit.

### `1<gamma<3/2`

Here `B_0>3`. If `B<B_0`, then
\[
\sup_{s\ge1}C_{B,s}\le\max\{B,3\}<B_0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
So the fourth-moment argument has a support-uniform margin.

### `gamma=3/2`

Here `B_0=3`. If `B<3`, then every fixed `s` obeys

but the margin vanishes as `s->infinity`.

### `3/2<gamma<2`

In the strict subcritical range `B<B_exact(gamma)<3`, condition `(6.4)<B_0` becomes

\boxed{

At

Thus immediately below the exact threshold, one- and two-coordinate obstructions are the final low-height relations; deeper below threshold, progressively larger fixed supports are excluded.

# 8. Global suppression of sparse source-scale witnesses

We now pass from a fixed coefficient vector to all supports and all coefficients without unioning over candidate row subsets.

Assume `C_{B,s}<B_0(gamma)` for one fixed `s`. Section 5 gives numbers `rho>0` and a gap `g>0` such that for every support `J` with `|J|<=s` and every unit vector `c` on that support,
\[
P\{|\langle R_J,c\rangle|\le\rho\}\le\beta-g,
\tag{8.1}
\]
where `R_J` is one iid row restricted to `J`.

Fix `J`, `j=|J|`. Let
\[
L_j^2=\frac{16j}{g}.
\]
Since `E||R_J||_2^2=j`, Markov gives
\[
P\{||R_J||_2>L_j\}\le\frac g{16}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
By Chernoff, with probability at least `1-exp(-c_1N)`, at most `gN/4` rows violate the norm cutoff.

Take an `epsilon`-net of the real or complex unit sphere on `J` with
\[
\varepsilon=\frac{\rho}{2L_j}.
\]
Its size is at most
\[
\left(1+\frac{4L_j}{\rho}\right)^{d_{\mathbb F}j},
\qquad d_{\mathbb R}=1,\quad d_{\mathbb C}=2.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Suppose some unit `c` has at least `n_m` rows satisfying
\[
|\langle R_{i,J},c\rangle|\le\rho/2.
\]
After removing the exceptional large-norm rows and replacing `c` by a net point `c'`, at least
\[
n_m-gN_m/4
\]
rows satisfy
\[
|\langle R_{i,J},c'\rangle|\le\rho.
\]
For all large `m`, `beta_m=n_m/N_m>=beta-g/4`, so this count is at least
\[
(\beta-g/2)N_m.
\]
But each row succeeds for a fixed net point with probability at most `beta-g`. A Chernoff bound therefore gives
\[
P\{\text{a fixed net point has this many successes}\}
\le e^{-c_2N_m}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Union over the finite net and over at most
\[
\sum_{j=1}^s\binom mj
\]
supports yields
\[
P\{\exists J,c:\#\text{ small rows}\ge n_m\}
\le
\exp[-cm+O_s(\log m)].
\tag{8.5}
\]

Finally, if for some `T,J`
\[
\sigma_{|J|}((A_m)_{T,J})\le\rho/2,
\]
there exists a unit `c` supported on `J` with
\[
||(A_m)_{T,J}c||_2\le\rho/2.
\]
Every one of the `n_m` row inner products is then at most `rho/2`, contradicting the complement of the event in `(8.5)`.

This proves `P12-B4-SPARSE-GLOBAL-43`.

## Growing support when `1<gamma<3/2`

If `B<B_0(gamma)` and `gamma<3/2`, `(6.5)` supplies one common `rho,g` for all supports. The net/support entropy in `(8.3)`--`(8.5)` is
\[
O(s_m\log m+s_m\log s_m).
\]
Therefore the same proof works whenever
\[
s_m\log m=o(m).
\]
This is a genuine compressible-range statement under bounded fourth moment alone.

## What is not proved

For `gamma>=3/2`, the fourth-moment ceiling approaches or exceeds the critical `B_0` on high-height vectors. The theorem does not control the fully delocalized leave-one-out normal family there. This is exactly the live adaptive/LCD problem, not a missing union bound in the sparse argument.
