## 3. Root equalizer identity — the useful algebraic reduction

For a non-reflected source-root action `(a,b)` with transformed coordinates

\[
u=1728a+2112b,\qquad v=2112a+1728b,

fix an integer child cell

\[

Let `F_H` and `F_L` denote any already-certified continuation lower bounds at the two corresponding integer children. In the orientation `b>=a`, so `m=b`, the two branch lower bounds are

\[
H=F_H+8S(8640Qa-9600Pb),
\]
\[

\]

Subtracting cancels the entire common parameter penalty:

\[
\boxed{H-L=F_H-F_L+8S\,(19200Q)a.}

Hence the branch equalizer in a fixed child cell is

\[
\boxed{a=\frac{F_L-F_H}{8S\,19200Q}.}

This is the key reason a large-wall attack is tractable: after the continuation table is recomputed at a new `P`, the equalizing `a` is determined exactly by two child values, and `b` only has to be chosen so that the transformed cell constraints hold. No two-dimensional denominator grid is required.

The same cancellation principle is transferable to other two-branch finite adversarial DPs: a parameter penalty common to both branches disappears from the equalizer equation, leaving continuation differences to determine the switching surface.
