### Proof: orientation `b>=a`

## 7. Transferable probability/control lemma

The reflected argument abstracts as follows.

> **One-boundary reflected gluing lemma.** Suppose a two-branch Bellman recursion has a reflected branch whose child remains in a core region with exact affine slope `q0`, while the forward branch may cross one boundary into slope `q1=q0-d`. If, after orientation reduction, the reflected and forward branch increments obey
> \[
> R\le C_Rx-AJ,\qquad H\le-C_Hx+dJ
> \]
> with positive `A,C_R,C_H`, then the full reflected Bellman inequality holds whenever
> \[
> dC_R\le C_HA.
> \]
> The proof is the two-case implication `R<=0`, or `R>0 => x>AJ/C_R => H<=0`.

This is useful in reflected random walks, robust stopping, queueing recursions and extremal-distribution dynamic programs: a Lyapunov slope can change immediately after the reflection core if the reflected branch provides enough state-proportional slack to pay for the forward branch's boundary loss. It avoids the much stronger requirement that every reachable child see one globally constant slope.
