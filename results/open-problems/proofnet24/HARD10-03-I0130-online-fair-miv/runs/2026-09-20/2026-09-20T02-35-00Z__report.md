## 4. B18(reuse) — transformed action cells and the four monotone directions

This run attacks the compact-core continuous separation problem directly.

For a fixed integer parent state `J` and a fixed nonincreasing continuation table `f`, the full structural Bellman branches, after suppressing a common positive scale, are

\[
H_J(a,b)=8(8640Qa-9600Pm)+f(J+\lceil u\rceil),
\]

and, when `J>=v`,

\[
L^{\rm nr}_J(a,b)=8(-10560Qa-9600Pm)+f(J-\lfloor v\rfloor),
\]

while in the reflected regime `J<v`,

\[
L^{\rm ref}_J(a,b)
=8(8640Qb-9600Pm)-40QJ+f(\lceil v\rceil-J).
\]

Partition `[0,1]^2` by

- the orientation wall `a=b`;
- all integer high-child walls `u=k`;
- all integer low-child walls `v=k`;
- the box faces.

(The reflection wall `v=J` is already one of the integer `v` walls because `J` is integer.) Inside every resulting open cell, orientation, reflection status and all continuation indices are fixed, so both branches are affine.

The key observation is that **every open two-dimensional cell has a coordinate direction that pushes the Bellman minimum weakly upward until a cell boundary is reached**.

### Case 1: `a>=b`, non-reflected

Here `m=a`. Both immediate rewards and both fixed continuation addends depend on `a` but not on `b`. Thus

\[
\frac{\partial H_J}{\partial b}
=\frac{\partial L^{\rm nr}_J}{\partial b}=0.
\]

Hold `a` fixed and move `b` until the cell boundary. The Bellman value is unchanged.

### Case 2: `a>=b`, reflected

Again `m=a`. The high branch is independent of `b`, whereas

\[
\frac{\partial L^{\rm ref}_J}{\partial b}=8\cdot8640Q=69120Q>0.
\]

Increase `b` while remaining in the cell. The high branch stays fixed and the reflected low branch increases, so

\[
\min\{H_J,L^{\rm ref}_J\}
\]

cannot decrease. Continue until a boundary is reached.

### Case 3: `b>=a`, non-reflected

Now `m=b`. Both branches have the same derivative in `b`:

\[
\frac{\partial H_J}{\partial b}
=\frac{\partial L^{\rm nr}_J}{\partial b}
=-8\cdot9600P=-76800P\le0.
\]

Decrease `b` while staying in the cell. Both branches weakly increase, hence their minimum weakly increases, until a boundary is reached.

### Case 4: `b>=a`, reflected

Here the reflected low branch is independent of `a`, while

\[
\frac{\partial H_J}{\partial a}=8\cdot8640Q=69120Q>0.
\]

Increase `a` until a boundary is reached. The low branch stays fixed and the high branch increases, so the Bellman minimum cannot decrease.

Therefore every interior action has a boundary representative with Bellman value at least as large.

## 5. B11(reuse) — integer walls are favorable, so the reduction is exact

The preceding directional argument would be incomplete if the floor/ceiling discontinuities could destroy the gain at the limiting wall. The inherited monotonicity of `f` rules this out exactly.

### High walls `u=k`

The high continuation is `f(J+ceil(u))`.

- Approaching `k` from below, `ceil(u)=k` already, so the continuation does not jump.
- Approaching from above and moving down to `k`, the child index changes from `J+k+1` to `J+k`. Because `f` is nonincreasing,

\[
f(J+k)\ge f(J+k+1).
\]

Thus landing on a high wall cannot reduce the high branch.

### Non-reflected low walls `v=k`

The continuation is `f(J-floor(v))`.

- Approaching from above, `floor(v)=k` already.
- Approaching from below and moving up to `k`, the child changes from `J-k+1` to `J-k`, a smaller state. Hence

\[
f(J-k)\ge f(J-k+1).
\]

Thus landing on a non-reflected low wall cannot reduce the low branch.

### Reflected low walls `v=k`

The continuation is `f(ceil(v)-J)`.

- Approaching from below, `ceil(v)=k` already.
- Approaching from above and moving down to `k`, the child changes from `k+1-J` to `k-J`, again a smaller state, so the continuation weakly increases.

### Reflection wall `v=J`

At `v=J`, the non-reflected and reflected formulas agree exactly. Their immediate-reward difference is

\[
8(8640Qb)-40QJ-8(-10560Qa)
=69120Qb+84480Qa-40Q(2112a+1728b)=0,
\]

and both continuation children equal state `0`.

The orientation wall is also continuous because `m=max(a,b)` agrees from both sides.

This proves the promised reduction.

Fix any integer parent state `J` and any nonincreasing continuation table `f`. For the full continuous structural action square, the maximum of

\[
\min\{H_J(a,b),L_J(a,b)\}

has a maximizer on the finite skeleton

\[
\boxed{
\{u=k\}\cup\{v=k\}\cup\{a=b\}\cup\partial[0,1]^2,

\]

Cut this skeleton at all pairwise intersections. On each resulting one-dimensional rational segment, the two branches are affine in one parameter. Hence their minimum is piecewise affine and its maximum on that segment occurs at a segment endpoint or at a branch-equality point `H=L`. If the continuation values are rational, every such candidate is rational.

**Consequences.**

1. Exact separation of a fixed 3840-core-plus-scalar candidate requires only finitely many one-dimensional rational problems; no two-dimensional interior optimization remains.

## 7. Transferable lemma for probability workers

This run adds a second general dynamic-programming device to the previous linear-cost tail envelope.

> **Monotone boundary-pushing lemma.** Suppose a max-min Bellman action space is partitioned into rational polyhedral cells on which both branches are affine and state-transition indices are constant. If in every open cell there is a coordinate direction along which one branch is invariant while the other improves, or both branches improve together, then every optimizer has a cell-boundary representative. If transition rounding at the walls moves the child only toward a state with weakly larger continuation value, wall discontinuities preserve the reduction. Consequently exact continuous separation reduces to finitely many one-dimensional edge equalizers/endpoints.

This is potentially reusable in finite-state probability DPs with monotone continuation values and quantized/reflected transitions. It is a structural proof lemma only; it makes no claim about the source problems owned by other tracks.
