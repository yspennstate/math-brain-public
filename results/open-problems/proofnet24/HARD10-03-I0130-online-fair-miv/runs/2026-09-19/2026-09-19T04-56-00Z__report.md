## 7. B30 — exact compatibility law for gluing adjacent cell duals

The global dual need not have one constant root branch shadow, as B2 proves. The correct object should be a piecewise rational occupation/super-solution dual glued across Bellman facets.

Let two final PWA cells `C_1,C_2` share a codimension-one facet

\[
F=\{z:n\cdot z=\beta\}.
\]

Suppose exact affine dual upper certificates on the two cells are

\[
u_1(z)=c_1+g_1\cdot z,
\qquad
u_2(z)=c_2+g_2\cdot z.
\]

A continuous piecewise-affine certificate on `C_1 union C_2` exists with these two pieces if and only if

\[
u_1(z)=u_2(z)\quad\text{for every }z\in F.
\]

Because the difference `u_2-u_1` is affine and vanishes on the affine hyperplane `F`, this is equivalent to the existence of a rational scalar `tau` such that

\[
\boxed{g_2-g_1=\tau n},
\qquad
\boxed{c_2-c_1=-\tau\beta}.
\]

**Proof.** If the two certificates agree on `F`, their affine difference vanishes on the entire hyperplane. The one-dimensional annihilator of the tangent space of `F` is spanned by `n`, so its gradient is `tau n`; evaluating at any point of `F` gives the constant relation. Conversely these two relations give `u_2-u_1=tau(n\cdot z-\beta)`, which vanishes on `F`. QED.

For a **single affine** dual across both cells, `tau=0`, so both gradient and constant must agree. B2 shows why expecting this globally is too rigid: there are open cells where the root complementary shadow is forced to `(0,1)` and other open cells where it is forced to `(1,0)`.
