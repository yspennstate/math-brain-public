## 5. B3 — a global finite-horizon theorem for the continuous one-shot root problem

The preceding run used one rational off-lattice root action and then a fixed 654-action continuation. The natural stronger question is not whether one nearby rational point works, but what the **entire continuous one-shot root objective** looks like.

### Theorem 5.1 — finite rational-PWA one-shot root theorem

Fix a finite horizon `H` and a finite continuation action family with rational action coordinates. Let `V_H(D)` denote its optional-stop Bellman value on the real gap state `D>=0`. Then:

1. `V_H(D)` is a rational piecewise-affine function of `D`, with finitely many affine pieces on its nonzero support.
2. If a one-shot root action `z=(a,b)` ranges over the legal square `[0,1]^2`, the two root branch values `H_H(z)` and `L_H(z)` are rational piecewise affine on a finite rational polyhedral refinement of `[0,1]^2`.
3. Consequently
   \[
   F_H(z):=\min\{H_H(z),L_H(z)\}
   \]
   is rational PWA on a finite rational polyhedral common refinement, and its maximum over the legal square is attained at a rational vertex of that refinement (possibly a vertex created by a root equalizer intersecting a continuation breakplane or a legality/reflection facet).

### Proof

The proof is an induction on the continuation horizon.

At horizon zero, `V_0(D)=0`, which is rational affine everywhere. Assume `V_{h-1}` is rational PWA with finitely many pieces on the finite region that can influence the root in `h-1` steps. For a fixed rational continuation action `a`, each branch expression at a real state `D` is the sum of a rational affine immediate reward and `V_{h-1}` composed with an affine state map. Reflection replaces a state map `D-c` by `|D-c|`; splitting at the rational reflection boundary makes this affine on each chamber. Composing a finite rational PWA with finitely many affine/reflected-affine maps produces another finite rational PWA after inserting the finitely many translated continuation breakpoints.

Taking the minimum of the two branch functions only adds their finitely many rational equalizer points. Taking the maximum over finitely many continuation actions adds finitely many pairwise equalizer points. Taking the maximum with the optional-stop value zero adds the zero equalizers. Thus `V_h` is rational PWA with finitely many pieces. Because each action changes the gap by a bounded amount and the optional-stop value is zero, only a finite gap interval can influence the root at finite horizon, so no infinite breakpoint accumulation is relevant.

Now let the root action `z=(a,b)` vary continuously. The root immediate rewards are affine on the chambers `a>=b` and `b>=a`, while each child gap is affine or reflected-affine in `z`. Composing the fixed rational PWA continuation `V_H` with those child maps partitions the legal square by finitely many rational lines: legality facets, `a=b`, reflection facets, translated continuation breakplanes, and branch equalizers. Refining by all of them makes both root branches affine on every cell. Refining once more by `H_H=L_H` makes `F_H=min(H_H,L_H)` affine on every final cell. A linear function on a compact rational polytope attains its maximum at a vertex, and every vertex of a rational polytope is rational. This proves all three claims. QED.
