## 6. B20 — exact necessary-and-sufficient zero-shadow theorem for a finite Bellman DAG

The previous runs had examples of positive residuals with zero root effect and examples with positive root effect. The missing theorem was an exact finite criterion distinguishing the two under tied Bellman faces.

### Theorem (finite-DAG tropical shadow criterion)

Let a finite acyclic Bellman graph have old value

\[
V(s)=\max_A\min_j Q_{A,j}(s).
\]

For each state `s`, let `Opt(s)` be the set of old optimal actions and, for `A in Opt(s)`, let

\[
T_A(s)=\{j:Q_{A,j}(s)=V(s)\}
\]

be the tight recipient branches. Suppose a nonnegative one-sided perturbation induces child directional gains `d(c)>=0` and no immediate reward derivative at the current node. Then

\[
\boxed{
 d(s)=\max_{A\in\operatorname{Opt}(s)}
      \min_{j\in T_A(s)} d(c_{A,j}).
}
\]

Define the positive-shadow set recursively from the terminal perturbed sources upward by

\[
s\in P
\iff
\exists A\in\operatorname{Opt}(s)
\text{ such that }
\forall j\in T_A(s),\ c_{A,j}\in P.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{s\in P\iff d(s)>0.}
\]

Equivalently, with `Z=P^c`,

\[
\boxed{
s\in Z
\iff
\forall A\in\operatorname{Opt}(s),
\exists j\in T_A(s)
\text{ with }c_{A,j}\in Z.
}
\]

The right-hand side is an exact **blocking-selector certificate** for zero root shadow.

### Proof

Proceed by reverse topological induction on the finite DAG. The formula for `d(s)` is the preserved one-sided PWA tangent rule: only old optimal actions can be first-order active at the outer maximum, and for any such action only its old tight branches can be first-order active at the inner minimum.

Because every child derivative is nonnegative,

\[
\min_{j\in T_A(s)}d(c_{A,j})>0
\]

if and only if **every** tight child of that action has strictly positive derivative. Therefore

\[
d(s)>0
\]

if and only if at least one old optimal action has all its tight children in `P`. This is exactly the recursion defining `P`. Taking complements gives the blocking-selector characterization of `Z`: the root has zero derivative if and only if every old optimal action admits at least one tight branch leading into the zero-shadow set. QED.

This theorem explains why simple reachability is too weak. A state may be reachable along one tight branch and still have zero root influence because another tight branch blocks the minimum. Conversely, a finite blocking selector is a complete dual certificate of first-order irrelevance.

## 7. B25 — count the same gain twice: primal Bellman slack equals a tropical min-cut path cost

The robust Bellman pathwise identity already writes physical adversarial progress as a telescoping sum of Bellman slack. The root-shadow analysis suggests that the same slacks should also be the capacities of a dual min-plus influence problem. They are.

Fix a finite old-optimal action policy `pi`. For an edge/recipient branch `e=(s,j)` under that policy define its old Bellman slack

\[
q_e=Q_e-V(s)\ge0.
\]

Let a nonnegative terminal/localized perturbation assign gain `g(l)` to leaves or designated terminal support nodes. Let `G_pi(s)` denote the exact gain propagated through the **fixed old policy** under the Bellman minimum. Then

\[
G_\pi(s)=\min_{e=(s,j)}\left(q_e+G_\pi(c_e)\right).
\]

### Theorem (min-plus path expansion)

For every state `s`,

\[
\boxed{
G_\pi(s)
=
\min_{p:s\leadsto l}
\left(g(l)+\sum_{e\in p}q_e\right),
}
\]

where the minimum is over root-to-terminal paths allowed by the fixed policy. Maximizing over old-optimal policies gives

\[
\boxed{
G(s)=\max_\pi\min_{p:s\leadsto l}
\left(g(l)+\sum_{e\in p}q_e\right).
}
\]

### Proof

At a terminal node the identity is the definition `G_pi(l)=g(l)`. Suppose it holds for all children of `s`. Then

\[
G_\pi(s)
=
\min_{e=(s,j)}
\left(q_e+\min_{p:c_e\leadsto l}
\left[g(l)+\sum_{f\in p}q_f\right]\right).
\]

Absorb `q_e` into the path sum. The nested minima are exactly the minimum over all policy-consistent paths beginning at `s`, proving the formula by induction. The outer maximization over old-optimal policies gives the second identity. QED.

This is the sought double count: the quantities `q_e` are simultaneously

1. the nonbinding Bellman slacks appearing in the primal robust telescoping argument, and
2. the capacities/costs in the tropical root-shadow min-cut problem.

Tight branches have `q_e=0`; nonbinding branches are precisely the positive-cost escape routes that cap how much a localized perturbation can reach the root. Complementary-slack minimizing paths are therefore the shadow bottlenecks.

### Exact localized check and its limitation

Localize the new cell improvement only at state `(294,572)`, with terminal gain equal to the exact local residual `6622300` in the old integer scale, and propagate it through all old optimal actions using the exact max-min slack recursion. The resulting old-root gain is

\[
\boxed{5600},
\]

exactly equal to the selected path bottleneck identified in B5.

However, if the **same physical action** `(527/960,947/960)` is added globally to the Bellman action family, it is available at every state, not only at `(294,572)`. Exact fixed-point recomputation at the old `epsilon_0` gives root gain

\[
\boxed{6671200}
\]

in the same scale, far larger than `5600`.

This is not a contradiction. The localized tropical dual is exact for a perturbation supported at one state. A globally reusable physical action induces a multi-state residual vector and can alter several support regions simultaneously. Therefore a final compact continuous dual certificate must price the **whole state footprint of one action**, not merely one local cell in isolation. This is the main B25 obstruction exposed by the run.
