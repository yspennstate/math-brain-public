## 4. B8 — symbolic breakpoint discretization: a finite exact completeness theorem

The uniform `1/Q` grids were useful discovery devices, but they do not explain why a particular mesh should contain a continuous optimum. The correct finite object at any fixed horizon is instead a **rational polyhedral breakpoint automaton**.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact finite symbolic Bellman reduction

Fix rational `delta in [0,1]`. Let `g:[0,infinity)->R` be continuous, rational piecewise affine, nonincreasing, `1`-Lipschitz, and zero above some rational cutoff `R`. Define

\[
(T_\delta g)(D)=\max_{(a,b)\in[0,1]^2}\min\{H(D,a,b),L(D,a,b)\},
\]

where

\[
H=\alpha a+g(D+\alpha a+\beta b)
\]

and

\[
L=-\beta a+(C_\delta-D)_++g(|D-C_\delta|).
\]

Then:

1. `T_delta g` is continuous rational piecewise affine and vanishes for `D>=R+2`;
2. there is an explicitly finite rational polyhedral complex in `(D,a,b)` such that on every cell both `H` and `L` are affine;
3. on each fixed cell the optimization is a three-variable parametric linear program after introducing a scalar `t` with `t<=H` and `t<=L`;
4. every optimum is attained at a basic feasible solution of one of finitely many bases, and every such basis gives rational-affine functions `(a(D),b(D),t(D))` on a rational interval of `D`;
5. consequently all finite-horizon continuous values `G_h^delta` and an attaining adversarial action at every state can, in principle, be generated **exactly without a uniform action mesh** by finite rational breakpoint propagation.

### Proof

Let the finite breakpoints of `g` be

\[
0=\xi_0<\xi_1<\cdots<\xi_m=R,
\]

with `g=0` above `R`. Partition the prism

\[
D\ge0,\qquad 0\le a,b\le1
\]

by the following affine hyperplanes:

\[
a=0,\ a=1,\ b=0,\ b=1,
\]

\[
D+\alpha a+\beta b=\xi_j,
\]

and

\[
C_\delta-D=0,\qquad C_\delta-D=\xi_j,\qquad D-C_\delta=\xi_j
\]

for every breakpoint `xi_j`.

On any cell, the argument of the high branch lies in one fixed affine piece of `g`, the sign of `C_delta-D` is fixed, and the absolute-value argument of the low branch lies in one fixed affine piece of `g`. Therefore both `H` and `L` are affine functions of `(D,a,b)` on that cell.

Introduce `t` and maximize it subject to

\[
t\le H(D,a,b),\qquad t\le L(D,a,b),
\]

together with the finitely many affine inequalities defining the cell and the action box. For fixed `D` this is a bounded linear program in `(a,b,t)`: the action set is compact and `t` is bounded by the two branch values. Hence an optimal face contains a basic feasible solution.

Choose any possible set of three linearly independent active constraints. Because every coefficient is rational and the only free parameter is `D`, solving that basis gives

\[
a(D)=a_0+a_1D,\qquad b(D)=b_0+b_1D,\qquad t(D)=t_0+t_1D
\]

with rational coefficients, unless the proposed basis is singular, in which case it is discarded. Feasibility of that basis is a finite collection of rational affine inequalities in `D`, hence holds on a rational interval (possibly empty or a point). There are finitely many cells and finitely many bases per cell, so there are finitely many rational-affine candidate value templates.

The value `T_delta g(D)` is the upper envelope of those finitely many feasible templates. Intersections of two rational-affine templates and endpoints of basis-feasibility intervals are rational. Thus `T_delta g` is rational piecewise affine and its maximizing actions can be selected from this finite generated family.

Finally, if `D>=R+2`, then `C_delta<=alpha+beta=2`, so the low child gap satisfies `D-C_delta>=R`, while the high child gap is at least `D>=R`. Both continuation values vanish. The high direct term is `alpha a>=0` and the nonreflection low direct term is `-beta a<=0`, so their minimum is at most zero; action `(0,0)` attains zero. Hence `T_delta g(D)=0` for `D>=R+2`.

Starting from `G_0=0`, induction proves all five claims for every finite horizon. QED.

This is stronger than the previously recorded statement that finite-horizon values are piecewise linear: it gives a **complete finite candidate-generation principle** for continuous optimal actions, and therefore a route to exact symbolic policies that does not assume a mesh contains the optimum.

**Third piece.** Here `G_2(x)=9/5-x<=A`, the lower feasibility bound is zero because `x>beta`, and the balancing point remains below the upper feasibility bound. Indeed at the worst endpoint the required inequality has exact slack

## 7. B22 — read the published bottlenecks, not just their headline constants

### 7.1 Du--Sun reciprocal-potential architecture

Du--Sun's exact-MIV lower-bound algorithm uses per-agent remaining margins and the reciprocal potential

\[
\Psi=\sum_i\frac1{q_i}.
\]

The paper's one-step accounting is tuned to the half-proportional target: the target coefficient is `1/(2n)`, and the aggregate potential-change budget closes at the corresponding factor `2 beta n = 1`. This is not merely a cosmetic choice of constant. Raising the same target while leaving the reciprocal architecture unchanged breaks the final one-step budget, consistent with I0130's earlier direct first-item obstruction to a naive parameter increase.

Thus the Du--Sun proof failure above `1/2` is architectural: the reciprocal barrier has insufficient recipient-side compensation once the target coefficient is raised.

### 7.2 Neoh--Teh v2 changes the potential family

Current v2 indexing for Neoh--Teh records the improved `19/30` guarantee. Its current full-text overview describes a different global potential of the form

\[
\Phi=\sum_i f(s_i+\rho),
\qquad
\rho=\frac{19}{30n},
\]

with a specially designed decreasing convex function `f`; the recipient is chosen to minimize the resulting potential. The improvement from `1/2` to `19/30` therefore does not come from simply retuning the Du--Sun reciprocal constant. It comes from changing the shape of the scalar barrier and proving additional calculus inequalities.

I did not recover a primary-text v2 PDF in this runtime, so no new theorem is imported from a secondary rendering and no claim is made about the exact optimality conditions of their chosen `f`. The current `19/30` theorem itself is supported by the current v2 record; the structural description above is used only to identify a route.

### 7.3 The missing structural inequality suggested by the upper game

Both lower-bound approaches are fundamentally **separable across agents**: their global certificate is a sum of per-agent scalar slack penalties. The two-agent upper game, in contrast, shows that the adversary's leverage is concentrated at the coupled reflection surface

\[
D=C_\delta=\beta a+\alpha b,
\]

where the identity of the lower tilted-safety agent changes. The reflection-credit identity from the preceding reports makes this coupling explicit.

This suggests a precise missing ingredient for a stronger universal lower theorem: a one-step potential certificate that depends on the **pairwise gap/reflection state**, not only on separate scalar slacks. Symbolically, one should look for

\[
P(x,D)=p(x)+h(D)
\quad\text{or more generally}\quad
P(z_1,z_2)
\]

whose allocation-side drift inequality remains nonpositive on both sides of `D=C_delta`, with the reflection term paid explicitly. A stronger separable `sum f(s_i)` may still exist, but nothing in the present route proves it; the Bellman geometry identifies exactly where such a proof must defeat the adversary.

This is a route diagnosis, not metric movement.
