## 4. B11 — dualize: robust Bellman value that prices parameter sensitivity

The previous run proved a fixed-tree transport lemma by charging a path the quantity `sum max(a_t,b_t)`. B11 asks to optimize that sensitivity charge *inside* the Bellman recursion.

Fix `epsilon>=0` and the legal action grid

\[
\mathcal A_{24}=\{(i/24,j/24):0\le i,j\le24\}.
\]

For an action `(a,b)` write

\[
m(a,b)=\max\{a,b\}.
\]

Define the robust Bellman value by

\[
R_0^\epsilon(D)=0
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
R_h^\epsilon(D)=
\max_{(a,b)\in\mathcal A_{24}}
\min\left\{
 c_H-\epsilon m(a,b)+R_{h-1}^\epsilon(D_H),
 c_L-\epsilon m(a,b)+R_{h-1}^\epsilon(D_L)
\right\}.
}
\]

This is a genuine dual/Pareto value: baseline safety decrease is rewarded, while valuation sensitivity to increasing `delta` is charged at price `epsilon` on every branch.

### Lemma 4.1 — policy interpretation

For every `h,D,epsilon`, there exists an adaptive adversarial policy tree extracted from the maximizing actions in the recurrence such that every root-to-leaf allocation branch `P` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_0(P)-\epsilon S(P)\ge R_h^\epsilon(D),
}
\]

where `Delta_0(P)` is the total baseline decrease of the lower tilted-safety coordinate accumulated along the sorted-state recursion and

\[
S(P)=\sum_{t\in P}\max\{a_t,b_t\}.
\]

#### Proof

Induct on `h`. The claim is trivial for `h=0`. For `h>=1`, choose any action attaining the finite maximum in the displayed recurrence. If the deterministic algorithm allocates to the branch labelled `H`, the first-step contribution to `Delta_0-epsilon S` is exactly `c_H-epsilon m(a,b)` and the child gap is `D_H`; apply the induction hypothesis to the extracted policy at that child. The `L` branch is identical with `c_L,D_L`. Since the recurrence takes the minimum of the two branch values, both choices satisfy the claimed lower bound. QED.

No probabilistic or average-branch statement is used.

### Theorem 6.1

Fix the baseline `delta_0=1/10`. Suppose for some `epsilon>=0`, horizon `h`, and legal finite action set, the robust Bellman value satisfies

\[

\]

\[
\delta_1=\delta_0+\epsilon
\]

and forces some agent to have PROP1 factor at most

\[
1-\delta_1.
\]

### Proof

Take the policy supplied by Lemma 4.1 and an arbitrary allocation-history leaf `P`. Let `Delta_0(P)` and `S(P)` be as above. The robust certificate gives

\[
\Delta_0(P)-\epsilon S(P)
\ge \frac{13}{10}+3\epsilon.
\]

At baseline `delta_0`, the lower starting tilted safety is `13/10`. Hence the final minimum baseline tilted safety satisfies

\[
\min_i z_i^{\delta_0}
\le \frac{13}{10}-\Delta_0(P)
\le -\epsilon(3+S(P)).
\]

Choose a physical agent `i` attaining that final minimum at this leaf. Changing only the target parameter from `delta_0` to `delta_1=delta_0+epsilon` leaves the goods, allocation history, bundle value, total value, and one-good correction unchanged. Therefore

\[
z_i^{\delta_1}=z_i^{\delta_0}+\epsilon V_i.
\]

The first three common unit goods contribute exactly `3` to `V_i`. On every post-unit good of the path, physical agent `i` values that good by one of the two displayed coordinates and therefore by at most `max(a_t,b_t)`. Consequently

\[
V_i\le 3+S(P).
\]

Thus

\[
z_i^{\delta_1}
\le -\epsilon(3+S(P))+\epsilon V_i
\le0.
\]

By the definition of tilted safety,

\[
2(B_i+R_i)\le (1-\delta_1)V_i,
\]

so agent `i` has PROP1 factor at most `1-delta_1`.

The action tree is finite, adaptive only to earlier allocation decisions, and consists entirely of legal values in `[0,1]`. The initial unit goods make `p_1=p_2=1` exactly. The horizon is not supplied to the deterministic algorithm. Hence the adversary is valid in the source model. QED.
