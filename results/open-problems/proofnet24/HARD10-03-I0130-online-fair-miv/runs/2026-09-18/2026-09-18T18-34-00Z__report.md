## 5. B2 ATTACK THE NEGATION — reachability alone is not the correct theorem

The stronger claim suggested by the previous run would be:

> every positive local residual at a root-reachable tied support state forces a positive root gain.

That statement is false in general, for an exact two-child Bellman reason.

### Exact tied-face counterexample

Consider a state `s` with one old optimal action `A` and two **distinct** tight children `x,y`, with old branch values both equal to zero:

\[
Q_{A,x}=Q_{A,y}=0,
\qquad V(s)=\min\{0,0\}=0.
\]

Both children are root-reachable tight support states. Now increase only the value of `x` by one unit while leaving `y` unchanged. Then

\[
V_{\rm new}(s)=\min\{1,0\}=0.
\]

So positive gain at a reachable tight child need not move its parent, hence need not move the root. The missing condition is **tropical coverage**: for at least one old optimal action, every distinct tight child must receive positive propagated gain.

This is precisely the tied-face tangent rule already proved in the previous run,

\[

      \min_{j:q_{A,j}=0}d(c_{A,j}).
\]
