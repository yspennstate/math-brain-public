## 4. B6 — continuous deformation to exact polyhedral separation

### 4.1 Exact cell theorem

The previous run left open a complete separation oracle over the continuous action square. This run proves the finite reduction needed for such an oracle.

Let `phi:[0,infinity)->R` be continuous rational piecewise affine with finitely many affine pieces on its support. For fixed rational `epsilon`, define

\[
H_\phi(D,a,b)
=\alpha_0a-\epsilon\max(a,b)
 +\phi(D+\alpha_0a+\beta_0b),
\]

and

\[
L_\phi(D,a,b)
=-\beta_0a+(\beta_0a+\alpha_0b-D)_+
 -\epsilon\max(a,b)
 +\phi\bigl(|D-(\beta_0a+\alpha_0b)|\bigr).
\]

The Bellman residual against `phi` is

\[
\rho_\phi(D,a,b)
=\min\{H_\phi(D,a,b),L_\phi(D,a,b)\}-\phi(D).
\]

Partition `(D,a,b)` space by all of the following finite data:

1. the affine interval of `phi` containing `D`;
2. the affine interval of `phi` containing `D+alpha_0 a+beta_0 b`;
3. the affine interval of `phi` containing `|D-(beta_0a+alpha_0b)|`;
4. the reflection sign `D >= beta_0a+alpha_0b` or its reverse;
5. the action-order sign `a>=b` or `b>=a`, which linearizes `max(a,b)`.

On each resulting cell every absolute value, positive part, maximum, and occurrence of `phi` has a fixed affine formula. Hence both `H_phi` and `L_phi`, as well as `phi(D)`, are affine rational functions of `(D,a,b)`.

Introduce one lifted variable `t` and solve

\[
\max\ t-\phi(D)
\]

subject to

\[
t\le H_\phi(D,a,b),\qquad
 t\le L_\phi(D,a,b),
\]

plus the rational linear inequalities defining that cell and `0<=a,b<=1`. This is a rational linear program in `(D,a,b,t)`.

Therefore every cell optimum is attained at a basic feasible solution whenever the cell is bounded in the relevant support region. Because there are finitely many cells and finitely many LP bases per cell, the global continuous Bellman-residual maximum over the supported region is obtained by a finite set of exact rational candidates. In particular, the necessary candidate set includes lifted equalizer vertices where `H_phi=L_phi`; it is **not** sufficient merely to inspect vertices of the unlifted `(D,a,b)` cell.

This proves:

> **Finite exact continuous-separation theorem.** For a continuous rational piecewise-affine finite-support continuation `phi`, the global continuous robust Bellman residual can be computed exactly by finitely many rational LP vertex/equalizer calculations.

This theorem is stronger than a uniform-mesh convergence statement: it gives an exact finite route to either a continuous super-solution certificate or a globally strongest violated cell.

### 4.2 Quantitative residual stability

There is also a coarse quantitative deformation bound. Suppose `phi` is 1-Lipschitz. For two points

\[
u=(D,a,b),\qquad u'=(D',a',b'),
\]

write

\[
d_D=|D-D'|,\qquad d_a=|a-a'|,\qquad d_b=|b-b'|.
\]

For the high branch, using `|max(a,b)-max(a',b')|<=d_a+d_b`,

\[
|H_\phi(u)-H_\phi(u')|
\le d_D+(2\beta_0+\epsilon)(d_a+d_b).
\]

For the low branch, the 1-Lipschitz properties of the positive part, absolute value, and `phi` give

\[
|L_\phi(u)-L_\phi(u')|
\le 2d_D+(3\beta_0+\epsilon)(d_a+d_b).
\]

Finally the subtraction of `phi(D)` costs at most another `d_D`, while `min` is nonexpansive in the sup norm. Thus

\[
\boxed{
|\rho_\phi(u)-\rho_\phi(u')|
\le
3d_D+(3\beta_0+\epsilon)(d_a+d_b).
}
\]

Consequently any exact positive violated action persists on an explicit neighborhood, and conversely a finite net with a rigorously negative margin can certify an entire cell. The exact LP theorem above is preferable because it eliminates the need for a mesh limit, but this stability estimate supplies a separate deformation guarantee.
