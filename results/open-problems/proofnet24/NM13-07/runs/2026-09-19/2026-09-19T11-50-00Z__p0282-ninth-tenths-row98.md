## 6. New group-algebra moment identities

Write `j=n-k`,

`F_j(n)=ell_{n,n-j}`,

and retain the exact hook factorization, valid in the stable long-first-row range,

`F_j(n)=((n)_j^2/j!) A_j(n)`.                                      (6.1)

For a partition `mu |- j`, let

`C(mu)=S_1(mu)=sum_{box in mu} c(box)`,

`S_q(mu)=sum_{box in mu} c(box)^q`.                                 (6.2)

Let `X_i=sum_{a<i}(a i)` be the Jucys--Murphy elements, `P_q=sum_i X_i^q`, and `T=P_1`, the transposition class sum. On an irreducible `V^mu`, symmetric polynomials in the `X_i` act with eigenvalues given by the same symmetric polynomials in tableau contents. For any central group-algebra element `Z`, normalized regular trace is its identity coefficient `[e]Z`, while regular-representation decomposition makes the same trace the Plancherel average of its scalar eigenvalue. Therefore products of the `S_q` may be computed as identity coefficients of products of `P_q`.

The four genuinely new averages needed at order eight are:

`E[C^4] = j(j-1)(3j^2+j-12)/4`,                                    (6.3)

`E[C S_5] = j(j-1)(5j^2-15j+11)/2`,                                (6.4)

`E[S_2 S_4] = j(j-1)(4j^3+3j^2-35j+32)/12`,                        (6.5)

`E[S_3^2] = j(j-1)(9j^2-29j+24)/4`.                                (6.6)

Here and below `E` means Plancherel expectation on partitions of `j`.

### 6.1 Proof of (6.3)

Put `N=binom(j,2)`. The class expansion of `T^2` has:

- identity coefficient `N`;
- coefficient `3` on each 3-cycle;
- coefficient `2` on each product of two disjoint transpositions.

Indeed, these are exactly the ways to multiply two transpositions to obtain the indicated cycle type. There are `2 binom(j,3)` 3-cycles and `3 binom(j,4)` double transpositions. Since `C` is the eigenvalue of `T`,

`E[C^4]=[e]T^4=[e](T^2)^2`

`= N^2 + 9*2 binom(j,3) + 4*3 binom(j,4)`

`= j(j-1)(3j^2+j-12)/4`.                                           (6.7)

### 6.2 Proof of (6.4)

`E[C S_5]=[e]T P_5`, the total transposition coefficient mass of `P_5`.

Fix a star center with `m` leaves. A length-five word in its star transpositions can have transposition product only with support on one, two, or three distinct leaves. The exact numbers per chosen support are respectively

`1, 30, 60`.                                                        (6.8)

For two leaves, all `2^5-2=30` words using both have odd product in `S_3`, hence a transposition. For three leaves, direct pairing by multiplicity pattern gives 60 transposition words; words using four or five leaves cannot reduce to a transposition because a leaf occurring exactly once cannot be fixed by any other star generator. Thus the total transposition mass for center size `m` is

`m + 30 binom(m,2) + 60 binom(m,3)`

`= m(10m^2-15m+6)`.                                                (6.9)

Summing `m=0,...,j-1` gives exactly (6.4).

### 6.3 Proof of (6.5)

The retained exact class identity is

`P_2 = N e + K_3`,                                                   (6.10)

where `K_3` is the sum of all 3-cycles. Also the retained fourth content moment is

`E[S_4]=j(j-1)(4j-5)/6`.                                            (6.11)

For one star with `m` leaves, a length-four word using exactly two leaves has 10 words with 3-cycle product, while one using exactly three leaves has 24; support four cannot give a 3-cycle. Hence the total 3-cycle coefficient mass in `P_4` is

`sum_{m=0}^{j-1} [10 binom(m,2)+24 binom(m,3)]`.                    (6.12)

Since `P_4` is central, every 3-cycle has the same coefficient. Dividing (6.12) by the number `2 binom(j,3)` of 3-cycles gives coefficient `3j-4`. Therefore

`E[S_2 S_4]=[e]P_2P_4`

`= N E[S_4] + 2 binom(j,3)(3j-4)`,                                 (6.13)

which simplifies to (6.5).

### 6.4 Proof of (6.6)

A length-three star word has transposition product from exact support one or two, with counts `1` and `6`, and 4-cycle product from exact support three, with count `6`. Summing over star centers shows

`P_3=(2j-3)T+K_4`,                                                   (6.14)

where `K_4` is the sum of all 4-cycles. There are `6 binom(j,4)` 4-cycles. Hence

`E[S_3^2]=[e]P_3^2`

`= N(2j-3)^2+6 binom(j,4)`,                                        (6.15)

which is (6.6).
