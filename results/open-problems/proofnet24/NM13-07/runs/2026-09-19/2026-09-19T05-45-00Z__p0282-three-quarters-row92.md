## 5. New theorem: Plancherel-content cancellation gives an `n^(3/4)` defect window

Write

`j=n-k`, `E_j(n)=ell_{n,n-j}`.

The earlier exact hook factorization gives, for `n>=2j`,

`E_j(n)=((n)_j^2/j!) A_j(n)`,                                      (5.1)

where under Plancherel measure on partitions `mu|-j`,

`A_j(n)=E_mu[R_mu(n,j)^2]`,                                         (5.2)

`R_mu(n,j)=prod_{c=1}^{mu_1} (n-j-c+1)/(n-j-c+1+mu'_c)`.           (5.3)

It also gives the exact Chen ratio

`Q_j(n):=E_j(n)^2/(E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n))`

`=((j+1)/j) ((n-j+1)/(n-j))^2 A_j(n)^2/(A_{j-1}(n)A_{j+1}(n)).`    (5.4)

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-THREE-QUARTERS-WINDOW-02`

For every integer `j>=1`, strict Chen log-concavity holds at defect `j` whenever

`n^3 >= 512 (j+1)^4`.                                               (5.5)

Equivalently, every source-valid index with

`j+1 <= n^(3/4)/512^(1/4)`                                         (5.6)

is strictly log-concave. Thus the previously proved uniform `Theta(n^(2/3))` long-LIS boundary window is enlarged to a source-faithful `Theta(n^(3/4))` window.

This is a restricted theorem. It does **not** settle the central regime where `j` is a positive fraction of `n`, so the parent source remains OPEN.

### Proof

Fix `j>=1`. For `mu|-j`, put

`h_c=mu'_c`, `a_c=j+c-1`, `d_c=n-a_c`, `z_c=h_c/d_c`.

Under (5.5), `n>=8(j+1)^(4/3)>=4(j+1)`, so in particular `n>=4j`. Therefore

`d_c >= n-2j+1 > n/2`, `0<=z_c<=1/2`.                              (5.7)

From (5.3),

`log R_mu = -sum_c log(1+z_c)`.                                    (5.8)

For `0<=z<=1/2`, alternating Taylor expansion gives

`-log(1+z)=-z+z^2/2+r(z)`, `|r(z)|<=z^3/3`.                         (5.9)

We now expand the first two sums one order deeper than the old `n^(2/3)` proof.

First,

`1/(n-a)=1/n+a/n^2+a^2/[n^2(n-a)]`.                                (5.10)

Since `a_c<2j`, `d_c>n/2`, and `sum h_c=j`,

`|sum_c h_c a_c^2/[n^2 d_c]| <= 8 j^3/n^3`.                        (5.11)

Second,

`0 <= 1/d_c^2-1/n^2`

`=a_c(n+d_c)/(n^2 d_c^2) <= 16j/n^3`.                              (5.12)

Because `sum h_c^2<=j^2`, the error made by replacing

`(1/2)sum h_c^2/d_c^2`

by `(1/(2n^2))sum h_c^2` is at most `8j^3/n^3`.                    (5.13)

Finally, by `d_c>n/2` and `sum h_c^3<=j^2 sum h_c=j^3`,

`sum_c z_c^3/3 <= 8j^3/(3n^3)`.                                    (5.14)

Combining (5.8)--(5.14), with a safe rounded constant, gives

`log R_mu`

`= -j/n + [-sum_c h_c(j+c-1)+(1/2)sum_c h_c^2]/n^2 + rho_mu`,      (5.15)

`|rho_mu| <= 20 j^3/n^3`.                                          (5.16)

The bracket in (5.15) has a clean representation-theoretic form. Let

`n(mu)=sum_r (r-1)mu_r`,

and let the content sum be

`C(mu)=sum_{(r,c) in mu}(c-r)=n(mu')-n(mu)`.                        (5.17)

The elementary Young-diagram identities

`sum_c h_c=j`,

`sum_c (c-1)h_c=n(mu')`,

`sum_c h_c^2=j+2n(mu)`                                              (5.18)

turn the bracket into

`-j^2+j/2-C(mu)`.                                                    (5.19)

Hence

`2 log R_mu = D_j(n) - 2C(mu)/n^2 + xi_mu`,                         (5.20)

where

`D_j(n)=-2j/n+(-2j^2+j)/n^2`,                                      (5.21)

`|xi_mu|<=40j^3/n^3`.                                               (5.22)

Now use Plancherel symmetry. Conjugation preserves tableau dimension:

`f^(mu')=f^mu`,

while

`C(mu')=-C(mu)`.                                                     (5.23)

Therefore the Plancherel law of `C(mu)` is symmetric and in particular has mean zero. Let

`J=j(j-1)/2`.

Every size-`j` diagram satisfies `|C(mu)|<=J`. For `t=2/n^2`, symmetry gives

`E[e^{-tC}]=E[cosh(tC)] >=1`.                                       (5.24)

Using the standard elementary inequality `cosh x<=exp(x^2/2)`,

`0 <= log E[e^{-2C/n^2}] <= 2J^2/n^4 <= j^4/(2n^4)`.              (5.25)

Since `n>=4j`, the last quantity is at most

`(1/8)j^3/n^3`.                                                      (5.26)

From (5.2), (5.20), and the uniform bound (5.22),

`e^{-40j^3/n^3} E[e^{-2C/n^2}]`

`<= e^{-D_j(n)} A_j(n)`

`<= e^{40j^3/n^3} E[e^{-2C/n^2}]`.                                 (5.27)

Thus

`log A_j(n)=D_j(n)+epsilon_j(n)`,                                   (5.28)

with the explicit bound

`|epsilon_j(n)| <= 41 j^3/n^3`.                                    (5.29)

This is the decisive gain: the old shape-uniform estimate paid `O(j^2/n^2)` in `log A_j`; conjugation symmetry removes that term **after Plancherel averaging**, leaving `O(j^3/n^3)`.

The deterministic approximation (5.21) has favorable adjacent-defect curvature:

`2D_j-D_{j-1}-D_{j+1}=4/n^2`.                                      (5.30)

Take logarithms in the exact ratio (5.4), insert (5.28), and use (5.30). Since (5.5) implies `n>=4(j+1)`, all three error bounds for `j-1,j,j+1` apply. We obtain

`log Q_j(n)`

`>= log(1+1/j)+2log(1+1/(n-j))+4/n^2`

`   - 41[2j^3+(j-1)^3+(j+1)^3]/n^3`.                               (5.31)

The cubic bracket is

`4j^3+6j <=10j^3`.                                                   (5.32)

Also `log(1+1/j)>1/(j+1)`, while the next two terms in (5.31) are positive. Hence

`log Q_j(n) > 1/(j+1) - 410j^3/n^3`.                               (5.33)

Under (5.5),

`410j^3/n^3 <= (410/512) j^3/(j+1)^4 < 1/(j+1)`.                   (5.34)

Therefore `log Q_j(n)>0`, so `Q_j(n)>1`, i.e.

`E_j(n)^2>E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n)`.

This is exactly strict Chen log-concavity at `k=n-j`. QED.
