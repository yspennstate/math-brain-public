### Proof

For `j<=66`, Chen is already proved for all source-valid `n` by the retained fixed-defect theorem. It suffices to treat `j>=67` under (5.5).

Since `1024=4^5`, (5.5) gives

`n >= 4 (j+1)^(6/5)`.                                                (5.7)

For `j>=67`, `(j+1)^(1/5)>2`, hence

`n >= 8(j+1) > 8j`.                                                  (5.8)

For `mu|-j`, put

`h_c=mu'_c`, `a_c=j+c-1`, `d_c=n-a_c`, `z_c=h_c/d_c`.

Since `a_c<=2j-1<n/4`,

`d_c>3n/4`, `0<=z_c<=1/6`.                                         (5.9)

From (5.3),

`log R_mu = -sum_c log(1+z_c)`.                                    (5.10)

Expanding through fourth order gives

`log R_mu`

`= -j/n + B_2(mu)/n^2+B_3(mu)/n^3+B_4(mu)/n^4+rho_mu`,             (5.11)

where the predecessor established

`B_2(mu)=-j^2+j/2-C(mu)`,                                           (5.12)

`B_3(mu)=-j^3+j^2-j/3 +(1-2j)C(mu)-S_2(mu)`.                       (5.13)

Here

`C(mu)=sum_{(r,c) in mu}(c-r)`,

`S_m(mu)=sum_{(r,c) in mu}(c-r)^m`.                                 (5.14)

The new fourth coefficient is, directly from the `z,z^2,z^3,z^4` terms,

`B_4(mu)`

`= -sum_c h_c a_c^3 +(3/2)sum_c h_c^2 a_c^2`

`  -sum_c h_c^3 a_c +(1/4)sum_c h_c^4`.                            (5.15)

Use the elementary forward-difference identities

`h^m=sum_{v=0}^{h-1}[(v+1)^m-v^m]`                                 (5.16)

for `m=1,2,3,4`, and write `u=c-1`, `v=r-1`, `x=u-v` for cell content. The contribution of a single cell to (5.15) simplifies to

`-j^3 +(3/2)j^2-j+1/4`

`+(-3j^2+3j-1)x+(-3j+3/2)x^2-x^3`.                                (5.17)

Summing over the `j` cells therefore yields the exact identity

`B_4(mu)`

`= -j^4 +(3/2)j^3-j^2+j/4`

`  +(-3j^2+3j-1)C(mu)+(-3j+3/2)S_2(mu)-S_3(mu)`.                  (5.18)

We now center its Plancherel statistics. Conjugation of Young diagrams preserves Plancherel weight while sending every content to its negative, so

`E[C]=0`, `E[S_3]=0`.                                                (5.19)

The predecessor proved, using Jucys--Murphy elements `X_i=sum_{a<i}(a i)`, that

`E[S_2]=J:=j(j-1)/2`.                                                (5.20)

The new second-moment identity is

`E[C^2]=J`.                                                         (5.21)

To prove (5.21), let

`T=sum_i X_i=sum_{1<=a<b<=j}(a b)`,                                 (5.22)

the central sum of all transpositions. On the irreducible `V^mu`, `T` acts by the scalar `C(mu)`, so `T^2` acts by `C(mu)^2`. In the regular representation the normalized trace of left multiplication by a group-algebra element equals its identity coefficient. The identity coefficient of `T^2` is exactly the number of transpositions, because a product of two transpositions is the identity iff they are equal. Hence that coefficient is `binom(j,2)=J`. Decomposing the regular representation as `oplus_mu f^mu V^mu` identifies the same normalized trace with the Plancherel average of `C(mu)^2`, proving (5.21).

Taking expectations in (5.18) and using (5.19)--(5.20),

`E[B_4]=-j^4+(5/4)j^2-j/2`.                                        (5.23)

The fourth-order deterministic part of `2 log R_mu` therefore contributes

`-2j^4+(5/2)j^2-j`.                                                  (5.24)

There is one more deterministic contribution because `A_j` is an expectation of an exponential. The centered order-`n^-2` random term in `2 log R_mu` is

`Y_2=-2C/n^2`.                                                       (5.25)

By (5.21),

`(1/2)E[Y_2^2]=2E[C^2]/n^4=j(j-1)/n^4`.                            (5.26)

Thus define

`D_j^(4)(n)`

`= -2j/n +(-2j^2+j)/n^2`

`  +(-2j^3+j^2+j/3)/n^3`

`  +(-2j^4+(7/2)j^2-2j)/n^4`.                                     (5.27)

It remains to make the error explicit.

For `0<=z<=1/6`, the alternating Taylor remainder after `z^4` is at most `z^5/5`. Expanding the reciprocal factors `(n-a)^-m` only as far as needed to collect terms through `n^-4`, and using `a<2j`, `d>3n/4`, `sum h_c=j`, `sum h_c^m<=j^m`, gives the following safe fifth-order bounds:

- from the linear reciprocal tail: `(64/3) j^5/n^5`;
- from the quadratic reciprocal tail: `24 j^5/n^5`;
- from the cubic reciprocal tail: `(40/3) j^5/n^5`;
- from the quartic reciprocal tail: `<(9/2) j^5/n^5`;
- from the logarithm Taylor remainder: `(1024/1215)j^5/n^5`.

Their sum is `<69 j^5/n^5`; hence

`|rho_mu| <=69 j^5/n^5`.                                            (5.28)

After doubling (5.11), subtracting the deterministic means in (5.27), and before the variance correction (5.26), write the centered random pieces as

`Y_2=-2C/n^2`,                                                       (5.29)

`Y_3=[2(1-2j)C-2(S_2-J)]/n^3`,                                     (5.30)

`Y_4=[2(-3j^2+3j-1)C+2(-3j+3/2)(S_2-J)-2S_3]/n^4`.               (5.31)

Each has mean zero. The elementary diagram bounds

`|C|<=j^2/2`, `|S_2-J|<=j^3`, `|S_3|<=j^4`                         (5.32)

imply, with `t=j/n<=1/8`,

`|Y_2|<=t^2`, `|Y_3|<=4t^3`, `|Y_4|<=11t^4`.                       (5.33)

Let `W=Y_2+Y_3+Y_4`, `Z=Y_3+Y_4`. Then

`|Z|<=(43/8)t^3`, `|W|<=(107/64)t^2<1/32`.                         (5.34)

Consequently

`|(1/2)E[W^2]-(1/2)E[Y_2^2]|`

`<= E|Y_2 Z| +(1/2)E[Z^2]`

`<= (43/8)t^5 +(1849/128)t^6 <7.2 t^5`.                            (5.35)

Because `E[W]=0` and `|W|<1/32`, Taylor's theorem for `e^W` and `log(1+u)` gives the elementary bound

`|log E[e^W]-(1/2)E[W^2]|<0.4t^5`.                                 (5.36)

For completeness, one may use `|e^x-1-x-x^2/2|<=e^(1/32)|x|^3/6`, `|W|<=2t^2`, Jensen's `E[e^W]>=1`, and `0<=u-log(1+u)<=u^2/2`; at `t<=1/8` these bounds are already below the displayed constant.

Finally, the doubled pointwise remainder in (5.28) has absolute value at most `138t^5`. Uniform perturbation of an exponential expectation by a term bounded by `delta` perturbs its logarithm by at most `delta`. Combining this with (5.26), (5.35), and (5.36) gives the safe explicit expansion

`log A_j(n)=D_j^(4)(n)+epsilon_j(n)`,                               (5.37)

`|epsilon_j(n)|<=150 j^5/n^5`,                                     (5.38)

whenever `n>=8j`.

The deterministic approximation has favorable exact discrete curvature. Direct calculation gives

`2D_j^(4)-D_{j-1}^(4)-D_{j+1}^(4)`

`=4/n^2 +(12j-2)/n^3 +(24j^2-3)/n^4 >0`.                           (5.39)

All three expansions for `j-1,j,j+1` are valid because (5.8) gives `n>=8(j+1)`. The error curvature satisfies

`2epsilon_j-epsilon_{j-1}-epsilon_{j+1}`

`>= -150[2j^5+(j-1)^5+(j+1)^5]/n^5`

`>= -600(j+1)^5/n^5`.                                               (5.40)

Taking logs in the exact ratio (5.4), using

`log(1+1/j)>=1/(j+1)`,                                               (5.41)

`600(j+1)^5/n^5 <= 75/[128(j+1)]`,                                 (5.43)

`log Q_j(n)>=53/[128(j+1)]>0`.                                      (5.44)
