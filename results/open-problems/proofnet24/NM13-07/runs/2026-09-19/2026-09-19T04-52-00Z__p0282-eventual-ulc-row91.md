## 3. New theorem: eventual strict ULC for every fixed defect

For `j=n-k`, put

`E_j(n) := ell_{n,n-j}`.

The stronger binomial-normalized inequality is

`ell_{n,k}^2 C(n-1,k-2) C(n-1,k)`

`> ell_{n,k-1} ell_{n,k+1} C(n-1,k-1)^2`.                    (ULC)

Since

`C(n-1,k-1)^2 / (C(n-1,k-2) C(n-1,k))`

`= k(n-k+1)/((k-1)(n-k)) > 1`

at every source-valid interior `k`, (ULC) strictly implies Chen's source inequality.

In defect coordinates, (ULC) is equivalent to

`M_j(n) := j(n-j-1)E_j(n)^2`

`          - (j+1)(n-j)E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n) > 0`.           (1)

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-EVENTUAL-ULC-FIXED-DEFECT-01`

For every fixed integer `j>=1`, there exists an integer `N_j` such that for every integer `n>=N_j`, with `k=n-j`, the strict inequality (ULC) holds. Consequently Chen's fixed-size LIS log-concavity inequality holds strictly for all sufficiently large `n` along **every fixed-defect diagonal** `n-k=j`.

This theorem is qualitative in `j`: it supplies an existential threshold `N_j`, not a uniform bound as `j` grows. It therefore does not settle the central regime where `j` grows with `n`.

### Proof

Fix `j>=1`. For `n>=2j`, set `N=n-j`. Every partition of `n` with first row exactly `N` is uniquely of the form

`lambda=(N,mu)`

with `mu |- j`, because `N>=j>=mu_1`. By RSK,

`E_j(n) = sum_{mu|-j} (f^(N,mu))^2`,                       (2)

where `f^lambda` is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape `lambda`.

Let `mu'_c` be the column lengths of `mu`. In the hook product of `(N,mu)`, every hook below the first row is exactly the corresponding hook of `mu`. In the first row, column `c<=mu_1` has hook length

`N-c+1+mu'_c`,

while columns `c>mu_1` have their ordinary one-row hook lengths `N-c+1`. Hence the hook-length formula factors exactly as

`f^(N,mu)`

`= C(n,j) f^mu prod_{c=1}^{mu_1} (N-c+1)/(N-c+1+mu'_c)`.   (3)

Here the factor `f^mu` appears because the hook product of `mu` is `j!/f^mu`.

For fixed `j` and fixed `mu|-j`, as `n -> infinity`,

`C(n,j)`

`= n^j/j! * (1 - j(j-1)/(2n) + O_j(n^-2))`,               (4)

and, because `sum_c mu'_c=j`,

`prod_c (N-c+1)/(N-c+1+mu'_c)`

`= 1 - j/n + O_j(n^-2)`.                                   (5)

There are only finitely many partitions of the fixed integer `j`, so the error can be taken uniformly over `mu|-j`. Multiplying (4) and (5) in (3) gives

`f^(N,mu)`

`= (f^mu/j!) n^j (1 - j(j+1)/(2n) + O_j(n^-2))`.           (6)

Squaring (6), substituting into (2), and using the classical regular-representation identity

`sum_{mu|-j} (f^mu)^2 = j!`,                               (7)

gives

`E_j(n)`

`= n^(2j)/j! * (1 - j(j+1)/n + O_j(n^-2))`.                (8)

Thus if

`a_j=1/j!`, `d_j=j(j+1)`,

then

`E_j(n)=a_j n^(2j)(1-d_j/n+O_j(n^-2))`.                    (9)

Insert (9) for `j-1,j,j+1` into (1). The first term is

`j(n-j-1)E_j(n)^2`

`= j a_j^2 n^(4j+1)`

`  * [1-(j+1)(2j+1)/n+O_j(n^-2)]`.                         (10)

For the second term, note

`(j+1)a_{j-1}a_{j+1}=j a_j^2`,                             (11)

and

`d_{j-1}+d_{j+1}=2j^2+2j+2`.

Therefore

`(j+1)(n-j)E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n)`

`= j a_j^2 n^(4j+1)`

`  * [1-(2j^2+3j+2)/n+O_j(n^-2)]`.                         (12)

The apparent `n^(4j+1)` terms cancel exactly. Since

`2j^2+3j+2 - (j+1)(2j+1) = 1`,

subtracting (12) from (10) yields

`M_j(n)`

`= j a_j^2 n^(4j) + O_j(n^(4j-1))`

`= j/(j!)^2 * n^(4j) + O_j(n^(4j-1)).`                    (13)

The leading constant `j/(j!)^2` is strictly positive. Hence `M_j(n)>0` for all sufficiently large `n`, proving (ULC), and therefore Chen, on the fixed-defect diagonal `n-k=j`. QED.

The ingredients (RSK, hook-length formula, and `sum(f^mu)^2=j!`) are classical and are not claimed as novel. What is potentially new is this particular eventual-ULC deduction for every fixed defect; its novelty and priority remain **UNVERIFIED** pending a deeper literature check.
