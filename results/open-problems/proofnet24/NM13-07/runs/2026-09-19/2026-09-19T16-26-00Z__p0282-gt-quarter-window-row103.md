## 5. New structural theorem: the correction is a Gelfand--Tsetlin stay probability

Retain the stable factorization from the preceding reports. With defect `j=n-k`, write

\[
F_j(n):=\ell_{n,n-j}
       =\frac{(n)_j^2}{j!}A_j(n),
\]

where under Plancherel measure on `mu |- j`,

\[
A_j(n)=\mathbb E_j R_{j,n}(\mu)^2,
\qquad
R_{j,n}(\mu)
 =\prod_{x\in\mu}\frac{k-c(x)}{k+1-c(x)},
\]

and `c(x)` denotes Young-diagram content. This stable form applies whenever the first row is long enough to admit every `mu |- j`; in particular it applies throughout the region used below.

Let `lambda=mu'`. By the hook-content formula,

\[
s_{\lambda}(1^k)
 =\prod_{x\in\lambda}\frac{k+c_{\lambda}(x)}{h_{\lambda}(x)}.
\]

Transposition changes content sign and preserves hook lengths, so

\[
\boxed{
R_{j,n}(\mu)
 =\frac{s_{\mu'}(1^k)}{s_{\mu'}(1^{k+1})}.
}
\]

Now use multiplicity-free branching from `U(k+1)` to `U(k)`. If `lambda` is the top highest weight, then

\[
s_\lambda(1^{k+1})
 =\sum_{\nu\prec\lambda}s_\nu(1^k),
\]

where the sum ranges over interlacing `U(k)` highest weights. The self-row `nu=lambda` is admissible in the stable range. Therefore

\[
\frac{s_\lambda(1^k)}{s_\lambda(1^{k+1})}
\]

is exactly the probability of choosing the self-branch `nu=lambda` when the next Gelfand--Tsetlin row is selected with probability proportional to its `U(k)` dimension. Equivalently, for a uniformly sampled Gelfand--Tsetlin pattern with top `lambda`, it is the conditional probability that the next row stays unchanged.

Thus:

### Proposition `NM13-07-P0282-GT-STAY-PROBABILITY-12`

In the stable regime,

\[
\boxed{
A_j(n)
 =\mathbb E_{\mu\sim\mathrm{Plancherel}(j)}
   \left[\Pr(\text{next GT row stays }\mu'\mid\mu')\right]^2.
}
\]

This is an exact reformulation, not an asymptotic statement.

## 6. Plancherel-growth edge identity

For a general defect level `s`, write

\[
K=n-s,
\qquad
R_{s,n}(\mu)=\prod_{x\in\mu}\frac{K-c(x)}{K+1-c(x)}.
\]

Let `lambda` cover `mu` by one new box of content `c_0`. At level `s+1`, the corresponding first-row parameter is `K-1`. Direct cancellation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R_{s+1,n}(\lambda)}{R_{s,n}(\mu)}
&=
\prod_{x\in\mu}
\frac{K-1-c(x)}{K-c(x)}
\frac{K+1-c(x)}{K-c(x)}
\cdot
\frac{K-1-c_0}{K-c_0}\\
&=
\boxed{
\prod_{x\in\mu}
\left(1-\frac1{(K-c(x))^2}\right)
\left(1-\frac1{K-c_0}\right).
}
\end{aligned}
\]

In the stable region used below, every factor is in `(0,1)`, so `R` strictly decreases along every Plancherel-growth edge.

The standard Plancherel growth transition is

\[
p(\mu\to\lambda)
 =\frac{f^\lambda}{(s+1)f^\mu}.
\]

It is stochastic because `Ind_{S_s}^{S_{s+1}}V^\mu` is the multiplicity-one sum over covers, and it sends Plancherel measure at size `s` to Plancherel measure at size `s+1` because restriction gives

\[
\sum_{\mu\nearrow\lambda}f^\mu=f^\lambda.
\]

Hence averaging the edge inequality with respect to this one-step coupling is legitimate.

## 7. A uniform lower edge bound

Apply the previous identity to the transition `j-1 -> j`. Put

\[
d=n-2j=k-j.
\]

At level `j-1`, `K=n-(j-1)=k+1`. Every old content satisfies `c(x)<=j-2`, and the new box content satisfies `c_0<=j-1`. Therefore

\[
K-c(x)\ge d+3,
\qquad
K-c_0\ge d+2.
\]

Since the factors in the edge identity increase with these denominators,

\[
\frac{R_{j,n}(\lambda)}{R_{j-1,n}(\mu)}
\ge
q_j(d):=
\left(1-\frac1{(d+3)^2}\right)^{j-1}
\frac{d+1}{d+2}.
\]

Squaring and averaging under Plancherel growth gives

\[
\boxed{
A_j(n)\ge q_j(d)^2 A_{j-1}(n).
}
\]

For the next transition `j -> j+1`, the exact edge factors are all strictly between zero and one in the same region, hence

\[
\boxed{
A_{j+1}(n)<A_j(n).
}
\]

Combining the two,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_j(n)^2}{A_{j-1}(n)A_{j+1}(n)}
>q_j(d)^2.
}
\]

## 8. New theorem: a linear quarter-defect window

The literal Chen ratio obtained from the stable factorization is

\[
Q_j(n):=
\frac{F_j(n)^2}{F_{j-1}(n)F_{j+1}(n)}
=
\frac{j+1}{j}
\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^2
\frac{A_j(n)^2}{A_{j-1}(n)A_{j+1}(n)}.
\]

Since `k=j+d`, Section 7 yields

\[
Q_j(n)>
\frac{j+1}{j}
\left(\frac{j+d+1}{j+d}\right)^2
\left(1-\frac1{(d+3)^2}\right)^{2(j-1)}
\left(\frac{d+1}{d+2}\right)^2.
\]

Assume now

\[
d\ge2j,
\]

which is exactly `n>=4j`. For `j>=4`, use the elementary inequalities

\[
\log(1+x)\ge x-\frac{x^2}{2}\quad(x\ge0),
\]

and

\[
\log(1-y)\ge-\frac{y}{1-y}\quad(0\le y<1).
\]

Taking logs of the preceding lower bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log Q_j(n)
&>
\frac1j-\frac1{2j^2}
+\frac2{j+d}-\frac1{(j+d)^2}
-\frac2{d+1}
-\frac{2(j-1)}{(d+2)(d+4)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The three `d>=2j` estimates needed are

\[
\frac2{j+d}-\frac2{d+1}
=-\frac{2(j-1)}{(j+d)(d+1)}
> -\frac1{3j},
\]

\[
-\frac1{(j+d)^2}\ge-\frac1{9j^2},
\]

and

\[
-\frac{2(j-1)}{(d+2)(d+4)}
> -\frac1{2j}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\log Q_j(n)
>
\frac1{6j}-\frac{11}{18j^2}
=
\frac{3j-11}{18j^2}>0
\qquad(j\ge4).
\]

The stable decomposition for `F_{j-1},F_j,F_{j+1}` is valid here because `n>=4j` implies `n>=2j+2` for `j>=1`.

Thus:

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-LINEAR-QUARTER-WINDOW-12`

For every `j>=4` and every `n>=4j`,

\[
\boxed{
F_j(n)^2>F_{j-1}(n)F_{j+1}(n).
}
\]

\ell_{n,n-j}^{\,2}>
\ell_{n,n-j-1}\ell_{n,n-j+1}.
}
