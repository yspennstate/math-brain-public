## 12. What this run learned

The decisive issue was not a lack of a sharper Plancherel tail inequality in the abstract. It was a mismatch of scales between the finite fixed-defect seam and the asymptotic typical-shape argument.

A homogeneous cutoff forces the asymptotic slope to be large enough to survive the low-rank tail. An affine cutoff lets the additive buffer absorb those finite ranks, while the asymptotic slope controls only the genuinely large-`s` regime. Even then, requiring an independently tiny bad mass at every finite `s` is wasteful: the source only needs the **product** of the deterministic good-parent gain and surviving good mass to exceed one. Checking that product exactly on the sporadic band unlocks a smaller asymptotic cutoff slope.

This is a reusable lesson for the next step: optimize the source lower bound itself, not arbitrary intermediate constants.
