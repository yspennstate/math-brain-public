## 5. New structural lemma: `D_m(j)` is a degree-at-most-`m` polynomial

### Lemma

For each fixed `m>=0`, the Taylor coefficient `D_m(j)` is a rational polynomial in `j` of degree at most `m`.

### Proof

Expand the difference in `B_s`. Every term is an explicit power `j^a` times a content power sum

`S_q(mu)=sum_{box in mu} c(box)^q`

with `a+q <= s-1`; the case `q=0` has `S_0=j` and therefore total ordinary degree `a+1<=s`.

For `q>=1`, `S_q(mu)` is the eigenvalue on the irreducible `S_j`-module `mu` of the central Jucys--Murphy power sum

`P_q = sum_{r=1}^j X_r^q`, `X_r=sum_{a<r}(a r)`.

Hence a Plancherel average of a product `prod_i S_{q_i}` is the coefficient of the identity in the corresponding product `prod_i P_{q_i}` in the regular representation.

Let `L=sum_i q_i`, the total number of transpositions in a word contributing to that product. Group identity words by their equality/support pattern. A symbol occurring in exactly one transposition cannot be fixed by the total product, so every support vertex has multiplicity at least two; consequently an identity word of length `L` uses at most `L` vertices. For each fixed abstract pattern with `v` support vertices, choosing its distinct labels in `[j]` contributes a falling-factorial polynomial `(j)_v`, of degree `v<=L`. There are finitely many patterns at fixed `L`. Thus

`E_Plancherel prod_i S_{q_i}`

is a polynomial in `j` of degree at most `L`.

The coefficient of `t^m` in

`R_mu(t)^2 = exp(2 sum_{s>=1} B_s t^s)`

`D_m(j)=a_m(j)-(1/m) sum_{s=1}^{m-1} s D_s(j) a_{m-s}(j)`.

Induction on `m` preserves the degree bound `deg D_m<=m`. This proves the lemma. `square`
