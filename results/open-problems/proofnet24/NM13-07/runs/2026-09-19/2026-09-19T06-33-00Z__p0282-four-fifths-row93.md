## 5. New theorem: third-order Plancherel centering gives an `n^(4/5)` defect window

Write

`j=n-k`, `E_j(n)=ell_{n,n-j}`.

The retained exact hook factorization gives, for `n>=2j`,

`E_j(n)=((n)_j^2/j!) A_j(n)`,                                      (5.1)

where under Plancherel measure on partitions `mu|-j`,

`A_j(n)=E_mu[R_mu(n,j)^2]`,                                         (5.2)

`R_mu(n,j)=prod_{c=1}^{mu_1} (n-j-c+1)/(n-j-c+1+mu'_c)`.           (5.3)

It also gives the exact Chen ratio

`Q_j(n):=E_j(n)^2/(E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n))`

`=((j+1)/j) ((n-j+1)/(n-j))^2 A_j(n)^2/(A_{j-1}(n)A_{j+1}(n)).`    (5.4)

### Proof

For `j<=66`, (5.5) lies inside the already retained source-valid all-`n` fixed-defect theorem. It therefore suffices to prove the new statement for `j>=67`.

Assume `j>=67` and (5.5). Then

`n >= 4 (j+1)^(5/4) >= 8(j+1) > 8j`.                               (5.7)

For `mu|-j`, put

`h_c=mu'_c`, `a_c=j+c-1`, `d_c=n-a_c`, `z_c=h_c/d_c`.

Because `a_c<=2j-1<n/4`,

`d_c>3n/4`, `0<=z_c<=1/6`.                                         (5.8)

From (5.3),

`log R_mu = -sum_c log(1+z_c)`.                                    (5.9)

For `0<=z<=1/6`, the alternating Taylor estimate gives

`-log(1+z)=-z+z^2/2-z^3/3+r_4(z)`,

`|r_4(z)|<=z^4/4`.                                                   (5.10)

We expand each reciprocal through the order needed. First,

`1/(n-a)=1/n+a/n^2+a^2/n^3+a^3/[n^3(n-a)]`.                        (5.11)

Since `a_c<2j`, `d_c>3n/4`, and `sum h_c=j`, the total remainder contributed by the linear term is at most

`(32/3) j^4/n^4`.                                                    (5.12)

For `x=a/n<=1/4`, the elementary inequalities

`0 <= (1-x)^(-2)-1-2x <= (16/3)x^2`,

`0 <= (1-x)^(-3)-1 <= 8x`                                           (5.13)

give, respectively, errors at most

`(32/3) j^4/n^4`                                                     (5.14)

when replacing `1/(2d_c^2)` by `1/(2n^2)+a_c/n^3`, and

`(16/3) j^4/n^4`                                                     (5.15)

when replacing `1/(3d_c^3)` by `1/(3n^3)`. Here we used `sum h_c^2<=j^2` and `sum h_c^3<=j^3`. Finally, (5.8) implies

`sum_c z_c^4/4 < j^4/n^4`.                                          (5.16)

Thus the total fourth-order remainder is bounded by

`|rho_mu| <= 28 j^4/n^4`.                                           (5.17)

The first two coefficients are the predecessor's ones. The third coefficient is

`B_3(mu)=-sum_c h_c a_c^2 + sum_c h_c^2 a_c -(1/3)sum_c h_c^3`.    (5.18)

Let

`C(mu)=sum_{(r,c) in mu}(c-r)`,

`S_2(mu)=sum_{(r,c) in mu}(c-r)^2`.                                 (5.19)

Using

`h^2=sum_{v=0}^{h-1}(2v+1)`,

`h^3=sum_{v=0}^{h-1}(3v^2+3v+1)`,                                  (5.20)

and writing `u=c-1`, `v=r-1`, the contribution of each cell to (5.18) is

`-j^2+j-1/3 +(1-2j)(u-v)-(u-v)^2`.

Summing over the `j` cells yields the exact identity

`B_3(mu)=-j^3+j^2-j/3 +(1-2j)C(mu)-S_2(mu)`.                       (5.21)

Together with the second-order identity this gives

`log R_mu`

`= -j/n + (-j^2+j/2-C(mu))/n^2`

`  + [-j^3+j^2-j/3 +(1-2j)C(mu)-S_2(mu)]/n^3 + rho_mu`.            (5.22)

The first random statistic is centered by conjugation:

`E_Plancherel[C(mu)]=0`.                                             (5.23)

The new ingredient is an exact squared-content mean:

`E_Plancherel[S_2(mu)] = j(j-1)/2`.                                 (5.24)

Here is a short proof of (5.24). In `C[S_j]`, let the Jucys--Murphy elements be

`X_i=sum_{a<i}(a i)`.

On the Young seminormal basis, `X_i` has eigenvalue equal to the content of the cell containing `i`; hence `sum_i X_i^2` acts on the irreducible `V^mu` by the scalar `S_2(mu)`. In the regular representation, the normalized trace of left multiplication by a group-algebra element is its identity coefficient. In `X_i^2`, the identity occurs exactly when the same transposition `(a i)` is chosen twice, giving coefficient `i-1`. Therefore the identity coefficient of `sum_i X_i^2` is

`sum_{i=1}^j (i-1)=j(j-1)/2`.

On the other hand, the regular representation decomposes with multiplicity `f^mu`, so its normalized trace is

`(1/j!) sum_{mu|-j} (f^mu)^2 S_2(mu)`,

which is exactly the Plancherel expectation. This proves (5.24).

Put

`J=j(j-1)/2`

and define the deterministic approximation

`D_j^(3)(n)`

`= -2j/n + (-2j^2+j)/n^2 + (-2j^3+j^2+j/3)/n^3`.                  (5.25)

Then (5.22)--(5.24) imply

`2 log R_mu = D_j^(3)(n) + Y_mu + xi_mu`,                           (5.26)

where

`Y_mu = [-2/n^2 + 2(1-2j)/n^3] C(mu)`

`       -2(S_2(mu)-J)/n^3`,                                        (5.27)

`E[Y_mu]=0`, and by (5.17)

`|xi_mu|<=56 j^4/n^4`.                                              (5.28)

We now bound the centered random term without any external Plancherel limit theorem. Every size-`j` diagram has

`|C(mu)|<=j^2/2`, `0<=S_2(mu)<j^3`, `J<=j^2/2`.                    (5.29)

Because `n>=8j`, the coefficient of `C` in (5.27) has absolute value at most `5/(2n^2)`. Consequently

`|Y_mu| <= (5/4)j^2/n^2 + 3j^3/n^3`

`        <= (13/8)j^2/n^2`.                                        (5.30)

Since `E[Y_mu]=0`, Jensen gives `log E[e^Y]>=0`, while Hoeffding's elementary bounded-variable lemma gives

`log E[e^Y] <= (1/2)(13j^2/(8n^2))^2 < 2j^4/n^4`.                 (5.31)

Using (5.2), (5.26), and the uniform pointwise remainder (5.28),

`e^{-56j^4/n^4} E[e^Y]`

`<= exp(-D_j^(3)(n)) A_j(n)`

`<= e^{56j^4/n^4} E[e^Y]`.                                         (5.32)

Hence

`log A_j(n)=D_j^(3)(n)+epsilon_j(n)`,                               (5.33)

with the safe explicit bound

`|epsilon_j(n)| <= 60 j^4/n^4`.                                    (5.34)

The deterministic approximation has favorable exact discrete curvature:

`2D_j^(3)-D_{j-1}^(3)-D_{j+1}^(3)`

`= 4/n^2 +(12j-2)/n^3 >0`.                                         (5.35)

All three expansions in (5.33) are valid for `j-1,j,j+1` because (5.7) gives `n>=8(j+1)`. Therefore

`2epsilon_j-epsilon_{j-1}-epsilon_{j+1}`

`>= -60[2j^4+(j-1)^4+(j+1)^4]/n^4`

`>= -240 (j+1)^4/n^4`.                                              (5.36)

Taking logs in (5.4), using `log(1+1/j)>=1/(j+1)`, discarding the positive factor `2log((n-j+1)/(n-j))`, and using (5.35)--(5.36),

`log Q_j(n)`

`>= 1/(j+1) - 240 (j+1)^4/n^4`.                                    (5.37)

Under (5.5),

`240 (j+1)^4/n^4 <= 15/[16(j+1)]`,                                 (5.38)

so

`log Q_j(n) >= 1/[16(j+1)] >0`.                                    (5.39)

Thus `Q_j(n)>1`, proving strict Chen log-concavity in the new range `j>=67`. Together with the retained all-`n` defect theorem `j<=66`, this proves the stated sufficient condition. QED.
