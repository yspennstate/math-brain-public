## 2. #18 SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE — exact Hilbert-function formulation and a ruled-out Hodge shortcut

A fresh literature read found a source-relevant algebraic formulation that this track had not previously exploited: Brendon Rhoades, *Increasing subsequences, matrix loci and Viennot shadows*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 12 (2024), e97, DOI `10.1017/fms.2024.75`.

Rhoades defines

`R_n = F[x_{n x n}] / I_n`,

where `I_n` is generated by the row sums, column sums, and products of two variables from the same row or the same column. He proves that the Toeplitz-term-order standard monomials are the squarefree Viennot shadow monomials `s(w)` indexed by `w in S_n`, and

`Hilb(R_n;q) = ell_{n,n} + ell_{n,n-1} q + ... + ell_{n,1} q^(n-1)`.

Thus Chen's source conjecture is exactly log-concavity of the Hilbert function of this concrete finite graded quotient, with the index reversed. Rhoades further gives a graded `S_n x S_n` module model and Conjecture 4.4, an equivariant injection between adjacent graded tensor products which would imply the stronger Novak-Rhoades conjecture and hence Chen. The paper states that this injection has been checked for `n<=15`. These are prior-art statements, not NM13-07 inventions.

The translation immediately suggests matroid/Hodge log-concavity, because all standard monomials are squarefree. That most obvious shortcut is **provably unavailable**.

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-SHADOW-COMPLEX-NONMATROID-01`

For every `n>=4`, the supports of the Toeplitz standard monomials of `R_n` form a finite simplicial complex `Delta_n` whose face-number sequence is

`f_d(Delta_n) = ell_{n,n-d}`  for `0<=d<=n-1`,

but `Delta_n` is not pure and therefore is not the independence complex of a matroid.

### Proof

A set of standard monomials for any monomial initial ideal is closed under divisibility. Rhoades's Theorem 3.12 says every standard monomial here is a squarefree shadow monomial. Therefore their supports are closed under taking subsets and form a simplicial complex `Delta_n`. The number of degree-`d` standard monomials is the degree-`d` Hilbert coefficient, hence `ell_{n,n-d}`.

There is exactly one degree-`n-1` standard monomial because `ell_{n,1}=1` (only the decreasing permutation has LIS 1). Thus `Delta_n` has rank `n-1` and exactly one face of maximum possible size `n-1`.

Suppose `Delta_n` were pure. Then every facet would have size `n-1`; since there is exactly one such face, there would be a unique facet `B`. Every face would then be a subset of `B`, so `Delta_n` would have at most `2^(n-1)` faces. But its standard monomial basis has total cardinality

`sum_k ell_{n,k} = n!`,

and `n! > 2^(n-1)` for `n>=4` (base case `24>8`, and the ratio grows by `(n+1)/2>1`). Contradiction. Therefore `Delta_n` is non-pure. Matroid independence complexes are pure, so `Delta_n` is not one. QED.

This does **not** rule out a different Hodge/Lorentzian or Lefschetz mechanism on `R_n`; it only eliminates the naive route “the shadow standard-monomial complex is a matroid, so invoke matroid f-vector log-concavity.” Novelty of this short deduction is **UNVERIFIED**.
