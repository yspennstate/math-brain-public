## 4. Retained factorization and exact edge identity

Put

`j=n-k`, `F_j(n)=ell_{n,n-j}`.

Retain the exact stable factorization

\[
F_j(n)=\frac{(n)_j^2}{j!}A_j(n),
\qquad
A_j(n)=\mathbb E_{\mu\sim\mathrm{Plancherel}(j)}R_{j,n}(\mu)^2,
\]

where, for a diagram `mu` of size `s` and `K=n-s`,

\[
R_{s,n}(\mu)=\prod_{x\in\mu}\frac{K-c(x)}{K+1-c(x)}.
\]

If `lambda` covers `mu` by adding a box of content `C`, the retained exact ratio is

\[
\frac{R_{s+1,n}(\lambda)}{R_{s,n}(\mu)}
=
B_\mu(K)\left(1-\frac1{K-C}\right),
\]

with

\[
B_\mu(K):=
\prod_{x\in\mu}\left(1-\frac1{(K-c(x))^2}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The Plancherel-growth transition is

\[
p(\mu\to\lambda)=\frac{f^\lambda}{(s+1)f^\mu},
\]

and its child marginal is Plancherel measure at size `s+1`.

## 5. Kerov transition measure collapses the old-box product

For a fixed parent `mu |- s`, let `C` be the content of the added box under the preceding Plancherel transition and define its Cauchy transform

\[
G_\mu(z):=\mathbb E_\mu\frac1{z-C}.
\]

The classical Kerov transition-measure product formula can be written

\[
G_\mu(z)
=\frac1z
\prod_{x\in\mu}
\frac{(z-c(x))^2}
     {(z-c(x))^2-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Comparing (5.1) with (4.1) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{B_\mu(K)\,K\,G_\mu(K)=1.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Expanding (5.1) at infinity gives

\[
G_\mu(z)=\frac1z+\frac{s}{z^3}+O(z^{-4}).
\]

Since a Cauchy transform has expansion

\[
G_\mu(z)=\frac1z+\frac{\mathbb E C}{z^2}
+\frac{\mathbb E C^2}{z^3}+\cdots,
\]

we obtain the exact shape-independent moments

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\mu C=0,\qquad \mathbb E_\mu C^2=s.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

These facts are classical consequences of the transition measure. Their use in the present source-bound is the new route being tested here; novelty of that application is unverified.

## 6. A shape-independent conditional-average edge bound

Assume `K>s`. Set

\[
m:=G_\mu(K)=\mathbb E\frac1{K-C}.
\]

The exact algebraic identity

\[
\frac1{K-C}
=
\frac1K+\frac{C}{K^2}
+
\frac{C^2}{K^2(K-C)}
\]

and (5.3) imply, because every addable-box content satisfies `C<=s`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
m
&=\frac1K+
\mathbb E\frac{C^2}{K^2(K-C)}\\
&\le
\frac1K+\frac{s}{K^2(K-s)}
=:U.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Now let

\[
h(\mu\to\lambda):=
\frac{R_{s+1,n}(\lambda)}{R_{s,n}(\mu)}.
\]

Using (5.2) and Jensen's inequality for the square,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[h^2\mid\mu]
&=B_\mu(K)^2
\mathbb E\left(1-\frac1{K-C}\right)^2\\
&\ge
B_\mu(K)^2(1-m)^2\\
&=\left(\frac{1-m}{Km}\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

In the region used below, `0<m<=U<1`; the function `(1-m)/m` decreases with `m`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[h^2\mid\mu]
\ge
\left(\frac{1-U}{KU}\right)^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Crucially, the right side depends only on `s` and `K`, not on the shape `mu`.

## 7. Averaging under the tilted parent law

Take `s=j-1` and therefore

\[

Using the Plancherel growth marginal and then (6.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}

\mathbb E[h^2\mid\mu]\\

\end{aligned}

where

\[
L:=\left(\frac{1-U}{KU}\right)^2,
\qquad

\tag{7.1}

\boxed{

\tag{7.3}

## 8. New theorem: the one-third defect window

\[
Q_j(n):=
\frac{F_j(n)^2}{F_{j-1}(n)F_{j+1}(n)}

\left(\frac{K}{K-1}\right)^2
\frac{A_j(n)^2}{A_{j-1}(n)A_{j+1}(n)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Write

\[
\delta:=\frac{j-1}{K(K-j+1)},
\qquad
U=\frac{1+\delta}{K}.
\]

Then (7.3) and (8.1) give

\[
Q_j(n)>
\frac{j+1}{j}
\left(
\frac{1-\delta/(K-1)}{1+\delta}
\right)^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Assume

\[
n\ge3j.
\]

Then `K=n-j+1>=2j+1` and `K-j+1>=j+2`, so

\[
\delta\le
\delta_0:=\frac{j-1}{(2j+1)(j+2)},
\qquad
\frac{\delta}{K-1}\le\frac{\delta_0}{2j}.

Therefore

\[
Q_j(n)>
\frac{j+1}{j}
\left(
\frac{1-\delta_0/(2j)}{1+\delta_0}
\right)^2.
\tag{8.3}
