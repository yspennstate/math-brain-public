## 6. Method #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: residual partitions at fixed defect

Write

`E_j(n) := ell_{n,n-j}`.

For fixed `j` and `n>=2j`, every partition of `n` whose first row has length `n-j` is uniquely

`lambda=(n-j,mu)`, where `mu |- j`.

Let `mu'_c` be the height of column `c` of `mu`, and let `f^nu` denote the number of standard Young tableaux of shape `nu`. Comparing hook products in `lambda` and `mu` gives the exact formula

`f^(n-j,mu) = binom(n,j) f^mu prod_{c=1}^{mu_1} [(n-j-c+1)/(n-j-c+1+mu'_c)].`  (6.1)

Indeed, all hook lengths strictly below the first row are exactly the hook lengths of `mu`. In the first row, columns `c>mu_1` have hook length `n-j-c+1`, while columns `c<=mu_1` acquire the additional `mu'_c` boxes below. Factoring `(n-j)!`, `j!`, and the hook product of `mu` from the hook-length formula yields (6.1).

For fixed `j,mu`, the product in (6.1) is `1+O_j(1/n)` and

`binom(n,j)=n^j/j!+O_j(n^(j-1))`.

Therefore

`f^(n-j,mu) = (f^mu/j!) n^j + O_j(n^(j-1)).`

Robinson--Schensted now gives

`E_j(n) = sum_{mu|-j} (f^(n-j,mu))^2`

`        = [sum_{mu|-j}(f^mu)^2]/(j!)^2 * n^(2j) + O_j(n^(2j-1)).`

The regular-representation identity on `S_j`,

`sum_{mu|-j}(f^mu)^2=j!`,

therefore yields the clean fixed-defect asymptotic

**`E_j(n)=n^(2j)/j! + O_j(n^(2j-1))`.**  (6.2)

This language is the useful structural reduction: fixed defect means a finite partition problem independent of `n`, and each `E_j(n)` is eventually an explicit polynomial/rational expression obtained from finitely many hook formulas.

**Outcome of #18:** successful structural reduction. It gives the exact engine used below and explains the boundary asymptotics without re-enumerating all partitions of `n`.
