## 6. Method #6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: fixed-defect polynomial recursion

Put

`E_j(n) := ell_{n,n-j}`.                                               (6.1)

Kauers–Wang recurrence (2) is

`(n+1)^2(a_{n,k}+a_{n,k+1})`
` = (n-k+1)a_{n+1,k} + (n+k+1)a_{n+1,k+1}`                            (6.2)

for their proved long-LIS range `n/2 <= k <= n`.

Set `k=n-j` in (6.2) and then write `N=n+1`. This gives, whenever `N>=2j+1`,

`N^2(E_j(N-1)+E_{j-1}(N-1))`
` = (j+1)E_{j+1}(N) + (2N-j-1)E_j(N)`.                               (6.3)

Define integer polynomials recursively by

`P_0(n)=1`, `P_1(n)=(n-1)^2`,

`P_{j+1}(n)`
` = n^2[P_j(n-1)+j P_{j-1}(n-1)] - (2n-j-1)P_j(n)`.                  (6.4)

### Polynomial/source identification lemma

For every `j>=0`,

`P_j(n) = j! E_j(n)`                                                   (6.5)

throughout the recurrence-valid range `n>=2j-1` (with the evident harmless interpretation for `j=0`).

**Proof.** The cases `j=0,1` are immediate: `E_0(n)=1` and `E_1(n)=(n-1)^2`. Assume (6.5) for defects `j` and `j-1`. For target `N>=2j+1`, the old row in (6.3) has `N-1>=2j`, so `k=(N-1)-j >= (N-1)/2`; hence Kauers–Wang applies. Multiply (6.3) by `j!` and substitute the induction hypotheses. The right-hand first term becomes `(j+1)!E_{j+1}(N)`, and the resulting equation is exactly (6.4). Thus `P_{j+1}(N)=(j+1)!E_{j+1}(N)` for every `N>=2j+1`, which is the stated threshold `N>=2(j+1)-1`. Induction proves the lemma. `square`

The checker independently cross-compares the generated polynomials to exact RSK/hook counts at **419** overlapping `(j,n)` positions with `j<=20`, `n<=40`; all comparisons agree exactly. That is a self-check of the algebra, not a substitute for the proof above.

**Outcome of #6:** success. The source boundary is reduced to exact integer polynomial positivity rather than shape-by-shape asymptotics.

## 7. Method #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: a complete top-20-defect theorem

For `j>=1`, define the integer Turán polynomial

`D_j(n) := (j+1)P_j(n)^2 - j P_{j-1}(n)P_{j+1}(n)`.                  (7.1)

Using (6.5), direct factorial cancellation gives

`D_j(n)`
` = (j+1)(j!)^2 [E_j(n)^2 - E_{j-1}(n)E_{j+1}(n)]`                  (7.2)

whenever the three fixed-defect polynomials have entered their source-valid range. Thus `D_j(n)>0` is exactly strict Chen log-concavity at `k=n-j`.

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-FIXED-DEFECT-20-07`

For every integer `j` with `1<=j<=20` and every source-valid integer `n>=j+2`,

`ell_{n,n-j}^2 > ell_{n,n-j-1} ell_{n,n-j+1}`.                      (7.3)

Equivalently, Chen's inequalities are strict at the top **20 defect positions** wherever those positions are interior.

### Proof

Fix `1<=j<=20`.

For `n>=2j+1`, put `m=n-(2j+1)>=0`. The exact integer checker generates `P_0,...,P_21` from (6.4), forms `D_j`, expands

`D_j(m+2j+1)`,                                                        (7.4)

and checks **every coefficient**. For each `j=1,...,20`, every coefficient in (7.4) is a strictly positive integer. The degrees are `4j`, so the checker examines `4j+1` exact coefficients in the `j`th polynomial. Therefore (7.4) is strictly positive for every integer `m>=0`, hence `D_j(n)>0` for every `n>=2j+1`. By (7.2), (7.3) follows in this infinite range.

It remains only the finite pre-polynomial bridge

`j+2 <= n <= 2j`.                                                     (7.5)

The checker independently computes the source counts by Robinson–Schensted plus the hook-length formula for every row through `n=40` and verifies (7.3) at every pair in (7.5). There are exactly
