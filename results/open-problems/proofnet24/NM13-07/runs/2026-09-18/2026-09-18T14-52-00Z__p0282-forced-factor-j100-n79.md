## 4. Method #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: Plancherel trace controls the top coefficient layers

The previous residual-partition formula can be written uniformly as follows. For `mu |- j`, pad `mu_i=0` past its last row and set

`R_mu(n-j)=prod_{i=1}^j (n-j+i-mu_i)`.

Then, as a polynomial identity in the stable fixed-defect normalization,

`P_j(n)=(1/j!) sum_{mu|-j} (f^mu)^2 R_mu(n-j)^2`.             (4.1)

The weights `(f^mu)^2/j!` are exactly Plancherel measure on the irreducible representations of `S_j`. This is the group action hidden in the previous coefficient calculations.

Put

`c_i=i-j-mu_i`, so `R_mu(n-j)=prod_i (n+c_i)`.

Two elementary symmetric data are then controlled uniformly in `mu`. First,

`sum_i c_i = -j(j+1)/2`,                                      (4.2)

because `sum_i mu_i=j`. Second, if

`C(mu)=sum_{(a,b) in mu}(b-a)=1/2 sum_i mu_i(mu_i-2i+1)`

is the content sum, then direct expansion gives

`sum_i c_i^2 = j(j+5)(2j-1)/6 + 2 C(mu)`.                    (4.3)

Now use the symmetric group rather than another finite census. The central sum of all transpositions in `C[S_j]` acts on the irreducible module indexed by `mu` by the scalar `C(mu)`. Its trace in the regular representation is zero, since each nonidentity group element acts by a permutation matrix with zero diagonal. Decomposing the regular representation into irreducibles therefore gives

`sum_{mu|-j} (f^mu)^2 C(mu)=0`,

or equivalently

`E_Plancherel C(mu)=0`.                                       (4.4)

Expanding the square in (4.1), and using (4.2)-(4.4), yields the following **all-j exact coefficient formulas**:

`P_j(n)=n^(2j) - j(j+1)n^(2j-1)`

`       + [j(3j^3+4j^2-6j+5)/6] n^(2j-2) + lower terms`.      (4.5)

Substituting (4.5) for `j-1,j,j+1` into `D_j` gives

`[n^(4j)]D_j = 1`,

`[n^(4j-1)]D_j = -2j^2`,

`[n^(4j-2)]D_j = j(6j^3-2j^2-9j+8)/3`.                       (4.6)

Finally set

`Q_j(m)=D_j(m+2j+1)`.

The top three shifted coefficients are therefore

`[m^(4j)]Q_j = 1`,

`[m^(4j-1)]Q_j = 2j(3j+2)`,

`[m^(4j-2)]Q_j = j(54j^3+58j^2-3j+2)/3`.                     (4.7)

Every quantity in (4.7) is strictly positive for `j>=1`. Hence **the first obstruction to all-j coefficient positivity can never occur in the top three coefficient layers**. This is an unbounded structural theorem, not merely a computation through a fixed defect.

## 5. Method #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: an exact evaluation law and a forced falling-factor divisor

The recurrence has a stronger conserved evaluation pattern than was visible in the previous raw coefficient tables.

### Theorem NM13-07-P0282-FACTORIAL-EVALUATION-09

For every pair of integers `r>=0` and `j>=2r`,

`P_j(r)=r! j!`.                                                (5.1)

### Proof

Induct first on `r` and, for fixed `r`, on `j`.

For `r=0`, evaluating the recurrence at `n=0` gives

`P_{j+1}(0)=(j+1)P_j(0)`.

Since `P_0(0)=1`, this gives `P_j(0)=j!`.

Assume (5.1) is known for `r-1`. The first admissible index at level `r` is `j=2r`. Apply the recurrence with recurrence-index `2r-1` and argument `n=r`. The coefficient of `P_{2r-1}(r)` vanishes, because

`2r-(2r-1)-1=0`.

Therefore

`P_{2r}(r)=r^2[P_{2r-1}(r-1)+(2r-1)P_{2r-2}(r-1)]`.

The outer induction hypothesis applies to both terms, so

`P_{2r}(r)=r^2 (r-1)![(2r-1)!+(2r-1)(2r-2)!]`

`           =2r^2(r-1)!(2r-1)!`

`           =r!(2r)!`.

Now suppose `j>=2r` and `P_j(r)=r!j!`. Again using the outer induction hypothesis at `r-1`,

`P_j(r-1)=(r-1)!j!`,

`P_{j-1}(r-1)=(r-1)!(j-1)!`.

The recurrence gives

`P_{j+1}(r)`

`=r^2[(r-1)!j!+j(r-1)!(j-1)!]-(2r-j-1)r!j!`

`=2r^2(r-1)!j!+(j+1-2r)r!j!`

`=(j+1)r!j!`

`=r!(j+1)!`.

This completes both inductions. QED.

### Corollary NM13-07-P0282-FORCED-TURAN-FACTOR-09

`0<=r<=floor((j-1)/2)`,

`D_j(r)=0`.                                                    (5.2)

`(j+1)(r!j!)^2-j(r!(j-1)!)(r!(j+1)!)=0`.

`(n)_{floor((j+1)/2)} | D_j(n)`,                              (5.3)

where `(n)_q=n(n-1)...(n-q+1)`. The quotient is integral because the divisor is a product of distinct monic linear factors with integer roots, so repeated synthetic division stays in `Z[n]`.

Write

`H_j(n)=D_j(n)/(n)_{floor((j+1)/2)}`.                         (5.4)

This removes a forced block of roughly `j/2` zero structure before any positivity argument. The degree drops from `4j` to

`4j-floor((j+1)/2)`.
