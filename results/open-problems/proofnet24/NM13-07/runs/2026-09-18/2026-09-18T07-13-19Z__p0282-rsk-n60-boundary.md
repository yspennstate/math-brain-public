## 8. Method #29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: a complete two-layer boundary theorem

The fixed-defect coordinate `j=n-k` makes the high-LIS boundary algebraic. The following source-relevant restricted theorem is elementary but complete.

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-TOP2-01` — strict log-concavity at the top two interior positions

For every `n>=3`,

`ell_{n,n-1}^2 > ell_{n,n-2} ell_{n,n}`.

For every `n>=4`,

`ell_{n,n-2}^2 > ell_{n,n-3} ell_{n,n-1}`.

Thus Chen's source inequality holds strictly at `k=n-1` for all valid `n`, and at `k=n-2` for all valid `n`.

### Proof

By Robinson--Schensted,

`ell_{n,k}=sum_{lambda |- n, lambda_1=k}(f^lambda)^2`.

At defect zero there is only the shape `(n)`, hence

`ell_{n,n}=1`.

At defect one there is only `(n-1,1)`, and the hook-length formula gives

`f^(n-1,1)=n-1`,

so

`ell_{n,n-1}=(n-1)^2`.

For `n>=4`, the shapes with first row `n-2` are exactly

`(n-2,2)` and `(n-2,1,1)`.

The hook-length formula gives

`f^(n-2,2)=n(n-3)/2`,

`f^(n-2,1,1)=(n-1)(n-2)/2`.

Therefore

`ell_{n,n-2}`

`= [n(n-3)/2]^2 + [(n-1)(n-2)/2]^2`

`= (n^4-6n^3+11n^2-6n+2)/2`.

Hence for `n>=4`,

`ell_{n,n-1}^2 - ell_{n,n-2} ell_{n,n}`

`= (n-1)^4 - ell_{n,n-2}`

`= n(n-2)(n^2+1)/2 > 0`.

For `n=3`, the exact sequence is `(1,4,1)`, so the same top inequality has margin `16-1=15>0`. This proves the first assertion.

Now assume `n>=6`. The shapes with first row `n-3` are exactly

`(n-3,3)`, `(n-3,2,1)`, `(n-3,1,1,1)`.

Their hook dimensions are respectively

`n(n-1)(n-5)/6`,

`n(n-2)(n-4)/3`,

`(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)/6`.

Squaring and summing gives

`ell_{n,n-3}`

`=(n^6-12n^5+52n^4-98n^3+79n^2-22n+6)/6`.

Substitution and exact simplification yield

`ell_{n,n-2}^2 - ell_{n,n-3} ell_{n,n-1}`

`= n(n-2) P(n) / 12`,

where

`P(n)=n^6-6n^5+8n^4+12n^3-39n^2+10n+2`.

Set `m=n-6`. Then

`P(m+6)`

`=m^6+30m^5+368m^4+2364m^3+8385m^2+15526m+11618`,

which is strictly positive for every integer `m>=0`. Hence the second margin is positive for every `n>=6`.

The remaining valid cases are direct from the exact RSK sequences:

- `n=4`: `(ell_{4,1},...,ell_{4,4})=(1,13,9,1)` and the `k=2=n-2` margin is `13^2-1*9=160>0`;
- `n=5`: `(1,41,61,16,1)` and the `k=3=n-2` margin is `61^2-41*16=3065>0`.

This proves the second assertion. QED.
