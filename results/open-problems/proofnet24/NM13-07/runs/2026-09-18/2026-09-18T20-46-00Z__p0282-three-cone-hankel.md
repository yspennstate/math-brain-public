## 4. The three-cone reduction

### Theorem `NM13-07-P0282-THREE-CONE-19`

Let `A_{k,j}>0` be a positive two-dimensional array on the cells needed below. Suppose that at a given interior cell `(k,j)`, with `k>=2` and `j>=1`, the following five local inequalities hold:

1. **k-direction log-concavity**

   `A_{k,j}^2 >= A_{k-1,j} A_{k+1,j}`;

2. **j-direction log-concavity**

   `A_{k,j}^2 >= A_{k,j-1} A_{k,j+1}`;

3. **adjacent TP2 at `(k-1,j)`**

   `A_{k-1,j} A_{k,j+1} >= A_{k,j} A_{k-1,j+1}`;

4. **adjacent TP2 at `(k,j-1)`**

   `A_{k,j-1} A_{k+1,j} >= A_{k,j} A_{k+1,j-1}`.

Then

`A_{k,j}^2 >= A_{k-1,j+1} A_{k+1,j-1}`.

For the LIS/Hankel coefficient array

`A_{k,j}=ell_{k+j,k}/((k+j)!)^2`,

this conclusion is exactly Chen's fixed-size log-concavity inequality at row `n=k+j` and LIS length `k`, because all three terms in the source inequality carry the same factorial normalization `n!^2`.

### Proof

From TP2 at `(k-1,j)` and positivity,

`A_{k-1,j+1} <= A_{k-1,j} A_{k,j+1}/A_{k,j}`.

From TP2 at `(k,j-1)`,

`A_{k+1,j-1} <= A_{k,j-1} A_{k+1,j}/A_{k,j}`.

Multiplying,

`A_{k-1,j+1} A_{k+1,j-1}`

`<= [A_{k-1,j}A_{k+1,j}] [A_{k,j-1}A_{k,j+1}] / A_{k,j}^2`.

The two coordinate log-concavity inequalities bound the two bracketed products by `A_{k,j}^2`. Therefore the right-hand side is at most

`A_{k,j}^2 A_{k,j}^2 / A_{k,j}^2 = A_{k,j}^2`.

This proves the claim. `QED`

This theorem is elementary but strategically useful: it turns the anti-diagonal Chen inequality into a conjunction of coordinate inequalities that are much closer to classical Hankel/total-positivity mechanisms. It is a conditional reduction, not a proof that the three cones hold for all `k,j`.

## 5. A determinantal stability lemma and the exact missing bridge

There is a second structural reason to take the coordinate cones seriously.

For fixed positive integers `k` and `M>=k`, let

`v_x=(1,x,...,x^{k-1})^T/x! in R^k`,

and define the multiaffine determinant

`Z_{k,M}(z_1,...,z_M)=det(sum_{x=1}^M z_x v_x v_x^T)`.

### Lemma

`Z_{k,M}` has the Cauchy-Binet expansion

`Z_{k,M}(z)=sum_{S subset {1,...,M}, |S|=k} det(V_S)^2 prod_{x in S} z_x`,

so its coefficients are nonnegative squared Vandermonde weights. Moreover, `Z_{k,M}` is real stable.

### Proof

Cauchy-Binet applied to the `k x M` matrix with columns `v_x` gives the displayed expansion. Because `det(V_S)` is a Vandermonde divided by `prod_{x in S} x!`, every coefficient is a nonnegative square.

For stability, suppose all `Im(z_x)>0` and the matrix

`M(z)=sum_x z_x v_x v_x^T`

were singular. Let `c != 0` satisfy `M(z)c=0`. Then

`0 = Im(c^* M(z)c) = sum_x Im(z_x) |v_x^T c|^2`.

Every term is nonnegative and every `Im(z_x)` is strictly positive, so `v_x^T c=0` for all `x=1,...,M`. Since `M>=k` and the first `k` vectors form a Vandermonde system, they span `R^k`, forcing `c=0`, contradiction. Hence `det M(z) != 0` in the upper-half-plane product and `Z_{k,M}` is real stable. `QED`

This is a standard determinantal-stability mechanism; I do **not** claim that mechanism as novel. What matters here is the precise obstruction after applying it. The univariate truncated Hankel determinant is obtained by the monomial specialization `z_x=q^x`. That operation groups coefficients by the weighted subset sum `sum_{x in S} x`; it is not a generic linear stability-preserving specialization. Generic stability alone therefore does not imply log-concavity of the resulting coefficient sequence. Even trivial stable multiaffine examples can acquire gaps under weighted monomial substitutions. The required missing theorem must use the special Vandermonde/moment-curve weights.

This sharply identifies a proof obligation that was absent from the earlier direct fixed-defect work:

> prove that this specific weighted Vandermonde projection preserves enough `TP2` / coordinate log-concavity to satisfy the three-cone package, or exhibit its first failure.
