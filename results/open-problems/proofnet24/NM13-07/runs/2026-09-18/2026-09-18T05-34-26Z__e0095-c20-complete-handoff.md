## 6. Method #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER

### Theorem `NM13-07-E0095-C20-SHORTGIRTH-01`

Let `G` be any connected cubic simple graph on 20 vertices with girth at most 5. Then

`lambda_2(A(G)) >= 2`.

This is stronger than the source-specific statement needed here: it does not assume Hamiltonicity, a distinguished `C_20`, or that the noncycle edges form the source matching.

### Proof

Let `C` be a shortest cycle and write its length as `g`, so `g in {3,4,5}`. A shortest cycle is chordless: a chord would split it into a strictly shorter cycle.

Set

`V0 = V(C)`,

`V1 = N(V0) \ V0`,

and let `V2` be the remaining vertices. Put `m=|V1|` and let `x` be the number of edges internal to `V1`.

Because `G` is cubic and `C` is chordless, every vertex of `V0` has exactly two edges in `C` and exactly one edge from `V0` to `V1`. Hence

`e00 = g`,

`e01 = g`.

Each vertex of `V1` has total degree 3, so counting degree incidences in `V1` gives

`e12 = 3m - g - 2x`.

The whole graph has `3*20/2=30` edges, so a second count gives

`e22 = 30 - e00 - e01 - e11 - e12`

`     = 30 - g - 3m + x`.

Consider the three-cell average quotient matrix of `A(G)` for the partition `(V0,V1,V2)`:

```
Q = [ 2                         1                              0 ]
    [ g/m                       2x/m                           (3m-g-2x)/m ]
    [ 0                         (3m-g-2x)/(20-g-m)            2(30-g-3m+x)/(20-g-m) ].
```

Let `D=diag(sqrt(|V0|),sqrt(|V1|),sqrt(|V2|))`. Then `D Q D^{-1}` is exactly the symmetric compression of `A(G)` to the span of the normalized cell-indicator vectors. Consequently the three eigenvalues of `Q` are real and interlace those of `A(G)`.

Every row of `Q` sums to 3, so 3 is an eigenvalue. Since the symmetric compression has largest eigenvalue at most `lambda_1(A)=3`, this 3 is its largest eigenvalue. Let the other two quotient eigenvalues be `r>=s`. Direct exact expansion gives the monic non-Perron quadratic

`q(lambda)=(lambda-r)(lambda-s)`

and, at the single test point `lambda=2`,

`q(2) = 2g(10-2m+x) / (m(g+m-20)).`

Now `m<=g<=5`, because each vertex of `V0` contributes one exit and `V1` is the set of distinct exit endpoints. Also `x>=0`, `m>0`, and `g+m<=10<20`. Therefore

`10-2m+x >= 0`

while

`m(g+m-20) < 0`.

Hence

`q(2) <= 0`.

Since `q` is a real monic quadratic, this says that 2 lies between its two roots:

`s <= 2 <= r`.

Thus the second quotient eigenvalue is at least 2. Cauchy interlacing for the symmetric compression gives

`lambda_2(A(G)) >= r >= 2`.

This proves the theorem. `□`

The equality possibility in the relaxed quotient sign occurs only when `m=5,x=0`, hence only in the five-cycle layer pattern with five distinct external neighbors and no edges internal to that first outer layer. The theorem does not need to classify when full-graph equality occurs.

Outcome of #3: **success**. The remaining source-specific short-girth class is swallowed by a stronger order-20 cubic theorem.
