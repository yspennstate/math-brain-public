\[
S_{\nu+1}\le S_\nu.

\frac{S_{\nu+1}}{S_\nu}\le1.

\[
S_{\nu+1}\ge1.

\[
\frac{S_{\nu+1}}{S_\nu}

# 8. Proof of `P23-RIDGE-COUNT-EFFRANK-07`

Let

\[
x_1,\dots,x_n
\]

be the eigenvalues of \(S_n\).

The eigenvalues of

\[
M_\mu
=
\frac1n
(S_n+\mu I)^{-1}
S_n
(S_n+\mu I)^{-1}
\]

are

\[
m_i
=
\frac1n
\frac{x_i}{(x_i+\mu)^2}.
\]

Define

\[
f_\mu(x)
=
\frac{x}{(x+\mu)^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{Tr}M_\mu
=
\frac1n
\sum_i f_\mu(x_i),
\]

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M_\mu^2)
=
\frac1{n^2}
\sum_i f_\mu(x_i)^2.
\]

## 8.1 Trace lower bound

On

\[
\mu\le x\le2\mu,
\]

the function \(f_\mu\) is bounded below by its value at \(2\mu\):

\[
f_\mu(x)
\ge
\frac{2}{9\mu}.
\]

By hypothesis there are at least

\[
cn\sqrt\mu
\]

eigenvalues in this interval.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}M_\mu
\ge
\frac{2c}{9}\mu^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

## 8.2 Frobenius upper bound

For \(x\le\mu\),

\[
f_\mu(x)\le\mu^{-1}.
\]

There are at most

\[
Cn\sqrt\mu
\]

such eigenvalues, so their contribution to

\[
\sum_i f_\mu(x_i)^2
\]

is at most

\[
Cn\mu^{-3/2}.
\]

For \(k\ge0\), consider the dyadic bin

\[
2^k\mu<x\le2^{k+1}\mu
\]

while

\[
2^{k+1}\mu\le1.
\]

Since

\[
f_\mu(x)
\le
\frac1x
\le
\frac1{2^k\mu},
\]

and the cumulative count is at most

\[
Cn\sqrt{2^{k+1}\mu},
\]

the \(k\)-th bin contributes at most

\[
Cn\mu^{-3/2}
2^{-3k/2+1/2}.
\]

The geometric sum converges.

For \(x\ge1\),

\[
f_\mu(x)\le1,
\]

so the remaining contribution is at most \(n\), which is also

\[
O(n\mu^{-3/2})
\]

because \(\mu<1\).

Therefore

\[
\sum_i f_\mu(x_i)^2
\le
C' n\mu^{-3/2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(M_\mu^2)
\le
\frac{C'}{n\mu^{3/2}}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Combining (8.1) and (8.2),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\operatorname{Tr}(M_\mu^2)
}{
(\operatorname{Tr}M_\mu)^2
}
\le
\frac{C''}{n\sqrt\mu}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This proves `P23-RIDGE-COUNT-EFFRANK-07`.

### Consequence for the \(1/d\) scale

If

\[
\mu\asymp d^{-1}
\]

and

\[
n=2^d,

then

\[
\frac1{n\sqrt\mu}
=
O\left(

\right)
\to0.
\]

Thus square-root lower-edge counting at the \(1/d\) scale is overwhelmingly sufficient for effective-rank divergence in the commuting ridge proxy.

# 9. What this says — and does not say — about the actual kernel

The new shell-scale theorem is exact for the actual Gaussian kernel.

The new effective-rank theorem is deterministic, but applies directly only to a commuting ridge proxy.

The actual kernel risk matrix is

\[
M_X
=
K^{-1}C_XK^{-1},
\]

with

\[
K
=
\sum_s\lambda_s\Phi_s\Phi_s^\top,
\]

\[
C_X
=
\sum_s\lambda_s^2\Phi_s\Phi_s^\top.
\]

Different empirical harmonic blocks do not commute.

Therefore one cannot replace the first crossing shell by a scalar ridge without proof.

The new calculation identifies a sharper sufficient program:

1. project/residualize the first crossing shell against the cumulative lower-harmonic span;
2. prove a square-root lower-edge count down to the natural scale
   \[
   t\asymp d^{-1};
   \]
3. control the later harmonic tail as a lower-order positive regularizer;
4. transfer this spectral count through the noncommuting \(K^{-1}C_XK^{-1}\) geometry.

This is much weaker than proving an \(n^{-2}\) hard-edge law.

It is also much more specific than asking for generic Marchenko--Pastur convergence.

### New open lemma `P23-RESIDUAL-SQRT-08`

Let \(P_-\) denote the projector away from the cumulative low-harmonic empirical span and let \(\Phi_+\) be the first crossing-shell feature matrix.

Prove, after the correct normalization, a bound of the schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\#\{
\lambda_j(
P_-\Phi_+\Phi_+^\top P_-

\}
\le
C\,r_-\,\sqrt t
}
\]

uniformly for

\[
d^{-1}\lesssim t\lesssim1,
\]

together with a matching lower count in one interval of width comparable to \(1/d\).

Here \(r_-\) is the residual dimension.

This is a **mesoscopic spectral-count theorem**, not a microscopic hard-edge universality theorem.

Whether it can be proved from harmonic addition identities plus a resolvent/leave-one-out argument is the next substantive question.
