## 2. Exact risk identity and harmonic normalization

Put

\[
a=h^{-2},\qquad K(x,y)=e^{-a}e^{a x\cdot y}.
\]

Let `H_{d,k}` denote the degree-`k` spherical-harmonic space on `S^{d-1}` with probability measure `P_d`, and choose a real `L^2(P_d)`-orthonormal basis `psi_{k,r}`, `1<=r<=N_{d,k}`. The addition theorem gives

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{N_{d,k}}\psi_{k,r}(x)^2=N_{d,k}
\quad\text{for every }x.
\]

The multiplicity is

\[
N_{d,k}
=\binom{d+k-1}{k}-\binom{d+k-3}{k-2}
=\frac{(2k+d-2)(k+d-3)!}{k!(d-2)!}.
\]

The cumulative dimension through degree `L` is

\[
D_{d,L}
=\sum_{k=0}^{L}N_{d,k}
=\binom{d+L-1}{L}+\binom{d+L-2}{L-1}.
\]

I checked these identities exactly for `2<=d<=7`, `0<=L<=7`, and also checked the exact consecutive-ratio identity

\[
\frac{N_{d,k+1}}{N_{d,k}}
=
\frac{2k+d}{2k+d-2}\frac{k+d-2}{k+1}
\]

for the same finite range. These calculations are diagnostics only; the displayed algebraic identities are exact.

The Gaussian-kernel Mercer eigenvalue on degree `k` is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{k,d}
=e^{-a}\Gamma(d/2)(2/a)^{d/2-1}I_{k+d/2-1}(a).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This normalization passes the two basic checks: in `d=2` it gives the standard circle Fourier coefficient `e^{-a}I_k(a)`, and in `d=3`, `k=0`, it gives `e^{-a}\sinh(a)/a`, the direct spherical average of `e^{-a}e^{a x\cdot y}`.

From the positive Bessel series

\[
I_\eta(a)=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(a/2)^{\eta+2r}}{r!\Gamma(\eta+r+1)},
\]

`I_{eta+1}(a)/I_eta(a)` is `(a/2)` times a positive weighted average of `1/(eta+r+1)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda_{k+1,d}}{\lambda_{k,d}}
\le \frac{a}{d+2k}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For fixed `h`, all degree eigenvalues are therefore decreasing once `d>a`.

Let `Phi_k` be the `n x N_{d,k}` evaluation matrix and

\[
K_k=\lambda_{k,d}\Phi_k\Phi_k^T,
\qquad
\mathbf K=\sum_{k\ge0}K_k.
\]

For zero signal and training noise `epsilon` with covariance `sigma^2 I_n`, Parseval gives the conditional integrated prediction variance

\[
\frac{V(X)}{\sigma^2}
=\sum_{k\ge0}\lambda_{k,d}q_k,
\qquad
q_k=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf K^{-1}K_k\mathbf K^{-1}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

This is the target excess risk averaged over the training noise. Gaussianity of the noise is stronger than needed for this expectation identity; covariance `sigma^2 I_n` suffices.

The Euclidean Gaussian kernel is strictly positive definite on distinct points. Since iid sphere samples are distinct almost surely, `\mathbf K` is invertible almost surely.

## 3. First complete proof: low-harmonic finite-block theorem

This is the cleanest closure proof because it avoids every critical-degree lower-edge issue.

### Lemma P23-L2 — distribution-free finite-block leverage lower bound

Suppose a positive Mercer kernel has an orthonormal block `psi_1,...,psi_D` with eigenvalues `lambda_j>=lambda_*>0` and

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{D}\psi_j(x)^2=D
\quad\text{for every }x.
\]

Let

\[
\Phi_{ij}=\psi_j(X_i),\qquad
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_D),
\]
\[
A=\Phi\Lambda\Phi^T,
\qquad
B=\mathbf K-A\succeq0,
\qquad
\tau=\sum_{j>D}\lambda_j.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{n,d}
\ge
\frac{\sigma^2}{nD}
\left[
D-\frac{4D(D-1)}n-\frac{2\tau}{\lambda_*}
\right]_+^2.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

Set

\[
S=\frac1n\Phi^T\Phi.
\]

A row `psi(X)` is isotropic and has deterministic squared norm `D`, so

\[
\mathbb E\|S-I_D\|_F^2
=\frac1n\mathbb E\|\psi(X)\psi(X)^T-I_D\|_F^2
=\frac{D(D-1)}n.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Let `r` be the number of eigenvalues of `S` at least `1/2`. Each missing eigenvalue contributes more than `1/4` to `||S-I||_F^2`, hence

\[
\mathbb E r
\ge D-\frac{4D(D-1)}n.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since

\[
A\succeq \lambda_*\Phi\Phi^T,
\]

`A` has at least `r` eigenvalues `kappa_i>=n lambda_*/2`. For corresponding unit eigenvectors `u_i`, the inverse-Rayleigh Cauchy inequality gives

\[
u_i^T\mathbf K^{-1}u_i
\ge \frac1{u_i^T\mathbf K u_i}
=\frac1{\kappa_i+u_i^TBu_i}.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
T=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf K^{-1}A),
\]

\[
T
\ge\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\kappa_i}{\kappa_i+u_i^TBu_i}
\ge r-\frac{2\operatorname{Tr}B}{n\lambda_*}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Taking expectation and using `E Tr B=n tau`,

\[
\mathbb ET
\ge D-\frac{4D(D-1)}n-\frac{2\tau}{\lambda_*}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For the selected basis function `j`, let

\[
\phi_j=(\psi_j(X_i))_{i=1}^{n},
\qquad
v_j=\lambda_j\mathbf K^{-1}\phi_j,
\qquad
\ell_j=\phi_j^Tv_j.
\]

The coefficient of the interpolant along `psi_j` is `v_j^T epsilon`, hence

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{n,d}
\ge\sigma^2\sum_{j=1}^{D}\mathbb E\|v_j\|^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Since `E||phi_j||^2=n`, Cauchy gives

\[
(\mathbb E\ell_j)^2
\le n\,\mathbb E\|v_j\|^2.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Also `sum_j ell_j=T`. Another Cauchy inequality yields

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{n,d}
\ge \frac{\sigma^2}{nD}(\mathbb ET)^2.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Combining (3.5)–(3.8) proves (3.1). No independence between `A` and `B` is used. `QED`.

### Applying the lemma to low spherical harmonics

Take the complete block of all degrees `0,...,L`. Then the addition theorem gives the constant diagonal condition with `D=D_{d,L}`, and monotonicity (2.2) lets us take

\[
\lambda_*=\lambda_{L,d}.
\]

Choose `L` maximal with

\[
D_{d,L}\le n/16,
\qquad n=2^d.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Stirling at `L=floor(d/5)` gives

\[
\frac1d\log D_{d,\lfloor d/5\rfloor}
\to
\frac65\log\frac65-\frac15\log\frac15
<\log2,
\]

so for all large `d`, `L>=d/6`.

Using the exact cumulative-dimension formula,

\[
\frac{D_{d,L+1}}{D_{d,L}}
\le \frac{d+L-1}{L}\le8
\]

for all large `d`. Maximality in (3.9) therefore implies

\[
\boxed{
\frac n{128}<D\le\frac n{16}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

For every `k>=L`, the exact multiplicity ratio obeys the deliberately loose bound

\[
\frac{N_{d,k+1}}{N_{d,k}}\le14.
\]

Together with (2.2), the degree mass `N_{d,k}lambda_{k,d}` contracts by at most

\[
q_d=\frac{14a}{d}
\]

at each step. For all sufficiently large `d`, `q_d<=1/2`, so the tail eigenvalue sum satisfies

\[
\frac{\tau}{\lambda_{L,d}}
=\sum_{k>L}\frac{N_{d,k}\lambda_{k,d}}{\lambda_{L,d}}
\le N_{d,L}\frac{q_d}{1-q_d}
\le \frac{28aD}{d}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Insert (3.10)–(3.11) into (3.1). Because `D<=n/16`,

\[
\frac{4D(D-1)}n\le\frac D4,
\]

and therefore the bracket in (3.1) is at least

\[
D\left(\frac34-\frac{56a}{d}\right)
\ge\frac D2
\]

for all sufficiently large `d` depending on fixed `h`. Consequently

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{2^d,d}
\ge\frac{\sigma^2D}{4n}
>\frac{\sigma^2}{512}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{d\to\infty}\mathbb E\mathcal E_{2^d,d}
\ge\frac{\sigma^2}{512}.
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

This proves the requested theorem with the explicit choice

\[
\boxed{c(h)=1/512}
\]

for every fixed `h>0`. The numerical constant is universal; only the dimension threshold depends on `h`.

### Why this proof is robust

It needs only an expectation Frobenius estimate for the low block and the rapid deterministic Mercer-tail ratio. It never asks for a smallest-eigenvalue estimate for a critical harmonic Gram matrix. It also never uses a Marchenko–Pastur limit, Gaussian harmonic coordinates, or any high-probability inverse-Gram estimate.

Let `s_d=max{s:N_{d,s}<=n}` and abbreviate

\[
N=N_{d,s_d},\qquad
\tau_s=N\lambda_s,\qquad
x_d=N/n.

Let `c_*` be the unique positive solution

\[
(1+c)\log(1+c)-c\log c=\log2.
\]

Numerically

\[
c_*=0.2938153733404154933\ldots,
\qquad
r_*=(1+c_*)/c_*=4.403497879062293\ldots,
\]
\[
\rho_*=r_*^{-1}=0.2270921952193481872\ldots.
\]

Stirling and the exact multiplicity ratio imply

\[
s_d/d\to c_*,\qquad
x_d\in[\rho_*+o(1),1],
\tag{4.1}
\]

and geometric backward-ratio domination gives

\[
\frac{D_{<s_d}}{N_{d,s_d}}\to c_*.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The Bessel ratio (2.2) gives

\[
\frac{\tau_{>s_d}}{\tau_{s_d}}=o(1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Now write

\[
A=K_{s_d},\qquad
H=\sum_{j>s_d}K_j,\qquad
L=\sum_{j<s_d}K_j,\qquad
M=A+H.
\]

For `epsilon>0`, define

\[
\widetilde t_\epsilon
=\operatorname{Tr}((M+\epsilon I)^{-1}A).
\]

Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy applied to
`(M+epsilon I)^{-1/2}A^{1/2}` and `(M+epsilon I)^{1/2}A^{1/2}` gives

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}A)^2
\le
\widetilde t_\epsilon\,
\operatorname{Tr}((M+\epsilon I)A).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `Tr A=n tau_s` deterministically, Jensen gives

\[
\mathbb E\widetilde t_\epsilon
\ge
\frac{(n\tau_s)^2}
{\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}((M+\epsilon I)A)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The addition theorem and cross-degree orthogonality give exactly

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)
=n\tau_s^2+n(n-1)\frac{\tau_s^2}{N},
\tag{4.6}
\]

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(HA)
=n\tau_s\tau_{>s}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Hence after `epsilon downarrow0`,

\[
\mathbb E\widetilde t
\ge
\frac{n}
{1+(n-1)/N+\tau_{>s}/\tau_s}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Adding the lower-degree block can reduce leverage by at most its rank. Precisely, with

\[
C=(M+\epsilon I)^{-1/2}L(M+\epsilon I)^{-1/2},
\quad
B=(M+\epsilon I)^{-1/2}A(M+\epsilon I)^{-1/2}\preceq I,
\]

\[
\widetilde t_\epsilon-
\operatorname{Tr}((M+L+\epsilon I)^{-1}A)
=
\operatorname{Tr}([I-(I+C)^{-1}]B)
\le\operatorname{rank}(L).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Passing to the limit and using (4.1)–(4.3),

\[
\frac{\mathbb Et_{s_d}}n
\ge
f(x_d)+o(1),
\qquad
f(x)=\frac{x}{1+x}-c_*x.
\tag{4.10}
\]

The pointwise P04 leverage inequality is

\[
\frac{V(X)}{\sigma^2}
\ge\frac{t_{s_d}^2}{nN}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Thus Jensen gives the slightly sharper estimate

\[
\frac{\mathbb EV(X)}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\frac{(\mathbb Et_{s_d}/n)^2}{x_d}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

P04 dropped the factor `1/x_d>=1` and obtained `0.0140048...`. Keeping it yields

\[
\liminf\frac{\mathbb EV}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\inf_{x\in[\rho_*,1]}
\underbrace{x\left(\frac1{1+x}-c_*\right)^2}_{g(x)}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

On `[rho_*,1]`, `1/(1+x)-c_*>0`, and

\[
g'(x)
=
\left(\frac1{1+x}-c_*\right)
\left(\frac{1-x}{(1+x)^2}-c_*\right).
\]

The second factor is strictly decreasing on `[0,1]`, so any interior critical point is a maximum. Therefore the minimum is at an endpoint. Numerically,

\[
g(\rho_*)=0.0616703701459451\ldots,
\qquad
g(1)=\left(\frac12-c_*\right)^2
=0.0425121002707522\ldots.

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{d\to\infty}\mathbb E\mathcal E_{2^d,d}
\ge
0.0425121002707522\ldots\,\sigma^2.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

This strengthening is not needed for closure; the low-block theorem (3.12) is simpler and already fully solves the user’s target.

## 5. What happened to the previous P23 lower-edge obstruction?

The previous run isolated the sufficient condition

\[
\Pr\{\lambda_{\min}(K_{k_d+1}/\tau_{k_d+1})\ge d^{-A}\}\ge p_0>0.
\]

That conditional theorem was correct, but it attacked the wrong bottleneck. The low-harmonic proof shows that expected excess risk can be forced by a block of dimension `Theta(n)` well **below** the critical interpolation degree. A crude Frobenius estimate supplies enough average leverage there, while the higher Mercer tail is only `O_h(1/d)` relative to the last retained eigenvalue.

Thus:

- a critical hard edge is unnecessary;
- a Marchenko–Pastur law is unnecessary;
- expected leverage capture does not require high-probability capture;
- no inverse-moment bound is needed.

The lesson is specific to the expectation-level target. A high-probability positive-risk lower bound would still require additional concentration and is not proved here.

**AUDIT_FOR=P20**

**CLAIM:** P20-L8, exact off-diagonal Gram entry for the normalized degree-2 traceless harmonic feature block  
**VERDICT:** **PROVED / PASS.**

Let `E_1,...,E_m` be a Frobenius-orthonormal basis of traceless symmetric matrices, with

\[
m=\dim\operatorname{Sym}_d^0=\frac{(d-1)(d+2)}2,
\]

and

\[
h_j(u)=\sqrt{\frac{d(d+2)}2}\,u^TE_ju.
\]

Orthogonal projection of `uu^T` onto the traceless subspace gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{m}(u^TE_ju)(v^TE_jv)
=
\left\langle uu^T-\frac{I}{d},vv^T-\frac{I}{d}\right\rangle_F
=(u\cdot v)^2-\frac1d.
\]

Therefore for `R=HH^T/m`,

\[
\boxed{
R_{ij}
=\frac{d}{d-1}\left((u_i\cdot u_j)^2-\frac1d\right),\quad i\ne j,
\qquad R_{ii}=1.
}
\]

Using `E(u\cdot v)^4=3/[d(d+2)]`,

\[
\mathbb ER_{ij}^2
=
\frac{d^2}{(d-1)^2}
\left(\frac{3}{d(d+2)}-\frac1{d^2}\right)
=
\frac{2}{(d-1)(d+2)}
=\frac1m.
\]

So P20-L8’s normalization and second moment are exactly correct. This audit is independent of Target 3.

## 7. Failed / superseded routes

### Superseded: critical-degree lower-edge attack

P23-L1 remains a correct sufficient condition but is no longer a necessary route. The direct low block closes the theorem without it.

### Rejected: capacity alone

`sum_s t_s=n` and `t_s<=N_s` do not by themselves force finite-window capture because the upper degrees have infinite total multiplicity. The successful proof adds the crucial Mercer-tail ratio and low-block Frobenius control.

### Rejected: tail trace plus `||K^{-1}||`

A bound such as

\[
t_{>B}\le\|K^{-1}\|\operatorname{Tr}K_{>B}
\]

reintroduces the hard edge and is unnecessary.

### Rejected: MP universality

No population-to-empirical universality principle is needed for the expectation lower bound. Bulk laws would be massive overkill and would not automatically control inverse moments anyway.

### Not proved: high-probability obstruction

The source target explicitly asks only for `E_training[ExcessRisk]`. Neither proof above implies

\[
\Pr\{\mathcal E_{n,d}\ge c\sigma^2\}\to1.
\]

That is a separate theorem.
