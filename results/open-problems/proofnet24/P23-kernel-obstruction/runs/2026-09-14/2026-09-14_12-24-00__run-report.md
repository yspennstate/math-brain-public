## Executive status

**Target 3 remains OPEN.** This run proves a new closure reduction that is substantially weaker than the previously implicit near-Marchenko–Pastur requirement.

The key new result is:

> **P23-L1 (finite-window capture from a polynomial lower edge).** Let `k_d` be the first spherical-harmonic degree with multiplicity `N_{d,k_d} >= n=2^d`. If there exist finite `A` and `p0>0` such that
> \[
> \liminf_{d\to\infty}\Pr\!\left\{\lambda_{\min}(K_{k_d+1})\ge d^{-A}\tau_{k_d+1}\right\}\ge p_0,
> \]
> where `tau_s=N_{d,s} lambda_{d,s}` is the diagonal mass of the degree-`s` Gaussian-kernel block, then the desired Target-3 lower bound follows with an explicit positive constant. No constant hard edge, no MP limit, and no high-probability lower edge are needed; a **polynomial lower edge on an event of only fixed positive probability** suffices.

This reduction uses only: (i) the exact leverage inequality already present in P04; (ii) harmonic multiplicity asymptotics at the exponential threshold; (iii) the `1/d` ratio of successive Gaussian-kernel harmonic masses at degree `s~c_* d`; and (iv) a new deterministic rank-suppression lemma proved below.

The missing theorem-sized obligation is now sharply isolated: prove any polynomial lower-tail control for the normalized degree-`k_d+1` harmonic Gram matrix with nonvanishing probability.

## 2. Independent audit of the P04 leverage inequality

For one degree `s`,
\[
K_s=\lambda_s\Phi_s\Phi_s^T,
\qquad
 t_s=\lambda_s\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_s^T K^{-1}\Phi_s).
\]
Using Frobenius Cauchy,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 t_s^2
 &=\lambda_s^2\langle \Phi_s,K^{-1}\Phi_s\rangle_F^2\\
 &\le \lambda_s^2\|\Phi_s\|_F^2\|K^{-1}\Phi_s\|_F^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
By the addition theorem, every feature row has squared norm `N_s`, so
\[
\|\Phi_s\|_F^2=nN_s.
\]
Also
\[
q_s=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}K_sK^{-1})
=\lambda_s\|K^{-1}\Phi_s\|_F^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{t_s^2\le nN_s\lambda_s q_s.}
\]
This part of P04 is **PROVED / independently verified**.

Weighted Cauchy over a finite window gives
\[
\left(\sum_{s\in B}t_s\right)^2
\le D_B\sum_{s\in B}\frac{t_s^2}{N_s}
\le nD_B\sum_{s\in B}\lambda_s q_s
\le nD_B\,V(X)/\sigma^2.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{V(X)/\sigma^2\ge T_B^2/(nD_B).}
\]

**Audit verdict:** P04’s finite-window reduction is correct.

## 3. Critical harmonic multiplicities — exact asymptotic geometry

The degree-`s` spherical-harmonic multiplicity is
\[
N_{d,s}
=\binom{d+s-1}{s}-\binom{d+s-3}{s-2}
=\frac{(2s+d-2)(s+d-3)!}{s!(d-2)!}.
\]
For `s=cd+o(d)`, Stirling gives
\[
\frac1d\log N_{d,s}\to
F(c):=(1+c)\log(1+c)-c\log c.
\]
Let `c_*` be the unique positive solution `F(c_*)=log 2`. Numerically,
\[
\boxed{c_*=0.293815373340415\ldots}.
\]
If
\[
k_d:=\min\{s:N_{d,s}\ge 2^d\},
\]
then
\[
k_d/d\to c_*.
\]
The exact consecutive ratio is
\[
\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
=
\frac{2s+d}{2s+d-2}\frac{s+d-2}{s+1}.
\]
Hence at `s~c_*d`,
\[
R_*:=\lim\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
=\frac{1+c_*}{c_*}
=4.40349\ldots,
\]
and
\[
\rho_*:=R_*^{-1}=\frac{c_*}{1+c_*}=0.227091\ldots.
\]

Because `k_d` is the first crossing, `N_{k_d-1}<n<=N_{k_d}`, so
\[
\frac{N_{k_d}}n\le R_*+o(1).
\]
Moreover, for degrees below the threshold the backward multiplicity ratio is at most `rho_*+o(1)`, giving
\[
D_{<k_d-1}:=\sum_{s<k_d-1}N_{d,s}
\le \frac{\rho_*^2+o(1)}{1-\rho_*}N_{d,k_d}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{d\to\infty}\frac{D_{<k_d-1}}n\le
\frac{\rho_*}{1-\rho_*}=c_*.
}
\]
This identity is especially useful: **all degrees strictly below `k_d-1` have total rank at most about `0.293815 n`.**

For any fixed integer `L>=1`, define
\[
B_{d,L}=\{k_d-1,k_d,\dots,k_d+L\}.
\]
Then
\[
D_{B_{d,L}}\le (C_L+o(1))n,
\qquad
\boxed{C_L=1+R_*+R_*^2+\cdots+R_*^{L+1}}.
\]
For `L=1`, `C_1=24.7943...`.

## 4. Gaussian-kernel harmonic mass falls by `1/d` per critical degree

Write `gamma=h^{-2}`. P04’s exact Mercer eigenvalue formula is
\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
\]
Let
\[
D_{d,s}:=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(d+2j).
\]
For `s>=c_0 d` with any fixed `c_0>0`, factor the `r=0` term:
\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=
 e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^s}{D_{d,s}}
\left[
1+\sum_{r\ge1}
\frac{(\gamma^2/2)^r}{r!\prod_{j=s}^{s+r-1}(d+2j)}
\right].
\]
The bracket is `1+O_h(1/d)` uniformly in this regime. Therefore
\[
\frac{\lambda_{d,s+1}}{\lambda_{d,s}}
=
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\left(1+O_h(d^{-1})\right).
\]
With `tau_s=N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}`,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau_{s+1}}{\tau_s}
=
\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}
\left(1+O_h(d^{-1})\right)
=O_h(d^{-1})
}
\]
uniformly for `s>=k_d-1`.

Consequently, for fixed `L`,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sum_{s>k_d+L}\tau_s}{\tau_{k_d+1}}
=O_h(d^{-L}).
}
\]
This is the spectral-scale separation that makes the new finite-window reduction work.

## 5. New deterministic lemma: low-rank suppression costs at most its rank

### Lemma P23-L0
Let `A,H,L` be positive semidefinite `n x n` matrices and suppose `M=A+H` is positive definite. If `rank(L)<=r`, then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}((M+L)^{-1}A)
\ge
\operatorname{Tr}(M^{-1}A)-r.
}
\]

### Proof
Set
\[
D=M^{-1}-(M+L)^{-1}\succeq0.
\]
Since `A<=M`,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(DA)\le\operatorname{Tr}(DM).
\]
Write `C=M^{-1/2}LM^{-1/2}`. Then
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(DM)
=
\operatorname{Tr}\left(I-(I+C)^{-1}\right).
\]
The matrix inside the trace is PSD, has rank at most `rank(C)<=r`, and all eigenvalues lie in `[0,1]`. Hence its trace is at most `r`. This proves the claim. `QED`.

A randomized finite-dimensional sanity check over 100 independent PSD triples produced no violation; the proof above is exact and does not rely on numerics.

## 6. Main new theorem: polynomial lower edge implies Target 3

Fix `L>=1` and write
\[
B=B_{d,L}=\{k_d-1,\ldots,k_d+L\}.
\]
Decompose
\[
K=L_0+A_B+H,
\]
where
\[
L_0=\sum_{s<k_d-1}K_s,
\qquad
A_B=\sum_{s\in B}K_s,
\qquad
H=\sum_{s>k_d+L}K_s.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}(L_0)\le D_{<k_d-1}\le(c_*+o(1))n.
\]

Assume the event
\[
E_d(A)=\left\{
\lambda_{\min}(K_{k_d+1})\ge d^{-A}\tau_{k_d+1}
\right\}.
\]
Because `A_B>=K_{k_d+1}`, on `E_d(A)` we have
\[
A_B+H\succeq d^{-A}\tau_{k_d+1}I.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}((A_B+H)^{-1}H)
&\le
\frac{d^A}{\tau_{k_d+1}}\operatorname{Tr}(H)\\
&=
\frac{d^A}{\tau_{k_d+1}}
 n\sum_{s>k_d+L}\tau_s\\
&=O_h(n d^{A-L}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since
\[
\operatorname{Tr}((A_B+H)^{-1}A_B)
=n-\operatorname{Tr}((A_B+H)^{-1}H),
\]
and Lemma P23-L0 says adding the low-rank block `L_0` can reduce this leverage by at most `rank(L_0)`, we obtain on `E_d(A)`:
\[
\boxed{
T_B:=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}A_B)
\ge
(1-c_*-o(1))n-O_h(nd^{A-L}).
}
\]
If `L>A`, the last term is `o(n)`.

Now suppose only that
\[
\liminf_{d\to\infty}\Pr(E_d(A))\ge p_0>0.
\]
Because `T_B>=0` always,
\[
\liminf\frac{\mathbb ET_B}{n}
\ge p_0(1-c_*).
\]
Using P04’s proved finite-window inequality and Jensen,
\[
\mathbb E[V(X)/\sigma^2]
\ge
\frac{(\mathbb ET_B)^2}{nD_B}.
\]
Since `D_B<=(C_L+o(1))n`, we conclude
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{d\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E V(X)}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\frac{p_0^2(1-c_*)^2}{C_L}>0,
\qquad
C_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L+1}R_*^j,
}
\]
provided `L>A`.

### Consequence
To close Target 3 it is enough to prove the following far weaker statement:

> There exist **some finite exponent** `A` and **some fixed probability** `p0>0` such that the normalized critical harmonic Gram block `K_{k_d+1}/tau_{k_d+1}` has smallest eigenvalue at least `d^{-A}` with probability at least `p0` along all sufficiently large `d`.

A constant lower edge, Marchenko–Pastur convergence, operator-norm isotropy, or high-probability control are **not necessary**.

This is the strongest closure-relevant result of the run.

## 7. Why this is materially weaker than existing attempted routes

P04’s failed generic strategy tried to obtain strong critical-degree row/Gram control from spherical hypercontractivity and high moments. The exponent calculation showed that route does not close at `s~c_*d` and `n=2^d`.

P23-L1 asks for much less:

- not `K_s/tau_s -> I`;
- not a limiting MP law;
- not even a constant lower edge;
- not probability tending to one;
- only one critical block (`s=k_d+1`);
- only a polynomial lower bound `d^{-A}`;
- only on an event with fixed nonzero probability.

This suggests switching from operator-norm kernel approximation to **invertibility / distance-to-span / small-ball** methods tailored to the single harmonic feature map.

## 9. Failed / killed routes this run

### Route A — capacity alone
`sum_s t_s=n` and `t_s<=N_s` do not force leverage into a finite `O(n)`-dimensional window because the degrees above the critical window have infinite total multiplicity. **Insufficient.**

### Route B — trace mass without a lower edge
The high harmonic diagonal mass decays by `1/d` per degree, but
\[
t_{>B}\le \|K^{-1}\|\operatorname{Tr}(K_{>B})
\]
is useless without some lower control on the relevant Gram block. **Insufficient alone.**

### Route C — import polynomial-scaling MP edge directly
The active degree grows like `c_*d`, whereas the available random-kernel edge results checked are in fixed polynomial-scaling regimes. **Hypothesis mismatch.**

### Route D — require a full MP theorem
Unnecessary after P23-L1. The closure obligation is much weaker and should not be over-strengthened.

## 11. Best next attack

Attack the single matrix
\[
R_d:=K_{k_d+1}/\tau_{k_d+1}
=\frac1{N_{d,k_d+1}}\Phi_{k_d+1}\Phi_{k_d+1}^T,
\]
whose feature dimension satisfies `N_{d,k_d+1}/n >= R_*+o(1) > 4.4`.

The exact next lemma to seek is deliberately weak:

\[
\exists A<\infty,\ p_0>0:\quad
\Pr\{\lambda_{\min}(R_d)\ge d^{-A}\}\ge p_0
\]
for all large `d`.

Three routes should be compared:

1. **Distance-to-span / small ball.** For a fresh harmonic feature row `u(X)` and an independent span of `n-1` previous rows, exploit the codimension `N-n+1 >= (R_*-1)n` plus irreducibility of the degree-`s` representation. A constant hard edge is unnecessary; polynomial anti-concentration suffices.
2. **Negative second moment / regularized inverse.** Try to bound a truncated inverse moment of `R_d`; by P23-L1 even a polynomial floor on a positive-probability event is enough.
3. **Growing-degree extension of polynomial-regime edge methods.** Audit which step in Kogan–Huang–Nandy or related tensor-edge arguments truly uses fixed degree. The new target needs only polynomial nondegeneracy, so many sharp local-law ingredients may be avoidable.

This should replace the previous over-strong goal of proving full critical-degree operator-norm isotropy.
