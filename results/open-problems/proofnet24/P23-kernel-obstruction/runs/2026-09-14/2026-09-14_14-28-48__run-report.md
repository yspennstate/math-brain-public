## 1. Exact harmonic normalization

Put

\[
a=h^{-2}.
\]

On the sphere,

\[
\|x-y\|^2=2-2x\cdot y,
\]

so

\[
K(x,y)=e^{-a}e^{a x\cdot y}.
\]

Let \(\mathcal H_{d,s}\) be the degree-\(s\) real spherical-harmonic space with respect to probability measure \(P_d\), and choose an \(L^2(P_d)\)-orthonormal basis

\[
\{\psi_{s,r}\}_{r=1}^{N_{d,s}}.
\]

The addition theorem gives

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{N_{d,s}}\psi_{s,r}(x)^2
=
N_{d,s}
\quad\text{for every }x.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The multiplicity is

\[
N_{d,s}
=
\binom{d+s-1}{s}
-
\binom{d+s-3}{s-2}
=
\frac{(2s+d-2)(s+d-3)!}{s!(d-2)!}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The cumulative dimension through degree \(L\) is

\[
D_{d,L}
=
\sum_{s=0}^{L}N_{d,s}
=
\binom{d+L-1}{L}
+
\binom{d+L-2}{L-1}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The exact consecutive cumulative ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{D_{d,L+1}}{D_{d,L}}
=
\frac{(d+L-1)(d+2L+1)}
{(L+1)(d+2L-1)}.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

The Gaussian-kernel Mercer eigenvalue on degree \(s\) is

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s}
=
e^{-a}
\Gamma(d/2)
(2/a)^{d/2-1}
I_{s+d/2-1}(a).
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

This normalization agrees with the circle and \(S^2\) checks:
for \(d=2\), \(\lambda_{2,s}=e^{-a}I_s(a)\); for \(d=3,s=0\),
\(\lambda_{3,0}=e^{-a}\sinh(a)/a\).

From the positive modified-Bessel series

\[
I_\nu(a)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(a/2)^{\nu+2r}}
{r!\Gamma(\nu+r+1)},
\]

one gets directly

\[
\frac{I_{\nu+1}(a)}{I_\nu(a)}
=
\frac a2
\frac{\sum_r w_r/(\nu+r+1)}
{\sum_r w_r}
\le
\frac{a}{2(\nu+1)}.
\]

With \(\nu=s+d/2-1\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda_{d,s+1}}{\lambda_{d,s}}
\le
\frac{a}{d+2s}.
}
\tag{1.6}
\]

Thus for fixed \(h\), the degree eigenvalues are decreasing for all sufficiently large \(d\).

## 3. New lemma P23-L3: optimized finite-block leverage

The previous P23 finite-block lemma used a spectral threshold \(1/2\), which produced the factor \(4D(D-1)/n\). There is no reason to freeze that threshold at \(1/2\).

### P23-L3 — PROVED

Suppose a positive Mercer kernel contains \(D\) orthonormal eigenfunctions
\(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_D\) with eigenvalues

\[
\lambda_j\ge\lambda_*>0
\]

and constant diagonal

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{D}\psi_j(x)^2=D
\quad\text{for all }x.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let

\[
\Phi_{ij}=\psi_j(X_i),
\qquad
\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_D),
\]
\[
A=\Phi\Lambda\Phi^\top,
\qquad
B=K-A\succeq0,
\]

and let

\[
\tau=\sum_{j>D}\lambda_j
\]

denote the tail Mercer trace, counting multiplicity.

Then for every fixed \(\eta\in(0,1)\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{n,d}
\ge
\frac{\sigma^2}{nD}
\left[
D
-
\frac{D(D-1)}
{n(1-\eta)^2}
-
\frac{\tau}{\eta\lambda_*}
\right]_+^2.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This strictly strengthens the previous P23-L2. Taking \(\eta=1/2\) recovers

\[
D-\frac{4D(D-1)}n-\frac{2\tau}{\lambda_*}.
\]

### Proof

Set

\[
S=\frac1n\Phi^\top\Phi.
\]

By orthonormality,

\[
\mathbb E S=I_D.
\]

By the constant-diagonal assumption,

\[
\|\psi(X)\|^2=D
\quad\text{deterministically}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\|S-I\|_F^2
&=
\frac1n
\mathbb E\|
\psi(X)\psi(X)^\top-I
\|_F^2\\
&=
\frac1n(D^2-D)\\
&=
\frac{D(D-1)}n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let \(r_\eta\) be the number of eigenvalues of \(S\) at least \(\eta\).
Every eigenvalue below \(\eta\) contributes at least \((1-\eta)^2\) to
\(\|S-I\|_F^2\). Thus pathwise

\[
D-r_\eta
\le
\frac{\|S-I\|_F^2}{(1-\eta)^2},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E r_\eta
\ge
D-
\frac{D(D-1)}
{n(1-\eta)^2}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since

\[
A
=
\Phi\Lambda\Phi^\top
\succeq
\lambda_*\Phi\Phi^\top,
\]

\(A\) has at least \(r_\eta\) eigenvalues

\[
\kappa_i\ge n\eta\lambda_*.
\]

Let \(u_i\) be corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors. With

\[
T=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}A),
\]

the inverse-Rayleigh inequality

\[
(u^\top Ku)(u^\top K^{-1}u)\ge1
\]

gives

\[
\kappa_i u_i^\top K^{-1}u_i
\ge
\frac{\kappa_i}
{\kappa_i+u_i^\top Bu_i}.
\]

Using \(x/(x+y)\ge1-y/x\),

\[
\kappa_i u_i^\top K^{-1}u_i
\ge
1-
\frac{u_i^\top Bu_i}
{n\eta\lambda_*}.
\]

Summing over the \(r_\eta\) selected eigenvectors,

\[
T
\ge
r_\eta
-
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}B}
{n\eta\lambda_*}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The tail diagonal is deterministic in expectation:

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}B
=
n\tau.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ET
\ge
D-
\frac{D(D-1)}
{n(1-\eta)^2}
-
\frac{\tau}{\eta\lambda_*}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

It remains to convert leverage to prediction risk.

For each selected eigenfunction let

\[
\phi_j
=
(\psi_j(X_1),\ldots,\psi_j(X_n))^\top,
\]
\[
v_j
=
\lambda_jK^{-1}\phi_j,
\qquad
\ell_j
=
\phi_j^\top v_j.
\]

The \(\psi_j\)-coefficient of the noise interpolant is \(v_j^\top\varepsilon\), so

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal E_{n,d}
\ge
\sigma^2
\sum_{j=1}^{D}
\mathbb E\|v_j\|^2.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Since

\[
\mathbb E\|\phi_j\|^2=n,
\]

Cauchy gives

\[
(\mathbb E\ell_j)^2
\le
n\,\mathbb E\|v_j\|^2.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{D}\ell_j
=
\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}A)
=
T.
\]

Thus another Cauchy inequality gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{D}
\mathbb E\|v_j\|^2
\ge
\frac{1}{nD}
(\mathbb ET)^2.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Combining (3.6)–(3.9) proves (3.2).

No lower singular-value theorem is used. No independence between \(A\) and \(B\) is used.

## 10. Expectation versus probability

The proof deliberately stays at expectation level.

What is proved is

\[
\liminf_d
\mathbb E_{X,\varepsilon}\mathcal E_{2^d,d}
\ge0.09\sigma^2.
\]

The argument does **not** prove

\[
\Pr\{\mathcal E_{2^d,d}>c\sigma^2\}\to1,
\]

nor even a fixed positive-probability lower bound.

The reason is structural: P23-L3 uses

\[
\mathbb E\|S-I\|_F^2
=
D(D-1)/n
\]

to lower-bound an **expected** count of moderately nondegenerate directions. This is exactly sufficient for the source theorem but does not provide concentration of that count.

No rare inverse-Gram event is integrated, so the proof is robust to severe lower-tail conditioning phenomena.
