# 4. New pathwise direct trace theorem

Fix a cumulative degree \(L\).  Write

\[
A=K_{\le L},
\qquad
B=K_{>L},
\qquad
K=A+B.
\]

Let \(\Phi\) contain an orthonormal basis of all harmonics through degree \(L\), let

\[
D=D_{d,L},
\qquad
G=\Phi\Phi^\top,
\qquad
\lambda=\lambda_{d,L}.
\]

By monotonicity of the Mercer weights on the low block,

\[
A\succeq A_0:=\lambda G.
\]

The Gaussian Gram matrix \(K\) is strictly positive definite for distinct training points; iid sphere samples are distinct almost surely.  To avoid needing any invertibility statement for \(A_0+B\), regularize.

For \(\epsilon>0\), set

\[
K_\epsilon=K+\epsilon I,
\qquad
C_\epsilon=A_0+B+\epsilon I.
\]

Then

\[
K_\epsilon\succeq C_\epsilon>0,
\qquad
K_\epsilon^{-1}\preceq C_\epsilon^{-1}.
\]

Now

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1}A)
&=n
-\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1}B)
-\epsilon\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1})
\\
&\ge
n
-\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1}B)
-\epsilon\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1})
\\
&=
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1}A_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The inequality uses only inverse order and the fact that for PSD \(M\) and \(0\preceq X\preceq Y\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(XM)\le\operatorname{Tr}(YM).
\]

Apply Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy to

\[
X=C_\epsilon^{-1/2}A_0^{1/2},
\qquad
Y=C_\epsilon^{1/2}A_0^{1/2}.
\]

Since

\[
\langle X,Y\rangle_F=\operatorname{Tr}A_0,
\]

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}A_0)^2
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1}A_0)
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon A_0).
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1}A)
\ge
\frac{(\operatorname{Tr}A_0)^2}
{\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon A_0)}.
\]

Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).  Since \(K\succ0\), the left side converges to \(T_{\le L}\); the denominator on the right converges to \(\operatorname{Tr}[(A_0+B)A_0]\).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{
T_{\le L}
\ge
\frac{(\operatorname{Tr}A_0)^2}
{\operatorname{Tr}[(A_0+B)A_0]}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Using

\[
A_0=\lambda G,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}G=nD,
\]

this is exactly

\[
\boxed{
T_{\le L}
\ge
\frac{(\lambda nD)^2}
{\lambda^2\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)+\lambda\operatorname{Tr}(BG)}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This proves `P23-DIRECT-TRACE-LEV-20`.

### Why this is genuinely different from the inherited route

The earlier expectation proof first established a thresholded spectral count for \(G/n\), then applied inverse Rayleigh mode-by-mode, and finally used Jensen in the Frobenius defect.  Equation (4.3) bypasses the threshold, spectral count, and threshold parameter \(\eta\) entirely.  It pays only the exact annealed second trace moments that the target leverage itself sees.

# 5. Exact annealed trace identities

Let

\[
\mathcal K_L(x,y)
=
\sum_{s\le L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{d,s}}
Y_{s\ell}(x)Y_{s\ell}(y)
\]

be the unweighted projection kernel through degree \(L\).  Then

\[
G_{ij}=\mathcal K_L(X_i,X_j).
\]

The addition theorem gives

\[
\mathcal K_L(x,x)=D.
\]

Orthonormality gives the Hilbert--Schmidt identity

\[
\int\!\!\int
\mathcal K_L(x,y)^2\,dP(x)dP(y)
=D.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)
&=
\sum_i E G_{ii}^2
+
\sum_{i\ne j}E G_{ij}^2
\\
&=
nD^2+n(n-1)D
\\
&=
\boxed{nD(D+n-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Now let the weighted tail kernel be

\[
\mathcal B_L(x,y)
=
\sum_{s>L}\lambda_{d,s}
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{d,s}}
Y_{s\ell}(x)Y_{s\ell}(y).
\]

Then

\[
B_{ij}=\mathcal B_L(X_i,X_j),
\]

and the addition theorem gives the constant tail diagonal

\[
\mathcal B_L(x,x)=\tau_{>L}.
\]

For an off-diagonal iid pair \((X,Y)\), low and tail harmonic orthogonality gives

\[
E[\mathcal K_L(X,Y)\mathcal B_L(X,Y)]=0.
\]

Indeed each term factors into an \(X\)-inner product and a \(Y\)-inner product between distinct harmonic basis functions, and the low/tail degree sets are disjoint.

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\operatorname{Tr}(BG)
&=
\sum_i E[B_{ii}G_{ii}]
+
\sum_{i\ne j}E[B_{ij}G_{ij}]
\\
&=
nD\tau_{>L}
\\
&=
\boxed{nD\tau_{>L}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

No independence between the random matrices \(B\) and \(G\) is asserted or needed.
