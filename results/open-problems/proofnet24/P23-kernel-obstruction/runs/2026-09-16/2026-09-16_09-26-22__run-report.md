# 3. Proof of `P23-T4-LOW-JORDAN-PAIR-DENSITY-44`

Fix a deterministic Frobenius-orthonormal pair `A,B` in `Sym_d^0`. Put

\[
C=A^2+B^2,

\eta=\operatorname{tr}C^2.

For `theta=(theta_1,theta_2) in S^1`, define

\[
A_\theta=\theta_1A+\theta_2B.

Then `||A_theta||_F=1`.

## 3.1 A deterministic fourth-mass bound for every direction in the plane

For every `v in R^d`, Cauchy--Schwarz in the two coefficient coordinates gives

\[
\|A_\theta v\|^2
=\|\theta_1Av+\theta_2Bv\|^2
\le
(\theta_1^2+\theta_2^2)(\|Av\|^2+\|Bv\|^2)
=v^TCv.
\]

Hence

\[
0\preceq A_\theta^2\preceq C.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Eigenvalue monotonicity for positive semidefinite matrices then gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}A_\theta^4
\le\operatorname{tr}C^2
=\eta.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is stronger than the operator-norm estimate alone: it controls the complete fourth spectral mass of **every** unit direction in the plane.

## 3.2 Exact characteristic function of the Gaussian quadratic pair

Let

\[
Q=
\left(\frac{G^TAG}{\sqrt2},\frac{G^TBG}{\sqrt2}\right).
\]

For `t=rho theta in R^2`, diagonalize `A_theta` with eigenvalues `lambda_1,...,lambda_d`. Since `A_theta` is traceless,

\[
\varphi_Q(t)
=E\exp\left(i\frac{\rho}{\sqrt2}G^TA_\theta G\right),
\]

and the standard one-dimensional Gaussian integral gives

\[
|\varphi_Q(\rho\theta)|
=
\prod_{j=1}^d
\left(1+2\rho^2\lambda_j^2\right)^{-1/4}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Set

\[
a_j=\lambda_j^2.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_ja_j=\|A_\theta\|_F^2=1,
\qquad
\sum_ja_j^2=\operatorname{tr}A_\theta^4\le\eta.
\]

Thus

\[
0\le a_j\le\sqrt\eta.
\]

For fixed `rho`, the function

\[
x\mapsto\log(1+2\rho^2x)
\]

is concave, increasing, and vanishes at zero. Therefore, for `0<=x<=b`,

\[
\log(1+2\rho^2x)
\ge
\frac{x}{b}\log(1+2\rho^2b).
\]

Taking `b=sqrt(eta)` and summing over `a_j` gives

\[
\sum_j\log(1+2\rho^2a_j)
\ge
\frac1{\sqrt\eta}
\log(1+2\rho^2\sqrt\eta).
\]

Substitution into (3.3) yields the uniform radial bound

\[
\boxed{
|\varphi_Q(\rho\theta)|
\le
\left(1+2\rho^2\sqrt\eta\right)^{-1/(4\sqrt\eta)}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

## 3.3 Fourier integrability and the explicit density bound

If `eta<1/16`, then

\[
\alpha:=\frac1{4\sqrt\eta}>1.
\]

Hence (3.4) is integrable over `R^2`. In polar coordinates,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\varphi_Q(t)|dt
&\le
2\pi\int_0^\infty
\rho(1+2\sqrt\eta\,\rho^2)^{-\alpha}d\rho\\
&=
\frac{2\pi}{1-4\sqrt\eta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Fourier inversion therefore gives a bounded continuous density `g_P` and

\[
\|g_P\|_\infty
\le
(2\pi)^{-2}
\int_{\mathbb R^2}|\varphi_Q(t)|dt
\le
\frac1{2\pi(1-4\sqrt\eta)}.
\]

This proves (0.1).

## 3.4 Gaussian-to-spherical small-ball transfer without a local limit theorem

Write

\[
G=RU,
\]

where `R=||G||`, `U` is uniform on the sphere, and `R,U` are independent. Define

\[
S:=\frac{R^2}{\sqrt{d(d+2)}}.
\]

For the normalized spherical pair `F_P(U)`, one has the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{Q=S F_P(U).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

The scalar `S` is independent of `F_P(U)`.

By Markov,

\[
P\{S>2\}
\le\frac{ES}{2}
=\frac12\sqrt{\frac d{d+2}}<\frac12.
\]

Thus

\[
P\{S\le2\}>\frac12.
\tag{3.7}
\]

If `R_P(U)=||F_P(U)||^2<=epsilon` and `S<=2`, then `||Q||<=2sqrt(epsilon)`. Independence plus (3.7) gives

\[
\frac12\mu\{R_P\le\varepsilon\}
<
P\{R_P\le\varepsilon,S\le2\}
\le
P\{\|Q\|\le2\sqrt\varepsilon\}.
\]

The last probability is at most the disk area times the density supremum:

\[
P\{\|Q\|\le2\sqrt\varepsilon\}
\le
4\pi\varepsilon\|g_P\|_\infty.
\]

Using (0.1),

\[
\mu\{R_P\le\varepsilon\}
\le
\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-4\sqrt\eta}.
\]

Together with the trivial bound by one, this proves (0.2).
