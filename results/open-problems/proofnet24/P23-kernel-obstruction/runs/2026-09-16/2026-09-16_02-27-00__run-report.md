# 2. Original Target-3 reduction reverified

Let

\[
K_s=\lambda_s\Phi_s\Phi_s^T
\]

be the degree-\(s\) harmonic block, with multiplicity \(N_s\). Define

\[
t_s=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}K_s),
\qquad
q_s=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}K_sK^{-1}).
\]

The clean-test pure-noise variance is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V(X)}{\sigma^2}
=\sum_s\lambda_sq_s.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The spherical addition theorem gives

\[
\|\Phi_s\|_F^2=nN_s.
\]

Also

\[
t_s=\lambda_s\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_s^T K^{-1}\Phi_s).
\]

Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy gives

\[
\left[\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_s^T K^{-1}\Phi_s)\right]^2
\le
\|\Phi_s\|_F^2\|K^{-1}\Phi_s\|_F^2.
\]

Since

\[
q_s=\lambda_s\|K^{-1}\Phi_s\|_F^2,
\]

\[
\boxed{
t_s^2\le nN_s\lambda_sq_s.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a finite deterministic degree window \(B\), put

\[
D_B=\sum_{s\in B}N_s,
\qquad
T_B=\sum_{s\in B}t_s.
\]

Weighted Cauchy then gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{V(X)}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\frac{T_B^2}{nD_B}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Taking expectations and Jensen,

\[
E\frac{V(X)}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\frac{(ET_B)^2}{nD_B}.
\]

Thus \(D_B\le Cn\) and \(ET_B\ge cn\) suffice.

This is precisely the requested **reduction**, not the leverage proof. The expected leverage capture was proved separately before audit closure. No high-probability control is silently demanded.

# 3. Proof of the new dimension-free scalar theorem

Let the eigenvalues of \(A\) be

\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d,
\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i\lambda_i^2=1,
\]

ordered by decreasing absolute value.

Then

\[
Q_A:=G^TAG
=\sum_i\lambda_i(Z_i^2-1),
\]

where the equality uses tracelessness.

Fix \(r\in(0,1)\).

We split according to

\[
a:=|\lambda_1|.
\]

## 3.1 Nonspiky case: \(a^2\le1/2\)

Here

\[
p_1=1/a^2\ge2
\]

in the notation of Choudhary--Kuchibhotla, and \(\Lambda_1=1\).

Their Theorem 1 gives, uniformly in signs and dimension, for \(0<\varepsilon\le1\),

\[
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le C\varepsilon\,[1+\mathcal I(\varepsilon,\lambda)].
\]

If \(p_1=2\), their explicit threshold formula gives

\[
\mathcal I
\le
\log(2/\varepsilon)^{1/2+1/p_2}
\le
\log(2/\varepsilon).
\]

If \(p_1>2\), write

\[
\delta_i=p_i/2-1>0,
\qquad x=\varepsilon/2.
\]

Their factor

\[
A_{p,i}
=\frac{p_i}{\delta_i}(1-x^{\delta_i})
\]

satisfies

\[
\frac{1-x^{\delta_i}}{\delta_i}
\le \log(1/x),
\]

hence

\[
A_{p,i}^{1/p_i}
\le
p_i^{1/p_i}\log(2/\varepsilon)^{1/p_i}.
\]

Since \(p^{1/p}\le e^{1/e}\) and

\[
\frac1{p_1}+\frac1{p_2}
=\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2\le1,
\]

\[
\mathcal I(\varepsilon,\lambda)
\le
C\log(2/\varepsilon).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le
C\varepsilon\,[1+\log(2/\varepsilon)]
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

uniformly throughout the nonspiky regime.

The layer-cake identity gives

\[
E|Q_A|^{-r}
\le
1+r\int_0^1 t^{-r-1}P\{|Q_A|<t\}\,dt.
\]

Using (3.1) with interval length \(2t\),

\[
E|Q_A|^{-r}
\le
1+C r\int_0^1 t^{-r}\log(e/t)\,dt
<\infty
\]

for every \(r<1\), with a constant depending only on \(r\).

This recovers and slightly repackages P20's nonspiky endpoint.

## 3.2 Spiky case: \(a^2>1/2\)

This is the new part.

After replacing \(A\) by \(-A\) if necessary, assume

\[
\lambda_1=a>1/\sqrt2.
\]

Write

\[
Q_A=a(Z_1^2-1)+Y,
\qquad
Y=\sum_{i=2}^d\lambda_i(Z_i^2-1),
\]

with \(Y\) independent of \(Z_1\).

It is enough to prove the result for \(r\in(1/2,1)\), because for any smaller exponent \(r_0\) and any \(r\in(\max\{r_0,1/2\},1)\),

\[
x^{-r_0}\le1+x^{-r}.
\]

Put

\[
s=r-1/2\in(0,1/2).
\]

### Lemma 3.2.1: one chi-square coordinate

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) and \(r\in(1/2,1)\),

\[
J_r(c):=E|Z^2-c|^{-r}
\le
C_r\left(1+|c|^{-s}\right),
\qquad s=r-1/2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Proof. Using the \(\chi_1^2\) density,

\[
J_r(c)
=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_0^\infty |x-c|^{-r}x^{-1/2}e^{-x/2}\,dx.
\]

For \(|c|\le2\), split at \(x=4\). The tail is uniformly bounded. On \([0,4]\), if \(c>0\), scale \(x=cy\); if \(c<0\), scale \(x=|c|y\). In both cases the prefactor is \(|c|^{1/2-r}=|c|^{-s}\), and the remaining integral is finite because:

- near zero the exponent is \(-1/2\);
- at the possible root the exponent is \(-r>-1\);
- at infinity the exponent is \(-r-1/2<-1\).

For \(c\le-2\), \(|Z^2-c|\ge|c|\). For \(c\ge2\), split the integral into \([c/2,3c/2]\) and its complement; on the central interval the \(\chi_1^2\) density contributes \(O(c^{-1/2}e^{-c/4})\), while away from it the denominator is \(\gtrsim c\). This gives a uniform constant. QED.

Conditional on \(Y\), set

\[
c=1-Y/a.
\]

Then

\[
E\left[|Q_A|^{-r}\mid Y\right]
=a^{-r}J_r(c)
\le
C_r\left(1+|a-Y|^{-s}\right),
\tag{3.3}
\]

because \(a\ge1/\sqrt2\).

It remains to control the shifted quadratic polynomial

\[
P:=a-Y.
\]

### Lemma 3.2.2: the dangerous shift has uniform \(s<1/2\) negative moments

Since \(EY=0\) and

\[
EY^2=2\sum_{i=2}^d\lambda_i^2=2(1-a^2),
\]

\[
\|P\|_2^2
=E(a-Y)^2
=a^2+2(1-a^2)
=2-a^2
\ge1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

As a Gaussian polynomial of degree at most two, hypercontractivity gives

\[
\|P\|_4\le3\|P\|_2.
\]

Interpolating \(L^1,L^2,L^4\),

\[
\|P\|_2
\le
\|P\|_1^{1/3}\|P\|_4^{2/3},
\]

so

\[
\|P\|_1
\ge
\frac{\|P\|_2^3}{\|P\|_4^2}
\ge
\frac{\|P\|_2}{9}
\ge\frac19.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Carbery--Wright for a degree-two polynomial under Gaussian measure therefore gives

\[
P\{|a-Y|\le t\}
\le C\sqrt t,
\qquad 0<t\le1,
\tag{3.6}
\]

with a universal constant.

Thus for every \(0<s<1/2\),

\[
E|a-Y|^{-s}
\le
1+Cs\int_0^1 t^{-s-1/2}\,dt
\le C_s.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Combining (3.3) and (3.7),

\[
E|Q_A|^{-r}\le C_r
\]

uniformly in \(d,A\) throughout the spiky regime.

Together with Section 3.1 this proves (N1).

### Why tracelessness is essential here

Without

\[
\sum_i\lambda_i=0,
\]

the quantity \(G^TAG\) would not equal the centered quadratic chaos \(\sum_i\lambda_i(Z_i^2-1)\), and the spiky decomposition could genuinely approach a one-sided \(\chi_1^2\) singularity whose negative-moment threshold is \(1/2\).

The traceless condition forces the dangerous root of the leading \(\chi_1^2\) coordinate to occur only when the residual quadratic polynomial \(Y\) reaches the remote shift \(a\). Carbery--Wright on the shifted polynomial supplies exactly the additional half power needed to reach every \(r<1\).

# 5. Determinant recursion and the sharp uniform de-selection window

Fix a deterministic rank-\(q\) coefficient plane \(P\). Choose an orthonormal coefficient basis and let

\[
V_P(U_j)\in\mathbb R^q,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,q,
\]

be iid projected fresh harmonic rows. Let \(Y\) be the \(q\times q\) matrix whose rows are \(V_P(U_j)^T\).

Expose rows sequentially and let \(D_j\) be the distance of row \(j\) from the span of the preceding rows. Then

\[
|\det Y|=\prod_{j=1}^q D_j.
\]

Condition on the first \(j-1\) rows. If the surviving orthogonal coefficient space is nonzero, choose any unit coefficient direction \(a_j\) in it and let \(A_j\in P\subset\operatorname{Sym}_d^0\) be the corresponding Frobenius-unit traceless matrix. Then

\[
D_j
\ge
|\langle a_j,V_P(U_j)\rangle|
=
|f_{A_j}(U_j)|.
\]

By (N2), conditionally,

\[
E[D_j^{-r}\mid U_1,\ldots,U_{j-1}]
\le C_r.
\]

Iterating gives

\[
E|\det Y|^{-r}
\le C_r^q,
\qquad 0<r<1,
\]

proving (N3).

Under determinant selection,

\[
dQ_P=\frac{(\det Y)^2}{q!}\,d\mu^q,
\qquad
\Lambda_P=\frac{q!}{(\det Y)^2}.
\]

For \(1<p<3/2\), put

\[
r=2p-2\in(0,1).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{Q_P}\Lambda_P^p
&=
\int
\left(\frac{q!}{(\det Y)^2}\right)^p
\frac{(\det Y)^2}{q!}
\,d\mu^q
\\
&=(q!)^{p-1}
E_{\mu^q}|\det Y|^{-r}
\\
&\le
(q!)^{p-1}C_r^q.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (N4).

The endpoint is sharp: the already-audited P04/P20 local regular-zero argument gives

\[
E_{Q_P}\Lambda_P^p=\infty
\qquad(p\ge3/2)
\]

for every deterministic residual plane in the live harmonic setting.
