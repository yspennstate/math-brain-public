# 4. Proof of the weighted annealed leverage theorem

Fix a finite degree set `B`. Put

\[
A=K_B,
\qquad
C=K-A,
\qquad
K=A+C.
\]

Choose `w_s` and `A_w` as in Section 0.2. Since `0<w_s<=lambda_s` degreewise,

\[
0\preceq A_w\preceq A.
\]

For `epsilon>0`, define

\[
K_\epsilon=K+\epsilon I,
\qquad
C_\epsilon=A_w+C+\epsilon I.
\]

Then

\[
K_\epsilon\succeq C_\epsilon>0,
\qquad
K_\epsilon^{-1}\preceq C_\epsilon^{-1}.
\]

Now use the exact complement identity

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1}A)
=n-\operatorname{Tr}[K_\epsilon^{-1}(C+\epsilon I)].
\]

Since `C+epsilon I` is PSD,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[K_\epsilon^{-1}(C+\epsilon I)]
\le
\operatorname{Tr}[C_\epsilon^{-1}(C+\epsilon I)].
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_\epsilon^{-1}A)
\ge
n-\operatorname{Tr}[C_\epsilon^{-1}(C+\epsilon I)]
=
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1}A_w).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy, applied to

\[
C_\epsilon^{-1/2}A_w^{1/2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
C_\epsilon^{1/2}A_w^{1/2},
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}A_w)^2
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon^{-1}A_w)
\operatorname{Tr}(C_\epsilon A_w).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

By the addition theorem, because complete harmonic degrees are used,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}A_w=na_1
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

pathwise. Letting `epsilon downarrow 0` in (4.1)--(4.2),

\[
T_B
\ge
\frac{n^2a_1^2}
{\operatorname{Tr}(A_w^2)+\operatorname{Tr}(CA_w)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The denominator has exact annealed moments.

For two independent sample points `X,Y`, harmonic orthogonality gives

\[
E[A_w(X,Y)^2]=a_2.
\]

On the diagonal `A_w(X,X)=a_1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
E\operatorname{Tr}(A_w^2)
=na_1^2+n(n-1)a_2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Likewise, on the diagonal `C(X,X)=tau_{B^c}`, and every off-diagonal low/complement cross-term vanishes by orthogonality of distinct harmonic degrees. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E\operatorname{Tr}(CA_w)
=n\tau_{B^c}a_1.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The map `z -> 1/z` is convex on the positive half-line. Taking expectation in (4.4) and applying Jensen to the denominator gives

\[
E T_B
\ge
\frac{n^2a_1^2}
{n[a_1^2+(n-1)a_2+\tau_{B^c}a_1]}.
\]

This is exactly (0.1).

### No hidden independence

Equation (4.6) does **not** assume that `C` and `A_w` are independent; they are functions of the same design. The off-diagonal expectation is zero because their Mercer expansions occupy orthogonal harmonic degree sets. This is the same dependence-sensitive mechanism as in the retained direct trace proof.

# 6. Proof of the water-fill structure

For fixed `a_1`, the second term `tau/a_1` in (0.3) is fixed. Thus maximizing the leverage bound is equivalent to minimizing

\[
a_2=\sum_{s\in B}N_sw_s^2
\]

under

\[
\sum_{s\in B}N_sw_s=a_1,
\qquad
0\le w_s\le\lambda_s.
\]

This is a strictly convex quadratic program. Its Lagrangian has derivative, at an uncapped coordinate,

\[
2N_sw_s-\mu N_s=0,
\]

so every uncapped degree has the same weight

\[
w_s=\mu/2=:c.
\]

A coordinate whose cap is below `c` sits at that cap. Because all caps are positive and `a_1>0`, no positive-mass coordinate is optimally stuck at zero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{w_s=\min(c,\lambda_s)}.
\]

Uniqueness follows from strict convexity.

For a global optimizer one can first condition on its attained `a_1`; replacing its weights by the unique fixed-`a_1` minimizer cannot increase `G`. Hence a global optimizer also has water-filled form.

This proves `P23-T3-WATERFILL-COMPARATOR-41`.
