## 6. Ideas before code: why complete graphs are the right new slice

The preceding path theorem shows that at ultra-low density a sparse geometry can retain an `n^2/theta` relaxation scale. That argument does not explain the opposite dense extreme.

### Move 14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

On `K_n`, the full symmetric group `S_n` acts transitively on states of each particle number. More importantly, at `theta=0` every non-singleton configuration has a death transition, so **the only absorbing configurations are the `n` singletons**. This is false on a general graph, where independent sets of size greater than one also become absorbing at `theta=0`.

That observation turns the sparse-`theta` problem on `K_n` into a finite singular perturbation around an `n`-state absorbing manifold.

### Move 18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

Rewrite the relaxation problem as metastable spectral perturbation. The fast `theta=0` dynamics collapses every multi-particle state to a singleton. A rare birth from singleton `i` creates a pair `{i,j}`; under the fast death dynamics the pair returns to `i` or lands at `j`, each with probability `1/2`. Thus the first-order slow motion should be the continuous-time complete-graph walk with rate `theta/2` on every ordered edge.

### Move 19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM

The heuristic becomes an explicit Schur-complement procedure: split singleton and multi-particle states, invert the fast block, and read off the effective generator on the singleton block. This produces a theorem before any code is written.

## 7. New theorem: exact first-order sparse relaxation on `K_n`

### `C31-I0035-KN-SPARSE-SPECTRUM-03 = PROVED`

Fix an integer `n>=2`. For the Aldous–Fill Open Problem 14.31 chain on the complete graph `K_n`, in the source normalization (4.1), as `theta downarrow 0`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}_{\rm BAB}(K_n,\theta)
=\frac n2\,\theta+O_n(\theta^2).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm BAB}(K_n,\theta)
=\frac{2}{n\theta}\bigl(1+O_n(\theta)\bigr)
=\frac{2}{n\theta}+O_n(1).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is a source-valid infinite family of exact first-order asymptotics. It is **PARTIAL** for Open Problem 14.31: the error constant is allowed to depend on fixed `n`, and this does not characterize all graphs or all `theta`.

### Proof

Let

\[
A=\{\{1\},\ldots,\{n\}\}
\]

be the singleton states and let `B` be all configurations of size at least two. Order the state space as `A,B` and write the generator in block form.

For a singleton `{i}`, the only possible transitions are births to `{i,j}`, one for each `j ne i`, each at rate `theta`. Hence

\[
Q_{AA}(\theta)=-\theta(n-1)I_n,
\qquad
Q_{AB}(\theta)=\theta R,
\tag{7.3}
\]

where

\[
R_{i,\{i,j\}}=1
\]

for each pair containing `i`, and all other entries of `R` are zero.

At `theta=0`, a `k`-particle state of `K_n`, `k>=2`, loses each of its `k` particles at rate `k-1`. Thus it jumps only to level `k-1`, with total rate `k(k-1)`. If the `B` states are ordered by particle number, the fast block

\[
B_0:=Q_{BB}(0)
\]

is triangular, with diagonal entries

\[
-k(k-1),\qquad k=2,\ldots,n.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore `B_0` is invertible; its spectrum is bounded away from zero, with the closest diagonal value `-2`.

The only direct `B -> A` transitions at `theta=0` are from pairs. If `D=Q_{BA}(0)`, then

\[
D_{\{i,j\},i}=D_{\{i,j\},j}=1.
\tag{7.5}
\]

All remaining dependence of the `B` block on `theta` is linear, so for a fixed matrix `B_1`,

\[
Q_{BB}(\theta)=B_0+\theta B_1.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Consider an eigenvalue `lambda` tending to zero with `theta`. Since `B_0` is invertible, for sufficiently small `theta` and `lambda`, the `B`-block eigenvalue equation can be solved as

\[
f_B=-(B_0+\theta B_1-\lambda I)^{-1}D f_A.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Substituting into the `A` equation gives the Schur complement

\[
\left[
-\lambda I
-\theta(n-1)I
-\theta R(B_0+\theta B_1-\lambda I)^{-1}D
\right]f_A=0.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Define

\[
H:=-B_0^{-1}D.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Probabilistically, `H_{S,i}` is the absorption probability that the pure-death `theta=0` chain starting from `S in B` ends at singleton `{i}`. Indeed `B_0H+D=0` with the singleton boundary values. Only pair rows of `H` enter `RH`. Starting from `{i,j}`, the two occupied vertices have identical death clocks of rate one, so

\[
H_{\{i,j\},i}=H_{\{i,j\},j}=\frac12.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Consequently,

\[
(RH)_{ii}=\frac{n-1}{2},
\qquad
(RH)_{ij}=\frac12\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{7.11}
\]

For `lambda=O(theta)`, finite-dimensional resolvent expansion gives

\[
(B_0+\theta B_1-\lambda I)^{-1}
=B_0^{-1}+O_n(\theta+|\lambda|).
\tag{7.12}
\]

Using `H=-B_0^{-1}D`, equation (7.8) becomes

\[
\left[-\lambda I+\theta M+O_n(\theta^2)\right]f_A=0,
\tag{7.13}
\]

where

\[
M:=-(n-1)I+RH.
\tag{7.14}
\]

By (7.11),

\[
M_{ii}=-\frac{n-1}{2},
\qquad
M_{ij}=\frac12\quad(i\ne j),
\tag{7.15}
\]

so

\[
M=\frac12(J-nI).
\tag{7.16}
\]

Thus `M` has eigenvalue `0` on the constants and eigenvalue `-n/2` with multiplicity `n-1` on their orthogonal complement.

It remains only to justify that these are precisely the eigenvalues controlling the small spectral gap. At `theta=0`, the block-triangular matrix has algebraic zero multiplicity `n` because `B_0` is invertible. Its zero eigenspace also has dimension `n`: arbitrary singleton boundary values extend uniquely to `B` by the harmonic relation `f_B=Hf_A`. Hence zero is semisimple. Standard finite-dimensional analytic perturbation, equivalently the Schur determinant (7.8), gives `n` eigenvalues near zero:

\[
0,
\qquad
-\frac n2\theta+O_n(\theta^2)
\quad\text{with multiplicity }n-1.
\tag{7.17}
\]

The eigenvalue zero is exact for every `theta` because `Q_theta` is a Markov generator. Every other eigenvalue at `theta=0` belongs to the invertible fast block and is bounded away from zero; by continuity of the finite-dimensional spectrum those eigenvalues remain bounded away from zero for all sufficiently small `theta`. For `theta>0` the source chain is reversible, so the spectrum is real. Therefore the smallest positive eigenvalue of `-Q_theta` is exactly the branch in (7.17), proving (7.1). Taking reciprocals gives (7.2). QED.

## 8. What the theorem teaches

The complete-graph result identifies the opposite extreme from the path theorem. In the sparse limit on `K_n`, the slow variable is not particle number itself but the **location of the surviving singleton**, and the first rare birth followed by fast coalescence moves that location across each complete-graph edge at effective rate `theta/2`.

For `K_2`, the formula specializes to the exact previously certified gap `theta`. For every fixed `n`, the first-order coefficient is `n/2`; increasing complete-graph connectivity accelerates the sparse chain rather than creating the `n^2` path obstruction.

This is also why the proof does not automatically extend to arbitrary graphs. At `theta=0`, a general graph has every nonempty independent set as an absorbing configuration, not only the singletons. The slow manifold is therefore combinatorially much larger, and the complete-graph Schur complement does not simply become a graph Laplacian. That failure is useful structural information rather than another lemma inside the old path argument.

A plausible next structural target, if the metric remains unchanged and the stuck protocol has not yet forced rotation, is to classify the `theta downarrow0` effective chain on the independent sets of a fixed graph and determine which independent-set geometries reproduce the path-scale obstruction. That is genuinely different from another CBSEP comparison certificate.
