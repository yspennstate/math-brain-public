## 7. Moves 24 and 25 — the extremal comparison object and a two-count Dirichlet proof

### Theorem C31-I0035-CBSEP-COMPARISON-01

For every finite connected simple graph `G` and every `theta>0`, with `p=theta/(1+theta)`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{\theta+2}\tau_{\rm CB}(G,p)
\le \tau_{\rm BAB}(G,\theta)
\le \frac1\theta\tau_{\rm CB}(G,p).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The upper constant is sharp for every `theta`, already on `K_2`.

### Proof

Both chains are reversible for the same conditioned Bernoulli law `mu`. Write `D_BAB` and `D_CB` for their Dirichlet forms.

Fix an underlying edge `{x,y}` and freeze every spin outside it. For the three nonempty local states

\[
a=10,\qquad b=01,\qquad c=11,
\]

the conditioned Bernoulli weights satisfy

\[
\mu(c)=\theta\mu(a)=\theta\mu(b).
\tag{7.2}
\]

In HMT's CBSEP, an edge in state `10` or `01` swaps its particle at rate

\[
\frac{1-p}{2-p}=\frac1{\theta+2},
\]

branches to `11` at rate

\[
\frac{p}{2-p}=\frac\theta{\theta+2},
\]

and from `11` each specified particle is killed at rate

\[
\frac{1-p}{2-p}=\frac1{\theta+2}.
\]

Let `D_bc` be only the branch/coalescence part of the CBSEP form and `D_sep` only the exclusion-swap part. Comparing rates with the source chain gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{D_{\rm BAB}=(\theta+2)D_{bc}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Now count the same local transition triangle two ways. If `r=mu(a)=mu(b)`, its SEP conductance is

\[
c_{ab}=\frac{r}{\theta+2},
\]

whereas the two branch/coalescence conductances are

\[
c_{ac}=c_{bc}=\frac{\theta r}{\theta+2}.
\]

For any three real values `f_a,f_b,f_c`,

\[
(f_a-f_b)^2
\le2(f_a-f_c)^2+2(f_b-f_c)^2.
\]

Multiplying by `c_ab` gives

\[
c_{ab}(f_a-f_b)^2
\le \frac2\theta\Bigl[c_{ac}(f_a-f_c)^2+c_{bc}(f_b-f_c)^2\Bigr].
\]

Summing this inequality over every edge and every frozen outside configuration yields

\[
\boxed{D_{sep}\le\frac2\theta D_{bc}.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{CB}=D_{bc}+D_{sep}
\le\left(1+\frac2\theta\right)D_{bc}
=\frac1\theta D_{BAB},
\]

while trivially

\[
D_{CB}\ge D_{bc}=\frac1{\theta+2}D_{BAB}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\theta D_{CB}\le D_{BAB}\le(\theta+2)D_{CB}.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

For a reversible chain,

\[
\tau_{rel}=\sup_{f\not\equiv\mathrm{const}}
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)}{D(f)}.
\]

Applying this variational formula to (7.5) gives (7.1). Section 6 shows equality in the upper comparison on `K_2`, so `B_*=1`. QED.

This proof is degree-free; the multiplicity of occupied neighbours cancels because it is already encoded in the edge conductances. That is the new route beyond P21's pointwise `k_v<=Delta` FA-1f comparison.

## 8. Immediate source-valid bounds transferred from HMT

Hartarsky-Martinelli-Toninelli study CBSEP on general finite graphs with exactly the same conditioned Bernoulli stationary law. Their Theorem 1 states, in particular:

- if `p_n=Omega(1)`, then `tau_CB=O(1)`;
- if `p_n->0`, then for an absolute constant `c`,
  \[
  \tau_{CB}\le c n\max_y \bar R_y,
  \]
  where `bar R_y=n^{-1} sum_x R_{x,y}`;
- for all `p`, they give a particle-number test-function lower bound;
- if `p_n=O(1/n)`, then `tau_CB` is bounded below by a constant multiple of the meeting time of two continuous-time random walks.

Combining only the first two published clauses with (7.1) gives source-model bounds

\[
\boxed{p_n=\Omega(1)\Longrightarrow \tau_{BAB}=O(1)}
\tag{8.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{p_n\to0\Longrightarrow
\tau_{BAB}\le \frac{c n}{\theta_n}\max_y\bar R_y.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since effective resistance is at most graph distance, a BFS-tree extremal argument gives `max_y bar R_y <= (n-1)/2`, hence in the sparse regime

\[
\tau_{BAB}=O\!\left(\frac{n^2}{\theta_n}\right).
\tag{8.3}
\]

These are genuine source-chain relaxation bounds.
