## 4. Exact source normalization and current-status check — 2026-09-16

The live Aldous–Fill Section 14.6.1 was checked again. On a finite connected simple graph `G=(V,E)`, the state is a nonempty subset `S` of occupied vertices. For each oriented adjacent pair `(w,v)` with `w` occupied:

- if `v` is occupied, it becomes vacant at rate `1`;
- if `v` is vacant, it becomes occupied at rate `theta`.

The reversible law is Bernoulli density

\[
p=\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}
\]

conditioned on nonemptiness, and Open Problem 14.31 asks for bounds on the relaxation time `tau_2`.

For a state `S`, write

\[
d_S(v)=|N(v)\cap S|.
\]

Then the exact source generator is

\[
(Q_\theta f)(S)
=\sum_{v\in S}d_S(v)\,[f(S\setminus\{v\})-f(S)]
+\theta\sum_{v\notin S}d_S(v)\,[f(S\cup\{v\})-f(S)].
\tag{4.1}
\]

No stationary-start substitute, normalized-degree walk, or loop-weighted model is used below.

## 7. New theorem: sparse BAB = killed SEP over independent-set sectors

### `C31-I0035-INDEPENDENTSET-SPARSE-04 = PROVED`

Let `G=(V,E)` be a fixed finite connected simple graph. Let `\mathcal I_+(G)` be its nonempty independent sets. For `r>=1`, let `\mathcal I_r(G)` be the independent `r`-sets.

For `r>=2`, define `S_r^D` to be the generator of continuous-time simple exclusion with jump rate `1/2` for each ordered particle-edge move, **restricted to `\mathcal I_r(G)` with Dirichlet killing whenever the proposed move lands in a non-independent `r`-set**. Let

\[
\lambda_D^{(r)}(G)>0
\]

be the smallest eigenvalue of `-S_r^D`. Let `L_G` be the combinatorial graph Laplacian and define

\[
\boxed{
\gamma(G)=
\min\left\{
\frac{\lambda_2(L_G)}2,
\min_{2\le r\le \alpha(G)}\lambda_D^{(r)}(G)
\right\},
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where the inner minimum is omitted if `alpha(G)=1`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}_{\rm BAB}(G,\theta)
=\theta\,\gamma(G)+O_G(\theta^2)
\qquad(\theta\downarrow0),
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm BAB}(G,\theta)
=\frac1{\theta\gamma(G)}+O_G(1).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The statement is for each fixed finite `G`; it is **not uniform in `|V|`**. It is a source-matching PARTIAL theorem for Open Problem 14.31, not a closure.

### 7.1 The `theta=0` absorbing manifold

Let

\[
A=\mathcal I_+(G),
\qquad
T=\{S\ne\varnothing:S\text{ is not independent}\}.
\]

At `theta=0`, every state in `A` is absorbing. Every state in `T` has at least one occupied edge, hence positive total death rate; every jump lowers particle number. Consequently the pure-death process reaches `A` almost surely after finitely many jumps.

Ordering states as `A,T`, write

\[
Q_\theta=
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_{AA}(\theta)&Q_{AT}(\theta)\\
Q_{TA}(\theta)&Q_{TT}(\theta)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Because the source generator is affine in `theta`, there are fixed finite matrices `R,B_1` and

\[
B_0:=Q_{TT}(0),\qquad D:=Q_{TA}(0)
\]

such that

\[
Q_{AT}(\theta)=\theta R,
\qquad
Q_{TT}(\theta)=B_0+\theta B_1.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For an independent set `I`, there are no deaths and every birth leaves `A`, so

\[
Q_{AA}(\theta)=-\theta\,\operatorname{diag}(b(I)),
\qquad
b(I):=\sum_{v\notin I}|N(v)\cap I|
=\sum_{u\in I}d(u).
\tag{7.5}
\]

If `T` is ordered by particle number, the pure-death block `B_0` is triangular with strictly negative diagonal (minus the total death rate of the state). Thus `B_0` is invertible.

Let

\[
H=-B_0^{-1}D.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Probabilistically, `H_{S,J}` is the absorption probability that the `theta=0` death-only process starting from `S in T` ends at the independent set `J in A`.

For eigenvalues `lambda=O(theta)` the `T` equation may be solved and substituted into the `A` equation. The finite-dimensional resolvent expansion gives the first-order Schur complement

\[
\lambda f_A
=\theta M f_A+O_G(\theta^2)f_A,
\qquad
\boxed{M=-\operatorname{diag}(b)+RH.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

The zero eigenvalue of `Q_0` is semisimple: its algebraic and geometric multiplicity are both `|A|`, because `B_0` is invertible and the `A` block is zero. Hence ordinary finite-dimensional degenerate perturbation applies, and the eigenvalues emerging from zero have first derivatives equal to the eigenvalues of `M`.

### 7.2 Exact one-birth absorption law

Fix `I in A` and a vacant vertex `v` with

\[
K=N(v)\cap I,
\qquad k=|K|\ge1.
\]

The birth `I -> I union {v}` occurs at source rate `theta k`. Immediately after that birth, the only occupied edges involving the new non-independent component form a star: center `v`, leaves `K`. Other occupied vertices of `I` are isolated from this component because `I` was independent and are untouched by the death-only dynamics.

When `m` leaves remain, the center dies at rate `m` and the leaves die at total rate `m`. Therefore the next death is the center with probability `1/2` and a leaf with probability `1/2`; conditional on a leaf death, that leaf is uniform among the `m` survivors.

It follows exactly that:

- the chain returns to `I` with probability `1/2`;
- for each nonempty `R subset K`, `|R|=r<k`, it is absorbed at `I\setminus R` with probability

\[
\frac{2^{-(r+1)}}{\binom{k}{r}};
\tag{7.8}
\]

- if all `k` leaves die before the center, it is absorbed at

\[
(I\setminus K)\cup\{v\}
\]

with probability

\[
2^{-k}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

The probabilities sum to one because

\[
\frac12+\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}2^{-(r+1)}+2^{-k}=1.
\]

Thus `M` never increases independent-set cardinality.

### 7.3 Diagonal blocks are killed simple exclusion

Fix an independent `r`-set `I`. A same-cardinality transition can occur only when `k=1`: if `K={u}`, the unique old leaf dies before the new center with probability `1/2`, producing

\[
I-u+v.
\]

This is precisely a legal token slide along the edge `uv`, and its first-order rate is `1/2`.

For every birth with `k` occupied neighbors, the return probability is `1/2`, so its contribution to the effective diagonal is `-k+k/2=-k/2`. Summing over vacant `v`,

\[
M(I,I)=-\frac12 b(I)
=-\frac12\sum_{u\in I}d(u).
\tag{7.10}
\]

But `b(I)/2` is exactly the total rate of all rate-`1/2` exclusion attempts from `I`, including attempted token moves that would make the set non-independent. Therefore, when `M` is ordered by independent-set size, its `r`th diagonal block is exactly

\[
\boxed{M_r=S_r^D.}
\tag{7.11}
\]

For `r=1`, there is never killing and

\[
\boxed{M_1=-\frac12L_G.}
\tag{7.12}
\]

All other effective transitions go strictly downward in cardinality. Hence `M` is block triangular by `r` and its spectrum is the union of the spectra of these diagonal blocks.

### 7.4 Every higher-particle block is genuinely killed

It remains to show `\lambda_D^{(r)}(G)>0` for every `r>=2` with `\mathcal I_r(G)` nonempty.

Take any independent `r`-set `I` and choose two occupied vertices `u,w`. Because `G` is connected, choose a shortest path

\[
u=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_d=w.
\]

Since `I` is independent, `d>=2`.

Try to slide the token currently at `x_j` to `x_{j+1}`. If `x_{j+1}` is adjacent to another occupied vertex besides `x_j`, that attempted exclusion move leaves the independent-set domain and is a killing edge. If not, the token slide is legal and produces another independent set, reducing its distance to `w` by one along the chosen path. Repeating, after finitely many legal slides one reaches distance two from `w`, where the next middle-vertex move is necessarily a killing move.

Thus from every state, within its legal token-sliding component, there is a path to the Dirichlet boundary. A finite killed continuous-time chain with this property has no zero eigenvalue; equivalently

\[
\lambda_D^{(r)}(G)>0.
\tag{7.13}
\]

The singleton block has one zero eigenvalue and, because `G` is connected, all its others are strictly negative. Therefore `M` has exactly one zero eigenvalue; every other eigenvalue is negative and its smallest absolute value is precisely `gamma(G)` from (7.1).

The eigenvalues of `Q_theta` away from this slow cluster remain bounded away from zero for small `theta`, because they perturb the nonzero spectrum of `B_0`. Since `Q_theta` is reversible for every `theta>0`, its spectrum is real. Combining this with the semisimple perturbation above proves (7.2), and inversion proves (7.3). QED.

## 8. Consistency check and a concrete obstruction to the singleton-only route

For `G=K_n`, `alpha(G)=1`, so there are no higher-particle independent-set blocks. Since `lambda_2(L_{K_n})=n`, (7.1) gives

\[
\gamma(K_n)=\frac n2,
\]

recovering the preceding C31 theorem exactly.

For a cycle `C_n`, the two-particle block already shows why the complete-graph intuition is not general. Consider labeled two-particle exclusion and quotient by translations. If `k in {2,3,...,n-2}` is the clockwise separation, then inside the independent-set domain the relative coordinate jumps

\[
k\to k-1\quad\text{at rate }1,
\qquad
k\to k+1\quad\text{at rate }1,
\]

with Dirichlet killing at separations `1` and `n-1`. The principal eigenfunction on this path is

\[
\sin\frac{\pi(k-1)}{n-2},
\]

which is symmetric under `k <-> n-k` and therefore descends to unordered particle pairs. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_D^{(2)}(C_n)=2-2\cos\frac{\pi}{n-2}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

On `C_8`, this gives

\[
\lambda_D^{(2)}(C_8)=2-\sqrt3,
\]

while the singleton coefficient is

\[
\frac{\lambda_2(L_{C_8})}{2}
=1-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
\]

Moreover

\[
2-\sqrt3<1-\frac{\sqrt2}{2},
\]

because this is equivalent to `sqrt(3)>1+sqrt(2)/2`, whose square reduces to `3/2>sqrt(2)`, i.e. `9/4>2`. Therefore a higher-particle independent-set well can be slower than the singleton random-walk mode.

This is the precise failure found by Move 20. Any source-level sparse-density characterization based only on `lambda_2(L_G)` misses genuine metastability in independent-set sectors. The relevant first-order objects are the whole family of killed-exclusion Dirichlet eigenvalues in (7.1).

As `n` grows, (8.1) is asymptotic to `pi^2/(n-2)^2`, whereas the singleton coefficient `1-cos(2pi/n)` is asymptotic to `2pi^2/n^2`; the factor-two separation is therefore structural, not an isolated small-cycle accident.
