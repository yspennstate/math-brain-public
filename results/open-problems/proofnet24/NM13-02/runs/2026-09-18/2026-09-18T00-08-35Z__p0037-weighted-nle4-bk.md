## 3. Previously certified rank-three reduction

The admitted run proved the following support lemma, which is measure-independent.

**Rank-`r` witness reduction.** If a probability law is supported on `r`-element subsets and `A,B` are increasing, then for checking `A square B` on that support one may delete minimal witnesses of size `r`: whenever disjoint occurrence happens at an `r`-set, the two nonempty disjoint minimal witnesses each have size at most `r-1`. Deleting size-`r` witnesses preserves the square event on the support and can only decrease the two marginal event probabilities.

For a spanning tree on four vertices, `r=3`. Thus every nontrivial event can be reduced to an antichain of singleton and pair edge-witnesses. The exact event list on `K4` has `40,068` nontrivial reduced events. The old uniform checker used this to prove BK for every unweighted simple graph on at most four vertices.

## 4. Method #18 — say it in another language: BK becomes one homogeneous polynomial inequality

Let the six edges of `K4` have positive weights `x_e`, and let `T_4` be the 16 spanning trees. Write

`m_T(x) = product_{e in T} x_e`,

`Z(x) = sum_{T in T_4} m_T(x)`.

For a reduced increasing event `A`, set

`F_A(x) = sum_{T in A cap T_4} m_T(x)`,

and similarly define `F_B` and `F_square` for `A square B` on spanning-tree support. The weighted-UST BK inequality is exactly

`D_{A,B}(x) := F_A(x) F_B(x) - Z(x) F_square(x) >= 0`.

`D_{A,B}` is homogeneous of degree six. Every monomial comes from a product of two tree monomials, so its exponent vector `alpha` lies in `{0,1,2}^6` with coordinate sum six. Exactly `101` such product-monomial cells actually occur.

This translation is the useful change of language: instead of testing uncountably many positive weight vectors, it is enough to prove a structured polynomial is nonnegative on the positive orthant for every finite reduced event pair.

## 5. Method #14 — find the group acting: the S4 quotient isolates one possible negative cell

The vertex-permutation group `S4` acts on the six edges, the 16 trees, the 40,068 reduced events, and the 101 exponent cells. The exact checker verifies:

- the reduced events form `1,958` `S4` orbits;
- the 101 exponent cells form only **9** `S4` orbits;
- the unique `S4`-fixed exponent cell is the center

`alpha_0 = (1,1,1,1,1,1)`,

corresponding to the monomial

`M_0 = product_{e in E(K4)} x_e`.

For every ordered reduced-event pair `(A,B)`, all coefficients of `D_{A,B}` on the eight noncentral cell orbits are nonnegative. The checker tests one representative cell from each noncentral orbit for **every** one of the

`40,068^2 = 1,605,444,624`

ordered event pairs. This is exhaustive despite checking cell representatives: if a coefficient were negative at another cell for some pair `(A,B)`, a vertex permutation carrying that cell to the chosen representative would carry `(A,B)` to another enumerated event pair with the same negative coefficient at the representative. The event list is itself checked to be closed under all 24 vertex permutations.

The only coefficient that can be negative is therefore the center coefficient `c_0` of `M_0`. Its exact minimum is `-2`. Across all ordered event pairs:

- `17,796` have center coefficient `-1`;
- `1,650` have center coefficient `-2`;
- total negative-center pairs: `19,446`.

Every other pair has `c_0 >= 0` and hence already has coefficientwise nonnegative `D_{A,B}`.

## 6. Method #3 — prove something stronger: exact weighted K4 theorem via complementary monomials

The weighted statement is strictly stronger than the preceding run's uniform `n<=4` theorem. The exact certificate proves it by pairing every possible negative center term with two positive complementary monomials.

For an exponent vector `beta`, call `gamma` complementary if

`beta + gamma = 2 alpha_0`.

Then the corresponding monomials satisfy

`M_beta M_gamma = M_0^2`.

There are exactly `36` complementary unordered cell pairs in the K4 product support. For every one of the 19,446 reduced-event pairs with center coefficient `c_0=-k`, where `k` is 1 or 2, the checker finds a complementary pair `(beta,gamma)` whose coefficients `p,q` in `D_{A,B}` satisfy

`p>0`, `q>0`, and `4 p q >= k^2`.

All other noncentral coefficients are nonnegative. Therefore, for positive weights,

`p M_beta + q M_gamma >= 2 sqrt(pq) sqrt(M_beta M_gamma)`

`= 2 sqrt(pq) M_0 >= k M_0`.

## 7. New analytic extension: every positively weighted connected simple graph on at most four vertices

The K4 result immediately extends to all connected simple four-vertex graphs by a boundary argument, but that transfer must be written rather than assumed.

Let `G` be a connected simple graph on four vertices with positive weights on `E(G)`. Regard it as a subgraph of `K4`. For `epsilon>0`, assign each missing K4 edge weight `epsilon` and keep every present edge at its source weight. Extend an increasing event `A` on `2^{E(G)}` to the K4 cube by ignoring missing-edge coordinates:

`A^+ := {F subset E(K4): F cap E(G) in A}`,

and likewise `B^+`.

The weighted K4 theorem gives BK for `(A^+,B^+)` for every `epsilon>0`. Since `G` is connected, the K4 spanning-tree partition function has a strictly positive limit as `epsilon downarrow 0`, namely the weighted spanning-tree partition function of `G`. Trees using any missing edge have total probability tending to zero. On a tree supported in `G`, disjoint occurrence for `A^+,B^+` is exactly disjoint occurrence for `A,B`: any witness using a missing coordinate can have that coordinate deleted because the cylinder event ignores it, while every G-witness is also a K4 witness. Hence every term in the BK inequality converges to its weighted-UST counterpart on `G`, and the inequality survives the limit.

For a connected simple graph with fewer than four vertices, attach enough new pendant vertices by bridge edges to obtain a connected simple four-vertex graph. Every spanning tree contains those bridges deterministically, and extending events by ignoring the bridges preserves both marginals and disjoint occurrence. Thus the four-vertex theorem projects back to the original graph.

Therefore:

**Theorem NM13-02-P0037-W4.** For every positively weighted connected **simple** graph `G` with `|V(G)|<=4`, and all increasing events `A,B subseteq 2^{E(G)}`,

`P(T in A square B) <= P(T in A) P(T in B)`

for the weighted uniform spanning tree `T`.

**Corollary.** Any counterexample to Lyons-Peres Conjecture 4.10 on a positively weighted simple graph must have at least **five vertices**.

This strictly strengthens the previous run's unweighted-simple `n>=5` cutoff to the full positive-weight simple-graph class at the same order. It remains a restricted theorem and does **not** close the source conjecture, which is not restricted to four vertices and may also permit nonsimple networks.
