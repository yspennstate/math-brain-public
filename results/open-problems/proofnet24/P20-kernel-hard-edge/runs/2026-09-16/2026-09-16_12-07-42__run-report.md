# 6. Local Fourier lemma

We record the analytic statement used to convert the rank-four Hessian into the sharp subcritical exponent.

## Lemma `P20-LOCAL-FOURIER-RANK4-64`

Let `H:U subset R^N -> R^2` be smooth near `x_0`, and let `a(x)` be a nonnegative smooth compactly supported density in a sufficiently small neighborhood. Assume one of the following at `x_0`:

1. `rank dH=2`; or
2. `rank dH=1`, and for the unique unit cokernel direction `theta_0`, the Hessian of `theta_0.H` has a rank-four restriction to some four domain coordinates; or
3. `rank dH=0`, and for every unit `theta in S^1`, the Hessian of `theta.H` has rank at least four.

Then, after shrinking the support,

```text
| integral exp(i t.H(x)) a(x) dx |
 <= C (1+|t|)^-2.
```

Consequently

```text
mu{|H|<=eps}
 <= C eps^2 log(e/eps),
0<eps<1/2,
```

and

```text
integral |H|^-q a(x) dx < infinity
for every q<2.
```

### Proof

If `rank dH=2`, the submersion theorem gives a locally bounded two-dimensional density, which is stronger than required.

Suppose `rank dH=1`. Away from a small angular cone around the unique cokernel direction, the phase

```text
theta.H
```

has gradient bounded away from zero at `x_0`; after shrinking the spatial neighborhood this remains true uniformly, and repeated integration by parts gives decay faster than any fixed power. In a small cone around the cokernel, the rank-four Hessian restriction persists by continuity. Choose four domain coordinates on which the corresponding `4 x 4` Hessian minor is uniformly nonsingular. Freezing the remaining variables, the standard four-dimensional nondegenerate stationary-phase/Morse estimate gives `O(|t|^-2)` uniformly in the frozen parameters. A finite partition of unity handles the cone.

If `rank dH=0`, every unit target direction has a rank-at-least-four Hessian at `x_0`. Rank four is an open condition. Compactness of `S^1` gives a finite angular cover and, on each piece, four domain coordinates with a uniformly nonsingular Hessian minor. The same four-dimensional stationary-phase estimate gives `O(|t|^-2)`.

For the small-ball estimate, let `X` have the finite measure obtained by pushing `a(x)dx` through `H`. Since

```text
1_{|X|<=eps} <= exp(1/2) exp(-|X|^2/(2eps^2)),
```

Fourier inversion of the two-dimensional Gaussian gives

```text
mu{|H|<=eps}
 <= C eps^2 int_{R^2}
      exp(-eps^2|t|^2/2)(1+|t|)^-2 dt
 <= C eps^2 log(e/eps).
```

Finally, dyadic decomposition gives, for `q<2`,

```text
int_{|H|<=1}|H|^-q dmu
 <= C sum_{n>=0}
       2^{q(n+1)} 2^{-2n}(n+1)
 < infinity.
```

This proves the lemma.

No dimension-uniform constant is claimed. The point is the exact local exponent.

