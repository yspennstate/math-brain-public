# 3. New theorem: exact radial-congruence hard-edge transfer

## `P20-RADIAL-CONGRUENCE-HARDEDGE-55` — PROVED

There is a deterministic sequence

\[
\varepsilon_d=8\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\to0
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{\max_{1\le i\le p}|D_{ii}-1|>\varepsilon_d\right\}
=O(d^{-3}).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

On the complementary event, for the microscopic variables

\[
X_d:=p^2\lambda_{\min}(G)=p\,s_{\min}(DM)^2,
\qquad
Y_d:=p^2\lambda_{\min}(G_0)=p\,s_{\min}(M)^2,
\]

one has the deterministic sandwich

\[
\boxed{
(1-\varepsilon_d)^2Y_d
\le X_d\le
(1+\varepsilon_d)^2Y_d.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, writing `F_X,F_Y` for their CDFs and `\eta_d=P(E_d^c)=O(d^{-3})`, for every `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
F_Y\!\left(\frac{t}{(1+\varepsilon_d)^2}\right)-\eta_d
\le F_X(t)
\le
F_Y\!\left(\frac{t}{(1-\varepsilon_d)^2}\right)+\eta_d.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, if `Y_d` converges in distribution to any random variable with continuous CDF `F`, then `X_d` converges to the **same** `F`. More generally, the same conclusion holds at **any deterministic scale** `a_d\lambda_{min}` in place of `p^2\lambda_{min}`. Thus the diagonal radial congruence cannot alter a continuous limiting hard-edge law or its scale.

### 3.1 Uniform concentration of all `p=Theta(d^2)` radial factors

Let `S\sim\chi_d^2`. Its mgf is

\[
E e^{\theta S}=(1-2\theta)^{-d/2},\qquad \theta<1/2.
\]

A standard one-line Chernoff optimization therefore yields, for `0<u<1`,

\[
P\{|S/d-1|>u\}\le2e^{-cdu^2}
\tag{3.4}
\]

for an absolute `c>0`. One can equally use the explicit bounds

\[
P\{S-d\ge2\sqrt{dx}+2x\}\le e^{-x},
\qquad
P\{d-S\ge2\sqrt{dx}\}\le e^{-x},
\tag{3.5}
\]

which follow from the same mgf calculation.

Take `x=5\log d`. Since

\[
p=\frac{d(d+1)}2\le d^2,
\]

a union bound gives, with failure probability at most `2d^{-3}`,

\[
\max_i|S_i-d|
\le2\sqrt{5d\log d}+10\log d.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Also

\[
\frac d{\sqrt{d(d+2)}}=(1+2/d)^{-1/2}=1+O(d^{-1}).
\]

Combining this deterministic normalization error with (3.6) gives (3.1) for all sufficiently large `d` with the displayed safe choice `\varepsilon_d=8\sqrt{\log d/d}`.

This is a **uniform maximum over every row**, not a fixed-row or average statement.

### 3.2 Deterministic least-singular-value sandwich

For any positive diagonal `D` and any square matrix `M`,

\[
s_{\min}(DM)\ge s_{\min}(D)s_{\min}(M)
=(\min_iD_{ii})s_{\min}(M).
\tag{3.7}
\]

For the reverse direction, if `v` is a unit right singular vector of `M` for `s_min(M)`, then

\[
s_{\min}(DM)
\le\|DMv\|
\le\|D\|\,\|Mv\|
=(\max_iD_{ii})s_{\min}(M).
\tag{3.8}
\]

On `E_d={\max_i|D_{ii}-1|\le\varepsilon_d}`, squaring (3.7)--(3.8) and multiplying by `p` yields (3.2).

Crucially, this argument is pointwise in `(D,M)`. It never conditions on one while pretending independence from the other. The fact that `D` and the source column `b` share every radius `S_i` is therefore harmless for this transfer theorem.

### 3.3 CDF sandwich

On `E_d`,

\[
Y_d\le\frac{t}{(1+\varepsilon_d)^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X_d\le t,
\]

while

\[
X_d\le t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Y_d\le\frac{t}{(1-\varepsilon_d)^2}.
\]

Adding the failure event gives (3.3).

If `F_Y=>F` pointwise at continuity points and `F` is continuous at `t`, both shifted arguments in (3.3) tend to `t`, hence `F_X(t)->F(t)`. This proves the hard-edge transfer.

Earlier P20 reports correctly retained the shared radial factor as a dependency rather than silently deleting it. The present theorem closes that dependency **at the level actually needed for a continuous hard-edge limit**:

### 4.1 Exterior derivation of the partition identity

Let `y_1,...,y_k` be iid centered isotropic rows in `R^m`, let `Y` be the `k x m` row matrix, and let `M>0`. Then

\[
\det(YMY^T)
=
\|M^{1/2}y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge M^{1/2}y_k\|^2.
\]

For a decomposable exterior vector `\xi=v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k`, independence and isotropy imply

\[
E\,|y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge y_k\rangle
\langle y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge y_k|
=k!\,I_{\wedge^k\mathbb R^m}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, expanding a squared Pluecker coordinate into two determinants, any unequal pair of index sets leaves at least one centered row coordinate unpaired and integrates to zero, while an equal `k`-set contributes `k!` by the determinant covariance identity. Equivalently this follows by iterating `E[y_iy_i^T]=I` in the permutation expansion.

Applying `\wedge^k M^{1/2}` and taking trace gives

\[
\boxed{
E\det(YMY^T)
=k!\operatorname{tr}(\wedge^kM)
=k!e_k(M).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus P04's claimed partition identity is exact and uses no fourth moment, density, rotational invariance, or Gaussian assumption.

### 4.2 Hessian check

For a fixed full-row-rank `Y`, let `Q=Y^T(YY^T)^{-1}Y` be the row-span projector. Differentiating

\[
\log\det(YMY^T)
\]

at `M=I` gives `tr(QH)`. The second derivative of the determinant itself is therefore

\[
\frac{D^2\det(YMY^T)|_I[H,K]}{\det(YY^T)}
=
\operatorname{tr}(QH)\operatorname{tr}(QK)
-
\operatorname{tr}(QHQK).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Differentiating `k!e_k(M)` at the identity gives the coefficient

\[
\frac{k(k-1)}{m(m-1)}
\big[\operatorname{tr}H\operatorname{tr}K-\operatorname{tr}(HK)\big].
\tag{4.4}
\]

This agrees with P04. Passing to the residual `P=I-Q` yields its dual rank-`s=m-k` formula. Contracting against traceless Jordan multiplication operators therefore controls only the difference between a squared-trace term and the Jordan-product term; P04's cancellation of the unknown `e(P)` is structurally forced, not a sign accident.

# 5. New stronger no-go: identical covariance partitions, radically different ordinary hard edges

## `P20-COVARIANCE-PARTITION-HARDEDGE-NOGO-55` — PROVED

P04's finite-`d` Jordan counterexample can be strengthened into a full ordinary-hard-edge separation.

For each coefficient dimension `m`, let `Y` be a row distributed uniformly on

\[
\{\pm\sqrt m\,e_1,\ldots,\pm\sqrt m\,e_m\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then `EY=0`, `EYY^T=I_m`, and `||Y||=\sqrt m` deterministically. Hence by (4.2), for **every** `1\le k\le m` and every positive-definite `M`,

\[
\boxed{
E\det(Y_kMY_k^T)=k!e_k(M),

\tag{5.2}

\[
\boxed{
P\{s_{\min}(A)>0\}
=\frac{m!}{m^m}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus

\[
P\{s_{\min}(A)=0\}\to1,
\]

whereas an iid Gaussian square matrix is nonsingular almost surely and has the classical `m^{-1/2}` least-singular-value / real-Wishart hard edge.

So the entire family

\[
\{Z_k(M):1\le k\le m,\ M>0\}

### 5.1 Determinant-volume selection makes the same discrete model perfectly conditioned

where `S` is diagonal with signs and `P` is a permutation matrix. Consequently

\[

The same row law thus has
