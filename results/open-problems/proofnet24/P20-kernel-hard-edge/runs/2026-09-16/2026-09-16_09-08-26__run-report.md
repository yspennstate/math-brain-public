If the two gradients were dependent at any common zero, there would exist `(alpha,beta) != (0,0)` with

```text
(alpha a_i+beta b_i)u_i=0
```

for every `i`. All nonzero coordinates of `u` would then have their projective pairs on one common line. Pairwise noncollinearity forces the support of `u` to contain at most one coordinate; but a one-coordinate common zero would require `(a_i,b_i)=(0,0)`, excluded. Hence every common zero is regular.
