\[
\Lambda_1^2=\sum_i\lambda_i^2=1,

\frac1{p_i}=a_i.

For `0<epsilon<=1`, put

\[

\tag{3.4}

\[

\le35\varepsilon\,[1+I(\varepsilon,\lambda)],

where the relevant formulas are

\[
I=A_{p,1}^{a_1}A_{p,2}^{a_2}
\quad(a_1<1/2),
\tag{3.6}

\[
I=L^{1/2+a_2}
\quad(a_1=1/2),

# 4. New theorem: spectral-spikiness interpolation for quadratic small ball

## `P20-SPECTRAL-AC-INTERPOLATION-54` — **PROVED**

### Statement

Fix `theta in [1/2,1]`. There is an absolute `C` such that, uniformly over every dimension `d`, every `A in Sym_d^0` with

\[
\|A\|_F=1,
\qquad
\|A\|_{\rm op}^2\le\theta,
\]

and every `0<epsilon<=1`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le
C\,\varepsilon^{\beta_\theta}
\bigl(1+\log(2/\varepsilon)\bigr),
\qquad
\beta_\theta:=\frac32-\theta.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently, for every

\[
0<r<\beta_\theta,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{d}\sup_{\substack{A\in\operatorname{Sym}_d^0,\ \|A\|_F=1\\
\|A\|_{\rm op}^2\le\theta}}
E|Q_A|^{-r}
\le C_{r,\theta}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the normalized spherical harmonic quadratic

\[
f_A(U)=\langle A,h(U)\rangle
=c_dU^TAU,
\]

the same dimension-free range holds:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{d,A:\ \kappa(A)\le\theta}
E|f_A(U)|^{-r}
\le 2^{r/2}C_{r,\theta}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.1 Case `a_1<1/2`

For `x in (0,1/2)`, use `1-e^{-y}<=y` in (3.9):

\[
A(x)\le\frac{L(1/2-x)/x}{1/2-x}
=\frac Lx.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence

\[
A(a_1)^{a_1}A(a_2)^{a_2}
\le
L^{a_1+a_2}a_1^{-a_1}a_2^{-a_2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The function `x^{-x}` is uniformly bounded on `(0,1]`, and `a_1+a_2<=1`. Therefore

\[
I(\varepsilon,\lambda)
\le C\max(1,L).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus

\[
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le C\varepsilon(1+L).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Because `beta_theta<=1`, this is stronger than (4.1).

### 4.2 Critical case `a_1=1/2`

The exact source formula is

\[
I=L^{1/2+a_2}.
\]

Since `a_2<=a_1=1/2`, the exponent is at most one. Therefore

\[
I\le C(1+L),
\]

and again

\[
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le C\varepsilon(1+L).
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is the important nonspiky endpoint: the worst possible singularity is logarithmic, not square-root.

### 4.3 Spiky case `a_1>1/2`

Put

\[
\delta=a_1-\frac12>0.
\]

Because the squared eigenvalues sum to one,

\[
a_2\le1-a_1<1/2.
\tag{4.9}
\]

From `e^y-1<=ye^y`, (3.10) gives

\[
B(a_1)
\le\frac La_1 e^{L\delta/a_1}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Hence

\[
B(a_1)^{a_1}
\le
(L/a_1)^{a_1}e^{L\delta}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Using (4.4) for `a_2`,

\[
A(a_2)^{a_2}\le(L/a_2)^{a_2}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Again `x^{-x}` is uniformly bounded and `a_1+a_2<=1`; therefore

\[
I(\varepsilon,\lambda)
\le
C(1+L)e^{L\delta}
=C(1+L)(2/\varepsilon)^\delta.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Substituting into (3.5),

\[
Q_{Q_A}(\varepsilon)
\le
C\varepsilon^{1-\delta}(1+L)
=C\varepsilon^{3/2-a_1}(1+L).
\tag{4.14}
\]

Since `a_1<=theta`,

\[
3/2-a_1\ge3/2-\theta=\beta_\theta,
\]

and for `epsilon<=1`, (4.1) follows.

### 4.4 Negative moments

For `0<t<=1/2`,

\[
P\{|Q_A|\le t\}
\le Q_{Q_A}(2t)
\le C t^{\beta_\theta}(1+\log(1/t)).
\tag{4.15}
\]

For every `r<beta_theta`, layer cake gives

\[
E|Q_A|^{-r}
\le
1+r\int_0^1 t^{-r-1}P\{|Q_A|\le t\}\,dt.
\tag{4.16}
\]

The integral near zero is bounded by a constant multiple of

\[
\int_0^1
 t^{\beta_\theta-r-1}(1+\log(1/t))\,dt
=
\frac1{\beta_\theta-r}
+
\frac1{(\beta_\theta-r)^2},
\tag{4.17}
\]

which proves (4.2).

### 4.5 Transfer from Gaussian to sphere

Write `G=RU`, with `R=||G||` independent of uniform `U`. Since `tr A=0`,

\[
Q_A=R^2U^TAU.
\]

Thus

\[
E|f_A(U)|^{-r}
=c_d^{-r}\frac{E|Q_A|^{-r}}{ER^{-2r}}.
\tag{4.18}
\]

By convexity,

\[
ER^{-2r}=E(R^2)^{-r}\ge d^{-r},
\]

while

\[
c_d=\sqrt{d(d+2)/2}\ge d/\sqrt2.
\]

Therefore

\[
c_d^{-r}/ER^{-2r}\le2^{r/2},
\]

which proves (4.3).

### 4.6 Endpoint checks

- With `theta=1`, `beta_theta=1/2`, so (4.2) gives every `r<1/2` dimension-free for **all** normalized traceless directions. This independently recovers the scalar core of P04/P26.
- With `theta=1/2`, `beta_theta=1`, so every `r<1` is dimension-free on spectrally nonspiky directions. This reaches the true pointwise integrability threshold proved by the previous P20 run, but now uniformly over dimension and direction in this nonspiky class.
- The logarithm at `theta=1/2` is not an artifact: the primary source explicitly notes that opposite-sign two-coordinate examples have `epsilon log(1/epsilon)` concentration.

No Gaussianity is claimed for `f_A(U)`; only a dimension-free concentration/negative-moment estimate is proved.

# 5. New theorem: residual-plane interpolation and full nonspiky `L^{3/2-}` de-selection

## `P20-PLANE-DESELECTION-INTERPOLATION-54` — **PROVED**

Let `P` be a deterministic `q`-dimensional coefficient plane in `E=Sym_d^0`, with Frobenius-orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_q`. Define

\[
V_P(U)=
(\langle A_1,h(U)\rangle,\ldots,
 \langle A_q,h(U)\rangle)\in\mathbb R^q.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For iid `U_1,...,U_q`, let `Y` be the `q x q` matrix with rows `V_P(U_j)^T`. Put

\[
\kappa(P)=
\sup_{\substack{A\in P\\\|A\|_F=1}}
\|A\|_{\rm op}^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume

\[
\kappa(P)\le\theta,
\qquad
\theta\in[1/2,1].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then for every

\[
0<r<\beta_\theta=3/2-\theta,
\]

\[
\boxed{
E|\det Y|^{-r}
\le C_{r,\theta}^{\,q}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The constant is independent of `d`, `P`, and `q` except for the explicit exponent `q`.

### Proof

Expose the rows sequentially and set

\[
D_j=\operatorname{dist}
(V_P(U_j),
 \operatorname{span}(V_P(U_1),...,V_P(U_{j-1}))).
\]

On every realized history choose a measurable unit coefficient vector `c_j` normal to the previous row span. Then

\[
D_j\ge|c_j^TV_P(U_j)|.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Set

\[
A(c_j)=\sum_{a=1}^q(c_j)_aA_a.
\]

Because the basis is Frobenius-orthonormal,

\[
\|A(c_j)\|_F=1,
\qquad
A(c_j)\in P,
\qquad
\|A(c_j)\|_{\rm op}^2\le\theta.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The current sphere point `U_j` is genuinely fresh relative to the history, so Section 4 gives

\[
E[D_j^{-r}\mid U_1,...,U_{j-1}]
\le C_{r,\theta}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since Gram–Schmidt gives

\[
|\det Y|=\prod_{j=1}^qD_j,
\]

iterating (5.7) proves (5.4).

### Exact determinant-selected likelihood consequence

The coordinate functions are orthonormal in `L^2(mu)`, hence

\[
E(\det Y)^2=q!.
\]

The determinant-selected law is

\[
dQ_P=\frac{(\det Y)^2}{q!}\,d\mu^q,
\]

and the exact P23/P04 de-selection density is

\[
\Lambda_P:=\frac{d\mu^q}{dQ_P}
=\frac{q!}{(\det Y)^2}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For `p>1`,

\[
E_{Q_P}\Lambda_P^p
=(q!)^{p-1}
E_{\mu^q}|\det Y|^{-2(p-1)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus if

\[
2(p-1)<3/2-\theta,
\]

i.e.

\[
\boxed{
1<p<\frac74-\frac\theta2,
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_P}\Lambda_P^p
\le
(q!)^{p-1}C_{p,\theta}^{\,q}.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

By Holder, for every event `E` in the final fresh rows,

\[
\boxed{
\mu^q(E)
\le
(q!)^{(p-1)/p}C_{p,\theta}^{q/p}
Q_P(E)^{1-1/p}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

### Important special cases

1. **Unrestricted plane.** Since `kappa(P)<=1`, choose `theta=1`. Then `p<5/4`, exactly the dimension-free P04/P26 window.
2. **Nonspiky plane.** If `kappa(P)<=1/2`, choose `theta=1/2`. Then every fixed `1<p<3/2` is dimension-free, i.e. the full sharp subcritical selected-likelihood range is uniform.
3. **Intermediate plane.** The available likelihood exponent varies continuously as
   \[
   p<7/4-\kappa(P)/2
   \]
   after replacing `theta` by any deterministic upper bound on `kappa(P)`.

This is the first exact certificate in the retained chain that identifies *which geometric residual planes* create the loss between the generic uniform `5/4` window and the pointwise sharp `3/2` endpoint.

# 6. New theorem: rank-two spectral spikiness is controlled by pointwise Jordan energy

## `P20-RANK2-SPIKINESS-JORDAN-54` — **PROVED**

Fix a rank-two coefficient plane `P` and let

\[
\kappa(P)
=
\sup_{A\in P,\ \|A\|_F=1}
\|A\|_{\rm op}^2.
\]

Choose a Frobenius-unit `A in P` attaining the supremum and complete it to a Frobenius-orthonormal basis `A,B` of `P`. Put

\[
C(P)=A^2+B^2,
\qquad
e(P)=\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2-\frac4d.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Lower bound

Because `A,B` are symmetric,

\[
\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2
=
\operatorname{tr}A^4+
\operatorname{tr}B^4+
2\operatorname{tr}A^2B^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Indeed

\[
\operatorname{tr}A^2B^2
=\operatorname{tr}(BA^2B)
=\|AB\|_F^2\ge0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Also

\[
\operatorname{tr}A^4
\ge\|A\|_{\rm op}^4
=\kappa(P)^2,
\tag{6.4}
\]

while Cauchy on the eigenvalues of `B` gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}B^4
\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}B^2)^2}{d}
=\frac1d.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2
\ge\kappa(P)^2+\frac1d,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
e(P)\ge\kappa(P)^2-\frac3d.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

### Upper bound

Because both `A` and `B` lie in `P`,

\[
\|A\|_{\rm op}^2,
\|B\|_{\rm op}^2
\le\kappa(P).
\]

Thus

\[
\|C(P)\|_{\rm op}
\le2\kappa(P).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Since `C(P)` is positive semidefinite with

\[
\operatorname{tr}C(P)=2,
\]

\[
\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2
\le\|C(P)\|_{\rm op}\operatorname{tr}C(P)
\le4\kappa(P).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
e(P)\le4\kappa(P)-\frac4d.}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Equations (6.6) and (6.9) prove the theorem.

### Corollaries

First,

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(P)\le\sqrt{e(P)+3/d}.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Hence whenever

\[
e(P)\le\frac14-\frac3d,
\]

we have

\[
\kappa(P)\le\frac12.
\tag{6.11}
\]

By Section 5, on every such rank-two plane the exact de-selection likelihood has dimension-free moments for **every** `p<3/2`.

Second, pointwise `e(P)>=0` because `C(P)` is positive semidefinite with trace two and

\[
\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2\ge4/d.
\]

For the actual volume residual plane `P_2`, write

\[
e_2=Ee(P_2).
\]

If `d>=24`, then `1/4-3/d>=1/8`. Thus

\[
\{\kappa(P_2)>1/2\}
\subseteq
\{e(P_2)>1/8\},
\]

and Markov gives

\[
\boxed{
P\{\kappa(P_2)>1/2\}\le8e_2.
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

### What this does *not* prove

Equation (6.12) is **not** a closure argument for `e_2=O(1/d)`: its right side is precisely the unknown expectation. Moreover a nonspiky plane can have an order-one Jordan defect below `1/4`, so a different angular constant need not be carried by the extreme spiky set. The theorem localizes the *loss of de-selection integrability*, not the full angular law defect.
