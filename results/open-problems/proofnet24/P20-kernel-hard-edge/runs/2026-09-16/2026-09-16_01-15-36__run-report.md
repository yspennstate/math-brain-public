# 4. New theorem: negative moments of every traceless spherical quadratic up to order one

## `P20-TRACELESS-QUAD-NEGATIVE-MOMENT-53` — **PROVED**

For `A in Sym_d^0`, define the normalized harmonic quadratic

\[
f_A(u)=\langle A,h(u)\rangle=c_d\,u^TAu.
\]

### Theorem

For every `d>=2` and every `0<r<1`,

\[
\boxed{
M_{d,r}:=
\sup_{\substack{A\in\operatorname{Sym}_d^0\\ \|A\|_F=1}}
E|f_A(U)|^{-r}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Moreover the proof gives the crude explicit growth bound

\[
\boxed{
M_{d,r}\le C_r\sqrt d\,[1+\log(2d)].
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No optimality of the dimension factor is claimed.

### Proof

Diagonalize `A`. Since `A` is nonzero and traceless, it has at least one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Let

\[
a=\max\{\lambda_i:\lambda_i>0\},
\qquad
b=\max\{-\lambda_i:\lambda_i<0\}.
\]

Let the common positive and negative trace mass be

\[
S=\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i
=\sum_{\lambda_i<0}(-\lambda_i).
\]

Because `||A||_F=1`,

\[
1
=\sum_{\lambda_i>0}\lambda_i^2
 +\sum_{\lambda_i<0}\lambda_i^2
\le(a+b)S.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `n_+` and `n_-` are the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues, then

\[
\frac Sa\le n_+,
\qquad
\frac Sb\le n_-.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{S(a+b)}{ab}
\le n_++n_-
\le d.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining (4.3)--(4.4) yields the useful deterministic bound

\[
\boxed{ab\ge\frac1d.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now let `G~N(0,I_d)` and write

\[
Q_A=G^TAG.
\]

Condition on every Gaussian coordinate except the two corresponding to eigenvalues `a` and `-b`. The remaining random part is

\[
aG_1^2-bG_2^2+c
\]

for a deterministic conditional shift `c`.

Put

\[
X=\sqrt a\,G_1-\sqrt b\,G_2,
\qquad
Y=\sqrt a\,G_1+\sqrt b\,G_2.
\]

Then

\[
XY=aG_1^2-bG_2^2.
\]

The pair `(X,Y)` is a nondegenerate centered Gaussian. Write `s=a+b`. The marginal density of `X` is bounded by

\[
M_X=(2\pi s)^{-1/2},
\]

and the conditional variance of `Y` given `X` is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)=\frac{4ab}{s},
\]

so every conditional density of `Y|X=x` is bounded by

\[
M_Y=\sqrt{\frac{s}{8\pi ab}}.
\]

Their product is

\[
M_XM_Y=\frac1{4\pi\sqrt{ab}}
\le\frac{\sqrt d}{4\pi}
\tag{4.6}
\]

by (4.5).

For every `t in (0,1]` and every shift `c`, conditioning on `X=x` gives

\[
P\{|XY+c|\le t\mid X=x\}
\le
\min\left(1,\frac{2M_Yt}{|x|}\right).
\]

Splitting the `x`-integral at `|x|=2M_Yt` and at one natural Gaussian scale gives the uniform bound

\[
\boxed{
\sup_c P\{|XY+c|\le t\}
\le
C\sqrt d\,t\,[1+\log(2d/t)].
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The logarithm is the expected product singularity; it is still integrable against every `t^{-r-1}` with `r<1`.

Indeed integration by parts gives, uniformly in the conditional shift `c`,

\[
E|XY+c|^{-r}
\le
1+r\int_0^1 t^{-r-1}P\{|XY+c|\le t\}\,dt
\le C_r\sqrt d\,[1+\log(2d)].
\tag{4.8}
\]

Averaging over the frozen Gaussian coordinates yields the same bound for `Q_A`.

Finally write `G=RU` with `U` uniform on the sphere and `R=||G||` independent of `U`. Since

\[
Q_A=R^2U^TAU,
\]

\[
E|Q_A|^{-r}
=E R^{-2r}\,E|U^TAU|^{-r}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Because `x -> x^{-r}` is convex,

\[
ER^{-2r}=E(R^2)^{-r}\ge(ER^2)^{-r}=d^{-r}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Also

\[
c_d=\sqrt{\frac{d(d+2)}2}\ge\frac d{\sqrt2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
E|f_A(U)|^{-r}
=c_d^{-r}\frac{E|Q_A|^{-r}}{ER^{-2r}}
\le2^{r/2}E|Q_A|^{-r},
\]

and (4.1)--(4.2) follow.

### Why the exponent one is natural

For the extremal rank-two traceless model with eigenvalues `+a,-a`, the Gaussian quadratic is proportional to

\[
G_1^2-G_2^2=(G_1-G_2)(G_1+G_2),
\]

whose density has only a logarithmic singularity at zero. Negative moments are finite for every `r<1` and fail at `r>=1`. Thus the scalar theorem has the correct local integrability threshold.

# 5. New theorem: determinant negative moments by sequential fresh-row exposure

## `P20-HARMONIC-DETERMINANT-NEGATIVE-MOMENT-53` — **PROVED**

Fix a deterministic harmonic coefficient `q`-plane `P` with orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_q` and define

\[
V(U)=\big(f_{A_1}(U),\ldots,f_{A_q}(U)\big)\in\mathbb R^q.
\]

Let `U_1,...,U_q` be iid uniform sphere points and let `Y` be the matrix with rows `V(U_j)^T`. For every `0<r<1`,

\[
\boxed{
E|\det Y|^{-r}
\le M_{d,r}^q
<\infty.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Expose the fresh vectors sequentially. Put

\[
D_j=\operatorname{dist}\big(V(U_j),\operatorname{span}(V(U_1),\ldots,V(U_{j-1}))\big).
\]

On every history choose a measurable unit vector `c_j` in the orthogonal complement of the previously exposed span. Then

\[
D_j\ge|c_j^TV(U_j)|.
\]

Since the coefficient basis is orthonormal,

\[
A(c_j):=\sum_{a=1}^q(c_j)_aA_a
\]

is again traceless symmetric with Frobenius norm one, and

\[
c_j^TV(U_j)=f_{A(c_j)}(U_j).
\]

The current row `U_j` is genuinely fresh and independent of the previous span. Therefore Theorem 4 gives

\[
E[D_j^{-r}\mid U_1,\ldots,U_{j-1}]
\le M_{d,r}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular each `D_j` is nonzero almost surely. Gram-Schmidt gives

\[
|\det Y|=\prod_{j=1}^qD_j.
\]

Iterating (5.2) proves (5.1).

This is exactly the P13-style methodological principle in a setting where it can be proved without importing any general random-distance theorem: at each step the only anti-concentration estimate is applied to one genuinely fresh harmonic row against a frozen normal direction.

# 6. Main new theorem: the inverse-wedge `3/2` threshold is exact

## `P20-INVERSE-WEDGE-SHARP-LP-53` — **PROVED**

Use the deterministic residual `q`-plane setup of Section 3. Under the determinant-selected final-row law

\[
dQ_P=\frac{(\det Y)^2}{q!}\,d\mu^q,
\qquad
R_P=(\det Y)^{-2}.
\]

For `1<=alpha<3/2`, put

\[
r=2(\alpha-1)\in[0,1).
\]

Then

\[
E_{Q_P}R_P^\alpha
=\frac1{q!}
E|\det Y|^{-r}
\le\frac{M_{d,r}^q}{q!}
<\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `alpha<1`, finiteness is immediate because the physical row norm is fixed and `det K` is bounded above. For `alpha>=3/2`, the independently audited P04 regular-zero argument gives divergence.

Hence for every deterministic residual plane,

\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_P}R_P^\alpha<\infty
\iff
\alpha<\frac32.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This resolves P04's explicit open interval `(1,3/2)`.

### Exact power-tail exponent

P04 supplies, for each fixed `P`, constants `c(P)>0,L_0(P)<infinity` such that

\[
Q_P\{R_P>L\}
\ge c(P)L^{-3/2},
\qquad L\ge L_0(P).
\tag{6.3}
\]

On the other hand, for every `alpha<3/2`, Markov and (6.1) give

\[
Q_P\{R_P>L\}
\le C_{d,q,\alpha}L^{-\alpha}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}
\frac{\log Q_P\{R_P>L\}}{\log L}
=-\frac32.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

No claim of a universal asymptotic coefficient is made; logarithmic corrections or plane-dependent constants are not ruled out. The theorem identifies the exact **power exponent**.

### Random actual residual planes

The bound (6.1) is uniform over `P` at fixed `d,q`, because `M_{d,r}` is. Therefore if `P` itself is the actual random volume-residual plane, then

\[
E R_P^\alpha<\infty
\quad(\alpha<3/2),
\]

while P04's conditional divergence for every deterministic plane implies

\[
E R_P^\alpha=\infty
\quad(\alpha\ge3/2).
\]

Thus the sharp threshold survives averaging over the adaptive residual geometry.

# 7. New route certificate: exact fractional de-selection below the barrier

## `P20-FRACTIONAL-DESELECTION-53` — **PROVED**

P23/P04 proved the exact conditional de-selection identity

\[
\frac{d\widetilde Q_P}{dQ_P}=q!R_P,
\tag{7.1}
\]

where `Q_P` is determinant-selected final-row law and `\widetilde Q_P` is the law with the same volume-biased base but `q` fully fresh product-law final rows.

Combining (7.1) with Section 6 gives the exact integrability classification

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\widetilde Q_P}{dQ_P}
\in L^\alpha(Q_P)
\iff
\alpha<\frac32.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus the chi-square divergence is indeed infinite, as P04 proved, but every fractional Renyi/Holder level strictly below `3/2` is finite.

For every event `E` and every `1<alpha<3/2`, Holder gives

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde Q_P(E)
\le
\left(E_{Q_P}(q!R_P)^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}
Q_P(E)^{1-1/\alpha}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Using (4.2) and (6.1), for fixed `q`,

\[
E_{Q_P}(q!R_P)^\alpha
\le
(q!)^{\alpha-1}
\left(C_{\alpha}\sqrt d\,[1+\log(2d)]\right)^q.
\tag{7.4}
\]

At rank two this is only a polynomial/logarithmic prefactor. Hence sufficiently strong polynomial or stretched-exponential selected bad-event bounds **can** be transferred to fresh-row law despite the infinite chi-square divergence.

This does not itself prove the Jordan endpoint: the rank-two base plane has the same marginal under selection and de-selection, and the bare de-selected polynomial replica was already shown by P23 to be circular. The theorem is instead a rigorous rare-event transfer tool for later observables that genuinely involve the final fresh rows.
