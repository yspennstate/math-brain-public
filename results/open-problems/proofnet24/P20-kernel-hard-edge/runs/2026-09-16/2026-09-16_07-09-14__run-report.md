\[

\qquad y=Rq,

\begin{cases}
\operatorname{tr}(z^3)/\|z\|^2,&z\ne0,\\

\end{cases}

\[
\phi(q)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(q^3)}{\|q\|^2}=\kappa_d.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `s<n_d`, expand the projected cubic:

\[
\operatorname{tr}((q-y)^3)
=
\operatorname{tr}(q^3)
-3\operatorname{tr}(q^2y)
+3\operatorname{tr}(qy^2)
-\operatorname{tr}(y^3).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Using the exact orbit identity (2.7), tracelessness of `y`, and `tr(qy)=s`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(q^2y)=\kappa_ds.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Subtracting `kappa_d` after division by `n_d-s` gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\phi(Pq)-\kappa_d
=
\frac{-2\kappa_ds+3\operatorname{tr}(qy^2)-\operatorname{tr}(y^3)}{n_d-s}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Assume first `s<=n_d/2`. Since

\[
\|q\|_{op}=\frac{d-1}{d}<1,
\]

we have

\[
|\operatorname{tr}(qy^2)|
\le\|q\|_{op}\operatorname{tr}(y^2)
\le s,
\]

and

\[
|\operatorname{tr}(y^3)|
\le\|y\|_{op}\|y\|_F^2
\le s^{3/2}
\le s.

Hence the numerator in (4.5) is bounded by `6s`, while

\[
n_d-s\ge n_d/2\ge3/8

\tag{4.6}

# 10. Primary-source nonapplicability certificate for correlated-entry universality

## `P20-CHE-LOPATTO-MIXING-NOGO-59` — PROVED / route rejected

Che--Lopatto, *Universality of the least singular value for sparse random matrices*, arXiv:1711.00580v2, Section 6.1, considers centered entries `x_ij` with a structured correlation profile and assumes exponential decay of correlations between sigma-algebras generated by separated entry sets. In their notation, for variance-one observables `Z_1,Z_2` measurable with respect to entry sets `A,B`,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_1,Z_2)
\le c_1e^{-c_2 d(A,B)}
\tag{10.1}
\]

with dimension-independent positive constants. They also impose high moment bounds and profile regularity.

The present quadratic-feature matrix violates (10.1) exactly.

Fix one row and consider the off-diagonal feature coordinates

\[
X_b=x_1x_b,
\qquad b=2,\dots,d.
\]

They have mean zero and variance one. For distinct `a,b>=2`,

\[
EX_a^2X_b^2
=E[x_1^4]E[x_a^2]E[x_b^2]=3,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_a^2,X_b^2)=2.
\]

Also

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X_b^2)
=E[x_1^4]E[x_b^4]-1=9-1=8.
\]

Hence the normalized square observables

\[
F_b=\frac{X_b^2-1}{\sqrt8}
\]

satisfy

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(F_a,F_b)=\frac14.}
\tag{10.2}
\]

These are variance-one functions measurable with respect to the individual feature coordinates.

There are `d-1` coordinates `X_b`. Under **any** ordering of the `p` feature columns, two of these `d-1` coordinates have column-index separation at least `d-2`. Taking singleton entry sets in one fixed matrix row therefore gives

\[
d(A,B)\ge d-2
\]

while (10.2) remains `1/4`. The right side of (10.1) would tend to zero exponentially in `d`, a contradiction.

Scaling the whole matrix by `p^{-1/2}` to match Che--Lopatto's variance convention does not change the generated sigma-algebras or the normalized square observables, so the obstruction is invariant under normalization.

Therefore the correlated-entry universality theorem in that primary source does **not** apply to the physical quadratic-feature matrix. This closes a potentially misleading shortcut without claiming that no other dependent-row universality theorem could ever apply.
