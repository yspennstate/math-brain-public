# 4. New theorem: the actual residual pencil is transverse almost surely

## `P20-ACTUAL-P2-ALGEBRAIC-TRANSVERSALITY-62 = PROVED`

The previous run left open whether the actual residual plane could land on the transversality discriminant with positive probability. This section closes the exact-discriminant event.

## 4.1 Singular common zero forces a multiple determinant root

Let `P=span{A,B} subset Sym_d^0`. Write the two homogeneous quadrics

```text
q_A(u)=u^T A u,
q_B(u)=u^T B u.
```

Suppose `u in S^{d-1}` is a singular point of their common zero set. At a common zero the spherical gradients are

```text
grad_S q_A(u)=2Au,
grad_S q_B(u)=2Bu.
```

Singularity means they are linearly dependent. Hence there is `(s,t)!=(0,0)` such that

```text
C:=sA+tB,
Cu=0.
```

Consider the binary determinant form

```text
F_P(s,t)=det(sA+tB).
```

If `corank(C)>=2`, all first derivatives of the determinant vanish at `C`, so `[s:t]` is a multiple root of `F_P`.

If `corank(C)=1`, symmetry gives

```text
adj(C)=gamma uu^T
```

for some nonzero scalar `gamma`. Therefore

```text
partial_s F_P=tr(adj(C)A)=gamma u^T A u=0,
partial_t F_P=tr(adj(C)B)=gamma u^T B u=0.
```

Again `[s:t]` is a multiple root. Thus

```text
boxed{
common-zero singularity
=> Disc det(sA+tB)=0.
}
```

So nonvanishing of this binary-form discriminant is a sufficient certificate for regularity of the entire common zero set.

## 4.2 The discriminant is an algebraic function of the residual two-plane

Changing the basis `(A,B)` by `g in GL_2` composes the binary form with the corresponding linear change of variables. The degree-`d` binary-form discriminant scales by

```text
Disc(F o g)=det(g)^[d(d-1)] Disc(F).
```

At the same time

```text
A' wedge B'=det(g)(A wedge B).
```

Hence the discriminant descends to a homogeneous polynomial function, of degree `d(d-1)`, on the decomposable Pluecker cone in `Lambda^2 E`. Denote it by

```text
mathfrak D(A wedge B).
```

Only its zero set is needed below. Equivalently, this can be obtained by eliminating `(s,t)` from `F_P=partial_s F_P=partial_t F_P=0`; the resulting polynomial is basis-invariant up to a nonzero scalar power.

For a base configuration `u_1,...,u_{m-2}`, let

```text
y_i=h(u_i) in E,
Y_0=(y_i)_{i<=m-2}.
```

Define the Hodge bivector

```text
omega(u_1,...,u_{m-2})
= * (y_1 wedge ... wedge y_{m-2}) in Lambda^2 E.
```

Its coordinates are maximal minors of `Y_0`, hence polynomial in the entries of the `y_i`, and therefore polynomial in the coordinates of the `u_i`. When `Y_0` has full row rank, `omega` spans `Lambda^2 ker(Y_0)`.

Consequently

```text
Psi(u_1,...,u_{m-2})
:=mathfrak D(omega(u_1,...,u_{m-2}))
```

is a real polynomial restricted to the product of spheres. Rank-deficient bases have `omega=0` and hence `Psi=0`; full-rank bases with singular residual pencil also have `Psi=0` by Section 4.1.

It remains to prove that `Psi` is not identically zero on the physical product manifold.

## 4.3 Constructive nonzero certificate using a diagonal smooth pencil

Choose vectors

```text
v_i=(a_i,b_i) in R^2,
1<=i<=d,
```

such that

```text
sum_i v_i=0,
v_i!=0,
det(v_i,v_j)!=0 for i!=j.
```

Such choices exist for every `d>=4`: inside the linear space `sum_i v_i=0`, the forbidden conditions are a finite union of proper algebraic subsets. For a concrete `d=4` certificate one may take

```text
v_1=(1,0),
v_2=(0,1),
v_3=(-2,1),
v_4=(1,-2).
```

The six pair determinants are all nonzero and the four vectors sum to zero.

Set

```text
A=diag(a_1,...,a_d),
B=diag(b_1,...,b_d).
```

Both are traceless and linearly independent. Their determinant pencil is

```text
det(sA+tB)=prod_i (s a_i+t b_i).
```

The projective roots are all distinct because the `v_i` are pairwise noncollinear. Hence its discriminant is nonzero.

Let

```text
X={u in S^{d-1}: u^T A u=u^T B u=0}.
```

The uniform point

```text
u_0=(1,...,1)/sqrt(d)
```

belongs to `X` because `sum a_i=sum b_i=0`.

The common zero set is smooth: if at some `u in X` the gradients were dependent, there would be `(s,t)!=(0,0)` such that

```text

```

for every `i`. The kernel line of `(s,t)` in `R^2` can contain at most one of the pairwise noncollinear `v_i`, so `u` would have support on at most one coordinate. A one-coordinate common zero would require that corresponding `v_i=0`, excluded.

## 4.4 Physical rows from the smooth intersection span exactly `P^perp`

Let `P=span{A,B}`. I now show

```text

```

Containment in `P^perp` is immediate because for `u in X`,

```text
<h(u),A>=c_d u^T A u=0,
<h(u),B>=c_d u^T B u=0.
```

For the reverse dimension statement, suppose `C in Sym_d^0` obeys

```text

for every u in X.
```

Because `A,B` are diagonal, `X` is invariant under independent coordinate sign flips. All `2^d` sign flips of `u_0` lie in `X`. Evaluating `u^T C u` on those sign patterns and using Walsh orthogonality forces every off-diagonal coefficient of `C` to vanish. Thus `C=diag(c_1,...,c_d)`.

The identity `c.w=0` on this relative open set implies

```text

```

```text

```

Therefore the annihilator of `span h(X)` inside `E` is exactly `P`, and


Choose `m-2` points `u_1,...,u_{m-2} in X` whose harmonic rows form a basis of `P^perp`. Then `Y_0` has full row rank, its residual plane is exactly this smooth diagonal `P`, and `Psi(u_1,...,u_{m-2})!=0`.
