## 6.2 The linear-form integrability lemma

For `0<gamma<1`, if four real linear forms `ell_1,ell_2,a,b` on `R^n`, `n>=4`, are linearly independent, then

\[
\int_{S^{n-1}}
|\ell_1\ell_2b|^{-\gamma}
(a^2+b^2)^{-(1-\gamma)/2}
\,d\sigma
<\infty.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Moreover the integral is locally uniform over coefficient quadruples whose norms are bounded and whose four-form covariance stays uniformly nondegenerate.

**Proof.** The integrand is homogeneous of degree `-(1+2 gamma)`, and

\[
1+2\gamma<3<n
\]

for `n>=4`. Let `G` be standard Gaussian in `R^n` and write `G=R\Omega`. The radial negative moment `E R^{-(1+2 gamma)}` is finite, so sphere integrability is equivalent to the Gaussian integral. The four linear forms applied to `G` give a nondegenerate four-dimensional Gaussian vector. Near the origin its density is bounded, and after a nonsingular linear change of variables the only local singularity is bounded by

\[
|x_1|^{-\gamma}|x_2|^{-\gamma}|x_3|^{-\gamma}
(x_3^2+x_4^2)^{-(1-\gamma)/2}.
\]

The `x_1,x_2` factors are integrable because `gamma<1`. In polar coordinates in `(x_3,x_4)`, the radial power is exactly `r^{-1}` and the Jacobian contributes `r dr`; the angular factor is `|sin theta|^{-gamma}`, again integrable because `gamma<1`. Gaussian decay handles infinity. Uniformity follows on compact nondegenerate covariance families. `□`

In our application

For nonzero small `tau`, the corresponding bound is

\[

The integral is strictly positive and finite by (6.7). Since `tau=tan t~t`, the same asymptotic power holds in `|t|`.
