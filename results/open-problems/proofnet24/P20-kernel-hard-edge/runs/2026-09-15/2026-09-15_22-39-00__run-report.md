# 4. New theorem: iid null-projector off-diagonal sign

## `P20-IID-NULL-OFFDIAGONAL-SIGN-50`

Let `H` have iid rows `X_1,...,X_{m+1}` in `R^m`. Assume the general-position conditions stated in the executive result. Let

\[
K=I-H(H^TH)^{-1}H^T=vv^T.

Fix two distinct row indices; by relabeling take them to be `1,2`. Freeze the remaining `(m-1)` rows in a matrix

\[

of rank `m-1`. Let `q` be a unit vector spanning `ker B`, put `P=I-qq^T`, and define

\[
L=(BB^T)^{-1}B.
\]

Then `B^TL=P` on `R^m` and `Lq=0`.

For a fresh row `X`, set

\[
x=q^TX,
\qquad
s(X)=x^{-1},
\qquad
a(X)=\frac{L X}{x},
\qquad
A(X)=s(X)^2+\|a(X)\|^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the two fresh rows write `s_i,a_i,A_i`.

## 4.1 Exact null vector after leave-two-out freezing

The `q`-component of any left-null relation requires

\[
c_1x_1+c_2x_2=0.
\]

Choose

\[
c_1=s_1,
\qquad
c_2=-s_2.
\]

Then

\[
s_1X_1-s_2X_2
\]

has zero `q` component, and the coefficients on the frozen rows can be chosen as

\[
c_B=-(a_1-a_2),
\]

because

\[
B^Tc_B
=-P(s_1X_1-s_2X_2)
=-(s_1X_1-s_2X_2).
\]

Thus an unnormalized left-null vector is

\[
c=(s_1,-s_2,-(a_1-a_2)).
\]

Its squared norm is

\[
D=A_1+A_2-2\langle a_1,a_2\rangle>0,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and therefore

\[
K_{12}=-\frac{s_1s_2}{D}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.2 Laplace/tensor-square representation

Use

\[
D^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-tD}\,dt.
\]

For each fixed `t`, expand

\[
e^{2t\langle a_1,a_2\rangle}
=
\sum_{k=0}^\infty
\frac{(2t)^k}{k!}\langle a_1,a_2\rangle^k.
\]

Because

\[
A_1+A_2-2|\langle a_1,a_2\rangle|
\ge s_1^2+s_2^2,
\]

the absolute series after multiplication by `|s_1s_2|e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}` is dominated, after integration in `t`, by

\[
\frac{|s_1s_2|}{s_1^2+s_2^2}\le\frac12.
\]

Hence Fubini/Tonelli and termwise expectation are justified.

For independent identically distributed fresh rows,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&E\left[
 s_1s_2 e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}
 \langle a_1,a_2\rangle^k
 \mid B
\right]\\
&=\left\|
E_X\left[
 s(X)e^{-tA(X)}a(X)^{\otimes k}
 \mid B
\right]
\right\|^2
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E[K_{12}\mid B]
=-\int_0^\infty
\sum_{k=0}^\infty
\frac{(2t)^k}{k!}
\left\|
E_X[s e^{-tA}a^{\otimes k}\mid B]
\right\|^2dt
\le0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Averaging the frozen base proves `E K_{12}<=0`. By relabeling, this holds for every off-diagonal entry.

This is the exact certificate promised in (0.1).

## 4.3 Equality under central row-sign symmetry

If `X` and `-X` have the same law, then under `X -> -X`,

- `s -> -s`;
- `a=LX/(q^TX)` is unchanged;
- `A` is unchanged.

Hence every tensor expectation in (4.5) is zero. Therefore `E K_{ij}=0` for `i!=j`, recovering the rank-one `j=1` case of P04's ordinary sign-symmetric theorem.

The actual harmonic orbit is not centrally symmetric, so equality need not hold; crucially, the sign can only move in the favorable direction for the source overlap.

## 4.4 Constant-direction consequence

Since `K` is rank one,

\[
\sum_iK_{ii}=1

pointwise. Thus

\[
E\,\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1
=1+2\sum_{i<j}E K_{ij}
\le1.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{E(\mathbf1^Tv)^2\le1.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This theorem is distribution-free under the stated general-position hypotheses. It is strictly stronger for the present source question than the preceding central-symmetry comparison route.

# 9. Microscopic source small-ball and inverse-vector tail

## 9.1 Uniform density of one radial source coordinate

The chi-square density is

\[
f_S(s)=\frac{s^{d/2-1}e^{-s/2}}{2^{d/2}\Gamma(d/2)},

The map

\[

\]

is monotone from `(0,infinity)` to `(-infinity,c_d)`, with

\[
\frac{ds}{dy}=\frac{s^2}{c_dd}.
\]

\[

=
\frac{s^{d/2+1}e^{-s/2}}
{c_dd\,2^{d/2}\Gamma(d/2)}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The right side is maximized at `s=d+2`. Writing `alpha=d/2`,

\[
\|f_b\|_\infty
=
\frac{(\alpha+1)^{\alpha+1/2}e^{-(\alpha+1)}}
{\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Stirling's lower bound gives a uniform absolute ceiling; in particular `||f_b||_infty<1` for all `d>=3`.

## 9.2 Rogozin convolution theorem

Rogozin's 1987 bounded-density convolution theorem implies that if independent scalar summands have density ceilings `M_i`, the maximum density of their sum is bounded by an absolute constant times

\[
\left(\sum_iM_i^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}.
\]

For the scaled variables `a_i b_i`, the density ceiling is at most `1/|a_i|` (ignoring zero coefficients). Since `sum a_i^2=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\left\|f_{\sum_i a_i b_i}\right\|_\infty\le C
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

uniformly in dimension and in the coefficient vector.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sup_uP\left\{\left|\sum_i a_ib_i-u\right|\le\epsilon\right\}
\le C\epsilon.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

This remains true conditional on the harmonic block when `a=v(H)`.

This is exactly where the present model differs from P13's smooth narrow-lattice counterexamples: the bounded-density constant here is uniform rather than exploding at the microscopic scale.
