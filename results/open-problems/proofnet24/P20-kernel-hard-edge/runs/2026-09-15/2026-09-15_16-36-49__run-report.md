# 3. New exact Helmholtz extractor

For every deterministic rank-`s` coefficient projection `P`,

\[
\boxed{
(\Delta+4(d+2))(q_P-1)
={4d(d+2)\over s}
\,u^\top M(P)u,
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
M(P)=C(P)-{s\over d}I_d
\]

and `Delta` is the spherical Laplace-Beltrami operator with degree-`ell` eigenvalue `-ell(ell+d-2)`.

### Proof

Let

\[
c_d=\sqrt{d(d+2)/2}.
\]

For a Frobenius-unit traceless symmetric matrix `A`,

\[
f_A(u)=c_d u^\top Au
\]

is a physical degree-two spherical harmonic, so

\[
\Delta f_A=-2d f_A.
\]

Its tangential gradient is

\[
\nabla f_A
=2c_d(Au-(u^\top Au)u),
\]

hence

\[
|\nabla f_A|^2
=4c_d^2u^\top A^2u-4f_A^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta(f_A^2)
=2f_A\Delta f_A+2|\nabla f_A|^2
=4d(d+2)u^\top A^2u-4(d+2)f_A^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Sum over the orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_s`:

\[
\Delta F_P
=4d(d+2)u^\top C(P)u-4(d+2)F_P.
\]

Since

\[
C(P)=M(P)+(s/d)I,
\qquad
F_P=sq_P,
\]

division by `s` and subtraction of the constant `1` give (3.1).

### Exact degree-two recovery

The operator `Delta+4(d+2)` acts by

- `2(d+4)` on degree two;
- `0` on degree four.

Since `q_P-1` has only physical degrees two and four,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_2(q_P-1)
={2d(d+2)\over s(d+4)}\,u^\top M(P)u.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For traceless `M`,

\[
\int(u^\top Mu)^2d\mu(u)
={2\operatorname{tr}M^2\over d(d+2)}.
\]

Because `tr M(P)^2=e(P)`, we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\|\Pi_2(q_P-1)\|_2^2
={8d(d+2)\over(d+4)^2}{e(P)\over s^2}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the P04 Christoffel/Jordan degree-two identity is independently rederived.

Squaring (3.1) gives the useful certificate

\[
\boxed{
e(P)
={s^2\over32d(d+2)}
\|(\Delta+4(d+2))(q_P-1)\|_2^2.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

At rank two, the immediate angular target is therefore equivalently

\[
E\|(\Delta+4(d+2))(q_{P_2}-1)\|_2^2=O(d).
\]

The large universal physical degree-four component is annihilated exactly rather than estimated.

## `P20-CHRISTOFFEL-FISHER-44` — PROVED

Write `f=(f_1,...,f_s)^T` and let `J` be its tangential derivative matrix, whose `a`-th row is `nabla f_a`. On the complement of the common zero set, put

\[

\]

The relative Fisher information is therefore

\[
\boxed{
I(q_P):=\int {|\nabla q_P|^2\over q_P}d\mu
={4\over s}\int\|J^T\widehat f\|^2d\mu.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Each `f_a` is an `L^2`-normalized degree-two harmonic, so

\[
\int|\nabla f_a|^2d\mu=2d.

\[
\boxed{I(q_P)\le8d.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

More precisely,

\[
\boxed{

={4\over s}\int
\|(I_s-\widehat f\widehat f^T)J\|_F^2d\mu.

\tag{6.3}
\]

At `s=2`, with `f=f_A` and `g=f_B`,

\[
\boxed{

\tag{6.4}

At rank one equality `I=8d` holds; at full rank `q_P=1` and `I=0`.
