# 4. Universal exterior moments under volume sampling

This is the main new structural theorem.

Let \(\nu\) be any isotropic probability law on \(\mathbb R^m\):
\[
E_\nu XX^\top=I_m.
\]
Take
\[
k=m-s
\]
independent rows
\[
X_1,\ldots,X_k\sim\nu,
\]
form the \(k\times m\) matrix \(B\), and volume-bias their joint law by
\[
\frac{\det(BB^\top)}{(m)_k}.
\]
The normalization is exact:
\[
E\det(BB^\top)=(m)_k.
\]

Let \(P_s\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\ker B\).

## 4.1 A wedge expectation lemma

For any \(r\)-vector \(\eta\in\wedge^r\mathbb R^m\),
\[
\boxed{
E_X\|X\wedge\eta\|^2
=
(m-r)\|\eta\|^2.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed
\[
\|X\wedge\eta\|^2
=
\|X\|^2\|\eta\|^2
-
\|\iota_X\eta\|^2,
\]
and isotropy gives
\[
E\|X\|^2=m,
\qquad
E\|\iota_X\eta\|^2=r\|\eta\|^2.
\]

By polarization, for \(\eta,\zeta\in\wedge^r\mathbb R^m\),
\[
E\langle X\wedge\eta,X\wedge\zeta\rangle
=
(m-r)\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Iterating over \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), for every
\[
\eta,\zeta\in\wedge^j\mathbb R^m,
\]
\[
\boxed{
E
\left\langle
X_1\wedge\cdots\wedge X_k\wedge\eta,
X_1\wedge\cdots\wedge X_k\wedge\zeta
\right\rangle
=
(m-j)_k
\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.2 Schur-complement identification

For a deterministic \(j\)-frame matrix \(A\),
\[
\det(BB^\top)\det(AP_sA^\top)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
B\\A
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
B\\A
\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
In exterior language, the left side is the quadratic form of
\[
\det(BB^\top)\,\wedge^jP_s.
\]
Equation (4.3) therefore yields the operator identity
\[
E\left[
\det(BB^\top)\,\wedge^jP_s
\right]
=
(m-j)_k I_{\wedge^j\mathbb R^m}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Divide by the volume-sampling normalizer \((m)_k\). Since \(k=m-s\),
\[
\frac{(m-j)_k}{(m)_k}
=
\frac{(s)_j}{(m)_j}.
\]

Hence:

## `P20-VOLUME-EXTERIOR-DESIGN-41` — PROVED

For every \(0\le j\le s\),
\[
\boxed{
E\big[\wedge^jP_s\big]
=

\tag{4.6}
