### 3.1 Independent polarized sixth-moment derivation

Let `A,B,H` be traceless symmetric matrices. For uniform `U` on the physical sphere,

`E[(U^T A U)(U^T B U)(U^T H U)] = 8 tr(ABH)/[d(d+2)(d+4)]`.

All trace-one terms vanish because `A,B,H` are traceless. With

`c_d=sqrt(d(d+2)/2)`

and `f_A(U)=c_d U^T A U`, the coefficient of the physical degree-two projection is therefore

`gamma_d=4 c_d/(d+4)`.

Because `A,B,H` are symmetric,

`tr(ABH)=<(AB+BA)/2,H>_F`.

After removing the scalar trace part, the exact projection is

`Pi_2(f_A f_B)(u)=gamma_d <h(u), J(A,B)>`,

where

`J(A,B)=(AB+BA)/2-[tr(AB)/d] I`.

Since the coefficient map `H -> <h(.),H>` is an isometry,

`||Pi_2(f_A f_B)||_2^2 = beta_d ||J(A,B)||_F^2`,

with `gamma_d^2=beta_d`.

### 3.2 Sum over an orthonormal coefficient basis

For symmetric `A,B`,

`||(AB+BA)/2||_F^2 = [tr(A^2 B^2)+tr(ABAB)]/2`.

Hence, summing over the orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_s`,

`sum_{a,b} ||(A_a A_b+A_b A_a)/2||_F^2 = [Acal(P)+R(P)]/2`,

where

`Acal(P)=tr C(P)^2`,

`R(P)=sum_{a,b} tr(A_a A_b A_a A_b)`.

Orthonormality gives

`sum_{a,b} tr(A_a A_b)^2=s`.

Therefore

`S_2(P)/beta_d = [Acal(P)+R(P)]/2-s/d`.

Since

`delta(P)=Acal(P)-R(P)`

and

`Acal(P)=s^2/d+e(P)`,

we get exactly

`S_2(P)/beta_d = e(P)+s(s-1)/d-delta(P)/2`.

This proves P04's pointwise identity independently.

### 3.3 Averaged baseline

`E delta(P_s)=d s(s-1)/(2(m-1))`,

with `m-1=(d^2+d-4)/2`. Then

`1/d-d/[4(m-1)] = (d-2)(d+4)/[2d(d^2+d-4)]`.

`E S_2(P_s)=beta_d [ e_s+s(s-1)(d-2)(d+4)/(2d(d^2+d-4)) ]`,

exactly as claimed.

# 8. Why a naive Lindeberg replacement still fails

The capped certificate is useful, but it is not itself a replacement proof.

There are `m-1 ~ d^2/2` product rows. To obtain total normalized error `O(1/d)`, a row-by-row telescope needs cancellations or average sensitivity far stronger than a generic Lipschitz estimate.

The functional

`Phi_T=min(D_1^2/D_0,T D_1)`

has bounded value relative to `D_1`, but a one-row perturbation still changes high-degree Gram determinants and their cofactors. The cap eliminates the **free terminal inverse determinant** on the bad branch; it does not prove that all first/second/third replacement derivatives have entropy-compatible expectations.

Moreover, harmonic coefficient rows are isotropic but do not match Gaussian fourth moments. Covariance matching alone cannot cancel the leading replacement error of a high-degree determinant statistic.

Therefore:

## `P20-NAIVE-CAPPED-LINDEBERG-NOGO-45` — ROUTE REJECTED AS CURRENTLY INSUFFICIENT

A direct covariance-only Lindeberg telescope applied to the capped determinant functional is not justified by the present bounds. A successful replacement theorem must retain coupled cofactor geometry and exploit the physical degree-two/Jordan structure, or prove a new averaged cofactor-sensitivity estimate.

This is consistent with P13's independent methodological warning: splitting determinant numerators from inverse-volume factors can destroy the endpoint cancellation.
