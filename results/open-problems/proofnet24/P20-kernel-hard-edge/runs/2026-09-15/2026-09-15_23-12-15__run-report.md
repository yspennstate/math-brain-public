# 4. New theorem: volume-sampled affine inverse is an exact martingale

## `P20-VOLUME-AFFINE-MARTINGALE-51` — **PROVED**

This theorem is distribution-free and requires only centered isotropic rows plus general position.

Let `Y_1,Y_2,...` be iid rows in `R^m` with

\[
EY=0,
\qquad
EYY^T=I_m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `0<=k<=m`, let `B_k` be the `k x m` row matrix. Under `k`-row Gram-volume sampling, define

\[
\frac{d\mathbb Q_k}{d\mathbb P_k}(B_k)
=
\frac{\det(B_kB_k^T)}{(m)_k},
\qquad
(m)_k=m(m-1)\cdots(m-k+1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let

\[
x_k=B_k^T(B_kB_k^T)^{-1}\mathbf1_k
\tag{4.3}
\]

be the minimum-norm solution of

\[
B_kx=\mathbf1_k,
\]

and put

\[
Q_k=\|x_k\|^2,
\qquad Q_0=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then the process

\[
\boxed{
M_k=(m-k+1)(1+Q_k),
\qquad 0\le k\le m,
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

is a martingale for the sequential Gram-volume chain. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
E_{\mathbb Q_k}Q_k=\frac{k}{m-k+1}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

In particular

\[
E_{\mathbb Q_{m-1}}Q_{m-1}=\frac{m-1}{2},
\qquad
E_{\mathbb Q_m}Q_m=m.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The final identity recovers P04's determinant-tilted affine inverse mean, but (4.5)--(4.7) give the entire sequential hierarchy.

## 4.1 Exact transition law

Freeze `B_k` and let `S=Row(B_k)`. For a fresh row `Y`, write

\[
r=P_{S^\perp}Y,
\qquad
R=\|r\|^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Gram--Schmidt gives

\[
\det(B_{k+1}B_{k+1}^T)
=
\det(B_kB_k^T)R.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Because isotropy gives

\[
ER=\operatorname{tr}P_{S^\perp}=m-k,
\tag{4.10}
\]

the exact conditional volume-selection density of the next row is

\[
\boxed{\frac{R}{m-k}\,d\mu(Y).}
\tag{4.11}
\]

No Gaussianity is used.

## 4.2 Exact affine update

Since `x_k` lies in `S`, any extension solving the first `k` equations is `x_k+y` with `y in S^\perp`. Let

\[
a=1-Y^Tx_k.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The minimum-norm correction satisfying the new equation is

\[
y=\frac aRr,
\]

because `Y^Tr=R`. Orthogonality gives

\[
\boxed{Q_{k+1}=Q_k+\frac{a^2}{R}.}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Under the selected density (4.11), the dangerous reciprocal cancels:

\[
E[Q_{k+1}\mid B_k]
=
Q_k+\frac1{m-k}Ea^2.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Centered isotropy yields

\[
Ea^2=E(1-Y^Tx_k)^2=1+\|x_k\|^2=1+Q_k.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Therefore

\[
E[1+Q_{k+1}\mid B_k]
=
\frac{m-k+1}{m-k}(1+Q_k).
\tag{4.16}
\]

Multiplying by `m-k` proves (4.5).

Taking expectations from `M_0=m+1` gives

\[
(m-k+1)(1+EQ_k)=m+1,
\]

which is (4.6).

## 4.3 Last-step identity

At `k=m-1`, let `n` be the unit normal to the row span and

\[
X=Y^Tn.
\]

Then `R=X^2`; the selected last-row density is exactly `X^2d\mu(Y)`, because `EX^2=1`. If `q_0=\|x_{m-1}\|^2` and `a=1-Y^Tx_{m-1}`, then

\[
Q_m=q_0+\frac{a^2}{X^2}.
\tag{4.17}
\]

Thus conditionally under the volume-selected last row,

\[
\boxed{
E_{\rm sel}[Q_m\mid B_{m-1}]
=q_0+Ea^2
=1+2q_0.
}
\tag{4.18}
\]

Averaging (4.18) together with `E_vol Q_m=m` gives again

\[
E_{\mathbb Q_{m-1}}q_0=\frac{m-1}{2}.
\]

This exact cancellation is the affine analogue of the coupled numerator/denominator principle repeatedly emphasized by P12/P13 and the earlier P20 cofactor calculations.

# 5. New theorem: the actual harmonic last facet has exact conditional tail exponents

## `P20-HARMONIC-FACET-TAIL-EXPONENTS-51` — **PROVED**

Let `B` contain `m-1` iid physical harmonic rows. Let `n(B)` be its unit coefficient-space null vector and identify it with a Frobenius-unit traceless symmetric matrix `N(B)`. Let `x_0` and `X_0` be as in (3.3). Put

\[
q(u)=u^TN(B)u,
\qquad
p(u)=1-c_du^TX_0u,
\qquad
q_0=\|x_0\|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then for almost every base `B` under both the ordinary and `(m-1)`-row volume laws:

1. `N(B)` is invertible;
2. the zero set

\[
\Sigma_B=\{u\in S^{d-1}:q(u)=0\}
\tag{5.2}
\]

is a smooth compact hypersurface;
3. the ordinary final-row tail satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}\sqrt L\,P\{Q_m>L\mid B\}
=C_{\rm ord}(B)\in(0,\infty);
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

4. under determinant-volume selection of the final row,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{L\to\infty}L^{3/2}P_{\rm sel}\{Q_m>L\mid B\}
=C_{\rm vol}(B)\in(0,\infty).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The constants have the explicit coarea forms

\[
C_{\rm ord}(B)
=
\frac{2}{c_d\,|S^{d-1}|}
\int_{\Sigma_B}
\frac{|p(u)|}{|\nabla_Sq(u)|}
\,d\sigma_{d-2}(u),
\tag{5.5}
\]

\[
C_{\rm vol}(B)
=
\frac{2}{3c_d\,|S^{d-1}|}
\int_{\Sigma_B}
\frac{|p(u)|^3}{|\nabla_Sq(u)|}
\,d\sigma_{d-2}(u).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Consequently, conditionally on almost every base,

\[
\boxed{
E[Q_m^\alpha\mid B]<\infty
\iff \alpha<\frac12,
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

and under the selected final row,

\[
\boxed{
E_{\rm sel}[Q_m^\alpha\mid B]<\infty
\iff \alpha<\frac32.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The ordinary divergence half of (5.7) strengthens the audited P04 theorem from a one-sided lower certificate to the exact conditional exponent. The volume statement (5.8) is new.

## 5.1 Generic invertibility of the coefficient normal

The point that makes a two-sided asymptotic possible is that `N(B)` is almost surely invertible.

Choose any fixed invertible traceless symmetric matrix `N_0`; for example a scaled version of

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-(d-1)).
\]

It is indefinite. Let

\[
\Sigma_0=\{u\in S^{d-1}:u^TN_0u=0\}.
\]

Claim:

\[
\operatorname{span}\{h(u):u\in\Sigma_0\}=N_0^\perp\subset E.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Indeed, if `X in E` is orthogonal to every `h(u)` on `Sigma_0`, then

\[
u^TXu=0\quad\text{whenever}\quad u^TN_0u=0.
\]

The quadratic-null-cone lemma forces `X=aN_0`. Hence the orthogonal complement of the span in (5.9) is exactly `span{N_0}`. Therefore one can choose `m-1` orbit points on `Sigma_0` whose harmonic rows span `N_0^\perp`.

Now form the unnormalized Hodge/cofactor normal `C(B)` of `m-1` rows. Its coefficient entries are real-analytic functions of the physical sphere coordinates, and so is

\[
F(B)=\det(T(C(B))),
\tag{5.10}
\]

where `T` identifies coefficient vectors with traceless symmetric matrices. The configuration above has `F(B)!=0`; hence `F` is not the zero analytic function. Its zero set has product spherical measure zero. On the full-rank set, normalization by `||C(B)||` does not change matrix rank, proving

\[
\boxed{\det N(B)\ne0\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{5.11}
\]

The `(m-1)`-row volume law is absolutely continuous with respect to the ordinary product law, so (5.11) also holds volume-a.s.

## 5.2 Coarea derivation of the ordinary exponent

Because `N` is invertible, every point of `Sigma_B` is regular:

\[
\nabla_Sq(u)=2Nu,
\qquad
\inf_{u\in\Sigma_B}|\nabla_Sq(u)|>0.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Also `p` does not vanish identically on `Sigma_B`; otherwise the null-cone lemma would imply `I-c_dX_0=aN`, impossible by trace.

For `L>q_0`, put

\[
T=\sqrt{L-q_0}.
\]

By (3.4)--(3.5),

\[
\{Q_m>L\}
=
\{|p(U)|>c_dT|q(U)|\}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Since `p` is bounded and `0` is a regular value of `q`, only a fixed tubular neighborhood of `Sigma_B` contributes for large `T`. Apply coarea in that tube and rescale the transverse coordinate `r=q(u)` by `s=Tr`. Dominated convergence gives

\[
T\,P\{|p|>c_dT|q|\mid B\}
\to
\frac{2}{c_d|S^{d-1}|}
\int_{\Sigma_B}
\frac{|p|}{|\nabla_Sq|}d\sigma.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Since `T/sqrt(L)->1`, this is (5.3).

## 5.3 Determinant-volume tilt shifts the exponent by one full power of `Q`

At the last step the residual scalar is

\[
X=\langle h(U),n\rangle=c_dq(U).
\tag{5.15}
\]

The exact conditional determinant-volume density is

\[
X^2d\mu(U),
\tag{5.16}
\]

and it is normalized because `EX^2=1` by isotropy.

Therefore

\[
P_{\rm sel}\{Q_m>L\mid B\}
=
\int c_d^2q(u)^2
\mathbf1_{\{|p|>c_dT|q|\}}
\,d\mu(u).
\tag{5.17}
\]

Use the same tubular coarea chart and `s=Tr`. The factor `q^2` contributes `s^2/T^2`, while `dr=ds/T`. Hence

\[
T^3P_{\rm sel}\{Q_m>L\mid B\}
\to
\frac{c_d^2}{|S^{d-1}|}
\int_{\Sigma_B}
\frac{1}{|\nabla_Sq|}
\left(
\int_{-|p|/c_d}^{|p|/c_d}s^2ds
\right)d\sigma.
\]

The inner integral is `2|p|^3/(3c_d^3)`, giving (5.4)--(5.6).

This is the exact geometric reason determinant-square tilt regularizes the affine pole: it inserts the square of the same transverse coordinate whose reciprocal creates the inverse tail.

## 5.4 Moment thresholds and global volume no-go

For any nonnegative random variable,

\[
E Q^\alpha
=
\alpha\int_0^\infty L^{\alpha-1}P\{Q>L\}\,dL.
\tag{5.18}
\]

The positive finite tail constants in (5.3)--(5.4) imply the exact conditional thresholds (5.7)--(5.8).

Because (5.8) holds for almost every base under the `(m-1)`-row volume law, Tonelli gives the global fixed-`d` statement

\[
\boxed{
E_{\rm volume}Q_d^\alpha=\infty
\quad\text{for every }\alpha\ge\frac32.
}
\tag{5.19}
\]

In particular,

\[
E_{\rm volume}Q_d=m<\infty,
\qquad
E_{\rm volume}Q_d^2=\infty.
\tag{5.20}
\]

Thus an `L^2`-based ordinary-volume transfer for this affine inverse observable is not merely unproved; it is false.

No dimension-uniform bound on `C_ord(B)` or `C_vol(B)` is claimed. That missing uniformity is precisely why (5.3) alone does not prove a useful asymptotic upper tail at the `d^3` scale.

### 6.1 Self-contained proof of the ordinary law

Choose a deterministic orthogonal row transformation `O` with

\[
Oc=\sqrt m\,e_m.
\]

The matrix `OA_G` is again iid standard Gaussian. Write its first `m-1` rows as `B` and last row as `g`. If `n` is the unit normal to `Row(B)`, then the equation

\[
(OA_G)y=\sqrt m\,e_m

forces

\[
y=t n,
\qquad
t=\frac{\sqrt m}{g^Tn}.

Conditional on `B`, `g^Tn~N(0,1)`. Thus

\[
Q_G=t^2=\frac m{Z^2},
\qquad Z\sim N(0,1),
\]

which is (6.2).

### 6.2 Self-contained proof under determinant tilt

The same row orthogonal transformation preserves `det(A_G)^2`. Gram--Schmidt gives

\[
\det(OA_G)^2
=
\det(BB^T)(g^Tn)^2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Conditional on `B`, determinant tilt therefore size-biases the scalar standard normal coordinate `Z=g^Tn` by `Z^2`. The squared coordinate changes from `chi_1^2` to `chi_3^2`. Since `Q_G=m/Z^2`, (6.4) follows.

No inverse-Wishart theorem is imported.

From (6.2),

\[
P\{Q_G>L\}
=P\left\{\chi_1^2<\frac mL\right\}
\sim
\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\left(\frac mL\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

From (6.4),

\[
P_{\rm vol}\{Q_G>L\}
\sim
\frac{\sqrt2}{3\sqrt\pi}

\tag{6.7}
\]

\[

\iff\alpha<1/2,
\tag{6.8}

\[
E_{\rm vol}Q_G^\alpha<\infty

\tag{6.9}

\[

\qquad
E_{\rm vol}Q_G=m.
\tag{6.10}
