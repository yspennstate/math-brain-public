# 4. New exact fixed-norm coefficient-sphere benchmark

## `P20-COEFFICIENT-SPHERE-BENCHMARK-46` — PROVED

Let `sigma_m` be the isotropic coefficient-spherical law on `R^m`:

`Y=sqrt(m) U`, `U ~ Haar(S^{m-1})`.

This is the natural comparator for a pure harmonic row because both laws are centered, isotropic, and have the exact deterministic norm `sqrt(m)`.

## 4.1 Residual volume planes are exactly Haar

Take any number `k<m` of independent coefficient-spherical rows and volume-bias their product law by the Gram determinant.

Both the product law and the Gram determinant are invariant under a common right action of `O(m)`. Hence the residual orthogonal complement, of dimension `s=m-k`, has an `O(m)`-invariant law on `Gr(s,m)`. The unique invariant probability law is Haar.

Therefore every residual plane `P_s^sph` in the coefficient-spherical volume chain is **exactly Haar**, not merely asymptotically Haar.

## 4.2 Exact selected pivot law

Fix any deterministic rank-`s` coefficient projection `P`, `1<=s<m`. For an unselected coefficient-spherical row,

`L=||P Y||^2`

has the exact law

`L = m B`,

where

`B ~ Beta(s/2,(m-s)/2)`.

The Christoffel/volume selection density is `L/s`. Size-biasing a beta variable by `B` increments its first parameter by one. Therefore the selected pivot is exactly

`L_s^sph = m B_s^*`,

`B_s^* ~ Beta((s+2)/2,(m-s)/2)`.                              (4.1)

Consequently

`E L_s^sph = m(s+2)/(m+2)`,                                   (4.2)

and the general selected-pivot identity gives

`E (L_s^sph)^(-1)=1/s`.                                       (4.3)

At residual rank two,

`L_2^sph ~ m Beta(2,(m-2)/2)`,

`E L_2^sph = 4m/(m+2) = 4-8/(m+2)`,                           (4.4)

`E (L_2^sph)^(-1)=1/2`.                                       (4.5)

Since `m=Theta(d^2)`, the exact finite-`m` mean correction in (4.4) is `O(d^-2)`, one full power of `d` below the live harmonic angular target `O(d^-1)`.

Thus

`E L_2^harm = 4+O(1/d)`

is equivalent to

`E L_2^harm - E L_2^sph = O(1/d)`.                             (4.6)

The same equivalence holds after the `C(log d)^2` truncation of the preceding P20 run.

## 4.3 Coefficient-spherical square hard edge is Wishart

Let `S_m` be square `m x m` with iid rows from `sigma_m`. Let `R_i~chi_m` be iid, independent of `S_m`, and put

`D_m=diag(R_i/sqrt(m))`.

Then

`K_m=D_m S_m`

has iid standard Gaussian entries.

Chi concentration and a union bound over `m` rows give

`delta_m=max_i |R_i/sqrt(m)-1| -> 0`

in probability. Hence, on `delta_m<1`,

`(1-delta_m) s_min(S_m) <= s_min(K_m) <= (1+delta_m) s_min(S_m)`.

Therefore

`m s_min(S_m)^2 / [m s_min(K_m)^2] -> 1`

in probability under this coupling.

Since the square Gaussian hard edge has the continuous limiting CDF `F_R`, Slutsky gives

`m s_min(S_m)^2 => F_R`.                                      (4.7)

The same radial sandwich works for `m+r` rows and `m` columns for every fixed surplus `r`: all singular values of the coefficient-spherical block are multiplicatively equivalent to those of the Gaussian rectangular block.

### Strategic consequence

Fixed row norm itself is **not** a mechanism for a different hard-edge law. Any harmonic deviation from Wishart must come from the special low-dimensional physical orbit inside the coefficient sphere, from its interaction with the scalar column, or from a downstream ordinary-law/secular failure.

# 5. New Gaussian-block theorem: an arbitrary independent first column is harmless if its norm is correct

## `P20-GAUSSIAN-BLOCK-ARBITRARY-COLUMN-46` — PROVED

Let `n>=2`. Let

`K_n in R^{n x (n-1)}`

have iid standard Gaussian entries. Let `v_n in R^n` be any random vector independent of `K_n` such that

`||v_n||/sqrt(n) -> 1`

in probability. No coordinate independence, centering, density, or entrywise Gaussian approximation is assumed for `v_n`.

Then

`n s_min([v_n,K_n])^2`

has the same real square hard-edge limit as an iid Gaussian `n x n` matrix.

## 5.1 Proof

Condition on `v_n`. By left orthogonal invariance of `K_n`, the conditional singular-value law of `[v_n,K_n]` depends on `v_n` only through

`r_n=||v_n||`.

It is therefore the same as the singular-value law of

`A(r_n)=[r_n e_1,K_n]`.

Define the deterministic-radius reference

`A_0=[sqrt(n) e_1,K_n]`.

Then

`A(r)=A_0 diag(r/sqrt(n),1,...,1)`.

Hence deterministically

`min(1,r/sqrt(n)) s_min(A_0)`

`<= s_min(A(r))`

`<= max(1,r/sqrt(n)) s_min(A_0)`.                              (5.1)

Now let `g_n~N(0,I_n)` be independent of `K_n`, and write `R_n=||g_n||`. Conditional on `R_n`, left rotational invariance gives the same singular-value law for the iid Gaussian square matrix `[g_n,K_n]` as for `A(R_n)`.

Since

`R_n/sqrt(n)->1`,

(5.1) shows that both

`n s_min(A(R_n))^2`

and

`n s_min(A(r_n))^2`

are multiplicatively `1+o_p(1)` relative to the common reference

`n s_min(A_0)^2`.

The first has the square real-Wishart hard-edge limit, so the reference is tight and has the same limit. The second therefore has that same limit.

This proves the theorem.

## 5.2 Application to the actual radial source column

For the exact source `b` in Section 2,

`||b||^2/p -> 1`

in probability. It is exactly independent of a Gaussian replacement block `K in R^{p x (p-1)}`.

Therefore

`p s_min([b,K])^2 => F_R`.                                    (5.2)

### Strategic consequence

If one succeeds in replacing the pure harmonic block `H` by an independent Gaussian block `K` at the `p^-2` hard edge **while keeping the exact source column `b`**, no separate theorem is needed to Gaussianize `b` coordinatewise. Its Euclidean norm is enough.

Thus the actual scalar source distribution is not an intrinsic different-law mechanism once the traceless block is fully Gaussianized.

This simplifies one branch of the dependency graph and is stronger than fixed-dimensional projection CLTs for `b`.

# 7. New exact rank-three localization of the capped determinant observable

## `P20-CAPPED-RANK3-LOCALIZATION-46` — PROVED

This theorem sharpens the preceding capped cofactor representation.

Let `nu` be any isotropic coefficient-row law in `R^m` for which the indicated Gram determinants are nonzero almost surely. The harmonic, Gaussian and coefficient-spherical laws all satisfy the needed nondegeneracy.

Take independent rows

`Y_1,...,Y_{m-3}, y, x ~ nu`.

Let

`D_- = det Gram(Y_1,...,Y_{m-3})`.

Let `P` be the rank-three orthogonal projection onto the complement of their span. Put

`a=P y`, `c=P x`,

`A=||a||^2`, `B=<a,c>`, `C=||c||^2`.

After appending `y`, let

`P_y=P-a a^T/A`

on the full-measure set `A>0`, and define

`R=||P_y x||^2=C-B^2/A`.

Then the two last Gram determinants factor exactly as

`D_0=D_{m-2}=D_- A`,

`D_1=D_{m-1}=D_- A R`.                                        (7.1)

Therefore the capped coupled integrand from P20-45 satisfies

`min(D_1^2/D_0, T D_1)`

`=D_- A R (R ∧ T)`.                                            (7.2)

For independent isotropic rows,

`E D_-=(m)_{m-3}`

and

`(m)_{m-1}=6 (m)_{m-3}`.

Let `Q_3^nu` denote the exact rank-three residual-plane law obtained by volume-biasing the first `m-3` rows with density `D_-/(m)_{m-3}`. Then the normalized capped target is

`E_vol^nu(L_2 ∧ T)`

`=(1/6) E_{P~Q_3^nu} E_{y,x~nu}`

`[ A R (R ∧ T) ]`.                                             (7.3)

Define the local kernel

`Psi_T^nu(P)=(1/6) E_{y,x~nu}[A R(R∧T)]`.                      (7.4)

Because `nu` is isotropic,

`E_y A=3`

and, after `y` is frozen,

`E_x R=2`.

Thus

`(1/6) E A R=1`,

and consequently

`0 <= Psi_T^nu(P) <= T`                                        (7.5)

for every deterministic rank-three plane.

### Interpretation

Equation (7.3) is precisely two sequential exact DPP/volume selections:

1. select `y` from `P` with density `A/3`;
2. delete its direction and select `x` from the surviving rank-two plane with density `R/2`;
3. record the final selected rank-two pivot `R∧T`.

So the high-degree determinant functional has an exact **rank-three, two-fresh-row localization**. A one-row replacement does not fundamentally require controlling derivatives of an `m`-degree determinant. The true microscopic debt is comparison of the three-dimensional projected fresh-row law under the adaptive mixed residual `Q_3`.

## 7.1 Exact mixed-row one-step formula

The same proof allows the first `m-3` frozen rows to have arbitrary independent isotropic laws, not necessarily identical, while `y` and `x` have specified isotropic laws `nu_y,nu_x`.

The universal normalizer remains `(m)_{m-3}` because sequential determinant expectation uses only covariance identity at each fresh row.

Hence, in a rowwise interpolation, replacing one base row `y` from `nu_0` to `nu_1`, while freezing all other row laws, changes the normalized capped functional by exactly

`(1/6) E_{P~Q_3^mix}`

`[ E_{y~nu_1,x}[A R(R∧T)] - E_{y~nu_0,x}[A R(R∧T)] ]`.       (7.6)

There is no hidden large determinant derivative in (7.6).

### Scale warning

# 8. New exact no-go for absolute direct Gaussian row replacement
