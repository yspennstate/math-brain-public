## 2. Exact feature-space representation

Let
\[
\mathcal H_d=\operatorname{Sym}_d
\]
with Frobenius inner product. Use the orthonormal basis
\[
E_{aa},
\qquad
F_{ab}=\frac{E_{ab}+E_{ba}}{\sqrt2},
\quad a<b.
\]
Then
\[
Y(x)=\frac{xx^\top-I_d}{\sqrt2}
\]
has coordinates exactly
\[
\left(\frac{x_a^2-1}{\sqrt2}\right)_a
\oplus
(x_ax_b)_{a<b}.
\]
Thus the user's row \(z(x)\) is simply the coordinate vector of \(Y(x)\).

For \(A\in\operatorname{Sym}_d\), define
\[
q_A(x)=\langle A,Y(x)\rangle_F
=
\frac{x^\top Ax-\operatorname{Tr}A}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Gaussian Wick calculus gives
\[
\mathbb E q_Aq_B=\operatorname{Tr}(AB).
\]
Hence \(Y\) is centered isotropic in the \(p\)-dimensional Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_d\).

The fourth cumulant follows from
\[
\log\mathbb E
\exp\!\left(
\sum_j t_jq_{A_j}
\right)
=
\sum_{\ell\ge2}
\frac{2^{\ell/2-1}}{\ell}
\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sum_jt_jA_j\right)^\ell.
\]
For symmetric \(A,B\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E q_A^2q_B^2
=
\operatorname{Tr}(A^2)\operatorname{Tr}(B^2)
+
2\operatorname{Tr}(AB)^2
+
8\operatorname{Tr}(A^2B^2)
+
4\operatorname{Tr}(ABAB).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
In particular, if \(\|A\|_F=1\),
\[
\mathbb E q_A^4
=
3+12\operatorname{Tr}(A^4)
\le15.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equation (2.1) is the exact fourth-tensor identity used below.

## 3. P20-L16(a): arbitrary-subspace projected-energy variance

Let
\[
E\subset\mathcal H_d,
\qquad
\dim E=k,
\]
and choose a Frobenius-orthonormal basis
\[
A_1,\dots,A_k.
\]
Put
\[
S_E=\|P_EY\|_F^2
=
\sum_{\alpha=1}^kq_{A_\alpha}^2.
\]
By isotropy,
\[
\mathbb ES_E=k.
\]

Define
\[
C_E=\sum_{\alpha=1}^kA_\alpha^2.
\]
Summing (2.1) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}S_E
=
2k
+
8\operatorname{Tr}(C_E^2)
+
4\sum_{\alpha,\beta}
\operatorname{Tr}
(A_\alpha A_\beta A_\alpha A_\beta).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Since
\[
|\operatorname{Tr}(ABAB)|
\le
\|AB\|_F^2
=
\operatorname{Tr}(A^2B^2),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}S_E
\le
2k+12\operatorname{Tr}(C_E^2).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Complete \(A_1,\dots,A_k\) to an orthonormal basis of all
\(\operatorname{Sym}_d\). For the standard symmetric-matrix basis,
\[
\sum_{\gamma=1}^{p}A_\gamma^2
=
\frac{d+1}{2}I_d.
\]
Every omitted square is positive semidefinite, so
\[
0\preceq C_E\preceq\frac{d+1}{2}I_d.
\]
Also
\[
\operatorname{Tr}C_E
=
\sum_{\alpha=1}^k\operatorname{Tr}(A_\alpha^2)
=k.
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(C_E^2)
\le
\|C_E\|_{\rm op}\operatorname{Tr}C_E
\le
\frac{d+1}{2}k.
\]
Substituting into (3.2),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}S_E
\le
(6d+8)k.
}
\tag{P20-L16a}
\]

This bound is uniform over **every orientation** of \(E\). It preserves the nonlinear coordinate dependence exactly.

## 4. P20-L16(b): one-step angular covariance lower bound

Define
\[
U_E=\frac{P_EY}{\|P_EY\|_F}.
\]
For \(k\ge1\), the denominator is nonzero almost surely: if
\(A\in E\setminus\{0\}\), then \(q_A(x)\) is a nonzero polynomial, so its zero set has Gaussian measure zero.

Fix a Frobenius-unit \(A\in E\). Put
\[
Z=q_A^2,
\qquad
S=S_E.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb EZ=1.
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
1=(\mathbb EZ)^2
=
\left(
\mathbb E
\sqrt{\frac ZS}\sqrt{ZS}
\right)^2
\le
\mathbb E\frac ZS
\cdot
\mathbb E[ZS].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose the orthonormal basis of \(E\) with \(A_1=A\). Using (2.1),
\[
\mathbb E[ZS]
=
k+2
+
8\operatorname{Tr}(A^2C_E)
+
4\sum_{\beta=1}^k
\operatorname{Tr}(AA_\beta AA_\beta).
\tag{4.2}
\]
The last sum in absolute value is bounded by
\[
\sum_\beta\operatorname{Tr}(A^2A_\beta^2)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(A^2C_E).
\]
Since
\[
C_E\preceq\frac{d+1}{2}I,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}A^2=1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\mathbb E[ZS]
\le
k+6d+8.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Combining (4.1)–(4.3),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\langle U_E,A\rangle_F^2
=
\mathbb E\frac ZS
\ge
\frac1{k+6d+8}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Since this holds for every unit \(A\in E\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[U_E\otimes U_E]
\succeq
\frac1{k+6d+8}P_E.
}
\tag{P20-L16b}
\]

This is the exact analogue of the one-step random-hyperplane smoothing estimate needed for a sequential argument, but with an unavoidable \(O(d)\) denominator defect in the uniform theorem.

## 5. P20-L17: sequential null-line smoothing

Let
\[
Y_1,\dots,Y_{p-1}
\]
be iid copies of \(Y\). Generic rank is full almost surely: the relevant minors are nonzero polynomials of the underlying Gaussian coordinates and there are explicit deterministic point configurations whose feature matrices span \(\operatorname{Sym}_d\).

Start with
\[
E_p=\mathcal H_d.
\]
Reveal rows sequentially and set
\[
E_{k-1}
=
E_k\cap Y^\perp
\]
when the current dimension is \(k\). Let \(P_k\) be the orthogonal projector onto \(E_k\). If
\[
U_k=\frac{P_kY}{\|P_kY\|},
\]
then pathwise
\[
P_{k-1}=P_k-U_k\otimes U_k.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Conditional on the past, \(E_k\) is fixed and the new row is fresh. P20-L16(b) gives
\[
\mathbb E[U_k\otimes U_k\mid E_k]
\succeq
\frac1{k+c_d}P_k,
\qquad
c_d=6d+8.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E[P_{k-1}\mid E_k]
\preceq
\left(1-\frac1{k+c_d}\right)P_k.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Iterating from \(p\) down to \(1\),
\[
\mathbb EP_1
\preceq
\prod_{k=2}^{p}
\left(1-\frac1{k+c_d}\right)I.
\]
The product telescopes:
\[
\prod_{k=2}^{p}
\frac{k+c_d-1}{k+c_d}
=
\frac{c_d+1}{p+c_d}.
\]
The final \(P_1\) is the right-null projector
\[
P_1=N\otimes N.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[N\otimes N]
\preceq
\frac{6d+9}{p+6d+8}
I_{\mathcal H_d}.
}
\tag{P20-L17}
\]
Since
\[
p=\frac{d(d+1)}2,
\]
the coefficient is asymptotic to \(12/d\).

This is an ordinary-law fixed-direction delocalization theorem. It does **not** use determinant weighting and does **not** assume the coordinates of \(Y\) are independent.

## 6. P20-L18: representation-theoretic sharpening

The row law obeys
\[
Y(gx)=gY(x)g^\top,
\qquad g\in O(d).
\]
Hence the null-projector law is conjugation invariant.

The real \(O(d)\)-representation
\[
\operatorname{Sym}_d
=
\mathbb RI_d
\oplus
\operatorname{Sym}_d^0
\]
splits into the one-dimensional scalar representation and the irreducible traceless-symmetric representation.

Let
\[
P_{\rm tr}
\]
be projection onto \(\mathbb RI_d\), and
\[
P_{\rm tl}=I-P_{\rm tr}.
\]
Schur's lemma gives
\[
\mathbb E[N\otimes N]
=
a_dP_{\rm tr}+b_dP_{\rm tl}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Because \(\|N\|_F=1\),
\[
a_d+(p-1)b_d=1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
P20-L17 implies
\[
0\le a_d\le
\frac{6d+9}{p+6d+8}
=
O(d^{-1}).
\tag{6.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
b_d
=
\frac{1-a_d}{p-1}
=
\frac{1+O(d^{-1})}{p-1}.
}
\tag{P20-L18}
\]

Consequences:

For every deterministic traceless Frobenius-unit \(B\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\langle N,B\rangle_F^2
=
\frac{1+O(d^{-1})}{p-1}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For \(v\in S^{d-1}\),
\[
vv^\top
=
\frac1dI
+
\left(vv^\top-\frac1dI\right),
\]
with traceless-part Frobenius norm squared \(1-1/d\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E(v^\top Nv)^2
=
\frac{a_d}{d}
+
b_d\left(1-\frac1d\right)
=
O(d^{-2}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus every **fixed rank-one quadratic direction** already has the correct \(p^{-1}\) second-moment scale.

This also shows precisely why second moments cannot solve `NEXT-A`: a conjugation-invariant rank-one-like random matrix can have the same fixed-direction second-order scale after averaging over its Haar eigenbasis while still having operator norm of order one. What remains is a high-moment/eigenvalue-profile theorem.

## 7. P20-OBS-04: deterministic star-subspace obstruction

A natural hope after P20-L16 is to strengthen it to
\[
\mathbb E[U_E\otimes U_E]
\asymp
\frac1kP_E
\]
uniformly over all \(E\). That is false.

Take
\[
E_\star
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
E_{11},
\frac{E_{1j}+E_{j1}}{\sqrt2}:2\le j\le d
\right\},
\qquad
\dim E_\star=d.
\]
Write
\[
a=x_1^2,
\qquad
T=\sum_{j=2}^dx_j^2.
\]
Then
\[
S_\star
=
\|P_{E_\star}Y\|_F^2
=
\frac{(a-1)^2}{2}+aT.
\tag{7.1}
\]
A direct chi-square moment calculation gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}S_\star
=
2d^2+10d+2.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus the \(O(dk)\) general variance scale in P20-L16(a) is sharp in order when \(k=d\); a uniform \(O(k)\) bound is impossible.

There is also a direct angular obstruction. In the \(E_{11}\) direction,
\[
\langle U_{E_\star},E_{11}\rangle_F^2
=
\frac{(a-1)^2/2}
     {(a-1)^2/2+aT}.
\]
On the event
\[
a\le\frac1{16d},
\qquad
T\le2d,
\]
this ratio is bounded below by an absolute constant. The two conditions are independent,
\[
\Pr\left(a\le\frac1{16d}\right)
\asymp d^{-1/2},
\qquad
\Pr(T\le2d)\ge\frac12.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\langle U_{E_\star},E_{11}\rangle_F^2
\ge
\frac{c}{\sqrt d}.
}
\tag{P20-OBS-04}
\]
But isotropy at dimension \(k=d\) would give \(1/d\).

Hence
\[
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
\mathbb E[U_{E_\star}\otimes U_{E_\star}]
\right)
\gtrsim d^{-1/2},
\]
a factor \(\sqrt d\) larger than \(1/k\).

**Conclusion.** A proof of full normal-matrix operator delocalization cannot rely on a deterministic uniform two-sided angular-isotropy statement for every intermediate subspace. It must exploit that the **actual evolving subspaces are random and atypical of this star obstruction**, or use a different hard-edge mechanism altogether.

This is the sharpest failed-route diagnosis of this run.

## 10. Literature audit

The newest relevant anisotropic theorem remains Ma–Misiakiewicz. Their assumptions are broad enough to include strongly nonlinear dependent coordinates and deep random features, but the theorem is on regular spectral domains and is not a microscopic aspect-one hard-edge result. It cannot be cited as H1/H2.

Their regime has independent entries and aspect ratio \(>1\), so it is not directly applicable. It does reinforce the adversarial point: bottom-vector delocalization is a theorem-sized input, not a consequence of bulk MP behavior.

I found no primary source establishing hard-edge universality for a square Veronese/quadratic-feature matrix whose rows lie on a \(d\)-dimensional nonlinear manifold inside ambient dimension \(p\asymp d^2\).
