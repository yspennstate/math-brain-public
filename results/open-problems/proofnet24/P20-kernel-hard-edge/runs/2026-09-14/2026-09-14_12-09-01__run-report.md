## New result 1 — exact product-form reduction

Choose an orthonormal Frobenius basis of Sym_d with
\[
E_0=I/\sqrt d,
\]
and \(E_1,\dots,E_m\) traceless, where \(m=p-1\). Write \(x=ru\), with \(u\) uniform on \(S^{d-1}\) and \(r^2\sim\chi_d^2\), independent. Define
\[
c(r)=\frac{r^2-d}{\sqrt{2d}},
\qquad h_j(u)=\sqrt{\frac{d(d+2)}2}\,u^\top E_j u,
\qquad a(r)=\frac{r^2}{\sqrt{d(d+2)}}.
\]
After an orthogonal change of feature coordinates, each row is exactly
\[
(c(r),a(r)h(u)).
\]
Hence, with \(H\in\mathbb R^{p\times m}\) having rows \(h(u_i)\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_i)\), and \(b=D^{-1}c\),
\[
ZO=D[b,H].
\]
Because radii and directions are independent, **b is exactly independent of H**.

The harmonic row law is exactly centered, isotropic and fixed norm:
\[
\mathbb E h(u)=0,\qquad \mathbb E[h(u)h(u)^\top]=I_m,
\qquad \|h(u)\|^2=m=p-1.
\]
The covariance identity follows from
\[
\mathbb E[(u^\top A u)(u^\top B u)]=\frac{2\operatorname{Tr}(AB)}{d(d+2)}
\]
for traceless symmetric \(A,B\); the norm identity follows from the addition theorem / the constant Frobenius norm of \(uu^\top-I/d\).

For the scalar column,
\[
b=\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{2}}\frac{r^2-d}{r^2}.
\]
For \(d>4\), direct inverse-chi-square moments give
\[
\mu_d:=\mathbb Eb=-\frac{\sqrt{2(d+2)}}{d-2},
\]
\[
\mathbb E b^2=\frac{(d+2)(d+4)}{(d-2)(d-4)},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}(b)=\frac{d^2(d+2)}{(d-4)(d-2)^2}=1+\frac{10}{d}+O(d^{-2}).
\]
Also \(b\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).

Since \(p\asymp d^2\), chi-square concentration plus a union bound yields
\[
\max_{i\le p}|a_i-1|=O_p\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right).
\]
Therefore, if \(\widetilde Z=[b,H]\),
\[
\frac{s_{\min}(Z)}{s_{\min}(\widetilde Z)}\to1
\]
in probability by the min/max singular-value bounds for left multiplication by D. Thus Target 4 is asymptotically equivalent to the hard edge of the cleaner matrix **[b,H]**, where b is independent of H. This avoids an additive operator-perturbation argument entirely.

## New result 2 — exact secular equation

Take an SVD \(H=U\Sigma V^\top\), with nonzero singular values \(s_1\le\cdots\le s_m\), and extend U by its unit left-null vector n. Write
\[
\xi_0=n^\top b,\qquad \xi_j=u_j^\top b.
\]
In this basis,
\[
\widetilde Z\widetilde Z^\top
=\operatorname{diag}(0,s_1^2,\dots,s_m^2)+\xi\xi^\top.
\]
Hence the smallest eigenvalue \(\lambda\in(0,s_1^2)\) satisfies exactly
\[
1-\frac{\xi_0^2}{\lambda}+\sum_{j=1}^m\frac{\xi_j^2}{s_j^2-\lambda}=0,
\]
or
\[
\lambda=\frac{\xi_0^2}{1+\sum_{j=1}^m\xi_j^2/(s_j^2-\lambda)}.
\]
This gives a precise adversarial check on P04: a Gaussian-looking final normal distance \(\xi_0\) cannot by itself imply the Wishart hard edge. The full near-zero spectrum of H and the projections of b onto the bottom left singular vectors enter the law.

A useful exact corollary is
\[
|\det[b,H]|=|n^\top b|\prod_{j=1}^m s_j(H),
\]
but determinant control alone is insufficient for the minimum singular value.

## New result 3 — leave-one-out normal is explicitly an inverse-vector problem

For \(p-1=m\) rows, the corresponding traceless matrix \(H_-\) is square \(m\times m\) and generically invertible. A normalized right-null vector of the full trace+traceless feature matrix, written \((\alpha,v)\), obeys
\[
\alpha b_-+H_-v=0,
\]
so
\[
v=-\alpha H_-^{-1}b_-,
\qquad
\alpha^2=\frac{1}{1+\|H_-^{-1}b_-\|^2}.
\]
Thus the trace weight of the leave-one-out normal is governed exactly by the critical inverse vector \(H_-^{-1}b_-\). This converts P04's qualitative circularity warning into an algebraic identity.

## New result 4 — quantitative conditional Gaussianity of a fresh quadratic form

For any symmetric A, independent of a fresh Gaussian x, with \(\|A\|_F=1\), diagonalize A with eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\). Then
\[
q_A=\frac{x^\top A x-\operatorname{Tr}A}{\sqrt2}
=\sum_j\lambda_j\frac{g_j^2-1}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Berry–Esseen therefore gives
\[
\sup_t|\Pr(q_A\le t\mid A)-\Phi(t)|
\le C\sum_j|\lambda_j|^3
\le C\|A\|_{\rm op},
\]
and also a bound by \(C\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(A^4)}\). Hence P04's desired operator-norm delocalization is sufficient for one-dimensional conditional Gaussianity.

But this condition is substantive. For the traceless rank-two example
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2,0,\ldots),
\]
we get
\[
q_A=(g_1^2-g_2^2)/2=UV
\]
with U,V independent standard Gaussians. Its density has a logarithmic singularity at zero. Therefore spiky/low-rank normal matrices can produce dramatically non-Gaussian small-ball behavior even though the row covariance is isotropic.

## New result 5 — radial column projections are easier than expected

Let \(\beta_d=(b-\mu_d)/\sigma_d\). Then \(\beta_d\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), its third moments are uniformly bounded, and \(W_1(\beta_d,N)\to0\). For any unit vector \(v\in\mathbb R^p\), split its coordinates at a threshold eta. Large coordinates number at most eta^{-2}; couple those scalar entries to Gaussians. On the small coordinates, weighted Berry–Esseen gives an error bounded by \(C\sum|v_i|^3\le C\eta\). If \(\delta_d=W_1(\beta_d,N)\), choosing \(\eta=\sqrt{\delta_d}\) gives
\[
\sup_{\|v\|=1}W_1(v^\top\beta,N(0,1))\to0.
\]
Thus **no coordinate delocalization of v is needed** for centered one-dimensional projections of the radial column. For uncentered b the residual mean is
\[
\mu_d\,v^\top\mathbf1,
\]
with \(\mu_d=O(d^{-1/2})\). It would therefore suffice, for the relevant bottom left singular vectors, to prove \(v^\top\mathbf1=o_p(\sqrt d)\); a full Haar-coordinate theorem is stronger than necessary.

## Benchmark law

For a square real Gaussian matrix with entries normalized at variance 1/p, the classical least-singular-value hard edge gives
\[
\Pr(p\,s_{\min}(H)^2\le t)\to 1-\exp(-\sqrt t-t/2).
\]
Equivalently this is the target CDF for \(p^2\lambda_{\min}(W_p)\). Independent-entry universality theorems establish this benchmark in broad product-entry classes, but their hypotheses do not automatically include the strongly dependent Veronese/harmonic row law here.

## Literature audit

- Che–Lopatto, *Universality of the least singular value for sparse random matrices* (arXiv:1711.00580): supplies the real square benchmark / independent-entry universality framework; not directly verified for these dependent harmonic rows.
- Nguyen–Vu, *Normal Vector of a Random Hyperplane* (IMRN 2018): iid-coordinate normal-vector delocalization; its product-coordinate hypotheses do not directly cover the degree-2 feature row.
- Fernandez V.–Livshyts–Mui, *On the Smallest Singular Value of Log-Concave Random Matrices* (arXiv:2508.17745): not directly applicable because a degree-2 harmonic row is supported on a low-dimensional orbit/manifold in ambient dimension about d^2/2.
- Brailovskaya–van Handel, *Universality and Sharp Matrix Concentration Inequalities* (GAFA 2024): useful broad comparison/concentration perspective, but no checked p^{-2} Bessel hard-edge theorem for this model.
- Hu–Hu–Bai, *Local Laws and Edge Universality for Noncentral Sample Covariance Matrices* (arXiv:2608.27307, Aug. 27 2026): regular right-edge universality under independent-entry assumptions; not this hard edge.

## Finite numerical diagnostics — evidence only

Direct simulations of \(X_d=p\,s_{\min}(Z)^2\) at modest d show a heavier lower tail than the limiting Wishart benchmark but movement in its direction as d increases; sample sizes are much too small for a limiting conclusion. Leave-one-out normal matrices numerically show \(\sqrt d\|A\|_{op}\) roughly around 2 and \(d\operatorname{Tr}(A^4)\) roughly around 2.4 over moderate dimensions, consistent with Frobenius-delocalized eigenvalues. These are diagnostics only, not proof.
