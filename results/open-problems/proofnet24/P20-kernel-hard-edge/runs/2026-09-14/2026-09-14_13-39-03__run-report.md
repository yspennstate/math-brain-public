\[
h_j(u)=\sqrt{\frac{d(d+2)}2}\,u^\top E_j u,

a(r)=\frac{r^2}{\sqrt{d(d+2)}},
\qquad
c(r)=\frac{r^2-d}{\sqrt{2d}},
\qquad
b(r)=\frac{c(r)}{a(r)}

\[
\boxed{ZO=D[b,H],\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(a_i),}
\tag{2.1}
\]
where the rows of \(H\) are \(h(u_i)\), and \(b\) is independent of \(H\).
Moreover
\[
\mathbb Eh=0,\qquad
\mathbb Ehh^\top=I_m,\qquad
\|h\|_2^2=m,
\tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
\max_{i\le p}|a_i-1|
=O_{\mathbb P}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]
Thus
\[
p\,s_{\min}(Z)^2
=
(1+o_{\mathbb P}(1))\,p\,s_{\min}([b,H])^2.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The radial mean is
\[
\mu_d=\mathbb Eb
=-\frac{\sqrt{2(d+2)}}{d-2}

## 3. New theorem P20-L13: density-level Gaussianity of diffuse quadratic normals

### Statement

Let \(A=A^\top\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\) satisfy
\[
\|A\|_F=1,
\qquad
\delta:=\|A\|_{\rm op}\le\delta_0
\]
for a sufficiently small universal \(\delta_0\). For \(x\sim N(0,I_d)\), set
\[
q_A=\frac{x^\top A x-\operatorname{Tr}A}{\sqrt2}.
\]
Let \(f_A\) be the density of \(q_A\) and \(\varphi\) the standard Gaussian density. Then
\[
\boxed{
\|f_A-\varphi\|_\infty\le C\delta
}
\tag{P20-L13}
\]
with a universal \(C\). Consequently, for every interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\),
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\Pr\{q_A\in I\}-\Pr\{g\in I\}
\right|
\le C\delta\,|I|.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
In particular, uniformly in \(y\in\mathbb R\) and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\Pr\{|q_A-y|\le\varepsilon\}
=
\int_{y-\varepsilon}^{y+\varepsilon}\varphi(t)\,dt
+O(\delta\varepsilon).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is substantially stronger, for hard-edge purposes, than the previous P20-L5 Kolmogorov estimate
\[
d_K(q_A,N(0,1))\le C\delta.
\]
The latter is useless when \(\varepsilon\ll\delta\); (3.2) remains informative at arbitrarily microscopic \(\varepsilon\).

### Proof

Diagonalize \(A\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\). Since
\[
\sum_j\lambda_j^2=1,\qquad
\max_j|\lambda_j|=\delta,
\]
we have
\[
q_A
=
\sum_j\frac{\lambda_j}{\sqrt2}(g_j^2-1).
\]
Its characteristic function is
\[
\psi_A(t)
=
\prod_j
e^{-it\lambda_j/\sqrt2}
(1-i\sqrt2\,t\lambda_j)^{-1/2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For \(|t|\delta\le c_0\), expansion of the logarithm gives
\[
\log\psi_A(t)
=
-\frac{t^2}{2}+R_A(t),
\qquad
|R_A(t)|
\le
C\delta |t|^3,
\tag{3.4}
\]
because
\[
\sum_j|\lambda_j|^3
\le
\delta\sum_j\lambda_j^2
=\delta.
\]
On the same range, the exact modulus
\[
|\psi_A(t)|
=
\prod_j(1+2t^2\lambda_j^2)^{-1/4}
\tag{3.5}
\]
and \(\log(1+x)\ge x-x^2/2\) imply
\[
|\psi_A(t)|\le e^{-c t^2}
\tag{3.6}
\]
for a universal \(c>0\).

Take
\[
T=(c_1/\delta)^{1/3}
\]
with \(c_1>0\) small. On \(|t|\le T\), (3.4) has bounded remainder and therefore
\[
|\psi_A(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|
\le
C\delta |t|^3e^{-c t^2}.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Hence
\[
\int_{|t|\le T}
|\psi_A(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\,dt
\le C\delta.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For \(T<|t|\le c_0/\delta\), (3.6) gives an
\(e^{-c\delta^{-2/3}}\) tail.

For the remaining frequencies, concavity of
\(x\mapsto\log(1+2t^2x)\) on \(0\le x\le\delta^2\) gives
\[
\sum_j\log(1+2t^2\lambda_j^2)
\ge
\delta^{-2}\log(1+2\delta^2t^2),
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
|\psi_A(t)|
\le
(1+2\delta^2t^2)^{-1/(4\delta^2)}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
The integral of the right side over \(|t|>c_0/\delta\) is
\(O(\delta^{-1}e^{-c/\delta^2})\). The Gaussian characteristic-function tail is smaller still. Therefore
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
|\psi_A(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\,dt
\le C\delta.
\tag{3.10}
\]
Fourier inversion now gives P20-L13.

### Hard-edge consequence

Let \(A_-\) be the Frobenius-unit symmetric matrix corresponding to a leave-one-out feature-space normal, independent of a fresh Gaussian row. If
\[
\|A_-\|_{\rm op}\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0,
\tag{3.11}
\]
then conditionally on \(A_-\), the fresh row distance has a Gaussian density at **all microscopic interval scales**. In particular, for \(\varepsilon=x/p\),
\[
\Pr\{|q_{A_-}|\le x/p\mid A_-\}
=
\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\frac{x}{p}
+o_{\mathbb P}(p^{-1}).
\tag{3.12}
\]
Thus the conjectured normal-matrix delocalization would not merely give a CLT; it would give the correct \(1/p\) row-distance intensity.

This is directly analogous to the density-level lesson in P12: CDF Berry–Esseen is too weak at a microscopic singular-value scale.

## 4. An explicit mechanism that could change the law if normal matrices stay spiky

P20-L13 also sharpens the adversarial countermechanism.

Take
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(2^{-1/2},-2^{-1/2},0,\ldots,0).
\]
Then
\[
q_A
=\frac{g_1^2-g_2^2}{2}
=UV,
\]
where \(U,V\) are independent standard Gaussians. Its density is
\[
f_A(x)=\frac1\pi K_0(|x|),
\]
so as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{|q_A|\le\varepsilon\}
\sim
\frac{2}{\pi}\,
\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
The Gaussian small-ball probability is only
\(\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\).

Therefore, if a nonvanishing fraction of leave-one-out normal matrices retained rank-\(O(1)\) spectral concentration, the distance-to-span process could have a logarithmically enhanced small-ball intensity. This is a concrete mechanism capable of changing the hard-edge scale/law.

I have **not** proved that such spiky normals occur. Existing finite-dimensional diagnostics point the other way: they suggest \(\|A_-\|_{\rm op}\asymp d^{-1/2}\) and \(d\,\operatorname{Tr}(A_-^4)=O(1)\). Those simulations are evidence only.

## 5. New theorem P20-L14: determinant-volume-biased isotropy of the left null line

Let
\[
H\in\mathbb R^{p\times m},
\qquad m=p-1,
\]
have independent centered isotropic rows in \(\mathbb R^m\); in particular this applies to the degree-two harmonic \(H\). Assume full column rank and let \(n\in S^{p-1}\) span the left nullspace:
\[
H^\top n=0.
\]
Set
\[
D=\det(H^\top H).
\]

### Theorem

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D\,nn^\top]=m!\,I_p.
}
\tag{P20-L14}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb ED=p\,m!=p!,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
and for every deterministic \(a\in\mathbb R^p\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D(a^\top n)^2]
=
m!\,\|a\|_2^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
For \(a=\mathbf1\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E[D(n^\top\mathbf1)^2]}
{\mathbb ED}
=1.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Let \(c\in\mathbb R^p\) be the signed cofactor vector
\[
c_i=(-1)^i\det(H_{-i}),
\]
where \(H_{-i}\) is the square \(m\times m\) matrix obtained by deleting row \(i\). Then
\[
H^\top c=0,
\qquad
D=\sum_i c_i^2
\]
by Cauchy–Binet, hence \(c=\pm\sqrt D\,n\).

For every \(i\), the rows of \(H_{-i}\) are independent centered isotropic vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\). Expanding its determinant,
\[
\mathbb E c_i^2=m!.
\tag{5.4}
\]
For \(i\ne j\), condition on the \(m-1\) rows common to
\(H_{-i}\) and \(H_{-j}\). Then \(c_i\) is a linear functional of the unique row \(h_j\), while \(c_j\) is a linear functional of the independent unique row \(h_i\). Both conditional means vanish because the rows are centered. Hence
\[
\mathbb E[c_i c_j]=0.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[cc^\top]=m!I_p.
\]
Since \(cc^\top=Dnn^\top\), the theorem follows.

### Interpretation and obstruction

The determinant-volume-biased law of the null line has exactly the correct isotropic second moment. In particular, the constant-vector overlap is exactly order one **under \(D\)-bias**.

This does not prove
\[
n^\top\mathbf1=O_{\mathbb P}(1)
\tag{5.6}
\]
under the original law, because \(D\) and \(n\) may be strongly correlated at the hard edge. Removing the \(D\)-bias by a crude negative-moment argument is not viable: for a Gaussian \(p\times(p-1)\) benchmark, Bartlett decomposition of \(D\) contains a \(\chi^2_2\) factor, and
\[
\mathbb E[(\chi^2_2)^{-1}]=\infty.
\]
Thus even the benchmark has the exact rare-event integrability obstruction that invalidates a naive \(D^{-1}\) transfer.

This is useful information: the intercept-overlap problem is not a missing symmetry identity. The correct symmetry identity is already exact; the missing step is hard-edge control of how the null direction correlates with exceptionally small determinant.

## 6. New theorem P20-L15: centered radial secular tails need no eigenvector delocalization

Write
\[
b=\mu_d\mathbf1+\sigma_d\beta,
\qquad
\mathbb E\beta_i=0,\quad
\mathbb E\beta_i^2=1.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(d\), the fourth moments of \(\beta_i\) are uniformly bounded.

Condition on \(H\). Let \(u_j\) be its positive left singular vectors and let
\[
w_j=w_j(\theta)=\frac1{\eta_j-\theta},
\qquad j>r.
\]
Define
\[
S_r
=
\sum_{j>r}
w_j\big((u_j^\top\beta)^2-1\big).
\]

Set
\[
A_r=\sum_{j>r}w_j u_j u_j^\top.
\]
Then
\[
S_r=\beta^\top A_r\beta-\operatorname{Tr}A_r.
\]
For iid centered variance-one coordinates,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\beta^\top A_r\beta\mid H)
=
2\operatorname{Tr}(A_r^2)
+
\kappa_4\sum_i(A_r)_{ii}^2,
\]
where
\[
\kappa_4=\mathbb E\beta_i^4-3.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\beta[|S_r|^2\mid H]
\le
C
\sum_{j>r}\frac1{(\eta_j-\theta)^2}.
}
\tag{P20-L15}
\]

Hence, if the hard-edge spectrum satisfies a Wishart-type inverse-square tail
\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}
\limsup_{d\to\infty}
\Pr\!\left\{
\sum_{j>r}\frac1{(\eta_j-\theta)^2}>\varepsilon
\right\}=0
\tag{6.1}
\]
uniformly on the spectral windows used in the secular-root argument, then the centered radial tail fluctuation is negligible. Its conditional mean is
\[
\sum_{j>r}\frac1{\eta_j-\theta},
\tag{6.2}
\]
so the corresponding inverse-first-power tail must also converge to the Gaussian rectangular hard-edge tail.

This isolates the role of eigenvectors:

- **centered radial column:** no bottom-eigenvector delocalization is needed for the tail;
- **finite bottom block:** P04-T4-JP supplies joint Gaussianity uniformly for every fixed orthonormal block;
- **small radial mean \(\mu_d\mathbf1\):** this is the only place where constant-vector overlaps remain.

For the mean part, a sufficient package is
\[
\mu_d\,n^\top\mathbf1\to_{\mathbb P}0,
\tag{6.3}
\]
\[
\max_{j\le r}|\mu_d\,u_j^\top\mathbf1|
\to_{\mathbb P}0
\quad\text{for every fixed }r,
\tag{6.4}
\]
and the weighted tails
\[
\mu_d^2\sum_{j>r}
\frac{(u_j^\top\mathbf1)^2}{\eta_j-\theta}
\to0,
\qquad
\mu_d^2\sum_{j>r}
\frac{(u_j^\top\mathbf1)^2}{(\eta_j-\theta)^2}
\to0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Since \(\mu_d^2\sim2/d\), these are much weaker than full Haar eigenvector universality, but they are not presently proved at the hard edge.

## 7. Conditional closure theorem / exact dependency graph

The preceding identities reduce Target 4 to the following package for the pure harmonic matrix \(H\).

### H1. Finite hard-edge point-process convergence

For every fixed \(r\),
\[
(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_r)
=
(p s_1(H)^2,\ldots,p s_r(H)^2)
\]
must converge jointly to the first \(r\) points of the real Gaussian
\(p\times(p-1)\) rectangular hard-edge process.

A global MP law or convergence of \(\eta_1\) alone is not enough.

### H2. Inverse-tail integrability

The first- and second-power tails in (6.1)–(6.2) must be uniformly negligible after \(r\to\infty\). Wishart rigidity \(\eta_j\asymp j^2\) would be more than sufficient:
\[
\sum_{j>r}\eta_j^{-1}=O(r^{-1}),
\qquad
\sum_{j>r}\eta_j^{-2}=O(r^{-3}).
\]

### H3. Mean-overlap control

The constant-vector quantities in (6.3)–(6.5) must vanish.

Under H1–H3, P04-T4-JP gives the joint Gaussian limit of the centered projections
\[
(n^\top\beta,u_1^\top\beta,\ldots,u_r^\top\beta)
\]
conditionally and uniformly in the random orthonormal frame. P20-L15 controls the rest of the centered secular series. The exact secular equation (2.8), followed by continuity of its smallest root away from the almost-surely simple limiting poles, then transfers the Gaussian square hard-edge law to \([b,H]\). Finally (2.4) transfers it to \(Z\).

Thus the remaining mathematical bottleneck is now not “radial non-Gaussianity”; it is **hard-edge point-process/inverse-tail universality for \(H\), plus an anisotropic constant-vector estimate for the small mean**.

\[

\[
U^\top\beta\Rightarrow N(0,I_r)
\]
in bounded-Lipschitz distance.

Let \(u_i^\top\) be the rows of \(U\), so
\[
\sum_i\|u_i\|^2=r.
\]
For a threshold \(\eta>0\), split
\[
L=\{i:\|u_i\|>\eta\},
\qquad |L|\le r/\eta^2.
\]
Couple \(\beta_i\) and \(g_i\sim N(0,1)\) optimally in \(W_1\) on \(L\). Then
\[
\mathbb E\left\|
\sum_{i\in L}u_i(\beta_i-g_i)
\right\|
\le
\delta_d\sum_{i\in L}\|u_i\|
\le
\frac{r\delta_d}{\eta}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
On \(L^c\), a fixed-dimensional multivariate Lindeberg/Stein bound, using the uniform third moment of \(\beta_i\), gives
\[
d_{\rm BL}
\left(
\sum_{i\notin L}u_i\beta_i,\,
\sum_{i\notin L}u_i g_i
\right)
\le
C_r\sum_{i\notin L}\|u_i\|^3
\le
C_r r\eta.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Choosing
\[
\eta=\sqrt{\delta_d}
\]
makes both errors vanish. Since \(r\) is fixed, there is no hidden growing-dimensional loss.

The centered radial law has uniformly bounded third moments for large \(d\), so the estimate is valid. The uncentered column adds exactly the deterministic shift
\[
\mu_d U^\top\mathbf1.
\]
Therefore P04-T4-JP is correct, including its stated uniformity.

This audit verifies the radial projection lemma only; it does **not** certify the unresolved harmonic hard edge.

## 9. Literature audit: what current theorems do and do not give

Ma–Misiakiewicz's September 8, 2026 anisotropic local law is genuinely relevant because it allows independent columns with strongly nonlinear dependent coordinates under quadratic-form concentration. However its theorem is stated on **regular spectral domains bounded away from the origin**. This is precisely the regime that excludes Target 4. The hard edge at aspect ratio tending one therefore remains outside that theorem.

Kogan–Huang–Nandy prove extremal spectral-edge convergence for polynomial/tensor kernel matrices and obtain well-conditioning when the sample/feature ratio stays a fixed amount below one. That is a valuable bulk/edge benchmark, but at the critical square ratio the MP lower edge itself is zero. Convergence to the zero edge does not identify the \(p^{-2}\) Gram-eigenvalue scale or the real Bessel/Wishart fixed-\(t\) law.

So there is still no verified primary-source theorem that can simply be imported to close H1–H2.

## 11. Route comparison

### A. Density-level distance-to-span — improved, but not closure

P20-L13 makes the fresh-row small-ball step sharp at microscopic widths once normal-matrix operator delocalization is proved. It eliminates the objection that a Berry–Esseen \(O(\delta)\) CDF error overwhelms a \(1/p\) interval.

It does **not** by itself prove the Bessel hard edge because one-dimensional row distances are strongly coupled and the exact secular equation depends on the whole bottom spectrum.

### B. Determinant/cofactor route — exact identity, wrong unbiasing tool

P20-L14 shows determinant-volume-biased null isotropy exactly. This is stronger than a heuristic symmetry claim.

However negative determinant moments are too singular. The Gaussian benchmark already has \(\mathbb ED^{-1}=\infty\). Therefore “prove a determinant identity and divide by the determinant” is a dead end for ordinary null-vector moments.

### C. Block/Green-function comparison

The recent dependent-coordinate anisotropic local law strongly suggests a comparison framework, but its present spectral domain stays away from zero. A new hard-edge extension would need a stability analysis of the Dyson equation precisely in the singular aspect-one regime and would have to resolve the \(p^{-2}\) eigenvalue scale.

### D. Direct replacement with Gaussian rows

A row-by-row Lindeberg comparison of the hard-edge CDF remains dangerous. Resolvent derivatives at spectral parameters of size \(p^{-2}\) carry powers of the inverse gap, and the quadratic-feature row has only \(d\) underlying Gaussian degrees of freedom for \(p\asymp d^2\) feature coordinates. The total replacement error is not controlled by existing bulk cumulant estimates.

### E. Secular finite-block plus inverse-tail — currently best decomposition

The exact equation (2.8), P04-T4-JP, and P20-L15 cleanly separate:

1. finite bottom hard-edge point process of \(H\);
2. inverse spectral tails;
3. the small deterministic mean's constant-vector overlaps.

This is the narrowest current closure package.

## 13. Exact next attack

The highest-value next lemma is now **normal-matrix delocalization at density level**, not another ordinary CLT:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(A_-^4)=O_{\mathbb P}(1/d)
\quad\text{or at minimum}\quad
\|A_-\|_{\rm op}=o_{\mathbb P}(1),
}
\tag{NEXT-A}
\]
for the Frobenius-unit symmetric matrix \(A_-\) spanning the right null line of \(p-1\) quadratic-feature rows.

P20-L13 would immediately convert (NEXT-A) into the exact microscopic Gaussian small-ball law for a fresh distance. The best constructive routes are:

1. derive the null-line density on \(\operatorname{Sym}_d\) through a Blaschke–Petkantschin/Kac–Rice formula and show it penalizes spiky eigenvalue profiles;
2. use row resampling plus a regularized inverse projector and exploit the \(O(d)\)-irreducible trace/traceless decomposition;
3. attempt a hard-edge extension of the quadratic-form-concentration resolvent method, but only after deriving estimates uniform as the deterministic MP lower edge collapses.

In parallel, for the full fixed-\(t\) law one should prove an H-only statement stronger than (NEXT-A):

\[
\boxed{
\{p\,s_j(H)^2\}_{j\ge1}
\Longrightarrow
\text{real rectangular Wishart hard-edge point process},
}
\tag{NEXT-B}
\]
with inverse-tail uniform integrability. This, plus the already-proved radial comparison and mean-overlap estimates, is the shortest rigorous route to Target 4.

**Current conclusion:** there is no rigorous evidence for a different scale yet, but P20-L13 shows exactly how a persistent spiky-normal component would produce one. The numerical evidence instead favors diffuse normals and therefore remains compatible with the Wishart law. Target 4 remains OPEN.
