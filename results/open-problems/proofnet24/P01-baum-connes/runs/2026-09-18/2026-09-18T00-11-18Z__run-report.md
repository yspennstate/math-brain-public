## Construction

For the lamp involution at \(x\in F_2\), write
\[
p_x=\frac{1+u_{a_x}}2,\qquad q_x=\frac{1-u_{a_x}}2.
\]
For \(k\ge1\), set
\[
M_k=3^{k-1},\qquad t_k=s_1^{M_k},\qquad x_k=s_2^k,
\]
and define the lamp projection
\[
p_k=p_{x_k}q_{t_kx_k}.
\]
Its translate is
\[
r_k=u_{t_k}p_ku_{t_k}^*=p_{t_kx_k}q_{t_k^2x_k}.
\]
The two projections are orthogonal, because \(p_k\) contains the factor \(q_{t_kx_k}\) while \(r_k\) contains \(p_{t_kx_k}\):
\[
p_kr_k=r_kp_k=0.
\]
Let
\[
v_k=u_{t_k}p_k,
\qquad e_k=p_k+r_k,
\qquad j_k=v_k-v_k^*.
\]
Then
\[
v_k^*v_k=p_k,\qquad v_kv_k^*=r_k,
\]
and orthogonality of source and range gives
\[
j_k^*=-j_k,\qquad j_k^2=-e_k,\qquad e_kj_k=j_ke_k=j_k.
\]

Choose the summable angles
\[
\theta_k=6^{-k}.
\]
Define
\[
R_k=(1-e_k)+\cos\theta_k\,e_k+\sin\theta_k\,j_k.
\]
On the corner \(e_k\), the relation \(j_k^2=-e_k\) makes this an exact rotation, while it is the identity on \(1-e_k\). Therefore
\[
R_k^*R_k=R_kR_k^*=1.
\]
Moreover
\[
\|R_k-1\|\le 2\sin(\theta_k/2)\le\theta_k,
\]
so \(\sum_k\|R_k-1\|<\infty\). The ordered products
\[
R^{(N)}=R_1R_2\cdots R_N
\]
are norm Cauchy and converge to a unitary
\[
R=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}R_k\in C_r^*(G).
\]
The same estimate is uniform for the paths \(R_k(t\theta_k)\), \(0\le t\le1\), hence
\[
R(t)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}R_k(t\theta_k)
\]
converges uniformly to a norm-continuous path from \(1\) to \(R\). Thus
\[
\boxed{[R]=0\in K_1(C_r^*(G)).}
\]

The quotient \(q:C_r^*(G)\to C_r^*(F_2)\) killing the lamp group sends every \(q_x\) to zero. Hence
\[
q(p_k)=q(r_k)=q(v_k)=0,\qquad q(R_k)=1,
\]
and by continuity
\[
\boxed{q(R)=1.}
\]

## The limit is genuinely infinite-support

It remains to prove that the construction did not secretly collapse to an algebraic finite-support group-ring unitary.

Let \(\ell_1:G\to\mathbb Z\) be the \(s_1\)-exponent homomorphism and let
\[
\gamma_z(u_g)=z^{\ell_1(g)}u_g,\qquad z\in\mathbb T,
\]
be the corresponding gauge action. Write the \(k\)-th factor as
\[
R_k=a_k+b_k+c_k,
\]
where
\[
a_k=(1-e_k)+\cos\theta_k\,e_k,
\qquad b_k=\sin\theta_k\,v_k,
\qquad c_k=-\sin\theta_k\,v_k^*.
\]
These pieces have gauge degrees \(0,+M_k,-M_k\), respectively.

The frequencies \(M_k=3^{k-1}\) have the balanced-ternary uniqueness property: every finite sum
\[
\sum_k\varepsilon_kM_k,\qquad \varepsilon_k\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
has a unique digit sequence. Let
\[
D_N=\sum_{k=1}^NM_k=\frac{3^N-1}{2}.
\]
For every \(L\ge N\), the only way to obtain gauge degree \(D_N\) in the expansion of \(R^{(L)}\) is to select \(b_k\) for \(k\le N\) and \(a_k\) for \(k>N\). Hence, writing \(E_m\) for the \(m\)-th gauge Fourier coefficient,
\[
E_{D_N}(R^{(L)})=b_1b_2\cdots b_N\,a_{N+1}\cdots a_L.
\]

The tail
\[
A_N=\prod_{k>N}a_k
\]
exists in the abelian lamp algebra and is invertible. Indeed each \(a_k\) has spectrum contained in \(\{1,\cos\theta_k\}\), and
\[
\sum_k(1-\cos\theta_k)<\infty,
\]
so the infinite product of the positive lower bounds \(\prod_{k>N}\cos\theta_k\) is strictly positive.

Also \(v_1\cdots v_N\neq0\). Moving the free shifts to the left merely translates the cylinder projections \(p_k\) by powers of \(s_1\). For a fixed \(k\), every lamp coordinate involved remains in the left coset
\[
\langle s_1\rangle s_2^k.
\]
These cosets are pairwise disjoint for distinct \(k\): if \(s_1^ms_2^k=s_1^ns_2^\ell\) in the free group, reduced normal form forces \(k=\ell\) and \(m=n\). Thus the translated cylinder constraints from different \(k\)'s never contradict each other, so their product is a nonzero projection. Consequently
\[
b_1\cdots b_N\neq0.
\]
Since \(A_N\) is invertible,
\[
E_{D_N}(R)=b_1\cdots b_NA_N\neq0
\]
for every \(N\). Therefore \(R\) has infinitely many nonzero gauge Fourier modes. A finite-support algebraic group-ring element has only finitely many \(\ell_1\)-degrees, so
\[
\boxed{R\notin\mathbb CG.}
\]
This is a genuinely infinite-support scalar unitary obtained as a norm limit of explicit algebraic rotations.

## Move 25: count the charge twice

There is an independent analytic readout of the same coordinates. For \(j=1,2\), let \(\ell_j:G\to\mathbb Z\) be the free exponent-sum homomorphism and let \(\delta_j\) be the generator of the corresponding gauge action,
\[
\delta_j(u_g)=i\ell_j(g)u_g.
\]
With \(\tau\) the canonical trace, define for a unitary in the derivation domain
\[
\nu_j(V)=\frac1i\tau(V^*\delta_j(V)).
\]
For a group unitary \(u_w\), \(\nu_j(u_w)=\ell_j(w)\).

For each \(R_k\), all lamp projections have degree zero and \(t_k=s_1^{M_k}\). Thus \(\delta_2(R_k)=0\). For \(\delta_1\),
\[
\delta_1(j_k)=iM_k(v_k+v_k^*).
\]
Writing \(a_k=(1-e_k)+\cos\theta_k e_k\), direct multiplication gives
\[
R_k^*\delta_1(R_k)
=iM_k\sin\theta_k\bigl(a_k(v_k+v_k^*)-\sin\theta_k\,j_k(v_k+v_k^*)\bigr).
\]
The first term has nonzero gauge degree and trace zero. Since \(v_k^2=(v_k^*)^2=0\),
\[
j_k(v_k+v_k^*)=r_k-p_k,
\]
and \(\tau(r_k)=\tau(p_k)\), so the second trace also vanishes. Therefore
\[
\nu_1(R_k)=\nu_2(R_k)=0.
\]

Moreover
\[
\sum_k M_k|\sin\theta_k|
\le\sum_{k\ge1}3^{k-1}6^{-k}
=\frac13<\infty.
\]
Hence the partial products converge not only in norm but in the graph norm of \(\delta_1\); closedness of the gauge generator puts \(R\) in the derivation domain. The trace pairing is additive under multiplication of unitaries in the domain, so
\[
\nu_j(R)=0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{(\nu_1(U_\infty),\nu_2(U_\infty))=(7,11),}
\]
independently matching the quotient/assembly coordinates.
