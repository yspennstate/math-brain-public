# 3. Primary-source gate for the frozen Austin group

Tim Austin, *Rational group ring elements with kernels having irrational dimension*, Proc. Lond. Math. Soc. 107 (2013), 1424–1448, DOI `10.1112/plms/pdt029`, arXiv `0909.2360`, Theorem 1.1 constructs a parameterized family of left-translation-invariant subgroups

\[
V_I\le \mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}
\]

and groups

\[
\Gamma_I=
(\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}/V_I)\rtimes F_2.
\]

The arXiv text explicitly states that `F_2` acts on the quotient by left-coordinate translation. We freeze the source-defined parameter `I=emptyset`; no new group is invented in this report.

Put

\[
U:=U_{\varnothing}=\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}/V_{\varnothing}.
\]

Then `U` is abelian, so it is amenable, and there is a split exact sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow G
\xrightarrow{\pi}F_2\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
\iota:F_2\hookrightarrow G,
\qquad
\pi\iota=\mathrm{id}_{F_2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

As a consistency check with retained P63, Chabert–Echterhoff, *Permanence properties of the Baum-Connes conjecture*, Doc. Math. 6 (2001), 127–183, DOI `10.4171/DM/100`, state that a group with an amenable normal subgroup inherits Baum–Connes with coefficients from the quotient. Lafforgue, JNCG 6 (2012), 1–197, DOI `10.4171/JNCG/89`, proves Baum–Connes with coefficients for hyperbolic groups, hence for `F_2`. Thus `G` is itself Baum–Connes-positive. This consistency fact is **not** used to fake the explicit class calculation; the representatives and their images are computed below.

# 4. Refutation-first check for the proposed two classes

Before proving rank two, try to kill the proposal in the two places where a shortcut could fail.

## 4.1 Failure mode A: the quotient need not extend to reduced C*-algebras

For an arbitrary group quotient this would be a fatal error and is one of the explicitly killed historical routes.

Here, however, the quotient kernel in `(3.1)` is amenable. The Hulanicki–Reiter weak-containment criterion gives

\[
\lambda_{G/U}\prec\lambda_G.
\]

Identifying `G/U` with `F_2`, this says

\[
\lambda_{F_2}\circ\pi\prec\lambda_G.
\]

Hence for every `a in C G`,

\[
\|\lambda_{F_2}(\pi(a))\|
\le
\|\lambda_G(a)\|,
\]

and therefore `pi` extends continuously to a genuine surjective *-homomorphism

\[
\boxed{
\pi_r:C_r^*(G)\longrightarrow C_r^*(F_2).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

So the forbidden arbitrary-quotient shortcut is **not** being used.

## 4.2 Failure mode B: the two free-group unitary classes might collapse in `K_1(C_r^*(G))`

The semidirect-product section gives a subgroup inclusion `iota:F_2 -> G`. For a subgroup inclusion the reduced norm is preserved: decompose `ell^2(G)` into cosets of `iota(F_2)`; the restriction of the left regular representation to each orbit is a copy of the left regular representation of `F_2`. Thus the group-ring inclusion extends isometrically to

\[
\boxed{
\iota_r:C_r^*(F_2)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(G).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

On the dense group algebra, `(pi_r o iota_r)=id`, hence on the whole reduced algebra

\[
\boxed{
\pi_r\circ\iota_r=\mathrm{id}_{C_r^*(F_2)}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
(\pi_r)_*\circ(\iota_r)_*=\mathrm{id}
\quad\text{on }K_1(C_r^*(F_2)),
\tag{4.4}
\]

so `(iota_r)_*` is split injective. The collapse scenario is therefore rigorously excluded.

# 5. Proof of `P01-P65A-AUSTIN-F2-RETRACT-K1-ASSEMBLY`

We now prove the claimed explicit rank-two assembly package.

## 5.1 `K_1(C_r^*(F_2))` and its canonical basis

Because `F_2` is torsion-free,

\[
K_1^{\mathrm{top}}(F_2)=K_1(BF_2).
\]

A classifying space is the wedge of two circles

\[
BF_2\simeq S^1\vee S^1.
\]

The Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence collapses in dimension one, so

\[
K_1(BF_2)\cong H_1(BF_2;\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `b_j` be the K-homology class of the `j`-th oriented circle.

Lafforgue's hyperbolic-group theorem gives the assembly isomorphism for `F_2`. Naturality with the inclusions

\[
\langle s_j\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow F_2
\]

reduces the image of `b_j` to the standard assembly computation for `Z`: under Fourier transform

\[
C_r^*(\mathbb Z)\cong C(S^1),
\]

and the oriented circle class assembles to the winding-one unitary. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_1^{F_2}(b_j)=[u_{s_j}],\qquad j=1,2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `(5.1)` has basis `b_1,b_2` and assembly is an isomorphism,

\[
\boxed{
K_1(C_r^*(F_2))
=\mathbb Z[u_{s_1}]\oplus\mathbb Z[u_{s_2}].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This agrees with the standard Pimsner–Voiculescu/Cuntz computation of the K-theory of a reduced free-group C*-algebra.

## 5.2 Explicit topological classes upstairs

There are two equivalent concrete constructions.

First, let

\[
H_j:=\langle (0,s_j)\rangle\le G,
\qquad H_j\cong\mathbb Z,
\]

and let

\[
\beta_{\mathbb Z}\in K_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb Z)
\]

be the oriented circle generator. Define

\[
\boxed{
x_j:=\operatorname{Ind}_{H_j}^G(\beta_{\mathbb Z})
\in K_1^{\mathrm{top}}(G).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Equivalently, Valette's naturality construction for the Baum–Connes map associates to every group element `g` a degree-one topological class whose assembly is the K1 class of the canonical unitary `u_g`; `(5.4)` is the cyclic-subgroup realization for `g=(0,s_j)`.

Naturality of assembly under subgroup induction gives a commutative square, and the standard `Z` computation gives

\[
\mu_1^G(x_j)
=(\iota_j)_*\mu_1^{\mathbb Z}(\beta_{\mathbb Z})
=[u_{s_j}].
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the assembly images are not merely existential; they are the two named group unitaries.

## 5.3 Exact rank

Suppose

\[
a[u_{s_1}]+b[u_{s_2}]=0
\quad\text{in }K_1(C_r^*(G))
\]

for integers `a,b`. Apply the reduced quotient map `(pi_r)_*` from `(4.1)`. Since `pi_r(u_{s_j})=u_{s_j}` in `C_r^*(F_2)`,

\[
a[u_{s_1}]+b[u_{s_2}]=0
\quad\text{in }K_1(C_r^*(F_2)).
\]

By `(5.3)`, `a=b=0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}L=2.}
\]

Finally, if

\[
a x_1+b x_2=0
\quad\text{in }K_1^{\mathrm{top}}(G),
\]

apply `mu_1^G` and use `(5.5)` plus target independence; again `a=b=0`. Hence the explicit source subgroup also has rank two.

This completes the proof of `P01-P65A`.

# 7. Mandatory spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let `A` be positive in a unital C*-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Since `{0}` is clopen in `sigma(A)`, the characteristic function of `{0}` is continuous on the spectrum. Continuous functional calculus therefore gives

\[
\boxed{P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).}
\]

On the kernel the functions below agree with `P_A`; on the positive spectrum `lambda>=delta`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}e^{-t\lambda}
\le e^{-t\delta}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` have the same positive gap `delta`. Their bounded Moore–Penrose inverses satisfy

\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]

Since `B P_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

so

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Similarly

\[
\|P_A(1-P_B)\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Using

\[
P_A-P_B
=P_A(1-P_B)-(1-P_A)P_B,
\]

we obtain the sufficient stability estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

This is **not** a proof that group approximants have a common gap.

For the shrinking-gap failure, take an `A>=0` with nonzero kernel and

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I.
\]

Then `||A-B||=epsilon -> 0`, but `ker B=0` and `P_B=0`, while `P_A != 0`. No positive `delta` works simultaneously for `A` and all `B` as `epsilon -> 0`. Norm closeness alone therefore does not control the kernel projection.
