# 0. Executive result

The universal scalar/coefficient-free Baum–Connes maps

\[
\mu_i^G:K_i^{\mathrm{top}}(G)\longrightarrow K_i(C_r^*(G)),\qquad i=0,1,
\]

remain **OPEN** for arbitrary second-countable locally compact `G`. This run gives neither a universal proof nor a scalar counterexample.

It does, however, close the remaining direct-reduced-membership question for the classical Austin irrational/transcendental kernel family. The prior P61 run had proved same-operator positive spectrum accumulating at zero, which closes the isolated-gap/one-variable-functional-calculus route but, after the P62E correction, does not by itself imply non-membership in the whole reduced group C*-algebra. The present run supplies the missing host theorem and trace argument.

## `P01-P63A-AUSTIN-BCC-PERMANENCE` — **PROVED**

For every Austin parameter `I`, the group has the form

\[
\Gamma_I=U_I\rtimes F_2,
\qquad
U_I=\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}/V_I,
\]

with `V_I` invariant under left translation. Hence there is an exact extension

\[
1\longrightarrow U_I\longrightarrow\Gamma_I\longrightarrow F_2\longrightarrow1.
\]

The kernel `U_I` is abelian, hence amenable and therefore has the Haagerup property. The quotient `F_2` is hyperbolic, so it satisfies Baum–Connes with coefficients. Chabert–Echterhoff, Corollary 3.14, then gives Baum–Connes **with coefficients** for `Γ_I`. In particular the scalar maps

\[
\boxed{
\mu_i^{\Gamma_I}:K_i^{\mathrm{top}}(\Gamma_I)
\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}
K_i(C_r^*(\Gamma_I)),\qquad i=0,1,
}
\]

are isomorphisms.

This is an application of a theorem proved for second-countable locally compact groups; the present `Γ_I` are discrete examples inside that framework. It is not a discrete-only replacement for P01.

## `P01-P63B-AUSTIN-REDUCED-KERNEL-PROJECTION-NOGO` — **PROVED**

Choose an Austin parameter `I` for which

\[
\alpha_I
=
\dim_{L\Gamma_I}\ker(Q_I-4)
\]

is irrational (or transcendental), and define

\[
D_I=(Q_I-4)^*(Q_I-4),
\qquad
P_I=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_I)\in L(\Gamma_I).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{P_I\notin C_r^*(\Gamma_I).}
\]

Indeed, if `P_I` were reduced, `[P_I]` would be a `K_0(C_r^*(Γ_I))` class and the canonical trace would satisfy

\[
\tau_*([P_I])=\tau(P_I)=\alpha_I.
\]

By P63A, scalar assembly is surjective. Lück's modified trace theorem, in the precise form quoted as Theorem 9 by Li–Nowak–Pooya, then forces the trace of every reduced `K_0` class into

\[
\Lambda_{\Gamma_I}\subset\mathbb Q.
\]

More specifically, every finite subgroup of `Γ_I` maps trivially to the torsion-free group `F_2`, so it lies in `U_I`; since `U_I` has exponent `2`, every finite subgroup has order a power of `2`, and therefore

\[
\Lambda_{\Gamma_I}\subseteq\mathbb Z[1/2].
\]

This contradicts irrationality of `α_I`.

The argument uses the broad scalar trace-range theorem for arbitrary reduced `K_0` classes. It does **not** invoke the finite-type hypotheses of Li–Nowak–Pooya Proposition 1, because the Austin operator is an independently supplied finite group-ring element rather than a cohomological Laplacian produced from an `F_{n+1}` model.

## `P01-P63C-AUSTIN-CLASSICAL-POOL-FULL-SCREEN` — **PROVED**

Combining P63B with the retained P61 same-operator soft-spectrum theorem closes the Austin irrational/transcendental pool at both relevant levels:

1. `0` is not isolated in the reduced spectrum of `D_I`; hence no retained positive-gap/SOS/heat/resolvent functional-calculus route exists for this matrix.
2. The actual von Neumann kernel projection `P_I` is not in the ambient reduced group C*-algebra at all.

Thus the classical Austin finite matrices cannot yield the required coefficient-free counterexample obstruction.

By retained `P01-P62C-SUBGROUP-SPECTRUM-PROJECTION-RIGIDITY`, placing the same operator inside any discrete overgroup cannot repair either defect: subgroup inclusion preserves its reduced spectrum, and reduced membership of the same kernel projection in an overgroup would force reduced membership back in `Γ_I` via the canonical subgroup conditional expectation.

This is stronger than the prior P61 conclusion and respects P62E: soft spectrum and ambient non-membership are logically separate statements; here they are proved by separate arguments.

## 2.1 Austin — exact host structure and irrational kernel

Primary source:

Tim Austin, *Rational group ring elements with kernels having irrational dimension*, Proc. London Math. Soc. 107 (2013), 1424–1448; arXiv:0909.2360.  
Current primary record: `https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.2360`.

Austin's Corollary 1.2 gives a finitely generated semidirect product

\[
(\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}/V)\rtimes F_2
\]

and a rational group-ring element with irrational, even transcendental, von Neumann kernel dimension. The paper's Section 2 explicitly treats semidirect products `U⋊Λ` with `U` abelian, and then specializes to `Λ=F_2` and `U=Z_2^{⊕F_2}/V`.

This checks all group-theoretic hypotheses used in P63A and P63B: the kernel is abelian/amenable and the quotient is exactly `F_2`.

## 2.2 Chabert–Echterhoff — exact extension permanence

Primary source:

Jérôme Chabert and Siegfried Echterhoff, *Permanence properties of the Baum-Connes conjecture*, Documenta Math. 6 (2001), 127–183, DOI `10.4171/DM/100`.  
Primary EMS page: `https://ems.press/journals/dm/articles/8965031`.

Their notation `BCC` means Baum–Connes with coefficients: the assembly map is an isomorphism for every coefficient algebra. Corollary 3.14 states:

> if `N` is a closed normal subgroup of `G` with the Haagerup property (in particular if `N` is amenable), and `G/N` satisfies BCC, then `G` satisfies BCC.

The paper explicitly works with locally compact second-countable Hausdorff groups, so there is no hidden discrete-only hypothesis in the permanence theorem.

For the quotient, `F_2` is a hyperbolic group. Vincent Lafforgue's theorem proves Baum–Connes with coefficients for hyperbolic groups:

Vincent Lafforgue, *La conjecture de Baum–Connes à coefficients pour les groupes hyperboliques*, J. Noncommut. Geom. 6 (2012), 1–197, DOI `10.4171/JNCG/89`; primary EMS page `https://ems.press/journals/jncg/articles/3925`.

Therefore every Austin `Γ_I` satisfies BCC.

## 2.3 Li–Nowak–Pooya / Lück — exact scalar trace range and finite-type separation

Primary source:

Kang Li, Piotr W. Nowak, Sanaz Pooya, *Higher Kazhdan projections, ℓ2-Betti numbers and Baum–Connes conjectures*, J. Noncommut. Geom. 18 (2024), 313–336, DOI `10.4171/JNCG/529`; primary EMS page `https://ems.press/journals/jncg/articles/12879482`.

The published PDF, Section 1.4, states:

- Theorem 9 (quoting Lück, Theorem 0.3): if the coefficient-free Baum–Connes assembly map is surjective, then `τ_*∘μ_0` takes values in the subring
  \[
  \Lambda_G\subset\mathbb Q
  \]
  generated from `Z` by adjoining inverses of cardinalities of finite subgroups.
- Proposition 1: if `G` is of type `F_{n+1}`, the finite degree-`n` cohomological Laplacian has zero isolated in `M_{k_n}(C_r^*(G))`, and the coefficient-free `μ_0` is surjective, then
  \[
  \beta_n^{(2)}(G)\in\Lambda_G,
  \]
  with integrality for torsion-free `G`.

The distinction is critical. P63B uses Theorem 9 for a hypothetical reduced Austin kernel projection; it does **not** need Austin's group to be `F_{n+1}`. The finite-type hypothesis belongs to Proposition 1's production of a finite cohomological Laplacian.

# 3. Proof of `P01-P63A-AUSTIN-BCC-PERMANENCE`

Fix an Austin parameter `I`. The primary construction has

\[
\Gamma_I=U_I\rtimes F_2,
\qquad
U_I=\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus F_2}/V_I.
\]

Because `V_I` is a subgroup, `U_I` is abelian. Hence `U_I` is amenable. Every amenable discrete group has the Haagerup property, so the closed normal subgroup `U_I◁Γ_I` satisfies the normal-kernel hypothesis of Chabert–Echterhoff Corollary 3.14.

The quotient is exactly

\[
\Gamma_I/U_I\cong F_2.
\]

The free group `F_2` is hyperbolic. Lafforgue's hyperbolic-group theorem gives BCC for `F_2`.

Applying Chabert–Echterhoff gives BCC for `Γ_I`. By definition, BCC means that for **every** `Γ_I`-C*-algebra `B`, the assembly map with coefficients in `B` is an isomorphism. Taking `B=C` with the trivial action gives the scalar maps

\[
\mu_i^{\Gamma_I}:K_i^{\mathrm{top}}(\Gamma_I)
\longrightarrow K_i(C_r^*(\Gamma_I))
\]

as isomorphisms for `i=0,1`.

No quotient map between reduced group C*-algebras is used. The group extension is used only through the verified Baum–Connes permanence theorem.

This proves P63A.

# 4. Proof of `P01-P63B-AUSTIN-REDUCED-KERNEL-PROJECTION-NOGO`

Choose `I` so that Austin's finite rational group-ring operator `Q_I` has

\[
\alpha_I=\dim_{L\Gamma_I}\ker(Q_I-4)\notin\mathbb Q.
\]

Set

\[
T_I=Q_I-4,
\qquad
D_I=T_I^*T_I\ge0.
\]

The von Neumann Borel spectral projection

\[
P_I=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_I)
\]

always exists in `L(Γ_I)` and its range is `ker T_I`. Hence

\[
\tau(P_I)=\alpha_I.
\]

Assume for contradiction that

\[
P_I\in C_r^*(\Gamma_I).
\]

Then `P_I` represents a class

\[
[P_I]\in K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_I)),
\]

and the extension of the canonical trace to `K_0` gives

\[
\tau_*([P_I])=\tau(P_I)=\alpha_I.
\]

P63A gives surjectivity of the scalar assembly map `μ_0`. By Lück's modified trace theorem as quoted in Li–Nowak–Pooya Theorem 9,

\[
\tau_*(K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_I)))
\subseteq
\Lambda_{\Gamma_I}
\subseteq
\mathbb Q.
\]

For this host one can make the torsion denominator even more explicit. Let `F≤Γ_I` be finite. Its image in `F_2` is a finite subgroup. Since `F_2` is torsion-free, that image is trivial, so

\[
F\le U_I.
\]

The group `U_I` is a quotient of an elementary abelian `2`-group, so every element has order at most `2`; every finite subgroup therefore has cardinality `2^m` for some `m`. Thus

\[
\Lambda_{\Gamma_I}\subseteq\mathbb Z[1/2].
\]

But `α_I` is irrational. Contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{P_I\notin C_r^*(\Gamma_I).}
\]

Exactly the same proof applies to any matrix amplification if one uses a matrix-valued Austin realization; the matrix trace is the usual sum of diagonal canonical traces.

### Distinctions kept explicit

- `T_I` is a finite rational group-ring operator.
- `P_I` is a von Neumann spectral projection and exists independently of any spectral gap.
- `τ(P_I)=α_I` is a von Neumann dimension statement.
- P63B proves `P_I` is **not** a reduced-C* projection.
- Hence the irrational trace does not produce a reduced `K_0` counterexample; it certifies failure of the candidate instead.

This proves P63B.

# 6. Mandatory spectral-stability seed — independently rederived

Let `A≥0` be an element of a unital C*-algebra with

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Because zero is isolated, the characteristic function of `{0}` is continuous on `σ(A)`. Continuous functional calculus therefore gives

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).
\]

For `t>0`, on the zero spectral point both `e^{-t\lambda}` and `P_A` equal `1`, while on `[δ,M]` the projection is zero. Hence

\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\]

Likewise the scalar function

\[
f_\eta(\lambda)=\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\]

has value `1` at zero, and on `[δ,M]` is at most `η/(δ+η)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let `A,B≥0` both have positive spectrum contained in `[δ,∞)`. Define the bounded pseudoinverse

\[
A^\dagger=g(A),
\qquad
g(0)=0,
\quad g(\lambda)=1/\lambda\ (\lambda\ge\delta).
\]

Then `||A^\dagger||≤1/δ` and

\[
1-P_A=A^\dagger A.
\]

Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

so

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the symmetric bound. Using

\[
P_A-P_B=P_A(1-P_B)-(1-P_A)P_B
\]

therefore yields the requested sufficient estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

This is **not** a proof of a uniform gap for group approximants. The elementary test

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I
\]

shows the failure mode: if `A` has nonzero kernel, then `||A-B||=ε→0` while `B` has trivial kernel. The common positive-gap hypothesis has failed because the newly lifted former zero eigenvalue is only `ε`.

## 8.1 Independent derivation

Work over a finitely generated free group `F(S)`. Let

\[
p,C,D_1,\ldots,D_m\in M_n(\mathbb CF),
\qquad A=C^*C,
\]

and assume in a marked quotient `G`

\[
p=p^*=p^2,
\qquad pA=0.
\]

Let

\[
E=A-\delta(1-p)-\sum_jD_j^*D_j,
\qquad
\|E\|_{1,\mathrm{op};G}\le\varepsilon<\delta.
\]

Write

\[
r_p=\operatorname{prop}_F(p),
\quad r_C=\operatorname{prop}_F(C),
\quad r_E=\operatorname{prop}_F(E).
\]

The formal identities to transfer have propagation bounds

\[
\operatorname{prop}(p^2-p)\le2r_p,
\qquad
\operatorname{prop}(pA)\le r_p+2r_C,
\]

and `p-p^*` has propagation at most `r_p`. Exact finite-word identity transfer needs twice these radii because equality of two support words is tested by a word `uv^{-1}`. Thus

\[
R_{\rm rob}=2\max\{2r_p,r_p+2r_C,r_E\}
\]

is sufficient to transfer the exact projection/annihilation identities and to preserve the collected coefficient partition of `E`. Therefore in any marked quotient `H` agreeing through `R_rob`, one has

\[
p_H=p_H^*=p_H^2,
\qquad p_HA_H=0,
\qquad \|E_H\|\le\varepsilon.
\]

Taking adjoints gives `A_Hp_H=0`. For `q_H=1-p_H`,

\[
A_H=q_HA_Hq_H.
\]

The residual identity becomes

\[
A_H=\delta q_H+\sum_jD_{j,H}^*D_{j,H}+E_H.
\]

Since `E_H` is self-adjoint,

\[
q_HE_Hq_H\ge-\varepsilon q_H.
\]

Compressing to `q_H` gives

\[
\boxed{A_H\ge(\delta-\varepsilon)q_H.}
\]

Because `δ-ε>0`, the kernel of `A_H` is exactly the `p_H` corner, so

\[
\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A_H)=p_H.
\]

Finally the representation-independent coefficient estimate gives

\[
\|A_H\|=\|C_H\|^2
\le
\left(\sum_w\|C_w\|\right)^2.
\]

Thus the claimed gap, kernel identity, and fixed propagation of the structural projection are correct. The strict threshold is genuinely sharp: at `ε=δ`, take `p=diag(1,0)`, `A=0`, and no `D_j`; then the residual is `-δ(1-p)` but the kernel projection is `1`, not `p`.
