# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A^*>=0` in a unital C*-algebra and suppose

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta\le M.
\]

Because `{0}` is clopen in `sigma(A)`, continuous functional calculus gives

\[
\boxed{P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

On the zero spectral subspace, `e^{-tA}=1`; on the positive spectral subspace its norm is at most `e^{-t delta}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Likewise the scalar function

\[
x\mapsto\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

has value one at zero and is at most `eta/(delta+eta)` on `[delta,M]`, so

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Now let `A,B>=0` share the common positive gap

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)
\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Their bounded pseudoinverses satisfy

\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le1/\delta.
\]

Using `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

thus

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the same bound for `(1-P_B)P_A`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The previously retained two-projection identity yields the sharper factor `1`; `(3.4)` is the requested sufficient estimate.

For the failure mode, if `A` has nonzero kernel and

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I,
\]

then `||A-B||=epsilon->0`, while `B` is invertible and its kernel projection is zero. The positive gap of `B` is only `epsilon`; there is no common fixed `delta`.

Thus norm closeness **plus a uniform common gap** is sufficient for projection stability. It is not a proof that group approximants possess such a gap.

# 4. Proof of `P01-P52A-TORUS-FULL-TORSION-FIXED-SET`

Fix

\[
A_L=C_L\times\mathbb Z
\]

with intrinsic word metric for `a,t`. Write vertices as

\[
(i,n),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/L,\ n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Then

\[
d((i,n),(j,m))
=|i-j|_L+|n-m|,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
|r|_L=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|r+kL|.
\]

The diameter of the horizontal cyclic factor is

\[
\max_{i,j}|i-j|_L=d_L=\lfloor L/2\rfloor.
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 4.1 Every invariant simplex is a union of full horizontal fibers

Let `sigma` be a simplex of `P_D A_L` stabilized by `C_L`. If `(i,n)` is a vertex of `sigma`, then the entire orbit

\[
\{(i+r,n):r\in\mathbb Z/L\}
=C_L\times\{n\}
\]

lies in the vertex set of `sigma`.

Therefore every invariant simplex is the union of full horizontal fibers over a finite set

\[
S\subset\mathbb Z.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Conversely, a union of full fibers over `S` is `C_L`-invariant.

## 4.2 Exact clique criterion

For two heights `n,m`, the largest distance between the two full fibers is, by `(4.1)` and `(4.2)`,

\[
\max_{i,j}
 d((i,n),(j,m))
=d_L+|n-m|.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence the union of full fibers over `S` spans a `D`-Rips simplex exactly when

\[
d_L+\operatorname{diam}(S)\le D.
\tag{4.5}
\]

If `D<d_L`, even one full fiber is not a simplex, so there are no invariant simplices and no fixed points.

Assume `D>=d_L`. Then `(4.5)` is precisely

\[
\operatorname{diam}(S)\le D-d_L,
\]

the simplex condition for `P_{D-d_L} Z`.

## 4.3 Fixed barycentric coordinates

Inside an invariant simplex, the `C_L`-action permutes the vertices of each horizontal fiber transitively. A point of the geometric simplex is fixed by `C_L` if and only if its barycentric coefficients are constant along each horizontal fiber.

Thus each full fiber contributes one barycentric coordinate, its total weight. The fixed simplex is exactly the simplex on the height set `S`.

These identifications are compatible under faces. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(P_DA_L)^{C_L}\cong P_{D-d_L}\mathbb Z
}
\]

for `D>=d_L`, proving `(0.1)`.

## 4.4 Contractibility threshold

If

\[
0\le r<1,
\]

then `P_r Z` has no edges and is the discrete set `Z`.

If `r>=1`, put `m=floor(r)>=1`. Every compact subset of `P_r Z` is contained in the Rips complex on some finite interval `[-N,N]`. The `m`-th power of the finite path graph is dismantlable: the left endpoint `-N` is dominated by `-N+1`, since its closed neighborhood is contained in that of `-N+1`; remove endpoints successively. Hence every finite interval clique complex collapses to a point.

Every map from a sphere into `P_r Z` has compact image, hence lies in such a finite contractible subcomplex and is nullhomotopic. Thus `P_r Z` is weakly contractible. Being a CW complex, it is contractible.

Consequently

\[
(P_DA_L)^{C_L}
\]

is discrete for `d_L<=D<d_L+1` and contractible for `D>=d_L+1`.

## 4.5 Relation to the exact cyclic barcode

The retained cyclic formula is

\[
\kappa_L=L/2
\qquad(L\text{ even}),
\]

while for odd

\[
L=\prod_jp_j^{a_j},\qquad
P(L)=\max_jp_j^{a_j},
\]

\[
\kappa_L
=
\frac{P(L)-1}{2P(L)}L.
\]

Therefore:

- for every even `L`, `kappa_L=d_L`;
- for every odd prime power `L=p^a`, `P(L)=L` and again `kappa_L=d_L`.

In particular the P51 one-step ambiguity on powers of two and odd primes crosses the exact full-torsion fixed-set connectivity threshold.

### Limitation

It is not an assertion that the induced metric on the subgroup inside the triangular semidirect product equals the intrinsic product metric. The retained full-source upper certificate only needs the 1-Lipschitz product-to-ambient map after subgroup induction. No hidden metric equality is used.

The fixed-set theorem does not by itself identify the Bott K-homology class at the critical scale. A finite-isotropy equivariant K-homology calculation is still required.

\tag{6.1}

\[

\tag{6.2}

\[
