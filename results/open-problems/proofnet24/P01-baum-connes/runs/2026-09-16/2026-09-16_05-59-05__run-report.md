\]

\[

\]

\[
K_1^K(\sigma,\partial\sigma)\cong R(K),\qquad
K_0^K(\sigma,\partial\sigma)=0.

\[
\partial_\sigma:
K_1^K(\sigma,\partial\sigma)

is an isomorphism onto the reduced summand
\[
\widetilde K_0^K(\partial\sigma)\cong R(K).

\[
\boxed{\partial_\sigma(r)=\pm r\,\beta_W\qquad(r\in R(K)),}

# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed

Let `A=A^*>=0` in a unital `C^*`-algebra and suppose
\[
\operatorname{spec}(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Because zero is clopen in the spectrum, continuous functional calculus gives
\[
P_A=1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).
\]
On the zero spectral subspace `e^{-tA}-P_A=0`, while on `[delta,M]` its scalar value is `e^{-t lambda}`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\]
Likewise the scalar function
\[
f_\eta(\lambda)=\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\]
equals `1` at zero after subtracting `P_A`, and on the positive spectrum has absolute value at most `eta/(delta+eta)`, so
\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` both satisfy the same lower positive gap `delta`. Their Moore–Penrose inverses satisfy
\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]
Since `BP_B=0`,
\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger AP_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]
hence
\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
Symmetrically,
\[
\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
Using
\[
P_A-P_B=P_A(1-P_B)-(1-P_A)P_B
\]
gives the requested sufficient estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]
(The standard two-projection identity yields the previously retained sharper factor `1`, but the displayed factor `2` is the requested elementary consequence.)

Finally, if `A` has nonzero kernel and
\[
B=A+\varepsilon I,
\]
then
\[
\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0,
\]
but `P_B=0` while `P_A!=0`. The positive gap of `B` is only `epsilon`, so there is no common uniform `delta`. This is the exact shrinking-gap failure. The seed is a **sufficient norm-plus-uniform-gap hypothesis**, not a proof that a sequence of group approximants has such a gap.

# 4. Proof of `P01-P56A-EVEN-ORBIT-SIMPLEX-ATTACHING-BOUNDARY`

Let
\[
K=C_q
\]
and suppose
\[
\Omega\cong K\times\{1,\ldots,s\},\qquad s\ge2\text{ even}.
\]
The real permutation representation on vertices is
\[
\mathbb R[\Omega]\cong s\,\mathbb R[K].
\]
After centering the simplex at its barycenter, its affine tangent representation is
\[
V=s\,\mathbb R[K]-\mathbb R.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Radial rescaling gives an equivariant homeomorphism of pairs
\[
(\sigma,\partial\sigma)
\cong_K
(D(V),S(V)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 4.1 Representation decomposition

I rederive rather than merely import P25's parity statement.

If `q` is odd, the real regular representation is
\[
\mathbb R[K]\cong\mathbb R\oplus U_{\mathbb R}
\]
for a complex `K`-representation `U`. Hence
\[
V\cong(s-1)\mathbb R\oplus sU_{\mathbb R}.
\]
Since `s` is even, `s-1` is odd; split off one trivial real line and pair the remaining trivial lines into complex trivial lines. Thus
\[
V\cong\mathbb R\oplus W_{\mathbb R}
\tag{4.3}
\]
for a complex representation `W`.

If `q=2r` is even, write `sigma` for the real sign representation. Then
\[
\mathbb R[K]\cong\mathbb R\oplus\sigma\oplus U_{\mathbb R}.
\]
So
\[
V\cong(s-1)\mathbb R\oplus s\sigma\oplus sU_{\mathbb R}.
\]
Again `s-1` is odd. Moreover `s sigma` is an even number of sign lines, hence is the underlying real representation of `(s/2)` copies of the complex character `chi^r`. Therefore (4.3) again holds.

## 4.2 Relative K-homology and the sphere boundary

Complex equivariant Bott periodicity on `W`, followed by the single trivial real suspension, gives
\[
K_1^K(D(V),S(V))\cong R(K),
\qquad
K_0^K(D(V),S(V))=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]
This independently reproduces the even-orbit half of `P25-CYCLIC-MULTIORBIT-SIMPLEX-KHOMOLOGY-45`.

Now use the long exact sequence of the pair. Since `D(V)` is `K`-equivariantly contractible to the origin,
\[
K_0^K(D(V))\cong R(K),
\qquad
K_1^K(D(V))=0.
\]
Together with (4.4), exactness yields
\[
0\longrightarrow K_1^K(D(V),S(V))
\xrightarrow{\partial}
K_0^K(S(V))
\xrightarrow{i_*}
R(K)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because
\[
V=\mathbb R\oplus W_{\mathbb R},
\]
stereographic projection from the positive fixed pole in the trivial real direction gives
\[
S(V)\cong_K W_{\mathbb R}^{+}=S^W.
\tag{4.6}
\]
The fixed pole supplies a splitting of the unreduced point summand, while complex Bott periodicity gives
\[
\widetilde K_0^K(S^W)\cong R(K).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Thus
\[
K_0^K(S(V))
\cong R(K)_{\rm pt}\oplus R(K)_{\rm red},
\]
and `i_*` in (4.5) is projection onto the point summand. Hence
\[
\operatorname{im}(\partial)
=\ker(i_*)
=R(K)_{\rm red}.
\]
Since the domain in (4.5) is itself `R(K)`, the boundary is an `R(K)`-module isomorphism onto the reduced summand.

If the relative generator is chosen by first applying the complex Bott class of `W` and then the positive orientation of the remaining trivial real suspension, and `beta_W` is the corresponding reduced Bott class in (4.7), naturality of the suspension boundary gives
\[
\partial(r)=\pm r\beta_W.
\tag{4.8}
\]
The sign is the ordinary real-suspension orientation convention. In particular there is **no local kernel and no smaller representation-ring quotient** hidden at this step.

This proves P56A.

# 6. Proof of `P01-P56C-POWER2-CRITICAL-FINITE-CERTIFICATE-REDUCTION`

Fix `L=2^r` and `d=L/2`. The group `Gamma=Gamma_{2,L}` is finitely generated, so its word-metric ball
\[
B_\Gamma(e,d)
\]
is finite.

Any simplex orbit of `P_d Gamma` has a representative containing the identity: left-translate one chosen vertex to `e`. Every remaining vertex then lies in `B_Gamma(e,d)`. Hence there are only finitely many simplex orbits in `P_d Gamma/Gamma`. The same holds for `P_{d-1} Gamma`, and therefore the critical pair
\[
(P_d\Gamma,P_{d-1}\Gamma)
\tag{6.1}
\]
is `Gamma`-cocompact with finitely many equivariant simplex-orbits.

The action is free on vertices, so every setwise simplex stabilizer is finite. Barycentric subdivision converts the simplicial action with inversions into a proper equivariant cellular model whose cell stabilizers are finite and act pointwise. Consequently the relative equivariant K-homology of (6.1) is governed by a **finite** cellular/Bredon certificate with representation-ring coefficient groups and finite restriction/induction incidence maps.

P55C adds a decisive localization for the nontrivial stabilizers: every nonfree critical simplex, after translation, has all vertices in
\[
B=\langle a,t\rangle\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z,
\]
with the metric inherited from the ambient group. Hence every nontrivial-stabilizer block in that finite certificate can be enumerated in the finite set
\[
B\cap B_\Gamma(e,d).
\]
P25-P45 exhibits one such block explicitly, the two-orbit `C_2` tetrahedron. P56A computes the local pair boundary for **every** even-orbit cyclic block, and P56B gives the first danger block as a literal `2x2` identity on the reduced representation-ring summand.

What remains is finite but genuinely global:

1. enumerate the actual critical cell orbits (with the inherited ambient metric);
2. record all face incidences and inclusions/conjugacies of stabilizers;
3. form the induced restriction/induction maps between the relevant representation rings;
4. express the stage-`d-1` Bott source in the finite cellular coordinates;
5. decide whether its image at stage `d` is zero.

No claim is made that the answer is automatically `d` or `d+1`. The retained P51/P55 result remains
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{2,2^r}\in\{2^{r-1},2^{r-1}+1\}.
}
\]
P56 replaces an unknown local boundary by an explicit one and narrows the missing object to the finite global incidence certificate.

  \]

# 9. Failed routes / adversarial checks

The following shortcuts remain dead and were not reused:

1. **Infinite projection sums.** Trace convergence of a sum of nonzero finite-subgroup projections does not imply norm convergence in `C_r^*(G)\otimes K`.
2. **Irrational L2-Betti alone.** An exotic von Neumann dimension does not create a reduced-`C^*` projection or a reduced `K_0` class.
3. **Reduced quotients.** A group quotient does not automatically induce `C_r^*` functoriality; weak containment / amenability of the kernel must be verified.
4. **Marked/strong convergence.** Local relation convergence or strong operator convergence does not by itself provide upper reduced-norm control.
5. **Embedding.** Embedding a matrix/operator into a larger group/algebra does not isolate zero in reduced spectrum.
6. **Shrinking-gap transport.** Norm closeness without one common positive gap cannot control kernel projections; `B=A+epsilon I` is the elementary certificate.
7. **One-orbit-is-enough shortcut.** P25-P44's harmlessness of a one-orbit regular cyclic simplex does not settle nested isotropy. P25-P45's two-orbit `C_2` tetrahedron gives relative `K_1=R(C_2)`.
8. **Newly closed inference: local killer module => global death.** Even after P56B, a locally invertible boundary does not imply the Bott source lies in its global image. The elementary nonequivariant analogue is a 1-cell: the local boundary of `(D^1,S^0)` detects the reduced `K_0(S^0)` generator, but identifying the two endpoints in the preceding space can cancel that boundary. Global face incidence is essential.
9. **Forgetful-dimension-only detector.** Passing from `R(C_2)` to ordinary K-homology via dimension sees `dim(1)=dim(epsilon)=1` and kills the virtual direction `1-epsilon`. It cannot by itself decide the equivariant critical boundary.

Adversarial check on P56A: the proof uses a compatible complex Bott class for `W` and the same real-suspension orientation in the relative and reduced boundary identifications. Without choosing those compatible generators, the lower block of the matrix is only determined up to an `R(K)`-unit. The theorem is therefore stated invariantly as “isomorphism onto the reduced summand,” with the `±I` matrix only after compatible Bott/suspension normalization.
