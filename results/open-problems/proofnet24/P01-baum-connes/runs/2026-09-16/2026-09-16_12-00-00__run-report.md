## `P01-P62A-PSZ-PATH-EXACT-SPECTRUM` — **PROVED**

For the Pichot–Schick–Żuk finite model `A^{l,1,1}` (a path of length `l`, hence `l+1` vertices, every edge of weight `2`),

\[

If `l≡1 (mod 3)`, the unique `-2` eigenvalue is at

## `P01-P62B-PSZ-QUANTITATIVE-SOFT-SPECTRUM` — **PROVED**

Let `G_I` and `A∈Q[G_I]` be the groups/operators of PSZ Theorem 10.1, and put

\[
Q=A+2,\qquad D=Q^*Q=Q^2.

For every admissible `I`, not merely the transcendental choices, the reduced spectrum of `D` has positive spectral values converging to zero. More explicitly, take

\[
l_1=1,\qquad l_2=3m+2,\qquad l_m=l_1+l_2+1=3m+4.

PSZ formula (10.2) gives a positive multiplicity module for that `(1,1)` hook. The neighboring path eigenline from P62A gives

\boxed{

\le \frac{16\pi^2}{(3m+6)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\]

For the Theorem-10 normalization `I={2,n_1,...}`, the same one-dimensional finite-model eigenline has positive von Neumann multiplicity; in the displayed hook family `min(l_1,l_2)=1`, hence formula (10.2) gives the exact multiplicity

\[

\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{0\text{ is not isolated in }\sigma_{C_r^*(G_I)}(D).}
\]

Thus the PSZ matrix cannot satisfy the retained isolated-zero criterion, cannot admit a positive-gap SOS certificate, and its heat/resolvent kernel cutoffs have no norm convergence to the kernel projection.

## `P01-P62C-SUBGROUP-SPECTRUM-PROJECTION-RIGIDITY` — **PROVED**

Let `G≤H` be discrete groups and let `x∈M_n(CG)`. Then:

1. the canonical map `C_r^*(G)→C_r^*(H)` is isometric;
2. `σ_{M_n(C_r^*(G))}(x)=σ_{M_n(C_r^*(H))}(x)`;
3. if `P_G=1_{\{0\}}(x^*x)∈M_n(L(G))` and `P_H` denotes the same Borel spectral projection in `M_n(L(H))`, then

\[
\boxed{
P_H\in M_n(C_r^*(H))
\iff
P_G\in M_n(C_r^*(G)).
}
\]

The forward implication uses the canonical subgroup conditional expectation

\[
E_G:C_r^*(H)\to C_r^*(G).
\]

This is a genuine subgroup statement; it is **not** the invalid reduced quotient-map shortcut.

## `P01-P62D-PSZ-FINITELY-PRESENTED-REDUCED-PROJECTION-NOGO` — **PROVED**

Choose the PSZ Theorem-10 option `Γ=Z wr Z` and a recursively enumerable admissible sequence, for example

\[
n_k=3k!+2.
\]

Then `G_I=(\bigoplus_Γ Z_2/V_{F_l,I})⋊Γ` is amenable (abelian-by-amenable), has a recursive presentation, and the PSZ formula gives a transcendental kernel dimension for `Q=A+2` after the standard rational scaling to an integral group-ring element.

Because amenable groups satisfy Baum–Connes (indeed with coefficients), scalar assembly for `G_I` is surjective. Lück's modified trace theorem therefore forces the trace of every reduced `K_0` class into

\[
\Lambda_{G_I}\subset\mathbb Q.
\]

The von Neumann kernel projection of `Q` has transcendental trace, hence

\[
P_G\notin C_r^*(G_I).
\]

PSZ Theorem 13.1 embeds this recursively presented `G_I` into a finitely presented Higman supergroup `H`, carrying the same finite group-ring element. P62C then gives

\[
\boxed{P_H\notin C_r^*(H).}
\]

Thus the actual finitely presented PSZ transcendental-kernel example cannot be rescued by the Higman embedding: neither an isolated reduced zero nor even direct membership of this particular von Neumann kernel projection in the reduced group algebra is possible.

## `P01-P62E-SOFT-SPECTRUM-MEMBERSHIP-SEPARATION` — **PROVED / correction of an overstrong inference**

Soft spectrum by itself does **not** imply that a kernel projection fails to belong to the ambient C*-algebra. For example, in

\[
\mathcal A=\mathcal K(\ell^2)+\mathbb C1,
\]

let

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1/2,1/3,\ldots).
\]

Then `0` is non-isolated in `σ(D)`, but the kernel projection onto the first coordinate is rank one and belongs to `K(ℓ²)⊂A`.

Therefore the valid content of P61B/P61C is: soft spectrum excludes the **isolated-gap / continuous-functional-calculus** route and yields the exact distance barrier inside `C^*(D,1)`. It does not, by itself, prove non-membership in the whole ambient reduced group algebra. For PSZ, P62D supplies the stronger non-membership theorem by a separate amenability + trace-range + conditional-expectation argument. For the amenable lamplighter pool P61D, the earlier trace-range non-membership proof remains valid.

This logical correction is now part of the cumulative ledger.

# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let `A=A*>=0` in a unital C*-algebra and suppose

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]

The point `0` is clopen in `σ(A)`, so continuous functional calculus gives

\[
\boxed{P_A=1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).}
\]

On `ker A`, both `e^{-tA}` and `P_A` equal `1`. On `[δ,M]`, `P_A=0` and the maximum of `e^{-tx}` is `e^{-tδ}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\]

Likewise, for `η>0`, on the kernel the resolvent cutoff `η(A+ηI)^{-1}` is `1`, while on `[δ,M]`

\[
0\le\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` both satisfy

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Let `A^\dagger` be the bounded inverse on `(ker A)^⊥` and zero on `ker A`; then `||A^\dagger||<=1/δ`. Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

so

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the symmetric bound. Using

\[
P_A-P_B=P_A(1-P_B)-(1-P_A)P_B
\]

gives the requested sufficient estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

This is a **norm-plus-common-uniform-gap sufficient hypothesis**, not a proof that marked/group approximants possess such a gap.

Shrinking-gap failure is exact. If `A` has nonzero kernel and positive spectrum above `δ`, then for

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I
\]

we have `||A-B||=ε→0`, but `ker B=0`; on the old kernel the new positive eigenvalue is `ε`, so no common positive gap survives.

# 4. Proof of P62A — exact PSZ path spectrum

For `(i,j)=(1,1)`, PSZ Proposition 5.3 says `V^{l,1,1}` is a path of length `l` whose every edge has weight `2`. Thus there are `N=l+1` vertices. Number them `1,...,N` and impose the Dirichlet boundary convention `v_0=v_{N+1}=0`.

The weighted adjacency operator is

\[
(A_l v)_j=2v_{j-1}+2v_{j+1}.
\]

For each `k=1,...,N`, put

\[
v_j^{(k)}=\sin\frac{jk\pi}{N+1}.
\]

The elementary identity

\[
\sin((j-1)\theta)+\sin((j+1)\theta)
=2\cos\theta\sin(j\theta)
\]

gives

\[
A_l v^{(k)}
=4\cos\frac{k\pi}{N+1}\,v^{(k)}.
\]

The discrete sine vectors form a basis, proving

\[
\sigma(A_l)
=
\left\{4\cos\frac{k\pi}{l+2}:1\le k\le l+1\right\}.
\]

If `l≡1 mod3`, then `l+2` is divisible by `3` and

\[
k_0=\frac{2(l+2)}3
\]

is an integer. Its angle is `2π/3`, hence the eigenvalue is `4 cos(2π/3)=-2`. Since the path eigenvalues are strictly decreasing in `k`, this eigenvalue is simple, independently rederiving the `(1,1)` part of PSZ Lemma 6.1.

For `l>=4`, `k_0+1<=l+1`. The adjacent eigenvalue is distinct and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|4\cos\frac{(k_0+1)\pi}{l+2}+2\right|
&=
4\left|\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}+\frac{\pi}{l+2}\right)-\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}\right|\\
&\le \frac{4\pi}{l+2},
\end{aligned}
\]

because cosine is `1`-Lipschitz. This proves P62A.

# 5. Proof of P62B — a quantitative same-matrix soft-spectrum theorem

PSZ Theorem 10.1 decomposes `A` by Proposition 5.3. For every pair

\[
l_1,l_2\ge1,\qquad l_1+l_2\equiv0\pmod3,
\]

there is a `(1,1)` hook through the identity, of path length

\[
l=l_1+l_2+1\equiv1\pmod3,
\]

and formula (10.2) assigns positive von Neumann multiplicity to its one-dimensional `-2` eigenspace.

Take, for `m>=0`,

\[
l_1=1,\qquad l_2=3m+2,
\qquad l=3m+4.
\]

Then `l+2=3m+6` and P62A gives the neighboring finite-model eigenvalue

\[
\lambda_m
=4\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}+\frac{\pi}{3m+6}\right).
\]

It is not `-2`, so for `Q=A+2` the corresponding scalar eigenvalue is

\[
q_m=\lambda_m+2\ne0.
\]

For `D=Q^*Q`, the corresponding eigenvalue is

\[
\nu_m=|q_m|^2>0.
\]

The Lipschitz estimate from P62A gives

\[
0<\nu_m\le\frac{16\pi^2}{(3m+6)^2}\to0.
\]

The finite-model restriction is

\[
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H_{P,R,\psi}}\otimes A_l.
\]

Every simple finite-model eigenline therefore carries the same positive multiplicity module. In the chosen hook family, `min(l_1,l_2)=1`, while Theorem 10.1 has `I={2,n_1,...}`; hence the correction term in (10.2) vanishes and the neighboring simple eigenline has von Neumann dimension

\[
2^{-3(3m+3)-8}=2^{-9m-17}>0.
\]

Thus each `ν_m` belongs to the regular spectrum with positive spectral mass. Since `ν_m→0`, zero is non-isolated in the reduced spectrum of `D`.

This is stronger than merely observing an irrational kernel dimension. It is a **same finite operator** soft-spectrum certificate with an explicit scale and positive mass sequence.

Consequences for this route:

- no `δ>0` satisfies `σ(D)⊂{0}∪[δ,∞)`;
- no exact gap identity `D^2-δD=ΣB_j^*B_j` with `δ>0` can hold;
- no P25 structural complement certificate `D-δ(1-p)=ΣD_j^*D_j` can hold with `p` equal the kernel projection;
- for any finite `t>0` and `η>0`, the positive eigenvalues `ν_m→0` force

\[
\|e^{-tD}-P\|=1,
\qquad
\|\eta(D+\eta I)^{-1}-P\|=1
\]

in the von Neumann representation, whenever `P=1_{0}(D)`.

The last statement concerns norm approximation by these functions; it does not by itself decide membership of `P` in a larger ambient C*-algebra. P62D supplies that stronger statement for the transcendental PSZ example.

# 6. Proof of P62C — subgroup rigidity

Let `G≤H` and choose right coset representatives `R` for `G\H`. Then

\[
\ell^2(H)^n
=
\bigoplus_{r\in R}\ell^2(Gr)^n.
\]

For `x∈M_n(CG)`, left multiplication by `G` preserves every `Gr`, and the unitary

\[
U_r:\ell^2(G)^n\to\ell^2(Gr)^n,
\qquad
U_r\delta_g=\delta_{gr},
\]

intertwines `λ_G^{(n)}(x)` with the restriction of `λ_H^{(n)}(x)`. Therefore

\[
\lambda_H^{(n)}(x)
\cong
\bigoplus_{r\in R}\lambda_G^{(n)}(x).
\]

In particular

\[
\|x\|_{M_n(C_r^*(H))}
=
\|x\|_{M_n(C_r^*(G))}.
\]

Hence the group-ring inclusion extends to an isometric *-monomorphism

\[
M_n(C_r^*(G))\hookrightarrow M_n(C_r^*(H)).
\]

C*-subalgebras are spectrally invariant, so the spectra of `x` in the two reduced algebras coincide. This proves the first two statements.

For the projection statement, let

\[
D=x^*x.
\]

The normal inclusion `L(G)→L(H)` sends the Borel spectral projection `P_G=1_{0}(D)` to `P_H=1_{0}(D)`.

There is a canonical faithful conditional expectation

\[
E_G:C_r^*(H)\to C_r^*(G),
\]

which on the group ring deletes coefficients outside `G`; its normal extension maps `L(H)` onto `L(G)` and fixes `L(G)` pointwise.

If `P_H∈M_n(C_r^*(H))`, then

\[
P_G=E_G^{(n)}(P_H)\in M_n(C_r^*(G)).
\]

Conversely, if `P_G∈M_n(C_r^*(G))`, its image under the reduced subgroup inclusion is exactly `P_H`, so `P_H∈M_n(C_r^*(H))`.

Thus reduced membership of the spectral kernel projection is **invariant under passing to an overgroup for the same subgroup matrix**.

This is the correct permanence phenomenon for embeddings. It sharply contrasts with quotient groups, where no reduced map may exist without weak containment / amenability of the kernel.

# 7. Proof of P62D and the PSZ finitely-presented screen

Take the `Γ=Z wr Z` option in PSZ Theorem 10.1. The group

\[
G_I=(\mathbb Z_2^{\oplus Γ}/V_{F_l,I})\rtimes Γ
\]

is amenable: its normal subgroup is abelian, and `Z wr Z` is amenable.

Choose an admissible recursively enumerable sequence, for example

\[
n_k=3k!+2.
\]

It obeys the required congruence `n_k≡2 mod3`. In the PSZ formula

\[
\dim_{G_I}\ker(A+2)
=\beta_1+\beta_2\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-6n_k+k},
\]

`β_1,β_2` are explicit rationals and `β_2≠0`. The series with factorially separated binary positions is Liouville and hence transcendental. Thus the kernel dimension is transcendental.

If desired, clear the finitely many rational denominators of `Q=A+2` by a positive integer `c` and set

\[
B=cQ\in\mathbb Z[G_I].
\]

The kernel and its von Neumann projection are unchanged; `B^*B=c^2Q^*Q` has the same zero/nonzero spectral topology up to scaling.

Because `G_I` is amenable, Tu's amenable-groupoid theorem gives Baum–Connes with coefficients, hence in particular the scalar assembly is surjective. By Li–Nowak–Pooya Theorem 9 / Lück's modified trace theorem, if the kernel projection belonged to the reduced group C*-algebra then its `K_0` trace would lie in

\[
\Lambda_{G_I}\subset\mathbb Q.
\]

But its trace is the transcendental von Neumann kernel dimension. Therefore

\[
\boxed{P_G\notin C_r^*(G_I).}
\]

Since `I` is recursively enumerable, PSZ Theorem 12.3 gives a recursive presentation. Theorem 13.1 uses Higman's embedding theorem to put `G_I` inside a finitely presented group `H`, and regards the same integral group-ring element `B` in `Z[H]`.

By P62C,

\[
P_H\in C_r^*(H)
\iff
P_G\in C_r^*(G_I).
\]

The right side is false, hence

\[
\boxed{P_H\notin C_r^*(H).}
\]

Also by P62C, the reduced spectrum of `B^*B` is identical in `G_I` and `H`; P62B therefore shows that zero remains non-isolated after the Higman embedding.

This proves a stronger screen than originally requested for this pool:

- the finitely presented PSZ operator has no reduced gap;
- the Higman embedding cannot create a gap;
- the Higman embedding cannot make the same von Neumann kernel projection enter the reduced algebra.

No assertion about the scalar Baum–Connes status of the **Higman supergroup itself** is needed or made.

## 8.1 Independent derivation of finite-identity transfer

Let

\[
X=\sum_{|w|\le r}X_w w\in M_n(\mathbb C F(S)).
\]

After evaluation in a marked quotient `F→G`, the coefficient of a group element `g∈G` is the sum of `X_w` over support words mapping to `g`. Thus `X_G=0` is determined exactly by the equivalence relation on the finite support

\[
u\sim_G v
\iff
\pi_G(u)=\pi_G(v)
\iff
uv^{-1}\in\ker\pi_G.
\]

Every comparison word `uv^{-1}` has length at most `2r`. Therefore if `G,H` have identical trivial relations through radius `2r`, their support equivalence partitions are identical, and

\[
X_G=0\iff X_H=0.
\]

The factor `2` is sharp in general. For `G=C_{2r}`, `H=Z` with the one-generator marking and

\[
X=a^r-a^{-r},
\]

there is no nontrivial relation in `G` of length `<2r`, so the groups agree through `2r-1`, but `X_G=0` while `X_H≠0`.

**Verdict on `P25-MARKED-FINITE-IDENTITY-TRANSFER-51`: PASS.**

## 8.2 Independent derivation of the structural kernel conclusion

Suppose in a reference quotient

\[
p=p^*=p^2,
\qquad
pA=0,
\qquad
A-\delta(1-p)=\sum_jD_j^*D_j,
\qquad
A=C^*C\ge0.
\]

The three exact identities have finite propagation bounded by

\[
W=
\max\{2r_p,\ r_p+2r_C,\ 2r_D\}.
\]

Relation agreement through `2W` transfers them by the preceding lemma. Since `A=A^*` and `pA=0`, also `Ap=0`, so `q=1-p` reduces `A`. On `q` the SOS identity gives

\[
qAq\ge\delta q.
\]

Therefore `ker A=p` exactly and

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\|C\|_{1,op}^2].
\]

The projection is the finite-support algebraic element `p` itself, so its propagation is `r_p` and does not inherit a `\sqrt{M/\delta}` Chebyshev cost.

**Verdict on the dependency core of `P25-STRUCTURAL-KERNEL-PORTABILITY-51`: PASS.**

The audit does **not** infer that such a structural certificate exists for a given exotic matrix. P62B proves that it cannot exist for the PSZ operator `Q^*Q` with any `δ>0`.

# 9. Required kernel operator-norm / decoupling audit

**AUDIT_FOR=P04 dependency chain**  
**Primary source:** Chiraag Kaushik, Justin Romberg, Vidya Muthukumar, *A general technique for approximating high-dimensional empirical kernel matrices*, arXiv:2511.03892v1 (2025), current PDF `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.03892`.

I checked the proof directly rather than importing a P04 status label.

The off-diagonal kernel matrix is first written as a matrix-valued U-statistic. The paper defines the decoupled matrix `\widetilde\Delta` with an independent copied sample and obtains

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|\Delta\|
\le8\,\mathbb E\|\widetilde\Delta\|.
}
\]

Only **after** that decoupling does the proof condition on the original sample `(x_1,...,x_n)`. Under that conditioning the summands `Z_j`, each depending on the independent copy `\tilde x_j`, are independent. Noncommutative Khintchine is then applied to `\sum_j Z_j`.

The exact conditional variance computation before relaxation is

\[
\boxed{
V=
\frac14
\left\|
\sum_j
\left(
\bar G_{\setminus j}
+
\sum_{i\ne j}\bar G_{ii}e_je_j^T
\right)
\right\|.
}
\]

Thus both the leave-one-out correlation term and the diagonal row-sum term are genuinely present; neither may be dropped before a separate bound.

**Verdict:** `P01-AUDIT-P04-KRM-DECOUPLING-62 = PASS`, limited to the chain

`U-statistic -> decouple (factor 8) -> condition -> copied summand independence -> NCK -> two-term variance`.

# 10. P62E — exact logical correction on soft spectrum

A recurring temptation is to say

> `0` non-isolated in `σ_A(D)` ⇒ `1_{0}(D)∉A`.

That implication is false for a general ambient C*-algebra `A`.

Let

\[
\mathcal A=\mathcal K(\ell^2)+\mathbb C1
\]

and let

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1/2,1/3,\dots)\in\mathcal K(\ell^2)\subset\mathcal A.
\]

Then

\[
\sigma(D)=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\ge1\},
\]

so zero is not isolated, but

\[
P=1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]

is the rank-one first-coordinate projection and belongs to `K(ℓ²)⊂A`.

What *is* true is:

- `0` non-isolated implies `P∉C^*(D,1)`;
- continuous or polynomial cutoffs in the single variable `D` cannot converge in norm to `P`;
- heat/resolvent cutoffs fail in norm when positive spectrum tends to zero.

This distinction matters for P01. P62D's non-membership theorem for the PSZ kernel projection is not deduced from soft spectrum; it uses the scalar trace theorem on the amenable subgroup and the subgroup conditional expectation.

Accordingly, the overstrong phrase from P61 that soft spectrum *alone* eliminates all possible ambient reduced-algebra membership is withdrawn. P61A–C's exact spectral and functional-calculus calculations remain **PROVED**.
