# 4. Proof of C1 — exact four-dimensional Green-overlap divergence

Fix `a in Z^4`. Let `p_n(a)=P_0(X_n=a)` for nearest-neighbor simple random walk.

## 4.1 Exact convolution identity

All summands below are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_yG(y)G(y-a)
&=
\sum_y\sum_{r\ge0}\sum_{s\ge0}p_r(y)p_s(y-a)\\
&=
\sum_{r,s\ge0}\sum_yp_r(y)p_s(a-y).
\end{aligned}
\]

By symmetry `p_s(y-a)=p_s(a-y)` and Chapman--Kolmogorov,

\[
\sum_yp_r(y)p_s(a-y)=p_{r+s}(a).
\]

For each `n=r+s` there are exactly `n+1` ordered pairs `(r,s)`, so

\[
\boxed{
\sum_yG(y)G(y-a)=\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)p_n(a).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The same identity holds for partial sums before any limiting argument.

## 4.2 The periodic local central limit constant in `Z^4`

The characteristic function of one step is

\[
\phi(\theta)
=\frac14\sum_{j=1}^4\cos\theta_j.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Fourier inversion gives

\[
p_n(a)=\frac1{(2\pi)^4}
\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}\phi(\theta)^n e^{-i\theta\cdot a}\,d\theta.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The only points where `|phi|=1` are

\[
0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\boldsymbol\pi=(\pi,\pi,\pi,\pi).
\]

Near zero,

\[
\phi(\theta)=1-\frac{|\theta|^2}{8}+O(|\theta|^4),
\tag{4.4}
\]

while near `boldsymbol pi`, writing `theta=boldsymbol pi+u`,

\[
\phi(\boldsymbol\pi+u)
=-1+\frac{|u|^2}{8}+O(|u|^4).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Away from small neighborhoods of these two points, `|phi|<=1-epsilon` for some `epsilon>0`, so that part of (4.3) is exponentially small.

At zero the Gaussian saddle contribution is

\[
\frac1{(2\pi)^4}\int_{\mathbb R^4}e^{-n|u|^2/8}\,du
=
\frac1{(2\pi)^4}\left(\frac{8\pi}{n}\right)^2
=
\frac4{\pi^2n^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

At `boldsymbol pi`, the phase contributes

\[
(-1)^n e^{-i\pi(a_1+\cdots+a_4)}
=(-1)^{n+|a|_1}.
\]

Simple random walk reaches `a` at time `n` exactly only when `n` and `|a|_1` have the same parity. On that parity subsequence the second saddle has the same sign and the same Gaussian mass as (4.6); on the incompatible parity subsequence `p_n(a)=0` exactly. Standard dominated Laplace expansion of (4.3) therefore yields, for compatible parity,

\[
\boxed{
p_n(a)=\frac8{\pi^2n^2}+o(n^{-2}).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 4.3 Summation

On compatible parity,

\[
(n+1)p_n(a)=\frac8{\pi^2n}+o(n^{-1}).
\]

Exactly one of the two parity classes is present. Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\n\equiv |a|_1\ (2)}}\frac1n
=\frac12\log N+O(1).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Combining (4.7) and (4.8), with an `epsilon` comparison on the `o(n^{-1})` term,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\le N}(n+1)p_n(a)
\sim\frac4{\pi^2}\log N.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

This proves (0.4)--(0.5), in particular

\[
\sum_yG(y)^2=\infty.
\]

Therefore the exact square-summability hypothesis used in the source's `d>=5` Lemma 8.2 fails in `d=4`.

# 5. Proof of C2 — the finite-horizon replacement is still logarithmic

The obvious attempted repair is to replace `G` and `q_x` by quantities truncated at time `t`. This section shows why that does not recover a small-overlap regime at the natural four-dimensional spacing.

## 5.1 Exact truncated convolution

Define

\[
G_t(a)=\sum_{r=0}^t p_r(a).
\]

Again by Tonelli and Chapman--Kolmogorov,

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_t(a)
&:=\sum_yG_t(y)G_t(y-a)\\
&=\sum_{r=0}^t\sum_{s=0}^t p_{r+s}(a)\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{2t}c_{n,t}p_n(a),
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where the number of pairs `(r,s) in {0,...,t}^2` with `r+s=n` is

\[
c_{n,t}=\begin{cases}
n+1,&0\le n\le t,\\
2t-n+1,&t<n\le2t.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular

\[
K_t(a)\ge\sum_{n=0}^{t}(n+1)p_n(a).
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 5.2 Comparison with finite-time hitting probabilities

Let

\[
q_{x,t}(v)=\mathbb P_v(T_x\le t).
\]

If the walk hits `x` by time `t`, it contributes at least one visit to `x` by time `t`, so

\[
q_{x,t}(v)\le G_t(v-x).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Conversely, condition on the first hitting time `T_x`. On `{T_x\le t}`, the expected number of visits to `x` from time `T_x` through time `t` is at most the expected total number of visits to `x` after a start at `x`, namely

\[
g:=G(0)<\infty.
\]

Thus

\[
G_t(v-x)\le g\,q_{x,t}(v).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Multiplying the inequalities for `x` and `z`, summing over `v`, and using (5.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
g^{-2}K_t(z-x)\le Q_t(x,z):=\sum_vq_{x,t}(v)q_{z,t}(v)\le K_t(z-x).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

## 5.3 Heat-kernel bounds

The periodic local central limit theorem yields constants `c_i,C_i>0` such that for parity-compatible `n` and all sufficiently large `n`, uniformly whenever `|a|<=c_1\sqrt n`,

\[
p_n(a)\ge c_2n^{-2}\exp\left(-C_2\frac{|a|^2}{n}\right),
\tag{5.7}
\]

while the standard Gaussian upper bound gives for all `n>=1`

\[
p_n(a)\le C_3n^{-2}\exp\left(-c_3\frac{|a|^2}{n}\right).
\tag{5.8}
\]

### Upper bound

Using `c_{n,t}<=n+1` for `n<=t` and `c_{n,t}<=2t-n+1<=n+1` up to an immaterial factor on the remaining range, (5.8) yields

\[
K_t(a)
\le C\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{2t}\frac1n
\exp\left(-c\frac{|a|^2}{n}\right)\right].
\tag{5.9}
\]

Split at `n=|a|^2`. The part `n>|a|^2` is bounded by

\[
C\left[1+\log\left(1+\frac{t}{1+|a|^2}\right)\right].
\]

For `n<=|a|^2`, compare the sum with the integral after the substitution `u=|a|^2/n`; the exponential factor makes it uniformly bounded. Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_t(a)\le C\left[1+\log\left(1+\frac{t}{1+|a|^2}\right)\right].
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

### Lower bound at the `t^{1/4}` scale

Choose `A` large enough that (5.7) gives

\[
p_n(a)\ge c n^{-2}
\tag{5.11}
\]

whenever `n>=A|a|^2`, `n` has the correct parity, and `n` is sufficiently large. By (5.3),

\[
K_t(a)
\ge c\sum_{\substack{A|a|^2\le n\le t\\n\equiv |a|_1\ (2)}}\frac1n.
\tag{5.12}
\]

If

\[
|a|\le c_0t^{1/4},
\]

then

\[
A|a|^2\le Ac_0^2t^{1/2}.
\]

Taking `c_0` fixed sufficiently small and `t` large, the parity-restricted harmonic sum in (5.12) satisfies

\[
\sum_{A|a|^2\le n\le t,\ n\equiv |a|_1(2)}\frac1n
\ge c'\log t.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{K_t(a)\ge c''\log t}
\tag{5.13}
\]

for `1<=|a|<=c_0t^{1/4}` and `t` large. Equations (5.6), (5.10), and (5.13) prove C2.

The significance is precise. The source's `d>=5` proof makes pair influence small by separating holes until the Green overlap decays. In `d=4`, even a time-truncated version has order `log t` overlap at distances no larger than the natural `t^{1/4}` scale. Thus the same positive-influence decorrelation mechanism cannot simply be time-truncated and reused.
