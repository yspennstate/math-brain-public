# 3. `P01-P77A-W12-GAUGE-CLOSURE` -- **PROVED**

## Statement

Let `delta_j` be the closed `L^2(A,tau)` generator of the one-parameter subgroup in coordinate `j`, normalized by
\[
\delta_j(\lambda_g)=i\ell_j(g)\lambda_g.
\]
For matrices use the tensor extension and the trace `Tr_N \otimes tau`. Define
\[
\mathcal W^{1,2}_N
=
M_N(A)\cap\operatorname{Dom}(\delta_1)\cap\operatorname{Dom}(\delta_2),
\]
where the generator domains are taken in `L^2(M_N(A),Tr_N\otimes tau)`.

If
\[
U\in U_N(M_N(A))\cap\mathcal W^{1,2}_N,
\]
then for `j=1,2`
\[
\kappa_j(U)
:=\frac1i(\operatorname{Tr}_N\otimes\tau)(U^*\delta_jU)
\]
is an integer, and it is exactly the `j`th coordinate of `[U]` in Pooya's basis:
\[
\boxed{
[U]=\kappa_1(U)[u_{s_1}]+\kappa_2(U)[u_{s_2}].
}
\]
If `v_1,v_2` are the inherited topological generators with
\[
\mu_1^G(v_j)=[u_{s_j}],
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\mu_1^G\bigl(\kappa_1(U)v_1+\kappa_2(U)v_2\bigr)=[U].
}
\]

This is a fixed-group extension/corollary assembled from standard torus smoothing, resolvent functional calculus, the P75 smooth pairing, and Pooya's known computation. **No literature novelty is claimed.**

## Proof

### 3.1 Sobolev product rule

For bounded `a,b` in the `L^2` domain of `delta_j`, the automorphism difference quotient gives
\[
\frac{\alpha_t(ab)-ab}{t}
=
\frac{\alpha_t(a)-a}{t}\alpha_t(b)
+a\frac{\alpha_t(b)-b}{t}.
\]
Because `alpha_t(b) -> b` in operator norm and multiplication by a bounded operator is continuous on `L^2`, passage to the `L^2` limit yields
\[
\delta_j(ab)=(\delta_j a)b+a\delta_jb.
\]
Similarly `delta_j(a^*)=(\delta_j a)^*`.

### 3.2 Torus smoothing is simultaneous norm/graph approximation

Choose a nonnegative smooth approximate identity `rho_epsilon` on `T^2` and set
\[
a_\varepsilon
=
\int_{\mathbb T^2}\rho_\varepsilon(t)\alpha_t(U)\,dt.
\]
Compact-group convolution gives a smooth vector `a_epsilon`. Strong continuity of `alpha` on `A` gives
\[
\|a_\varepsilon-U\|\to0.
\]
Since `U` lies in the `L^2` domain of each generator, convolution commutes with the closed generator and is an approximate identity in `L^2`, hence
\[
\|\delta_j a_\varepsilon-\delta_jU\|_2\to0.
\]

For small `epsilon`, `a_epsilon` is invertible. Put
\[
h_\varepsilon=a_\varepsilon^*a_\varepsilon.
\]
Then
\[
\|h_\varepsilon-1\|\to0,
\]
so after shrinking `epsilon` there is a fixed `m>0` with
\[
h_\varepsilon\ge m1.
\]
Also, by the product rule,
\[
\delta_jh_\varepsilon
=(\delta_ja_\varepsilon)^*a_\varepsilon
+a_\varepsilon^*\delta_ja_\varepsilon.
\]
Using norm convergence of `a_epsilon`, `L^2` convergence of its derivative, and
\[
(\delta_jU)^*U+U^*\delta_jU
=\delta_j(U^*U)=0,
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{\|\delta_jh_\varepsilon\|_2\to0.}
\]

### 3.3 The missing polar-correction estimate

For a positive invertible `h>=m1`,
\[
h^{-1/2}
=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}(h+t)^{-1}\,dt
\]
in operator norm. The resolvent identity and the Sobolev product rule give
\[
\delta_j((h+t)^{-1})
=-(h+t)^{-1}(\delta_jh)(h+t)^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\delta_j(h^{-1/2})
=-\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
 t^{-1/2}(h+t)^{-1}(\delta_jh)(h+t)^{-1}\,dt
\]
in `L^2`. Since `||(h+t)^{-1}|| <= (m+t)^{-1}`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\delta_j(h^{-1/2})\|_2
&\le
\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty
\frac{t^{-1/2}}{(m+t)^2}\,dt\;\|\delta_jh\|_2\\
&=
\boxed{\frac1{2m^{3/2}}\|\delta_jh\|_2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
(The scalar integral is `Beta(1/2,3/2)/(pi m^(3/2)) = 1/(2m^(3/2))`.)

Apply this to `h=h_epsilon`. Define the polar unitary
\[
b_\varepsilon
=a_\varepsilon h_\varepsilon^{-1/2}.
\]
The smooth-vector algebra for a compact Lie-group action is stable under holomorphic/smooth functional calculus on positive invertibles, so `b_epsilon` is a smooth unitary. Moreover,
\[
\|b_\varepsilon-U\|\to0.
\]
For the derivatives,
\[
\delta_jb_\varepsilon
=(\delta_ja_\varepsilon)h_\varepsilon^{-1/2}
+a_\varepsilon\delta_j(h_\varepsilon^{-1/2}).
\]
Now `h_epsilon^{-1/2}->1` in norm, the first term converges to `delta_jU` in `L^2`, and the boxed estimate plus `delta_jh_epsilon->0` in `L^2` makes the second term tend to zero. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\|b_\varepsilon-U\|\to0,
\qquad
\|\delta_jb_\varepsilon-\delta_jU\|_2\to0.
}
\]
This is exactly the graph-norm bridge missing in P76.

### 3.4 Passage of the winding coordinates

For each small `epsilon`, P75 applies to the smooth unitary `b_epsilon` and gives
\[
[b_\varepsilon]
=\kappa_1(b_\varepsilon)[u_{s_1}]
 +\kappa_2(b_\varepsilon)[u_{s_2}],
\qquad
\kappa_j(b_\varepsilon)\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For sufficiently small `epsilon`, `||b_epsilon-U||<2`, so the two unitaries are norm-homotopic and
\[
[b_\varepsilon]=[U].
\]
Thus the two integer coordinates `kappa_j(b_epsilon)` are eventually constant, because Pooya's two basis classes are independent.

On the other hand, norm/graph convergence and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left| (\operatorname{Tr}_N\otimes\tau)(b_\varepsilon^*\delta_jb_\varepsilon)
-(\operatorname{Tr}_N\otimes\tau)(U^*\delta_jU)\right|\\
&\qquad\le
\|b_\varepsilon^*-U^*\|\,\|\delta_jb_\varepsilon\|_2
+\|\delta_jb_\varepsilon-\delta_jU\|_2
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\kappa_j(U)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\kappa_j(b_\varepsilon),
\]
and the right side is the eventually constant integer coordinate of `[U]`. This proves the theorem and the displayed assembly formula.

# 4. Move 26 boundary object: a nonsmooth infinite-support class

## `P01-P77B-CUSP-W12-ASSEMBLY-CLASS` -- **PROVED**

Define on the circle
\[
\psi(e^{i\theta})
=e^{i\theta}\exp\!\left(\frac i2|\sin\theta|\right),
\]
and in the frozen group algebra completion put
\[
U_{\rm cusp}=\psi(u_{s_1})u_{s_2}^{-2}.
\]
This is an explicit unitary in `C_r^*(G)`.

### 4.1 Why it is a genuine boundary example

The phase
\[
\phi(\theta)=\theta+\frac12|\sin\theta|
\]
is Lipschitz. Hence `psi` belongs to the first `L^2` Sobolev generator domain. It is not `C^1`: the derivative of `|sin theta|` jumps at integer multiples of `pi`. Therefore `psi` cannot be a finite Laurent polynomial, since every finite Laurent polynomial is smooth. Thus `U_cusp` is a genuine nonsmooth, infinite-Fourier-support representative, unlike the finite-support P73/P74 examples and the analytic P75 example.

### 4.2 Exact gauge coordinates

Away from the two cusp points (which are measure zero),
\[
\phi'(\theta)
=1+\frac12\cos\theta\,\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\theta).
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac1i\psi(e^{i\theta})^*\frac d{d\theta}\psi(e^{i\theta})
=\phi'(\theta).
\]
The derivative of `|sin theta|` has integral zero over a full circle, so
\[
\kappa_1(\psi(u_{s_1}))
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\phi'(\theta)\,d\theta
=1.
\]
The second generator acts trivially on the `s_1` functional-calculus factor and gives exponent `-2` on `u_{s_2}^{-2}`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{(\kappa_1(U_{\rm cusp}),\kappa_2(U_{\rm cusp}))=(1,-2).}
\]
P77A now gives
\[
\boxed{
[U_{\rm cusp}]=[u_{s_1}]-2[u_{s_2}]
}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\mu_1^G(v_1-2v_2)=[U_{\rm cusp}].
}
\]

### 4.3 Independent topological self-check

There is also a direct norm-continuous path, independent of the Sobolev pairing:
\[
H_t
=u_{s_1}\exp\!\left(\frac{it}{2}|\sin|(u_{s_1})\right)u_{s_2}^{-2},
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
It joins `u_{s_1}u_{s_2}^{-2}` to `U_cusp`, so it independently confirms
\[
[U_{\rm cusp}]=[u_{s_1}]-2[u_{s_2}].
\]
This is a **self-check**, not an OTHER-track audit.
