# 3. Move 14 — find the group acting: a local Bernoulli transposition

Write

\[
U=\bigoplus_{F_2}C_2,
\qquad
G=U\rtimes F_2.
\]

The abelian reduced algebra `C_r^*(U)` is naturally a commutative Cantor algebra. Concretely, each lamp involution `a_g` has spectral projections

\[
e_g^+=\frac{1+u_{a_g}}2,
\qquad
e_g^-=\frac{1-u_{a_g}}2.
\]

These commute because `U` is abelian. Under Fourier transform they are the indicator functions of the two cylinder conditions on the `g`-th Bernoulli coordinate.

Choose `s=s_1` and the cylinder projection

\[
p=e_e^+e_s^-.
\]

Conjugation by `u_s` shifts the lamp coordinates, so

\[
q=u_s p u_s^*=e_s^+e_{s^2}^-.
\]

The projections are orthogonal because `p` contains `e_s^-` while `q` contains `e_s^+`:

\[
pq=e_e^+e_s^-e_s^+e_{s^2}^-=0.
\]

Let

\[
v=u_s p.
\]

Then

\[
v^*v=p,
\qquad
vv^*=q.
\]

Because `p q=0`, the partial isometry goes from a cylinder to a disjoint translated cylinder. In particular

\[
v=qvp,
\qquad
v^2=0,
\qquad
(v^*)^2=0.
\]

With

\[
r=1-p-q
\]

we have `rv=vr=rv^*=v^*r=0`. Therefore

\[
t=v+v^*+r
=u_s p+p u_s^*+1-p-q
\]

satisfies

\[
t^*=t
\]

and

\[
t^2=vv^*+v^*v+r=q+p+r=1.
\]

Thus `t` is a self-adjoint involution in the **finite algebraic group ring** `C G`: `p` is a four-term lamp polynomial, `q` is its translate, and all terms above have finite support.

In transformation-groupoid language, `t` swaps two disjoint cylinder pieces by `s` and `s^{-1}` and fixes the complement. This is the concrete new representative mechanism found by move 14.

# 5. Move 20 — find why the obvious PV algorithm fails

A simpler idea would be to take a finite lamp projection `e` and try

\[
z_e=(1-e)+u_s e.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `e` commuted with `u_s`, this would be a block unitary: identity off `e` and the shift on `e`. The question is whether some nontrivial finite-support lamp projection can make (5.1) unitary without already being globally shift-invariant.

The answer is no.

## Proof of `P01-P71B`

Assume `z_e` is unitary. Relative to the decomposition `1=e+(1-e)`, the off-diagonal compression of `z_e^*z_e=1` gives

\[
(1-e)u_s e=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed the remaining terms lie in diagonal corners, while `(1-e)u_s e` is precisely the `(1-e,e)` corner. Thus

\[
u_s e=e u_s e.
\]

Put

\[
q=u_s e u_s^*.
\]

Equation (5.2) says `q<=e`. The canonical trace on `C_r^*(G)` is invariant under conjugation, so

\[
\tau(q)=\tau(e).
\]

Faithfulness of the trace on the abelian base algebra implies `q=e`. Thus unitarity forces

\[
u_s e u_s^*=e.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now use that `e in C[U]` has finite support. It lies in the group algebra of a finite elementary abelian `2`-subgroup, so under Fourier transform it is the characteristic function of a clopen cylinder depending on finitely many lamp coordinates. Let `S(e)` be its **minimal** finite coordinate-dependence set. Conjugation by `s` sends that set to `sS(e)`. From (5.3), minimality gives

\[
S(e)=sS(e).
\]

Left multiplication by the infinite-order element `s` has no nonempty finite orbit in `F_2`; hence the only finite `s`-invariant subset is the empty set. Therefore `S(e)=emptyset`, so `e` is constant. A constant projection is `0` or `1`.

This proves

\[
\boxed{z_e\ \text{unitary}\Longrightarrow e\in\{0,1\}.}
\]

Hence the one-cylinder algorithm cannot yield anything beyond `1` and `u_s`. The two-cylinder transposition of Sections 3–4 is not cosmetic: it is exactly what removes the shift-invariance obstruction.
