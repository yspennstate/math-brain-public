# 3. Finite matrices, reduced projections, and von Neumann dimensions remain distinct

Let
\[
C\in M_{u,v}(\mathbb CG),
\qquad D=C^*C\ge0.
\]
The von Neumann spectral projection
\[
P^{\mathrm{vN}}=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]
always exists in `M_v(L(G))`, and
\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_G)(P^{\mathrm{vN}})
\]
is the von Neumann dimension of the kernel.

This alone does **not** imply
\[
P^{\mathrm{vN}}\in M_v(C_r^*(G)).
\]
A sufficient reduced-`C^*` condition is genuine isolation of zero in the spectrum of `D` computed in `M_v(C_r^*(G))`; continuous functional calculus then puts
\[
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]
in the reduced matrix algebra and gives a genuine reduced `K_0` class.

Thus the following three statements remain logically separate:

1. `C` is a finite group-ring matrix;
2. its kernel has a specified von Neumann dimension;
3. its kernel projection is a reduced-`C^*` projection defining a class in `K_0(C_r^*(G))`.

No step in this report collapses those distinctions.

# 4. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A^*>=0` in a unital `C^*`-algebra and suppose
\[
\sigma(A)\subseteq\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Since `0` is clopen in the spectrum, continuous functional calculus gives
\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).
\]

For `t>=0`, the scalar function `e^{-tx}` equals `1` at `x=0` and has supremum at most `e^{-t\delta}` on `[\delta,M]`. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\]

Similarly,
\[
r_\eta(x)=\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]
equals `1` at zero, while on `[\delta,M]`
\[
0\le r_\eta(x)\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` both satisfy the same gap condition with gap `\delta`. Their Moore–Penrose inverses satisfy
\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]
Because `AP_A=0` and `BP_B=0`,
\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]
so
\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
Interchanging `A,B` gives
\[
\|(1-P_B)P_A\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
Since the difference of the projections is controlled by these two off-diagonal defects,
\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]
(The sharper factor `1` from the standard two-projection identity is compatible with this requested sufficient estimate.)

Finally take a positive `A` with nonzero kernel and define
\[
B=A+\varepsilon I.
\]
Then `\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0`, but `\ker B=0`, so `P_B=0` while `P_A\neq0`. The smallest positive spectral value of `B` is `\varepsilon`; there is no common gap bounded below independently of `\varepsilon`. This exactly exposes the shrinking-gap failure.

The seed is a **sufficient norm-plus-uniform-gap hypothesis**. It is not a proof that any group approximants possess such a common gap.

## 5.1 Geometry and the full `C_4` critical tetrahedra

Put
\[
C=\langle a\rangle\cong C_4,
\qquad c=a^2,
\qquad B=\langle a,t\rangle\cong C_4\times\mathbb Z.
\]
Use the same genuine circle action as in the retained depth-two work:
\[
a:x\mapsto x+1,
\qquad t:x\mapsto x+\tfrac12,
\qquad b:x\mapsto-x
\]
on `S_4=\mathbb R/4\mathbb Z`, together with the Bass–Serre tree of the HNN decomposition with vertex group `B` and the half-height coordinate. The orbit map is `1`-Lipschitz for the `\ell^1` product metric, so
\[
|g|_{\Gamma_4}
\ge
 d_{S_4}(0,\rho(g)0)
+d_T(v_0,gv_0)
+\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every integer `h`, the four vertices
\[
\mathcal O_h=\{a^jt^h:0\le j<4\}
\]
form a simplex of `P_2\Gamma_4`, because the diameter of `C_4` in its cyclic word metric is `2`.

We need the exact borderline word length
\[
|a^2t^{\pm1}|=3.
\tag{5.2}
\]
The displayed word gives the upper bound. The affine circle/height estimate gives a lower bound `2`, so only a hypothetical word of length two must be excluded. Height `\pm1` forces exactly one `t^{\pm1}` letter. If the other letter is `a^{\pm1}`, the element inside the abelian vertex group `B` has `a`-exponent `\pm1`, not `2`. If the other letter is `b^{\pm1}`, the HNN normal-form theorem shows that a reduced word with one stable letter cannot represent an element of the vertex group `B`. Hence length two is impossible, proving `(5.2)`.

Fix an arbitrary vertex `y` and compare it with `\mathcal O_h`. Put
\[
\tau=d_T(v_0,yv_0),
\qquad n=\nu(y)-h.
\]
As `j` varies, the circle coordinates of `(a^jt^h)^{-1}y` run through all integral translates of a point with fractional part `n/2`. Therefore
\[
\max_j d_{S_4}(0,\rho((a^jt^h)^{-1}y)0)
=
\begin{cases}
2,&n\text{ even},\\
3/2,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Equation `(5.1)` gives
\[
\max_jd(a^jt^h,y)
\ge
\begin{cases}
2+\tau+|n|/2,&n\text{ even},\\
3/2+\tau+|n|/2,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]
If `\tau>0` or `|n|>=2`, `(5.3)` is at least `3` unless `y` is already in the horizontal orbit. In the remaining case `\tau=0, |n|=1`, one has `y=a^rt^{h\pm1}`; choosing `j=r-2` produces the difference `a^2t^{\pm1}`, whose length is `3` by `(5.2)`. Thus no outside vertex can enlarge `\mathcal O_h` inside `P_2\Gamma_4`.

Hence every `\mathcal O_h` is a maximal tetrahedron.

Now suppose a simplex is stabilized by a conjugate of the full `C_4`. Translate so that `C_4` itself stabilizes it. If `h` is a vertex, its full four-point orbit `Ch` lies in the simplex. In particular `h` and `ch` are at distance at most `2`. Applying `(5.1)` to `h^{-1}ch`, the circle term is already exactly `2`; therefore the Bass–Serre tree term must vanish. Since the HNN edge groups are torsion-free and `c` is nontrivial torsion, `\operatorname{Fix}_T(c)=\{v_0\}`, so `h\in B`. Writing `h=a^rt^n` and using `[a,t]=1`, the orbit `Ch` is precisely the horizontal tetrahedron `\mathcal O_n`.

Therefore the full-`C_4` critical simplices are exactly the translates of the maximal horizontal tetrahedra `\mathcal O_h`. There is one `\Gamma_4`-orbit of them because left multiplication by `t` shifts `h` by one.

## 5.4 Proper-family Wang detector is nonzero for `C_4`

Let
\[

\]
Every proper subgroup of `C_4` lies in `N`, and `C_4/N\cong C_2`. Inflate a free contractible `C_2`-CW complex along the quotient `C_4\to C_2`; it is a model for `E_{\mathcal P_C}C_4`, where `\mathcal P_C` is the proper-subgroup family.

E^2_{p,q}=H_p(C_2;K_q^N(pt))

with trivial conjugation action on `R(N)`. Hence
\[
E^2_{1,0}=R(N)/2R(N).

K_1^{\{1\}}(pt)
\to
K_1^{C_4}(E_{\mathcal P_C}C_4)
\oplus K_1^{\mathbb Z}(E\mathbb Z)
\to
K_1^H(E_{\mathcal P_H}H).

## 5.5 The first full-isotropy tetrahedron attachment is K0-injective

By equivariant excision and induction, the relative group for attaching the single orbit of full horizontal tetrahedra is
\[
K_*^{\Gamma_4}(X,Y)
\cong
K_*^{C_4}(\Delta^3,\partial\Delta^3).
\tag{5.8}
\]

The centered tangent representation of the regular `C_4` tetrahedron is
\[
\mathbb R[C_4]-\mathbb R
\cong
\mathbb R_{\varepsilon}\oplus W_{\mathbb R},
\]
where `\varepsilon` is the real sign representation with kernel `N=C_2`, and `W` is a one-dimensional complex character representation. Complex equivariant Bott periodicity reduces the parity question to the sign-line disk pair. Its long exact sequence contains
\[
K_1^{C_4}(D(\mathbb R_\varepsilon),S(\mathbb R_\varepsilon))
\longrightarrow
R(N)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}_N^{C_4}}
R(C_4).
\tag{5.9}
\]

If `1,\eta` are the two characters of `N` and `\chi` generates the characters of `C_4`, then
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_N^{C_4}(1)=1+\chi^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ind}_N^{C_4}(\eta)=\chi+\chi^3.
\]
These images have disjoint irreducible supports, so induction is injective. Thus
\[
\boxed{
K_1^{C_4}(\Delta^3,\partial\Delta^3)=0.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The pair exact sequence for `(X,Y)` now gives an injection
\[
K_0^{\Gamma_4}(Y)
\hookrightarrow
K_0^{\Gamma_4}(X).
\tag{5.11}
\]
Combining `(5.7)` and `(5.11)` proves
