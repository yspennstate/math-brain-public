The theorem proved below is a deliberately scoped discrete finite-Rips control result used to attack one proof-side bottleneck inside the full program.

\[

\[

\]

\[

\[

This run resolves that question for every odd prime `p>=7`.

## `P01-P54A-PRIME-FIRST-ISOTROPY-MAXIMAL-SIMPLEX` — **PROVED**

For every odd prime `p`, with `d=(p-1)/2`, every nonfree simplex of

\[
P_d\Gamma_p
\]

is a left translate of the single `p`-vertex simplex

\[
C=\{1,a,\ldots,a^{p-1}\},
\qquad C=\langle a\rangle\cong C_p.
\]

That simplex is maximal. Its proper faces have trivial stabilizer. Thus if `X=P_d\Gamma_p` and `X^-` is obtained by deleting the interiors of the `\Gamma_p`-orbit of these maximal `p`-vertex simplices, then `X^-` is a free cocompact `\Gamma_p`-subcomplex and

\[
(X,X^-)
\cong
\Gamma_p\times_C(\Delta^{p-1},\partial\Delta^{p-1})
\]

as a proper equivariant CW pair.

The proof uses a new three-coordinate calibration: a circle action, the Bass–Serre tree of a second HNN decomposition, and the stable-letter height.

## `P01-P54B-EVEN-REPRESENTATION-ATTACHMENT-K0-INJECTIVITY` — **PROVED**

For the pair above,

\[
K_1^{\Gamma_p}(X,X^-)=0.
\]

Hence the inclusion induces an injection

\[
\boxed{
K_0^{\Gamma_p}(X^-)
\hookrightarrow
K_0^{\Gamma_p}(P_d\Gamma_p).
}
\]

Reason: the centered simplex is the unit ball in the reduced real regular representation of `C_p`; for odd `p` this is the underlying real representation of a complex vector space of dimension `(p-1)/2`. Equivariant Bott periodicity therefore puts the relative group in odd `K`-degree of the finite group `C_p`, which vanishes.

This gives a concrete answer to the newest P18 isotropy-separation question: arbitrary finite isotropy can kill a free-origin class, but the **first** isotropy attachment in this prime triangular family cannot kill this degree-zero source class.

## `P01-P54C-ODD-PRIME-EXACT-FULL-SOURCE-BARCODE` — **PROVED**

For every odd prime

\[
\boxed{p\ge7,}
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{2,p}=\frac{p+1}{2}.
}
\]

Equivalently, with `d=(p-1)/2`,

\[
q_{2,d,*}(z_{2,p})\ne0,
\qquad
q_{2,d+1,*}(z_{2,p})=0.
\]

The lower statement follows by transporting the already-validated nonzero free classifying-space/Wang class from `P_{d-1}\Gamma_p` into the free subcomplex `X^-` and then using P54B. The upper statement is the retained torus-product/cyclic-barcode certificate from P51, since the exact cyclic death scale is `\kappa_p=d`.

Thus, on every odd prime `p>=7`, the extra `+1` in the earlier upper bound is **not** a disposable triangulation artifact. It is a genuine equivariant finite-isotropy phenomenon:

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_p^{\partial}=\frac{p-1}{2}
\quad\text{but}\quad
\Delta_{2,p}=\frac{p+1}{2}.
}
\]

The first height boundary dies at the first isotropy scale, while the full scalar source class survives exactly one further Rips step.

### Exact limitations

- `p=5` is **not** included in P54C. # 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A>=0` in a unital `C^*`-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Since `{0}` is clopen in `\sigma(A)`, continuous functional calculus gives

\[
P_A=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).
\]

On the zero spectral point, both approximants below equal `1`; on `[\delta,M]` their absolute values are bounded by the endpoint values. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}
}
\]

and, for `\eta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now suppose `A,B>=0` both satisfy a common positive gap `\delta`. Their pseudoinverses obey

\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]

Because `AP_A=0` and `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

and similarly

\[
(1-P_B)P_A=B^\dagger(B-A)P_A.
\]

Hence

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,
\ \|(1-P_B)P_A\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

The elementary decomposition of `P_A-P_B` then gives the requested sufficient bound

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

The previously retained two-projection sharpening gives factor `1`; the requested factor `2` is already sufficient.

This is a **norm-plus-common-uniform-gap** theorem. It is not a proof that group approximants possess such a gap. The standard failure certificate remains exact: if `A` has nonzero kernel and `B=A+\varepsilon I`, then

\[
\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0
\]

but `B` has zero kernel. Its smallest positive spectral point is only `\varepsilon`, so the common-gap hypothesis collapses.

# 5. A second HNN decomposition and a calibrated 1-Lipschitz orbit map

## 5.1 HNN rewrite

From

\[
tbt^{-1}b^{-1}=a
\]

we obtain

\[
tb=abt,
\qquad
bt=ta^{-1}b,
\qquad
\boxed{btb^{-1}=ta^{-1}.}
\]

Therefore `\Gamma_p` is also the HNN extension with base

\[
B=\langle a,t\mid a^p=1,[a,t]=1\rangle
\cong C_p\times\mathbb Z,
\]

stable letter `b`, and associated infinite cyclic subgroups

\[
\langle t\rangle,
\qquad
\langle ta^{-1}\rangle.
\]

Let `T` be its Bass–Serre tree and let `v_0` be the vertex stabilized by `B`.

The associated edge groups are torsion-free. Hence for every `s` not divisible by `p`, the nontrivial torsion element `a^s` fixes `v_0` but fixes no edge incident to `v_0`. Since a tree fixed set is connected,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Fix}_T(a^s)=\{v_0\}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is the tree input that was absent from the earlier circle-only lower bound.

## 5.2 Circle action

Retain the exact P51 action on

\[
S_p=\mathbb R/p\mathbb Z
\]

with its circular metric:

\[
A(x)=x+1,
\qquad
B_0(x)=-x,
\qquad
T_0(x)=x+\frac12.
\]

It satisfies

\[
A^p=1,
\qquad
T_0AT_0^{-1}=A,
\qquad
T_0B_0T_0^{-1}=AB_0.
\]

Thus there is a homomorphism

\[
\rho:\Gamma_p\to\operatorname{Isom}(S_p)
\]

with `a->A`, `b->B_0`, `t->T_0`.

Conjugating a rotation by any circle isometry changes its exponent only by sign. Hence, for the circular residue

\[
|s|_p=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|s-kp|,
\]

\[
d_{S_p}(0,\rho(h^{-1}a^sh)0)=|s|_p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 5.3 Height coordinate

Let

\[
\nu:\Gamma_p\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\nu(t)=1,
\quad \nu(a)=\nu(b)=0.
\]

Consider

\[
\Phi(g)
=
\bigl(\rho(g)0,\ gv_0,\ \nu(g)/2\bigr)
\]

in the `\ell^1` product

\[
S_p\times T\times\mathbb R.
\]

For the generators:

- `a` moves the circle by `1`, tree by `0`, height by `0`;
- `b` moves the circle basepoint by `0`, the tree by `1`, height by `0`;
- `t` moves the circle by `1/2`, tree by `0`, height by `1/2`.

Thus every generator and inverse moves the product basepoint by at most `1`. Therefore `\Phi` is 1-Lipschitz from the word metric:

\[
\boxed{
|g|_{\Gamma_p}
\ge
 d_{S_p}(0,\rho(g)0)
+d_T(v_0,gv_0)
+\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This single inequality drives the critical-scale classification.

# 6. Proof of `P01-P54A-PRIME-FIRST-ISOTROPY-MAXIMAL-SIMPLEX`

## 6.1 Classify `C`-invariant simplices at scale `d`

Let `\sigma` be a nonempty `C`-invariant simplex of `P_d\Gamma_p`, and choose a vertex `h in \sigma`. Then the whole orbit

\[
Ch\subset\sigma
\]

has diameter at most `d`. In particular

\[
|h^{-1}a^sh|\le d
\qquad\text{for all }s.
\]

Choose `s` with

\[
|s|_p=d.
\]

By `(5.2)` the circle term in `(5.3)` for `h^{-1}a^sh` is exactly `d`. If `hv_0\ne v_0`, then `(5.1)` implies

\[
d_T(hv_0,a^shv_0)\ge1,
\]

so `(5.3)` would give

\[
|h^{-1}a^sh|\ge d+1,
\]

a contradiction. Hence `hv_0=v_0`, so

\[
h\in B=C_p\times\mathbb Z.
\]

Write `h=a^rt^n`. Since `a` and `t` commute,

\[
Ch=Ct^n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Now suppose `\sigma` contained two distinct `C`-orbits `Ct^n` and `Ct^m`, and put `k=m-n\ne0`. A cross-distance has the form

\[
|a^qt^k|.
\]

The tree term is zero and the height term is `|k|/2`.

If `k` is even, varying `q` makes the circle point range over all integer residues, so the maximal circle distance is `d`; since nonzero even `k` has `|k|/2>=1`, some cross-distance is at least `d+1`.

If `k` is odd, varying `q` makes the circle point range over all half-integer residues; because `p` is odd, one reaches circular distance

\[
\frac p2=d+\frac12.
\]

Together with `|k|/2>=1/2`, again a cross-distance is at least `d+1`.

Therefore no two distinct such orbits fit in a scale-`d` simplex. Since `C` acts transitively on each orbit, a nonempty invariant subset of one orbit is the whole orbit. Hence every `C`-invariant simplex is exactly

\[
Ct^n=t^nC,
\]

a left translate of the standard `p`-vertex simplex `C`.

## 6.2 The standard `C`-simplex is maximal

Suppose a vertex `h` can be adjoined to the standard simplex `C`. Then

\[
|a^{-s}h|\le d
\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb Z/p.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Put

\[
r_T=d_T(v_0,hv_0),
\qquad n=\nu(h),
\qquad x=\rho(h)0.
\]

The orbit of `0` under `\rho(\Gamma_p)` is the half-integer lattice modulo `p`. As `s` varies, the maximal circular distance of `A^{-s}x` is

\[
\begin{cases}
d,&x\in\mathbb Z/p,\\
d+\frac12,&x\in(\frac12+\mathbb Z)/p.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `r_T>=1`, `(5.3)` and `(6.3)` violate `(6.2)`. Thus `r_T=0` and `h in B`, say `h=a^rt^n`.

If `n` is nonzero even, the maximal circle term is `d` and the height term is at least `1`; if `n` is odd, the maximal circle term is `d+1/2` and the height term is at least `1/2`. Both contradict `(6.2)`. Hence `n=0`, so

\[
h\in C.
\]

Therefore the standard `C`-simplex is maximal, and so are all its translates.

## 6.3 All finite isotropy is of this form

Any finite subgroup of an HNN extension acts on its Bass–Serre tree with a global fixed vertex. Hence every finite subgroup of `\Gamma_p` is conjugate into a vertex group `B`. The only nontrivial finite subgroup of

\[
B\cong C_p\times\mathbb Z
\]

is `C_p`, because `p` is prime. Therefore every nontrivial finite subgroup of `\Gamma_p` is conjugate to `C`.

If a simplex has nontrivial stabilizer, translate it so that its stabilizer contains `C`; Section 6.1 then says the translated simplex is precisely a translate `t^nC`, hence the original simplex is a left translate of `C`.

A proper face of the standard `p`-vertex simplex cannot have nontrivial stabilizer: a nontrivial subgroup is `C_p` and its regular action on the vertices is transitive, so no nonempty proper subset is invariant.

Distinct left cosets of `C` are disjoint as vertex sets. Thus the nonfree simplices form one `\Gamma_p`-orbit of disjoint maximal simplices.

Let `X=P_d\Gamma_p` and delete those open maximal simplices while retaining their boundaries. The resulting `X^-` is a `\Gamma_p`-subcomplex. It is free, because any point stabilizer would stabilize the support simplex and hence force that simplex to be one of the deleted nonfree simplices. It is cocompact because a fixed-scale Rips complex of a finitely generated group has finitely many simplex orbits.

Finally the cell pair is exactly

\[
\boxed{
(X,X^-)
\cong
\Gamma_p\times_C(\Delta^{p-1},\partial\Delta^{p-1}).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This proves P54A.

# 7. Proof of `P01-P54B-EVEN-REPRESENTATION-ATTACHMENT-K0-INJECTIVITY`

The affine simplex `\Delta^{p-1}` has vertex set the regular `C_p`-set. Translate its barycenter to the origin. Its linear span is the reduced real regular representation

\[
\mathbb R[C_p]-\mathbb R.
\]

For odd prime `p`, this real representation decomposes into `(p-1)/2` oriented real two-planes. Equivalently it is the underlying real representation of the complex `C_p`-representation

\[
W=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{(p-1)/2}\chi^j,
\qquad
\dim_\mathbb C W=\frac{p-1}{2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
