## 3. Section-B move 1 — change the category: from the global crossed product to one cyclic subgroup

The first new-method attempt changes the question from “can a lamp decoration create a new global `K_1` direction?” to “what does assembly do to the canonical Bott class of the cyclic subgroup generated by a lamp-decorated word?”

Let

\[
U=\bigoplus_{F_2}C_2,
\qquad G=U\rtimes F_2,
\]

and choose `b in U`, `1 != w in F_2`. Put

\[
h=bw.
\]

Let `q:G->F_2` be the semidirect-product quotient. Then

\[
q(h)=w.
\]

Since `F_2` is torsion-free and `w != 1`, `w` has infinite order. If `h^k=1`, then `w^k=q(h^k)=1`, hence `k=0`. Therefore

\[
H_h:=\langle h\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `\beta_{H_h}` denote the canonical generator of `K_1^{top}(H_h)`. For subgroup inclusion `H_h -> G`, induction/naturality of coefficient-free assembly gives the commuting relation

\[
\mu_1^G\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{H_h}^G\beta_{H_h}\bigr)
=
( C_r^*(H_h)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(G))_*
\mu_1^{H_h}(\beta_{H_h}).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The cyclic assembly map sends `\beta_{H_h}` to the canonical implementing unitary, so

\[
\boxed{
\mu_1^G\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{H_h}^G\beta_{H_h}\bigr)=[u_h].
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Reduced membership is genuine here: for a subgroup of a discrete group the regular representation restricts as a direct sum of regular subgroup representations, so the canonical `C_r^*(H_h)->C_r^*(G)` map is isometric. This is the valid subgroup mechanism already isolated in P62C, not a quotient shortcut.

## 5. Section-B move 11 — dualize: quotient the lamp group away and check the coordinate independently

The previous proof used finite-order torsion cancellation. A separate dual check uses the quotient

\[
q:G=U\rtimes F_2\to F_2.

A reduced quotient map is not automatic. Here it is valid because `U` is amenable. Equivalently, the quasi-regular representation of `G` on `G/U` is weakly contained in the left regular representation. Hence there is a surjective reduced map

\[
q_r:C_r^*(G)\to C_r^*(F_2),
\qquad q_r(u_{bw})=u_w.
\tag{5.1}

The semidirect-product section `F_2 -> G` gives an isometric inclusion

\[
j_r:C_r^*(F_2)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(G),
\qquad q_r\circ j_r=\operatorname{id}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

On `K_1`, Pooya identifies `j_*([u_{s_1}]),j_*([u_{s_2}])` with a basis of the whole target `K_1(C_r^*(G))\cong\mathbb Z^2`. Thus `j_*` is an isomorphism and `q_*` is its inverse. Therefore

\[
q_*([u_{bw}])=[u_w]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[u_{bw}]=j_*[u_w],
\tag{5.3}
\]

which independently reproduces `(4.3)`.

This quotient use satisfies the historically killed-shortcut gate: the amenability/weak-containment hypothesis is explicitly verified. No marked-group convergence or unsupported reduced quotient is invoked.

**Outcome of move 11:** success as an independent coordinate calculation. It confirms that lamp decoration is invisible to the frozen degree-one `K_1` class.
