# 3. Proof of `P01-P73A-FINITE-SCALAR-UNITARY-QUOTIENT-CLASSIFICATION`

We prove every operator-algebraic and algebraic step used in (0.1)–(0.2).

## 3.1 The reduced quotient is legal because the lamp kernel is amenable

Write
\[
1\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow G\overset q\longrightarrow F_2\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
B=\bigoplus_{F_2}C_2.
\]
The subgroup `B` is abelian, hence amenable.

The representation of `G` obtained by pulling the left regular representation of `F_2=G/B` back through `q` is the quasi-regular representation
\[
\lambda_{G/B}=\operatorname{Ind}_B^G(1_B).
\]
Amenability of `B` gives
\[
1_B\prec\lambda_B.
\]
Induction preserves weak containment, hence
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_B^G(1_B)
\prec
\operatorname{Ind}_B^G(\lambda_B).
\]
The right-hand induced representation is equivalent to the regular representation `lambda_G`. Therefore
\[
\lambda_{F_2}\circ q\prec\lambda_G.
\]
By the universal characterization of the reduced norm, the algebraic quotient extends to a surjective reduced `*`-homomorphism
\[
\boxed{
q_r:C_r^*(G)\longrightarrow C_r^*(F_2).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
This is exactly the weak-containment hypothesis that was missing in historically killed quotient shortcuts; it is verified here, not assumed.

## 3.2 The section is isometric on reduced algebras

The semidirect product has a group section
\[
j:F_2\hookrightarrow G.
\]
For any discrete subgroup `H<=G`, restricting `lambda_G` to `H` decomposes `ell^2(G)` as a direct sum over the left `H`-orbits/cosets, each summand equivalent to `lambda_H`. Hence the inclusion of group rings is isometric for reduced norms and gives
\[
\boxed{
j_r:C_r^*(F_2)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(G).}
\tag{3.2}
\]
At the algebraic and reduced levels,
\[
q_r\circ j_r=\operatorname{id}_{C_r^*(F_2)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Consequently `q_* circ j_*=id` on `K_1`.

## 3.3 `q_*` is actually an isomorphism in degree one

Pooya's computation specializes to
\[
K_1(C_r^*(G))
 =\mathbb Z[j_r(u_{s_1})]\oplus\mathbb Z[j_r(u_{s_2})],
\tag{3.4}
\]
while the standard free-group computation gives
\[
K_1(C_r^*(F_2))
 =\mathbb Z[u_{s_1}]\oplus\mathbb Z[u_{s_2}].
\tag{3.5}
\]
The map `q_*` sends the basis in (3.4) to the basis in (3.5), because `q(j(s_k))=s_k`. Thus
\[
\boxed{
q_*:K_1(C_r^*(G))\overset\cong\longrightarrow K_1(C_r^*(F_2))
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
is an isomorphism, with inverse `j_*`.

This is stronger than merely knowing there is a split surjection: Pooya's explicit basis proves no additional kernel remains in degree one.

## 3.4 The quotient of a scalar finite-support unitary is a trivial group-ring unit

Let
\[
W\in\mathbb C G
\]
be unitary in `C_r^*(G)`. The canonical representation of the algebraic group ring in `C_r^*(G)` is faithful, so
\[
W^*W=WW^*=1
\]
holds already as a group-ring identity.

Apply the algebraic quotient
\[
q_{\rm alg}:\mathbb CG\longrightarrow\mathbb C F_2.
\]
Then `q_alg(W)` is a unit of `C[F_2]` and is in fact unitary under the group-ring involution.

Since `F_2` is a unique-product group, the classical trivial-unit theorem yields unique
\[
\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times,
\qquad w\in F_2
\]
with
\[
q_{\rm alg}(W)=\lambda w.
\tag{3.7}
\]
Unitary normalization implies
\[
1=(\lambda w)^*(\lambda w)=|\lambda|^2,
\]
so
\[
\lambda\in\mathbb T.
\tag{3.8}
\]
This proves the promised unique finite quotient normal form.

## 3.5 Convert the quotient normal form to the two `K_1` coordinates

Let `(m,n)` be the exponent sums of `s_1,s_2` in the reduced free word `w`, equivalently the image of `w` under
\[
F_2\longrightarrow F_2^{\rm ab}\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
In `K_1`, multiplication of unitaries is addition of classes. Hence
\[
[u_w]=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}].
\tag{3.9}
\]
The scalar unitary `lambda` contributes zero because `U(1)` is path connected: a path from `lambda` to `1` gives
\[
[\lambda u_w]=[u_w].
\tag{3.10}
\]
Therefore
\[
q_*([W])=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}].
\tag{3.11}
\]
Since `q_*` is injective by (3.6),
\[
\boxed{
[W]=m[j_r(u_{s_1})]+n[j_r(u_{s_2})]
}
\]
in `K_1(C_r^*(G))`. We henceforth suppress `j_r` from the notation, as in the preceding P01 reports. This proves (0.1).

## 3.6 Explicit assembly preimage

Pooya computes topological generators `v_1,v_2` for the degree-one assembly map with
\[
\mu_1^G(v_k)=[u_{s_k}].
\tag{3.12}
\]
By homomorphism property,
\[
\mu_1^G(mv_1+nv_2)
=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}]
=[W].
\tag{3.13}
\]
Thus (0.2) follows.

This completes the proof. No arbitrary coefficient algebra, no unverified reduced quotient, and no change of group or degree enters.
