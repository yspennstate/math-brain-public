## `P01-P55A-P5-INITIAL-SOURCE-AVOIDS-FIRST-ISOTROPY-INTERIORS` — **PROVED**

\[
\Gamma_5=\langle a,b,t\mid a^5=1,\ tat^{-1}=a,\ tbt^{-1}=ab\rangle,
\qquad C=\langle a\rangle\cong C_5,
\]
and put
\[
X=P_2\Gamma_5.
\]
By P54A, the nonfree simplices of `X` are exactly the left translates of the single five-vertex simplex `C`; each is maximal and every proper face is free. Let `X^-` be obtained by deleting the interiors of the `Gamma_5`-orbit of these maximal four-simplices.

\[

\[
Z=P_1C_5\times P_1\mathbb Z,

\[
\phi:Z\longrightarrow P_2A\subseteq P_2\Gamma_5,
\qquad (a^j,n)\mapsto a^jt^n.

\[
F:\Gamma_5\times_A Z\longrightarrow X.

\widetilde F:\Gamma_5\times_A Z\longrightarrow X^-.

\[
\widetilde z_{2,5}\ne0.

\boxed{z_{2,5}\ne0\text{ in }K_0^{\Gamma_5}(P_2\Gamma_5).}

This is exactly the missing initial-scale factorization; it does not use the unavailable predecessor `P_1\Gamma_5`.

\[
L=2^r\ge4,
\qquad d=L/2,
\]
and write
\[
\Gamma_L

Use the alternative HNN decomposition from P54 with vertex group
\[
B=\langle a,t\mid a^L=1,[a,t]=1\rangle\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z,

At the critical first-isotropy scale `d`, every nonfree simplex of
\[
P_d\Gamma_L

is a `\Gamma_L`-translate of a simplex whose vertices all lie in the HNN vertex group `B`.

Proof: after translating a nonfree simplex, its nontrivial finite stabilizer is a subgroup `K<=C_L`. Because `C_L` is a cyclic 2-group, every nontrivial `K` contains the unique involution
\[
c=a^{L/2}=a^d.
\]
If `h` is any vertex of the stabilized simplex, then `ch` is another vertex, so
\[
|h^{-1}ch|\le d.
\]
Retain the P54 1-Lipschitz calibration
\[
\Phi(g)=\bigl(\rho_L(g)0,gv_0,\nu(g)/2\bigr)
\in S_L\times T\times\mathbb R
\]
with the `ell^1` product metric. Conjugation of the half-turn `a^d` by any circle isometry is again the half-turn, so the circle contribution to `h^{-1}ch` is exactly `d`; its height contribution is zero. Hence
\[
|h^{-1}ch|
\ge
 d+d_T(hv_0,chv_0).
\]
The inequality `|h^{-1}ch|<=d` forces
\[
d_T(hv_0,chv_0)=0.
\]
The element `c` is nontrivial torsion, while every HNN edge stabilizer is infinite cyclic and torsion-free, so
\[
\operatorname{Fix}_T(c)=\{v_0\}.
\]
Therefore `hv_0=v_0`, i.e.
\[
h\in B.
\]
This holds for every vertex of the translated simplex.

Thus the entire critical singular locus is induced from the full subcomplex on the HNN vertex group:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sing}(P_d\Gamma_L)
=\Gamma_L\cdot\operatorname{Sing}(P_d\Gamma_L[B]),
}
\]
and, at the simplicial-set level,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Sing}(P_d\Gamma_L)
\cong
\Gamma_L\times_B
\operatorname{Sing}(P_d\Gamma_L[B]).
}
\]
Here `P_d\Gamma_L[B]` denotes the full subcomplex of the ambient Rips complex on the vertex set `B`; no unjustified replacement by the intrinsic product metric on `B` is made.

This does **not** solve the power-of-two barcode. It reduces the missing first-singular calculation from the full nonamenable semidirect product to one HNN vertex-group slice with inherited metric. The remaining issue is the internal overlap/nesting of the cyclic-isotropy cells inside that slice.

# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A>=0` in a unital `C^*`-algebra and assume
\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Since `{0}` is clopen in `\sigma(A)`, continuous functional calculus gives
\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).
\]
On the positive spectral interval,
\[
|e^{-t\lambda}|\le e^{-t\delta},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\]
Likewise
\[
\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}
\]
for `lambda>=delta`, hence
\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let `A,B>=0` have the same positive gap `delta`. Their Moore–Penrose inverses satisfy
\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]
Using `AP_A=BP_B=0`,
\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]
and symmetrically
\[
(1-P_B)P_A=B^\dagger(B-A)P_A.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,
\ \|(1-P_B)P_A\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
The elementary two-term decomposition gives the requested sufficient estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]
The previously retained two-projection identity sharpens the constant to `1`; the factor `2` above is enough for the requested seed.

This is a **norm-plus-common-uniform-gap** statement. It is not a proof of a gap for group approximants. If `A` has nonzero kernel and `B=A+\varepsilon I`, then `\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0` while `P_B=0`; the common gap collapses to zero.

# 4. Detailed proof of P55A and P55B

## 4.1 The exact P54 critical pair at `p=5`

Set
\[
\Gamma=\Gamma_5,
\qquad C=\langle a\rangle\cong C_5,
\qquad X=P_2\Gamma.
\]
The P54 HNN/circle/tree calculation applies to every odd prime, including `5`. Thus all nonfree simplices of `X` are the left translates of the maximal regular `C_5` simplex
\[
C=\{1,a,a^2,a^3,a^4\},
\]
and every proper face is free. If `X^-` is obtained by deleting the interiors of these maximal simplices, then
\[
(X,X^-)
\cong
\Gamma\times_C(\Delta^4,\partial\Delta^4)
\]
and `X^-` is free and cocompact.

The reduced real regular representation of `C_5` is the realification of
\[
\chi\oplus\chi^2,
\]
so complex equivariant Bott periodicity gives
\[
K_1^\Gamma(X,X^-)=0.
\]
Hence
\[
K_0^\Gamma(X^-)\hookrightarrow K_0^\Gamma(X).
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.2 The retained torus source has a two-dimensional controlled domain

Let
\[
A=C\times\langle t\rangle\cong C_5\times\mathbb Z.
\]
The retained source construction is
\[
x_{5,1}\boxtimes\eta
\in
K_0^A(P_1C\times P_1\mathbb Z),
\]
followed by the product-to-Rips map and induction `A->Gamma`.

Give
\[
Z=P_1C\times P_1\mathbb Z
\]
its standard product triangulation. Each factor is one-dimensional, so every simplex of `Z` has dimension at most two. The vertex map
\[
(a^j,n)\mapsto a^jt^n
\]
is simplicial into `P_2A`: if two product vertices occur in one product simplex, their `C`-coordinates differ by at most one Cayley edge and their integer coordinates differ by at most one, so the ambient word distance is at most two.

After induction, every target simplex met by the induced map has at most three support vertices. By contrast, every deleted open simplex of `X` has support on all five vertices of a translate of `C`. Interiors of distinct simplices in a simplicial complex are disjoint. Therefore the induced product map lands in `X^-`.

This produces the factorization class
\[
\widetilde z_{2,5}\in K_0^\Gamma(X^-)
\]
with
\[
i_*\widetilde z_{2,5}=z_{2,5}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 4.3 Nonvanishing in the free complement

Because `X^-` is free, choose its canonical equivariant classifying map
\[
c:X^-\to E\Gamma.
\]
The map `c` composed with the induced torus source is exactly the ordinary induced torus classifying construction defining the retained free source class. Hence
\[
c_*(\widetilde z_{2,5})=\bar z_{2,5}.
\]
The independently retained Wang detector gives
\[
\partial_W(\bar z_{2,5})=\pm i_*(\xi_5)\ne0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\widetilde z_{2,5}\ne0.
\]
Injectivity `(4.1)` and `(4.2)` imply
\[
z_{2,5}\ne0
\quad\text{in }K_0^\Gamma(P_2\Gamma).
\]

## 4.4 Exact death one step later

The exact cyclic barcode gives

\kappa_5=2.
\]
At scale `3`, the cyclic factor is already zero, and the standard product triangulation
\[
P_2C_5\times P_1\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow

therefore kills the torus product class. Induction to `Gamma_5` preserves zero. Thus

\quad\text{in }K_0^{\Gamma_5}(P_3\Gamma_5).

This proves P55A and closes the omitted edge case in P54C, yielding P55B.

# 5. Detailed proof of P55C: power-of-two critical singularity lies in one HNN vertex slice

Fix
\[
L=2^r\ge4,
\qquad d=L/2,
\qquad X=P_d\Gamma_L.
\]
Rewrite `Gamma_L` as the HNN extension with vertex group
\[
B=C_L\times\langle t\rangle
\]
and stable letter `b`, using
\[
btb^{-1}=ta^{-1}.
\]
The associated edge groups are infinite cyclic, hence torsion-free.

Let `T` be the Bass–Serre tree and `v_0` the vertex fixed by `B`. Let
\[
c=a^d.
\]
Then `c` is the unique involution of `C_L`. It fixes `v_0`; it fixes no edge, because edge stabilizers are torsion-free. Since a tree fixed set is connected,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Fix}_T(c)=\{v_0\}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Retain the P51/P54 circle action on
\[
S_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z
\]
with
\[
a:x\mapsto x+1,
\qquad b:x\mapsto-x,
\qquad t:x\mapsto x+1/2,
\]
and the height homomorphism `nu(t)=1`, `nu(a)=nu(b)=0`. The orbit map
\[
\Phi(g)=\bigl(\rho_L(g)0,gv_0,\nu(g)/2\bigr)
\]
into the `ell^1` product is 1-Lipschitz, so
\[
|g|
\ge
 d_{S_L}(0,\rho_L(g)0)
+d_T(v_0,gv_0)
+\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let `sigma` be a nonfree simplex of `X`. Its stabilizer is finite. By the HNN fixed-point theorem, after translating `sigma` we may assume its nontrivial stabilizer `K` lies in `C_L`. Every nontrivial subgroup of a cyclic 2-group contains the unique involution `c`.

For a vertex `h` of the translated simplex, both `h` and `ch` lie in that simplex, hence
\[
|h^{-1}ch|\le d.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Circle conjugacy preserves the half-turn, so
\[
d_{S_L}(0,\rho_L(h^{-1}ch)0)=d.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Also `nu(h^{-1}ch)=0`. Applying `(5.2)` to `(5.3)` and `(5.4)` gives
\[
d+d_T(hv_0,chv_0)\le d,
\]
so `hv_0` is fixed by `c`. By `(5.1)`, `hv_0=v_0`, hence `h in B`.

Thus every vertex of the translated simplex lies in `B`, proving the first assertion. Conversely, any nonfree simplex in the full subcomplex on `B` is of course nonfree in `X`. Left cosets of `B` partition the vertex set of `Gamma_L`, so the singular subcomplex is the induced simplicial complex stated in P55C.

### Exact limitation

The inherited metric on the vertex slice `B` is the **ambient `Gamma_L` word metric**. No claim is made that it equals the intrinsic product metric on `C_L x Z`. The remaining power-of-two problem is therefore to compute the finite-isotropy filtration of this inherited-metric slice, not to substitute the easier intrinsic torus Rips complex without proof.

# 6. Finite matrices, reduced projections, and von Neumann dimensions remain distinct

For
\[
C\in M_{u,v}(\mathbb CG),
\qquad D=C^*C,
\]
`C` and `D` are finite group-ring matrices. In the group von Neumann algebra the spectral projection
\[
P^{\rm vN}=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]
always exists, and
\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_G)(P^{\rm vN})
=\dim_{L(G)}\ker C.
\]
But
\[
P^{\rm vN}\in M_v(C_r^*(G))
\]
requires a direct reduced-`C^*` argument, for example isolation of zero in the reduced spectrum. Therefore:

- an exotic or irrational von Neumann kernel dimension is not by itself a reduced-`C^*` `K_0` counterexample;
- finite support of the matrix does not put its kernel projection in `C_r^*(G)`;
- the Li–Nowak–Pooya/Lück trace obstruction applies to the reduced-`C^*` class after the gap is established.

The classical lamplighter-supported exotic dimensions remain screened by P46: their hosts are scalar-Baum–Connes positive, so a forbidden trace there forces soft spectrum rather than a reduced-`C^*` kernel projection.

## Independent derivation

Let `K=C_q` act regularly on the vertices of `Delta^{q-1}`. Centering at the barycenter identifies the pair with
\[
(D(V_q),S(V_q)),
\qquad
V_q=\mathbb R[K]-\mathbb R.
\]

If `q` is odd, `V_q` is the realification of
\[
W_q=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{(q-1)/2}\chi^j,
\]
so equivariant complex Bott periodicity gives
\[
K_0^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))\cong R(K),
\qquad
K_1^K(D(V_q),S(V_q))=0.
\]

If `q=2r` is even, split
\[
V_q\cong\sigma\oplus(W_q)_\mathbb R,
\]
where `sigma` is the real sign line and `H=ker(sigma)=C_r`. Bott periodicity for the complex summand reduces the relative group to the sign-line pair. Since
\[
S(\sigma)\cong K/H,
\]
the pair exact sequence is
\[
0\to K_1^K(D\sigma,S\sigma)
\to R(H)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}_H^K}
R(K)
\to K_0^K(D\sigma,S\sigma)
\to0.
\]
If `eta_j` is the `j`-th character of `H`, then
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_H^K(\eta_j)=\chi^j+\chi^{j+r}.
\]
