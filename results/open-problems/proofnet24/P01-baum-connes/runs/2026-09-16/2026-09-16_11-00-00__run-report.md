The paper's Theorem 9, quoting Lück's modified trace theorem, says:

> If the coefficient-free Baum–Connes assembly map is surjective, then the canonical trace on its `K_0` image takes values in the subring `Lambda_G` generated by `Z` and inverses of orders of finite subgroups.

Thus if a finite group-ring matrix independently produces a reduced-C* kernel projection `P`, surjectivity of scalar assembly alone forces

\[
\tau_*([P])\in\Lambda_G.
\]

No `F_{n+1}` hypothesis is needed for this **general K-theory trace-range statement**.

Proposition 1 says: if `G` is of type `F_{n+1}`, the degree-`n` cohomological Laplacian

\[
\Delta_n\in M_{k_n}(C_r^*(G))

has zero isolated in its reduced spectrum, and the coefficient-free map

\[
K_0^G(\underline EG)\longrightarrow K_0(C_r^*(G))

is surjective, then

\[
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)\in\Lambda_G,

and torsion-free `G` has integer `\beta_n^{(2)}`. The preceding passage identifies the higher-Kazhdan projection trace with that `L^2`-Betti number.

The `F_{n+1}` assumption belongs to the production of the finite cohomological matrix. It is not silently transferred to an independently supplied finite matrix.

# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A` be positive in a unital matrix C*-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta\le M.
\]

Because `{0}` is clopen in `\sigma(A)`, the characteristic function of `{0}` is continuous on the spectrum. Hence

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the same C*-algebra.

For heat regularization, the scalar error is zero at `0` and at most `e^{-t\delta}` on the positive spectrum, so

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\]

For resolvent regularization,

\[
f_\eta(x)=\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

satisfies `f_eta(0)=1` and is decreasing for positive `x`, hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` satisfy the **same** positive-gap hypothesis

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Define the bounded pseudoinverse

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,
\qquad
A^\dagger(x)=x^{-1}\quad (x\ge\delta),
\]

so `||A^dagger||<=1/delta`. Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
A P_B=(A-B)P_B.
\]

Multiplying by `A^dagger` gives

\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

and therefore

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives

\[
\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

The elementary projection decomposition yields the user-requested sufficient bound

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

(The sharper factor `1` retained in earlier P01 reports is compatible with this.)

This is **not** a proof that a marked-group or quotient approximation possesses a common gap.

The failure mode is exposed by

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I.
\]

If `A` has nonzero kernel, then `||A-B||=varepsilon ->0`, while `B` has no kernel. The positive gap at zero has collapsed, so norm closeness alone does not control kernel projections.

# 4. Finite matrices, reduced projections, and von-Neumann dimensions remain distinct

For a finite matrix

\[
C\in M_{u,v}(\mathbb C G),
\qquad D=C^*C,
\]

the von-Neumann spectral projection

\[
P^{\mathrm{vN}}=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\]

always exists in `M_v(L(G))`, and

\[
\tau_v(P^{\mathrm{vN}})=\dim_{L(G)}\ker C.
\]

This does **not** imply

\[
P^{\mathrm{vN}}\in M_v(C_r^*(G)).
\]

A sufficient condition for reduced membership is that zero be isolated in the reduced spectrum of `D`; then continuous functional calculus produces

\[
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)\in M_v(C_r^*(G)).
\]

Accordingly:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite support}
\neq
\text{exotic vN kernel dimension}
\neq
\text{reduced-C* K}_0\text{ class}.
}
\]

The new Austin and Grabowski results below are precisely about the missing middle-to-right implication.

# 5. `P01-P61A-AUSTIN-COMB-EXACT-SPECTRUM` — PROVED

## 5.1 The finite graph

Fix `\ell>=1`. Let `A_\ell` denote the ordinary unweighted adjacency matrix of the path on `\ell` central vertices. Figure 1(a) in Austin's paper has:

- central path vertices `v_1,...,v_\ell`;
- two pendant leaves at every `v_i`;
- every positive edge weight equal to `2`.

Write a vector as

\[
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb C^\ell\oplus\mathbb C^\ell\oplus\mathbb C^\ell,
\]

where `x` is central and `y,z` are the two leaf layers. The weighted adjacency operator is exactly

\[
Q_\ell(x,y,z)
=
(2A_\ell x+2y+2z,\ 2x,\ 2x).
\tag{5.1}
\]

## 5.2 Antisymmetric leaf sector

On the `\ell`-dimensional subspace

\[
\{(0,y,-y):y\in\mathbb C^\ell\},
\]

we have

\[
Q_\ell(0,y,-y)=0.
\]

Thus `0` is an eigenvalue with multiplicity at least `\ell`; dimension count below shows exactly `\ell`.

## 5.3 Symmetric leaf sector

On the complementary symmetric sector write `y=z=w`. If

\[
A_\ell x=\mu x
\]

and `\lambda\ne0` is an eigenvalue of `Q_\ell`, then

\[
2\mu x+4w=\lambda x,
\qquad
2x=\lambda w.
\]

Eliminating `w` gives

\[
\lambda^2-2\mu\lambda-8=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\lambda_\pm(\mu)
=
\mu\pm\sqrt{\mu^2+8}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The path eigenvalues are the classical simple values

\[
\mu_k=2\cos\frac{k\pi}{\ell+1},
\qquad k=1,\ldots,\ell.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the **complete spectrum** is

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(Q_\ell)
=
\{0\}^{(\ell)}
\cup
\left\{
\mu_k\pm\sqrt{\mu_k^2+8}:
1\le k\le\ell
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

There are `\ell+2\ell=3\ell` eigenvalues counted with multiplicity, equal to the number of vertices, so no spectrum is missing.

## 5.4 Exact `4`-eigenvalue criterion

By (5.2), `\lambda_+(\mu)=4` iff

\[
16-8\mu-8=0,
\]

i.e.

\[
\mu=1.
\]

By (5.3), this occurs iff

\[
2\cos\frac{k\pi}{\ell+1}=1,
\]

which in the interval `(0,pi)` means

\[
\frac{k\pi}{\ell+1}=\frac\pi3.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
4\in\sigma(Q_\ell)
\iff
3\mid(\ell+1)
\iff
\ell\equiv2\pmod3,
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

and the multiplicity is one.

Austin's Lemma 4.2 states the sufficient subfamily `\ell\equiv-1 (mod 6)`. Equation (5.5) is an independent strengthening for the bare finite comb of Figure 1(a), not a contradiction: the paper only needs a sufficient congruence compatible with its active-class construction.

## 5.5 A positive eigenvalue quantitatively close to `4`

Assume `4` is an eigenvalue and set

\[
k_0=\frac{\ell+1}{3}.
\]

Take `k_0+1` (which is valid for every `\ell>=2` in this congruence class). Since the function `2 cos(theta)` is `2`-Lipschitz,

\[
|\mu_{k_0+1}-1|
\le
\frac{2\pi}{\ell+1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `\mu\in[-2,2]`,

\[
\lambda_+'(\mu)
=1+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{\mu^2+8}},
\]

so

\[
|\lambda_+'(\mu)|<2.
\]

The mean-value theorem and (5.6) give a distinct positive spectral value

\[
0<|\lambda_+(\mu_{k_0+1})-4|
\le
\frac{4\pi}{\ell+1}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

For

\[
D_\ell=(Q_\ell-4)^*(Q_\ell-4),
\]

we obtain the explicit positive eigenvalue

\[
\boxed{
0<\nu_\ell
\le
\frac{16\pi^2}{(\ell+1)^2}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This inverse-square scale is the quantitative seed for the global soft-spectrum theorem.

# 6. `P01-P61B-AUSTIN-IRRATIONAL-KERNEL-SOFT-SPECTRUM` — PROVED

## 6.1 Statement

Let `I` be any Austin parameter for which

\[
\alpha_I
:=
\dim_{L\Gamma_I}\ker(Q_I-4)
\]

is irrational. Put

\[
D_I=(Q_I-4)^*(Q_I-4).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
0\text{ is not isolated in }\sigma_{C_r^*(\Gamma_I)}(D_I).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

More precisely, there is a sequence of positive reduced spectral values

\[
\nu_j\downarrow0
\]

coming from active finite comb blocks, and each such value has positive von-Neumann spectral mass.

## 6.2 Irrational total trace forces infinitely many nonzero active blocks

Austin's Corollary 4.3 and Lemma 5.4 express the kernel dimension as

\[
\alpha_I
=
\sum_{i\ge1}\dim_{L\Gamma_I}h_{C_i},
\tag{6.2}
\]

where every nonzero summand is the Haar measure of a finite cylinder and hence is rational.

If only finitely many summands in (6.2) were nonzero, `\alpha_I` would be a finite sum of rational numbers, hence rational. Therefore irrationality implies infinitely many nonzero active classes.

## 6.3 Their central lengths are unbounded

Fix `L`. Up to left translation in the `4`-regular Cayley tree of `F_2`, there are only finitely many paths with at most `L` central vertices: after fixing one vertex at the identity, there are finitely many reduced edge-label words of bounded length.

For each such path `P`, the pattern `\psi` lives on the finite set `B(P,10)` and is binary, so there are only finitely many possible patterns. Hence there are only finitely many equivalence classes `C` with central size `\ell<=L`.

Since infinitely many classes contribute nontrivially, their central sizes satisfy

\[
\ell_j\longrightarrow\infty
\]

along a subsequence.

## 6.4 Each active block carries positive spectrum at inverse-square distance

Proposition 3.12 gives, on every active class,

\[
Q_I|_{H_C}
\cong
\operatorname{id}_{h_C}\otimes Q_{\ell}^{1,1}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because the class contributes to the `4`-eigenspace, `h_C\ne0` and `4` is an eigenvalue of the finite comb block. By P61A, that block also has a distinct eigenvalue whose squared distance from `4` obeys

\[
0<\nu_\ell\le\frac{16\pi^2}{(\ell+1)^2}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Tensoring its finite-dimensional eigenspace with `h_C` gives a spectral subspace for `D_I` of positive von-Neumann dimension. Therefore `\nu_\ell` is not merely an approximate spectral value: it occurs in the regular operator with positive spectral mass.

Along the unbounded active sequence,

\[
0<\nu_{\ell_j}
\le
\frac{16\pi^2}{(\ell_j+1)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\]

## 6.5 Reduced-spectrum conclusion

The left regular representation is faithful on `C_r^*(\Gamma_I)` by definition. Therefore the spectrum of `D_I` in the reduced C*-algebra equals its spectrum as the left-regular bounded operator.

The positive sequence above proves (6.1).

### Consequence for route (a)

The von-Neumann kernel projection

\[
P_I^{\mathrm{vN}}=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_I)
\]

exists in `L(\Gamma_I)` and has irrational trace, but zero is not isolated in the reduced spectrum. Hence continuous functional calculus does **not** put `P_I^{vN}` in `C_r^*(\Gamma_I)`.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Austin's irrational/transcendental family cannot be a reduced-gapped scalar BC counterexample.}
}
\]

This is stronger than the generic warning “irrational `L^2`-Betti numbers alone are insufficient”: it identifies the exact same-matrix spectral obstruction and gives the scale `O(\ell^{-2})`.

# 7. `P01-P61C-AUSTIN-FUNCTIONAL-CALCULUS-NORM-BARRIER` — PROVED

Fix an irrational Austin parameter `I`, let

\[
D=D_I,
\qquad
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)\in L(\Gamma_I).
\]

By P61B, `P\ne0` and there are positive spectral values `\nu_j\to0`.

For any continuous `f` on `\sigma(D)`, write `c=f(0)`. On the kernel,

\[
(f(D)-P)|_{\ker D}=(c-1)I.
\]

Along positive spectral values `\nu_j\to0`, continuity gives

\[
f(\nu_j)\to c,
\]

while `P=0` on those spectral spaces. Hence

\[
\|f(D)-P\|
\ge
\max\{|c-1|,|c|\}
\ge\frac12.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The constant function `f\equiv1/2` attains the bound, so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}(P,C^*(D,1))=\frac12.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

If `f(0)=1`, then the positive sequence forces

\[
\boxed{
\|f(D)-P\|\ge1.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

In particular, because the scalar multipliers tend to `1` as `\nu_j\downarrow0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tD}-P\|=1
\qquad(t>0),
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(D+\eta I)^{-1}-P\|=1
\qquad(\eta>0).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus the failure is maximal in operator norm; there is no hidden slow convergence.

Finally, no exact SOS gap identity

\[
D^2-\delta D=\sum_jB_j^*B_j,
\qquad\delta>0,
\tag{7.6}
\]

can hold in the group ring, because (7.6) would imply

\[
\sigma(D)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty)
\]

by the P59 spectral-polynomial argument, contradicting P61B.

This run does **not** claim a uniform positive approximate-SOS residual floor for the whole Austin family analogous to P60A's GLSZ constant; the exact no-gap/no-exact-SOS conclusion is what is proved.

The base

\[
\bigoplus_\mathbb Z C_p
\]

is locally finite and amenable, and the quotient `\mathbb Z` is amenable. Therefore `L_p` is amenable.

Tu's amenable-groupoid theorem gives Baum–Connes with coefficients for amenable discrete groups. Hence the coefficient-free assembly map

\[
\mu_0^{L_p}:K_0^{\mathrm{top}}(L_p)
\longrightarrow
K_0(C_r^*(L_p))
\]

is surjective (indeed an isomorphism).

## 8.3 Exact trace ring

Let `F<=L_p` be finite. Its image under the quotient map

\[
L_p\to\mathbb Z
\]

is a finite subgroup of `\mathbb Z`, hence trivial. Thus

\[
F\subset\bigoplus_\mathbb Z C_p.
\]

For prime `p`, every such finite subgroup has order `p^m` for some `m`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{L_p}=\mathbb Z[1/p]\subset\mathbb Q.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

## 8.4 Contradiction from a hypothetical reduced gap

Suppose, contrary to (8.1), that zero were isolated in the reduced spectrum of `D`. Then

\[
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)
\in M_v(C_r^*(L_p))
\]

would be a genuine reduced-C* projection and

\[
\tau_v(P)=\dim_{L(L_p)}\ker C
\]

would be irrational.

But scalar assembly for `L_p` is surjective. By Lück's modified trace theorem as quoted in Li–Nowak–Pooya Theorem 9,

\[
\tau_v(P)\in\Lambda_{L_p}=\mathbb Z[1/p],
\]

which is rational. Contradiction.

Therefore zero cannot be isolated.

## 8.5 Scope

The same argument applies to finite direct powers `L_p^k`: they remain amenable, every finite subgroup has `p`-power order, and the scalar assembly theorem is known. Thus explicit Grabowski constructions on lamplighter products are also screened out as reduced-gap scalar counterexamples.

This is a **host-theoretic no-gap theorem**. It does not require an explicit diagonalization of each Grabowski matrix.
