# 3. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A^*>=0` in a unital C*-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta\le M.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since `{0}` is clopen in `sigma(A)`, continuous functional calculus gives

\[
\boxed{P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

On the zero spectral subspace `e^{-tA}=1`, while on the positive spectral subspace its norm is at most `e^{-t delta}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Similarly the scalar function

\[
f_\eta(x)=\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

has value `1` at zero and is at most `eta/(delta+eta)` on `[delta,M]`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now let `A,B>=0` satisfy the common-gap condition

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)
\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Let `A^dagger` and `B^dagger` be the bounded pseudoinverses, so

\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le1/\delta.
\]

Since `AP_A=0` and `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

and hence

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives

\[
\|(1-P_B)P_A\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Using

\[
P_A-P_B=P_A(1-P_B)-(1-P_A)P_B
\]

and the triangle inequality,

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

The previously retained two-projection identity gives the sharper constant `1`, but `(3.8)` is the requested sufficient estimate.

### Shrinking-gap failure

If `A` has nonzero kernel and

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I,
\]

then

\[
\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0,
\]

but `B` is invertible and its kernel projection is zero. The positive gap of `B` has shrunk to `epsilon`; there is no common fixed `delta` satisfying `(3.5)`.

Thus norm closeness plus a **uniform common positive gap** is a sufficient spectral-stability hypothesis. It is not a proof that group approximants possess such a gap.

# 4. Exact depth-two metric detector

This section independently rederives the newest P25 lemma before strengthening it.

Let

\[
S_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z
\]

with geodesic metric

\[
d_L(x,y)=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|x-y+kL|.
\]

Define circle isometries

\[
A(x)=x+1,
\qquad
B(x)=-x,
\qquad
T(x)=x+\frac12.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
A^L=1,
\qquad
TAT^{-1}=A,
\]

and

\[
TBT^{-1}(x)
=T(-x+1/2)
=-x+1
=AB(x).
\]

Therefore the assignment

\[
a\mapsto A,
\qquad
b\mapsto B,
\qquad
t\mapsto T
\]

respects every defining relation and gives a genuine homomorphism

\[
\boxed{
\rho_L:\Gamma_{2,L}\to\operatorname{Isom}(S_L).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let the standard generating set be

\[
\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1},t^{\pm1}\}.
\]

At the basepoint zero,

\[
d_L(0,A^{\pm1}0)=1,
\qquad
d_L(0,B^{\pm1}0)=0,
\qquad
d_L(0,T^{\pm1}0)=1/2.
\]

Hence the orbit map

\[
g\longmapsto\rho_L(g)0
\]

is 1-Lipschitz. Thus

\[
|g|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}
\ge d_L(0,\rho_L(g)0).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every isometry of a circle is orientation-preserving or orientation-reversing, so conjugation of a rotation by a circle isometry changes its exponent only by sign. Therefore, for every `u in Gamma_{2,L}` and `s in Z`,

\[
\rho_L(u)^{-1}A^s\rho_L(u)=A^{\pm s}.
\]

Write

\[
|s|_L=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|s+kL|.
\]

Then `(4.3)` gives

\[
\boxed{
|u^{-1}a^su|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}
\ge |s|_L.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For the un-conjugated element one also has the reverse inequality by writing the shortest residue as a word in `a`, so

\[
\boxed{|a^s|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}=|s|_L.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equation `(4.4)` is exactly the P25 length detector, independently rederived here.

No reduced group-C*-algebra quotient map is used. The map `(4.2)` is only a group representation into a metric isometry group.

# 5. Finite subgroups: rigorous simultaneous conjugacy

The newest P25 report states that finite subgroups are conjugate into the cyclic factor. The statement is correct, but an elementwise torsion argument alone is not sufficient to prove **simultaneous** conjugacy of a finite subgroup. Here is the complete proof used in P51.

The height homomorphism

\[
\Gamma_{2,L}\to\mathbb Z
\]

sends every finite subgroup to zero. Thus every finite subgroup lies in

\[
H_{2,L}=C_L*\mathbb Z.
\]

Let `T_BS` be the Bass–Serre tree of the free product `C_L*Z`. Every finite group acting on a tree fixes a vertex: the convex hull of a finite orbit is a finite tree, whose center is fixed by the group after subdivision if necessary. Vertex stabilizers in `T_BS` are conjugates of the free factors.

Therefore every finite subgroup of `H_{2,L}` is conjugate into either `C_L` or `Z`. Since the infinite cyclic factor is torsion-free, every nontrivial finite subgroup is conjugate into `C_L`.

Thus if `F<Gamma_{2,L}` is finite of order `q>=2`, then `q|L` and for some `u in Gamma_{2,L}`,

\[
\boxed{
F=u^{-1}\langle a^{L/q}\rangle u.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This proves the common-conjugation statement needed for the exact isotropy threshold.

# 6. Proof of `P01-P51A`: exact finite-isotropy threshold

Fix a divisor `q|L`, `q>=2`, and set

\[
F_q=\langle a^{L/q}\rangle.
\]

For `0<=j,k<q`, equation `(4.5)` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(a^{jL/q},a^{kL/q})
&=|a^{(k-j)L/q}|_{\Gamma_{2,L}}\\
&=\left|(k-j)\frac Lq\right|_L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The largest circular residue among the `q` equally spaced points is

\[
\boxed{
r(q)
:=
\left\lfloor\frac q2\right\rfloor\frac Lq.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence the orbit `F_q e` has exact diameter `r(q)`.

## 6.1 Freeness below `tau_L`

Suppose `D<tau_L` and a nontrivial finite subgroup `F` stabilizes a simplex of `P_D Gamma_{2,L}`. By `(5.1)`, write

\[
F=u^{-1}F_qu
\]

for some divisor `q|L`, `q>=2`.

Choose an element

\[
f=u^{-1}a^{s}u\in F
\]

whose exponent has maximal circular residue

\[
|s|_L=r(q).
\]

Let `g` be any vertex of the stabilized simplex. Since the simplex is `F`-stable, `fg` is another vertex of the same simplex. Therefore

\[
d(g,fg)
=|g^{-1}fg|
\le D.
\tag{6.3}
\]

But

\[
g^{-1}fg
=(ug)^{-1}a^s(ug),
\]

so the circle detector `(4.4)` gives

\[
|g^{-1}fg|
\ge |s|_L
=r(q)
\ge\tau_L.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Equations `(6.3)` and `(6.4)` contradict `D<tau_L`.

It remains to pass from simplex stabilizers to point stabilizers of the geometric realization. A point has finite support in the vertices of one simplex. Its stabilizer permutes this finite support. The kernel of the permutation action fixes every support vertex; left translation has trivial vertex stabilizers, so that kernel is trivial. Hence every point stabilizer is finite. Since no nontrivial finite subgroup can stabilize its support simplex below `tau_L`, every point stabilizer is trivial.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
D<\tau_L
\Longrightarrow
P_D\Gamma_{2,L}\text{ is a free }\Gamma_{2,L}\text{-space}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

## 6.2 Non-freeness at and above `tau_L`

Choose a divisor `q|L`, `q>=2`, attaining the minimum in `(0.1)`. The finite set of vertices

\[
F_qe=\{e,a^{L/q},\ldots,a^{(q-1)L/q}\}
\]

has diameter exactly

\[
r(q)=\tau_L.
\]

Hence it spans a simplex in `P_{tau_L} Gamma_{2,L}`. The subgroup `F_q` stabilizes this simplex by left translation, and its barycenter is a fixed point. Therefore the action is non-free at `D=tau_L`, and remains non-free for every larger Rips scale.

Together with `(6.5)`,

\[
\boxed{
P_D\Gamma_{2,L}\text{ is free}
\iff D<\tau_L.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This proves `P01-P51A-M2-EXACT-FINITE-ISOTROPY-THRESHOLD`.

# 7. Arithmetic evaluation of `tau_L`

For each divisor `q>=2`, define

\[
f(q)=\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}.
\]

If `q` is even, then

\[
f(q)=1/2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `q` is odd, then

\[
f(q)=\frac{q-1}{2q}
=\frac12-\frac1{2q}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The right side of `(7.2)` is strictly increasing in odd `q`.

If `L` is a power of two, every nontrivial divisor is even, so the minimum is `1/2` and
