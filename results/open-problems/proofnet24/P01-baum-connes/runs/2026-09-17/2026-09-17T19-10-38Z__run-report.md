## 4. Move 6 — deform to the solvable point: freeze the discrepancy paths

Fix a site `y` and two distinct target sites `x,z`. Couple three systems whose initial values at `y` are `-1,0,+1`, while all other initial values agree. Construct the three systems by two successive applications of the source's one-particle coupling:

\[
-1\longrightarrow0\longrightarrow+1.
\]

Condition on a baseline realization of all common randomness. For the moment also freeze the two discrepancy couplings. Write the final hole indicators as

\[
A_-,A_0,A_+\in\{0,1\}\quad\text{at }x,
\qquad
B_-,B_0,B_+\in\{0,1\}\quad\text{at }z.
\]

Monotonicity under adding particles gives

\[
A_-\ge A_0\ge A_+,
\qquad
B_-\ge B_0\ge B_+.
\]

Define adjacent flip indicators

\[
R_x=A_--A_0,\qquad S_x=A_0-A_+,
\]
\[
R_z=B_--B_0,\qquad S_z=B_0-B_+.
\]

Lemma 2.3 says a **single** added-particle discrepancy can change the hole field at only one site at the terminal time. Hence, for `x!=z`, pointwise

\[
R_xR_z=0,
\qquad
S_xS_z=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now sample the actual sparse initial value

\[
\mathbb P(\eta(y)=-1)=p,
\quad
\mathbb P(\eta(y)=0)=1-2p,
\quad
\mathbb P(\eta(y)=+1)=p.
\]

At the frozen-discrepancy point, a direct three-state calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_{\eta(y)}(A_{\eta},B_{\eta})
=p^2\bigl(R_xS_z+S_xR_z\bigr).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The same-step `O(p)` terms vanish because of (4.1). This was the hoped-for cancellation and explains why the joint-block route looked promising.

The exact finite checker enumerates all binary nested patterns satisfying monotonicity and (4.1). There are 14, and identity (4.2) holds for every one of them.

This is the solvable deformation point. The key question is what happens when the discrepancy paths are unfrozen.

## 5. Move 25 — count the covariance twice: the cross-switch term reappears

Keep the common baseline evolution fixed, but now average over the two discrepancy couplings. Set

\[
\rho_x=\mathbb E R_x,
\quad
\rho_z=\mathbb E R_z,
\quad
\sigma_x=\mathbb E S_x,
\quad
\sigma_z=\mathbb E S_z,
\]

and define the two-discrepancy cross-switch probability

\[
\chi_y(x,z)
:=\mathbb E\bigl(R_xS_z+S_xR_z\bigr).
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are two honest ways to compute the covariance: first condition on the discrepancy paths and use (4.2), or expand the three possible initial values after averaging the paths. Equating them gives the exact total-covariance decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A_{\eta},B_{\eta})
=
 p\,\chi_y(x,z)
-
\bigl((1-p)\rho_x+p\sigma_x\bigr)
\bigl((1-p)\rho_z+p\sigma_z\bigr).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A_{\eta},B_{\eta})_+
\le p\,\chi_y(x,z).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the same-step first-order charge was not simply destroyed. It was transferred to the event that **the first discrepancy changes one target while the second discrepancy changes the other**.

This is the main strategic information from P84. It gives a much sharper object than the raw Step-2 product envelope. A successful `d=4` repair through this route would need to prove that the cross-switch kernel `chi_y(x,z)` carries an extra geometric factor beyond `q_x(y)q_z(y)`.

### Source-compatible bound available today

The source's proof of equation (54) gives, for either adjacent addition, a discrepancy path which, after waiting times are deleted, is a simple random walk from `y`. Hence

\[
\mathbb P(R_x=1)\le q_x(y),
\qquad
\mathbb P(R_z=1)\le q_z(y).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Conditional on the full middle evolution after the first addition, the second one-particle discrepancy has the same fresh-walk upper bound. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E[R_xS_z]
\le q_x(y)q_z(y),
\qquad
\mathbb E[S_xR_z]
\le q_x(y)q_z(y).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\chi_y(x,z)\le 2q_x(y)q_z(y),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(A_{\eta},B_{\eta})_+
\le 2p\,q_x(y)q_z(y).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This has the same spatial product kernel and the same first-order `p` scale as the original Step-2 control, up to constants. In dimension four, the already-certified logarithmic divergence of

\[
\sum_yq_x(y)q_z(y)
\]

therefore remains an obstruction for fixed `p>0`.

This does **not** prove that `chi_y(x,z)` is actually comparable to the product kernel in the particle–hole dynamics. It proves only that the current one-particle lemmas do not improve it. That distinction is the entire reason the next route should study `chi` directly rather than continue martingale algebra.

## 6. Move 27 — swap the quantifiers: a universal `p^2` gain is not implied by the source constraints

A tempting stronger statement would be:

> for every random mixture of nested three-state responses satisfying the one-discrepancy constraint, joint blocking upgrades the positive covariance from order `p` to order `p^2`.

That is false at the level of the structural information supplied by Lemma 2.3.

The exact diagnostic uses two admissible deterministic nested patterns. With probability `epsilon`, take

\[
(A_-,A_0,A_+)=(1,0,0),
\qquad
(B_-,B_0,B_+)=(1,1,0),
\tag{6.1}
\]

so the first adjacent discrepancy switches `x` and the second switches `z`. With probability `1-epsilon`, take the no-change pattern

\[
(A_-,A_0,A_+)=(1,1,1),
\qquad
(B_-,B_0,B_+)=(1,1,1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Each realization separately satisfies monotonicity and same-step mutual exclusion. For

\[
p=\epsilon=\frac18,
\]

the exact mixed covariance is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(A_\eta,B_\eta)=\frac{57}{4096}>0.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Here

\[
p^2\epsilon=\frac1{512},
\qquad
p\epsilon=\frac1{64},
\]

and the covariance lies at the first-order `p epsilon` scale rather than the deterministic `p^2 epsilon` scale.

This diagnostic is **not claimed to be an explicit realizable particle–hole history**. Its role is narrower and exact: Lemma 2.3's monotonicity plus one-discrepancy-at-one-site constraint, by themselves, do not imply a universal `p^2` gain after averaging the discrepancy paths. Any successful improvement must use additional geometry of the sequential discrepancy walks.

## 8. Strategy decision

### What survives

The joint-block route produced one genuinely useful reduction:

\[
\text{positive initial-coordinate covariance}
\quad\leadsto\quad
p\times\text{two-discrepancy cross-switch probability }\chi_y(x,z).
\]

That object is more geometric than the raw product envelope. If the dynamics forces the two sequential discrepancies to repel, coalesce, screen one another, or pay an additional capacity/gradient factor before they can hit opposite targets, the critical logarithm could disappear.

### What failed

The hoped-for automatic `p^2` improvement does not survive averaging the discrepancy paths. With only the current source lemmas, the best immediate bound is still

\[
\chi_y(x,z)\le2q_x(y)q_z(y),
\]

which reproduces the same logarithmically divergent four-dimensional kernel.

### What a successful next method must contain

It must prove a **genuinely two-discrepancy estimate** that is not obtainable from separate one-walk hitting probabilities. Schematic examples would be

\[
\chi_y(x,z)
\le C\,q_x(y)q_z(y)\,\Theta(y;x,z),
\]

with a factor `Theta` that makes

\[
\sum_y q_x(y)q_z(y)\Theta(y;x,z)
\]

summable or uniformly small at the isolation scale in `d=4`.

No further first-order martingale rearrangement should be attempted unless it creates such a two-path factor explicitly.
