# 4. Move 9 -- RANDOMIZE: one-operator randomness is a dead end for `K_1`

Randomizing the coefficients of a **single selfadjoint** comparison operator does not escape its singly generated abelian C*-algebra. If `T=T^*`, then
\[
C^*(1,T)\cong C(\sigma(T)),\qquad \sigma(T)\subset\mathbb R.
\]
Any continuous unitary obtained solely by functional calculus, including a Cayley transform, a phase, an exponential, or a randomized scalar spectral multiplier, remains in a matrix algebra over `C(\sigma(T))`.

The relevant topological fact is elementary: for compact `K\subset\mathbb R`, every continuous map `K\to U(N)` extends across the complementary intervals to the convex hull `[\min K,\max K]`. On small gaps choose the short path between nearby endpoint values (uniform continuity controls these); on the finitely many large gaps choose arbitrary paths. The resulting extension is null-homotopic because the interval is contractible. Hence
\[
K_1(C(K))=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
[f(T)]=0\quad\text{in }K_1(C_r^*(G))
}
\]
for every matrix unitary produced purely by continuous functional calculus of one selfadjoint `T`.

**Outcome of move 9:** randomizing PSZ/Austin coefficients within a one-operator construction can change spectral measures and kernel dimensions, but it cannot create a nonzero degree-one class. Randomness only becomes potentially relevant after introducing at least two genuinely noncommuting operators.

# 5. Move 13 -- FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES: noncommuting-generator complexity
