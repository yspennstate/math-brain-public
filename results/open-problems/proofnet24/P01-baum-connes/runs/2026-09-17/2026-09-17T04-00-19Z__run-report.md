## 4. P01-P81A-HALMOS-WALL-DILATION

**Verdict: PROVED / classical dilation mechanism applied to the frozen P80 family.**

Let

\[
A=C_r^*(G),
\]

fix a nontrivial free word `w in F_2`, let `a=a_e`, and put

\[
g=w^{-1}a.
\]

The quotient of `g` to `F_2` is `w^{-1}`, so `g` has infinite order. Therefore

\[
C_r^*(\langle g\rangle)\cong C(\mathbb T),
\qquad \sigma(u_g)=\mathbb T.
\]

For every `c in C`, define

\[
q_c=\frac{1+c u_g}{1+|c|}.
\]

Since `u_g` is unitary with full circle spectrum,

\[
\|1+c u_g\|=1+|c|,
\]

so `q_c` is a normal contraction. Define its defect operator

\[
D_c=(1-q_c^*q_c)^{1/2}.
\]

Normality gives `q_c^*q_c=q_cq_c^*`, hence the two defect operators agree, and functional calculus makes `D_c` commute with `q_c` and `q_c^*`.

Now define

\[
J_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_c&D_c\\
D_c&-q_c^*
\end{pmatrix}\in M_2(A).
\]

A direct multiplication gives

\[
J_cJ_c^*
=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_cq_c^*+D_c^2 & q_cD_c-D_cq_c\\
D_cq_c^*-q_c^*D_c & D_c^2+q_c^*q_c
\end{pmatrix}
=I_2,
\]

and similarly `J_c^*J_c=I_2`. Thus `J_c` is a reduced matrix unitary for **every** `c`, including the scalar singular wall `|c|=1`.

The map `c -> J_c` is norm-continuous: `c -> q_c` is norm-continuous and positive square root is norm-continuous on positive contractions.

Moreover `J_c` is K1-null. For `0<=s<=1`, `s q_c` remains a normal contraction, so

\[
s\longmapsto J(sq_c)
\]

is a unitary path from `J_c` to

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The flip `F` is itself null-homotopic: it is a selfadjoint involution, and with `p_-=(1-F)/2`,

\[
t\longmapsto e^{i\pi t p_-}
\]

joins `1` to `F`. Therefore

\[
[J_c]=0\in K_1(A).
\]

Finally set

\[
\mathcal W_c
=\operatorname{diag}(u_w,1)J_c.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{[\mathcal W_c]=[u_w]\quad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb C.}
\]

So the broad conjectured wall obstruction from P80 is false after matrix stabilization: there **is** a norm-continuous reduced-unitary continuation across the entire critical circle.

This is the outcome of Section-B move **2, ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST**.

## 5. P01-P81B — why the scalar algorithm fails, and why the matrix lift succeeds

**Verdict: PROVED.**

Assume first `|c|!=1`. Then `1+c u_g` is invertible. Let

\[
\psi_c=(1+c u_g)|1+c u_g|^{-1}.
\]

Write the polar decomposition

\[
q_c=\psi_c |q_c|.
\]

Because `q_c` is normal, for `0<=t<=1` the operators

\[
q_{c,t}=\psi_c\big((1-t)|q_c|+t1\big)
\]

are normal contractions and stay invertible. The Julia construction therefore supplies a unitary homotopy

\[
J_c\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
\psi_c&0\\0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal W_c
\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
u_w\psi_c&0\\0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
U_c&0\\0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where

\[
U_c=u_w\psi_c
\]

is exactly P80's scalar polar.

Now compare the two sides of the wall.

### Inside: `|c|<1`

The circle `1+c z` does not enclose zero, so `psi_c` has winding zero in `C(T)`. Hence

\[
[\psi_c]=0,
\qquad
[U_c]=[u_w],
\qquad
[-\psi_c^*]=0.
\]

All K1 charge is in the scalar polar block.

### Outside: `|c|>1`

The circle `1+c z` encloses zero once with positive orientation, so

\[
[\psi_c]=[u_g].
\]

But

\[
g=w^{-1}a,
\]

and the lamp involution has trivial K1 class: with `p=(1-u_a)/2`,

\[
e^{i\pi p}=u_a.
\]

Thus

\[
[u_g]=-[u_w].
\]

P80 gives

\[
[U_c]=[u_w]+[u_g]=0.
\]

At the same time

\[
[-\psi_c^*]=-[\psi_c]=-[u_g]=[u_w].
\]

So after crossing the wall, the scalar polar loses the class but the complementary Julia channel acquires exactly the missing class. The total class of the matrix completion is conserved:

\[
\boxed{[\mathcal W_c]=[u_w]\text{ on both sides and at the wall}.}
\]

This is the precise obstruction/repair produced by Section-B move **20, FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS**: the scalar polar algorithm fails because it forbids the compensating defect channel. Matrix stabilization succeeds by transporting K1 charge through that channel.
