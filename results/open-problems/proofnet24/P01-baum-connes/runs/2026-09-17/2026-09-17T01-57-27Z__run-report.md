# 2. Idea written before code: cross the one-selfadjoint barrier with a noncommuting pair

P78 proved that a unitary obtained by continuous functional calculus of one selfadjoint lies in `C^*(T)\cong C(\sigma(T))` with `\sigma(T)\subset\mathbb R`, hence contributes no nonzero `K_1` class. The next attack therefore has to use at least two genuinely noncommuting selfadjoints.

Let `a=a_e` be the nontrivial lamp at the identity, so `u_a=u_a^*=u_a^{-1}`. Fix a nontrivial word `w\in F_2` and a real parameter `r>0`. Define
\[
X_r=\frac{u_w+u_w^*}{2}+r u_a,
\qquad
Y=\frac{u_w-u_w^*}{2i},
\qquad
F_r=X_r+iY=u_w+r u_a.
\]
Both `X_r` and `Y` are selfadjoint. The family is deliberately asymmetric: the lamp term appears only in `X_r`.

# 3. `P01-P79A-TWO-OPERATOR-POLAR-PHASE-TRANSITION = PROVED`

## Theorem

For every nontrivial `w\in F_2` and `r>0`, the two selfadjoints `X_r,Y` above are noncommuting. Moreover:

1. `F_r` is invertible in `C_r^*(G)` **iff** `r\ne1`.
2. For `r\ne1`, let
   \[
   U_r=F_r(F_r^*F_r)^{-1/2}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   [U_r]=
   \begin{cases}
   [u_w],&0<r<1,\\
   0,&r>1.
   \end{cases}
   \]
3. If the abelianization of `w` is `(m,n)`, then
   \[
   \mu_1^G(mv_1+nv_2)=[U_r]\quad(0<r<1),
   \qquad
   \mu_1^G(0)=[U_r]\quad(r>1),
   \]
   where the inherited Pooya generators satisfy `\mu_1^G(v_j)=[u_{s_j}]`.

## Proof: genuine noncommutativity

The real and imaginary parts of `u_w` commute, so
\[
[X_r,Y]=r[u_a,Y].
\]
Expanding in the group ring gives
\[
2i[u_a,Y]
=u_{aw}-u_{wa}-u_{aw^{-1}}+u_{w^{-1}a}.
\]
The first two terms have free-group quotient `w`; the last two have quotient `w^{-1}`. Since `w` has infinite order, `w\ne w^{-1}`. Also `aw\ne wa`: equality would imply `a=waw^{-1}`, but conjugation by the nontrivial free word moves the identity lamp to the lamp at `w`. The same argument gives `aw^{-1}\ne w^{-1}a`. Thus all four group elements occurring above are distinct and the displayed group-ring element is nonzero. Hence
\[
\boxed{[X_r,Y]\ne0.}
\]

## Proof: exact invertibility threshold

Factor
\[
F_r=u_w(1+r u_g),
\qquad
g=w^{-1}a.
\]
Under the quotient `G\to F_2`, `g` maps to `w^{-1}`. Since `w^{-1}` has infinite order, so does `g`. Therefore `\langle g\rangle\cong\mathbb Z`, and the canonical subgroup inclusion gives
\[
C_r^*(\langle g\rangle)\cong C(\mathbb T)\subset C_r^*(G),
\qquad
\sigma(u_g)=\mathbb T.
\]
Thus
\[
0\in\sigma(1+r u_g)
\iff -1/r\in\mathbb T
\iff r=1.
\]
Since `u_w` is unitary,
\[
\boxed{F_r\text{ is invertible iff }r\ne1.}
\]

## Proof: the subcritical class

Fix `0<r<1`. For `0\le s\le r`, the same spectrum calculation shows `F_s=u_w+s u_a` is invertible. Polar decomposition depends norm-continuously on an invertible operator, so the polar unitaries form a path from `U_0=u_w` to `U_r`. Hence
\[
\boxed{[U_r]=[u_w]\qquad(0<r<1).}
\]

## Proof: the supercritical class

Fix `r>1`. The path
\[
F_{r,t}=t u_w+r u_a,\qquad0\le t\le1,
\]
is invertible because
\[
F_{r,t}=r u_a\bigl(1+(t/r)u_a u_w\bigr)
\]
and `t/r<1`. Its polar path connects `U_r` to the polar unitary of `r u_a`, namely `u_a`. If
\[
p=\frac{1-u_a}{2},
\]
then `p` is a projection and
\[
e^{i\pi p}=1-2p=u_a.
\]
Thus `t\mapsto e^{i\pi t p}` joins `1` to `u_a`, proving `[u_a]=0`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{[U_r]=0\qquad(r>1).}
\]

## Proof: assembly coordinates

The map from group unitaries to `K_1` is abelian, so the class of `u_w` is its free-group abelianization coordinate. If `w` has exponent sums `(m,n)`, Pooya's explicit basis gives
\[
[u_w]=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}]
=\mu_1^G(mv_1+nv_2).
\]
Combining with the two regimes above proves the assembly formula.

This is an explicit fixed-group corollary built from standard polar decomposition plus Pooya's known assembly basis. I make **no novelty claim** for the general operator-algebraic mechanism.

# 5. Move 23: attack the opposite assumption through the legal quotient

Assume, contrary to the theorem, that the subcritical polar `U_r` were `K_1`-trivial for a word with nonzero abelianization. The lamp kernel
\[
N=\bigoplus_{F_2}C_2
\]
is amenable, so the quotient `G\to F_2` induces the already-verified reduced quotient
\[
q_r:C_r^*(G)\to C_r^*(F_2).
\]
It sends
\[
F_r\longmapsto u_w+r.
\]
Functional calculus commutes with `q_r`, hence `q_r(U_r)` is the polar phase of `u_w+r`. On `C^*(u_w)\cong C(\mathbb T)`, this is
\[
z\longmapsto\frac{z+r}{|z+r|}.
\]
For `0<r<1`, the circle centered at `r` with radius one winds once around zero. Therefore
\[
q_{r,*}[U_r]=[u_w].
\]
For the explicit word `s_1^2s_2^3`, the latter has coordinate `(2,3)` and is nonzero. Thus the negation `[U_{1/2}]=0` is impossible. For `r>1` the same circle has winding zero, independently matching the supercritical computation.

**Outcome of move 23:** the quotient supplies an independent falsification test for the subcritical class rather than merely repeating the homotopy proof.

# 6. Move 28: the single exceptional parameter and an exact reduced/von-Neumann boundary

The family is constant in `K_1` on each of the two typical open regimes and changes only at the exceptional radius
\[
r=1.
\]
At that point
\[
F_1=u_w(1+u_g),\qquad g=w^{-1}a,
\]
and `0` lies in the spectrum because `-1\in\sigma(u_g)=\mathbb T`. Thus `F_1` is not invertible in the reduced algebra.

There is nevertheless a von Neumann polar phase. On
\[
L(\langle g\rangle)\cong L^\infty(\mathbb T),
\]
the phase of `1+u_g` is represented almost everywhere by
\[
\phi(z)=\frac{1+z}{|1+z|}.
\]
The bilateral shift has no `-1` eigenvector, so the kernel of `1+u_g` is zero; the adjoint has zero kernel as well, and the polar part is a unitary in the group von Neumann algebra. But `\phi` is discontinuous at `z=-1`: approaching the point from the two sides gives opposite limits `i` and `-i`. Therefore
\[
\phi\notin C(\mathbb T)=C_r^*(\langle g\rangle).
\]

For every subgroup `H\le G`, the canonical Fourier restriction gives a conditional expectation
\[
E_H:C_r^*(G)\to C_r^*(H),
\qquad
E_H(u_x)=\begin{cases}u_x,&x\in H,\\0,&x\notin H.\end{cases}
\]
Take `H=\langle g\rangle`. If the von Neumann phase `\phi(u_g)` belonged to `C_r^*(G)`, then, because it belongs to `L(H)`, the normal extension of `E_H` would fix it, forcing it into `C_r^*(H)`, a contradiction. Hence
\[
\boxed{\phi(u_g)\in L(G)\setminus C_r^*(G).}
\]
The polar unitary of `F_1` in `L(G)` is `u_w\phi(u_g)`. If that unitary belonged to `C_r^*(G)`, multiplication by `u_w^*` would put `\phi(u_g)` in the reduced algebra, again impossible. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{polar}_{L(G)}(F_1)\notin C_r^*(G).
}
\]

`P01-P79B-CRITICAL-POLAR-PHASE-REDUCED-NOGO = PROVED`.

It is a sharp toy model of the reduced-versus-von-Neumann membership obstruction that made the Austin comparison route dangerous: here the obstruction occurs at one exactly identified critical parameter. It is **not** permission to revive the Austin or PSZ projections themselves.

**Outcome of move 28:** for all `r\ne1` the polar representative is reduced and its class is completely decided; the sole exceptional `r=1` has only a von Neumann polar phase, which is provably not reduced.
