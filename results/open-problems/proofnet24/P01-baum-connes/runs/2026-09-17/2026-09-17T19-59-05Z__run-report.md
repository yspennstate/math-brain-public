## 3. Section-I stuck report

### (a) Exact statement that blocks

The source proof would go through in dimension four if one could replace the non-summable ever-hit envelope accumulation by a source-valid **relative terminal cross-switch bound** strong enough to yield a uniform two-hole estimate. The missing bridge can be written as the following sufficient target:

\[
\boxed{
\text{terminal cross-switch conservation under the reveal filtration}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Pr(H_t(x)=H_t(z)=1)\le C(p)h_t^2
\ \text{at the isolation scale, uniformly in }t.
}
\]

What the published sequential exposure currently gives instead is a multiplicative cost controlled by
\[
\boxed{
\sum_y q_x(y)q_z(y),
}
\]
which is finite with spatial decay for \(d\ge5\) but logarithmically divergent at the \(d=4\) critical dimension.

### (b) Routes tried and precise failure

**Direct source specialization.** Proposition 8.3 reduces to a Green-overlap convolution. In \(d=4\), the positive convolution has the critical logarithmic divergence. Failure: no summable spatial decorrelation.

**Finite-time cutoff.** Replacing infinite Green functions by time-truncated kernels makes every expression finite but leaves an \(O(\log t)\) accumulation. Failure: the loss grows on the same limit in which the isolation argument is needed.

**Instruction-channel gradient cancellation.** Rewriting the centered neighbor covariance as a difference form would turn the envelope calculation into a Dirichlet-energy convolution with the desired extra derivative. Failure: the actual influences \(\alpha_v,\beta_v\) are known only through pointwise boxes \(0\le\alpha_v\le q_x(v)\), \(0\le\beta_v\le q_z(v)\); no gradient control transfers from envelopes to influences. The independent initial-value channel also survives.

**Joint three-state initial/discrepancy block.** For frozen discrepancy paths, Lemma 2.3 forces same-discrepancy mutual exclusion and gives a \(p^2\) cross-switch expression. Failure: after path randomization, total covariance contains the first-order term \(p\chi_y(x,z)\), and source-valid separate hitting bounds give only \(\chi_y\le2q_xq_z\). An exact finite diagnostic from P84 showed that the structural one-discrepancy constraints alone do not prevent first-order covariance after mixing paths.

**Terminal-endpoint mass-transport idea (this run).** This is the first route that genuinely changes the potential-theoretic object rather than rearranging the same Green product. Pathwise, each one-particle discrepancy can alter the terminal hole field at at most one site. Therefore, for a fixed displacement \(r\), the *formal pathwise diagonal count* obeys
\[
\sum_u\bigl(R_uS_{u+r}+S_uR_{u+r}\bigr)\le2.
\]
After unconditional averaging and translation, this points to the candidate diagonal mass-transport bound
\[
\sum_y \bar\chi_y(x,z)\le2,
\]
where \(\bar\chi\) is the fully **unconditional** terminal cross-switch kernel. This is dramatically different from the divergent ever-hit Green convolution.

I am **not promoting that observation as a new P01 theorem claim in this stuck run**. Its precise failure point is already visible: Proposition 8.3 is a *conditional, multiplicative, sequential-reveal* argument. Conditioning on the previously exposed environment breaks the direct translation-invariant mass-transport reduction, and the factors are relative to conditional hole probabilities. The useful unconditional \(\ell^1\) conservation therefore does not yet imply the relative conditional estimate needed by the source proof.

### (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A successful \(d=4\) argument now needs at least one genuinely new ingredient of one of the following equivalent flavors:

- a reveal formalism that preserves terminal endpoint mass conservation after the conditioning needed for the two-hole martingale;
- a stationarity/Palm or random-priority formulation converting unconditional diagonal cross-switch mass into relative conditional correlation;
- or a critical four-dimensional last-exit/capacity/nonintersection estimate that inserts the missing logarithmic improvement before the two one-particle influences are multiplied.

Merely rearranging the product \(q_xq_z\), or proving another one-discrepancy hitting bound, cannot supply this.

### (d) Simplest special case still unproved

The cleanest source-faithful special case is finite-volume and finite-time:

> On a four-dimensional discrete torus, reveal site blocks in an i.i.d.-priority (translation-covariant) order and determine the two target-hole indicators at a fixed time. Prove a volume- and time-uniform bound on the **relative cross-switch accumulation** entering the product of conditional covariance factors.

An unnormalized mass-transport bound is not enough; the special case must control the normalization by the conditional target-hole probabilities, because that is exactly what turns local covariance into the multiplicative factor in Proposition 8.3.

### (e) Nearest solved problem and the missing ingredient

There are two relevant solved neighbors.

1. The source’s own \(d\ge5\) Theorem 1.6. Its proof uses summability/decay of the two Green envelopes and then an isolation/mass-transport argument. Dimension four lacks that summability.
2. Classical four-dimensional random-walk intersection theory, especially Gregory Lawler’s 1982/1985 work and the later capacity literature. In the critical dimension, crude Green-product counting carries logarithms, while last-exit/nonintersection/capacity methods extract reciprocal-logarithmic or otherwise renormalized behavior. Asselah–Schapira–Sousi likewise treat the capacity of a random-walk range in \(\mathbb Z^4\) as a genuinely critical object.

What I0005 currently lacks is an event-level representation identifying the parking-model two-discrepancy cross-switch with a Lawler-style last-exit, avoidance, or capacity quantity. Without that identification, citing four-dimensional intersection theory does not itself improve \(\chi_y\).

## 4. The three new-method moves

### Move 7 — TAKE THE LIMIT

I replaced the “ever hit target” picture by the terminal endpoint picture. In the idealized case of two independent fixed-time simple-random-walk endpoints,
\[
\sum_y p_n(x-y)p_n(z-y)=p_{2n}(z-x),
\]
so the overlap is a heat kernel rather than a Green-product logarithm. This explains exactly what is being lost by replacing a terminal endpoint law with an ever-hit potential.

**Outcome:** productive heuristic, but not a proof. Parking discrepancies stop, wait, swap representation between an active particle and an unmatched hole, and interact through the common environment. No source-valid heat-kernel endpoint bound is currently available.

### Move 14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

The acting group is translation by \(\mathbb Z^4\). Combining translation invariance with the pathwise “one terminal discrepancy site per adjacent coupling” constraint exposes the candidate diagonal mass-transport bound \(\sum_y\bar\chi_y(x,z)\le2\) at the unconditional level.

**Outcome:** this is the best new route found in the run. It shows that the logarithm can be an artifact of the ever-hit domination rather than an intrinsic count of terminal pivotal pairs. The obstruction is that the existing reveal proof uses a non-translation-invariant conditional history. A random-priority reveal is the natural next experiment.

### Move 24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

I tested whether endpoint conservation alone could force spatial decay. It cannot. Even with at most one endpoint in each discrepancy channel, the pair of terminal endpoints can be concentrated at a fixed deterministic separation \(r\), saturating the diagonal count while giving no decay in \(r\).

**Outcome:** this kills the naive “\(\ell^1\) conservation is enough” hope. The missing ingredient is anti-concentration of the endpoint gap, a relative/Palm estimate, or a capacity/nonintersection effect—not merely total mass conservation.
