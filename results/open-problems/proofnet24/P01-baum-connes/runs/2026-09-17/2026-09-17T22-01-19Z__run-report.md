## 2. Move 18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: pass to the free quotient

Write
\[
1\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow G\xrightarrow{\pi}F_2\longrightarrow1,
\qquad B=\bigoplus_{F_2}C_2.
\]
The lamp kernel \(B\) is locally finite and therefore amenable. For an amenable normal subgroup, the quotient regular representation is weakly contained in the regular representation of the original group, so the group quotient induces a surjective reduced-C*-homomorphism
\[
q:C_r^*(G)\longrightarrow C_r^*(F_2),
\qquad q(u_g)=u_{\pi(g)}.
\]
On K1,
\[
q_*([u_{s_j}])=[u_{s_j}],\qquad j=1,2.
\]
Pooya gives the two classes on the left as a basis of \(K_1(C_r^*(G))\), while the standard free-group computation gives the two classes on the right as a basis of \(K_1(C_r^*(F_2))\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
q_*:K_1(C_r^*(G))\xrightarrow{\cong}K_1(C_r^*(F_2))
}
\]
is an isomorphism.

This is a **known consequence of standard quotient functoriality plus the published K-theory computations**, not a novelty claim.

The strategic consequence is decisive:
\[
\boxed{
q_*[U]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [U]=0
}
\]
for every matrix unitary \(U\) over \(C_r^*(G)\). Thus there is no hidden degree-one K-theory direction carried purely by the lamp kernel. Lamps can alter representatives dramatically, but they cannot create an ambient \(K_1\)-class invisible in the free quotient.

**Outcome of move 18:** productive as a route screen. It explains in one invariant why the many lamp/polar/Sobolev/Halmos representatives never produced a third direction.

## 3. Move 19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: quotient-classify any explicit unitary

The quotient isomorphism turns the fixed-group class-decision problem into a direct procedure.

Given an explicit matrix unitary
\[
U\in U_m(C_r^*(G)),
\]
apply \(q\) entrywise to obtain
\[
\bar U=(\operatorname{id}_{M_m}\otimes q)(U)
\in U_m(C_r^*(F_2)).
\]
Compute its standard free-group K1 coordinate vector
\[
[\bar U]=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}]
\in K_1(C_r^*(F_2))\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]
Because \(q_*\) is an isomorphism,
\[
\boxed{
[U]=m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}]
=\mu_1^G(mv_1+nv_2).
}
\]
So for this frozen target the free quotient is a complete K1 classifier and, combined with Pooya's explicit assembly generators, a complete assembly-membership decision procedure at the level of K1 classes.

This compiler subsumes the representational routes developed earlier: group words, gauge/Sobolev representatives, polar families, Toeplitz-wall representatives, Halmos completions, and finite-support lamp splices all ultimately reduce to the same ordered pair \((m,n)\).

**Outcome of move 19:** productive as a universal fixed-target classifier, but incapable of rank movement because its codomain is already \(\mathbb Z^2\).

## 4. Move 29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: inspect the relative kernel ideal

Let
\[
J=\ker(q:C_r^*(G)\to C_r^*(F_2)).
\]
The six-term exact sequence for
\[
0\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow C_r^*(G)\xrightarrow q C_r^*(F_2)\longrightarrow0
\]
contains
\[
K_1(J)\longrightarrow K_1(C_r^*(G))\xrightarrow{q_*}K_1(C_r^*(F_2)).
\]
Since \(q_*\) is injective, exactness gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}\bigl(K_1(J)\to K_1(C_r^*(G))\bigr)=0.
}
\]
Equivalently, any degree-one relative/lamp-kernel class dies when included in the ambient reduced group algebra. The lamp ideal may carry interesting internal K-theory, but it supplies no additional ambient \(K_1\) direction for this target.

This is the clean relative form of the saturation obstruction. A two-lamp/two-channel construction can still make a new explicit representative, but if its free quotient is K1-trivial then the ambient class is trivial; if its quotient has coordinates \((m,n)\), then the ambient class is exactly the already-accounted class \(m[u_{s_1}]+n[u_{s_2}]\).

**Outcome of move 29:** productive as a no-go boundary. It screens an entire family of future lamp-engineering attempts from being mistaken for movement on the source conjecture.
