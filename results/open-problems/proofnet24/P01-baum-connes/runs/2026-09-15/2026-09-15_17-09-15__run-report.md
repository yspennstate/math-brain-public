\[
C\in M_{u,v}(\mathbb C\Gamma),\qquad D=C^*C.

\[
\sigma(D_\infty)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M]
\tag{0.1}

\[
\alpha=\dim_{L(\Gamma)}\ker C_\infty.

\[

\[
P_L=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_L)

belongs to the reduced group C*-algebra and

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(D_L)\subset
\begin{cases}
\{0\}\cup[\delta_0,\infty),&\alpha>0,\\[1mm]
[\delta_0,\infty),&\alpha=0.

\tag{0.2}

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma_L})(P_L)=\alpha.

\[
g_\infty=\min(\sigma(D_\infty)\setminus\{0\})>0

\[
g_L=\inf(\sigma(D_L)\setminus\{0\}),

\[

The lower bound follows from the plateau-rank-sandwich theorem. The upper bound uses only finite-support approximate eigenvectors and local marked-group agreement; it does **not** infer upper reduced-norm convergence from marked convergence.

  \[

# 3. Spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let `A>=0` be bounded in a unital matrix C*-algebra and suppose

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad\delta>0.
\]

Then the function which is `1` at `0` and `0` on `[delta,M]` is continuous on the spectrum, so

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the algebra.

For `t>0`, on the kernel `e^{-tA}=1`, while on the positive spectral subspace its norm is at most `e^{-t delta}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `eta>0`, the function

\[
x\mapsto\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

is `1` at `0` and at most `eta/(delta+eta)` on `[delta,M]`, so

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` have the **same** positive gap `delta`. Let `A^dagger` be the continuous pseudoinverse on

\[
\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty),
\]

so `||A^dagger||<=1/delta`. Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
AP_B=(A-B)P_B.
\]

Multiplying by the pseudoinverse gives

\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

hence

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` and using the standard two-projection identity

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|
=
\max\{\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\}
\]

gives the sharper estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the requested factor `2` estimate is valid but nonsharp.

The shared gap is essential. If `A` has nontrivial kernel and `B=A+epsilon I`, then `||A-B||=epsilon` but `P_B=0`. If the positive gap is allowed to collapse with `epsilon`, no uniform projection estimate follows.

This seed is only a **sufficient norm-plus-uniform-gap theorem**. It does not manufacture the gap for group approximants.

# 5. New plateau polynomial

## `P01-P43A-MAJORITY-PLATEAU-POLYNOMIAL` — **PROVED**

Fix

\[
0<\delta_0<\delta<M.
\]

Put

\[
t=\frac{\delta+\delta_0}{2},
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{\delta-\delta_0}{2M}>0,
\qquad
z(x)=\frac{t-x}{M}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every odd integer `n=2m+1`, define

\[
q_n(x)
=
\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}
\binom nk
\left(\frac{1+z(x)}2\right)^k
\left(\frac{1-z(x)}2\right)^{n-k}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a real polynomial of degree at most `n`.

For `x in [0,M]`, the number

\[
p(x)=\frac{1+z(x)}2
\]

lies in `[0,1]`, and `q_n(x)` is exactly the upper-tail probability

\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,p(x))\ge(n+1)/2\}.
\]

Define

\[
\varepsilon_n=e^{-n\gamma^2/2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
0\le q_n(x)\le1\qquad(0\le x\le M),
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
q_n(x)\ge1-\varepsilon_n
\qquad(0\le x\le\delta_0),
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
q_n(x)\le\varepsilon_n
\qquad(\delta\le x\le M).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Proof

If `x<=delta_0`, then

\[
z(x)\ge\gamma,
\]

so

\[
p(x)\ge\frac12+\frac\gamma2.
\]

The failure event for a strict majority is at least `gamma/2` below the mean proportion. Hoeffding therefore gives

\[
1-q_n(x)
\le
\exp\left(-2n(\gamma/2)^2\right)
=e^{-n\gamma^2/2}.
\]

If `x>=delta`, then `z(x)<=-gamma`, hence

\[
p(x)\le\frac12-\frac\gamma2,
\]

and the same Hoeffding estimate gives

\[
q_n(x)\le e^{-n\gamma^2/2}.
\]

This proves (5.4)–(5.6).

### Why this differs from the previous Chebyshev cutoff

The P38 minimax polynomial was optimized for small sup norm on the positive spectral interval subject to the interpolation condition `p(0)=1`. Its `1/2` crossing therefore moves toward zero when the degree grows.

The new polynomial deliberately sacrifices minimax optimality. It places its transition in the **known empty spectral interval** `(delta_0,delta)`. Increasing the degree makes the two plateaux flatter without moving the `1/2` transition toward zero.

That distinction is the entire source of the uniform-gap improvement below.

# 6. Uniform stage-gap locking

## `P01-P43B-NONFREE-UNIFORM-STAGE-GAP` — **PROVED**

Assume (0.1). Fix `delta_0 in (0,delta)` and let `gamma` be (5.1).

For each `L`, choose `n_L` to be the least odd integer satisfying

\[
\boxed{
n_L
\ge
\frac{2}{\gamma^2}\log(12vL)
=
\frac{8M^2}{(\delta-\delta_0)^2}\log(12vL).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
\varepsilon_L:=e^{-n_L\gamma^2/2}
\le\frac1{12vL}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because `n_L=O(log L)`, for all sufficiently large `L` one has

\[
L\ge16n_L\rho+1
\tag{6.3}
\]

and

\[
\Gamma_+(2n_L\rho,L)\le2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define

\[
A_{\infty,L}=q_{n_L}(D_\infty),
\qquad
A_L=q_{n_L}(D_L).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Let

\[
P_\infty=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_\infty).
\]

By the parent spectral-gap assumption and (5.5)–(5.6),

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{\infty,L}-P_\infty\|\le\varepsilon_L.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Furthermore, on the parent spectrum the scalar values of `A_infty,L` lie either in `[0,epsilon_L]` or in `[1-epsilon_L,1]`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{\infty,L}^2-A_{\infty,L}\|\le\varepsilon_L.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

The first-factor support radius of the defect is at most

\[
2n_L\rho.
\]

By (4.1), (6.3), and (6.4), coefficientwise stage evaluation gives

\[
\eta_L
:=
\|A_L^2-A_L\|
\le2\varepsilon_L
\le\frac1{6vL}
<\frac14.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus the genuine reduced-C* projection

\[
Q_L
=
\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(A_L)
\in M_v(C_r^*(\Gamma_L))
\tag{6.9}
\]

is well defined.

For self-adjoint `A` with `||A^2-A||<=eta<1/4`, spectral calculus gives

\[
\left\|\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(A)-A\right\|
\le
r(\eta):=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\eta}}2
\le2\eta.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_L-A_L\|\le4\varepsilon_L.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

## 6.1 Exact trace locking

Since `n_L rho<L` for large `L`, no nontrivial word in the support of the group-ring polynomial `A_infty,L` can collapse to the identity under the single new relation `a^L=1`. The extra direct-product `Z` coordinate is unchanged. Therefore the identity coefficient, and hence the canonical matrix trace, is preserved exactly:

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma_L})(A_L)
=
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma})(A_{\infty,L}).
\tag{6.11}
\]

By (6.6),

\[
\left|
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma})(A_{\infty,L})-\alpha
\right|
\le v\varepsilon_L.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Using (6.10) and (6.11),

\[
\left|
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma_L})(Q_L)-\alpha
\right|
\le5v\varepsilon_L
\le\frac5{12L}
<\frac1{2L}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

By the stage trace lattice (4.3), the trace of `Q_L` lies in `L^{-1}Z`. By the Sylvester/Linnell rank theorem, `alpha` is an integer. Hence (6.13) forces

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma_L})(Q_L)=\alpha.
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

No claim about the full `K_0` class of `Q_L` is needed.

## 6.2 Rank sandwich identifies the true kernel

Let

\[
P_L=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_L)
\in M_v(L(\Gamma_L)).
\]

At `x=0`, (5.5) gives

\[
q_{n_L}(0)\ge1-\varepsilon_L>\frac12.
\]

Therefore the entire kernel of `D_L` lies inside the `Q_L` spectral subspace:

\[
P_L\le Q_L.
\tag{6.15}
\]

Hence

\[
\dim_{L(\Gamma_L)}\ker C_L
\le
(\operatorname{Tr}_v\otimes\tau_{\Gamma_L})(Q_L)
=\alpha.
\tag{6.16}
\]

The algebraic quotient-rank theorem (4.2) gives the reverse inequality

\[
\dim_{L(\Gamma_L)}\ker C_L\ge\alpha.
\tag{6.17}
\]

Thus equality holds. Since `P_L<=Q_L` and the finite von-Neumann trace is faithful,

\[
\boxed{P_L=Q_L.}
\tag{6.18}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{P_L\in M_v(C_r^*(\Gamma_L))}
\]

and its canonical trace is exactly `alpha`.

## 6.3 The plateau gives a uniform gap

For every

\[
0\le x\le\delta_0,
\]

(5.5) gives

\[
q_{n_L}(x)\ge1-\varepsilon_L>\frac12.
\tag{6.19}
\]

Suppose `D_L` had a positive spectral point in `(0,delta_0]`. By continuity of `q_{n_L}`, a nonzero spectral subprojection around that point would lie inside `Q_L` but be orthogonal to `P_L`. This contradicts (6.18).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sigma(D_L)\cap(0,\delta_0]=\varnothing.
}
\tag{6.20}
\]

If `alpha=0`, then (6.14) and faithfulness imply `Q_L=0`, and since `q_{n_L}(0)>1/2`, zero itself cannot belong to the stage spectrum. Hence the stage is invertible and

\[
\sigma(D_L)\subset[\delta_0,\infty).
\]

If `alpha>0`, then

\[
\sigma(D_L)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta_0,\infty).
\]

This proves (0.2)–(0.3).

### Quantifier summary

For **every fixed** `delta_0<delta`, the required degree is only

\[
n_L=O_{\delta,\delta_0,M,v}(\log L),
\]

so the first-factor propagation is `O(log L)`, safely inside the P40/P25 local cb window.

The theorem does not require trace to classify full `K_0`.

# 7. Spectral-edge convergence

## `P01-P43C-POSITIVE-SPECTRAL-EDGE-CONVERGENCE` — **PROVED**

Assume `C` is nonzero and the parent zero is isolated. Define

\[
g_\infty
=
\min(\sigma(D_\infty)\setminus\{0\})>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For sufficiently large `L`, P43B shows the stage has an isolated zero or is invertible, so define

\[
g_L
=
\inf(\sigma(D_L)\setminus\{0\}).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{g_L\to g_\infty.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Lower bound

Take any `delta_0<g_infty`. Apply P43B with `delta=g_infty` and an upper spectral bound `M>||D_infty||`. Then eventually

\[
g_L\ge\delta_0.
\]

Because `delta_0<g_infty` was arbitrary,

\[
\liminf_{L\to\infty}g_L\ge g_\infty.
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Upper bound from a finite-support approximate eigenvector

Because `g_infty` lies in the spectrum of the bounded self-adjoint operator `D_infty`, it lies in the approximate point spectrum. For every `epsilon>0` there is a unit vector `xi` with

\[
\|(D_\infty-g_\infty)\xi\|<\varepsilon/2.
\]

Finite-support vectors are dense, so after a small perturbation and renormalization one may choose `xi` with finite support and

\[
\|(D_\infty-g_\infty)\xi\|<\varepsilon.
\tag{7.5}
\]

All group elements involved in `xi` and `D_infty xi` lie in one finite marked ball. For sufficiently large `L`, the quotient

\[
F_2\times\mathbb Z\to(C_L*\mathbb Z)\times\mathbb Z
\]

is injective on every word relation needed to evaluate this finite vector and its image under `D`. Transfer the coefficients of `xi` to a stage vector `xi_L`. Then

\[
\|\xi_L\|=1
\]

and the residual is **exactly the same**:

\[
\|(D_L-g_\infty)\xi_L\|
=
\|(D_\infty-g_\infty)\xi\|
<\varepsilon.
\tag{7.6}
\]

For a self-adjoint operator,

\[
\operatorname{dist}(g_\infty,\sigma(D_L))
\le
\|(D_L-g_\infty)\xi_L\|,
\]

so the stage spectrum contains a point within `epsilon` of `g_infty`. Taking `epsilon<g_infty/2` ensures that this point is positive, not zero. Therefore

\[
\limsup_{L\to\infty}g_L\le g_\infty.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Combining (7.4) and (7.7) proves (7.3).

### Why this does not revive the killed marked-convergence shortcut

The upper-edge argument uses marked convergence only for **one fixed finitely supported approximate eigenvector**. It proves existence of nearby stage spectrum at one fixed spectral point.

It does **not** prove

\[
\|D_L\|\to\|D_\infty\|,
\]

nor upper norm control for arbitrary moving group-ring elements, nor convergence of spectral projections. Those stronger steps still require the independent cb norm theorem.
