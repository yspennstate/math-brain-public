# 3. Spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A>=0` in a unital `C^*`-algebra and suppose

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Because `{0}` is clopen in the spectrum, the characteristic function of `{0}` is continuous on `sigma(A)`. Continuous functional calculus therefore gives

\[
\boxed{P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)\in C^*(A,1).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `t>0`, the function `e^{-tx}` is `1` at zero and at most `e^{-t\delta}` on `[delta,M]`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `eta>0`, the function

\[
f_\eta(x)=\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

is `1` at zero and at most `eta/(delta+eta)` on the positive spectral part. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Now let `A,B>=0` and assume both have the same positive lower gap `delta`:

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Let `A^\dagger,B^\dagger` be the bounded pseudoinverses, so

\[
\|A^\dagger\|,\|B^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]

Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

and therefore

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the symmetric estimate. Decomposing `P_A-P_B` into the two off-diagonal projection pieces yields the requested sufficient bound

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{2\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Using the standard two-orthogonal-projection norm identity sharpens (3.5) to

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta},
\]

as retained in earlier P01 checkpoints, but the factor-2 estimate is already sufficient for the stated seed.

This is a **sufficient norm-plus-uniform-gap hypothesis**. It does not produce the gap for a group approximation.

To see the failure when the common gap collapses, take an `A` with nonzero kernel and set

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I.
\]

Then

\[
\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0,
\]

but `P_B=0` while `P_A\ne0`, so

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|=1.
\]

The positive spectral gap of `B` near the old zero is only `epsilon`, which shrinks with the perturbation. This is exactly the failure mode that prevents marked/local convergence by itself from controlling kernel projections.

# 4. Arithmetic refinement of finite-isotropy visibility

The newest P25 argument uses a nilpotent abelianized-base quotient to show that no nontrivial finite isotropy is visible below root scale. The only deliberately coarse step in its constant is the statement that every nontrivial subgroup of `C_L` has an element whose circular residue is at least `L/3`. We now compute the exact optimal arithmetic constant.

## 4.1 Exact cyclic subgroup radius

Let `q|L`, `q>=2`. The unique subgroup of `C_L=<x_1>` of order `q` is

\[
\langle x_1^{L/q}\rangle.
\]

Its circular residues are the multiples

\[
0,\frac Lq,\frac{2L}q,\ldots,\frac{(q-1)L}q
\]

measured modulo `L` with

\[
|s|_L=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|s+kL|.
\]

The maximum circular residue in this subgroup is therefore

\[
\boxed{
R(q,L)
=
\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}L.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For even `q`, this is `L/2`. For odd `q`, it is

\[
\frac{q-1}{2q}L.
\]

Hence the weakest nontrivial subgroup of `C_L`, from the viewpoint of this residue lower bound, has normalized radius exactly `chi(L)` from (0.1).

## 4.2 Closed form for `chi(L)`

For even `q`,

\[
\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}=\frac12.
\]

For odd `q`,

\[
\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}
=
\frac{q-1}{2q}
=
\frac12-\frac1{2q},
\]

which is strictly increasing as an odd integer `q>=3` increases.

Therefore:

- if `L` is a power of two, every divisor `q>=2` is even and `chi(L)=1/2`;
- otherwise, the minimum is achieved by the smallest odd divisor of `L`, which is the smallest odd prime divisor `p_odd(L)`, and
  \[
  \chi(L)=\frac{p_{\rm odd}(L)-1}{2p_{\rm odd}(L)}.
  \]

This proves (0.2). Since the smallest possible odd prime is `3`, (0.3) follows, and equality `chi(L)=1/3` holds exactly when `3|L`.

# 5. Proof of `P01-P49-ARITHMETIC-ROOT-SOURCE-PERSISTENCE`

## 5.1 The coordinate lower bound retained from P25

Define the abelianized-base quotient

\[
\mathcal N_{m,L}=A_{m,L}\rtimes_T\mathbb Z,
\]

where

\[
A_{m,L}
=(\mathbb Z/L)e_1\oplus\mathbb Ze_2\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Ze_m
\]

and

\[
T(e_1)=e_1,
\qquad
T(e_j)=e_{j-1}+e_j.
\]

There is a quotient

\[
\pi:\Gamma_{m,L}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal N_{m,L}.
\]

The element `e_1` is central in `N_{m,L}`.

If a word `w` in the standard generators of `Gamma_{m,L}` has length at most `R` and

\[
\pi(w)=e_1^s,
\]

then P25 proves

\[
\boxed{
|s|_L
\le
R\binom{R+m-2}{m-1}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For completeness, the proof is short. Write `T=I+N`, `N^m=0`. For every integer height `h`, including negative `h`,

\[
T^he_j
=
\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}\binom hr e_{j-r}.
\]

Thus the `e_1` coefficient contributed by a base letter inserted at height `h` is a generalized binomial coefficient `binom(h,j-1)`. A word of length `R` contains at most `R` base letters and all insertion heights satisfy `|h|<=R`. The generalized-binomial identity

\[
\left|\binom{-n}{r}\right|=\binom{n+r-1}{r}
\]

and monotonicity give the uniform bound in (5.1).

## 5.2 Arithmetic finite-isotropy theorem

### `P01-P49A-ARITHMETIC-TORSION-VISIBILITY` — **PROVED**

If

\[
\boxed{
D\binom{D+m-2}{m-1}<\chi(L)L,
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

then the action of `Gamma_{m,L}` on its `D`-Rips complex is free.

### Proof

Let `F<=Gamma_{m,L}` be a nontrivial finite subgroup. The quotient

\[
\Gamma_{m,L}\to\mathbb Z
\]

kills every finite subgroup, so `F<=H_{m,L}`. Bass–Serre theory for

\[
H_{m,L}=C_L*F_{m-1}
\]

implies that `F` is conjugate to a nontrivial subgroup of `C_L`. Let its order be `q|L`, `q>=2`.

By (4.1), `F` contains an element `f` conjugate to a power `x_1^s` satisfying

\[
|s|_L
=\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}L
\ge\chi(L)L.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Suppose `F` stabilizes a simplex of the `D`-Rips complex. Choose a vertex `g` in its support. Both `g` and `fg` occur in the same finite support, so for the usual strict Rips convention

\[
|g^{-1}fg|<D.
\]

(The displayed theorem uses the safe integer threshold `D`; the same conclusion survives the non-strict convention after the standard harmless endpoint adjustment.)

Because `e_1` is central in the quotient `N_{m,L}`, conjugation does not change its `e_1` coordinate, so

\[
\pi(g^{-1}fg)=e_1^s.
\]

Applying (5.1) with radius `D` gives

\[
|s|_L
\le
D\binom{D+m-2}{m-1}
<\chi(L)L,
\]

contradicting (5.3).

Thus no nontrivial finite subgroup stabilizes a Rips simplex. A point stabilizer is finite because it permutes the finite vertex support of the point; the kernel of that permutation fixes a vertex and hence is trivial under left translation. Therefore every point stabilizer is trivial. ∎

## 5.3 The topological detector

The free product has

and `B alpha_m` fixes the `BC_L` wedge summand. Thus

\[

\simeq

be the cyclic class underlying the finite-Rips source. P25 checks with the homological Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence on finite lens-space skeleta that `xi_L` is nonzero. The inclusion

Naturality of the mapping-torus Wang boundary gives

\boxed{

Take any integer

By definition (0.4), inequality (5.2) holds. By P49A the `D`-Rips action is free. Hence there is a classifying map

The ordinary scalar assembly of the stage class is zero by the already validated torus-Bott collapse in the P25/P48 chain. Consequently the definition of the scalar finite-Rips injectivity modulus gives

## 5.5 Asymptotic and exact `m=2` evaluation

For fixed `m`,

\[
\binom{D+m-2}{m-1}
=
\frac{D^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}
+O_m(D^{m-2}).

# 6. Sharpness and what the refinement does not prove

## `P01-P49B-DIVISOR-UNIFORM-ONE-THIRD-SHARPNESS` — **PROVED / ROUTE CERTIFICATE**

The arithmetic improvement cannot replace `L/3` by any larger **uniform-in-L** constant using only the strategy “every nontrivial finite subgroup of `C_L` contains an element with a large circular residue.”

Indeed, whenever `3|L`, `C_L` contains a subgroup of order three,

\[
\langle x_1^{L/3}\rangle.
\]

Its two nontrivial elements both have circular residue exactly `L/3`. Hence `chi(L)=1/3` for every multiple of three.

## 8.1 `P04-T4-DETTILT-AFFINE-INVERSE-ISOTROPY-44`

Let `A` have iid centered isotropic rows in `R^m`, nonsingular almost surely. Under determinant-square tilt

\[
\frac{d\mathbb Q}{d\mathbb P}
=
\frac{\det(A)^2}{E\det(A)^2},
\]

P04 claims that, for deterministic `c` and `x=A^{-1}c`,

\[
E_Q[xx^T]=\frac{\|c\|^2}{m}I.
\]

Independent rederivation:

Expanding determinants over permutations and using row independence plus isotropy gives

\[
E\det(A)^2=m!.
\]

For the minor obtained by deleting row `i` and column `j`, covariance of two `(m-1)`-minors gives

\[
E\det A_{-i,-j}\det A_{-i,-k}
=(m-1)!\delta_{jk}.
\]

Cramer’s rule yields

\[
\det(A)x_j=\det A^{(j)}(c),
\]

where `A^{(j)}(c)` replaces column `j` by `c`. Expanding along that column,

\[
\det A^{(j)}(c)
=
\sum_i(-1)^{i+j}c_i\det A_{-i,-j}.
\]

In the product of two such expansions, terms with different deleted rows vanish by conditioning on a row that enters one minor linearly and is absent from the other; centeredness kills the term. Same-deleted-row terms give

\[
E[\det A^{(j)}(c)\det A^{(k)}(c)]
=\|c\|^2(m-1)!\delta_{jk}.
\]

Dividing by `m!` proves

\[
E_Q[x_jx_k]
=\frac{\|c\|^2}{m}\delta_{jk}.
\]

For `c=1_m`, `||c||^2=m`, hence

\[
E_Q\|A^{-1}1_m\|^2=m.
\]

**Verdict: PASS.**

This audit is deliberately local to the stated determinant-tilt theorem; it does not certify every downstream Target-4 claim.

## 10.1 Infinite trace-convergent projection sums — **DISPROVED / retained**

An infinite sum of nonzero finite-subgroup projections need not converge in reduced-`C^*` norm merely because the traces form a convergent numerical series. Orthogonal nonzero projection tails have norm one. Trace convergence is not norm convergence.

An embedding or finite-complex realization does not by itself isolate zero in the reduced spectrum.
