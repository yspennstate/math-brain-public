## `P01-P46A-GLSZ-FINITE-MATRIX-SOFT-SPECTRUM` — **PROVED**

There is a finitely presented elementary amenable group `G` and a scalar integral group-ring element

\[
A\in\mathbb ZG
\]

such that

\[
\dim_{L(G)}\ker A=\frac13\notin\Lambda_G=\mathbb Z[1/2],
\]

but the von-Neumann kernel projection

\[
P=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(A^*A)
\]

**does not belong to** `C_r^*(G)`, and `0` is **non-isolated** in the reduced spectrum of `A^*A`.

More strongly, if `D=A^*A`, its canonical spectral measure has an explicit sequence of positive atoms converging to zero. For every `n>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n(D)
=16\sin^2\!\frac{\pi}{4n+2}
\le \frac{4\pi^2}{(2n+1)^2}
}
\]

occurs with von-Neumann spectral mass at least

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{2^{2n+1}-1}.
}
\]

Consequently, for sufficiently small `eta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_D((0,\eta))
\ge 2^{-\,2\pi/\sqrt\eta-2}.
}
\]

Thus this example satisfies **finite matrix + forbidden kernel trace** but fails the indispensable **isolated reduced zero** condition in the strongest possible explicit way.

The source is the classical Grigorchuk–Linnell–Schick–Żuk construction. Their group `G` is metabelian; every finite subgroup is an elementary abelian `2`-group; `G'` is a countable direct sum of copies of `C_2`; and an element induced from the lamplighter subgroup has kernel dimension `1/3`. Their primary spectral calculation records atoms at

\[
4\cos(p\pi/q)
\]

of mass `1/(2^q-1)` for `(p,q)=1`. The quantitative soft-spectrum estimate above is the direct specialization `q=2n+1`, `p=n`.

# 3. Spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A*>=0` in a unital matrix `C*`-algebra with

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Then `0` is clopen in the spectrum, so continuous functional calculus gives

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

inside the algebra.

For `t>0`, functional calculus gives

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\]

For `eta>0`, the scalar function `eta/(x+eta)` equals `1` at `x=0`, hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le \frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` satisfy the **same** gap condition

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Define the bounded pseudoinverse by

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,\qquad A^\dagger(x)=x^{-1}\ (x\ge\delta).
\]

Because `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

so

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le \frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the same bound for `(1-P_B)P_A`. The standard two-projection identity

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|
=
\max\{\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\}
\]

therefore yields the sharper estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le \frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

Hence the requested bound `2||A-B||/delta` is valid but nonsharp.

The example `B=A+epsilon I` remains the correct warning: if `A` has nonzero kernel, then an arbitrarily small positive perturbation can remove the kernel completely while the new positive spectral gap shrinks to `epsilon`. Norm closeness without a **uniform common gap** does not control kernel projections.

# 4. `P01-P46A-GLSZ-FINITE-MATRIX-SOFT-SPECTRUM`

## 4.1 The primary finite matrix

Let

\[
H=C_2\wr\mathbb Z
=(\bigoplus_{\mathbb Z}C_2)\rtimes\mathbb Z
\]

be the classical lamplighter group. Write `t` for the shift and `a` for the lamp at the origin. Grigorchuk–Linnell–Schick–Żuk use the self-adjoint integral group-ring element

\[
A=t+at+t^{-1}+(at)^{-1}\in\mathbb ZH.
\]

Their spectral calculation gives eigenvalues

\[
4\cos(p\pi/q),\qquad (p,q)=1,
\]

with von-Neumann spectral mass

\[
\frac1{2^q-1}.
\]

For `p=1,q=2`, the eigenvalue is zero, so

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{L(H)}\ker A=\frac13.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

They then embed/induce this operator into their finitely presented metabelian group `G`, and explicitly note

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{L(G)}\ker A
=\dim_{L(H)}\ker A
=\frac13.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The same equality of spectral measures follows directly because, for `A in C H`, the restriction of the left regular representation of `G` to `H` is a Hilbert direct sum of copies of `lambda_H` over left cosets.

## 4.2 The scalar assembly trace range for `G`

The primary GLSZ source proves that `G` is metabelian and that

\[
G'\cong\bigoplus_{\mathbb N}C_2,
\qquad
G/G'\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Thus `G'` is amenable and the quotient `Z^2` satisfies Baum–Connes with coefficients. Chabert–Echterhoff permanence therefore gives Baum–Connes with coefficients for `G`, hence in particular surjectivity of the scalar degree-zero assembly map.

Every finite subgroup of `G` is an elementary abelian `2`-group, and `G'` contains elementary abelian subgroups of order `2^r` for every `r`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_G=\mathbb Z[1/2].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since

\[
\frac13\notin\mathbb Z[1/2],
\]

Lück's trace-range theorem immediately forbids the kernel projection from living in the reduced group `C*`-algebra.

More explicitly, put

\[
D=A^*A=A^2\ge0
\]

and let

\[
P_{vN}=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D)\in L(G).
\]

If `P_vN` belonged to `C_r^*(G)`, then its `K_0` class would be assembled by scalar surjectivity and its trace would lie in `Lambda_G`; but

\[
\tau_G(P_{vN})=\frac13,
\]

contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_{vN}\notin C_r^*(G).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

If zero were isolated in the reduced spectrum of `D`, continuous functional calculus would produce precisely `P_vN` in `C_r^*(G)`, contradicting (4.4). Hence

\[
\boxed{
0\text{ is non-isolated in }\sigma_{C_r^*(G)}(D).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is already a rigorous near-miss certificate for route (a): finite matrix and forbidden trace are present, but isolated reduced zero is impossible.

## 4.3 Quantitative low-energy atoms

The primary spectral formula gives more.

For `n>=1`, choose

\[
q=2n+1,\qquad p=n.
\]

Since `gcd(n,2n+1)=1`, the number

\[
\xi_n
=4\cos\frac{n\pi}{2n+1}
=4\sin\frac{\pi}{4n+2}
\]

is an eigenvalue of `A` with spectral mass

\[
\frac1{2^{2n+1}-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\lambda_n=\xi_n^2
=16\sin^2\frac{\pi}{4n+2}
\]

is a positive eigenvalue of `D=A^2`, and its spectral mass is at least the mass of the `+xi_n` eigenspace:

\[
\boxed{
\mu_D(\{\lambda_n\})
\ge\frac1{2^{2n+1}-1}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using `sin x<=x`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\le\frac{4\pi^2}{(2n+1)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This gives an explicit quantitative lower bound on low-energy spectral mass. For sufficiently small `eta>0`, choose an odd integer `q=2n+1` with

\[
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt\eta}<q\le \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt\eta}+2.
\]

Then `lambda_n<eta` and

\[
\mu_D((0,\eta))
\ge\frac1{2^q-1}
>2^{-q}
\ge
\boxed{
2^{-2\pi/\sqrt\eta-2}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The estimate is not claimed optimal. Its value for P01 is conceptual: the missing gap is witnessed by an explicit sequence of small positive spectral atoms, not merely by an abstract contradiction with assembly.

## 4.4 Why this matters for the program

This example simultaneously has:

| Property | Status |
|---|---|
| finite integral group-ring matrix | **YES** |
| finitely presented ambient group | **YES** |
| kernel trace outside `Lambda_G` | **YES: `1/3` outside `Z[1/2]`** |
| scalar Baum–Connes surjectivity | **YES** |
| von-Neumann kernel projection | **YES** |
| reduced-`C*` kernel projection | **NO** |
| isolated reduced zero | **NO** |
| explicit positive spectrum tending to zero | **YES** |

So finite presentation, amenability, a finite matrix, and even a forbidden strong-Atiyah trace do not come close to supplying route (a) unless the reduced spectral gap is proved independently.

# 5. `P01-P46B-GRABOWSKI-LAMPLIGHTER-GAPLESS`

Let

\[
L_p=C_p\wr\mathbb Z
=(\bigoplus_{\mathbb Z}C_p)\rtimes\mathbb Z,
\qquad p>1.
\]

Grabowski's Theorem 2 constructs a finite matrix

\[
T\in M_k(\mathbb ZL_p)
\]

whose von-Neumann kernel dimension is transcendental.

## 5.1 Exact scalar trace ring

Every finite subgroup of `L_p` maps trivially to the torsion-free quotient `Z`, so it lies in the base

\[
\bigoplus_{\mathbb Z}C_p.
\]

Every finite subgroup order divides `p^N` for some `N`. Conversely the base contains finite subgroups `C_p^N` of order `p^N`. Hence the ring generated by inverses of finite subgroup orders is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{L_p}=\mathbb Z[1/p].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

## 5.2 Scalar Baum–Connes is already true

The base is amenable normal and the quotient is `Z`. Chabert–Echterhoff permanence, together with Baum–Connes with coefficients for `Z`, gives Baum–Connes with coefficients for `L_p`. In particular scalar assembly is surjective.

Let

\[
\alpha=\dim_{L(L_p)}\ker T.
\]

Grabowski proves `alpha` is transcendental, hence

\[
\alpha\notin\mathbb Z[1/p].
\]

Exactly as in Section 4, if

\[
P_T=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(T^*T)
\]

belonged to a matrix algebra over `C_r^*(L_p)`, Lück's trace theorem would force its trace into `Lambda_{L_p}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_T\notin M_k(C_r^*(L_p)).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
0\text{ is non-isolated in }\sigma_{C_r^*(L_p)}(T^*T).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

By faithfulness of the canonical von-Neumann trace, for every `eta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}\otimes\tau_{L_p})
\bigl(\mathbf1_{(0,\eta)}(T^*T)\bigr)>0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Otherwise the spectral projection of `(0,eta)` would vanish, giving a gap and contradicting (5.3).

## 5.3 Important non-transfer

Grabowski's paper also studies irrational Novikov–Shubin invariants for certain lamplighter operators. Those operators are not automatically the same matrix as the transcendental-kernel matrix in Theorem 2.

Therefore this run **does not** import a quantitative small-spectrum asymptotic from the Novikov–Shubin part of the paper into the Theorem-2 matrix. For `T`, the rigorous conclusion here is non-isolation and positive spectral mass in every punctured neighborhood of zero, with no asserted rate.

This distinction prevents a new version of the old error “a von-Neumann invariant alone supplies reduced spectral control.”

# 6. `P01-P46C-LAMPLIGHTER-CANDIDATE-POOL-SCREEN`

The preceding proofs actually give the following screening principle specialized to a concrete family.

Let `p>1`, let `L_p=C_p wr Z`, and let

\[
C\in M_{r,s}(\mathbb C L_p).
\]

If

\[
\dim_{L(L_p)}\ker C\notin\mathbb Z[1/p],
\]

then

\[
\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(C^*C)
\notin M_s(C_r^*(L_p)),
\]

and zero is non-isolated in the reduced spectrum of `C^*C`.

Thus the entire finite-matrix exotic-trace pool supported on ordinary lamplighter groups is **structurally incompatible** with route (a).

This is stronger than saying no gap is currently known. The gap is forbidden because the ambient group is already scalar-Baum–Connes positive.

### Consequence for candidate selection

A future route-(a) search should screen candidates in this order:

1. Is the kernel trace genuinely outside the scalar assembly trace range?
2. Is scalar assembly already known surjective for the ambient group by amenability, Haagerup, hyperbolicity, or permanence?
3. If yes, **stop**: any such finite matrix is necessarily soft-spectrum at zero.
4. Only if scalar surjectivity is unresolved should substantial effort be spent proving an isolated reduced zero.

This saves the program from repeatedly rediscovering attractive but mathematically impossible gap candidates.

# 7. What the newest P25 result changes for route (b)

The latest complete P25 report proves a new invariant-congruence-cover mechanism for the triangular cyclic fillings

\[
H_{m,L}=\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\mid x_1^L=1\rangle,
\]

with triangular automorphism

\[
\alpha_m(x_1)=x_1,
\qquad
\alpha_m(x_j)=x_{j-1}x_j.
\]

The map

\[
H_{m,L}\to(\mathbb Z/L)^m,
\qquad x_j\mapsto e_j,
\]

has an `alpha_m`-invariant torsion-free free kernel of index `L^m`. P25 uses the corresponding finite-index free-by-cyclic subgroup to obtain the reduced-`C*` trace mesh

\[
\boxed{
\tau_*K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}))
\subseteq L^{-m}\mathbb Z.
}
\]

For the quadratic triangular family, combining this mesh with P18's matrix-stable local upper norm theorem and the pseudo-Sylvester rank sandwich gives true-kernel identification and a uniform fixed-matrix gap.

This is a real advance for P01. It shows that **nonperiodic monodromy does not itself destroy trace locking**.

The remaining gap to universal Baum–Connes is still large, because these theorems concern a fixed finite matrix. The scalar assembly isomorphism requires control of arbitrary/moving `K`-classes and their relations. The inherited P25 cyclic calibrations show that relation witnesses can require propagation linear in the relation length, while the available nonperiodic triangular cb windows are much smaller.

So the proof-side bottleneck can now be stated more sharply:

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed resolvents increasingly controlled; moving K-relations still uncontrolled.}
}

## Verdict: **PASS**

The proof was independently rederived rather than accepted from the status label.

### Step 1: invariant finite-index kernel

Define

\[
\pi:H_{m,L}\to(\mathbb Z/L)^m,
\qquad x_j\mapsto e_j.
\]

The induced unipotent linear map

\[
T(e_1)=e_1,
\qquad T(e_j)=e_{j-1}+e_j
\]

satisfies

\[
\pi\circ\alpha_m=T\circ\pi.
\]

Since `T` is invertible over `Z/L`,

\[
K=\ker\pi
\]

is `alpha_m`-invariant and

\[
[H_{m,L}:K]=L^m.
\]

### Step 2: `K` is torsion-free free

Every torsion element in the free product

\[
C_L*F_{m-1}
\]

is conjugate into the finite factor `C_L`. If

\[
g x_1^r g^{-1}\in K,
\]

then, because the target of `pi` is abelian,

\[
\pi(gx_1^r g^{-1})=r e_1.
\]

The restriction of `pi` to `C_L` is injective, so `r=0 mod L`. Thus `K` is torsion-free. Kurosh then makes `K` free; finite index makes it finite rank.

### Step 3: the free-by-cyclic subgroup

Let

\[
\Gamma=H_{m,L}\rtimes_{\alpha_m}\mathbb Z,
\qquad
N=K\rtimes_{\alpha_m}\mathbb Z.
\]

The subgroup `N` need not be normal; P25 correctly does not require normality. Since the stable letter lies in `N`, every coset of `N` has a representative in `H`, and two representatives define the same coset exactly when they differ by `K`. Hence

\[
\boxed{[\Gamma:N]=L^m.}
\]

The group `N` is torsion-free because any finite-order element maps trivially to `Z` and hence lies in torsion-free `K`.

### Step 4: trace integrality for `N`

`K` is free and therefore Haagerup; the quotient `Z` is amenable. The standard a-T-menability extension theorem for amenable quotient gives the Haagerup property for `N`. Higson–Kasparov gives Baum–Connes with coefficients, hence scalar Baum–Connes, for `N`.

Schick's trace theorem then gives, because `N` is torsion-free,

\[
\boxed{
\tau_*K_0(C_r^*(N))=\mathbb Z.
}
\]

### Step 5: finite-index reduced embedding and scaling

For **any** finite-index subgroup, normal or not, choose coset representatives. Rewriting the left regular representation gives a faithful reduced representation

\[
C_r^*(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow M_d(C_r^*(N)),
\qquad d=[\Gamma:N]=L^m.
\]

On group elements, the unnormalized matrix trace composed with the canonical trace is zero away from the identity and equals `d` at the identity, so

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_d\otimes\tau_N)(\iota(x))
=d\tau_\Gamma(x).
\]

For a projection `q` over `C_r^*(Gamma)`, the left side is an integer by Schick, therefore

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}\otimes\tau_\Gamma)(q)
\in L^{-m}\mathbb Z.
}
\]

The proof establishes only a containment in the mesh, exactly as P25 states. No periodic monodromy, normal finite-index subgroup, or reduced quotient map is hidden.

# 10. Failed routes and exact limitations

## 10.1 Lamplighter irrationality/transcendence does not revive route (a)

The bound

## 10.4 Fixed-matrix permanence is not universal assembly permanence

P25/P18 now control fixed matrices in increasingly nontrivial triangular fillings. This does not settle scalar Baum–Connes because a general `K`-class can require moving representatives and moving relations. The exact remaining scales are:

## 10.5 No locally compact bridge

Every new theorem in Sections 4–9 is discrete. No reduction from arbitrary second-countable locally compact groups to these discrete families has been proved. The original scope therefore remains untouched.
