## 0. Status and strongest new result

The original scalar Baum–Connes conjecture remains **OPEN** in this program:

\[
\mu_i^G:K_i^{\mathrm{top}}(G)\longrightarrow K_i(C_r^*(G)),\qquad i=0,1,
\]

for every second-countable locally compact group \(G\).

No universal proof and no rigorous scalar counterexample is obtained in this run. Every new theorem below is for finitely generated **discrete** groups; no reduction from arbitrary second-countable locally compact groups is asserted.

The strongest new package is `P01-P38A/B/C`.

1. `P01-P38A-SINGLE-MATRIX-HODGE-COMPRESSION` removes the last disjunction in the higher-Kazhdan counterexample route. Under a reduced degree-\(n\) Laplacian gap, one explicit finite rectangular group-ring matrix
   \[
   C_n=\operatorname{diag}(d_{n-1},d_n)
   \]
   has an isolated reduced zero and kernel projection \(Q_n\) satisfying
   \[
   \boxed{[Q_n]=[p_n]+k_{n-1}[1].}
   \]
   Hence \([Q_n]\) is scalar-assembled iff \([p_n]\) is scalar-assembled, and
   \[
   \tau(Q_n)=\beta_n^{(2)}(G)+k_{n-1}.
   \]
   A forbidden higher-Kazhdan class/trace is therefore **literally one single finite-matrix obstruction**, not merely a statement that one of two adjacent kernels must be bad.

2. `P01-P38B-OPTIMAL-CHEBYSHEV-CUTOFF` proves the exact minimax polynomial degree needed to approximate an isolated kernel projection. If
   \[
   \sigma(D)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad 0<\delta<M,
   \]
   then among real degree-\(N\) polynomials with \(p(0)=1\), the smallest possible error on \([\delta,M]\) is exactly
   \[
   \boxed{E_N=\operatorname{sech}(N\theta),\qquad
   \theta=\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}
   =2\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M}.}
   \]
   Thus the least degree giving error at most \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) is
   \[
   \boxed{N_{\min}=\left\lceil
   \frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\varepsilon)}
        {2\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M}}
   \right\rceil.}
   \]
   This strengthens the inherited P25 Chebyshev upper bound by proving **minimax optimality and an exact degree threshold**.

3. `P01-P38C-POLYNOMIAL-RELATION-BUDGET` combines the two. If the finite matrix has formal support radius \(r\), then \(D=C^*C\) has support radius at most \(2r\). With marked relation depth \(L\), let
   \[
   J=\left\lfloor\frac{L}{8r}\right\rfloor.
   \]
   A degree-at-most-\(J\) polynomial cutoff gives a representative of propagation at most \(L/4\), and such a polynomial achieves kernel-projection error at most \(\varepsilon\) **iff**
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{sech}(J\theta)\le\varepsilon
   }
   \]
   or equivalently
   \[
   \boxed{
   \frac\delta M\ge
   \tanh^2\!\left(
   \frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\varepsilon)}{2J}
   \right).
   }
   \]
   For fixed \(\varepsilon\) and \(L/r\to\infty\), the threshold is
   \[
   \frac\delta M
   \gtrsim
   \frac{16r^2\operatorname{arcosh}^2(1/\varepsilon)}{L^2}.
   \]
   Thus the previously observed diffusive \(L^{-2}\) scale is not an artifact of one rough polynomial estimate: it is the exact minimax scale for the Chebyshev/polynomial functional-calculus route.

These results do **not** produce a gap, an upper reduced-norm comparison, or a counterexample. They identify exactly what the finite-matrix/higher-Kazhdan route reduces to and exactly what polynomial functional calculus can and cannot do once a gap is supplied.

## 3. Spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let \(A\ge0\) in a unital matrix C*-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Because \(\{0\}\) is clopen in \(\sigma(A)\),

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the same matrix C*-algebra.

For \(t>0\), functional calculus gives

\[
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
=
\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}e^{-t\lambda}
\le e^{-t\delta}.
\]

For \(\eta>0\),

\[
\left\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\right\|
=
\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
\]

Now suppose positive \(A,B\) have the **same** gap:

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Define the bounded pseudoinverse

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,
\qquad
A^\dagger(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\quad(\lambda\ge\delta).
\]

Since \(BP_B=0\),

\[
AP_B=(A-B)P_B,
\]

hence

\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B
\]

and

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging \(A,B\) gives the same bound for \((1-P_B)P_A\). For orthogonal projections,

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|
=
\max\{\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\},
\]

so in fact

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

The requested factor-2 inequality follows, but is nonsharp.

The common-gap hypothesis is essential. If \(A\) has nonzero kernel and \(B=A+\varepsilon I\), then \(P_B=0\), \(\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\), but \(\|P_A-P_B\|=1\). The estimate is a sufficient norm-plus-uniform-gap criterion, not a proof that marked approximants possess such a gap or norm comparison.

# 4. `P01-P38A-SINGLE-MATRIX-HODGE-COMPRESSION` — PROVED

## Statement

Let \(G\) be discrete of type \(F_{n+1}\), \(n\ge1\), and use a finite cellular cochain segment

\[
C^{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}C^n\xrightarrow{d_n}C^{n+1},
\qquad
C^j=\ell^2(G)^{k_j}.
\]

Assume the reduced degree-\(n\) cohomological Laplacian

\[
\Delta_n=d_n^*d_n+d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*
\]

has a spectral gap at zero.

Form the **single** finite rectangular group-ring matrix

\[
\boxed{
C_n:=\operatorname{diag}(d_{n-1},d_n):
C^{n-1}\oplus C^n
\longrightarrow
C^n\oplus C^{n+1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
D_n=C_n^*C_n
=
\operatorname{diag}(d_{n-1}^*d_{n-1},d_n^*d_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then zero is isolated in the reduced spectrum of \(D_n\), the reduced kernel projection

\[
Q_n=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_n)
\]

belongs to

\[
M_{k_{n-1}+k_n}(C_r^*(G)),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
[Q_n]=[p_n]+k_{n-1}[1]
\quad\text{in }K_0(C_r^*(G)).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
[Q_n]\in\operatorname{im}\mu_0^G
\iff
[p_n]\in\operatorname{im}\mu_0^G.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For the canonical unnormalised matrix trace,

\[
\boxed{
\tau(Q_n)=\beta_n^{(2)}(G)+k_{n-1}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since \(\Lambda_G\) contains \(\mathbb Z\),

\[
\boxed{
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)\notin\Lambda_G
\iff
\tau(Q_n)\notin\Lambda_G.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus every Li–Nowak–Pooya higher-Kazhdan counterexample candidate with a reduced gap is already **one explicit finite-matrix candidate** satisfying all analytic requirements except the still-unknown forbidden trace/class itself.

## Proof

The reduced gap for \(\Delta_n\) implies gaps for the two partial Laplacians

\[
d_n^*d_n,
\qquad
d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*.
\]

The nonzero spectra of \(d_{n-1}^*d_{n-1}\) and \(d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*\) coincide, so \(d_{n-1}^*d_{n-1}\) is also gapped. Therefore the block diagonal \(D_n\) has gap

\[
\delta=\min\{\delta_{n-1},\delta_n\}>0.
\]

Write

\[
K_j=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(d_j^*d_j).
\]

Functional calculus on the block diagonal gives

\[
Q_n=K_{n-1}\oplus K_n.
\]

The canonically rederived `P01-P36-HODGE-KERNEL-SPLITTING` says

\[
[p_n]=[K_{n-1}]+[K_n]-k_{n-1}[1].
\]

Hence

\[
[Q_n]=[K_{n-1}]+[K_n]=[p_n]+k_{n-1}[1],
\]

proving (4.3).

The unit class is scalar-assembled from the trivial subgroup orbit, so adding \(k_{n-1}[1]\) does not change membership in the scalar assembly image. This proves (4.4).

Taking the canonical trace in the same identity gives

\[
\tau(Q_n)
=
\tau(p_n)+k_{n-1}
=
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)+k_{n-1},
\]

which proves (4.5); (4.6) follows because \(k_{n-1}\in\mathbb Z\subset\Lambda_G\).

## Significance

P36 gave the disjunctive conclusion that a nonassembled higher-Kazhdan class forces one adjacent kernel class to be nonassembled. P38A is stronger for the counterexample search: **no choice is needed**. The direct sum of the two adjacent differentials is one finite matrix whose kernel class is exactly the higher-Kazhdan class modulo an assembled free summand.

Therefore the higher-Kazhdan forbidden-trace program is not merely morally related to route (a); it is literally a special case of route (a) through the explicit matrix (4.1).

# 5. `P01-P38B-OPTIMAL-CHEBYSHEV-CUTOFF` — PROVED

## Statement

Let \(D\ge0\) in a unital C*-algebra satisfy

\[
\sigma(D)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad
0<\delta<M.
\]

Let

\[
P=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D).
\]

For each integer \(N\ge1\), consider all real polynomials \(p\) of degree at most \(N\) satisfying \(p(0)=1\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\deg p\le N,\ p(0)=1}
\sup_{x\in[\delta,M]}|p(x)|
=
\operatorname{sech}(N\theta),
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\theta
=
\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}
=
2\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The minimizer is the affine Chebyshev polynomial

\[
\boxed{
p_N(x)
=
\frac{T_N\!\left(\frac{M+\delta-2x}{M-\delta}\right)}
{T_N\!\left(\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}\right)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the least degree for which one can have

\[
p(0)=1,
\qquad
\sup_{[\delta,M]}|p|\le\varepsilon
\]

is exactly

\[
\boxed{
N_{\min}(\varepsilon;\delta,M)
=
\left\lceil
\frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\varepsilon)}
{2\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M}}
\right\rceil.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For the functional-calculus element \(q_N=p_N(D)\),

\[
\boxed{
\|q_N-P\|\le\operatorname{sech}(N\theta),
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

and if \(E_N=\operatorname{sech}(N\theta)\), then

\[
\boxed{
\|q_N^2-q_N\|\le E_N(1+E_N).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

If \(E_N<1/2\), spectral cutoff at \(1/2\) recovers exactly \(P\).

The degenerate case \(\delta=M\) is simpler: \(p(x)=1-x/M\) is exact on the spectrum, so degree one suffices.

## Proof of the upper bound

Let

\[
y(x)=\frac{2x-(M+\delta)}{M-\delta}.
\]

Then \(y\) maps \([\delta,M]\) onto \([-1,1]\), while

\[
y(0)=-\alpha,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}>1.
\]

Formula (5.3) differs from \(T_N(y(x))\) only by the harmless parity sign and satisfies \(p_N(0)=1\). Since \(|T_N|\le1\) on \([-1,1]\),

\[
\sup_{[\delta,M]}|p_N|
\le
\frac1{|T_N(\alpha)|}.
\]

For \(\alpha>1\),

\[
T_N(\alpha)=\cosh(N\operatorname{arcosh}\alpha),
\]

so the right side is \(\operatorname{sech}(N\theta)\).

Finally, putting \(s=\sqrt{\delta/M}\),

\[
\cosh(2\operatorname{artanh}s)
=
\frac{1+s^2}{1-s^2}
=
\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta},
\]

proving (5.2).

## Proof of minimax optimality

Let \(q\) be any degree-at-most-\(N\) polynomial in the \(y\)-variable with

\[
q(-\alpha)=1.
\]

Suppose, toward contradiction, that

\[
\|q\|_{[-1,1]}
<
\frac1{|T_N(\alpha)|}.
\]

Set

\[
r(y)=T_N(y)-T_N(-\alpha)q(y).
\]

At the \(N+1\) Chebyshev extrema

\[
y_j=\cos(j\pi/N),\qquad 0\le j\le N,
\]

we have \(T_N(y_j)=(-1)^j\), while

\[
|T_N(-\alpha)q(y_j)|<1.
\]

Therefore \(r(y_j)\) has sign \((-1)^j\). By the intermediate value theorem, \(r\) has at least one zero in each of the \(N\) intervals between consecutive extrema. Also

\[
r(-\alpha)=0.
\]

This gives at least \(N+1\) distinct real zeros for a polynomial of degree at most \(N\), a contradiction. Thus the Chebyshev error is minimax, proving (5.1).

Equation (5.4) is exactly the condition

\[
\operatorname{sech}(N\theta)\le\varepsilon.
\]

Functional calculus proves (5.5). On the kernel, \(q_N=1\); on the positive spectral subspace, its spectrum lies in \([-E_N,E_N]\). Hence

\[
|t^2-t|\le E_N(1+E_N)
\]

there, proving (5.6). If \(E_N<1/2\), the spectrum above \(1/2\) consists only of the exact kernel eigenvalue \(1\), so rounding recovers \(P\).

## Quantitative-K tolerance

If one wants the standard approximate-projection defect below \(1/4\), it is enough to require

\[
E_N(1+E_N)<\frac14,
\]

i.e.

\[
E_N<\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}.
\]

No upper reduced-norm comparison across different groups is being assumed in this theorem; it is an intrinsic statement inside one C*-algebra.

# 6. `P01-P38C-POLYNOMIAL-RELATION-BUDGET` — PROVED

## Statement

Let \(C\in M_{a,b}(\mathbb CG)\) have formal word-support radius at most \(r\), and put

\[
D=C^*C.
\]

Then \(D\) has support radius at most \(2r\). Assume

\[
\sigma(D)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta<M.
\]

Fix a relation-depth budget \(L>8r\), and set

\[
\boxed{J=\left\lfloor\frac{L}{8r}\right\rfloor.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every polynomial \(p(D)\) of degree at most \(J\) has propagation at most

\[
2rJ\le L/4.
\]

Among degree-at-most-\(J\) polynomials satisfying \(p(0)=1\), the best possible uniform kernel-projection error is exactly

\[
\boxed{
E_J
=
\operatorname{sech}\!\left(
2J\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M}
\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore, for any \(0<\varepsilon<1\), a degree-at-most-\(J\) polynomial cutoff with

\[
\|p(D)-P\|\le\varepsilon
\]

exists if and only if

\[
\boxed{E_J\le\varepsilon.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac\delta M
\ge
\tanh^2\!\left(
\frac{\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\varepsilon)}{2J}
\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For fixed \(\varepsilon\) and \(L/r\to\infty\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac\delta M
\ge
\frac{16r^2\operatorname{arcosh}^2(1/\varepsilon)}{L^2}
\,(1+o(1))
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

is the corresponding asymptotic threshold for this polynomial-calculus degree budget.

## Proof

A degree-\(J\) polynomial in an element of support radius \(2r\) has support radius at most \(2rJ\), proving the propagation statement.

The exact error formula (6.2) is P38B. Thus (6.3) is necessary and sufficient **within the class of degree-at-most-\(J\) polynomials with \(p(0)=1\)**.

Writing

\[
\theta=2\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\delta/M},
\]

condition \(\operatorname{sech}(J\theta)\le\varepsilon\) is equivalent to

\[
J\theta\ge\operatorname{arcosh}(1/\varepsilon),
\]

which is exactly (6.4). Since

\[
J=\frac{L}{8r}(1+o(1))
\]

and \(\tanh x=x+O(x^3)\), (6.5) follows.

## Important limitation

The minimax theorem is an exact lower bound on **polynomial degree**, not on the actual propagation of every conceivable representative. Special algebraic cancellations in a particular matrix can make \(p(D)\) propagate less than the naive degree-times-radius bound, and non-polynomial controlled constructions may behave differently. Thus (6.4) is an exact certificate/barrier for the standard polynomial functional-calculus route, not a universal lower bound for all possible controlled K-theory representatives.

## Application to P38A

If the two adjacent differential matrices in P38A have formal radius at most \(r\), then the single block matrix \(C_n\) has radius at most \(r\), while

\[
D_n=C_n^*C_n
\]

has radius at most \(2r\). Therefore the higher-Kazhdan class, modulo the assembled free summand \(k_{n-1}[1]\), has a finite-propagation approximate projection at the exact Chebyshev degree scale (5.4).

This gives a fully explicit bridge from the higher-Kazhdan obstruction to the controlled finite-matrix language used by P25.

## 7. Consequences for route (a): finite gapped counterexample

The decisive criterion remains

\[

\text{finite group-ring matrix}
+
\text{isolated reduced zero}
+
\text{actual reduced-C* kernel projection}
+
\text{forbidden scalar assembly class/trace}.
