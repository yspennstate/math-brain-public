# 3. Spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let \(A\ge0\) in a unital matrix \(C^*\)-algebra and assume

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Then the function which is \(1\) at zero and \(0\) on \([\delta,M]\) is continuous on \(\sigma(A)\), so

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the algebra.

Functional calculus gives

\[
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}e^{-t\lambda}
\le e^{-t\delta},
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
=\sup_{\lambda\ge\delta}\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If positive \(A,B\) have the **same** positive gap \(\delta\), let

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,\qquad A^\dagger(t)=t^{-1}\quad(t\ge\delta).
\]

Since \(BP_B=0\),

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]

so

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Reversing \(A,B\) and using the standard two-projection identity gives the sharper estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the requested factor-2 estimate is valid but nonsharp.

The common gap is indispensable: if \(A\) has a kernel and \(B=A+\varepsilon I\), then \(\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\) but \(P_B=0\), so the kernel projection can jump completely as the positive gap shrinks.

# 4. `P01-P41A-CYCLIC-FILLING-TRACE-LATTICE` — PROVED

Let

\[
G_L=C_L*\mathbb Z=\langle a,b\mid a^L=1\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{G_L,*}\bigl(K_0(C_r^*(G_L))\bigr)=\frac1L\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This statement is stronger than the generic Lück-ring restriction for this group.

## 4.1 The index-\(L\) free subgroup

Define

\[
\pi_L:G_L\to C_L,
\qquad
\pi_L(a)=a,
\quad
\pi_L(b)=e,
\]

and put

\[
H_L=\ker\pi_L.
\]

The sequence splits through the subgroup \(\langle a\rangle\cong C_L\).

A Reidemeister–Schreier transversal is

\[
1,a,\dots,a^{L-1}.
\]

For the generator \(a\), all Schreier generators are trivial. For \(b\), the Schreier generators are

\[
b_j=a^jba^{-j},\qquad j=0,\dots,L-1.
\]

The relator \(a^L\) rewrites trivially in the kernel. Hence

\[
\boxed{H_L\cong F_L.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Conjugation by \(a\) cyclically permutes the free generators \(b_j\), and

\[
G_L\cong F_L\rtimes C_L.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.2 Reduced crossed-product embedding

Put

\[
A_L=C_r^*(F_L).
\]

Since \(C_L\) is finite,

\[
C_r^*(G_L)\cong A_L\rtimes_r C_L.
\]

The regular crossed-product representation on the finite Hilbert \(A_L\)-module

\[
\ell^2(C_L,A_L)
\]

gives a faithful \(*\)-homomorphism

\[
\iota_L:C_r^*(G_L)\hookrightarrow M_L(A_L).
\tag{4.4}
\]

For

\[
x=\sum_{t\in C_L}x_tu_t,
\]

the canonical group trace is

\[
\tau_{G_L}(x)=\tau_{F_L}(x_e).
\]

In the regular matrix representation, the diagonal entries of \(\iota_L(x)\) are automorphic images of \(x_e\). The free-group trace is invariant under those automorphisms. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}_L\otimes\tau_{F_L})(\iota_L(x))
=L\tau_{G_L}(x).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The same identity holds after matrix stabilization and on \(K_0\).

## 4.3 Quantization

For \(L\ge2\), the primary Li–Nowak–Pooya free-group example recalls

\[
K_0(C_r^*(F_L))\cong\mathbb Z[1],
\]

and the canonical trace sends the generator to \(1\). Hence for every

\[
\xi\in K_0(C_r^*(G_L)),
\]

(4.5) implies

\[
L\tau_{G_L,*}(\xi)
=
\tau_{F_L,*}(\iota_{L,*}\xi)
\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Thus

\[
\tau_{G_L,*}K_0(C_r^*(G_L))
\subseteq\frac1L\mathbb Z.
\]

Conversely the finite-subgroup averaging projection

\[
e_L=\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}a^j
\]

belongs to \(C_r^*(G_L)\) and has

\[
\tau_{G_L}(e_L)=\frac1L.
\]

Therefore equality holds, proving (4.1).

## 4.4 Important distinction from Lück's ring

For this group, Lück's ring contains

\[
\mathbb Z[1/L],
\]

which permits denominators \(L^n\) of arbitrarily high power. It has no spacing \(1/L\).

Therefore the exact lattice (4.1) **cannot** be deduced from the generic modified trace theorem alone. It is a special finite-index-free-subgroup fact.

This matters below because trace locking needs an actual discrete spacing.

# 5. `P01-P41B-QUANTIZED-ROUNDING` — PROVED

Let \(A=A^*\) in \(M_k(C_r^*(G_L))\) satisfy

\[
\|A^2-A\|\le q<\frac14.
\]

Define

\[
Q=\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(A).
\]

Then \(Q\) belongs to \(M_k(C_r^*(G_L))\) and

\[
\boxed{
\|Q-A\|\le r(q),
\qquad
r(q)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4q}}2\le2q.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

For real \(t\) with

\[
|t^2-t|\le q,
\]

the spectrum lies in two intervals around \(0\) and \(1\). The maximal distance to the nearer point in \(\{0,1\}\) is

\[
r(q)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4q}}2.
\]

Functional calculus yields (5.1). The algebraic identity

\[
r(q)=\frac{2q}{1+\sqrt{1-4q}}
\]

gives \(r(q)\le2q\).

Consequently, for the unnormalized matrix trace,

\[
\boxed{
|\tau_{G_L}(Q)-\tau_{G_L}(A)|\le k r(q).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the quantitative bridge from an approximate polynomial cutoff to an exact lattice-valued projection trace.

\boxed{

# 8. What P41 does and does not prove

P41 gives a genuine exact scalar permanence statement:

> A fixed reduced-gapped finite matrix over \(F_2\), transferred through the nonamenable cyclic filling family and rounded by the P40 controlled functional calculus, has eventually **exactly the same canonical projection trace** as its limit kernel projection.

But the following stronger conclusions are **not** justified:

1. `Q_{N,L}` is **not** proved equal to the stage von Neumann kernel projection
   \[
   \mathbf1_{\{0\}}(D_L).
   \]
2. P41 does **not** prove that \(0\) is isolated in \(\sigma(D_L)\).
3. Exact equality of traces does **not** prove
   \[
   [Q_{N,L}]=\alpha[1]
   \]
   in \(K_0(C_r^*(G_L))\). The trace map has a nontrivial kernel.
4. The argument is restricted to this discrete virtually-free filling family and does not bridge to arbitrary second-countable locally compact groups.

The distinction between the rounded reduced-\(C^*\) projection and a genuine kernel projection remains essential.

# 9. New failed-route / no-go record

## 9.1 Lück-ring spacing is not available — DISPROVED shortcut

A tempting argument would be:

> `tau(Q)` and the limit trace both lie in `Lambda_G`, so sufficiently close traces must agree.

This is false in general and false as stated even for \(G_L\). Lück's ring is generated as a **ring** by inverse finite-subgroup orders; because \(C_L<G_L\), it contains powers \(1/L^n\). There is no \(1/L\) spacing.

P41 succeeds only because the special finite-index free subgroup proves the much sharper actual \(K_0\)-trace lattice

\[
\frac1L\mathbb Z.
\]

This distinction is now part of the permanent failed-route record.

## 9.2 Exact trace does not manufacture a gap

Even if

\[
\tau(Q_{N,L})=\alpha,
\]

positive stage spectrum may still accumulate at zero inside the low-energy sector captured by \(Q_{N,L}\). Trace locking alone does not separate the zero eigenspace from positive low spectrum.

A new ingredient would be required to promote P41 to an actual stage-kernel theorem: for example a quantitative low-energy mass estimate strong enough to beat the \(1/L\) lattice spacing, or a direct uniform lower spectral bound.

## 9.3 Two-sided local norm control still does not give global reduced functoriality

P25's new reverse theorem is finite-propagation and quantitative. It does not produce a global reduced quotient map. The nonamenable kernel obstruction remains respected.
