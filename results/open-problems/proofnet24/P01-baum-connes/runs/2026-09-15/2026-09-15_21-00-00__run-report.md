\[

# 3. Triangular family and current local cb theorem

Fix `m>=2`. Let

\[
F_m=\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\rangle
\]

and define the triangular automorphism

\[
\alpha_m(x_1)=x_1,\qquad
\alpha_m(x_j)=x_{j-1}x_j\quad (2\le j\le m).
\]

Put

\[
\Gamma_m=F_m\rtimes_{\alpha_m}\mathbb Z
\]

with stable letter `t`. The cyclic filling imposes `x_1^L=1` and gives

\[
\Gamma_{m,L}
=(C_L*F_{m-1})\rtimes_{\alpha_{m,L}}\mathbb Z.
\]

Define

\[
B_m=\frac{m^{m-1}}{(m-1)!},
\qquad
\Lambda_m=(8B_m)^{1/m},
\qquad
q_{m,L}=\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\Lambda_m}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The latest P25 report proves, uniformly over all matrix sizes, the reverse local norm estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|X\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
10\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)\|X_L\|
}
\tag{3.2}

for stage elements of propagation at most `R<=q_{m,L}/4`, where `X` is the unique no-wrap finite-support coefficient lift.

The P18/P25 forward estimate is

\[
\boxed{

on the common smaller range `R<=q_{m,L}/8`.

Therefore the local coefficient correspondence is a complete near-isometry whenever

\[
R_L\log L=o(L^{1/m}).
\tag{3.4}

# 4. The Bott class and its ordinary stage collapse

Because `alpha_m(x_1)=x_1`, the subgroup

\[
A=\langle x_1,t\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2
\]

is abelian. At the stage,

\[
A_L=\langle x_1,t\rangle\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z.
\]

Let `P_B` be a Bott projection over `C(T^2)=C_r^*(A)` and let `e` be a constant projection of the same rank. Write

\[
\beta=[P_B]-[e]\in\widetilde K_0(C(\mathbb T^2))\cong\mathbb Z
\]

and let

\[
\beta_m\in K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_m))
\]

be its image under the subgroup inclusion.

## 4.1 Parent nonvanishing

P25's proof is rederived here. Put `B=C_r^*(F_m)`. Naturality of the Pimsner–Voiculescu exact sequence for the invariant subgroup generated by `x_1` gives a commutative boundary square. For the identity action on `C(T)`, the reduced Bott class has boundary, up to sign, equal to the circle generator `[x_1]`.

Pimsner–Voiculescu compute

\[
K_1(C_r^*(F_m))\cong\mathbb Z^m
\]

with the free-generator unitary classes as the standard basis. Hence `[x_1]` is nonzero, and therefore

\[
\boxed{\beta_m\ne0.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a stable `K_0` statement, not an unstable projection-cancellation assertion.

## 4.2 Stage vanishing

On the amenable subgroup, the quotient is an honest C*-homomorphism

\[
\rho_L:C(\mathbb T^2)
\longrightarrow
C(\mu_L\times\mathbb T)
\cong
\bigoplus_{\zeta^L=1}C(\mathbb T).
\]

The reduced Bott line-bundle difference restricts on each circle component to a rank-zero class. Since `K_0(C(T)) ~= Z` is detected by rank,

\[
(\rho_L)_*(\beta)=0.
\]

After subgroup inclusion into the stage algebra,

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{m,L}=0\in K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}))
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for every `L`.

Again, (4.2) uses the genuine abelian quotient map only on `A -> A_L`; it does not invoke a global reduced quotient map `C_r^*(Gamma_m)->C_r^*(Gamma_{m,L})`.

# 5. New theorem P47A — tolerance-dependent stable relation escape

## `P01-P47A-TOLERANCE-BOTT-RELATION-ESCAPE` — PROVED

Fix

\[
m\ge2,
\qquad
0<\eta<\frac14.
\]

Then there is a fixed self-adjoint finite Laurent-polynomial matrix

\[
p_{B,\eta}=p_{B,\eta}^*\in M_k(\mathbb C[\mathbb Z^2])
\subset M_k(\mathbb C\Gamma_m)
\]

of some finite propagation `r_{B,eta}`, independent of `L`, such that:

1. `p_{B,eta}` rounds at `1/2` to `P_B`;
2. `||p_{B,eta}^2-p_{B,eta}||<eta`;
3. its stage evaluation rounds to a projection representing the zero class (4.2);
4. for all sufficiently large `L`, **every stable self-adjoint `eta`-idempotent null relation** from the stage Bott representative to the matching constant projection, allowing arbitrary matrix stabilization and an arbitrary common auxiliary `eta`-idempotent block, has propagation `R` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
R\ge
\frac{\log(1/(4\eta))}{20\Lambda_m}
\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\log L}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The constant is independent of stabilization size.

## 5.1 Constructing the fixed representative

Laurent polynomial matrices are dense in matrix algebras over `C(T^2)`. Choose

\[
d_\eta<\min\left\{\frac18,\frac\eta8\right\}
\]

and a self-adjoint finite Laurent polynomial `p_{B,eta}` with

\[
\|p_{B,\eta}-P_B\|<d_\eta.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Writing `h=p_{B,eta}-P_B`,

\[
p_{B,\eta}^2-p_{B,\eta}
=P_Bh+hP_B+h^2-h,
\]

so

\[
\|p_{B,\eta}^2-p_{B,\eta}\|
\le3d_\eta+d_\eta^2<\eta.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `d_eta<1/8`, the spectrum stays on the correct sides of `1/2`, and spectral rounding recovers `P_B`. The stage subgroup map is contractive, so the same approximation and defect bounds hold after restriction; rounding gives the stage restricted Bott class, which is zero by (4.2).

The propagation `r_{B,eta}` is fixed once `eta` is fixed.

## 5.2 Definition of a stable controlled null relation

A stable stage `eta`-relation of propagation at most `R` means a norm-continuous self-adjoint path

\[
a_t=a_t^*
\]

in some matrix algebra over the finite-support stage algebra, with

\[
\sup_t\|a_t^2-a_t\|\le\eta,
\qquad
\operatorname{prop}(a_t)\le R,
\]

joining

\[
p_{B,\eta,L}\oplus f_L
\quad\text{to}\quad
e\oplus f_L,
\]

where the same auxiliary self-adjoint `eta`-idempotent `f_L` occurs at both endpoints.

Ordinary stage `K_0`-vanishing guarantees existence of some finite relation after stabilization and finite-support approximation; no uniform propagation bound is supplied by ordinary `K_0`.

## 5.3 Lift under a subcritical-radius assumption

Assume for contradiction that

\[
R<
\frac{\log(1/(4\eta))}{20\Lambda_m}
\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\log L}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For fixed `eta` and all sufficiently large `L`, (5.4) implies

\[
R\le q_{m,L}/8.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Indeed the extra factor `1/log L` tends to zero.

Use the no-wrap finite-support coefficient lift to obtain a norm-continuous parent path `\widetilde a_t`. Because the relation depth is below the base wrap radius, multiplication of two lifted radius-`R` elements is exact at the coefficient level. Hence the lift of the stage defect is exactly

\[
\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t.
\]

The defect has propagation at most `2R`. Apply the independently audited reverse cb theorem (3.2) at radius `2R`:

\[
\|\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
20\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)
\|a_t^2-a_t\|.
\tag{5.6}
\]

By (5.4), the exponential factor is strictly smaller than `1/(4eta)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde a_t^2-\widetilde a_t\|<\frac14
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

uniformly in `t`.

## 5.4 Parent spectral rounding contradiction

For a self-adjoint element with idempotent defect strictly below `1/4`, the spectrum avoids `1/2`. Therefore

\[
\widetilde P_t
=
\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(\widetilde a_t)
\]

is a norm-continuous parent projection path.

At the first endpoint, `p_{B,eta}` rounds to `P_B`; the lifted auxiliary block rounds to some parent projection `F`. At the final endpoint, `e` is already a projection and the same lifted auxiliary block rounds to the same `F`. Hence

\[
[P_B]+[F]=[e]+[F]
\]

in stable parent `K_0`, so cancellation inside the abelian group `K_0` gives

\[
[P_B]-[e]=0,
\]

contradicting (4.1).

Therefore (5.4) is impossible and (5.1) follows.

## 5.5 Recovery and strengthening of P25's constant

For the tolerance used by P25,

\[
\eta=\frac1{64},
\]

(5.1) becomes

\[
R\ge
\frac{\log16}{20\Lambda_m}
\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\log L},
\]

exactly recovering `P25-TRIANGULAR-BOTT-RELATION-ESCAPE-33`.

The new point is that the proof works for **every fixed** `0<eta<1/4`, with the exact tolerance dependence `log(1/(4eta))`.

For `m=2`, `B_2=2` and `Lambda_2=4`, so

\[
\boxed{
R\ge
\frac{\log(1/(4\eta))}{80}
\frac{\sqrt{L-1}}{\log L}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

At `eta=1/64`, the constant is `log 16 / 80`.

# 6. New theorem P47B — the escaping class is scalar assembled

## `P01-P47B-ASSEMBLED-BOTT-CONTROL-JUMP` — PROVED

For every fixed `m>=2`:

1. the parent `Gamma_m` satisfies Baum–Connes with coefficients, hence scalar assembly is an isomorphism in degrees `0,1`;
2. every stage `Gamma_{m,L}` satisfies Baum–Connes with coefficients, hence scalar assembly is an isomorphism in degrees `0,1`;
3. the nonzero parent Bott class `beta_m` is therefore an **actual coefficient-free assembled class**;
4. its finite-support stage evaluation represents zero in ordinary stage `K_0`;
5. nevertheless every stable stage `eta`-relation has the lower bound (5.1).

### Proof of (1)

There is an extension

\[
1\to F_m\to\Gamma_m\to\mathbb Z\to1.
\]

`F_m` has the Haagerup property and the quotient `Z` is amenable. The standard a-T-menability extension theorem for amenable quotient gives the Haagerup property for `Gamma_m`. Higson–Kasparov gives Baum–Connes with coefficients for Haagerup groups.

### Proof of (2)

The previously audited triangular congruence construction gives a torsion-free free-by-cyclic subgroup

\[
N_{m,L}\le\Gamma_{m,L}
\]

with

\[
[\Gamma_{m,L}:N_{m,L}]=L^m.
\]

The same free-by-cyclic Haagerup argument applies to `N_{m,L}`. Haagerup is stable under finite extensions, hence `Gamma_{m,L}` is Haagerup. Higson–Kasparov again gives Baum–Connes with coefficients.

### Consequence

This is not a counterexample to scalar Baum–Connes; it is the opposite. The family is a **Baum–Connes-positive calibration family** in which the exact quantitative relation obstruction survives.

Thus even the very strong information

\[
\mu_i^{\Gamma_m}\text{ and }\mu_i^{\Gamma_{m,L}}\text{ are isomorphisms}
\]

does not imply that a fixed short assembled target representative which becomes ordinary-`K_0` trivial at the stages has any null relation inside the subcritical local norm window.

This cleanly separates:

- **ordinary scalar assembly** — an isomorphism here;
- **local reduced norm comparison** — asymptotically complete-isometric on (3.4);
- **controlled relation propagation** — forced to the boundary scale by (5.1).

# 7. New theorem P47C — Baum–Connes does not restore reduced quotient functoriality

## `P01-P47C-BC-DOES-NOT-RESTORE-REDUCED-QUOTIENT-FUNCTORIALITY` — PROVED

Let

\[
q_L:\Gamma_m\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma_{m,L}
\]

be the triangular cyclic filling quotient. For every `m>=2` and `L>=2`, its kernel is nonamenable. Therefore the algebraic group quotient does **not** extend to a *-homomorphism

\[
C_r^*(\Gamma_m)\longrightarrow C_r^*(\Gamma_{m,L}).
\tag{7.1}
\]

This remains true even though scalar Baum–Connes is an isomorphism for both groups.

## 7.1 Nonamenability certificate

Inside the free kernel `F_m`, the quotient imposes `x_1^L=1`. Hence the kernel contains

\[
x_1^L
\quad\text{and}\quad
x_2x_1^Lx_2^{-1}.
\]

These two elements do not commute in the free group and generate a nonabelian free subgroup. Thus the quotient kernel is nonamenable.

## 7.2 Reduced-map criterion

For a discrete quotient `G -> G/N`, the algebraic quotient extends to the reduced group C*-algebras exactly when the quotient representation is weakly contained in the left regular representation, equivalently when `N` is amenable. This was independently audited in the recovered legacy program and remains a required functoriality gate.

Since `ker q_L` is nonamenable, (7.1) does not exist.

## 7.3 Exact lesson

One can of course use the two scalar assembly isomorphisms to manufacture an **abstract group homomorphism on K-groups** by transporting the natural topological map through assembly. That abstract K-group map is not a reduced C*-algebra quotient homomorphism, and it must not be silently identified with finite-support coefficient evaluation.

Accordingly, P47B does not contradict naturality: the finite-support class `beta_{m,L}` is produced by the genuine abelian subgroup quotient and stage inclusion, not by a nonexistent global reduced quotient map.

This is a concrete theorem-level example showing why “Baum–Connes is true at both endpoints” does not repair the killed quotient-functoriality shortcut.

# 8. Consequences for the P01 approximation program

## 8.1 Exact four-scale separation

For the scalar-assembled Bott family, the four relevant scales are now explicit:

1. **parent representative scale:** `r_{B,eta}=O_eta(1)`, independent of `L`;
2. **stage ordinary class:** zero for every `L`;
3. **stage stable relation scale:** at least
   \[
   c_{m,\eta}\frac{L^{1/m}}{\log L};
   \]
4. **two-sided local cb near-isometry scale:** every
   \[
   R_L=o\!\left(\frac{L^{1/m}}{\log L}\right)
   \]
   has distortion tending to one.

Hence the required relation is forced to the precise boundary order of the current norm theorem.

## 8.2 A rigorous no-go for a common permanence shortcut

The following principle is **DISPROVED**:

> If a class is scalar assembled, has a fixed-propagation representative, and the parent/stage finite-propagation operator spaces become completely near-isometric, then stage ordinary `K`-relations can be chosen inside the same near-isometric propagation regime.

P47B supplies a counterexample inside Baum–Connes-positive groups.

## 8.3 Why this does not solve scalar Baum–Connes

The theorem does not produce a nonassembled class. It instead proves that an approximation proof of the universal scalar conjecture cannot stop after proving local cb norm convergence for representatives. It needs one of the following genuinely stronger ingredients:

- a source-side controlled assembly relation whose target propagation can be bounded **at the boundary scale**;
- a class-specific controlled naturality theorem that bypasses an explicit target nullhomotopy;
- a stronger local norm theorem extending beyond the relation-escape threshold;
- or a different global permanence mechanism not based on transporting controlled representatives through quotient stages.

This identifies a sharper proof bottleneck than “fixed resolvent control”. Fixed resolvents are increasingly tractable; moving relations remain the hard gate.

## 8.4 Trace-zero sector is genuinely different from the finite-matrix trace route

The Bott difference has canonical trace zero. Therefore the trace-mesh / rank-sandwich mechanism that solved many fixed kernel-projection problems cannot detect this relation escape.

This cleanly separates two live sectors:

- **trace-sensitive fixed kernel projections**, where trace quantization plus rank monotonicity can identify the true kernel;
- **trace-zero moving K-relations**, where ordinary class equality may require propagation at the limit of the cb norm window.

The universal scalar Baum–Connes problem contains both phenomena and a complete proof must control both.

# 9. Mandatory spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A^*>=0` in a unital C*-algebra satisfy

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta\le M.
\]

Then zero is isolated, so continuous functional calculus gives

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

inside the C*-algebra.

## 9.1 Heat cutoff

On the kernel, `e^{-tA}=1`; on the positive spectrum,

\[
|e^{-tx}|\le e^{-t\delta}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

## 9.2 Resolvent cutoff

For `eta>0`, on the kernel

\[
\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}=1,
\]

while for `x>=delta`,

\[
0\le\frac\eta{x+\eta}\le\frac\eta{\delta+\eta}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le
\frac\eta{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

## 9.3 Common-gap perturbation

Let positive `A,B` satisfy

\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]

Let `A^\dagger` be the bounded pseudoinverse, so

\[
\|A^\dagger\|\le\delta^{-1}.
\]

Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B.
\]

Thus

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the same bound for `(1-P_B)P_A`. The standard two-projection identity yields

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le
\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Hence the requested weaker estimate

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|\le2\|A-B\|/\delta
\]

is valid but nonsharp.

## 9.4 Shrinking-gap warning

Take a nonzero positive `A` with kernel and set

\[
B=A+\varepsilon I.
\]

Then

\[
\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\to0,
\]

but `P_B=0` while `P_A\ne0`. The positive gap of `B` is only `epsilon`; there is no common lower gap independent of the perturbation.

Thus norm closeness plus a **uniform common gap** is a sufficient spectral-stability hypothesis, not a proof that approximating group operators have such a gap.
