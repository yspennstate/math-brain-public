For every fixed matrix-valued self-adjoint group-ring element

\[
X\in M_k(\mathbb C F_2),

\[
\boxed{
 d_H\!\left(

 \right)\to0.
}
\]

The proof uses only the **forward** completely bounded P40 upper-norm theorem together with exact local canonical-trace transport. It does not use a global reduced group-C* quotient map.

## `P01-P44C-FIXED-RESOLVENT-CALCULUS-AND-SINGULAR-SPECTRA` — **PROVED**

For fixed self-adjoint `X`, every fixed resolvent norm converges:

\[
\boxed{
\|(X_L-z)^{-1}\|
\longrightarrow
\|(X-z)^{-1}\|,
\qquad z\notin\sigma(X).
}
\]

\[
\|f(X_L)\|\to\|f(X)\|
\]

and the canonical traces of `f(X_L)` converge to the parent trace. For arbitrary fixed (not necessarily self-adjoint) `Y`, the positive singular spectra satisfy

\[
\boxed{

and in particular `||Y_L|| -> ||Y||`.

# 4. Elementary spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A*>=0` in a unital matrix C*-algebra and suppose

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],
\qquad 0<\delta\le M.
\]

The characteristic function of `{0}` is continuous on `sigma(A)`, so

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the C*-algebra.

For `t>0`, scalar functional calculus gives

\[
\boxed{
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `eta>0`, the scalar function `eta/(x+eta)` is `1` at `x=0` and at most `eta/(delta+eta)` on the positive spectral interval, hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now let positive `A,B` both satisfy the **same** gap lower bound `delta`, and let `P_A,P_B` be their kernel projections. Define

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,
\qquad
A^\dagger(x)=x^{-1}\quad(x\ge\delta).
\]

Since `BP_B=0`,

\[
(1-P_A)P_B
=A^\dagger A P_B
=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` gives the same bound for `(1-P_B)P_A`. The standard two-projection norm identity gives

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|
\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Hence the requested estimate

\[
\|P_A-P_B\|\le2\|A-B\|/\delta
\]

is valid but nonsharp.

The common gap is essential. If `B=A+epsilon I`, a kernel of `A` disappears while `||A-B||=epsilon`; if the positive gap is allowed to shrink with `epsilon`, projection stability fails completely.

P44 below does not assume such a common gap. It proves full spectral-set convergence under a different pair of hypotheses, but it cannot by itself prevent a positive spectral branch from converging into a parent zero eigenvalue.

# 5. New abstract theorem: upper polynomial norm + trace determines the full spectral set

## `P01-P44A-ONE-SIDED-NORM-PLUS-TRACE-HAUSDORFF` — **PROVED**

Let `(A_j,tau_j)` and `(A,tau)` be unital C*-algebras with faithful finite traces. Fix `k>=1`, and let

\[
X_j=X_j^*\in M_k(A_j),
\qquad
X=X^*\in M_k(A).
\]

Write

\[
\tau_j^{(k)}=\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_j,
\qquad
\tau^{(k)}=\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau.
\]

Assume:

### HN — one-sided upper polynomial norm control

For every real polynomial `p`,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\|p(X_j)\|
\le
\|p(X)\|.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### HT — polynomial trace convergence

For every real polynomial `p`,

\[
\boxed{
\tau_j^{(k)}(p(X_j))
\longrightarrow
\tau^{(k)}(p(X)).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
 d_H(\sigma(X_j),\sigma(X))\to0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No reverse norm inequality is assumed.

### Proof

Put

\[
K_j=\sigma(X_j),
\qquad
K=\sigma(X).
\]

Applying HN to the polynomial `p(t)=t` shows that the `X_j` are eventually uniformly bounded. Choose `B>0` with

\[
K_j,K\subset[-B,B]
\]

for all sufficiently large `j`.

Define finite positive spectral measures on `[-B,B]` by

\[
\int f\,d\mu_j
=
\tau_j^{(k)}(f(X_j)),
\qquad
\int f\,d\mu
=
\tau^{(k)}(f(X)).
\tag{5.4}
\]

for continuous `f`.

By HT, integrals of every polynomial converge. Since polynomials are uniformly dense in `C([-B,B])` and the total masses converge by `p=1`, it follows that

\[
\boxed{\mu_j\Rightarrow\mu}
\tag{5.5}
\]

weakly.

Because `tau^(k)` is faithful, the support of `mu` is the **entire** spectrum `K`. Indeed, if `U` is an open interval meeting `K`, choose a nonzero continuous `f>=0` on `K` supported in `U cap K`. Then `f(X)` is a nonzero positive element, so faithfulness gives

\[
\tau^{(k)}(f(X))>0.
\]

Hence `mu(U)>0`.

We now prove both Hausdorff inclusions.

### Lower inclusion: parent spectral points cannot disappear

Fix `lambda in K` and `epsilon>0`. Let

\[
U=(\lambda-\epsilon,\lambda+\epsilon).
\]

As just observed,

\[
\mu(U)>0.
\]

Portmanteau gives

\[
\liminf_j\mu_j(U)\ge\mu(U)>0.
\]

Thus, for all sufficiently large `j`,

\[
\mu_j(U)>0,
\]

which forces

\[
K_j\cap U\ne\varnothing.
\]

By compactness of `K`, finitely many such neighborhoods give

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x\in K}\operatorname{dist}(x,K_j)\to0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Upper inclusion: stage outliers are forbidden by one-sided norm control

Suppose, toward contradiction, that there are `epsilon>0`, a subsequence (still indexed by `j`), and points

\[
x_j\in K_j
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{dist}(x_j,K)\ge\epsilon.
\tag{5.7}
\]

After passing to a further subsequence, compactness of `[-B,B]` gives

\[
x_j\to x
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{dist}(x,K)\ge\epsilon.
\]

Choose a continuous real function `f` on `[-B,B]` with

\[
f|_K=0,
\qquad
f(x)=1.
\]

By Weierstrass approximation, choose a real polynomial `p` such that

\[
\|p-f\|_{\infty,[-B,B]}<\frac18.
\]

Then

\[
\|p(X)\|
=
\sup_{y\in K}|p(y)|
<\frac18.
\tag{5.8}
\]

But for all sufficiently large `j`, continuity of `p` and `x_j->x` gives

\[
|p(x_j)|>\frac34.
\]

Since `x_j in K_j`, spectral calculus gives

\[
\|p(X_j)\|
\ge |p(x_j)|
>\frac34,
\tag{5.9}
\]

contradicting HN and (5.8).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x\in K_j}\operatorname{dist}(x,K)\to0.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Combining (5.6) and (5.10) proves (5.3). QED.

# 6. Why both hypotheses in P44A are genuinely doing work

The theorem separates two logically different spectral failures.

## 6.1 Upper norm control alone can lose parent spectral points

Take

\[
A=\mathbb C^2,
\qquad
X=(0,1),
\]

and stages

\[
A_j=\mathbb C,
\qquad
X_j=0.
\]

For every polynomial `p`,

\[
|p(0)|
\le
\max\{|p(0)|,|p(1)|\}
=
\|p(X)\|.
\]

Thus HN holds perfectly. But

\[
\sigma(X_j)=\{0\},
\qquad
\sigma(X)=\{0,1\},
\]

so the point `1` disappears. A faithful trace on the parent detects the missing spectral mass; HT fails.

## 6.2 Trace moments alone can hide vanishing-mass spectral outliers

Let

\[
A_j=M_j(\mathbb C)
\]

with normalized trace and

\[
X_j=\operatorname{diag}(2,0,\ldots,0).
\]

Let the limit be `X=0` in `C`.

For every polynomial `p`,

\[
\tau_j(p(X_j))
=\frac1j p(2)+\left(1-\frac1j\right)p(0)
\to p(0).
\]

Thus HT holds, but the spectral outlier `2` persists forever. HN fails, as it must.

These two examples show that P44A is not a disguised consequence of weak trace convergence or of one-sided norm semicontinuity separately.

# 7. Application to the cyclic free-product filling family

We now apply P44A to the established P40 family.

Let

\[
G_\infty=F_2=\langle a,b\rangle,
\qquad
G_L=C_L*\mathbb Z=\langle a,b\mid a^L=1\rangle.
\]

Fix a matrix size `k` and a self-adjoint finite group-ring matrix

\[
X=X^*\in M_k(\mathbb C F_2).
\]

Let `X_L` be the coefficientwise algebraic quotient evaluation in

\[
M_k(C_r^*(G_L)).
\]

No global homomorphism

\[
C_r^*(F_2)\to C_r^*(G_L)
\]

is asserted.

## 7.1 HN from the genuine P40 upper reduced-norm theorem

The canonical P01 result `P01-P40B-POWER-AMPLIFIED-UPPER-NORM` states that, for any matrix-valued formal element `Y` of support radius `R` and `L>=8R+1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|Y_L\|
\le
\Gamma(R,L)\|Y\|,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(R,L)
=
\exp\!\left[
\frac{4R}{L-1}
\left(
\log 13+3\log\frac{L-1}{2}
\right)
\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The constant is independent of matrix size, and for every fixed `R`,

\[
\Gamma(R,L)\to1.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Now fix a real polynomial `p`. Since algebraic quotient evaluation respects addition, multiplication and adjoint,

\[
p(X_L)=(p(X))_L.
\tag{7.4}
\]

The formal group-ring matrix `p(X)` has some fixed finite support radius `R_p`. Therefore P40B gives

\[
\|p(X_L)\|
\le
\Gamma(R_p,L)\|p(X)\|,
\]

for all sufficiently large `L`. By (7.3),

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{L\to\infty}\|p(X_L)\|
\le\|p(X)\|.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus HN holds.

## 7.2 HT from exact local canonical-trace transport

For a finite group-ring matrix `Y`, the unnormalized matrix canonical trace

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_G
\]

is the sum of the identity coefficients of the diagonal entries.

Fix `p`. The support of `p(X)` is finite. In the quotient

\[
F_2=\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z
\to
C_L*\mathbb Z,
\]

a nontrivial reduced free-product word can map to the identity only if an `a`-syllable becomes trivial modulo `L`; in particular the shortest new relation has length `L`. Hence, once `L` is larger than the fixed word radius needed by `p(X)`, no nonidentity support word of `p(X)` becomes the identity.

Therefore, for every fixed polynomial `p`,

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_{G_L})(p(X_L))
=
(\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_{F_2})(p(X))
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

for all sufficiently large `L`.

Thus HT holds, in fact eventually exactly for each fixed polynomial.

## `P01-P44B-FREE-PRODUCT-FIXED-SPECTRAL-CONVERGENCE` — **PROVED**

Applying P44A gives

\[
\boxed{
 d_H\!\left(
 \sigma_{M_k(C_r^*(G_L))}(X_L),
 \sigma_{M_k(C_r^*(F_2))}(X)
 \right)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This is a **full spectral-set convergence theorem for every fixed self-adjoint finite matrix**, not merely convergence of one bottom spectral edge.

The proof uses:

- a genuine matrix-stable upper reduced-norm theorem (P40), and
- exact local trace transport,

but not:

- a quotient-induced reduced-C* homomorphism;
- reverse completely bounded norm control;
- a parent or stage spectral gap;
- coefficient Baum–Connes;
- strong convergence as a substitute for operator-norm control.

# 8. Resolvents, continuous functional calculus and singular spectra

## `P01-P44C-FIXED-RESOLVENT-CALCULUS-AND-SINGULAR-SPECTRA` — **PROVED**

Continue in the setting of P44B.

## 8.1 Continuous functional-calculus norms

Choose a common compact interval `[-B,B]` containing all late spectra. If `f` is any fixed continuous complex-valued function on this interval, Hausdorff convergence of compact sets implies

\[
\sup_{x\in\sigma(X_L)}|f(x)|
\to
\sup_{x\in\sigma(X)}|f(x)|.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|f(X_L)\|\to\|f(X)\|.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The weak spectral-measure convergence proved inside P44A also gives

\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_{G_L})(f(X_L))
\to
(\operatorname{Tr}_k\otimes\tau_{F_2})(f(X)).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

for every fixed continuous `f`.

## 8.2 Fixed resolvent norms

Let

\[
z\in\mathbb C\setminus\sigma(X).
\]

Hausdorff convergence implies that `z` is outside `sigma(X_L)` for every sufficiently large `L`, and

\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(X_L))
\to
\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(X)).
\]

Because `X_L` and `X` are self-adjoint,

\[
\|(X_L-z)^{-1}\|
=
\frac1{\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(X_L))},
\]

and similarly at the parent. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|(X_L-z)^{-1}\|
\longrightarrow
\|(X-z)^{-1}\|.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is a genuine fixed-observable **reduced resolvent-norm theorem** across a nonamenable marked approximation.

It is numerical norm convergence of separately defined resolvents in different reduced C*-algebras; it does **not** assert a nonexistent global homomorphism carrying one resolvent to the other.

## 8.3 Arbitrary fixed matrices and singular spectra

Let

\[
Y\in M_{r,s}(\mathbb C F_2)
\]

be arbitrary. Then

\[
D=Y^*Y\in M_s(\mathbb C F_2)
\]

is positive and fixed, and stage evaluation gives exactly

\[
D_L=Y_L^*Y_L.
\]

Applying P44B to `D` gives

\[
\boxed{
 d_H(\sigma(Y_L^*Y_L),\sigma(Y^*Y))\to0.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\|Y_L\|\to\|Y\|.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Equivalently, one can apply P44B to the self-adjoint dilation

\[
H(Y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&Y\\
Y^*&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

## 8.4 Uniform invertibility when zero is absent

If

\[
0\notin\sigma(Y^*Y),
\]

then (8.4) implies that for all sufficiently large `L`,

\[
0\notin\sigma(Y_L^*Y_L),
\]

and the lower singular spectral edge converges to the parent lower edge. Thus a **fixed invertible/bounded-below matrix has eventual uniform bounded-below control** along the cyclic fillings.

This is a clean permanence result requiring no trace lattice and no rank sandwich because there is no parent zero eigenvalue to split.

# 9. Exact limitation: Hausdorff convergence does not preserve an isolated kernel gap

Suppose instead

\[
\sigma(D)
\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M]
\]

with a nonzero parent kernel.

P44 gives

\[
d_H(\sigma(D_L),\sigma(D))\to0.
\]

This **does not** imply that the stage positive spectrum is eventually bounded below by, say, `delta/2`. The compact sets

\[
K_L=\{0,1/L\}\cup[\delta,M]
\]

converge in Hausdorff distance to

\[
K=\{0\}\cup[\delta,M]
\]

while the stage positive gap collapses.

Therefore:

- P44 settles fixed spectral/resolvent convergence away from the kernel;
- P42/P43's algebraic rank lower bound, trace quantization and plateau rounding remain genuinely necessary to identify the actual kernel and rule out a small positive branch near zero.

This distinction prevents a circular use of spectral convergence as if it already supplied the common-gap hypothesis needed by the elementary kernel-projection stability lemma.

# 10. Relation to route (b) and to moving controlled K-theory

P44 changes the proof-side obstruction in a precise way.

For **fixed self-adjoint finite matrices**, the P40 forward upper reduced-norm theorem is already strong enough, together with canonical trace transport, to recover the **entire reduced spectrum** and all fixed resolvent norms. A reverse cb map is not needed for this fixed spectral question.

But this does **not** settle ordinary `K`-theory continuity or scalar Baum–Connes permanence.

Current P25 results show that, even on the same family, a finite-stage class may have a short representative but require a stable nullhomotopy/equivalence relation at propagation of order at least `L/log L` (and exact relations can require a linear relation-depth scale). Thus the remaining global proof bottleneck is not merely locating fixed spectra. It is controlling **moving relations**:

1. moving matrix/support radii;
2. source Rips/direct-system relation scales;
3. target stable `K`-equivalence or nullhomotopy propagation;
4. matrix-stable upper reduced norm on the support scale of those moving defects.

P44 should therefore be viewed as a fixed-observable spectral theorem inside route (b), not as a Baum–Connes proof.

# 11. Counterexample route after P44

The scalar counterexample criterion remains unchanged:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite group-ring matrix}
+\text{ isolated reduced zero}
+\text{ actual reduced-C* kernel projection}
+\text{ scalar-assembly-forbidden class/trace}.
}
\]

The distinctions remain:

- a finite matrix may have a von-Neumann kernel projection without that projection lying in the reduced C*-algebra;
- an exotic von-Neumann dimension is not itself a `K_0(C_r^*(G))` class;
- a reduced gap is what upgrades the kernel projection into the reduced C*-algebra by continuous functional calculus;
- a trace outside the scalar assembly range only obstructs scalar surjectivity once it is the trace of an actual reduced-C* `K_0` class.

P44 gives no new forbidden trace and no counterexample. It instead screens fixed-matrix spectral behavior on a family where upper norm is now controlled.

The best unscreened counterexample candidates remain genuinely coupled finite differentials — mapping-cone, graph-of-groups, or higher-dimensional complexes — over groups where no known scalar-assembly theorem or Sylvester/free-field rank rigidity already forces the kernel class.

Q_L=\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(A_L)

\|Q_L-A_L\|

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\tau_L^{(v)}(Q_L)-\beta|
&\le
v\|Q_L-A_L\|
+
|\tau_\infty^{(v)}(q_n(D_\infty)-P_\infty)|\\
&\le
v(1+2\Lambda_L)\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]

The quantitative hypothesis makes this strictly smaller than the trace-mesh spacing `h_L`. H3 says both numbers lie in the same discrete subgroup of spacing at least `h_L`, so

\[
\tau_L^{(v)}(Q_L)=\beta.
\]

\[
P_L\le Q_L.

H4 gives

\[
\tau_L^{(v)}(P_L)\ge\beta,

\tau_L^{(v)}(P_L)=\tau_L^{(v)}(Q_L).

# 14. Failed routes and adversarial checks retained

The following remain **DISPROVED / REJECTED** as proof shortcuts.

1. **Infinite projection-sum shortcut.** Infinite orthogonal sums of nonzero finite-subgroup projections need not converge in norm in `C_r^*(G) tensor K`, even when normal traces converge.
2. **Irrational Betti shortcut.** An irrational/exotic `L2`-Betti number alone does not give a reduced-C* `K_0` class.
3. **Reduced quotient shortcut.** A group quotient does not automatically induce a reduced group-C* homomorphism; weak containment/amenability must be checked.
4. **Marked/strong-to-upper-norm shortcut.** Local or strong convergence is not a substitute for an upper reduced operator-norm theorem.
5. **Embedding-to-gap shortcut.** Embedding a group or matrix into a larger construction does not isolate a nonisolated zero.
6. **Short representative => short relation.** P25's cyclic/free-product calibration disproves this implication quantitatively.
7. **Hausdorff spectrum => kernel-gap locking.** Newly emphasized in this run: even full Hausdorff spectral convergence allows small positive stage spectrum to collapse into a parent zero eigenvalue. Kernel identification requires extra rank/trace information.
8. **Trace moments alone => Hausdorff spectrum.** False by the vanishing-mass outlier example in Section 6.2.
9. **One-sided polynomial norm alone => Hausdorff spectrum.** False by the disappearing-parent-point example in Section 6.1.

To revive any killed route, the missing ingredient must be stated explicitly rather than inferred from local convergence.

# 16. Exact remaining obligations and best next attack

P44 shows that, on the first nonamenable cyclic filling family, fixed-observable spectral and resolvent convergence can be recovered from **one-sided** upper reduced norm plus exact trace transport. Therefore the next proof-side norm target can be separated into two levels.

### Fixed-matrix permanence level

For a new filling family, it is enough to seek:

- polynomially controlled matrix-stable upper norm on an `O(log L)` or other fixed-polynomial window;
- exact/asymptotic canonical trace transport;
- if the parent has a kernel, rank/trace locking to rule out a small positive branch.

This is substantially weaker than constructing a global reduced-C* map.

### Ordinary K-theory / assembly level

For scalar Baum–Connes permanence one still needs a moving theorem. The decisive quantities are:

\[
\text{support radius of the representative},
\]

\[
\text{source relation/death scale},
\]

\[
\text{target stable relation propagation},
\]

and

\[
\text{cb upper reduced-norm distortion at the defect radius}.
\]

The current best next attack is therefore:

> **Infinite-order free-by-cyclic monodromy.** Build even a polynomial completely bounded upper-norm estimate on `O(log L)` support for a genuinely twisted parent `F_r rt_phi Z` and its cyclic fillings, then combine it with the plateau/rank-sandwich theorem. If successful at fixed scale, next quantify how far the norm window can be enlarged toward the propagation required by moving K-relations.

On the counterexample side, prioritize a genuinely coupled finite differential rather than another free-product/wedge construction, and screen its ambient group against known scalar Baum–Connes permanence before investing in a gap proof.
