## 2. Current primary-source verification

### 2.1 Li–Nowak–Pooya: exact scalar trace-range theorem

Primary source: Kang Li, Piotr Nowak, Sanaz Pooya, *Higher Kazhdan projections, \(\ell^2\)-Betti numbers and Baum–Connes conjectures*, J. Noncommut. Geom. 18 (2024), 313–336, DOI `10.4171/JNCG/529`.

EMS article page:
`https://ems.press/journals/jncg/articles/12879482`

The published Proposition 1 has exactly the hypotheses that must remain attached to it. If \(G\) is of type \(F_{n+1}\), the degree-\(n\) cohomological Laplacian
\[
\Delta_n=d_n^*d_n+d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*
\]
has a spectral gap at zero in the **reduced** completion, and the **coefficient-free** degree-zero assembly map is surjective, then
\[
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)\in\Lambda_G.
\]
For torsion-free \(G\), this forces integrality. The same paper identifies the canonical trace of the reduced higher Kazhdan projection with \(\beta_n^{(2)}(G)\).

The finite-type hypothesis belongs to obtaining the finite cochain matrices. It does **not** belong to the separate arbitrary finite-matrix criterion: if an independently supplied
\[
C\in M_{r,s}(\mathbb CG),\qquad D=C^*C
\]
satisfies
\[
\sigma_{C_r^*(G)}(D)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty),
\]
then
\[
P=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(D)\in M_s(C_r^*(G))
\]
and
\[
\tau(P)=\dim_{\mathcal NG}\ker C.
\]
A trace outside the scalar assembly range therefore gives a genuine scalar non-surjectivity counterexample without an extra \(F_{n+1}\) assumption.

## 3. Spectral-stability seed — rederived

Let \(A\ge0\) in a unital matrix C*-algebra and assume
\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Then the characteristic function of \(\{0\}\) is continuous on the spectrum, so
\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]
belongs to the same C*-algebra.

For \(t>0\), functional calculus gives
\[
\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|
=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}e^{-t\lambda}
\le e^{-t\delta}.
\]
Likewise, for \(\eta>0\),
\[
\left\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\right\|
=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{\eta}{\lambda+\eta}
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
\]

Now let positive \(A,B\) satisfy the **same** gap condition
\[
\sigma(A),\sigma(B)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,\infty).
\]
Define the bounded pseudoinverse
\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,\qquad A^\dagger(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}\quad(\lambda\ge\delta).
\]
Since \(BP_B=0\),
\[
A P_B=(A-B)P_B.
\]
Multiplying on the left by \(A^\dagger\),
\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B,
\]
so
\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
Interchanging \(A,B\) gives
\[
\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]
For orthogonal projections,
\[
\|P_A-P_B\|
=\max\{\|(1-P_A)P_B\|,\|(1-P_B)P_A\|\},
\]
hence the sharper estimate
\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]
In particular the requested bound with constant \(2\) is valid but nonsharp.

The common-gap hypothesis is indispensable. If \(A\) has nonzero kernel and \(B=A+\varepsilon I\), then \(P_B=0\), \(\|A-B\|=\varepsilon\), but \(\|P_A-P_B\|=1\). A shrinking/lifted zero cannot be ignored.

This seed is only a **sufficient** norm-plus-uniform-gap criterion; it does not prove such norm control for marked-group approximants.

# 4. `P01-P36-HODGE-KERNEL-SPLITTING` — PROVED / canonical rederivation

Let \(G\) be discrete of type \(F_{n+1}\), \(n\ge1\), and use a finite cellular cochain segment
\[
C^{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}C^n\xrightarrow{d_n}C^{n+1},
\qquad C^j=\ell^2(G)^{k_j}.
\]
Assume the reduced degree-\(n\) Laplacian
\[
\Delta_n=d_n^*d_n+d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*
\]
has a spectral gap at zero.

Define the adjacent kernel projections
\[
K_j:=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(d_j^*d_j)\in M_{k_j}(C_r^*(G)),
\qquad j=n-1,n.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
[p_n]=[K_{n-1}]+[K_n]-k_{n-1}[1]
}
\tag{P36}
\]
in \(K_0(C_r^*(G))\).

### Proof

Li–Nowak–Pooya show that a gap for \(\Delta_n\) gives gaps for the two partial Laplacians
\[
d_n^*d_n,
\qquad
d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*.
\]
The nonzero spectra of \(d_{n-1}^*d_{n-1}\) and \(d_{n-1}d_{n-1}^*\) coincide, so \(d_{n-1}^*d_{n-1}\) also has isolated zero.

By continuous functional calculus, on the positive spectrum define
\[
v_j=d_j(d_j^*d_j)^{-1/2},
\]
with value zero on the kernel. Then \(v_j\) is a rectangular matrix over \(C_r^*(G)\),
\[
v_j^*v_j=1-K_j,
\]
and its range projection \(R_j=v_jv_j^*\) satisfies
\[
[R_j]=[1-K_j]=k_j[1]-[K_j].
\]

Closed ranges give the orthogonal Hodge decomposition
\[
C^n=\operatorname{im}d_{n-1}\oplus\ker\Delta_n\oplus\operatorname{im}d_n^*.
\]
The first summand has projection class
\[
k_{n-1}[1]-[K_{n-1}],
\]
and the last summand has class
\[
k_n[1]-[K_n].
\]
Therefore
\[
k_n[1]
=
\bigl(k_{n-1}[1]-[K_{n-1}]\bigr)
+[p_n]
+\bigl(k_n[1]-[K_n]\bigr),
\]
which rearranges to (P36).

Taking canonical traces gives
\[
\boxed{
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)
=
\dim_{\mathcal NG}\ker d_{n-1}
+
\dim_{\mathcal NG}\ker d_n
-k_{n-1}.
}
\tag{P36-trace}
\]

### Counterexample compression

Let
\[
S_G=\operatorname{im}\mu_0^G\subseteq K_0(C_r^*(G)).
\]
The unit class \([1]\) is scalar-assembled from the trivial subgroup orbit. Hence
\[
[p_n]\notin S_G
\Longrightarrow
[K_{n-1}]\notin S_G
\text{ or }
[K_n]\notin S_G.
\]

Numerically, for any additive subgroup \(T\subset\mathbb R\) containing \(\mathbb Z\),
\[
\beta_n^{(2)}(G)\notin T
\Longrightarrow
\dim\ker d_{n-1}\notin T
\text{ or }
\dim\ker d_n\notin T.
\]
Taking \(T=\Lambda_G\), every Li–Nowak–Pooya forbidden-trace higher-Kazhdan candidate already contains one adjacent **finite group-ring differential** whose squared modulus has isolated reduced zero and forbidden kernel trace.

Thus the higher-Kazhdan forbidden-trace route is subsumed by the finite-matrix route.

# 5. `P01-P37-FREE-PRODUCT-HKP-PERMANENCE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) be finitely generated groups admitting finite aspherical CW models \(X_i=BA_i\). For each \(i\), use the finite cellular cochain complex and assume that **all** regular cohomological Laplacians of \(A_i\) have a spectral gap at zero.

Let
\[
G=A_1*\cdots*A_m*F_r,
\]
and assume \(G\) is nonamenable.

For each factor inclusion \(\iota_i:A_i\hookrightarrow G\), let
\[
(\iota_i)_*:K_0(C_r^*(A_i))\to K_0(C_r^*(G))
\]
be the map induced by the reduced subgroup inclusion.

Then:

1. for every \(k\ge2\), the regular degree-\(k\) Laplacian of the wedge model for \(BG\) has a spectral gap and
   \[
   \boxed{
   [p_k^G]=\sum_{i=1}^m(\iota_i)_*[p_k^{A_i}];
   }
   \tag{P37.1}
   \]
2. the degree-one Laplacian of \(G\) also has a spectral gap;
3. if every factor class \([p_k^{A_i}]\) is in the **scalar** assembly image of \(A_i\), then every \([p_k^G]\) is in the scalar assembly image of \(G\).

No coefficient Baum–Connes theorem is used.

## Proof of the block decomposition in degrees \(k\ge2\)

Take
\[
BG=X_1\vee\cdots\vee X_m\vee\bigvee^rS^1.
\]
This is a finite aspherical CW model for the free product.

In degrees at least two, every cell belongs to a unique factor \(X_i\). The cellular cochain modules therefore split
\[
C^k(G;\ell^2G)
=
\bigoplus_i C_i^k
\qquad(k\ge2),
\]
and the coboundaries are block diagonal across factor cells. The map
\[
d_1:C^1\to C^2
\]
is also block diagonal on the factor one-cells; the \(r\) free-circle columns map to zero because there are no two-cells attached to them.

Consequently, for every \(k\ge2\),
\[
\Delta_k^G
=
\bigoplus_i \Delta_k^{A_i}
\]
after evaluating the factor group-ring matrices in the regular representation of \(G\).

For any subgroup \(A_i<G\), the restriction \(\lambda_G|_{A_i}\) is a direct sum of copies of \(\lambda_{A_i}\), indexed by left cosets. Hence
\[
C_r^*(A_i)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(G)
\]
is isometric and preserves spectra of the factor matrices. The assumed factor gaps therefore persist in \(G\), and functional calculus gives exactly the block-sum kernel projection. This proves the gap and (P37.1).

## Degree one

We use the standard criterion recalled in Pooya–Wang/Li–Nowak–Pooya: \(\Delta_1\) has a spectral gap iff regular cohomology in degrees one and two is reduced.

Because \(G\) is nonamenable, the regular degree-zero Laplacian has a positive gap; equivalently, \(\operatorname{im}d_0\) is closed. Thus \(H^1(G;\ell^2G)\) is reduced.

For degree two, the preceding block description gives
\[
\operatorname{im}d_1
=
\bigoplus_i\operatorname{im}d_1^{A_i}.
\]
Each factor image is closed because the factor degree-one/degree-two regular cohomology is reduced under the factor gap hypothesis. A finite direct sum of closed subspaces is closed. Therefore \(H^2(G;\ell^2G)\) is reduced.

Hence \(\Delta_1^G\) has a spectral gap.

## Scalar assembly of every class

For \(k\ge2\), (P37.1) reduces the class to subgroup images of factor classes. Scalar Baum–Connes assembly is compatible with subgroup induction: if \(x_i\in K_0^{\mathrm{top}}(A_i)\) assembles to \([p_k^{A_i}]\), its induced proper \(G\)-class maps under scalar assembly to the class obtained from the reduced subgroup inclusion. Thus every \([p_k^G]\), \(k\ge2\), is scalar-assembled.

It remains to handle \(k=1\). Since all Laplacians of the finite wedge complex are now gapped, all cellular ranges are closed and the finite Hilbert-complex Euler identity holds in reduced \(K_0\):
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k[p_k^G]=\chi(G)[1].
}
\tag{P37.2}
\]
For completeness, this follows degree by degree from
\[
[C^k]=[\operatorname{im}d_{k-1}]+[p_k]+[\operatorname{im}d_k^*]
\]
and cancellation of the range classes in the alternating sum.

Because \(G\) is infinite/nonamenable, \(p_0=0\). The unit class is scalar-assembled. All \(k\ge2\) classes have already been shown scalar-assembled, so solving (P37.2) for \([p_1^G]\) shows that \([p_1^G]\) is also scalar-assembled.

This proves `P01-P37-FREE-PRODUCT-HKP-PERMANENCE`.

### What the theorem does not say

It does not claim scalar Baum–Connes surjectivity for \(G\). It only proves that this entire family of analytically natural higher-Kazhdan classes lies in the scalar assembly image.

It also depends on the stated gap hypotheses for the factors; it does not manufacture them for arbitrary free-product factors.

# 6. `P01-P37B-MULTIDEGREE-F2-FREE-PRODUCT` — explicit corollary

Let
\[
A_i=(F_2)^{n_i},\qquad n_i\ge2,
\]
and
\[
G=\Bigl(*_{i=1}^mA_i\Bigr)*F_r.
\]
Put
\[
m_k:=\#\{i:n_i=k\}.
\]
Assume \(m+r\ge2\) so that \(G\) is nonamenable (in fact each \(A_i\) is already nonamenable, so any nonempty list also suffices).

Pooya–Wang Lemma 4.13 and Theorem 4.14 give, for each \(A_i\):

- spectral gaps in all degrees for the product resolution;
- the only nonzero regular higher harmonic projection is in top degree \(n_i\);
- its class is
  \[
  [p_{n_i}^{A_i}]=[1].
  \]

Therefore P37 gives, for every \(k\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
[p_k^G]=m_k[1].
}
\tag{P37B.1}
\]

For the degree-one class compute the Euler characteristic. Since
\[
\chi((F_2)^n)=(-1)^n,
\qquad
\chi(F_r)=1-r,
\]
and Euler characteristic of a free product of finite CW \(K(\pi,1)\)'s satisfies \(\chi(A*B)=\chi(A)+\chi(B)-1\),
\[
\chi(G)=1-r-m+\sum_{i=1}^m(-1)^{n_i}.
\]
The Euler identity is
\[
-[p_1^G]+\sum_{i=1}^m(-1)^{n_i}[1]
=\chi(G)[1].
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
[p_1^G]=(r+m-1)[1].
}
\tag{P37B.2}
\]

Taking canonical traces,
\[
\boxed{
\beta_k^{(2)}(G)=m_k\quad(k\ge2),
\qquad
\beta_1^{(2)}(G)=r+m-1.
}
\]

Thus this family can have nonzero higher Kazhdan projections in arbitrarily many distinct degrees, but **every individual class is a multiple of the assembled unit class**.

This strictly strengthens the previous single-factor calibration: merely having several nonzero higher Kazhdan projections is not a plausible counterexample mechanism on free products of these benign factors.

## 7. Consequences for the two live P01 routes

### Route (a): finite gapped matrix with forbidden trace/class

The decisive target remains exactly
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite group-ring matrix}
+
\text{isolated zero in the reduced spectrum}
+
\text{actual reduced-C* kernel projection}
+
\text{forbidden scalar assembly class/trace}.
}
\]

`P01-P36` proves that every forbidden-trace higher-Kazhdan candidate under the Li–Nowak–Pooya gap hypothesis compresses to one adjacent finite differential. `P01-P37` now excludes a broad natural way of creating “multiple higher projections” by free-producting already-benign factors.

So the counterexample search should prioritize **genuinely coupled** finite complexes/differentials where the higher-degree blocks do not split as a wedge/free-product sum, or arbitrary finite group-ring matrices not forced by Euler/Hodge identities into the known finite-isotropy span.

The FFS exotic-real construction remains insufficient by itself: Remark 6.4 blocks the finite-type degree-two Laplacian route, and an irrational von-Neumann dimension alone does not produce an ordinary reduced-C* kernel projection.

### Route (b): controlled approximation / upper reduced norm

No new universal upper reduced-norm theorem is proved here.

Current P25 shows that visible finite-isotropy representation generators have exact finite-propagation group-ring projections, but relations among their K-classes can still require long controlled homotopies. The withdrawn Finn-Sell preprint confirms that norm control in the K-theory component is exactly the missing interface.

The spectral-stability seed says what would be sufficient for a fixed positive matrix: norm control plus a common positive gap. It does not prove either hypothesis for a nonamenable marked-group approximation.

## 8. Failed / killed routes retained

The following remain **DISPROVED / NON-CLOSING** and must not be reintroduced without a genuinely new ingredient.

1. An infinite orthogonal sum of nonzero finite-subgroup projections need not converge in norm in `C_r^*(G)\otimes K`, even if its normal trace converges.
2. An irrational or exotic \(L^2\)-Betti number alone is only a von-Neumann-dimension statement; it is not an ordinary reduced-C* \(K_0\) counterexample.
3. A quotient \(G\to G/N\) does not automatically give `C_r^*(G)->C_r^*(G/N)`; weak containment/amenability of the kernel must be checked.
4. Marked/local or strong convergence does not give the upper reduced-norm control required for spectral projections and K-theory witnesses.
5. Embedding a group or operator into a larger algebra does not turn nonisolated zero into an isolated reduced spectral point.
6. Fixed-scale representative locality does not imply locality of the relation/nullhomotopy killing that class; prior cyclic calibrations force relation-propagation escape.
7. **Newly screened:** free products of already-benign gapped higher-Kazhdan factors do not generate individual nonassembled higher-Kazhdan classes under the hypotheses of P37.

### Mercer ratio

For the positive Gaussian-kernel Mercer series
\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^rr!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)},
\]
termwise comparison gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}
\le
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\]
Indeed, every term in \(\lambda_{d,s+1}\) is the corresponding term in \(\lambda_{d,s}\) multiplied by \(\gamma/(d+2(s+r))\le\gamma/(d+2s)\). This is uniform in growing \(s\); no fixed-degree asymptotic is extrapolated.

### Dimension-free spherical hypercontractivity

Let \(Y_s\) be a pure degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic and let its homogeneous Gaussian extension be
\[
H_s(G)=R^sY_s(U),
\]
with \(G=RU\), \(R\) independent of \(U\). Gaussian chaos hypercontractivity gives
\[
\|H_s\|_p\le(p-1)^{s/2}\|H_s\|_2.
\]
Independence gives
\[
\|R^s\|_p\|Y_s\|_p
\le
(p-1)^{s/2}\|R^s\|_2\|Y_s\|_2.
\]
Since \(\|R^s\|_p\ge\|R^s\|_2\) for \(p\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_p\le(p-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2
}
\]
with no hidden dimension factor.

### Prescribed sequence

Set
\[
R=2^\ell,
\qquad d=2^R,
\qquad n=d^R,
\qquad k=R+\ell-1.
\]
Then
\[
\log_d\left(dR\frac{3^k}{\sqrt n}\right)
=-\frac R2+O(1)\to-\infty,
\]
and
\[
\log_d\left(Rd^2\frac{(2R)^k}{n}\right)
=-R+\ell+O(1)\to-\infty.
\]

The cyclic calibrations in prior P01/P25 work show that relation witnesses can be \(\Theta(L_j)\) even when representatives are born at bounded scale, so this complexity requirement is necessary, not cosmetic.

# 12. Bottom line

The coefficient-free Baum–Connes conjecture remains unresolved.

The new exact structural result is that a wide multiple-higher-Kazhdan construction is benign: free products of gapped factors with individually assembled higher-Kazhdan classes inherit only assembled higher-Kazhdan classes. For
