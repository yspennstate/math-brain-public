  \[
  \Delta_n\in M_{k_n}(C_r^*(G))
  \]
  has zero isolated in the **reduced** spectrum;

## 2.4 Algebraic-rank source used in P42

Two classical facts are needed, and were checked against current sources:

1. Cohn–Lewin: for a field `K` and a free group `F`, the free group algebra `K[F]` is a free ideal ring / Sylvester domain. Lewin, *Fields of fractions for group algebras of free groups*, Trans. AMS 192 (1974), 339–346, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-1974-0338055-4`, proves that the field generated by `K[F]` in a Malcev–Neumann embedding is its universal field of fractions. A current 2026 JLMS paper by Seidel–Ernst-West–Puder explicitly restates the classical Cohn–Lewin FIR theorem.
2. Linnell’s free-group result: the division closure of the free Haar generators in the affiliated operators is the free skew field. Mai–Speicher–Yin, arXiv `1905.08187v2`, explicitly recovers this result and states that in a free-field realization algebraic inner rank equals analytic von-Neumann rank. Standard Linnell-skew-field comparison computes `L2` dimensions of finite free complexes as skew-field dimensions.

Together these identify, for a matrix over `C F_2`, its von-Neumann rank with its rank over the universal/free skew field, hence with its Cohn inner rank over `C F_2`.

This is the only additional algebraic input in P42.

# 3. Spectral-stability seed rederived

Let `A=A*>=0` in a unital matrix `C*`-algebra and

\[
\sigma(A)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta,M],\qquad \delta>0.
\]

Then `0` is clopen in the spectrum, so

\[
P_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(A)
\]

belongs to the same `C*`-algebra by continuous functional calculus.

For `t>0`, on the zero spectral point `e^{-tA}=1`, while on `[δ,M]` its norm is at most `e^{-tδ}`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\|e^{-tA}-P_A\|\le e^{-t\delta}.}
\]

Similarly the function

\[
f_\eta(x)=\frac{\eta}{x+\eta}
\]

equals `1` at zero and is at most `η/(δ+η)` on `[δ,M]`, so

\[
\boxed{
\|\eta(A+\eta I)^{-1}-P_A\|
\le\frac{\eta}{\delta+\eta}.
}
\]

If positive `A,B` have the **same** gap `δ`, let

\[
A^\dagger(0)=0,
\qquad
A^\dagger(x)=x^{-1}\quad(x\ge\delta).
\]

Then `||A^dagger||<=1/δ` and

\[
(1-P_A)P_B=A^\dagger(A-B)P_B.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|(1-P_A)P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
\]

Interchanging `A,B` and using the standard two-projection norm identity gives the sharper bound

\[
\boxed{
\|P_A-P_B\|\le\frac{\|A-B\|}{\delta}.
}
\]

Thus the requested `2||A-B||/δ` estimate is valid but nonsharp. The example `B=A+εI` shows exactly why the shared positive gap cannot be removed: the kernel can disappear while the operator perturbation tends to zero.

This is a sufficient norm-plus-common-gap theorem. It does not prove a common gap for group approximants.

# 4. New theorem: the free-group rank sandwich

## `P01-P42A-QUOTIENT-KERNEL-RANK-MONOTONICITY` — **PROVED**

Let

\[
R=\mathbb C F_2,
\qquad
C\in M_{u,v}(R).
\]

Let `rho_R(C)` be the Cohn inner rank: the least integer `r` for which

\[
C=AB,
\qquad
A\in M_{u,r}(R),\quad B\in M_{r,v}(R).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
\alpha=\dim_{L(F_2)}\ker C_\infty.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\rho_R(C)=v-\alpha.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover, for **every** group quotient

\[
\pi:F_2\twoheadrightarrow H,
\]

with coefficientwise evaluated matrix `C_H`, one has

\[
\boxed{
\dim_{L(H)}\ker C_H\ge\alpha.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Because `C F_2` is a FIR/Sylvester domain, its universal skew field of fractions preserves inner rank. By the Cohn–Lewin/Linnell identification described in Section 2.4, this skew-field rank is the analytic von-Neumann rank of `C_∞`. Hence

\[
\rho_R(C)
=
\operatorname{rank}_{L(F_2)} C_\infty
=v-\alpha,
\]

proving `(4.2)`.

Put

\[
r=v-\alpha.
\]

By the definition of inner rank there is a factorization `(4.1)` through exactly `r` free coordinates.

Now apply the purely algebraic group-ring quotient map

\[
\mathbb C F_2\to\mathbb C H.
\]

This is **not** a claimed map of reduced group `C*`-algebras. It simply evaluates the finite group-ring coefficients. The factorization survives:

\[
C_H=A_HB_H.
\]

As an operator

\[
\ell^2(H)^v\longrightarrow\ell^2(H)^u,
\]

its closed image is contained in the closed image of `A_H`, which is generated from an `r`-copy Hilbert `L(H)` module. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{L(H)} C_H\le r.
\]

By rank-nullity for finite von-Neumann modules,

\[
\dim_{L(H)}\ker C_H
=v-\operatorname{rank}_{L(H)}C_H
\ge v-r
=\alpha.
\]

This proves `(4.3)`.

### Interpretation

Quotient relations can only **increase** the von-Neumann kernel dimension of a fixed free-group matrix once its free-group analytic rank is identified with Cohn inner rank. This is a genuine algebraic one-sided semicontinuity statement and uses no reduced quotient map, no amenability of the quotient kernel, and no marked/strong convergence.

# 6. Uniform stage spectral gap obtained after the identification

## `P01-P42B-UNIFORM-STAGE-GAP` — **PROVED**

The fixed polynomial `(5.3)` already contains an explicit positive neighborhood of zero on which its value exceeds `1/2`.

Set

\[
y_0=\frac{M+\delta}{M-\delta}=\cosh\theta,
\qquad
c_0=\cosh(N_0\theta).
\]

Because `(5.2)` gives `N_0 theta >= log 1024`, we have `c_0/2>1`. Define

\[
s_*
=
\frac1{N_0}\operatorname{arcosh}\left(\frac{c_0}{2}\right),
\qquad
y_*=\cosh s_*,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\delta_*
=
\frac{M+\delta-(M-\delta)y_*}{2}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Since

\[
1<y_*<y_0,
\]

we have

\[
0<\delta_*<\delta.
\]

For `0<=x<=δ`, the affine Chebyshev variable

\[
y(x)=\frac{M+\delta-2x}{M-\delta}
\]

decreases from `y_0` to `1`. On `[1,∞)`,

\[
T_{N_0}(y)=\cosh(N_0\operatorname{arcosh}y)
\]

is strictly increasing. Hence `p_{N_0}(x)` decreases on `[0,δ]`, and by construction

\[
p_{N_0}(\delta_*)=\frac12,
\qquad
p_{N_0}(x)>\frac12
\quad(0\le x<\delta_*).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Suppose some positive spectral point

\[
\lambda\in\sigma(D_L)\cap(0,\delta_*)
\]

existed. By continuity there is a small open interval around `λ` on which `p_{N_0}>1/2`. Since `λ` is in the spectrum, the spectral projection of `D_L` on that open interval is nonzero. That projection is contained in `Q_L` but is orthogonal to the true kernel `P_L`, contradicting `(5.11)`.

Therefore

\[
\sigma(D_L)\cap(0,\delta_*)=\varnothing.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `α>0`, `(5.10)` says zero is an actual kernel spectral point, and hence

\[
\sigma(D_L)\subset\{0\}\cup[\delta_*,\infty).
\]

If `α=0`, then `(5.11)` gives `Q_L=P_L=0`. Were `0` itself in `σ(D_L)`, every sufficiently small spectral neighborhood of zero would be nonzero and, by `(6.2)`, would lie in `Q_L`, contradiction. Thus

\[
\sigma(D_L)\subset[\delta_*,\infty).
\]

The same `δ_*` works for every sufficiently large `L`.

### Why this is not circular

The proof order is essential:

1. the limit gap constructs a fixed controlled cutoff;
2. P25 transports the **ordinary `K_0` relation** of that cutoff, not a stage kernel gap;
3. Cohn inner-rank monotonicity supplies a stage kernel-dimension **lower** bound;
4. inclusion `P_L<=Q_L` supplies the opposite upper bound;
5. only after equality of projections is established do we infer a stage gap.

At no point is a stage spectral gap assumed to prove itself.

# 8. What P42 changes in the two P01 routes

## 8.1 Route (a): finite-matrix counterexample search

The four non-negotiable requirements remain

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite matrix}
+
\text{isolated reduced zero}
+
\text{actual reduced-}C^*\text{ kernel projection}
+
\text{forbidden scalar assembly class/trace}.
}
\]

P42 does **not** produce a forbidden trace. In fact, it screens the specific cyclic filling family in the opposite direction: every **fixed** gapped matrix from the free-group limit has eventually stable integral kernel dimension, actual reduced kernel projection, and a uniform stage gap.

So a counterexample cannot arise on this family merely by taking one fixed limit-gapped matrix and hoping torsion filling creates an exotic kernel trace.

This is useful pruning: if route (a) succeeds, it needs a genuinely different group/matrix mechanism or a moving matrix whose algebraic inner rank and controlled relation complexity are not fixed.

## 8.2 Route (b): quantitative approximation

P42 is a genuine permanence theorem across a nonamenable quotient family, obtained without a global reduced `C*` quotient map.

Its mechanism separates three ingredients cleanly:

- **upper analytic control:** P40 transports one fixed finite-propagation approximate `K_0` relation;
- **lower algebraic control:** free-group inner rank cannot increase under quotient, so stage kernel dimension cannot decrease;
- **spectral identification:** a controlled projection of exactly the limiting trace, containing the true kernel, must equal the true kernel.

This is stronger than norm convergence of spectral projections: it proves eventual **uniform stage gaps** from a limit gap in this fixed-matrix family.

But it remains a fixed-class theorem. P25's cyclic and nonamenable relation-escape examples still show that moving `K`-relations may require propagation comparable to relation depth. P42 does not contradict that obstruction.

# 9. Failed routes / exact limitations preserved

1. **Infinite finite-subgroup projection sums:** still dead. Trace convergence does not imply norm convergence in `C_r^*(G)⊗K`.
2. **Irrational `L2`-Betti number alone:** still dead. A von-Neumann dimension without a finite matrix plus reduced isolated zero is not a scalar Baum–Connes `K_0` counterexample.

5. **Embedding repairs nonisolated zero:** still false.
6. **P41 trace locking alone identifies the stage kernel:** previously open; P42 shows how to repair it in this family by adding the independent lower kernel-dimension bound from Cohn inner rank.
7. **Lück ring as a discrete trace lattice:** still false. The `1/L` stage spacing in P41 comes from the actual `K_0` trace image via the finite-index free subgroup, not from discreteness of `Lambda_{G_L}`.

9. **P42 handles moving matrices/classes:** not proved. The rank factorization width may be controlled, but the finite `K_0` relation radius used by P25 can diverge for moving classes.

# 11. Adversarial audit of P42

The theorem was checked against the main failure modes of the program.

### 11.1 No reduced quotient map

The only quotient operation before stage functional calculus is

\[
\mathbb C F_2\to\mathbb C G_L.
\]

The proof never extends this to a global map of reduced group `C*`-algebras.

### 11.2 No stage gap is smuggled into the proof

The stage kernel projection `P_L` is initially used only in the group von Neumann algebra, where it always exists measurably. The reduced projection `Q_L` is obtained by rounding an approximate idempotent. The stage gap is concluded **after** `P_L=Q_L` has been proved by trace/rank sandwiching.

### 11.3 Trace equality is not used as class equality in the dangerous direction

P25 first proves the actual class identity `[Q_L]=alpha[1]`; only then is trace taken. P42 uses the trace solely to compare the nested von-Neumann projections `P_L<=Q_L`. Faithfulness converts equality of traces of nested projections into equality of projections. There is no use of injectivity of the trace on `K_0`.

### 11.4 Finite matrices remain finite matrices

The Cohn factorization of a finite `u×v` group-ring matrix passes through a finite number `r` of free coordinates. No infinite orthogonal sum or multiplier-algebra projection appears.

### 11.5 The algebraic-rank input is special

The equality between analytic rank and Cohn inner rank uses the free-group FIR / universal-free-field structure. It is not asserted for arbitrary groups.

### 11.6 Locally compact scope is not claimed

Every new P42 statement is restricted to the discrete family `C_L*Z -> F_2`. The original all-second-countable-locally-compact problem remains open.

# 12. Exact remaining obligations and best next attacks

## Counterexample direction

The best route-(a) target remains a genuinely coupled finite differential or finite group-ring matrix over a group whose scalar assembly surjectivity is unresolved, with all four requirements certified simultaneously.

P42 suggests a useful pre-screening step for any quotient construction:

1. compute or bound the matrix inner rank over the parent group ring / division closure;
2. derive the unavoidable stage kernel lower bound under algebraic quotient;
3. compare it with any controlled reduced-`C*` cutoff trace.

If these bounds already match, the candidate is automatically benign and should be discarded before investing in a separate gap proof.

Promising unscreened settings remain genuinely coupled mapping-cone or graph-of-groups matrices where the parent group ring is not a FIR and the relevant division-rank function is not rigid enough to force the stage kernel.

## Proof / permanence direction

P42 supplies a template for a stronger permanence theorem:

> **Rank-sandwich permanence template.** Let `Gamma_j -> Gamma` be a quotient/marked family and `C` a fixed finite matrix. If (i) one has an algebraic lower bound `dim ker C_j >= dim ker C`, and (ii) controlled `K`-theory constructs a reduced-`C*` projection `Q_j` containing `ker C_j` with trace exactly `dim ker C`, then `Q_j` is the actual kernel projection and any fixed spectral cutoff window yields a stage gap.

The next useful theorem would establish this template for a non-free parent group, for example a group with a Hughes/Linnell division ring and a quotient family where P40-type matrix-stable upper norm control is available.

The deeper moving-class obstruction remains untouched. To approach the universal Baum–Connes problem, one still needs source direct-system relation bounds and target `K`-witness propagation bounds at scales compatible with the norm theorem, or a replacement argument that does not require uniform relation compression.
