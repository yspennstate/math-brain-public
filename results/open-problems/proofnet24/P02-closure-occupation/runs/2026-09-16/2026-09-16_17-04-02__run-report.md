## 4. Combinatorial definitions

Let `V` be the finite state space, `P=(p_xy)` the irreducible transition matrix, and

\[
L=I-P.
\]

Ignore self-loops as graph edges; the diagonal of `L` already equals the total outgoing non-loop weight.

For nonempty `A subset V`, an **A-rooted converging spanning forest** is a spanning directed forest such that

- each `a in A` has outdegree `0`;
- each `v notin A` has outdegree `1`;
- following arrows from any `v notin A` eventually reaches a vertex of `A`.

Its weight is

\[
w(F)=\prod_{(x\to y)\in F}p_{xy}.
\]

Write

\[
\mathsf F_A=\sum_{F\text{ A-rooted}}w(F).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a single root `r`, these are the directed spanning trees converging to `r`. Put

\[
\mathsf T=\sum_{r\in V}\mathsf F_{\{r\}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `i,j notin A`, define

\[
\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)
\tag{4.3}
\]

to be the total weight of forests rooted at `A union {j}` in which the tree containing `i` is rooted at `j`.

This last object has a source-facing interpretation. On the marked boundary `{i,j} union A`, it is the rooted-grove connectivity type

\[
i\longrightarrow j,\qquad a\text{ is a root for each }a\in A,
\]

while all unmarked states are internal vertices and are summed out.

The classical directed matrix-tree / all-minors matrix-tree theorem gives

\[
\mathsf F_A=\det L_{UU}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)
=
C_{ji}(L_{UU}),
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `C_{ji}` is the cofactor which occurs in the `(i,j)` entry of `L_{UU}^{-1}`.

# 5. `P02-OCC-C5-ROOTED-GROVE-PORTMANTEAU` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `P` be any finite irreducible discrete-time Markov chain, not assumed reversible. Let `A` be nonempty and let `U=V\A`.

### Part I: first-hit occupation

For `i,j in U`,

\[
\boxed{
E_i\!\left[\sum_{t<T_A}\mathbf 1_{\{X_t=j\}}\right]
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)}
     {\mathsf F_A}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `pi` be stationary and let `Z` be the Aldous–Fill fundamental matrix. With the frozen denominator (2.1),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_A=\frac{\mathsf F_A}{\mathsf T}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For the inherited C3 bordered numerator

\[
\mathcal N_{A;i,j}:=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_A^T&-\pi_A&Z_{iA}^T\\
\mathbf 1^T&0&1\\
Z_{Aj}^T&-\pi_j&Z_{ij}
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{5.3}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{A;i,j}
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)}
     {\mathsf T}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
E_i\!\left[\sum_{t<T_A}\mathbf 1_{\{X_t=j\}}\right]
=
\frac{\mathcal N_{A;i,j}(\pi,Z)}
     {\Delta_A(\pi,Z)}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the retained numerator and denominator are not merely signed determinant expansions: each is a normalized positive rooted-grove partition function.

### Part II: arbitrary stopping / boundary data

Retain C3’s setup. Let `sigma` be the initial law; let `T` be an integrable stopping time whose terminal law `tau` is supported on nonempty `A`; and set

\[
x_v=E_\sigma\sum_{t<T}\mathbf 1_{\{X_t=v\}},
\qquad
c=x_A.
\]

Define, for `q in U`,

\[
\mu_q
=
\sigma_q+\sum_{a\in A}c_a p_{aq}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then for every `j in U`,

\[
\boxed{
x_j
=
\frac{1}{\mathsf F_A}
\sum_{q\in U}
\mu_q\,
\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(q\to j).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Equivalently, if `\mathcal N_{\sigma,c;A,j}` is the inherited C3 marked-data bordered numerator, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal N_{\sigma,c;A,j}
=
\frac1{\mathsf T}
\sum_{q\in U}
\mu_q\,
\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(q\to j).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The boundary coordinates themselves are `x_a=c_a`.

This gives an intrinsic rooted-forest semantics to the complete C3 boundary-data theorem, including strict return times.

## 6. Proof of C5

### 6.1 First-hit Green kernel as a forest ratio

Since the chain is finite and irreducible and `A` is nonempty, `T_A<infinity` almost surely from every state, so the killed matrix `P_{UU}` has spectral radius below one. Hence

\[
G_A=(I-P_{UU})^{-1}=L_{UU}^{-1}.
\]

Its `(i,j)` entry is exactly expected visits to `j` before `T_A`.

By Cramer’s rule,

\[
(G_A)_{ij}
=
\frac{C_{ji}(L_{UU})}{\det L_{UU}}.
\]

Apply the directed all-minors matrix-tree theorem to the denominator and numerator. Equations (4.4) and (4.5) give

\[
(G_A)_{ij}
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)}{\mathsf F_A}.
\]

This proves (5.1).

### 6.2 The frozen denominator is exactly normalized A-rooted forest weight

This is the main new bridge.

Let

\[
\Pi=\mathbf 1\pi^T.
\]

The Aldous–Fill `Z` satisfies

\[
LZ=ZL=I-\Pi,\qquad Z\Pi=\Pi Z=0.
\]

For a scalar `t != 0`, define

\[
L_t=L+t\Pi.
\]

Then

\[
L_t^{-1}=Z+t^{-1}\Pi.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let `\mathsf T_r` be the total weight of trees converging to root `r`. The Markov-chain tree theorem says

\[
\pi_r=\frac{\mathsf T_r}{\mathsf T}.
\]

The matrix-tree theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{adj}(L)
=
\mathsf T\,\mathbf1\pi^T.
\]

Since `det L=0`, the rank-one determinant identity gives

\[
\det L_t
=
t\,\pi^T\operatorname{adj}(L)\mathbf1
=
t\mathsf T.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now use Jacobi’s complementary principal-minor identity for the invertible matrix `L_t`:

\[
\det\!\left((L_t^{-1})_{AA}\right)
=
\frac{\det((L_t)_{UU})}{\det L_t}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The left side is

\[
\det\!\left(Z_A+t^{-1}\mathbf1_A\pi_A^T\right).
\]

A rank-one determinant is affine in the rank-one parameter even when `Z_A` is singular:

\[
\det\!\left(Z_A+s\mathbf1_A\pi_A^T\right)
=
\det Z_A
+
s\,\pi_A^T\operatorname{adj}(Z_A)\mathbf1_A.
\tag{6.4}
\]

But transposing the frozen bordered matrix gives

\[
\Delta_A
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_A&\mathbf1_A\\
-\pi_A^T&0
\end{pmatrix}
=
\pi_A^T\operatorname{adj}(Z_A)\mathbf1_A.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus the left side of (6.3) is

\[
\det Z_A+t^{-1}\Delta_A.
\tag{6.6}
\]

On the right,

\[
(L_t)_{UU}
=
L_{UU}+t\mathbf1_U\pi_U^T,
\]

and by (6.2),

\[
\frac{\det((L_t)_{UU})}{\det L_t}
=
\frac{\det(L_{UU}+t\mathbf1_U\pi_U^T)}
     {t\mathsf T}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Multiply (6.3) by `t` and send `t -> 0`. The left tends to `Delta_A`; the right tends to

\[
\frac{\det L_{UU}}{\mathsf T}
=
\frac{\mathsf F_A}{\mathsf T}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta_A=\frac{\mathsf F_A}{\mathsf T}.
\]

This proves (5.2). In particular `Delta_A>0`, so the inherited C3 invertibility also receives a direct combinatorial proof.

### 6.3 The frozen numerator is the normalized rooted-grove weight

Inherited C3 proved

\[
(G_A)_{ij}
=
\frac{\mathcal N_{A;i,j}}{\Delta_A}.
\]

Combine this with (5.1) and (5.2):

\[
\frac{\mathcal N_{A;i,j}}{\mathsf F_A/\mathsf T}
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)}
     {\mathsf F_A}.
\]

Cancel `\mathsf F_A`, which is positive. This gives

\[
\mathcal N_{A;i,j}
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(i\to j)}
     {\mathsf T}.
\]

So (5.4) and (5.5) follow.

### 6.4 Boundary-data extension

The expected occupation row vector satisfies the standard conservation law

\[
x(I-P)=\sigma-\tau.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Because `tau` is supported on `A`, restricting (6.8) to the columns indexed by `U` gives

\[
x_U L_{UU}-cP_{AU}
=
\sigma_U.
\]

Hence

\[
x_U
=
(\sigma_U+cP_{AU})L_{UU}^{-1}
=
\mu G_A,
\tag{6.9}
\]

where `mu` is (5.6). Taking coordinate `j` and applying (5.1),

\[
x_j
=
\sum_{q\in U}\mu_q
\frac{\mathsf F_{A\cup\{j\}}(q\to j)}
     {\mathsf F_A}.
\]

That is (5.7).

Inherited C3 also says

\[
x_j
=
\frac{\mathcal N_{\sigma,c;A,j}}{\Delta_A}.
\]

Use (5.2) and compare with (5.7) to obtain (5.8).

QED.

## 7. Exact source example: the four marked states

Take distinct marks `i,j,k,l` and

\[
A=\{k,l\}.
\]

The source’s model expression is

\[
E_i(\#j\text{ before }\min(T_k,T_l)).

C5 associates to it the following **rooted-grove type**:

- roots: `k,l,j`;
- marked connectivity constraint: `i` lies in the tree converging to `j`;
- every unmarked state is an internal forest vertex and is summed over.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E_i(\#j\text{ before }\min(T_k,T_l))
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{\{k,l,j\}}(i\to j)}
     {\mathsf F_{\{k,l\}}}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Now associate to that combinatorial structure the formal marked-data function

\[
\mathcal D_{k,l}(p,z)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
z_{kk}&z_{lk}&-p_k\\
z_{kl}&z_{ll}&-p_l\\
1&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{7.2}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal N_{k,l;i,j}(p,z)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
z_{kk}&z_{lk}&-p_k&z_{ik}\\
z_{kl}&z_{ll}&-p_l&z_{il}\\
1&1&0&1\\
z_{kj}&z_{lj}&-p_j&z_{ij}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

These use only the source-requested formal variables whose indices lie in `{i,j,k,l}`.

C5 proves the independent combinatorial identities

\[
\mathcal D_{k,l}(\pi,Z)
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{\{k,l\}}}{\mathsf T},
\tag{7.4}
\]

\[
\mathcal N_{k,l;i,j}(\pi,Z)
=
\frac{\mathsf F_{\{k,l,j\}}(i\to j)}{\mathsf T}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Dividing (7.5) by (7.4) gives (7.1).

This is materially different from C3’s old signed-perfect-matching presentation. The combinatorial object in (7.1) is defined in the transition graph before any determinant is written. The determinant is a second evaluation of its partition function after all unmarked states have been eliminated from the data.

The only remaining source-interpretation subtlety is linguistic rather than algebraic: the rooted grove lives on the full transition graph, while its **boundary type** is specified only by the marked states. Open Problem 2.34 says “a combinatorial structure involving `{i,j,k,l}`”, not “a combinatorial structure whose vertex set is exactly `{i,j,k,l}`”. ## 10. Prior-art search and classification

Fresh bounded search on 2026-09-16 used queries around:

- exact phrase `"Open Problem 2.34" Aldous Fill occupation`;

- `Markov chain occupation rooted forest Green hitting set`;

- `principal minors group inverse spanning forest`;

### What is definitely old / known

1. **All-minors matrix-tree theorem.**  
   Arbitrary Laplacian minors as weighted forests are classical; Chaiken’s 1982 all-minors theorem is a standard source.

2. **Principal minors as root-prescribed forests.**  
   Chebotarev–Agaev’s survey/history explicitly records the older Fiedler–Sedláček theorem: deleting the rows and columns indexed by a root set counts forests rooted at that set.

3. **Forest matrices and generalized inverses.**  
   Chebotarev–Agaev, *Forest matrices around the Laplacian matrix*, LAA 356 (2002), develops matrices whose `(i,j)` entry is the total weight of in-forests in which `i` belongs to a tree rooted at `j`, and relates these to generalized inverses and the Markov-chain tree theorem.

4. **Modern explicit Green/hitting forest formulas.**  
   Chung–Zeng, *Forest formulas of discrete Green’s functions*, JGT 102 (2023), gives forest interpretations of Green functions for directed and weighted graphs and derives hitting-time forest formulas.

5. **Occupation-measure unification.**  
   Pitman, *Occupation measures for Markov chains*, Adv. Appl. Probab. 9 (1977), states already in its abstract that a basic occupation-measure identity gives a unified approach to a variety of Markov-chain moment identities. Aldous–Fill themselves cite Pitman immediately before posing OP 2.34.

Therefore **the forest mechanism is not claimed as new**. It is built from classical ingredients.

I did not find a paper explicitly saying “Aldous–Fill Open Problem 2.34 is solved”, nor a source presenting exactly the package

Absence from this bounded search is **not** evidence of novelty. The exact bridge may be folklore or an immediate corollary in the generalized-inverse / forest literature.

### A. Removable non-cut vertex distinct from the distinguished origin

Let `G` be any finite connected graph with at least two vertices and let `0` be distinguished.

Every finite connected graph on at least two vertices has at least two non-cut vertices. At most one of these can be the distinguished vertex `0`. Hence there exists `v != 0` such that `G-v` remains connected.

Iterating this deletion reduces every connected vertex set containing `0` to `{0}` through connected prefixes. Reversing the sequence proves that an enumeration which starts at `{0}` and repeatedly adds an outside neighbor, deduplicating sets, is complete for all connected subsets containing `0`.

The cube’s bipartiteness is not needed for this lemma.

\[
B(M)=7m-2e(M).

Let `e_max(m)` be the maximum possible induced-edge count at size `m`. Harper’s optimum is

\[
g_7(m)=7m-2e_{\max}(m).
\]

Therefore
