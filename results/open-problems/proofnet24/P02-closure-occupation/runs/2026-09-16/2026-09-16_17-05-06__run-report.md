z_{\sigma j}:=\sum_i\sigma_iZ_{ij}.

# 4. Proof of C3: conservation law and marked boundary system

This section is the theorem proof, not a sketch.

## 4.1 The stopping-time conservation law

Let

\[
x_k=E_\sigma\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf1_{\{X_t=k\}}.
\]

Because `E_sigma T<infinity`, all coordinates are finite and

\[
\sum_kx_k=E_\sigma T=s.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a fixed state `j`, using that `{T>t}` is measurable at time `t`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
(xP)_j
&=\sum_kx_kP_{kj}\\
&=E_\sigma\sum_{t<T}P(X_{t+1}=j\mid\mathcal F_t)\\
&=E_\sigma\sum_{t<T}\mathbf1_{\{X_{t+1}=j\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last sum counts visits to `j` at times `1,...,T`, whereas `x_j` counts visits at times `0,...,T-1`. Therefore

\[
(xP)_j=x_j-\sigma_j+\tau_j,
\]

or in row-vector form

\[
\boxed{x(I-P)=\sigma-\tau.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the basic conservation law behind the stopping-rule/exit-frequency literature.

## 4.2 Insert the fundamental matrix

The fundamental matrix satisfies

\[
(I-P)Z=I-\Pi,
\qquad
Z\mathbf1=0,
\qquad
\pi^TZ=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Multiply (4.2) on the right by `Z`:

\[
x(I-\Pi)=(\sigma-\tau)Z.
\]

Using (4.1),

\[
x(I-\Pi)=x-s\pi^T.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
x=\sigma Z-\tau Z+s\pi^T.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This identity already contains all occupation coordinates simultaneously.

## 4.3 Restrict to the marked terminal boundary

Assume `supp(tau) subseteq A`. For each `a in A`, equation (4.4) gives

\[
c_a=x_a=z_{\sigma a}-\sum_{b\in A}\tau_bZ_{ba}+s\pi_a.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\sum_{b\in A}Z_{ba}\tau_b-\pi_as=z_{\sigma a}-c_a.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Together with

\[
\sum_{b\in A}\tau_b=1,
\tag{4.6}
\]

these equations are exactly the bordered system (0.2).

Thus only invertibility of `M_A` remains to prove.

# 5. Proof that the frozen denominator never vanishes

The invertibility proof is characteristic-free, does not use reversibility, and works for periodic chains.

Suppose

\[
M_A\binom{u}{t}=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus

\[
\sum_{b\in A}Z_{ba}u_b-\pi_at=0\quad(a\in A),
\qquad
\sum_{b\in A}u_b=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define a vector `d` on all of `V` by

\[
d_b=
\begin{cases}
u_b/\pi_b,&b\in A,\\
0,&b\notin A.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
\pi^Td=\sum_{b\in A}u_b=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Let `P_hat` be the time-reversed chain,

\[
\widehat P_{ab}=\frac{\pi_bP_{ba}}{\pi_a},
\]

and let

\[
\widehat Z=D_\pi^{-1}Z^TD_\pi,
\qquad
\widehat Z_{ab}=\frac{\pi_b}{\pi_a}Z_{ba}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Divide the first equations of (5.2) by `pi_a` and use `u_b=pi_bd_b`. For `a in A`,

\[
(\widehat Zd)_a=t.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Set

\[
f:=\widehat Zd-t\mathbf1.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Then `f=0` on `A`. Also

\[
(I-\widehat P)\widehat Z=I-\widehat\Pi,
\]

and (5.4) gives `widehat Pi d=0`, so

\[
(I-\widehat P)f=d.
\tag{5.8}
\]

By construction `d=0` on `A^c`. Hence `f` is `P_hat`-harmonic on `A^c` and vanishes on `A`.

Because the reversed chain is again finite and irreducible, it hits every nonempty set almost surely. Let `T_A` be its first hit of `A`. Optional stopping for the bounded harmonic function on the finite state space gives, for `v notin A`,

\[
f(v)=E_vf(X_{T_A})=0.
\]

Therefore `f=0` everywhere. Equation (5.8) then gives `d=0`, hence `u=0`; (5.6) gives `t=0`.

Thus the kernel of `M_A` is trivial, proving

\[
\boxed{\Delta_A\ne0.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This completes the proof of uniqueness in (0.2).

# 6. Proof of the occupation determinant and the matching compiler

## 6.1 Schur/Cramer step

Let

\[
b_{\sigma,A,c}:=
\binom{z_{\sigma,A}-c}{1},
\qquad
r_j^T:=(Z_{Aj}^T,-\pi_j).
\]

By (0.2),

\[
\binom{\tau_A}{s}=M_A^{-1}b_{\sigma,A,c}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equation (4.4) at coordinate `j` is

\[
x_j=z_{\sigma j}-r_j^TM_A^{-1}b_{\sigma,A,c}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The scalar Schur-complement identity gives

\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
M_A&b_{\sigma,A,c}\\
r_j^T&z_{\sigma j}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\Delta_A
\left(z_{\sigma j}-r_j^TM_A^{-1}b_{\sigma,A,c}\right).
\]

Using (6.2) proves (0.3).

The coordinates of `(tau_A,s)` are likewise Cramer ratios with the **same** denominator `Delta_A`; replacing the column of the desired unknown in `M_A` by `b_{sigma,A,c}` gives the corresponding numerator.

## 6.2 Strict return is inside the same theorem

For ordinary first hitting

\[
T_A=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\in A\}
\]

from a point `i notin A`, no boundary state is occupied before stopping, hence `c=0`.

For the strict first hit/return

\[
T_A^+=\inf\{t\ge1:X_t\in A\}
\]

from `i in A`, time zero contributes exactly one occupation at `i` and no other boundary state can be seen before `T_A^+`. Hence

\[
c=e_i|_A.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is the point at which C3 is strictly more unified than the prior C1 formula: Lemmas 2.5–2.6 no longer require a separate first-step transformation.

## 6.3 Explicit signed-perfect-matching structure

Fix an ordering of `A`. For point start `sigma=e_i`, define a bipartite graph with row vertices

\[
R_D=A\sqcup\{\rho\}
\]

and column vertices

\[
C_D=A\sqcup\{\theta\}.
\]

Assign edge weights

\[
w_D(a,b)=z_{ba},\qquad
w_D(a,\theta)=-p_a,
\]

\[
w_D(\rho,b)=1,\qquad
w_D(\rho,\theta)=0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For a perfect matching, equivalently a bijection `m:R_D -> C_D`, let `sgn(m)` be its permutation sign in the frozen ordering. Define

\[
\mathcal D_A(p,z)
:=
\sum_{m:R_D\overset\sim\to C_D}
\operatorname{sgn}(m)
\prod_{r\in R_D}w_D(r,m(r)).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is exactly the Leibniz expansion of the denominator determinant, so

\[
\mathcal D_A(\pi,Z)=\Delta_A\ne0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

For the occupation numerator add row role `jmath` and column role `iota`:

\[
R_N=A\sqcup\{\rho,\jmath\},
\qquad
C_N=A\sqcup\{\theta,\iota\}.
\]

Keep the weights (6.4) and add

\[
w_N(a,\iota)=z_{ia}-c_a,
\]

\[
w_N(\rho,\iota)=1,
\]

\[
w_N(\jmath,b)=z_{bj},\qquad
w_N(\jmath,\theta)=-p_j,
\qquad
w_N(\jmath,\iota)=z_{ij}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

All unspecified edges have weight zero. Define the signed perfect-matching sum

\[
\mathcal N_{A;i,j,c}(p,z)
:=
\sum_{m:R_N\overset\sim\to C_N}
\operatorname{sgn}(m)
\prod_{r\in R_N}w_N(r,m(r)).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Then (0.3) is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
E_i\sum_{t<T}\mathbf1_{\{X_t=j\}}
=
\frac{\mathcal N_{A;i,j,c}(\pi,Z)}{\mathcal D_A(\pi,Z)}.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This is a literal sum over a combinatorial class of matchings, not merely a renamed list of the eleven formulas.

### The exact Aldous–Fill four-mark example

For the source’s displayed example `T=min(T_k,T_l)`, take

\[
A=\{k,l\},\qquad \sigma=e_i,\qquad c=0.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal D_{k,l}
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
z_{kk}&z_{lk}&-p_k\\
z_{kl}&z_{ll}&-p_l\\
1&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{6.10}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal N_{k,l;i,j}
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
z_{kk}&z_{lk}&-p_k&z_{ik}\\
z_{kl}&z_{ll}&-p_l&z_{il}\\
1&1&0&1\\
z_{kj}&z_{lj}&-p_j&z_{ij}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_i(\#\text{ visits to }j\text{ before }\min(T_k,T_l))
=
\frac{\mathcal N_{k,l;i,j}(\pi,Z)}
{\mathcal D_{k,l}(\pi,Z)}.
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

Both numerator and denominator are signed sums over perfect matchings of fixed auxiliary bipartite graphs whose chain-state labels are exactly `{i,j,k,l}` and whose formal weights are only the requested `p_v,z_vw`.

This is the closest match yet to the literal Feynman-diagram wording of Open Problem 2.34.

## 7.4 Lemma 2.9

A=\{l\},\qquad \sigma=e_i,\qquad T=T_l,\qquad c=0.

\[

\[
Z_{jj}-Z_{ij}=\pi_jE_iT_j,

Subtracting yields

\[

\[
x_j=\pi_j(E_iT_l+E_lT_j-E_iT_j),

which is Lemma 2.9.

Take

\[
A=\{j,l\},\qquad \sigma=e_i,\qquad T=T_A,\qquad c=0.

\frac{E_iT_l+E_lT_j-E_iT_j}

This is Corollary 2.10.
