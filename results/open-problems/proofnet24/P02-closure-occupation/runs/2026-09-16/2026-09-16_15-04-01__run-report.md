This is the first mathematical run of the revised P02 assignment. The old Bass–Quillen reports are preserved as an inactive handoff and are not treated as I0023 history.

The counterexample-first gate was passed with exact arithmetic: on a three-state reversible lazy path, the source's own tempting subset extension
\[

\]
fails exactly, with `1/2 != 5/9`; a second natural unification that substitutes the conditioned stationary boundary law for the actual entrance law also fails exactly, `2 != 13/6`.

The new theorem is a single formula for the killed occupation Green function at an arbitrary nonempty boundary set `A`, expressed only in the stationary probabilities and fundamental-matrix entries on the marked states. The formula is a ratio of bordered determinants with a denominator depending only on `A`.

Let `I` be the finite state space, `P` the transition matrix, `pi` the unique stationary distribution, `Pi = 1 pi^T`, and
\[
Z=(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}-\Pi.
\]
This is the group inverse normalization used by Aldous–Fill; in a periodic chain it agrees with their Cesàro/continuized convention.

For nonempty `A subset I`, put
\[
T_A=\inf\{t\ge 0:X_t\in A\},
\quad
G_A(i,j)=E_i\sum_{s=0}^{T_A-1}{\bf1}_{\{X_s=j\}},
\]
\[
\omega_i^A(a)=P_i(X_{T_A}=a),\quad a\in A,
\qquad
t_i^A=E_iT_A.
\]
Freeze, once and for all for this target,
\[
M_A=
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_A^T&-\pi_A\\
{\bf1}^T&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\boxed{\Delta_A:=\det M_A}.
\tag{0.1}
\]
For every finite irreducible chain, `Delta_A != 0`, and
\[
\binom{\omega_i^A}{t_i^A}
=M_A^{-1}
\binom{Z_{iA}^T}{1}.
\tag{0.2}
\]
If
\[
r_j^T=(Z_{Aj}^T,-\pi_j),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
G_A(i,j)=
\frac{
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
M_A&\binom{Z_{iA}^T}{1}\\
r_j^T&Z_{ij}
\end{pmatrix}
}{\Delta_A}.
}
\tag{PORT}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
G_A(i,j)=
\frac{
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_A^T&-\pi_A&Z_{iA}^T\\
{\bf1}^T&0&1\\
Z_{Aj}^T&-\pi_j&Z_{ij}
\end{pmatrix}
}{
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_A^T&-\pi_A\\
{\bf1}^T&0
\end{pmatrix}
}.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

This is the source-requested kind of rule: the combinatorial object is a signed permutation/cycle-cover expansion of one bordered determinant; its monomials are products of formal `p_v` and `z_vw` variables, evaluated as `p_v=pi_v` and `z_vw=Z_vw`, and only the marked states `A union {i,j}` occur. For fixed stopping boundary `A`, every occupation numerator has the same frozen denominator `Delta_A`.

The theorem covers all 11 numbered Section 2.2.2 identities. It also gives, without new ideas, the three “Further results” explicitly requested by the source for `min(T_k,T_l)`. This is a theorem-sized mathematical candidate for Open Problem 2.34, but novelty/known-status is still unresolved because adjacent literature already contains broad occupation-measure and exit-frequency frameworks.

## 4. `P02-OCC-C1-PORTMANTEAU-BORDERED-GREEN` — PROVED

### Theorem

Let `P` be the transition matrix of any finite irreducible discrete-time Markov chain on `I`, with stationary law `pi`, and let
\[
Z=(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}-\Pi,\qquad \Pi={\bf1}\pi^T.
\]
Let `empty != A subset I`. Define `T_A`, `G_A`, `omega_i^A`, and `t_i^A` as in Section 0. Define `M_A` and `Delta_A` by (0.1).

Then:

1. `M_A` is invertible.
2. Equation (0.2) holds.
3. The single determinant formula (PORT)/(0.3) holds for every `i,j`.
4. Every coordinate of `omega_i^A` and `t_i^A` is a Cramer determinant with the same denominator `Delta_A`.
5. For `j notin A`,
   \[
   P_i(T_j<T_A)=\frac{G_A(i,j)}{G_A(j,j)}.
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
6. The determinant expansion is a signed sum over permutations, hence provides a canonical combinatorial expression in the formal variables `p_v,z_vw` on the marked state set, evaluated at `pi_v,Z_vw`.

### Proof

Fix `j` and let
\[
h_j(x)=Z_{xj}.
\]
The group-inverse equations are
\[
(I-P)Z=Z(I-P)=I-\Pi,
\qquad
Z{\bf1}=0,\qquad
\pi^TZ=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
(I-P)h_j=e_j-\pi_j{\bf1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
For bounded stopping time `T_A wedge n`,
\[
h_j(X_m)-h_j(X_0)+
\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}(I-P)h_j(X_s)
\]
is a martingale. Finite irreducibility implies `E_iT_A<infinity`; all state functions are bounded, so optional stopping and dominated convergence give
\[
E_i h_j(X_{T_A})-h_j(i)
+
G_A(i,j)-\pi_jt_i^A=0.
\]
Since `X_{T_A}` lies in `A`,
\[
\boxed{
G_A(i,j)
=
Z_{ij}
-
\sum_{a\in A}\omega_i^A(a)Z_{aj}
+
\pi_jt_i^A.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now take `j=b in A`. By the convention that time `T_A` is excluded,
`G_A(i,b)=0`. Thus for every `b in A`,
\[
\sum_{a\in A}Z_{ab}\omega_i^A(a)-\pi_b t_i^A=Z_{ib}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Together with
\[
\sum_{a\in A}\omega_i^A(a)=1,
\tag{4.6}
\]
these equations are precisely
\[
M_A
\binom{\omega_i^A}{t_i^A}
=
\binom{Z_{iA}^T}{1}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

It remains to prove `M_A` is invertible without assuming reversibility. Suppose
\[
M_A\binom{x}{c}=0.
\]
Extend `x` by zero outside `A`, obtaining a column vector `xi` on `I`. The last row says
\[
{\bf1}^T\xi=0.
\]
Set
\[
u=Z^T\xi.
\]
Transposing the second identity in (4.2),
\[
(I-P^T)u=(I-\Pi^T)\xi=\xi
\tag{4.8}
\]
because `1^T xi=0`. The first block equation says
\[
u_b=c\pi_b,\qquad b\in A.
\tag{4.9}
\]
For `b notin A`, (4.8) gives
\[
u_b=\sum_yP_{yb}u_y.
\]
Put `v_b=u_b/pi_b`. The reversed transition matrix
\[
\widehat P_{by}=\frac{\pi_yP_{yb}}{\pi_b}
\]
is a finite irreducible Markov chain, and
\[
v_b=\sum_y\widehat P_{by}v_y,\qquad b\notin A,
\]
while (4.9) gives `v=c` on `A`. Uniqueness of the finite Dirichlet problem, equivalently optional stopping at the almost surely finite hitting time of `A` for the reversed chain, gives
\[
v\equiv c.
\]
Hence `u=c pi`. But
\[
{\bf1}^Tu=(Z{\bf1})^T\xi=0,
\]
whereas `1^T(c pi)=c`. Therefore `c=0`, so `u=0`; (4.8) then gives `xi=0`. Thus the kernel of `M_A` is zero and
\[
\Delta_A\ne0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Equation (0.2) is now (4.7). Substitute its solution into (4.4). The Schur-complement determinant identity
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
M&b\\r^T&z
\end{pmatrix}
=
\det(M)(z-r^TM^{-1}b)
\]
gives exactly (PORT). Cramer's rule gives the common-denominator formulas for `omega` and `t`.

Finally, for `j notin A`, on the event `T_j<T_A`, the strong Markov property at `T_j` says that the expected subsequent number of visits to `j` before `T_A` is `G_A(j,j)`; on the complementary event the occupation is zero. Hence
\[
G_A(i,j)=P_i(T_j<T_A)G_A(j,j),
\]
which proves (4.1).

The determinant expansion over permutations is the requested finite combinatorial rule. QED.

## 5. Two-boundary form: the source's explicit example

Write
\[
H_{xy}=E_xT_y=\frac{Z_{yy}-Z_{xy}}{\pi_y}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
For `A={k,l}`, define
\[
D=H_{kl}+H_{lk}.
\]
Then
\[
\Delta_{\{k,l\}}=\pi_k\pi_lD.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Solving the two boundary equations gives
\[
\boxed{
P_i(T_k<T_l)
=
\frac{H_{il}+H_{lk}-H_{ik}}{D},
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
\[
\boxed{
P_i(T_l<T_k)
=
\frac{H_{ik}+H_{kl}-H_{il}}{D}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
The mean time to the first boundary hit is
\[
\boxed{
E_i\min(T_k,T_l)
=
\frac{
H_{ik}H_{kl}+H_{il}H_{lk}-H_{kl}H_{lk}
}{D}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
If `omega_k,omega_l` denote (5.3),(5.4), then for every `j`,
\[
\boxed{
G_{\{k,l\}}(i,j)
=
\pi_j\left(
t_i+\omega_kH_{kj}+\omega_lH_{lj}-H_{ij}
\right).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
For `j notin {k,l}`,
\[
\boxed{
P_i(T_j<\min(T_k,T_l))
=
\frac{G_{\{k,l\}}(i,j)}
{G_{\{k,l\}}(j,j)}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Thus the three “Further results” named by Aldous–Fill are direct specializations of the same formula.

## 6. `P02-OCC-C2-SOURCE-11-OF-11` — PROVED coverage certificate

### Singleton absorption

Take `A={l}`. Then `Delta_A=pi_l`. From (4.4) with `j=l`,
\[
0=Z_{il}-Z_{ll}+\pi_lE_iT_l,
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{
E_iT_l=\frac{Z_{ll}-Z_{il}}{\pi_l}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
This is Lemma 2.12.

For arbitrary `j`, substitute (6.1) in (4.4):
\[
G_{\{l\}}(i,j)
=
Z_{ij}-Z_{lj}+\pi_jE_iT_l.
\]
Using
\[
Z_{xj}=Z_{jj}-\pi_jE_xT_j
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
G_{\{l\}}(i,j)
=
\pi_j(E_iT_l+E_lT_j-E_iT_j),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
which is Lemma 2.9. Setting `i=j` and `l=i` in the source notation gives Lemma 2.7. Dividing (6.2) by the same formula with start `j`, via (4.1), gives Corollary 2.10.

Averaging (6.1) over `i~pi` and using `pi^TZ=0` gives
\[
\pi_lE_\pi T_l=Z_{ll},
\]
Lemma 2.11. Summing (6.1) over targets `l` against `pi_l` and using `Z1=0` gives
\[
\sum_l\pi_lE_iT_l=\sum_lZ_{ll},
\]
Corollary 2.13, and its independence of `i` is Corollary 2.14. Averaging (6.2) over `i~pi` and using Lemma 2.11 yields
\[
E_\pi[\#j\text{ before }T_l]
=
\frac{\pi_j}{\pi_l}Z_{ll}-Z_{lj},
\]
which is Lemma 2.15 after renaming `l` as the source's `i`.

### First return

For `A={i}`, define the first-return occupation
\[
G_i^+(i,j)
=
E_i\sum_{s=0}^{T_i^+-1}{\bf1}_{\{X_s=j\}}.
\]
One step of the chain followed by singleton killing gives
\[
G_i^+(i,j)
=
{\bf1}_{\{i=j\}}
+
\sum_xp_{ix}G_{\{i\}}(x,j).
\tag{6.3}
\]
Use (4.4) and
\[
PZ=Z-I+\Pi.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Also, from (6.1),
\[
\sum_xp_{ix}E_xT_i
=
\frac{Z_{ii}-(PZ)_{ii}}{\pi_i}
=
\frac{1-\pi_i}{\pi_i}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Equations (6.3)–(6.5) simplify exactly to
\[
\boxed{G_i^+(i,j)=\pi_j/\pi_i,}
\tag{6.6}
\]
which is Lemma 2.6. Summing (6.6) over `j` gives
\[
E_iT_i^+=1/\pi_i,
\]
Lemma 2.5.

For `j!=i`, the number of `j`-visits in one `i`-return excursion is zero unless `j` is hit before returning to `i`, and conditional on that event its expected size is `G_{\{i\}}(j,j)`. Therefore
\[
P_i(T_j<T_i^+)
=
\frac{G_i^+(i,j)}{G_{\{i\}}(j,j)}
=
\frac{1}{\pi_i(E_iT_j+E_jT_i)},
\]
Corollary 2.8.

This accounts for all eleven frozen identities from one theorem.
