## 5. B4 — prove a weaker moment hypothesis completely

We now prove Theorem `P11-I0062-LLOG-L-BLOCK-WALL-03`.

### 5.1 Discrete convention

To make the tiling exact, write the global base as `K x L` vertical lattice columns. The source's box `[0,k] x [0,l]` has `(k+1)(l+1)` such columns, so

\[
\frac{KL}{kl}\to1
\]

as `k,l->infinity`; the convention does not alter any normalized limit. Let the vertical levels be `0,1,...,M`.

Write

\[
\mu=ET<\infty,
\]

which follows from (1.3).

### 5.2 A fixed local block scale

For each integer `n>=2`, put

\[
h_n=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor,
\qquad D_n=2h_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `X_n` be the top-to-bottom min-cut capacity in a straight `n x n`-column box of vertical depth `D_n`, using only edges whose endpoints lie in that local induced box.

Up to the harmless `n` versus `n-1` lattice-boundary convention, `X_n` is exactly a translate of Rossignol--Théret's `phi(nA,h_n)` for `A=[0,1]^2 x {0}`. Here `h_n->infinity` and

\[
\frac{\log h_n}{n^2}\to0.
\]

Their Theorem 3.16 under finite mean therefore gives

\[
\frac{EX_n}{n^2}\longrightarrow \nu_G(e_3).
\tag{5.2}
\]

(The theorem gives `L^1` convergence of the local normalized flow, hence convergence of expectations; replacing `n^2` by the exact lattice-area convention changes the ratio by `1+o(1)`.)

### 5.3 The local cut variable is itself `L log L`

A fixed horizontal plane is a local cut, so for some deterministic `m_n=O(n^2)`,

\[
0\le X_n\le S_n:=\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}T_j.
\tag{5.3}
\]

A finite sum of nonnegative `L log L` variables is again `L log L`. For completeness, if `S=sum_{j=1}^m s_j` and `M=max_j s_j`, then

\[
S\log(1+S)
\le mM\log(1+mM)
\le m\log m\sum_j s_j+m\sum_j s_j\log(1+s_j).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Taking expectations and using (1.3) proves

\[
E[X_n\log(1+X_n)]<\infty
\tag{5.5}
\]

for every fixed `n`.

### 5.4 Partition an arbitrary anisotropic base after fixing `n`

This is the crucial quantifier order. **Fix `n` first.** For arbitrary large `K,L`, write

\[
K=r_x+q_xn,
\qquad L=r_y+q_yn,
\qquad 0\le r_x,r_y<n.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The region `x>=r_x, y>=r_y` is tiled by `q_x q_y` disjoint `n x n` blocks; the strips `x<r_x` or `y<r_y` form a remainder.

For `M>=D_n`, look only at the bottom slab `0<=z<=D_n`. In each full base block choose a local minimum bottom-to-top cut of that block inside the slab. Add two further deterministic families of edges:

1. **inter-block walls:** every horizontal lattice edge crossing a boundary between two distinct base blocks or between the full-block region and a remainder strip, at each level `z=0,...,D_n`;
2. **remainder plane:** the vertical edges crossing one fixed horizontal level inside the slab above every remainder column.

Call the union `E_{K,L}^{(n)}`.

### 5.5 Deterministic gluing lemma

`E_{K,L}^{(n)}` is a bottom-to-top cut of the whole global box.

Indeed, consider any nearest-neighbour path from global bottom to global top. It must traverse the bottom slab. The wall edges prevent a path inside that slab from changing between distinct full-block components or between a full block and a remainder component. If its slab component is a full block, traversing from level `0` to level `D_n` crosses that block's chosen local cut. If its slab component is in the remainder, traversing the slab crosses the remainder-plane edge set. Thus every bottom-to-top path meets `E_{K,L}^{(n)}`.

By max-flow/min-cut,

\[
\Phi(K,L,M)\le V(E_{K,L}^{(n)}).
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the only finite combinatorial core. The recovered exact artifact checks it exhaustively in two small three-dimensional cylinders for all 65,536 local-cut choices, with and without a remainder, and checks the wall-count inequality in 2,520 exact integer parameter cases. The theorem does not rely on finite testing; the preceding paragraph is the general proof.

### 5.6 Strong law for the local-block term

For a fixed `n` and fixed residue pair `(r_x,r_y)`, the local cut costs over the tiled blocks are iid copies of `X_n`, because their internal edge sets are disjoint. By (5.5) and the two-parameter iid strong law of Smythe/Klesov--Molchanov,

\[
\frac1{q_xq_y}\sum_{i<q_x,\,j<q_y}X_{n;i,j}
\longrightarrow EX_n
\qquad\text{a.s. as }q_x,q_y\to\infty.
\tag{5.8}
\]

There are only `n^2` residue pairs, so (5.8) holds simultaneously for all offsets on one probability-one event. Since `K,L->infinity`, both `q_x,q_y->infinity`; moreover

\[
\frac{q_xq_yn^2}{KL}\to1.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{\text{local-block cut cost}}{KL}
\longrightarrow \frac{EX_n}{n^2}
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

for every fixed `n`.

### 5.7 Strong law for the wall term

At any fixed slab level, the number of x-oriented wall edges is at most `q_x L`, and the number of y-oriented wall edges at most `q_y K`. Thus deterministically

\[
\#W_{K,L}^{(n)}
\le (D_n+1)(q_xL+q_yK),
\tag{5.10}
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{\#W_{K,L}^{(n)}}{KL}
\le \frac{2(D_n+1)}n.
\tag{5.11}
\]

For fixed `n`, fixed residue, fixed level, and fixed orientation, the capacities on successive selected wall edges form an iid two-parameter array. The same `L log L` rectangular strong law applied to `T` shows that their average capacity tends to `mu` along arbitrary rectangular growth. Intersecting the finitely many level/orientation/residue events gives

\[
\limsup_{K,L\to\infty}
\frac{\text{wall capacity}}{KL}
\le \frac{2\mu(D_n+1)}n
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

for each fixed `n`.

### 5.8 The remainder cost is negligible

The remainder consists of at most `r_x<n` full-length column strips in one direction and at most `r_y<n` in the other. The chosen horizontal remainder-plane capacity is therefore a sum over `O(nL+nK)` iid vertical edges.

For each of the finitely many possible strip indices, the ordinary one-dimensional strong law (finite mean is enough) gives the row/column average `->mu`. Consequently

\[
\frac{\text{remainder-plane capacity}}{KL}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{5.13}
\]

for fixed `n`, because both `K` and `L` tend to infinity.

### 5.9 Take the outer limit first, then the block scale

On the countable intersection over `n` of the probability-one events above, (5.7), (5.9), (5.12), and (5.13) yield, for every fixed `n`,

\[
\limsup_{K,L,M\to\infty}
\frac{\Phi(K,L,M)}{KL}
\le
\frac{EX_n}{n^2}
+
\frac{2\mu(D_n+1)}n.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Now let `n->infinity`. By (5.1),

\[
\frac{D_n+1}{n}\to0,
\]

and by (5.2), `EX_n/n^2 -> nu_G(e_3)`. Hence

\[
\limsup_{K,L,M\to\infty}
\frac{\Phi(K,L,M)}{KL}
\le \nu_G(e_3)
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]

which proves (1.4).

### 5.10 Combine with the inherited lower bracket

On every corrected intended Kesten/Zhang source sequence, `P11-I0062-TRUNCATION-LIMINF-01` gives (1.1) with the same flow constant. Combining (1.1) and (1.4) proves the a.s. convergence (1.5).

This step uses no numerical approximation and no unsupported identification of a flat-cylinder theorem with the source geometry: the lower bracket comes from bounded truncations on the full source geometry, while the new upper theorem is proved directly on arbitrary anisotropic straight bases.

### 5.11 `L^1` convergence is proved separately, not inferred from a.s. convergence

Because `ET<infinity`, de la Vallée Poussin gives a convex increasing superlinear function `Psi` with

\[
E\Psi(T)<\infty.
\]

A fixed horizontal plane is always a cut. Thus, up to a deterministic boundary ratio tending to one,

\[
0\le \frac{\Phi(K,L,M)}{KL}
\le \frac1{N_{K,L}}\sum_{e\in P_{K,L}}T_e,
\tag{5.15}
\]

where `P_{K,L}` is one horizontal layer and `N_{K,L}=Theta(KL)`.

By Jensen,

\[
E\Psi\!\left(\frac1{N_{K,L}}\sum_{e\in P_{K,L}}T_e\right)
\le E\Psi(T).
\tag{5.16}
\]

Hence these plane averages are uniformly integrable, and so are the normalized flows in (5.15). Uniform integrability plus (1.5) gives convergence in `L^1` to the finite constant `nu_G(e_3)`.

This completes the theorem. QED.

## 6. B12 — the conserved quantity is a dimension-matched log moment

The proof explains exactly where the new hypothesis comes from.

The source difficulty is **two-parameter arbitrary base growth**. Raw iid rectangular sums in two parameters satisfy their unrestricted strong law precisely at the `L log L` threshold. Klesov--Molchanov restate the classical equivalence

\[
\frac{1}{n_1n_2}\sum_{i\le n_1,j\le n_2}Z_{ij}\to EZ
\quad\text{over }\min(n_1,n_2)\to\infty
\]

if and only if

\[
E[|Z|\log^+|Z|]<\infty
\]

for iid summands (in the standard rectangular-net formulation).

Thus `L log L` is not a random exponent inserted into the proof: it is the exact moment cost of controlling the **raw two-dimensional rectangular block and wall arrays** used here. This does **not** prove that `L log L` is sharp for maximal flow; it proves only that this specific raw-array architecture cannot be expected to drop to bare `L^1` without using more structure.

There is a second useful invariant from the previous heavy-tail witness. If `ET<infinity`, then

\[
a\,P(T\ge a)
\le E[T;T\ge a]\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence for every integer height `h>=1`,

\[
a\,P(T\ge a)^h\le a\,P(T\ge a)\to0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

So the explicit “many all-huge vertical columns” mechanism that destroyed the unrestricted no-moment theorem cannot survive finite mean. This makes finite mean a mathematically natural next threshold rather than an arbitrary wish.

## 7. B27 — swap the quantifiers

The successful proof does **not** seek a finite-size estimate uniform simultaneously in block size, aspect ratio, and outer dimensions.

The order is:

1. fix a local block side `n` and local slab height `D_n`;
2. let the arbitrary outer base dimensions `K,L` and height `M` tend to infinity;
3. use a rectangular strong law at that fixed local scale;
4. only then let `n->infinity` and invoke the fixed-shape Rossignol--Théret LLN.

This quantifier swap is what turns a difficult anisotropic FPP estimate into a deterministic gluing statement plus classical multiparameter laws of large numbers. It also isolates the next obstruction cleanly: replacing the raw wall/block averages by a genuinely subadditive object may remove the `L log L` cost.

## 8. Why finite mean is still OPEN here

The theorem should not be overstated.

- Rossignol--Théret already give the fixed-shape straight `phi` LLN under finite mean.
- The present proof extends to arbitrary aspect ratios only because `L log L` gives a raw two-parameter rectangular SLLN for block costs and wall capacities.
- For generic iid two-parameter arrays, finite mean alone is not enough for that unrestricted rectangular strong law.
- Therefore simply deleting `log` from (1.3) in the proof above leaves a real probabilistic gap, not a bookkeeping gap.

The promising genuinely different route is the auxiliary flow `tau`. Théret's HDR explicitly explains that `phi` is not subadditive because adjacent top-bottom cutsets do not glue, whereas `tau` was designed so that adjacent cuts do glue and multiparameter subadditive ergodic theorems apply under finite mean. A finite-mean solution of the remaining source problem should try to compare anisotropic `phi` to a suitably embedded `tau` without reintroducing a raw two-dimensional wall sum.
