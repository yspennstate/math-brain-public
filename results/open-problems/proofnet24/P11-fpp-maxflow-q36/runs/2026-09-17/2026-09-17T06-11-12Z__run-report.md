# 6. B1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: deterministic-height one-sided anchored surface energy

The key category change is to stop constructing a free-boundary top-bottom cut directly. Instead use the exact one-sided slab geometry behind Rossignol--Theret's gluable alternative flow, but freeze the height deterministically.

Let `A` be a finite axis-aligned horizontal base hyperrectangle. Use Rossignol--Theret's lattice discretization `V(A)` and their infinite horizontal slab notation. For integer `H>=1`, define

\[
\Psi_H^G(A)
=
\inf\{T_G(E): E\text{ separates }V(A)\text{ from the horizontal plane at height }H
\text{ inside the one-sided slab}\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For axis-aligned integral rectangles this is the obvious discrete one-sided cut, modulo the harmless boundary convention already present in the literature.

Two deterministic properties are immediate and are the reason this object is useful.

### 6.1 Exact gluing / subadditivity

If coplanar hyperrectangles `A_1,...,A_q` have disjoint interiors and union `B`, and `E_i` is a valid height-`H` cut for `A_i`, then

\[
E=\bigcup_iE_i
\]

is a valid height-`H` cut for `B`. Indeed, every path from `V(B)=\cup_iV(A_i)` to the common top plane starts in one of the `V(A_i)` and must cross its corresponding `E_i`. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Psi_H^G(B)\le\sum_i\Psi_H^G(A_i).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

No connector edge is paid. This is the exact gluing mechanism that ordinary `phi` lacks.

### 6.2 Base monotonicity

If `A\subseteq B` in the same horizontal plane, every cut separating `V(B)` from the top plane also separates `V(A)`, so

\[
\boxed{\Psi_H^G(A)\le\Psi_H^G(B).}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This will remove aspect-ratio restrictions by enlarging an arbitrary rectangle to a block-multiple rectangle.

# 7. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: the anchored source/top homology class

## Lemma 7.1 — finite-column-minimum shell domination

Let

\[
r=r_*(G)<\infty.
\]

Then, for every fixed finite base `A`, there exists an integrable random variable `Z_A` such that

\[
\boxed{\Psi_H^G(A)\le Z_A}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for every sufficiently large deterministic height `H`.

### Proof

Because `E M_r<infinity`, necessarily `G({+infinity})=0`.

Work in the one-sided lattice slab around the finite source `V(A)`. Choose `r` graph-distance radii

\[
R_j=1+2j,\qquad j=0,\ldots,r-1,
\]

and let `E_j` be the edge boundary of the graph-distance ball of radius `R_j` around `V(A)`. Once `H>2r+1`, each `E_j` is finite and every path from `V(A)` to the top plane must leave that ball, so `E_j` is a valid cut. The spacing by two makes the edge sets `E_j` pairwise disjoint.

Put

\[
S_j=\sum_{e\in E_j}T_e,
\qquad
Z_A=\min_{0\le j<r}S_j.
\]

Because the edge sets are disjoint and capacities are iid, `S_0,...,S_{r-1}` are independent. Let `N_j=|E_j|` and `N=\max_jN_j`. For `t>0`, if `S_j>t`, some edge in `E_j` has capacity larger than `t/N_j`, hence

\[
\Pr(S_j>t)
\le N_j\Pr(T>t/N_j)
\le N_j\Pr(T>t/N).
\]

Therefore

\[
\Pr(Z_A>t)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\Pr(S_j>t)
\le
\Bigl(\prod_jN_j\Bigr)\Pr(T>t/N)^r.
\]

Integrating the tail and substituting `u=t/N`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb EZ_A
&\le
\Bigl(\prod_jN_j\Bigr)N
\int_0^\infty \Pr(T>u)^r\,du\\
&=
\Bigl(\prod_jN_j\Bigr)N\,\mathbb E\min(T_1,\ldots,T_r)
<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally `Psi_H(A)` is no larger than the capacity of any valid `E_j`, hence no larger than `Z_A`. QED.

This lemma is the exact finite-height analogue of the classical finite-minimum moment regularization for paths: the integrable object is a **minimum among finitely many disjoint surface shells**, not a prescribed raw edge or a horizontal plane sum.

\[

\[

\le
(1+o(1))\frac1{N_{k,l}}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal R_{k,l}}Y_z.
\tag{11.1}
\]

No independence of the `Y_z` is needed for uniform integrability. Here is the elementary argument. Let

\[
A_N=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i
\]

for any finite family of identically distributed nonnegative copies/translates. Fix `M>0` and write

\[
Y_i=(Y_i\wedge M)+R_i,
\qquad
R_i=(Y_i-M)_+\le Y_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>M\}}.
\]

Then

\[
A_N\le M+\overline R_N,
\qquad
\overline R_N=\frac1N\sum_iR_i.
\]

On `{A_N>2M}` one has `overline R_N>M`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[A_N;A_N>2M]
&\le
M\Pr(\overline R_N>M)+\mathbb E\overline R_N\\
&\le 2\,\mathbb E[Y_0;Y_0>M].
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.2}
\]

The right side tends to zero uniformly in `N`. Thus these rectangular averages are uniformly integrable, and so is the dominated normalized-flow family in (11.1). Combining uniform integrability with the almost-sure convergence (10.7) yields

\[

\frac{\Phi_G(k,l,m(k,l))}{kl}
