# 3. B7 — take the power-tail limit and locate the exact finite-height thresholds

Fix the Pareto family

\[
\Pr(T>t)=(1+t)^{-\alpha},\qquad t\ge0,
\tag{3.1}
\]

with `alpha>0`. Let `Phi_{K,L,h}` be the top-to-bottom maximal flow in a finite straight box of fixed integer height `h` and finite nonempty base. Put

\[
N=(K+1)(L+1),
\]

the number of vertical edges in each horizontal layer cut.

## Proposition 3.1 — exact finite-box moment threshold

For every `p>0`,

\[
\boxed{
E\,\Phi_{K,L,h}^{\,p}<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p<\alpha h.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

For the lower bound, one fixed vertical column is an admissible flow path. If `T_1,...,T_h` are its vertical capacities, then

\[
\Phi_{K,L,h}\ge M_h:=\min(T_1,\ldots,T_h).
\]

By (3.1),

\[
\Pr(M_h>t)=(1+t)^{-\alpha h}.
\]

Thus `E M_h^p=infinity` whenever `p>=alpha h`, proving necessity.

For the upper bound let `S_1,...,S_h` be the capacities of the `h` disjoint horizontal layer cuts. They are iid and

\[
\Phi_{K,L,h}\le\min_j S_j.
\]

If `S_1>t`, at least one of its `N` summands exceeds `t/N`, so

\[
\Pr(S_1>t)
\le N\Pr(T>t/N)
\le C_{N,\alpha}(1+t)^{-\alpha}.
\]

Independence of the layer cuts gives

\[
\Pr(\Phi_{K,L,h}>t)
\le C_{N,\alpha}^{\,h}(1+t)^{-\alpha h}.
\]

The tail integral for the `p`th moment is finite for `p<alpha h`. This proves (3.2). QED.

Consequently the invariant from claim 05 is explicit for this family:

\[
\boxed{
r_*(G_\alpha)=\min\{h\ge1:\alpha h>1\}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, the benchmark `alpha=3/4` has `r_*=2`, exactly as the previous report identified from `E min(T_1,T_2)=2`.

This is more than a restatement of claim 05: it determines **every finite moment threshold** of a finite-height flow in the Pareto family. It also suggests the correct scale for a surface construction: choose a fixed depth `h` with `alpha h>1`, and try to turn the `h` local vertical alternatives into an area-order global cut.

# 4. B16 + B26 — a cluster-adaptive fixed-slab surface for every Pareto tail

The previous ultra-heavy counterexample used tails so slow that no finite number of series edges regularized the mean. The simplest unresolved sporadic case is instead `alpha=3/4`, where two vertical alternatives already have an integrable minimum. This section proves that for **every** Pareto `0<alpha<1`, once `h` is chosen with `alpha h>1`, an `h`-layer adaptive surface has finite area density even though `E T=infinity`.

## Theorem `P11-I0062-PARETO-CLUSTER-SURFACE-06` — PROVED / PARTIAL

Let the iid edge capacities satisfy (3.1) with `0<alpha<1`. Fix an integer

\[
h\ge2,\qquad \alpha h>1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then there exists a stationary, translation-covariant random cut rule supported entirely in the bottom `h` layers and a deterministic finite constant `C_{alpha,h}` such that, for every sequence of straight boxes with both base side lengths `K,L->infinity` and every global height `m>=h`, eventually,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{K,L\to\infty}
\frac{\Phi_{K,L,m}}{(K+1)(L+1)}
\le C_{\alpha,h}<\infty
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The convergence of the cut-cost density holds along arbitrary rectangles: no restriction on `K/L` is imposed.

Therefore every power-law Pareto tail has **area-order** maximal flow once a fixed depth exceeds its column-minimum threshold. In particular for `alpha=3/4`, one may take `h=2`; normalized flow cannot diverge along a corrected Kesten sequence. Any counterexample in that `r_*=2` law would have to be a finite overshoot/oscillation phenomenon, not the previous blow-up mechanism.

### Proof

Work in the infinite base lattice `Z^2` and use only levels `0,1,...,h` of the three-dimensional environment.

For each base site `x` and `j=0,...,h-1`, let

\[
a_{x,j}
\]

be the capacity of the vertical edge from `(x,j)` to `(x,j+1)`. For every nearest-neighbor base bond `e={x,y}` and middle level `z=1,...,h-1`, let

\[
c_{e,z}
\]

be the capacity of the horizontal edge joining `(x,z)` to `(y,z)`.

All these variables are iid copies of `T`.

### Step 1. A sparse graph of dangerous horizontal bonds

Choose a finite threshold `M` so large that

\[
q_M:=\Pr\{\max_{1\le z\le h-1}c_{e,z}>M\}<\frac1{32}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is possible because `q_M<= (h-1)P(T>M)->0`.

Call a base bond **heavy** when the event in (4.3) occurs. Heavy bonds are independent Bernoulli bonds with parameter `q_M`.

Let `C(x)` be the heavy-bond component of `x`. These components are finite with an exponential size tail. For completeness, no exact `p_c` value is needed: if `|C(0)|>=n`, there is a rooted embedded tree on `n` vertices whose `n-1` bonds are all heavy. Fixing an ordering of lattice directions, each such tree has a deterministic depth-first traversal of length `2(n-1)`. There are at most

\[
4^{2(n-1)}=16^{n-1}
\]

such nearest-neighbor traversal words, while a specified tree is heavy with probability `q_M^{n-1}`. Hence

\[
\Pr(|C(0)|\ge n)
\le (16q_M)^{n-1}
\le 2^{-(n-1)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular `|C(0)|` has moments of every order.

### Step 2. Give each heavy component one common cut height

For a finite heavy component `C` and each `j=0,...,h-1`, define the vertical cost

\[
A_{C,j}=\sum_{x\in C}a_{x,j}.
\]

Choose

\[
\ell(C)\in\arg\min_{0\le j<h} A_{C,j},
\tag{4.5}
\]

breaking ties by the smallest `j`, and put `ell_x=ell(C(x))`.

Interpret `ell_x` as placing the cut in column `x` on the vertical edge from level `ell_x` to `ell_x+1`. The source side in this column consists of levels `z<=ell_x`; the sink side consists of `z>=ell_x+1`.

If `x,y` lie in the same heavy component, then `ell_x=ell_y`, so **no** middle horizontal edge on that base bond is crossed. If `ell_x!=ell_y`, then the base bond is not heavy, and therefore every crossed middle horizontal capacity is at most `M`. A base bond contributes at most

\[
M|\ell_x-\ell_y|\le M(h-1).
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the deterministic geometric mechanism checked exactly by the finite artifact in Section 7 below.

### Step 3. The selected vertical cost has an `L^p` moment for some `p>1`

Choose numbers

\[
1<p<\alpha h,
\qquad
\frac{p}{h}<\beta<\alpha.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `alpha<1`, also `beta<1`. Put `mu_beta=E T^beta<infinity`.

Conditional on a heavy component `C` of size `n`, the `h` random variables `A_{C,0},...,A_{C,h-1}` are independent copies of

\[
S_n=T_1+\cdots+T_n,
\]

and they are independent of the horizontal capacities that generated `C`.

Because `0<beta<1`,

\[
S_n^\beta\le\sum_{i=1}^nT_i^\beta,
\qquad
E S_n^\beta\le n\mu_\beta.
\]

Thus Markov gives

\[
P(S_n>t)\le\min\{1,n\mu_\beta t^{-\beta}\}.
\]

For

\[
W_n:=\min_{0\le j<h}A_{C,j},
\]

independence yields

\[
P(W_n>t)
\le\min\{1,(n\mu_\beta)^h t^{-h\beta}\}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Since `p<h beta`, integration at the threshold `t_0=(n mu_beta)^{1/beta}` gives the explicit polynomial bound

\[
E W_n^p
\le
\left(1+\frac{p}{h\beta-p}\right)
(n\mu_\beta)^{p/\beta}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Let `V_x=a_{x,ell_x}` be the selected vertical cut capacity at site `x`. Since the selected component sum is `W_{|C(x)|}`, every summand is nonnegative and

\[
V_x\le W_{|C(x)|}.
\]

Equations (4.4) and (4.9) imply

\[
E V_0^p<\infty.
\tag{4.10}
\]

### Step 4. A stationary `L^p` local cut-energy field

Define

\[
U_x
=
V_x+
\frac12\sum_{y:\,|y-x|=1}
\sum_{z=\min(\ell_x,\ell_y)+1}^{\max(\ell_x,\ell_y)} c_{\{x,y\},z}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Every horizontal capacity appearing in (4.11) is at most `M`, by Step 2. There are four neighboring base bonds and at most `h-1` crossed middle levels per bond, so

\[
0\le U_x\le V_x+2M(h-1).
\]

Therefore

\[
U_0\in L^p,\qquad p>1.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The rule is a translation-covariant measurable function of the iid edge environment. Hence `(U_x)_{x in Z^2}` is stationary and ergodic.

By the classical Dunford--Zygmund multiparameter pointwise ergodic theorem for `L^p`, `p>1`, rectangular averages converge with no aspect-ratio restriction:

\[
\frac1{|R_{K,L}|}\sum_{x\in R_{K,L}}U_x
\longrightarrow E U_0
=:C_{\alpha,h}<\infty
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{4.13}
\]

whenever both `K,L->infinity`.

### Step 5. The labels produce an admissible cut in every taller straight box

Restrict the infinite label field to the finite base rectangle `R_{K,L}`. In column `x`, put levels `0,...,ell_x` on the source side and all levels `ell_x+1,...,m` on the sink side. This separates the bottom face from the top face for every `m>=h`.

Its vertical cut edges have total capacity `sum_x V_x`. Its internal horizontal cut edges are exactly those counted by the half-edge terms in (4.11); summing `U_x` over the rectangle counts each internal crossed horizontal edge once and may also count harmless nonnegative boundary-to-outside terms. Hence the finite cut capacity is at most

\[
\sum_{x\in R_{K,L}}U_x.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Max-flow/min-cut and (4.13) give

\[
\limsup_{K,L\to\infty}
\frac{\Phi_{K,L,m}}{|R_{K,L}|}
\le C_{\alpha,h}<\infty
\quad\text{a.s.},
\]

uniformly in every choice of `m>=h`. This proves (4.2). QED.

# 5. What the theorem does and does not decide

For the Pareto family, (3.3) and Theorem 06 now give a clean picture.

- `alpha>1`: finite mean, so claim 04 gives the full corrected source LLN a.s. and in `L^1`.
- `0<alpha<1`: the mean is infinite, but `r_*<infinity`. Choosing any fixed `h` with `alpha h>1`, Theorem 06 gives a finite area-order upper bound on **all** taller boxes, arbitrary aspect.
- `alpha=3/4`: `r_*=2`; the simplest unresolved law is now proved not to have the blow-up pathology of claim 02/05.
- The previous divergent example belongs to a qualitatively heavier class: its tail is so slow that `r_*=infinity`.

This does **not** show

\[
\limsup\frac{\Phi}{KL}\le\nu_G
\]

for Pareto `alpha<1`, and it does not prove convergence. The constructed constant `C_{alpha,h}` can be much larger than the no-moment flow constant `nu_G`; the crude bounded horizontal overhead does not vanish as the threshold `M` grows. Thus no auxiliary stronger inequality has been confused with the source statement.

The result nevertheless closes one plausible negation route: a Pareto `r_*=2` counterexample cannot be obtained merely by making the normalized flow diverge to infinity along very slow heights. A genuine failure would have to produce finite positive overshoot or oscillation above `nu_G`.

The next structural issue is therefore not integrability but **comparison to the truncated optimal surface**: can one repair a `G^M` min-cut around sparse `T>M` clusters at an `o(area)` cost? That is a different route from another finite-mean block lemma.
