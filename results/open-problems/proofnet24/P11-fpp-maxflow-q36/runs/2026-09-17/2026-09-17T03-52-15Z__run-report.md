# 3. B2 + B30: exact finite-box tail threshold

Define, for iid copies `T_1,T_2,...` of the edge capacity `T`,

\[
M_h := \min(T_1,\ldots,T_h),
\qquad
r_*(G):=\inf\{h\ge1:\mathbb E M_h<\infty\}\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}.
\]

Equivalently, if `bar G(t)=P(T>t)`, then

\[
\mathbb E M_h
=\int_0^\infty \bar G(t)^h\,dt.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The new result is:

## Theorem `P11-I0062-COLUMN-MIN-MOMENT-05` — PROVED / PARTIAL

Let `Phi_{K,L,h}` denote the maximal vertical flow in the finite straight box

\[
[0,K]\times[0,L]\times[0,h]\subset\mathbb Z^3.
\]

### (A) Exact finite-box integrability criterion

For every positive integer height `h` and every nonempty finite base,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\Phi_{K,L,h}<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E M_h<\infty.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### (B) Necessary tail regularity for a source-universal LLN

Assume `r_*(G)=infinity`, i.e.

\[
\mathbb E M_h=\infty\qquad\text{for every finite }h.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then there exists a deterministic corrected-Kesten height function `m(k,l)` with

\[
m(k,l)\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\log m(k,l)}{k^{1/2}}\to0
\tag{3.4}
\]

(as `k>=l->infinity`) and a diagonal subsequence `k=l=N_r`, `m(N_r,N_r)=r`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi_{N_r,N_r,r}}{N_r^2}\longrightarrow+\infty
\qquad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular, any theorem asserting the finite flow-constant LLN for **every** corrected Kesten height function must exclude `r_*(G)=infinity`. A necessary tail regularity is therefore

\[
\boxed{
r_*(G)<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists h<\infty:\int_0^\infty\bar G(t)^h\,dt<\infty.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is a necessary condition for a source-universal extension, not a proved sufficient condition.

## Proof of (A)

Pick one fixed vertical column. Sending water only along this column is an admissible flow of value equal to the minimum of its `h` vertical edge capacities. Therefore

\[
\Phi_{K,L,h}\ge M_h^{(0)}
\]

for a copy `M_h^{(0)}` of `M_h`. Hence `E M_h=infinity` implies `E Phi_{K,L,h}=infinity`.

Conversely, let `N=(K+1)(L+1)` be the number of vertical edges in one horizontal layer. For each of the `h` layer interfaces let

\[
S_j=\sum_{i=1}^{N}T_{j,i},\qquad j=1,\ldots,h,
\]

where the `S_j` are iid because the horizontal layer cutsets use disjoint vertical-edge sets. Every such layer is a top-bottom cut, so

\[
\Phi_{K,L,h}\le\min_{1\le j\le h}S_j.
\tag{3.7}
\]

For `t>0`, if `S_1>t` then at least one summand exceeds `t/N`; hence

\[
\Pr(S_1>t)\le N\,\bar G(t/N).
\]

Independence of the `h` layer sums yields

\[
\Pr\!\left(\min_jS_j>t\right)
=\Pr(S_1>t)^h
\le N^h\bar G(t/N)^h.
\]

Integrating the tail,

\[
\mathbb E\min_jS_j
\le N^h\int_0^\infty\bar G(t/N)^h\,dt
=N^{h+1}\mathbb E M_h.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus `E M_h<infinity` implies `E Phi_{K,L,h}<infinity`. This proves (3.2).

## Proof of (B)

Assume (3.3). For each integer `r>=1`, let

\[
H_r:=\min(T_1,\ldots,T_r).
\]

Since `E H_r=infinity`, choose a finite truncation level `b_r` so that

\[
\mu_r:=\mathbb E(H_r\wedge b_r)\ge 4r.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For a box with height `r`, the minima of the capacities along distinct vertical columns are iid copies of `H_r`. Because `H_r wedge b_r` is bounded, its empirical average converges in probability (indeed a.s.) to `mu_r`. Therefore we may choose deterministic integers `N_r`, recursively with

\[
N_r> N_{r-1},\qquad N_r\ge r^2,
\tag{3.10}
\]

so large that for `(N_r+1)^2` iid copies `H_{r,j}`,

\[
\Pr\!\left[
\frac1{(N_r+1)^2}
\sum_{j=1}^{(N_r+1)^2}(H_{r,j}\wedge b_r)<2r
\right]
\le 2^{-r}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Now use the `(N_r+1)^2` vertical columns as edge-disjoint flow paths. Sending `H_{r,j}` units along column `j` gives a feasible flow, so

\[
\Phi_{N_r,N_r,r}
\ge\sum_{j=1}^{(N_r+1)^2}H_{r,j}
\ge\sum_{j=1}^{(N_r+1)^2}(H_{r,j}\wedge b_r).
\tag{3.12}
\]

Equations (3.11)--(3.12) give

\[
\Pr\!\left[
\frac{\Phi_{N_r,N_r,r}}{N_r^2}<2r
\right]
\le2^{-r},
\]

up to the harmless factor `(N_r+1)^2/N_r^2>1`. The failure probabilities are summable. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma, which requires no independence between different `r`, yields

\[
\frac{\Phi_{N_r,N_r,r}}{N_r^2}\ge2r
\]

eventually almost surely, proving (3.5).

It remains to put these boxes on one legal height function. Let `s=min(k,l)` and define `m(k,l)=r` whenever

\[
N_r\le s<N_{r+1}.
\]

Then `m(k,l)->infinity`. Because `N_r>=r^2`, on the block where `m=r` we have `r<=sqrt(s)`, and for `k>=s`,

\[
0\le\frac{\log m(k,l)}{k^{1/2}}
\le\frac{\tfrac12\log s}{s^{1/2}}\to0.
\]

Thus this is a corrected sub-surface-exponential Kesten height function and contains the divergent diagonal subsequence. QED.

# 4. What this says about the weakest moment hypothesis

The theorem creates a clean three-level boundary.

### Level 1: finite mean — sufficient

P11 claim 04 gives the full arbitrary-aspect source LLN a.s. and in `L^1` under

\[
\mathbb E T<\infty.
\]

This is `r_*(G)=1`.

### Level 2: no finite-order minimum moment — impossible

If

\[
r_*(G)=\infty,
\]

then the source-universal theorem is false by Theorem 05. Moreover, part (A) says every finite box at height `h` has infinite expected maximal flow, so an inherited finite-constant `L^1` statement is impossible even before taking a limit.

### Level 3: the genuine unresolved infinite-mean gap

There are infinite-mean laws with `1<r_*(G)<infinity`. The simplest clean example is the Pareto-type law

\[
\Pr(T>t)=(1+t)^{-3/4},\qquad t\ge0.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb E T
=\int_0^\infty(1+t)^{-3/4}dt
=\infty,
\]

but

\[
\mathbb E\min(T_1,T_2)
=\int_0^\infty(1+t)^{-3/2}dt
=2<\infty.
\]

So `r_*(G)=2`. This law is not covered by the finite-mean theorem and is not killed by the vertical-column obstruction. It is now the simplest explicit special case separating “finite mean is sufficient” from “finite mean is necessary.”

This also explains why the earlier ultra-heavy witness worked: its tail was so heavy that no fixed number of series edges regularized the mean.

# 5. B22: reading the failures rather than adding another block lemma

The literature and the repository failures line up around one geometric issue: **height supplies alternatives that can regularize heavy capacities, but the source allows the height to grow arbitrarily slowly.**

- Rossignol--Theret 2018 can remove all moments in mild flat cylinders only after requiring `h/log p -> infinity`; this creates enough vertical room to bypass high-capacity clusters by bounded-capacity cutsets.
- Kesten's corrected source class requires only `m->infinity` plus an upper growth bound. It permits `m` far smaller than `log(base area)`, exactly where rare columns can pierce every low-cost horizontal surface.
- P11's infinite-mean witness exploits that slow-height freedom.
- P11's finite-mean proof pays for deterministic straight interface walls by ordinary integrable sums; this is robust to arbitrarily extreme base aspect but cannot directly survive `E T=infinity`.
- The exact finite-box criterion (3.2) shows that “infinite mean” itself is too crude: a height of two already regularizes many infinite-mean laws at the level of integrability.

The nearest solved analogue is the ordinary FPP time constant: Rossignol--Theret 2018 recall the classical exact condition

\[
\mathbb E\min(T_1,\ldots,T_{2d})<\infty
\]

as necessary and sufficient for a.s. and `L^1` point-to-point LLN. For maximal surfaces the corresponding sufficient condition is not established here. Treating the time-constant criterion as if it automatically transferred to surfaces would be an unjustified analogy.

The full source question is internally answered affirmatively by finite mean, but the “weakest exact moment” obligation remains separate. The new invariant `r_*(G)` lets the residual be split honestly:

1. **a.s. source-universal LLN:** `r_*(G)<infinity` is now proved necessary; finite mean (`r_*=1`) is proved sufficient. Sufficiency for `1<r_*<infinity` is OPEN.
2. **`L^1` source-universal LLN:** `r_*(G)<infinity` is also necessary, because `r_*=infinity` makes every finite-height flow nonintegrable. Finite mean is sufficient. Uniform integrability for `1<r_*<infinity` remains OPEN.
3. **fixed mild-flat geometry:** no moment at all can be sufficient by Rossignol--Theret 2018, so any sharp condition for the source must depend on the quantification over much slower heights, not on an intrinsic need for moments in every geometry.

A tempting but invalid shortcut is to use the minimum of finitely many horizontal-plane sums. For a heavy-tail law of index `alpha<1`, each plane sum still lives at scale much larger than its area average; taking finitely many whole-plane alternatives need not restore an area-order bound. A successful extension into `r_*>1` must allow the cut surface to **mix alternatives locally**, not merely choose one entire plane globally.
