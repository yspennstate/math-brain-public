## 7. B18 — recast extreme anisotropy as an offset family of one-dimensional strip problems

Define the bottom-slab depth

\[
D=D(s,m):=\min\{\lfloor\sqrt s\rfloor,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Along every source sequence, `D->infinity` and `D<=sqrt(s)`. We build a cut entirely in levels `0,...,D`; therefore no upper-height irregularity enters the extreme-aspect proof.

For a square `s x s` base and depth `D`, let `X_{s,D}` be the local bottom-to-top min-cut capacity using only edges of that induced local box.

### 7.1 Local mean tends to the flow constant under finite mean

Rossignol--Théret Theorem 3.16 gives the fixed-shape `L^1` LLN under finite mean for any deterministic height function `h(s)->infinity` satisfying `log h(s)/s^2->0`. It follows sequentially that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E X_{s,D}}{s^2}\to\nu_G(e_3)
\quad\text{whenever }s\to\infty,\ D\to\infty,\ D\le\sqrt s.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Indeed, if (7.2) failed, take an increasing bad subsequence `(s_j,D_j)` and define a single height function equal to `D_j` at `s_j` and `floor(sqrt s)` elsewhere. It still tends to infinity and satisfies the Rossignol--Théret height condition, contradicting their `L^1` theorem.

### 7.2 A local second moment from three independent planes

Finite mean alone does not give `E T^2<infinity`. The local min-cut nevertheless has a second moment of the correct scale.

Choose three distinct horizontal levels in the slab. Let `S_1,S_2,S_3` be the capacities of the three corresponding horizontal plane cuts. They use disjoint edge sets and are iid. Each plane has at most `C_0 s^2` edges, hence

\[
E S_i\le C_0\mu s^2=:M_s.
\]

Because

\[
X_{s,D}\le\min(S_1,S_2,S_3),
\]

Markov and independence give

\[
P(X_{s,D}>t)\le\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{M_s}{t}\right)^3\right\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
E X_{s,D}^2
=\int_0^\infty 2tP(X_{s,D}>t)\,dt
\le 3M_s^2
\le C_1\mu^2s^4.
\tag{7.3}
\]

This finite-variance gain comes from taking a minimum of three independent cut candidates; it does not assume a second moment of `T`.

### 7.3 Offset partitions and the deterministic gluing cut

For each offset

\[
a\in\{0,1,\ldots,s-1\},
\]

put a long-direction interface at every internal coordinate `y` satisfying

\[
y\equiv a\pmod s.
\]

These interfaces partition the `s x L` base into strips of width at most `s`; every interior strip has width exactly `s`, and there are at most two boundary partial strips.

Inside each strip choose a local bottom-to-top minimum cut in the bottom slab. Add every horizontal edge crossing an interface at each slab level `0,...,D`. Call these crossing edges the **walls**.

The union of all local cuts and walls is a global bottom-to-top cut. Any path through the bottom slab either crosses a wall, or stays inside one strip from slab bottom to slab top and therefore hits that strip's local cut. This is a deterministic graph-theoretic statement; the finite exact checker below exhaustively verifies small instances but is not the proof of the asymptotic theorem.

## 8. Uniform control of full strip-block costs

For fixed `(s,D,a)`, enumerate the width-`s` full strips along the long direction. Their local cut costs `X_1,X_2,...` are iid copies of `X_{s,D}`, because the induced strip edge sets are disjoint. By (7.3),

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X_j)\le C_1\mu^2s^4.
\]

Put `Q_s=floor(s^6/2)`. Kolmogorov's maximal inequality on dyadic ranges gives, for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
P\left(
\sup_{n\ge Q_s}
\frac{|\sum_{j=1}^n(X_j-EX_{s,D})|}{n}>\epsilon s^2
\right)
\le \frac{C_2\mu^2}{\epsilon^2Q_s}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For completeness, on `2^rQ_s<=n<2^{r+1}Q_s`, crossing the threshold forces

\[
\max_{j\le2^{r+1}Q_s}|S_j|
>\epsilon s^2 2^rQ_s,
\]

and Kolmogorov bounds this by `C/(epsilon^2 2^rQ_s)`; summing over `r>=0` yields (8.1).

There are `s` offsets and at most `sqrt(s)` possible integer slab depths. Thus the union bound over all `(a,D)` is

\[
O\left(\frac{s^{3/2}}{s^6}\right)=O(s^{-9/2}),
\]

which is summable. Borel--Cantelli gives: almost surely, for every rational `epsilon>0`, eventually in `s`, simultaneously for every offset, every allowed `D`, and every `n>=Q_s`,

\[
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nX_j
\le EX_{s,D}+\epsilon s^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Under `L>=s^7`, the number of full strips is at least `s^6-2`, hence eventually exceeds `Q_s`.

## 9. Uniform control of the two partial boundary strips

Let `R_{s,D,a,L}` be the sum of the local cut costs in the at most two partial strips. Their total base area is less than `2s^2`. Choosing the same three horizontal levels as before gives

\[
E R_{s,D,a,L}^2\le C_3\mu^2s^4.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Hence

\[
P(R_{s,D,a,L}>\epsilon sL)
\le \frac{C_3\mu^2s^2}{\epsilon^2L^2}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Summing (9.2) over all integers `L>=s^7`, then over the `s` offsets and at most `sqrt(s)` depths, gives `O(s^{-7/2})`, again summable. Therefore almost surely, eventually and uniformly over every extreme-aspect `L`, every offset, and every allowed `D`,

\[
R_{s,D,a,L}\le\epsilon sL.
\tag{9.3}
\]

## 10. B11 — dualize wall control to a weak-(1,1) maximal inequality, then exploit offset independence

This is the step that removes the previous `L log L` requirement.

For fixed `(s,D,a)`, list the wall batches in increasing long-coordinate order. Let `Y_{a,j}` be the total capacity of the `j`th interface wall through the slab. The sequence `(Y_{a,j})_{j>=1}` is iid and nonnegative, and

\[
E Y_{a,1}\le C_4\mu s(D+1).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Define the bad event

\[
A_{s,D,a}(\epsilon)
:=\left\{
\sup_{n\ge1}\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nY_{a,j}>\epsilon s^2
\right\}.
\]

The classical maximal ergodic theorem, applied to the iid shift and the nonnegative integrable coordinate function, gives the weak-`(1,1)` bound

\[
P(A_{s,D,a}(\epsilon))
\le\frac{E Y_{a,1}}{\epsilon s^2}
\le\frac{C_5\mu D}{\epsilon s}
\tag{10.2}
\]

for all large `s` (absorbing the harmless `+1` in the constant).

The crucial geometric fact is that distinct offsets use disjoint sets of long-direction interface edges. Therefore the events

\[
A_{s,D,0},\ldots,A_{s,D,s-1}
\]

are independent. The probability that **every** offset is bad is consequently

\[
P\left(\bigcap_{a=0}^{s-1}A_{s,D,a}(\epsilon)\right)
\le
\left(\frac{C_5\mu D}{\epsilon s}\right)^s.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Since `D<=sqrt(s)`, for all sufficiently large `s` the right side is at most `(C/(epsilon sqrt(s)))^s`. Summing this over `D<=sqrt(s)` and `s` is finite. Borel--Cantelli yields:

> almost surely, for every rational `epsilon>0`, eventually for every `(s,D)` there exists at least one offset `a=a(s,D,omega)` for which **every wall prefix** satisfies
> \[
> \sum_{j=1}^nY_{a,j}\le\epsilon s^2n\qquad\forall n\ge1.
> \tag{10.4}
> \]

The offset is allowed to depend on the environment: we are constructing one admissible cut after seeing the capacities. This is exactly the flexibility that the raw two-parameter rectangular strong law ignored in the preceding `L log L` proof.

## 11. Finish the extreme-aspect upper bound

Work on the probability-one event on which (8.2), (9.3), and (10.4) eventually hold for a countable sequence of rational `epsilon>0`. For an extreme box, choose the wall-good offset from (10.4). The full-block and partial-strip estimates hold for **every** offset, so they also hold for this environment-dependent choice.

Let `q` be the number of full width-`s` strips. Then `q<=L/s+1`. The full-block contribution obeys

\[
\sum_{j=1}^qX_j
\le q(EX_{s,D}+\epsilon s^2).
\]

After division by `sL`, this is at most

\[
\frac{EX_{s,D}}{s^2}+\epsilon+o(1).
\tag{11.1}
\]

The number of walls is at most `L/s+1`, so (10.4) gives normalized wall cost at most `epsilon+o(1)`. Equation (9.3) gives normalized partial-strip cost at most `epsilon`.

By the deterministic gluing lemma, the union is a global cut, hence max-flow/min-cut gives

\[
\limsup_{\substack{s,L,m\to\infty\\L\ge s^7}}
\frac{\Phi(s,L,m)}{sL}
\le
\limsup\frac{EX_{s,D}}{s^2}+3\epsilon
=\nu_G(e_3)+3\epsilon
\tag{11.2}
\]

using (7.2). Letting rational `epsilon downarrow0` proves the upper bracket in the extreme branch. Together with Section 6, this proves (4.3) on the entire corrected source geometry.

Combining with the inherited lower bracket (4.4) proves the a.s. limit (4.2).

## 12. `L^1` convergence is proved separately

Do not infer `L^1` convergence from the a.s. theorem. A fixed horizontal plane is always a cut, so after the harmless lattice-boundary factor,

\[
0\le\frac{\Phi(s,L,m)}{sL}
\le \frac1{N_{s,L}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{s,L}}T_j\,(1+o(1)),
\qquad N_{s,L}\asymp sL.
\tag{12.1}
\]

Because `ET<infinity`, de la Vallée Poussin gives a convex superlinear function `Psi` with `E Psi(T)<infinity`. Jensen yields

\[
E\Psi\left(\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^NT_j\right)
\le E\Psi(T)
\tag{12.2}
\]

uniformly in `N`. Thus the plane averages, and therefore the normalized flows, are uniformly integrable. The a.s. convergence plus uniform integrability gives convergence in `L^1`.

This establishes the same convergence modes as the source theorem under finite first moment rather than finite exponential moment.
