### 4.1 Exact capacity law

Let

\[
\Pr(T=1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\Pr(T=2^{s^3})=2^{-s-1},\quad s=1,2,\ldots .
\tag{4.1}
\]

The masses sum to one. The law is nonnegative, finite a.s., has `F(0)=0`, and has no atom at `+infinity`. For every `gamma>0`,

\[
E e^{\gamma T}\ge 2^{-s-1}e^{\gamma 2^{s^3}}
\]

for each `s`, and the right-hand side does not tend to zero as `s->infinity`, so no positive exponential moment exists. In fact `ET=infinity`, which will matter only for scope bookkeeping below.

Define

\[
a_r=2^{r^3}.
\]

Then the exact tail is

\[
q_r:=P(T\ge a_r)=\sum_{s\ge r}2^{-s-1}=2^{-r}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The already archived exact checker for claim02 verifies the distribution/tail identities and the finite algebraic core with exact arithmetic; its output is PASS. The asymptotic probability argument is proved here and in the original claim02 report, not inferred from finite testing.

### 4.2 Corrected-Kesten-admissible geometry

For `k>=l`, set

\[
m(k,l)=\max\left\{1,\left\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2 l}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then `m(k,l)->infinity` as `k>=l->infinity`. Moreover for, say, `delta=1/2`,

\[
k^{-1/2}\log m(k,l)\to0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the witness lies safely inside the corrected Kesten sub-surface-exponential geometry and does not exploit the ADH transcription error.

Take

\[
N_r=2^{r^2},\qquad k=l=N_r.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then `m(N_r,N_r)=r` exactly.

### 4.3 Independent high columns and Borel--Cantelli

The box `[0,N_r]^2 x [0,r]` contains

\[
M_r=(N_r+1)^2
\]

pairwise edge-disjoint vertical columns, each consisting of exactly `r` vertical edges.

Call a column **r-high** if every one of its `r` vertical edges has capacity at least `a_r`. Its probability is

\[
p_r=q_r^r=(2^{-r})^r=2^{-r^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because distinct columns have disjoint edge sets, these indicators are independent. If `Y_r` is their number, then

\[
Y_r\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(M_r,2^{-r^2}),
\qquad
\mu_r:=EY_r=M_r2^{-r^2}\ge 2^{r^2}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Chebyshev gives

\[
P\left(Y_r<\frac{\mu_r}{2}\right)
\le \frac{4\operatorname{Var}Y_r}{\mu_r^2}
\le \frac4{\mu_r}
\le 4\,2^{-r^2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The right side is summable. By the first Borel--Cantelli lemma,

\[
Y_r\ge\frac{\mu_r}{2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `r`, almost surely.

### 4.4 First derivation: explicit primal flow

On each r-high column send `a_r` units of flow vertically upward, and send zero on every other edge. The high columns are edge-disjoint, every used edge has capacity at least `a_r`, and flow is conserved at every interior column vertex. Hence this is an admissible bottom-to-top flow, so

\[
\Phi_r\ge a_rY_r.
\tag{4.9}
\]

On the probability-one eventual event from (4.8),

\[
\frac{\Phi_r}{N_r^2}
\ge
\frac{a_r\mu_r}{2N_r^2}
\ge
2^{r^3-r^2-1}
\longrightarrow +\infty.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Therefore the normalized maximal flow cannot converge to the finite positive constant in (7.2).

This is already a source-exact counterexample.

## 5. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: the independent dual/min-cut derivation

The previous claim02 proof used an explicit admissible flow. The source itself foregrounds max-flow/min-cut, so I rederived the decisive lower bound without using the primal flow construction.

Fix a realization and an `r`. Let `E` be **any** edge cut separating the bottom face from the top face of the box. Every vertical column is itself a bottom-to-top path. Therefore `E` must intersect every vertical column. In particular, for each of the `Y_r` r-high columns, choose one edge

\[
e_C\in E\cap C.

Different vertical columns are edge-disjoint, so the selected `e_C` are distinct. Every selected edge belongs to an r-high column, hence

\[
T(e_C)\ge a_r.
\]

Consequently every cut satisfies

\[
\sum_{e\in E}T(e)
\ge
\sum_{C\ \mathrm{r\text{-}high}}T(e_C)
\ge a_rY_r.
\tag{5.1}

Taking the minimum over cuts and invoking the source's Theorem 7.1 gives

\[
\Phi_r
=\min_E\sum_{e\in E}T(e)
\ge a_rY_r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is exactly the same deterministic lower bound (4.9), obtained from the dual side. Combining it with the same binomial/Borel--Cantelli estimate gives (4.10) again.
