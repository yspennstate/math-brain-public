## 5. B7 — take the limit: the endpoint amplitude index differs from `r_*` by at most one

Let `T` be a nonnegative, almost-surely finite capacity and

`bar F(t)=P(T>t)`.

For integer `h>=1`, define

\[
M_h=\min(T_1,\ldots,T_h),
\qquad
A_h=\sup_{t\ge1} t\,\bar F(t)^h,
\]

and introduce the **amplitude index**

\[
s_*(G)=\inf\{h\ge1:A_h<\infty\},
\]

with the convention `s_*=infinity` if no finite `h` works.

Claim09 already proved

\[
r_*(G)<\infty\iff s_*(G)<\infty.
\]

The endpoint question is whether these two integer indices agree exactly.

### Proposition — exact one-step endpoint bound

If `s_*(G)<infinity`, then

\[
\boxed{s_*(G)\le r_*(G)\le s_*(G)+1.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Both equality patterns occur.

#### Proof

If `r_*=r<infinity`, then

\[
E M_r=\int_0^\infty \bar F(t)^r\,dt<\infty.
\]

Claim09's monotonicity argument gives

\[
t\bar F(t)^r\to0,
\]

hence `A_r<infinity`; therefore `s_*<=r_*`.

Conversely suppose `s_*=s<infinity`, so

\[
\bar F(t)^s\le A_s/t
\qquad(t\ge1).
\]

Then

\[
\bar F(t)^{s+1}
=\bigl(\bar F(t)^s\bigr)^{1+1/s}
\le A_s^{1+1/s}t^{-1-1/s},
\]

which is integrable on `[1,infinity)`. Therefore

\[
E M_{s+1}<\infty
\]

and `r_*<=s+1`. This proves (5.1). QED.

The significance is conceptual: the supremum functional `A_h` detects the correct finite/infinite phase exactly, but at the integrability endpoint it can turn finite one independent alternative before the actual mean becomes integrable.

## 6. B26 — hunt the sporadic case: exact power-log and power-log-log endpoints

The one-step gap in (5.1) is not an artefact. It is attained by standard borderline tails and can be completely classified.

Fix an integer `h>=1`. Suppose for all sufficiently large `t`, up to positive multiplicative constants,

\[
\bar F(t)\asymp t^{-1/h}(\log t)^{-\beta}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `j<h`,

\[
t\bar F(t)^j
\asymp t^{1-j/h}(\log t)^{-\beta j}\to\infty,
\]

so `A_j=infinity`; also `E M_j=infinity`.

At the critical power `j=h`,

\[
t\bar F(t)^h\asymp(\log t)^{-\beta h}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore:

- if `beta>=0`, then `A_h<infinity`;
- if `beta<0`, then `A_h=infinity`.

Meanwhile

\[
E M_h<\infty
\iff
\int^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t(\log t)^{\beta h}}<\infty
\iff \beta h>1.
\tag{6.3}
\]

At `j=h+1`, the extra polynomial factor makes both `A_{h+1}` and `E M_{h+1}` finite for every fixed real `beta`.

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
\beta>1/h:& s_*=r_*=h,\\
0\le\beta\le1/h:& s_*=h,\quad r_*=h+1,\\
\beta<0:& s_*=r_*=h+1.
\end{array}}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the upper one-step gap in (5.1) is sharp.

The final logarithmic endpoint also behaves exactly as expected. If

\[
\bar F(t)\asymp t^{-1/h}(\log t)^{-\beta}(\log\log t)^{-\gamma},
\tag{6.5}
\]

then at `j=h`,

\[
E M_h<\infty
\iff
\beta h>1
\quad\text{or}\quad
\bigl[\beta h=1\text{ and }\gamma h>1\bigr].
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the standard iterated-log integral test. For `j<h` divergence is forced by the residual positive power of `t`; for `j>h` convergence is forced by a negative power strictly below `-1`. Therefore no hidden sporadic endpoint escapes the finite-alternative classification.

## 7. A cleaner equivalent description of finite `r_*`: existence of a polynomial tail envelope

The endpoint analysis yields a useful restatement of claim09.

### Corollary — polynomial-envelope equivalence

For an almost-surely finite nonnegative capacity law,

\[
\boxed{
r_*(G)<\infty
\iff
\exists\alpha>0,\ C<\infty,\ t_0<\infty:
\bar F(t)\le C t^{-\alpha}\quad(t\ge t_0).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

#### Proof

If `r_*<infinity`, choose `r` with `E M_r<infinity`. Then

\[
t\bar F(t)^r\to0.
\]

For all sufficiently large `t`, `t\bar F(t)^r<=1`, so

\[
\bar F(t)\le t^{-1/r}.
\]

This gives (7.1) with `alpha=1/r`.

Conversely suppose

\[
\bar F(t)\le C t^{-\alpha}
\]

for large `t`. Choose an integer `h` with `alpha h>1`. Then

\[
\int^\infty \bar F(t)^h\,dt
\le C^h\int^\infty t^{-\alpha h}\,dt<\infty,
\]

so `E M_h<infinity` and `r_*<infinity`. QED.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
r_*(G)=\infty
\iff
\text{the survival tail admits no eventual polynomial upper envelope of any positive exponent.}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is stronger and clearer than saying “infinite mean.” Infinite-mean Pareto laws can have finite `r_*` and are covered by repaired 07R; the universal high-column obstruction occurs on the genuinely heavier no-polynomial-envelope side.

This corollary is a structural repackaging of claim09, not frozen-metric movement and not an additional source closure claim.
