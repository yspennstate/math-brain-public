## 5. B23 — exact heavy-tail witness

### Claim `P11-I0062-HEAVYTAIL-VERTICAL-COLUMNS-02` — PROVED

There exists an iid nonnegative capacity law `G` satisfying `G(0)=0` and no finite exponential moment, together with a corrected Kesten-admissible straight-cylinder height function, such that the normalized maximal flow has infinite limsup almost surely. Consequently the unrestricted no-moment upper bracket

\[
\limsup \frac{\Phi}{kl}\le \nu_G
\]

is false.

### 5.1 The capacity law

Set

\[
\Pr(T=1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\Pr(T=2^{s^3})=2^{-s-1},\quad s=1,2,\ldots.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a probability law because

\[
\frac12+\sum_{s\ge1}2^{-s-1}=1.
\]

It has `G(0)=0` and no atom at `+infinity`. For every `gamma>0`,

\[
E e^{\gamma T}
\ge 2^{-s-1}e^{\gamma 2^{s^3}}
\]

for each `s`, and the displayed term does not tend to zero. Hence every positive exponential moment is infinite. In fact

\[
ET\ge 2^{s^3-s-1}
\]

for every `s`, so `ET=+infinity`.

For

\[
a_r:=2^{r^3},
\]

the exact tail is

\[
q_r:=\Pr(T\ge a_r)
=\sum_{s\ge r}2^{-s-1}
=2^{-r}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### 5.2 A legal slow-height straight-cylinder sequence

Define, for large `k>=l`,

\[
m(k,l):=\max\left\{1,\left\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2 l}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then `m(k,l)->infinity` as `k>=l->infinity`. Moreover, for every fixed `delta in (0,1)`,

\[
\frac{\log m(k,l)}{k^\delta}\to0,
\]

and likewise `k^{-1+delta} log m(k,l)->0` for the corresponding corrected Kesten form. Thus this height is safely sub-surface-exponential and also obeys the qualitative upper-growth direction in Zhang's theorem.

Take the diagonal subsequence

\[
N_r:=2^{r^2},
\qquad k=l=N_r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then (5.3) gives exactly `m(N_r,N_r)=r`.

### 5.3 Many independent high-capacity vertical columns

In the box

\[
[0,N_r]\times[0,N_r]\times[0,r]
\]

there are

\[
M_r=(N_r+1)^2
\]

vertical columns joining bottom to top. These columns are edge-disjoint, and each contains exactly `r` vertical edges.

Call a column **r-high** if all of its `r` vertical edges have capacity at least `a_r`. By (5.2), a fixed column is r-high with probability

\[
p_r=q_r^r=(2^{-r})^r=2^{-r^2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because distinct vertical columns use disjoint edge sets, their high-column indicators are independent. If `Y_r` is the number of r-high columns, then

\[
Y_r\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(M_r,2^{-r^2})
\]

and

\[
\mu_r:=EY_r=M_r2^{-r^2}\ge N_r^2 2^{-r^2}=2^{r^2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Chebyshev's inequality is already enough along this subsequence:

\[
\Pr\left(Y_r<\frac{\mu_r}{2}\right)
\le \frac{4\operatorname{Var}(Y_r)}{\mu_r^2}
\le \frac4{\mu_r}
\le 4\,2^{-r^2}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The right-hand side is summable. The first Borel--Cantelli lemma, which requires no independence between different values of `r`, gives

\[
Y_r\ge\frac{\mu_r}{2}
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }r
\]

almost surely.

### 5.4 Explicit admissible flow and divergence

On every r-high vertical column, send flow `a_r` straight upward and send zero flow on every other edge. Since the selected columns are edge-disjoint, every used edge has capacity at least `a_r`; at each interior vertex of a selected column, inflow equals outflow. This is therefore a valid bottom-to-top flow in the sense of the source definition.

Consequently

\[
\Phi_r\ge a_rY_r.
\tag{5.8}
\]

On the probability-one eventual event from (5.7),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Phi_r}{N_r^2}
&\ge \frac{a_r\mu_r}{2N_r^2}\\
&=\frac{2^{r^3}}2\,
\frac{(N_r+1)^2}{N_r^2}\,2^{-r^2}\\
&\ge 2^{r^3-r^2-1}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{k,l\to\infty}
\frac{\Phi([0,k]\times[0,l]\times[0,m(k,l)])}{kl}
=+\infty
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

because the diagonal subsequence (5.4) already diverges.

Rossignol--Theret's no-moment construction gives a finite deterministic flow constant `nu_G(v)` whenever `G({+infinity})<p_c(d)`. Here `G({+infinity})=0`. Therefore (5.10) in particular disproves

\[
\limsup \Phi/(kl)\le\nu_G
\]

for unrestricted no-moment straight cylinders. QED.

## 6. Why the mechanism is structural

For height `h`, a threshold `a`, and tail `q(a)=P(T>=a)`, a vertical column is all-at-least-`a` with probability `q(a)^h`. When the base has order `A` disjoint columns, the expected number of such columns is order `A q(a)^h`, and the normalized flow contributed by them is heuristically order

\[
a\,q(a)^h.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The witness forces this quantity to explode along `h=r` by taking

\[
a_r=2^{r^3},\qquad q(a_r)=2^{-r},
\]

so

\[
a_rq(a_r)^r=2^{r^3-r^2}\to\infty.
\]

This identifies a genuine tail--height obstruction: in very slowly growing cylinders, rare gigantic capacities can create many parallel high-capacity channels. A proof architecture that only controls minimal-cut cardinality cannot suppress this phenomenon without additional tail information or a stronger height regime.

This also explains why Rossignol--Theret's later no-moment theorem imposes a *mild flat-cylinder* height condition rather than asserting the result for all arbitrarily slow heights: their geometry and repair mechanism are doing real work, not merely cosmetic bookkeeping.

## 7. B5 — truncation excess: what survives and what fails

The inherited lower bracket works because monotone truncation points in the correct direction:

\[
\Phi^{(K)}\le\Phi.
\]

For the missing upper bracket, the obvious attempt is: take a near-minimal cut `E_K` for the truncated environment and write

\[
\sum_{e\in E_K}T_e
=\sum_{e\in E_K}(T_e\wedge K)
+\sum_{e\in E_K}(T_e-K)_+.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The second sum is dependent on the chosen truncated cut and need not be negligible under arbitrary heavy tails. The witness in Section 5 shows that no source-wide no-moment estimate can rescue (7.1) in the full corrected geometry: the desired upper bracket itself is false.

The important positive comparison is Rossignol--Theret's Proposition 3.5. Rather than paying raw excess on a `G^K`-minimal cut, they remove edges with huge capacities and replace them by local well-behaved cutsets. Combined with cutset-cardinality control this yields a no-moment result in their **mild flat-cylinder** regime. That is the correct B5 architecture, but its geometry does not cover the present slow-height witness.

Outcome of B5: **the naive truncation-excess route is structurally impossible for the unrestricted source scope; local cut replacement remains the right architecture for weaker-moment/stronger-height variants.**

## 8. B21 — minimal-cutset machinery is necessary but not sufficient

Rossignol--Theret's 2010 maximal-flow paper records Zhang's exponential tail for the cardinality of minimal cutsets under the appropriate zero-mass condition, including the top-to-bottom flow analogue. Such a bound is exactly what one wants to control the number of locations at which a truncation repair must be performed.

However, cardinality alone gives no bound on the magnitudes of selected untruncated capacities. Section 5 exhibits a distribution where rare enormous capacities dominate a large collection of parallel columns despite entirely ordinary cut cardinalities. Rossignol--Theret's later no-moment proof therefore combines cardinality with *local replacement* and a height regime giving summable failure probabilities.

Outcome of B21: **cutset size is a component, not the missing theorem.** A source-matching weaker-moment proof needs a tail-aware replacement estimate uniform over the anisotropic Kesten/Zhang geometry.
