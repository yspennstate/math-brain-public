# 7. Explicit Bloch-phase Lipschitz constant

The usual finite-cell Floquet matrix puts the whole phase `e^{i beta_j}` on the wrap-around edge. That convention gives a correct matrix but a poor derivative constant.

Conjugate instead by the diagonal unitary on the cell

\[
(U_\beta\psi)_{n_1,n_2}
=
\exp\left(
-i\frac{n_1\beta_1}{P_1}
-i\frac{n_2\beta_2}{P_2}
\right)
\psi_{n_1,n_2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This distributes the quasi-momentum uniformly: every forward step in direction `j` acquires the same factor

\[
e^{i\beta_j/P_j}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The pre-existing magnetic Peierls phase is untouched except for this harmless scalar step factor.

Let `T_j` denote the forward weighted magnetic shift in direction `j`. By `(3.8)`,

\[
\|T_j\|\le A.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|&(e^{i\beta_j/P_j}-e^{i\beta_j'/P_j})T_j
+(e^{-i\beta_j/P_j}-e^{-i\beta_j'/P_j})T_j^*\|\\
&\le
2A
\left|e^{i\beta_j/P_j}-e^{i\beta_j'/P_j}\right|\\
&\le
\frac{2A}{P_j}|\delta\beta_j|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Summing `j=1,2` proves the Bloch part of `(M41.2)`.

This distributed-gauge step is important: the interpolation cost scales as `1/P_j`, not as an order-one boundary-twist constant.
