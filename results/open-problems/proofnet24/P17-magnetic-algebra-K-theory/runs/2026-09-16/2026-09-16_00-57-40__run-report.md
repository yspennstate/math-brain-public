# 4. Proof of `P17-M40-LANDAU-GAUGE-MAGNETIC-HOLDER-36`

The main difficulty is that different magnetic fluxes do not give a useful global same-gauge norm estimate: in Landau gauge the phase difference contains `(b-b')n_1` and is not uniformly small over the infinite lattice. The correct cure is to localize first and **then recenter the gauge at the localization center**.

## 4.1 Landau-gauge regular representation

Use magnetic translations on `ell^2(Z^2)`

\[
(T_1\psi)(n_1,n_2)=\psi(n_1-1,n_2),
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
(T_{2,b}\psi)(n_1,n_2)
=e^{-2\pi i b n_1}\psi(n_1,n_2-1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
T_1T_{2,b}=e^{2\pi ib}T_{2,b}T_1.
\]

This is cohomologous to the symmetric cocycle convention in the crossed product, hence gives the same `C^*`-spectrum.

For each hull point `omega`, the regular Hamiltonian has the form

\[
H_{b,\omega}
=
M_{a_1^+}T_1+M_{a_1^-}T_1^*
+M_{a_2^+}T_{2,b}+M_{a_2^-}T_{2,b}^*,
\tag{4.3}
\]

where all four multiplication coefficients have norm at most `A` from `(2.5)`. Their exact covariance placement is immaterial for the estimates because the same coefficients occur at `b` and `b'`.

The family of regular representations is faithful for the amenable twisted transformation groupoid, so the bulk `C^*`-spectrum is the closure of the union of the fiber spectra. Every estimate below is uniform in `omega`.

## 4.2 Strip localization

Fix

\[
\lambda\in\sigma(H_{b,\omega}).
\]

For arbitrary `epsilon>0`, choose a finitely supported unit vector `psi` with

\[
\|(H_{b,\omega}-\lambda)\psi\|<\epsilon.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Use the same compact cosine window as M33:

\[
\chi(x)=
\begin{cases}
\cos(\pi x/2),&|x|\le1,\\
0,&|x|>1.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\chi(x)^2dx=1,
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi'(x)|^2dx=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For real strip center `s` and width `L>0`, define

\[
\chi_{s,L}(n_1,n_2)
=L^{-1/2}\chi((n_1-s)/L).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Fubini gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\|\chi_{s,L}\psi\|^2ds=1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Because the cutoff depends only on `n_1`, it commutes with both `y`-direction magnetic hopping terms. Only the two `x`-direction nearest-neighbor terms contribute to the commutator. Exactly as in the one-dimensional M33 localization estimate,

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
\|[H_{b,\omega},\chi_{s,L}]\psi\|^2ds
\le
\frac{\pi^2A^2}{L^2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Indeed, each discrete first difference of the window has integrated square at most `pi^2/(4L^2)`, the two hopping terms are each bounded by `A`, and `|x+y|^2<=2|x|^2+2|y|^2` gives `(4.8)`.

Combining `(4.4)`, `(4.7)`, `(4.8)` by Minkowski in `L^2(ds;ell^2)` shows that for some `s`, after normalizing

\[
\varphi
=
\frac{\chi_{s,L}\psi}{\|\chi_{s,L}\psi\|},
\]

we have

\[
\|(H_{b,\omega}-\lambda)\varphi\|
\le
\epsilon+\frac{\pi A}{L},
\tag{4.9}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{supp}\varphi
\subset\{|n_1-s|\le L\}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

## 4.3 Gauge recentering

Put

\[
\delta=b-b'.
\]

Define the unitary

\[
(G_s\xi)(n_1,n_2)
=e^{-2\pi i\delta s n_2}\xi(n_1,n_2).
\tag{4.11}
\]

It commutes with multiplication coefficients and with the `x` translation `T_1`. For the `y` translation,

\[
G_sT_{2,b'}G_s^*
=e^{-2\pi i(b'n_1+\delta s)}S_2,
\]

where `S_2` is the ordinary unit shift in the second coordinate. Comparing with

\[
T_{2,b}=e^{-2\pi ibn_1}S_2
\]

gives the residual phase

\[
e^{-2\pi i\delta(n_1-s)}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

On the support `(4.10)`,

\[
|e^{-2\pi i\delta(n_1-s)}-1|
\le2\pi|\delta|L.
\tag{4.13}
\]

There are two `y` hopping directions, each with coefficient norm at most `A`. Hence

\[
\|(H_{b,\omega}-G_sH_{b',\omega}G_s^*)\varphi\|
\le
4\pi A L|\delta|.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Combining `(4.9)` and `(4.14)`,

\[
\|(G_sH_{b',\omega}G_s^*-\lambda)\varphi\|
\le
\epsilon
+\frac{\pi A}{L}
+4\pi A L|b-b'|.
\tag{4.15}
\]

For a bounded self-adjoint operator, an approximate eigenvector gives an upper bound on distance to the spectrum. Letting `epsilon` tend to zero,

\[
\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,\sigma(H_{b',\omega}))
\le
\frac{\pi A}{L}+4\pi A L|b-b'|.
\tag{4.16}
\]

The estimate is uniform in `omega`; pass to the faithful family spectrum. Swapping `b,b'` gives the Hausdorff estimate

\[

\tag{4.17}
\]

For `b!=b'`, optimize at

\[

to obtain

\[
\boxed{d_H(\Sigma_b,\Sigma_{b'})\le4\pi A\sqrt{|b-b'|}.}
\tag{4.18}
\]

For `b=b'` the assertion is trivial. Since normalized magnetic flux is periodic modulo integers, one may use the nearest real lift of `b-b'`; the present application stays in a tiny neighborhood of zero.
