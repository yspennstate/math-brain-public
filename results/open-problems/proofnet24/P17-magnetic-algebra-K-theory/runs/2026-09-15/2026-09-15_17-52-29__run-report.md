# 4. Proof of `P17-M33-SOURCE-JACOBI-FREQUENCY-CONTINUITY-29`

Fix \(\lambda\in\Sigma(\theta)\). It suffices to treat \(\lambda\in\sigma(H_{\theta,\omega})\) and close the union afterward. Choose a finite-support unit Weyl vector \(\psi\) with

\[
\|(H_{\theta,\omega}-\lambda)\psi\|<\varepsilon.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Such vectors exist because finitely supported vectors are dense and the Jacobi operator is bounded.

Let

\[
\chi(x)=
\begin{cases}
\cos(\pi x/2),&|x|\le1,\\
0,&|x|>1.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\chi(x)^2dx=1,
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb R}|\chi'(x)|^2dx=\frac{\pi^2}{4}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a real center \(s\), put

\[
\chi_{s,L}(n)=L^{-1/2}\chi((n-s)/L).
\]

Fubini gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\|\chi_{s,L}\psi\|^2ds=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For every fixed integer \(n\), the translation-energy estimate gives

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
|\chi_{s,L}(n+1)-\chi_{s,L}(n)|^2ds
\le
\frac1{L^2}\int|\chi'|^2
=
\frac{\pi^2}{4L^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For the nearest-neighbor Jacobi operator, using \(|a_n|\le A\), reindexing the two neighboring-edge terms, and applying \(|x+y|^2\le2|x|^2+2|y|^2\),

\[
\int\|[H_{\theta,\omega},\chi_{s,L}]\psi\|^2ds
\le
\frac{\pi^2A^2}{L^2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Minkowski in \(L^2(ds;\ell^2)\), `(4.1)`, `(4.3)` and `(4.5)` imply that for some center \(s\), after normalizing

\[
\varphi=\frac{\chi_{s,L}\psi}{\|\chi_{s,L}\psi\|},
\]

we have

\[
\|(H_{\theta,\omega}-\lambda)\varphi\|
\le
\varepsilon+\frac{\pi A}{L}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Now put \(\Delta=\theta'-\theta\) and recenter the comparison phase by

\[
\omega'=\omega-s\Delta.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Writing

\[
f(x,\vartheta)
=e^{-1-r(\sin2\pi(x+\vartheta)-\sin2\pi x)},
\]

we have uniformly

\[
|\partial_xf|\le4\pi rA,
\qquad
|\partial_\vartheta f|\le2\pi rA.
\tag{4.8}
\]

At site \(n\), the recentering makes the base-phase displacement exactly \((n-s)\Delta\). Every edge coefficient acting on the support of \(\varphi\) has \(|n-s|\le L+1\). Hence the mean-value theorem gives

\[
|a_{\theta',\omega'}(n)-a_{\theta,\omega}(n)|
\le
2\pi rA(2L+3)|\Delta|.
\tag{4.9}
\]

A Jacobi matrix whose edge coefficients differ by at most \(c\) has operator norm difference at most \(2c\) on vectors supported on the relevant interval. Therefore

\[
\|(H_{\theta',\omega'}-H_{\theta,\omega})\varphi\|
\le
4\pi rA(2L+3)|\Delta|.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Combining `(4.6)` and `(4.10)`,

\[
\|(H_{\theta',\omega'}-\lambda)\varphi\|
\le
\varepsilon+rac{\pi A}{L}
+4\pi rA(2L+3)|\Delta|.
\]

For a self-adjoint operator, distance from \(\lambda\) to the spectrum is at most the residual norm of any unit vector, so after \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\),

\[
\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,\Sigma(\theta'))
\le
\frac{\pi A}{L}
+4\pi rA(2L+3)|\theta-\theta'|.
\]

Interchanging \(\theta\) and \(\theta'\) proves the Hausdorff estimate `(M33.1)`.

QED.

### Scope check

This is a spectrum-of-family theorem, not a same-phase operator-norm estimate. The phase recentering `(4.7)` is essential; without localization and recentering, close frequencies do not produce a small global same-phase operator-norm difference on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\).

d_H(\sigma(h_{\rm NN}(\theta_1,\theta_2)),

\tag{5.2}

For every point on the straight path from \((\theta_1',\theta_2')\) to the source pair, the coordinatewise displacement from the rational base is no larger than at the endpoint. Thus the same endpoint budget controls the whole path.
