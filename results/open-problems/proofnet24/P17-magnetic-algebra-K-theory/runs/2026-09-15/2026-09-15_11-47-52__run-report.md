# 3. General coarse-properness lemma

## `P17-M26-COARSE-PROPER-EXPONENTIAL-18`

Assume a continuous canonically labeled family
\[

\[
|p_{n+q}(\omega)-p_n(\omega)|\ge c\|q\|_1-C
\]
uniformly in \(n,q,\omega\).

Define the source exponential coefficient
\[
a_q(\omega)=e^{-\beta|p_q(\omega)-p_0(\omega)|}.
\]
Continuity follows from the source generating algorithm: for fixed \(q\), the displacement is a finite sum of continuous generating functions \(\Gamma_e\circ\tau_n\).

From coarse properness,
\[
|a_q(\omega)|
\le
\exp[-\beta(c\|q\|_1-C)]
=
e^{\beta C}e^{-\beta c\|q\|_1}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\omega\) gives (M26.2).

For any \(N\),
\[
\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z^d}(1+\|q\|_1)^N e^{-\beta c\|q\|_1}<\infty,
\]
so (M26.3) follows.

The twisted Fourier series
\[
\sum_q a_qU_q
\]
therefore converges absolutely in the twisted crossed product because the C*-norm of a finite Fourier sum is bounded by its coefficient \(\ell^1\)-norm. The magnetic multiplier has modulus one and changes neither the coefficient norm nor the support estimate.

For the dual/gauge derivations normalized by
\[
\nabla_j(a_qU_q)=2\pi iq_j a_qU_q,
\]
every iterated derivation is absolutely summable because (M26.3) holds for every polynomial weight. Hence the Hamiltonian lies in the usual gauge-smooth Wiener/Schwartz subalgebra.

For a cut normal to \(e_d\), regroup
\[
H_B
=
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}h_kV^k,
\qquad
h_k
=
\sum_{q_\parallel\in\mathbb Z^{d-1}}

Then
\[
\|h_k\|
\le

\|a_{(q_\parallel,k)}\|_\infty,

# 4. A two-step seam lemma

Both source incommensurate examples use the same geometric mechanism.

A flat coordinate circle has total length
\[
L=2\pi+\theta,
\qquad 0<\theta<2\pi,
\]
and the dynamical action advances by arc length \(\theta\). The generating rule projects the motion to a physical horizontal coordinate: away from the transition seam the full increment is \(\theta\); when a step crosses the seam, the increment is the corresponding partial distance. In Example 4.3.2 this is stated explicitly in equations (4.10)–(4.13) and the accompanying case description. In Example 4.3.4 equations (4.17)–(4.20) repeat the same construction independently in the \(x\)- and \(y\)-coordinates.

## `P17-M26-TWO-STEP-SEAM-18` — PROVED

For either one-dimensional coordinate algorithm, if \(s_n\ge0\) is the physical horizontal increment produced by the \(n\)-th positive \(\theta\)-step, then
\[
\boxed{s_n+s_{n+1}\ge\theta.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Two consecutive dynamical steps traverse an oriented arc of length \(2\theta\) on the flat coordinate loop. Since
\[
2\theta<2\pi+\theta=L,
\]
the two-step arc cannot pass a transition seam twice. If neither step is truncated at a seam, at least one step already contributes \(\theta\). If one step is split by a transition, the two complementary partial physical increments across the transition sum to \(\theta\). In Example 4.3.2 this complementarity is the explicit source identity
\[
d_+(\omega)+d_-(\tau_1\omega)=\theta
\]
for the small-circle transition. The coordinatewise equations (4.17)–(4.20) in Example 4.3.4 are the same seam rule on the flat torus.

Therefore (4.1) holds uniformly in the seed. ∎

A useful feature is that the vertical functions \(Y\) and \(Z\) do not enter (4.1). Hence the bound is uniform in the smoothing parameter \(g\).

# 12. Tests and adversarial checks

## 12.1 Why Proposition 3.25 was insufficient

The source proves only
\[
|p_{n+q}-p_n|\le D\|q\|.
\]
This is an upper Lipschitz estimate. It cannot imply decay of
\[
e^{-\beta|p_{n+q}-p_n|}
\]
in the label \(q\). Using it in that direction would reverse an inequality and is invalid.

M26 obtains an independent lower coarse-properness bound from the explicit seam geometry.

## 12.2 No irrationality is used
