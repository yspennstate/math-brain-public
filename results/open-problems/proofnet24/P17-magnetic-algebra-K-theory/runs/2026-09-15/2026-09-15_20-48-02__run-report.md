# 5. Proof of `P17-M36-EXPLICIT-EVANESCENT-RIESZ-32`

Let

\[
k_\beta(x)=e^{-\beta|x|},
\qquad x\in\mathbb R^2.
\]

Use the Fourier convention

\[
\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,dx.
\]

A direct radial Fourier calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{k_\beta}(\xi)
=
\frac{2\pi\beta}{(\beta^2+|\xi|^2)^{3/2}}>0.
}
\]

For `0<a<delta/(2sqrt(2))`, define the one-dimensional tent

\[
\psi_a(t)=(1-|t|/a)_+,
\]

and the tensor bump

\[
\eta_a(x_1,x_2)=\psi_a(x_1)\psi_a(x_2).
\]

Put

\[
\phi_a=\eta_a*\widetilde\eta_a.
\]

Since

\[
\operatorname{supp}\phi_a\subset[-2a,2a]^2
\]

and `2sqrt(2)a<delta`, separation gives

\[
\phi_a(p_n-p_m)=0
\qquad(n\ne m).
\]

The one-dimensional Fourier transform is

\[
\widehat{\psi_a}(s)
=a\,\operatorname{sinc}^2(as/2),
\]

so

\[
|\widehat{\eta_a}(\xi)|^2
=
a^4
\operatorname{sinc}^4(a\xi_1/2)
\operatorname{sinc}^4(a\xi_2/2).
\]

Let `rho=|xi|`. Since one coordinate has magnitude at least `rho/sqrt(2)` and

\[
|\operatorname{sinc}y|\le\min(1,|y|^{-1}),
\]

we have

\[
|\widehat{\eta_a}(\xi)|^2
\le
\min\left(a^4,\frac{64}{\rho^4}\right).
\]

Set

\[
\rho_0=\frac{2\sqrt2}{a}.
\]

For `rho<=rho_0`, the ratio to `widehat{k_beta}` is bounded by the increasing function

\[
\frac{a^4}{2\pi\beta}(\beta^2+\rho^2)^{3/2}.
\]

For `rho>=rho_0`, it is bounded by

\[
\frac{64}{2\pi\beta}
\frac{(\beta^2+\rho^2)^{3/2}}{\rho^4},
\]

whose logarithmic derivative is strictly negative:

\[
\frac{3\rho}{\beta^2+\rho^2}-\frac4\rho
=
-\frac{\rho^2+4\beta^2}{\rho(\beta^2+\rho^2)}<0.
\]

Therefore

\[
M_a
:=
\sup_\xi
\frac{|\widehat{\eta_a}(\xi)|^2}{\widehat{k_\beta}(\xi)}
\le
\frac{a^4}{2\pi\beta}
\left(\beta^2+\frac8{a^2}\right)^{3/2}.
\]

Now let `c=(c_n)` be finitely supported. Fourier inversion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle c,K_Pc\rangle
&=
(2\pi)^{-2}\int
\widehat{k_\beta}(\xi)
\left|\sum_nc_ne^{ip_n\cdot\xi}\right|^2\,d\xi\\
&\ge
M_a^{-1}(2\pi)^{-2}\int
|\widehat{\eta_a}(\xi)|^2
\left|\sum_nc_ne^{ip_n\cdot\xi}\right|^2\,d\xi\\
&=
M_a^{-1}
\sum_{n,m}\overline{c_n}c_m\phi_a(p_n-p_m).
\end{aligned}
\]

All off-diagonal terms vanish. Also

\[
\|\eta_a\|_2^2
=
\left(\int_{-a}^a(1-|t|/a)^2dt\right)^2
=
\left(\frac{2a}{3}\right)^2
=
\frac{4a^2}{9}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_P
\ge
c(a)I,
\qquad
c(a)
=
\frac{8\pi\beta a}
{9(\beta^2a^2+8)^{3/2}}.
}
\]

For

\[
0<a<\frac\delta{2\sqrt2},
\]

the derivative of `c(a)` is positive whenever `beta a<2`. Thus, under

\[
\beta\delta<4\sqrt2,
\]

we may let `a` increase to `delta/(2sqrt(2))`. Since the quadratic-form inequality holds for every smaller `a`, it also holds with the supremal limiting constant. Straight simplification gives

\[
\boxed{
K_P
\ge
\frac{64\pi\beta\delta}
{9(\beta^2\delta^2+64)^{3/2}}I.
}
\]

Uniform separation also gives a uniform packing bound for the row sums of the exponential kernel, so `K_P` is bounded on `l^2`; the quadratic-form estimate therefore gives genuine bounded invertibility.

This completes the proof.

# 7. Proof of `P17-M36-PUBLIC-KRONECKER-SUBORDINATION-32`

\[
p_{(n_1,n_2)}(\omega_1,\omega_2)
=
(x_{n_1}(\omega_1),y_{n_2}(\omega_2)).
\]

Hence

\[
|p_n-p_m|
=
\sqrt{|x_{n_1}-x_{m_1}|^2+|y_{n_2}-y_{m_2}|^2}.
\]

The scalar subordination identity follows from the standard Gaussian integral:

\[
e^{-\beta R}
=
\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt\pi}
\int_0^\infty
t^{-3/2}e^{-\beta^2/(4t)}e^{-tR^2}\,dt.
\]

With `s=beta^2/(4t)`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{
 e^{-\beta R}
 =
 \frac1{\sqrt\pi}
 \int_0^\infty
 s^{-1/2}e^{-s}
 e^{-\beta^2R^2/(4s)}\,ds.
}
\]

Substituting

\[
R^2=u^2+v^2
\]

factorizes the Gaussian:

\[
e^{-\beta\sqrt{u^2+v^2}}
=
\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty
s^{-1/2}e^{-s}
 e^{-\beta^2u^2/(4s)}
 e^{-\beta^2v^2/(4s)}\,ds.
\]

For fixed `s`, define the one-dimensional Gaussian kernel operators `G_{j,s}` as in the executive section. Since the coordinate maps satisfy

\[
|x_n-x_m|,\ |y_n-y_m|
\ge\delta|n-m|,
\qquad
\delta=1-2r,
\]

the Schur row bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|G_{j,s}\|
&\le
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\beta^2\delta^2k^2}{4s}\right]\\
&\le
1+\int_{\mathbb R}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\beta^2\delta^2x^2}{4s}\right]dx\\
&=
1+\frac{2\sqrt{\pi s}}{\beta\delta}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\|G_{1,s}\otimes G_{2,s}\|
\le
\left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\pi s}}{\beta\delta}\right)^2.
\]

Multiplication by `s^{-1/2}e^{-s}` makes this norm integrable on `(0,infty)`. The Bochner integral therefore converges in norm.

Coefficientwise it has exactly the public evanescent coefficient for every `(q_1,q_2)`, so absolute Fourier convergence and faithfulness identify the integral with the public all-hopping element in the zero-field source algebra.

With

\[
C=\frac{2\sqrt\pi}{\beta\delta},
\]

the bound expands as

\[
s^{-1/2}e^{-s}(1+C\sqrt s)^2
=
e^{-s}\left(s^{-1/2}+2C+C^2s^{1/2}\right).
\]

Integrating below `epsilon` or above `S` gives the incomplete-gamma tail formulas in Section 0.

Finally `s=x^2` yields

\[
 h_{\rm full,0}
 =
 \frac2{\sqrt\pi}
 \int_0^\infty
 e^{-x^2}
 G_{1,x^2}\otimes G_{2,x^2}\,dx.
\]

For nonzero displacement, the Gaussian coefficient is flat at `x=0`; the diagonal coefficient tends to one. This removes the explicit `s^{-1/2}` endpoint singularity and is the preferred representation for validated quadrature.

### What this theorem does not prove

It does not imply

\[
\sigma\!\left(\int A_sds\right)
=
\int\sigma(A_s)ds.
\]

The factors at different `s` need not commute. Therefore internal gap claims still require an actual norm/spectral certificate for the quadrature approximation plus its error terms.

### Independent check

**Surjectivity and index.** The images of the standard generators are the standard basis of `(Z/L)^m`, so `pi` is surjective and

\[
[H_{m,L}:K_{m,L}]=L^m.
\]

**Automorphism invariance.** On the quotient,

\[
e_1\mapsto e_1,
\qquad
e_j\mapsto e_{j-1}+e_j.

\[
\pi\circ\alpha_m=U\circ\pi

with `U` invertible, and therefore

\[
\alpha_m(K_{m,L})=K_{m,L}.
\]

\[
k e_1\ne0\pmod L,
\]

a contradiction. Thus `K` contains no nontrivial torsion.

**Freeness.** Kurosh's subgroup theorem decomposes a subgroup of a free product as a free product of conjugate intersections with the factors and a free group. The intersections of `K` with all conjugates of the finite `C_L` factor are trivial by the preceding torsion argument; the other factor is already free. Hence `K` is free.

[\Gamma_{m,L}:N_{m,L}]=L^m.

No normality of `N` in `Gamma` is required for this index argument.

**Torsion-freeness of N.** A finite-order element maps trivially to the `Z` quotient, hence lies in the torsion-free free kernel `K` and is trivial.
