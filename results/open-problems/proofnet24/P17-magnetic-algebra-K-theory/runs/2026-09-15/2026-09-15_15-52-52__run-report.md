# 4. New theorem: gauge amplification makes Gao's metric faithful

## `P17-M30-FAITHFUL-GAUGE-AMPLIFICATION-26` — PROVED

Let `A_sigma` be the noncommutative `d`-torus generated by `u_1,...,u_d` with

\[
u_j u_k=\sigma_{jk}u_k u_j.
\]

Let `gamma_z`, `z in T^d`, be the gauge action

\[
\gamma_z(u_j)=z_j u_j.
\]

Suppose a unitary tuple `U=(U_1,...,U_d)` on a Hilbert space `H` satisfies the `sigma` relations. It defines a unital representation

\[
\pi_U:A_\sigma\to B(H).
\]

Define the gauge amplification on

\[
\widetilde H=L^2(\mathbb T^d,H)
\]

by

\[
(\widetilde\pi_U(a)\xi)(z)
=
\pi_U(\gamma_z(a))\xi(z).
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — the amplification is faithful

Let

\[
I=\ker\widetilde\pi_U
=
\bigcap_{z\in\mathbb T^d}\gamma_z^{-1}(\ker\pi_U).
\]

Then `I` is gauge invariant.

The gauge average

\[
E(a)=\int_{\mathbb T^d}\gamma_z(a)\,dz
\]

is the canonical conditional expectation onto the fixed-point algebra, which here is the scalars:

\[
E(a)=\tau_\sigma(a)1.
\]

The canonical trace `tau_sigma` is faithful because `A_sigma` is the twisted group C*-algebra of the amenable group `Z^d`, so full and reduced coincide and the regular trace is faithful.

If `0 != a in I`, then `a^*a in I` and gauge invariance gives

\[
E(a^*a)=\tau_\sigma(a^*a)1\in I.
\]

Faithfulness makes the scalar strictly positive, so `1 in I`, contradicting that a unital representation has proper kernel. Hence

\[
\boxed{I=0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.2 — generator distance is unchanged

Let `U` satisfy the `sigma` relations and `V` the `sigma'` relations on the same `H`. Amplify both by the same gauge parameter. The amplified generators are

\[
\widetilde U_j(z)=z_jU_j,
\qquad
\widetilde V_j(z)=z_jV_j.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|\widetilde U_j-\widetilde V_j\|
=
\|U_j-V_j\|.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus **every** admissible pair in Gao's metric can be replaced by a faithful pair at exactly the same generator distance.

### Corollary 4.3 — faithful and unrestricted Gao metrics agree

If `rho_faith` denotes the infimum restricted to pairs of faithful representations, then

\[
\boxed{ho_{\rm faith}(\sigma,\sigma')=\rho(\sigma,\sigma').}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently Gao Proposition 4.4 yields faithful pairs with

\[
\boxed{
\max_j\|U_j-V_j\|
\le
24(d-1)\max_{j,k}|\sigma_{jk}-\sigma'_{jk}|^{1/2}+\varepsilon
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

for every `epsilon>0`.

Letting `epsilon -> 0` in polynomial estimates gives the explicit full-spectrum modulus `(0.4)`.

## 4.4 Explicit Wiener spectral theorem

For ordered monomials

\[
U^m=U_1^{m_1}\cdots U_d^{m_d},
\]

the elementary telescope gives

\[
\|U^m-V^m\|
\le
|m|_1\max_j\|U_j-V_j\|.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For self-adjoint Wiener sums

\[

\qquad

\]

put

\[
D_0=\sum_m\|A_m(\sigma)-A_m(\sigma')\|,

\[

\]

If these are finite, then faithful amplification, `(4.5)`, and `(4.6)` give

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
d_H(\operatorname{Spec}H_\sigma,\operatorname{Spec}H_{\sigma'})
&\le
D_0
+24(d-1)

# 7. Explicit Jackson finite-Fourier theorem

P18 correctly observed that a spatial truncation still has coefficient functions with infinite hull Fourier series. This section closes that step constructively.

## 7.1 One-dimensional Jackson kernel

On the angular circle `[-pi,pi]`, let

\[
F_n(t)
=
\frac1n
\left(
\frac{\sin(nt/2)}{\sin(t/2)}
\right)^2
\]

be the normalized Fejer kernel. With normalized Haar measure `dt/(2 pi)`,

\[
\int F_n=1,
\qquad
c_n:=\int F_n^2
=
\frac{2n^2+1}{3n}.
\]

Set

\[
J_n=F_n^2/c_n.
\]

Then `J_n` is an even positive trigonometric polynomial of degree `2(n-1)` and has integral one.

Using

\[
F_n(t)\le n,
\qquad
F_n(t)\le\frac{\pi^2}{nt^2}
\quad(0<|t|\le\pi),
\]

split the second-moment integral at `pi/n`. One obtains

\[
\int t^2J_n(t)\frac{dt}{2\pi}
\le
\frac{2\pi^2}{n^2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `f in C^2(T)`, Taylor's theorem, evenness of `J_n`, and `(7.1)` give

\[
\boxed{
\|f-J_n*f\|_\infty
\le
\frac{\pi^2}{n^2}\|f''\|_\infty.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The coordinate-invariant version on a circle of circumference `L` is

\[
\boxed{
\|f-J_{n,L}*f\|_\infty
\le
\frac{L^2}{4n^2}\|f''\|_\infty.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This displays all `2 pi` normalization factors explicitly.

## 7.2 Two-dimensional product kernel

Applying `(7.3)` one coordinate at a time yields

\[
\boxed{
\|f-J_{n_1,L_1}^{(1)}*J_{n_2,L_2}^{(2)}*f\|_\infty
\le
\frac{L_1^2}{4n_1^2}\|\partial_1^2f\|_\infty
+
\frac{L_2^2}{4n_2^2}\|\partial_2^2f\|_\infty.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The approximant has finite Fourier support

\[
|k_i|\le2(n_i-1).
\]

## 7.3 Source second-derivative bound

For `q !=0`, put

\[
d_q(\omega)=|p_{-q}(\omega)|.
\]

In angular coordinates,

\[
|\partial_{\omega_i}p_{-q}|\le2r,
\qquad
|\partial_{\omega_i}^2p_{-q}|\le2r.
\]

Since `d_q>=M-2r`, the Hessian formula for the Euclidean norm gives

\[
|\partial_{\omega_i}d_q|\le2r,
\]

\[
|\partial_{\omega_i}^2d_q|
\le
2r+\frac{4r^2}{M-2r}.
\]

For `w_q=e^{-d_q}`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\partial_{\omega_i}^2w_q\|_\infty
\le
 e^{2r-M}
\left(
2r+4r^2+\frac{4r^2}{M-2r}
\right).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Passing to the normalized unit torus multiplies the second derivative by `(2 pi)^2` and simultaneously replaces the Jackson prefactor by `1/(4n^2)`, giving the same final `pi^2/n^2` contribution. There is no hidden normalization ambiguity in the final estimate.

## 7.4 Finite 4-torus polynomial

Let

\[
T_{q,n}=J_n^{(1)}*J_n^{(2)}*w_q
\]

and define

\[
P_{Q,n,\theta}
=
\sum_{\|q\|_\infty\le Q}T_{q,n}(\theta,\cdot)u_q.
\]

Expanding each `T_{q,n}` in the two hull Fourier generators turns `P_{Q,n,theta}` into a finite polynomial in the source 4-torus generators:

\[
P_{Q,n,\theta}
=
\sum_{\substack{\|q\|_\infty\le Q\\|k_i|\le2(n-1)}}
 c_{k,q}(\theta)
 u_1^{k_1}u_2^{k_2}u_3^{q_1}u_4^{q_2}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Summing `(7.4)`–`(7.5)` over the shell of `8M` translation indices gives exactly `(0.8)`.

A convenient closed but coarser bound uses

\[
B_r(1)=2r+4r^2+\frac{4r^2}{1-2r}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{M\ge1}Me^{-M}=\frac{x}{(1-x)^2}.
\]

For `r=0.4`, the Jackson part is bounded by

\[
\boxed{1501.33974374/n^2.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Using the exact convergent sum instead of `B_r(1)` improves the coefficient to approximately

\[
953.62142891/n^2.
\]

The theorem is constructive: the finite coefficients `c_{k,q}(theta)` are ordinary compact-torus integrals and can be enclosed by interval quadrature.

If a numerical implementation introduces a tiny self-adjointness asymmetry, replace the polynomial by `(P+P^*)/2`; this does not increase its distance from the self-adjoint source Hamiltonian.
