## 3. Exact Gaussian-kernel Mercer eigenvalues and whole-tail control

Write

a := h^(-2).

On the unit sphere,

K_h(x,y)=exp(-a) exp(a <x,y>).

With P_s the degree-s population projection kernel, the Mercer expansion is

K_h = sum_(s>=0) lambda_(d,s) P_s,

with exact eigenvalues

lambda_(d,s)
= exp(-a) sum_(r>=0)
  a^(s+2r)
  /
  [2^r r! d(d+2)...(d+2(s+r)-2)].

This is equivalently the standard modified-Bessel formula. NIST DLMF 10.25.2 was refreshed this run; it gives the positive series

I_nu(z)=(z/2)^nu sum_(r>=0) (z^2/4)^r/[r! Gamma(nu+r+1)].

URL: `https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25`

Because every term is positive, termwise comparison yields the exact, all-degree inequality

lambda_(d,s+1) <= [a/(d+2s)] lambda_(d,s).

There is no fixed-degree asymptotic hidden here.

Let

mass_s := N_(d,s) lambda_(d,s).

N_(d,s+1)/N_(d,s)
= (d+2s)(d+s-2)/[(s+1)(d+2s-2)]
<= (d+2s)/(s+1).

Therefore

mass_(s+1)/mass_s <= a/(s+1).

This factorial contraction is exact. In particular, for the high tail

tau := sum_(s>=k) mass_s,

tau = mass_k [1+O_h(1/k)],

That final inequality also follows immediately from lambda_s<=lambda_k and tau>=N_k lambda_k.

## 4. Growing-degree spherical hypercontractivity

Let Y_s be a degree-s spherical harmonic and H_s its homogeneous harmonic extension to R^d:

H_s(r u)=r^s Y_s(u).

For a standard Gaussian G=R_g U, with U uniform on the sphere and R_g independent, H_s is a pure degree-s Gaussian chaos. Gaussian Ornstein-Uhlenbeck hypercontractivity gives, for p>=2,

||H_s(G)||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2) ||H_s(G)||_2.

Gross's dimension-free Gaussian hypercontractivity is the imported primary theorem. The radial factors do not introduce a degree-dependent constant because

( E R_g^(2s) )^(1/2)
<= ( E R_g^(ps) )^(1/p)

for p>=2. Hence

||Y_s||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2) ||Y_s||_2.

For normalized zonal shell

r_s(x,z):=P_s(x,z)/N_s,

this gives, for fixed z,

||r_s(X,z)||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2)/sqrt(N_s).

This is dimension-free and has the exact explicit growing-degree dependence needed in the proof.

The high normalized tail is

where

omega_s = mass_s/tau.

No sign symmetry, no Gaussian replacement, no independence between numerator and denominator, no local law, and no detached reciprocal inverse bound is used.
