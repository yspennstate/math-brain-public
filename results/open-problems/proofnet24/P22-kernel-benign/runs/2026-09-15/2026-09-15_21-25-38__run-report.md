## 2.1 KRM decoupling / noncommutative Khintchine source

Primary source:

Chiraag Kaushik, Justin Romberg, Vidya Muthukumar, *A general technique for approximating high-dimensional empirical kernel matrices*, arXiv:2511.03892v1, submitted 5 Nov 2025.

The complete primary PDF was reopened this run and proof pages 6, 7, and 8 were visually inspected.

The source-exact facts used by Target 2 are:

1. The off-diagonal kernel fluctuation is written as a matrix-valued U-statistic.
2. The decoupled copy is introduced **before conditioning**.
3. The displayed decoupling inequality is exactly `E||Delta|| <= 8 E||Delta_tilde||`.
4. The proof then conditions on the original sample.
5. Conditioned on the original sample, the copied column matrices `Z_j` are independent.
6. Noncommutative Khintchine is applied only at that conditionally independent stage.
7. The scalar kernel is centered in the **second argument**.
8. The exact pre-relaxation variance proxy has factor `1/4` and contains the leave-one-out centered correlation matrix plus the diagonal row-sum term.
9. Only afterwards is this proxy relaxed.

The Gaussian kernel in the target is bounded and measurable. The growing harmonic pieces satisfy the explicit moments checked below, so the integrability/Bochner hypotheses used by KRM are met.

No projected-coordinate iid assumption is imported.

## 2.2 DLMF modified-Bessel series

NIST DLMF 10.25.2 states the positive series

`I_nu(z)=(z/2)^nu sum_{r>=0}(z^2/4)^r/[r! Gamma(nu+r+1)]`.

For positive real `z`, all terms are positive. This is exactly the fact used to compare corresponding Mercer-series terms. No large-order Bessel asymptotic is imported.

## 2.3 Gaussian hypercontractivity

The semigroup input used is the Nelson/Gross Ornstein-Uhlenbeck contraction:

if `1<p<=q<infinity` and `q-1 <= exp(2t)(p-1)`, then

`||T_t F||_q <= ||F||_p`.

For a pure Gaussian chaos of degree `s`,

`T_t F_s=exp(-s t)F_s`.

Taking input exponent `2`, output exponent `p>=2`, and `t=(1/2)log(p-1)` gives

`||F_s||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2)||F_s||_2`.

The spherical estimate is re-derived by homogeneous Gaussian lifting, so no hidden dimension- or degree-dependent spherical theorem is imported.

## 2.4 Rosenthal constant dependence

Adam Osekowski, *A Note on the Burkholder-Rosenthal Inequality*, Bulletin of the Polish Academy of Sciences. Mathematics 60 (2012), proves the Hilbert-valued martingale-difference inequality with constant `C_p=O(p/log p)`.

Independent centered scalar variables are a special case. The P22/P04 proof budgets only a coarser `O(p)` factor, hence its constant dependence is safe at growing `p=R`.

## 2.5 Matrix Chernoff

The low block uses the matrix Chernoff framework from Joel Tropp, *User-Friendly Tail Bounds for Sums of Random Matrices*:

- independent PSD self-adjoint summands;
- a deterministic upper eigenvalue bound for each summand;
- a known matrix expectation.

The low harmonic row outer products satisfy these exactly: row independence comes from iid training points; the spherical addition theorem makes the row norm deterministic; orthonormality gives expectation `I`. No within-row coordinate independence is required.

Let `H` be `n x m`, `n=m+r`, with iid centered rows. Assume full column rank a.s. and finite positive `E det(H^T H)`. Put

`D=det(H^T H)`,

`dQ/dP=D/E D`,

and

`K=I-H(H^T H)^(-1)H^T`,

the rank-`r` left-nullspace projector.

P04 claims that for every `0<=j<=r`,

`E_Q[wedge^j K]=C(r,j)/C(n,j) * I_{wedge^j R^n}`.

### Independent derivation

Let the columns of `H` be `g_1,...,g_m` and define

`eta=g_1 wedge ... wedge g_m`.

Then `||eta||^2=D`. Let

`Omega=*eta/sqrt(D)`

be the unit decomposable `r`-vector spanning `wedge^r ker(H^T)`. For every `r`-subset `R` of `[n]`, its Pluecker coordinate is

`Omega_R=sign_R det(H_{R^c})/sqrt(D)`.

For `j`-subsets `I,J`, the matrix element of the exterior projector is the standard contraction formula

`<e_I,(wedge^j K)e_J>
 = sum_L sign(I,L)sign(J,L) Omega_{I union L} Omega_{J union L}`,

where `L` ranges over `(r-j)`-subsets disjoint from `I union J`.

Multiplying by `D` turns every summand into a product of two square `m x m` row minors.

If `I != J`, choose `a in I\J`. In each nonzero term, row `a` is absent from the first minor but appears linearly in the second. Conditional on all other rows, the first factor is fixed and the second is linear in the fresh centered row `h_a`. Therefore every off-diagonal expectation is zero.

`E D=C(n,r) delta_m`.

`E_Q <e_I,(wedge^j K)e_I>

This proves the claim exactly.
