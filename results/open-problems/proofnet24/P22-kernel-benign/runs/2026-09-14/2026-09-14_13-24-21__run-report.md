## 3. P22-B1 — Exact Gaussian-kernel Mercer eigenvalues, independently rederived

**Status: PROVED.**

Let \(\{\psi_{s,a}\}_{a=1}^{N_s}\) be an orthonormal basis of degree-\(s\) spherical harmonics for normalized Haar probability on \(S^{d-1}\), and let
\[
P_s(x,y)=\sum_{a=1}^{N_s}\psi_{s,a}(x)\psi_{s,a}(y)
\]
be the projection kernel.

The Gaussian kernel has the Mercer expansion
\[
K_h(x,y)=\sum_{s\ge0}\lambda_{d,s}P_s(x,y).
\]

I rederive the exact eigenvalue formula rather than taking it from P04.

Fix a homogeneous harmonic polynomial \(H_s\) of degree \(s\), and let \(G\sim N(0,I_d)\). For \(\|x\|=1\), Cameron–Martin plus harmonicity gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
H_s(G)e^{t\langle x,G\rangle-t^2/2}
\right]
=
\mathbb E H_s(G+tx)
=
e^{\frac12\Delta}H_s(tx)
=
t^sH_s(x),
\]
because \(\Delta H_s=0\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
H_s(G)e^{t\langle x,G\rangle}
\right]
=
t^s e^{t^2/2}H_s(x).
\]
Comparing the coefficient of \(t^{s+2r}\),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\langle x,G\rangle^{s+2r}H_s(G)
\right]
=
\frac{(s+2r)!}{2^r r!}H_s(x).
\]

Write \(G=RU\), where \(U\) is uniform on the sphere and \(R\) is independent. Since
\[
\mathbb E R^{2(s+r)}
=
d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2),
\]
we obtain
\[
\int_{S^{d-1}}
\langle x,u\rangle^{s+2r}H_s(u)\,d\sigma(u)
=
\frac{(s+2r)!}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}
H_s(x).
\]

Expanding \(e^{\gamma\langle x,u\rangle}\) now gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s}
=
e^{-\gamma}
\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
}
\tag{B1}
\]

This validates the exact P04/P22 formula with no normalization ambiguity.

A useful exact corollary is termwise monotonicity:
\[
\lambda_{d,s+1}
\le
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
\tag{B2}
\]
Indeed, for each fixed \(r\), the \(s+1\) summand is the \(s\) summand multiplied by
\[
\frac{\gamma}{d+2(s+r)}
\le
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}.
\]
For fixed \(h\) and large \(d\), \(\lambda_{d,s}\) is therefore strictly decreasing for every \(s\ge0\).

Define
\[
\tau_s=N_s\lambda_{d,s}.
\]
For \(s=o(\sqrt d)\), the \(r=0\) term and the multiplicity estimate below give
\[
\tau_s
=
e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^s}{s!}
\left(1+O_\gamma\!\left(\frac{s^2+1}{d}\right)\right).
\tag{B3}
\]

\boxed{

## 6. P22-B3 — Growing-degree hypercontractivity with no hidden dimension constant

**Status: PROVED.**

Fix a degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic \(Y_s\), and let
\[
H_s(ru)=r^sY_s(u)
\]
be its homogeneous harmonic extension.

Because
\[
\Delta H_s=0,
\qquad
x\cdot\nabla H_s=sH_s,
\]
the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator satisfies
\[
LH_s=-sH_s.
\]
Gross hypercontractivity therefore yields, for integer \(q\ge1\),
\[
\|H_s(G)\|_{2q}
\le
(2q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\tag{B11}
\]

Write \(G=RU\) with independent radius and direction. Then
\[
\|R^s\|_{2q}\|Y_s(U)\|_{2q}
\le
(2q-1)^{s/2}
\|R^s\|_2\|Y_s(U)\|_2.
\]
Since \(L^p\)-norms increase with \(p\),
\[
\|R^s\|_{2q}\ge\|R^s\|_2,
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_{2q}
\le
(2q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2.
}
\tag{B12}
\]
The constant is exactly dimension-free.

For fixed \(z\),
\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_2=N_s^{-1/2},
\]
so
\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_{2q}
\le
\frac{(2q-1)^{s/2}}{\sqrt{N_s}}.
\tag{B13}
\]

This closes the main potential nonuniformity in the growing-degree passage \(s\sim R\).

## 7. P22-B4 — Infinite-tail mixture remains controlled

**Status: PROVED.**

The positive Taylor expansion gives
\[
\tau_s
\le
e^{-\gamma}
\sum_{m\ge s}\frac{\gamma^m}{m!}.
\tag{B14}
\]
For \(k^2/d\to0\), the \(r=0\) term of (B1) and (B5) give
\[
\tau_k
\ge
c_\gamma e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^k}{k!}
\tag{B15}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(d\).

Since \(k>2\gamma\) eventually, the Poisson tail is dominated by its first term, so for every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\frac{\tau_{k+r}}{\tau_k}
\le
C_\gamma
\frac{\gamma^r k!}{(k+r)!}.
\tag{B16}
\]

By monotonicity of \(N_s\) and Minkowski,
\[
\|r_T(X,z)\|_{2q}
\le
\frac{(2q-1)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{N_k}}
\sum_{r\ge0}
w_{k+r}(2q-1)^{r/2}.
\]
Using (B16),
\[
\sum_{r\ge0}
w_{k+r}(2q-1)^{r/2}
\le
C_\gamma
\exp\!\left(
\frac{\gamma\sqrt{2q-1}}{k}
\right).
\tag{B17}
\]

At the required choice
\[
q=R,
\qquad
k=R+\ell-1\sim R,
\]
the exponential in (B17) is uniformly bounded and in fact tends to \(1\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\|r_T(X,z)\|_{2R}
\le
C_h
\frac{(2R)^{{k}/{2}}}{\sqrt{N_k}}.
}
\tag{B18}
\]

Orthogonality gives exactly
\[
\mathbb E r_T(X,z)^2
=
\sum_{s\ge k}\frac{w_s^2}{N_s}
=
\frac{1+o(1)}{N_k},
\tag{B19}
\]
because \(w_k=1-O(1/k)\) and \(N_s\) increases.

In particular
\[
\|r_T(X,z)^2\|_2
\le
C_h\frac{3^k}{N_k},
\tag{B20}
\]
and
\[
\|r_T(X,z)^2\|_R
\le
C_h\frac{(2R)^k}{N_k}.
\tag{B21}
\]

The infinite tail therefore introduces no hidden loss at the growing cutoff.

## 8. P22-B5 — Robust Rosenthal audit: the sharp \(\sqrt R\) factor is unnecessary

**Status: PROVED.**

This is the main new audit contribution.

Fix \(z\) and write
\[
S(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n r_T(X_i,z)^2.
\]
Set
\[
Y_i=r_T(X_i,z)^2-\mathbb E r_T(X_i,z)^2.
\]
The \(Y_i\) are centered and independent.

The previous P22 report used a sharp Rosenthal form
\[
\left\|\sum_iY_i\right\|_q
\lesssim
\sqrt q\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
+
q\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_q^q\right)^{1/q}.
\]
That form is valid, but it is stronger than needed.

By symmetrization and the Johnson–Schechtman–Zinn sharp Rosenthal constant \(B_q=O(q/\log q)\), it is enough to use the crude consequence
\[
\boxed{
\left\|\sum_iY_i\right\|_q
\le
Cq
\left[
\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
+
\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_q^q\right)^{1/q}
\right].
}
\tag{B22}
\]
Thus, from (B20)–(B21),
\[
\|S(z)-\mathbb ES(z)\|_q
\le
C_h q
\left[
\sqrt n\,\frac{3^k}{N_k}
+
n^{1/q}\frac{(2q)^k}{N_k}
\right].
\tag{B23}
\]

Take \(q=R\). Since
\[
n=d^R,
\qquad
n^{1/R}=d,
\]
and for arbitrary random variables \(Z_1,\dots,Z_n\),
\[
\|\max_j|Z_j|\|_R
\le
\left(\sum_j\|Z_j\|_R^R\right)^{1/R},
\]
passing to the maximum over the \(n\) decoupled test columns costs at most one additional factor \(d\). No independence between the \(S(\widetilde X_j)\) is required for this step.

Relative to the mean
\[
\mathbb ES(z)
=
\frac{1+o(1)}{N_k}n,
\]
the two error ratios are bounded by
\[
C_h\,dR\,\frac{3^k}{\sqrt n}
=
d^{-R/2+O(1)}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{B24}
\]
and
\[
C_h\,R\,d^2\,\frac{(2R)^k}{n}
=
d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{B25}
\]

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\max_{1\le j\le n}
\sum_{i=1}^n
r_T(X_i,\widetilde X_j)^2
=
(1+o(1))\frac n{N_k}.
}
\tag{B26}
\]

This proves that even a deliberately weakened Rosenthal constant closes the argument. There is no delicate sharp-moment-constant bottleneck here.

## 9. P22-B6 — Exact KRM decoupling/NCK step

**Status: PROVED.**

The use of Kaushik–Romberg–Muthukumar is valid at the proof level.

Their Theorem 1 assumes iid inputs, a PSD kernel, and integrability. For our normalized tail kernel \(r_T\):

- the inputs are iid uniform on \(S^{d-1}\);
- \(r_T\) is PSD because it is a positive Mercer tail divided by \(\tau>0\);
- \(r_T\) is continuous and bounded on the compact sphere;
- \(r_T(x,x)=1\);
- \(\mathbb E_Z r_T(x,Z)=0\) exactly because only positive harmonic degrees occur.

KRM decouple the off-diagonal U-statistic. Conditional on the original sample, their decoupled column matrices \(Z_j\) are independent. Their Eq. (2) gives
\[
\mathbb E\|\widetilde\Delta\mid X\|
\lesssim
\sqrt{\log n\,V}
+
\log n\,L
\tag{B27}
\]
because the conditional mean term vanishes here.

Crucially, before their later scalar maximum-entry relaxation, they prove
\[
L^2
\lesssim
\mathbb E_{\widetilde X}
\max_j
\sum_i
r_T(X_i,\widetilde X_j)^2.
\tag{B28}
\]
Thus (B26) yields
\[
\mathbb E L
\lesssim
\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}.
\tag{B29}
\]

The correlation matrix is
\[
G_{ij}
=
\mathbb E_z
r_T(X_i,z)r_T(z,X_j).
\]
Using (B10),
\[
G
=
\tau^{-2}
\sum_{s\ge k}
\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X).
\tag{B30}
\]
Since \(\lambda_s\le\lambda_k\) for \(s\ge k\),
\[
G
\preceq
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}R_T.
\tag{B31}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}
=
\frac{\tau_k/\tau}{N_k}
\le
\frac1{N_k}.
\tag{B32}
\]

Let
\[
A=\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E\|G\|
\le
\frac{1+A}{N_k}.
\tag{B33}
\]

Combining KRM decoupling, (B29), and (B33),
\[
A
\le
C_h\left[
\sqrt{
\frac{n\log n}{N_k}(1+A)
}
+
\log n\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}
\right]
+o(1).
\tag{B34}
\]

By (B8),
\[
\frac n{N_k}=d^{-\delta+o(1)},
\qquad
\log n=\Theta(R^2).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|\to0.
}
\tag{B35}
\]

This proves the entire infinite-tail operator-norm isotropy required by the regression argument.

### Quantitative summable form

For every fixed
\[
0<\delta'<\delta,
\]
eventually
\[
\frac n{N_k}\le d^{-\delta'}.
\]
Solving (B34) gives, for all sufficiently large \(\ell\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|
\le
C_h(\log n)\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}
\le
C_h R^2 d^{-\delta'/2}.
}
\tag{B36}
\]
For example, taking \(\delta'=\delta/2\),
\[
\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|
\le
C_hR^2d^{-\delta/4}.
\tag{B37}
\]
Since \(d=2^R\) and \(R=2^\ell\),
\[
\sum_{\ell\ge1}

\tag{B38}
\]

Thus

## 10. P22-B7 — Low-harmonic block concentration with exact hypotheses

\[

\qquad

\[
Z_i=\Psi(X_i)\Psi(X_i)^T.

\lambda_{\max}(Z_i)=D,

and
\[
\sum_i\mathbb EZ_i=nI_D.

\frac1n\Psi^T\Psi-I_D

2D\exp\!\left(-c_\eta\frac nD\right).
\tag{B40}
\]
Since
\[
\frac Dn
=
d^{-(1-\delta)+o(1)},
\]
the right-hand side is not merely \(o(1)\): it is summable in \(\ell\).

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac1n\Psi^T\Psi
\to I_D
}
\tag{B41}
\]
in operator norm in probability, and along the prescribed sequence almost surely.

\[
T=K_T(X,X),
\]
and let \(\Lambda\) denote the diagonal matrix of Mercer eigenvalues attached to the low harmonic coordinates in \(\Psi\).

Any interpolant decomposes orthogonally in the RKHS into a low part and a tail part. If its low coefficient vector is \(a\), then the tail must interpolate
\[
r=y-\Psi a.
\]
Because the tail Gram matrix is \(T\), the minimum possible squared tail RKHS norm equals
\[
r^TT^{-1}r.
\]
Therefore the exact minimum-norm objective in low coordinates is
\[
J(a)
=
a^T\Lambda^{-1}a
+
(y-\Psi a)^TT^{-1}(y-\Psi a).
\tag{B42}
\]

On the event
\[
\|T/\tau-I\|\le\frac12,
\tag{B43}
\]
define
\[
W=\tau T^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac23I\preceq W\preceq2I.
\tag{B44}
\]

Differentiating (B42),
\[
\boxed{
\widehat a
=
G^{-1}\Psi^TWy,
\qquad
G=\tau\Lambda^{-1}+\Psi^TW\Psi.
}
\tag{B45}
\]

If additionally
\[
\left\|
\frac1n\Psi^T\Psi-I
\right\|\le\frac12,
\tag{B46}
\]
then
\[
G
\succeq
\Psi^TW\Psi
\succeq
\frac23\Psi^T\Psi
\succeq
\frac n3I.
\tag{B47}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\|G^{-1}\|\le\frac3n.}
\tag{B48}
\]

This gives all inverse-Gram stability required later. No hard-edge estimate for the full \(n\times n\) Gaussian-kernel Gram matrix is needed.

## 12. P22-B9 — Noise risk with explicit good-event bounds

**Status: PROVED.**

First set the signal to zero:
\[
y=\varepsilon,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^T]=\sigma^2I.
\]

The low coefficient vector is
\[
\widehat a_{\rm noise}
=
G^{-1}\Psi^TW\varepsilon.
\]
Using (B44), (B46), and (B48),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\varepsilon
\|\widehat a_{\rm noise}\|^2
&=
\sigma^2
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
G^{-1}\Psi^TW^2\Psi G^{-1}
\right)\\
&\le
C\sigma^2\frac Dn.
\end{aligned}
\tag{B49}
\]
Because the low harmonic basis is \(L^2\)-orthonormal, this is exactly the low-frequency prediction variance.

Now let
\[
r_{\rm noise}
=
\varepsilon-\Psi\widehat a_{\rm noise}.
\]
The residual operator is
\[
I-\Psi G^{-1}\Psi^TW.
\]
On the same good event,
\[
\|\Psi\|^2\le\frac32n,
\qquad
\|G^{-1}\|\le\frac3n,
\qquad
\|W\|\le2,
\]
so
\[
\|I-\Psi G^{-1}\Psi^TW\|\le C
\tag{B50}
\]
with an absolute constant. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon\|r_{\rm noise}\|^2
\le
Cn\sigma^2.
\tag{B51}
\]

The population covariance of the tail kernel sections is
\[
H_T
=
\int
K_T(X,x)K_T(x,X)\,d\sigma(x)
=
\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X).
\tag{B52}
\]
By eigenvalue monotonicity,
\[
H_T\preceq\lambda_kT.
\tag{B53}
\]

The tail interpolant has coefficient vector \(T^{-1}r_{\rm noise}\), hence population \(L^2\) energy
\[
r_{\rm noise}^TT^{-1}H_TT^{-1}r_{\rm noise}
\le
\lambda_k r_{\rm noise}^TT^{-1}r_{\rm noise}.
\]
Since
\[
T^{-1}\preceq\frac2\tau I,
\]
we obtain
\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon
\|\widehat f_{T,\rm noise}\|_{L^2}^2
\le
C\sigma^2 n\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}.
\tag{B54}
\]
But
\[
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}
=
\frac{\tau_k/\tau}{N_k}
\le
\frac1{N_k},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\varepsilon
\|\widehat f_{T,\rm noise}\|_{L^2}^2
\le
C\sigma^2\frac n{N_k}.
}
\tag{B55}
\]

Thus on the good design event,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\varepsilon
[\mathcal R_{\rm noise}\mid X]
\le
C\sigma^2
\left(
\frac Dn+\frac n{N_k}
\right)
\to0.
}
\tag{B56}
\]

Only centered independent noise with covariance \(\sigma^2I\) is used in this expectation calculation; Gaussianity is part of the stated target but is stronger than needed here.

## 13. P22-B10 — Fixed-degree signal bias and tail leakage

\to0.

\boxed{

\to0.

Thus both low-frequency signal bias and high-frequency signal leakage vanish.

## 16. P22-OBS-01 — Counterexample to an overstrong growing-degree signal claim

**Status: PROVED.**

The fixed-degree hypothesis is essential. The proof cannot be upgraded to “every bounded-\(L^2\) spherical-polynomial signal whose degree may grow with \(\ell\).”

Take the critical degree
\[
s=k.
\]
Then
\[
N_k/n\to\infty.
\]
Consider the noiseless problem restricted to the unit sphere of coefficients in \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\). Let \(\theta\) be uniformly random on the coefficient sphere in \(\mathbb R^{N_k}\), independently of the design. The \(n\) labels provide at most \(n\) linear measurements of \(\theta\).

Conditional on the design, even an optimal estimator of \(\theta\) leaves the component orthogonal to the row span unobserved. Averaging over the random coefficient direction,
\[
\mathbb E_\theta
\|\theta-\widehat\theta\|_2^2
\ge
1-\frac{\operatorname{rank}\Phi_k}{N_k}
\ge
1-\frac n{N_k}
\to1.
\tag{B72}
\]
For an arbitrary function estimator, project its output onto \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\); orthogonality of spherical harmonics implies total \(L^2\) prediction error is at least the error of this degree-\(k\) projection. Therefore the same Bayes lower bound applies to every estimator.

By averaging, for every \(d\) there exists a deterministic unit-\(L^2\) degree-\(k\) signal with expected prediction error at least
\[
1-\frac n{N_k}.
\tag{B73}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{bounded }L^2\text{ norm alone is not enough if the signal degree grows to }k.
}
\]

This counterexample validates the exact scope of Target 2: **fixed maximal signal degree** is a real assumption, not merely a proof convenience.
