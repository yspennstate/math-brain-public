## Exact setup and notation

Let \(\gamma=h^{-2}\). On the unit sphere \(S^{d-1}\),

\[
K(x,y)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2h^2}\right)
=e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma\langle x,y\rangle}.
\]

For degree \(s\), let \(\{\psi_{s,a}\}_{a=1}^{N_s}\) be an orthonormal basis of spherical harmonics with respect to uniform probability measure, and put

\[
P_s(x,y)=\sum_{a=1}^{N_s}\psi_{s,a}(x)\psi_{s,a}(y),
\qquad r_s=P_s/N_s.
\]

The addition theorem gives \(P_s(x,x)=N_s\), hence \(r_s(x,x)=1\). Orthogonality gives, for \(s,t>0\),

\[
\mathbb E_Y r_s(x,Y)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_Y[r_s(x,Y)r_t(Y,z)]
=\delta_{st}\frac{r_s(x,z)}{N_s}.
\]

Write the Mercer decomposition

\[
K(x,y)=\sum_{s\ge0}\lambda_{d,s}P_s(x,y),
\qquad
\tau_s=N_s\lambda_{d,s}.
\]

The exact eigenvalue formula carried by P04 is

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
\tag{E1}
\]

Checks at \(s=0,1\) agree directly with the spherical moment formulas, and the formula is consistent with Funk–Hecke / harmonic projection of each Taylor monomial.

For the special sequence define

\[
R=2^\ell,\qquad d=2^R,\qquad n=d^R,
\qquad k=R+\ell-1.
\]

Let the high-frequency tail be

\[
K_T=\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_sP_s,
\qquad
\tau=K_T(x,x)=\sum_{s\ge k}\tau_s,
\qquad
r_T=K_T/\tau=\sum_{s\ge k}w_s r_s,
\quad w_s=\tau_s/\tau.
\]

For the sample let \(R_T=(r_T(X_i,X_j))_{i,j\le n}\) and \(\Delta=R_T-I\).

## P22-A1 — Exact eigenvalue monotonicity on the entire tail

**Status: PROVED.**

This is a useful strengthening/cleanup of P04's monotonicity assertion. From (E1), term by term,

\[
\lambda_{d,s+1}
=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma}{d+2(s+r)}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}
\le \frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\tag{A1}
\]

For fixed \(\gamma\) and all sufficiently large \(d\), the Mercer eigenvalues are strictly decreasing for every \(s\ge0\), not merely through the active window. Consequently, on the whole tail,

\[
H_T:=\mathbb E_x[K_T(X,x)K_T(x,X)]
=\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X)
\preceq \lambda_k K_T(X,X).
\tag{A2}
\]

This validates the PSD comparison used in P04's tail-risk bound without any unproved eventual-monotonicity assumption.

## P22-A2 — Dimension-uniform spherical hypercontractivity at growing degree

**Status: PROVED.**

This was the most plausible place for a hidden nonuniform constant, because \(k\sim R\to\infty\). It can be proved directly without appealing to a delicate sphere-semigroup theorem.

Fix a spherical harmonic \(Y_s\) of degree \(s\), and let \(H_s\) be its homogeneous harmonic extension, so \(H_s(ru)=r^sY_s(u)\). Let \(G=RU\sim N(0,I_d)\), where \(U\) is uniform on the sphere and \(R\) is independent of \(U\). Gaussian polynomial hypercontractivity gives for every integer \(q\ge1\)

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_{2q}
\le (2q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\]

Using independence of \(R,U\),

\[
\|R^s\|_{2q}\|Y_s(U)\|_{2q}
\le (2q-1)^{s/2}\|R^s\|_2\|Y_s(U)\|_2.
\]

Since \(\|R^s\|_{2q}\ge\|R^s\|_2\),

\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_{2q}\le (2q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2,
}
\tag{A3}
\]

with constant exactly 1 and **uniform in dimension**. For fixed \(z\), \(r_s(X,z)\) is a degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic in \(X\), and orthogonality gives \(\|r_s(X,z)\|_2=N_s^{-1/2}\). Hence

\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_{2q}
\le \frac{(2q-1)^{s/2}}{\sqrt{N_s}}.
\tag{A4}
\]

Thus the growing-degree step does not hide a dimension-dependent hypercontractive constant.

## P22-A3 — Uniform control of the infinite harmonic mixture

**Status: PROVED.**

The positive Taylor expansion implies the crude but useful mass bound

\[
\tau_s\le e^{-\gamma}\sum_{m\ge s}\frac{\gamma^m}{m!}.
\tag{A5}
\]

On the other hand, the \(r=0\) term in (E1), together with \(k^2/d\to0\), gives

\[
\tau_k\ge c_\gamma e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^k}{k!}
\tag{A6}
\]

for all large \(d\). For \(k>2\gamma\), the Poisson tail in (A5) is bounded by a constant multiple of its first term, so uniformly for \(r\ge0\),

\[
\frac{\tau_{k+r}}{\tau_k}
\le C_\gamma\frac{\gamma^r k!}{(k+r)!}.
\tag{A7}
\]

Since \(\tau/\tau_k=1+O(\gamma/k)\), we also have \(w_k=1-O(1/k)\). Because \(N_s\) is increasing in \(s\), Minkowski plus (A4) yields

\[
\|r_T(X,z)\|_{2q}
\le \frac{(2q-1)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{N_k}}
\sum_{r\ge0}w_{k+r}(2q-1)^{r/2}.
\]

Using (A7),

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}w_{k+r}(2q-1)^{r/2}
\le C_\gamma
\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(\gamma\sqrt{2q-1})^r k!}{(k+r)!}
\le C_\gamma\exp\!\left(\frac{\gamma\sqrt{2q-1}}{k}\right).
\tag{A8}
\]

At the required moment \(q=R\) and \(k\sim R\), the exponential factor is \(1+o(1)\). Therefore uniformly in the growing degree,

\[
\boxed{
\|r_T(X,z)\|_{2R}
\le C_\gamma\frac{(2R)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{N_k}}.
}
\tag{A9}
\]

Also

\[
\mathbb E r_T(X,z)^2
=\sum_{s\ge k}\frac{w_s^2}{N_s}
=\frac{1+o(1)}{N_k}.
\tag{A10}
\]

This closes the infinite-tail truncation/uniformity concern.

## P22-A4 — Harmonic dimension gap

**Status: PROVED.**

For \(s=o(d)\),

\[
N_s=\binom{d+s-1}{s}-\binom{d+s-3}{s-2}
=\frac{d^s}{s!}\exp(O(s^2/d)).
\tag{A11}
\]

Since \(k=R+\ell-1\), \(d=2^R\), and \(\ell=\log_2R\), Stirling gives

\[
\frac{\log N_k}{\log d}
=R+\delta+o(1),
\qquad
\delta=\frac1{\log 2}-1=0.442695\ldots
\]

so

\[
\boxed{N_k/n=d^{\delta+o(1)}.}
\tag{A12}
\]

Moreover consecutive dimensions satisfy \(N_{s-1}/N_s\sim s/d\), so the total dimension below \(k\),

\[
D_{<k}=\sum_{s<k}N_s,
\]

is dominated by \(N_{k-1}\), yielding

\[
\boxed{D_{<k}/n=d^{\delta-1+o(1)}=d^{-0.557305\ldots+o(1)}.}
\tag{A13}
\]

## P22-A5 — Exact audit of the KRM decoupling/NCK step and tail isotropy

**Status: PROVED.**

KRM's source proof explicitly gives, after decoupling and conditioning, a matrix-moment bound of the form

\[
\mathbb E_{\widetilde X}\|\widetilde\Delta\|
\lesssim
\sqrt{\log n\,V}+\log n\,L
\tag{A14}
\]

when the conditional mean term vanishes. For \(r_T\), it vanishes exactly because every harmonic degree in the tail is positive:

\[
\mathbb E_{\widetilde X}r_T(x,\widetilde X)=0.
\]

The KRM proof itself gives the pre-relaxation row-sum bound

\[
L^2\le
\mathbb E_{\widetilde X}
\max_{1\le j\le n}
\sum_{i=1}^n r_T(X_i,\widetilde X_j)^2.
\tag{A15}
\]

This is exactly the quantity used by P04; no fixed-degree corollary is being extrapolated.

Now fix \(z\), put \(S(z)=\sum_i r_T(X_i,z)^2\), and set \(q=R\). Scalar Rosenthal plus (A9) gives

\[
\|S(z)-\mathbb ES(z)\|_q
\le C\left[
\sqrt{qn}\frac{3^k}{N_k}
+q n^{1/q}\frac{(2q)^k}{N_k}
\right].
\tag{A16}
\]

Passing to the maximum over \(n\) decoupled columns costs at most another factor \(n^{1/q}=d\); independence of those maxima is not needed for this elementary \(L^q\) bound. Relative to the mean \(n/N_k\), the two errors are

\[
d\sqrt R\,3^k/\sqrt n=d^{-R/2+O(1)}\to0,
\tag{A17}
\]

and

\[
R d^2(2R)^k/n=d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}\to0.
\tag{A18}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbb E\max_j S(\widetilde X_j)
=(1+o(1))\frac n{N_k},
\qquad
\mathbb EL\lesssim\sqrt{n/N_k}.
\tag{A19}
\]

The correlation kernel is exactly

\[
G=\mathbb E_z[r_T(X,z)r_T(z,X)]
=\tau^{-2}\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_sK_s(X,X).
\tag{A20}
\]

By P22-A1,

\[
G\preceq \frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}R_T,
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}=\frac{\tau_k/\tau}{N_k}\le\frac1{N_k}.
\tag{A21}
\]

Let \(A=\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|\). Then

\[
\mathbb E\|G\|\le\frac{1+A}{N_k}.
\]

Putting (A19)–(A21) into the exact KRM architecture yields

\[
A\le C\left[
\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{N_k}(1+A)}
+\log n\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}
\right].
\tag{A22}
\]

Because \(n/N_k=d^{-\delta+o(1)}\) while \(\log n=\Theta(R^2)\), both small parameters vanish. Solving the scalar inequality gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|\to0.}
\tag{A23}
\]

Markov therefore gives \(\|R_T-I\|\to0\) in probability. This is the required full **infinite-tail operator-norm isotropy**.

## P22-A6 — Low-frequency empirical Gram concentration

**Status: PROVED.**

Let \(\Psi\in\mathbb R^{n\times D}\) collect all harmonics of degree \(<k\), with \(D=D_{<k}\). By the addition theorem every row has exactly

\[
\|\Psi(X_i)\|^2=D,
\]

and isotropy gives \(\mathbb E[\Psi(X_i)\Psi(X_i)^T]=I_D\). Matrix Chernoff/Bernstein therefore yields

\[
\left\|\frac1n\Psi^T\Psi-I_D\right\|\to0
\quad\text{in probability}
\tag{A24}
\]

because \((D/n)\log D\to0\) by (A13). This step is uniform at the growing cutoff \(k\).

## P22-A7 — Complete conditional risk calculation

**Status: PROVED.**

On the event

\[
\|T/\tau-I\|\le\eta_d,\qquad
\|\Psi^T\Psi/n-I\|\le\eta_d,
\qquad \eta_d\to0,
\]

where \(T=K_T(X,X)\), define \(W=\tau T^{-1}\). Then \(W=I+o_{op}(1)\).

Let \(\Lambda\) be the diagonal matrix of low Mercer eigenvalues. For any low coefficient vector \(a\), the minimum RKHS norm needed to interpolate labels \(y\) is exactly

\[
J(a)=a^T\Lambda^{-1}a+(y-\Psi a)^TT^{-1}(y-\Psi a).
\]

Hence the low coefficients of the minimum-norm interpolant satisfy

\[
\widehat a=G^{-1}\Psi^TWy,
\qquad
G=\tau\Lambda^{-1}+\Psi^TW\Psi.
\tag{A25}
\]

From (A24) and \(W=I+o(1)\), \(G\succeq c n I\) with probability tending to one.

### Noise contribution

For \(y=\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_n)\),

\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon\|\widehat a\|^2
=\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}
\left(G^{-1}\Psi^TW^2\Psi G^{-1}\right)
\le C\sigma^2\frac{D}{n}=o(1).
\tag{A26}
\]

Let \(r=\varepsilon-\Psi\widehat a\) be the tail residual. The operator \(I-\Psi G^{-1}\Psi^TW\) has bounded Euclidean norm on the same event, so

\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon\|r\|^2\le Cn\sigma^2.
\tag{A27}
\]

The population \(L^2\) energy of the corresponding tail interpolant is

\[
r^TT^{-1}H_TT^{-1}r
\le \lambda_k r^TT^{-1}r
\le C\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}\|r\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon\|\widehat f_{T,\mathrm{noise}}\|_{L^2}^2
\le C\sigma^2 n\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}
\le C\sigma^2\frac n{N_k}
=o(1).
\tag{A28}
\]

Combining (A26) and (A28), total noise-induced excess prediction risk vanishes.

### Fixed-degree signal contribution

Let the target degree be a fixed \(q_0\), and for all large \(d\), \(q_0<k\). Write the target as \(f=\Psi\theta\) with \(\|\theta\|\le B\) uniformly. From (A25),

\[
\widehat a_{\mathrm{sig}}-\theta
=-G^{-1}\tau\Lambda^{-1}\theta,
\]

so, since the low eigenvalues decrease with degree,

\[
\|\widehat a_{\mathrm{sig}}-\theta\|
\le C\frac{\tau}{n\lambda_{q_0}}B\to0.
\tag{A29}
\]

For fixed \(q_0\), \(\lambda_{q_0}\asymp d^{-q_0}\), while \(n=d^R\) and \(\tau\) is at most Poisson-tail size near \(k\sim R\), so the ratio in (A29) decays super-polynomially in the relevant scale.

The associated signal tail residual obeys

\[
\|r_{\mathrm{sig}}\|^2
\le Cn\|\widehat a_{\mathrm{sig}}-\theta\|^2,
\]

and therefore, using (A2), its population tail energy is at most

\[
C\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}\|r_{\mathrm{sig}}\|^2
\le C\frac{\lambda_k\tau}{n\lambda_{q_0}^2}B^2=o(1).
\tag{A30}
\]

Thus signal bias also vanishes.

### Convergence mode

Let \(\mathcal R(X,\varepsilon)\) be the test-distribution excess squared prediction risk of the learned interpolant. The preceding bounds imply that on design events of probability tending to one,

\[
\mathbb E_\varepsilon[\mathcal R(X,\varepsilon)\mid X]\to0.
\]

Markov in the training noise then yields

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R(X,\varepsilon)\xrightarrow{P}0.}
\tag{A31}
\]

This is exactly the requested in-probability benign-overfitting conclusion for every fixed-degree target sequence with uniformly bounded \(L^2\) norm.
