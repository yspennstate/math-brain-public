## 2. Exact model and harmonic normalization

Write
\[
\gamma=h^{-2}.
\]
On \(S^{d-1}\),
\[
K_h(x,y)
=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2h^2}\right)
=
e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma\langle x,y\rangle}.
\]

Let \(\mathcal H_{d,s}\) be the degree-\(s\) spherical-harmonic space and let
\[
N_s=\dim\mathcal H_{d,s}
=
\binom{d+s-1}s-\binom{d+s-3}{s-2}.
\]
If \(\{\psi_{s,a}\}_{a=1}^{N_s}\) is population-\(L^2\) orthonormal, define
\[
P_s(x,y)=\sum_{a=1}^{N_s}\psi_{s,a}(x)\psi_{s,a}(y),
\qquad
r_s=P_s/N_s.
\]
The addition theorem gives
\[
P_s(x,x)=N_s,\qquad r_s(x,x)=1.
\]
For \(s>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_Y r_s(x,Y)=0,
\]
and orthogonality gives
\[
\mathbb E_Y r_s(x,Y)r_t(Y,z)
=
\delta_{st}\frac{r_s(x,z)}{N_s}.
\]

## 3. P22-CHECK-01 — exact Gaussian-kernel Mercer eigenvalues

### Claim

The Mercer expansion
\[
K_h=\sum_{s\ge0}\lambda_{d,s}P_s
\]
has
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s}
=
e^{-\gamma}
\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Verification

Let \(H_s\) be the homogeneous harmonic extension of a degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic:
\[
H_s(ru)=r^sY_s(u).
\]
For \(G\sim N(0,I_d)\), harmonicity and the Gaussian shift identity give
\[
\mathbb E[
H_s(G)e^{t\langle x,G\rangle-t^2/2}
]
=
t^sH_s(x).
\]
Comparing the \(t^{s+2r}\) coefficients,
\[
\mathbb E[
\langle x,G\rangle^{s+2r}H_s(G)
]
=
\frac{(s+2r)!}{2^r r!}H_s(x).
\]
Writing \(G=RU\), with \(U\) uniform on the sphere and independent of \(R\),
\[
\mathbb ER^{2(s+r)}
=
d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2),
\]
which gives (3.1) after expanding \(e^{\gamma\langle x,U\rangle}\).

### Whole-tail monotonicity

For every summand,
\[
d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+1+r)-2)
\ge
(d+2s)\,
d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2).
\]
After matching the \(r\)-terms,
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}
\le
\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Thus the Mercer eigenvalues decrease through the entire active tail. This exact inequality is later used in two different places and must not be confused with the trace-mass ratio.

Set
\[
\tau_s=N_s\lambda_{d,s}.
\]
For \(s=o(\sqrt d)\),
\[
\boxed{
\tau_s
=
e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^s}{s!}
\left(1+O_h\!\left(\frac{s^2+1}{d}\right)\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

**Verdict P22-CHECK-01: PROVED.**

## 5. P22-CHECK-03 — growing-degree spherical hypercontractivity

For a degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic \(Y_s\), let \(H_s(ru)=r^sY_s(u)\). It is an exact degree-\(s\) Gaussian chaos. Gaussian OU hypercontractivity gives
\[
\|H_s(G)\|_{2q}
\le
(2q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\]
Because
\[
\|H_s(G)\|_p
=
\|R^s\|_p\,\|Y_s(U)\|_p
\]
and \(L^p\)-norms of \(R^s\) are increasing in \(p\),
\[
\frac{\|R^s\|_2}{\|R^s\|_{2q}}\le1.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_{2q}
\le
(2q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
There is no hidden dimension-dependent constant.

For fixed \(z\), the addition theorem gives
\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_2=N_s^{-1/2},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\|r_s(X,z)\|_{2q}
\le
\frac{(2q-1)^{s/2}}{\sqrt{N_s}}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the correct replacement for any fixed-degree Gegenbauer corollary whose constants are not uniform in \(s\).

**Verdict P22-CHECK-03: PROVED.**

## 6. P22-CHECK-04 — the entire infinite harmonic tail

Define
\[
K_T=\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_sP_s,
\qquad
\tau=K_T(x,x)=\sum_{s\ge k}\tau_s,
\qquad
r_T=K_T/\tau.
\]
Write
\[
r_T=\sum_{s\ge k}w_sr_s,\qquad
w_s=\tau_s/\tau.
\]

Positivity of the Taylor expansion gives
\[
\tau_s
\le
e^{-\gamma}\sum_{m\ge s}\frac{\gamma^m}{m!}.
\]
The \(r=0\) Mercer term and \(k=o(\sqrt d)\) give
\[
\tau_k\ge c_h e^{-\gamma}\frac{\gamma^k}{k!}.
\]
Consequently, for \(r\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau_{k+r}}{\tau_k}
\le
C_h\frac{\gamma^r k!}{(k+r)!}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At the active Rosenthal moment \(q=R\), the ratio of consecutive terms after multiplying by the hypercontractive growth is of size
\[
\frac{\gamma\sqrt{2R}}{k+r}
=
O(R^{-1/2}),
\]
uniformly for \(r\ge0\). Thus the infinite tail is genuinely first-degree dominated:
\[
\boxed{
\|r_T(X,z)\|_{2R}
\le
C_h\frac{(2R)^{k/2}}{\sqrt{N_k}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Orthogonality gives exactly
\[
\mathbb E r_T(X,z)^2
=
\sum_{s\ge k}\frac{w_s^2}{N_s}
=
\frac{1+o(1)}{N_k},
\tag{6.3}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\|r_T(X,z)^2\|_2
\le
C_h\frac{3^k}{N_k},

\|r_T(X,z)^2\|_R

C_h\frac{(2R)^k}{N_k}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 7. P22-CHECK-05 — Rosenthal plus KRM decoupling

For fixed \(z\), put
\[
S(z)=\sum_{i=1}^n r_T(X_i,z)^2.
\]
The summands are iid conditional on \(z\). For
\[
Y_i=r_T(X_i,z)^2-\mathbb E r_T(X_i,z)^2
\]
it is enough to use the deliberately conservative estimate
\[
\left\|\sum_iY_i\right\|_q
\le
Cq
\left[
\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
+
\left(\sum_i\|Y_i\|_q^q\right)^{1/q}
\right].
\]
With (6.4),
\[
\left\|S(z)-\mathbb ES(z)\right\|_q
\le
C_hq
\left[
\sqrt n\,\frac{3^k}{N_k}
+
n^{1/q}\frac{(2q)^k}{N_k}
\right].
\tag{7.1}
\]

Take \(q=R\). Since \(n=d^R\),
\[
n^{1/R}=d.
\]
For \(n\) decoupled test columns, the elementary maximum inequality gives one further factor \(n^{1/q}=d\); no independence among the resulting maxima is needed. Relative to the mean \(n/N_k\), the two errors are
\[
dR\,\frac{3^k}{\sqrt n}
=
d^{-R/2+O(1)}\to0,
\tag{7.2}
\]
and
\[
Rd^2\,\frac{(2R)^k}{n}
=
d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}\to0.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\max_{1\le j\le n}
\sum_{i=1}^n
r_T(X_i,\widetilde X_j)^2
=
(1+o(1))\frac n{N_k}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Exact KRM hypothesis check

The normalized tail kernel \(r_T\) is continuous, PSD, has unit diagonal, and is bounded in absolute value by one by the RKHS Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. It is centered because every degree in the tail is positive:
\[
\mathbb E_Zr_T(x,Z)=0.
\]
Thus KRM's iid/PSD/integrability requirements are satisfied.

Their proof-level \(L\) term is exactly controlled by (7.4), not by a maximum kernel entry. The correlation kernel is
\[
G
=
\tau^{-2}
\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X).
\]
By the exact eigenvalue monotonicity,
\[
G\preceq
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}R_T,
\qquad
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}
=
\frac{\tau_k/\tau}{N_k}
\le
\frac1{N_k}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Writing
\[
A=\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|,
\]
the KRM decoupling/NCK proof gives
\[
A
\le
C_h
\left[
\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{N_k}(1+A)}
+
\log n\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}
\right]
+o(1).
\tag{7.6}
\]
Since
\[
n/N_k=d^{-\delta+o(1)}
\]
and \(\log n=O(R^2)\),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|\to0.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

More quantitatively, for every fixed \(\delta'<\delta\),
\[
\mathbb E\|R_T-I\|
\le
C_hR^2d^{-\delta'/2}
\]
eventually, a summable sequence in \(\ell\).

**Verdict P22-CHECK-05: PROVED.**

## 8. P22-CHECK-06 — low block, exact variational decomposition, inverse control

Let \(\Psi\) collect all orthonormal harmonic coordinates of degrees \(s<k\). Its dimension is
\[
D=D_{<k}.
\]
The addition theorem gives deterministically
\[
\|\Psi(X_i)\|_2^2=D,
\]
and orthonormality gives
\[
\mathbb E[\Psi(X_i)\Psi(X_i)^\top]=I_D.
\]

Tropp's matrix Chernoff inequality therefore applies to the independent PSD matrices
\[
\Psi(X_i)\Psi(X_i)^\top,
\]
whose top eigenvalue is exactly \(D\), while the summed expectation is \(nI_D\). For fixed \(0<\eta<1\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(
\left\|\frac1n\Psi^\top\Psi-I_D\right\|>\eta
\right)
\le
2D e^{-c_\eta n/D}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Because \(D/n=d^{-(1-\delta)+o(1)}\), the failure probability is summable.

Let \(T=K_T(X,X)\), and let \(\Lambda\) be the diagonal matrix of low Mercer eigenvalues. For any low coefficient vector \(a\), the minimum possible squared RKHS norm of an interpolant whose low coefficients equal \(a\) is exactly
\[
\boxed{
J(a)
=
a^\top\Lambda^{-1}a
+
(y-\Psi a)^\top T^{-1}(y-\Psi a).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
No approximation is made here.

On
\[
\|T/\tau-I\|\le1/2,
\]
define
\[
W=\tau T^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac23I\preceq W\preceq2I.
\]
Differentiating (8.2) gives
\[
\boxed{
\widehat a
=
G_0^{-1}\Psi^\top Wy,
\qquad
G_0=\tau\Lambda^{-1}+\Psi^\top W\Psi.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
If also
\[
\|\Psi^\top\Psi/n-I\|\le1/2,
\]
then
\[
G_0\succeq(n/3)I,
\qquad
\boxed{\|G_0^{-1}\|\le3/n.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus no hard-edge theorem for the full \(n\times n\) Gaussian-kernel Gram matrix is required.

**Verdict P22-CHECK-06: PROVED.**

## 11. P22-C1 — NEW sharp uniform theorem for the entire low harmonic block

This run removes the previous report's open question about how far the signal degree can grow, at least for the natural **uniform bounded-\(L^2\) class**.

### Theorem P22-C1

Let
\[
k=R+\ell-1.
\]
Allow an arbitrary deterministic signal
\[
f_d\in\bigoplus_{s<k}\mathcal H_{d,s},
\qquad
\|f_d\|_2\le B,
\]
with no restriction on how its energy is distributed among the \(D_{<k}\) low coordinates. Then all conclusions of Target 2 remain valid, uniformly over this class:
\[
\mathcal R_\ell\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0,
\]
and in fact the same almost-sure strengthening holds along the prescribed sequence.

### Proof

Write \(f_d=\Psi\theta\), \(\|\theta\|\le B\). Because the Mercer eigenvalues decrease in \(s\),
\[
\|\Lambda^{-1}\theta\|
\le
\lambda_{k-1}^{-1}B.
\]
From the exact normal equation (10.1),
\[
\|\widehat a_{\rm sig}-\theta\|
\le
\frac{3B\tau}{n\lambda_{k-1}}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

The factorial tail bound (6.1) gives
\[
\tau\le C_h\tau_k=C_hN_k\lambda_k.
\tag{11.2}
\]
The exact one-step eigenvalue ratio (3.2) gives
\[
\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_{k-1}}
\le
\frac{\gamma}{d+2k-2}
\le
\frac{C_h}{d}.
\tag{11.3}
\]
Combining (11.1)–(11.3),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau}{n\lambda_{k-1}}
\le
C_h
\frac{N_k}{n}
\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_{k-1}}
\le
d^{-(1-\delta)+o(1)}.
}
\tag{11.4}
\]
Hence, uniformly over all \(\|\theta\|\le B\),
\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat a_{\rm sig}-\theta\|^2
\le
C_hB^2
d^{-2(1-\delta)+o(1)}.
}
\tag{11.5}
\]

For the tail leakage, the training residual is
\[
r_{\rm sig}
=
\Psi(\theta-\widehat a_{\rm sig}).
\]
On the low-Gram good event,
\[
\|r_{\rm sig}\|^2
\le

Using \(H_T\preceq\lambda_kT\),
\[

\le

\|\theta-\widehat a_{\rm sig}\|^2
\le
C\frac n{N_k}

\]
Since \(n/N_k=d^{-\delta+o(1)}\),
\[
\boxed{

\le
C_hB^2d^{-2+\delta+o(1)}.
}
\tag{11.6}
\]

Both exponents are strictly negative. Noise estimates are unchanged. Thus the conditional mean risk tends to zero uniformly over the entire low harmonic block.

### Sharp obstruction at degree \(k\)

At degree \(k\),
\[
N_k/n\to\infty.
\]
Take \(\theta\) uniformly on the unit coefficient sphere of \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\). Noiseless labels give at most \(n\) linear measurements of an \(N_k\)-dimensional vector. Conditional on the design, every estimator leaves the orthogonal complement of the sample row span unidentified, so
\[
\mathbb E_\theta
\|\widehat\theta-\theta\|^2
\ge
1-\frac{\operatorname{rank}\Phi_k}{N_k}
\ge
1-\frac n{N_k}
\to1.
\]
Projection of any function estimator onto \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\) can only decrease population \(L^2\) error. Averaging over \(\theta\) therefore produces deterministic unit-\(L^2\) degree-\(k\) signals with nonvanishing expected prediction error.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\text{all bounded-\(L^2\) signals supported in degrees }<k
\text{ are benign,}
}
\]
while
\[
\boxed{
\text{uniform benignity over the whole degree-}k
\text{ unit ball is impossible.}
}
\]

This is a sharp one-degree threshold for the natural uniform signal class.
