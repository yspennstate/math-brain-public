### 2.1 Kaushik–Romberg–Muthukumar (2025)

Primary source:

Chiraag Kaushik, Justin Romberg, Vidya Muthukumar,  
*A general technique for approximating high-dimensional empirical kernel matrices*,  
arXiv:2511.03892v1, 5 Nov 2025.

I checked the actual proof of Theorem 1, not merely the theorem statement.

The theorem assumes iid inputs and a PSD kernel with the displayed integrability. The proof:

1. separates the diagonal and off-diagonal kernel matrix;
2. decouples the off-diagonal U-statistic;
3. conditions on the original sample, after which the decoupled column matrices $Z_j$ are independent;
4. applies the noncommutative Khintchine/matrix-moment inequality in the form
   \[
   \sqrt{\log n\,V}+\log n\,L;
   \]
5. defines
   \[
   L^2
   =
   \mathbb E_{\widetilde X}
   \max_j
   \|Z_j-\mathbb E_{\widetilde X}Z_j\|^2;
   \]
6. before making its later crude maximum-entry relaxation, proves the stronger row-square estimate
   \[
   \boxed{
   L^2
   \lesssim
   \mathbb E_{\widetilde X}
   \max_j
   \sum_i
   \bigl(
   k(X_i,\widetilde X_j)
   -
   \mathbb E_{\widetilde X}k(X_i,\widetilde X_j)
   \bigr)^2.
   }
   \tag{KRM-L}
   \]

This is exactly the proof-level quantity P04/P22 use.

For the normalized P22 tail kernel $r_T$:

- $r_T(x,x)=1$;

  \[
  \mathbb E_Yr_T(x,Y)=0.

Therefore all required KRM integrability/centering properties hold.
