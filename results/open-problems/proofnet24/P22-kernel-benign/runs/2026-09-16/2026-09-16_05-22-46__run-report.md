\[
K_h(x,y)=e^{-a}e^{a\langle x,y\rangle}.

\[

=e^{-a}\Gamma(d/2)(2/a)^{d/2-1}I_{s+d/2-1}(a).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{a^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Primary source checked again: NIST DLMF 10.25.2,

`https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25.E2`,

which states

\[
I_\nu(z)=(z/2)^\nu\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{(z^2/4)^r}{r!\Gamma(\nu+r+1)}.
\]

Here `a>0` and `nu=s+d/2-1` is real, so every term is positive. Comparing corresponding terms at degrees `s+1` and `s` gives, for **every** `s>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}
\le \frac a{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The exact multiplicity ratio is

\[
\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
=\frac{2s+d}{2s+d-2}\frac{s+d-2}{s+1}.

Multiplying by (4.3), with `d+2s=2s+d`, yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{N_{d,s+1}\lambda_{d,s+1}}
{N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}}

\tag{4.4}
\]

\[

\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.5}

Likewise for squared Mercer mass

\[

# 5. Growing-degree hypercontractivity and Rosenthal budget

## 5.1 Dimension-free degree-explicit hypercontractivity

For a pure degree-`s` spherical harmonic `Y_s`, let

\[
H_s(z)=\|z\|^sY_s(z/\|z\|)
\]

be its homogeneous harmonic extension. For a standard Gaussian `G=R_gU`, `U` is uniform on the sphere and independent of the radius `R_g`, while `H_s(G)` lies in homogeneous Gaussian chaos degree `s`.

The Gaussian Ornstein-Uhlenbeck hypercontractive criterion is

\[
\|P_tF\|_q\le\|F\|_p
\quad\text{when}\quad
q-1\le e^{2t}(p-1).
\]

For homogeneous chaos degree `s`, `P_tH_s=e^{-st}H_s`. Taking `p=2` and `e^{2t}=q-1` gives

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\]

Radial independence gives

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_q=(ER_g^{sq})^{1/q}\|Y_s\|_q,
\]

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_2=(ER_g^{2s})^{1/2}\|Y_s\|_2.
\]

Because `q>=2`, monotonicity of radial `L^p` norms gives

\[
(ER_g^{2s})^{1/2}/(ER_g^{sq})^{1/q}\le1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

There is no hidden dimension factor and no hidden `C_s`.

Source check: Gross's dimension-free Gaussian log-Sobolev/hypercontractivity theorem is the classical input; the exact criterion above is also recorded in modern expositions. The historical Gross/Nelson primary article was not line-inspected in this runtime. I therefore do **not** claim a fictitious historical-page verification. This source-access qualification does not change the mathematical statement, whose exact constant and dimension independence are explicit in the criterion used here.

\qquad L=\Psi C,
\qquad H=I-L,

\]

Then the exact zero-signal prediction risk is

\[
\mathcal R_{0,\ell}
=\varepsilon^TM\varepsilon,
\qquad

\tag{8.1}
\]

The previous sharp-noise derivation, independently rechecked in the preceding P22 checkpoint, gives on the good event

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}M
=\frac n{N_{d,k}}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
\|M\|_{op}=O(n^{-1}).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

The first identity uses (4.7), the exact trace `Tr S=n zeta`, the PSD inequality `S\preceq\lambda_{d,k}T`, and the fact that the low correction has rank at most `D_{<k}`.

## 8.2 No single noise direction controls the variance

Let the eigenvalues of `M` be

\[
\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\ge0.
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz gives the deterministic lower bound

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)
=\sum_j\mu_j^2
\ge\frac{(\sum_j\mu_j)^2}{n}
=\frac{(\operatorname{Tr}M)^2}{n}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Combining (8.2)--(8.3),

\[
\frac{\max_j\mu_j}{\sqrt{2\sum_j\mu_j^2}}
\le
C\frac{N_{d,k}}{n^{3/2}}
=d^{-R/2+\delta_*+o(1)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the standardized quadratic form has vanishing maximal weight. This is much stronger than merely having `Tr(M^2)/(Tr M)^2 ->0`.

## 8.3 Self-contained characteristic-function calculation

Condition on a design in the good event and diagonalize `M`. Write

\[
\varepsilon=\sigma U\xi,
\qquad \xi_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\]

in an eigenbasis of `M`. Then

\[
Z_0
:=
\frac{\mathcal R_{0,\ell}-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M}
{\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}}
=\sum_{j=1}^n b_j(\xi_j^2-1),
\]

where

\[
b_j=\frac{\mu_j}{\sqrt{2\sum_i\mu_i^2}},
\qquad
\sum_jb_j^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\max_j|b_j|\to0.
\tag{8.5}
\]

For fixed real `t`, the one-coordinate characteristic function is exact:

\[
E\exp(itb(\xi^2-1))
=e^{-itb}(1-2itb)^{-1/2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\log E e^{itZ_0}
=\sum_j\left[-itb_j-\frac12\log(1-2itb_j)\right].
\]

For sufficiently large `d`, (8.4) makes `2|t|\max|b_j|<1/2`, and Taylor expansion is uniform:

\[
-itb-\frac12\log(1-2itb)
=-t^2b^2+O_t(|b|^3).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log E e^{itZ_0}
=-t^2\sum_jb_j^2
+O_t\left(\sum_j|b_j|^3\right)
=-\frac{t^2}{2}+o(1),
\]

because

\[
\sum_j|b_j|^3
\le(\max_j|b_j|)\sum_jb_j^2\to0.
\]

Thus, uniformly on the good-design event,

\[
E_\varepsilon[e^{itZ_0}\mid X]\to e^{-t^2/2}
\]

for every fixed `t`. By the standard characteristic-function continuity theorem, the conditional law converges weakly to `N(0,1)`. Since the good event has probability tending to one, the conditional convergence holds in probability over the design.

No Berry-Esseen theorem, fourth-moment theorem, or extra random-matrix input is imported here.

## 8.4 Uniform fixed-degree signal perturbation is negligible at CLT scale

For an allowed deterministic signal `f`, linearity of kernel interpolation gives in population `L^2`

\[
\widehat f(f+\varepsilon)-f
=e_{sig}(f)+e_{noise}(\varepsilon).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal R_\ell(f)
=\mathcal R_{0,\ell}
+S_f
+2\langle e_{sig}(f),e_{noise}(\varepsilon)\rangle,
\tag{8.6}
\]

where

\[
S_f=\|e_{sig}(f)\|_2^2.
\]

By (7.4) and (7.7), uniformly over the fixed-degree `L^2` ball,

\[
S_f\le S_d
:=CB^2d^{2q_0}\left(\frac1{n^2}+\frac1{nN_{d,k}}\right).
\tag{8.7}
\]

Let

\[
v_d(X)=\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}.
\]

From (8.3) and (8.2),

\[
v_d(X)\ge c\sigma^2\frac{\sqrt n}{N_{d,k}}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

The deterministic shift satisfies

\[
\sup_f\frac{S_f}{v_d}
\le C\left[
\frac{d^{2q_0}N_{d,k}}{n^{5/2}}
+\frac{d^{2q_0}}{n^{3/2}}
\right]\to0.
\tag{8.9}
\]

For the cross term, conditional Cauchy-Schwarz in the Gaussian noise gives

\[
E_\varepsilon
|2\langle e_{sig}(f),e_{noise}\rangle|
\le2\sqrt{S_f\,E_\varepsilon\mathcal R_{0,\ell}}
=2\sqrt{S_f\,\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M}.
\]

Using (8.3),

\[
\sup_f
E_\varepsilon\left[
\frac{|2\langle e_{sig}(f),e_{noise}\rangle|}{v_d}
\middle|X\right]
\le\frac C\sigma\sqrt{S_dN_{d,k}}.
\tag{8.10}
\]

The two terms in the square root are

\[
\frac{d^{q_0}\sqrt{N_{d,k}}}{n}
=d^{q_0-R/2+\delta_*/2+o(1)}\to0,
\]

and

\[
\frac{d^{q_0}}{\sqrt n}=d^{q_0-R/2}\to0.
\]

So the complete signal perturbation in (8.6) is `o(1)` in conditional `L^1` after normalization by `v_d`, uniformly over the allowed fixed-degree ball.

Consequently, for every bounded 1-Lipschitz test function `phi`, if `G~N(0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{f\in V_{\le q_0}\\\|f\|_2\le B}}
\left|
E_\varepsilon\left[
\phi\!\left(
\frac{\mathcal R_\ell(f)-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M}
{\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}}
\right)
\middle|X\right]
-E\phi(G)
\right|
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

This proves `P22-T2-QUENCHED-SELFNORMALIZED-CLT-42`.

### Why the center is not simplified

The ratio-one theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}M=(n/N_{d,k})(1+o(1)),
\]

but the CLT scale is at most microscopic relative to the mean. The low trace correction alone is of order `D_{<k}/n`, which is negligible relative to `n/N_k` but can be enormous relative to `sqrt{Tr(M^2)}`. Therefore replacing the exact conditional center by `sigma^2 n/N_k` is **not justified** by the present estimates. Any deterministic-centering CLT would require a substantially sharper trace expansion.

This is a concrete nonuniformity certificate, not an open-ended caveat.
