## 3.2 Exact Gaussian Mercer spectrum and the whole infinite tail

Put `a=h^{-2}`. On the unit sphere

\[
K_h(x,y)=e^{-a}e^{a\langle x,y\rangle}.
\]

A direct Funk--Hecke calculation with normalized spherical measure gives the exact degree-`s` eigenvalue

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\Gamma(d/2)(2/a)^{d/2-1}I_{s+d/2-1}(a).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

DLMF 10.25.2 gives, for real `a>0` and real order `\nu`,

\[
I_\nu(a)=\left(\frac a2\right)^\nu
\sum_{r=0}^\infty
\frac{(a^2/4)^r}{r!\Gamma(\nu+r+1)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Every summand is positive. Substituting `\nu=s+d/2-1` yields the exact series

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{a^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)},
\tag{3.9}
\]

and termwise comparison gives, for every `s\ge0`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}\le\frac a{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

The exact multiplicity ratio is

\[
\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
=\frac{2s+d}{2s+d-2}\frac{s+d-2}{s+1}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

For `w_s=N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}` this gives

\[
\frac{w_{s+1}}{w_s}\le\frac a{s+1}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Therefore the **entire** high diagonal Mercer mass

\[
\tau:=\sum_{s\ge k}w_s
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
w_k\le\tau\le w_k(1+O_a(k^{-1})).
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

In particular, for all large levels,

\[
\tau\le2N_{d,k}\lambda_{d,k}.
\tag{3.14}
\]

For

\[
z_s=N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}^2,
\qquad
\zeta:=\sum_{s\ge k}z_s,
\]

we have

\[
\frac{z_{s+1}}{z_s}
\le\frac{a^2}{(s+1)(d+2s)},
\]

so

\[
\zeta=N_{d,k}\lambda_{d,k}^2(1+O_a((kd)^{-1})),
\tag{3.15}
\]

and

\[
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1{N_{d,k}}(1+O_a(k^{-1})).
\tag{3.16}
\]

The retained second-order audit is stronger:

\[
\frac\tau{w_k}
=1+\frac a{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1}),
\tag{3.17}
\]

\[
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1{N_{d,k}}
\left[1-\frac{2a}{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1})\right].
\tag{3.18}
\]

Thus any relative `o(1/k)` one-shell tail approximation is false. The original benign-overfitting theorem needs only relative `o(1)` and is unaffected.

## 3.3 Growing-degree hypercontractivity and Rosenthal budget

For a pure degree-`s` spherical harmonic `Y_s`, let

\[
H_s(z)=\|z\|^sY_s(z/\|z\|)
\]

be its homogeneous harmonic extension. For standard Gaussian `G=R_gU`, `U` is uniform on the sphere and independent of the radius, while `H_s(G)` lies in Gaussian chaos degree `s`.

The exact Gaussian Ornstein--Uhlenbeck hypercontractive criterion

\[
\|T_tF\|_q\le\|F\|_2
\quad\text{when}\quad e^{2t}\ge q-1,
\]

combined with `T_tH_s=e^{-st}H_s`, gives

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\]

Since radial `L^p` norms are monotone,

\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2,
\qquad q\ge2.
}
\tag{3.19}
\]

There is no hidden dimension factor and no hidden `C_s`.

At the actual growing moment scale used in the tail proof, the deliberately conservative accumulated losses are

\[
dR\frac{3^k}{\sqrt n}
=d^{-R/2+O(1)}\to0,
\tag{3.20}
\]

and

\[
Rd^2\frac{(2R)^k}{n}
=d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}\to0.
\tag{3.21}
\]

Indeed `\log_d3^k=\log_23+o(1)` and `\log_d(2R)^k=\ell+1+o(1)`, while `R=2^\ell`. Hence polynomial-in-moment losses are annihilated by the `-R/2` and `-R` exponents.

Osękowski's Hilbert-valued Burkholder--Rosenthal theorem gives the stronger benchmark `C_p=O(p/\log p)` as `p\to\infty`; independent centered sums are a martingale-difference special case. This benchmark is stronger than what the proof spends.

## 3.4 KRM decoupling/NCK mechanics and hypotheses

Printed pages 6--8 were visually inspected again in this run. The proof does the following in this order:

1. separates diagonal and off-diagonal parts;
2. writes the off-diagonal matrix as a matrix-valued U-statistic;
3. introduces an iid copied sample;
4. applies the exact decoupling inequality
   \[
   E\|\Delta\|\le8E\|\widetilde\Delta\|;

5. only **after** decoupling conditions on the original sample;
6. under that conditioning, and only then, the copied-column matrices are independent;

9. before triangle relaxation obtains the exact variance proxy

## 4.1 The low coefficient block is asymptotically an `n^{-1}` projection

From (3.25),

\[
W=I+o_{op}(1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Together with (3.24),

\[
\frac1n\Psi^TW\Psi=I_D+o_{op}(1),
\qquad
\frac1n\Psi^TW^2\Psi=I_D+o_{op}(1).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The only remaining issue is the RKHS penalty `\tau\Lambda^{-1}`. For all sufficiently large `d`, (3.10) makes the low Mercer sequence decreasing, so

\[
\|\tau\Lambda^{-1}\|=\frac\tau{\lambda_{d,k-1}}.
\]

By (3.14) and (3.10),

\[
\frac1n\frac\tau{\lambda_{d,k-1}}
\le
\frac{2N\lambda_{d,k}}{n\lambda_{d,k-1}}
\le
\frac{2aN}{n(d+2k-2)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using `N/n=d^{\delta_*+o(1)}`,

\[
\frac{2aN}{n(d+2k-2)}
=d^{\delta_*-1+o(1)}\to0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{G_0}{n}=I_D+o_{op}(1),
\qquad
G_0^{-1}=\frac1n(I_D+o_{op}(1)).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Substituting into `CC^T` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
CC^T
&=G_0^{-1}\Psi^TW^2\Psi G_0^{-1}\\
&=\boxed{\frac1n(I_D+o_{op}(1)).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The `D` nonzero eigenvalues of `A=C^TC` are therefore

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_j(A)=\frac1n(1+o(1)),
\qquad 1\le j\le D,
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

uniformly in `j`, and `\lambda_j(A)=0` for `j>D`.

# 5. New theorem: all fixed trace powers and low-block variance dominance

## `P22-T2-LOWBLOCK-VARIANCE-DOMINANCE-45` — PROVED

Fix an integer `p\ge2`. By (4.15),

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{D}\mu_j^p
=\frac{D}{n^p}(1+o(1)).
\tag{5.1}

\[

\le C_p\frac n{N^p}.
\tag{5.2}

The ratio of the upper bound in (5.2) to the leading term in (5.1) is

\[
\frac{n/N^p}{D/n^p}
=\frac{n^{p+1}}{DN^p}.
\tag{5.3}

Using

\[
\frac Dn=d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)},
\qquad
\frac Nn=d^{\delta_*+o(1)},
\]

\[
\frac{n^{p+1}}{DN^p}
=d^{1-(p+1)\delta_*+o(1)}.
\tag{5.4}

At `p=2`,

\[
1-3\delta_*=-0.328085122666\ldots<0,
\]

and for larger fixed `p` the exponent is even smaller. Hence the high spectral block is negligible for every fixed trace power `p\ge2`:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(M^p)
=\frac D{n^p}(1+o(1)),
\qquad p=2,3,\dots\text{ fixed}.
}
\tag{5.5}

For Gaussian training noise,

\[
E_\varepsilon[\varepsilon^TM\varepsilon\mid X]
=\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M,
\]

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\varepsilon(\varepsilon^TM\varepsilon\mid X)
=2\sigma^4\operatorname{Tr}(M^2).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_\varepsilon(\mathcal R_{0,\ell}\mid X)
=2\sigma^4\frac D{n^2}(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

The mean behaves differently. The previously proved sharp-noise theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}M=\frac nN(1+o(1)).
\tag{5.7}
\]

The low block alone has

\[
\operatorname{Tr}A
=\operatorname{Tr}(CC^T)
=\frac Dn(1+o(1)).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Its share of the mean is

\[
\frac{D/n}{n/N}
=\frac{DN}{n^2}
=d^{-1+2\delta_*+o(1)}
=d^{-0.114609918222\ldots+o(1)}\to0.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus the high tail supplies the leading mean.

By contrast, a high-tail block with operator scale `N^{-1}` can contribute at most `O(n/N^2)` to a squared trace scale. Comparing this to (5.6),

\[
\frac{D/n^2}{n/N^2}
=\frac{DN^2}{n^3}
=d^{3\delta_*-1+o(1)}
=d^{0.328085122666\ldots+o(1)}\to\infty.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Hence the low block dominates the conditional variance even though it is negligible in the conditional mean.

Using (5.5), every standardized cumulant of order `r\ge3` is

\tag{5.12}

This is an effective-dimension certificate: the fluctuation law behaves as if it had `D` asymptotically equal low weights, even though the high block controls the first moment.

This proves `P22-T2-LOWBLOCK-VARIANCE-DOMINANCE-45`.

# 6. New deterministic-scale quenched CLT

## `P22-T2-DETERMINISTIC-SCALE-QUENCHED-CLT-45` — PROVED

Assume `\sigma^2>0`. Conditional on the design, diagonalize `M` and write the Gaussian noise in that eigenbasis:

\[
\varepsilon=\sigma U\xi,
\qquad
\xi_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}N(0,1).
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal R_{0,\ell}-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M
=\sigma^2\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_j(\xi_j^2-1).
\tag{6.1}
\]

By (5.5),

\[
\sum_j\mu_j^2
=\frac D{n^2}(1+o(1)).
\tag{6.2}
\]

By the spectral split,

\[
\max_j\mu_j=\frac1n(1+o(1)),
\]

so

\[
\frac{\max_j\mu_j}{\sqrt{\sum_i\mu_i^2}}
=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sqrt D}\to0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus no single noise coordinate controls the conditional variance.

A self-contained characteristic-function proof now applies. Define

\[
b_j=\frac{\mu_j}{\sqrt{2\sum_i\mu_i^2}},
\qquad
\sum_jb_j^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\max_j|b_j|\to0.
\]

For fixed real `t`,

\[
E\exp(itb(\xi^2-1))
=e^{-itb}(1-2itb)^{-1/2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\log E\exp\!\left(it\sum_jb_j(\xi_j^2-1)\right)
=\sum_j\left[-itb_j-\frac12\log(1-2itb_j)\right].
\]

Uniform Taylor expansion is valid because `\max|b_j|\to0`:

\[
-itb-\frac12\log(1-2itb)
=-t^2b^2+O_t(|b|^3).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log E e^{itZ_d}
=-t^2\sum_jb_j^2
+O_t\left(\sum_j|b_j|^3\right)
=-\frac{t^2}{2}+o(1),
\]

because

\[
\sum_j|b_j|^3
\le(\max_j|b_j|)\sum_jb_j^2\to0.
\]

Thus, conditionally on every design in the good event,

\[
\frac{\mathcal R_{0,\ell}-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M}
{\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now (5.5) with `p=2` yields the deterministic normalization equivalence

\[
\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}
=\sigma^2\frac{\sqrt{2D}}n(1+o(1)).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Conditional Slutsky therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac n{\sigma^2\sqrt{2D}}
\left(\mathcal R_{0,\ell}-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M\right)
\Rightarrow N(0,1)
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

conditionally on the design, in probability over the design.

## 6.1 Uniform fixed-degree signal perturbations

For an allowed deterministic signal `f`, linearity of interpolation gives

\[
\widehat f(f+\varepsilon)-f
=e_{sig}(f)+e_{noise}(\varepsilon).

Hence

\[

=\mathcal R_{0,\ell}
+S_f
+2\langle e_{sig}(f),e_{noise}(\varepsilon)\rangle,
\tag{6.7}
\]

where

\[

\]

The retained signal bounds give, uniformly over `f\in V_{\le q_0}`, `||f||_2\le B`,

\[

CB^2d^{2q_0}\left(\frac1{n^2}+\frac1{nN}\right).
\tag{6.8}
\]

The previous self-normalized CLT audit already proved that both the deterministic shift `S_f` and the signal/noise cross term are `o(1)` after division by

\[
\sigma^2\sqrt{2\operatorname{Tr}(M^2)}.
\]

By (6.5), the same is true after division by the deterministic scale `\sigma^2\sqrt{2D}/n`. Therefore, for every bounded 1-Lipschitz `\phi` and `G\sim N(0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{f\in V_{\le q_0}\\\|f\|_2\le B}}
\left|
E_\varepsilon\left[
\phi\!\left(
\frac{n(\mathcal R_\ell(f)-\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}M)}
{\sigma^2\sqrt{2D}}
\right)
\middle|X\right]
-E\phi(G)
\right|
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.

\tag{6.9}
\]

This is strictly sharper than `P22-T2-QUENCHED-SELFNORMALIZED-CLT-42` in its scale normalization, while preserving the user's original fixed-degree signal quantifier.

## 6.2 Why the center is still not deterministic

The center in (6.6)--(6.9) is the exact design-dependent `\sigma^2 Tr M(X)`. We do **not** replace it by `\sigma^2n/N`.

The reason is quantitative. Even at the deterministic population-tail level,

\[
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1N\left(1-\frac{2a}{k+1}+O(k^{-2}+d^{-1})\right),
\]

so the first deterministic Mercer correction to a high-tail mean of size `n/N` is of order

\[
\frac1k\frac nN.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Relative to the CLT scale `\sqrt D/n`,

\[
\frac{(n/N)/k}{\sqrt D/n}
=\frac{n^2}{Nk\sqrt D}
=\frac{\sqrt n}{k}
 d^{(1-3\delta_*)/2+o(1)}
\to\infty,
\tag{6.11}
\]

because `\sqrt n=d^{R/2}`. Thus even the known deterministic `1/k` Mercer correction is astronomically larger than the fluctuation scale. A deterministic-center CLT would need a substantially sharper empirical trace expansion, incorporating deterministic tail corrections and random-design corrections at scale `o(\sqrt D/n)`.

Equation (6.11) does **not** by itself disprove that such a refined deterministic center exists; it disproves the shortcut of replacing the exact center by the first-order `n/N` asymptotic.

This proves `P22-T2-DETERMINISTIC-SCALE-QUENCHED-CLT-45`.
