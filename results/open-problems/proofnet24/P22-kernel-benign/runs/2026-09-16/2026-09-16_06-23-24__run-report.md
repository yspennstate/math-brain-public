## 4.1 Uniform expansion of the exact Bessel series

Define

\[
D_s:=\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(d+2j).
\]

Factor the `r=0` term from (3.8):

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\frac{a^s}{D_s}Q_s,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
Q_s
:=\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{a^{2r}}
{2^r r!\prod_{j=s}^{s+r-1}(d+2j)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every `s>=0`, the `r=1` term is

\[
\frac{a^2}{2(d+2s)}.
\]

For `r>=2`, since every denominator factor is at least `d`,

\[
0\le\sum_{r\ge2}
\frac{a^{2r}}
{2^r r!\prod_{j=s}^{s+r-1}(d+2j)}
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{a^2}{2d}\right)
-1-\frac{a^2}{2d}
=O_a(d^{-2}).
\]

Hence, uniformly in all `s>=0`, and in particular at `s=O(log d)`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_s=1+\frac{a^2}{2(d+2s)}+O_a(d^{-2}).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The difference between the displayed first corrections at `s+1` and `s` is itself `O_a(d^{-2})`, so

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_{s+1}}{Q_s}=1+O_a(d^{-2}).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining (4.1) and (4.4),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda_{d,s+1}}{\lambda_{d,s}}
=\frac a{d+2s}\left[1+O_a(d^{-2})\right]
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

uniformly for the actual growing range.

=\frac{a}{s+1}

\left[1+O_a(d^{-2})\right].
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

\[
\frac{d+k-2}{d+2k-2}

\]

so

\[
\boxed{

}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The exact global bound `rho_s<=a/(s+1)` from (3.11) controls all later products. Therefore

\[
\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{w_{k+j}}{w_k}

Equations (4.7)--(4.8) prove

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau}{w_k}

}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\tau=w_k+w_{k+1}+O_a(w_k/k^2),
\qquad
\frac{\tau-w_k}{w_{k+1}}\to1.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus the statement `tau/w_k=1+o(1/k)` is false for every fixed `a>0`: the relative `1/k` correction is real and equals `a/(k+1)` to first order.

## 4.3 Squared Mercer mass and effective dimension

Let

\[
\theta_s:=\frac{z_{s+1}}{z_s}.
\]

Using (3.10) and (4.5),

\[
\boxed{
\theta_s
=\frac{a^2(d+s-2)}
{(s+1)(d+2s-2)(d+2s)}
\left[1+O_a(d^{-2})\right].
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

At `s=k`,

\[
\theta_k
=\frac{a^2}{(k+1)d}\left[1+O(k/d)\right].
\tag{4.12}
\]

The global squared-shell bound from Section 3 shows the remaining terms after `z_{k+1}` total only `O_a(k^{-2}d^{-2})z_k`. Consequently

\[
\frac\zeta{z_k}=1+O_a((kd)^{-1}).
\tag{4.13}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{z_k}{w_k^2}=\frac1{N_{d,k}},
\]

and (4.9) implies

\[
\left(\frac{w_k}{\tau}\right)^2
=1-\frac{2a}{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1}),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1{N_{d,k}}
\left[1-\frac{2a}{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1})\right].
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Inverting gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau^2}{\zeta}
=N_{d,k}
\left[1+\frac{2a}{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1})\right].
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

### Consequence and limitation

The leading Target-2 theorem and the ratio-one noise theorem only need `zeta/tau^2=(1/N_k)(1+o(1))`, so (4.14) strengthens rather than threatens them.

However, a deterministic-centering fluctuation theorem cannot be obtained merely by replacing `zeta/tau^2` with `1/N_k` to `o(1/k)` precision; (4.14) explicitly shows a relative `-2a/(k+1)` correction. Moreover the empirical high-tail scalarization currently has only `o_op(1)` relative control, not `o(1/k)` or CLT-scale control. Therefore I do **not** claim that the full conditional mean risk itself has (4.14) as its complete second-order expansion. The deterministic-center CLT remains OPEN.

The diagonal traceless basis commutes with `B`, so summing over a complete orthonormal basis of `E` gives

\sum_a\|[E_a,B]\|_F^2

\tag{8.5}
