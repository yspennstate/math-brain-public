\[

\tag{3.2}

N_{d,s}=\frac{d^s}{s!}\exp(O(s^2/d)).

and because the final shell dominates the cumulative low dimension,

\[

\frac{D_{<k}}n

## 3.2 Exact Gaussian Mercer spectrum and whole infinite tail

Put `a=h^{-2}`. On the unit sphere,

\[
K_h(x,y)=e^{-a}e^{a\langle x,y\rangle}.
\]

With normalized spherical measure, the exact degree-`s` Mercer eigenvalue is

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\Gamma(d/2)(2/a)^{d/2-1}I_{s+d/2-1}(a).
\tag{3.7}
\]

DLMF 10.25.2 gives the exact positive modified-Bessel series, yielding

\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=e^{-a}\sum_{r\ge0}
\frac{a^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Termwise positivity gives, for every `s\ge0`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}\le\frac a{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

The exact multiplicity ratio is

\[
\frac{N_{d,s+1}}{N_{d,s}}
=\frac{2s+d}{2s+d-2}\frac{s+d-2}{s+1}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Hence for `w_s=N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}`,

\[
\frac{w_{s+1}}{w_s}
\le\frac{a(s+d-2)}{(s+1)(2s+d-2)}
\le\frac a{s+1}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Therefore the **entire** high diagonal Mercer mass

\[
\tau=\sum_{s\ge k}w_s
\]

obeys

\[
w_k\le\tau\le w_k(1+O_a(k^{-1})).
\tag{3.12}
\]

For `z_s=N_{d,s}\lambda_{d,s}^2`, similarly

\[
\frac{z_{s+1}}{z_s}
\le\frac{a^2}{(s+1)(d+2s)},
\]

so

\[
\zeta:=\sum_{s\ge k}z_s
=N_{d,k}\lambda_{d,k}^2(1+O_a((kd)^{-1})),
\tag{3.13}
\]

and

\[
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1{N_{d,k}}(1+O_a(k^{-1})).
\tag{3.14}
\]

The previous P22 run sharpened this to

\[
\frac\tau{w_k}
=1+\frac a{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1}),
\tag{3.15}
\]

\[
\frac\zeta{\tau^2}
=\frac1{N_{d,k}}
\left[1-\frac{2a}{k+1}+O_a(k^{-2}+d^{-1})\right].
\tag{3.16}
\]

Thus the full infinite Gaussian tail is controlled and the first omitted shell is known not to be `o(1/k)` relative to the leading shell.

## 3.3 Growing-degree hypercontractivity and moment budget

For a pure degree-`s` spherical harmonic `Y_s`, let

\[
H_s(z)=\|z\|^sY_s(z/\|z\|)
\]

be its homogeneous harmonic extension. For a standard Gaussian `G=R_gU`, `U` is uniform on the sphere and independent of the radius. `H_s(G)` belongs to homogeneous Gaussian chaos degree `s`.

The Gaussian OU hypercontractive criterion says that for `q\ge2`, choosing `e^{2t}=q-1` gives

\[
\|H_s(G)\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|H_s(G)\|_2.
\]

Since radial `L^p` norms are monotone,

\[
\boxed{
\|Y_s\|_q\le(q-1)^{s/2}\|Y_s\|_2.
}
\tag{3.17}
\]

The constant is dimension-free and the degree dependence is explicit rather than hidden in `C_s`.

At the actual growing moment scale the conservative accumulated terms retained in the KRM-tail proof satisfy

\[
dR\frac{3^k}{\sqrt n}
=d^{-R/2+O(1)}\to0,
\tag{3.18}
\]

and

\[
Rd^2\frac{(2R)^k}{n}
=d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}\to0.
\tag{3.19}
\]

Indeed `\log_d3^k=\log_23+o(1)` and `\log_d(2R)^k=\ell+1+o(1)`, while `R=2^\ell`. Thus polynomial-in-moment Rosenthal losses are annihilated by the negative `-R/2` and `-R` exponents. Osękowski's Hilbert-valued Burkholder--Rosenthal result, used only as a stronger uniformity benchmark, has `C_p=O(p/\log p)` as `p\to\infty`; independent sums are a martingale-difference special case.

P20 claim audited: `P20-ONEDELETE-SPHERE-CROSS-58` from

`proofnet24/P20-kernel-hard-edge/runs/2026-09-16/2026-09-16_06-10-53__run-report.md`.

For an orthogonal projector `P` on `E=\operatorname{Sym}_d^0`, define

\[
\phi(y)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{tr}(y^3)/\|y\|_F^2,&y\ne0,\\
0,&y=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `y\ne0`, differentiation in `E` gives

\[
\nabla\phi(y)
=
\frac{3\Pi_E(y^2)}{\|y\|^2}
-
\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(y^3)y}{\|y\|^4}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `\|\Pi_E(y^2)\|_F\le\|y\|_F^2` and `|\operatorname{tr}(y^3)|\le\|y\|_F^3`,

\[
\|\nabla\phi(y)\|_F\le5.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The homogeneous degree-one bound `|\phi(y)|\le\|y\|` makes the extension at zero continuous, and (5.3) gives global `5`-Lipschitz control.

For a unit tangent vector `\xi\perp U`,

\[
D_\xi q(U)=U\xi^T+\xi U^T,
\qquad
\|D_\xi q(U)\|_F=\sqrt2,
\]

hence

\[
\|\nabla_S[\phi(Pq(U))]\|\le5\sqrt2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For deterministic traceless symmetric `a`,

\[
c_a(U)=\langle a,q(U)\rangle=U^TaU
\]

is a degree-two spherical harmonic, so

\[
-\Delta_Sc_a=2d\,c_a.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because `E[q\otimes q]=c_d^{-2}I_E`,

\[
Ec_a^2=\frac{\|a\|_F^2}{c_d^2},
\qquad
E\|\nabla_Sc_a\|^2
=2d\frac{\|a\|_F^2}{c_d^2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Spherical integration by parts and Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
\boxed{
\left|E[c_a(U)\phi(Pq(U))]\right|
\le\frac{5\|a\|_F}{c_d\sqrt d}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This reproduces P20's claim with the same constant and no independence assumption between `c_a` and `Pq`; indeed the point is to control their physical dependence. **Verdict: PASS.**

## 5.2 `P22-AUDIT-P20-AFFINE-MOMENT-44 = PASS`

This exact formula appears in P20's Ward calculation. I give an independent cofactor proof because it will be used below.

Let `Y` have `j` physical rows `q_1,\dots,q_j`, let

\[
G_j=YY^T,
\]

and let `Q_j` denote determinant-volume selection:

\[
\frac{dQ_j}{d\mu^j}(q_1,\dots,q_j)
=\frac{\det G_j}{Z_j}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Write `r=m-j`. The physical rows are centered and isotropic:

\[
Eq=0,
\qquad
E[q\otimes q]=c_d^{-2}I_E.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### Exact partition function

By the Gram determinant Schur complement, conditionally on the first `j-1` rows,

\[
\det G_j
=\det G_{j-1}\,\|P_{j-1}q_j\|^2,
\]

where `P_{j-1}` has rank `m-j+1`. Therefore

\[
E[\|P_{j-1}q_j\|^2\mid q_1,\dots,q_{j-1}]
=\frac{m-j+1}{c_d^2},
\]

and recursively

\[
\boxed{
Z_j=\frac{(m)_j}{c_d^{2j}},
\qquad
(m)_j=m(m-1)\cdots(m-j+1).
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

### Exact affine norm

The minimum-norm affine interpolant of the constant vector is

\[
a=Y^TG_j^{-1}{\bf1}_j,
\qquad
\|a\|^2={\bf1}_j^TG_j^{-1}{\bf1}_j.
\]

Multiplying by `\det G_j`,

\[
\det G_j\,\|a\|^2
={\bf1}_j^T\operatorname{adj}(G_j){\bf1}_j.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Each diagonal adjugate entry is a principal `(j-1)`-row Gram determinant and has expectation `Z_{j-1}`. Every off-diagonal adjugate entry has expectation zero: after deleting the appropriate row and column, the determinant is linear in one remaining centered row `q_i` while all other rows are held fixed, so conditioning and `Eq_i=0` kill it. Thus

\[
E_{\mu^j}[\det G_j\,\|a\|^2]
=jZ_{j-1}.
\]

Dividing by `Z_j` from (5.10),

\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_j}\|a\|^2
=\frac{j c_d^2}{m-j+1}
=\frac{j c_d^2}{r+1}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This exactly matches P20's selected affine second moment. **Verdict: PASS.**

# 6. New theorem: physical one-delete mark persists through every submacroscopic top window

## `P22-T4-SUBMACRO-ONEDELETE-PERSISTENCE-44 = PROVED`

We now combine two elementary geometric facts with the audited inputs above.

Let `j` selected physical rows have determinant-volume law `Q_j`; set `r=m-j`, residual projector `P`, complement `Q=I-P`, and affine interpolant `a`. Let a fresh `q=q(U)` be independent with the original spherical law `\mu`. Define `\phi` by (5.1). Then P04's product-law expression for the mean mark is

\[
\bar M_r
:=E_{Q_j}M_r
=\frac{c_d^2}{r}
E_{Q_j,\mu}\left[(1-\langle a,q\rangle)\phi(Pq)\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

## 6.1 The full physical row has constant cubic ratio

For every unit `U`, `q=UU^T-I/d` has one eigenvalue `(d-1)/d` and `d-1` eigenvalues `-1/d`. Therefore

\[
\|q\|_F^2=\frac{d-1}{d}
=:n_d,
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
\operatorname{tr}(q^3)
=\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{d^2},
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\phi(q)=\frac{d-2}{d}=:\kappa_d
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

pointwise, not merely in expectation.

## 6.2 Removing a `j`-dimensional row span changes the uncentered cubic ratio by at most `O(\sqrt j/d)`

For a frozen base, `Q` has rank `j`. By the `5`-Lipschitz property,

\[
|\phi(Pq)-\phi(q)|
\le5\|Qq\|.
\]

Using covariance isotropy of the fresh row,

\[
E_\mu\|Qq\|^2
=\frac{j}{c_d^2}.
\]

Therefore, for every frozen base,

\[
\boxed{
\left|E_\mu\phi(Pq)-\kappa_d\right|
\le\frac{5\sqrt j}{c_d}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

No distributional approximation to a Haar plane is used.

## 6.3 The affine cross term is smaller

For each frozen base, apply the independently rederived sphere-cross bound (5.7) with its actual affine `a`:

\[
\left|E_\mu[\langle a,q\rangle\phi(Pq)]\right|
\le\frac{5\|a\|}{c_d\sqrt d}.
\]

Average over `Q_j` and use Cauchy--Schwarz plus (5.12):

\[
E_{Q_j}\|a\|
\le\sqrt{E_{Q_j}\|a\|^2}
=c_d\sqrt{\frac{j}{r+1}}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\left|
E_{Q_j,\mu}[\langle a,q\rangle\phi(Pq)]
\right|
\le5\sqrt{\frac{j}{d(r+1)}}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This retains the actual dependence between the affine vector and the residual projector; only the **fresh** row is independent of the selected base, exactly as in P04's mark formula.

## 6.4 Quantitative mark theorem

Substitute (6.4)--(6.5) into (6.1):

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\bar M_r-\frac{c_d^2}{r}\kappa_d
\right|
\le
\frac{c_d^2}{r}
\left[
\frac{5\sqrt j}{c_d}
+5\sqrt{\frac{j}{d(r+1)}}
\right].
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{c_d^2}{r}\kappa_d
=
\frac{(d-2)(d+2)}{d^2+d-2j-2},
\tag{6.7}
\]

(6.6) is exactly the announced estimate (0.3).

Now suppose `j=j_d=o(m)`. Then `r/m\to1`, `c_d\asymp d`, and

\[
\frac{\sqrt j}{c_d}=o(1),
\qquad
\sqrt{\frac{j}{d(r+1)}}=o(1),
\]

while the deterministic factor in (6.7) tends to one. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_{j_d}}M_{m-j_d}\to1.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

More explicitly, uniformly for `j\le m/2`,

\[
E M_{m-j}
=1+O\!\left(\frac1d+\frac{j}{d^2}+\frac{\sqrt j}{d}\right).
\tag{6.9}
\]

This proves `P22-T4-SUBMACRO-ONEDELETE-PERSISTENCE-44`.

### Consistency checks

- `j=0`: (6.6) has zero error and (6.7) gives exactly
  \[
  M_m=\frac{d-2}{d-1},
  \]
  recovering P20/P22/P04's top endpoint.
- `j=1`: (6.9) gives `M_{m-1}=1+O(1/d)`, consistent with P04's sharper exact formula `1-1/d+O(d^{-2})`.
- Gaussian coefficient comparator: P20 proves the corresponding mark is identically zero for every frozen base because orthogonal Gaussian projections are independent and centrally symmetric. Thus (6.8) is a genuine non-Gaussian persistence result, not a selection artifact common to both laws.

### Route falsified

Any argument of the form

> after `o(d^2)` determinant-selected deletions the physical one-delete mark is already asymptotically Gaussian/odd and therefore small

is **DISPROVED** by (6.8). A finite number, `O(d)`, `d^{3/2}`, or any other `o(d^2)` number of top deletions is still inside the persistence regime.

This does **not** imply Target 4 fails: the recurrence weights at those high ranks are tiny.

# 7. Corollary: the entire submacroscopic top window is cumulatively negligible

## `P22-T4-TOPWINDOW-WEIGHT-44 = PROVED`

Let `J_d=o(m)`. The bound (6.6) is uniform for `0\le j\le J_d`, so

\[
\sup_{0\le j\le J_d}|E M_{m-j}-1|\to0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\sum_{j=0}^{J_d}
\frac{E M_{m-j}}{m-j-1}
\right|
&\le(1+o(1))
\sum_{j=0}^{J_d}\frac1{m-j-1}\\
&=O\!\left(\frac{J_d+1}{m-J_d}\right)
=o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

So the physical mark can be maximally non-Gaussian throughout a very long top window while that entire window contributes vanishingly to P04's signed recurrence.

This reconciles three facts that can otherwise look contradictory:

1. `M_m\to1` and `M_{m-1}\to1`;
2. the new theorem gives `M_{m-j}\to1` uniformly through every `j=o(m)` top window;
3. P23/P26 localize the live `O(1)` obstruction to the final `O(d)` **residual-rank** window, i.e. very deep in the deletion chain, not near the top.

The relevant hard-edge difficulty is therefore not “when do individual top marks become small?” It is the law-specific signed geometry after nearly all `m=\Theta(d^2)` coefficient directions have been deleted.
