## B. Exact source scope

The current arXiv page identifies v2 as the latest version, revised 11 July 2019. Question 4.5 asks, in the ambient class of infinite, simple, locally finite, transitive graphs, whether there can exist a transitive `Gamma <= Aut(G)` and a **non-normal** subgroup `A <= Gamma` for which:

1. `G_A` is transitive; and
2. there is no normal subgroup `N` of any transitively acting `Gamma' <= Aut(G)` such that `G_N` is isomorphic to `G_A`.

The subsidiary sentence asks whether it might matter to assume that `A` acts freely. The construction below in fact has `A` acting freely, but the proof answers the main question as written rather than replacing it by the subsidiary variant.

## E. Full source-matching proof

### E1. Ambient graph hypotheses

`Lambda` is finitely generated because it is a free product of two finite groups. It is infinite: a reduced alternating word in nonidentity elements of the two free factors cannot be the identity, so there are reduced words of arbitrary length.

It is not virtually abelian. For example, choose an element of order 2 in the `S_3` factor and an element of order 3 in the `A_5` factor. By the free-product normal-form theorem they generate a copy of `C_2 * C_3`, a non-elementary virtually free group and hence not virtually abelian. A subgroup of a virtually abelian group would itself be virtually abelian, so `Lambda` is not virtually abelian. Therefore the Leemann--de la Salle theorem applies.

Because `S` is finite, symmetric, generates `Lambda`, and omits the identity, `G=Cay(Lambda,S)` is an **infinite, simple, locally finite, connected, transitive** graph. Take the root to be the identity. Thus `G` lies in the source ambient class.

### E2. `Gamma` is transitive and `A` is non-normal (indeed free)

`Gamma=Lambda_L` acts regularly, hence transitively, on `V(G)=Lambda`.

The subgroup `H=< (12) >` has order 2 and index 3 in `S_3`, and it is not normal: conjugating `(12)` by `(123)` gives a different transposition. Since `pi` is onto, the preimage `A=pi^{-1}(H)` is non-normal in `Lambda`: if `A` were normal, its image `H=pi(A)` would be normal in `S_3`.

As a subgroup of a left-regular action, `A` acts freely on vertices: if left multiplication by `a in A` fixes one vertex `g`, then `ag=g`, hence `a=1`. Thus the construction also satisfies the source's subsidiary free-action suggestion.

### E3. Exact identification `G_A ~= K_3`

The `A`-orbits under left multiplication are the left cosets `A g`. Since `A=pi^{-1}(H)`, the map

`A g  |->  H pi(g)`

is a well-defined bijection from the three `A`-orbits in `Lambda` to the three left cosets of `H` in `S_3`.

Now take any two distinct `H`-cosets. Choose representatives `r,t in S_3` from those cosets. Then `r^{-1}t != 1` lies in `S_3\{1}`, hence belongs to `S_0 subseteq S`. By the Cayley-graph definition, `r` and `t=r(r^{-1}t)` are adjacent in `G`. Therefore every two distinct quotient vertices are adjacent.

The quotient convention suppresses loops and records adjacency between distinct orbits, so

`G_A ~= K_3`.

In particular `G_A` is transitive. This proves source condition (i).

### E4. The universal quantifier over every transitive `Gamma' <= Aut(G)` collapses

The GRR property gives `Aut(G)=Lambda_L`, and this action is regular. If `Gamma' <= Lambda_L` is transitive on `Lambda`, then the orbit of the identity under `Gamma'` is all of `Lambda`. But in a left-regular representation, that orbit is precisely the set of group elements represented by `Gamma'`. Hence `Gamma'=Lambda_L`.

Therefore **there is only one transitively acting subgroup of `Aut(G)`**, namely `Gamma` itself. This is exactly why the GRR construction removes the wall identified in the old attempt D0072-A001.

### E5. No normal quotient of that transitive group is `K_3`

Suppose for contradiction that `N normal Lambda_L` and

`G_N ~= G_A ~= K_3`.

The vertices of `G_N` are the `N`-orbits. Under the regular identification these are the left cosets of the corresponding normal subgroup `N_0 normal Lambda`. Since `K_3` has exactly three vertices, `[Lambda:N_0]=3`. Normality then makes `Lambda/N_0` a group of order 3, hence `C_3`. Thus `Lambda` would have a quotient `C_3`.

But abelianization preserves free products as coproducts in the category of abelian groups:

`Lambda^ab = (S_3 * A_5)^ab ~= S_3^ab x A_5^ab ~= C_2 x 1 ~= C_2`.

Here `[S_3,S_3]=A_3` and `A_5` is perfect. Every map from `Lambda` to the abelian group `C_3` factors through `Lambda^ab ~= C_2`, and no surjection `C_2 -> C_3` exists. Contradiction.

Thus no normal subgroup of the only possible transitive `Gamma'` yields a quotient isomorphic to `G_A`. This proves source condition (ii), with its full overgroup quantifier.
