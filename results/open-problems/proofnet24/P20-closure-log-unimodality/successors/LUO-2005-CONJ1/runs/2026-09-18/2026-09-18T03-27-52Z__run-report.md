- `v(p*)=s+2`;

**Proof.** The bad-measure inequality gives

`2(|q|+1)-r <= 2|q|-s`,

`mu(p*) = 2(|q|+1)-(s+2) = 2|q|-s = mu(q)`.

# 4. Method #5 — ADD A PARAMETER: constrained-skeleton gaps

The stuck report treated the `P_y` as a family indexed by singleton q variables. That overcounts: many different singleton variables produce **exactly the same affine piece**. The right parameter is the number of singleton runs separated by genuinely constrained q symbols.

Call a q-position **constrained** if it is either a literal or an occurrence of a variable whose total multiplicity in q is at least two. The remaining positions are precisely the singleton-variable positions. A **singleton gap** is a maximal nonempty run of singleton-variable positions between consecutive constrained positions, including the two end gaps.

For a singleton variable y at q-position j, write `D_j=dir(P_y)` for the direction space of its affine piece in `F_2^{|q|+1}`.

## P20-LUO-D1-GAP-RIGIDITY-09 = PROVED

**Claim.** Let j<k be positions of two singleton q variables.

1. If j and k lie in the same singleton gap, then `P_j=P_k`.
2. If `dir(P_j)=dir(P_k)`, then j and k lie in the same singleton gap; hence again `P_j=P_k`.

Consequently, **distinct d=1 singleton-expansion pieces have distinct directions**, and the number of distinct pieces is exactly the number `g(q)` of nonempty singleton gaps.

**Proof.** For fixed j, inspect which standard coordinate vectors belong to `D_j`. The expanded singleton contributes two independently free output coordinates; every other singleton q variable contributes one independently free coordinate. A constant output coordinate is fixed, while an occurrence of a repeated q variable cannot vary alone because changing that variable changes all of its occurrences simultaneously. Hence

`F_j := {i : e_i in D_j}`

is exactly the set obtained from all singleton positions of q by inserting one extra free coordinate at j and shifting every later q-position by one.

Equivalently, the complement of `F_j` is the order-preserving image of the constrained-position set of q under the same one-coordinate insertion.

If j<k and some constrained q-position lies strictly between them, take the first such constrained position r. Under insertion at k it remains at output coordinate r, while under insertion at j it is shifted to r+1; by minimality of r there is no preceding constrained position in the interval that can replace it. Thus the constrained-coordinate sets, and hence `F_j,F_k`, differ. Therefore `D_j=D_k` is impossible.

Conversely, if no constrained q-position lies between j and k, then every constrained q-position is either before both insertion sites (hence unshifted in both pieces) or after both (hence shifted by one in both pieces). The fixed literal coordinates and every repeated-variable equality block therefore coincide exactly. Singleton coordinates are free in either piece. Hence `P_j=P_k`.

This proves both assertions. QED.

The useful correction to the raw coefficient count is therefore:

> singleton multiplicity inside one unconstrained run buys **no new d=1 cover direction at all**.

Only crossings of constants or repeated-variable occurrences create a genuinely new piece.

# 5. Method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the two-gap cover is impossible

The coefficient obstruction at d=1 only says that q has at least two singleton variables. Gap rigidity lets us sharpen this from a raw count to a structural count.

## P20-LUO-D1-TWO-GAP-10 = PROVED

**Claim.** In any bad d=1 pair, q must have at least **three distinct singleton gaps**. Equivalently, `g(q)>=3` is necessary.

**Proof.** By P20-LUO-D1-EXTREMAL-SPECIALIZATION-08, replace p by an extremal specialization and write

`C=M_n(p*)`, `dim C=s+2`, `n=|q|+1`.

Every distinct q-piece P has dimension `s+1`.

If `g(q)=1`, then `M_n(q)` is one such P and has only `2^{s+1}` points, so it cannot contain C, which has `2^{s+2}` points.

Suppose `g(q)=2`, with the two distinct pieces P and Q. If `C subseteq P union Q`, then

`|C| <= |C cap P| + |C cap Q| <= 2^{s+1}+2^{s+1}=2^{s+2}=|C|`.

Every inequality is therefore equality. Thus `C cap P=P` and `C cap Q=Q`; in particular P and Q are affine hyperplanes of C. Equality in the first union bound also forces them to be disjoint.

Two disjoint affine hyperplanes of one affine space are parallel, so `dir(P)=dir(Q)`. P20-LUO-D1-GAP-RIGIDITY-09 then forces `P=Q`, contradicting that the two pieces were distinct.

Therefore two distinct singleton gaps cannot cover C. Hence `g(q)>=3`. QED.

`|p|=m+1=s+2` and `v(p)=s+2`.

Therefore every position of p is occupied by a distinct variable: p has no constants and no repeated variables. Its minimum slice is the entire Boolean cube

`M_{m+1}(p)=F_2^{m+1}`.

Let the two occurrences of x in q be at positions `a<b` (one-indexed). For any singleton q variable y, its expansion piece P_y imposes the equality of the two shifted x-occurrence coordinates. There are only three possible equalities:

- if y occurs before a, then `w_{a+1}=w_{b+1}`;
- if y occurs strictly between a and b, then `w_a=w_{b+1}`;
- if y occurs after b, then `w_a=w_b`.

All other q variables are singleton and impose no equality constraints, and there are no q literals.

If `b>=a+2`, the four coordinates `a,a+1,b,b+1` are distinct. Choose a binary word w with

`w_a=w_{a+1}=0`, `w_b=w_{b+1}=1`,

and assign arbitrary bits elsewhere. Then all three displayed equalities fail. Hence w belongs to no P_y.

If `b=a+1`, there is no singleton position strictly between the two x occurrences, so the middle equality never occurs. Choose

`w_a=0`, `w_b=1`, `w_{b+1}=0`.

Then both possible remaining equalities fail: `w_a!=w_b` and `w_b!=w_{b+1}`. Again w belongs to no P_y.

Thus in all cases

`M_{m+1}(q)=union_y P_y != F_2^{m+1}=M_{m+1}(p)`,

contradicting `L(p) subseteq L(q)`. Therefore the assumed bad measure inequality is impossible, and `mu(p)>mu(q)`. QED.
