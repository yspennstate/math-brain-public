## 2. Counterexample-first setup: two necessary constraints

Write

`mu(r) = 2|r| - #var(r)`.

Let `v(r)=#var(r)`.

### Lemma 2.1 — minimum word length

For every pattern `r`, the shortest word in `L(r)` has length exactly `|r|`: every variable image is nonempty, and assigning one terminal symbol to every variable attains length `|r|`.

Therefore

`L(p) subseteq L(q)  =>  |p| >= |q|`.

Indeed, choose a word of length `|p|` from `L(p)`; it belongs to `L(q)`, whose words all have length at least `|q|`.

If in addition a prospective counterexample satisfies `mu(p) <= mu(q)`, then with `d=|p|-|q|>=0`,

`v(p)-v(q) >= 2d`.                                                    (2.1)

For `|p|=9`, equation (2.1) immediately rules out `|q|<=4`, because it would require `v(p)-v(q)>=10` while `v(p)<=9` and `v(q)>=0`.

## 3. Exact all-length equal-length lemma

### Claim P20-LUO-EQUAL-LENGTH-MORPHIC-01 — PROVED / P20 SELF-CHECK

Over any alphabet with at least two symbols, if `|p|=|q|=n` and `L(p) subseteq L(q)`, then `p` is obtained from `q` by a nonerasing substitution sending every variable of `q` to a *single pattern symbol* (one constant or one variable). Consequently, if the inclusion is strict then

`mu(p) > mu(q)`.

#### Proof

Let

`M_n(r) = L(r) intersect Sigma^n`.

Because `|r|=n` and all variable images are nonempty, every word in `M_n(r)` is obtained by replacing each variable of `r` by exactly one terminal symbol.

Assume `M_n(p) subseteq M_n(q)`, which follows from `L(p) subseteq L(q)`.

1. If position `i` of `q` is a constant `a`, then every word of `M_n(q)` has letter `a` at position `i`. Hence every word of `M_n(p)` has `a` there. Since the alphabet has at least two symbols, the symbol of `p` at `i` cannot be a variable and cannot be another constant; it is the same constant `a`.

2. If positions `i,j` contain the same variable of `q`, then every word of `M_n(q)` has equal letters at positions `i,j`. The same is therefore true in every word of `M_n(p)`. Again using two distinct alphabet symbols, this forces the symbols of `p` at `i,j` to be identical: two distinct variables can be assigned different letters, a variable can be assigned a letter different from a fixed constant, and two distinct constants differ.

Thus each variable `x` of `q` has one well-defined single pattern symbol `sigma(x)` occupying all its positions in `p`, while constants are fixed. Hence `p=sigma(q)`.

Every variable appearing in `p` is the image of at least one variable of `q`, so `v(p)<=v(q)`. If equality held, no variable of `q` could map to a constant and the induced map from the `v(q)` variables onto the `v(p)` variables would be a bijection. Then `p` and `q` differ only by variable renaming and `L(p)=L(q)`. Therefore strict inclusion forces `v(p)<v(q)`. Since `|p|=|q|`,

`mu(p)-mu(q) = v(q)-v(p) > 0`.

This proves the claim. QED.

This lemma handles the entire `|p|=|q|` case at every length. It also says that genuinely non-morphic strict inclusions must have a positive length gap.

## 4. Exact finite length-9 exclusion

For a binary pattern `r`, define the exact length-9 slice

`M_9(r) = L(r) intersect {0,1}^9`.

If `L(p) subseteq L(q)`, necessarily

`M_9(p) subseteq M_9(q)`.                                              (4.1)

For `|p|=9`, every variable image in a length-9 word has length one, so `M_9(p)` consists exactly of all one-bit substitutions and has cardinality `2^{v(p)}`.

`sum_x c_x(ell_x-1) = 9-m`.                                           (4.2)

Equation (4.2) gives a finite exact enumeration of every possible length vector; enumerating every binary image of those lengths gives *all* of `M_9(q)`.
