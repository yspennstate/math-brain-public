## 3. Idea before proof: stress the BFU port at the exact source capacity

The stuck report identified BFU's key advantage correctly: it does not leave a frozen fresh residual core. But BFU pays for adversarial-terminal regularization by sending each endpoint to a **global, nearly uniform interface**, and then pays another mixing-scale leg to connect the randomized pair. That phase separation is harmless when the number of routed pairs is a small absolute fraction of the natural capacity, which is exactly BFU's regime. H0023 is different: at the minimum source depth,

`m = (1+o(1)) n / ((1+eps) L)`, where `L = log n/loglog n`,

so the vertex budget is saturated to first order. A path of normalized length coefficient `lambda` consumes `lambda/(1+eps)` of the entire graph after summing over all `m` pairs. There is no room for three separate global-distance-scale legs when `eps` is small.

The right test is therefore not to copy BFU constants. It is to ask whether **any optimized BFU-style architecture which still randomizes both endpoints to diffuse global interfaces before a separate central connection** can fit inside the H0023 depth and vertex budget. The answer is no for every fixed `0<eps<2`.

## 4. #4 + #5 — rigorous diffuse-interface capacity barrier

### Claim `P20-H0023-DIFFUSE-INTERFACE-CAPACITY-BARRIER-04`

**Status:** `PROVED / P20 SELF-CHECK / METHOD BARRIER ONLY`.

Fix a constant `0<eps<2` and let

`p=(1+eps) log n/n`, `D=np=(1+eps)log n`, and `L_D=log n/log D=(1+o(1))log n/loglog n`.

Consider any randomized routing architecture on `G~G(n,p)` which, for a prescribed terminal pair `(a,b)`, chooses two ordered interface vertices `X,Y` after seeing the graph and any prior routing history, with the following BFU-type diffuseness property for some fixed constant `C`:

`max_v P(X=v | G, history) <= C/n`,

`max_v P(Y=v | G, history, X) <= C/n`.

Assume that the final simple `a`-to-`b` route visits `X` and then `Y` in this order. Then, for every fixed `eta>0`, with high probability over `G`, conditional on that good graph and on any admissible prior history,

`dist(a,X), dist(X,Y), dist(Y,b) >= (1-eta)L_D`

simultaneously with probability `1-o(1)` over the architecture's randomization.

Consequently, if `eta>0` is chosen so that

`3(1-eta)>1+eps`,

then such a two-interface phase-separated architecture cannot with high probability produce a route of exact source length

`k=(1+eps+o(1)) log n/loglog n`.

Moreover, for `m=(1+o(1))n/((1+eps)L_D)` disjoint prescribed pairs, all but `o(m)` of the routed paths in any such diffuse-interface realization have length at least `3(1-eta)L_D`. Hence their total vertex demand alone exceeds `n` when `3(1-eta)>1+eps`. Thus the architecture fails from **global capacity even before** one asks for exact equal lengths or all-permutation uniformity.

### Full proof

By a standard Chernoff bound and a union bound, since `D=(1+eps)log n`,

`Delta(G) <= 3D`

with probability `1-o(1)`. Indeed, for a fixed vertex, the upper-tail probability at three times the mean is exponentially small in `D`, with exponent strictly larger than `log n`, uniformly for fixed `eps>0`.

Fix a graph satisfying `Delta(G)<=3D`, and put

`t=floor((1-eta)L_D)`.

For every vertex `u`, the radius-`t` ball satisfies deterministically

`|B_G(u,t)| <= 1+3D+(3D)^2+...+(3D)^t <= 2(3D)^(t+1)`.

Since `log(3D)=log D+O(1)` and `t=(1-eta)log n/log D+O(1)`,

`2(3D)^(t+1) = n^(1-eta+o(1))`.

In particular, for all sufficiently large `n`, uniformly over all `u`,

`|B_G(u,t)| <= n^(1-eta/2)`.

Now condition on this graph and on any admissible prior history. By the first diffuseness inequality,

`P(dist(a,X)<=t | G,history) <= C |B_G(a,t)|/n <= C n^(-eta/2)`.

After conditioning additionally on `X=x`, the second diffuseness inequality gives

`P(dist(x,Y)<=t | G,history,X=x) <= C |B_G(x,t)|/n <= C n^(-eta/2)`.

The same second diffuseness bound, now applied to the fixed ball around `b`, yields

`P(dist(Y,b)<=t | G,history,X) <= C |B_G(b,t)|/n <= C n^(-eta/2)`.

A union bound gives

`P(min{dist(a,X),dist(X,Y),dist(Y,b)}<=t | G,history) <= 3C n^(-eta/2)=o(1)`.

If the final simple route visits `X` and then `Y`, the three corresponding subsegments of that route have lengths at least the three graph distances, so on the complementary event its total length is at least

`3t=(3(1-eta)+o(1))L_D`.

Because `L_D=(1+o(1))log n/loglog n`, when `3(1-eta)>1+eps` this is strictly larger than the source-minimum `k=(1+eps+o(1))log n/loglog n`. This proves the one-pair exact-length obstruction.

For `m` pairs, call a pair exceptional if at least one of its three interface distances is at most `t`. The conditional expected number of exceptional pairs is at most

`3C m n^(-eta/2)`.

Markov's inequality therefore implies that the exceptional count is `o(m)` with probability `1-o(1)`, without requiring independence between different pairs. Hence `(1-o(1))m` paths have length at least `3t`. If the paths are vertex-disjoint, their total number of used vertices is at least their total number of edges up to an additive `m`, and the leading term is

`(1-o(1)) m 3(1-eta)L_D = (3(1-eta)/(1+eps)+o(1)) n`.

This exceeds `n` under the same inequality `3(1-eta)>1+eps`, contradiction. □

### Scope and what this does *not* prove

This is **not** a refutation of Conjecture 1.6. It only rules out a broad phase-separated port of the BFU idea in which both prescribed endpoints are deliberately randomized to globally diffuse interface points and are then joined by a separate central leg.

The point is architectural: BFU's successful theorem has a small absolute pair-capacity constant and can afford several mixing-scale legs. H0023 operates at near-saturated pair capacity, where a successful path gets only about **one** graph-distance unit, not three. The useful BFU idea therefore cannot be ported literally; endpoint regularization and pair connection must be merged into the same near-geodesic route.

This is exactly the first place the BFU recovery loses the source slack. It fails **before** the more delicate BFU expansion/eigenvalue estimates become the main issue.

## 5. #5 — utilization parameter pinpoints the first failure

The clean parameter is

`theta := m L_D/n`.

At the H0023 minimum depth,

`theta = 1/(1+eps)+o(1)`.

If a routing architecture needs normalized route length `lambda L_D` per pair, then its first-order vertex utilization is at least

`U = lambda theta = lambda/(1+eps)+o(1)`.

Source saturation corresponds to the exact identity `lambda=1+eps`, hence `U=1+o(1)`. A BFU-style separated global randomization architecture has the rigorous diffuse-interface lower bound `lambda>=3-o(1)`. Therefore

`U >= 3/(1+eps)-o(1)`.

For every `eps<2`, `U>1`. Thus as the routed-pair load is increased from BFU's small-`alpha` regime toward H0023's `theta=1/(1+eps)`, the **first unavoidable failure is global vertex capacity**. One does not yet reach the point where tuning the Menger-flow constants or preserving `delta(H)>=cd` under depletion is the decisive issue.

## 6. #28 — fixed permutation versus a typical permutation

There is an exact symmetry observation which prevents `replace for all with for typical` from becoming a fake simplification.

For a graph `G`, let `R(G) subset S_m` be the set of endpoint permutations realized by spanning exact-length `(a,b;k)` path systems. For any two fixed permutations `pi,sigma in S_m`, a relabeling which fixes the `a`-tuple and permutes the labels inside the fixed `b`-tuple maps the event `pi in R(G)` to `sigma in R(G)`. Since `G(n,p)` is invariant under vertex relabeling,

`P_G(pi in R(G)) = P_G(sigma in R(G))`.

Therefore if `Pi` is a uniformly random requested permutation independent of `G`,

`P_{G,Pi}(Pi in R(G)) = P_G(id in R(G))`.

So a random requested permutation is **not an easier annealed existence problem** than one fixed permutation.

There is nevertheless a useful one-way bridge. Let

`q(G)=|R(G)|/m!`.

By the same symmetry,

`E[1-q(G)] = P_G(id notin R(G))`.

Hence, if the fixed-permutation sharp path-system problem were proved with probability `1-o(1)`, Markov's inequality would immediately imply

`q(G)=1-o_P(1)`:

with high probability the graph would realize **almost all** endpoint permutations. The remaining source difficulty would then be a genuinely quenched upgrade from density `1-o(1)` in `S_m` to density `1`.

The source's Lemma 3.2 performs exactly such an upgrade by composing two large permutation sets coming from two spanning halves, but `P20-H0023-HALF-SPLIT-BARRIER-01` shows that the two halves cannot each exist at the conjectured minimum depth when `eps<1`. Thus #28 cleanly separates the two bottlenecks:

1. first obtain one sharp fixed-permutation spanning exact-length system;
2. then replace the unavailable second critical-depth half by a **zero-first-order-cost uniformization mechanism**.

No such uniformization mechanism is currently known to P20.
