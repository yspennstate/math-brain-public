# 2. New idea before code

## Move #11: dualize the obstruction

At the boundary mean, any complete mix satisfies

`X_1+X_2+X_3=1` a.s.

For atomless identical marginals, at least one coordinate is strictly larger than `1/3` almost surely. Therefore the linear tail functional

`L(F)=P_F(X>1/3)`

must satisfy `L(F)>=1/3` for every 3-CM law. The hyperplane `L(F)=1/3` is thus a natural separating boundary.

The design objective is to build a family in which this *same* hyperplane is exactly the point at which the density enters the known monotone-density cone, because the latter cone has a complete mixability theorem.

## Move #19: turn the design into an algorithm

Instead of hand-tuning each number of pieces, split `[0,1/3]` into `k=m-1` equal cells and ask for an affine-in-cell-index perturbation whose two moments vanish in exactly the amounts needed to compensate a one-parameter tail perturbation. The normalization and mean constraints are two linear equations, so this is an explicit two-equation design problem for the two coefficients of an affine sequence.

## Move #30: one scale/direction at a time

Keep the entire tail `[1/3,1]` as one fixed cell. All combinatorial complexity is confined to equal cells on `[0,1/3]`. The ordering of the pre-tail heights is controlled by one constant adjacent difference, while the mean is controlled by the exact first-moment weights `(2j-1)` of those cells. This isolates the only two things that matter.

# 3. The all-m family

Fix any integer `m>=3` and write

`k=m-1>=2`.

Let

`I_j=[(j-1)/(3k),j/(3k))`, `1<=j<=k`,

and

`I_{k+1}=[1/3,1]`.

For a parameter `t in [-1/10,1/10]`, equivalently

`c=(1+t)/3 in [3/10,11/30]`,

define

`A_k=(8k+1)/(k-1)`,

`B_k=-18k/(k^2-1)`,

`a_{k,j}=A_k+B_k j`,

and the piecewise-constant density

`f_{k,t}(x)=2+a_{k,j} t` for `x in I_j`, `1<=j<=k`,

`f_{k,t}(x)=(1+t)/2` for `x in [1/3,1]`.

The finite algebraic core is

`sum_{j=1}^k a_{k,j}=-k`,                                      (3.1)

`sum_{j=1}^k (2j-1)a_{k,j}=-4k^2`.                             (3.2)

Also

`a_{k,1}=(8k-1)/(k+1)`,

`a_{k,k}=-(10k+1)/(k+1)`,                                     (3.3)

and

`a_{k,j}-a_{k,j+1}=18k/((k-1)(k+1))>0`.                       (3.4)

# 4. Exact normalization and boundary mean

Each pre-tail cell has length `1/(3k)`. From (3.1), its total mass is

`(1/(3k)) sum_j (2+a_{k,j}t)`
` = (1/(3k))(2k-kt)`
` = 2/3-t/3`.

The tail mass is

`(2/3)*(1+t)/2=(1+t)/3`.

Hence the total mass is exactly `1`.

For `I_j`,

`int_{I_j} x dx=(2j-1)/(18k^2)`.

Therefore (3.2) gives the pre-tail first moment

`sum_j ((2j-1)/(18k^2))(2+a_{k,j}t)`
` = 1/9-2t/9`.

The tail first moment is

`((1+t)/2) int_{1/3}^1 x dx`
` = ((1+t)/2)*(4/9)`
` = 2(1+t)/9`.

Thus

`E[X]=1/9-2t/9+2(1+t)/9=1/3`.                                 (4.1)

So every member lies exactly on the source-highlighted tight mean boundary.

# 5. Positivity and unimodality

The coefficient sequence `a_{k,j}` is strictly decreasing in `j` by (3.4).

For `t>=0`, the smallest pre-tail height occurs at `j=k`, and using `t<=1/10`,

`2+a_{k,k}t >= 2+a_{k,k}/10`
` = (10k+19)/(10(k+1))>0`.                                    (5.1)

For `t<=0`, the smallest pre-tail height occurs at `j=1`, and using `t>=-1/10`,

`2+a_{k,1}t >= 2-a_{k,1}/10`
` = 3(4k+7)/(10(k+1))>0`.                                     (5.2)

The tail height is at least `9/20`.

It remains to compare the last pre-tail height with the tail. Their difference is

`(2+a_{k,k}t)-(1+t)/2`
` = 3/2+(a_{k,k}-1/2)t`.                                      (5.3)

Since `a_{k,k}-1/2<0`, for `t<=0` this is at least `3/2`. For `0<=t<=1/10`, its minimum occurs at `t=1/10` and equals

`9(k+3)/(20(k+1))>0`.                                         (5.4)

Consequently:

- if `t>0`, the pre-tail heights are strictly decreasing and then drop again at `1/3`; `f_{k,t}` is globally nonincreasing;
- if `t<0`, the pre-tail heights are strictly increasing and then drop at `1/3`; `f_{k,t}` is unimodal with its peak on `I_k`;
- if `t=0`, the pre-tail density is constant `2` and drops to `1/2` at `1/3`.

For `t!=0`, all adjacent pre-tail heights are distinct, so the construction is genuinely `m=k+1`-piece rather than a subdivision of a lower-piece density. The last pre-tail and tail heights are always distinct by (5.3)–(5.4).

# 6. Exact tail statistic and necessity

The upper-tail probability is

`P(X>1/3)=(2/3)*(1+t)/2=(1+t)/3=c`.                            (6.1)

Assume this law is 3-CM. Then there exist identically distributed `X_1,X_2,X_3` with

`X_1+X_2+X_3=1` a.s.

The marginal has a density, hence no atom at `1/3`. If all three coordinates were at most `1/3`, their sum being `1` would force

`X_1=X_2=X_3=1/3`,

an event of probability zero. Thus almost surely at least one coordinate is strictly larger than `1/3`. Therefore

`1 <= E[# {i:X_i>1/3}]`
`   = sum_i P(X_i>1/3)`
`   = 3c`.

No exchangeability is used; identical marginals suffice. Hence

`c>=1/3`, equivalently `t>=0`.                                 (6.2)

# 7. Sufficiency

If `c>=1/3`, then `t>=0`, so Section 5 proves that `f_{k,t}` is nonincreasing on `[0,1]`. Section 4 proves its mean is exactly `1/3`.

The classical Wang–Wang monotone-density theorem states that a distribution with monotone density on bounded support `[a,b]` is `n`-CM if and only if its mean lies in

`[a+(b-a)/n, b-(b-a)/n]`.

Here `a=0`, `b=1`, `n=3`, and the mean is exactly `1/3`, so every member with `c>=1/3` is 3-CM.

Combining with Section 6 gives the sharp classification

**Theorem `P20-D0010-ALL-M-PIECE-IFF-02`.** For every integer `m>=3`, the explicit `m`-piece unimodal boundary family above satisfies

`f_{m,c} is 3-CM  <=>  c>=1/3`.

Therefore

`J_boundary=UNBOUNDED`.                                       (7.1)

# 10. Directed P20 -> P21 audit duty

The newest P21 report contains an exact phase-zero continued-fraction block identity. I independently checked the local argument.

Let

`F(t)=1_[0,1/2)(t)-1_[1/2,1)(t)`,

`F_N(x)=sum_{r=0}^{N-1}F(x+r theta)`,

and let `p_k/q_k` be convergents, with `Delta_k=q_k theta-p_k`. Under P21’s parity assumptions all `q_k` are odd. P21 claims that for sufficiently large `k` and

`1<=m<=a_{k+1}/4`, 

`F_{m q_k}(0)=1+(m-1) s_k`, `s_k=sgn(Delta_k)`.

The check is sound:

1. Split into blocks `jq_k+r`.
2. For `r!=0`, the displacement from the rational grid `r p_k/q_k` is `(j+r/q_k)Delta_k`, whose modulus is `<1/(4q_k)` in the stated range.
3. Because `q_k` is odd, every nonzero rational grid point is at distance at least `1/(2q_k)` from the discontinuity set `{0,1/2}`. Hence no sign changes.
4. The `q_k-1` nonzero residues split equally across the two half-circles, so their signs sum to zero.
5. The `r=0` term is `+1` in the first block. In later blocks, `jDelta_k mod 1` lies in `(0,1/2)` if `Delta_k>0` and in `(1/2,1)` if `Delta_k<0`, because `j|Delta_k|<1/2`; hence the contribution is `s_k`.
