# 3. Exact source scope and current literature check

Wang's Section 2.8 explicitly records that the mean condition is **not** sufficient for arbitrary unimodal densities on a bounded interval and asks:

> Under what extra conditions a unimodal distribution on a bounded interval is n-CM?

The source emphasizes the equality case of the mean condition, where the mean divides the support into lengths `(b-a)/n` and `(b-a)(n-1)/n`. This is exactly the lower-boundary normalization used here.

The source also records the monotone-density theorem of Wang and Wang (2011): for a distribution on `[a,b]` with a monotone density, the mean condition

\[
a+\frac{b-a}{n}\le \mu\le b-\frac{b-a}{n}
\tag{3.1}
\]

is sufficient (indeed necessary and sufficient) for `n`-complete mixability.

Fresh literature checks on 16 September 2026 included the exact source title/problem wording, `complete mixability unimodal bounded interval`, `monotone density mean condition Wang Wang 2011`, and later complete-mixability characterization papers. The 2024 Komisarski–Labuschagne paper still states that for `n>=3` characterization of complete mixability is only partially solved and repeats the monotone-density theorem among the known sufficient/iff classes. I found no source establishing the full Wang Open Problem 10 in the searched material.

This is a **bounded dated literature search, not proof of openness and not a novelty certificate**.

Primary/current references used for scope:

1. Ruodu Wang, _Current Open Questions in Complete Mixability_, Probability Surveys 12 (2015), 13–32, arXiv:1411.6190, especially Section 2.8/Open Problem 10 and the discussion around the mean condition.
2. Bin Wang and Ruodu Wang, _The complete mixability and convex minimization problems with monotone marginal densities_, J. Multivariate Anal. 102 (2011), 1344–1360.
3. Andrzej Komisarski and Jacques Labuschagne, _The Distributions of the Mean of Random Vectors with Fixed Marginal Distribution_, J. Theor. Probab. 37 (2024), 2121–2129.

That route by itself is **not sufficient**: a feasible count vector does not automatically produce the required continuous within-cell marginals or a constant-sum transport. An initial generic three-cell choice such as breakpoints `1/7,2/7` exposed this mismatch. The useful remnant was to make one cell boundary equal the critical lower mean `1/3`. That led to the partition

\[

## Move #24 — find the extremal object

At mean `1/3`, the statistic

\[
c=P(X>1/3)

I then engineered (1.1) so that the density becomes monotone **at exactly the same threshold** `c=1/3`. That makes the necessary tail obstruction and the known monotone-density sufficiency theorem meet with no gap.

# 5. The frozen three-piece family

For `c in [1/4,2/5]`, write

\[
h_1=15c-3,\qquad h_2=9-21c,\qquad h_3=\frac32c.
\]

Then (1.1) is constant with heights `h_1,h_2,h_3` on intervals of lengths `1/6,1/6,2/3`.

## 5.1 Positivity and support

At the parameter endpoints,

\[
h_1\ge h_1(1/4)=\frac34>0,
\]

\[
h_2\ge h_2(2/5)=\frac35>0,
\]

and `h_3>0`. Therefore every member has essential support exactly `[0,1]`.

## 5.2 Normalization

Directly,

\[
\int_0^1f_c(x)\,dx
=\frac16h_1+\frac16h_2+\frac23h_3
=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed

\[
\frac16(15c-3)+\frac16(9-21c)+\frac23\frac{3c}{2}
=1.
\]

## 5.3 Boundary mean

Using

\[
\int_0^{1/6}x\,dx=\frac1{72},\quad
\int_{1/6}^{1/3}x\,dx=\frac1{24},\quad
\int_{1/3}^{1}x\,dx=\frac49,
\]

we get

\[
\int_0^1x f_c(x)\,dx
=\frac1{72}h_1+\frac1{24}h_2+\frac49h_3
=\frac13.
\tag{5.2}
\]

So every member lies exactly on the lower equality boundary of (3.1) for `n=3`.

## 5.4 Unimodality for the full parameter interval

The relevant exact differences are

\[
h_2-h_1=12-36c=12(1-3c),
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
h_2-h_3=9-\frac{45}{2}c.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For `1/4<=c<=1/3`, (5.3) and (5.4) are nonnegative, so

\[
h_1\le h_2\ge h_3.
\]

Thus `f_c` is unimodal with a middle plateau as a mode (strictly nonmonotone for `c<1/3`).

For `1/3<=c<=2/5`,

\[
h_1-h_2=36c-12\ge0
\]

and (5.4) remains nonnegative, hence

\[
h_1\ge h_2\ge h_3.
\]

Thus `f_c` is nonincreasing. At `c=1/3`, the first two heights agree; at `c=2/5`, the last two heights agree. Unimodality therefore holds on the entire closed parameter interval.

This proves `P20-D0010-THREE-PIECE-FAMILY-01`.

# 6. Sharp non-mixability for c < 1/3

Let `X_1,X_2,X_3` have common marginal density `f_c` and suppose, toward 3-complete mixability, that

\[
X_1+X_2+X_3=1\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define

\[
N=\sum_{i=1}^3\mathbf1_{\{X_i>1/3\}}.
\]

If `N=0`, then all three variables are at most `1/3`. Together with (6.1), this forces

\[
X_1=X_2=X_3=1/3.
\]

But `f_c` is an ordinary density, so each marginal is atomless and

\[
P(X_i=1/3)=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
P(N=0)=0,
\]

and hence

\[
N\ge1\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Taking expectations gives

\[
1\le E[N]
=\sum_{i=1}^3P(X_i>1/3).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The tail mass is exactly

\[
P(X>1/3)
=\frac23h_3
=\frac23\frac{3c}{2}
=c.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Therefore (6.3) becomes

\[
1\le3c.
\]

So

\[
\boxed{c<1/3\Longrightarrow f_c\text{ is not 3-CM}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

No exchangeability assumption was used; identical marginals suffice. This proves `P20-D0010-TAIL-OBSTRUCTION-01`.

# 7. Complete mixability for c >= 1/3

For `1/3<=c<=2/5`, Section 5.4 proved that `f_c` is a nonincreasing density on its full support `[0,1]`. Section 5.3 proved its mean is `1/3`.

Apply the Wang–Wang monotone-density theorem. Its hypotheses are met:

1. the distribution is supported on the bounded interval `[0,1]`;
2. it has a monotone density (piecewise constant is allowed; the distribution function is continuous because there are no atoms);
3. for `n=3` the mean condition is

\[
\frac13\le\mu\le\frac23;
\]

4. our mean is exactly `mu=1/3`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{c\ge1/3\Longrightarrow f_c\text{ is 3-CM}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Combining (6.5) and (7.1) proves the claimed iff classification (1.2), hence `P20-D0010-THREE-PIECE-IFF-01`.

The argument also shows why this family is source-relevant rather than arbitrary: it lies entirely on the equality case singled out immediately after Open Problem 10, and the sharp threshold is where a genuinely nonmonotone unimodal density crosses into the already-understood monotone class.

# 10. Directed P20 -> P21 audit duty

I read P21's current report

and independently checked the probabilistic algebra behind its new claim `P21-I0004-DISCREPANCY-ENERGY-61` for the positive-excursion Galton–Watson representation.

Let `N_h` be the number of upcrossings `h -> h+1` in one positive simple-random-walk excursion. The standard branching decomposition gives critical geometric offspring

\[
P(\zeta=k)=2^{-(k+1)},\qquad E\zeta=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\zeta)=2,
\]

so `E N_h=1`. Visits to height `h>=1` equal `N_{h-1}+N_h`. Writing

\[
A=\varepsilon_1+\sum_{h\ge1}(\varepsilon_h+\varepsilon_{h+1})N_h
=\varepsilon_1+\sum_{h\ge1}c_hN_h,
\]

put `C_h=sum_{j<=h}c_j` and

\[
M_H=\sum_{h=1}^H C_h(N_h-N_{h+1}).
\]

The martingale differences are orthogonal and have conditional variance `2N_h`, hence

\[
E[M_H^2]=2\sum_{h=1}^HC_h^2=2V_\alpha(H).
\]

Summation by parts gives

\[
\sum_{h=1}^Hc_hN_h=M_H+C_HN_{H+1}.
\]

On extinction through level `H+1`, the last term and all higher generations vanish, so `A=eps_1+M_H`. For a critical geometric Galton–Watson process starting from one ancestor,

\[
P(N_{H+1}>0)=\frac1{H+2}.
\]

Using `1-cos x <= x^2/2` on extinction and the crude bound `1-cos x<=2` on its complement yields

\[
E[1-\cos(uA)]
\le \frac{2}{H+2}
 +\frac{u^2}{2}\left(1+2V_\alpha(H)\right).
\]
