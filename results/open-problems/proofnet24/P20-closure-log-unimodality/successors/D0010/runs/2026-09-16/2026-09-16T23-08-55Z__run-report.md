## 2. Move #18 — say a finite-step unimodal density in interval-layer language

The previous parameterized examples were written as piecewise densities. The more useful language is a finite **uniform-interval layer mixture**.

Let

\[
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j\mathbf 1_{I_j}(x),\qquad \alpha_j>0,
\]

where the `I_j=[a_j,b_j]` are nondegenerate intervals. Write

\[
\ell_j=b_j-a_j,\qquad c_j=\frac{a_j+b_j}{2},\qquad p_j=\alpha_j\ell_j.
\]

If `f` is a probability density then `sum_j p_j=1`, and its law is exactly

\[
F=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j\,U(I_j),
\]

because `U(I_j)` has density `ell_j^{-1} 1_{I_j}`.

For a finite-step unimodal density, the usual superlevel/layer-cake decomposition can be chosen with **nested intervals** `I_j`, so this is not an artificial language: it is a finite representation of the geometry already present in the source class.

The complete-mixability question is now a transport question on layer labels plus a known joint-mixability question inside each selected tuple of intervals.

## 3. Move #25 — count the same mixture twice: layer marginals versus constant-sum tuples

### Theorem `P20-D0010-LAYER-TRANSPORT-03`

**Claim.** Let `F` have a finite uniform-interval mixture representation

\[
F=\sum_{j=1}^m p_j U(I_j),\qquad p_j>0,\quad \sum_jp_j=1,
\]

with interval lengths `ell_j>0` and midpoints `c_j`. Let `mu` be the mean of `F`. Fix `n>=2`.

Assume there is a finite probability tensor

\[
q_{j_1,\ldots,j_n}\ge0,\qquad \sum q_{j_1,\ldots,j_n}=1,
\]

such that:

1. **correct layer marginals:** for every coordinate `r` and label `j`,
   \[
   \sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_n:\,j_r=j}q_{j_1,\ldots,j_n}=p_j;
   \]
2. **common center on every supported tuple:** whenever `q_{j_1,...,j_n}>0`,
   \[
   \sum_{r=1}^n c_{j_r}=n\mu;
   \]
3. **uniform-interval polygon inequality on every supported tuple:** whenever `q_{j_1,...,j_n}>0`,
   \[
   \sum_{r=1}^n\ell_{j_r}\ge2\max_r\ell_{j_r}.
   \]

Then `F` is `n`-completely mixable with center `mu`.

**Status:** `PROVED / PARTIAL SOURCE RESULT / P20 SELF-CHECK`.

### Proof

Fix a supported tuple `J=(j_1,...,j_n)`. By the exact uniform-interval joint-mixability criterion of Wang–Wang (2016), condition 3 is necessary and sufficient for the tuple

\[
(U(I_{j_1}),\ldots,U(I_{j_n}))
\]

to be jointly mixable. Its joint center must equal the sum of the marginal means, namely

\[
\sum_{r=1}^n c_{j_r}=n\mu
\]

by condition 2. Therefore choose, once for each supported finite tuple `J`, a coupling

\[
(Y_{J,1},\ldots,Y_{J,n})
\]

with `Y_{J,r}~U(I_{j_r})` and

\[
\sum_{r=1}^nY_{J,r}=n\mu\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

There is no measurable-selection issue because only finitely many tuples have positive `q`-mass.

Now first sample the layer tuple `J` with law `q`, and conditional on `J` sample the selected constant-sum coupling. For coordinate `r`, its marginal law is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^m
\left(\sum_{J:j_r=j}q_J\right)U(I_j)
=
\sum_{j=1}^m p_jU(I_j)
=F
\]

by condition 1. On every conditional component the coordinate sum is `n mu`, hence the unconditional coordinate sum is also `n mu` almost surely. Thus all `n` coordinates have marginal `F` and constant sum `n mu`; `F` is `n`-CM. QED.

### Interpretation

For a fixed finite-step unimodal density, extracting its interval layers converts this theorem into a **finite transportation feasibility problem** on the label tensor `q`. This is a genuine sufficient test. I do **not** claim it is necessary, and I do **not** infer a solution of Open Problem 10 from it.

## 4. Move #3 — prove a stronger all-`n` nonmonotone tight-boundary class

The source specifically asks for extra conditions beyond unimodality. A useful test is whether the new layer criterion reaches examples outside the standard monotone-density cone, and whether it does so for arbitrary `n`, not just `n=3`.

### Construction

Fix any integer `n>=3` and put

\[
\varepsilon_n=\frac{1}{2n(n-1)(n-2)}.
\]

Define `n` outer intervals `I_j=[0,L_j]`, `j=0,...,n-1`, by

\[
L_0=1,
\]

and, for `1<=j<=n-1`,

\[
L_j=\frac1{n-1}+\varepsilon_n(n-2j).
\]

Their lengths satisfy

\[
L_1>L_2>\cdots>L_{n-1}>0,
\]

and the centered arithmetic perturbations cancel:

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}L_j=2.
\]

The smallest one is exactly

\[
L_{n-1}=\frac{2n-1}{2n(n-1)}
=\frac1n+\frac1{2n(n-1)}.
\]

Let

\[
\delta_n=\frac1{2n(n-1)}
\]

and define the interior bump interval

\[
J_n=
\left[
\frac1n-\frac{\delta_n}{2},
\frac1n+\frac{\delta_n}{2}
\right].
\]

Then `J_n` lies strictly inside `[0,L_{n-1}]`.

For any parameter `0<theta<1`, define

\[
F_{n,\theta}
=
\frac{\theta}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}U([0,L_j])
+(1-\theta)U(J_n).
\]

### Theorem `P20-D0010-NONMONOTONE-ALL-N-03`

For every integer `n>=3` and every `0<theta<1`, `F_{n,theta}` has all of the following properties:

1. support exactly `[0,1]`, with density strictly positive throughout the support;
2. mean exactly `1/n`, i.e. the **tight lower mean boundary** for `n`-CM on `[0,1]`;
3. an atomless unimodal density;
4. a density that is **not monotone**;
5. `F_{n,theta}` is `n`-completely mixable, with constant sum `1`.

Hence Open Problem 10 contains, for every `n>=3`, an explicit full-support tight-boundary nonmonotone unimodal `n`-CM family.

**Status:** `PROVED / PARTIAL SOURCE RESULT / P20 SELF-CHECK`.

### Proof of the input hypotheses

The density is

\[
f_{n,\theta}(x)
=
\frac{\theta}{n}
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\mathbf1_{[0,L_j]}(x)}{L_j}
+
\frac{1-\theta}{\delta_n}\mathbf1_{J_n}(x).
\]

Because `L_0=1` and its mixture weight is `theta/n>0`, this density is positive on the whole support `[0,1]`. There are no atoms.

Every outer interval contains `[0,L_{n-1}]`; therefore the outer part of the density is constant on `[0,L_{n-1}]`. The inner term adds one positive plateau on `J_n`: the density is constant before `J_n`, jumps **up** on entering `J_n`, and jumps back **down** on leaving it. After `L_{n-1}`, the nested outer layers disappear one at a time as `x` increases, so the density is nonincreasing thereafter. Thus the density is nondecreasing up to its bump and nonincreasing after it: it is unimodal. Because of the strict upward jump at the left endpoint of `J_n`, it is not globally monotone.

For the mean, the sum of the outer interval midpoints is

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{L_j}{2}
=1.
\]

The inner interval has midpoint exactly `1/n`. Hence

\[
E X
=
\frac{\theta}{n}\cdot1
+(1-\theta)\frac1n
=
\frac1n.
\]

This equals the lower necessary mean bound `a+(b-a)/n` for `[a,b]=[0,1]`.

### Proof of `n`-complete mixability

There are two constant-sum components.

**Outer component.** Use each of the `n` intervals `I_0,...,I_{n-1}` exactly once. Their lengths obey

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}L_j=2=2\max_jL_j
\]

because `L_0=1` is the maximum. Therefore the Wang–Wang uniform-interval criterion gives a joint mix of the `n` uniforms. The sum of their centers is

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}L_j/2=1,
\]

so the joint sum is exactly `1`.

To make the coordinate marginals identical, choose a uniformly random permutation of the `n` interval labels before using such a joint mix. Each coordinate then sees each outer uniform with probability `1/n`.

**Inner component.** Use `n` copies of `U(J_n)`. All lengths are `delta_n`, so

\[
n\delta_n\ge2\delta_n
\]

for `n>=3`, and their centers sum to `n(1/n)=1`. The same uniform-interval criterion therefore gives a joint mix with constant sum `1`.

Finally, with probability `theta` choose the outer permutation/coupling, and with probability `1-theta` choose the inner coupling. Every coordinate marginal is exactly

\[
\frac\theta n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}U(I_j)+(1-\theta)U(J_n)=F_{n,\theta},
\]

while in either branch the coordinate sum is `1`. Thus `F_{n,theta}` is `n`-CM. QED.

### Why this is not just the old monotone theorem in disguise

For `0<theta<1`, the density has an interior strict upward jump and is therefore nonmonotone. It is also not symmetric about the midpoint of its support: its mean is `1/n != 1/2` for `n>=3`. Its interior jumps exclude concavity. Its minimum density is the contribution from `U([0,1])`, namely `theta/n`, which is below the familiar sufficient lower-bound threshold `3/n` on `[0,1]` when `theta<1`. I claim only that this construction lies outside these standard sufficient subclasses, not that it evades every sufficient criterion in the literature.
