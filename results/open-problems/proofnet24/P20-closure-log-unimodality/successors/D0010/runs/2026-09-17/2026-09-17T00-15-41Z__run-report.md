## 2. Move #7 — take the limit: a closure theorem for complete mixes

The previous report proved a finite interval-layer transport criterion. Before trying to approximate arbitrary unimodal densities by such finite objects, one must know that complete mixability survives the limiting operation. It does on a fixed compact support.

### Theorem `P20-D0010-WEAK-LIMIT-CLOSURE-04`

Let `[a,b]` be compact and let `n>=2`. Suppose `F_k` is `n`-completely mixable for every `k`, all `F_k` are supported on `[a,b]`, their means are `mu_k`,

\[
F_k\Rightarrow F,\qquad \mu_k\to\mu.
\]

Then `F` is `n`-completely mixable with center `mu`.

**Status:** `PROVED / PARTIAL SOURCE TOOL / P20 SELF-CHECK`.

### Proof

For each `k`, choose a complete-mix law `gamma_k` on `[a,b]^n` whose `n` coordinate marginals are all `F_k` and which is supported on

\[
H_k=\{x\in[a,b]^n:\ x_1+\cdots+x_n=n\mu_k\}.
\]

The space of probability measures on compact `[a,b]^n` is weakly compact. Pass to a subsequence, still denoted `gamma_k`, with

\[
\gamma_k\Rightarrow\gamma.
\]

For every coordinate projection `pi_r`, continuity of pushforward under weak convergence gives

\[
(pi_r)_\#\gamma_k=F_k\Rightarrow F,
\]

hence `(pi_r)_# gamma=F` for each `r`.

Let

\[
S(x)=x_1+\cdots+x_n.
\]

For every `k`,

\[
\int (S-n\mu_k)^2\,d\gamma_k=0.
\]

The integrands converge uniformly on `[a,b]^n` to `(S-n\mu)^2`, because `mu_k->mu`; weak convergence therefore gives

\[
\int (S-n\mu)^2\,d\gamma=0.
\]

Thus `S=n mu` `gamma`-almost surely. All coordinate marginals are `F`, so `gamma` is an `n`-complete mix of `F`. QED.

### Corollary

If a bounded-support law `F` is the weak limit (in particular, an `L^1` density limit) of finite interval-mixture laws `F_k` for which the preceding P20 layer-transport certificate constructs `n`-complete mixes with centers converging to the mean of `F`, then `F` is `n`-CM.

This is a legitimate continuum extension of the finite sufficient criterion. It is **not** a characterization: the next section proves that even within a classical positive class, the most canonical nested-layer certificate can fail.

## 3. Move #20 — exact failure of the canonical nested-layer algorithm

The finite layer theorem is only useful as a source-level route if its label transport is not artificially tied to one decomposition. I tested the strongest natural specialization: for a decreasing density, take its canonical superlevel/layer-cake decomposition into intervals anchored at the left endpoint.

Consider

\[
F_* = \frac56 U[0,3/5]+\frac16 U[0,1].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Its density is

\[
f_*(x)=
\begin{cases}
14/9,&0\le x\le3/5,\\
1/6,&3/5<x\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

It is atomless, decreasing, supported on `[0,1]`, and has mean

\[
\frac56\frac{3}{10}+\frac16\frac12=\frac13.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore the Wang–Wang monotone-density theorem implies that `F_*` is `3`-CM at the tight lower mean boundary.

### Canonical layer representation

For an anchored mixture

\[
F=\int U[0,L]\,\pi(dL),
\]

the right-continuous density satisfies

\[
f(x)=\int_{[x,1]}\frac1L\,\pi(dL).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the Stieltjes tail measure `L^{-1} pi(dL)` is determined by `f`. For (3.2), the only jumps are at `L=3/5` and at the support endpoint `L=1`, giving exactly the canonical masses in (3.1): `5/6` at length `3/5` and `1/6` at length `1`.

Now apply the common-center condition from the preceding P20 finite layer-transport theorem. A supported triple of anchored uniforms `U[0,L_1],U[0,L_2],U[0,L_3]` can have joint center `1` only if

\[
\frac{L_1+L_2+L_3}{2}=1,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
L_1+L_2+L_3=2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

But if exactly `k` of the three canonical labels have length `1` and `3-k` have length `3/5`, the possible length sums are

\[
\frac95,\quad\frac{11}{5},\quad\frac{13}{5},\quad3,
\tag{3.6}
\]

none of which equals `2`.

Hence there is **no supported tuple at all** satisfying the canonical nested-layer common-center condition. In particular, no label tensor `q` using this canonical decomposition can satisfy the preceding sufficient criterion.

### Claim `P20-D0010-CANONICAL-LAYER-FAILURE-04`

There exists an atomless decreasing tight-boundary `3`-CM law for which the canonical anchored nested-layer decomposition admits **no** feasible tuple in the finite layer-transport certificate.

**Status:** `PROVED / PARTIAL SOURCE STRUCTURAL RESULT / P20 SELF-CHECK`.

## 4. Move #10 — derandomize the rescue by exact recutting

The failure above is not merely existential. The same `F_*` admits a short exact recutting into nonnested interval pieces, and each supported interval triple has a one-parameter constant-sum coupling.

Let `U~U[0,1]`. Define three tuple types.

### Type A, weight `1/5`

Intervals:

\[
[0,1/5],\quad[0,1/5],\quad[3/5,1].
\]

Coupling:

\[
(X_1,X_2,X_3)=
\left(\frac U5,\frac U5,1-\frac{2U}{5}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Each coordinate is uniform on its displayed interval (the third with reversed orientation) and the sum is identically `1`.

### Type B, weight `8/15`

Intervals:

\[
[0,1/5],\quad[1/5,3/5],\quad[2/5,3/5].
\]

Coupling:

\[
(X_1,X_2,X_3)=
\left(\frac U5,\frac35-\frac{2U}{5},\frac25+\frac U5\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Again the sum is identically `1`.

### Type C, weight `4/15`

Intervals:

\[
[1/5,2/5],\quad[1/5,2/5],\quad[1/5,3/5].
\]

Coupling:

\[
(X_1,X_2,X_3)=
\left(\frac15+\frac U5,\frac15+\frac U5,\frac35-\frac{2U}{5}\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Again the sum is identically `1`.

For each chosen tuple type, independently choose one of the three cyclic permutations of the coordinates uniformly. This makes the three coordinate marginals identical while preserving the constant sum. Finally choose types A, B, C with probabilities

\[
\lambda_A=\frac15,\qquad
\lambda_B=\frac{8}{15},\qquad
\lambda_C=\frac4{15}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The exact density of any one coordinate on the five cells

\[
[0,1/5], [1/5,2/5], [2/5,3/5], [3/5,4/5], [4/5,1]
\]

is respectively

\[
\frac{14}{9},\frac{14}{9},\frac{14}{9},\frac16,\frac16,
\tag{4.5}
\]

which is exactly (3.2). Thus this gives a completely explicit `3`-complete mix of `F_*`.

### Claim `P20-D0010-RECUT-COUPLING-04`

The canonical nested-layer certificate can fail although a finite **noncanonical recutting** admits a three-type exact interval coupling. For the explicit law `F_*`, equations (4.1)–(4.5) give such a complete mix.

### `P20-AUDIT-P21-STABLE-BLOCK-PARITY-07 = PASS (COMPONENT SCOPE ONLY)`

The local proof checks independently:

1. away from rational-grid points `0` and `1/2`, the continued-fraction perturbation is strictly smaller than the distance to either discontinuity, so signs are frozen;

3. if `q` is even, coprimality forces `p` odd and there are exactly two boundary residues, `0` and `q/2`. Their perturbed positions lie on opposite sides with opposite square-wave signs; all other residues pair under the half-period involution and cancel. Hence the block sum is exactly zero.
