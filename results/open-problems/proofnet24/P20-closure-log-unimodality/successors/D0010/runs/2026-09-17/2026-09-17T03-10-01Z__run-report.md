## 3. Idea before proof: pair the two monotone flanks at the radius level

The previous run showed that common-center n-CM laws form a convex set. A bounded unimodal density has a second convex structure that had not yet been exploited in this track: each monotone flank can be written as a mixture of uniforms having one endpoint at a mode.

When the **mode equals the mean**, the signed radius law of those one-sided uniforms has mean zero. The extremal zero-mean signed-radius laws are binary: one left radius `A` and one right radius `B`, with weights chosen so the signed radius has mean zero. Under the Khintchine/uniform transform, each binary radius law becomes a two-level **monotone** density. The 2011 Wang–Wang theorem then decides that component exactly. The only remaining question is whether left and right radii can be paired so that the component's support satisfies the moderate-mean condition.

For an n-complete mix, that support condition is simply

\[
\frac{1}{n-1}\le \frac{B}{A}\le n-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This turns the asymmetric-unimodal problem into a one-dimensional transport condition between two size-biased flank-radius measures.

## 4. New theorem — paired-flank quantile criterion

### Claim `P20-D0010-PAIRED-FLANK-CM-06`

Let `n>=2`. Let `F` be a nondegenerate absolutely continuous distribution with bounded essential support `[a,b]` and a bounded unimodal density `f`. Assume that `f` has a mode `m` equal to its mean:

\[
E_F X=m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose monotone representatives so that `f` is nondecreasing on `[a,m]` and nonincreasing on `[m,b]`. Put

\[
R_-:=m-a,\qquad R_+:=b-m.
\]

Define the flank Stieltjes measures `sigma_-` on `(0,R_-]` and `sigma_+` on `(0,R_+]` by the tail identities

\[
\sigma_-([r,R_-])=f(m-r),\qquad
\sigma_+([s,R_+])=f(m+s)
\tag{4.2}
\]

at continuity points of the monotone representatives. Set

\[
M_-:=\int r^2\,\sigma_-(dr),\qquad
M_+:=\int s^2\,\sigma_+(ds).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then (4.1) implies `M_-=M_+`. Write their common positive value as `M`, and define probability measures

\[
\alpha(dr):=\frac{r^2}{M}\sigma_-(dr),\qquad
\beta(ds):=\frac{s^2}{M}\sigma_+(ds).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let `Q_-` and `Q_+` be generalized quantile functions of `alpha` and `beta`. If

\[
\boxed{
\frac{1}{n-1}Q_-(u)\le Q_+(u)\le (n-1)Q_-(u)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }u\in(0,1),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

then `F` is n-completely mixable with center `m`.

Equivalently, on logarithmic radii, the monotone quantile coupling of `alpha` and `beta` has essential displacement at most `log(n-1)`; (4.5) is the direct multiplicative form and does not require any logarithmic moment assumption.

Status: **PROVED / PARTIAL SOURCE RESULT / P20 SELF-CHECK**.

This is an infinite-dimensional sufficient condition for asymmetric unimodal densities; it is not a full characterization of the source class, so D0010 remains PARTIAL.

## 5. Full proof

### 5.1 One-sided uniform representation

Because the left profile `r -> f(m-r)` is nonincreasing, the Stieltjes measure `sigma_-` in (4.2) exists; similarly for `sigma_+`. Define finite radius measures

\[
G_-(dr):=r\,\sigma_-(dr),\qquad
G_+(ds):=s\,\sigma_+(ds).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `x=m-r<m`,

\[
\int_{[r,R_-]}\frac1t\,G_-(dt)
=\sigma_-([r,R_-])
=f(x),
\]

and similarly on the right. Hence, up to the irrelevant value at the single point `m`, the law `F` is exactly

\[
F
=
\int U[m-r,m]\,G_-(dr)
+
\int U[m,m+s]\,G_+(ds),
\tag{5.2}
\]

where `U[I]` denotes the uniform probability law on the interval `I`.

Tonelli's theorem gives

\[
G_-((0,R_-])
=\int_0^{R_-} f(m-r)\,dr,
\qquad
G_+((0,R_+])
=\int_0^{R_+} f(m+s)\,ds,
\tag{5.3}
\]

so the two masses sum to one.

The mean of `U[m-r,m]` is `m-r/2`, while that of `U[m,m+s]` is `m+s/2`. Therefore (5.2) and `E X=m` imply

\[
\int r\,G_-(dr)=\int s\,G_+(ds).
\tag{5.4}
\]

By (5.1), these two quantities are exactly `M_-` and `M_+` from (4.3). Nondegeneracy makes the common value `M` strictly positive. Thus (4.4) indeed defines two probability measures.

### 5.2 Pair the size-biased flank radii

Let `U` be uniform on `(0,1)` and set

\[
A:=Q_-(U),\qquad B:=Q_+(U).
\]

Let `pi` be the law of `(A,B)`. Its marginals are `alpha` and `beta`, and (4.5) says that `pi` is supported on

\[
\mathcal R_n
:=\{(r,s): r>0,s>0,\ s\le(n-1)r,\ r\le(n-1)s\}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Define a measure `Lambda` on `mathcal R_n` by

\[
d\Lambda(r,s)
:=M\frac{r+s}{rs}\,d\pi(r,s).
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is a probability measure. Indeed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda(\mathcal R_n)
&=M\int\left(\frac1r+\frac1s\right)d\pi(r,s)\\
&=M\int\frac1r\,d\alpha(r)+M\int\frac1s\,d\beta(s)\\
&=G_-((0,R_-])+G_+((0,R_+])=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

### 5.3 The extremal binary component is monotone and exactly decidable

For `(r,s) in mathcal R_n`, define

\[
H_{r,s}
:=\frac{s}{r+s}U[m-r,m]
+\frac{r}{r+s}U[m,m+s].
\tag{5.8}
\]

Its mean is exactly `m`, since

\[
\frac{s}{r+s}\left(m-\frac r2\right)
+
\frac{r}{r+s}\left(m+\frac s2\right)=m.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Its density on its essential support `[m-r,m+s]` has the two constant values

\[
\frac{s}{r(r+s)}\quad\text{on }[m-r,m),
\qquad
\frac{r}{s(r+s)}\quad\text{on }(m,m+s].
\tag{5.10}
\]

If `s>=r`, the first height is at least the second, so the density is nonincreasing. If `s<=r`, it is nondecreasing. Thus `H_{r,s}` has a monotone density.

The moderate-mean condition for `H_{r,s}` to be n-CM is

\[
m-r+\frac{r+s}{n}\le m
\le m+s-\frac{r+s}{n},
\tag{5.11}
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
s\le(n-1)r,\qquad r\le(n-1)s.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Those inequalities hold because `(r,s) in mathcal R_n`. The Wang–Wang monotone-density theorem therefore implies that **every** `H_{r,s}` occurring in (5.8) is n-CM with center `m`.

### 5.4 Reconstruct the original asymmetric unimodal law

The barycenter of the `H_{r,s}` under `Lambda` is exactly `F`. On the left side, the coefficient of the uniform law `U[m-r,m]` is

\[
\int \frac{s}{r+s}\,d\Lambda(r,s)
=\int \frac Mr\,d\pi(r,s)
=\frac Mr\,d\alpha(r)
=dG_-(r),
\tag{5.13}
\]

as an identity of measures in `r`. Similarly,

\[
\int \frac{r}{r+s}\,d\Lambda(r,s)
=dG_+(s)
\tag{5.14}
\]

on the right. Hence

\[
\boxed{F=\int H_{r,s}\,d\Lambda(r,s).}
\tag{5.15}
\]

Finite convex combinations of the laws `H_{r,s}` are n-CM because they all have the same center `m` (Puccetti–Wang–Wang 2012, Theorem 3.2(a)). Approximate the probability measure `Lambda` weakly by finitely supported probability measures on the closed ratio band, with the limiting point `(0,0)` interpreted as `delta_m` if needed. The corresponding barycenters converge weakly to (5.15). The fixed-center n-CM class is weakly closed by Puccetti–Wang–Wang 2012, Theorem 3.1(a). Therefore `F` is n-CM with center `m`.

This proves the claim. `square`

## 6. NEW-METHOD #5 — add a radius parameter and differentiate in it

The theorem above is stated with Stieltjes flank measures so that it covers step densities and other bounded monotone flanks without differentiability assumptions. For a density that is absolutely continuous on each flank, the criterion becomes a direct differential shape test.

Let `R_-=m-a`, `R_+=b-m`. Then

\[
\sigma_-(dr)
=f'(m-r)\,dr+f(a+)\,\delta_{R_-}(dr),
\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
\sigma_+(ds)
=-f'(m+s)\,ds+f(b-)\,\delta_{R_+}(ds),
\tag{6.2}
\]

in the Stieltjes sense. Consequently the two probability laws to compare are the **second-radius-weighted flank-slope laws**

\[
\alpha(dr)
=\frac{r^2 f'(m-r)\,dr+R_-^2f(a+)\,\delta_{R_-}(dr)}{M},
\tag{6.3}
\]

\[
\beta(ds)
=\frac{s^2[-f'(m+s)]\,ds+R_+^2f(b-)\,\delta_{R_+}(ds)}{M}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

If the endpoint densities vanish, the endpoint atoms disappear. Thus the radius parameter is not bookkeeping: differentiating the two monotone flanks exposes exactly the two probability measures whose quantiles must remain within the factor `n-1` band.

At the **lower source boundary** emphasized by Wang,

\[
m=E X=a+\frac{b-a}{n},
\tag{6.5}
\]

so

\[
R_+=(n-1)R_-.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The allowable quantile distortion in (4.5) therefore matches the source's extremal support ratio exactly. The upper-boundary case follows by reflection.

Outcome of #5: **a source-facing differential sufficient condition, not another finite family**.

## 8. NEW-METHOD #24 — identify the extremal object

The convex set of signed-radius laws with fixed mean zero has binary laws as its elementary mean-preserving building blocks. The explicit size-biased construction (5.6) realizes exactly such a decomposition: each component uses one left radius and one right radius, with weights `s/(r+s)` and `r/(r+s)`.
