## 3. Stuck blocker — exact statement

At the lower tight boundary for `n=3`, after affine normalization to support `[0,1]` and mean `1/3`, complete mixability is exactly the existence of a probability measure on the constant-sum triangle with all three marginals equal to `F`:

\[
\boxed{
\exists\,\gamma\in\mathcal P(T),\qquad
T=\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in[0,1]^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=1\},\qquad
(\pi_i)_\#\gamma=F\ (i=1,2,3).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The unresolved source-level step is not (3.1) itself; it is to turn unimodality plus a **usable additional condition** into a verifiable criterion guaranteeing (3.1), in a way that materially extends the already-known monotone, symmetric-unimodal, concave and pointwise-lower-bound classes.

Known marginal duality gives an exact abstract reformulation. Because the marginals and support constraint are permutation-symmetric, a separating triple of continuous potentials can be symmetrized. Thus the route reduces to controlling the cone of scalar tests

\[
\boxed{
\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)+\varphi(1-x-y)\ge0
\quad
( x\ge0,\ y\ge0,\ x+y\le1 )
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and proving the corresponding nonnegative integral condition against `F`. This is a **known-duality reframing**, not a new P20 theorem claim. The blocker is to use unimodality to reduce or control the infinite cone (3.2) by structural conditions that are simpler than complete mixability itself.

## 4. Routes already tried, and the precise reason each stops

### Route A — boundary tail counting

For an atomless complete mix at the lower boundary, counting coordinates above the boundary threshold gives

\[
P(X>1/3)\ge1/3.
\]

This was powerful enough to make the earlier special families sharp. It is not a characterization of general unimodal laws: it is one necessary inequality and does not construct a constant-sum coupling. Wang's source already warns that the mean condition alone fails in the unimodal class, and later detection work contains borderline cases not settled by one tail statistic.

### Route B — parameterized special families

The three-piece family and then the all-`m` affine family make the tail inequality collide exactly with the monotone-density theorem, yielding sharp iff thresholds and saturating `J_boundary` at `UNBOUNDED`.

This route is exhausted for the frozen metric: adding more pieces cannot move `UNBOUNDED`, and the special affine relations were engineered so that the positive side falls directly into the known monotone theorem. They therefore do not reveal the missing generic unimodal mechanism.

### Route C — finite interval-layer transport

A finite mixture of interval uniforms can be certified by a tensor of layer tuples whose coordinate marginals match the layer weights and whose interval lengths/midpoints meet a joint-mixability condition. This gives real nonmonotone sufficient classes.

The obstacle is completeness: a completely mixable marginal need not be certifiable from its **canonical** nested layers. The preceding run's law

\[
F_* = \frac56 U[0,3/5]+\frac16 U[0,1]
\]

is 3-CM by the monotone-density theorem but has no eligible canonical layer tuple. A noncanonical recutting succeeds. Hence canonical-layer infeasibility cannot be a necessary condition.

### Route D — recutting algorithms

The same `F_*` admits a short explicit recutting coupling, proving that recutting is genuinely more flexible than the canonical decomposition.

What is missing is a termination/feasibility theorem: there is no proved rule that converts an arbitrary unimodal density satisfying a simple extra condition into a finite or limiting sequence of recuts with constant sum. Without such a rule, “allow recutting” is a representation language, not a criterion.

### Route E — weak limits

On a common compact interval, weak limits of `n`-CM laws are `n`-CM by compactness of the coupling space. This validates approximation-based proofs once a dense certifiable subclass has been found.

But the missing premise is exactly that density: no argument yet shows that every member of a useful new unimodal subclass can be approximated by the certifiable recutting families while preserving the source boundary condition and the required coupling geometry.

### Route F — finite/discrete search

Discretization can reveal feasible transport tensors and dual certificates. It cannot by itself give the source-level continuous theorem. Haus's matrix complete-mixability results also warn that unrestricted discrete complete-mixability feasibility is computationally hard in general; a useful continuous theorem must exploit one-dimensional unimodality much more strongly than a generic assignment formulation does.

## 7. NEW-METHOD #21 — ask who else has this shape

Three neighboring subjects have the same mathematical shape:

1. **Marginal problems / multi-marginal optimal transport.** Kellerer gives support-sensitive marginal duality; modern MOT gives the same primal-dual language on compact/Polish products. This supplies an exact cone of obstructions.
2. **Complete/joint mixability.** The solved one-dimensional classes succeed because they have additional order/shape structure: monotone density converts the mean condition into sufficiency; symmetric unimodality admits special rearrangements; concavity and lower-density hypotheses give other constructive criteria.
3. **Discrete matrix mixability / multidimensional bottleneck assignment.** This provides algorithmic analogues but also hardness results. It cautions that a general finite LP/assignment description without exploiting unimodality is unlikely to become a simple theorem.

What the nearest solved cases possess and the generic bounded unimodal case currently lacks is therefore quite specific: a structural principle reducing the full marginal-duality obstruction cone to finitely or monotonically controlled tests, or an equivalent constructive recutting algorithm whose feasibility follows from a transparent shape inequality.

Outcome of #21: **the missing ingredient is identified more sharply, but not supplied**.
