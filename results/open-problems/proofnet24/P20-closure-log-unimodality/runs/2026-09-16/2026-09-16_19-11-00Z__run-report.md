# 5. Move #4 — `L^1` openness of finitely many strict physical modes

The previous correction proved local branch stability under Gaussian smoothing. The same mechanism gives the precise topology needed for a diagonal limit.

## Theorem `P20-I0081-L1-MODE-OPENNESS-10 = PROVED`

Let `h` be a probability density on `R` and let `mu=exp_* h`. Suppose `rho=mu boxtimes mu` has strict positive log-density local maxima in pairwise disjoint compact intervals

`I_1,...,I_m subset (0,infinity)`,

and suppose on a neighborhood of each `I_j` the physical self-product subordination branch is an interior simple branch. Then there exists `eps>0` such that every probability density `htilde` satisfying

`||htilde-h||_1 < eps`

and sufficiently close in the same positive-line class has, for `mutilde=exp_* htilde`, at least one positive log-density local maximum in every `I_j`. If `htilde` is even then the output is inversion symmetric and the corresponding negative mirrors are also present.

### Proof

For `z in C^+` write

`M_h(z)=int_R h(u)/(1-z e^u) du`,

`eta_h(z)=1-1/M_h(z)`.

Fix a compact set `K compact C^+` containing the finitely many physical subordination curves over slightly smaller neighborhoods of the intervals `I_j`. For `r=0,1,2`,

`partial_z^r (1-z e^u)^(-1)=r! e^{ru}(1-z e^u)^(-r-1)`.

For `z` in compact `K`, the right side is uniformly bounded over all real `u`: with `t=e^u`, it is continuous for `t>0`, bounded as `t->0`, and is `O(t^{-1})` as `t->infinity`. Hence constants `C_{K,r}<infinity` exist with

`||M_htilde-M_h||_{C^2(K)} <= C_K ||htilde-h||_1`.       (5.1)

For positive-line inputs `Im M_h(z)>0` on `C^+`; on the fixed compact physical neighborhoods `M_h` is therefore bounded away from zero. Thus (5.1) gives

`eta_htilde -> eta_h` in `C^2(K)` as `||htilde-h||_1->0`. (5.2)

For the self-product, the physical boundary root is characterized locally by

`Phi_h(q;y)=q^2-e^{-y} eta_h(q)=0`.                      (5.3)

By hypothesis each relevant branch is simple, so `partial_q Phi_h` is bounded away from zero after shrinking the finitely many neighborhoods. The analytic implicit-function theorem and (5.2) therefore produce a unique nearby branch `q_htilde(y)` for every `y` in those neighborhoods, uniformly in `y`.

The branch is physical rather than an arbitrary algebraic continuation: the root remains in `C^+`; the corresponding multiplicative-subordination self-map is non-automorphic for our atomless density inputs; Denjoy-Wolff uniqueness identifies the interior fixed point with the physical subordination value. This is exactly the branch-stability dependency made explicit in the immutable correction to run 09.

On this branch the output log-density is the same analytic boundary expression used throughout P20,

`g_h(y)=(1/pi) Im [1/(1-w_h(y))]`,

where `w_h(y)=eta_h(q_h(y))`.                           (5.4)

Equations (5.2)–(5.4) imply local uniform convergence

`g_htilde -> g_h`

on the finitely many mode neighborhoods.

For each strict local maximum choose a point `x_j in int(I_j)` and `delta_j>0` such that

`g_h(x_j) >= max_{y in boundary I_j} g_h(y)+3 delta_j`. (5.5)

For sufficiently small `||htilde-h||_1`, uniform convergence makes the error below `delta_j` simultaneously for all `j`. Then

`g_htilde(x_j) > max_{y in boundary I_j} g_htilde(y)+delta_j`. (5.6)

The maximum of `g_htilde` on compact `I_j` is therefore attained in the interior, hence is a local maximum. The intervals are disjoint, so the maxima are distinct. This proves the theorem. `square`

# 6. Move #27 — swap the quantifiers

The previous theorem was

`for every finite K there exists h_K with at least K output modes`.

The stronger quantifier order is

`there exists one h_infinity whose output has infinitely many modes`.

That stronger statement is true.

## Theorem `P20-I0081-SINGLE-INFINITE-MODE-10 = PROVED / P20 SELF-CHECK`

There exists an even, nonnegative, nonincreasing-on-`[0,infinity)` probability density `h_infinity` on `R`, positive at every finite point, such that for

`mu_infinity=exp_* h_infinity`

the free multiplicative self-convolution

`mu_infinity boxtimes mu_infinity`

has infinitely many distinct **strict** local maxima in its logarithmic density on the positive half-line. By inversion symmetry it also has their negative mirrors.

Consequently there is a **single** multiplicatively symmetric log-unimodal source-admissible input whose output log-density has infinitely many modes.

### Proof

We use only the already-proved finite-stage outer-plateau adjunction theorem and the new openness theorem.

#### Step 1. Finite-stage induction

Start from any compact even log-density seed, for example

`h_0(u)=1/2 * 1_{[-1,1]}(u)`.

At stage `n`, assume `h_n` is an even compactly supported probability density, nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`, and that `mu_n=exp_* h_n` has `n` already-created pairwise disjoint positive strict-mode intervals

`I_1,...,I_n`.

For `n=0` there is nothing to protect.

Apply the inherited theorem `P20-I0081-OUTER-SCALE-WINDOW-09` with the concrete scale choice

`c_R=e^{-R/2}`.

For all sufficiently large `R`, the normalized update

`T_R h_n(u)=[h_n(u)+c_R 1_{[-R,R]}(u)]/[1+2R c_R]` (6.1)

preserves all old finite-stage modes and creates one new positive sign pair strictly to their right. The finite-stage input is compact and atomless, so Ji continuity plus the analytic argument in the run-09 correction upgrades the new sign pair to a strict local maximum in a compact interval `I_{n+1}` disjoint from the preceding intervals.

Set `h_{n+1}=T_{R_{n+1}}h_n` after choosing `R_{n+1}` as below.

#### Step 2. The update can be made arbitrarily small in `L^1`

Put

`epsilon_R=2R c_R`.

Since `int h_n=1`, (6.1) gives exactly

`T_R h_n-h_n=[c_R 1_{[-R,R]}-epsilon_R h_n]/[1+epsilon_R]`.

Therefore

`||T_R h_n-h_n||_1
 <= [2R c_R+epsilon_R]/[1+epsilon_R]
 = 2 epsilon_R/(1+epsilon_R)
 <= 2 epsilon_R
 = 4R e^{-R/2}`.                                      (6.2)

The right side tends to zero. Thus the same large-`R` choice which creates the new remote mode can also satisfy an arbitrarily severe `L^1` perturbation budget.

#### Step 3. Assign persistence radii

After constructing `h_n`, apply `P20-I0081-L1-MODE-OPENNESS-10` to its first `n` strict-mode intervals. Obtain a persistence radius `delta_n>0` such that every probability density within `delta_n` in `L^1` retains at least one mode in each of `I_1,...,I_n`.

When selecting `R_{n+1}`, require simultaneously:

1. the outer-plateau theorem creates the new interval `I_{n+1}` to the right of all previous ones;
2. `epsilon_{n+1}=2R_{n+1}e^{-R_{n+1}/2} < 2^{-n-4}`;
3. with `d_n=||h_{n+1}-h_n||_1`,

`d_n < 2^{-n-4} min(1,delta_1,...,delta_n)`.          (6.3)

All three conditions can be met because the mode-adjunction theorem holds for every sufficiently large `R` while both `epsilon_R` and the bound in (6.2) tend to zero.

#### Step 4. Take the diagonal `L^1` limit

From (6.3), `sum_n d_n<infinity`, so `(h_n)` is Cauchy in `L^1`. Let

`h_n -> h_infinity` in `L^1`.                         (6.4)

Every `h_n` is a probability density, so `h_infinity>=0` and `int h_infinity=1` after choosing the standard nonnegative representative.

Evenness is closed under `L^1` convergence. The cone of nonincreasing functions on `[0,infinity)` is also `L^1`-closed: by Helly selection a subsequence of the restrictions has a pointwise limit at all continuity points which is nonincreasing; uniqueness of the `L^1` limit identifies that monotone function with `h_infinity` almost everywhere. Hence `h_infinity` has an even nonincreasing representative.

Therefore `mu_infinity=exp_*h_infinity` is exactly multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal.

#### Step 5. No previously created mode disappears in the limit

Fix `j>=1`. For every `n>=j`, condition (6.3) includes `delta_j` in the minimum, so

`d_n < 2^{-n-4} delta_j`.

Consequently

`||h_infinity-h_j||_1
 <= sum_{n=j}^infinity d_n
 < delta_j sum_{n=j}^infinity 2^{-n-4}
 < delta_j`.                                          (6.5)

By the definition of `delta_j`, the final output `mu_infinity boxtimes mu_infinity` retains a local maximum inside every one of the disjoint intervals `I_1,...,I_j`. Since `j` is arbitrary, it has a local maximum in every `I_j`, hence infinitely many distinct positive local maxima.

The local physical branch in each protected `I_j` is analytic and the value-gap construction makes the output nonconstant there. A nonconstant real-analytic function cannot have a non-strict flat local maximum. Thus the retained maxima can be taken strict.

#### Step 6. The limiting input has full logarithmic support

The construction may be chosen with `R_n -> infinity`. Fix finite `u`. Choose `N` with `R_N>|u|`. At stage `N`, the newly added plateau gives `h_N(u)>0`. Later updates satisfy

`h_{n+1}(u) >= h_n(u)/(1+epsilon_{n+1})`.

Because `sum epsilon_n<infinity` by condition 2, the product

`prod_{n>N}(1+epsilon_n)`

is finite. Hence

`h_infinity(u) >= h_N(u) / prod_{n>N}(1+epsilon_n) >0`. (6.6)

So the final input is not merely unbounded-support: its logarithmic density is positive at every finite point.

Finally `h_infinity` is even, so `mu_infinity=mu_infinity^{-1}`. The source inversion covariance gives

`(mu_infinity boxtimes mu_infinity)^{-1}=mu_infinity boxtimes mu_infinity`.

Thus every positive mode has a negative mirror. The theorem follows. `square`

# 7. Move #29 — what “adding the point at infinity” accomplished

The finite theorem alone permits a logical failure mode: the `j`th mode might be pushed farther and farther out or erased by later normalizations, so no single limiting input need inherit infinitely many modes. The diagonal budgets (6.3)–(6.5) rule out exactly that escape-to-infinity pathology.

The construction separates two uses of a remote scale:

- `R_{n+1}` is taken **large enough** to create a new mode beyond the old ones;
- the plateau mass `2R_{n+1}e^{-R_{n+1}/2}` is simultaneously **small enough** that the full infinite tail of all later modifications stays inside every previously assigned `L^1` persistence radius.

Thus infinity is used as a source of new scales, but the modes already created remain at fixed finite intervals in the final object. This is the successful outcome of Move #29.

The theorem is stronger than the frozen metric but does not change its displayed value: once one input has infinitely many modes, the family-wide maximum is already `UNBOUNDED`.
