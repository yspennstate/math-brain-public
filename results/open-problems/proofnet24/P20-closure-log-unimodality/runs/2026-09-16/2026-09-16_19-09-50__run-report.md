## 10. From derivative signs to four positive modes

The regularity/subordination theorem and the certified simple-root boxes imply that `g` is real analytic in neighborhoods of the eight displayed points. In particular it is continuous on each of the four compact intervals below and differentiable near both endpoints.

Consider the four pairwise disjoint intervals

\[
[8,18],\qquad[29,37],\qquad[46,48],\qquad[53,54].
\tag{10.1}
\]

On each interval `[a,b]`, (9.1) gives `g'(a)>0` and `g'(b)<0`. Hence for sufficiently small positive `epsilon`,

\[
g(a+\epsilon)>g(a),
\qquad
g(b-\epsilon)>g(b).
\]

The continuous function `g` attains a maximum on `[a,b]`, and those two inequalities exclude both endpoints. Therefore a maximizer lies in the open interval `(a,b)` and is a local maximum of `g` on the real line. Since the four intervals in (10.1) are disjoint, this yields **four distinct positive logarithmic-density modes**.

No assumption is made about whether further positive modes occur between or beyond these intervals.

## 11. Inversion symmetry doubles four positive modes to eight

The input satisfies `mu=mu^{-1}`. If free positive variables `X,Y` both have law `mu`, then

\[
(X^{1/2}YX^{1/2})^{-1}
=X^{-1/2}Y^{-1}X^{-1/2}.
\]

Functional calculus preserves freeness of the generated algebras, so `X^{-1}` and `Y^{-1}` are free with law `mu^{-1}=mu`. Therefore

\[
\rho^{-1}
=(\mu\boxtimes\mu)^{-1}
=\mu^{-1}\boxtimes\mu^{-1}
=\mu\boxtimes\mu
=\rho.
\tag{11.1}
\]

Thus `log_* rho` is symmetric under `y -> -y`; its density satisfies

\[
g(-y)=g(y).
\tag{11.2}
\]

Every one of the four positive local maxima from Section 10 therefore has a distinct negative mirror. None can coincide with zero. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
N_{\rm modes}(\log_* (\mu\boxtimes\mu))\ge 8.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
