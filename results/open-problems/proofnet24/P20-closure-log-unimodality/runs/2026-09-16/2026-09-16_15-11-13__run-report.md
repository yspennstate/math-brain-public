# 3. Counterexample-first search: exact failed family

Before attempting a proof, I tested an explicit known admissible family where `\boxtimes` is algebraically closed.

For `0<alpha<1`, the positive Boolean stable law `b_alpha:=b_alpha^1` has density

\[
f_\alpha(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\,
\frac{x^{\alpha-1}}{x^{2\alpha}+2x^\alpha\cos(\pi\alpha)+1},\qquad x>0.
\]

It is multiplicatively symmetric because direct substitution gives

\[
x^{-2}f_\alpha(1/x)=f_\alpha(x).
\]

Its log-push density is

\[
g_\alpha(y)=e^y f_\alpha(e^y)
=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{2\pi}
\frac{1}{\cosh(\alpha y)+\cos(\pi\alpha)}.
\]

Hence `g_alpha` is even and

\[
g_\alpha'(y)
=-\frac{\alpha\sin(\pi\alpha)}{2\pi}
\frac{\sinh(\alpha y)}{(\cosh(\alpha y)+\cos(\pi\alpha))^2},
\]

so it has exactly one mode, at zero.

Arizmendi-Hasebe Proposition 5.1(1) gives, after writing
`alpha=1/(1+t)` and `beta=1/(1+s)`, the exact identity

\[
b_\alpha\boxtimes b_\beta=b_\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta-\alpha\beta}.
\]

Thus this broad explicit admissible family preserves one log-density mode and does **not** refute Problem 4.3. This failed route is retained rather than discarded.

# 4. Explicit admissible witness

For `L>0`, define the log-uniform probability measure

\[
\boxed{
\mu_L(dx)=\frac{1}{2Lx}\mathbf 1_{[e^{-L},e^L]}(x)\,dx.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Its logarithmic push-forward is exactly `Unif[-L,L]`. Therefore:

- `mu_L` is log-unimodal (with mode 1; a flat top is allowed by the source definition), and
- `mu_L=mu_L^{-1}` because `Unif[-L,L]` is symmetric.

So `(mu_L,mu_L)` is admissible for Problem 4.3 for every `L>0`.

The remainder proves that `L=12` is a counterexample.

# 5. Exact transform of the log-uniform input

Put

\[
a=e^{-L}\in(0,1).
\]

For `z` off the reciprocal-support cut, direct integration gives

\[
\psi_L(z)
=\int\frac{xz}{1-xz}\,d\mu_L(x)
=\frac1{2L}\log\frac{1-az}{1-a^{-1}z},
\tag{5.1}
\]

with the branch normalized by `psi_L(0)=0`.

Let `w=eta_L(z)` and recall

\[
\eta=\frac{\psi}{1+\psi},
\qquad
\psi=\frac{w}{1-w}.
\]

Set

\[
E(w)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2Lw}{1-w}\right).
\]

Exponentiating (5.1) and solving the resulting linear equation in `z` gives the local inverse

\[
\boxed{
q_L(w):=\eta_{\mu_L}^{-1}(w)
=\frac{1-E(w)}{a-a^{-1}E(w)}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed `(1-aq_L)/(1-a^{-1}q_L)=E` exactly.

Now set

\[
\rho_L=\mu_L\boxtimes\mu_L.
\]

The source's Sigma-transform identity

\[
\Sigma_{\mu\boxtimes\nu}=\Sigma_\mu\Sigma_\nu,
\qquad
\Sigma_\mu(w)=\frac{\eta_\mu^{-1}(w)}{w},
\]

therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
Q_L(w):=\eta_{\rho_L}^{-1}(w)=\frac{q_L(w)^2}{w}
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

as an analytic germ around the negative real axis.

# 6. Unit-circle phase and the physical center branch

Write

\[
w=e^{i\theta}=e^{2it},\qquad 0<t<\frac\pi2.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{w}{1-w}=-\frac12+\frac{i}{2}\cot t.
\]

Put

\[
\phi=L\cot t.
\]

Since `a=e^{-L}`,

\[
E(w)=ae^{i\phi}.
\]

Let

\[
B_a(v)=\frac{v-a}{1-av}.
\]

From (5.2), on the unit circle,

\[
q_L(w)=\frac{1-ae^{i\phi}}{a-e^{i\phi}}
=-\frac1{B_a(e^{i\phi})}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The Blaschke factor has modulus one. Let `beta_a(phi)` be its continuously unwrapped argument on `[0,pi]`, normalized by `beta_a(0)=0`. Then

\[
\beta_a'(\phi)=P_a(\phi)
:=\frac{1-a^2}{1-2a\cos\phi+a^2}>0,
\tag{6.2}
\]

and `beta_a(pi)=pi`.

Define the unwrapped phase of `Q_L` on this arc by

\[
\varphi_L(\theta)
=2\pi-\theta-2\beta_a\!\left(L\cot\frac\theta2\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus

\[
Q_L(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\varphi_L(\theta)}.
\]

Let

\[
t_0=\arctan(L/\pi),\qquad \theta_0=2t_0.
\]

At `t=t_0` we have `phi=pi`, hence

\[
\varphi_L(\theta_0)=-2t_0<0.
\]

At `t=pi/2`, `phi=0` and

\[
\varphi_L(\pi)=\pi.
\]

Differentiating (6.3) gives, with `t=theta/2`,

\[
\boxed{
\varphi_L'(\theta)
=L\csc^2 t\,P_a(\phi)-1.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Since

\[
P_a(\phi)\ge\frac{1-a}{1+a},
\]

for `L=12` and `a=e^{-12}<10^{-5}` we have throughout the arc

\[
\varphi_{12}'(\theta)
>12\frac{99999}{100001}-1
=\frac{1099987}{100001}>0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Consequently there is a unique

\[
t_*\in(t_0,\pi/2),\qquad \theta_*=2t_*,
\]

such that

\[
\varphi_{12}(\theta_*)=0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

The branch identification matters because the explicit function `Q_L` can have multiple preimages of a positive real value after phase winding. Here is why (6.6) selects the physical one.

First, at `w=-1` (that is, `t=pi/2`) formula (5.2) gives `q_L(-1)=-1`, hence `Q_L(-1)=-1`. This is on the genuine inverse germ supplied by the Sigma-transform. Second, by (6.5), `Q_L'(w)` does not vanish on the arc from `w=-1` to `w_*=e^{i\theta_*}`. Third, (6.3) and strict monotonicity show that this arc is mapped homeomorphically onto the upper unit semicircle from `-1` to `1`; except at the endpoint, its image lies in the upper half-plane, away from the positive-real cut of `eta_{rho_L}`.

Therefore the identity

\[
\eta_{\rho_L}(Q_L(w))=w
\]

which holds on the inverse germ near `-1`, analytically continues step-by-step along this noncritical arc. Taking the upper boundary limit at the endpoint gives

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{\rho_L}(1+i0)=w_*=e^{i\theta_*}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

# 7. Log-density formula near the center

For any probability measure on the positive line,

\[
G_\rho(1/z)
=z(1+\psi_\rho(z))
=\frac{z}{1-\eta_\rho(z)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `x>0`, approach `z=1/x` from the upper half-plane, and set

\[
w=\eta_\rho(1/x+i0).
\]

Then `1/z` approaches `x` from the lower half-plane. Stieltjes inversion applied to (7.1) yields

\[
xf_\rho(x)
=\frac1\pi\Im\frac1{1-w}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence for the log-push density

\[
g(y)=e^y f_{\rho_L}(e^y),
\]

putting `r=e^{-y}` and `w(r)=eta_{rho_L}(r+i0)` gives

\[
\boxed{
g(y)=\frac1\pi\Im\frac1{1-w(e^{-y})}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Because `Q_L'(w_*)` is nonzero, the inverse branch `w(r)` is analytic near `r=1`. Therefore `g` is real-analytic near `y=0` and is positive there.

# 8. Exact center-curvature formula

Work in the logarithmic coordinate

\[
\zeta=\log w
\]

near

\[
\zeta_*=i\theta_*=2it_*.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal Q(\zeta)=\log Q_L(e^\zeta)
\]

using the local logarithm for which `mathcal Q(zeta_*)=0`. Along the unit arc,

\[
\mathcal Q(i\theta)=i\varphi_L(\theta).
\]

Set

\[
A=\varphi_L'(\theta_*)>0.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal Q'(\zeta_*)=A,
\qquad
\mathcal Q''(\zeta_*)=-i\varphi_L''(\theta_*).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Since `Q_L(w(e^{-y}))=e^{-y}`,

\[
\mathcal Q(\zeta(y))=-y.
\]

Differentiating twice at zero gives

\[
\zeta'(0)=-\frac1A,
\qquad
\zeta''(0)=\frac{i\varphi_L''(\theta_*)}{A^3}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Now define

\[
H(\zeta)=\frac1{1-e^\zeta}.
\]

At `zeta_*=2it_*`, direct differentiation gives

\[
H'(\zeta_*)=-\frac14\csc^2 t_*,
\qquad
H''(\zeta_*)=-\frac{i}{4}\csc^2t_*\cot t_*.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Combining (7.3), (8.2), and (8.3) yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 g''(0)
 =-\frac{\csc^2 t_*}{4\pi A^3}
 \left[\varphi_L''(\theta_*)+A\cot t_*\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

It remains only to determine the sign of the bracket.

# 9. Cancellation and the explicit L=12 sign

Write

\[
D=1-2a\cos\phi+a^2,
\qquad
\phi=L\cot t_*.
\]

From (6.4),

\[
A=L\csc^2t_*\frac{1-a^2}{D}-1.
\]

Differentiating with respect to `theta` and adding `A cot t_*`, the terms coming from differentiating `csc^2 t` cancel exactly. One obtains

\[
\boxed{
\varphi_L''(\theta_*)+A\cot t_*
=-\cot t_*
+\frac{L^2a(1-a^2)\sin\phi}{D^2\sin^4t_*}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Because `t_*` lies between `t_0` and `pi/2`,

\[
0<\phi=L\cot t_*<\pi.
\]

Hence `sin(phi)<phi=L cot(t_*)`. Also

\[
D\ge(1-a)^2,
\qquad
\sin t_*>\sin t_0=\frac{L}{\sqrt{L^2+\pi^2}}.
\]

Therefore the positive correction term in (9.1) is strictly bounded by

\[
\cot t_*\,C_L,
\qquad
C_L=
\frac{a(1-a^2)(L^2+\pi^2)^2}{L(1-a)^4}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

For `L=12`, every numerical ingredient can be replaced by exact rational inequalities:

1. `e > 1+1+1/2+1/6+1/24 = 65/24 > 27/10`.
2. `27^12 = 150094635296999121 > 10^17`, so `e^12>100000` and therefore `a=e^{-12}<10^{-5}`.
3. The classical rational bound `pi<22/7` gives `pi^2<484/49<10`.
4. `1-a^2<1` and `1-a>1/2`.

Thus

\[
C_{12}
<10^{-5}\frac{(144+10)^2}{12}\,16
=\frac{5929}{18750}
<1.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Combining (9.1)-(9.3),

\[
\varphi_{12}''(\theta_*)+A\cot t_*
<-(1-C_{12})\cot t_*<0.
\]

Since the prefactor outside the bracket in (8.4) is negative, we obtain

\[
\boxed{g''(0)>0.}
\tag{9.4}
\]

# 10. Why this refutes Problem 4.3

The source itself records the identity

\[
(\mu\boxtimes\nu)^{-1}=\mu^{-1}\boxtimes\nu^{-1}.
\]

Since `mu_12=mu_12^{-1}`, the output

\[
\rho_{12}=\mu_{12}\boxtimes\mu_{12}
\]

is multiplicatively symmetric. Its log-push density `g` is therefore even.

By (9.4), `y=0` is a **strict local minimum** of this even density.

An even unimodal probability measure on the real line cannot have a strict local density minimum at zero. Indeed, if its unimodal mode is zero, its density is nonincreasing on `(0,infinity)`. If a mode is `m>0`, then for `0<x<m`, monotonicity on `(-infinity,m)` and evenness give

\[
f(x)=f(-x)\le f(0)\le f(x),
\]

so the density must be constant on `[-m,m]`; again zero cannot be a strict local minimum. The case `m<0` follows by symmetry.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{12}\boxtimes\mu_{12}\ \text{is not log-unimodal}.
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

Both inputs are multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal. Hence the universal assertion asked in Hasebe-Ueda-Wang Problem 4.3 is false.

Claim ID: **P20-I0081-COUNTEREXAMPLE-01 — PROVED / SOURCE STATEMENT REFUTED.**

# 11. Certified mode count: at least two

The source-level refutation already follows from the strict center minimum and does not need a global regularity theorem. The tracked mode count does.

The input `mu_12` is a compactly supported Jacobi-type measure on

\[
[e^{-12},e^{12}]
\]

with density `1/(24x)`. Relative to its two finite support edges it has power exponents `(0,0)`, and the remaining factor `1/(24x)` is smooth and strictly positive on that compact interval. Thus the hypotheses of Ji's compact-Jacobi regularity theorem apply. Ji proves that

\[
\rho_{12}=\mu_{12}\boxtimes\mu_{12}
\]

is again a Jacobi measure, with density decaying like a square root at its support edges.

Consequently its log-density `g` is continuous on its compact log-support and vanishes at the support edges. Since `g''(0)>0`, there exists a small `y_0>0` with `g(y_0)>g(0)`. Continuity and edge decay force `g` to attain a maximum at some positive interior point. Evenness gives a distinct mirrored maximum on the negative side.

Therefore the output has at least two distinct log-density modes:

\[
\boxed{M_{\mathrm{cert}}\ge2.}
\tag{11.1}
\]

No claim is made that there are exactly two modes or that two is the global maximum over all admissible inputs.

Claim ID: **P20-I0081-TWO-MODE-01 — PROVED using Ji's published Jacobi regularity theorem.**

# 13. Adversarial checks and possible failure points

I specifically checked the following places where an apparent counterexample could be spurious.

1. **Input admissibility.** `mu_12` is not a multimodal/discrete proxy. Its log-push is exactly the uniform density on `[-12,12]`, hence unimodal under the source definition, and it is inversion symmetric exactly.
2. **Not the Brownian special case.** No `sigma_t` factor is used.
3. **Correct transform.** Equation (5.1) is an elementary integral and (5.2) solves it algebraically. Equation (5.3) is exactly the source's Sigma multiplicativity.
4. **Physical branch rather than a spurious phase root.** The chosen root is connected to the actual inverse germ at `w=-1`; the entire connecting arc is noncritical and maps into the upper half-plane until the endpoint.
5. **Boundary-density sign.** The upper approach in the `z` variable corresponds to the lower approach in the Cauchy variable `1/z`; this is why (7.2) has the displayed positive sign.
6. **Second derivative sign.** The exact cancellation (9.1) was rederived after differentiating with respect to `theta`, not `t`; the factor-of-two bookkeeping is included explicitly in (6.4) and (8.1).
7. **No plotting claim.** The refutation is the analytic inequality `g''(0)>0`; no plot or root finder is used as proof.
8. **Mode count separated from refutation.** Ji's theorem is used only to turn the local strict minimum into a certified global count of at least two modes. Even if that imported regularity step were removed, (10.1) would still refute Problem 4.3.
