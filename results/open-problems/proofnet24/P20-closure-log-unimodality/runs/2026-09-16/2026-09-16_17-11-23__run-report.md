# 7. (c) What a successful argument must contain that no tried route contains

A successful metric-moving argument must control **global critical-point topology of the physical output density**, not only center curvature and support-edge decay.

For an inversion-symmetric output, the log-density `g` is even. There are two economical ways to certify more than two modes.

### Three-mode pattern

Make the center a strict maximum and prove a second symmetric pair of outer maxima. On `y>0`, this requires a certified derivative sign pattern of the form

\[
g'(0^+)<0,\qquad
g'(a)<0,\qquad
g'(b)>0,\qquad
g'(c)<0
\]

for explicit `0<a<b<c` (with continuity and positivity on the intervening physical branch). Then the positive side has a local minimum followed by an outer local maximum, and evenness gives three modes total: one at zero and two outer modes.

### Four-mode pattern

Keep the current strict central minimum, but force two maxima on each side. Then one needs a positive-side sign pattern such as

\[
g'(a)>0,\qquad g'(b)<0,\qquad g'(c)>0,\qquad g'(d)<0
\]

for explicit `0<a<b<c<d`.

In either pattern the missing ingredient is the same: a **certified physical-branch derivative sign sequence at several nonzero log coordinates**, plus support/positivity control. None of the existing routes supplies that. Center curvature alone cannot.

The certification should be based on exact arithmetic where the statement is finite and on outward-rounded interval arithmetic for analytic branch enclosures. Ordinary floating-point plots are only candidate generators.
