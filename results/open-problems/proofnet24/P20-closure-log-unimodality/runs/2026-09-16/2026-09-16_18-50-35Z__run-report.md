## 4. Move #5 — general outer-plateau scale window

### 4.1 General statement

Let `h` be an even compactly supported probability density on `R`, nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`, and let `mu=exp_* h`. Assume `mu boxtimes mu` has finitely many already-certified positive simple physical sign pairs

`g'(a_j)>0>g'(b_j)`,  `0<a_j<b_j`,  `j=1,...,m`,

on pairwise disjoint intervals.

For `R` larger than the support radius, let `c_R>0` satisfy

`R c_R -> 0`,                                      (4.1)

`e^{-R}/c_R -> 0`.                                (4.2)

Define

`h_R(u)=[h(u)+c_R 1_{[-R,R]}(u)]/[1+2R c_R]`.      (4.3)

**Theorem `P20-I0081-OUTER-SCALE-WINDOW-09 = PROVED`.** Under (4.1)–(4.2), for all sufficiently large `R`, all old sign pairs persist for `mu_R boxtimes mu_R`, and the same universal limiting model from run 08 creates an additional positive sign pair strictly to their right. Hence one additional positive local maximum is created.

In particular, taking

`c_R=e^{-alpha R}`

works for **every fixed `0<alpha<1`**. The previous `alpha=1/2` was only a convenient point in the interval.

### 4.2 Exact admissibility

Equation (4.3) is exactly normalized. Adding a centered constant floor on `[-R,R]` preserves evenness and monotonicity in `|u|`; normalization by a positive scalar does the same. Thus every `h_R` is exactly a source-admissible logarithmic density. No limiting argument is used for the source hypotheses.

### 4.3 Exact transform formula

Use the same transform convention as the previous report:

`M_h(z)=int h(u)/(1-z e^u) du`,

`eta_h(z)=1-1/M_h(z)`.

For the centered plateau,

`F_R(z)=int_{-R}^R du/(1-z e^u)`

has the exact analytic expression

`F_R(z)=2R-log(1-z e^R)+log(1-z e^{-R})`.          (4.4)

Hence

`M_R(z)=[M_0(z)+c_R F_R(z)]/[1+2R c_R]`.           (4.5)

This is not an approximation.

### 4.4 Old physical modes persist under the sole condition `R c_R -> 0`

Fix a compact union `K` of the old physical root neighborhoods inside `C^+`, bounded away from zero and the real axis. From the integral representation,

`sup_K |F_R| = O(R)`,

`sup_K |F_R'| = O(1)`.                             (4.6)

The derivative estimate can be seen directly after `t=e^u`:

`F_R'(z)=int_{e^{-R}}^{e^R} dt/(1-zt)^2`,

which is uniformly bounded for `z` in such a compact `K`.

By (4.1), (4.5) therefore gives

`M_R -> M_0` in `C^1(K)`, hence `eta_R -> eta_0` in `C^1(K)`.     (4.7)

Every inherited boundary root is simple and every inherited density derivative sign has a nonzero margin. The implicit-function theorem preserves the root and the sign. For nearby interior spectral parameter the continued root is an interior fixed point of the multiplicative subordination map, and AHK Theorem 1.3 / Denjoy-Wolff uniqueness identifies it with the physical branch. Thus the persistence statement is branch-safe, not merely algebraic.

### 4.5 Remote scale under the second condition `e^{-R}/c_R -> 0`

Put

`q=e^{-R} xi`,

`H_R(xi)=eta_R(e^{-R}xi)/c_R`,

`L(xi)=-log(1-xi)`.                                (4.8)

On any fixed compact subset of `C^+`, compact support of `h` gives

`M_0(e^{-R}xi)=1+O(e^{-R})` in `C^1`.              (4.9)

Equation (4.4) gives exactly

`F_R(e^{-R}xi)=2R+L(xi)+log(1-e^{-2R}xi)`.         (4.10)

Insert (4.9)–(4.10) into (4.5). The `2R c_R` terms cancel between numerator and denominator. Uniformly in `C^1`,

`M_R(e^{-R}xi)
 = 1 + c_R L(xi)
   + O(e^{-R}) + O(R c_R^2) + O(c_R e^{-2R}).`     (4.11)

Divide the error by `c_R`. Condition (4.2) kills `e^{-R}/c_R`; condition (4.1) kills `R c_R`; the last term is automatic. Therefore

`M_R(e^{-R}xi)=1+c_R L(xi)+o(c_R)` in `C^1`.       (4.12)

Since `eta=(M-1)/M`,

`H_R -> L` in `C^1`.                               (4.13)

Thus the universal outer model does not depend on the old staircase or on a special exponent.

### 4.6 The universal sign pair transfers unchanged

For the self-product boundary relation

`q^2=e^{-y} eta_R(q)`,

set

`tau=c_R e^{2R-y}`.                                (4.14)

Then

`xi^2=tau H_R(xi) -> tau L(xi)`.                  (4.15)

The already archived exact interval certificate proves simple upper-half-plane roots for the limiting equation

`xi^2=tau[-log(1-xi)]`

with

`D(100)>0>D(10)`.                                  (4.16)

No new numerical assertion is needed in this run and the checker was not duplicated.

By (4.13), the two simple roots and their derivative signs transfer to finite large `R`. Their logarithmic locations are

`y_R(tau)=2R+log c_R-log tau`.                     (4.17)

Condition (4.2) implies `c_R e^R -> infinity`, so

`2R+log c_R = R + log(c_R e^R) -> infinity`.       (4.18)

Therefore both new sign points eventually lie to the right of every old finite interval. As in run 08, compact-support continuity gives an interior local maximum between them. This proves `P20-I0081-OUTER-SCALE-WINDOW-09`.

### 4.7 Exponential family

If `c_R=e^{-alpha R}`, then

`R c_R ->0` iff `alpha>0`,

`e^{-R}/c_R=e^{-(1-alpha)R}->0` iff `alpha<1`.

Hence the whole open interval

`0<alpha<1`                                         (4.19)

`y_R(tau)=(2-alpha)R-log tau`.                     (4.20)

## 5. Move #20 — exact failure boundary of this universal algorithm

The two conditions above are not asserted to be necessary for **all possible** mode-creation schemes. They are the exact scale-separation obligations of this particular base-independent outer-model proof. That distinction matters.

### 5.1 Too-heavy plateau: `R c_R` does not vanish

On every fixed old root neighborhood, the added transform is size `c_R F_R=Theta(R c_R)`. If `R c_R` fails to tend to zero, (4.7) is unavailable: the new input is not a small perturbation of the old one on the old physical scale. Old modes may survive or may not, but this algorithm has no reason to preserve them.

For the exponential family this is the obstruction at `alpha<=0`.

### 5.2 Too-light plateau: `c_R` is not asymptotically larger than `e^{-R}`

The compact old base has the sharper expansion

`M_0(e^{-R}xi)=1+m_1 e^{-R}xi+O(e^{-2R})`,         (5.1)

where

`m_1=int e^u h(u)du>0`.                            (5.2)

If `c_R ~ C e^{-R}`, then dividing the remote perturbation by `c_R` no longer removes the base term. Instead the first-order limit becomes

`eta_R(e^{-R}xi)/c_R
 -> L(xi)+(m_1/C)xi`,                            (5.3)

up to the harmless normalization which still tends to one. The remote equation is therefore base-dependent. If `c_R=o(e^{-R})`, the old-base term dominates the proposed plateau scaling entirely.

Thus the clean universal logarithmic model necessarily disappears at and beyond the `alpha=1` boundary for `c_R=e^{-alpha R}`. This does **not** prove that no new modes exist for `alpha>=1`; it proves that the base-independent induction from run 08 no longer follows from this scaling.

This identifies the real obstruction of the algorithm rather than papering it over with another lemma.

Claim:

`P20-I0081-OUTER-ALGORITHM-FAILURE-WINDOW-09 = PROVED` — the run-08 universal mode-adjunction proof extends whenever `R c_R->0` and `e^{-R}/c_R->0`; outside those two separations, the respective old-mode or universal-remote step loses its controlling small parameter, and at `c_R~C e^{-R}` the limiting model explicitly acquires the base moment term `(m_1/C)xi`.

## 6. Move #28 — unbounded modes survive smoothness, full log-support and unequal factors

The remaining obvious objection to the unbounded theorem was geometric rather than numerical: every inductive witness was a compact staircase and used `mu boxtimes mu`. I now remove all three artifacts at once for every finite target `K`.

### 6.1 Structural form of every staircase produced by the induction

The seed staircase is a positive finite combination of centered interval indicators. Each induction step adds another positive centered indicator and rescales by a positive normalizing constant. Therefore every finite-stage logarithmic density has the exact form

`h(u)=sum_{j=1}^N a_j 1_{[-R_j,R_j]}(u)`,          (6.1)

with `a_j>0`, `R_j>0`, and exact normalization.

Choose a finite stage whose self-product has at least `K` modes. Run 08 proves such a stage exists for every finite `K`.

### 6.2 Gaussian smoothing produces strict source inputs

Let

`gamma_sigma(u)=(2 pi sigma^2)^(-1/2) exp(-u^2/(2sigma^2))`,

and set

`h_sigma=h*gamma_sigma`.                            (6.2)

Then `h_sigma` is `C^infinity`, even, normalized, and positive on all of `R`. From (6.1), for `x>0`,

`h_sigma'(x)
 = sum_j a_j [gamma_sigma(x+R_j)-gamma_sigma(x-R_j)]`.   (6.3)

Because `gamma_sigma` is even and strictly decreasing in `|x|`, and

`|x+R_j|>|x-R_j|` for every `x,R_j>0`,

every summand in (6.3) is strictly negative. Hence

`h_sigma'(x)<0` for every `x>0`.                    (6.4)

So `mu_sigma=exp_* h_sigma` is multiplicatively symmetric, has full support `(0,infinity)`, and is **strictly** log-unimodal. This is stronger input regularity than the source asks for.

### 6.3 Two different smoothing widths break factor equality

For `sigma>0`, compare `h_sigma` and `h_{2sigma}`. They are not equal: since the base `h` has finite variance,

`Var(h_sigma)=Var(h)+sigma^2`,

`Var(h_{2sigma})=Var(h)+4sigma^2`.                  (6.5)

Thus

`mu_sigma != mu_{2sigma}`.                          (6.6)

Both retain the required **multiplicative symmetry**; what is deliberately broken is the accidental equality of the two factors.

### 6.4 Transform convergence needed for local mode persistence

As `sigma->0`, `h_sigma->h` in `L^1(R)`. For a compact `K_q` in the upper half-plane, the kernels

`k_0(z,u)=1/(1-z e^u)`,

`k_1(z,u)=e^u/(1-z e^u)^2`                         (6.7)

are uniformly bounded over `(z,u) in K_q x R`. For `k_1`, writing `t=e^u>0` reduces this to boundedness of

`t/|1-zt|^2`

when `z` ranges in a compact subset of `C^+`; the small- and large-`t` ends both vanish and the middle range is compact. Consequently

`M_{mu_sigma}->M_mu`,

`M'_{mu_sigma}->M'_mu`                              (6.8)

uniformly on every such compact set. The same holds for `mu_{2sigma}`.

Hence the corresponding `eta` transforms converge in `C^1` on each finite physical root neighborhood whose transform denominator is separated from zero.

### 6.5 Strict modes exist for the compact staircase witness

Run 08 gives disjoint derivative sign-pair intervals for the compact self-product and Ji gives continuity of its density. On each such compact interval the density attains an interior maximum. The maximum value is positive. AHK Theorem 1.9 says the density is analytic wherever it is positive and finite.

At least one maximizer in each sign-pair interval is a **strict** local maximum. Indeed, if a positive interior maximizer were non-strict in every neighborhood, there would be a sequence of distinct equal-height maximizers accumulating at it. Analyticity would force the density to be locally constant. By analytic continuation on that positive-density component this would contradict the strict derivative sign already present in the same certified component; if the component ended inside the interval, Ji continuity would force the positive constant to tend to zero at the component boundary, also impossible. Thus choose one strict mode `x_j` from every certified interval.

Because only finitely many modes are needed for a fixed `K`, choose pairwise disjoint compact neighborhoods `U_j` and margins `delta_j>0` such that

`g(x_j) >= max_{partial U_j} g + 4 delta_j`.        (6.9)

### 6.6 Physical subordination is locally stable under the unequal smoothing perturbation

On each `U_j`, the compact-witness density is positive. Therefore the boundary subordination values lie in the upper half-plane and are interior Denjoy-Wolff fixed points. For a nondegenerate input the relevant fixed-point map is not an automorphism, so Schwarz-Pick/Denjoy-Wolff gives fixed-point derivative modulus strictly below one. On the finite union of compact neighborhoods we may take a uniform gap from one.

Equations (6.8) and AHK Theorem 1.3 then give, by the analytic implicit-function theorem for the fixed-point equation, uniform convergence of the physical subordination functions for

`mu_sigma boxtimes mu_{2sigma}`

to those of `mu boxtimes mu` on the finite union `U_j` as `sigma->0`. The output `M`- and Cauchy-transform formulas therefore give uniform convergence of the logarithmic densities on those neighborhoods:

`g_sigma,2sigma -> g` uniformly on `union_j U_j`.   (6.10)

This step is physical-branch safe because AHK characterizes the interior solution uniquely as the Denjoy-Wolff point; no nearby algebraic impostor is selected.

Choose `sigma` so small that the uniform error in (6.10) is below `delta_j` for every `j`. Then from (6.9),

`g_sigma,2sigma(x_j)
 > max_{partial U_j} g_sigma,2sigma + 2 delta_j`.   (6.11)

The perturbed density therefore attains a local maximum in the interior of every `U_j`. The neighborhoods are disjoint, so the modes are distinct.

### 6.7 Stronger theorem

**Theorem `P20-I0081-SMOOTH-DISTINCT-UNBOUNDED-09 = PROVED`.** For every finite integer `K`, there exist two **distinct** probability measures `mu,nu` on `(0,infinity)` such that

- each is multiplicatively symmetric;
- each is strictly log-unimodal;
- each logarithmic density is `C^infinity`, everywhere positive, and has full support `R`;
- `log_*(mu boxtimes nu)` has at least `K` distinct local maxima.

Therefore unbounded modal complexity is not caused by a self-convolution identity, staircase discontinuities, compact logarithmic support, or non-strict input plateaux.

This theorem still respects the source’s required multiplicative symmetry of **each** factor; deliberately breaking that required hypothesis would no longer address Problem 4.3.

### 6.8 Robust, not sporadic

The same argument is open in the two smoothing parameters. Once one sufficiently small pair `(sigma_1,sigma_2)` with `sigma_1!=sigma_2` satisfies (6.11), continuity of the transforms/subordination solution leaves a nonempty open neighborhood `U_K` of `(sigma_1,sigma_2)` on which all `K` strict modes survive. Shrink `U_K` away from the diagonal if desired.

Thus the counterexample mechanism is not a single tuned pair even after requiring smooth, full-support, distinct inputs.

Claim:

`P20-I0081-ROBUST-K-MODES-09 = PROVED` — for every finite `K`, a nonempty open two-parameter family of smooth source-admissible distinct input pairs has at least `K` output log-density modes.

This is the successful outcome of Move #28.
