# 4. The new unequal admissible inputs

Let the common radii be

`R = (10,20,28,50,80,130)`.

Define integer indicator weights

`w_mu = (5000000,15000000,5000000,10000,10,1)`,

`w_nu = (5000001,15000000,5000000,10000,10,1)`.

The exact normalization constants are

`N_mu = 2 sum_j R_j w_mu,j = 981001860`,

`N_nu = 2 sum_j R_j w_nu,j = 981001880`.

Define logarithmic densities on `R` by

`h_mu(y) = N_mu^{-1} sum_j w_mu,j 1_{[-R_j,R_j]}(y)`,

`h_nu(y) = N_nu^{-1} sum_j w_nu,j 1_{[-R_j,R_j]}(y)`.

Then define positive-line laws by

`d mu(x) = h_mu(log x) dx/x`,

`d nu(x) = h_nu(log x) dx/x`.

## Theorem `P20-I0081-ASYM-SIXLEVEL-ADMISSIBLE-07` — PROVED

`mu` and `nu` are distinct multiplicatively symmetric log-unimodal probability laws.

### Proof

Normalization is exact by construction:

`int_R h_mu = 2 sum R_j w_mu,j / N_mu = 1`, and similarly for `nu`.

Every weight is positive. Therefore the plateau heights on `y>0` are the successive tail sums of the normalized weights and are strictly decreasing as `|y|` crosses `10,20,28,50,80,130`. The checker records the exact rational plateau heights and verifies strict decrease. Consequently each log law is unimodal with mode zero.

Both densities are even. Under `x -> 1/x`, logarithmic coordinate changes as `y -> -y`, so evenness is exactly multiplicative symmetry. Finally the first indicator coefficient differs by one and the normalizing constants differ, so the two log densities, hence the two probability laws, are distinct. QED.

# 5. Exact transforms and the unequal-product boundary system

For one centered log-indicator `1_{[-R,R]}`, set

`F_R(z) = 2R - log(1-z e^R) + log(1-z e^{-R})`.

Indeed, after `x=e^y`,

`int_{-R}^R (1-z e^y)^{-1} dy = F_R(z)`,

because `y - log(1-z e^y)` is an antiderivative. Hence, with

`M_lambda(z)=int (1-zx)^{-1} d lambda(x)`

and `eta_lambda(z)=1-1/M_lambda(z)`, the two exact input transforms are the corresponding integer-weighted sums of the `F_R` divided by `N_mu,N_nu`.

Let `rho=mu boxtimes nu`. For a physical boundary point `s=e^{-y}>0`, write

`q = omega_mu(s)`, `r = omega_nu(s)`, `w = eta_rho(s)`.

Multiplicative subordination gives

`w = eta_mu(q) = eta_nu(r) = q r / s`.

Therefore the physical boundary pair solves the two-complex-variable system

`G1(q,r) = eta_mu(q)-eta_nu(r) = 0`,

`G2(q,r) = q r - s eta_mu(q) = 0`.                         (5.1)

The Jacobian is

`J = [[eta_mu'(q), -eta_nu'(r)], [r-s eta_mu'(q), q]]`.    (5.2)

## Theorem `P20-I0081-UNEQUAL-PHYSICAL-SYSTEM-07` — PROVED

At every certified box in Section 7, the unique enclosed solution of (5.1) is the physical boundary subordination pair.

### Proof

The interval certificate places both `q` and `r` strictly in the upper half-plane and separates `det J` from zero. Hence the analytic implicit-function theorem continues the enclosed solution to `z` in a complex neighborhood of the real boundary value `s`, with the pair remaining in the upper half-plane for interior `z` sufficiently close to `s`.

Write `h_lambda(u)=eta_lambda(u)/u`. Equations (5.1) imply

`r = z h_mu(q)`, `q = z h_nu(r)`,

so `q` is an interior fixed point of

`f_z(u)=z h_nu(z h_mu(u))`.

The multiplicative subordination construction uses the Denjoy--Wolff point of this self-map and gives the corresponding analytic physical subordination functions. The interior fixed point in the present local branch is therefore the physical one. Continuous boundary extension then identifies the certified real-boundary limit with `(omega_mu(s),omega_nu(s))`.

This is precisely the unequal-input version of the physical-branch argument used in the inherited P20 self-convolution proof; it does not accept an arbitrary algebraic root merely because (5.1) is numerically small. QED.

# 8. From twelve signs to twelve modes

AHK regularity gives a continuous density, analytic where it is positive and finite; the certified endpoints themselves lie in such positive analytic regions. On each of the six disjoint compact intervals

`[8,18]`, `[29,37]`, `[46,48]`, `[53,54]`, `[70,86]`, `[90,95]`,

the left derivative is positive and the right derivative is negative. Therefore neither endpoint can maximize the continuous density on that interval. A maximizer occurs in its interior and is a local maximum. The six intervals are disjoint, so there are six distinct positive log-density modes.

Both inputs are inversion symmetric. If free positive `X,Y` have laws `mu,nu`, then

`(X^{1/2} Y X^{1/2})^{-1} = X^{-1/2} Y^{-1} X^{-1/2}`.

`rho^{-1} = mu^{-1} boxtimes nu^{-1} = mu boxtimes nu = rho`.

Thus the logarithmic output density is even, and each positive mode has a distinct negative mirror. Consequently

`N_modes(log_* (mu boxtimes nu)) >= 12`.                  (8.1)

Every `nu_tau` is multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal; `mu boxtimes nu_0 = mu` is exactly log-unimodal, while `mu boxtimes nu_1` has at least twelve log-density modes.

# 10. Smooth full-support robustness

The strongest adversarial question after the staircase construction is whether the phenomenon depends on discontinuous plateaus, compact support, or equality of the two factors. The last issue is already removed by `mu != nu`; the first two can also be removed.

Let `gamma_sigma` be the centered Gaussian density on the log line and put

`h_{mu,sigma}=h_mu * gamma_sigma`, `h_{nu,sigma}=h_nu * gamma_sigma`.

Let `mu_sigma,nu_sigma` be the corresponding positive-line laws.

## Theorem `P20-I0081-SMOOTH-ROBUST-TWELVEMODES-07` — PROVED

For all sufficiently small `sigma>0`, `mu_sigma` and `nu_sigma` are distinct, multiplicatively symmetric, smooth everywhere-positive, full-log-support, **strictly** log-unimodal inputs, and `mu_sigma boxtimes nu_sigma` has at least twelve log-density modes.

### Proof

Each original staircase is a positive linear combination of centered interval indicators. For `R>0`,

`d/dx [1_{[-R,R]} * gamma_sigma](x) = gamma_sigma(x+R)-gamma_sigma(x-R)`.

For every `x>0`, `|x+R|>|x-R|`, and the centered Gaussian is strictly decreasing as a function of absolute value on `(0,infinity)`. Therefore the derivative above is strictly negative. A positive combination remains strictly negative on `x>0`. Evenness is preserved by convolution. Thus both smoothed log densities are real analytic, positive on all of `R`, even, and strictly decreasing on `(0,infinity)`: they are strictly log-unimodal and multiplicatively symmetric with full log support.

They remain distinct for every `sigma>0`: Gaussian convolution is injective on finite measures because its Fourier multiplier `exp(-sigma^2 xi^2/2)` never vanishes.

As `sigma downarrow 0`, Gaussian approximate-identity convergence gives

`||h_{lambda,sigma}-h_lambda||_1 -> 0` for `lambda=mu,nu`.

Fix any one of the twelve certified root boxes. It is a compact subset of the upper half-plane. On such a box, uniformly in real log-coordinate `u`, the kernels

`(1-z e^u)^{-1}` and `e^u(1-z e^u)^{-2}`

are bounded. Hence `L^1` convergence of the log densities implies uniform convergence on the box of `M_{lambda,sigma}` and its first `z` derivative to the unsmoothed transforms. Consequently `eta_{lambda,sigma}` and its derivative converge uniformly as well, because the certified unsmoothed denominators are separated from zero.

The Jacobian (5.2) is nonsingular at every certified root. The analytic implicit-function theorem therefore gives, for all sufficiently small `sigma`, a unique nearby solution of the perturbed unequal subordination system. The physical-branch argument of Section 5 identifies it with the physical root. Formulae (6.1)--(6.2) depend continuously on the transform values, first derivatives and root. Every derivative sign in (7.1) has a strict nonzero margin, so each sign persists for sufficiently small `sigma`. There are finitely many boxes; choose one common positive `sigma_0` for all twelve signs.

The six disjoint positive intervals therefore still contain six local maxima for `0<sigma<sigma_0`. Both smoothed inputs are inversion symmetric, so the six negative mirrors also persist. QED.

This theorem is qualitative in `sigma`; no explicit numerical smoothing threshold is asserted.
