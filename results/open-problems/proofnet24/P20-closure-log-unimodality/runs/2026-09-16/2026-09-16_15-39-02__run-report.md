# 4. Negative-axis physical branch anchor

## Claim `P20-I0081-BRANCH-ANCHOR-02` — PROVED

For every `L>0`, equations (3.3)-(3.4) represent the physical inverse branches along the negative real axis from a neighborhood of zero through `w=-1`. In particular

\[
Q_L(-1)=-1
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the identity

\[
\eta_{\rho_L}(Q_L(w))=w
\tag{4.2}
\]

holds in a complex neighborhood of `w=-1`.

### Proof

For an arbitrary probability measure `lambda` supported in `(0,infinity)`, on `z<0` we have

\[
\psi_\lambda'(z)=\int\frac{x}{(1-xz)^2}\,d\lambda(x)>0,
\]

and

\[
\eta_\lambda'(z)
=\frac{\psi_\lambda'(z)}{(1+\psi_\lambda(z))^2}>0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus `eta_lambda` is locally invertible at every negative real point; its negative-real inverse branch is unique and real analytic.

For `mu_L`, direct substitution of (3.3) into (3.2) verifies the inverse equation on negative real `w` near zero. The explicit denominator in (3.3) does not vanish for `-1<=w<0`: there

\[
E(w)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2Lw}{1-w}\right)\in[a,1),
\]

whereas a zero of the denominator would require `E=a^2<a`. Hence the explicit branch continues analytically to `w=-1` and, by uniqueness of the negative-real inverse branch, remains the physical inverse there.

The identity (3.4) follows from Sigma multiplicativity near zero. Both the explicit right side and the physical inverse of `eta_{rho_L}` continue locally and uniquely along the connected negative-real interval, where (4.3) prevents a physical inverse critical point. Analytic continuation therefore preserves their equality through `w=-1`.

At `w=-1`,

\[
E(-1)=e^{-L}=a,
\qquad
q_L(-1)=-1,
\]

and hence `Q_L(-1)=-1`. Since the derivative of the physical inverse is nonzero there, (4.2) holds in a complex neighborhood. QED.

This removes the only branch-selection step in the preceding proof that deserved a more explicit continuation argument.
