# 3. Inherited exact transform formulas

For `X>0` define the admissible log-uniform law

\[
\mu_X(dx)=\frac{1}{2Xx}\mathbf 1_{[e^{-X},e^X]}(x)\,dx.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Its log push-forward is exactly `Unif[-X,X]`, hence it is log-unimodal, and inversion `x -> 1/x` preserves it, hence it is multiplicatively symmetric.

Put

\[
a_X=e^{-X},\qquad E_X(w)=\exp\!\left(\frac{2Xw}{1-w}\right).
\]

The exact eta-inverse from the previous P20 proof is

\[
q_X(w):=\eta_{\mu_X}^{-1}(w)
=\frac{1-E_X(w)}{a_X-a_X^{-1}E_X(w)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For two possibly unequal inputs

\[
\rho_{L,M}:=\mu_L\boxtimes\mu_M,
\]

Sigma-transform multiplicativity gives the local inverse germ

\[
Q_{L,M}(w):=\eta_{\rho_{L,M}}^{-1}(w)
=\frac{q_L(w)q_M(w)}{w}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The negative-axis branch argument from the previous run applies verbatim to each factor. For every positive-line law, `eta'(z)>0` on `z<0`; hence the physical negative-real inverse is unique and analytic. For every `X`, equation (3.2) continues without a denominator zero from `w=0^-` through `w=-1` and satisfies `q_X(-1)=-1`. Therefore

\[
Q_{L,M}(-1)=-1,
\tag{3.4}
\]

and (3.3) is the physical product inverse in a neighborhood of `w=-1`.

## 4.1 Unit-circle phase

Put

\[
w=e^{i\theta}=e^{2it},\qquad 0<t<\frac\pi2,
\]

and for `X in {L,M}` define

\[
\phi_X=X\cot t,
\qquad
B_{a_X}(v)=\frac{v-a_X}{1-a_Xv}.
\]

On the unit circle,

\[
q_X(w)=-\frac1{B_{a_X}(e^{i\phi_X})}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `beta_X(phi)` be the continuously unwrapped argument of `B_{a_X}(e^{i phi})`, normalized by `beta_X(0)=0`. Then

\[
\beta_X'(\phi)=P_X(\phi)
:=\frac{1-a_X^2}{1-2a_X\cos\phi+a_X^2}>0,
\qquad \beta_X(\pi)=\pi.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The product inverse has unwrapped phase

\[
\varphi(\theta)
=2\pi-\theta-\beta_L(\phi_L)-\beta_M(\phi_M),
\tag{4.5}
\]

so

\[
Q_{L,M}(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\varphi(\theta)}.
\]

Differentiating with `t=theta/2` gives

\[
\boxed{
A(\theta):=\varphi'(\theta)
=\frac12\csc^2t\,[L P_L(\phi_L)+M P_M(\phi_M)]-1.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For every `X>=6`, the exact finite certificate below proves `e^{-X}<1/400`, hence

\[
P_X(\phi)\ge\frac{1-a_X}{1+a_X}>\frac{399}{401}.
\]

Since `L+M>=12` and `csc^2 t>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
A(\theta)
>\frac12\,12\frac{399}{401}-1
=\frac{1993}{401}>0.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus the unit-circle phase is strictly increasing in `theta` throughout the relevant half-circle.

## 4.2 Existence and location of the physical center root

Write

\[
R=\max\{L,M\},\qquad r=\min\{L,M\},
\]

and define

\[
t_0=\arctan(R/\pi).
\]

At `t=t_0`, the larger phase is `phi_R=pi`, while `phi_r=pi r/R` lies in `(0,pi]`.

For `0<=phi<=pi`, the real Blaschke factor has the exact half-angle relation

\[
\tan\frac{\beta_X(\phi)}2
=\frac{1+a_X}{1-a_X}\tan\frac\phi2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The multiplier is greater than one, so

\[
\beta_X(\phi)\ge\phi
\qquad(0\le\phi\le\pi).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore, at `t_0`,

\[
\varphi(2t_0)
\le \pi-2t_0-\pi\frac rR.
\]

Using

\[
2t_0=\pi-2\arctan(\pi/R)
\]

and `arctan u<u` for `u>0`,

\[
\varphi(2t_0)
<\frac{2\pi}{R}-\frac{\pi r}{R}
=\frac{\pi(2-r)}R<0,
\tag{4.10}
\]

because `r>=6`. At `t=pi/2`, both internal phases vanish and

\[
\varphi(\pi)=\pi>0.
\]

The strict derivative (4.7) now gives a unique

\[
t_*\in(t_0,\pi/2)
\]

such that

\[
\varphi(2t_*)=0.
\tag{4.11}
\]

In particular,

\[
0<\phi_X=X\cot t_*<\pi
\qquad(X=L,M).
\tag{4.12}
\]

Starting at `w=-1`, equation (3.4) identifies the physical inverse branch. Along the upper unit-circle arc from `theta=pi` down to `theta=2t_*`, equation (4.7) prevents a critical point and (4.5) keeps the image phase in `(0,pi)`. Analytic continuation therefore yields the physical boundary value

\[
\boxed{
\eta_{\rho_{L,M}}(1+i0)=e^{2it_*}.
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

No algebraic branch is substituted for the physical eta inverse.

## 4.3 Generic center-curvature identity

The curvature formula used in the self-convolution proof is actually generic for this symmetric two-input product. I record the short derivation to avoid importing a hidden factor-of-two assumption.

Let

\[
s_*=2it_*,\qquad F(s)=\log Q_{L,M}(e^s)
\]

with the local logarithm chosen so `F(s_*)=0`. On the imaginary axis near `s_*`,

\[
F(i\theta)=i\varphi(\theta).
\]

Hence, with

\[
A=\varphi'(2t_*)>0,
\]

we have

\[
F'(s_*)=A,
\qquad
F''(s_*)=-i\varphi''(2t_*).
\tag{4.14}
\]

For real log-coordinate `y`, the physical boundary solution satisfies

\[
F(s(y))=-y.
\]

Implicit differentiation gives

\[
s'(0)=-\frac1A,
\qquad
s''(0)=\frac{i\varphi''(2t_*)}{A^3}.
\tag{4.15}
\]

The density identity is

\[
g_{L,M}(y)=\frac1\pi\Im\frac1{1-e^{s(y)}}.
\tag{4.16}
\]

For `h(s)=(1-e^s)^{-1}`, direct evaluation at `s_*` gives

\[
h'(s_*)=-\frac14\csc^2 t_*,
\qquad
h''(s_*)=-\frac{i}{4}\csc^2t_*\cot t_*.
\tag{4.17}
\]

Combining (4.15)-(4.17),

\[
\boxed{
 g_{L,M}''(0)
 =-\frac{\csc^2t_*}{4\pi A^3}
 \left[\varphi''(2t_*)+A\cot t_*\right].
}
\tag{4.18}
\]

Thus it remains only to prove the bracket is negative.

## 4.4 Exact curvature cancellation for unequal inputs

For brevity write

\[
D_X=1-2a_X\cos\phi_X+a_X^2.
\]

From (4.4),

\[
P_X'(\phi)
=-\frac{2a_X(1-a_X^2)\sin\phi}{(1-2a_X\cos\phi+a_X^2)^2}.
\tag{4.19}
\]

Differentiating (4.6) and adding `A cot t` cancels all first-derivative terms exactly:

\[
\boxed{
\varphi''(2t_*)+A\cot t_*
=-\cot t_*
+\frac12\csc^4t_*\sum_{X\in\{L,M\}}
\frac{X^2a_X(1-a_X^2)\sin\phi_X}{D_X^2}.
}
\tag{4.20}
\]

By (4.12), `0<phi_X<pi`, and therefore

\[
\sin\phi_X<\phi_X=X\cot t_*.
\]

Also `D_X>=(1-a_X)^2`. Consequently

\[
\varphi''(2t_*)+A\cot t_*
<\cot t_*\left[-1+\frac12\csc^4t_*\{E(L)+E(M)\}\right],
\tag{4.21}
\]

where

\[
E(X)=\frac{X^3e^{-X}(1-e^{-2X})}{(1-e^{-X})^4}
=X^3\frac{a_X(1+a_X)}{(1-a_X)^3}.
\tag{4.22}
\]

The function `E` is strictly decreasing for `X>=6`, because

\[
\frac{d}{dX}\log E(X)
=\frac3X-1-\frac{a_X}{1+a_X}-\frac{3a_X}{1-a_X}
<\frac3X-1\le-\frac12.
\tag{4.23}
\]

Thus

\[
E(L)+E(M)\le2E(6).
\tag{4.24}
\]

Because `t_*>t_0=arctan(R/pi)` and `R>=6`,

\[
\csc^4t_*
<\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{R^2}\right)^2
\le\left(1+\frac{\pi^2}{36}\right)^2
<\left(1+\frac{10}{36}\right)^2
=\frac{529}{324}.
\tag{4.25}
\]

The exact certificate gives `e^{-6}<1/400`, so from the first form of (4.22),

\[
E(6)
<\frac{216}{400}\left(\frac{400}{399}\right)^4
=\frac{512000000}{938702163}.
\tag{4.26}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac12\csc^4t_*\{E(L)+E(M)\}
<\frac{529}{324}\cdot\frac{512000000}{938702163}
=\frac{67712000000}{76034875203}
<1.
\tag{4.27}
\]

Equations (4.21) and (4.27) show

\[
\varphi''(2t_*)+A\cot t_*<0.
\]

The prefactor in (4.18) is negative, hence

\[
\boxed{g_{L,M}''(0)>0.}
\]

This proves (4.2).

Finally, inversion commutes with free positive multiplicative convolution. Since both inputs are inversion symmetric, `rho_{L,M}` is inversion symmetric and its log-push density is even. An even unimodal density cannot have a strict local minimum at zero. Therefore (4.1) follows. QED.

# 7. Adversarial self-audit of the new theorem

I checked the points most likely to hide an illegitimate extension from `L=M` to `L!=M`.

1. **Product inverse normalization.** For unequal inputs the correct formula is `Q=q_L q_M/w`, not either square formula. It gives `Q(-1)=-1` because each factor has `q_X(-1)=-1`.
2. **Phase factor-of-two.** Each unequal factor contributes only `(X/2)csc^2(t)P_X` to `varphi'`; summing the two factors gives (4.6). The self-convolution formula is recovered when `L=M`.
3. **Root location.** The proof does not assume both internal phases equal `pi`. It uses the larger parameter `R`, the exact half-angle inequality `beta_X(phi)>=phi`, and `r>=6` to force a sign change before `pi/2`.
4. **Physical branch.** On the arc from `theta=pi` down to the root, `varphi` runs from `pi` to `0`; hence `Q` stays in the upper half-plane. This resolves the apparent sign ambiguity that would arise if one looked at the auxiliary point `t_0` rather than the physical arc.
5. **Curvature identity.** Section 4.3 derives the generic formula from `F(s)=log Q(e^s)` and does not import a self-convolution-only differentiation.
6. **Curvature envelope.** The unequal positive corrections add, but monotonicity of `E(X)` bounds both by `E(6)`. The exact rational ratio is strictly below one with a positive certified margin.
7. **Source admissibility.** Each input separately is multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal. Nothing in Problem 4.3 requires the two inputs to coincide.
8. **Mode claim separated from refutation.** Ji's theorem is used only to convert the strict center minimum into a certified global two-mode lower bound. The source statement is already refuted by the strict central minimum and evenness.
