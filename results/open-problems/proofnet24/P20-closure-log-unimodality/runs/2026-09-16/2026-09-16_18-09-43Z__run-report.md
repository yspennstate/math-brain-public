## 6. One-step outer-plateau mode adjunction theorem

### 6.1 Statement

Let `h` be an even probability density on the real line, nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`, with compact support in `[-A,A]`. Let `mu` be its exponential push-forward to `R_+`, so `mu` is multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal.

Assume the logarithmic density `g` of `mu boxtimes mu` has finitely many already-certified positive sign pairs

`g'(a_j)>0>g'(b_j)`,   `0<a_j<b_j`,   `j=1,...,m`,

on the physical simple-root branch, with the intervals pairwise disjoint.

For `R>A`, set

`c_R = exp(-R/2)`

and define the new logarithmic density

`h_R(u) = [ h(u) + c_R 1_{[-R,R]}(u) ] / [1+2R c_R]`.

Let `mu_R` be its exponential push-forward.

**Theorem `P20-I0081-OUTER-PLATEAU-ADJOIN-08`.** For all sufficiently large `R`, every old sign pair persists for `mu_R boxtimes mu_R`, and there is one additional positive sign pair strictly to the right of all the old intervals. Consequently the number of positive local maxima increases by at least one.

### 6.2 Admissibility is exact

The numerator integrates to `1+2Rc_R`, so `h_R` is exactly normalized. It is even. On `[0,A]`, it is the old nonincreasing `h` plus the constant `c_R`; on `(A,R]` it equals the smaller constant `c_R`; after `R` it is zero. Hence it is nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`.

Therefore every `mu_R` is again multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal. No approximation is used for the source hypotheses.

### 6.3 Exact transform formula

For a logarithmic density `h`, write

`M(z)= integral_R h(u)/(1-z exp(u)) du`,

`eta(z)=1-1/M(z)`.

Let `M_0` denote the transform of `h`. Define

`F_R(z)= integral_{-R}^R du/(1-z exp(u))`.

For `z` in the upper half-plane,

`F_R(z)=2R-log(1-z exp(R))+log(1-z exp(-R))`,

with the analytic branch obtained by following the lower-half-plane arguments `1-z exp(u)`.

The new transform is exactly

`M_R(z) = [ M_0(z) + c_R F_R(z) ] / [1+2R c_R]`.

### 6.4 Old modes persist

Fix a compact neighborhood `K` of the finitely many old physical roots, chosen inside the upper half-plane and away from zero. On `K`, the integral representation gives

`F_R(z)=O(R)`,   `F_R'(z)=O(1)`

uniformly as `R -> infinity`. (For the derivative, the negative-`u` tail is bounded by an integrable multiple of `exp(u)` and the positive-`u` tail by an integrable multiple of `exp(-u)` because `K` stays away from the real axis and zero.) Since

`R c_R -> 0`,

we have uniformly in `C^1(K)`

`M_R -> M_0`,   hence `eta_R -> eta_0`.

For a fixed old endpoint `y_j`, the self-product boundary equation is

`Phi_R(q;y_j) := q^2 - exp(-y_j) eta_R(q)=0`.

The inherited interval certificate separates `partial_q Phi_0` from zero at every old endpoint. By the complex implicit-function theorem, for all sufficiently large `R` there is a unique nearby simple root `q_R` in the upper half-plane. The density-derivative formula is continuous in `eta_R`, `eta_R'`, and `q_R`, so every strict old derivative sign persists.

For interior spectral parameter `z` near the positive boundary point, the continued root obeys

`q = z eta_R(q)/q`.

It is therefore an interior fixed point of the standard multiplicative subordination iteration; by the Denjoy-Wolff uniqueness in the current subordination theorem, it is the physical branch. Thus persistence does not accidentally switch to a nonphysical algebraic root.

### 6.5 Remote scale and the universal limit

Now look for a new mode at the new outer scale. Put

`q = exp(-R) xi`,

and define

`H_R(xi) := eta_R(exp(-R)xi)/c_R`.

Let

`L(xi) := -log(1-xi)`.

For `xi` in any fixed compact subset of the upper half-plane,

`M_0(exp(-R)xi)=1+O(exp(-R))`

in `C^1`, because the old density has fixed compact support. Also

`F_R(exp(-R)xi)=2R + L(xi) + log(1-exp(-2R)xi)`.

Since `c_R=exp(-R/2)`, both

`R c_R -> 0`,   `exp(-R)/c_R -> 0`.

Therefore

`M_R(exp(-R)xi)
 = 1 + c_R L(xi) + o(c_R)`

uniformly in `C^1` on those compact sets. Writing `M_R=1+delta_R` and using

`eta_R = delta_R/(1+delta_R)`

gives the key limit

`H_R -> L`

uniformly in `C^1`.

This is the point where the remote staircase ceases to depend on the old input: every compactly supported base sees the same limiting logarithmic map.

### 6.6 Scale the boundary equation

For the self-product, the exact boundary equation is

`q^2 = s eta_R(q)`,   `s=exp(-y)`.

Substitute `q=exp(-R)xi`. Define

`tau := c_R exp(2R-y)`.

Equivalently,

`y_R(tau)=2R+log(c_R)-log(tau) = (3/2)R-log(tau)`.

The boundary equation becomes

`xi^2 = tau H_R(xi)`.

As `R -> infinity`, this converges in `C^1` to the **universal model**

`xi^2 = tau L(xi)`,   `L(xi)=-log(1-xi)`.

### 6.7 Universal model: certified finite core

The new interval checker proves the following two simple upper-half-plane roots:

For `tau=100`,

`xi_100 = 5.05018321329254... + 17.77049123932137... i`,

and for `tau=10`,

`xi_10 = 2.05562016505342... + 4.39419538982903... i`.

At both roots the interval Krawczyk map is strictly self-contained, the contraction norm is `<1`, the model derivative

`2xi - tau/(1-xi)`

is separated from zero, and the logarithm branch is certified safe.

Along `tau=c_R exp(2R-y)` one has `tau_y=-tau`. Differentiating

`xi^2-tau H_R(xi)=0`

gives

`xi_y = - tau H_R(xi) / [2xi-tau H_R'(xi)]`.

With `w=eta_R(q)=c_R H_R(xi)`, the logarithmic density derivative is

`g_R'(y)
 = (1/pi) Im{ c_R H_R'(xi) xi_y / [1-c_R H_R(xi)]^2 }`.

Hence

`g_R'( y_R(tau) ) / c_R -> D(tau)`,

where

`D(tau) = (1/pi) Im{ L'(xi) [-tau L(xi)/(2xi-tau L'(xi))] }`

at the corresponding simple model root.

The certified intervals are

`D(100)` in
`[0.0053629777917197978788181615814761730337,
  0.0053629777917197978788181615814761730352]`,

and

`D(10)` in
`[-0.0062313786105843620814724520997611684619,
 -0.0062313786105843620814724520997611684539]`.

Thus

`D(100)>0>D(10)`.

This is a **self-check**, not an OTHER-track audit.

### 6.8 Transfer the universal signs back to finite R

The two model roots are simple. Since `H_R -> L` in `C^1`, the implicit-function theorem gives nearby roots `xi_R(100)` and `xi_R(10)` for all sufficiently large `R`; they stay in the upper half-plane and remain simple. The derivative convergence above then gives

`g_R'( y_R(100) ) > 0`,

`g_R'( y_R(10) ) < 0`

for all sufficiently large `R`.

Because `100>10`,

`y_R(100) < y_R(10)`.

Both tend to `+infinity` like `(3/2)R`, so after increasing `R` they lie strictly to the right of every old interval.

Physicality is again not assumed from an algebraic equation: at each finite `R` the root lies in the upper half-plane and is simple, so it continues for nearby interior spectral parameter; there it is the unique Denjoy-Wolff subordination fixed point. The boundary root is therefore the physical nontangential value.

Ji's continuity theorem applies because `mu_R` is compactly supported in `(0,infinity)` and atomless. Thus `g_R` is continuous. On the compact interval `[y_R(100),y_R(10)]`, its maximum cannot occur at the left endpoint (the derivative there is positive) or at the right endpoint (the derivative there is negative). Hence an interior local maximum exists.

All old sign pairs persist, and this new interval is disjoint from them. The one-step theorem is proved. QED.

## 9. Adversarial proof audit: possible failure points checked

### 9.1 Does adding a plateau violate source log-unimodality?

No. The added term is a centered constant floor on `[-R,R]`. After exact renormalization it preserves evenness and monotonic decrease in `|u|`.

### 9.2 Does the remote limit silently depend on the old input?

No at leading order. Compact support gives `M_0(exp(-R)xi)=1+O(exp(-R))`, whereas the new plateau contributes at scale `c_R=exp(-R/2)`. Since `exp(-R)/c_R ->0`, the old input disappears from the first-order outer model. This scale separation is precisely why the induction closes.

### 9.3 Could the new root be a nonphysical solution?

The proof uses a simple upper-half-plane root and continues it to nearby interior spectral parameter. There it is an interior fixed point of the multiplicative subordination iteration. Denjoy-Wolff uniqueness identifies it with the physical subordination value. No mere algebraic root is accepted.

### 9.4 Could adding the new remote plateau destroy old modes?

For finitely many old root neighborhoods, `eta_R -> eta_0` in `C^1`; every old root is simple and every old derivative sign is strict. The implicit-function theorem and continuity preserve each sign. The theorem chooses `R` after fixing those finitely many neighborhoods, so there is no uniform-in-infinitely-many-past-points circularity at one induction step.

### 9.5 Does the sign pair really force a mode?

Yes. Ji continuity gives a continuous density. Positive derivative at the left endpoint excludes a maximum there; negative derivative at the right excludes a maximum there. A maximum on the compact interval is therefore attained in the interior, giving a local maximum. The intervals are chosen disjoint, so the maxima are distinct.

### 9.6 Is this merely the already-published Brownian theorem in disguise?

No. No Brownian factor appears. The constructed inputs are explicit symmetric logarithmic staircases; the conclusion is a failure, indeed arbitrarily severe failure, of arbitrary-input preservation. Theorem 4.2 remains untouched.

I found no proof-changing gap in this adversarial pass. The new theorem still requires independent external-track audit before it can be treated as independently verified.
