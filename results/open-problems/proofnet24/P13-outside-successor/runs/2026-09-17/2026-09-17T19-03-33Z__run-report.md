# 4. Primary Hessian certificate

Use the Holmes–Kleptsyn sign convention

\[
L(x)=\sum_e x_e-\frac1\alpha\sum_v p_v\log S_v,
\qquad
S_v:=\sum_{e\ni v}x_e^\alpha.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Their coordinate change `y_e=2 sqrt(x_e)` makes the flow the anti-gradient flow of `L`. At an equilibrium, therefore, a necessary condition for being not linearly unstable is that the Hessian of `L` be positive semidefinite on supported directions.

For a logarithmic variation

\[
x_e(s)=x_e e^{s z_e},
\]

the equilibrium is a critical point, so the sign of the second derivative along this curve is the Hessian sign. Direct differentiation of (4.1) gives

\[
Q_x(z):=\left.\frac{d^2}{ds^2}L(x(s))\right|_{s=0}
=D_x(z)-\alpha V_x(z),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
D_x(z)=\sum_e x_e z_e^2,
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
V_x(z)=\sum_v p_v\left[
\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e^2
-\left(\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e\right)^2
\right],
\qquad
q_{v,e}=\frac{x_e^\alpha}{S_v}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus if one supported direction has `Q_x(z)<0`, the equilibrium is linearly unstable.

# 5. B3 theorem: exact length-specific obstruction for every embedded cycle

## Claim `P13-P0082-CYCLE-SPECTRAL-EXTREMAL-02` — PROVED

Let `x` be a WARM equilibrium on an arbitrary finite graph with arbitrary positive firing rates. Suppose its support contains a simple cycle

\[
C=(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1}),
\]

with indices modulo `k`; write `v_i=e_{i-1}\cap e_i`. Then:

1. if `k` is even, `x` is linearly unstable for every `alpha>1`;
2. if `k` is odd, `x` is linearly unstable whenever
   \[
   \boxed{\alpha>\sec^2\!\frac{\pi}{2k}};
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
3. the constant in (5.1) is sharp for this cycle-local Hessian mechanism: over all positive cycle edge weights and positive endpoint factors, the worst Rayleigh constant is attained by the uniform cycle. The resulting threshold is exactly the 2016 uniform-cycle threshold.

### Proof

Put

\[
r_i:=x_{e_i}^{\alpha}>0,
\qquad
t_i:=\frac{p_{v_i}}{S_{v_i}}>0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because the two endpoints of `e_i` are `v_i,v_{i+1}`, the equilibrium equation gives the exact identity

\[
x_{e_i}=r_i(t_i+t_{i+1}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Take a direction `z` supported only on the cycle. Summing (5.3) against `z_i^2` double-counts the cycle endpoint incidences and yields

\[
D_x(z)
=\sum_i t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr).
\tag{5.4}
\]

At the cycle vertex `v_i`, all off-cycle direction coordinates equal zero. Its contribution to (4.4) is therefore

\[
t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr)
-\frac{t_i}{S_{v_i}}
 (r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i)^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Since `S_{v_i}>=r_{i-1}+r_i`,

\[
V_x(z)\ge D_x(z)-R_{r,t}(z),
\tag{5.6}
\]

where

\[
R_{r,t}(z):=
\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}
(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i)^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Introduce `y_i=r_i z_i`. Then

\[
D_{r,t}(y):=D_x(z)
=\sum_i t_i\left(\frac{y_{i-1}^2}{r_{i-1}}+\frac{y_i^2}{r_i}\right),
\tag{5.8}
\]

and

\[
R_{r,t}(y)
=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus (4.2) and (5.6) imply

\[
Q_x(z)\le (1-\alpha)D_{r,t}(y)+\alpha R_{r,t}(y).
\tag{5.10}
\]

Define the generalized Rayleigh minimum

\[
\lambda_k(r,t):=
\inf_{y\ne0}\frac{R_{r,t}(y)}{D_{r,t}(y)}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

The denominator is strictly positive for `y!=0`.

**Even `k`.** Set `y_i=(-1)^i`. Because the signs close around an even cycle, every `y_{i-1}+y_i=0`, so `R=0` and `lambda_k=0`. Equation (5.10) gives `Q<0` for every `alpha>1`.

**Odd `k`: universal upper bound.** Let

\[
\phi_i(\theta)=\cos\left(\theta+\frac{i\pi}{k}\right),
\qquad
y_i(\theta)=(-1)^i\phi_i(\theta).
\tag{5.12}
\]

Since `k` is odd and `phi_{i+k}=-phi_i`, the anti-periodic phase exactly compensates the alternating sign at the wrap-around edge. Consequently for every cycle vertex the squared numerator in (5.9) is

\[
(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2=(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})^2.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Average `theta` uniformly over a period. Then

\[
\mathbb E_\theta\phi_i^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\theta(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})^2
=2\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Write `s_k=sin(pi/(2k))`. From (5.8)–(5.9),

\[
\mathbb E R
=2s_k^2\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i},
\tag{5.15}
\]

whereas

\[
\mathbb E D
=\frac12\sum_i t_i\left(\frac1{r_{i-1}}+\frac1{r_i}\right).
\tag{5.16}
\]

For positive `a,b`,

\[
\frac{2}{a+b}\le \frac12\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right).
\tag{5.17}
\]

Applying (5.17) termwise gives

\[
\mathbb E R\le s_k^2\,\mathbb E D.
\tag{5.18}
\]

Hence not every phase can have `R>s_k^2D`; for some `theta_*`,

\[
\frac{R(y(\theta_*))}{D(y(\theta_*))}\le s_k^2.
\tag{5.19}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\lambda_k(r,t)\le \sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}}
\qquad(k\text{ odd}).
\tag{5.20}
\]

Substitution into (5.10) yields some supported direction with

\[
Q_x(z)\le
\left[1-\alpha+\alpha\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}\right]D
=
\left[1-\alpha\cos^2\frac{\pi}{2k}\right]D.
\tag{5.21}
\]

This is strictly negative exactly when (5.1) holds. Parts 1 and 2 follow.

**B24 sharpness over the weighted cycle-local problem.** It remains to show that (5.20) cannot be improved uniformly in positive `r,t`. Set `r_i=r` and `t_i=t`. Then

\[
D=\frac{2t}{r}\sum_i y_i^2,
\qquad
R=\frac{t}{2r}\sum_i(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2.
\tag{5.22}
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{R}{D}
=\frac14\,
\frac{\sum_i(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2}{\sum_i y_i^2}.
\tag{5.23}
\]

The quadratic form in the numerator is the signless cycle Laplacian `2I+A(C_k)`. Its Fourier eigenvalues are

\[
2+2\cos\frac{2\pi j}{k}
=4\cos^2\frac{\pi j}{k},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,k-1.
\tag{5.24}
\]

For odd `k` the smallest one is `4 sin^2(pi/(2k))`; for even `k` it is zero. Therefore

\[
\lambda_k(\mathbf r,\mathbf t)
=\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}
\quad(k\text{ odd, uniform }r,t),
\tag{5.25}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{r_i,t_i>0}\lambda_k(r,t)
=\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}
}
\quad(k\text{ odd}).
\tag{5.26}
\]

The upper bound is (5.20); equality is (5.25). This completes the proof. `□`

# 6. B11 interpretation: the obstruction is a weighted signless-cycle eigenvalue

Equations (5.8)–(5.11) turn the stability problem into a generalized eigenvalue problem

\[
R y=\lambda D y
\]

with positive diagonal `D` and a weighted signless-cycle form `R`. The odd-cycle frustration is exactly the impossibility of a globally alternating zero mode; the antiperiodic two-dimensional Fourier family in (5.12) supplies the lowest universal substitute. This is why the constant is trigonometric rather than an artifact of the WARM nonlinearities.

This dual formulation also explains why off-cycle incident edges do not hurt the certificate: they enlarge `S_v`, which only decreases the subtractive square coefficient in (5.5) and therefore strengthens the lower bound (5.6) on `V`.

\[
\sec^2\frac{\pi}{2k}\le \sec^2\frac{\pi}{6}=\frac43,
\tag{7.1}

## 7.2 Graph-structure refinement

If a finite graph has odd girth `g` (smallest odd cycle length) then every not-linearly-unstable equilibrium has forest support whenever

\[
\alpha>\sec^2\frac{\pi}{2g},
\tag{7.2}
\]

because even cycles are impossible for `alpha>1` and every odd cycle has length at least `g`, for which the right side only decreases as the length grows. In particular, triangle-free graphs need only

\[
\alpha>\sec^2\frac{\pi}{10}\approx1.1055728,
\]

and bipartite graphs need only `alpha>1`.

Equivalently, if a not-linearly-unstable support contains an odd `k`-cycle, then necessarily

\[
\alpha\le\sec^2\frac{\pi}{2k}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For fixed `alpha>1`, only finitely many short odd cycle lengths can survive this necessary condition.

## 7.3 Exact endpoint obstruction

The deterministic equilibrium statement cannot be strengthened from `alpha>4/3` to `alpha>=4/3`: the 2016 paper proves that the uniform full-support triangle equilibrium is **critical** at `alpha=4/3`. It is therefore not linearly unstable, yet its support is a 3-cycle.

This does **not** by itself prove that the stochastic triangle WARM has a cyclic surviving limit with positive probability at the endpoint; it proves only that the standard transfer through “the limit cannot be linearly unstable” cannot settle the endpoint. Any endpoint stochastic theorem would need an additional mechanism excluding convergence to that critical equilibrium.
