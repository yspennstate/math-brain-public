# 2. Source-exact check and a source-formula correction — 17 September 2026

The original 2025 MATRIX/Springer source was reopened from

`https://www.matrix-inst.org.au/wp_Matrix2016/wp-content/uploads/2023/Aletti.pdf`

and pages 9–10 were inspected directly. For a finite graph, a positive firing-rate function `p:V->(0,infinity)` can be normalized to vertex-selection probabilities. The source writes the reinforcement rate

\[
f_e(x)=\sum_{v\sim e}p_v\frac{x_e^\alpha}{\sum_{e'\sim v}x_{e'}^\alpha}
\]

(with normalized `p_v` after passing from continuous to discrete time), defines equilibria by `x_e=f_e(x)`, and states that for `alpha>1` the finite-graph process converges almost surely to an equilibrium that cannot be linearly unstable. It then records the known `alpha>2` forest conclusion and asks:

> Open Problem 12: improve the lower bound `alpha>2` for which every connected component is a tree.

The source also records the uniform-triangle stochastic obstruction: an edge dies almost surely for `alpha>4/3`, while this fails for `alpha<4/3`, and explicitly suggests that the strict bound `alpha>4/3` may be sharp.

There is one important source-exposition mismatch that must be frozen into any closure package. On source page 9 the displayed general-rate vector field contains the factors `p_v`, but the immediately displayed Lyapunov function is written without `p_v`. As rendered, differentiating that unweighted expression cannot recover the preceding nonuniform-rate vector field. I therefore do **not** use that overview display for nonuniform firing rates.

Instead I use the primary general-WARM formula from Holmes–Kleptsyn (2017), equation (11). In their notation the randomly chosen reinforcement set `A` has probability `p_A`, and

\[
L(x)=\sum_e x_e-\frac1\alpha\sum_A p_A\log\!\left(\sum_{j\in A}x_j^\alpha\right).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For graph WARM, `A` is the star of a vertex and `p_A=p_v`. Their Lemma 1 gives `F_e=-x_e\partial_eL`; after `y_e=2\sqrt{x_e}` the flow is an anti-gradient flow. Thus a not-linearly-unstable equilibrium has nonnegative Hessian of `L` on its supported coordinates. This is the correct primary formula for arbitrary positive firing rates.

The primary 2016 van der Hofstad–Holmes–Kuznetsov–Ruszel paper was also rechecked. It proves that the uniform pure triangle equilibrium is linearly stable for `alpha<4/3` and **critical at equality**. Therefore the deterministic implication “not linearly unstable => forest” cannot be extended to `alpha>=4/3` without an additional endpoint mechanism.

# 4. Exact logarithmic Hessian on an embedded cycle

Normalize the finite positive firing rates so that `sum_v p_v=1`; multiplying all rates by a common constant only changes the clock normalization. Let `x` be a WARM equilibrium and let

\[
C=(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1})
\]

be a simple cycle contained in its positive support. Indices are modulo `k`, and `v_i=e_{i-1}\cap e_i`. Put

\[
r_i:=x_{e_i}^{\alpha}>0,
\qquad
S_i:=\sum_{e\ni v_i}x_e^\alpha,
\qquad
 t_i:=\frac{p_{v_i}}{S_i}>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The equilibrium equation gives the exact incidence identity

\[
 x_{e_i}=r_i(t_i+t_{i+1}).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Take a logarithmic variation supported only on the cycle,

\[
x_{e_i}(s)=x_{e_i}e^{s z_i},
\qquad z_e=0\text{ off }C.
\]

Since `x` is a critical point of (2.1), the second derivative along this curve is the Hessian quadratic form. Direct differentiation gives

\[
Q_x(z)=D_x(z)-\alpha V_x(z),
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
D_x(z)=\sum_i x_{e_i}z_i^2
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
V_x(z)=\sum_v p_v\left[
\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e^2-
\left(\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e\right)^2
\right],
\quad q_{v,e}=\frac{x_e^\alpha}{S_v}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using (4.2),

\[
D_x(z)=\sum_i t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr).
\tag{4.6}
\]

At `v_i`, off-cycle direction coordinates are zero, so its exact contribution to (4.5) equals

\[
t_i(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2)
-\frac{t_i}{S_i}(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i)^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Summing the first terms in (4.7) gives `D_x`. Hence, with `y_i=r_i z_i`,

\[
\boxed{Q_x=(1-\alpha)D+\alpha R_S}
\tag{4.8}
\]

where

\[
D(y)=\sum_i t_i\left(\frac{y_{i-1}^2}{r_{i-1}}+\frac{y_i^2}{r_i}\right),
\tag{4.9}
\]

\[
R_S(y)=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{S_i}(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Since `S_i>=r_{i-1}+r_i`, define

\[
R_0(y):=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2,
\tag{4.11}
\]

and note the pointwise domination

\[
0\le R_S(y)\le R_0(y).
\tag{4.12}
\]

For a not-linearly-unstable equilibrium, Holmes–Kleptsyn's anti-gradient representation forces `Q_x(z)>=0` for every supported direction. At `alpha=4/3`, equations (4.8)–(4.10) therefore give the exact endpoint constraint

\[
\boxed{R_S(y)\ge\frac14D(y)\quad\text{for every }y.}
\tag{4.13}
\]

# 5. Main new theorem: endpoint triangle rigidity

## Claim `P13-P0082-ENDPOINT-TRIANGLE-RIGIDITY-03` — PROVED

Let `G` be any finite graph, let all firing rates be positive, let `alpha=4/3`, and let `x` be a WARM equilibrium that is not linearly unstable. Then every connected component of the positive support of `x` is either a tree or an isolated triangle. Moreover, on every cyclic component:

1. the component is exactly a triangle;
2. its three positive edge weights are equal;
3. the three normalized firing probabilities at its vertices are equal;
4. no other positive-support edge is incident to a triangle vertex.

Equivalently, the only possible cyclic obstruction at the endpoint is the fully symmetric isolated triangle already visible in the 2016 pure-triangle model.

### Proof

We prove four rigidity steps.

### Step 1. Only triangles can be cyclic at `alpha=4/3`

Suppose a support component contains a simple cycle of length `k`.

If `k` is even, choose `y_i=(-1)^i`. Then every `y_{i-1}+y_i=0`, so `R_S=0`, whereas `D>0`. Equation (4.8) at `alpha=4/3` gives

\[
Q_x=-\frac13D<0,
\]

contradicting non-linear-instability.

Now let `k` be odd. For `theta` define the anti-periodic Fourier family

\[
\phi_i(\theta)=\cos\left(\theta+\frac{i\pi}{k}\right),
\qquad
y_i(\theta)=(-1)^i\phi_i(\theta).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because `k` is odd, `phi_{i+k}=-phi_i`, so this closes consistently around the cycle. Averaging over `theta`,

\[
\mathbb E\phi_i^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb E(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})^2
=2\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E R_0
=2\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}
\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i},
\tag{5.3}
\]

while

\[
\mathbb E D
=\frac12\sum_i t_i
\left(\frac1{r_{i-1}}+\frac1{r_i}\right).
\tag{5.4}
\]

For positive `a,b`,

\[
\frac{2}{a+b}\le\frac12\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb E R_0\le
\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}\,\mathbb E D,
\]

so some phase has

\[
\frac{R_S}{D}\le\frac{R_0}{D}\le
\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

If `k>=5`, then

\[
\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k}<\sin^2\frac\pi6=\frac14,
\]

contradicting (4.13). Hence every support cycle has length exactly `3`.

### Step 2. A surviving endpoint triangle has equal edge weights

Now let `k=3`. Constraint (4.13) and domination (4.12) imply, pointwise,

\[
R_0(y)\ge R_S(y)\ge\frac14D(y)
\quad\text{for every }y.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Use the family (5.1) with `k=3`. Since `sin(pi/6)=1/2`, equations (5.3)–(5.4) become

\[
\mathbb E R_0=\frac12\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i},
\tag{5.8}
\]

\[
\frac14\mathbb E D
=\frac18\sum_i t_i
\left(\frac1{r_{i-1}}+\frac1{r_i}\right).
\tag{5.9}
\]

But the exact reciprocal identity

\[
\frac1a+\frac1b-\frac4{a+b}
=\frac{(a-b)^2}{ab(a+b)}\ge0
\tag{5.10}
\]

shows termwise that `E R_0 <= (1/4)E D`. Pointwise (5.7) gives the reverse inequality after averaging. Hence equality holds, and because every `t_i>0`, equality in (5.10) holds for each adjacent pair. Thus

\[
r_0=r_1=r_2=:r.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Since `r_i=x_{e_i}^{4/3}`, the three cycle edge weights are equal.

### Step 3. The three firing factors are equal

With `r_i=r`, the quadratic form `R_0-D/4` must be positive semidefinite by (5.7). Write

\[
a=t_0,\quad b=t_1,\quad c=t_2.
\]

A direct expansion gives

\[
4r\left(R_0-\frac14D\right)=y^TM(a,b,c)y,
\tag{5.12}
\]

with

\[
M(a,b,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
a+b&2b&2a\\
2b&b+c&2c\\
2a&2c&c+a
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Positive semidefiniteness implies `det M>=0`. Exact expansion gives

\[
\det M=-3\left[(a+b+c)(ab+bc+ca)-9abc\right].
\tag{5.14}
\]

By AM–GM,

\[
a+b+c\ge3(abc)^{1/3},
\qquad
ab+bc+ca\ge3(abc)^{2/3},
\]

so the bracket in (5.14) is nonnegative, with equality iff `a=b=c`. Hence `det M<=0`; PSD forces equality, so

\[
t_0=t_1=t_2=:t.
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is the B14 symmetry-rigidity step: the endpoint PSD condition itself restores full `D_3` symmetry even though no symmetry was assumed.

### Step 4. No extra positive-support edge can touch the triangle

With `r_i=r` and `t_i=t`, the comparison form is

\[
D=\frac{2t}{r}\sum_i y_i^2,
\qquad
R_0=\frac{t}{2r}\sum_i(y_{i-1}+y_i)^2.
\tag{5.16}
\]

For the triangle, the signless-cycle matrix has eigenvalues `4,1,1`. Therefore the minimum generalized ratio is exactly `1/4`, and its two-dimensional eigenspace is

\[
E=\{y\in\mathbb R^3:y_0+y_1+y_2=0\}.
\tag{5.17}
\]

Thus every nonzero `y in E` satisfies

\[
R_0(y)=\frac14D(y).
\tag{5.18}
\]

Suppose some cycle vertex `v_i` has additional positive-support incident mass. Then

\[
S_i>r_{i-1}+r_i=2r,
\]

so the `i`th coefficient in `R_S` is strictly smaller than the corresponding coefficient in `R_0`. Choose `y in E` with `y_{i-1}+y_i !=0`; this is always possible because on `E`, `y_{i-1}+y_i` is the negative of the remaining coordinate. For that `y`,

\[
R_S(y)<R_0(y)=\frac14D(y),
\]

contradicting (4.13). Hence

\[
S_i=2r\quad(i=0,1,2),
\tag{5.19}
\]

and no other positive-support edge is incident to a triangle vertex.

Finally, `t_i=p_{v_i}/S_i`; equations (5.15) and (5.19) imply

\[
p_{v_0}=p_{v_1}=p_{v_2}.
\tag{5.20}
\]

Indeed (4.2) also gives `x_{e_i}=2rt`, so under the normalized finite-graph convention the common triangle edge weight equals the common triangle vertex-selection probability.

Every cyclic support component contains a simple cycle. We have proved that any such cycle is a triangle and that none of its vertices can support another positive edge, so that whole connected component is exactly the triangle. All remaining support components are acyclic, hence trees. This proves the claim. `square`

# 6. What the theorem does and does not settle

The theorem gives an exact deterministic endpoint classification:

\[
\boxed{
\alpha=4/3,\ x\text{ not linearly unstable}
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{supp}(x)
=\text{forest components}\ \sqcup\ \text{isolated symmetric triangles}.
}
\]

It strengthens the preceding strict-threshold theorem without changing `A_forest`.

It also proves that there is no hidden endpoint obstruction coming from a weighted odd cycle, a nonuniform firing-rate triangle, a triangle with positive support attached to it, or a longer odd cycle. The **only** deterministic cyclic critical geometry is the symmetric isolated triangle.

The 2016 pure-triangle result supplies an actual critical example at equality. Therefore one cannot replace the source's strict statement `alpha>4/3` by the deterministic implication at `alpha>=4/3`. The stochastic endpoint remains a separate question: the source states edge death almost surely above `4/3` and failure below `4/3`, but does not state what happens exactly at equality. The present theorem does not claim that stochastic endpoint is resolved. It says that any endpoint stochastic proof would only need an additional mechanism excluding convergence to the symmetric critical triangle; every other cyclic limit has now been ruled out deterministically.
