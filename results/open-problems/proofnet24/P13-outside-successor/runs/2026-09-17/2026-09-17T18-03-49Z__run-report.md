- G. Aletti et al., _Open Problems in Stochastic Reinforcement Models_, MATRIX Annals/Springer (2025), source PDF: `https://www.matrix-inst.org.au/wp_Matrix2016/wp-content/uploads/2023/Aletti.pdf`.

The surrounding text fixes the intended scope. For finite graphs, the normalized WARM weight vector converges to an equilibrium and the limiting equilibrium cannot be linearly unstable; for \(\alpha>2\), Holmes--Kleptsyn had shown that such an equilibrium is supported on a forest. Open Problem 12 asks to improve that universal exponent. Unlike Open Problem 13 immediately below it, Open Problem 12 does **not** impose uniform firing rates \(p(v)\equiv1\).

The source also supplies the sharp lower obstruction: on the triangle with \(p(v)\equiv1\), an edge dies almost surely for \(\alpha>4/3\), but this fails for \(\alpha<4/3\); it explicitly says that a universal lower bound cannot be below \(4/3\) and suggests that \(\alpha>4/3\) may be sharp.

For the finite graph WARM, write \(x_e>0\) for an equilibrium edge weight on its support, and at a vertex \(v\)

\[
S_v:=\sum_{f\ni v}x_f^\alpha,
\qquad
q_{v,e}:=\frac{x_e^\alpha}{S_v}\quad(e\ni v).
\]

The equilibrium equation is

\[
\boxed{x_e=\sum_{v\in e}p_vq_{v,e}.}\tag{E}
\]

Global normalization of all firing rates is irrelevant: multiplying all \(p_v\) merely rescales the equilibrium total mass/time scale, while the argument below uses only (E) and positivity.

# 4. Three new-method attacks

## B2 — attack the negation / search for an odd-cycle obstruction

If the hoped-for \(4/3\) theorem were false, the smallest source-faithful obstruction should already be a positive equilibrium whose support contains an **odd** cycle and whose Hessian has no destabilizing direction. The triangle is therefore not just a lower-bound example; it is the extremal parity test.

The old even-cycle direction cancels each vertex competition term exactly, but on an odd cycle the signs fail to close by one minus sign. Rather than hide this failure, I treated it as a boundary-condition problem: the correct odd-cycle object should be an **anti-periodic mode**, with a phase twist of \(\pi\) over one lap. This leads to B6.

Result of B2: no counterexample survived. Instead the triangle determines the worst spectral factor \(3/4\), giving the source's \(4/3\) constant.

## B6 — deform the solvable even-cycle direction

For an even cycle the exact alternating vector is a zero-frequency anti-competition mode. For an odd cycle I deform it to

\[
z_i(\theta)=\frac{(-1)^i}{x_i^\alpha}\cos\!\left(\theta+\frac{i\pi}{k}\right),
\]

where \(k\) is the odd cycle length. The cosine factor supplies exactly the missing half-turn after one lap: \(\phi_{i+k}=-\phi_i\). Thus the parity mismatch is converted into a controlled discrete derivative of size \(2\sin(\pi/(2k))\), instead of an uncontrolled endpoint defect.

Result of B6: the defect costs at most \(\sin^2(\pi/(2k))\) of the Hessian variance; the worst odd cycle is \(k=3\), giving \(1-\sin^2(\pi/6)=3/4\).

## B25 — count the same cycle mass twice

The equilibrium identity (E) lets the positive diagonal part of the Hessian be counted exactly as the sum of its two endpoint contributions. This avoids any comparison between different firing rates or cycle edge weights. The same endpoint masses also govern the vertex variance term. After this double count, the only inequality needed is the two-variable harmonic/arithmetic inequality

\[
\frac{2}{a+b}\le \frac12\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right).
\]

Result of B25: arbitrary positive firing rates and arbitrary off-cycle incident edges drop out of the threshold. This is the key source-scope point: the proof is not restricted to regular graphs, uniform firing, pure cycles, or symmetric equilibria.

# 5. Main theorem

**Claim `P13-P0082-WARM-FOREST-4OVER3-01` (PROVED mathematically; not closure-certified).**

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite graph with positive firing rates \((p_v)_{v\in V}\). Let \(\alpha>4/3\). Then every WARM equilibrium that is not linearly unstable has acyclic support. Equivalently, every connected component of its support is a tree.

Consequently the source threshold in Open Problem 12 can be improved from \(\alpha>2\) to

\[
\boxed{\alpha>\frac43}.
\]

Together with the source's uniform-triangle obstruction for every \(\alpha<4/3\), this strict universal threshold is optimal in the sense asked by the source.

## Proof

### Step 1: the full quadratic necessary condition

Holmes--Kleptsyn use the Lyapunov function

\[
L(x)=\sum_{e\in E}x_e-\frac1\alpha\sum_{v\in V}p_v\log\!\left(\sum_{e\ni v}x_e^\alpha\right).
\]

At an equilibrium, the first derivative vanishes in every supported coordinate. Use logarithmic variations

\[
x_e(s)=x_e e^{s z_e}.
\]

Because the first derivative is zero at the equilibrium, definiteness of the second variation is invariant under this local coordinate change. Direct differentiation gives

\[
\frac{d^2}{ds^2}L(x(s))\Big|_{s=0}
=
\sum_e x_ez_e^2
-
\alpha\sum_v p_v\left(
\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e^2
-
\left(\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e\right)^2
\right).
\]

Set

\[
D(z):=\sum_e x_ez_e^2,
\quad
V(z):=\sum_v p_v\left(
\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e^2
-
\left(\sum_{e\ni v}q_{v,e}z_e\right)^2
\right).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{Q(z):=D(z)-\alpha V(z)}.\tag{1}
\]

Holmes--Kleptsyn's gradient-coordinate argument implies that a not-linearly-unstable equilibrium must satisfy

\[
\boxed{Q(z)\ge0\quad\text{for every real supported direction }z.}\tag{2}
\]

As a sign check, choosing one coordinate recovers exactly their equation (17):

\[
x_e-\alpha\sum_{v\in e}p_vq_{v,e}(1-q_{v,e})\ge0.
\]

So to prove instability it is enough to construct one \(z\) with \(Q(z)<0\).

### Step 2: restrict to one simple cycle and double-count endpoint mass

Assume for contradiction that the support contains a simple cycle

\[
C=(e_0,e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1}),\qquad k\ge3,
\]

with indices modulo \(k\). Let \(v_i\) be the cycle vertex shared by \(e_{i-1}\) and \(e_i\). Put

\[
r_i:=x_{e_i}^\alpha>0,
\qquad
t_i:=\frac{p_{v_i}}{S_{v_i}}>0.
\]

Take \(z\) supported only on the cycle. The equilibrium equation (E), multiplied by \(z_i^2\) and summed over cycle edges, gives the exact endpoint double count

\[
\boxed{
D(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr).
}\tag{3}
\]

At \(v_i\), off-cycle coordinates of \(z\) vanish. Hence its contribution to \(V\) is

\[
V_i=t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr)
-\frac{t_i}{S_{v_i}}\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i\bigr)^2.
\]

Since \(S_{v_i}\ge r_{i-1}+r_i\),

\[
V_i\ge t_i\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}^2+r_i z_i^2\bigr)
-\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i\bigr)^2.
\]

Summing and using (3),

\[
\boxed{V(z)\ge D(z)-R(z)},\tag{4}
\]

where

\[
R(z):=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}
\bigl(r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i\bigr)^2.
\tag{5}
\]

No regularity of the edge weights, no equality of firing rates, and no assumption that the cycle is an isolated component has been used.

### Step 3: even cycles

If \(k\) is even, set

\[
z_i=\frac{(-1)^i}{r_i}.
\]

Then every summand in (5) vanishes, including the wrap-around term. Hence \(R=0\), so by (4), \(V\ge D\). In fact here equality of the lower bound is enough. From (1),

\[
Q(z)\le(1-\alpha)D(z)<0
\]

for every \(\alpha>1\). Thus a not-linearly-unstable equilibrium can contain no even cycle. This recovers the preserved historical P0082 argument.

### Step 4: odd cycles by an anti-periodic phase mode

Now let \(k\ge3\) be odd. For a phase \(\theta\in[0,2\pi)\), define

\[
\phi_i(\theta):=\cos\!\left(\theta+\frac{i\pi}{k}\right),
\qquad
z_i(\theta):=\frac{(-1)^i\phi_i(\theta)}{r_i}.
\tag{6}
\]

The sequence is anti-periodic over one lap:

\[
\phi_{i+k}=-\phi_i.
\]

Therefore at every cycle vertex, including the wrap-around vertex,

\[
r_{i-1}z_{i-1}+r_i z_i
=\pm(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1}),
\]

where for \(i=0\) we use \(\phi_{-1}=-\phi_{k-1}\). Consequently

\[
R(\theta)=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i}(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})^2,
\tag{7}
\]

while (3) becomes

\[
D(\theta)=\sum_i t_i\left(\frac{\phi_{i-1}^2}{r_{i-1}}+\frac{\phi_i^2}{r_i}\right).
\tag{8}
\]

Average over uniform \(\theta\). With

\[
s_k:=\sin\frac{\pi}{2k},
\]

we have

\[
\mathbb E_\theta\phi_i^2=\frac12,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\theta(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})^2
=1-\cos\frac\pi k
=2s_k^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb E R
=2s_k^2\sum_i\frac{t_i}{r_{i-1}+r_i},
\tag{9}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E D
=\frac12\sum_i t_i\left(\frac1{r_{i-1}}+\frac1{r_i}\right).
\tag{10}
\]

For all positive \(a,b\),

\[
\frac{2}{a+b}\le\frac12\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right),
\tag{11}
\]

because \(4ab\le(a+b)^2\). Applying (11) term by term in (9)--(10) yields

\[
\mathbb E R\le s_k^2\,\mathbb E D.
\]

Hence for at least one phase \(\theta_*\),

\[
R(\theta_*)\le s_k^2D(\theta_*).
\tag{12}
\]

Also \(D(\theta)>0\) for every \(\theta\): the positive weights in (8) could give zero only if all the distinct phase-shifted cosines vanished simultaneously, which is impossible for \(k\ge3\).

Combining (4) and (12),

\[
V(\theta_*)\ge(1-s_k^2)D(\theta_*)
=\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2k}\right)D(\theta_*).
\]

Therefore by (1),

\[
Q(z(\theta_*))
\le
\left[1-\alpha\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2k}\right)\right]D(\theta_*).
\tag{13}
\]

For every odd \(k\ge3\),

\[
\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2k}\right)
\ge\cos^2\!\left(\frac\pi6\right)=\frac34.
\]

Thus if \(\alpha>4/3\), the bracket in (13) is strictly negative. Hence \(Q(z(\theta_*))<0\), contradicting the necessary condition (2).

So no odd cycle occurs either. The support is acyclic, and every connected component is a tree. QED.

# 7. Why the old \(2\) proof stopped and why this route crosses it

Holmes--Kleptsyn's general leaf-free argument extracts, for each supported edge, a single incident urn where

\[
q\ge1-\frac1\alpha.
\]

To declare that edge a strict `champion` they require \(q>1/2\), which is exactly \(\alpha>2\). This is a one-coordinate localization and necessarily discards the cyclic coupling between vertices.

The present argument instead uses the **full Hessian quadratic form on an entire cycle**. Its anti-periodic mode sees a discrete Poincaré constant. For odd cycle length \(k\), the loss is exactly bounded by

\[
\sin^2\frac{\pi}{2k},
\]

so the instability threshold is

\[
\alpha>\sec^2\frac{\pi}{2k}.
\]

This decreases with \(k\), and the triangle gives the worst value \(4/3\). The source's extremal example is therefore not an accident: it is the shortest odd anti-periodic cycle.

# 8. Counterexample search and failure modes

Counterexample-first search focused on the only structural class not already eliminated by the preserved argument: odd support cycles.

- **Symmetric triangle:** does not refute the theorem; it is exactly critical at \(4/3\) and supplies the lower obstruction below it.
- **Highly unequal cycle weights:** the proof survives because (11) is uniform in \(r_i/r_{i-1}\); imbalance can only make the averaged comparison easier, not break it.
- **Nonuniform firing rates:** the proof survives because all rate dependence is through positive multipliers \(t_i\), and (11) is termwise before summation.
- **Extra chords/off-cycle support at cycle vertices:** these only increase \(S_v\), which decreases the subtractive square coefficient in the variance and therefore strengthens the lower bound (4).
- **Long odd cycles:** easier, not harder; \(\cos^2(\pi/(2k))\uparrow1\).

The route would fail for a genuinely non-graph WARM hypergraph because a support `cycle` need not have exactly two selected colors at each involved urn; this is outside Open Problem 12's finite graph scope and is not claimed.
