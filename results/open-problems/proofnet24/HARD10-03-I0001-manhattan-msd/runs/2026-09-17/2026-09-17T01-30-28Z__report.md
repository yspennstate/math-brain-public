## 4. New mathematical idea before any code

The preceding run identified the right failure of the 2018 lower bound: the first-chaos trial `f` pays the degree-2 penalty generated by `Af` against the *bare* symmetric resolvent. Bou-Rabee–Peres' 2026 transience proof contains exactly the higher-chaos algebra needed to test the next correction: their Fourier–Walsh decomposition uses degrees 1 and 3 and constructs a degree-3 corrector that partially cancels the degree-2 image of a degree-1 function.

The key point is that this is not merely analogous to LTV. At spatial Fourier frequency `p=0`, the fixed-frequency environment operator is the same environment-seen-from-the-particle algebra, with the same continuous-time rate normalization. For a horizontal degree-1 trial, the degree-0 lowering term and the same-species degree-2 component vanish at `p=0`; the surviving cross-species degree-2 term is proportional to `i sin(r) f(r)`. Thus Bou-Rabee–Peres' degree-3 corrector can be inserted directly into the LTV *lower* variational formula.

That one higher-chaos elimination does not yet change the power `1/4`, but it does rigorously produce a logarithmic gain. This is the first concrete instance in this track where an RG-style renormalized denominator beats LTV's bare denominator.

## 5. B11 — exact Schur-complement dualization

Write `H_n` for the positive symmetric operator `lambda-S` restricted to degree `n`, and write `D_n:H_n -> H_{n+1}` for the chaos-raising part of the antisymmetric operator, with adjoint lowering operator `D_n^*`.

For a degree-1 trial `f` and degree-3 correction `k`, the p=0 quadratic cost has the form

\[
\mathcal Q_\lambda(f,k)
=\langle f,H_1f\rangle
+\langle k,H_3k\rangle
+\|D_1f-D_2^*k\|_{H_2^{-1}}^2
+\|D_3k\|_{H_4^{-1}}^2.
\]

For fixed `f`, set

\[
y=D_1f,\quad B=D_2^*,\quad
C=H_3+D_3^*H_4^{-1}D_3.
\]

Then the part depending on `k` is

\[
\langle k,Ck\rangle+\langle y-Bk,H_2^{-1}(y-Bk)\rangle.
\]

Completing the square gives, first on finite-dimensional spectral truncations and then by monotone closure,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_k\left\{
\langle k,Ck\rangle+\|y-Bk\|_{H_2^{-1}}^2
\right\}
=
\langle y,(H_2+BC^{-1}B^*)^{-1}y\rangle.
}
\tag{I0001-SC}
\]

Indeed the minimizer solves

\[
(C+B^*H_2^{-1}B)k=B^*H_2^{-1}y,
\]

and the displayed identity is the Woodbury/Schur-complement identity. This gives an exact interpretation of the degree-3 correction: it raises the degree-2 denominator from `H_2` to

\[
H_2+BC^{-1}B^*.
\]

**Outcome B11.** The correct object is an operator-valued continued fraction, not a better scalar first-chaos ansatz. A full `1/3` proof would have to control this renormalized denominator recursively through all relevant chaos degrees/scales.

## 6. B5 — interpolation and monotonicity of the renormalized penalty

Introduce

\[
K_\theta:=H_2+\theta\,BC^{-1}B^*,\qquad 0\le\theta\le1,
\]

and

\[
F(\theta):=\langle y,K_\theta^{-1}y\rangle.
\]

Differentiating the resolvent gives

\[
\boxed{
F'(\theta)
=-\left\langle
K_\theta^{-1}y,
BC^{-1}B^*K_\theta^{-1}y
\right\rangle
\le0.
}
\tag{I0001-MONO}
\]

Thus opening the degree-3 correction channel can only decrease the antisymmetric penalty. This is a rigorous monotonicity statement, but it does **not** by itself imply any power improvement: one needs a quantitative infrared lower bound on the self-energy `BC^{-1}B^*`.

A counterexample to overpromotion is immediate at the metric level: even a lower bound of the form `lambda^(-1/4)(log 1/lambda)^a` for fixed `a` is `o(lambda^(-1/4-delta))` for every `delta>0`. Therefore a logarithmic gain is real progress but is not movement in `rho_res`.

**Outcome B5.** The interpolation has the right sign; the missing content is quantitative scale accumulation.

## 7. B30 — one chaos shell at a time: a proved logarithmic improvement

### 7.1 Input from Bou-Rabee–Peres' degree-3 corrector

Use normalized Haar measure `dm` on the torus and put

\[
d(s):=1-\cos s.
\]

The p=0 specialization of the degree-1/degree-3 corrector estimate in Bou-Rabee–Peres gives the following. For an even real horizontal first-chaos Fourier trial `\widehat f(r)`, there exists a degree-3 `k` for which

\[
\mathcal Q_\lambda(f,k)
\le C\int_{\mathbb T} D_\lambda(r)|\widehat f(r)|^2\,dm(r),
\tag{7.1}
\]

where constants are independent of small `lambda`,

\[
D_\lambda(r)
:=\lambda+d(r)+\sin^2(r)J_\lambda(r),
\]

\[
J_\lambda(r)
:=\int_{\mathbb T}\frac{dm(\beta)}{W_\lambda(r,\beta)},
\]

and the source corrector denominator has the form

\[
W_\lambda(r,\beta)
=\lambda+d(r)+d(\beta)
+\frac{\sin^2\beta}{4\pi}
\log\!\left(1+\frac{\pi}{\sqrt\lambda+|r|+|\beta|}\right).
\tag{7.2}
\]

The exact numerical normalization of the harmless universal constants is irrelevant for the asymptotic lower bound below. What matters is the positive logarithmic self-energy in (7.2), which is a theorem-level input from the 2026 source, not a heuristic insertion.

Because `phi` is degree 1, `(<phi,k>)=0`. Substituting `psi=f+k` into LTV's exact variational formula and optimizing the scalar Fourier amplitude therefore yields

\[
R(\lambda)
\ge c\int_{\mathbb T}\frac{dm(r)}{D_\lambda(r)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The remaining work is a one-dimensional infrared estimate.

### 7.2 Infrared bound for `J_lambda`

Let

\[
a:=\sqrt\lambda+|r|,
\qquad
L(a):=1+\log\frac1a.
\]

For all sufficiently small `lambda` and `|r|<=1/8`,

\[
\boxed{
J_\lambda(r)\le \frac{C}{a\sqrt{L(a)}}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

**Proof.** Since `lambda+d(r) >= c a^2` up to universal constants in this regime, and for `|beta|<=1/4` we have `d(beta)>=c beta^2` and `sin^2(beta)>=c beta^2`, (7.2) gives

\[
W_\lambda(r,\beta)
\ge c\left[a^2+\beta^2\left(1+\log\left(1+\frac{c}{a+|\beta|}\right)\right)\right].
\]

Split the integral into three regions.

For `0<=|beta|<=a`, the logarithm is at least `c L(a)`, hence

\[
\int_0^a\frac{d\beta}{W_\lambda(r,\beta)}
\le C\int_0^a\frac{d\beta}{a^2+L(a)\beta^2}
\le \frac{C}{a\sqrt{L(a)}}.
\]

For `a<=|beta|<=1/4`,

\[
W_\lambda(r,\beta)
\ge c\beta^2\left(1+\log\frac{c}{\beta}\right),
\]

so, after `y=c/beta`,

\[
\int_a^{1/4}\frac{d\beta}{W_\lambda(r,\beta)}
\le C\int_a^{1/4}
\frac{d\beta}{\beta^2(1+\log(c/\beta))}
\le \frac{C}{aL(a)}.
\]

For completeness, the last inequality follows by splitting the transformed `y`-integral at the square root of its upper endpoint: the upper half has denominator at least a fixed multiple of `log(1/a)`, while the lower half has only `O(a^{-1/2})` length. Finally `|beta|>=1/4` contributes `O(1)` because `d(beta)` is bounded below. For small `a`, both latter contributions are absorbed by `C/(a sqrt(L(a)))`. This proves (7.4). `□`

### 7.3 Logarithmic gain

Set

\[
L_0:=\log(1/\lambda),
\qquad
w_\lambda:=c_*\lambda^{3/4}L_0^{1/4},
\]

with `c_*>0` a sufficiently small universal constant. Since

\[
\frac{w_\lambda}{\sqrt\lambda}
=c_*\lambda^{1/4}L_0^{1/4}\longrightarrow0,
\]

for small `lambda` and `|r|<=w_lambda` we have `a \asymp sqrt(lambda)` and `L(a)\asymp L_0`. Hence (7.4) implies

\[
J_\lambda(r)
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt\lambda\sqrt{L_0}}.
\]

Using `d(r)<=Cr^2` and `sin^2 r<=r^2`,

\[
D_\lambda(r)
\le C\left(\lambda+r^2+\frac{r^2}{\sqrt\lambda\sqrt{L_0}}\right).
\]

On `|r|<=w_lambda`,

\[
\frac{r^2}{\sqrt\lambda\sqrt{L_0}}
\le c_*^2\lambda,
\]

and also `r^2=o(lambda)`. Choosing `c_*` once and for all gives

\[
D_\lambda(r)\le C\lambda
\quad\text{throughout }|r|\le w_\lambda.
\]

Substituting this interval into (7.3),

\[
R(\lambda)
\ge c\frac{w_\lambda}{\lambda}
= c\lambda^{-1/4}L_0^{1/4}.
\]

We therefore obtain the following partial theorem.

There exist `c,lambda_0>0` such that for all `0<lambda<lambda_0`, in the exact randomly oriented two-dimensional Manhattan model and the LTV continuous-time normalization,

\[

\ge c\lambda^{-1/4}\bigl(\log(1/\lambda)\bigr)^{1/4}.
}
\tag{NEW-I0001-L1}
\]

Consequently the additive-functional part satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\widehat E_G(\lambda)
\ge c\lambda^{-9/4}\bigl(\log(1/\lambda)\bigr)^{1/4},

and, after absorbing the order-`lambda^{-2}` martingale contribution exactly as in LTV, the annealed mean-square displacement has the same lower bound at the Laplace-transform level.

This is a **strict asymptotic strengthening** of LTV's `lambda^(-9/4)` lower bound, but it does not change the frozen power metric: for every `delta>0`,

Nor does it prove pointwise `E(t) >= c t^(5/4)(log t)^(1/4)`: the same Tauberian obstruction explicitly identified by LTV remains. No source closure is claimed.
