## 3. Exact outside source recovery

Primary source:

Maryam Abdi and Ebrahim Ghorbani, “Minimum algebraic connectivity and maximum diameter: Aldous–Fill and Guiduli–Mohar conjectures,” *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B* 167 (2024), 164–188, DOI `10.1016/j.jctb.2024.02.005`, arXiv `2212.03571v3`.

The paper assumes throughout that graphs are **simple and connected**. For an `n`-vertex `d`-regular graph, algebraic connectivity is the second Laplacian eigenvalue `mu(G)`.

Exact locator, Conjecture 5.4 (p. 24 in the arXiv PDF): for any fixed `d>=3`, if `Gamma_n` is a sequence of graphs of minimum degree `d` (or a sequence of `d`-regular graphs) with asymptotically minimum algebraic connectivity, then it has asymptotically maximum diameter

`diam(Gamma_n) = (1+o(1)) 3n/(d+1)`.

P16 owns only the regular fixed-degree branch with **fixed `d>=5`**. The minimum-degree branch is excluded.

The source's exact maximum-diameter theorem for connected simple regular graphs is Theorem 5.2. For `n>=2d+4`, let `m=floor(n/(d+1))`. Then

- odd `d`: `D_reg(n,d)=3m-3` if `n ≡ 0 (mod d+1)`, and `D_reg(n,d)=3m-1` otherwise;
- even `d`: `D_reg(n,d)=3m-3` if `n ≡ 0`, `3m-2` if `n ≡1 or 2`, and `3m-1` otherwise, modulo `d+1`.

This exact formula is a normalization/checking tool. It is not a replacement for the asymptotic hypothesis “asymptotically minimum algebraic connectivity.”

The same source proves, among regular graphs already constrained to diameter `3n/(d+1)+O(1)`, the asymptotic minimum algebraic connectivity

- odd `d`: `(1+o(1))(d-1) pi^2/n^2`;
- even `d`: `(1+o(1))2(d-2) pi^2/n^2`.

These constants identify the source's path-like extremal scale but do **not** by themselves prove that global fixed-`d>=5` minimizers must lie in that diameter class.

## 6. Move #2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I tried first to build the shape a counterexample would need rather than proving the conjecture.

The source itself contains the nearest warning. The converse direction “asymptotically maximum diameter implies asymptotically minimum mu” is false for `d>=5`: Theorem 5.3(iii) produces shorter graphs with smaller `mu` than some maximum-diameter graphs. This does **not** refute Conjecture 5.4. In fact the source also exhibits, within the nearly maximum-diameter class, better path-like choices with constants `(d-1)pi^2` for odd d and `2(d-2)pi^2` for even d.

A genuine refutation would need a fixed-degree family with two competing features:

1. a Fiedler/Rayleigh mode whose energy remains on the globally extremal `Theta(n^-2)` scale, and
2. literal graph diameter smaller by `Theta(n)` than `3n/(d+1)`.

The obvious counterexample surgery is to add a positive density of shortcuts to a long path-like regular construction. But a shortcut joining layers whose normalized Fiedler values differ by `Theta(1/sqrt(n))` contributes `Theta(1/n)` energy per edge, while the full target energy is only `Theta(1/n^2)`. Even one macroscopic shortcut at generic locations is too expensive by a factor of n. A bounded number of shortcuts can be hidden near equal-value levels, but cannot reduce diameter by a positive fraction. Thus a counterexample would need a much subtler “spectrally invisible but metrically macroscopic” rewiring.

This suggests the right invariant is not diameter alone but a **longitudinal resistance/spectral length**: to keep `mu=Theta(n^-2)`, an extremal graph must support `Theta(n)` layers across which the Fiedler function changes gradually, and regularity limits how much those layers can be collapsed metrically. I did not turn this into a theorem this run, but it identifies what a counterexample must evade.

## 7. Move #16 — COMPLETE SMALL CASE: d=5, n=8

Before implementing a large checker I found the smallest complete noncubic regular case with a clean hand classification.

Every simple 5-regular graph on 8 vertices is the complement of a simple 2-regular graph on 8 vertices. Up to isomorphism a 2-regular graph is a disjoint union of cycles of length at least 3, so there are exactly three possibilities:

`C8`, `C5 + C3`, `C4 + C4`.

For a graph `H` on 8 vertices, on the subspace orthogonal to the all-ones vector,

`L(complement(H)) = 8 I - L(H)`.

Hence the algebraic connectivity of the complement is `8 - lambda_max(L(H))`, and all quantities are exact.

The exact checker gives:

| complement of | diameter | Laplacian characteristic polynomial | mu |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| `C8` | 2 | `x (x-6)^2 (x-4) (x^2-12x+34)^2` | `4` |
| `C5+C3` | 2 | `x (x-8) (x-5)^2 (x^2-11x+29)^2` | `(11-sqrt(5))/2` |
| `C4+C4` | 2 | `x (x-8) (x-6)^4 (x-4)^2` | `4` |

Therefore the exact finite `mu`-minimizers at `(d,n)=(5,8)` are `complement(C8)` and `complement(C4+C4)`, both with diameter 2 and `mu=4`.

The raw asymptotic benchmark `3n/(d+1)` is 4, so the diagnostic finite excess of those minimizers is `2-4=-2`. This is **not** a counterexample to Conjecture 5.4: `n=8` is a single pre-asymptotic order, and even lies below Theorem 5.2's stated range `n>=2d+4=14`. It remains PARTIAL evidence only.

## 8. Move #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT; exact obstruction to the naive Zhu-local route

The most promising proof architecture is to strengthen Zhu's ordered-Fiedler-vector method from a universal noncubic lower bound to a fixed-degree **stability** statement.

Order a Fiedler vector `f(v_1)<=...<=f(v_n)`. Let `S_i={v_1,...,v_i}`, `q_i=|E(S_i,V\S_i)|`, and `y_i=f(v_{i+1})-f(v_i)>=0`. Zhu's exact identity writes the energy as

`E_G(f) = y^T K y`,

where `K_ij` counts an intersection of cut edge sets. Local degree constraints imply inequalities of the form

`q_i+q_{i+t} >= t(d-t+1)`.

Zhu's universal argument then discards most nonadjacent entries of the positive Gram matrix `K` and retains a tridiagonal/lower-envelope quadratic form. I tested whether that *local* relaxation could plausibly reach the source's sharp odd-degree constant already at d=5.

For Zhu Lemma 2.5, take the smallest cut `k=q_a=1` and d=5. The lower-envelope coefficients are

`p=(1,4,7,8,7)`.

For the nonnegative unit-sum vector

`x=(124,31,0,8,12)/175`,

the exact tridiagonal quadratic form is

`Q(x)=124/175`.

Under the usual block interpolation scaling, its coefficient is

`d Q(x)=124/35 = 3.542857... < 4 = d-1`.

Thus the **tridiagonal/lower-envelope relaxation by itself cannot certify the source's sharp odd-degree path coefficient `d-1` at d=5**. ## 9. New route selected for continuation

The universal bound drops all nonadjacent entries of `K`. The d=5 calculation above proves that this loss is too large to reach the source's sharp odd-degree constant using only local lower-envelope cuts. The next route should retain an aggregate of the nonadjacent overlaps, or dualize the quadratic form to a flow/resistance problem. The target is a stability inequality of the schematic form

`mu(G) >= [C_d pi^2/n^2] + Phi_d(defect(G))/n^2 - o(n^-2)`, 

where `C_d=d-1` for odd d and `C_d=2(d-2)` for even d, and `Phi_d` is positive whenever the graph lacks enough longitudinal distance layers. A theorem of this form would immediately imply Conjecture 5.4 for any asymptotic global minimizer once the matching upper construction is available.

This is only a research target, not a claim. A key difficulty is that the global fixed-`d>=5` minimum constant itself is not yet known in the source, so the proof may have to compare directly against explicit path-like upper constructions and simultaneously establish stability.
