## 2. Exact source and current-literature check

> For any fixed `d>=3`, if `Gamma_n` is a sequence of graphs of minimum degree `d`, or a sequence of `d`-regular graphs, with asymptotically minimum algebraic connectivity, then it has asymptotically maximum diameter, namely `(1+o(1)) 3n/(d+1)`.

## 3. Move #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: exact minimizers are self-consistent global connected `d`-factors

The previous run isolated local proper Fiedler 2-switches. A local first-order condition is too weak for induction. The stronger exact statement is global and elementary.

For fixed admissible `n,d`, define

`mu_min(n,d) := min{ mu(H) : H connected simple d-regular on n vertices }`.

Let `G` attain `mu_min(n,d)`, and let `f` be a unit Fiedler vector of `G`, normalized by

`f perp 1`, `||f||_2=1`.

For a graph `H` on the same labelled vertex set put

`E_H(f)=sum_{uv in E(H)} (f_u-f_v)^2`.

### Claim `P16-SELFCONSISTENT-CONNECTED-DFACTOR-18` — PROVED

For every connected simple `d`-regular graph `H` on the same labelled vertex set,

`E_G(f) <= E_H(f)`.

Equivalently, with edge costs

`c_f(u,v)=(f_u-f_v)^2`,

`G` is a **global minimum-cost connected `d`-factor** for the cost metric generated by its own Fiedler vector.

#### Proof

Rayleigh–Ritz gives

`E_G(f)=mu(G)=mu_min(n,d)`.

For any connected simple `d`-regular `H`, the same vector `f` is an admissible mean-zero unit test vector, so

`mu(H) <= E_H(f)`.

By the definition of `mu_min(n,d)`,

`mu(G) <= mu(H)`.

Therefore

`E_G(f)=mu(G) <= mu(H) <= E_H(f)`.

This is the claimed global variational inequality. QED.

### Why this is stronger than local switch stability

A degree-preserving connected 2-switch is only one feasible move in the connected-`d`-factor polytope. The claim says an exact spectral minimizer optimizes **all** connected `d`-factors simultaneously for the one-dimensional squared-distance costs induced by its own Fiedler vector.

This is a self-consistency/fixed-point formulation:

1. the graph determines its Fiedler coordinate `f`;
2. the coordinate `f` determines a Monge-type edge-cost matrix `(f_i-f_j)^2`;
3. the graph must globally minimize connected `d`-factor cost for that matrix.

It is a PARTIAL route for Conjecture 5.4 because the source conjecture concerns asymptotically near-minimum sequences, not only exact finite minimizers.

### Conditional quantitative near-minimizer form

There is also a clean quantitative statement, but I label it **CONDITIONAL ON AN EXPLICIT NORMALIZATION** because the source's displayed Conjecture 5.4 does not separately formalize the phrase “asymptotically minimum algebraic connectivity.”

Assume one uses the natural ratio convention

`mu(G_n)/mu_min(n,d) -> 1`.

Let

`epsilon(G)=mu(G)-mu_min(n,d) >= 0`.

If a connected degree-preserving 2-switch sends `G` to `H` and lowers the old Fiedler energy by `delta>=0`, then

`delta <= epsilon(G)`.

Indeed,

`mu_min(n,d) <= mu(H) <= E_H(f)=mu(G)-delta`.

Hence a ratio-near-minimal sequence at the expected `Theta(n^-2)` scale can admit no connected proper switch that saves more than `o(n^-2)`. This is a potentially useful stability debt, but it is **not** imported as the source's definition until that normalization is source-pinned.

## 4. Move #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: Monge uncrossing in Fiedler order

Order the vertices by a Fiedler vector,

`f(v_1) >= f(v_2) >= ... >= f(v_n)`,

breaking ties arbitrarily. For four real values with

`a,b >= c,d`,

the cross-to-within replacement satisfies

`(a-c)^2+(b-d)^2 - (a-b)^2-(c-d)^2
 = 2(a-d)(b-c) >= 0`.

Thus replacing two edges from the left side of a Fiedler prefix to the right side by one left-left and one right-right edge is an **uncrossing** operation for squared line distance. This is the same convex/Monge phenomenon behind one-dimensional matching and seriation, but the graph itself need not be a Robinson/unit-interval graph.

The key issue is Fiedler ties: a switch can preserve, rather than strictly reduce, the energy. Instead of assuming a simple Fiedler vector, use a second exact integer potential.

For a vertex ordering `pi`, define the linear-arrangement span

`L_pi(G)=sum_{uv in E(G)} |pi(u)-pi(v)|`.

Among all triples `(G,f,pi)` such that

- `G` is a connected simple `d`-regular exact `mu`-minimizer of order `n`;
- `f` is a unit Fiedler vector of `G`;
- `pi` is a nonincreasing ordering of the coordinates of `f`;

choose one minimizing the integer `L_pi(G)`. Such a choice exists: there are finitely many labelled `d`-regular graphs and finitely many orderings; only compatibility with some Fiedler vector matters.

### Claim `P16-MONGE-SWITCH-CUTWIDTH-18` — PROVED

For such a span-minimal Fiedler-ordered minimizer `(G,f,pi)`, if the edge-connectivity satisfies

`lambda'(G) >= 3`,

then every prefix cut of `pi` has size at most

`d(2d-1)`.

In particular, the Fiedler-order cutwidth is at most `d(2d-1)`.

#### Proof

Fix a prefix

`S={v_1,...,v_k}`

and let `B` be the bipartite graph consisting of the crossing edges between `S` and its complement. Let `M` be a maximum matching in `B`, of size `m`, with matching edges

`u_i v_i`, `1<=i<=m`,

where `u_i in S` and `v_i notin S`.

We first prove

`m <= 2d-1`.

Suppose instead that two matching edges `u_i v_i` and `u_j v_j` satisfy both

`u_i u_j notin E(G)`

and

`v_i v_j notin E(G)`.

Delete `u_i v_i` and `u_j v_j`, and add `u_i u_j` and `v_i v_j`. The resulting graph `H` is simple and `d`-regular. Since `lambda'(G)>=3`, deleting two edges cannot disconnect `G`; adding edges therefore leaves `H` connected.

Set

`a=f(u_i)`, `b=f(u_j)`, `c=f(v_i)`, `d0=f(v_j)`.

Because `u_i,u_j` lie in a Fiedler prefix and `v_i,v_j` lie outside it,

`a,b >= c,d0`.

The exact energy difference is

`[(a-c)^2+(b-d0)^2] - [(a-b)^2+(c-d0)^2]
 = 2(a-d0)(b-c) >= 0`.

Hence

`E_H(f) <= E_G(f)=mu_min(n,d)`.

But `H` is a connected simple `d`-regular graph, so by minimality

`mu_min(n,d) <= mu(H) <= E_H(f)`.

All inequalities are equalities. In particular,

`mu(H)=E_H(f)=mu_min(n,d)`,

so `H` is another exact minimizer and `f` is also a Fiedler vector of `H`. The same ordering `pi` is therefore a valid nonincreasing Fiedler order for `H`.

Now compare the integer span. Let the two left endpoint positions be `p_1,p_2<=k` and the two right endpoint positions `q_1,q_2>k`. Writing

`p_- = min(p_1,p_2)`, `p_+=max(p_1,p_2)`,
`q_- = min(q_1,q_2)`, `q_+=max(q_1,q_2)`,

we have `p_+<q_-`. The two old crossing edges have total span

`q_1+q_2-p_1-p_2=q_-+q_+-p_--p_+`,

whereas the two new same-side edges have total span

`(p_+-p_-)+(q_+-q_-)`.

Their difference is

`2(q_- - p_+) > 0`.

Thus `L_pi(H)<L_pi(G)`, contradicting the defining secondary minimality of `(G,f,pi)`.

Therefore for every pair of matching edges, at least one of

`u_i u_j`, `v_i v_j`

is an edge of `G`.

Fix `i`. The vertex `u_i` already uses at least one of its `d` incident edges on the crossing matching edge `u_i v_i`, so it can be adjacent to at most `d-1` of the other matched left endpoints `u_j`. Similarly `v_i` can be adjacent to at most `d-1` of the other matched right endpoints `v_j`. Since for each `j!=i` at least one of these two adjacencies must occur,

`m-1 <= (d-1)+(d-1)=2d-2`.

Hence

`m <= 2d-1`.

Finally, `B` is bipartite. By König's matching-cover theorem, `B` has a vertex cover of size `m`. Every cover vertex is incident to at most `d` crossing edges, so

`|E(B)| <= d m <= d(2d-1)`.

Since the prefix was arbitrary, every Fiedler prefix cut has that size bound. QED.

### What the theorem does and does not say

It does **not** prove that every exact minimizer has bounded Fiedler cutwidth. Equality switches caused by Fiedler ties can move among exact minimizers. The theorem proves that one may choose an exact minimizer/Fiedler ordering, after an exact secondary descent, so that either its edge-connectivity is at most 2 or its Fiedler cutwidth is bounded solely by `d`.

This is already much stronger than the previous vague “switch obstruction” heuristic and requires no quartic-specific finite block classification.

## 5. Move #20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: exact switch-descent dichotomy

The preceding proof has an algorithmic form.

Start from any exact `mu`-minimal connected simple `d`-regular graph `G`, choose a Fiedler vector and a nonincreasing order `pi`, and while

1. the current graph has edge-connectivity at least 3, and
2. some Fiedler prefix has more than `d(2d-1)` crossing edges,

use the matching argument above to find two crossing edges whose same-side pairs are nonedges, then perform the cross-to-within 2-switch.

Minimality forces every such non-increasing switch to be an **energy equality**; therefore the same `f` remains a Fiedler vector, the graph stays `mu`-minimal, and the integer span `L_pi` strictly decreases. Hence the process terminates.

### Corollary `P16-MINIMIZER-SWITCH-DESCENT-18` — PROVED

Every exact `mu`-minimal connected simple `d`-regular graph can be transformed by finitely many `mu`-preserving Fiedler-uncrossing 2-switches into another exact minimizer `G_*` for which at least one of the following holds:

1. `lambda'(G_*) <= 2`; or
2. `G_*` has Fiedler-order cutwidth at most `d(2d-1)`.

#### Proof

Each switch is connected as long as the pre-switch graph has edge-connectivity at least 3, preserves degree and simplicity by construction, preserves the minimum value and Fiedler vector by the variational sandwich in Section 4, and strictly lowers the positive integer span. Thus infinitely many switches are impossible. If the process stops because edge-connectivity has fallen below 3, branch 1 holds; otherwise no prefix cut can exceed the bound, so branch 2 holds. QED.

This is the strongest structural reduction P16 currently has for exact minimizers. The obvious hope would be that either branch already forces the source diameter coefficient. It does not.

## 6. Exact obstruction to the naive conclusion: bounded width / tiny cuts do not force the `3/(d+1)` diameter coefficient

The failure is visible in a completely explicit fixed-degree family. This is a proof-strategy obstruction, **not** a counterexample to Conjecture 5.4.

Fix `d>=5` and put `s=d+1`. For any `m>=2`, start from `m` disjoint copies

`B_1,...,B_m` of `K_s`.

For each boundary `i` between `B_i` and `B_{i+1}`, choose an internal edge in `B_i` and an internal edge in `B_{i+1}`, delete those two edges, and replace them by the two parallel-matching cross edges between their endpoints. In every internal block choose the deleted edge used for its left boundary vertex-disjoint from the deleted edge used for its right boundary. This is possible because `s=d+1>=6`.

Call the resulting graph `C_{m,d}`.

### Claim `P16-CLIQUECHAIN-WIDTH-DIAMETER-18` — PROVED

`C_{m,d}` is connected, simple and `d`-regular, has

`n=m(d+1)`

vertices, has cutwidth `O_d(1)` in the block order, has 2-edge cuts between every pair of consecutive blocks, and satisfies

`diam(C_{m,d}) = 2m + O(1)
                 = 2n/(d+1)+O(1)`.

Therefore neither bounded cutwidth nor the mere existence of a long chain of 1/2-edge bottlenecks can by itself imply the source target

`diam = 3n/(d+1)+o(n)`.

#### Proof

**Regularity and simplicity.** Every endpoint of a deleted internal clique edge loses one incident edge and gains one cross edge. All other incidences are unchanged. Hence every degree remains `d`. Cross edges join distinct blocks, where no edge existed before, so the graph is simple. The cross edges connect consecutive blocks, so it is connected.

**Small cuts and bounded cutwidth.** Exactly two edges join `B_i` to `B_{i+1}`, so each block boundary is a 2-edge cut. Order vertices block by block. A cut inside one block crosses at most

`floor(s^2/4)`

edges of the underlying clique plus at most two cross edges to the preceding block and two to the next. Deleting clique edges only lowers this count. Thus the cutwidth is at most

`floor((d+1)^2/4)+4`,

a constant depending only on `d`.

**Diameter lower bound.** Any path from `B_1` to `B_m` must cross each of the `m-1` consecutive block boundaries. In each internal block `B_i`, `2<=i<=m-1`, the two endpoints available from the left boundary and the two endpoints available for the right boundary are disjoint by construction. Therefore after entering that block, a path needs at least one internal edge before it can leave through the next boundary. Thus any path from the first block to the last uses at least

`(m-1)+(m-2)=2m-3`

edges before any endpoint corrections. Hence the diameter is at least `2m-3`.

**Diameter upper bound.** Each modified clique `K_s` with at most two disjoint deleted edges has diameter at most 2. More importantly, in every internal block any left connector is adjacent to any right connector, because the only deleted internal edges are the edge within the left connector pair and the edge within the right connector pair. Thus a route through `r` consecutive block boundaries uses one cross edge per boundary and one internal connector-to-connector edge per intermediate block, with only `O(1)` endpoint corrections. In particular every pair of vertices is at distance at most `2m+1`.

Combining the bounds gives

`diam(C_{m,d})=2m+O(1)=2n/(d+1)+O(1)`.

QED.

### Spectral diagnostic, deliberately not overclaimed

For a test vector constant on each block, with value `a_i` on `B_i`, the only energy comes from the two cross edges at each boundary:

`E=2 sum_{i=1}^{m-1} (a_{i+1}-a_i)^2`,

while

`||f||_2^2=(d+1) sum_i a_i^2`.

Choosing the first nonconstant eigenvector of the path on `m` block sites gives the rigorous **upper bound**

`mu(C_{m,d}) <= [2/(d+1)] lambda_2(P_m)
              = [4/(d+1)] (1-cos(pi/m))
              = (1+o(1)) 2(d+1)pi^2/n^2`.

This upper bound is numerically larger than the source path-like candidate coefficient for all fixed `d>=5`, but an upper bound alone does **not** prove that the clique chain is spectrally nonminimal. I therefore do not promote a matching asymptotic or a nonminimality claim without a block-Poincaré / homogenization lower bound.

### Route verdict

The switch-descent reduction has isolated the missing invariant very sharply:

> low spectral cost requires not only a one-dimensional sequence of small cuts, but the **right longitudinal efficiency per vertex**. The source extremizers gain roughly three graph-distance units per `d+1` vertices; a dense clique chain gains only two.

A proof must therefore price the geometry **inside** the bounded-width/small-cut blocks, not just count the cuts.

## 7. What a source-level proof would now have to contain

For exact finite minimizers, Sections 3–5 reduce much of the combinatorial freedom to a self-consistent one-dimensional connected-`d`-factor problem. But Conjecture 5.4 is about asymptotically near-minimal sequences. Two separate upgrades are still missing.

### 7.1 Stability of the switch descent

Under the natural ratio normalization, every connected improving 2-switch can save only `o(n^-2)`. A source-level proof needs to convert that small local saving into an **approximate Monge/uncrossing structure**. Merely knowing that no switch saves a fixed constant is useless because individual Fiedler gaps are themselves small.

### 7.2 Longitudinal block penalty

Even exact bounded cutwidth or repeated 2-edge bottlenecks do not force maximum diameter, by the clique-chain family. One needs a theorem of the following qualitative kind:

> among fixed-`d` bounded-frontier chains carrying an eigenfunction at global energy `(1+o(1))mu_min(n,d)`, any positive density of blocks with diameter/volume efficiency strictly below the source extremal block incurs a positive `Theta(n^-2)` coefficient penalty.

This is where the Fiedler eigen-equation, not only the Rayleigh numerator, should enter. The natural technical language is a finite-state / transfer-matrix or Schur-complement homogenization of fixed-width chains.

The simplest nontrivial model to settle is the explicit clique chain above: prove a matching asymptotic for its algebraic connectivity and identify the effective one-dimensional conductance constant. That theorem would not solve the source conjecture, but it would supply exactly the block-penalty mechanism missing from the current route.

and claims that boundedness

### Independent audit

Take dense simple `G,F`, with `F` supported away from the time diagonal so all displayed integrals are initially absolutely legitimate. Pair in the natural `L^q-L^(q')` duality:

`<T_A G,F>
 = int_0^T int_0^t
   < A^(1/2)S(t-s)G(s), F(t,s)> ds dt`.

Fubini gives

`= int_0^T <G(s), int_s^T [A^(1/2)S(t-s)]^* F(t,s) dt> ds`.

For the sectorial functional calculus,

`[A^(1/2)S(t-s)]^*=S(t-s)^* A^(*1/2)`

on the natural dense class, giving exactly P19's formula.

For `1<p,q<infinity`, the mixed-norm spaces are reflexive and

`[L^p_t(L^q_x(L^2_s))]^*=L^(p')_t(L^(q')_x(L^2_s))`.

Hence boundedness of an operator is equivalent to boundedness of its Banach adjoint, with the same operator norm after fixing the concrete equivalent realization. The triangular indicator is correctly converted into the integration range `t>s`.

The only bookkeeping caveat is that gamma-Fubini may be introduced through equivalent norms; exact norm identity refers to the chosen concrete Banach-space realization, while boundedness equivalence is invariant under the equivalence constants. P19's theorem is a boundedness claim and already phrases the realization accordingly.
