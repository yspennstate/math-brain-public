## 3. Move #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: cut-Gram geometry is an electrical network

The previous run showed that Zhu's tridiagonal/lower-envelope relaxation discards too much of the cut Gram matrix to recover the source's sharp fixed-`d` path coefficient already at `d=5`. I therefore changed category: instead of treating the full cut matrix as a graph-combinatorial nuisance, interpret it as the metric tensor of a unit-resistance electrical network.

The exact identity below is linear/electrical algebra and is likely folklore in this coordinate form. **No novelty claim is made.** Its value here is that it identifies exactly what the nonadjacent cut-overlap terms encode and tells us what information is lost by collapsing them.

### Claim `P16-CUTGRAM-ELECTRICAL-DUALITY-17` — PROVED

Let `G` be any connected simple graph with ordered vertices `v_1,...,v_n`. Orient each edge from its lower-index endpoint to its higher-index endpoint. Let `m=|E|` and define the interval/cut matrix

`A in R^{(n-1) x m}`

by

`A_{i,e}=1` if the oriented edge `e=v_a v_b`, `a<b`, satisfies `a<=i<b`, and `0` otherwise.

Thus column `e` is the indicator of the consecutive prefix cuts crossed by that edge. Put

`K=A A^T`.

Then `K_{ij}=|partial S_i cap partial S_j|` for `S_i={v_1,...,v_i}`: this is exactly Zhu's full cut-Gram matrix.

For `1<=a<b<=n`, let `s_{ab} in R^{n-1}` be the interval indicator

`(s_{ab})_i=1_{a<=i<b}`.

Then

`R_eff(v_a,v_b) = s_{ab}^T K^{-1} s_{ab}`,

where every edge of `G` has unit resistance. In particular,

`R_eff(v_1,v_n)=1^T K^{-1} 1`.

#### Proof

First `A` has full row rank. Suppose `A^T y=0`. Define potentials

`p_1=0`, `p_k=sum_{i=1}^{k-1} y_i` for `2<=k<=n`.

For every edge `v_a v_b` with `a<b`,

`0=(A^T y)_e=sum_{i=a}^{b-1} y_i=p_b-p_a`.

Connectedness forces `p` to be constant, hence identically zero, and therefore every `y_i=p_{i+1}-p_i=0`. Thus `K=A A^T` is positive definite.

Now let `z in R^m` be a signed flow in the chosen edge orientations. The quantity `(Az)_i` is exactly the signed net flow leaving the prefix `S_i`, because every edge crossing `S_i` is oriented from the prefix to its complement. Consequently

`Az=s_{ab}`

is equivalent, by taking consecutive differences of prefix fluxes, to the divergence constraints

`div(z)(v_a)=+1`, `div(z)(v_b)=-1`, and `div(z)=0` elsewhere.

Thomson's principle for a unit-resistance network therefore gives

`R_eff(v_a,v_b)=min{||z||_2^2 : Az=s_{ab}}`.

Because `A` has full row rank, the minimum-norm solution is

`z_*=A^T(AA^T)^{-1}s_{ab}=A^T K^{-1}s_{ab}`.

Hence

`||z_*||_2^2 = s_{ab}^T K^{-1} A A^T K^{-1}s_{ab}=s_{ab}^T K^{-1}s_{ab}`,

which proves the formula. QED.

### Interpretation

Zhu's nonadjacent entries of `K` are not merely extra positive error terms. Together they determine **all effective resistances** through `K^{-1}`. The previous tridiagonal counterexample therefore has a precise meaning: local cut sizes and adjacent overlaps do not retain enough of the global electrical shortcut geometry to force the source's sharp path coefficient.

## 4. Move #11 — DUALIZE: exact generalized eigenproblem and the endpoint-resistance barrier

The electrical identity suggests dualizing the entire spectral quotient, not only individual cuts.

### Claim `P16-CUTGRAM-GENERALIZED-EIGEN-17` — PROVED

For the same fixed vertex order, let a vector of increments be `y=(y_1,...,y_{n-1})`. Choose any scalar function `f` with

`f(v_{i+1})-f(v_i)=y_i`.

Its Dirichlet energy is exactly

`E_G(f)=y^T K y`.

After subtracting the mean of `f`, its squared norm is

`||f-mean(f)1||_2^2=y^T T y`,

where

`T_{ij}=min(i,j)(n-max(i,j))/n`.

Equivalently,

`T=(1/n) sum_{1<=a<b<=n} s_{ab}s_{ab}^T`.

Therefore

`mu(G)=min_{y!=0} (y^T K y)/(y^T T y)`

and hence

`mu(G)=1/lambda_max(K^{-1/2} T K^{-1/2})`.

If the order is a Fiedler order, a minimizing increment vector may be chosen coordinatewise nonnegative.

#### Proof

Every edge `v_a v_b`, `a<b`, contributes

`(f(v_b)-f(v_a))^2=(s_{ab}^T y)^2`.

Summing gives `y^T K y`. For the norm use the elementary variance identity

`sum_i (f_i-bar f)^2 = (1/n) sum_{a<b}(f_b-f_a)^2`.

Substitution gives

`T=(1/n) sum_{a<b}s_{ab}s_{ab}^T`.

For fixed `i,j`, the number of pairs `a<b` whose interval contains both cut coordinates `i,j` is

`min(i,j)(n-max(i,j))`,

which gives the displayed entry formula. Every nonconstant mean-zero vertex function corresponds bijectively to a nonzero increment vector, so Rayleigh–Ritz yields the generalized-eigenvalue formula. If vertices are ordered by a Fiedler vector, its increments are nonnegative. QED.

### Corollary: Kirchhoff index in the same coordinates

Combining Section 3 with the expression for `T`,

`tr(K^{-1}T)=(1/n) sum_{a<b} R_eff(v_a,v_b)`.

The right side is the Kirchhoff index divided by `n`, hence

`tr(K^{-1}T)=sum_{k=2}^n 1/lambda_k(G)`.

Again, this is a coordinate reformulation of standard electrical/spectral facts, not a novelty claim.

### What duality does *not* buy: a sharp barrier for the endpoint-only route

If one collapses the full generalized problem to a single endpoint voltage drop, the method becomes exactly the classical McKay–Mohar diameter bound. Let `f` be a unit Fiedler vector with mean zero, and let `u,v` be vertices attaining its minimum and maximum. By the Dirichlet principle,

`mu(G)=E_G(f) >= (f(u)-f(v))^2 / R_eff(u,v)`.

Popoviciu's variance inequality gives

`(f(u)-f(v))^2 >= 4/n`,

while

`R_eff(u,v)<=dist(u,v)<=diam(G)`.

Therefore

`mu(G) >= 4/(n diam(G))`,

or equivalently

`diam(G) >= 4/(n mu(G))`.

This exact inequality is the diameter lower bound attributed by Mohar (1991, Theorem 4.2) to Brendan McKay.

Now compare only against the explicit source path-like upper constructions. Write

`C_d=d-1` for odd `d`, `C_d=2(d-2)` for even `d`.

Those constructions give the candidate scale

`mu_min(n,d) <= (1+o(1)) C_d pi^2/n^2`.

Using only this information in the endpoint-resistance inequality can force no more than

- odd `d`: `diam/n >= 4/((d-1)pi^2)+o(1)`;
- even `d`: `diam/n >= 2/((d-2)pi^2)+o(1)`.

The source target is instead

`diam/n -> 3/(d+1)`.

For `d=5`, the endpoint-resistance route gives only

`diam/n >= 1/pi^2+o(1)`

versus the required `1/2`; the ratio between the two coefficients is exactly `2/pi^2`. As `d` grows, the ratio of this resistance-only coefficient to the desired coefficient tends to `4/(3pi^2)` for odd degrees and `2/(3pi^2)` for even degrees.

### Route verdict

`P16-ENDPOINT-RESISTANCE-BARRIER-17` — **PROVED as a proof-strategy limitation**:

> The package consisting only of an endpoint effective-resistance bound, the generic Fiedler-range inequality, and the source's known path-like upper construction is quantitatively too weak to derive Conjecture 5.4 at the expected fixed-degree scale.

This is **not** a graph counterexample and not a no-go theorem for all electrical methods. The full matrix pair `(K,T)` contains much more information than the scalar endpoint resistance. In particular, a successful electrical proof must preserve the distribution of voltage drop / resistance across many ordered intervals, or an equivalent structural constraint.

## 5. Move #22 — READ THE FAILURES: the closest solved fixed-degree proof uses Fiedler-aware 2-switch descent

The most useful literature lesson this run came not from another successful bound, but from asking what mechanism the solved cubic/quartic cases possess that the fixed `d>=5` problem lacks.

### Zhu 2026: where the current cut method stops

Zhu orders a Fiedler vector, defines the prefix cuts and increments, and writes the exact energy as `y^T K y`. For cubic graphs, a special four-term quadratic identity yields the sharp path constant and the discarded nonnegative remainder supplies stability. For `d>=4`, Zhu instead uses a coarser `d`-term principal-block estimate to get a uniform strict noncubic spectral-gap improvement. It is sufficient for I0010, but it does not identify fixed-`d>=5` near-minimizers or prove their diameter is `(1+o(1))3n/(d+1)`.

That matches the exact d=5 obstruction from the previous P16 run: the local tridiagonal/lower-envelope relaxation is not sharp enough to recover the source's odd-degree path coefficient.

### Abdi–Ghorbani–Imrich 2021: structural descent by elementary switches

The predecessor paper “Regular Graphs with Minimum Spectral Gap” gives a different mechanism for the quartic structural theorem. Its main tool is a degree-preserving two-edge switch guided by the Fiedler vector.

Suppose `ab` and `cd` are edges, `ac` and `bd` are nonedges, and switch

`ab, cd  ->  ac, bd`.

For any scalar vector `rho`, the exact change in its Dirichlet energy is

`E_new(rho)-E_old(rho)
 = (rho_a-rho_c)^2+(rho_b-rho_d)^2
   -(rho_a-rho_b)^2-(rho_c-rho_d)^2
 = 2(rho_a-rho_d)(rho_b-rho_c)`.

Thus if

`rho_a>=rho_d` and `rho_c>=rho_b`,

the switch does not increase the Rayleigh numerator. Abdi–Ghorbani–Imrich call such a move proper (Lemma 3.3), develop reconnection lemmas that keep the graph connected, prove that every connected quartic graph can be transformed by proper switches into a path-like finite block family (Theorem 3.11), and then classify the equality case (Theorem 3.15). Their equality proof explicitly uses

`2(rho_a-rho_d)(rho_c-rho_b)`.

This is the missing shape in the fixed-degree attack: the solved quartic proof does not obtain the extremal structure from a scalar diameter inequality. It **descends in graph space** using the Fiedler vector itself.

### Immediate fixed-degree consequence, and why it suggests a new route

The two-switch energy identity is degree-independent. Therefore, for a truly `mu`-minimal connected `d`-regular graph, any degree-preserving two-switch that remains connected and satisfies both Fiedler inequalities strictly would produce a connected `d`-regular graph with strictly smaller Rayleigh quotient at the old Fiedler vector, hence strictly smaller algebraic connectivity. Such a switch cannot exist.

This gives a concrete first-order optimality principle:

> A fixed-degree `mu`-minimizer is **Fiedler-2-switch-stable**, except where the candidate move disconnects the graph or one of the relevant Fiedler inequalities is an equality.

The important point is not the one-line observation itself; it is what can obstruct the descent. If a graph is at least 3-edge-connected, deletion of the two switched edges cannot disconnect it, so the obstruction must be Fiedler-order / adjacency structure or ties. If the graph has many one- or two-edge-cut obstructions instead, those are exactly the kinds of bottlenecks that can accumulate into a long path-like metric structure.

This suggests a structural dichotomy that neither the local cut relaxation nor endpoint resistance exposed:

1. **switch-rich branch:** enough connected strict proper switches exist to decrease `mu`, contradicting minimality; or
2. **switch-obstructed branch:** obstructions force either many small edge cuts in Fiedler order or a rigid monotone adjacency pattern, both potential sources of linear diameter.

I have **not** proved that this dichotomy already yields `3n/(d+1)`, and I do not label it a theorem. It is the new route selected for the next run.

`Q_0(x)^2 = int_0^infty ||B^{1/2}e^{-tB}x||^2 dt <= C^2||x||^2`

`R_lambda=(I+lambda B^{-1})^{1/2}`.

`(B+lambda)^{1/2}=R_lambda B^{1/2}`

`e^{-t(B+lambda)}=e^{-lambda t}e^{-tB}`.

`Q_lambda(x)
