## 4.1 Restricted nearest-neighbor serial model

Let `G` be a finite simple 5-regular graph with a cyclic vertex partition

\[
V(G)=V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_\ell,
\]

indices modulo `ell`, such that every edge has both endpoints in one layer or joins consecutive layers. Put

\[
s_i=|V_i|,
\qquad
m_i=|E(G[V_i])|,
\qquad
e_i=|E(V_i,V_{i+1})|.
\]

Define the numbers of missing edges relative to the complete local geometry,

\[
h_i=\binom{s_i}{2}-m_i\ge0,
\qquad
c_i=s_i s_{i+1}-e_i\ge0,
\]

and the local layer-capacity defect

\[
\delta_i=s_{i-1}+s_i+s_{i+1}-6.
\]

The first point is that `delta_i` is automatically nonnegative. Any vertex in `V_i` has at most

\[
(s_i-1)+s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}
\]

possible neighbors under the nearest-neighbor hypothesis, while it has degree five. Hence

\[
s_{i-1}+s_i+s_{i+1}\ge6.
\tag{4.1}
\]

So `delta_i>=0` for every `i`.

# 5. Method #25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE

The key identity comes from counting the degree incidences of layer `V_i` in two ways.

Five-regularity gives

\[
5s_i=2m_i+e_{i-1}+e_i.
\tag{5.1}
\]

On the other hand the complete local neighborhood geometry would contribute

\[
s_i(s_i-1)+s_i s_{i-1}+s_i s_{i+1}
=s_i\bigl(s_{i-1}+s_i+s_{i+1}-1\bigr).
\]

Subtract (5.1). The missing internal incidences contribute `2h_i`; the two missing interlayer sets contribute `c_{i-1}+c_i`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 s_i\delta_i=2h_i+c_{i-1}+c_i.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is exact, with no asymptotics and no completeness assumption.

There is also the equivalent interface-budget identity

\[
\boxed{
 e_{i-1}+e_i=s_i(6-s_i)+2h_i.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed `2m_i=s_i(s_i-1)-2h_i`, and substitution into (5.1) gives (5.3).

These identities identify precisely what the complete-interface deformation costs: every missing local edge must be paid for by positive layer-capacity defect.

\[

\[
\boxed{

\[
h_i=0,\qquad c_{i-1}=c_i=0

Also `delta_i=0` says

\[
s_{i-1}+s_i+s_{i+1}=6.
\tag{6.3}

Subtracting consecutive instances of (6.3) gives

\[
s_{i+2}=s_{i-1},
\]

so the layer-size word is 3-periodic. Every period is a positive integer triple `(a,b,c)` with

\[
a+b+c=6.

> **P16-D5-NEAREST-SERIAL-EQUALITY-21 — PROVED / RESTRICTED.**

## 6.2 Spectral selection inside the equality class

\[
S_0=a+b+c=6

\[
R_0=\frac1{ab}+\frac1{bc}+\frac1{ca}
=\frac{a+b+c}{abc}=\frac6{abc}.

\[
C_0=\frac{S_0}{R_0}=abc.

## 6.3 Quantitative structural stability of the serial geometry

Let

\[
B=\{i:\delta_i>0\}.
\]

Because the defects are nonnegative integers,

\[
|B|\le\sum_i\delta_i=3(S-2\ell).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Moreover, since neighboring layers are nonempty,

\[
\delta_i=s_{i-1}+s_i+s_{i+1}-6\ge s_i-4,

so for `i in B`, `s_i<=delta_i+4`. Therefore

\[
\sum_{i\in B}s_i
\le\sum_{i\in B}\delta_i+4|B|
\le5\sum_i\delta_i
=15(S-2\ell).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Consequently, if a sequence of such serial bulks has

\[
\frac{\ell}{S}\to\frac12,

Thus:

> **P16-D5-NEAREST-SERIAL-STABILITY-21 — PROVED / RESTRICTED.**
