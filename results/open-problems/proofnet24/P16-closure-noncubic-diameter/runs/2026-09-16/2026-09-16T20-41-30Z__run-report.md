## 4.1 The restricted model — explicitly not the full source class

Consider a bi-infinite sequence of finite vertex layers `(V_i)_{i in Z}` with periodic positive layer sizes

\[
s_i=|V_i|,
\qquad s_{i+\ell}=s_i.
\]

Every consecutive pair `V_i,V_{i+1}` is joined **completely**. Inside `V_i`, put a simple `r_i`-regular graph. There are no other edges. We require every vertex to have total degree five. Therefore

\[
r_i=5-s_{i-1}-s_{i+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a strict structural restriction on an arbitrary 5-regular graph. It is broader than the source’s repeating clique-cell model because the internal graph in a layer need only be regular, not complete. It is nevertheless only a serial complete-interface model and remains **PARTIAL** for Conjecture 5.4.

A simple `r_i`-regular graph on `s_i` vertices can occur only if

\[
0\le r_i\le s_i-1,
\qquad s_i r_i\equiv0\pmod2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For the inequalities below I deliberately relax away the parity condition. From (4.1)–(4.2), every consecutive triple

\[
(a,b,c)=(s_{i-1},s_i,s_{i+1})
\]

must satisfy

\[
a+c\le5,
\qquad a+b+c\ge6.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Applying the first inequality one index later also shows every layer size is at most four. Thus all local possibilities lie in the finite set

\[
\mathcal T=\{(a,b,c)\in\{1,2,3,4\}^3:a+c\le5,\ a+b+c\ge6\},
\]

which has exactly 30 elements.

For one period define

\[
S:=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}s_i,
\qquad
R:=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\frac1{s_i s_{i+1}},
\qquad
C_{\rm long}:=\frac{S}{R},
\qquad
D_{\rm long}:=\frac{\ell}{S}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Here `R` is exactly the electrical series resistance of one longitudinal period when interface `i,i+1` has conductance `s_i s_{i+1}`. `C_long` is the corresponding mass-to-resistance coefficient. `D_long` is the asymptotic number of layer steps per vertex along a long serial segment.

For any potential drop `Delta` across a period, Cauchy–Schwarz gives the exact one-period variational identity

\[
\min_{\sum_i d_i=\Delta}
\sum_i s_i s_{i+1}d_i^2
=
\frac{\Delta^2}{R}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equality occurs for drops proportional to `1/(s_i s_{i+1})`. Thus `S/R` is not an ad hoc statistic; it is the exact longitudinal stiffness per period mass. Standard one-dimensional periodic homogenization turns it into the leading quotient spectral coefficient. In the source’s three-cell clique bulk, where `S=a+b+c`,

\[
R=\frac1{ab}+\frac1{bc}+\frac1{ca}
 =\frac{a+b+c}{abc},
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac SR=abc,
\]

exactly reproducing the source’s `abc` coefficient.

# 6. Move #30 — ONE SCALE / ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME

The scale localized here is deliberately one-dimensional: complete interfaces, fixed `d=5`, longitudinal variation only. At this scale the conjectural mechanism works perfectly and quantitatively.

That lets us identify the **failure to glue** rather than merely say “arbitrary graphs are more complicated.” The global source problem still lacks a theorem that turns an arbitrary near-minimizer into a serial object whose coarse cells obey a comparable mass/resistance budget.

Three concrete obstructions have to be overcome simultaneously:

1. **Interfaces are not complete.** In an arbitrary graph, a cut between consecutive coarse slabs has a bipartite graph, not a complete join, so its Kron-reduced conductance is not simply the product of slab sizes.
2. **Edges may skip slabs.** Long edges in a Fiedler or metric ordering destroy a literal series decomposition; they must either be shown spectrally expensive or absorbed into a nonlocal effective-conductance functional.
3. **Near-minimality is approximate.** The earlier exact 2-switch/Monge descent applies cleanly to an exact finite minimizer. Source Conjecture 5.4 is asymptotic and permits `o(1)` relative losses. A global proof needs a stable replacement that converts small switch savings into a controlled exceptional mass, not an exact forbidden pattern.

The complete serial theorem therefore narrows the missing global ingredient to the following kind of statement:

> From fixed-degree spectral near-minimality, construct a longitudinal coarse-graining with total exceptional mass `o(n)` whose consecutive effective interface resistances satisfy a serial corrector inequality asymptotically comparable to (4.14).

If such a structure theorem were available, (4.10) is exactly the kind of quantitative penalty that could turn a positive diameter deficit into a positive spectral excess.

This is stronger information than “try homogenization”: the homogenized inequality is now solved in a nontrivial serial class, and the unsolved part is the graph-to-serial reduction.
