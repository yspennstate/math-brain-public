## 3. The exact blocking statement

The source-matching obstruction is still

\[
\boxed{
\mu(G_n)\text{ asymptotically minimum among connected simple fixed-}d\text{ regular graphs}
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{diam}(G_n)=(1+o(1))\frac{3n}{d+1},\quad d\ge5\text{ fixed}.
}
\tag{B}
\]

No prior P16 route proves `(B)` even for `d=5`.

Under the explicit ratio interpretation above, there is a useful way to state exactly what a stability proof would have to establish. Let `R_{n,d}` denote the connected simple `d`-regular graphs of admissible order `n`. For fixed `delta>0`, define the diameter-deficient spectral ratio

\[
\rho_{n,d}(\delta)
:=
\min\left\{
\frac{\mu(G)}{\mu_{\min}(n,d)}:
G\in R_{n,d},\quad
\operatorname{diam}(G)\le\left(\frac{3}{d+1}-\delta\right)n
\right\},
\tag{3.1}
\]

with the convention `+infinity` if the constrained class is empty.

Then the needed quantitative gap is

\[
\boxed{
\forall\delta>0,\qquad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n,d}(\delta)>1.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a **working reformulation conditional on the ratio meaning of “asymptotically minimum,” not a new source theorem**. It isolates the correct denominator: a proof may not assume that `mu_min(n,d)` already has the source candidate leading constant, because that leading constant is itself unknown for fixed `d>=5`.

A tempting stronger sufficient statement would compare every linearly diameter-deficient graph directly with the source maximum-diameter coefficients

\[
C_d=
\begin{cases}
d-1,&d\text{ odd},\\
2(d-2),&d\text{ even},
\end{cases}
\]

but that is strictly stronger than what is presently justified. The source obtains these constants **inside its nearly-maximum-diameter path-like class**; it does not establish them as the global fixed-`d>=5` minima.

## 5. Move #5 — ADD A PARAMETER: diameter deficit as the stability variable

The useful new parameter is not an auxiliary local cut count but the macroscopic diameter deficit `delta` in (3.1). It forces the proof to distinguish two different tasks that earlier routes conflated:

1. identify the global near-minimum spectral scale `mu_min(n,d)` without assuming the source candidate coefficient;
2. prove a positive multiplicative spectral penalty when a fixed fraction of the source diameter is lost.

For the simplest owned case `d=5`, the required ratio statement becomes

\[
\forall\delta>0,\qquad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\min_{\substack{G\in R_{n,5}\\
\operatorname{diam}(G)\le(1/2-\delta)n}}
\frac{\mu(G)}{\mu_{\min}(n,5)} >1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is the **simplest source-valid special case still unproved**. It is more honest than trying to prove “coefficient 4” directly, because the global statement `n^2 mu_min(n,5) -> 4 pi^2` is not presently available.

The source itself supplies useful but one-sided calibration. For odd fixed `d>=5` it constructs a shorter `d`-regular family with

\[
\operatorname{diam}=\frac{3n}{d+3}+O(1),
\qquad
\mu\le(1+o(1))\frac{(d+1)\pi^2}{n^2},
\]

and for even `d>=6` a shorter family with

\[
\operatorname{diam}=\frac{3n}{d+2}+O(1),
\qquad
\mu\le(1+o(1))\frac{2(d-1)\pi^2}{n^2}.
\]

For `d=5`, this gives a family of diameter `3n/8+O(1)` with the **upper bound** `mu <= (1+o(1))6 pi^2/n^2`. It would be a mistake to infer an exact “+2 spectral penalty” from this: the source gives an upper bound for those shorter families, not a matching lower bound or their optimality under a diameter constraint.

So differentiating in the diameter-deficit parameter exposes the real missing object: a spectral **lower** cost of longitudinal compression.

## 6. Move #7 — TAKE THE LIMIT: the source’s path-like cells as a one-dimensional electrical medium

The source defines `G(a,b,c;m)` as the sequential join of repeating complete cells

`K_a + K_b + K_c + K_a + K_b + K_c + ...`,

with `a+b+c=d+1` in the regular path-like regime. Consecutive cell interfaces are complete bipartite and therefore have conductances

\[
ab,\qquad bc,\qquad ca,
\]

repeated in series from one period to the next.

For one period, the corresponding series effective conductance is

\[
\kappa(a,b,c)
=
\left(\frac1{ab}+\frac1{bc}+\frac1{ca}\right)^{-1}
=
\frac{abc}{a+b+c}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Since one period has vertex mass `s=a+b+c=d+1`,

\[
s\kappa=abc.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is not asserted as a new theorem. It is an electrical recasting of the source’s own cell-energy calculation: Corollary 3.10 gives exactly

\[
\mu(G)=(1+o(1))\frac{abc\,\pi^2}{n^2}
\]

when all major subgraphs have the same `G(a,b,c;m)` type.

The two source candidate cells therefore have:

- odd `d`: `(a,b,c)=(1,d-1,1)`, so `s kappa=d-1`;
- even `d`: `(a,b,c)=(1,2,d-2)`, so `s kappa=2(d-2)`.

This identifies the continuum object P16 actually wants: not merely “a path,” but a **serial medium whose low-frequency coefficient is mass per period times effective conductance per period**. The source’s algebra already contains this one-dimensional homogenized coefficient.

The literature confirms that this is the right neighboring language once periodic cells are given. Chapman–Shabala (SIAM J. Appl. Math. 77 (2017), DOI `10.1137/16M1092039`) derive macroscopic drift/diffusion for random walks on periodic lattices by discrete/continuous multiple scales. Korotyaev–Saburova (Math. Ann. 377 (2020), DOI `10.1007/s00208-019-01842-3`, arXiv:`1802.00686`) analyze periodic discrete Laplacians and effective masses at band bottoms.

The reason this does not solve P16 is exact: those methods accept the periodic cell structure as input. Conjecture 5.4 asks us to **derive longitudinal serial structure from spectral near-minimality**.

## 7. Move #21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: resistance decomposition and stability

A second neighboring literature is effective-resistance clustering. Alev–Anari–Lau–Oveis Gharan (ITCS 2018, DOI `10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2018.41`, arXiv:`1711.06530`) prove that large effective resistance forces low-conductance separation and can partition graphs after removing a small edge-weight fraction into pieces of bounded resistance diameter.

This is qualitatively aligned with P16: very small algebraic connectivity should create serial bottlenecks. But the available clustering result is far too coarse for the fixed-degree sharp constant. It does not identify three-cell source blocks, does not retain exact vertex mass per longitudinal step, and does not convert a near-minimal spectral ratio into `3/(d+1)` graph-distance efficiency.

The **nearest solved problem in the literature** is therefore still Zhu’s cubic stability theorem, not generic homogenization. Zhu has the missing architecture in the one case where it closes:

1. a sharp base spectral inequality that already has the cubic extremal constant;
2. explicit retained slack terms;
3. a stability argument showing that small slack forces the diamond-chain structure;
4. the structural closeness then yields diameter `3n/4-o(n)`.

For `d=5`, P16 lacks step 1. Zhu’s noncubic local inequality has coefficient `575/171<4` after conversion to algebraic connectivity, and P16’s own exact local cut-profile stress test reaches only `124/35<4`. Therefore “retain more slack around the same local relaxation” cannot produce the source candidate. The base functional itself must be strengthened.

The most plausible replacement is to retain **Kron-reduced / two-terminal conductance of longitudinal slabs**, which uses the full cut-Gram information discarded by a tridiagonal prefix-cut relaxation.

## 8. What a successful proof must contain that no attempted route contains

A source-valid proof of `(B)` must simultaneously contain all of the following.

First, it needs denominator control relative to the actual `mu_min(n,d)`. It may not assume the unproved high-degree candidate coefficient `C_d`.

Second, it needs a sharp fixed-degree inequality using more than local prefix-cut sizes. The earlier exact obstruction shows that nonadjacent cut interactions or equivalent Kron/electrical data are quantitatively necessary already at `d=5`.

Third, it needs **near-minimizer stability**, not only exact finite-minimizer optimality. Any switch/uncrossing argument must tolerate `o(n^-2)` Rayleigh debts and show that their total structural effect is only `o(n)` vertices/edges.

Fourth, it must convert spectral slack into **linear graph-distance efficiency**. The clique-chain obstruction shows that serial bottlenecks alone can spend `(d+1)` vertices for only two units of longitudinal distance rather than three.

Fifth, if it uses homogenization, it must justify the slab/cell decomposition for arbitrary near-minimizers. Periodicity, bounded span, absence of transverse bypasses, and small exceptional mass are conclusions to prove, not assumptions.

Finally, it must survive one- and two-edge cuts, Fiedler ties, irregular cell sizes, and correlations between the graph and its Fiedler vector.

### Independent audit

Uniform R-boundedness in particular gives the uniform operator-norm resolvent control needed for the sectorial direct sum, so the phrase “common sectorial angle” is not being used by itself to smuggle in a missing uniform constant.

For finitely many block-diagonal family elements `T_j=direct_sum_n T_{j,n}` and vectors `x_j=(x_{j,n})`, set

`Y_n=sum_j eps_j T_{j,n}x_{j,n}` and `Z_n=sum_j eps_j x_{j,n}`.

Since `q>=2`, monotonicity of probability `L^r`, Fubini for the `ell^q` sum, the block R-bound, and Kahane–Khintchine give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(\mathbb E\left\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jT_jx_j\right\|_X^2\right)^{1/2}
&\le
\left(\sum_n\mathbb E\|Y_n\|_{X_n}^q\right)^{1/q}\\
&\le K_q M
\left(\sum_n\mathbb E\|Z_n\|_{X_n}^q\right)^{1/q}\\
&=K_qM
\left(\mathbb E\left\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jx_j\right\|_X^q\right)^{1/q}\\
&\le K_qK'_qM
\left(\mathbb E\left\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jx_j\right\|_X^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the direct-sum resolvent family is R-bounded. The sectorial functional calculus and semigroup are block diagonal. If `A` had stochastic maximal regularity with constant `C`, choose a progressively measurable input supported in a single reducing block `X_n`. The stochastic convolution remains in that block and the embedding `X_n -> X` is isometric, forcing `C_p(A_n;T)<=C` for every `n`, contradiction.

I found no gap in the stated claim. The only wording caveat is already neutralized by the uniform R-bound hypothesis: a common angle alone would not imply uniform sectorial resolvent constants, but the actual assumption supplies them.
