## 3. Exact depth-six critical comparison on `T_12 square T_8`

Root the `T_12` factor. Whenever the exploration reaches a new `T_8` fibre, expose only the radius-six open cluster of the seed *inside that fibre*. Let its size be `Z_6`. For each of the eleven forward `T_12` neighbours of the current fibre, use all vertices of this exposed fibre cluster to attempt a crossing into that forward fibre. Conditional on `Z_6`, the crossing succeeds with probability

`1-(1-p)^{Z_6}`.                                                       (3.1)

The edge sets used for different forward directions are disjoint. Distinct particles occupy distinct forward fibres because the rooted first coordinate is a tree. After a successful crossing, choose one successful crossing edge as the seed of the child fibre and repeat with fresh fibre edges. Hence the construction is an honest Galton--Watson subprocess of Bernoulli bond percolation, with mean offspring

`m_6(p)=11(1-E[(1-p)^{Z_6}]).`                                         (3.2)

It remains to compute the truncated-cluster pgf exactly. Put `s=1-p`. For a rooted forward branch of `T_8`, let `h_j` be the pgf value `E[s^{|Y_j|}]` for the depth-`j` cluster including its root. The root has seven forward child edges, so

`h_0=s`,
`h_{j+1}=s(s+p h_j)^7`.                                                (3.3)

The actual fibre root has eight branches. Therefore

`C_6:=E[s^{Z_6}]=s(s+p h_5)^8`.                                        (3.4)

Take

`p_0=277/5000`.

The exact `Fraction` checker archived with this report evaluates (3.3)--(3.4) and proves

`m_6(p_0)>1+1/1000`.                                                    (3.5)

(The exact numerator and denominator of `m_6-1` have about 3.86 million bits, so the output records the exact comparison and bit lengths rather than printing a useless multi-megabyte integer.) The floating value is

`m_6(p_0)=1.001251754245120...`.

Thus the Galton--Watson subprocess is supercritical at `p_0`. Since `m_6(p)` is a polynomial/rationally constructed continuous function of `p` and the inequality in (3.5) is strict, it remains supercritical for some `p<p_0`. Therefore

`boxed(p_c(T_12 square T_8)<277/5000).`                                (3.6)

Every graph in Theorem A retains the standard product as a spanning subgraph, so monotonicity gives (1.2) for `G`.

## 4. Adjacency norm with one free two-pair block

Write the full adjacency as

`A=A_0+K+L_3+L_4`,

where `A_0` is the standard product adjacency, `K` is the symmetric adjacency from `c_1,c_2`, and `L_i=R_{c_i}+R_{c_i^{-1}}` for `i=3,4`.

For the standard product,

`||A_0|| = 2 sqrt(11)+2 sqrt(7)`.                                      (4.1)

Indeed the two factor adjacencies act as a tensor sum, and the regular tree adjacency norms are `2 sqrt(11)` and `2 sqrt(7)`.

Since `H=<c_1,c_2>` is freely generated by this marked pair, the right-regular action of `K` preserves every left coset of `H`, and on each coset is exactly the standard adjacency of the 4-regular tree. Hence

`||K||=2 sqrt(3)`.                                                      (4.2)

No property at all of `c_3,c_4` is needed beyond the simplicity assumptions on the final marked graph. Since each right translation is unitary,

`||L_3||<=2`, `||L_4||<=2`.                                            (4.3)

Consequently

`||A|| <= 2sqrt(11)+2sqrt(7)+2sqrt(3)+4`.                              (4.4)

Use the exact rational upper bounds

`sqrt(11)<199/60`, `sqrt(7)<127/48`, `sqrt(3)<26/15`,                  (4.5)

whose squared margins are respectively `1/3600`, `1/2304`, and `1/225`. From (4.4)--(4.5),

`boxed(||A||<2327/120).`                                               (4.6)

## 5. Nonbacktracking `L^2` transfer and the strict uniqueness floor

The graph in Theorem A is simple and `D=28` regular, so put

`Q=D-1=27`.

Let `N_n` be the operator whose kernel counts length-`n` nonbacktracking walks. The standard recurrence is

`N_0=I`, `N_1=A`, `N_2=A^2-DI`,
`N_n=A N_{n-1}-Q N_{n-2}` for `n>=3`.                                  (5.1)

If `||A||<=a`, spectral calculus applied to (5.1) shows that the exponential `ell^2` growth is bounded by the larger root of

`r^2-a r+Q=0`

when `a>2sqrt(Q)` (and by `sqrt(Q)` in the complementary case). Equivalently, if `z>sqrt(Q)` and

`z+Q/z>a`,                                                               (5.2)

then the nonbacktracking growth exponent is strictly smaller than `z`.

Every open connection contains a shortest open path, and every shortest path is nonbacktracking. Therefore, entrywise,

`T_p(x,y) <= sum_{n>=0} p^n N_n(x,y)`.                                 (5.3)

If `p` times the nonbacktracking growth exponent is less than one, the operator series in (5.3) converges in `ell^2`, proving `p<p_{2->2}`.

Now take

`p_1=559/10000`, `z=1/p_1=10000/559`.

The exact checker verifies

`z^2-27 = 91563013/312481 >0`,                                         (5.4)

and, using (4.6),

`z+27/z-2327/120 = 112711/16770000 >0`.                                (5.5)

Hence the characteristic growth exponent is strictly less than `z`, and therefore

`p_{2->2}>559/10000`.                                                   (5.6)

Finally, `p_{2->2}<=p_u`: for every `p>p_u` there is a unique infinite cluster, and FKG gives

`T_p(x,y)>=theta(p)^2>0`

for every pair `x,y`. A kernel with a uniform positive lower bound on every row cannot define a bounded operator on `ell^2` of an infinite graph. Thus (5.6) implies

`boxed(p_u>559/10000).`                                                 (5.7)

Combining (3.6) and (5.7),

`559/10000 - 277/5000 = 1/2000`,

which proves Theorem A.
