## 3. Recalled transfer lemma, with proof

Let `G=Cay(Gamma,S)` be a simple `D`-regular Cayley graph. Let `A` be its adjacency operator and suppose

\[
\|A\|_{2\to2}\le a.
\]

Put `Q=D-1` and define

\[
\beta_D(a)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt Q,&a\le2\sqrt Q,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{a+\sqrt{a^2-4Q}}2,&a>2\sqrt Q.
\end{cases}
\]

### Lemma 3.1

\[
p_{2\to2}(G)\ge \beta_D(a)^{-1},
\qquad
p_{2\to2}(G)\le p_u(G).
\]

### Proof

Let `N_n(x,y)` be the number of length-`n` nonbacktracking walks from `x` to `y`. On a `D`-regular graph,

\[
N_0=I,\quad N_1=A,\quad N_2=A^2-DI,
\]

and for `n>=3`,

\[
N_n=A N_{n-1}-Q N_{n-2}.
\]

Thus `N_n=P_n(A)`. At a spectral value `lambda`, the characteristic equation is

\[
z^2-\lambda z+Q=0.
\]

Uniformly for `|lambda|<=a`, the exponential growth rate of the scalar recurrence is at most `beta_D(a)`; at a double root there is only an additional polynomial factor. Spectral calculus gives

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|N_n\|_{2\to2}^{1/n}\le\beta_D(a).
\]

If `p<1/beta_D(a)`, then `sum_n p^n N_n` converges in `l^2` operator norm. Every open connection has a self-avoiding witness path and every self-avoiding path is nonbacktracking, so for the connectivity kernel

\[
T_p(x,y)=P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)
\le\sum_{n\ge0}p^nN_n(x,y).
\]

Positivity of the kernels therefore implies that `T_p` is `l^2` bounded, proving `p_{2->2}>=1/beta_D(a)`.

For the other inequality, if `p>p_u` then transitivity gives a unique infinite cluster almost surely and `theta(p)=P_p(o<->infinity)>0`. FKG plus uniqueness yields

\[
T_p(x,y)\ge P_p(x\leftrightarrow\infty,\ y\leftrightarrow\infty)
\ge\theta(p)^2
\]

for every `x,y`. A kernel bounded below by a positive constant on an infinite vertex set cannot define a bounded `l^2` operator. Hence `p_{2->2}<=p_u`. `square`

This is the percolation transfer: the operator estimate is not being counted without the displayed chain to `p_u`.

## 4. New exact free-core/complement criterion

Let `S=S_H sqcup B` be a disjoint symmetric generating set. Assume `H=<S_H>` is freely generated by `m>=2` generators whose inverses are also in `S_H`, so `Cay(H,S_H)` is the `(2m)`-regular tree. Put

\[
q=2m-1,
\qquad r=|B|,
\qquad
K_B=\sum_{b\in B}R_b,
\qquad
\kappa=\|K_B\|_{2\to2}.
\]

The full graph has degree

\[
D=q+1+r.
\]

On `l^2(Gamma)`, the `S_H` adjacency operator is a direct sum over right cosets of `H`, so its norm is `2sqrt(q)`. Consequently

\[
\|A_G\|\le a:=2\sqrt q+\kappa.
\]

Also the free-tree subgraph gives

\[
p_c(G)\le\frac1q.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — operator-norm form

If

\[
q^2>q+r
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
q^2-(2\sqrt q+\kappa)q+(q+r)>0,
\tag{4.2}
\]

then

\[
 p_c(G)\le\frac1q
 <\beta_{q+1+r}(2\sqrt q+\kappa)^{-1}
 \le p_{2\to2}(G)\le p_u(G),
\]

so `p_c(G)<p_u(G)` with the explicit gap

\[
p_u(G)-p_c(G)
\ge
\beta_{q+1+r}(2\sqrt q+\kappa)^{-1}-\frac1q>0.
\]

### Proof

Let `Q=q+r` and consider

\[
P(z)=z^2-a z+Q,
\qquad a=2\sqrt q+\kappa.
\]

If `a<=2sqrt(Q)`, then `beta=sqrt(Q)<q` by (4.1). Otherwise `P` has positive real roots `z_-<=z_+` with product `Q`, and `z_+=beta`. Condition (4.2) says `P(q)>0`. If `q<z_-`, then `q^2<z_-z_+=Q`, contradicting (4.1). Therefore `q>z_+=beta`. Hence `1/q<1/beta`, and Lemma 3.1 plus the embedded free-tree bound on `p_c` proves the claim. `square`

Condition (4.2) is equivalently the transparent operator threshold

\[
\boxed{
\kappa<(\sqrt q-1)^2+\frac rq.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

provided (4.1) also holds. Thus many complementary edges are permitted if their *operator norm* is small; cardinality itself is not the real obstruction.

### Corollary 4.2 — cardinality-only form

Using only `kappa<=r`, a sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
 r<q\frac{\sqrt q-1}{\sqrt q+1}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
P(q)=q^2-(2\sqrt q+r)q+(q+r)
=(\sqrt q-1)
\bigl[q(\sqrt q-1)-r(\sqrt q+1)\bigr],
\]

so (4.4) gives `P(q)>0`. It also implies `q^2>q+r`, because

\[
q\frac{\sqrt q-1}{\sqrt q+1}<q(q-1)
\]

for `q>1`. Theorem 4.1 applies. `square`

This is the requested weakening step after obtaining a positive gap: the earlier qualitative subgroup theorem is converted into an exact free-core/complement region, and then the crude `kappa<=r` assumption is explicitly separated from the sharper operator-norm hypothesis (4.3).

## 5. Rank five plus four complements

Take `m=5`, hence `q=9`. Formula (4.4) becomes

\[
r<9\frac{3-1}{3+1}=\frac92.
\]

Therefore **any** such graph with `r<=4` has a nonuniqueness phase.

At the boundary `r=4`, the crude norm bound is

\[
a=2\sqrt9+4=10,
\qquad D=14,
\qquad Q=13.
\]

Hence

\[
\beta_{14}(10)
=\frac{10+\sqrt{100-52}}2
=5+2\sqrt3<9.
\]

The transfer gives

\[
p_c(G)\le\frac19,
\qquad
p_u(G)\ge p_{2\to2}(G)
\ge\frac1{5+2\sqrt3}
=\frac{5-2\sqrt3}{13}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 p_u(G)-p_c(G)
 \ge\frac{32-18\sqrt3}{117}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `sqrt(3)<26/15`,

\[
\frac{32-18\sqrt3}{117}
>
\frac{32-18(26/15)}{117}
=\frac4{585}>0.
\]

The checker records both the exact algebraic expression and this rational certificate.

As a useful failure boundary, the same cardinality-only proof does **not** reach `q=7,r=4`: the threshold is `7(sqrt(7)-1)/(sqrt(7)+1)<4`. This is not a counterexample and is not recorded as zero progress; it identifies where a sharper `kappa<r` estimate or another comparison is genuinely required.

## 6. Explicit uncovered higher-rank-lattice family

Let `Gamma=SL_3(Z)` and define

\[
x=I+E_{12},
\qquad
P=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then `P^3=I`. Put

\[
B=\{x,x^{-1},P,P^{-1}\}.
\]

### 6.1 `B` generates `SL_3(Z)`

Conjugation by `P` cyclically permutes coordinate indices, so

\[
P xP^{-1}=I+E_{23},
\qquad
P^2xP^{-2}=I+E_{31}.
\]

The elementary commutator identity

\[
[I+E_{ij},I+E_{jk}]=I+E_{ik}
\]

for distinct indices yields

\[
I+E_{13},\qquad I+E_{21},\qquad I+E_{32}
\]

from these three transvections; inverses give the negative elementary transvections. Hence `B` generates every elementary unit transvection and therefore `SL_3(Z)`.

This generation calculation is checked exactly by integer `3x3` matrix multiplication in the run artifact.

### 6.2 A free rank-five core disjoint from `B`

Inside the upper-left `SL_2(Z)` block set

\[
u=I+2E_{12},
\qquad
v=I+2E_{21}.
\]

On the projective coordinate `z=X/Y`, every nonzero power of `u` acts by

\[
u^n:z\mapsto z+2n,
\]

while

\[
v^n:z\mapsto\frac{z}{2nz+1}.
\]

Let

\[
U=\{|z|>1\},\qquad V=\{|z|<1\}.
\]

For every nonzero integer `n`, `u^n(V) subset U`; and if `|z|>1`, then

\[
|2nz+1|\ge2|n||z|-1>|z|,
\]

so `v^n(U) subset V`. Ping-pong therefore gives

\[
\langle u,v\rangle\cong F_2.
\]

Map this free group onto `Z` by `u mapsto 1`, `v mapsto 0`. Standard Schreier rewriting gives the free basis

\[
\{u^jvu^{-j}:j\in\mathbb Z\}
\]

for the kernel. Consequently, for **every** five-element set `J subset Z`, the elements

\[
a_j=u^jvu^{-j},\qquad j\in J,
\]

freely generate `F_5`.

Set

\[
S_H(J)=\{a_j^{\pm1}:j\in J\},
\qquad
S_J=S_H(J)\sqcup B.
\]

Every `a_j` is congruent to `I` modulo `2`, whereas none of the four elements of `B` is congruent to `I` modulo `2`; therefore the union is disjoint. Freeness gives ten distinct directed core generators. Thus `S_J` is a simple symmetric generating set of size 14, because `B` alone already generates `SL_3(Z)`.

Applying (5.1) proves the uniform theorem:

### Theorem 6.1

For every five-element `J subset Z`, iid Bernoulli bond percolation on

\[
G_J=Cay(SL_3(Z),S_J)
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
 p_c(G_J)<p_u(G_J),
\qquad
p_u(G_J)-p_c(G_J)
\ge\frac{32-18\sqrt3}{117}
>\frac4{585}.
}
\]

This is an explicit fixed-generating-set family, not merely the existential statement that `SL_3(Z)` has some favorable Cayley graph.

## 7. Why this is not knowingly absorbed by the requested current scopes

The literature check was refreshed on 20 September 2026. It is bounded evidence, not a worldwide novelty certificate.

1. **arXiv:1804.10191v3 — Hutchcroft, _Percolation on hyperbolic graphs_.** Current arXiv metadata says v3 was last revised 26 March 2019. Its theorem proves `p_c<p_u` for every nonamenable **Gromov-hyperbolic quasi-transitive graph**. `SL_3(Z)` is a higher-rank lattice and is not a hyperbolic group, so this theorem does not cover the present Cayley graphs.

2. **arXiv:2409.12283v1 — Hutchcroft--Pan, _Percolation at the uniqueness threshold via subgroup relativization_.** Current arXiv still lists only v1 (18 September 2024). The stated new class concerns **nonuniqueness at `p_u`** for groups containing an amenable `wq`-normal subgroup of exponential growth, together with a co-heredity theorem for a strong endpoint property. That is important endpoint information but is not itself the fixed-graph quantitative `p_c<p_u` separation proved here.

3. **arXiv:2502.02560v2 — Terlov--Timar, _Weighted-amenability and percolation_.** Current v2 is dated 14 September 2025. It develops weighted/level amenability in the nonunimodular setting and proves a **relaxed** Pak--Smirnova-Nagnibeda-style version of a conjectured `p_h<p_u` statement, where `p_h` is the heavy-cluster threshold. This is not the ordinary fixed Cayley-graph `p_c<p_u` theorem used as T09's source metric.

4. **Later scope check: arXiv:2508.08932 — Choi--Seo.** This paper proves `p_c<p_u` for **every Cayley graph of every acylindrically hyperbolic group**, a substantial enlargement of the hyperbolic class. Higher-rank lattice rigidity excludes `SL_3(Z)` from this group class: current literature on hyperbolic actions of higher-rank lattices (Bader--Caprace--Furman--Sisto, arXiv:2206.06431, Corollary 1.2 for a lattice in a simple rank-at-least-two group) rules out nontrivial coarsely minimal hyperbolic actions, and current group-theory literature also explicitly records `SL_n(Z), n>=3`, as not acylindrically hyperbolic. Therefore Choi--Seo does not absorb the present `SL_3(Z)` family.

5. **Property-(T) endpoint results.** `SL_3(Z)` has property (T), and current 2026 percolation work re-proves that for property-(T) Cayley graphs the configuration at `p_u` is not in the uniqueness phase. This endpoint statement still does not supply a strict positive `p_u-p_c` gap for the fixed graph.

6. **Pak--Smirnova-Nagnibeda (2000).** Their theorem constructs, for every finitely generated nonamenable group, some finite generating system having a nonuniqueness phase. Thus I do not claim that exhibiting *some* favorable Cayley graph of `SL_3(Z)` is new. The source-level content here is the proved fixed generating-set criterion and the explicit degree-14 family with a uniform numerical gap. A bounded exact-phrase/current search did not locate this precise family or this numerical criterion, but external novelty remains **UNVERIFIED**.

## 9. Honest residual obstruction and next mathematical attack

The full conjecture is unchanged: arbitrary Cayley graphs need not expose a large free subtree through their **chosen** generators, and the operator norm of the complementary directions can be too large for Theorem 4.1. The present theorem therefore does not approach universality merely by adding more free generators; doing so would slide back toward the known existential Pak--Smirnova-Nagnibeda phenomenon.

The next useful weakening is already isolated by (4.3): replace the wasteful `kappa<=r` estimate with an actual estimate on

\[
\left\|\sum_{b\in B}R_b\right\|.
\]

A successful next run should target a fixed Cayley graph where the cardinality test fails (for example a rank-four free core with four complementary generators), but a cancellation/spectral estimate proves `kappa<(sqrt(q)-1)^2+r/q`. That would expand source-level fixed-graph scope again. If the complement norm cannot be lowered, a materially different colored/nonbacktracking comparison is required rather than further polishing the `q=9,r=4` constants.
