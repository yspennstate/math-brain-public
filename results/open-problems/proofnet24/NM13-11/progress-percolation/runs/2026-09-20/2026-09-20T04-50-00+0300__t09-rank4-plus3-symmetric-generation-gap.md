 r<q\frac{\sqrt q-1}{\sqrt q+1}

> \[
> \boxed{p_u(G)-p_c(G)>\frac1{287}.}

The hypothesis can be stated more concretely as: `Gamma` is generated by an involution `s` and one further element `t`, so that `B={s,t,t^{-1}}`, and contains `F_2`. No order condition on `t` is used.

## 3. The percolation/operator transfer

I restate the needed transfer so this report is self-contained.

Let `G` be a simple `D`-regular graph with adjacency operator `A` satisfying

\[
\|A\|_{2\to2}\le a.
\]

Put `Q=D-1` and

\[
\beta_D(a)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt Q,&a\le2\sqrt Q,\\
\dfrac{a+\sqrt{a^2-4Q}}2,&a>2\sqrt Q.
\end{cases}
\]

Let `N_n(x,y)` count length-`n` nonbacktracking walks. Then

\[
N_0=I,\qquad N_1=A,\qquad N_2=A^2-DI,
\]

and for `n>=3`,

\[
N_n=A N_{n-1}-Q N_{n-2}.
\]

Thus `N_n=P_n(A)`, and the scalar recurrence at spectral parameter `lambda` has characteristic polynomial

\[
z^2-\lambda z+Q.
\]

For `|lambda|<=a`, its exponential growth rate is at most `beta_D(a)` (a double root contributes only a polynomial factor). Spectral calculus therefore gives

\[
\limsup_n \|N_n\|_{2\to2}^{1/n}\le\beta_D(a).
\]

For bond percolation, every open connection has a self-avoiding witness and every self-avoiding path is nonbacktracking. The union bound gives entrywise

\[
T_p(x,y):=\mathbb P_p(x\leftrightarrow y)
\le \sum_{n\ge0}p^nN_n(x,y).
\]

If `p beta_D(a)<1`, the right-hand side converges as an `l^2` operator. Positivity then gives `||T_p||_{2->2}<infinity`. Hence

\[
p_{2\to2}(G)\ge\beta_D(a)^{-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

On an infinite Cayley graph, if `p>p_u`, there is a unique infinite cluster a.s. and `theta(p)=P_p(o<->infinity)>0`. By FKG and uniqueness,

\[
T_p(x,y)\ge
P_p(x\leftrightarrow\infty,\ y\leftrightarrow\infty)
\ge\theta(p)^2.
\]

A kernel bounded below by a positive constant on an infinite vertex set is not a bounded `l^2` operator. Therefore

\[
p_{2\to2}(G)\le p_u(G).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equations (3.1)-(3.2) are the demonstrated percolation transfer used below.

## 4. Rank four plus three complements

Assume `Gamma` contains a free subgroup with free rank-four generators `a_1,...,a_4`, and let

\[
A_0=\{a_1^{\pm1},\ldots,a_4^{\pm1}\}.
\]

Assume also that `B` is a disjoint symmetric generating set of cardinality three. Put

\[
S=A_0\sqcup B,
\qquad G=Cay(\Gamma,S).
\]

Then `D=11`.

### 4.1 Critical-probability upper bound

The edges labeled by `A_0` preserve every left coset of the free subgroup `H=<a_1,...,a_4>` and induce an 8-regular tree on each coset. Hence the full Cayley graph contains an 8-regular tree through every vertex as a subgraph. Therefore

\[
p_c(G)\le p_c(T_8)=\frac17.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.2 Adjacency norm

On `l^2(Gamma)`, the adjacency operator of the free-core edges is the orthogonal direct sum, over left cosets of `H`, of the adjacency operator of `T_8`. Its norm is

\[
2\sqrt7.
\]

The complementary operator is the sum of three unitary right translations, so by the triangle inequality its norm is at most `3`. Thus

\[
\|A_G\|_{2\to2}\le a:=2\sqrt7+3.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For comparison, the classical Benjamini–Schramm criterion quoted in Pak–Smirnova-Nagnibeda is `rho p_c d<1`. Since normalized `rho d=||A||`, the bounds (4.1)-(4.2) only give

\[
\rho p_c d\le\frac{2\sqrt7+3}{7}>1,
\]

so those coarse bounds do not verify that criterion. This is not a claim that the exact unknown spectral radius fails it; it explains why the nonbacktracking refinement is doing real work.

### 4.3 Exact nonbacktracking exponent and rational gap

Here `D=11`, so `Q=10`. Since `a>2\sqrt{10}`, the larger recurrence root is

\[
\beta
=\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-40}}2
=\frac{3+2\sqrt7+\sqrt{12\sqrt7-3}}2.
\]

We need a strict rational upper bound. First,

\[
127^2=16129>7\cdot48^2=16128,
\]

so

\[
\sqrt7<\frac{127}{48}.
\]

Consequently

\[
12\sqrt7-3<\frac{115}{4}.
\]

Also

\[
\left(\frac{43}{8}\right)^2=\frac{1849}{64}
>\frac{1840}{64}=\frac{115}{4},
\]

hence

\[
\sqrt{12\sqrt7-3}<\frac{43}{8}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\beta
<\frac12\left(3+\frac{127}{24}+\frac{43}{8}\right)
=\frac{41}{6}<7.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (3.1), (3.2), (4.1), and (4.3),

\[
p_u(G)\ge p_{2\to2}(G)>\frac6{41},
\qquad
p_c(G)\le\frac17.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 p_u(G)-p_c(G)
 >\frac6{41}-\frac17
 =\frac1{287}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This proves the percolation statement for every such fixed generating set.

The exact root is numerically

\[
\beta=6.826656742666714778\ldots,
\]

so the direct algebraic lower bound `1/beta-1/7` is about `0.00362744508910`; (4.4) is the cleaner fully rational certificate.

## 5. Removing the prescribed free rank-four subgroup

The rank-four core need not be part of the data in advance.

### Lemma 5.1

If `Gamma` contains `F_2` and `B subset Gamma` is finite, then there exist four elements `c_1,...,c_4` that freely generate `F_4` and satisfy

\[
\{c_i^{\pm1}:1\le i\le4\}\cap B=\varnothing.
\]

### Proof

Choose a free subgroup `F=<u,v>~=F_2`. Map `F` onto `Z` by `u mapsto 1`, `v mapsto 0`. Reidemeister–Schreier rewriting gives the kernel the free basis

\[
\{u^jvu^{-j}:j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]

Only finitely many members of this basis or their inverses can belong to the finite set `B`. Choose four other indices. The corresponding four basis elements freely generate `F_4` and their symmetric set is disjoint from `B`. `square`

Applying the lemma to a symmetric 3-element generating set `B` proves Theorem A.

This is the promised weakening of the group-theoretic hypotheses: one does **not** need a specified free rank-four factor, a free product decomposition, hyperbolicity, or an order-three complement. A three-element symmetric generating set and the existence of some `F_2` subgroup suffice.

## 6. Higher-rank-lattice application

For `n>=5`, `SL_n(Z)` is generated by an element of order two and an element of order three; the `(2,3)`-generation classification gives this precisely in the `SL_n(Z)` family for `n>=5`. Thus

\[
B_n=\{s_n,t_n,t_n^{-1}\}
\]

is a symmetric generating set of cardinality three.

Every `SL_n(Z)`, `n>=3`, contains a copy of `F_2`: embed the upper-left `SL_2(Z)` block and take

\[
u=I+2E_{12},\qquad v=I+2E_{21}.
\]

For completeness, in the projective coordinate `z=X/Y`, nonzero powers act by

\[
u^k:z\mapsto z+2k,
\qquad
v^k:z\mapsto\frac{z}{2kz+1}.
\]

With `U={|z|>1}` and `V={|z|<1}`, every nonzero power of `u` sends `V` into `U`, while for `z in U`,

\[
|2kz+1|\ge2|k||z|-1>|z|,
\]

so every nonzero power of `v` sends `U` into `V`. Ping-pong gives `<u,v>~=F_2`.

Lemma 5.1 now chooses four free generators disjoint from `B_n`, and Section 4 applies. Therefore:

### Corollary 6.1

For every `n>=5`, there are degree-11 Cayley graphs `G_n` of `SL_n(Z)` satisfying

\[
\boxed{p_u(G_n)-p_c(G_n)>1/287.}
\]

In fact there are infinitely many choices of the free rank-four core for each fixed `B_n`.

This family is outside the checked hyperbolic scope: `SL_n(Z)` contains `Z^2` (for example commuting independent elementary unipotents), so it is not a hyperbolic group. It is also a higher-rank lattice, hence not an acylindrically hyperbolic group, so the 2025 Choi–Seo theorem covering every Cayley graph of acylindrically hyperbolic groups does not absorb this class.

## 7. Current literature scopes checked on 2026-09-20

The requested source refresh was performed against the current arXiv records.

1. **Hutchcroft, arXiv:1804.10191v3**, last revised **26 March 2019**, proves `p_c<p_u` for every connected locally finite **nonamenable Gromov-hyperbolic quasi-transitive graph**. This is a powerful all-Cayley result for hyperbolic groups, but not for the higher-rank-lattice class above.

2. **Hutchcroft–Pan, arXiv:2409.12283v1**, submitted **18 September 2024**, studies the behavior **at `p_u`**. It proves nonuniqueness at `p_u` for groups containing an amenable `wq`-normal subgroup of exponential growth and proves a co-heredity principle for a strong endpoint property. It does not by itself furnish the quantitative fixed-graph `p_c<p_u` gap established here.

3. **Terlov–Timár, arXiv:2502.02560v2**, last revised **14 September 2025**, develops weighted amenability/level amenability in the nonunimodular setting and proves a relaxed Pak–Smirnova-Nagnibeda-style statement involving the weighted threshold `p_h<p_u`. It is not the ordinary unimodular fixed-Cayley `p_c<p_u` theorem used here.

4. **Choi–Seo, arXiv:2508.08932** (submitted 12 August 2025) proves `p_c<p_u` for **every Cayley graph of every acylindrically hyperbolic group**. This is an important later enlargement of the 2019 scope. Higher-rank lattices such as `SL_n(Z)`, `n>=3`, are not acylindrically hyperbolic, so it does not cover Corollary 6.1.

5. **Pak–Smirnova-Nagnibeda (2000)** already proves that every finitely generated nonamenable group has *some* finite symmetric generating system with a nonuniqueness phase. Their displayed construction for groups containing free subgroups adjoins free generators and proves success asymptotically as the free rank tends to infinity. Their quoted Benjamini–Schramm sufficient condition is `rho p_c d<1`. The present `q=7,r=3` proof is therefore **not** claimed as a new group-level existence theorem. The bounded check did not find their note certifying this low-rank degree-11 fixed family; indeed, the same coarse norm and tree bounds do not verify their displayed criterion, while the nonbacktracking transform does. This distinction is the candidate novelty. Worldwide novelty remains UNVERIFIED.

## 9. Failure boundary and next mathematical attack

The new low-degree mechanism still needs a free rank-four core. A free rank-three core has `q=5`. With a three-element complement the crude criterion would require

\[
\kappa<(\sqrt5-1)^2+\frac35
=\frac{33}{5}-2\sqrt5
\approx2.1279,
\]

while the triangle bound only gives `kappa<=3`. Thus simply repeating the scalar estimate cannot reach degree `2*3+3=9`.

This is a genuine next obstruction, not a zero result. The next route should exploit the **colored** structure of a three-element complement—especially an involution plus an inverse pair—in the nonbacktracking recurrence, instead of collapsing the complement to the scalar norm `kappa`. A successful colored transfer could lower the required free rank from four to three and would be materially different from further cardinality polishing.
