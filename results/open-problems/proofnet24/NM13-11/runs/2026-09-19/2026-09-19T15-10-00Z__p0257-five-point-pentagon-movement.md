## 6. Deterministic five-point closed-4NN lemma

For a finite tie-free point configuration, call `S={x0,...,x4}` a **closed 4-nearest five-set** if for every `v in S` its four nearest neighbors in the whole configuration are exactly `S\{v}`.

Two facts are elementary but load-bearing.

### 6.1 Distinct closed 4NN five-sets are disjoint

If `S` and `S'` shared `v`, the unique set of the four nearest neighbors of `v` would have to equal both `S\{v}` and `S'\{v}`. Hence `S=S'`.

### 6.2 NUV consumes a closed five-set consecutively

Once NUV first enters `S`, every unvisited point of `S` is closer to the current gadget vertex than every point outside `S`. Therefore NUV consumes all five members consecutively, independent of the entry vertex and of the internal visit order.

Let `z` be the last gadget vertex visited. Unless `z` is the global terminal vertex, its incoming edge is internal and its outgoing edge leaves `S`. If `r_j(z)` is the `j`th nearest-neighbor distance at `z`, the generic degree-two lower charge uses `r_1(z)+r_2(z)` on the `2L` side, whereas the actual two incident edges total at least `r_1(z)+r_5(z)`. Thus a nonterminal closed five-set contributes the extra deterministic bonus

`r_5(z)-r_2(z)`

on the `2L` side.

At most one disjoint five-set can contain the global terminal vertex, so omitting it costs only one bounded gadget bonus before division by `n`.
