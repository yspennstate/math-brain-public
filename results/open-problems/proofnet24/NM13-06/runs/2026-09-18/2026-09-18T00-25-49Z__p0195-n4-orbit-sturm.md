## 6. Method #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING

Let `P_G(beta)` be the random-scan heat-bath transition matrix on `{−1,+1}^4`. Order configurations coordinatewise.

### 6.1 Monotonicity and an increasing second eigenfunction

For a vertex `v`, conditional on the other spins,

`Pr(sigma_v'=+1 | sigma) = exp(beta h_v)/(exp(beta h_v)+exp(-beta h_v))`,

where `h_v=sum_{u~v} sigma_u`. This conditional probability is increasing in every neighboring spin because `beta>=0`. A common-uniform update coupling therefore shows that the one-site heat-bath kernel, and hence their random-scan average, is monotone. The Gibbs sampler is reversible with respect to the Ising measure.

LPW Lemma 22.17 therefore supplies an increasing eigenfunction `f` for the second eigenvalue `lambda_2`.

### 6.2 Force the second eigenfunction into the odd sector

Let `R sigma=-sigma` be global spin flip. Zero field implies `P_G R=R P_G`. Set

`h(sigma)=f(sigma)-f(-sigma)`.

Then `h` is another `lambda_2` eigenfunction and is odd. It is also increasing: if `sigma<=tau`, then `f(sigma)<=f(tau)` and `-tau<=-sigma`, so `f(-tau)<=f(-sigma)`, hence `h(sigma)<=h(tau)`.

Moreover `h` is nonzero. If it vanished identically, then `f` would be even under global spin flip. Since all-minus `<= sigma <=` all-plus and an even increasing function has equal values at the two endpoints, it would be constant, impossible for a nontrivial second-eigenvalue eigenfunction.

Thus `lambda_2` occurs in the odd subspace.

### 6.3 Average over graph automorphisms without losing the mode

Every graph automorphism permutes coordinates, preserves the partial order, and commutes with `P_G`. Average `h` over `Aut(G)`. The average remains increasing, odd, and a `lambda_2` eigenfunction. It is nonzero because a nonzero increasing odd function has strictly positive value at the all-plus state, which every graph automorphism fixes.

Therefore the exact second eigenvalue belongs to

`V_G = {functions invariant under Aut(G) and odd under global spin flip}`.

Conversely all eigenvalues of `P_G|V_G` are nonconstant eigenvalues of the full chain. Hence

`lambda_2(P_G) = largest eigenvalue of P_G restricted to V_G`.

This is the decisive symmetry quotient: for the eleven four-vertex graph classes, `dim V_G` is only

`2,4,6,2,4,4,4,6,2,4,2`.

Outcome of #14: **the full 16-state spectral comparison is reduced exactly to odd automorphism quotients of dimension at most six, with a proof that the quotient still contains the true `lambda_2`.**

## 8. Method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: certify the top root branch globally

A beta grid cannot prove this theorem. The remaining task is to compare the largest real roots of exact polynomials on the whole half-line `z>1`.

The certificate uses the following elementary continuity/resultant principle.

Let `F(z,lambda)` and `G(z,lambda)` be two of the exact factor polynomials above, with nonvanishing leading coefficients for `z>1`. Their roots are eigenvalues of restrictions of a reversible finite Markov chain, so they are real. The largest roots vary continuously with `z`. On an interval on which

`Res_lambda(F,G) != 0`,

the sign of `maxroot(F)-maxroot(G)` cannot change: a sign change would force equality at some intermediate `z`, hence a common `lambda` root and a zero resultant.

The checker therefore performs two exact stages.

### Stage A: identify the true top factor for every graph for every z>1

At the rational sample `z=2`, exact Sturm isolation chooses the factor with largest root. It then compares that factor against every other factor. For each pair it computes the exact resultant in `z`, removes the harmless `z=1` factor, Sturm-isolates **all** resultant roots in `(1,+infinity)`, samples one exact rational point in every complementary interval, and compares largest roots there by exact rational Sturm intervals. Every comparison passes. By continuity, weak dominance also holds at the resultant roots themselves.

Thus the selected factor really carries `lambda_2(P_G)` for every `z>1`, not merely near `z=2`.

### Stage B: compare source and target lambda_2 for all fourteen edge-addition types

For each transition `i -> j`, apply the same exact resultant/Sturm partition to the selected top factors and certify

`lambda_2(P_j(z)) >= lambda_2(P_i(z))`

throughout every connected component of `(1,+infinity)` cut by the resultant roots. The output reports fourteen PASS lines. At `z=1` (`beta=0`) every graph gives the independent-spin random-scan chain, whose second eigenvalue is `3/4`, so all edge comparisons are equal there.
