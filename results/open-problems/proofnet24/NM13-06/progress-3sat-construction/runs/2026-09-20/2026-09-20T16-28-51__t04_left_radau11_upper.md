## 3. New theorem: left Gauss-Radau is a rigorous lower envelope for the complete tail

### Theorem 3.1 — left-Radau absolutely-monotone lower bound

Let `sigma` be a probability measure on `[0,1]` with enough support for the degree-`r` orthogonal polynomial below. Let `p_r` be the monic degree-`r` polynomial orthogonal with respect to the positive measure

\[
x\,d\sigma(x),
\]

i.e.

\[
\int_0^1 x^j p_r(x)\,x\,d\sigma(x)=0,
\qquad 0\le j\le r-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `xi_1,...,xi_r` be its roots. Then `0<xi_i<1`, and there are positive quadrature weights `w_0,w_1,...,w_r` at the nodes

\[
0,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r
\]

such that the quadrature is exact for every polynomial of degree at most `2r`.

Moreover, if `f` is `C^{2r+1}` on `[0,1)` and

\[
f^{(2r+1)}(x)\ge0,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\int f\,d\sigma
\ge w_0f(0)+\sum_{i=1}^r w_i f(\xi_i).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `f=h_K`, because `h_K(0)=0`, this simplifies to

\[
\boxed{
E_\sigma h_K(X)
\ge\sum_{i=1}^r w_i h_K(\xi_i).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof: exactness

Let `P` be any polynomial with `deg P<=2r`. Divide it as

\[
P(x)=x p_r(x)q(x)+s(x),
\]

with `deg q<=r-1` and `deg s<=r`. By (3.1),

\[
\int x p_r(x)q(x)\,d\sigma(x)=0.
\]

At all Radau nodes `0,xi_1,...,xi_r`, the factor `x p_r(x)` vanishes, so `P` and `s` have identical node values. Define the weights by integrating the degree-`r` Lagrange cardinal polynomials on these `r+1` nodes. The resulting quadrature is exact on `s`, hence on `P`. Therefore the rule is exact through degree `2r`.

#### Proof: positivity of the weights

For the fixed endpoint, the degree-`r` cardinal polynomial is

\[
\ell_0(x)={p_r(x)\over p_r(0)}.
\]

Since `ell_0^2` has degree `2r`, exactness gives

\[
w_0=\int \ell_0(x)^2\,d\sigma(x)>0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For an interior node `xi_i`, define

\[
\ell_i(x)=
{x p_r(x)\over \xi_i p_r'(\xi_i)(x-\xi_i)}.
\]

Again `deg ell_i=r`, it equals one at `xi_i` and zero at all other Radau nodes. Exactness on `ell_i^2` yields

\[
w_i=\int \ell_i(x)^2\,d\sigma(x)>0.
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof: one-sided error

First take a polynomial truncation `f_T` with nonnegative coefficients, as will be used for `h_K`. Let `H_T` be the Hermite interpolant of degree at most `2r` matching `f_T` at the endpoint `0` and matching both `f_T` and `f_T'` at each interior node `xi_i`. The interpolation remainder is

\[
f_T(x)-H_T(x)
={f_T^{(2r+1)}(\zeta_x)\over(2r+1)!}
\,x\prod_{i=1}^r(x-\xi_i)^2\ge0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Integrating and using degree-`2r` exactness gives

\[
\int f_T\,d\sigma
\ge w_0f_T(0)+\sum_iw_if_T(\xi_i).
\]

For `h_K`, apply this to

\[
h_{K,T}(x)=\sum_{t=1}^T{x^t\over K+t}
\]

and let `T->infinity`. Monotone convergence applies to the integral and every node evaluation, proving (3.3). QED.

### Corollary 3.2 — one extra moment over Gauss11

The ordinary `r`-node Gaussian lower envelope is exact through degree `2r-1` and requires tilted moments through degree `2r-1`. The left-Radau rule with the same `r` interior nodes plus the zero endpoint is exact through degree `2r` and requires moments through degree `2r`. For `r=11`, this upgrades exactness from degree `21` to `22`, using only the single additional tilted moment

\[
E_{\sigma_K}X^{22}
=\left({\mu_{K+22}\over\mu_K}\right)^2.
\]

Because `h_K(0)=0`, the added endpoint consumes no tail objective directly.

This is the structural inequality gained in this run.
