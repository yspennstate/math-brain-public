## 4. Counterexample-first result A: cycle-membership events do not factor negatively

A natural but invalid block shortcut would be to treat membership of separated vertices in the root permutation cycle as negatively correlated or approximately independent merely because exclusion occupations are strongly Rayleigh. Those are different observables.

Consider the continuous-time interchange process on the three-vertex path

\[
0\;--\;1\;--\;2,
\]

with rate-one clocks on both edges and start from the identity. Let `C_0(t)` be the permutation cycle of vertex `0` at time `t`. Exact diagonalization of the integer generator on `S_3` gives, with `x=e^t>1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(1\in C_0)&=\frac{(x-1)(3x-1)(x^2+x+1)}{6x^4},\\
P(2\in C_0)&=\frac{(x-1)^2(3x^2+2x+1)}{6x^4},\\
P(\{1,2\}\subset C_0)&=\frac{(x-1)^2(x^2+x+1)}{3x^4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Subtracting the product of the marginals factors exactly as

\[
\boxed{
P(1,2\in C_0)-P(1\in C_0)P(2\in C_0)
=
\frac{(x-1)^2(x^2+x+1)
(3x^4+6x^3+2x^2+2x-1)}{36x^8}>0
}
\]

for every `t>0`: the last polynomial equals `12` at `x=1` and has positive derivative for `x>0`.

Thus even on `P_3`, the two cycle-membership events are **strictly positively correlated**. This exactly refutes any proposed block product inequality of the form

\[
P(1,2\in C_0)\le P(1\in C_0)P(2\in C_0).
\]

This does **not** contradict the archived strong-Rayleigh argument, which applies to the set of sites occupied by a fixed color class, not to membership in one permutation cycle.

## 5. Counterexample-first result B: the archived 1/4 random-cut exponent is method-optimal

The archived proof of `NEW-L53A` chooses an iid fair red/blue coloring of the `k` blocks and a spanning tree of the block support. It uses that a binomial number of spanning-tree edges are bichromatic and that directed red-to-blue crossings equal directed blue-to-red crossings.

Could the coefficient `1/4` in `s_k` be improved simply by a sharper treatment of this same one-cut step? No.

Take a possible projected block support that is a **directed simple k-cycle**, one cycle-arrow from block `i` to block `i+1 mod k`. Under a fair binary coloring, let `N` be the number of directed red-to-blue transitions. The number `T` of bichromatic cycle edges is even and `N=T/2`. Every even subset of the `k` cycle edges is the transition set of exactly two binary colorings. Therefore, exactly

\[
2\binom{k}{2j}
\]

of the `2^k` colorings have `N=j`, and

\[
\boxed{
P_\xi(N=j)=2^{1-k}\binom{k}{2j}.
}
\]

Equivalently, `2N` is a `Bin(k,1/2)` variable conditioned to be even.

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, the binomial Chernoff bound gives

\[
P_\xi\left(N\ge \left(\frac14+\epsilon\right)k\right)
\le 2\exp(-8\epsilon^2 k)\to0.
\]

Hence no uniform one-fair-cut argument that needs a probability bounded below away from zero can replace the asymptotic threshold coefficient `1/4` by any larger constant. In particular,

\[
\boxed{\theta_{\rm block}=1/4}
\]

is a genuine ceiling for this proof mechanism, not merely a loose estimate from Paley–Zygmund.

The exact checker enumerated all fair colorings algebraically through `k=64` and verified the stronger finite statement that the largest integer threshold with tail probability at least `1/2` is exactly

\[
\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{4}\right\rceil
\]

for every tested `2<=k<=64`; it also checks that the next integer threshold has tail below `1/2` whenever it exists.

**Route consequence:** another hour spent only sharpening the binary random-cut combinatorics cannot move the fixed metric. To progress toward macroscopic cycles, the new ingredient must exploit more than one binary cut, primitive-return structure, a nonanticipative exploration/restart mechanism, or a lattice-specific local-escape estimate.

## 6. New partial theorem: explicit finite-torus positive density at mesoscopic cycle scale

This does not solve the fixed-`beta`, macroscopic-in-volume conjecture, but it gives an explicit finite-box theorem for the actual unweighted interchange process and turns the old `O(K/sqrt(beta))` statement into concrete constants.

Let `T_m^3=(Z/mZ)^3`, start the rate-one-edge interchange process from the identity, and let `L_{m,beta}(v)` be the cycle length of `v` in the time-`beta` permutation.

### 6.1 Explicit heat-kernel bound on the torus

For the one-dimensional continuous-time walk on the cycle of length `m`, the Fourier formula and

\[
1-\cos\theta\ge \frac{2\theta^2}{\pi^2},\qquad |\theta|\le\pi,
\]

give

\[
\sup_{a,b}p_t^{(m)}(a,b)
\le \frac1m+\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4\sqrt t}.
\]

If `m>=sqrt(t)`, then using `pi<4`,

\[
\sup_{a,b}p_t^{(m)}(a,b)\le \frac{3}{2\sqrt t}.
\]

The three coordinates are independent, hence

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{x,y\in T_m^3}p_t(x,y)\le \frac{27}{8t^{3/2}}
}
\]

whenever `m>=sqrt(t)`.

### 6.2 Explicit first-same-phase interaction bound

Use the same cyclic-time driver as in the archived `HARD10-03-L1`. Before the first same-phase interaction, the cyclic-time walk equals the ordinary tagged-particle SRW `X`. For lag `r beta`, define

\[
D_r(t)=X(t)-X(t-r\beta),\qquad r\beta\le t\le K\beta.
\]

If a same-phase interaction occurs by `K beta`, then for some `1<=r<=K`, `D_r` either starts in the l-infinity ball `B_1` or enters it at an upper/lower endpoint jump. Immediately before such an entering jump it lies in `B_2`.

There are `|B_1|=27`, `|B_2|=125` sites, and each endpoint changes at rate `6`, so the two endpoint mechanisms have total rate `12`. For `m>=sqrt(K beta)` and `beta>=1`, the torus heat bound gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(\tau_{\rm int}\le K\beta)
&\le \frac{729}{8}\beta^{-3/2}\sum_{r=1}^K r^{-3/2}\\
&\quad+\frac{40500}{8}\beta^{-1/2}
\sum_{r=1}^{K-1}(K-r)r^{-3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Using `sum_{r>=1}r^{-3/2}<3`, `K>=1`, and `beta>=1`,

\[
P(\tau_{\rm int}\le K\beta)
<\frac{123687}{8}\frac{K}{\sqrt\beta}
<15461\frac{K}{\sqrt\beta}.
\]

If the origin cycle has length at most `K`, its cyclic-time path returns exactly to the origin after at most `K` periods, so a same-phase interaction has occurred. Therefore:

### Theorem C27-MESO-BOX-01 — PROVED

For every integer `K>=1`, `beta>=1`, and torus side

\[
m\ge \max\{5,\sqrt{K\beta}\},
\]

\[
\boxed{
P\bigl(L_{m,\beta}(0)\le K\bigr)
\le 15461\frac{K}{\sqrt\beta}.
}
\]

By translation invariance, if

\[
D_{>K}:=m^{-3}\#\{v\in T_m^3:L_{m,\beta}(v)>K\},
\]

then

\[
E D_{>K}\ge 1-\delta,
\qquad
\delta:=15461K/\sqrt\beta.
\]

Since `0<=D_{>K}<=1`, Markov applied to `1-D_{>K}` gives

\[
\boxed{
P\bigl(D_{>K}\ge1-\sqrt\delta\bigr)
\ge1-\sqrt\delta.
}
\]

In particular, if

\[
\boxed{\beta\ge (61844K)^2}
\]

and `m>=max{5,sqrt(K beta)}`, then `delta<=1/4` and

\[
\boxed{
P\bigl(D_{>K}\ge1/2\bigr)\ge1/2.
}
\]

This is an explicit finite-box positive-density theorem: at sufficiently large time, with explicit `beta_0(K)`, at least half the sites are in cycles longer than `K` with probability at least one half.

It is **PARTIAL**, not the Tóth conjecture: for fixed `beta`, `K` is bounded independently of `m`, whereas macroscopic means cycle length proportional to `m^3` as `m->infinity`. One may choose `K=floor(sqrt(beta)/61844)` and obtain positive density in cycles of order `sqrt(beta)` (when `K>=1` and the side condition holds), but this still does not make the cycle scale grow with the box at fixed beta.

## 7. Why the new theorem does not close the source question

The finite-box source conjecture requires a fixed `beta>beta_c(3)` and then `m->infinity`, with a positive fraction of vertices in cycles of order `m^3`. The theorem above controls cycles only up to order `sqrt(beta)`. Increasing `beta` with the box is an easier scaling regime and cannot be substituted for fixed beta.

The newest HARD10-03 handoff already identifies the same structural barrier in another language: exact supports with high fragmentation pay strong leakage/random-cut penalties, while the unsuppressed alternatives form near-diffusive logarithmic spanning skeletons. The remaining work is therefore not another deterministic support-leakage estimate; it is to show that a cyclic-time exploration cannot keep realizing such a skeleton forever without accumulating a primitive-return/local-escape cost.

This is also where Hammond/Elboim–Sly ideas are relevant conceptually, but any restart/local-escape lemma must be re-proved for the Z^3 geometry and the actual unweighted measure.
