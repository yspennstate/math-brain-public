# 3. Move 27 — swap the quantifiers: a uniform phase-occupancy theorem for one fresh trace

The first obstacle in a localized-complement algorithm is that a complement trajectory could, in principle, contaminate many candidate future phase windows near a spatial buffer. A fixed-center estimate is not robust enough if the center will be chosen after the trace is seen. The right quantifier order is a **single high-probability event on which every spatial center is controlled simultaneously**.

The following theorem supplies that stronger order of quantifiers for a genuinely fresh chronological trace.

## Proposition `C27-TRACE-PHASE-01` — PROVED / PARTIAL auxiliary theorem

Let `(X_t)_{t>=0}` be continuous-time nearest-neighbor simple random walk on `Z^3`, with rate one in each of the six directions. Let

\[
I_1,\ldots,I_m\subset[0,\beta]
\]

be deterministic pairwise disjoint half-open intervals, each of length at least `1`. For `x in Z^3` and integer `r>=1`, define

\[
J_{x,r}
:=\#\{\ell:\ X(I_\ell)\cap B_2(x,r)\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

There are absolute constants `C,c>0` such that, for every `beta>=2` and `1<=r<=beta`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\sup_{x\in\mathbb Z^3}J_{x,r}
>C r^2\log(\beta+2)
\right)
\le C\beta^{-10}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The exponent `10` is arbitrary: changing `C` gives any fixed polynomial error exponent.

### Proof

Let `(Y_n)` be the embedded discrete-time simple random walk and, for a lattice ball `B=B_2(x,r)`, write

\[
V_B:=\sum_{n\ge0}1_{\{Y_n\in B\}}
\]

for its total number of embedded visits.

The standard three-dimensional Green bound gives

\[
G(z,y)\le \frac{C_0}{1+|z-y|_2}.
\]

Hence, uniformly in the starting point `z` and the ball center `x`,

\[
\mathbb E_z V_B
=\sum_{y\in B}G(z,y)
\le C_1 r^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed the worst case is within distance `O(r)` of the ball, and summing the Green kernel over shells gives

\[
\sum_{s\le O(r)}\frac{O(s^2)}{1+s}=O(r^2).
\]

Put `M=ceil(2C_1r^2)`. Markov's inequality gives

\[
\sup_z\mathbb P_z(V_B\ge M)\le\frac12.
\]

Apply the strong Markov property at the embedded time of the `M`-th visit, then the `2M`-th visit, and so on. For every integer `q>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_z\mathbb P_z(V_B\ge qM)
\le 2^{-(q-1)}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

We also need to control the possibility that one unusually long holding interval intersects many deterministic phase intervals. Let

\[
A_B:=\int_0^\infty1_{\{X_t\in B\}}\,dt
\]

be total occupation time. The continuous-time Green bound gives

\[
\sup_z\mathbb E_z A_B\le C_2r^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The same strong-Markov/Khasminskii argument, now at the stopping times when accumulated occupation first reaches successive multiples of `2C_2r^2`, yields

\[
\boxed{
\sup_z\mathbb P_z(A_B\ge C_3 q r^2)
\le 2^{-q}
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

for an absolute `C_3`.

There is a deterministic bridge from `(V_B,A_B)` to the interval count. Every interval `I_ell` that intersects `B` is of one of two types.

1. It contains a jump whose pre-jump or post-jump state lies in `B`. Distinct half-open intervals contain distinct such jump times, so the number of these intervals is at most `2V_B+1`.
2. It contains no such jump. Then, because the path is piecewise constant and `|I_ell|>=1`, the path is in `B` throughout that interval, contributing at least one unit to `A_B`.

Therefore, pathwise,

\[
J_{x,r}\le2V_B+A_B+1.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equations (3.3)--(3.6) imply that for every fixed center `x`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
J_{x,r}>C_4 q r^2
\right)
\le C_5 2^{-q}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

It remains to make the statement simultaneous in `x`. A standard coordinate exponential-martingale bound gives

\[
\mathbb P\left(
\sup_{0\le t\le\beta}|X_t-X_0|_2>\beta
\right)
\le C e^{-c\beta}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

On the complement of (3.8), if `J_{x,r}>0` and `r<=beta`, then `x` lies within Euclidean distance `2beta` of `X_0`. There are at most `C beta^3` such lattice centers. Take

\[
q=A\log(\beta+2)
\]

with the absolute constant `A` large enough that the right side of (3.7), after multiplication by `C beta^3`, is at most `C beta^{-10}`. Adding (3.8) proves (3.1). `square`

## 3.1 Several preselected fresh traces

No independence between traces is required for the following union-bound corollary. For `q` deterministic labels, or labels selected before the relevant slab, apply (3.1) to each chronological strand. With probability at least `1-Cq beta^{-10}`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x
\#\{\ell:\ \exists i\le q,\ X^{(i)}(I_\ell)\cap B_2(x,r)\ne\varnothing\}
\le C q r^2\log(\beta+2).
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

This is useful because the supremum is outside the probability: the spatial center may be chosen **after** the traces are seen.

For the earlier C27 diagnostic scales

\[
q=\beta^{1/8},\qquad
r=\beta^{1/8},\qquad
T=\beta^{3/16},
\]

a partition into length-`T` windows has `m asymp beta/T=beta^{13/16}` windows, while (3.9) gives at most

\[
C\beta^{3/8}\log\beta
\]

contaminated windows at **any single spatial center**. The fraction is therefore

\[
O(\beta^{-7/16}\log\beta)=o(1).
\tag{3.10}
\]

This proves that simple fixed-center phase-volume exhaustion is not the bottleneck at these scales.

# 4. Move 19 — make the renewal idea an explicit algorithm

The natural attempt to avoid conditioning on all of the complement map is the following localized algorithm.

For a deterministic candidate phase window

\[
I_\ell=[t_\ell,t_\ell+T],
\]

factor one period as

\[
P
=S_\ell\circ W_\ell\circ R_\ell,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
R_\ell=\Phi_{0,t_\ell},\qquad
W_\ell=\Phi_{t_\ell,t_\ell+T},\qquad
S_\ell=\Phi_{t_\ell+T,\beta}.
\]

Suppose the current cyclic anchor is `a_j`.

**Localized-restart procedure.**

1. Search for a phase index `ell` such that the incident-edge space-time tube around
   \[
   b_\ell:=R_\ell(a_j)
   \]
   during `I_ell` is still untouched.
2. Reveal only that local `W_ell` tube. The archived fresh-window theorem then gives SRW anti-concentration and path escape inside the tube.
3. Reveal only the minimum suffix information needed to compute
   \[
   a_{j+1}=S_\ell(W_\ell(b_\ell)),
   \]
   instead of conditioning on the entire map `S_ell`.
4. Mark only the actually exposed phase-indexed spatial regions as contaminated, rotate to another phase, and repeat.

If Step 1 could be justified indefinitely with a positive-density reserve, this would have the correct source measure: identity-start, unweighted interchange, no stationary replacement, no loop weight, and no monotonicity in `beta`.

The point of writing the algorithm explicitly is that it identifies exactly what must be proved next rather than hiding the difficulty inside “choose another fresh phase.”

# 5. Move 20 — the algorithm fails at a diagonal phase-selection problem

Proposition 3.1 does **not** justify Step 1. The reason is a quantifier mismatch that survives even after the spatial-center supremum has been moved outside the probability.

For each candidate phase `ell`, the required center is not one fixed `x`; it is the moving point

\[
b_\ell=R_\ell(a_j).
\]

Let `mathcal R_ell` denote the spatial set whose incident clocks during `I_ell` have already been exposed. The actual cleanliness condition is

\[
\operatorname{dist}(b_\ell,\mathcal R_\ell)>r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

A theorem of the form

\[
\sup_x\#\{\ell:\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathcal R_\ell)\le r\}=o(m)
\tag{5.2}
\]

is **not enough** to imply that some diagonal pair `(ell,b_ell)` is clean. Deterministically, take distinct vertices `x_1,...,x_m` and put

\[
\mathcal R_\ell=\{x_\ell\},\qquad b_\ell=x_\ell.
\]

Then every fixed spatial center belongs to at most one contaminated window, so the left side of (5.2) is `1`, yet every diagonal window fails (5.1).

This is the precise obstruction exposed by Move 20. It is not a counterexample to Tóth's conjecture; it is a counterexample to the inference “uniform fixed-center sparsity implies a usable rotating phase.”

The next needed statement is genuinely phase-diagonal. A source-compatible version would have to control, for an admissibly selected anchor `a` and phase-indexed revealed sets `mathcal R_ell`,

\[
\boxed{
\#\left\{\ell:
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\Phi_{0,t_\ell}(a),\mathcal R_\ell\bigr)
\le r
\right\}=o(m)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

with high **conditional** probability, while guaranteeing that the unused `I_ell` clocks entering (5.3) have not already been inspected.

This is a much sharper remaining obligation than “prove another escape lemma.” It couples the moving phase center to the contamination field and is exactly where the final-cycle anticipativity identified by the `P_3` certificate can re-enter.
