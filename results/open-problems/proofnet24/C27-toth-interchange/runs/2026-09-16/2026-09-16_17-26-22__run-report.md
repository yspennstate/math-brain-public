## 3. Counterexample-first: a proposed total-order upgrade fails exactly on P3

The previous C27 run proved that the coefficient `1/4` is an asymptotic ceiling for the **single fair binary-cut** mechanism. A natural attempted escape is to replace one binary cut by a random total order on blocks and count every permutation arrow that points upward in the order. Roughly half of directed arrows point upward under a random order, so if the resulting label-specific assignment indicators enjoyed the same negative-association/product-mgf inequality as one-color exclusion occupations, one might hope to replace the `1/4` bottleneck by a larger coefficient.

That dependence claim is false even for the actual continuous-time interchange process on the three-vertex path.

Take `P3: 0--1--2`, rate one on both edges, start from the identity, and evaluate at

\[
t=\log 2.
\]

Let a state `p` mean that initial label `i` is at final position `p(i)`. The exact generator has characteristic polynomial

\[
\chi_Q(x)=x(x+1)^2(x+3)^2(x+4).
\]

Since `Q` is real symmetric, it is diagonalizable, and on its spectrum the function `2^x` is represented by

\[
q(x)=\frac{3x^3+32x^2+125x+192}{192}.
\]

Hence

\[
e^{(\log2)Q}=q(Q)
\]

exactly. The identity-row probabilities are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
p&(012)&(021)&(102)&(120)&(201)&(210)\\ \hline
\mathbb P(p)&37/96&7/32&7/32&7/96&7/96&1/32.
\end{array}
\]

Now order the singleton blocks as

\[
1\prec0\prec2.
\]

The two nontrivial upward-arrow indicators include

\[
A=\{p(0)=2\},
\qquad
B=\{p(1)\in\{0,2\}\}.
\]

Exact summation gives

\[
\mathbb P(A)=\frac5{48},\qquad
\mathbb P(B)=\frac7{12},\qquad
\mathbb P(A\cap B)=\frac7{96}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(A\cap B)-\mathbb P(A)\mathbb P(B)=\frac7{576}>0.
}
\]

So these total-order assignment indicators are **positively correlated**, not negatively associated. Equivalently, at `z=2` the naive product-mgf inequality already fails:

\[
\mathbb E 2^{\mathbf1_A+\mathbf1_B}
-
\mathbb E2^{\mathbf1_A}\,\mathbb E2^{\mathbf1_B}
=\frac7{576}>0.
\]

### Interpretation

This does **not** contradict strong Rayleigh for the exclusion process. In `NEW-L53A`, for a fixed binary cut, all counted particles come from one fixed initial color class and are tested against one fixed target class. In the total-order statistic, the admissible target set depends on the initial label, so it is a different multi-species assignment observable.

Thus a direct “replace the binary cut by a total order and reuse the same Chernoff proof” route is exactly refuted before it can be used to claim metric movement.

## 4. Why the Elboim–Sly local-escape proof does not transfer verbatim to d=3

The fresh-annulus idea remains motivated by Elboim–Sly's successful high-dimensional local-escape mechanism, but their quantitative history-avoidance estimate has an explicit dimension barrier.

Their Claim 4.2 assumes a sparse past set satisfying

\[
|A\cap[-r,r]^d|\le r^{8/3}
\]

and combines this with their inductive transition bound

\[
\mathbb P(W(s)=u)\le s^{-d/2+0.1}.
\]

At scale `s~4^ell=2^{2ell}`, their cardinality-times-kernel estimate has exponential scale

\[
2^{8\ell/3}\,2^{-\ell(d-0.2)}
=
2^{-\ell(d-8/3-1/5)}.
\]

For `d=5`, the exponent is

\[
5-\frac83-\frac15=\frac{32}{15}>2,
\]

which leaves room for their subsequent union over roughly `4^ell=2^{2ell}` integer times. This is exactly where their proof says `d>=5` is used.

For `d=3`, the same exponent is only

\[
3-\frac83-\frac15=\frac2{15},
\]

so the subsequent time union would cost `2^{2ell}` and overwhelm the estimate by a factor on the scale `2^{28ell/15}` rather than produce decay.

Therefore Hammond/tree intuition or the Elboim–Sly cardinality-based history-avoidance lemma cannot simply be copied into Z^3. Any viable fresh-annulus route must exploit stronger *conditional freshness/capacity/separation* information than the global cardinality estimate used in Claim 4.2.

This is a route diagnosis, not a theorem about impossibility of all local-escape arguments in `d=3`.
