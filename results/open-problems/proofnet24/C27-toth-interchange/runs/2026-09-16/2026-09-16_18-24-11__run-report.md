## 3. The exact statement that now blocks the route

The retained `NEW-L85` theorem says that for every desired `B>0`, after choosing `K_B`, a deterministic exact `n`-point root-cycle support `A` either pays an arbitrarily high polynomial fixed-support penalty or is connected in the geometric graph of radius

\[
R_{\beta,n}
=K_B\bigl(\sqrt{\beta\log(en)}+\log(en)\bigr).
\]

The disconnected-support bound is powerful for a fixed `A`, but it is not summable over all spatially arbitrary supports. The unresolved branch is therefore the collection of near-diffusively connected supports. A source-level proof would follow from a bound of the following type, combined with the existing explicit short-cycle estimate:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\exists\,\beta_0<\infty,\ c>0\quad
\forall\beta\ge\beta_0\quad
\exists K<\infty:\\[1mm]
&\qquad
\sum_{n>K}
\sum_{\substack{A\subset\mathbb Z^3,\ 0\in A,\ |A|=n\\
A\text{ connected at radius }R_{\beta,n}}}
\mathbf P\bigl(C_{\pi_\beta}(0)=A\bigr)
\le 1-c.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

Here `C_{pi_beta}(0)` denotes the exact finite permutation cycle of the origin. The already certified estimate

\[
\mathbf P(L_\beta(0)\le K)
\lesssim K/\sqrt\beta
\]

(and its explicit finite-torus version with constant `756`) controls the short-cycle sector. What is missing is a **summable primitive-return / closure cost** in the long finite, `R_{beta,n}`-connected sector. No existing C27 theorem supplies it.

This is more precise than saying merely “prove an infinite cycle”: it identifies the remaining branch after the strongest current fixed-support reduction.

## 4. Routes already tried, and the precise reason each fails

### Route 1: improve the single fair binary random cut

**Attempt.** Strengthen `NEW-L53A` by extracting more than `k/4` effective directed crossings from one random two-coloring of the block support.

**Failure.** A directed simple `k`-cycle gives the exact law

\[
\mathbf P_\xi(N=j)=2^{1-k}\binom{k}{2j},
\]

so `N/k` concentrates at `1/4`. For every fixed positive `epsilon`, the probability of obtaining `(1/4+epsilon)k` directed crossings tends to zero. The exact finite checker through `k=64` also found the median threshold `ceil((k-1)/4)`. Thus `theta_block=1/4` is a ceiling for this mechanism; algebraic polishing cannot move the metric.

### Route 2: import negative association from exclusion / strong Rayleigh

**Attempt.** Treat membership of separated sites or blocks in one root permutation cycle as though it inherited negative dependence from fixed-color exclusion occupations.

**Failure.** The exact `P_3` checker shows root-cycle membership events are strictly **positively** correlated for every positive time in the tested pair. These are different observables. Strong Rayleigh for a fixed exclusion color class does not imply the needed root-cycle factorization.

### Route 3: replace the fair cut by a total order and use upward-arrow indicators

**Attempt.** Orient crossings by a random/ordered hierarchy and seek a product mgf or negative-correlation inequality for the resulting label-specific events.

**Failure.** On the actual continuous-time interchange process on `P_3` at `t=log 2`, the previous C27 run exactly found

\mathbf P(B)=\frac7{12},\qquad
\mathbf P(A\cap B)=\frac7{96},

hence covariance `7/576>0`. The natural product-mgf shortcut is false even before any asymptotic argument.

### Route 4: sharpen the first-same-phase-interaction estimate

**Attempt.** Use exact torus heat-kernel and boundary geometry to push the driver coupling farther.

**Progress but failure to close.** This improved the explicit finite-torus constant from `15461` to `756`:

It gives an explicit mesoscopic positive-density statement, but at fixed `beta` it does not make `K` grow with the box. It therefore cannot produce positive density of truly macroscopic cycles or an infinite cycle on `Z^3` by itself.

### Route 5: fixed-support leakage / cross-heat penalties

**Attempt.** Penalize a deterministic exact support by its boundary leakage, fragmentation, or cross-component heat; then use `NEW-L85` to force a near-diffusive spanning skeleton.

**Progress but failure to close.** For any fixed support, the penalties can be extremely small, even arbitrarily high polynomial in `n` after enlarging the separation constant. But the number and spatial freedom of connected supports is enormous, and the fixed-support estimate is not summable in the `R_{beta,n}`-connected branch. A naive spanning-tree encoding has too much entropy. The missing term is not another deterministic leakage bound; it is a **cycle-closure/transitivity probability** along the skeleton.

### Route 6: orbit averaging, self-avoidance, ball occupancy, and capacity

**Attempt.** Use the driver coupling plus point-shift/orbit averaging to show that a hypothetical finite long cycle is spatially thin. Historical `NEW-L90/91` give strong cycle-wide occupancy/Green-energy/capacity information.

**Progress but failure to close.** These statements control geometry of a finite cycle, but do not give repeated conditional independence or a multiplicative probability cost for returning to close the same cycle. The cyclic-time walk reuses the same physical Poisson slab every period, so geometric thinness alone does not turn successive escape attempts into independent trials.

### Route 7: port Elboim–Sly local escape from `d>=5`

**Attempt.** Apply the high-dimensional history-avoidance estimate directly in `d=3`.

**Failure.** In the earlier C27 check, their history-cardinality input `|A cap [-r,r]^d| <= r^(8/3)` combined with the transition estimate of order `s^{-d/2+0.1}` yields, at the relevant multiscale time, an exponent before the time union of

\[

For `d=5` this is `32/15>2`, leaving enough room for the scale-time union. For `d=3` it is only `2/15`, and the same union loses badly. Therefore their *same global history-cardinality mechanism* does not port. What might port is the conceptual local-escape architecture, but only after a substantially sharper three-dimensional freshness/capacity input.

### Route 8: Hammond / Björnberg–Ueltschi tree exploration

**Attempt.** Transfer a restart/escape theorem from infinite regular trees.

**Failure.** The load-bearing graph hypotheses are wrong. Trees have unique geodesics and genuine branching barriers; `Z^3` has many short cycles and repeated routes. Björnberg–Ueltschi also analyze a broader loop-weighted family. Even where `theta=1` reproduces interchange, their theorem is a large-degree regular-tree result, not a boundary-condition-free `Z^3` theorem. Only the qualitative idea of nonanticipative restart is portable.

### Route 9: stationary split-and-merge / Poisson–Dirichlet theory

**Attempt.** Use the proved stationary torus result to infer macroscopic cycles at fixed finite physical time from the identity.

**Failure.** The measures and time regimes differ. Ioffe–Tóth explicitly separate their stationary theorem from Conjecture 1, which starts at the identity. Stationarity is therefore not a source-matching input.

## 5. What a proof must contain that no current route contains

All surviving routes point to the same missing ingredient:

> a **nonanticipative, filtration-respecting fresh-region escape estimate in three dimensions** that can be repeated a positive density of times along a hypothetical long finite cycle, and whose conditional success probability is bounded away from zero despite the reuse of the same period-`beta` Poisson slab.

More concretely, a successful proof must specify an exploration filtration `F_j` that does not reveal the arrows needed for the next escape trial; identify a fresh annular/box region whose remaining arrows are still conditionally independent Poisson processes under `F_j`; prove a uniform lower bound for escaping through that region before a same-phase return/closure; and show that a long `R_{beta,n}`-connected finite cycle necessarily offers linearly or at least summably many such fresh trials. The accumulated failure probability must beat the entropy of possible connected skeletons.

None of the existing leakage, occupancy, heat-kernel, or capacity estimates supplies all four pieces simultaneously. In particular, “the support is thin” is not enough unless thinness is converted into **unrevealed** spatial volume at a stopping time.

## 6. Simplest special case still unproved

The smallest useful missing statement is a **one-fresh-annulus lemma**, not the full multiscale theorem.

Fix a constant `a>0`. Consider a nonanticipative exploration of the cyclic-time walk at a stopping stage with current location `x`. Suppose, by construction of the exploration filtration, that the space-time annulus

\[
\mathcal A_x
=
\{y:a\sqrt\beta\le |y-x|_2\le2a\sqrt\beta\}
\times[0,\beta]
\]

has not yet been revealed, and suppose the previously exposed skeleton does not enter its interior. The still-unproved special case is to find absolute `a,delta>0` and `beta_1<infinity` such that, for every `beta>=beta_1`, the conditional probability—given the exploration filtration—of crossing this fresh annulus outward before a same-phase interaction or return to the inner ball is at least `delta`.

This is intentionally only **one attempt**. It avoids the much harder issue of proving that a long finite cycle creates many fresh attempts. If even this statement fails, the failure should be sought by an exact finite-space reduction/counterexample before inventing a more elaborate exploration.

No assertion of this lemma is made in the present run.

## 7. Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that `Z^3` currently lacks

The nearest solved problem is Elboim–Sly’s `d>=5` theorem, not the tree theorems. It studies the same unweighted interchange law on `Z^d`, the same cyclic-time walk, and the same large-`beta` regime. Its proof has precisely the structure C27 needs: multiscale diffusion plus a local-escape mechanism that prevents complicated history from trapping the cyclic-time walk.

What `d>=5` supplies and the present `d=3` route lacks is **enough transience margin after unioning over the exposed history and over time/scales**. The earlier exponent audit shows that their coarse history-cardinality/heat-kernel combination has this margin in dimension five and loses it in dimension three. A successful `d=3` port therefore cannot merely tune constants; it needs a sharper conditional description of the relevant history, e.g. capacity rather than cardinality, stronger separation/freshness, or an exploration that exposes much less of the past.

Hammond/Björnberg–Ueltschi are farther analogies: their restart ideas benefit from tree branching/unique paths and, in the latter case, a broader loop model. Those hypotheses are absent here.
