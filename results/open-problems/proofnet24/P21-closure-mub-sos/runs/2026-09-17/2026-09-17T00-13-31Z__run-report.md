## 5. Move #8 — discretize: an exact convergent tower

Let

\[
F(x)=\mathbf1_{[0,1/2)}(x)-\mathbf1_{[1/2,1)}(x),
\]

so `epsilon_h=F(h alpha)`. Fix a convergent `p/q=p_k/q_k`, put

\[
a=a_{k+1},\qquad \Delta=q\alpha-p,
\]

and assume `q` is even. Since `(p,q)=1`, `p` is odd.

### Exact even-tower lemma

For every

\[
0\le j\le a-1,\qquad 1\le r\le q,
\]

the length-`q` word

\[
F((jq+1)\alpha),\ldots,F((j+1)q\alpha)
\]

is independent of `j`, and its sum is zero. Equivalently,

\[
D_{jq+r}=D_r
\qquad(0\le j\le a-1,\ 0\le r\le q).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
(jq+r)\alpha
=jp+\frac{rp}{q}+\left(j+\frac rq\right)\Delta.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The continued-fraction estimate and `q_{k+1}=a q+q_{k-1}` give

\[
|\Delta|<\frac1{q_{k+1}}<\frac1{a q}.
\]

Hence throughout the entire `a`-block tower,

\[
\left|\left(j+\frac rq\right)\Delta\right|<\frac1q.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Multiplication by the odd residue `p` permutes the `q`-grid. Because `q` is even, the two discontinuity grid points are exactly obtained at

\[
r=q\quad(0),
\qquad
r=q/2\quad(1/2).
\]

Every other grid point lies at distance at least `1/q` from `{0,1/2}`, so (5.3) says its `F`-value cannot change anywhere in the tower.

At the two boundary residues the signs are opposite. If `s=sgn(Delta)`, then

\[
F((j+1)q\alpha)=s,
\qquad
F((jq+q/2)\alpha)=-s.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus these two boundary values cancel and are themselves independent of `j`. All other letters are fixed by the rational grid. Therefore every block is the same word. The rational `q`-grid has exactly `q/2` points in each half-circle; replacing the boundary pair by the opposite pair in the case `Delta<0` preserves zero total sum. Each repeated block is therefore balanced, proving (5.1). QED.

This is the useful discretization: on an even-denominator tower, the irrational scenery is not merely close to a rational periodic word; it is **exactly a repeated balanced word for all `a_{k+1}` convergent blocks**.

## 6. Move #19 — make it an algorithm: compress all tower discrepancy to one block

\[

\[

\[

\tag{8.1}

For `t in I_k`, put

\[

\[
\frac1{2A|t|}\le H\le\frac1{A|t|},

\[

with an absolute constant `C` (for example `C=85` works from (8.2)). Hence, once `I_k` lies inside the small-frequency range of (4.2),

\[
\int_{I_k}\Re\frac{dt}{1-\Phi_\alpha(t)}

\frac{c}{A}\log\frac{a q}{4}.
\tag{8.4}

\[
\frac1A\log\frac{a q}{4}

Equivalently, by (4.2)--(4.3), the return-epoch walk is recurrent, and every return of that walk is an actual return of the original partially oriented walk to the origin. This proves the theorem. QED.
