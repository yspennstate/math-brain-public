## 3. Frozen formulation and exact meaning of degree four

The frozen metric formulation uses real and imaginary vector coordinates for seven labeled orthonormal bases. Same-basis orthonormality constraints are quadratic. Cross-basis constraints are

\[
q_{k\ell ij}:=
|\langle v_i^{(k)},v_j^{(\ell)}\rangle|^2-\frac16=0
\qquad(k\ne\ell),
\]

which are quartic in the real coordinates.

A degree-four pseudoexpectation is a real linear functional

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}:\mathbb R[x]_{\le4}\to\mathbb R
\]

such that

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[1]=1,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[p^2]\ge0\quad(\deg p\le2).
\]

For an equality `q=0`, the pseudoexpectation must satisfy

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[hq]=0
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
\deg(hq)\le4.
\]

Therefore a same-basis quadratic equality may have a degree-two multiplier, but a cross-basis quartic equality can have only a constant multiplier at degree four. For a Hermitian matrix polynomial of degree at most two, degree-four localizing positivity tests affine-linear vector multipliers.

This degree convention is the central reason the direct vector-coordinate quartic system is weak: degree four only sees the **mean** of a quartic cross-overlap constraint, while a projector lifting makes the same relation quadratic.

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}p
