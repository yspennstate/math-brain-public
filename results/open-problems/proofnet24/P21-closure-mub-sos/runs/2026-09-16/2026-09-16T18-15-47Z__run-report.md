# 5. Move 18: rewrite the successor as one scalar additive functional

The first genuinely new route in this run is to integrate out the geometric horizontal noise *before* attacking the deterministic scenery. Define

\[
Z_n:=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{Y_j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
\phi(t)=E e^{it\xi}=\frac{2}{3-e^{it}}
       =r(t)e^{i\psi(t)},
\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi.
\]

Then

\[
r(t)^2=\frac{2}{5-3\cos t},
\qquad \psi'(0)=\frac12,
\qquad \log r(t)=-\frac38t^2+O(t^4).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Conditional on the *entire* vertical path `Y`, independence of the geometric runs gives the exact factorization

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[e^{itX_n}\mid Y]
&=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi(t\varepsilon_{Y_j})\\
&=r(t)^n\exp\{i\psi(t)Z_n\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is stronger than merely controlling the local-time vector: after the geometric noise is averaged, **all dependence on the deterministic irrational scenery is compressed to the single scalar `Z_n`**.

Fourier inversion therefore gives an exact one-dimensional return kernel

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(X_n=0\mid Y)=K_n(Z_n),
\qquad
K_n(z)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 r(t)^n e^{i\psi(t)z}\,dt.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(X_n=0,Y_n=0)
=E[\mathbf1_{\{Y_n=0\}}K_n(Z_n)].}
\tag{5.5}
\]

There is also an exact positive series representation. If

\[
a=(n+z)/2,\qquad b=(n-z)/2
\]

are positive integers, then the plus-oriented and minus-oriented geometric sums must be equal, hence

\[
\boxed{
K_n(z)=\left(\frac23\right)^n
\sum_{r\ge0}
\binom{r+a-1}{r}\binom{r+b-1}{r}
\left(\frac19\right)^r.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Proof of (5.6): the sum of `a` iid geometric variables with success parameter `2/3` has mass

\[
\Pr(S_a=r)=\binom{r+a-1}{r}(2/3)^a(1/3)^r,
\]

and similarly for `S_b`; `X_n=0` is exactly `S_a=S_b`. Summing the product over the common value `r` gives (5.6). Edge cases `a=0` or `b=0` can be handled directly and are irrelevant to the central bridge regime.

# 6. Move 2: attack the source authors' expected badly-approximable behavior from the opposite side

The canonical quadratic irrational `phi=(sqrt(5)-1)/2` is badly approximable, so the 2008 heuristic points toward transience. I did not assume that heuristic. I first looked for a recurrence mechanism and found an exact bridge identity that any transience proof must overcome.

For every irrational alpha and every nonzero integer `y`,

\[
\{-y\alpha\}=1-\{y\alpha\},
\]

and neither side can equal `0` or `1/2`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_{-y}=-\varepsilon_y\quad(y\ne0),\qquad \varepsilon_0=+1.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
L_n(0)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\mathbf1_{\{Y_j=0\}}.
\]

Condition on the even bridge `Y_{2m}=0`. At every intermediate time, the bridge law is invariant under `Y\mapsto -Y`; by (6.1), all nonzero heights cancel in expectation. Hence

\[
E[Z_{2m}\mid Y_{2m}=0]
=E[L_{2m}(0)\mid Y_{2m}=0].
\tag{6.2}
\]

Put

\[
p_{2j}=\Pr(Y_{2j}=0)=\binom{2j}{j}4^{-j}.
\]

By the Markov property,

\[
E[L_{2m}(0);Y_{2m}=0]
=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}p_{2j}p_{2(m-j)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The generating function is

\[
\sum_{j\ge0}p_{2j}z^j=(1-z)^{-1/2},
\]

so the *full* convolution from `j=0` through `m` equals the coefficient of `z^m` in `(1-z)^{-1}`, namely `1`. Removing the `j=m` endpoint gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}p_{2j}p_{2(m-j)}=1-p_{2m}.
\]

Dividing by `p_{2m}` yields the exact bridge identity

\[
\boxed{
E[Z_{2m}\mid Y_{2m}=0]
=\frac1{p_{2m}}-1
\sim\sqrt{\pi m}-1.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus every irrational alpha, including the golden ratio, has a deterministic positive bridge bias of order `sqrt(n)`, exactly the same scale as the standard deviation of the independent geometric smoothing in (5.3). This does **not** prove recurrence: the fluctuations of `Z_n` around its bridge mean may be much larger. It does show that any transience proof based on saying the square-wave scenery is simply centered along bridges is wrong.

Equivalently, if `g` is the centered square wave on the circle with its Fourier midpoint value `g(0)=0`, then the source scenery differs from the circle representative at the single orbit point `y=0`. Along the vertical walk,

\[
Z_n=\sum_{j<n}g(\{\alpha Y_j\})+L_n(0).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The local-time correction is order `sqrt(n)` on bridges and therefore cannot be silently discarded when importing stationary circle results.

# 7. A rigorous no-go for the naive stationary CLT route

Move 18 also exposes a clean failure mechanism. Consider the stationary circle Markov operator

\[
(Pf)(x)=\frac12 f(x+\alpha)+\frac12f(x-\alpha).
\]

Its Fourier eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_h=\cos(2\pi h\alpha),\qquad h\in\mathbb Z.
\]

For the centered square wave `g`,

\[
\widehat g(h)=\frac{1-(-1)^h}{\pi i h},
\]

so for odd `h`,

\[
|\widehat g(h)|^2=\frac4{\pi^2h^2}.
\]

The standard reversible `H_{-1}` quantity contains

\[
\sum_{h\ne0}\frac{|\widehat g(h)|^2}{1-\lambda_h}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For every irrational alpha this sum diverges. Indeed, let `q_j` be continued-fraction denominators. Infinitely many `q_j` are odd, because consecutive denominators are coprime and hence cannot eventually all be even. For those odd denominators,

\[
\|q_j\alpha\|<1/q_{j+1},
\]

and

\[
1-\cos(2\pi q_j\alpha)
\le2\pi^2\|q_j\alpha\|^2
<\frac{2\pi^2}{q_{j+1}^2}.
\]

Therefore each such Fourier mode contributes at least

\[
\frac{4}{\pi^2q_j^2}\frac{q_{j+1}^2}{2\pi^2}
=\frac{2}{\pi^4}\left(\frac{q_{j+1}}{q_j}\right)^2
\ge\frac{2}{\pi^4}.
\]

There are infinitely many such terms, proving divergence of (7.1).

Verdict: a naive Kipnis--Varadhan / finite-asymptotic-variance stationary CLT is unavailable for **every** irrational alpha in this discontinuous square-wave problem. This is a route no-go, not a recurrence or transience theorem.
