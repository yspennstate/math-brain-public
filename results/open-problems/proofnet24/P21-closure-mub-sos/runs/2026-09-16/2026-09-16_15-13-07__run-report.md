# 3. What “degree four” means here

Work over the real coordinate ring generated by the real and imaginary parts of all vector entries. With 42 vectors in `C^6`, there are 504 real scalar coordinates before gauge reduction.

A degree-four pseudoexpectation is a real linear functional

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}:\mathbb R[x]_{\le4}\to\mathbb R
\]

such that

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[1]=1,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[f^2]\ge0
\quad\text{for every }\deg f\le2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a polynomial equality `q=0`, degree-four feasibility requires

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[h q]=0
\quad\text{whenever }\deg(hq)\le4.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore:

* the same-basis orthonormality equations are quadratic and may be tested against arbitrary quadratic multipliers;
* the cross-basis unbiasedness equations are quartic, so at degree four they can be tested only against constants.

A degree-four equality-system SoS refutation would have

\[
-1=\sum_r f_r^2+\sum_s h_s q_s,
\qquad \deg f_r\le2,
\qquad \deg(h_s q_s)\le4.
\tag{3.3}
\]

A feasible degree-four pseudoexpectation satisfying `(3.1)`–`(3.2)` rules out `(3.3)` immediately by applying `\widetilde{\mathbb E}`.

For the alternative `2 x 2` Hermitian matrix inequality formulation, the degree-four localizing condition additionally asks

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[g(x)^*M(x)g(x)]\ge0
\tag{3.4}
\]

for affine-linear vector polynomials `g`, because `M` has degree at most two. Sarkar proves the Haar functional satisfies those localizing inequalities too. My exact finite checker below is for the frozen **quartic equality** model; the LMI result is cited from the primary paper and its proof was separately inspected.

# 6. Independent proof of the quartic-equality obstruction

The central argument is short enough to re-prove rather than merely trust the literature.

## Theorem `P21-HAAR-DEG4-NOREFUTATION-01`

For every integers `d>=2` and `m>=1`, the direct vector-coordinate MUB quartic equality system has a feasible degree-four pseudoexpectation. Consequently it admits no degree-four SoS refutation, whether or not actual `m` MUBs exist.

### Proof

Let

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_m
\]

be independent Haar-random matrices in `U(d)`, and define

\[
v_i^{(k)}:=U_k e_i.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For every real polynomial `p` of total degree at most four in all real and imaginary vector coordinates, define

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[p]
:=
\mathbb E\,p(v).
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is an actual expectation, so

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[1]=1,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[f^2]\ge0
\]

for every real polynomial `f` of degree at most two.

For each fixed basis `k`, the columns of `U_k` are orthonormal pointwise:

\[
\langle v_i^{(k)},v_j^{(k)}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore every same-basis quadratic equality `q` vanishes pointwise. In particular,

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[h q]=0
\]

for **every** multiplier `h` for which the product is integrable, hence certainly for every `h` allowed by the degree-four truncation.

Now fix `k!=l` and indices `i,j`. The random vectors

\[
x=v_i^{(k)},\qquad y=v_j^{(\ell)}
\]

are independent Haar-random unit vectors in `C^d`. Conditional on `x`, unitary invariance lets us rotate `x` to `e_1`; hence

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[|\langle x,y\rangle|^2\mid x\right]
=
\mathbb E|y_1|^2
=
\frac1d.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The last equality follows from exchangeability of the `d` squared coordinate magnitudes and `\sum_r |y_r|^2=1`. Thus

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}
\left[
|\langle v_i^{(k)},v_j^{(\ell)}\rangle|^2-\frac1d
\right]
=0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Each cross equation in `(6.5)` has real-coordinate degree four. Therefore a degree-four multiplier for it has degree zero: only constants are allowed. Equation `(6.5)` is exactly all that degree-four feasibility requires for every cross equation.

Hence `(6.2)` is a feasible degree-four pseudoexpectation for the full vector-coordinate equality system.

If a degree-four SoS refutation `(3.3)` existed, applying `\widetilde{\mathbb E}` would give

\[
-1
=
\sum_r \widetilde{\mathbb E}[f_r^2]
+
\sum_s \widetilde{\mathbb E}[h_s q_s]
\ge0,
\]

a contradiction. Therefore no such refutation exists. QED.

For the frozen `d=6,m=7` instance, the same single functional satisfies all 756 quartic cross equations simultaneously at degree four, so all 21 blocks survive.

# 7. Exact rational Gram certificate and executed checker

The global pseudoexpectation is positive because it is an actual product-Haar expectation. To meet the finite-certificate requirement with exact arithmetic, I also constructed an explicit rational Gram certificate for the only nontrivial orbit-representative cross-pair moment block.

For one pair of vectors from distinct Haar bases, write `x,y in C^6` as real unit vectors in `R^12`. Set

\[
D=12.
\]

Because distinct bases are independent, `(x,y)` has product uniform measure on

\[
S^{11}\times S^{11}.
\]

Take the monomial basis of all real polynomials of degree at most two in the 24 real coordinates of `(x,y)`. Its size is

\[
1+24+\binom{25}{2}=325.
\]

Let `M` be the `325 x 325` degree-four moment Gram matrix

\[
M_{\alpha\beta}=\mathbb E[m_\alpha(x,y)m_\beta(x,y)].
\]

All entries are rational because the needed sphere moments are

\[
\mathbb E r_a r_b=\frac{\delta_{ab}}D,
\tag{7.1}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E r_a r_b r_c r_e
=
\frac{
\delta_{ab}\delta_{ce}
+
\delta_{ac}\delta_{be}
+
\delta_{ae}\delta_{bc}
}{D(D+2)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

## 7.1 Closed-form weighted-square decomposition

Any real polynomial of degree at most two can be written

\[
f(x,y)=c+a\cdot x+b\cdot y+x^TAx+x^TCy+y^TBy,
\tag{7.3}
\]

with `A,B` symmetric. Let

\[
A_0=A-\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{D}I,
\qquad
B_0=B-\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{D}I,
\]

and

\[
\mu=c+\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{D}+\frac{\operatorname{tr}B}{D}.
\]

Using `(7.1)`–`(7.2)` and independence of `x,y`, all cross terms between distinct irreducible sectors vanish and one obtains the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E f^2
=
\mu^2
+
\frac{\|a\|^2+\|b\|^2}{D}
+
\frac{\|C\|_F^2}{D^2}
+
\frac{2\|A_0\|_F^2+2\|B_0\|_F^2}{D(D+2)}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is an explicit Gram/sum-of-weighted-squares certificate of PSD. In the monomial-coefficient basis used by the checker, `D=12` gives the positive rational weights

\[
\frac1{12},\qquad
\frac1{144},\qquad
\frac1{1008},\qquad
\frac1{168}
\tag{7.5}
\]

for, respectively, linear coefficients, mixed `x_i y_j` coefficients, traceless diagonal-difference squares, and off-diagonal pure-quadratic squares, together with the square `\mu^2`.

The redundant but exact decomposition has 433 weighted-square terms.

## 7.2 Exact unbiasedness moment

The exact rational moment matrix gives

\mathbb E R^2=\frac1{12},

\boxed{\mathbb E|\langle x,y\rangle|^2=\frac16.}
\tag{7.6}

# 8. The alternative LMI encoding and why the equality proof alone is not enough

The source also proposes, for every cross pair, the Hermitian constraint

\[
M(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/\sqrt d & \langle x,y\rangle\\
\langle y,x\rangle & 1/\sqrt d
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Classically, together with an orthonormal target basis, the bound `|<x,y>|<=1/sqrt(d)` forces all `d` overlaps to have squared modulus `1/d` because their squares sum to one.

At degree four, however, `(8.1)` has nontrivial affine-linear localizing multipliers. Merely knowing

\[
\mathbb E|\langle x,y\rangle|^2=1/d
\]

is not by itself enough to verify all of `(3.4)`.

Sarkar therefore proves a separate affine Haar multiplier estimate and establishes Theorem 7.1: the same independent-Haar pseudoexpectation satisfies every required degree-four `2 x 2` matrix localizing condition. Corollary 9.2 then gives the same negative answer for the source’s LMI encoding.

I do not silently substitute the easier equality proof for that matrix-localizing theorem. The frozen metric in this report is explicitly the quartic equality formulation; the LMI result is a separately verified primary-source theorem.

# 9. Projector variables: exact scope boundary

The negative vector-coordinate result must not be overgeneralized.

If

\[
P_i^{(k)}=v_i^{(k)}v_i^{(k)*},
\]

then cross-unbiasedness becomes quadratic in the projector entries:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_i^{(k)}P_j^{(\ell)})=\frac1d.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Degree four in projector variables can therefore test products of these constraints that are degree eight in the original vector coordinates. It is genuinely stronger.

Sarkar’s Theorem 8.1 gives a degree-four projector-coordinate SoS proof of the universal bound

\[
m\le d+1.
\tag{9.2}
\]

At `d=6`, `(9.2)` says only `m<=7`. It refutes eight proposed MUBs but is exactly tight for the unresolved seven-basis case. Therefore:

* vector-coordinate degree four: **cannot** prove nonexistence of seven MUBs in `C^6`;
* projector-coordinate degree four: recovers the elementary `m<=7` bound but, from the cited theorem alone, **does not** prove `m<=6`;
* unrestricted Conjecture 22 (`MUB(6)<7`): remains outside this result and stays with C29.
