## 3. Precise meaning of degree four in the frozen formulation

Write the seven labeled orthonormal bases as vector variables

\[
v_i^{(k)}\in\mathbb C^6,
\qquad
1\le i\le6,\quad 1\le k\le7,
\]

and interpret the system in the real polynomial ring generated by all real and imaginary vector coordinates.

A degree-four pseudoexpectation is a linear functional

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}:\mathbb R[x]_{\le4}\to\mathbb R
\]

with

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[1]=1,
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[p^2]\ge0
\quad(\deg p\le2),
\]

and which annihilates each equality constraint multiplied by every polynomial multiplier whose product still has degree at most four.

This degree bookkeeping is decisive:

- same-basis orthonormality equations are quadratic, so degree-four SoS may multiply them by polynomials of degree at most two;
- cross-basis equations
  \[
  |\langle v_i^{(k)},v_j^{(\ell)}\rangle|^2-\frac16=0
  \]
  are quartic, so at degree four they can only be multiplied by constants;
- for the `2 x 2` Hermitian LMI, whose matrix entries have degree at most two, degree-four localizing positivity tests affine-linear vector multipliers.

Treating a quartic relation as though it could be multiplied by a nonconstant quadratic at degree four would certify a different, stronger hierarchy and is rejected.

### 5.1 Setup

Fix one cross pair

\[
x=U_ke_i,\qquad y=U_\ell e_j,\qquad k\ne\ell,
\]

where the two unitary matrices are independent Haar. Put

\[
z=\langle x,y\rangle=x^*y,
\qquad
c=\frac1{\sqrt d}.
\]

The source LMI is

\[
M(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
c&z\\ \bar z&c\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

At degree four, the localizer must satisfy for all affine-linear complex polynomials `p,q`

\[
\widetilde E\left[
\begin{pmatrix}\bar p&\bar q\end{pmatrix}
M(z)
\begin{pmatrix}p\\q\end{pmatrix}
\right]\ge0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Complexification is harmless: positivity for all complex affine `p,q` is equivalent to, and slightly more convenient than, checking the corresponding real and imaginary affine tests.

Let `H` be the complex Hilbert space obtained by complexifying the real affine coordinate span. Under Haar expectation it has orthonormal basis

\[
1,
\quad
\sqrt d\,U_s(a,b),
\quad
\sqrt d\,\overline{U_s(a,b)},
\]

for all proposed bases `s` and all matrix coordinates `(a,b)`. Orthogonality follows from Haar first and second moments and independent basis factors.

Define the compression of multiplication by `z` to `H` by

\[
\langle f,Tg\rangle_H
=
E[\bar f\,z g].
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then (5.1) is precisely the quadratic form of the block operator

\[
\mathcal L=
\begin{pmatrix}
cI&T\\T^*&cI\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on }H\oplus H.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.2 Exact computation of the multiplication compression

Expand

\[
z=\sum_{r=1}^d\bar x_r y_r.
\]

For a selected coordinate `r`, Haar second moments give

\[
E[y_a\bar y_r]=\frac{\delta_{ar}}d,
\qquad
E[\bar x_a x_r]=\frac{\delta_{ar}}d.
\]

Therefore

\[
T(\sqrt d\,\bar y_r)
=P_H(z\sqrt d\,\bar y_r)
=\frac1d\sqrt d\,\bar x_r,
\tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
T(\sqrt d\,x_r)
=P_H(z\sqrt d\,x_r)
=\frac1d\sqrt d\,y_r.
\tag{5.5}
\]

All other affine basis vectors are killed by `T`. There are three reasons, all exact:

1. `E z=0`, so the constant channel vanishes;
2. independent global/column phase balance kills the wrong conjugations and unmatched selected-basis coordinates;
3. Haar second moments include a Kronecker delta in the column index, so coordinates from nonselected columns have zero projection; coordinates from a third independent basis factor out with zero first moment.

Hence `T` is exactly `1/d` times a partial isometry of rank `2d`:

\[
\boxed{\|T\|_{op}=\frac1d,\qquad \operatorname{rank}T=2d.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is an exact operator statement, not an asymptotic estimate.

### 5.3 Localizer positivity

For arbitrary `p,q in H`, equations (5.2)-(5.6) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[(p,q)^*M(z)(p,q)]
&=c(\|p\|^2+\|q\|^2)+2\Re\langle p,Tq\rangle\\
&\ge c(\|p\|^2+\|q\|^2)-\frac2d\|p\|\|q\|\\
&\ge\left(c-\frac1d\right)(\|p\|^2+\|q\|^2)\\
&\ge0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

because `c=1/sqrt(d)>=1/d` for `d>=1`.

Thus the Haar pseudoexpectation satisfies the degree-four LMI localizer even though a generic pair of independent Haar bases is **not** mutually unbiased pointwise. Requiring the sampled LMI itself to hold pointwise is the wrong criterion for a pseudoexpectation.

## 10. Method comparison and failure modes

### 10.1 Quartic equality route

At degree four the cross relations are maximally weak: only their expectations are constrained. Independent Haar bases match those expectations exactly. This gives a one-line conceptual non-refutation once the hierarchy degree bookkeeping is fixed.

### 10.2 LMI route

The LMI encoding is stronger at the same nominal degree because localizer tests couple the quadratic inner product to affine multipliers. Nevertheless the exact compression calculation above shows the relevant multiplication operator has norm exactly `1/d`, below the diagonal threshold `1/sqrt(d)`. The resulting localizer is PSD.

### 10.3 Projector route

In projector variables the cross-unbiasedness relation is quadratic rather than quartic. Degree four can then test squared deviations, and the centered projector Gram system recovers `m<=d+1`. Thus “degree four” without a specified coordinate formulation is not a well-defined strength comparison.

### 10.4 Rejected routes and mistakes

The following routes are explicitly rejected:

1. **Wrong normalization.** Encoding the source's typographical `1/sqrt(6)` as a squared overlap certifies the wrong feasibility problem.
2. **Pointwise Haar feasibility.** Independent Haar bases are not MUBs and generally do not satisfy the LMI pointwise. SoS pseudoexpectation feasibility only requires the truncated equality/localizer conditions.
3. **Failed numerical search => nonexistence of a proof.** A numerical search failure is not a lower bound on SoS. Here non-refutation follows from an explicit feasible pseudoexpectation.
4. **Overpowered quartic multipliers.** Multiplying a degree-four cross equation by a positive-degree multiplier belongs to degree above four.
5. **Formulation leakage.** The vector-coordinate lower bound does not extend automatically to projector variables or noncommutative lifts.
6. **Unrestricted-MUB leakage.** Feasibility of a pseudoexpectation does not construct seven actual MUBs and does not settle Conjecture 22.
7. **Old P21 kernel work.** That target is consolidated into P04 and is not resumed here.
