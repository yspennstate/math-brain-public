# 4. STUCK REPORT

## (a) Exact blocking statement

The current P21 phase-zero transience route reaches, on even-convergent-denominator scales, only the inherited dispersion lower bound

\[
\phi_+(m q_k) \gtrsim m q_k a_k,
\]

so the corresponding absolute reciprocal contribution is controlled only by a harmonic cost of order

\[
\sum_{1\le m\lesssim a_{k+1}}\frac{1}{m a_k}
\asymp \frac{\log(2+a_{k+1})}{a_k}.
\]

The exact blocker is therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{classify the source-phase walk when infinitely many }q_k\text{ are even and }
\sum_{k:q_k\ \mathrm{even}}
\frac{\log(2+a_{k+1})}{a_k}=\infty.}
\]

The previous sufficient transience theorem cannot close this regime, and failure of that sufficient series is **not** a recurrence theorem.

## (b) Every route tried so far and why it stops

1. **Vertical-skeleton / excursion Fourier reduction.** The walk can be sampled at vertical returns and reduced to an iid return-epoch increment law, with deterministic rotation information entering through an additive functional of the vertical excursion. This is the correct reduction, but by itself it gives no sign-sharp Green estimate for exceptional arithmetic parameters.

2. **Bounded-discrepancy / bounded-type recurrence.** Denjoy--Koksma/Ostrowski control gives logarithmic discrepancy and enough Green mass in bounded type. Later literature checking showed that this mechanism is substantially prior art in Brémont; it does not classify rapidly growing partial quotients.

3. **Reciprocal-series recurrence criteria and a.e.-parameter arguments.** These produced useful sufficient conditions in prior P21 work, but the strong claims were then corrected against Brémont's Theorem 3.2 and older phase results. They are not a route to a new source-exact all-irrational classification.

4. **Direct phase-zero supergrowth transience via odd denominators.** At odd `q_k`, the source phase has a nonzero convergent-block drift channel. This can emulate the key growth mechanism in Brémont's a.e.-phase proof and yields strong dispersion. It fails when infinitely many relevant `q_k` are even.

5. **Break parity by perturbing the phase.** This fails structurally at even `q_k`: the square wave satisfies the half-period antisymmetry, and the two rational-grid boundary contributions pair off rather than creating the needed persistent block drift. Perturbing the phase is also not source-faithful because Bou-Rabee--Peres fix phase zero.

6. **Nested block-profile variance.** The previous run replaced missing block drift by the internal running-profile variance of a zero-sum block, producing the parity-free lower bound `phi_+(m q_k) \gtrsim m q_k a_k` on even scales. This is genuine progress, but it is linear in the number of repeated blocks. Its reciprocal sum incurs `log a_{k+1}/a_k`, which can be non-summable for enormous next digits.

7. **Move #4 this run — prove a weaker case completely.** I tested the cleanest explicit arithmetic sequence sitting beyond both current sufficient regimes (defined below). Brémont's recurrence series is summable for it, while the inherited P21 transience series is not. Thus merely weakening to this explicit one-parameter example does **not** make the existing criteria decide the case.

8. **Move #18 this run — another language: words/towers rather than variances.** At an even convergent denominator, view one `q_k`-block as a nearly repeated rotation-coding word with zero/near-zero net drift but a nontrivial internal profile. The current argument counts the profile energy independently in each repeated block. What it does **not** exploit is the deterministic sequence of tiny phase shifts by `Delta_k=q_k alpha-p_k` from block to block. Across an entire continued-fraction tower there can be rare boundary-crossing defects. The missing question is whether those defects accumulate enough coherent separation to create a superlinear-in-`m` dispersion gain. Current variance bookkeeping loses their signs and correlations.

9. **Move #26 this run — hunt the sporadic case.** The explicit test case below maximizes the obstruction in a transparent way. It is not classified by the present recurrence or transience sufficient conditions. This identifies a concrete next target instead of another general lemma.

## (c) What a proof would have to contain that no route so far contains

A transience proof for the blocker regime needs an **even-scale gain that depends on the next continued-fraction digit**, not just on the current profile size. Quantitatively, any robust improvement such as

\[
\phi_+(m q_k) \gtrsim q_k a_k\, m^{1+\varepsilon}
\]

on a sufficiently long subrange, or another gain strong enough to replace the harmonic `sum 1/m`, would remove the `log a_{k+1}/a_k` obstruction. The present proofs do not contain such a mechanism.

The natural place to look is the exact evolution of block defects as the phase advances by `Delta_k` across the tower. One must retain correlations/signs rather than collapse them immediately to an internal-profile variance.

Alternatively, a recurrence proof would have to leave Brémont's **absolute reciprocal-summability** transience criterion altogether and obtain a lower Green-function estimate that uses cancellation/return structure on the exceptional even scales. No route so far provides that either.

## (d) Simplest special case still unproved by the current P21 machinery

Take the explicit irrational

\[
\alpha_*= [0;a_1,a_2,\ldots],\qquad
 a_1=1,\qquad a_{n+1}=2^{a_n}+1.
\]

The digits begin

\[
1,\ 3,\ 9,\ 513,\ldots
\]

and are all odd. Modulo two, the denominator recurrence

\[
q_{n+1}\equiv a_{n+1}q_n+q_{n-1}\equiv q_n+q_{n-1}\pmod 2
\]

therefore cycles with period three, so even convergent denominators occur infinitely often. Along those even scales,

\[
\frac{\log(2+a_{k+1})}{a_k}\to \log 2,
\]

so the inherited sufficient transience sum definitely fails. On the other hand Brémont's recurrence criterion

\[
\sum_n \frac{\log(1+a_n)}{a_1+\cdots+a_n}=\infty
\]

does not apply: here the denominator is dominated by `a_n` while `log a_n` is of order `a_{n-1}`, making the displayed terms rapidly summable. Brémont Proposition 3.3 also does not apply to the exact source phase: it requires a different digit-parity pattern and is an a.e.-phase theorem.

This is a **diagnostic gap case**, not a claimed new recurrence/transience theorem. The next research run should try to classify this single `alpha_*` before returning to arbitrary irrationals.

## (e) Nearest solved problem and the ingredient it has that this one lacks

The nearest solved problem is Brémont Proposition 3.3: fast continued-fraction growth with `a_1` odd and all later `a_n` even gives transience for Lebesgue-a.e. phase. The proof has two ingredients absent from the exact phase-zero even-denominator obstruction:

1. a nonzero convergent-block cocycle/drift (`|f_{q_k}(x)|>=1` in the relevant odd-denominator setting), and
2. an a.e.-phase Borel--Cantelli event that makes many consecutive `q_k` blocks evolve linearly with that drift.

Those together yield quadratic separation in block index and a much stronger dispersion estimate (the proof obtains an `m^4 q_k^2` scale for the squared quantity used there). At source phase zero and even `q_k`, that drift channel collapses. The P21 internal-profile replacement restores only linear-in-`m` dispersion, leaving the harmonic cost above.

\[
dZ_s=-Z_s\,ds+(\beta Z_s+Y_s)\,dW_s,
\]

with `p>=2` and `beta^2(p-1)<2`. P24 claims the exact operator norm

\[
R_*(p,\beta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2/(p-1)}-\beta}.
\]

Independent check. Put

\[
I=E\int_0^L\|Z_s\|^p ds,\qquad
J=E\int_0^L\|Y_s\|^p ds,\qquad
\delta=1-\frac{\beta^2(p-1)}2>0.
\]

Hilbert-space Itô plus

\[
D^2\|z\|^p[h,h]\le p(p-1)\|z\|^{p-2}\|h\|^2
\]

gives, after dropping the nonnegative terminal term and applying Hölder,

\[
\delta I\le (p-1)\beta I^{(p-1)/p}J^{1/p}
+\frac{p-1}{2}I^{(p-2)/p}J^{2/p}.
\]

For `R=(I/J)^{1/p}` this is

\[
\delta R^2\le (p-1)\beta R+\frac{p-1}{2}.
\]

The positive root is

\[
R\le
\frac{(p-1)\beta+\sqrt{2(p-1)}}{2\delta}.
\]

Writing `q=sqrt(2/(p-1))`, one has `delta=(q^2-beta^2)/q^2`, and exact algebra gives

\[
\frac{(p-1)\beta+\sqrt{2(p-1)}}{2\delta}
=\frac{\beta+q}{q^2-\beta^2}
=\frac1{q-\beta}=R_*.
\]

For sharpness, restrict to one real line, seed a nonzero state during `[0,1]`, then use the adapted feedback

\[
Y=(q-\beta)Z.
\]

The closed multiplicative coefficient is exactly `q`, and its scalar `p`-moment exponent is

\[
-p+\frac{p(p-1)}2q^2=0.
\]

Hence the state `p`-moment stays constant after the seed while the input has asymptotic norm `(q-beta)` times the state norm, so the norm ratio tends to `1/(q-beta)=R_*` on long horizons. This matches the upper bound.
