\[
L(1)=1,\qquad L(f^2)\ge0\quad(\deg f\le2).
\tag{2.2}

If `q=0` is a same-basis quadratic relation, degree four requires

\[
L(hq)=0\qquad \deg(hq)\le4,
\tag{2.3}

Let `R_{<=4}` be the real polynomial space through degree four. Let `Sigma_4` be the cone of sums of squares of real polynomials of degree at most two. Let `I_same,4` be the degree-four truncation of the ideal generated by all same-basis quadratic equations (3.1). Let

\[

\[
\boxed{-1\in \Sigma_4+I_{\rm same,4}+G_4.}

## 6. Move 14 — actual symmetry representation before another checker

### 6.1 The group that genuinely preserves the frozen vector formulation

A compact symmetry group of the exact polynomial system is

\[
\mathcal G
=
U(6)_{\rm global}
\times
\left((\mathbb T^6\rtimes S_6)^7\rtimes S_7\right),
\tag{6.1}
\]

up to the harmless kernel of the action. Here:

- the one global `U(6)` acts simultaneously on every vector;
- `S_7` permutes the seven basis labels;
- each `S_6` permutes vectors inside one basis;
- each phase torus rephases vectors inside one basis.

The real-coordinate same-basis ideal is preserved: phases rotate the real/imaginary orthogonality generators inside their two-dimensional spans. The quartic cross equations are preserved because they use squared moduli.

If a degree-four refutation existed, Reynolds averaging over `G` would preserve degree, the SOS cone, the truncated equality ideal, and the constant `-1`. Thus a `G`-invariant degree-four refutation would also exist.

### 6.2 Why the top-degree cross part has only one invariant channel

The discrete subgroup

\[
H=S_6^7\rtimes S_7
\]

acts transitively on the 756 labels `( {k,l}, i,j )`: `S_7` moves any basis pair to any other pair and the two relevant `S_6` factors move `i,j` independently.

Because the cross relations are already quartic, their degree-four multipliers are constants. Hence the cross-constraint coefficient space is literally the 756-dimensional permutation module on these labels. The `H`-fixed subspace is one-dimensional. Therefore after Reynolds averaging, the entire cross part of a degree-four refutation has the form

\[
c\sum_{k<\ell}\sum_{i,j=1}^{6}g_{k\ell ij}
\tag{6.2}
\]

for a single scalar `c`.

This does **not** prove impossibility by itself: symmetry averaging could in principle leave an SOS plus same-basis ideal term that closes a contradiction. The Haar functional from section 5 is the decisive dual obstruction. It annihilates every individual `g`, and hence annihilates the one invariant channel as well as every symmetry-breaking channel.

### 6.3 The 21 labeled blocks as the edge representation of K7

Before averaging over basis labels, but after collapsing the `6 x 6` index orbit inside each block, the block coefficients form

\[
\mathbb R^{E(K_7)},\qquad |E(K_7)|=21.

Define the unsigned incidence map

\[

\qquad
(Aa)_{\{k,\ell\}}=a_k+a_\ell.
\tag{6.3}

For `K_7`, `A` is injective. Indeed, if `a_k+a_l=0` for all distinct `k,l`, then using any three distinct indices gives `a_l=a_m=-a_k` and also `a_l+a_m=-2a_k=0`, hence all `a_k=0`. Thus `rank A=7`.

\[
V_0=\{a\in\mathbb R^7:\sum_k a_k=0\}.

\[
A\mathbf1_7=2\mathbf1_{21}

\[
\langle Aa,\mathbf1_{21}\rangle
=6\sum_k a_k=0
\qquad(a\in V_0),

\[
\boxed{

=
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_{21}\}

\[

\tag{6.5}

The first channel is the fully `S_7`-invariant block sum. The six-dimensional channel records vertex/basis-label imbalances. \[

\[

So deliberately breaking `S_7` symmetry cannot evade the obstruction.
