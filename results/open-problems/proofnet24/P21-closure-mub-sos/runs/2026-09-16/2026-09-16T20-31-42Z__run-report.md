## 3. Exact embedded process retained from the previous run

For the simple walk on the partially oriented lattice, look only at epochs immediately after successive vertical moves. Let `Y_n` be the vertical coordinate at those epochs and `X_n` the horizontal coordinate. Then

- `Y_n` is a simple symmetric random walk on `Z`;
- independently, the number `xi_n` of horizontal steps made before the next vertical move satisfies

  `P(xi_n=r)=(2/3)(1/3)^r`, `r=0,1,...`;

- hence

  `X_n = sum_{j=0}^{n-1} epsilon_{Y_j} xi_j`.

Its one-step horizontal-run characteristic function is

`phi(t):=E exp(i t xi)=2/(3-exp(i t)) = r(t) exp(i theta(t))`.

Near zero,

`1-r(t) asymp t^2`, `|theta(t)| <= C|t|`, and `1-Re phi(t)=O(t^2)`.

Because `alpha` is irrational,

`epsilon_{-h}=-epsilon_h` for every `h!=0`, while `epsilon_0=1`.

Let `rho_0=0<rho_1<...` be successive positive return times of the vertical skeleton to zero, and set

`S_k := X_{rho_k}`.

By regeneration, `S_k` is a one-dimensional random walk with iid integer-valued increments. Reflecting a negative vertical excursion through zero gives the exact increment representation

`J =_d xi_0 + sigma W`,

where `sigma` is a fair independent sign and `W` is the horizontal contribution of a positive vertical excursion.

Therefore the increment characteristic function is

`Phi(t)=phi(t) q(t)`,

where

`q(t):=E cos(tW)`.

A return `S_k=0` is an actual return of the original walk to `(0,0)`. It is therefore enough to prove recurrence of `S`.

## 4. Positive-excursion Galton--Watson representation

Consider one positive vertical excursion. For `h>=0`, let `N_h` be the number of upcrossings from height `h` to `h+1`, with `N_0=1` for the initial step from `0` to `1`.

Given `N_h`, the descendants contributing to `N_{h+1}` are independent geometric random variables with law

`P(zeta=k)=2^{-(k+1)}`, `k=0,1,...`.

Thus `(N_h)` is a critical Galton--Watson process with

`E zeta=1`, `Var(zeta)=2`,

and offspring pgf `f(s)=1/(2-s)`. Iterating the linear-fractional pgf gives

`P(N_h>0)=1/(h+1)`.

In particular

`E N_h=1`,

and

`E[(N_{h+1}-N_h)^2 | N_h]=2N_h`.

Let `L` be the number of positive-height vertical epochs before returning to zero and let

`A := sum_{positive-height epochs j} epsilon_{Y_j}`

be the signed unweighted vertical-occupation sum during the positive excursion.

The number of visits to height `h>=1` is `N_{h-1}+N_h`. Therefore

`A = epsilon_1 + sum_{h>=1} (epsilon_h+epsilon_{h+1}) N_h`

`  = epsilon_1 + sum_{h>=1} c_h N_h`.

Conditioning on the vertical excursion and multiplying the independent geometric characteristic factors gives the exact identity

`q(t)=E[ r(t)^L cos(theta(t) A) ]`.

The hitting-time pgf from height one to zero is

`F(s):=E[s^L]=(1-sqrt(1-s^2))/s`.

Since `1-r(t) asymp t^2`,

`1-F(r(t)) asymp |t|`.

This square-root singularity is the unavoidable baseline damping of the excursion characteristic function.

## 5. Move #5 — add a parameter: discrepancy energy rather than discrepancy maximum

The previous bounded-type proof used the envelope `|D_h|=O(log h)`. That loses too much information for typical continued fractions, where isolated very large partial quotients are unavoidable. The new parameter is the energy

`V_alpha(H)=sum_{h=1}^H C_h^2`.

Fix `H>=1` and define

`M_H := sum_{h=1}^H C_h (N_h-N_{h+1})`.

Because `C_h` is deterministic and `E[N_{h+1}|N_h]=N_h`, this is a martingale transform with mean zero. Orthogonality of martingale differences and the conditional variance above give exactly

`E M_H^2 = 2 sum_{h=1}^H C_h^2 E N_h = 2 V_alpha(H)`.

On the extinction event

`E_H := {N_{H+1}=0}`,

summation by parts yields

`sum_{h=1}^H c_h N_h`

`= sum_{h=1}^H C_h(N_h-N_{h+1}) + C_H N_{H+1}`

`= M_H`.

Since `P(E_H^c)=P(N_{H+1}>0)=1/(H+2)`, and on `E_H` all higher `N_h` vanish,

`A = epsilon_1+M_H` on `E_H`.

For every real `u`, using `1-cos x <= x^2/2`,

`E[1-cos(uA)]`

`<= 2 P(E_H^c) + (u^2/2) E[(epsilon_1+M_H)^2]`

`= 2/(H+2) + (u^2/2)(1+2V_alpha(H))`.

This estimate is the main new inequality. It depends on the full discrepancy energy, not the worst discrepancy.

### 5.1 From energy to the excursion characteristic function

Because `0<r(t)<=1`,

`1-q(t)`

`= E[1-r(t)^L] + E[r(t)^L (1-cos(theta(t)A))]`.

The first term is `1-F(r(t))`, so for small `t`

`1-q(t) >= c|t|`.

For the upper bound, discard `r(t)^L` in the second term and apply the preceding estimate with `u=theta(t)`, using `|theta(t)|<=C|t|`. After optimizing in `H`,

`1-q(t) <= C Omega_alpha(t)`,

where

`Omega_alpha(t)`

`:= |t| + inf_{H>=1} [ 1/(H+2) + t^2(1+V_alpha(H)) ]`.

For every fixed irrational scenery, the crude bound `|C_h|<=2h+1` gives `V_alpha(H)=O(H^3)`, so choosing e.g. `H=floor(|t|^{-1/2})` shows `Omega_alpha(t)->0`. Hence `q(t)>0` for all sufficiently small nonzero `t`.

### 5.2 Chung--Fuchs lower bound

Write

`B(t):=Re(1-Phi(t)) = 1-q(t) Re phi(t)`

`=(1-q(t)) + q(t)(1-Re phi(t))`.

For small `t`, the bounds above imply

`c|t| <= B(t) <= C Omega_alpha(t)`.

Also

`|Im Phi(t)| = |q(t) Im phi(t)| <= C|t| <= C' B(t)`.

Therefore

`Re 1/(1-Phi(t))`

`= B(t)/( B(t)^2 + (Im Phi(t))^2 )`

`>= c/B(t)`

`>= c/Omega_alpha(t)`.

For `0<rho<1`, Fourier inversion gives the Abel Green function of the return-epoch walk:

`sum_{n>=0} rho^n P(S_n=0)`

`= (1/(2pi)) integral_{-pi}^{pi} Re[1/(1-rho Phi(t))] dt`.

The integrand is nonnegative. If

`integral_0^{t_0} dt/Omega_alpha(t)=infinity`,

Fatou as `rho upward 1` forces the Abel Green functions, hence the ordinary Green series, to diverge. Thus zero is recurrent for `S`, and the original two-dimensional walk returns to `(0,0)` infinitely often.

This proves `P21-I0004-DISCREPANCY-ENERGY-61`.

## 6. Move #17 — recognize the continued-fraction scale: a divergent-series recurrence criterion

Let

`alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,...]`

and let `q_n` be its convergent denominators. Define a slightly padded partial-quotient sum

`A_n := 1 + sum_{j=1}^{n+2} a_j`.

The padding only avoids endpoint/index bookkeeping below and does not affect convergence of the reciprocal series.

### 6.1 Denjoy--Koksma plus Ostrowski expansion

The source sign function on the circle,

`g(x)=1_[0,1/2)(x)-1_[1/2,1)(x)`,

has zero integral and bounded variation. Denjoy--Koksma gives a uniform absolute bound for its ergodic sum over every convergent-denominator block of length `q_j`.

Every integer `h<=q_n+1` has an Ostrowski expansion into at most `a_{j+1}` blocks of length `q_j` at level `j`. Decomposing the first `h` orbit points into those blocks therefore yields

`|D_h| <= C A_n`, `h<=q_n+1`,

with an absolute constant `C`.

Hence for `1<=h<=q_n`,

`|C_h|=|D_h+D_{h+1}-epsilon_1| <= C' A_n`,

and consequently

`V_alpha(q_n) <= C q_n A_n^2`.

### 6.2 Frequency windows

Set

`t_n := 1/(q_n A_n)`.

For every

`t in I_n := [t_n/2,t_n]`,

choose `H=q_n` in the definition of `Omega_alpha`. Then

`|t| <= 1/q_n`,

and

`t^2 V_alpha(q_n) <= C/q_n`.

Therefore

`Omega_alpha(t) <= C/q_n`,

so the energy theorem gives

`Re 1/(1-Phi(t)) >= c q_n`

on `I_n`. The contribution of this interval to the Green integral is at least

`c q_n |I_n| = c/(2A_n)`.

The continued-fraction recurrence gives

`q_{n+2} >= q_{n+1}+q_n >= 2q_n`,

while `A_{n+2}>=A_n`. Thus

`t_{n+2} <= t_n/2`.

So the intervals `I_n` are pairwise disjoint within each parity of `n`, up to harmless endpoints.

If

`sum_n 1/A_n = infinity`,

then at least one parity subseries diverges, and the disjoint corresponding windows force the Chung--Fuchs integral to diverge. Since padding/index shift does not change convergence,

`sum_n 1/(1+sum_{j=1}^n a_j)=infinity`

is sufficient for recurrence.

This proves `P21-I0004-CF-SERIES-RECURRENCE-61`.

### 6.3 Explicit unbounded-type examples

This criterion is strictly larger than bounded type. For example, take the irrational with

`a_n=ceil(log(n+1))`.

Then

`sum_{j<=n} a_j = O(n log n)`,

so

`sum_n 1/(1+sum_{j<=n}a_j)`

dominates a constant multiple of `sum_n 1/(n log n)` and diverges. Thus the exact source-phase walk is recurrent for this explicitly unbounded-type irrational.

The same argument covers, for example,

`a_n = O(log n (log log n)^delta)`

for any fixed `delta<=1`.

## 7. Move #27 — swap the quantifiers: typical alpha instead of every alpha

The source asks for a classification for fixed irrational `alpha`. The preceding sufficient criterion suggests asking first whether it holds for Lebesgue-typical `alpha`.

Let

`S_n:=sum_{j=1}^n a_j`,

`M_n:=max_{1<=j<=n} a_j`.

Work first under the invariant Gauss probability measure

`dmu(x) = dx/((1+x) log 2)`,

which is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on `(0,1)` with density bounded above and below.

The continued-fraction digits are stationary under `mu`, and

`mu(a_1>=m) = log(1+1/m)/log 2 <= C/m`.

Diamond--Vaaler's trimmed-sum theorem says that for `mu`-almost every `alpha`,

`(S_n-M_n)/(n log n) -> 1/log 2`.

Fix such an `alpha`. Thus there is a random finite constant `K(alpha)` and `n_0(alpha)` such that

`S_n-M_n <= K n log n`

for all `n>=n_0`.

### 7.1 Large maxima occur in a zero-density set of dyadic blocks

For integers `k>=2`, define the bad block event

`B_k := { M_{2^{k+1}} > 2^k k }`.

By stationarity, the tail bound, and a union bound,

`mu(B_k)`

`<= 2^{k+1} mu(a_1>2^k k)`

`<= C/k`.

No independence is needed. Let

`N_K:=sum_{k=2}^K 1_{B_k}`.

Then

`E N_K <= C log K`.

Along `K=2^r`, Markov gives, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

`mu(N_{2^r}>epsilon 2^r) <= C r/(epsilon 2^r)`.

The right side is summable in `r`. By Borel--Cantelli,

`N_{2^r}/2^r -> 0`

almost surely. Monotonicity between successive dyadic `K` then implies

`N_K/K ->0`.

Thus the set of good indices `k` for which `B_k` does not occur has natural density one.

### 7.2 Every good dyadic block contributes order `1/k`

For a good `k` and every

`2^k <= n < 2^{k+1}`,

we have

`M_n <= M_{2^{k+1}} <= 2^k k`.

For all sufficiently large `k`, the trimmed theorem also gives

`S_n-M_n <= K n log n <= K' 2^k k`.

Hence

`S_n <= K'' 2^k k`

uniformly across that entire dyadic block. It follows that

`sum_{n=2^k}^{2^{k+1}-1} 1/(1+S_n)`

`>= 2^k/(1+K''2^k k)`

`>= c/k`.

A density-one subset of the positive integers has divergent harmonic subseries: indeed each sufficiently large interval `[N/2,N]` contains a positive fraction of good indices. Therefore

`sum_{k good} 1/k = infinity`.

Summing the preceding dyadic-block lower bounds gives

`sum_{n>=1} 1/(1+S_n)=infinity`

for `mu`-almost every `alpha`, hence for Lebesgue-almost every `alpha`.

The continued-fraction recurrence criterion of Section 6 now applies. This proves

`P21-I0004-AE-RECURRENCE-61`:

> For Lebesgue-almost every irrational `alpha`, the exact phase-zero deterministic irrational-rotation partially oriented walk is recurrent.

## 9. Why the old arithmetic heuristic is not decisive here

Guillotin-Plantard--Le Ny's discussion suggested an arithmetic transition and heuristically associated badly approximable rotations with transience-like behavior. The present phase-zero theorem goes the other way for bounded type and for almost every parameter.

The load-bearing structural point is phase zero:

`epsilon_{-h}=-epsilon_h` for every `h!=0`, while `epsilon_0=1`.

At vertical excursion times this turns all nonzero-height contributions into a symmetric sign `sigma W`; the only unpaired deterministic contribution is the geometric run at height zero. A translated rotation scenery generally loses this exact origin-centered antisymmetry. Thus the theorem should not be silently promoted to arbitrary phase, nor should it be read as a contradiction of results for random environments.

Ralston's discrepancy constructions are particularly informative for the remaining problem: they show that very large sublinear discrepancy profiles occur on dense sets. Those parameters are natural places to seek an exceptional transient example, but a successful route now has to convert that arithmetic behavior into a **lower** estimate on Fourier damping of the positive-excursion occupation functional.
