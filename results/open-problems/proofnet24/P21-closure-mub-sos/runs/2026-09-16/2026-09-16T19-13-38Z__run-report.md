# 5. Full proof

## 5.1 Excursion-time horizontal motion is an iid one-dimensional random walk

Let

\[
\rho_0=0,\qquad
\rho_{r+1}=\inf\{n>\rho_r:Y_n=0\},
\]

and set

\[
S_r=X_{\rho_r}.
\]

Successive vertical excursions are iid, and each uses a disjoint fresh set of geometric horizontal-run variables. Hence the increments

\[
J_r=S_{r+1}-S_r
\]

are iid.

Consider one excursion. Its first horizontal run is taken at height zero, hence contributes an independent `xi_0` with positive sign. The first vertical step is `+1` or `-1` with probability `1/2` each. Conditional on that sign, reflection of the simple-random-walk excursion shows that the absolute excursion shape has the same law in both cases and is independent of the sign. By (3.1), all nonzero scenery signs reverse under reflection. Consequently

\[
\boxed{J\ \stackrel d=\ \xi_0+\sigma W,}                                  \tag{5.1}
\]

where `sigma` is a fair independent sign and

\[
W=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\epsilon_{H_j}\xi_j                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

is the horizontal contribution of a *positive* vertical excursion. Here `H` is simple random walk started at `1` and stopped on first hitting `0`, and `L` is that hitting time (equivalently, the number of interior vertical-skeleton sites carrying geometric runs).

The geometric characteristic function is

\[
\phi(t)=Ee^{it\xi}=\frac{2}{3-e^{it}}=r(t)e^{i\theta(t)}.                 \tag{5.3}
\]

Averaging the fair excursion sign in (5.1) gives the increment characteristic function

\[
\boxed{\Phi(t)=Ee^{itJ}=\phi(t)q(t),\qquad q(t):=E\cos(tW)\in\mathbb R.} \tag{5.4}
\]

Thus recurrence of the original two-dimensional walk will follow from recurrence of this one-dimensional iid excursion-time random walk `S`.

## 5.2 Bounded type gives logarithmic signed-height discrepancy

Define

\[
D_H:=\sum_{h=1}^{H}\epsilon_h.                                            \tag{5.5}
\]

Because the square wave `g` has mean zero and bounded variation, Denjoy--Koksma gives a constant bound for every orbit block whose length is a continued-fraction denominator `q_k`. If all partial quotients satisfy `a_k<=A`, write `H` in its Ostrowski expansion

\[
H=\sum_{k=0}^{K} b_kq_k,\qquad 0\le b_k\le a_{k+1}\le A.
\]

Splitting the first `H` orbit points into these convergent-denominator blocks yields at most `A(K+1)` blocks, each with bounded discrepancy. Since `q_k` grows at least at Fibonacci rate, `K=O(log H)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{|D_H|\le C_\alpha\log(H+2).}                                      \tag{5.6}
\]

The irrational orbit never hits either discontinuity in the nonzero-height sums, so no endpoint ambiguity affects (5.6).

## 5.3 Positive excursions are a critical geometric Galton--Watson tree

For one positive excursion, let `N_h` be the number of upcrossings from height `h` to `h+1`. The first step is `0->1`, so `N_0=1`. At each level, before the final downward departure, each visit generates a geometric number of upward subexcursions. The strong Markov property gives the critical Galton--Watson recursion

\[
N_{h+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_h}\zeta_{h,j},
\qquad
\Pr(\zeta=k)=2^{-(k+1)},\ k\ge0,                                         \tag{5.7}
\]

with

\[
E\zeta=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\zeta)=2.                              \tag{5.8}
\]

Its offspring pgf is

\[
f(s)=\frac1{2-s}.
\]

A direct induction gives

\[
f^{\circ h}(0)=\frac{h}{h+1},
\]

hence the exact survival law

\[
\boxed{\Pr(N_h>0)=\frac1{h+1},\qquad EN_h=1.}                            \tag{5.9}
\]

The local time of the positive excursion at height `h>=1` is

\[
L_h=N_{h-1}+N_h.                                                          \tag{5.10}
\]

Define its *signed visit count*

\[
A:=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\epsilon_{H_j}
 =\sum_{h\ge1}\epsilon_hL_h.                                             \tag{5.11}
\]

Using `N_0=1` and (5.10),

\[
A=\epsilon_1+\sum_{h\ge1}c_hN_h,
\qquad c_h:=\epsilon_h+\epsilon_{h+1}.                                   \tag{5.12}
\]

Let

\[
C_h:=\sum_{k=1}^{h}c_k=D_h+D_{h+1}-\epsilon_1.                            \tag{5.13}
\]

By (5.6),

\[
|C_h|\le C\log(h+2).                                                      \tag{5.14}
\]

## 5.4 A logarithmic-over-linear tail for the excursion signed count

Let `F_h=sigma(N_0,...,N_h)`. From (5.7),

\[
E[N_{h+1}-N_h\mid F_h]=0,
\qquad
E[(N_{h+1}-N_h)^2\mid F_h]=2N_h.                                         \tag{5.15}
\]

Set

\[
M_H:=\sum_{h=1}^{H} C_h(N_h-N_{h+1}).                                    \tag{5.16}
\]

This is a martingale transform (up to the harmless overall sign), and orthogonality of its differences plus `EN_h=1` gives

\[
EM_H^2=2\sum_{h=1}^{H}C_h^2
\le C H\log^2(H+2).                                                       \tag{5.17}
\]

On the event `N_{H+1}=0`, extinction has already occurred, all later `N_h` vanish, and summation by parts in (5.12) gives exactly

\[
A-\epsilon_1=M_H.                                                         \tag{5.18}
\]

For `x>=3`, choose

\[
H=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\log(e+x)}\right\rfloor\vee1.
\]

Using (5.9), (5.17), (5.18), and Chebyshev,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(|A|>x)
&\le \Pr(N_{H+1}>0)+\Pr(|M_H|>x-1)\\
&\le \frac1{H+2}+\frac{C H\log^2(H+2)}{(x-1)^2}\\
&\le C\frac{\log(e+x)}{x}.
\end{aligned}                                                            \tag{5.19}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\Pr(|A|>x)\le C\,\frac{\log(e+x)}{x}.}                            \tag{5.20}
\]

This is the point where bounded-type arithmetic enters the probabilistic excursion law.

## 5.5 Small-frequency behavior of the excursion characteristic factor

Conditional on the positive vertical excursion path, independence of the geometric runs and (5.3) give

\[
E[e^{itW}\mid H]
=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\phi(\epsilon_{H_j}t)
=r(t)^L e^{i\theta(t)A}.                                                  \tag{5.21}
\]

Hence

\[
q(t)=E[r(t)^L\cos(\theta(t)A)].                                          \tag{5.22}
\]

The first-hitting-time pgf for simple random walk started at `1` and killed at `0` is

\[
F(s):=E_1s^L=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-s^2}}{s},\qquad 0<s\le1.                    \tag{5.23}
\]

Indeed the bounded solution of

\[
u_k=\frac{s}{2}(u_{k-1}+u_{k+1}),\qquad u_0=1,
\]

has `u_1=F(s)`.

For the geometric law,

\[
r(t)^2=\frac{2}{5-3\cos t}.                                              \tag{5.24}
\]

Therefore, as `t->0`,

\[
1-r(t)\asymp t^2,
\qquad |\theta(t)|\le C|t|,
\qquad 1-F(r(t))\asymp |t|.                                              \tag{5.25}
\]

Write

\[
a(t):=1-q(t).
\]

Since `cos<=1`, (5.22) gives the lower bound

\[
a(t)\ge 1-Er(t)^L=1-F(r(t))\ge c|t|.                                   \tag{5.26}
\]

For the upper bound,

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(t)
&=E[1-r^L\cos(\theta A)]\\
&\le 1-F(r)+E[1-\cos(\theta A)]\\
&\le C|t|+C E\min(1,t^2A^2).
\end{aligned}                                                            \tag{5.27}
\]

Let `R=1/|t|`. From (5.20),

\[
E[A^2;|A|\le R]
\le E\min(A^2,R^2)
\le 2\int_0^R x\Pr(|A|>x)\,dx
\le C R\log(e+R),                                                        \tag{5.28}
\]

and

\[
\Pr(|A|>R)\le C\frac{\log(e+R)}{R}.                                     \tag{5.29}
\]

Consequently

\[
E\min(1,t^2A^2)
\le C|t|\log(e/|t|).                                                      \tag{5.30}
\]

Combining (5.26)--(5.30), for all sufficiently small nonzero `t`,

\[
\boxed{c|t|\le a(t)\le C|t|\log(e/|t|).}                                \tag{5.31}
\]

## 5.6 Chung--Fuchs/Abelian Fourier divergence

For the same geometric characteristic function,

\[
1-\Re\phi(t)=O(t^2),\qquad |\Im\phi(t)|\le C|t|.                         \tag{5.32}
\]

For small `t`, (5.31) implies `q(t)=1-a(t)>1/2`. Define

\[
B(t):=1-q(t)\Re\phi(t)
=a(t)+q(t)(1-\Re\phi(t)).                                                 \tag{5.33}
\]

Since `1-Re phi(t)>=0`, (5.31)--(5.33) imply

\[
c|t|\le B(t)\le C|t|\log(e/|t|),
\qquad |q(t)\Im\phi(t)|\le C|t|\le C'B(t).                              \tag{5.34}
\]

Because `Phi=q phi`,

\[
\Re\frac1{1-\Phi(t)}
=\frac{B(t)}{B(t)^2+q(t)^2(\Im\phi(t))^2}
\ge \frac{c}{B(t)}
\ge \frac{c'}{|t|\log(e/|t|)}.                                          \tag{5.35}
\]

The right-hand side is not integrable at zero:

\[
\int_0^{t_0}\frac{dt}{t\log(e/t)}=\infty.                               \tag{5.36}
\]

For `0<rho<1`, Fourier inversion for the integer-valued iid random walk `S` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_\rho
&:=\sum_{n\ge0}\rho^n\Pr(S_n=0)\\
&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
\Re\frac1{1-\rho\Phi(t)}\,dt.                                           \tag{5.37}
\end{aligned}
\]

The integrand is nonnegative because

\[
\Re\frac1{1-\rho\Phi}
=\frac{1-\rho\Re\Phi}{|1-\rho\Phi|^2}>0.
\]

Fatou's lemma and (5.35)--(5.36) imply

\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}G_\rho=\infty.
\]

Since the coefficients in `G_rho` are nonnegative,

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\Pr(S_n=0)=\infty.                                          \tag{5.38}
\]

For an iid one-dimensional random walk, divergence of the Green function at the origin is equivalent, via the standard first-return renewal identity, to return probability one. Thus `S` returns to zero infinitely often almost surely.

Whenever `S_r=0`, we also have `Y_{rho_r}=0`, and at physical time `T_{rho_r}` the original partially oriented walk is exactly at `(0,0)`. Therefore the original source walk is recurrent. This proves the theorem.

# 7. The three new-method moves and what each taught us

## Move 6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: direct rational-approximant coupling fails

For a convergent `p/q` to a bounded-type irrational, `|alpha-p/q|=O(q^{-2})`. On heights `|y|<=c q`, the rotation phases shift by only `O(1/q)`, so the irrational and rational orientation sequences differ at only `O(c)` exceptional height levels. This looked promising because the rational `1/(2q)` strip model is recurrent.

But at vertical time of order `q^2`, each exceptional level can collect local time of order `q`. Its horizontal contribution is therefore order `q`, exactly the diffusive/return scale one needs to control. A naive pathwise coupling does **not** give a perturbatively small error through the denominator time scale. This route is rejected as a direct proof, although rational approximants may still organize the unbounded-type regime later.

## Move 12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED/RESET: success

The useful object is not conserved mass but **regeneration**. Every return of the vertical skeleton to zero resets the excursion shape and exposes fresh independent geometric horizontal-run variables. The horizontal displacement at those epochs is therefore an iid random walk `S_r`. This removes the long-range local-time dependence that obstructed the fixed-time route.

## Move 15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE: success

Condition on the sign of each vertical excursion. Positive and negative excursions have the same absolute shape, but the source phase-zero rotation obeys exact antisymmetry `epsilon_{-h}=-epsilon_h`. The interior horizontal excursion contribution therefore becomes `+W` or `-W` with a fair sign, while only the height-zero geometric run remains unsymmetrized. This is exactly the decomposition `J=xi_0+sigma W` that makes the one-dimensional Fourier argument work.

The old Guillotin-Plantard--Le Ny discussion heuristically associated badly approximable/type-near-one rotations with transience. The theorem above goes the other way for the **specific phase-zero source environment**. This should not be oversold as a contradiction to a theorem: their statement was a heuristic in a broader dynamical-system/phase setting, whereas phase zero has the special exact antisymmetry (3.1), which is the mechanism exploited here.
