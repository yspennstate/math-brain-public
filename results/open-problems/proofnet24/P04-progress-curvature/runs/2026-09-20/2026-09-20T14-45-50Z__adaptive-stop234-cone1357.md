## 4. New mechanism: stop only at a fresh first-hit column

The preceding width-seven, length-four slab always ran to its right boundary. The new observation is that a block may be ended **adaptively at the first hit of an intermediate new column**, while still preserving the fresh-environment regeneration needed for exact concatenation.

Use the same local graph

`V={0,1,2,3,4} x {-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3}`.

Allowed physical edges are:

1. every nearest-neighbor horizontal edge between columns `x` and `x+1`, `x=0,1,2,3`, traversable in either direction;
2. every vertical nearest-neighbor edge in departure columns `x=0,1,2,3`;
3. no terminal-column vertical edge at `x=4`.

Start at `(0,0)`. The infection/FPP exploration is ordinary continuous-time Richardson growth on this finite graph.

The policy may stop at the first hit of column 1, 2, or 3, or must stop at the first hit of column 4. Crucially, a decision is made **at the first instant a new column is hit**. At that instant exactly one vertex in the new column is infected, and no edge whose two relevant departure endpoints lie at or to the east of that new column has been used. If the policy stops there, those east/vertical edges are therefore untouched iid `Exp(1)` edges for the next translated cycle.

The executed certificate below in fact proves that the chosen policy never stops at column 1, so its horizontal advance is always

`D in {2,3,4}`.

Let `C` be its passage cost and `xi` its terminal vertical displacement.

## 5. Dinkelbach-type transformed Bellman certificate

Set

`a := 16139/31250 = 0.516448`.

Instead of separately computing `E C` and `E D`, optimize the transformed cycle cost

`C + a(4-D)`.

If an admissible policy satisfies

`E[C+a(4-D)] < 4a`,

then immediately

`E C < a E D`.                                                   (5.1)

This gives an average cost per horizontal renewal advance below `a` without needing a deterministic block length.

For an infected set `S`, write `b(S)` for the number of active boundary physical edges and `m_S(v)` for the number of such edges leading to an uninfected vertex `v`. Let `V_k(S)` be the transformed future cost while waiting for the first hit of column `k`, where `k=1,2,3,4`.

Before column `k` is hit, exponential memorylessness gives

`V_k(S) = 1/b(S) + sum_v [m_S(v)/b(S)] V_k(S union {v}).`          (5.2)

At the first hit of column `k<4`, there are two admissible actions:

- stop now, paying transformed terminal penalty `a(4-k)`;
- continue, with future value `V_{k+1}(S)`.

Thus the exact Bellman relation is

`V_k(S) = min{a(4-k), V_{k+1}(S)}`                                (5.3)

at such first-hit states, and `V_4(S)=0` at the first hit of column 4.

All nonterminal transitions strictly enlarge the infected set. Hence (5.2)-(5.3) form a finite acyclic recursion when ordered by infected-set size and stage.

### Rigorous fixed-point upward implementation

Take `Q=10^15`, for which `aQ=516448000000000` is an integer. Suppose a child integer `U_child` satisfies

`actual child policy cost <= U_child/Q`.

At a waiting state define

`U(S,k) = ceil( [Q + sum_v m_S(v) U(S union {v},k)] / b(S) ).`    (5.4)

At a first-hit state use

`U(S,k)=min{(4-k)aQ, U(S,k+1)}`                                  (5.5)

for `k<4`, and zero at `k=4`.

The positive recurrence and monotonicity of `min` imply by backward induction that `U/Q` upper-bounds the transformed cost of the actual recursively selected policy. If the stop branch is selected it is exact; if continuation is selected, induction supplies an actual continuation policy whose expected cost is bounded by the stored integer. Thus this is not merely a lower value-function approximation: it certifies a realizable stopping policy.

In parallel, using the **same selected branch**, propagate an upper bound on terminal reward `xi^2`: at a stop state the exact reward is the squared terminal row, while waiting states use the convex Richardson recurrence without the holding-time term. Therefore the second integer recursion certifies `E xi^2` for that same policy.

### Proof

Run the certified finite policy in translated slabs. Cycle `j` begins at a random point `(X_{j-1},S_{j-1})`. Its local first-hit stopping rule produces cost `C_j`, horizontal advance `D_j in {2,3,4}`, and vertical displacement `xi_j`; set

`X_j=X_{j-1}+D_j`, `S_j=S_{j-1}+xi_j`.

Because a cycle stops exactly at the first hit of its terminal column, its explored physical edges all have departure x-coordinate strictly smaller than that terminal column. In particular, the terminal-column vertical edges and all east-going edges from that column have not been inspected. The next translated slab uses only those terminal/east departure columns and later columns, never the west crossing edges just used by the previous cycle. Therefore different cycles use disjoint sets of revealed physical-edge variables.

Conditional on the entire past, the next translated environment is fresh iid `Exp(1)`. Vertical translation invariance gives the same law at every random height. Hence `(C_j,D_j,xi_j)` are iid copies of `(C,D,xi)`.

Reflection in the horizontal center line preserves the local graph, the transformed Bellman recursion, its integer rounding, and the stop/continue tie rule. Thus the certified policy is reflection-equivariant. Consequently

`(D,xi) =_law (D,-xi)`,

so in particular

`E[xi | D]=0`, `E xi^2 <= vbar`.                                  (7.3)

Fix `n>=4` and define the bounded renewal stopping index

`K_n := min{k>=1 : X_k >= n-3}`.

Since every `D_j>=2`,

`K_n <= ceil((n-3)/2)`.                                           (7.4)

Since the previous renewal position was `<n-3` and the last advance is at most four,

`X_{K_n} <= n`.                                                    (7.5)

The index `K_n` is a bounded stopping time for the iid cycle sequence. Wald's identity applied to the integrable pair `(C,D)` and (6.3) gives

`E sum_{j<=K_n} C_j`

` = E K_n E C`

` <= a E K_n E D`

` = a E X_{K_n}`

` <= a n`.                                                        (7.6)

For the transverse coordinate, cross terms vanish despite the random stopping index. Indeed `{K_n>=j}` is determined by `D_1,...,D_{j-1}`, while (7.3) gives zero conditional mean for each `xi_j` given its own `D_j`; conditioning on the entire `D` sequence kills every mixed term. The diagonal terms give

`E S_{K_n}^2 = E K_n E xi^2`

` <= vbar ceil((n-3)/2)`.                                        (7.7)

Therefore

`E |S_{K_n}| <= sqrt(vbar ceil((n-3)/2))`.                        (7.8)

At the final renewal point `(X_{K_n},S_{K_n})`, the vertical physical edges in column `X_{K_n}` are fresh because the final cycle stopped at its first hit there. Correct vertically to `(X_{K_n},0)` using that column. Conditional expected repair cost is exactly `|S_{K_n}|`. Then move east along row zero to `(n,0)`; by (7.5) this uses at most three fresh horizontal edges and has conditional expected cost at most three. Adding (7.6)-(7.8) proves (7.1).

Divide by `n` and let `n->infinity`; standard `L^1` time-constant convergence yields (7.2). QED.
