## Main result: a proof of the remaining four-type uniqueness case

**Theorem.** Every strictly non-Volterra quadratic stochastic operator on the four-type simplex
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in[0,\infty)^4:\sum_i x_i=1\}
\]
has exactly one fixed point.

The assumptions are
\[
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,\quad
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad
\sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\quad
P_{ij,k}=0\ \text{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\]
No positivity of allowed coefficients, interior-fixed-point assumption, genericity,
nondegeneracy, or invariant-face hypothesis is required.

### Lemma 1: fixed-point coordinate bound

Put \(c=(3-\sqrt5)/2<1/2\). Every fixed point \(x\) satisfies \(x_k\le c\) for every \(k\).

Indeed \(0\le P_{ij,k}\le1\), and the strictly non-Volterra condition eliminates every term
having \(i=k\) or \(j=k\). Consequently
\[
x_k=V_k(x)\le\sum_{i,j\ne k}x_ix_j=(1-x_k)^2.
\]
Thus \(x_k^2-3x_k+1\ge0\). The larger root \((3+\sqrt5)/2\) is bigger than 1,
so \(0\le x_k\le1\) forces \(x_k\le c\).

### Lemma 2: sign pattern of the difference of two fixed points

If \(x\ne y\) are fixed points, \(z=x-y\) has at least two strictly positive and at least two
strictly negative coordinates.

The sums of the coordinates agree, so there is at least one of each sign.
Suppose \(z_i>0\) is the only positive coordinate. For every \(j\ne i\), \(x_j\le y_j\).
The polynomial \(V_i\) has nonnegative coefficients and does not involve its own coordinate
\(x_i\). Therefore \(V_i(x)\le V_i(y)\), contrary to \(x_i>y_i\).
Interchanging \(x,y\) rules out exactly one negative coordinate.

For four types this means exactly two coordinates of each sign and no zero coordinate.
In particular this argument already excludes two distinct fixed points whose difference
has any zero coordinate, including all possible problematic boundary configurations.

### Lemma 3: the two-versus-two transfer bound

Fix any partition \(G,H\) of \(\{1,2,3,4\}\) into two pairs, and any \(m\in\Delta_3\).
Set
\[
A(m)_{rj}=\sum_{a=1}^4P_{aj,r}m_a,\qquad
p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}\in[0,1].
\]
Let \(i\in G,j\in H\), and let \(k\) and \(\ell\) be the other members of \(G\) and \(H\),
respectively. Then
\[
\mathbf1_G^\mathsf T A(m)(e_i-e_j)\le \max(m_i,m_j).
\]

**Proof.** The cross pair inside \(G\), namely \(\{i,k\}\), cannot produce either member of
\(G\), so \(p_{ki}=0\). The cross pair inside \(H\), namely \(\{j,\ell\}\), must produce a
member of \(G\), so \(p_{\ell j}=1\). Also \(p_{ij}=p_{ji}\) by parent symmetry. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^\mathsf TA(m)(e_i-e_j)
 &=\sum_a m_a(p_{ai}-p_{aj})\\
 &=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
       -m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
 &\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}\\
 &\le \max(m_i,m_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
All omitted terms have the indicated nonpositive signs. This lemma holds even when
some coordinates of \(m\) or some allowed coefficients are zero. \(\square\)

### Proof of the theorem

Existence follows from Brouwer's fixed-point theorem: \(V\) is continuous and maps the
nonempty compact convex simplex to itself, since its coordinates are nonnegative and
\(\sum_kV_k(x)=(\sum_i x_i)^2=1\).

For uniqueness suppose \(x\ne y\) are fixed. Let \(z=x-y\), \(m=(x+y)/2\), and let \(G,H\)
be the positive and negative coordinate sets of \(z\). By Lemma 2 these are pairs.
Put
\[
h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=-\sum_{j\in H}z_j>0,\qquad
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}\quad(i\in G,j\in H).
\]
Then \(\theta_{ij}\ge0\), \(\sum_{ij}\theta_{ij}=1\), and
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\]
Parent symmetry and the quadratic form give the exact polarization identity
\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A(m)(x-y)=2A(m)z.
\]
For completeness,
\(x_ax_b-y_ay_b=m_a z_b+z_a m_b\), and the two sums become equal by
\(P_{ab,r}=P_{ba,r}\).

Every coordinate of \(m\) is at most \(c\) by Lemma 1. Therefore Lemma 3 yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
h
 &=\mathbf1_G^\mathsf T(x-y)
  =\mathbf1_G^\mathsf T(V(x)-V(y))\\
 &=2h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}
       \mathbf1_G^\mathsf TA(m)(e_i-e_j)\\
 &\le 2ch=(3-\sqrt5)h<h,
\end{aligned}
\]
a contradiction. This proves uniqueness, including boundary fixed points. \(\square\)

### Immediate consequence for the archived determinant question

At any four-type fixed point \(x\), let \(J=DV(x)=2A(x)\), restricted to
\(T=\{z:\sum_i z_i=0\}\). Every positive real eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(J|_T\)
satisfies
\[
\lambda\le2\max_i x_i\le3-\sqrt5<1.
\]
To prove it, take a real eigenvector. Since \(J\) has nonnegative entries and zero diagonal,
an eigenvector with just one positive coordinate cannot have positive eigenvalue;
the same holds with just one negative coordinate. Thus its sign pattern is two-versus-two.
Apply the transfer decomposition above to \(z\) and
\(\mathbf1_G^\mathsf T Jz=\lambda h\).

Consequently \(\det(I-sJ|_T)\ne0\) for \(0\le s\le1\): a zero would give a positive real
eigenvalue \(1/s\ge1\). Its value at \(s=0\) is 1, so by continuity
\[
\det(I-J|_T)>0.
\]
As in the archived manuscript, \(A(x)\) is column-stochastic with zero diagonal, and
\[
\det(I-J|_T)=3+4e_3(A(x))-12\det A(x)>0.
\]
Thus its Question \(\texttt{q:det}\) also has a positive answer. This corollary is not needed
for the elementary uniqueness proof.

### Scope and limitations

The proof settles the four-type uniqueness question, provided the derivation survives
independent audit. It has not yet been formalized in Lean, and no human referee has
reviewed it. It is independent of the archived finite searches and index-count argument.

It does not prove convergence of iterates, attractiveness of the fixed point, or any
higher-dimensional cycle classification. Uniqueness is already false for five types by
the archived explicit rational example. The transfer lemma uses the fact that both
parts of the sign partition have exactly two members; this is exactly what fails at
five types.

A claim of worldwide novelty is separate from the proof. Literature verification and
companion-repository status are recorded below.

## What the companion repositories had already established

The QSO manuscript and its earlier E0064 report were inspected at the canonical snapshot,
including the archived referee reports. The repository already contains the following
substantive results; none should be claimed as a new result of this run.

1. For five types, with \(F=\{1,2,3\}\), the rational operator
   \[
   W_k(x)=\tfrac12(x_a^2+x_b^2)+2x_ax_b+\tfrac23x_4x_5
   \quad(\{a,b,k\}=F),
   \]
   \[
   W_4=x_5(2x_1+2x_2+2x_3+x_5),\qquad
   W_5=x_4(2x_1+2x_2+2x_3+x_4)
   \]
   fixes both \((1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0)\) and
   \((1/9,1/9,1/9,1/3,1/3)\). Direct substitution verifies these identities.
   The stronger archived swap-extension theorem gives exactly two fixed points for
   every three-type operator used on \(F\) and every probability vector used as the
   offspring distribution of the pair \(\{4,5\}\). Its proof forces
   \(x_4=x_5\in\{0,1/3\}\), with the second case reduced to a \(2/3\)-contraction.
2. On six types, two disjoint invariant triples supply distinct fixed points; the
   symmetric cross rules yield the exact segment
   \((a,a,a,1/3-a,1/3-a,1/3-a)\), \(0\le a\le1/3\).
3. The equiprobable operator has tangent Jacobian eigenvalue \(-2/(m-1)\) at its
   barycenter. For \(m\ge4\) it is attracting; the archived manuscript attributes
   global convergence to Jamilov–Ladra–Mukhitdinov, QTDS 16 (2017), 645–655.
4. General four-type uniqueness, and the determinant inequality answered above,
   were explicitly left open in both the companion README and manuscript. Random
   searches and all 5,184 deterministic coefficient vertices were only evidence.
5. The archived early five-type report incorrectly called the boundary point's
   simplex fixed-point index \(-1\). The final manuscript already corrects this to
   0, distinguishing the affine-hull Jacobian determinant from the simplex index.
   This is an existing correction, not a new one found here.

The new proof therefore completes the **dimension threshold for uniqueness**:
uniqueness holds for every strictly non-Volterra QSO on three or four types, and
fails in general from five types onward. (For three types Lemma 2 itself proves
uniqueness by pigeonhole, and Brouwer gives existence.) The broad 2009 question
about extending Theorem 6 was already negatively answered by failure of its
attractiveness clause; the genuinely remaining subquestion was uniqueness at four
types. The cycle-classification clause remains outside the present proof.

## Free Brownian sharp constant: already solved internally, with a checked core

The free Brownian companion claims the following answer to Hasebe–Ueda Problem 3.3:
for support diameter \(D\), \(\mu\boxplus S(0,t)\) is unimodal for every \(t\ge D^2\),
with sharp universal constant 1 and the symmetric endpoint pair as unique extremal.
I re-derived its elementary moment proof, which is short and mathematically coherent.
It should not be selected as a still-unsolved problem in this session.

Here is the checked derivation. At a stationary point, translated to 0, of
\(\xi_R(u)=\int((x-u)^2+R^2)^{-1}\,d\mu(x)\), put \(y=R^2>0\) and reweight by
\((x^2+y)^{-2}\), obtaining a probability law \(Q\). Stationarity gives
\(E_QX=0\). Write \(a=E_QX^2,b=E_QX^4\). If the diameter is at most \(D\), then
\[
4a\le D^2,\qquad b+3a^2\le D^2a.
\]
The second inequality follows directly from
\(E(X-X')^4\le D^2E(X-X')^2\) for independent copies.
Nonnegative second derivative of the profile implies
\[
0\le3-4yE_Q[(X^2+y)^{-1}]\le3-\frac{4y}{a+y},
\]
so \(y\le3a\). Normalization gives
\[
\xi_R(0)^{-1}=\frac{b+2ay+y^2}{a+y}.
\]
It follows that
\[
\xi_R(0)^{-1}-D^2
\le\frac{(y-3a)(y+a)+(4a-D^2)y}{a+y}\le0.
\]
Thus a local minimum has height at least \(D^{-2}\). Equality forces a symmetric
endpoint pair and \(y=3D^2/4\); for this pair the stationary point is a strict local
maximum of order four, so it cannot be the local minimum. Three intersections of a
positive profile with the level \(1/t\) would force such a minimum. Hasebe–Ueda's
Lemma 3.1, itself based on Biane's density parametrization, therefore implies
unimodality for \(t\ge D^2\).

The symmetric pair \(\tfrac12(\delta_{-c}+\delta_c)\) has \(D=2c\) and fails
unimodality for \(c^2<t<4c^2\), since its profile parameter satisfies
\[
v_t''(0)=\frac{4c^2-t}{t\sqrt{t-c^2}}>0.
\]
This already proves sharpness of the universal constant. The original source's
Example 2.1 confirms non-unimodality for all earlier positive times as well.

The primary arXiv PDF really prints the bracket \(2\le C\le4\) after noting
\(D=2\) and the pair threshold \(t=4\). Its own normalization gives only \(C\ge1\)
from that example. The discrepancy is a source arithmetic/normalization error,
not a reason to reject the companion's \(C=1\) derivation.

The companion's Lean claim is explicitly limited to the polynomial core. Biane's
analytic density theorem, the extreme-point/measure-theoretic assembly, and the
full unimodality theorem are not formalized there. I did not rerun Lean.

Two ancillary cautions from inspection, neither affecting the displayed moment proof:

- In its general symmetric-kernel section, the manuscript defines
  \(\psi=-p''/p'\) on \((0,\infty)\), where \(p'<0\). Therefore
  \(p''=\psi|p'|\), and the stationary two-atom mixture has
  \(f''(0)=\theta(\psi(a)+\psi(b))\), **with a plus sign**. The proof prints a
  minus sign in that identity and then reasons as if the plus sign held.
  This is a real displayed-sign inconsistency. The broad kernel theorem's
  degenerate equality case has not been fully audited in this run.
- Its proposed unbounded-support variance/moment extension needs a carefully
  specified structural assumption. The original Hasebe–Ueda paper constructs,
  for an arbitrary prescribed nonnegative Borel integrability function, an
  unbounded-support law with the required integrability and no unimodality at
  any time. Thus merely demanding finitely many moments, or one very strong
  integrability condition, cannot supply the desired universal statement.

The remaining free companion questions concern additional hypotheses for
unbounded support, other free stable laws, continuity of the last non-unimodal time,
and possible deformations of convolution. They are new research directions rather
than the still-open form of its already-addressed named Problem 3.3.
