# Janson's Problem 20.2: the outdegree counts of a conditioned Galton–Watson tree are not asymptotically normal in general

Ledger row F0064 (intake F). Source: S. Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), Section 20.2. Written 14 September 2026, between about 02:55 and 03:30.

## The problem

Let $T_n$ be the simply generated tree with $n$ vertices for a weight sequence $w$, with $\rho$, $\nu$, $\pi$ and $\sigma^2$ as in the survey, and let $N_d = N_d(T_n)$ be the number of vertices of outdegree $d$. Kolchin proved that when $\nu = 1$ and $\sigma^2 < \infty$ (with a little more), $(N_d - n\pi_d)/\sqrt n \to N(0, \sigma_d^2)$ with
$$\sigma_d^2 = \pi_d\Big(1 - \pi_d - \frac{(d-1)^2\pi_d}{\sigma^2}\Big), \tag{20.6}$$
and Janson proved the joint version with covariances (20.7) under $\mathbb{E}\xi^3 < \infty$. Problem 20.2 asks three things: (a) do these results hold when $\nu = 1$, $\sigma^2 < \infty$ with no extra moment condition; (b) do they extend to $\nu = 1$, $\sigma^2 = \infty$; (c) what happens when $0 \le \nu < 1$?

## What the literature settled after 2012

(a) Yes. Janson, *Asymptotic normality of fringe subtrees and additive functionals in conditioned Galton–Watson trees*, Random Structures Algorithms 48 (2016), Example 2.2 with Theorem 1.13 and Section 8: for $\nu = 1$ and $0 < \sigma^2 < \infty$ and nothing more, $N_d$ is asymptotically normal with the variance (20.6), jointly for different $d$ with the covariances $-\pi_r\pi_s - (r-1)(s-1)\pi_r\pi_s/\sigma^2$, and the paper notes that the joint statement had not been proved before without $\mathbb{E}\xi^3 < \infty$.

(b) and (c), under regular variation. P. Thévenin, *Vertices with fixed outdegrees in large Galton–Watson trees*, Electron. J. Probab. 25 (2020), paper 64. Theorem 6.2: if $\nu = 1$, $\sigma^2 = \infty$ and $\pi$ is in the domain of attraction of a stable law, then $(N_A - n\pi_A)/\sqrt n \to N(0, \pi_A(1-\pi_A))$ for every $A \subset \mathbb{Z}_+$, jointly; this is (20.6) read with $\sigma^2 = \infty$. Theorem 6.7: if $\nu < 1$ and $\pi_k \sim ck^{-\beta}$ with $\beta > 2$, the same conclusion holds for finite $A$, with covariances $\pi_{A \cap B} - \pi_A\pi_B$. The proof of the second removes the largest outdegree and uses that the others are asymptotically i.i.d. with law $\pi$. The paper closes with the conjecture that both conclusions hold "for any $\mu$ critical with infinite variance, as well as for $\mu$ subcritical with no exponential moment".

## What is new here: the general answer to (b) and (c) is no

The conjecture fails, and so the unconditional forms of (b) and (c) are answered in the negative. The mechanism is the two-branch version of the construction that refutes Theorem 18.7(ii) at $\lambda = \nu$ (memo on F0058): a law with two rare atoms whose sizes straddle the mass the conditioning has to supply, at a distance $\pm A\sqrt n$ from each other. The conditioned tree then contains exactly one of the two, each with probability tending to one half, and the count of outdegree-one vertices is pinned to one of two values $\sqrt n$ apart.

**Theorem A (subcritical).** Fix $q \in (0, 1/2)$, $A > 0$ and $\eta > 0$. There is a probability law $\pi$ on $\mathbb{Z}_+$ with $\pi_0 > 0$, $\pi_1 = q$, no exponential moment (so $\rho = 1$), finite variance, mean $\nu \in (q, 1)$, and a sequence $n_r \to \infty$ such that, along $n = n_r$,
$$\limsup_{r\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{N_1(T_n) - n\pi_1}{\sqrt n}\Big| \le \frac{A}{2}\Big) \le \eta, \qquad \mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{N_1(T_n) - n\pi_1}{\sqrt n} > 0\Big) \to \frac12 .$$
With $A = \sqrt{q(1-q)}$ and $\eta < 0.38$ this rules out the limit $N(0, \pi_1(1-\pi_1))$ of (20.6), which puts mass $0.383$ on $[-A/2, A/2]$; more generally, since $\eta$ can be made as small as we like, the limit points of the law along $n_r$ are as close as we like to the two-point law $\tfrac12\delta_{-A} + \tfrac12\delta_{A}$. The tree has a unique vertex of macroscopic degree w.h.p. throughout.

**Theorem B (critical, infinite variance; proof at sketch level).** There is a probability law with $\nu = 1$, $\sigma^2 = \infty$, and a sequence $n_r$ along which $(N_1(T_n) - n\pi_1)/\sqrt n$ converges to $\tfrac12 N(-a, v) + \tfrac12 N(a, v)$ with $a > 0$ and $v = \pi_1(1-\pi_1)$; in particular not to $N(0, \pi_1(1-\pi_1))$.

Both use the standard identification (survey, Section 15): the multiset of outdegrees of $T_n$ has the law of the box counts $(Y_1, \dots, Y_n)$ of $B_{n-1,n}$, i.e. of $n$ i.i.d. copies of $\xi \sim \pi$ conditioned on $\sum Y_i = n - 1$; so $N_d(T_n) \overset{d}{=} \#\{i : Y_i = d\}$.

### Construction for Theorem A

Choose $n_1 < n_2 < \dots$ with $n_{r+1} \ge n_r^4$ and $n_1$ large. The law $\pi$ has the atoms $0$ and $1$, with $\pi_1 = q$, and for each stage $r$ two atoms
$$L_r^{\pm} = \big\lfloor (1 - q - c_{r-1})\,n_r \pm A\sqrt{n_r}\big\rfloor, \qquad \pi(L_r^{\pm}) = p_r := n_r^{-4},$$
where $c_{r-1} = \sum_{s<r} p_s(L_s^+ + L_s^-)$ is the mean mass of the earlier stages; $\pi_0$ takes the rest. Then $\sum_r p_r(L_r^+ + L_r^-) \le \sum_r 2n_r^{-3} < 1 - q$, so $\pi_0 > 0$ and $\nu = q + \sum_r p_r(L_r^+ + L_r^-) \in (q, 1)$; $V := \sum_r p_r\big((L_r^+)^2 + (L_r^-)^2\big) \le \sum_r 2n_r^{-2}$ is finite and, by taking $n_1$ large, smaller than any prescribed $\varepsilon > 0$; and $\sum_r p_r e^{\theta L_r^+} = \infty$ for every $\theta > 0$, so $\rho = 1$ and $\nu = \Psi(1)$ is the survey's $\nu$. The law has span one because $\pi_0, \pi_1 > 0$.

### Proof of Theorem A

Fix $r$, put $n = n_r$, $m = n - 1$. Call a box *bulk* if $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$, *medium* if $Y_i \in \{L_s^{\pm} : s < r\}$, *rare* if $Y_i \in \{L_r^{\pm}\}$, *huge* if $Y_i \in \{L_s^{\pm} : s > r\}$. A huge box has $Y_i \ge (1-q-c_s)n_s - A\sqrt{n_s} > n_r > m$ for $s > r$ (since $q < 1/2$ and $n_{r+1} \ge n_r^4$), so under the conditioning there are no huge boxes, exactly. Let $J$ be the number of rare boxes. Since $2L_r^- \ge 2(1-q-c_{r-1})n - 2A\sqrt n - 2 > n - 1 = m$ for large $n$, because $q < 1/2$ and $c_{r-1} \le 2n_1^{-2}$, two rare boxes would already exceed $m$; so $J \le 1$ under the conditioning.

Let $R$ denote a sum of $k$ i.i.d. copies of $\xi$ conditioned to be bulk or medium (the law $\pi$ restricted to $\{0, 1\} \cup \{L_s^{\pm}: s < r\}$ and renormalised; the renormalisation constant is $1 - 2p_r - \sum_{s>r} 2p_s = 1 - O(n_r^{-4})$). Its per-summand mean is $\mu_r = (q + c_{r-1})(1 + O(n_r^{-4}))$ and variance $\sigma_r^2 = q(1-q) + V_{r-1} + O(n_r^{-3})$, with $V_{r-1} = \sum_{s<r} p_s((L_s^+)^2 + (L_s^-)^2) \le V$; every summand is bounded by $L_{r-1}^+ \le n_{r-1}$, so the Lyapunov ratio of $k = n - 1$ summands is at most $n_{r-1}/(\sigma_r\sqrt{n_r}) \to 0$, and the span is one. The local limit theorem for triangular arrays therefore gives, uniformly in $|x| \le 2A/\sigma_r$,
$$\mathbb{P}(R_{n-1} = \lfloor (n-1)\mu_r + x\sigma_r\sqrt{n}\rfloor) = \frac{\varphi(x)}{\sigma_r\sqrt n}(1 + o(1)). \tag{1}$$

*The branch $J = 1$ with sign $\pm$.* Its probability is $n p_r (1 - O(n^{-4}))^{n-1}\,\mathbb{P}(R_{n-1} = m - L_r^{\pm})$, and $m - L_r^{\pm} - (n-1)\mu_r = \mp A\sqrt n + O(1)$ (the $O(1)$ collects the floors, the $-1$ in $m$, the one missing summand, and $n\,O(n^{-4})$). By (1) both signs have probability $n p_r\,\varphi(A/\sigma_r)(1 + o(1))/(\sigma_r\sqrt n)$; call this $\beta_n$.

*The branch $J = 0$.* We need $R_n = m$, i.e. $R_n$ exceeds its mean by $D := (1 - q - c_{r-1})n - O(1) \ge n/2 - O(1)$. Split $R_n = B + M$ into the bulk part (a sum of at most $n$ indicators of the event "$=1$", each with probability $\le q$) and the medium part. $\mathbb{P}(B \ge \mathbb{E}B + D/2) \le e^{-D^2/(2n)} \le e^{-n/9}$ by Hoeffding. The medium part exceeds its mean by $D/2 \ge n/5$ only if for some $s < r$ the stage-$s$ count exceeds $n/(5 r L_s^+) \ge n/(5rn_s)$; that count is binomial with mean $n p_s = n n_s^{-4}$, so by the Chernoff bound $\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Bin} \ge k) \le (e\,np_s/k)^k$ with $k = n/(5rn_s)$ the probability is at most $(5er/n_s^3)^{n/(5rn_s)} \le 2^{-n/(5 r n_{r-1})}$. All of this is $o(\beta_n)$, since $\beta_n \ge n^{-4}\varphi(A/\sigma_r)/(2\sigma_r\sqrt n)$ decays polynomially while the bounds decay exponentially in $n/(5rn_{r-1}) \ge n_r^{1/2}$.

Hence, conditionally on $\sum Y_i = m$: $J = 1$ with probability $1 - o(1)$, and the sign is $+$ or $-$ with conditional probability $\tfrac12 + o(1)$ each.

*The count $N_1$ given the branch.* On $\{J = 1, \text{sign} \pm\}$ the other $n - 1$ boxes are i.i.d. bulk-or-medium conditioned on $B + M = m - L_r^{\pm}$, where now $B$ is the number of bulk boxes equal to $1$ and $M$ the medium total. No medium or rare atom equals $1$, so $N_1 = B$, and $B = m - L_r^{\pm} - M = (n-1)\mu_r \mp A\sqrt n + O(1) - M$. Write $M = \mathbb{E}M + \Delta$; $\mathbb{E}M = (n-1)(\mu_r - q')$ with $q' = q(1 + O(n^{-4}))$, so
$$N_1 - nq = \mp A\sqrt n - \Delta + O(1).$$
Under the conditioning, $\Delta$ is controlled without any joint limit theorem: for $K > 0$,
$$\mathbb{P}(|\Delta| > K\sqrt{nV} \mid B + M = c) \le \frac{\mathbb{P}(|\Delta| > K\sqrt{nV})\,\sup_b \mathbb{P}(B = b)}{\mathbb{P}(B + M = c)} \le \frac{K^{-2}\cdot C/\sqrt n}{\varphi(A/\sigma_r)(1+o(1))/(\sigma_r\sqrt n)} \le C' K^{-2},$$
by Chebyshev ($\mathrm{Var}\,M \le nV$), the uniform bound $\sup_b \mathbb{P}(B = b) \le C/\sqrt n$ for a binomial with $q' \in (0,1)$ fixed, and (1) for the denominator (here $c = m - L^\pm_r$ and $B$, $M$ are independent); the constant $C'$ depends on $q$ and $A$ only, since $\sigma_r^2 \in [q(1-q), q(1-q) + V]$. So on the $\pm$ branch, $|(N_1 - nq)/\sqrt n \pm A| \le K\sqrt V + o(1)$ with conditional probability at least $1 - C'K^{-2}$. Choose $K$ with $C'K^{-2} \le \eta$ and then $n_1$ so large that $K\sqrt V \le A/4$; both claims of Theorem A follow with $\pi_1 = q$. $\square$

A remark on (1): the local limit theorem is applied to a triangular array, and its uniformity comes from the atoms $0$ and $1$ carrying probability at least $\min(\pi_0, q)/2$ in every row, which bounds the characteristic function away from $1$ off the lattice points uniformly in $r$; the rate $O(\rho_3/(\sigma_r^3\sqrt n))$ of the local Berry–Esseen theorem (Petrov, Ch. VII, Theorem 13) then holds with a constant depending on that lower bound only. With a two-dimensional local limit theorem for $(B, M)$ one gets the full limit $\tfrac12 N(-A', v) + \tfrac12 N(A', v)$ along $n_r$ with $A' = Aq(1-q)/(q(1-q) + V_\infty)$ and $v = q(1-q)V_\infty/(q(1-q)+V_\infty)$; the proof above avoids it because the weaker statement already refutes normality.

*Remark.* Exact toy (`toy_202_bimodal.py sub 3.0`, $n = 4000$, $q = 1/2$, no medium atoms, $L^{\pm} = 1999.5 \pm 3\sqrt{n}$ rounded, $n\pi(L^{\pm}) = 0.025$ each): branch weights $0.4976$ and $0.5024$, $N_1$ conditionally equal to $2000 \mp 190$ with all its mass in $z \in [-3.5, -3) \cup [2.5, 3)$, $\mathbb{P}(|z| \le \sqrt{\pi_1(1-\pi_1)}) = 0.000$ against $0.683$ for the conjectured Gaussian.

### Construction and sketch for Theorem B

Now $\nu$ must be exactly $1$ and $\sigma^2 = \infty$, and the deficit is no longer macroscopic: it comes, as in the F0058 memo, from rarer atoms that are absent w.h.p. Stage $r$ carries: a *rarer* atom $B_r$ of size $L_{B,r} = \Sigma_r/\varepsilon$ and mass $\varepsilon/n_r$ ($\varepsilon = 1/10$), whose mean contribution $\Sigma_r := \sqrt{n_r}\log n_r$ per $n_r$ boxes is the deficit when it is absent; two *rare* atoms $L_r^{\pm} = \lfloor \Sigma_r' \pm t_r\rfloor$, where $\Sigma_r'$ is the deficit corrected for the rare pair's own mean share and for the masses of later stages, and $t_r = x_r\sigma_r\sqrt{n_r}$ with $x_r = a\,\sigma_r/\pi_1(1-\pi_1)$ (so that the branch shift in $N_1$ is $\mp a\sqrt n$ after the medium boxes absorb their share; see below); their probabilities $p_r^{\pm}$ are chosen with $p_r^+ + p_r^- = \delta_r/n_r$, $\delta_r = 1/r$, and, this is the one new device, in the ratio
$$p_r^+ : p_r^- = \mathbb{P}(R_{n_r-1} = m - L_r^-) : \mathbb{P}(R_{n_r-1} = m - L_r^+),$$
which is a fixed positive number once the stages $< r$ are fixed (the bulk probability is fixed in advance since all stage masses $\delta_r/n_r$ and $\varepsilon/n_r$ are). With this choice the two branches have exactly equal weight, and no moderate-deviation estimate for the branch weights is needed. The masses: $\pi_1$ is fixed in advance and $\pi_0 = 1 - \pi_1 - \sum_r(\text{stage probabilities})$; the mean is $\pi_1 + \sum_r (p_r^+L_r^+ + p_r^-L_r^- + \Sigma_r/n_r)$; to make it exactly $1$, each stage also carries a *mass-adjusting* atom of size larger than $n_r$ and probability $n_r^{-4}$ whose size is chosen last so that the stage's total mass equals a prescribed $\mu_r$ with $\pi_1 + \sum_r\mu_r = 1$; being larger than $n_r$ it never appears at stage $r$ and is a medium atom later. Then $\sigma^2 \ge \sum_r (\varepsilon/n_r)(\Sigma_r/\varepsilon)^2 = \sum_r (\log n_r)^2/\varepsilon = \infty$, $\rho = 1$, and $V_{r-1} \asymp (\log n_{r-1})^2/\varepsilon \to \infty$ slowly, which is why $x_r \to \infty$ slowly (it is of order $\log n_{r-1}$).

At $n = n_r$: the rare route costs $\delta_r\,\mathbb{P}(R = m - L^{\pm}) \ge \delta_r e^{-Cx_r^2}/\sqrt n$ (lower bound through the bulk binomial alone, as in the proof of Theorem A, since the medium total is within $K\sqrt{nV}$ of its mean with probability $1 - K^{-2}$); the bulk-and-medium route has to supply $\Sigma_r$ and costs at most $e^{-\Sigma_r^2/(2n\sigma_r^2)}\cdot(1+o(1)) + (\text{one-big-medium-jump terms}) = e^{-(\log n_r)^2/(2\sigma_r^2)}(1+o(1))$ by Bernstein for summands bounded by $n_{r-1}$, and this is $o$ of the rare route as soon as $\log n_r \gg \log n_{r-1}$, e.g. $n_r = n_{r-1}^{r}$; a present $B_r$, two rare atoms, or a later-stage atom overshoots by at least $9\Sigma_r$ and costs a Gaussian factor in $(9\Sigma_r)^2/(2n\sigma_r^2)$ times the atom's probability; so $J = 1$ w.h.p. and the sign is fair by construction. Given the sign, $N_1 = B = c - M$ with $c = m - L^{\pm}$, and $\mathbb{P}(B = b \mid B + M = c) \propto \mathbb{P}(B = b)\,\mathbb{P}(M = c - b)$. Over the window $|b - \mathbb{E}B| \le K\sqrt n$ the second factor varies by the factor $\exp(\mp x_r\sigma_r (b - \mathbb{E}B)/(\sqrt n\,V_{r-1})(1 + o(1)))$: this is the local moderate-deviation expansion for $M$ (a sum of lattice summands bounded by $n_{r-1}$ with variance $nV_{r-1}$, evaluated at deviations $u = O(x_r\sqrt{nV})$ where $u^2/(nV) = O(x_r^2)$ and the cubic Cramér term is $O(\kappa_3 x_r^3/(\sqrt n V^{3/2})) = O(n_{r-1}x_r^3/\sqrt{n_r}) \to 0$; Petrov, *Sums of Independent Random Variables*, Ch. VIII, Theorem 6, in the lattice form, with the remainder tracked through the tilting proof to see that it depends only on the bound $n_{r-1}$, $V$, and $n$). Since $x_r\sigma_r/V_{r-1} \to a/(\pi_1(1-\pi_1))$ by the choice of $x_r$ (note $\sigma_r^2 = \pi_1(1-\pi_1) + V_{r-1}$ and $V_{r-1} \to \infty$), the conditional law of $B$ is the binomial exponentially tilted by $e^{\mp a(b - \mathbb{E}B)/(\sqrt n\,\pi_1(1-\pi_1))}$, i.e. $N(\mathbb{E}B \mp a\sqrt n,\ n\pi_1(1-\pi_1))$ to leading order, and the mixture of the two branches is the limit stated. $\square$ (sketch)

The toy for this case (`toy_202_bimodal.py crit 6.0 6 100 0.5`: bulk $\pi_1 = 1/2$, a medium atom at $6$ with $np = 100$, a rarer atom at $20000$ with $np = 0.05$, the rare pair at $1020$ and $1779$ with $np = 0.025$ each) gives a bimodal conditional law with $\mathbb{P}(|z| \le \sqrt{\pi_1(1-\pi_1)}) = 0.28$ against $0.68$, but branch weights $0.986 : 0.014$ rather than $1:1$, because at $n = 4000$ the medium total is a Poisson$(100)$ count and the deviation $\pm 6\sqrt n$ is $5.6$ of its standard deviations, where the Poisson skew is large. Refining the medium atoms at fixed medium variance (size $3$ with $np = 400$, size $2$ with $np = 900$, the deficit held at about $1400$) moves the weights to $0.90 : 0.10$ and $0.80 : 0.20$, as the cubic term predicts; the equal-weight device above removes the issue entirely in the construction.

## Why the regular-variation results survive, and the repaired question

In Thévenin's Theorem 6.7 the giant vertex's degree has a law that is flat on the scale $\sqrt n$ ($\pi_{k+j}/\pi_k \to 1$ uniformly for $j = O(\sqrt k)$ when $\pi_k \sim ck^{-\beta}$), so the giant absorbs the Gaussian fluctuation of the other $n - 1$ outdegrees at no cost and they stay i.i.d. In Theorem A the giant's law has two atoms $2A\sqrt n$ apart and nothing between them, so it is the other vertices that must adjust, by exactly $\mp A\sqrt n$. Uniqueness of the condensate is not the issue: the tree of Theorem A has a unique giant w.h.p. What matters is the local flatness of $\pi$ near the size the giant has to take.

## New open problems

1. For $\nu < 1$: is asymptotic normality of $(N_d - n\pi_d)/\sqrt n$ with variance $\pi_d(1 - \pi_d)$ equivalent to local flatness of $\pi$ at the scale of the condensate, in the sense that $\pi_{k+j}/\pi_k \to 1$ uniformly for $|j| \le C\sqrt k$ along $k \approx (1-\nu)n$? The direction "flatness implies normality" should follow the one-big-jump argument of Armendáriz and Loulakis; the direction "normality implies flatness" is what Theorem A suggests and is open.
2. For $\nu = 1$, $\sigma^2 = \infty$: is there a law outside every stable domain of attraction for which $N_d$ is nevertheless asymptotically normal for every $d$? Theorem B needs a very lacunary law; a slowly varying but non-regularly varying tail is the natural test.
3. The case $\nu = 0$ (the third question of Problem 20.2 at its endpoint): with $\rho = 0$ there is no tilted law and $N_0 = n - o(n)$; what are the orders of magnitude and the fluctuations of $N_d$ for fixed $d \ge 1$? For $w_k = k!$ (complete condensation, Example 18.35) the survey gives the size of the largest box; the counts of the small boxes are not addressed.
4. Along the full sequence $n \to \infty$ (not a subsequence), can the limit fail to exist for a law with $\nu < 1$ whose condensate is unique w.h.p. for every $n$? The construction above says nothing about $n$ between stages.

## Status

Literature: (a) resolved (Janson 2016); (b) and (c) resolved positively under regular variation (Thévenin 2020). New: (b) and (c) answered negatively in general, Theorem A with a complete elementary proof (local limit theorem for triangular arrays, Hoeffding, Chernoff, Chebyshev), Theorem B at sketch level (its one non-elementary input is a lattice local moderate-deviation expansion for sums of bounded variables with the remainder tracked in a triangular array). Both refute the closing conjecture of Thévenin's paper. Verification: author checked; the toy computations are exact multinomial sums, not simulations. Prior art: none found for the negative answer in a bounded search (the two papers above, plus a search for "outdegree" with "condensation" and "not asymptotically normal"); not a clearance.
