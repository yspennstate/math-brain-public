# Sinclair's inequality with the diameter in place of $\log n$

Aldous and Fill, *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs*, Open Problem 4.49. Intake row
I0026. Written 14 September 2026. Author-checked, no independent read; the one literature citation I
lean on is flagged where it is used.

## The question

For a reversible chain on a finite set $V$ with stationary distribution $\pi$, let $f = (f^{(a)})_{a\in V}$
be a family in which $f^{(a)}$ is a unit flow from $a$ to $\pi$, and set

$$\psi(f) \;=\; \max_{(i,j)} \; \frac{\sum_a \pi_a\,|f^{(a)}_{ij}|}{\pi_i p_{ij}} .$$

Sinclair's inequality is $\inf_f \psi(f) \le K\,\tau_2 \log n$, with $\tau_2$ the relaxation time.
Open Problem 4.49 asks (i) for a simple proof, and (ii) whether $\log n$ may be replaced by the
diameter $\Delta$.

Write

$$R(G) \;=\; \frac{\inf_f \psi(f)}{\tau_2\,\Delta}$$

for the simple random walk on a connected graph $G$; part (ii) asks whether $R$ is bounded.

## What this note contains

Everything below rests on one observation: $\inf_f \psi(f)$ is the minimum congestion of a
multicommodity flow, so it has a shortest-path-metric dual, and the question becomes a question about
a flow–cut gap. Once that is in place:

1. $\tau_2 \ge 1$ for every connected graph that is not complete, and $\tau_2 = (n-1)/n < 1$ for $K_n$.
2. $R(K_n) = 2$ exactly, at every $n$.
3. $R \le 2\,\mathrm{gap}(G)/\Delta(G)$, where $\mathrm{gap}$ is the flow–cut gap of the instance. So a
   counterexample to $R \le 2$ must be a graph whose flow–cut gap exceeds its diameter.
4. If the uniform metric is optimal in the dual, $R < 2$ strictly for every non-complete graph.
5. If $G$ has no $K_4$ minor, $\inf_f \psi(f) \le 2\tau_2$ — the diameter is not even needed.
6. Over all $992$ connected graphs on four to seven vertices, and over forty further graphs up to
   $n = 133$ including Paley graphs, hypercubes, Petersen, and the polarity graphs of projective
   planes, $\max R = 2$, attained by the complete graphs and by nothing else.

None of this settles (ii). What it does is say exactly what a counterexample has to look like, and it
gives the constant, which the problem does not state.

## The dual

**Proposition 1.** For the simple random walk on a connected graph, with $c_e = \pi_i p_{ij} = 1/(2m)$
for every edge,

$$\inf_f \psi(f) \;=\; \max\Big\{ \sum_{a,b} \pi_a \pi_b\, d(a,b) \;:\; d \text{ a metric on } V,\;
\sum_{e} c_e\, d(e) = 1 \Big\}.$$

*Proof.* Shipping one unit from $a$ to $\pi$, for each $a$, with $a$ weighted by $\pi_a$, is the
multicommodity flow problem with demand $\pi_a \pi_b$ between $a$ and $b$; $\psi(f)$ is its congestion
against the capacities $c_e$. The stated identity is the standard linear-programming duality between
minimum congestion and fractional sparsest cut. The optimal $d$ may be taken to be the shortest-path
metric generated by its own values on the edges, since replacing $d$ by that metric leaves the
constraint alone and cannot decrease the objective. $\square$

Two feasible metrics are worth naming. The **uniform** metric $d = 2\,d_G$ has
$\sum_e c_e d(e) = 2m\cdot 2/(2m)\cdot\tfrac12 = 1$ and objective $2\bar d$, where
$\bar d = \sum_{a,b}\pi_a\pi_b d_G(a,b)$ is the mean $\pi$-weighted distance. The **cut** metric of a
subset $A$, scaled to be feasible, has objective $2\pi(A)\pi(A^c)/Q(A,A^c)$, where
$Q(A,A^c) = \sum_{e \in \delta A} c_e$. Write

$$B(G) \;=\; \max_A \frac{2\pi(A)\pi(A^c)}{Q(A,A^c)}$$

for the best cut metric, and $\mathrm{gap}(G) = \inf_f\psi(f)/B(G) \ge 1$ for the flow–cut gap of the
instance. Both $2\bar d$ and $B(G)$ are lower bounds on $\inf_f \psi(f)$.

## Three elementary facts

**Proposition 2.** If $G$ is connected and not complete, $\tau_2 \ge 1$. If $G = K_n$,
$\tau_2 = (n-1)/n < 1$.

*Proof.* Take $u \not\sim v$ and $f = \pi_v \mathbf 1_u - \pi_u \mathbf 1_v$, which has $E_\pi f = 0$.
Then $\langle f, Pf\rangle_\pi = \sum_{i,j}\pi_i p_{ij} f_i f_j$, and the only pairs $(i,j)$ with both
$f_i \ne 0$ and $f_j \ne 0$ are inside $\{u,v\}$; there $p_{uu} = p_{vv} = 0$ because the graph is
simple and $p_{uv} = 0$ because $u$ and $v$ are not adjacent. So the quadratic form vanishes and
$\lambda_2 \ge 0$, whence $\tau_2 = 1/(1-\lambda_2) \ge 1$. For $K_n$ the non-trivial eigenvalue is
$-1/(n-1)$. $\square$

So the complete graphs are exactly the graphs whose relaxation time is below one, which is why they are
the extremal case: they are also the only graphs of diameter one.

**Proposition 3.** $B(G) \le 2\tau_2$.

*Proof.* With $g = \mathbf 1_A/\pi(A) - \mathbf 1_{A^c}/\pi(A^c)$ one has $E_\pi g = 0$,
$\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g) = 1/\pi(A) + 1/\pi(A^c)$ and Dirichlet form
$\mathcal E(g,g) = Q(A,A^c)\,(1/\pi(A)+1/\pi(A^c))^2$. The Poincaré inequality
$\mathrm{Var}_\pi \le \tau_2 \mathcal E$ gives $\pi(A)\pi(A^c)/Q(A,A^c) \le \tau_2$ for every $A$. $\square$

**Proposition 4.** $R(G) \le 2\,\mathrm{gap}(G)/\Delta(G)$.

*Proof.* $\inf\psi = \mathrm{gap}\cdot B(G) \le 2\,\mathrm{gap}\cdot\tau_2$ by Proposition 3; divide by
$\tau_2\Delta$. $\square$

That is the reduction. **Open Problem 4.49(ii), with constant $2$, holds for a graph as soon as its
flow–cut gap is at most its diameter.** Part (i) reads off the same way: the Leighton–Rao theorem, in
the product-demand form of Linial, London and Rabinovich, bounds $\mathrm{gap}$ by $O(\log n)$, and
Proposition 3 is elementary, so $\inf\psi \le O(\log n)\,\tau_2$. That is a short modern proof of
Sinclair's inequality, and it explains the $\log n$: it is the $\ell_1$-distortion of the optimal dual
metric and nothing else.

## Where the constant $2$ comes from

**Proposition 5.** $R(K_n) = 2$ for every $n \ge 2$.

*Proof.* Route $f^{(a)}$ along the single edges out of $a$, so $f^{(a)}_{av} = 1/n$ for $v \ne a$. An
edge $(u,v)$ then carries $\pi_u/n + \pi_v/n = 2/n^2$ in the weighted sum, and $c_e = 1/(n(n-1))$, so
$\psi(f) = 2(n-1)/n$. This is optimal because the uniform metric already gives
$2\bar d = 2(n-1)/n$ in the dual. With $\tau_2 = (n-1)/n$ and $\Delta = 1$, $R = 2$. $\square$

**Proposition 6.** If the uniform metric is optimal in the dual — that is, $\inf_f\psi(f) = 2\bar d$ —
then $R(G) < 2$ for every connected $G$ other than a complete graph.

*Proof.* $R = 2\bar d/(\tau_2\Delta) \le 2\bar d/\Delta$ by Proposition 2, and
$\bar d < \Delta$ because $d_G \le \Delta$ everywhere while $d_G(a,a) = 0$ carries the positive weight
$\sum_a \pi_a^2$. $\square$

So a counterexample has to have a genuine flow–cut gap. The gaps found are tiny. Over the $992$
connected graphs on four to seven vertices the optimal dual metric is a single cut metric in $887$
cases and the uniform metric in $40$; in the remaining $85$ it is neither, and even there the largest
gap $\inf\psi/B(G)$ is $1.219$. The largest gap anywhere in this note is $1.30$, at $K_{5,5}$. So
Proposition 3 alone, with no reference to the diameter, already settles $R \le 2$ for the overwhelming
majority of small graphs.

## Graphs with no $K_4$ minor

**Proposition 7.** If $G$ has no $K_4$ minor then $\inf_f \psi(f) = B(G) \le 2\tau_2$, so
$R(G) \le 2/\Delta(G)$.

*Proof.* By Proposition 1 the optimal dual metric is the shortest-path metric $d_w$ of some
non-negative weights $w$ on the edges of $G$. For a graph with no $K_4$ minor every such metric lies in
the cut cone: $d_w = \sum_S \lambda_S \delta_S$ with $\lambda_S \ge 0$. *(This is the classical
equality of the metric cone and the cut cone for series-parallel graphs; I am citing it from memory —
Deza and Laurent, "Geometry of Cuts and Metrics", carry it, with the multicommodity flow form due to
Seymour. It should be checked against the source before the statement is used.)* Substituting,
the objective is $\sum_S \lambda_S \cdot 2\pi(S)\pi(S^c)$ and the constraint is
$\sum_S \lambda_S Q(S,S^c) = 1$, so the value is a weighted average of the cut values and is at most
$B(G)$. The reverse inequality is immediate since cut metrics are feasible. $\square$

Fifteen $K_4$-minor-free graphs were checked directly — paths, cycles, trees, stars, two triangles
glued at a vertex, and eight randomly generated series-parallel graphs — and in every case the exact
linear program returned $\inf_f\psi(f) = B(G)$ to machine precision, with $\inf\psi/(2\tau_2)$ between
$0.44$ and $1$; the value $1$ is attained at the triangle.

## What the computation says

The infimum is a linear program: the flows are free variables, conservation is linear, and the maximum
of sums of absolute values linearises with one auxiliary variable per source and edge. It was written
twice, once densely and once sparsely, and the two agree to zero relative error on nine test graphs.
The dual of Proposition 1 was written separately, as a program in the pair variables with the triangle
inequalities, and agrees with the primal to $2\times 10^{-15}$ on thirteen graphs including Petersen,
the cube, the barbell and Paley $13$. Complete graphs return the hand value $2(n-1)/n$ exactly.

Over all $992$ connected graphs on four to seven vertices, the largest $R$ is $2$, attained by
$K_4,\dots,K_7$ and by nothing else. Steepest-ascent search over single edge additions and deletions,
started from random graphs and from complete graphs with a few edges removed, was run at $n = 8,9,10$;
away from the complete graph the local optima sit near $R = 1$.

The bottlenecked graphs — barbell, glued cliques, lollipops — are where $\inf\psi$ is far above
$2\bar d$, and they are exactly where $\tau_2$ is large enough to pay for it.

The most interesting family is the polarity graph of $PG(2,q)$: $q^2+q+1$ points, $x \sim y$ when
$x\cdot y = 0$, the absolute points losing their loops. These have diameter $2$, adjacency eigenvalues
$q$ and $\pm\sqrt q$, hence $\lambda_2 = \sqrt q/(q+1)$ exactly, and mean distance tending to $2$. They
are the graphs that should climb toward the complete graph's value, and they do:

| $q$ | $n$ | $\tau_2$ | $2\bar d$ | $\inf\psi$ | $R$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 7 | 1.7676 | 2.6296 | 3.0000 | 0.8486 |
| 3 | 13 | 1.7637 | 3.1042 | 3.2500 | 0.9214 |
| 5 | 31 | 1.5941 | 3.4926 | 3.5244 | 1.1055 |
| 7 | 57 | 1.4941 | 3.6529 | 3.6618 | 1.2254 |
| 11 | 133 | 1.3820 | 3.7906 | (program too large) | $\ge 1.3715$ |

Asymptotically $\tau_2 = 1 + q^{-1/2} + O(1/q)$ and $2\bar d = 4 - 2/q + O(q^{-2})$, so
$R \to 2$ like $2 - 2q^{-1/2}$. The flow–cut gap along this family is negligible: at $q = 7$ it is
$3.6618/3.6529 = 1.0024$. So the bound $2$ is approached by sparse diameter-two graphs from below, and
attained only in the degenerate diameter-one case.

## What I could not do

I did not prove $\mathrm{gap}(G) \le \Delta(G)$, which by Proposition 4 would settle 4.49(ii) with
constant $2$. Two things make it delicate. The dual metric $d_w$ is the shortest-path metric of
*arbitrary* weights, so the graph's diameter puts no bound on the metric's own aspect ratio, and the
worst known instances for the flow–cut gap are constant-degree expanders, where the gap is
$\Theta(\log n)$ and the diameter is also $\Theta(\log n)$ — the conjecture is tight there up to
constants, which is why it cannot be proved by any bound that throws the diameter away.

Nor did I search hard enough to be confident that no counterexample exists. The steepest-ascent search
covers $n \le 10$ and the exact scan $n \le 7$; the quantity to maximise for a counterexample is
$\inf\psi/\tau_2$ over diameter-two graphs, where the target is $4$ and the largest value seen anywhere
is $2.82$, at Paley $17$.

## Problems this leaves

1. Is $\mathrm{gap}(G) \le \Delta(G)$ for every connected graph, with $\mathrm{gap}$ the flow–cut gap
   at demand $\pi\otimes\pi$ and capacity $\pi_i p_{ij}$? This implies 4.49(ii) with $K = 2$.
2. Is $\sup_G \inf_f\psi(f)/\tau_2$ over graphs of diameter two equal to $3$? The complete bipartite
   graphs $K_{k,k}$ give $3 - 2/k$, Paley $17$ gives $2.82$, and nothing found exceeds $3$. A bound of
   $3$ here would give $R \le 3/2$ for every graph of diameter two.
3. For which graphs is the uniform metric optimal in the dual? Proposition 6 makes this a sufficient
   condition for $R < 2$. Vertex-transitivity is not enough: the circulant $C_{12}(1,2,3)$ has
   $\inf\psi = 3$ against $2\bar d = 8/3$, and the pentagonal prism has $3.6$ against $3.4$. In both
   the optimal dual is a cut metric instead, so Proposition 3 covers them; is every graph covered by
   one of the two?
4. Is $2$ the right constant in Sinclair's inequality itself — that is, is
   $\inf_f\psi(f) \le 2\tau_2\log_2 n$, with the complete graph extremal at $n = 2$? The computed
   values are far below this.
5. The polarity graphs approach $R = 2$ at rate $q^{-1/2}$, that is $n^{-1/4}$. Is there a family
   approaching $2$ faster, and is $2 - cn^{-1/4}$ the true envelope for non-complete graphs?
