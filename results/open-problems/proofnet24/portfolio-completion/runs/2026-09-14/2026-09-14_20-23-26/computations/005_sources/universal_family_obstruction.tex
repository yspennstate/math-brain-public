\section{Two open problems through the entropy lens}
\label{sec:universal}

The finite-lift principle of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a sufficient condition: a
class with a bounded lift to a block algebra of entropy $O(n)$ satisfies Matrix
Spencer. Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruct} is the matching necessary obstruction
for one specific way of using the method, namely an \emph{exact} (surjective)
finite block lift of the abstract algebra $\M_D(\C)$. This section sharpens that
obstruction and applies it to two famous open problems.

We address the natural strategy ``reduce the general Matrix Spencer conjecture to
the conjecture for one universal structured family'' -- all circulants, or all
Toeplitz matrices, or all matrices in a fixed maximal abelian subalgebra. The
outcome is a clean no-go theorem (\S\ref{subsec:nogo}): no such reduction can
proceed through an operator-norm faithful lift, because any faithful encoding of
$\M_D(\C)$ -- exact or approximate, surjective or not, bounded or completely
bounded -- is forced to feed entropy at least of order $D^2$ into the engine, and
therefore cannot beat the trivial rate $\sqrt{n\log(e+D^2/n)}$. The mechanism that
makes Toeplitz and group-convolution classes tractable is exactly the mechanism
that forbids them, at affordable size, from being universal.

Two points make this more than a restatement of Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruct}.
First, the obstruction is no longer about the abstract algebra: the relevant
quantity is the dimension of the operator system the lifts span, which we isolate
as a faithful complexity measure, the \emph{lift entropy}
(Definition~\ref{def:liftentropy}), interpolating between rank and ambient
dimension and answering, in the negative, the question of whether a sub-quadratic
such measure can exist for the full algebra. Second, the bounded-lift hypothesis
is shown to be \emph{not} where the difficulty lives: for a fixed maximal abelian
subalgebra of $\M_N(\C)$ with $N=\Theta(D^2)$ there is a genuine simultaneous
contractive lift of every tuple of $D\times D$ Hermitian contractions
(Proposition~\ref{prop:masalift}), so the reduction ``succeeds'' at the level of
lifts and is defeated only downstream, by the entropy it must pay. We close with
an honest account of the Matrix Koml\'os analogue (\S\ref{subsec:komlos}), where
the same accounting shows the present engine is structurally a Spencer-rate
machine and yields nothing of Koml\'os type for unstructured inputs, while giving
a precise conditional statement for low-dimensional operator systems.

Throughout, $\Herm(d)\subset\M_d(\C)$ is the real vector space of $d\times d$
Hermitian matrices, of real dimension $d^2$, equipped with the
Hilbert--Schmidt inner product $\langle X,Y\rangle_{\HS}=\operatorname{tr}(XY)$
and norms $\norm{\cdot}_{\op}\le\norm{\cdot}_{\HS}$. We write $\Herm_0(d)$ for the
traceless hyperplane, of real dimension $d^2-1$.

\subsection{What a reduction would have to be}
\label{subsec:redef}

We first fix the notion of reduction that the finite-lift machinery supports, so
that the no-go statement is precise. The defining feature of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
is that one signing must serve both the lifts and their images: the signs are
chosen for the lifted block-diagonal matrices and then pushed through a fixed map.
A reduction in this sense is therefore a single fixed linear map together with
bounded lifts, valid for an arbitrary input tuple.

\begin{definition}[Faithful finite-lift reduction]\label{def:reduction}
Let $\mcF$ be a class of matrices closed under the operations of the framework. A
\emph{$(K,\Lambda)$-faithful finite-lift reduction} of $\M_D(\C)$ to $\mcF$ is a
rule that assigns to every tuple $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\M_D(\C)$ of contractions a
finite block algebra $\mcB=\bigoplus_\alpha\M_{r_\alpha}(\C)$ realizing members of
$\mcF$, elements $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\in\mcB$, and a fixed linear map
\[
\Pi:\Span\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\}\longrightarrow\M_D(\C)
\]
such that
\[
\Pi(Y_i)=A_i,\qquad
\norm{\Pi(Y)}_{\op}\le\Lambda\norm{Y}_{\mcB}
\ \ (Y\in\Span\{Y_i\}),\qquad
\norm{Y_i}_{\mcB}\le K,
\]
with $K,\Lambda$ absolute constants, independent of $D$ and of the tuple. The
reduction is \emph{universal} if it is defined for all tuples, i.e. the assignment
covers every contraction tuple in $\M_D(\C)$.
\end{definition}

This is exactly the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, made into a reduction by
demanding it for arbitrary tuples with absolute constants. If such a reduction
exists with $\Ent(\mcB)=O(n)$ uniformly, then Matrix Spencer for $\mcF$ at the
relevant sizes implies it for $\M_D(\C)$ in the regime $D\le n$, because the
signs furnished for the $Y_i$ transfer through $\Pi$ with a loss of only
$\Lambda K$.

\subsection{The lift succeeds; the entropy does not}
\label{subsec:masa}

It is tempting to guess that the universal reduction fails because the lift
constants $K,\Lambda$ must blow up: that forcing a single fixed map $\Pi$ to
reproduce many non-commuting targets with bounded block norm is impossible. This
guess is wrong, and it is instructive to see why, because it locates the real
obstruction. We record that a fixed maximal abelian subalgebra already admits a
genuine simultaneous contractive lift of \emph{every} Hermitian tuple, once its
size is quadratic.

\begin{proposition}[A bounded simultaneous lift through a fixed MASA]
\label{prop:masalift}
There is an absolute constant $K_0$ with the following property. For every $d$ and
every $N\ge 4d^2$ there is a fixed unitary $U\in\M_N(\C)$ -- so that
$\mcA_U=\{U^*\Lambda U:\Lambda\ \text{diagonal}\}$ is a fixed maximal abelian
subalgebra of $\M_N(\C)$ -- and a fixed isometry $V:\C^d\hookrightarrow\C^N$ with
the following feature. For \emph{every} tuple $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\Herm(d)$ of
operator-norm contractions there are real diagonal matrices
$\Lambda_1,\ldots,\Lambda_n\in\M_N(\R)$ with
\[
V^*\,U^*\Lambda_i U\,V=A_i,
\qquad
\norm{U^*\Lambda_iU}_{\op}=\norm{\Lambda_i}_\infty\le K_0
\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\]
Equivalently, $A_i=\Phi(D_i)$ for the single fixed completely positive contraction
$\Phi(\cdot)=V^*U^*(\cdot)UV$ and the diagonal contractions $D_i=\Lambda_i/K_0$,
so the tuple has a $(K_0,1)$-lift to the commutative algebra
$\mcB=\bigoplus_{k=1}^N\C$ of entropy $\Ent(\mcB)=N=\Theta(d^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $U$ to be any unitary and absorb it into the choice of frame; it is harmless,
since $\mcA_U$ is a maximal abelian subalgebra for every $U$ and the diagonal of
$\Lambda$ is unchanged in norm under $\Lambda\mapsto U^*\Lambda U$. It therefore
suffices to produce a fixed isometry $V:\C^d\hookrightarrow\C^N$ such that the
compression $\Phi(\Lambda)=V^*\Lambda V$ from real diagonal $N\times N$ matrices to
$\Herm(d)$ carries every operator-norm contraction in its range with a bounded
diagonal preimage. Writing $v_1,\ldots,v_N\in\C^d$ for the rows of $V$, we have
\[
V^*\Lambda V=\sum_{k=1}^N\lambda_k\,v_kv_k^*,
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^N v_kv_k^*=V^*V=I_d,
\]
so $\{v_kv_k^*\}$ is the rank-one resolution of the identity associated with a
unit-norm tight frame $\{\sqrt{N/d}\,v_k\}$ for $\C^d$. The map
$\Phi:\Lambda\mapsto V^*\Lambda V$ is the analysis-to-synthesis map of this frame.

Choose $V$ so that $\{v_k\}$ is a frame for which the synthesis map is
\emph{uniformly bounded below} on $\Herm(d)$: concretely, choose
$\{\sqrt{N/d}\,v_k\}_{k\le N}$ to be a union of $\lceil N/d^2\rceil\ge 4$ rotated
copies of an orthonormal-basis-derived Parseval frame whose rank-one projections
$\{v_kv_k^*\}$ linearly span $\Herm(d)$ and are well-conditioned in
Hilbert--Schmidt geometry; such frames exist for $N\ge 4d^2$ and may be taken
fixed (independent of the tuple), for instance from a complete set of mutually
unbiased bases when $d$ is a prime power, and from a generic union of $4d^2/d=4d$
orthonormal bases otherwise. For such a $V$ the operator
\[
T:\Herm(d)\to\Herm(d),
\qquad
T(A)=\sum_{k=1}^N\langle A,v_kv_k^*\rangle_{\HS}\,v_kv_k^*=\Phi\big(\Phi^*(A)\big)
\]
(the frame operator on $\Herm(d)$, with $\Phi^*(A)=(\langle A,v_kv_k^*\rangle)_k$
the diagonal of $V A V^*$) is positive definite with
$\kappa I\preceq T\preceq \kappa' I$ for absolute constants $0<\kappa\le\kappa'$,
by the tightness and conditioning of the frame. Given a target contraction $A$,
set
\[
\Lambda=\diag\!\big(\Phi^*(T^{-1}A)\big),
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\lambda_k=\big\langle T^{-1}A,\,v_kv_k^*\big\rangle_{\HS}.
\]
Then $\Phi(\Lambda)=T(T^{-1}A)=A$, and
\[
\abs{\lambda_k}
=\abs{\langle T^{-1}A,v_kv_k^*\rangle_{\HS}}
\le\norm{T^{-1}A}_{\HS}\,\norm{v_kv_k^*}_{\HS}
\le\kappa^{-1}\norm{A}_{\HS}\cdot\frac dN
\le\kappa^{-1}\sqrt d\cdot\frac dN,
\]
using $\norm{v_kv_k^*}_{\HS}=\norm{v_k}_2^2=d/N$ and
$\norm{A}_{\HS}\le\sqrt d\,\norm{A}_{\op}\le\sqrt d$. For $N\ge 4d^2$ this is at
most $\kappa^{-1}/(4\sqrt d)\le K_0$. The bound is uniform over the tuple because
$V$, hence $T$ and the frame, are fixed; only $\Lambda_i$ depends on $A_i$, and
each obeys the same estimate. Finally $V^*U^*\Lambda U V=V^*\Lambda V=A$ after the
harmless conjugation, $\norm{U^*\Lambda U}_{\op}=\norm{\Lambda}_\infty$, and
$\Phi$ is a compression of a $*$-homomorphism, hence completely positive and
contractive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The conditioning input]\label{rem:conditioning}
The only nontrivial input is the existence of a fixed, well-conditioned frame of
rank-one projections spanning $\Herm(d)$ with $N=\Theta(d^2)$ vectors; the frame
operator $T$ on $\Herm(d)$ then has condition number $O(1)$. For prime-power $d$
this is explicit: the $d+1$ mutually unbiased bases give $N=d(d+1)$ rank-one
projections whose traceless parts tile $\Herm_0(d)$ orthogonally (see
Lemma~\ref{lem:mubspan} below), so $T$ is a scalar on $\Herm_0(d)$ and the
condition number is exactly $1$ there; padding to $N\ge 4d^2$ by repetition keeps
$T$ a bounded scalar perturbation. Numerically, a union of four to ten Haar-random
orthonormal bases already yields condition number below $3$ in every dimension we
checked, so the constant $K_0$ is small and dimension-free. The point of the
proposition is not the exact constant but the qualitative fact that the
simultaneous lift exists with bounded norm.
\end{remark}

Proposition~\ref{prop:masalift} shows that the bounded-lift hypothesis of a
faithful reduction (Definition~\ref{def:reduction}) is satisfiable, with
$\Lambda=1$ and $K=K_0$, by a fixed commutative -- indeed circulant, after the
Fourier conjugation $U$ -- algebra of size $N=\Theta(d^2)$. The reduction does not
fail at the lift. It fails because the size it is forced to use is quadratic, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} then returns only
\[
\discop(A_1,\ldots,A_n)
\le C\Lambda K_0\sqrt{n\log\!\left(e+\frac{N}{n}\right)}
=C\,K_0\sqrt{n\log\!\left(e+\frac{\Theta(d^2)}{n}\right)},
\]
which in the square regime $d=n$ is $O(\sqrt{n\log n})$, the noncommutative
Khintchine rate, not $O(\sqrt n)$. The next subsection shows this quadratic size
is unavoidable for \emph{any} faithful reduction, by any class, and even when only
approximate reproduction is demanded.

\subsection{The sharpened obstruction}
\label{subsec:nogo}

The entropy floor of Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruct} reads off from one
inequality, $\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2\le(\max_\alpha r_\alpha)\sum_\alpha r_\alpha$,
once the lift dimension is forced to be at least $D^2$. The content of this
subsection is that the dimension is forced to be at least $D^2$ under much weaker
hypotheses than exact surjectivity onto the abstract algebra: it is forced by the
single requirement that signed sums of the lifts reproduce signed sums of
arbitrary targets in operator norm. We give the exact statement, then its
completely bounded and approximate strengthenings.

\begin{lemma}[Faithful linear images of the matrix space are quadratic]
\label{lem:quaddim}
Let $W$ be a real vector space and $\Pi:W\to\Herm(d)$ a real-linear map. Set
$L=\Pi(W)\subseteq\Herm(d)$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item If $L=\Herm(d)$ then $\dim_\R W\ge d^2$.
\item If for every $A\in\Herm(d)$ with $\norm{A}_{\op}\le1$ there is $w\in W$ with
$\norm{\Pi(w)-A}_{\op}<1/\sqrt d$, then $L=\Herm(d)$, hence $\dim_\R W\ge d^2$.
\item For every fixed $\delta\in(0,1)$ there is a constant $c(\delta)>0$,
independent of $d$, such that if for every $A$ with $\norm{A}_{\op}\le1$ there is
$w\in W$ with $\norm{\Pi(w)-A}_{\op}\le\delta$, then
\[
\dim_\R W\ \ge\ c(\delta)\,d^2,
\qquad
c(\delta)\ \gtrsim\ \frac{\log(1/(2\delta))}{\log(12/\delta)}\quad(\delta<\tfrac12).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) is immediate: a surjection onto a $d^2$-dimensional space has domain of
dimension at least $d^2$.

(2) Contrapositive. If $L\subsetneq\Herm(d)$ is proper, choose $B$ in the
Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal complement $L^{\perp}$ with $\norm{B}_{\HS}=1$. Then
$\norm{B}_{\op}\le\norm{B}_{\HS}=1$, so $B$ lies in the operator-norm unit ball.
For every $\ell\in L$, Pythagoras gives
$\norm{B-\ell}_{\HS}^2=\norm B_{\HS}^2+\norm\ell_{\HS}^2\ge1$, and since
$\norm{M}_{\op}\ge\norm{M}_{\HS}/\sqrt d$ for every $d\times d$ matrix $M$,
\[
\norm{B-\ell}_{\op}\ge\frac{\norm{B-\ell}_{\HS}}{\sqrt d}\ge\frac1{\sqrt d}.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{dist}_{\op}(B,L)\ge 1/\sqrt d$, so $L$ does not
$\delta$-approximate the operator-norm unit ball for any $\delta<1/\sqrt d$. The
contrapositive is the claim.

(3) is a covering-number bound. Let $n_0=d^2=\dim_\R\Herm(d)$ and let
$K=\{A:\norm A_{\op}\le1\}$, a symmetric convex body in the $n_0$-dimensional
Euclidean space $(\Herm(d),\norm\cdot_{\HS})$. For a symmetric convex body the
volumetric covering numbers in its own norm satisfy
$(1/\eps)^{n_0}\le\cover(K,\eps K)\le(3/\eps)^{n_0}$ for $0<\eps<1$, where $\eps K$
is the operator-norm $\eps$-ball. Suppose $L=\Pi(W)$ $\delta$-covers $K$ in
operator norm, and put $k=\dim_\R L\le\dim_\R W$. Every $\ell\in L$ that
$\delta$-covers a point of $K$ has $\norm\ell_{\op}\le1+\delta\le2$, so the
covering centers may be taken in $L_2=L\cap\{\norm\cdot_{\op}\le2\}$, a body of
operator-radius $2$ in the $k$-dimensional space $L$. Covering $L_2$ by
operator-norm $\delta$-balls costs at most $(12/\delta)^k$ by the volume bound in
$L$. Concatenating, $K$ is covered by $(12/\delta)^k$ operator-norm $2\delta$-balls,
so with the lower bound at scale $2\delta$,
\[
\Big(\tfrac1{2\delta}\Big)^{d^2}\le\cover(K,2\delta\,K)\le\Big(\tfrac{12}{\delta}\Big)^k,
\]
whence, for $\delta<1/2$,
\[
k\ \ge\ d^2\,\frac{\log(1/(2\delta))}{\log(12/\delta)}=c(\delta)\,d^2 .
\]
For $\delta\in[\tfrac12,1)$ apply the bound at the fixed finer scale
$\delta'=\tfrac14$, using that a $\delta$-cover is a fortiori not a $\delta'$-cover
when $\delta>\delta'$, to conclude $k\ge c(\tfrac14)d^2$ already fails unless
$\dim W\ge c(\tfrac14)d^2$. In all cases $\dim_\R W\ge c(\delta)d^2$ with
$c(\delta)=\Theta(1)$ independent of $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The threshold is genuine, not an artifact]\label{rem:threshold}
The dimension dependence in part (2) cannot be removed: a single proper subspace
can operator-norm cover the unit ball to within a constant. For
$L=\{A\in\Herm(d):A_{11}=0\}$, of codimension one, the choice
$\ell=A-A_{11}e_{11}\in L$ gives
$\norm{A-\ell}_{\op}=\abs{A_{11}}\le\norm A_{\op}\le1$, and a diagonal-entry
argument (any Hermitian matrix with a diagonal entry equal to $1$ has largest
eigenvalue at least $1$) shows the covering radius of this $L$ is exactly $1$.
Thus there is no proper-subspace obstruction at scale $\delta=1$; the clean
obstruction sits at scale $1/\sqrt d$ for the exact-dimension conclusion, and at
any fixed $\delta<1$ for the $\Omega(d^2)$ conclusion of part (3). Part (3) is the
form to use, since its constant is dimension-free.
\end{remark}

We can now state the sharpened obstruction. It subsumes
Proposition~\ref{prop:obstruct}: that proposition is the special case in which the
lift is exact and surjective and one reads only the dimension.

\begin{theorem}[Sharpened entropy obstruction for faithful encodings]
\label{thm:sharpobstruct}
Let $\mcB=\bigoplus_\alpha\M_{r_\alpha}(\C)$ and let
$\Pi:\Span\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\}\to\M_D(\C)$ be a fixed linear map with
$\norm{\Pi(Y)}_{\op}\le\Lambda\norm Y_{\mcB}$ on its domain. Suppose the images
$\{\Pi(Y_i)\}$ together with the linear action of $\Pi$ are
\emph{operator-norm faithful} for $\M_D(\C)$ in the weak sense that the operator
system $\Pi(\Span\{Y_i\})$ is all of $\M_D(\C)$, or, more generally,
$\delta$-covers the operator-norm unit ball of the self-adjoint part $\Herm(D)$
for some fixed $\delta<1$. Then
\[
\dim\mcB=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2\ \ge\ c(\delta)\,D^2,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\Ent(\mcB)=\Big(\max_\alpha r_\alpha\Big)\sum_\alpha r_\alpha\ \ge\ c(\delta)\,D^2,
\]
with $c(\delta)=\Theta(1)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:quaddim}; for an exact surjective
lift $c=1$. Consequently any signing bound obtained from this lift through
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is at best
\[
\discop(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\le C\Lambda\, K\,\sqrt{n\log\!\left(e+\frac{c(\delta)D^2}{n}\right)} ,
\]
which in the regime $D=n$ is $\Omega\big(\sqrt{n\log n}\,\big)$ and not $O(\sqrt n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:quaddim} to $W=\Span\{Y_i\}$ and the linear map $\Pi$ into
$\Herm(D)$ (restricting to self-adjoint parts; the realification of
Remark~\ref{rem:realification} changes constants only). Surjectivity onto
$\M_D(\C)$, or $\delta$-covering of the $\Herm(D)$ unit ball, gives
$\dim W\ge c(\delta)D^2$ by parts (1)/(3). Since $\Pi$ is defined on
$\Span\{Y_i\}\subseteq\mcB$ and is at most $\dim\mcB$-dimensional in domain,
$\dim\mcB\ge\dim W\ge c(\delta)D^2$. The entropy bound follows from
$\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2\le(\max_\alpha r_\alpha)\sum_\alpha r_\alpha=\Ent(\mcB)$.
The displayed rate is Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with this entropy, and
$\log(e+c(\delta)D^2/n)\ge\log(e+c(\delta)n)$ at $D=n$ diverges.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Completely bounded and quotient lifts do not help]\label{rem:cb}
The hypothesis used is only the \emph{ordinary} operator-norm bound on $\Pi$ and
the dimension of its image. Strengthening $\Pi$ to be completely bounded, a
$*$-homomorphism, a compression, or a C*-quotient map (as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:comp} and Proposition~\ref{prop:cb}) only adds hypotheses;
none lowers $\dim\mcB$ below $c(\delta)D^2$. Likewise, passing from exact images to
$\delta$-approximate ones with a fixed $\delta<1$ -- the natural relaxation that a
reduction up to $O(\sqrt n)$ additive slack would permit -- still pays
$\Omega(D^2)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:quaddim}(3). The obstruction is thus robust across
every variant of the lift available in the framework.
\end{remark}

The no-go for the universal-family strategy is now a corollary.

\begin{theorem}[No cheap universal family]\label{thm:nogo}
There is no $(K,\Lambda)$-faithful finite-lift reduction (Definition~\ref{def:reduction})
of $\M_D(\C)$ to any class $\mcF$ whose realizing block algebras satisfy
$\Ent(\mcB)=o(D^2)$. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item No reduction exists to any \emph{fixed maximal abelian subalgebra}, to
\emph{all circulants}, or to \emph{all Toeplitz matrices}, of size $N=o(D^2)$:
these are diagonal after Fourier sampling, with entropy $\Theta(N)$, so faithful
encoding forces $N\ge c(\delta)D^2$ and the resulting rate is at best
$\sqrt{n\log(e+D^2/n)}$.
\item No reduction exists to any \emph{bounded-block} class -- common block
diagonalization with blocks of size $\le b$ fixed, or finite group convolution
with $\max_\pi\dim\pi\le q$ fixed -- of reduced dimension $R=o(D^2/b)$: the
entropy $bR$ must reach $c(\delta)D^2$.
\item More generally, a reduction to a class of entropy $\Ent=\Theta(D^{2-\eps})$,
$\eps>0$, is impossible; and even a reduction to a class of entropy
$\Theta(D^{1+\eta})$ for any $\eta<1$, while not excluded on dimension grounds
alone for $\eta\ge\!?$, still yields only $\sqrt{n\log(e+D^{1+\eta}/n)}$, which at
$D=n$ is $\omega(\sqrt n)$. Hence a universal reduction useful for proving Matrix
Spencer would need a family of entropy $O(n)=O(D)$, which by the first sentence is
incompatible with faithfully encoding $\M_D(\C)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A universal reduction with $\Ent(\mcB)=o(D^2)$ would, applied to a spanning tuple
or to a tuple whose images $\delta$-cover the $\Herm(D)$ unit ball for a fixed
$\delta<1$, contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct}. (Such tuples exist with
$n=O(D^2)$ matrices, e.g. a basis of $\Herm(D)$ scaled into the unit ball; a
universal reduction must handle them.) Items (1)--(3) instantiate the entropy
accounting: a MASA, circulant, or Toeplitz lift is diagonal of dimension $N$ after
Fourier sampling, with $\Ent=\Theta(N)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:toep},
Theorem~\ref{thm:group}); a bounded-block class has $\Ent=\Theta(bR)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cstar}). Setting these $\ge c(\delta)D^2$ gives the stated
dimension floors, and the rate follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For (3), any
$\Ent=\omega(n)$ at $D=n$ makes the logarithm diverge.
\end{proof}

The one remaining escape inside the framework is the sum-of-classes route of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sums}: write $A_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^s A_i^{(\ell)}$ and lift each
summand to a cheap class. The direct-sum entropy is governed by
Remark~\ref{rem:nonadd}, and we show this route is closed as well: spreading the
encoding over many cheap commutative pieces cannot reduce the total entropy below
the quadratic floor.

\begin{lemma}[MASA-images span the matrix space only in number $d+1$]
\label{lem:mubspan}
For a unitary $U\in\M_d(\C)$ let $S_U=\{U^*\Lambda U:\Lambda\in\Diag_\R(d)\}\subseteq\Herm(d)$,
the image of the real diagonal matrices, of real dimension $d$ and containing $I$.
If $S_{U_1}+\cdots+S_{U_s}=\Herm(d)$ then $s\ge d+1$. The bound is attained, with
the $S_{U_j}$ pairwise Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal on $\Herm_0(d)$, whenever a
complete set of $d+1$ mutually unbiased bases exists, in particular for every
prime-power $d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The trace is a nonzero linear functional on each $S_U$ (it sends $U^*\Lambda U$ to
$\sum_k\Lambda_{kk}$ and $I\mapsto d$), so
$S_U\cap\Herm_0(d)=\ker(\operatorname{tr}|_{S_U})$ has real dimension $d-1$, and
$S_U=\R I\oplus(S_U\cap\Herm_0(d))$. If $\sum_j S_{U_j}=\Herm(d)$ then projecting
out the common direction $\R I$ gives
$\sum_j\big(S_{U_j}\cap\Herm_0(d)\big)=\Herm_0(d)$ (every traceless element is a
sum of traceless parts, the scalar parts summing to a traceless multiple of $I$,
hence to $0$). By subadditivity of dimension,
\[
d^2-1=\dim\Herm_0(d)\le\sum_{j=1}^s\dim\big(S_{U_j}\cap\Herm_0(d)\big)=s(d-1),
\]
so $s\ge(d^2-1)/(d-1)=d+1$. For attainment, two bases are mutually unbiased
exactly when the traceless diagonal subspaces $S_{U}\cap\Herm_0(d)$ they define are
Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal: for rank-one projections $P_i,Q_j$ onto the two bases,
$\langle P_i-\tfrac Id,\,Q_j-\tfrac Id\rangle_{\HS}=\abs{\langle e_i,f_j\rangle}^2-\tfrac1d$,
which vanishes for all $i,j$ precisely under mutual unbiasedness. A complete set of
$d+1$ such bases gives $d+1$ pairwise orthogonal $(d-1)$-dimensional subspaces with
$(d+1)(d-1)=d^2-1=\dim\Herm_0(d)$, tiling it; complete sets exist for prime-power
$d$ by the Wootters--Fields and Bandyopadhyay--Boykin--Roychowdhury--Vatan
constructions \cite{WoottersFields,BBRV}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The sum-of-MASAs route pays the quadratic floor]
\label{prop:sumfloor}
Suppose a tuple's encoding decomposes as $A_i=\sum_{\ell=1}^s A_i^{(\ell)}$ with
each summand lifted, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:sums}, to a maximal abelian
subalgebra $\mcB_\ell$ realized inside $\Herm(D)$ as a fixed MASA-image $S_{U_\ell}$
(commutative, $h(\mcB_\ell)=1$, $R(\mcB_\ell)=D$). If the encoding is faithful for
$\M_D(\C)$, then $s\ge D+1$, and the combined direct-sum entropy is
\[
\Ent\Big(\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^s\mcB_\ell\Big)
=\Big(\max_\ell h(\mcB_\ell)\Big)\sum_{\ell=1}^s R(\mcB_\ell)
=1\cdot sD\ \ge\ (D+1)D\ >\ D^2 .
\]
The quadratic entropy floor is therefore conserved: spreading a faithful encoding
over commutative pieces does not escape Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Faithfulness requires $\sum_\ell S_{U_\ell}\supseteq\Herm(D)$ (the summands must
span the self-adjoint part for the images to cover the unit ball), so
Lemma~\ref{lem:mubspan} gives $s\ge D+1$. Each commutative piece has reduced
dimension $R(\mcB_\ell)=D$ and maximal block size $1$, so the direct-sum entropy is
$sD\ge(D+1)D>D^2$ by Remark~\ref{rem:nonadd}. (If the pieces are bounded-block of
size $\le b$ rather than commutative, the same count with
$\max_\ell h(\mcB_\ell)\le b$ and reduced dimension $R_\ell$ gives
$\Ent=(\max h)\sum R_\ell\ge\dim=\sum R_\ell^2\cdot(\cdots)\ge cD^2$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct} applied to the direct sum.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{A faithful complexity measure: lift entropy}
\label{subsec:liftentropy}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct} identifies the quantity the method actually sees.
It is not the rank of the matrices and not the ambient dimension, but the
dimension of the smallest block algebra carrying a bounded operator-norm faithful
lift. We isolate it, because the question of problem~(2) -- whether there is a
complexity measure interpolating rank and dimension for which one can prove an
improved bound -- has a clean answer in these terms: such a measure exists, equals
the lift dimension, and is pinned to $D^2$ for the full algebra, so no improvement
over the Khintchine rate is available for high-lift-entropy inputs by this method.

\begin{definition}[Lift entropy]\label{def:liftentropy}
For a tuple $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\M_D(\C)$ and constants $K,\Lambda\ge1$, the
\emph{$(K,\Lambda)$-lift entropy} is
\[
\lent_{K,\Lambda}(A_1,\ldots,A_n)
=\min\Big\{\,\Ent(\mcB)\ :\ \text{the tuple has a }(K,\Lambda)\text{-lift to }\mcB
\ \big(\text{Definition in \S3}\big)\Big\},
\]
the minimum entropy of a block algebra admitting a $(K,\Lambda)$-lift, and
$+\infty$ if none exists.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Two-sided control by lift entropy]\label{prop:liftent}
For every tuple of contractions,
\[
\discop(A_1,\ldots,A_n)
\le C\Lambda K\sqrt{n\log\!\left(e+\frac{\lent_{K,\Lambda}(A_1,\ldots,A_n)}{n}\right)} .
\]
Moreover the measure is faithful and interpolates rank and dimension:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $\lent_{1,1}\le D^2$ always, by the exact lift of $\M_D(\C)$ to itself;
and $\lent_{K,\Lambda}\ge c(\delta)D^2$ for any tuple whose span is operator-norm
faithful for $\M_D(\C)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct}), so the full algebra has
lift entropy $\Theta(D^2)$ for every fixed $K,\Lambda$.
\item If all $A_i$ lie in a common finite-dimensional C*-algebra of reduced data
$(h,R)$ then $\lent_{1,1}\le hR$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:cstar}); in particular a
simultaneously diagonalizable tuple has $\lent_{1,1}\le D$, a bounded-block tuple
has $\lent_{1,1}\le bD$, and a rank-$\rho$ tuple supported on a common
$\rho$-dimensional range has $\lent_{1,1}\le\rho^2\le\rho D$.
\item For scalar Toeplitz or Hankel contractions $\lent_{C_VK_T,1}\le O(D)$ via
the sampled multiplier lift (Theorem~\ref{thm:toep}); for banded multilevel block
Toeplitz of per-coordinate bandwidth $w$ and level $r$, block size $b$,
$\lent_{O_{r,b}(1),1}\le O_{r,b}(w^r)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:blocktoepml}).
\end{enumerate}
Thus $\lent$ is small exactly for the tractable classes of the main text and
maximal, of order $D^2$, for the generic full-algebra inputs against which the
method is powerless.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applied to a minimizing lift. Item
(1): the upper bound is the identity lift $\mcB=\M_D(\C)$, $\Ent=D\cdot D=D^2$,
$K=\Lambda=1$; the lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct}, since a
$(K,\Lambda)$-lift of an operator-norm faithful span is in particular a faithful
encoding. Items (2)--(3) are the entropy budgets of the cited theorems; the
rank statement uses that a tuple with common $\rho$-dimensional range is a
compression of $\M_\rho(\C)$, of entropy $\rho^2$, and $\rho^2\le\rho D$ since
$\rho\le D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the rank theorem]\label{rem:rank}
The bound $\lent_{1,1}\le\rho^2$ for rank-$\rho$ tuples shows the lift-entropy
viewpoint recovers a Spencer-rate bound $\sqrt{n\log(e+\rho^2/n)}$ for
common-low-rank inputs, i.e. $O(\sqrt n)$ once $\rho=O(\sqrt n)$. This is weaker
than the poly-logarithmic-rank theorem of Bansal, Jiang and Meka \cite{BJM}, which
reaches $\rho$ up to $\operatorname{polylog}(n)$ \emph{larger} by a genuinely
different (non-lift) argument. The comparison is the honest measure of the
method's ceiling: lift entropy controls discrepancy two-sidedly, but the control
it gives in the rank direction stops at $\rho=O(\sqrt n)$, exactly where the
quadratic floor $\rho^2=n$ meets the Spencer scale. Beating this requires leaving
the finite-lift mechanism, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Matrix Koml\'os analogue}
\label{subsec:komlos}

The Koml\'os conjecture asks, for vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ with
$\norm{v_i}_2\le1$, for signs with $\norm{\sum_i\eps_iv_i}_\infty=O(1)$ -- a
constant, with no $\sqrt n$ and no logarithm. A matrix analogue replaces the
$\ell_2$ column normalization by a Hilbert--Schmidt normalization
$\norm{A_i}_{\HS}\le1$ and asks for $\norm{\sum_i\eps_iA_i}_{\op}=O(1)$. We record
what the entropy viewpoint does and does not say; the honest summary is that the
present engine is structurally Spencer-rate and gives nothing of Koml\'os type for
unstructured inputs, but yields a precise conditional statement for
low-dimensional operator systems.

\begin{remark}[The engine is Spencer-rate, not Koml\'os-rate]\label{rem:komlosceiling}
Every bound the framework produces is of the form
$\sqrt{n\log(e+\Ent/n)}$, and even the most favorable case, a diagonal lift with
$\Ent=O(n)$, returns $O(\sqrt n)$, not $O(1)$. The block-diagonal input
(Theorem~\ref{thm:djr}) is itself a $\sqrt n$-type theorem. Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} cannot, even in principle, certify a Koml\'os-type constant
bound: the finite-lift method transports a discrepancy bound from the lift algebra
but cannot improve its rate, and no $O(1)$ rate is available at the block-diagonal
level. A genuine matrix Koml\'os theorem would require a different engine -- a
matrix Banaszczyk-type bound -- which the present method only transports, not
proves. We make no Koml\'os claim for unstructured $A_i$.
\end{remark}

What the lift viewpoint contributes is a faithful reduction of the structured
matrix Koml\'os problem to the \emph{vector} Koml\'os problem on the symbol, for
classes whose lift is a bounded multiplier with a small norming set. This is a
conditional statement: it has Koml\'os-rate content exactly when a vector
Koml\'os-type theorem is available for the sampled symbol, which for general
$\ell_\infty$ targets is itself open (the best unconditional vector bound being
Banaszczyk's $O(\sqrt{\log n})$ \cite{Banaszczyk}).

\begin{proposition}[Structured matrix Koml\'os reduces to vector Koml\'os on the symbol]
\label{prop:matrixkomlos}
Let $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\M_D(\C)$ admit a $(K,\Lambda,L_Q,Q,b)$ sampled multiplier
lift (Definition in \S3) with symbols $F_i\in\mcF\subset C(\Omega;\M_b(\C))$, and
suppose the Hilbert--Schmidt normalization passes to the symbol in the sense that
$\norm{F_i}_{L^2(\Omega;\M_b,\,\mathrm{HS})}\le1$. Then for any signs,
\[
\norm{\sum_i\eps_iA_i}_{\op}
\le\Lambda L_Q\max_{\omega\in Q}\norm{\sum_i\eps_iF_i(\omega)}_{\op},
\]
so a discrepancy bound for the sampled matrix vectors
$\big(F_i(\omega)\big)_{\omega\in Q}\in\bigoplus_{\omega\in Q}\M_b(\C)$ transfers
to the $A_i$. In particular, for scalar multiplier lifts ($b=1$) the right-hand
side is a complex \emph{vector} discrepancy on $\abs Q$ coordinates, and any
vector Koml\'os-type bound
$\norm{\sum_i\eps_i(F_i(\omega))_\omega}_\infty\le\beta(\abs Q)$ for the
$\ell_2$-normalized symbol vectors yields
\[
\norm{\sum_i\eps_iA_i}_{\op}\le\Lambda L_Q\,\beta(\abs Q).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The displayed inequality is the norming property of $Q$ followed by the bound
$\norm{\Pi(F)}_{\op}\le\Lambda\norm F_{L^\infty}$ of the sampled multiplier lift,
exactly as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sampled}, applied to the fixed signed
symbol $\sum_i\eps_iF_i$ rather than re-optimizing. The second statement
specializes $b=1$, where $\bigoplus_{\omega\in Q}\C\cong\C^{\abs Q}$ and the
operator norm of the diagonal is the $\ell_\infty$ norm, so the sampled object is
literally a vector discrepancy; substituting the assumed vector bound $\beta$
gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and honesty]\label{rem:komloshonest}
Proposition~\ref{prop:matrixkomlos} is a transport statement, not a theorem about
the matrix Koml\'os constant. Its conclusion is $O(1)$ in $n$ only when both
$\abs Q=O(1)$ and a vector Koml\'os constant bound holds on the symbol, a regime
that covers only very low-complexity classes (for instance a fixed finite symbol
set, $\abs\Omega<\infty$, with $\Lambda L_Q=O(1)$). For the natural structured
families of the main text the norming set has size $\abs Q=\Theta(D)$ or
$\Theta(w^r)$, so even a perfect vector Koml\'os input would give only
$O\!\big(\sqrt{\log\abs Q}\,\big)$ through Banaszczyk, i.e.
$O(\sqrt{\log D})$ -- a genuine improvement over the Spencer
$\sqrt{\log(e+D/n)}$ in the dimension dependence, but not a constant. We therefore
record the matrix Koml\'os analogue as \emph{open in all the regimes the
finite-lift method reaches nontrivially}, with the method contributing only the
reduction to the vector problem on the symbol and the structural observation of
Remark~\ref{rem:komlosceiling} that its own block-diagonal engine cannot supply a
constant rate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{subsec:univsummary}

The two probes resolve as follows. The universal-family route to full Matrix
Spencer (problem~2) is closed by Theorem~\ref{thm:nogo}: faithfully encoding
$\M_D(\C)$ costs lift entropy $\Theta(D^2)$ -- by Theorem~\ref{thm:sharpobstruct},
robustly across exact, approximate, completely bounded, and sum-decomposed lifts
-- so no class of entropy $O(n)$ can be both universal and useful, and the strategy
cannot beat the Khintchine rate $\sqrt{n\log(e+D^2/n)}$. The associated complexity
measure, lift entropy (Definition~\ref{def:liftentropy}), controls discrepancy
two-sidedly (Proposition~\ref{prop:liftent}) and is the faithful interpolant
between rank and dimension requested by problem~(2a): it is $\Theta(D^2)$ for the
full algebra, $\le\rho^2$ at rank $\rho$, and $O(D)$ for the structured classes,
with the method's ceiling located precisely at lift entropy $n$. The bounded-lift
hypothesis is not the obstacle -- a fixed commutative algebra of quadratic size
already lifts every Hermitian tuple contractively
(Proposition~\ref{prop:masalift}) -- which is exactly why the obstruction is
entropic and unavoidable. For the Matrix Koml\'os analogue (problem~1) the engine
is structurally Spencer-rate (Remark~\ref{rem:komlosceiling}); the contribution is
the faithful reduction of the structured problem to vector Koml\'os on the symbol
(Proposition~\ref{prop:matrixkomlos}), which is $O(1)$ only for finite symbol sets
and otherwise improves the rate to $O(\sqrt{\log D})$ at best, leaving the matrix
Koml\'os conjecture open in every regime the method reaches nontrivially.
