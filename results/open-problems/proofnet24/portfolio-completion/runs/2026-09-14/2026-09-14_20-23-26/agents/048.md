## Results and limitations

This bounded attack yields three focused results.

1. **PROVED: exact centered-inverse L-ensemble Hessian.** For an affine kernel path, let `M=I-K`, `R=M^{-1}`, `L=R-I`, `B=RAR`, and let `Z_S` be the principal inverse `(L_S)^{-1}` embedded into the full point space. With `Delta_S=Z_S-M`,

   \[
   s_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB),\qquad
   \kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB\Delta_SB),
   \]

   the exact score and second atom derivative satisfy

   \[
   p_S'/p_S=s_S,\quad p_S''/p_S=s_S^2-\kappa_S,\quad
   \mathbb E\kappa=\mathbb E s^2=\mathcal I.
   \]

   This identity includes the full compulsory term `L''=2RARAR`. It is valid for arbitrary Hermitian directions, even when `kappa_S` is not pointwise nonnegative.

2. **PROVED: a nontrivial class of semidefinite directions in every dimension.** If `A` is semidefinite of rank `r>=2`, and `W=log(max_S p_S/min_S p_S)`, then

   \[
   \boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]\le
   -\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r}W\right)\mathcal I_K(A).}
   \tag{A}
   \]

   Thus entropy is directionally concave for every arbitrarily oriented semidefinite rank-`r` direction whenever `W<=r/(r-1)`. An explicit global consequence in three sites is: if

   \[
   \tfrac7{20}I\le K_0,K_1\le\tfrac{13}{20}I,
   \qquad K_1-K_0\text{ is semidefinite of rank two},
   \]

   then their entire affine segment satisfies entropy concavity, with the quantitative margin in (15) below. No fixed point-coordinate block, commuting eigenbasis, equal-cardinality atoms, or scalar-ray assumption is imposed.

3. **DISPROVED: domination using only the score projection onto one-point indicators.** The natural sufficient inequality `I_1>=C`, where `I_1` is the exact Fisher cost of that linear projection and `C=-sum p_S'' log p_S`, fails by an unbounded factor near a rank-one three-site kernel. A noncommuting rational positive rank-two example has `I_1<452/25` and `C>544/25`, while its actual entropy Hessian is less than `-269960`. The exact remaining residual-score comparison is stated in (19).

The unrestricted Lyons conjecture is **not solved**, even for all semidefinite directions. Formula (A) loses its force when the atom ratio is large. No historical novelty or priority claim is made. The proof below does not use the known rank-one or cardinality entropy concavity theorems to infer its new restricted class.

## 1. The full affine-kernel Hessian in L coordinates

Fix an interior complex Hermitian kernel `0<K<I` of size `n` and any Hermitian direction `A`. A sufficiently small open interval around zero has `0<K+tA<I`. Set

\[
M=I-K,\qquad R=M^{-1},\qquad L=K(I-K)^{-1}=R-I.
\tag{1}
\]

For a subset `S`, let `E_S` denote the coordinate inclusion matrix, put `L_S=E_S^*LE_S`, and define

\[
Z_S=E_S(L_S)^{-1}E_S^*,\qquad Z_\varnothing=0.
\tag{2}
\]

All nonempty principal matrices `L_S` are positive definite. The standard L-ensemble formula is

\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det(I+L)}=\frac{\det L_S}{\det R}>0.
\tag{3}
\]

The point basis remains fixed. An algebraic square root or spectral norm estimate below never replaces the measured subset law by that of a unitarily rotated kernel.

### 1.1 The nonlinear acceleration is compulsory

Differentiate the inverse in (1) along `K+tA`:

\[
L'=RAR=:B,\qquad
L''=2RARAR=:2C,\qquad C=BMB.
\tag{4}
\]

`B` is Hermitian, and `C>=0` even when `A` is indefinite, since `C=BMB` and `M>0`.

For shortness define the real scalars

\[
\begin{aligned}
g_S&=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB),&
d_S&=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB Z_SB),&
c_S&=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SC),\\
\alpha&=\operatorname{tr}(MB),&
\gamma&=\operatorname{tr}(MBMB).
\end{aligned}
\]

Log-determinant differentiation gives

\[
(\log\det L_S)'=g_S,\quad
(\log\det L_S)''=2c_S-d_S,
\]

whereas

\[
(\log\det R)'=\alpha,\qquad
(\log\det R)''=\gamma.
\]

The latter second derivative equals `gamma`, not `-gamma`: the curve is affine in `K`, so both terms in (4) must be retained. Consequently,

\[
\frac{p_S'}{p_S}=g_S-\alpha,
\qquad
\frac{p_S''}{p_S}=(g_S-\alpha)^2+2c_S-d_S-\gamma.
\tag{5}
\]

### 1.2 Centering absorbs exactly the acceleration

Put

\[
\Delta_S=Z_S-M,\qquad
s_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB),\qquad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB\Delta_SB).
\]

Cyclicity of trace and `C=BMB` give

\[
\kappa_S=d_S-2c_S+\gamma.
\]

Therefore (5) becomes the particularly simple exact formula

\[
\boxed{\frac{p_S'}{p_S}=s_S,\qquad
\frac{p_S''}{p_S}=\tau_S:=s_S^2-\kappa_S.}
\tag{6}
\]

In particular the subtraction of `M` inside both copies of `Delta_S` is exactly what accounts for `L''`; it is not an assumption that `L` is affine.

Normalization and differentiation imply

\[
\mathbb Es=0,\qquad \mathbb E\tau=0,\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb E\kappa=\mathbb Es^2=\mathcal I_K(A),}
\tag{7}
\]

where `I_K(A)=sum (p_S')^2/p_S` is the full atom Fisher information. For arbitrary Hermitian `A`, an individual `kappa_S` can have either sign, but its expectation in (7) is nonnegative.

For completeness, differentiating `sum_S det L_S=det(I+L)` in an arbitrary Hermitian L-direction proves the matrix identity

\[
\mathbb E Z_S=M.
\]

The same determinant differentiation and (7) also yield

\[
\mathbb E c=\gamma,\qquad \mathbb E d=\mathcal I+\gamma.
\]

These identities are checked exactly in the accompanying script.

Twice differentiating Shannon entropy, using normalization, now gives

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\mathcal I_K(A)-\mathbb E[(\log p_S)\tau_S]
=-\mathcal I_K(A)-\operatorname{Cov}(\log\det L_S,\tau_S).
}
\tag{8}
\]

The covariance form is valid because `E tau=0`, and `log p` differs from `log det L_S` by a constant. Formula (8) is the exact full-entropy obligation; no term has a universally favorable sign merely because `C>=0`.

One may also keep the coordinate correction separate. If `F(L)` denotes the entropy of the L-ensemble, then

\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=D^2F(L)[B,B]+2DF(L)[C],
\qquad
2DF(L)[C]=-2\operatorname{Cov}(\log p_S,c_S).
\tag{9}
\]

The last term is strictly **positive** in the rational example below, approximately `20.01877729315`. Thus deleting it as if it were a favorable acceleration would be incorrect in a genuine three-site positive-plane direction.

## 2. Semidefinite directions with bounded atom ratio

### 2.1 A quantitative theorem

Suppose first `A>=0` has rank `r>=2`. Then `B=RAR>=0` has the same rank. Define the random Hermitian matrix

\[
Y_S=B^{1/2}\Delta_SB^{1/2}.
\]

Its rank is at most `r`, and cyclicity gives

\[
s_S=\operatorname{tr}Y_S,\qquad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(Y_S^2)\ge0.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on its at most `r` nonzero real eigenvalues gives

\[
0\le s_S^2\le r\kappa_S,
\qquad
-\kappa_S\le\tau_S\le(r-1)\kappa_S.
\tag{10}
\]

Let `tau_+=max(tau,0)` and `tau_-=max(-tau,0)`. Since the two supports are disjoint, (10) implies pointwise

\[
\kappa\ge\frac{\tau_+}{r-1}+\tau_-.
\]

By (7), `E tau_+=E tau_-=m_tau`. Taking expectations therefore proves

\[
\boxed{m_\tau\le\frac{r-1}{r}\mathcal I.}
\tag{11}
\]

Let `ell_min=min_S log p_S`, `ell_max=max_S log p_S`, and `W=ell_max-ell_min`. The equality of the positive and negative masses gives

\[
-\mathbb E[(\log p_S)\tau_S]
\le Wm_\tau
\le W\frac{r-1}{r}\mathcal I.
\]

Substitution in (8) proves

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le
-\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r}\log\frac{\max_Sp_S}{\min_Sp_S}\right)
\mathcal I_K(A).
}
\tag{12}
\]

If `A<=0`, replace `A` by `-A`; both the second variation and Fisher information are unchanged. This proves (12) for all semidefinite directions of rank `r>=2`, including complex directions.

A nonzero semidefinite direction has positive Fisher information: differentiating `E|X|=tr K` gives `E(|X|s)=tr A!=0`. Thus (12) is strictly negative when `W<r/(r-1)`.

For rank two, (10) simply says `|tau|<=kappa`, and (11) gives `E tau_+<=I/2`. The threshold is `W<=2`, equivalently `max p/min p<=e^2`. Rank one has `tau=0` pointwise and recovers the already known rank-one theorem; that case is not claimed as a new result.

### 2.2 A convex spectral region on which the criterion persists along segments

Assume

\[
\tfrac12I-\delta I\le K\le\tfrac12I+\delta I,
\qquad 0\le\delta<\tfrac12.
\]

Write `E=K-I/2` and let `J_S` be diagonal with entries `+1` on `S`, `-1` off `S`. The exact mixed-column atom matrix is

\[
F_S=\tfrac12I+EJ_S.
\]

Because `||EJ_S||=||E||<=delta`, every singular value of `F_S` lies between `1/2-delta` and `1/2+delta`. Its determinant is the positive probability `p_S`, so

\[
(\tfrac12-\delta)^n\le p_S\le(\tfrac12+\delta)^n,
\qquad
W\le n\log\frac{1+2\delta}{1-2\delta}.
\tag{13}
\]

This uses singular values of the actual atom matrices, not invariance of subset entropy under a basis change.

Consequently (12) applies uniformly to semidefinite rank-`r` directions on the convex spectral band whenever

\[
2n\operatorname{atanh}(2\delta)\le\frac r{r-1},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\delta\le\tfrac12\tanh\!\left(\frac{r}{2n(r-1)}\right).
\tag{14}
\]

If two endpoints lie in this band and their difference is semidefinite of rank `r`, their entire segment lies in the same band. Integrating (12) proves the global entropy chord inequality for that pair. This is a sufficient class of ordered endpoint pairs, not full concavity of entropy on the band in arbitrary indefinite directions.

For a simple rational three-site band take `delta=3/20`. Since

\[
\log(13/7)=2\operatorname{atanh}(3/10)
<\frac{2(3/10)}{1-(3/10)^2}=\frac{60}{91},
\]

(13) gives `W<180/91<2`. Thus for every three-site kernel with `7I/20<=K<=13I/20` and every semidefinite rank-two direction,

\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\frac1{91}\mathcal I_K(A).
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz for the score of `|X|` gives

\[
\mathcal I_K(A)\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{\operatorname{Var}|X|}
=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{\operatorname{tr}K(I-K)}
\ge\frac43(\operatorname{tr}A)^2.
\]

For endpoints as in the summary, put `A=K_1-K_0`. Integration yields the explicit global strengthening

\[
\boxed{
H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)
\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
+\frac{2(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{273}t(1-t),
\quad 0\le t\le1.
}
\tag{15}
\]

## 3. The one-point score projection and its exact obstruction

### 3.1 The natural proposed Fisher lower bound

Let `xi_i=1_{i in X}-K_ii`, `a_i=A_ii`, and let `Sigma=Cov(xi)` be the covariance of inclusion indicators. DPP two-point probabilities give

\[
\Sigma_{ii}=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}),\qquad
\Sigma_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Since every atom has positive probability, no nonzero linear combination of these indicators is constant, so `Sigma` is positive definite. Differentiating the fixed statistic `1_{i in X}` gives `E(xi_i s)=a_i`. Thus the orthogonal projection of the full score onto the linear span of the centered one-point indicators is

\[
s_1=a^T\Sigma^{-1}\xi,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal I_1=\mathbb Es_1^2=a^T\Sigma^{-1}a\le\mathcal I.}
\tag{17}
\]

This is a **linear score projection**. It is not conditioning on the full vector of inclusion indicators, which would reveal the entire configuration and lose no information.

Let

\[
\mathcal C=-\sum_Sp_S''\log p_S
=-\mathbb E[(\log p_S)\tau_S].
\]

A natural sufficient inequality would be

\[
\mathcal I_1\ge\mathcal C.
\tag{18}
\]

It would prove `H''=-I+C<=0` using a tractable covariance inverse. In fact (17) is the best Fisher lower bound obtainable by optimizing an arbitrary linear combination of the one-point indicators, so testing (18) examines the whole such projection, not merely separate marginal variances.

By Pythagoras and (8), the exact remaining obligation after this projection is

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-
\mathcal I_1-\mathbb E(s-s_1)^2+\mathcal C,
\qquad
\mathbb E(s-s_1)^2\ge\mathcal C-\mathcal I_1.
}
\tag{19}
\]

The inequality in (19) is necessary and sufficient for directional concavity. The report does not assert it universally; the spectral-band theorem proves a sufficient comparison in its stated region.

### 3.2 No finite multiple of the projected Fisher cost works universally

The failure is systematic. In three sites fix `0<lambda<1`, a unit vector `v` with every coordinate nonzero, and any unit vector `z`. Put

\[
K_\varepsilon=\varepsilon I+(\lambda-\varepsilon)vv^*,
\qquad A=I-zz^*,\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\lambda.
\tag{20}
\]

The kernel eigenvalues are `lambda,epsilon,epsilon`; the direction is positive of rank two. Set `h_i=|z_i|^2` and

\[
q_\varepsilon=z^*K_\varepsilon z
=\varepsilon+(\lambda-\varepsilon)|v^*z|^2.
\]

The three-dimensional determinant coefficient identity, using `adj A=zz*`, gives

\[
p_S''=2(-1)^{3-|S|}
\left(q_\varepsilon-\sum_{i\notin S}h_i\right).
\tag{21}
\]

In particular, the sums of the coefficients `p_S''/2` over pairs and over the full set are respectively `1-3q_epsilon` and `q_epsilon`.

Write `b_i=|v_i|^2>0`. Empty and singleton atom probabilities have positive limits, whereas

\[
p_{123}=\lambda\varepsilon^2,
\qquad
p_{ij}=\lambda\varepsilon(1-b_k)
+\varepsilon^2(b_k-\lambda),\quad \{i,j,k\}=[3].
\]

Thus every pair atom is a positive constant times `epsilon` to first order. Using (21) in the definition of `C` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(K_\varepsilon,A)
=2\big(1-\lambda|v^*z|^2\big)\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(1)
\longrightarrow+\infty.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Indeed the coefficient of `-2 log epsilon` is `(1-3q)+2q=1-q`, and `q_epsilon` tends to `lambda|v*z|^2<1`.

Meanwhile,

\[
\Sigma\longrightarrow\Sigma_0
=\lambda\operatorname{diag}(b)-\lambda^2bb^T>0,
\]

and elementary inversion gives

\[
\Sigma_0^{-1}=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(1/b_i)
+(1-\lambda)^{-1}\boldsymbol1\boldsymbol1^T.
\]

For `a_i=1-|z_i|^2` and `sum a_i=2`, therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I_1(K_\varepsilon,A)\longrightarrow
\lambda^{-1}\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{b_i}
+\frac4{1-\lambda}<\infty.
}
\tag{23}
\]

The limit is positive. Equations (22)--(23) prove that `C/I_1` tends to infinity. No fixed finite multiple of this entire one-point score projection can dominate the adverse logarithmic curvature for all positive rank-two directions. This obstruction holds without cardinality-uniform atoms or commutation assumptions.

For exact numerical certification specialize to

\quad z=(2,-1,0)^T/\sqrt5,
\quad A=P\text{ from (16)}.
\tag{24}
\]

`K_epsilon` and `A` are rational matrices and do not commute. +\frac{52/25}{1+2\varepsilon-2\varepsilon^2}
