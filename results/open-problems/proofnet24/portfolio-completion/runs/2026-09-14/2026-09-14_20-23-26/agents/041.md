## 1. Main result and attribution

There is a large, explicitly described class in every number of types
\(n\ge5\) for which **the original, unaveraged iteration converges globally
to a unique fixed point**, including every boundary starting point.

Write the closed simplex and strict-parent-exclusion QSO as
\[
\Delta_{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:x_k\ge0,\ \sum_kx_k=1\},\qquad
V_P(x)_k=\sum_{i,j=1}^nP_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad(k\in\{i,j\}).                         \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P_{ij}\) denote the entire offspring probability vector for parents
\(i,j\). The exact global \(\ell^1\) Lipschitz constant is
\[
\boxed{L(P)=\max_{a,i,j}\|P_{ai}-P_{aj}\|_1.}           \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the following finite coefficient condition suffices:
\[
\min_{a,i,j}\sum_k\min(P_{ai,k},P_{aj,k})>\tfrac12.     \tag{3}
\]
It requires that every two offspring distributions sharing one parent have
more than half their probability mass in common. It is not an assumption
about an unknown fixed point or Jacobian along an unknown trajectory.

For the equiprobable tensor \(E\),
\[
E_{ii,k}=\frac1{n-1}\ (k\ne i),\qquad
E_{ij,k}=\frac1{n-2}\ (i,j,k\text{ distinct}),           \tag{4}
\]
with the excluded entries zero, one has **exactly**
\[
L(E)=\frac2{n-2}.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Consequently each of the following is an explicit sufficient class:

1. **Rowwise perturbation neighborhood.** If
   \[
   \varepsilon=\max_{i,j}\|P_{ij}-E_{ij}\|_1
      <R_n:=\frac{n-4}{2(n-2)},                        \tag{6}
   \]
   then \(L(P)\le q_n:=2/(n-2)+2\varepsilon<1\).
2. **Uniform mixing with arbitrary strict coefficients.** If
   \[
   P=(1-\theta)E+\theta Q,\quad Q\text{ satisfies (1)},\qquad
   0\le\theta<\frac{n-4}{2(n-3)},                      \tag{7}
   \]
   then
   \[
   L(P)\le\frac2{n-2}+\frac{2(n-3)}{n-2}\theta<1.      \tag{8}
   \]
   Equivalently, it is enough that every allowed coefficient satisfy
   \(P_{ij,k}\ge\alpha E_{ij,k}\), for some
   \(\alpha>(n-2)/(2(n-3))\).

Both thresholds are sharp for their respective *uniform guarantees of
strict global \(\ell^1\) contraction*, as established below. This does not
claim sharp thresholds for uniqueness or convergence by other methods.

| Types \(n\) | Exact \(L(E)\) | Sufficient row error \(\varepsilon<R_n\) | Sufficient arbitrary share \(\theta\) in (7) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | \(2/3\) | \(1/6\) | \(<1/4\) |
| 6 | \(1/2\) | \(1/4\) | \(<1/3\) |
| 7 | \(2/5\) | \(3/10\) | \(<3/8\) |
| 10 | \(1/4\) | \(3/8\) | \(<3/7\) |

the contraction conclusion, and the general \(2\varepsilon\) perturbation
bound are established theory: after translating indices, they occur in
Fasino–Tudisco, *Ergodicity coefficients for higher-order stochastic
processes*, arXiv:1907.04841v3, Corollary 5.2, Theorem 5.5, and Theorem 6.1.
Their exact tensor coefficient reduces to (2) under parental symmetry.

## 2. Proof of the exact coefficient criterion

This section applies to all parent-symmetric stochastic tensors; exclusion
of the parents is not needed yet. Set
\[
A_P(m)_{ki}=\sum_aP_{ai,k}m_a,\qquad
T=\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i z_i=0\}.
\]
The matrix \(A_P(m)\) is column-stochastic, and parental symmetry gives
\[
V_P(x)-V_P(y)=2A_P((x+y)/2)(x-y),\qquad
DV_P(m)z=2A_P(m)z.                                   \tag{9}
\]

For any matrix \(B\), its induced \(\ell^1\) norm on \(T\) is
\[
\|B|_T\|_{1\to1}=\tfrac12\max_{i,j}\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1.  \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, if \(z\in T\setminus\{0\}\), put \(h=\|z\|_1/2\), let \(I_+\)
and \(I_-\) be its positive and negative index sets, and put
\(\beta_{ij}=z_i(-z_j)/h^2\). Then
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in I_+,j\in I_-}\beta_{ij}(e_i-e_j),\qquad
\beta_{ij}\ge0,\quad\sum_{ij}\beta_{ij}=1.
\]
The triangle inequality proves the upper bound in (10), and each
\(e_i-e_j\) proves the reverse bound.

For any \(m\in\Delta_{n-1}\),
\[
\|2A_P(m)|_T\|_{1\to1}
=\max_{i,j}\Big\|\sum_a m_a(P_{ai}-P_{aj})\Big\|_1
\le\max_{a,i,j}\|P_{ai}-P_{aj}\|_1.                   \tag{11}
\]
Together with (9), this proves the global Lipschitz upper bound in (2).

For equality fix \(a,i,j\), let \(u=(1/n,\ldots,1/n)\), and take
\(m_\eta=(1-\eta)e_a+\eta u\), with \(\eta>0\).
Since \(m_\eta\) is interior, both sufficiently small signed displacements
along \(e_i-e_j\) remain in the simplex. The derivative quotient gives
\[
\operatorname{Lip}_1(V_P)\ge
\frac{\|DV_P(m_\eta)(e_i-e_j)\|_1}{2}
=\|A_P(m_\eta)(e_i-e_j)\|_1.
\]
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) yields
\(\operatorname{Lip}_1(V_P)\ge\|P_{ai}-P_{aj}\|_1\).
Taking the maximum establishes (2). This argument avoids incorrectly
assuming that every tangent direction is feasible at a boundary vertex.

For probability vectors \(r,s\),
\(\|r-s\|_1=2(1-\sum_k\min(r_k,s_k))\), which proves the equivalence of
\(L(P)<1\) and (3). In particular, (3) is necessary and sufficient for
strict global contraction in this particular norm, while only sufficient
for the desired dynamical conclusions.

## 3. Equiprobable constant, including its pointwise Dobrushin formula

Let \(d_n=(n-1)(n-2)\). Direct summation gives
\[
A_E(m)_{ki}=
\begin{cases}
0,&k=i,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1-m_k}{n-2}-\frac{m_i}{d_n},&k\ne i.
\end{cases}                                         \tag{12}
\]
This agrees with the parent's proposed formula.

For distinct \(i,j\), suppose \(m_i\ge m_j\). The column difference
\(A_E(m)(e_i-e_j)\) has a nonpositive entry at \(i\), a nonnegative entry
at \(j\), and entries \((m_j-m_i)/d_n\le0\) elsewhere. Its only possible
positive contribution is \(A_E(m)_{ji}\). Since the difference has sum
zero, half its \(\ell^1\) norm is exactly that contribution.
Consequently, if \(m_{(1)}\le m_{(2)}\) are the two smallest coordinates,
\[
\boxed{\delta(A_E(m))=
\frac{(n-1)(1-m_{(1)})-m_{(2)}}{d_n}
\le\frac1{n-2}.}                                    \tag{13}
\]
Equality is attained when two coordinates of \(m\) vanish. The derivative
or coefficient argument in Section 2 converts this into the sharp global
constant \(2/(n-2)\); the factor two must not be omitted.

There is an even shorter coefficient check. If \(a,i,j\) are distinct,
the distributions \(E_{ai}\) and \(E_{aj}\) agree on \(n-3\) entries,
and differ by \(1/(n-2)\) at each of \(i,j\). Thus their distance is
\(2/(n-2)\). When \(a=i\) or \(a=j\), the distance is instead
\(2/(n-1)\). Formula (2) again proves (5).

Permutation symmetry shows that \(u\) is fixed: \(V_E(u)\) has all
coordinates equal and sum one. For \(n\ge5\), its uniqueness and global
raw convergence now follow from contraction. At \(n=4\), (5) is exactly
one; that equality neither proves nor disproves global convergence, which
can require a different argument. This report does not extend the strict
one-step contraction claim to four types.

## 4. Perturbation neighborhood and its sharp contraction radius

Set \(R_{ij}=P_{ij}-E_{ij}\) and
\[
\rho=\max_{a,i,j}\|R_{ai}-R_{aj}\|_1\le2\varepsilon.
\]
By (2),
\[
L(P)\le L(E)+\rho\le\frac2{n-2}+2\varepsilon.          \tag{14}
\]
The intermediate quantity \(\rho\), or just the exact formula (2), can be
used if actual coefficient cancellation improves the rowwise error bound.
The strict inequality in (6) proves the claimed class.

The coefficient 2 in (14) cannot be reduced uniformly, even for arbitrarily
small perturbations preserving every exclusion in (1). More strongly,
\(R_n\) is the exact radius of the largest open rowwise ball on which
strict contraction is guaranteed for every admissible tensor.

Here is an explicit witness. Fix three distinct types \(a,i,j\), put
\(C=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{a,i,j\}\), and partition \(C=B\sqcup D\)
into two nonempty sets of sizes differing by at most one. Let \(u_B,u_D\)
be uniform probability vectors on these sets. For
\[
t=\varepsilon/2\le\frac{\min(|B|,|D|)}{n-2},
\]
change only these two unordered parent rows:
\[
P_{ai}=E_{ai}+t e_j-t u_B,\qquad
P_{aj}=E_{aj}+t e_i-t u_D.                            \tag{15}
\]
Enforce parental symmetry on the reversed rows. The changed rows remain
nonnegative normalized distributions and still exclude their parents.
Each row changes by \(2t=\varepsilon\). Their difference has the original
positive/negative entries at \(j,i\), enlarged by \(t\), and disjoint new
negative/positive contributions on \(B,D\). Therefore
\[
\|P_{ai}-P_{aj}\|_1=\frac2{n-2}+4t
=\frac2{n-2}+2\varepsilon.                           \tag{16}
\]
At \(\varepsilon=R_n\), the donor-capacity condition is satisfied for every
\(n\ge5\): it reduces to
\((n-4)/4\le\lfloor(n-3)/2\rfloor\). Thus (14) and (16) give
\(L(P)=1\) at that radius. Every larger open ball contains this tensor.
For smaller positive \(\varepsilon\), the same construction also proves
the local sharpness of the coefficient 2.

This is a sharp *contraction* obstruction. It is not an example of multiple
fixed points or nonconvergent raw dynamics at the threshold.

## 5. A second explicit class: arbitrary strict tensor mixed with E

Because the expression (2) is a maximum of norms of linear coefficient
differences, it is convex in \(P\). Every stochastic \(Q\) satisfies
\(L(Q)\le2\). Thus
\[
L((1-\theta)E+\theta Q)
\le(1-\theta)\frac2{n-2}+2\theta,
\]
which is (8). If \(P\ge\alpha E\) on every allowed coefficient and
\(\alpha<1\), then \(Q=(P-\alpha E)/(1-\alpha)\) is an admissible strict
tensor, giving the equivalent coefficient lower-bound formulation in
Section 1. For \(\alpha=1\), normalization forces \(P=E\).

The threshold in (7) is also sharp for uniform strict contraction over all
\(Q\). Take three distinct \(a,i,j\), set \(Q_{ai}=e_j\) and
\(Q_{aj}=e_i\), and choose all other unordered rows as any admissible
probability distributions. The column difference for these rows has the
same sign pattern as the corresponding difference for \(E\), so equality
holds in (8) for that pair. At the displayed threshold it equals one.

The mixing class need not satisfy the more restrictive sufficient test
obtained by first bounding its full rowwise distance to \(E\). It is
therefore useful to retain (7), not only (6), when coefficients are given
in a mixing representation.

## 6. Global constructive conclusions, including boundary points

Suppose (2) gives \(L(P)\le q<1\), for example by either explicit class.
The closed simplex is a nonempty complete metric space in \(\ell^1\), and
\(V_P\) maps it to itself. Banach's fixed-point theorem gives exactly one
fixed point \(p\) and convergence of the **raw iteration**
\[
x_{t+1}=V_P(x_t),\qquad x_0\in\Delta_{n-1}.
\]
All starting points are included; allowed coefficients may vanish under
the general criterion or the rowwise perturbation criterion. No assumed
interior fixed point, invariant face, irreducibility, or positivity of
every allowed coefficient is needed.

The following constructive error bounds hold:
\[
\|x_t-p\|_1\le q^t\|x_0-p\|_1\le2q^t,                \tag{17}
\]
\[
\|x_t-p\|_1\le\frac{q^t}{1-q}\|x_1-x_0\|_1,
\qquad
\|x-p\|_1\le\frac{\|V_P(x)-x\|_1}{1-q}.              \tag{18}
\]
For \(0<q<1\), achieving \(\ell^1\) error at most \(\eta\in(0,2)\)
therefore needs at most
\(\lceil\log(2/\eta)/(-\log q)\rceil\) raw iterations. The constant-map
case \(q=0\) reaches its fixed point in one step.
Exact coefficients make the coefficient test finite and exact; a
floating-point implementation needs rounding control before claiming a
certified residual error. No such floating-point certification is claimed
in this report.

The usual perturbation estimate against the equiprobable center also gives
\[
\|p-u\|_1\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-2/(n-2)}
=\frac{n-2}{n-4}\varepsilon,                          \tag{19}
\]
by adding and subtracting \(V_E(p)\) in the fixed-point identity. A
nontrivial periodic orbit cannot occur under \(q<1\), since (17) forces
every orbit to converge to \(p\).

## 7. Quantitative comparison with a capped transfer-cut criterion

This section separates the stronger objective of global raw contraction
from a larger sufficient class for uniqueness and **averaged** convergence.
It generalizes the transfer setup used in `agents/006.md` and
`agents/008.md`; it does not assert that the unrestricted four-type
transfer bound continues to hold in higher dimensions.

Put
\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
K_q=\{x\in\Delta_{n-1}:x_k\le q\text{ for all }k\},
\qquad c\le q<\tfrac12.
\]
Every fixed point of any tensor satisfying (1) lies in \(K_c\), because
\[
p_k=V_P(p)_k\le(1-p_k)^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_k\le c. \tag{20}
\]

For each nonempty proper cut \(G\), each \(i\in G,j\notin G\), define
\[
d_a(G;i,j)=\sum_{k\in G}(P_{ai,k}-P_{aj,k}).
\]
Sort these \(n\) real numbers in decreasing order as
\(d_{[1]}\ge d_{[2]}\ge d_{[3]}\ge\cdots\), and define the finite
coefficient statistic
\[
\boxed{\tau_q(P)=\max_{G,i\in G,j\notin G}
\{q(d_{[1]}+d_{[2]})+(1-2q)d_{[3]}\}.}                \tag{21}
\]
There are finitely many cuts and indices; this is a direct coefficient
test rather than a condition involving unknown fixed points. Its
equivalent transfer formulation is
\[
\tau_q(P)=\max_{G,i\in G,j\notin G,m\in K_q}
\mathbf1_G^TA_P(m)(e_i-e_j).                          \tag{22}
\]
To prove equality, maximize the linear form \(\sum_a d_a m_a\) over the
cap. Since \(1/3<q<1/2\), fill the largest two coefficients with mass
\(q\) each, and the third with the remaining \(1-2q\le q\). Any mass
placed on a smaller coefficient can be moved to a larger unfilled one
without decreasing the objective. This proves (21).

**Transfer sufficient theorem.** If \(\tau_q(P)<1/2\), then \(V_P\) has a
unique fixed point on the entire closed simplex. For every
\(0<\lambda\le q\), the averaged map
\(T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V_P\) contracts \(K_q\) with factor
\[
L_{\rm av}\le
\max\{1-\lambda+2\lambda\tau_q(P),\ 2\lambda\}<1.    \tag{23}
\]

**Proof.** First,
\[
(T_\lambda x)_k\le f_\lambda(x_k),\qquad
f_\lambda(s)=(1-\lambda)s+\lambda(1-s)^2.
\]
The function is convex, \(f_\lambda(0)=\lambda\le q\), and
\(f_\lambda(q)\le q\) by \(q\ge c\). Therefore \(K_q\) is invariant.
It is nonempty since \(u\in K_q\).

Polarization gives the matrix
\(B(m)=(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A_P(m)\). Its columns all sum to
\(1+\lambda\), so each column difference has sum zero. Formula (10)
therefore equals the largest column-difference sum over all output
subsets. If a subset contains \(i\) but not \(j\), its contribution is
at most \(1-\lambda+2\lambda\tau_q(P)\). If it contains both or neither,
the identity contribution vanishes and stochasticity gives at most
\(2\lambda\). In the reverse-membership case the bound is
\(3\lambda-1\le2\lambda\). This proves (23). Banach gives a unique
averaged fixed point on \(K_q\), which is also a \(V_P\)-fixed point.
Equation (20) places every possible fixed point inside \(K_q\), proving
global uniqueness. This proof includes zero coordinates and equality in
the cap bounds. \(\square\)

For the equiprobable tensor, with \(g=|G|\), formula (12) gives exactly
\[
\mathbf1_G^TA_E(m)(e_i-e_j)
=\frac{-(n-1)+(n-g)m_i+g m_j}{d_n}.                   \tag{24}
\]
Its maximum on \(K_q\) is reached by \(m_i=m_j=q\), placing the remaining
mass on another coordinate. Thus
\[
\tau_q(E)=-\kappa_n(q),\qquad
\kappa_n(q)=\frac{n-1-nq}{(n-1)(n-2)}>0.              \tag{25}
\]
The sign is meaningful: the transfer from a positive input index to an
output cut containing that index is uniformly *negative* for \(E\) on
this cap. A raw norm bound discards this information.

Each perturbation vector \(R_{ai}\) has sum zero. Thus the magnitude of
its mass on any cut is at most \(\|R_{ai}\|_1/2\le\varepsilon/2\), and
\[
\tau_q(P)\le-\kappa_n(q)+\varepsilon.                 \tag{26}
\]
In particular, the much larger explicit rowwise neighborhood
\[
\varepsilon<S_n:=\tfrac12+
\frac{n-1-nc}{(n-1)(n-2)}                            \tag{27}
\]
guarantees global uniqueness and constructive averaged convergence from
the cap, for instance starting at \(u\). It does **not** by itself give
raw contraction or raw convergence.

| Types \(n\) | Raw-contraction radius \(R_n\) | Sufficient capped-transfer radius \(S_n\), approximately |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | \(0.166667\) | \(0.674181\) |
| 6 | \(0.250000\) | \(0.635410\) |
| 7 | \(0.300000\) | \(0.610875\) |
| 10 | \(0.375000\) | \(0.571949\) |

The cap restriction in the averaged-convergence statement can also be
removed under the strict inequality (27). Choose \(q>c\) sufficiently
close to \(c\) that \(q<1/2\) and
\(\varepsilon<1/2+\kappa_n(q)\), then take \(0<\lambda\le q\).
While a coordinate exceeds \(q\), its next value decreases by at least
\[
b=\lambda(3q-q^2-1)>0,
\]
since \(s^2-3s+1\) decreases for \(s\in[q,1]\). Once that coordinate is
at most \(q\), it stays there by the coordinatewise cap argument. Every
initial point therefore enters \(K_q\) after at most
\(\lceil(1-q)/b\rceil\) steps, and (23) then gives geometric convergence.
This remains convergence of the **averaged** map.

## 8. An exact example separating the two tests

For \(n=5\), apply (15) with \(a=1,i=2,j=3\), \(B=\{4\}\),
\(D=\{5\}\), and \(\varepsilon=1/5\). The changed rows are
\[
P_{12}=(0,0,13/30,7/30,1/3),\qquad
P_{13}=(0,13/30,0,1/3,7/30),                         \tag{28}
\]
and all other unordered rows equal \(E\). This tensor satisfies (1),
has row error exactly \(1/5\), and by (14), (16),
\[
L(P)=\frac{16}{15}>1.
\]
It therefore is not a strict global \(\ell^1\) contraction. Nevertheless,
its row error lies well inside (27), so the transfer theorem proves a
unique fixed point and averaged convergence.

The accompanying exact finite computation goes further: for the rational
cap \(q=2/5\), it checks every cut in (21) and finds
\[
\tau_{2/5}(P)=-43/300.
\]
With \(\lambda=3/10\), (23) gives the rational factor
\[
L_{\rm av}\le\max\{307/500,3/5\}=307/500.
\]
This illustrates a concrete advantage of signed transfer information over
the raw norm. It is not presented as an example with divergent raw
iterates; their convergence is not decided here.
