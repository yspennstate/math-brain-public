## Common definitions and identities

Let

\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\},
\qquad V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]

where \(P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0\), \(\sum_kP_{ij,k}=1\), and \(P_{ij,k}=0\) whenever \(k\in\{i,j\}\). The last condition applies also to repeated parents. Allowed coefficients may be zero.

Write \(T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V\), and let \(Q_k(x,y)=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij,k}x_i y_j\). Then \(Q_k\) is symmetric bilinear, \(V_k(x)=Q_k(x,x)\), and strict exclusion gives, for probability vectors,

\[
V_k(x)\le(1-x_k)^2,\qquad
Q_k(x,y)\le(1-x_k)(1-y_k).
\tag{A1}
\]

For \(m\in\Delta_3\), define \(A(m)_{kj}=\sum_iP_{ij,k}m_i\). This matrix is nonnegative, column-stochastic, and has zero diagonal. Its dependence on \(m\) is linear. Symmetry of \(P\) gives the exact polarization identity

\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A((x+y)/2)(x-y).
\tag{A2}
\]

At a fixed point \(p\), (A1) implies \(p_k\le c=(3-\sqrt5)/2<2/5\). In particular, a fixed point has at least three positive coordinates. Brouwer provides existence, although the contraction argument below can also provide existence and uniqueness independently through Banach's theorem.

### 1.1 Independent expansion and the actual feasible variables

Set \(a=1-\lambda\), \(s=x_k\), \(v=V_k(x)\). The actual pair satisfies

\[
0\le s\le1,\qquad0\le v\le(1-s)^2.
\]

Expanding the iterate before introducing any inequalities gives

\[
(T_\lambda^2x)_k
=a^2s+\lambda a(1+a)v
 +2\lambda^2a Q_k(x,Vx)+\lambda^3V_k(Vx).
\tag{B2}
\]

Every coefficient multiplying either of the last two quantities is nonnegative on the assigned interval. Since \(Vx\in\Delta_3\), (A1) therefore bounds (B2) by

\[
H_\lambda(s,v)=a^2s+\lambda a(1+a)v
 +2\lambda^2a(1-s)(1-v)+\lambda^3(1-v)^2.
\tag{B3}
\]

There is no assumption that \(s,v,Q_k(x,Vx),V_k(Vx)\) vary independently, or that all upper bounds can be attained simultaneously. Equation (B3) is a valid relaxation at each actual feasible point. For fixed \(s\), \(\partial_v^2 H=2\lambda^3>0\), so the relaxed maximum occurs at \(v=0\) or \(v=(1-s)^2\).

At \(v=0\), direct expansion gives

\[
H_\lambda(s,0)=\lambda^2(2-\lambda)+a(a-2\lambda^2)s.
\]

Here \(a-2\lambda^2=0\) at \(\lambda=1/2\), and its derivative is \(-1-4\lambda<0\). Thus the coefficient of \(s\) is nonpositive. The derivative of \(\lambda^2(2-\lambda)\) is \(\lambda(4-3\lambda)>0\) on the whole interval, giving

\[
H_\lambda(s,0)\le\frac{387549}{1000000}
=\frac25-\frac{12451}{1000000}<\frac25.
\tag{B4}
\]

At \(v=(1-s)^2\), put \(u=1-s\). Reexpansion of (B3) gives exactly the manuscript's polynomial

\[
F_\lambda(u)=a^2(1-u)+\lambda a(1+a)u^2
 +2\lambda^2au(1-u^2)+\lambda^3(1-u^2)^2.
\tag{B5}
\]

This endpoint itself has a feasible interpretation: one can choose every parent pair excluding type \(k\) to produce \(k\) with probability one, and assign pairs containing \(k\) any allowed offspring. Then \(V_k(x)=(1-x_k)^2\) identically, and the corresponding scalar second iterate is exactly \(F_\lambda(1-s)\). No such attainability assertion is needed for (B4), which is only a relaxation.

### 1.3 Independent third-image overlap proof, including the boundary

Let \(S_j=T_\lambda^j(\Delta_3)\), let \(x,y\in S_3\), and set \(m=(x+y)/2\). Because the images are nested, (B6) implies \(x,y\in K_q\) for \(q=2/5\), hence \(m\in K_q\). Set \(d=1-2q=1/5\) and \(\varepsilon=1/50\).

For any distinct columns \(i,j\), the column \(i\) contains the nonnegative vector \(m_jP_{ij,\cdot}\), and column \(j\) contains \(m_iP_{ij,\cdot}\). Thus their overlap is at least \(\min(m_i,m_j)\). If both masses are at least \(\varepsilon\), this proves the desired estimate directly.

Otherwise, one tested index \(r\) satisfies \(m_r=t<\varepsilon\). For each \(a\ne r\), the other two coordinates have total at most \(2q\), so

\[
m_a\ge1-t-2q=d-t\ge d-\varepsilon>\varepsilon.
\tag{B7}
\]

There cannot be a second small coordinate. Choose whichever of \(x,y\) has coordinate \(r\) at most their mean \(t\), and write that point as \(T_\lambda z\), \(z\in S_2\subset K_q\). Nonnegativity of both terms in \(T_\lambda z=az+\lambda Vz\) gives

\[
z_r\le t/a\le100t/49,\qquad V_r(z)\le t/\lambda\le2t.
\]

Therefore every \(z_b\), \(b\ne r\), is at least \(d-100t/49\). The smallest possible denominator on the closed comparison interval is

\[
d-\frac{100\varepsilon}{49}=\frac{39}{245}>0.
\]

For distinct \(b,c\ne r\), parental symmetry makes the nonnegative term in \(V_r(z)\) equal to \(2P_{bc,r}z_bz_c\), not \(P_{bc,r}z_bz_c\). Thus

\[
P_{bc,r}\le\theta(t):=\frac{t}{(d-100t/49)^2}.
\tag{B8}
\]

For \(C=100/49\), \(\theta'(t)=(d+Ct)/(d-Ct)^3>0\), and exact arithmetic gives

\[
\theta(\varepsilon)=\frac{2401}{3042}<1.
\]

Let \(j\) be the other tested column and let \(k,\ell\) be the other two types. The pair \(\ell,j\) can produce only \(k,r\), so

\[
A_{kj}\ge m_\ell(1-\theta(t)),\qquad
A_{\ell j}\ge m_k(1-\theta(t)).
\]

The offspring of the pair \(j,r\) all lie in \(\{k,\ell\}\), yielding \(A_{kr}+A_{\ell r}\ge m_j\). Put \(h=(d-t)(1-\theta(t))\). Both displayed entries of column \(j\) are at least \(h\ge0\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_a\min(A_{ar},A_{aj})
&\ge\min(A_{kr},h)+\min(A_{\ell r},h)\\
&\ge\min(A_{kr}+A_{\ell r},h)=h.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality is valid because the sum is at least \(m_j\ge d-t\ge h\). Both nonnegative factors defining \(h\) are decreasing in \(t\), so

\[
h\ge(d-\varepsilon)(1-\theta(\varepsilon))
=\frac{641}{16900}
=\frac1{50}+\frac{303}{16900}>\frac1{50}.
\tag{B9}
\]

At \(t=0\) the same proof applies with \(\theta(0)=0\); no coordinate or coefficient positivity is assumed. At \(t=\varepsilon\), the direct common-parent branch applies. Hence every pair of columns has overlap at least \(\varepsilon\), including all boundary and coefficient-degenerate cases. Equivalently,

\[
\delta(A(m)):=\max_{i,j}\tfrac12\|A(m)(e_i-e_j)\|_1\le49/50.
\tag{B10}

### 1.4 Transfer inequality and all 192 cut cases

For \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\), write \(p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}\). Strict exclusion and the two-pair partition force \(p_{ki}=0\), \(p_{\ell j}=1\); symmetry gives \(p_{ij}=p_{ji}\). Recomputing the column cut yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&:=\mathbf1_G^TA(m)(e_i-e_j)\\
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
 -m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}\le\max(m_i,m_j)\le q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{B11}
\]

This is a universal analytic proof. An additional exact finite certificate checks all \(32\) vertices of the five free probability variables and all \(12\) vertices of \(K_{2/5}\), for \(384\) combinations. The cap vertices are precisely the permutations of \((2/5,2/5,1/5,0)\). The expression is affine in each group of variables separately, so these checks certify its maximum over their product polytope. The five probability variables can each attain their endpoint choices with allowed parental rows. The attained maximum is \(2/5\). This finite certificate concerns (B11), not the more constrained third-image overlap property.

Let \(E=\{z:\sum_i z_i=0\}\) and \(B=aI+2\lambda A(m)\). Its columns sum to \(1+\lambda\). For any equal-column-sum matrix,

\[
\|B|_E\|_{1\to1}
=\max_{i\ne j}\frac12\|B(e_i-e_j)\|_1
=\max_{i\ne j,G}\mathbf1_G^TB(e_i-e_j).
\tag{B12}
\]

To justify both the norm and its normalization, write any nonzero \(z\in E\) as \(h\sum_{ij}\alpha_{ij}(e_i-e_j)\), where \(h=\|z\|_1/2\), \(\alpha_{ij}\ge0\), and \(\sum\alpha_{ij}=1\), using transfers between its positive and negative supports. This proves the upper bound, and a vector \(e_i-e_j\) realizes each candidate lower bound. Every output difference has zero sum, so its largest subset sum equals half its \(\ell^1\) norm.

There are twelve ordered distinct column pairs and sixteen subsets, giving exactly 192 cases. They partition as follows:

| Cut type | Count | Bound on \(\mathbf1_G^TB(e_i-e_j)\) |
|---|---:|---|
| Empty or full | 24 | \(0\) |
| Both column indices on the same side, nontrivial cut | 72 | \(2\lambda\delta(A)\le2\lambda(49/50)\) |
| \(i\in G,j\notin G\), singleton | 12 | \(a-2\lambda A_{ij}\le a\) |

For the triple case \(G^c=\{j\}\), the \(A\)-cut equals \((1-A_{ji})-(1-A_{jj})=-A_{ji}\); the zero diagonal is essential. The reverse case uses only that any difference of probability-column subset sums is at most one. Same-side cases use the overlap estimate only after the identity contribution cancels. The table proves the full operator norm estimate on every vector in \(E\), not merely the particular vector \(x-y\). No sign-support configuration is omitted when some coordinates of that vector vanish. Substituting \(q=2/5\) gives

\[
\|B|_E\|_{1\to1}\le
\max\left\{2\lambda\frac{49}{50},1-\frac\lambda5,3\lambda-1\right\}
\le\max\left\{\frac{2499}{2500},\frac9{10},\frac{53}{100}\right\}
=\frac{2499}{2500}<1.
\tag{B13}

The exact final slack is \(1/2500\).

### 1.5 Invariance, completeness, and support issues

The map \(T_\lambda\) is continuous and maps the compact simplex into itself. Thus \(S_3\) is nonempty and compact, hence complete in the induced \(\ell^1\) metric. Image nesting is a set-theoretic consequence of \(T_\lambda(\Delta_3)\subseteq\Delta_3\):

\[
T_\lambda(S_3)=S_4\subseteq S_3.
\]

Convexity of \(S_3\) is neither known nor needed. Its pair midpoints lie in the cap and have exactly the third-image representations used in the overlap proof. The proof never asserts invariance of the entire cap for weights above \(1/2\).

For \(x,y\in S_3\), (A2) and (B13) give the contraction estimate for \(T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y\). Banach's theorem therefore gives a unique fixed point on \(S_3\). Every fixed point of \(T_\lambda\) belongs to \(S_3\), and its fixed points coincide with those of \(V\) because \(\lambda>0\). This also shows that this part of the proof need not rely circularly on the separate uniqueness theorem.

Starting from any simplex point, the third iterate belongs to \(S_3\). The fixed point does also, and the simplex has diameter two. Applying (B13) repeatedly proves (B1), including \(n=3\), \(\lambda=1/2\), and \(\lambda=51/100\).

Every point in \(K_{2/5}\) has at least three positive coordinates, since two coordinates can sum to at most \(4/5\). If the chosen third-image point has coordinate \(r=0\), then its preimage has both \(z_r=0\) and \(V_r(z)=0\), while the other three coordinates are positive. Thus all contributing probabilities into \(r\) vanish; this is consistent with the manuscript's invariant-face description. The overlap proof required only the off-diagonal vanishing that follows from (B8). For a boundary fixed point, the positive support is a triple, all missing-output coefficients on that triple vanish by nonnegativity, and strict exclusion already removes coefficients containing the missing type. Its missing output polynomial therefore vanishes identically. Nothing in the convergence argument breaks in this case.

## Claim 2 — all \(0<\lambda<1/2\) and the stated entrance time

**Verified:** let

\[
q=\max\{2/5,\lambda\},\quad
d=\lambda(3q-q^2-1),\quad
N=\left\lceil\frac{1-q}{d}\right\rceil,\quad
L=1-\lambda(1-2q).
\]

Then \(d>0\), \(0<L<1\), every orbit enters \(K_q\) by step \(N\), and

\[
\|T_\lambda^n x-p\|_1\le2L^{n-N},\qquad n\ge N.
\tag{C1}
\]

The scalar upper envelope is \(f(u)=(1-\lambda)u+\lambda(1-u)^2\). Its second derivative is \(2\lambda>0\), and

\[
r-f(r)=\lambda(3r-r^2-1).
\]

The polynomial \(g(r)=3r-r^2-1\) has derivative \(3-2r\ge1\) on \([2/5,1]\), and \(g(2/5)=1/25\). Since \(q\ge2/5\), this gives \(d\ge\lambda/25>0\). If the current maximum coordinate is at most \(r\in[q,1]\), convexity on \([0,r]\) gives

\[
\max_i(T_\lambda x)_i\le\max\{f(0),f(r)\}
\le\max\{q,r-d\},
\]

using \(f(0)=\lambda\le q\). An induction yields \(\max_i(T_\lambda^n x)_i\le\max\{q,1-nd\}\). In the inductive step, when the previous maximum bound has already reached \(q\), the same estimate returns \(q\), so there is no hidden overshoot or loss of validity at the floor. The stated ceiling therefore suffices.

The cap is invariant because \(f(0)=\lambda\le q\) and \(f(q)\le q\), the latter following from \(q\ge2/5>c\). The cut calculation on \(K_q\), without needing any overlap improvement, bounds the norm by the maximum of \(2\lambda\), \(1-\lambda\), \(3\lambda-1\), and \(1-\lambda+2\lambda q\). Since \(0<\lambda\le q<1/2\),

\[
1-\lambda+2\lambda q-2\lambda
=1-\lambda(3-2q)
\ge1-q(3-2q)=(1-q)(1-2q)>0.
\]

The other terms are no larger either, so the full norm is at most \(L=1-\lambda(1-2q)<1\). It is positive since \(1-\lambda>0\). The fixed point lies in \(K_c\subset K_q\); alternatively cap invariance and contraction give it directly. This proves (C1). The entrance bound is deliberately coarse and is not contradicted by the much shorter special entrance time at \(\lambda=2/5\).

There is no missing interval between this claim and Claim 1: the former covers all strictly smaller positive weights, and Claim 1 includes the half-step endpoint.

## Claim 3 — the separate perturbative range through \(251/500=0.502\)

### 3.1 Half-step dependency checked independently

For \(T=T_{1/2}\), \(s=x_k\), \(v=V_k(x)\), and \(y=Tx\), the coefficient defect gives

\[
(1-y_k)^2-V_k(y)
=\sum_{i,j\ne k}(1-P_{ij,k})y_iy_j
\ge\frac14[(1-s)^2-v],
\]

because \(y_i\ge x_i/2\) and \(0\le P_{ij,k}\le1\). Substituting \(y_k=(s+v)/2\) and expanding yields

\[
(T^2x)_k\le\frac{3+(2s-1)v+v^2}{8}.
\]

Its endpoint values are \(3/8\) and \([3+s^2(1-s)^2]/8\). Since \(s(1-s)\le1/4\), the two-step cap is \(q_h=49/128\). This cap is attainable by choosing \(V_k(x)=(1-x_k)^2\) and \(s=1/2\), though attainability is unnecessary here.

Repeat the preimage overlap proof in Claim 1 with

\[
d_h=15/64,\quad \varepsilon_h=d_h^2/2=225/8192,
\quad\theta_h(t)=t/(d_h-2t)^2.
\]

All denominator and monotonicity conditions are valid, and exact arithmetic gives

\[
\theta_h(\varepsilon_h)=2048/2401<1,
\quad(d_h-\varepsilon_h)(1-\theta_h(\varepsilon_h))
=598335/19668992>\varepsilon_h.
\]

The gap in the last comparison is \(29055/9834496>0\). Therefore \(\delta(A(m_0))\le R=1-\varepsilon_h=7967/8192\) for midpoints of points in \(T^3(\Delta_3)\). The full cut argument gives

\[
\|(\tfrac12 I+A(m_0))|_E\|_{1\to1}
\le\max\{R,\tfrac12+q_h\}=R,
\tag{D2}
\]

since \(1/2+q_h=113/128<R\). This is the full zero-sum operator norm required by the perturbation argument, rather than merely a bound on an individual image difference.

### 3.2 Perturbation algebra and rate

For every real vector \(w\), linearity and parental stochasticity yield

\[
\|A(w)\|_{1\to1}
\le\max_j\sum_{k,i}|w_i|P_{ij,k}=\|w\|_1.
\tag{D3}
\]

Equation (A2) makes \(V\) globally \(2\)-Lipschitz on the simplex. Thus \(T_\lambda\) is \((1+\lambda)\)-Lipschitz for \(0\le\lambda\le1\), and

\[
\|T_\lambda x-Tx\|_1=e\|Vx-x\|_1\le2e.
\]

Applying the triangle inequality at each step from the same initial point gives

\[
\|T_\lambda^3x-T^3x\|_1
\le a_3=2e[1+(1+\lambda)+(1+\lambda)^2].
\tag{D4}
\]

For two initial points, let \(m_\lambda\) be the midpoint of their \(T_\lambda^3\) images and \(m_0\) that of their \(T^3\) images. Averaging (D4) gives \(\|m_\lambda-m_0\|_1\le a_3\). The matrix difference expands exactly to

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A(m_\lambda)
 -(\tfrac12I+A(m_0))\\
&\hspace{1cm}=e(2A(m_\lambda)-I)+A(m_\lambda-m_0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{D5}
\]

Since \(A(m_\lambda)\) is stochastic, \(\|2A(m_\lambda)-I\|_{1\to1}\le3\). Combining (D2)–(D5) proves the full zero-sum norm bound \(R+3e+a_3\). At the allowed endpoint,

\[
3+2\left[1+\frac{751}{500}+\left(\frac{751}{500}\right)^2\right]
=\frac{1564501}{125000}<13,
\]

so the bound is at most \(R+13e\). Finally, \(e\le1/500\), and

\[
R+13/500=\frac{1022499}{1024000},\qquad
1-R-13/500=\frac{1501}{1024000}>0.
\]

The set \(T_\lambda^3(\Delta_3)\) is compact and invariant as before. Every pair in it admits the comparison representations just used, so the estimate contracts the entire set and proves (D1).

This proof uses closeness of third images to their half-step counterparts and the stronger half-step constant \(R\). Claim 1 uses a uniform \(2/5\) cap and a new overlap bound directly at weights up to \(0.51\). Their endpoints and rates must not be conflated. The earlier perturbative result remains correct and supplies its own parameter-dependent rate \(R+13e\); it is not the argument establishing the larger \(0.51\) endpoint.
