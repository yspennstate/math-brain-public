## 1. Definitions and exact audited theorem

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\sum_{i=1}^4x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\quad
P_{ij,k}=0\text{ whenever }k\in\{i,j\}.
\]
Put
\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad K_q=\{x\in\Delta_3:x_i\le q\text{ for all }i\},
\qquad T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V.
\]
All norms in this report are the ordinary, unweighted \(\ell^1\) norm.

**Audited theorem.** If
\[
c\le q<\frac12,\qquad 0<\lambda\le q,
\]
then \(T_\lambda(K_q)\subseteq K_q\), and for all \(x,y\in K_q\),
\[
\|T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y\|_1\le L\|x-y\|_1,
\qquad L=1-\lambda(1-2q)<1.
\tag{1}
\]
There is a unique fixed point \(p\) of \(V\) in the entire simplex. For every \(x_0\in K_q\), the sequence \(x_{n+1}=T_\lambda x_n\) converges to \(p\) at rate \(L^n\).

The parent's displayed factor \(\max\{1-\lambda+2\lambda q,2\lambda\}\) is valid. Under the invariant-cap assumptions its first term strictly dominates, giving (1).

## 2. Cap invariance, including endpoints and existence of the cap

For any \(x\in\Delta_3\), strict non-Volterra coefficients give
\[
0\le V_k(x)\le \sum_{i,j\ne k}x_ix_j=(1-x_k)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(0\le s\le q\), define
\[
f_\lambda(s)=(1-\lambda)s+\lambda(1-s)^2.
\]
This function is convex. Hence
\[
(T_\lambda x)_k\le f_\lambda(x_k)
\le \max\{f_\lambda(0),f_\lambda(q)\}.
\]
Here \(f_\lambda(0)=\lambda\le q\), while
\[
f_\lambda(q)-q=\lambda(q^2-3q+1)\le0
\]
because \(q\in[c,1/2)\). Therefore every coordinate remains at most \(q\). Nonnegativity and sum one hold because \(T_\lambda\) is a convex average of two simplex maps.

The cap \(K_q\) is nonempty: the uniform vector belongs to it since \(q\ge c>1/4\). It is compact and convex, hence complete in the inherited \(\ell^1\) metric. No assertion of strict interiority is required; all inequalities include zero coordinates and coordinates equal to the cap.

Every fixed point \(p\) of \(V\), if any, satisfies \(p_k\le(1-p_k)^2\), so \(p_k\le c\). Thus all fixed points lie in \(K_c\).

The cap restrictions are exact for a theorem uniform over all such operators. To see this, fix an offspring coordinate \(k\) and choose every parental pair avoiding \(k\) to produce \(k\) with probability one. Then \(V_k(x)=(1-x_k)^2\) for all \(x\). If \(q<c\) and \(K_q\) is nonempty, take \(x_k=q\), with the other coordinates equal to \((1-q)/3\); the cap is immediately violated for every \(\lambda>0\). Once \(q\ge c>1/3\), a vector with \(x_k=0\) and the other coordinates \(1/3\) belongs to \(K_q\), and its next coordinate is exactly \(\lambda\). Consequently \(\lambda>q\) prevents universal cap invariance. This concerns invariance of the whole set \(K_q\), and does not prevent use of a different invariant set below.

## 3. Polarization and the zero-sum Dobrushin formula

For \(x,y\in\Delta_3\), put \(m=(x+y)/2\) and
\[
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_iP_{ij,k}m_i.
\]
This matrix is nonnegative, has zero diagonal, and has column sum one. Symmetry in the parent indices gives the exact identity
\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A(m)(x-y).
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(x_ix_j-y_iy_j=m_i(x_j-y_j)+(x_i-y_i)m_j\), and symmetry identifies the two resulting sums.

Thus
\[
T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y=B(m)(x-y),\qquad
B(m)=(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A(m).
\]
The column sum of \(B\) is \(1+\lambda\), not one. Nevertheless it preserves the zero-sum subspace
\[
E=\{z:\sum_i z_i=0\},
\]
and its induced norm on this subspace is
\[
\|B|_E\|_{1\to1}
=\frac12\max_{i,j}\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1
=\max_{G\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\},\,i,j}
\sum_{k\in G}(B_{ki}-B_{kj}).
\tag{4}
\]
No additional factor \(1+\lambda\) belongs in (4).

For a direct proof, write a nonzero zero-sum vector as
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in I_+,j\in I_-}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j),\qquad
h=\|z\|_1/2,\quad
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2},
\]
where \(I_+=\{i:z_i>0\}\), \(I_-=\{j:z_j<0\}\), and \(\sum\theta_{ij}=1\). The triangle inequality proves the upper bound in the first equality of (4); the vectors \(e_i-e_j\) prove the reverse bound. For the second equality, each column difference has coordinate sum zero, so half its \(\ell^1\) norm equals the sum of its positive coordinates, which is the maximum of its sums over subsets. This proof requires equal column sums, not column sum one.

## 4. Exhaustive subset audit

Fix distinct \(i,j\), a subset \(G\), and let
\[
D_A(G;i,j)=\sum_{k\in G}(A_{ki}-A_{kj}).
\]
Because \(A\) is column stochastic, \(D_A\le1\). The subset sum in (4) equals
\[
(1-\lambda)\bigl(\mathbf1_G(i)-\mathbf1_G(j)\bigr)
+2\lambda D_A(G;i,j).
\tag{5}
\]
The following cases exhaust every subset, including all exceptional sizes.

| Membership or subset size | Bound on (5) | Justification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(G=\varnothing\) or all four indices | \(0\) | Empty sum or equal column sums. |
| Both \(i,j\) in \(G\), or both outside | \(2\lambda\) | The identity terms cancel; \(D_A\le1\). |
| \(i\notin G,\ j\in G\) | \(3\lambda-1\le2\lambda\) | The identity contribution is \(-(1-\lambda)\), and \(D_A\le1\). |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=1\) | \(1-\lambda\) | Here \(G=\{i\}\), so \(D_A=-A_{ij}\le0\). |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=3\) | \(1-\lambda\) | The complement is \(\{j\}\), so \(D_A=-A_{ji}\le0\). |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=2\) | \(1-\lambda+2\lambda q\) | The pair transfer estimate proved next. |

The trivial case \(i=j\) contributes zero and can be discarded before this table.

For the last row, write \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\), and
\[
p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}.
\]
Then \(0\le p_{ab}\le1\), \(p_{ab}=p_{ba}\), \(p_{ki}=0\), and \(p_{\ell j}=1\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_A
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
-m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}
\le\max(m_i,m_j)\le q.
\end{aligned}
\]
All discarded terms are nonpositive, including when coefficients or masses are zero. This is precisely the step special to four types.

Combining the rows proves the useful more general estimate
\[
\|T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y\|_1
\le \max\{1-\lambda+2\lambda q,2\lambda\}\|x-y\|_1
\tag{6}
\]
for \(x,y\in K_q\) and \(0<\lambda\le1\), without any assumption that \(K_q\) is invariant. The cap need only be nonempty and have \(m_i\le q\). In the audited invariant-cap range,
\[
(1-\lambda+2\lambda q)-2\lambda
=1-\lambda(3-2q)
\ge1-q(3-2q)=(1-q)(1-2q)>0.
\]
Thus (6) reduces to (1), which is strictly less than one because \(\lambda>0\) and \(q<1/2\).

## 5. Banach uniqueness and explicit certificates

Banach's fixed-point theorem applied to the complete nonempty invariant set \(K_q\) gives a unique \(T_\lambda\)-fixed point \(p\in K_q\), and convergence of every averaged iteration started in \(K_q\). Because \(\lambda>0\),
\[
T_\lambda x=x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V(x)=x.
\]
Every possible \(V\)-fixed point lies in \(K_c\subseteq K_q\), so uniqueness holds on all of \(\Delta_3\). This proves existence without invoking Brouwer.

Let \(x_{n+1}=T_\lambda x_n\), with \(x_0\in K_q\). The following are rigorous in exact arithmetic:
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le L^n\|x_0-p\|_1,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le \frac{L^n}{1-L}\|x_1-x_0\|_1,
\tag{8}
\]
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le\frac{\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|_1}{1-L},
\tag{9}
\]
and, for \(n\ge1\),
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le\frac{L}{1-L}\|x_n-x_{n-1}\|_1.
\tag{10}
\]
The residual certificate, valid for any \(x\in K_q\), is particularly simple:
\[
\boxed{\quad\|x-p\|_1\le\frac{\|V(x)-x\|_1}{1-2q}.\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
To prove it, combine
\(\|x-p\|_1\le\|x-T_\lambda x\|_1+\|T_\lambda x-T_\lambda p\|_1\)
with (1), and cancel \(\lambda\). Thus a computed residual with an independently certified upper bound gives a certified bound on fixed-point error. Merely evaluating floating-point arithmetic without rounding control does not itself create a rigorous numerical certificate.

For a coordinatewise certificate, \(x-p\) has sum zero, so
\[
\|x-p\|_\infty\le\tfrac12\|x-p\|_1
\le\frac{\|V(x)-x\|_1}{2(1-2q)}.
\]

A completely explicit a priori bound follows from
\[
\operatorname{diam}_1(K_q)=8q-2\qquad(q\ge1/3,\ q<1/2).
\tag{12}
\]
For a subset of size two, the largest possible difference of its masses is \(2q-(1-2q)=4q-1\). Subsets of size one or three give at most \(q\le4q-1\); the zero-sum subset formula doubles these bounds. Equality is attained, for example, by
\(x=(q,q,1-2q,0)\) and \(y=(0,1-2q,q,q)\).
Consequently (7) is at most \((8q-2)L^n\).

## 6. Best constants for the invariant-cap framework, and sharpness

For a fixed \(q\), the factor \(L=1-\lambda(1-2q)\) decreases with \(\lambda\). Universal cap invariance requires \(\lambda\le q\), so its best permitted choice is \(\lambda=q\). The resulting
\[
L(q)=1-q+2q^2
\]
is strictly increasing for \(q\ge c>1/4\). Thus the best universal guarantee obtainable from this invariant-cap framework is
\[
q=\lambda=c,\qquad
L=1-c+2c^2=5c-1=\frac{13-5\sqrt5}{2}
\approx0.9098300563.
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(1-L=(5\sqrt5-11)/2\), and (11) becomes
\[
\|x-p\|_1\le(2+\sqrt5)\|V(x)-x\|_1\qquad(x\in K_c).
\]

A convenient rational choice improves the parent's \(q=2/5,\lambda=1/3\) example:
\[
q=\lambda=\frac25,\qquad L=\frac{23}{25},\qquad
\|x-p\|_1\le5\|V(x)-x\|_1.
\tag{14}
\]
For this choice, (9) multiplies the next-step difference by \(25/2\), and (10) multiplies the previous-step difference by \(23/2\).

The factor in (1) cannot be reduced uniformly over all allowed operators. Here is an explicit deterministic offspring table; the entry in the right column is the unique offspring of the unordered parent pair:

| Parents | Offspring |
| --- | --- |
| \(11\) | \(2\) |
| \(12\) | \(3\) |
| \(13\) | \(2\) |
| \(14\) | \(2\) |
| \(22\) | \(1\) |
| \(23\) | \(4\) |
| \(24\) | \(1\) |
| \(33\) | \(4\) |
| \(34\) | \(1\) |
| \(44\) | \(1\) |

Fix the permitted \(q,\lambda\), let \(a=q-\varepsilon\), \(b=1/2-q+\varepsilon\), and \(m=(a,b,a,b)\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(0<t\le\varepsilon\) and set
\[
x=m+t(e_1-e_3),\qquad y=m-t(e_1-e_3).
\]
These are distinct points of \(K_q\). Direct evaluation gives
\[
B(m)(e_1-e_3)=
(1-\lambda-2\lambda b,\ \lambda,\ -(1-\lambda)+2\lambda b,\ -\lambda).
\]
Its first two coordinates are positive and its last two negative. Therefore
\[
\frac{\|T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y\|_1}{\|x-y\|_1}
=1-2\lambda b
=1-\lambda+2\lambda(q-\varepsilon).
\]
Taking \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves sharpness. The limiting midpoint has two capped coordinates, so it is appropriate to use a supremum; a nonzero feasible symmetric difference cannot point beyond an exactly capped coordinate.

## 7. Global convergence of averaged iteration

The averaged iteration converges from every simplex point whenever \(0<\lambda<1/2\), even if the starting point is outside a chosen cap. To see this, choose
\[
\max(c,\lambda)<q<1/2.
\]
Once a coordinate is at most \(q\), it remains so by the same scalar endpoint argument used for cap invariance, independently of the other coordinates. For a coordinate \(s>q\), (2) gives
\[
(T_\lambda x)_k\le s-\lambda\bigl[s-(1-s)^2\bigr]
\le s-\lambda\bigl[q-(1-q)^2\bigr].
\]
The bracket is strictly positive because \(q>c\). Thus every coordinate enters the cap after at most
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{1-q}{\lambda\,[q-(1-q)^2]}\right\rceil
\]
steps, and then (1) applies. This is a simple sufficient entry bound, not an optimized one.

For the useful rational choice (14), a much better exact entry bound is three steps. Convexity of
\(f(s)=(3/5)s+(2/5)(1-s)^2\) gives the successive uniform coordinate bounds
\[
1\ \longmapsto\ \frac35\ \longmapsto\ \frac{53}{125}\ \longmapsto\ \frac25.
\]
Indeed the last endpoint value is
\[
f(53/125)=\frac{30243}{78125}<\frac25,
\]
while \(f(0)=2/5\). Therefore
\[
T_{2/5}^{,3}(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_{2/5}.
\]
For any \(x_0\in\Delta_3\) and \(n\ge3\),
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le\frac65\left(\frac{23}{25}\right)^{n-3},
\qquad
\|x_n-p\|_1\le5\|V(x_n)-x_n\|_1.
\tag{15}
\]

## 8. A sharper rational variant using an invariant image

The restriction \(\lambda\le q\) is necessary for universal invariance of the entire cap, but is unnecessary for the Lipschitz estimate (6). A compact invariant image gives a slightly better rate:
\[
\boxed{\quad\lambda=\frac9{20},\quad
S=T_\lambda^2(\Delta_3),\quad
S\subseteq K_Q,\quad Q=\frac{3149}{8000},\quad
L=\frac{72341}{80000}\approx0.9042625.\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
Here \(K_Q\) itself is not claimed invariant. The image \(S\) is invariant because
\(T_\lambda S=T_\lambda^3(\Delta_3)\subseteq T_\lambda^2(\Delta_3)=S\).

### Two-step coordinate estimate

Fix a coordinate \(k\), put \(s=x_k\), \(v=V_k(x)\), \(y=T_\lambda x\), and \(a=1-\lambda\). Then \(0\le v\le(1-s)^2\), \(y_k=as+\lambda v\), and \(y_i\ge ax_i\). The nonnegative coefficient defect gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-y_k)^2-V_k(y)
&=\sum_{i,j\ne k}(1-P_{ij,k})y_i y_j\\
&\ge a^2\sum_{i,j\ne k}(1-P_{ij,k})x_i x_j
=a^2\bigl[(1-s)^2-v\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
(T_\lambda^2x)_k
\le F_\lambda(s,v)
\equiv f_\lambda(as+\lambda v)-\lambda a^2\bigl[(1-s)^2-v\bigr].
\tag{17}
\]
The coefficient of \(v^2\) in \(F_\lambda\) is \(\lambda^3>0\), so for fixed \(s\) the maximum over allowed \(v\) occurs at an endpoint. At the first endpoint,
\[
F_\lambda(s,0)=\lambda^2(2-\lambda)
+(1-\lambda)(1+\lambda)(1-2\lambda)s.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(\lambda=9/20\), this increases with \(s\), so its maximum is
\[
F_\lambda(1,0)=(1-\lambda)^2+\lambda^3=\frac{3149}{8000}=Q.
\]
At the other endpoint, the defect vanishes and
\[
F_\lambda(s,(1-s)^2)=f_\lambda(f_\lambda(s)).
\]
For \(\lambda=9/20\), the range of \(f_\lambda\) on \([0,1]\) is
\[
\left[\frac{55}{144},\frac{11}{20}\right].
\]
Convexity of \(f_\lambda\) therefore bounds its composition by the maximum of
\[
f_\lambda(55/144)=\frac{5867}{15360}<Q,
\qquad f_\lambda(11/20)=Q.
\]
This proves \(T_\lambda^2(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_Q\). The cap \(Q\) is sharp as a uniform two-step coordinate bound for this \(\lambda\): take \(x=e_1\), \(P_{11,2}=1\), \(P_{22,1}=1\); then its first coordinate after two averaged steps is exactly \((1-\lambda)^2+\lambda^3\). The remaining coefficients can be any allowed stochastic choices.

### Contraction and error estimate on the invariant image

For \(x,y\in S\subseteq K_Q\), (6) gives
\[
\|T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y\|_1
\le\max\left\{\frac{11}{20}+\frac9{10}\frac{3149}{8000},\frac9{10}\right\}
\|x-y\|_1
=\frac{72341}{80000}\|x-y\|_1.
\]
The image \(S\) is nonempty compact, hence complete, and is invariant. Banach applies directly. Every initial point enters \(S\) at step two. With \(x_n=T_\lambda^n x_0\), for \(n\ge2\),
\[
\|x_n-p\|_1\le\frac{1149}{1000}
\left(\frac{72341}{80000}\right)^{n-2}.
\]
The same residual rearrangement yields
\[
\boxed{\quad
\|x-p\|_1\le\frac{4000}{851}\|V(x)-x\|_1
\qquad (x\in K_Q).
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
This certificate actually holds on the whole cap \(K_Q\): both \(x\) and the fixed point \(p\in K_c\subseteq K_Q\) satisfy the pointwise Lipschitz estimate, even though the cap need not be invariant. This variant improves (13), but (13) remains the exact optimum for the original universally invariant-cap framework. I have not claimed that (16) is the globally optimal averaging parameter or contraction estimate.

## 9. The endpoint \(\lambda=1/2\): a proved cap, but no contraction conclusion

The same calculation gives an attractive but insufficient endpoint fact:
\[
T_{1/2}^{,2}(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_{49/128},
\tag{20}
\]
and \(49/128\) is sharp. Specifically, (17) simplifies to
\[
F_{1/2}(s,v)=\frac{3+(2s-1)v+v^2}{8}.
\]
The two endpoints in \(v\) give \(3/8\) and
\[
\frac{3+s^2(1-s)^2}{8}\le\frac{49}{128},
\]
respectively. Equality is attained by an operator with
\(V_k(x)=(1-x_k)^2\), started at a vector having \(x_k=1/2\).

However, (6) at \(\lambda=1/2\) still contains the term \(2\lambda=1\), coming from the subset cases with cancelling identity contributions. The estimate is only nonexpansive; the cap (20) alone does not prove Banach contraction or convergence. This is an explicit limit of this audit's argument. Neither (20) nor uniqueness should be advertised as a proof of convergence at \(\lambda=1/2\).

## 10. Raw discrete iteration is different, with an explicit counterexample

Nothing in this report asserts that the original sequence \(V^n x\) converges. A valid strictly non-Volterra operator can satisfy
\[
P_{11,2}=1,\qquad P_{22,1}=1,
\]
with every other unordered parental pair assigned any permissible offspring. It then has
\[
V(e_1)=e_2,\qquad V(e_2)=e_1.
\]
Thus raw iteration has a nontrivial period-two orbit, even though the fixed point is unique and the averaged iterations covered above converge. A complete deterministic example is to assign each unordered pair \(\{i,j\}\) the smallest index outside \(\{i,j\}\); its two diagonal choices above are automatic.

The continuous-time ODE mentioned in the parent's working document was outside this bounded audit. I make no additional claim about it here.
