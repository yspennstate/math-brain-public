## Results and distinction from existing work

Reports 016/018 already prove complex two-site entropy concavity and its conditional extension to a fixed two-coordinate block. Those concavity theorems are **inputs, not new claims here**. Report 050 classifies interior star-slice flatness; report 042 treats rank-one projection endpoints.

The additions proved here are:

1. **Complete chord equality for arbitrary changing two-by-two blocks, including every boundary case:** an interior chord equality holds exactly when the two kernels coincide. Explicit determinant/marginal bounds establish strictness.
2. **A sharp universal quartic chord modulus for a changing off-diagonal entry:** if both diagonal entries are also fixed and \(v=(K_1-K_0)_{ij}\), then
   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal G(t)\ge
   \frac83t(1-t)(1-t+t^2)|v|^4.}
   \tag{A}
   \]
   The coefficient \(8/3\) cannot be increased, for any fixed \(0<t<1\). This holds in arbitrary dimension with the other entries fixed.
3. **Complete zero-curvature classification at every interior point of a feasible nonconstant block chord, even if the full kernel is singular:** the direction must be purely off-diagonal, and every positive-probability conditional two-site kernel must be diagonal at that point. There is at most one such point. An explicit strictly negative quartic coefficient describes it.
4. A conditional-correlation variance gives an additional quadratic term in (A), and a spectral gap alone does **not** give uniform strong concavity.

No historical novelty claim or solution of unrestricted Lyons concavity is made.

## 1. Setting and the fixed conditional decomposition

Let \(E\) be finite, \(U=\{i,j\}\), and \(O=E\setminus U\). Let \(0\preceq K_0,K_1\preceq I\) be complex Hermitian matrices agreeing outside \(U\times U\). Write
\[
K(t)=(1-t)K_0+tK_1,\qquad
d=(K_1-K_0)_{UU}=
\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \delta=us-|v|^2.
\]
All logarithms are natural. Let \(H(K)\) denote the Shannon entropy of the DPP \(P_K\), and put
\[
F(t)=H(K(t)),\qquad
\mathcal G(t)=F(t)-(1-t)F(0)-tF(1).
\]

For \(Y\subseteq O\), set \(Z=O\setminus Y\), let \(P_Z\) be its diagonal projection, and define
\[
Q_Y=K_{OO}-P_Z,\qquad
q_Y=(-1)^{|Z|}\det Q_Y.
\]
These are the fixed outside-atom probabilities. Whenever \(q_Y>0\), \(Q_Y\) is invertible, and report 018's exact conditioning identity gives
\[
C_Y(t)=K_{UU}(t)-K_{UO}Q_Y^{-1}K_{OU}
      =C_Y(0)+td,\qquad 0\preceq C_Y(t)\preceq I.
\tag{1}
\]
Zero-weight atoms are omitted. The entropy chain rule is
\[
F(t)=H((q_Y)_Y)+\sum_{Y:q_Y>0}q_YH(C_Y(t)).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus conditional directions have the same \(u,s,v,\delta\), with no rescaling. Equations (1)–(2) are valid for closed contractions, including singular ones.

For a strict two-site kernel
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},
\]
write its four probabilities in order \(00,10,01,11\) as
\[
p=(x,y,z,w)=
((1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2,\ a(1-b)+|c|^2,\
(1-a)b+|c|^2,\ ab-|c|^2).
\]
Along direction \(d\),
\[
p''=2\delta(1,-1,-1,1),\qquad
L=\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0,
\]
because \(yz-xw=|c|^2\). Hence
\[
\boxed{H''=-\operatorname{FI}+2\delta L,\qquad
\operatorname{FI}=\sum_p\frac{(p')^2}{p}.}
\tag{3}
\]
This identity includes arbitrary complex phases.

The existing definite-direction bound is
\[
H''\le-8\delta\quad(\delta>0).
\tag{4}
\]
For context, its proof bounds the empty/full Fisher contributions by
\(4\delta(1+T)\), where \(T=(y+z)/(2\sqrt{xw})\ge1\), while
\(L\le2\log T\). Then \(1+T-\log T\ge2\). We use (4), without claiming this earlier argument as an addition.

## 2. Complete strict-chord theorem

For every closed-contraction pair as above,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G(t)\ge t(1-t)
\begin{cases}
4\delta,&\delta>0,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\max\left\{
2u^2,\ 2s^2,\ \frac{16}{9}(1-t+t^2)\delta^2
\right\},&\delta\le0.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5}
\]

The positive-determinant branch is report 018's bound. Here is the other branch.

For any event of probability \(m\), Cauchy–Schwarz on the event and its complement gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}\ge\frac{(m')^2}{m(1-m)}\ge4(m')^2.
\tag{6}
\]
When \(\delta\le0\), (3) gives \(-H''\ge\operatorname{FI}\). The one-coordinate events have derivatives \(u,s\), yielding the first two terms of (5).

For the parity event \(\{00,11\}\), its probability is \(m=x+w\), and
\[
m'=\beta+4\delta r
\]
at line parameter \(r\), for some constant \(\beta\). The Dirichlet Green kernel
\[
G(t,r)=
\begin{cases}r(1-t),&r\le t,\\t(1-r),&r\ge t\end{cases}
\]
satisfies
\[
\mathcal G(t)=\int_0^1G(t,r)(-H''(r))\,dr.
\]
Its moments are
\[
M_0=\frac{t(1-t)}2,\quad
M_1=\frac{t(1-t)(1+t)}6,\quad
M_2=\frac{t(1-t)(1+t+t^2)}{12},
\]
and
\[
M_2-\frac{M_1^2}{M_0}
=\frac{t(1-t)(1-t+t^2)}{36}.
\]
Minimizing \(4\int G(\beta+4\delta r)^2dr\) over \(\beta\) therefore gives the third term in (5). Apply these two-site bounds in (2). All right-hand sides are independent of \(Y\).

This proves (5) first for strict endpoints. For arbitrary endpoints, replace
\(K_j\) by \((1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I\). The direction entries scale by \(1-2\varepsilon\), and its determinant by \((1-2\varepsilon)^2\). Apply the interior bound and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Polynomial atom probabilities and continuity of \(-p\log p\) justify the limit.

If \(d\ne0\), either \(\delta\ne0\), or \(\delta=0\) and at least one of \(u,s\) is nonzero. Thus (5) is strictly positive whenever \(0<t<1\). Conversely \(d=0\) means \(K_0=K_1\). This proves the complete equality classification, also for segments lying entirely in a boundary face. The constants in the determinant-squared term of (5) are sufficient; no optimality is claimed for that term.

## 3. Sharp pure-off-diagonal stability

Now assume \(u=s=0\). For one conditional two-site law its diagonals \(a,b\) are fixed. Put \(r=|c|^2\), and let \(J_{a,b}(r)\) be its mutual information. Then
\[
H(C)=h(a)+h(b)-J_{a,b}(|c|^2),
\]
where \(h\) is binary entropy. On the strict probability interval,
\[
J'(r)=\log\frac{yz}{xw},\qquad
J''(r)=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z+\frac1w\ge16,
\qquad J'(0)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
The reciprocal bound follows from \(x+y+z+w=1\). Therefore
\(J(r)-8r^2\) is convex and nondecreasing on \(r\ge0\). Its composition with the convex function \(|c|^2\) is convex. Consequently
\[
c\longmapsto H(C)+8|c|^4
\quad\hbox{is concave on its feasible disk.}
\tag{8}
\]
Continuity includes the disk boundary. If a fixed diagonal is 0 or 1, feasibility forces the corresponding off-diagonal entry to vanish, so that degenerate case is trivial.

For complex numbers \(c_1-c_0=v\ne0\), set \(c_t=(1-t)c_0+tc_1\) and \(c_t/v=\xi+i\eta\). Direct expansion and completing the square give
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(1-t)|c_0|^4+t|c_1|^4-|c_t|^4\\
&=t(1-t)|v|^4
\left[
6\left(\xi+\frac{1-2t}{3}\right)^2
+2\eta^2+\frac{1-t+t^2}{3}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)–(9) prove (A) for each conditional law, hence by (2) in arbitrary dimension.

**Sharpness.** Fix \(t\in(0,1)\). Take \(a=b=1/2\), real \(v\downarrow0\), and
\[
c_0=-\frac{1+t}{3}v,\qquad
c_1=\frac{2-t}{3}v.
\]
These endpoints are strict contractions for sufficiently small \(v\), and the squared terms in (9) vanish. Since
\[
H\!\begin{pmatrix}1/2&c\\\bar c&1/2\end{pmatrix}
=2\log2-8|c|^4+O(|c|^8),
\]
the ratio of the chord gap to
\(t(1-t)(1-t+t^2)|v|^4\) tends to \(8/3\).
Thus the constant is sharp at every fixed interpolation parameter; sharpness is asymptotic, not a claimed nontrivial equality example.

There is a useful refinement. Write \(c_Y(t)=(C_Y(t))_{ij}\), and define the fixed conditional variance
\[
\bar c(t)=\sum_Yq_Yc_Y(t),\qquad
\sigma^2=\sum_Yq_Y|c_Y(t)-\bar c(t)|^2.
\]
It is independent of \(t\), because all \(c_Y\) have the same derivative \(v\). Averaging (9), the variance contributions in its squares give
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G(t)\ge t(1-t)
\left[
\frac83(1-t+t^2)|v|^4+16|v|^2\sigma^2
\right].}
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, if \(R_Y=\Re(\bar v c_Y)\), \(I_Y=\Im(\bar v c_Y)\), then
\(6\operatorname{Var}R_Y+2\operatorname{Var}I_Y
\ge2|v|^2\sigma^2\). Keeping the excess gives an additional valid term
\(32t(1-t)\operatorname{Var}R_Y\).

The corresponding pointwise estimate is
\[
\boxed{
-F''(t)\ge
64\sum_Yq_Y[\Re(\overline{c_Y(t)}v)]^2
+32|v|^2\sum_Yq_Y|c_Y(t)|^2.}
\tag{11}
\]
It follows directly from (7) and \(J'(r)\ge16r\). In particular,
\(-F''\ge32|v|^2\sigma^2\). A positive conditional-correlation variance supplies a uniform quadratic curvature term along this off-diagonal slice.

## 4. Complete zero-curvature classification, including singular full kernels

Assume \(d\ne0\), and consider an interior parameter \(t_0\in(0,1)\). Then \(F\) is real analytic near \(t_0\), and
\[
\boxed{
F''(t_0)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u=s=0
\ \text{and}\ 
(C_Y(t_0))_{ij}=0
\ \text{for every }Y\text{ with }q_Y>0.}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the two selected indicators are conditionally independent, given the entire outside configuration, at precisely such a flat point. This is an exact finite conditional-kernel criterion; no graph criterion at arbitrary boundary kernels is assumed.

We first justify differentiability and all boundary cases in (12). If
\(A+r d\) is a two-site positive contraction for both signs of small \(r\), every vector in the 0- or 1-eigenspace of \(A\) lies in \(\ker d\). For a zero eigenvector, its quadratic form in \(A\pm\varepsilon d\) is zero; positivity makes it a null vector of both matrices, hence \(d\) kills it. Apply the same argument to \(I-A\) for a unit eigenvector.

Therefore, if \(\delta\ne0\), every \(C_Y(t_0)\) is strict. If \(\delta=0\) and \(d\ne0\), a boundary conditional kernel can only have the form
\[
C_Y(t)=\lambda(t)P
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
C_Y(t)=I-\lambda(t)P,
\]
where \(P\) is a fixed rank-one projection and \(0<\lambda(t_0)<1\).
The direction is nonzero on that rank-one range. With \(\alpha=P_{ii}\),
\[
H(\lambda P)=h(\lambda)+\lambda h(\alpha),
\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(\lambda(t)P)
=-\frac{(\lambda')^2}{\lambda(1-\lambda)}<0.
\tag{13}
\]
The complement form has the same entropy. These formulas give fixed positive atom support and analyticity locally, omitting atoms that vanish identically.

At a strict conditional kernel, \(\delta>0\) gives strict negative curvature by (4). If \(\delta=0\), (3) gives \(-H''=\operatorname{FI}>0\), since a nonzero rank-one Hermitian direction has a nonzero diagonal entry. If \(\delta<0\), equality in (3) requires both \(\operatorname{FI}=0\) and \(L=0\). Marginal derivatives give \(u=s=0\); the identity \(yz-xw=|c|^2\) gives \(c=0\). Conversely these conditions make both terms vanish.

Every term in the finite weighted sum (2) is nonpositive. The preceding analysis proves (12), including full kernels with zero or unit eigenvalues. Because
\(c_Y(t)=c_Y(0)+tv\), with \(v\ne0\) in (12), there is at most one zero-curvature parameter on a nonconstant chord. Endpoint derivatives can be singular and are not asserted here.

## 5. Exact flat-point loss and the obstruction to quadratic strong concavity

At a flat point from (12), write
\(C_Y(t_0)=\operatorname{diag}(a_Y,b_Y)\). All these conditional kernels are strict, since \(\det d=-|v|^2<0\). The conditional marginals remain fixed along the line, so for every feasible \(\tau\),
\[
\boxed{
F(t_0+\tau)=F(t_0)
-\sum_Yq_Y
D_{\rm KL}\!\left(
P_{C_Y(t_0+\tau)}
\,\middle\|\,
\operatorname{Ber}(a_Y)\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(b_Y)
\right).}
\tag{14}
\]
The loss is exactly the conditional mutual information of the two selected indicators given the outside configuration.

Each conditional probability vector is exactly its product value plus
\(|v|^2\tau^2(-1,1,1,-1)\). Expanding relative entropy on its positive support yields
\[
\boxed{
F(t_0+\tau)=F(t_0)
-\frac{|v|^4\tau^4}{2}
\sum_Y\frac{q_Y}{a_Y(1-a_Y)b_Y(1-b_Y)}
+O(\tau^6).}
\tag{15}
\]
The coefficient of \(-\tau^4\) is at least \(8|v|^4>0\), and
\[
F^{(4)}(t_0)=
-12|v|^4\sum_Y
\frac{q_Y}{a_Y(1-a_Y)b_Y(1-b_Y)}
\le-192|v|^4.
\]
Equality in this coefficient bound occurs when every positive-weight conditional diagonal is \((1/2,1/2)\); a decoupled uniform two-site block realizes it. Every odd Taylor coefficient vanishes at a flat point. This extends the quartic mechanism to all feasible flat two-coordinate block lines, without requiring the full kernel to be interior.

Uniform quadratic strong concavity fails even on a fixed spectral-interior set. Take
\[
K_\pm=\frac12I_2\pm\varepsilon
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The midpoint gap is \(8\varepsilon^4+O(\varepsilon^8)\), whereas
\(\|K_+-K_-\|_F^2=8\varepsilon^2\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), both endpoints lie in any prescribed spectral strip
\(\eta I\preceq K\preceq(1-\eta)I\), \(0<\eta<1/2\).
Thus a positive universal quadratic modulus cannot follow from a spectral gap alone. Equations (10)–(11) provide an explicit additional hypothesis that does suffice on pure-off-diagonal slices.
