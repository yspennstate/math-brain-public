## 1. Results and exact scope

Let $K$ be a Hermitian positive contraction in the actual point-process coordinate basis, $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)$ with real entries, and
\[
A=i[D,K].
\]
Every derivative below is along the **fixed affine line** $K+tA$, unless an orbit is explicitly identified.

1. **Principal-minor curvature, every dimension.** At $0<K<I$, every principal determinant has zero first derivative and
   \[
   D^2\det K_S[A_S,A_S]
   =-\det K_S\,\operatorname{tr}[(K_S^{-1}A_S)^2]\le0.
   \tag{1}
   \]
   Equality holds exactly when $A_S=0$. The inequality, interpreted polynomially, extends to boundary contractions.

2. **Every supermodular function, three sites.** If $n=3$ and $f$ is supermodular, then
   \[
   D^2F_f(K)[A,A]\le0,\qquad F_f(K)=\mathbb E_{P_K}f(X).
   \tag{2}
   \]
   Section 3 gives an exact formula and the full interior equality condition.

3. **Strict entropy curvature, three sites.** If $0<K<I$, then
   \[
   \boxed{D^2H(K)[i[D,K],i[D,K]]<0
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [D,K]\ne0.}
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Every atom first derivative and the entire Fisher term vanish. Thus the strictness in (3) comes entirely from the frozen log-atom curvature. The directions can have rank three and can arise from a genuinely nonreal cycle phase.

4. **Precise boundary extension.** At a three-site boundary kernel, suppose the affine direction $A=i[D,K]$ is feasible: $K+t_0A$ is a positive contraction for some $t_0\ne0$. Then the line is feasible on a two-sided neighborhood of zero, its entropy is smooth there, and (3) holds for the entropy restricted to that line. Nonzero orbit tangents at boundary kernels are often not affine feasible. No ambient boundary Hessian or neighborhood-concavity theorem is asserted.

5. **A sufficient cone in every dimension.** Statement (2) holds when, modulo a modular function,
   \[
   f(T)=\sum_{|S|\ge2}\alpha_S\mathbf1_{S\subseteq T}
        +\sum_{|S|\ge2}\beta_S\mathbf1_{S\cap T=\varnothing},
   \qquad \alpha_S,\beta_S\ge0.
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Equality at an interior kernel is exactly $A_S=0$ for every support with $\alpha_S+\beta_S>0$. This cone contains every three-site supermodular function modulo modular terms, but is a proper subcone of the supermodular cone already on four sites. For entropy, (4) is a sufficient condition on the matched function $f=-\log p_K$; it is not shown to hold for arbitrary kernels in higher dimensions.

These conclusions do not establish concavity in arbitrary Hermitian three-site directions, and the gauge condition at one base generally does not persist along its affine line. They therefore do not prove full three-site or general Lyons concavity.

## 2. Principal minors and the distinction between a tangent and an orbit

For a principal set $S$, diagonality of $D$ gives
\[
A_S=i[D_S,K_S].
\]
If $K_S>0$, cyclicity yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}(K_S^{-1}A_S)
=i\operatorname{tr}(K_S^{-1}D_SK_S-D_S)=0.
\]
Jacobi's determinant derivative formula now gives (1). To check its sign without treating a non-Hermitian matrix as Hermitian, write
\[
B_S=K_S^{-1/2}A_SK_S^{-1/2}=B_S^*.
\]
The matrix $K_S^{-1}A_S$ is similar to $B_S$, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}[(K_S^{-1}A_S)^2]
=\operatorname{tr}(B_S^2)=\|B_S\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\ge0,
\]
and it vanishes exactly when $A_S=0$. In particular, for a pair,
\[
D^2\det K_{ij}[A_{ij},A_{ij}]=-2|A_{ij}|^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Here the $A_{ij}$ inside the absolute value is the single off-diagonal entry. Singletons have zero curvature, and $A$ has zero diagonal.

For comparison, the actual diagonal-unitary orbit is
\[
K^{\rm orb}(t)=e^{itD}Ke^{-itD},\qquad
(K^{\rm orb})'(0)=A,\qquad
(K^{\rm orb})''(0)=B:=-[D,[D,K]].
\tag{6}
\]
Every principal minor and every DPP atom is constant on this curved orbit. The chain rule reads
\[
D^2p_S(K)[A,A]+Dp_S(K)[B]=0,
\tag{7}
\]
not $D^2p_S(K)[A,A]=0$. At an interior base the same distinction gives
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]+DH(K)[B]=0.
\tag{8}
\]
The negative affine curvature is compatible with constant entropy on the orbit because the orbit has nonzero acceleration. The exact certificate checks (7) separately for every atom in three complex examples.

## 3. Three-site supermodularity gives an exact curvature formula

Write the Boolean Möbius expansion
\[
f(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}c_S,\qquad
F_f(K)=\sum_Sc_S\det K_S.
\tag{9}
\]
On three sites, supermodularity is precisely the six nonlinear inequalities
\[
c_{ij}\ge0,\qquad c_{ij}+c_{123}\ge0
\quad(ij=12,13,23).
\tag{10}
\]
These are the elementary second differences at the empty set and at the remaining singleton. Set
\[
\gamma=|c_{123}|,
\qquad
(J,b_{ij})=
\begin{cases}
(K,c_{ij}),&c_{123}\ge0,\\
(I-K,c_{ij}+c_{123}),&c_{123}<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
Then $b_{ij}\ge0$, and
\[
\boxed{
D^2F_f(K)[A,A]
=-2\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2
-\gamma\det J\,\operatorname{tr}[(J^{-1}A)^2].}
\tag{12}
\]

For $c_{123}\ge0$, this follows directly from (1), (5), and (9). For $c_{123}<0$, put $f^c(T)=f(E\setminus T)$. Its pair coefficients are $c_{ij}+c_{123}$, its triple coefficient is $-c_{123}$, and
\[
F_f(K)=F_{f^c}(I-K),\qquad -A=i[D,I-K].
\]
The transformed trace square is unchanged by replacing $A$ by $-A$. This proves (12) with all signs fixed.

Because $J>0$, (12) gives the complete interior equality classification:

- If $c_{123}\ne0$, equality holds if and only if $A=0$.
- If $c_{123}=0$, equality holds if and only if $A_{ij}=0$ on every pair with $c_{ij}>0$.

For a general supermodular $f$, nonzero equality directions can occur in the second case. For example, modular $f$ has zero curvature in every such direction. The additional entropy argument below is needed to rule them out for its matched $f$.

## 4. The frozen entropy function and strictness

Assume $0<K<I$. Put
\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0,
\qquad p_S=p_S(K)=\det(I-K)\det L_S>0.
\tag{13}
\]
The atom identity follows by expanding the generating determinant
\[
\det(I-K+KZ)=\det(I-K)\det(I+LZ),
\]
with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(z_i)$. For completeness, $f(S)=-\log p_S$ is supermodular: the positive $2\times2$ Schur complement of $L_T$ gives
\[
\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}\det L_T
\le\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}\det L_{T\cup\{j\}},
\]
and taking negative logarithms gives the required elementary inequality.

Freeze this function at the base $K$. Differentiating entropy and using normalization gives
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S}
+D^2F_f(K)[A,A].
\tag{14}
\]
Diagonal-unitary conjugation preserves all principal determinants, hence preserves all atoms by inclusion-exclusion. Its tangent therefore satisfies
\[
Dp_S(K)[i[D,K]]=0\quad\text{for every }S.
\tag{15}
\]
The Fisher term in (14) is exactly zero. Equations (12)–(15) already prove nonpositive entropy curvature.

If $c_{123}\ne0$, (12) makes the curvature strictly negative whenever $A\ne0$. It remains to handle $c_{123}=0$.

Let $\ell_i=L_{ii}>0$ and normalize the three edges by
\[
a=\frac{L_{12}}{\sqrt{\ell_1\ell_2}},\qquad
b=\frac{L_{23}}{\sqrt{\ell_2\ell_3}},\qquad
c=\frac{L_{31}}{\sqrt{\ell_3\ell_1}}.
\]
Put $x=|a|^2,y=|b|^2,z=|c|^2$, each in $[0,1)$, and
\[
\Delta=1-x-y-z+2\Re(abc)>0.
\]
The nonlinear Möbius coefficients are
\[
c_{12}=-\log(1-x),\quad
c_{23}=-\log(1-y),\quad
c_{13}=-\log(1-z),
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
c_{123}=\log\frac{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}{\Delta}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus $c_{123}=0$ is exactly the identity
\[
\boxed{2\Re(abc)=xy+xz+yz-xyz.}
\tag{18}
\]
If, for example, $a=0$, then (18) gives $yz=0$. A zero edge therefore forces another zero edge. The graph with an edge $ij$ when $L_{ij}\ne0$ can only be a triangle, a single edge with an isolated vertex, or the empty graph. It is a union of cliques.

If it is a triangle, every pair coefficient in (16) is positive. Otherwise $L$ is block diagonal with blocks of size at most two, and so is
\[
K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\]
Consequently every nonzero off-diagonal entry of $K$ lies on a nonzero edge of $L$, and its pair coefficient in (16) is positive. Any nonzero commutator entry
\[
A_{ij}=i(d_i-d_j)K_{ij}
\]
is therefore detected by a strictly negative pair term in (12). This proves (3), including the zero-triple-coefficient case.

The graph argument is special to three sites. It does not assume, or imply, that a general matrix function preserves the exact edge support of a dense matrix. It uses only the specific triangle/single-edge/empty alternatives forced by (18).

## 5. Exact genuinely complex rank-three examples

Take
\[
K=\frac1{10}
\begin{pmatrix}
4&1&-i\\
1&4&1\\
i&1&4
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,2),
\qquad
A=\frac1{10}
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-i&-2\\
i&0&-i\\
-2&i&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{19}
\]
The Gershgorin interval for $K$ is $[1/5,3/5]$, so $0<K<I$. The cycle product
\[
K_{12}K_{23}K_{31}=\frac{i}{1000}
\]
is invariant under diagonal coordinate phases and is nonreal; this kernel cannot be made real by a diagonal-unitary change of coordinates. For every three-site kernel,
\[
\det(i[D,K])
=2(d_1-d_2)(d_2-d_3)(d_3-d_1)
\Im(K_{12}K_{23}K_{31}).
\tag{20}
\]
Here $\det A=1/250\ne0$, so this is genuinely a rank-three direction, outside the rank-two indefinite argument in 019.

The exact atoms of (19) are

| Atom class | Probability of each atom |
|---|---:|
| Empty set | $99/500$ |
| Each singleton | $76/500$ |
| Each pair | $49/500$ |
| Full set | $26/500$ |

All atom first derivatives vanish. The common pair coefficient and triple coefficient are
\[
c_{ij}=\log\frac{5776}{4851}>0,
\qquad
c_{123}=\log\frac{11647251}{11413376}>0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\sum_{i<j}|A_{ij}|^2=\frac3{50},\qquad
D^2\det K[A,A]=-\frac6{125}.
\]
Thus the exact entropy curvature is
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\frac3{25}\log\frac{5776}{4851}
-\frac6{125}\log\frac{11647251}{11413376}<0.}
\tag{21}
\]

The verifier also changes the diagonal of $K$, leaving its off-diagonal entries and $A$ fixed:

- With diagonal $1/2$, the empty and full atoms are $11/100$, the other six atoms are $13/100$, $c_{123}=0$, and
  \[
  D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\frac3{25}\log\frac{13}{11}<0.
  \tag{22}
  \]
  This tests the nontrivial zero-triple branch of the strictness proof, still with $\det A=1/250$.
- With diagonal $3/5$, the triple coefficient is negative. Its exponential is $11413376/11647251$. The complement proof applies and gives exactly (21). This checks the complement signs rather than only the nonnegative-coefficient case.

For a real three-site base, $i[D,K]$ is $i$ times a real antisymmetric matrix, hence has rank at most two. Formula (20) explains precisely why the complex examples enlarge that previously covered class.

## 6. Boundary feasibility, strictness, and regularization

### 6.1 An affine-feasibility criterion in every dimension

Let $0\le K\le I$, $A=i[D,K]$. If $v\in\ker K$, then $v^*Av=0$. If $K+t_0A\ge0$ for any $t_0\ne0$, positivity and
\[
v^*(K+t_0A)v=0
\]
force $(K+t_0A)v=0$, so $Av=0$. Applying the same argument to $I-K-t_0A\ge0$ gives
\[
A\ker K=0,\qquad A\ker(I-K)=0.
\tag{23}
\]
Conversely, (23) makes $A$ vanish on the two endpoint eigenspaces. On their orthogonal complement $K$ is a strict contraction. A sufficiently small two-sided interval $K+tA$ is therefore feasible. Thus (23) is necessary and sufficient for affine feasibility; in particular, one-sided feasibility of this gauge direction already implies two-sided local feasibility.

For a simple obstruction, take
\[
P=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
\]
Its nonzero gauge tangent satisfies
\[
\det(P+t\,i[D,P])=-t^2/4<0\quad(t\ne0).
\]
Its curved gauge orbit is feasible, but its straight tangent line is not.

### 6.2 Three-site feasible boundary directions reduce to an exact two-site law

Suppose now $n=3$, $K$ is boundary, (23) holds, and $A\ne0$. A nonzero commutator cannot act on an interior spectral space of dimension at most one. Hence there are exactly two interior eigenvalues and one endpoint eigenvalue $\kappa\in\{0,1\}$, with unit endpoint eigenvector $w$.

Equation (23) implies $Dw\in\operatorname{span}(w)$: for $Kw=0$, $Aw=-iKDw=0$; for $Kw=w$, $Aw=i(I-K)Dw=0$. Since $D$ is diagonal, every coordinate in the support of $w$ has the same $d_i$. If that support were all three sites, $D$ would be scalar and $A=0$. Thus, after relabeling, $w$ is supported in $\{1,2\}$. Choose a unit $v\in\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)$ orthogonal to $w$. Both $v$ and $e_3$ are eigenvectors of $D$. This also covers a one-coordinate $w$; in that case $v$ is the other coordinate vector and no equality of the two ambient diagonal entries of $D$ is required.

After complementing if $\kappa=1$, write
\[
K=VCV^*,\qquad V=[\,v\ e_3\,],\qquad 0<C<I,
\]
and
\[
A=VA_CV^*,\qquad A_C=i[D_C,C],
\qquad D_C=\operatorname{diag}(d_v,d_3).
\tag{24}
\]
No unitary invariance of subset entropy is invoked. Instead, the physical-coordinate atom law is explicit. Let $q=|v_1|^2\in[0,1]$. Sample a two-site DPP with kernel $C$, with virtual sites $v$ and 3. Whenever virtual site $v$ is present, replace it by site 1 with probability $q$, and by site 2 with probability $1-q$, independently of the two-site sample. The principal minors of $VCV^*$ verify this law directly: sites 1 and 2 cannot both occur; the probabilities containing exactly one of them are $q$ or $1-q$ times the corresponding virtual probability.

It follows, throughout the locally feasible affine line, that
\[
H(K+tA)=H(C+tA_C)+C_{vv}\,h(q),
\qquad h(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q).
\tag{25}
\]
The added term is constant because the commutator has zero diagonal. The two-site version of Sections 3–4 has
\[
D^2H(C)[A_C,A_C]=-2c_{v3}|(A_C)_{v3}|^2,
\]
where
\[
c_{v3}=-\log\left(1-
\frac{|[C(I-C)^{-1}]_{v3}|^2}
{[C(I-C)^{-1}]_{vv}[C(I-C)^{-1}]_{33}}\right).
\]
In two dimensions, $[C(I-C)^{-1}]_{v3}=C_{v3}/\det(I-C)$; hence $c_{v3}>0$ whenever $A_C\ne0$. Equation (25) proves strict negative boundary curvature for every feasible nonzero gauge direction. When $A=0$, the line is constant and its curvature is zero.

An exact non-coordinate boundary example in the certificate is
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
18/125&24/125&3/50\\
24/125&32/125&2/25\\
3/50&2/25&3/5
\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1).
\tag{26}
\]
Here $v=(3/5,4/5,0)$, $w=(-4/5,3/5,0)$, $q=9/25$, and
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&1/10\\1/10&3/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The line is feasible at least for $|t|\le1$, $Aw=Kw=0$, the zero atoms remain identically zero along it, and
\[
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(K+tA)\bigg|_{t=0}
=-\frac1{50}\log\frac{629}{529}<0.}
\tag{27}
\]

### 6.3 Exactly what regularization preserves

For arbitrary boundary $K$, define
\[
K_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I,
\qquad A_\varepsilon=i[D,K_\varepsilon]=(1-2\varepsilon)A.
\]
For a fixed finite $f$, $D^2F_f(K_\varepsilon)[A_\varepsilon,A_\varepsilon]$ is polynomial in the matrix entries and converges to the boundary polynomial derivative. This extends (2), and the cone statement (4), without requiring affine feasibility.

For entropy, $f_\varepsilon=-\log p_{K_\varepsilon}$ is not fixed and may diverge. Thus a blanket claim of a continuous ambient entropy Hessian would be unjustified. In the three-site affine-feasible case just proved, (25) shows the atom support is constant near $t=0$ and the entropy along that line is smooth. A zero base atom is identically zero on the face line. Its regularized second derivative is $O(\varepsilon)$, while its positive regularized probability is a polynomial in $\varepsilon$ with finite positive leading order, so its log is $O(|\log\varepsilon|)$. Such atoms contribute $o(1)$ to the entropy second derivative. Positive base atoms converge ordinarily, and all regularized Fisher terms vanish by (15). Consequently
\[
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}
D^2H(K_\varepsilon)[A_\varepsilon,A_\varepsilon]
=\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(K+tA)\bigg|_{t=0}\le0,
\tag{28}
\]
with strictness exactly as in (3). Without affine feasibility, only the interior regularized inequalities are asserted; there need not be a physical affine entropy line through the boundary point.

## 7. A sufficient all-dimensional cone, exact equality, and its limitation

Let $\mathcal C_n$ consist of functions admitting (4), plus arbitrary modular terms. Every inclusion unanimity function $\mathbf1_{S\subseteq T}$ and exclusion unanimity function $\mathbf1_{S\cap T=\varnothing}$ is supermodular: each elementary second difference is either zero or one. Hence $\mathcal C_n$ is contained in the supermodular cone.

For $f\in\mathcal C_n$, the DPP inclusion and complement identities give
\[
F_f(K)=\text{an affine function of the diagonal of }K
+\sum_S\alpha_S\det K_S
+\sum_S\beta_S\det(I-K)_S.
\]
Applying (1) to $K$ with $A$, and to $I-K$ with $-A=i[D,I-K]$, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2F_f(K)[A,A]
={}&-\sum_S\alpha_S\det K_S\,
\|K_S^{-1/2}A_SK_S^{-1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2\\
&-\sum_S\beta_S\det(I-K)_S\,
\|[(I-K)_S]^{-1/2}A_S[(I-K)_S]^{-1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
At an interior kernel, every determinant weight in (29) is positive. Thus equality is exactly
\[
A_S=0\quad\text{whenever }\alpha_S+\beta_S>0.
\tag{30}
\]
In particular, a positive full-set coefficient in either part makes the curvature strict for every $A\ne0$. Formula (30) is tied to an exhibited representation, but is an exact equality criterion for that representation; no cancellation between its terms is possible.

Every three-site supermodular function lies in $\mathcal C_3$ modulo modular terms. If $c_{123}\ge0$, use its nonnegative nonlinear Möbius coefficients as the $\alpha$'s. If $c_{123}<0$, use
\[
\beta_{123}=-c_{123},\qquad
\alpha_{ij}=c_{ij}+c_{123},
\]
and adjust the modular part. Expanding $\prod_{i=1}^3(1-x_i)$ verifies the coefficients.

The inclusion is proper already on four sites. Use the explicit function from 030,
\[
f(T)=\mathbf1_{34\subseteq T}+
\mathbf1_{123\subseteq T}+\mathbf1_{124\subseteq T}
-\mathbf1_{1234\subseteq T}.
\tag{31}
\]
Its elementary second differences, as functions of the remaining coordinates, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
12&x_3+x_4-x_3x_4\\
13&x_2(1-x_4)\\
14&x_2(1-x_3)\\
23&x_1(1-x_4)\\
24&x_1(1-x_3)\\
34&1-x_1x_2
\end{array}
\]
and are nonnegative on the Boolean cube. Suppose (31) had a representation (4). For every pair except 34, its second difference at the empty context is zero. A positive $\beta_S$ containing any such pair would contribute positively there, which is impossible because every other contribution is nonnegative. Thus only $\beta_{34}$ could remain. But $\Delta_{34}f(\{1,2\})=0$, while that coefficient would contribute positively there as well. Therefore all $\beta_S=0$. The nonlinear Möbius coefficients would then all equal nonnegative $\alpha_S$, contradicting $c_{1234}=-1$.

This proves that $\mathcal C_4$ is proper. Coordinate embedding gives the same conclusion for $n>4$: the zero elementary differences involving an extra coordinate forbid every nonlinear positive support involving that coordinate. The certificate checks the four-site zero-difference exclusion for every possible nonlinear $\beta$ support.

The example being outside this cone is **not** a positive gauge-curvature certificate. It only marks the limitation of this sufficient condition. Neither all-dimensional gauge curvature for every supermodular $f$, nor all-dimensional matched-entropy gauge curvature, is settled by this report.
