   \[
   \Omega(K)=\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb R^n}
   \operatorname{osc}_{S\subseteq E}
   \left(\log p_S(K)-\sum_{i\in S}\theta_i\right).

   \boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]

## 1. Exact Hessian and its modularly centered log integral

### 1.1 Target and compulsory nonlinear acceleration

Let `0<K<I` be a complex Hermitian kernel on a finite coordinate set `E`, with `|E|=n`. Its DPP satisfies

\[
P_K(U\subseteq X)=\det K_U,\qquad
p_S(K)=P_K(X=S),\qquad
H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq E}p_S(K)\log p_S(K).
\]

The interior condition makes every atom positive. Along an affine kernel path `K+tA`, with `A=A*`, put

\[
M=I-K,\quad R=M^{-1},\quad L=R-I,
\quad B=RAR,\quad C_L=BMB=RARAR.
\tag{1}
\]

Direct inverse differentiation gives

\[
L'=B,\qquad L''=2C_L.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, `L` is not affine, even though `K` is. The standard L-ensemble atom formula is

\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det R}.
\tag{3}
\]

For nonempty `S`, let `Z_S` be `L_S^{-1}` embedded into the full coordinate space; put `Z_empty=0`. Define

\[
\Delta_S=Z_S-M,\quad
s_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB),\quad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB\Delta_SB),\quad
\tau_S=s_S^2-\kappa_S.
\tag{4}
\]

Here all quantities are evaluated at the base kernel. To check the acceleration explicitly, log-determinant differentiation gives

\[
(\log p_S)'=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB)-\operatorname{tr}(MB),
\]

\[
(\log p_S)''
=2\operatorname{tr}(Z_SC_L)
-\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB Z_SB)
-\operatorname{tr}(MBMB).
\tag{5}
\]

The last sign in (5) follows because `(log det R)''=tr(MBMB)` along the actual affine-`K` path. Cyclicity and `C_L=BMB` show that the right side of (5) equals `-kappa_S`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{p'_S=p_Ss_S,\qquad p''_S=p_S\tau_S.}
\tag{6}
\]

This is the centered-inverse identity established in 048, with its derivation included to make the dependence on (2) explicit. It holds for indefinite as well as semidefinite directions. For indefinite directions, individual `kappa_S` need not be nonnegative.

Normalization and the affine marginal identities `E_K 1_{i in X}=K_ii` imply

\[
\mathbb E s=0,\quad \mathbb E\tau=0,\quad
\mathbb E(\tau\mathbf1_{i\in X})=0\quad(i\in E),
\tag{7}
\]

\[
\mathbb E\kappa=\mathbb Es^2
=\mathcal I_K(A):=\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently the exact entropy Hessian is

\[
\boxed{H''=-\mathcal I_K(A)+\mathcal C_K(A),\qquad
\mathcal C_K(A)=-\sum_Sp''_S\log p_S.}
\tag{9}
\]

The essential cancellation used here beyond normalization is

\[
\sum_Sp''_S\left(c+\sum_{i\in S}\theta_i\right)=0
\quad\text{for every fixed }c\in\mathbb R,\ \theta\in\mathbb R^n.
\tag{10}
\]

The coefficients are frozen during differentiation. They may be chosen afresh at each base point when evaluating a Hessian; this does not differentiate a moving reference measure or omit any derivative of `L`.

### 1.2 Nonnegative resolvent functions and the exact remaining integral

For `u>=0` and `S!=empty`, define

\[
\mathcal R_u(S)
=\operatorname{tr}(L_S+uI_S)^{-1}
-\sum_{i\in S}\frac1{L_{ii}+u},
\qquad \mathcal R_u(\varnothing)=0.
\tag{11}
\]

Each `R_u(S)` is nonnegative. Indeed, the `i`th diagonal entry of the inverse of a positive definite matrix is the reciprocal of its positive scalar Schur complement. This Schur complement is at most its `i`th diagonal entry. Thus

\[
[(L_S+uI_S)^{-1}]_{ii}\ge(L_{ii}+u)^{-1},
\]

and summing proves the assertion.

Let `Gamma=D_L^{-1/2}LD_L^{-1/2}` as in the summary. Integrating the elementary scalar logarithm identity over the eigenvalues of `L_S` and subtracting the diagonal reference gives

\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty\mathcal R_u(S)\,du
=\sum_{i\in S}\log L_{ii}-\log\det L_S
=-\log\det\Gamma_S.}
\tag{12}
\]

This integral is absolutely convergent. At zero all principal inverses are finite. At infinity, inverse expansion gives

\[
\mathcal R_u(S)
=\frac{\sum_{i\ne j\in S}|L_{ij}|^2}{u^3}+O(u^{-4}),
\tag{13}
\]

because the `u^{-1}` and `u^{-2}` terms of the two traces in (11) cancel. There are only finitely many subsets, so the expansion also justifies every subsequent finite sum/integral interchange.

Equations (3) and (10) remove both the normalizing constant and the modular term `sum log L_ii`. Thus

\[
\mathcal C_K(A)
=\sum_Sp''_S[-\log\det\Gamma_S]
=\int_0^\infty J_{K,A}(u)\,du,
\tag{14}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{J_{K,A}(u)
=\sum_Sp_S\tau_S\mathcal R_u(S)
=\sum_Sp''_S\operatorname{tr}(L_S+uI_S)^{-1}.}
\tag{15}
\]

The empty trace is zero. The equality of the two sums is exactly the `u`-dependent modular cancellation in (10). All resolvent matrices in (15) are fixed at the base kernel; only `p''` represents the earlier differentiation. There is no extra differentiation of those resolvent matrices in this frozen-function formula.

The remaining necessary and sufficient compensation inequality is therefore

\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty J_{K,A}(u)\,du\le\mathcal I_K(A).}
\tag{16}
\]

It is not proved in general here. Nonnegativity of each `R_u(S)` does not determine the sign of the sum because `p''_S` has both signs.

As a useful exact tail check, differentiating `E 1_{i,j in X}=det K_{ij}` twice gives

\[
\boxed{\lim_{u\to\infty}u^3J_{K,A}(u)
=4\sum_{i<j}|L_{ij}|^2\det A_{ij}.}
\tag{17}
\]

This follows by substituting (13) into (15). In particular, favorable sign at large `u` is not automatic for semidefinite directions either.

One may also write the full Hessian using only scalar atom resolvents:

\[
H''=\int_0^\infty u\sum_S
\left(\frac{p''_S}{(p_S+u)^2}
-\frac{2(p'_S)^2}{(p_S+u)^3}\right)du.
\tag{18}
\]

For proof, use `-p log p=int_0^infty[p/(p+u)-p/(1+u)]du`, differentiate the finite atom sum twice, and use `sum p''=0`. At infinity the summed integrand is `O(u^{-2})`, and it is bounded by a constant times `u` at zero. Equation (18) is included as an equivalent representation; Claim 3 concerns the principal-`L` integrand (15), not a claimed universal sign failure of (18).

## 2. A dependence criterion for semidefinite directions

### 2.1 Proof of the modular oscillation bound

First take `A>=0` with rank `r>=2`. Then `B=RAR>=0` has rank `r`. The matrix

\[
Y_S=B^{1/2}\Delta_SB^{1/2}
\]

is Hermitian with rank at most `r`, and

\[
s_S=\operatorname{tr}Y_S,\qquad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(Y_S^2)\ge0.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz for its real eigenvalues gives `s_S^2<=r kappa_S`. It follows that

\[
-\kappa_S\le\tau_S\le(r-1)\kappa_S.
\]

Let `tau_+=max(tau,0)`, `tau_-=max(-tau,0)`. The supports are disjoint, so pointwise

\[
\kappa_S\ge\frac{(\tau_S)_+}{r-1}+(\tau_S)_-.
\]

By (7)--(8), `E tau_+=E tau_-=m_tau` and

\[
\boxed{m_\tau\le\frac{r-1}{r}\mathcal I_K(A).}
\tag{19}
\]

This trace-mass inequality is the argument from 048. Its use there was with the unadjusted oscillation of `log p_S`.

For any fixed `theta`, put `ell_S=log p_S-sum_{i in S}theta_i`. By (10), `C=-E(tau ell)`. If `ell_min<=ell_S<=ell_max`, the positive and negative masses give

\[
\mathcal C\le(\ell_{\max}-\ell_{\min})m_\tau
\le\frac{r-1}{r}\operatorname{osc}(\ell)\,\mathcal I.
\]

Taking the infimum over `theta` and substituting in (9) proves (A). Negative semidefinite `A` is treated by replacing it by `-A`, which leaves every second variation unchanged. The infimum is finite; for the inequality it need not be attained. It can, if desired, be computed as a finite linear program with lower and upper bounds on all residual values.

For a nonzero semidefinite direction, `I>0`: differentiation of `E|X|=tr K` gives `E[(|X|-E|X|)s]=tr A!=0`. Thus (A) is strictly negative when `Omega<r/(r-1)`. Rank one has `p''=0` and is the established rank-one theorem; no new rank-one claim is being made.

### 2.2 A closed-form sufficient criterion that ignores marginal scaling

Choose `theta_i=log L_ii`. Then `log p_S-sum theta_i` differs from `log det Gamma_S` by a constant. The matrix `Gamma` is positive definite with diagonal entries one. Hadamard's inequality gives `det Gamma_S<=1`.

Also `det Gamma_S>=det Gamma` for every `S`. To see this when `S` is nonempty and not full, write

\[
\det\Gamma=\det\Gamma_S\det Q,
\]

where `Q` is the positive Schur complement on `E\S`. Each diagonal entry of `Q` is at most one. Hadamard gives `det Q<=1`, proving the claim. Empty and full subsets are immediate. Therefore

\[
\log\det\Gamma\le\log\det\Gamma_S\le0,
\]

with the extrema attained at the full and empty subsets. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Omega(K)\le-\log\det\Gamma=T(K).}
\tag{20}
\]

Combining (A) and (20) proves the usable estimate

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r}T(K)\right)\mathcal I_K(A)
\quad(A\text{ semidefinite of rank }r\ge2).}
\tag{21}
\]

This is a real restriction on the interactions of `L`, not a requirement that all atoms be comparable. If `L` is replaced by `DLD`, with an arbitrary positive diagonal `D`, then its normalized correlation matrix `Gamma` is unchanged. Moreover,

\[
\log p^{\mathrm{new}}_S-\log p_S
=2\sum_{i\in S}\log D_{ii}+\text{constant},
\]

so `Omega` itself is unchanged. Thus all diagonal rescalings of any normalized correlation matrix with `det Gamma>=exp(-r/(r-1))` satisfy the same sign theorem at their corresponding kernels. No transformation of the point-coordinate basis is being made, and no arbitrary unitary invariance is assumed.

In particular `T=0` at every interior diagonal kernel, regardless of its diagonal entries. By continuity, each such kernel has an open convex ball on which `T<beta` for any preassigned `beta>0` if the ball is small enough. Choosing `beta<n/(n-1)` shows entropy concavity along every Loewner-comparable pair in that ball. This is a uniform local statement for semidefinite directions. It does **not** assert entropy concavity for arbitrary pairs in the ball, because their difference may be indefinite.

### 2.3 A complete explicit segment outside the raw atom-ratio criterion

Set

\[
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2&1/2\\
1/2&1&1/3\\
1/2&1/3&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L_0=20C_0,
\qquad K_0=I-(I+L_0)^{-1},
\tag{22}
\]

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}
1/5&2/5&0\\
2/5&4/5&0\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\eta=1/1000,\qquad K_1=K_0+\eta P.
\tag{23}
\]

The leading principal determinants of `C0` are `1,3/4,5/9`, so `C0>0`. Also `P^2=P`, `tr P=2`; it is a positive rank-two projection. For complete rational specification,

\[
K_0=\frac1{49149}
\begin{pmatrix}
45580&1290&1290\\
1290&46080&360\\
1290&360&46080
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
\]

All diagonal entries of `P` are positive, and `P` does not commute with `K0`. No actual point coordinate has a fixed diagonal on this segment. Pair atom probabilities at `K0` are unequal since the relevant principal minors of `C0` are `3/4` and `8/9`, so the base law is not uniform within each cardinality. The direction is neither rank one nor indefinite rank two, and the segment does not lie in a fixed complementary principal block or a scaled-cross-matrix slice.

Here is a uniform proof on the **whole** segment. The maximum absolute row sum of `C0` is two, hence `R0=I+20C0<=41I`. For `0<=v<=eta`, put `K(v)=K0+vP`. Then

\[
I-K(v)\ge(1/41-v)I>0,\qquad K(v)\ge K_0>0.
\]

Let `L(v)=(I-K(v))^{-1}-I`. The inverse difference formula gives

\[
0\le L(v)-L_0,\qquad
\|L(v)-L_0\|
\le\frac{v\|R_0\|^2}{1-v\|R_0\|}
\le\frac{1681}{959}<2.
\tag{25}
\]

For clarity, write the inverse as
`R0^{1/2}(I-v R0^{1/2}PR0^{1/2})^{-1}R0^{1/2}` and apply the scalar inverse bound to the middle positive matrix; this proves both assertions in (25).

Consequently each diagonal of `L(v)` is less than 22, and determinant monotonicity gives `det L(v)>=det L0=40000/9`. Hence

\[
\det\Gamma(K(v))
=\frac{\det L(v)}{\prod_iL(v)_{ii}}
\ge\frac{40000}{9\cdot22^3}
=\frac{5000}{11979}.
\tag{26}
\]

In particular `T(K(v))<=log(11979/5000)<1`; one elementary certificate is
`11979/5000<12/5<8/3<e`, the last inequality following from the positive exponential series. For the rank-two direction, (21) therefore gives `H''<=-I/2` at every point.

For the unit-parameter chord `Kt=(1-t)K0+tK1`, the direction is `A=eta P`, with `tr A=2eta`. The score Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the DPP number variance give

\[
\mathcal I_K(A)\ge
\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{\operatorname{Var}_K|X|},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_K|X|
=\operatorname{tr}K(I-K)\le3/4.
\]

Thus the second derivative on this whole chord is at most `-8 eta^2/3`. Integration proves

\[
\boxed{H(K_t)\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
+\frac{t(1-t)}{750000},\qquad0\le t\le1.}
\tag{27}
\]

The raw atom ratio from 048 is too large throughout this segment: the full-to-empty ratio equals `det L(v)>=40000/9`, so its logarithm exceeds two. In contrast, (26) controls the interaction criterion. This shows a strict enlargement of the earlier sufficient class, without claiming that no other proof could cover this particular segment.

No assertion is made that the entire set `{K:T(K)<=c}` is convex. A general finite segment is covered by (21) only when the sufficient bound is verified throughout it, as it was in (25)--(26), or when a convex neighborhood known to lie inside that set is used.

## 3. Exact failure of a termwise principal-resolvent sign argument

### 3.1 The trace-resolvent set function is not supermodular

Use the normalized matrix `C0` from (22). At parameter zero,

\[
\operatorname{tr}C_0^{-1}=43/10,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(C_0)_{12}^{-1}
=\operatorname{tr}(C_0)_{13}^{-1}=8/3,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(C_0)_{1}^{-1}=1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Delta_2\Delta_3
\left[S\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(C_0)_S^{-1}\right](\{1\})
=43/10-8/3-8/3+1=-1/30.}
\tag{28}
\]

The same second difference holds after subtracting any modular function, including the diagonal term in (11). Thus the nonnegative functions in (11) are not generally supermodular. One cannot apply the supermodular-expectation sign argument from 010 separately to each resolvent and then integrate.

### 3.2 Realization at its own matched DPP kernel

It is stronger to realize the unfavorable pairing at the kernel belonging to this very `L`. Use `L0=20C0`, `K0` from (22), and the actual coordinate-edge direction

\[
A=E_{23}+E_{32}.
\tag{29}
\]

It is Hermitian with eigenvalues `1,-1,0`; a sufficiently short signed interval `K0+tA` stays in the strict contraction set. Write `a=(K0)11=1060/1143`. For any frozen real set function `g` on three sites, principal determinant differentiation gives

\[
\sum_Sp''_Sg(S)
=-2\left[(1-a)\Delta_2\Delta_3g(\varnothing)
+a\Delta_2\Delta_3g(\{1\})\right].
\tag{30}
\]

For a direct proof, the coefficient of `t^2` in the signed atom determinant `(-1)^{3-|S|}det(K0+tA-I_{E\S})` is the negative of its `(1,1)` entry, times that sign. Grouping the resulting eight coefficients by membership of site 1 gives (30), with the factor two from the second derivative.

Apply (30) to the trace resolvent in (15). At `u=0`, the two second differences for the normalized `C0` are `1/4` and `-1/30`. Scaling by twenty multiplies both by `1/20`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{J_{K_0,A}(0)
=-\frac1{10}\left[\frac{1-a}{4}-\frac{a}{30}\right]
=\frac{35}{27432}>0.}
\tag{31}
\]

At `u=20`, the normalized resolvent is `(C0+I)^{-1}/20`. Its two second differences are `1/35` and `1/150`, giving

\[
\boxed{J_{K_0,A}(20)
=-\frac1{10}\left[\frac{1-a}{35}+\frac{a}{150}\right]
=-\frac{991}{1200150}<0.}
\tag{32}
\]

The integrand is continuous, so the positive value at zero persists on an interval of positive length. Formula (17) also gives

\[
\lim_{u\to\infty}u^3J_{K_0,A}(u)=-1600/9<0.
\tag{33}
\]

### 3.3 The integrated curvature and entropy Hessian remain negative

For `g(S)=log det L0_S`, the two differences in (30) are

\[
\Delta_2\Delta_3g(\varnothing)=\log(8/9),
\qquad
\Delta_2\Delta_3g(\{1\})=\log(80/81).
\]

Using the minus sign in the definition of `C` gives the exact integral

\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty J_{K_0,A}(u)\,du
=2\left[(1-a)\log(8/9)+a\log(80/81)\right]<0.}
\tag{34}
\]

Both weights are positive and both logarithms are negative. Thus the counterexample lies within a direction where the **integrated** frozen-log curvature has the desired sign. It specifically defeats a proposed refinement requiring that sign at every resolvent parameter.

The exact Fisher information is `24241/294894`. Rational logarithm intervals certify

\[
-0.040146742184\le\mathcal C\le-0.040146742183,
\]

\[
-0.122349160680\le H''\le-0.122349160679.
\]

This is not a positive entropy Hessian, a failure of the row/column theorem, or a failure of the integrated inequality (16).
