## 2. Conventions and claims under audit

Write \(P_K\) for the subset law with inclusion probabilities \(\det K_A\), and use natural logarithms:
\[
 h(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\quad
 H(K)=H(P_K),\quad S(K)=\operatorname{tr}h(K),\quad
 U(K)=\sum_i h(K_{ii}).
\]
Set \(C=H-S\), \(T=U-H\), and let \(G(K)\) have the unordered edges \(ij\), \(i\ne j\), for which \(K_{ij}\ne0\). Empty matchings and forests are allowed.

The audited results are
\[
 S(K)\le H(K)\le U(K),
\tag{A}
\]
\[
 T(K)\ge \max_{\mathcal F}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}I(X_i;X_j)
 \ge 8\max_{\mathcal F}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}|K_{ij}|^4,
\tag{B}
\]
\[
 C(K)\ge\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}C(K_B),
\tag{C}
\]
and, writing \(c_{ij}=|K_{ij}|^2\),
\[
 r_{ij}=K_{ii}+K_{jj}-2K_{ii}K_{jj}+2c_{ij},\qquad
 g(v)=\log2-h((1+v)/2),
\]
\[
 C(K)\ge
 \max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal M}r_{ij}g(2\sqrt{c_{ij}}/r_{ij})
 \ge 2\max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal M}c_{ij}/r_{ij}.
\tag{D}
\]
The whole summand is defined as zero at \(r_{ij}=0\); the ratio inside \(g\) is asserted to belong to \([0,1]\) when \(r_{ij}>0\).

## 3. Independent Fock construction and entropy sandwich

Use the exterior Fock space
\[
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{m=0}^n\bigwedge^m\mathbb C^n
\]
with its coordinate occupation basis. For an orthonormal eigenbasis \(u_j\) of \(K\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\in[0,1]\), put
\[
 q_J=\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j\prod_{j\notin J}(1-\lambda_j),\qquad
 \rho_K=\sum_Jq_J|u_J\rangle\langle u_J|.
\]
The weights sum to one. Rotations inside an eigenspace of \(K\) preserve every fixed-occupation-number subspace of that eigenspace and its common weight, so the construction is independent of the chosen eigenbasis. It directly includes eigenvalues zero and one. Its spectrum is the independent Bernoulli product list \((q_J)\), hence
\[
 S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\rho_K)=\sum_jh(\lambda_j)=S(K).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Take the inner product conjugate-linear in its first argument. Exterior multiplication \(a^\dagger(f)\) is linear in \(f\), and \(a(f)\) is its adjoint. Expanding in eigenmodes gives the normal-ordered moment identity
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K
 a^\dagger(f_1)\cdots a^\dagger(f_m)a(g_m)\cdots a(g_1)
 =\det[\langle g_i,Kf_j\rangle]_{i,j=1}^m.
\tag{3.2}
\]
For completeness, the expansion is the Cauchy--Binet sum
\[
 \sum_{\substack{A\subseteq[n]\\|A|=m}}
 \left(\prod_{\alpha\in A}\lambda_\alpha\right)
 \det[\langle g_i,u_\alpha\rangle]_{i,\alpha\in A}
 \det[\langle u_\alpha,f_j\rangle]_{\alpha\in A,j}.
\]
In the eigenmode product mixture, unequal index sets vanish; an equal set has occupation expectation \(\prod_{\alpha\in A}\lambda_\alpha\). The ordering of the annihilators gives the displayed signs. Unequal total creation and annihilation counts have zero expectation by total-number invariance.

In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K a^\dagger(e_j)a(e_i)=K_{ij}.
\]
There is no transpose or complex-conjugation error. In the one-particle sector, this follows immediately from
\(\operatorname{Tr}K|e_j\rangle\langle e_i|=K_{ij}\).
Taking matched coordinate vectors in (3.2) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K\prod_{i\in A}n_i=\det K_A.
\]
Consequently the occupation law is \(P_K\): explicitly every atom is recovered by
\[
 P_K(X=S)=\sum_{T\supseteq S}(-1)^{|T\setminus S|}\det K_T.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Complete coordinate dephasing \(\Delta\) therefore has entropy \(H(K)\).

Let \(V\) be the unitary from the eigenbasis of \(\rho_K\) to the occupation basis and \(B_{SJ}=|V_{SJ}|^2\). The measured distribution is \(p=Bq\), with \(B\) doubly stochastic. Strict concavity of \(-x\log x\) on \([0,1]\) proves
\[
 H(p)\ge\sum_{S,J}B_{SJ}(-q_J\log q_J)=H(q).
\]
Equality requires every row to mix only equal values of \(q_J\). Thus each occupation vector is an eigenvector of \(\rho_K\), including when some eigenvalues are zero. Equivalently \(\rho_K\) is occupation diagonal. Since every off-diagonal hopping operator has zero occupation diagonal, this implies \(K_{ij}=0\) for \(i\ne j\). Conversely diagonal \(K\) gives the independent Bernoulli occupation state. This proves the lower half of (A) with its full equality statement.

For the upper half, \(X_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(K_{ii}\) and
\[
 U(K)-H(K)=D\left(P_K\middle\|\bigotimes_iP_{X_i}\right)\ge0.
\]
A supported joint atom has positive probability in each of its marginal values, so this relative entropy is finite, even with deterministic coordinates. Equality is precisely full independence. The determinantal two-point identity gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=-|K_{ij}|^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Independence forces diagonal \(K\), which also suffices. If \(K_{ii}=0\), positivity of \(K\) forces its entire off-diagonal row to vanish; if \(K_{ii}=1\), apply the same fact to \(I-K\). Thus deterministic coordinates introduce no exceptional equality cases.

The finite construction and (3.1) also agree with the entropy formula in [Dierckx--Fannes--Pogorzelska, Proposition 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.1061). The proof above does not use a boundary inverse such as \((I-K)^{-1}\).

## 4. Forest bound, singular classical laws, and exact Markov equality

This part can first be proved for any finite joint distribution \(P\). Root each tree of a forest \(\mathcal F\) and set
\[
 Q_{\mathcal F}(x)=\prod_{\text{roots }r}P_r(x_r)
 \prod_{u\to v}P(X_v=x_v\mid X_u=x_u).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Choose conditional distributions arbitrarily at unsupported parent values. Induction away from the root shows that every one-coordinate marginal of \(Q_{\mathcal F}\) is the corresponding marginal of \(P\); therefore those unsupported parent values remain unreachable. The same induction verifies normalization.

If \(P(x)>0\), every root factor and every conditional factor selected by \(x\) is positive. Thus \(P\ll Q_{\mathcal F}\), and direct expectation of the logarithm of (4.1) gives the finite identity
\[
 D(P\|Q_{\mathcal F})
 =\sum_iH(X_i)-H(X)-\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}I(X_i;X_j).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Equality holds exactly when \(P=Q_{\mathcal F}\), with no strict-positivity hypothesis.

Suppose \(P=P_K=Q_{\mathcal F}\). Adjacent edges \(ij,jk\) in a tree imply \(X_i\perp X_k\mid X_j\). This holds for supported conditioning values by summing the other branches in (4.1), whether or not the law is strictly positive. If both edge covariances are nonzero, \(\operatorname{Var}(X_j)>0\). For a binary \(X_j\),
\[
 E[X_i\mid X_j]
 =E[X_i]+\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)}
 {\operatorname{Var}(X_j)}(X_j-E[X_j]).
\]
Applying conditional independence and the law of total covariance yields
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_k)=
 \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)\operatorname{Cov}(X_j,X_k)}
 {\operatorname{Var}(X_j)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Its right side is positive by (3.4), while its left side is nonpositive. Hence the nonzero covariance edges of the forest form a matching.

For binary variables, covariance zero is equivalent to independence: it fixes the \(11\) atom to the product of the means, and the remaining three atoms follow from the marginals. This includes deterministic variables. Accordingly a zero-covariance edge in (4.1) can be removed. At its unsupported parent values choose the conditional distribution to be the unconditional marginal as well; this does not change \(Q_{\mathcal F}\). Removing all these edges leaves a product of independent pair and singleton laws. All covariances outside the surviving matching therefore vanish. Equation (3.4) now proves that \(G(K)\) is a matching contained in \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, if \(G(K)\) is such a matching, \(K\) is a direct sum of its pair and singleton blocks. Inclusion determinants factor across blocks, and uniqueness (3.3) proves independence of the corresponding subset laws. Thus (4.2) is an equality. We have independently obtained both:
* fixed-forest equality iff \(G(K)\) is a matching contained in that forest;
* equality after maximizing the mutual-information sum iff \(G(K)\) is a matching.

For the coefficient 8, let \(a=K_{ii}\), \(b=K_{jj}\), \(c=|K_{ij}|^2\). In the order \(00,10,01,11\), the independent table is
\[
 Q=((1-a)(1-b),\,a(1-b),\,(1-a)b,\,ab)
\]
and the DPP table is \(P=Q+(-c,c,c,-c)\). Therefore
\(\|P-Q\|_1=4c\).

Here is a direct proof of the exact strictness needed from Pinsker. For \(P\ne Q\), group \(A=\{x:P(x)>Q(x)\}\), set
\(\delta=P(A)-Q(A)=\|P-Q\|_1/2>0\), and \(q=Q(A)\).
Log-sum yields \(D(P\|Q)\ge d(q+\delta\|q)\). When the relative entropy is finite, \(0<q<1\), and
\[
 d(q+\delta\|q)
 =\int_q^{q+\delta}\frac{q+\delta-t}{t(1-t)}\,dt
 > 2\delta^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]
The strict inequality follows because \(1/[t(1-t)]\ge4\), with equality only at \(t=1/2\), a set of zero length. The endpoint \(q+\delta=1\) is allowed as an improper integral. If \(q=0\) and \(\delta>0\), relative entropy is infinite and the claimed bound is immediate; \(q=1,\delta>0\) cannot occur.

In the actual DPP pair problem \(c>0\) forces \(a,b\in(0,1)\), so all entries of \(Q\) are positive and no infinite relative entropy arises. Hence
\[
 I(X_i;X_j)>8c^2\quad(c>0),\qquad I(X_i;X_j)=0\quad(c=0).
\tag{4.5}
\]
A forest maximizing \(\sum c^2\) has a positive edge whenever \(K\) is not diagonal. Applying (4.5) on that forest proves both inequalities in (B), and strictness of its second inequality and of the total quartic bound for every non-diagonal kernel.

## 5. Reordered block marginals and singular quantum support

For a coordinate partition, first order the one-particle modes block by block. Implement this operation by the exterior lift of the mode permutation, not by an unsigned permutation of occupation tensor factors. The lift is a signed permutation of occupation vectors, so it preserves the measured entropy as well as the density-matrix spectrum.

With blocks contiguous, identify
\[
 \mathcal F\left(\bigoplus_B\mathbb C^B\right)
 \cong\bigotimes_B\mathcal F(\mathbb C^B)
\]
by concatenating the block wedges in that order. A creation or annihilation operator in a given block is its local operator multiplied by the parity of the preceding blocks. In a normal-ordered monomial containing \(m\) creators and \(m\) annihilators from that block, the preceding-block factor is parity to power \(2m\), hence identity. This is the needed ordinary tensor identity; it is not an identification of arbitrary disjoint CAR algebras as ungraded tensor factors.

The global state commutes with total particle number. Partial trace and invariance under the simultaneous gauge rotation show that each ordinary block marginal commutes with its own number operator. Its balanced local moments are then exactly (3.2) with \(K_B\). These moments determine the marginal state. Explicitly, gauge-invariant matrix units \(|S\rangle\langle T|\), \(|S|=|T|\), are obtained from creators, the vacuum projection \(\prod_i(1-n_i)\), and annihilators; expanding the vacuum projection expresses them in balanced monomials. The other matrix units have expectation zero by gauge invariance. Consequently the ordinary marginal, after the stated ordering, is exactly \(\rho_{K_B}\). Covariance alone has not been used to assume Gaussianity.

The warning example in 085 is also correct. For
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0&1/2\\0&1/2&0\\1/2&0&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\]
the occupied eigenmode \((e_1+e_3)/\sqrt2\) is accompanied by an independent fair occupation of \(e_2\). The two pure components are
\[
 (|100\rangle+|001\rangle)/\sqrt2,\qquad
 (|110\rangle-|011\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
Tracing out the middle occupation qubit cancels the coherence on the outer two qubits. Moving the modes to order \(1,3,2\) changes the sign of \(|011\rangle\), restores the common outer-mode state, and gives the correct pure restriction to \(\{1,3\}\). This example rules out the tempting uncorrected noncontiguous marginal argument.

Now let \(\rho=\rho_K\), let \(\rho_B\) be its correctly ordered block marginals, and put \(\sigma=\bigotimes_B\rho_B\). If \(Q_B\) projects onto the kernel of a marginal, then
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho(Q_B\otimes I)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho_BQ_B=0.
\]
For a positive operator, zero expectation on a projection implies
\(\rho^{1/2}(Q_B\otimes I)=0\). Thus, writing \(P_B=\operatorname{supp}\rho_B\),
\[
 \operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\bigotimes_BP_B
 =\operatorname{supp}\sigma.
\tag{5.1}
\]
This proves finiteness of
\[
 D(\rho\|\sigma)=\sum_BS_{\mathrm{vN}}(\rho_B)-S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\rho).
\tag{5.2}
\]
On the product support, the logarithm of \(\sigma\) is the sum of the marginal logarithms, which proves (5.2) without requiring full rank.

Under product occupation dephasing, \(\Delta\sigma=\bigotimes_B\Delta_B\rho_B\), and
\[
 D(\Delta\rho\|\Delta\sigma)=\sum_BH(K_B)-H(K).
\tag{5.3}
\]
This is also finite: a positive joint diagonal entry has positive diagonal entries in every marginal. Applying quantum relative-entropy data processing to the completely positive, trace-preserving map \(\Delta\) and subtracting (5.3) from (5.2) proves (C).

The precise external inequality needed is \(D(\Phi\rho\|\Phi\sigma)\le D(\rho\|\sigma)\) for finite density matrices and a trace-preserving positive map; dephasing is a special case. This formulation, with singular states allowed, is present in [Frenkel, Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12194), which was checked during this audit. No equality or recovery theorem from that source is invoked.

## 6. Independent block-number pinching proves all partition equality cases

Let \(\Pi_{\mathbf m}\) be the joint projections for the separate block numbers \(N_B\), and set
\[
 E(\tau)=\sum_{\mathbf m}\Pi_{\mathbf m}\tau\Pi_{\mathbf m}.
\]
This is pinching by every block number independently. Pinching only total number would be ineffective because \(\rho_K\) already commutes with total number.

Every marginal is gauge invariant, so \(E\sigma=\sigma\), and \(\Delta E=\Delta\). Three support facts ensure that no infinite expressions are being subtracted:
\[
 \operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma,\quad
 \operatorname{supp}E\rho\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma,\quad
 \operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\operatorname{supp}E\rho.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The first was proved in (5.1). For the second, each \(P_B\) commutes with \(N_B\), so the product support of \(\sigma\) is preserved by every \(\Pi_{\mathbf m}\). For the third, if \(E\rho\,v=0\), then
\[
 0=\langle v,E\rho\,v\rangle
 =\sum_{\mathbf m}\|\rho^{1/2}\Pi_{\mathbf m}v\|^2.
\]
Every summand vanishes, and summing \(\Pi_{\mathbf m}v=v\) gives \(\rho^{1/2}v=0\).

The logarithms of \(\sigma\) and \(E\rho\), on their supports, are fixed by \(E\). Extend these logarithms arbitrarily as bounded block-diagonal operators on their kernels; (6.1) makes the extension irrelevant in every trace below. Self-adjointness of pinching in the trace inner product gives
\[
 D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(E\rho\|\sigma)
 =S_{\mathrm{vN}}(E\rho)-S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\rho)
 =D(\rho\|E\rho).
\]
Therefore the partition gap is exactly
\[
 \begin{split}
 C(K)-\sum_BC(K_B)
 &=D(\rho\|E\rho)\\
 &\quad+D(E\rho\|\sigma)
       -D(\Delta E\rho\|\Delta\sigma)
 \ \ge D(\rho\|E\rho)\ge0.
 \end{split}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The first inequality is ordinary data processing. Equality in the last inequality occurs only if \(\rho=E\rho\). This can be seen without a recovery theorem, or even without a general relative-entropy equality theorem: choose an eigenbasis of \(E\rho\) inside its number sectors. Complete dephasing of \(\rho\) in this basis equals \(E\rho\). The strict scalar-Jensen argument of Section 3 then shows
\(S_{\mathrm{vN}}(E\rho)=S_{\mathrm{vN}}(\rho)\) iff \(\rho=E\rho\).

Thus equality in (C) forces \(\rho\) to commute with every \(N_B\). A hopping operator between distinct blocks changes their separate occupation numbers; all of its diagonal number-sector blocks vanish, so its expectation in \(\rho=E\rho\) is zero. By (3.2), every cross-block \(K_{ij}\) vanishes. Conversely, block diagonal \(K\) has eigenmodes chosen within blocks, and its spectral Bernoulli weights factor. In the ordered identification its density matrix and its occupation law are products of their block versions. Entropies add, proving equality.

This proves the advertised iff block-diagonal characterization directly on the closed contraction cone. It does not infer equality by taking a limit of full-rank arguments.

## 7. Exact two-mode formula, matching equality, and both optimal constants

For
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\\bar z&b\end{pmatrix},\quad
 c=|z|^2,\quad d=ab-c,
\]
the two-particle weight is \(d\), and the vacuum weight is \(1-a-b+d\). Hopping covariance shows that the one-particle matrix is
\[
 R=J-dI,\qquad r=\operatorname{tr}R=a+b-2d.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Indeed the two-particle sector contributes \(dI\) to the one-particle covariance; the one-particle sector contributes \(R\). Alternatively, in an eigenbasis of \(J\), the eigenvalues of \(R\) are
\(\lambda_1(1-\lambda_2)\) and \(\lambda_2(1-\lambda_1)\).
Both are nonnegative throughout the closed cone.

If \(r=0\), then \(R=0\) and \(z=0\). More exactly,
\(\lambda_1(1-\lambda_2)=\lambda_2(1-\lambda_1)=0\)
forces \(J=0\) or \(J=I\). All coherence terms vanish. If \(r>0\), define
\[
 u=(a-b)/r,\qquad v=2|z|/r.
\]
The normalized one-particle matrix has eigenvalues
\((1\pm\sqrt{u^2+v^2})/2\), so positivity is exactly
\(u^2+v^2\le1\). Its normalized dephasing has eigenvalues
\((1\pm u)/2\). The common scalar sector entropies, including the term \(-r\log r\), cancel. Therefore
\[
 C(J)=r\{g(\sqrt{u^2+v^2})-g(|u|)\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since \(g'(x)=\operatorname{arctanh}x\) on \([0,1)\), termwise integration gives
\[
 g(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{x^{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The coefficients are positive and summable. Hence the series is absolutely and uniformly convergent on \([0,1]\), with its endpoint value fixed by continuity. Put \(x=u^2\), \(y=v^2\). Every term in
\[
 (x+y)^k-x^k-y^k
\]
is nonnegative for \(k\ge2\), and the \(k=2\) term is \(2xy\). This proves
\[
 g(\sqrt{u^2+v^2})-g(|u|)\ge g(v),
\]
with equality iff \(uv=0\). Also \(g(v)\ge v^2/2\), strictly for \(v>0\), already from the positive \(k=2\) term. Thus
\[
 C(J)\ge r\,g(2|z|/r)\ge 2|z|^2/r.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Equality in the first inequality is exactly \(z=0\) or \(a=b\); equality in the second, and in the total polynomial pair bound, is exactly \(z=0\).

All boundary cases are included. A diagonal pair has \(v=0\) and coherence zero even if \(u=\pm1\). A rank-one \(R\) gives \(u^2+v^2=1\), which is allowed by (7.3). The case \(v=1\) has \(u=0\) and gives \(C(J)=r\log2>r/2\) when \(r>0\). Finally positivity yields
\[
 c\le r^2/4,\quad 0\le c/r\le r/4,\quad
 0\le r\,g(2\sqrt c/r)\le r\log2.
\]
Both complete summands therefore extend continuously by zero at \(r=0\).

For a matching, apply (C) to its pair blocks and remaining singletons, and then (7.4). Taking the maximum proves (D). If equality holds in its first inequality, choose a maximizing matching. Both intermediate gaps are nonnegative, so equality in (C) forces every nonzero entry of \(K\) to be within one of those pairs, and equality in (7.4) forces equal endpoint diagonals on each nonzero edge. Conversely those conditions make every relevant gap zero. Thus equality is exactly:
\[
 G(K)\text{ is a matching, and }K_{ii}=K_{jj}
 \text{ on each of its edges}.
\]
A matching maximizing \(\sum c/r\) has a positive term whenever \(K\) is not diagonal. Strictness in (7.4) then makes both the second maximized inequality and the total polynomial lower bound strict. For diagonal \(K\), all gaps and corrections vanish.

For sharpness of both universal constants, consider
\[
 K_t=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&t\\t&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<t<1/2.
\]
This is strictly interior, \(c=t^2\), and \(r=1/2+2t^2\). Its joint table has two entries \(1/4+t^2\) and two entries \(1/4-t^2\), giving
\[
 T(K_t)=g(4t^2),\qquad C(K_t)=r\,g(2t/r).
\]
Using \(g(x)=x^2/2+O(x^4)\),
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{T(K_t)}{t^4}=8,\qquad
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{C(K_t)}{t^2/r}=2.
\]
No larger dimension-independent coefficient can work. Non-diagonal equality is impossible by the strict estimates; nonzero diagonal kernels remain equality cases. Combining (B) and (D) gives the reported computable interval for \(H(K)\), with both endpoints strict whenever \(K\) is not diagonal.

## 8. Exact scope of the proposed Lyons bridge

For two contractions \(K,L\), define
\[
 A=(K+L)/2,\quad B=(K-L)/2,\quad
 M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix},\quad
 W=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}I&I\\I&-I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The identity \(M=W(K\oplus L)W^*\) proves that \(M\) is a contraction and
\[
 S(M)=S(K)+S(L).
\]
Consequently
\[
 H(M)\ge H(K)+H(L)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C(M)\ge C(K)+C(L).
\tag{8.1}
\]
This equivalence is correct. It is between the two-copy assertion and the coherence comparison, not between either assertion and Lyons concavity.

The two output coordinate restrictions of \(M\) are both \(A\), so classical subadditivity gives \(H(M)\le2H(A)\). Therefore (8.1), if established for all \(K,L\), would imply midpoint concavity of \(H\). Each atom (3.3) is a polynomial in the entries of \(K\), and entropy is continuous on a finite probability simplex. Dyadic approximation then extends midpoint concavity to every convex weight.

The exact decomposition of the desired midpoint gap is
\[
 2H(A)-H(K)-H(L)
 =[2H(A)-H(M)]+[C(M)-C(K)-C(L)].
\tag{8.2}
\]
The first bracket is nonnegative. Concavity by itself would constrain the sum; it does not, from this identity, force the second bracket to be nonnegative. Thus necessity of the stronger bridge has not been established.

Partition superadditivity only supplies \(C(M)\ge2C(A)\). Its equality occurs iff \(B=0\), or \(K=L\). The unitary \(W\) changes the coordinate partition and generally changes occupation coherence, so one cannot transport the input partition inequality across \(W\) as an entropy inequality. No ordering correction or data-processing equality theorem repairs that missing comparison.

The source's two-site diagnostic is correct: for \(K=L=P=J_2/2\), \(M=P\oplus P\), \(H(M)=2\log2\), while the spectral lower bound is zero and the polynomial matching correction is only 1.

Even the stronger \(g\)-matching bound can fall short at an equality case of the bridge. Take
\[
 K=L=P=J_3/3,
\]
where \(J_3\) is the all-ones matrix. This is the rank-one projection whose DPP chooses one of three coordinates uniformly. Thus
\[
 S(P)=0,\quad H(P)=\log3,\quad
 M=P\oplus P,\quad H(M)=2\log3.
\]
Each nonzero pair within a copy has \(a=b=1/3\), \(c=1/9\), \(r=2/3\), and \(2\sqrt c/r=1\). Its stronger pair weight is therefore \((2/3)\log2\). A matching can select only one positive edge per three-coordinate copy; cross-copy edges have zero weight. The exact maximum of the stronger lower correction is consequently
\[
 \frac43\log2<2\log3.
