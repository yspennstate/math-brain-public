Accept P23's inherited design-conditional mean lower bound with

\[
c=\frac{12103}{50000}=0.24206.
\]

Then Gaussian label noise already gives a realized risk bounded away from zero **in probability**, in the precise sense

\[
\lim_{a\downarrow0}\limsup_{d\to\infty}
P\{\mathcal R_d\le a\sigma^2\}=0.
\tag{A}
\]

More explicitly, for every fixed failure probability \(0<\delta<1\),

\[
\boxed{\liminf_d P\left\{\mathcal R_d>
c\big[-W_0(-\delta^2/e)\big]\sigma^2\right\}\ge1-\delta.}
\tag{B}
\]

Here \(W_0\) is the principal real Lambert-W branch. No effective-rank, spectral-count, Marchenko–Pastur, or hard-edge estimate is needed. This proves nonconvergence of the realized risk to zero in probability. It also proves \(P\{\mathcal R_d>a_d\sigma^2\}\to1\) for **every deterministic** \(a_d\downarrow0\).

The threshold in (B) depends on the confidence level. These statements do **not** assert that one fixed positive threshold holds with probability tending to one. The Laplace envelope is exactly optimized within the stated exponential-Markov argument and has the optimal small-\(\delta\) order \(\delta^2\); it is not claimed to be the exact extremal Gaussian CDF or optimal numerical quantile.

The separate equivalence

\[
\frac{\mathcal R_d}{E[\mathcal R_d\mid X_d]}\to1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(M_{X_d}^2)}{\operatorname{tr}(M_{X_d})^2}\to0
\tag{C}
\]

is independently proved below. It concerns **relative concentration**, not the existence of some positive threshold exceeded with probability tending to one. The latter property does not require (C), even with a constant conditional mean.

## Claim 1 — Universal lower-tail and fixed-confidence transfer

Let \(X\) be arbitrary, let \(M_X\) be a measurable finite-dimensional positive semidefinite matrix with finite trace almost surely, and let \(Z\mid X\sim N(0,I)\). Write

\[
Q=Z^\top M_XZ,\qquad \mu=\operatorname{tr}M_X.
\]

For every design with \(\mu>0\) and every \(0<\theta<1\),

\[
\boxed{P\{Q\le\theta\mu\mid X\}\le
\psi(\theta):=\sqrt{\theta e^{1-\theta}}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

**Proof.** Conditional diagonalization gives

\[
Q/\mu\ \overset{d}{=}\ \sum_j p_jG_j^2,
\qquad p_j\ge0,\quad \sum_jp_j=1,
\]

with independent standard real Gaussian \(G_j\). For \(t>0\),

\[
E[e^{-tQ/\mu}\mid X]
=\prod_j(1+2tp_j)^{-1/2}
\le(1+2t)^{-1/2}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed, expansion of the product \(\prod_j(1+2tp_j)\) has constant term one, linear term \(2t\), and nonnegative higher terms. Exponential Markov therefore gives

\[
P\{Q/\mu\le\theta\mid X\}
\le\inf_{t>0}\frac{e^{t\theta}}{\sqrt{1+2t}}.
\]

The logarithm has derivative \(\theta-(1+2t)^{-1}\) and positive second derivative \(2(1+2t)^{-2}\). Its unique minimum is at \(t=(1-\theta)/(2\theta)\), yielding (1.1). This proof covers arbitrary rank and arbitrary conditional eigenvalues. The comparison (1.2) is attained by rank one; the subsequent Markov step is not an equality for a nondegenerate Gaussian quadratic form. \(\square\)

The function \(\psi\) increases continuously from zero to one. Its exact inverse on \((0,1)\) is

\[
\theta_\delta=-W_0(-\delta^2/e),\qquad
\theta_\delta e^{1-\theta_\delta}=\delta^2.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently \(P\{Q>\theta_\delta\mu\mid X\}\ge1-\delta\). The simpler choice \(\theta=\delta^2/e\) gives an even smaller failure bound than \(\delta\). The fully rational choice \(\theta=\delta^2/3\) is also valid because \(e<3\).

There is an exact finite-sample design transfer. If \(v>0\) and \(P_X\{\mu\ge v\}\ge1-\eta\), then

\[
P\{Q\le\theta v\}
\le\eta+(1-\eta)\psi(\theta).
\tag{1.4}
\]

On the good design event, \(\theta v/\mu\le\theta\), so (1.1) bounds the conditional failure probability by \(\psi(\theta)\); on its complement use one. Thus a conditional noise failure level \(\delta\) gives total failure at most \(\eta+(1-\eta)\delta\). If a total finite-sample failure \(\delta>\eta\) is desired, substitute \((\delta-\eta)/(1-\eta)\) into (1.3).

For completeness, the existing second-moment bound can be retained when it is better:

\[
P\{Q\le\theta\mu\mid X\}\le B(\theta):=
\min\left\{\psi(\theta),\frac{2}{2+(1-\theta)^2}\right\}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

To verify the second term, the conditional variance of \(Q/\mu\) is \(2\sum p_j^2\le2\). For a centered variable \(Y\) of variance \(v_Y\), applying Markov to \((Y+b)^2\) on \(\{Y\ge s\}\), then choosing \(b=v_Y/s\), gives \(P(Y\ge s)\le v_Y/(v_Y+s^2)\). Use \(Y=1-Q/\mu\) and \(s=1-\theta\). Formula (1.4) remains true with \(B\) in place of \(\psi\). Cantelli is stronger near the mean; the Laplace bound supplies arbitrarily high confidence at smaller thresholds.

**Exact order, not an exact best constant.** From (1.3), \(\theta_\delta\sim\delta^2/e\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\). Rank one gives \(Q/\mu=G^2\), for which

\[
P\{G^2\le\theta\}
=\sqrt{2/\pi}\int_0^{\sqrt\theta}e^{-u^2/2}\,du
\sim\sqrt{2/\pi}\sqrt\theta.
\]

Its lower \(\delta\)-quantile is asymptotic to \((\pi/2)\delta^2\). Therefore no universal guarantee can improve the order \(\delta^2\). The numerical constant in (1.3) is not asserted optimal.

An additional exact consequence is the equivalence

\[
Q_d\to0\text{ in probability}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu_d\to0\text{ in probability}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

For sufficiency, conditional Markov gives \(P(Q_d>u)\le P(\mu_d>a)+a/u\); first let \(d\to\infty\), then \(a\downarrow0\). For necessity, (1.1) gives

\[
P(Q_d>\theta a)\ge[1-\psi(\theta)]P(\mu_d\ge a)
\]

for every fixed \(a>0\), \(0<\theta<1\). Thus convergence of \(Q_d\) to zero forces \(P(\mu_d\ge a)\to0\). No unconditional integrability of \(\mu_d\) is needed.

## Claim 2 — The realized P23 obstruction under (H)

Assume (D) and (H), with \(\sigma^2>0\) fixed. For every \(0<\theta<1\),

\[
\boxed{\limsup_dP\{\mathcal R_d\le\theta c\sigma^2\}
\le B(\theta)\le\psi(\theta)<1.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** Fix \(0<\epsilon<c(1-\theta)\). On \(\{\mu_d\ge c-\epsilon\}\), the threshold divided by the conditional mean is at most \(\theta c/(c-\epsilon)<1\). Apply Claim 1 and (H), take \(\limsup_d\), then let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and use continuity of \(B\). This step is essential: (H) alone does not say \(\mu_d\ge c\) with high probability. \(\square\)

Taking \(\theta=\theta_\delta\) proves (B), including its threshold at the full inherited constant \(c\), without an additional fixed \(\epsilon\)-loss. Representative decimal diagnostics are below; (1.3), not the rounded table, defines the exact thresholds.

| Asymptotic success probability at least | \(\theta_\delta\) | Risk threshold as a multiple of \(\sigma^2\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 50% | 0.101828431094 | 0.024648590031 |
| 80% | 0.014936622400 | 0.003615558818 |
| 90% | 0.003692403113 | 0.000893783097 |
| 95% | 0.000920545617 | 0.000222827272 |
| 99% | 0.000036789298 | 0.000008905217 |

For example, an exact rational 95% version is

\[
\liminf_dP\left\{\mathcal R_d>
\frac{12103}{60000000}\sigma^2\right\}\ge\frac{19}{20}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The simpler large-threshold consequence of (1.5) is

\[
\liminf_dP\left\{\mathcal R_d>
\frac{12103}{100000}\sigma^2\right\}\ge\frac19,
\tag{2.3}
\]

using \(\theta=1/2\). This alone rules out convergence to zero in joint design-and-noise probability. Equation (A) is stronger: let \(a\downarrow0\) in (2.1). Equivalently, \(\sigma^2/\mathcal R_d=O_P(1)\).

For the claimed statement at every vanishing threshold \(a_d\downarrow0\), use (H) once at \(\epsilon=c/2\). Eventually \(2a_d/c<1\), and

\[
P\{\mathcal R_d\le a_d\sigma^2\}
\le P\{\mu_d<c/2\}+\psi(2a_d/c)\to0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

**Why the confidence-dependent threshold cannot be replaced by one fixed positive threshold from (H) alone.** Take deterministic rank-one matrices \(M_d=\operatorname{diag}(c,0,\ldots,0)\). Then \(\mu_d=c\) exactly, but for every fixed \(a>0\),

\[
P\{Q_d>a\}=P\{cG^2>a\}<1
\]

at every dimension. Replacing the zero eigenvalues by positive eigenvalues of total mass tending to zero, while adjusting the leading eigenvalue to keep trace \(c\), gives the same limiting example with full rank. These are counterexamples to an implication from the mean hypothesis alone, **not** counterexamples within the actual sphere-kernel model.

Gaussianity matters. For scalar mean-zero noise with probabilities \(P(Z_d=\sqrt d)=P(Z_d=-\sqrt d)=1/(2d)\) and \(P(Z_d=0)=1-1/d\), one has \(E Z_d^2=1\) but \(Z_d^2\to0\) in probability. Therefore isotropic covariance and a positive conditional mean by themselves do not supply the realized-risk conclusion outside the Gaussian setting.

## Claim 3 — Necessary and sufficient effective rank for relative concentration

Continue with the conditional Gaussian model and suppose \(\mu_d>0\) almost surely. Define

\[
\rho_d=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(M_d^2)}{\operatorname{tr}(M_d)^2}
=\sum_jp_{dj}^2\in(0,1],\qquad
S_d=Q_d/\mu_d-1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{S_d\to0\text{ in joint probability}
\iff\rho_d\to0\text{ in design probability}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The same result holds if \(P(\mu_d=0)\to0\), with arbitrary consistent definitions on that exceptional event.

**Sufficiency.** Conditional Gaussian moments give \(E(S_d^2\mid X_d)=2\rho_d\). Since \(0\le\rho_d\le1\), convergence of \(\rho_d\) to zero in probability implies \(E\rho_d\to0\). Conditional Chebyshev followed by expectation proves

\[
P\{|S_d|>u\}\le\frac{2E\rho_d}{u^2}\to0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Necessity, with a quantitative witness.** For \(Y=G^2-1\), direct Gaussian moments give \(E Y^2=2\), \(E Y^4=60\). Expanding \(S_d=\sum_jp_{dj}(G_j^2-1)\) using independence gives

\[
E(S_d^4\mid X_d)
=12\rho_d^2+48\sum_jp_{dj}^4
\le60\rho_d^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For completeness, conditional Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the nonnegative random variable \(S_d^2\) gives the usual Paley–Zygmund inequality. With threshold one half its conditional mean,

\[
P\{S_d^2>\rho_d\mid X_d\}
\ge\frac{(E[S_d^2\mid X_d]/2)^2}{E[S_d^4\mid X_d]}
\ge\frac1{60}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently, for each \(0<a\le1\),

\[
\boxed{P\{|S_d|>\sqrt a\}\ge\frac1{60}P\{\rho_d\ge a\}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

If \(S_d\to0\) in probability, the left side tends to zero for every \(a>0\), proving necessity. This argument avoids any unjustified inference from convergence in probability directly to convergence of second moments. \(\square\)

The criterion is also equivalent to

\[
\frac{\|M_d\|_{\rm op}}{\operatorname{tr}M_d}\to0,
\]

because, for \(m_d=\max_jp_{dj}\), one has \(m_d^2\le\rho_d\le m_d\). Thus it states exactly that no eigenvalue holds a nonvanishing fraction of the trace.

Combining (3.1) with (H) would give \(P\{\mathcal R_d>b\sigma^2\}\to1\) for every fixed \(0<b<c\). The converse implication is false. A counterexample **with trace fixed at \(c\)** is

\[
M_d=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac c2,
\underbrace{\frac{c}{2d},\ldots,\frac{c}{2d}}_{d\text{ entries}}\right).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Here \(\rho_d=\frac14+\frac1{4d}\to1/4\). Yet

\[
Q_d\ge\frac{c}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^dG_j^2\to c/2
\quad\text{in probability as a lower bound},
\]

so \(P(Q_d>b)\to1\) for every \(b<c/2\). Meanwhile \(Q_d/c\) converges in distribution to \((1+G^2)/2\), which is nonconstant. Therefore the P23 source's executive phrase making a fully realized positive-threshold probability-\(1-o(1)\) theorem “logically equivalent” to effective rank is too strong if that phrase denotes merely a positive-threshold obstruction. Its precise relative-risk equivalence in Section 16 is correct.

## What the actual P23 estimates establish about Claim 3

The inspected source proves or imports:

- macroscopic Frobenius control of the cumulative feature covariance \(S=\Phi^\top\Phi/n\), including \(E\|S-I\|_F^2=D(D-1)/n\) and concentration at \(D\asymp n\);
- inverse-Rayleigh lower bounds on cumulative harmonic leverage, a rank upper bound for the low block, and disjoint-window inequalities that lower-bound \(\operatorname{tr}M_X\);
- a first-crossing-shell scale of order \(1/d\), and an effective-rank estimate for a **commuting proxy conditional on additional spectral counts**.

None of the displayed proved bounds supplies an upper bound tending to zero for \(\|M_X\|_{\rm op}/\operatorname{tr}M_X\), or for \(\operatorname{tr}(M_X^2)/\operatorname{tr}(M_X)^2\). A feature-covariance Frobenius identity at macroscopic scale is not a bound on the inverse-weighted spectrum of \(K^{-1}C_XK^{-1}\). The source explicitly leaves residualized spectral counts at scale \(1/d\) and their noncommutative transfer to \(M_X\) open.

Thus Claim 3's criterion is **not established for the actual P23 matrices by the inspected estimates**. This is an identified missing proof obligation, not a claim that the criterion is false in the model. Nor does failure to prove relative concentration establish that every alternative route to a fixed positive-threshold obstruction is impossible; (3.6) shows why those objectives differ.
