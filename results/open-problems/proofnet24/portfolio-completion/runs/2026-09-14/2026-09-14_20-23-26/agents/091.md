## 3. Complete tensor comparison

Use \(F=\{1,2,3\}\) and \(S=\sum_{i=1}^5x_i\). The source's equation (7) is
\[
V_k(x)=\frac{x_a^2+x_b^2}{2}+2x_ax_b+\frac23x_4x_5,
\quad k\in F,\quad\{a,b\}=F\setminus\{k\},
\]
\[
V_4(x)=x_5^2+2x_1x_5+2x_2x_5+2x_3x_5,
\qquad
V_5(x)=x_4^2+2x_1x_4+2x_2x_4+2x_3x_4.
\tag{A}
\]
These are exactly equation (1) of QSO_FIVE_TYPE_PERTURBATION.md after expanding its last two factorizations. Hereafter call this common map \(W\).

Since the summation is over **ordered** parents, a cross-monomial coefficient is \(2P_{ij,k}\) for \(i<j\). Thus the coefficient \(2/3\) of \(x_4x_5\) means \(P_{45,k}=1/3\), not \(2/3\). The full symmetric tensor is determined by the following fifteen rows; set \(P_{ji,k}=P_{ij,k}\).

Every row sums to one and has zero in each parental output position. Among unordered-parent entries, five diagonal rows each have four allowed outputs and ten off-diagonal rows each have three, for \(5\cdot4+10\cdot3=50\) allowed coefficients. Exactly 20 are positive and 30 are zero. For instance, \(P_{11,4}=0\) is an **allowed** zero.

The unrestricted five-type class has \(5(4-1)+10(3-1)=35\) independent coefficient parameters. This single tensor lies on its boundary. The all-allowed-positive condition is the relative interior of that coefficient polytope, not the meaning of the word “strictly” in “strictly non-Volterra.”

## 4. Independent verification of the fixed points and tangent information

This section is my derivation from the reconstructed tensor. Its endpoint conclusions agree with the already published Theorem 3.

On the simplex, for distinct \(i,j,k\in F\),
\[
W_i-W_j=-(x_i-x_j)\left(\frac{x_i+x_j}{2}+2x_k\right),
\qquad
W_4-W_5=-(x_4-x_5)(2-x_4-x_5).
\tag{B}
\]
All displayed brackets are nonnegative. At a fixed point, \(d=-cd\) with \(c\ge0\) forces \(d=0\). Hence every fixed point, including any boundary one, is
\[
(r,r,r,s,s),\qquad r=(1-2s)/3,\qquad0\le s\le1/2.
\]
The fourth output is \(s(2-3s)\), so its fixed equation is \(s(1-3s)=0\). Stochasticity and equality of the first three outputs make this equation sufficient. Therefore
\[
p=M_1=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0),\qquad
q=M_2=(1/9,1/9,1/9,1/3,1/3)
\tag{C}
\]
are exactly all simplex fixed points. The first has support three and the second support five.

The tangent space \(\sum_i z_i=0\) is spanned by
\[
e_1-e_2,\quad e_1-e_3,\quad e_4-e_5,\quad h=(-2,-2,-2,3,3).
\]
Direct differentiation gives the source's spectra in precisely these directions:
\[
DW(p)|_T:\quad -1,-1,-2,2,\qquad
DW(q)|_T:\quad -1/3,-1/3,-4/3,0.
\tag{D}
\]
These were also checked by exact integer arithmetic, recorded in Section 9.

A useful distinction for perturbation arguments is
\[
\det(I-DW(p)|_T)=-12,\qquad
\det(I-DW(q)|_T)=112/27.
\tag{E}
\]
Although \(p\) is dynamically nonhyperbolic because of the eigenvalue \(-1\), it is nondegenerate for the **fixed-point equation**, which requires absence of eigenvalue \(+1\). Thus a local affine continuation exists. Its remaining within the simplex is a separate question.

## 5. Exact comparison with the positive-mixture theorem and audit 052

For the equiprobable five-type operator, equal parents distribute probability \(1/4\) to each other type and distinct parents distribute \(1/3\) to each of the three permitted types. For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), the mixture has every allowed coefficient positive. A boundary fixed point is then impossible: if \(x_k=0\), choose \(i\ne k\) with \(x_i>0\), giving
\[
(W_\varepsilon)_k(x)\ge (P_\varepsilon)_{ii,k}x_i^2>0.
\]
This positivity implication is an elementary argument, not a theorem about the source's tensor.

The equiprobable difference identity is
\[
(E_5)_i-(E_5)_j
=-(x_i-x_j)\left(\frac23-\frac5{12}(x_i+x_j)\right).
\tag{F}
\]
Combining (F) with (B) still forces all fixed points into \((r,r,r,s,s)\). There
\[
W_4=s(2-3s),\qquad (E_5)_4=(11-20s+5s^2)/36,
\]
and the necessary and sufficient fixed equation is
\[
Q_\varepsilon(s)=(-108+113\varepsilon)s^2+
(36-92\varepsilon)s+11\varepsilon=0.
\tag{G}
\]
For positive \(\varepsilon\),
\[
Q_\varepsilon(0)=11\varepsilon>0,\qquad
Q_\varepsilon(1/2)=-9-\frac{27}{4}\varepsilon<0.
\]
A polynomial of degree at most two with opposite nonzero endpoint signs has exactly one root in that interval. This proves the all-parameter simplex count, including the linear case \(\varepsilon=108/113\). The further evaluations at \(1/5\) and \(1/3\) locate \(s_\varepsilon\in[1/5,1/3)\). These arguments reproduce the portion of the current theorem needed for the scope comparison.

## 6. Interpreting the 2017 dynamics without overstating its printed conditions

Here is an independent recurrence calculation, useful both to check the scope and to remove the ambiguity of the printed third condition in Theorem 5.

Put \(t=x_4+x_5\) and \(d=|x_4-x_5|\). From the common tensor (A),
\[
d'=d(2-t),\qquad
t'=2t-\frac32t^2+\frac12d^2.
\tag{H}
\]
If \(d_0>0\), then \(d_n\) is nondecreasing, bounded by one, and has a positive limit \(L\). Hence \(d_{n+1}/d_n\to1\), so \(t_n\to1\). Passing to the second recurrence gives \(1=1/2+L^2/2\), therefore \(L=1\). The signed difference changes sign every step because its multiplier is \(-(2-t)\). It follows that the even and odd iterates tend to opposite vertices \(e_4,e_5\). This proves the unequal-coordinate conclusion also when exactly one of the last coordinates is initially zero. If the initial point is already a vertex of that pair, it is exactly on the period-two orbit.

If \(d_0=0\) and \(s=x_4=x_5>0\), then
\[
s'=s(2-3s).
\]
The first iterate lies in \((0,1/3]\), and thereafter the sequence increases to \(1/3\), unless already fixed. Consequently \(x_1+x_2+x_3\to1/3\). For a first-group coordinate difference, the multiplier in (B) is at most \(2(x_1+x_2+x_3)\); it is therefore eventually bounded above by a constant below one. All such differences tend to zero, and the full trajectory tends to \(q\). This is a direct proof for this regime without the paper's auxiliary operator.

If both last coordinates are zero, the invariant face carries the equal-probability three-type operator, exactly the recalled Theorem 2 case. The source's stated convergence theorem there gives the limit \(p\). I have not independently re-proved that cited three-type global convergence result here.

This explains the almost-everywhere statement: the exceptional equality \(x_4=x_5\) is a lower-dimensional hyperplane in the five-type simplex. It concerns **initial states** and does not impose positive hereditary coefficients.

## 7. Relation to four types and the 2016 source limitation

The 2017 introduction on printed p. 67 summarizes reference [10] as a symmetric strictly non-Volterra class on the three-dimensional simplex, with fixed-point uniqueness, a sufficient attractiveness condition, classes with infinitely many periodic points, and some asymptotically period-two trajectories. Reference [10] on p. 74 identifies the 2016 Discontinuity, Nonlinearity, and Complexity article, pp. 263–283.

This is now an author-written primary retrospective confirmation of the same stated scope. It supplies no formula for “symmetric,” no coefficient positivity restriction, and no numbered uniqueness theorem from the 2016 paper. Parent symmetry is already in ordinary QSO equation (2) on p. 66 of the 2017 paper. Consequently the new text does not identify that ordinary condition with the additional adjective in the 2016 title. It neither verifies unrestricted four-type coverage nor proves the 2016 theorem is a proper subclass. The exact comparison remains unclosed, as in agents/035.md.

The five-type result causes no contradiction with the current unrestricted four-type theorem:

1. \(S^4\) has five types, while the current four-type theorem acts on \(S^3\).
2. The old boundary fixed point \(p\) has support **three**, not four. Thus the support-four uniqueness consequence in audit 052 does not apply to it.
3. The common operator does not have an identically zero fourth or fifth output. In particular the four-coordinate face obtained by merely deleting type 4 or type 5 is not its invariant three-dimensional simplex.
4. The second fixed point \(q\) has all five coordinates positive, so no proper face contains both old fixed points.

For completeness, combining the established three-type result, the current four-type proof, and this prior five-type example gives the threshold **five types for possible fixed-point nonuniqueness in the nonnegative strict class**. Any claim that this threshold was first established in the current project still requires the unresolved four-type literature comparison. The five-type obstruction itself must be credited to the verified 2017 paper.

There is also a simple independent extension to every \(m\ge5\). Retain (A)'s rows for parents in \(\{1,\ldots,5\}\). For a parental pair with at least one index greater than 5, assign all offspring probability to any fixed choice
\[
k(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,5\}\setminus\{i,j\},
\qquad k(i,j)=k(j,i).
\]
Such a choice always exists. All outputs greater than 5 have zero polynomial, the first-five face is invariant, and its restriction is exactly \(W\). Every fixed point lies in that face, so precisely the two padded points \((p,0,\ldots,0)\) and \((q,0,\ldots,0)\) remain. This padding argument is given here as an elementary deduction, not attributed as a theorem in Jamilov's target paper. It says nothing about the all-allowed-positive class.
