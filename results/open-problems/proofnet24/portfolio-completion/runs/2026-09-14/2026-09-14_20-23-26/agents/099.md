### Four-type domains, coefficients and norm

For \(V_r(x)=\sum_{ij}P_{ij,r}x_ix_j\), all derivatives and norm estimates act on real simplex differences. Parent symmetry gives exactly \(V(x)-V(y)=2A((x+y)/2)(x-y)\), whereas type-permutation symmetry is unnecessary. At a fixed point, \(x_r\le(1-x_r)^2\), so every coordinate is at most \(c=(3-\sqrt5)/2<1/2\).

The contradiction is complete at boundary points: a difference cannot have a singleton positive or negative support because the corresponding output polynomial omits its own coordinate and has nonnegative coefficients. In four coordinates the remaining sign pattern is precisely two plus and two minus, with no residual zero coordinate. Writing the difference as positive-to-negative transfers and using the two-pair estimate gives \(h\le2ch<h\). The proof is not an extrapolation of a three-type theorem.

###088: support, sign and boundary hypotheses survive the extension

Let \(D_i=I-E_i\) and let active affine retentions have \(c_i=b_i/r_i\). The derivation correctly includes \(c_i'=-c_i^2\); because \(D_i^2=D_i\),
\[
g'=\sum_i c_iD_ig,\qquad
g''=2\sum_{i<j}c_ic_jD_iD_jg.
\]
Consequently the full relative-entropy curvature is a sum of individual squares and pair brackets. The pair bracket on a positive two-bit fiber equals
\[
p(1-p)q(1-q)
\{X(z^{-1}-1+\log z)+Y(z-1-\log z)\},
\]
which is nonnegative. Common slope sign makes every\(c_ic_j\) nonnegative. The proof does not infer parameter convexity merely from noise monotonicity.

Untouched-coordinate fibers are treated explicitly. A fiber absent from the input marginal stays identically zero; all other fibers become positive in active coordinates throughout the open parameter interval. Differentiation never treats\(\log0\) as finite. If curvature vanishes, every active\(D_ig\) vanishes, and invertibility of the active factors implies that the whole output law is constant.

The DPP transformation is
\[
M(t)=\operatorname{diag}((1-r_i)d_i)+R^{1/2}KR^{1/2}.
\]
Its inclusion determinants and contraction property are proved directly over complex Hermitian kernels. Constant law implies constant active marginals and covariances; the common sign makes \(d(r_ir_j)/dt\ne0\) whenever one index is active and the other is not permanently erased. This proves the exact equality criterion. General DPP kernel nonidentifiability is not used to infer matrix constancy.

For boundary diagonal references, convex total correlation passes to the limit first. The polynomial positive-coefficient resampling formula gives fixed interior support and permits differentiation afterward. A changing diagonal provides strict marginal curvature. If no diagonal changes, the deterministic boundary coordinates have zero off-diagonal rows by positivity of\(K\) or\(I-K\), leaving an interior diagonal block. Two-sided feasible lines through boundary diagonals likewise force those rows of the direction to vanish. These arguments supply the strictness that an ordinary limiting argument would not preserve.

The opposite-slope example has uniform product endpoint laws and lower-entropy correlated interior laws. It is a counterexample to extending the retention theorem to arbitrary signs, not a counterexample on an affine kernel line. The current ledger retains the necessary nonlinear-curve qualification.
