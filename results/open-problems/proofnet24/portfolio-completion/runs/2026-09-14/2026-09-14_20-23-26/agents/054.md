### Audited theorem

Let one fixed real law have density bounded by \(K<\infty\), mean zero, and second moment one. At each level \(m\), let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with this law. Suppose

\[
N_m/m\longrightarrow\gamma\in(1,\infty),\qquad
k_m\in\mathbb N,\quad k_m\ge1,\qquad k_m\log m=o(m).
\]

For all sufficiently large \(m\), \(m+k_m-1\le N_m\). Define

\[
M_m=\min_{|T|=m+k_m-1}\sigma_m((A_m)_T),\qquad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]

Then for each fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), some constants \(C,c>0\) satisfy, eventually,

\[
\boxed{\Pr\left\{\left|\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_m+h(\gamma)\right|>
\varepsilon\right\}\le Ce^{-cm}.}
\tag{054-T}
\]

The constants and the dimension after which this holds may depend on the fixed law, \(\gamma\), \(\varepsilon\), and the prescribed sequences. Uniformity over all bounded-density variance-one triangular laws is not asserted. Absolute continuity makes all required ranks full, and \(M_m>0\), almost surely at each level.

## 2. Exact two-tier failure and the corrected three-tier algebra

Suppress \(m\) in \(k=k_m\), and write

\[
s=\frac{e^{-\lambda m/k}}{\sqrt k},\qquad \lambda>0.
\]

If the old recurrence is \(M_j\ge(\theta s)^jL\), \(e_j\le(As)^je_0\), its normalized edge estimate is exactly

\[
\frac{e_j}{sM_j^2}
\le\frac{e_0}{L^2}\left(\frac A{\theta^2}\right)^j s^{-(j+1)}.
\]

At the last stage where an invariant is needed, \(j=k-1\), this gives

\[
\log\frac{e_{k-1}}{sM_{k-1}^2}
\le-(\kappa-\lambda)m+\frac k2\log k+(k-1)\log(A/\theta^2)
\tag{054-2T}
\]

if \(e_0/L^2\le e^{-\kappa m}\). The missing \(+\lambda m\) is present even for \(k=1\). Thus the original displayed inference from merely \(\kappa>0\) is false. This is an algebraic inference failure, not a counterexample to the theorem.

For the repair, classify ordered distinct pairs as ordinary, medium, or bad, with current counts \(E,B\) and \(M\) vertices. Suppose a fresh row has individual survival probabilities in \([a\ell s,Cs]\), with \(0<\ell\le1\), and joint probabilities bounded by

\[
Cs^2,\qquad Cd_ms^2,\qquad Cs
\]

in the respective classes. The family is fixed after conditioning on the complete current history. Assume

\[
sM\ge1,\qquad d_mE/M^2\le1,\qquad B/(sM^2)\le1.
\tag{054-I}
\]

If \(Z\) is the surviving vertex count, expansion into diagonal terms and ordered distinct pairs yields

\[
\mathbb EZ\ge a\ell sM,\qquad
\mathbb EZ^2\le CsM+Cs^2M^2+Cd_ms^2E+CsB
\le C_1s^2M^2.
\]

Paley–Zygmund therefore gives probability at least \(p_0\ell^2\) of \(Z\ge(a/2)\ell sM\), where \(p_0=a^2/(4C_1)\). Expected surviving medium and bad counts are at most \(Cd_ms^2E\) and \(CsB\). Markov thresholds \(A\ell^{-2}d_ms^2E\) and \(A\ell^{-2}sB\), with \(A\ge4C/p_0\), have combined failure probability at most \(p_0\ell^2/2\). Zero initial counts cause no exception. Consequently one simultaneous successful update has conditional probability at least \(p\ell^2\), and obeys

\[
M'\ge c\ell sM,\qquad
E'\le A\ell^{-2}d_ms^2E,\qquad
B'\le A\ell^{-2}sB.
\tag{054-S}
\]

No pair or vertex independence was assumed. Increase \(A\ge1\) and decrease \(c\le1\) if needed. Iteration along successful rows gives the two exact normalized estimates

\[
d_m\frac{E_j}{M_j^2}
\le d_m\frac{E_0}{M_0^2}
\left(\frac A{c^2}\right)^j\ell^{-4j}d_m^j,
\tag{054-M}
\]

\[
\frac{B_j}{sM_j^2}
\le\frac{B_0}{M_0^2}
\left(\frac A{c^2}\right)^j\ell^{-4j}s^{-(j+1)}.
\tag{054-B}
\]

Both the squared vertex loss and the Markov enlargement contribute to \(\ell^{-4j}\). Omitting either contribution would be an error.

Assume \(\log M_0=Hm+o(m)\), \(H>\lambda\), \(E_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_E m}\), and \(B_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_Bm}\), with \(\kappa_E>0\), \(\kappa_B>\lambda\). For \(0\le j\le k-1\), the worst bounds are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(sM_j)&\ge(H-\lambda)m-\frac k2\log k
-(k-1)\log(1/\ell)-O(k)+o(m),\\
\log(d_mE_j/M_j^2)&\le-\kappa_E m+k\log d_m
+4(k-1)\log(1/\ell)+O(k),\\
\log(B_j/(sM_j^2))&\le-(\kappa_B-\lambda)m+\frac k2\log k
+4(k-1)\log(1/\ell)+O(k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{054-L}
\]

For the fixed law, \(\ell=1\) and \(d_m\) is either \(\sqrt m\) or \(m\); the given \(k\log m=o(m)\) makes every nonleading term negligible. The same calculation gives \(\log M_k\ge(H-\lambda)m-o(m)>0\). An invariant at \(j=k\) is unused: imposing it would incorrectly demand an additional \(\lambda m/k\) in the bad-edge budget.

## 3. Fresh-row geometry: conditional bounds checked directly

Take \(n_0=\lfloor\beta m\rfloor\), \(1<\beta<\gamma\), base rows, and all \(L=\binom{n_0}{m-1}\) labeled base subsets. Their unit normals can be selected measurably, for instance using normalized cofactors and a deterministic sign rule outside the null rank-failure set. A pair of subsets \(S,T\) has

\[
D=m-|S\cap T|,\qquad Y=1-(u_S\cdot u_T)^2.
\]

There are \(D-1\) private rows on each side. Condition on the actual shared rows and all private rows for \(S\). The residual subspace \(F\) has dimension \(D\), \(v=u_S\in F\) is fixed, and the \(D-1\) private rows for \(T\) remain independent **original** product-law rows. This is the conditioning used throughout this section.

Input A gives for every unit \(u\)

\[
\Pr\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le s\}\le2\sqrt2Ks.
\]

For a pair \(u,v\), the transformation from orthonormal projection coordinates to the two linear forms has determinant magnitude \(\sqrt{1-(u\cdot v)^2}\). Thus when this is nonzero,

\[
\Pr\{|\langle X,u\rangle|,|\langle X,v\rangle|\le s\}
\le \frac{8K^2s^2}{\sqrt{1-(u\cdot v)^2}}.
\tag{054-2S}
\]

Projected coordinates were not asserted to be independent. Full-dimensional projections in the very smallest ambient dimensions can be handled by their original product density; all applications here are eventually in proper dimensions.

### Ultra-angle estimate

Let \(w=u_T\). On \(Y\le y\), orient \(w\) toward \(v\). Since

\[
\|v-w\|^2=2(1-|v\cdot w|)\le2Y,
\]

each private row must satisfy

\[
|\langle X_i,v\rangle|
\le\sqrt{2y}\,\|P_FX_i\|.
\]

Isotropy gives \(\mathbb E\|P_FX_i\|^2=D\). Splitting according to \(\|P_FX_i\|\le R\sqrt D\), the slab upper bound and Markov give

\[
\Pr\{|\langle X_i,v\rangle|\le\sqrt{2y}\|P_FX_i\|\}
\le C_KR\sqrt{Dy}+R^{-2}.
\]

The choice \(R=(Dy)^{-1/6}\) balances both terms. Independence of these necessary one-row events yields

\[
\boxed{\Pr\{Y\le y\mid F,v,\text{exposed rows}\}
\le[C_K(Dy)^{1/3}]^{D-1}.}
\tag{054-U}
\]

The notation on the left means the conditioning described above. Conditioning only on shared rows and \(v\) follows by averaging. No independence between \(v\) and its own private block was needed.

### A fixed-angle estimate using only density and isotropy

Expose \(T\)'s private rows sequentially. At a current subspace \(F_d\) of dimension \(d\), let \(q=P_{F_d}v/\|P_{F_d}v\|\) whenever the numerator is nonzero. Adding the next row restricts to its orthogonal complement and multiplies squared projected length by \(1-Z_d\), where

\[
Z_d=\frac{(q\cdot X)^2}{\|P_{F_d}X\|^2}.
\]

Consequently

\[
(v\cdot w)^2=\prod_{d=2}^D(1-Z_d).
\tag{054-P}
\]

If a projected length vanishes, a near-parallel outcome is already impossible; the bookkeeping can continue with any measurable unit \(q\in F_d\).

Set \(a=\min\{1,(8\sqrt2K)^{-1}\}\) and \(z_0=a^2/4\). Conditional on the complete past, the next original row satisfies

\[
\Pr\{|q\cdot X|<a\}\le1/4,\qquad
\Pr\{\|P_{F_d}X\|^2>4d\}\le1/4.
\]

Let \(J_d\) indicate the intersection of the complementary events. Its conditional probability is at least \(1/2\), and \(Z_d\ge z_0J_d/d\ge z_0J_d/D\). With \(y_*=1-e^{-z_0/16}\), the event \(Y\le y_*\) implies

\[
\sum_{d=2}^D Z_d\le-\log\prod_{d=2}^D(1-Z_d)\le z_0/16,
\qquad\sum_{d=2}^D J_d\le D/16.
\]

Iterated conditional expectation gives \(\mathbb E2^{-\sum J_d}\le(3/4)^{D-1}\). Markov then yields

\[
\Pr\{Y\le y_*\mid\text{exposed rows}\}
\le2^{D/16}(3/4)^{D-1}\le Ce^{-cD}.
\tag{054-A}
\]

The sign and the direction of the logarithmic inequality are correct. The constants are uniform at a common density ceiling and centered variance-one normalization. The stronger fixed-law uniform-integrability arguments in the older archive are unnecessary for this particular bound.

## 4. Initialization: the overlap cutoff must precede the ultra-angle cutoff

Since \(h'(\beta)=\log(\beta/(\beta-1))>0\), any \(\lambda<h(\gamma)\) permits a choice \(\beta<\gamma\) with \(H=h(\beta)>\lambda\). Stirling gives \(\log L=Hm+o(m)\).

For a fixed \(S\), the exact number of \(T\)'s with residual dimension \(D\) is

\[
\binom{m-1}{D-1}\binom{n_0-m+1}{D-1}.
\tag{054-C}
\]

Its \(D=1\) term is the diagonal. Including that term in an upper bound is harmless. Let

\[
g_\beta(x)=\mathcal H(x)+(\beta-1)\mathcal H(x/(\beta-1)),
\quad\mathcal H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
\]

The derivative is

\[
g_\beta'(x)=\log\frac{(1-x)(\beta-1-x)}{x^2}>0
\quad\text{for }0<x<(\beta-1)/\beta.
\]

Thus, with a fixed small \(\delta\) in this range,

\[
L^{-2}\#\{(S,T):D<\delta m\}
\le\exp[-(H-g_\beta(\delta))m+o(m)].
\tag{054-H}
\]

The \(O(m)\) possible strata cost only \(O(\log m)\) in the logarithm. This is a deterministic combinatorial bound, so no Markov loss is required.

An explicit safe order of choices is as follows. Put \(\eta=(H-\lambda)/8\), choose \(\delta\) so small that \(g_\beta(\delta)<\eta\), and set

\[
\kappa_B=\lambda+2\eta,\qquad
\kappa_* =\lambda+3\eta,\qquad
\kappa_H=\lambda+5\eta.
\]

The high-overlap count has exponent strictly greater than \(\lambda+7\eta>\kappa_H\). For low-overlap pairs, use an ultra cutoff \(b/m\). By (054-U),

\[
\Pr\{Y<b/m\}\le[C_Kb^{1/3}]^{\delta m-1}
\]

once the bracket is below one. Only now choose the fixed \(b>0\) small enough to make this probability at most \(C e^{-A_0m}\), where \(A_0>\kappa_H\). Averaging over at most \(L^2\) pairs and applying Markov at the target \(e^{-\kappa_*m}L^2\) has failure at most \(C e^{-(A_0-\kappa_*)m}\). Adding the deterministic high-overlap pairs gives \(B_0\le2e^{-\kappa_*m}L^2\) eventually.

For medium pairs use

\[
D\ge\delta m,\qquad b/m\le Y<y_*.
\]

Equation (054-A) bounds their expected fraction by \(Ce^{-c\delta m}\), so Markov gives an exponentially small fraction with exponential failure. Their rowwise joint survival probability is bounded by \(C_b\sqrt m\,s^2\) via (054-2S). Ordinary pairs have bound \(Cs^2\); all bad pairs have bound \(Cs\).

If the useful subfamily has \(M_0\ge L/2\), deleting other vertices multiplies normalized edge bounds by at most four. Therefore

\[
B_0/M_0^2\le8e^{-\kappa_*m}\le e^{-\kappa_Bm},\qquad
E_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_Em}
\]

for some \(\kappa_E>0\), eventually. The strict margins absorb constants. Pair dependence never enters the expectation/Markov calculation.

The alternative cutoff \(m^{-2}\) is also valid: its medium cost is \(d_m=m\), and its low-overlap ultra probability is bounded by

\[
[C_Km^{-1/3}]^{\delta m-1}
=\exp[-(\delta/3+o(1))m\log m].
\]

This avoids tuning \(b\) but does not remove the need to choose the high-overlap entropy rate above \(\lambda\). Both versions fit \(k\log m=o(m)\).

## 5. Fixed-law one-normal density floor

Use the primary paper's compressibility convention: distance from the collection of sparse vectors in all of \(\mathbb R^m\), not just sparse unit vectors. For \(u\notin\mathrm{Comp}(\delta_0,\rho_0)\), discard coordinates exceeding \(1/\sqrt{\delta_0m}\). Their number is at most \(\delta_0m\), and the remaining squared mass exceeds \(\rho_0^2\). Coordinates smaller than \(\rho_0/\sqrt{2m}\) account for at most \(\rho_0^2/2\). Consequently some deterministic set \(I=I(u)\) satisfies

\[
q=|I|\ge c_0m,\qquad
a_0/\sqrt m\le|u_i|\le b_0/\sqrt m\ (i\in I),\qquad
v_0\le V:=\sum_{i\in I}u_i^2\le1.
\]

The constants depend only on the fixed compressibility parameters. This argument uses the correct sparse-vector convention, so its tail-mass inequality does not need a factor-of-two repair.

Set \(b_i=u_i/\sqrt V\). Then \(\sum b_i^2=1\), and fixed \(A,B>0\) give

\[
A/\sqrt q\le|b_i|\le B/\sqrt q.
\]

For the one fixed law, let \(\varphi(t)=\mathbb Ee^{it\xi}\). Finite second moment and centering give \(\log\varphi(z)=-z^2/2+o(z^2)\) near zero, using the branch continuous at zero. In particular there are fixed \(r,c>0\) with \(|\varphi(z)|\le e^{-cz^2}\) for \(|z|\le r\). The logarithmic error summed over the coefficients is bounded on \(|t|\le T\) by

\[
T^2\sup_{0<|z|\le BT/\sqrt q}
\frac{|\log\varphi(z)+z^2/2|}{z^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

This proves compact-uniform convergence of \(\psi_q(t)=\prod_i\varphi(b_it)\) to \(e^{-t^2/2}\), uniformly over the allowed signed coefficient arrays. No symmetry or third moment is required.

Because \(f\in L^1\cap L^\infty\), Plancherel gives \(\int|\varphi|^2\le2\pi K\). Absolute continuity gives \(|\varphi(z)|<1\) for \(z\ne0\); equality would force lattice-coset support. Riemann–Lebesgue and continuity then imply

\[
\zeta:=\sup_{|z|\ge rA/B}|\varphi(z)|<1.
\]

For \(|t|\le r\sqrt q/B\), the product is at most \(e^{-ct^2}\). Outside that range all coefficient arguments have magnitude at least \(rA/B\), so, reserving two factors for Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{|t|>r\sqrt q/B}|\psi_q(t)|dt
&\le\zeta^{q-2}\int|\varphi(b_1t)\varphi(b_2t)|dt\\
&\le\frac{2\pi K}{\sqrt{|b_1b_2|}}\zeta^{q-2}
\le C\sqrt q\,\zeta^{q-2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Compact convergence, the Gaussian inner domination, and this tail bound imply \(\|\psi_q-e^{-t^2/2}\|_1\to0\), uniformly over the coefficient arrays. Fourier inversion therefore gives uniform convergence of the density of \(\sum_i b_i\xi_i\) to the standard normal density.

The scaling by \(\sqrt V\), with \(V\ge v_0>0\), causes no growing target. For example, density control on the fixed interval \([-3/\sqrt{v_0},3/\sqrt{v_0}]\) gives an eventual positive floor \(a_\xi>0\) for the density of \(S_I=\sum_{i\in I}u_i\xi_i\) throughout \([-3,3]\). Its independent remainder \(R_I\) is centered with variance at most one. Hence \(\Pr\{|R_I|\le2\}\ge3/4\), and for \(0<s\le1\),

\[
\Pr\{|S_I+R_I|\le s\}
\ge(3/4)(2a_\xi s)=c_\xi s.
\tag{054-F}
\]

Together with the slab upper bound, this is exactly the uniform useful-normal floor needed in (054-S), with \(\ell=1\). Its validity at exponentially small \(s\) follows from an actual density floor; it is not an inference from an additive CDF error. The law-dependent onset dimension is deterministic. No rate of convergence in the local limit calculation is needed to obtain the eventual exponential probability estimate.

## 6. Livshyts hypothesis matching

For a fixed base subset, transpose its \(m-1\) rows to form \(m-1\) columns and append one independent product-law column. The resulting square \(m\times m\) matrix is iid. Its selected-column normal is exactly the original base normal; the appended column does not change that normal.

In Section 6 of the primary paper take ambient dimension \(N=m\), column dimension \(n=m\), and its codimension parameter equal to **one**, not to the surplus \(k_m\). The aspect condition is automatic, and \(1\le\widetilde c m\) holds eventually. The entry concentration radius \(a=1/(4K)\) gives interval probability at most \(1/2<3/4\). Take its moment parameter \(p=1\) and moment constant one. Each square-matrix row has expected squared norm \(m\); for every required \(m-1\)-element index set, (48) reads

\[
\sum_{i\in\sigma}\mathbb E\|A^Te_i\|^2
=m(m-1)\le m(m-1).
\]

All independence and identical-row conditions hold. Lemma 6.4, as explicitly stated, thus gives a fixed \((\delta_0,\rho_0)\) and exponential incompressibility failure. Its convention for compressibility is exactly the one used in Section 5 above. No fourth moment, uniform-integrability rate, or operator-norm event has been substituted for its hypotheses.

There is nevertheless a source-level proof issue worth retaining in an audit: the primary proof of Lemma 6.4 invokes Lemma 5.3 for its matrix \(B\) of size \((N-1)\times N\), whereas Lemma 5.3 is stated with number of rows at least number of columns. That cross-reference needs the natural near-square wide extension. This does not invalidate the published statement used by 037, and the following direct derivation removes the dependence on that cross-reference.

## 7. Direct rectangular incompressibility proof avoiding that cross-reference

Let \(B\) be the original \(r\times m\) matrix, \(r=m-1\), with iid bounded-density coordinates and \(\mathbb E\xi^2=1\). The proof in this section needs neither centering nor symmetry. It uses Input A and Livshyts' general net theorem, Theorem 3, already identified as Input B′.

First, for every deterministic \(y\) with \(\|y\|\ge1/2\), the independent coordinates of \(By\) each have density at most \(2\sqrt2K\). A ball-volume bound gives

\[
\Pr\{\|By\|\le a_*\sqrt r\}
\le(2\sqrt2K)^rv_r(a_*\sqrt r)^r
\le(4K\sqrt{\pi e}\,a_*)^r.
\]

Choose \(a_*=e^{-4}/(4K\sqrt{\pi e})\). The probability is at most \(e^{-4r}\le e^{-2m}\) for \(m\ge2\).

Now select a fixed net parameter \(\Gamma>1\) so large that

\[
\frac{C_2\log(C_1\Gamma)}{\Gamma^{0.08}}<1/4,
\]

where \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) are the absolute constants of the general net theorem. Set

\[
\kappa=2^{1/\Gamma^{0.09}},\qquad
s_*=\frac{2}{\kappa^2-1},\qquad p=1.
\]

For sufficiently large \(m\), the theorem permits \(\Gamma<\sqrt m\). Choose a fixed mesh \(\tau>0\) such that

\[
\tau<\frac1{20\Gamma},\qquad
C_3\tau\Gamma\sqrt{s_*}\le a_*.
\]

Let \(\rho=\tau/2\). For \(S=\mathrm{Comp}(\delta,\rho)\), an ordinary Euclidean cover has, with \(d=\lfloor\delta m\rfloor\),

\[
N(S,\tau B_2^m)\le\binom md(1+4/\tau)^d.
\]

Indeed, every such vector is within \(\rho\) of its coordinate projection onto some support of size at most \(d\); this projection has norm at most one. Pad supports to size \(d\), and cover each coordinate unit ball at radius \(\tau/2\). Since \(\mathcal H(\delta)+\delta\log(1+4/\tau)\to0\), take a fixed \(\delta>0\) small enough that this covering number is at most \(e^{m/4}\) eventually.

The theorem now provides a deterministic net of size at most \(e^{m/2}\), contained in \(S+4\tau\Gamma B_2^m\), and therefore with every net vector of norm at least \(1-4\tau\Gamma>1/2\). Its exceptional probability is

\[
\kappa^{-2m}(1+1/s_*)^m
=\left(\frac{\kappa^2+1}{2\kappa^2}\right)^m=:q_*^m,
\qquad q_*<1.
\]

Since \(B\) has independent columns and

\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E\|Be_i\|^2=mr,
\]

its comparison error is at most

\[
\frac{C_3\tau\Gamma\sqrt{s_*}}{\sqrt m}\sqrt{mr}
\le a_*\sqrt r.
\]

If \(Bx=0\) for some \(x\in S\) on the comparison event, a net vector \(y\) satisfies \(\|By\|\le a_*\sqrt r\). The pointwise estimate and union bound therefore prove

\[
\boxed{\Pr\{\ker B\cap\mathrm{Comp}(\delta,\rho)\ne\varnothing\}
\le q_*^m+e^{m/2}e^{-2m}\le Ce^{-cm}.}
\tag{054-N}
\]

## 9. Rectangular lower extreme and completion

For any fixed row subset of size \(n=m+k-1\), write its columns as \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\). If its least singular value is at most \(t\), some unit minimizing vector \(x\) has \(|x_i|\ge1/\sqrt m\). Projecting the identity \(A_Tx=\sum_jx_jC_j\) orthogonally to the other columns' span gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}(C_i,\operatorname{span}(C_j:j\ne i))
\le\frac{\|A_Tx\|}{|x_i|}\le\sqrt m\,t.
\]

Conditional on the other columns, their span has dimension \(m-1\) almost surely. Its orthogonal complement has dimension \(n-(m-1)=k\). The column \(C_i\) remains independent and has product density with coordinate ceiling \(K\). Input A and ball volume therefore give

\[
\Pr\{\sigma_m(A_T)\le t\}
\le m(\sqrt2K)^kv_k(\sqrt m\,t)^k
\le\exp[O_K(k\log m)]t^k.
\tag{054-D}
\]

For \(k=1\) this is the familiar distance-to-hyperplane estimate. No additive \(e^{-cm}\) error appears in (054-D), which matters before taking an exponential union bound. The finite-dimensional full-projection case is covered by the original product density.

The entropy calculation is valid for variable \(k\): since \(k=o(m)\),

\[
\log\binom{N_m}{m+k-1}=h(\gamma)m+o(m).
\]

At \(t=e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k}\), union bounding (054-D) over row subsets yields

\[
\Pr\{M_m\le e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k}\}
\le\exp[-\varepsilon m+o(m)]\le e^{-\varepsilon m/2}
\tag{054-LOW}
\]

eventually. The \(O_K(k\log m)\) term is negligible precisely in the stipulated regime. A slowly converging sequence \(N_m/m\to\gamma\) changes only the eventual onset, not the conclusion.

Equations (054-UP) and (054-LOW) establish (054-T) for small positive tolerances. For a larger tolerance use any smaller positive tolerance below \(h(\gamma)\). At every positive rational tolerance the failure probabilities are summable. Borel–Cantelli and a countable intersection therefore give
