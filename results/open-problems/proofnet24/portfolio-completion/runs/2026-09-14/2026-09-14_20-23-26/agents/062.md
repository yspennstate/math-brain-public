## 3. Target and the independent finite-law lemma

The result to establish is the existence of one distribution on the nonnegative integers with

\[
\pi_1=q=\frac14,\qquad \pi_2=\frac18,\qquad \pi_0>\frac12,
\qquad E_\pi X=1,\qquad E_\pi X^\alpha=\infty\quad(\alpha>1),
\tag{062.1}
\]

and a computable deterministic sequence \(n_r\to\infty\) for which

\[
\mathcal L\left(\frac{N_1(T_{n_r})-n_rq}{\sqrt{n_r}}\right)
\Longrightarrow
\tfrac12\mathcal N(-1,3/16)+\tfrac12\mathcal N(1,3/16).
\tag{062.2}
\]

Here \(N_1\) counts outdegree-one vertices and \(T_n\) is the Galton–Watson tree conditioned on total vertex count \(n\).

### 3.1 Covariance and nondegeneracy

Fix one finitely supported distribution \(Q\) with positive masses at 0, 1 and 2. All limits in this subsection keep this entire law fixed. Define

\[
Y=\mathbf1_{\{X=1\}},\quad q=E_QY,\quad\mu=E_QX,\quad
V=\operatorname{Var}_QX,\quad b=q(1-q),\quad
\gamma=\operatorname{Cov}_Q(X,Y)=q(1-\mu).
\]

The last identity follows from \(XY=Y\). Thus the relevant covariance is not \(q(1-q)\). Both \(V>0\) and

\[
v=b-\gamma^2/V>0.
\tag{062.3}
\]

Indeed, equality would make \(Y-q=c(X-\mu)\) almost surely. Its equal values at 0 and 2 force \(c=0\); its different value at 1 then gives a contradiction.

### 3.2 Fourier proof, including domination and the lattice issue

For independent copies let \(S_N=\sum X_i\), \(B_N=\sum Y_i\). Suppose the integer targets obey
\((k_N-N\mu)/\sqrt N\to a\in\mathbb R\). For a fixed real \(s\), lattice inversion in the sum coordinate gives

\[
E_Q\left[e^{is(B_N-Nq)/\sqrt N};S_N=k_N\right]
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-it(k_N-N\mu)}
\left(E_Qe^{it(X-\mu)+is(Y-q)/\sqrt N}\right)^Ndt.
\tag{062.4}
\]

After \(t=u/\sqrt N\), its integrand converges, for every fixed \(u,s\), to

\[
e^{-iau}\exp\{-\tfrac12(Vu^2+2\gamma us+bs^2)\}.
\tag{062.5}
\]

This follows directly by Taylor expansion of the characteristic function of a fixed bounded vector, followed by \(N\log(1+z_N)\). The linear terms vanish because both coordinates are centered.

To justify integration, set \(\phi_N(t,s)=E_Qe^{itX+isY/\sqrt N}\). For independent copies of \((X,Y)\), the identity for \(1-|\phi_N|^2\), keeping only the two ordered pairs of atoms 0 and 1, gives

\[
1-|\phi_N(t,s)|^2\ge2Q(0)q\bigl(1-\cos(t+s/\sqrt N)\bigr).
\tag{062.6}
\]

Fix \(0<\delta<\pi/4\). For large \(N\) and \(|t|\le\delta\), the elementary quadratic lower bound on \(1-\cos x\) and \(1-z\le e^{-z}\) imply

\[
|\phi_N(t,s)|^N\le e^{-cN(t+s/\sqrt N)^2}.
\]

After rescaling, \(e^{-c(u+s)^2}\) dominates the integrand on the expanding central interval and is integrable on the whole real line. On \(\delta\le|t|\le\pi\), the same cosine term is bounded below by a positive constant for large \(N\), so even \(\sqrt N\) times the remaining integral tends to zero exponentially. Centering factors have modulus one and do not alter these bounds. Dominated convergence now applies to (062.4), multiplied by \(\sqrt N\).

At \(s=0\), the Gaussian integral gives

\[
\sqrt N\,P_Q(S_N=k_N)\longrightarrow
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi V}}e^{-a^2/(2V)}>0.
\tag{062.7}
\]

For general \(s\), completing the square in \(u\) and dividing by the \(s=0\) integral gives

\[
\exp\left\{i\frac{\gamma a}{V}s-\tfrac12\left(b-\frac{\gamma^2}{V}\right)s^2\right\}.
\tag{062.8}
\]

Thus the conditional count law converges to \(\mathcal N(\gamma a/V,v)\). Its CDF converges uniformly, since weak convergence to a continuous CDF implies uniform CDF convergence: choose finitely many endpoints with small limiting tail probabilities and small intervening increments, then use monotonicity.

No two-dimensional aperiodicity assertion is used. The pair \((X,Y)\) can have a proper sublattice, for example a parity restriction when the support is just \(\{0,1,2\}\). Inversion is only in \(S_N\); its span is one because 0 and 1 both occur. Equation (062.6) explicitly rules out extra Fourier peaks in that coordinate. A possible parity restriction on \(B_N\) has scaled spacing tending to zero and does not invalidate (062.8).

**Dependency conclusion:** the finite-law LLT and conditional count CLT in principal Section 2 are correct, with the stated signs, variance and Kolmogorov conclusion.

## 5. Deterministic computability, including the evaluator convention

### 5.1 Exact finite probability calculation

Every finite-stage probability is rational. With

\[
F(z,u)=Q(0)+qzu+\sum_{j\ge2}Q(j)z^j,
\quad P_{k,b}=[z^ku^b]F(z,u)^N,
\quad P_k=\sum_{b=0}^N P_{k,b},
\]

all coefficients and conditional cumulative probabilities
\(C_b=(\sum_{j=0}^bP_{k,j})/P_k\), \(C_{-1}=0\), are computed by finite rational arithmetic. No claim of a feasible number of operations is being made.

For \(x_b=(b-Nq)/\sqrt N\) and \(G(x)=\Phi((x-m)/\sqrt v)\), \(m=\mp1\), the exact Kolmogorov distance is

\[
K=\max_{0\le b\le N}\max\{|C_b-G(x_b)|,|C_{b-1}-G(x_b)|\}.
\tag{062.15}
\]

Between consecutive potential jumps the discrete CDF is constant and \(G\) is increasing. Its absolute difference is therefore maximized at one of the two limiting endpoints. The first and last endpoints cover the unbounded tails, whose differences tend to zero. Zero-probability potential jumps do not change the formula. Thus G3 is a finite, uniformly computable real comparison, not an unperformed supremum over a continuum.

### 5.2 Exact G1, semidecision for strict gates, and termination

The floor arguments are algebraic. In fact \(N=2^\ell-1\equiv3\pmod4\) for every eligible \(\ell\ge8\), so \(\sqrt N\) is irrational, as is its nonzero rational multiple \(D\). Rational interval refinement can therefore determine both floors without an equality stall. The only nonrational G1 inequality can also be decided exactly: write \(B=\varepsilon n\Delta-\Delta-1\); if \(B<0\) it fails, and otherwise \(D\le B\) is equivalent to the rational comparison
\((V/(q\Delta))^2N\le B^2\). The other G1 tests are rational or integer comparisons.

For a G1-passing candidate the six strict margins are

\[
g_{2,\pm}=\varepsilon-\left|\sqrt NP_{k^\pm}/c-1\right|,
\quad g_{3,\pm}=\varepsilon-K_\pm,
\quad g_4=\varepsilon c n^{-3}/\sqrt N-e^{-E_r(n)},
\quad g_5=\varepsilon c n^{-3}/\sqrt N-2n^{-6}.
\tag{062.16}
\]

Each is uniformly computable from finite rational stage data. The normal CDF, exponential and positive square root have effective rational enclosures; finite arithmetic, absolute values and maxima preserve this property. Division by \(c\) is effective because \(c>0\), so refinement eventually produces a strictly positive lower bound for the denominator.

Let \(\ell_0\) be the least exponent with \(2^{\ell_0}\ge\max(2^{8r},2n_{r-1})\). Enumerate \((\ell,j)\), \(\ell\ge\ell_0,j\ge1\), by increasing \(\ell-\ell_0+j\), breaking ties by increasing \(\ell\). At each visit, check G1 exactly and, if it passes, produce rational intervals enclosing all six margins with width at most \(2^{-j}\). Accept precisely when all six lower endpoints are positive.

Every visit terminates. Some exponent has all six margins strictly positive by Section 4. At that exponent, if \(2^{-j}\) is smaller than the least margin, every enclosing interval of that width has positive lower endpoint. The enumeration reaches such a pair after only finitely many visits. Hence the stage terminates. A zero margin is never accidentally accepted and does not block later visits. A negative margin is likewise never accepted. This proves the totality of the recursion without deciding equality of arbitrary computable real numbers.

The literal minimum among all passing powers is a valid separate existential definition, but this argument does not prove that minimum computable. The integrated note correctly distinguishes those laws.

### 5.3 A concrete globally fixed interval routine

For absolute determinism, the following convention suffices and can be inserted before the selector. It is an audit clarification, not an executed program or a new stage condition.

Fix the arithmetic expression order appearing in (062.15)–(062.16), and use ordinary rational interval arithmetic in that order. At internal accuracy \(2^{-m}\), use the following deterministic approximations, always choosing the first admissible integer cutoff or bisection depth:

1. Enclose the constant \(\pi\) by the alternating Gregory series \(4\sum_{j\ge0}(-1)^j/(2j+1)\), taking enough terms that its first omitted term is less than the requested error. Slow convergence is irrelevant to termination.
2. For square roots of positive rational endpoints, bisect the rational bracket \([0,\lceil x\rceil+1]\), selecting halves by exact squared comparisons until the desired width is reached. Use monotonic endpoint evaluation for interval arguments.
3. For \(e^x\) at a rational \(x\ge0\), use its Taylor partial sum through degree \(K\), with \(K+1>2x\), and upper remainder bound \(2x^{K+1}/(K+1)!\). Increase \(K\) until that bound is below the requested error. For \(x<0\), use the reciprocal of the positive interval for \(e^{-x}\). Evaluate interval arguments monotonically at endpoints.
4. For rational \(t\ge0\), approximate the integral in \(\Phi(t)=1/2+(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_0^t e^{-u^2/2}du\) by
\[
\sum_{j=0}^K\frac{(-1)^j t^{2j+1}}{2^j j!(2j+1)}.
\]
Taylor's integral remainder has absolute value at most
\(t^{2K+3}/[2^{K+1}(K+1)!(2K+3)]\), which tends to zero at fixed \(t\). Enclose the integral using this bound and the factor using the positive \(\pi\) and square-root intervals. For negative \(t\), use \(\Phi(t)=1-\Phi(-t)\). For interval arguments, use monotonic endpoints.

For the complete margin evaluation, increase \(m=1,2,\ldots\) until all required denominator intervals are positive and every final margin interval has width at most \(2^{-j}\). All functions are continuous on the relevant domains and all denominators are strictly positive; therefore this refinement terminates. Rational probability coefficients are exact throughout. This convention, the expression order, and the pair order define one literal deterministic selector. No transcendence theorem or equality test for Gaussian CDF expressions is needed.

Why fixing a routine is a real specification choice: even for a computable margin equal to \(1/4\), the rational width-\(1/2\) intervals \([-1/4,1/4]\) and \([1/8,5/8]\) are both valid, but only one certifies positivity. This is an exact illustration of possible certificate-timing dependence, not a claim that a particular critical-stage margin equals \(1/4\), or that two actual critical stages have been computed. Any globally fixed routine still gives a single law and the same theorem.

### 5.4 Computability of the infinite distribution

The next section proves that all positive stage masses are rational, support points strictly exceed every previous maximum, and \(h_r<3n_r^{-4}\). Consequently, to determine the mass at an integer \(k>0\), generate the finite stages until \(H_R\ge k\); later support points cannot equal \(k\). The known masses at 1 and 2 can of course be handled immediately.

The final zero mass has the effective tail bound

\[
\sum_{s>R}h_s<\frac{3\,2^{-32(R+1)}}{1-2^{-32}}.
\tag{062.17}
\]

Thus \(\pi_0\) is a computable real. More strongly, the final law differs from the rational finite law \(Q^{(R)}\) in total variation by exactly the omitted tail mass: that mass is moved from zero to new positive atoms. Equation (062.17) therefore supplies an effective approximation of the whole probability measure by finite rational measures.

## 6. Probability, support and exact criticality

At a selected stage write \(n=n_r\), set \(p=n^{-4}\), \(H=n^4\), and define

\[
a=\frac{\Delta/2-p(L^-+L^+)}{H},\qquad h=2p+a.
\tag{062.18}
\]

Add masses \(p,p,a\) at \(L^-,L^+,H\), respectively, and remove \(h\) from zero. From (062.14) and G1,

\[
M<L^-<L^+<n<H,
\quad (1-\varepsilon)n\Delta\le L^-\le L^+\le(1+\varepsilon)n\Delta.
\tag{062.19}
\]

All three support points are fresh integers, none is 0, 1 or 2, and no previously fixed positive probability changes. The next stage's true maximum is \(M_{r+1}=H_r=n_r^4\). Applying (062.19) at that next stage gives \(n_{r+1}>n_r^4\), in addition to the explicit doubling condition.

The pair's mean contribution obeys

\[
0<p(L^-+L^+)<2n^{-3}\le\Delta/4,
\tag{062.20}
\]

where \(n\ge2^{8r}\) makes the last inequality valid for every \(r\ge1\). Hence

\[
\frac{\Delta}{4H}<a<\frac{\Delta}{2H},\qquad 0<h<3n^{-4}.
\tag{062.21}
\]

The update preserves total probability and increases the first moment by exactly

\[
p(L^-+L^+)+aH=\Delta/2.
\tag{062.22}
\]

Floors do not impair exact criticality: the adjustment variable is a rational probability at the already fixed integer \(H\), not an integer location forced to solve a real equation. Starting from mean \(1/2\), (062.22) establishes the next mean \(1-2^{-(r+1)}\), proving the induction used to define all stages.

By doubling,

\[
\sum_{s\ge r}h_s<3n_r^{-4}\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-4j}<4n_r^{-4}.
\tag{062.23}
\]

At \(r=1\), this is less than \(4\cdot256^{-4}<1/8\). Every partial zero mass and its limit therefore exceed \(1/2\). The final law is

\[
\pi_0=5/8-\sum_{r\ge1}h_r,\quad
\pi_1=1/4,\quad \pi_2=1/8,\quad
\pi_{L_r^-}=\pi_{L_r^+}=p_r,\quad \pi_{H_r}=a_r,
\tag{062.24}
\]

with zero mass at other integers. Its total mass is exactly one. Nonnegative summation of the first moments gives

\[
E_\pi X=\frac12+\sum_{r\ge1}2^{-r-1}=1.
\tag{062.25}
\]

This is exact criticality, not merely convergence of an unspecified sequence of approximate means. The future values of \(n_r\) do not depend on a subsequently requested conditioning size. Once the recursion is fixed, (062.24) is one law for every \(n\).

## 7. Moments, generating-function parameters and support span

For any fixed real \(\alpha>1\), the adjustment atoms alone satisfy

\[
a_rH_r^\alpha>\frac{2^{-r}}4\,n_r^{4(\alpha-1)}.
\tag{062.26}
\]

Because \(n_{r+1}>n_r^4\) and \(n_1\ge256\),
\(\log n_r\ge4^{r-1}\log256\). The logarithm of the right side of (062.26) tends to infinity. Its individual terms therefore do not tend to zero and in fact diverge; the entire nonnegative \(\alpha\)-moment sum is infinite. The first moment remains finite by the exact summable increments (062.22). For \(0<\alpha\le1\), \(x^\alpha\le1+x\) proves finiteness.

The partial second moments increase to infinity, while partial means remain bounded by one. Thus \(V_r\to\infty\), and

\[
v_r=3/16-q^2\Delta_r^2/V_r\longrightarrow3/16.
\tag{062.27}
\]

A positive exponential moment would imply a finite second moment, so none exists. The probability generating function has radius exactly one: it converges at one, and convergence at any positive real argument larger than one would be a positive exponential moment. For simply generated weights \(w=\pi\), \(\Phi(1)=1\), \(\Phi'(1)=1\) and \(\Psi(1)=1\). For \(0<t<1\), strict monotonicity of the tilted mean follows, for example, from \(t\Psi'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{(t)}}X>0\); all tilted moments there are finite. Hence the canonical critical parameter is \(\tau=1\), the radius is \(\rho=1\), and Janson's \(\nu=\Psi(\rho)=1\). Positivity at 0 and 1 makes the lattice span one.

## 8. Future zero-mass changes: an eventwise relative bound

Fix a stage, set \(A=\operatorname{supp}Q^{(r-1)}\), \(z=Q^{(r-1)}(0)>1/2\), \(n=n_r\), and

\[
\tau_r=\sum_{s\ge r}h_s<4n^{-4}.
\]

For every \(x\in A^k\), \(k\le n\), the exact product likelihood ratio is

\[
R(x)=\frac{\prod_i\pi_{x_i}}{\prod_iQ(x_i)}
=\left(1-\frac{\tau_r}{z}\right)^{\#\{i:x_i=0\}}.
\tag{062.28}
\]

This includes the current stage's pair and adjustment removal as well as every later stage's removal. With

\[
\eta_r=(1-\tau_r/z)^n\ge1-n\tau_r/z\ge1-8n^{-3},
\tag{062.29}
\]

we have \(\eta_r\le R(x)\le1\) for every configuration. Summing over any event \(E\subseteq A^k\) gives

\[
\eta_rP_Q(E)\le P_\pi(E)\le P_Q(E),
\tag{062.30}
\]

where the final-law probability includes the restriction to background coordinates. No normalization of \(\pi|_A\) is silently performed.

If \(P_Q(E)>0\), the conditional density with respect to \(Q(\cdot\mid E)\) is
\(R/E_Q[R\mid E]\). Its numerator and denominator both lie in \([\eta_r,1]\), so the density lies in \([\eta_r,\eta_r^{-1}]\). In particular the conditional total variation distance is at most

\[
\eta_r^{-1}-1=O(n^{-3}),
\tag{062.31}
\]

uniformly over these events. This is the needed statement on very small probabilities. An additive product-measure estimate alone would not survive division by the conditioning probability, but the proof never uses such a division of additive errors. This relative comparison also shows exactly why not knowing the future zero mass while performing the finite-stage tests is harmless.

## 9. Every conditioning branch and the exact denominator comparison

\(\mathcal C=\{\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i=n-1\}\). The current adjustment point \(H_r=n^4\) and every future support point exceed the total \(n-1\). Nonnegative summands therefore exclude all these points from \(\mathcal C\) exactly. Every earlier adjustment atom lies in \(A\) and was retained in \(M,V\) and the coefficient calculation.

Let \(J\) count coordinates in the current pair \(\{L^-,L^+\}\). On \(\mathcal C\), the cases \(J=0\), \(J=1\) with either sign, and \(J\ge2\) exhaust all possibilities. There is no assumption that multiple current atoms are impossible or that the number of possible such atoms is bounded uniformly in \(r\).

Set

\[
w=np\,c/\sqrt N=c n^{-3}/\sqrt N>0.
\tag{062.32}
\]

### 9.1 One current atom, both signs and their weights

For the joint probability \(\beta_\pm=P_\pi(\mathcal C,\text{exactly one current atom of sign }\pm)\), the position has \(n\) choices and the remaining coordinates are in \(A\). Consequently (062.30) gives

\[
\eta_r npP_Q(S_N=k^\pm)\le\beta_\pm\le npP_Q(S_N=k^\pm).
\]

G2 yields

\[
\eta_r(1-\varepsilon)w\le\beta_\pm\le(1+\varepsilon)w.
\tag{062.33}
\]

Equal masses alone would not establish equal conditional branch weights. Here the equality is proved asymptotically by the same local density \(c\) at the two opposite targets, enforced with relative error by G2. Floors and skewness are covered by that test.

### 9.2 No current atom

The event \((\mathcal C,J=0)\) contains only background coordinates, so
\(P_\pi(\mathcal C,J=0)\le P_Q(S_n=n-1)\). Let \(Z=X-(1-\Delta)\) and \(D_0=n\Delta-1>0\). Then \(EZ=0\), \(EZ^2=V\), \(|Z|\le M\). For \(0<t<3/M\),

\[
E e^{tZ}\le1+V\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{t^kM^{k-2}}{k!}
\le1+\frac{Vt^2}{2(1-tM/3)},
\]

using \(E Z^k\le E|Z|^k\le M^{k-2}V\) and \(k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}\). Hence the logarithm has the corresponding upper bound. Exponential Markov and the admissible choice

\[
t=\frac{D_0}{nV+MD_0/3}<\frac3M
\]

give

\[
P_Q(S_n-n(1-\Delta)\ge D_0)
\le\exp\left\{-\frac{D_0^2}{2(nV+MD_0/3)}\right\}
<\varepsilon w
\tag{062.34}
\]

by G4. This value of \(t\) need not be the exact optimizer; its substitution gives the displayed valid bound. Calling this step a choice rather than an optimization is more precise. The \(M\) here is the true old maximum, which may be \(n_{r-1}^4\), not \(n_{r-1}\).

### 9.3 All multiple-current-atom branches at once

Before conditioning, \(J\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,2p)\) exactly, because the final pair masses never change. Therefore

\[
P_\pi(\mathcal C,J\ge2)\le P(J\ge2)
\le E\binom J2=\binom n2(2p)^2
\le2n^{-6}<\varepsilon w
\tag{062.35}
\]

by G5. This includes every count \(J\ge2\), both signs, every placement, and every admissible background. No independence between the sum and the category counts is asserted. In particular, conditioning could favor multiple current atoms, but their entire unconditional probability is already negligible relative to a proved lower bound for the conditional denominator.

### 9.4 Exhaustive denominator squeeze

Equations (062.33)–(062.35) imply

\[
2\eta_r(1-\varepsilon)w
\le P_\pi(\mathcal C)
\le2(1+\varepsilon)w+2\varepsilon w.
\tag{062.36}
\]

Since \(\eta_r\to1\) and \(\varepsilon_r\to0\),

\[
P_\pi(\mathcal C)\sim2w,\qquad
P_\pi(J=1\mid\mathcal C)\to1,\qquad
P_\pi(\text{single sign }\pm\mid\mathcal C)\to\tfrac12.
\tag{062.37}
\]

The total conditional bad probability is bounded explicitly by
\(\varepsilon/[\eta_r(1-\varepsilon)]\to0\). The stage-dependent \(c_r\) may be much smaller than any convenient expression in \(r\); all three comparisons remain valid because G2, G4 and G5 compare to the same changing \(w_r\) at each selected stage.

## 10. Conditional count limit, centering and mixture

The current pair points exceed \(M\ge2\), so neither is one. On the one-current-atom branch, the total one count is exactly \(B_N\) from the background. Given the current position, its conditional distribution is the final background product measure conditioned on the appropriate sum. Its count law is the same for every position, so mixing over positions does not change it.

G3 and (062.31) show that its Kolmogorov distance, after centering at \(Nq\) and scaling by \(\sqrt N\), from \(\mathcal N(\mp1,v_r)\) is bounded by
\(\varepsilon_r+\eta_r^{-1}-1\to0\). Combining with (062.27) gives the two branch limits \(\mathcal N(\mp1,3/16)\).

The requested centering at \(nq\) is handled by the exact identity

\[
\frac{B_N-nq}{\sqrt n}
=\sqrt{\frac{N}{n}}\frac{B_N-Nq}{\sqrt N}-\frac q{\sqrt n}.
\tag{062.38}
\]

Its multiplicative and additive corrections tend to one and zero, respectively. The count's mass \(q\) is exactly the same in every finite law and the final law; there is no renormalized background \(q\) requiring an unaccounted \(\sqrt n\)-order correction.

Against a bounded continuous test function, the two good branches contribute their conditional limits with weights tending to one half, and the bad branches contribute at most the function's sup norm times their probability tending to zero. This proves (062.2) for the independent sample conditioned on its sum.

The resulting characteristic function is

\[
e^{-3t^2/32}\cos t.
\tag{062.39}
\]

At \(t=\pi/2\) it is zero; the characteristic function of any Gaussian probability law, degenerate or nondegenerate, is never zero. Hence the limiting mixture is not any Gaussian. This is stronger than merely disagreeing with variance \(3/16\). No moment convergence is needed or inferred; in particular the mixture's moments are not being asserted to equal limiting moments of the finite count statistics.

## 11. Exact cyclic transfer, including the endpoint and size one

For a nonnegative integer vector \((x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) with sum \(n-1\), put \(a_i=x_i-1\), \(H_0=0\), \(H_j=\sum_{i\le j}a_i\). Thus \(H_n=-1\). Let \(j\) be the first index attaining the minimum among \(H_0,\ldots,H_{n-1}\), and rotate to start with \(a_{j+1}\).

For the nonwrapped partial sums ending at an index \(k<n\), their values are \(H_k-H_j\ge0\). If the old endpoint \(H_n\) is encountered before the final rotated step, then \(j>0\). Because the first minimum is not at 0, \(H_j<0\), so the integer \(H_j\le-1=H_n\). This explicitly proves nonnegativity at that old endpoint, the case that a reference only to the minimum among \(H_0,\ldots,H_{n-1}\) would otherwise omit.

For a wrapped partial sum ending at an earlier index \(0\le\ell<j\), the value is

\[
H_n-H_j+H_\ell=-1-H_j+H_\ell\ge0,
\tag{062.40}
\]

because the first-minimum condition gives \(H_\ell>H_j\) and the heights are integers. The full sum is \(-1\). Conversely, validity of a rotation at \(j\) requires later pre-endpoint heights to be at least \(H_j\) and all earlier heights to be at least \(H_j+1\); these force exactly the first minimum. Thus exactly one rotation is a valid depth-first offspring sequence.

A nontrivial cyclic period is impossible: if the increment vector were a repetition of \(m>1\) blocks, its total sum \(-1\) would be divisible by \(m\). Every orbit therefore has exactly \(n\) distinct vectors, with one tree sequence.

Independent-sample weights conditioned on the sum are proportional to \(\prod_i\pi_{x_i}\), invariant under rotation. Mapping an orbit to its tree accumulates the common factor \(n\) times that product. After normalization the tree has weight proportional to \(\prod_v\pi_{d^+(v)}\), exactly the Galton–Watson law conditioned on size \(n\). Every fixed-degree count is rotation invariant, so its entire conditional law transfers with no approximation.

For \(n=1\), the only sum-zero vector is \((0)\), its single rotation is valid, and the same argument applies. More generally the path tree has probability \(\pi_1^{n-1}\pi_0>0\) for every \(n\ge1\). Thus no subsequence is being selected merely to evade impossible conditioning sizes. Criticality and nondegeneracy also imply almost-sure extinction, but the transfer only needs these positive finite-size probabilities.

## 12. Stable-domain exclusion and original source match

### 12.1 Direct truncated-variance calculation

Write

\[
U(x)=E_\pi[X^2\mathbf1_{\{X\le x\}}],\qquad
W(x)=\operatorname{Var}_\pi(X\mathbf1_{\{X\le x\}}).
\]

At \(H=H_r=n_r^4\), every old point and the current pair lie below \(n_r<H/2\), and every future point lies above \(H\). Therefore

\[
U(H/2)=E_QX^2+p_r((L_r^-)^2+(L_r^+)^2)
\le M_r+2n_r^{-2}
\le\varepsilon_rn_r\Delta_r+2n_r^{-2}.
\tag{062.41}
\]

The inequality \(E_QX^2\le M_r\) uses \(X^2\le M_rX\) and \(E_QX=1-\Delta_r<1\). No final zero-mass term contributes to a second moment. At \(H\), the current adjustment atom gives
\(U(H)>\Delta_rn_r^4/4\). Every truncated first moment is between zero and the full first moment one, so \(U(x)-1\le W(x)\le U(x)\). The denominator \(W(H/2)\) is strictly positive because the truncated variable has positive probabilities of being 0 and 1. It follows that

\[
\frac{W(H_r)}{W(H_r/2)}
\ge\frac{\Delta_r n_r^4/4-1}{\varepsilon_rn_r\Delta_r+2n_r^{-2}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{062.42}
\]

For an explicit divergence check, eventually the numerator is at least \(\Delta_rn_r^4/8\) and the denominator is at most \(2\varepsilon_rn_r\Delta_r\); their ratio is then at least \(n_r^3/(16\varepsilon_r)\to\infty\). The claimed eventual bounds follow from \(n_r\ge2^{8r}\), \(\Delta_r=2^{-r}\) and \(\varepsilon_r=(r+10)^{-1}\).

If \(W(x)\sim x^{2-\alpha}L(x)\) with \(L\) slowly varying, its ratio at \(H_r\) and \(H_r/2\) must tend to \(2^{2-\alpha}\), a finite constant. Equation (062.42) rules this out for every \(\alpha\in(1,2]\), including the infinite-variance normal domain. This is a direct hypothesis check, not an inference from the non-Gaussian count limit. It uses exactly the truncated-variance quantity in the supplied primary source. It makes no unsupported assumption that infinite variance by itself excludes normal attraction.
