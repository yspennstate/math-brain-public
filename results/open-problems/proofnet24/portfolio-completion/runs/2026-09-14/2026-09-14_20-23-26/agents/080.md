The inheritance step in paper 3 is valid. The precise primary theorem covers its hypotheses and gives a stronger DC1 conclusion than the manuscript requires. A complete proof of the resulting open-dense theorem follows below, including a replacement horseshoe-density construction that handles endpoint fixed points and preserves surjectivity explicitly.

Its remaining horseshoe-density question needs correction: the stated one-step horseshoe class is empty throughout every \(L\)-Lipschitz stratum with \(L\le2\). **Exact audited import.** Write \(F_0=\operatorname{id}\) and \(F_n=f_n\circ\cdots\circ f_1\). Theorem B(i), page 2 of Štefánková’s author preprint, has the following applicable content, paraphrased in our notation:

If every \(f_n:[0,1]\to[0,1]\) is continuous and surjective, \(f_n\to f\) uniformly, and \(h(f)>0\), then the nonautonomous system is DC1.

The definition on page 1 supplies an uncountable set \(S\) and a **single** \(\varepsilon_0>0\) such that every distinct \(x,y\in S\) satisfies
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\#\{0\le j<n:|F_j(x)-F_j(y)|<\varepsilon_0\}=0
\]
and, for every \(t>0\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
\#\{0\le j<n:|F_j(x)-F_j(y)|<t\}=1.
\]
No convergence rate or summability assumption appears. [Author preprint, Theorem B(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.4083)

The published article’s abstract explicitly confirms DC1 inheritance under uniform convergence of continuous surjective interval maps. Its metadata are DCDS **36**(6), 3435–3443, 2016, DOI `10.3934/dcds.2016.36.3435`. The exact theorem body checked here is the author preprint; the published abstract was also checked, but the published proof was not independently replayed. Surjectivity cannot simply be discarded. Take \(f_1\equiv0\) and \(f_n=T\) for \(n\ge2\), where \(T(x)=1-|2x-1|\). The limit is the positive-entropy tent map, but all initial points have identical trajectories after time 1, so the system has no DC1 pair.

**Theorem 080.1 — the precise complete result.** Let
\[
C_s(I)=\{f:I\to I:f\text{ is continuous and surjective}\},
\]
\[
\mathcal F=
\{(f_n)\in C_s(I)^{\mathbb N}:f_n
\text{ converges uniformly}\},
\qquad
D((f_n),(g_n))=\sup_n\|f_n-g_n\|_\infty.
\]
Write \(\lambda((f_n))=\lim_n f_n\).

For \(m\ge2\), let \(\mathcal O_m\) consist of maps \(f\in C_s(I)\) for which there exist pairwise disjoint nondegenerate closed intervals
\[
A_1<\cdots<A_m,\qquad [u,v]=\operatorname{conv}\bigcup_iA_i,
\]
and \(\theta>0\), such that
\[
[u-\theta,v+\theta]\subset(0,1),
\qquad
f(A_i)\supseteq[u-\theta,v+\theta]\quad(1\le i\le m).
\]

Then:

1. \((\mathcal F,D)\) is complete.
2. Every \(U_m=\lambda^{-1}(\mathcal O_m)\) is open and dense.
3. Every element of \(U_2\) is DC1, with an uncountable scrambled set and a common positive separation threshold.
4. Every element of \(U_m\) has nonautonomous topological entropy at least \(\log m\). Consequently \(\bigcap_{m\ge2}U_m\) is a dense \(G_\delta\) consisting of systems with infinite entropy.

In particular, the complement of the DC1 class is nowhere dense: it is contained in the closed nowhere-dense set \(\mathcal F\setminus U_2\).

**Proof: completeness and the limit map.** A uniform limit of continuous interval maps is continuous. To verify preservation of surjectivity, let \(g_k\to g\) uniformly, with every \(g_k\) onto. For any \(y\in I\), choose \(x_k\) with \(g_k(x_k)=y\), and pass to a subsequence \(x_k\to x\). Then
\[
|g(x)-y|
\le |g(x)-g(x_k)|+\|g-g_k\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus \(C_s(I)\) is complete.

If \((f^{(k)})\) is \(D\)-Cauchy, each coordinate converges to some \(g_n\in C_s(I)\), and the convergence is uniform in the coordinate \(n\). Given \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(k\) with \(D(f^{(k)},g)<\varepsilon/3\). Since the sequence of maps \(f^{(k)}_n\) converges, its tail is \(\varepsilon/3\)-Cauchy. The triangle inequality shows that \(g_n\) is eventually \(\varepsilon\)-Cauchy. Therefore \(g\in\mathcal F\), proving completeness.

Also,
\[
\|\lambda(f)-\lambda(g)\|_\infty
=\lim_n\|f_n-g_n\|_\infty\le D(f,g).
\]
Hence \(\lambda\) is 1-Lipschitz.

**Proof: openness of \(\mathcal O_m\).** Suppose \(f\) has the displayed witnesses. For each \(i\),
\[
\min_{A_i}f\le u-\theta,\qquad
\max_{A_i}f\ge v+\theta.
\]
If \(\|g-f\|_\infty<\theta/2\), then
\[
\min_{A_i}g<u-\theta/2,\qquad
\max_{A_i}g>v+\theta/2.
\]
Since the continuous image of an interval is an interval,
\[
g(A_i)\supseteq[u-\theta/2,v+\theta/2].
\]
These are witnesses for \(g\in\mathcal O_m\).

**Proof: density, including endpoint fixed points.** Fix \(f\in C_s(I)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). There is a fixed point \(p\in I\), because \(f(0)-0\ge0\) and \(f(1)-1\le0\).

Choose \(0<\kappa<\varepsilon/4\). By continuity at \(p\), one can choose a nondegenerate interval
\[
J=[a,b]\subset(0,1)
\]
sufficiently close to \(p\) that
\[
J\cup f(J)\subset(p-\kappa,p+\kappa).
\]
This also works when \(p=0\) or \(p=1\), by taking \(J\) on the available side of the endpoint.

Set
\[
\alpha=\min\{a,\min_J f\},\qquad
\beta=\max\{b,\max_J f\}.
\]
Choose
\[
0<\tau<\min\{\kappa,a,1-b\},
\qquad
K=[\max(0,\alpha-\tau),\min(1,\beta+\tau)].
\]
Then
\[
f(J)\subset K,\qquad
[a-\tau,b+\tau]\subset K,
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}K<4\kappa<\varepsilon.
\]

Partition \(J\) into \(2m+2\) nondegenerate consecutive intervals, with endpoints
\[
a=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_{2m+2}=b.
\]
Define \(h=f\) outside \(J\), set \(h(a)=f(a)\), \(h(b)=f(b)\), and prescribe
\[
h(t_{2i-1})=\min K,\qquad
h(t_{2i})=\max K\quad(1\le i\le m),
\]
together with \(h(t_{2m+1})=\min K\). Interpolate linearly between consecutive prescribed values.

The resulting map is continuous and satisfies \(h(J)=K\). On \(J\), both \(h(x)\) and \(f(x)\) lie in \(K\); outside \(J\) they agree. Therefore
\[
\|h-f\|_\infty<\varepsilon.
\]
Moreover,
\[
h(I)\supseteq f(I\setminus J)\cup K
\supseteq f(I\setminus J)\cup f(J)=I,
\]
so \(h\) remains surjective.

Take
\[
A_i=[t_{2i-1},t_{2i}],\qquad 1\le i\le m.
\]
These intervals have positive gaps, lie inside \(J\), and satisfy \(h(A_i)=K\). Their hull \([u,v]\) obeys
\[
[u-\tau/2,v+\tau/2]
\subset[a-\tau,b+\tau]\subset K\cap(0,1).
\]
Thus \(h\in\mathcal O_m\), proving density.

This construction avoids both an unquantified replacement of an endpoint fixed point and any shrinking of a branch after its image has been specified.

**Proof: openness and density in the system space.** Openness of \(U_m\) follows from continuity of \(\lambda\). For density, fix \((f_n)\to f\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). Choose \(h\in\mathcal O_m\) with
\[
\|h-f\|_\infty<\varepsilon/3
\]
and choose \(N\) such that \(\|f_n-f\|_\infty<\varepsilon/3\) for \(n>N\). The sequence
\[
(g_n)=(f_1,\ldots,f_N,h,h,\ldots)
\]
belongs to \(U_m\) and satisfies \(D(f,g)\le2\varepsilon/3<\varepsilon\).

**Proof: entropy and DC1.** First suppose that, for all \(n>N\),
\[
f_n(A_i)\supseteq[u,v],\qquad 1\le i\le m,
\]
where the \(A_i\) have minimum gap \(\gamma>0\).

For every word \(\omega=(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_{L-1})\), choose \(y_{L-1}\in A_{\omega_{L-1}}\). Successively use the covering property to choose
\[
y_j\in A_{\omega_j},\qquad
f_{N+j+1}(y_j)=y_{j+1},
\quad j=L-2,\ldots,0.
\]
The prefix \(F_N\) is surjective, so choose \(x_\omega\) with \(F_N(x_\omega)=y_0\). Then
\[
F_{N+j}(x_\omega)=y_j.
\]
Different words yield trajectories separated by at least \(\gamma\) at some time before \(N+L\). Consequently
\[
s(N+L,\gamma/2)\ge m^L,
\qquad
h(f_{1,\infty})\ge\log m.
\]

Applied to the constant sequence generated by \(f\in\mathcal O_m\), this proves \(h(f)\ge\log m\). Thus, if \((f_n)\in U_2\), all hypotheses of Štefánková’s Theorem B(i) hold, and the system is DC1.

For general \(U_m\), uniform convergence to its limit makes every sufficiently late \(f_n\) preserve the horseshoe covering, with half the original margin. The preceding nonautonomous counting argument gives entropy at least \(\log m\). Completeness and Baire’s theorem finish the dense-\(G_\delta\) assertion. ∎

**Corrections to the archived proof.** The source is [paper3/dc1_generic.tex](https://github.com/yspennstate/math-open-problems/blob/2784d79d396e308cc6ec51465f105109815fd5da/paper3/dc1_generic.tex).

Its main mathematical conclusion survives. The following local edits would make its displayed proof accurate:

- Replacing an endpoint fixed point by an interior approximate fixed point introduces an error \(|f(z)-z|\). The displayed radius \(R\) does not contain that error. The density proof above handles endpoints without this omission.
- Shrinking an interval on a strictly increasing linear branch generally destroys the equality \(h(A_i)=K\). Use the full separated increasing branches; their connector intervals already place them inside the implantation interval.
- The stated quantitative conclusion that the displayed margin is at least the implantation scale does not follow from its formula. For example, its formula yields \(\theta=\eta/2\) when \(R-\eta=\eta\) and the boundary distance is larger. A positive margin suffices for all genericity conclusions.
- In entropy counting, using separation parameter \(\gamma/2\) avoids ambiguity between definitions requiring distances \(>\gamma\) and those allowing \(\ge\gamma\).

None of these repairs requires an additional research theorem.

**Theorem 080.2 — the correct conditional extension for restricted classes.** Let \(M\subset C_s(I)\) be closed in the supremum metric, and let \(\mathcal F_M\) consist of uniformly convergent sequences of maps in \(M\). Define
\[
\mathcal H_M
=\{f\in M:\text{for some }q\ge1,\ f^q
\text{ has a strict two-branch horseshoe}\}.
\]
If \(\mathcal H_M\) is dense in \(M\), then the DC1 systems contain an open dense subset of \(\mathcal F_M\).

**Proof.** For fixed \(q\), the map \(f\mapsto f^q\) is continuous in the supremum topology. Indeed, writing \(\omega_f\) for a modulus of continuity,
\[
\|g^{q+1}-f^{q+1}\|_\infty
\le\|g-f\|_\infty+
\omega_f(\|g^q-f^q\|_\infty),
\]
and induction proves continuity. Therefore \(\mathcal H_M\) is relatively open.

Completeness, the limit-map argument, and constant-tail replacement work exactly as above inside the closed class \(M\). Finally, a two-branch horseshoe for \(f^q\) supplies \(2^L\) separated orbit segments at times \(0,q,\ldots,(L-1)q\), so
\[
h(f)\ge\frac{\log2}{q}>0.
\]
The audited inheritance theorem applies. ∎

For a common modulus class
\[
M=C_s^\mu(I),\qquad
|f(x)-f(y)|\le\mu(|x-y|),\qquad \mu(r)\to0,
\]
pointwise convergence is uniform: use a finite sufficiently fine net and the shared modulus to control the error away from that net. Thus paper 4 correctly reaches the uniform-convergence hypothesis.

The integral and supremum metrics are also uniformly equivalent on such a class. If \(\|f-g\|_\infty=a>0\), choose a maximizer \(x_0\) and \(r>0\) with \(2\mu(r)\le a/2\). On the intersection of \(I\) with the radius-\(r\) neighborhood of \(x_0\),
\[
|f(x)-g(x)|\ge a/2.
\]
For \(r\le1\), that intersection has length at least \(r\), giving
\[
\int_I|f-g|\ge ar/2.
\]
Together with \(\int_I|f-g|\le\|f-g\|_\infty\), this provides the required uniform metric comparison, also after taking the supremum over time.

**Proved obstruction for paper 4’s stated one-step class.** Suppose an \(L\)-Lipschitz map has a strict \(m\)-branch horseshoe with hull length \(D=v-u>0\), branch lengths \(\ell_i\), and margin \(\theta>0\). Then
\[
L\ell_i\ge\operatorname{diam}f(A_i)\ge D+2\theta.
\]
Because the branches have positive intervening gaps,
\[
\sum_i\ell_i<D.
\]
Summing gives
\[
LD>L\sum_i\ell_i\ge m(D+2\theta)>mD,
\]
hence
\[
\boxed{L>m.}
\]
In particular, the one-step class \(\mathcal O_2\cap C_s^L(I)\) is empty for \(1\le L\le2\).

For an iterate \(f^q\), the corresponding necessary condition becomes \(L^q>2\). Allowing iterates removes this numerical obstruction when \(L>1\), but does not prove density. Theorem 080.2 states exactly what a successful density argument would yield.

The source [paper4/pointwise_baire.tex](https://github.com/yspennstate/math-open-problems/blob/2784d79d396e308cc6ec51465f105109815fd5da/paper4/pointwise_baire.tex) also describes horseshoe density and DC1 genericity as equivalent. Its proved theorem establishes only sufficiency; the converse is not supplied. The corrected formulation should retain that direction.

**Scope and final ledger.** The verified theorem concerns uniform convergence and the uniform-in-time supremum topology. It does not decide the genuinely pointwise problem. Balibrea–Rucká themselves distinguish the positive uniformly convergent/equicontinuous case from the pointwise question in Section 4. Accordingly, this audit verifies the stated theorem and its stronger open-dense formulation, without certifying historical novelty or a resolution of every question discussed in that paper. [Balibrea–Rucká, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10288)
