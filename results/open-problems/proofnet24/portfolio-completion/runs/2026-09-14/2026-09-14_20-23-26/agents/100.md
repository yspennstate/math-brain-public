## 3. Famous-problem attribution and exact limits

### Four-type QSO

The present main title, “Fixed-point uniqueness for four-type strictly non-Volterra quadratic stochastic operators,” is accurate. The coefficient condition means parental exclusion, not positivity of every permitted coefficient. Boundary fixed points are included. The main note explicitly distinguishes parent symmetry from symmetry among types and does not assume interiority.

The 2009 survey's Problem 11 asks about extending a three-type theorem that contains uniqueness, non-attractiveness, and cycle assertions. The new theorem supplies the four-type uniqueness clause and the exact archived question. It should not be advertised as a new resolution of every clause or every higher dimension. The already published five-type example obstructs an unrestricted higher-dimensional uniqueness extension. [Survey, Section 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.4207).

The 2016 scope remains genuinely unknown in this record. Its abstract and the author's 2017 retrospective announce symmetric four-type uniqueness but do not supply the needed definition. Consequently neither “the new result strictly extends Jamilov 2016” nor “Jamilov 2016 already proves the full theorem” is verified. This does not prevent presenting the current proof as a complete proof of its stated theorem.

### Five-type endpoint versus positive-mixture result

The directly retrieved original equation (7) agrees with \(W\) in the mixture note. Theorem 3 already classifies its two fixed points and the displayed tangent spectra. The endpoint is a nonnegative tensor with allowed zeros. Its interior fixed point does not mean the tensor is strictly positive.

The current segment is
\[
W_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)W+\varepsilon E_5,\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1.
\]
The note classifies exactly this family: one interior simplex fixed point, with raw-map instability on the stated parameter subinterval. Audit 091 distinguishes this calculation from the old endpoint and from general uniqueness for all positive five-type tensors. The latter remains unproved here. The old interior saddle also supports an elementary local perturbation argument, so merely continuing an unstable interior fixed point should not be advertised as an independent major discovery. [Original 2017 issue](https://drive.google.com/file/d/15VyaBFQM0YYz89y0lTXk1Unu4gx-LZao/view).

### Trees

Thévenin's closing conjecture is unnumbered. It extends the Gaussian degree-count conclusion to critical infinite-variance laws and to subcritical laws without a positive exponential moment. The stated counterexamples use the allowed finite set \(A=\{1\}\), a fixed aperiodic nondegenerate law, centering \(n\pi_1=n/4\), and scale \(\sqrt n\). Thus their stated non-Gaussian subsequence limits contradict those literal universal clauses. The proved stable-domain and power-law theorems have stronger hypotheses. [Thévenin, Section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.07365v2#S6).

Janson Problem 20.2 has three unlettered questions. The critical construction gives a negative answer to an unrestricted Gaussian extension in the second; the subcritical construction supplies a counterexample relevant to the third, open-ended question. It does not classify subcritical fluctuations or settle all moment questions. Labels (a), (b), and (c) are editorial, not original numbering. [Janson, Section 20.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0510).

The finite-variance degree-count CLT question already has the later positive result identified in audit 067. I independently checked Theorem 1.13 and Example 2.2: bounded local degree indicators yield normality and joint convergence without an extra third moment. This is prior work, not a new completion by this run. [Janson's later paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1211).

The titles of both counterexample notes are appropriately specific. Their law, centering, scale, and subsequence qualifiers must travel with any shorter announcement. The subcritical law has finite variance and lies in a normal attraction domain after centering; one must not say that both examples are outside every stable domain. The critical construction is the one explicitly shown to fail the relevant stable-domain condition. Computability of its selector does not mean any critical-stage integer was numerically produced.

The source match does not establish historical priority. Audit 067 explicitly records an inaccessible July 2026 lead and limits to its search. A later source may change the historical-status assessment. No “worldwide first,” “open until today,” or “all of Janson 20.2 solved” conclusion is supported.
