## Precise hypotheses and notation

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in[0,\infty)^4:\ \sum_{i=1}^4x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{k=1}^4P_{ij,k}=1.
\]
Assume
\[
0\le\delta:=\max_{i,j}\sum_{k\in\{i,j\}}P_{ij,k}
<1-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\tag{A}
\]
For `i = j`, the sum in (A) is `P_{ii,i}`, not twice this coefficient.
For distinct parents it is the sum of their two offspring probabilities. In
particular, (A) implies both `P_{bi,i} <= delta` for every `b,i` and the two-parent
group bounds used below. A hypothesis bounding each parental coefficient
separately by `delta` would not be the stated hypothesis and would not justify
the same two-parent group bound.

Set
\[
a=1-\delta,\qquad d=\sqrt{1+4a^2},\qquad
r=\frac{d-1}{2a}=\frac{2a}{d+1},\qquad q=1-r,
\]
and fix
\[
0<\lambda\le q,\qquad T=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V.
\]
All formulas are well defined since `a > sqrt(3)/2 > 0`. Useful exact identities
are
\[
ar=\frac{d-1}{2},\qquad ar^2+r=a,
\qquad q=\delta+a(1-q)^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Writing `a = r/(1-r^2)` shows that `r` increases strictly with `a` in `(0,1)`.
At `a = sqrt(3)/2` it is `1/sqrt(3)`, and at `a = 1` it is
`(sqrt(5)-1)/2`. Consequently
\[
2<d\le\sqrt5,\qquad
c:=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\le q<1-\frac1{\sqrt3}<\frac12,
\qquad q>\frac14.
\tag{2}
\]
For any cap `s` let `K_s = {x in Delta_3 : x_i <= s for all i}`. In particular,
`K_q` is nonempty, convex, and complete in the `l1` metric.

## 1. Coordinate bound and fixed-cap invariance

Fix an offspring type `i` and write `u=x_i`. For every ordered parent pair having
at least one parent of type `i`, (A) gives `P_{ab,i} <= delta`. For the remaining
pairs use `P_{ab,i} <= 1`. The total weights of these two classes of pairs are
`1-(1-u)^2` and `(1-u)^2`, respectively. Thus
\[
V_i(x)\le (1-u)^2+\delta\bigl[1-(1-u)^2\bigr]
=\delta+a(1-u)^2=:g(u).
\tag{3}
\]
This calculation includes the diagonal pair `(i,i)` exactly once as a term of
the ordered double sum; its coefficient is bounded by `delta` under the
repeated-parent convention above.

The function `g(u)-u` has derivative `-2a(1-u)-1 < 0` on `[0,1]`, and (1)
identifies its unique zero as `q`. Therefore every fixed point of `V` belongs to
`K_q`. No positivity of allowed coefficients or interior assumption is used.

Define
\[
f(u)=(1-\lambda)u+\lambda g(u).
\tag{4}
\]
It is convex, with `f(0)=lambda <= q` and `f(q)=q`. For `0 <= u <= q`, convexity
therefore gives `f(u) <= q`. Together with (3) this proves `T(K_q) subset K_q`.
Because `0<lambda<1`, the map `T` also maps the entire simplex to itself.

## 2. Polarization and the exact zero-sum norm formula

For `x,y in Delta_3` put `m=(x+y)/2` and define
\[
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_{b=1}^4P_{bj,k}m_b,
\qquad B(m)=(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A(m).
\]
The matrix `A` is nonnegative and column stochastic. Also
\[
A_{ii}=\sum_bm_bP_{bi,i}\le\delta.
\tag{5}
\]
Parent symmetry and the identity
`x_b*x_j-y_b*y_j = m_b*(x_j-y_j)+(x_b-y_b)*m_j` give
\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A(m)(x-y),\qquad T(x)-T(y)=B(m)(x-y).
\tag{6}
\]
Every column of `B` sums to `1+lambda`, so `B` preserves the zero-sum subspace.
On that subspace its induced `l1` norm is exactly
\[
\|B\|_{1,0}
=\frac12\max_{i,j}\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1
=\max_{G\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\},\ i,j}
\sum_{k\in G}(B_{ki}-B_{kj}).
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, if `z` is zero-sum and nonzero, let
`h = sum_i z_i^+ = ||z||_1/2`. Then
\[
z=h\sum_{i:z_i>0,\ j:z_j<0}
\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}(e_i-e_j),
\]
and the coefficients in this sum are nonnegative and sum to one. This proves
the first equality in (7), with the reverse inequality attained on a column
difference. Each column difference has coordinate sum zero, so its positive
coordinate sum equals half its `l1` norm; maximizing over subsets proves the
second equality. This argument does not require `B` itself to be stochastic.

## 3. Two-versus-two transfer estimate under total leakage

Let `G,H` be a partition into two pairs, let `i in G`, `j in H`, and call the
other members `k in G` and `ell in H`. Put
\[
p_{uv}=\sum_{t\in G}P_{uv,t},\qquad
\alpha_j=\sum_{t\in G}A_{tj}=\sum_bm_bp_{bj}.
\]
Since `{k,i}=G` is exactly the parental set, (A) gives `p_{ki} <= delta`.
Since `{ell,j}=H` is exactly the parental set, it gives
`p_{ell,j} >= 1-delta`. Parent symmetry gives `p_{ji}=p_{ij}`. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_i-\alpha_j
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
  +m_k(p_{ki}-p_{kj})+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-p_{\ell j})\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}+\delta(m_k+m_\ell).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
An explicit certificate for this inequality is obtained by subtracting the first
line from the second:
\[
m_i(1-p_{ii})+m_jp_{jj}
+m_k(\delta-p_{ki}+p_{kj})
+m_\ell(\delta-p_{\ell i}+p_{\ell j})\ge0.
\tag{9}
\]
Each summand is nonnegative: for the last summand use
`p_{ell,j} >= 1-delta` and `p_{ell,i} <= 1`.

If `m in K_s`, let `M=max(m_i,m_j)` and `N=min(m_i,m_j)`. Since
`p_{ij} in [0,1]`, (8) implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_i-\alpha_j
&\le M+\delta(1-M-N)\\
&=\delta+aM-\delta N\\
&\le\delta+a s=:\beta_s.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
This estimate holds for every `s in [q,1]`, including the whole-simplex cap
`s=1`. It does not require `s<1/2`.

## 4. Exhaustive cut audit and dominance

For each subset `G` and ordered pair `i,j`, the expression in (7) is
\[
(1-\lambda)(\mathbf1_G(i)-\mathbf1_G(j))
+2\lambda(\alpha_i-\alpha_j).
\tag{11}
\]
The cases are exhaustive as follows. The case `i=j` and the empty/full cuts
give zero and cause no difficulty.

| Position of distinct `i,j` relative to `G` | Bound on (11) | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Both in `G`, or both outside `G` | `2*lambda` | Identity terms cancel and `0 <= alpha_i,alpha_j <= 1`. |
| `i` outside `G`, `j` inside `G` | `3*lambda-1 <= 2*lambda` | Identity contribution is `-(1-lambda)`; use `alpha_i-alpha_j <= 1` and `lambda <= 1`. |
| `i` inside `G`, `j` outside, `card(G)=1` | `1-lambda+2*lambda*delta` | Here `G={i}`, hence `alpha_i-alpha_j=A_ii-A_ij <= delta`. |
| `i` inside `G`, `j` outside, `card(G)=3` | `1-lambda+2*lambda*delta` | Here `G^c={j}`, hence `alpha_i-alpha_j=A_jj-A_ji <= delta`. |
| `i` inside `G`, `j` outside, `card(G)=2` | `1-lambda+2*lambda*beta_s` | Equation (10). |

Since `beta_s >= delta`, (7) yields
\[
\|B(m)\|_{1,0}\le
\max\{2\lambda,\ 1-\lambda+2\lambda\beta_s\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Write `beta=beta_q`. One has
\[
\beta-q=\delta(1-q)\ge0.
\]
At the smallest cap the second entry in (12) strictly dominates the first:
\[
\begin{aligned}
1-\lambda+2\lambda\beta-2\lambda
&=1-\lambda(3-2\beta)\\
&\ge1-\lambda(3-2q)\\
&\ge1-q(3-2q)\\
&=(1-q)(1-2q)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Here the second inequality uses `lambda <= q` and `3-2q>0`. For `s>=q` the
second entry only increases, so it continues to dominate. Thus the same
calculation proves the entire cap family
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\le
\bigl[1-\lambda+2\lambda(\delta+a s)\bigr]\|x-y\|_1,
\qquad x,y\in K_s,\quad q\le s\le1.
\tag{14}
\]
This is an upper bound on the Lipschitz constant, not a claim that every
individual operator attains it.

## 5. Contraction, existence, and uniqueness

The cap constant satisfies
\[
\beta=\delta+a q=1-ar=\frac{3-d}{2},
\]
so (14) at `s=q` is
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\le L\|x-y\|_1,
\qquad L:=1-\lambda(d-2).
\tag{15}
\]
Since `d>2` and `lambda>0`, one has `L<1`. Also
`L=1-lambda+2*lambda*beta >= 1-lambda > 0`. Thus `T` is a strict contraction on
the nonempty complete invariant set `K_q`. Banach's theorem gives its unique
fixed point there. Since `lambda>0`, the fixed points of `T` are exactly the
fixed points of `V`; since every fixed point of `V` is already in `K_q`, the
fixed point is unique in the entire simplex.

The threshold in (A) is exactly the condition `d>2`, because `a>0` and
`d^2=1+4*a^2`. At equality `d=2`, this argument supplies `L=1` and therefore
does not establish uniqueness by contraction. No claim about the sharpness of
that threshold follows.

## 6. Independent audit of the global envelope

The algebraic identity claimed in `agents/011.md` is exact:
\[
\begin{aligned}
1-qd
&=1-d+\frac{2ad}{d+1}\\
&=\frac{1-d^2+2ad}{d+1}\\
&=\frac{2a(d-2a)}{d+1}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Strict positivity holds because `d^2-4*a^2=1` and `a>0`. Hence
\[
f'(q)=1-\lambda(1+2ar)=1-\lambda d
\ge1-qd>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Since `f''=2*lambda*a>0`, the map `f` is increasing on `[q,1]`, and
`f'(u)<=1-lambda` there, as suggested in the parent's note.

An exact factorization gives the stronger envelope ratio:
\[
f(s)-q=(s-q)\{1-\lambda[1+a(2-s-q)]\}.
\tag{18}
\]
For `q<=s<=1`, the bracket is increasing in `s`, with minimum
`1-lambda*d>0` and maximum
\[
\rho:=1-\lambda(1+ar)
=1-\lambda\frac{d+1}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]
It follows from (17) and `d>1` that `rho>0`; since `lambda>0` and `1+ar>0`,
one has `rho<1`. Consequently
\[
0\le f(s)-q\le\rho(s-q),\qquad q\le f(s)\le s.
\tag{20}
\]

For every larger cap `s in [q,1]`, convexity of `f` on `[0,s]` and
`f(0)=lambda<=q<=f(s)` give `f(u)<=f(s)` for all `0<=u<=s`. Equation (3) thus
proves
\[
T(K_s)\subseteq K_{f(s)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Defining `q_0=1` and `q_{n+1}=f(q_n)`, equations (20)--(21) give genuine global
coordinate caps:
\[
T^n(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_{q_n},\qquad
q\le q_{n+1}\le q_n,\qquad
q_n-q\le(1-q)\rho^n=r\rho^n.
\tag{22}
\]
Finite-time entry into `K_q` is neither assumed nor needed. Indeed, the bracket
in (18) is strictly positive, so this deterministic envelope remains above `q`
at every finite index when it starts from `q_0=1>q`.

## 7. Global geometric factor and exact prefactor calculation

Equation (14) and the identity for `beta` give, for every `s>=q`,
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\le
[L+2\lambda a(s-q)]\|x-y\|_1,
\qquad x,y\in K_s.
\tag{23}
\]
For arbitrary simplex points `x,y`, apply this bound at step `j` with
`s=q_j`, using (22):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T^n x-T^n y\|_1
&\le\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}[L+2\lambda a(q_j-q)]\|x-y\|_1\\
&\le L^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}
\left(1+\frac{2\lambda ar}{L}\rho^j\right)\|x-y\|_1\\
&\le L^n\exp\left(
\frac{2\lambda ar}{L}\frac{1-\rho^n}{1-\rho}
\right)\|x-y\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
The last step is `log(1+u)<=u` for nonnegative `u`. All divisions are legitimate
because `L>0` and `0<rho<1`. Since `1-rho=lambda*(1+ar)`,
\[
\frac{2\lambda ar}{L(1-\rho)}
=\frac{2ar}{L(1+ar)}
=\frac{2(d-1)}{L(d+1)}.
\tag{25}
\]
Dropping the factor `1-rho^n<=1` proves precisely the claimed bound:
\[
\boxed{
\|T^n x-T^n y\|_1
\le\exp\!\left(\frac{2(d-1)}{L(d+1)}\right)
L^n\|x-y\|_1
}\qquad(x,y\in\Delta_3,\ n\ge0).
\tag{26}
\]
Taking `y` to be the unique fixed point proves global geometric convergence of
the averaged iteration. The rate `L` is a certified uniform factor; it need not
be the true asymptotic rate of a particular operator.

As an internal consistency check, at `delta=0` and `lambda=q=c`, one obtains
`d=sqrt(5)`, `rho=c`, `L=(13-5*sqrt(5))/2`, and
`2*(d-1)/(L*(d+1))=2*c/L`. Thus (26) agrees exactly with the strict-case formula
already displayed in `agents/011.md`.
