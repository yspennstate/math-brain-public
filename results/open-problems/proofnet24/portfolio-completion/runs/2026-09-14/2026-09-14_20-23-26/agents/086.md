## 1. Definitions and the exact support identities

Let M=[m], m >= 3, and

\[
\Delta_{m-1}=\{x\in\mathbb R^m:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\}.
\]

Write

\[
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^m P_{ij,k}x_i x_j,
\qquad
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad
\sum_k P_{ij,k}=1,\quad
P_{ij,k}=0\ \text{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For a set S of types, define its offspring set

\[
\Phi(S)=\{k:\text{there exist }i,j\in S
                     \text{ with }P_{ij,k}>0\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Repeated parents i=j are included. The two parents in (1.2) must both be present. Thus an ordinary directed graph obtained by replacing a two-parent condition by separate single-parent arrows can give an incorrect reachability test. The exact combinatorial object is the map on subsets, or equivalently a directed hypergraph with unordered parental pairs as tails.

For 0<lambda<1 let

\[
T_\lambda(x)=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda V(x),
\qquad
\Psi(S)=S\cup\Phi(S).
\tag{1.3}
\]

**Proposition 1.** For every state x with support S,

\[
\operatorname{supp}V(x)=\Phi(S),\qquad
\operatorname{supp}T_\lambda(x)=\Psi(S).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{supp}V^n(x)=\Phi^n(S),\qquad
\operatorname{supp}T_\lambda^n(x)=\Psi^n(S).
\tag{1.5}
\]

**Proof.** A nonnegative sum defining V_k(x) is positive exactly when at least one term has positive coefficient and both positive parental coordinates. For the averaged map, its two summands have strictly positive scalar weights, so their supports are united. Induction proves (1.5). These arguments involve no cancellation and no limiting argument. \(\square\)

The endpoint restrictions matter: lambda=0 leaves every support unchanged, while lambda=1 gives Phi rather than Psi.

Both Phi and Psi are monotone with respect to inclusion. Psi is extensive, whereas Phi need not be extensive.

Call S **offspring-closed** if Phi(S) is contained in S. This is equivalent to invariance of the face Delta_S, where

\[
\Delta_S=\{x\in\Delta_{m-1}:\operatorname{supp}x\subseteq S\}.
\]

Indeed, the forward implication follows from (1.4). For the reverse implication, apply invariance to a point with every coordinate in S positive.

If x is a fixed point and S=supp(x), the exact necessary support equation is

\[
\Phi(S)=S.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Closedness alone is enough to obtain a fixed point somewhere in Delta_S by Brouwer's theorem. It does not ensure a fixed point with exact support S. Even the stronger equation Phi(S)=S does not ensure a fixed point with exact support S in general; Section 8 gives a counterexample with S=M and every output polynomial nonzero.

## 2. Closed faces, the minimal support lattice, and dimensions at most five

**Lemma 2.1.** Every nonempty offspring-closed set has at least three members.

**Proof.** If S={i}, its repeated-parent row must produce a type other than i. If S={i,j} with i distinct from j, the parental row ij must produce outside S. Each case contradicts closedness. \(\square\)

In particular every fixed point has at least three positive coordinates.

**Lemma 2.2.** Intersections of offspring-closed sets are offspring-closed, allowing the empty intersection result.

**Proof.** For closed S and F,

\[
\Phi(S\cap F)\subseteq\Phi(S)\cap\Phi(F)\subseteq S\cap F.
\]

The same argument applies to any finite intersection. \(\square\)

Starting from a nonempty S, iterate S_0=S and S_{n+1}=Psi(S_n). The chain is increasing, and every nonstationary step adds at least one member. Hence

\[
C(S):=\Psi^{m-|S|}(S)
\tag{2.1}
\]

is closed and is the smallest closed set containing S. To prove minimality, any closed set containing S contains every S_n by induction. Once a step is stationary, all later steps are stationary.

Define the common closure core

\[
K=\bigcap_{i\in M} C(\{i\}).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Distinct minimal nonempty closed sets are disjoint: a nonempty intersection would be a closed subset of each, and minimality would force equality.

There is a useful precise interpretation of K. If there is a unique minimal nonempty closed set F, every nonempty closed set contains F, and K=F. Conversely, if there are at least two minimal nonempty closed sets, they are disjoint, and K is empty. For the latter assertion, if i belongs to a minimal closed F, then C({i})=F; apply this to two disjoint minima. For the former, every finite nonempty family of closed subsets of a given nonempty closed set has a minimal member, so uniqueness forces that member to be F; moreover C({i})=F for i in F.

**Corollary 2.3.** For m <= 5 there is exactly one minimal nonempty offspring-closed set K. Every nonempty closed set and every fixed-point support contains K.

**Proof.** There is a minimal nonempty closed set because M is closed and the set system is finite. Two different minima would be disjoint sets of at least three types, requiring m >= 6. The preceding argument identifies the unique minimum with (2.2). \(\square\)

The bound m <= 5 is structural, not an assumption about positivity of allowed coefficients.

**Theorem 2.4 (all fixed points interior).** In any dimension, the following are equivalent:

1. Every simplex fixed point of V is interior.
2. There is no nonempty proper offspring-closed set.
3. C({i})=M for every i.
4. K=M.
5. For one, equivalently every, 0<lambda<1, the map T_lambda^{m-1} sends the whole simplex into its interior.

**Proof.** A boundary fixed point has a proper closed support. Conversely, Brouwer's theorem on a proper invariant face gives a boundary fixed point. This proves equivalence of 1 and 2. Closure minimality proves 2, 3, and 4 equivalent. If 3 holds, every initial support contains some i, and (1.5), monotonicity, and (2.1) yield 5. If a proper closed S exists, an initial point in Delta_S stays in that proper face under every averaged iterate, contradicting 5. \(\square\)

This theorem characterizes **all** fixed points being interior. It does not characterize the mere existence of at least one interior fixed point from coefficient zeros alone. Section 8 shows that the latter distinction is essential in five types.

## 3. The exact four-type criterion and the five-type boundary trichotomy

First record the general codimension-one support fact.

**Lemma 3.1.** If a fixed point has support F=M\{ell}, then

\[
P_{ij,\ell}=0\quad\text{for every }i,j,\qquad V_\ell\equiv0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The same conclusion follows if the face F is offspring-closed, without requiring a specified fixed point.

**Proof.** For a fixed point positive on F,

\[
0=V_\ell(x)=\sum_{i,j\in F}P_{ij,\ell}x_i x_j
\]

is a sum of nonnegative terms with all parental products positive. Thus all coefficients in this sum vanish. For closed F the same coefficient vanishing follows directly from its definition. Every remaining parental pair contains ell and is forbidden from producing ell by (1.1). \(\square\)

Let

\[
R=\Phi(M)=\{k:V_k\text{ is not identically zero}\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For any number of types, R contains at least three members: a global output set of size one is excluded by the repeated-parent row of that type, and a global output set of size two is excluded by the distinct-parent row of those two types. Also Phi(R) is contained in R, so R is closed.

**Theorem 3.2 (exact four-type answer).** For m=4, let p be the unique fixed point. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}p=R=\Phi(M).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular the following are equivalent:

\[
\boxed{
p\text{ is interior}
\iff V_k\not\equiv0\ \text{for every }k
\iff \forall k\ \exists i,j:\ P_{ij,k}>0
\iff \Phi(M)=M.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If p is on the boundary, precisely one output polynomial is globally zero; p has the other three coordinates positive, and V sends the entire simplex into that three-type face in one step.

**Proof.** Every fixed support has size at least three. If it has size three, Lemma 3.1 makes the missing output globally zero, so R is exactly that support because |R|>=3. If R=M, this rules out a boundary fixed point. If |R|=3, the whole map takes values in Delta_R, every fixed point lies there, and support size at least three forces its support to equal R. Existence and uniqueness are established in Section 9. A second globally zero output is impossible because |R|>=3. \(\square\)

Thus in four types a test of all output coefficient columns is an exact interiority test. Strong connectivity of a graph built only from diagonal rows is unnecessary.

For four types the unique minimal closed set is also R. If R=M, no proper closed set exists. If |R|=3, the only nonempty closed sets are R and M: a different closed triple would force a second globally zero output.

This gives the exact averaged closure classification in the boundary case:

\[
C(S)=
\begin{cases}
R,&S\subseteq R,\\
M,&S\not\subseteq R.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.5}
\]

If ell is the missing output, every 0<lambda<1 satisfies the exact identity

\[
(T_\lambda^n x)_\ell=(1-\lambda)^n x_\ell.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Accordingly, a state initially outside Delta_R never enters that face in a finite averaged time, although its omitted coordinate tends to zero. For lambda=2/5, the previously proved global convergence theorem for four types says that every state converges to p. Equation (3.6) and (3.5) show explicitly why eventual full support is compatible with a boundary limit when a proper closed face exists.

**Corollary 3.3 (five-type trichotomy).** For m=5 let K be the unique minimal nonempty closed set.

| Size of K | Fixed-point consequence |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Every fixed point is interior. No general uniqueness claim follows. |
| 4 | There is exactly one fixed point, of exact support K. |
| 3 | There is exactly one boundary fixed point, of support K. Any additional fixed point must have support all five types. |

**Proof.** The first row is Theorem 2.4. For the second, Brouwer gives a fixed point in Delta_K and minimality forces its support to equal K. Lemma 3.1 makes the omitted output globally zero, so every fixed point belongs to this face; the four-type theorem gives uniqueness.

For the third, the restriction to K is a three-type strictly non-Volterra QSO with a unique interior fixed point, by Section 9. Every fixed support contains K. Any distinct boundary fixed point would therefore have support four, but Lemma 3.1 and four-type uniqueness would then imply uniqueness of all fixed points, contradicting the existing fixed point on K. Another support-three fixed point would have to lie on K and would violate three-type uniqueness. \(\square\)

In particular a five-type operator has at most one boundary fixed point, even when it has additional interior fixed points.

## 4. Quantitative four-type interiority and exact distance to the boundary locus

Let

\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
B=\frac{1+2c^2}{1+2c},\qquad
d=1-B=\frac{2c(1-c)}{1+2c}>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Numerically d is approximately 0.267661084.

At any strictly non-Volterra fixed point, in any dimension,

\[
p_i=V_i(p)\le(1-p_i)^2,
\]

hence p_i<=c. Also, for distinct i,j,

\[
p_i+p_j\le1-2p_i p_j,
\tag{4.2}
\]

because the parental row ij, with total ordered weight 2p_i p_j, cannot produce either type, and all other parental rows contribute at most their full weight to these two outputs.

Since (c-p_i)(c-p_j)>=0,

\[
p_i p_j\ge c(p_i+p_j)-c^2.
\]

Substituting into (4.2) gives p_i+p_j<=B. In four types, applying this to the complementary pair yields the useful two-sided pair bound

\[
d\le p_i+p_j\le B\qquad(i\ne j).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Define the total ordered-parent coefficient weight of output k,

\[
\sigma_k=\sum_{i,j=1}^4 P_{ij,k}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The pairs containing k make no contribution. This is a sum of coefficients, not a stochastic output probability evaluated at a state.

For a>=0 and z>=0 put

\[
r_a(z)=
\frac{2a^2z}{1+2az+\sqrt{1+4az}}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For z>0 this is the unique root in [0,a) of u=z(a-u)^2; r_a(0)=0.

**Theorem 4.1.** Every four-type fixed point satisfies

\[
\boxed{p_k\ge r_d(\sigma_k)\quad\text{for all }k.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, if every output is nonzero and mu is the smallest positive coefficient of P, then

\[
\min_k p_k\ge r_d(\mu)>0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

**Proof.** By (4.3), p_j>=d-p_k for every j distinct from k. If p_k>=d then (4.6) is immediate, because r_d(z)<d for finite z>0. If p_k<d, every parent coordinate that can contribute to output k is at least d-p_k. Therefore

\[
p_k=\sum_{i,j\ne k}P_{ij,k}p_i p_j
   \ge \sigma_k(d-p_k)^2.
\]

The function u-z(d-u)^2 is strictly increasing on [0,d], so this inequality implies u>=r_d(z). Formula (4.5) follows by solving and rationalizing the quadratic. If every output is nonzero, sigma_k>=mu and the root increases with z, proving (4.7). \(\square\)

A simpler, weaker rational bound follows by dropping the nonnegative quadratic term after expansion:

\[
p_k\ge\frac{d^2\sigma_k}{1+2d\sigma_k}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For a boundary fixed point supported on three types, every positive coordinate is at least d, by applying (4.3) to it and the missing coordinate. The output-strength bounds above are not claimed sharp.

There is also an exact coefficient-space robustness radius. On the four-type coefficient class define

\[
\eta(P,Q)=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|,
\]

and let \(\mathcal B\) denote the arrays whose unique fixed point is on the boundary.

**Theorem 4.2.**

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{dist}_{\eta}(P,\mathcal B)
=2\min_k\max_{i,j}P_{ij,k}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

**Proof.** By Theorem 3.2, \(\mathcal B\) is the union, over k, of arrays whose output k vanishes identically. For a fixed k, replacing a row entry P_{ij,k}=a by zero requires row l1 distance at least 2a: the removed mass a must appear in other entries because both rows sum to one. Thus the distance to this component is at least 2 max_{i,j}P_{ij,k}.

Conversely, for each unordered parental row in which k is allowed, choose any other allowed output and transfer the entire mass P_{ij,k} to it. Such another output always exists with four types: a distinct-parent row has two allowed outputs, and a repeated-parent row has three. Mirror the change for reversed parents. The resulting array remains stochastic, symmetric, and parent-excluding, its output k is globally zero, and the distance of each changed row is exactly 2P_{ij,k}. Taking the minimum over k proves (4.9). \(\square\)

Therefore every admissible perturbation with row distance strictly below (4.9) preserves four-type interiority, and the radius is attained by an admissible array with a boundary fixed point.

## 5. Averaged entry, the exact common positivity core, and explicit bounds

Let mu be the minimum among the finitely many positive coefficients of P. It exists and is positive because every parental row is stochastic. Fix 0<lambda<1 and set

\[
q=\min\{1-\lambda,\lambda\mu\}>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

**Theorem 5.1 (quantitative support propagation).** Suppose the coordinates of a state x indexed by a nonempty S are at least b, where 0<b<=1. Then for every n>=0 and every k in Psi^n(S),

\[
(T_\lambda^n x)_k
\ge q^{2^n-1}b^{2^n}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently all coordinates in C(S) are positive after at most m-|S| steps. For any initial support S this time bound is coefficient independent.

**Proof.** Suppose all tracked coordinates at step n are at least b_n. A retained coordinate is at least (1-lambda)b_n, which is at least q b_n^2 because b_n<=1. A newly born coordinate has a parental pair among the tracked coordinates with coefficient at least mu and is at least lambda mu b_n^2, also at least q b_n^2. Thus b_{n+1}=q b_n^2 is a valid lower bound. Its exact solution is b_n=q^{2^n-1}b^{2^n}. Closure stabilization was proved in Section 2. \(\square\)

The starting set S need not be the entire support; this matters for a bound uniform over all starting states.

**Theorem 5.2 (exact common positivity core).** With K from (2.2), a coordinate k has the following equivalent properties:

1. k belongs to K.
2. Every averaged orbit eventually has a positive k-th coordinate.
3. There exist N and delta>0 such that (T_lambda^N x)_k>=delta for all x in the simplex.
4. The explicit bound below holds for every x and every n>=m-1:

\[
\boxed{
(T_\lambda^n x)_k\ge
q^{\,2^{m-1}-1}m^{-2^{m-1}}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** Some initial coordinate x_i is at least 1/m. If k belongs to K, it belongs to C({i})=Psi^{m-1}({i}); apply Theorem 5.1 with the singleton seed and b=1/m. This proves the bound at n=m-1. Apply the same bound to T_lambda^{n-(m-1)}x to get every later n. If k does not belong to K, choose i with k absent from C({i}); by (1.5), the averaged orbit from e_i has k-th coordinate zero at every time. This disproves 2 and 3 and finishes the equivalences. \(\square\)

Thus K is precisely the set of coordinates having a positive coefficient-dependent bound uniform over all initial states and all sufficiently late averaged times. This remains useful when K is a proper face. For m<=5, it is the unique minimal closed set from Corollary 2.3.

In the four-type interior case K=M, all initial states enter the interior by step three, with the explicit universal-for-this-array bound

\[
\min_k (T_\lambda^n x)_k\ge q^7/4^8,\qquad n\ge3.
\tag{5.4}
\]

An initial support of size two needs at most two steps, and one of size three at most one. In Section 6 an exact deterministic example shows that three steps from a vertex can really be necessary.

These results concern positivity and support. They make no convergence assertion for arbitrary lambda in (0,1).

## 6. Raw support dynamics, exact finite-face entry, and synchronization

Raw support is the finite deterministic dynamical system S -> Phi(S). A trajectory on the 2^m-1 nonempty subsets must eventually be periodic, but the coordinates of the corresponding QSO trajectory need not be periodic merely because its supports are periodic.

For a closed F, the exact finite-entry basin of its face under V is

\[
\mathcal E_F=
\bigcup_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq M\\
                   \exists n\ge0:\ \Phi^n(S)\subseteq F}}
       \operatorname{relint}\Delta_S.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This follows immediately from (1.5), and closedness ensures the state stays in the face after entry. If an entry occurs, the first entry occurs by time 2^m-2: before the first hit the support trajectory cannot repeat, since a repeat would trap it in a cycle avoiding the target. This is a finite exact decision procedure.

For an averaged map with 0<lambda<1, the finite-entry basin of Delta_F is exactly Delta_F itself, because every initially positive coordinate stays positive. This does not describe its asymptotic basin.

**Theorem 6.1 (exact raw synchronization criterion).** The following are equivalent:

1. There is N>=1 such that V^N maps the whole simplex into its interior.
2. There is a common N such that Phi^N({i})=M for every i.
3. For every i, its singleton support trajectory eventually reaches M.

Whenever these hold, Phi(M)=M, one can choose N<=2^m-2, and

\[
\boxed{
\min_k(V^n x)_k\ge
\mu^{2^N-1}m^{-2^N}>0
\quad\text{for all }x,\ n\ge N.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

**Proof.** The first implication follows by testing vertices. Conversely, every support contains a singleton, and monotonicity of Phi gives 2 implies 1. If any iterate of any set equals M, then Phi(M)=M: for any nonempty set S and n>=1, Phi^n(S) is contained in Phi(M), so reaching M forces Phi(M)=M. Thus M is absorbing, and the finitely many individual times in 3 can be replaced by their maximum. A singleton trajectory that reaches M has no repeated state before its first hit, so the first-hit bound is 2^m-2.

For the quantitative statement choose x_i>=1/m. Track the sets Phi^n({i}) and use nonnegative coefficients exactly as in Theorem 5.1, now with b_{n+1}=mu b_n^2. At the common time N, every coordinate is at least mu^{2^N-1}m^{-2^N}. Applying V^N to an arbitrary later state proves the bound for n>=N. \(\square\)

A positive power of the Boolean diagonal-offspring matrix, whose entry k,i is 1 when P_{ii,k}>0, is a sufficient test for Theorem 6.1: every path using diagonal offspring is also a valid Phi support path. The subset criterion above is exact and includes offspring requiring two different parents.

Here is a four-type deterministic example separating raw synchronization from interiority and making the averaged three-step bound sharp.

| Unordered parents | Offspring |
| --- | --- |
| 11 | 2 |
| 22 | 1 |
| 33 | 4 |
| 44 | 1 |
| 12 | 3 |
| 13 | 2 |
| 14 | 2 |
| 23 | 1 |
| 24 | 1 |
| 34 | 1 |

Every listed output excludes its parents. Every output type occurs, so the unique fixed point is interior by Theorem 3.2. Nevertheless

\[
V(e_1)=e_2,\qquad V(e_2)=e_1,
\tag{6.3}
\]

so no raw iterate maps the whole simplex into its interior. For every 0<lambda<1 the averaged support from e_1 follows exactly

\[
\{1\},\quad\{1,2\},\quad\{1,2,3\},\quad M.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus an interior unique fixed point, full averaged closure, and raw uniform interior entry are distinct notions.

## 7. Uniform positivity when allowed coefficients, or only diagonal coefficients, are positive

Assume first that every allowed coefficient has the common lower bound epsilon>0:

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge\varepsilon\quad(k\notin\{i,j\}).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Necessarily epsilon<=1/(m-1), from a repeated-parent row.

For every x and k,

\[
V_k(x)\ge\varepsilon(1-x_k)^2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is also a useful strict upper cap. For a parent row avoiding k, a diagonal coefficient into k is at most 1-(m-2)epsilon, while a distinct-parent coefficient into k is at most 1-(m-3)epsilon. With s=x_k,

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_k(x)
&\le[1-(m-3)\varepsilon](1-s)^2
        -\varepsilon\sum_{i\ne k}x_i^2\\
&\le U(1-s)^2,\qquad
U=1-\frac{(m-2)^2}{m-1}\varepsilon<1,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
\]

where Cauchy's inequality gives sum_{i not k}x_i^2 >= (1-s)^2/(m-1). The stochastic bound on epsilon makes U positive.

At every fixed point, (7.2) and (7.3) imply

\[
\boxed{
r_1(\varepsilon)\le p_k\le r_1(U)<c.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The first inequality uses that u-epsilon(1-u)^2 is strictly increasing on [0,1]; the second uses the analogous function with U and the inequality u<=U(1-u)^2.

More strongly, put y=V(x). Equation (7.3) gives y_k<=U, and (7.2) applied to y gives

\[
\boxed{
(V^n x)_k\ge
\varepsilon(1-U)^2
=\varepsilon^3\frac{(m-2)^4}{(m-1)^2}
\quad\text{for every }x,\ k,\ n\ge2.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

For n=2 this is direct; for larger n apply the same two-step statement to V^{n-2}x. Thus all allowed positive coefficients imply uniform raw interiority in two steps, regardless of convergence or uniqueness. One step cannot work on the whole simplex because V_i(e_i)=0. In four types (7.5) is 16 epsilon^3/9.

Only diagonal positivity is already sufficient for a weaker two-step bound. Suppose

\[
P_{ii,k}\ge h>0\quad(i\ne k),
\tag{7.6}
\]

and impose no positivity on distinct-parent allowed coefficients. Then

\[
\frac{h}{m-1}(1-x_k)^2
 \le V_k(x)
 \le\left(1-\frac{(m-2)h}{m-1}\right)(1-x_k)^2.
\tag{7.7}
\]

For the lower bound retain only diagonal terms and use Cauchy's inequality. For the upper bound, diagonal rows avoiding k have at least (m-2)h probability on other outputs, while distinct-parent rows contribute at most their full weight; again use Cauchy's inequality. The same two-step argument gives

\[
\boxed{
(V^n x)_k\ge
\frac{h^3(m-2)^2}{(m-1)^3}
\quad(n\ge2).
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Every fixed point also satisfies p_k>=r_1(h/(m-1)).

None of the estimates in this section asserts uniqueness for arbitrary positive five-type operators. They prove interiority and uniform positivity only, and their constants are not claimed optimal.

## 8. A complete five-type family with identical support pattern and different boundary basins

This section proves that exact coefficient support cannot determine an asymptotic boundary basin or the existence of an interior fixed point in general.

Let F={1,2,3}, and take

\[
0<a<1,\qquad 0<b<1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Define V^{a,b} by the following table; unlisted coefficients are zero and reversed parental rows are identical.

| Unordered parents | Output distribution |
| --- | --- |
| ii, i in F | Each of the other two members of F has probability 1/2 |
| Distinct i,j in F | The third member of F has probability 1 |
| i4, i in F | Output 5 has probability a; each k in F other than i has probability (1-a)/2 |
| i5, i in F | Output 4 has probability a; each k in F other than i has probability (1-a)/2 |
| 44 | Output 5 has probability b; each k in F has probability (1-b)/3 |
| 55 | Output 4 has probability b; each k in F has probability (1-b)/3 |
| 45 | Each k in F has probability 1/3 |

Every row sums to one, every output excludes both parents, and every parameter choice in (8.1) has exactly the same positive and zero coefficient locations. The face Delta_F is closed. It is the unique minimal nonempty closed set by Section 2. Every output polynomial is nonzero, so Phi(M)=M for this whole family.

Write

\[
u=x_4,\quad v=x_5,\quad t=u+v,\quad d_0(x)=u-v.
\]

For clarity d_0(x) here denotes the outside-coordinate contrast, not the constant d from Section 4. Along an orbit we write this contrast as D_n.

The last two coordinates are exactly

\[
V_4=2av(1-t)+bv^2,\qquad
V_5=2au(1-t)+bu^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
V_4+V_5=2at(1-t)+b(u^2+v^2),
\tag{8.3}
\]

\[
V_4-V_5=-(u-v)\,[2a(1-t)+bt].
\tag{8.4}
\]

For distinct i,j,k in F, direct subtraction of the coefficient table gives

\[
V_i-V_j=-(x_i-x_j)
 \left[\frac{x_i+x_j}{2}+2x_k+(1-a)t\right].
\tag{8.5}
\]

To verify (8.5) explicitly, the terms with both parents in F give the first two terms in the bracket. Cross-parent rows involving one outside parent contribute

\[
(1-a)t\sum_{\ell\in F\setminus\{i\}}x_\ell
\]

to V_i. Subtracting the analogous expression for V_j gives -(1-a)t(x_i-x_j). The 44, 55, and 45 rows contribute equally to all three F outputs and cancel.

### 8.1. Exact fixed points

The brackets in (8.4) and (8.5) are nonnegative throughout the simplex. At a fixed point a tested difference z therefore satisfies z=-Cz with C>=0, so it must be zero. Every fixed point has the form

\[
x=\left(\frac{1-t}{3},\frac{1-t}{3},\frac{1-t}{3},
              \frac t2,\frac t2\right),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Its scalar fixed equation is

\[
t=2at(1-t)+\frac b2t^2.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Conversely, a root of (8.7) in [0,1] fixes the last two coordinates; the first three outputs are equal and their sum is 1-t, so they are fixed as well.

There is always the boundary fixed point

\[
p=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0,0).
\tag{8.8}
\]

If a<=1/2, a positive t would require

\[
1=2a(1-t)+(b/2)t<1,
\]

because 2a<=1 and b/2<1 with t>0. Thus p is the only fixed point.

If a>1/2, the right side of that equation is strictly decreasing in t, starts above one, and ends below one. Hence exactly one further fixed point exists, and it is interior:

\[
t_*=\frac{2a-1}{2a-b/2}\in(0,1),
\qquad
q=\left(\frac{1-t_*}{3},\frac{1-t_*}{3},\frac{1-t_*}{3},
              \frac{t_*}{2},\frac{t_*}{2}\right).
\tag{8.9}
\]

In particular the two parameter choices

\[
(a,b)=(1/4,1/2),\qquad (a,b)=(3/4,1/2)
\tag{8.10}
\]

have identical Phi maps on all subsets. The first has only the boundary fixed point p; the second has p and the five-type barycenter q=(1/5,1/5,1/5,1/5,1/5). Thus Phi(M)=M and even the entire offspring support pattern do not decide existence of an interior fixed point in five types.

### 8.2. Contrast contraction for a specified averaged map

The remaining basin claims concern precisely

\[
T=\frac35 I+\frac25 V^{a,b}.
\tag{8.11}
\]

For every F contrast, (8.5) gives the multiplier

\[
\frac35-\frac25 C_F,\qquad
C_F=\frac{x_i+x_j}{2}+2x_k+(1-a)t.
\tag{8.12}
\]

Here 0<=C_F<=2, since its F contribution is at most 2(1-t), and its outside contribution is at most t. For the outside contrast, (8.4) gives the multiplier

\[
\frac35-\frac25 C_O,\qquad
C_O=2a(1-t)+bt,\quad 0<=C_O<=2.
\tag{8.13}
\]

Each multiplier belongs to [-1/5,3/5], so every contrast has absolute value reduced by a factor at most 3/5 per step:

\[
|x_{n,i}-x_{n,j}|\le(3/5)^n|x_{0,i}-x_{0,j}|,
\qquad
|D_n|\le(3/5)^n|D_0|.
\tag{8.14}
\]

No restriction beyond (8.1) is used.

### 8.3. If a<=1/2, every state converges to the boundary fixed point

If a<1/2, (8.3) and u^2+v^2<=t^2 give

\[
V_4+V_5
\le t[2a(1-t)+bt]
\le \rho t,\qquad
\rho=\max\{2a,b\}<1.
\tag{8.15}
\]

Thus under T,

\[
t_{n+1}\le(3/5+(2/5)\rho)t_n,
\tag{8.16}
\]

and t_n tends to zero geometrically. If a=1/2, the same calculation gives

\[
t_{n+1}\le t_n-\frac25(1-b)t_n^2.
\tag{8.17}
\]

This nonnegative sequence is decreasing and its limit must be zero, by taking limits in (8.17). In both cases the first three coordinates have total mass 1-t_n tending to one, and all their pairwise differences tend to zero by (8.14). Therefore every initial state converges to p.

For (a,b)=(1/4,1/2), the concrete outside-mass rate in (8.16) is 4/5, and every contrast decays by at most 3/5.

### 8.4. If a>1/2, the exact two basins are the face and its complement

Suppose now a>1/2 and let

\[
\alpha=2a-b/2>0,\qquad t_*=(2a-1)/\alpha.
\]

Using u^2+v^2=(t^2+(u-v)^2)/2 in (8.3), the exact averaged outside-mass recurrence is

\[
t_{n+1}
=t_n+\frac25[(2a-1)t_n-\alpha t_n^2]
       +\frac b5 D_n^2.
\tag{8.18}
\]

Subtract t_* and use alpha t_*=2a-1:

\[
\boxed{
t_{n+1}-t_*=
(t_n-t_*)\left(1-\frac25\alpha t_n\right)
+\frac b5D_n^2.
}
\tag{8.19}
\]

This is the complete scalar recurrence including the nonnegative contrast error; no symmetry of the initial state is assumed.

Let

\[
g(t)=t+\frac25\alpha t(t_*-t).
\]

Because alpha<2, the factor 1-(2/5)alpha t is strictly positive for 0<=t<=1. Consequently g(t)>=t_* when t>=t_*, and g(t)>=t when t<=t_*. If t_0>0, (8.18) and induction therefore imply

\[
t_n\ge\delta_0:=\min\{t_0,t_*\}>0.
\tag{8.20}
\]

By (8.14) and (8.19),

\[
|t_{n+1}-t_*|
\le L|t_n-t_*|+\frac b5(9/25)^nD_0^2,
\qquad
L=1-\frac25\alpha\delta_0\in(0,1).
\tag{8.21}
\]

Iterating this inequality gives

\[
|t_n-t_*|
\le L^n|t_0-t_*|
+\frac b5D_0^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}L^{n-1-j}(9/25)^j.
\tag{8.22}
\]

The sum tends to zero: it is a difference of two geometric sequences divided by L-9/25 when those numbers differ, and it is nL^{n-1} when they agree. Hence t_n tends to t_*. The contrast estimates then show that every initial state with t_0>0 converges to q.

If t_0=0, the closed face stays invariant, the first three coordinates have total mass one, and (8.14) shows convergence to p. Therefore the exact basins are

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Basin}_T(p)=\Delta_F,\qquad
\operatorname{Basin}_T(q)=\Delta_{4}\setminus\Delta_F
\quad(a>1/2).
}
\tag{8.23}
\]

Here Delta_4 is the five-type simplex.

For a<=1/2, the basin of p is all Delta_4 by Section 8.3. Since the coefficient support pattern never changes anywhere in (8.1), this proves the asserted non-combinatoriality of asymptotic boundary basins.

### 8.5. What the same example says about raw boundary-face attraction

For a,b>0 and any t>0, the expression in (8.3) is positive: if t<1 its first term is positive, and if t=1 its second term is positive. Thus no raw orbit starting outside Delta_F enters that face in a finite time. The exact raw finite-entry basin of this face is always Delta_F.

For a<1/2, (8.15) nevertheless makes every raw orbit approach the face in distance, with t_n<=rho^n t_0. For a=1/2 the raw counterpart of (8.17) again forces t_n to zero. The l1 distance to the face is exactly 2t_n, since at least t_n outside mass must be removed and the same mass added inside.

For a>1/2,

\[
t_{n+1}\ge 2a\,t_n(1-t_n)
\tag{8.24}
\]

under the raw map. If a positive sequence t_n were to tend to zero, it would eventually lie below 1-1/(2a), where (8.24) makes it strictly increasing, a contradiction. Hence only points initially on Delta_F approach that face in distance. This statement does not assert convergence of raw iterates to p or q.

Thus the same support pattern also has different raw asymptotic face-attraction basins, while its raw finite-entry basin is unchanged.

## 9. Self-contained uniqueness dependency for three and four types

Existence is Brouwer's theorem. At every fixed point p, parent exclusion gives p_k<=c as above.

If two fixed points x,y differ and z=x-y has only one positive coordinate i, then x_j<=y_j for every j other than i. The coordinate polynomial V_i uses only those other coordinates and has nonnegative coefficients. Hence V_i(x)<=V_i(y), contrary to x_i>y_i. Interchanging x and y also rules out only one negative coordinate. In three dimensions this is impossible for a nonzero zero-sum vector, proving three-type uniqueness.

For four dimensions the signs must be exactly two positive and two negative, with no zeros. Write their two-pair partition as G={i,k}, H={j,ell}. For a probability vector m define

\[
A(m)_{rj}=\sum_a P_{aj,r}m_a.
\tag{9.1}
\]

It is nonnegative and column-stochastic. Parent symmetry gives polarization:

\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A((x+y)/2)(x-y).
\tag{9.2}
\]

To see this, expand x_a x_b-y_a y_b with midpoint m as m_a(x_b-y_b)+(x_a-y_a)m_b and use parent symmetry to identify the two resulting sums.

Put p_{ab}=sum_{r in G}P_{ab,r}. The within-G distinct-parent row has p_{ki}=0, while the within-H distinct-parent row has p_{ell,j}=1. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf 1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
  -m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}\\
&\le\max\{m_i,m_j\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.3}
\]

This holds for every i in G, j in H.

Let h=sum_{i in G}z_i>0 and theta_{ij}=z_i(-z_j)/h^2. These nonnegative numbers sum to one and

\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\]

At m=(x+y)/2 every coordinate is at most c. Applying (9.2) and (9.3) yields

\[
h
=\mathbf 1_G^{\mathsf T}(V(x)-V(y))
\le2h\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}\max\{m_i,m_j\}
\le2ch<h,
\]

a contradiction because 2c<1. This proves four-type uniqueness with arbitrary zero allowed coefficients and includes boundary fixed points.

The only four-type dynamical statement imported from the source note, rather than reproved here, is the cited global convergence of T=3I/5+2V/5 in the paragraph following (3.6). All support and positivity results above stand independently of that convergence statement.
