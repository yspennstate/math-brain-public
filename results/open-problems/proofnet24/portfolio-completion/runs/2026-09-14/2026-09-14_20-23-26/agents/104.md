## Main result and precise status

Let \(0<K<I\) be any finite complex Hermitian kernel, let
\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1},\qquad
D_L=\operatorname{diag}(L_{11},\ldots,L_{nn}),\qquad
\Gamma=D_L^{-1/2}LD_L^{-1/2}.
\]
For every real vector \(a\), put \(A=\operatorname{diag}(a)\). The signs of the entries of \(a\) are unrestricted.

**PROVED, dimension-independent sufficient class.** If
\[
\operatorname{cond}_2(\Gamma)
=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)}{\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)}
\le 3,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\eta_0\|a\|_2^2
\le-\frac12\|a\|_2^2,\qquad
\eta_0=2-\frac{16\log(4/3)}{2+\sqrt3}>\frac12 .
}
\tag{1}
\]
The constant does not depend on the dimension or on marginal imbalance. In particular, the hypothesis holds if
\[
\|\Gamma-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac12.
\]

The proof reconstructs the exact signed diagonal derivative identities and separates the Hessian into the full Fisher matrix and nonnegative conditional-odds coefficients. A covariance estimate gives a dimension-independent Fisher lower bound. A common-outside-set reindexing controls each entire row of the conditional-odds matrix with two matrix-row bounds.

**PROVED, exact route obstructions.** The mixed coordinate entry \(\partial_i\partial_jH\) can have either sign, even inside the class (1). Also, feasible endpoints of a mixed-sign diagonal segment need not give a feasible coordinate rectangle. These are obstructions to particular proof shortcuts, not counterexamples to entropy concavity.

**OPEN IN THIS RUN.** Unrestricted diagonal-direction entropy concavity, arbitrary fixed-off-diagonal diagonal segments, and full Lyons entropy concavity remain unproved. The condition in (1) is sufficient; its failure says nothing about the sign of the Hessian.

## 1. Exact diagonal derivatives and signed nilpotent shifts

Let \(E=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Write configurations as \(x\in\{0,1\}^E\), and let \(p(x)=P_K(X=x)\). If \(I\subseteq E\), write \(p_{-I}(x_{-I})\) for the exact marginal probability after forgetting all coordinates in \(I\), including the convention \(p_{-E}=1\). Let \(\partial_i\) mean differentiation of the real diagonal entry \(K_{ii}\), with every other matrix entry fixed.

The atom determinant is
\[
p(x)=(-1)^{|\{i:x_i=0\}|}
\det\bigl(K-I_{\{i:x_i=0\}}\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]
For example, the probability generating determinant
\(\det(I-K+K\operatorname{diag}(z))\) gives an atom by choosing a \(K\)-column on present sites and an \(I-K\)-column on absent sites. Multiplying the latter columns by \(-1\) gives (2).

The determinant is multiaffine in its diagonal entries. Differentiating in a set of distinct diagonal coordinates leaves the principal determinant on its complement. Its sign in (2) gives
\[
\boxed{
\partial_I p(x)
=(-1)^{|\{i\in I:x_i=0\}|}p_{-I}(x_{-I}).
}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
\partial_i p(x)=(-1)^{1-x_i}p_{-i}(x_{-i}),\qquad
\partial_{ij}p(x)=(-1)^{x_i+x_j}p_{-ij}(x_{-ij}),
\qquad \partial_{ii}p(x)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The marginal law on \(E\setminus I\) has kernel \(K_{E\setminus I}\); it does not depend on the removed diagonal entries. Formula (3) is also a polynomial identity, so its algebraic validity does not require positivity of the atoms.

For a real array \(f\) on configurations, define
\[
(D_if)(x)=(2x_i-1)
\bigl[f(x_i=0,x_{-i})+f(x_i=1,x_{-i})\bigr].
\]
Then \(D_i^2=0\), the operators \(D_i,D_j\) commute, and (3) gives the exact finite identity
\[
\boxed{
p_{K+\operatorname{diag}(h)}
=\prod_{i=1}^n(I+h_iD_i)p_K
=\exp\!\left(\sum_i h_iD_i\right)p_K.
}
\tag{5}
\]
A feasible kernel on the left has a probability law. No feasibility claim is made for arbitrary \(h\).

These are signed nilpotent shifts, not ordinary coordinate resampling. On a two-entry column \((f(0),f(1))^T\),
\[
I+hD_i=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-h&-h\\
h&1+h
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]
Its columns sum to one, but one off-diagonal entry is negative for every nonzero real \(h\). Therefore it is not a Markov transition matrix. Positivity on a particular feasible DPP path does not make it a positive operator on all probability arrays. No product-noise theorem is invoked here.

## 2. Scores and the exact diagonal Hessian

The interior assumption \(0<K<I\) implies \(L>0\) and
\[
p(S)=\frac{\det L_S}{\det(I+L)}>0
\]
for every subset \(S\), including the empty set. Thus entropy is twice differentiable in a neighborhood of \(K\).

Define the real coordinate scores and their full Fisher matrix by
\[
s_i(x)=\frac{\partial_i p(x)}{p(x)},\qquad
J_{ij}=\mathbb E[s_i(X)s_j(X)].
\tag{7}
\]
If
\[
q_i(x_{-i})=P_K(X_i=1\mid X_{-i}=x_{-i}),
\]
then (4) gives
\[
s_i(x)=\frac{x_i-q_i(x_{-i})}
{q_i(x_{-i})(1-q_i(x_{-i}))}.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
J_{ii}
=\mathbb E_{X_{-i}}\frac1{q_i(X_{-i})(1-q_i(X_{-i}))}
\ge4.
\tag{9}
\]
This conditional \(q_i\) should not be confused with the unconditional marginal \(K_{ii}\).

For \(i\ne j\), let \(T\subseteq E\setminus\{i,j\}\) be the included sites outside the pair; all other outside sites are absent. Write
\[
p_{ab}(T)
=P_K(X_i=a,X_j=b,X_{E\setminus\{i,j\}}=T),
\quad
m_{ij}(T)=\sum_{a,b}p_{ab}(T)=p_{-ij}(T).
\]
These are unconditioned joint atoms and an outside marginal. Define
\[
\boxed{
c_{ij}
=\sum_T m_{ij}(T)
\log\frac{p_{10}(T)p_{01}(T)}
{p_{00}(T)p_{11}(T)},\qquad c_{ii}=0.
}
\tag{10}
\]
The weights are exactly \(m_{ij}(T)\), are positive, and sum to one. One may replace all four \(p_{ab}(T)\) inside the odds ratio by probabilities conditional on the outside, since their common normalization cancels.

Twice differentiating \(H=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)\), using normalization, gives
\[
\partial_{ij}H
=-J_{ij}-\sum_x\partial_{ij}p(x)\log p(x).
\]
For \(i=j\), the second term vanishes. For \(i\ne j\), group configurations by their outside set \(T\). The signs in (4) are positive at \(00,11\) and negative at \(10,01\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\nabla_{\mathrm{diag}}^2H=-J+C,\qquad
D^2H(K)[\operatorname{diag}(a),\operatorname{diag}(a)]
=-a^TJa+2\sum_{i<j}a_i a_jc_{ij}.
}
\tag{11}
\]
This is an exact identity for arbitrary signs of \(a\). In particular, \(c_{ij}\) is not itself the mixed entropy Hessian entry.

## 3. Conditional odds as Schur-complement correlations

Fix \(i,j,T\) as above, and put \(V=T\cup\{i,j\}\). Conditioning on all outside coordinates means that sets on \(V\) must contain \(T\), while every site outside \(V\) is absent. The two-site conditional law is an L-ensemble with matrix
\[
Q^L_{ij\mid T}=L_V/L_T.
\tag{12}
\]
Here and below an empty conditioning set gives the original relevant principal matrix. Indeed, for \(Y\subseteq\{i,j\}\),
\[
\det L_{T\cup Y}=\det L_T\,\det(Q^L_{ij\mid T})_Y,
\]
so the conditional probabilities of \(Y\) are proportional to these two-site principal minors. Excluded outside sites do not enter this Schur complement.

Writing \(L=D_L^{1/2}\Gamma D_L^{1/2}\), the same factorization gives
\[
Q^L_{ij\mid T}
=(D_L)_{\{i,j\}}^{1/2}
Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T}
(D_L)_{\{i,j\}}^{1/2},
\qquad
Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T}=\Gamma_V/\Gamma_T.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus positive diagonal scalings cancel from the normalized correlation
\[
\rho_{ij}(T)^2
=\frac{|(Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T})_{ij}|^2}
{(Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T})_{ii}(Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T})_{jj}}
=\frac{|(Q^L_{ij\mid T})_{ij}|^2}
{(Q^L_{ij\mid T})_{ii}(Q^L_{ij\mid T})_{jj}}.
\]
Positive definiteness gives \(0\le\rho_{ij}(T)^2<1\). The four two-site L-weights are \(1,Q_{ii},Q_{jj},\det Q\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{p_{10}(T)p_{01}(T)}{p_{00}(T)p_{11}(T)}
=-\log\bigl(1-\rho_{ij}(T)^2\bigr)\ge0.
}
\tag{14}
\]
In particular, every \(c_{ij}\ge0\), for real symmetric and complex Hermitian kernels alike. This argument verifies all inclusions, exclusions, and normalizations in (10); it uses no replacement of the measured point basis.

## 4. Dimension-independent Fisher lower bound

Let \(\xi_i=X_i-K_{ii}\), and let \(\Sigma=\mathbb E[\xi\xi^T]\) be the real occupation covariance matrix. The one- and two-point DPP formulas give
\[
\Sigma_{ii}=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}),\qquad
\Sigma_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{15}
\]
Every atom has positive probability. A nonzero linear combination of the occupation indicators therefore cannot be constant, so \(\Sigma>0\).

Differentiating \(\mathbb E X_j=K_{jj}\) with respect to \(K_{ii}\) gives
\[
\mathbb E s_i=0,\qquad \mathbb E[s_i\xi_j]=\delta_{ij}.
\]
Consequently, for the score vector \(s\),
\[
\mathbb E[(s-\Sigma^{-1}\xi)(s-\Sigma^{-1}\xi)^T]
=J-\Sigma^{-1}\succeq0.
\]
This proves the full linear-score projection or Cramer--Rao inequality
\[
\boxed{J\succeq\Sigma^{-1}.}
\tag{16}
\]
It uses the entire occupation vector as a collection of fixed statistics and projects linearly onto its centered span. It does not replace the full Fisher information by a conditional expectation that reveals the whole configuration.

Since \(K^2\prec K\),
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}|K_{ij}|^2
=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})-[K(I-K)]_{ii}.
\]
The absolute row sum of \(\Sigma\) is therefore
\[
\Sigma_{ii}+\sum_{j\ne i}|\Sigma_{ij}|
=2K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})-[K(I-K)]_{ii}
<\frac12.
\tag{17}
\]
Gershgorin's bound for the real symmetric matrix \(\Sigma\) gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)<\frac12,\qquad
\boxed{J\succeq\Sigma^{-1}\succ2I.}
\tag{18}
\]
For a possibly boundary positive contraction, (15) and the non-strict bound \(\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\le1/2\) remain valid. An inverse or classical Fisher matrix need not be available there.

The universal constant \(2\) in (18) cannot be increased uniformly over all interior kernels. For
\[
K_s=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&s\\s&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\quad 0<s<1/2,\quad a=(1,-1),
\]
the four atoms are \(1/4-s^2,1/4+s^2,1/4+s^2,1/4-s^2\). The directional first derivatives are \(0,1,-1,0\), so
\[
\frac{a^TJa}{\|a\|^2}
=\frac1{1/4+s^2}\longrightarrow2.
\]

Set
\[
r(K)=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}.
\]
Nonnegativity of \(c_{ij}\) and \(2a_i a_j\le a_i^2+a_j^2\) imply
\[
2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}a_i a_j\le r(K)\|a\|^2.
\]
Combining this with (11) and (16) yields
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[\operatorname{diag}(a),\operatorname{diag}(a)]
\le-\left(\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)^{-1}-r(K)\right)\|a\|^2
\le-(2-r(K))\|a\|^2.
}
\tag{19}
\]
Thus \(r(K)<2\) gives a uniform strict margin. In fact \(r(K)\le2\) already gives strict negativity for every nonzero \(a\) at any interior kernel, by the first inequality and (17). There is no claim that the Fisher bound alone dominates the curvature for every kernel or for general Hermitian directions; the row-budget restriction is essential to this argument.

## 5. Two matrix estimates

Let \(B\) be any positive definite Hermitian matrix with
\[
mI\preceq B\preceq MI,\qquad
\kappa=M/m,\qquad d=(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1).
\]
For distinct entries define
\[
r_{ij}(B)=\frac{|B_{ij}|^2}{B_{ii}B_{jj}}.
\]
Two estimates will be used:
\[
\boxed{
r_{ij}(B)\le d^2,\qquad
\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}(B)\le(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2.
}
\tag{20}
\]

For the first estimate, a two-by-two principal submatrix has eigenvalues \(\alpha\le\beta\) with \(\beta/\alpha\le\kappa\). If its diagonal entries are \(u,v\) and its normalized squared correlation is \(r\), then
\[
\frac4{1-r}
\le\frac{(u+v)^2}{uv(1-r)}
=\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^2}{\alpha\beta}
\le\frac{(\kappa+1)^2}{\kappa}.
\]
Rearranging proves \(r\le d^2\).

For the second estimate, functional calculus gives
\[
(B-mI)(MI-B)\succeq0.
\]
Its \(i\)-th diagonal entry implies
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}|B_{ij}|^2
\le(M-B_{ii})(B_{ii}-m).
\]
Since \(B_{jj}\ge m\), putting \(b=B_{ii}\) gives
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}(B)
\le\frac{(M-b)(b-m)}{mb}
=\kappa+1-\frac bm-\frac Mb
\le\kappa+1-2\sqrt\kappa
=(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2.
\]
The last step is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. No dimension factor occurs.

If \(mI\preceq\Gamma\preceq MI\), every principal matrix of \(\Gamma\) has the same bounds, and every Schur complement has eigenvalues in \([m,M]\). To prove the latter directly, for a complement vector \(v\),
\[
v^*(\Gamma/\Gamma_U)v
=\min_w (w,v)^*\Gamma(w,v).
\]
The lower bound is at least \(m\|v\|^2\), and choosing \(w=0\) gives the upper bound \(M\|v\|^2\). Every inverse of a principal matrix has eigenvalues in \([1/M,1/m]\), hence the same upper bound \(\kappa\) on its eigenvalue ratio. These facts hold without change over the complex field.

## 6. A common-outside-set row bound removes the dimension factor

Put \(g(t)=-\log(1-t)\). Fix \(i\), and now use one common random outside configuration
\[
U=X_{E\setminus\{i\}}\subseteq E\setminus\{i\}.
\]
For each \(j\ne i\), the function \(\rho_{ij}(U\setminus\{j\})\) forgets whether \(j\) belongs to \(U\). The exact weight identity is
\[
p_{-ij}(T)=p_{-i}(T)+p_{-i}(T\cup\{j\}).
\]
Summing (10) after this reindexing proves
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}
=\mathbb E_{U=X_{-i}}
\left[\sum_{j\ne i}g\!\left(\rho_{ij}(U\setminus\{j\})^2\right)\right].
}
\tag{21}
\]
This is one common expectation for the whole row, not a separate supremum over the conditioning set for every pair.

Fix a deterministic \(U\), and split the inside sum into two groups.

* If \(j\notin U\), then \(T=U\). All correlations in this group are entries of the single row \(i\) of
  \[
  B_{\mathrm{out}}=\Gamma/\Gamma_U
  \]
  on \(E\setminus U\). Its principal two-by-two block on \(i,j\) is precisely \(Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid U}\).

* If \(j\in U\), put \(V=U\cup\{i\}\). All correlations in this group are entries of row \(i\) of the single matrix
  \[
  B_{\mathrm{in}}=(\Gamma_V)^{-1}.
  \]
  Indeed \(T=U\setminus\{j\}\), and block inversion gives
  \[
  (B_{\mathrm{in}})_{\{i,j\}}=(Q^\Gamma_{ij\mid T})^{-1}.
  \]
  Inverting a positive two-by-two matrix preserves the magnitude of its normalized off-diagonal correlation:
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix}u&z\\\bar z&v\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
  =\frac1{uv-|z|^2}
  \begin{pmatrix}v&-z\\-\bar z&u\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  The sign and complex phase do not affect the squared correlation.

For \(U=\varnothing\) the inside group is empty; for \(U=E\setminus\{i\}\) the outside group is empty. Thus the argument includes all conditioning events.

Suppose \(\operatorname{cond}_2(\Gamma)\le\kappa\). Section 5 shows that both matrices in the split have eigenvalue ratio at most \(\kappa\). Applying (20) to both rows gives, pointwise in \(U\),
\[
\rho_{ij}(U\setminus\{j\})^2\le d^2,\qquad
\sum_{j\ne i}\rho_{ij}(U\setminus\{j\})^2
\le2(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Because \(g\) is convex and \(g(0)=0\),
\[
g(t)\le \frac{g(d^2)}{d^2}\,t,\qquad 0\le t\le d^2,
\]
when \(d>0\). Substitute this in (21)-(22):
\[
\boxed{
r(K)\le
2(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2
\frac{-\log(1-d^2)}{d^2},
\qquad d=\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}.
}
\tag{23}
\]
For \(\kappa=1\), take the continuous value zero. All correlations then vanish.

This bound is independent of \(n\). It controls the sum of expected conditional-odds logs, including the configuration-dependent conditioning sets, rather than only controlling each log separately.

### 6.1 The explicit condition-number-three theorem

For \(\kappa=3\), one has \(d=1/2\), and (23) becomes
\[
r(K)\le 8(\sqrt3-1)^2\log(4/3)
=\frac{16\log(4/3)}{2+\sqrt3}.
\tag{24}
\]
The right side is strictly less than \(3/2\). For an elementary certificate,
\[
\log(4/3)<1/3,\qquad \sqrt3>5/3,
\]
so it is less than \(16/11<3/2\). Combining (24) with (19) proves (1).

The result does not assume that \(a\) is positive, negative, trace zero, supported on two sites, or balanced. It concerns precisely real diagonal directions in the original point-coordinate basis.

### 6.2 The root's normalized-operator-norm seed and its sharpening

If \(\|\Gamma-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta<1\), then all two-site conditional Schur complements have eigenvalues in \([1-\delta,1+\delta]\). Formula (20) gives \(\rho_{ij}(T)\le\delta\). Therefore the direct pairwise bound is
\[
c_{ij}\le-\log(1-\delta^2),\qquad
r(K)\le(n-1)[-\log(1-\delta^2)].
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the seed condition
\[
(n-1)[-\log(1-\delta^2)]<2
\]
is valid. Equality also gives strict local diagonal negativity at an interior point by (17)-(19).

The common-outside-set argument gives the stronger dimension-independent bound
\[
\boxed{
r(K)\le F(\delta)
=\frac{4[-\log(1-\delta^2)]}
{(1-\delta)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^2})},
\qquad F(0)=0.
}
\tag{26}
\]
Indeed substitute \(\kappa=(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)\) into (23) and use
\[
(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2
=\frac{2\delta^2}{(1-\delta)(1+\sqrt{1-\delta^2})}.
\]
Both (25) and (26) are available; one can take their minimum. Condition \(F(\delta)<2\) suffices in every dimension, and \(\delta\le1/2\) gives the explicit margin in (1).

## 7. Marginal scaling, nontrivial examples, segments, and boundaries

If \(W\) is any positive diagonal matrix and \(L\) is replaced by \(WLW\), its normalized matrix \(\Gamma\) is unchanged. The atom probabilities and the outside weights in (10) can change, but the deterministic bounds (22) hold for every outside configuration and remain unchanged. Thus (1) allows arbitrary positive diagonal L-scalings, without a lower bound on marginal probabilities or one-site variances.

For a nontrivial family in every size \(n\), let \(\Gamma_n\) have diagonal entries one, nearest-neighbor entries \(1/4\) on a path, and all other off-diagonal entries zero. Its deviation from \(I\) has operator norm at most \(1/2\), by the absolute row-sum bound. It is positive definite, since its exact path eigenvalues are \(1+(1/2)\cos(k\pi/(n+1))>1/2\). Alternatively, strict positivity follows directly from the quadratic form or the strict endpoint row inequalities and connectedness. Consequently every \(L=W\Gamma_nW\), with arbitrary positive diagonal \(W\), gives an interior kernel covered by (1). The neighboring interaction magnitude need not shrink with \(n\).

For a finite segment, assume
\[
K_t=(1-t)K_0+tK_1,\qquad
K_1-K_0=\operatorname{diag}(a),
\]
and suppose \(0<K_t<I\) and \(\operatorname{cond}_2(\Gamma(K_t))\le3\) throughout the segment. Then \(H''(K_t)\le-\eta_0\|a\|^2\), and elementary integration gives
\[
\boxed{
H(K_t)\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
+\frac{\eta_0}{2}t(1-t)\|a\|^2
\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
+\frac14t(1-t)\|a\|^2.
}
\tag{27}
\]
The kernel path is affine in \(K\). Its \(L\)-matrix and \(\Gamma\)-matrix are generally nonlinear. Checking the condition at the two endpoints alone has not been proved sufficient, and no convexity of the condition-number sublevel set is asserted.

Every interior diagonal kernel has \(\Gamma=I\). Continuity therefore gives a neighborhood with the sufficient condition. On any convex portion of a fixed-off-diagonal diagonal slice contained in that neighborhood, (27) applies to all its endpoint pairs. The theorem does not address arbitrary off-diagonal directions in that neighborhood.

At boundary kernels, some atoms may vanish and the ordinary Hessian or the logarithms in (10) need not exist. The polynomial identities in Section 1 and the non-strict covariance bound remain valid. Formula (27) extends to boundary endpoints by continuity if the open segment is interior and satisfies the stated uniform condition. No unsupported boundary Hessian or automatic regularization claim is made.

## 8. Exact obstructions to two tempting shortcuts

### 8.1 The mixed entropy Hessian entry has neither universal sign

For two sites, write the four probabilities in the order \(00,10,01,11\) as \(d,b,c,e\), with \(d+b+c+e=1\). The score identities give
\[
J_{12}=(bc-de)\left(\frac1d+\frac1b+\frac1c+\frac1e\right),
\qquad
\partial_{12}H=\log\frac{bc}{de}-J_{12}.
\tag{28}
\]
Here \(bc-de=|K_{12}|^2\ge0\), but the difference in (28) has either sign.

Take
\[
K_+=
\begin{pmatrix}
9/10&1/20\\
1/20&1/10
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both \(K_+\) and \(I-K_+\) are positive definite; their determinant is \(7/80\). The atom vector is
\[
(d,b,c,e)=\left(\frac7{80},\frac{13}{16},\frac1{80},\frac7{80}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\partial_{12}H(K_+)
=\log(65/49)-\frac{592}{2275}
>\frac{16}{57}-\frac{592}{2275}
=\frac{2656}{129675}>0.
}
\tag{29}
\]
The logarithm lower bound is \(\log z>2(z-1)/(z+1)\) for \(z>1\), obtained from the positive series for \(2\operatorname{atanh}((z-1)/(z+1))\).

In contrast, take
\[
K_-=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/10\\
1/10&1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its atoms are \((6/25,13/50,13/50,6/25)\), and
\[
\boxed{
\partial_{12}H(K_-)
=2\log(13/12)-\frac{25}{156}<0.
}
\tag{30}
\]
Indeed
\[
\log(13/12)=2\operatorname{atanh}(1/25)
<\frac{2/25}{1-1/625}=\frac{25}{312}.
\]

These examples both lie inside the sufficient class (1). Their normalized L-correlations are respectively \(4/\sqrt{65}<1/2\) and \(5/13<1/2\); the associated two-by-two normalized matrices therefore have condition number less than three. Positive or negative off-diagonal Hessian entries can coexist with negative definiteness of the full diagonal Hessian. The examples disprove universal entrywise-sign shortcuts, not entropy concavity.

### 8.2 Mixed-sign endpoint rectangles can leave the contraction cone

Let
\[
K_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&3/10\\3/10&4/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}4/5&3/10\\3/10&1/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both endpoints are strict positive contractions: the determinants of \(K_j\) and \(I-K_j\) are \(7/100\). Their difference is \(\operatorname{diag}(3/5,-3/5)\), and the whole affine segment is feasible by convexity.

But the coordinate rectangle also contains
\[
K_{\mathrm{low}}=
\begin{pmatrix}1/5&3/10\\3/10&1/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{high}}=
\begin{pmatrix}4/5&3/10\\3/10&4/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The first has determinant \(-1/20\); the second has
\(\det(I-K_{\mathrm{high}})=-1/20\). Neither is a positive contraction. Thus a feasible mixed-sign segment does not justify treating all coordinate corners as probability kernels.
