# Agent 029: complex tangent spectrum and a quantitative determinant bound

Date: 2026-09-14. Actual worker 029. Bounded task: restrictions on all complex
tangent eigenvalues at a fixed point of a four-type strictly non-Volterra QSO.

This report contains **two focused claims**: a spectral region, with its local
averaging consequence, and a quantitative lower bound for the fixed-point
determinant. The first uses a conditional-variance argument in addition to the
previous real-eigenvalue bound. The second uses only the resulting spectral
restrictions and the tangent trace. Neither bound is claimed optimal or novel
in the literature. No children, GitHub writes, shared manuscript changes, or

## 1. Exact statements and ambient-versus-tangent distinction

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\sum_i x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad\text{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1}
\]
All allowed coefficients may vanish. Let \(p\in\Delta_3\) be any fixed point,
and define
\[
A_{kj}=\sum_iP_{ij,k}p_i,\qquad J=DV(p)=2A,\qquad
E=\{z\in\mathbb R^4:\sum_i z_i=0\},\qquad J_E=J|_E.
\tag{2}
\]
Spectra below are taken over \(\mathbb C\), with algebraic multiplicity.
Put
\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\quad k=2c=3-\sqrt5,\quad L=\sqrt2,
\quad a=1-k=\sqrt5-2,\quad b=1+\sqrt2.
\tag{3}
\]

The ambient matrix \(A\) is column-stochastic with zero diagonal and \(Ap=p\).
The ambient matrix \(J\) has column sum **2**, zero diagonal, and eigenvector
\(p\) with eigenvalue **2**. Since \(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}p=1\),
\[
\mathbb C^4=\mathbb C p\oplus E_{\mathbb C}
\]
is an invariant direct sum. Consequently
\[
\sigma(J)=\{2\}\mathbin{\uplus}\sigma(J_E),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}J_E=-2.
\tag{4}
\]
The ambient eigenvalue 2 is not a simplex tangent multiplier. In particular,
neither an ambient stochastic-matrix estimate nor the zero ambient trace can
be inserted unchanged into the three-dimensional restriction.

**Claim 1 — all-complex spectral region and local averaging.** Every tangent
eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\boxed{|\lambda|<\sqrt2.}
\tag{5}
\]
Every real tangent eigenvalue lies in
\[
\boxed{-\sqrt2<\lambda\le3-\sqrt5.}
\tag{6}
\]
If the tangent spectrum has a nonreal pair \(\alpha\pm i\beta\), \(\beta\ne0\),
then its remaining real eigenvalue \(r\) obeys
\[
r=-2-2\alpha,\qquad -\sqrt2<r\le3-\sqrt5,
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sqrt5-5}{2}\le\alpha<-1+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}<0,
\qquad \alpha^2+\beta^2<2.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus (7) is substantially smaller than the stochastic radius-2 disk or the
half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\le3-\sqrt5\).

For the averaged map
\[
V_\eta=(1-\eta)I+\eta V,
\]
every fixed point is locally exponentially asymptotically stable relative to
the affine hyperplane \(\sum x_i=1\), and therefore relative to the simplex,
whenever
\[
\boxed{
0<\eta\le\eta_0:=\frac{4-\sqrt2}{5-\sqrt2}
=\frac{18-\sqrt2}{23}
\approx0.7211211494620393.
}
\tag{8}
\]
This is a sufficient local range, including its upper endpoint. No assertion
that this is the largest valid universal range is made. No conclusion about
global convergence for \(\eta>1/2\) follows from this spectral argument.

**Claim 2 — determinant bounded away from zero.** At every fixed point,
\[
\boxed{
\det(I-J_E)>
D:=(\sqrt5-2)(1+\sqrt2)(6-\sqrt5-\sqrt2)
\approx1.3391480505809678>\frac43.
}
\tag{9}
\]
The strict lower bound is proved; optimality is not. Equivalently, the
previously archived identity
\(\det(I-J_E)=3+4e_3(A)-12\det A\) has the stronger lower bound (9),
where \(e_3\) is the third elementary symmetric function of the four ambient
eigenvalues of \(A\). Formula (9) concerns \(\det(I-J_E)\), whereas
\(\det(I-J)=-\det(I-J_E)\).

## 2. Fixed-point support and the previously established real bound

Strict exclusion gives
\[
p_i=V_i(p)\le(1-p_i)^2,
\]
so \(p_i\le c<1/2\). Therefore the support
\(S=\{i:p_i>0\}\) has at least three elements. It has either three or four
elements; all boundary fixed points in the present four-type problem are thus
covered by the three-element case.

For completeness, the real bound in (6) is rederived from the transfer lemma
of `agents/006.md`. Suppose \(\lambda>0\) is a real tangent eigenvalue and take
a nonzero real eigenvector \(z\in E\). If its only positive coordinate were
\(z_i\), nonnegativity and \(J_{ii}=0\) would give \((Jz)_i\le0\), contrary to
\(\lambda z_i>0\). Applying the same argument to \(-z\) excludes exactly one
negative coordinate. In four coordinates, \(z\) therefore has two positive
and two negative coordinates, with no zero coordinates.

Let \(G\) and \(H\) be its positive and negative pairs. For \(i\in G,j\in H\),
let \(k'\) and \(\ell\) be the other members of \(G\) and \(H\), and write
\(q_{uv}=\sum_{s\in G}P_{uv,s}\). Symmetry, stochasticity, and exclusion give
\(q_{k'i}=0\), \(q_{\ell j}=1\), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(e_i-e_j)
&=p_i(q_{ii}-q_{ij})+p_j(q_{ij}-q_{jj})
-p_{k'}q_{k'j}+p_\ell(q_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le p_i(1-q_{ij})+p_jq_{ij}\le c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Put \(h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=-\sum_{j\in H}z_j>0\). Then
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}(e_i-e_j),
\qquad
\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}=1.
\]
Using \(J=2A\) and (10) gives \(\lambda h\le2ch\), hence \(\lambda\le k\).
Every nonpositive real eigenvalue also satisfies this upper bound. This part
is an existing input, not a new claim of worker 029.

## 3. Independent-parent variance bound and strictness

The following elementary argument applies on the positive support of an
arbitrary symmetric QSO fixed point. The special four-type exclusion rule is
needed below to control the possible missing coordinates.

First suppose all coordinates of \(p\) are positive. On \(\{1,2,3,4\}\), take
independent parents \(X,Y\) with law \(p\), and conditional offspring
\[
\mathbb P(Z=k\mid X=i,Y=j)=P_{ij,k}.
\]
Because \(Vp=p\), the law of \(Z\) is also \(p\). Let \(f\) be any complex
function with \(\mathbb E_p f=0\), and put
\[
g=f(Z),\qquad h=A^{\mathsf T}f.
\]
Parent symmetry gives both conditional expectations
\[
\mathbb E[g\mid X]=h(X),\qquad \mathbb E[g\mid Y]=h(Y).
\tag{11}
\]
Moreover \(\mathbb E_p h=0\), since \(Ap=p\). Independence implies that
\(h(X)\) and \(h(Y)\) are orthogonal in the complex Hilbert space \(L^2\).
Each conditional-expectation residual is orthogonal to the corresponding
parent function. Expanding the square, with complex conjugates in inner
products, therefore gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\big|g-h(X)-h(Y)\big|^2
=\|f\|_{L^2(p)}^2-2\|A^{\mathsf T}f\|_{L^2(p)}^2\ge0.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus on the centered subspace
\(H_p=\{f:\sum_i p_if_i=0\}\),
\[
\|A^{\mathsf T}|_{H_p}\|_{L^2(p)\to L^2(p)}\le1/\sqrt2.
\tag{13}
\]
Both constants and \(H_p\) are invariant under \(A^{\mathsf T}\), and they
form a direct sum. The constant eigenvalue is 1, and (13) excludes 1 from
the centered block. Hence every other ambient eigenvalue \(\zeta\) of
\(A\) satisfies \(|\zeta|\le1/\sqrt2\), with multiplicities accounted for by
this invariant decomposition.

For a finite positive support, equality cannot hold for an eigenvalue.
Suppose \(A^{\mathsf T}f=\zeta f\) with nonzero centered \(f\) and
\(|\zeta|=1/\sqrt2\). Equality in (12) forces
\[
f(Z)=\zeta(f(X)+f(Y))\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{14}
\]
Choose \(i\) with \(|f_i|=M=\max_j|f_j|>0\). The diagonal-parent event
\(X=Y=i\) has positive probability \(p_i^2\). At least one possible offspring
on this event has positive conditional probability, and every possible
offspring lies in the support. Equation (14) would then require
\[
|f(Z)|=2|\zeta|M=\sqrt2 M>M,
\]
contradicting maximality of \(M\). Therefore all these eigenvalues satisfy the
strict inequality \(|\zeta|<1/\sqrt2\). Multiplying by 2 and using (4) proves
(5) for interior fixed points.

Now suppose \(|S|=3\), with missing index \(r\). Since
\[
0=p_r=V_r(p)=\sum_{i,j\in S}P_{ij,r}p_ip_j
\]
is a sum of nonnegative terms with all \(p_ip_j>0\), one has
\(P_{ij,r}=0\) for every \(i,j\in S\). After ordering the missing coordinate
last, the matrix consequently has the block form
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}B&u\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{15}
\]
The lower right entry is zero by strict exclusion. The matrix \(B\) is the
column-stochastic derivative-half matrix of the restricted three-type QSO,
with strictly positive stationary vector \(p_S\). The same argument (11)--(14)
applies to \(B\). Its two non-Perron eigenvalues have modulus less than
\(1/\sqrt2\), and the fourth ambient eigenvalue in (15) is zero. Removing
the ambient eigenvalue 2 from \(J=2A\) proves (5) in the boundary case as well.

This proof does not apply a degenerate weighted norm on a zero-mass coordinate.
Nor does it assume that all tangent directions at a boundary point are
two-sided feasible simplex directions: the full linear tangent spectrum is
obtained from the block matrix (15).

Finally, if the tangent cubic has the pair \(\alpha\pm i\beta\), its third
eigenvalue is real and (4) gives \(r+2\alpha=-2\). Equations (5) and (6) now
give (7). In particular, the suggested weaker conclusion \(\alpha\le0\)
already follows from the elementary ambient stochastic bound \(r\ge-2\);
the variance argument strengthens it to the strictly negative bound in (7).

## 4. Consequence for averaged local stability, including the endpoint

The tangent multipliers of \(V_\eta\) are
\[
\mu=1-\eta+\eta\lambda.
\tag{16}
\]
For a real eigenvalue, (6) gives
\[
1-\eta(1+\sqrt2)<\mu\le1-\eta(\sqrt5-2)<1.
\]
These multipliers are strictly inside the unit interval provided
\(0<\eta\le\eta_0\), because
\[
\eta_0<\frac{2}{1+\sqrt2}=2(\sqrt2-1).
\tag{17}
\]

For a nonreal eigenvalue, put \(\alpha_0=-1+\sqrt2/2\). For
\(0<\eta\le\eta_0<1\), equations (5) and (7) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mu|^2
&=(1-\eta)^2+2\eta(1-\eta)\alpha+\eta^2|\lambda|^2\\
&<(1-\eta)^2+2\eta(1-\eta)\alpha_0+2\eta^2\\
&=1-(4-\sqrt2)\eta+(5-\sqrt2)\eta^2\le1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The first strict inequality remains strict at \(\eta=\eta_0\). Thus every
tangent multiplier is strictly inside the complex unit disk for the full
closed upper-endpoint range in (8).

For clarity about the dynamical inference: a finite-dimensional linear map
with spectral radius less than 1 admits an equivalent norm with operator
norm less than 1. The polynomial map \(V_\eta\), restricted to the invariant
affine hyperplane \(\sum_i x_i=1\), then has derivative norm less than 1 in a
sufficiently small neighborhood of \(p\). This proves local exponential
asymptotic stability by the mean-value inequality, including boundary fixed
points when restricted back to the simplex. This argument does not require
diagonalizability and does not use fixed-point uniqueness as a stability
criterion.

The ambient derivative of \(V_\eta\) has the additional radial eigenvalue
\(1+\eta>1\). There is no claim of stability in all of \(\mathbb R^4\).

## 5. Proof of the quantitative determinant bound

All real tangent eigenvalues are less than 1, and every nonreal pair
contributes a positive squared modulus to \(\det(I-J_E)\). There are two
cases.

### 5.1. Three real eigenvalues

Let \(t_i=1-\lambda_i\). Equations (4)--(6) imply
\[
a\le t_i<b,\qquad t_1+t_2+t_3=5,\qquad
\det(I-J_E)=t_1t_2t_3.
\tag{20}
\]
Consider the closed polytope obtained by replacing \(t_i<b\) with
\(t_i\le b\). Its positive product is minimized at a vertex: equivalently,
\(\sum_i\log t_i\) is strictly concave, so a nonvertex point cannot be a
minimum. Every vertex has two coordinates at interval endpoints. The
possibilities with two lower endpoints or two upper endpoints are infeasible,
because
\[
2a+b<5<a+2b.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the vertices are exactly the permutations of
\[
(a,b,5-a-b)=(\sqrt5-2,1+\sqrt2,6-\sqrt5-\sqrt2).
\]
Their product is \(D\). Every such vertex has a coordinate equal to \(b\),
which is excluded by the strict spectral bound in (20). Therefore the
actual determinant is strictly larger than \(D\).

### 5.2. One real eigenvalue and a nonreal pair

Let the real eigenvalue be \(r\). By the trace identity,
\(\alpha=-1-r/2\), and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(I-J_E)
&=(1-r)\big((1-\alpha)^2+\beta^2\big)\\
&>(1-r)\frac{(r+4)^2}{4}=:f(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(1-r>0\) and \(\beta^2>0\). On \([-\sqrt2,k]\),
\[
f'(r)=-\frac34(r+4)(r+2)<0.
\]
Since \(-\sqrt2<r\le k\), one gets
\[
f(r)\ge f(k)=\frac{41\sqrt5-89}{2}.
\]
Writing \(a=1-k\) and \(b=1+\sqrt2\) as in (3), the exact identity
\[
f(k)-D
=a\left(b-\frac{5-a}{2}\right)^2\ge0
\tag{23}
\]
proves the same strict bound in this case.

Finally,
\[
D=6\sqrt5+7\sqrt{10}-15\sqrt2-13.
\]
For a short rational certificate of \(D>4/3\), use
\(2.236<\sqrt5<2.237\) and \(1.414<\sqrt2<1.415\), verified simply by
squaring their positive rational endpoints. The factored form of \(D\) gives
\[
D>
\frac{59}{250}\frac{1207}{500}\frac{587}{250}
=\frac{41802031}{31250000}>\frac43.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (20)--(24) prove Claim 2 in all cases, including repeated real
eigenvalues, boundary fixed points, and nondiagonalizable tangent matrices.
