## Result and limits

**PROVED by independent hand audit:** entropy of a two-site Hermitian determinantal law is concave on the entire closed set of positive contractions. The proposed definite-direction proof is valid. In the strict interior it gives the sharper bound

\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K+tD})\bigg|_{t=0}\le -8\det D
\qquad(\det D>0).
\tag{1}
\]

The constant 8 is sharp. Equality in (1), for a nonzero definite direction, holds exactly when \(K=I_2/2\) and \(D\) is a real scalar multiple of \(I_2\).

**PROVED:** for arbitrary finite \(n\), entropy is concave on any convex slice in which all matrix entries outside one fixed two-coordinate principal block are fixed. In particular, if \(K_1-K_0\) is supported on that block, then Lyons's concavity inequality holds for this pair. If \(d=(K_1-K_0)_{UU}\) has positive determinant, the stronger chord inequality is

\[
H(P_{(1-t)K_0+tK_1})\ge(1-t)H(P_{K_0})+tH(P_{K_1})
       +4\det(d)\,t(1-t).
\tag{2}
\]

These are restricted results. They do **not** establish full Lyons concavity, or concavity along every rank-two direction in arbitrary coordinates. DPP entropy is not invariant under arbitrary unitary change of the coordinate basis. No novelty or exhaustive current-status clearance is claimed.

## 1. Exact two-site identity, with complex entries

Write

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\overline c&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\overline v&s\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<K<I.
\]

Set the four exact atom probabilities to

\[
x=p_{00}=(1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2,\quad
y=p_{10}=a(1-b)+|c|^2,\quad
z=p_{01}=(1-a)b+|c|^2,\quad
w=p_{11}=ab-|c|^2.
\]

All are strictly positive. Direct expansion gives

\[
yz-xw=|c|^2\ge0,\qquad
p''=2\det(D)(1,-1,-1,1).
\]

Both identities hold for arbitrary complex \(c,v\): the relevant derivatives of \(|c+tv|^2\) are \(2\operatorname{Re}(\overline c v)\) and \(2|v|^2\). Define

\[
F=\frac{(x')^2}{x}+\frac{(y')^2}{y}+\frac{(z')^2}{z}+\frac{(w')^2}{w},
\qquad L=\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0.
\]

Differentiating entropy and using \(\sum p'=\sum p''=0\) gives the exact identity

\[
H''=-F+2\det(D)L. \tag{3}
\]

Thus \(\det D\le0\) implies \(H''\le0\) immediately, including the rank-zero, rank-one, and indefinite cases. There is no omitted complex phase term.

## 2. Positive determinant: the complete endpoint-Fisher argument

If \(\delta=\det D>0\), a Hermitian two-by-two matrix is either positive or negative definite. Negating \(D\) preserves its second variation, so suppose \(D>0\). Initially normalize \(\det D=1\); homogeneity restores the factor \(\delta\) at the end.

Let

\[
m=y+z,\qquad T=\frac{m}{2\sqrt{xw}},\qquad
A=\frac{\operatorname{adj}K}{\sqrt w},\qquad
B=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-K)}{\sqrt x},\qquad C=A+B.
\]

The matrices \(A,B,C\) are positive definite Hermitian and \(\det A=\det B=1\). Also

\[
m\ge2\sqrt{yz}\ge2\sqrt{xw},\quad T\ge1,
\qquad L\le\log\frac{m^2}{4xw}=2\log T. \tag{4}
\]

The determinant derivatives are

\[
w'=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}K\,D),\qquad
x'=-\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(I-K)\,D).
\]

They are real. Therefore the endpoint-only Fisher expression satisfies

\[
F\ge F_{\rm end}:=\frac{(w')^2}{w}+\frac{(x')^2}{x}
=(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2
\ge\frac12(\operatorname{tr}CD)^2
\ge2\det C. \tag{5}
\]

The last inequality applies the scalar arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the two positive eigenvalues of \(C^{1/2}DC^{1/2}\). Its determinant is \(\det C\), and its trace is \(\operatorname{tr}CD\). In particular, no commutativity of \(C\) and \(D\) is being assumed.

For any two-by-two matrices, \(\det(A+B)=\det A+\det B+\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}A\,B)\). Since \(\operatorname{adj}(\operatorname{adj}K)=K\),

\[
\det C
=2+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}(I-K))}{\sqrt{xw}}
=2+\frac{a(1-b)+b(1-a)+2|c|^2}{\sqrt{xw}}
=2+2T. \tag{6}
\]

Combining (3)--(6) and then restoring \(\delta\) gives

\[
H''\le-4\delta(1+T)+4\delta\log T
     =-4\delta(1+T-\log T)\le-8\delta,
\tag{7}
\]

because \(T-\log T\ge1\) for \(T\ge1\). This settles every remaining definite direction. Each inequality is valid at every strict interior point of its feasible line.

### Exact minimizer of the endpoint expression

The exact, unique minimizer of \(F_{\rm end}\) over \(D>0\), \(\det D=1\), is

\[
D_* =\sqrt{\det C}\,C^{-1}
     =\frac{K/\sqrt w+(I-K)/\sqrt x}{\sqrt{2+m/\sqrt{xw}}},
\qquad \min F_{\rm end}=2\det C=4(1+T). \tag{8}
\]

To check attainment in the first inequality of (5), note that
\(\operatorname{tr}(A\operatorname{adj}C)=\operatorname{tr}(B\operatorname{adj}C)=\det C\).
Indeed, \(\operatorname{adj}C=\operatorname{adj}A+\operatorname{adj}B\), both separate determinants are one, and the two mixed traces agree. Hence
\(\operatorname{tr}(AC^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(BC^{-1})=1\), so both traces at \(D_*\) equal \(\sqrt{\det C}\). Uniqueness follows from equality in the eigenvalue arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. This verifies the **endpoint-only** minimizer. No assertion about the exact minimum of the full four-atom Fisher expression follows merely by dropping its two middle terms, and no full-Fisher minimizer is claimed here.

### Equality in the sharp curvature bound

For equality in (7), necessarily \(T=1\). Equality in both inequalities defining \(T\ge1\) implies \(y=z\), \(yz=xw\), and hence \(c=0\), \(a=b\); thus \(K=aI\). Then \(A=B=I\), and equality in (5) requires the normalized positive direction \(D=I\). The omitted middle terms vanish exactly when \(y'=z'=1-2a=0\). Thus \(a=1/2\). Undoing normalization and sign gives precisely \(K=I/2\), \(D=dI\), \(d\ne0\). Conversely these matrices attain \(H''=-8d^2=-8\det D\).

## 3. Two-site boundary extension

Entropy is continuous in \(K\) on the closed contraction set: each of its finitely many atom probabilities is polynomial in the entries, and \(-r\log r\) extends continuously to zero. For any feasible segment \(K(t)=(1-t)K_0+tK_1\), regularize it by

\[
K_\varepsilon(t)=(1-2\varepsilon)K(t)+\varepsilon I,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\]

This whole segment is strict interior. Its direction is \((1-2\varepsilon)(K_1-K_0)\), whose determinant has the same sign as the original direction. The preceding Hessian proof implies concavity of the regularized entropy, and continuity passes the chord inequality to the original segment. When \(\det(K_1-K_0)>0\), integrating (7) first gives the stronger regularized chord bound with correction \(4(1-2\varepsilon)^2\det(K_1-K_0)t(1-t)\); its limit is the two-site instance of (2).

This handles singular kernels, eigenvalues equal to one, zero atom probabilities, and segments lying entirely in a boundary face. The proof does not assert that a finite classical Hessian exists at such boundary points.

## 4. Exact conditioning on an outside atom

The following formula holds for any size of the retained block, although only a block of size two is needed here. Fix \(U\subset E\), put \(O=E\setminus U\), and condition on the exact outside atom \(X\cap O=Y\), where \(Y\subset O\) and \(Z=O\setminus Y\). Let \(P_Z\) denote the diagonal projection onto \(Z\), acting on the outside coordinates. Define

\[
Q_Y=K_{OO}-P_Z,
\qquad q_Y=P_K(X\cap O=Y)=(-1)^{|Z|}\det Q_Y. \tag{9}
\]

For each \(q_Y>0\), the conditional law on \(U\) is determinantal with Hermitian kernel

\[
\boxed{\quad C_Y=K_{UU}-K_{UO}\,(K_{OO}-P_Z)^{-1}K_{OU}.\quad} \tag{10}
\]

The sign in (9) is essential, while no additional sign appears in (10).

### Direct proof of the formula

For every \(W\subset U\), inclusion-exclusion gives

\[
P_K(W\subset X,\ X\cap O=Y)
=\sum_{A\subset Z}(-1)^{|A|}\det K_{W\cup Y\cup A}
=(-1)^{|Z|}\det\begin{pmatrix}
K_{WW}&K_{WO}\\K_{OW}&Q_Y
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The last equality is the determinant expansion in the diagonal shifts on \(Z\). Since \(q_Y>0\), \(Q_Y\) is invertible. Taking its Schur complement gives

\[
P_K(W\subset X,\ X\cap O=Y)=q_Y\det(C_Y)_{WW}.
\]

Division by \(q_Y\) proves every conditional inclusion probability, so it proves the claimed conditional DPP law. Formula (9) is the same calculation with \(W=\varnothing\).

### Positive-contraction and degenerate cases

For \(0<K<I\), every outside atom has positive weight. One way to verify directly that \(0<C_Y<I\) is to condition first on inclusion of \(Y\). With \(V=U\cup Z\), put

\[
R=K_{VV}-K_{VY}K_{YY}^{-1}K_{YV}.
\]

Schur complementation gives \(R>0\); also \(R\le K_{VV}<I\). Conditioning next on exclusion of \(Z\) gives

\[
C_Y=R_{UU}+R_{UZ}(I_Z-R_{ZZ})^{-1}R_{ZU}.
\tag{11}
\]

This is positive definite, and \(I_U-C_Y\) is the Schur complement of \(I_Z-R_{ZZ}\) in \(I-R>0\), hence is positive definite. Block elimination in \(Q_Y\) identifies (11) with (10). Empty included/excluded sets use the ordinary empty-matrix conventions.

For an arbitrary closed contraction and an atom with \(q_Y>0\), formula (10) remains well-defined and the preceding inclusion-exclusion proof is unchanged. Regularizing \(K\) proves \(0\le C_Y\le I\): since \(Q_Y\) is invertible, its inverse and the conditional kernel vary continuously under regularization. Atoms with \(q_Y=0\) are omitted; their conditional kernels need not be defined. Thus no inverse of a zero-probability conditioning event is used.

## 5. Affine conditional kernels, fixed weights, and entropy

Assume now \(|U|=2\), and that along a feasible segment only \(K_{UU}(t)\) varies:

\[
K_{UU}(t)=K_{UU}(0)+td,\qquad
K_{UO},\ K_{OU},\ K_{OO}\ \text{are fixed}.
\]

The outside law is \(P_{K_{OO}}\), so (9) shows that every \(q_Y\) is independent of \(t\). For each atom with positive weight, (10) gives

\[
C_Y(t)=C_Y(0)+td. \tag{12}
\]

In particular, the derivative is the same matrix \(d\) for every outside atom, with no weight-dependent rescaling. Each \(C_Y(t)\) is a positive contraction throughout the original feasible segment.

The exact Shannon chain rule now reads

\[
H(P_{K(t)})=H((q_Y)_{Y\subset O})+
                \sum_{Y:q_Y>0}q_Y H(P_{C_Y(t)}). \tag{13}
\]

The first term is constant; all weights are constant and nonnegative. Two-site concavity applied to every affine conditional kernel (12), followed by (13), proves concavity of the full entropy on this entire fixed-block slice. This argument works directly on the boundary by omitting zero weights and using the proved boundary version of the two-site theorem.

For a strict interior base and \(\det d>0\), differentiating (13) and applying (1) yields

\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K(t)})\le\sum_Yq_Y(-8\det d)=-8\det d.
\tag{14}
\]

Applying the strong two-site chord inequality conditionally gives (2) for all closed-contraction endpoints, since \(\sum q_Y=1\). Sharpness persists in every dimension: take \(K=I_2/2\oplus K_O\), \(d=aI_2\), and any strict outside contraction \(K_O\). The outside is independent and contributes only a constant entropy.
