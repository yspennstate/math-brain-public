## New self-contained consequence: the optimal capped step converges globally at the same geometric rate

Assume the four-type strictly non-Volterra QSO hypotheses in agents/006.md. Write

\[
c=(3-\sqrt5)/2,\qquad T=(1-c)I+cV,\qquad L=1-c+2c^2=(13-5\sqrt5)/2<1.
\]

The capped transfer argument in QSO_EXTENSION.md proves, in fact for every cap q in[c,1],

\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\le \max(1-c+2cq,2c)\|x-y\|_1
=(1-c+2cq)\|x-y\|_1
\tag{1}
\]

whenever all coordinates of x,y are at most q. The equality uses q≥c and L>2c. The combinatorial proof of this estimate does not require q<1/2; that restriction is needed only to make its factor strictly below one.

Define q_0=1 and q_{n+1}=f(q_n), where f(s)=(1-c)s+c(1-s)^2. Since f is convex, f(0)=c, and f(q)≥c for q≥c, the coordinate estimate V_i(x)≤(1-x_i)^2 implies T(K_q)⊆K_{f(q)} whenever q≥c. Here K_q is the capped simplex, and q=1 gives the entire simplex. Algebra using c^2=3c−1 gives

\[
f(q)-c=(q-c)[1-c(3-q-c)].
\]

For c≤q≤1, the bracket lies in[c^2,c]. Thus c≤q_{n+1}≤q_n and

\[
0\le q_n-c\le(1-c)c^n.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, for arbitrary x,y in the entire simplex, iteration of(1) and(2) yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T^n x-T^n y\|_1
&\le L^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{2c(1-c)}L c^j\right)\|x-y\|_1\\
&\le \exp(2c/L)L^n\|x-y\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

The last bound uses log(1+u)≤u and sums the geometric series. Taking y to be the unique fixed point established in006 proves global geometric convergence with the same asymptotic factor L as the optimal fixed-cap proof. No finite entry into K_c is required. In fact the deterministic envelope stays above c for every finite n when q_0>c.

This is a direct new synthesis of the parent’s capped-contraction extension, not a worldwide novelty assertion. It neither proves convergence of V itself nor claims that L is the actual asymptotic rate of every individual operator. The global finite prefactor is sufficient and not asserted optimal.

## All-dimensional scaled-cross-matrix entropy slice

This coordinator synthesis extends036. The completed independent proof/audit in045 passes, and the parent independently read and accepted its finite-chord strengthening. Assume the fully proved two-site theorem from016/018. Fix an outside Hermitian contraction C on any finite number of sites and a complex2-by-r matrix B. Consider
\[
K(A,z)=\begin{pmatrix}A&zB\\\bar z B^*&C\end{pmatrix},
\quad A=A^*\in\mathbb C^{2\times2},\quad z\in\mathbb C,
\quad0\le K(A,z)\le I.
\]
Then H(P_K) is jointly concave in the four real entries of A and the two real entries of z. The domain is convex because the displayed block matrix depends real-affinely on these six variables.

Here is a full interior proof. For an outside atom Y let q_Y=p_Y(C), let P_{Y^c} denote the diagonal projector onto absent outside sites, and put
\[
R_Y=(C-P_{Y^c})^{-1},\qquad D_Y=B R_YB^*.
\]
All q_Y are positive for0<C<I. The usual conditional-DPP Schur formula gives the inside conditional kernel
\[
C_Y(A,z)=A-|z|^2D_Y.
\]
The q_Y are fixed during the whole slice. Moreover
\[
\sum_Yq_YR_Y=0,\qquad\sum_Yq_YD_Y=0.\tag{4}
\]
To justify the first identity, write q_Y=(-1)^{|Y^c|}det(C-P_{Y^c}) and differentiate the exact identity sum_Y q_Y=1 in every Hermitian direction E. Jacobi's formula gives sum_Y q_Y tr(R_Y E)=0. Nondegeneracy of the real Hermitian trace pairing proves(4).

At a fixed feasible interior A define
\[
F_A(s)=\sum_Yq_Y H_2(A-sD_Y),\qquad0\le s\le |z|^2.
\]
Every kernel in this interval is a valid strict conditional kernel: the block Schur complements for K(A,z) stay positive as |z|^2 is decreased. Two-site concavity makes F_A concave, and(4) gives F_A'(0)=0. Consequently F_A'(s)≤0 on this interval.

Along an arbitrary affine line A(t),z(t), differentiation of the entropy chain rule gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K(A(t),z(t))})
=\sum_Yq_Y D^2H_2(C_Y)[A'-2\operatorname{Re}(\bar z z')D_Y,\ A'-2\operatorname{Re}(\bar z z')D_Y]
+2|z'|^2F_A'(|z|^2)\le0.\tag{5}
\]
The first summands are nonpositive by the two-site theorem; the last term has the sign just proved. Integration proves the chord inequality. Boundary endpoints follow by regularizing both kernels with K_epsilon=(1-2epsilon)K+epsilon I, which preserves a common outside block and a common cross-matrix direction, then taking epsilon down to zero.

No order sign for individual D_Y or matrix-gradient difference is asserted; D_Y may be indefinite. Their weighted cancellation is exactly what the argument needs. The same proof is a conditional lifting principle for any inside size k if full k-site entropy concavity is already known. Here only k=2 is used unconditionally. It does not imply general n-site concavity because arbitrary cross-matrix variations need not be complex scalar multiples of one fixed B. No novelty claim is made.

## Three-site gauge-commutator curvature lemma

This is a coordinator derivation independently checked by053. 053 completed and froze its full proof, strictness extension, and affine-feasible boundary analysis before this report's final delivery. Let0<K<I on three sites and A=i[D,K], where D is real diagonal. For every principal set S, A_S=i[D_S,K_S], and therefore tr(K_S^{-1}A_S)=0 by cyclicity. Jacobi's formula yields
\[
D^2\det K_S[A_S,A_S]
=-\det K_S\,\operatorname{tr}[(K_S^{-1}A_S)^2]\le0.\tag{6}
\]
The last trace is nonnegative because K_S^{-1}A_S is similar to the Hermitian matrix K_S^{-1/2}A_SK_S^{-1/2}.

For a supermodular function f on the three-site Boolean lattice, write c_S for its Möbius coefficients. Its nonlinear coefficients satisfy c_{ij}≥0 and c_{ij}+c_{123}≥0. If c_{123}≥0, all nonlinear coefficients are nonnegative, and the identity E_K f=sum_S c_S det K_S combines with(6) to give D^2E_Kf[A,A]≤0. If c_{123}<0, replace K by I−K and f(S) by f(S^c). The new pair coefficients are c_{ij}+c_{123}≥0 and the new triple coefficient is−c_{123}>0. The transformed direction is−A=i[D,I−K], so the same proof applies. Modular coefficients contribute zero because A has zero diagonal.

At an interior entropy base, freeze f(S)=−log p_S(K), which is supermodular. Every atom first derivative in a gauge-commutator direction is zero, since diagonal-unitary conjugation leaves the entire subset law invariant. Thus the Fisher term vanishes and the actual entropy Hessian equals D^2E_K f[A,A]≤0. This controls a genuine full-rank class: in three dimensions a complex cycle phase can make det(i[D,K]) nonzero. It does not cover arbitrary Hermitian directions, and regularization proves only the corresponding limiting directional statements at the boundary, not an invented open-neighborhood theorem. No novelty claim is made.

## Boundary-specific parental-leakage uniqueness up to one half

This coordinator derivation is assigned for independent checking in060. Let the total probability of offspring belonging to either parent be at most delta, counting a repeated parent only once.

First, every three-type QSO with delta≤1/2 has exactly one fixed point. Existence is Brouwer. If two fixed points differ, reverse their order if necessary so that h=x−y has exactly one positive coordinate i. Write m=(x+y)/2 and J=DV(m), so h=Jh by quadratic polarization. All entries of J are nonnegative, and
\[
J_{ii}=2\sum_a P_{ia,i}m_a\le2\delta\le1.
\]
Thus h_i=J_{ii}h_i+sum_{j≠i}J_{ij}h_j≤h_i, with strict inequality unless J_{ii}=1 and all terms at negative h_j vanish. The only remaining case has delta=1/2. Equality in the convex average for J_{ii} forces P_{ij,i}=1/2 for every j with m_j>0. Choose j with h_j<0; then m_j>0 and m_i>0, so J_{ij}≥2P_{ij,i}m_i=m_i>0, contradiction. This also handles zero state coordinates and needs no irreducibility assumption.

Now suppose a four-type QSO with delta≤1/2 has a boundary fixed point p. Any fixed point supported on one or two types would force total parental offspring probability one on a positive parent pair, impossible for delta<1. Thus p has support S of size three. Positivity at p forces P_{ab,r}=0 for every a,b∈S, where r is the missing type. For any other fixed point x,
\[
x_r=V_r(x)\le\delta\{2x_r(1-x_r)+x_r^2\}
=\delta x_r(2-x_r).
\]
If x_r>0, dividing gives1≤delta(2−x_r)<1, a contradiction. Therefore every fixed point lies in the same three-type face. Its restricted coefficient array still has leakage at most delta, and the preceding three-type theorem gives uniqueness. This proves a boundary-specific four-type theorem through delta=1/2; it does not extend the uniform all-interior uniqueness threshold to one half.

An initially tempting shortcut must be avoided: two distinct fixed points may lie on the same support-three face, so counting intersections of distinct faces alone is insufficient. The separate three-type singleton-sign argument is what closes that case.
