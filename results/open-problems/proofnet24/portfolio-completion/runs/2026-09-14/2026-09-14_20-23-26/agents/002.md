## 2. C0033: exact statement, current gap, and a proved directional reduction

For a finite ground set E and Hermitian positive contraction K, let P_K be the determinantal law characterized by P(A⊆X)=det K_A. The conjecture says that Shannon entropy H(P_K) is concave as a function of K. The statement appears as Conjecture 2.6 in [Lyons's 2014 survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707). Finite-kernel concavity implies stationary entropy-rate concavity by restriction and limiting; the converse has not been established in the inspected material.

Repository sources read:

- `probability_1000_intake_claude_20260907/problems.jsonl` and `attempts.jsonl`, C0033-A001/A002.
- `probability_1000/prob1000_lean_20260908/REPORT_five_attacked_2026-09-08.md`, section 2.
- The later `c0033L.py`, `c0033m.py`, and `probability_1000_attempts_I_20260914/c0033_test.py`.

The last file is a midpoint search in dimensions 2–5, not a proof. Its atom formula is the determinant with each absent coordinate's column replaced by the corresponding column of I−K; the latest script tests real symmetric kernels, not arbitrary complex Hermitian kernels. The stored old trial counts were not rerun here and are not independent evidence from this run.

### Existing partial results to credit

[Yuzhou Gu's project report](https://sevenkplus.com/data/dpp.pdf) states cardinality-entropy concavity (Theorem 1), the zero-endpoint inequality (Corollary 6), and full entropy concavity along rank-one directions (Theorem 7). The rank-one result also has the immediate explanation that every atom probability is affine under a rank-one kernel perturbation, so ordinary probability-simplex entropy concavity applies. These are existing results, not a new solution to Lyons. Gu's PDF is a project report; no refereed-publication status for it is asserted here.

The bounded searches for later literature did not locate a full solution. That is not an exhaustive open-status or novelty clearance.

### The two-site Hessian identity

Write

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&s\end{pmatrix},
\]

where D is an arbitrary Hermitian direction. In the strict interior 0<K<I the four atom probabilities, in order 00,10,01,11, are

\[
p=((1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2,\quad a(1-b)+|c|^2,\quad
(1-a)b+|c|^2,\quad ab-|c|^2).
\]

Every atom is positive. Direct expansion gives

\[
p_{10}p_{01}-p_{00}p_{11}=|c|^2,
\qquad L:=\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}\ge0.
\]

Along K+tD the second atom derivatives are

\[
p''=2\det(D)(1,-1,-1,1).
\]

Since the first and second derivatives of the atom sum vanish,

\[
\boxed{\quad H''(0)=-\sum_x\frac{(p'_x)^2}{p_x}
                  +2L\det(D).\quad} \tag{C1}
\]

This identity includes the complex case: the first derivative of |c+tv|² is 2 Re(\bar c v) and its second derivative is 2|v|².

**Proved partial result.** If det(D)≤0, then H''≤0 on every interior part of the line. Thus two-site entropy is concave along every indefinite or rank-deficient Hermitian direction. Interior regularization and continuity extend the line-segment inequality to boundary contractions. Specifically replace every point K(t) by (1−2η)K(t)+ηI, whose direction is (1−2η)D and has the same determinant sign, and let η↓0.

Only positive- or negative-definite two-site directions can therefore cause failure. At a diagonal base kernel c=0, L=0 and (C1) also proves nonpositive second variation in every direction. These are finite-dimensional observations, not the full conjecture.

### Exact remaining obligation

For det(D)>0 one must prove

\[
\sum_x\frac{(p'_x)^2}{p_x}\ \ge\ 2\det(D)\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}. \tag{C2}
\]

The direction can be negated, so it suffices to consider positive-definite D. Homogeneity permits the normalization det(D)=1. This gives a concrete minimization of the Fisher quadratic form over the positive-definite determinant-one surface.

In the real coordinates (a,b,c), define g_x=∇p_x and G=Σ_x g_xg_xᵀ/p_x, and

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then −Hess H=G−LM. The repository reduces positivity to the Schur complement of its upper 2×2 block B. For c≠0, with S=Σ1/p_x and q=(G13/c,G23/c), the remaining scalar inequality is

\[
4S+2L/c^2\ge qB^{-1}q^T,
\]

**provided B>0 is first proved.** Sampling B's determinant does not establish that assumption. The recorded experiments show that dropping the logarithmic term fails near the boundary. Those experiments explain why the attempted lower bound is too weak; they do not close (C2).

The nonpositive-determinant reduction gives a cleaner next problem than another broad midpoint search: establish (C2) for definite D, with sharp boundary control. No such proof was obtained here. Passing from all two-site kernels to larger ground sets is another separate obligation.

## 3. F0059: explicit counterexample to the new record-gap question

Audited file:
`probability_1000_attempts_I_20260914/Probability_F0059_Janson_problem_18_39_reduction_2026-09-14.md`.
The older `probability_1000/prob1000_lean_20260908/ultracode_20260909/F0059/memo.md` was also read. It correctly retains moving-truncation and near-record-count gaps; its Lean result concerns only a deterministic telescoping statement.

The latest memo asks whether every finite-mean law π of generating-function radius one has infinitely many record sizes a with

\[
\Lambda(a)-\max_{1\le b\le\varepsilon a}\Lambda(b)>a^{-1/2},
\qquad\Lambda(a)=\frac{\log\pi_a+\log a}{a}, \tag{J1}
\]

for some fixed ε∈(0,1). It claims equivalence with Janson 18.39, despite stating only a sufficient conditional proposition.

**Counterexample to (J1).** For integers a≥1 set

\[
\pi_a=\frac1{4a^3},\qquad
\pi_0=1-\frac{\zeta(3)}4.
\]

This is a positive probability law, since ζ(3)<2; its mean is ν=ζ(2)/4∈(0,1), and its generating-function radius is one by the root test. For a≥1,

\[
\Lambda(a)=\frac{-\log4-2\log a}{a}.
\]

Its continuous extension has derivative (log4+2log a−2)/a², positive for a≥2. Also Λ(1)=Λ(2), so every integer a≥3 is a strict running record. For each fixed ε∈(0,1), for all sufficiently large a the maximum in (J1) occurs at b=⌊εa⌋, and

\[
\Lambda(a)-\Lambda(\lfloor\varepsilon a\rfloor)
=\frac{2(\varepsilon^{-1}-1)\log a+O_\varepsilon(1)}a
=o(a^{-1/2}).
\]

Thus (J1) eventually fails at every record, for every fixed ε. Adding the memo's constant [1−log(λ−ν)]/a to Λ does not change this conclusion.

This law has an ordinary power-law tail. Its macroscopic one-box condensation for λ>ν is covered by [Janson's Theorem 18.33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0510). It therefore disproves the alleged necessity/equivalence particularly decisively: the proposed property fails in a regime where the desired condensation conclusion is already a theorem.

The memo's equal-size-class estimate is heuristic and does not control arbitrary mixed sizes or moving local probabilities. Existence of a maximizing deterministic class alone does not give concentration around that class. I have not certified its conditional proposition independently. The original 18.39 remains open in this audit; no law violating its actual conclusion was constructed.

## 4. F0060: two independent exact defects

Audited file:
`probability_1000_attempts_I_20260914/Probability_F0060_Janson_problem_18_40_answered_no_2026-09-14.md`.

### 4.1 Factorial weights disprove the stated theorem

The file asserts that for every radius-zero weight sequence and every m/n→λ>0, some subsequence has P(Y_(1)=m)→1.

Take w_k=k! for every k≥0. The radius is zero. For m>2, the total weight of configurations with all m balls in one box is A=n m!. The disjoint class with sizes m−1 and 1 in two different boxes has weight B=n(n−1)(m−1)!. Therefore

\[
\mathbb P(Y_{(1)}=m)=\frac A{Z(m,n)}
\le\frac A{A+B}
=\frac1{1+(n-1)/m}\longrightarrow\frac\lambda{\lambda+1}<1.
\]

In the tree case m=n−1 the bound is exactly 1/2 for every n≥4. No subsequence can satisfy the asserted conclusion. This does not contradict condensation on the scale n: a giant with a bounded number of balls left outside it is compatible with Y_(1)/n→λ.

The invalid final inference is that strict record status makes comparison against all sizes in (εm,m) “immediate.” It gives neither a diverging weight ratio nor a sum bound; the class m−1 already prevents exact concentration.

### 4.2 The preceding partition-function bound also fails

The file's displayed bound (3) asserts

\[
\log Z_{\le t}(m,n)\le m\max_{1\le s\le t}F(s)+O(\sqrt m),
\quad F(s)=\frac{\log w_s+\log(s/\lambda)+1}s. \tag{J2}
\]

Set λ=1, m=n, w0=w1=w2=1 and, for k≥3, wk=exp(k²). This single fixed weight sequence has radius zero. At t=2,

\[
F(1)=1,\qquad F(2)=\tfrac12(1+\log2)<1.
\]

For n divisible by three, configurations with n/3 boxes of each size 0,1,2 are allowed and have total mass n. Their total weight is n!/((n/3)!)³. By Stirling,

\[
\log Z_{\le2}(n,n)\ge n\log3-O(\log n)>n+O(\sqrt n).
\]

This contradicts (J2). The missing factor is the multinomial assignment of distinct sizes to occupied boxes. For a type with counts r_s the multiplicity is

\[
\frac{n!}{(n-\sum_s r_s)!\prod_s r_s!},
\]

not merely the binomial choice of the occupied sites. Its extra entropy can have order n. The partition-number estimate exp(O(√m)) counts size multisets and cannot absorb those assignments.

The particular counterexample uses fixed t=2 and refutes the displayed general bound as written. It does not itself exclude a different bound restricted to diverging truncations with an explicitly controlled error. Such a replacement would require a new proof, and none is supplied by the memo. This is a failure before the overly strong final theorem, so merely replacing Y_(1)=m by Y_(1)≥εn does not repair the archived argument.

Two additional hypotheses are misstated: radius zero implies limsup wk^(1/k)=∞, not a limit along every support point; and m/n→λ does not imply that m(n) visits every large integer or any prescribed record subsequence. For example m(n)=2n skips all odd records. These defects also require correction.

Recommended ledger action: mark the archived F0060 proof invalid pending repair; retain the original problem as unresolved by this argument. Do not erase the historical report.

## 5. F0063: audit of the promising alternative

Read the complete latest file
`probability_1000_attempts_I_20260914/Probability_F0063_Janson_conjecture_20_1_level_widths_uniformly_integrable_2026-09-14.md`.

For a critical nondegenerate offspring law ξ, let T_n be the Galton–Watson tree conditioned to have n vertices, Z_j the unconditioned generation sizes, and N=1+Z1+⋯+Z_(k−1). The memo's exact truncated-tree Radon–Nikodym weight is

\[
W_n=\frac{n Z_k\,\mathbb P(S_{n-N}=n-N-Z_k)}
{(n-N)\mathbb P(S_n=n-1)}.
\]

The forest cycle lemma yields this formula. The condition n−N≥Z_k is necessary for a compatible finite tree; incompatible truncations are assigned zero weight.

Under finite positive variance, lattice local limit estimates along admissible tree sizes give

\[
W_n\le C Z_k(n/(n-N))^{3/2},
\qquad W_n\le C n^{3/2}Z_k^{-1/2}.
\]

I checked the two-region argument:

- If N≤n/2, the rth-power tail is bounded by C E[Z_k^(r+1); Z_k>K].
- If N>n/2 and n>2k, some Z_j>n/(2k). For r≥1, condition on Z_j and use E[Z_k^(r−1/2)|Z_j]≤C Z_j^(r−1/2). Multiplication by n^(3/2), followed by the threshold bound, leaves C Σ_(j<k) E[Z_j^(r+1); Z_j>n/(2k)], which vanishes.

Finite E ξ^(r+1) supplies every moment used. Thus the stated real-r≥1 proof appears mathematically sound, and lower positive powers follow when the variance is finite. This is an independent hand audit, not a formal proof check or novelty clearance.

The genuinely remaining case is 0<r<1, infinite variance, E ξ^(r+1)<∞. The missing ingredient is a useful uniform bound on the central conditioning denominator relative to numerator local probabilities, or another way to control the weighted tails without that bound. Arbitrary finite-mean laws may be highly lacunary, so a stable-domain local limit theorem alone would settle only a restricted class.

There is a small but consequential wording correction to the memo's final paragraph: bounded rth moments are **not equivalent** to uniform integrability. For k=1 the actual necessary-and-sufficient tail condition is the vanishing, as K→∞, of

\[
\sup_{n\ge2}\frac{n}{n-1}\sum_{d>K}d^{r+1}\pi_d
\frac{\mathbb P(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{\mathbb P(S_n=n-1)},
\]

where the supremum is over admissible n. The memo instead asks only for finiteness of the same whole sum, which cannot establish its asserted equivalence. A family that puts mass 1/n at n has bounded first moments but is not uniformly integrable; moment convergence needs a tail bound.
