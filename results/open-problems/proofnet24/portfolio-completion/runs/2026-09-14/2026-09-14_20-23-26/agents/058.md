## Results and exact scope

This report addresses three concrete targets.

1. **Complete positive-finite-order two-site classification.** For
   \[
   H_\alpha(K)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\sum_{S\subseteq E}P_K(S)^\alpha,
   \qquad 0<\alpha<\infty,\quad\alpha\ne1,
   \]
   with the Shannon limit at one, kernel concavity on the entire two-site positive-contraction domain holds **if and only if \(\alpha=1\)**. The positive case is the proved session theorem of worker 016; a short reconstruction is included below. Every other order fails through an interior diagonal kernel along a direction that creates correlation while leaving both marginals fixed. Thus this is not the high-order Bernoulli obstruction. A noncommuting, rational \(\alpha=2\) chord witness has an exact deficit \(\log(225/221)>0\).

2. **A surviving product-reference weighted comparison.** Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}K\), let \(\mu=P_D\), and keep the diagonal fixed. Coordinate resampling realizes \(P_{D+t(K-D)}\) exactly and implies monotonicity of every positive finite-order Rényi divergence to \(\mu\). At order two an exact positive-coefficient polynomial yields sharp fourth-power contraction. The corrected weighted order-two entropy is concave on every fixed-diagonal two-site disk. In every dimension and at every positive finite order, this corrected entropy recovers the negative quartic expansion at a product law.

3. **The logarithmic frozen-score mechanism is unique in a precise class.** A twice continuously differentiable scalar atom score \(q(p)\) is supermodular for every interior DPP if and only if \(q(p)=c\log p+d\), with \(c\le0\). Necessity already follows from three-site product laws and a two-site correlated example. Thus, among smooth trace entropies, the universal supermodular frozen-score mechanism selects a nonnegative multiple of Shannon entropy, up to terms independent of the probability vector.

The general Shannon/Lyons conjecture is **not solved**. No historical novelty claim is made. Orders zero, infinity, and negative orders are outside the classification asserted here. The weighted statements do not prove ordinary Rényi entropy concavity or a general weighted kernel-concavity theorem.

## 1. Exact two-site Rényi classification

### 1.1 A diagonal second variation that decides every non-Shannon order

For a complex Hermitian two-site kernel
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},
\]
write its atom probabilities in the order \(00,10,01,11\) as
\[
x=(1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2,\quad
y=a(1-b)+|c|^2,\quad
z=(1-a)b+|c|^2,\quad
w=ab-|c|^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(0<a,b<1\) and the real affine path
\[
K(t)=\begin{pmatrix}a&t\\t&b\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]
It is a strict positive contraction whenever
\[
t^2<\min\{ab,(1-a)(1-b)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, both diagonal entries of \(K(t)\) and \(I-K(t)\) are positive, and (3) makes their determinants positive. Both one-point marginals remain fixed.

At zero, all atom first derivatives vanish and
\[
(x'',y'',z'',w'')=(-2,2,2,-2).
\]
Put \(\beta=\alpha-1\) and \(Z_\alpha(p)=p^\alpha+(1-p)^\alpha\). Direct differentiation gives the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
H_\alpha''(0)=
\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}
\frac{\big((1-a)^{\alpha-1}-a^{\alpha-1}\big)
      \big((1-b)^{\alpha-1}-b^{\alpha-1}\big)}
     {Z_\alpha(a)Z_\alpha(b)}.}
\tag{4}
\]
To verify the factor and sign, if \(Z(t)=\sum p_S(t)^\alpha\), then \(Z'(0)=0\) and
\[
Z''(0)=-2\alpha
\big((1-a)^\beta-a^\beta\big)
\big((1-b)^\beta-b^\beta\big).
\]
Also \(Z(0)=Z_\alpha(a)Z_\alpha(b)\); division by \(1-\alpha\) proves (4).

For \(\alpha>1\), choose \(a=1/4,b=1/3\). Both parenthesized factors in (4) are strictly positive, so \(H_\alpha''(0)>0\).

For \(0<\alpha<1\), choose \(a=1/5,b=4/5\). The two parenthesized factors have opposite signs, and \(\alpha-1<0\), again giving \(H_\alpha''(0)>0\).

All atoms are positive near zero, so this entropy is real analytic there. The path is even in \(t\), hence for sufficiently small nonzero \(t\),
\[
H_\alpha(K(t))=H_\alpha(K(-t))>H_\alpha(K(0)).
\]
Because \(K(0)=(K(t)+K(-t))/2\), the concavity inequality fails. The permitted size of \(t\) may depend on \(\alpha\). In particular, no common nonzero perturbation size as \(\alpha\to1\) is asserted.

This proves failure for **every** positive finite \(\alpha\ne1\). The same obstruction embeds in every dimension at least two by taking a block direct sum with a fixed diagonal interior kernel. Its law is a product, and the power sum factors, so Rényi entropy adds a constant along the embedded path.

### 1.2 A finite rational order-two certificate

Take
\[
K_\pm=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&\pm1/6\\\pm1/6&1/3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad K_0=\frac{K_++K_-}{2}=\operatorname{diag}(1/4,1/3).
\tag{5}
\]
Their diagonal entries lie in \((0,1)\), and
\[
\det K_\pm=\frac1{18}>0,\qquad
\det(I-K_\pm)=\frac{17}{36}>0.
\]
Thus the entire endpoint segment consists of strict contractions. The kernels do not commute:
\[
[K_+,K_-]=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/36\\-1/36&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
\]

The complete atom table is

| Atom | \(P_{K_0}\) | \(P_{K_+}=P_{K_-}\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(00\) | \(1/2\) | \(17/36\) |
| \(10\) | \(1/6\) | \(7/36\) |
| \(01\) | \(1/4\) | \(5/18\) |
| \(11\) | \(1/12\) | \(1/18\) |

The collision probabilities are
\[
\sum P_{K_0}^2=\frac{225}{648}=\frac{25}{72},\qquad
\sum P_{K_\pm}^2=\frac{221}{648}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{H_2(K_+)+H_2(K_-)}2-H_2(K_0)
=\log\frac{225}{221}>0.}
\tag{6}
\]
This is a finite exact certificate; no approximate logarithm or numerical Hessian is involved. In the parametrization (2), with \(a=1/4,b=1/3\), one can also check
\[
\sum_Sp_S(t)^2=\frac{25}{72}-\frac{t^2}{3}+4t^4,
\qquad H_2''(0)=\frac{48}{25}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The obstruction is genuinely in the determinantal dependence. A single Bernoulli order-two entropy satisfies
\[
\frac{d^2}{dp^2}\big[-\log(p^2+(1-p)^2)\big]
=-\frac{8p(1-p)}{(p^2+(1-p)^2)^2}\le0.
\]
Both marginals in (5) stay fixed, while \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,X_2)=-|c|^2\) changes. In particular (6) also disproves the ordinary Rényi analogue of “the independent law maximizes entropy among these fixed-marginal DPPs.”

For an exact low-order local check, the choice \(\alpha=1/2,a=1/5,b=4/5\) has base atoms \((4,1,16,4)/25\). Their square roots are rational, and direct differentiation yields
\[
Z(0)=9/5,\quad Z''(0)=5/4,\quad H_{1/2}''(0)=25/18>0.
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) is a local analytic certificate, not an additional finite chord witness.

### 1.3 Why one is the positive case

This part uses the established session result of report 016, not a newly claimed proof of the general conjecture. For clarity, its decisive two-site argument is short enough to reconstruct.

Let \(D=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) be any Hermitian direction at an interior two-site kernel. With \(\delta=\det D\),
\[
H_1''=-\mathcal I+2\delta L,
\quad \mathcal I=\sum_S(p_S')^2/p_S,
\quad L=\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0,
\tag{9}
\]
because \(yz-xw=|c|^2\) and \(p''=2\delta(1,-1,-1,1)\). If \(\delta\le0\), (9) is nonpositive. Otherwise negate \(D\) if necessary so that \(D>0\), and put
\[
A=\frac{\operatorname{adj}K}{\sqrt w},\quad
B=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-K)}{\sqrt x},\quad
T=\frac{y+z}{2\sqrt{xw}}\ge1.
\]
The extreme atom derivatives and two scalar/eigenvalue inequalities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I
&\ge(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2\\
&\ge\tfrac12\big(\operatorname{tr}(A+B)D\big)^2
\ge2\det(A+B)\det D
=4\delta(1+T).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the last inequality applies arithmetic-geometric mean to the positive matrix \((A+B)^{1/2}D(A+B)^{1/2}\). The last equality uses the two-dimensional determinant identity \(\det(A+B)=2+(y+z)/\sqrt{xw}\), as derived in 016. Also \(L\le2\log T\). Therefore
\[
H_1''\le-4\delta(1+T-\log T)\le-8\delta<0.
\]
Integrating proves concavity on strict segments. Replacing each endpoint by \((1-2\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I\) and letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) proves it on the closed domain, by continuity of the finite atom entropy. These inequalities are valid for complex Hermitian matrices. Together with (4), this proves the stated exact two-site classification.

## 2. What survives with product-reference weighting

Fix a finite kernel \(0\le K\le I\) whose diagonal entries \(d_i\) lie in \((0,1)\). Put
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i),\quad A=K-D,\quad
K_t=D+tA\quad(0\le t\le1),\quad
\mu=P_D=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_i).
\]
In this section \(D\) denotes the diagonal reference matrix, not the direction of §1.3. The reference \(\mu\) has full support and remains fixed. Define
\[
D_\alpha(P\Vert\mu)=\frac1{\alpha-1}\log\sum_x P(x)^\alpha\mu(x)^{1-\alpha},
\qquad
\mathcal W_\alpha(K;\mu)=H_\alpha(\mu)-D_\alpha(P_K\Vert\mu),
\tag{10}
\]
using KL divergence at \(\alpha=1\). This is an explicitly defined weighted functional, not an identification with ordinary Rényi entropy. At one, however, the fixed marginals make
\[
\mathcal W_1(K;\mu)=H_1(P_K),
\tag{11}
\]
because \(\log\mu(x)\) is a sum of one-coordinate functions and therefore \(-\mathbb E_{P_K}\log\mu=H_1(\mu)\).

### 2.1 Exact resampling and divergence monotonicity

Given \(X\sim P_K\), independently at each coordinate keep \(X_i\) with probability \(t\); otherwise replace it by a fresh independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_i)\) value. Call the output \(Y\). Then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Law}(Y)=P_{K_t}.}
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed, for every set \(S\), conditional independence gives
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}Y_i
=\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}\big(tX_i+(1-t)d_i\big)
=\sum_{J\subseteq S}t^{|J|}(1-t)^{|S\setminus J|}
\det K_J\prod_{i\in S\setminus J}d_i.
\]
Expanding \(\det(tK_S+(1-t)D_S)\) by choosing its diagonal columns gives exactly this sum. Inclusion probabilities determine the finite law, proving (12).

Let this Markov transition be \(T_t\). It preserves \(\mu\), and \(T_sT_t=T_{st}\), directly from the one-coordinate resampling definition. Thus for \(0\le s\le t\le1\), with \(t>0\), \(P_{K_s}=P_{K_t}T_{s/t}\).

For completeness, the finite data-processing comparison needs no citation as a proof dependency. Under the reference joint law \(\mu(x)T(x,y)\), if \(r(x)=P(x)/\mu(x)\), the output likelihood ratio is \(\mathbb E[r(X)\mid Y=y]\). Conditional Jensen, using convexity of \(r^\alpha\) for \(\alpha>1\) and concavity for \(0<\alpha<1\), proves the power-sum comparison. Taking \(\log/(\alpha-1)\) yields the same divergence direction in both cases. At one use convexity of \(r\log r\). Hence
\[
\boxed{D_\alpha(P_{K_s}\Vert\mu)\le D_\alpha(P_{K_t}\Vert\mu)
\quad(0\le s\le t\le1,\;0<\alpha<\infty).}
\tag{13}
\]
Equivalently, \(\mathcal W_\alpha\) decreases as the correlation scale increases. This proves monotonicity along these rays, not concavity along arbitrary kernel chords.

### 2.2 Exact order-two contraction and the negative quartic term

Let \(v_i=d_i(1-d_i)\), and for \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\) set
\[
z_i(x)=\frac{x_i-d_i}{v_i},\qquad z_J=\prod_{i\in J}z_i.
\]
The mixed-column atom determinant, divided by its diagonal atom \(\mu(x)\), gives the exact finite polynomial
\[
\frac{P_{K_t}(x)}{\mu(x)}
=1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq[n]}t^{|J|}\det(A_J)z_J(x).
\tag{14}
\]
One can see this by selecting the columns of \(tA\) in the atom determinant. A selected included coordinate contributes \(1/d_i\), an excluded coordinate contributes \(-1/(1-d_i)\), exactly the two values of \(z_i\). Since \(A\) has zero diagonal, singleton terms vanish.

Under \(\mu\), the contrasts are orthogonal:
\[
\mathbb E_\mu z_Jz_L=0\ (J\ne L),\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu z_J^2=\prod_{i\in J}v_i^{-1}.
\]
Hermitian principal determinants are real. Squaring (14) therefore proves
\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_{K_t}\Vert\mu)
=\sum_{|J|\ge2}t^{2|J|}
\frac{(\det A_J)^2}{\prod_{i\in J}v_i}.}
\tag{15}
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_{K_s}\Vert\mu)
\le(s/t)^4\chi^2(P_{K_t}\Vert\mu),\qquad0\le s\le t\le1,\ t>0.}
\tag{16}
\]
The exponent four is sharp: any nonzero two-site off-diagonal perturbation has only one nonzero term in (15), so equality holds. Equivalently,
\[
D_2(P_{K_s}\Vert\mu)
\le\log\left(1+(s/t)^4\big(e^{D_2(P_{K_t}\Vert\mu)}-1\big)\right).
\tag{17}
\]
For the endpoint in (5), the exact weighted collision excess is \(\chi^2(P_{K_+}\Vert\mu)=1/54\), and scaling its off-diagonal entry by \(t\) gives \(t^4/54\).

There is also an all-order local consequence. Write the density in (14) as \(1+u_t\), where
\[
u_t=t^2F_2+t^3F_3+\cdots,\qquad
F_2=-\sum_{i<j}|A_{ij}|^2z_iz_j.
\]
Normalization gives \(\mathbb Eu_t=0\); orthogonality gives \(\mathbb E F_2F_3=0\). Expanding \(\mathbb E(1+u_t)^\alpha\), then its logarithm, yields
\[
\boxed{
D_\alpha(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu)
=\frac{\alpha t^4}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{|A_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}+O(t^6)
\quad(0<\alpha<\infty).}
\tag{18}
\]
For \(\alpha\ne1\), the quadratic binomial coefficient is \(\alpha(\alpha-1)/2\), which cancels the denominator \(\alpha-1\); all cubic powers of \(u_t\) start at degree six. At one, expand \((1+u)\log(1+u)\) instead. The finite full-support reference ensures real analyticity near zero and justifies the remainders. Thus \(\mathcal W_\alpha\) has a strictly negative leading quartic term in every nonzero zero-diagonal Hermitian direction, at every positive finite order. This recovers report 023 at one. As in that report, it is a statement along each fixed line; it does not establish a matrix-neighborhood theorem.

### 2.3 A full surviving two-site fiber theorem at order two

Fix \(0<a,b<1\) and allow the complex entry \(c\) to vary on the feasible disk
\[
|c|^2\le\min\{ab,(1-a)(1-b)\}.
\]
Put \(V=a(1-a)b(1-b)>0\). Formula (15) gives exactly
\[
\mathcal W_2(K;P_{\operatorname{diag}(a,b)})
=H_2(P_{\operatorname{diag}(a,b)})-\log(1+|c|^4/V).
\tag{19}
\]
For \(r=|c|\), the nonconstant radial function \(f(r)=-\log(1+r^4/V)\) has real-plane Hessian eigenvalues
\[
f''(r)=-\frac{4r^2(3V-r^4)}{(V+r^4)^2},\qquad
\frac{f'(r)}r=-\frac{4r^2}{V+r^4}.
\]
Feasibility implies \(r^4\le V\), since the square of the minimum of two nonnegative numbers is at most their product. Both eigenvalues are nonpositive; at zero their continuous limits are zero. Integration on this convex disk proves **full concavity in the real and imaginary parts of \(c\)**, including the boundary. The product reference is fixed throughout. This theorem does not allow the diagonal to vary.

## 3. Why the logarithmic supermodular proof does not transfer

### 3.1 The precise Hessian distinction

For arbitrary positive atoms along an affine kernel path, let \(Z=\sum p^\alpha\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\alpha''={}&-\frac\alpha Z\sum p^{\alpha-2}(p')^2
+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)Z}\sum p^{\alpha-1}p''\\
&-\frac{\alpha^2}{(1-\alpha)Z^2}
\left(\sum p^{\alpha-1}p'\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
At the diagonal obstruction of §1, \(p'=0\) for every atom: both quadratic derivative terms vanish, regardless of their signs. The remaining frozen-score term is already strictly positive. Thus neither a better Fisher estimate nor control of the normalization term can fix this attempted Rényi extension at that point.

For Shannon entropy the corresponding score is \(-\log p\), up to an irrelevant constant. At a product law,
\[
-\log\mu(x)=\sum_i[-x_i\log d_i-(1-x_i)\log(1-d_i)]
\]
is modular. Any perturbation preserving the marginals has constant expectation against this score. This kills the second-order correlation term. For positive DPPs, the multiplicative principal-minor inequality makes \(-\log p_S\) supermodular, enabling reports 019 and the fixed-row/block arguments. Powers of probabilities do not convert the multiplicative inequality into the required additive one; (4) exhibits the exact failure.

The same logarithmic property makes fixed-marginal cross-entropy against a product reference constant. Ordinary Rényi entropy uses the escort weights \(\mu^\alpha\) in its expansion, under which the centered contrasts in (14) need not have zero mean. The weighted divergence in (10) instead expands under \(\mu\), retaining normalization and orthogonality. This explains the different quadratic behavior without identifying the two functionals.

### 3.2 A characterization of the scalar score mechanism

**Theorem.** Let \(q\in C^2((0,1))\). For every finite interior DPP, suppose the set function
\[
S\longmapsto q(P_K(S))
\]
is supermodular. Then
\[
q(p)=c\log p+d\quad(0<p<1),\qquad c\le0.
\tag{21}
\]
Conversely, every function in (21) has the stated property. Necessity needs only three-site product laws and a correlated two-site block with an independent third site.

**Proof of the form.** Consider a three-site product law with first two Bernoulli parameters \(a,b\), and condition the discrete second difference on the third site being present, with probability \(\lambda\in(0,1)\). Supermodularity implies
\[
F_\lambda(a,b):=
q(\lambda ab)+q(\lambda(1-a)(1-b))
-q(\lambda a(1-b))-q(\lambda(1-a)b)\ge0.
\tag{22}
\]
Replacing the first marginal by \(1-a\) gives another allowed product law and negates (22). Therefore \(F_\lambda(a,b)=0\) for all \(a,b,\lambda\in(0,1)\).

Differentiate first in \(a\), then in \(b\), at \(a=b=1/2\). With \(x=\lambda/4\), the result is
\[
0=4\lambda\big(q'(x)+xq''(x)\big).
\]
Hence \((xq'(x))'=0\) on \((0,1/4)\), so \(q(x)=c\log x+d\) there. Continuity gives the formula also at \(1/4\).

Taking \(\lambda\uparrow1\) in (22) is legitimate for fixed \(a,b\in(0,1)\). For any \(x\in[1/4,1)\), choose \(a=b=\sqrt x\). Then \(a(1-a)\) and \((1-a)^2\) are in \((0,1/4]\), and (22) says
\[
q(x)=2q(a(1-a))-q((1-a)^2)=c\log(a^2)+d=c\log x+d.
\]
This proves the form on all of \((0,1)\).

**Sign and sufficiency.** For a genuine two-site interior DPP, \(yz>xw\), so the supermodular second difference of this score is
\[
c\log\frac{xw}{yz}.
\]
It is nonnegative only if \(c\le0\). Adding an independent third site multiplies all four atoms by the same positive number and does not change this difference, so the necessity follows under the three-site hypothesis as well.

Conversely, write \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\). The atom formula is \(p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S\). A positive two-by-two Schur complement gives
\[
p_{T\cup\{i,j\}}p_T\le p_{T\cup\{i\}}p_{T\cup\{j\}}.
\]
Thus \(\log p_S\) is submodular and (21), with \(c\le0\), is supermodular. This completes the proof. ∎

For a smooth trace functional \(\sum_S\phi(p_S)\), the frozen atom score is \(q=\phi'\). If \(\phi\in C^3\) and this score mechanism is universally supermodular, integration of (21) gives
\[
\phi(p)=c\,p\log p+(d-c)p+e,\qquad c\le0.
\]
After summation, this is a nonnegative multiple of Shannon entropy plus terms independent of the probability vector. This is a characterization of this **specific scalar frozen-score mechanism**. It does not classify every possible entropy, every weighted construction, or every proof strategy.
