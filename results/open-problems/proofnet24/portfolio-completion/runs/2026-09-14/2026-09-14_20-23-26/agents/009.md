## Result and attribution boundary

The closest prior uniqueness result verified in this audit is **U. U. Jamilov (2016),
the symmetric strictly non-Volterra class on the three-dimensional simplex**. This
is four types, not three. Its publisher abstract supports that limited statement.
The paper's full text was not obtained, so the exact additional coefficient
condition meant by “symmetric” remains an explicit unresolved comparison.

The unrestricted theorem in Agent006 assumes only standard parent symmetry,
nonnegative normalized coefficients, and offspring exclusion of both parents.
**No retrieved primary theorem was verified to prove that full four-type result.**
This is a bounded search outcome, not proof of worldwide novelty or current open
status. In particular, the unavailable 2016 paper prevents a definitive novelty
clearance. Its title and abstract alone must not be inflated into an exact
coefficient-level comparison.

One additional four-type primary paper was found and checked beyond its abstract:
**Ganikhodjaev–Jusoo (2018)** treats a proper, two-parameter family. It does not
establish unrestricted four-type uniqueness.

There is also an unresolved, potentially important historical warning: a
ResearchGate citation excerpt attributed to the 2017 equiprobable paper says
that its reference [13] constructed an example having two fixed points; [13] in
the publisher bibliography is Jamilov's 2016 symmetric paper. This is a lead,
not a checked example. Its dimension and coefficients have not been verified.
It matters to any claim that the existing five-type counterexample is new.

## Exact target

Let
\[
S^3=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4:\sum_{i=1}^4x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
with
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad\sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\quad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad\hbox{when }k\in\{i,j\}.
\]
The target is uniqueness of a fixed point for every such coefficient array,
including arrays with zero allowed coefficients and boundary fixed points.
Agent006 supplies a proof; this report audits literature scope, not that proof.

The distinction between simplex dimension and number of types is essential:
\(S^2\) has three types, \(S^3\) four types, and \(S^4\) five types.

## Sources checked and what they actually establish

### 1. Original question: Ganikhodzhaev–Rozikov, arXiv:0902.4207v2

R. N. Ganikhodzhaev and U. A. Rozikov, *Quadratic Stochastic Operators: Results
and Open Problems*, arXiv:0902.4207v2, 12 March 2009, Section 8, equations
(13)–(15), Theorem 6, Problem 11.

- [Versioned full text](https://arxiv.org/html/0902.4207v2)
- [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.4207)

Theorem 6 concerns all strictly non-Volterra operators on \(S^2\): uniqueness,
non-attractiveness, and the occurrence of periods two and three in parameter
families. Problem 11 asks whether this theorem extends to \(m\ge4\).
Consequently, unrestricted four-type uniqueness answers **the uniqueness
clause at \(m=4\)**. It does not itself settle every assertion bundled into
Problem 11, nor prove universal convergence.

The arXiv version is authored by Ganikhodzhaev and Rozikov. Later bibliographies
also cite a broader 2011 article with Mukhamedov as a third author and “and
processes” in its title. These are not interchangeable version labels; the
question number checked here is from the stated 2009 version.

### 2. Original three-type theorem: Zhamilov–Rozikov (2009)

U. U. Zhamilov and U. A. Rozikov, *The dynamics of strictly non-Volterra quadratic
stochastic operators on the 2-simplex*, **Sbornik: Mathematics 200(9)** (2009),
1339–1351, DOI `10.1070/SM2009v200n09ABEH004039`.

- [Math-Net journal record and abstract](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/sm3962)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1070/SM2009v200n09ABEH004039)

The journal record explicitly gives arbitrary strictly non-Volterra operators
on the **2-simplex**, a unique non-attracting fixed point, and further results
for subclasses' limit sets and cycles. This is the \(m=3\) source, not an
unrestricted \(m=4\) theorem. Jamilov and Zhamilov are transliterations used for
the same author in the checked literature; searches should include both.

### 3. Closest verified prior result: Jamilov (2016)

U. U. Jamilov, *On Symmetric Strictly non-Volterra Quadratic Stochastic
Operators*, **Discontinuity, Nonlinearity, and Complexity 5(3)** (2016),
263–283, DOI `10.5890/DNC.2016.09.006`.

- [Publisher abstract, bibliography, and metadata](https://lhscientificpublishing.com/index.php/DNC/article/view/1965)
- [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5890/DNC.2016.09.006)
- [Author's publication page, no full text available](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/308598201_On_Symmetric_Strictly_non-Volterra_Quadratic_Stochastic_Operators)

The abstract advertises uniqueness for a **symmetric** operator on \(S^3\), a
sufficient condition for attractiveness, and further limit-set/periodic
behavior. The publisher PDF link redirects to a payment/login page; the
author's ResearchGate record offers a copy request, which was not sent.

**Verified:** four types and the explicitly named symmetric scope.
**Not verified:** the exact definition of symmetry, full theorem hypotheses,
proof, or all examples. Do not silently equate this word with standard parent
symmetry \(P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\), already required for ordinary QSOs.

### 4. A correction to the archived description of Jamilov's symmetry

The supplied manuscript `sources/qso_fixed_points.tex` describes the 2016
subfamily using “coefficients depending only on whether \(i=j\)”. The exact
2016 definition was not located, so that phrase is **not source-verified**.

There is a mathematical reason to avoid that wording pending full-text review.
Taken literally among allowed coefficients, suppose
\(P_{ii,k}=a\) for every \(k\ne i\), and \(P_{ij,k}=b\) for every distinct
\(i,j,k\). Normalization on four types forces
\[
3a=1,\quad2b=1,\qquad a=\tfrac13,\ b=\tfrac12.
\]
That describes only the equiprobable operator, not a freely parameterized
symmetric family. This is my inference from the target's normalization, not a
claim about the unavailable 2016 text. Use the original title's terminology
until the actual hypotheses can be quoted precisely.

### 5. Equiprobable family: Jamilov–Ladra–Mukhitdinov (2017; online 2016)

U. U. Jamilov, M. Ladra, and R. T. Mukhitdinov, *On the Equiprobable Strictly
Non-Volterra Quadratic Stochastic Operators*, **Qualitative Theory of Dynamical
Systems 16(3)** (2017), 645–655, DOI `10.1007/s12346-016-0209-9`.

- [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12346-016-0209-9)
- [Publisher preview hosted at ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305749753_On_the_Equiprobable_Strictly_Non-Volterra_Quadratic_Stochastic_Operators)

The publisher dates online publication to 30 July 2016, despite the 2017 issue.
Its abstract gives a unique attracting fixed point and singleton orbit-limit
sets for an **equiprobable** strictly non-Volterra operator on a finite simplex.
The full theorem's dimension conditions were not recovered from this preview.
This audit therefore does not infer them from the broad abstract.

Reference [13] in the publisher bibliography is Jamilov's 2016 symmetric paper.
A citation excerpt on that paper's ResearchGate page attributes a two-fixed-point
example to [13]. The excerpt is not a substitute for the original example:
dimension and admissibility are unresolved. This warning is a retrieval lead,
not an independently established mathematical fact.

### 6. Additional primary four-type paper: Ganikhodjaev–Jusoo (2018)

N. Ganikhodjaev and S. H. Bt Jusoo, *Strictly non-Volterra quadratic stochastic
operator (QSO) on 3-dimensional simplex*, **AIP Conference Proceedings 1974**
(2018), 030020, DOI `10.1063/1.5041664`.

- [Publisher abstract](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/acp/article/1974/1/030020/948918/Strictly-non-Volterra-quadratic-stochastic)
- [Institutional record](https://irep.iium.edu.my/66206/)
- [Full primary text uploaded by coauthor Siti Hasanah Jusoo](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326061167_Strictly_non-Volterra_quadratic_stochastic_operator_QSO_on_3-dimensional_simplex)

The institutional PDF is restricted, but the coauthor-uploaded original paper
was readable in ResearchGate's extracted full text. Pages 030020-4–6 first give
the general coefficient form (13), then impose the special assignments (14),
obtaining family (15), with \(f,g,h\ge0\), \(f+g+h=1\). Theorem 1 is expressly
about (15), with separate cases \(g=0\) and \(g>0\). The surrounding discussion
uses software/numerical analysis; this audit does not certify its dynamics proof.

Thus this is a restricted family with two independent parameters. In contrast,
the unrestricted target has fourteen: four diagonal offspring distributions
with two freedoms each, plus six unequal-parent distributions with one each.
The paper does not prove general four-type uniqueness.

### 8. Nearby papers checked as exclusions

- **Hardin–Rozikov**, *A Quasi-strictly Non-volterra Quadratic Stochastic
  Operator*, arXiv:1808.00229v2, later QTDS 18 (2019), 1013–1029.
  [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.00229). Equations (1.6)–(1.7) and
  Theorem 1 concern a four-parameter family on \(S^2\), allowing
  \(P_{33,3}=e\). For \(e\ne1\) it has a unique fixed point; for \(e=1\)
  there are two. Four parameters do not mean four types, and the exceptional
  example is not strictly non-Volterra under the target hypotheses.
- **Jamilov–Ladra**, *On a family of non-Volterra quadratic operators acting
  on a simplex*, [arXiv:2601.17494v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17494v1).
  It explicitly studies a particular parameter-dependent convex combination.
  Agent006 records that the journal reference is QTDS 19 (2020), Article 95;
  I used the full-text introduction to verify the restricted scope, and do not
  treat the 2026 arXiv upload date as a general-solution publication date.
- **Jamilov**, *A family of non-Volterra quadratic operators corresponding to
  permutations*, [primary journal PDF](https://cm.episciences.org/10135/pdf),
  Communications in Mathematics 31(1) (2023), 31–41. This is a selected
  parameter/permutation family. Its introduction explicitly says that the
  studied operators have two fixed points; this is not a theorem for the
  unrestricted strict class. Its reference [15] identifies Jamilov's 2017
  \(S^4\) paper, which is a five-type lead rather than a four-type one.
