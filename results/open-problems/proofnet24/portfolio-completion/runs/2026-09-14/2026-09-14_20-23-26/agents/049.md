## Results and scope

For four-type symmetric quadratic stochastic operators with strict parent
exclusion, let \(p(P)\) be the unique simplex fixed point and let

\[
\eta(P,Q)=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|,
\qquad
K_*:=\sup_{P\ne Q}\frac{\|p(P)-p(Q)\|_1}{\eta(P,Q)}.
\]

The coefficient norm is the **full row \(\ell^1\) norm**, not total variation.
The results below give

\[
\boxed{
\frac{93+14\sqrt3}{108}
\le K_*\le
\frac{204+95\sqrt5}{121}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The lower and upper constants are approximately \(1.085636216\) and
\(3.441540974\). The upper improves \(2\sqrt5-1\approx3.472135955\).
Neither endpoint in (1) is claimed to be the optimal universal constant.

* **Claim 1:** Singleton and pair inequalities exactly characterize all
  attainable fixed-point vectors. An exact optimization of this geometry
  sharpens the forced-parent-pair estimate and proves the upper bound in (1).
* **Claim 2:** The lower constant in (1) is an attained **feasible derivative
  ratio**. It is also the exact maximum derivative norm over all operators
  whose fixed point is \((1/3,1/3,1/3,0)\).
* **Claim 3:** A displayed pair of rational coefficient arrays has displayed
  rational fixed points and the exact finite ratio
  \(514813/480000\approx1.072527083\).

The algebraic lower constant is obtained as a limit of actual finite
coefficient quotients; I do not claim that a distinct finite pair attains
that algebraic constant. Claim 3 separately supplies an attained finite
quotient. A final example distinguishes coefficient sensitivity from inverse
residual conditioning.

## 1. Setup and existing inputs

The coefficient polytope \(\mathcal P\) consists of arrays satisfying

\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad\text{if }k\in\{i,j\},\qquad 1\le i,j,k\le4.
\tag{2}
\]

Equal parental indices are included in the exclusion rule. Define

\[
V_P(x)_k=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,\qquad
\mathcal B_H(x,y)_k=\sum_{i,j}H_{ij,k}x_i y_j,
\qquad E=\{v:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}v=0\}.
\]

I use the established uniqueness theorem audited in `agents/007.md`, the
forced-pair comparison inequality from `agents/013.md`, and the analytic
coefficient derivative from `agents/044.md`. In the latter notation,

\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\quad a=1-2c>0,\quad
J=DV_P(p)=2\mathcal B_P(p,\cdot),\quad
S_P=(I-J|_E)^{-1},
\]

\[
\boxed{Dp(P)[H]=S_P\mathcal B_H(p,p).}
\tag{3}
\]

Here \(H\) belongs to the full real vector space of symmetric,
parent-excluding, zero-row-sum arrays. Equation (3) is valid at coefficient
and fixed-point boundaries as a derivative of the analytic extension.
It does not assert that every signed \(H\) is feasible at a boundary array.
The existing coordinate cap is \(p_i\le c\).

## 2. Claim 1: exact attainable geometry and an improved universal upper

### 2.1. Attainable fixed-point vectors

**Claim 1(a).** For every integer \(n\ge3\), a vector
\(x\in\Delta_{n-1}\) is a fixed point of at least one symmetric
parent-excluding QSO if and only if

\[
\boxed{
x_i\le(1-x_i)^2\quad\text{for all }i,
\qquad
x_i+x_j+2x_i x_j\le1\quad\text{for all }i\ne j.
}
\tag{4}
\]

This statement concerns existence of an operator with the prescribed fixed
point. It makes no higher-dimensional uniqueness or sensitivity assertion.

**Proof.** Fix \(x\). For an unordered parent pair \(i\le j\), let

\[
w_{ij}=\begin{cases}x_i^2&i=j,\\2x_i x_j&i<j,\end{cases}
\qquad A_{ij}=[n]\setminus\{i,j\}.
\]

The set of all attainable offspring vectors from this particular state is
the compact convex Minkowski sum

\[
\mathcal C_x=\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}\Delta_{A_{ij}},
\qquad
\Delta_A=\operatorname{conv}\{e_k:k\in A\}.
\tag{5}
\]

The sets \(A_{ij}\) are nonempty because \(n\ge3\). Each distribution in a
summand supplies that parent row of \(P\), and the same row is used after
reversing distinct parents. Rows of weight zero may be completed arbitrarily
subject to (2).

The necessary and sufficient subset conditions for \(x\in\mathcal C_x\) are

\[
x(G)\le\sum_{i\le j:\,A_{ij}\cap G\ne\varnothing}w_{ij}
\quad\text{for every }G\subseteq[n].
\tag{6}
\]

Here is a short sufficiency proof, so no unexamined transportation-feasibility
assertion is needed. For any real vector \(z\), list its distinct values
\(t_1<\cdots<t_m\) and put \(G_\ell=\{k:z_k\ge t_\ell\}\). Then

\[
z=t_1\mathbf1+\sum_{\ell=2}^m(t_\ell-t_{\ell-1})\mathbf1_{G_\ell},
\]

and for every nonempty allowed set \(A\),

\[
\max_{k\in A}z_k
=t_1+\sum_{\ell=2}^m(t_\ell-t_{\ell-1})
  \mathbf1_{A\cap G_\ell\ne\varnothing}.
\]

Because \(\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}=\sum_i x_i=1\), applying (6) to the nested
sets gives

\[
z\cdot x\le\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}\max_{k\in A_{ij}}z_k
=\max_{y\in\mathcal C_x}z\cdot y.
\]

Finite-dimensional separation of a point from a compact convex set now
gives \(x\in\mathcal C_x\). Necessity of (6) follows directly by summing
offspring mass in \(G\).

For a singleton \(G=\{k\}\), the right side of (6) is \((1-x_k)^2\).
For \(G=\{k,\ell\}\), the only parent row with no allowed output in \(G\)
is the distinct pair \(k\ell\), of weight \(2x_kx_\ell\). For
\(|G|\ge3\), every parent pair has an allowed output in \(G\), so the right
side is one and the condition is automatic. Empty \(G\) is also automatic.
Thus (6) reduces exactly to (4). \(\square\)

The coordinator independently suggested the weighted-Hall formulation
during this task. I independently verified it and supplied the sorted-linear-
functional proof above. The argument applies to every \(n\ge3\); only the
optimization and sensitivity conclusions below are four-type statements.

### 2.2. Exact minimum of the forced-pair product

Put

\[
b=\frac{1-c}{1+2c},\qquad d=2cb,\qquad B=c+b=1-d,
\qquad m_0=bd=\frac{27-7\sqrt5}{121}.
\tag{7}
\]

Here \(c>b>d>0\), and numerically

\[
(c,b,d)\approx(0.381966011,0.350372906,0.267661084),
\qquad m_0\approx0.09378119138.
\]

**Claim 1(b).** Over all attainable four-type fixed-point vectors and all
partitions \(\{i,k\}\sqcup\{j,\ell}=[4]\),

\[
\boxed{p_i p_k+p_jp_\ell\ge m_0,}
\tag{8}
\]

and equality is attained by an admissible deterministic operator.

**Proof of the lower bound.** For any two coordinates \(x,y\le c\),
\((c-x)(c-y)\ge0\) gives \(xy\ge c(x+y)-c^2\). Combined with the pair
inequality in (4), this gives

\[
x+y\le\frac{1+2c^2}{1+2c}=B.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus it suffices to minimize the product expression under the weaker
constraints \(p_i\le c\), \(p_i+p_j\le B\), and simplex normalization.

Sort the four coordinates as \(A\ge C\ge D\ge F\ge0\). Of the three
pairings, \(AF+CD\) is the smallest, by the elementary rearrangement
identities. Write \(s=1-A-C=D+F\). Then

\[
AF+CD=Cs+(A-C)F
\ge Cs+(A-C)\max\{0,1-A-2C\}.
\tag{10}
\]

We use \(1/4\le A\le c\), \(C\le A\), and \(A+C\le B\).

If \(A+2C\ge1\), then \(A\ge1/3\), and the right side of (10) is
\(C(1-A-C)\). It is concave in \(C\) on

\[
\frac{1-A}{2}\le C\le\min\{A,B-A\}.
\]

At its lower endpoint the value is \((1-A)^2/4\ge c/4\ge bd\), using
\((1-c)^2=c\) and \(b+d=1-c\). At the upper endpoint:

* If \(A\le B/2\), the value is \(A(1-2A)\). This function decreases
  for \(A\ge1/3\), so it is at least \(B(1-B)/2\ge(B-c)(1-B)=bd\).
* If \(A\ge B/2\), the value is \((B-A)(1-B)\ge(B-c)(1-B)=bd\).

If \(A+2C\le1\), the right side of (10) is
\(A(1-A-2C)+C^2\), which decreases as \(C\) increases while \(C\le A\).
For \(A\le1/3\), use \(C\le A\) to bound it below by
\(A-2A^2\ge1/9>bd\), since \(1/4\le A\le1/3\).
For \(A\ge1/3\), use \(C\le(1-A)/2\) to bound it below by
\((1-A)^2/4\ge c/4\ge bd\). This exhausts all cases and proves (8).
The comparisons used above include \(2/3<B<2c\), so the interval endpoints
and signs of \(1-B\) are as stated. \(\square\)

**Attainment.** Specify the offspring of each unordered pair by

| Parents | Offspring |
| --- | --- |
| \(11\) | \(2\) |
| \(22\) | \(1\) |
| \(33\) | \(1\) |
| \(12\) | \(3\) |
| \(13\) | \(2\) |
| \(23\) | \(1\) |
| \(14\) | \(3\) |
| \(24\) | \(1\) |
| \(34\) | \(1\) |
| \(44\) | \(1\) |

Every listed offspring excludes its parents. This operator has fixed point

\[
p=(c,b,d,0).
\]

Indeed \(p_1=(b+d)^2=c\), \(p_3=2cb=d\), and
\(p_2=c^2+2cd=b\). The last identity follows from
\(b(1-4c^2)=c^2\). The fourth output is identically zero. For the partition
\(\{1,4\}\sqcup\{2,3}\), the product sum is exactly \(bd=m_0\).
Thus (8) is sharp even on the actual fixed-point locus, not only on a relaxed
capped simplex.

### 2.3. Consequence for coefficient sensitivity

I do not repeat the upper-bound proof of worker 013. Its intermediate
forced-parent-pair inequality says, in the two-positive/two-negative case,

\[
(1-2c)\|p(P)-p(Q)\|_1
\le\eta(P,Q)\,[1-2(q_iq_k+q_jq_\ell)],\qquad q=p(Q),
\tag{11}
\]

where the partition is the sign partition of \(p(P)-p(Q)\). If one sign
occurs only once, the existing bound has constant one. These statements
include zero coefficients and zero state coordinates.

Inserting (8) into (11) gives

\[
\boxed{
\|p(P)-p(Q)\|_1
\le\min\left\{2,\frac{204+95\sqrt5}{121}\eta(P,Q)\right\},
}
\tag{12}
\]

because

\[
\frac{1-2m_0}{1-2c}
=\frac{204+95\sqrt5}{121}<2\sqrt5-1.
\]

The new product minimum is sharp; this does **not** make the coefficient
constant in (12) sharp, because other inequalities in (11) need not have
simultaneous equality.

### Why the old capped-simplex extremizers fail

Worker 013 used

\[
p_i p_k+p_jp_\ell-c(1-2c)
=(c-p_i)(c-p_k)+(c-p_j)(c-p_\ell)\ge0.
\]

Equality requires one coordinate equal to \(c\) in each of two disjoint
pairs, hence two distinct coordinates equal to \(c\). But their pair
inequality would require

\[
2c+2c^2=10-4\sqrt5\le1,
\]

which is false. Equivalently, equality in the individual cap forces every
positive-weight parent row avoiding that output to produce it with
probability one; two capped outputs conflict on the diagonal row of a third
positive type. Therefore these capped-simplex extremizers cannot be fixed
points of any admissible coefficient array. Condition (4) gives the exact
additional feasibility constraints, rather than only this obstruction.

## 3. Claim 2: an exact feasible derivative extremizer

### 3.1. All operators with the uniform three-type boundary fixed point

Consider the fixed point \(p_0=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0)\). Every operator having this
fixed point has no type-4 output on any parent row. On the active triple,
each distinct-parent pair is forced to produce the third active type. Its
diagonal rows must have the form, for some \(t\in[0,1]\),

| Parents | Offspring distribution |
| --- | --- |
| \(11\) | \(t e_2+(1-t)e_3\) |
| \(22\) | \(t e_3+(1-t)e_1\) |
| \(33\) | \(t e_1+(1-t)e_2\) |
| \(12\) | \(e_3\) |
| \(13\) | \(e_2\) |
| \(23\) | \(e_1\) |

To check completeness, write initially separate probabilities \(t_1,t_2,t_3\)
in the three diagonal rows. The three fixed-point equations at \(p_0\)
force \(t_1=t_2=t_3\). Parent rows \(14,24,34,44\) may be arbitrary under
parent exclusion. In the Jacobian, they enter only through

\[
u=\frac13(P_{14,\cdot}+P_{24,\cdot}+P_{34,\cdot})\big|_{\{1,2,3\}}.
\]

The attainable \(u\)'s form the hexagon

\[
u_i\ge0,\qquad\sum_i u_i=1,\qquad u_i\le2/3,
\tag{13}
\]

whose vertices are the six permutations of \((2/3,1/3,0)\). For example,
enumerating the two allowed choices in each of the three parent rows gives
these six vertices and the center; taking their convex hull gives (13).
The (44) row has no effect on either \(J\) or the first coefficient
derivative at \(p_0\), because its parental weight is zero.

By reversing the active cyclic order, it suffices to take \(1/2\le t\le1\).
Write

\[
z=\frac{2t-1}{3}\in[0,1/3],\qquad D_z=4+3z^2,
\]

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}J_3&2u\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J_3=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1-z&1+z\\1+z&0&1-z\\1-z&1+z&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{14}
\]

### 3.2. Exact maximum over this entire fixed-point family

**Claim 2.** On the full normalized affine coefficient direction space,

\[
\boxed{
\max_{P:\,p(P)=p_0}\|Dp(P)\|_{\mathrm{row}\to1}
=\frac{93+14\sqrt3}{108}.
}
\tag{15}
\]

The maximum is attained by a direction feasible for a nonzero forward
interval inside the coefficient polytope.

**Upper bound within the family.** Let \(v=Dp(P)[H]\) and
\(\|H\|_{\rm row}\le1\). If one sign of \(v\) occurs only once, the
singleton-sign argument of worker 044 gives \(\|v\|_1\le1\). In all other
nonzero cases there are two positive and two negative coordinates. Replacing
\(H\) by \(-H\) if needed and rotating the three active types reduces its
positive cut to \(G=\{1,4\}\). This reasoning includes vectors with zero
coordinates, since such vectors have a singleton sign unless they vanish.

Solve \((I-J)^{\mathsf T}r=\mathbf1_G\). Direct algebra gives

\[
r_1=\frac{-z^2}{D_z},\qquad
r_2=\frac{-2-z-z^2}{D_z},\qquad
r_3=\frac{-2+z-z^2}{D_z},\qquad
r_4=1+2u\cdot(r_1,r_2,r_3).
\tag{16}
\]

There is no ambiguity from using an ambient inverse here: its restriction
to \(E\) gives the same pairing with every zero-sum forcing vector. The
ambient radial eigenvalue of \(J\) is 2, so that radial eigenvalue does not
make \(I-J\) singular.

For a fixed cut, an allowed zero-sum coefficient row of norm at most one
maximizes \(r\cdot H_{ij,\cdot}\) by transferring \(1/2\) from a minimum
allowed \(r\)-value to a maximum allowed \(r\)-value. Thus the exact
unrestricted row-ball support function is

\[
\sup_{\|H\|_{\rm row}\le1}2\sum_{k\in G}v_k
=\sum_{i\le j}w_{ij}(p_0)
 \left(\max_{k\notin\{i,j\}}r_k-\min_{k\notin\{i,j\}}r_k\right).
\tag{17}
\]

In particular diagonal weights are \(1/9\) and distinct active-parent
weights are \(2/9\); weights involving parent 4 are zero. With \(R=r_4\),
the right side becomes

\[
L(R)=\frac19\sum_{i=1}^3
 \operatorname{range}\bigl(\{R\}\cup\{r_j:j\ne i,1\le j\le3\}\bigr)
 +\frac29\sum_{i=1}^3|R-r_i|.
\tag{18}
\]

This is convex as a function of \(R\). Since \(r_2\le r_3\le r_1\),
the hexagon (13) gives the interval endpoints

\[
R_- =\frac{3z^2-2z}{3D_z},\qquad
R_+ =\frac{8+2z+3z^2}{3D_z}.
\]

On \(0\le z\le1/3\), the orderings are

\[
r_2\le r_3\le R_-\le r_1<R_+.
\]

Substituting these orderings into (18) gives

\[
L(R_-)=\frac{14-z+6z^2}{9D_z},\qquad
L(R_+)=\frac{38+7z+18z^2}{9D_z}=:\Lambda(z).
\tag{19}
\]

The second value is larger, and \(\Lambda(z)>1\). Convexity therefore
bounds every two-sign cut by \(\Lambda(z)\), while the singleton-sign
case was bounded by one. This proves the full derivative-norm upper bound
\(\|Dp(P)\|\le\Lambda(z)\) for every operator in this fixed-point family.

Finally,

\[
\Lambda'(z)=\frac{7(4-12z-3z^2)}{9(4+3z^2)^2}.
\]

Its unique maximum on \([0,1/3]\) occurs at

\[
z_*=-2+\frac{4\sqrt3}{3},\qquad
t_*=\frac{1+3z_*}{2}=2\sqrt3-\frac52\in(0,1),
\tag{20}
\]

and

\[
\Lambda(z_*)=\frac{93+14\sqrt3}{108}.
\tag{21}
\]

**Attainment by an admissible coefficient perturbation.** Complete the
six-row table above by

\[
14\to3,\qquad24\to1,\qquad34\to1,\qquad44\to1.
\tag{22}
\]

Then \(u=(2/3,0,1/3)\), giving \(R_+\). For the six active parent rows,
define \(H\) by transferring \(1/2\) to output 4 from the following output:

| Parents | Output decreased by \(1/2\) | Output increased by \(1/2\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(11\) | \(2\) | \(4\) |
| \(22\) | \(3\) | \(4\) |
| \(33\) | \(2\) | \(4\) |
| \(12\) | \(3\) | \(4\) |
| \(13\) | \(2\) | \(4\) |
| \(23\) | \(1\) | \(4\) |

All other rows of \(H\) are zero. The opposite ordering of each distinct
parent pair has the same perturbation. Hence \(H\) has the required
symmetry, exclusions, and zero row sums, and \(\|H\|_{\rm row}=1\).
Its forcing is

\[
g=\mathcal B_H(p_0,p_0)=
\left(-\frac19,-\frac29,-\frac16,\frac12\right).
\]

Solving (3) exactly gives, for any \(z\in[0,1/3]\) in this completion,

\[
v=Dp(P)[H]=
\left(
\frac{2+7z-9z^2}{18D_z},
\frac{-26+7z-9z^2}{18D_z},
\frac{-12-14z-9z^2}{18D_z},
\frac12
\right).
\tag{23}
\]

The first and fourth coordinates are positive, and the other two are
negative. Thus \(\|v\|_1=1+2v_1=\Lambda(z)\).

At \(t=t_*\), every coefficient being decreased is strictly positive.
The entire affine ray \(P+\tau H\) is admissible for

\[
0\le\tau\le2(1-t_*)=7-4\sqrt3>0.
\tag{24}
\]

This verifies forward feasibility at the coefficient boundary, not just
formal signed differentiation. The fixed point begins on the face \(x_4=0\),
and its fourth coordinate has slope \(1/2\). More explicitly, along this
ray all active parental rows give output 4 probability \(\tau/2\), so its
fixed-point equation is

\[
p_4(\tau)=\frac\tau2(1-p_4(\tau))^2,
\qquad
p_4(\tau)=\frac{\tau}{1+\tau+\sqrt{1+2\tau}}>0\quad(\tau>0).
\]

The derivative formula and feasibility imply

\[
\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}
\frac{\|p(P+\tau H)-p(P)\|_1}{\eta(P+\tau H,P)}
=\|v\|_1=\frac{93+14\sqrt3}{108},
\tag{25}
\]

since \(\eta(P+\tau H,P)=\tau\). This proves the lower bound in (1)
and the attained within-family derivative maximum (15). \(\square\)

At the deterministic endpoint \(t=1\), the same formal \(H\) would decrease
\(P_{33,2}=0\), so it is infeasible in the forward direction. Its full-affine
norm \(127/117\) is therefore not, by itself, an attained feasible endpoint
derivative certificate. The maximizing value \(t_*<1\) removes this issue.

## 4. Claim 3: an attained finite rational coefficient ratio

Its fixed point is \(p=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0)\). Put

\delta=\frac{200}{39601},\qquad
\alpha=\frac{1922136413}{793010025000},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{1449033423}{629434134400}.
\tag{26}
\]

Define \(Q\) by the following complete table. Unlisted outputs in a row

| Parents | Offspring distribution for \(Q\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(11\) | \((19/20-\delta)e_2+(1/20)e_3+\delta e_4\) |
| \(22\) | \((1/20)e_1+(19/20-\delta)e_3+\delta e_4\) |

Exact rational arithmetic gives

\[
0<\alpha<\delta,\quad0<\gamma<\delta<1/20,

so every row is nonnegative, normalized, and parent-excluding. Direct

\[
q=\left(\frac{267}{800},\frac{19807}{60000},
         \frac{4967}{15000},\frac1{200}\right),\qquad V_Q(q)=q.
\tag{27}
\]

The independent exact script checks all four rational equations rather than
calling a numerical fixed-point solver. To indicate how the certificate
was constructed, start with \(Q_0=P+2\delta H\). Its fourth equation at
(27) is exact because \(q_4=\delta(1-q_4)^2\). Let
\(r=q-V_{Q_0}(q)\). Set the (24) transfer to \(\delta\), then solve the
two remaining independent residual equations by

\[

\alpha=\frac{r_2-2q_3q_4\gamma}{2q_1q_4}.
\]

These formulas give precisely (26); the third active equation follows from
normalization and is also checked directly in the script.

The exact coefficient distance and fixed-point distance are

\[
\eta(P,Q)=2\delta=\frac{400}{39601},\qquad
\|q-p\|_1=\frac{13}{1200}.
\]

Indeed \(q_1-p_1=1/2400>0\) and \(q_4-p_4=1/200>0\), while the other
two differences are negative. Therefore this finite, fully admissible pair
attains

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|q-p\|_1}{\eta(P,Q)}=\frac{514813}{480000}>1.
}
\tag{28}
\]

This is a lower witness, not a proposed maximizer among finite pairs.

## 5. Coefficient sensitivity is not inverse residual conditioning

The derivative map is \(H\mapsto S_P\mathcal B_H(p,p)\), not \(S_P\) on
an arbitrary unit residual vector. Its forcing rows have exclusions,
zero-sum constraints, and parental weights. Ignoring these restrictions can
produce an incorrect coefficient lower bound.

There is an exact distinction already at the preceding family with
\(t=1/2\), \(u=(2/3,0,1/3)\):

\[
\boxed{
\|S_P\|_{E,1\to1}=\frac76,
\qquad
\|Dp(P)\|_{\mathrm{row}\to1}=\frac{19}{18}.
}
\tag{29}
\]

For example,

\[
S_P(e_4-e_2)=\left(\frac16,-1,-\frac16,1\right),
\]

which gives residual ratio \(7/6\). The unit ball of \(E\) in the
\(\ell^1\) norm is the convex hull of \((e_i-e_j)/2\); checking these six
unordered pairs gives the matching inverse upper bound. Active-active pairs
give ratio \(1/2\), while the three pairs involving type 4 give ratios
\(1,7/6,7/6\). The coefficient norm \(19/18\) follows from (19) at \(z=0\)
and is attained by the feasible \(H\) above. These computations concern the
full linear tangent space even though \(p_0\) is on the simplex boundary.
