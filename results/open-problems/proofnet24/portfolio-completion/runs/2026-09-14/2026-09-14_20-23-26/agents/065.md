# Worker 065: guaranteed exact rational grid algorithm for four-type QSOs

Date: 2026-09-14. Actual worker 065 of the user-authorized 100-worker research
run. Assignment: replace the *termination limitation* of floating-point search
with a separate algorithm having a proved finite tolerance guarantee and
polynomial bit complexity. Only `agents/065.md` and `computations/065_*` were

were made.

**Result: PROVED.** For every rational four-type strictly non-Volterra QSO
and every positive rational tolerance, the algorithm below returns a rational
candidate whose exact residual certificate is at most that tolerance. It
requires polynomially many bit operations in the explicit rational input
length. It does not enumerate the grid. The standard-library implementation
uses integer arithmetic during iteration and exact fractions for certification.

The parent's proposed constants are valid. I choose a denominator divisible
by **20**, so that both the `2/5` cap and the uniform starting point align with
the grid. Largest-remainder rounding preserves the cap for every denominator
divisible by five. Its four-coordinate error is actually at most `2/N`, but
the implementation deliberately uses the proposed conservative `4/N`,
`50/N`, and `1500/epsilon` bounds.

## 1. Mathematical contract and analytic foundation

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\sum_i x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=0}^3P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where all coefficients are rational and
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,
\qquad P_{ij,k}=0\quad\text{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Allowed coefficients may vanish. Set
\[
K=\{x\in\Delta_3:x_i\le2/5\text{ for every }i\},\quad
T=\frac35I+\frac25V,\quad L=\frac{23}{25}.
\tag{2}
\]

The required analytic facts are Theorem 2 and equation (10) of
`FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md`, audited in `agents/008.md` and applied to certificates
in `agents/026.md`. For completeness, their specialization used here has the
following short proof.

Strict non-Volterra coefficients imply
\[
0\le V_k(x)\le(1-x_k)^2.
\]
For `0 <= s <= 2/5`, the convex function
\[
f(s)=\frac35s+\frac25(1-s)^2
\]
has endpoint values `f(0)=2/5` and `f(2/5)=48/125 < 2/5`.
Thus `T(K)` is contained in `K`. The set is nonempty and complete, containing
the uniform probability vector.

For `m=(x+y)/2`, define
\[
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_iP_{ij,k}m_i.
\]
The matrix is nonnegative, column-stochastic and zero on the diagonal.
Parent symmetry gives polarization:
\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A(m)(x-y),\qquad
T(x)-T(y)=B(m)(x-y),\quad B=\frac35I+\frac45A.
\tag{3}
\]
For a matrix with equal column sums, its induced `l1` norm on the zero-sum
subspace is
\[
\max_{i,j,G}\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}B(e_i-e_j).
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed a zero-sum vector is a positive linear combination of transfers
`e_i-e_j`, with total transfer weight half its `l1` norm; the largest subset
sum of a zero-sum column difference is half its `l1` norm. The columns of `B`
sum to `7/5`, which suffices for (4).

The special two-pair estimate in (4) can be checked directly. Write
`G={i,k}`, its complement `{j,l}`, and
`p_ab=sum_{r in G} P_ab,r`. Then `p_ki=0`, `p_lj=1`, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(e_i-e_j)
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
  -m_kp_{kj}+m_l(p_{li}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}\le\max(m_i,m_j)\le\frac25.
\end{aligned}
\]
For all cuts, the resulting bounds on (4) are:

| Cut case | Upper bound |
|---|---:|
| Empty or full cut | `0` |
| Both tested indices inside, or both outside | `4/5` |
| First index outside and second inside | `1/5` |
| First index inside, second outside, cut size one or three | `3/5` |
| First index inside, second outside, cut size two | `3/5+(4/5)(2/5)=23/25` |

Consequently
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\le L\|x-y\|_1\qquad(x,y\in K).
\tag{5}
\]
Banach's theorem supplies a unique fixed point `x*` in `K`. Fixed points of
`T` and `V` coincide. Every fixed point of `V` satisfies
\[
x_i\le(1-x_i)^2,\qquad
x_i\le\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}<\frac25,
\]
so this is the unique fixed point throughout the simplex.

Write `R(x)=||V(x)-x||_1`. For every `x in K`, (5) yields
\[
\|x-x^*\|_1
\le\frac25R(x)+\frac{23}{25}\|x-x^*\|_1,
\qquad \boxed{\|x-x^*\|_1\le5R(x).}
\tag{6}
\]
This is the residual of **V**. The residual of `T` would instead have
multiplier `25/2`.

There is also an upper bound on the *size of the certificate*. Because `A`
in (3) is column-stochastic, for arbitrary simplex points
\[
\|V(x)-V(y)\|_1\le2\|x-y\|_1.
\]
Using `V(x*)=x*` gives
\[
R(x)\le3\|x-x^*\|_1,
\qquad 5R(x)\le15\|x-x^*\|_1.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6) and (7) serve different purposes: (6) validates the emitted
certificate, and (7) proves that the algorithm eventually meets its requested
certificate tolerance.

## 2. Exact rounding preserves the invariant cap

Let `N` be a positive integer divisible by five and `y in K`. Put
\[
Ny_i=a_i+f_i,\qquad a_i=\lfloor Ny_i\rfloor,\quad 0\le f_i<1.
\]
Since `sum Ny_i=N`,
\[
r=N-\sum_i a_i=\sum_i f_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]
Choose the `r` largest remainders, breaking ties by smaller coordinate index,
add one to their floors, and divide every resulting integer by `N`. Denote
this map by `Q_N`.

All output counts are nonnegative and their sum is `N`. If `r>0`, more than
`r` remainders must be strictly positive: otherwise their sum would be less
than or equal to the number of positive remainders and strictly less than
`r`. Thus no zero remainder is incremented. Each output coordinate is exactly
the floor or ceiling of `Ny_i`, divided by `N`.

The cap count `2N/5` is an integer. Since `Ny_i <= 2N/5`, both its floor and
its ceiling are at most `2N/5`. Therefore
\[
Q_N(K)\subseteq K\cap (N^{-1}\mathbb Z)^4.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, a coordinate already at the cap has remainder zero and cannot
be rounded above it. Every coordinate changes by less than `1/N`, so
\[
\boxed{\|Q_N(y)-y\|_1\le4/N.}
\tag{9}
\]
The non-strict inequality is the convenient bound used below.

There is a sharper exact bound. If `S` is the selected set of `r` indices,
\[
\|Q_N(y)-y\|_1
=\frac2N\left(r-\sum_{i\in S}f_i\right)
\le\frac{r(4-r)}{2N}\le\frac2N,
\tag{10}
\]
because, when `r>0`, the average of the `r` largest remainders is at least
the overall average `r/4`; the `r=0` case has zero error. Equality in the last bound occurs at `N=20` and
`y=(7,9,11,13)/40`, where all four remainders equal `1/2`. This improvement
would permit `N >= 750/epsilon` with the same transient budget. It is not
needed or silently substituted into the implementation's conservative plan.

Grid alignment is a real condition. With `N=6` and
`y=(2/5,1/5,1/5,1/5)`, ordinary largest-remainder rounding gives
`(1/2,1/6,1/6,1/6)`, outside the cap. This is an exact counterexample to
dropping the divisibility condition while retaining this unmodified rounding
rule.

## 3. Rounded iteration and its finite certificate guarantee

For positive rational `epsilon`, set
\[
N=20\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{75}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right\},
\qquad x_0=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4),
\qquad x_{t+1}=Q_N(T(x_t)).
\tag{11}
\]
Then `N >= 1500/epsilon`, `20` divides `N`, and the initial point is on the
grid and in the cap. Equations (5), (8), and (9) imply, with
`d_t=||x_t-x*||_1`,
\[
d_{t+1}\le Ld_t+4/N.
\]
The simplex diameter is two. Summing the geometric recurrence gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&\le2L^t+\frac4N\frac{1-L^t}{1-L}\\
&=2L^t+\frac{50}{N}(1-L^t)
\le2L^t+\frac{50}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

No floating logarithm is used to choose the horizon. Let
\[
k=\min\{j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:2^j\ge60/\varepsilon\},\qquad t_*=9k.
\tag{13}
\]
The elementary integer comparison
\[
2\cdot23^9=3602305322926
\le3814697265625=25^9
\]
proves `L^9 <= 1/2`. Hence
\[
2L^{t_*}\le2^{1-k}\le\varepsilon/30,
\qquad 50/N\le\varepsilon/30.
\]
Combining (7) and (12) gives the requested guarantee:
\[
\boxed{\quad
5R(x_{t_*})\le15d_{t_*}
\le15\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{30}+\frac{\varepsilon}{30}\right)
=\varepsilon.
\quad}
\tag{14}

The program evaluates the exact residual at every step, including zero, and
returns as soon as `5R <= epsilon`. It must return by `t_*`. The positive
tolerance is arbitrary; no user-selected precision or discretionary step
limit can invalidate the guarantee. If the proved horizon were exhausted
without certification, the implementation raises an arithmetic error rather
than producing a false success. That branch is unreachable under the proved
mathematical and exact-arithmetic premises.

For `epsilon=a/b > 0` in lowest terms, the planner computes
\[
N=20\max\{1,\lfloor(75b+a-1)/a\rfloor\},\qquad
M=\lfloor(60b+a-1)/a\rfloor,
\]
and obtains `k` as the integer bit length of `M-1` (zero when `M=1`). This is
exactly (13). The output records the valid horizon bound `2^(1-k)+50/N`;
it need not calculate the rational power `(23/25)^(9k)`.

Fixed-denominator rounded orbits need not converge indefinitely to the exact
fixed point. The theorem asserts a finite stopping guarantee with the chosen
denominator. In particular, a fixed point outside the grid cannot be returned
with zero residual; the two nontrivial rational-oracle examples below have
deliberately unaligned fixed points.

## 4. Integer implementation and polynomial bit complexity

Let `D` be the least common multiple of the denominators of the forty
coefficients for the ten unordered parent pairs, and write
`C_ij,k = D P_ij,k`. These are nonnegative integers and each row sums to `D`.
Store a grid state as four integer counts `n_i` with `x_i=n_i/N`.

Compute
\[
S_k=\sum_{i\le j}(2-\mathbf1_{i=j})C_{ij,k}n_in_j.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus `V_k(x)=S_k/(DN^2)` and the scaled coordinate needed for rounding is
\[
N T_k(x)=\frac{3DNn_k+2S_k}{5DN}.
\tag{16}
\]
All four scaled coordinates share the denominator `5DN`. Four integer
`divmod` operations supply the floors and remainders, and sorting four
remainders implements `Q_N`. The factor two for distinct parents in (15) is
essential and is independently checked by Fraction evaluation in the runner.

The exact residual is
\[
R(x)=\frac{\sum_k|S_k-DNn_k|}{DN^2}.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently every step starts with grid counts again. Denominators from a
previous exact quadratic iterate are never carried forward and squared.
There is no loop over the grid's `N` counts or its possible states.

Here is an explicit bit model. Let `b` be the explicit binary encoding length
of the input coefficient numerators and denominators, and `h` that of the
positive rational tolerance. Put `s=1+b+h`. The number of types is fixed at
four. Equivalently, ordinary explicit decimal numerator/denominator strings
have encoding lengths within constant factors; parsing noncanonical padding
adds a polynomial cost in the actual input text length.

* `log_2 D <=` the sum of input denominator bit lengths, hence `O(b)`.
* `log_2 N = O(1+log^+(1/epsilon)) = O(1+h)`.
* `k=O(1+h)` and `t_*=9k=O(1+h)`.
* `0 <= C_ij,k <= D`, `0 <= n_i <= N`, and
  `0 <= S_k <= DN^2`. The numerators in (16), their common denominator,
  the residual in (17), and all certificate arithmetic have `O(s)` bits.
* Each iteration performs a fixed number of integer operations on `O(s)`-bit
  operands, plus a fixed number of fraction operations for its residual test.

Using even elementary arithmetic and a conservative cubic bound for GCD and
rational operations, the complete computation takes
\[
O\bigl((1+h)s^3\bigr)
\]
bit operations, with `O(s)` live arithmetic storage. The certificate has
`O(s)` length. These are safe polynomial upper bounds, not claimed optimal
complexity estimates for Python. Faster standard big-integer algorithms only
improve them. The guarantee is in input *bit length*, not polynomial in
`1/epsilon`; representing a large `N` requires only its binary digits.

The CLI accepts integer or explicit numerator/denominator strings. It rejects
JSON floating-point values, booleans, scientific exponent strings and decimal
fraction strings such as `"0.25"`. Use `"1/4"` instead. This deliberately
avoids measuring complexity in an exponentially compressed exponent notation.
Python API coefficient entries may additionally be `Fraction` values; API
tolerances must be positive `int` or `Fraction` values, excluding booleans.

The implementation parses and serializes decimal integers in nine-digit
chunks. Therefore Python's optional 4300-decimal-digit conversion limit does
not introduce an implicit precision cutoff in this solver. The test with
tolerance `1/10^4400` exercised this path and returned the exact uniform fixed
point at step zero; it is an encoding/planning check, not an empirical run of
131607 nonzero iterations. The pre-existing independent verifier uses ordinary
Fraction string parsing and needed its decimal conversion limit temporarily
disabled for this one unusually long tolerance string. The runner restores
that process setting afterward. Normal-size recorded examples need no such
adjustment.

## 5. Exact certificates and software interface

The separate solver is `computations/065_exact_grid_solver.py`. It does not
import or modify the float-search solver. Its records reuse the existing
`four-type-qso-residual-v1` schema and embed the exact canonical operator.

The final record contains the rational candidate, exact residual `R`, and
\[
B=\min\{2,5R\},\qquad
x_i^*\in\left[\max(0,x_i-B/2),\ \min(2/5,x_i+B/2)\right].
\tag{18}
\]
The half-radius is valid because the difference of two probability vectors
has sum zero, so the absolute value of each coordinate is at most half its
`l1` norm. The fixed point belongs to the cap, justifying clipping. The
algorithm stops on the stronger untruncated comparison `5R <= epsilon`, so
the emitted `B <= epsilon` flag is necessarily true.

Additional `exact_grid_plan` fields describe the denominator, counts and
proved horizon. The independent root verifier checks the finite residual
certificate, coefficient binding, intervals and exact tolerance comparison;
it does **not** validate or replay these additional algorithm-description
fields. Their mathematical justification is this report. The focused runner
also checks the planned budget, returned grid alignment and iteration count.

Reproduce the complete assigned computations from the session directory:

```bash
python3 computations/065_verify_exact_grid_solver.py
```

Run the new solver on a supplied operator and check its certificate:

```bash
python3 computations/065_exact_grid_solver.py examples/qso-boundary-operator.json --tolerance 1/100000000 > computations/065_example_certificate.json
python3 computations/qso_verify_certificate.py computations/065_example_certificate.json --operator examples/qso-boundary-operator.json
```

The example filename in those commands is a prospective reproducibility
output; the actually executed CLI output is `065_cli_certificate.json`.
Exit status zero from the new solver means a certificate was returned.
Invalid input uses argparse's status two. No completed valid-input search has
an optional "tolerance unmet" outcome.

The interior construction is symmetric under permutations of types `1,2,3`.
Every parent pair avoiding type zero assigns probability `3/4` to type zero
and distributes its remaining `1/4` equally over its other allowed offspring.
Parent pairs containing zero distribute probability equally over their
allowed offspring. On points `(a,b,b,b)`,
\[
V_0(x)=\frac34(1-a)^2,
\]
and the other outputs are equal. At `(1/3,2/9,2/9,2/9)`, this equals `1/3`,

oracle is outside the grid too. The runner independently verifies both
