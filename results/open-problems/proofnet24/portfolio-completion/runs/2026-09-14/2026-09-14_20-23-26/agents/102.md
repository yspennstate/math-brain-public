## 1. Hypotheses and common algebra

For \(n=3\) or \(n=4\), let
\[
\Delta_{n-1}=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^n:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\right\},
\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{a,b=1}^n P_{ab,k}x_ax_b.
\]
The full assumptions are
\[
P_{ab,k}=P_{ba,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ab,k}=1.
\]
There is no assumption of coefficient positivity, type-permutation symmetry, irreducibility, an interior fixed point, or strictly non-Volterra coefficients.

Define
\[
\delta(P)=\max_{a,b}\sum_{k\in\{a,b\}}P_{ab,k}.
\]
The braces mean a set. A repeated-parent row contributes \(P_{aa,a}\), once. A distinct-parent row contributes the sum \(P_{ab,a}+P_{ab,b}\). Bounding these two entries separately by \(d\) would not imply the required total bound \(d\).

Nonnegative coefficients imply \(V(x)\ge0\), and stochasticity gives
\[
\sum_kV_k(x)=\left(\sum_ax_a\right)^2=1
\]
on the simplex. Continuity and Brouwer therefore supply existence for every operator under discussion. Only uniqueness remains to be proved.

For simplex points \(x,y\), set \(m=(x+y)/2\), \(z=x-y\), and
\[
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_aP_{aj,k}m_a,\qquad J=2A(m).
\]
Expanding \(x_ax_b-y_ay_b=m_az_b+z_am_b\), and using symmetry in the two parents, gives the exact identity
\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A(m)z=Jz.
\]
Thus two fixed points satisfy \(z=Jz\), with \(\sum_i z_i=0\).

The entries of \(A(m)\) are nonnegative and each column sums to one. Consequently \(0\le J_{ij}\le2\). If \(\delta(P)\le d\), then each coefficient \(P_{ia,i}\le d\), including \(a=i\), and
\[
J_{ii}=2\sum_a m_aP_{ia,i}\le2d.
\]
All these statements hold at zero coordinates and on simplex faces.

## 2. Three-type uniqueness, with the equality case fully checked

Suppose \(x\ne y\) are three-type fixed points. A nonzero zero-sum vector in three coordinates has a singleton positive support or a singleton negative support. Switching \(x,y\) if needed, write \(z_i>0\) and \(z_j\le0\) for all \(j\ne i\). Zero entries of \(z\) are allowed.

For \(d\le1/2\),
\[
z_i=J_{ii}z_i+\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}z_j
\le J_{ii}z_i
\le2d\,z_i
\le z_i.
\]
For \(d<1/2\) this immediately contradicts \(z_i>0\).

At \(d=1/2\), every inequality in this chain must be equality. Thus
\[
J_{ii}=1,\qquad J_{ij}=0\quad\hbox{whenever }z_j<0.
\]
Since \(P_{ia,i}\le1/2\),
\[
0=\frac12-\sum_am_aP_{ia,i}
=\sum_am_a\left(\frac12-P_{ia,i}\right)
\]
is a sum of nonnegative terms. It follows that
\[
P_{ia,i}=\frac12\qquad\text{for every }a\text{ with }m_a>0.
\]

Choose \(j\) with \(z_j<0\), which exists because \(z\) is nonzero and zero-sum. Both \(m_i\) and \(m_j\) are strictly positive: a nonzero difference of two nonnegative coordinates has a positive midpoint. Saturation with \(a=j\) therefore gives \(P_{ij,i}=1/2\), and
\[
J_{ij}=2\sum_a m_aP_{aj,i}
\ge 2m_iP_{ij,i}=m_i>0.
\]
This contradicts the required equality \(J_{ij}=0\).

A coordinate that is zero in both candidate points has zero midpoint weight and need not satisfy the saturation conclusion; it is never selected as \(i\) or \(j\). The endpoint proof does not divide by a zero coordinate or require strict positivity of the tensor.

This proves the first claim, including \(\delta(P)=1/2\).

## 3. Boundary support and four-type boundary uniqueness

First establish the support fact used in report 060. For any fixed point \(p\), write \(S=\{a:p_a>0\}\). If \(r\notin S\), then
\[
0=p_r=\sum_{a,b\in S}P_{ab,r}p_ap_b.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, and every factor \(p_ap_b\) indexed by \(S\) is positive. Hence \(P_{ab,r}=0\) for all \(a,b\in S\). Thus the face spanned by \(S\) is invariant.

If \(|S|=1\), say \(S=\{i\}\), the fixed-point equation forces \(P_{ii,i}=1\), so \(\delta(P)=1\).

If \(|S|=2\), say \(S=\{i,j\}\), face invariance and stochasticity force the distinct-parent row \(ij\) to satisfy
\[
P_{ij,i}+P_{ij,j}=1.
\]
Again \(\delta(P)=1\). It is essential to use this distinct-parent row. The diagonal row \(ii\) might send all its offspring to \(j\), which would have zero parental leakage in that row.

Consequently every boundary fixed point of a four-type operator with \(\delta(P)<1\) has support exactly three. Let this support be \(S\), with omitted type \(r\). Its three-type restriction is stochastic, symmetric in the parents, and has leakage no greater than \(d\). The zero coefficients \(P_{ab,r}=0\) for \(a,b\in S\) have already been proved.

For any other fixed point \(x\), put \(t=x_r\). Only parent pairs containing \(r\) can produce output \(r\), and for each such pair its probability is at most \(d\). Counting the diagonal row once in the polynomial and the two parent orders for each distinct pair gives
\[
t=V_r(x)
\le d t^2+2dt\sum_{a\in S}x_a
=dt(2-t).
\]
If \(t>0\) and \(d\le1/2\), division by this positive \(t\) gives
\[
1\le d(2-t)\le1-\frac t2<1,
\]
a contradiction. If \(t=0\), no division is performed.

All fixed points lie in the same invariant three-type face. The proved three-type theorem, including its endpoint, then gives uniqueness there. This covers candidates on the same face as well as any initially proposed candidates on different faces. Existence was already given, or follows directly from the stipulated boundary fixed point.

## 4. Exact counterexample family and what is positive

Fix \(1/2<\eta<1\), and let the first three types be \(S=\{1,2,3\}\). Define the ten unordered rows as follows and use the same row in reversed parent order:

| Parents | Offspring row |
|---|---|
| \(ii,\ i\in S\) | \(1/2\) to each of the other two types of \(S\) |
| \(ij,\ i\ne j,\ i,j\in S\) | probability one to the remaining type of \(S\) |
| \(i4,\ i\in S\) | \(\eta\) to type 4 and \((1-\eta)/2\) to each member of \(S\setminus\{i\}\) |
| \(44\) | \(\eta\) to type 4 and \((1-\eta)/3\) to each member of \(S\) |

All omitted coefficients are zero. Each row is nonnegative and sums to one. The six rows entirely within \(S\) have zero parental leakage. The three rows \(i4\) have leakage \(\eta+0=\eta\), and row \(44\) has leakage \(\eta\), counted once. Hence the exact total leakage is \(\eta\).

The coefficients give
\[
V_4(x)=\eta x_4^2+2\eta x_4(x_1+x_2+x_3)
=\eta x_4(2-x_4)
\]
on the simplex. The tensor is equivariant under every permutation of the first three types, so a point
\[
u(t)=((1-t)/3,(1-t)/3,(1-t)/3,t)
\]
has an image with its first three coordinates equal. Since the image sums to one, \(u(t)\) is fixed whenever its fourth coordinate is fixed.

The scalar equation \(t=\eta t(2-t)\) has \(t=0\) and \(t=2-1/\eta\). Therefore
\[
p=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0),\qquad
q_\eta=\left(\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},
\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},2-\frac1\eta\right)
\]
are fixed. For the full open parameter interval, \(1<1/\eta<2\). Thus every coordinate of \(q_\eta\) is strictly positive, \(0<(q_\eta)_4<1\), its coordinates sum to one, and \(q_\eta\ne p\).

At \(\eta=3/5\), the second point is exactly \((2/9,2/9,2/9,1/3)\). At \(\eta=1/2\), the second formula collapses to \(p\), so it does not contradict the endpoint theorem. As \(\eta\) reaches 1, the formula becomes the fourth vertex; strict interior positivity was asserted only for \(\eta<1\).

The JavaScript certificate independently constructs all ten rows, checks their symbolic sums and exact leakages, and verifies both full four-coordinate identities \(V(p)=p\) and \(V(q_\eta)=q_\eta\) as Laurent-polynomial identities. Row coefficients are affine in \(\eta\), and nonnegativity at 0 and 1 verifies their nonnegativity throughout the parameter range. General positivity of \(q_\eta\) rests on the elementary inequalities above, not a numerical sample.

This establishes sharpness from above of the boundary-specific threshold. It does not classify all fixed points of the family or determine the uniform four-type threshold.

## 5. Uniform four-type uniqueness through \(7/50\)

Assume \(\delta(P)\le d=7/50\). Using this fixed upper bound covers all smaller leakages as well; no unstated monotonicity of a final optimized formula is needed. Set
\[
Q=\frac{17}{40},\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{1-2d}{3-2d}=\frac9{34},\qquad
R=1-2Q=\frac3{20}.
\]

### 5.1 Coordinate cap

If \(x_i=u\), the total ordered parent-pair weight containing type \(i\) is \(2u-u^2\), and the complementary weight is \((1-u)^2\). Output into \(i\) has coefficient at most \(d\) on the first collection and at most 1 on the second. Hence
\[
V_i(x)\le d(2u-u^2)+(1-u)^2
=d+(1-d)(1-u)^2=:g(u).
\]
The derivative of \(g(u)-u\) is
\[
-2(1-d)(1-u)-1<0\qquad (0\le u\le1).
\]
The exact calculation is
\[
Q-g(Q)=\frac{53}{80000}>0.
\]
For a fixed point, \(g(x_i)-x_i\ge0\). Strict decrease therefore implies \(x_i<Q\). Every fixed point, and every midpoint of two fixed points, belongs to the closed capped simplex
\[
K_Q=\{m\in\Delta_3:m_i\le Q\text{ for every }i\}.
\]

### 5.2 Sign pattern and weights

Suppose distinct fixed points exist. Since \(2d<1\), the singleton-sign argument in Section 2 immediately excludes either a singleton positive support or a singleton negative support for \(z=x-y\). In four coordinates, each sign must therefore occur exactly twice, and there are no zero entries.

Let \(G,H\) be the positive and negative pairs. Define
\[
h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=\sum_{j\in H}(-z_j)>0,\qquad
\alpha_i=z_i/h,\quad \beta_j=-z_j/h.
\]
Both pairs of weights are positive and sum to one. For \(i\in G\) with companion \(k\in G\), the identity \(z=Jz\) gives
\[
z_i\le J_{ii}z_i+J_{ik}z_k\le2dz_i+2z_k.
\]
Applying the same estimate with \(i,k\) interchanged yields
\[
(1-2d)z_i\le2z_k,\qquad (1-2d)z_k\le2z_i.
\]
After dividing by \(h>0\), these are precisely
\[
\varepsilon\le\alpha_i,\alpha_k\le1-\varepsilon.
\]
Applying this reasoning to \(-z\) proves the same interval for each \(\beta_j\).

Each signed coordinate thus carries at least \(\varepsilon\) of its own sign's total mass. These inequalities follow from being a fixed-point difference; they are not hypotheses satisfied by arbitrary simplex differences.

### 5.3 Complete capped-simplex weighted-minimum reduction

For fixed admissible weights, define
\[
F(m)=\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\alpha_i\beta_j\min(m_i,m_j).
\]
The minimum of two linear functions is concave, and each multiplier is nonnegative, so \(F\) is concave in \(m\).

Every vertex of \(K_Q\) has at most one coordinate strictly between 0 and \(Q\): if two did, a small opposite perturbation of them would express that point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points. Since
\[
2Q<1<3Q,\qquad 0<R<Q,
\]
the vertices are exactly the 12 permutations of \((Q,Q,R,0)\). Conversely each such permutation has three coordinates at box bounds and is a vertex.

There are only two placements of the two entries \(Q\), up to relabeling within pairs and swapping \(G,H\).

* If both \(Q\)'s lie in one pair, each cross minimum equals the coordinate in the other pair. Hence \(F\) is the weight of its \(R\) coordinate times \(R\), so \(F\ge\varepsilon R\).
* If the \(Q\)'s split between pairs, label the pairs \((Q,R)\) and \((Q,0)\). If \(a,b\) are their respective weights on \(Q\), then
  \[
  F=b[aQ+(1-a)R]\ge bR\ge\varepsilon R.
  \]

For a convex decomposition \(m=\sum_vt_vv\) into cap vertices, concavity gives
\[
F(m)\ge\sum_vt_vF(v)\ge\varepsilon R.
\]
Thus, for every capped midpoint and every admissible pair of weights,
\[
F(m)\ge\varepsilon R=\frac{27}{680}.
\]

This is an exact continuum proof. The lower bound is attained on the closed cap by putting both \(Q\)'s in one pair and assigning weight \(\varepsilon\) to the \(R\) coordinate in the other. An actual nonzero fixed-point difference has all midpoint coordinates positive; allowing the larger closed cap only weakens the bound and is harmless.

For the independent finite certificate, vertices were generated from active box constraints, not supplied as a permutation list. With fixed \(m\), \(F\) is affine in either free pair weight when the other is fixed. Minimizing over their rectangle can therefore be done successively at endpoints. This justifies the 12 vertices times 4 weight corners, giving 48 exact checks and 8 equality corners. It is not an inference from sampling.

### 5.4 Transfer inequality with the retained negative term

For \(i\in G,j\in H\), let \(k\) and \(\ell\) be their companions in \(G\) and \(H\), respectively. Put
\[
p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}.
\]
The distinct-parent pair \(\{k,i\}\) equals \(G\), so \(p_{ki}\le d\). The pair \(\{\ell,j\}\) equals \(H\), whose total output probability into its parental set is at most \(d\), so \(p_{\ell j}\ge1-d\). This is the exact step requiring the sum of the two parental coefficients to be bounded by \(d\).

Using parent symmetry and \(0\le p_{ab}\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{ij}
&:=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)\\
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
 +m_k(p_{ki}-p_{kj})+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-p_{\ell j})\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}+d(m_k+m_\ell)\\
&\le\max(m_i,m_j)+d(1-m_i-m_j)\\
&=d+(1-d)\max(m_i,m_j)-d\min(m_i,m_j).
\end{aligned}
\]
All multiplier signs are correct. In particular, the diagonal-parent group probability \(p_{ii}\) was bounded by 1, not incorrectly by \(d\); offspring of type \(k\) may contribute to this group without being parental in row \(ii\).

The normalized difference admits the exact product-weight decomposition
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\alpha_i\beta_j(e_i-e_j).
\]
Its weights sum to one. Since \(\max(m_i,m_j)\le Q\) and the weighted minimum is at least \(\varepsilon R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_jC_{ij}
&\le d+(1-d)Q-d\varepsilon R\\
&=\frac{1011}{2000}-\frac{189}{34000}\\
&=\frac{8499}{17000}
=\frac12-\frac1{17000}<\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
Finally \(z=2A(m)z\) gives
\[
h=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}z
=2h\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_jC_{ij}
\le\frac{8499}{8500}h<h.
\]
This contradiction proves uniqueness at the closed endpoint \(d=7/50\), and Brouwer proves existence.
