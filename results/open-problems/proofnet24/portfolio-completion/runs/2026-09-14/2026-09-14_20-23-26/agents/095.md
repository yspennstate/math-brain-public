Fix \(m\ge3\), \(n=m-1\), and an \(n\times m\) matrix \(X\) with iid entries from one fixed centered, variance-one real law with bounded density. Let \(\Pi(X)\) be its orthogonal null-line projector. Bounded density ensures rank \(n\) almost surely. The geometric target is
\[
R_D=E_A\operatorname{tr}(M_A^2)\le C_\xi/D,\qquad
M_A=E[\Pi\mid A],\quad |A|=m-D.
\]
Two independent original-row completions of the actual shared rows \(A\) give this identity. It is an average over the shared block; it does not assert a bound for every deterministic realized apex. The projected private-row coordinates are never declared independent.

Write
\[
\Pi=\sum_{S\subset[n]}\Pi_S,\qquad
W_s=\sum_{|S|=s}E\|\Pi_S\|_F^2,\qquad
W_s\ge0,\quad\sum_{s=0}^nW_s=1.
\]
Row permutation symmetry gives
\[
R_D=\sum_{s=0}^n
\frac{\binom{m-D}{s}}{\binom ns}W_s.
\tag{1}
\]
Here and below a binomial coefficient is zero when its lower index exceeds its nonnegative upper index.

## 2. The universal anchor from independent columns — PROVED

We first prove a more general statement. Let \(X=[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\) have iid columns in \(\mathbb R^r\), with any probability law. Let \(P\) project onto \(\ker X\), and put
\[
\bar k=E\dim\ker X,\qquad M=EP.
\]
No finite moments or fixed-rank hypothesis are required for the following conclusions:
\[
M_{ij}\le0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
\frac{\bar k^2}{m}\le\|M\|_F^2
\le\frac{\bar k^2}{m-1},
\tag{2}
\]
and
\[
\left\|M-\frac{\bar k}{m}I_m\right\|_F^2
\le\frac{\bar k^2}{m(m-1)}.
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof of the off-diagonal sign.** For \(\lambda>0\), consider the ridge hat matrix
\[
H_\lambda=X^\top(\lambda I_r+XX^\top)^{-1}X.
\]
Fix columns 1 and 2 and condition on all other columns. Set
\[
A=\lambda I_r+\sum_{j=3}^m x_jx_j^\top,\quad
u=A^{-1/2}x_1,\quad v=A^{-1/2}x_2.
\]
Conditionally, \(u,v\) are iid. A rank-two inverse identity gives
\[
(H_\lambda)_{12}
=k(u,v):=
\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}
{(1+\|u\|^2)(1+\|v\|^2)-\langle u,v\rangle^2}.
\tag{4}
\]
This identity follows, for example, by inverting the \(2\times2\) matrix
\(I_2+[u,v]^\top[u,v]\).

Put \(a=1+\|u\|^2\), \(b=1+\|v\|^2\), and \(t=\langle u,v\rangle\). Since \(t^2<ab\),
\[
k(u,v)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
\left\langle
\frac{u^{\otimes(2\ell+1)}}{(1+\|u\|^2)^{\ell+1}},
\frac{v^{\otimes(2\ell+1)}}{(1+\|v\|^2)^{\ell+1}}
\right\rangle.
\tag{5}
\]
Each tensor-valued feature in this formula has norm at most one, so its expectation exists without moments. Every partial sum has the sign of \(t\), and
\[
|\text{partial sum}|\le|k(u,v)|\le\frac12.
\]
Indeed \(ab-t^2\ge1+\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2\ge2|t|\). Dominated convergence and conditional iid factorization therefore yield
\[
E[k(u,v)\mid x_3,\ldots,x_m]
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
\left\|
E\left[
\frac{u^{\otimes(2\ell+1)}}{(1+\|u\|^2)^{\ell+1}}
\,\middle|\, x_3,\ldots,x_m
\right]\right\|^2
\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
As \(\lambda\downarrow0\), \(I_m-H_\lambda\to P\) by the singular-value decomposition. The matrices are positive contractions, so bounded convergence proves \(EP_{12}\le0\). Exchangeability proves the same for every off-diagonal entry.

**Proof of the anchor and stability bounds.** Column exchangeability makes all diagonal entries \(d=\bar k/m\) and all off-diagonal entries a common \(c\le0\). With
\[
e=m^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)^\top,
\]
the eigenvalue of \(M\) on \(e\) is
\[
\alpha=d+(m-1)c,\qquad 0\le\alpha\le\bar k/m,
\]
and its other \(m-1\) eigenvalues equal
\[
\beta=\frac{\bar k-\alpha}{m-1}.
\]
Thus
\[
\|M\|_F^2
=\alpha^2+\frac{(\bar k-\alpha)^2}{m-1}
=\frac{\bar k^2}{m}
+\frac{m}{m-1}\left(\alpha-\frac{\bar k}{m}\right)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
This proves (2) and (3).

In the P24 model the columns are iid and \(\bar k=1\), proving
\[
R_m=W_0=\|E\Pi\|_F^2\in[1/m,1/(m-1)]
\tag{8}
\]
and the stability estimate in the opening paragraph. In particular,
\[
0\le R_m-\frac1m\le\frac1{m(m-1)}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is an exact finite-dimensional theorem for the actual matrix model.

## 3. Sharpness and the scope of anchor stability — PROVED

The upper bounds (8)–(9) are sharp over the class of centered bounded-density variance-one laws, already at each fixed \(m\).

Let \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), let \(U\) be uniform on \([-1,1]\), and let \(B_\varepsilon\), independent of \(U\), have distribution
\[
B_\varepsilon=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{with probability }1-\varepsilon,\\
-(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon,&\text{with probability }\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
\xi_\varepsilon
=\frac{B_\varepsilon+\varepsilon U}
{\sqrt{(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2/3}}.
\]
This law is centered, variance one, and has bounded density for every fixed \(\varepsilon\).

With probability at least \(1-m(m-1)\varepsilon\), all Bernoulli choices in \(X\) equal 1. On that event, scalar normalization does not affect the null space, so the rows have entries \(1+\varepsilon U_{ij}\). For a unit null vector \(v\), its first-row equation gives
\[
|e^\top v|
=\frac{\varepsilon|\sum_j U_{1j}v_j|}{\sqrt m}
\le\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently
\[
0\le\alpha=E[e^\top\Pi e]
\le\varepsilon^2+m(m-1)\varepsilon\longrightarrow0
\]
as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) at fixed \(m\). Formula (7) proves convergence to equality in both upper bounds. This is uniform-in-law sharpness; it does not claim that a single fixed centered law attains this asymptotic defect as \(m\to\infty\).

A useful distinction for approximation arguments is the following exact identity. For any random rank-one projector \(Q\),
\[
\|EQ\|_F^2
=\frac1m+\left\|EQ-\frac{I_m}{m}\right\|_F^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus an arbitrary comparison argument needs mean-projector error \(O(m^{-1/2})\) to retain an \(O(m^{-1})\) anchor; qualitative \(o(1)\) convergence is insufficient. This threshold is sharp: a mixture of a Haar projector with weight \(1-a_m\) and a fixed projector \(ee^\top\) with weight \(a_m\) has anchor
\[
\frac1m+a_m^2(1-1/m).
\]
It is \(O(m^{-1})\) exactly when \(a_m=O(m^{-1/2})\). This mixture is a counterexample to an unrestricted projector-law transfer, not to the iid-entry theorem (8).

## 4. Complete ANOVA, reveal, and smoothing equivalence — PROVED

Define
\[
\delta_D=R_{D-1}-R_D,\quad 2\le D\le m,
\]
and, at dyadic integers \(2\le q<m\),
\[
\eta_q=1-\frac{q}{4n},\qquad
B_m(q)=E\left\|
T_{\sqrt{\eta_q}}\Pi(X)
-T_{\sqrt{\eta_q}}\Pi(X^{(1)})
\right\|_F^2.
\]
Here \(X^{(1)}\) replaces one original row, and \(T_\rho\) retains each original row independently with probability \(\rho\).

The following four statements are equivalent up to universal constants, uniformly in \(m\):

- \(R_D=O(D^{-1})\) for all \(D\).
- \(\delta_D=O(D^{-2})\) for all \(D\ge2\).
- \(B_m(q)=O(q^{-2})\) at the indicated dyadic \(q\).
- \(\sum_{s=0}^{k}W_s=O((k+1)/m)\) for \(0\le k\le n\).

No unproved anchor assumption occurs in this equivalence for iid-entry null projectors.

**Exact identities.** For \(M_j=E[\Pi\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j]\), conditional expectation removes ANOVA terms involving unrevealed rows. Row symmetry and orthogonality give
\[
E\|M_j\|_F^2
=\sum_s\frac{\binom js}{\binom ns}W_s
=R_{m-j},
\]
and
\[
\delta_D=E\|M_{m-D+1}-M_{m-D}\|_F^2
=\sum_{s\ge1}\frac{\binom{m-D}{s-1}}{\binom ns}W_s.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence \(\delta_D\) is nonnegative and nonincreasing in \(D\).

The noise operator multiplies \(\Pi_S\) by \(\rho^{|S|}\). If
\(N_m(\theta)=\sum_s\theta^sW_s\), row exchangeability and conditional iid resampling imply
\[
B_m(q)=\frac2n\sum_{s\ge1}s\eta_q^sW_s
=\frac{2\eta_q}{n}N_m'(\eta_q).
\tag{12}
\]
For \(H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,1-\theta)\), the binomial factorial-moment identity gives
\[
N_m'(\theta)=nE[\delta_{H+2}],
\tag{13}
\]
since \(E\binom{n-1-H}{s-1}=\binom{n-1}{s-1}\theta^{s-1}\).
Thus
\[
B_m(q)=2\eta_q E[\delta_{H_q+2}],
\quad
H_q\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(n-1,\frac{q}{4n}\right).
\tag{14}
\]

**Purity to reveal energy.** Suppose \(R_D\le C/D\). Monotonicity of \(\delta_D\) gives
\[
\lfloor D/2\rfloor\,\delta_D
\le R_{\lceil D/2\rceil}-R_D
\le\frac C{\lceil D/2\rceil},
\]
so \(\delta_D\le5C/D^2\).

**Reveal energy to purity.** If \(\delta_D\le V/D^2\), the anchor (8) and
\(k^{-2}\le[k(k-1)]^{-1}\) give
\[
R_D\le\frac1{m-1}
+V\left(\frac1D-\frac1m\right)
\le\frac{\max(3/2,V)}D.
\tag{15}
\]

**Reveal energy to smoothed influence.** Put \(p=q/(4n)\) and
\(\mu=(n-1)p\ge q/8\). For \(H\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,p)\),
\[
E(H+2)^{-2}
\le E\frac1{(H+1)(H+2)}
=\int_0^1t(1-pt)^{n-1}\,dt
\le\mu^{-2}\le64/q^2.
\]
Equation (14) yields \(B_m(q)\le128V/q^2\).

**Smoothed influence to reveal energy.** Assume \(B_m(q)\le S/q^2\) at dyadic \(q\). Since \(\eta_q\ge3/4\),
\[
E\delta_{H_q+2}\le2S/(3q^2).
\]
For \(D\ge8\), take the largest dyadic \(q<D\); then \(D/2\le q<D\), including \(D=m\). We have
\(E(H_q+2)\le q/4+2\le D/2\).
Markov's inequality gives \(P(H_q+2\le D)\ge1/2\). Monotonicity now gives
\[
\delta_D\le2E\delta_{H_q+2}
\le\frac{16S}{3D^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(2\le D\le7\), \(\delta_D\le1\le49/D^2\).

Explicitly, with
\[
P=\sup_D DR_D,\quad V=\sup_{D\ge2}D^2\delta_D,\quad
S=\sup_{q\text{ dyadic}}q^2B_m(q),
\]
we have
\[
V\le5P,\qquad P\le\max(3/2,V),\qquad
S\le128V,\qquad V\le\max(49,16S/3).
\tag{17}
\]

**Cumulative ANOVA equivalence.** Put \(F(k)=\sum_{s=0}^kW_s\).
If \(R_D\le C/D\), choose
\(D=1+\lfloor n/[2(k+1)]\rfloor\).
A uniform \(s\)-subset avoids any fixed set of \(D-1\) rows with probability at least
\(1-s(D-1)/n\ge1/2\) for \(s\le k\). Therefore
\[
F(k)\le2R_D\le6C(k+1)/m.
\]
Conversely, suppose \(F(k)\le A(k+1)/m\). For \(D\ge2\), set
\(r=1-(D-1)/n\). The coefficient in (1) is at most \(r^s\).
Summation by parts gives
\[
R_D\le\sum_s r^sW_s
=(1-r)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}r^kF(k)+r^nF(n)
\le\frac A m\sum_{k=0}^nr^k
\le\frac A{m(1-r)}
\le\frac{2A}D.
\]
The case \(D=1\) follows from \(F(n)=1\le A\).

## 5. The necessary powers are Gaussian-sharp — PROVED

For Gaussian original rows, two private null lines are conditionally independent Haar lines in the actual residual \(D\)-space. Hence
\[
R_D^{\rm G}=1/D,\qquad
\delta_D^{\rm G}=\frac1{D(D-1)}.
\]
The triangular system (1), or its elementary binomial identity, gives
\(W_s^{\rm G}=1/m\) for every \(0\le s\le n\). Therefore
\[
B_m^{\rm G}(q)
=\frac2{nm}\sum_{s=1}^n s\eta_q^s.
\tag{18}
\]
Uniformly for all integers \(2\le q<m\), not only dyadic ones,
\[
\frac1{8q^2}\le B_m^{\rm G}(q)\le\frac{32}{q^2}.
\tag{19}
\]
For the upper bound, extend the sum to infinity and use
\(\sum_{s\ge1}s\eta^s=\eta/(1-\eta)^2\).
For the lower bound, set \(L=\lfloor n/q\rfloor\ge n/(2q)\).
For \(s\le L\), Bernoulli's inequality gives
\(\eta_q^s\ge1-sq/(4n)\ge3/4\).
Thus the left side of (18) is at least
\(3L^2/(4nm)\ge3n/(16mq^2)\ge1/(8q^2)\).

Consequently no estimate \(o(q^{-2})\) uniformly along \(q\to\infty\), or \(O(q^{-2-\epsilon})\), can replace the desired smoothed one-row scale for a class containing Gaussian rows. Total smoothed influence is \(nB_m(q)/2\), so its necessary scale is \(m/q^2\). The necessary reveal power is \(D^{-2}\), and the necessary purity power is \(D^{-1}\).

At zero smoothing, the Gaussian one-row resampling square is exactly 1:
\[
\frac2n\sum_{s=1}^n\frac{s}{m}=1.
\]
Its total Efron–Stein influence equals \(n/2\). A small raw one-row influence is therefore an invalid replacement target.

## 6. What these equivalences cannot prove

An arbitrary nonnegative normalized spectrum need not satisfy the required bounds. The source's spectrum
\[
W_0=1/m,\quad W_1=1-1/m,\quad W_s=0\ (s\ge2)
\]
has the correct anchor but violates the cumulative ANOVA bound already at \(k=1\). At \(q\) comparable to \(m\), its one-row smoothed square is of order \(m^{-1}\), instead of \(m^{-2}\).

Conversely, all influence and reveal-increment quantities annihilate the constant ANOVA component. A constant rank-one projector has zero influence and anchor 1. Thus the anchor cannot be recovered from influence identities alone.

The column-independence hypothesis is substantive: independent Gaussian rows supported on a fixed \((m-1)\)-dimensional hyperplane have a deterministic null line, anchor 1, and zero smoothed influence. These rows have dependent coordinates and a singular ambient law, so they do not satisfy the source's iid-entry hypotheses.

Finally, after conditioning on arbitrary shared rows and projecting, the residual matrix generally lacks independent columns. The anchor theorem cannot be reapplied to that residual matrix to conclude \(M_A=O(D^{-1})\). This is exactly where the unresolved null-space geometry re-enters.
