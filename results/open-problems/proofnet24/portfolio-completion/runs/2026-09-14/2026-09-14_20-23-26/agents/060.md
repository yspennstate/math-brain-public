## Definitions, inputs, and common algebra

For $n=3$ or $n=4$, let
\[
\Delta_{n-1}=\{x\in[0,\infty)^n:\ \sum_i x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{a,b=1}^nP_{ab,k}x_ax_b,
\]
where
\[
P_{ab,k}=P_{ba,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ab,k}=1.
\]
The exact leakage convention throughout is
\[
\delta(P)=\max_{a,b}\sum_{k\in\{a,b\}}P_{ab,k}.
\tag{1}
\]
The braces denote a set: when $a=b$, the row leakage is $P_{aa,a}$, counted
once. When $a\ne b$, it is the **sum** $P_{ab,a}+P_{ab,b}$. A hypothesis
bounding the two coefficients separately would not justify the later two-type
cut estimate.

For simplex points $x,y$, write
\[
m=(x+y)/2,\qquad z=x-y,\qquad
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_aP_{aj,k}m_a,\qquad J=2A(m)=DV(m).
\]
Parent symmetry gives the exact quadratic polarization identity
\[
V(x)-V(y)=Jz.
\tag{2}
\]
The matrix $A(m)$ is nonnegative with column sums one. If leakage is bounded
by $d$, then
\[
0\le J_{ij}\le2,\qquad
J_{ii}=2\sum_aP_{ia,i}m_a\le2d.
\tag{3}
\]
These statements include zero coordinates and diagonal parent pairs.

## Claim 1 — three types, including the endpoint $d=1/2$

Existence follows from Brouwer's fixed-point theorem: $V$ is continuous and
maps the compact simplex into itself.

Suppose $x\ne y$ are fixed points. Their difference $z$ is nonzero with
coordinate sum zero. In three coordinates, either its positive support or its
negative support is a singleton. Interchange $x,y$, if needed, so that
\[
z_i>0,\qquad z_j\le0\quad(j\ne i).
\]
This statement allows a zero component of $z$. Equations (2)--(3) imply
\[
z_i=J_{ii}z_i+\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}z_j
\le J_{ii}z_i\le2d\,z_i\le z_i.
\tag{4}
\]
If $d<1/2$, the last inequalities give an immediate strict contradiction.

It remains to exclude equality when $d=1/2$. Equality in (4) requires

- $J_{ii}=1$; and
- $J_{ij}=0$ for every $j$ with $z_j<0$.

But $J_{ii}=1$ says
\[
0=\frac12-\sum_a m_aP_{ia,i}
=\sum_a m_a\left(\frac12-P_{ia,i}\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Every summand is nonnegative. Hence $P_{ia,i}=1/2$ for every $a$ with
$m_a>0$. There is a $j$ with $z_j<0$, because $z$ sums to zero. Then
$m_j>0$, and also $m_i>0$ because $z_i>0$. Thus (5), symmetry of the parent
indices, and nonnegativity give
\[
J_{ij}=2\sum_aP_{aj,i}m_a
\ge2P_{ij,i}m_i=m_i>0,
\]
contradicting the second equality requirement.

This proves uniqueness for all $d\le1/2$. A coordinate that is zero in both
states has midpoint weight zero and contributes nothing to (5); the chosen
strictly positive and negative difference coordinates always have positive
midpoint weights. No irreducibility or coefficient positivity is used.

## Claim 2 — four-type boundary uniqueness and sharpness from above

### Support sizes one and two really are excluded

More generally, a fixed point cannot have support of size at most two if
$\delta(P)<1$.

If its support is the singleton $\{i\}$, it is the vertex $e_i$, so its
fixed-point equation forces $P_{ii,i}=1$.

If its support is $\{i,j\}$, with both coordinates positive, every missing
output coordinate has a zero nonnegative sum in its fixed-point equation.
Consequently, each parent row with both parents in $\{i,j\}$ is supported
inside this same set. In particular, the **distinct-parent** row $ij$ satisfies
\[
P_{ij,i}+P_{ij,j}=1,
\]
which violates (1) when $\delta(P)<1$. It is important to specify this row:
a diagonal row $ii$ could output type $j$ without parental leakage, and
would not by itself prove the exclusion.

### The missing-coordinate estimate, including $d=1/2$

Now let $p$ be a boundary fixed point of a four-type operator with leakage at
most $d\le1/2$. The support exclusion shows that $p$ has support exactly
three, say $S$, with missing type $r$. Since every $p_a$, $a\in S$, is
strictly positive,
\[
0=p_r=V_r(p)=\sum_{a,b\in S}P_{ab,r}p_ap_b
\]
forces $P_{ab,r}=0$ for all $a,b\in S$. Thus the three-type face is
invariant, and its restricted coefficient rows remain stochastic and satisfy
the same total leakage bound.

For any other fixed point $x$, the only remaining parent rows capable of
producing type $r$ contain that type as a parent. Each such output probability
is at most $d$. Therefore, with $t=x_r$,
\[
t=V_r(x)
\le d\left(t^2+2t\sum_{a\in S}x_a\right)
=dt(2-t).
\tag{6}
\]
If $t>0$, division gives
\[
1\le d(2-t)\le\frac{2-t}{2}=1-\frac t2<1,
\]
a contradiction. This strict inequality works at $d=1/2$; no division by a
possibly zero coordinate occurs in the $t=0$ case.

Every fixed point therefore lies in the same invariant three-type face, where
Claim 1 gives uniqueness. This also covers the possibility that two candidate
fixed points lie on the same face. Merely counting intersections of distinct
faces would not have covered that case.

### Exact family showing sharpness of this boundary threshold

Fix $\eta\in(1/2,1)$. Define the ten unordered parent rows as follows;
unlisted coefficients are zero, and reverse parent orders use the same row.

| Parent row | Offspring probabilities |
|---|---|
| $ii$, $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ | $1/2$ to each of the other two types in $\{1,2,3\}$ |
| $ij$, distinct $i,j\in\{1,2,3\}$ | Probability one to the remaining type in $\{1,2,3\}$ |
| $i4$, $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ | $\eta$ to type $4$; $(1-\eta)/2$ to each of the two types in $\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}$ |
| $44$ | $\eta$ to type $4$; $(1-\eta)/3$ to each of types $1,2,3$ |

All coefficients are nonnegative and every row sums to one. Rows entirely
inside $\{1,2,3\}$ have zero parental leakage. Rows containing type $4$
have parental leakage exactly $\eta$, including the row $44$, where the
parent is counted once. Thus the operator's exact leakage is $\eta$.

On the simplex,
\[
V_4(x)=\eta x_4^2+2\eta x_4(x_1+x_2+x_3)
=\eta x_4(2-x_4).
\tag{7}
\]
The operator is invariant under permutations of the first three types. Hence
it maps a point $((1-t)/3,(1-t)/3,(1-t)/3,t)$ to another point whose first
three coordinates are equal. If its fourth coordinate is unchanged, the first
three are also unchanged because the output sums to one.

The two scalar fixed points of (7) are $t=0$ and $t=2-1/\eta$, giving the
exact distinct fixed points
\[
p=\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13,0\right),\qquad
q_\eta=\left(\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},
\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},\frac{1/\eta-1}{3},2-\frac1\eta\right).
\tag{8}
\]
For $1/2<\eta<1$, every coordinate of $q_\eta$ is strictly positive.
For example, $\eta=3/5$ gives
$q_\eta=(2/9,2/9,2/9,1/3)$.

This proves failure of uniform uniqueness immediately above $1/2$, even
under the additional hypothesis that a boundary fixed point exists. It does
not determine what happens for four-type operators with only interior fixed
points and leakage in $(7/50,1/2]$.

## Claim 3 — uniform four-type uniqueness through $7/50$

Assume throughout this section that $\delta(P)\le d=7/50$, and set the
following rational constants:
\[
Q=\frac{17}{40},\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac{1-2d}{3-2d}=\frac9{34},\qquad
R=1-2Q=\frac3{20}.
\tag{9}
\]
Write \(K_Q=\{u\in\Delta_3:\max_i u_i\le Q\}\) for the closed capped simplex.

### A rational coordinate cap

For a simplex point with $x_i=u$, the total ordered-pair weight containing
parent $i$ is $1-(1-u)^2$. The output probability of type $i$ is at most
$d$ on those pairs and at most one on all other pairs. Therefore
\[
V_i(x)\le g(u):=d+(1-d)(1-u)^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The function $g(u)-u$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,1]$, and exactly
\[
Q-g(Q)=\frac{53}{80000}>0.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently, any fixed point has $x_i<Q$ for every $i$; in particular it
lies in the closed cap $K_Q$. The midpoint of any two fixed points also lies
in $K_Q$.

### Each sign must carry a definite fraction of the difference

Suppose for contradiction that $x\ne y$ are fixed points. A singleton
positive or negative support of $z=x-y$ is impossible by (3)--(4), since
$2d<1$. In four coordinates the only remaining sign pattern is exactly two
strictly positive and two strictly negative entries, with no zero entries.
Write these two pairs as $G,H$, and put
\[
h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=\sum_{j\in H}(-z_j)>0,\qquad
\alpha_i=z_i/h,\quad\beta_j=(-z_j)/h.
\]
Both pairs of weights sum to one.

For $i\in G$, let $k$ be its companion in $G$. Using $z=Jz$ and
discarding the nonpositive contributions from $H$ gives
\[
z_i\le J_{ii}z_i+J_{ik}z_k\le2d\,z_i+2z_k.
\]
Thus $(1-2d)z_i\le2z_k$. Applying this with the two indices interchanged
gives
\[
\alpha_i,\alpha_k\ge\frac{1-2d}{3-2d}=\varepsilon.
\tag{12}
\]
Apply the same argument to $-z$ to obtain
$\beta_j\ge\varepsilon$ for both $j\in H$. These restrictions are specific
to a putative fixed-point difference; they are not available for arbitrary
differences of simplex points.

### Weighted-minimum bound on the capped simplex

For any $m\in K_Q$, and any weights on $G,H$ satisfying (12),
\[
\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\alpha_i\beta_j\min(m_i,m_j)
\ge\varepsilon R.
\tag{13}
\]
Here is a complete reduction and proof. For fixed weights, the left side is
a concave function of $m$: each $\min(m_i,m_j)$ is the minimum of two linear
functions, hence concave. Its minimum on the compact capped simplex is therefore
attained at a vertex. At a vertex there cannot be two coordinates strictly
between zero and $Q$, since one could perturb them in opposite directions.
Since $2Q<1<3Q$, all vertices are permutations of $(Q,Q,R,0)$, and $R<Q$.

There are two cases, using the symmetry between $G$ and $H$.

- If both entries $Q$ belong to $G$, every cross minimum is the corresponding
  coordinate in $H$. The weighted sum is the weight on the $R$ coordinate
  times $R$, and that weight is at least $\varepsilon$.
- If one entry $Q$ belongs to each pair, relabel so that the pairs are
  $(Q,R)$ and $(Q,0)$. If their weights on $Q$ are $a,b$, respectively,
  the weighted sum is $b[aQ+(1-a)R]\ge bR\ge\varepsilon R$.

Thus (13) holds at every vertex and, by concavity and convex decomposition,
throughout $K_Q$. The argument includes zero coordinates of $m$, though
a putative difference here in fact forces all four midpoint coordinates positive.

### Retain the negative minimum term in the two-pair transfer estimate

For $i\in G,j\in H$, let $k\in G\setminus\{i\}$ and
$\ell\in H\setminus\{j\}$. Set $p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}$.
The total leakage hypothesis gives
\[
p_{ki}\le d,\qquad p_{\ell j}\ge1-d.
\]
The other group probabilities lie in $[0,1]$. Expanding the two columns of
$A(m)$, using parent symmetry, and bounding the four terms gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
 +m_k(p_{ki}-p_{kj})+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-p_{\ell j})\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}+d(m_k+m_\ell)\\
&\le\max(m_i,m_j)+d[1-m_i-m_j]\\
&=d+(1-d)\max(m_i,m_j)-d\min(m_i,m_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The old uniform transfer bound dropped the last, nonpositive term. Here the
sign weights provide a uniform lower bound on its weighted contribution.

Indeed, $z=h\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_j(e_i-e_j)$, with all summation indices
in $G\times H$. Combining $m\in K_Q$, (13), and (14) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_j\,
 \mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)
&\le d+(1-d)Q-d\varepsilon R\\
&=\frac{8499}{17000}<\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
All constants are exact; in particular
$\varepsilon R=27/680$. Finally, polarization and the fixed-point equations
give
\[
h=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}z
=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}(2A(m)z)
\le\frac{8499}{8500}h<h,
\]
a contradiction. Brouwer supplies existence. This proves Claim 3.

The endpoint is a genuine enlargement because $43/50<\sqrt3/2$, hence
$1-\sqrt3/2<7/50$. The rational cap $Q$ was chosen for a short exact proof;
no optimization of $7/50$, $Q$, or the comparison factor is claimed.

## Exploratory route, failures, and precise limitations

with two prescribed fixed points. Its full reproducible code and raw result are

- `computations/060_leakage_search.py`;
- `computations/060_leakage_search_output.json`.

For $x=m+tz$, $y=m-tz$, the script imposes row stochasticity, the total
parental leakage constraints, and the two fixed-point equations in the equivalent
midpoint/divided-difference form. It minimizes leakage by a linear program for
each proposed pair and searches pair parameters using differential evolution.
The recorded run used seed 60, 100 iterations, and 7,272 linear programs.
Its smallest reported floating-point objective was approximately
$0.5000008335947002$, with a midpoint fourth coordinate approximately
$1.6672\times10^{-6}$ and $t\approx1.6083\times10^{-6}$.

This near-boundary, near-coincident numerical result is not an exact certificate.
It neither proves a universal threshold nor certifies its displayed decimal
coefficient array as having exact distinct fixed points. No negative search
result is used in Claims 1--3. The exact family (8) replaces any need to rely
on this numerical candidate for sharpness above $1/2$.

Two other limitations of initial proof routes matter:

- Keeping only $J_{ii}\le1$ would leave the endpoint $d=1/2$ unresolved.
  Equality in the diagonal average forces the positive cross derivative used
  in (5), which is the missing endpoint argument.
- The unweighted capped transfer estimate in Theorem 6 is insufficient to
  reach $7/50$: its old contraction gap closes at $1-\sqrt3/2$. The new
  estimate uses (12), which follows from the equation $z=Jz$ for a candidate
  pair of fixed points. Arbitrary differences need not satisfy (12), so the
  uniqueness contradiction is **not** a Lipschitz contraction bound on a cap.

The boundary theorem and the uniform four-type theorem must remain separate.
