## Result and scope

**The proposed all-dimensional extension is valid.** Fix an actual subset
of two point coordinates $U$, its complement $O$ of size $r$, an
outside Hermitian contraction $C$, and an arbitrary complex $2\times r$
matrix $B$. Then

\[
\mathcal H(A,z)=H\!\left(P_{K(A,z)}\right),\qquad
K(A,z)=\begin{pmatrix}A&zB\\\bar zB^*&C\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1}
\]

is jointly concave on

\[
\mathcal D_{B,C}=
\{(A,z): A=A^*,\ z\in\mathbb C,\ 0\le K(A,z)\le I\}.
\tag{2}
\]

The domain is closed and convex in real coordinates. If $B\ne0$, the
matrix parametrization has six real coordinates: four in $A$ and two in
$z$. The theorem includes singular outside blocks, zero-probability atoms,
boundary endpoints, arbitrary complex entries, and arbitrary finite $r$.

The weighted-resolvent cancellation, conditional-kernel signs, radial
admissibility, and the factor $2|z'|^2$ in the proposed Hessian proof all
check exactly. No correction to the coordinator's displayed argument is
needed. Two details deserve explicit proof: all Hermitian directions must
be used to obtain the matrix cancellation, and the radial interval must be
justified by both Schur-complement inequalities.

A focused strengthening is also proved below. For $0<t<1$, write

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_t&=(1-t)A_0+tA_1,& z_t&=(1-t)z_0+tz_1,\\
\bar s_t&=(1-t)|z_0|^2+t|z_1|^2,&
\Delta_t&=t(1-t)|z_1-z_0|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

If $\bar s_t>0$, the auxiliary kernel

\[
\bar K_t=K(A_t,\sqrt{\bar s_t})
\tag{4}
\]

is feasible, and the entropy Jensen gap satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H(A_t,z_t)-(1-t)\mathcal H(A_0,z_0)-t\mathcal H(A_1,z_1)
\ \ge\ \frac{\Delta_t}{\bar s_t}
 I_{P_{\bar K_t}}(X\cap U\,;\,X\cap O).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently, **if $B\ne0$ and $z_0\ne z_1$, the entropy chord inequality
is strict for every $0<t<1$, including for closed-contraction endpoints.**
If $\bar s_t=0$, both endpoint scalars vanish and the ordinary concavity
conclusion applies. Formula (5) does not assert a complete equality
classification when $z_0=z_1$.

The same analytic argument is a conditional lifting principle for an inside
block of size $k$, assuming full $k$-site determinantal entropy concavity.
Here the application $k=2$ is unconditional using the theorem proved in
016 and audited in 018. This is not arbitrary cross-matrix variation and
does not resolve the unrestricted Lyons conjecture. Historical novelty has
not been checked.

## 1. Audit of the two-site input

Only ordinary concavity of $H_2(M)=H(P_M)$ on $0\le M\le I_2$ is needed.
For completeness, the decisive argument in 016/018 can be checked in a few
lines. Write the atom probabilities in order $00,10,01,11$ as $x,y,u,w$,
and let $E=E^*$ be a direction with $\delta=\det E$. At a strict
contraction,

\[
yu-xw=|M_{12}|^2\ge0,\qquad
D^2H_2(M)[E,E]=-
\sum_a\frac{(p'_a)^2}{p_a}
+2\delta\log\frac{yu}{xw}.
\tag{6}
\]

When $\delta\le0$, both displayed contributions are nonpositive. When
$\delta>0$, change the sign of $E$ if needed so $E>0$, and set

\[
P=\frac{\operatorname{adj}M}{\sqrt w},\quad
Q=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-M)}{\sqrt x},\quad
T=\frac{y+u}{2\sqrt{xw}}\ge1.
\]

The empty and full Fisher terms alone give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_a\frac{(p'_a)^2}{p_a}
&\ge (\operatorname{tr}PE)^2+(\operatorname{tr}QE)^2\\
&\ge\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}(P+Q)E)^2
\ge2\delta\det(P+Q)=4\delta(1+T).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

The last inequality applies arithmetic-geometric mean to the two positive
eigenvalues of $(P+Q)^{1/2}E(P+Q)^{1/2}$; no commutativity is used.
The determinant identity $\det(P+Q)=2+2T$ is the two-by-two mixed
determinant calculation in 016/018. Since
$\log(yu/(xw))\le2\log T$, (6)–(7) imply

\[
D^2H_2(M)[E,E]\le-4\delta(1+T-\log T)\le-8\delta<0.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus every complex Hermitian direction is covered. Regularization by
$(1-2\varepsilon)M+\varepsilon I$ and entropy continuity give the closed
theorem. This confirms the required premise without interpreting the
endpoint-only Fisher minimizer as the full Fisher minimizer.

## 2. Exact outside atoms and conditional kernels

The next calculations hold for arbitrary inside size $k$. First assume

\[
0<K(A,z)<I.
\tag{9}
\]

In particular $0<C<I$ and $0<A<I$. For an outside atom $Y\subset O$,
put $Z=O\setminus Y$, let $P_Z$ be the diagonal projector onto the
absent outside coordinates, and define

\[
Q_Y=C-P_Z,\qquad
q_Y=P_{K(A,z)}(X\cap O=Y)=(-1)^{|Z|}\det Q_Y.
\tag{10}
\]

The sign follows directly from the atom matrix: multiplying column $j$
of $Q_Y$ by $+1$ when $j\in Y$, and by $-1$ when $j\in Z$, gives
the corresponding column of $C$ or $I-C$. The outside law depends only
on $C$, so the $q_Y$ are fixed along the entire slice (1).

Every $q_Y$ is strictly positive under (9). For a direct verification,
let $L=C(I-C)^{-1}>0$. Factoring $I-C$ out of the outside atom matrix
gives

\[
q_Y=\det(I-C)\det L_Y>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Empty principal minors have determinant one. Hence every $Q_Y$ is
invertible. Define the Hermitian matrices

\[
R_Y=Q_Y^{-1},\qquad D_Y=BR_YB^*.
\tag{12}
\]

There is no assertion that $R_Y$ or $D_Y$ has a fixed order sign.

For every $W\subset U$, inclusion-exclusion on the absent outside sites
gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P(W\subset X,\ X\cap O=Y)\\
&\quad=\sum_{T\subset Z}(-1)^{|T|}
\det K_{W\cup Y\cup T}\\
&\quad=(-1)^{|Z|}
\det\begin{pmatrix}A_{WW}&zB_{WO}\\
\bar zB_{WO}^*&Q_Y\end{pmatrix}\\
&\quad=q_Y\det\big(A-|z|^2D_Y\big)_{WW}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

The second equality is the determinant expansion in the diagonal shifts
on $Z$; the last is the Schur complement. Thus the exact conditional
inside law is a DPP with kernel

\[
\boxed{M_Y(A,z)=A-|z|^2D_Y.}
\tag{14}
\]

In particular the sign in (14) is minus; the sign from absent coordinates
has already entered $q_Y$ and $R_Y$. For one outside site of marginal
$c$, (14) reads $A+|z|^2BB^*/(1-c)$ on absence and
$A-|z|^2BB^*/c$ on presence, agreeing with 036.

### Why every conditional kernel is a strict contraction

Condition first on inclusion of $Y$. On $V=U\cup Z$, the kernel becomes

\[
T=K_{VV}-K_{VY}K_{YY}^{-1}K_{YV}.
\]

The Schur complement gives $T>0$, while $T\le K_{VV}<I$. Conditioning
next on exclusion of $Z$ gives

\[
M_Y=T_{UU}+T_{UZ}(I_Z-T_{ZZ})^{-1}T_{ZU}>0.
\tag{15}
\]

Moreover $I_U-M_Y$ is the Schur complement of $I_Z-T_{ZZ}$ in the
positive-definite matrix $I-T$, so $I_U-M_Y>0$. Block elimination
identifies (15) with (14). This covers all included/absent patterns,
including either empty set. It uses the original point-coordinate blocks.

The same full-support argument as (11) applies to $M_Y$, so the finite
atom entropy $H_k(M_Y)$ is smooth under (9).

## 3. The weighted-resolvent cancellation

The exact normalization identity

\[
\sum_{Y\subset O}(-1)^{|O\setminus Y|}\det(C-P_{O\setminus Y})=1
\tag{16}
\]

holds for every strict outside contraction $C$. Differentiate it at the
fixed $C$ in an arbitrary Hermitian direction $E$. The strict-contraction
set is open in the real vector space of Hermitian matrices, so every such
direction is allowed for a sufficiently short parameter interval. Jacobi's
formula gives

\[
0=\sum_Yq_Y\operatorname{tr}(R_YE)
=\operatorname{tr}\left[\left(\sum_Yq_YR_Y\right)E\right].
\tag{17}
\]

The matrix in parentheses is Hermitian. The real Hermitian trace pairing
is nondegenerate; for example, take $E$ equal to that matrix and use
$\operatorname{tr}(E^2)=0\Rightarrow E=0$. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\sum_Yq_YR_Y=0,\qquad \sum_Yq_YD_Y=0.}
\tag{18}
\]

Differentiation only in diagonal $E$ would establish only the diagonal
part of the first identity, so the all-Hermitian qualification matters.
Differentiating the universal identity (16) is an algebraic proof of (18);
it does not make $C$ or the weights variable along the entropy slice.

## 4. The entire required radial interval is feasible

Still assume $0<C<I$. Define positive-semidefinite matrices

\[
T_+=BC^{-1}B^*,\qquad T_-=B(I-C)^{-1}B^*.
\]

For $s\ge0$, the two block Schur-complement conditions are exactly

\[
0<K(A,\sqrt s)<I
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A-sT_+>0,\qquad I-A-sT_->0.
\tag{19}
\]

Feasibility depends on a complex scalar only through its squared modulus,
so (19) applies to any scalar of squared modulus $s$. If the conditions
hold at $s_0=|z|^2$, they hold for every $0\le s\le s_0$: reducing $s$
adds a positive-semidefinite matrix to both Schur complements. They also
hold slightly beyond $s_0$, since the inequalities at $s_0$ are strict.

Consequently every $A-sD_Y$ is a strict conditional kernel throughout
the interval used below. This is stronger than merely asserting that the
formal endpoints of a conditional path are legal. The case $s_0=0$
causes no derivative problem: $A$ is strict, there are finitely many
$D_Y$, and $A-sD_Y$ remains strict for sufficiently small signed $s$.

Let

\[
F_A(s)=\sum_Yq_YH_k(A-sD_Y),\qquad 0\le s\le s_0.
\tag{20}
\]

If $H_k$ is concave, (20) is concave because all its arguments are affine
in $s$. With the real Hermitian pairing $\langle M,N\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(MN)$,
(18) gives

\[
F_A'(0)=-\left\langle\nabla H_k(A),\sum_Yq_YD_Y\right\rangle=0.
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{F_A'(s)\le0\quad(0\le s\le s_0).}
\tag{22}
\]

The derivative at the upper endpoint is legitimate by the strict extension
just noted. Individual summands in (20) need not have nonpositive first
derivative; only their fixed weighted sum is controlled.

## 5. Exact Hessian along every real affine line

The entropy chain rule is

\[
\mathcal H(A,z)=H(P_C)+\sum_Yq_YH_k(M_Y(A,z))
=H(P_C)+F_A(|z|^2).
\tag{23}
\]

Take an arbitrary real affine line $A(t)=A+tE$, $z(t)=z+tv$, where
$E=E^*$ and $v\in\mathbb C$. At any strict point on it, let

\[
\sigma=2\operatorname{Re}(\bar z v),\qquad
U_Y=E-\sigma D_Y.
\]

Differentiating the actual conditional kernel, rather than treating it
as affine, yields

\[
M_Y'=U_Y,\qquad M_Y''=-2|v|^2D_Y.
\tag{24}
\]

Thus the complete second derivative is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H''
&=\sum_Yq_YD^2H_k(M_Y)[U_Y,U_Y]
-2|v|^2\sum_Yq_Y\langle\nabla H_k(M_Y),D_Y\rangle\\
&=\sum_Yq_YD^2H_k(M_Y)[U_Y,U_Y]
+2|v|^2\,\partial_sF_A(|z|^2)\le0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{25}
\]

Here $\partial_sF_A$ means differentiation in $s$ with the current
$A=A(t)$ fixed. All dependence of $A(t)$ has already entered $U_Y$
and the conditional Hessians. In particular, no mixed $A,z$ term is
missing, the last sign is plus times the nonpositive derivative, and the
coefficient is exactly $2|v|^2$. At $z=0$, the last term vanishes by
(21), but (25) still applies.

For $k=2$, Section 1 supplies every conditional Hessian sign. Integrating
(25) along a strict feasible chord proves the interior theorem. Formula
(25) also proves the conditional lifting principle for any $k$ whose
full kernel entropy concavity is known.

## 6. Closed boundary, degenerate outside blocks, and point basis

For arbitrary feasible endpoints of (1), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_j^{(\varepsilon)}&=(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I,\\
A_j^{(\varepsilon)}&=(1-2\varepsilon)A_j+\varepsilon I_U,\\
C^{(\varepsilon)}&=(1-2\varepsilon)C+\varepsilon I_O,\\
z_j^{(\varepsilon)}&=(1-2\varepsilon)z_j,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

For each fixed $\varepsilon$, both regularized endpoints are strict,
share one outside block $C^{(\varepsilon)}$, and retain the original
fixed cross matrix $B$. The regularization commutes with affine
interpolation. Apply the already proved interior theorem in that fixed
slice and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Every finite DPP atom is a
polynomial in the entries of $K$, and $-p\log p$ extends continuously
to $p=0$, proving (2) on the closed domain.

This argument permits $C$ to have eigenvalues zero or one and permits
the original feasible segment to lie wholly in a boundary face. It never
uses $R_Y$ for an original atom with $q_Y=0$. Indeed, the raw identity
$\sum_{q_Y>0}q_YR_Y=0$ need not hold at a singular outside block after
discarding zero atoms; it is not asserted. The boundary theorem is a limit
of the valid strict inequalities, not a claim of a finite boundary Hessian.

If $B=0$, (23) reduces to $H_k(A)+H(P_C)$, independent of $z$, so this
degeneracy is covered. If $r=0$, the theorem is just the inside theorem
with the empty-matrix conventions.

The decomposition $U\sqcup O$ is a decomposition of the DPP's actual
point labels. Relabeling sites is harmless; arbitrary unitary rotation of
the kernel generally changes the subset law and its entropy. No such
rotation is used. Algebraic spectral inequalities inside a proof of a
Schur complement or positive-definiteness do not change this point basis.

## 7. Stronger chord bound and strictness

This is also a second, finite-chord proof of the concavity mechanism.
Assume first strict endpoints and $0<t<1$. Put $s_j=|z_j|^2$ and use
(3). The pair $(A_t,\bar s_t)$ satisfies (19), since

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_t-\bar s_tT_+
&=(1-t)(A_0-s_0T_+)+t(A_1-s_1T_+)>0,\\
I-A_t-\bar s_tT_-
&=(1-t)(I-A_0-s_0T_-)+t(I-A_1-s_1T_-)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

Hence $\bar K_t$ in (4) is strict and has conditional kernels

\[
A_t-\bar s_tD_Y=(1-t)(A_0-s_0D_Y)+t(A_1-s_1D_Y).
\]

Applying $k$-site concavity to each such kernel and summing with the fixed
weights yields

\[
H(P_{\bar K_t})\ge(1-t)\mathcal H(A_0,z_0)+t\mathcal H(A_1,z_1).
\tag{28}
\]

The complex squared-modulus identity is

\[
|z_t|^2=\bar s_t-\Delta_t,\qquad 0\le\Delta_t\le\bar s_t.
\tag{29}
\]

If $\bar s_t>0$, put $\alpha=\Delta_t/\bar s_t$. Concavity of
$F_{A_t}$, now applied between zero and $\bar s_t$, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{A_t}(|z_t|^2)
&\ge(1-\alpha)F_{A_t}(\bar s_t)+\alpha H_k(A_t)\\
&=F_{A_t}(\bar s_t)
+\alpha\big[H_k(A_t)-F_{A_t}(\bar s_t)\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]

The bracket is exactly the mutual information of the inside and outside
subsets under $P_{\bar K_t}$, because their marginal kernels are
$A_t$ and $C$. Combining (23), (28), and (30) proves (5). Its right
side is nonnegative by entropy subadditivity. This finite-chord proof can
therefore establish concavity without differentiating (16), although the
normalization derivative remains the clean exact justification of the
Hessian formula's sign.

For closed endpoints use (26). Then

\[
\bar s_t^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)^2\bar s_t,\quad
\Delta_t^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)^2\Delta_t,
\]

so $\alpha$ is unchanged, and the corresponding auxiliary kernel is

\[
\bar K_t^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)\bar K_t+\varepsilon I.
\tag{31}
\]

The strict construction proves that these matrices are feasible. Taking
their limit proves feasibility of $\bar K_t$, even when $C$ is
singular. Entropy and mutual information are continuous for these finite
laws, so (5) passes to the closed boundary unchanged.

Finally, if $B\ne0$ and $z_0\ne z_1$, then $\Delta_t>0$ and
$\bar s_t>0$. Some actual cross entry $B_{ij}$ is nonzero, and the
two-site inclusion determinant under $\bar K_t$ gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf1_{i\in X},\mathbf1_{j\in X})
=-\bar s_t|B_{ij}|^2<0,
\qquad i\in U,\ j\in O.
\tag{32}
\]

Thus the inside and outside subsets are dependent, their mutual
information is strictly positive, and (5) proves the stated strict chord
inequality. Equality in entropy subadditivity is equivalent to
independence for finite laws, including laws with zero probabilities.

Another precise consequence, with $A,C,B$ fixed on an admissible radial
interval, is that

\[
I(s)=H_k(A)+H(P_C)-H(P_{K(A,\sqrt s)})=H_k(A)-F_A(s)
\tag{33}
\]

is convex and nondecreasing in $s\ge0$, and is strictly increasing when
$B\ne0$ and the interval contains a positive $s$. In the strict
interior, $I'(0)=0$. For strict increase, use $I(0)=0$, $I(s)>0$ for
$s>0$ by (32), and convexity:
$I(s_1)\le(s_1/s_2)I(s_2)<I(s_2)$ whenever $0\le s_1<s_2$.
The closed version follows from the same regularization. No assertion of
operator-gradient monotonicity is used or implied.

## 9. Limits, provenance, and claim ledger

The exact restriction is that the entire cross block is one fixed matrix
times one complex scalar. Allowing an arbitrary affine cross matrix
$B_0+tV$ gives conditional second derivatives
$-2VR_YV^*$. Although $\sum q_YVR_YV^*=0$, the gradients multiplying
these matrices are evaluated at
$A-B_0R_YB_0^*$, which generally lie on different matrix paths from
those generated by $VR_YV^*$. The common scalar radial function (20)
then does not supply the required sign. Thus the cancellation identity
alone does not prove arbitrary cross-block concavity. Agent 036's exact
failure of operator-gradient antitonicity is consistent with this limit.

The result also does not permit an arbitrary unitary coordinate change to
turn a general direction into the supported form. A rank-two $B$ can
yield rank-four directions in (1), but that fact does not classify all
rank-four or all rank-two kernel directions.
