## 1. Statements and conventions

Let \(K=K^*\) be an \(n\times n\) complex Hermitian contraction, \(0\le K\le I\). Write \(P_K\) for its determinantal subset law, characterized by
\[
 \Pr(S\subseteq X)=\det K_S,
\]
and put
\[
 h(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\qquad
 H(K)=H(P_K),\qquad
 S(K)=\operatorname{tr}h(K),\qquad
 U(K)=\sum_i h(K_{ii}).
\]
All logarithms are natural and \(0\log0=0\). Define
\[
 C(K)=H(K)-S(K),\qquad T(K)=U(K)-H(K).
\]
Let \(G(K)\) be the graph whose edges are the pairs \(ij\) with \(K_{ij}\ne0\).

**Baseline.** For every such kernel,
\[
 \boxed{S(K)\le H(K)\le U(K).}
\]
Equality in either inequality holds if and only if \(K\) is diagonal in the specified coordinate basis.

**Forest correction.** Let \(\mathcal F\) range over forests on \([n]\), allowing isolated vertices, and let \(I_{ij}=I(X_i;X_j)\). Then
\[
 \boxed{T(K)\ge \max_{\mathcal F}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}I_{ij}
           \ge 8\max_{\mathcal F}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}|K_{ij}|^4.}
 \tag{F}
\]
For a fixed forest, equality in \(T(K)\ge\sum_{\mathcal F}I_{ij}\) holds exactly when \(G(K)\) is a matching contained in that forest. Consequently equality in the first maximized inequality holds exactly when \(G(K)\) is a matching. Equality in the final quartic bound holds exactly when \(K\) is diagonal. The universal constant \(8\) is optimal.

**Coordinate partitions.** For every partition \(\mathcal B\) of \([n]\),
\[
 \boxed{C(K)\ge\sum_{B\in\mathcal B}C(K_B).}
 \tag{P}
\]
Equality holds exactly when \(K\) is block diagonal for this partition. The proof below treats the fermionic ordering explicitly and proves equality without a Petz recovery theorem.

**Matching correction.** For \(i\ne j\), put
\[
 c_{ij}=|K_{ij}|^2,\qquad
 r_{ij}=\Pr(X_i+X_j=1)
       =K_{ii}+K_{jj}-2K_{ii}K_{jj}+2c_{ij}.
\]
A term with \(r_{ij}=0\) is defined as zero. Let
\[
 g(v)=\log2-h((1+v)/2),\qquad 0\le v\le1.
\]
Then, with maxima over matchings,
\[
 \boxed{
 C(K)\ge
 \max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal M}
 r_{ij}g\!\left(\frac{2|K_{ij}|}{r_{ij}}\right)
 \ge 2\max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal M}
             \frac{|K_{ij}|^2}{r_{ij}}.}
 \tag{M}
\]
The argument of \(g\) always lies in \([0,1]\). The constant \(2\) in the last bound is optimal, and equality in that quadratic bound holds exactly for diagonal \(K\). Equality in the stronger first bound holds exactly when \(G(K)\) is a matching and \(K_{ii}=K_{jj}\) on each of its nonzero edges.

In particular, the computable interval
\[
 S(K)+2\max_{\mathcal M}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal M}\frac{|K_{ij}|^2}{r_{ij}}
 \ \le H(K)\le\
 U(K)-8\max_{\mathcal F}\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}|K_{ij}|^4
 \tag{I}
\]
is valid throughout the closed contraction cone. Each side is strict when \(K\) is not diagonal.

## 2. A finite Fock construction and the baseline

Let
\[
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{m=0}^n\bigwedge^m\mathbb C^n
\]
with occupation basis \(|S\rangle=e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_m}\), where \(i_1<\cdots<i_m\), including the vacuum. Diagonalize
\(K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)U^*\).
For the orthonormal eigenvectors \(u_j=Ue_j\), define
\[
 q_J=\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j\prod_{j\notin J}(1-\lambda_j),
 \qquad
 \rho_K=\sum_{J\subseteq[n]}q_J\,|u_J\rangle\langle u_J|,
 \qquad u_J=\bigwedge_{j\in J}u_j.
 \tag{2.1}
\]
This is a density matrix without any invertibility assumption. Its definition does not depend on the choice of eigenbasis: on each eigenspace of \(K\), the Bernoulli weights depend only on the number of occupied eigenmodes. Its eigenvalue list is \((q_J)\), so
\[
 S_{\rm vN}(\rho_K)=H((q_J))=\sum_jh(\lambda_j)=S(K).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Let \(a^\dagger(f)\) be exterior multiplication by \(f\), and \(a(f)\) its adjoint. The covariance convention is
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K a^\dagger(e_j)a(e_i)=K_{ij}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]
More generally,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K
 a^\dagger(f_1)\cdots a^\dagger(f_m)a(g_m)\cdots a(g_1)
 =\det\big[\langle g_i,Kf_j\rangle\big]_{i,j=1}^m.
 \tag{2.4}
\]
To verify this, expand in the eigenmodes \(u_j\). Only equal creation and annihilation index sets survive in the diagonal mixture (2.1). For a set \(J\) of distinct surviving indices the occupation expectation is \(\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j\); summing the antisymmetrized coefficients is precisely Cauchy–Binet. Monomials with unequal creation and annihilation counts have zero expectation.

Taking \(f_i=g_i=e_{s_i}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\rho_K\prod_{i\in S}n_i=\det K_S.
\]
Thus the occupation measurement has exactly the law \(P_K\), since inclusion probabilities determine every atom by inclusion–exclusion. Complete occupation dephasing \(\Delta\) consequently satisfies
\[
 S_{\rm vN}(\Delta\rho_K)=H(K).
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Here is an elementary proof of the lower bound and its equality case. The eigenbasis and occupation basis are related by the unitary
\(\Gamma(U)=\bigoplus_m\bigwedge^mU\). If \(B_{SJ}=|\Gamma(U)_{SJ}|^2\), then \(B\) is doubly stochastic and the measured probabilities are \(p=Bq\). Strict concavity of \(f(x)=-x\log x\) gives
\[
 H(p)\ge\sum_{S,J}B_{SJ}f(q_J)=H(q).
\]
Equality means that for each row \(S\), all \(q_J\) with \(B_{SJ}>0\) coincide. The occupation vector \(|S\rangle\) therefore belongs to a single eigenspace of \(\rho_K\). Hence equality is equivalent to \(\rho_K\) being occupation diagonal. Equation (2.3) then makes every off-diagonal \(K_{ij}\) zero, because \(a^\dagger(e_j)a(e_i)\), \(i\ne j\), has zero occupation diagonal. Conversely, diagonal \(K\) makes (2.1) the independent occupation product state. This also proves equality at eigenvalues \(0\) or \(1\).

For the upper bound, each \(X_i\) is Bernoulli with mean \(K_{ii}\), and ordinary entropy subadditivity gives \(H(K)\le U(K)\). Its gap is
\[
 T(K)=D\!\left(P_K\,\middle\|\,\bigotimes_i P_{X_i}\right).
\]
Equality means independence of all coordinates. For distinct \(i,j\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)
 =\det K_{\{i,j\}}-K_{ii}K_{jj}
 =-|K_{ij}|^2.
 \tag{2.6}
\]
Independence thus forces diagonal \(K\); the converse was already established. A deterministic coordinate creates no exception: positivity of \(K\) or \(I-K\) forces its off-diagonal row entries to vanish when \(K_{ii}=0\) or \(1\).

## 3. Forest bound and the complete equality characterization

This step first applies to any finite joint law. Root each component of a forest \(\mathcal F\), and define the tree law
\[
 Q_{\mathcal F}(x)
 =\prod_{\text{roots }r}P_r(x_r)
  \prod_{u\to v}P(X_v=x_v\mid X_u=x_u).
 \tag{3.1}
\]
Conditional distributions at zero-probability parent values may be chosen arbitrarily: those values remain unreachable in (3.1), whose one-site marginals are the prescribed marginals. Also \(P(x)>0\) implies \(Q_{\mathcal F}(x)>0\). Direct expansion gives the exact identity
\[
 D(P\|Q_{\mathcal F})
 =\sum_iH(X_i)-H(X)-\sum_{ij\in\mathcal F}I(X_i;X_j).
 \tag{3.2}
\]
This proves the first bound, with equality exactly when \(P=Q_{\mathcal F}\).

Suppose now \(P=P_K=Q_{\mathcal F}\). If two edges \(ij,jk\) in the tree law have nonzero covariance, then \(\operatorname{Var}(X_j)>0\), and the tree factorization gives \(X_i\perp X_k\mid X_j\). Binary conditional expectations are affine in \(X_j\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_k)
 =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)
             \operatorname{Cov}(X_j,X_k)}
        {\operatorname{Var}(X_j)}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
By (2.6), the numerator is strictly positive, whereas the left side must be nonpositive. This contradiction shows that the nonzero covariance edges of the forest form a matching.

For two binary variables, zero covariance is equivalent to independence, also when a variable is deterministic. Such an edge can be deleted from (3.1): its conditional distribution is its unconditional marginal at every supported parent value. Deleting all those edges reduces \(Q_{\mathcal F}\) to independent pairs and singletons. Therefore all covariances outside the resulting matching vanish; by (2.6), \(G(K)\) is exactly that matching and is contained in \(\mathcal F\).

Conversely, if \(G(K)\) is a matching contained in \(\mathcal F\), then \(K\) is a direct sum of the associated one- and two-coordinate blocks. Its DPP factors over those blocks (for example, factor all inclusion determinants and use uniqueness). Equation (3.2) is then an equality. This proves both the fixed-forest and maximized equality statements.

For the quantitative pair estimate write \(a=K_{ii}\), \(b=K_{jj}\), \(c=|K_{ij}|^2\). The joint and independent tables are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
(x_i,x_j)&00&10&01&11\\ \hline
P&(1-a)(1-b)-c&a(1-b)+c&(1-a)b+c&ab-c\\
Q&(1-a)(1-b)&a(1-b)&(1-a)b&ab
\end{array}
\]
so \(\|P-Q\|_1=4c\). The classical Pinsker inequality yields
\(I_{ij}=D(P\|Q)\ge8c^2\).

For completeness, its needed strictness has a short proof. Group the positive differences into \(A=\{p>q\}\), with
\(\delta=P(A)-Q(A)=\|P-Q\|_1/2\). Log-sum gives
\(D(P\|Q)\ge d(q+\delta\|q)\), where \(q=Q(A)\) and \(d\) is binary relative entropy. As a function of its first argument, \(d\) has second derivative \(1/[t(1-t)]\ge4\), and its value and first derivative at \(t=q\) vanish. Integration gives \(d(q+\delta\|q)\ge2\delta^2\), strictly for \(\delta>0\), since the second derivative equals \(4\) only at one point. Infinite relative entropy causes no difficulty. Consequently \(I_{ij}>8c^2\) whenever \(c>0\).

If \(K\) has a nonzero off-diagonal entry, a forest maximizing the quartic sum has at least one such edge; summing the strict pair estimate on it makes the final bound in (F) strict.

## 4. Coordinate partitions: tensor ordering, singular support, equality

Ordinary tensor marginals of noncontiguous fermionic modes cannot be identified automatically with Gaussian restrictions. First permute the modes so that the blocks of \(\mathcal B\) are contiguous. The implementing Fock unitary is a signed permutation of occupation vectors, so it preserves both \(S_{\rm vN}(\rho_K)\) and the entropy of its occupation law.

Use the resulting ordered identification
\[
 \mathcal H\cong\bigotimes_{B\in\mathcal B}
       \left(\bigoplus_m\bigwedge^m\mathbb C^B\right).
\]
A creation or annihilation operator in a later block includes the parity operator of the preceding blocks. Those parity factors cancel in every equal-creation/equal-annihilation monomial within one block. The ordinary reduced state on a block is gauge invariant, and its moments (2.4) are therefore exactly those of \(\rho_{K_B}\). Such monomials span the gauge-invariant block operators; hence the reduced state is precisely \(\rho_{K_B}\).

An explicit warning example is
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0&1/2\\0&1/2&0\\1/2&0&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In the original order its Fock state is the equal mixture of
\[
 (|100\rangle+|001\rangle)/\sqrt2,\qquad
 (|110\rangle-|011\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]
Tracing out the middle *qubit* gives an incoherent mixture of \(|10\rangle,|01\rangle\), with entropy \(\log2\). The fermionic restriction to modes \(\{1,3\}\) is a pure rank-one Gaussian state, with entropy zero. The mode permutation before taking the tensor marginal supplies the required sign.

Now abbreviate \(\rho=\rho_K\), \(\rho_B=\rho_{K_B}\), and
\(\sigma=\bigotimes_B\rho_B\). Finite quantum relative entropy obeys
\[
 D(\rho\|\sigma)=\sum_BS_{\rm vN}(\rho_B)-S_{\rm vN}(\rho).
\]
The support of \(\rho\) lies in \(\bigotimes_B\operatorname{supp}\rho_B\): the expectation of each marginal kernel projection is zero, so positivity forces \(\rho\) to vanish on it. All displayed relative entropies are consequently finite on their supports. Under the product occupation dephasing \(\Delta\),
\[
 D(\Delta\rho\|\Delta\sigma)=\sum_BH(K_B)-H(K).
\]
Subtracting and applying quantum relative-entropy monotonicity under \(\Delta\) proves (P). This finite-dimensional data-processing theorem is the only general quantum information inequality used in this partition proof; see [Frenkel, Section 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.12194).

Equality can be settled without importing any recovery-map equality condition. Let \(E\) pinch the joint eigenspaces of the block particle numbers \(N_B\):
\[
 E(\tau)=\sum_{\mathbf m}\Pi_{\mathbf m}\tau\Pi_{\mathbf m}.
\]
Each \(\rho_B\) commutes with \(N_B\), so \(E\sigma=\sigma\), and \(\Delta E=\Delta\). Pinching gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C(K)-\sum_B C(K_B)
 &=D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\Delta\rho\|\Delta\sigma)\\
 &=D(\rho\|E\rho)
   +D(E\rho\|\sigma)-D(\Delta\rho\|\Delta\sigma)\\
 &\ge D(\rho\|E\rho)\ge0.
 \tag{4.1}
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle identity follows from
\[
 D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(E\rho\|\sigma)
 =S_{\rm vN}(E\rho)-S_{\rm vN}(\rho)
 =D(\rho\|E\rho).
\]
Indeed, \(\log\sigma\) and \(\log(E\rho)\) are fixed by the pinching on their supports. Positivity implies \(\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\operatorname{supp}E\rho\); \(E\) preserves \(\operatorname{supp}\sigma\). These facts justify the identities for singular states, or equivalently one may regularize the logarithms on these supports. The remaining inequality in (4.1) is data processing of \(E\rho,\sigma\) under \(\Delta\).

Equality in (P) forces \(D(\rho\|E\rho)=0\), hence \(\rho=E\rho\). Thus \(\rho\) commutes with each \(N_B\). A hopping operator \(a^\dagger(e_j)a(e_i)\) between different blocks changes their particle numbers, so its expectation in such a state is zero. Equation (2.3) forces every cross-block \(K_{ij}\) to vanish. Conversely, block diagonal \(K\) makes (2.1) a tensor product of its block states, giving equality. This proves the claimed equality characterization on the entire closed cone.

## 5. Two-mode coherence, the matching bound, and sharp constants

For a two-coordinate kernel
\[
 J=\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\\bar z&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 c=|z|^2,\quad d=ab-c,
\]
the vacuum and double-occupation weights of \(\rho_J\) are respectively
\(p_{00}=1-a-b+d\) and \(p_{11}=d\). Its one-particle block is
\[
 R=J-dI=
 \begin{pmatrix}a-d&z\\\bar z&b-d\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad r=\operatorname{tr}R=a+b-2d.
 \tag{5.1}
\]
For example, its eigenvalues are
\(\lambda_1(1-\lambda_2)\) and \(\lambda_2(1-\lambda_1)\), so \(R\ge0\), also at the boundary. If \(r=0\), then \(R=0\), hence \(z=0\). If \(r>0\), put
\[
 u=(a-b)/r,\qquad v=2|z|/r.
\]
Positivity gives \(u^2+v^2\le1\). Vacuum and double-occupation sectors make identical contributions before and after dephasing, so
\[
 C(J)=r\left[g\!\left(\sqrt{u^2+v^2}\right)-g(|u|)\right].
 \tag{5.2}
\]
The absolutely convergent endpoint-valid series
\[
 g(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{2k(2k-1)},\qquad 0\le x\le1,
\]
now gives
\[
 g(\sqrt{u^2+v^2})-g(|u|)
 \ge g(v)\ge v^2/2.
\]
The first inequality follows termwise from
\((u^2+v^2)^k-u^{2k}\ge v^{2k}\). It is an equality exactly when \(uv=0\). The second is strict for \(v>0\). Therefore
\[
 C(J)\ge r g(2|z|/r)\ge 2|z|^2/r,
 \tag{5.3}
\]
and the last inequality is strict whenever \(z\ne0\). The first is an equality precisely when \(z=0\) or \(a=b\).

Apply (P) to the pairs of any matching and its remaining singleton blocks, whose coherences are zero, and then sum (5.3). Taking maxima proves (M). Equality in its first bound requires, and is ensured by, block diagonality along a maximizing matching together with equal endpoint diagonals on every nonzero matched edge. This is exactly the graph characterization stated in Section 1. If \(K\) has any nonzero off-diagonal entry, a matching maximizing \(\sum c_{ij}/r_{ij}\) has a positive term; strictness in (5.3) proves strictness of the quadratic bound.

There is no hidden \(0/0\) boundary issue. Positivity of \(R\) gives
\(c\le r^2/4\), so \(c/r\le r/4\) and
\(0\le r g(2\sqrt c/r)\le r\log2\).
Both quantities extend continuously by zero at \(r=0\).

Finally, take the interior two-site family
\[
 K_t=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&t\\t&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<t<1/2.
\]
Here \(c=t^2\), \(r=1/2+2t^2\), and direct substitution gives
\[
 T(K_t)=I(X_1;X_2)=g(4t^2),\qquad
 C(K_t)=r\,g(2t/r).
\]
Since \(g(x)=x^2/2+O(x^4)\),
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{T(K_t)}{|(K_t)_{12}|^4}=8,\qquad
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{C(K_t)}{|(K_t)_{12}|^2/r}=2.
\]
Thus neither universal constant can be increased, even for real, strictly interior two-site kernels. Optimality is a limiting statement; nonzero kernels do not attain equality in the two polynomial corrections.

## 6. Exact limit of the implication toward Lyons concavity

These are pointwise entropy estimates and a coordinate-partition theorem. They do not prove concavity in the kernel. In particular, set
\[
 A=(K+L)/2,\quad B=(K-L)/2,\quad
 M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The one-particle balanced unitary makes \(M\) unitarily equivalent to \(K\oplus L\), so
\[
 S(M)=S(K)+S(L).
\]
Consequently the stronger two-copy assertion
\[
 H(M)\ge H(K)+H(L)
\]
is *exactly equivalent* to the unproved comparison
\[
 C(M)\ge C(K)+C(L).
 \tag{6.1}
\]
Partition superadditivity only gives \(C(M)\ge2C(A)\), using the output coordinate blocks; it gives no comparison between that right-hand side and \(C(K)+C(L)\). The balanced unitary does not preserve the input coordinate partition.

A concrete gap in the bare spectral/quadratic comparison is
\[
 K=L=P=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(M=P\oplus P\), \(S(M)=0\), and
\(H(M)=H(K)+H(L)=2\log2\).
The spectral lower bound gives only zero; the quadratic matching correction gives only \(1<2\log2\). The stronger formula in (M) happens to be exact for this balanced pair, but does not supply the general transformation comparison (6.1).

If (6.1) were established universally, classical subadditivity of the two output blocks would give
\(H(M)\le2H(A)\), hence midpoint concavity, and finite-dimensional continuity would extend that to full concavity. That transformation inequality remains an additional obligation. No counterexample to it, or to general Lyons concavity, is asserted here.
