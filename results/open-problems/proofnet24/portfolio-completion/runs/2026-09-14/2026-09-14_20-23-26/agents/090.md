# Agent 090: the cubic four-type parent-exclusion theorem

Status: complete proof, with an executed exact symbolic and rational certificate.

This report concerns the literal higher-degree analogue of the assumptions in
[FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md](../FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md), read at repository commit
dac08564ea4df3115aa309c6cbf18620364be6e2. It introduces no change in the parent
sampling law or in the meaning of a stochastic coefficient row.

## 1. Model and result

For a positive integer \(d\), a full symmetric \(d\)-parent stochastic tensor on
four types is an array
\[
P_{i_1\cdots i_d,r}\geq 0,\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^4P_{i_1\cdots i_d,r}=1,
\]
symmetric in all parent indices \(i_1,\ldots,i_d\), for every ordered parent
tuple in \(\{1,2,3,4\}^d\), including repeated types. Strict exclusion of every
parental type means
\[
P_{i_1\cdots i_d,r}=0
\quad\hbox{whenever }r\in\{i_1,\ldots,i_d\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The associated homogeneous map is
\[
V_r(x)=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_d}
P_{i_1\cdots i_d,r}x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_d},
\qquad
x\in\Delta_3=\{x\geq0:\sum_r x_r=1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Full row stochasticity gives \(\sum_rV_r(x)=(\sum_r x_r)^d=1\).

**Theorem.** For \(d=3\), every tensor satisfying (1) has exactly one fixed
point. It is interior, and each of its coordinates belongs to
\[
[1-3c,c],\qquad
c=(1-c)^3,\qquad \frac14<c<\frac13.
\tag{3}
\]
The averaged map
\[
T=\frac7{10}I+\frac3{10}V
\tag{4}
\]
sends every point of the simplex into
\(K=\{x\in\Delta_3:\max_r x_r\leq1/3\}\) within five iterations and contracts
on \(K\) in \(\ell^1\) with factor \(9/10\). Thus, for the unique fixed point
\(x^*\),
\[
\|T^n x-x^*\|_1\leq2(9/10)^{n-5},
\qquad n\geq5.
\tag{5}
\]
For every \(d\geq4\), no full tensor satisfying (1) exists.

The cubic class is nonempty: for each parent tuple, assign equal probability
to all types missing from that tuple. At most three distinct types occur, so
the allowed set is nonempty; this construction is symmetric and stochastic.

For perspective, the resulting small-degree classification is:

| Parent degree | Full four-type class | Fixed-point conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(d=1\) | Nonempty | Uniqueness can fail |
| \(d=2\) | Nonempty | Unique by the existing quadratic note |
| \(d=3\) | Nonempty | Unique and interior by this report |
| \(d\geq4\) | Empty | A parent tuple containing all four types is impossible |

The degree-one failure is exact: \(V(x)=(x_2,x_1,x_4,x_3)\) satisfies (1)
and fixes every \((t,t,1/2-t,1/2-t)\), \(0\leq t\leq1/2\).
This observation prevents reading the theorem as a statement covering all
smaller positive degrees automatically.

## 2. The cubic coordinate cap and derivative

From now on \(d=3\). The polynomial \(V_r\) does not contain its own
coordinate \(x_r\), and
\[
0\leq V_r(x)\leq(1-x_r)^3.
\tag{6}
\]
The function \((1-s)^3-s\) is strictly decreasing on \([0,1]\).
Its values at \(1/4\) and \(1/3\) have opposite signs, so its unique zero is
the number \(c\) in (3). Every fixed point has \(x_r\leq c\), and summing
the caps of the other three coordinates gives \(x_r\geq1-3c>0\).
Equivalently, \(c\) is the unique real root of
\(c^3-3c^2+4c-1=0\).

For \(m\in\Delta_3\), define
\[
A(m)_{rj}=\sum_{a,b=1}^4P_{abj,r}m_a m_b.
\tag{7}
\]
This matrix is nonnegative, column-stochastic, and has zero diagonal.
Parent symmetry gives
\[
DV(m)=3A(m).
\tag{8}
\]
For \(m_t=y+t(x-y)\), the exact secant identity is
\[
V(x)-V(y)=3\int_0^1A(m_t)(x-y)\,dt.
\tag{9}
\]
All these statements include points on the boundary of the simplex.

## 3. Sharp cubic transfer inequality

Let \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(H=\{j,\ell\}\) be a partition into two pairs, with
\(i\in G\) and \(j\in H\). Then
\[
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)
\leq
\max\{m_i^2,2m_i m_j,m_j^2\}-2m_k m_\ell.
\tag{10}
\]
For a fixed \(m\), the right-hand side is the exact maximum over all
admissible cubic tensors.

**Proof.** Relabel \(i,k,j,\ell\) as \(1,2,3,4\), respectively, and put
\[
a=m_1,\quad b=m_2,\quad c_0=m_3,\quad d_0=m_4,\qquad
p_{uvw}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{uvw,r}.
\]
The temporary symbols \(c_0,d_0\) are coordinate values, not the cap \(c\)
or the parent degree \(d\).

Each \(p_{uvw}\) lies in \([0,1]\) and is symmetric. If the parent tuple
contains both \(1,2\), then \(p_{uvw}=0\). If it contains both \(3,4\),
then \(p_{uvw}=1\). A cubic tuple cannot contain both pairs, so these
requirements are consistent.

Expanding the left-hand side of (10) and substituting the forced rows
gives exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
D={}&a^2p_{111}
 +(2ac_0-a^2)p_{113}
 +2ad_0p_{114}
 +(c_0^2-2ac_0)p_{133}
 +d_0^2p_{144}\\
&-b^2p_{223}
 -2bc_0p_{233}
 -c_0^2p_{333}
 -2ad_0-2bd_0-d_0^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
For example, the forced rows containing both \(3,4\) contribute
\(2c_0d_0-2ad_0-2bd_0-2c_0d_0-d_0^2\), which is the final constant
in (11). The remaining four free rows have zero coefficients in this
particular difference.

Writing \(u_+=\max\{u,0\}\), maximizing every free row in (11) independently
gives
\[
D\leq a^2+(2ac_0-a^2)_+ +(c_0^2-2ac_0)_+-2bd_0.
\tag{12}
\]
For nonnegative \(a,c_0\),
\[
a^2+(2ac_0-a^2)_+ +(c_0^2-2ac_0)_+
=\max\{a^2,2ac_0,c_0^2\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed the three regions \(c_0\leq a/2\),
\(a/2\leq c_0\leq2a\), and \(c_0\geq2a\) give respectively
\(a^2,2ac_0,c_0^2\); these include the cases with zero coordinates.

Every non-forced row contains at most one distinct member of each pair,
so it has an allowed offspring in each group. Its probability \(p\) can
therefore be chosen independently anywhere in \([0,1]\). Choosing \(p=1\)
for each positive coefficient and \(p=0\) for each negative coefficient
in (11) attains (12). This proves both (10) and the exactness claim.
\(\square\)

Two useful consequences are
\[
\max_r m_r\leq\frac13
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)\leq\frac29,
\tag{14}
\]
and the sharper cap estimate
\[
m\in K_q,\quad \frac14\leq q\leq\frac13
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)\leq2q(4q-1).
\tag{15}
\]
To prove (15), observe that
\[
m_km_\ell
\geq q(m_k+m_\ell)-q^2
\geq q(1-3q),
\]
where the first inequality is
\((q-m_k)(q-m_\ell)\geq0\), and the second uses
\(m_i+m_j\leq2q\). The maximum term in (10) is at most \(2q^2\).

Estimate (15) is sharp over all \(m\in K_q\) and all admissible tensors:
take \(m_i=m_j=m_k=q\), \(m_\ell=1-3q\), and choose the maximizing rows
from (11). This is a sharp statement about the transfer bound on a cap;
it does not impose a fixed-point condition on \(m\).

## 4. Uniqueness

Existence follows from Brouwer's theorem since \(V\) is a continuous
self-map of the simplex.

Suppose \(x\ne y\) are two fixed points, and set \(z=x-y\).
A nonzero zero-sum vector has both positive and negative entries.
It cannot have only one positive entry, say \(z_i>0\): then
\(x_r\leq y_r\) for every \(r\ne i\), and the nonnegative polynomial
\(V_i\), which omits \(x_i\), gives \(V_i(x)\leq V_i(y)\), contradicting
\(x_i>y_i\). Interchanging \(x,y\) rules out only one negative entry.
There are therefore exactly two strictly positive and two strictly
negative coordinates.

Let \(G=\{i:z_i>0\}\), \(H=\{j:z_j<0\}\), and
\(h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i>0\). Define
\[
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2},\qquad i\in G,\ j\in H.
\]
Then \(\theta_{ij}\geq0\), \(\sum_{ij}\theta_{ij}=1\), and
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\tag{16}
\]
Every point \(m_t=y+t(x-y)\) satisfies \(\max_r(m_t)_r\leq c<1/3\).
Using (9), (14), and (16),
\[
\begin{aligned}
h
&=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}(V(x)-V(y))\\
&=3h\int_0^1\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}
\theta_{ij}\,\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m_t)(e_i-e_j)\,dt\\
&\leq\frac23h<h,
\end{aligned}
\]
a contradiction. This establishes uniqueness without any strict
positivity assumption on the allowed coefficients. The coordinate
argument in Section 2 already proves interiority. \(\square\)

## 5. Rational averaged convergence and a residual certificate

Let \(K=K_{1/3}\), \(\lambda=3/10\), and \(T=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V\).
For \(s=x_r\),
\[
(Tx)_r\leq f(s):=\frac7{10}s+\frac3{10}(1-s)^3.
\tag{17}
\]
The function \(f\) is convex on \([0,1]\), because
\(f''(s)=9(1-s)/5\geq0\). Therefore its maximum on \([0,u]\) is
\(\max\{f(0),f(u)\}\).
Here \(f(0)=3/10\) and \(f(1/3)=29/90<1/3\), proving invariance of \(K\).

For completeness, if a real matrix \(B\) has equal column sums, its
induced \(\ell^1\) norm on the zero-sum subspace
\(E=\{z:\sum_rz_r=0\}\) is
\[
\|B|_E\|_1
=\max_{i\ne j}\frac12\|B(e_i-e_j)\|_1
=\max_{S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\},\,i\ne j}
\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}B(e_i-e_j).
\tag{18}
\]
The first equality follows by decomposing a zero-sum vector into
transfers between its positive and negative entries, as in (16).
The reverse inequality uses \(e_i-e_j\). The second equality is the
positive-mass formula for a vector whose entries sum to zero.

At \(m\in K\), put
\[
B(m)=DT(m)=\frac7{10}I+\frac9{10}A(m).
\]
Its column sums are \(8/5\), so (18) applies. Empty and full cuts vanish.
The other cuts are exhausted by the following cases.

| Position of \(i,j\) relative to \(S\) | Upper bound on \(\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}B(m)(e_i-e_j)\) |
| --- | --- |
| Both inside, or both outside | \(9/10\), since the identity term cancels and \(A\) is stochastic |
| \(i\notin S,\ j\in S\) | \(-7/10+9/10=1/5\) |
| \(i\in S,\ j\notin S,\ |S|=1\) | \(7/10\), since the \(A\)-term is \(-A_{ij}\leq0\) |
| \(i\in S,\ j\notin S,\ |S|=3\) | \(7/10\), since the \(A\)-term is \(-A_{ji}\leq0\) |
| \(i\in S,\ j\notin S,\ |S|=2\) | \(7/10+(9/10)(2/9)=9/10\), by (14) |

Thus \(\|B(m)|_E\|_1\leq9/10\) for every \(m\in K\).
The segment between two points of \(K\) remains in \(K\); integrating
the derivative proves
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\leq\frac9{10}\|x-y\|_1,\qquad x,y\in K.
\tag{19}
\]

The following exact rational cap chain proves global entry:
\[
1\ \longmapsto\ \frac7{10}\ \longmapsto\ \frac12\
\longmapsto\ \frac25\ \longmapsto\ \frac7{20}\
\longmapsto\ \frac13.
\tag{20}
\]
These are upper bounds, rather than an assertion that every bound is
attained. For a coordinate cap \(u\), convexity of \(f\) gives the next
cap \(\max\{3/10,f(u)\}\). The exact endpoint calculations are:

| \(u\) | \(f(u)\) | Chosen next cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(1\) | \(7/10\) | \(7/10\) |
| \(7/10\) | \(4981/10000\) | \(1/2\) |
| \(1/2\) | \(31/80\) | \(2/5\) |
| \(2/5\) | \(431/1250\) | \(7/20\) |
| \(7/20\) | \(26191/80000\) | \(1/3\) |

Each chosen cap exceeds both endpoint values. Hence \(T^5(\Delta_3)\)
is contained in \(K\). The diameter of the simplex is two, so (19)
proves (5). The fixed points of \(T\) and \(V\) coincide.

For any \(x\in K\), contraction also gives the useful exact residual
certificate
\[
\|x-x^*\|_1
\leq\frac{\|T(x)-x\|_1}{1-9/10}
=3\|V(x)-x\|_1.
\tag{21}
\]
The proof makes no claim that the unaveraged iterates of \(V\) converge.

## 6. A local determinant consequence

At the unique fixed point, every positive real eigenvalue \(\rho\) of
\(DV(x^*)|_E\) satisfies
\[
\rho\leq6c(4c-1)<\frac23.
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, choose a real eigenvector \(z\in E\) for \(\rho>0\).
The matrix \(DV(x^*)=3A(x^*)\) is nonnegative with zero diagonal.
A single positive entry of \(z\) would give
\((DV(x^*)z)_i\leq0<\rho z_i\), and a single negative entry is
excluded in the same way. Its signs therefore form a two-pair cut.
Applying (16) and then (15), now at \(m=x^*\), proves (22).
The strict comparison with \(2/3\) follows from
\(c<1/3\) and the fact that \(6q(4q-1)\) is increasing for \(q\geq1/4\).

In particular,
\[
\det(I-DV(x^*)|_E)>0.
\tag{23}
\]
For \(0\leq s\leq1\), the real determinant
\(\det(I-sDV(x^*)|_E)\) cannot vanish: at \(s>0\), a zero would give
a positive real eigenvalue \(1/s\geq1\), contrary to (22).
Its value at \(s=0\) is one, so it remains positive.
This is a local consequence, not an assertion of stability under the
original, unaveraged iteration.

## 7. Why the full degree-four and higher classes are empty

For \(d=4\), consider the parent row \((1,2,3,4)\).
Every possible offspring \(r\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) is one of its parents, so
(1) forces all four entries in that row to be zero. Their sum cannot
be one. For \(d>4\), the same contradiction uses the tuple
\((1,2,3,4,1,\ldots,1)\).

A model forbidding such parent tuples, using a restricted sampling
support, dropping full row stochasticity, or renormalizing the sampled
offspring law is a different model. The contradiction just given does
not analyze such variants. In the literal full homogeneous tensor
analogue (1)-(2), however, the degree-four obstruction is definitive.
