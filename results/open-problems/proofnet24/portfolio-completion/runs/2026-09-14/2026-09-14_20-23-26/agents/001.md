## A. Correct conditional local central limit theorem

This lemma addresses exactly the false independence step in F0064. It is an elementary Fourier proof, not a novelty claim about conditional limit theorems.

### Statement

For each row `r`, let `X_r` be a nonnegative integer-valued random variable, and let `N_r -> infinity`. Assume:

* `P(X_r=0)` and `P(X_r=1)` are both at least a fixed `epsilon>0`;
* `X_r` converges in distribution to `X`, and its squared values are uniformly integrable;
* `mu=E X`, `sigma^2=Var X>0`, and `q=P(X=1)`.

Write `mu_r=E X_r`, `q_r=P(X_r=1)`, and take independent copies within each row. Set

\[
 S_r=\sum_{i=1}^{N_r}X_{r,i},\qquad
 B_r=\sum_{i=1}^{N_r}\mathbf 1_{\{X_{r,i}=1\}}.
\]

Let the integer targets `k_r` satisfy

\[
 \frac{k_r-N_r\mu_r}{\sqrt{N_r}}\longrightarrow a\in\mathbb R.
\]

Put

\[
 b=q(1-q),\qquad \gamma=q(1-\mu).
\]

Then

\[
 \sqrt{N_r}\,\mathbb P(S_r=k_r)
 \longrightarrow \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
                e^{-a^2/(2\sigma^2)},
\tag{A1}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal L\left(
  \frac{B_r-N_r q_r}{\sqrt{N_r}}\,\middle|\,S_r=k_r
 \right)
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathcal N\left(\frac{\gamma a}{\sigma^2},
                 b-\frac{\gamma^2}{\sigma^2}\right).
\tag{A2}
\]

A zero limiting variance is allowed. All conditioning probabilities are positive eventually by (A1).

### Proof

Write `Y_r=1_{X_r=1}`. The limiting covariances are

\[
 \operatorname{Var}Y_r\to b,
 \quad\operatorname{Var}X_r\to\sigma^2,
 \quad\operatorname{Cov}(Y_r,X_r)
 =q_r(1-\mu_r)\to\gamma.
\]

The covariance formula uses `Y_r X_r=Y_r`; it is precisely the term lost by pretending that the counts in different categories are independent.

For fixed real `s`, Fourier inversion in the integer variable gives

\[
 \mathbb E\left[e^{is(B_r-N_rq_r)/\sqrt{N_r}};
                    S_r=k_r\right]
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 e^{-it(k_r-N_r\mu_r)}
 \left(\mathbb E e^{it(X_r-\mu_r)
                  +is(Y_r-q_r)/\sqrt{N_r}}\right)^{N_r}dt.
\tag{A3}
\]

Set `t=v/sqrt(N_r)`. Uniform integrability of squared values, together with boundedness of `Y_r`, gives the ordinary quadratic characteristic-function expansion for every fixed `s,v`:

\[
 \left(\mathbb E e^{i[v(X_r-\mu_r)+s(Y_r-q_r)]/\sqrt{N_r}}\right)^{N_r}
 \to\exp\left[-\frac12(\sigma^2v^2+2\gamma sv+bs^2)\right].
\tag{A4}
\]

For completeness, the remainder is controlled by truncating the centered linear combination at a fixed level, using the Taylor remainder there, and then using uniform integrability of its square on the complement. This yields `N_r * remainder -> 0`; no third moment or stable-domain assumption is needed.

The atoms at 0 and 1 provide the required integrable Fourier bound. For

\[
 \phi_r(t,s)=\mathbb E e^{itX_r+isY_r/\sqrt{N_r}},
\]

an independent-copy expansion gives

\[
 1-|\phi_r(t,s)|^2
 \ge 2\mathbb P(X_r=0)\mathbb P(X_r=1)
            [1-\cos(t+s/\sqrt{N_r})].
\tag{A5}
\]

Fix a small `delta>0`. For `|t|<=delta` and all sufficiently large rows, (A5) implies

\[
 |\phi_r(t,s)|^{N_r}
 \le \exp[-cN_r(t+s/\sqrt{N_r})^2].
\]

After the substitution this is `exp[-c(v+s)^2]`, integrable on the real line. On `delta<=|t|<=pi`, the right side of (A5) is bounded below by a positive row-independent constant for large `r`, so that part of the integral is `O(exp(-c_delta N_r))`. Centering does not affect the modulus.

Dominated convergence in (A3) therefore yields

\[
 \sqrt{N_r}\mathbb E\left[e^{is(B_r-N_rq_r)/\sqrt{N_r}};
                    S_r=k_r\right]
 \to\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-iav}\exp[-(\sigma^2v^2+2\gamma sv+bs^2)/2],dv.
\tag{A6}
\]

At `s=0` this is (A1). Dividing the Gaussian integral at general `s` by its value at zero gives

\[
 \exp\left(i\frac{\gamma a}{\sigma^2}s
      -\frac12\left(b-\frac{\gamma^2}{\sigma^2}\right)s^2\right).
\]

The variance is nonnegative by Cauchy-Schwarz. Levy's continuity theorem proves (A2). This completes the lemma.

### Application to the archived rare-pair mechanism

For a fixed subcritical law `pi` of mean `nu<1`, finite variance `sigma^2>0`, and `pi_0,pi_1>0`, take `X_r` to have `pi` restricted to the bulk and earlier stages and renormalized. Truncations of a fixed finite-second-moment distribution satisfy the uniform-integrability hypothesis. If the two surviving rare branches give residual totals

\[
 k_r^\pm=(n_r-1)\mu_r\mp A\sqrt{n_r}+O(1),
\]

and `sqrt(n_r)(q_r-q)->0`, then (A2) gives conditional branch limits

\[
 \mathcal N(\mp a_*,v_*),\qquad
 a_*=\frac{Aq(1-\nu)}{\sigma^2},
 \quad v_*=q(1-q)-\frac{q^2(1-\nu)^2}{\sigma^2}.
\tag{A7}
\]

If the construction additionally proves that exactly one rare atom occurs with conditional probability `1-o(1)` and that its sign has asymptotic probabilities `1/2,1/2`, the resulting limit is

\[
 \tfrac12\mathcal N(-a_*,v_*)+
 \tfrac12\mathcal N(a_*,v_*).
\tag{A8}
\]

This is not Gaussian when `A>0`, since `q>0`, `nu<1`, and its characteristic function `exp(-v_*s^2/2)cos(a_*s)` has zeros. Thus the corrected conditional argument really would finish the analytic count step of the subcritical refutation.

The full lacunary construction and its conditioning dominance are **not asserted proved in this report**. They are explicit remaining assembly obligations. Formula (A8) is conditional on them.

## B. Janson memo audit: exact defects and remaining obligations

The source is
`probability_campaign_2026_09/downloads_memos/Probability_F0064_Janson_problem_20_2_outdegree_counts_not_normal_in_general_2026-09-14.md`.

### Defect B1: false independence

For one truncated summand write

\[
 Y=\mathbf1_{\{X=1\}},\qquad Z=X\mathbf1_{\{X\ne0,1\}}.
\]

Then `YZ=0` almost surely, so

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Y,Z)=-q'\mathbb E Z.
\]

It is strictly negative whenever there is medium mass. For `n-1` boxes,
`Cov(B,M)=-(n-1)q' E Z`. The memo explicitly says the variables are independent when bounding the conditional medium deviation. They are not. The product factorization used again in Theorem B is likewise unjustified.

### Defect B2: omitted variance terms

Ignoring the tiny renormalization only to display the issue, put `c=E Z` and `V=E Z^2`. Then

\[
 \operatorname{Var}X=q+V-(q+c)^2
 =q(1-q)+V-2qc-c^2.
\]

The memo uses `q(1-q)+V`. The missing correction is a fixed nonzero quantity as `r` tends to infinity; it is not part of the displayed vanishing error. Its optional two-Gaussian formula also treats the Bernoulli and medium components as independent, so it must be replaced by (A7).

### Defect B3: nonuniform union-bound estimate

The subcritical no-rare branch estimate claims, for every `s<r`,

\[
 (5er/n_s^3)^{n/(5rn_s)}\le 2^{-n/(5rn_{r-1})}.
\]

For fixed `n_1`, the base `5er/n_1^3` eventually exceeds 1. Thus this displayed uniform step is false as `r->infinity`.

A promising repair is a single Bernstein inequality for the entire truncated sum. If its variance stays bounded, its maximum value is at most a constant times `n_{r-1}`, and its needed excess is at least `c n_r`, then

\[
 \mathbb P(R_{n_r}-\mathbb ER_{n_r}\ge c n_r)
 \le \exp[-c' n_r/n_{r-1}].
\]

With `n_r>=n_{r-1}^4`, this is smaller than the polynomial rare-route weight. A final revised proof should write all constants and ensure the asserted support bound; I did not certify that full assembly here.

### Critical sketch: do not label solved

RANKING itself notes that the stated `n_r=n_{r-1}^r` comparison need not dominate the rare-route moderate-deviation cost; it requires a substantially stronger scale separation. Additional issues are visible directly:

* The same false `B,M` independence appears in the tilted conditional law.
* A mass-adjusting atom with probability `n_r^{-4}` used to supply a prescribed mean contribution may have size of order `n_r^4` times that contribution. At the next stage this contradicts an unqualified claim that all earlier summands are bounded by `n_{r-1}`. The actual maximum support must be carried through every estimate.
* The claim `V_{r-1} asymp (log n_{r-1})^2/epsilon` ignores the possible variance of those mass-adjusting atoms.
* Exact stage-mean adjustment using integer atom sizes, while retaining the announced probabilities and the recursive branch ratio, is not specified.
* The one-rare-atom domination must exclude all other rare/adjusting/later possibilities relative to the exact denominator, with estimates uniform in the final recursively constructed law.

The finite-variance conditional lemma above does not settle any of these infinite-variance obligations. In particular, simply increasing scale growth would not repair the probabilistic factorization.

## C. Exact counterexample to the Aldous-Fill memo's Proposition 7

The source is
`probability_campaign_2026_09/downloads_memos/Probability_I0026_aldous_fill_449_flow_cut_2026-09-14.md`.

That memo defines the optimal source-to-stationary flow congestion `F(G)=inf_f psi(f)` and the cut lower bound

\[
 B(G)=\max_{S}\frac{2\pi(S)\pi(S^c)}{Q(S,S^c)}.
\]

Its Proposition 7 claims `F(G)=B(G)<=2 tau_2` whenever `G` has no `K_4` minor. It cites from memory a false assertion that every weighted shortest-path metric on such a graph belongs to the cut cone.

### The graph and exact values

Let `G=K_{2,3}`, with bipartition `A={a_1,a_2}`, `B={b_1,b_2,b_3}`. It has six edges. For simple random walk,

\[
 \pi(a_i)=\tfrac14,\qquad \pi(b_j)=\tfrac16,
 \qquad c_e=\tfrac1{12}.
\]

Its graph distance is 1 across parts and 2 within a part between distinct vertices. Therefore

\[
 \overline d=\sum_{x,y}\pi(x)\pi(y)d_G(x,y)
 =\tfrac14+\tfrac13+\tfrac12=\tfrac{13}{12}.
\]

The normalized metric `d=2d_G` is feasible for the memo's LP dual, since the sum over the six undirected edges of `c_e d(e)` is 1. It yields

\[
 F(G)\ge2\overline d=\tfrac{13}{6}.
\tag{C1}
\]

In fact equality holds. Assign nonnegative lengths to the six edges with their fixed weighted sum. The product-demand mean shortest-path distance is a concave function of the edge-length vector, being a positive sum of minima of linear path lengths. Averaging an arbitrary vector over the automorphism group preserves its normalization and does not decrease this objective. Edge transitivity makes the averaged vector constant. Thus the uniform vector is optimal and

\[
 F(K_{2,3})=\tfrac{13}{6}.
\tag{C2}
\]

For any cut containing `a` vertices in the two-vertex part and `b` vertices in the three-vertex part,

\[
 \pi(S)=\frac a4+\frac b6,
 \qquad |\delta S|=3a+2b-2ab,
\]

so its cut value is

\[
 \frac{24(a/4+b/6)(1-a/4-b/6)}{3a+2b-2ab}.
\]

Checking the finitely many `0<=a<=2`, `0<=b<=3` possibilities, excluding the trivial cuts, gives the maximum at `(a,b)=(1,1)` or `(1,2)`:

\[
 B(K_{2,3})=\tfrac{35}{18},\qquad
 \frac{F(K_{2,3})}{B(K_{2,3})}=\tfrac{39}{35}>1.
\tag{C3}
\]

This graph has no `K_4` minor: it has a tree decomposition with bags `{a_1,a_2,b_j}` arranged along a path, so its treewidth is at most 2. Alternatively it is a standard series-parallel graph obtained as three length-two paths in parallel.

Even the proposed bound `F<=2 tau_2` fails. The transition eigenvalues are `1,0,0,0,-1`, hence `tau_2=1`, while `13/6>2`. Its diameter is 2, however, so

\[
 \frac{F}{\tau_2\Delta}=\tfrac{13}{12}<2.
\]

Thus the original diameter conjecture survives this example.

### Direct cut-cone obstruction

The five-point metric gives a simple explanation of the mistaken citation. Put coefficient `+1` on each `b_j` and `-1` on each `a_i`. Their sum is 1. Every cut metric satisfies the associated pentagonal inequality

\[
 \sum_{j<k}d(b_j,b_k)+d(a_1,a_2)
 -\sum_{i,j}d(a_i,b_j)\le0.
\]

Indeed for a cut the left side is `z(1-z)` for an integer `z`, so it is nonpositive; nonnegative sums of cut metrics inherit the inequality. For `d_G` the value is `6+2-6=2>0`. Hence this shortest-path metric is not in the cut cone, with an exact certificate requiring no literature premise.
