## Results and scope

The following three claims are proved, with the star center always an **actual point coordinate**.

1. **Complete interior zero-curvature classification.** Let
   \[
   0\prec K=\begin{pmatrix}a&b^*\\b&C\end{pmatrix}\prec I,
   \qquad
   A=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&v^*\\v&0\end{pmatrix},
   \]
   where the first coordinate is a specified point \(i\). Let \(B\) be the connected component of \(i\) in the undirected coordinate graph with edges \(\{j,k\}\) whenever \(K_{jk}\ne0\), \(j\ne k\). Then
   \[
   \boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]=0
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \alpha=0\ \text{and}\ v_j=0\ \text{for every }j\in B\setminus\{i\}.}
   \tag{A}
   \]
   Thus a nonzero zero-curvature direction creates edges from \(i\) only into other coordinate components. In particular, if the graph of \(K\) is connected, the entropy Hessian is negative definite on its star subspace.

2. **Every nonzero interior flat direction has an exact KL identity and a strictly negative leading quartic term.** Under the right side of (A), every atom is exactly
   \[
   p_S(K+tA)=p_S(K)+t^2c_S.
   \]
   For every feasible \(t\),
   \[
   \boxed{H(K+tA)=H(K)-D_{\rm KL}(P_{K+tA}\Vert P_K).}
   \tag{B}
   \]
   If \(A\ne0\), then
   \[
   \boxed{H(K+tA)=H(K)-\frac{t^4}{2}\kappa+O(t^6),
   \qquad \kappa=\sum_S\frac{c_S^2}{p_S(K)}>0.}
   \tag{C}
   \]
   Every odd Taylor coefficient vanishes on this star line; \(H^{(4)}(0)=-12\kappa<0\). This differs from general zero-diagonal lines through diagonal kernels, where higher odd coefficients can occur.

3. **Complete chord-equality classification, including every boundary case.** Suppose \(K_0,K_1\) are arbitrary Hermitian positive contractions with the same complementary block \(C\), and write
   \[
   K_1-K_0=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&v^*\\v&0\end{pmatrix}.
   \]
   For \(0\le t\le1\), define the chord gap
   \[
   \mathcal G(t)=H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)-(1-t)H(K_0)-tH(K_1).
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal G(t)\ge t(1-t)
   \max\left\{2\alpha^2,
       \frac49(1-t+t^2)\|v\|_\infty^4\right\}.}
   \tag{D}
   \]
   For \(R=E\setminus\{i\}=\varnothing\), set \(\|v\|_\infty=0\). Thus, at every \(0<t<1\), chord equality holds **if and only if \(K_0=K_1\)**. Distinct star-slice kernels always give strict chords, including when the whole segment is singular. The constants in (D) are not claimed to be sharp.

The structural input behind (A) is a more general exact statement: for an increasing Boolean set function \(g\) and an interior DPP kernel \(C\), the kernel of \(\nabla\mathbb E_Cg\) consists precisely of the coordinate directions in variables on which \(g\) does not depend. Positive definiteness is proved on the essential-variable subspace; it is not assumed.

These results do not prove unrestricted Lyons entropy concavity. Historical novelty is not claimed.

## 1. Inputs and fixed-coordinate notation

For a finite coordinate set \(E\), let \(P_K\) be the DPP with inclusion probabilities \(P_K(U\subseteq X)=\det K_U\). Write \(p_S(K)=P_K(X=S)\), use natural logarithms, and put
\[
H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq E}p_S(K)\log p_S(K).
\]

The existing star-slice proof in `agents/010.md`, independently audited in `agents/017.md`, supplies the following identity at an interior kernel. Freeze
\[
f(S)=-\log p_S(K),\qquad
g(T)=f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T)=\log\frac{p_T(K)}{p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K)},
\quad T\subseteq R.
\]
For any real set function \(u\), define
\[
F_u(C)=\mathbb E_{P_C}u,
\qquad DF_u(C)[D]=\operatorname{tr}(Q_u(C)D),\qquad Q_u(C)=Q_u(C)^*.
\]
Then \(g\) is increasing, and
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]=-
\underbrace{\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}}_{\operatorname{FI}}
-2v^*Q_g(C)v,\qquad Q_g(C)\succeq0.}
\tag{1}
\]

For completeness, the established non-elementary input is that \(0\preceq C_1\preceq C_2\preceq I\) implies \(P_{C_1}\preceq_{\rm st}P_{C_2}\). Differentiation in a rank-one positive direction gives \(Q_u(C)\succeq0\) for every increasing \(u\) at an interior \(C\). This is Theorem 2.9 of [Lyons (2014)](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2707v1). The same paper's equation (2.12) gives the standard atom identity
\[
p_S(K)=\det(I-K)\det L_S,
\qquad L=K(I-K)^{-1}\succ0.
\tag{2}
\]
The source was directly inspected in this run.

Useful elementary details from the star proof are recorded to keep the equality analysis independently checkable. For \(T\subseteq R\), set
\[
N_T=C-I_{R\setminus T},\qquad r_T=P_C(T)>0,
\qquad q_T=a-b^*N_T^{-1}b\in(0,1).
\]
The exact atoms and their first derivatives are
\[
p_{T\cup\{i\}}=r_Tq_T,\qquad p_T=r_T(1-q_T),
\qquad
q_T'=\alpha-2\Re(v^*N_T^{-1}b).
\tag{3}
\]
Every atom on a star line is a polynomial of degree at most two. One proof is to expand its signed mixed determinant along the distinguished row and column: the diagonal entry occurs affinely, and the off-diagonal column and row occur in a quadratic form. Equivalently,
\[
p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K+tA)
=r_T\big[a+t\alpha-(b+tv)^*N_T^{-1}(b+tv)\big],
\tag{4}
\]
with the other atom obtained by subtracting from \(r_T\). The polynomial assertion extends to boundary kernels without inverses.

The matrix \(C\), its coordinate basis, and the table \(g\) are fixed whenever \(Q_g(C)\) is differentiated. No arbitrary unitary rotation is applied to the entropy problem.

## 2. Strict gradient lemma for every increasing set function

Let \(R\) have \(m\ge1\) elements and let \(g:2^R\to\mathbb R\) be increasing. Define its essential-coordinate set by
\[
J(g)=\{j\in R:\Delta_jg(T)>0\text{ for at least one }T\subseteq R\setminus\{j\}\},
\qquad \Delta_jg(T)=g(T\cup\{j\})-g(T).
\]

**Lemma 1.** If \(0\prec C\prec I\), then
\[
\boxed{\ker Q_g(C)=\{v\in\mathbb C^R:v_j=0\text{ for all }j\in J(g)\}.}
\tag{5}
\]
More quantitatively, choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{\lambda_{\min}(C),\,1-\lambda_{\max}(C),\,1/2\},
\qquad
\delta_j=\varepsilon^{m-1}
\sum_{T\subseteq R\setminus\{j\}}\Delta_jg(T).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{Q_g(C)\succeq\operatorname{diag}(\delta_j:j\in R).}
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\delta_j>0\) exactly on \(J(g)\). The bound may be small; only its strict positivity is needed.

**Proof.** Write \(s=1-2\varepsilon>0\) and \(D=(C-\varepsilon I)/s\), an interior positive contraction. For a deterministic subset \(X\), form \(Y\) by flipping each membership bit independently with probability \(\varepsilon\). Let
\[
h(X)=\mathbb E[g(Y)\mid X].
\]
If \(X\sim P_D\), then \(Y\sim P_{\varepsilon I+sD}=P_C\). Indeed, for every \(U\subseteq R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(U\subseteq Y)
&=\mathbb E\prod_{j\in U}(\varepsilon+s1_{\{j\in X\}})\\
&=\sum_{V\subseteq U}\varepsilon^{|U|-|V|}s^{|V|}\det D_V
=\det(\varepsilon I+sD)_U.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The last equality is the principal-minor expansion of a determinant. Thus, as an identity in a neighborhood of \(D\),
\[
F_h(D)=F_g(\varepsilon I+sD),\qquad Q_h(D)=sQ_g(C).
\tag{8}
\]

For \(j\notin X\), the distribution of \(Y\setminus\{j\}\) is the same whether \(j\) is added to \(X\) or not. The probability that \(j\in Y\) increases by \(s\). Consequently
\[
\Delta_jh(X)=s\,\mathbb E[\Delta_jg(Y\setminus\{j\})\mid X\setminus\{j\}].
\tag{9}
\]
Every possible value of \(Y\setminus\{j\}\) has probability at least \(\varepsilon^{m-1}\), since all its independent bit probabilities belong to \(\{\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon\}\). All the differences \(\Delta_jg\) are nonnegative. Hence \(\Delta_jh(X)\ge s\delta_j\).

It follows that
\[
\widetilde h(X)=h(X)-s\sum_{j\in X}\delta_j
\]
is increasing. The established gradient-positivity consequence of stochastic domination, applied at \(D\), gives
\[
Q_h(D)-s\operatorname{diag}(\delta_j)=Q_{\widetilde h}(D)\succeq0.
\]
Equation (8) proves (6).

If \(j\notin J(g)\), every \(\Delta_jg(T)\) is zero, so \(g\) is independent of that variable. Thus \(g\) depends only on \(X\cap J(g)\), and \(F_g(C)\) depends only on \(C_{J(g)}\) by the DPP marginal identity. Therefore every row and column of \(Q_g(C)\) outside \(J(g)\) is zero. Together with (6), this proves (5). The empty \(R\) case has an empty gradient and is immediate. ∎

This lemma covers arbitrary complex Hermitian \(C\). The independent flips act on the original subset coordinates; they do not rotate the kernel. It is false to replace its interior hypothesis by arbitrary positive contractions: for example, for \(g(X)=1_{\{1,2\}\subseteq X}\) one has \(Q_g(C)=\operatorname{adj}C\), which is zero at \(C=0\) although both variables are essential.

## 3. Essential variables of the frozen entropy odds

**Lemma 2.** For the entropy odds \(g\) in Section 1,
\[
J(g)=B\setminus\{i\},
\tag{10}
\]
where \(B\) is the coordinate graph component of \(i\) in \(K\).

**Proof.** First use the coordinate graph of \(L\) from (2). The graph-component partitions of \(K\) and \(L\) coincide. In fact, for every coordinate partition, \(K\) is block diagonal across that partition if and only if \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}\) is; the converse uses \(K=L(I+L)^{-1}\). This asserts equality of connected-component partitions, not equality of the individual edge sets.

If \(j\) is outside the component of \(i\) in \(L\), all principal determinants factor over the components. In
\[
g(T)=\log\frac{\det L_T}{\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}},
\tag{11}
\]
every factor from components not containing \(i\) cancels. Thus \(g\) is independent of \(j\).

Conversely, suppose \(j\) is in the same component as \(i\). Take a shortest graph path
\[
x_0=i,x_1,\ldots,x_d=j
\]
in \(L\), and condition on the internal vertex set \(T=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{d-1}\}\). A shortest path is induced: its selected principal matrix has no edges skipping a path vertex. In particular, \(L_T\), in path order, is Hermitian tridiagonal with nonzero neighboring entries.

Let \(z\) be the off-diagonal \((i,j)\) entry in the Schur complement of \(L_T\) in \(L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}\). The tridiagonal endpoint cofactor gives
\[
\boxed{z=(-1)^{d-1}
\frac{L_{x_0x_1}L_{x_1x_2}\cdots L_{x_{d-1}x_d}}
{\det L_T}\ne0.}
\tag{12}
\]
For \(d=1\), this is simply \(z=L_{ij}\), with the empty determinant equal to one. For \(d\ge2\), expand
\(z=-L_{i,x_1}(L_T^{-1})_{x_1,x_{d-1}}L_{x_{d-1},j}\); the inverse endpoint cofactor is the product of the internal upper off-diagonal entries, with its alternating sign, divided by \(\det L_T\). This proves (12). There is no cancellation among multiple paths, because only this shortest path's principal submatrix is conditioned on.

Write the full two-by-two Schur complement as
\[
\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\\overline z&w\end{pmatrix}\succ0.
\]
Using (11),
\[
\Delta_jg(T)
=\log\frac{\det L_{T\cup\{j\}}\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}}
{\det L_T\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}}
=\log\frac{uw}{uw-|z|^2}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
All denominators are strictly positive. The modulus square handles arbitrary complex phases. Thus \(j\) is essential, proving (10). ∎

Combining Lemmas 1 and 2 gives the concrete matrix-kernel statement
\[
\boxed{\ker Q_g(C)=\mathbb C^{E\setminus B},
\qquad \operatorname{rank}Q_g(C)=|B|-1,}
\tag{14}
\]
where the space on the right is embedded as a coordinate subspace of \(\mathbb C^R\).

## 4. Proof of the complete interior curvature classification

Both terms on the right side of (1) are nonpositive. If their sum is zero, the Fisher term is zero, hence every atom derivative is zero. Summing these derivatives over atoms containing \(i\) gives
\[
0=\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb P_{K+tA}(i\in X)\Big|_{t=0}=\alpha.
\tag{15}
\]
The other term is zero exactly when \(v\in\ker Q_g(C)\), which by (14) means \(v_j=0\) on \(B\setminus\{i\}\). This proves necessity in (A).

For sufficiency, assume these two conditions. Put \(J=B\setminus\{i\}\) and \(O=E\setminus B\). The matrix \(C\) is block diagonal on \(J\sqcup O\), and the vector \(b\) is supported on \(J\). The vector \(v\) is supported on \(O\). For every \(T\subseteq R\), \(N_T^{-1}\) has this same block structure, so
\[
v^*N_T^{-1}b=0.
\]
Together with \(\alpha=0\), equation (3) gives \(q_T'=0\), hence every atom derivative is zero. Equation (14) gives \(Q_g(C)v=0\). Thus both terms in (1) vanish, completing the proof of (A).

Two useful consequences are immediate.

- The real nullity of the star Hessian is exactly \(2|E\setminus B|\) for complex Hermitian variations, and its rank is \(2|B|-1\). For real symmetric variations, the nullity is \(|E\setminus B|\). These are dimensions within the specified star subspace, not assertions about the full matrix Hessian.
- On any nonconstant affine star line, there is at most one interior-kernel point of zero curvature. If \(\alpha\ne0\), there are none. If \(\alpha=0\), choose \(j\) with \(v_j\ne0\). At a zero-curvature point, \(j\) is outside the component of \(i\), so in particular \(b_j+tv_j=0\). This affine complex equation has at most one real solution. The additional component conditions in (A) determine whether that candidate actually works.

In particular, first-order invariance of the DPP law is not enough for zero entropy curvature; the matrix term in (1) remains essential. Section 7 gives an exact complex example.

## 5. Exact KL identity and the quartic term

Retain a nonzero direction satisfying (A), and the partition \(B\sqcup O\). At the base point, \(K\) is block diagonal on this partition, so
\[
P_K=P_{K_B}\otimes P_{K_O}.
\tag{16}
\]
This factorization follows directly from the atom determinant or generating polynomial. Along \(K+tA\), both principal blocks \(K_B,K_O\) stay fixed. Hence the two block marginals of the DPP stay fixed, and its cross entropy with the product reference (16) stays constant. The entropy/KL identity consequently gives (B) at every feasible \(t\). Equivalently, the entropy loss is exactly the mutual information between \(X\cap B\) and \(X\cap O\).

Every atom is at most quadratic by (4), and all its first derivatives vanish by Section 4. Thus, exactly,
\[
p_S(t)=p_S(0)+t^2c_S,\qquad
\sum_Sc_S=0.
\tag{17}
\]
The KL identity also implies the exact cross-entropy cancellation
\[
\sum_Sc_S\log p_S(0)=0.
\tag{18}
\]
Since all \(p_S(0)>0\), the scalar logarithm expansion on the finite alphabet is convergent in a neighborhood of zero, yielding
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(P_t\Vert P_0)
&=\sum_Sp_S(0)\left(1+t^2\frac{c_S}{p_S(0)}\right)
\log\left(1+t^2\frac{c_S}{p_S(0)}\right)\\
&=\frac{t^4}{2}\sum_S\frac{c_S^2}{p_S(0)}
-\frac{t^6}{6}\sum_S\frac{c_S^3}{p_S(0)^2}+O(t^8).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
All odd powers vanish because the law is exactly even in \(t\).

To show strictness, select \(j\) with \(v_j\ne0\). It lies in \(O\), so \(K_{ij}=0\) at the base point. The two-point inclusion probability has the exact form
\[
\mathbb P_{K+tA}(i,j\in X)=K_{ii}K_{jj}-t^2|v_j|^2.
\tag{20}
\]
Therefore not all \(c_S\) can be zero. This proves \(\kappa>0\), (C), and
\[
H^{(4)}(0)=-12\kappa
=-3\sum_S\frac{(p_S''(0))^2}{p_S(0)}<0.
\tag{21}
\]
It also proves \(P_{K+tA}\ne P_K\) for every nonzero feasible \(t\), so (B) makes \(t=0\) a strict entropy maximum over the whole feasible line. In particular, a zero Hessian never supplies a nontrivial affine entropy interval here.

An explicit conditional formula, useful for evaluating \(\kappa\), is
\[
\kappa=\sum_{T\subseteq R}
\frac{r_T\big(v^*N_T^{-1}v\big)^2}{q_T(1-q_T)}.
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, equation (4) gives \(c_{T\cup\{i\}}=-r_Tv^*N_T^{-1}v\) and \(c_T=+r_Tv^*N_T^{-1}v\); pairing the two atom contributions gives (22). No individual \(N_T^{-1}\) is asserted to be positive.

### Carefully delimited boundary extension of the KL mechanism

The interior graph classification (A) is not claimed for singular kernels. However, the argument for (B)–(C) has the following separate valid extension. Suppose any positive contraction \(K\) is block diagonal on a coordinate partition \(B\sqcup O\) with \(i\in B\), and \(A\) has \(\alpha=0\) and \(v\) supported on \(O\). Assume there is at least one nonzero feasible \(t_1\).

The block marginals stay fixed, so (16) remains the product reference, and any atom absent from this product reference is absent from every feasible law. On its positive product support, (B) still holds and all logarithms are finite. Atoms outside that support are identically zero along the line, because they vanish on the feasible interval between zero and \(t_1\), and are polynomials. Diagonal sign conjugation by \(+1\) on \(B\), \(-1\) on \(O\) maps \(K+tA\) to \(K-tA\), so feasibility also holds on a two-sided interval about zero. This uses a coordinate sign change, not an arbitrary unitary rotation.

The cross-block atom derivatives vanish at zero even when the diagonal blocks are singular: in the mixed-determinant permutation expansion, every term crossing the partition uses at least two cross-block entries, so no term has degree one in \(t\). The atoms have degree at most two. Thus (17)–(21) apply with sums restricted to the positive product support. If \(A\ne0\), (20) again gives a strictly positive \(\kappa\). This proves the flat mechanism on these feasible boundary lines without differentiating a zero atom. It does not classify other possible boundary Hessian phenomena.

## 6. Strict chord theorem and its quantitative bound

This proof uses (1) but does not require the new gradient-kernel lemma. It thus independently rules out the false inference from isolated zero curvature to chord equality.

First assume \(K_0,K_1\) are interior and let \(F(t)=H(K_0+tA)\). At every \(t\in[0,1]\), (1) gives
\[
F''(t)\le-\operatorname{FI}(t).
\tag{23}
\]
For any event \(\mathcal A\), put \(m(t)=P_{K_0+tA}(\mathcal A)\). If it is a nonempty proper event, interior positivity gives \(0<m<1\). Applying Cauchy–Schwarz separately to the event and its complement gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}(t)\ge
\frac{m'(t)^2}{m(t)}+\frac{m'(t)^2}{1-m(t)}
=\frac{m'(t)^2}{m(t)(1-m(t))}\ge4m'(t)^2.
\tag{24}
\]

For \(\mathcal A=\{i\in X\}\), one has \(m'(t)=\alpha\). Hence \(F''\le-4\alpha^2\), and integrating with fixed endpoint values gives
\[
\mathcal G(t)\ge2\alpha^2t(1-t).
\tag{25}
\]

Now choose \(j\in R\), use \(\mathcal A=\{i,j\in X\}\), and write \(b_0\) for the off-diagonal vector of \(K_0\). Define
\[
c=|v_j|^2\ge0,
\qquad d=\alpha C_{jj}-2\Re(\overline{b_{0,j}}v_j).
\]
Since the complementary block is fixed,
\[
m(t)=(a_0+t\alpha)C_{jj}-|b_{0,j}+tv_j|^2,
\qquad m'(t)=d-2ct.
\tag{26}
\]
Equations (23)–(24) give \(F''(s)\le-4(d-2cs)^2\).

For \(0<t<1\), let the nonnegative Dirichlet Green kernel be
\[
G(t,s)=\begin{cases}s(1-t),&0\le s\le t,\\
t(1-s),&t\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
\]
The chord gap is \(\mathcal G(t)=\int_0^1G(t,s)(-F''(s))\,ds\). Define its first three moments in \(s\):
\[
M_0=\frac{t(1-t)}2,\quad
M_1=\frac{t(1-t)(1+t)}6,\quad
M_2=\frac{t(1-t)(1+t+t^2)}{12}.
\]
Completing the square gives the explicit lower bound
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G(t)
&\ge4\int_0^1G(t,s)(d-2cs)^2\,ds\\
&=4M_0\left(d-2c\frac{M_1}{M_0}\right)^2
  +16c^2\left(M_2-\frac{M_1^2}{M_0}\right)\\
&\ge\frac49c^2t(1-t)(1-t+t^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Here the exact moment identity is
\[
M_2-\frac{M_1^2}{M_0}=rac{t(1-t)(1-t+t^2)}{36}>0.
\]
Taking a coordinate of largest \(|v_j|\) and combining (25) with (27) proves (D) for interior endpoints. The endpoint parameters \(t=0,1\) follow by continuity.

For arbitrary boundary endpoints, regularize them by
\[
K_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I,
\qquad0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\tag{28}
\]
They are interior, have a common complementary block, and their direction parameters are \(\alpha_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)\alpha\), \(v_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)v\). Apply the proved bound and let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). All atom probabilities are polynomial in the kernel entries, and finite Shannon entropy is continuous with \(0\log0=0\). Both sides of (D) therefore converge to their stated values. No differentiation at a boundary kernel is used.

If \(K_0\ne K_1\), then either \(\alpha\ne0\) or \(v\ne0\). For \(0<t<1\), one of the two expressions inside the maximum in (D) is strictly positive. This proves every claimed strict chord, including chords of subsegments after renormalizing their endpoints. If \(K_0=K_1\), all gaps are zero. There is no unclassified boundary exception.

## 7. Nontrivial examples and exact certificates

### 7.1 A complex non-diagonal base with zero Hessian and a negative quartic

In the coordinate order \((1,2,3)\), distinguish point \(i=1\) and put
\[
w=\frac{1+i}{8},\quad z=\frac{1+2i}{10},\qquad
K=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&\overline w&0\\w&1/2&0\\0&0&1/3
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\overline z\\0&0&0\\z&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{29}
\]
The base is interior and non-diagonal. Its component containing point 1 is \(B=\{1,2\}\), and \(A\) joins it to point 3. Thus \(H''(0)=0\) by (A), even though the distinguished point already has a nonzero complex edge within its component.

The conditional probabilities in the first block are \(q=9/16,7/16\), each with marginal weight \(1/2\), so
\[
\sum_{T\subseteq\{2\}}\frac{r_T}{q_T(1-q_T)}=\frac{256}{63}.
\]
The outside point has marginal \(1/3\) and \(|z|^2=1/20\), giving the other factor in (22):
\[
\sum_{U\subseteq\{3\}}r_U\big(z^*(K_{\{3\}}-I_{\{3\}\setminus U})^{-1}z\big)^2
=\frac{|z|^4}{(1/3)(2/3)}=\frac9{800}.
\]
The product is \(\kappa=8/175\). Hence
\[
\boxed{H(K+tA)=H(K)-\frac4{175}t^4+O(t^6),
\qquad H''(0)=0,\quad H^{(4)}(0)=-\frac{96}{175}.}
\tag{30}
\]
This is not merely the diagonal-base example from worker 023.

### 7.2 A connected kernel with zero Fisher information but strict curvature

Let
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/8&0\\1/8&1/2&1/8\\0&1/8&1/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-i/10\\0&0&0\\i/10&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{31}
\]
All first atom derivatives vanish: a purely imaginary Hermitian variation through this real matrix has zero first derivative in every real principal determinant. The coefficient check below also verifies this directly. Yet the graph of \(K\) is connected, so (A) predicts strictly negative curvature.

For \(R=\{2,3\}\), the conditional odds \(\exp g(T)\), in the order \(\varnothing,\{2\},\{3\},\{2,3\}\), are
\[
(7/8,\ 9/8,\ 8/9,\ 8/7).
\]
Their multiplicative mixed difference equals one, so \(g\) is modular and
\[
Q_g(C)=\operatorname{diag}\big(\log(9/7),\log(64/63)\big).
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{FI}=0,
\qquad H''(0)=-\frac1{50}\log\frac{64}{63}<0.}
\tag{32}
\]
