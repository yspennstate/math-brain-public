## Outcome and status

Three focused claims are proved below.

1. **PROVED:** For any fixed rank-one Parseval compression covering every self-adjoint contraction with coefficient bound \(K\), the balanced-reflection lower bound is
   \[
   K\ge \frac{4^m}{\binom{2m}{m}}\quad(\mathbb C),\qquad
   K\ge \frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(m/2+1)}{\Gamma((m+1)/2)}
   \quad(\mathbb R),\qquad m=\lfloor d/2\rfloor.
   \]
   These are exact evaluations of a particular obstruction, not assertions that these are the optimal universal coefficients.
2. **PROVED:** In both fields there are fixed finite Parseval frames with \(N=O(d^2)\) and universal bounded preimages with \(K=O(\sqrt d)\). A self-contained Gaussian construction below gives \(K\le 2\sqrt{11d}\) over \(\mathbb C\), and \(K\le\sqrt{34d}\) over \(\mathbb R\), with an explicit, deliberately large sufficient \(N\). Hence the optimal growth order is \(\Theta(\sqrt d)\), and quadratic frame cardinality is enough.
3. **PROVED, exact constants:** If the preimage assignment must be real-linear in the target matrix, the optimum over all finite rank-one Parseval frames and their linear right inverses is instead \(2d-1\) over \(\mathbb C\), and \(d\) over \(\mathbb R\). Finite weighted projective 2-designs attain these constants. The manuscript's inverse-frame-operator assignment is linear, so it cannot achieve even the optimal nonlinear \(\sqrt d\) order.

**DISPROVED:** The manuscript's dimension-independent coefficient bound for a single fixed positive compression. Both the claim and its stated linear construction fail quantitatively.

**NOT CLAIMED:** A solution of Matrix Spencer; a lower bound for arbitrary tuple-dependent lifts; exact optimal nonlinear constants; a frame with the displayed upper bound at every \(N\ge 4d^2\); a practical small numerical value for the sufficient cardinality constant; novelty of these connections to quantum measurement norms.

## Setup and the indispensable quantifiers

Let \(\mathbb F=\mathbb C\) or \(\mathbb R\), and \(d\ge2\). The case \(d=1\) has optimal universal and linear constants both equal to one. Write \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d)\) for Hermitian matrices or real symmetric matrices, respectively. This is a real vector space of dimension
\[
r_{\mathbb C}=d^2,\qquad r_{\mathbb R}=d(d+1)/2.
\]
All linearity below is over \(\mathbb R\). Use the pairing \(\langle A,H\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(AH)\), the operator norm \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}\), trace norm \(\|\cdot\|_1\), and Hilbert–Schmidt norm \(\|\cdot\|_2\).

Fix vectors \(v_j\in\mathbb F^d\), \(1\le j\le N\), with
\[
M_j=v_jv_j^*,\qquad \sum_jM_j=I_d,\qquad
\Phi(\lambda)=\sum_j\lambda_jM_j.
\]
The property under study is
\[
\forall A\in\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d),\quad
\|A\|_{\rm op}\le1
\ \Longrightarrow\
\exists\lambda\in[-K,K]^N:\ \Phi(\lambda)=A. \tag{U}
\]
The frame and \(\Phi\) are fixed before \(A\) or any tuple is supplied. The coefficients may depend on \(A\) without any linearity, regularity, or uniqueness requirement.

Taking \((Vx)_j=v_j^*x\) gives an isometry \(V:\mathbb F^d\to\mathbb F^N\) and \(\Phi(\lambda)=V^*\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)V\), exactly the manuscript's positive compression after absorbing its fixed unitary. Property (U) automatically gives simultaneous lifts of any finite tuple using that same \(V\), by choosing a preimage for each target separately.

The compact symmetric zonotope \(Z=\Phi([-1,1]^N)\) has support function
\[
h_Z(H)=\sum_j|\operatorname{tr}(HM_j)|.
\]
The operator-norm unit ball has support function \(\|H\|_1\). Finite-dimensional separation therefore gives the exact criterion
\[
\boxed{\text{(U)}\iff
\sum_j|v_j^*Hv_j|\ge K^{-1}\|H\|_1
\quad\text{for every }H\in\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d).} \tag{1}
\]
In particular, (U) forces \(\Phi\) to be surjective and \(N\ge r_{\mathbb F}\). This dimension conclusion concerns a fixed map covering the entire ball. It does not follow for a map designed only for a supplied short tuple.

## Claim 1: exact balanced-reflection moments and lower bounds

For any \(a>0\), if \(B\sim\operatorname{Beta}(a,a)\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|2B-1|
&=\frac{2}{\mathrm B(a,a)}
  \int_{1/2}^1(2x-1)[x(1-x)]^{a-1}\,dx\\
&=\frac{2}{a4^a\mathrm B(a,a)}
=\frac{\Gamma(a+1/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(a+1)}. \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
The integral follows by differentiating \([x(1-x)]^a\); the last equality is the gamma duplication formula.

First let \(d=2m\), and set \(H_0=\operatorname{diag}(I_m,-I_m)\). For uniform \(z\) on the unit sphere, write \(B=\sum_{i\le m}|z_i|^2\). Normalize independent standard Gaussian coordinates to obtain:

| Field | Distribution of \(B\) | \(c_{\mathbb F}(m):=\mathbb E|z^*H_0z|\) |
|---|---|---|
| Complex | \(\operatorname{Beta}(m,m)\) | \(\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(m+1/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(m+1)}=\frac{\binom{2m}{m}}{4^m}\) |
| Real | \(\operatorname{Beta}(m/2,m/2)\) | \(\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma((m+1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(m/2+1)}\) |

For a Haar unitary or orthogonal \(U\), put \(H=UH_0U^*\). For each fixed \(v\),
\[
\mathbb E_U|v^*Hv|=\|v\|^2c_{\mathbb F}(m).
\]
Since \(\sum_j\|v_j\|^2=d\), some \(U\) has
\[
\sum_j|v_j^*Hv_j|\le d\,c_{\mathbb F}(m).
\]
If (U) holds, represent this \(H\) by coefficients bounded by \(K\), and pair the identity with \(H\):
\[
d=\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j^*Hv_j
\le Kd\,c_{\mathbb F}(m).
\]
Thus \(K\ge c_{\mathbb F}(m)^{-1}\), as claimed. No condition on \(N\), on the individual vector lengths, or on the manner of selecting preimages is used.

For odd \(d=2m+1\), append one zero eigenvalue to \(H_0\). The squared mass \(T\) of a uniform sphere vector in its first \(2m\) coordinates has mean \(2m/d\). The direction in that subspace is independent of \(T\) and is uniform in dimension \(2m\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E|z^*H_0z|=\frac{2m}{d}c_{\mathbb F}(m),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(H_0^2)=2m,
\]
and the same proof gives exactly the same lower bound with \(m=\lfloor d/2\rfloor\), for \(d\ge2\).

For even \(d\to\infty\), the respective bounds are
\[
\boxed{
K\ge
\sqrt{\frac{\pi d}{2}}\left(1+\frac1{4d}+O(d^{-2})\right)
\quad(\mathbb C),
\qquad
K\ge
\frac{\sqrt{\pi d}}{2}\left(1+\frac1{2d}+O(d^{-2})\right)
\quad(\mathbb R).
} \tag{3}
\]
These gamma-ratio asymptotics strengthen the complex second-moment bound \(\sqrt{d+1}\) from agent 005. In particular, \(K\ge2\) for complex \(d=2\), \(K\ge8/3\) for complex \(d=4\), and \(K\ge\pi/2\) for real \(d=2\).

## Claim 2: a finite quadratic-size construction with \(K=O(\sqrt d)\)

The proof is included to avoid resting the order assertion entirely on a cited sparsification theorem. The cardinality constant is not optimized.

Set
\[
(\nu,b,r)=
\begin{cases}
(1,11,d^2),&\mathbb F=\mathbb C,\\
(2,17,d(d+1)/2),&\mathbb F=\mathbb R.
\end{cases}
\]
For each integer
\[
N\ge N_0:=
\left\lceil393216\,b\left[
r\log(1+32\sqrt b)+\log4\right]\right\rceil, \tag{4}
\]
sample independent standard Gaussian \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\in\mathbb F^d\), with \(\mathbb E g_jg_j^*=I_d\). In the complex case, each coordinate is \((X+iY)/\sqrt2\) with independent real \(N(0,1)\) variables. Put
\[
S=\sum_jg_jg_j^*,\qquad v_j=S^{-1/2}g_j.
\]
With probability at least \(1/2\), \(S\) is positive definite and these vectors satisfy (U) with
\[
\boxed{K=2\sqrt{\frac{bd}{\nu}}
=
\begin{cases}
2\sqrt{11d},&\mathbb F=\mathbb C,\\
\sqrt{34d},&\mathbb F=\mathbb R.
\end{cases}} \tag{5}
\]
In particular such a deterministic realization exists, and it is fixed independently of every future target.

### Gaussian moment bounds

For fixed \(H\), let \(q=g^*Hg\), \(t=\operatorname{tr}H\), and \(s_k=\operatorname{tr}(H^k)\). Define the Euclidean norm
\[
\sigma(H)=\sqrt{t^2+\nu s_2}.
\]
Gaussian independence gives \(\mathbb E q^2=\sigma(H)^2\). Direct fourth moments give
\[
\mathbb E q^4=
\begin{cases}
t^4+6t^2s_2+8ts_3+3s_2^2+6s_4,&\mathbb C,\\
t^4+12t^2s_2+32ts_3+12s_2^2+48s_4,&\mathbb R.
\end{cases} \tag{6}
\]
For completeness these follow from the centered cumulants of \(g^*Hg\): in the complex case \(\kappa_k=(k-1)!s_k\), and in the real case \(\kappa_k=2^{k-1}(k-1)!s_k\), for \(k\ge2\).

Use \(|s_3|\le s_2^{3/2}\), \(s_4\le s_2^2\). In the complex case,
\[
8|t|s_2^{3/2}\le8t^2s_2+2s_2^2,
\]
so (6) is at most \(t^4+14t^2s_2+11s_2^2\le11(t^2+s_2)^2\). In the real case,
\[
32|t|s_2^{3/2}\le32t^2s_2+8s_2^2,
\]
so (6) is at most \(t^4+44t^2s_2+68s_2^2\le17(t^2+2s_2)^2\).

Hölder's inequality in the form
\[
\mathbb E|q|\ge\frac{(\mathbb E q^2)^{3/2}}{(\mathbb E q^4)^{1/2}}
\]
and Cauchy–Schwarz now imply, writing \(f(H)=\mathbb E|g^*Hg|\) and \(a=b^{-1/2}\),
\[
a\,\sigma(H)\le f(H)\le\sigma(H),\qquad
f(H)\ge a\sqrt\nu\,\|H\|_2. \tag{7}
\]
Zero matrices cause no difficulty; apply the ratio inequality only when \(\sigma(H)>0\).

### Uniform concentration with an explicit sufficient sample size

Here are details of the elementary concentration estimate used in (4). Assume \(\sigma(H)=1\). Diagonalize \(H\), with eigenvalues \(h_i\). For \(|\theta|\le1/8\), the logarithmic moment generating function of \(q-t\) is
\[
\sum_i\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\nu^{k-1}\theta^kh_i^k}{k}.
\]
Bounding this series absolutely, using \(\nu s_2\le1\), \(\max_i|h_i|\le1\), and \(\nu\le2\), gives
\[
\log\mathbb E e^{\theta(q-t)}
\le\frac{\nu\theta^2s_2}{2(1-\nu|\theta|\max_i|h_i|)}
\le2\theta^2.
\]
Since \(|t|\le1\),
\[
\mathbb E e^{|q|/8}\le 2e^{1/8+1/32}<3.
\]
For \(Y=|q|-\mathbb E|q|\), this yields
\[
\mathbb E|Y|^k\le3\,16^k k!,\qquad k\ge2.
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb E e^{\theta Y}\le e^{1536\theta^2}\) for \(|\theta|\le1/32\), by summing its power series starting at order two. Chernoff's inequality with \(\theta=u/3072\), followed by the same argument for \(-Y\), gives
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
\left|\frac1N\sum_j|g_j^*Hg_j|-f(H)\right|>u
\right\}
\le2\exp(-Nu^2/6144),\qquad 0<u\le1. \tag{8}
\]

Let \(F_N(H)=N^{-1}\sum_j|g_j^*Hg_j|\), a seminorm. Take an \(\eta\)-net of the \(\sigma\)-unit sphere, where
\[
\eta=a/16,\qquad\delta=a/8.
\]
A volume packing argument gives a net of cardinality at most \((1+2/\eta)^r=(1+32\sqrt b)^r\). By (8), the union bound, and (4), with probability at least \(1/2\),
\[
|F_N(H_0)-f(H_0)|\le\delta
\]
at every net point \(H_0\).

On this event put \(A_*=\sup_{\sigma(H)=1}F_N(H)\), which is finite. The net and the triangle inequality give
\[
A_*\le\frac{1+\delta}{1-\eta}\le\frac65.
\]
For any \(\sigma\)-unit \(H\), approximation by a net point then gives
\[
|F_N(H)-f(H)|
\le\delta+\eta(A_*+1)
\le\frac{21a}{80}<\frac a3.
\]
Using homogeneity and (7), this proves simultaneously for all \(H\),
\[
\frac23 f(H)\le F_N(H)\le\frac43 f(H). \tag{9}
\]
For \(H=xx^*\), \(f(H)=\|x\|^2\); hence (9) also yields
\[
\frac{2N}{3}I_d\preceq S\preceq\frac{4N}{3}I_d. \tag{10}
\]

### Covariance normalization and bounded preimages

The normalized vectors form an exact Parseval frame. For any \(H\), let \(Q=S^{-1/2}HS^{-1/2}\). Equations (7)–(10) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j|v_j^*Hv_j|
&=NF_N(Q)\\
&\ge\frac{2N}{3}\,a\sqrt\nu\,\|Q\|_2\\
&\ge\frac{a\sqrt\nu}{2}\|H\|_2\\
&\ge\frac{a\sqrt\nu}{2\sqrt d}\|H\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate step uses \(\|H\|_2=\|S^{1/2}QS^{1/2}\|_2\le\|S\|_{\rm op}\|Q\|_2\). Criterion (1) proves (5).

This is a probabilistic existence construction with explicit matrix formulas. The report does not contain a sampled numerical frame at the large \(N_0\), and does not claim that the universal norm inequality has been certified for a particular realization. The proof supplies positive probability and hence existence.

Because \(r=\Theta(d^2)\), the quadratic cardinality order matches the necessary dimension bound. Combining Claims 1 and 2, the infimum of admissible universal \(K\) over all finite Parseval frames is \(\Theta(\sqrt d)\) in either field. The fixed constant \(4\) multiplying \(d^2\) in the manuscript is not established by this construction.

## Claim 3: linear right inverses cost exactly \(2d-1\) complex and \(d\) real

Now impose the additional requirement that a single real-linear map
\[
R:\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d)\longrightarrow\mathbb R^N,\qquad \Phi R=\mathrm{id},
\]
selects all preimages. Its norm is the largest coefficient over operator-norm contractions.

Discard zero frame vectors. Write
\[
M_j=w_jP_j,\qquad w_j=\|v_j\|^2>0,\qquad P_j=u_ju_j^*,\quad\|u_j\|=1.
\]
There are unique \(B_j\in\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d)\) with
\[
(RA)_j=\operatorname{tr}(B_jA),\qquad
\|R\|_{{\rm op}\to\infty}=\max_j\|B_j\|_1=:K_{\rm lin}.
\]
Taking the trace of \(\Phi R\) as an endomorphism of the real Hilbert space \(\mathcal H_{\mathbb F}(d)\) gives
\[
\sum_j w_j\operatorname{tr}(B_jP_j)=r.
\]
Applying \(\Phi R=\mathrm{id}\) to \(I_d\) and taking the ordinary matrix trace gives
\[
\sum_jw_j\operatorname{tr}B_j=d.
\]
Finally \(\|2P_j-I_d\|_{\rm op}=1\), so duality yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{\rm lin}d
&\ge\sum_jw_j\operatorname{tr}\!\left(B_j(2P_j-I_d)\right)\\
&=2r-d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore every such frame/right-inverse pair satisfies
\[
\boxed{K_{\rm lin}\ge2r/d-1
=
\begin{cases}
2d-1,&\mathbb C,\\
d,&\mathbb R.
\end{cases}} \tag{11}
\]
This proof does not assume equal frame-vector lengths or a particular inverse-frame formula. In particular, if \(N=r\) and (U) holds, then \(\Phi\) is an isomorphism, so its unique preimage rule is linear and incurs (11). Achieving the \(\sqrt d\) order requires more than the dimension-minimal number of frame vectors for sufficiently large \(d\).

### Finite attainment of the linear bound

A finite weighted projective 2-design exists in every dimension in either field. One direct justification is to take the Haar average of
\[
P_z^{\otimes2}=|z^{\otimes2}\rangle\langle z^{\otimes2}|
\]
on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb F^d)\). This is in the convex hull of its compact set of rank-one projectors. That space embeds into the Hermitian matrices of size \(D_2=\binom{d+1}{2}\), and the projectors have trace one. Carathéodory's theorem therefore gives weights \(p_j>0\), summing to one, on at most \(D_2^2\) unit vectors \(u_j\), matching this tensor moment exactly. Partial trace also gives \(\sum_jp_jP_j=I_d/d\).

The matched second-moment identities are
\[
\sum_jp_jP_j\operatorname{tr}(P_jA)
=
\begin{cases}
(A+(\operatorname{tr}A)I_d)/[d(d+1)],&\mathbb C,\\
(2A+(\operatorname{tr}A)I_d)/[d(d+2)],&\mathbb R.
\end{cases} \tag{12}
\]
They follow either from sphere coordinate moments or from the corresponding Gaussian identity and its independent radius.

Set \(v_j=\sqrt{dp_j}\,u_j\). In the complex case define
\[
(RA)_j=(d+1)u_j^*Au_j-\operatorname{tr}A,
\qquad B_j=(d+1)P_j-I_d.
\]
Equation (12) gives \(\Phi R(A)=A\), while the eigenvalues of \(B_j\) are \(d,-1,\ldots,-1\), giving \(\|R\|_{{\rm op}\to\infty}=2d-1\).

In the real case take
\[
(RA)_j=\frac{d+2}{2}u_j^\top Au_j-\frac12\operatorname{tr}A,
\qquad B_j=\frac{d+2}{2}P_j-\frac12I_d.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \((d+1)/2,-1/2,\ldots,-1/2\), giving norm exactly \(d\), and (12) again gives a right inverse. Thus the bounds in (11) are attained by finite frames.

This exact linear-selection result and Claim 2 concern different requirements. The \(\sqrt d\) upper bound guarantees a bounded preimage for each target by convex separation; it does not supply a linear preimage map. The manuscript's \(R=\Phi^*(\Phi\Phi^*)^{-1}\), wherever defined, is linear and is subject to (11).

## Consequences for the manuscript and limits of the repair

The proposed proposition can be replaced by a fixed universal positive-compression result with a growing \(K=\Theta(\sqrt d)\), permitting nonlinear choices of coefficients and a sufficiently large absolute multiple of \(d^2\) coordinates. Its assertion that bounded-lift constants present no obstruction is false: increasing the number of coordinates cannot make \(K\) absolute.

The exact normalization error identified by agent 005 remains: from \(\Phi(\Lambda)=A\) and \(D=\Lambda/K\), one obtains \(\Phi(D)=A/K\). The displayed “equivalent” contraction formulation in the source cannot lose this factor.

For the stated inverse-frame proof, merely correcting the spectral scale of \(\Phi\Phi^*\) cannot restore the claimed conclusion. Any linear selection has the larger loss in (11). A replacement that achieves \(\sqrt d\) must also change the manner in which preimages are selected.

Neither the lower-bound Haar averaging nor the dimension argument supplies a lower bound for a map chosen after a tuple of length \(n=d\) is supplied. As a basic quantifier check, a single contraction has a tuple-dependent spectral compression with \(K=1\), and any commuting tuple does likewise in its common eigenbasis. This does not contradict any result above.

An upper discrepancy estimate transported through such a lift includes the actual coefficient factor \(K\). Its inflation does not establish a lower bound on discrepancy itself. No unrestricted finite-lift no-go theorem for Matrix Spencer is obtained here.

## Primary-source check and attribution

The connection is established literature, not a new resolution of a famous open problem. In the usual language \(h_Z(H)\) is a rank-one POVM distinguishability norm; for the uniform measurement it is \(d\,\mathbb E_z|z^*Hz|\), also equal to \(\mathbb E_g|g^*Hg|\).

- Guillaume Aubrun and Cécilia Lancien, *Zonoids and sparsification of quantum measurements*, [arXiv:1309.6003](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6003), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.6003). Proposition 4.1 compares the uniform norm with \(\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(H^2)+(\operatorname{tr}H)^2}\). Theorem 4.3 constructs a rank-one POVM by normalizing \(N\) independent Haar vectors and preserves the uniform norm within \(1\pm\varepsilon\) for \(N>C\varepsilon^{-2}|\log\varepsilon|d^2\). At any fixed small \(\varepsilon\), this already implies the complex quadratic-cardinality, \(\sqrt d\)-coefficient conclusion. Our Gaussian proof above is included independently.
- William Matthews, Stephanie Wehner, Andreas Winter, *Distinguishability of quantum states under restricted families of measurements with an application to quantum data hiding*, [arXiv:0810.2327](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2327), [full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.2327). Theorem 9 addresses the uniform POVM's domination constant on traceless inputs and gives its asymptotic \(\sqrt{2/(\pi d)}\). Its discussion calls the balanced-rank exact minimizer a conjecture and explicitly says it was not proved there. That theorem should not be silently imported as an exact all-Hermitian minimization statement for (1).
