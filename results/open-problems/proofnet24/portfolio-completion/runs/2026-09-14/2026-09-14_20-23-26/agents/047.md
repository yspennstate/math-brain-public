## Result

**P15-AVG-LOOGATE-047 — PROVED, pending independent audit.** Distribution-averaged squared deletion energies and averaged functional second moments suffice for the actual random full-fit squared-risk gate. For a scalar functional family, the central bound is

\[
\left\|Pf_S-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f_{S^{-i}}(Z_i)\right\|_{L^2(S,W)}
\le
\beta_n+
\sqrt{\frac{B_{n-1}^2+(n-1)\beta_{n-1}^2}{n}}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here the deletion norms average over training samples, a uniformly chosen deletion, an independent deployment query, and any independent auxiliary randomness \(W\). No uniform-over-training-set hypothesis appears. The bound targets \(Pf_S\), not its expectation over fitting samples. The leave-two-out covariance calculation removes the earlier coarse \(\sqrt n\) stability penalty.

**P15-AVG-SEPARATION-047 — PROVED.** An explicit Gaussian sample-mean correction has infinite uniform functional moments and infinite worst-training-set deletion norms, while all averaged assumptions hold and the gate is nontrivial with explicit finite-sample probability.

**P15-AVG-PLUGIN-CE-047 — PROVED.** Replacing theoretical averaged energies by observed same-sample energies is invalid without a separate upper-confidence argument. A bounded, fully honest pipeline has every observed full and double deletion energy equal to zero on an event of probability \(0.0475525\ldots\); at claimed failure level \(\delta=0.04\), its resulting plug-in gate strictly increases population risk on that event. This remains true when the averaged moment constants are supplied exactly.

These are deductions relative to the inspected P15 report. Global novelty is not established. The underlying stability and covariance methods have substantial prior literature; Section 6 records the relevant checks. This is not an unconditional guarantee for the historical neural pipeline or a solution of a famous universal conjecture.

## 1. Setup and exact averaged hypotheses

Let \(n\ge3\), let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) be iid with law \(P\), and write \(S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\). In P15, \(Z_i=(U_i,G(U_i))\), so integration over an independent \(Z\) is equivalently integration over an independent deployment input \(U\). Deletions preserve the order of the remaining sequence. Thus the argument also permits order-sensitive learners.

Let \(W\) be independent of these observations and of a fresh deployment observation. It may contain an independent master random seed and genuinely disjoint auxiliary validation data. Given \((T,W)\), the entire fitted pipeline is a measurable function. Every occurrence of the same \((T,W)\) denotes the same fitted output. The coupling used in the stability energies must be the coupling actually used for the full and deleted fits. A same-seed stability calculation cannot justify arbitrary differently seeded refits.

For the scalar lemma, write this output as \(f_{T,W}(z)\), and suppress \(W\) when unambiguous. Fits at sizes \(n,n-1,n-2\) must be defined. For an iid ordered sample \(T_m\), an independent uniform index \(I\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and an independent fresh \(Z\), define

\[
\beta_m^2
=\mathbb E_{W,T_m,I,Z}
\left|f_{T_m,W}(Z)-f_{T_m^{-I},W}(Z)\right|^2,
\qquad m\in\{n,n-1\},
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
B_{n-1}^2=\mathbb E_{W,T_{n-1},Z}|f_{T_{n-1},W}(Z)|^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Assume only that these three numbers are finite. Known deterministic upper bounds can replace their exact values everywhere below. All expectations include the training sample; (2) and (3) are not bounds for every realized fit. Separate moment assumptions at sizes \(n\) and \(n-2\) are unnecessary: averaging

\[
|f_T|^2\le2|f_{T^{-I}}|^2+2|f_T-f_{T^{-I}}|^2
\]

and the reversed inequality shows finite averaged second moments at those sizes from (2)–(3).

For every realized full fit, \(Pf_{S,W}=\mathbb E_Z[f_{S,W}(Z)\mid S,W]\) is its deployment target. Define

\[
\widehat f_{\rm loo}=\frac1n\sum_i f_{S^{-i},W}(Z_i),
\qquad F_f=Pf_{S,W}-\widehat f_{\rm loo}.
\tag{4}
\]

Each held-out point must be absent from every fitted stage: mean training, learned representation, residual construction, kernel/ridge/scale selection, preprocessing fitted from data, and final correction fitting. Selection using the fitting rows is part of \(T\mapsto f_{T,W}\) and must be recomputed after deletion. It cannot be conditioned away as independent auxiliary information. The final certifying scalar gate is handled after the two confidence bounds and needs no separate tuning penalty.

The conclusions below hold jointly over \((S,W)\). With only unconditional averages over \(W\), no conditional guarantee for every fixed seed or auxiliary validation sample is asserted. Conditional averaged bounds, if available, give the same theorem conditional on that auxiliary information.

## 2. Claim 1: full-fit LOO bound and safe scalar gate

### 2.1 Scalar second-moment theorem

Under (2)–(3), define

\[
Q_f=\frac{B_{n-1}^2+(n-1)\beta_{n-1}^2}{n},
\qquad K_f=\beta_n+\sqrt{Q_f}.
\tag{5}
\]

Then (1) holds. In particular, for every fixed \(0<\alpha<1\),

\[
\mathbb P\{|F_f|>K_f/\sqrt\alpha\}\le\alpha.
\tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Decompose the random error as

\[
F_f=D+H,
\quad
D=\frac1n\sum_i P(f_S-f_{S^{-i}}),
\quad
H=\frac1n\sum_i h_i,
\quad
h_i=Pf_{S^{-i}}-f_{S^{-i}}(Z_i).
\tag{7}
\]

Jensen, first over deletions and then over the fresh query, gives

\[
\mathbb ED^2
\le\frac1n\sum_i\mathbb E[P(f_S-f_{S^{-i}})]^2
\le\frac1n\sum_i\mathbb E P(f_S-f_{S^{-i}})^2
=\beta_n^2.
\tag{8}
\]

This is an \(L^2(S,W)\) estimate, not a pointwise bound on the random full-fit discrepancy \(D\).

For each \(i\), honesty of the held-out observation gives

\[
\mathbb E[h_i\mid W,S^{-i}]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb Eh_i^2
=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}_Z(f_{S^{-i}}(Z)\mid W,S^{-i})
\le B_{n-1}^2.
\tag{9}
\]

The \(h_i\) are not mutually independent. To bound their covariances, fix \(i\ne j\), put \(T=S^{-\{i,j\}}\), and define

\[
h_i^{(j)}=Pf_T-f_T(Z_i),
\quad
\Delta_{ij}=f_{S^{-i}}-f_T,
\quad
d_{ij}=P\Delta_{ij}-\Delta_{ij}(Z_i).
\tag{10}
\]

Then \(h_i=h_i^{(j)}+d_{ij}\), and analogously \(h_j=h_j^{(i)}+d_{ji}\). There are three exact cancellations:

\[
\mathbb E[h_i^{(j)}h_j^{(i)}]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[h_i^{(j)}d_{ji}]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[d_{ij}h_j^{(i)}]=0.
\tag{11}
\]

For the first, condition on \((W,T)\); the centered evaluations at \(Z_i,Z_j\) are independent. For the second, condition on \((W,S^{-j})\): \(h_i^{(j)}\) is measurable there, while \(d_{ji}\) is centered over the independent evaluation point \(Z_j\). The third is symmetric. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E h_i h_j=\mathbb E d_{ij}d_{ji}.
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(e_{ij}=\mathbb E P\Delta_{ij}^2\). Since \(Z_i\) is absent from both fits in \(\Delta_{ij}\),

\[
\mathbb E d_{ij}^2
=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}_Z(\Delta_{ij}(Z)\mid W,S^{-i})
\le e_{ij}.
\]

Consequently, Cauchy–Schwarz and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give

\[
\mathbb E h_i h_j
\le\sqrt{e_{ij}e_{ji}}
\le\tfrac12(e_{ij}+e_{ji}).
\tag{13}
\]

No permutation invariance is needed in summing this bound. For any fixed \(i\), the ordered sequence \(S^{-i}\) has the iid size-\((n-1)\) law, and as \(j\ne i\) varies, deleting its corresponding position runs once through all \(n-1\) deletion positions. Hence

\[
\sum_{i\ne j}e_{ij}=n(n-1)\beta_{n-1}^2.
\tag{14}
\]

Combining the diagonal terms (9) with (13)–(14),

\[
\mathbb EH^2
=\frac1{n^2}\left(\sum_i\mathbb Eh_i^2+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb Eh_i h_j\right)
\le\frac{B_{n-1}^2}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\beta_{n-1}^2
=Q_f.
\tag{15}
\]

Finally, \(D\) and \(H\) need not be independent. Minkowski in joint \(L^2(S,W)\), together with (8) and (15), yields

\[
\|F_f\|_2\le\|D\|_2+\|H\|_2\le\beta_n+\sqrt{Q_f}.
\]

Markov's inequality applied to \(F_f^2\) proves (6). If \(K_f=0\), then \(F_f=0\) almost surely and the assertion is immediate. This proves (1) and (6). \(\square\)

### 2.2 Application to the actual P15 gate

For the complete fitted mean and correction, let

\[
\ell_T(z)=\langle G(u)-m_T(u),c_T(u)\rangle,
\qquad v_T(z)=\|c_T(u)\|^2.
\tag{16}
\]

Apply (2)–(3) separately to these functionals, obtaining \(\beta_{A,n},\beta_{A,n-1},B_{A,n-1}\) and \(\beta_{V,n},\beta_{V,n-1},B_{V,n-1}\). Assume also that the full-fit baseline risk \(P\|G-m_S\|^2\) is finite almost surely, so its comparison with corrected risk is meaningful. This additional baseline assumption is not implied merely by finite alignment and correction moments.

Define the actual full-fit quantities and honest estimates

\[
A_S=P\ell_S,\quad V_S=Pv_S,
\qquad
\widehat A=\frac1n\sum_i\ell_{S^{-i}}(Z_i),
\quad
\widehat V=\frac1n\sum_i v_{S^{-i}}(Z_i).
\]

For predetermined \(\alpha_A,\alpha_V>0\) with \(\alpha_A+\alpha_V\le\delta\), set

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_j=
\frac1{\sqrt{\alpha_j}}
\left[
\beta_{j,n}+
\sqrt{\frac{B_{j,n-1}^2+(n-1)\beta_{j,n-1}^2}{n}}
\right],
\qquad j\in\{A,V\}.
}
\tag{17}
\]

In particular, the balanced choice \(\alpha_A=\alpha_V=\delta/2\) multiplies the bracket by \(\sqrt{2/\delta}\). Put \(L=\widehat A-\varepsilon_A\), \(U=\widehat V+\varepsilon_V\), and

\[
\widehat\theta=
\begin{cases}
0,&L\le0\text{ or }U=0,\\
\min\{1,L/U\},&L>0,\ U>0.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
\]

Since \(v_T\ge0\), \(U\ge0\). By (6) and a union bound, with probability at least \(1-\delta\),

\[
A_S\ge L,\qquad V_S\le U.
\tag{19}
\]

For the risk of the actual full-data fit, conditional on \((S,W)\),

\[
\mathcal R_S(\theta)=P\|G-m_S-\theta c_S\|^2
=\mathcal R_S(0)-2\theta A_S+\theta^2V_S.
\tag{20}
\]

On (19), simultaneously for all \(\theta\in[0,1]\),

\[
\mathcal R_S(0)-\mathcal R_S(\theta)
\ge2\theta L-\theta^2U.
\tag{21}
\]

The gate (18) maximizes this lower quadratic whenever \(U>0\). If \(U=0\), the good event forces \(V_S=0\); finite baseline risk then forces \(A_S=0\), and taking the zero gate is safe. Thus, with probability at least \(1-\delta\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_S(\widehat\theta)
\le\mathcal R_S(0)-\underline g
\le\mathcal R_S(0),
\qquad
\underline g=2\widehat\theta L-\widehat\theta^2U\ge0.
}
\tag{22}
\]

For \(L>0,U>0\), the certified gain is \(L^2/U\) when \(L\le U\), and \(2L-U\) when \(L\ge U\); it is strictly positive. Choosing the scalar gate from these intervals is legitimate because (21) holds for every scalar simultaneously. No independence between \(\widehat\theta\), \(A_S\), and \(V_S\) is claimed or needed. This completes Claim 1.

### 2.3 Comparison with the preceding P15 theorem

The old all-training-set assumptions imply \(\beta_{j,n},\beta_{j,n-1}\le\gamma_j\) and \(B_{j,n-1}\le B_j\), but the converse fails, as Section 3 proves. At fixed confidence, if the averaged moments remain bounded, (17) tends to zero whenever

\[
\beta_{j,n}\to0,\qquad\beta_{j,n-1}\to0.
\]

The preceding Efron–Stein widths required the stronger sufficient rate \(\sqrt n\,\gamma_j\to0\). Under \(\beta_{j,m}=O(m^{-1})\), (17) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\). These are sufficient conditions, not asserted necessary conditions or universally optimal constants. Only polynomial \(\delta^{-1/2}\) confidence is claimed. A naive simultaneous bound for \(H\) unrelated candidate pipelines still incurs \(\sqrt{H/\delta}\); a logarithmic selection cost is not proved here.

## 3. Claim 2: strict separation with a nontrivial gate

Let \(U_i=X_i\sim N(0,1)\) independently, \(G(u)=1\), and for every nonempty training sequence \(T_m\), define

\[
m_{T_m}(u)=0,
\qquad c_{T_m}(u)=1+\overline X_m.
\tag{23}
\]

There is no auxiliary randomness. These are constant functions of the deployment query; the fitting sample still makes the deployed correction, alignment, and energy random. Here

\[
\ell_{T_m}=1+\overline X_m,
\qquad v_{T_m}=(1+\overline X_m)^2,
\]

and the exact averaged quantities are, for \(m\ge2\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{A,m}^2&=1+1/m,\\
B_{V,m}^2&=1+6/m+3/m^2,\\
\beta_{A,m}^2&=\frac1{m(m-1)},\\
\beta_{V,m}^2&=\frac4{m(m-1)}
+\frac{4m-1}{m^2(m-1)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

To check the deletion formula for \(v\), write \(H=\overline X_{m-1}\) for the mean after deleting the chosen observation and \(D=\overline X_m-H\). These are centered jointly Gaussian with

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H)=s=\frac1{m-1},
\quad\operatorname{Var}(D)=d=\frac1{m(m-1)},
\quad\operatorname{Cov}(H,D)=-d.
\]

The energy difference is \(2D+2HD+D^2\). Gaussian moments give
\(\mathbb EH^2D^2=sd+2d^2\), \(\mathbb EHD^3=-3d^2\), and \(\mathbb ED^4=3d^2\); odd terms vanish. Therefore its squared expectation is \(4d+4sd-d^2\), exactly the last line of (24). The other three identities follow immediately from Gaussian second and fourth moments.

For any fixed \(m\ge2\), however,

\[
\sup_{T_m,i}\|\ell_{T_m}-\ell_{T_m^{-i}}\|_{L^2(P)}=\infty,
\qquad
\sup_{T_m,i}\|v_{T_m}-v_{T_m^{-i}}\|_{L^2(P)}=\infty,
\]

and \(\sup_{T_m}\|\ell_{T_m}\|_2=\sup_{T_m}\|v_{T_m}\|_2=\infty\). Let the deleted observation tend to infinity while the remaining observations stay bounded to obtain the deletion divergences. Gaussian training laws assign positive probability to arbitrarily large such configurations, so even essential-supremum versions fail. Thus no finite constants in the old uniform theorem cover this example, while (24) verifies every averaged hypothesis.

The new gate is not merely identically zero. Honest LOO averaging satisfies

\[
\widehat A=1+\overline X_n=A_S.
\]

At \(n=1000\), \(\delta=0.05\), the balanced widths from (17) are

\[
\varepsilon_A=0.2065279202\ldots,
\qquad\varepsilon_V=0.2136613771\ldots.
\]

Chebyshev gives

\[
\mathbb P\{L\le0\}
\le\frac1{n(1-\varepsilon_A)^2}.
\]

Combining this with Claim 1, the probability of strictly positive certified gain is at least

\[
1-\delta-\frac1{n(1-\varepsilon_A)^2}
=0.9484116847\ldots.
\tag{25}
\]

The baseline risk equals one. More generally, \(\beta_{A,m},\beta_{V,m}=O(m^{-1})\), the averaged moments are bounded, the widths vanish, and the probability of a positive gate tends to one. This is an existence example separating hypotheses; it is not a model of the historical neural implementation. Claim 2 is proved.

## 4. Claim 3: observed deletion energies do not automatically certify the gate

For a single fitted sample, consider even the idealized statistics using exact deployment integrals,

\[
\widehat\beta_{f,n}^2
=\frac1n\sum_i P(f_S-f_{S^{-i}})^2,
\]

\[
\widehat\beta_{f,n-1}^2
=\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}
P(f_{S^{-i}}-f_{S^{-\{i,j\}}})^2.
\tag{26}
\]

Their expectations are the corresponding averaged energies (2), including for ordered learners. Being unbiased does not make them upper bounds. The following counterexample shows actual failure of the risk gate when these observed values replace the theoretical energies in (17).

Take \(n=100\), \(\delta=1/25=0.04\). Let \(C\) be a measurable input set with \(P(C)=p=97/100\), and put \(q=3/100\). Take \(G\equiv1\), \(m_T\equiv0\), and let the whole correction algorithm be

\[
I_T=\mathbf1\{\text{every training input in }T\text{ lies in }C\},
\qquad
c_T(u)=I_T r(u),
\]

\[
r(u)=
\begin{cases}1,&u\in C,\\-34,&u\notin C.\end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]

This bounded learner is symmetric, deterministic, and fully honest under every deletion. All moments and energies are finite. Define

\[
M_2=Pr^2=p+34^2q=713/20,
\qquad
M_4=Pr^4=p+34^4q=801821/20.
\]

Since \(\ell_T=I_T r\) and \(v_T=I_T r^2\), the exact population quantities for every training size \(m\) are

\[
B_{A,m}^2=p^mM_2,\qquad B_{V,m}^2=p^mM_4,
\]

\[
\beta_{A,m}^2=qp^{m-1}M_2,
\qquad\beta_{V,m}^2=qp^{m-1}M_4.
\tag{28}
\]

For the deletion identity, a change occurs precisely when the deleted observation lies outside \(C\) and every remaining observation lies in \(C\), an event of probability \(qp^{m-1}\).

Now consider \(E=\{U_1,\ldots,U_n\in C\}\). On \(E\), all full, LOO, and double-deleted fits have \(I_T=1\). Hence all four observed energies in (26), for both \(A\) and \(V\), are exactly zero. Nevertheless,

\[
\widehat A=\widehat V=1,
\qquad A_S=Pr=p-34q=-1/20,
\qquad V_S=M_2=713/20.
\tag{29}
\]

Supply the true averaged moment constants from (28), so that the only invalid substitution is the observed deletion energy. The balanced plug-in widths satisfy

\[
(\varepsilon_A^{\rm plug})^2
=\frac{2B_{A,99}^2}{n\delta}
=\frac{713}{40}p^{99}<1,
\]

\[
(\varepsilon_V^{\rm plug})^2
=\frac{801821}{40}p^{99}.
\tag{30}
\]

The strict inequality follows, for example, from the exact rational inequality \(p^{99}<1/20\), which implies \((713/40)p^{99}<713/800<1\). Thus \(L^{\rm plug}=1-\varepsilon_A^{\rm plug}>0\) and \(U^{\rm plug}=1+\varepsilon_V^{\rm plug}>0\). The plug-in gate selects a strictly positive value, and by (29),

\[
\mathcal R_S(\widehat\theta^{\rm plug})-\mathcal R_S(0)
=\frac{\widehat\theta^{\rm plug}}{10}
+\frac{713}{20}(\widehat\theta^{\rm plug})^2>0
\quad\text{on }E.
\tag{31}
\]

But

\[
\mathbb P(E)=\left(\frac{97}{100}\right)^{100}
=0.047552507925\ldots>0.04=\delta.
\tag{32}
\]

The inequality in (32), and the rational comparison used in (30), were verified by exact integer/fraction arithmetic. Numerically, \(\varepsilon_A^{\rm plug}=0.93479335\ldots\), \(\varepsilon_V^{\rm plug}=31.34798019\ldots\), and the selected gate is \(0.002015787\ldots\). Its positive risk increase is approximately \(0.000346439\). Magnitude is immaterial to the failure of the claimed nonworsening probability.

Therefore even exact, unbiased, fully integrated observed deletion energies, including all double deletions, cannot automatically replace the distribution-averaged energies in the confidence theorem. Additional query-sampling noise is not involved in this counterexample. The true nonzero values in (28) remain valid inputs to Claim 1; the theorem itself is not contradicted. Claim 3 is proved.

**A precise sufficient repair.** If a separate argument supplies random upper bounds on all required theoretical \(\beta_{j,m}\) and \(B_{j,n-1}\) simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\eta\), use those upper bounds in (17). Widths are monotone in these nonnegative parameters. On that upper-bound event and the oracle confidence event (19), the computed intervals still cover \(A_S,V_S\). A union bound gives nonworsening probability at least \(1-\delta-\eta\). Independence between the two events is unnecessary. Establishing such an upper-confidence event is a substantive extra condition; unbiasedness of (26) does not establish it.

## 5. Verification, rejected shortcuts, and remaining obligations

Reproducible code: `computations/047_verify.py`; actual output: `computations/047_verification.json`. Executed successfully with

```bash
python computations/047_verify.py > computations/047_verification.json
```

The script uses only the Python standard library. Exact fraction arithmetic checks the pair-covariance identity and the stated second-moment inequalities by full enumeration for two finite examples of order-sensitive learners with an independent two-valued shared seed, at \(n=3,4\). It checks all 18 ordered pair-covariance cancellations. It independently expands the Gaussian deletion fourth moment for \(m=2,\ldots,20\). It checks the counterexample's strict rational probability and gate-positivity inequalities. Floating-point fields merely display the numerical sizes. These finite checks supplement the analytic proof; they do not prove the theorem for general learners.

Rejected or deliberately unused shortcuts:

1. Directly averaging the preceding Efron–Stein proof is feasible but retains an avoidable \(\sqrt n\) stability factor. Equations (10)–(15) give a sharper route.
2. The bound \(|\mathbb EF_f|\le\beta_n\) does not imply \(|D|\le\beta_n\) sample by sample. The full-fit deletion discrepancy remains random and is controlled in (8), then included in the confidence width through its \(L^2\) norm.
3. Treating all LOO terms as independent discards the generally nonzero covariance (12). Only specified held-out evaluations are independent conditional on the appropriate fits.
4. Conditioning on an independent random seed does not create uniform conditional stability constants from unconditional average bounds. The proof conditions only to obtain centering identities and then integrates over the seed.
5. Empirical local deletion energies cannot be inserted without an upper-confidence theorem; Section 4 is a quantitative disproof even for exact integrated energies.

Remaining limitations and obligations:

- **Historical neural application remains conditional.** The complete fitted mean, representation, correction, and tuning map must satisfy or be certified to satisfy the averaged hypotheses. No numerical or analytic bound for that historical implementation was obtained here.
- **Moment requirements remain material.** An averaged second moment of \(v_T=\|c_T\|^2\) is an averaged fourth-moment requirement on the correction. Deletion stability alone does not supply all required moments.
- **Honesty is mandatory.** Dropping a row only from the final kernel solve while retaining its influence on fitting or tuning does not meet the setup. An auxiliary validation set may be conditioned on only if independent of the fitting rows used in this LOO argument.
- **Probability is over the fitting experiment.** The target is the actual full fit, but the theorem is not a confidence statement conditional on every realized fitting sample or, absent conditional hypotheses, every auxiliary seed.
- **Risk is squared population risk under the same deployment law.** This does not order the manuscript's unsquared mean-relative error, address distribution shift, or assert benefit from an ungated unit-amplitude correction.
- **Independent audit and novelty review remain outstanding.** The complete proof is provided for audit. No claim of first publication, complete P15 closure, or solution of a universal neural generalization problem is made.
