## 1. Results in brief

For the iteration

\[
x_{t+1}=T_t(x_t),\qquad T_t=\frac35I+\frac25V_{P_t},
\tag{1}
\]

every initial state enters the **common** cap \(K_{2/5}\) after at most three
steps, even though all three operators may differ. Three steps cannot be
replaced by two. No cap smaller than \(2/5\) can absorb all switching sequences
after a coefficient-uniform finite time.

Let \(p_t\) be the unique fixed point of \(V_{P_t}\), and write

\[
e_t=\|x_t-p_t\|_1,\qquad d_t=\|p_{t+1}-p_t\|_1,
\qquad s_t=\|x_{t+1}-x_t\|_1.
\]

The useful mixed-cap constant is

\[
\rho=\frac{34-5\sqrt5}{25}<\frac{23}{25}<1.
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(t\ge3\),

\[
\boxed{|e_{t+1}-d_t|\le\rho e_t,\qquad
 (1-\rho)e_t\le s_t\le(1+\rho)e_t.}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently the following exact distinctions hold:

1. **Tracking:** \(e_t\to0\) if and only if \(d_t\to0\); equivalently,
   \(s_t\to0\).
2. **Convergence:** \(x_t\) converges if and only if \(p_t\) converges, and
   their limits agree.
3. **Finite variation:** \(\sum_t d_t<\infty\),
   \(\sum_t e_t<\infty\), and \(\sum_t s_t<\infty\) are equivalent.

Coefficient increments tending to zero suffice for tracking but need not
produce a limit. Convergent coefficients suffice for convergence without
summable increments. Periodic coefficients produce a unique globally
attracting periodic orbit; it is constant exactly when all phase operators
have the same fixed point. Sections 7–8 give explicit admissible arrays
separating these statements.

The numerical constants in (2), the upstream sensitivity constant, and the
tracking gains are sufficient bounds; their optimality is not asserted.

## 2. Definitions and dependencies

Let

\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\textstyle\sum_i x_i=1\},
\qquad V_{P,k}(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]

where, at every time independently,

\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,
\qquad P_{ij,k}=0\quad(k\in\{i,j\}).
\tag{4}
\]

The class of such arrays is a closed compact convex polytope. All distances
on states below are ordinary full \(\ell^1\) distances, not half-distance
total variation. Put

\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\quad q=\frac25,\quad
K_a=\{x\in\Delta_3:\max_i x_i\le a\},\quad
L=\frac{23}{25},\quad C=2\sqrt5-1.
\]

The coefficient metric is

\[
\eta(P,Q)=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|.
\tag{5}
\]

Upstream inputs read: `SESSION.md`, `repository-policy.md`, `agents/008.md`,
`agents/013.md`, and `FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md`. The needed facts are:

* Every valid \(P\) has exactly one fixed point \(p(P)\), lying in \(K_c\).
* \(T_P=(3/5)I+(2/5)V_P\) contracts on the common invariant cap \(K_q\)
  with factor \(L\).
* On the full closed coefficient polytope,
  \(\|p(P)-p(Q)\|_1\le C\eta(P,Q)\).

The fixed-point cap follows directly from
\(p_k=V_{P,k}(p)\le(1-p_k)^2\). The third fact is the sign-cut estimate of
agent 013; its constant is an upper bound, not a claimed optimum. We will
also use the elementary operator bound

\[
\|V_P(u)-V_Q(u)\|_1
\le\sum_{i,j}u_i u_j\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|
\le\eta(P,Q),\quad u\in\Delta_3.
\tag{6}
\]

### The stronger mixed-cap estimate

The contraction proof in agent 008 actually gives, for any two simplex
points \(x,y\), with \(M=\max_i(x_i+y_i)/2\),

\[
\|T_Px-T_Py\|_1
\le\max\left\{\frac35+\frac45M,\frac45\right\}\|x-y\|_1.
\tag{7}
\]

This estimate does not require that the cap at height \(M\) be invariant.
For completeness, polarization gives
\(V_P(x)-V_P(y)=2A_P(m)(x-y)\), where
\(m=(x+y)/2\), \(A_{kj}=\sum_iP_{ij,k}m_i\), and \(A\) is nonnegative,
column stochastic, with zero diagonal. The induced zero-sum norm of
\(B=(3/5)I+(4/5)A\) is

\[
\frac12\max_{i,j}\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1
=\max_{G,i,j}\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}B(e_i-e_j).
\]

For a two-pair cut \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\), let
\(a_{uv}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{uv,r}\). Parent exclusion forces
\(a_{ki}=0\), \(a_{\ell j}=1\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(e_i-e_j)
&=m_i(a_{ii}-a_{ij})+m_j(a_{ij}-a_{jj})
  -m_ka_{kj}+m_\ell(a_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-a_{ij})+m_j a_{ij}\le M.
\end{aligned}
\]

This gives \(3/5+(4/5)M\). Singleton and three-element separating cuts
give at most \(3/5\); the remaining subset cases give at most \(4/5\).
This proves (7), including boundaries and zero coordinates.

If \(x\in K_q\) and \(y\in K_c\), (7) gives

\[
\|T_Px-T_Py\|_1\le\rho\|x-y\|_1,
\quad \rho=\frac35+\frac25(q+c)=\frac{34-5\sqrt5}{25}.
\tag{8}
\]

If both points are in \(K_c\), the bound is

\[
\rho_c=\frac35+\frac45c=\frac{9-2\sqrt5}{5}<1,
\qquad 1-\rho_c=\frac25(1-2c).
\tag{9}
\]

The fact that \(2/5>c\) does not obstruct (9); no invariance of \(K_c\)
under this particular averaging parameter is being asserted.

For later finite bounds, elementary simplex geometry gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{diam}_1(K_q)&=\frac65,\\
D_p:=\operatorname{diam}_1(K_c)&=8c-2=10-4\sqrt5,\\
D_{xp}:=\sup_{x\in K_q,y\in K_c}\|x-y\|_1
&=4(q+c)-2=\frac{28}{5}-2\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

For the mixed diameter, use the zero-sum subset formula. A two-coordinate
subset has maximal mass difference \(2q-(1-2c)\); singleton or
three-coordinate subsets give no more than \(q\), which is smaller here.
Equality is realized by
\(x=(q,q,1-2q,0)\), \(y=(0,1-2c,c,c)\).
The same calculation with equal caps gives the other two formulas. These
are diameters of geometric caps, not claims that every extremizer can occur
as an actual fixed point.

## 3. Common global entry, with sharp uniform time and cap

For every valid array and every coordinate, parent exclusion gives

\[
(T_Px)_k\le f(x_k),\qquad
f(s)=\frac25-\frac15s+\frac25s^2.
\tag{11}
\]

The convex function \(f\) satisfies

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{current coordinate bound }a&f(0)&f(a)\\ \hline
1&2/5&3/5\\
3/5&2/5&53/125\\
53/125&2/5&30243/78125<2/5\\
2/5&2/5&48/125<2/5.
\end{array}
\]

Thus successive uniform bounds are

\[
1\longmapsto\frac35\longmapsto\frac{53}{125}\longmapsto\frac25,
\]

and \(K_q\) is invariant for **every** map in the family. Each use of
(11) concerns only the current state and current valid array, so the proof
does not reuse a coefficient across times. Hence

\[
T_{P_{t+2}}T_{P_{t+1}}T_{P_t}(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_q
\tag{12}
\]

for every three valid arrays.

**Two steps are insufficient.** Complete a deterministic choice with
\(11\mapsto2\), \(22\mapsto1\), \(12\mapsto3\) arbitrarily on other
parent pairs while preserving (4). From \(x_0=e_1\), two applications of
this single valid map give

\[
x_1=(3/5,2/5,0,0),\qquad
x_2=(53/125,48/125,24/125,0),
\]

whose first coordinate exceeds \(2/5\).

**No smaller cap absorbs all switching sequences in uniform finite time.**
For any prescribed integer \(N\ge1\), start with \(x_{0,4}=0\) and use a
valid array with \(V_4\equiv0\) for the first \(N-1\) updates. Such an
array exists: for parents in \(\{1,2,3\}\), choose an allowed offspring
in that triple; rows involving parent 4 already cannot produce 4.
Then \(x_{N-1,4}=0\). At update \(N-1\), choose every parent pair avoiding
4 to produce 4. This is valid and gives \(x_{N,4}=2/5\). Therefore no
\(q'<2/5\) can work uniformly over all switching sequences after any
fixed finite entry time. This is a statement about switching and common
caps; it does not exclude smaller operator-dependent autonomous images.

In particular two trajectories driven by the **same specified sequence**
forget their initial states:

\[
\|x_n-y_n\|_1\le L^{n-m}\|x_m-y_m\|_1
\le\frac65L^{n-3},\qquad n\ge m=3.
\tag{13}
\]

This synchronization assertion compares the same realized forcing. If a
feedback rule chooses different arrays on two different state histories,
the arrays must instead be compared using the perturbation bound in
Section 6.

## 4. Tracking with actual movement, coefficient variation, or operator drift

Let \(p_t=p(P_t)\). For \(t\ge3\), \(x_t\in K_q\),
\(p_t\in K_c\), and \(T_t p_t=p_t\). Consequently (8) gives

\[
\|x_{t+1}-p_t\|_1\le\rho e_t.
\tag{14}
\]

Apply the reverse triangle inequality to the distances from
\(x_{t+1}\) and \(p_t\) to \(p_{t+1}\). It proves the first part of
(3). Applying both triangle inequalities to
\(x_t-p_t=(x_t-x_{t+1})+(x_{t+1}-p_t)\) proves the second part.

For \(n\ge m\ge3\), the standard finite geometric convolution is thus

\[
\boxed{e_n\le \rho^{n-m}e_m+
\sum_{t=m}^{n-1}\rho^{n-1-t}d_t.}
\tag{15}
\]

It can always be intersected with \(e_n\le D_{xp}\), and in particular
\(e_m\le D_{xp}\) provides a fully uniform initial constant.

For a bound in coefficient increments, put
\(\eta_t=\eta(P_{t+1},P_t)\). For a bound in actual operator drift, put

\[
\omega_t=\sup_{u\in K_c}\|V_{P_{t+1}}(u)-V_{P_t}(u)\|_1.
\tag{16}
\]

Then \(\omega_t\le\eta_t\), and

\[
\boxed{d_t\le b_t:=
\min\{D_p,\ C\eta_t,\ (2+\sqrt5)\omega_t\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

The first two terms follow from (10) and agent 013. For the third,

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_t
&=\|T_{t+1}p_{t+1}-T_t p_t\|_1\\
&\le\|T_{t+1}p_{t+1}-T_{t+1}p_t\|_1
   +\|(T_{t+1}-T_t)p_t\|_1\\
&\le\rho_c d_t+\frac25\omega_t.
\end{aligned}
\]

Divide by \(1-\rho_c=(2/5)(1-2c)\) and use
\((1-2c)^{-1}=2+\sqrt5\). The same argument permits replacing
\(\omega_t\) in this term by the smaller of the two drifts evaluated
at \(p_t\) and \(p_{t+1}\). These pointwise quantities may be useful
when the fixed points are available. If operator drift is defined using
\(T\), rather than \(V\), its multiplier is
\(1/(1-\rho_c)=(5/2)(2+\sqrt5)\); the averaging factor must not be lost.

Replacing \(d_t\) by \(b_t\) in (15) yields the requested coefficient or
operator drift tracking estimate. Summing, for every \(N\ge m\ge3\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{t=m}^{N}e_t
\le\frac{e_m+\sum_{t=m}^{N-1}d_t}{1-\rho}
\le\frac{e_m+\sum_{t=m}^{N-1}b_t}{1-\rho}.}
\tag{18}
\]

This follows by summing \(e_{t+1}\le\rho e_t+d_t\); the omitted final
term \(-\rho e_N\) is nonpositive. Thus cumulative tracking error is
controlled by cumulative actual movement, coefficient variation, or
operator drift, rather than just the worst single increment.

There is also a quantitative persistent-input statement. Write
\(D=\limsup d_t\), \(E=\limsup e_t\). Boundedness and (3) imply

\[
\boxed{\frac{D}{1+\rho}\le E\le
\min\left\{D_{xp},\frac{D}{1-\rho}\right\}.}
\tag{19}
\]

For the upper bound, take limsups in
\(e_{t+1}\le\rho e_t+d_t\); for the lower bound use
\(d_t\le e_{t+1}+\rho e_t\). In particular persistent movement of the
fixed point forces a nonzero asymptotic tracking error, regardless of the
initial condition. The upper bound is at most
\(C\limsup\eta_t/(1-\rho)\) or
\((2+\sqrt5)\limsup\omega_t/(1-\rho)\).

## 5. Exact qualitative distinctions and summable variation

### Tracking is equivalent to asymptotically stationary fixed points

If \(d_t\to0\), for any \(\varepsilon>0\) choose \(m\) such that
\(d_t\le(1-\rho)\varepsilon\) for all \(t\ge m\). Formula (15)
then gives
\(e_n\le\rho^{n-m}e_m+\varepsilon\), proving \(e_n\to0\).
Conversely, \(d_t\le e_{t+1}+\rho e_t\) proves \(d_t\to0\) whenever
\(e_t\to0\). The second inequality in (3) shows that both are
equivalent to \(s_t\to0\).

Thus it is movement of the fixed points, rather than coefficient movement
itself, that is necessary and sufficient. In particular \(\eta_t\to0\)
or \(\omega_t\to0\) is sufficient; neither is stated as necessary.

### The state has a limit exactly when the fixed points do

If \(p_t\to p\), then \(d_t\to0\), so tracking proves \(x_t\to p\).
Conversely, if \(x_t\to x\), then \(s_t\to0\). From (3),
\(e_t\le s_t/(1-\rho)\to0\), whence \(p_t\to x\). This proves the
second exact equivalence in Section 1 without assuming that \(P_t\) has
a limit.

### Finite total variation is equivalent in the state and fixed-point paths

If \(\sum d_t<\infty\), (18) implies \(\sum e_t<\infty\).
Conversely \(d_t\le e_{t+1}+\rho e_t\) shows that summable tracking error
implies \(\sum d_t<\infty\). The inequalities
\((1-\rho)e_t\le s_t\le(1+\rho)e_t\) prove equivalence with
\(\sum s_t<\infty\). Finite prefixes have no effect on these assertions.
In particular,

\[
\sum_{t=m}^{\infty}s_t
\le\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
\left(e_m+\sum_{t=m}^{\infty}d_t\right).
\tag{20}
\]

If \(\sum_t\eta_t<\infty\), then the coefficient arrays are Cauchy in
the finite-dimensional metric (5), so they converge to a valid boundary
or interior array \(P_\infty\). Equations (17)–(20) give finite total
variation of both state and fixed-point paths, and
\(x_t\to p(P_\infty)\).

### Convergent coefficients need no summability hypothesis

If merely \(P_t\to P_\infty\), the global sensitivity estimate implies
\(p_t\to p(P_\infty)\), hence \(x_t\to p(P_\infty)\). An explicit
bound follows by comparing directly with this limiting fixed point:

\[
\|x_n-p(P_\infty)\|_1\le
\rho^{n-m}\|x_m-p(P_\infty)\|_1
 +\frac25\sum_{t=m}^{n-1}\rho^{n-1-t}
       \eta(P_t,P_\infty),\quad n\ge m\ge3.
\tag{21}
\]

Indeed subtract \(T_{P_\infty}p(P_\infty)\) from
\(T_{P_t}x_t\), use (8) on the state difference and (6) on the
coefficient difference. The forcing in (21) tends to zero, and the
same geometric-tail argument as above proves convergence.

## 6. A comparison bound for different forcing sequences

Let \(x_{t+1}=T_{P_t}x_t\) and \(y_{t+1}=T_{Q_t}y_t\), from any two
simplex states. Both enter \(K_q\) by time 3. Put
\(a_t=\eta(P_t,Q_t)\). For \(t\ge3\),

\[
\|x_{t+1}-y_{t+1}\|_1
\le L\|x_t-y_t\|_1+\frac25 a_t.
\]

Hence, for \(n\ge m\ge3\),

\[
\|x_n-y_n\|_1\le L^{n-m}\|x_m-y_m\|_1
 +\frac25\sum_{t=m}^{n-1}L^{n-1-t}a_t,
\tag{22}
\]

and

\[
\limsup_n\|x_n-y_n\|_1
\le\min\left\{\frac65,5\limsup_t a_t\right\}.
\tag{23}
\]

The factor five is \((2/5)/(1-L)\). This is a pathwise disturbance bound
for even history-dependent coefficient choices; it does not require
independence, probability distributions on arrays, or slowly changing
operators. It reduces to (13) for identical coefficient sequences.

## 7. Periodic forcing

Suppose \(P_{t+r}=P_t\) for a positive integer \(r\). The phase-zero
composition

\[
F=T_{r-1}\circ\cdots\circ T_0
\]

preserves \(K_q\) and contracts there with factor \(L^r\). Banach gives
a unique \(z_0\in K_q\) with \(Fz_0=z_0\). Define
\(z_{j+1}=T_jz_j\) for \(0\le j<r\), and extend periodically. This
gives an \(r\)-periodic trajectory, whose minimal period may divide
\(r\). Every periodic trajectory on the full simplex must already lie
in \(K_q\), because advancing sufficiently many periods leaves its state
unchanged while the common entry theorem applies. Thus this periodic
trajectory is globally unique for the chosen phase convention.

By same-forcing synchronization,

\[
\|x_n-z_{n\bmod r}\|_1\le\frac65L^{n-3},\qquad n\ge3.
\tag{24}
\]

The attracting periodic orbit is constant if and only if all phase maps
share a fixed point, equivalently \(p_0=p_1=\cdots=p_{r-1}\). One
direction is immediate from a constant trajectory. In the other direction,
the common fixed point itself is a periodic trajectory and uniqueness
applies. In particular, two alternating maps with distinct fixed points
produce a genuinely nonconstant attracting two-cycle, and the state
does not converge.

Writing \(d_j=\|p_{j+1}-p_j\|_1\) cyclically, its phase errors satisfy

\[
\|z_j-p_j\|_1\le
\frac{\sum_{h=1}^{r}\rho^{h-1}d_{j-h}}{1-\rho^r}.
\tag{25}
\]

This follows from (15) over \(kr\) preceding periodic steps, followed by
\(k\to\infty\), and summation of each of the \(r\) geometric series.
Also
\(\max_j\|z_j-p_j\|_1\ge\max_j d_j/(1+\rho)\), by (3).

## 8. Explicit examples separating the hypotheses

### A family with explicitly different fixed points

Define \(A\) by distributing offspring uniformly over the types not
among the parents: each diagonal row has three probabilities \(1/3\),
and each distinct-parent row has two probabilities \(1/2\). Define
\(B\) to agree with \(A\) on every row involving parent 1, and to produce
offspring 1 with probability one for every parent pair avoiding 1. Both
arrays satisfy (4), and

\[
\eta(A,B)=\frac43.
\]

For \(0\le\theta\le1\), put
\(P(\theta)=(1-\theta)A+\theta B\). These maps are equivariant under
permutation of types 2,3,4. Their unique fixed points therefore have the
form \((r,a,a,a)\), \(a=(1-r)/3\). Directly,

\[
V_{A,1}(r,a,a,a)=\frac49(1-r)^2,
\qquad V_{B,1}(r,a,a,a)=(1-r)^2.
\]

Consequently, with \(\alpha(\theta)=(4+5\theta)/9\),

\[
p(\theta)=\left(r(\theta),\frac{1-r(\theta)}3,
                 \frac{1-r(\theta)}3,\frac{1-r(\theta)}3\right),
\qquad
r(\theta)=1-\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+4\alpha(\theta)}}.
\tag{26}
\]

This is obtained from \(r=\alpha(1-r)^2\); the other coordinates follow
from symmetry and sum one. In particular

\[
p(0)=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4),\qquad
p(1)=\left(c,\frac{1-c}{3},\frac{1-c}{3},\frac{1-c}{3}\right),
\]

which are distinct. For all \(\theta,\phi\),
\(\eta(P(\theta),P(\phi))=(4/3)|\theta-\phi|\).

**Slow wandering: tracking without convergence.** Choose

\[
\theta_t=\frac{1+\sin\log(t+1)}2.
\tag{27}
\]

By the Lipschitz property of sine,

\[
|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t|
\le\frac12\log\left(1+\frac1{t+1}\right)
\le\frac1{2(t+1)}.
\]

Thus \(\eta_t\to0\), and every trajectory tracks \(p(\theta_t)\).
There are integer subsequences with
\(\log(t+1)\) tending modulo \(2\pi\) to \(\pi/2\) and
\(3\pi/2\): take \(t+1\) to be a nearest integer to the corresponding
exponentials. Their logarithmic rounding errors tend to zero. Hence
\(\theta_t\) has subsequential limits 1 and 0, and the state has the
two distinct subsequential limits \(p(1)\) and \(p(0)\). It does not
converge despite its tracking error tending to zero. Its coefficient
variation is necessarily nonsummable, since finite variation would force
the coefficients to converge.

**Convergence without summable coefficient variation.** Alternatively take

\[
\theta_t=\frac12+\frac{(-1)^t}{4\sqrt{t+1}}.
\tag{28}
\]

These parameters lie in \([0,1]\) and converge to \(1/2\), while

\[
|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t|
=\frac14\left(\frac1{\sqrt{t+2}}+\frac1{\sqrt{t+1}}\right)
\]

has divergent sum. Nevertheless all trajectories converge to
\(p(1/2)\) by (21).

**Persistent alternating forcing: a nonconstant attracting two-cycle.**
Take \(P_{2n}=A\), \(P_{2n+1}=B\). Section 7 applies, and the distinct
fixed points above exclude a constant orbit. On the symmetric line, the
first-coordinate maps are

\[
t_A(s)=\frac35s+\frac8{45}(1-s)^2,\qquad
t_B(s)=\frac35s+\frac25(1-s)^2.
\]

The attracting phase-zero coordinate \(a\) solves
\(t_B(t_A(a))=a\), and the other phase is \(b=t_A(a)\). The exact
rational script isolates them in disjoint intervals. Outward decimal
enclosures, also checked by exact rational comparison, are

\[
0.37580092219<a<0.37580092224,
\]
\[
0.29474712909<b<0.29474712911.
\]

Universal existence, uniqueness, and nonconstancy do not depend on these
particular enclosures.

### Coefficient increments need not vanish even for convergence

Starting from \(A\), define a distinct array \(C_0\) by changing only
the following three diagonal rows, listed as offspring coordinates 1–4:

\[
\begin{aligned}
(C_0)_{11,\cdot}&=(0,0,2/3,1/3),\\
(C_0)_{22,\cdot}&=(2/3,0,0,1/3),\\
(C_0)_{33,\cdot}&=(0,2/3,0,1/3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

The three row perturbations sum to zero, and at the uniform state each
diagonal row has the same weight \(1/16\). Thus the uniform state is a
fixed point of both \(A\) and \(C_0\), hence their common unique fixed
point. Their coefficient distance is \(2/3\). Alternating, or otherwise
arbitrarily switching, between these arrays makes every state converge
to the uniform fixed point even though consecutive coefficient distances
can remain \(2/3\). This demonstrates why coefficient variation is only
a sufficient proxy for the exact fixed-point movement criterion.
