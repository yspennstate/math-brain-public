## 1. Setting and the reconstructed theorem

Write
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in[0,\infty)^4:\ \sum_{i=1}^4x_i=1\},
\qquad
K_q=\{x\in\Delta_3:\ \max_i x_i\le q\}.
\]
All norms on state vectors in this report are the full \(\ell^1\) norm, not half-distance total variation.

At almost every time \(t\), let
\[
P_{ij,k}(t)=P_{ji,k}(t)\ge0,\qquad
\sum_kP_{ij,k}(t)=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}(t)=0\quad\hbox{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every coefficient is measurable. All allowed coefficients may vanish; the diagonal-parent exclusion is included. Put
\[
V_{t,k}(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}(t)x_ix_j,\qquad
F(t,x)=V_t(x)-x.
\]
The same specified coefficient path is used when comparing unforced solutions.

Set
\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\quad
d=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2},\quad
\delta=d-c=\sqrt5,\quad
r=1-2c=\sqrt5-2,
\]
\[
C_1=1+c=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
C_2=(1+c)^2=5c=\frac{15-5\sqrt5}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(\tau\ge0\), define
\[
H_a(\tau)=\frac{1+a}{1+ae^{-\delta\tau}}\quad(0\le a\le c),
\qquad
\kappa_1(\tau)=e^{-r\tau}H_c(\tau),\qquad
\kappa_2(\tau)=e^{-r\tau}H_c(\tau)^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(p:[0,\infty)\to\Delta_3\) be locally absolutely continuous and satisfy
\[
F(t,p(t))=0\quad\hbox{for almost every }t.
\tag{4}
\]
Such a path is an absolutely continuous instantaneous fixed-point path. Its existence as an absolutely continuous path is a separate hypothesis; mere measurability of the coefficients does not imply it.

Let \(R(t,x)\) be a Caratheodory forcing on \(\Delta_3\), and let \(b\ge0\) belong to \(L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\), with
\[
\|R(t,x)\|_1\le b(t).
\tag{5}
\]
For almost every \(t\), these bounds are uniform over \(x\in\Delta_3\). Let \(x\) be any locally absolutely continuous viable solution:
\[
x'(t)=F(t,x(t))+R(t,x(t)),\qquad x(t)\in\Delta_3.
\tag{6}
\]
Section 6 gives explicit conditions ensuring that viable solutions exist. Uniqueness of the forced equation is not needed for the estimate.

**Reconstructed theorem.** Under (1), (4), (5), and (6), for every \(0\le s\le t\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|x(t)-p(t)\|_1
\le{}& \kappa_1(t-s)\|x(s)-p(s)\|_1\\
&+\int_s^t\kappa_2(t-v)b(v)\,dv
+\int_s^t e^{-r(t-v)}\|p'(v)\|_1\,dv.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently the retained 069 formula is valid:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|x(t)-p(t)\|_1
\le{}&C_1e^{-r(t-s)}\|x(s)-p(s)\|_1\\
&+\int_s^t e^{-r(t-v)}
       \bigl(C_2b(v)+\|p'(v)\|_1\bigr)\,dv.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8}
\]
There is no smallness hypothesis on \(b\) for either estimate. Each bound holds on every finite interval on which the stated viable solution and target exist. The hypotheses above give global forward viable solutions as detailed below.

The initial kernel can be improved further: replace \(H_c(t-s)\) in the first term of (7) by \(H_{a_s}(t-s)\), where
\[
M_s=\max\{c,\|x(s)\|_\infty\},\qquad
a_s=\frac{M_s-c}{d-M_s}.
\tag{9}
\]

## 2. Complete cut calculation and the nondifferentiable norm

For a fixed valid coefficient array and \(m\in\Delta_3\), define
\[
A(m)_{aj}=\sum_bP_{bj,a}m_b.
\]
The matrix is nonnegative, column stochastic, and has zero diagonal. For a nonempty proper set \(G\), \(i\in G\), and \(j\notin G\), put
\[
B_{Gij}(m)=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j).
\]
Then
\[
B_{Gij}(m)\le
\begin{cases}
0,&|G|=1\text{ or }3,\\
\max(m_i,m_j),&|G|=2.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]

For a singleton \(G=\{i\}\), the expression is \(-A_{ij}\). For a three-element set, \(G^c=\{j\}\), it is \(-A_{ji}\). Both are nonpositive.

For a two-element set, write \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\), and
\[
q_{ab}=\sum_{\alpha\in G}P_{ab,\alpha}.
\]
Parent exclusion gives \(q_{ki}=0\) and \(q_{\ell j}=1\), while symmetry gives \(q_{ij}=q_{ji}\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{Gij}(m)
&=m_i(q_{ii}-q_{ij})+m_j(q_{ij}-q_{jj})
   -m_kq_{kj}+m_\ell(q_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-q_{ij})+m_jq_{ij}\\
&\le\max(m_i,m_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Every dropped term is nonpositive. Equivalently, the difference between the first upper bound and the exact first line is
\[
m_i(1-q_{ii})+m_jq_{jj}+m_kq_{kj}
 +m_\ell(1-q_{\ell i})\ge0.
\]
This is the only step that uses the special four-type cut structure.

Take two unforced solutions for the same time-dependent array. Let
\[
z=x-y,\qquad m=(x+y)/2.
\]
Parent symmetry gives, at almost every time,
\[
V_t(x)-V_t(y)=2A_t(m)z,\qquad
z'=(2A_t(m)-I)z,\qquad \sum_i z_i=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Put
\[
h=\tfrac12\|z\|_1
  =\max_{G\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}}\sum_{i\in G}z_i.
\tag{13}
\]
An active set \(G\) contains every positive coordinate of \(z\), contains no negative coordinate, and may contain any selection of zero coordinates.

In the autonomous case, the paths are differentiable at every time. The upper right Dini derivative of the finite maximum in (13) is the maximum of the derivatives over all active sets. This follows from first-order expansions; inactive sets have a positive gap. In the measurable nonautonomous case, the same calculation is used only at the almost-everywhere times where the absolutely continuous paths are differentiable. The norm is also absolutely continuous, so this suffices for integration. No everywhere Dini statement is being imposed on a merely measurable field.

If \(z=0\), (12) gives \(z'=0\) at every applicable time. Otherwise let \(P_+\) and \(P_-\) denote the positive and negative coordinate sets. Then \(h>0\) and
\[
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}\quad(i\in P_+,j\in P_-)
\]
satisfies
\[
\theta_{ij}\ge0,\qquad \sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}=1,\qquad
z=h\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\tag{14}
\]
Every pair in (14) is separated by every active set \(G\). If \(\mu=\max_km_k\), equations (10)--(14) give, for every active set,
\[
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}z'
=2h\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}B_{Gij}(m)-h
\le(2\mu-1)h.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\|x(t)-y(t)\|_1
\le\bigl(\max_k(x_k(t)+y_k(t))-1\bigr)
       \|x(t)-y(t)\|_1
\quad\hbox{a.e.}}
\tag{15}
\]
For the autonomous flow, the same inequality holds with the upper right Dini derivative at every time.

This checks every zero-coordinate assignment, rather than differentiating only a fixed strictly positive set. The norm need not be divided by its value; an integrating factor applies directly to its absolutely continuous differential inequality.

## 3. Nonautonomous unforced process and exact scalar envelope

For \(x,y\in\Delta_3\), polarization and column stochasticity give
\[
\|V_t(x)-V_t(y)\|_1\le2\|x-y\|_1,\qquad
\|F(t,x)-F(t,y)\|_1\le3\|x-y\|_1.
\tag{16}
\]
The time dependence is measurable and the spatial Lipschitz constant is uniform. Hence the unforced Caratheodory equation has a unique process, denoted \(U(t,s,\xi)\).

For completeness, it preserves the simplex including its boundary. On a sufficiently short interval its integral equation is
\[
x(t)=e^{-(t-s)}\xi+\int_s^t e^{-(t-v)}V_v(x(v))\,dv.
\tag{17}
\]
Picard iteration starting with a nonnegative constant path remains nonnegative and converges. Moreover, for the sum \(S=\sum_i x_i\),
\[
S'=S^2-S,\qquad S(s)=1,
\]
so scalar uniqueness gives \(S\equiv1\). Compactness then gives global forward existence. Uniqueness gives the process identity
\[
U(t,v,U(v,s,\xi))=U(t,s,\xi).
\tag{18}
\]

Parent exclusion gives, at almost every time,
\[
V_{t,k}(x)\le(1-x_k)^2,\qquad
x_k'\le g(x_k),\qquad
g(u)=(1-u)^2-u=(u-c)(u-d).
\tag{19}
\]
The estimate is uniform in the coefficient path. Scalar comparison for absolutely continuous differential inequalities follows from a positive-part Gronwall argument, using the Lipschitz property of \(g\) on bounded intervals.

Since \(g(q)\le0\) for \(c\le q\le1\), every \(K_q\), \(q\ge c\), is invariant for every unforced coefficient path. Let
\[
M_\xi=\max\{c,\|\xi\|_\infty\},\qquad
a_\xi=\frac{M_\xi-c}{d-M_\xi}\in[0,c].
\]
The scalar solution starting at \(M_\xi\) is exactly
\[
u_\xi(\tau)
=c+\frac{\delta a_\xi e^{-\delta\tau}}
              {1+a_\xi e^{-\delta\tau}},
\qquad
U_k(s+\tau,s,\xi)\le u_\xi(\tau).
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed
\[
\frac{u_\xi(\tau)-c}{d-u_\xi(\tau)}
=a_\xi e^{-\delta\tau},
\]
and differentiation gives \(u_\xi'=g(u_\xi)\). The upper endpoint \(a_\xi\le c\) follows from
\[
\frac{1-c}{d-1}=c.
\]

The scalar excess integrates without loss:
\[
\int_0^\tau (u_\xi(v)-c)\,dv
=\log\frac{1+a_\xi}{1+a_\xi e^{-\delta\tau}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Apply (15) and (20) to two solutions, and integrate (21). For all \(\xi,\eta\in\Delta_3\),
\[
\boxed{
\|U(t,s,\xi)-U(t,s,\eta)\|_1
\le e^{-r(t-s)}
 H_{a_\xi}(t-s)H_{a_\eta}(t-s)\|\xi-\eta\|_1.}
\tag{22}
\]
This holds under arbitrary measurable switching, because every ingredient was pointwise in the current coefficient array.

Since \(a\mapsto H_a(\tau)\) is increasing for \(\tau\ge0\), equation (22) implies the three estimates used later:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\hbox{initial states} & \hbox{Lipschitz bound at duration }\tau\\ \hline
\xi,\eta\in\Delta_3 & \kappa_2(\tau)\\
\xi\in\Delta_3,\ \eta\in K_c & \kappa_1(\tau)\\
\xi,\eta\in K_c & e^{-r\tau}.
\end{array}
\tag{23}
\]
In the mixed row, \(H_{a_\xi}\) can replace \(H_c\). In particular
\[
\kappa_1(\tau)\le C_1e^{-r\tau},\qquad
\kappa_2(\tau)\le C_2e^{-r\tau}.
\tag{24}
\]
The identity \(C_2=5c\) uses \(c^2=3c-1\).

## 4. Autonomous equilibrium, exact entrance, and sharpness audit

When \(P\) is constant, write its flow as \(\Phi_t\). On \(K_q\), (15) and invariance give
\[
\|\Phi_t(x)-\Phi_t(y)\|_1
\le e^{-(1-2q)t}\|x-y\|_1.
\tag{25}
\]
This is a positive contraction rate for \(c\le q<1/2\); at \(q=1/2\) it states nonexpansion. The expression \(1-2q\) is not a positive contraction rate for \(q>1/2\).

Every equilibrium \(p\) satisfies
\[
p_k=V_k(p)\le(1-p_k)^2.
\]
Because \(d>1\), every coordinate is at most \(c\); hence \(p\in K_c\).

Existence and uniqueness require no unproved uniqueness input. The set \(K_c\) is nonempty, closed, and complete. For every fixed \(\tau>0\), the map \(\Phi_\tau:K_c\to K_c\) is a strict contraction by (25). Let \(p\) be its Banach fixed point. For every \(v\ge0\),
\[
\Phi_\tau(\Phi_v(p))=\Phi_v(\Phi_\tau(p))=\Phi_v(p).
\]
The point \(\Phi_v(p)\) belongs to \(K_c\), so uniqueness of the fixed point of \(\Phi_\tau\) implies \(\Phi_v(p)=p\). Thus \(p\) is an equilibrium, not just a periodic point of a time map. Every other equilibrium lies in \(K_c\) and is the same Banach fixed point.

Taking \(\eta=p\) in (22) gives report 012's estimate
\[
\|\Phi_t(\xi)-p\|_1
\le e^{-rt}H_{a_\xi}(t)\|\xi-p\|_1
\le C_1e^{-rt}\|\xi-p\|_1.
\tag{26}
\]
The full two-trajectory estimate is \(\kappa_2(t)\|\xi-\eta\|_1\), at most \(C_2e^{-rt}\|\xi-\eta\|_1\). Both are valid from time zero. No optimality of these exponential constants follows from this proof.

For \(q>c\), every unforced trajectory, including a nonautonomous one, enters \(K_q\) in finite time. If \(M=\|\xi\|_\infty>q\), solving (20) for \(u_\xi(\tau)\le q\) gives
\[
T_q(\xi)=\frac1\delta
 \log\frac{(M-c)(d-q)}{(d-M)(q-c)}.
\tag{27}
\]
If \(M\le q\), the entrance time is zero. For \(c<q<1\), the universal bound is
\[
T_q=\frac1{\sqrt5}
 \log\frac{c(d-q)}{q-c}.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(q\ge1\), it is zero. Direct substitution at \(q=2/5\) gives
\[
T_{2/5}
=\frac1{\sqrt5}\log\frac{47+21\sqrt5}{2},
\]
which verifies the exact constant in report 012.

An explicit admissible autonomous array attains (27)--(28). Let \(J=\{2,3,4\}\). For both parents in \(J\), produce type 1 with probability one. For parents \((1,1)\), produce each type in \(J\) with probability \(1/3\). For parents \((1,j)\) or \((j,1)\), \(j\in J\), produce each of the two types in \(J\setminus\{j\}\) with probability \(1/2\). This specifies all pairs and satisfies (1).

The line
\[
L=\{(u,(1-u)/3,(1-u)/3,(1-u)/3):0\le u\le1\}
\]
is invariant, because on it
\[
V_1=(1-u)^2,\qquad V_j=(2u-u^2)/3\quad(j\in J).
\]
Thus \(u'=g(u)\). Starting from \(e_1\) gives
\[
u(t)=c+\frac{\sqrt5\,c\,e^{-\sqrt5t}}
                     {1+ce^{-\sqrt5t}}.
\tag{29}
\]
This coordinate is strictly greater than \(c\) at every finite time. The other coordinates are less than \(c\). Therefore the trajectory never enters \(K_c\) in finite time, and reaches \(K_q\), \(c<q<1\), precisely at (28). Starting on \(L\) at \(M>q\) attains (27).

For \(1/4\le q<c\), the point on \(L\) with \(u=q\) lies in \(K_q\), but \(u'=g(q)>0\), so it immediately exits. Thus \(K_c\) is the smallest universally invariant nonempty coordinate cap. Caps below \(1/4\) are empty. Finally, the same array's raw discrete map has the two-cycle
\[
e_1\longmapsto(0,1/3,1/3,1/3)\longmapsto e_1.
\]
This verifies all sharpness and endpoint qualifications made in report 012, without promoting continuous convergence to convergence of the raw iteration.

## 5. A perturbation lemma that avoids a boundary Alekseev gap

The following elementary lemma is sufficient for the entire reconstruction.

Let \(K\) be a compact invariant set for a uniquely defined process \(U\). Suppose its field is \(L\)-Lipschitz in the state on \(K\), uniformly in time, and
\[
\|U(t,v,\xi)-U(t,v,\eta)\|_1
\le k(t-v)\|\xi-\eta\|_1,\qquad \xi,\eta\in K,
\tag{30}
\]
where \(k\) is continuous on finite duration intervals. Let \(z\) be an absolutely continuous path in \(K\) with
\[
z'=F(t,z)+u,\qquad u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\|z(t)-U(t,s,z(s))\|_1
\le\int_s^t k(t-v)\|u(v)\|_1\,dv.}
\tag{31}
\]

**Proof.** First, for \(a<b\), comparison with the unforced solution starting at \(z(a)\), followed by the ordinary Lipschitz Gronwall estimate, gives
\[
\|z(b)-U(b,a,z(a))\|_1
\le\int_a^b e^{L(b-v)}\|u(v)\|_1\,dv.
\tag{32}
\]
Both paths stay in \(K\), so only the stated Lipschitz bound on \(K\) is used.

Choose a partition \(s=t_0<\cdots<t_n=t\), and set
\[
w_i=U(t,t_i,z(t_i)).
\]
By the process identity,
\[
w_{i-1}=U(t,t_i,U(t_i,t_{i-1},z(t_{i-1}))).
\]
Both arguments of this last-time process belong to \(K\). Equations (30)--(32) and the triangle inequality therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|z(t)-U(t,s,z(s))\|_1
&\le\sum_{i=1}^n
  k(t-t_i)\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_i}
       e^{L(t_i-v)}\|u(v)\|_1\,dv.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
As the partition mesh tends to zero, continuity of \(k\) is uniform on the compact duration interval, \(e^{L(t_i-v)}\to1\) uniformly, and a bounded multiple of \(\|u(v)\|_1\) dominates the integrands. Dominated convergence gives (31). This proof uses no flow derivative and no differentiability in the initial time. QED.

For this problem \(L=3\) by (16). We can apply the lemma with \(K=\Delta_3\), \(k=\kappa_2\), or with \(K=K_c\), \(k(\tau)=e^{-r\tau}\).

Why this proof is useful here:

* At a boundary state, a forcing vector tangent to the affine hyperplane need not itself be a viable simplex direction.
* A Lipschitz estimate stated only on the simplex cannot simply be quoted as an unrestricted derivative estimate at a boundary point without an additional extension or continuity argument.
* With measurable coefficients, differentiation of the initial-time argument of a nonautonomous flow also requires care.
* In (33), every compared point actually lies in the invariant set, and every unforced segment is restarted at a genuine simplex point. The universal scalar cap belongs to that unforced segment; no cap for the perturbed trajectory is assumed.

A carefully justified Alekseev formula could also be used here, but none is needed.

## 6. Viability hypotheses and two instructive counterexamples

A readily checked sufficient condition for viable forced solutions is the following. For almost every \(t\), for all \(x\in\Delta_3\),
\[
\sum_iR_i(t,x)=0,
\qquad
V_{t,i}(x)+R_i(t,x)\ge0\quad\hbox{whenever }x_i=0.
\tag{34}
\]
Together with the Caratheodory and integrable bound assumptions, these state that the total field \(F+R\) belongs to the tangent cone of the simplex: it has zero total mass and is inward at every vanishing coordinate. At a boundary coordinate the term \(-x_i\) is zero, giving exactly the second condition in (34).

For completeness these assumptions give global viable existence even without a spatial Lipschitz assumption on \(R\). Let \(\pi\) be Euclidean projection onto the closed convex simplex, and extend the total field to
\[
\widetilde F(t,z)=F(t,\pi(z))+R(t,\pi(z)).
\]
This is a globally bounded-on-finite-time-intervals Caratheodory field:
\(\|\widetilde F(t,z)\|_1\le2+b(t)\). The Caratheodory existence theorem gives solutions on every finite interval and continuation globally. For \(q=\pi(z)\), the vector \(z-q\) is normal to the simplex at \(q\), while (34) puts \(\widetilde F(t,z)\) in its tangent cone. Therefore along any such solution
\[
\frac d{dt}\operatorname{dist}(z(t),\Delta_3)^2
=2(z(t)-\pi(z(t)))\cdot\widetilde F(t,z(t))\le0
\quad\hbox{a.e.}
\]
Starting with zero distance forces the solution to remain in \(\Delta_3\), where the extension equals the prescribed total field. The estimate (7) applies to every such viable solution. If \(R\) is locally Lipschitz in \(x\) with an integrable Lipschitz modulus on finite intervals, uniqueness follows as well. Under mere Caratheodory regularity, uniqueness is not asserted.

**Tangency alone does not imply viability.** Let \(E\) be the equiprobable parent-exclusion array: a diagonal-parent row is uniform over its three allowed types, and a distinct-parent row is uniform over its two allowed types. Set the constant forcing
\[
R=(-1,1,0,0).
\]
It is continuous, bounded, and has zero coordinate sum. At \(x=e_2\),
\[
V_E(e_2)=(1/3,0,1/3,1/3),\qquad
(F+R)_1(e_2)=-2/3<0.
\]
The first coordinate of the unique ambient solution becomes negative immediately. Thus the affine tangency condition alone is insufficient.

**A viable forcing does not preserve the unforced cap envelope.** Fix any valid autonomous array and choose
\[
R(x)=x-V(x)=-F(x).
\]
Then \(\|R(x)\|_1\le2\), the total field is zero everywhere, and (34) holds. The solution starting at \(e_1\) remains \(e_1\) forever. In particular it violates the unforced bound \(x_1(t)\le u_{e_1}(t)<1\) for every \(t>0\). This is a direct counterexample to a potential but invalid proof that applies (19)--(20) to a forced trajectory. It does not contradict (7) or (8), whose nonzero input term remains present.

## 7. Proof of the reconstructed tracking theorem

First, (4) and the frozen coordinate inequality imply \(p(t)\in K_c\) for almost every \(t\). Absolute continuity makes \(p\) continuous, and \(K_c\) is closed. Hence
\[
p(t)\in K_c\quad\hbox{for every }t.
\tag{35}
\]

Fix \(s\le t\). Consider two unforced paths for the same actual coefficient path:
\[
z(v)=U(v,s,x(s)),\qquad
y(v)=U(v,s,p(s)).
\]
The path \(y\) stays in \(K_c\), although it need not equal the moving instantaneous fixed point \(p(v)\).

Apply the perturbation lemma on the full simplex to the viable forced path \(x\). Its residual is \(u(v)=R(v,x(v))\). Equations (5), (23), and (31) give
\[
\|x(t)-z(t)\|_1
\le\int_s^t\kappa_2(t-v)b(v)\,dv.
\tag{36}
\]
The initial mixed-cap estimate gives
\[
\|z(t)-y(t)\|_1
\le\kappa_1(t-s)\|x(s)-p(s)\|_1,
\tag{37}
\]
with the improvement in (9).

Finally, view \(p\) itself as a path in \(K_c\). Its residual relative to the unforced field is
\[
p'(v)-F(v,p(v))=p'(v)
\quad\hbox{a.e.}
\]
The perturbation lemma on \(K_c\), with its contraction kernel \(e^{-r\tau}\), gives
\[
\|p(t)-y(t)\|_1
\le\int_s^t e^{-r(t-v)}\|p'(v)\|_1\,dv.
\tag{38}
\]
Sum (36)--(38) by the triangle inequality to obtain (7); apply (24) to obtain (8).

This proves exactly why the sufficient forcing coefficient is \(C_2\), while the target-velocity coefficient is 1. The force error can be introduced at an arbitrary simplex point and is propagated using the full-simplex process bound. The target path and its comparison unforced path remain in \(K_c\), where the process contracts with prefactor 1. The distinction is geometric. Neither coefficient is claimed necessary or optimal.

## 8. Bounded-input and cap consequences within the reconstructed scope

The following are fresh consequences of the proved inequalities; they do not purport to restore all topics from lost report 069.

If \(b(v)\le B\) and \(\|p'(v)\|_1\le D\) almost everywhere on \([s,t]\), then
\[
\|x(t)-p(t)\|_1
\le C_1e^{-r(t-s)}\|x(s)-p(s)\|_1
 +\frac{C_2B+D}{r}(1-e^{-r(t-s)}).
\tag{39}
\]
It may always be intersected with the simplex diameter bound 2. In particular, under eventual uniform bounds,
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-p(t)\|_1
\le\min\{2,(C_2B+D)/r\}.
\tag{40}
\]

Let \(f(v)=C_2b(v)+\|p'(v)\|_1\ge0\). A sufficient bounded-window tracking condition is
\[
\int_t^{t+1}f(v)\,dv\longrightarrow0.
\tag{41}
\]
Indeed, split the exponential convolution in (8) into an initial finite interval and subsequent unit intervals counted backward from its endpoint. The first part decays exponentially. Once every late unit-window integral is at most \(\varepsilon\), the remaining part is bounded by a geometric sum at most
\(\varepsilon/(1-e^{-r})\), up to an initial piece that also decays. Letting \(\varepsilon\) tend to zero proves
\[
\|x(t)-p(t)\|_1\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is a sufficient condition, not an equivalence. If \(p(t)\) also converges, then \(x(t)\) converges to the same limit. No discrete-time equivalence from report 038 is being transferred to continuous time.

There is also an independent perturbed coordinate envelope when a constant input bound is available. Under tangency and \(\|R\|_1\le B\), each coordinate satisfies \(R_i\le B/2\), because the sum of the positive coordinates of a zero-sum vector is half its \(\ell^1\) norm. Therefore every viable solution obeys
\[
x_i'\le x_i^2-3x_i+1+B/2.
\tag{42}
\]
For \(0\le B<2\), define
\[
c_B=\frac{3-\sqrt{5-2B}}2,\qquad
d_B=\frac{3+\sqrt{5-2B}}2,\qquad
\delta_B=d_B-c_B.
\]
Then \(c\le c_B<1<d_B\). The same scalar comparison proves invariance of every forced cap \(K_q\), \(q\ge c_B\), for solutions initially in it. Starting anywhere in the simplex, the universal scalar envelope is
\[
u_B(\tau)=c_B+
 \frac{\delta_B a_B e^{-\delta_B\tau}}
      {1+a_Be^{-\delta_B\tau}},
\qquad a_B=\frac{1-c_B}{d_B-1}.
\]
For \(c_B<q<1\), it gives finite entry by
\[
T_{B,q}=\frac1{\delta_B}
 \log\frac{(1-c_B)(d_B-q)}
          {(d_B-1)(q-c_B)}.
\tag{43}
\]
These perturbed constants are sufficient and are not claimed sharp. The hypothesis \(B<2\) is used only for this nontrivial cap statement, not for (7) or (8).
