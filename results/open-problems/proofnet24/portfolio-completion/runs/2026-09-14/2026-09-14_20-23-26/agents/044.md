## 1. Setup and exact scope

Let \(\mathcal P\) be the closed coefficient polytope defined by

\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad\text{if }k\in\{i,j\},\qquad 1\le i,j,k\le4.
\tag{1}
\]

All allowed coefficients may vanish. Let \(\mathscr A\) be the real affine
space obtained by retaining symmetry, row normalization, and the exclusion
equalities in (1), but dropping nonnegativity. Its vector space of directions
\(\mathscr H\) has the same symmetry and exclusions and row sums zero. Set

\[
\|H\|_{\rm row}:=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|H_{ij,k}|,\qquad H\in\mathscr H.
\tag{2}
\]

This is the full row \(\ell^1\) norm, not total variation. There are exactly
14 independent real coefficient parameters: each of the four diagonal
parent rows has three allowed outputs and hence two independent coordinates;
each of the six distinct unordered parent pairs has two allowed outputs and
one independent coordinate. Thus \(\mathscr A\cong\mathbb R^{14}\), while
\(\mathcal P\cong(\Delta_2)^4\times[0,1]^6\).

For any real coefficient array \(R\), write

\[
\mathcal B_R(u,v)_k=\sum_{i,j}R_{ij,k}u_iv_j,
\qquad V_R(x)=\mathcal B_R(x,x).
\tag{3}
\]

The bilinear map is symmetric in \(u,v\). Put

\[
\mathcal X=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=1\},\qquad
E=\{z\in\mathbb R^4:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}z=0\},\qquad
\Delta_3=\mathcal X\cap\mathbb R_+^4.
\]

Normalization gives \(\sum_k\mathcal B_R(u,v)_k=(\sum_i u_i)(\sum_jv_j)\)
for \(R\in\mathscr A\), even if the coefficients or state coordinates are
negative. For \(H\in\mathscr H\), the corresponding sum is zero for every
\(u,v\). Consequently

\[
F(R,x):=x-V_R(x):\mathscr A\times\mathcal X\longrightarrow E
\tag{4}
\]

is polynomial on affine spaces of dimensions 14 and 3.

The existing four-type uniqueness theorem supplies a unique fixed point
\(p(P)\in\Delta_3\) for every \(P\in\mathcal P\). Its coordinate cap is

\[
p_i(P)\le c:=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad
a:=1-2c=\sqrt5-2>0,\qquad
C:=\frac1a=\sqrt5+2,
\quad D:=C-2c=2\sqrt5-1.
\tag{5}
\]

The cap follows from \(p_i\le(1-p_i)^2\). Denote the capped simplex by
\(K_c=\{x\in\Delta_3:\max_i x_i\le c\}\).

### Quantitative input from the capped contraction proof

For \(P\in\mathcal P\), \(m\in K_c\), set

\[
J_P(m)v=2\mathcal B_P(m,v),\qquad
M_P(m)=(1-c)I+cJ_P(m).
\]

The cut/column proof of Theorem 2 in `FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md` gives the
matrix estimate

\[
\boxed{\|M_P(m)|_E\|_{1\to1}\le1-ca<1.}
\tag{6}
\]

Crucially, this is a bound on **every vector of the full linear space
\(E\)**. Its proof uses the column-difference norm and the coefficient
constraints, not the assertion that every direction can be followed inside
\(K_c\) at a boundary point. Equivalently, the cut proof first gives
\(\max\{2c,1-c+2c^2\}=1-ca\). We use this algebraic matrix statement
directly. The same source gives invariance of \(K_c\) under
\(T_P=(1-c)I+cV_P\).

The ambient columns of \(J_P(m)\) sum to 2, and those of \(M_P(m)\) sum
to \(1+c\). Both maps preserve \(E\); neither ambient matrix is being
mistaken for a stochastic matrix of column sum 1.

## 2. Claim 1: one analytic extension across the entire coefficient boundary

**Claim 1.** Define

\[
r=\frac a4,\qquad
\varepsilon_0=\frac{a^2}{9}
=0.006192010000093468\ldots,
\]

\[
\mathcal U=\{R\in\mathscr A:\operatorname{dist}_{\rm row}
                  (R,\mathcal P)<\varepsilon_0\},
\qquad
\mathcal C_r=K_c+\{z\in E:\|z\|_1\le r\}.
\tag{7}
\]

There is a single real-analytic map

\[
\widetilde p:\mathcal U\longrightarrow\mathcal X
\]

such that \(\widetilde p(R)=V_R(\widetilde p(R))\) and
\(\widetilde p(P)=p(P)\) for every \(P\in\mathcal P\).
For every \(R\in\mathcal U\), this is the unique fixed point in
\(\mathcal C_r\). No uniqueness elsewhere in the signed affine state
space is asserted. The extension can have negative state coordinates when
\(R\notin\mathcal P\).

At an admissible array, with \(p=p(P)\), let

\[
J=J_P(p),\qquad S_P=(I-J|_E)^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
S_P=c\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(M_P(p)|_E)^n,\qquad
\|S_P\|_{1\to1}\le C=\sqrt5+2.
}
\tag{8}
\]

In particular, \(p\) is \(C^\infty\) and real analytic at boundary arrays
in the precise sense of being the restriction of a real-analytic function
on an open neighborhood in the 14-dimensional affine coefficient space.
This is not an application of the implicit function theorem to a simplex
incorrectly regarded as open.

### Proof

Equation (6) implies (8), since on \(E\)
\(I-M_P(p)=c(I-J)\). This already proves local real-analytic extension at
each admissible array by the classical real-analytic implicit function
theorem applied to (4). In particular, boundary fixed points have an
invertible derivative on the full affine tangent space. We give the
following quantitative construction to obtain a single extension on all
of \(\mathcal U\), avoiding an unproved patching of local branches.

Fix \(R\in\mathcal U\), choose \(P\in\mathcal P\) with
\(\eta=\|R-P\|_{\rm row}<\varepsilon_0\), and put \(H=R-P\).
For arbitrary real vectors,

\[
\|\mathcal B_P(u,v)\|_1\le\|u\|_1\|v\|_1,
\qquad
\|\mathcal B_H(u,v)\|_1\le\eta\|u\|_1\|v\|_1.
\tag{9}
\]

The first inequality uses nonnegative normalized rows of \(P\); the
second uses the definition of the row norm.

For every \(m\in\mathcal C_r\), choose \(m_0\in K_c\) with
\(\|m-m_0\|_1\le r\). Then \(\|m\|_1\le1+r\), and (9) yields

\[
\|(M_R(m)-M_P(m_0))|_E\|_{1\to1}
\le2c\,[r+\eta(1+r)].
\]

Together with (6), this bounds the derivative of
\(T_R=(1-c)I+cV_R\) throughout the convex set \(\mathcal C_r\) by

\[
L_\eta=1-c\,[a-2r-2\eta(1+r)]
\le L_*:=1-\frac{4ca}{9}<1.
\tag{10}
\]

Here the elementary identities \(a^2+4a=1\) and

\[
a-2r-2\varepsilon_0(1+r)=\frac{4a}{9},\qquad
\frac{4a}{9}r=\varepsilon_0
\tag{11}
\]

verify the constants exactly. The mean-value formula along segments in
\(\mathcal C_r\) proves its \(\ell^1\) contraction bound for \(T_R\).

For \(x=u+z\), \(u\in K_c\), \(z\in E\), \(\|z\|_1\le r\),
invariance of \(K_c\) for \(T_P\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}_1(T_Rx,K_c)
&\le\|T_Rx-T_Pu\|_1\\
&\le L_\eta r+c\eta
\le L_*r+c\varepsilon_0=r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The last inequality is strict when \(\eta<\varepsilon_0\): the expression
\(L_\eta r+c\eta\) is strictly increasing in \(\eta\). Thus \(T_R\)
maps the complete, compact, convex set \(\mathcal C_r\) into itself and
has a unique fixed point there by Banach's theorem. Its fixed points coincide
with those of \(V_R\). This defines \(\widetilde p(R)\) without reference
to the choice of \(P\).

At this fixed point, (10) gives an invertible tangent derivative of (4),
with the additional uniform extension bound

\[
\|(I-J_R(\widetilde p(R))|_E)^{-1}\|_{1\to1}
\le\frac{9}{4a}.
\tag{13}
\]

Also, strictness in (12) puts the fixed point in the relative interior of
\(\mathcal C_r\) in \(\mathcal X\). The local real-analytic branch
provided by the implicit function theorem therefore remains in
\(\mathcal C_r\) for nearby parameter values and equals its unique fixed
point there. These local branches agree with the already defined function
\(\widetilde p\), proving that it is real analytic on \(\mathcal U\).
For admissible \(P\), its known simplex fixed point lies in \(K_c\), so
the two definitions agree. This proves Claim 1. \(\square\)

## 3. Claim 2: exact derivatives and uniform remainder bounds

All derivatives below are derivatives of the analytic extension, evaluated
at \(P\in\mathcal P\). The bounds apply to **all** directions
\(H,K\in\mathscr H\), including directions that are not feasible within
the coefficient polytope.

**Claim 2.** With \(p=p(P)\) and \(S_P\) as in (8),

\[
\boxed{v_H:=Dp(P)[H]=S_P\mathcal B_H(p,p).}
\tag{14}
\]

The exact second derivative is

\[
\boxed{
D^2p(P)[H,K]
=S_P\big[2\mathcal B_P(v_H,v_K)
       +2\mathcal B_H(p,v_K)+2\mathcal B_K(p,v_H)\big].
}
\tag{15}
\]

Uniformly over the closed coefficient polytope,

\[
\boxed{\|Dp(P)[H]\|_1\le D\|H\|_{\rm row},
\qquad D=2\sqrt5-1,}
\tag{16}
\]

\[
\boxed{\|D^2p(P)[H,K]\|_1
\le38(\sqrt5+2)\|H\|_{\rm row}\|K\|_{\rm row}.}
\tag{17}
\]

For any two admissible arrays \(P,Q\), put \(H=Q-P\) and
\(\eta=\|H\|_{\rm row}\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\|p(Q)-p(P)-Dp(P)[Q-P]\|_1
\le19(\sqrt5+2)\eta^2.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The constants in (17) and (18) are approximately 160.971 and 80.4853.
Equation (18) is a non-asymptotic bound for every admissible pair, although
its useful regime is small coefficient perturbations. No sharpness is claimed.

### Exact columns for the 14 independent parameters

For each unordered pair \(i\le j\), choose one allowed reference output
\(b\). Parameterize every other allowed coefficient \(P_{ij,k}\) freely
and set the reference coefficient to one minus their sum. A unit parameter
direction increases \(P_{ij,k}\) and decreases \(P_{ij,b}\), also changing
the reversed parental row when \(i<j\). Equation (14) gives the exact
Jacobian column

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\partial p}{\partial P_{ij,k}}
=w_{ij}(p)\,S_P(e_k-e_b),\qquad
w_{ij}(p)=
\begin{cases}p_i^2,&i=j,\\2p_ip_j,&i<j.\end{cases}
}
\tag{19}
\]

The derivative notation in (19) means this normalized coordinate chart,
not variation of one coefficient while leaving its row sum incorrect.
In particular,

\[
\left\|\frac{\partial p}{\partial P_{ii,k}}\right\|_1
\le\sqrt5-1,
\qquad
\left\|\frac{\partial p}{\partial P_{ij,k}}\right\|_1
\le2(\sqrt5-1)\quad(i<j).
\tag{20}
\]

Indeed (8) bounds the column by \(2Cw_{ij}\), and
\(2Cc^2=\sqrt5-1\). The two different parental multiplicities in (19)
are essential.

### Proof of the derivative formulas and bounds

Differentiating \(p=\mathcal B_P(p,p)\) on the open affine parameter
neighborhood gives (14). Differentiating once more gives (15). Every right
side to which \(S_P\) is applied has coordinate sum zero: this follows from
the normalization identities following (3). Thus the inverse is always
being used on its correct domain \(E\).

Equations (8), (9), and (14) immediately give the bound
\(\|v_H\|_1\le C\|H\|_{\rm row}\). To retain the stronger constant
\(D\), adapt the forced-parent-pair argument from `agents/013.md`
directly to the linear equation

\[
v-Jv=g,\qquad g=\mathcal B_H(p,p),\qquad
\eta=\|H\|_{\rm row}.
\tag{21}
\]

If \(v=0\), there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let \(G\) be its positive
support and \(h=\sum_{i\in G}v_i=\|v\|_1/2\). If there is only one
positive coordinate, nonnegativity and the zero diagonal of \(J\) give
\(\sum_GJv\le0\). Since every coefficient row of \(H\) sums to zero,
the sum over any output group has absolute value at most \(\eta/2\).
Thus \(h\le\sum_Gg\le\eta/2\). The case of one negative coordinate is
the same after replacing \(v,H\) by \(-v,-H\). These cases give
\(\|v\|_1\le\eta\).

Otherwise the four coordinates have exactly two positive and two negative
entries. Write \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\). The transfer lemma
used in (6) gives
\(\sum_GJv\le2ch\). Moreover, for both parental pairs
\(\{i,k\}\) and \(\{j,\ell\}\), the group sum of their coefficient
perturbation is exactly zero: strict exclusion forces the first pair to
output outside \(G\), while normalization forces the second pair's total
perturbation inside \(G\) to sum to zero. Therefore

\[
\sum_Gg\le\frac\eta2
 [1-2(p_ip_k+p_jp_\ell)].
\tag{22}
\]

The coordinate cap implies

\[
0\le(c-p_i)(c-p_k)+(c-p_j)(c-p_\ell)
=2c^2-c+p_ip_k+p_jp_\ell,
\]

so \(p_ip_k+p_jp_\ell\ge ca\). Taking the group sum of (21) now gives
\(ah\le\eta(1-2ca)/2\), or
\(\|v\|_1\le(C-2c)\eta=D\eta\). This proves (16) for arbitrary signed
directions; no appeal to two-sided feasibility at a boundary array was made.

Combining (15), (9), (8), and (16),

\[
\|D^2p(P)[H,K]\|_1
\le C(2D^2+4D)\|H\|_{\rm row}\|K\|_{\rm row}.
\]

The exact identity \(D(D+2)=19\) yields (17).

For completeness, (18) also has a direct finite-increment proof. Integration
of (16) along the admissible segment \(P+tH\) recovers the already known
coefficient Lipschitz bound \(\|p(Q)-p(P)\|_1\le D\eta\).
Set \(q=p(Q)\), \(p=p(P)\), \(d=q-p\), and subtract the two fixed-point
equations. Bilinearity gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
d-S_P\mathcal B_H(p,p)
=S_P\big[\mathcal B_P(d,d)+\mathcal B_H(d,p+q)\big].
}
\tag{23}
\]

Since \(\|p+q\|_1=2\), (8) and (9) bound the right side by
\(C(\|d\|_1^2+2\eta\|d\|_1)\le CD(D+2)\eta^2\), which is (18).
This also checks the factor one-half between the Hessian bound and the
first-order Taylor remainder. \(\square\)

## 4. Claim 3: exact feasible boundary activation

**Claim 3.** Suppose \(P\in\mathcal P\) has boundary fixed point \(p\),
and let \(r_0\) be its missing type and \(S=\{1,2,3,4\}\setminus\{r_0\}\).
Then \(|S|=3\),

\[
a\le p_i\le c\quad(i\in S),\qquad
P_{ij,r_0}=0\quad\hbox{for every }i,j,
\qquad V_{P,r_0}\equiv0.
\tag{24}
\]

The full missing output row of \(J_P(p)\) is zero. Consequently, for every
affine coefficient direction \(H\in\mathscr H\),

\[
\boxed{
Dp_{r_0}(P)[H]=\mathcal B_H(p,p)_{r_0}
=\sum_{i\in S}H_{ii,r_0}p_i^2
 +2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in S}}H_{ij,r_0}p_ip_j.
}
\tag{25}
\]

An affine ray \(P+tH\) is feasible for all sufficiently small \(t\ge0\)
exactly when

\[
H\in\mathscr H,\qquad
H_{ij,k}\ge0\ \text{whenever }P_{ij,k}=0.
\tag{26}
\]

For such directions all the incoming coefficients in (25) are nonnegative,
and the following equivalence is exact:

\[
\boxed{
Dp_{r_0}(P)[H]>0
\iff H_{ij,r_0}>0\text{ for at least one }i,j\in S.
}
\tag{27}
\]

If (27) holds, the missing type is activated to first order. If it does not,
every incoming perturbation is zero and \(p_{r_0}(P+tH)=0\) for the entire
feasible affine ray interval. Thus for feasible rays there is no additional
case of zero first derivative followed by higher-order activation.

Let
\(\Gamma(H)=\sum_{i\in S}H_{ii,r_0}
+2\sum_{i<j,\,i,j\in S}H_{ij,r_0}\). The activation slope satisfies

\[
a^2\Gamma(H)\le Dp_{r_0}(P)[H]
\le\min\{c^2\Gamma(H),\ \|H\|_{\rm row}/2\}.
\tag{28}
\]

For an arbitrary admissible comparison array \(Q\), with \(H=Q-P\) and
\(\eta=\|H\|_{\rm row}\), the missing coordinate has the smaller
non-asymptotic remainder bound

\[
\boxed{
|p_{r_0}(Q)-Dp_{r_0}(P)[Q-P]|
\le\frac D2\eta^2.
}
\tag{29}
\]

### Proof and parameter-face interpretation

The cap \(p_i\le c<1/2\) rules out supports of size at most two. A boundary
fixed point therefore has exactly three positive coordinates; their sum is
one, so each is at least \(1-2c=a\). At the missing output,

\[
0=V_{P,r_0}(p)=\sum_{i,j\in S}P_{ij,r_0}p_ip_j.
\]

Every term is nonnegative and all parental products are positive, forcing
\(P_{ij,r_0}=0\) for every \(i,j\in S\). Pairs containing \(r_0\) are
already forbidden from outputting \(r_0\). This proves (24) and the zero
Jacobian row. The \(r_0\)-coordinate of (21) is then simply
\(v_{r_0}=g_{r_0}\), proving (25).

Condition (26) is precisely the one-sided tangent cone of this finite
coefficient polytope: an initially zero coefficient cannot have negative
forward derivative, while all initially positive coefficients remain
positive for small enough \(t\). Positivity of the weights in (25) gives
(27). If its right side fails, the entire output row remains zero along
the affine ray, so every fixed point of that operator has missing mass zero.
Equation (28) follows from (24) and from
\(0\le H_{ij,r_0}\le\|H\|_{\rm row}/2\), the latter using the zero sum of
each coefficient-direction row.

More globally, for each missing type \(r_0\), the locus

\[
\mathcal F_{r_0}
=\{P\in\mathcal P:P_{ij,r_0}=0\ \text{for }i,j\ne r_0\}
\tag{30}
\]

is a face of codimension six (dimension eight) in the coefficient polytope,
and

\[
p_{r_0}(P)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\in\mathcal F_{r_0}.
\tag{31}
\]

There is one independent extra zero condition for each of the six unordered
parent pairs in the remaining triple. Equivalently, the residual free
parameters consist of three diagonal-parent probabilities in that triple,
three distinct-parent probabilities involving \(r_0\), and two probabilities
in the \(r_0r_0\) row: dimension eight. Necessity in (31) was just proved;
sufficiency follows from \(V_{P,r_0}\equiv0\). Hence the entire boundary
fixed-point locus is the union of these four faces. The five free parameters
whose parent pair contains \(r_0\) have zero first derivative at such a
fixed point, as also follows immediately from (19).

To prove (29), use (24) and the fixed-point equation for \(q=p(Q)\):
\(q_{r_0}=\mathcal B_H(q,q)_{r_0}\). Put \(d=q-p\) and
\(m=(p+q)/2\). Every coefficient \(H_{ij,r_0}=Q_{ij,r_0}\) is nonnegative.
With \(\beta=\max_{i,j}H_{ij,r_0}\le\eta/2\), define
\(b_j=\sum_iH_{ij,r_0}m_i\), so \(0\le b_j\le\beta\). Polarization gives

\[
q_{r_0}-\mathcal B_H(p,p)_{r_0}=2b^{\mathsf T}d.
\]

Because \(\sum_jd_j=0\), a vector of range at most \(\beta\) obeys
\(|b^{\mathsf T}d|\le\beta\|d\|_1/2\). Thus the last display has
absolute value at most \(\beta\|d\|_1\le(D/2)\eta^2\), proving (29).
\(\square\)

### What does and does not follow for general feasible paths

The exact zero-slope/invariant-face dichotomy is a statement about affine
rays with one-sided feasibility. It is not true for arbitrary curved paths.
If \(K\) is a feasible activating direction, the analytic path
\(P(t)=P+t^2K\) is feasible for both positive and negative small \(t\), has
zero first derivative at zero, and satisfies

\[
p_{r_0}(P(t))=t^2\mathcal B_K(p,p)_{r_0}+O(t^4)>0
\quad(t\ne0\text{ sufficiently small}).
\tag{32}
\]

In fact, any two-sided differentiable path inside the polytope through
\(\mathcal F_{r_0}\) must have zero derivatives of all six nonnegative
incoming coefficients at zero. For a one-sided feasible real-analytic path,
suppose the earliest nonzero order among these six coefficient functions is
\(m\), with coefficients
\(P_{ij,r_0}(t)=b_{ij}t^m+O(t^{m+1})\), allowing some \(b_{ij}=0\).
Then every \(b_{ij}\ge0\), at least one is positive, and

\[
p_{r_0}(P(t))
=t^m\sum_{i,j\in S}b_{ij}p_ip_j+O(t^{m+1}),
\tag{33}
\]

with strictly positive leading coefficient. This follows directly from
the missing-coordinate fixed-point equation and \(p_i(P(t))=p_i+O(t)\).
For a path feasible on both sides, the earliest such order must be even.
If every incoming analytic function vanishes identically locally, so does
the missing fixed-point coordinate.

For unrestricted signed affine directions, cancellation in (25) can also
give zero slope without each incoming coefficient being zero. Therefore
the kernel of the unrestricted linear functional in (25) is not identified
with the tangent space to the codimension-six face. Nonnegativity in the
feasible tangent cone is exactly what makes (27) an equivalence.

Take deterministic offspring for the ten unordered parent pairs as follows,
with symmetric rows after reversing the parents:

| 22 | 3 |
| 33 | 1 |

| 23 | 1 |

This is the boundary example also used in `agents/029.md`. Its fixed point
is \(p=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0)\). Let \(H\) change only the 11 row, with
\(H_{11,2}=-1\), \(H_{11,4}=1\). The ray is admissible for \(0\le t\le1\).
Exact rational linear algebra gives

\[
Dp(P)[H]=\frac1{351}(-4,-25,-10,39),

p_4(P+tH)=\frac t9-\frac{8t^2}{1053}+O(t^3).
\tag{34}
\]

In particular, the analytic extension has negative fourth coordinate for
small negative \(t\); analytic continuation across the coefficient boundary
is not a stochastic-operator or simplex-preserving continuation. Replacing
\(t\) by \(t^2\) supplies the concrete two-sided feasible curved-path
example (32). A corresponding perturbation of the distinct-parent row 12
