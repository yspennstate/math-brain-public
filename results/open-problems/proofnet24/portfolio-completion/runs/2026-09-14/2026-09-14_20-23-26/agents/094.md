# Agent 094: finite rational continuation certificates for the four-type fixedpoint path

Research date: 2026-09-15. Scope: a deterministic certified continuation algorithm for a continuous rational piecewise-affine path of four-type strictly non-Volterra coefficients, including zero coefficients, simplex-boundary fixedpoints, an explicit finite stopping bound, and an actual certificate for every parameter in the path. No convergence assertion about unaveraged QSO trajectories is made.

## 1. Result and input contract

Let
\[
 V_P(x)_k=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,\qquad
 P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\quad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\quad
 P_{ij,k}=0\quad(k\in\{i,j\}).
\]
Write \(\mathcal P\) for this closed coefficient polytope and \(p(P)\) for its unique simplex fixedpoint. For coefficient differences use the full row norm
\[
 \eta(P,Q)=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|.
\]
Thus the norm is twice the maximum row total-variation distance.

The input consists of rational knots
\[
 t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m,
\]
rational arrays \(P_0,\ldots,P_m\in\mathcal P\), and a positive rational tolerance \(\varepsilon\). On the j-th original segment the coefficient path is
\[
 P(t)=P_j+\frac{t-t_j}{t_{j+1}-t_j}(P_{j+1}-P_j).
 \tag{1}
\]
Endpoint validity certifies validity throughout each segment because all coefficient conditions are affine equalities or nonnegative affine inequalities. In particular, coefficient-boundary points require no positivity margin.

**Continuation theorem.** The algorithm below terminates with finitely many rational nodes \(a_0<\cdots<a_M\), including every original knot, and rational vectors \(x_\nu\) in
\[
 K=\{x\in\Delta_3:x_i\le2/5\}.
\]
It returns a continuous rational polygon \(c(t)\), affine from \(x_\nu\) to \(x_{\nu+1}\) on each cell, and rational radii \(E_\nu\le\varepsilon\), such that
\[
 \boxed{\ \|p(P(t))-c(t)\|_1\le E_\nu
       \quad(a_\nu\le t\le a_{\nu+1}).\ }
 \tag{2}
\]
All coefficient and residual arithmetic, cell acceptance decisions, and certificate checks are exact rational comparisons. An independent certificate consumer need not trust a floating-point root finder, the search history, a claimed Newton inverse, or a sampling density.

For each original segment, set \(\eta_j=\eta(P_j,P_{j+1})\), and let \(d_j\) be the least nonnegative integer such that at least one of
\[
 \frac72\eta_j2^{-d_j}\le\varepsilon,\qquad
 162\eta_j^2\,4^{-d_j}\le4\varepsilon
 \tag{3}
\]
holds. The number of accepted cells is at most \(\sum_j2^{d_j}\). The algorithm may stop much earlier using an exact cubic-residual certificate. In the principal actual example it uses four cells where the conservative depth bound allows 128.

The certificate also identifies exact zero coordinates and the support on every open cell. Any fixedpoint selected in its simplex slices must be \(p(P(t))\); no branch choice or branch switch is possible. The proof of this statement uses certified coverage at every parameter, not only uniqueness at the sampled nodes.

## 2. Mathematical dependencies and rational constants

The following established inputs are used, with their original provenance retained.

1. On \(K\), \(T_P=3I/5+2V_P/5\) is invariant and contracts in \(\ell^1\) with factor \(23/25\). Its fixedpoint is the unique simplex fixedpoint, and
   \[
    \|x-p(P)\|_1\le5\|V_P(x)-x\|_1\quad(x\in K).
    \tag{4}
   \]
   Every simplex fixedpoint has coordinates at most
   \(c=(3-\sqrt5)/2<2/5\).
2. The geometry note proves
   \[
    \|p(P)-p(Q)\|_1
      \le\frac{204+95\sqrt5}{121}\eta(P,Q)
      \le K_0\eta(P,Q),\qquad K_0=\frac72.
    \tag{5}
   \]
   The rational relaxation follows from \(\sqrt5<9/4\):
   the radical constant is below \(1671/484<7/2\).
3. Report 044 proves analytic extension across the closed coefficient boundary on the full affine coefficient space, not by treating the simplex as open. It gives
   \[
    \|(I-DV_P(p)|_E)^{-1}\|_{1\to1}\le C=\sqrt5+2,
    \quad
    \|Dp(P)[H]\|_1\le D\|H\|_{\rm row},\quad D=2\sqrt5-1,
    \tag{6}
   \]
   where \(E=\{v:\sum v_i=0\}\). These estimates hold at boundary fixedpoints on the full tangent space.

For clarity, the second-derivative consequence actually needed here can be checked directly from those inputs. Define
\(\mathcal B_R(u,v)_k=\sum_{i,j}R_{ij,k}u_iv_j\). For
\(f(s)=p(P+sH)\), differentiation on the affine extension gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (I-DV)f'&=\mathcal B_H(f,f),\\
 (I-DV)f''&=2\mathcal B_{P+sH}(f',f')
                       +4\mathcal B_H(f,f').
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
The right sides have sum zero. Stochastic rows imply
\(\|\mathcal B_R(u,v)\|_1\le\|u\|_1\|v\|_1\) for admissible R, and a direction H gives the same bound with the extra factor \(\|H\|_{\rm row}\). Since \(\|f\|_1=1\), (6) yields
\[
 \|f''(s)\|_1
 \le C(2D^2+4D)\|H\|_{\rm row}^2
 =38C\|H\|_{\rm row}^2
 <162\|H\|_{\rm row}^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
Here \(D(D+2)=19\) and \(C<17/4\), so \(38C<323/2<162\). Set \(H_0=162\). Only this conservative rational bound is used by the program.

The explicit analytic extension in report 044 justifies the endpoint derivatives in (7), even when the coefficient segment or its fixedpoint lies on a boundary. The present continuation algorithm itself does not evaluate a derivative or perform signed-coefficient extrapolation.

## 3. Three rigorous whole-cell bounds

Consider one cell contained in an original segment. Rescale its parameter to \(s\in[0,1]\), write its coefficient endpoints as \(P,Q\), and let
\[
 \delta=\eta(P,Q),\qquad
 c_s=(1-s)x+sy,\qquad x,y\in K.
\]
Suppose the endpoint errors have been certified by exact residuals:
\[
 e_x=5\|V_P(x)-x\|_1,\qquad
 e_y=5\|V_Q(y)-y\|_1.
 \tag{9}
\]
The notation \(c_s\) denotes the polygonal candidate, distinct from the universal coordinate cap c used above.

### 3.1. Coefficient-Lipschitz chord bound

Let \(f(s)=p((1-s)P+sQ)\). The identity
\[
 f(s)-(1-s)f(0)-sf(1)
 =(1-s)(f(s)-f(0))+s(f(s)-f(1))
\]
and (5) give
\[
 \|f(s)-c_s\|_1
 \le (1-s)e_x+se_y+2K_0\delta s(1-s).
\]
Consequently a rational uniform radius is
\[
 E_L=\max(e_x,e_y)+\frac{7\delta}{4}.
 \tag{10}
\]

### 3.2. Curvature chord bound

For a twice continuously differentiable vector-valued f, subtract its endpoint chord. The solution with zero endpoint values is
\[
 f(s)-(1-s)f(0)-sf(1)
 =-\int_0^1G(s,u)f''(u)\,du,
\]
where
\[
 G(s,u)=
 \begin{cases}
 u(1-s),&u\le s,\\
 s(1-u),&u\ge s.
 \end{cases}
\]
This formula follows by integrating twice; \(G\ge0\) and
\(\int_0^1G(s,u)\,du=s(1-s)/2\). Applying (8) with \(H=Q-P\) therefore gives
\[
 \|f(s)-c_s\|_1
 \le(1-s)e_x+se_y+\frac{H_0\delta^2}{2}s(1-s),
\]
and the uniform rational radius
\[
 E_H=\max(e_x,e_y)+\frac{81\delta^2}{4}.
 \tag{11}
\]
This is a chord enclosure with a proved global remainder; it is not an unverified derivative predictor.

### 3.3. Exact cubic Bernstein residual bound

A stronger, directly checkable local bound requires no derivative input at all. Put
\[
 A=Q-P,\qquad d=y-x,\qquad
 R(s)=V_{P+sA}(x+sd)-(x+sd).
\]
Bilinearity gives exactly
\[
 R(s)=r_0+r_1s+r_2s^2+r_3s^3,
\]
with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_0&=\mathcal B_P(x,x)-x,\\
 r_1&=2\mathcal B_P(x,d)+\mathcal B_A(x,x)-d,\\
 r_2&=\mathcal B_P(d,d)+2\mathcal B_A(x,d),\\
 r_3&=\mathcal B_A(d,d).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
\]
There are no omitted higher-degree terms: the coefficients depend affinely on s and the candidate has degree one in each of its two state factors.

The cubic Bernstein coefficients are
\[
 b_0=r_0,\quad
 b_1=r_0+r_1/3,\quad
 b_2=r_0+2r_1/3+r_2/3,\quad
 b_3=r_0+r_1+r_2+r_3.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus
\[
 R(s)=(1-s)^3b_0+3s(1-s)^2b_1+3s^2(1-s)b_2+s^3b_3.
\]
The four weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Therefore
\[
 \|R(s)\|_1\le\max_{\ell=0,1,2,3}\|b_\ell\|_1.
\]
The whole candidate chord lies in K by convexity. Applying the pointwise residual estimate (4) to every s proves the whole-cell bound
\[
 \boxed{\ E_B=5\max_{\ell=0,1,2,3}\|b_\ell\|_1.\ }
 \tag{14}
\]
Every number in (12)-(14) is rational and is independently recomputable from the two endpoint candidates and the original coefficient path.

The emitted cell radius is
\[
 \boxed{\ E=\min\{2,E_L,E_H,E_B\}.\ }
 \tag{15}
\]
The diameter bound two is also valid because both f(s) and the chord belong to the simplex. Taking the minimum preserves validity. In every nontrivial cell of the actual activation example the Bernstein bound is the selected one.

As a useful exact degeneracy, if \(x=y\) and x is an exact fixedpoint for both endpoint operators, it is an exact fixedpoint for every intervening operator because V is affine in its coefficients. Equations (12)-(14) automatically recognize this: all four Bernstein coefficients vanish and the radius is zero.

## 4. Finite exact endpoint solver and explicit step choice

Set the anchor target
\[
 \tau=\varepsilon/2,\qquad
 N=20\max\{1,\lceil75/\tau\rceil\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The denominator N is the same for all anchors. In particular, \(20\mid N\) and \(N\ge1500/\tau\). The exact grid construction is a port of report 065, with two continuation-specific additions: reuse of a preceding certified anchor and a zero-coordinate-preserving restart on an exact boundary face.

Let \(Q_N\) be largest-remainder rounding to the simplex grid with denominator N: take coordinate floors of Ny, allocate the remaining units to the largest fractional remainders, and break ties by lower type index. For y in K, the floor and ceiling of every Ny_i are at most \(2N/5\), which is an integer. A zero remainder never receives an additional unit: if r units remain, the sum of the remainders is the integer r and there must be more than r positive remainders when r is positive. Hence
\[
 Q_N(K)\subset K,\qquad
 \|Q_N(y)-y\|_1\le4/N.
 \tag{17}
\]
This includes cap equality and zero state coordinates.

The default initial point is the uniform point, already on the grid. If an output column is identically zero, start instead from the largest-remainder rounding of the uniform distribution on its three remaining types. That distribution lies in K; the rounded start remains in K, and the missing coordinate is exactly zero. Invariance under T and the zero-remainder rule preserve that zero throughout the anchor calculation.

A warm start is a preceding grid candidate \(x_{\rm old}\). If its certified error was \(e_{\rm old}\) for \(P_{\rm old}\), then at the new coefficient array P its initial error is at most
\[
 D_0=\min\{2,e_{\rm old}+K_0\eta(P,P_{\rm old})\}.
 \tag{18}
\]
If a newly encountered exact missing coordinate is positive in that warm start, the implementation performs the face restart above and uses \(D_0=2\). Thus no projection whose error is unaccounted for is hidden in the warm-start estimate.

The rounded iteration is
\[
 x_{n+1}=Q_N\left(\frac35x_n+\frac25V_P(x_n)\right).
\]
With \(L=23/25\) and \(d_n=\|x_n-p(P)\|_1\), (17) gives
\[
 d_n\le L^nD_0+\frac{50}{N}.
 \tag{19}
\]
The integer inequality \(2\cdot23^9\le25^9\) gives \(L^9\le1/2\). Choose k to be the smallest nonnegative integer with
\[
 2^k\ge30D_0/\tau,\qquad n_*=9k.
 \tag{20}
\]
This choice is made by integer comparisons, including \(D_0=0\); no floating logarithm is needed.

For all simplex states, polarization and stochasticity give
\(\|V_P(x)-V_P(p)\|_1\le2\|x-p\|_1\), hence
\[
 5\|V_P(x)-x\|_1\le15\|x-p\|_1.
\]
At the proved horizon,
\[
 5\|V_P(x_{n_*})-x_{n_*}\|_1
 \le15\left(D_0\,2^{-k}+50/N\right)\le\tau.
 \tag{21}
\]
The program checks the exact residual before each update and returns as soon as its untruncated value \(5\|V_P(x)-x\|_1\) is at most tau. It must return by (20). Exhausting that proved limit raises an error instead of issuing a false certificate.

The integer implementation has no loop over N grid points. If D is a common denominator of the coefficients, \(C_{ij,k}=DP_{ij,k}\), and \(x_i=n_i/N\), it computes
\[
 S_k=\sum_{i\le j}(2-\mathbf1_{i=j})C_{ij,k}n_in_j,\quad
 V_P(x)_k=\frac{S_k}{DN^2},
 \tag{22}
\]
\[
 NT_P(x)_k=\frac{3DNn_k+2S_k}{5DN},\qquad
 \|V_P(x)-x\|_1=
 \frac{\sum_k|S_k-DNn_k|}{DN^2}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Four integer divisions and a four-entry remainder ordering implement each rounded step. The fixed grid prevents repeated squaring of denominators from earlier exact quadratic iterates.

## 5. Adaptive partition, coverage, and finite complexity

The deterministic search is as follows.

1. Validate the rational input and all coefficient endpoints. Solve the original knot anchors with target tau, reusing a preceding anchor when permitted by Section 4.
2. On each original coefficient segment, compute the exact integer depth limit (3). Begin with its two knot anchors as one candidate cell.
3. Compute (12)-(15). If \(E\le\varepsilon\), accept the cell. Otherwise bisect its parameter interval exactly, solve the midpoint anchor using the left endpoint as the warm start, and process the left subcell before the right subcell.
4. Stop when every leaf cell has been accepted. Emit the ordered shared endpoints, coefficient binding, residuals, four Bernstein vectors, radius terms, and boundary labels.

The implementation uses an explicit stack, so recursion depth is not a hidden runtime cutoff.

Every midpoint is rational, its coefficients are exactly those prescribed by (1), and it is certified by (21). At depth d within the j-th original segment, its coefficient distance is precisely \(\eta_j2^{-d}\), including \(\eta_j=0\). Since both anchor errors are at most \(\varepsilon/2\), either inequality in (3) forces (10) or (11) to be at most epsilon. Therefore subdivision beyond \(d_j\) is never needed.

There are at most \(2^{d_j}\) leaf cells for that segment. The leaves cover it without gaps, with disjoint interiors. Shared midpoints are reused, original knot anchors are shared between adjacent segments, and their ordered union covers \([t_0,t_m]\). Exactly M+1 distinct anchors are solved for M final cells.

Let \(V_{\rm path}=\sum_j\eta_j\) be coefficient variation. The proved bound implies
\[
 M\le\sum_j2^{d_j}
 \le2m+2V_{\rm path}
      \min\left\{\frac{7}{2\varepsilon},
                       \frac9{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}\right\}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The square root here describes the bound; the algorithm uses only the rational tests (3). For small epsilon the worst-case cell count is \(O(m+V_{\rm path}/\sqrt\varepsilon)\). Residual-based acceptance can be far cheaper; no optimality claim is made.

For an explicit bit model, let B be the total binary encoding length of the rational input knots and coefficient arrays, h the encoding length of epsilon, and \(s=1+B+h\). The dimension is fixed at four. Since \(\eta_j\le2\), every \(d_j=O(1+h)\); anchor times and coefficients obtained by dyadic interpolation have \(O(s)\)-bit numerators and denominators. So do N, the common coefficient denominators, grid counts, (22)-(23), and all cell certificate data. The anchor horizon is \(O(1+h)\).

Using a conservative cubic upper bound for ordinary exact integer and rational arithmetic, generation costs
\[
 O((M+1)(1+h)s^3)
\]
bit operations, in addition to parsing and input validation. The output uses \(O((M+1)s)\) bits. Reconstructing coefficients at sorted nodes by binary lookup adds at most an \(O(\log(m+1))\) factor to certificate verification, which otherwise uses a fixed amount of rational arithmetic per node and cell. The implementation keeps the output in memory; it is not a claim of constant-memory streaming.

This is an output-sensitive polynomial bound. It is deliberately not described as polynomial in tolerance bit length for the entire explicitly partitioned path: an allowed number of cells of order \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\) can be exponential in h. Report 065's polynomial-bit single-point guarantee does not by itself imply a polynomial-bit explicit whole-path output.

The JSON interface accepts integers and explicit numerator/denominator strings. It rejects floating-point fractions, booleans, nonpositive tolerances, and compressed exponent strings. Mathematical termination is conditional on exact arithmetic and sufficient finite machine resources; there is no discretionary accuracy, subdivision, or iteration cutoff in the algorithm.

## 6. Rational tube certificate and no branch switching

Each accepted cell specifies a rational polyhedral tube
\[
 \mathcal T_\nu=
 \{(t,z):a_\nu\le t\le a_{\nu+1},\
             z\in K,\ \|z-c(t)\|_1\le E_\nu\}.
 \tag{25}
\]
The norm inequality can, if desired, be written as the sixteen rational linear inequalities
\(\sigma\cdot(z-c(t))\le E_\nu\), \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^4\).
Thus this is a concrete finite rational tube, not an unspecified neighborhood from the implicit function theorem.

For any two probability vectors the absolute error of one coordinate is at most half their l1 distance. Each cell therefore also emits the rational, static coordinate bounds
\[
 p_i(P(t))\in
 \left[
 \max\{0,\min(x_{\nu,i},x_{\nu+1,i})-E_\nu/2\},
 \min\{2/5,\max(x_{\nu,i},x_{\nu+1,i})+E_\nu/2\}
 \right].
 \tag{26}
\]
Exact zero-output coordinates are set to the singleton interval [0,0]. A tighter affine moving interval follows directly from (2); the stored static intervals suffice for positivity deductions.

The verifier recomputes the coefficient array at each rational node from the embedded original path. It checks:

- Valid stochastic parent-excluding arrays, increasing original knots, and positive tolerance.
- Increasing nodes with the exact original endpoints and adjacency of every cell.
- Containment of each cell in its declared original affine segment, so no input knot is silently crossed.
- Candidate membership in K, each exact residual, and the anchor comparison \(e_\nu\le\varepsilon/2\).
- All four Bernstein vectors, every term in (15), the selected radius, and \(E_\nu\le\varepsilon\).
- Exact boundary/support labels and every coordinate interval.

The generator and verifier share the rational cell-formula implementation, but the verifier does not trust stored formula results. The accompanying runner additionally checks all emitted Bernstein identities coefficient by coefficient using a separate ordered-parent polynomial convolution. The residual at every anchor is also recomputed by that separate expansion, independently of the integer grid solver.

The search-history fields (grid plan, update counts, warm-start bounds, and depth descriptions) are explanatory metadata. Mathematical certificate soundness does not require replaying them, and the main verifier does not claim to validate that history. The focused runner checks grid/count binding and recorded update counts against their limits; Sections 4-5 establish the universal algorithmic termination claims.

For the branch statement, (5) first supplies a single continuous fixedpoint function along the entire path, including its boundary parameters. More substantially, Sections 3 and 6 place that fixedpoint inside the emitted tube for every real parameter of every cell. Adjacent closed tubes share \((a_\nu,p(P(a_\nu)))\), and the polygon has a common rational endpoint there. Any other simplex fixedpoint selected in a tube slice equals \(p(P(t))\) by the four-type uniqueness theorem. Consequently neither a root-tracking implementation nor gluing at a coefficient corner can select a different simplex branch.

The assertion concerns the simplex fixedpoint graph. It makes no claim that a tube consists entirely of roots, that signed affine roots elsewhere are absent, or that unaveraged dynamics converge.

## 7. Exact boundary strata and quantitative activation

There is an exact coefficient test for every boundary event:
\[
 \boxed{\ p_r(P)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_{ij,r}=0\ \hbox{for all parents }i,j.\ }
 \tag{27}
\]
Here is the complete argument. The coordinate cap \(2/5<1/2\) rules out supports of size at most two. If \(p_r=0\), precisely the other three coordinates are positive, each at least \(1-2(2/5)=1/5\). The equation
\[
 0=\sum_{i,j\ne r}P_{ij,r}p_ip_j
\]
has positive parental weights and nonnegative summands, forcing every displayed coefficient to vanish. Parent pairs containing r already exclude output r. Conversely an identically zero output forces \(V_r\equiv0\), so its fixedpoint coordinate is zero. No two output columns can both vanish for an admissible array, since that would force a fixedpoint with support at most two.

An allowed coefficient is nonnegative and affine on each original segment. At an interior parameter it is zero exactly when both its endpoint values are zero. Therefore:

- If the same complete output column vanishes at both endpoints, that coordinate is identically zero throughout the closed segment.
- If it vanishes at exactly one endpoint, that coordinate is zero there and strictly positive at every interior parameter.
- If no complete output column vanishes at both endpoints, every fixedpoint coordinate is strictly positive in the open segment.

It follows that boundary changes can occur only at the original knots. The algorithm labels those knots separately, and its open-cell support labels are exact even when a numerical interval includes zero. This includes a path which leaves one boundary face and ends at a different boundary face.

There is also a rational quantitative activation estimate. Suppose \(P_0\) has missing type r, \(P(t)=P_0+tA\), \(0\le t\le1\), and \(\eta=\|A\|_{\rm row}\). All incoming \(A_{ij,r}\) are nonnegative. Put
\[
 \Gamma=\sum_{i,j\ne r}A_{ij,r}.
\]
The ordered sum includes the factor two for distinct parents. If \(\Gamma=0\), the output stays identically zero for the full segment. If \(\Gamma>0\), it is activated at every \(t>0\).

For \(\eta>0\), set the explicit rational neighborhood
\[
 t_*=\min\{1,1/(5K_0\eta)\}
     =\min\{1,2/(35\eta)\}.
\]
Equation (5) and the coordinate half-distance bound imply
\[
 p_i(P(t))\ge1/5-K_0\eta t/2\ge1/10
 \quad(i\ne r,\ 0\le t\le t_*).
\]
The missing-coordinate equation is exactly
\[
 p_r(P(t))=t\sum_{i,j\ne r}A_{ij,r}p_i(P(t))p_j(P(t)).
\]
It gives the explicit bounds
\[
 \boxed{\ \frac{\Gamma t}{100}\le p_r(P(t))
 \le t\min\{4\Gamma/25,\eta/2\}
 \quad(0\le t\le t_*).\ }
 \tag{28}
\]
The upper bounds actually hold on the whole segment. The \(\eta/2\) bound uses that a single component of a zero-sum row direction is at most half its row l1 norm.

A certified boundary slope can also be obtained without inverting a derivative matrix. Let x be a boundary anchor with error e, and put \(L_x=\mathcal B_A(x,x)_r\), a rational number. The exact slope is \(L=\mathcal B_A(p(P_0),p(P_0))_r\). To bound the difference, set \(\beta=\max_{ij}A_{ij,r}\le\eta/2\). For simplex u,v, polarization gives
\[
 \mathcal B_A(u,u)_r-\mathcal B_A(v,v)_r=2b^{\mathsf T}(u-v),
 \quad 0\le b_j\le\beta.
\]
Because u-v has sum zero,
\(|2b^{\mathsf T}(u-v)|\le\beta\|u-v\|_1\).
Combining this with (5) proves
\[
 |L-L_x|\le\eta e/2,\qquad
 \boxed{\ |p_r(P(t))-tL_x|
   \le(\eta e/2)t+(7\eta^2/4)t^2.\ }
 \tag{29}
\]
Continuity suffices to identify the slope from the exact missing-coordinate equation. In particular \(L\ge\Gamma/25>0\) for an activating affine direction. There is no zero-first-order/later-activation case for a feasible affine segment; curved paths are outside this input contract and need not have that property.
