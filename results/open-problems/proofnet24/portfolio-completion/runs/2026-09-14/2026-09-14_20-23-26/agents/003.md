## Result and recommendation

The strongest finishable target in these six tracks is a **strengthening of P22's actual Gaussian-kernel benignity theorem**. The latest report establishes consistency for uniformly bounded, fixed-degree signals. Its own low/high argument actually proves uniform consistency over the entire growing low-harmonic ball of degrees

\[
0\le s<k_\ell,
\qquad k_\ell=2^\ell+\ell-1.
\]

The key improvement is to retain the tail mass in the signal penalty instead of replacing it by one:

\[
\frac{\tau}{n\lambda_{k-1}}
\le
\frac{N_k}{n}\frac{\gamma}{d+2k-2}
\frac1{1-\gamma/(k+1)}
=d^{\delta_*-1+o(1)}\to0,
\quad \delta_*=(\log 2)^{-1}-1.
\]

The result holds with conditional mean-zero noise and conditional covariance bounded by \(\sigma^2I\); Gaussian noise is unnecessary. It gives convergence almost surely along the prescribed sequence, under arbitrary cross-level coupling with the specified marginal assumptions. Section 5 gives a complete deterministic transfer proof and precise probabilistic conclusion. The extension is sharp at the level of whole harmonic balls: uniform recovery on degree \(k\) is impossible because \(N_k>n\).

This is a substantive new deduction relative to the six newest reports inspected. Global publication novelty is **not established**. The exact subpolynomial dimension sequence and predicted benignity already appear in Medvedev–Vardi–Srebro (2024), under a Gaussian/eigenframework ansatz. The significance of P22 is a direct actual-kernel proof replacing that ansatz. The quadratic-feature Wishart hard-edge conjecture remains open; this run does not solve it.

Two additional bounded proof checks are recorded: P19's mixed Haar lemma needs uncentered weight norms, and P21's same-coupling difference follows automatically whenever both risks have the asserted common deterministic probability limit.

\[
\epsilon_j=\gamma_j+\sqrt{(24n\gamma_j^2+12B_j^2/n)/\delta}.
\]

The clipped lower-alignment/upper-energy gate then does not worsen squared population risk with probability \(1-\delta\). The result is a valid conditional stability theorem, not an unconditional guarantee for the historical neural pipeline. Actual full-pipeline deletion energies, tuning stability, average-stability certificates, and the unsquared relative-error metric remain open. This is a good applied theorem-development target but does not close a famous universal conjecture. ## 4. Independent P22 concentration check

Put \(\gamma=h^{-2}\). Let \(P_s\) be the projection kernel of degree-s harmonics, \(N_s=P_s(x,x)\), \(r_s=P_s/N_s\). The exact Mercer series is

\[
\lambda_s=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{j\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2j}}{2^j j!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+j)-2)}.
\]

Termwise comparison yields \(\lambda_{s+1}/\lambda_s\le\gamma/(d+2s)\). For \(d\ge3\), the exact multiplicity ratio is

\[
\frac{N_{s+1}}{N_s}=
\frac{(2s+d)(d+s-2)}{(s+1)(2s+d-2)}.
\]

Consequently the trace masses \(\tau_s=N_s\lambda_s\) satisfy the particularly useful all-degree bound

\[
\frac{\tau_{s+1}}{\tau_s}
\le\frac{\gamma(d+s-2)}{(s+1)(d+2s-2)}
\le\frac{\gamma}{s+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This implies, for \(\tau=\sum_{s\ge k}\tau_s\),

\[
\tau_k\le\tau\le\frac{\tau_k}{1-\gamma/(k+1)}
\quad(k+1>\gamma),\qquad
\lambda_k/\tau\le1/N_k.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The Gaussian homogeneous harmonic lift lies in the pure degree-s chaos. Gaussian hypercontractivity followed by radial-angular independence therefore gives exactly

\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_p\le(p-1)^{s/2}/\sqrt{N_s},\qquad p\ge2.
\]

With \(r_T=\sum_{s\ge k}(\tau_s/\tau)r_s\), (4.1) sums the entire infinite tail: at \(q=R\), consecutive weighted hypercontractive terms have ratio at most \(\gamma\sqrt{2R}/(k+1)=O(R^{-1/2})\). Thus, uniformly in z,

\[
\|r_T\|_{2R}\le C_h(2R)^{k/2}/\sqrt{N_k},\quad
\|r_T^2\|_2\le C_h3^k/N_k,
\]

and \(E r_T(X,z)^2=(1+o(1))/N_k\). Rosenthal with its conservative factor \(C R\), followed by the \(L^R\) bound for a maximum over n decoupled test columns, bounds the relative row-square errors by

\[
E_1=C_h dR3^k/\sqrt n=d^{-R/2+O(1)},
\quad E_2=C_h Rd^2(2R)^k/n=d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}.
\]

Both vanish. Shared training rows among the columns do not invalidate the maximum bound; no independence between those row-square statistics is needed.

Writing \(A=E\|R_T-I\|\), the cited decoupling/independent-column inequality before its entrywise relaxation, together with

\[
G_T=\tau^{-2}\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X)
\preceq(\lambda_k/\tau)R_T\preceq R_T/N_k,
\]

gives

\[
A\le C_h\sqrt{(n\log n/N_k)(1+A)}
+C_h\log n\sqrt{n/N_k}\sqrt{1+E_1+E_2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The scalar inequality closes, for example by \(a\sqrt A\le A/2+a^2/2\). Correct Stirling yields

\[
N_k/n=d^{\delta_*+o(1)},\qquad
D/n=d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)},\quad D=\sum_{s<k}N_s.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For every fixed \(0<\eta<\delta_*\), eventually \(A\le C_{h,\eta}R^2d^{-\eta/2}\). This is summable along ell. For the low feature matrix \(\Psi\), constant row norm \(\sqrt D\) and isotropy give matrix Chernoff:

\[
P\{\|\Psi^T\Psi/n-I\|>1/2\}\le2D e^{-c n/D},
\]

also summable. Define the good design event

\[
\mathcal E_\ell=
\{\|T/\tau-I\|\le1/2,\ \|\Psi^T\Psi/n-I\|\le1/2\},
\quad T=K_T(X,X).
\]

Then \(\sum_\ell P(\mathcal E_\ell^c)<\infty\). No empirical full-Gram hard-edge estimate, iid replacement of harmonic coordinates, or Gaussian-design ansatz is needed in this chain. The matrix concentration inequalities and Gaussian hypercontractivity are imported standard results, not proved from first principles here.

## 5. New theorem: uniform growing low-harmonic benignity

### 5.1 General deterministic transfer lemma

Assume a low/high kernel split with low feature matrix \(\Psi\in\mathbb R^{n\times D}\), positive diagonal low Mercer matrix \(\Lambda\), high empirical matrix T, and high population covariance \(H_T\preceq\lambda_k T\). On the good event of Section 4 put

\[
W=\tau T^{-1},\quad
G=\tau\Lambda^{-1}+\Psi^T W\Psi.
\]

Then \((2/3)I\preceq W\preceq2I\), \(G\succeq nI/3\), and \(\|G^{-1}\|\le3/n\). Exact high-RKHS elimination shows that the minimum-norm interpolant at labels y has low coefficients and high residual

\[
\widehat a=Ly,\quad L=G^{-1}\Psi^T W,\quad
r=Qy,\quad Q=I-\Psi L.
\]

The high prediction norm obeys

\[
\|\widehat f_T\|_2^2
=r^TT^{-1}H_TT^{-1}r
\le(\lambda_k/\tau)r^TWr.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define \(B_0=W^{1/2}\Psi\). Since \(G=\tau\Lambda^{-1}+B_0^TB_0\),

\[
0\preceq I-B_0G^{-1}B_0^T\preceq I.
\]

Conjugating and squaring gives the exact weighted contraction

\[
Q^TWQ\preceq W.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a noiseless low signal \(f=\Psi\theta\) with \(\|\theta\|\le B\),

\[
e:=\widehat a-\theta=-G^{-1}\tau\Lambda^{-1}\theta,
\qquad r=-\Psi e.
\]

Let \(\mu=\min\operatorname{diag}\Lambda\), \(a=\tau/(n\mu)\), \(b=n\lambda_k/\tau\). Then

\[
\|e\|\le3aB,
\quad
\|\widehat f_T\|_2^2\le3b\|e\|^2,
\]

because \(\Psi^TW\Psi\preceq3nI\). Low/high harmonics are orthogonal in population. Hence, uniformly over the entire low signal ball,

\[
\|\widehat f[f(X)]-f\|_2^2\le9B^2a^2(1+3b).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now assume only

\[
E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0,
\quad E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^T\mid X]\preceq\sigma^2 I_n.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since \(W^2\preceq2W\),

\[
LL^T
=G^{-1}\Psi^T W^2\Psi G^{-1}
\preceq2G^{-1}\preceq6I_D/n.
\]

The low noise risk is at most \(6\sigma^2D/n\). Equations (5.1)–(5.2) give high noise risk at most \(2\sigma^2b\). Therefore

\[
E[\|\widehat f[\varepsilon]\|_2^2\mid X]
\le\sigma^2(6D/n+2b).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For each deterministic signal, linearity and conditional centering remove the signal/noise cross term. Its conditional mean risk is at most the sum of (5.3) and (5.5). More strongly, with **the same noise vector used for every signal** to define the random supremum,

\[
E\left[\sup_{\|f\|_2\le B,\ f\in\mathcal H_{<k}}
\|\widehat f[f(X)+\varepsilon]-f\|_2^2\mid X\right]
\le18B^2a^2(1+3b)+\sigma^2(12D/n+4b).
\tag{5.6}
\]

This follows from \(\|u+v\|^2\le2\|u\|^2+2\|v\|^2\). It is a genuine simultaneous bound, rather than merely a bound for a fixed function.

### 5.2 Application to the actual Gaussian kernel

For all sufficiently large d, eigenvalue monotonicity makes \(\mu=\lambda_{k-1}\). Equations (4.1)–(4.2) give

\[
a\le
\frac{N_k}{n}\frac{\gamma}{d+2k-2}
\frac1{1-\gamma/(k+1)}
=d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Also \(b\le n/N_k=d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}\). Thus on \(\mathcal E_\ell\), the conditional expectation of the supremum in (5.6) is bounded by

\[
C_{h,B,\sigma}\left[
d^{-2(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}
+d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}
+d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}\right]
=O(d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

All these bounds are summable along ell. For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\), conditional Markov and Section 4 imply a summable upper bound for the probability that the random supremum exceeds epsilon. Apply first Borel–Cantelli for the countable thresholds \(\epsilon=1,1/2,1/3,\ldots\).

**Theorem 003-P22-GROWING-LOW-BALL.** Fix \(h>0\), \(B<\infty\), and the prescribed sequence. For iid uniform spherical design and any levelwise noise satisfying (5.4),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{f\in\bigoplus_{s<k_\ell}\mathcal H_{d_\ell,s},\ \|f\|_2\le B}
\|\widehat f_\ell[f(X)+\varepsilon]-f\|_2^2
\longrightarrow0\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
\]

The conclusion holds under any joint coupling of levels with the specified levelwise assumptions. It therefore implies convergence in probability for every uniformly bounded growing-degree signal sequence in that ball. The signal component alone has the faster displayed rate \(d^{-2(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}\) on the good designs.

This theorem does **not** assert convergence of the unconditional all-training expectation: the argument bounds risk only on good designs, and bad-design inverse tails have not been integrated. It also does not treat arbitrary bounded-L2 high-degree signals, varying bandwidth, or the P20 hard edge.

## 6. Sharp whole-space cutoff and failed shortcuts

The first high degree satisfies \(N_k/n\to\infty\). For every fixed design with \(N_k>n\), evaluation from \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\) to \(\mathbb R^n\) has a nonzero kernel. Choose a norm-B harmonic f in that kernel. Its noiseless labels are all zero, and the minimum-RKHS-norm interpolant is zero. Its population error equals \(B^2\). Hence

\[
\sup_{f\in\mathcal H_{d,k},\|f\|_2\le B}
\|\widehat f[f(X)]-f\|_2^2\ge B^2.
\]

So uniform consistency on the whole degree-k ball is impossible, whereas the whole degree-\((k-1)\) ball is covered by Section 5. This is a pathwise uniform impossibility; it does not assert that each fixed degree-k signal fails.

Specific failed routes: replacing tau by one loses the growing-degree result; extending the signal space through degree k contradicts dimension; Gaussianity is not used by the risk upper bound; bulk ESD does not replace operator-norm tail control; a deterministic population spectrum alone does not control the empirical inverse; summable good-design failure does not establish unconditional expected-risk convergence.

## 7. Two further exact proof checks

### 7.1 P19 mixed-Haar norms must be uncentered

P19 H1 defines centered \(L_2(D)\) and \(L_\infty(D)\). H2 subsequently writes the same symbols for an independent left/right mixed block. Taken literally this is false: let \(n=m\), \(D=I_n\), and use fixed one-dimensional frames. Centered norms are zero, while \(u^TO_LO_R^Tv\) is nonzero almost surely and has variance \(1/n\). Taking the tail parameter s growing with \(s=o(n)\) directly contradicts the stated zero right-hand side and exponentially small failure probability.

The repair is

\[
L_2^{\rm mix}=\|D\|_F/\sqrt n,
\qquad L_\infty^{\rm mix}=\|D\|_{\rm op}
\]

with aspect-ratio constants for rectangular D. P19's bilinear Gaussian proof already uses these uncentered norms, and its edge resolvent estimates control them. Thus this is a precise statement/notation correction, not evidence that the intended Gaussian edge theorem fails.

### 7.2 P21 deterministic limits imply coupling convergence

P21 correctly distinguishes arbitrary distributional equivalence from pathwise coupling. However, in the deterministic-limit regime of its own corollary CL3, the recorded same-coupling gap disappears. If both \(R_A\to_P r_*\) and \(R_G\to_P r_*\), then for **any** coupling,

\[
P\{|R_A-R_G|>\epsilon\}
\le P\{|R_A-r_*|>\epsilon/2\}
+P\{|R_G-r_*|>\epsilon/2\}\to0.
\]

No independent coupling theorem is needed there. If only a common nondegenerate limiting law is known, a same-coupling difference conclusion remains a separate matter. This deduction is conditional on the deterministic-limit premises; it does not fill any unverified covariance or ghost-Lindeberg step.
