## Result and scope

A complete sufficient theorem is proved for the **actual independent radial source**, conditional on an arbitrary deterministic inverse or spectral-shell map. It has **no lower bound on rank or effective rank**.

Write \(Y=T(Lb)\), where \(L\ne0\) is deterministic, \(T\) is the Frobenius isometry onto traceless symmetric matrices, and \(b\) is P20's exact radial vector. Define
\[
 B_i=T(Le_i),\quad C=\sum_iB_i^2,\quad
 M_0=E Y=\mu_dT(L{\bf1}),\quad
 \mathcal E=E\|Y\|_F^2.
\]
If
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}(C^2)+\operatorname{tr}(M_0^4)}
 {\mathcal E^2}\le \frac Kd,
 \quad}
 \tag{A}
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{tr}\left[
 T\left(\frac{Lb}{\|Lb\|}\right)^4\right]=O_P(d^{-1}).}
 \tag{B}
\]
The constants are uniform in the deterministic map. More precisely the tail is bounded by \(C_*(K/R)^{1/7}\) at level \(R/d\). The exponent is a sufficient bound, not an optimality claim.

Two ingredients remove the archived thick-shell restriction: the exact source has Fisher information \(1+6/d\), yielding a uniform small-ball estimate for every linear image; and the fourth-cumulant contraction is bounded by \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)\), without a maximum-weight loss.

The source mean cannot simply be discarded. We construct deterministic invertible maps with centered contraction \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)=O(1/d)\), but the exact uncentered radial source gives \(d\,\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)/\|Y\|_F^4\to\infty\) in probability. For one choice this normalized fourth trace tends to \(1/2\). This demonstrates sharpness of the archived \(d^{-3/2}\) maximum inverse-weight condition when only centered contraction geometry is given and left singular directions are unrestricted.

**This is not a counterexample to an actual harmonic design. The random-matrix hypotheses needed to apply (A) to P20 remain unproved here. The fixed-\(t\) hard-edge law remains open.**

## 2. Exact radial law and Fisher information

Throughout \(d\ge16\). Let
\[
 S\sim\chi_d^2,\qquad c=\sqrt{(d+2)/2},\qquad
 b=c(1-d/S).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
The \(m\) coordinates of the P20 radial column are independent copies. Write \(b=\mu+\xi\), \(E\xi=0\), \(E\xi^2=\sigma^2\). Direct gamma integration gives
\[
 \mu=-\frac{2c}{d-2},\qquad
 \sigma^2=\frac{(d+2)d^2}{(d-2)^2(d-4)},
 \tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{E\xi^4}{\sigma^4}
 =\frac{3(d-4)(d+10)}{(d-6)(d-8)}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]
In particular \(1\le\sigma^2\le2\) and \(E\xi^4\le48\) for \(d\ge16\). To verify the formulas without a distributional import, use
\[
 E S^{-j}=\prod_{\ell=1}^j(d-2\ell)^{-1}
 \quad(d>2j)
\]
and expand the centered second and fourth powers. The displayed uniform inequalities follow by clearing the positive denominators.

Let \(f\) be the density of \(b\). Its support is \((-\infty,c)\). With \(s=dc/(c-y)\), change of variables gives
\[
 f(y)=
 \frac{s^{d/2+1}e^{-s/2}}{2^{d/2}\Gamma(d/2)\,cd},
 \qquad
 \frac{f'(y)}{f(y)}
 =\frac{s(d+2-s)}{2cd}.
 \tag{2.4}
\]
The density tends to zero at both ends. Its score has mean zero. Its Fisher information is exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I(b)
 &=\int\frac{f'(y)^2}{f(y)}\,dy\\
 &=\frac{E[S^2(d+2-S)^2]}{4c^2d^2}
 =\boxed{1+\frac6d}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2.5}
\]
Indeed \(E S^j=d(d+2)\cdots(d+2j-2)\), so the numerator is
\(2d(d+2)(d+6)\). This also proves integrability of \(f'\).

### Lemma 105-SCORE

For independent copies \(b_i\), every nonzero coefficient vector \(u\), and the density \(f_u\) of \(\sum_i u_i b_i\),
\[
 \boxed{\|f_u\|_\infty\le
 \frac{\sqrt{1+6/d}}{2\|u\|_2}.}
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Proof. Put \(X=\sum_i u_i b_i\) and \(R_i=f'(b_i)/f(b_i)\). Integration by parts against smooth test functions shows that the score of \(X\) is
\[
 E\left[\left.
 \frac{\sum_i u_iR_i}{\|u\|_2^2}\right|X\right].
\]
This identity also follows by differentiating a convolution, ignoring zero coefficients. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, independence and \(E R_i=0\) give
\[
 I(X)\le I(b)/\|u\|_2^2.
\]
Thus \(\int|f_u'|\le\sqrt{I(X)}\). An absolutely continuous probability density with integrable derivative tends to zero at both infinities. Its total variation is at least twice its maximum, proving (2.6). The density here exists because at least one coefficient is nonzero. These arguments can equivalently be read in weak-derivative form. QED.

## 3. Uniform small-ball theorem for every inverse map

Let \(L:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m\) be any nonzero deterministic map; \(n=m\) in P20. Let \(b\) have iid law (2.1). Define
\[
 S_2=\|L\|_F^2,\quad a=\mu L{\bf1},\quad
 \mathcal E=E\|Lb\|^2=\sigma^2S_2+\|a\|^2,\quad
 \lambda=\|L\|_{\rm op}^2.
\]

### Theorem 105-INVERSE-SMALL-BALL

There is an absolute constant \(C\), uniform in \(d,n,L\), such that
\[
 \boxed{\Pr\{\|Lb\|\le t\sqrt{\mathcal E}\}\le C t^{2/3}
 \quad(t>0).}
 \tag{3.1}
\]
In particular \(Lb\ne0\) almost surely and its squared norm divided by its conditional mean has an inverse that is tight uniformly over all these maps. This does not assert concentration at one or a finite negative second moment.

Proof. Put \(A=L^\top L\) and \(Q=\|L\xi+a\|^2\). Independence gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\xi^\top A\xi)
 =2\sigma^4\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
 +(E\xi^4-3\sigma^4)\sum_i A_{ii}^2
 \le C\lambda S_2.
\]
Also
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(2a^\top L\xi)
 \le C\lambda\|a\|^2.
\]
Using \(\operatorname{Var}(U+V)\le2\operatorname{Var}U+2\operatorname{Var}V\) handles any nonzero third-moment cross term. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}Q\le C\lambda\mathcal E.
 \tag{3.2}
\]
For \(0<t\le1/\sqrt2\), Chebyshev yields, with \(r=\lambda/\mathcal E>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{Q\le t^2\mathcal E\}\le C r.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
Choose unit singular vectors \(u,v\) with \(v^\top L=\sqrt\lambda\,u^\top\). Lemma 105-SCORE gives the independent second bound
\[
 \Pr\{Q\le t^2\mathcal E\}
 \le\Pr\{|u^\top b|\le t\sqrt{\mathcal E/\lambda}\}
 \le C t/\sqrt r.
 \tag{3.4}
\]
The minimum of \(Cr\) and \(Ct/\sqrt r\) is at most \(Ct^{2/3}\). Enlarging the constant covers larger \(t\). Letting \(t\downarrow0\) proves the almost-sure nonvanishing assertion. QED.

This replaces a high-effective-rank denominator argument even for one dominant inverse mode. It uses the actual radial law, including its mean.

## 4. Matrix fourth moments without a maximum-weight loss

Let \(T:\mathbb R^m\to\operatorname{Sym}_d^0\) be any Frobenius isometry, and set
\[
 B_i=T(Le_i),\qquad C=\sum_i B_i^2,\qquad
 Y_0=\sum_i\xi_iB_i,\qquad M_0=\mu\sum_iB_i,\qquad Y=Y_0+M_0.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{tr}C=S_2\) and \(\mathcal E=\sigma^2S_2+\|M_0\|_F^2\).

Exact fourth-moment pairing gives
\[
 E\operatorname{tr}(Y_0^4)
 =\sigma^4\left[
 2\operatorname{tr}(C^2)+
 \sum_{i,j}\operatorname{tr}(B_iB_jB_iB_j)\right]
 +(E\xi^4-3\sigma^4)\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(B_i^4).
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Terms with a singleton index vanish by centering, so no third-moment term occurs in (4.1).

For symmetric \(A,B\),
\[
 |\operatorname{tr}(ABAB)|\le\operatorname{tr}(A^2B^2).
 \tag{4.2}
\]
For example apply Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz to \(AB\) and \(BA\). Summing proves the absolute value of the double sum in (4.1) is at most \(\operatorname{tr}(C^2)\). Crucially,
\[
 \boxed{\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(B_i^4)\le\operatorname{tr}(C^2),}
 \tag{4.3}
\]
because each cross term \(\operatorname{tr}(B_i^2B_j^2)\) is nonnegative.

Thus \(E\operatorname{tr}(Y_0^4)\le C\operatorname{tr}(C^2)\), uniformly in all ranks and singular weights. The Schatten-4 triangle inequality and \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\) give
\[
 E\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)
 \le C\left[\operatorname{tr}(C^2)+\operatorname{tr}(M_0^4)\right].
 \tag{4.4}
\]
There is also a matching lower comparison:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)
 &\ge \operatorname{tr}\big((E Y^2)^2\big)\\
 &=\operatorname{tr}\big((\sigma^2 C+M_0^2)^2\big)\\
 &\ge \operatorname{tr}(C^2)+\operatorname{tr}(M_0^4).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.5}
\]
The first inequality is Jensen for the squared Frobenius norm of \(Y^2\); the cross trace is nonnegative and \(\sigma^2\ge1\).

For a shell decomposition \(L=VDU^\top\), with columns \(v_j\) of \(V\), \(M_j=T(v_j)\), and positive weights \(w_j=D_{jj}\),
\[
 \boxed{C=\sum_j w_j^2M_j^2.}
 \tag{4.6}
\]
Orthogonality of the columns of \(U\) proves this identity by summing over source coordinates. Unlike a bound on individual source-coordinate leverages, (4.3) does not introduce \(\rho=\max_j w_j^2/\sum_jw_j^2\).

## 5. Complete deterministic sufficient theorem

### Theorem 105-SOURCE-S4

Under the hypotheses above, put
\[
 K_L=
 \frac{d\,[\operatorname{tr}(C^2)+\operatorname{tr}(M_0^4)]}
 {\mathcal E^2}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]
Then for every \(R>0\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \Pr\left\{
 d\,\frac{\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)}{\|Y\|_F^4}>R
 \right\}
 \le \min\{1,C(K_L/R)^{1/7}\}.
 }
 \tag{5.2}
\]
Consequently \(K_L=O(1)\) implies (B), uniformly over arbitrary deterministic maps, including thin spectral shells.

Proof. For \(0<t\le1/\sqrt2\), split according to whether \(\|Y\|_F\le t\sqrt{\mathcal E}\). Theorem 105-INVERSE-SMALL-BALL and Markov applied to (4.4) give
\[
 \Pr\{d\,\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)/\|Y\|_F^4>R\}
 \le Ct^{2/3}+\frac{C K_L}{Rt^4}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]
Choose \(t\) of order \((K_L/R)^{3/14}\) when that value is small. For the complementary range enlarge the constant and use probability at most one. This proves (5.2). QED.

If \(L\) is random and independent of \(b\), the theorem holds conditionally on \(L\). Thus **the probabilistic hypothesis \(K_L=O_P(1)\) is sufficient**. No claim is made that the actual harmonic inverse satisfies it.

For the centered source \(\xi=b-\mu{\bf1}\), the identical proof has \(M_0=0\), \(\mathcal E=\sigma^2S_2\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(C^2)/S_2^2=O(1/d)
 \tag{5.4}
\]
alone is sufficient, with no effective-rank restriction. A rank-one map whose right matrix direction is itself S4-flat is an immediate thin-shell example covered by the theorem.

For the uncentered source, an easy stronger sufficient pair is
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(C^2)/S_2^2=O(1/d),\qquad
 \mu^4\operatorname{tr}(T(L{\bf1})^4)/S_2^2=O(1/d).
 \tag{5.5}
\]
Using \(\mathcal E\) in (5.1) also covers a large mean whose matrix direction is already delocalized.

The result is a probability-scale conclusion. The tail exponent in (5.2) does not establish \(E\operatorname{tr}(T(Lb/\|Lb\|)^4)=O(1/d)\). That expectation claim requires a separate argument.

## 6. Sharp source-mean obstruction

This section concerns arbitrary deterministic invertible \(H\), **not** the actual row-constrained harmonic design.

Choose
\[
 M=\frac1{\sqrt2}\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)
\]
and extend it to a Frobenius-orthonormal basis \(M_1=M,\ldots,M_m\) of \(\operatorname{Sym}_d^0\). The complete-basis identity is
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m M_j^2=(m/d)I.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
It follows by taking the usual full symmetric basis, whose sum of squares is \((d+1)I/2\), and deleting \(I/\sqrt d\).

Choose an orthogonal \(U\) with first column \({\bf1}/\sqrt m\). Let
\[
 d^{-3/2}\ll\rho_d\le d^{-1/2},\qquad
 \beta_d=\frac{1-\rho_d}{m-1}.
\]
Let \(V\) have columns \(T^{-1}(M_j)\), and set
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\rho_d},\sqrt{\beta_d},\ldots,\sqrt{\beta_d}),
 \qquad L=VDU^\top,\qquad H=UD^{-1}V^\top.
\]
All weights are positive, so \(H=L^{-1}\) is an explicit invertible deterministic matrix. Here \(S_2=1\), and
\[
 C=(\rho-\beta)M^2+\beta(m/d)I.
\]
As \(\operatorname{tr}M^4=1/2\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(C^2)
 =\frac{(\rho-\beta)^2}{2}
 +2(\rho-\beta)\beta m/d+\beta^2m^2/d
 \le 6/d
 \tag{6.2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(d\). Indeed \(\rho\ge\beta\), \(\beta m\le2\), and \(\rho\le d^{-1/2}\).

But the source mean is exactly
\[
 M_0=\mu\sqrt{m\rho}\,M=-\sqrt q\,M,\qquad
 q=\mu^2m\rho\sim d\rho.
 \tag{6.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(M_0^4)=q^2/2,\qquad
 \mathcal E=\sigma^2+q.
\]

We prove actual failure in probability, rather than just a large expected numerator. For \(u=e_1\),
\[
 E(u^\top Yu)=-\sqrt{q/2}.
\]
Since \(\sum_j (M_j)_{11}^2=1-1/d\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(u^\top Yu)
 =\sigma^2\left[\rho/2+\beta(1/2-1/d)\right]
 \le C\rho.
\]
Its variance divided by its squared mean is \(O(1/d)\). Hence
\[
 |u^\top Yu|^4=(1+o_P(1))q^2/4.
 \tag{6.4}
\]
Also \(E\|Y\|_F^2=\sigma^2+q\), so \(\|Y\|_F^2=O_P(1+q)\). Since
\(\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)\ge|u^\top Yu|^4\),
\[
 d\,\frac{\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)}{\|Y\|_F^4}
 \longrightarrow\infty\quad\hbox{in probability},
 \tag{6.5}
\]
because
\[
 \frac{dq^2}{(1+q)^2}\to\infty.
\]
For \(q\le1\), the latter is of order \(d^3\rho^2\to\infty\); for \(q\ge1\), it is at least a constant times \(d\).

At the especially transparent choice \(\rho=d^{-1/2}\), \(q\to\infty\), while \(E\|Y-M_0\|_F^2=\sigma^2=O(1)\). Thus
\[
 Y/\|Y\|_F\to -M\quad\hbox{in probability},\qquad
 \boxed{\operatorname{tr}(Y^4)/\|Y\|_F^4\to1/2.}
 \tag{6.6}
\]
With the same maps and the centered source \(\xi\), Theorem 105-SOURCE-S4 instead gives \(O_P(1/d)\).

### Meaning of sharpness

The archived generic mean estimate is
\[
 \|M_0\|_F^2
 \le \mu^2m\,\rho S_2\le C d\rho S_2.
\]
Therefore \(\rho=O(d^{-3/2})\), together with centered contraction (5.4), guarantees (5.5). The construction above fails for every asymptotic relaxation \(d^{-3/2}\ll\rho\le d^{-1/2}\). This is sharp **for a uniform sufficient implication using only centered contraction and a maximum-weight bound with unrestricted left frames**. It is not a necessary restriction for individual maps; the explicit mean-direction test (5.1) removes it.

Neither isotropy of an actual harmonic row, the harmonic row manifold, nor the iid harmonic-row distribution is imposed on this constructed \(H\). It therefore refutes an omitted-mean deterministic inference, not P20 itself.

## 7. Exact remaining gate for the actual problem

For \(L=H^{-1}\), write its inverse singular weights \(w_j=s_j(H)^{-1}\), let \(v_j\) be the right singular vectors of \(H\), and put \(M_j=T(v_j)\). The new sufficient actual-matrix gate is
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{d\left[
 \operatorname{tr}\left[\left(\sum_j s_j^{-2}M_j^2\right)^2\right]
 +\mu_d^4\operatorname{tr}\big(T(H^{-1}{\bf1})^4\big)
 \right]}
 {\left[
 \sigma_d^2\sum_j s_j^{-2}
 +\mu_d^2\|H^{-1}{\bf1}\|^2
 \right]^2}
 =O_P(1).
 }
 \tag{7.1}
\]
This condition must be established for the **actual harmonic \(H\)**. It does not follow from the scalar singular values, physical conjugation invariance, or correct second-order coefficient isotropy.

The same theorem applies to a selected bottom map \(P_{E_k}H^{-1}\). One may then combine it with the already archived \(O_P(d^{-1/4})\) normalized tail estimate and the Schatten triangle inequality. No growing family of shells may be summed without controlling the combined weighted contraction and source mean; bounded individual-shell statements alone are not a uniform summation theorem.

Even completion of (7.1) only gives the immediate inverse-normal fourth-Schatten estimate. It leaves the separate bottom tangent modes, joint bottom spectral/secular comparison, annealed graph-factor integrability, and final fixed-\(t\) transfer in (1.1).
