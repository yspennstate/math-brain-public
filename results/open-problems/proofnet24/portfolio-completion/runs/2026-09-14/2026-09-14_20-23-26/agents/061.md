## 1. Three claims and exact scope

Let the actual point coordinates be partitioned as
\(E=E_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup E_m\). At an interior block-diagonal kernel put
\[
K=\bigoplus_{a=1}^m K_a,\qquad 0<K_a<I,
\]
and decompose an arbitrary complex Hermitian direction as
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(D_1,\ldots,D_m)+B,
\qquad B_{aa}=0,\qquad B_{ba}=B_{ab}^*.
\]
All logarithms are natural. Write \(H(K)=H(P_K)\) for the entropy of the
determinantal subset law. Every block below is a set of point labels,
not a subspace obtained by arbitrarily rotating the measurement basis.

### Claim 1: exact Hessian gluing, including its radical

For arbitrary Hermitian directions \(A,C\),
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,C]
=\sum_a D^2H(K_a)[A_{aa},C_{aa}].
}
\tag{1}
\]
The analogous block sum holds for the first and third differentials.
Consequently the full Hessian is negative semidefinite at this one base
if and only if every block Hessian is negative semidefinite at its base.
Its radical, meaning the directions pairing to zero with every direction,
is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\{A:A_{aa}\in\operatorname{rad}D^2H(K_a)\text{ for all }a\};
\quad B\text{ is arbitrary}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
If all block Hessians are negative semidefinite, this is also the exact
zero-quadratic-curvature set. Without that sign hypothesis, quadratic
contributions from different blocks could cancel; no componentwise
zero-curvature criterion is asserted then.

In particular, if every connected component of the coordinate graph
\(K_{ij}\ne0\), \(i\ne j\), has at most two sites, then
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0,
\qquad D^2H(K)[A,A]=0
\Longleftrightarrow A_{aa}=0\text{ on every component}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
This includes arbitrary simultaneous changes between all components.
The two-site strictness needed for (3) follows from the already proved
two-site theorem in the session; a short proof is given in Section 5.

### Claim 2: exact pair-sum quartic coefficient and quantitative positivity

For a block atom \(x\subseteq E_a\), set
\[
q_a(x)=P_{K_a}(x),\qquad
R_a(x)=(K_a-I_{E_a\setminus x})^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every \(q_a(x)>0\), so these inverses exist. For each unordered block pair
define the real random variable and nonnegative number
\[
T_{ab}(x,y)=\operatorname{tr}
\big(R_a(x)B_{ab}R_b(y)B_{ab}^*\big),
\qquad
\kappa_{ab}=\mathbb E_{q_a\otimes q_b}T_{ab}^2,
\qquad \kappa=\sum_{a<b}\kappa_{ab}.
\tag{5}
\]
Then, for a pure cross-block line,
\[
\boxed{
H(K+tB)=H(K)-D_{\rm KL}(P_{K+tB}\Vert P_K)
=H(K)-\frac{t^4}{2}\kappa+O(t^6).
}
\tag{6}
\]
The KL identity holds at every feasible parameter, while the Taylor
expansion is local at zero. Precisely,
\[
\kappa_{ab}=0\Longleftrightarrow B_{ab}=0,
\quad \kappa=0\Longleftrightarrow B=0,
\quad H^{(4)}(0)=-12\kappa.
\tag{7}
\]
There are no cross-pair terms in \(\kappa\), even for block pairs sharing
a block. The fifth coefficient vanishes for any number of blocks. Every
fixed nonzero pure cross-block line is therefore strictly concave on a
sufficiently small interval about zero, despite zero Hessian at zero.

The coefficient has a coordinate-covariance lower bound. Define
\[
\Gamma_a=\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{K_a}}
\big((\mathbf1_{i\in X})_{i\in E_a}\big),\qquad
(W_{ab})_{ij}=|(B_{ab})_{ij}|^2,
\quad v_i=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}).
\]
Each \(\Gamma_a\) is a real positive-definite matrix. Then
\[
\boxed{
\kappa_{ab}\ge
\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_a^{-1}W_{ab}\Gamma_b^{-1}W_{ab}^{\mathsf T})
\ge
\sum_{i\in E_a,\ j\in E_b}\frac{|B_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
The first inequality is an orthogonal-projection identity with an explicit
nonnegative residual, so its equality condition is also exact: writing
\(\xi_a=(\mathbf1_{i\in X}-K_{ii})_{i\in E_a}\), equality holds exactly when
\[
T_{ab}=\xi_a^{\mathsf T}
\Gamma_a^{-1}W_{ab}\Gamma_b^{-1}\xi_b
\quad\text{on every product atom}.
\tag{9}
\]
The bound itself depends only on entry magnitudes, but the exact
\(\kappa_{ab}\) generally depends on their phases relative to the internal
block entries. Section 7 gives an exact same-magnitude counterexample to
discarding that phase information.

Internal changes are separated exactly. Set
\(K_a(t)=K_a+tD_a\), and form \(\kappa(t)\) by (4)–(5) using these current
block kernels and the fixed \(B\). Then
\[
\boxed{
H(K+tA)=\sum_a H(K_a+tD_a)-\frac{t^4}{2}\kappa(t)+O(t^6).
}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the first interaction deficit is always fourth order; moving
within the blocks can produce a fifth-order term
\(-\kappa'(0)t^5/2\). No concavity premise for the individual block
entropies is needed for (5)–(10).

### Claim 3: a precise feasible-boundary extension for pure cross lines

Now allow \(0\le K_a\le I\), still with \(K=\bigoplus_aK_a\) and
\(B_{aa}=0\). There is a nonzero feasible parameter \(t_0\) if and only if
\[
\boxed{
B\ker K=0,\qquad B\ker(I-K)=0.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Here the equivalence means existence of some nonzero feasible parameter;
when (11) holds a whole two-sided neighborhood of zero is feasible.
For any such line, (5)–(7) hold with sums over positive block atoms.
Consequently every nonzero feasible pure cross direction still has
strictly positive quartic loss and the stated local strict concavity.

This boundary statement is about entropy on the specified affine line.
It does not assert an ambient boundary Hessian, nor does it extend the
interior Hessian identity (1) to arbitrary boundary directions. No inverse
is used at a zero-probability atom, and the unrestricted boundary identity
\(\sum_{q_a>0}q_aR_a=0\) is explicitly **not** used: it can fail.

## 2. Determinant identities and the product reference

The determinantal inclusion probabilities imply the generating identity
\[
\mathbb E_K\prod_i z_i^{\mathbf1_{i\in X}}
=\det(I-K+K\operatorname{diag}(z_i)).
\tag{12}
\]
Expanding by columns gives the atom formula
\[
p_x(K)=(-1)^{|E\setminus x|}\det(K-I_{E\setminus x}).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(0<K<I\), the alternative expression
\[
p_x(K)=\det(I-K)\det\big(K(I-K)^{-1}\big)_x>0
\tag{14}
\]
shows full support. In particular (4) is legitimate.

For a block-diagonal kernel, the determinant in (13) factors over the
blocks, so
\[
P_K=\bigotimes_aP_{K_a}.
\tag{15}
\]
For every kernel, restriction of its subset law to the point set \(E_a\)
has kernel \(K_{aa}\), directly by the inclusion identities. Hence the
block marginals along \(K+tA\) are \(q_{a,t}=P_{K_a+tD_a}\). Define
\[
\mu_t=\bigotimes_aq_{a,t},\qquad P_t=P_{K+tA}.
\]
Because \(\log\mu_t(x)=\sum_a\log q_{a,t}(x_a)\), matching these marginals
gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\sum_aH(K_a+tD_a)-H(K+tA)
=D_{\rm KL}(P_t\Vert\mu_t).
}
\tag{16}
\]
This is the total dependence of the block subsets. With two blocks it
is their mutual information. In the interior it is analytic near zero.

At a fixed set of current interior block kernels, write
\[
Q(x)=\bigoplus_a(K_a-I_{E_a\setminus x_a}),\qquad
R(x)=Q(x)^{-1}=\bigoplus_aR_a(x_a).
\]
For a real scalar \(s\), (13) gives the exact density polynomial
\[
\boxed{
\frac{p_x(K+sB)}{\prod_aq_a(x_a)}
=\det(I+sR(x)B).
}
\tag{17}
\]
The same identity holds with current blocks \(K_a(t)\) and \(s=t\).
All these quantities vary analytically while the blocks are interior.

## 3. Centered matrix scores and pair/triple orthogonality

Differentiate \(\sum_xq_a(x)=1\) at \(K_a\) in an arbitrary Hermitian
direction \(V\). From (13) and Jacobi's formula,
\[
Dq_a(x)[V]=q_a(x)\operatorname{tr}(R_a(x)V).
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(\mathbb E_{q_a}R_a\,V\right)=0
\quad\text{for all Hermitian }V,
\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb E_{q_a}R_a=0.}
\tag{18}
\]
The last conclusion uses the nondegenerate real Hermitian trace pairing,
not just diagonal variations. It does not assert that individual
\(R_a\) have a fixed sign.

Put \(M=RB\). Its diagonal blocks vanish, so \(\operatorname{tr}M=0\).
The first determinant coefficients in (17) are consequently
\[
\det(I+sM)=1+s^2F_2+s^3F_3+O(s^4),
\]
\[
F_2=-\frac12\operatorname{tr}(M^2)
=-\sum_{a<b}T_{ab},
\tag{19}
\]
\[
F_3=\frac13\operatorname{tr}(M^3)
=2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{a<b<c}
\operatorname{tr}(R_aB_{ab}R_bB_{bc}R_cB_{ca}).
\tag{20}
\]
These formulas follow, for example, by expanding
\(\log\det(I+sM)=\sum_{r\ge1}(-1)^{r+1}s^r\operatorname{tr}(M^r)/r\)
near zero; they are polynomial coefficient identities. Each trace in
(19) is real because it pairs two Hermitian matrices. In (20), cyclic
rotations contribute three identical traces, and reversing a cycle gives
their complex conjugates. A length-three block walk cannot repeat a
block when \(B_{aa}=0\).

Every \(T_{ab}\) is centered in each of its two block variables separately
by (18). Every triangle term in (20) is similarly centered in each of its
three block variables. Under the independent product measure in (15):

- Two different unordered pairs have a block appearing in only one of
  them, even when they share the other block. Integrating that block
  gives \(\mathbb E[T_{ab}T_{cd}]=0\).
- A pair and a triple have at least one block appearing in the triple
  alone. Integrating it gives zero.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EF_2^2=\sum_{a<b}\mathbb ET_{ab}^2=\kappa,
\qquad \mathbb E(F_2F_3)=0.
}
\tag{21}
\]
This is the all-dimensional orthogonality argument; it does not rely on
each block being a single Bernoulli coordinate. It also explains why the
absence of a fifth coefficient survives triangles in the block graph.

## 4. KL expansion, varying blocks, and quantitative coefficient

### 4.1 Fourth order, and why there is no pure fifth order

Let \(u_s=p_{K+sB}/P_K-1\). Equation (17) gives
\(u_s=s^2F_2+s^3F_3+O(s^4)\), with \(\mathbb Eu_s=0\). On a sufficiently
small interval the alphabet is finite and \(\max_x|u_s(x)|<1\). The
convergent scalar series
\[
(1+u)\log(1+u)=u+\frac{u^2}{2}-\frac{u^3}{6}+\cdots
\]
therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\rm KL}(P_{K+sB}\Vert P_K)
&=\tfrac12\mathbb Eu_s^2+O(s^6)\\
&=\frac{s^4}{2}\mathbb EF_2^2
  +s^5\mathbb E(F_2F_3)+O(s^6)\\
&=\frac{s^4}{2}\kappa+O(s^6).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Normalization removes the linear-in-\(u\) term, and cubic powers of
\(u_s\) start at order six. Equations (16) and (22) prove (6).

There is also a uniform version: allow all the block kernels to range
over a sufficiently small closed neighborhood of their interior bases.
There are finitely many atoms, and their positive probabilities and
invertible atom matrices are uniformly bounded away from singularity.
The determinant and logarithm expansions then have uniformly bounded
remainders. Applying (22) with these current blocks \(K_a(t)\) and
\(s=t\) proves (10), including the \(O(t^6)\) remainder. In particular
\(\kappa(t)\) is analytic.

For an explicit check of its derivative, set
\[
S_a(x)=\operatorname{tr}(R_a(x)D_a),\qquad
\dot R_a(x)=-R_a(x)D_aR_a(x).
\]
Let \(\dot T_{ab}\) be the sum of the two traces in (5) obtained by
replacing respectively \(R_a,R_b\) by \(\dot R_a,\dot R_b\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\kappa'(0)=\sum_{a<b}\mathbb E\left[
(S_a+S_b)T_{ab}^2+2T_{ab}\dot T_{ab}\right].
}
\tag{23}
\]
This accounts for the changing atom weights as well as the changing
resolvents. Omitting the weight derivatives would give a wrong fifth
coefficient. The exact verifier checks (23) against the direct formal KL
series with moving internal blocks.

### 4.2 Covariance projection and strict positivity

For a point \(i\in E_a\), differentiate
\(\mathbb E_{q_a}\mathbf1_{i\in X}=K_{ii}\) in an arbitrary Hermitian
direction \(V\). Together with (18), this gives the matrix moment identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{q_a}\big[(\mathbf1_{i\in X}-K_{ii})R_a(X)\big]
=e_ie_i^*.
}
\tag{24}
\]
Consequently, for \(i\in E_a,j\in E_b\),
\[
\mathbb E[T_{ab}\xi_{a,i}\xi_{b,j}]=|B_{ij}|^2=(W_{ab})_{ij}.
\tag{25}
\]
The covariance of these product coordinates is
\[
\mathbb E[(\xi_{a,i}\xi_{b,j})(\xi_{a,k}\xi_{b,l})]
=(\Gamma_a)_{ik}(\Gamma_b)_{jl}.
\tag{26}
\]
Each \(\Gamma_a\) is positive definite: a linear combination of the
coordinate indicators with zero variance must be constant on every atom;
full support includes the empty set and every singleton, forcing all its
coefficients to be zero.

Equations (25)–(26) show that the orthogonal projection of \(T_{ab}\)
onto the span of the product coordinates is precisely
\[
T_{ab}^{\rm proj}=\xi_a^{\mathsf T}
\Gamma_a^{-1}W_{ab}\Gamma_b^{-1}\xi_b.
\]
Its squared norm is
\[
\|T_{ab}^{\rm proj}\|_2^2
=\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_a^{-1}W_{ab}\Gamma_b^{-1}W_{ab}^{\mathsf T}).
\]
The exact Pythagorean identity is
\[
\boxed{
\kappa_{ab}=
\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_a^{-1}W_{ab}\Gamma_b^{-1}W_{ab}^{\mathsf T})
+\mathbb E(T_{ab}-T_{ab}^{\rm proj})^2.
}
\tag{27}
\]
This proves the first inequality of (8) and its equality condition (9).

For the second inequality, the two-point determinantal identity gives
\[
(\Gamma_a)_{ii}=v_i>0,\qquad
(\Gamma_a)_{ik}=-|(K_a)_{ik}|^2\le0\quad(i\ne k).
\tag{28}
\]
Here is an elementary proof of the needed inverse properties. Write
\(\Gamma_a=V_a^{1/2}(I-N_a)V_a^{1/2}\), where
\(V_a=\operatorname{diag}(v_i)\), \(N_a\) is symmetric, entrywise
nonnegative, and has zero diagonal. Positive definiteness of
\(I-N_a\) gives \(\lambda_{\max}(N_a)<1\). For a symmetric nonnegative
matrix, \(|x^{\mathsf T}N_ax|\le |x|^{\mathsf T}N_a|x|\) implies
\(\|N_a\|_{\rm op}=\lambda_{\max}(N_a)\). Thus its Neumann series
converges and
\[
\Gamma_a^{-1}=V_a^{-1/2}\sum_{r\ge0}N_a^rV_a^{-1/2}.
\]
It is entrywise nonnegative, with
\((\Gamma_a^{-1})_{ii}\ge1/v_i\). The matrix \(W_{ab}\) is also
entrywise nonnegative. Expanding the trace in (27), retaining only terms
with the same two coordinate indices, and applying these diagonal bounds
proves the second inequality of (8).

If \(B_{ab}\ne0\), at least one summand in the last expression in (8)
is strictly positive. This proves all the equivalences in (7). If all
blocks are singletons, each score is the ordinary centered Bernoulli
contrast, the projection residual in (27) is zero, and (8) is equality.
This recovers 023 as a special case while (27) explains the additional
contributions from correlated blocks.

### 4.3 Strict local lines and the global maximum statement actually obtained

For fixed \(B\ne0\), analyticity and (22) give
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(K+tB)=-6\kappa t^2+O(t^4).
\tag{29}
\]
It is negative at every sufficiently small nonzero \(t\), and zero at
zero. Integrating over any nondegenerate subinterval shows that the first
derivative strictly decreases across the whole small interval; hence
entropy is strictly concave there.

For any feasible \(t\ne0\), a nonzero cross entry gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{K+tB}}
(\mathbf1_{i\in X},\mathbf1_{j\in X})=-t^2|B_{ij}|^2<0
\quad(i,j\text{ in different blocks}).
\tag{30}
\]
Thus the block subsets are not independent. The KL term in (6) is then
strictly positive, proving that zero is the unique entropy maximum on
the whole feasible pure-cross line. This maximum statement is not a
claim of concavity on the whole line.

## 5. Proof of the Hessian gluing and the small-component classification

The KL expansion in (10) starts at order four. Differentiating through
order three along any fixed Hermitian \(A\) therefore gives the sum of
the corresponding block derivatives. These derivatives are symmetric
real multilinear forms. Polarization of the directional identities gives
the mixed identities, in particular (1).

Alternatively, (17) starts at degree two in the cross matrix for every
nearby choice of diagonal blocks, so (16) starts at degree four in that
cross matrix. This also directly shows that all differentials of total
order at most three containing a cross-block argument vanish at the
block-diagonal base.

The vector space of Hermitian matrices is the direct sum of the diagonal
block spaces and the off-block space. Equation (1) is zero on the latter
and has no mixed terms between distinct diagonal blocks. It proves (2)
and the negative-semidefiniteness equivalence. For a negative-semidefinite
real symmetric bilinear form, zero quadratic value is equivalent to
membership in the radical, proving the stated equality conclusion under
that sign hypothesis.

For completeness, a connected interior two-site kernel has a negative
definite entropy Hessian. Write it as
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
with \(c\ne0\), and write \(\delta=\det D\) for a nonzero Hermitian
direction. The two-site formula in the session is
\[
H''=-\operatorname{FI}+2\delta L,
\qquad L=\log\frac{p_{10}p_{01}}{p_{00}p_{11}}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
If \(\delta<0\), this is strictly negative. If \(\delta>0\), the proved
two-site bound gives \(H''\le-8\delta<0\). If \(\delta=0\), a nonzero
Hermitian two-by-two matrix is rank one, so some diagonal entry \(D_{ii}\)
is nonzero. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the event \(i\in X\) and its
complement gives
\(\operatorname{FI}\ge D_{ii}^2/[K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})]>0\), again proving
strictness. A singleton has \(h''(p)=-1/[p(1-p)]<0\).

Apply (1) using the actual graph-component partition. Components of size
two are connected and hence satisfy the strict statement just proved;
singletons do as well. The resulting sum is zero exactly when all its
within-component directions vanish, proving (3). If a presented
two-coordinate block happens to be diagonal, it must be refined into
its two singleton components before using that formulation.

For larger blocks, (1) retains the entire unknown within-block Hessian.
It does not assume it is negative semidefinite and cannot make that
unknown part disappear by adding independent blocks.

## 6. Proof of the boundary statement and its limitations

### 6.1 Feasibility is exactly endpoint annihilation for these directions

Assume \(K+t_0B\) and \(I-K-t_0B\) are positive semidefinite for some
\(t_0\ne0\). Let \(v\) be supported in one block \(E_a\) and satisfy
\(K_av=0\). Since \(B_{aa}=0\),
\(v^*(K+t_0B)v=0\). For a positive semidefinite matrix, a vector with
zero quadratic value is in its kernel, so
\((K+t_0B)v=0\) and consequently \(Bv=0\). The same reasoning applied
to \(I-K-t_0B\) shows \(Bv=0\) for every one-block unit-eigenvalue
vector. These vectors span \(\ker K\) and \(\ker(I-K)\), proving
necessity of (11).

Conversely, let \(F_a\) be the spectral subspace of \(K_a\) for its
eigenvalues in \((0,1)\), and let \(\Pi_a\) be its orthogonal projector.
Equation (11) and Hermiticity imply
\[
B_{ab}=\Pi_a B_{ab}\Pi_b.
\tag{32}
\]
On \(F=\bigoplus_aF_a\), the restriction of \(K\) has a strictly positive
gap \(\eta\) from both zero and one, provided \(F\ne0\). For
\(|t|\|B\|_{\rm op}<\eta\), the restriction of \(K+tB\) remains a strict
contraction, while the endpoint eigenspaces are unchanged. This gives
a two-sided feasible interval. If \(F=0\), (11) forces \(B=0\), and
feasibility is immediate. This establishes the equivalence (11).

For three or more blocks, this argument only gives a local two-sided
interval; it does not claim the entire feasible parameter interval is
symmetric. A single block sign flip negates all cross entries only for
an appropriate bipartite block graph.

### 6.2 Fixed marginals force the zero atoms to stay absent

Put \(\mu=\bigotimes_aP_{K_a}\), possibly with zero atoms. If
\(\mu(x)=0\), one block atom \(x_a\) has zero marginal probability.
Every feasible \(P_{K+tB}\) has those same block marginals, so its joint
atom \(x\) is also zero. As there is a feasible open interval about zero,
its atom polynomial is identically zero. Every atom with \(\mu(x)>0\)
remains positive on a sufficiently small common interval by continuity.

Thus the support of the law is locally constant along this line, and its
entropy is analytic after omitting the identically zero atoms. The exact
KL identity (6) holds at every feasible \(t\), with zero conventions on
the fixed product support.

### 6.3 The correct compressed resolvent cancellation

For a positive block atom, (13) still makes
\(Q_a=K_a-I_{E_a\setminus x_a}\) invertible, so (17) remains valid on
the positive product support. Define
\[
M_a=\sum_{x:q_a(x)>0}q_a(x)R_a(x).
\]
The valid cancellation is
\[
\boxed{\Pi_aM_a\Pi_a=0.}
\tag{33}
\]
To prove it, take any Hermitian \(V=\Pi_aV\Pi_a\). The kernels
\(K_a+sV\) are contractions for sufficiently small signed \(s\).
Every atom that is zero at \(s=0\) is a nonnegative differentiable
function on this two-sided interval, so its first derivative is zero.
For positive atoms its derivative is \(q_a\operatorname{tr}(R_aV)\).
Differentiating normalization therefore gives
\(\operatorname{tr}(M_aV)=0\) for every such \(V\), which is (33).
This argument needs no assertion that an atom stays zero under all
variations within the spectral face.

Every cross factor satisfies (32). Hence the traces in (19)–(20) use
only \(\Pi_aR_a\Pi_a\), whose mean is zero by (33). Pair and triangle
centering, all orthogonality in (21), and the entire support-restricted
Taylor argument in (22) go through unchanged. This proves the boundary
version of the exact coefficient (5) and of (6).

### 6.4 Positivity without an invertible boundary covariance matrix

Let \(i\in E_a,j\in E_b\). The exact cross inclusion probability is
\(K_{ii}K_{jj}-t^2|B_{ij}|^2\), so comparison of its second coefficient
with (19) gives
\[
\mathbb E_\mu[F_2\xi_{a,i}\xi_{b,j}]=-|B_{ij}|^2.
\]
All pair terms other than \(T_{ab}\) vanish against this product by
their separate centering. Thus (25) remains valid on the boundary for
the contracted traces under consideration. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\kappa_{ab}\ge\frac{|B_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}
\quad\text{whenever }B_{ij}\ne0.
\tag{34}
\]
The denominator is positive in that case: if \(K_{ii}=0\) or 1, the
coordinate vector \(e_i\) belongs to an endpoint eigenspace, and (11)
forces the corresponding row of \(B\) to vanish. Hence a nonzero pair
has positive \(\kappa_{ab}\), proving (7) and the boundary local strict
concavity. No inverse of a singular covariance matrix is taken.

The small-component Hessian classification and the varying-block formula
(10) are not being extended here to arbitrary boundary directions.
The pure-cross support and endpoint arguments are precisely the extra
hypotheses used for Claim 3.

## 7. Exact examples, failures, and point-coordinate dependence

### 7.1 Two correlated blocks: same entry magnitudes, different quartics

Take
\[
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/8\\1/8&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&1/10\\1/10&2/3\end{pmatrix},
\]
and compare the two cross matrices
\[
B_{12}^{+}=\frac1{20}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_{12}^{-}=\frac1{20}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{35}
\]
All four entry magnitudes agree. Their coefficients are nevertheless
\[
\kappa_+=\frac{11022204}{18094313375},\qquad
\kappa_-=\frac{2150268}{3618862675},\qquad
\kappa_+-\kappa_-=\frac{270864}{18094313375}>0.
\tag{36}
\]
For both matrices the covariance projection bound is \(12/23875\), and
the last bound in (8) is \(9/20000\). The exact projection residuals are
respectively \(385536/3618862675\) and
\(1656816/18094313375\), both strictly positive. Thus neither dropping
the residual nor replacing (5) by the diagonal-base magnitude formula
is valid for correlated blocks.

This example also rejects termwise positivity of the later KL
coefficients. For \(B^-\), the sixth KL coefficient is
\[
-\frac{2078675868664}{2619233412101631125}<0.
\tag{37}
\]
The positive leading quartic is unaffected. With two blocks, all atom
polynomials and the entropy are even in \(t\), since the coordinate
unitary \(\operatorname{diag}(I_{E_1},-I_{E_2})\) sends \(K+tB\) to
\(K-tB\). The verifier checks their zero odd coefficients exactly.

### 7.2 Moving both internal blocks produces the predicted fifth coefficient

Keep \(K_1,K_2,B^-\) from (35) and set
\[
D_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/20&i/60\\-i/60&-1/30\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/40&1/70\\1/70&1/50\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{38}
\]
Direct formal expansion of the joint law relative to the product of its
moving block marginals gives the nonzero fifth KL coefficient
\[
[t^5]D_{\rm KL}(P_{K+tA}\Vert\mu_t)
=\frac{260625476903067}{36669267769422835750}
=\frac12\kappa'(0).
\tag{39}
\]
The verifier computes \(\kappa'(0)\) independently by (23), including
the score terms arising from the moving probabilities.

### 7.3 Three blocks, complex phases, and a nonzero seventh coefficient

Use block sizes \((2,2,1)\), with

K_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&(1+i)/12\\(1-i)/12&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
K_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&(1+2i)/15\\(1-2i)/15&3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
K_3=(2/3),

\[
B_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}1/25&(1+i)/30\\(2-i)/40&-1/35\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}(1+2i)/45\\1/30\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}1/40\\(1-i)/50\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{40}

\kappa=\frac{386408171652394313593}{647946120024462336000000}>0.
\tag{41}
\]
Some atoms have nonzero cubic coefficients. All distinct pair inner

\frac{7972387023269953738990771377}
{70401901872119289302161408000000000}\ne0.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus a multi-block pure-cross entropy line need not be even. The
pair/triple orthogonality proves the fifth cancellation, not cancellation
of every odd order. This is a non-diagonal, three-block check rather than
a repetition of the fully diagonal example in 023.

### 7.4 A feasible boundary with a non-coordinate endpoint eigenspace

Let
\[
v=\begin{pmatrix}3/5\\4/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_1=\frac25vv^*,\qquad
K_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/10\\1/10&1/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
w=\begin{pmatrix}1/20\\(1+i)/30\end{pmatrix},\quad B_{12}=vw^*.
\tag{43}
\]
The zero eigendirection in the first block is \((-4/5,3/5)\), which
\(B\) annihilates. Both signs of the line are feasible at least through
\(t=\pm1\). Four of the 16 joint atoms vanish identically. The positive
support gives
\[
\kappa=\frac{4562057}{21502869984}>0.
\tag{44}
\]
As an independent check, the first block is a Bernoulli virtual point of
parameter \(2/5\), which, when present, is placed at physical point 1 or 2
with probabilities \(9/25,16/25\). This explicit coordinate-splitting
law, checked from the minors, gives
\[
\kappa=\frac1{(2/5)(3/5)}
\mathbb E_{P_{K_2}}(w^*R_2w)^2,
\]
matching (44). This is an actual point-law calculation, not unitary
invariance of subset entropy.

The raw positive-support resolvent average in the first block is
\[
M_1=\begin{pmatrix}-32/125&24/125\\24/125&-18/125\end{pmatrix}\ne0,
\qquad v^*M_1v=0.
\tag{45}
\]
This exactly demonstrates why the compressed cancellation (33) is needed.

For an infeasible contrast, take a first block
\(P=\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
a second block \((1/2)\), and cross column \((1/10,1/10)^{\mathsf T}\).
The unit-eigenvalue space is not annihilated, and the exact obstruction is
\[
\det(I-K-tB)=-t^2/50<0\quad(t\ne0).
\tag{46}
\]
There is no feasible entropy line there to which an interior Hessian
limit can simply be assigned.

### 7.5 Why arbitrary spectral rotations are not allowed

For example, \(\operatorname{diag}(1/4,3/4)\) and
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&-1/4\\-1/4&1/2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
have the same spectrum, but their physical atom probabilities, in order
\(\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\), are respectively
\[
(3,1,9,3)/16,\qquad (3,5,5,3)/16.
\tag{47}
\]
Their entropies differ: keeping the total singleton mass fixed, strict
concavity of \(-x\log x\) makes the balanced singleton pair have larger
entropy. Thus diagonalizing each internal block and applying a
singleton theorem changes the problem. The quantities \(R_a,\Gamma_a\)
in (4) and (8) retain the required point-coordinate law.
