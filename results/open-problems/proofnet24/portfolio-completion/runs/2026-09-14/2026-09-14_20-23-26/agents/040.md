## Mathematical audit: definitions and three substantive claims

Let G be a finite simple undirected graph, let W be a nonempty list of n distinct host vertices, and let (T,r) be a rooted tree with t ≥ 2 vertices. Extra vertices of G outside W are irrelevant to the word count. For the final edge formula, W must list **all** host vertices.

A state is a pair (l,k), where l = (b,q_1,...,q_{n-1}) is a permutation of W, 1 ≤ k ≤ n−1, and bq_k is an edge. Its outer support is X = {q_1,...,q_k}. It is T-good if an injective edge-preserving map f:T→G has f(r)=b and image contained in {b}∪X. The existence of a copy, not the number of copies, is counted.

Write M for the number of all states, C(T,r) for the number of T-good states, and P=n!. The proposed bound is

\[
M\le C(T,r)+(t-2)P. \tag{1}
\]

The actual Mathlib definitions were checked at the pinned revision: Copy is an injective graph homomorphism, IsContained is Nonempty Copy, and IsTree is connected plus acyclic. In particular, extra host edges are allowed: this is the intended **non-induced** subgraph problem. The relevant source excerpts, blob hashes, and URLs are in computations/040_sources/mathlib_api_excerpts.md.

### Claim 1: first-prefix rotation proves the exact branch inequality

Fix a host root b and its marking predicate N, and consider all permutation words of the outer vertices. Let A,B,C be families of subsets satisfying:

- C(X) implies A(X);
- whenever R and X are disjoint, A(R) and B(X) imply C(R∪X).

No monotonicity of these families is required for the abstract counting lemma. Let good(A) denote the marked-prefix states whose support lies in A. Let allowed contain every marked-prefix state **and the empty cut of every word**.

There is an injection

\[
\operatorname{good}(A)\setminus\operatorname{good}(C)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{allowed}\setminus\operatorname{good}(B). \tag{2}
\]

Take a source word and cut. Decompose it as rxy, where rx is the original prefix and r is its first prefix that is marked, A-good, and not C-good. This r exists because the original prefix qualifies. Rotate to xry and put the new cut immediately after x.

If x is nonempty, its last letter is the original marked last letter, so the target is marked. If x is empty, the empty cut is allowed. If B held on x's support, disjointness of r and x and the gluing hypothesis would force C on rx, a contradiction. Therefore the output is in the required complement.

Reconstruction is exact: the output cut reveals x, and in the remaining word ry the first marked A-but-not-C prefix is r. Every shorter prefix lies inside r and failed by r's defining minimality. Consequently r and y, and hence the original word/cut, are uniquely recovered. There is no need to assume that C fails on every earlier prefix, and there is no hidden choice of a graph embedding in this reconstruction.

Since good(C)⊆good(A) and good(B)⊆allowed, (2) gives

\[
|\operatorname{good}(A)|+|\operatorname{good}(B)|
\le |\operatorname{allowed}|+|\operatorname{good}(C)|.
\]

The extra empty-cut term is exactly one outer word per host root. Summing over all choices of b gives exactly P=n!, not n·n! or (n−1)!.

Apply this to two rooted branches T_A,T_B meeting only at r. A full rooted T-copy restricts to a T_A-copy. Conversely, two branch copies on disjoint outer support sets can be glued: their roots both map to b, and injectivity of each branch copy prevents any other vertex from mapping to b. All other cross-collisions are excluded by disjoint outer supports. The separator hypothesis ensures that every edge crossing between branches is incident to r. Thus

\[
C(T_A,r)+C(T_B,r)\le M+P+C(T,r). \tag{3}
\]

This verifies the substantive mechanism of marked_word_gluing_count and rooted_word_branch_gluing_count. Dropping the empty-cut allowance would generally be wrong.

### Claim 2: reversing a cut gives the leaf-root transfer with loss at most P

This lemma does not require the smaller rooted graph S to be a tree. Let S⁺ be obtained from S by adjoining one leaf to its root r, and root S⁺ at the new leaf.

For each full word, discard only its first S-good state, if there is one. There are at most P discarded states in total. Consider any remaining S-good state at cut k, with an earlier S-good cut j<k. Write the full word

\[
b\,p\,w\,z,
\]

where w is the last vertex of the outer prefix at cut k, so |p|=k−1 and bw is an edge. Choose an S-copy supported inside b and the earlier j-prefix. Because j<k and the word has no repeated vertices, w is outside that copy. Attach w as the new leaf.

Reverse the initial block through w and reverse the suffix, obtaining

\[
w\,p^{\mathrm{rev}}\,b\,z^{\mathrm{rev}},
\]

with the same outer cut k. The new root is w. The new prefix ends at b, which is adjacent to w by symmetry. Its support contains the old copy and the new leaf, so it is S⁺-good with the required leaf root.

The map depends only on (word,k), not on the earlier cut or a choice of embedding. Reversing the same two blocks again recovers the exact input. Hence it is injective on the retained states and

\[
C(S,r)\le C(S^+,\text{new leaf})+P. \tag{4}
\]

The word being repetition-free is essential to ensuring w is not already in the selected copy. The prior state is essential as well: the first S-good prefix may use its final vertex. This is precisely why the proof loses at most one state per full word. Merely declaring a leaf attachment without this first-state loss would leave a genuine gap, but the archive explicitly handles it.

### Claim 3: the induction, numerical constant, and strict edge conclusion

For t=2, every marked state supports the two-vertex rooted tree, so C(T,r)=M. Now let t≥3.

If r is a leaf, delete r and root the remaining (t−1)-vertex tree S at its former neighbor. By induction and (4),

\[
M\le C(S)+(t-3)P
 \le C(T,r)+(t-2)P.
\]

If r is not a leaf, split the tree at r into two smaller trees of orders a,b≥2 with a+b=t+1. Such a split exists by taking one component across an incident edge together with r, and its complement together with r; a second incident edge ensures both are smaller than T. The branches share exactly r. Applying induction to each branch and then (3),

\[
2M
\le C(T_A,r)+C(T_B,r)+(a+b-4)P
\le M+C(T,r)+(t-2)P,
\]

which yields (1). The bounds a,b≥2 are necessary for the natural-number subtraction identities in the Lean induction; they are proved explicitly by tree_root_partition.

If W lists all n>0 host vertices, each word beginning at b has exactly deg_G(b) marked states, and there are (n−1)! words beginning at b. Therefore

\[
M=(n-1)!\sum_b\deg_G(b)=2e(G)(n-1)!.
\]

If G has no copy of T then C(T,r)=0 for every r. Substituting into (1) and using n!=n(n−1)! with positive factorial gives

\[
2e(G)\le (t-2)n. \tag{5}
\]

This covers arbitrary t≥2 and n>0; the tree-free lemma does not require n≥t. The named benchmark's k=0 singleton-tree case is separately and correctly handled by choosing any host vertex.

The constant t−2 is sharp even in (1): for G=K_n and any t-vertex tree with 2≤t≤n, a state qualifies precisely when its prefix including the root has at least t vertices. Thus C(T,r)=(n−t+1)n!, while M=(n−1)n!, and (1) is equality. For the tree-free edge bound, disjoint unions of K_{t−1} attain (5) when t−1 divides n.

Finally, the benchmark writes t=k+1 and assumes

\[
e(G)\ge (k-1)n/2+1.
\]

The classical strict threshold is e(G)>(k−1)n/2. If (k−1)n is odd, the benchmark excludes one allowed integer edge count: for k=4,n=5, the strict threshold permits e=8 whereas the benchmark requires e≥9. This is a comparison-of-hypotheses issue, not a flaw in (5). Contraposition of (5) gives the exact classical threshold directly.

The new Verify.lean makes this last step explicit with the natural-number hypothesis

\[
(t-2)n<2e(G),
\]

which is exactly the strict rational threshold. Its proof invokes tree_free_edge_bound and obtains a contradiction by order; it assumes no additional density lemma. This wrapper is mathematically immediate but has not been compiled in this run.
