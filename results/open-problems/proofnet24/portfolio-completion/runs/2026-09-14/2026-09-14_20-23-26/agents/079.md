1. **The mixed derivative and compression identity are correct.**

   Write
   \[
   F_f(K)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}c_S\det K_S,\qquad
   q_f(K;u,v)=D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*].
   \]
   Here \(q_f\) is exactly the coefficient of \(st\) in
   \(F_f(K+suu^*+tvv^*)\). There is no factor of two in this identification.

   For a fixed \(S\), set \(V_S=[u_S\ v_S]\). If its rank is less than two, the coefficient vanishes because the two updates occupy one rank-one direction.

   Otherwise choose an orthonormal basis in the coordinate space \(\mathbb C^S\) with its first two vectors spanning \(\operatorname{range}V_S\). Both updates then occupy the leading \(2\times2\) block. A term containing both update parameters uses those two rows and columns, leaving precisely the determinant of the complementary compression. Therefore
   \[
   [st]\det(K_S+s u_Su_S^*+t v_Sv_S^*)
   =
   \det(V_S^*V_S)\det(K_S|_{\operatorname{range}(V_S)^\perp}).
   \]
   This works for singular or indefinite \(K_S\); no inverse or Schur-complement assumption is hidden here.

   Every vector in that perpendicular space, extended by zero outside \(S\), is orthogonal to both original vectors \(u,v\). Consequently, if \(R=P_{\operatorname{span}(u,v)^\perp}\), its compressed quadratic form is unchanged by replacing \(K\) with \(RKR\). Summing over \(S\) proves
   \[
   q_f(K;u,v)=q_f(RKR;u,v).
   \]

   This reasoning never rotates the underlying coordinate set or the values of \(f\).

2. **The orthonormalization factor is correct.**

   If \([u\ v]=[e_1\ e_2]B\) for an orthonormal basis of their plane, every relevant Gram determinant acquires the same factor
   \[
   |\det B|^2=\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-|u^*v|^2.
   \]
   The restricted perpendicular spaces are unchanged. Thus
   \[
   q_f(K;u,v)=
   \bigl(\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-|u^*v|^2\bigr)q_f(K;e_1,e_2).
   \]
   Dependent vectors give zero. This also handles zero vectors.

3. **The spectral interpolation is a genuine convex combination.**

   With \(u,v\) orthonormal, write
   \[
   C=RKR=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_jw_jw_j^*,\qquad m=n-2.
   \]
   Since \(0\le K\le I\), the eigenvalues of this compression on \(U^\perp\) lie in \([0,1]\).

   A determinant is affine in each rank-one update parameter. Extracting its \(st\) coefficient preserves that separate affinity. Consequently,
   \[
   q_f(C;u,v)=
   \sum_{J\subseteq[m]}
   \prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j
   \prod_{j\notin J}(1-\lambda_j)
   q_f(P_{W_J};u,v).
   \]
   All weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Zero and unit eigenvalues cause no difficulty.

   Separate affinity is sufficient; joint affinity is neither assumed nor needed.

4. **The rank-zero and rank-one projection arguments are complete.**

   For \(C=0\), only size-two principal determinants contribute:
   \[
   q_f(0;u,v)=\sum_{i<j}
   |u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2c_{ij}.
   \]
   These squared minors are exactly the probabilities of the rank-two projection DPP on \(U\). Supermodularity gives \(c_{ij}\ge0\).

   For \(C=ww^*\), with \(w\perp U\) unit, only size-two and size-three determinants contribute. Cauchy–Binet gives
   \[
   q_f(ww^*;u,v)
   =\mathbb E_{\mu_U}c_Y+
     \mathbb E_{\mu_{U\oplus[w]}}c_Z.
   \]
   There are no multiplicity factors: each size-three determinant has coefficient
   \(\lvert\det[u\ v\ w]_S\rvert^2\).

   The established coupling supplies \(Y\subseteq Z\), with \(|Y|=2\) and \(|Z|=3\). For \(Y=\{i,j\}\), \(Z=\{i,j,k\}\),
   \[
   c_Y+c_Z=c_{ij}+c_{ijk}
   =\Delta_i\Delta_jf(\{k\})\ge0.
   \]
   Taking expectations proves the claim in every ambient dimension.

   No monotonicity of the Möbius coefficients is required. No simultaneous coupling of more than two laws is used.

5. **Complement duality gives exactly the dimension-five cutoff.**

   Complementation preserves supermodularity because
   \[
   \Delta_i\Delta_j f^c(T)
   =
   \Delta_i\Delta_j f\bigl(E\setminus(T\cup\{i,j\})\bigr).
   \]
   Moreover,
   \[
   F_f(K)=F_{f^c}(I-K),
   \qquad
   q_f(K;u,v)=q_{f^c}(I-K;u,v).
   \]
   The two derivative signs cancel.

   For a projection onto \(W\subseteq U^\perp\), compression of \(I-P_W\) to \(U^\perp\) is
   \[
   R-P_W=P_{U^\perp\cap W^\perp}.
   \]
   Its rank is \(m-r\), where \(r=\dim W\). Thus every vertex reduces to rank \(\min(r,m-r)\).

   When \(n\le5\), \(m\le3\), so this minimum is at most one. This proves
   \[
   \boxed{q_f(K;u,v)\ge0}
   \]
   for every supermodular real \(f\), every positive Hermitian contraction, and arbitrary vectors \(u,v\).

   Boundary differentiation of the complement identity is legitimate: both sides are polynomials in the real coordinates of a Hermitian matrix, agreeing on the open set \(0<K<I\). Their polynomial identity therefore holds at projections too.

6. **The entropy argument has the correct supermodularity, sign, and constant.**

   At an interior kernel every atom is positive. With
   \[
   L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0,\qquad
   p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S,
   \]
   the positive-definite Schur complement on two added coordinates proves
   \[
   p_{T\cup\{i,j\}}p_T
   \le p_{T\cup\{i\}}p_{T\cup\{j\}}.
   \]
   Hence \(f(S)=-\log p_S\) is supermodular.

   Freeze this table at the chosen kernel. Along any Hermitian direction \(A\), direct differentiation of entropy and \(\sum_Sp_S''=0\) give
   \[
   D^2H(K)[A,A]
   =-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}
     +D^2F_f(K)[A,A].
   \]
   The sign before the frozen-expectation derivative is positive.

   Principal determinants, and therefore \(F_f\), have zero pure rank-one second derivatives. For
   \(A=\lambda uu^*-\mu vv^*\),
   \[
   D^2F_f(K)[A,A]=-2\lambda\mu q_f(K;u,v).
   \]
   Thus
   \[
   \boxed{
   D^2H(K)[A,A]
   =-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}
    -2\lambda\mu q_f(K;u,v)\le0.
   }
   \]
   The factor is \(2\), not \(1\) or \(4\). The function \(f\) is refrozen at each base point; its own derivatives are not included in the auxiliary derivative.

   A direct two-coordinate check agrees: for \(u=e_1,v=e_2\), \(q_f\) is the nonnegative log odds ratio
   \(\log(p_{\{1\}}p_{\{2\}}/(p_\varnothing p_{\{1,2\}}))\), and the mixed entropy contribution is \(-2\lambda\mu q_f\).

7. **The finite-segment and boundary conclusions follow.**

   Atom probabilities are polynomials, and \(-p\log p\) extends continuously at zero. Passing to the limit proves the closed-segment claim. No differentiability of entropy at a boundary kernel is asserted.

**Additional proved corollary: the rank/corank-one compression condition also gives entropy concavity in arbitrary dimension.**

Agent019 proves arbitrary-dimensional mixed-derivative positivity when the compression has rank or corank at most one. Its direct interior entropy argument does not automatically transfer that statement to boundary kernels: regularization by \(\varepsilon I\) destroys the compression-rank condition. The following estimate supplies that missing extension.

Let \(K_t=K_0+tA\), \(0\le t\le1\), be a feasible segment in any finite dimension, with
\[
A=\lambda uu^*-\mu vv^*,\qquad \lambda,\mu>0,
\]
and orthonormal \(u,v\). Set \(R=P_{\operatorname{span}(u,v)^\perp}\). Its compression
\[
C=RK_tR
\]
is independent of \(t\). If
\[
\min\{\operatorname{rank}C,\operatorname{rank}(R-C)\}\le1,
\]
then \(t\mapsto H(K_t)\) is concave.

To prove this, first suppose \(\operatorname{rank}C\le1\), and regularize:
\[
K_t^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K_t+\varepsilon I,\qquad
h_\varepsilon(t)=H(K_t^\varepsilon).
\]
Its spectrum lies in \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\). The atom formula and
\(L_\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon I\) yield the convenient uniform bound
\[
p_S(K_t^\varepsilon)
\ge\varepsilon^n\varepsilon^{|S|}
\ge\varepsilon^{2n}.
\]
Thus its frozen log table satisfies
\[
0\le f_{t,\varepsilon}(S)\le M_\varepsilon,
\qquad M_\varepsilon=2n\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]

On \(U^\perp\), the regularized compression has at most one eigenvalue different from \(\varepsilon\). In spectral interpolation, projection vertices of rank at least two therefore have total weight
\[
\beta_\varepsilon\le (n-3)_+\varepsilon.
\]
Indeed, such a vertex must select at least one of the \(n-3\) other eigenvectors, each selected with probability \(\varepsilon\). For \(n\le3\), no bad vertex occurs.

Rank-zero and rank-one vertices have nonnegative \(q\), by the already audited arbitrary-dimensional lemmas. For any remaining vertex, separate affinity gives
\[
q_f(P_W;u,v)
=
F_f(P_W+P_U)-F_f(P_W+uu^*)-F_f(P_W+vv^*)+F_f(P_W).
\]
All four kernels are projections. Since \(0\le f\le M_\varepsilon\), each expectation lies in that interval, so
\[
q_f(P_W;u,v)\ge-2M_\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently,
\[
q_{f_{t,\varepsilon}}(K_t^\varepsilon;u,v)
\ge-4n(n-3)_+\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]

The entropy Hessian formula now gives uniformly in \(t\)
\[
h_\varepsilon''(t)\le
8n(n-3)_+\lambda\mu\,
\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)
=:\delta_\varepsilon,
\]
where we used \((1-2\varepsilon)^2\le1\) and discarded the nonpositive Fisher term.

Integrating this upper bound,
\[
h_\varepsilon(t)\ge
(1-t)h_\varepsilon(0)+t h_\varepsilon(1)
-\frac{\delta_\varepsilon}{2}t(1-t).
\]
Since \(\delta_\varepsilon\to0\), entropy continuity proves the desired concavity. The corank-one case follows by replacing \(K_t\) with \(I-K_t\), which preserves entropy and replaces \(C\) with \(R-C\). Rank-one directions are already covered in arbitrary dimension.
