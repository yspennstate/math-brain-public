## Model, existence, and fixed-point bounds

Write
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^4:\textstyle\sum_r x_r=1\}.
\]
For every ordered triple of parent types, including repetitions, let
\[
P_{abc,r}\geq0,\qquad \sum_{r=1}^4P_{abc,r}=1,
\]
with symmetry in \(a,b,c\), and require
\[
P_{abc,r}=0\quad\text{if }r\in\{a,b,c\}.
\]
The offspring map is
\[
V_r(x)=\sum_{a,b,c=1}^4P_{abc,r}x_ax_bx_c.
\]
This is full independent homogeneous parent sampling with strict exclusion of every parental type. The audit uses these exact assumptions throughout. Cubic rows always have at least one missing type, so the class is nonempty; choosing uniformly among the missing types is one admissible construction.

Nonnegative coefficients and full row sums imply
\[
V(x)\geq0,\qquad \sum_rV_r(x)=(\sum_r x_r)^3=1.
\]
Thus \(V\) continuously maps the compact simplex to itself, and Brouwer gives a fixed point.

Strict exclusion implies that \(V_r\) omits \(x_r\). Since every coefficient is at most one,
\[
0\leq V_r(x)\leq\Big(\sum_{s\ne r}x_s\Big)^3=(1-x_r)^3.
\]
The function \(g(s)=(1-s)^3-s\) has derivative
\[
g'(s)=-3(1-s)^2-1<0.
\]
Its endpoint values are \(g(1/4)=11/64>0\) and \(g(1/3)=-1/27<0\). Hence its unique zero \(c\) satisfies \(1/4<c<1/3\). At a fixed point, \(x_r\leq(1-x_r)^3\), so \(x_r\leq c\). Summing the other three caps yields
\[
1-3c\leq x_r\leq c,\qquad 1-3c>0.
\]
Every fixed point is therefore interior. The equivalent cubic
\[
c^3-3c^2+4c-1=0
\]
has derivative \(3(c-1)^2+1>0\) on the whole real line, so the source's “unique real root” wording is also correct. The proof does not assert that the coordinate bounds are optimal over fixed points.

## Derivative and exact secant identity

For \(m\in\Delta_3\), set
\[
A(m)_{rj}=\sum_{a,b}P_{abj,r}m_am_b.
\]
Every entry is nonnegative. For each column \(j\),
\[
\sum_r A(m)_{rj}=\sum_{a,b}m_am_b=1.
\]
Strict exclusion gives \(A(m)_{jj}=0\). Differentiating each of the three parent slots and using symmetry gives the ambient derivative
\[
DV(m)=3A(m).
\]
In particular, \(DV(m)\) has column sums three, not one. It nevertheless preserves the zero-sum tangent space
\[
E=\{z\in\mathbb R^4:\sum_rz_r=0\}.
\]
For \(m_t=y+t(x-y)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
V(x)-V(y)=3\int_0^1 A(m_t)(x-y)\,dt.
\]
This is an exact integrated derivative formula. No midpoint replacement or quadratic secant identity is used. The polynomial derivative and identity remain valid at boundary points.

## Exact two-pair transfer maximum

Relabel the groups and selected transfer as
\[
G=\{1,2\},\quad H=\{3,4\},\quad e_1-e_3,
\]
and put \(m=(a,b,u,v)\) to avoid confusing a coordinate with the cap \(c\). Define
\[
p_{rst}=\sum_{\ell\in G}P_{rst,\ell}.
\]
A parent multiset containing both 1 and 2 forces \(p=0\); one containing both 3 and 4 forces \(p=1\). No cubic multiset can contain both pairs. The remaining twelve multisets each admit an offspring in each group, so their group probability can be selected independently in \([0,1]\). Specifying the row on its parent multiset and then extending to permutations preserves the required symmetry.

The independent certificate generates parent rows as multiplicity vectors \(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)\), \(\sum n_r=3\). A grouped cubic row has monomial coefficient \(3!/\prod n_r!\). Its contribution to column \(j\) of \(A=DV/3\) is therefore

Returning to arbitrary labels gives exactly

The maximum is over admissible full symmetric cubic tensors at a fixed \(m\). It may be negative; no unjustified nonnegative truncation is present.

## Uniqueness by sign transport

Suppose two distinct fixed points \(x,y\) exist and let \(z=x-y\). The vector \(z\) is nonzero and zero-sum. If its only positive coordinate were \(z_i\), then \(x_r\leq y_r\) for every \(r\ne i\). The nonnegative polynomial \(V_i\), which omits its own coordinate, is nondecreasing in all its other coordinates. Thus
\[
x_i=V_i(x)\leq V_i(y)=y_i,
\]
a contradiction. Apply the same argument with \(x,y\) reversed to exclude exactly one negative coordinate. In four dimensions there must therefore be exactly two positive and two negative coordinates; a zero coordinate cannot remain.

Let \(G=\{i:z_i>0\}\), \(H=\{j:z_j<0\}\), and \(h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i>0\). With
\[
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2},
\]
one has \(\theta_{ij}\geq0\), \(\sum_{ij}\theta_{ij}=1\), and
\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\]
Both fixed points lie in \(K_c\), which is convex, so every secant point \(m_t\) also lies there. Integrating the transfer bound gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h
&=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}(V(x)-V(y))\\
&=3h\int_0^1\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}
\,\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m_t)(e_i-e_j)\,dt\\
&\leq\frac23h<h.
\end{aligned}
\]
The stronger constant \(6c(4c-1)\) could also be used, but \(2/3\) suffices. This is a contradiction. Together with Brouwer and the coordinate cap, it proves exactly one fixed point, and that fixed point is interior. No positive lower bound on permitted tensor coefficients is required.

## Averaged algorithm and error certificate

Define
\[
T=\frac7{10}I+\frac3{10}V.
\]
It maps the simplex to itself, and \(T(x)=x\) is equivalent to \(V(x)=x\). For a coordinate \(s=x_r\),
\[
(Tx)_r\leq f(s)=\frac7{10}s+\frac3{10}(1-s)^3.
\]
Here
\[
f''(s)=\frac95(1-s)\geq0\quad(0\leq s\leq1).
\]
Thus if all current coordinates lie in \([0,u]\), all next coordinates are at most \(\max\{f(0),f(u)\}\). The endpoints
\[
f(0)=\frac3{10},\qquad f(1/3)=\frac{29}{90}<\frac13
\]
prove \(T(K_{1/3})\subseteq K_{1/3}\).

For any real matrix \(B\) with equal column sums, every \(B(e_i-e_j)\) is zero-sum. Transport decomposition of an arbitrary zero-sum vector and the identity
\[
\frac12\|w\|_1=\max_{S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}}\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}w
\quad(\sum_rw_r=0)
\]
give
\[
\|B|_E\|_1
=\max_{i\ne j}\frac12\|B(e_i-e_j)\|_1
=\max_{S,i\ne j}\mathbf1_S^{\mathsf T}B(e_i-e_j).
\]
The reverse inequality follows by testing the transfer \(e_i-e_j\), whose \(\ell^1\) norm is two. Positivity of \(B\) is not needed for this identity; equal column sums suffice.

At a point of \(K_{1/3}\),
\[
B=DT=\frac7{10}I+\frac9{10}A.
\]
Its column sums are \(8/5\). The source correctly applies the equal-column-sum identity rather than treating this ambient derivative as a stochastic matrix. The exhaustive cut cases are:

| Cut and transfer configuration | Upper bound |
| --- | --- |
| Empty or full cut | \(0\) |
| Both selected indices in the cut, or both outside it | \(9/10\) |
| Source index outside, subtracted index inside | \(-7/10+9/10=1/5\) |
| Source inside, subtracted index outside, singleton cut | \(7/10\) |
| Source inside, subtracted index outside, three-element cut | \(7/10\) |
| Source inside, subtracted index outside, two-element cut | \(7/10+(9/10)(2/9)=9/10\) |

For the singleton cut \(\{i\}\), the \(A\)-difference is \(-A_{ij}\leq0\), using the zero diagonal. For a three-element cut with complement \(\{j\}\), the difference is \(-A_{ji}\leq0\), using equal column sums and the zero diagonal. For a two-element forward cut, the sharp transfer estimate applies. The remaining bounds use that a group probability under a stochastic column lies between zero and one.

Therefore \(\|DT(m)|_E\|_1\leq9/10\) on the entire cap. Integrating the derivative along the segment between two points of the convex cap yields
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|_1\leq\frac9{10}\|x-y\|_1
\qquad(x,y\in K_{1/3}).
\]
This conclusion includes the boundary of the cap.

Exact endpoint arithmetic validates all five entry steps:

Every next cap is at least \(3/10\) and at least its listed endpoint. Hence
\[
T^5(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_{1/3}.
\]
The chain consists of certified upper bounds; it does not claim equality of successive worst-case caps or minimality of five steps.

Let \(x^*\) denote the unique fixed point. The simplex has \(\ell^1\) diameter two, so for every initial point and every \(n\geq5\),
\[
\|T^n(x)-x^*\|_1\leq2(9/10)^{n-5}.
\]
For any \(x\in K_{1/3}\), the contraction and triangle inequality give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|x-x^*\|_1
&\leq\|x-Tx\|_1+\|Tx-Tx^*\|_1\\
&\leq\|x-Tx\|_1+\frac9{10}\|x-x^*\|_1,
\end{aligned}
\]
and therefore
\[
\|x-x^*\|_1\leq10\|Tx-x\|_1=3\|Vx-x\|_1.
\]
The cap condition on this residual certificate is essential to this proof and is correctly stated in the source. These results certify the averaged map \(T\). They make no claim of convergence or stability for the raw iteration of \(V\), and no optimal-rate claim.

## Positive real eigenvalues and determinant

At \(x^*\), the tangent operator \(L=DV(x^*)|_E\) is real. A positive real eigenvalue \(\rho\) has a nonzero real eigenvector \(z\in E\). The full matrix \(DV=3A\) is nonnegative with zero diagonal. If \(z\) had just one positive coordinate \(z_i\), then
\[
(DV\,z)_i=\sum_{r\ne i}(DV)_{ir}z_r\leq0,
\]
contradicting \((DV\,z)_i=\rho z_i>0\). Applying the same reasoning to \(-z\) excludes a single negative coordinate. Its signs must be exactly two positive and two negative. The same transport decomposition, now applied to \(DV\,z=\rho z\) at \(x^*\in K_c\), gives
\[
\rho\leq3\,[2c(4c-1)]=6c(4c-1).
\]
Since \(6q(4q-1)\) has derivative \(48q-6>0\) on \([1/4,1/3]\),
\[
6c(4c-1)<6(1/3)(1/3)=\frac23.
\]

For \(0\leq s\leq1\), a zero of \(\det(I-sL)\) at \(s>0\) would imply a positive real eigenvalue \(1/s\geq1\), which is impossible. The determinant is a continuous real function of \(s\) and equals one at \(s=0\). Thus
\[
\det(I-L)>0.
\]
This argument requires neither diagonalizability nor information about the moduli of negative or nonreal eigenvalues. It establishes nonsingularity and positive determinant, not raw-iteration stability.

No optional rational-coefficient field-degree extension is needed for this verdict and none is asserted here.

## Higher degrees and scope of the classification

For full sampling with \(d\geq4\), the model requires a row whose parent tuple contains all four types. At degree four, take \((1,2,3,4)\); at larger degrees append repetitions of type 1. Strict parent exclusion forces every possible offspring probability in that row to zero, contradicting row sum one. Thus the full four-type strict class is empty for every \(d\geq4\).

This conclusion does not classify a model that removes parent tuples, restricts sampling support, weakens row stochasticity, changes exclusion, or renormalizes the offspring law. Those are different definitions.

The degree-one comparison is also correct: the parent-excluding stochastic map
\[
V(x)=(x_2,x_1,x_4,x_3)
\]
fixes every \((t,t,1/2-t,1/2-t)\), \(0\leq t\leq1/2\). The degree-two row of the source's comparison table cites the separate existing quadratic theorem; it is not an input to any part of the cubic argument, and that separate theorem is outside this bounded audit. No literature novelty claim is evaluated or made.
