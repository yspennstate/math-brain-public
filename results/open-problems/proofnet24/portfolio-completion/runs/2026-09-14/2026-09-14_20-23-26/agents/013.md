## Findings

The previously stated coefficient estimate

\[
\|x^*(P)-x^*(Q)\|_1\le(\sqrt5+2)\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon=\max_{i,j}\sum_k|P_{ij,k}-Q_{ij,k}|,
\]

is correct without any positivity or interior assumption. The same sign-cut
argument, retaining the parent pairs whose group offspring probability is
forced, gives the strictly smaller universal constant

\[
\boxed{\|x^*(P)-x^*(Q)\|_1
       \le\min\{2,(2\sqrt5-1)\varepsilon\}.}
\tag{A}
\]

No optimality of this improved constant is claimed.

For an arbitrary approximation \(x\in K_q\), where
\(c=(3-\sqrt5)/2\), \(c\le q<1/2\), and
\(K_q=\{x\in\Delta_3:\max_i x_i\le q\}\), the residual estimate improves to

\[
\boxed{\|x-x^*\|_1
 \le\frac{\|V(x)-x\|_1}{1-c-\|x\|_\infty}
 \le\frac{\|V(x)-x\|_1}{1-c-q}.}
\tag{B}
\]

This uses the coordinate bound on the true fixed point, rather than assigning
both endpoints the looser cap \(q\). For the averaged map
\(T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V\), its residual must instead be multiplied
by \(1/[\lambda(1-c-\|x\|_\infty)]\).

A residual-only cap also certifies arbitrary simplex approximations, even if
membership in a prescribed \(K_q\) was not assumed. The exact admissible range
for that certificate is derived below. The proofs do not depend on votes,
numerical experiments, or the separate averaged-map contraction proof.

## Hypotheses and preliminary facts

Let

\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\},
\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]

with

\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad
\sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,\qquad
P_{ij,k}=0\quad\hbox{if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1}
\]

These conditions include diagonal parent pairs \(i=j\), and allow every
otherwise admissible coefficient to vanish. In particular, “strictly
non-Volterra” in this report refers to the prohibition in (1), not to strict
positivity of allowed coefficients.

The map preserves the simplex and is continuous, so Brouwer's fixed-point
theorem gives at least one fixed point. Every fixed point \(y\) obeys

\[
y_k=V_k(y)\le(1-y_k)^2,
\qquad y_k\le c:=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}<\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]

The root calculation uses \(0\le y_k\le1\); the other root of
\(s^2-3s+1=0\) exceeds one. Below we also obtain uniqueness directly from a
residual inequality, so all uses of \(x^*\) are justified independently of the
upstream uniqueness theorem.

For \(m\in\Delta_3\), put

\[
A_P(m)_{rj}=\sum_aP_{aj,r}m_a.
\]

The matrix is nonnegative, column-stochastic, and has zero diagonal. If
\(m=(x+y)/2\), coefficient symmetry gives the exact identity

\[
V(x)-V(y)=2A_P(m)(x-y).
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed \(x_ax_b-y_ay_b=m_a(x_b-y_b)+(x_a-y_a)m_b\), and the two resulting
quadratic sums coincide by \(P_{ab,r}=P_{ba,r}\).

## Claim 1: a sign-cut lower bound for the fixed-point equation

**Claim.** Let \(x,y\in\Delta_3\), \(m=(x+y)/2\), and
\(M=\max_i m_i<1/2\). Define \(F(x)=x-V(x)\). Then

\[
\boxed{\|F(x)-F(y)\|_1\ge(1-2M)\|x-y\|_1.}
\tag{4}
\]

If \(x-y\) has exactly one positive coordinate or exactly one negative
coordinate, the multiplier on the right can be replaced by one.

**Proof.** There is nothing to prove if \(x=y\). Otherwise set

\[
z=x-y,\qquad G=\{i:z_i>0\},\qquad
h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=\frac12\|z\|_1>0.
\]

We first prove

\[
\sum_{r\in G}(V_r(x)-V_r(y))\le2Mh.
\tag{5}
\]

If \(|G|=1\), say \(G=\{i\}\), all coordinates other than \(i\) are
nonincreasing from \(y\) to \(x\). Since \(V_i\) is a polynomial with
nonnegative coefficients in those other coordinates, the left side of (5) is
nonpositive. If \(|G|=3\), its complement is a single negative coordinate
\(j\). All inputs of \(V_j\) are nondecreasing from \(y\) to \(x\), so
\(V_j(x)-V_j(y)\ge0\). Both \(V(x)\) and \(V(y)\) sum to one, giving the
same nonpositive bound for the group \(G\).

For \(|G|=2\), let \(H=G^c\). Zero coordinates of \(z\) may lie in \(H\);
they cause no problem. For \(i\in G,j\in H\), let \(k\) and \(\ell\) be the
other members of \(G\) and \(H\), respectively, and set

\[
p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}.
\]

Here \(0\le p_{ab}\le1\), \(p_{ab}=p_{ba}\), \(p_{ki}=0\), and
\(p_{\ell j}=1\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A_P(m)(e_i-e_j)
 &=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
   -m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
 &\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}
 \le\max(m_i,m_j)\le M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Writing

\[
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}\quad(i\in G,j\in H),
\]

we have \(\theta_{ij}\ge0\), \(\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}=1\), and
\(z=h\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j)\). Equations (3) and (6) prove (5).

It follows that

\[
\sum_{r\in G}(F_r(x)-F_r(y))\ge(1-2M)h.
\]

Every \(F(x)\) has coordinate sum zero. For any zero-sum vector \(w\) and
any subset \(G\),
\(\|w\|_1\ge2|\sum_{r\in G}w_r|\). Applying this fact proves (4).
The singleton-sign cases used an upper bound of zero in (5), giving multiplier
one. The same conclusion when there is one negative coordinate and additional
zero coordinates follows by interchanging \(x\) and \(y\). \(\square\)

If \(x,y\) are both fixed points, (2) gives \(M\le c<1/2\), while
\(F(x)-F(y)=0\). Thus (4) proves \(x=y\). In conjunction with Brouwer, this
establishes the unique fixed point \(x^*\) under exactly (1).

## Claim 2: computable residual certificates, including the averaging factor

Let \(x\in\Delta_3\), set \(s=\max_i x_i\), and assume
\(s<1-c\). Then

\[
M=\max_i\frac{x_i+x_i^*}{2}\le\frac{s+c}{2}<\frac12.
\]

Apply (4) with \(y=x^*\) to obtain the following entirely computable bound:

\[
\boxed{\|x-x^*\|_1
 \le\min\left\{2,\frac{R}{1-c-s}\right\},
 \qquad R=\|x-V(x)\|_1.}
\tag{7}
\]

The minimum with two is the diameter bound for the simplex. This proves (B)
for every \(x\in K_q\), since \(q<1/2<1-c\). In particular,

\[
\|x-x^*\|_1\le\frac{R}{1-c-q}
              \le\frac{R}{1-2q},\qquad x\in K_q.
\tag{8}
\]

The second inequality uses \(q\ge c\). It is strict at the level of constants
when \(q>c\). For \(q=2/5\), the first uniform multiplier is
\(1/(3/5-c)\), which is strictly smaller than five. At \(q=c\), the
multiplier is exactly \(1/(1-2c)=\sqrt5+2\).

For \(T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V\), with \(\lambda>0\),

\[
T_\lambda(x)-x=\lambda(V(x)-x).
\]

Therefore the certificate in terms of the **averaged-map residual** is

\[
\boxed{\|x-x^*\|_1
 \le\min\left\{2,
 \frac{\|T_\lambda(x)-x\|_1}{\lambda(1-c-s)}\right\}.}
\tag{9}
\]

This algebraic identity does not require invariance of \(K_q\). For the
upstream invariant averaged iteration, its usual hypotheses
\(c\le q<1/2\), \(0<\lambda\le q\) are sufficient. Omitting the factor
\(1/\lambda\) in (9) would be incorrect: for any fixed nonfixed \(x\), the
averaged-map residual tends to zero as \(\lambda\downarrow0\), while its
distance from the fixed point does not change. The original residual
\(V(x)-x\) and its negative \(x-V(x)\) have the same norm.

### A residual-only cap for arbitrary simplex approximations

Put \(r=x-V(x)\) and let \(\delta\ge0\) be any known upper bound for all
\(r_i\). One may take the sharper signed quantity
\(\delta=\max_i r_i\), or the more conservative
\(\delta=\|r\|_\infty\). Because \(\sum_i r_i=0\), both choices are
nonnegative. Suppose

\[
0\le\delta<\delta_0:=2(1-2c)=2\sqrt5-4.
\tag{10}
\]

From the strictly non-Volterra condition,

\[
x_i=V_i(x)+r_i\le(1-x_i)^2+\delta.
\]

Solving the resulting quadratic inequality on \([0,1]\) gives

\[
x_i\le q_\delta:=\frac{3-\sqrt{5-4\delta}}2.
\tag{11}
\]

Under (10) the discriminant is positive and the larger root exceeds one, so
the indicated branch is forced. Moreover,

\[
q_\delta<1-c
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\delta<2\sqrt5-4.
\tag{12}
\]

For example, equality in (12)'s threshold follows by substituting
\(q=1-c\), using \((1-c)^2=c\), into
\(\delta=3q-q^2-1\). The smaller root \(q_\delta\) increases strictly with
\(\delta\), which gives the strict equivalence on the range considered.

Thus (7) gives the residual-only certificate

\[
\boxed{\|x-x^*\|_1
 \le\min\left\{2,\frac{R}{1-c-q_\delta}\right\}
 =\min\left\{2,
 \frac{2R}{\sqrt5+\sqrt{5-4\delta}-4}\right\}.}
\tag{13}
\]

This is valid for any \(x\in\Delta_3\), without an a priori \(K_q\)
assumption, whenever (10) holds. Actual knowledge of \(s=\|x\|_\infty\)
gives the at least as good bound (7), since \(s\le q_\delta\); the value of
(13) is that the residual alone guarantees entry into the range where a
positive denominator is available.

If only \(R\) is available, the zero-sum identity gives
\(\max_i r_i\le R/2\). Therefore one may use \(\delta=R/2\) provided
\(R<4(\sqrt5-2)\). At equality or above the threshold in (10), this argument
does **not** yield a positive-denominator residual-only certificate; no
failure of uniqueness or of other possible error bounds is implied.

## Claim 3: a sharper coefficient-to-fixed-point bound

Let \(P,Q\) both satisfy (1), and let

\[
x=x^*(P),\quad y=x^*(Q),\quad z=x-y,\quad
\varepsilon=\max_{a,b}\sum_r|P_{ab,r}-Q_{ab,r}|.
\]

For any parent pair \((a,b)\), the two coefficient rows are probability
vectors. For every subset \(G\) of the offspring types, the zero-sum
property of their difference implies

\[
\left|\sum_{r\in G}(P_{ab,r}-Q_{ab,r})\right|
\le\frac12\sum_r|P_{ab,r}-Q_{ab,r}|
\le\frac\varepsilon2.
\tag{14}
\]

### Verification of the parent's original constant

For any \(u\in\Delta_3\),

\[
\|V_P(u)-V_Q(u)\|_1
\le\sum_{a,b}u_au_b\sum_r|P_{ab,r}-Q_{ab,r}|
\le\varepsilon.
\tag{15}
\]

The residual of \(y\) for \(P\) is
\(y-V_P(y)=V_Q(y)-V_P(y)\). Since \(y\in K_c\), (8) gives

\[
\|x-y\|_1\le\frac\varepsilon{1-2c}
             =(\sqrt5+2)\varepsilon.
\tag{16}
\]

This confirms the stated constant and its normalization. In particular,
\(\varepsilon\) here is the full row \(\ell^1\) difference, not the total
variation distance; total variation is half as large.

### Retaining the forced zero perturbations

We can improve (16). The case \(x=y\) is immediate, so suppose otherwise,
and put \(G=\{i:z_i>0\}\), \(h=\|z\|_1/2\). The fixed-point equations give

\[
h=\sum_{r\in G}\bigl(V_{P,r}(x)-V_{P,r}(y)\bigr)
 +\sum_{r\in G}\bigl(V_{P,r}(y)-V_{Q,r}(y)\bigr).
\tag{17}
\]

If \(|G|=1\) or \(3\), the first sum is nonpositive by Claim 1's
monotonicity argument. Equation (14), summed against \(y_ay_b\), bounds
the second sum above by \(\varepsilon/2\). Consequently

\[
\|x-y\|_1\le\varepsilon
\tag{18}
\]

in those cases.

Suppose \(|G|=2\), writing \(G=\{i,k\}\) and \(H=G^c=\{j,\ell\}\).
Both fixed points lie in \(K_c\), so the first sum in (17) is at most
\(2ch\) by (5). For the second sum, the group offspring probability is
exactly zero for the parent pair \(\{i,k\}\), for both \(P\) and \(Q\).
It is exactly one for the parent pair \(\{j,\ell\}\), for both operators.
Thus these pairs have identically zero perturbation of their group output.
Keeping this information when summing (14), including both orders of each
distinct parent pair, gives

\[
\left|\sum_{r\in G}(V_{P,r}(y)-V_{Q,r}(y))\right|
\le\frac\varepsilon2
     \bigl[1-2(y_i y_k+y_j y_\ell)\bigr].
\tag{19}
\]

Since every \(y_a\le c\),

\[
0\le(c-y_i)(c-y_k)+(c-y_j)(c-y_\ell)
   =2c^2-c+y_i y_k+y_j y_\ell.
\]

Hence

\[
y_i y_k+y_j y_\ell\ge c(1-2c).
\tag{20}
\]

Combining (17), (19), and (20),

\[
(1-2c)h\le\frac\varepsilon2\,[1-2c(1-2c)],
\]

so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|x-y\|_1
&\le\left(\frac1{1-2c}-2c\right)\varepsilon\\
&=\bigl[(\sqrt5+2)-(3-\sqrt5)\bigr]\varepsilon\\
&=(2\sqrt5-1)\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

This constant exceeds one, so it also covers (18). The simplex diameter
bound completes the proof of (A). \(\square\)

### Boundary validity and limitations

Every line above uses nonnegativity, row normalization, the strict
non-Volterra prohibitions, and parent symmetry. No line divides by a state
coordinate or by a coefficient. The only denominators used in the uniform
stability theorem are positive constants depending on \(c\). Therefore
(21) holds on the **entire closed coefficient polytope**, including its
vertices and all cases with boundary fixed points. It is already a global
Lipschitz statement on that closed set, not merely an interior estimate
awaiting an extension argument. It also implies that sequences of
admissible coefficient arrays approaching the boundary have fixed points
converging to the unique fixed point of the limiting array.

If strictly positive allowed coefficients are chosen as a narrower starting
parameter domain, the same proof supplies a unique uniformly Lipschitz
extension to its admissible boundary. No differentiability or analytic
extension across the boundary is claimed.

The improved constant \(2\sqrt5-1\) is an upper bound, not a claimed sharp
constant. Although (20) is sharp on the capped simplex \(K_c\), its equality
cases need not be simultaneously realizable with equality in the fixed-point
and transfer inequalities. Establishing the optimal coefficient Lipschitz
constant remains outside this report.

These results are restricted to four types. They do not establish convergence
of the unaveraged discrete dynamics, a new optimal averaged iteration, or
analogous uniqueness and stability in dimensions where uniqueness fails.
