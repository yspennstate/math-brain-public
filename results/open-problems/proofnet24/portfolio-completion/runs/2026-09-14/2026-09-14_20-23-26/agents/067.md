## 2. The original statements, with exact numbering

### Janson

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, arXiv:1112.0510v1, 2 December 2011; published in *Probability Surveys* 9 (2012), 103–252, DOI 10.1214/11-PS188. I used the retrieved [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0510) and checked its [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.0510).

Manuscript §20.2, pp. 134–135, gives
\[
(N_d-n\pi_d)/\sqrt n\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\sigma_d^2),\qquad
\sigma_d^2=\pi_d(1-\pi_d)-(d-1)^2\pi_d^2/\sigma^2
\]
in (20.5)–(20.6), and joint covariance (20.7).

**Problem 20.2 consists of three unlettered questions**:

| Order | Scope |
|---|---|
| First | Remove extra moments when \(\nu=1,\sigma^2<\infty\). |
| Second | Extend the preceding results to \(\nu=1,\sigma^2=\infty\). |
| Third | Determine behavior for \(0\le\nu<1\). |

The labels “(a), (b), (c)” used internally are editorial shorthand. The third question does not itself assert a universal Gaussian theorem. The adjacent discussion also concerns moments, so a statement that every aspect of Problem 20.2 is settled would be broader than the two counterexamples. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.0510).

### Thévenin

Paul Thévenin, *Vertices with fixed outdegrees in large Galton–Watson trees*, arXiv:1812.07365v2, 26 February 2020 (v1: 18 December 2018); *Electronic Journal of Probability* 25 (2020), paper 64, DOI 10.1214/20-EJP465. The [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07365), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.07365) and [versioned HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.07365v2) were accessible.

The **unnumbered closing conjecture**, §6.2, manuscript p. 23, asserts for every \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\)
\[
\frac{N^A(T_n)-n\mu_A}{\sqrt n}\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\mu_A(1-\mu_A))
\tag{C}
\]
for either critical infinite-variance offspring laws or subcritical laws having no positive exponential moment.

The paper's §2 assumptions include \(\mu_0+\mu_1<1\) and aperiodicity. Theorems **6.1–6.2** retain criticality and stable attraction with index \(\alpha\in(1,2]\); (15) specifies
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X\mathbf1_{\{X\le x\}})\sim x^{2-\alpha}L(x).
\]
**Theorem 6.7** assumes \(\mu_k\sim ck^{-\beta}\), \(c>0,\beta>2\), and finite degree sets; subcriticality is the standing assumption of its subsection. Its covariance is \(\mu_{A\cap B}-\mu_A\mu_B\). The conjecture deliberately extends these hypotheses. It is not numbered 6.8. [Primary text, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.07365v2#S6).

## 3. Independent logical match to the supplied constructions

Here \(\mu=\pi\), \(A=\{1\}\), and \(q=\pi_1=1/4\). The required test distribution in (C) is therefore \(\mathcal N(0,3/16)\).

| Gate | Subcritical manuscript | Critical manuscript |
|---|---|---|
| One fixed offspring law | Explicit recursive pair law | Fixed law selected by terminating stage rules |
| Mean | \(1/4<\nu<5/16<1\) | Exactly \(1\) |
| Variance | Finite and positive; even a finite third moment | Infinite; every moment of order \(>1\) diverges |
| Exponential moments | None positive | None positive |
| Nondegeneracy | Positive mass on rare degrees \(>1\) | \(\pi_2=1/8\) |
| Aperiodicity and size conditioning | \(\pi_0,\pi_1>0\); a path realizes every positive size | Same |
| Centering and scale | \(nq,\sqrt n\) | \(nq,\sqrt n\) |
| Proved subsequence asserted in the manuscript | \(\frac12\mathcal N(-a_*,v_*)+\frac12\mathcal N(a_*,v_*)\), \(a_*,v_*>0\) | \(\frac12\mathcal N(-1,3/16)+\frac12\mathcal N(1,3/16)\) |

Taking weights \(w_k=\pi_k\), the lack of positive exponential moments makes the generating-function radius \(1\); the supplied mean calculations give \(\Phi(1)=1,\tau=1\). Thus the subcritical law has canonical \(\nu<1\), while the critical law has canonical \(\nu=1,\sigma^2=\infty\). There is no mismatch between raw offspring parameters and the parameters used for the simply generated model.

For either mixture, its characteristic function has the form
\[
e^{-vs^2/2}\cos(as),\qquad a>0.
\]
It has a zero, whereas every Gaussian characteristic function is nonzero. Consequently each displayed limit is non-Gaussian. If the full sequence had the Gaussian limit in (C), every subsequence would share it. This proves the contradiction once the supplied convergence theorems are accepted. No convergence of moments, expectation-centering result, or full-sequence classification is needed for this implication.

The positive masses at 0 and 1 also imply \(\pi_0+\pi_1<1\) because each law assigns positive mass above 1. The restriction \(A=\{1\}\) is finite, so the contradiction does not depend on the conjecture's additional allowance of infinite sets.

The proof obligations for the subsequential convergences remain in the manuscripts and reports 015/028/033/034. This worker independently verifies the source correspondence and the last logical implication, not their entire Fourier/diagonal construction anew.

## 4. Why the narrower results survive

**The subcritical law is in a normal attraction domain after centering.** Finite positive variance ensures this. Its failure of criticality prevents applying the critical stable-domain theorems. Its arbitrarily long support gaps prevent a positive power-law mass asymptotic. Thus it would be incorrect to describe both constructions as lying outside every stable domain.

There is a stronger direct tail check for this particular subcritical law. Put \(\overline F(x)=P(\xi>x)\), \(p_r=n_r^{-4}\), and \(R_r=2\sum_{s>r}p_s\). At the smaller current atom,
\[
\frac{\overline F(L_r^-)}{\overline F(L_r^--1)}
=\frac{p_r+R_r}{2p_r+R_r}\longrightarrow\frac12,
\]
since \(n_{r+1}=n_r^4\) implies \(R_r/p_r\to0\). Therefore the law is not even long-tailed under a unit shift. Also every fixed-width interval misses the support infinitely often. These are direct deductions from the supplied support, not inferences from failure of the CLT.

This explains the boundary with Inés Armendáriz and Michail Loulakis, *Conditional Distribution of Heavy Tailed Random Variables on Large Deviations of their Sum*, [arXiv:0912.1516v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.1516), 22 January 2011, *Stochastic Processes and their Applications* 121(5), 1138–1147. Their **Theorem 1**, manuscript p. 3, assumes \(\Delta\)-subexponentiality and a sufficiently large threshold \(x\ge q_n\); the local definition in (2.4)–(2.5) includes eventual positive interval masses and shift regularity. It is not a theorem for every distribution lacking exponential moments. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.1516).

Igor Kortchemski's *Limit theorems for conditioned non-generic Galton–Watson trees*, [arXiv:1205.3145v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3145), 14 September 2013, *Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques* 51(2) (2015), 489–511, gives **Theorem 2.1** as the tree-context use of that result. Its standing **Assumption \((H_\theta)\)** has subcritical mean and regularly varying positive masses; **Remark 2.3** explains extensions under suitable large-deviation conditions. This is additional controlled-tail evidence, not an unrestricted no-exponential-moment CLT. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.3145).

For the critical law, the supplied direct test uses
\[
W(x)=\operatorname{Var}(\xi\mathbf1_{\{\xi\le x\}}),\qquad
W(H_r)/W(H_r/2)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
A regularly varying \(W\) of index \(2-\alpha\) would instead give the finite limit \(2^{2-\alpha}\). Thus it fails (15) for every \(\alpha\in(1,2]\), including the infinite-variance normal case. This exclusion is independent of the non-Gaussian count conclusion.

## 5. Later primary literature actually inspected

### The finite-variance question already has a positive result

Svante Janson, *Asymptotic normality of fringe subtrees and additive functionals in conditioned Galton–Watson trees*, [arXiv:1312.1211v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1211), 4 December 2013; *Random Structures & Algorithms* 48(1) (2016), 57–101. **Theorem 1.13 and Example 2.2**, with the explicit covariance calculation in §8, establish joint degree-count normality for critical finite-variance laws without an additional third moment; Example 2.2 also states mean/variance convergence. This supplies the positive degree-count CLT answer to the first question above. I am not attributing unrestricted convergence of every higher moment to this result. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.1211).

### A direct 2023 degree-count restatement retains the restrictions

Gabriel Berzunza Ojeda, Cecilia Holmgren and Svante Janson, *Fringe trees for random trees with given vertex degrees*, [arXiv:2312.04243v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04243), 7 December 2023. **Theorem 7.6**, (7.12)–(7.14), invokes **Theorem 7.2(i)–(iii)**: canonical critical finite variance; canonical critical infinite variance in stable attraction; or canonical subcritical positive power-law masses. The text attributes the first regime to Janson and the other two to Thévenin. Appendix B gives an alternative degree-count proof in the critical regimes.

The unrestricted assertion (7.7) in **Theorem 7.2** concerns fixed fringe counts centered by their expectation conditional on the entire random degree statistic. Its deterministic-centering assertion (7.9) requires the listed regimes. Confusing these statements could produce a false claim that this paper settles the unrestricted degree-count conjecture. [PDF, §7 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.04243).

### An April 2026 extension also remains narrower

The same three authors, *Large fringe trees for random trees with given vertex degrees*, [arXiv:2604.06012v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06012), 7 April 2026. **Theorem 6.2**, manuscript p. 26, treats counts of specified fringe trees whose size grows. It assumes critical offspring and either finite variance or infinite variance with stable attraction of index \(\alpha\in(1,2]\). Its Poisson/normal limits are not an unrestricted fixed-outdegree theorem. The discussion immediately preceding it explicitly distinguishes the extra work needed to account for random degree-statistic fluctuations. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06012).

### A relevant-looking counterexample search hit concerns another assertion

Rémy Poudevigne and Paul Thévenin, *Existence of critical tiltings and local limits of general size-conditioned Bienaymé–Galton–Watson multitype trees*, [arXiv:2503.11501v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11501), 14 March 2025. **Corollary 3.8** assumes entire offspring generating functions and other regularity conditions. **Remark 3.9 and Appendix 8** discuss failure of critical tilting without entirety. The paper concerns multitype tiltings/local limits, so its counterexample is not evidence that either fixed-outdegree Gaussian clause was previously refuted. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11501). This is a scope check, not a correctness audit of that paper.
