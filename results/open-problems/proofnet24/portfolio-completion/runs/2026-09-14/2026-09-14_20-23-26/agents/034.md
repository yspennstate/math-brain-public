## 3. Law construction and the exact analytic class

### 3.1 Integer recursion and absence of collisions

For any \(a>0\), the identity \(n_{r+j}=n_r^{4^j}\) and inequality \(4^j\ge j+1\) give

\[
\sum_{s\ge r}n_s^{-a}\le
\frac{n_r^{-a}}{1-n_r^{-a}}.
\tag{034.2}
\]

For \(a=2,3,4\), this is at most \(2n_r^{-a}\). Define

\[
c_r=\sum_{s\le r}p_s(L_s^-+L_s^+).
\]

The sum of the pair is \(2d_{r-1}n_r\), so

\[
c_r-c_{r-1}=2d_{r-1}n_r^{-3}=d_{r-1}-d_r.
\]

Consequently \(c_r=3/4-d_r\) exactly. This telescoping identity is valid algebraically before the integrality assertion and creates no circular dependency.

The induction proving integrality is explicit. At stage one, \(H_1=192\) and the two atoms are 176 and 208. If earlier atoms are integers, then

\[
H_r=\frac34n_r-
\sum_{s<r}\frac{n_r}{n_s^4}(L_s^-+L_s^+).
\tag{034.3}
\]

Every ratio \(n_r/n_s^4\) in this expression is an integer: its power of two is \(8(4^{r-1}-4^s)\), which is nonnegative for \(s<r\). Also \(n_r/4\) and \(\sqrt{n_r}=2^{4\cdot4^{r-1}}\) are integers. Thus no rounding, size-dependent adjustment, or infinite lookahead is being concealed in the support.

Each factor defining \(d_r\) is positive. Since \(d_{r-1}\le3/4\),

\[
0<c_r\le\frac32\sum_{s\ge1}n_s^{-3}
\le3\cdot256^{-3}<1/16.
\]

It follows that \(11/16<d_r\le3/4\). Because \(\sqrt{n_r}\le n_r/16\),

\[
\frac58n_r<L_r^-<L_r^+\le\frac{13}{16}n_r<n_r.
\tag{034.4}
\]

In addition \(L_{r+1}^->(5/8)n_r^4>n_r\). Therefore different pairs cannot collide, no rare atom is 0 or 1, and all atoms earlier than stage \(r\) are less than \(n_{r-1}\). These last two deterministic facts are important for the later conditioning argument; they do hold for this recursion.

### 3.2 Probability, moments, and radius

The total rare probability satisfies \(2\sum p_r\le4\cdot256^{-4}<1/4\). The remaining mass at zero is therefore greater than \(1/2\), and the displayed masses sum exactly to one.

The decreasing positive sequence \(d_r\) has a limit \(d_\infty>11/16\), using the same uniform bound on \(c_r\). Its first factor is strictly below one, so \(d_\infty<3/4\). Monotone summation gives

\[
E\xi=q+\lim_rc_r=1-d_\infty\in(1/4,5/16).
\]

Using the strict upper support bound in (034.4),

\[
E\xi^2\le\frac14+2\sum_rn_r^{-2}
\le\frac14+4\cdot256^{-2}<\frac12.
\]

For independent copies \(\xi,\xi'\), the two events \((\xi,\xi')=(0,1),(1,0)\) alone contribute \(2\pi_0q\) to \(E(\xi-\xi')^2\). Hence

\[
\sigma^2=\tfrac12E(\xi-\xi')^2\ge\pi_0q>1/8.
\]

For every fixed \(\theta>0\),

\[
p_re^{\theta L_r^-}\ge n_r^{-4}e^{(5\theta/8)n_r}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

In particular the exponential-moment series diverges for every positive \(\theta\). The probability generating function converges at one and cannot have radius greater than one; its radius is exactly one.

With simply generated weights \(w_k=\pi_k\), one has \(w_0>0\), positive weights at degrees above one, \(\Phi(1)=1\), \(\rho=1\), and \(\Psi(1)=\nu<1\). The canonical choice is therefore \(\tau=\rho=1\), and \(\tau^kw_k/\Phi(\tau)=\pi_k\). No exponential tilting to a different canonical offspring law changes the theorem's intended model. Since 1 is in the support, its one-dimensional span is one.

An additional direct consequence, not needed by the proof, is the moment threshold

\[
E\xi^h<\infty\quad(0<h<4),\qquad E\xi^h=\infty\quad(h\ge4).
\]

For the first assertion bound the rare contribution by \(2\sum n_r^{h-4}\); for the second use \(p_r(L_r^-)^h\ge(5/8)^hn_r^{h-4}\). Thus the example even has finite third moment. This observation concerns the subcritical law and supplies no inference about critical finite-third-moment results.

## 4. The truncated rows and exact exceptional-atom decomposition

Let \(n=n_r\), let \(\mathcal A_r\) consist of 0, 1, and all earlier rare atoms, and put

\[
t_r=2\sum_{s\ge r}p_s,\quad z_r=1-t_r,\quad
u_r=q+c_{r-1}=1-d_{r-1}.
\]

The law \(X_r\) obtained by conditioning \(\xi\) on \(\mathcal A_r\) has

\[
\mu_r=EX_r=u_r/z_r,\qquad q_r=P(X_r=1)=q/z_r.
\]

Here \(t_r\le4n^{-4}\), \(z_r>3/4\), and the normalizations are essential: replacing \(\mu_r\) by the unnormalized first moment would discard an error that must be checked. The exact formulas show that both \(\mu_r-u_r\) and \(q_r-q\) are \(O(n^{-4})\), with constants uniform in \(r\).

Furthermore

\[
\mu_r<5/12,\quad \operatorname{Var}X_r\le EX_r^2<1,
\quad P(X_r=0)>1/2,\quad P(X_r=1)\ge1/4.
\]

For \(r\ge2\), \(0\le X_r\le M_r:=n_{r-1}=n^{1/4}\). The first row is supported on \(\{0,1\}\) and is irrelevant to the asymptotic argument.

The truncation sets increase to the full support and their probabilities approach one. Thus \(X_r\Rightarrow\xi\). For every \(K\),

\[
\sup_r E[X_r^2;X_r>K]
\le\frac43 E[\xi^2;\xi>K]\longrightarrow0.
\tag{034.5}
\]

This supplies uniform integrability of the squares, convergence of both moments, and convergence of the row variances to \(\sigma^2\).

Now take \(n\) independent original variables and impose

\[
\mathcal C_r=\{\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_n=n-1\}.
\]

A later atom is larger than \(n\), so it is impossible on this event. Two current atoms total more than \(5n/4\), so they too are impossible. There are therefore exactly three types: no current atom; one \(L_r^+\); or one \(L_r^-\). This is an exact partition, not a truncation of an unexamined collection of exceptional events.

For \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), the joint probability of the current-sign type with \(\mathcal C_r\) equals

\[
\beta_r^\varepsilon=np_rz_r^{n-1}
 P\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{r,i}=k_r^\varepsilon\right),
\quad k_r^\varepsilon=n-1-L_r^\varepsilon.
\tag{034.6}
\]

The factor \(n\) counts disjoint positions for the exceptional atom; it is not a further asymptotic approximation. The integer target belongs to \([0,n-1]\), so even the 0/1 part of the row law shows that this event has positive probability. Direct subtraction yields

\[
k_r^\varepsilon-(n-1)\mu_r
=-\varepsilon\sqrt n-d_{r-1}
 -(n-1)u_rt_r/z_r
=-\varepsilon\sqrt n-d_{r-1}+O(n^{-3}).
\tag{034.7}
\]

Consequently the normalized sum target tends to \(-\varepsilon\). There is no asymptotically nonzero target shift hidden in the mean renormalization.

## 5. Independent Fourier verification, including the joint lattice issue

I give the conditional limit argument explicitly instead of appealing to a general bivariate theorem. Suppose rows satisfy (034.5), distributional convergence, and uniformly positive atoms at 0 and 1, and let \(N=N_r\to\infty\). Write \(Y_r=1_{\{X_r=1\}}\). The relevant limiting covariance matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^2&\gamma\\\gamma&b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad b=q(1-q),\quad\gamma=q(1-\nu).
\tag{034.8}
\]

Indeed \(X_rY_r=Y_r\) exactly, giving \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_r,Y_r)=q_r(1-\mu_r)\). No independence between category counts is available or used.

Fix \(s\in\mathbb R\) and integers \(k_r\) with \((k_r-N\mu_r)/\sqrt N\to a\). With \(S_r=\sum X_{r,i}\), \(B_r=\sum Y_{r,i}\), Fourier inversion in the **one integer coordinate** gives

\[
E\left[e^{is(B_r-Nq_r)/\sqrt N};S_r=k_r\right]
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
e^{-it(k_r-N\mu_r)}
\left(Ee^{it(X_r-\mu_r)+is(Y_r-q_r)/\sqrt N}\right)^Ndt.
\tag{034.9}
\]

For fixed \(v,s\), the centered variable
\(W_r=v(X_r-\mu_r)+s(Y_r-q_r)\) has uniformly integrable squares: \(Y_r\) is bounded, the means are bounded, and (034.5) controls its remaining tail. The elementary Taylor remainder bound

\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min(|x|,1)
\]

then proves

\[
E e^{iW_r/\sqrt N}
=1-\frac{EW_r^2}{2N}+o(N^{-1}).
\]

For completeness, multiply the remainder by \(N\), split at \(|W_r|\le K\), and first send \(N\to\infty\); the bounded part vanishes. Then send \(K\to\infty\) using uniform integrability. No third moment, rate of variance convergence, or quantitative Berry–Esseen bound is required.

After \(t=v/\sqrt N\), the integrand in (034.9) converges pointwise to

\[
e^{-iav}\exp[-(\sigma^2v^2+2\gamma vs+bs^2)/2].
\tag{034.10}
\]

Here is a complete domination check. Let

\[
\phi_r(t,s)=E e^{itX_r+isY_r/\sqrt N},
\]

and choose \(\eta>0\) with \(P(X_r=0),P(X_r=1)\ge\eta\). Expanding against an independent copy gives a sum of nonnegative quantities \(1-\cos\) of phase differences. Keeping just the ordered 0/1 pairs yields

\[
1-|\phi_r(t,s)|^2
\ge2\eta^2[1-\cos(t+s/\sqrt N)].
\tag{034.11}
\]

Choose \(\delta=\pi/4\). For each fixed \(s\), all large rows satisfy \(|s|/\sqrt N\le\delta/2\). For \(|t|\le\delta\), the argument of the cosine has absolute value below \(\pi\), and \(1-\cos x\ge2x^2/\pi^2\) gives

\[
|\phi_r(t,s)|^N\le
\exp[-(2\eta^2/\pi^2)N(t+s/\sqrt N)^2].
\]

Thus the scaled central integrand has the fixed integrable majorant \(e^{-c(v+s)^2}\). On \(\delta\le|t|\le\pi\), the cosine argument stays uniformly away from \(2\pi\mathbb Z\); (034.11) gives \(|\phi_r(t,s)|^N\le e^{-c_\delta N}\). Even after multiplication by \(\sqrt N\), this part of the integral tends to zero. Centering only multiplies characteristic functions by unit complex numbers and does not alter these estimates.

Dominated convergence now applies to the central integral. The resulting Gaussian integral is

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(s)&=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
e^{-iav}e^{-(\sigma^2v^2+2\gamma vs+bs^2)/2}\,dv\\
&=\frac{e^{-a^2/(2\sigma^2)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}
\exp\left(i\frac{\gamma a}{\sigma^2}s
-\frac12\left(b-\frac{\gamma^2}{\sigma^2}\right)s^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{034.12}
\]

At \(s=0\), this proves

\[
\sqrt N\,P(S_r=k_r)\to
\frac{e^{-a^2/(2\sigma^2)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}>0.
\tag{034.13}
\]

Dividing (034.12) by \(I(0)\) and applying the continuity theorem for characteristic functions proves

\[
\frac{B_r-Nq_r}{\sqrt N}\ \bigg|\ S_r=k_r
\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal N(\gamma a/\sigma^2,b-\gamma^2/\sigma^2).
\tag{034.14}
\]

This independently verifies both the local probability and the regression sign in the manuscript.

### The lattice point that must remain explicit

Every rare degree is a multiple of 16. One induction uses (034.3): \(H_1\) is a multiple of 16, every earlier pair sum is a multiple of 32, and \((3/4)n_r\) and \(\sqrt{n_r}\) are multiples of 16. Hence \(H_r,L_r^\pm\) retain this divisibility. The first pair has gcd \(\gcd(176,208)=16\), so the gcd of the entire rare support is exactly 16.

Consequently \(X_r-Y_r\in16\mathbb Z\), and for rows containing the first pair the difference lattice of \((X_r,Y_r)\) is generated by \((1,1)\) and \((16,0)\). Its index in \(\mathbb Z^2\) is 16. In particular \(B_r\equiv k_r\pmod {16}\) under the sum conditioning. The original tree count similarly satisfies \(N_1(T_{n_r})\equiv n_r-1\equiv15\pmod {16}\).

This does not obstruct a weak Gaussian branch limit, because the scaled spacing is \(16/\sqrt N\to0\). More importantly, (034.9)–(034.14) prove the needed result without assuming joint aperiodicity. The positive 0/1 atoms control the only integrated Fourier coordinate. An assertion that the *joint* law is aperiodic, or an application of a bivariate lattice local limit theorem with unit lattice volume, would be false here. No such assertion or application occurs in the audited proof.

## 6. Conditioning dominance: all branches and constants

### 6.1 The two current branches

Apply (034.13) to (034.7), taking \(N=n-1\). With

\[
C_\sigma=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}e^{-1/(2\sigma^2)}>0,
\]

both signs have local probability \(C_\sigma n^{-1/2}(1+o(1))\). There are just two signs, so no uniformity over an expanding collection of targets is being assumed. Also

\[
0\le1-z_r^{n-1}\le(n-1)t_r=O(n^{-3}).
\]

Substituting in (034.6), with \(p_r=n^{-4}\), yields

\[
\beta_r^+=C_\sigma n^{-7/2}(1+o(1)),\qquad
\beta_r^-=C_\sigma n^{-7/2}(1+o(1)).
\tag{034.15}
\]

The signs need not be equally probable at finite sizes. Equality of the limiting constants follows from the evenness of the centered Gaussian density at residual targets \(\pm1\).

### 6.2 The entire no-current branch

Its joint probability is

\[
\beta_r^0=z_r^nP\left(\sum_{i=1}^nX_{r,i}=n-1\right).
\]

Write \(D=n-1-n\mu_r\). The bound \(\mu_r<5/12\) gives

\[
D>7n/12-1\ge n/3
\]

for all \(n\ge256\); also \(D\le n\). For \(r\ge2\), the centered summands have absolute value at most \(M=n^{1/4}\). Their total variance \(V=n\operatorname{Var}X_r\) lies in \((0,n)\).

For a centered random variable with \(|Z|\le M\), the exponential series and \(k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}\), \(k\ge2\), give, for \(0<\lambda<3/M\),

\[
\log E e^{\lambda Z}\le
\frac{\lambda^2EZ^2}{2(1-\lambda M/3)}.
\]

The chosen \(\lambda=D/(V+MD/3)\) is admissible because \(V>0\). Applying the bound to the whole independent sum and substituting this \(\lambda\) gives

\[
P\left(\sum X_{r,i}-n\mu_r\ge D\right)
\le \exp\left[-\frac{D^2}{2(V+MD/3)}\right].
\]

Finally,

\[
D^2\ge n^2/9,\qquad
2(V+MD/3)\le2n(1+M/3)\le(8/3)nM,
\]

where the last inequality uses only \(M\ge1\). Therefore

\[
\beta_r^0\le \exp[-n/(24M)]
=\exp[-n^{3/4}/24].
\tag{034.16}
\]

The stated constant \(1/24\) is valid, and the argument uses one bound on the entire truncated sum. It has none of the growing-number-of-stages nonuniformity identified in agent 001's original-memo audit.

Equations (034.15)–(034.16), combined with the exact three-type partition, prove

\[
P(\mathcal C_r)\sim2C_\sigma n^{-7/2},\qquad
\frac{\beta_r^0}{\beta_r^++\beta_r^-}
=O(n^{7/2}e^{-n^{3/4}/24})\to0.
\]

Thus exactly one current rare atom occurs with conditional probability tending to one, and each sign has conditional probability tending to \(1/2\). No other rare configuration remains to be bounded.

## 7. Count centering, non-Gaussianity, and the tree transfer

Conditional on the current atom's position and sign, the other \(n-1\) variables are independent row variables before conditioning on their residual sum. Conditional on that sum, (034.14) and (034.7) show that their count of ones obeys

\[
\frac{B_r-(n-1)q_r}{\sqrt{n-1}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(-\varepsilon a_*,v_*).
\]

The exceptional atom is not one, so it contributes nothing to this count. The required deterministic centering correction is exactly

\[
(n-1)q_r-nq=-q+(n-1)qt_r/z_r=-q+O(n^{-3}).
\tag{034.17}
\]

After division by \(\sqrt n\) this vanishes; \(\sqrt{(n-1)/n}\to1\) as well. Averaging the two branch limits with their limiting probabilities and discarding the no-current branch against bounded continuous test functions proves the mixture limit for the conditioned independent sample.

Because \(q>0\), \(\nu<1\), and \(\sigma^2>0\), one has \(a_*>0\). Equality \(v_*=0\) in the covariance Cauchy–Schwarz inequality would make \(1_{\{\xi=1\}}-q\) a constant multiple of \(\xi-\nu\) almost surely. Comparing the positive-probability values \(\xi=0\) and \(\xi=176\) forces that multiple to vanish, contradicting the value at \(\xi=1\). Thus \(v_*>0\).

The mixture characteristic function is \(e^{-v_*s^2/2}\cos(a_*s)\). It has a zero at \(s=\pi/(2a_*)\), whereas a Gaussian characteristic function has no zeros. This proves non-Gaussianity without relying on numerical parameter separation or moment convergence.

### 7.1 Cyclic encoding, with the wraparound endpoint checked

For a nonnegative integer vector \((x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) with sum \(n-1\), put \(a_i=x_i-1\) and \(H_j=\sum_{i\le j}a_i\), \(H_0=0\). Then \(H_n=-1\). Let \(j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) be the **first** index where \(H_0,\ldots,H_{n-1}\) attain their minimum. Starting the increments after \(j\) gives a valid tree word:

* Until the end of the original word, partial sums at original indices \(\ell<n\) equal \(H_\ell-H_j\ge0\).
* If \(j>0\), the partial sum at original index \(n\) occurs before the final rotated step. Because \(j\) is the first minimum and \(H_0=0\), integer heights imply \(H_j\le-1\); hence \(H_n-H_j=-1-H_j\ge0\). If \(j=0\), this is the final step and is allowed to equal \(-1\).
* After wrapping, for \(\ell<j\) the partial sum is \(-1-H_j+H_\ell\ge0\), since earlier heights are strictly greater integers than \(H_j\). The final step is \(-1\).

Conversely, a valid rotation forces these same inequalities: later heights are at least the starting height, and earlier heights are strictly greater. The starting index is therefore this first minimum. The increments are at least \(-1\), so this valid word is exactly the depth-first offspring word of a unique plane tree; the active-vertex count starts at one, remains positive until the final vertex, and then becomes zero.

The word cannot have a nontrivial cyclic period. If it repeated \(m>1\) times, its total integer increment sum \(-1\) would be divisible by \(m\). Thus every rotation orbit has exactly \(n\) distinct members and exactly one valid tree word.

The conditioned product law assigns the same weight \(\prod_i\pi_{x_i}\) to all members of an orbit. Sending each member to its unique valid rotation gives a tree word probability proportional to its product weight, which is precisely the Galton–Watson law conditioned on size. The count of ones and sorted degree sequence are invariant under rotation; their conditional distributions transfer exactly. This completes the proof of (034.1) for trees.

The manuscript's brief cycle proof omits the separate sentence treating original index \(n\) when \(j>0\). The second bullet supplies it. This is a presentational clarification of an elementary immediate implication, not an obstruction to the argument or a change to the theorem.

The path with \(n\) vertices has probability \(\pi_0\pi_1^{n-1}>0\), so all positive conditioning sizes are available. As an independent check on the normalizing factor, cyclic encoding also gives

\[
P(|T|=n_r)=\frac1{n_r}P(\mathcal C_r)
\sim2C_\sigma n_r^{-9/2}.
\]

There is therefore no extra tree-size factor in the branch *ratios*. On the dominant event, the largest degree is \(L_r^\varepsilon\), all others are bounded by \(n_r^{1/4}\), and \(L_r^\varepsilon/n_r\to d_\infty=1-\nu\). The unique-macroscopic-degree conclusion holds along the audited subsequence.
