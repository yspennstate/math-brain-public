## Result and precise scope

This report does **not** solve the general oriented positive rank-two case.
It provides three bounded results beyond repeating the two-coordinate-block
argument:

1. **PROVED:** the exact minimum of the **full eight-atom Fisher objective**
   over determinant-one positive directions on an arbitrary fixed complex
   two-plane. It is the unique positive root of an explicit scalar equation,
   equivalently a specified positive root of a quartic polynomial. The
   optimizer is explicit from that root. The remaining entropy domination
   condition becomes one scalar Schur-complement inequality, equation (15).
2. **DISPROVED:** extending the two-site proof by retaining only the empty
   and full Fisher atoms. An exact rational kernel and non-coordinate
   rank-two positive direction have endpoint Fisher `12401/2500 < 5` and
   logarithmic curvature `> 132/25 > 5`. A one-parameter family makes their
   ratio unbounded. These are counterexamples to that proposed bound,
   **not** to entropy concavity.
3. **PROVED restricted Hessian theorem:** at any interior three-site kernel
   whose atom probabilities depend only on subset cardinality, every
   arbitrarily oriented positive rank-two direction satisfies
   
   \[
   D^2H(K)[UDU^*,UDU^*]\le-2\det(D)\log3.
   \]
   
   This uses the established concavity of cardinality entropy. It is a
   restriction on the base law, not permission to rotate arbitrary kernels
   or integrate through nonsymmetric intermediate laws.

Historical novelty of these derivations is **unverified**. The known
rank-one and cardinality results are credited to the literature, not claimed
as discoveries here.

## 1. Coordinate-aware quadratic atom paths

Fix `0 < K < I` in dimension three. Let `U` be a `3 x 2` complex isometry,
let `z` be a unit vector spanning its orthogonal complement, and put

\[
A=UDU^*,\qquad D=D^*>0,\qquad \delta=\det D>0,
\qquad q=z^*Kz,\qquad h_i=|z_i|^2.
\]

Thus `q` lies in `(0,1)`, the `h_i` sum to one, and
`adj(A)=delta z z*`. The matrix `U` only parameterizes the **direction**;
the DPP's subset labels and the base kernel's coordinate system remain
fixed throughout.

For a subset `S`, write

\[
N_S=K-I_{S^c},\quad \epsilon_S=(-1)^{3-|S|},\quad
p_S=\epsilon_S\det N_S>0.
\]

Because `A` has rank two, every atom path is quadratic. The three-dimensional
mixed-determinant identity gives

\[
p_S''=2\delta r_S,\qquad
r_S=\epsilon_S\left(q-\sum_{i\notin S}h_i\right).
\tag{1}
\]

In subset order `empty, i, ij, 123`, with `k` the complement of `ij`, these
coefficients are respectively

\[
r_\varnothing=1-q,\qquad r_i=q+h_i-1,\qquad
r_{ij}=h_k-q,\qquad r_{123}=q.
\tag{2}
\]

They need not have a uniform sign at a fixed cardinality.

Define

\[
\mathcal I_K(A)=\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S},\qquad
\beta(K,z)=-\sum_S r_S\log p_S.
\]

The entropy Hessian is exactly

\[
\boxed{H''=-\mathcal I_K(A)+2\delta\beta(K,z).}
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, the logarithmic coefficient depends on the plane, not the
positive matrix `D` within it.

For clarity, `beta >= 0` also follows from the three-site supermodularity
calculation in agent 010. With frozen `f(S)=-log p_S`, write `c_T` for its
Boolean Möbius coefficients. Then

\[
\beta=\sum_i h_i c_{[3]\setminus\{i\}}+q c_{123}.
\tag{4}
\]

Hadamard--Fischer gives `c_ij >= 0` and `c_ij+c_123 >= 0`.
If `c_123 >= 0`, (4) is nonnegative termwise. Otherwise rewrite it as
`sum_i h_i(c_complement(i)+c_123)+(1-q)(-c_123)`. This verifies the sign
without assuming invariance of subset entropy under unitary conjugation.

## 2. Exact extension of the endpoint minimization

The algebraic endpoint minimization does extend, but the resulting bound
does not suffice for entropy. Let

\[
x=\det(I-K),\qquad w=\det K,
\]

\[
B_1=\frac{U^*\operatorname{adj}(K)U}{\sqrt w},\qquad
B_0=\frac{U^*\operatorname{adj}(I-K)U}{\sqrt x}.
\tag{5}
\]

Both are positive definite. Jacobi's complementary-minor identity gives

\[
\det B_1=q,\qquad \det B_0=1-q.
\]

The endpoint Fisher contribution is

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm end}(D)
=(\operatorname{tr}B_1D)^2+(\operatorname{tr}B_0D)^2.
\]

**General two-matrix lemma.** If `B_1,B_0 > 0` are `2 x 2` Hermitian,
`a=det B_1`, `b=det B_0`, and
`m=tr(adj(B_1)B_0)>0`, then

\[
\min_{D>0,\det D=1}\mathcal I_{\rm end}(D)
=2\left(a+b+\sqrt{(a-b)^2+m^2}\right).
\tag{6}
\]

**Proof, including attainment.** Let

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}a&m/2\\m/2&b\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its top eigenvalue `rho` has a unit eigenvector `(alpha,beta_0)` with both
entries positive. Put `C=alpha B_1+beta_0 B_0`. Scalar Cauchy--Schwarz and
the two-eigenvalue arithmetic--geometric mean inequality give

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm end}(D)\ge(\operatorname{tr}CD)^2
\ge4\det C=4\rho.
\]

At `D_*=sqrt(rho) C^{-1}`, the determinant is one and

\[
\operatorname{tr}(B_1D_*)
=\frac{2a\alpha+m\beta_0}{\sqrt\rho}=2\sqrt\rho\alpha,
\]

with the analogous identity for `B_0`. Both inequalities are equalities.
The explicit formula for the top eigenvalue gives (6). No commutativity
assumption is used. This generalizes the determinant-equal case in 016.

For the compressed matrices (5), define

\[
m=\frac{z^*K(I-K)z-xq-w(1-q)}{\sqrt{xw}}.
\tag{7}
\]

Then the exact endpoint minimum is

\[
\boxed{\min_{D>0,\det D=1}\mathcal I_{\rm end}(D)
=2\left(1+\sqrt{(2q-1)^2+m^2}\right).}
\tag{8}
\]

To verify (7), the mixed determinant of the two compressed adjugates equals

\[
z^*\left(K(I-K)-xK-w(I-K)\right)z.
\]

This is a covariant matrix identity. In an eigenbasis of `K`, its `i`th
diagonal entry is
`lambda_i(1-lambda_i)(lambda_j+lambda_k-2 lambda_j lambda_k)`, exactly the
mixed determinant of the two complementary diagonal adjugate blocks.
Covariance proves the identity in every basis. This verification rotates
only a matrix identity; it never replaces the measured subset law.

## 3. Exact obstruction to using only endpoints

Let `e=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3)` and, for `0 < epsilon < 1/2`, set

\[
K_\varepsilon=\varepsilon I+(1/2-\varepsilon)ee^*,\qquad
z=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2,\qquad
A=I-zz^*=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/2&0\\1/2&1/2&0\\0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]

The eigenvalues of `K_epsilon` are `1/2,epsilon,epsilon`. The direction
has eigenvalues `1,1,0`, is supported on a non-coordinate plane, commutes
with `K_epsilon`, and changes all three coordinate diagonals. There is a
nontrivial feasible open interval about `t=0` for `K_epsilon+tA`.

The atom probabilities, uniform within each cardinality, are

\[
p_0=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^2}{2},\quad
p_i=\frac{1-\varepsilon^2}{6},\quad
p_{ij}=\frac{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}6,\quad
p_{123}=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}.
\]

Here `q=epsilon`, and (4) simplifies to

\[
\beta=(1-\varepsilon)
\log\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^2}{3\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}
+\varepsilon\log\frac{(2-\varepsilon)^2}{3(1-\varepsilon^2)}.
\tag{10}
\]

For the displayed direction,

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm end}=5-4\varepsilon+4\varepsilon^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus `I_end -> 5` while `beta ~ log(1/(6 epsilon)) -> infinity`.
No fixed finite multiple of endpoint Fisher can dominate the logarithmic
curvature for this family. The exact *minimum* (8) is also bounded: here
`m=1`, so it tends to `2(1+sqrt 2)`.

An entirely rational example is `epsilon=1/100`:

\[
K=\frac1{300}\begin{pmatrix}52&49&49\\49&52&49\\49&49&52\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For it,

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm end}=\frac{12401}{2500}<5,
\qquad
\beta=\frac{99}{100}\log\frac{10201}{597}
+\frac1{100}\log\frac{39601}{29997}.
\]

Since `10201/597 > 16`, `39601/29997 > 1`, and `log 2 > 2/3`,

\[
\beta>\frac{99}{100}\log16
>\frac{66}{25},\qquad
2\beta>\frac{132}{25}>5>\mathcal I_{\rm end}.
\tag{12}
\]

The elementary logarithm bound follows, for example, by integrating the
positive series for `2 atanh(1/3)` and retaining its first term; its omitted
terms are strictly positive. Thus (12) is an exact certificate, not a
floating-point sign check.

The full Fisher objective in this same direction is
`19161514366/414541875`, approximately `46.2233504540`. The logarithmic
curvature is approximately `5.6254365021`, and the entropy Hessian is
approximately `-40.5979139520`. The example refutes only the endpoint-only
proof strategy.

## 4. Exact full Fisher minimum on a fixed arbitrary plane

Unlike the endpoint bound, the following calculation retains all eight
atoms and actually minimizes the **entire** Fisher objective.

For every atom, form the Hermitian `2 x 2` matrix

\[
C_S=\epsilon_S U^*\operatorname{adj}(N_S)U.
\]

Then `p_S'=tr(C_S D)`. Let `sigma_0=I_2` and let
`sigma_1,sigma_2,sigma_3` be the usual Hermitian Pauli matrices, with
`sigma_2=[[0,-i],[i,0]]`. Put

\[
b_{S\mu}=\operatorname{tr}(C_S\sigma_\mu)\in\mathbb R,
\qquad
G_{\mu\nu}=\sum_S\frac{b_{S\mu}b_{S\nu}}{p_S}.
\]

Write

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}g_{00}&g^T\\g&B\end{pmatrix}\ge0,
\qquad J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1).
\]

Every Hermitian `D` is uniquely `D=s I+x dot sigma`, and

\[
\det D=s^2-|x|^2,\qquad
\mathcal I_K(UDU^*)=(s,x)^TG(s,x).
\]

Positive determinant-one directions correspond precisely to the future
unit hyperboloid `s>0`, `s^2-|x|^2=1`.

### The scalar equation and its attained optimizer

Define, for `lambda>0`,

\[
F(\lambda)=g_{00}-\lambda-g^T(B+\lambda I_3)^{-1}g.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\min_{D>0,\det D=1}\mathcal I_K(UDU^*)
=\lambda_*,\quad F(\lambda_*)=0,}
\tag{13}
\]

where `lambda_*` is the **unique positive root**. Equivalently it is the
unique positive root of

\[
(g_{00}-\lambda)\det(B+\lambda I_3)
-g^T\operatorname{adj}(B+\lambda I_3)g=0.
\tag{14}
\]

This is an explicit quartic; no minimization over positive `D` remains.

**Proof of existence, uniqueness, and attainment.** Let
`V=Var_K(|X|)=tr K(I-K)>0`. The score identity for the statistic `|X|` gives,
for every Hermitian `D`,

\[
(\operatorname{tr}D)^2
=\left(\sum_Sp_S'(|S|-\mathbb E|X|)\right)^2
\le V\mathcal I_K(UDU^*).
\]

Thus `G >= (4/V) e_0 e_0^T`. In particular, `g` lies in the range of `B`,
and the Schur complement at zero obeys
`F(0+)=g_00-g^T B^dagger g >= 4/V > 0`.
Also `F(lambda) -> -infinity`, and

\[
F''(\lambda)=-2g^T(B+\lambda I)^{-3}g\le0.
\]

Continuity and concavity imply exactly one positive root. Its derivative
is strictly negative: the left chord from zero already has negative slope.
Set

\[
r=-(B+\lambda_*I)^{-1}g,\quad
s_*=(1-|r|^2)^{-1/2},\quad
D_*=s_*(I+r\cdot\sigma).
\]

Since `F'(lambda_*)=-1+|r|^2<0`, this is well defined, positive definite,
and has determinant one. The Schur-complement criterion gives

\[
G-\lambda_*J\ge0,
\]

with null vector `(1,r)`. Therefore every determinant-one positive `D`
has Fisher at least `lambda_*`, and `D_*` attains equality. This proves
(13), including singular `B` and complex off-diagonal components of `D`.

### The precise remaining inequality

When `beta=0`, entropy concavity is immediate. When `beta>0`, (3) and (13)
show that concavity for **every positive direction on this fixed plane** is
equivalent to either of the following explicit statements:

\[
\boxed{
g_{00}-2\beta\ \ge\ g^T(B+2\beta I_3)^{-1}g.
}
\tag{15}
\]

\[
G-2\beta J\ge0.
\tag{16}
\]

Equation (15) is the specific open inequality left by this attack. Its
coefficients are the eight explicit fixed-coordinate atom probabilities and
their first derivatives; `beta` is given by (1) or (4). It is not asserted
to hold universally. A failure would construct an actual entropy
counterexample direction: take `D_*` from (13), for which
`H''=2 beta-lambda_*>0`.

There is also a proved easy region. The score estimate gives
`lambda_* >= 4/V`, so (15) holds whenever `beta <= 2/V`; in particular it
holds if `beta <= 8/3`, because `V <= 3/4`. This does not settle the
boundary regime: in the family (9), `beta` is unbounded while `2/V -> 8`.

## 5. A new exact restricted case: uniform atoms within cardinality

Suppose, at the **base kernel** `K`, that

\[
p_S(K)=\frac{P_{|S|}(K)}{\binom3{|S|}},\qquad
P_j(K)=\mathbb P_K(|X|=j).
\tag{17}
\]

This includes every strict contraction `K=a I+b 11*` with real `a,b`,
and their diagonal-unitary gauges. No classification of all kernels
satisfying (17) is needed.

Let `A` now be any Hermitian direction, not necessarily rank two. Define

\[
I_{\rm card}=\sum_{j=0}^3\frac{(P_j')^2}{P_j},\qquad
e_2(A)=\tfrac12\big((\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\big).
\]

**Exact identity at the base (17):**

\[
\boxed{
H_{\rm full}''=H_{\rm card}''
-(I_{\rm full}-I_{\rm card})-2e_2(A)\log3.
}
\tag{18}
\]

**Proof.** At the base, substitute `log p_S=log P_|S|-log binom(3,|S|)`
into the entropy Hessian. Summing second derivatives within each level
gives

\[
H_{\rm full}''=H_{\rm card}''-(I_{\rm full}-I_{\rm card})
+(P_1''+P_2'')\log3.
\]

The three-dimensional determinant identity gives

\[
P_3''=2\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}A),\quad
P_0''=2\operatorname{tr}((I-K)\operatorname{adj}A).
\]

Hence `P_0''+P_3''=2 tr adj(A)=2e_2(A)`. Normalization supplies
`P_1''+P_2''=-2e_2(A)`, proving (18). This proof uses (17) only at the
base; it does **not** assume the path preserves exchangeability.

For each cardinality, scalar Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_{|S|=j}\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S}
\ge\frac{(P_j')^2}{P_j},
\]

so `I_full-I_card >= 0`. Cardinality entropy is concave in `K` by the
established Shepp--Olkin theorem and spectral majorization, recalled below.
Therefore (18) proves

\[
\boxed{H_{\rm full}''\le-2e_2(A)\log3\quad\text{if }e_2(A)\ge0.}
\tag{19}
\]

For `A=UDU*` positive rank two, `e_2(A)=det D`, giving the strict bound
announced above for **every complex orientation**. More generally (19)
also covers all positive-semidefinite `3 x 3` directions.

This is a local Hessian theorem at the kernels satisfying (17). It proves
finite segment concavity only when the whole segment remains in that
family (or when another argument controls the other base points). It does
not prove the requested full rank-two theorem at arbitrary kernels.

### Established cardinality input, with the spectral step repaired

Let `h(lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3)` be the entropy of the sum of independent
Bernoulli variables with those success probabilities. The Shepp--Olkin
theorem says that `h` is concave. It is symmetric, hence Schur-concave.
The Ky Fan variational principle gives

\[
\lambda((1-t)K_0+tK_1)
\prec(1-t)\lambda(K_0)+t\lambda(K_1),
\]

where the eigenvalues on the right are sorted compatibly. Schur-concavity
followed by ordinary concavity yields

\[
h(\lambda((1-t)K_0+tK_1))
\ge h((1-t)\lambda(K_0)+t\lambda(K_1))
\ge(1-t)h(\lambda(K_0))+t h(\lambda(K_1)).
\]

Finally, the DPP count generating function is
`det(I-K+sK)=product_i(1-lambda_i+s lambda_i)`, so this is exactly
cardinality entropy concavity. This avoids the problematic displayed
intermediate inequality in the Gu report noted in `TARGET.md`.
