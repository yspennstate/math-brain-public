All logarithms are natural; a different logarithm base multiplies every entropy and curvature by the same positive constant. Write
\[
h(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u),\qquad 0\log0=0.
\]
For a Hermitian contraction \(0\preceq K\preceq I_n\), let \(P_K\) denote its determinantal subset law, characterized by
\[
\Pr_{P_K}(S\subseteq X)=\det K_S.
\]
Its Shannon entropy is \(H(P_K)=-\sum_xP_K(x)\log P_K(x)\).

## 1. Statements

### Theorem A: All diagonal references, including boundary entries

Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\) with \(0\le d_i\le1\), and let \(K\) be any Hermitian contraction. Then
\[
t\longmapsto H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})
\]
is concave on \([0,1]\), and is strictly concave if and only if \(K\ne D\). If \(K\ne D\), its second derivative is strictly negative at every \(0<t<1\). This remains true when the law has zero atoms.

Put \(\delta_i=K_{ii}-d_i\) and \(m_i(t)=d_i+t\delta_i\). On \(0<t<1\),
\[
-\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})
\ge
\sum_i\frac{\delta_i^2}{m_i(t)(1-m_i(t))}.
\tag{A1}
\]
A term is defined to be zero when its marginal is identically zero or identically one. Every other denominator in (A1) is positive. In particular, for \(0\le u<v\le1\), \(0<\lambda<1\), and \(w=(1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_{(1-w)D+wK})
\ge{}&(1-\lambda)H(P_{(1-u)D+uK})
+\lambda H(P_{(1-v)D+vK})\\
&+2\lambda(1-\lambda)(v-u)^2\sum_i\delta_i^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A2}
\]

More generally, for any Hermitian \(A\), define the entire feasible parameter set
\[
J=\{s\in\mathbb R:0\preceq D+sA\preceq I_n\}.
\]
Then \(s\mapsto H(P_{D+sA})\) is concave on \(J\). If \(A\ne0\) and \(J\) has at least two points, it is strictly concave on \(J\). If \(A=0\), it is constant. A singleton \(J=\{0\}\) has no nontrivial concavity chord. Thus a boundary diagonal reference does not require a two-sided feasible line.

### Theorem B: Co-monotone affine product noise

Fix a product reference
\[
\mu=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Bern}(d_i),\qquad 0<d_i<1,
\]
and any input law \(P\) on \(\{0,1\}^n\). Let
\[
r_i(t)=a_i+b_i t\in[0,1]\qquad(\alpha\le t\le\beta,\ \alpha<\beta)
\]
be affine retention probabilities. Assume all nonzero \(b_i\) have the same sign. Independently at each coordinate, retain the input bit with probability \(r_i(t)\), and otherwise redraw it from \(\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\). Let \(P_t\) be the output law.

Then:
1. \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t\Vert\mu)\) is convex in \(t\).
2. \(H(P_t)\) is concave in \(t\).
3. Both assertions are strict on every nontrivial parameter chord if and only if the output law \(P_t\) is nonconstant. More precisely, when the output law is nonconstant,
   \[
   \frac{d^2}{dt^2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_t\Vert\mu)>0,
   \qquad
   \frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_t)<0
   \quad(\alpha<t<\beta).
   \]
4. Retentions equal to zero or one at endpoints are allowed. Coordinates whose retention is identically zero or identically one are also allowed, as are zero atoms of the input and output laws.

For a DPP input \(P=P_K\), the output is \(P_{M(t)}\), where
\[
\boxed{
M(t)=\operatorname{diag}((1-r_i(t))d_i)
+R(t)^{1/2}KR(t)^{1/2},\qquad
R(t)=\operatorname{diag}(r_i(t)).
}
\tag{B1}
\]
This kernel curve is generally not contained in an affine kernel line.

There is an explicit equality condition. Define
\[
\mathcal A=\{i:b_i\ne0\},\qquad
\mathcal Z=\{i:r_i(t)\equiv0\},\qquad
\mathcal W=[n]\setminus\mathcal Z.
\]
The DPP output law, and equivalently the matrix curve \(M(t)\), is constant exactly when
\[
\boxed{
K_{ii}=d_i\ \text{for every }i\in\mathcal A,\qquad
K_{ij}=0\ \text{for every }i\in\mathcal A,\
j\in\mathcal W\setminus\{i\}.
}
\tag{B2}
\]
Absent these conditions, \(H(P_{M(t)})\) is strictly concave. Correlations with permanently redrawn coordinates in \(\mathcal Z\) do not enter (B2).

## 2. The full weighted product-noise entropy identity

Write \(f=P/\mu\), so \(f\ge0\) and \(\mathbb E_\mu f=1\). Let \(E_i\) average coordinate \(i\) with its reference marginal, leaving all other coordinates fixed, and put \(D_i=I-E_i\). These operators commute and
\[
E_i^2=E_i,\quad D_i^2=D_i,\quad E_iD_i=D_iE_i=0.
\]
The one-coordinate transition
\[
Q_{i,r}(x,y)=r\mathbf1_{\{x=y\}}+(1-r)\mu_i(y)
\]
satisfies detailed balance
\[
\mu_i(x)Q_{i,r}(x,y)=\mu_i(y)Q_{i,r}(y,x).
\]
Consequently the output density relative to \(\mu\) is
\[
g_t=\frac{P_t}{\mu}
=N_{r(t)}f,\qquad
N_r=\prod_i(E_i+r_iD_i).
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is an identity for densities, justified by reversibility; the averages \(E_i\) are taken under the fixed product reference.

For \(i\in\mathcal A\), one has \(0<r_i(t)<1\) throughout the open parameter interval. Indeed, a nonconstant affine function taking values in \([0,1]\) cannot attain either boundary value in the interior. Define
\[
c_i(t)=\frac{b_i}{r_i(t)}\qquad(i\in\mathcal A).
\]
Only active indices are used in expressions involving \(c_i\); thus a constant zero retention causes no division by zero.

Because all factors commute and
\[
D_i(E_i+r_iD_i)=r_iD_i,
\]
differentiating (2.1) gives
\[
g_t'=\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}c_iD_i g_t.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Moreover \(c_i'=-c_i^2\), so a second differentiation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_t''
&=-\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}c_i^2D_i g_t
+\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal A}c_ic_jD_iD_jg_t\\
&=2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in\mathcal A}}
c_ic_jD_iD_jg_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]
The diagonal terms cancel by \(D_i^2=D_i\). In particular, the term involving the second derivative of the density has not been omitted.

### Fixed support in the presence of untouched coordinates

Let
\[
\mathcal U=\{i:r_i(t)\equiv1\},\qquad
\mathcal V=[n]\setminus\mathcal U.
\]
Every \(i\in\mathcal V\) has \(1-r_i(t)>0\) on the open interval. Put
\[
q(x_{\mathcal U})=E_{\mathcal V}f(x_{\mathcal U}),
\qquad
E_{\mathcal V}=\prod_{i\in\mathcal V}E_i.
\]
The term in which all coordinates of \(\mathcal V\) are redrawn yields
\[
g_t(x)\ge
\left(\prod_{i\in\mathcal V}(1-r_i(t))\right)q(x_{\mathcal U}).
\tag{2.4}
\]
If \(q(x_{\mathcal U})=0\), nonnegativity of \(f\) and full support of \(\mu_{\mathcal V}\) imply that the entire input fiber at that \(x_{\mathcal U}\) is zero, and hence the entire output fiber is zero for every \(t\). If \(q(x_{\mathcal U})>0\), (2.4) gives strict positivity at every point of that fiber.

Thus the support of \(g_t\) is independent of \(t\) in the open interval. On supported \(\mathcal U\)-fibers it is strictly positive. Each \(D_i\) with \(i\in\mathcal A\) preserves these fibers. On unsupported fibers, \(g_t\), its derivatives, and all relevant \(D_i g_t,D_iD_jg_t\) vanish.

All following quotients and logarithmic products are evaluated on the supported fibers and are assigned value zero on the unsupported fibers. This convention is justified by the identically zero summands of the original entropy sum, rather than by differentiation of \(\log0\).

Let
\[
F(t)=\mathbb E_\mu[g_t\log g_t].
\]
Normalization gives \(\mathbb E_\mu g_t'=\mathbb E_\mu g_t''=0\). Finite differentiation on the fixed support therefore gives
\[
F''(t)=\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(g_t')^2}{g_t}
+\mathbb E_\mu[g_t''\log g_t].
\]
Using (2.2)--(2.3), with \(g=g_t\),
\[
\boxed{
F''(t)=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}c_i^2\,
\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_i g)^2}{g}
+
2\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in\mathcal A}}
c_ic_j B_{ij}(g),
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
where
\[
B_{ij}(g)=
\mathbb E_\mu\frac{D_i gD_jg}{g}
+\mathbb E_\mu[(D_iD_jg)\log g].
\tag{2.6}
\]

### The biased two-coordinate bracket

Condition on all coordinates other than active \(i,j\). On a supported fiber write
\[
a=g(0,0),\quad b=g(0,1),\quad c=g(1,0),\quad e=g(1,1),
\]
all strictly positive, and put \(p=d_i,q=d_j\), \(w=p(1-p)q(1-q)\).

The conditional contribution from \(D_i gD_jg/g\), divided by \(w\), is
\[
\frac{(a-c)(a-b)}a+
\frac{(b-e)(b-a)}b+
\frac{(c-a)(c-e)}c+
\frac{(e-b)(e-c)}e
=
\frac{bc}{a}+\frac{ae}{b}+\frac{ae}{c}+\frac{bc}{e}-(a+b+c+e).
\]
The four reference-weighted values of \(D_iD_jg\) are
\[
w(a-b-c+e)\,(+1,-1,-1,+1)
\]
in the order \(00,01,10,11\). Their logarithmic contribution is therefore
\[
w(a+e-b-c)\log\frac{ae}{bc}.
\]
Set \(z=ae/(bc)>0\), \(X=a+e>0\), \(Y=b+c>0\). The full fiber bracket equals
\[
\boxed{
w\left[X(z^{-1}-1+\log z)+Y(z-1-\log z)\right]\ge0.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
Indeed \(bc/a+bc/e=X/z\), \(ae/b+ae/c=zY\), and both scalar factors are nonnegative by \(\log u\le u-1\), applied to \(u=z\) and \(u=z^{-1}\). Equality in a supported fiber occurs exactly when \(ae=bc\). Unsupported fibers contribute zero. Averaging proves
\[
B_{ij}(g)\ge0.
\tag{2.8}
\]

All \(c_i\) have the same sign. Hence every coefficient \(c_ic_j\) in (2.5) is nonnegative, and (2.5) proves \(F''\ge0\).

### Exact strictness for the arbitrary input law

Define the fixed preprocessing
\[
h_0=\prod_{i\notin\mathcal A}(E_i+r_iD_i)f,
\]
where the retentions in this product are constant. Then
\[
g_t=\prod_{i\in\mathcal A}(E_i+r_i(t)D_i)h_0.
\tag{2.9}
\]
For an interior parameter, every active factor is invertible as a linear operator:
\[
(E_i+r_iD_i)^{-1}=E_i+r_i^{-1}D_i.
\]
If \(F''(t_0)=0\), the nonnegative square terms in (2.5), with \(c_i(t_0)\ne0\), force
\[
D_i g_{t_0}=0\quad\text{for every }i\in\mathcal A.
\]
This conclusion holds on all fibers, including the identically zero ones. The active factors commute with every \(D_i\), so their invertibility in (2.9) gives
\[
D_i h_0=0\quad\text{for every }i\in\mathcal A.
\tag{2.10}
\]
Each active noise factor then fixes \(h_0\), even at endpoint retentions zero or one. Thus \(g_t=h_0\) for the entire closed interval.

Conversely, if the output law is constant then \(F''=0\). We have proved
\[
F''(t_0)=0\text{ at one interior point}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_t\text{ is constant on the whole interval}.
\tag{2.11}
\]
The empty-active-set case is also constant. Therefore a nonconstant output law has \(F''(t)>0\) at every interior parameter. This is why the condition is not simply \(P\ne\mu\).

An equivalent input-only criterion is
\[
D_iE_{\mathcal Z}f=0\quad(i\in\mathcal A).
\tag{2.12}
\]
To see this, factor the fixed preprocessing into \(E_{\mathcal Z}\) followed by the factors with constant positive retention. The latter are invertible and commute with \(D_i\). In probabilistic language, the input marginal on \(\mathcal W\) must factor as \(\mu_{\mathcal A}\) times a law on \(\mathcal W\setminus\mathcal A\).

### Shannon entropy and endpoint extension

Let \(p_i=P(X_i=1)\), \(\Delta_i=p_i-d_i\). The output marginals are
\[
m_i(t)=d_i+r_i(t)\Delta_i,
\]
which are affine in \(t\). Hence the cross-entropy
\[
C(t)=-\sum_xP_t(x)\log\mu(x)
=-\sum_i\left[m_i(t)\log d_i+(1-m_i(t))\log(1-d_i)\right]
\tag{2.13}
\]
is affine. The identity
\[
H(P_t)=C(t)-F(t)
\tag{2.14}
\]
proves concavity, and strict negative curvature exactly for a nonconstant output law.

Every output atom is a polynomial in the retentions, and \(z\log z\) is continuous at zero. Thus both entropies extend continuously to \(\alpha,\beta\), and their convexity/concavity inequalities extend by limits. Strict curvature throughout the open interval rules out every nontrivial affine subinterval; equality on a convexity or concavity chord would force such an affine subinterval. Therefore strictness includes endpoint chords. This proves Theorem B for arbitrary input laws, including all the stated support and retention boundary cases.

## 3. Quantitative marginal curvature

For \(i\in\mathcal A\), its output marginal lies in \((0,1)\) at an interior parameter. Product averaging gives
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathbb E_\mu[g\mid X_i=1]=m_i/d_i,
&
\mathbb E_\mu[D_i g\mid X_i=1]=(m_i-d_i)/d_i,
\\[2mm]
\mathbb E_\mu[g\mid X_i=0]=(1-m_i)/(1-d_i),
&
\mathbb E_\mu[D_i g\mid X_i=0]=-(m_i-d_i)/(1-d_i).
\end{array}
\]
Here \(\mathbb E_\mu[E_i g\mid X_i]=1\), because \(E_i g\) is independent of \(X_i\) and has total reference average one.

Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, followed by averaging \(X_i\), yields
\[
\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_i g)^2}{g}
\ge
\frac{(m_i-d_i)^2}{m_i}
+\frac{(m_i-d_i)^2}{1-m_i}
=
\frac{r_i^2\Delta_i^2}{m_i(1-m_i)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
On zero fibers both numerator and denominator are zero as specified above, so the same finite-sum Cauchy--Schwarz argument applies.

Combining (2.5), (2.8), (2.14), and (3.1) gives
\[
\boxed{
-H''(P_t)\ge
\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{b_i^2\Delta_i^2}{m_i(t)(1-m_i(t))}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\frac{(m_i'(t))^2}{m_i(t)(1-m_i(t))}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Here \(H''(P_t)\) denotes the second derivative of \(t\mapsto H(P_t)\).

Consequently the total correlation
\[
T(t)=\sum_i h(m_i(t))-H(P_t)
\tag{3.3}
\]
is convex. Inactive marginals are constant, including any that are identically zero or one, and make no curvature contribution. This assertion extends to the closed interval by continuity. No strictness claim about total correlation is needed below.

## 4. The determinantal transformation and its equality condition

This section's distributional calculation permits all \(0\le d_i,r_i\le1\); a nondegenerate reference is needed only when invoking the density proof above.

Conditional on the input set \(X\), the output bits are independent with means
\[
r_iX_i+(1-r_i)d_i.
\]
For every subset \(S\),
\[
\Pr(S\subseteq Y)
=
\sum_{T\subseteq S}
\det K_T\,
\prod_{i\in T}r_i
\prod_{i\in S\setminus T}(1-r_i)d_i.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Put \(R=\operatorname{diag}(r_i)\) and
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}((1-r_i)d_i)+R^{1/2}KR^{1/2}.
\]
Expanding \(\det M_S\) in its diagonal summand gives exactly (4.1). A choice of diagonal columns forces the matching rows; deleting these equal row and column index sets has positive cofactor sign. The remaining principal minor is
\[
\det\big((R^{1/2}KR^{1/2})_T\big)
=\det K_T\prod_{i\in T}r_i.
\]
This identity holds even when some \(r_i=0\).

The transformed matrix is a contraction, since
\[
M\succeq0,\qquad
I-M=\operatorname{diag}((1-r_i)(1-d_i))
+R^{1/2}(I-K)R^{1/2}\succeq0.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Inclusion-exclusion determines the full subset law from all inclusion probabilities. Thus the output is exactly \(P_M\), proving (B1). The argument works for complex Hermitian kernels and assumes no commutation of \(K\) with the diagonal matrices.

The atoms are polynomials in the retentions by (4.1) and inclusion-exclusion. Thus possible square-root behavior of individual off-diagonal kernel entries at a zero retention causes no issue for the entropy argument.

To verify (B2), suppose first that the output law is constant. For \(i\in\mathcal A\),
\[
m_i(t)=d_i+r_i(t)(K_{ii}-d_i)
\]
is constant only if \(K_{ii}=d_i\). For \(i\in\mathcal A\) and \(j\in\mathcal W\setminus\{i\}\), the two-site determinant identity gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{M(t)}}(X_i,X_j)
=-r_i(t)r_j(t)|K_{ij}|^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Both retentions are positive at an interior parameter, and
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(r_i r_j)=b_i r_j+b_j r_i
\]
is nonzero: \(b_i\ne0\), while \(b_j\) is either zero or has the same sign as \(b_i\). Constancy of (4.3) therefore forces \(K_{ij}=0\).

Conversely, assume (B2). Every active diagonal entry of \(M(t)\) is the fixed value \(d_i\). All off-diagonal entries incident to an active index vanish: this follows from (B2) for another index in \(\mathcal W\), and from \(r_j=0\) for an index in \(\mathcal Z\). Entries involving only inactive indices are constant. Hence the whole matrix curve is constant. This proves (B2), including equivalence of constant matrix and constant output law.

For example, take \(D=I_2/2\),
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r_1(t)=t,\quad r_2(t)\equiv0.
\]
The output kernel is identically \(D\), although \(K\ne D\) and a retention changes. The correlation with the permanently redrawn coordinate disappears. This is an equality case covered by (B2).

### A curve outside affine kernel lines

Take
\[
D=I_2/2,\qquad
K=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/8\\1/8&1/3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
r_1(t)=t,\quad r_2(t)=\frac{1+t}{2},
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]
The eigenvalues of \(K\) are \(7/24\) and \(17/24\), so it is a strict contraction. The output is
\[
M(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\frac12+\frac t6
&
\frac18\sqrt{\frac{t(1+t)}2}
\\
\frac18\sqrt{\frac{t(1+t)}2}
&
\frac12-\frac{1+t}{12}
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Its first diagonal entry is injectively affine in \(t\), while its off-diagonal entry is not affine. If this curve lay in any affine matrix line, the first diagonal entry would force the line parameter to be affine in \(t\), contradicting the off-diagonal entry. Thus (4.4) is not even a reparametrization of a segment in an affine kernel line. Theorem B proves its entropy strictly concave.

### Why the sign condition is substantive

With \(D=I_2/2\) and \(K\) having diagonal \(1/2\) and off-diagonal \(1/4\), take instead
\[
r_1(t)=t,\qquad r_2(t)=1-t.
\]
The output kernel has diagonal \(1/2\) and off-diagonal \(\sqrt{t(1-t)}/4\). Both endpoint laws are the uniform product law with entropy \(2\log2\). Every interior law is nonuniform with the same marginals: its four probabilities are
\[
\frac14-z,\quad \frac14+z,\quad \frac14+z,\quad \frac14-z,
\qquad z=\frac{t(1-t)}{16}>0.
\]
Its entropy is strictly less than \(2\log2\). The parameterized entropy is therefore not concave. Mixed signs cannot be added to Theorem B without further conditions.

## 5. Strict chords from boundary diagonal kernels

First take an interior diagonal \(D\). Uniform retention \(r_i(t)=t\) makes (B1)
\[
M(t)=(1-t)D+tK.
\]
Its law is constant if and only if \(K=D\), either by (B2), or directly from its one- and two-site probabilities. Thus the product-noise proof already establishes Theorem A, including strict interior curvature and (A1), for an interior diagonal reference.

Now allow \(0\le d_i\le1\), and, for \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), set
\[
D_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)D+\varepsilon I_n,
\qquad
M_\varepsilon(t)=(1-t)D_\varepsilon+tK.
\]
Each \(D_\varepsilon\) is an interior diagonal contraction. For every fixed \(t\), its atom probabilities and entropy converge to those for
\[
M(t)=(1-t)D+tK.
\]
Passing to the limit in each concavity inequality proves that \(H(P_{M(t)})\) is concave on the entire closed interval.

Strictness does not follow from this limit alone. We retain quantitative information as follows. Let
\[
m_{i,\varepsilon}(t)=(M_\varepsilon(t))_{ii},\qquad
T_\varepsilon(t)=\sum_i h(m_{i,\varepsilon}(t))-H(P_{M_\varepsilon(t)}).
\]
By (3.2)--(3.3), \(T_\varepsilon\) is convex on \([0,1]\). Its pointwise limit is
\[
T(t)=\sum_i h(m_i(t))-H(P_{M(t)}),
\tag{5.1}
\]
so \(T\) is convex on \([0,1]\).

To justify differentiating (5.1) at an interior parameter even with boundary \(D\), use the resampling law calculation, which remains valid for degenerate reference marginals. For each exact output configuration \(y\),
\[
P_{M(t)}(y)=
\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}
t^{|S|}(1-t)^{n-|S|}
P_K(X_S=y_S)\prod_{i\notin S}\mu_i(y_i),
\tag{5.2}
\]
where \(\mu_i=\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\), possibly degenerate. Every coefficient in (5.2) is nonnegative. Hence an atom either vanishes identically on \(0<t<1\), or is strictly positive throughout that open interval. The support is constant there, and entropy is real analytic on this support. Each marginal is either strictly between zero and one on the open interval, or identically zero or one. Therefore (5.1) is twice differentiable there.

Since a twice differentiable convex function has nonnegative second derivative, \(T''(t)\ge0\). Substituting \(h''(u)=-1/[u(1-u)]\) proves (A1), with the stated zero convention for fixed deterministic marginals.

If some \(\delta_i\ne0\), its marginal is interior on \(0<t<1\), and (A1) is strictly positive. Thus \(H''(P_{M(t)})<0\) throughout the open interval. Moreover \(m_i(1-m_i)\le1/4\) gives
\[
H''(P_{M(t)})\le-4\sum_i\delta_i^2.
\]
It follows that
\[
H(P_{M(t)})+2t^2\sum_i\delta_i^2
\]
is concave; continuity extends this to \([0,1]\). Its chord inequality is exactly (A2).

It remains to prove strictness when all \(\delta_i=0\) but \(K\ne D\). Put
\[
\mathcal B=\{i:d_i\in\{0,1\}\},\qquad
\mathcal I=[n]\setminus\mathcal B.
\]
For a positive semidefinite matrix \(L\), its two-by-two principal minors imply
\[
|L_{ij}|^2\le L_{ii}L_{jj}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
If \(d_i=K_{ii}=0\), apply (5.3) to \(K\) to conclude \(K_{ij}=0\) for every \(j\ne i\). If \(d_i=K_{ii}=1\), apply it to \(I-K\) to reach the same conclusion. Hence
\[
K=D_{\mathcal B}\oplus K_{\mathcal I},\qquad
D=D_{\mathcal B}\oplus D_{\mathcal I}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Coordinates in \(\mathcal B\) are deterministic and do not contribute entropy; the remaining law is the DPP with kernel
\[
(1-t)D_{\mathcal I}+tK_{\mathcal I}.
\]
The reference \(D_{\mathcal I}\) is interior. Since \(K\ne D\), this block is nontrivial and \(K_{\mathcal I}\ne D_{\mathcal I}\). The already proved interior-reference result therefore gives strict negative second derivative for every \(0<t<1\).

This covers all \(K\ne D\). Strict curvature and continuity give strict concavity on every nontrivial closed-interval chord, including chords with singular endpoints. If \(K=D\), the entropy is constant. The chord portion of Theorem A is proved.

## 6. The entire feasible line, with exact degeneracy cases

For any diagonal \(D\) with entries in \([0,1]\), feasibility is closed and convex in \(s\), so \(J\) is a closed interval containing zero. If \(A=0\), \(J=\mathbb R\) and entropy is constant. If \(A\ne0\), every feasible \(s\) satisfies
\[
|s|\|A\|
\le\|D+sA\|+\|D\|
\le1+\|D\|,
\]
so \(J\) is bounded.

We first give the two-sided gluing argument for an interior diagonal reference, to keep the proof independent of an unstated extension. If all \(0<d_i<1\) and \(A\ne0\), the interval is
\[
J=[s_-,s_+],\qquad s_-<0<s_+,
\]
because sufficiently small perturbations in either direction remain strict contractions. For \(\sigma=s_-\) or \(s_+\), set \(K_\sigma=D+\sigma A\). On the corresponding half,
\[
D+sA=(1-s/\sigma)D+(s/\sigma)K_\sigma,
\qquad 0\le s/\sigma\le1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Each half is strictly concave by the chord theorem. The factor in the second derivative under this affine change of parameter is \(1/\sigma^2>0\), even on the negative half.

At \(s=0\), the law is the full-support product law \(P_D\). Its atom probabilities are polynomials in \(s\), so entropy is real analytic near zero. In particular the two derivatives agree; explicitly,
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}H(P_{D+sA})\right|_{s=0}
=
\sum_iA_{ii}\log\frac{1-d_i}{d_i}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
One may obtain (6.2) from the affine cross-entropy (2.13); the relative-entropy derivative at a density equal to one is zero by normalization.

The derivative is nonincreasing on each half and agrees at zero. Thus it is nonincreasing across the union, proving concavity on the full interval. Every nontrivial subinterval contains an open subinterval away from zero where the second derivative is strictly negative, so no nontrivial interval is affine. This proves strict concavity for the entire interior-reference line, including intervals crossing zero.

Now return to a possibly boundary diagonal \(D\), with \(A\ne0\).

If \(J=\{0\}\), there is no nontrivial chord, and the concavity assertion is immediate. This case really can occur: for
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the determinant of \(D+sA\) is \(-s^2\), so feasibility forces \(s=0\).

If \(J\) lies on one side of zero and has positive length, zero is one endpoint. Let \(\sigma\ne0\) be its other endpoint. Formula (6.1), now using the boundary diagonal chord theorem, identifies the entire interval with one strictly concave chord from \(D\) to \(D+\sigma A\). There is no gluing at the boundary point to justify.

Finally suppose \(J\) contains both positive and negative parameters. For a boundary index \(i\), feasibility at both signs forces \(A_{ii}=0\):
- if \(d_i=0\), the condition \(sA_{ii}\ge0\) at both signs gives \(A_{ii}=0\);
- if \(d_i=1\), the condition \(-sA_{ii}\ge0\) at both signs gives the same conclusion.

Choose any nonzero feasible \(s\). If \(d_i=0\), the zero diagonal entry of \(D+sA\succeq0\) forces its whole off-diagonal row to vanish by (5.3), hence \(A_{ij}=0\) for \(j\ne i\). If \(d_i=1\), apply the same argument to \(I-D-sA\succeq0\). Thus all boundary rows and columns of \(A\) vanish:
\[
D+sA=D_{\mathcal B}\oplus(D_{\mathcal I}+sA_{\mathcal I}).
\tag{6.3}
\]
The first block is deterministic. The second reference is interior, and \(A_{\mathcal I}\ne0\) since \(A\ne0\). The feasible interval and entropy are exactly those of the second block, so the proved interior full-line result applies.

These cases exhaust the feasible interval. Equivalently, any nontrivial two-sided line through a boundary diagonal kernel must leave every boundary coordinate isolated and deterministic; every remaining nontrivial case is a one-sided chord. This proves the full feasible-line assertion of Theorem A.

## 7. Scope and proof provenance

The co-monotone theorem concerns affine retention vectors, whose determinantal kernel entries generally vary nonlinearly. Its sign restriction is necessary for a universal statement, as the explicit opposite-slope example shows. Its equality criterion concerns the actual output curve, including coordinates erased by zero retention.

The diagonal theorem now allows every diagonal contraction, including entries zero and one, and covers its complete feasible line with the singleton, one-sided, and two-sided cases distinguished. Strictness at a degenerate reference was proved using marginal curvature or deterministic-coordinate reduction; it was not inferred from a strict inequality by a limiting argument.

The baseline unweighted Hessian and two-coordinate factorization were read from the two source files identified above. This note supplies the full weighted derivative identity, fixed-support justification, exact co-monotone equality condition, boundary strictness argument, and full-line degeneracy analysis. No numerical experiment or unverified general entropy-curvature principle is used. No unrestricted entropy-concavity conclusion for arbitrary affine segments between nondiagonal kernels follows from these results.
