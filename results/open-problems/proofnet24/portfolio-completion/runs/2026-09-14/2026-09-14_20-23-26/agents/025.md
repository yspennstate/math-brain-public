## Result and scope

**PROVED HERE, subject to the displayed hypotheses:** Let the critical, nondegenerate offspring law satisfy a uniform upper local probability estimate and a matching central lower estimate on a positive, nondecreasing **doubling** scale. For every fixed generation $k\ge1$ and every $0<r<1$, the condition $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$ implies uniform integrability of $Z_k(T_n)^r$. An explicit uniform tail estimate is proved below.

This includes offspring laws in the domain of attraction of an $\alpha$-stable law, $1<\alpha\le2$, with arbitrary slowly varying normalizations. It therefore includes a substantial infinite-variance class. Within that class the offspring moment condition is necessary and sufficient, already for the root degree.

**NOT CLAIMED:** a solution for all finite-mean critical laws, a lacunary counterexample to Janson's conjecture, or novelty/publication clearance. The unrestricted case remains open in this report. The result is a completed conditional theorem and a standard-class corollary, not a proof that its local estimates hold universally.

The useful fractional-moment point is that a negative power of a positive generation size is controllable after conditioning on an earlier large generation. Extinction must be excluded explicitly; writing an unqualified negative moment would be wrong. A weighted version below avoids negative powers entirely by defining the weight to be zero at zero.

Let $\xi$ be a nondegenerate random variable in $\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, with law $\pi$ and $\mathbb E\xi=1$. Write

\[
S_m=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_m,\qquad
q_n=\mathbb P(S_n=n-1),\qquad
\mathcal A=\{n\ge1:q_n>0\}.
\]

Only $n\in\mathcal A$ are used for conditioning. By the cycle lemma, $\mathbb P(|T|=n)=q_n/n$, so this is exactly the admissible set of tree sizes. All upper estimates below hold for every positive integer sample size, while lower estimates are required only on $\mathcal A$. This handles lattice periodicity without silently inserting impossible conditioning events.

Let $T$ be the ordinary critical Galton–Watson tree, $T_n$ its law conditioned on $|T|=n$, $Z_j=Z_j(T)$, and $Z_0=1$. For fixed $k\ge1$, put

\[
N=N_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad Z=Z_k,\qquad m=n-N.
\]

For a compatible truncation with $Z\ge1$, necessarily $m\ge Z\ge1$. The exact weight supplied by the forest cycle lemma is

\[
W_n=\frac{nZ}{m}\,
\frac{\mathbb P(S_m=m-Z)}{q_n}.
\tag{1}
\]

Set $W_n=0$ for incompatible positive-generation truncations. On $Z=0$, a whole tree of size $n$ can have positive conditional probability, so (1) with a zero right side is **not** asserted to be the full Radon–Nikodym derivative there. The precise identity needed is only

\[
\mathbb E\big[g(Z_k(T_n));Z_k(T_n)>0\big]
=\mathbb E\big[g(Z)W_n;Z>0\big]
\tag{2}
\]

for nonnegative $g$. This avoids every $0/0$, zero times infinity, and extinction ambiguity.

## 2. A precisely stated local condition

Assume there are constants $a_1>0$, $D\ge1$, $c>0$, $C<\infty$, and a sequence of finite positive real numbers $a_m$, $m\ge1$, such that

\[
a_m\text{ is nondecreasing},\qquad a_{2m}\le D a_m\quad(m\ge1),
\tag{L1}
\]

\[
q_n\ge\frac{c}{a_n}\quad(n\in\mathcal A),
\tag{L2}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb P(S_m=m-d)\le\frac{C}{a_m}
\quad(m\ge1, 1\le d\le m).
\tag{L3}
\]

Only the negative side of the centered sum is required in (L3). A usual bound on all lattice local probabilities is more than sufficient. Finite initial exceptions in these three assumptions can be absorbed by changing the sequence at finitely many indices and enlarging constants, provided the eventual sequence has the stated properties.

This is a checkable hypothesis about the offspring convolution probabilities. It is stronger than the still-unproved weighted-tail assertion for a completely arbitrary critical law, and is not disguised as a consequence of the moment condition.

Two consequences of (L1), used repeatedly, are

\[
a_v\le D^{\lceil\log_2 b\rceil}a_u
\quad\text{if }u,v\ge1, v\le bu, b\ge1,
\tag{3}
\]

and the polynomial growth estimate

\[
a_z\le D a_1 z^\gamma,\qquad \gamma=\log_2 D,\quad z\ge1.
\tag{4}
\]

For (4), compare $z$ with the next power of two. Both statements are valid also when $D=1$.

## 3. Root degree: sharp moment condition under a uniform ratio bound

For $n\ge2$, (1) at $k=1$ gives, for $d\ge1$,

\[
\mathbb P(Z_1(T_n)=d)
=\frac n{n-1},d\pi_d
\frac{\mathbb P(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{q_n}.
\tag{5}
\]

The probability is zero if $d>n-1$. Therefore, for any $K\ge0$, the **exact** integrability condition is

\[
\lim_{K\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathcal A, n\ge2}
\frac n{n-1}\sum_{d>K}d^{r+1}\pi_d
\frac{\mathbb P(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{q_n}=0.
\tag{6}
\]

Finiteness of the same whole sum uniformly in $n$ is only boundedness of moments, not uniform integrability. The correction in agent 002's audit is essential.

If the root local ratios obey

\[
R:=\sup_{n\in\mathcal A, n\ge2, 1\le d\le n-1}
\frac{\mathbb P(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{q_n}<\infty,
\tag{7}
\]

then (5) immediately yields the explicit estimate

\[
\sup_{n\in\mathcal A}\mathbb E\big[Z_1(T_n)^r;Z_1(T_n)>K\big]
\le2R\,\mathbb E\big[\xi^{r+1};\xi>K\big].
\tag{8}
\]

The $n=1$ term is zero. Thus the offspring moment assumption suffices with no lost power.

Conditions (L1)–(L3) imply (7), with $R\le DC/c$: indeed, $n\le2(n-1)$ for $n\ge2$. No stable-density asymptotics for growing $d$ are needed.

Conversely, the critical local limit tree has root-degree law

\[
\mathbb P(\widehat\xi=d)=d\pi_d.
\]

The known local weak convergence $T_n\Rightarrow\widehat T$ therefore implies that uniform integrability of $Z_1(T_n)^r$ requires $\mathbb E\widehat\xi^r=\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$. Hence, for every critical offspring law satisfying (7), the condition $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$ is necessary and sufficient for root-degree $r$th-power uniform integrability. This is a sharp statement within the stated local-ratio class; it is not an assertion that (7) always holds.

## 4. The weighted-sum lemma, including extinction

The next elementary lemma is the part that extends the conditioning proof to the entire fractional range without an extra moment.

**Lemma.** Assume (L1), fix $0<r<1$, and define

\[
f(0)=0,\qquad f(x)=\frac{x^r}{a_x}\quad(x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}).
\]

If $Y_1,Y_2,\ldots$ are independent, identically distributed nonnegative integer-valued random variables with $\mathbb EY_1=1$, and $U_z=Y_1+\cdots+Y_z$, then there is a finite constant $B_Y$ such that

\[
\mathbb Ef(U_z)\le B_Y f(z)\quad(z\ge1).
\tag{9}
\]

No moment of $Y_1$ above its mean is required.

**Proof.** Since $\mathbb EY_1=1$,

\[
\mathbb E e^{-tY_1}=1-t+o(t)\quad(t\downarrow0).
\]

Choose $t>0$ sufficiently small that

\[
\rho=e^{t/2}\mathbb E e^{-tY_1}<1.
\]

Markov's inequality gives the exponential lower-tail estimate

\[
\mathbb P(U_z<z/2)\le\rho^z.
\tag{10}
\]

On $\{U_z\ge z/2\}$, integer-valuedness gives $U_z\ge\lceil z/2\rceil$. By monotonicity and doubling,

\[
a_z\le a_{2\lceil z/2\rceil}\le D a_{\lceil z/2\rceil}\le D a_{U_z}.
\]

Consequently, Jensen's inequality for $x^r$ gives

\[
\mathbb E\big[f(U_z);U_z\ge z/2\big]
\le\frac D{a_z}\mathbb E U_z^r
\le\frac D{a_z}(\mathbb EU_z)^r
=D f(z).
\tag{11}
\]

On $\{0<U_z<z/2\}$, use $a_{U_z}\ge a_1$, $U_z^r\le z^r$, and (10):

\[
\mathbb E\big[f(U_z);0<U_z<z/2\big]
\le\frac{z^r}{a_1}\rho^z
=f(z)\frac{a_z}{a_1}\rho^z
\le D f(z)z^\gamma\rho^z.
\tag{12}
\]

The last factor is uniformly bounded in $z$. The event $U_z=0$ contributes exactly zero by the definition of $f$. Equations (11)–(12) prove (9). \(\square\)

**Negative-power interpretation.** When $a_z=z^\beta$, the lemma says

\[
\mathbb E\big[U_z^{r-\beta};U_z>0\big]
\le B_Y z^{r-\beta}.
\tag{13}
\]

In particular, it remains valid when $r<\beta$. This addresses the apparent sign obstruction in merely substituting a stable exponent into the archived finite-variance proof. The indicator $U_z>0$ in (13) cannot be omitted.

For the Galton–Watson process, conditionally on $Z_j=z\ge1$, $Z_k$ is a sum of $z$ independent copies of the $(k-j)$th generation descended from one individual. Each summand has mean one, even though it has positive extinction probability. Thus, for each $0\le j<k$,

\[
\mathbb E[f(Z_k)\mid Z_j=z]\le B_{k-j}f(z).
\tag{14}
\]

All constants here may depend on the fixed generation gap and on the offspring law; none depends on $z$ or the conditioned tree size $n$.

## 5. Fixed-generation theorem and explicit uniform tail estimate

**Theorem.** Suppose (L1)–(L3), $0<r<1$, $k\ge1$, and $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$. Then $\{Z_k(T_n)^r:n\in\mathcal A\}$ is uniformly integrable. More precisely, there is $H_k<\infty$ such that, for every real $K\ge2k$,

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\sup_{n\in\mathcal A}
\mathbb E\big[Z_k(T_n)^r;Z_k(T_n)>K\big]
\le H_k\Bigg(&\mathbb E\big[Z_k^{r+1};Z_k>K\big]\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb E\big[Z_j^{r+1};Z_j>K/(2k)\big]\Bigg).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{15}
\]

The sum is empty if $k=1$. Every generation variable on the right belongs to the **unconditioned** process.

**Proof.** Put $B=C/c$. From (1), (L2), and (L3), every compatible positive-generation truncation satisfies

\[
W_n\le B\frac{n a_n Z}{m a_m}.
\tag{16}
\]

There are two useful consequences. If $N\le n/2$, then $m\ge n/2$, so $n/m\le2$ and $a_n/a_m\le D$; hence

\[
W_n\le2DBZ.
\tag{17}
\]

For all compatible truncations, $m\ge Z\ge1$ and monotonicity imply

\[
m a_m\ge Z a_Z,\qquad
Z^rW_n\le Bn a_n\frac{Z^r}{a_Z}=Bn a_n f(Z).
\tag{18}
\]

Fix $K\ge2k$. If $n\le K$, then $Z_k(T_n)\le n\le K$, so the tail in (15) is zero. It suffices to handle $n>K\ge2k$.

On $N\le n/2$, equations (2) and (17) give

\[
\mathbb E\big[Z^rW_n;Z>K, N\le n/2\big]
\le2DB\,\mathbb E\big[Z_k^{r+1};Z_k>K\big].
\tag{19}
\]

On $N>n/2$, one of $Z_0,\ldots,Z_{k-1}$ exceeds $n/(2k)$. Since $n>2k$ and $Z_0=1$, the exceptional index is some $1\le j\le k-1$. Thus (18), followed by (14), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\big[Z^rW_n;Z>K, N>n/2\big]\\
&\quad\le Bn a_n\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}
\mathbb E\big[f(Z_k);Z_j>n/(2k)\big]\\
&\quad\le Bn a_n\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}B_{k-j}
\mathbb E\big[f(Z_j);Z_j>n/(2k)\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

Dropping compatibility after (18) is harmless because the upper integrand $f(Z_k)$ is nonnegative and well-defined also on extinction.

Let $h=\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil$. If $z>n/(2k)$, then $n<2kz\le2^h z$, whence $a_n\le D^h a_z$. Therefore

\[
n a_n f(z)=n\frac{a_n}{a_z}z^r
\le2kD^h z^{r+1}.
\tag{21}
\]

Using (21) in (20), then $n>K$, bounds that contribution by

\[
2kBD^h\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}B_{k-j}
\mathbb E\big[Z_j^{r+1};Z_j>K/(2k)\big].
\tag{22}
\]

Together (19) and (22) prove (15), with an appropriate fixed $H_k$.

It remains to verify that the right side tends to zero. Let $s=r+1>1$. Given $Z_{j-1}=z$, the convexity inequality for a sum of $z$ nonnegative terms gives

\[
\mathbb E[Z_j^s\mid Z_{j-1}=z]
\le z^s\mathbb E\xi^s.
\]

Induction yields

\[
\mathbb E Z_j^s\le(\mathbb E\xi^s)^j<\infty.
\tag{23}
\]

Every term on the right of (15) consequently vanishes as $K\to\infty$. Since the threshold for $Z_k(T_n)^r$ is $K^r$, this is precisely uniform integrability of the powered family. \(\square\)

This proof establishes actual tails, not just a uniform moment bound. There is no exchange of a supremum and a limit and no omission of a finite collection of tree sizes; the case $n\le K$ was disposed of explicitly.

## 6. Moment limit and sharpness

The Kesten tree has, at any fixed depth $k$, the ordinary Galton–Watson truncation law tilted by $Z_k$. One can see this by summing over the possible depth-$k$ endpoints of the distinguished spine: each possible endpoint contributes the ordinary truncation probability, and there are $Z_k$ such endpoints. Thus

\[
\mathbb E\big[Z_k(\widehat T)^r\big]
=\mathbb E Z_k^{r+1}.
\tag{24}
\]

Combining the critical local convergence $T_n\Rightarrow\widehat T$ from Janson's Theorem 7.1 with (15) gives

\[
\mathbb E\big[Z_k(T_n)^r\big]
\longrightarrow\mathbb E\big[Z_k(\widehat T)^r\big]
=\mathbb E Z_k^{r+1}<\infty.
\tag{25}
\]

The known local-convergence theorem, not merely the upper and lower local bounds (L1)–(L3), supplies distributional convergence here. The uniform-integrability conclusion (15) itself did not require a separate local ratio limit.

The moment hypothesis is also necessary under the local-scale class. If $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}=\infty$, conditional Jensen and $\mathbb E[Z_k\mid Z_1]=Z_1$ give

\[
\mathbb E Z_k^{r+1}\ge\mathbb E Z_1^{r+1}=\infty.
\]

The limit in (24) then has infinite $r$th moment. Local convergence and Fatou's lemma imply that $\mathbb E Z_k(T_n)^r\to\infty$, so uniform integrability is impossible. Consequently, within (L1)–(L3), $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$ is the exact criterion for every fixed $k\ge1$.

## 7. Stable-domain corollary and a concrete infinite-variance family

Suppose $\xi-1$ belongs to the domain of attraction of a nondegenerate stable law of index \(\alpha\in(1,2]\). There is an increasing normalizing sequence $B_n$, regularly varying with index $1/\alpha$, and a bounded stable density $g_\alpha$ with $g_\alpha(0)>0$. In span one, the standard uniform local limit theorem is

\[
\sup_{x\in\mathbb Z}\left|
B_m\mathbb P(S_m-m=x)-g_\alpha(x/B_m)
\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{26}
\]

This is the input displayed as equation (8) in Kortchemski's paper. For a lattice of span $h>1$, the same theorem on the attainable coset has the usual factor $h$, absorbed in the constants below; outside the attainable coset the probabilities are zero.

Regular variation with positive index gives $B_{2m}/B_m\to2^{1/\alpha}$, hence a finite doubling constant after the finitely many initial indices are included. Boundedness of $g_\alpha$ and (26) give (L3) for $a_m=B_m$; applying (26) at the attainable point $-1$ along $n\in\mathcal A$, with $g_\alpha(0)>0$, gives (L2). Initial admissible sizes have $q_n>0$, so they can be included by reducing $c$. The sequence $B_m$ in the cited formulation is increasing; equivalently a positive-index regularly varying normalization can be replaced by an asymptotically equivalent increasing one.

**Corollary.** For a critical nondegenerate stable-domain offspring law, $1<\alpha\le2$, a fixed $k\ge1$, and $0<r<1$,

\[
\{Z_k(T_n)^r:n\in\mathcal A\}\text{ is uniformly integrable}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty.
\tag{27}
\]

When finite, its expectation converges to the quantity in (24). No extra assumption that $B_n/n^{1/\alpha}$ tends to a nonzero constant is needed; slowly varying factors are allowed. If $\alpha=2$, this also permits infinite-variance laws in the normal domain of attraction. At $r+1=\alpha<2$, the moment is not inferred automatically from the tail index: it must still be checked, as stated in (27).

A concrete family showing that this includes genuinely infinite-variance cases is as follows. Fix $1<\alpha<2$, set

\[
A_\alpha=\sum_{d=2}^\infty d^{-\alpha},\qquad
B_\alpha=\sum_{d=2}^\infty d^{-1-\alpha},
\]

and define

\[
\pi_1=\frac12,\qquad
\pi_d=\frac{d^{-1-\alpha}}{2A_\alpha}\ (d\ge2),\qquad
\pi_0=\frac{A_\alpha-B_\alpha}{2A_\alpha}.
\tag{28}
\]

All probabilities are nonnegative, $\pi_0>0$, and direct summation gives total mass one and mean one. Its variance is infinite, and

\[
\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r<\alpha-1.
\]

The power tail places this aperiodic law in the $\alpha$-stable domain, with normalization of order $n^{1/\alpha}$. For every $0<r<\alpha-1$, (15) therefore proves fixed-generation fractional uniform integrability. This example is illustrative, not a new offspring family or a claim of new stable limit theory.

## 8. Remaining gap, failed shortcuts, and verification ledger

The unrestricted problem asks whether (6), and its fixed-generation analogue, follow from only criticality and $\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty$. This report has **not** proved that an arbitrary such law admits (L1)–(L3), nor that root ratios (7) are bounded. A law with highly irregular scales may have no such matching central and negative-side estimates. Failure of our sufficient condition would not itself disprove the conjecture, because the desired estimate contains the weights $d^{r+1}\pi_d$, which can compensate for individually large local ratios.

Specific shortcuts ruled out or repaired:

- Replacing uniform integrability by bounded $r$th moments is invalid; (6) and (15) retain the necessary tails.
- Replacing $n^{1/2}$ by $n^{1/\alpha}$ while keeping only positive-power conditional moment inequalities leaves a gap for $r<1/\alpha$. Equations (9)–(14) repair it, with extinction treated explicitly.
- Replacing a stable normalization by a pure power can lose a slowly varying factor. Conditions (L1)–(L3) and the weighted lemma retain the actual scale.
- A full Radon–Nikodym formula that assigns zero weight to every extinct truncation would be false. Only the positive-generation identity (2) is used.
- A bound obtained after $n\to\infty$ alone needs finite-size handling to imply a supremum over $n$. Equation (15) includes that handling and gives a direct uniform estimate.
