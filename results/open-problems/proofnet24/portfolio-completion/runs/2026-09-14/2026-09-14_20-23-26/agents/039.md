## Result and attribution boundary

**PROVED: the entire requested scalar ray is concave.** If `K` is any
nonzero finite complex Hermitian positive contraction, set
`T = 1 / ||K||`. Then

\[
F_K(t):=H(P_{tK}),\qquad 0\le t\le T,
\]

is strictly concave. More precisely, throughout `0 < t < T`,

\[
\boxed{
F_K''(t)\le
-\sum_{i:K_{ii}>0}\frac{K_{ii}}{t(1-tK_{ii})}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The curvature bound is **sharp**, with equality at an interior point if
and only if `K` is diagonal. When `K=0`, the entropy is identically zero.

This is **not a solution of the full Lyons conjecture**. It settles the
bounded scalar task. The basic ray theorem is a consequence of the
**existing convex relative-entropy argument in the proof of Gu's Lemma 5**,
as explained in Section 3. It is not inferred from Gu's weaker
zero-endpoint corollary. No novelty is claimed for the ray theorem, its
arbitrary-Boolean-law extension, or the sharper decomposition below.
The decomposition and equality case are derived explicitly here; their
historical priority has not been investigated beyond a bounded search.

The underlying theorem is stronger in its class of laws: **every finite
random subset, with arbitrary dependence, has concave Shannon entropy
under independent common-parameter thinning**. Neither determinantal
structure nor ultra-log-concavity is required.

## 1. Definitions and support control

Let `X` be an arbitrary random subset of `[n]`, with law `P`. Write

\[
m_i=\mathbb P(i\in X),\qquad m=\sum_i m_i=\mathbb E|X|.
\]

Independently retain each member of `X` with probability `t` and denote
the resulting law by `P_t=T_tP`. Its atom probabilities are

\[
p_t(S)=t^{|S|}\sum_{R\supseteq S}P(R)(1-t)^{|R|-|S|}.
\tag{2}
\]

All logarithms are natural. Coordinates with `m_i=0` can be omitted.
For each remaining coordinate, define the probability law

\[
Q_i=\mathcal L(X\setminus\{i\}\mid i\in X),\qquad
Q_{i,t}=T_tQ_i,
\]

on the same Boolean state space, with coordinate `i` always absent.
Write `q_{i,t}` for its atom probabilities. Conditional on retaining `i`,
the other retained elements have law `Q_{i,t}`. Thus, for `i` not in `S`,

\[
\boxed{p_t(S\cup\{i\})=t m_i q_{i,t}(S).}
\tag{3}
\]

For every `0<t<1`, the positive support of `p_t` is the fixed downward
closure of the support of `P`. The positive support of `q_{i,t}` is also
fixed and downward closed, and is contained in that of `p_t`. To check
the last statement, a set attainable by thinning a configuration of
`X\setminus{i}` can also be obtained from that original configuration of
`X` by deleting `i`. Consequently all relative entropies used below are
finite and smooth for `0<t<1`, even when the original laws have zeros.
Terms with zero probability are omitted. Entropy itself extends
continuously to `t=0` and `t=1`.

## 2. An exact entropy derivative and the short concavity proof

Differentiating (2), or using the deletion generator, gives

\[
\boxed{
t p_t'(S)=|S|p_t(S)-\sum_{i\notin S}p_t(S\cup\{i\}).
}
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, with all `q` understood to vanish on sets containing `i`,

\[
p_t'(S)=\sum_i m_i
\left(\mathbf1_{i\in S}q_{i,t}(S\setminus\{i\})
-\mathbf1_{i\notin S}q_{i,t}(S)\right).
\]

Since the derivative of total mass is zero, differentiation of entropy,
followed by collecting the two ends of each deletion edge, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H'(P_t)
&=-\sum_S p_t'(S)\log p_t(S)\\
&=\sum_i m_i\sum_{S:i\notin S}q_{i,t}(S)
\log\frac{p_t(S)}{p_t(S\cup\{i\})}\\
&=-\sum_i m_i\log(t m_i)
-\sum_i m_i D(Q_{i,t}\Vert P_t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

This is an identity for the actual polynomial law (2); no affine-law
assumption is made.

For `0<s<t<1`, thinning composes:

\[
P_s=T_{s/t}P_t,\qquad Q_{i,s}=T_{s/t}Q_{i,t}.
\]

The same stochastic channel acts on both arguments of each relative
entropy. Data processing therefore gives

\[
D(Q_{i,s}\Vert P_s)\le D(Q_{i,t}\Vert P_t).
\tag{6}
\]

Let `d_i(t)=D(Q_{i,t}||P_t)`. Equations (5)--(6) and smoothness prove

\[
\boxed{
H''(P_t)=-\frac m t-\sum_i m_i d_i'(t)
\le-\frac m t.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus entropy is concave for **every** Boolean law and strictly concave
unless `X` is almost surely empty. The boundary cases follow by
continuity. Notice that (6) controls the derivative of entropy at every
interior parameter. It proves all chords, not merely the chord from zero.

## 3. Precise relation to the known relative-entropy result

Define the nonnegative relative entropy

\[
\mathcal D(t)=D\!\left(P_t\middle\Vert
\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Poisson}(t m_i)\right).
\]

Because each coordinate of `P_t` is zero or one, its log-factorial term
vanishes identically. Directly expanding this definition gives

\[
H(P_t)=-\mathcal D(t)-L(t),\qquad
L(t)=\sum_i\big[t m_i\log(t m_i)-t m_i\big].
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (5) and (8) yield

\[
\mathcal D'(t)=\sum_i m_i d_i(t),\qquad L''(t)=m/t.
\tag{9}
\]

By (6), `Dcal` is convex. Substituting into (8) proves (7).

This is the relevant existing ingredient in Gu's
[author-hosted report](https://sevenkplus.com/data/dpp.pdf): Lemma 5 is
stated for arbitrary distributions on the nonnegative integer lattice
with finite mean; it does **not** assume ULC or stability. Its proof
explicitly concludes convexity of the Poisson-relative-entropy function,
using size-biased deletion and data processing. The ULC hypothesis belongs
to the separate convolution statement, Theorem 4. Gu's Corollary 6 states
only the zero-endpoint DPP inequality. The full ray result here follows
from the stronger convexity ingredient and the Boolean identity (8),
not from treating Corollary 6 as full concavity. All finite-support
differentiation and support details needed for this implication were
independently supplied in Sections 1--2.

## 4. Exact deletion-edge dissipation

The data-processing step has a useful exact differential version. It
produces the sharper bound (1) and its equality conditions.

Let `q_t=T_tQ` and `p_t=T_tP`, with the positive support of `q_t` contained
in that of `p_t` for `0<t<1`; suppose both supports are downward closed.
Set

\[
r_t(S)=q_t(S)/p_t(S),\qquad
\Phi(a,b)=a\log(a/b)-a+b.
\]

Here `Phi(0,b)=b`, `Phi(0,0)=0`, and `Phi(a,b)>=0` for nonnegative
arguments whenever finite. If `r_t(U)>0` and `V` is obtained from `U`
by deleting one element, then `r_t(V)>0` by downward closure, so no
infinite terms arise.

**Exact dissipation identity:**

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}D(q_t\Vert p_t)
=\frac1t\sum_{U:p_t(U)>0}\sum_{j\in U}
p_t(U)\Phi\!\left(r_t(U),r_t(U\setminus\{j\})\right).
}
\tag{10}
\]

**Derivation.** The derivative of the relative entropy is

\[
\sum_S q_t'(S)\log r_t(S)
-\sum_S q_t(S)\frac{p_t'(S)}{p_t(S)}.
\]

Use (4) for both probability vectors and collect terms along the edge
`U -> V=U\setminus{j}`. After multiplication by `t`, the first sum
contributes `q_t(U)(log r_t(U)-log r_t(V))`, and the second contributes
`p_t(U)(r_t(V)-r_t(U))`. Their sum is exactly the term in (10).
If a `q` probability is zero, it is identically zero throughout the open
parameter interval. Terms are therefore interpreted by the displayed
continuous conventions, or obtained by first replacing `q` by
`(1-epsilon)q+epsilon p` and letting `epsilon` decrease to zero.

All summands in (10) are nonnegative. This independently verifies the
differential monotonicity used in (7).

### The edge that deletes the conditioned coordinate

Apply (10) to `q_t=q_{i,t}` and `p_t`. The numerator distribution has
coordinate `i` always zero. On the edges deleting `i`, therefore,
`r_t(S\cup{i})=0`. Using (3), their total contribution to `d_i'(t)` is

\[
\frac1t\sum_{S:i\notin S}p_t(S\cup\{i\})r_t(S)
=m_i\sum_{S:i\notin S}\frac{q_{i,t}(S)^2}{p_t(S)}.
\tag{11}
\]

Since `sum q=1` and `sum_{S:i notin S}p_t(S)=1-t m_i`,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\boxed{d_i'(t)\ge\frac{m_i}{1-t m_i}.}
\tag{12}
\]

Combining (7) and (12),

\[
\begin{aligned}
H''(P_t)
&\le-\sum_i\left(\frac{m_i}{t}+\frac{m_i^2}{1-t m_i}\right)\\
&=\boxed{-\sum_i\frac{m_i}{t(1-t m_i)}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

### A nonnegative exact remainder

Let

\[
\pi_{i,t}(S)=\frac{p_t(S)}{1-t m_i},\qquad i\notin S.
\]

This is the conditional law of all other retained coordinates given
that `i` was not retained. In contrast, `q_{i,t}` is their conditional
law given that `i` was retained. Define

\[
C_i(t)=\chi^2(q_{i,t}\Vert\pi_{i,t})
=\sum_{S:i\notin S}\frac{q_{i,t}(S)^2}{\pi_{i,t}(S)}-1\ge0,
\]

and let `E_i(t)` denote the part of the right side of (10) on edges
deleting a coordinate `j` different from `i`. Thus `E_i(t)>=0` and

\[
d_i'(t)=\frac{m_i}{1-t m_i}(1+C_i(t))+E_i(t).
\]

Substitution into (7) proves the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
H''(P_t)
=-\sum_i\frac{m_i}{t(1-t m_i)}
-\sum_i\frac{m_i^2}{1-t m_i}C_i(t)
-\sum_i m_iE_i(t).
}
\tag{14}
\]

This is a decomposition into one explicit marginal curvature and two
nonnegative dependence penalties. It controls the **full** entropy,
including configurations within the same cardinality.

For comparison with the usual DPP Hessian, direct differentiation also
gives

\[
H''(P_t)=-\sum_S\frac{p_t'(S)^2}{p_t(S)}
-\sum_Sp_t''(S)\log p_t(S).
\]

Equation (14) evaluates and controls the combined expression; it does
not assume a sign for its logarithmic term in isolation.

## 5. Sharpness, equality, and a stronger chord inequality

**Equality in (13) holds at an interior parameter if and only if the
original coordinates of `X` are independent.** Degenerate Bernoulli
coordinates with mean zero or one are allowed.

To prove necessity, equality in (14) forces `C_i(t)=0` for every
`m_i>0`. Hence the conditional laws of the other retained coordinates
given `i` present and absent coincide. Each nonconstant coordinate of
`P_t` is independent of the entire remaining vector; coordinates with
`m_i=0` are deterministic. Consequently `P_t` is a product law.
For any subset `S`, thinning and independence now imply

\[
t^{|S|}\mathbb P(S\subseteq X)
=\prod_{i\in S}(t m_i).
\]

Cancel `t^{|S|}`. The original inclusion probabilities are those of
independent Bernoulli coordinates. Inclusion-exclusion identifies the
whole original law, proving independence.

Conversely, for an original product law, `P_t` is the product of
`Bernoulli(t m_i)` laws, so its entropy is exactly
`sum_i h(t m_i)`. Its second derivative is the right side of (13).
Equivalently, all `C_i` and `E_i` in (14) vanish. This also proves
sharpness for every feasible marginal vector and every interior `t`.

There is a useful equivalent global statement. Put

\[
B_P(t)=\sum_i h(t m_i),\qquad
\operatorname{TC}(P_t)=B_P(t)-H(P_t),
\]

where `h` is binary entropy and `TC` is total correlation. Formula (14)
shows that **total correlation is convex under common thinning**;
it is strictly convex unless `P` is a product law.
Thus, for `0<=a<b<=1`, `0<=theta<=1`, and
`c=(1-theta)a+theta b`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
H(P_c)\ge{}&(1-\theta)H(P_a)+\theta H(P_b)\\
&+B_P(c)-(1-\theta)B_P(a)-\theta B_P(b).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{15}
\]

At a nondegenerate chord, equality in (15) holds precisely for a product
law. The ordinary entropy chord inequality is strict for every
nonempty law, because the first sum in (13) is strictly negative.

## 6. Application to the entire allowable DPP ray

For a DPP with kernel `G` and any `0<=s<=1`, thinning multiplies all
inclusion probabilities by `s^{|S|}`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb P(S\subseteq T_sX_G)
=s^{|S|}\det G_S=\det(sG)_S,
\]

so `T_sP_G=P_{sG}`. This argument uses fixed subset coordinates and is
valid for arbitrary complex Hermitian kernels.

Now fix a nonzero `0<=K<=I`, set `alpha=||K||>0`, and let
`G=K/alpha`. It is a positive contraction, possibly on the boundary,
and has operator norm one. The maximal nonnegative scalar interval for
`tK` to be a positive contraction is `0<=t<=1/alpha`. Throughout it,

\[
P_{tK}=T_{\alpha t}P_G.
\tag{16}
\]

The original marginal means for `P_G` are `m_i=K_{ii}/alpha`.
Apply (13) with parameter `s=alpha t` and the chain rule:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_K''(t)
&=\alpha^2\left.\frac{d^2}{ds^2}H(T_sP_G)\right|_{s=\alpha t}\\
&\le-\alpha^2\sum_i
\frac{K_{ii}/\alpha}{(\alpha t)(1-tK_{ii})}
=-\sum_i\frac{K_{ii}}{t(1-tK_{ii})}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (1), including kernels with zero eigenvalues and rays ending
at an eigenvalue one. No invertibility of `K` or of individual atom
matrices is required. Boundary entropy concavity follows by continuity
of the finitely many probabilities. If `K=0`, no rescaling is needed.

Equality in (1) means that `P_G` is a product law. For every pair `i,j`,
the DPP inclusion identity then gives

\[
G_{ii}G_{jj}-|G_{ij}|^2
=\mathbb P(i,j\in X_G)=G_{ii}G_{jj},
\]

so `G_{ij}=0`. Thus equality is equivalent to `K` being diagonal.

The stronger chord inequality (15) also holds on the entire interval
`[0,1/||K||]` after replacing `B_P(t)` by
`B_K(t)=sum_i h(tK_{ii})`. For a nondegenerate chord its equality
condition is exactly that `K` is diagonal.
