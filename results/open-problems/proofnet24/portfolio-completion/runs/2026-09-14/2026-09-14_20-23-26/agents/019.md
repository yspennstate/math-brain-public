## Result and scope

**PROVED, using established stochastic domination for projection DPPs:** for every supermodular set function on a ground set of size **at most five**, every positive Hermitian contraction $K$, and all vectors $u,v$,

$$
D^2 F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*]\geq0,
\qquad F_f(K)=\mathbb E_{P_K}f(X).
\tag{1}
$$

Consequently, in dimensions at most five, DPP Shannon entropy is concave along every feasible affine segment whose direction has rank at most two and at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue. This extends the arbitrarily oriented rank-two indefinite result of Agent 010 from dimension three to dimensions four and five.

The proof establishes positivity on **decomposable two-vectors**, which is sufficient for these entropy directions. It does **not** establish positivity of the full exterior-square coefficient operator.

The same argument proves (1) in every dimension whenever the compression of $K$ to $\operatorname{span}(u,v)^\perp$ has rank at most one, or its complement within that subspace has rank at most one. The general all-dimensional lemma remains unresolved. The first unresolved projection case after the reductions below is dimension six with a rank-two compressed projection. No counterexample to (1), or to Lyons entropy concavity, was obtained. Historical novelty of the restricted result has not been established.

## 1. Definitions and the established input

Let $E=[n]$ be the actual point-process coordinate set. For a positive Hermitian contraction $K$, $P_K$ is the probability measure with

$$
P_K(S\subseteq X)=\det K_S.
$$

For an arbitrary real set function $f$, let $c_S$ be its Boolean Möbius coefficients:

$$
f(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}c_S,
\qquad
F_f(K)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}c_S\det K_S.
\tag{2}
$$

The latter identity also defines a polynomial on all Hermitian matrices. Derivatives at boundary contractions below refer to that polynomial. No expectation outside the positive-contraction domain is needed for the proof.

Supermodularity means $\Delta_i\Delta_j f(T)\geq0$ whenever $i,j\notin T$, where

$$
\Delta_i\Delta_j f(T)
=f(T\cup\{i,j\})+f(T)-f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T\cup\{j\}).
$$

The non-elementary input is established stochastic domination: if subspaces $H_1\subseteq H_2$, their projection DPPs admit a coupling $(Y,Z)$ with $Y\subseteq Z$ almost surely. A projection DPP on a $d$-dimensional subspace has cardinality $d$ almost surely. Both facts are stated in Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures* (2014), equation (2.2), Theorem 2.7, and the preceding explanation of monotone coupling and Strassen's theorem; see the [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707) or [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2707v1).

Write

$$
q_f(K;u,v):=D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*].
\tag{3}
$$

Each principal determinant is affine along any rank-one update. Thus pure rank-one second derivatives of $F_f$ vanish.

## 2. Compression to the orthogonal complement of the update plane

**Lemma 1.** Suppose $u,v$ are linearly independent, put $U=\operatorname{span}(u,v)$, and let $R=P_{U^\perp}$. Then, for every Hermitian $K$,

$$
q_f(K;u,v)=q_f(RKR;u,v).
\tag{4}
$$

**Proof.** It suffices to prove this for each determinant in (2). Restrict to $S\subseteq E$ and put $V_S=[u_S\ v_S]$. If $V_S$ has rank less than two, the coefficient of $st$ in

$$
\det(K_S+s u_Su_S^*+t v_Sv_S^*)
$$

is zero. Otherwise, choose an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb C^S$ whose first two vectors span $\operatorname{range}(V_S)$. In this basis, both updates lie in the leading $2\times2$ block. Extracting the coefficient of $st$ gives

$$
\det(V_S^*V_S)\,
\det\left(K_S\big|_{\operatorname{range}(V_S)^\perp}\right),
\tag{5}
$$

where the second factor means the orthogonal compression, and the determinant of a zero-dimensional matrix is one. The leading-block identity follows because the coefficient of $st$ in $\det(B\operatorname{diag}(s,t)B^*)$ is $|\det B|^2=\det(V_S^*V_S)$; selecting these two update contributions leaves the complementary principal determinant. It does not require $K_S$ to be invertible or positive.

Every vector in $\operatorname{range}(V_S)^\perp$, extended by zero outside $S$, belongs to $U^\perp$. Therefore the compression of $K_S$ in (5) is unchanged when $K$ is replaced by $RKR$. This proves (4). ∎

**Orthonormalization.** If $e_1,e_2$ are any orthonormal basis of $U$, then

$$
q_f(K;u,v)
=\big(\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-|u^*v|^2\big)
q_f(K;e_1,e_2).
\tag{6}
$$

Indeed, write $[u\ v]=[e_1\ e_2]B$ in (5); every Gram determinant gains the common factor $|\det B|^2$. If $u,v$ are dependent, the mixed derivative is zero. Thus it suffices to treat orthonormal $u,v$.

This is not a unitary-invariance claim for the subset entropy. All principal determinants and all evaluations of $f$ remain in the original coordinate basis. The orthonormal basis is used only inside the update plane and its orthogonal complement.

## 3. Reduction to compressed projection kernels

Take $u,v$ orthonormal. By Lemma 1, replace $K$ by

$$
C=RKR=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_j w_jw_j^*,
\qquad m=n-2,
$$

where $w_1,\ldots,w_m$ form an orthonormal basis of $U^\perp$ and $0\leq\lambda_j\leq1$.

Because a determinant is affine in a rank-one update, $q_f(C;u,v)$ is affine in each $\lambda_j$ separately. Consequently it has the exact interpolation formula

$$
q_f(C;u,v)=
\sum_{J\subseteq[m]}
\left(\prod_{j\in J}\lambda_j\prod_{j\notin J}(1-\lambda_j)\right)
q_f(P_{W_J};u,v),
\quad
W_J=\operatorname{span}\{w_j:j\in J\}.
\tag{7}
$$

The coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. Thus only projections onto subspaces of $U^\perp$ need to be checked. Compression and interpolation are valid for complex Hermitian kernels as well as real symmetric kernels.

## 4. Projection rank zero and rank one

For a subspace $H$, use $P_H$ for its projection matrix and $\mu_H$ for its projection DPP law. This separates the matrix from the measure.

**Lemma 2 (rank zero).** For supermodular $f$,

$$
q_f(0;u,v)=\mathbb E_{Y\sim\mu_U} c_Y\geq0.
\tag{8}
$$

**Proof.** Only the size-two terms in (2) contribute. Their mixed coefficients are $|u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2=\mu_U(\{i,j\})$. Supermodularity gives $c_{ij}=\Delta_i\Delta_j f(\varnothing)\geq0$. ∎

**Lemma 3 (rank one, in arbitrary ambient dimension).** Let $w$ be a unit vector orthogonal to $U$. Then

$$
q_f(ww^*;u,v)\geq0
\tag{9}
$$

for every supermodular $f$.

**Proof.** The matrix $ww^*+suu^*+tvv^*$ has rank at most three. Its size-two principal determinants have mixed coefficients $|u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2$. By Cauchy–Binet, its size-three principal determinants have mixed coefficients

$$
\left|\det[u\ v\ w]_S\right|^2=\mu_{U\oplus[w]}(S).
$$

Therefore (2) gives the exact identity

$$
q_f(ww^*;u,v)
=\mathbb E_{Y\sim\mu_U}c_Y
+\mathbb E_{Z\sim\mu_{U\oplus[w]}}c_Z.
\tag{10}
$$

Stochastic domination supplies a coupling with $Y\subseteq Z$. Their sizes are two and three, so write $Y=\{i,j\}$ and $Z=\{i,j,k\}$. Pointwise in this coupling,

$$
c_Y+c_Z=c_{ij}+c_{ijk}=\Delta_i\Delta_j f(\{k\})\geq0.
\tag{11}
$$

Taking expectations proves (9). Neither $c_Y$ nor $c_Z$ is presumed positive individually in (10). The nonnegative quantity is their coupled sum. ∎

Combining these two lemmas with rank-one interpolation proves the mixed-derivative inequality in every dimension when $\operatorname{rank}(RKR)\leq1$.

## 5. Complement duality and dimensions four and five

Define $f^c(S)=f(E\setminus S)$. Complementation preserves supermodularity. The DPP complement identity gives

$$
F_f(K)=F_{f^c}(I-K).
$$

Differentiating twice changes both direction signs, which cancel:

$$
q_f(K;u,v)=q_{f^c}(I-K;u,v).
\tag{12}
$$

For $W\subseteq U^\perp$, use the compression lemma on the right side:

$$
q_f(P_W;u,v)
=q_{f^c}(P_{U^\perp\cap W^\perp};u,v).
\tag{13}
$$

If $r=\dim W$ and $m=n-2$, the new compressed projection has rank $m-r$. Thus each projection term in (7) can be reduced to rank $\min(r,m-r)$.

When $n\leq5$, one has $m\leq3$, and every such minimum is zero or one. Lemmas 2 and 3 then prove every term in (7) nonnegative. Equations (4), (6), and (7) prove (1) for arbitrary $u,v$ and all positive Hermitian contractions in dimensions at most five. ∎

The same complement argument proves the arbitrary-dimensional statement when $R-RKR$ has rank at most one, with the rank understood on $U^\perp$.

## 6. Entropy consequence, including finite segments and boundary kernels

Fix an interior kernel $0<K<I$, let $p_S=P_K(X=S)>0$, and **freeze** the function

$$
f(S)=-\log p_S.
$$

This function is supermodular. To see this, put $L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0$. The atom formula is $p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S$. The positive-definite Schur complement of $L_T$ gives

$$
\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}\det L_T
\leq\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}\det L_{T\cup\{j\}},
$$

and taking negative logarithms proves the claim. These atom and complement identities are standard; the atom formula is equation (2.12) in [Lyons (2014)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707).

For a Hermitian direction $A$, normalization and differentiation give

$$
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S}
+D^2F_f(K)[A,A].
\tag{14}
$$

Suppose $n\leq5$ and $A=\lambda uu^*-\mu vv^*$ with orthonormal $u,v$ and $\lambda,\mu\geq0$. Pure rank-one second derivatives vanish, so

$$
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S}
-2\lambda\mu\,q_f(K;u,v)\leq0.
}
\tag{15}
$$

The function $f$ is frozen only while evaluating the Hessian at the chosen base kernel. At a different point of a segment, the argument uses the corresponding frozen atom logarithms.

It follows by integration that, if $K_0,K_1$ are strict contractions of size at most five and $K_1-K_0$ has at most one positive and at most one negative eigenvalue, then

$$
H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)\geq(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
\qquad(0\leq t\leq1).
\tag{16}
$$

For boundary contractions, replace $K_j$ by $(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I$. Their difference has the same rank and inertia. Apply (16) and pass to the limit using continuity of the finite atom law and of $-x\log x$ at zero.

This does not prove the full entropy conjecture even in these dimensions: directions with more than two nonzero eigenvalues, and rank-two semidefinite directions, are not covered by the sign argument. The latter produce the opposite sign in the mixed part, requiring quantitative use of the Fisher term.

## 7. Precisely what remains in higher dimensions

The exterior coefficient operator $T_f(K)$ may be characterized by

$$
D^2F_f(K)[A,A]=2\operatorname{tr}(T_f(K)\,\bigwedge^2 A).
$$

Our result says $\langle u\wedge v,T_f(K)(u\wedge v)\rangle\geq0$ in the proved cases. This is a statement about decomposable two-vectors. Positivity on arbitrary elements of $\bigwedge^2\mathbb C^n$ is stronger and has not been proved here.

There is a useful exact formula for every remaining compressed projection. If $W=\operatorname{span}(w_1,\ldots,w_r)\subseteq U^\perp$ with an orthonormal basis, Cauchy–Binet in (2) gives

$$
\boxed{
q_f(P_W;u,v)
=\sum_{J\subseteq[r]}
\mathbb E_{X\sim\mu_{U\oplus W_J}}c_X,
\quad
W_J=\operatorname{span}\{w_j:j\in J\}.
}
\tag{17}
$$

Indeed, a principal determinant of size $s$ has mixed coefficient equal to the sum of squared minors of $[u\ v\ w_J]$ over $|J|=s-2$. Grouping these terms produces (17).

For $n=6$, complement duality leaves one first unresolved case, $r=2$. Its exact obligation is

$$
\mathbb E_{\mu_U}c_X
+\mathbb E_{\mu_{U\oplus[w_1]}}c_X
+\mathbb E_{\mu_{U\oplus[w_2]}}c_X
+\mathbb E_{\mu_{U\oplus[w_1,w_2]}}c_X
\ \geq0
\tag{18}
$$

for all supermodular $f$ and orthonormal $u,v,w_1,w_2$ in $\mathbb C^6$.

A **sufficient, unproved** coupling lemma would realize these four projection DPPs as

$$
Y,\quad Y\cup\{i\},\quad Y\cup\{j\},\quad Y\cup\{i,j\},
\qquad |Y|=2,\ i\ne j,\ i,j\notin Y.
\tag{19}
$$

Then the coupled sum of the four Möbius coefficients would be $\Delta_a\Delta_b f(\{i,j\})\geq0$, where $Y=\{a,b\}$, proving (18). Ordinary pairwise stochastic domination does **not** by itself furnish this compatible four-law coupling. No assertion of its existence is made. Likewise, failure of this sufficient coupling would not by itself disprove (18).

Lyons (2014), Question 2.8, separately poses a union-marginal coupling problem for orthogonal subspaces. This shows that related compatibility questions are nontrivial; it does not establish an equivalence with (19). Targeted literature searches in this run did not establish the current resolution of that union-coupling question or novelty of the present restricted theorem.

The proof above is analytic and uses no random sampling. The standard-library-only script

`computations/019_rank_two_exact.py`

performs deterministic rational-arithmetic consistency checks; its executed output is

`computations/019_rank_two_exact_output.json`.

Run it with:

```bash
python computations/019_rank_two_exact.py
```

The example has $n=5$. Let $a=(1,2,3,4,5)^T$ and $Q=I-(2/55)aa^T$, an exactly orthogonal rational Householder matrix. The update vectors are the first two columns of $Q$. Let $K=QBQ^T$, where

$$
\operatorname{diag}B=(1/3,2/3,1/5,2/5,3/5),
\quad B_{13}=B_{31}=1/100,
\quad B_{24}=B_{42}=1/80,
$$

and all other off-diagonal entries vanish. The indices in this display are one-based. The exact Gershgorin intervals of $B$ lie strictly between zero and one, certifying $0<K<I$.

The function is

$$
f(S)=\max\left(0,\sum_{i\in S}(1,1,2,3,4)_i-4\right)^2.
$$

All elementary supermodularity inequalities are checked exactly. Some higher Möbius coefficients are negative. Exact arithmetic verifies:

- the compression identity (4), despite nonzero cross terms between the update plane and its complement;
- the eight-term spectral interpolation identity (7);
- the complement reductions (13) for all rank-two and rank-three vertices;
- six rank-one coupling representations (10)–(11), including those obtained by complementing $f$.

The coupling checks use an explicit rational augmenting-path flow on the inclusion graph of pairs and triples, and verify both marginals and every nonnegative coupled second difference. The common mixed derivative is

$$
q_f(K;u,v)=\frac{14398}{6875}\approx2.0942545454545454.
$$

This computation verifies the implementation of the identities on one nontrivial example; it is not a universal certificate or evidence for the unproved higher-dimensional case. No pilot random search from Agent 010 was repeated.
