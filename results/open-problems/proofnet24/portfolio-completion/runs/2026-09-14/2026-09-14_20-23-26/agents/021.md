## 1. Exact statement and sources

At level \(\ell\), put

\[
R=2^\ell,\qquad d=2^R,\qquad n=d^R,\qquad
k=R+\ell-1,\qquad
\delta_*=\frac{1}{\log 2}-1\in(0,1/2).
\]

Let \(\mu_d\) be uniform probability measure on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^d\). Fix \(h>0\), \(0\le B<\infty\), and \(0\le\sigma<\infty\), independent of \(\ell\), and write \(\gamma=h^{-2}\). The kernel is

\[
K_h(x,z)=e^{-\|x-z\|^2/(2h^2)}
        =e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma\langle x,z\rangle}.
\]

The rows \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) are iid \(\mu_d\). Conditional on the entire design \(X\), the one training-noise vector \(\varepsilon\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfies

\[
E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon\mid X)
=E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^\top\mid X]\preceq\sigma^2I_n.
\tag{021.2}
\]

Let \(\mathcal I_X y\) be the minimum-\(K_h\)-RKHS-norm interpolant with labels \(y\). For the degree-\(s\) harmonic space \(\mathcal H_{d,s}\), set

\[
\mathcal F_\ell(B)=
\left\{f\in\bigoplus_{0\le s<k}\mathcal H_{d,s}:
                 \|f\|_{L^2(\mu_d)}\le B\right\}.
\]

The audited theorem is

\[
S_\ell:=
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F_\ell(B)}
\|\mathcal I_X(f(X)+\varepsilon)-f\|_{L^2(\mu_d)}^2
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{021.3}
\]

Any joint distribution of the levels having these levelwise assumptions is allowed.

## 2. Spectrum and the retained tail mass

Use a population-orthonormal harmonic basis and its projection kernels

\[
P_s(x,z)=\sum_{a=1}^{N_s}\psi_{s,a}(x)\psi_{s,a}(z),
\qquad
N_s=\binom{d+s-1}{s}-\binom{d+s-3}{s-2}.
\]

Rotational invariance and orthonormality give

\[
P_s(x,x)=N_s,\qquad
\int P_s(x,u)P_t(u,z)\,d\mu_d(u)
=\mathbf 1_{\{s=t\}}P_s(x,z).
\tag{021.4}
\]

The Mercer eigenvalue formula used in the reports is

\[
\lambda_s=e^{-\gamma}
\sum_{j\ge0}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2j}}
{2^j j!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+j)-2)}.
\tag{021.5}
\]

There is an empty product when \(s=j=0\).

An independent derivation also checks this normalization. Let \(H_s\) be the homogeneous harmonic extension of a degree-\(s\) spherical harmonic \(Y_s\), and let \(G\sim N(0,I_d)\). Gaussian translation and harmonicity yield, for unit \(x\),

\[
E[e^{t\langle x,G\rangle}H_s(G)]
=e^{t^2/2}H_s(tx)
=t^s e^{t^2/2}Y_s(x).
\]

Write \(G=\rho U\), with \(\rho\) and \(U\) independent. Comparing the coefficient of \(t^{s+2j}\), and using
\(E\rho^{2(s+j)}=d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+j)-2)\), gives the coefficient in (021.5). The other powers have zero integral. Expanding \(e^{\gamma\langle x,U\rangle}\) completes the calculation.

All terms in (021.5) are positive. Comparing the summand at \(s+1,j\) with that at \(s,j\) gives

\[
\frac{\lambda_{s+1}}{\lambda_s}
\le\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}.
\tag{021.6}
\]

For \(d\ge3\), the exact multiplicity ratio is

\[
\frac{N_{s+1}}{N_s}
=\frac{(2s+d)(d+s-2)}
{(s+1)(2s+d-2)}.
\tag{021.7}
\]

Define \(\tau_s=N_s\lambda_s\). Combining (021.6) and (021.7), including the cancellation of \(2s+d=d+2s\), gives

\[
\frac{\tau_{s+1}}{\tau_s}
\le
\frac{\gamma(d+s-2)}
{(s+1)(d+2s-2)}
\le\frac{\gamma}{s+1}.
\tag{021.8}
\]

This bound holds for every \(s\ge0\), not just bounded degrees.

Let

\[
K_T=\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_sP_s,\qquad
\tau=\sum_{s\ge k}\tau_s,\qquad
T=K_T(X,X).
\]

For \(k+1>\gamma\), summing (021.8) gives

\[
\tau_k\le\tau\le
\frac{\tau_k}{1-\gamma/(k+1)},\qquad
\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}\le\frac1{N_k}.
\tag{021.9}
\]

Once \(d>\gamma\), all the Mercer eigenvalues are decreasing. Thus the smallest eigenvalue in the entire low block is \(\lambda_{k-1}\), and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\tau}{n\lambda_{k-1}}
&\le
\frac{N_k}{n}
\frac{\lambda_k}{\lambda_{k-1}}
\frac1{1-\gamma/(k+1)}\\
&\le
\frac{N_k}{n}
\frac{\gamma}{d+2k-2}
\frac1{1-\gamma/(k+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{021.10}
\]

Every factor in this inequality is justified. There is no replacement of a Mercer ratio by a trace-mass ratio.

## 3. Dimension arithmetic

For the degrees here, \(s=O(R+\ell)\), the exact multiplicity formula gives

\[
N_s=\frac{d^s}{s!}\exp(O(s^2/d)).
\]

Since \(\log d=R\log2\) and \(\log R=\ell\log2\), Stirling yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log_d(N_k/n)
&=k-R-\frac{\log(k!)}{R\log2}
  +O(k^2/(dR))\\
&=\ell-1-\ell+\frac1{\log2}
  +O(\ell^2/R)+o(1)\\
&=\delta_*+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{021.11}
\]

Also, directly from (021.7),

\[
\frac{N_{k-1}}{N_k}=\frac{k}{d}(1+o(1)).
\]

The ratios of consecutive lower-degree multiplicities imply that
\(D:=\sum_{s<k}N_s=N_{k-1}(1+o(1))\). For example, every backward ratio in this sum is at most \(k/(d-2)\), which tends to zero. Consequently

\[
\frac{N_k}{n}=d^{\delta_*+o(1)},\qquad
\frac{D}{n}=d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}.
\tag{021.12}
\]

Writing

\[
a=\frac{\tau}{n\lambda_{k-1}},\qquad
b=\frac{n\lambda_k}{\tau},
\]

(021.9), (021.10), and (021.12) give

\[
a\le d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)},\qquad
b\le d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}.
\tag{021.13}
\]

In particular, \(a\to0\). This is precisely the point lost if one substitutes the much coarser bound \(\tau\le1\).

## 4. The imported matrix step gives summable good-design failures

Here is the needed consequence of the KRM proof-level estimate. For a bounded symmetric PSD kernel \(r\) satisfying \(E_zr(x,z)=0\), let \(\mathsf R=r(X,X)\), let
\(\mathsf C_{ij}=E_z[r(X_i,z)r(z,X_j)]\), and let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\) be an independent copy of the design. The decoupled independent-matrix inequality gives

\[
E\|\operatorname{offdiag}\mathsf R\|
\le C\left[
\sqrt{n\log n\,E\|\mathsf C\|}
+\log n\sqrt{
E\max_{j\le n}\sum_{i\le n}r(X_i,Z_j)^2}
\right].
\tag{021.14}
\]

This uses the row-square quantity before the paper bounds it by maximum entries. The centering assumption removes the conditional-mean term. The variance contribution is controlled by \(Cn\|\mathsf C\|\). These are exactly the two terms needed here, with dimension-independent constants. [Primary source: Theorem 1 proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03892v1#S2).

For the normalized tail \(r=K_T/\tau\), all the hypotheses hold: it is continuous, PSD, has diagonal one, satisfies \(|r|\le1\), and has conditional mean zero because every included harmonic has positive degree.

I now check the application of (021.14), rather than assuming the claimed tail-isotropy conclusion.

Set \(r_s=P_s/N_s\). Its homogeneous Gaussian lift is in pure degree-\(s\) Gaussian chaos. Hypercontractivity and radial/angular independence therefore give

\[
\|r_s(X,z)\|_p
\le
\frac{(p-1)^{s/2}}{\sqrt{N_s}},
\qquad p\ge2,
\tag{021.15}
\]

uniformly in \(z\). Specifically, dividing the Gaussian estimate by
\(\|\rho^s\|_p\) only improves its constant because
\(\|\rho^s\|_p\ge\|\rho^s\|_2\).

Write \(w_s=\tau_s/\tau\), so \(r=\sum_{s\ge k}w_sr_s\). Since \(N_s\) is increasing and (021.8) holds, the consecutive ratio in the upper bound on this series at moment \(2R\) is at most

\[
\frac{\gamma\sqrt{2R-1}}{k+1}=O(R^{-1/2}).
\]

The entire infinite series is thus bounded by a geometric series, giving, eventually,

\[
\|r(X,z)\|_{2R}
\le C_h(2R)^{k/2}/\sqrt{N_k},\qquad
\|r(X,z)^2\|_2\le C_h3^k/N_k.
\tag{021.16}
\]

No finite-tail truncation or unspecified growing-degree constant is involved.

Orthogonality gives the exact second moment

\[
m=E_X r(X,z)^2=\sum_{s\ge k}\frac{w_s^2}{N_s}.
\]

Because \(w_k\ge1-\gamma/(k+1)\) and \(N_s\ge N_k\),

\[
\frac{(1-\gamma/(k+1))^2}{N_k}
\le m\le\frac1{N_k}.
\tag{021.17}
\]

It is independent of \(z\). For \(U(z)=\sum_i r(X_i,z)^2\), scalar Rosenthal at \(q=R\), including the conservative factor \(CR\), gives

\[
\|U(z)-nm\|_R
\le C_hR\left[
\frac{\sqrt n\,3^k}{N_k}
+\frac{n^{1/R}(2R)^k}{N_k}
\right].
\tag{021.18}
\]

Conditioning on each \(Z_j=z\) justifies this use of independence among the \(X_i\). The \(n\) variables \(U(Z_j)\) share the same \(X_i\), but the elementary maximum-moment bound does not require independence:

\[
E\max_j|U(Z_j)-nm|
\le\left(\sum_j E|U(Z_j)-nm|^R\right)^{1/R}.
\]

Since \(n^{1/R}=d\), it follows that

\[
E\max_j U(Z_j)
\le\frac n{N_k}(1+E_1+E_2),
\tag{021.19}
\]

where one can take

\[
E_1=C_hRd\,3^k/\sqrt n=d^{-R/2+O(1)},\qquad
E_2=C_hRd^2(2R)^k/n=d^{-R+\ell+O(1)}.
\tag{021.20}
\]

Both tend to zero.

The correlation matrix in (021.14) is

\[
\mathsf C=\tau^{-2}\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X)
\preceq\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}\frac T\tau
\preceq\frac1{N_k}\frac T\tau.
\tag{021.21}
\]

Let \(A=E\|T/\tau-I\|\). Because \(T/\tau\) has diagonal one, its off-diagonal part is exactly \(T/\tau-I\), and
\(E\|T/\tau\|\le1+A\). Substituting (021.19) and (021.21) into (021.14) gives

\[
A\le
C_h\sqrt{\frac{n\log n}{N_k}(1+A)}
+C_h\log n\sqrt{\frac n{N_k}}\sqrt{1+E_1+E_2}.
\tag{021.22}
\]

This verifies the specific recursion used in 003. Put \(x=n/N_k\).
Using \(\sqrt{1+A}\le1+\sqrt A\) and
\(c\sqrt A\le A/2+c^2/2\) closes (021.22) to

\[
A\le C_h\{\log n\sqrt x+x\log n\}
\le C_h\log n\sqrt x
\]

eventually. Hence for each fixed \(0<\eta<\delta_*\),

\[
A\le C_{h,\eta}R^2d^{-\eta/2}.
\tag{021.23}
\]

These bounds are summable in \(\ell\).

For the low feature matrix \(\Psi\in\mathbb R^{n\times D}\), (021.4) gives the exact row norm \(\|\Psi(X_i)\|^2=D\) and population covariance \(I_D\). Standard matrix Chernoff therefore gives

\[
P\{\|\Psi^\top\Psi/n-I_D\|>1/2\}
\le2D\exp(-cn/D).
\tag{021.24}
\]

This is summable: \(\log D=O(R^2)\), whereas
\(n/D=\exp((1-\delta_*+o(1))R\log2)\).

Define the joint good-design event

\[
\mathcal E_\ell=
\{\|T/\tau-I\|\le1/2,\;
  \|\Psi^\top\Psi/n-I_D\|\le1/2\}.
\tag{021.25}
\]

Markov's inequality, (021.23), and (021.24) yield

\[
\sum_\ell P(\mathcal E_\ell^c)<\infty.
\tag{021.26}
\]

The two good events need not be independent.

## 5. Complete deterministic low/high transfer

All the calculations in this section take place on \(\mathcal E_\ell\). Let \(\Lambda\) be the positive diagonal Mercer matrix of the low basis. Write a low signal as
\(f_\theta(x)=\sum_{s<k,a}\theta_{s,a}\psi_{s,a}(x)\); its label vector is \(\Psi\theta\), and \(\|f_\theta\|_2=\|\theta\|\).

Fixing low coefficients \(a_0\), the minimum high-RKHS-norm interpolant of the residual \(y-\Psi a_0\) is its representer in the tail sections. Its squared high RKHS norm is
\((y-\Psi a_0)^\top T^{-1}(y-\Psi a_0)\). Thus minimizing the full norm is exactly minimizing

\[
a_0^\top\Lambda^{-1}a_0+
(y-\Psi a_0)^\top T^{-1}(y-\Psi a_0).
\tag{021.27}
\]

Put

\[
W=\tau T^{-1},\qquad
M=\tau\Lambda^{-1},\qquad
G=M+\Psi^\top W\Psi.
\]

The two spectral events imply

\[
\tfrac23I\preceq W\preceq2I,\qquad
\tfrac n3 I_D\preceq\Psi^\top W\Psi\preceq3nI_D,\qquad
G^{-1}\preceq(3/n)I_D.
\tag{021.28}
\]

Differentiating (021.27) gives the exact formulas

\[
\widehat a=Ly,\qquad
L=G^{-1}\Psi^\top W,\qquad
r=Qy,\qquad Q=I-\Psi L.
\tag{021.29}
\]

The high predictor equals \(K_T(\cdot,X)T^{-1}r\). Its squared population norm is

\[
\|\widehat f_T\|_2^2
=r^\top T^{-1}H_TT^{-1}r,\qquad
H_T=\sum_{s\ge k}\lambda_s^2P_s(X,X).
\]

Because \(\lambda_s\le\lambda_k\) for \(s\ge k\), the following is a PSD matrix comparison:

\[
H_T\preceq\lambda_kT.
\]

Consequently

\[
\|\widehat f_T\|_2^2
\le\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}r^\top Wr.
\tag{021.30}
\]

### 5.1 The weighted residual contraction

Set \(B_0=W^{1/2}\Psi\). Since \(G=M+B_0^\top B_0\) and \(M\succ0\), the singular values of \(B_0G^{-1/2}\) are at most one. Therefore

\[
0\preceq C_0:=I-B_0G^{-1}B_0^\top\preceq I.
\]

The exact conjugation identity is

\[
W^{1/2}QW^{-1/2}=C_0.
\]

It follows that

\[
Q^\top WQ=W^{1/2}C_0^2W^{1/2}\preceq W.
\tag{021.31}
\]

This argument does not commute \(W\), \(G\), or \(\Lambda\). In particular, treating \(Q\) itself as a symmetric Euclidean projection is unnecessary and would generally be false.

### 5.2 Uniform noiseless signal error

For \(y=\Psi\theta\), define \(e=\widehat a-\theta\). From (021.29),

\[
e=G^{-1}(G-M)\theta-\theta=-G^{-1}M\theta,\qquad
r=-\Psi e.
\tag{021.32}
\]

For \(\mu=\min\operatorname{diag}\Lambda=\lambda_{k-1}\) and
\(a=\tau/(n\mu)\),

\[
\|e\|\le3a\|\theta\|.
\tag{021.33}
\]

Using (021.28) and (021.30),

\[
\|\widehat f_T\|_2^2
\le\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}e^\top\Psi^\top W\Psi e
\le3b\|e\|^2,
\qquad b=n\lambda_k/\tau.
\tag{021.34}
\]

Low and high harmonics are population-orthogonal, so (021.33)–(021.34) prove

\[
\sup_{\|\theta\|\le B}
\|\mathcal I_X(\Psi\theta)-f_\theta\|_2^2
\le9B^2a^2(1+3b).
\tag{021.35}
\]

This is a deterministic operator bound for the entire low ball. No fixed-degree bound on individual basis functions is used.

## 6. Conditional covariance noise and the actual random supremum

Write \(\Sigma=E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^\top\mid X]\preceq\sigma^2I_n\).
First, since \(W^2\preceq2W\), congruence gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
LL^\top
&=G^{-1}\Psi^\top W^2\Psi G^{-1}\\
&\preceq2G^{-1}(\Psi^\top W\Psi)G^{-1}
\preceq2G^{-1}
\preceq(6/n)I_D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{021.36}
\]

Thus the low noise risk obeys

\[
E[\|L\varepsilon\|^2\mid X]
=\operatorname{tr}(L\Sigma L^\top)
\le6\sigma^2D/n.
\tag{021.37}
\]

For the high noise predictor, (021.30) and (021.31) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[\|\mathcal I_X(\varepsilon)_T\|_2^2\mid X]
&\le\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma Q^\top WQ)\\
&\le\sigma^2\frac{\lambda_k}{\tau}\operatorname{tr}W
\le2\sigma^2 b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{021.38}
\]

Population orthogonality then gives

\[
E[\|\mathcal I_X(\varepsilon)\|_2^2\mid X]
\le\sigma^2(6D/n+2b).
\tag{021.39}
\]

The PSD covariance comparison alone is sufficient for these steps. Noise-coordinate independence, Gaussianity, and independence of the noise law from \(X\) are not used.

For any fixed signal, linearity and conditional mean zero remove the signal/noise cross term in conditional expected risk. More importantly, the random supremum in (021.3) requires a simultaneous bound. The same-noise convention ensures the one function \(\mathcal I_X(\varepsilon)\) is common to all signals. Hence pathwise

\[
S_\ell\le
2\sup_{\|\theta\|\le B}
\|\mathcal I_X(\Psi\theta)-f_\theta\|_2^2
+2\|\mathcal I_X(\varepsilon)\|_2^2.
\]

Combining (021.35) and (021.39),

\[
E[S_\ell\mid X]
\le18B^2a^2(1+3b)+\sigma^2(12D/n+4b)
\quad\text{on }\mathcal E_\ell.
\tag{021.40}
\]

This verifies all constants in 003's corresponding bound. The supremum is measurable: at each fixed level its parameter set is a finite-dimensional compact ball, and its objective is a continuous function of the coefficients. Equivalently, one may take the supremum over a countable dense subset.

The assumptions in (021.2) are sufficient. In fact, for (021.40) alone, the direct second-moment bound \(E[\varepsilon\varepsilon^\top\mid X]\preceq\sigma^2I\) suffices without a separate centering hypothesis. Centering is needed if one phrases that input only as a covariance bound or removes the fixed-signal cross term exactly.

## 7. Almost-sure closure under arbitrary coupling of levels

By (021.12), (021.13), and \(\delta_*<1/2\), the right side of (021.40) is bounded by a deterministic sequence

\[
q_\ell\le C_{h,B,\sigma}\left[
d^{-2(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}
+d^{-(1-\delta_*)+o(1)}
+d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}
\right]
=d^{-\delta_*+o(1)}O_{h,B,\sigma}(1).
\tag{021.41}
\]

In particular, \(\sum_\ell q_\ell<\infty\): for any fixed
\(0<\beta<\delta_*\), eventually \(q_\ell\le C_\beta d^{-\beta}\), and \(d^{-\beta}=2^{-\beta2^\ell}\) is summable.

For every \(t>0\), conditional Markov gives the unconditional bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
P\{S_\ell>t\}
&\le P(\mathcal E_\ell^c)
   +E\!\left[\mathbf1_{\mathcal E_\ell}
       P\{S_\ell>t\mid X\}\right]\\
&\le P(\mathcal E_\ell^c)+q_\ell/t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{021.42}
\]

Equations (021.26) and (021.41) make this summable. First Borel–Cantelli therefore says that \(\{S_\ell>1/m\}\) occurs only finitely often, almost surely, for each positive integer \(m\). Intersecting these countably many probability-one events proves (021.3).

No step invokes independence between levels. First Borel–Cantelli is valid for every coupling consistent with the given levelwise assumptions.

For completeness, the minimum-norm interpolant is defined almost surely even outside the good event. The design points are distinct almost surely, and the Gaussian kernel is strictly positive definite on finitely many distinct points. A direct check is

\[
c^\top K_h(X,X)c
=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{m\ge0}\frac{\gamma^m}{m!}
\left\|\sum_i c_iX_i^{\otimes m}\right\|^2.
\]

If this were zero, the signed coefficients \(c_i\) would annihilate every polynomial evaluated on the design. Polynomial interpolation on finitely many distinct points then forces every \(c_i=0\). Thus the Gram matrix is invertible. A countable union of levelwise collision null events is null under every coupling.

## 8. Exact degree boundary and scope

The endpoint is strict: \(s<k\). Since \(N_k>n\) eventually, the evaluation map from \(\mathcal H_{d,k}\) into \(\mathbb R^n\) has a nonzero kernel at every design. For \(B>0\), choose \(v\) in this kernel with \(\|v\|_2=B\). In the noiseless case the minimum-norm interpolant of \(v(X)=0\) is zero, so its squared error is \(B^2\).

The failure also holds for any one shared noise vector: the two signals \(v\) and \(-v\) give identical labels \(\varepsilon\). Their two squared errors average to
\(\|\mathcal I_X\varepsilon\|_2^2+B^2\), so the supremum is at least \(B^2\). Therefore uniform consistency over the whole degree-\(k\) ball is impossible.

This is a pathwise supremum argument. It does not imply that every predetermined degree-\(k\) signal fails, and no such claim is needed.

Other exact limits of the result:

- The risk is population squared \(L^2\) prediction error for the realized training labels.
- The simultaneous result uses one noise vector per level, shared across the indexed signal family. Independent noise draws for infinitely many candidate signals would describe a different supremum.
- The signal radius, bandwidth, and covariance bound are fixed across levels.
- The proof controls conditional expected risk on good designs and realized risk almost surely. It does not integrate inverse-Gram tails on bad designs, so it does not establish convergence of the unconditional all-training expectation.
- The original P22 wording that “the fixed-degree hypothesis is essential” is too restrictive if read literally. The valid boundary statement is that one cannot extend this whole-ball conclusion through degree \(k\); degrees up to \(k-1\) are covered by the audited extension.
- No microscopic full-Gram hard-edge theorem, Gaussian-design ansatz, or independence of harmonic coordinates enters this proof.
