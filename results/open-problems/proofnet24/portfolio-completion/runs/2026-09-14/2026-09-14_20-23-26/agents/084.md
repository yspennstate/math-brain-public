## 2. Conventions and primary normalization

All representation rings are complex representation rings. The canonical trace is
\[
\tau_G\!\left(\sum_g a_g g\right)=a_e,
\]
extended to matrices by the unnormalized matrix trace.

The primary technical source checked is Wolfgang Lück, *The relation between the Baum-Connes Conjecture and the Trace Conjecture*, Invent. Math. 149 (2002), 123–152, [author-hosted paper](https://him-lueck.uni-bonn.de/data/trc.pdf). Section 3 defines \(\theta_C\) by its character, the indicator of the generators of \(C\). Section 4 describes the finite-orbit Chern map; Theorem 5.4 identifies assembly of cyclic Chern data with ordinary pushforward followed by representation induction. These are the only general Chern-theoretic inputs. The finite lattice calculation below is proved directly.

For \(C=C_p\), write \(\rho_C\) for the regular representation. Then
\[
\theta_C=1-\rho_C/p,\qquad
\theta_C(V)=V-\frac{\dim V}{p}\rho_C.
\]
Consequently
\[
R_C^{\rm prim}=\ker(\dim:R_{\mathbb C}(C)\to\mathbb Z).
\]
In particular this lattice has basis \(\chi_a-\chi_0\), \(a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). It must not be replaced silently by either the projected lattice \(\theta_C R(C)\) or the whole ring \(R(C)\).

For \(G=C_p^2\), all Weyl groups \(N_GC/C_GC\) are trivial and all fixed-point quotient spaces for \(X=\mathrm{pt}\) are points. Thus the archived Chern matrix is exactly the map
\[
\Phi:\mathbb Z\oplus\bigoplus_{|C|=p}\ker(\dim:R(C)\to\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow R(G)
\]
given by induction, with the first summand inducing the trivial representation from the identity subgroup.

## 3. Exact primitive-lattice theorem

Identify \(\widehat G=\mathbb F_p^2\). For \(u\in\mathbb F_p^2\), let
\[
\chi_u(g)=\exp(2\pi i\,u\cdot g/p).
\]
Thus \(R(G)\cong\mathbb Z^{\mathbb F_p^2}\), with coefficient basis \(\chi_u\).

Choose one generator \(v\) for each subgroup of order \(p\):
\[
\mathcal V=\{(1,t):t\in\mathbb F_p\}\cup\{(0,1)\}.
\]
For \(a\in\mathbb F_p\), define the coefficient vector
\[
H_{v,a}(u)=1_{\{u\cdot v=a\}},
\qquad
D_{v,a}=H_{v,a}-H_{v,0}\quad(a\ne0).
\]
Restriction of \(\chi_u\) to \(\langle v\rangle\) has label \(u\cdot v\), so
\[
H_{v,a}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle v\rangle}^G\chi_a.
\]
The regular representation has coefficient vector \(\mathbf1\), the all-ones vector.

Let \(M_p\) be the square \(p^2\times p^2\) matrix with columns
\[
\mathbf1,\quad D_{v,a}\quad(v\in\mathcal V,\ a\ne0),
\]
and put \(\Lambda_{\rm prim}=M_p\mathbb Z^{p^2}\).

**Theorem 084-PRIMITIVE-SMITH.** For every prime \(p\),
\[
R(G)/\Lambda_{\rm prim}
\cong
\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z
\oplus
(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{p(p+1)/2-1}.
\]
Equivalently, the Smith diagonal is
\[
\boxed{
1^{\,p(p-1)/2},\quad
p^{\,p(p+1)/2-1},\quad p^2.
}
\]
Therefore the archive's specifically defined saturation exponent is
\[
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{pt}}=p^2.}
\]

### Exact inverse and membership criterion

For \(z\in\mathbb Q^{\mathbb F_p^2}\), set
\[
T(z)=\sum_u z(u),\qquad
S_v(a;z)=\sum_{u\cdot v=a}z(u).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
z=\frac{T(z)}{p^2}\mathbf1+
\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\sum_{a\ne0}
\left(\frac{S_v(a;z)}p-\frac{T(z)}{p^2}\right)D_{v,a}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

To verify this, define
\[
h_v(a)=S_v(a;z)/p-T(z)/p^2.
\]
The \(p\) values of \(h_v\) sum to zero, so
\[
\sum_{a\ne0}h_v(a)D_{v,a}(u)=h_v(u\cdot v).
\]
For fixed \(u,w\), the equation \(u\cdot v=w\cdot v\) holds for all \(p+1\) directions when \(u=w\), and for exactly one direction when \(u\ne w\). Hence
\[
\sum_v S_v(u\cdot v;z)=pz(u)+T(z).
\]
Substitution proves (1). Since the displayed matrix is square, (1) proves its invertibility.

Its inverse has first row \(p^{-2}\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\); its row indexed by \((v,a)\) is
\[
p^{-2}\bigl(pH_{v,a}^{\mathsf T}-\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\bigr).
\]
In particular \(p^2M_p^{-1}\) is integral. For integral \(z\), (1) gives the exact criterion
\[
\boxed{
z\in\Lambda_{\rm prim}
\iff
T(z)\equiv0\pmod {p^2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
S_v(a;z)\equiv0\pmod p\ \text{for every }v,a.
}
\tag{2}
\]
It is enough to check \(a\ne0\); the \(a=0\) condition then follows from their sum.

Thus \(p^2R(G)\subset\Lambda_{\rm prim}\). For a coefficient basis vector \(\delta_u\), \(T(\delta_u)=1\), so its class in the quotient has exact order \(p^2\). In particular
\[
p\delta_u\notin\Lambda_{\rm prim}.
\]
Any substitution \(E=p\) for this primitive-lattice convention would be false.

### Determinant and full quotient structure

Use the ordinary Euclidean inner product on coefficient vectors. The regular column is perpendicular to every \(D_{v,a}\). Distinct direction blocks are perpendicular because two affine lines in distinct directions meet once. Within a direction,
\[
\langle D_{v,a},D_{v,b}\rangle
=p(\delta_{ab}+1).
\]
Therefore \(M_p^{\mathsf T}M_p\) has one block \(p^2\) and \(p+1\) blocks
\[
p(I_{p-1}+J_{p-1}).
\]
Here \(J\) is the all-ones matrix; \(I+J\) has determinant \(p\). Each direction block has determinant \(p^p\). Thus
\[
|\det M_p|
=p^{m_p},
\qquad
m_p=\frac{p^2+p+2}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(Q=R(G)/\Lambda_{\rm prim}\). By (2), total coefficient sum gives a surjective homomorphism
\[
\overline T:Q\to\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z.
\]
The class of \(\delta_0\), which has exact order \(p^2\), splits this map. If \(\overline T([z])=0\), then \(T(z)\) is divisible by \(p^2\). The vector \(pz\) satisfies (2), so \(\ker\overline T\) is annihilated by \(p\).

Equation (3) now gives
\[
|\ker\overline T|=p^{m_p-2}.
\]
Hence it is an \(\mathbb F_p\)-vector space of dimension
\[
m_p-2=p(p+1)/2-1.
\]
This proves the claimed quotient and Smith form without assuming a classification result for affine-plane incidence matrices.

## 4. Exact trace row and trace-visible saturation

Fourier decomposition gives mutually orthogonal minimal projections
\[
e_u=\frac1{p^2}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\chi_u(g)}\,g,
\qquad
\tau_G(e_u)=p^{-2}.
\]
For completeness, the coefficient of \(g\) in \(e_ue_w\) is obtained by summing
\(\overline{\chi_u(h)}\overline{\chi_w(g-h)}\) over \(h\); character orthogonality gives \(e_ue_w=\delta_{u,w}e_u\). Their sum is the identity, so \(C^*(G)=C_r^*(G)\cong\mathbb C^{p^2}\) and its \(K_0\) is the stated representation lattice.

Thus
\[
\tau(z)=T(z)/p^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\tau(R(G))=p^{-2}\mathbb Z.}
\tag{4}
\]
On the primitive Chern basis,
\[
\ell=(1,0,\ldots,0),
\]
since \(\tau(\mathbf1)=1\) and each difference column has dimension zero. Formula (1) gives
\[
\boxed{\ell M_p^{-1}=p^{-2}(1,\ldots,1).}
\tag{5}
\]
This is a complete concrete realization of the newer P25 trace-row formula.

Moreover \(\tau(\Lambda_{\rm prim})=\mathbb Z\). The induced map
\[
Q\longrightarrow p^{-2}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z
\]
is \(\overline T/p^2\). Its kernel is exactly the elementary abelian summand in the proof above. The scalar trace detects the cyclic \(p^2\)-quotient; the other \(p\)-torsion factors are invisible to it.

Here the cyclic lcm is \(L_{\mathrm{pt}}=p\). Consequently the original bound \((E L)^{-1}\mathbb Z=p^{-3}\mathbb Z\) is valid but weaker than the exact \(p^{-2}\mathbb Z\). The archive correctly says that cyclic orders alone are insufficient. The exact value of its defined \(E\) is \(p^2\).

## 5. A different integral lattice has saturation exponent \(p\)

Let
\[
\Lambda_{\rm cyc}
=\sum_{\substack{C\le G\\C\ {\rm cyclic}}}
\operatorname{Ind}_C^G R(C).
\]
This uses whole cyclic representation rings. It is larger than the primitive intersection lattice.

**Theorem 084-CYCLIC-SMITH.**
\[
\boxed{
R(G)/\Lambda_{\rm cyc}
\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{p(p-1)/2},
\qquad
\exp(R(G)/\Lambda_{\rm cyc})=p.
}
\]
Also
\[
\boxed{\tau(\Lambda_{\rm cyc})=p^{-1}\mathbb Z.}
\]

Proof. It suffices to adjoin the \(p+1\) vectors \(H_{v,0}\) to \(\Lambda_{\rm prim}\), because all other fibers differ from these by primitive columns. Since
\[
pH_{v,0}=\mathbf1-\sum_{a\ne0}D_{v,a},
\]
their classes modulo \(\Lambda_{\rm prim}\) have order dividing \(p\).

The primitive coordinates of \(\sum_v n_vH_{v,0}\) are
\[
\alpha=\frac{\sum_v n_v}{p},
\qquad
\beta_{v,a}=-n_v/p.
\]
They are all integral exactly when every \(n_v\) is divisible by \(p\). Hence
\[
\Lambda_{\rm cyc}/\Lambda_{\rm prim}
\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^{p+1}.
\]
Using (3),
\[
[R(G):\Lambda_{\rm cyc}]
=p^{m_p-(p+1)}=p^{p(p-1)/2}.
\]

For every \(u\),
\[
p\delta_u=\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}H_{v,u\cdot v}-\mathbf1,
\]
by the same affine-line counting used in (1). Thus this quotient is annihilated by \(p\), proving its structure. Finally every \(H_{v,a}\) has \(p\) coefficients equal to one, hence trace \(1/p\). This proves the trace assertion.

The conventions therefore give the exact comparison:

| Integral lattice inside \(R(G)\) | Quotient exponent | Trace image |
|---|---:|---:|
| Primitive intersection Chern lattice \(\Lambda_{\rm prim}\) | \(p^2\) | \(\mathbb Z\) |
| Whole cyclic-induction lattice \(\Lambda_{\rm cyc}\) | \(p\) | \(p^{-1}\mathbb Z\) |
| Full representation lattice \(R(G)\) | \(1\) | \(p^{-2}\mathbb Z\) |

The product \(p\cdot p\) is a sharp denominator bound with the second lattice. It does not compute the saturation exponent of the first lattice.

## 6. Conditional general refinement of the archived bound

**Corollary 084-PRIMITIVE-TRACE-REFINEMENT.** Assume the archived construction of \(\mathcal C_X\) and its stated Lück trace compatibility for a finite proper \(G\)-CW complex. For exactly that primitive intersection lattice,
\[
\boxed{
\tau\mu_X(\operatorname{ch}_X(\mathcal C_X))\subset\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\tau\mu_X(K_X)\subset E_X^{-1}\mathbb Z.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, for every nontrivial finite cyclic \(C\), the identity does not generate \(C\). Lück's definition of \(\theta_C\) therefore gives
\[
\dim(V)=\chi_V(e)=0
\quad\text{for }V\in\operatorname{im}\theta_C.
\]
The trace of a Chern tensor with ordinary pushforward index \(n\) is
\[
n\,\dim(V)/|C|=0.
\]
Only \(C=\{1\}\) contributes; its pushforward index is integral and its Weyl group is trivial. Integer-generated tensors and their images under the Weyl relations preserve this scalar integrality. This uses precisely the definition and compatibility checked in [Lück, Section 3 and Theorem 5.4](https://him-lueck.uni-bonn.de/data/trc.pdf).

Finally \(E_XK_X\subset K_X\cap\operatorname{ch}_X(\mathcal C_X)\) proves (6). Thus the additional \(L_X\) factor is unnecessary for this particular choice of primitive integral lattice. The archived inclusion remains true.

For the archived partial sums \(\sum_{i=1}^N p^{-k_i}\), with strictly increasing positive \(k_i\), the reduced denominator is \(p^{k_N}\). Under the same general setup, a preimage at scale \(d\) would therefore satisfy the sharper implication
\[
\boxed{k_N\le v_p(E_d).}
\]
This is a deduction from the archived general setup, not an independent construction or audit of all its integral Chern lattices. The finite-group calculations above require no such unresolved construction.

## 7. Rips realization and exact arithmetic checks

For any finite word metric on \(G=C_p^2\), if \(d\ge\operatorname{diam}(G)\), then \(P_d(G)\) is the full simplex on \(G\). Linear contraction to its barycenter is \(G\)-equivariant. It restricts to each fixed-point space and descends to every fixed-point quotient. Thus the point computation gives, at this full-simplex scale,
\[
E_d=p^2,\qquad q_d=p^{-2},\qquad L_d=p.
\]
For the standard symmetric coordinate generators, \(\operatorname{diam}(G)=2\lfloor p/2\rfloor\).

Exact JavaScript integer calculations independently built the matrices for \(p=2,3,5,7\). Fraction-free Bareiss elimination used BigInt arithmetic for determinants. The checks verified \(p^2M_p^{-1}M_p=p^2I\) using the displayed integral inverse numerator, and computed ranks modulo \(p\).

For \(p=2\), one explicit primitive matrix is
