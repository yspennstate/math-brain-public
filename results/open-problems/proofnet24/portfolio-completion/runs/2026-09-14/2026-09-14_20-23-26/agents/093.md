## 2. Required repair: rational input scope in four-type Section 8

The opening of Section 8 promises a finite exact algorithm at every positive rational tolerance, while the standing coefficient hypotheses in Section 1 permit arbitrary real coefficients. Only later does Section 8 discuss clearing coefficient denominators. Report065 states the missing premise explicitly: all coefficients are rational, supplied with an explicit finite rational encoding.

The finite bit-complexity theorem therefore needs that premise in its principal statement. Suggested insertion:

> In this section, assume every coefficient \(P_{ij,k}\) is rational and supplied by explicit integer numerators and denominators. Let \(b\) denote the total coefficient encoding length, and let \(h\) denote the encoding length of the positive rational tolerance.

This is a scope correction, not a change to the rounding argument or its constants. It leaves the earlier mathematical results, which apply to arbitrary real coefficient arrays satisfying their stated hypotheses, intact.

The distinction has mathematical content. Here is a concrete obstruction to an unqualified computability reading. Choose a noncomputable real \(\alpha\in(0,1)\). On types \(1,2,3\), specify the unordered parental rows by
\[
11\longmapsto \alpha\,2+(1-\alpha)\,3,\quad
22\longmapsto3,\quad33\longmapsto1,\quad
12\longmapsto3,\quad13\longmapsto2,\quad23\longmapsto1.
\]
For rows involving type 4, use
\[
14\longmapsto2,\quad24\longmapsto3,\quad34\longmapsto1,\quad44\longmapsto1.
\]
Parent symmetry completes the array. It is a four-type strictly non-Volterra QSO and \(V_4\equiv0\). Its unique fixed point \(p\) has support exactly \(\{1,2,3\}\): a fixed support of size at most two is impossible by Section 2. In particular \(p_1>0\). On that face,
\[
p_2=\alpha p_1^2+2p_1p_3,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{p_2-2p_1p_3}{p_1^2}.
\]
Thus a computable fixed point would make \(\alpha\) computable. A uniform finite-accuracy computation for arbitrary real inputs requires an input/oracle model; it cannot be the finite rational bit model of report065. The repaired rational-input theorem has no such problem.

## 4. Four-type integration check: dimensions, norms, and exact horizon

### 4.1 The four-type mechanism is stated at its correct dimension

The transfer lemma uses a partition into two pairs. The forced group probabilities \(p_{ki}=0\) and \(p_{\ell j}=1\) use both strict non-Volterra exclusion and the fact that each part has exactly two elements. Nothing in the principal note imports that estimate into a larger-dimensional simplex.

For two distinct fixed points, monotonicity excludes a singleton positive or negative support of their difference. In four coordinates this forces a two-positive/two-negative partition, with no zero coordinate in the difference. The coordinate cap
\[
c=(3-\sqrt5)/2
\]
then gives the strict contradiction \(h\le2ch<h\). No positivity of all allowed coefficients or of all fixed-point coordinates was introduced.

The boundary statement is consistent: an invariant three-type support forces the omitted output to vanish on parent pairs within that support, while strict exclusion already forces it to vanish on pairs containing the omitted type. Hence the omitted output coordinate vanishes identically.

### 4.2 Equal column sums, rather than stochasticity of the averaged derivative, are used correctly

The polarization matrix \(A(m)\) is column-stochastic. For
\[
B=(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A(m)
\]
the columns sum to \(1+\lambda\), not one. Equation (8) correctly uses the induced \(\ell^1\) norm on the zero-sum subspace for equal-column-sum matrices:
\[
\|B|_{\sum z_i=0}\|_{1\to1}
=\max_{i,j}\frac12\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1.
\]
There is no missing factor of two in the transfer decomposition.

The cap invariance, the four cut cases, and the comparison with \(2\lambda\) establish the stated
\[
L(q,\lambda)=1-\lambda(1-2q).
\]
In particular the residual multiplier is
\[
\|x-x^*\|_1\le5\|V(x)-x\|_1
\]
on \(K_{2/5}\). A residual of the averaged map \(T\) would instead have multiplier \(25/2\); report065 and the principal Section 8 consistently certify the residual of \(V\).

The perturbation norm is the maximum parental-row \(\ell^1\) distance. Each signed group perturbation is bounded by half that row distance, and the two forced within-pair rows contribute zero. This yields
\[
(1-2c)^{-1}-2c=2\sqrt5-1
\]
with the norm convention actually stated. The singleton-sign case gives the stronger bound \(\|x_P-x_Q\|_1\le\eta\), so zero coordinates in a difference do not leave a gap.

The determinant argument restricts to the three-dimensional tangent space and removes the radial eigenvalue two with the correct minus sign. The strict positive-real-eigenvalue bound implies \(\det(I-J|_E)>0\); it does not claim that all original discrete eigenvalues have modulus below one.

### 4.3 The small-parental-offspring extension keeps its needed strict threshold

In Section 6, \(a=1-\delta\), \(s=\sqrt{1+4a^2}\), and the strict hypothesis gives \(s>2\). Thus \(\kappa=s-2>0\) and \(L<1\). The repeated-parent definition of \(\delta\) counts its single parental type once, as required by the coordinate majorant.

The formulas
\[
a(1-q)=\frac{s-1}{2},\quad
1-qs=\frac{2a(s-2a)}{s+1}>0
\]
give \(f'(q)=1-\lambda s>0\) for \(\lambda\le q\). They justify the monotone majorizing cap sequence and its rate
\[
\rho=1-\lambda(s+1)/2\in(0,1).
\]
The geometric sum of the varying contraction excess is exactly the exponent in equation (17). No endpoint assertion at \(\delta=1-\sqrt3/2\) is silently included.

## 5. Entropy integration check: curvature, equality, and full-line strictness

All displayed entropy constants use natural logarithms, as in the differentiations of \(f\log f\). An explicit “all logarithms are natural” convention would make the units especially clear; entropy measured in bits would divide these curvature and Taylor coefficients by \(\log 2\).

The density convention is coherent: \(N_t\) acts on densities relative to the fixed product measure \(\mu=P_D\). Reversibility makes this the law's resampling transition expressed as a density. For \(0<t<1\), redrawing every coordinate makes every density value strictly positive, allowing the divisions and logarithms in equation (1).

The pair bracket has exactly the common factor \(p(1-p)q(1-q)\). Setting \(r=ad/(bc)\), \(X=a+d\), and \(Y=b+c\) gives
\[
X(r^{-1}-1+\log r)+Y(r-1-\log r)\ge0.
\]
The corrupted auxiliary fraction commands do not affect this identity. Positivity of \(X,Y\) makes equality equivalent to \(r=1\).

The resampling inclusion formula uses principal minors and diagonal columns, so it is valid for complex Hermitian kernels with changing diagonals and without simultaneous diagonalization. The cross-entropy against the fixed product reference is affine in the one-site marginals. Hence relative-entropy convexity converts to the claimed Shannon-entropy concavity.

The sharp curvature extension agrees with report068 Section 7. If \(\delta_i=K_{ii}-d_i\) and \(m_i=d_i+t\delta_i\), the conditional Cauchy–Schwarz remainder can be written as
\[
R_i=\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_i g-c_i(X_i)g)^2}{g},
\quad
c_i(1)=\frac{m_i-d_i}{m_i},\quad
c_i(0)=-\frac{m_i-d_i}{1-m_i}.
\]
With \(B_{ij}\) denoting the nonnegative pair brackets, the exact identity is
\[
-H''(t)-\sum_i\frac{\delta_i^2}{m_i(1-m_i)}
=t^{-2}\left(\sum_iR_i+2\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}\right).
\]
Thus equality in the final marginal bound cannot occur through cancellation of errors. If equality holds, all two-coordinate fibers satisfy \(ad=bc\), the positive log-density has no mixed discrete differences, and the law is a product. The two-site determinant identity then gives \(tK_{ij}=0\), so \(K\) is diagonal. Conversely diagonal \(K\) gives equality. The one-site case is included.

On the full feasible line, the positive and negative halves have parameters \(t=s/s_+\) and \(t=s/s_-\) with nonzero endpoint parameters \(s_\pm\). The second-derivative factor is \(1/s_\pm^2\), while \(\delta_i=s_\pm A_{ii}\), so the curvature bound transforms to the expected numerator \(A_{ii}^2\). There is no missing scale or sign on the negative half.

Near zero the law has full support and its entropy is analytic, so the two derivatives agree. For a nonzero Hermitian direction, the feasible interval is bounded and contains a neighborhood of zero; strict curvature away from zero prevents any nontrivial affine interval. If \(A=0\), the entropy is constant and the assertion of concavity on the possibly unbounded feasible interval is immediate.

Subtracting the entropy from the sum of binary marginal entropies gives the same nonnegative remainder for total correlation. It is strictly positive away from zero when \(A\) has an off-diagonal entry. Total correlation has zero value and derivative at zero, so the two halves glue. Its strict convexity is compatible with a zero second derivative at the midpoint.

For the zero-diagonal expansion, the absence of an \(s^5\) term is justified by product orthogonality, not by an assumed evenness of the entire entropy function. The order-two density term uses two-coordinate contrasts, the order-three term uses three-coordinate contrasts, and their product has expectation zero. This yields the stated \(O(s^6)\) remainder and quartic coefficient. These observations establish the exact equality/strictness claims included in the current principal, even though the frozen source snapshot predates their insertion.

## 6. Critical integration check: order of limits, conditioning, and computability

### 6.1 The local limit theorem is used with a fixed finite law

For \(Y=\mathbf1_{\{X=1\}}\), the covariance is \(q(1-\mu)=q\Delta_r\), not \(q(1-q)\). Positive mass at 0, 1, and 2 gives the needed nondegeneracy. The Fourier domination using the 0–1 atom pair is valid for each fixed Fourier parameter and each fixed stage.

At stage \(r\), the standardized targets tend to
\[
\mp V_r/(q\Delta_r).
\]
These numbers may be extremely large as \(r\) varies, but are fixed while candidate \(n\) tends to infinity at that stage. The diagonal gates compare both local probabilities with the same positive \(c_r\), and both conditional laws with the corresponding mean \(\mp1\). No unproved uniform triangular-array CLT is required.

All five gates eventually pass simultaneously at a fixed stage. In particular G4 compares an exponential smallness bound to the actual changing rare-branch scale, and G5 compares the full multiple-atom bound to the same scale. A tiny \(c_r\) does not invalidate eventual feasibility.

### 6.2 The final-law restriction is relative, and the denominator decomposition is exhaustive

The mean adjustment is exact and all newly added positive masses are rational. G1 separates the two current pair atoms, the adjustment atom, and the previous support. Each later positive support point exceeds the preceding \(H_r\).

The future change affects only the background zero mass. On every background configuration of at most \(n\) coordinates, its likelihood ratio against the finite \(Q\) law lies in
\[
[\eta_r,1],\qquad
\eta_r\ge1-8n^{-3}.
\]
Conditioning on a positive-probability background event divides by a ratio lying in the same interval, giving a conditional density ratio in \([\eta_r,\eta_r^{-1}]\). This remains valid when the event probability is exceedingly small.

On the total-sum event, current adjustment and future atoms are impossible exactly. The routes \(J_r=0\), \(J_r=1\) with either sign, and \(J_r\ge2\) therefore cover every case. For the last route,
\[
\Pr(J_r\ge2)\le\tfrac12\mathbb E[J_r(J_r-1)]
\le2n^2p_r^2=2n^{-6}.
\]
No independence after conditioning is asserted.

Writing \(w_r=np_rc_r/\sqrt{n-1}\), the two single-sign branches have probabilities between \(\eta_r(1-\varepsilon_r)w_r\) and \((1+\varepsilon_r)w_r\), and the two bad-route bounds sum to at most \(2\varepsilon_rw_r\). This proves the denominator asymptotic, conditional good-route probability, and equal limiting branch weights used in Section 8.

The shift from \(Nq\) to \(nq\) is exactly \(-q/\sqrt n\), with scale factor \(\sqrt{(n-1)/n}\). It vanishes. The mixture characteristic function \(e^{-3t^2/32}\cos t\) establishes a non-Gaussian subsequential limit. The tree transfer preserves counts because the unique admissible cyclic rotation changes no coordinate multiset. The first-minimum argument includes the original endpoint and size-one case.

### 6.3 The fixed evaluator closes the earlier selector specification issue

The current Section 10.3 explicitly adopts the deterministic evaluator from report062 Section 5.3. This is a real integration improvement: a freely chosen enclosure routine could alter which candidate is first certified, whereas fixing the routine defines one law.

The routine's ingredients terminate:

- rational square-root bisection on a known finite bracket;
- alternating Gregory enclosures for the circle constant;
- exponential Taylor enclosures with the stated geometric remainder bound;
- Gaussian-integral Taylor enclosures, whose displayed remainder tends to zero at every fixed finite argument;
- monotonically evaluated interval endpoints and increasing internal precision until all denominators are positive and all requested output widths are achieved.

All actual denominator values are strictly positive. The positive normalizing probability in the conditional CDF formula follows from G1: \(0\le k^\pm\le N\), and a configuration of \(k^\pm\) ones and \(N-k^\pm\) zeros has positive mass. The CDF supremum is therefore a finite maximum over the two sides of its jumps.

Each candidate/precision visit is finite. A strict positive margin is eventually certified; a zero margin need never be decided. Dovetailing ensures that some simultaneously passing pair is eventually reached. Floors in G1 are decidable algebraically, and eligible \(N=2^\ell-1\) are nonsquares. This gives termination without a practical time bound.

For positive integer \(k\), generating stages until \(H_R\ge k\) decides its mass, because later positive support lies above \(H_R\). The effective zero-mass tail
\[
\sum_{s>R}h_s<
\frac{3\,2^{-32(R+1)}}{1-2^{-32}}
\]
then proves computability of the infinite law. The principal correctly distinguishes this certificate-selected law from the optional literal-minimum law.

The other substantive suggestions in report062 Section 13 are now present: attainable conditioning targets are explicit, and the largest-degree description is qualified by conditional probability tending to one. The remaining “optimize” wording is the optional edit discussed above.
