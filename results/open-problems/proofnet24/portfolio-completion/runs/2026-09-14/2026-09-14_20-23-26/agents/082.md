## 1. Setting and precise rate quantifiers

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}x=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where \(P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0\), every parent row sums to one, and \(P_{ij,k}=0\) when \(k\in\{i,j\}\). Zero allowed coefficients are permitted unless positivity is explicitly asserted. Put
\[
T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V,\qquad 0<\lambda\le1.
\]

An exact orbit with error proportional to \(q^n\), \(q>0\), rules out an upper rate \(r<q\), even if its finite prefactor may depend on that orbit. Separately, if a local estimate
\[
\|T_\lambda^n(p+h)-p\|\le C r^n\|h\|
\]
holds for all sufficiently small \(h\) and every \(n\), differentiation at an interior fixed point gives
\[
\|(DT_\lambda(p))^n h\|\le Cr^n\|h\|,
\]
and hence \(r\ge\rho(DT_\lambda(p)|_{\mathbf1^\perp})\). A global estimate of this form must obey the same necessary condition. This spectral obstruction alone is not an optimal global upper bound.

## 2. A two-parameter family with an exact slow invariant line

Write
\[
s=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},\quad c=s^2=1-s=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
For \(\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]\), specify the following unordered parent rows; omitted offspring probabilities are zero.

| Parent pair | Offspring probabilities |
|---|---|
| \(11\) | \(3:\alpha,\ 4:1-\alpha\) |
| \(22\) | \(4:\alpha,\ 3:1-\alpha\) |
| \(33\) | \(1:\beta,\ 2:1-\beta\) |
| \(44\) | \(2:\beta,\ 1:1-\beta\) |
| \(12\) | \(3:1/2,\ 4:1/2\) |
| \(34\) | \(1:1/2,\ 2:1/2\) |
| \(13,14\) | \(2:1\) |
| \(23,24\) | \(1:1\) |

Every row is admissible. Use coordinates
\[
A=x_1+x_2,\quad B=1-A,\quad u=x_1-x_2,\quad v=x_3-x_4.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
A'=1-A^2,\qquad
u'=-2Bu+(2\beta-1)Bv,\qquad
v'=(2\alpha-1)Au.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently
\[
p=(s/2,s/2,c/2,c/2)
\]
is an interior fixed point for every parameter pair. The tangent eigenvalues are
\[
-2s=1-\sqrt5,\qquad
-c\pm\sqrt{c^2+cs(2\alpha-1)(2\beta-1)},
\tag{2}
\]
with the complex square-root interpretation when necessary.

Take \(\alpha=\beta=1\). Since \(c^2+cs=c=s^2\), the three eigenvalues become
\[
\boxed{\,1-\sqrt5,\ -1,\ \rho:=\sqrt5-2\,}.
\tag{3}
\]
The positive eigenvalue here is attained, not merely approached.

The plane \(A=s\) is invariant, and its \((u,v)\) dynamics in (1) is exactly linear. In particular, with
\[
h=(c,-c,1,-1),\qquad |t|\le c/2,
\]
the entire segment \(p+th\) lies in the simplex and
\[
V(p+th)=p+\rho th.
\]
Thus, for every \(0<\lambda\le1\), every nonzero such \(t\), and every \(n\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
T_\lambda^n(p+th)-p
=\bigl[1-(3-\sqrt5)\lambda\bigr]^n th.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
The multiplier in brackets lies in \([\rho,1)\). Formula (4) proves a necessary universal asymptotic rate lower bound
\[
R(\lambda)\ge1-(3-\sqrt5)\lambda
\tag{5}
\]
with the strongest per-orbit quantifier described in Section 1.

| Weight | Exact necessary lower rate from (4) | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| \(1/2\) | \((\sqrt5-1)/2\) | \(0.6180339887\ldots\) |
| \(51/100\) | \((51\sqrt5-53)/100\) | \(0.6103946685\ldots\) |
| \(c=(3-\sqrt5)/2\) | \(3\sqrt5-6\) | \(0.7082039325\ldots\) |

For example, the parent's capped-framework upper factor \((13-5\sqrt5)/2\) at weight \(c\) remains a valid sufficient bound. Its distinction from the lower value in this table leaves a genuine gap; neither value is certified as the best global factor.

For completeness, positivity does not remove the limiting local obstruction. Mix this tensor with \(\delta E\), where \(E\) is the equiprobable strictly excluded map defined in Section 4. For \(\delta>0\) every allowed coefficient is positive. At \(\delta=0\), no tangent eigenvalue in (3) equals one, so the implicit-function theorem gives an interior fixed point \(p_\delta\to p\), and its simple positive eigenvalue tends to \(\rho\). Therefore the supremum of necessary local rates over the class with every allowed coefficient positive is at least (5). This continuation assertion is a local spectral statement; formula (4) itself is asserted only for the displayed tensor.

### A separate obstruction above weight \(2/\sqrt5\)

In the same family, the averaged group mass always obeys the autonomous scalar map
\[
A_{n+1}=f_\lambda(A_n)
=(1-\lambda)A_n+\lambda(1-A_n^2),
\qquad f_\lambda'(s)=1-\sqrt5\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(\lambda>2/\sqrt5\), choose a neighborhood of \(s\) on which
\[
|f_\lambda(A)-s|>(1+\gamma)|A-s|
\]
for some \(\gamma>0\) whenever \(A\ne s\). An orbit converging to \(s\) must therefore eventually hit \(s\) exactly. The set of points eventually hitting \(s\) is countable: each iterate is a nonconstant polynomial, hence has finitely many preimages of \(s\). All other initial masses fail to converge to \(s\). Taking states
\[
x=(A/2,A/2,(1-A)/2,(1-A)/2)
\]
keeps \(u=v=0\) and realizes this scalar obstruction inside the simplex.

Hence global fixed-point convergence for every admissible tensor and every initial state is false for every
\[
\boxed{\lambda>2/\sqrt5\approx0.8944271910.}
\tag{7}
\]
This is an explicit upper obstruction, not the sharp threshold. At equality the tangent multiplier is \(-1\), so no local uniform geometric estimate with factor below one can hold there. No claim about global convergence at that equality is needed here.

## 3. A cyclic family giving exact complex spectral obstructions

Number types cyclically modulo four. Define \(C\) by:

- \(ii\) produces \(i+1\);
- the adjacent pair \(\{i,i+1\}\) produces \(i+2\);
- the opposite pair \(\{i,i+2\}\) splits equally between its two allowed offspring.

This is an unambiguous admissible tensor. At \(p_0=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\), its Jacobian is circulant, with first column
\[
(0,5/4,1/2,1/4)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The ambient eigenvalue two is radial; the tangent eigenvalues are exactly
\[
\boxed{-1,\ -1/2+i,\ -1/2-i.}
\tag{8}
\]

For \(0\le\delta\le1\), let \(C_\delta=(1-\delta)C+\delta E\). Both constituent maps fix \(p_0\), and \(DE(p_0)|_{\mathbf1^\perp}=-(2/3)I\). Thus its averaged tangent multipliers are
\[
m_0=1-\lambda(2-\delta/3),\qquad
m_\pm=1-\lambda(3/2+\delta/6)\pm i\lambda(1-\delta).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(\delta>0\), all allowed coefficients are at least \(\delta/3\).

At \(\delta=0\), the complex pair supplies the local rate obstruction
\[
R(\lambda)\ge
\sqrt{1-3\lambda+\frac{13}{4}\lambda^2}.
\tag{10}
\]
The fixed point is locally exponentially stable for this particular tensor precisely when \(0<\lambda<12/13\); above \(12/13\) the complex pair has modulus greater than one. At equality its modulus is one, so local exponential stability is excluded. These assertions do not determine nonlinear stability at equality.

For any fixed \(\lambda>12/13\), the same strict complex instability persists for sufficiently small positive \(\delta\), giving examples with every allowed coefficient positive. This complex example is different from the stronger real obstruction (7).

Combining the two explicit tensors, any universal local geometric rate, and hence any global estimate satisfying Section 1's local-uniform condition, must obey
\[
\boxed{
R(\lambda)\ge
\max\left\{
1-(3-\sqrt5)\lambda,\
|1-\sqrt5\lambda|,\
\sqrt{1-3\lambda+\frac{13}{4}\lambda^2}
\right\}.}
\tag{11}
\]
The first term also has the exact individual-orbit realization (4). We do not optimize (11) over weights or identify it with the optimal upper rate.

## 4. Independently reconstructed positive raw two-cycle theorem

Define the deterministic tensor \(V\) by

| Parent pair | Offspring |
|---|---|
| \(11\) | \(2\) |
| \(22,33,44\) | \(1\) |
| \(12\) | \(3\) |
| \(13,14\) | \(2\) |
| \(23,24,34\) | \(1\) |

On the simplex,
\[
V_1=(1-x_1)^2,\quad
V_2=x_1^2+2x_1(x_3+x_4),\quad
V_3=2x_1x_2,\quad V_4=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Every offspring excludes both parents.

Let \(E\) give probability \(1/3\) to each allowed diagonal-parent offspring and probability \(1/2\) to each allowed unequal-parent offspring. For every coordinate \(k\),
\[
E_k(x)=\frac12(1-x_k)^2-\frac16\sum_{j\ne k}x_j^2,
\qquad
\frac13(1-x_k)^2\le E_k(x)\le\frac49(1-x_k)^2.
\tag{13}
\]
The inequalities follow respectively from \(\sum_{j\ne k}x_j^2\le(1-x_k)^2\) and Cauchy–Schwarz.

Put
\[
W=W_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)V+\varepsilon E,\quad
0<\varepsilon\le1/50,\quad r=21/1000.
\]
All allowed coefficients of \(W\) are at least \(\varepsilon/3>0\). Define the disjoint closed phase caps
\[
C_r=\{x:x_1\ge1-r\},\qquad
D_r=\{x:x_1\le r^2\}
\]
inside \(\Delta_3\).

**Theorem.** For every displayed \(\varepsilon\), \(W\) has an attracting exact period-two orbit \(\{x_\varepsilon,y_\varepsilon\}\), with \(x_\varepsilon\in C_r\) and \(y_\varepsilon\in D_r\). Both points are interior, and each coordinate is at least \(25\varepsilon^3/243\). The return map \(W^2\) is a contraction on \(C_r\) with factor
\[
\kappa_\varepsilon=24(\varepsilon+r)\le123/125<1.
\tag{14}
\]
The orbit is the only periodic orbit meeting \(C_r\cup D_r\). All states in either cap converge to it with the corresponding phase. No uniqueness claim is made for cycles or attractors outside these caps.

### 4.1. Strict phase-cap mapping

For \(x\in C_r\), (12)–(13) give
\[
(Wx)_1\le(1-5\varepsilon/9)(1-x_1)^2
\le(1-5\varepsilon/9)r^2<r^2,
\]
so \(W(C_r)\) lies strictly inside the inequality defining \(D_r\).

If \(y\in D_r\), then
\[
(Wy)_1\ge(1-\varepsilon)(1-y_1)^2
\ge(1-\varepsilon)(1-r^2)^2,
\]
and
\[
1-(1-\varepsilon)(1-r^2)^2
\le\varepsilon+2r^2
\le\frac{10441}{500000}
<\frac{21}{1000}=r.
\tag{15}
\]
The last strict margin is \(59/500000\). Thus \(W(D_r)\) lies strictly inside the inequality defining \(C_r\), and \(W^2(C_r)\subset C_r\).

### 4.2. Zero base return derivative and uniform contraction

Write \(p=e_1,q=e_2\), and \(H=\{h:\sum_i h_i=0\}\). Directly from (12),
\[
V(p)=q,\quad V(q)=p,
\]
\[
DV(p)h=(0,-2h_2,2h_2,0),\qquad
DV(q)h=(-2h_1,0,2h_1,0).
\tag{16}
\]
Both ordered compositions of these tangent maps are zero.

For any symmetric QSO \(U\) at a simplex state, the full Jacobian has nonnegative entries and column sums two. Therefore its full and tangent \(\ell^1\) operator norms are at most two. From
\[
(DV(z))_{kj}=2\sum_iP_{ij,k}z_i
\]
and stochasticity,
\[
\|DV(z)-DV(z')\|_1\le2\|z-z'\|_1,\qquad
\|DW(z)-DV(z)\|_1\le4\varepsilon.
\tag{17}
\]
Also \(\|Wz-Vz\|_1\le2\varepsilon\).

For \(x\in C_r\), let \(y=Wx\). Since \(\|x-p\|_1\le2r\), the QSO Lipschitz bound yields
\[
\|y-q\|_1\le2\varepsilon+4r.
\]
Consequently
\[
\|DW(x)-DV(p)\|_1\le4(\varepsilon+r),
\]
\[
\|DW(y)-DV(q)\|_1
\le4\varepsilon+2(2\varepsilon+4r)
=8(\varepsilon+r).
\]
On \(H\), the zero product in (16) gives
\[
D(W^2)(x)=
[DW(y)-DV(q)]DW(x)
+DV(q)[DW(x)-DV(p)].
\]
Hence
\[
\|D(W^2)(x)|_H\|_1
\le 8(\varepsilon+r)\cdot2+2\cdot4(\varepsilon+r)
=24(\varepsilon+r)
\le24(1/50+21/1000)=123/125.
\tag{18}
\]
The cap \(C_r\) is convex; integrating (18) along segments proves its return-map contraction. This argument uses tangent zero products, not an erroneous ambient zero-product claim.

### 4.3. Explicit uniform interior bound

For every \(x\in\Delta_3\) and coordinate \(k\), strict exclusion and (13) give
\[
(Wx)_k\le(1-5\varepsilon/9)(1-x_k)^2
\le1-5\varepsilon/9.
\]
With \(y=Wx\), again using (13),
\[
\boxed{\quad
(W^2x)_k\ge\varepsilon E_k(y)
\ge\frac{\varepsilon}{3}(1-y_k)^2
\ge\frac{25\varepsilon^3}{243}.
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
This holds for every initial state, including vertices, and for all four coordinates.

### 4.4. Banach conclusion, exact period, uniqueness in the caps, and attraction

The nonempty compact convex cap \(C_r\) is complete. Equations (15) and (18) let Banach's theorem produce exactly one \(W^2\)-fixed point \(x_\varepsilon\) there. Set \(y_\varepsilon=Wx_\varepsilon\). Then \(Wy_\varepsilon=x_\varepsilon\). The caps are disjoint since \(1-r>r^2\); hence the two points are distinct and the least period is exactly two. Both points are \(W^2\)-fixed, so (19) proves the stated coordinate floor for each.

For \(z\in C_r\),
\[
\|W^{2n}z-x_\varepsilon\|_1
\le\kappa_\varepsilon^n\|z-x_\varepsilon\|_1,
\]
\[
\|W^{2n+1}z-y_\varepsilon\|_1
\le2\kappa_\varepsilon^n\|z-x_\varepsilon\|_1.
\tag{20}
\]
For \(z\in D_r\), first apply \(Wz\in C_r\), obtaining convergence with the opposite parity and the same return factor after this initial step.

Any periodic orbit meeting \(C_r\) gives a periodic point of its contraction \(W^2|_{C_r}\), which must be its unique fixed point. An orbit meeting \(D_r\) meets \(C_r\) on the next step. This proves exactly the uniqueness scope stated in the theorem.

The phase-cap inequalities are strict at the two points, and (19) places them in the simplex interior. Each therefore has a relative neighborhood contained in its respective cap. Equations (20) and the one-step Lipschitz bound prove local asymptotic attraction and stability of the two-point orbit. This supplies the full attracting-cycle conclusion rather than only the existence of a return fixed point.
