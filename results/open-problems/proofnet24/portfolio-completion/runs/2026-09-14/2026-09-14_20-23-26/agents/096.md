## 1. Atom probabilities, conditional probabilities, and scores

For a Hermitian interior contraction \(0<K<I_n\), put
\[
Q_x=K-I_{x^c},\qquad R_x=Q_x^{-1}.
\]
Expanding the columns of the probability generating determinant
\[
\det(I-K+K\operatorname{diag}z)
\]
gives
\[
p_x=(-1)^{n-|x|}\det Q_x.
\]
Indeed, each excluded column is \(e_j-K_{\cdot j}\); factoring out its minus sign leaves the corresponding column of \(Q_x\).

The equivalent \(L\)-ensemble identity is
\[
p_x=\det(I-K)\det L_x,\qquad L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0.
\]
The positivity follows by diagonalizing \(K\): the eigenvalues of \(L\) are \(\lambda/(1-\lambda)>0\). Thus every atom is positive and every \(Q_x\) is invertible, including the empty-principal-minor convention. These observations also prove that entropy is real analytic on the open set \(0<K<I\).

For a Hermitian perturbation \(A\), Jacobi's formula yields
\[
D\log p_x[A]=\frac{Dp_x[A]}{p_x}
=\operatorname{tr}(R_xA).
\]
There is no need to take a logarithm of an indefinite determinant: the displayed derivative is the logarithmic derivative of the positive atom probability. The trace is real because both matrices are Hermitian. Differentiating normalization gives
\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{tr}(R_XA)=0.
\]
Since Hermitian matrices form a real vector space with nondegenerate trace pairing, this is exactly \(\mathbb ER_X=0\).

At reveal stage \(i\), let \(F=\{1,\ldots,i-1\}\), with observed included set \(h\subseteq F\). The restriction law has kernel \(K_{FF}\), itself an interior contraction. Consequently
\[
Q_h=K_{FF}-I_{F\setminus h},\qquad \pi_h=\Pr(X\cap F=h)
\]
are well defined with \(Q_h\) invertible and \(\pi_h>0\). The numerator for conditional inclusion at \(i\) is the corresponding atom probability for the restriction to \(F\cup\{i\}\). Its determinant sign agrees with that of the denominator. The Schur complement therefore gives
\[
q_h=K_{ii}-K_{iF}Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi},\qquad 0<q_h<1.
\]
This conditions only on the revealed prefix, not on future coordinates.

Set \(w_F=-Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi}\), \(w_i=1\), and \(w_j=0\) for \(j>i\). Differentiating all four factors in the Schur complement, including the inverse, gives
\[
Dq_h[A]=A_{ii}-A_{iF}Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi}
-K_{iF}Q_h^{-1}A_{Fi}
+K_{iF}Q_h^{-1}A_{FF}Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi}
=w_h^*Aw_h.
\]
The Hermitian property of \(Q_h^{-1}\) is what makes the last expression the stated rank-one Hermitian form. The signs and complex conjugations in 081 are correct.

The chain rule for the sequential Bernoulli factorization now gives the pointwise score identity
\[
\operatorname{tr}(R_XA)=\sum_i\Delta_i[A],\qquad
\Delta_i[A]=
\frac{X_i-q_h}{q_h(1-q_h)}w_h^*Aw_h.
\]
At the fixed parameter \(K\), these are real martingale differences. If \(i<j\), the \(i\)-th increment is measurable before reveal \(j\), while the conditional expectation of the \(j\)-th increment is zero. Both off-diagonal cross terms vanish in the bilinear covariance calculation. At one stage the conditional Bernoulli variance supplies \(q_h(1-q_h)\), so
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{tr}(R_XA)\operatorname{tr}(R_XC)\right]
=\sum_h\omega_h(w_h^*Aw_h)(w_h^*Cw_h),\qquad
\omega_h=\frac{\pi_h}{q_h(1-q_h)}>0.
\tag{A}
\]
The history index includes the reveal stage; there are \(\sum_i2^{i-1}=2^n-1\) indices, even if some vectors coincide.

Equation (A) is a real bilinear identity on Hermitian matrices. Testing against all Hermitian \(C\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[R_X\operatorname{tr}(R_XA)\right]
=\sum_h\omega_h(w_h^*Aw_h)w_hw_h^*.
\]
Real polarization is sufficient. If an arbitrary complex-matrix version is desired, complex bilinear extension is legitimate after decomposing each matrix into Hermitian real and imaginary parts. A sesquilinear version must conjugate the second scalar factor. The quartic proof below requires neither extension: both matrices to which (A) is applied are Hermitian.

Selecting any one history at each stage gives \(n\) triangularly supported vectors with unit diagonal coordinates. Thus the vectors span \(\mathbb C^n\). This does not imply that their projectors span the real Hermitian space; 081 correctly distinguishes those statements.

## 2. Quartic representation, Hessian, and flatness

For independent block resolvents \(R_a,R_b\), define
\[
T_B=\operatorname{tr}(R_aBR_bB^*),\qquad
\kappa(B)=\mathbb E T_B^2.
\]
The trace is real, since \(BR_bB^*\) is Hermitian. Independence and \(\mathbb ER_a=\mathbb ER_b=0\) give \(\mathbb ET_B=0\). The definition is the expectation of a square, never the square of the expectation.

Condition on \(R_b\) and apply (A) to the Hermitian matrix \(BR_bB^*\). For each resulting term apply (A) in block \(b\) to the positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix \(B^*w_aw_a^*B\). Its evaluation at \(w_b\) is \(|w_a^*Bw_b|^2\). This proves, without any positivity assumption on the resolvents,
\[
\kappa(B)=\sum_{h,g}\omega_{a,h}\omega_{b,g}
|w_{a,h}^*Bw_{b,g}|^4.
\tag{B}
\]
There is no missing complex polarization or conjugation.

The evaluation map is injective: selecting spanning square families with column matrices \(U,V\) reduces vanishing of all evaluations to \(U^*BV=0\), whence \(B=0\). Because \(z\mapsto |z|^4\) is strictly convex on the real plane, the positive sum in (B) is strictly convex on the real space underlying the complex matrices. Injectivity also gives strict positivity away from zero, and compactness of the unit sphere gives \(\kappa(B)\ge c\|B\|_F^4\).

For \(z=w_a^*Bw_b\) and \(u=w_a^*Cw_b\), direct expansion gives
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}|z+tu|^4\bigg|_{t=0}
=4|z|^2|u|^2+8(\operatorname{Re}(\bar zu))^2.
\tag{C}
\]
Every summand is nonnegative, and it vanishes exactly when \(z=0\) or \(u=0\). Hence the Hessian vanishes precisely when every evaluation cell is zero for at least one of \(B,C\).

Strict convexity does not rule out this pointwise Hessian degeneracy. Independently, when both blocks are \(\frac12I_2\), their resolvents are diagonal with independent entries \(\pm2\). Expanding the square and averaging the independent signs gives
\[
\kappa(B)=16\sum_{i,j}|B_{ij}|^4.
\]
Therefore
\[
\kappa(E_{11}+tE_{22})=16(1+t^4),
\qquad D^2\kappa(E_{11})[E_{22},E_{22}]=0.
\tag{D}
\]
The coefficient 16 and the claimed nonzero-base counterexample are correct.

## 3. The all-block entropy expansion

Let \(\mu=\bigotimes_a P_{K_a}\), and let \(\widehat B\) have zero diagonal blocks. Restriction of the perturbed DPP to each original block retains kernel \(K_a\). Thus its block marginals equal those of \(\mu\), and
\[
\sum_aH(K_a)-H(K_{\mathrm{diag}}+\widehat B)
=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{K_{\mathrm{diag}}+\widehat B}\Vert\mu).
\]
For each product atom its density relative to \(\mu\) is
\[
\rho=\det(I+R\widehat B),\qquad R=\bigoplus_aR_a.
\]
Because the diagonal blocks of \(\widehat B\) vanish, the linear determinant coefficient is zero. The degree-two and degree-three coefficients are
\[
F_2=-\sum_{a<b}\operatorname{tr}(R_aB_{ab}R_bB_{ab}^*),
\qquad
F_3=\frac13\operatorname{tr}(R\widehat B)^3.
\]
Every term of \(F_3\) uses three distinct block indices. Each pair term is centered separately in its two resolvents, and each cubic-cycle term is centered separately in its three resolvents.

Distinct unordered pairs have at least one block occurring only once in their product, so their expectations vanish. In a pair times a cubic cycle, at least one of the three cycle blocks likewise occurs only once. Consequently
\[
\mathbb EF_2^2=\sum_{a<b}\kappa_{ab}(B_{ab}),
\qquad \mathbb EF_2F_3=0.
\]
Writing \(u=\rho-1=O(\|\widehat B\|^2)\), normalization gives \(\mathbb Eu=0\), and
\[
(1+u)\log(1+u)=u+\tfrac12u^2-\tfrac16u^3+\cdots.
\]
The degree-four term is therefore \(\frac12\mathbb EF_2^2\), the degree-five term is \(\mathbb EF_2F_3=0\), and
\[
H(K_{\mathrm{diag}}+\widehat B)
=\sum_aH(K_a)-\frac12\sum_{a<b}\kappa_{ab}(B_{ab})
+O(\|\widehat B\|^6).
\tag{E}
\]
The identity holds for every choice of interior internal blocks. All atom probabilities and ratios are jointly analytic near a fixed interior base, so the coefficient cancellations hold identically as functions of the internal parameters. That fact, not an undifferentiated big-O bound alone, is the input for differentiated remainder estimates.

## 4. Why connected two-site blocks give the stronger Hessian bound

For
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad c\ne0,
\]
the three reveal vectors are
\[
u_0=(1,0)^T,\quad
u_1=(-c/a,1)^T,\quad
u_2=(c/(1-a),1)^T.
\]
Every pair is linearly independent: the first has zero second coordinate, and the two remaining first coordinates differ by \(-c/[a(1-a)]\ne0\). The second connected block similarly supplies three pairwise independent vectors \(v_g\).

Suppose nonzero matrices \(B,C\) had no grid cell on which both \(u_h^*Bv_g\) and \(u_h^*Cv_g\) were nonzero. Fix \(h\). If both row functionals \(u_h^*B\) and \(u_h^*C\) were nonzero, each could vanish on at most one of the three pairwise independent \(v_g\). Their two zero sets could not cover three columns. Thus, for every \(h\), one entire row functional must vanish.

A nonzero \(2\times2\) matrix has left kernel of complex dimension at most one. Among the three pairwise independent \(u_h\), at most one can annihilate \(B\), and at most one can annihilate \(C\). These two sets cannot cover all three rows, a contradiction. This proof is valid over \(\mathbb C\) and does not assume full rank of \(B\) or \(C\).

By positive weights and (C), the quartic Hessian is strictly positive whenever \(B,C\ne0\). Joint continuity on the two unit spheres and homogeneity give
\[
D_B^2\kappa_{M,N}(B)[C,C]
\ge\beta_{M,N}\|B\|_F^2\|C\|_F^2,\qquad \beta_{M,N}>0.
\tag{F}
\]
The vectors and weights vary continuously with the connected interior blocks. On any compact family remaining in that set, the minimum over the family and both unit spheres is attained and remains positive. This proves exactly the uniformity needed later.

## 5. Full audit of the internal entropy Hessian

Write \(s=|c|^2\) and the four probabilities as
\[
x=(1-a)(1-b)-s,\quad y=a(1-b)+s,\quad
u=(1-a)b+s,\quad w=ab-s.
\]
They are positive and satisfy \(yu-xw=s>0\). For a Hermitian direction \(D\), set \(\delta=\det D\). The four atom second derivatives are \(2\delta,-2\delta,-2\delta,2\delta\). Direct differentiation of entropy yields
\[
H''[D,D]=-\mathrm{FI}+2\delta L,\quad
\mathrm{FI}=\sum_\alpha\frac{(p'_\alpha)^2}{p_\alpha},\quad
L=\log\frac{yu}{xw}>0.
\tag{G}
\]
All signs are correct.

If \(\delta<0\), strict negativity follows immediately. If \(\delta=0\) and \(D\ne0\), the Hermitian matrix has rank one and at least one nonzero diagonal entry \(D_{ii}\). Differentiation of the marginal mean gives
\[
D_{ii}=\mathbb E[(X_i-M_{ii})S_X[D]],
\]
so Cauchy-Schwarz yields
\[
\mathrm{FI}\ge\frac{D_{ii}^2}{M_{ii}(1-M_{ii})}>0.
\]

If \(\delta>0\), the two real eigenvalues have the same sign. Replacing \(D\) by \(-D\) if needed, assume \(D>0\). Define
\[
P=\frac{\operatorname{adj}M}{\sqrt w},\quad
Q=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-M)}{\sqrt x},\quad
T=\frac{y+u}{2\sqrt{xw}}.
\]
Here \(P,Q>0\), \(\det P=\det Q=1\), and direct expansion of the determinant cross coefficient gives
\[
\det(P+Q)=2+\frac{y+u}{\sqrt{xw}}=2+2T.
\]
The cross coefficient before division by \(\sqrt{xw}\) is
\[
b(1-a)+a(1-b)+2|c|^2=y+u.
\]
Thus no commutativity assumption on \(P,Q,D\) is being used.

The empty and full atoms alone give
\[
\mathrm{FI}\ge(\operatorname{tr}PD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}QD)^2
\ge\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}(P+Q)D)^2
\ge2\delta\det(P+Q)=4\delta(1+T).
\]
The last step applies the two-eigenvalue trace-determinant inequality to the positive matrix \((P+Q)^{1/2}D(P+Q)^{1/2}\). Also \(yu\le(y+u)^2/4\), hence \(L\le2\log T\); and \(T\ge\sqrt{yu/(xw)}>1\). Substituting in (G) gives
\[
H''[D,D]\le-4\delta(1+T-\log T)\le-8\delta<0,
\]
using \(\log T\le T-1\). This establishes negative definiteness for every nonzero Hermitian direction, including indefinite, rank-one, and definite directions.

The Fisher covariance itself is only rank three. For completeness, if a Hermitian \(A\) is annihilated by the three rank-one features, the \(u_0\) equation gives \(A_{11}=0\), and the other two give \(A_{22}=0\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(\bar cA_{12})=0\). Its kernel is exactly \(A_{12}=itc\), \(t\in\mathbb R\). Such nonzero directions have negative determinant, and (G) supplies strict entropy curvature even though their Fisher information is zero. This independently confirms that no false implication from positive Fisher information is hidden in the proof.

## 6. Moving-block Taylor estimates and absorption

Let \(P=(M,N)\), \(h(P)=H(M)+H(N)\), and
\[
K(P,B)=\begin{pmatrix}M&B\\B^*&N\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Choose a compact neighborhood of \(P_0=(M_0,N_0)\), contained in an open neighborhood of connected interior pairs. By continuity and the preceding negative-definiteness proof, there is \(\alpha>0\) with
\[
h''(P)[D,D]\le-\alpha d^2,\qquad
d^2=\|D_M\|_F^2+\|D_N\|_F^2.
\]
Equation (F) holds there with one common \(\beta>0\).

For two blocks, the density is exactly
\[
\rho=\det(I-R_NB^*R_MB),
\]
so it is even in the real cross coordinates and has no degree-one term. This also directly verifies the absence of degree-five terms in the two-block entropy expansion. Consequently
\[
H(K(P,B))=h(P)-\tfrac12\kappa_P(B)+E(P,B),
\]
where every cross Taylor coefficient of \(E\) through degree five vanishes identically for all \(P\) in the open internal neighborhood.

This supplies the required uniform derivative bounds rigorously. For a real multi-index \(\gamma\), \(|\gamma|\le5\), one has \(\partial_B^\gamma E(P,0)=0\) identically. Differentiating this identity in \(P\) preserves zero. Applying Taylor's theorem in \(B\) to each mixed derivative, on a smaller compact product neighborhood, gives
\[
|E_{PP}[D,D]|\le Lr^6d^2,\quad
|E_{PB}[D,C]|\le Lr^5ds,\quad
|E_{BB}[C,C]|\le Lr^4s^2,
\]
where \(r=\|B\|_F\) and \(s=\|C\|_F\). Analyticity gives bounded derivatives of every order needed on that compact set. Homogeneity and smooth internal dependence similarly give
\[
|(\kappa_P)_{PP}[D,D]|\le Lr^4d^2,\qquad
|(\kappa_P)_{PB}[D,C]|\le Lr^3ds.
\]
The dependence of both features and weights on \(P\) is included.

The second directional derivative contains twice the mixed \(P,B\) derivative. Incorporating this factor and all numerical constants into \(L\), and taking \(r\le1\), yields
\[
H''[A,A]\le-(\alpha-Lr^4)d^2-\frac{\beta}{2}r^2s^2
+Lr^3ds+Lr^4s^2.
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
Lr^3ds\le\frac{\alpha}{4}d^2+\frac{L^2}{\alpha}r^6s^2.
\]
For example, it is sufficient to choose \(\rho\le1\) such that
\[
L\rho^4\le\frac{\alpha}{4},\qquad
L\rho^2+\frac{L^2}{\alpha}\rho^4\le\frac{\beta}{4}.
\]
For \(r\le\rho\), one obtains exactly
\[
H''(K(P,B))[A,A]\le
-\frac{\alpha}{2}d^2-\frac{\beta}{4}\|B\|_F^2\|C\|_F^2.
\tag{H}
\]
Such \(\rho>0\) always exists. Thus the absorption is uniform in the moving internal blocks and works all the way down to \(B=0\); there is no division by \(r\) at zero.

## 7. Full dimensional neighborhood and strict chords

Choose a sufficiently small open Frobenius ball centered at \(\operatorname{diag}(M_0,N_0)\), whose internal projections and cross norms remain in the preceding region, and whose kernels remain in \(0<K<I_4\). The positive spectral gaps at the base guarantee the last condition. This is an open convex subset of the real vector space of Hermitian \(4\times4\) matrices.

The two Hermitian internal blocks contribute \(4+4=8\) real coordinates, and the complex \(2\times2\) cross block contributes 8 more. Hence the ball is full dimensional, with 16 real coordinates. The use of \(\|C\|_F\) rather than the full off-diagonal Hermitian norm merely omits the harmless factor \(\sqrt2\).

For a nonconstant affine segment, its direction decomposes as \(D,C\). If \(D\ne0\), (H) makes the entropy second derivative strictly negative at every point. If \(D=0\), then \(C\ne0\), and the affine matrix \(B(t)=B(0)+tC\) can vanish at at most one real parameter. Thus the second derivative is strictly negative except possibly at that one point. Integrating against the positive kernel for the chord deficit gives a strict chord inequality for every interior parameter. The conclusion is strict concavity on the entire ball.

At \(B\ne0\), (H) makes the full Hessian negative definite. At \(B=0\), the cross Taylor series starts in degree four, so the full Hessian is exactly the direct sum of the two negative-definite internal Hessians and zero on all cross directions. It has rank 8 and radical dimension 8. Along a nonzero pure cross line at such a point,
\[
H(K(P,tC))=h(P)-\tfrac12t^4\kappa_P(C)+O(t^6).
\]
Its first three directional derivatives vanish and its fourth derivative is \(-12\kappa_P(C)<0\). Pointwise second-order flatness is therefore fully consistent with strict chords, and a uniform negative-definite Hessian bound through the block-diagonal points is impossible.
