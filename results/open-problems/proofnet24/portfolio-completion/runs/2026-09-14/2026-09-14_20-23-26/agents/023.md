# Worker 023 — exact entropy expansion at an interior diagonal kernel

## Status and precise scope

**PROVED in this report, without a novelty claim:** an exact convergent Taylor-coefficient formula in every finite dimension at every diagonal interior kernel; the explicit general-direction expansion through degree four; and, for every nonzero zero-diagonal complex Hermitian direction \(A\), the expansion
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=\sum_i h(p_i)
 -\frac{t^4}{2}\sum_{i<j}
 \frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{p_i(1-p_i)p_j(1-p_j)}
 +O(t^6).
 \tag{1}
\]

The cubic and fifth coefficients vanish. The quartic coefficient is strictly negative unless \(A=0\). This implies strict concavity on a sufficiently short two-sided interval along **each fixed nonzero line through \(D\)**. It does not establish concavity on an open matrix neighborhood, nor the global conjecture.

The proof uses orthogonal product-Bernoulli contrasts. In particular, cross-pair terms vanish in all dimensions, including pairs sharing a vertex; this is not inferred from low-dimensional examples.

The finite exact checks in the accompanying script validate the displayed formulas but are not the all-dimensional proof. The theorem is proved algebraically below.

## 1. Hypotheses and notation

Let \(n\geq 1\), let
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_n),\qquad 0<p_i<1,
 \qquad q_i=1-p_i,\qquad v_i=p_iq_i,
\]
and let \(A=A^*\) be an arbitrary complex Hermitian matrix. Put
\[
 K(t)=D+tA,\qquad a_i=a_{ii}\in\mathbb R,\qquad
 h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p).
\]
All logarithms are natural. The parameter \(t\) is real.

If \(A\neq0\), then \(K(t)\) remains an interior positive contraction whenever
\[
 |t|\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\eta,\qquad
 \eta=\min_i\{p_i,q_i\}>0.
\]
All atoms are strictly positive on some such interval: at \(t=0\) they are the positive product probabilities, and there are finitely many atoms. Consequently the entropy is real analytic near \(t=0\).

Write \(\mu=P_D=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i)\). For \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\), define
\[
 z_i(x_i)=\frac{x_i-p_i}{v_i}
 =\begin{cases}p_i^{-1},&x_i=1,\\-q_i^{-1},&x_i=0,\end{cases}
 \qquad z_J(x)=\prod_{i\in J}z_i(x_i),\quad z_\varnothing=1.
\]
The one-coordinate moments are
\[
 m_{i,d}:=\mathbb E_\mu z_i^d
 =p_i^{1-d}+(-1)^d q_i^{1-d},\qquad d=0,1,2,\ldots.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
 m_{i,0}=1,\quad m_{i,1}=0,\quad m_{i,2}=v_i^{-1},
 \quad m_{i,3}=\frac{1-2p_i}{v_i^2},\quad
 m_{i,4}=p_i^{-3}+q_i^{-3}.
 \tag{3}
\]

## 2. Exact atom-density expansion

For completeness, the mixed-column atom determinant follows from the generating function. The DPP inclusion identities give
\[
 \mathbb E_K\prod_i s_i^{X_i}
 =\sum_{J\subseteq[n]}\det(K_J)\prod_{j\in J}(s_j-1)
 =\det(I-K+K\operatorname{diag}(s)).
\]
Expanding the last determinant by columns shows that the coefficient corresponding to \(x\) is \(\det M_x(K)\), where column \(j\) of \(M_x(K)\) is \(Ke_j\) if \(x_j=1\), and \((I-K)e_j\) otherwise.

Set \(r_i=p_i\) if \(x_i=1\), and \(r_i=q_i\) otherwise, and set \(\epsilon_i=2x_i-1\). Then
\[
 M_x(D+tA)=\operatorname{diag}(r)+tA\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon)
 =\bigl(I+tA\operatorname{diag}(z(x))\bigr)\operatorname{diag}(r).
\]
Thus, exactly as a polynomial in \(t\),
\[
 \frac{P_{D+tA}(x)}{\mu(x)}
 =\det\bigl(I+tA\operatorname{diag}(z(x))\bigr)
 =1+\sum_{\varnothing\neq J\subseteq[n]}
 t^{|J|}\det(A_J)z_J(x).
 \tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
 F_k(x)=\sum_{|J|=k}\det(A_J)z_J(x),\qquad
 u_t(x)=\sum_{k=1}^n t^kF_k(x).
 \tag{5}
\]
All \(\det(A_J)\), \(F_k\), and \(u_t\) are real because \(A\) is Hermitian.

### Orthogonality and the exact chi-square identity

Independence and \(\mathbb E z_i=0\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_\mu[z_Jz_L]
 =\begin{cases}
 0,&J\neq L,\\
 \displaystyle\prod_{i\in J}v_i^{-1},&J=L.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, if \(J\neq L\), an index in their symmetric difference appears exactly once and has mean zero. This argument covers disjoint pairs and overlapping pairs alike.

Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E F_k=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E[F_kF_\ell]
 =\boldsymbol 1_{\{k=\ell\}}
 \sum_{|J|=k}\frac{\det(A_J)^2}{\prod_{i\in J}v_i}.
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular, the following is an exact polynomial identity:
\[
 \chi^2(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu)=\mathbb E_\mu u_t^2
 =\sum_{\varnothing\neq J\subseteq[n]}
 \frac{t^{2|J|}\det(A_J)^2}{\prod_{i\in J}v_i}.
 \tag{8}
\]

## 3. Exact entropy identity and all Taylor coefficients

The logarithm of the reference product law is affine in the coordinates:
\[
 \log\mu(x)=\sum_i\bigl[x_i\log p_i+(1-x_i)\log q_i\bigr].
\]
Only the singleton terms in (4) can have nonzero inner product with this function. Specifically,
\[
 \mathbb E_\mu[z_i\log\mu]=\log(p_i/q_i),\qquad
 \mathbb E_\mu[z_J\log\mu]=0\quad (|J|\geq2).
\]
Since \(\mathbb E_\mu u_t=0\), the entropy therefore satisfies
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})
 =H(\mu)+t\sum_i a_i\log(q_i/p_i)
 -\mathbb E_\mu[(1+u_t)\log(1+u_t)].
 \tag{9}
\]
The last expectation is \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu)\).

For \(|u|<1\),
\[
 (1+u)\log(1+u)
 =u+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m(m-1)}u^m.
 \tag{10}
\]
Because there are finitely many atoms, inserting \(u_t\) gives an absolutely convergent exact expansion whenever \(\max_x|u_t(x)|<1\):
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})
 =H(\mu)+t\sum_i a_ih'(p_i)
 -\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^m}{m(m-1)}
 \mathbb E_\mu\left(\sum_{k=1}^nt^kF_k\right)^m.
 \tag{11}
\]

An explicit sufficient interval, for \(A\neq0\), is
\[
 |t|<
 \frac{\eta(2^{1/n}-1)}{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, \(|\det(A_J)|\leq\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{|J|}\) and \(|z_i|\leq\eta^{-1}\), so
\[
 |u_t(x)|\leq(1+|t|\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}/\eta)^n-1<1.
\]
This bound also places \(K(t)\) in the interior.

In particular, if
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=H(\mu)+t\sum_i a_ih'(p_i)+\sum_{r\geq2}c_rt^r,
\]
then the exact coefficient formula in every dimension is
\[
 c_r=-\sum_{m=2}^{r}\frac{(-1)^m}{m(m-1)}
 \sum_{\substack{J_1,\ldots,J_m\neq\varnothing\\
                  |J_1|+\cdots+|J_m|=r}}
 \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^m\det(A_{J_\ell})\right)
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^n m_{i,d_i}\right),
 \qquad
 d_i=\#\{\ell:i\in J_\ell\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
For each fixed \(r\), this is a finite sum. Every term containing an index with \(d_i=1\) vanishes. When \(A\) has zero diagonal, all singleton determinants vanish, so the outer sum may stop at \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), with every \(J_\ell\) having size at least two. Derivatives are \(H^{(r)}(0)=r!c_r\).

## 4. General Hermitian directions through fourth order

For a general Hermitian \(A\), write
\[
 b_{ij}=a_ia_j-|a_{ij}|^2=\det(A_{\{i,j\}}).
\]
Then
\[
 F_1=\sum_i a_iz_i,\qquad F_2=\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}z_iz_j.
\]
Equations (7) and (11) give
\[
 c_2=-\tfrac12\mathbb EF_1^2,\qquad
 c_3=\tfrac16\mathbb EF_1^3,
\]
\[
 c_4=-\tfrac12\mathbb EF_2^2
       +\tfrac12\mathbb E(F_1^2F_2)
       -\tfrac1{12}\mathbb EF_1^4.
 \tag{14}
\]
The omitted quadratic cross terms vanish by (7).

The needed exact moments are
\[
 \mathbb EF_1^2=\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{v_i},\qquad
 \mathbb EF_1^3=\sum_i a_i^3m_{i,3},
\]
\[
 \mathbb EF_2^2=\sum_{i<j}\frac{b_{ij}^2}{v_iv_j},\qquad
 \mathbb E(F_1^2F_2)=2\sum_{i<j}\frac{a_ia_jb_{ij}}{v_iv_j},
\]
\[
 \mathbb EF_1^4=\sum_i a_i^4m_{i,4}
       +6\sum_{i<j}\frac{a_i^2a_j^2}{v_iv_j}.
\]
For a fixed pair, its net numerator in (14) is
\[
 -\frac12b_{ij}^2+a_ia_jb_{ij}-\frac12a_i^2a_j^2
 =-\frac12(b_{ij}-a_ia_j)^2
 =-\frac12|a_{ij}|^4.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H(P_{D+tA})={}&\sum_i h(p_i)
 +t\sum_i a_i\log(q_i/p_i)
 -\frac{t^2}{2}\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{v_i}\\
 &+\frac{t^3}{6}\sum_i a_i^3\frac{1-2p_i}{v_i^2}
 -\frac{t^4}{12}\sum_i a_i^4(p_i^{-3}+q_i^{-3})\\
 &-\frac{t^4}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}
 +O(t^5).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]
There are no mixed diagonal/off-diagonal terms, cross-pair terms, or off-diagonal phase terms through order four.

In particular, the Hessian at \(D\) is
\[
 \mathrm d^2H_D[A,C]=-\sum_i\frac{a_{ii}c_{ii}}{v_i}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Its kernel is exactly the real vector space of zero-diagonal Hermitian matrices. The third derivative is purely diagonal. The fourth directional derivative is
\[
 \mathrm d^4H_D[A,A,A,A]
 =-2\sum_i a_i^4(p_i^{-3}+q_i^{-3})
  -12\sum_{i<j}\frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j},
 \tag{17}
\]
which is strictly negative for every nonzero Hermitian \(A\). This fourth-derivative statement is not itself a neighborhood-concavity theorem.

### A compact version also determining the fifth coefficient

Let \(p_i(t)=p_i+ta_i\), \(v_i(t)=p_i(t)(1-p_i(t))\), and \(B=A-\operatorname{diag}(a_i)\). Apply the same density calculation with reference product law \(\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_i(t))\) and off-diagonal perturbation \(tB\). The density perturbation has no singleton term, and the orthogonality identities hold for each \(t\). All weights are analytic and bounded on a small interior interval. Therefore
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=\sum_i h(p_i(t))
 -\frac{t^4}{2}\sum_{i<j}
 \frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_i(t)v_j(t)}
 +O(t^6).
 \tag{18}
\]
For clarity, its fifth coefficient is
\[
 c_5=\frac1{20}\sum_i a_i^5(p_i^{-4}-q_i^{-4})
 +\frac12\sum_{i<j}|a_{ij}|^4
 \left(\frac{a_im_{i,3}}{v_j}+\frac{a_jm_{j,3}}{v_i}\right).
 \tag{19}
\]
Formula (18) does not replace the exact all-order formula (13); it is a convenient finite-order consequence.

## 5. Pure off-diagonal directions: the first nonzero term

Now assume \(a_{ii}=0\) for every \(i\). Then \(F_1=0\), and
\[
 F_2=-\sum_{i<j}|a_{ij}|^2z_iz_j.
 \tag{20}
\]
The cross-entropy term in (9) is constant, so
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=H(\mu)-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu).
 \tag{21}
\]
Because \(u_t=t^2F_2+t^3F_3+\cdots\), (11) shows immediately that the linear, quadratic, and cubic entropy coefficients are zero.

The quartic coefficient is
\[
 c_4=-\frac12\mathbb EF_2^2
     =-\frac12\sum_{i<j}\frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}.
 \tag{22}
\]
No cross-pair term survives: if the unordered pairs differ, their product contains at least one coordinate contrast to the first power, even if they share a vertex.

The only possible fifth term is
\[
 c_5=-\mathbb E(F_2F_3)=0,
 \tag{23}
\]
again by orthogonality. All cubic powers of \(u_t\) start at order six. This proves (1), including its \(O(t^6)\) remainder.

An alternative remainder justification is explicit. If \(\|u_t\|_\infty\leq\rho<1\), (10) gives
\[
 \left|D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu)
             -\frac12\mathbb Eu_t^2\right|
 \leq \frac{\rho}{6(1-\rho)}\mathbb Eu_t^2.
\]
Here one may take \(\rho=O(t^2)\), while the exact identity (8) gives
\[
 \mathbb Eu_t^2
 =t^4\sum_{i<j}\frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}+O(t^6).
\]

Every denominator in (22) is positive. Hence the quartic coefficient is zero if and only if \(a_{ij}=0\) for all \(i<j\). Under the zero-diagonal Hermitian hypothesis, this is equivalent to \(A=0\). Thus every nonzero allowed direction has its **first nonconstant entropy term exactly at degree four**.

The off-diagonal phases play no role at this order. The theorem includes imaginary entries and arbitrary complex Hermitian directions.

For context, (21) gives the elementary global entropy maximum on a fixed-diagonal fiber: \(H(P_K)\leq\sum_i h(p_i)\) whenever \(K_{ii}=p_i\). Equality forces \(P_K=\mu\); comparison of two-point inclusion probabilities then gives \(|K_{ij}|^2=0\) for every pair, so \(K=D\). This maximum-entropy fact is weaker than concavity and is not claimed as new.

## 6. Higher coefficients: neither termwise negativity nor evenness

These additional coefficients help prevent incorrect extrapolations from the quartic result.

Retain zero diagonal, and write
\[
 c_{ij}=|a_{ij}|^2,\qquad
 r_{ijk}=2\operatorname{Re}(a_{ij}a_{jk}a_{ki})\quad(i<j<k).
\]
Then \(F_3=\sum_{i<j<k}r_{ijk}z_iz_jz_k\).
At order six, (11) and orthogonality give
\[
 c_6=-\frac12\mathbb EF_3^2+\frac16\mathbb EF_2^3.
\]
In the expectation of three pair contrasts, a coordinate appearing once kills the term. The only surviving edge multisets are one edge repeated three times, or the three distinct edges of a triangle. Therefore
\[
 c_6=
 -\frac16\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}^3m_{i,3}m_{j,3}
 -\sum_{i<j<k}
 \frac{r_{ijk}^2/2+c_{ij}c_{ik}c_{jk}}{v_iv_jv_k}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The repeated-edge contribution can be positive when the two marginals lie on opposite sides of \(1/2\). For example,
\[
 p=(1/3,2/3),\qquad
 A=\frac1{10}\begin{pmatrix}0&1+i\\1-i&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=H(P_D)-\frac{81}{20000}t^4
              +\frac{243}{4000000}t^6+O(t^8).
 \tag{25}
\]
The positive sixth coefficient does not alter the negative leading quartic term or the local concavity conclusion.

At order seven,
\[
 c_7=\frac12\mathbb E(F_2^2F_3).
\]
The surviving terms are two distinct edges in a triangle, with the common vertex appearing three times after multiplication by the triangle contrast. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_7=\sum_{i<j<k}r_{ijk}\bigg(
 &\frac{c_{ij}c_{ik}m_{i,3}}{v_jv_k}
 +\frac{c_{ij}c_{jk}m_{j,3}}{v_iv_k}\\
 &+\frac{c_{ik}c_{jk}m_{k,3}}{v_iv_j}\bigg).
\end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
For the exact example \(p=(1/3,1/2,1/2)\) and \(a_{ij}=1/10\) for \(i\neq j\), this yields
\[
 H(P_{D+tA})=H(P_D)
 -\frac{13}{5000}t^4
 -\frac{27}{125000}t^6
 +\frac{27}{1250000}t^7+O(t^8).
 \tag{27}
\]
Thus the entropy along a pure off-diagonal line is not generally an even function. The vanishing cubic and fifth coefficients must not be promoted to vanishing of all odd coefficients.

## 7. Rigorous local concavity along each fixed line

Fix \(A\neq0\) with zero diagonal, and define
\[
 C(D,A)=\sum_{i<j}\frac{|a_{ij}|^4}{v_iv_j}>0.
\]
The entropy is analytic, so the expansion (1) may be differentiated term by term. The vanishing fifth coefficient gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}H(P_{D+tA})=-2C(D,A)t^3+O(t^5),
\]
\[
 \frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{D+tA})
 =-6C(D,A)t^2+O(t^4).
 \tag{28}
\]
Choose \(\delta>0\) sufficiently small that the path remains interior and the remainder in (28) has magnitude at most \(3C(D,A)t^2\) when \(0<|t|<\delta\). Then
\[
 H''(t)<0\quad(0<|t|<\delta),\qquad H''(0)=0.
\]
Therefore \(H'\) is strictly decreasing on the whole interval \((-\delta,\delta)\): the integral of \(H''\) over any nondegenerate subinterval is strictly negative. It follows that \(t\mapsto H(P_{D+tA})\) is strictly concave on this interval. Its unique maximum there is at \(t=0\).

If a general nonzero Hermitian \(A\) has at least one nonzero diagonal entry, then (15) instead gives
\[
 H''(0)=-\sum_i a_i^2/v_i<0,
\]
and strict concavity on a sufficiently short interval follows directly from continuity. Thus **every fixed nonzero Hermitian line through an interior diagonal kernel is locally strictly concave**, with the zero-diagonal case requiring the quartic argument.

### Why this does not prove matrix-neighborhood concavity

The argument controls \(\mathrm d^2H_{D+tA}[A,A]\): the direction of differentiation is the direction from \(D\) to the current point. A neighborhood theorem would require
\[
 \mathrm d^2H_K[C,C]\leq0
\]
for every nearby \(K\) and every independent Hermitian direction \(C\). In particular, the base-point Hessian has a large kernel, so its continuity alone gives no such conclusion.

The logical distinction already occurs for the analytic function
\[
 f(x,y)=-x^4-y^4+x^2y^4.
\]
Every nonzero line through the origin has negative leading quartic term and is locally strictly concave, but \(f_{xx}(0,y)=2y^4>0\) at arbitrarily small nonzero \(y\). This is only an illustrative analytic example, **not a DPP counterexample**.

No whole-neighborhood or global concavity assertion is made here.
