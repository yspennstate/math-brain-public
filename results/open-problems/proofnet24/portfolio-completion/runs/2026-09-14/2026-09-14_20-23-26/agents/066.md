## 2. Independent derivation of the centered Hessian

Fix `K(t)=K+tA`, `A=A*`, in a sufficiently short open interval contained in the interior. Define

\[
M=I-K,\qquad R=M^{-1},\qquad L=R-I,
\qquad B=RAR,\qquad C=BMB=RARAR.
\]

The derivative of the inverse follows by differentiating `(M-tA)R(t)=I`:

\[
R'=RAR=B,\qquad R''=2RARAR=2C.
\tag{1}
\]

No commutation of `A` with `K` is used. `C>=0` because `C=BMB`, but this order fact alone says nothing about the sign of the entropy gradient paired with `C`.

Every principal `L_S` is positive definite. For the empty subset take determinant one and inverse trace zero. Put

\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det R},\qquad
Z_S=E_S L_S^{-1}E_S^*,\qquad Z_\varnothing=0.
\]

Log-determinant differentiation along the actual curved `L(t)` gives

\[
(\log\det L_S)'=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB),
\]

\[
(\log\det L_S)''
=2\operatorname{tr}(Z_SC)-\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB Z_SB).
\tag{2}
\]

Independently, `log det R(t)=-log det(M-tA)`, so

\[
(\log\det R)'=\operatorname{tr}(RA)=\operatorname{tr}(MB),
\qquad
(\log\det R)''=\operatorname{tr}(RARA)
=\operatorname{tr}(MBMB).
\tag{3}
\]

The second sign in (3) is positive. This is where treating `L` as affine would give the wrong answer.

Let `Delta_S=Z_S-M`. Writing

\[
s_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB),\qquad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_SB\Delta_SB)
\]

and expanding cyclically yields

\[
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}(Z_SB Z_SB)
-2\operatorname{tr}(Z_SC)+\operatorname{tr}(MBMB).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{p'_S=p_Ss_S,\qquad
p''_S=p_S\tau_S,\qquad
\tau_S=s_S^2-\kappa_S.}
\tag{4}
\]

All traces displayed here are real: the trace of a product of two Hermitian matrices is real, and each alternating four-factor trace equals the trace of its adjoint after cyclic permutation. For indefinite `A`, `tr(Delta B Delta B)` can be negative. Indeed, the edge example of 056 has `kappa_{ {2} }=-3529/90`.

Normalization and the affine singleton marginals imply

\[
\mathbb E s=\mathbb E\tau=0,\qquad
\mathbb E(\tau\mathbf1_{i\in X})=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E\kappa=\mathbb E s^2=\mathcal I,
\tag{5}
\]

where `I=sum_S (p'_S)^2/p_S`. For any Hermitian test matrix `D`, differentiating
`sum_S det L_S=det(I+L)` in the **linear** test direction `D` gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}((\mathbb E Z_S-M)D)=0.
\]

The difference is Hermitian, so testing all Hermitian `D` proves `E Z_S=M` as a matrix identity. It follows that `E c=gamma`, and (4) gives `E d=I+gamma`, in 048's notation.

Twice differentiating the finite entropy sum proves

\[
\boxed{H''=-\mathcal I+\mathcal C,\qquad
\mathcal C=-\sum_Sp''_S\log p_S.}
\tag{6}
\]

Because `E tau=0`, the covariance formulation with `log det L_S` is identical. For `F(L)=H(P_{L(I+L)^{-1}})`, the ordinary chain rule gives

\[
H''=D^2F(L)[B,B]+2DF(L)[C],\qquad
2DF(L)[C]=-2\operatorname{Cov}(\log p_S,\operatorname{tr}(Z_SC)).
\tag{7}
\]

The 066 code independently computes the two `L`-affine quotient-determinant derivatives, rather than defining the `L`-affine Hessian by subtracting the correction. It verifies the **atomwise** chain rule

\[
p''_{K,S}=p''_{L,B,S}+2p'_{L,C,S}
\]

before any logarithm is evaluated. Thus neither the analytic derivation nor the certificate can silently omit the acceleration.

## 3. Semidefinite mass domination and modular improvement

For `A>=0` of rank `r`, `B>=0` has rank `r`. The matrix

\[
Y_S=B^{1/2}\Delta_SB^{1/2}
\]

is Hermitian of rank at most `r`; it need not be positive. Nonetheless,

\[
s_S=\operatorname{tr}Y_S,\qquad
\kappa_S=\operatorname{tr}Y_S^2\ge0,
\qquad s_S^2\le r\kappa_S.
\]

For `r>=2`, this yields `-kappa<=tau<=(r-1)kappa`. On the disjoint supports of `tau_+` and `tau_-`,

\[
\kappa\ge\frac{\tau_+}{r-1}+\tau_-.
\]

Using `E tau=0`, the positive and negative masses both equal `m`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{m=\sum_S(p''_S)_+
\le\frac{r-1}{r}\mathcal I.}
\tag{8}
\]

This proof is valid for complex Hermitian directions. For `A<=0`, use `-A`; `p''`, `kappa`, and `I` do not change. For a rank-one Hermitian direction, the one-eigenvalue trace identity gives `tau=0` atomwise. For `A=0` all quantities vanish.

Every fixed modular function is annihilated by the signed measure `p''`:

\[
\sum_Sp''_S\left(c+\sum_{i\in S}\theta_i\right)=0.
\tag{9}
\]

For any such choice, put `ell_S=log p_S-sum_{i in S}theta_i`. Pairing its maximum and minimum with the equal positive and negative masses gives

\[
\mathcal C=-\sum_Sp''_S\ell_S
\le m\operatorname{osc}\ell.
\]

Thus both reports' inequalities follow:

\[
H''\le-\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r}W\right)\mathcal I,
\qquad W=\operatorname{osc}(\log p),
\tag{10}
\]

\[
\boxed{H''\le-\left(1-\frac{r-1}{r}\Omega(K)\right)\mathcal I,\qquad
\Omega(K)=\inf_\theta\operatorname{osc}\left(\log p_S-\sum_{i\in S}\theta_i\right).}
\tag{11}
\]

Taking an infimum requires no optimizer or differentiability of the optimizing coefficients. In (9), coefficients are frozen at the current kernel. A different choice may be made at each point of a segment without differentiating those coefficients; the already computed Hessian is what is being bounded.

For nonzero semidefinite `A`, the Fisher information is strictly positive. Indeed,

\[
\mathbb E[(|X|-\mathbb E|X|)s]=\operatorname{tr}A\ne0.
\]

The score Cauchy--Schwarz inequality further gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I\ge
\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{\operatorname{tr}K(I-K)}
\ge\frac4n(\operatorname{tr}A)^2.}
\tag{12}
\]

No such proof of positive-mass domination is available for arbitrary indefinite directions: the explicit negative `kappa` above rules out that intermediate step as written. Nor does failure of the oscillation threshold imply positive entropy curvature.

### Correlation determinant bound and its invariance

Let `D_L=diag(L_ii)` and `Gamma=D_L^{-1/2}LD_L^{-1/2}`. This matrix is positive definite with diagonal one. Hadamard's inequality gives `det Gamma_S<=1`. For a proper nonempty `S`, its Schur complement `Q` in `Gamma` is positive definite with `Q_ii<=1`; hence

\[
\det\Gamma=\det\Gamma_S\det Q\le\det\Gamma_S.
\]

The empty and full cases are immediate. The exact range is consequently

\[
\log\det\Gamma\le\log\det\Gamma_S\le0.
\]

Choosing `theta_i=log L_ii` in (11) proves

\[
\boxed{\Omega(K)\le T(K):=-\log\det\Gamma.}
\tag{13}
\]

If a positive diagonal matrix `D` replaces `L` by `DLD`, principal log determinants acquire `2 sum_{i in S}log D_ii`, and the log normalization changes by a constant. These are precisely the terms removed in (9). Both `Gamma` and `Omega` are unchanged. This is a statement at the corresponding kernels; it does not assert that diagonal congruence in `L` carries affine kernel chords to affine kernel chords.

At every interior diagonal kernel, `T=0`. Continuity provides a sufficiently small convex ball in the interior with `T<beta`. For `n>=2`, choose `beta<n/(n-1)` to cover every semidefinite rank between two and `n` on that ball; rank one is already covered. Integrating gives concavity for every comparable endpoint pair in that ball, as claimed. Nothing here controls all indefinite directions in that ball or proves convexity of a global sublevel set of `T`.

## 6. Principal-resolvent identities and convergence

For `u>=0`, define

\[
\mathcal R_u(S)=\operatorname{tr}(L_S+uI)^{-1}
-\sum_{i\in S}(L_{ii}+u)^{-1}.
\]

The empty value is zero. Each inverse diagonal entry is the reciprocal of a positive scalar Schur complement no larger than the original diagonal entry; therefore `R_u(S)>=0`.

At zero all inverse matrices are finite. At infinity, the inverse power series is uniformly valid after `u` exceeds, for example, `2||L||`. Its first two terms cancel against the diagonal reference, leaving

\[
\mathcal R_u(S)
=u^{-3}\sum_{i\ne j\in S}|L_{ij}|^2+O(u^{-4}).
\tag{21}
\]

There are finitely many subsets. The elementary scalar logarithm integral then gives

\[
\int_0^\infty\mathcal R_u(S)\,du
=\sum_{i\in S}\log L_{ii}-\log\det L_S
=-\log\det\Gamma_S.
\]

Absolute integrability follows both from the finite zero behavior and the `u^-3` tail. In particular,

\[
\int_0^\infty\left|\sum_Sp''_S\mathcal R_u(S)\right|du
\le\sum_S|p''_S|\int_0^\infty\mathcal R_u(S)du<\infty.
\]

Using the modular cancellation (9) gives exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C=\int_0^\infty J(u)du,\qquad
J(u)=\sum_Sp''_S\mathcal R_u(S)
=\sum_Sp''_S\operatorname{tr}(L_S+uI)^{-1}.}
\tag{22}
\]

The uncentered traces individually have divergent integrals. It is their **summed, cancelled** expression in (22) that may be integrated. The audited proof uses the integrable centered terms to justify its sum/interchange and is correct on this point. All resolvents are frozen at the base kernel; differentiating them again would change the expression.

The two-point inclusion identity gives
`sum_S p''_S 1_{i,j in S}=2 det A_{ij}`. Combining this with the ordered off-diagonal sum in (21) proves the exact factor four:

\[
\lim_{u\to\infty}u^3J(u)
=4\sum_{i<j}|L_{ij}|^2\det A_{ij}.
\tag{23}
\]

For semidefinite directions this coefficient is nonnegative, and can be positive; therefore nonpositivity of the tail is not a generally available argument. In the 056 edge direction it is `-1600/9`.

### Scalar-atom resolvent formula

The separate identity

\[
-p\log p=\int_0^\infty\left(\frac p{p+u}-\frac p{1+u}\right)du
\]

is correct. In a sufficiently small compact parameter interval about any interior kernel, all atom probabilities are bounded below by a positive constant and their first two derivatives are bounded. Differentiated integrands are then uniformly integrable: bounded near zero and `O(u^-2)` at infinity after the displayed cancellations. Differentiating the finite atom sum twice and using `sum p''=0` yields

\[
H''=\int_0^\infty u\sum_S\left(
\frac{p''_S}{(p_S+u)^2}-\frac{2(p'_S)^2}{(p_S+u)^3}\right)du.
\tag{24}
\]

Its final summed integrand is `O(u)` at zero and `O(u^-2)` at infinity. This validates the additional representation in 056 without confusing its integrand with the principal-`L` integrand `J`.
