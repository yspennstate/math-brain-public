## 2. Density-versus-law convention, positivity and derivatives

Write \(\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i\), with \(\mu_i(1)=d_i\in(0,1)\). The one-coordinate transition is

\[
Q_{i,t}(x,y)=t\mathbf1_{\{x=y\}}+(1-t)\mu_i(y).
\]

It satisfies detailed balance exactly:

\[
\mu_i(x)Q_{i,t}(x,y)=\mu_i(y)Q_{i,t}(y,x).
\]

Thus the product transition \(Q_t\) is reversible for \(\mu\). If the input law is \(P(x)=\mu(x)f(x)\), its output density at \(y\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(PQ_t)(y)}{\mu(y)}
&=\sum_x\frac{\mu(x)Q_t(x,y)}{\mu(y)}f(x)\\
&=\sum_xQ_t(y,x)f(x)
=\left[\prod_i(E_i+tD_i)f\right](y),
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(E_i\) averages coordinate \(i\) under its fixed reference marginal and \(D_i=I-E_i\). These are reference-product averages, not conditional expectations under the dependent input law. This distinction is essential and is handled correctly in the source.

The term in which all coordinates are redrawn has weight \((1-t)^n\), so, for every normalized nonnegative density,

\[
g_t=N_tf\ge (1-t)^n\quad(0\le t<1).
\]

Consequently every differentiated density is strictly positive on the required open interval even if the input law has zero atoms. No logarithm of zero is differentiated.

The operators commute and satisfy \(D_i^2=D_i\), \(E_iD_i=0\). Decomposing into products of the ranges of \(E_i\) and \(D_i\), a term involving exactly \(k\) of the latter receives multiplier \(t^k\). Hence, with \(L=\sum_iD_i\),

\[
t g_t'=Lg_t,\qquad
t^2g_t''=(L^2-L)g_t.
\tag{1}
\]

In particular, \(N_t\) is invertible for \(t>0\): all its eigenvalues \(t^k\) are positive. This is linear invertibility; the inverse need not preserve positivity and no such claim is used.

For \(F(t)=\mathbb E_\mu[g_t\log g_t]\), normalization gives \(\mathbb E_\mu g_t'=\mathbb E_\mu g_t''=0\). Finite-sum differentiation therefore yields

\[
F''(t)=\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(g_t')^2}{g_t}
+\mathbb E_\mu g_t''\log g_t.
\]

Since \(L^2-L=2\sum_{i<j}D_iD_j\), the complete identity is

\[
t^2F''(t)
=\sum_i\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_ig)^2}{g}
+2\sum_{i<j}\left[
\mathbb E_\mu\frac{D_igD_jg}{g}
+\mathbb E_\mu(D_iD_jg)\log g
\right].
\tag{2}
\]

Both parts of the usual nonlinear-law entropy Hessian are present. The factor two comes from ordered pairs in both \((Lg)^2\) and \((L^2-L)g\).

## 3. Universal two-coordinate audit

Fix all coordinates except \(i,j\), and set \(p=d_i,q=d_j\). Use density values \(a,b,c,d>0\) in the order \(00,01,10,11\), and write \(\Delta=a-b-c+d\) and \(w=p(1-p)q(1-q)>0\).

The individual values are:

| State | \(D_i g\) | \(D_j g\) | \(D_iD_jg\) | Reference weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(00\) | \(p(a-c)\) | \(q(a-b)\) | \(pq\Delta\) | \((1-p)(1-q)\) |
| \(01\) | \(p(b-d)\) | \((1-q)(b-a)\) | \(-p(1-q)\Delta\) | \((1-p)q\) |
| \(10\) | \((1-p)(c-a)\) | \(q(c-d)\) | \(-(1-p)q\Delta\) | \(p(1-q)\) |
| \(11\) | \((1-p)(d-b)\) | \((1-q)(d-c)\) | \((1-p)(1-q)\Delta\) | \(pq\) |

There is no need to normalize the four density values within this fiber. Each weighted product \(D_i gD_jg/g\) has the common factor \(w\), leaving

\[
\frac{(a-c)(a-b)}a+
\frac{(b-d)(b-a)}b+
\frac{(c-a)(c-d)}c+
\frac{(d-b)(d-c)}d.
\]

Its expansion is exactly

\[
\frac{bc}{a}+\frac{ad}{b}+\frac{ad}{c}+\frac{bc}{d}-(a+b+c+d).
\]

The four weighted \(D_iD_jg\) values are \(w\Delta\) with signs \(+,-,-,+\). Thus the logarithmic contribution is exactly \(w\Delta\log(ad/(bc))\), with the displayed positive sign in (2).

Let

\[
r=\frac{ad}{bc}>0,\qquad X=a+d>0,\qquad Y=b+c>0.
\]

The entire bracket on this fiber is therefore

\[
\boxed{
w\left[X(r^{-1}-1+\log r)+Y(r-1-\log r)\right].}
\tag{3}
\]

For an independent algebra check, the two fractional sums are \(bc/a+bc/d=X/r\) and \(ad/b+ad/c=rY\), while \(\Delta=X-Y\). Equation (3) follows immediately.

Both scalar functions in (3) are nonnegative by \(u-1-\log u\ge0\), applied to \(u=r\) and \(u=r^{-1}\). Their simultaneous zero set is exactly \(r=1\). Thus every fiber bracket is nonnegative, and is positive exactly when \(ad\ne bc\). Averaging over the other coordinates proves every pair bracket in (2) nonnegative.

This identity has been checked symbolically, for algebraically independent \(a,b,c,d,p,q\), by the supplied exact script. It is not a numerical sign test. The elementary logarithmic inequality is the analytic positivity argument.

## 4. Convexity and strictness of the resampling lemma

Equation (2) now proves \(F''(t)\ge0\) on \((0,1)\). If \(F''(t)=0\), the nonnegative single-coordinate terms force \(D_ig=0\) for every \(i\), since \(g>0\) and \(\mu\) has full support. Hence \(g\) is independent of every coordinate and therefore constant. Normalization makes \(g=1\), and invertibility of \(N_t\) gives \(f=1\).

Conversely \(f=1\) gives \(F=0\). Thus \(F''(t)>0\) for every \(t\in(0,1)\) unless the input law equals the product reference. This does not require any determinantal assumption.

The map \(z\mapsto z\log z\), with value zero at zero, is continuous on \([0,\infty)\). Since there are finitely many density coordinates, \(F\) extends continuously to \(t=0,1\). Convexity extends to the closed interval by taking limits in the convexity inequality. Strictness at closed-interval chords follows from strict curvature on the interior: equality on any nondegenerate convexity chord would force an affine subinterval, which is impossible.

## 5. Affine DPP resampling and Shannon conversion

For \(X\sim P_K\), retain each bit with probability \(t\) and otherwise independently redraw it with parameter \(d_i\). Conditional on \(X\), the output bits \(Y_i\) are independent with conditional means \(tX_i+(1-t)d_i\). Hence

\[
\mathbb P(S\subseteq Y)
=\sum_{T\subseteq S}t^{|T|}(1-t)^{|S\setminus T|}
\det K_T\prod_{i\in S\setminus T}d_i.
\tag{4}
\]

Column multilinearity of \(\det(tK_S+(1-t)D_S)\) gives precisely (4). A chosen diagonal column fixes its matching row, so deleting all selected diagonal rows and columns leaves the same-index principal minor \(K_T\). The cofactor sign is positive because row and column deletion sets agree. This calculation works over the complex numbers and does not require \(D\) to commute with \(K\).

Finite inclusion-exclusion determines each exact atom from the inclusion probabilities. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Law}(Y)=P_{(1-t)D+tK}.
\tag{5}
\]

The reference has full support, so \(f=P_K/\mu\) is a legitimate density even for boundary \(K\). Let \(m_i(t)=d_i+t(K_{ii}-d_i)\). The cross-entropy is

\[
C(t)=-\sum_i\{m_i(t)\log d_i+[1-m_i(t)]\log(1-d_i)\},
\]

which is affine. Therefore \(H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})=C(t)-F(t)\), proving the required concavity and strict curvature unless \(P_K=P_D\).

If \(P_K=P_D\), single-site events give \(K_{ii}=d_i\); two-site events then give

\[
d_id_j-|K_{ij}|^2=\det K_{\{i,j\}}=d_id_j.
\]

Thus every off-diagonal entry is zero and \(K=D\). The known gauge ambiguity of general DPP kernels causes no problem when one kernel is diagonal.

## 6. Entire feasible line, midpoint gluing, and singular endpoints

For \(A=0\), the feasible parameter set is \(\mathbb R\) and entropy is constant. Suppose \(A\ne0\). With \(\eta=\min_i\{d_i,1-d_i\}>0\), every \(|s|<\eta/\|A\|\) is strictly feasible. Feasibility is closed and convex. It is bounded, for instance because

\[
|s|\|A\|\le\|D+sA\|+\|D\|\le 1+\|D\|
\]

on the feasible set. Thus the feasible interval is \([s_-,s_+]\) with \(s_-<0<s_+\).

For either \(\sigma=s_-\) or \(\sigma=s_+\), set \(K_\sigma=D+\sigma A\). The corresponding half is parametrized by

\[
D+sA=D+(s/\sigma)(K_\sigma-D),\qquad 0\le s/\sigma\le1.
\]

The negative half reverses the parameter orientation but contributes the positive second-derivative factor \(1/\sigma^2\), so there is no sign reversal of concavity. Each open half has strictly negative second derivative because \(K_\sigma\ne D\).

At zero, every atom equals a positive product probability. The atoms are polynomials in \(s\), and their entropy is therefore real analytic near zero. More explicitly,

\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}H(P_{D+sA})\right|_{s=0}
=\sum_i A_{ii}\log\frac{1-d_i}{d_i}.
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, \(F'(0)=0\) because \(g_0=1\) and the derivative has total mass zero. Formula (6) holds from either parametrization and exhibits the matching first derivatives. Consequently the decreasing derivative on the negative half joins continuously to the decreasing derivative on the positive half. The full function is concave.

Every nondegenerate subinterval contains an open interval away from zero where the second derivative is negative. Therefore no nondegenerate subinterval is affine. Equality on any concavity chord would imply such an affine interval, proving strict concavity on the entire closed feasible interval, including chords crossing zero and chords using singular outer endpoints.

At a singular endpoint, individual probabilities can vanish; the proof takes only limits of the finite continuous entropy sum there. It does not assume finite endpoint derivatives or full support at the boundary.

The source's separate extension to a **single chord** from a possibly degenerate diagonal reference is also valid. Replace \(D\) by \(D_\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)D+\varepsilon I\), apply the proved concavity to \((1-t)D_\varepsilon+tK\), and take \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in every chord inequality. Strictness does not follow from this continuity argument alone; the source correctly refrains from asserting it on that basis.

## 7. Quantitative curvature: verified bound and exact sharpness

Write \(\delta_i=K_{ii}-d_i\), \(m_i=d_i+t\delta_i\), and \(g=f_t\). Conditional reference averages over all coordinates other than \(i\) satisfy

\[
\mathbb E_\mu[g\mid X_i=1]=\frac{m_i}{d_i},\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu[g\mid X_i=0]=\frac{1-m_i}{1-d_i}.
\]

Because \(\mu\) is a product and \(E_i g\) is independent of \(X_i\), its conditional expectation is always \(\mathbb E_\mu g=1\). Thus

\[
\mathbb E_\mu[D_i g\mid X_i=1]=\frac{t\delta_i}{d_i},\qquad
\mathbb E_\mu[D_i g\mid X_i=0]=\frac{-t\delta_i}{1-d_i}.
\]

Conditional Cauchy–Schwarz yields

\[
\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_i g)^2}{g}
\ge\frac{t^2\delta_i^2}{m_i}+\frac{t^2\delta_i^2}{1-m_i}
=\frac{t^2\delta_i^2}{m_i(1-m_i)}.
\]

Combining with (2) proves the source's quantitative statement with the stated factor:

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})
\ge\sum_i\frac{\delta_i^2}{m_i(t)(1-m_i(t))}.}
\tag{7}
\]

For a precise remainder, let

\[
c_i(1)=\frac{m_i-d_i}{m_i},\qquad
c_i(0)=-\frac{m_i-d_i}{1-m_i},
\]

\[
R_i=\mathbb E_\mu\frac{(D_i g-c_i(X_i)g)^2}{g}\ge0,
\]

and let \(B_{ij}\ge0\) be the complete pair bracket of (2). Expanding this square separately at each value of \(X_i\) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
-H''(t)-\sum_i\frac{\delta_i^2}{m_i(1-m_i)}
=\frac1{t^2}\left[\sum_iR_i+2\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}\right].}
\tag{8}
\]

Equality \(R_i=0\) is equivalent to coordinate \(i\) being independent of all the others under the positive law \(\mu g\). Indeed, \(D_i g=c_i(X_i)g\) gives

\[
E_i g=\frac{d_i}{m_i}g(1,\cdot)
=\frac{1-d_i}{1-m_i}g(0,\cdot),
\]

so the ratio of the two density slices is constant in the remaining coordinates. Conversely independence gives these equalities. If all \(R_i\) vanish, each coordinate is independent of the rest, and the whole law is a product. If \(g\) is a product density, all \(R_i\) and all \(B_{ij}\) vanish.

For a DPP with kernel \((1-t)D+tK\), a product law forces \(tK_{ij}=0\) for all \(i\ne j\), by the two-site determinant formula. As \(t>0\), this is precisely that \(K\) is diagonal. Conversely diagonal \(K\) produces a product law at every parameter. Hence equality in (7) at any interior parameter holds if and only if \(K\) is diagonal; otherwise the inequality is strict everywhere on \((0,1)\).

Let \(B(t)=\sum_i h(m_i(t))\). Its curvature is exactly the negative right side of (7), so

\[
\operatorname{TC}(t):=B(t)-H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})
\]

is convex, strictly convex if \(K\) is nondiagonal, and identically zero if \(K\) is diagonal. This is the total correlation of the actual subset law. The same half-line gluing argument applies to \(\operatorname{TC}(s)\) on a full line through \(D\). On that full line,

\[
-\frac{d^2}{ds^2}H(P_{D+sA})
\ge\sum_i\frac{A_{ii}^2}{(d_i+sA_{ii})(1-d_i-sA_{ii})}
\]

for \(s\ne0\) in the interior, with strict inequality exactly when \(A\) has a nonzero off-diagonal entry. At \(s=0\), equality always holds; the next section explains the flat off-diagonal Hessian.

## 8. Independent audit of the flat midpoint expansion

For \(A_{ii}=0\), the product-reference atom expansion is

\[
\frac{P_{D+sA}(x)}{\mu(x)}
=1+s^2F_2(x)+s^3F_3(x)+\cdots,
\qquad
F_k=\sum_{|J|=k}\det(A_J)\prod_{j\in J}z_j,
\]

where \(z_i=(x_i-d_i)/(d_i(1-d_i))\). Product independence and \(\mathbb E z_i=0\) imply \(\mathbb EF_2F_3=0\), since every product of a two-index and a three-index contrast has some coordinate occurring exactly once. Also

\[
F_2=-\sum_{i<j}|A_{ij}|^2z_iz_j,
\qquad
\mathbb EF_2^2=\sum_{i<j}\frac{|A_{ij}|^4}{d_i(1-d_i)d_j(1-d_j)}.
\]

The expansion \((1+u)\log(1+u)=u+u^2/2+O(u^3)\), with \(u=O(s^2)\), therefore gives

\[
H(P_{D+sA})=\sum_i h(d_i)
-\frac{s^4}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{|A_{ij}|^4}{d_i(1-d_i)d_j(1-d_j)}
+O(s^6).
\tag{9}
\]

The fifth coefficient vanishes by the displayed orthogonality. No assertion that every odd coefficient vanishes is needed or valid in general. Equation (9) agrees with report023 and confirms that the zero midpoint second derivative is compatible with strict concavity on the full line. The global concavity proof does not depend on the Taylor expansion.
