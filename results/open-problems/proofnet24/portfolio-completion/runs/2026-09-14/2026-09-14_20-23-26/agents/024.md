## 1. Exact theorem and quantifiers

Let
\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R_+^4:\sum_{i=1}^4x_i=1\},\qquad
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^4P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\]
where, for every choice of indices,
\[
P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}\ge0,\qquad \sum_kP_{ij,k}=1,
\qquad P_{ij,k}=0\text{ if }k\in\{i,j\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix any such coefficient array, and put
\[
T=\tfrac12(I+V),\qquad S_j=T^j(\Delta_3),\qquad
q=\frac{49}{128},\quad d=1-2q=\frac{15}{64},\quad
\varepsilon=\frac{d^2}{2}=\frac{225}{8192}.
\]
All norms are the ordinary unweighted \(\ell^1\) norm.

**Theorem.** For every coefficient array satisfying (1),
\[
\|Tu-Tv\|_1\le(1-\varepsilon)\|u-v\|_1
\qquad(u,v\in S_3).
\tag{2}
\]
There is exactly one fixed point \(p\) of \(V\) in \(\Delta_3\), and
\[
\boxed{\quad\|T^nx-p\|_1\le2\left(\frac{7967}{8192}\right)^{n-3}
\quad(x\in\Delta_3,\ n\ge3).\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
The coefficient array is the same throughout each comparison and in all selected preimages. The constants are independent of that array. Neither positivity of every coefficient nor interior starting points are assumed.

## 2. The two-step cap and the required image property

For a fixed coordinate \(r\), write \(s=x_r\), \(v=V_r(x)\), and \(y=Tx\). From (1),
\[
0\le v\le(1-s)^2,\qquad y_r=(s+v)/2,\qquad y_i\ge x_i/2.
\]
Each coefficient belongs to \([0,1]\), so every term in the defect sum is nonnegative and
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-y_r)^2-V_r(y)
&=\sum_{i,j\ne r}(1-P_{ij,r})y_iy_j\\
&\ge\frac14\sum_{i,j\ne r}(1-P_{ij,r})x_ix_j
=\frac14[(1-s)^2-v].
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
(T^2x)_r
\le\frac{y_r}{2}+\frac{(1-y_r)^2}{2}
-\frac{(1-s)^2-v}{8}
=\frac{3+(2s-1)v+v^2}{8}.
\tag{4}
\]
For fixed \(s\in[0,1]\), the final expression is convex in \(v\). Its maximum over \(0\le v\le(1-s)^2\) is therefore at an endpoint. The two endpoint values are
\[
\frac38,\qquad
\frac{3+s^2(1-s)^2}{8}\le\frac{3+1/16}{8}=q.
\]
This includes degenerate intervals, such as \(s=1\). Thus, with
\(K_q=\{z\in\Delta_3:z_i\le q\ \forall i\}\),
\[
S_2\subseteq K_q.
\tag{5}
\]

The map \(T\) sends the simplex to itself. Its images are nested because
\(S_{j+1}=T^j(T\Delta_3)\subseteq T^j\Delta_3=S_j\).
They are invariant under \(T\), since \(TS_j=S_{j+1}\subseteq S_j\).
In particular \(S_3\subseteq S_2\subseteq K_q\).

For arbitrary \(u,v\in S_3\), choose \(x,y\in\Delta_3\) with
\(u=T^3x\), \(v=T^3y\), and set \(m=(u+v)/2\).
The set \(K_q\) is convex, so \(m\in K_q\). We do not need either \(S_3\) to be convex or \(K_q\) to be invariant.

## 3. Uniform column overlap, with every case covered

Define
\[
A(m)_{ai}=\sum_bm_bP_{bi,a}.
\]
This matrix is nonnegative, each column sums to one, and \(A(m)_{ii}=0\). For columns \(i,j\), their overlap is
\[
O_{ij}(m)=\sum_a\min(A(m)_{ai},A(m)_{aj}).
\]
We prove
\[
O_{ij}(m)\ge\varepsilon
\quad\text{whenever }m=(T^3x+T^3y)/2.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(i=j\), the overlap is exactly one. It remains to consider distinct columns.

### 3.1. Both indexed masses are at least the threshold

If \(m_i,m_j\ge\varepsilon\), symmetry of the coefficient array gives, for every row \(a\),
\[
A(m)_{ai}\ge m_jP_{ij,a},\qquad
A(m)_{aj}\ge m_iP_{ij,a}.
\]
Summing the coordinatewise minima yields
\[
O_{ij}(m)\ge\min(m_i,m_j)\sum_aP_{ij,a}
=\min(m_i,m_j)\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{7}
\]
Equality of either mass to the threshold is included in this case.

### 3.2. One indexed mass lies below the threshold

Otherwise rename an index in the tested pair as \(r\), with
\(t=m_r<\varepsilon\); write the other tested index as \(j\), and let \(k,\ell\) denote the two remaining types. For every \(a\ne r\), the other two coordinates are at most \(q\), hence
\[
m_a\ge1-t-2q=d-t.
\tag{8}
\]
The exact inequality \(d-2\varepsilon=735/4096>0\) shows that \(d-t>\varepsilon\). There is therefore at most one coordinate below the threshold. In particular the present naming covers the entire alternative case without an omitted pair of small indices.

Since \(t\) is the average of \((T^3x)_r\) and \((T^3y)_r\), one of these two numbers is at most \(t\). Choose the corresponding point
\[
z\in\{T^2x,T^2y\}\quad\text{with}\quad(Tz)_r\le t.
\]
Both summands in \((Tz)_r=(z_r+V_r(z))/2\) are nonnegative, so
\[
z_r\le2t,\qquad V_r(z)\le2t.
\tag{9}
\]
Also \(z\in S_2\subseteq K_q\), and for each \(a\ne r\),
\[
z_a\ge1-z_r-2q\ge d-2t>0.
\tag{10}
\]
For distinct \(a,b\ne r\), the nonnegative sum defining \(V_r(z)\) contains the two terms with ordered parents \((a,b)\) and \((b,a)\). Their equality by symmetry gives
\[
2P_{ab,r}(d-2t)^2
\le2P_{ab,r}z_az_b\le V_r(z)\le2t.
\]
Division uses the strictly positive denominator in (10), never \(t\). Thus
\[
P_{ab,r}\le\theta(t):=\frac{t}{(d-2t)^2}
\qquad(a\ne b,\ a,b\ne r).
\tag{11}
\]
No estimate on diagonal parent coefficients is required.

The only permitted offspring of the distinct parental pair \(\ell,j\) are \(k,r\), so
\(P_{\ell j,k}=1-P_{\ell j,r}\ge1-\theta(t)\).
Likewise \(P_{kj,\ell}\ge1-\theta(t)\). It follows that
\[
A(m)_{kj}\ge m_\ell(1-\theta(t)),\qquad
A(m)_{\ell j}\ge m_k(1-\theta(t)).
\tag{12}
\]
The distinct parental pair \(j,r\) produces only \(k\) or \(\ell\). Hence
\[
A(m)_{kr}+A(m)_{\ell r}
\ge m_j(P_{jr,k}+P_{jr,\ell})=m_j\ge d-t.
\tag{13}
\]

We establish immediately below that \(0\le\theta(t)<1\) for \(0\le t\le\varepsilon\). Define
\(h(t)=(d-t)(1-\theta(t))\). Equations (8) and (12) imply that both entries of column \(j\) displayed in (12) are at least \(h(t)\). Moreover \(0<h(t)\le d-t\). For nonnegative \(a,b,h\),
\[
\min(a,h)+\min(b,h)\ge\min(a+b,h).
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed if either \(a\) or \(b\) is at least \(h\), the left side is at least \(h\); otherwise it equals \(a+b\). Using (13), (14), and monotonicity of a minimum in each argument gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
O_{rj}(m)
&\ge\min(A_{kr},A_{kj})+\min(A_{\ell r},A_{\ell j})\\
&\ge\min(A_{kr},h(t))+\min(A_{\ell r},h(t))\\
&\ge\min(A_{kr}+A_{\ell r},h(t))=h(t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The last equality uses \(A_{kr}+A_{\ell r}\ge d-t\ge h(t)\); this comparison is an essential part of the overlap step.

### 3.3. Monotonicity, exact constants, and zero coordinates

For \(0\le t\le\varepsilon\), we have \(d-2t\ge735/4096>0\). Differentiation gives
\[
\theta'(t)=\frac{d+2t}{(d-2t)^3}>0.
\]
The endpoint value is
\[
\theta(\varepsilon)=\frac{2048}{2401}<1.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(0\le\theta(t)<1\) throughout the closed interval. Furthermore
\[
h'(t)=-(1-\theta(t))-(d-t)\theta'(t)<0,
\]
so \(h\) is strictly decreasing. In particular,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h(t)&\ge h(\varepsilon)
=\left(\frac{1695}{8192}\right)\left(\frac{353}{2401}\right)
=\frac{598335}{19668992},\\
h(\varepsilon)-\varepsilon
&=\frac{29055}{9834496}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The threshold case \(t=\varepsilon\) did not need this second branch, but using its closed interval endpoint gives a valid uniform lower bound for all \(t<\varepsilon\). Equations (15)--(17) establish (6).

At \(t=0\), (9) gives \(z_r=V_r(z)=0\), while all three other coordinates of \(z\) are at least \(d>0\). Equation (11) then gives \(P_{ab,r}=0\) for distinct \(a,b\ne r\). Every displayed inequality remains valid with \(\theta(0)=0\), \(h(0)=d\). There is no division by a zero mass, excluded boundary case, or assumption of full support.

## 4. From stochastic overlap to the correct zero-sum norm

For a column-stochastic matrix \(A\), define
\[
\delta(A)=\frac12\max_{i,j}\|Ae_i-Ae_j\|_1
=1-\min_{i,j}O_{ij}.
\]
The equality follows from \(|a-b|=a+b-2\min(a,b)\) and the two unit column sums. Equation (6) gives
\[
\delta(A(m))\le1-\varepsilon.
\tag{18}
\]

Put \(B(m)=\tfrac12I+A(m)\), which has column sum \(3/2\), not one. On \(E=\{w:\sum_iw_i=0\}\), any matrix with equal column sums satisfies
\[
\|B|_E\|_{1\to1}
=\frac12\max_{i,j}\|Be_i-Be_j\|_1
=\max_{G,i,j}\sum_{a\in G}(B_{ai}-B_{aj}).
\tag{19}
\]
To prove the first equality, let \(w\ne0\) have sum zero and let \(c=\|w\|_1/2\). The coefficients \(\eta_{ij}=w_i^+w_j^-/c^2\) sum to one and give
\(w=c\sum_{i,j}\eta_{ij}(e_i-e_j)\).
The triangle inequality proves the upper bound, and individual vectors \(e_i-e_j\) give equality. For the second equality, each column difference has sum zero, so half its \(\ell^1\) norm is the sum of its positive entries, equivalently its largest subset sum. Formula (19) requires no stochastic normalization of \(B\).

For fixed distinct \(i,j\), write
\(D_A(G;i,j)=\sum_{a\in G}(A_{ai}-A_{aj})\).
Then \(D_A(G;i,j)\le\delta(A)\), and the cut in (19) is
\[
\tfrac12(\mathbf1_G(i)-\mathbf1_G(j))+D_A(G;i,j).
\]
The following cases exhaust all subsets and index pairs.

| Case | Upper bound |
| --- | --- |
| Empty or full subset, or equal column indices | \(0\) |
| Both indices inside, or both outside | \(\delta(A)\le1-\varepsilon\) |
| \(i\notin G,\ j\in G\) | \(-1/2+\delta(A)\le1/2-\varepsilon\) |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=1\) | \(1/2-A_{ij}\le1/2\) |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=3\) | \(1/2-A_{ji}\le1/2\) |
| \(i\in G,\ j\notin G,\ |G|=2\) | \(1/2+q\) |

Here the last row is the four-type transfer estimate. For its full proof, let \(G=\{i,k\}\), \(G^c=\{j,\ell\}\), and \(p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r}\). Then \(0\le p_{ab}\le1\), \(p_{ab}=p_{ba}\), \(p_{ki}=0\), and \(p_{\ell j}=1\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_A(G;i,j)
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})+m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
-m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
&\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}
\le\max(m_i,m_j)\le q.
\end{aligned}
\]
All omitted terms are nonpositive, including at zero coefficients and masses. The size-three row follows by passing to the complement \(\{j\}\) and using equal column sums and \(A_{jj}=0\).

Consequently
\[
\|B(m)|_E\|_{1\to1}
\le\max\left\{\frac12+q,1-\varepsilon\right\}
=\frac{7967}{8192}=R<1,
\tag{20}
\]
since \(1/2+q=113/128\) and
\(R-113/128=735/8192>0\). In particular, neither a factor of \(3/2\) nor a bare triangle bound \(1/2+\delta(A)\) is being inserted into this estimate.

For \(u,v\in S_3\), symmetry in (1) gives the exact polarization identity
\[
V(u)-V(v)=2A((u+v)/2)(u-v),
\]
and hence
\[
Tu-Tv=B((u+v)/2)(u-v).
\]
The vector \(u-v\) belongs to \(E\), and its midpoint has the required image form. Equation (20) proves (2).

## 5. Existence, uniqueness, and all starting points

The image \(S_3\) is nonempty and compact because \(T^3\) is continuous on the nonempty compact simplex. It is therefore complete in the inherited metric, even without convexity. Nested images give \(TS_3\subseteq S_3\). Banach's theorem applied to (2) supplies a unique fixed point \(p\) of \(T\) in \(S_3\).

The equation \(Tp=p\) is equivalent to \(Vp=p\). Every possible fixed point \(w\) of \(V\) in the entire simplex is also fixed by \(T\), and therefore belongs to \(S_3\), because \(w=T^3w\). Uniqueness on \(S_3\) thus gives uniqueness throughout \(\Delta_3\), without assuming an earlier fixed-point theorem.

For arbitrary \(x\in\Delta_3\), the point \(T^3x\) lies in \(S_3\). Repeated use of (2) yields, for \(n\ge3\),
\[
\|T^nx-p\|_1\le R^{n-3}\|T^3x-p\|_1
\le2R^{n-3},
\]
because the simplex has \(\ell^1\) diameter two. This is exactly the proposed rate (3), including the initial exponent-zero case \(n=3\).
