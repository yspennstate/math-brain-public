## Result and scope

**PROVED:** for arbitrary complex Hermitian two-by-two positive contractions, the full determinantal subset entropy is concave in the kernel, including on the boundary. This closes the definite-direction obligation (C2) in agent 002's report and, together with its elementary nonpositive-determinant case, establishes the complete two-site case.

The new quantitative estimate is

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[D,D]\le -8\det D\quad
\text{if }0<K<I\text{ and }\det D>0.}
\tag{1}
\]

The constant 8 is sharp: equality holds at \(K=I_2/2\), \(D=I_2\). Here and throughout, logarithms are natural.

**Exact minimization obtained:** the sum of the Fisher contributions of the empty and full atoms, over positive-definite directions of determinant one. **The minimum of the entire four-atom Fisher objective has not been computed.** The two-atom minimum is a lower bound for that full objective and already proves the required inequality. No assertion of a full-objective optimizer is made.

This does **not** solve the arbitrary-dimensional Lyons conjecture. Historical novelty of this two-site theorem and its proof has not been established. Primary sources and limited searches are recorded below; there is no worldwide priority claim.

## 1. Two-site law and its Hessian

Let

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&s\end{pmatrix},
\]

where \(0<K<I\) and \(D=D^*\) is arbitrary. Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=p_{00}=(1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2=\det(I-K),\\
y&=p_{10}=a(1-b)+|c|^2,\\
z&=p_{01}=(1-a)b+|c|^2,\\
w&=p_{11}=ab-|c|^2=\det K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

All four probabilities are positive and sum to one. Direct multiplication gives

\[
yz-xw=|c|^2\ge0,
\qquad L:=\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

Primes denote differentiation at zero along \(K+tD\), and
\(\delta=\det D=us-|v|^2\). The four second derivatives, in the order in (2), are

\[
p''=2\delta(1,-1,-1,1).
\tag{4}
\]

Normalization gives \(\sum p'=\sum p''=0\). Differentiating
\(H(K)=-\sum p\log p\) therefore gives

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[D,D]=-\mathcal I_K(D)+2\delta L,\qquad
\mathcal I_K(D):=\sum_{q\in\{x,y,z,w\}}\frac{(q')^2}{q}.}
\tag{5}
\]

This is the identity already established in agent 002. In particular, if
\(\delta\le0\), both terms on the right of (5) are nonpositive. Only
\(\delta>0\) needs a new quantitative bound.

For a two-by-two Hermitian matrix, \(\delta>0\) implies definiteness. Negating
\(D\) leaves the second variation and the Fisher objective unchanged, so we
may take \(D>0\). Dividing \(D\) by \(\sqrt\delta\) reduces to
\(\det D=1\) when desired.

## 2. A two-by-two matrix minimization lemma

**Lemma.** Suppose \(A,B\) are positive-definite complex Hermitian
two-by-two matrices with \(\det A=\det B=1\). Then

\[
\min_{D>0,\,\det D=1}
\left\{(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2\right\}
=2\det(A+B).
\tag{6}
\]

An optimizer is

\[
D_*:=\sqrt{\det(A+B)}\,(A+B)^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof of the lower bound.** Put \(C=A+B>0\). Scalar Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2
\ge\frac12(\operatorname{tr}CD)^2.
\]

The matrix \(C^{1/2}DC^{1/2}\) is positive definite. Applying the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality to its two positive eigenvalues gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}CD\ge2\sqrt{\det C\det D}.
\]

Thus the objective is at least \(2\det C\) when \(\det D=1\).
This argument does not assume that \(A,B,D\) commute, and is valid over the
complex Hermitian cone.

**Attainment.** The matrix in (7) is positive definite, has determinant one,
and satisfies \(CD_*=\sqrt{\det C}\,I_2\), so equality holds in the eigenvalue
inequality. To check equality in scalar Cauchy–Schwarz, it remains to show
\(\operatorname{tr}AD_*=\operatorname{tr}BD_*\). For two-by-two matrices,
the adjugate is linear, and mixed determinant terms are symmetric:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A\operatorname{adj}B)
=\operatorname{tr}(B\operatorname{adj}A).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}((A-B)\operatorname{adj}(A+B))
&=2\det A-2\det B\\
&\quad+\operatorname{tr}(A\operatorname{adj}B)
-\operatorname{tr}(B\operatorname{adj}A)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(D_*=\operatorname{adj}C/\sqrt{\det C}\), the two traces agree. This
proves attainment and (6). ∎

For general \(D>0\), homogeneity gives the immediately useful form

\[
(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2
\ge2\det D\det(A+B).
\tag{8}
\]

## 3. Exact minimum for the two extreme Fisher contributions

By the determinant derivative identity,

\[
w'=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}K\,D),\qquad
-x'=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(I-K)\,D).
\tag{9}
\]

Define

\[
A:=\frac{\operatorname{adj}K}{\sqrt w},\qquad
B:=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-K)}{\sqrt x}.
\tag{10}
\]

These matrices are positive-definite complex Hermitian, and
\(\det A=\det B=1\), because in dimension two the adjugate preserves
the determinant. Thus the contribution of the empty and full atoms is

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm extreme}(D):=\frac{(x')^2}{x}+\frac{(w')^2}{w}
=(\operatorname{tr}AD)^2+(\operatorname{tr}BD)^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Write \(m=y+z=1-x-w\). For two-by-two matrices,

\[
\det(U+V)=\det U+\det V+
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}U\,V).
\]

Also,

\[
\operatorname{adj}(I-K)=I-\operatorname{adj}K,
\qquad K\operatorname{adj}K=wI.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}(I-K))
&=\operatorname{tr}K-2w\\
&=1-x-w=m,
\end{aligned}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\det(A+B)=2+\frac{m}{\sqrt{xw}}.}
\tag{12}
\]

The lemma proves the exact restricted minimization

\[
\boxed{\min_{D>0,\,\det D=1}\mathcal I_{\rm extreme}(D)
=4+\frac{2(y+z)}{\sqrt{xw}}.}
\tag{13}
\]

The optimizer is (7) with (10). For the full Fisher objective, all other
terms are nonnegative, so for every \(D>0\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I_K(D)\ge\mathcal I_{\rm extreme}(D)
\ge\det D\left(4+\frac{2(y+z)}{\sqrt{xw}}\right).}
\tag{14}
\]

**Important distinction.** Formula (13) is exact for the two-atom objective.
The omitted middle terms need not vanish at its optimizer, so (13) is not
an assertion about the exact minimum of \(\mathcal I_K\). For example, at
\(K=aI\), \(a\ne1/2\), the extreme-atom optimizer is \(D=I\), its extreme
Fisher value is 8, but its middle Fisher contributions are positive. The
bound (14), rather than an exact full-objective minimum, is what is needed.

## 4. Domination of the entropy logarithm

Set

\[
T:=\frac{y+z}{2\sqrt{xw}}.
\]

By \(yz\ge xw\) and the scalar arithmetic–geometric mean inequality,
\(T\ge1\). Also,

\[
L=\log\frac{yz}{xw}
\le\log\frac{(y+z)^2}{4xw}
=2\log T.
\tag{15}
\]

Combine (14) and (15) with \(\log T\le T-1\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_K(D)-2\det D\,L
&\ge4\det D\,(1+T-\log T)\\
&\ge8\det D.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

For a positive-definite direction of determinant one this is, in particular,
\(\mathcal I_K(D)\ge2L+8\), stronger than the required \(\mathcal I_K(D)\ge2L\).
Substitution into (5) proves (1). Negative-definite directions follow by
negating \(D\), and the nonpositive-determinant case was already handled.
Thus the full Hessian is negative semidefinite at every interior kernel.

The positive margin has a sharp equality example. At \(K=I_2/2,D=I_2\),
the atoms are all \(1/4\), their first derivatives are \((-1,0,0,1)\),
\(L=0\), and \(H''=-8\). No larger universal coefficient than 8 can replace
8 in (1).

## 5. Complex transverse directions are included

The proof above directly uses complex Hermitian matrices and the positive
matrix \(C^{1/2}DC^{1/2}\). It makes no real-matrix restriction and no
assumption that the phase of \(v\) agrees with the phase of \(c\).

For an additional explicit check of this issue, use a diagonal unitary gauge
to make \(c\ge0\) real, and write \(v=q+i\eta\). The first derivatives of
all atoms depend on \(q\) but not on \(\eta\), whereas

\[
\det D=us-q^2-\eta^2.
\]

If \(D>0\) has determinant one, deleting the imaginary off-diagonal part
produces \(D_0>0\) with determinant \(1+\eta^2\) and the same Fisher value.
Rescaling gives

\[
\widetilde D:=D_0/\sqrt{1+\eta^2}>0,
\qquad \det\widetilde D=1,
\qquad
\mathcal I_K(\widetilde D)=\frac{\mathcal I_K(D)}{1+\eta^2}.
\tag{17}
\]

Thus a nonzero transverse component cannot decrease the determinant-one
full Fisher infimum. This observation is optional for the proof, since the
matrix lemma already controls it without gauging. It explains why simply
discarding transverse terms without compensating for their effect on the
determinant would have been an incomplete reduction.

## 6. The global two-site theorem, including boundary kernels

**Theorem.** Let \(K_0,K_1\) be any two-by-two complex Hermitian matrices
satisfying \(0\le K_j\le I\). Then, for every \(t\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)
\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1).}
\tag{18}
\]

**Proof.** If both endpoints are strict contractions, every point of the
segment is strict, and the established nonpositive second variation along
\(D=K_1-K_0\) proves the ordinary one-variable concavity inequality.

For general endpoints set

\[
K_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\]

These are strict contractions and their affine interpolants are the same
regularization of the original interpolant. Apply the strict inequality and
let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). The atoms are polynomial in the matrix
entries, and \(-p\log p\) extends continuously to zero, so the limit proves
(18). ∎

There is also a quantitative consequence when
\(\det(K_1-K_0)>0\):

\[
H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)
\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)
+4\det(K_1-K_0)t(1-t).
\tag{19}
\]

Indeed (1) says that the second derivative on the segment is at most the
constant \(-8\det(K_1-K_0)\). Subtracting the endpoint chord and the
quadratic \(4\det(K_1-K_0)t(1-t)\) leaves a concave function vanishing at
the two endpoints. The boundary case follows by the same regularization,
whose direction determinant is multiplied by \((1-2\varepsilon)^2\).
