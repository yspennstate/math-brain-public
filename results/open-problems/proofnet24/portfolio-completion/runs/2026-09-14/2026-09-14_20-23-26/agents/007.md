## 1. Statement, coefficient bounds, and existence

Let

\[
\Delta_3=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:x_i\ge0,\ \sum_i x_i=1\},
\qquad
V_r(x)=\sum_{a,b=1}^4P_{ab,r}x_ax_b,
\]

where for every parent pair `(a,b)`:

\[
P_{ab,r}=P_{ba,r}\ge0,
\qquad \sum_{r=1}^4P_{ab,r}=1,
\qquad P_{ab,r}=0\quad\text{if }r\in\{a,b\}.
\]

These hypotheses imply `0 <= P_{ab,r} <= 1`; no coefficientwise upper bound is an additional assumption. In particular `P_{rr,r}=0`, because the exclusion condition includes equal parent indices.

For `x` in the simplex every `V_r(x)` is nonnegative, and finite-sum rearrangement gives

\[
\sum_r V_r(x)
=\sum_{a,b}x_ax_b\sum_rP_{ab,r}
=\sum_{a,b}x_ax_b
=\left(\sum_a x_a\right)^2=1.
\]

Thus `V` maps the simplex into itself. It is polynomial, hence continuous. The simplex is nonempty, compact, and convex, so Brouwer's theorem gives at least one fixed point. This use of Brouwer does not require interior preservation or positive coefficients.

## 2. Audit of the coordinate cap

For a fixed point `x`, every summand in `V_r(x)` with `a=r` or `b=r` is zero. All remaining coefficients are at most one and all monomials are nonnegative. Therefore

\[
x_r=V_r(x)
\le\sum_{a\ne r,\ b\ne r}x_ax_b
=\left(\sum_{a\ne r}x_a\right)^2
=(1-x_r)^2.
\]

Equivalently `x_r^2-3x_r+1 >= 0`. Its roots are

\[
c=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2},\qquad d=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]

The quadratic is nonnegative only outside `(c,d)`. Since `d>1` and `0<=x_r<=1`, we obtain `x_r<=c`. Also `2<sqrt(5)<3`, so `0<c<1/2` and

\[
2c=3-\sqrt5<1.
\]

Every inequality is non-strict until this last constant comparison. Equality in the coordinate cap would not invalidate uniqueness. A zero fixed-point coordinate is explicitly allowed. This bound actually works in any number of types under the same parent-exclusion rule; the four-type restriction enters later.

## 3. Audit of the sign lemma

Let `x != y` be fixed points and let `z=x-y`. Since `sum_i z_i=0` and `z != 0`, `z` has a strictly positive and a strictly negative coordinate.

Suppose `z_i>0` is its only positive coordinate. For all `a != i`, `x_a<=y_a`. The polynomial `V_i` uses only these other coordinates and has nonnegative coefficients. For each contributing pair, the inequalities of nonnegative factors give `x_a x_b <= y_a y_b`. Hence

\[
x_i=V_i(x)\le V_i(y)=y_i,
\]

contradicting `z_i>0`. Applying this same argument to `-z=y-x` excludes exactly one negative coordinate.

Therefore both sign sets have cardinality at least two. There are only four coordinates, so there are exactly two positive and two negative coordinates, with no zero coordinate. This remains valid when `x` or `y` has zero entries: the signs concern their **difference**, and the product comparisons require only nonnegativity.

The lemma does not assume strict monotonicity. Vanishing coefficients make the monotone inequality weak, which still contradicts `x_i>y_i`.

## 4. Independent boundary audit

The main proof does not need a boundary classification, but one is available and removes any possible concern about the wording in Lemma 2.

By the coordinate cap, a fixed point has at least three positive coordinates, since a support of size at most two would imply

\[
1=\sum_r x_r\le2c<1.
\]

If a fixed point is on the boundary, its support is therefore a triple `S`, with a unique missing type `t`. For every `a,b in S`, the product `x_a x_b` is strictly positive. In

\[
0=x_t=V_t(x)=\sum_{a,b\in S}P_{ab,t}x_ax_b
\]

all terms are nonnegative, so each `P_{ab,t}=0` for `a,b in S`. For a pair with at least one parent equal to `t`, the coefficient `P_{ab,t}` is already zero by strict non-Volterra exclusion. These two cases exhaust all pairs, hence

\[
V_t\equiv0.
\]

Every fixed point of this operator then has coordinate `t` equal to zero. Two fixed points would have a zero coordinate in their difference, contradicting the sign lemma. Thus a boundary fixed point, if present, is already unique without the transfer lemma.

This argument covers a prospective boundary/interior pair as well: the interior member is impossible because all fixed points must have the missing coordinate zero. It also covers fixed points on two proposed distinct boundary faces. It uses neither the known three-type uniqueness theorem nor an index calculation.

## 5. Transfer inequality: complete expansion and analytic falsification attempt

Fix a two-by-two partition `G={i,k}`, `H={j,ell}` and a nonnegative vector `m`. Define

\[
p_{ab}=\sum_{r\in G}P_{ab,r},\qquad
A(m)_{rb}=\sum_a P_{ab,r}m_a,
\]

and let

\[
L=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j).
\]

Here rows of `A` are output types and columns are parent types. With this convention,

\[
L=\sum_a m_a(p_{ai}-p_{aj}).
\]

The required coefficient facts are all exact:

- `0<=p_{ab}<=1` by nonnegative stochasticity;
- `p_{ab}=p_{ba}` by parent symmetry;
- `p_{ki}=0`, since the parents `i,k` exhaust `G` and neither may be produced;
- `p_{ell j}=1`, since the parents `ell,j` exhaust `H`, and all stochastic mass must go to the complementary pair `G`.

The last identity uses both the fact that there are exactly four types and the fact that `H` has exactly two members. It is not an assumption about positive allowed entries.

Splitting the four summands and using the shared variable `p_{ij}=p_{ji}` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
L
&=m_i(p_{ii}-p_{ij})
 +m_j(p_{ij}-p_{jj})
 -m_kp_{kj}
 +m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1)\\
&=m_ip_{ii}+(m_j-m_i)p_{ij}-m_jp_{jj}
  -m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(p_{\ell i}-1).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular the dropped terms in Agent 006 have the correct signs. Its displayed estimate follows by `p_{ii}<=1`, `p_{jj}>=0`, `p_{kj}>=0`, and `p_{ell i}<=1`:

\[
L\le m_i(1-p_{ij})+m_jp_{ij}\le\max(m_i,m_j).
\]

### Explicit nonnegative slack identities

To test the inequality independently, rather than just repeat its two upper bounds, subtract the exact expansion from the proposed extremum.

If `m_i>=m_j`, then

\[
\boxed{
m_i-L
=(m_i-m_j)p_{ij}
 +m_i(1-p_{ii})+m_jp_{jj}
 +m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(1-p_{\ell i}).
}
\]

If `m_j>=m_i`, then

\[
\boxed{
m_j-L
=(m_j-m_i)(1-p_{ij})
 +m_i(1-p_{ii})+m_jp_{jj}
 +m_kp_{kj}+m_\ell(1-p_{\ell i}).
}
\]

Every term is nonnegative. When `m_i=m_j`, the two identities agree. Zero coordinates, zero coefficients, and coefficients equal to one cause no exception. The normalization `sum_a m_a=1` is not needed for this inequality itself.

### Exact optimization and an attainable extremizer

The bound is sharp for each fixed `m`. Relabel to `G={1,2}`, `H={3,4}`, `i=1`, `j=3`. For unordered parent pairs, assign offspring by the following full admissible table; the symmetric ordered pair has the same distribution.

| Parent pair | Offspring distribution |
|---|---|
| `(1,1)` | type 2 with probability 1 |
| `(1,2)` | type 3 with probability 1 |
| `(1,3)` | type 2 with probability `t`, type 4 with probability `1-t` |
| `(1,4)` | type 2 with probability 1 |
| `(2,2)` | type 1 with probability 1 |
| `(2,3)` | type 4 with probability 1 |
| `(2,4)` | type 1 with probability 1 |
| `(3,3)` | type 4 with probability 1 |
| `(3,4)` | type 1 with probability 1 |
| `(4,4)` | type 1 with probability 1 |

For `0<=t<=1`, every row has nonnegative mass one and excludes both parents. The relevant aggregated coefficients are

\[
p_{11}=1,\quad p_{33}=0,\quad p_{13}=t,
\quad p_{23}=0,\quad p_{41}=1,
\quad p_{21}=0,\quad p_{43}=1.
\]

Consequently

\[
L=m_1(1-t)+m_3t.
\]

Choose `t=0` when `m_1>=m_3` and `t=1` otherwise. This proves the exact optimization statement

\[
\max_{P\text{ admissible}}\,
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A_P(m)(e_i-e_j)
=\max(m_i,m_j).
\]

This attempted falsification therefore confirms the asserted bound, including its constant. Treating `p_{ij}` and `p_{ji}` as independent variables would give a false maximization problem; their symmetry is essential and was retained throughout.

### Boundary weight checks and the dimension limit

At `m=e_i`, the expression is `p_{ii}-p_{ij}<=1`; at `m=e_j` it is `p_{ij}-p_{jj}<=1`; at `m=e_k` it is `-p_{kj}<=0`; at `m=e_ell` it is `p_{ell i}-1<=0`. At `m=(e_i+e_j)/2`, symmetry cancels `p_{ij}` and gives `L=(p_{ii}-p_{jj})/2<=1/2`. These are also the vertices of the two simplex regions cut by `m_i>=m_j` and `m_j>=m_i`, so they provide another complete elementary reduction in the weight variable.

The obvious five-type generalization is false: take `G={1,2}`, `H={3,4,5}`, `i=1`, `j=3`, `m=e_4`, and admissible deterministic rules `(4,1)->2`, `(4,3)->5`. Complete other parent rules arbitrarily subject to exclusion and symmetry. Then `L=1` but `max(m_1,m_3)=0`. This is an out-of-scope counterexample, not a flaw in the four-type lemma. It identifies exactly why the proof cannot be copied to a two-versus-three partition.

## 6. Transfer decomposition, polarization, and the final contradiction

For two hypothetical distinct fixed points, the sign lemma makes `G={i:z_i>0}` and `H={j:z_j<0}` pairs. Set

\[
h=\sum_{i\in G}z_i=-\sum_{j\in H}z_j>0,
\qquad
\theta_{ij}=\frac{z_i(-z_j)}{h^2}.
\]

The only division is by `h`, which is strictly positive. The weights satisfy

\[
\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}
=\frac{(\sum_{i\in G}z_i)(\sum_{j\in H}(-z_j))}{h^2}=1.
\]

For each positive coordinate, `h sum_j theta_{ij}=z_i`; for each negative coordinate, `-h sum_i theta_{ij}=z_j`. Thus, exactly,

\[
z=h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}(e_i-e_j).
\]

All weights are nonnegative, as required when summing upper bounds.

Let `m=(x+y)/2`. It lies in the simplex and satisfies `m_r<=c` by averaging the two coordinate caps. For each ordered pair of indices,

\[
x_ax_b-y_ay_b=m_a z_b+z_a m_b.
\]

Both cross terms are needed. Multiplying by `P_{ab,r}`, summing, and swapping the two parent indices in the second term gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
V_r(x)-V_r(y)
&=\sum_{a,b}P_{ab,r}(m_a z_b+z_a m_b)\\
&=2\sum_b\left(\sum_aP_{ab,r}m_a\right)z_b
=2(A(m)z)_r.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the exact quadratic polarization formula. There is no approximation or appeal to a derivative at an unspecified intermediate point, and its factor of two is correct.

Since `x,y` are fixed,

\[
\begin{aligned}
h
&=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}z
=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}(V(x)-V(y))\\
&=2h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}
\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}A(m)(e_i-e_j)\\
&\le2h\sum_{i\in G,j\in H}\theta_{ij}\max(m_i,m_j)\\
&\le2hc\sum_{i,j}\theta_{ij}
=2ch<h.
\end{aligned}
\]

The strict final inequality uses only `h>0` and `2c<1`. Negative values of individual transfer expressions present no issue: all are upper bounded, and all weights are nonnegative. Every equality and inequality in the displayed proof is justified. This contradiction proves at most one fixed point, and Brouwer supplies exactly one.

## 7. Audit of positive real tangent eigenvalues

At a fixed point `x`, define the **ambient** matrix `J=DV(x)=2A(x)`. Direct differentiation gives this identity using parent symmetry. The entries of `A(x)` are nonnegative and

\[
A(x)_{jj}=\sum_aP_{aj,j}x_a=0,
\qquad
\sum_r A(x)_{rj}=\sum_a x_a\sum_rP_{aj,r}=1.
\]

Thus `A(x)` is column-stochastic with zero diagonal, and `J` is nonnegative with zero diagonal. The sum-zero hyperplane `T` is invariant because

\[
\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}A(x)z=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}z.
\]

If `lambda>0` is a real eigenvalue of `J|_T`, choose a nonzero real eigenvector `z in T`. Such a real eigenvector exists because the matrix and eigenvalue are real. It has both signs. If its only positive coordinate were `z_i`, the ambient zero diagonal and nonnegative off-diagonal entries would imply

\[
(Jz)_i=\sum_{b\ne i}J_{ib}z_b\le0,
\]

contradicting `(Jz)_i=lambda z_i>0`. Negating the eigenvector excludes exactly one negative coordinate. Hence the same two-versus-two transfer decomposition applies, and

\[
\lambda h=\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}Jz
\le2h\max_r x_r.
\]

Dividing by `h>0` yields

\[
0<\lambda\le2\max_r x_r\le3-\sqrt5<1.
\]

No irreducibility, simplicity of eigenvalues, interiority, or diagonalizability has entered. This estimate concerns positive **real** eigenvalues. It does not bound all eigenvalue moduli by one and therefore does not establish attraction or convergence of the original iteration.

## 8. Audit of the determinant positivity and polynomial identity

For `0<s<=1`, singularity of `I-sJ|_T` would yield a real eigenvalue `1/s>=1` of the real operator `J|_T`, contradicting the preceding estimate. At `s=0` the determinant equals one. Since this determinant is a continuous real polynomial in `s` and never vanishes on `[0,1]`,

\[
\det(I-J|_T)>0.
\]

This is valid at boundary fixed points as well. It is an affine-hull nondegeneracy statement; it does not assign the same local fixed-point index to boundary and interior fixed points.

For completeness, the archived closed formula can be derived without assuming any eigenvalue is simple. Write `A=A(x)` and `B=A|_T`. Since `Ax=V(x)=x` and `sum_i x_i=1`,

\[
\mathbb R^4=\operatorname{span}\{x\}\oplus T
\]

is an invariant decomposition, also when `x` is on the boundary. Thus

\[
p_A(t)=\det(tI-A)=(t-1)\det(tI_T-B).
\]

It follows that

\[
\det(I-J|_T)=\det(I_T-2B)
=8\det(\tfrac12 I_T-B)
=-16p_A(\tfrac12).
\]

Let `e_j(A)` be the coefficients given by elementary symmetric functions of the eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity; equivalently use the characteristic-polynomial coefficients. Since `tr(A)=0`,

\[
p_A(t)=t^4+e_2t^2-e_3t+e_4.
\]

The equality `p_A(1)=0` gives `e_2=e_3-e_4-1`. Substitution, with every sign retained, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\tfrac12)
&=\frac1{16}+\frac{e_2}{4}-\frac{e_3}{2}+e_4\\
&=-\frac3{16}-\frac{e_3}{4}+\frac{3e_4}{4},
\end{aligned}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\det(I-J|_T)=3+4e_3(A)-12\det A>0.
}
\]

This proves the determinant question as claimed, independently of the archived index-count machinery.
