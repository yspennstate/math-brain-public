## Bounded August source extract

\[
A_i\in\operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb R),\quad \|A_i\|_{\rm op}\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\min_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^n}
\Big\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iA_i\Big\|_{\rm op}\le C\sqrt n.
\tag{S}

## Independently proved scope reductions

All conclusions in this section are implications **conditional on (S)**. They do not certify (S).

### Complex Hermitian square case

For a complex matrix \(A=B+iC\), with real \(B,C\), define
\[
\mathcal R(A)=
\begin{pmatrix}B&-C\\ C&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Identify \(z=x+iy\in\mathbb C^d\) with \((x,y)\in\mathbb R^{2d}\). This identification preserves Euclidean length and intertwines \(A\) with \(\mathcal R(A)\). Therefore
\[
\|\mathcal R(A)\|_{\rm op}=\|A\|_{\rm op},\qquad
\mathcal R(A^*)=\mathcal R(A)^T,\qquad
\mathcal R\Big(\sum_i t_iA_i\Big)=\sum_i t_i\mathcal R(A_i)
\]
for real \(t_i\).

If \(A_1,\ldots,A_n\) are Hermitian \(n\times n\) contractions, their realifications are symmetric \(2n\times2n\) contractions. Append \(n\) zero matrices and apply (S) at size \(2n\). The signs of zero terms are immaterial, giving
\[
\operatorname{disc}_{\rm op}(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\le \sqrt2\,C\sqrt n.
\]
Thus a valid proof of the real square theorem settles the archive's complex square signing target.

### Bounded aspect ratio and nonsymmetric matrices

For \(n\) Hermitian \(d\times d\) matrices, use realification, enlarge the matrices by zero blocks to size
\[
N=\max\{n,2d\},
\]
and add zero matrices until the family also has \(N\) members. Equation (S) then gives \(C\sqrt N\). In particular, \(d\le a n\), with fixed \(a\), gives \(C\sqrt{\max(1,2a)}\sqrt n\). This includes the addendum's entire \(D\le n\) regime.

For nonsymmetric matrices use the Hermitian dilation
\[
\mathcal D(A)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\ A^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal D(A)^2=\operatorname{diag}(AA^*,A^*A)\), its norm equals \(\|A\|_{\rm op}\). The dilation is real-linear and preserves signed sums. Thus complex nonsymmetric matrices require \(N=\max\{n,4d\}\) after realification; real nonsymmetric matrices require \(N=\max\{n,2d\}\). The conclusion remains \(O(\sqrt n)\) at bounded aspect ratio.

Padding alone gives \(O(\sqrt d)\) when \(d/n\) is large. It supplies no optimal logarithmic dependence.

## Conditional endpoint audit and the aspect-ratio gap

I checked the external partial-coloring premise directly in [Dadush–Jiang–Reis, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03171). A symmetric convex set in \(m\) variables with Gaussian measure at least \(2^{-\theta m}\), for fixed \(\theta>0\), admits a randomized polynomial-time shifted partial coloring with an absolute scaling loss and at least half the variables fixed. This primary statement permits convex sets; a kernel making a discrepancy set unbounded is therefore not a bounded-body obstruction.

Here is an independent general-parameter calculation. Assume (H) and that partial-coloring result. Let \(d\ge n\), set
\[
L=1+\log(2d/n),\qquad
K_S=\left\{x:\Big\|\sum_{i\in S}x_iA_i\Big\|_{\rm op}
\le\kappa(\log2)\sqrt{|S|}\big(L+\log(n/|S|)\big)^2\right\}.
\]
Equation (H) applies to every active subset \(S\), because \(|S|\le n\le d\). Its Gaussian measure is at least \(2^{-|S|}\). Successive shifted partial colorings produce increments supported on active coordinates, and active sizes satisfy \(m_{k+1}\le m_k/2\). Frozen coordinates are never changed.

For each nonzero \(m_k\), let
\[
j_k=\left\lfloor\log_2(n/m_k)\right\rfloor.
\]
The integers \(j_k\) strictly increase. Since \(L\ge\log2\),
\[
\sqrt{m_k}\big(L+\log(n/m_k)\big)^2
\le \sqrt n\,2^{-j_k/2}L^2(j_k+2)^2.
\]
Consequently, the total discrepancy is at most
\[
C_{\rm pc}\kappa(\log2)\,\mathcal S\,\sqrt n\,L^2,\qquad
\mathcal S=\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j/2}(j+2)^2
=\frac{4-3r+r^2}{(1-r)^3},\quad r=2^{-1/2}.
\tag{R}
\]
The series identity follows by differentiating the geometric series twice; \(\mathcal S\approx94.6690475583\). The triangle inequality completes the signing implication, and halving gives finitely many rounds.

For \(d=n\), \(L\) is an absolute constant: the endpoint reduction indeed yields (S). There is no accumulating \(\log n\) loss. Using dyadic buckets is useful because the radius as a function of active-set size need not be monotone.

For \(d/n\to\infty\), (R) has \((1+\log(2d/n))^2\), whereas the general-parameter conjecture has a square root of a logarithm. Their ratio grows like \((\log(d/n))^{3/2}\). This proves a mismatch between these particular bounds, not an impossibility theorem about deriving the optimal bound by additional arguments. I did not establish the present global literature status of that stronger formulation.

A nonhereditary Gaussian probability estimate with exponent proportional to \(d\) cannot simply replace (H): once an active set has \(m\ll d\), a lower bound \(e^{-cd}\) need not meet the partial-coloring requirement \(e^{-O(m)}\). The hereditary input is a substantive proof obligation.

## What has and has not been validated

The endpoint signing implication above is independently established. The proposed analytical theorem (H) remains an imported claim in this report.

I inspected the theorem statements, the final partial-coloring section, the parameter-selection section, the Gaussian polynomial-moment appendix, the differentiation-under-the-integral appendix, and the displayed curvature argument. I did **not** complete an independent proof of the matrix-weighted Poincaré lemmas, the boundary/exhaustion argument, or every inequality feeding the curvature and interpolation estimates. The concrete remaining validation route is to certify those analytical inputs and their use in (H), including dimension-independent constants. No algorithm implementation or bit-complexity audit was performed.

A few endpoint and auxiliary calculations being consistent does not certify a 47-page proof. No claim of peer-review acceptance, formal verification, or community consensus is made. Conversely, the lack of such verification in this bounded run is not a refutation.

The August claim also does not repair the fixed-MASA proposition refuted in agents/005.md and agents/043.md. That proposition asks one fixed positive compression to cover every contraction with an absolute coefficient bound; its quantifiers and positive-lift restrictions are absent from (S). The \(\Omega(\sqrt d)\) coefficient obstruction can coexist with a direct signing theorem. Likewise, the full-unit-ball \(d^2\) dimensional obstruction is not a discrepancy lower bound for a tuple-dependent reduction.

The separate Hilbert–Schmidt-normalized Matrix Komlós assertion remains false, as already recorded by worker 005 and [Dadush–Jiang–Reis, Corollary 1.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03171). A Spencer-scale \(O(\sqrt n)\) claim does not imply a Komlós-scale \(O(1)\) theorem.
