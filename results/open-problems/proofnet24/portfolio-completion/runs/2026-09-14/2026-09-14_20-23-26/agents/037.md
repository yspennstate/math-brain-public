**The fixed-law real P12 theorem is recovered by a three-tier construction.** The two-tier displayed proof in the archived P12 report is incorrect: it drops `+lambda*m`. ### Theorem 037-FIXED-LAW

Let one fixed real random variable \(\xi\) satisfy
\[
\mathbb E\xi=0,\qquad \mathbb E\xi^2=1,
\qquad \|f_\xi\|_\infty\le K<\infty.
\]
For each \(m\), let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries distributed as \(\xi\). Assume
\[
N_m/m\longrightarrow\gamma>1,
\qquad k_m\ge1\text{ is an integer},
\qquad k_m\log m=o(m).
\]
Write
\[
M_{m,k_m-1}(A_m)
=\min_{|T|=m+k_m-1}\sigma_m((A_m)_T),
\quad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,k_m-1}(A_m)
\longrightarrow-h(\gamma)\quad\text{in probability}.}
\]
In fact, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), there are \(C,c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb P\!\left\{
\left|\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,k_m-1}+h(\gamma)\right|>\varepsilon
\right\}\le Ce^{-cm}.}
\tag{T}
\]
Consequently the convergence is almost sure under any common coupling of the levelwise matrices. No cross-dimension independence is needed. There is no expectation-convergence assertion here.

The proof consists of three focused claims: the exact correction, the three-tier staging theorem with its geometric initialization, and the fixed-law assembly. The newer triangular balanced-tail local-density module is **not** certified by this report; its staging consequence is stated separately in Section 7.

## 1. Claim 037-TWO-TIER: the exact missing term

Suppose the archived two-tier recurrence is
\[
M_j\ge\theta sM_{j-1},\qquad e_j\le As e_{j-1},
\quad M_0=L,
\quad e_0\le e^{-\kappa m}L^2.
\]
Iteration gives exactly
\[
M_j\ge(\theta s)^jL,
\qquad e_j\le(As)^je_0,
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e_j}{sM_j^2}
\le\frac{e_0}{L^2}
\left(\frac A{\theta^2}\right)^j s^{-(j+1)}.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]
With
\[
s=\frac{e^{-\lambda m/k}}{\sqrt k},
\qquad 0\le j\le k-1,
\]
the last required stage yields
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{e_{k-1}}{sM_{k-1}^2}
\le-(\kappa-\lambda)m
+\frac k2\log k
+(k-1)\log(A/\theta^2).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]
The cost \(+\lambda m\) is present even at \(k=1\), when the invariant is simply \(e_0/(sL^2)\).

Therefore \(\kappa>0\) alone does not imply the needed invariant. The displayed recurrence certifies it when \(\kappa>\lambda\), under \(k\log m=o(m)\). If \(\kappa\le\lambda\), the estimate is insufficient; that is not a theorem counterexample, nor proof that the actual graph necessarily fails. Equality recurrences already show that the omitted algebraic simplification cannot follow from the displayed inequalities.

Retaining only the two-tier inputs would certify the construction at rates \(\lambda<\min\{h(\beta),\kappa\}\). It would not certify all rates \(\lambda<h(\gamma)\). This is the precise obstruction to the archived proof, rather than an obstruction to the desired law.

## 2. Claim 037-THREE-TIER: exact one-stage and multi-stage statements

### 2.1 Abstract one-stage lemma

Fix a current finite family of \(M\) unit vectors, with ordered distinct pairs classified as ordinary, medium, or bad. Write \(E\) and \(B\) for the medium and bad counts. For an independent fresh row \(X\), suppose
\[
a\ell s\le\mathbb P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le s\}\le Cs
\]
for every current vector, where \(a,C>0\) are fixed, \(0<\ell\le1\), and \(0<s\le1\). Suppose joint survival probabilities are at most
\[
Cs^2\quad\text{(ordinary)},\qquad
Cd_m s^2\quad\text{(medium)},\qquad
Cs\quad\text{(bad)},
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(d_m\ge1\). These are conditional statements once the family is fixed.

Assume the current invariants
\[
sM\ge1,\qquad d_mE/M^2\le1,\qquad B/(sM^2)\le1.
\tag{2.2}
\]
If \(Z\) is the survivor count, then
\[
\mathbb EZ\ge a\ell sM,
\qquad
\mathbb EZ^2\le C_1s^2M^2.
\]
The diagonal contribution is at most \(CsM\); (2.2) bounds it and both exceptional contributions by constant multiples of \(s^2M^2\).

Paley–Zygmund gives
\[
\mathbb P\{Z\ge(a/2)\ell sM\}\ge p_0\ell^2,
\qquad p_0=a^2/(4C_1)>0.
\]
The surviving edge counts satisfy
\[
\mathbb EE_X\le Cd_m s^2E,
\qquad \mathbb EB_X\le CsB.
\]
Markov's inequality at sufficiently large constant multiples of \(\ell^{-2}\) times these bounds makes the union of the two failures at most \((p_0/2)\ell^2\). If a starting count is zero its surviving count is identically zero. Thus with conditional probability at least \(p\ell^2\), where \(p>0\) is fixed,
\[
\boxed{
M'\ge c\ell sM,\qquad
E'\le A\ell^{-2}d_m s^2E,\qquad
B'\le A\ell^{-2}sB.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
This proves the actual one-stage mechanism used in P13. No independence between different vertices or edges is assumed.

### 2.2 Exact iteration

Iterate only on successful rows. Decrease \(c\) and increase \(A\) if necessary so that the bounds below are monotone in the displayed worst-case direction. Then
\[
M_j\ge(c\ell s)^jM_0,
\quad
E_j\le(A\ell^{-2}d_m s^2)^jE_0,
\quad
B_j\le(A\ell^{-2}s)^jB_0.
\]
Consequently the exact normalized bounds are
\[
\boxed{
d_m\frac{E_j}{M_j^2}
\le d_m\frac{E_0}{M_0^2}
\left(\frac A{c^2}\right)^j\ell^{-4j}d_m^j,
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\frac{B_j}{sM_j^2}
\le\frac{B_0}{M_0^2}
\left(\frac A{c^2}\right)^j\ell^{-4j}s^{-(j+1)}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
The factor \(\ell^{-4j}\), rather than \(\ell^{-2j}\), pays both the Markov enlargement and the squared vertex loss.

Suppose
\[
\log M_0=Hm+o(m),\quad H>\lambda>0,
\quad E_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_E m},
\quad B_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_Bm},
\]
with \(\kappa_E>0\), \(\kappa_B>\lambda\), and
\[
s=e^{-\lambda m/k}/\sqrt k.
\]
Uniformly before the next stage, \(0\le j\le k-1\), the relevant worst-case logarithms are bounded by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log(sM_j)
&\ge(H-\lambda)m-\tfrac k2\log k
-(k-1)\log(1/\ell)-O(k)+o(m),\\
\log(d_mE_j/M_j^2)
&\le-\kappa_E m+k\log d_m
+4(k-1)\log(1/\ell)+O(k),\\
\log(B_j/(sM_j^2))
&\le-(\kappa_B-\lambda)m+\tfrac k2\log k
+4(k-1)\log(1/\ell)+O(k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]
These imply (2.2) for all large \(m\) if
\[
k\{1+\log k+\log d_m+\log(1/\ell)\}=o(m).
\tag{2.7}
\]
The final vertex count also tends to infinity exponentially, since
\(\log M_k\ge(H-\lambda)m-o(m)\).

There is no need to prove a bound on \(B_k/(sM_k^2)\) after the last success. Requiring that unused invariant would incorrectly impose an extra \(\lambda m/k\) cost for fixed \(k\).

### 2.3 Sequential reservoir, including the fixed-law improvement

Use \(R\) independent fresh rows in sequence. On failure leave the family unchanged; on success replace it by the surviving family. Before the \(k\)-th success each trial has conditional success probability at least \(p\ell^2\). After \(k\) successes, set all bookkeeping indicators to one. If \(I_i\) are these indicators, then
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(-\sum_{i=1}^RI_i\right)
\le\exp[-p\ell^2R(1-e^{-1})].
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{\text{fewer than }k\text{ successes}\}
\le\exp[k-p\ell^2R(1-e^{-1})].
}
\tag{2.8}
\]
In particular, if \(k=o(R\ell^2)\), this is \(e^{-cR\ell^2}\). This argument is valid for adapted families because every unexamined row is independent of the full history. Failed rows are discarded, not reused.

For fixed-law P12 take \(\ell=1\), \(R\asymp m\). The conditional failure bound is \(e^{-cm}\), improving the fixed-equal-batch bound \(Ck e^{-cm/k}\) in the archive. No additional hypothesis is needed for this improvement.

## 3. Geometric initialization: the required bad-edge rate really is available

Let \(n_0=\lfloor\beta m\rfloor\), \(1<\beta<\gamma\), and let \(\mathcal H\) be all \((m-1)\)-subsets of the base rows. Set
\[
L=\binom{n_0}{m-1},\qquad \log L=h(\beta)m+o(m)=Hm+o(m).
\]
Absolute continuity gives all required ranks almost surely. Write \(u_S\) for a measurable choice of unit normal. For a pair \(S,T\), set
\[
D=m-|S\cap T|,
\qquad Y=1-(u_S\cdot u_T)^2.
\]

### 3.1 Density upper bounds

Input A gives, for every deterministic unit \(u\),
\[
\mathbb P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le s\}\le2\sqrt2Ks.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For deterministic unit \(u,v\), if \(Y=1-(u\cdot v)^2>0\), the orthonormal two-dimensional projected density is at most \(2K^2\). The linear change to the two forms has determinant \(\sqrt Y\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le s,
|\langle X,v\rangle|\le s\}
\le8K^2s^2/\sqrt Y.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.2 Ultra-angle estimate, rederived

Condition on the actual shared original rows and on all private rows for \(S\). Let \(F\) be the residual subspace, of dimension \(D\), and \(v=u_S\in F\). The \(D-1\) private rows for \(T\) are still independent original product-law rows. Let \(w=u_T\).

On \(1-(v\cdot w)^2\le y\), choose the sign of \(w\) so that
\(\|v-w\|\le\sqrt{2y}\). Each of the \(D-1\) private rows must then obey
\[
|\langle X_i,v\rangle|
\le\sqrt{2y}\,\|P_FX_i\|.
\]
Since \(\mathbb EX=0\) and \(\mathbb EXX^T=I\),
\(\mathbb E\|P_FX\|^2=D\). For any \(R>0\), (3.1) and Markov give
\[
\mathbb P\{|\langle X,v\rangle|
\le\sqrt{2y}\|P_FX\|\}
\le C_KR\sqrt{Dy}+R^{-2}.
\]
Set \(R=(Dy)^{-1/6}\). Independence of the original private rows yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{Y\le y\mid\text{shared rows},v\}
\le[C_K(Dy)^{1/3}]^{D-1}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
When the bracket exceeds one the bound is just uninformative. Its useful small-\(Dy\) regime is all that will be used.

### 3.3 Fixed-angle exponential estimate, also rederived

This step only needs bounded density, centering, and variance one; no fixed-law uniform-integrability argument is necessary.

Expose the private rows successively. At a current residual subspace \(F_d\) of dimension \(d\), let
\(q=P_{F_d}v/\|P_{F_d}v\|\) when this is defined. The next orthogonal restriction multiplies the squared projected length by \(1-Z_d\), where
\[
Z_d=\frac{(q\cdot X)^2}{\|P_{F_d}X\|^2}.
\]
If a preceding projected length has become zero, the near-parallel event is already impossible; arbitrary continued choices of \(q\) cause no problem. Otherwise the exact product identity is
\[
(v\cdot w)^2=\prod_{d=2}^D(1-Z_d).
\tag{3.4}
\]
Let \(K_1=\sqrt2K\), and choose
\[
a=\min\{1,(8K_1)^{-1}\},\qquad z_0=a^2/4.
\]
Conditional on the past,
\[
\mathbb P\{|q\cdot X|<a\}\le1/4,
\qquad
\mathbb P\{\|P_{F_d}X\|^2>4d\}\le1/4.
\]
Thus \(Z_d\ge z_0/d\) with conditional probability at least \(1/2\). Let \(J_d\) indicate this sufficient event. Put
\(y_*=1-e^{-z_0/16}>0\). If \(Y\le y_*\), then
\[
\sum_{d=2}^DZ_d\le-\log(1-y_*)=z_0/16,
\]
and therefore \(\sum J_d\le D/16\). The adapted exponential-moment estimate gives
\[
\mathbb P\{\sum J_d\le D/16\}
\le2^{D/16}(3/4)^{D-1}\le Ce^{-cD}.
\]
Hence uniformly in the realized shared rows and first line,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb P\{Y\le y_*\mid\text{shared rows},v\}
\le Ce^{-cD}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]
The constants are uniform for a common density ceiling and centered variance-one laws. Projected coordinates have never been treated as independent.

### 3.4 High-overlap entropy and the correct order of choices

For fixed \(S\), the number of \(T\) at residual dimension \(D\) is
\[
\binom{m-1}{D-1}\binom{n_0-m+1}{D-1}.
\]
For a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta>0\), Stirling's formula and summation over \(D<\delta m\) give
\[
\frac{\#\{(S,T):D<\delta m\}}{L^2}
\le\exp[-(H-g_\beta(\delta))m+o(m)],
\tag{3.6}
\]
where
\[
g_\beta(\delta)=\mathcal H(\delta)
+(\beta-1)\mathcal H(\delta/(\beta-1)),
\quad \mathcal H(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
\]
Take \(\delta\) below the increasing part of this entropy function; equivalently use its supremum over \([0,\delta]\). In either formulation the function tends to zero with \(\delta\).

First choose \(\beta\) so that \(H>\lambda\). Next choose constants
\[
\lambda<\kappa_B<\kappa_H<H,
\]
and then choose \(\delta\) so small that
\(H-g_\beta(\delta)>\kappa_H\).
All high-overlap pairs are assigned to the bad graph. Their deterministic count has a rate strictly larger than \(\kappa_B\).

This choice cannot be replaced by a generic statement that high-overlap pairs have some positive exponential rate. Nor should one unnecessarily halve this deterministic entropy rate by Markov. Both mistakes can lose the margin over \(\lambda\).

### 3.5 Actual P13 cutoff and counts

Take a sufficiently small fixed \(b>0\), and set the ultra-angle cutoff \(y_B=b/m\). Assign pairs as follows:

| Pair class | Definition | Fresh-row joint survival bound |
|---|---|---|
| Bad | \(D<\delta m\), or \(D\ge\delta m\) and \(Y< b/m\) | \(Cs\) |
| Medium | \(D\ge\delta m\) and \(b/m\le Y<y_*\) | \(C_b\sqrt m\,s^2\) |
| Ordinary | Remaining distinct pairs | \(Cs^2\) |

For \(D\ge\delta m\), (3.3) gives
\[
\mathbb P\{Y<b/m\}\le[C_Kb^{1/3}]^{\delta m-1}.
\]
By decreasing the fixed \(b\), this exponent can be made larger than any prescribed constant. Choose it with enough margin beyond \(\kappa_H\). Average over the at most \(L^2\) pairs and use Markov with a target rate strictly between \(\kappa_B\) and \(\kappa_H\). Together with (3.6), this gives, with failure at most \(Ce^{-cm}\),
\[
B_0/L^2\le e^{-\widetilde\kappa_Bm},
\qquad \widetilde\kappa_B>\kappa_B>\lambda.
\]
Similarly (3.5), averaged over low-overlap pairs, gives
\(E_0/L^2\le e^{-\widetilde\kappa_Em}\) with exponential failure and some \(\widetilde\kappa_E>0\).

If a useful subfamily has \(M_0\ge L/2\), restricting both graphs to that subfamily multiplies the normalized bounds by at most four. The strict margins absorb this constant, giving
\[
\boxed{E_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_Em},\qquad
B_0/M_0^2\le e^{-\kappa_Bm},\quad\kappa_B>\lambda.}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Thus the precise initialization required by (2.6) is proved. Pair independence is not used in the averaging or Markov steps.

### 3.6 A simpler polynomial-cutoff variant

Alternatively set \(y_B=m^{-2}\). Then medium pairs cost \(Cm s^2\), so \(d_m=m\), and low-overlap ultra pairs satisfy
\[
\mathbb P\{Y<m^{-2}\}
\le[C_Km^{-1/3}]^{\delta m-1}
=\exp[-(\delta/3+o(1))m\log m].
\]
Their rate automatically exceeds every fixed constant. The same high-overlap entropy choice remains essential. Since \(k\log m=o(m)\), the extra medium cost is still harmless in (2.6). This variant avoids selecting an extremely small fixed angular constant while preserving the same theorem.

## 4. Fixed-law one-normal density floor, with a complete local argument

Here the law is one fixed centered variance-one law with bounded density. This fixed-law restriction is essential to the following uniform Fourier argument.

Let \(u\) be a unit vector outside \(\mathrm{Comp}(\delta_0,\rho_0)\). There are constants \(a_0,b_0,v_0,c_0>0\) and a subset \(I\) with
\[
q=|I|\ge c_0m,\qquad
a_0/\sqrt m\le |u_i|\le b_0/\sqrt m\ (i\in I),
\qquad V=\sum_{i\in I}u_i^2\in[v_0,1].
\tag{4.1}
\]
For example discard the coordinates exceeding \(1/\sqrt{\delta_0m}\); there are at most \(\delta_0m\) of them, and incompressibility leaves more than \(\rho_0^2\) mass outside. Coordinates below \(\rho_0/\sqrt{2m}\) carry at most \(\rho_0^2/2\) mass. The remaining coordinates give (4.1).

Normalize \(b_i=u_i/\sqrt V\). Then \(\sum b_i^2=1\), and fixed constants \(A,B>0\) satisfy
\[
A/\sqrt q\le |b_i|\le B/\sqrt q.
\]
Let \(\varphi(t)=\mathbb Ee^{it\xi}\). Finite variance and centering imply
\(\varphi(t)=1-t^2/2+o(t^2)\) near zero, and for some fixed \(r,c>0\),
\(|\varphi(t)|\le e^{-ct^2}\) when \(|t|\le r\).
The density assumption gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|\varphi(t)|^2dt
=2\pi\int f_\xi(x)^2dx\le2\pi K.
\]
It also gives \(\varphi(t)\to0\) at infinity and \(|\varphi(t)|<1\) for each \(t\ne0\), because an absolutely continuous law cannot be supported on a lattice coset.

The characteristic function \(\psi_q(t)=\prod_{i\in I}\varphi(b_it)\) converges uniformly on compact sets to \(e^{-t^2/2}\), uniformly over these arrays of coefficients. On \(|t|\le r\sqrt q/B\), it is bounded by \(e^{-ct^2}\). On the complement, every \(|b_it|\ge rA/B\), so with
\(\eta=\sup_{|z|\ge rA/B}|\varphi(z)|<1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{|t|>r\sqrt q/B}|\psi_q(t)|dt
&\le\eta^{q-2}\int|\varphi(b_1t)\varphi(b_2t)|dt\\
&\le\frac{2\pi K}{\sqrt{|b_1b_2|}}\eta^{q-2}
\le C\sqrt q\,\eta^{q-2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Dominated convergence on the inner region and this tail estimate show
\[
\int|\psi_q(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|dt\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly over the coefficient arrays. Fourier inversion therefore gives uniform convergence of the normalized sum's density to the standard normal density.

Since \(V\in[v_0,1]\), the density of
\(S_I=\sum_{i\in I}u_i\xi_i\) is eventually bounded below by a fixed positive constant on \([-3,3]\), uniformly in useful \(u\).
The independent remainder \(R_I=\sum_{i\notin I}u_i\xi_i\) has mean zero and variance at most one, so
\(\mathbb P\{|R_I|\le2\}\ge3/4\).
For \(0<s\le1\), the interval centered at \(-R_I\) of radius \(s\) lies in \([-3,3]\) on that event. Integration gives
\[
\boxed{c_\xi s\le\mathbb P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le s\}\le C_Ks,
\qquad0<s\le1.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
The upper bound is (3.1). The lower bound is valid at exponentially small \(s\): it is a density bound, not a CDF approximation. No rate of local convergence is needed, since eventual validity is deterministic for the fixed law.

## 5. Useful base normals: exact use of the imported normal theorem

For one fixed base subset \(S\), take the transposes of its selected rows as the first \(m-1\) columns and adjoin one independent product-law column. The resulting \(m\times m\) matrix is iid, and its extra column is irrelevant to their normal. This puts \(u_S\) exactly in the codimension-one setting of Input B.

The entry concentration bound holds, for example, with radius \(1/(4K)\) and concentration threshold \(3/4\), because interval probability is at most \(1/2\). Every square-matrix row has second moment of its norm equal to \(m\); the sum in assumption (48), for a set of \(m-1\) rows and moment parameter \(p=1\), is \(m(m-1)\). Thus the constants in Lemma 6.4 are fixed across dimensions. It gives
\[
\mathbb P\{u_S\in\mathrm{Comp}(\delta_0,\rho_0)\}\le e^{-c_0m}.
\]
Let \(Q\) be the number of compressible normals among all \(L\) base subsets. Marginal equality and linearity of expectation imply
\(\mathbb EQ\le Le^{-c_0m}\), regardless of overlap dependence. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathbb P\{M_0<L/2\}\le2e^{-c_0m}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]
