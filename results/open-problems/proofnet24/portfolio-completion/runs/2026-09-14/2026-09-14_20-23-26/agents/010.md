## Result and limits

**PROVED, using the established stochastic-domination theorem for DPPs:** Shannon entropy is concave on every affine slice of positive Hermitian contractions on which the complementary principal block to a single coordinate is fixed. Thus all entries in one row and its matching column, including the diagonal entry, may vary jointly and affinely. Such a direction generally has rank two and one eigenvalue of each sign; it need not be a rank-one direction.

The proof produces an actual positive-semidefinite matrix controlling the previously unresolved Hessian term, not merely a reformulation of the Hessian identity. It also establishes a more general lemma for the determinantal expectation of any supermodular set function.

**Additional proved restricted result:** in dimension three, every Hermitian direction of rank at most two whose two nonzero eigenvalues have opposite signs is an entropy-concavity direction, with arbitrary orientation. This follows from a separate exterior-square calculation.

**Not proved:** full Lyons entropy concavity, general rank-two indefinite directions in dimensions at least four, or positivity of the full exterior-square coefficient operator in all dimensions. Historical novelty of the restricted results has not been established. They are derivations from this session, beyond the rank-one statement in the specific Gu report checked below.

## 1. Notation and established inputs

For a positive Hermitian contraction $K$ on a finite coordinate set $E$, let $P_K$ be the DPP with inclusion probabilities

$$
P_K(T\subseteq X)=\det K_T.
$$

Write $p_S(K)=P_K(X=S)$ and

$$
H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq E}p_S(K)\log p_S(K).
$$

All logarithms are natural. Work first with $0<K<I$, so all atoms are strictly positive. The usual continuity convention $0\log0=0$ will give the boundary cases.

For a real set function $f$, write

$$
F_f(K):=\mathbb E_{P_K} f(X).
$$

This is a real polynomial in the real coordinates of a Hermitian matrix: expanding $f$ in its Boolean Möbius coefficients gives a linear combination of principal determinants.

The one non-elementary input is the established DPP stochastic-domination theorem:

$$
0\le C_1\le C_2\le I
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_{C_1}\preceq_{\rm st}P_{C_2}.
\tag{SD}
$$

For the exact statement, see Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures* (2014), Theorem 2.9, [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707). The same source gives the L-ensemble atom formula in equation (2.12), and states the entropy conjecture as Conjecture 2.6. These are established inputs, not new results of this report.

### Lemma 1: positive gradient of an increasing determinantal expectation

If $g:2^R\to\mathbb R$ is increasing and $0<C<I$, then the Hermitian gradient

$$
Q_g(C):=\nabla F_g(C)
$$

is positive semidefinite, where the gradient is defined by

$$
D F_g(C)[B]=\operatorname{tr}(Q_g(C)B)
$$

for Hermitian $B$.

**Proof.** For any vector $v$, $C+s vv^*$ remains a positive contraction for all sufficiently small $s\ge0$. By (SD), $F_g(C+s vv^*)\ge F_g(C)$. Differentiating at $s=0$ gives $v^*Q_g(C)v\ge0$. This holds for every $v$. No entrywise monotonicity assumption on $C$ is used. ∎

## 2. Exact block identity for an arbitrary set function

Distinguish a coordinate $i$, write $R=E\setminus\{i\}$, and put $i$ first in the matrix order:

$$
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&b^*\\b&C\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For a fixed real set function $f$, define functions on $2^R$ by

$$
f_0(T)=f(T),\qquad g(T)=f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T).
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{F_f(K)=F_{f_0}(C)+aF_g(C)-b^*Q_g(C)b.}
\tag{1}
$$

Here $f$ is fixed when taking any matrix derivative.

**Proof from conditional atoms.** Set

$$
N_T=C-I_{R\setminus T},\qquad r_T=P_C(X=T).
$$

The signed determinant atom formula gives

$$
r_T=(-1)^{|R\setminus T|}\det N_T>0.
$$

Consequently $N_T$ is invertible, though it need not be positive definite. The Schur-complement determinant identity gives

$$
p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K)
=r_T\big(a-b^*N_T^{-1}b\big).
\tag{2}
$$

The marginal law on $R$ is $P_C$; hence $p_T(K)=r_T-p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K)$. It follows that

$$
F_f(K)=\sum_T r_Tf_0(T)
+\sum_T r_Tg(T)\big(a-b^*N_T^{-1}b\big).
$$

On the other hand, differentiating the signed determinant for $r_T$ shows

$$
Q_g(C)=\sum_{T\subseteq R}r_Tg(T)N_T^{-1}.
\tag{3}
$$

Substitution yields (1). This proves the identity in the interior; both sides are polynomial expressions when the gradient is defined by $F_g$, so the identity extends by continuity. ∎

### Lemma 2: row/column concavity of supermodular expectations

Suppose $f$ is supermodular, meaning that all discrete second differences satisfy

$$
f(T\cup\{i,j\})+f(T)-f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T\cup\{j\})\ge0.
$$

Then $g=\Delta_i f$ is increasing. By Lemma 1, $Q_g(C)\ge0$. For every Hermitian direction

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&v^*\\v&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R,
\tag{4}
$$

equation (1) gives the sign-controlled formula

$$
\boxed{D^2F_f(K)[A,A]=-2v^*Q_g(C)v\le0.}
\tag{5}
$$

In fact, for fixed $C$, (1) is an affine function of $a$ plus a negative-semidefinite quadratic form in $b$, so $F_f$ is jointly concave in all real coordinates of $(a,b)$. This statement is valid for every supermodular $f$, not only the logarithm of a DPP atom law.

## 3. Application to the entropy Hessian

Fix an interior base kernel $K$, and **freeze**

$$
f(S)=-\log p_S(K).
$$

This $f$ is supermodular. Indeed, set $L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0$. The L-ensemble formula gives

$$
p_S(K)=\det(I-K)\det L_S.
$$

For distinct $i,j\notin T$, the positive-definite Schur complement of $L_T$ implies

$$
\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}\det L_T
\le \det L_{T\cup\{i\}}\det L_{T\cup\{j\}}.
$$

Taking logarithms proves the asserted supermodularity of $-\log p_S(K)$. This is the usual Hadamard–Fischer inequality; it can also be verified directly as nonnegativity of the squared off-diagonal modulus in the $2\times2$ Schur complement.

Along any affine kernel path $K+tA$, differentiation of entropy and normalization yields

$$
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}
+D^2F_f(K)[A,A].
\tag{6}
$$

For a direction of the form (4), equations (5) and (6) therefore give the proved sum-of-squares representation

$$
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}
-2\|Q_g(C)^{1/2}v\|^2\le0.
}
\tag{7}
$$

The new sign content is $Q_g(C)\ge0$, established through increasing differences and (SD). In particular, the second term of the general entropy Hessian has genuinely been controlled on this family of directions.

An explicit, usable matrix inequality embedded in this proof is

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{T\subseteq R}P_C(T)
\log\frac{p_T(K)}{p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K)}
\big(C-I_{R\setminus T}\big)^{-1}\ge0.
}
\tag{8}
$$

The matrices inside this sum can be indefinite. Positivity is a consequence of the gradient argument, not a termwise assertion.

### Theorem: finite entropy concavity on a row/column slice

Let $0\le K_0,K_1\le I$ be Hermitian, and suppose their principal submatrices on $E\setminus\{i\}$ coincide. Then, for every $t\in[0,1]$,

$$
H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1).
\tag{9}
$$

**Proof.** For strict contractions, (7) holds at every point of the segment, with the appropriate frozen $f$ at that point, and implies (9). For possibly singular endpoints, apply the strict result to

$$
K_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1/2.
$$

Their complementary blocks still coincide. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and use continuity of the finite atom law and of entropy. ∎

This includes changing many off-diagonal entries at once, provided they share one endpoint. It also allows the corresponding diagonal entry to change simultaneously. The restriction is made in the actual point-process coordinate basis; an arbitrary unitary rotation cannot be applied to it because the measured subset entropy is not unitarily invariant.

## 4. Additional dimension-three exterior-square lemma

This section proves a different useful restricted case and clarifies a possible generalization.

Let $f:2^{[3]}\to\mathbb R$ be supermodular, and denote its Boolean Möbius coefficients by $c_T$. Then

$$
F_f(K)=c_\varnothing+\sum_i c_iK_{ii}
+\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\det K_{ij}+c_{123}\det K.
$$

In the ordered wedge basis $(12,13,23)$, define

$$
\mathcal C(K)=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{33}&-K_{23}&K_{13}\\
-K_{32}&K_{22}&-K_{12}\\
K_{31}&-K_{21}&K_{11}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is a unitary permutation/sign conjugate of $K^T$. Thus $0\le\mathcal C(K)\le I$ whenever $0\le K\le I$. Equivalently, for invertible $K$,

$$
\mathcal C(K)=\det(K)\,\bigwedge{}^2(K^{-1}).
$$

The determinant second derivative gives

$$
D^2F_f(K)[A,A]=2\operatorname{tr}\left(T_f(K)\,\bigwedge{}^2A\right),
\tag{10}
$$

where

$$
T_f(K)=\operatorname{diag}(c_{12},c_{13},c_{23})+c_{123}\mathcal C(K).
\tag{11}
$$

Supermodularity gives, separately for each pair,

$$
c_{ij}\ge0,\qquad c_{ij}+c_{123}\ge0.
$$

If $c_{123}\ge0$, (11) is a sum of PSD matrices. If $c_{123}<0$, rewrite it as

$$
T_f(K)=\operatorname{diag}(c_{12}+c_{123},c_{13}+c_{123},c_{23}+c_{123})
+(-c_{123})(I-\mathcal C(K)),
$$

again a sum of PSD matrices. Therefore

$$
\boxed{T_f(K)\ge0\quad(n=3).}
\tag{12}
$$

If the eigenvalues of a Hermitian $A$ are $\lambda,-\mu,0$, with $\lambda,\mu\ge0$, then the eigenvalues of $\bigwedge^2A$ are $-\lambda\mu,0,0$. Hence (10) is nonpositive. Freezing $f=-\log p_K$ and using (6) proves $D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0$. Integration and the same regularization argument give entropy concavity along every feasible affine segment with such a direction. Rank-one cases have zero exterior square and are included, but are already known.

The full $3\times3$ conjecture is **not** proved by (12): a general Hermitian $A$ has an exterior square with both positive and negative eigenvalues. Moreover, rank-two semidefinite $A$ produces a nonnegative rather than nonpositive second term, so the Fisher term must then be used quantitatively.

## 5. Exact obstruction to discarding the second Hessian term

Take

$$
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=I_2.
$$

The eigenvalues of $K$ are $1/4,3/4$, so this is an interior point. In the atom order $00,01,10,11$,

$$
p=(3,5,5,3)/16,\qquad p'=(-1,0,0,1),\qquad p''=(2,-2,-2,2).
$$

Consequently

$$
-\sum_Sp_S''\log p_S=4\log(5/3)>0.
\tag{13}
$$

The Fisher term is $32/3$, and

$$
H''=-32/3+4\log(5/3)<0.
$$

Thus the proposed universal inequality $-\sum p''\log p\le0$ is false, even for a two-point real kernel and a positive-definite direction. This is an obstruction to a proof strategy, not a counterexample to Lyons's conjecture. The row/column theorem avoids this obstruction by a genuine directional restriction.

Before finding the row/column proof, I tested the stronger exterior-operator hypothesis

$$
T_f(K)\ge0\quad\text{for every supermodular }f\text{ and }0\le K\le I
\tag{14}
$$

in higher dimensions. Here $T_f$ is the operator characterized by (10), equivalently in the interior by

$$
T_f(K)=\sum_S p_S(K)f(S)\,\bigwedge{}^2\big(K-I_{E\setminus S}\big)^{-1}.
$$

Pilot calculations found no negative eigenvalue for the entropy choice $f=-\log p_K$ in 600 random kernels per dimension $3,4,5,6$ (seed `201010`). Linear programs over bounded supermodular $f$ also found no negative pairing with a sampled decomposable wedge in 200 examples per dimension $3,4,5,6$ (seed `10100`), or with a sampled arbitrary wedge vector in 1,000 examples per dimension $4,5,6$ (seed `100100`). Each LP minimized the proposed curvature over $f(S)\in[-1,1]$, subject to all elementary supermodularity inequalities. These pilots were exploratory; no exact or interval certificate was obtained and no higher-dimensional assertion is made. Their absence of counterexamples motivated the proved $n=3$ case only.

The row/column argument proves positivity of the blocks of this exterior operator indexed by pairs sharing a fixed coordinate. Positivity of those overlapping blocks is insufficient by itself to prove positivity of the entire operator. Even (14), if proved, would immediately control only directions whose exterior square is negative semidefinite; it would not settle the full entropy Hessian.
