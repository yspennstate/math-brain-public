## Result and status

For positive Hermitian contractions $K,L\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$, put

$$
 A=(K+L)/2,\qquad B=(K-L)/2,\qquad
 M(K,L)=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The assigned auxiliary statement is

$$
\tag{BS} H_{\rm DPP}(M(K,L))\ge H_{\rm DPP}(K)+H_{\rm DPP}(L).
$$

**The general statement remains OPEN in this report.** No full proof and no
counterexample to (BS), or to Lyons's conjecture, is claimed. The substantive
completed results are:

1. **PROVED:** (BS) for any two complex Hermitian contractions of rank at most
   one, in arbitrary dimension, including mixed eigenvalues and boundary cases.
2. **PROVED:** the corresponding corank-at-most-one class, by complementation.
3. **PROVED:** equality in (BS) whenever $L=DKD$, where $D$ is an arbitrary
   diagonal matrix with entries $+1,-1$. In fact, there is a separate-input
   sign-conjugation symmetry of the entire output law up to permutation.
4. **DISPROVED:** the tempting stronger assertion that entropy increases within
   every fixed vector of sitewise total occupations. A four-site pair of
   complementary rank-two projections gives an exact negative conditional
   entropy difference. Its **unconditional (BS) difference is positive**.
5. **PROVED representation:** (BS) is exactly a measurement-coherence increase
   for a specific fermionic unitary acting on a product of gauge-invariant
   Gaussian states. This identifies, but does not establish, the needed operator
   inequality. A two-site phase example rules out a state-independent classical
   channel acting only on the measured input laws.

All logarithms are natural. Results here are derived within this run. No claim
of bibliographic novelty is made for the restricted theorems.

## Why (BS) would settle the target

The two output blocks of the DPP with kernel $M(K,L)$ both have kernel $A$.
Classical subadditivity gives

$$
 H_{\rm DPP}(M(K,L))\le 2H_{\rm DPP}(A).
$$

Consequently (BS) implies midpoint concavity of $H_{\rm DPP}$. Continuity of
the finite list of atom probabilities and of $-x\log x$, including at zero,
then upgrades midpoint concavity to full concavity by dyadic approximation.
However, (BS) itself is stronger than midpoint concavity: if $Y_1,Y_2$ are
the two output blocks, its exact equivalent is

$$
\tag{MI}
 2H_{\rm DPP}(A)-H_{\rm DPP}(K)-H_{\rm DPP}(L)
 \ge I(Y_1;Y_2).
$$

Thus a negative example for (BS), if found, would not automatically contradict
Lyons's inequality. This distinction is essential for the sector counterexample
below as well.

## 1. Quantum/channel representation and its exact missing step

For a contraction $K$, let $\rho_K$ be its gauge-invariant fermionic Gaussian
state on the occupation Fock space. One may construct it by diagonalizing $K$:
its natural orbitals have independent Bernoulli occupation probabilities equal
to the eigenvalues. Wick's determinant rule then gives

$$
 \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_K\prod_{i\in S}n_i)=\det K_S.
$$

Accordingly, complete occupation-basis dephasing $\Delta_n$ has diagonal law

$$
 \Delta_n(\rho_K)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}p_K(S)|S\rangle\langle S|,
 \qquad S(\Delta_n\rho_K)=H_{\rm DPP}(K).
$$

Here $S$ denotes von Neumann entropy. In the interior one can also write

$$
 \rho_K=\det(I-K)\exp\!\left(
 \sum_{ij}\big[\log(K(I-K)^{-1})\big]_{ij}a_i^\dagger a_j\right),
$$

with the covariance convention chosen consistently with Wick's rule. Boundary
states follow by spectral construction or continuity. The spectral entropy is

$$
 S(\rho_K)=\operatorname{tr}h(K),\qquad
 h(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
$$

Let $W=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&I\\I&-I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and let $U=\Gamma(W)$ be its fermionic second quantization. The input is

$$
 R=\rho_K\otimes\rho_L=\rho_{K\oplus L},\qquad
 \sigma=URU^*=\rho_{M(K,L)}.
$$

The ordinary product description is valid here with a fixed block ordering of
the modes; both input states are even. Unitary invariance gives

$$
 S(\sigma)=S(R)=S(\rho_K)+S(\rho_L).
$$

Define relative entropy of measurement coherence by

$$
 C_\Delta(\rho)=S(\Delta\rho)-S(\rho)
                =D(\rho\Vert\Delta\rho).
$$

Then the exact missing operator inequality is

$$
\tag{COH}
 C_{\Delta_{2n}}\!\left(U(\rho_K\otimes\rho_L)U^*\right)
 \ge C_{\Delta_n}(\rho_K)+C_{\Delta_n}(\rho_L).
$$

There is no missing algebra between (COH) and (BS): subtracting their identical
von Neumann entropy terms proves their equivalence. The unproved point is the
sign of this coherence change for **all** Gaussian product inputs.

Dephasing alone only gives $S(\Delta\sigma)\ge S(\sigma)$, whose right-hand
side is $\operatorname{tr}h(K)+\operatorname{tr}h(L)$. This is weaker than the
required input measurement entropy. The unitary is not an incoherent operation
in the occupation basis, so standard coherence monotonicity does not supply
(COH), in either the needed direction or an equivalent form.

### An exact obstruction to a classical measured-input channel

Take $n=2$, $u=(1,1)/\sqrt2$, $v=(1,i)/\sqrt2$, and $P=uu^*$, $Q=vv^*$.
Both DPP laws are uniform on the two singleton sets. Thus the measured input
law is the same uniform distribution on four cross-block configurations for
the pairs $(P,P)$ and $(P,Q)$.

For $(P,P)$, $M=P\oplus P$, so the output remains uniform on those four
configurations. For $(P,Q)$, the two configurations occupying both copies of
one site each have probability $1/4$, and the four configurations occupying
two different sites each have probability $1/8$. These output laws differ,
despite identical measured input laws.

Therefore no state-independent stochastic map on the measured input
configuration space can represent all these output laws. In particular, one
cannot simply take the squared matrix elements of $U$ and invoke a doubly
stochastic entropy increase. That squared-element channel describes dephasing
the input *before* applying $U$, which changes the experiment.

## 2. Exact theorem for arbitrary rank-at-most-one contractions

**Theorem.** For unit vectors $u,v\in\mathbb C^n$ and

$$
 K=\alpha uu^*,\quad L=\beta vv^*,\quad 0\le\alpha,\beta\le1,
$$

(BS) holds. Zero rank is included by setting the corresponding scalar to zero.

**Proof.** Set $a_i=|u_i|^2$, $b_i=|v_i|^2$. The first measured input is empty
with probability $1-\alpha$ and otherwise is the singleton $i$ with
probability $a_i$; likewise for the second. The beam splitter preserves total
particle count $N$, whose common input and output distribution is

$$
 \Pr(N=0)=(1-\alpha)(1-\beta),\quad
 \Pr(N=1)=c:=\alpha(1-\beta)+(1-\alpha)\beta,\quad
 \Pr(N=2)=\alpha\beta.
$$

It suffices to compare conditional entropies given $N$, because the entropy
of $N$ cancels.

**The $N=0$ sector.** Both entropies are zero.

**The $N=1$ sector.** If $c>0$, let

$$
 w=\frac{\alpha(1-\beta)}{c}.
$$

The measured input conditional entropy is

$$
 H_{\rm in}(\cdot\mid N=1)=h(w)+wH(a)+(1-w)H(b).
$$

At either output port, site $i$ has conditional probability

$$
 \frac{wa_i+(1-w)b_i}{2}.
$$

This follows by mixing the two incoherent one-particle alternatives; there is
no off-diagonal coherence between the two input number sectors. Therefore

$$
 H_{\rm out}(\cdot\mid N=1)=\log2+H(wa+(1-w)b).
$$

Classical entropy concavity and $h(w)\le\log2$ imply the desired comparison.
When $c=0$, this sector has zero weight and is omitted.

**The $N=2$ sector.** If it has positive weight, its quantum input is the
product of one particle in orbital $u$ and one in orbital $v$. The occupied
output orbitals are the orthonormal vectors

$$
 f=2^{-1/2}(u,u),\qquad g=2^{-1/2}(v,-v).
$$

The rank-two projection DPP atom at two modes $x,y$ is

$$
 |f_xg_y-f_yg_x|^2.
$$

Condition additionally on the unordered pair of physical sites, including
possible equality. This coarse variable has the same law before and after the
beam splitter: it is the vector of total occupations of the physical sites.

For the collision at site $i$, the probability is $a_i b_i$, and there is
one configuration before and after; its conditional entropy is zero.

For $i<j$, put

$$
 x=u_iv_j,\quad y=u_jv_i,\quad r=|x|^2+|y|^2=a_ib_j+a_jb_i.
$$

When $r>0$, the input conditional probabilities are $|x|^2/r,|y|^2/r$, so
its conditional entropy is $h(t)$, where $t=|x|^2/r$. The four output atoms
are

$$
 \frac{|x-y|^2}{4r},\quad\frac{|x-y|^2}{4r},\quad
 \frac{|x+y|^2}{4r},\quad\frac{|x+y|^2}{4r}.
$$

Define $s=|x+y|^2/(2r)$. Since

$$
 |x+y|^2+|x-y|^2=2r,
$$

this is a probability distribution with entropy $\log2+h(s)$. Hence

$$
 H_{\rm out}(\cdot\mid\{i,j\},N=2)
 -H_{\rm in}(\cdot\mid\{i,j\},N=2)
 =\log2+h(s)-h(t)\ge0.
$$

Zero-weight pairs are omitted. Averaging proves the $N=2$ comparison and then
the theorem. The argument applies to arbitrary complex phases. $\square$

For completeness, the proof provides the exact nonnegative gap formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
 H_{\rm DPP}(M)-H_{\rm DPP}(K)-H_{\rm DPP}(L)
 ={}&c\,[\log2-h(w)+H(wa+(1-w)b)-wH(a)-(1-w)H(b)]\\
 &+\alpha\beta\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}
       [\log2+h(s_{ij})-h(t_{ij})].
\end{aligned}
$$

The bracket on the first line contains the nonnegative Jensen entropy gap.
This proof does not make the invalid inference that an arbitrary spectral
mixture of projection-pair inequalities preserves (BS): instead it explicitly
retains the shared total-count register, and analyzes the overlapping
one-particle alternatives.

**Corollary.** (BS) also holds when $\operatorname{rank}(I-K)\le1$ and
$\operatorname{rank}(I-L)\le1$. Indeed,

$$
 M(I-K,I-L)=I_{2n}-M(K,L),\qquad H_{\rm DPP}(I-J)=H_{\rm DPP}(J),
$$

because complementation of the random subset is a bijection.

## 3. Sign-conjugate equality, valid at every rank

Let $D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)$, with $d_i\in\{-1,1\}$, and let $T_D$
be the $2n$-mode permutation that exchanges the two output copies of site
$i$ precisely when $d_i=-1$. Direct entrywise calculation gives, for any
two matrices $K,L$,

$$
\tag{SIGN}
 T_D M(K,DLD)T_D^*=M(K,L).
$$

One can see this by separating the two input contributions:

$$
 M(K,L)=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}K&K\\K&K\end{pmatrix}
       +\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}L&-L\\-L&L\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Local exchanges leave the first contribution fixed and remove both signs from
the second contribution involving $DLD$. DPP entropy is invariant under
coordinate permutation and diagonal-unitary conjugation. In particular,
putting $L=K$ in (SIGN) yields

$$
 T_D M(K,DKD)T_D^*=K\oplus K,
$$

and hence

$$
 H_{\rm DPP}(M(K,DKD))=2H_{\rm DPP}(K)
 =H_{\rm DPP}(K)+H_{\rm DPP}(DKD).
$$

This explains the nonidentical projection pairs with numerically zero gaps in
the bounded optimization. The invariant uses signs, not arbitrary independent
complex phases; the two-site phase example above demonstrates that difference.

All proved classes tensorize across a common coordinate block decomposition,
because both input DPPs and the beam-splitter output then factor across blocks.

## 4. Exact four-site failure of sectorwise entropy monotonicity

The rank-one proof worked conditionally on the vector of sitewise total
occupations. That comparison fails for higher rank, even for pure Gaussian
inputs. The following certificate uses exact algebra.

Let

$$
 C=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&1&1&1\\
 1&0&-i&i\\
 1&i&0&-i\\
 1&-i&i&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P=\tfrac12(I+C/\sqrt3),\qquad Q=I-P.
$$

The identities $C=C^*$, $C^2=3I$, and $\operatorname{tr}C=0$ show that
$P,Q$ are complementary rank-two projections. Their diagonals are $1/2$
and the squared modulus of every off-diagonal entry is $1/12$. Thus every
two-element principal minor is $1/4-1/12=1/6$; each input is uniform on the
six two-subsets, and

$$
 H_{\rm DPP}(P)+H_{\rm DPP}(Q)=2\log6.
$$

These are the projection kernels associated with a complex tetrahedral tight
frame. The matrix above, not any numerical frame reconstruction, is the exact
definition used by the certificate.

The output is

$$
 M(P,Q)=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}I&C/\sqrt3\\C/\sqrt3&I\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Put $V=C/\sqrt3$, a Hermitian unitary. An orthonormal frame for this rank-four
projection is $2^{-1/2}(I,V)^T$. If $S$ denotes occupied top sites and $T$
occupied bottom sites, with $|S|+|T|=4$, expansion against the identity rows
therefore gives the exact output atom formula

$$
\tag{ATOM}
 p(S,T)=\frac1{16}|\det V_{T,S^c}|^2
       =\frac{|\det C_{T,S^c}|^2}{16\,3^{|T|}}.
$$

Let $E$ be the event that each of the four physical sites has total
occupation one. Equivalently $T=S^c$. This coarse event is preserved by the
local beam splitter. Its input probability is

$$
 \Pr(E)=6(1/6)^2=1/6.
$$

Conditioned on $E$, the input is uniform over its six allowed weight-two
assignments, so its measured entropy is $\log6$. Formula (ATOM) gives the
conditional output law:

| Conditional probability | Number of configurations |
|---:|---:|
| $3/8$ | 2 |
| $1/24$ | 6 |
| $0$ | 8 |

The two largest atoms are the all-top and all-bottom assignments. Thus

$$
 H_{\rm out}(\cdot\mid E)=\log8-\tfrac12\log3,
$$

and the conditional entropy difference is

$$
 \log8-\tfrac12\log3-\log6
 =\log\!\frac4{3\sqrt3}
 =2\log2-\tfrac32\log3<0.
$$

The last sign is certified by the integer comparison $16<27$. In an
interleaved spin description this is the four-spin, weight-two uniform Dicke
state rotated by the local balanced transform. Formula (ATOM) avoids any
fermionic basis-sign ambiguity in that interpretation.

### This is not a counterexample to (BS)

The full output atoms have the following exact multiplicities among its 70
potential rank-four atoms:

| Output probability | Multiplicity |
|---:|---:|
| $0$ | 8 |
| $1/16$ | 2 |
| $1/48$ | 24 |
| $1/144$ | 30 |
| $1/36$ | 6 |

They sum to one. Their entropy is

$$
 H_{\rm DPP}(M(P,Q))=\tfrac{11}{3}\log2+\tfrac54\log3,
$$

so the full auxiliary gap is

$$
 \tfrac53\log2-\tfrac34\log3
 =0.331286084432\ldots>0.
$$

For an exact sign certificate, multiply by 12 and compare
$2^{20}=1048576>3^9=19683$. The positive contributions from other occupation
sectors more than compensate for the negative contribution of $E$.

The conclusion is precisely that the *sectorwise* proof route is false.
Neither (BS) nor Lyons's conjecture is refuted by this example.
