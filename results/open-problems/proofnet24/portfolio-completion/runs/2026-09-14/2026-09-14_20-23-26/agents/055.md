## 1. Results and their scope

Let $F(G)=\inf_f\psi(f)$ use Aldous–Fill's signed source-to-stationary flows, with capacity $c_{\{x,y\}}=\pi_xp_{xy}$ counted once per undirected edge. Define

\[
B(G)=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\ne V}
\frac{2\pi(S)\pi(S^c)}{Q(S,S^c)},
\qquad
Q(S,S^c)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\delta S}c_{\{x,y\}}.
\tag{1}
\]

For simple random walk on $K_{a,b}$, $a,b\ge1$, the following are proved below.

1. **Exact flow optimum, with explicit primal and dual certificates:**
   \[
   F(K_{a,b})=3-\frac1a-\frac1b.
   \tag{2}
   \]
2. **Exact cut optimum for all part sizes:** if $\min(a,b)=1$, then $B=2-1/\max(a,b)$. If $g=\gcd(a,b)>1$, then $B=2$. If $a,b\ge2$ are coprime, let $(r,s)$ be the unique pair with
   \[
   1\le r<a,\quad 1\le s<b,\quad br-as=1.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   B(K_{a,b})=
   2-\frac{1}{ab(br+as-2rs)}.
   \tag{3}
   \]
   In this coprime case the maximizing cut counts are exactly $(r,s)$ and $(a-r,b-s)$. Section 5 proves this using a periodic quadratic inequality, rather than assuming the smallest nonzero numerator wins the ratio.
3. **Series-parallel obstruction with a limiting constant:**
   \[
   B(K_{2,m})=
   \begin{cases}
   2,&m\text{ even},\\
   2-\dfrac{1}{2m^2},&m\text{ odd},
   \end{cases}
   \qquad
   \sup_{m\ge1}\frac{F(K_{2,m})}{B(K_{2,m})}=\frac54.
   \tag{4}
   \]
   The supremum is approached and is not attained. Every $K_{2,m}$ is series-parallel and has no $K_4$ minor. Any bound $F\le C\tau_2$ valid on this class must have $C\ge5/2$.
4. **Balanced family and exact family suprema:** for $m\ge2$,
   \[
   F(K_{m,m})=3-\frac2m,\quad B(K_{m,m})=2,
   \quad \tau_2=1,\quad\Delta=2,
   \]
   \[
   \frac FB=\frac{F}{\tau_2\Delta}=\frac32-\frac1m.
   \tag{5}
   \]
   Over all complete bipartite graphs, $\sup F/B=3/2$; every finite instance has $F/B<3/2$. Over complete bipartite graphs with at least three vertices, $\sup F/(\tau_2\Delta)=3/2$. Thus even the restriction to bipartite diameter-two graphs forces a diameter-inequality constant at least $3/2$.
5. Uniform lazification divides $F,B,\tau_2$ by the same move probability. Half laziness doubles all three and leaves both ratios unchanged.

These are constant lower bounds and exact special-family calculations. **They neither prove nor refute Aldous–Fill's universal diameter replacement.** The unrestricted inequality already requires constant at least 2 from complete graphs, as established in the source memo; the new bipartite-family lower bound $3/2$ is not an improvement of that unrestricted lower bound.

## 3. Exact product-demand formulation and duality

Fix a finite connected undirected graph with at least two vertices and positive capacities $c_e=\pi_xp_{xy}=\pi_yp_{yx}$ on its nonloop edges. Orient each undirected edge once, solely for notation. Self-loops carry zero antisymmetric flow and can be omitted.

A source flow satisfies

\[
\operatorname{div}f^{(x)}(v)=\mathbf1_{\{v=x\}}-\pi_v,
\qquad
\psi(f)=\max_e\frac{\sum_x\pi_x|f_e^{(x)}|}{c_e}.
\tag{6}
\]

The equivalent path-routing problem has demand $\pi_x\pi_y$ for **each ordered** pair $x\ne y$, and sums all path loads without canceling opposite directions on an undirected edge. An equivalent unordered convention assigns demand $2\pi_x\pi_y$ to each unordered pair. Assigning only $\pi_x\pi_y$ per unordered pair would halve both $F$ and $B$, although it would preserve $F/B$.

Here is a proof that source-flow aggregation does not create an unnoticed relaxation. Given an ordered-pair routing, aggregate the routed paths with a fixed source $x$ and divide by $\pi_x$. The result satisfies (6), and the triangle inequality shows that its weighted absolute load is at most the routing's load. Conversely, multiply a source flow by $\pi_x$, orient every edge according to its sign, and remove directed cycles. This does not increase any absolute edge flow and leaves divergence unchanged. The resulting acyclic nonnegative flow has its only positive-divergence vertex at $x$, with sink demand $\pi_x\pi_y$ at each $y\ne x$. The usual finite path decomposition, obtained by following a positive-flow path and subtracting the minimum residual flow or unmet sink demand, routes exactly those demands. Thus the two optimal congestions coincide.

For completeness, write the finite path LP with $\rho\ge0$ and $z_{xy,p}\ge0$ for every simple $x$-to-$y$ path:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{minimize }&\rho,\\
\sum_{p:x\to y}z_{xy,p}&=\pi_x\pi_y\quad(x\ne y),\\
\sum_{x\ne y}\sum_{p\ni e}z_{xy,p}&\le\rho c_e\quad(e\in E).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

It is feasible and bounded below. Introducing nonnegative edge dual variables $\ell_e$ gives $\sum_ec_e\ell_e\le1$. The demand dual variable for $x,y$ is at most the length of every $x$-to-$y$ path, so its optimal value is the shortest-path distance $d_\ell(x,y)$. Finite linear-programming duality therefore yields

\[
F=\max_{\ell\ge0,\ \sum_ec_e\ell_e\le1}
\Phi(\ell),
\qquad
\Phi(\ell)=\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_\ell(x,y).
\tag{8}
\]

The objective is positive for some lengths, so the normalization can be equality. All capacities are positive, making the length simplex compact; the objective is a continuous minimum-of-finitely-many-linear-functions sum. Consequently this maximum is attained. Zero lengths and the resulting pseudometrics are allowed. In particular, a normalized cut pseudometric is legitimate; calling every dual distance a strictly positive metric would unnecessarily exclude boundary solutions.

One may check weak duality directly in the source formulation: for a fixed $x$, sum its divergence against the potential $-d_\ell(x,\cdot)$. Since distance changes by at most $\ell_e$ across an edge,

\[
\sum_y\pi_y d_\ell(x,y)
\le\sum_e\ell_e|f_e^{(x)}|.
\]

Multiply by $\pi_x$, sum over sources, and use (6). This independently verifies the factor of two implicit in (8).

The cut demand crossing $S$ is $2\pi(S)\pi(S^c)$. Equivalently, summing the required absolute net crossing separately over all source flows gives that same expression. Hence $B\le F$. The indicator-function Poincaré inequality gives

\[
\pi(S)\pi(S^c)\le\tau_2Q(S,S^c),
\qquad B\le2\tau_2.
\tag{9}
\]

This valid inequality does not imply $F\le2\tau_2$, because $F/B$ can exceed 1.

## 4. Complete bipartite flow optimum: two attainment proofs

Write $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_a\}$, $B_0=\{b_1,\ldots,b_b\}$; the subscript on $B_0$ separates the vertex part from the cut parameter $B(G)$. Detailed balance for simple random walk gives

\[
\pi(a_i)=\frac1{2a},\quad
\pi(b_j)=\frac1{2b},\quad
c_{a_ib_j}=\frac1{2ab}.
\tag{10}
\]

### 4.1 Dual symmetry and the value

The product $S_a\times S_b$ acts transitively on the edges and preserves $\pi$. For fixed vertices, shortest-path distance is the minimum of linear path-length functions, and is concave in the nonnegative edge-length vector. Thus $\Phi$ in (8) is concave. Averaging a feasible length vector over this finite automorphism group preserves its normalization and does not decrease $\Phi$, by Jensen's inequality and invariance. The averaged vector is constant. Its value is 2 because $\sum_ec_e=1/2$.

Therefore the dual is maximized by $d=2d_G$. Ordered pairs from opposite parts have total probability $1/2$ and graph distance 1. Distinct ordered pairs in $A$ contribute $(a-1)/(2a)$ to the mean graph distance; the corresponding contribution in $B_0$ is $(b-1)/(2b)$. Hence

\[
2\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_G(x,y)
=2\left(\frac12+\frac{a-1}{2a}+\frac{b-1}{2b}\right)
=3-\frac1a-\frac1b.
\tag{11}
\]

This proves exact dual optimality, not merely feasibility of the uniform metric.

### 4.2 Explicit primal certificate

Route every ordered cross-part demand directly. Route each same-part demand equally over all two-edge paths through the opposite part. On an edge $a_ib_j$, the respective total path loads are

\[
\frac1{2ab},\qquad
\frac{a-1}{2a^2b},\qquad
\frac{b-1}{2ab^2}.
\tag{12}
\]

Their sum, divided by $c_e=1/(2ab)$, is (2), uniformly on every edge.

For an entirely explicit certificate in the original signed convention, orient every edge $a_i\to b_j$ and set

\[
f_{a_ib_j}^{(a_r)}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2a-1}{2ab},&i=r,\\
-\dfrac1{2ab},&i\ne r,
\end{cases}
\qquad
f_{a_ib_j}^{(b_s)}=
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{2b-1}{2ab},&j=s,\\
\dfrac1{2ab},&j\ne s.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]

At an $A$-source the outward sum is $(2a-1)/(2a)=1-\pi(a_r)$; at every other $A$-vertex it is $-1/(2a)$, and at every $B_0$-vertex it is $-1/(2b)$. The analogous check applies to a $B_0$-source. Thus these are admissible source flows. Their weighted absolute edge load is

\[
\frac{3a-2}{4a^2b}+\frac{3b-2}{4ab^2}
=\frac{3-1/a-1/b}{2ab}.
\tag{14}
\]

This meets the dual lower bound (11) exactly. It also proves that no cancellation issue in aggregating path demands affects the announced optimum. Formulas (10)–(14) include $a=1$ or $b=1$.

## 5. Exact finite cut maximum for every $K_{a,b}$

A cut with $k$ vertices in $A$ and $\ell$ vertices in $B_0$ has

\[
\pi(S)=\frac{k}{2a}+\frac\ell{2b},\qquad
|\delta S|=bk+a\ell-2k\ell.
\]

Put $u=k/a$, $v=\ell/b$ and $D=u+v-2uv$. Excluding $(k,\ell)=(0,0),(a,b)$, one has $D>0$, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
C(k,\ell)
&=\frac{(u+v)(2-u-v)}{u+v-2uv}\\
&=2-\frac{(u-v)^2}{u+v-2uv}\\
&=2-\frac{(bk-a\ell)^2}{ab(bk+a\ell-2k\ell)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular $B\le2$.

### 5.1 Stars and common divisors

If $a=1$, by taking complements it is enough to consider $k=0$, $1\le\ell\le b$. Then $C=2-\ell/b$, with maximum $2-1/b$. Interchange the parts for $b=1$. This proves the star formula, including $K_{1,1}$. For $a=1$, the maximizing count pairs are exactly $(0,1)$ and $(1,b-1)$; swap the coordinates for $b=1$.

For $g=\gcd(a,b)>1$, equation (15) equals 2 precisely when $u=v$. The nontrivial integer solutions are exactly

\[
(k,\ell)=\left(\frac{ja}{g},\frac{jb}{g}\right),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,g-1.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus these, and only these count pairs, attain $B=2$.

### 5.2 Coprime sizes: a periodic quadratic lemma

Define the continuous periodic function

\[
h(t)=\{t\}(1-\{t\}),
\]

where $\{t\}$ is the fractional part. It is even and subadditive:

\[
h(x+y)\le h(x)+h(y),
\qquad h(qx)\le |q|h(x)\quad(q\in\mathbb Z).
\tag{17}
\]

To prove the first assertion, reduce $x,y$ modulo integers to $x,y\in[0,1)$. If $x+y\le1$, subtracting the left side from the right side gives $2xy\ge0$. If $x+y\ge1$, it gives $2(1-x)(1-y)\ge0$. The second assertion follows by induction for nonnegative integers and evenness for negative integers.

Now suppose $a,b\ge2$ are coprime. Let $r\in\{1,\ldots,a-1\}$ be the inverse of $b$ modulo $a$, and put $s=(br-1)/a$. This gives the unique interior solution $br-as=1$. Its complement has difference $-1$.

For an arbitrary nontrivial cut set

\[
q=bk-a\ell\ne0,\qquad
\varepsilon=\frac1{ab},\qquad
H=h(r/a)+h(s/b)>0.
\]

The Bézout identity implies

\[
k\equiv qr\pmod a,\qquad
\ell\equiv qs\pmod b.
\tag{18}
\]

For $u,v\in[0,1]$, the ordinary quadratic $t(1-t)$ agrees with $h(t)$, also at the endpoints. The denominator in (15) therefore obeys the identity and bound

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=h(u)+h(v)+(u-v)^2\\
&=h(qr/a)+h(qs/b)+q^2\varepsilon^2\\
&\le |q|H+q^2\varepsilon^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Consequently the cut's deficit below 2 is bounded by

\[
2-C(k,\ell)=\frac{q^2\varepsilon^2}{D}
\ge\frac{\varepsilon^2}{H/|q|+\varepsilon^2}
\ge\frac{\varepsilon^2}{H+\varepsilon^2}.
\tag{20}
\]

The last inequality is strict whenever $|q|>1$, since $H>0$. For $q=1$, $(k,\ell)=(r,s)$, and both inequalities are equalities; for $q=-1$, the cut is its complement. Thus precisely these count pairs maximize the cut value. Substituting them into (15) gives (3).

This argument resolves the nontrivial optimization issue: a cut with larger $|bk-a\ell|$ cannot compensate by having a larger denominator. Simply minimizing that integer difference without (19)–(20) would not be a proof.

## 6. The series-parallel family $K_{2,m}$

The elementary specialization can also be verified without Bézout arithmetic. A cut contains $k\in\{0,1,2\}$ vertices in the two-vertex part, and $\ell$ in the other part.

For $k=0$, nontriviality requires $1\le\ell\le m$, and (15) gives $C=2-\ell/m\le2-1/m$. For $k=2$, use the complementary cut to get the same bound.

For $k=1$, $u=1/2$, $D=1/2$, and

\[
C(1,\ell)=2-2\left(\frac12-\frac\ell m\right)^2.
\tag{21}
\]

If $m$ is even the unique maximizing $\ell$ is $m/2$, giving 2. If $m$ is odd the two maximizing values are $(m-1)/2,(m+1)/2$, giving $2-1/(2m^2)$. This exceeds $2-1/m$ for every positive odd $m$. Therefore (4) is the exact finite maximum, including all boundary cuts.

Combining with $F=5/2-1/m$, the exact gap is

\[
\frac FB=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac54-\dfrac1{2m},&m\text{ even},\\[4pt]
\dfrac{m(5m-2)}{4m^2-1},&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{22}
\]

For odd $m$, the distance below $5/4$ is

\[
\frac54-\frac FB=\frac{8m-5}{4(4m^2-1)}>0.
\]

Both parity subsequences approach $5/4$. The gap is 1 for $m=1,2$, and exceeds 1 for every $m\ge3$. In particular $m=3$ reproduces agent 001's $F=13/6$, $B=35/18$, gap $39/35$.

With the two $A$-vertices as terminals, $K_{2,m}$ consists of $m$ parallel paths of length two, so it is series-parallel. It has a width-two tree decomposition with bags $\{a_1,a_2,b_j\}$, arranged in a path; this also certifies the absence of a $K_4$ minor. Since $\tau_2=1$ throughout this family,

\[
\sup_m\frac{F(K_{2,m})}{\tau_2}=\frac52,
\qquad
\sup_m\frac{F(K_{2,m})}{\tau_2\Delta}=\frac54.
\tag{23}
\]

Thus a universally valid series-parallel product-demand flow–cut factor must be at least $5/4$, and a universally valid series-parallel $F\le C\tau_2$ factor must be at least $5/2$. No matching upper bound on all series-parallel graphs is proved here.

## 7. Spectral normalization, balanced family, and comparison scope

For $K_{a,b}$, $P$ sends a function on either part to its average on the opposite part. On the two-dimensional space of functions constant on each part, it swaps the two constants, giving eigenvalues $1,-1$. Functions with zero sum on each part are killed, giving eigenvalue 0 with multiplicity $a+b-2$. Hence

\[
\operatorname{spec}(P)=\{1,0^{(a+b-2)},-1\}.
\tag{24}
\]

If $a+b\ge3$, $\lambda_2=0$, so $\tau_2=1$, and the graph diameter is 2. If $a=b=1$, the zero eigenspace is absent: $\tau_2=1/2$, $\Delta=1$, $F=B=1$, and $F/(\tau_2\Delta)=2$.

For $m\ge2$, the balanced family has $B=2$ by (16), and (5) follows. Its stationary distribution is uniform, so the limiting obstruction also applies under a uniform-stationarity restriction.

For clarity, the following constants concern different comparisons.

| Proposed comparison | Scope of the lower bound supplied here | Necessary constant |
|---|---|---|
| $F\le C B$ | All series-parallel graphs, already forced by $K_{2,m}$ | $C\ge5/4$ |
| $F\le C\tau_2$ | All series-parallel graphs, already forced by $K_{2,m}$ | $C\ge5/2$ |
| $F\le C B$ | Complete bipartite graphs | $C\ge3/2$, with supremum exactly $3/2$ |
| $F\le C\tau_2$ | Complete bipartite graphs | $C\ge3$, with supremum exactly 3 |
| $F\le C\tau_2\Delta$ | Bipartite graphs of diameter 2, already forced by $K_{m,m}$ | $C\ge3/2$ |
| $F\le C\tau_2\Delta$ | Unrestricted connected simple graphs | The previously known complete-graph examples already force $C\ge2$ |

There is also an elementary upper bound that makes the flow–cut statement an exact family supremum, rather than only a lower-bound sequence. Assume $a\le b$. The cut consisting of a single $B_0$-vertex gives $B\ge2-1/b$. Consequently

\[
3B-2F\ge3(2-1/b)-2(3-1/a-1/b)
=\frac2a-\frac1b>0.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus $F/B<3/2$ for every finite $K_{a,b}$, while $K_{m,m}$ approaches $3/2$. Similarly, when $a+b\ge3$, $F/(\tau_2\Delta)=F/2<3/2$, with the same limiting sequence.

The balanced examples with $m\ge3$ cannot be used as series-parallel examples. $K_{3,3}$ has a $K_4$ minor with connected branch sets $\{a_1,b_1\}$, $\{a_2,b_2\}$, $\{a_3\}$, $\{b_3\}$, every pair joined by an edge; larger balanced graphs contain $K_{3,3}$. This keeps the two comparison scopes distinct.

## 8. Laziness and continuous time

For $0\le\alpha<1$, let

\[
P_\alpha=\alpha I+(1-\alpha)P.
\]

The stationary law and the nonloop transition graph do not change. Every nonloop capacity is multiplied by $1-\alpha$, whereas the admissible source-flow family depends only on the stationary law. Therefore

\[
F_\alpha=\frac{F}{1-\alpha},\qquad
B_\alpha=\frac{B}{1-\alpha}.
\tag{26}
\]

Eigenvalues are mapped increasingly by $\lambda\mapsto\alpha+(1-\alpha)\lambda$, so

\[
\tau_{2,\alpha}=\frac{\tau_2}{1-\alpha}.
\tag{27}
\]

Both $F/B$ and $F/(\tau_2\Delta)$ are invariant. In particular $\alpha=1/2$ doubles $F,B,\tau_2$ and gives nonnegative spectrum. This removes any possible ambiguity about absolute versus signed spectral gap in the counterexamples without weakening their ratios. The rate-one continuization with generator $P-I$ preserves off-diagonal rates and hence capacities, and its negative generator has nonzero eigenvalues $1-\lambda_i(P)$ for $i\ge2$; its $F,B,\tau_2$ coincide with the original discrete-time values.
