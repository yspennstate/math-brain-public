## Verdict and precise theorem

**PASS.** The all-dimensional star-slice entropy-concavity theorem in Agent 010's Sections 1–3 is mathematically sound. Its sole non-elementary input is a correctly applicable established theorem: Loewner order of positive-contraction kernels implies stochastic domination of their DPP laws. The complex Hermitian gradient has the claimed sign; no real-symmetric restriction is needed. The supplied boundary regularization preserves the slice exactly.

Here is the exact statement validated:

> Let $E$ be a finite coordinate set and fix $i\in E$. If $0\preceq K_0,K_1\preceq I$ are Hermitian and their principal blocks on $R=E\setminus\{i\}$ are equal, then
> \[
> H\bigl((1-t)K_0+tK_1\bigr)
> \ge (1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
> \]

Here $H(K)$ is the Shannon entropy, with natural logarithms, of the subset-valued DPP with inclusion probabilities $\mathbb P_K(U\subseteq X)=\det K_U$.

The difference of the kernels may have rank two and opposite-sign nonzero eigenvalues. This is a genuine enlargement of the directions covered by an atom-affinity argument for rank-one perturbations. It does **not** prove the full Lyons conjecture, or all arbitrarily oriented indefinite rank-two directions. No historical novelty claim is justified by this bounded audit.

The proof below reconstructs the argument from Boolean Möbius expansion and independently verifies it through the conditional binary entropy formula. All algebraic dependencies are supplied; the classical stochastic-domination theorem is explicitly stated and cited rather than reproved.

## 1. Notation and the classical input

For a real set function $f:2^E\to\mathbb R$, define

\[
F_f(K)=\mathbb E_{P_K}f(X).
\]

If $\widehat f(U)=\sum_{V\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U\setminus V|}f(V)$ is its Boolean Möbius coefficient, then

\[
f(S)=\sum_{U\subseteq S}\widehat f(U),
\qquad
F_f(K)=\sum_{U\subseteq E}\widehat f(U)\det K_U.
\tag{1}
\]

The second identity follows by taking the expectation of the first and using the defining inclusion probabilities. Thus $F_f$ is a real polynomial on the real vector space of Hermitian matrices. Empty determinants are one.

For a differentiable real-valued function of a Hermitian matrix $C$, use the Hermitian gradient convention

\[
DF(C)[B]=\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\nabla F(C))B\bigr),
\qquad B=B^*.
\tag{2}
\]

The trace in (2) is real. In particular, for a column vector $w\in\mathbb C^R$,

\[
DF(C)[ww^*]=w^*(\nabla F(C))w.
\]

**Established stochastic-domination theorem.** If $0\preceq C_1\preceq C_2\preceq I$, then

\[
\mathbb E_{P_{C_1}}g\le\mathbb E_{P_{C_2}}g
\tag{SD}
\]

for every increasing real set function $g$. This applies to complex Hermitian positive contractions. See Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures* (2014), Theorem 2.9 and the definition of stochastic domination immediately preceding it: [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707).

Consequently, if $0\prec C\prec I$ and $g$ is increasing, then

\[
Q_g(C):=\nabla F_g(C)\succeq0.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, for each $w$, the kernel $C+sww^*$ is a positive contraction for all sufficiently small $s\ge0$. Equation (SD) implies $F_g(C+sww^*)\ge F_g(C)$. The right derivative at zero is $w^*Q_g(C)w\ge0$. This holds for every complex $w$, proving (3). Negative values of $g$ do not cause a problem: (SD) concerns all bounded increasing real functions, and this finite $g$ is bounded.

## 2. Polynomial block identity from Möbius coefficients

Place $i$ first in the coordinate order and write

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&b^*\\ b&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a\in\mathbb R,\quad b\in\mathbb C^R,\quad C=C^*.
\]

For a **fixed** set function $f$, define $f_0(T)=f(T)$ and

\[
g(T)=f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T),\qquad T\subseteq R.
\]

Its Möbius coefficients satisfy $\widehat g(W)=\widehat f(W\cup\{i\})$. For $W\subseteq R$, the bordered determinant identity is

\[
\det K_{W\cup\{i\}}
=a\det C_W-b_W^*\operatorname{adj}(C_W)b_W.
\tag{4}
\]

For $W=\varnothing$, the last quadratic term is defined as zero, so (4) says that this determinant is $a$. For nonempty $W$, (4) follows from a Schur complement when $C_W$ is invertible and then extends as a polynomial identity. The adjugate is Hermitian when its argument is Hermitian.

Let $J_W:\mathbb C^W\to\mathbb C^R$ be coordinate inclusion. Differentiating (1) for $g$ gives

\[
Q_g(C)=\sum_{\varnothing\ne W\subseteq R}\widehat g(W)
J_W\operatorname{adj}(C_W)J_W^*.
\tag{5}
\]

Split the sum (1) according to whether $i\in U$, and use (4)–(5). The exact result is

\[
\boxed{F_f(K)=F_{f_0}(C)+aF_g(C)-b^*Q_g(C)b.}
\tag{6}
\]

This derivation does not use an inverse or strict positivity. It proves the identity as a polynomial identity, hence also at every boundary kernel.

Now consider precisely a star direction

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&v^*\\v&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \alpha\in\mathbb R,\quad v\in\mathbb C^R.
\tag{7}
\]

The set function $f$, the block $C$, and consequently $g,Q_g(C)$ are fixed in this differentiation. Equation (6) yields

\[
\boxed{D^2F_f(K)[A,A]=-2v^*Q_g(C)v.}
\tag{8}
\]

There is no $\alpha^2$ term and no mixed $\alpha v$ term: the bordered determinant is affine in $a$ and quadratic in $b$, with its quadratic coefficient independent of $a$.

For this chosen coordinate $i$, the sufficient hypothesis is merely that $\Delta_i f=g$ be increasing. Full supermodularity of $f$ implies this hypothesis for every $i$. Under it, (3) and (8) prove that $F_f$ is jointly concave in the real variables $(a,\Re b,\Im b)$, on the feasible slice.

Increasing $g$ does not imply nonnegative Möbius coefficients. Therefore positivity in (5) is not a termwise argument; (SD) is doing essential work.

## 3. Independent exact-atom and curvature check

For a subset $S\subseteq E$, inclusion-exclusion gives the atom polynomial

\[
p_S(K)=\sum_{U\supseteq S}(-1)^{|U\setminus S|}\det K_U.
\tag{9}
\]

Equivalently, writing $I_B$ for the diagonal coordinate projection onto $B$,

\[
p_S(K)=(-1)^{|E\setminus S|}\det(K-I_{E\setminus S}).
\tag{10}
\]

For a star direction, every determinant in (9) is constant if $i\notin U$. If $U=W\cup\{i\}$, its second derivative is, by (4),

\[
\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\det(K+tA)_{W\cup\{i\}}\right|_{t=0}
=-2v_W^*\operatorname{adj}(C_W)v_W.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus every atom is a polynomial of degree at most two along the slice. Its second derivative generally does not vanish. Applying (9) to (11) gives the exact atom curvature without any matrix inverses.

For a second check, assume $0\prec K\prec I$ and define, for $T\subseteq R$,

\[
N_T=C-I_{R\setminus T},\qquad
r_T=P_C(X=T)=(-1)^{|R\setminus T|}\det N_T.
\]

All $r_T$ are positive, as follows for instance from the positive-definite L-ensemble formula in the next section. Hence $N_T$ is invertible. It is Hermitian but usually indefinite. Schur complement in (10) gives

\[
p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K)=r_Tq_T,
\qquad
p_T(K)=r_T(1-q_T),
\qquad
q_T=a-b^*N_T^{-1}b\in(0,1).
\tag{12}
\]

The second equality also follows because the marginal on $R$ is $P_C$. Since $N_T^{-1}$ is Hermitian, along (7)

\[
q_T'=\alpha-2\Re(v^*N_T^{-1}b),
\qquad
q_T''=-2v^*N_T^{-1}v.
\tag{13}
\]

In particular,

\[
p_{T\cup\{i\}}''=-2r_Tv^*N_T^{-1}v,
\qquad
p_T''=2r_Tv^*N_T^{-1}v.
\tag{14}
\]

This verifies both the sign and factor two in the claimed atom curvature, including complex $b,v$. Finally, differentiating the signed determinant defining $r_T$ gives

\[
\boxed{Q_g(C)=\sum_{T\subseteq R}r_Tg(T)N_T^{-1}.}
\tag{15}
\]

There is no missing transpose or conjugation in (15) under convention (2): the differential is $r_T\operatorname{tr}(N_T^{-1}B)$.

## 4. Exact atoms are log-submodular; frozen conditional odds are increasing

Fix an interior kernel $K_*$, and from now on define the fixed function

\[
f(S)=-\log p_S(K_*).
\tag{16}
\]

Set $L=K_*(I-K_*)^{-1}\succ0$. Its positive definiteness follows by spectral calculus, since the scalar transformation is $\lambda\mapsto\lambda/(1-\lambda)$. The exact atom formula is

\[
p_S(K_*)=\det(I-K_*)\det L_S.
\tag{17}
\]

This standard formula is equation (2.12) of [Lyons's primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707). It also proves that all interior atom probabilities are positive.

For distinct $i,j\notin T$, take the Schur complement of $L_T$ in $L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}$, and write its two-by-two matrix as

\[
\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\\bar z&w\end{pmatrix}\succ0.
\]

Then

\[
\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}\det L_{T\cup\{j\}}
-\det L_T\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}
=(\det L_T)^2|z|^2\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]

The same formula holds when $T$ is empty with determinant one. Multiplying (18) by the common positive normalizing factor from (17) gives

\[
p_{T\cup\{i\}}p_{T\cup\{j\}}
\ge p_Tp_{T\cup\{i,j\}}.
\tag{19}
\]

These are **exact atom probabilities**, not merely inclusion probabilities. Taking minus logarithms proves that (16) is supermodular. More explicitly, for $T\subseteq R$,

\[
g(T)=\Delta_i f(T)=\log\frac{p_T(K_*)}{p_{T\cup\{i\}}(K_*)},
\tag{20}
\]

and for $j\in R\setminus T$,

\[
g(T\cup\{j\})-g(T)
=\log\frac{p_{T\cup\{j\}}p_{T\cup\{i\}}}
{p_Tp_{T\cup\{i,j\}}}\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently $g$ is increasing, and (3) applies. The complex case is covered by the modulus square in (18).

Although $g$ was obtained from $K_*$, it is a fixed table of $2^{|R|}$ real numbers when differentiating $F_g$ in (3), (6), or (15). The auxiliary matrix argument of $F_g$ is free to vary; the table $g$ is not recomputed during that derivative. This distinction is essential and is respected in Agent 010's proof.

## 5. Entropy Hessian and the independent conditional-binary formula

For any affine direction through the interior base point, normalization gives $\sum_S p_S'=\sum_Sp_S''=0$. Direct differentiation yields

\[
D^2H(K_*)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S}-\sum_Sp_S''\log p_S.
\tag{22}
\]

With $f$ frozen as in (16), the last term is precisely $D^2F_f(K_*)[A,A]$. Combining (3), (8), and (21) therefore yields, for every star direction (7),

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K_*)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S}-2v^*Q_g(C)v\le0.
}
\tag{23}
\]

To cross-check (23) without the frozen-$f$ notation, use the chain rule for the pair $(X\cap R,1_{i\in X})$ and (12). With $h(q)=-q\log q-(1-q)\log(1-q)$,

\[
H(K)=H(C)+\sum_{T\subseteq R}r_Th(q_T).
\tag{24}
\]

Both $r_T$ and $H(C)$ stay fixed on the star slice. Since

\[
h'(q)=\log\frac{1-q}{q},\qquad
h''(q)=-\frac1{q(1-q)},
\]

equations (13) and (20) give the completely explicit second derivative

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2H}{dt^2}
=-\sum_{T\subseteq R}
\frac{r_T\,[\alpha-2\Re(v^*N_T^{-1}b)]^2}{q_T(1-q_T)}
-2v^*\left[\sum_{T\subseteq R}r_T
\log\frac{1-q_T}{q_T}N_T^{-1}\right]v
\le0.
}
\tag{25}
\]

The bracket is $Q_g(C)\succeq0$ by (15) and (21). Pairing the two atoms in (12) gives

\[
\frac{(p_T')^2}{p_T}+\frac{(p_{T\cup\{i\}}')^2}{p_{T\cup\{i\}}}
=\frac{r_T(q_T')^2}{q_T(1-q_T)},
\]

so the first terms of (23) and (25) agree exactly. Pairing (14) likewise gives the same second term. No inequality is obtained by assigning a common sign to the individual $N_T^{-1}$; only their weighted sum has the required sign.

## 6. Integration and every boundary case

If $K_0,K_1$ are strict positive contractions with the same complementary block, their full segment consists of strict positive contractions and its direction is (7). At each interior point of the segment, apply (23), freezing $f$ at that point. The scalar function of $t$ therefore has nonpositive second derivative on the segment's interior, hence is concave. This proves the theorem for strict kernels.

For arbitrary positive-contraction endpoints, let

\[
K_j^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-2\varepsilon)K_j+\varepsilon I,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\tfrac12,\quad j=0,1.
\tag{26}
\]

Both are strict positive contractions. Their complementary blocks still agree, and their difference is $(1-2\varepsilon)(K_1-K_0)$, still a star direction. Apply the strict inequality. As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, each atom converges by the polynomial formula (9), and finite Shannon entropy converges because $-x\log x$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ with $0\log0=0$. Passing to the limit proves the claimed inequality even when some atoms vanish, the fixed complementary block is singular, or endpoints are projections.

For $n=1$, $R$ is empty and the matrix term is absent; the conclusion is ordinary binary entropy concavity. The proof handles that case with the empty conventions above.

## 7. Exact directional scope and obstructions to overclaiming

The allowed perturbations are exactly those Hermitian $A$ for which $A_{R,R}=0$ for one **original coordinate** $i$. Equivalently, $A=e_iw^*+we_i^*$ for some $w$, choosing $w_i=\alpha/2\in\mathbb R$ in (7). The diagonal entry $a$ and all incident off-diagonal entries may vary affinely together. Entries of $C$, including every other diagonal entry, may not vary.

If $v\ne0$, the two nonzero eigenvalues of (7) are

\[
\frac{\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^2+4\|v\|^2}}2>0,
\qquad
\frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^2+4\|v\|^2}}2<0.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus it is generally an indefinite rank-two direction. If $v=0$, it is a coordinate rank-one direction or zero. A general rank-one perturbation $uu^*$ with several nonzero coordinates is not itself a star direction; its separate concavity theorem is already established by Gu.

For fixed $0\prec C\prec I$, the strict feasible slice can also be described exactly by Schur complements:

\[
b^*C^{-1}b<a<1-b^*(I-C)^{-1}b.
\tag{28}
\]

The proof asserts concavity only on this feasible set and its positive-contraction boundary, not for an unrestricted quadratic family of matrices.

Two exact checks prevent common overextensions:

1. **Star atoms need not be affine.** For
   \[
   K(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&t\\t&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad |t|<1/2,
   \]
   the atom probabilities, ordered as $\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}$, are
   \[
   (1/4-t^2,\ 1/4+t^2,\ 1/4+t^2,\ 1/4-t^2).
   \]
   This is a feasible star path with rank-two direction. Its atom curvatures are $(-2,2,2,-2)$, so the rank-one proof based on $p_S''=0$ cannot establish this case directly. The sign-controlled sum in (23) is an actual additional argument.

2. **Entropy is not invariant under arbitrary unitary changes of basis.** The projection $K=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ produces a deterministic subset and entropy zero. The unitarily conjugate projection
   \[
   \widetilde K=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
   \]
   produces the two singletons with probabilities $(1/2,1/2)$, so its entropy is $\log 2$. Therefore one cannot rotate an arbitrary indefinite rank-two direction into star form and then apply this theorem while preserving the entropy problem.

Concavity on the separate star subspaces also does not imply concavity for their sums: a Hessian quadratic form contains mixed terms between different star subspaces. This audit supplies no bound for those terms. In particular, (23) supplies neither a full positive-semidefinite exterior coefficient operator nor a proof that the Fisher term dominates the remaining curvature in general directions.
