## Result

Let the actual coordinate ground set split as \(E=A\sqcup B\). Let \(a\in\mathbb C^A\) and \(b\in\mathbb C^B\) be unit vectors, extended by zero to \(E\), and put
\[
W=[a]\oplus[b],\qquad P_W=aa^*+bb^*.
\]
For every supermodular real set function \(f\), define
\[
F_f(K)=\mathbb E_{P_K}f(X),\qquad
q_f(K;u,v)=D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*].
\]

**Theorem 1.** If \(u,v\in W^\perp\) and
\[
\min\{\dim\operatorname{span}(u_A,v_A),
       \dim\operatorname{span}(u_B,v_B)\}\le1,
\tag{1}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{q_f(P_W;u,v)\ge0.}
\tag{2}
\]
The theorem holds over the complex numbers in every finite ambient dimension.

In particular, if one of the two disjoint support blocks has size at most two, (1) is automatic for every update plane \(U\subseteq W^\perp\). This gives a genuine six-site compressed rank-two class: take blocks of sizes two and four, with a nonzero rank-one projection on each. The compressed projection and its complement on \(U^\perp\) can both have rank two, so the earlier global rank/corank-one criterion does not apply.

There is a stronger operator statement for that automatic case.

**Theorem 2.** If \(|A|\le2\), the exterior coefficient operator satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\left.T_f(P_W)\right|_{\bigwedge^2 W^\perp}\succeq0,
}
\tag{3}
\]
where restriction means orthogonal compression to the indicated exterior subspace. The full operator on \(\bigwedge^2\mathbb C^E\) is not asserted positive.

The same class gives mixed positivity for structured rank-two contraction compressions and entropy concavity along the corresponding feasible indefinite rank-two segments, including boundary kernels. These statements are proved below.

**Open boundary.** General six-site compressed rank-two positivity, general disjoint-support rank-two positivity when both block projections of \(U\) have rank two, and full Lyons entropy concavity remain unresolved here. No counterexample is asserted. The unretained Agent074 prescribed-basis coupling witness was neither reconstructed nor verified. This report does not use it.

## 1. Inputs, conventions, and a block-degree identity

Write the Boolean Möbius expansion
\[
f(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}c_S,\qquad
F_f(K)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}c_S\det K_S.
\tag{4}
\]
This polynomial defines every boundary derivative used here. Supermodularity means
\[
\Delta_i\Delta_j f(T)
=f(T\cup\{i,j\})+f(T)-f(T\cup\{i\})-f(T\cup\{j\})\ge0
\tag{5}
\]
for distinct \(i,j\notin T\).

The following established or previously proved inputs are used.

* Projection DPPs on nested subspaces admit a coupling by inclusion, and have cardinality equal to subspace dimension. The original source used in Agent019 and independently audited in Agent079 is Russell Lyons, Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures, arXiv:1406.2707v1, equation (2.2), Theorem 2.7, and the preceding Strassen discussion: https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2707v1.
* Agent019 proves \(q_g(C;x,y)\ge0\) for every supermodular \(g\) when \(C\) has rank at most one, in every dimension. Its compression identity, Gram-determinant basis factor, and spectral interpolation were independently audited in Agent079.
* Agent030, Sections 6–7, proves the stronger full-operator result \(T_g(C)\succeq0\) when \(C\) or \(I-C\) has rank at most one. This input is needed only for Theorem 2, not Theorem 1 or the entropy corollary.

For completeness, the operator convention is
\[
\langle u\wedge v,T_f(C)(u\wedge v)\rangle=q_f(C;u,v).
\tag{6}
\]
If \(I,J\) are coordinate pairs contained in \(S\), the contribution of \(\det C_S\) to its matrix entry is
\[
[T_{\det S}(C)]_{I,J}
=(-1)^{\sigma_S(I)+\sigma_S(J)}
\det C_{S\setminus J,S\setminus I}.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(\sigma_S\) is the sum of the positions in the ordered set \(S\); the empty determinant is one. Formula (7) follows by extracting the coefficient of the two rank-one updates in a determinant and is valid at singular matrices.

**Block-degree lemma.** If \(C=C_A\oplus C_B\) in the actual coordinate decomposition, then \(T_f(C)\) preserves
\[
\bigwedge^2\mathbb C^A,\qquad
\mathbb C^A\wedge\mathbb C^B,\qquad
\bigwedge^2\mathbb C^B.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, the minor in (7) is zero unless its numbers of rows and columns in \(A\) agree. This condition is
\[
|S\cap A|-|J\cap A|=|S\cap A|-|I\cap A|,
\]
or \(|I\cap A|=|J\cap A|\). The same condition holds for every summand of \(T_f(C)\). This argument uses the coordinate block structure; it does not rotate the point-process ground set.

Suppose now that \(u_A=\alpha a_0\) and \(v_A=\beta a_0\), where \(a_0\in\mathbb C^A\) is unit. Put
\[
z=\alpha v_B-\beta u_B.
\]
Exterior bilinearity and (8) give the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
u\wedge v&=a_0\wedge z+u_B\wedge v_B,\\
q_f(C;u,v)
&=q_f(C;a_0,z)+q_f(C;u_B,v_B).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficients \(\alpha,\beta\) are complex, and the displayed formula uses ordinary exterior bilinearity; no real-basis assumption is made.

## 2. Positivity of a cross-block term

The following fact works for projection subspaces of arbitrary ranks.

Let \(V_A\subseteq\mathbb C^A\), \(V_B\subseteq\mathbb C^B\), let \(x\in V_A^\perp\cap\mathbb C^A\), and let \(y\in V_B^\perp\cap\mathbb C^B\). Then, for supermodular \(f\),
\[
\boxed{
q_f(P_{V_A}\oplus P_{V_B};x,y)\ge0.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Zero vectors are immediate. Otherwise normalize \(x_0=x/\|x\|\), \(y_0=y/\|y\|\). Nested projection couplings give
\[
X_A\subset Y_A,\quad
X_A\sim\mu_{V_A},\quad Y_A\sim\mu_{V_A\oplus[x_0]},
\]
and
\[
X_B\subset Y_B,\quad
X_B\sim\mu_{V_B},\quad Y_B\sim\mu_{V_B\oplus[y_0]}.
\]
Choose these two couplings independently. Each inclusion adds exactly one coordinate, say \(i\in A\) and \(j\in B\).

The four projection laws in the finite mixed difference factor across the coordinate blocks. Separate affinity in the two rank-one update parameters therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&q_f(P_{V_A}\oplus P_{V_B};x,y)\\
&=\|x\|^2\|y\|^2\,
\mathbb E\big[
f(Y_A\cup Y_B)+f(X_A\cup X_B)
-f(Y_A\cup X_B)-f(X_A\cup Y_B)
\big]\\
&=\|x\|^2\|y\|^2\,
\mathbb E\big[\Delta_i\Delta_j f(X_A\cup X_B)\big]\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The coefficient is \(1\), with the indicated norm factors: \(q\) is the coefficient of \(st\), not twice that coefficient.

This compatible four-law coupling exists here because the two independently coupled inclusions occur on disjoint coordinate blocks. No general four-law compatibility assertion is being used.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1

By symmetry, assume \(\dim\operatorname{span}(u_A,v_A)\le1\). If both restrictions vanish, only the second term below is present. Otherwise write
\[
u_A=\alpha a_0,\qquad v_A=\beta a_0,\qquad \|a_0\|=1.
\]
The orthogonality \(u,v\perp W\) implies
\[
a_0\perp a,\qquad u_B,v_B\perp b,\qquad z=\alpha v_B-\beta u_B\perp b.
\]
Apply (9) at \(C=P_W\).

The first term \(q_f(P_W;a_0,z)\) is nonnegative by the cross-block result (10).

For the second term define the averaged function on \(B\):
\[
g_B(S)=\mathbb E_{X_A\sim\mu_{[a]}} f(X_A\cup S)
=\sum_{i\in A}|a_i|^2 f(\{i\}\cup S).
\tag{12}
\]
Every elementary second difference of \(g_B\) is the same nonnegative weighted average of elementary second differences of \(f\). Thus \(g_B\) is supermodular. Independence of DPP blocks gives the polynomial identity
\[
F_f(P_{[a]}\oplus C_B)=F_{g_B}(C_B).
\]
Consequently
\[
q_f(P_W;u_B,v_B)=q_{g_B}(bb^*;u_B,v_B)\ge0
\tag{13}
\]
by the rank-one theorem of Agent019. Together, (9), (10), and (13) prove (2).

The proof also works with an arbitrary projection \(P_{V_A}\) on the first block, provided the update restrictions on that block have span of dimension at most one and the second block has the required local mixed-positivity property. In particular, the first block need not itself have rank one for this lifting argument.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 2

Put
\[
A_0=\mathbb C^A\cap a^\perp,\qquad
B_0=\mathbb C^B\cap b^\perp.
\]
Then \(W^\perp=A_0\oplus B_0\) and \(\dim A_0\le1\). If \(\dim A_0=1\), choose a unit vector \(a_0\) spanning it. Every bivector in \(\bigwedge^2W^\perp\), including nondecomposable ones, has the form
\[
\xi=a_0\wedge z+\eta,\qquad z\in B_0,\quad \eta\in\bigwedge^2B_0.
\tag{14}
\]
The first term is omitted if \(\dim A_0=0\).

By the block-degree lemma, the two summands have no mixed pairing. The first pairing is \(q_f(P_W;a_0,z)\ge0\) by (10). On the pure \(B\) sector the operator is exactly \(T_{g_B}(bb^*)\), with \(g_B\) from (12). This equality follows directly from (7), since the complementary minor factors into its \(A\) and \(B\) minors; equivalently it is the exterior coefficient version of the polynomial identity preceding (13). Agent030's rank-one full-operator theorem gives
\[
\langle\eta,T_{g_B}(bb^*)\eta\rangle\ge0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\langle\xi,T_f(P_W)\xi\rangle\ge0
\]
for every \(\xi\in\bigwedge^2W^\perp\), proving (3).

This restricted PSD theorem does not contradict Agent030's counterexample to full exterior PSD: restriction to \(\bigwedge^2W^\perp\) is an essential hypothesis.

## 5. Structured contraction compressions

Let \(u,v\) be orthonormal, \(U=\operatorname{span}(u,v)\), and \(R=P_{U^\perp}\). Suppose
\[
RKR=C=\lambda aa^*+\mu bb^*,\qquad 0\le\lambda,\mu\le1,
\tag{15}
\]
where \(a,b\in U^\perp\) are unit and supported on disjoint coordinate blocks, and (1) holds. In particular (1) is automatic if the block supporting \(a\) has size at most two.

**Corollary 3.** For every positive Hermitian contraction \(K\) satisfying (15) and every supermodular \(f\),
\[
\boxed{q_f(K;u,v)\ge0.}
\tag{16}
\]

Agent019's compression and interpolation identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_f(K;u,v)=q_f(C;u,v)
={}&(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)q_f(0;u,v)\\
&+\lambda(1-\mu)q_f(aa^*;u,v)
+(1-\lambda)\mu q_f(bb^*;u,v)\\
&+\lambda\mu q_f(P_W;u,v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The first three terms are nonnegative by the rank-zero/rank-one results; the last is nonnegative by Theorem 1. All weights are nonnegative.

The same conclusion holds if \(R-C\), in place of \(C\), has the structured form (15). Apply complement duality to \(f^c(S)=f(E\setminus S)\), which is also supermodular.

## 6. Entropy concavity, including boundary segments

Let
\[
K_t=K_0+tD,\quad 0\le t\le1,\qquad
D=\rho uu^*-\sigma vv^*,\qquad \rho,\sigma>0,
\tag{18}
\]
where \(u,v\) are orthonormal and every \(K_t\) is a positive Hermitian contraction. Its compression \(C=RK_tR\) is independent of \(t\).

**Corollary 4.** If \(C\), or \(R-C\), satisfies the structured rank-two condition (15) and (1), then
\[
\boxed{
H(K_t)\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1),\qquad 0\le t\le1.
}
\tag{19}
\]
Here \(H(K)\) denotes Shannon entropy of its DPP atom law. There is no interior assumption in this conclusion.

We give the boundary argument explicitly, because isotropic regularization does not retain rank-two compression.

First assume \(C\) has the form (15). For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), set
\[
K_t^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K_t+\varepsilon I,\qquad
h_\varepsilon(t)=H(K_t^\varepsilon).
\]
The eigenvalues lie in \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\). Using
\[
p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S,\qquad L=K(I-K)^{-1},
\]
one has \(p_S(K_t^\varepsilon)\ge\varepsilon^{2n}\), because
\(\det(I-K_t^\varepsilon)\ge\varepsilon^n\) and \(L_\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon I\). Thus the frozen log-atom table satisfies
\[
0\le f_{t,\varepsilon}(S)=-\log p_S(K_t^\varepsilon)
\le M_\varepsilon:=2n\log(1/\varepsilon).
\tag{20}
\]
This table is supermodular by the positive-definite principal-minor inequality, as established in Agent019 and audited in Agent079.

On \(U^\perp\), the regularized compression is
\[
C^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)C+\varepsilon R.
\]
Choose an orthonormal basis \(a,b,d_1,\ldots,d_{n-4}\) of \(U^\perp\). Its first two eigenvalues are
\((1-2\varepsilon)\lambda+\varepsilon\) and
\((1-2\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon\); the remaining \(n-4\) eigenvalues are \(\varepsilon\). The four spectral vertices that select no \(d_j\) have nonnegative \(q_{f_{t,\varepsilon}}\) by Theorem 1 and the rank-zero/rank-one results. The total weight of all other vertices is at most
\[
\beta_\varepsilon\le(n-4)\varepsilon.
\tag{21}
\]
We are in the rank-two setting \(n\ge4\); if no remaining basis vectors occur the right side is zero.

For any projection vertex \(P_Z\) on a subspace of \(U^\perp\), separate affinity yields
\[
q_f(P_Z;u,v)
=F_f(P_Z+P_U)-F_f(P_Z+uu^*)-F_f(P_Z+vv^*)+F_f(P_Z).
\]
All four kernels are projections. For \(0\le f\le M_\varepsilon\), every displayed expectation lies in \([0,M_\varepsilon]\), so \(q_f(P_Z;u,v)\ge-2M_\varepsilon\). Therefore
\[
q_{f_{t,\varepsilon}}(K_t^\varepsilon;u,v)
\ge-4n(n-4)\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon).
\tag{22}
\]

At an interior kernel, the frozen-entropy identity is
\[
D^2H(K)[D,D]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[D])^2}{p_S}
-2\rho\sigma\,q_{-\log p_K}(K;u,v).
\tag{23}
\]
For the regularized path its direction is \((1-2\varepsilon)D\). Discarding the nonpositive Fisher term and using \((1-2\varepsilon)^2\le1\), (22) gives uniformly in \(t\)
\[
h_\varepsilon''(t)
\le8n(n-4)\rho\sigma\,\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)
=:\delta_\varepsilon.
\tag{24}
\]
Integrating the scalar upper bound gives
\[
h_\varepsilon(t)\ge
(1-t)h_\varepsilon(0)+t h_\varepsilon(1)
-\frac{\delta_\varepsilon}{2}t(1-t).
\]
Now \(\delta_\varepsilon\to0\), and finite DPP entropy is continuous, including at zero atom probabilities. Passing to the limit proves (19). If \(R-C\) has the required form, replace \(K_t\) by \(I-K_t\); complementation preserves entropy and changes the compression to \(R-C\).

This proof adapts the valid boundary method of Agent079 to the new four good vertices. It does not apply Corollary 3 directly to the regularized rank-\((n-2)\) compression.

## 7. A rational example using all six sites

This deterministic example illustrates the new class without being used as evidence for its universal proof.

Take \(A=\{1,2\}\), \(B=\{3,4,5,6\}\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=(3,4,0,0,0,0)^T/5,\\
a_0&=(-4,3,0,0,0,0)^T/5,\\
b&=(0,0,14,-2,-3,-4)^T/15,\\
c&=(0,0,-2,11,-6,-8)^T/15,\\
d&=(0,0,-1,-2,2,-4)^T/5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
The vectors \(b,c,d\), together with the fourth column, are the embedded columns of
\[
Q=I-\frac{hh^T}{15},\qquad h=(1,2,3,4)^T,\qquad Q^TQ=I.
\]
Put
\[
\widetilde u=\tfrac35a_0+\tfrac45c,\quad
\widetilde v=d,\quad
u=\tfrac35\widetilde u+\tfrac45\widetilde v,\quad
v=-\tfrac45\widetilde u+\tfrac35\widetilde v.
\]
Explicitly,
\[
u=\frac1{125}(-36,27,-28,4,16,-112)^T,
\quad
v=\frac1{375}(144,-108,-13,-266,186,-52)^T.
\tag{26}
\]
The four columns \(a,b,u,v\) are exactly orthonormal, and both update vectors have nonzero entries at every site. The update plane is not a direct sum of a line supported on \(A\) and a line supported on \(B\): it intersects \(\mathbb C^B\) in \([d]\) but contains no nonzero vector supported on \(A\).

Let
\[
f(S)=\max\left(0,\sum_{i\in S}(1,1,2,2,3,3)_i-4\right)^2.
\tag{27}
\]
It is supermodular because increments of the convex function \(x\mapsto\max(0,x-4)^2\) are nondecreasing and all coordinate weights are nonnegative. Its higher Möbius coefficients need not be positive; for instance \(c_{\{3,4,5,6\}}=-6\). The exact verifier checks all 240 elementary inequalities.

At \(P_W=aa^T+bb^T\),
\[
q_f(P_W;a_0,d)=\frac{212}{45},\qquad
q_f(P_W;c,d)=\frac{2848}{225}.
\]
The block-degree identity and update-plane basis invariance give
\[
\boxed{
q_f(P_W;u,v)
=\frac9{25}\frac{212}{45}
+\frac{16}{25}\frac{2848}{225}
=\frac{55108}{5625}.
}
\tag{28}
\]
Both \(P_W\) and \(R-P_W\) have rank two on the four-dimensional space \(U^\perp\).

For the contraction compression
\[
C=\frac25aa^T+\frac35bb^T,
\]
the exact vertex values and weights are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{vertex}&\text{weight}&q_f(\text{vertex};u,v)\\\hline
0&6/25&2176/1875\\
aa^T&4/25&20708/5625\\
bb^T&9/25&4587/625\\
P_W&6/25&55108/5625
\end{array}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{q_f(C;u,v)=\frac{164839}{28125}.}
\tag{29}
\]
At
\[
K=C+\frac13uu^T+\frac23vv^T
\]
the compression identity gives the same value. Here \(\operatorname{rank}C=2\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(R-C)=4\), so the rank/corank-one condition still does not apply.

For an explicit entropy segment covered by Corollary 4, take
\[
K_t=C+\left(\frac13+\frac t6\right)uu^T
       +\left(\frac23-\frac t6\right)vv^T,\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{30}
\]
Its spectrum is contained in \([0,1]\), its two zero eigenvalues persist, and its direction is \((uu^T-vv^T)/6\). Corollary 4 proves entropy concavity on this boundary segment without evaluating logarithms numerically.
