# Global convergence through averaging weight 0.51

Parent derivation, 14 September 2026. This strengthens the smaller perturbative range in [QSO_AVERAGING_RANGE.md](QSO_AVERAGING_RANGE.md). The proof below is complete as a proposed theorem; independent audit077 is still required before its promotion in the principal claim ledger. No optimality or historical-novelty claim is made.

Throughout, \(V\) is a four-type strictly non-Volterra QSO with symmetric parental coefficients, and \(T_\lambda=(1-\lambda)I+\lambda V\). All allowed coefficients may vanish. The simplex is closed. Its unique fixed point \(p\) is established in [the main theorem](FOUR_TYPE_UNIQUENESS.md).

**Theorem.** For every \(0<\lambda\le51/100\), every orbit of \(T_\lambda\) converges to \(p\). For \(1/2\le\lambda\le51/100\), the following uniform estimate holds:
\[
\|T_\lambda^n x-p\|_1
\le2\left(\frac{2499}{2500}\right)^{n-3},\qquad n\ge3.
\tag{1}
\]
The proof uses a two-step coordinate cap and an overlap bound on the third image. The below-half interval follows from Section 3 of QSO_AVERAGING_RANGE.md.

## 1. A uniform two-step cap

Fix \(1/2\le\lambda\le51/100\), and write \(a=1-\lambda\). For a coordinate \(i\), put \(s=x_i\) and \(v=V_i(x)\). Then \(0\le v\le(1-s)^2\). The symmetric bilinear form associated to \(V_i\), denoted \(Q_i\), satisfies
\[
Q_i(x,Vx)\le(1-s)(1-v),\qquad V_i(Vx)\le(1-v)^2,
\]
because any parental occurrence of \(i\) contributes zero. Expanding \(T_\lambda^2\) gives
\[
(T_\lambda^2x)_i
\le a^2s+\lambda a(1+a)v
 +2\lambda^2a(1-s)(1-v)+\lambda^3(1-v)^2.
\tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(s\), the right side is convex in \(v\), so its maximum on the allowed interval occurs at \(v=0\) or \(v=(1-s)^2\).

At \(v=0\), (2) equals
\[
\lambda^2(2-\lambda)+a(a-2\lambda^2)s.
\]
The coefficient of \(s\) is nonpositive when \(\lambda\ge1/2\). The first term is increasing on the stated interval, and is at most
\[
\left(\frac{51}{100}\right)^2\frac{149}{100}
=\frac{387549}{1000000}<\frac25.
\tag{3}
\]

At the other endpoint, put \(u=1-s\). The resulting polynomial is
\[
F_\lambda(u)=a^2(1-u)+\lambda a(1+a)u^2
 +2\lambda^2au(1-u^2)+\lambda^3(1-u^2)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
In the Bernstein basis of degree four,
\[
F_\lambda(u)=\sum_{k=0}^4 b_k(\lambda)\binom4k u^k(1-u)^{4-k}.
\]
The coefficients and uniform upper bounds are:

| \(k\) | \(b_k(\lambda)\) | Upper bound on \([1/2,51/100]\) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | \(1-2\lambda+\lambda^2+\lambda^3\) | \(3/8\) |
| 1 | \(3/4-3\lambda/2+5\lambda^2/4+\lambda^3/2\) | \(752901/2000000\) |
| 2 | \((3-4\lambda+6\lambda^2-\lambda^3)/6\) | \(795983/2000000\) |
| 3 | \(1/4+\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4-\lambda^3/2\) | \(3/8\) |
| 4 | \(2\lambda-3\lambda^2+\lambda^3\) | \(3/8\) |

These bounds follow directly by differentiation: \(b_0,b_3,b_4\) decrease and \(b_1,b_2\) increase throughout this interval. For example \(b_0'= -2+2\lambda+3\lambda^2\) is at most its negative value at \(51/100\), and \(b_2'=(-4+12\lambda-3\lambda^2)/6\) is at least its positive value at \(1/2\). The other three signs follow from their displayed quadratic derivatives. Every upper bound is strictly below \(2/5\).

The Bernstein weights are nonnegative and sum to one on \([0,1]\). Therefore \(F_\lambda(u)<2/5\), uniformly in \(u\). Together with (2)–(3), this proves
\[
T_\lambda^2(\Delta_3)\subseteq K_{2/5}.
\tag{5}
\]
Only the image set is claimed invariant; the entire cap need not be invariant for these weights.

## 2. Uniform overlap of the derivative-half columns

Set
\[
q=\frac25,\qquad d=1-2q=\frac15,\qquad\varepsilon=\frac1{50}.
\]
Take two points in \(T_\lambda^3(\Delta_3)\), and let \(m\) be their midpoint. Nested images and (5) imply \(m_i\le q\). Write
\[
A(m)_{kj}=\sum_i P_{ij,k}m_i.
\]
This matrix is column-stochastic and has zero diagonal. We prove that every pair of its columns has total overlap at least \(\varepsilon\).

For columns indexed by \(i,j\) with \(m_i,m_j\ge\varepsilon\), the common probability vector \(P_{ij,\cdot}\), weighted by \(m_j\) in column \(i\) and by \(m_i\) in column \(j\), already supplies overlap at least \(\varepsilon\).

Suppose instead that \(m_r=t<\varepsilon\). Each other mass is at least \(d-t\), since the remaining two coordinates have total at most \(2q\). In particular there cannot be a second small coordinate. Among the two third-image points forming \(m\), choose one whose \(r\)-coordinate is at most \(t\), and write it as \(T_\lambda z\), with \(z\in T_\lambda^2(\Delta_3)\). Nonnegativity gives
\[
z_r\le\frac{t}{1-\lambda}\le\frac{100t}{49},\qquad
V_r(z)\le\frac{t}{\lambda}\le2t.
\]
Since \(z\in K_q\), each other coordinate is at least \(d-100t/49>0\). For distinct \(b,c\ne r\), the off-diagonal parental term appears twice, so
\[
P_{bc,r}\le\frac{V_r(z)}{2z_bz_c}
\le\theta(t):=\frac{t}{(d-100t/49)^2}.
\tag{6}
\]
This function is increasing on the interval in question. At its right endpoint,
\[
\theta(1/50)=\frac{2401}{3042}<1.
\tag{7}
\]

Now test columns \(r,j\), and let \(k,\ell\) be the other two types. Strict exclusion and (6) give
\[
A_{kj}\ge m_\ell(1-\theta(t)),\qquad
A_{\ell j}\ge m_k(1-\theta(t)),
\]
while the parent pair \(j,r\) gives
\[
A_{kr}+A_{\ell r}\ge m_j.
\]
Put \(h=(d-t)(1-\theta(t))\). The sum of the overlaps on just rows \(k,\ell\) is at least
\[
\min\{h,A_{kr}\}+\min\{h,A_{\ell r}\}
\ge\min\{h,A_{kr}+A_{\ell r}\}=h.
\]
Here \(A_{kr}+A_{\ell r}\ge m_j\ge d-t\ge h\). Finally,
\[
h\ge\left(\frac15-\frac1{50}\right)
       \left(1-\frac{2401}{3042}\right)
=\frac{641}{16900}>\frac1{50}.
\tag{8}
\]
This covers every column pair, including zero masses. Thus the Dobrushin coefficient of \(A(m)\) is at most \(1-\varepsilon=49/50\).

## 3. The full zero-sum norm and convergence

The difference matrix for \(T_\lambda\) at midpoint \(m\) is
\[
B=(1-\lambda)I+2\lambda A(m).
\]
It has equal column sums \(1+\lambda\). Its induced \(\ell^1\) norm on the zero-sum subspace is therefore the maximum, over column pairs \(i,j\) and row subsets \(G\), of
\(\mathbf1_G^{\mathsf T}B(e_i-e_j)\).

If \(G\) contains both or neither of \(i,j\), the identity term cancels and the cut is at most \(2\lambda(49/50)\). If \(i\in G,j\notin G\), the strict four-type transfer inequality gives at most \(1-\lambda+2\lambda q\) for a two-element cut. Singleton or three-element cuts give the smaller bound \(1-\lambda\), by the zero diagonal and column sums of \(A\). In the reversed case \(j\in G,i\notin G\), stochasticity gives the bound \(3\lambda-1\). Empty and full cuts vanish. These cases exhaust the subsets.

Consequently
\[
\|B|_E\|_{1\to1}
\le\max\left\{2\lambda\frac{49}{50},\ 1-\frac\lambda5,\ 3\lambda-1\right\}
\le\frac{2499}{2500}<1.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed the last two terms are at most \(9/10\) and \(53/100\), respectively.

The compact set \(S=T_\lambda^3(\Delta_3)\) is invariant by nesting of images. For each of its pairs the exact identity
\[
T_\lambda x-T_\lambda y
=B((x+y)/2)(x-y)
\]
and (9) give contraction on \(S\). The fixed point \(p\) belongs to \(S\); its distance from any point of the simplex is at most two. This proves (1), including the upper endpoint and all boundary or coefficient degeneracies.

The proof of convergence for \(0<\lambda<1/2\) in QSO_AVERAGING_RANGE.md uses its scalar entrance bound and the main capped contraction theorem, so no interval of positive weights is omitted. This result is global; the larger local stability range in [QSO_SPECTRAL_BOUNDS.md](QSO_SPECTRAL_BOUNDS.md) is a different statement.
