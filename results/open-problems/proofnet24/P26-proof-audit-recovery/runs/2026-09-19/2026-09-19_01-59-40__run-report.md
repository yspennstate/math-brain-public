## 4. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I first tried to break the theorem at every convention-sensitive point: the smallest prime, deleted boundary points, fiber collisions, clone adjacency, character denominators, and the theta normalization.

Because `p ≡ 1 mod 4`, `-1` is a square, so both deleted points `+1,-1` lie in `Q`. For `q in Q`, write `q=r^2`. Then

\[
\pi(q)=\left(\frac{r+r^{-1}}2\right)^2,
\qquad
\pi(q)-1=\left(\frac{r-r^{-1}}2\right)^2.
\]

Thus every nondeleted `q` maps into `V_2`. Conversely, given `x in V_2`, choose `a^2=x`, `b^2=x-1`. Since `(a+b)(a-b)=1`, put `r=a+b`, so `r^{-1}=a-b`, and set `q=r^2`. Then `q in Q`, `pi(q)=x`, and `q=1` would force `b=0` while `q=-1` would force `a=0`; both are excluded by `x in V_2`.

Direct subtraction gives

\[
\pi(q)-\pi(r)=\frac{(q-r)(qr-1)}{4qr}.
\]

Hence equal images imply `q=r` or `qr=1`. Since `q=±1` were deleted, `q` and `q^{-1}` are distinct. Therefore every fiber is exactly `{q,q^{-1}}`; no hidden third lift or ramification point remains in `X`.

Because `q,r` are nonzero squares, `4qr` is a square, so

\[
\chi(\pi(q)-\pi(r))=\chi((q-r)(qr-1)).

Inside one fiber `r=q^{-1}`, the factor `qr-1` is zero, so the two clones are nonadjacent. Between distinct fibers the sign is exactly the Paley sign of the two common images. Thus the lifted graph really is the independent two-clone blowup.

## 5. Independent proof of the theta clause

Let `H=G[\overline K_s]`. Use

\[
\vartheta(\overline G)=
\max\{\langle J,Y\rangle:
Y\succeq0,\ \operatorname{Tr}Y=1,\
Y_{uv}=0\text{ for distinct nonedges }uv\text{ of }G\}.
\]

Average any feasible matrix for `H` over independent permutations of clones inside every fiber. A diagonal fiber block becomes `a_v I_s`; a cross block over a base edge becomes `c_{vw}J_s`; a base-nonedge cross block is zero. On the fiber-constant subspace the resulting matrix is the base matrix with diagonal `a_v` and off-diagonal `s c_{vw}`; on each fiberwise zero-sum subspace it acts by `a_v>=0`.

Define the base matrix by `Y_{vv}=s a_v` and `Y_{vw}=s^2 c_{vw}`. Its scaled matrix `Y/s` is exactly the fiber-constant compression, so `Y` is PSD; it has trace one, the required base-nonedge zeros, and exactly the same objective as the lifted feasible point. Therefore

\[
\vartheta(\overline H)\le\vartheta(\overline G).
\]

Conversely, from base-feasible `Y`, use lifted blocks `(Y_{vv}/s)I_s` and `(Y_{vw}/s^2)J_s`. The fiber-constant compression is `Y/s>=0` and each zero-sum eigenvalue is `Y_{vv}/s>=0`; trace and objective are preserved. Hence

\[
\vartheta(\overline H)\ge\vartheta(\overline G).
\]

So equality holds. Taking `s=2` proves the Claim-11 theta clause.
