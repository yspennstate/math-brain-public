## (a) Exact statement that blocks

Write `R(H;p)` for all-terminal reliability. For any 3-edge defect set `P` in `K_n` not containing the terminal edge `st`, define

`H_P = K_n - P`,  and  `G_P = K_n - (P union {st})`.

The already-audited bridge identity gives

`R(H_P;p) - R(G_P;p) = p SR_{G_P}^{s,t}(p)`.

Let the balloon/max-star defect be

`P_* = {su, sv, sw}`

for three distinct nonterminals `u,v,w`. The exact all-p statement needed to make the max-minus-four balloon uniformly most split reliable after the standard terminal-edge reduction is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{st}R(K_n-P;p)
\;\le\;
\Delta_{st}R(K_n-P_*;p)
\quad\text{for every admissible 3-edge defect }P
\text{ and every }0<p<1,
}
\]

where

\[
\Delta_{st}R(H;p):=R(H;p)-R(H-st;p).
\]

Equivalently, the obstruction is a **distinguished-edge reliability-derivative extremal theorem**: among the dense 3-defect hosts produced after adding `st`, the 3-star at terminal `s` must maximize the marginal reliability loss caused by deleting `st`.

This formulation is diagnostic, not a new theorem claim.

## (b) Routes tried and the precise reason each fails

### Route 1: bridge identity plus separate all-terminal extremal orderings

This route is what made max-minus-two and max-minus-three close. It reduces split reliability to a difference of two all-terminal reliability values. Published dense all-terminal extremal theorems can order many of the values `R(K_n-P;p)` and `R(K_n-(P union {st});p)` separately.

**Failure at max-minus-four:** separate inequalities do not order the difference. From `A>=B` and `C>=D` one cannot infer `A-C >= B-D`. The new object is the discrete derivative `Delta_{st}R`, not either reliability value alone.

### Route 2: Kelmans/compression monotonicity

Kelmans-type compression, and the Satyanarayana--Schoppmann--Suffel reliability-improving transformation, give monotonicity statements for total all-terminal reliability. Kahl's later compression work explicitly summarizes that compression cannot increase and typically decreases all-terminal reliability.

**Failure at max-minus-four:** I found no published theorem saying that the distinguished-edge marginal `Delta_{st}R` is monotone under the same compression. The compression can change the position of `s` and `t` relative to the moved neighborhood, so subtracting the two compressed reliabilities is not licensed by the total-reliability theorem.

### Route 3: independent-deletion maximizer

Satyanarayana--Schoppmann--Suffel (Networks 22, 1992) supply the classical theorem used successfully in the max-minus-two proof: at fixed order/size, deleting independent edges from `K_n` maximizes all-terminal reliability.

**Failure at max-minus-four:** this identifies an extremal total reliability value for a fixed number of deletions. It does not compare the loss incurred by deleting one distinguished terminal edge from two already-defective hosts.

### Route 4: dense uniformly-least-reliable theorem

Petingi--Saccoman--Schoppmann (Networks 27, 1996) characterize uniformly least reliable graphs in the sufficiently dense range. In the defect language, star-like concentrations of missing edges are extremal for total all-terminal reliability.

**Failure at max-minus-four:** again the theorem orders `R`, not the edge derivative `Delta_{st}R`. Even if both the 3-defect host and 4-defect child are individually extremal in their density classes, the difference of the two extremal values need not dominate every matched parent-child difference without an additional coupling or majorization theorem.

### Route 5: the max-minus-three switching/conic decomposition

R06's max-minus-three proof closes because, after classifying the relevant defect types, every target margin can be written as a nonnegative combination of a small set of known all-terminal gaps.

**Failure at max-minus-four:** the 3-edge host defect poset is larger. The natural margins introduce distinguished-edge marginal gaps that are not generated by the same known positive cone. The bridge survives, but the cone of certified differences no longer obviously spans every comparison.

### Route 6: exact finite coefficientwise enumeration

R06's `n=8` theorem proves every remaining density, including `m=24=binom(8,2)-4`, by exact split-coefficient comparison after complete finite classification.

**Failure as an all-n method:** it certifies the `n=8` layer but supplies no uniform symbolic inequality in `n`. Repeating exhaustive enumeration at isolated `n` would be evidence accumulation, not an all-n proof mechanism. It also does not reveal why the marginal derivative should be ordered under a graph transformation.

### Route 7: endpoint/local asymptotics

Romero's local theory and low/high-p coefficient arguments can distinguish graphs near an endpoint.

**Failure:** uniform split optimality requires all `p in [0,1]`. Endpoint dominance does not prevent a crossing in the interior. The obstruction is therefore genuinely global in `p`.

## (c) What a proof must contain that no tried route contains

A successful all-n proof needs at least one new ingredient of the following strength.

The cleanest form would be a **derivative-level compression/majorization lemma**: under a precisely specified compression that preserves the distinguished edge `st` and the dense simple-graph class,

\[
\Delta_{st}R(H;p)
\le
\Delta_{st}R(C(H);p)
\qquad(0<p<1),
\]

with the orientation chosen so repeated compression drives the three host defects to the terminal-centered 3-star. A usable theorem would have to track `st` explicitly; ordinary reliability compression is insufficient.

A second possible form would be an **exact positive conic decomposition** for every 3-edge defect type `P`:

\[
\Delta_{st}R(K_n-P_*;p)-\Delta_{st}R(K_n-P;p)
=
\sum_j c_j(p,n) D_j(p,n),
\]

where each `c_j>=0` and each primitive `D_j>=0` is a published or independently proved all-terminal comparison. The max-minus-three proof has precisely this flavor, but no such spanning cone is currently known for max-minus-four.

What is missing is therefore not another bridge identity, another endpoint coefficient, or another finite checker. It is a theorem controlling a **marginal** reliability functional under defect concentration.

## (e) Nearest solved problem in the literature, and what it has that this problem lacks

The nearest published solved machinery is the dense all-terminal reliability extremal theory:

- A. Satyanarayana, L. Schoppmann, C. L. Suffel, *A reliability-improving graph transformation with applications to network reliability*, Networks 22 (1992), 209--216.
- L. Petingi, J. T. Saccoman, L. Schoppmann, *Uniformly least reliable graphs*, Networks 27 (1996), 125--131.
- The modern compression literature (e.g. Nathan Kahl, 2021) records the same total-reliability monotonicity viewpoint.

These results have a graph transformation or extremal theorem that orders the scalar **total reliability** `R(G;p)` uniformly in `p`. The max-minus-four split problem lacks the corresponding theorem for the **discrete derivative with respect to one distinguished terminal edge**, `Delta_{st}R`. That missing distinguished-edge monotonicity is the exact structural gap.

The nearest solved split problem in the repository is R06's max-minus-three theorem. It succeeds because its finite defect-type classification collapses all margins into a four-generator cone of already-known nonnegative total-reliability gaps. Max-minus-four lacks that cone closure.
