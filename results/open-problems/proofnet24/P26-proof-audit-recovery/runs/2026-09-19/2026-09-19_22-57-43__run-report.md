## 4. Independent reconstruction of the source-to-Laplace reduction

I independently checked the algebra from Malrieu's displayed Theorem-4.1 formula.

On `theta in (-pi/2,0)` set

`t=-tan(theta)>0`, `phi(t)=t-1/t`, and `u_r(t)=r H(theta;r)`.

Since `2 cot(2 theta)=t-1/t`, the source formula gives exactly

`H(t;r)=exp(-r phi(t)) int_0^t exp(r phi(s)) ds`,

hence

`u_r'(t)=r[1-(1+t^{-2})u_r(t)]`,

with `u_r(0)=0`, `u_r(infinity)=1`.

Using the source quadrant symmetries and the exact Jacobian `dtheta=-dt/(1+t^2)`, normalization and the radial-growth integral reduce to

`J(r)=int_0^infinity [1+(t^{-2}-1)u_r(t)] dt`,

`K(r)=int_0^infinity [t/(1+t^2)-u_r(t)/t] dt`,

and

`G(r)=K(r)/J(r)`.

Now set

`x=t-1/t`,
`t(x)=(x+sqrt(x^2+4))/2`,
`a(x)=t(x)^2/(1+t(x)^2)=(1+x/sqrt(x^2+4))/2`.

The scalar ODE becomes

`du/dx=r(a-u)`

and therefore

`u_r(x)=int_0^infinity exp(-q) a(x-q/r) dq`.

Pairing `x` and `-x`, using `t(-x)=1/t(x)` and `a(-x)=1-a(x)`, gives exactly

`K(r)=int_0^infinity [1-u_r(x)-u_r(-x)] / sqrt(x^2+4) dx`,

`J(r)=int_0^infinity [1+(x/sqrt(x^2+4))(u_r(-x)-u_r(x))] dx`.

After `q=r delta` this yields

`G(r)=L[k](r)/L[j](r)`,  `L[f](r)=int_0^infinity e^{-r delta}f(delta) ddelta`,

with

`k(delta)=int_0^infinity [a(x+delta)-a(x-delta)]/sqrt(x^2+4) dx`,

`j(delta)=int_0^infinity [1-(x/sqrt(x^2+4))(a(x+delta)+a(x-delta)-1)] dx`.

No sign, factor-two, or left/right convention discrepancy survives this reconstruction.

### 5.2 Convolution monotonicity failure

Let

`b(x)=a'(x)=2/(x^2+4)^(3/2)`,
`w(x)=1/sqrt(x^2+4)`.

Direct differentiation gives

`k'(delta)=(b*w)(delta)`

on the full real line. Although `w` is not integrable, the standard symmetric-decreasing convolution argument still applies by truncating `w` to centered finite intervals and passing monotonically. Hence `k'` is nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)` and `k''<=0` pointwise by smoothness.

For `g(x)=x/sqrt(x^2+4)`, direct splitting of the full-line convolution gives

`j'(delta)=(g*b)(delta)`,

and since `g'=2b`,

`j''(delta)=2(b*b)(delta)>0`.

Thus the claimed structural signs are genuine, not a rearrangement shortcut that needs integrability of `w`.

### 5.3 Endpoint failure

`k'(0)=int_R 2/(x^2+4)^2 dx=pi/8`,

`j(0)=pi`,

`j''(0)=2 int_R b(x)^2 dx=3pi/32`.

`k(delta)=(pi/8)delta+O(delta^3)`,
`j(delta)=pi+(3pi/64)delta^2+O(delta^4)`,

and

`G(r)=1/(8r)+O(r^{-3}) -> 0`.

At the other endpoint, there is an even cleaner independent tail check than the owner's transition-layer prose. Since `b` is a probability density on `R` and has finite first moment,

`k'(delta)=(b*w)(delta) ~ w(delta) ~ 1/delta`,

`j'(delta)->1`

and in fact `j(delta)=delta+O(1)`. Consequently

`L[k](r)~r^{-1}log(1/r)`,
`L[j](r)~r^{-2}`,

so

`G(r)~r log(1/r)->0`.

Thus neither endpoint can hide a failure of the one-hump conclusion.

## 6. One-turn structure of the input quotient

Set

`h(delta)=k(delta)/j(delta)`

and

`q(delta)=k'(delta)j(delta)-k(delta)j'(delta)=j(delta)^2 h'(delta)`.

The structural identities above imply

`q'(delta)=k''(delta)j(delta)-k(delta)j''(delta)<0`

for every `delta>0`, because `k''<=0`, `j>0`, `k>0`, and `j''>0`.

Moreover

`q(0)=k'(0)j(0)=pi^2/8>0`.

Since `h(delta)>0` for `delta>0`, `h(0)=0`, and the tail asymptotics give `h(delta)->0`, `q` cannot stay nonnegative forever. Strict decrease therefore gives exactly one zero `delta_*`, so `h` is strictly increasing on `(0,delta_*)` and strictly decreasing on `(delta_*,infinity)`.

This part of the owner proof is complete.

## 7. #11 DUALIZE — direct variation-diminishing and moment certificates

The owner's proof cites Karp--Vishnyakova--Zhang (arXiv:2408.01755v2; Results in Mathematics 80 (2025), 112). I independently checked the actual theorem and its Laplace example.

Their Theorem 2.6 says that an `SR_3` integral kernel sends a unimodal input ratio `A/B` to a unimodal transform ratio, with orientation determined by the sign-regular signature. Their Laplace example explicitly states that

`K(x,y)=e^{-xy}`

has signature `(+,-,-)` and therefore preserves the input monotonicity pattern when the output is nonmonotone.

Applying this with `A=k`, `B=j>0`, and unit weight is legitimate: both transforms converge uniformly on compact `r`-sets, and `h=k/j` is strictly one-peaked. Since `G(r)>0` while `G(r)->0` at both ends, `G` cannot be monotone; it is therefore in the strictly-unimodal alternative and has the same increase-then-decrease orientation.

The same step can be read dually without treating the ratio theorem as a black box. For each level `c`,

`phi_c(delta)=k(delta)-c j(delta)=j(delta)(h(delta)-c)`

has at most two sign changes. The exponential kernel is variation diminishing, so

`F_c(r)=L[phi_c](r)=L[j](r)(G(r)-c)`

also has at most two sign changes. The constant sign pattern for two-crossing levels is exactly the level-set characterization of unimodality. This independently verifies the theorem orientation relevant here.

The strict derivative statement has an elementary dual certificate. For `0<c<h_*`, where `h_*=h(delta_*)`, `phi_c` has exactly two zeros `alpha<beta` and sign pattern `(-,+,-)`. If an off-mode point `r_0` had `G'(r_0)=0`, strict order monotonicity plus analyticity would make `G-G(r_0)` vanish to odd order at least three, hence

`int delta^m e^{-r_0 delta} phi_c(delta) ddelta=0`,  `m=0,1,2`.

But the quadratic

`P(delta)=-(delta-alpha)(delta-beta)`

has exactly the same sign as `phi_c`, so

`int P(delta)e^{-r_0 delta}phi_c(delta) ddelta>0`,

while the three moment identities force that same integral to be zero. Contradiction.

Thus no stationary inflection survives away from the unique mode, and

`G'(r)>0` for `r<r_*`, `G'(r_*)=0`, `G'(r)<0` for `r>r_*`.

**Outcome of #11:** the proof can be certified both by the published sign-regular Laplace theorem and by its level-set/moment dual. The strict derivative upgrade is not a numerical inference.
