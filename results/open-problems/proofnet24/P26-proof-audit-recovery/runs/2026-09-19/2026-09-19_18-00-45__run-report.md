## 2. Frozen theorem scope

Let `H_N` be the real symmetric Gaussian circular band matrix with independent upper-triangular coordinates,

\[
\mathbb E H_{xy}=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(H_{xy})=v\,\mathbf 1_{\{d_N(x,y)\le W\}},
\qquad
v=(2W+1)^{-1},
\]

on the domain

\[
2W+1\le N.
\]

At `E=0`, with semicircle density `rho_sc(0)=1/pi`, let

\[
I_N=[-\pi/N,\pi/N],
\qquad
X_N=\mathcal N(H_N,I_N).
\]

The audited C2 package states that an exact Gaussian covariance decomposition embeds `H_N` into a uniformly scaled deformed block-GOE ensemble to which Peled--Schenker--Shamis--Sodin Theorem 3 applies. In particular, with

\[
s=W+1,
\qquad
K=\left\lceil\frac Ns\right\rceil,
\qquad
q=\left\lfloor\frac NK\right\rfloor,
\qquad
a^2=\frac{q}{2(2W+1)},
\]

one gets

\[
\mathbb E X_N^2\le Y+Y^2,
\qquad
Y=2\pi C_{\rm PSSS}\sqrt{\frac{2(2W+1)}q},
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E X_N^2
\le
2\pi\sqrt6\,C_{\rm PSSS}
+24\pi^2 C_{\rm PSSS}^2
}
\]

uniformly on `2W+1<=N`. For the critical sequence `W=floor(c sqrt N)`, `q/W -> 1`, so

\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb E X_N^2
\le 4\pi C_{\rm PSSS}+16\pi^2 C_{\rm PSSS}^2.
\]

The same reduction gives uniform fixed-order factorial-moment bounds for every bounded unfolded interval. This is a rigorous **PARTIAL** theorem in the Fyodorov--Mirlin program; it does not identify the fixed-`c` critical point process.

## 3. Counterexample-first / adversarial proof audit

Before accepting the theorem I tried to break every place where the covariance embedding could silently leave the model class.

### 3.1 Balanced blocks stay inside the circular band

Partition `0,...,N-1` into `K` consecutive nonwrapping blocks with sizes

\[
m_j\in\{q,q+1\},\qquad \sum_jm_j=N.
\]

Since `K>=N/(W+1)`,

\[
\max_jm_j=\left\lceil\frac NK\right\rceil\le W+1.
\]

Any two indices in one block therefore have ordinary distance at most `W`, hence circular distance at most `W`. No out-of-band coordinate is inserted by the GOE extraction. The circular seam is harmless because it is left in the Gaussian remainder rather than forced into a block.

### 3.2 Exact smallest-block bound

Writing `s=W+1`, the owner claims

\[
q\ge\left\lceil\frac{2W+1}{3}\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{2s-1}{3}\right\rceil.
\]

I rederived it from the integer cases.

If `K=1`, the retained domain forces the trivial endpoint. If `K=2`,

\[
q=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\ge s-1
\ge\left\lceil\frac{2s-1}{3}\right\rceil.
\]

If `K>=3`, `K=ceil(N/s)` implies the integer inequality

\[
N\ge(K-1)s+1.
\]

Hence

\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac NK\right\rfloor
\ge s-\left\lceil\frac{s-1}{K}\right\rceil
\ge s-\left\lceil\frac{s-1}{3}\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{2s-1}{3}\right\rceil.
\]

Thus

\[
a^{-2}=rac{2(2W+1)}q\le6.
\]

The small endpoints, including `W=0`, do not create a denominator or partition defect.

### 3.3 Covariance subtraction is genuinely legal

Let `V_j` be independent standard GOE(`m_j`) blocks in the PSSS normalization

\[
\operatorname{Var}(V_{aa})=2/m_j,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V_{ab})=1/m_j\quad(a<b).
\]

With

\[
a^2=\frac{vq}{2},
\]

we have inside block `j`

\[
\operatorname{Var}(aV_{aa})=v\frac q{m_j}\le v,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(aV_{ab})=v\frac q{2m_j}\le v.
\]

Because the original Gaussian upper-triangular coordinates are independent, this is not a questionable matrix-level PSD subtraction: each scalar coordinate variance is reduced by a nonnegative amount. Put the leftover within-block variances and every interblock band coordinate into an independent Gaussian real-symmetric remainder `R`. Then exactly

\[
\boxed{H_N\stackrel d=R+a\bigoplus_jV_j.}
\]

No target coordinate is removed and no non-band coordinate is created.

### 3.4 Conditioning and rescaling do not change the theorem interface

Condition on `R` and divide by `a`:

\[
H_N/a=R/a+\bigoplus_jV_j.
\]

The deformation is then deterministic under the conditional law, while the GOE blocks stay independent and have total block dimension

\[
\sum_jm_j=N.
\]

PSSS Theorem 3 is stated for deformed block-Gaussian matrices with arbitrary block sizes and supplies the required factorial-moment/Minami estimate. Applying it to `I_N/a` gives

\[
\mathbb E X_N\le C_{\rm PSSS}N|I_N|/a=Y,
\]

\[
\mathbb E(X_N)_2\le Y^2.
\]

Since `X_N^2=(X_N)_2+X_N`,

\[
\mathbb E X_N^2\le Y+Y^2.
\]

The conditional estimate is uniform in the deterministic deformation, so averaging over `R` is legitimate.

No counterexample survived the band-seam, GOE-normalization, diagonal/off-diagonal variance, small-`W`, `K=2`, conditioning, or critical-asymptotic checks.

## 4. Method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY

The clean way to see the theorem is not primarily as a spectral-statistics construction. Move it to the Euclidean covariance space of the independent upper-triangular Gaussian coordinates.

The target law is a product Gaussian in those coordinates. A block-GOE component is another product Gaussian supported on a subset of legitimate band coordinates. Once its coordinate variances lie below the target variances, independent Gaussian variance splitting is exact. The random-matrix statement therefore reduces to a coordinatewise domination problem plus a published spectral-averaging theorem.

This category change exposes the only real geometric constraint: find large disjoint coordinate blocks on which a common GOE scale can be extracted without exceeding the smallest available coordinate variance. That is precisely what the balanced partition solves.

## 5. Method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

I next optimized the extraction itself rather than merely checking the owner's chosen partition.

Restrict attention to the method class used by C2: disjoint consecutive blocks of sizes `m_j<=W+1`, one common GOE scale `a`, and a Gaussian remainder carrying everything else. The diagonal GOE coordinate imposes

\[
\frac{2a^2}{m_j}\le v
\qquad\text{for every }j,
\]

so necessarily

\[
a^2\le\frac{v}{2}\min_jm_j.
\]

Any such cover needs at least

\[
K\ge\left\lceil\frac{N}{W+1}\right\rceil
\]

blocks. For fixed `K`, the largest possible minimum block size is `floor(N/K)`, attained by the balanced partition. Increasing `K` cannot improve `floor(N/K)`. Hence the R04 choice

\[
K=\left\lceil\frac{N}{W+1}\right\rceil,
\qquad
m_j\in\{q,q+1\},
\qquad
q=\left\lfloor\frac NK\right\rfloor
\]

maximizes the admissible common scale `a` inside this entire restricted method class.

There is also an infinite exact extremal family for the coarse uniform constant. Take

\[
W=3r+1,
\qquad
N=2W+3=6r+5.
\]

Then `s=3r+2`, `K=3`, and

\[
q=2r+1=\frac{2W+1}{3},
\]

so

\[
a^{-2}=\frac{2(2W+1)}q=6.
\]

Thus the constant `6` in the all-domain common-scale extraction bound cannot be improved by another rearrangement of consecutive disjoint blocks. This is **method-class sharpness only**, not a claim that the random-matrix second-moment constant itself is globally sharp.
