## 3. Exact claim

Let `G=S_3`, so `|G|=6`. Let `E subseteq G` be the relative positions that are graph edges in one invariant regular-orbit block, with

`k=|E|`, `0<k<6`.

Let `b:G -> R` satisfy the edge-fixed constraints

\[
b(g)=1\qquad(g\in E),
\]

and put

\[
a=\sum_{g\in G}b(g).
\]

For the sign and two-dimensional standard irreducibles, use unnormalized Fourier transforms

\[
\widehat b_{\rm sgn}=\sum_g b(g)\operatorname{sgn}(g),
\qquad
\widehat b_{\rm std}=\sum_g b(g)\rho_{\rm std}(g).
\]

Claim 14 asserts

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
\ge \frac{k(6-a)^2}{6-k}
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

with equality iff every one of the `6-k` free nonedge values equals

\[
\boxed{m=\frac{a-k}{6-k}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The associated corollary says that an edge-fixed correction that vanishes on at least one edge cannot change only the trivial Fourier sector: if its sign and standard transforms both vanish, the correction is identically zero.

This is structural partial progress inside the I0101 route. It is not a proof of Randomstrasse Conjecture 27.

## 4. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I first tried to break the literal theorem rather than accepting the owner algebra.

### 4.1 Endpoint `k=0`

If there are no fixed edges, a constant block can change its trivial Fourier coefficient with zero nontrivial energy. The claimed formula would have right side zero, so there is no coercive leakage. The owner theorem correctly assumes `k>0`.

### 4.2 Endpoint `k=6`

There are no free entries and the denominator `6-k` vanishes. The only edge-fixed block is `b=1`, whose nontrivial Fourier energy is zero. The owner theorem correctly assumes `k<6`; this endpoint must not be smuggled into the displayed fraction.

### 4.3 The apparently dangerous case `a=6`

The lower bound in (3.1) becomes zero. This does **not** mean every block of total sum six can avoid nontrivial leakage. The stronger exact decomposition proved below shows that zero is attained only when every free entry is also one. Any nonconstant free perturbation at fixed total sum produces strictly positive nontrivial energy.

### 4.4 Representation normalization

For an unnormalized Fourier transform on `S_3`, Plancherel weights irreducibles by their dimensions `1,1,2`. Thus the coefficient `2` in front of the Frobenius norm is correct. Replacing the standard representation by an orthogonally equivalent realization leaves the Frobenius norm unchanged.

### 4.5 Sign and equality tests

The right side is nonnegative for every real `a` and `1<=k<=5`. The proposed equality value (3.2) really has free sum `a-k`. There is no hidden sign assumption on the free values. Large positive, negative, or mixed free entries are all covered.

No literal-scope counterexample survived.

## 5. #11 DUALIZE — exact Pythagorean identity and a finite-group strengthening

The cleanest audit is stronger than the owner's inequality.

Let

\[
F=G\setminus E,
\qquad
m=\frac{a-k}{6-k}.
\]

Since the free entries have sum `a-k`, write

\[
b(g)=m+\delta_g\qquad(g\in F),
\qquad
\sum_{g\in F}\delta_g=0.
\]

Finite-group Plancherel gives

\[
a^2+|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
=6\sum_{g\in G}b(g)^2.
\]

But

\[
\sum_g b(g)^2
=k+(6-k)m^2+\sum_{g\in F}\delta_g^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
&=6k+6(6-k)m^2-a^2
+6\sum_{g\in F}\delta_g^2\\
&=\boxed{
\frac{k(6-a)^2}{6-k}
+6\sum_{g\notin E}\left(b(g)-\frac{a-k}{6-k}\right)^2
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Claim 14 is exactly (5.1) with the final nonnegative term dropped. Its equality condition is immediate and unique.

The same argument works for **every finite group**, which exposes the theorem's real content. Let `H` be a finite group of order `n`, `E subset H` with `0<k=|E|<n`, and `b:H->R` satisfy `b=1` on `E`; put `a=sum_H b` and `m=(a-k)/(n-k)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\rho\ne\mathbf1}d_\rho\|\widehat b(\rho)\|_F^2
=
\frac{k(n-a)^2}{n-k}
+n\sum_{g\notin E}(b(g)-m)^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

So the `S_3` result is not a special representation accident. It is the orthogonal distance from an affine edge-fixed slice to the constant functions, expressed in the group Fourier decomposition. The first term is the forced distance caused by fixing `k` entries at one while prescribing the total sum; the second is the within-free-set variance.

This also proves the correction corollary. A function with all nontrivial Fourier coefficients zero is constant by Fourier inversion. If such a correction is zero at any fixed edge location, that constant is zero.

I make no novelty claim for (5.2); it is a Pythagorean/Plancherel identity built from classical finite-group Fourier analysis.
