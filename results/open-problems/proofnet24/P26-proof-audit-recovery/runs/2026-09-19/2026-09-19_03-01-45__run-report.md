## 4. #3 PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: sharp Fejer decrement and all-t convergence

Let C be in E cap C_t. Projection onto C_t gives the variational/Pythagorean inequality

\[
\|B-C\|_F^2\ge \|Z\|_F^2+\|D-C\|_F^2.
\]

Because E is affine and B^+=P_E(D), exact orthogonality gives

\[
\|D-C\|_F^2
=\|D-B^+\|_F^2+\|B^+-C\|_F^2.
\]

Now `D-B^+=-(1-N)\circ Z`, so

\[
\boxed{
\|B^+-C\|_F^2
\le
\|B-C\|_F^2
-\|Z\|_F^2
-\|(1-N)\circ Z\|_F^2.
}
\]

Since

\[
\|Z\|_F^2=\|N\circ Z\|_F^2+\|(1-N)\circ Z\|_F^2,
\]

this is strictly stronger than the owner's displayed decrement by `||N o Z||_F^2`. In particular, in the feasible case

\[
\sum_k\|Z_k\|_F^2<\infty,
\qquad Z_k\to0.
\]

Fejer boundedness supplies a cluster point, continuity of the positive-part map puts every cluster point in C_t, membership in E is closed, and Fejer monotonicity with respect to such a cluster point forces the whole sequence to converge. This independently proves the owner theorem.

There is a stronger global statement: the same iteration converges for every real t, even when E cap C_t is empty. The next section gives a self-contained proof.

## 5. #18 SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: projected gradient on a Moreau envelope

Define

\[
F_t(B)=\frac12\operatorname{dist}(B,\mathcal C_t)^2
=\frac12\|(B-tI)_+\|_F^2.
\]

For a closed convex set in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,

\[
\nabla F_t(B)=B-P_{\mathcal C_t}(B)=(B-tI)_+=Z.
\]

The tangent/free subspace of E is

\[
T=\{H:H_{ii}=0,\ H_{ij}=0\text{ on graph edges}\}.
\]

Its orthogonal projector is `P_T(H)=N o H`. Hence, restricted to E,

\[
\boxed{B^+=B-P_T\nabla F_t(B)}.
\]

So the owner's alternating projection is exactly unit-step gradient descent on the convex smooth function `F_t|_E`.

This formulation makes convergence in the inconsistent case transparent. First, `F_t|_E` is coercive. Otherwise there would be B_n=B_0+H_n in E with ||H_n||_F -> infinity but bounded distance to C_t. Choose Y_n in C_t within bounded distance of B_n and pass to a subsequence with H_n/||H_n|| -> H, ||H||=1. Then Y_n/||H_n|| -> H and `Y_n \preceq tI` implies H \preceq 0. But H is in T, so it has zero diagonal. A negative-semidefinite matrix with zero diagonal is zero: apply the PSD inequality `|(-H)_{ij}|^2 <= (-H)_{ii}(-H)_{jj}=0`. Contradiction. Therefore a minimizer B_* of `F_t|_E` exists for every t.

Second, the tangent gradient is 1-cocoercive. The residual `R=I-P_Ct` is firmly nonexpansive, and for B,C in E their difference lies in T, so

\[
\langle P_T(RB-RC),B-C\rangle
=\langle RB-RC,B-C\rangle
\ge \|RB-RC\|_F^2
\ge \|P_T(RB-RC)\|_F^2.
\]

Thus the gradient-descent map is firmly nonexpansive on E. Relative to any minimizer it is Fejer monotone and the gradient residuals are square-summable. Finite-dimensional compactness plus continuity then gives

\[
\boxed{B^{(k)}\to B_*\in\arg\min_{B\in E}F_t(B)\quad\text{for every real }t.}
\]

This all-t convergence is stronger than Claim 12 and is recorded only as audit diagnosis, not as a separate metric package or novelty claim.

## 6. #20 FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: infeasibility becomes a lower certificate

If E cap C_t is empty, the minimizer B_* from the previous section has

\[
Z_*=(B_*-tI)_+\ne0.
\]

Stationarity of `F_t|_E` is exactly

\[
\boxed{N\circ Z_*=0.}
\]

Thus the failure mode is not divergence. The iteration converges to a fixed point whose positive spectral excess is supported only on the diagonal and graph edges.

Normalize

\[
X=\frac{Z_*}{\operatorname{Tr}Z_*}.
\]

Then X is PSD, has trace 1 and vanishes on graph nonedges, so it is feasible for the owner's theta-primal convention. Since B_* equals 1 on the diagonal and graph edges while Z_* vanishes on nonedges,

\[
\langle J,Z_*\rangle=\langle B_*,Z_*\rangle.
\]

Because B_* and Z_* are simultaneous spectral functions and `Z_*=(B_*-tI)_+`,

\[
B_*Z_*=tZ_*+Z_*^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\langle J,X\rangle
=t+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Z_*^2)}{\operatorname{Tr}Z_*}>t.
}
\]

So below feasibility the same algorithm converges to an explicit strict lower certificate rather than merely failing. This independently recovers the neighboring fixed-point certificate mechanism, but I do not count that neighboring owner claim as a second package in this run.
