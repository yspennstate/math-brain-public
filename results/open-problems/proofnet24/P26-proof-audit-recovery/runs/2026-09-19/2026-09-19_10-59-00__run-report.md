## 2. Source and prior-art boundary, refreshed 2026-09-19

The primary source is Pablo Romero, *Characterization of locally most split reliable graphs*, Theoretical Computer Science 1047 (2025), 115327, arXiv:2503.15453. Romero works with connected simple two-terminal graphs `T_{n,m}` and states that the existence/nonexistence problem remains open beyond the cases settled by Brown--McMullin and Romero's own nonexistence range. Romero proves nonexistence for

`n>=7` and `n <= m <= binom(n-3,2)+3`.

Since the simple complete and complete-minus-one cases were already known positive, the still-open upper range includes `m=binom(n,2)-2`. Thus the all-`n` max-minus-two theorem is genuinely source-responsive; for the source it gives new content from `n>=8` onward, while small `n` overlap known finite classifications.

Brown--McMullin (Networks 82 (2023), 177--185, DOI 10.1002/net.22166) must be read with its graph convention: its headline global theorem allows an edge **multiset**. Its Section 3 separately identifies the simple-graph problem as subtler and leaves additional simple cases open. I do not use the multigraph theorem to declare Romero's simple-graph source solved.

The crucial prior theorem is A. Satyanarayana, L. Schoppmann and C. L. Suffel, *A reliability-improving graph transformation with applications to network reliability*, Networks 22 (1992), 209--216, DOI 10.1002/net.3230220206. Its published abstract states exactly the needed strict extremal fact: if `H` is obtained by deleting `r` independent edges from a complete graph, then for any other graph `G` with the same order and size,

`Rel(H,p) > Rel(G,p)` for every `0<p<1`.

The present audit uses only `r=2`.

Fresh title/arXiv/author/follow-up searches also recover Romero's 2025 papers on ordinary locally most reliable two-terminal graphs and distance-constrained two-terminal reliability, but no later primary paper settling this split-reliability upper diagonal. That is bounded negative evidence only. The 1992 all-terminal extremal theorem is classical prior machinery; priority for this exact split-reliability specialization remains **UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. Negation-first audit: where could the theorem fail?

I first tried to break the theorem at the interfaces where an elegant reduction is most likely to hide a gap.

### 3.1 Class preservation in the edge swap

R06's terminal-edge swap lemma is stated for a graph `G` containing the terminal edge `st` and a missing edge `e`, with `G'=G-st+e`. For a completely arbitrary connected graph, `G'` need not automatically remain connected; so the lemma by itself should not be read as a class-preserving map on every sparse `T_{n,m}`.

This does **not** affect the theorem audited here. At density

`m=M-2`, `M=binom(n,2)`,

`G'` is `K_n` with exactly two edges deleted. For every `n>=4` such a graph is connected. Thus the only place class preservation is needed is safe.

### 3.2 Terminal-isomorphism types

After forcing `st` to be absent, there is exactly one other missing edge. Up to relabeling nonterminals and swapping the terminals, there are only two types:

`A_n = K_n - {st,su}`,

with the second missing edge incident to a terminal, and

`B_n = K_n - {st,uv}`,

with `u,v` nonterminals, so the two deleted edges are independent.

There is no third terminal type hidden by the quotient.

### 3.3 Endpoint and sign checks

The all-terminal theorem is strict only for `0<p<1`, while the source uniform comparison is on `[0,1]`. At `p=0`, no connected `n>=4` graph produces exactly two spanning components; at `p=1`, a connected base graph remains connected. Hence all relevant split reliabilities vanish at both endpoints. The interior strict comparison therefore extends to the required closed interval as a weak inequality.

No counterexample survives these checks.

## 4. Method #25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: the terminal-edge bridge

Let `X` be any graph in which `st` is absent. Couple the random operational subgraph of `X` with the independent state of the added edge `st`.

Count connected operational states of `X+st` in two disjoint classes:

1. the state of `X` is already connected; this contributes `Rel_X(p)` regardless of `st`;
2. the state of `X` is disconnected but becomes connected when `st` is added.

A single added edge can connect a disconnected spanning graph exactly when that graph has exactly two components and the edge joins them. Here that means precisely an `(s,t)` split state of `X`, followed by `st` being operational. Therefore

`Rel_{X+st}(p) = Rel_X(p) + p SR_X^{s,t}(p)`.

Equivalently,

`p SR_X^{s,t}(p) = Rel_{X+st}(p)-Rel_X(p)`.

This is an exact state partition, not an asymptotic or numerical identity.

## 5. Independent proof of the terminal-edge swap inequality

Suppose `st in E(G)`, let `e` be a missing edge, put

`H=G-st=G'-e`, where `G'=G-st+e`,

and condition on one fixed operational state `F` of the common edge set `H`.

For `G`, split success is possible only when `st` fails. If `st` fails, success occurs exactly when `F` already has precisely two components separating `s` and `t`. Hence

`P(G split | F) = (1-p) 1_{F is an (s,t)-split}`.

For `G'`, if `F` is an `(s,t)` split, then:

- if the endpoints of `e` lie in the same split component, either state of `e` preserves the split, giving conditional success probability `1`;
- if `e` crosses the two components, success occurs exactly when `e` fails, giving `1-p`.

If `F` is not already such a split, `G` contributes zero while `G'` contributes a nonnegative probability. Thus pointwise in `F`,

`P(G' split | F) >= P(G split | F)`.

Averaging proves

`SR_{G'}^{s,t}(p) >= SR_G^{s,t}(p)`

for every `p in [0,1]`.

At density `M-2`, as noted above, `G'` remains in the connected simple source class.

## 6. Method #24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

Apply the bridge to the two terminal-missing types. Adding `st` gives

`A_n+st = K_n-su`,

`B_n+st = K_n-uv`.

These are isomorphic one-edge-deleted complete graphs, so their all-terminal reliabilities are identical. Subtracting the two bridge identities gives, for `p>0`,

`p(SR_{A_n}(p)-SR_{B_n}(p)) = Rel(B_n,p)-Rel(A_n,p)`.

Now `B_n` is obtained from `K_n` by deleting the two independent edges `st` and `uv`, whereas `A_n` deletes two adjacent edges. The `r=2` Satyanarayana--Schoppmann--Suffel theorem therefore gives

`Rel(B_n,p) > Rel(A_n,p)` for every `0<p<1`.

Consequently

`SR_{A_n}(p) > SR_{B_n}(p)` for every `0<p<1`.

For an arbitrary `G in T_{n,M-2}`:

- if `st` is already missing, `G` is terminal-isomorphic to `A_n` or `B_n`;
- if `st` is present, one terminal-edge swap produces a connected terminal-missing `G'` with `SR_{G'}>=SR_G`, and `G'` is of type `A_n` or `B_n`.

Since `A_n` weakly dominates both types and strictly dominates `B_n` in the interior,

`SR_{A_n}(p) >= SR_G(p)`

for every `G in T_{n,M-2}` and every `p in [0,1]`.

Thus the audited theorem is valid:

**For every `n>=4`, the diameter-terminal graph `A_n=K_n-{st,su}` is uniformly most split reliable in `T_{n,binom(n,2)-2}`. It strictly beats the other terminal-missing type `B_n` for every `0<p<1`.**

## 7. Method #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: uniqueness test stops short

I tested whether the same proof actually gives a uniqueness theorem for the uniform maximizer. It does **not** do so for free.

The strict Satyanarayana comparison proves only that `A_n` strictly beats `B_n`. The terminal-edge swap is merely weak: for a graph with `st` present, its conditional split probability can equal that of the swapped graph on some or even many states. The current argument therefore gives existence and the stated strict `A_n` versus `B_n` comparison, but it does not by itself exclude every possible `st`-present graph from being split-equivalent to `A_n`.

I found no counterexample to uniqueness, but I also found no proof of it in this route. Accordingly I **do not strengthen the owner theorem to uniqueness**. This is the main proof-diagnosis outcome of method #3: the exact point where the stronger statement would need new information is strictness in the terminal-edge swap, not another all-terminal lemma.
