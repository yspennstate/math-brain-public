## 4. Independent reconstruction of the exact 64-mask identity

Work in the clean free-action family

\[
p\equiv5\pmod{24},
\qquad
q=\frac{p-5}{24},
\]

so the two-localized vertex set splits into `q` regular `S_3`-orbits.

Let `G=S_3`. A local edge-mask rule assigns to every subset `E subseteq G` a real function

\[
b_E:G\to\mathbb R
\]

with the edge-fixed condition

\[
b_E(g)=1\quad(g\in E).
\]

Use the unnormalized finite-group Fourier transform and write

\[
a_E=\sum_g b_E(g),
\quad
s_E=\sum_g b_E(g)\operatorname{sgn}(g),
\quad
U_E=\sum_g b_E(g)\rho_{\rm std}(g),
\]

where `rho_std` is any orthogonal two-dimensional standard representation. Put

\[
\mu=\frac1{64}\sum_E a_E,
\qquad
c=\frac{\mu-3}{3}.
\]

Plancherel on `S_3`, whose irreducible dimensions are `1,1,2`, gives for each mask

\[
a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2
=6\sum_{g\in G}b_E(g)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now average over all 64 masks. Every fixed group element is an edge in exactly 32 masks and a nonedge in exactly 32 masks. Therefore the edge-fixed entries contribute exactly

\[
6\cdot\frac1{64}\sum_E |E|=6\cdot3=18.
\]

The free entries satisfy

\[
\frac1{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}b_E(g)=\mu-3
\]

and there are, on average, three free entries per mask. Completing the square around `c=(mu-3)/3` gives

\[
\frac1{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}b_E(g)^2
=3c^2+
\frac1{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)-c)^2.
\]

Substituting into (4.1) yields the exact owner identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{64}\sum_E
\left(a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2\right)
=
18+2(\mu-3)^2+
\frac6{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)-c)^2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No inequality has entered yet.

If the rule is centered, `mu=0`, then `c=-1`, so

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{64}\sum_E
\left(a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2\right)
=
36+
\frac6{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)+1)^2
\ge36.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equality holds if and only if every free value equals `-1`. Because every edge value is fixed at `+1`, the unique centered minimizer is exactly

\[
\boxed{
b_E(g)=2\mathbf1_E(g)-1.}
\]

This independently proves the exact algebraic core of Claim 15.

## 6. Method #3 — stronger two-obstruction dichotomy

The owner states the eventual absolute operator-norm floor. The same argument gives a stronger diagnostic that keeps the trivial and Frobenius obstructions separate.

Let `B_p` be a symmetric, uniformly bounded, `S_3`-equivariant mask-local edge-fixed completion on the regular-orbit family. Let `mu_p` be the uniform-mask mean trivial coefficient of its local rule. Claim 13's uniform six-bit pattern law implies, for every target orbit and every fixed bounded translation-invariant mask statistic, empirical row averages equal the uniform 64-mask average up to `O_K(p^{-1/2})`, where `K` bounds the local entries.

For a block determined by `b_E`, each row sum is `a_E`. Hence the all-ones Rayleigh quotient gives

\[
\boxed{
\|B_p\|_{\rm op}
\ge q|\mu_p|-O_K(\sqrt p).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the other hand, a regular `6 x 6` group-circulant block determined by `b_E` has

\[
\|B_E\|_F^2
=6\sum_g b_E(g)^2
=a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2.
\]

Applying the rowwise pattern law to this bounded statistic and summing over the `q x q` orbit blocks gives

\[
\|B_p\|_F^2
\ge q^2\left(18+2(\mu_p-3)^2-O_K(p^{-1/2})\right).
\]

Since the full regular part has dimension `6q`,

\[
\boxed{
\|B_p\|_{\rm op}^2
\ge
\frac q6
\left(18+2(\mu_p-3)^2-O_K(p^{-1/2})\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equations (6.1)--(6.2) are a two-obstruction version of Claim 15. If a sequence has `\|B_p\|_op=O(sqrt(p))`, then (6.1) forces

\[
\mu_p=O_K(p^{-1/2}).
\]

Substitution into (6.2) gives

\[
\|B_p\|_{\rm op}^2
\ge6q-o(p)
=\frac{p-5}{4}-o(p),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|B_p\|_{\rm op}
\ge\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\sqrt p.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This independently reconstructs the owner's global mask-local obstruction and slightly strengthens its diagnosis: a rule either leaves a nonnegligible mean trivial coefficient and pays an `Omega(p)` trivial-sector norm, or it asymptotically centers and pays the `1/2 sqrt(p)` Frobenius/absolute-norm floor.

The strengthening receives **no additional metric point** and carries **no novelty claim**.

Crucially, (6.3) controls the largest absolute eigenvalue, not the positive spectral edge. A completion can in principle pay the forced norm on a large negative eigenvalue. Hence this theorem cannot by itself prove the source's one-sided theta upper bound.

## 7. Method #28 — typical masks are enough, and this identifies the exact scope boundary

The local identity is an average over an abstract uniform distribution on all 64 masks. It becomes a theorem about the Paley localization only because the previously proved block-Bernoulli law says that a fixed target orbit sees each six-bit mask with frequency

\[
\frac p{256}+O(\sqrt p),
\]

uniformly in the mask.

For a **uniformly bounded** mask-local rule, the statistics used above (`a_E`, the Fourier energy, and the block Frobenius energy) are uniformly bounded. Therefore the `O(sqrt(p))` discrepancy in each of 64 mask counts becomes only `O(p^{-1/2})` after row normalization. Exact equidistribution is unnecessary; typical-mask equidistribution at square-root error suffices.

This also identifies why the boundedness hypothesis is not cosmetic. If one lets the local completion values grow with `p`, a rule can put very large mass on the masks whose empirical counts deviate from `p/256`; the square-root count error can then be amplified until the transfer from the uniform 64-mask identity to the actual Paley matrix is no longer `o(1)`. Claim 15 correctly scopes the global operator-floor corollary to bounded mask-local rules.

This is the right failure diagnosis: the theorem rules out a large, natural finite-local ansatz class, but it does not rule out completions whose coefficients depend on orbit-pair arithmetic or whose nonlocal correlations grow with the prime. Those are precisely the kinds of coherent high-rank effects left in P04's final handoff.
