## 4. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST — failure modes checked

Before accepting the theorem I tried to falsify each convention-sensitive step.

### 4.1 Conference normalization

For `p == 1 mod 4`, `chi(-1)=1`, so `C` is symmetric. Exact character correlation gives

`C^2=pI-J`.

The diagonal is `p-1`, matching the right side `p-1`; distinct rows have inner product `-1`. There is no missing factor of 2 or sign convention.

### 4.2 Two anchor columns

For every `x in A`,

`C_{x0}=chi(x)=1`,

`C_{x1}=chi(x-1)=1`.

Thus the two anchor columns each contribute `J_A` to the `A x A` block of `C^2`. With the remaining exterior block `U`,

`C_A^2 + UU^T + 2J_A = pI_A - J_A`,

so

`C_A^2+UU^T=pI_A-3J_A`.

The coefficient 3 is therefore exact: one `J` comes from the full conference identity and two more from the anchors.

### 4.3 Theta support convention

For the convention `theta(overline G)` used by the owner, theta-feasible off-diagonal entries may survive only on edges of `G`. On every localized Paley edge, `C_A(x,y)=+1`; on the diagonal `C_A=0`. Hence

If the theta zero pattern were reversed this identity would fail, so this was an essential scope check; the owner and P03 audit use the same correct convention.

### 4.4 Positivity of the exterior term

`UU^T` is PSD, and for PSD `X`,

`E=Tr(UU^T X)=Tr(U^T X U)>=0`.

No entrywise positivity assumption on `X` is being smuggled in.

### 4.5 State variance inequality

`Tr[X(M-mu I)^2] >= 0`.

Expanding gives

No literal-scope counterexample survived these tests.
