## 4. Independent reconstruction of the positive theorem

Let

`delta = sqrt(6 k log n)`,

`q = (k-delta)/(n-1)`.

Since `k/log n -> infinity`, eventually `k>2 delta`, so `0<q<1`. Retain every complete-graph edge whose weight is at most `q`; call the threshold degree at vertex `v` by `B_v`. Then

`B_v ~ Bin(n-1,q)`, `E B_v = k-delta`.

The vertex degrees `B_v` are not independent, because adjacent vertices share an edge. Nothing below requires their independence.

For one vertex, standard Bernstein/Chernoff inequalities give

`P(B_v >= k) <= exp(-delta^2/(2(k-delta)+2delta/3)) <= n^-3`,

because the denominator is at most `2k`, and

`P(B_v <= k-2delta) <= exp(-delta^2/(2(k-delta))) <= n^-3`.

Thus a union bound over the `n` vertices gives the event

`A = {k-2delta <= B_v <= k for every v}`

with

`P(A) >= 1-2/n^2`.

On `A`, every threshold edge is kept by `LK(n,k)`. Indeed, if edge `{u,v}` has weight at most `q`, then it is one of the `B_u <= k` threshold edges incident to `u` and one of the `B_v <= k` threshold edges incident to `v`; with continuous weights it therefore has rank at most `k` at both endpoints. Consequently, on `A`, simultaneously for all vertices,

`k-2delta <= D_v <= k`.

The upper bound `D_v<=k` is deterministic in the source model.

Now center at the true mean. Since `D_v>=0` always and `D_v>=k-2delta` on `A`,

`mu_n = E D_v >= (k-2delta) P(A) >= (k-2delta)(1-2/n^2)`.

Also `mu_n<=k`. Hence

`0 <= k-mu_n <= 2delta + 2(k-2delta)/n^2 <= 2delta+2k/n^2`.

On `A`, for every `v`,

`mu_n-D_v <= k-(k-2delta)=2delta`,

while

`D_v-mu_n <= k-mu_n <= 2delta+2k/n^2`.

Therefore

`max_v |D_v-mu_n| <= 2delta+2k/n^2`

on `A`. Dividing by the displayed positive lower bound on `mu_n` gives exactly the owner's `r_n`. Finally

`delta/k = sqrt(6 log n/k) -> 0`,

so `r_n->0`, and the complement of `A` has probability at most `2/n^2 -> 0`.

The theorem is therefore correct as stated.

## 5. Method #2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I tried to break each place where the dependence structure could matter.

### Shared-edge dependence

The threshold degrees `B_v` are dependent across vertices, but the proof uses only one-vertex binomial tails plus a union bound. Independence across vertices is never invoked.

### Threshold edge to bilateral edge

This implication is deterministic on `A`. A threshold edge incident to `u` belongs to a set of at most `k` threshold edges at `u`, so its local rank is at most `k`; likewise at the other endpoint. No exchangeability or conditional independence is needed.

### Mean bridge on the bad event

A rare bad event cannot shift the mean by macroscopic relative size, because `0<=D_v<=k` deterministically. The lower bound

`mu_n >= (k-2delta)P(A)`

requires no independence between `D_v` and `A`.

### Slowly superlogarithmic and near-complete regimes

The most adverse admissible sequences are the slowest ones with `k/log n -> infinity`; nevertheless `delta/k=sqrt(6log n/k)->0`. At the other endpoint, `k=n-1` is deterministic `K_n`, and the threshold argument only becomes easier. No endpoint or integer-rounding counterexample survived.

### Negation verdict

No counterexample and no proof defect was found in the claimed regime.

The source itself gives the expected obstruction on the other side: for `k=floor(t log n)` with `0<t<1`, isolated vertices occur with high probability, while the source's mean asymptotics keep `mu_n>0`; hence simultaneous relative concentration around the mean cannot hold throughout the whole logarithmic region. This does not prove the positive regime sharp.

## 6. Method #11 — DUALIZE: work with degree deficits from the deterministic ceiling

The cleanest independent proof language is the deficit

`X_v := k-D_v >= 0`,

and its deterministic mean

`a_n := k-mu_n = E X_v`.

On the good event `A`,

`0 <= X_v <= 2delta` for every vertex.

Also, because `X_v<=k` always,

`a_n <= 2delta P(A)+kP(A^c) <= 2delta + 2k/n^2`.

The centered degree is exactly

`D_v-mu_n = a_n-X_v`.

Thus on `A`, both terms lie in a short interval from zero and

`|D_v-mu_n| = |a_n-X_v| <= max(a_n,X_v) <= 2delta+2k/n^2`.

This deficit formulation makes the proof's real mechanism transparent: the hard dependence never has to be decorrelated. One only needs a common high-probability deterministic strip below the hard ceiling `k`, plus a deterministic cap on the bad-event contribution to the mean.
