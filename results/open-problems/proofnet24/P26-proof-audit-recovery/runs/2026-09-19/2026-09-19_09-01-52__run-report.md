## 3. Independent reconstruction of the frozen quartic theorem

Fix integers `d>=2`, `m>=1`, and let

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_m\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}{\rm Haar}(U(d)).
\]

Use the columns of `U_k` as the proposed basis vectors. For every real polynomial of degree at most four in all real and imaginary vector coordinates, define

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[p]=\mathbb E[p(U_1,\ldots,U_m)].
\]

Because this is an actual expectation,

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}[f^2]\ge0\qquad (\deg f\le2).
\]

Every same-basis orthonormality relation is quadratic and holds pointwise for each sampled unitary matrix. Therefore it vanishes after multiplication by every polynomial allowed by the degree-four truncation.

For a column `x` from one basis and a column `y` from a distinct independent basis, conditioning on `x` gives

\[
\mathbb E_y |\langle x,y\rangle|^2
=x^*\,\mathbb E(yy^*)\,x
=x^*(I/d)x
=1/d.
\]

Therefore

\[
\widetilde{\mathbb E}\left[|\langle x,y\rangle|^2-1/d\right]=0.
\]

The bracket is already quartic in the real vector coordinates. At degree four it admits only constant multipliers. Hence every cross-basis quartic equality is satisfied to the full degree-four pseudoexpectation definition.

A degree-four SoS refutation would be an identity whose square terms have nonnegative pseudoexpectation and whose constraint multiples have zero pseudoexpectation, while the left side is `-1`. Applying `\widetilde E` would give `-1>=0`, a contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{the direct quartic vector-coordinate system has no degree-four SoS refutation for any }d,m.}
\]

For `d=6,m=7`, there are `C(7,2)=21` labeled cross-basis blocks and `21*36=756` quartic cross equations. One single joint pseudoexpectation satisfies all 756 at once, so the frozen P21 metric is exactly `0/21` eliminated blocks.

## 4. Falsification pass before acceptance

I attacked the literal statement at the places where a low-degree pseudoexpectation proof most often hides a scope error.

**Normalization.** If one literally imposed the source typo `|<x,y>|^2=1/sqrt(6)`, the Haar expectation would fail: `E|<x,y>|^2=1/6`. The theorem therefore depends on correcting the typo, and the correction is independently forced by the source definition, by the source LMI, and by Sarkar Remark 2.1.

**Degree bookkeeping.** The quartic cross equation cannot be multiplied by a nonconstant quadratic at degree four. Doing so would move to a stronger hierarchy and invalidate the argument. The P21/Sarkar convention correctly allows only constants against the quartic cross relation.

**Joint versus pairwise feasibility.** It is not enough to show that each of 21 blocks separately has some pseudoexpectation. The construction uses one joint probability space of seven independent Haar unitary matrices, so all same-basis constraints and all 756 cross equations are satisfied by one and the same truncated functional.

**Same-basis correlations.** The finite two-vector checker does not by itself encode the correlations among six columns of one unitary basis. The theorem does not need it to: those correlations are handled pointwise by sampling entire Haar unitary matrices, not independent columns within a basis.

No counterexample survives inside the stated frozen quartic scope.
