## 3. NEW-METHOD #13 — a two-defect Lyapunov proof

I did not accept the R06 proof merely because its algebra looked familiar. I searched for a different proof invariant.

Let `G` be an infinite simple `d`-regular graph. For finite nonempty `K`, write

\[
E(K)=|\partial_E K|,
\qquad
V(K)=|\partial_V K|,
\qquad
h=\Phi_E(G).
\]

Assume

\[
 h\ge d-3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Start from arbitrary finite nonempty `K_0`. Whenever there is a vertex `v` outside the current set `K` with

\[
r=|N(v)\cap K|\ge2,
\]

adjoin `v`.

The exact edge-boundary update is

\[
E(K\cup\{v\})-E(K)=d-2r.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now define the **edge-expansion defect**

\[
D_E(K):=E(K)-h|K|.
\]

By definition of `h`, every finite nonempty `K` has `D_E(K)>=0`. But one closure step satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_E(K\cup\{v\})-D_E(K)
&=d-2r-h\\
&\le d-4-h\\
&\le -1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus a nonnegative defect drops by at least one at every step. **Termination is immediate**, with at most `floor(D_E(K_0))+1` possible additions. This is an independent route to the termination statement and is cleaner than the cumulative-size estimate used in R06/R05.

For the outer vertex boundary, adjoining `v` removes `v` from the old boundary and can expose at most its `d-r` outside neighbors, so

\[
V(K\cup\{v\})-V(K)\le -1+(d-r).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Define the second defect

\[
D_V(K):=V(K)-h|K|.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_V(K\cup\{v\})-D_V(K)
&\le d-r-1-h\\
&\le d-3-h\\
&\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

So `D_V` is nonincreasing along the closure process.

At the terminal set `K_*`, every outside boundary vertex has at least one neighbor in `K_*` by definition and at most one by terminality. Hence every such vertex has exactly one neighbor inside. Because the graph is simple, crossing edges and outer boundary vertices are in bijection:

\[
V(K_*)=E(K_*).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
D_V(K_*)=D_E(K_*)\ge0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Since `D_V` only decreased on the way from `K_0` to `K_*`,

\[
D_V(K_0)\ge D_V(K_*)\ge0.
\]

Thus

\[
V(K_0)\ge h|K_0|.
\]

As `K_0` was arbitrary, `Phi_V(G)>=h=Phi_E(G)`. The reverse inequality `Phi_V<=Phi_E` is automatic because every outside boundary vertex supports at least one crossing edge. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Phi_V(G)=\Phi_E(G)\quad\text{whenever }\Phi_E(G)\ge d-3.}
\]

This independently proves `R06-I0056-CLOSURE-01`.

## 4. NEW-METHOD #18 — say the proof as a bootstrap-closure theorem

The same argument becomes more transparent in a different language. The iterative operation is exactly a **2-neighbor bootstrap closure of a finite set**, except that we use it as a deterministic proof device rather than a stochastic growth process.

In that language:

- `D_E=boundary excess above the global edge-Cheeger floor` is a strictly decreasing Lyapunov function;
- `D_V=outer-boundary excess above the same floor` cannot increase;
- the closure stops exactly when every exterior boundary point has multiplicity one;
- multiplicity one converts edge boundary into vertex boundary without loss.

The theorem is therefore not a mysterious identity special to hyperbolic tilings. It is a general high-expansion closure principle on infinite simple regular graphs. Planarity and face structure enter only afterwards, through the source's exact formula for `Phi_E`.

This reformulation also exposes the genuine scope conditions. If multiple edges were allowed, terminal multiplicity-one **vertices** need not correspond one-for-one with crossing **edges**. If the source used a finite-graph half-volume Cheeger convention, the closure could leave the admissible size range. Neither issue occurs in the infinite simple tessellation scope actually audited here.

## 5. NEW-METHOD #30 — one face degree at a time; the exact failure boundary

I then tested the source corollary one face-degree regime at a time rather than treating the formula as a single symbolic block.

Byung-Geun Oh's sharp edge-isoperimetric theorem gives, for the regular/co-regular tessellation parameters `(d,f)` in the source class,

\[
\Phi_E(d,f)
=(d-2)\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{(d-2)(f-2)}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `d>=4` and `f>=5`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_E(d,f)^2-(d-3)^2
&=2d-5-\frac{4(d-2)}{f-2}\\
&\ge 2d-5-\frac{4(d-2)}3\\
&=\frac{2d-7}{3}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `d=3`, the plane-tessellation admissibility condition forces `f>=6`, and the required threshold is merely `Phi_E>=0=d-3`. Thus every admissible `f>=5` source case satisfies the closure theorem and

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_V(d,f)=
(d-2)\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{(d-2)(f-2)}}
\qquad(f\ge5).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The neighboring `f=4` scale is an exact adversarial boundary, not a cosmetic exception:

\[
\Phi_E(d,4)^2-(d-3)^2=-1
\tag{5.4}
\]

for every `d>=4`. The closure hypothesis fails exactly there, and the known Haslegrave–Panagiotis quadrangular vertex constants are not equal to the edge constants. The triangular/quadrangular cases `f=3,4` are precisely the already-published branch.

Thus the new theorem glues cleanly onto the known literature rather than overwriting it:

- `f=3,4`: Haslegrave–Panagiotis;
- `f>=5`: the audited R06 closure theorem plus Oh's edge formula.

The first regular/co-regular clause of Question 6.20 is therefore mathematically accounted for in this package/literature combination. The general-transitive second clause remains OPEN.
