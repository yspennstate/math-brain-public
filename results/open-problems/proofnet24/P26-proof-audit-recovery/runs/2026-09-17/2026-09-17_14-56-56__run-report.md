## 3. Independent proof audit

I reconstructed the proof from the statement and source geometry rather than accepting P11's conclusion.

### 3.1 Heavy-tail law

Define

\[
\Pr(T=1)=\frac12,
\qquad
\Pr(T=2^{s^3})=2^{-s-1},\quad s\ge1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The masses sum to

\[
\frac12+\sum_{s\ge1}2^{-s-1}=1.
\]

Thus `T` is nonnegative and finite almost surely, and `F(0)=0`.

For any `gamma>0`,

\[
E e^{\gamma T}\ge 2^{-s-1}e^{\gamma 2^{s^3}}
\]

for every `s`; the displayed lower-bound terms do not tend to zero. Hence `E exp(gamma T)=infinity` for every positive `gamma`.

Set

\[
a_r=2^{r^3}.
\]

Then the tail is exact:

\[
q_r:=\Pr(T\ge a_r)
=\sum_{s\ge r}2^{-s-1}
=2^{-r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### 3.2 Corrected-height geometry

For `k>=l`, take

\[
m(k,l)=\max\{1,\lfloor\sqrt{\log_2 l}\rfloor\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then `m(k,l)->infinity` as `k>=l->infinity`. Moreover, for every fixed `delta<1`,

\[
k^{-1+\delta}\log m(k,l)
\le l^{-1+\delta}O(\log\log l)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the family is safely in the corrected sub-surface-exponential height regime used by the package.

Choose the diagonal subsequence

\[
N_r=2^{r^2},\qquad k=l=N_r.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then `m(N_r,N_r)=r` exactly.

### 3.3 Many independent high columns

The box `[0,N_r]^2 x [0,r]` has

\[
M_r=(N_r+1)^2
\]

pairwise edge-disjoint vertical bottom-to-top columns, each using exactly `r` vertical edges.

Call a column `r`-high if all its `r` edges have capacity at least `a_r`. By (3.2),

\[
\Pr(\text{column is r-high})=q_r^r=2^{-r^2}=\frac1{N_r}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Disjoint columns depend on disjoint edge sets, hence the column indicators are independent. If `Y_r` counts high columns,

\[
Y_r\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left((N_r+1)^2,\frac1{N_r}\right),
\qquad
\mu_r:=EY_r=\frac{(N_r+1)^2}{N_r}\ge N_r.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Since `Var(Y_r)<=mu_r`, Chebyshev gives

\[
\Pr(Y_r<\mu_r/2)
\le \frac{4}{\mu_r}
\le \frac4{N_r}
=4\,2^{-r^2}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The right-hand side is summable. The first Borel-Cantelli lemma therefore implies

\[
Y_r\ge\mu_r/2
\]

for all sufficiently large `r`, almost surely. No independence across different values of `r` is needed.

### 3.4 B19 — make the obstruction constructive

The witness is an explicit algorithmic flow, not an existential estimate. On every `r`-high vertical column, send `a_r` units upward on every vertical edge, and send zero on all other edges. The selected columns are edge-disjoint; every used edge has capacity at least `a_r`; and conservation holds at every interior column vertex. Thus this is a feasible bottom-to-top flow of value

\[
a_rY_r.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Phi_r\ge a_rY_r.
\tag{3.9}
\]

On the probability-one eventual event from (3.8),

\[
\frac{\Phi_r}{N_r^2}
\ge
\frac{a_r\mu_r}{2N_r^2}
\ge
\frac{a_r}{2N_r}
=2^{r^3-r^2-1}
\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Every finite box has finite flow because every edge capacity is finite almost surely. Thus the failure is genuinely asymptotic: the normalized sequence cannot converge to the finite deterministic limit of the exponential-moment theorem.

### 3.5 B25 — count the same obstruction twice

I also derived (3.9) from the dual side, independently of the explicit flow assignment.

Any edge cut separating bottom from top must meet every vertical bottom-to-top column. In particular, it meets every `r`-high column. Different columns are edge-disjoint, so one may choose a distinct cut edge from each high column. Each such edge has capacity at least `a_r`. Hence every separating cut has total capacity at least

\[
a_rY_r.
\]

Max-flow/min-cut gives again

\[
\Phi_r\ge a_rY_r.
\]

The primal channel construction and the dual cut count therefore certify the same lower bound for independent structural reasons. This rules out a hidden artifact of the particular flow routing.
