The tail mass is

\[
\frac23\cdot\frac{1+t}{2}=\frac{1+t}{3}.

## 6. NEW-METHODS #15 and #25 — break symmetry, then count twice

The necessity argument should not secretly assume the complete-mix coupling is exchangeable. I therefore deliberately broke that symmetry: let `(X_1,X_2,X_3)` be **any** complete mix with identical marginal law `f_{k,t}`, with no exchangeability assumption. Then

\[
X_1+X_2+X_3=1\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Define

\[
N=\#\{i:X_i>1/3\}.
\]

Count `N` two ways. First, pathwise: if `N=0`, then every `X_i<=1/3`; since their sum is exactly one, all three must equal `1/3`. The marginal law has a density and hence no atom at `1/3`, so this event has probability zero. Thus

\[
N\ge1\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Second, by linearity of expectation and equality of the **marginal laws only**,

\[
E[N]
=\sum_{i=1}^3P(X_i>1/3)
=3c.
\]

Therefore

\[
1\le E[N]=3c,
\qquad c\ge1/3,
\qquad t\ge0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is immune to nonexchangeable dependence. It is the exact necessary half of (3.2).

For audit reuse, the same proof gives the elementary boundary-tail lemma: if an atomless law on `[a,b]` has lower-boundary mean `a+(b-a)/n` and is `n`-CM, then

\[
P\!\left(X>a+\frac{b-a}{n}\right)\ge\frac1n.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Proof: affine-normalize to `[0,1]`; in a complete mix the normalized coordinates sum to one a.s.; if all are at most `1/n`, equality forces every coordinate to equal `1/n`, a null event; count the number exceeding `1/n` and take expectations. I record (6.2) as an **audit lemma, not a novelty claim**.

Verdict on the necessity half: **PASS**.
