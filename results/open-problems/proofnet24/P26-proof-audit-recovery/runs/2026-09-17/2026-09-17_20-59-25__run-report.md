## 3. Frozen claim

For prime `p == 5 mod 24`, let `chi` be the quadratic character of `F_p` and

\[
V=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}.
\]

Let the anharmonic group be generated by

\[
r(x)=1-x,\qquad s(x)=x^{-1},
\]

and define

\[
J(x)=\frac{(x^2-x+1)^3}{x^2(1-x)^2}.
\]

P04 claims that the resulting `S_3` action on `V` is free, `J` is constant on and separates its six-element orbits, so `|J(V)|=(p-5)/24`, and for distinct orbit representatives `x,y`, with

\[
k(x,y)=\#\{g\in S_3:\chi(x-g y)=1\},
\]

one has

\[
\chi(J(x)-J(y))=(-1)^{k(x,y)}.
\]

Hence the graph on orbit labels in which two labels are adjacent exactly when `k` is even is the Paley graph induced on `U_p=J(V)`.

## 4. Method #14 — find the group acting

The first audit task is to verify that the group action really preserves the source-localized vertex set; otherwise all orbit language is cosmetic.

Because `p == 5 mod 24`, in particular `p == 1 mod 4`, so `chi(-1)=1`. If `x in V`, then

\[
\chi(1-x)=\chi(-1)\chi(x-1)=1,
\qquad
\chi((1-x)-1)=\chi(-x)=1.
\]

Thus `r(V)=V`. Also

\[
\chi(x^{-1})=\chi(x)=1,
\qquad
\chi(x^{-1}-1)
=\chi((1-x)/x)=1,
\]

so `s(V)=V`. The usual relations `r^2=s^2=(rs)^3=1` give the anharmonic `S_3` action.

The only possible nontrivial stabilizers in `V` come from the three involution classes' finite fixed points

\[
-1,\quad2,\quad1/2,
\]

or the two order-three elements' fixed points, the roots of

\[
x^2-x+1=0.
\]

Now `p == 5 mod 8`, so `(2/p)=-1`. Consequently `2` and `1/2` are not in `V`; for `-1`, the second condition is `chi(-2)=-1`, so it is not in `V` either. Also `p == 2 mod 3`, so `F_p^*` has no element of order 3 and `x^2-x+1` has no root. Therefore every stabilizer is trivial and every nonempty orbit has size six.

This part of the theorem is correct.

## 5. Method #25 — count the same thing twice

### 5.1 Character-sum count of `V`

Since endpoints 0 and 1 are excluded,

\[
|V|=\frac14\sum_{x\ne0,1}(1+\chi(x))(1+\chi(x-1)).
\]

The constant term is `p-2`. The two linear character sums are both `-1`: the complete nontrivial character sum is zero, and the one omitted nonzero endpoint contributes `1`. Finally

\[
\sum_x\chi(x(x-1))=-1
\]

for the nondegenerate quadratic `x(x-1)`; the two excluded endpoints contribute zero. Hence

\[
|V|=\frac{p-5}{4}.
\]

Together with freeness this gives

\[
|V/S_3|=\frac{|V|}{6}=\frac{p-5}{24}.
\]

### 5.2 Invariant count through `J`

Direct substitution gives `J(1-x)=J(x)` and `J(1/x)=J(x)`, so `J` is constant on each orbit. The exact polynomial identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(x^2-x+1)^3y^2(1-y)^2-(y^2-y+1)^3x^2(1-x)^2\\
&=(x-y)(xy-1)(x+y-1)(xy-x+1)(xy-x-y)(xy-y+1)
\end{aligned}
\]

shows that equality `J(x)=J(y)` forces one of the six factors to vanish. Since `x,y` avoid 0 and 1, solving those six equations gives exactly

\[
x\in\left\{y,1-y,1/y,1/(1-y),y/(y-1),(y-1)/y\right\}.
\]

So `J` separates distinct orbits. Therefore `|J(V)|=|V/S_3|=(p-5)/24`, independently matching the orbit count.

## 6. The quadratic-character parity identity

For distinct orbits, all six differences below are nonzero. Relative to the six transforms of `y`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x-y&=x-y,\\
x-(1-y)&=x+y-1,\\
x-y^{-1}&=(xy-1)/y,\\
x-(1-y)^{-1}&=-(xy-x+1)/(1-y),\\
x-y/(y-1)&=(xy-x-y)/(y-1),\\
x-(y-1)/y&=(xy-y+1)/y.
\end{aligned}
\]

On `V`, both `y` and `y-1` are squares. Since `chi(-1)=1`, `1-y=-(y-1)` is also a square, and the explicit minus sign contributes character `+1`. Therefore the character of the product of the six differences equals the character of the six polynomial factors in the displayed factorization.

The denominator of `J(x)-J(y)` is

\[
x^2(1-x)^2y^2(1-y)^2,
\]

also a square. Hence

\[
\chi(J(x)-J(y))
=\prod_{g\in S_3}\chi(x-g y).
\]

Exactly `k=k(x,y)` factors are `+1` and `6-k` are `-1`, so

\[
\prod_g\chi(x-g y)=(-1)^{6-k}=(-1)^k.
\]

Because `p == 1 mod 4`, the Paley adjacency convention is precisely `chi(a-b)=+1`. Thus the induced Paley graph on the distinct labels `J(V)` joins two labels exactly when `k` is even. The formal theorem is correct.

## 8. Terminology correction found by the audit

There is one wording issue worth freezing because it could cause a future proof error.

The formal quantity

\[
k(x,y)=\#\{g:\chi(x-g y)=1\}
\]

is the number of neighbors that **one vertex `x`** has in the opposite six-element orbit. It is a cross-orbit **row valency**. By `S_3` symmetry the same `k` holds for every vertex in the first orbit, so the total number of undirected edges between the two six-element orbits is

\[
6k.
\]

Therefore phrases such as "cross-orbit edge count `k`" are potentially misleading if read as the total block edge count: that total is always even and cannot encode the claimed parity. The theorem's formal definition (4.5) and its conclusion use the row valency `k`, so this is a terminology correction, not a mathematical failure. Any downstream quotient argument must preserve this distinction.
