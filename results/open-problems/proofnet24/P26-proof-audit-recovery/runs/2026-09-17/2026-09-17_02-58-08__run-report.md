## 4. NEW-METHOD #1 — change category: percolation to a branching process

Ignore the stem first. At the root of the infinite binary tail, open offspring have the exact distribution `Binomial(2,p)`. If `q` is the extinction probability, then

\[
q=(1-p+pq)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The polynomial factors exactly as

\[
q-(1-p+pq)^2
=-p^2(q-1)\left(q-\frac{(1-p)^2}{p^2}\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `p<=1/2`, the second algebraic root is at least one, so the physical extinction probability is `q=1`. For `p>1/2`, the second root lies in `(0,1)`. The usual Galton-Watson extinction probability is the smallest fixed point in `[0,1]`, so

\[
q(p)=\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\right)^2,
\qquad p>1/2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the binary-tail survival probability is

\[
\beta(p)=1-q(p)
=\frac{2p-1}{p^2},
\qquad p>1/2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This also fixes the physical branch explicitly: the always-present extinction root `q=1` is not incorrectly selected above criticality.

## 5. NEW-METHOD #4 — prove the periodic-tree clause completely

### 5.1 Source periodicity

For every `k`, `T_k` is `k`-periodic in Tang's exact Definition 4.1.

If `x=v_i` is a stem vertex, `0<=i<=k`, take the identity map `T^x -> T^x`. It is an adjacency-preserving bijection, sends `x` to itself, and `|x|=i<=k`.

If `x` is a strict descendant of `v_k`, then `T^x` and `T^{v_k}` are both full rooted binary trees. Choose any rooted binary-tree isomorphism `T^x -> T^{v_k}`. It sends `x` to `v_k`, whose depth is exactly `k`. Hence every vertex has the source-required forward-subtree bijection to a root at depth at most `k`.

Thus `T_k` is an infinite, locally finite periodic tree under the source definition. No transitivity or strong-connectivity assumption has been added.

### 5.2 Percolation probability

For the root `o=v_0` to reach infinity, all `k` stem edges must be open and the binary tail from `v_k` must survive. These events use disjoint edge sets. Therefore

\[
\theta_k(p)=p^k\beta(p).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Combining with (4.4), for `p>1/2`,

\[
\theta_k(p)=p^{k-2}(2p-1).
\tag{5.2}
\]

For `p<=1/2`, the binary tail dies almost surely, so `theta_k(p)=0`. A finite stem cannot change the positivity threshold of survival, hence

\[
p_c(T_k)=1/2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `k=3`,

\[
\theta_3(p)=p(2p-1)=2p^2-p,
\qquad p\in(1/2,1),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\theta_3''(p)=4>0\quad\text{for every }p\in(1/2,1).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

So `theta_3` is strictly convex on the entire supercritical interval. This is a direct counterexample to concavity in Tang's periodic-tree class.

As a boundary falsification check, `k=2` gives `theta_2(p)=2p-1`, whose second derivative is zero. Thus the positive curvature is not being smuggled in through a differentiation error at the shortest neutral stem; `k=3` is the first member of this simple stem family with strictly positive curvature.
