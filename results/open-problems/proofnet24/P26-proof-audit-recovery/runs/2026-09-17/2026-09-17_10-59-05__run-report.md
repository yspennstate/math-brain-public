## 4. NEW-METHOD #18 — say it in another language: finite BV-budget feasibility

The useful reformulation is not “find a coloring.” It is a finite convex-feasibility problem for directional BV seminorms.

Let `K` be a nonempty bounded open convex set and let `u_1,...,u_N` be a finite list containing a coordinate basis. Fix budgets `b_i>0`. For a probability density `rho` supported in `K`, write `V_{u_i}(rho)` for directional total variation. Define

\[
S:=\{z\in\mathbb R^N:\exists\rho\in\mathcal P(K),\ z_i\ge V_{u_i}(\rho)\ \forall i\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The correct common-density lemma is:

> If for every `beta_i>=0`, with `H_beta(xi)=sum_i beta_i |u_i cdot xi|`, one has
> \[
> h_{H_\beta}(K)\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i,
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> then there is one probability density `rho` supported in `K` satisfying `V_{u_i}(rho)<=b_i` for all `i`.

### Full repair of R16-BF-01

`S` is convex and coordinatewise upward closed. It is also **closed**, which is the point omitted from the R16 paragraph. Indeed, suppose `z^(k) in S` and `z^(k)->z`. Choose witnessing densities `rho_k`. Since the finite list contains the coordinate basis and the coordinates of `z^(k)` are uniformly bounded, the zero extensions of `rho_k` have uniformly bounded BV norm on the fixed bounded support. BV compactness gives an `L^1`-convergent subsequence to a probability density `rho`; lower semicontinuity gives

\[
V_{u_i}(\rho)\le\liminf_k V_{u_i}(\rho_k)\le z_i.
\]

Thus `z in S`.

If `b=(b_i)` were not in `S`, strong separation of the point `b` from the closed convex set `S` would give a nonzero vector `beta` with

\[
\inf_{z\in S}\beta\cdot z>\beta\cdot b.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Upward closure forces every `beta_i>=0`; otherwise an arbitrarily large increase in a coordinate with negative coefficient would drive the left side to `-infinity`. Therefore

\[
\inf_{z\in S}\beta\cdot z
=\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal P(K)}\sum_i\beta_iV_{u_i}(\rho)
=h_{H_\beta}(K)
\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i,
\]

contradicting (4.3). Hence `b in S`, proving the common-density claim.

This repairs R16-BF-01 without changing its statement or any later constant.

## 5. Prescribed-section lemma and transform stability

Let `B subset R^m x R` be bounded, open, convex and symmetric under `(y,s)->(y,-s)`, and let `D_a={y:(y,a) in B}`. Suppose a probability density `R` supported in `B` satisfies horizontal budgets

\[
V_{(u_i,0)}(R)\le b_i
\]

and mean absolute height

\[
M:=\int |s|R(y,s)\,dy\,ds\ge a.
\]

For fixed nonnegative `beta`, let `g(r)=h_{H_beta}(D_r)`. Symmetry and convexity make the positive-height sections nested; the Minkowski-convexity input makes `g` convex, and domain monotonicity makes it nondecreasing in `r`. If `R_s` is a horizontal slice and `q(s)=int R_s`, then

\[
q(s)g(|s|)\le E_{H_\beta}(R_s).
\]

Slicing/Fatou and the horizontal BV budgets give

\[
\int q(s)g(|s|)\,ds\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i.
\]

Jensen plus monotonicity now gives

\[
h_{H_\beta}(D_a)=g(a)\le g(M)
\le\int q(s)g(|s|)\,ds
\le\sum_i\beta_i b_i.
\]

The repaired finite common-density lemma applies to `D_a`. Also, `M>=a` forces the symmetric open convex lift to have vertical extent beyond `a`, so `D_a` is nonempty; there is no hidden empty-section case.

Now apply this to the Guo–Fang–Lu lift for a listed direction `v`,

\[

after fiberwise symmetric decreasing rearrangement. Horizontal directional variations do not increase, and the exact retained-height identity gives

\[
\int|s|R^*\ge\frac32-\frac32V_v(\rho).
\]

Thus `V_v(rho)<=1/3` leaves mean absolute height at least one, so the height-one section, exactly the open Banaszczyk transform

\[
\mathcal T_vK=((K-v)\cap(K+v))+\{sv:-2<s<2\},

supports a new density with the same finite list of budgets. This proves R16-BF-02.

The transform preserves central symmetry. Hence every nonempty `K_j` contains `0`. The containment

then allows exact backward sign recovery: from `0 in K_n`, recursively choose one of the two translates to obtain signs `epsilon_j` with `sum_j epsilon_j v_j in K_0`. There is no quantifier or sign-convention gap.

## 6. R16-BF-03 from a one-dimensional law

Take the product cosine-squared density on the cube `(-C,C)^m`. The directional-variation formula is

\[
V_u(\rho_C)=\frac\pi C\,\mathbb E\left|\sum_i u_iT_i\right|,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where the `T_i` are iid with density

\[
f_T(x)=\frac2{\pi(1+x^2)^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

If a binary column `v` has support `k<=t`, then its variation is `(pi/C)E|S_k|`, with `S_k=T_1+...+T_k`. Because `T` is centered,

\[
\mathbb E|S_{k+1}|
=\mathbb E\big[\mathbb E(|S_k+T_{k+1}|\mid S_k)\big]
\ge\mathbb E|S_k|
\]

by Jensen. Therefore all input columns have variation at most `1/3` when

\[
C=3\pi\mathbb E|S_t|.
\tag{6.3}
\]

R16-BF-02 can be iterated through the finite list of columns, and backward sign recovery gives discrepancy `<C`. It remains only to evaluate `C` exactly.

The characteristic function of (6.2) is

\[
\varphi_T(s)=(1+|s|)e^{-|s|}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For a symmetric integrable random variable,

\[
\mathbb E|X|=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{1-\varphi_X(s)}{s^2}\,ds.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbb E|S_t|
=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty
\frac{1-(1+s)^te^{-ts}}{s^2}\,ds.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Integration by parts yields

\[
\mathbb E|S_t|
=\frac{2t}{\pi}\int_0^\infty(1+s)^{t-1}e^{-ts}\,ds
=\frac2\pi\frac{t!}{t^t}
\sum_{r=0}^{t-1}\frac{t^r}{r!}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Multiplying by `3 pi` gives exactly (2.1), proving (2.2).

For strict comparison, for every `s>0`,

\[
\log(1+s)-s+\frac{s^2}{2}>0
\]

because its derivative is `s^2/(1+s)>0`. Therefore

\[
(1+s)^te^{-ts}>e^{-ts^2/2}.
\]

Substituting in (6.5) makes `E|S_t|` strictly smaller than the absolute first moment of `N(0,t)`, namely `sqrt(2t/pi)`. Thus

\[
R_t=3\pi\mathbb E|S_t|<3\sqrt{2\pi t},
\]

proving (2.3).
