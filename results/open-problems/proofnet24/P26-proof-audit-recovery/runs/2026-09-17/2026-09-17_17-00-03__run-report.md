## 2. Frozen claim and source scope

Source: Ruodu Wang, *Current Open Questions in Complete Mixability*, Probability Surveys 12 (2015), Open Problem 10, §2.8 / arXiv:1411.6190.

The source asks for extra conditions under which a unimodal distribution on a bounded interval is `n`-completely mixable. The package audited here is a **restricted explicit family theorem**, not a characterization of all bounded unimodal laws and not source-level closure.

For every integer `m>=3`, put `k=m-1>=2` and partition `[0,1/3]` into

\[
I_j=\left[\frac{j-1}{3k},\frac{j}{3k}\right),\qquad 1\le j\le k,

with tail interval `[1/3,1]`. For `t in [-1/10,1/10]`, set

\[
A_k=\frac{8k+1}{k-1},\qquad
B_k=-\frac{18k}{k^2-1},\qquad

and

\[
f_{k,t}(x)=2+a_{k,j}t\quad(x\in I_j),\qquad
f_{k,t}(x)=\frac{1+t}{2}\quad(x\in[1/3,1]).

The frozen claim `P20-D0010-ALL-M-PIECE-IFF-02` is

For `t!=0` the first `k` plateau heights are distinct, so this gives arbitrarily many genuine pieces. At `t=0` the pre-tail plateaus coalesce; this does not affect the theorem.

## 3. NEW-METHOD #2 — attack the negation first

The dangerous direction is `t<0`: can a nonmonotone-but-unimodal member nevertheless be 3-completely mixable?

The exact algebra gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^k a_{k,j}=-k,
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^k(2j-1)a_{k,j}=-4k^2,
\]

so the law has total mass one and mean exactly `1/3`; also

\[
P(X>1/3)=\frac{1+t}{3}.
\]

Suppose a 3-complete mix existed: `X_1+X_2+X_3=1` almost surely with all three marginals equal to this atomless law. If no coordinate exceeded `1/3`, then all three would be exactly `1/3`; because the marginal law has no atom there, that event has probability zero. Therefore

\[
\#\{i:X_i>1/3\}\ge1\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

and hence

\[
1\le E\#\{i:X_i>1/3\}
=3P(X>1/3)=1+t.
\]

Thus `t>=0`. The negation attack therefore kills every `t<0` member with a single source-matching tail statistic. No exchangeability assumption is used.

I also rechecked the construction-side failure points for all `k>=2` and `|t|<=1/10`:

\[
a_{k,1}=\frac{8k-1}{k+1},\qquad
a_{k,k}=-\frac{10k+1}{k+1},\qquad
a_{k,j}-a_{k,j+1}=\frac{18k}{(k-1)(k+1)}>0,
\]

\[
2+\frac{a_{k,k}}{10}=\frac{10k+19}{10(k+1)}>0,
\]

\[

and at the pre-tail/tail boundary the worst `t>=0` gap is

\[

\]

Hence no small-`k`, endpoint, positivity, or mode-location counterexample survives.

## 4. NEW-METHOD #11 — dualize the necessity obstruction

\[

satisfies

\[
\phi(x_1)+\phi(x_2)+\phi(x_3)\ge1.

Integrating any candidate complete-mix coupling gives the marginal inequality

\[

\]

Thus the whole necessity half is a one-function dual witness against `t<0`. In this language the theorem's sharp boundary is transparent: the family was engineered so that

\[
