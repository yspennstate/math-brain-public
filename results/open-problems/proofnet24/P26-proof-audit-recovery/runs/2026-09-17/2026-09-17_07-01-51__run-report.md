## 3. New-method audit — a stronger theorem on 1-walk-regular graphs

The substantive audit did not begin by rerunning R05's census. I first tried to find the minimal structural hypothesis under which the proof survives.

### #14 FIND THE GROUP ACTING — remove the group

Arc-transitivity is more symmetry than the proof needs. What matters is that, for every power of the adjacency matrix, diagonal entries are constant and entries on edges are constant. This is precisely the standard **1-walk-regular** condition.

Every arc-transitive graph is 1-walk-regular: automorphisms preserve powers of `A`, and transitivity on vertices and arcs forces the diagonal and edge entries of every `A^k` to be constant. Conversely, the analytic argument below does not need an explicit automorphism group once those two constancies are available.

Thus the natural stronger audit theorem is:

**P26 stronger theorem.** On every finite connected 1-walk-regular graph, the normalized heat ratio across every edge is strictly increasing in time.

This is stronger than C1 and, in particular, independently implies C1.

### #3 PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER — full proof

Let `G` be finite, connected and 1-walk-regular. Such a graph is regular; write its degree as `d`, put `P=A/d`, and let

\[
H_t=e^{t(P-I)}=e^{-tL},\qquad L=I-P.
\]

Because the exponential is an absolutely convergent power series in `A`, 1-walk-regularity gives scalar functions

\[
a(t)=H_t(x,x),\qquad b(t)=H_t(x,y)\quad(x\sim y),
\]

independent of the chosen vertex and edge.

The Kolmogorov equation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
a'(t)
&=(H_t(P-I))_{xx}\\
&=\frac1d\sum_{z\sim x}H_t(x,z)-H_t(x,x)\\
&=b(t)-a(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{r(t):=\frac{b(t)}{a(t)}=1+\frac{a'(t)}{a(t)}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since `L` is real symmetric positive semidefinite, let `mu_x` be the spectral probability measure of `L` at `delta_x`. Then

\[
a(t)=\int e^{-t\lambda}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Define the exponentially tilted probability law

\[
d\nu_t(\lambda)=\frac{e^{-t\lambda}}{a(t)}\,d\mu_x(\lambda).
\]

Now

\[
\frac{a'(t)}{a(t)}=-\mathbb E_{\nu_t}\lambda,
\]

so (3.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{r(t)=1-\mathbb E_{\nu_t}\lambda.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### #18 SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE — monotonicity is variance

Differentiating the tilted expectation gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{r'(t)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the whole monotonicity mechanism in probabilistic language.

Strictness is automatic for every finite `t`. Because `G` is connected, eigenvalue `0` of `L` is simple and has local spectral mass

\[
\mu_x(\{0\})=1/|V|>0.
\]

Because `|V|>=2`, the remaining local mass is `1-1/|V|>0` and lies on positive eigenvalues. Exponential tilting at finite `t` does not kill either part. Hence `nu_t` is not a point mass and

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_t}(\lambda)>0.
\]

Thus `r'(t)>0` for all finite `t`.

At `t=0`, `H_0=I`, so for an edge `x~y`, `b(0)=0`, `a(0)=1`, and `r(0)=0`. Since the continuous-time walk is irreducible on a finite regular connected graph,

\[
H_t(x,x),H_t(x,y)\longrightarrow 1/|V|,
\]

hence `r(t)->1`.

This proves the stronger 1-walk-regular theorem and therefore independently proves R05-I0030-C1. QED.

### Adversarial diagnosis

I also tried to identify where this argument could fail rather than merely restating it. The only two structural joints are:

- constancy of `H_t(x,x)` on vertices;
- constancy of `H_t(x,y)` on edges, so the neighbor average in `a'` collapses to the chosen edge value.

Vertex-transitivity alone supplies the first but need not supply the second. For a general regular graph the identity is only

\[
a'(t)=\frac1d\sum_{z\sim x}H_t(x,z)-a(t),
\]

which controls the **average neighbor ratio**, not every individual edge ratio. So the proof does not accidentally establish all-pairs MNHD or even an arbitrary edge-orbit statement without the required edge uniformity. This is the precise obstruction to overgeneralizing C1.
