## 3. Independent proof reconstruction

Let `G=(V,E)` be a connected simple graph, `m=|E|`, degrees `d_v`, and stationary law `pi_v=d_v/(2m)`. For effective resistance `R_G(u,v)`, the commute-time identity gives

\[
E_uT_v+E_vT_u=2mR_G(u,v).
\]

Averaging with `pi_u pi_v` gives the basic identity

\[
K(G):=\tau_0(G)
 =\frac1{2m}\sum_{u<v}d_ud_vR_G(u,v)
 =m\,E_{\pi\otimes\pi}R_G(X,Y).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This identity is the correct bridge between the source parameter and the resistance decomposition used below.

### 3.1 Sparse edge deletion comparison

Let `H` be a connected spanning subgraph of `G`, with `m_H=|E(H)|`, `m_G=|E(G)|`, and `q=m_G-m_H`. Write

\[
d_v^G=d_v^H+r_v,
\qquad
\sum_v r_v=2q.
\]

Rayleigh monotonicity gives `R_G(u,v)<=R_H(u,v)`, while for a connected unweighted `n`-vertex graph `R_H(u,v)<=dist_H(u,v)<=n-1`. Hence, using (3.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
K(G)
&\le \frac1{2m_G}\sum_{u<v}
(d_u^H+r_u)(d_v^H+r_v)R_H(u,v)\\
&\le \frac{m_H}{m_G}K(H)
 +\frac{n-1}{2m_G}
 \left[
 \sum_{u<v}(d_u^Hr_v+r_ud_v^H)+\sum_{u<v}r_ur_v
 \right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Now

\[
\sum_{u<v}(d_u^Hr_v+r_ud_v^H)
\le \left(\sum_ud_u^H\right)\left(\sum_vr_v\right)
=4qm_H,
\]

and

\[
\sum_{u<v}r_ur_v\le\frac12\left(\sum_vr_v\right)^2=2q^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K(G)\le\frac{m_H}{m_G}K(H)
 +(n-1)\frac{2qm_H+q^2}{m_G}
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and in particular

\[
K(G)\le K(H)+2q(n-1)+\frac{q^2(n-1)}{m_G}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is R04-C4, independently rederived.

### 3.2 Quotient-tree resistance decomposition

Suppose the vertex set of a connected graph `H` is partitioned into connected induced clusters `V_1,...,V_h`, and exactly `h-1` inter-cluster edges are retained so that the quotient graph `Q` is a tree. Every retained inter-cluster edge is then a bridge.

Fix `u` and `v`. The unique quotient path between their clusters consists of unit-resistance bridges in series. Within each visited cluster, current enters at one attachment vertex and exits at another; all quotient branches off that path attach at a single cut vertex and carry no net current between `u` and `v`. Consequently the effective resistance is exactly the sum of the quotient-bridge resistances plus the internal cluster entry-to-exit resistances.

If every cluster has effective-resistance diameter at most `epsilon`, at most `n` cluster pieces can occur, and therefore

\[
R_H(u,v)\le d_Q(c(u),c(v))+n\epsilon.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Averaging (3.4) in (3.1), the quotient-distance contribution is no larger than the contribution `K_br(H)` of all bridge edges of `H`. Hence

\[
\boxed{K(H)\le K_{\rm br}(H)+mn\epsilon.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the needed R04-C7 bound.

### 3.3 Exact bridge-block identity

Delete every bridge of `H`. Let the resulting 2-edge-connected components (singletons allowed) be `C_1,...,C_k`, with `m_i` internal edges. Their bridge quotient `Q` is a tree. Define

\[
w_i=m_i+1,\qquad W=\sum_iw_i=m+1,\qquad p_i=w_i/W.
\]

For a bridge `e`, let `S_e` be one side of `Q-e`. If `p(S_e)=\sum_{i\in S_e}p_i`, then the volume in the original graph on that side is

\[
\operatorname{vol}(S_e)
=2\sum_{i\in S_e}m_i+2(|S_e|-1)+1
=2Wp(S_e)-1.
\]

Thus the bridge contribution to (3.1) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{\rm br}(H)
&=\frac1{2m}\sum_{e\in E(Q)}
(2Wp(S_e)-1)(2W(1-p(S_e))-1)\\
&=\frac{2W^2}{m}\sum_e p(S_e)(1-p(S_e))
 -\frac{(2m+1)|E(Q)|}{2m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For independent `Q`-vertices `X,Y` sampled with law `p`, a tree edge `e` lies on the `X-Y` path with probability `2p(S_e)(1-p(S_e))`. Therefore

\[
E\,d_Q(X,Y)=2\sum_ep(S_e)(1-p(S_e)),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm br}(H)
=\frac{(m+1)^2}{m}E\,d_Q(X,Y)
 -\frac{(2m+1)|E(Q)|}{2m}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is R04-C6's exact bridge-block formula.

### 3.4 Sharp asymptotic bridge bound

Consider any sequence of connected simple `n`-vertex graphs `H_n` with

\[
\frac{m}{n^2}\to\alpha>0.
\]

We prove

\[
K_{\rm br}(H_n)\le\left(\frac1{54}+o(1)\right)n^3.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Fix `eta>0`. Call a bridge block **large** if it has `n_i>=eta n` vertices. Let `L` be the large set and

\[
P=\sum_{i\in L}p_i,
\qquad
x_i=n_i/n,
\qquad
s=\sum_{i\in L}x_i,
\qquad
\widetilde p_i=p_i/P.
\]

Since a simple block on `n_i` vertices has `m_i<=n_i^2/2`, the total weight of the small blocks obeys

\[
\sum_{i\notin L}(m_i+1)
\le \frac{\eta n}{2}\sum_{i\notin L}n_i+n
\le \frac{\eta n^2}{2}+n.
\]

As `m+1~alpha n^2`, first `n->infinity` and then `eta->0` gives

\[
P\to1.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For the finitely many large blocks, capacity gives

\[
\sqrt{p_i}\le \frac{x_i}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}+o(1).
\]

Set

\[
S=\sum_{i\in L}\sqrt{\widetilde p_i},
\qquad
Q_2=\sum_{i\in L}\widetilde p_i^2.
\]

Since `sum_{i in L} sqrt(p_i)=sqrt(P)S`, summing the preceding capacity bound yields

\[
PS^2\le\frac{s^2}{2\alpha}+o(1),
\qquad
\alpha\le\frac{s^2}{2PS^2}+o(1).
\tag{3.9}
\]

If two distinct large blocks are sampled, the quotient-tree path between them can contain at most `(1-s)n+O_eta(1)` small-block vertices/edges, plus only `O_eta(1)` large blocks. If at least one sampled block is small, use the trivial distance bound `n`. Therefore

\[
\frac1n E\,d_Q(X,Y)
\le P^2(1-s)(1-Q_2)+2(1-P)+o(1).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Using (3.6), discarding its negative correction, (3.8)--(3.10), and then sending `eta` to zero,

\[
\limsup\frac{K_{\rm br}(H_n)}{n^3}
\le \frac{s^2(1-s)}{2}\frac{1-Q_2}{S^2}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Hölder applied as

\[
\sum_i\widetilde p_i
=\sum_i \widetilde p_i^{2/3}\widetilde p_i^{1/6}\widetilde p_i^{1/6}
\le Q_2^{1/3}S^{2/3}
\]

gives

\[
1\le Q_2S^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{1-Q_2}{S^2}
\le Q_2(1-Q_2)\le\frac14.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Finally

\[
\frac1{54}-\frac{s^2(1-s)}8
=\frac{(3s-2)^2(3s+1)}{216}\ge0,
\tag{3.13}
\]

with equality at `s=2/3`. Combining (3.11)--(3.13) proves (3.7).

The equality pattern is informative: (3.12) is tight for two equal macroscopic weights, and (3.13) is tight at total macroscopic vertex mass `2/3`. This is exactly the balanced two-bell/barbell geometry rather than an artifact of the proof.

### 3.5 Assemble the all-graph upper bound

For every connected graph, (3.1) and `R_G(u,v)<=n-1` give

\[
K(G)\le m(n-1).
\tag{3.14}
\]

The balanced barbell family has

\[
K(G)=\left(\frac1{54}+o(1)\right)n^3;
\tag{3.15}
\]

this is computed in Aldous–Fill's barbell example and explicitly in Faught–Kempton–Knudson, *A 1-Separation Formula for the Graph Kemeny Constant and Braess Edges*, arXiv:2108.01061, §3.1. Rounding the three near-`n/3` parameters changes only lower-order terms. Thus an extremizer `G_n` satisfies `m(G_n)=Theta(n^2)` by (3.14).

Now invoke Alev–Anari–Lau–Oveis Gharan, *Graph Clustering using Effective Resistance*, ITCS 2018, Theorem 3 (arXiv:1711.06530). For sufficiently large `delta>1`, an unweighted graph with `m` edges admits a partition with

\[
q_{\rm cross}=O(m/\delta)
\]

cross edges and induced-cluster resistance diameter

\[
\epsilon=O(\delta^3 n/m).
\]

Take `delta=n^{1/10}`. Since `m=Theta(n^2)`,

\[
q_{\rm cross}=o(n^2),
\qquad
\epsilon=O(n^{-7/10})=o(1).
\]

Retain all intra-cluster edges and only a spanning-tree set of inter-cluster edges, obtaining a connected spanning `H_n`. The number `q` of deleted edges is `O(m/delta)`. Applying (3.3),

\[
K(G_n)\le K(H_n)
 +O(n^3/\delta)+O(n^3/\delta^2)
=K(H_n)+o(n^3).
\tag{3.16}
\]

Moreover `m(H_n)=Theta(n^2)`. Applying (3.5),

\[
K(H_n)\le K_{\rm br}(H_n)+m(H_n)n\epsilon
=K_{\rm br}(H_n)+O(n^2\delta^3)
=K_{\rm br}(H_n)+o(n^3).
\tag{3.17}
\]

The sharp bridge bound (3.7) gives

\[
K(G_n)\le\left(\frac1{54}+o(1)\right)n^3.
\]

Together with (3.15),

\[
\boxed{
\max_{|V(G)|=n}\tau_0(G)
=\left(\frac1{54}+o(1)\right)n^3.
}
\]

This proves the exact tau_0 half of Aldous–Fill Open Problem 6.13.
