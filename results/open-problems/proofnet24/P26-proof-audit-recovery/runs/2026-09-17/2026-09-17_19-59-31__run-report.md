## 2. Exact source and frozen R07 theorem

Primary source rechecked on 2026-09-17:

Krzysztof Burdzy and Michael Scheutzow, *Forward Brownian Motion*, Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 160 (2014), 95--126; arXiv:1302.6958v4.

The source's Problem 7.8 is exactly:

> Can one generalize Theorem 4.3 from parabolas to other space-time shapes?

The following forward-Levy sentence is Problem 7.9 and is not part of I0077.

For `-infinity <= c1 < c2 <= infinity`, Section 4 uses the killed Ornstein--Uhlenbeck principal exponent `lambda_0(c1,c2)` for

`A = (1/2)(d^2/dx^2 - x d/dx)`.

Remark 4.1 gives continuity and strict domain monotonicity. Remark 4.2 gives the exact critical values

`lambda_0(-infinity,-1) = lambda_0(-1,1) = lambda_0(1,infinity) = 1`.

Theorem 4.3 says, in the source's infinite-endpoint convention:

1. if `lambda_0(c1,c2) >= 1`, every FBM must leave the corresponding normalized interval asymptotically on at least one side;
2. if `lambda_0(c1,c2) <= 1`, there exists a decomposable FBM whose far-past normalized liminf/limsup stay within the interval;
3. if additionally `c1 <= 0 <= c2` and the exponent is strictly below one, there is a decomposable FBM pointwise confined to the parabolic strip for all nonpositive times.

The source proof explicitly handles infinite endpoints, so using Theorem 4.3 with half-lines is source-valid.

R07's frozen theorem `I0077-R07-T22` is the following. For a closed `D subset R`, put

`F_D = {(-u,x): u>0, x/sqrt(u) in D} union {(0,0)}`.

For every nondegenerate connected component `C` of `D`, define `lambda(C)` to be the source principal exponent of that interval/half-line, and set `lambda(R)=0`.

**T22(A), strict supercritical phase.** If every nondegenerate component `C` has `lambda(C)>1`, then every FBM `X` satisfies

`P(exists R<infinity: X_{-u}/sqrt(u) in D for all u>=R)=0`.

**T22(B), strict subcritical phase.** If `D` has a nondegenerate component `C` with `lambda(C)<1`, then there exists a decomposable FBM that is almost surely eventually confined to `D`.

Exact exponent-one components are deliberately left unclassified for exact eventual membership.

Its exterior-fan corollary takes

`D_{a,b}=(-infinity,-a] union [b,infinity)`, `a,b>0`,

and

`lambda_ext(a,b)=min(lambda_0(-infinity,-a), lambda_0(b,infinity))`.

Then `lambda_ext>1` gives universal failure of eventual exterior confinement, while `lambda_ext<1` gives decomposable existence. In the symmetric family `D_c=(-infinity,-c] union [c,infinity)`, the strict transition is `c<1` existence and `c>1` universal obstruction; `c=1` is left open for the exact-eventual event.

## 3. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I first tried to break T22 at the quantifier-sensitive places rather than reproduce R07's proof in its original order.

### 3.1 Infinitely many components / uncountably many singleton components

The most dangerous case is a closed `D` with infinitely many components, possibly a Cantor-type set whose nondegenerate-component hypothesis is vacuous. An invalid proof could silently take an uncountable union over singleton components.

Suppose eventual confinement occurs from some deterministic or random tail time `R`. The normalized path

`y(u)=X_{-u}/sqrt(u)`

is continuous for `u>0`. The image of the connected interval `[R,infinity)` is connected. Hence that entire tail image lies in one connected component of `D`.

Every nondegenerate connected component of a closed subset of `R` contains a rational in its interior, so there are at most countably many such components. They can be union-bounded after proving each individually impossible in the strict supercritical phase.

If the selected component is a singleton `{c}`, connectedness forces `y(u)=c` for the entire tail. This is where an uncountable union would be wrong, but no such union is needed: the open unit intervals

`J_q=(q-1/2,q+1/2)`, `q in Q`,

form a countable cover of `R`. Choose a rational `q` with `c` in the interior of `J_q`. I independently prove below that every unit interval has principal exponent strictly greater than one. Eventual constancy at `c` then has both liminf and limsup strictly inside `J_q`, contradicting source Theorem 4.3(i). Thus **all singleton components are disposed of by one countable cover**, including a Cantor-type `D`.

This negation attack survives.

### 3.2 Half-line direction of the monotonicity perturbation

For a left component `C=(-infinity,b]` with `lambda(C)>1`, enlarging to `(-infinity,b+epsilon)` lowers the exponent, so the perturbation direction is potentially dangerous. Continuity, however, gives a sufficiently small positive `epsilon` for which the exponent is still greater than one. Eventual membership yields `limsup y <= b < b+epsilon`; Theorem 4.3(i) for the half-line forces `limsup y >= b+epsilon`, contradiction.

For a right component `[a,infinity)`, enlarging to `[a-epsilon,infinity)` again lowers the exponent but preserves `>1` for small `epsilon`, and source Theorem 4.3(i) forces a liminf below the enlarged endpoint, contradicting eventual membership.

In the subcritical direction the perturbations reverse: shrink `(-infinity,b]` to `(-infinity,b']` with `b'<b`, or `[a,infinity)` to `[a',infinity)` with `a'>a`; the exponent rises but continuity preserves `<1` for a sufficiently small inward move. The strict spatial margin then converts Theorem 4.3(ii)'s limsup/liminf statement into eventual membership in the original component.

No inequality direction is reversed in the frozen proof.

### 3.3 Critical endpoint leakage

I also tested whether the proof accidentally classifies `lambda=1`. It does not. Both directions use a strict gap from one before perturbing the endpoints. The symmetric exterior case `c=1` is explicitly excluded. This is necessary: Theorem 4.3(ii) at equality gives liminf/limsup control but not, by itself, exact eventual membership in a closed component.

The negation attack therefore found no counterexample to the stated strict-phase theorem and confirms that its exclusion of the critical phase is mathematically substantive rather than cosmetic.

## 4. Method #11 — dualize the singleton obstruction through the OU quadratic form

The only auxiliary estimate not directly quoted from the source is the claim that every interval of length one is strictly supercritical. I rederived it independently through the Dirichlet/Rayleigh side of the killed Ornstein--Uhlenbeck problem.

For an interval `I` of length `d` and a Dirichlet test function `f`, conjugate by the Gaussian ground-state weight:

`g(x)=f(x) exp(-x^2/4)`.

The OU Rayleigh quotient becomes

`lambda_0(I) = inf_g [ integral_I ( (1/2)|g'|^2 + (x^2/8 - 1/4) g^2 ) dx ] / [ integral_I g^2 dx ]`.

Indeed the cross term integrates to `-(1/4) integral g^2` because `g` vanishes at the endpoints. Dropping the nonnegative `x^2/8` term and applying the sharp Dirichlet Poincare inequality gives

`lambda_0(I) >= pi^2/(2 d^2) - 1/4`.

For `d=1`, even the elementary bound `pi>3` gives

`lambda_0(I) > 9/2 - 1/4 = 17/4 > 1`.

Thus the countable unit-interval cover used against singleton tail images is completely rigorous and does not depend on a floating-point eigenvalue.

This dual/Rayleigh derivation also clarifies the theorem's mechanism: the topological component selection reduces the arbitrary closed set to one interval-like component, and the source's principal eigenvalue is the spectral dual variable deciding whether that component can support an asymptotic FBM tail.

## 5. Independent full proof of T22(A)

Let `E_N` be the event that `y(u)=X_{-u}/sqrt(u)` belongs to `D` for every `u>=N`.

On `E_N`, continuity and connectedness put the whole tail in a single connected component of `D`.

There are only countably many nondegenerate components. Fix one such component `C`.

- If `C=[a,b]` and `lambda(C)>1`, continuity of the source exponent permits an `epsilon>0` with `lambda_0(a-epsilon,b+epsilon)>1`. Eventual membership in `[a,b]` implies `liminf y >= a > a-epsilon` and `limsup y <= b < b+epsilon`, contradicting Theorem 4.3(i) for the enlarged interval.
- If `C=(-infinity,b]`, choose `epsilon>0` with `lambda_0(-infinity,b+epsilon)>1`. Eventual membership gives `limsup y<=b`, while Theorem 4.3(i) for the enlarged half-line forces `limsup y>=b+epsilon`.
- If `C=[a,infinity)`, choose `epsilon>0` with `lambda_0(a-epsilon,infinity)>1`. Eventual membership gives `liminf y>=a`, while Theorem 4.3(i) forces `liminf y<=a-epsilon`.
- `C=R` cannot occur under the hypothesis because by convention and by the unconstrained OU semigroup its exponent is zero.

Hence eventual confinement to every fixed nondegenerate component has probability zero; countability eliminates all of them.

If the selected component is a singleton, the countable unit-interval argument of Sections 3--4 gives probability zero without any uncountable union.

Thus `P(E_N)=0` for each integer `N`. Taking the countable union over `N` proves T22(A).

## 6. Independent full proof of T22(B)

Assume `D` has a nondegenerate component `C` with `lambda(C)<1`.

- If `C=[a,b]`, continuity permits a small `epsilon>0` such that `a+epsilon<b-epsilon` and `lambda_0(a+epsilon,b-epsilon)<1`. Source Theorem 4.3(ii) gives a decomposable FBM with `liminf y>=a+epsilon` and `limsup y<=b-epsilon`. By the definitions of liminf/limsup, the strict margin implies `y(u) in [a,b]` for all sufficiently large `u`.
- If `C=(-infinity,b]`, choose `b'<b` close enough that `lambda_0(-infinity,b')<1`. Theorem 4.3(ii) yields `limsup y<=b'`, hence `y(u)<=b` eventually.
- If `C=[a,infinity)`, choose `a'>a` close enough that `lambda_0(a',infinity)<1`. Theorem 4.3(ii) yields `liminf y>=a'`, hence `y(u)>=a` eventually.
- If `C=R`, eventual membership is tautological; alternatively the source's exact `lambda_0(-1,1)=1`, strict domain enlargement, and Theorem 4.3 provide a decomposable witness inside a larger finite strip.

In every case the resulting FBM is decomposable because that is exactly what Theorem 4.3(ii) supplies. This proves T22(B).

## 7. Exterior-fan corollary and sharp strict threshold

For

`D_{a,b}=(-infinity,-a] union [b,infinity)`,

the nondegenerate components are precisely the two rays. Therefore:

- `lambda_ext(a,b)>1` means both ray exponents exceed one, so T22(A) gives universal failure of eventual exterior confinement;
- `lambda_ext(a,b)<1` means at least one ray is subcritical, so T22(B) gives a decomposable FBM eventually confined to that ray and therefore to the exterior fan.

For `D_c=(-infinity,-c] union [c,infinity)`, reflection gives equal ray exponents. The source identity `lambda_0(1,infinity)=1` plus strict domain monotonicity gives

`c<1 => lambda_0(c,infinity)<1`,

`c>1 => lambda_0(c,infinity)>1`.

Hence the frozen strict transition is exactly correct. The case `c=1` remains outside the claim.

A useful falsification of a tempting but wrong extension rule also survives independent checking: `conv(D_{a,b})=R`, so classifying disconnected shapes only by their convex hull would predict no obstruction, while T22 shows universal obstruction when both rays are supercritical. The missing invariant is component selection by a continuous normalized tail.
