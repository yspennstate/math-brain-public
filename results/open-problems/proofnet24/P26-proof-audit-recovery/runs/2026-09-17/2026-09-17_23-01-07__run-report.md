## 3. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST — try to break T28

I treated T28 as false until the following failure modes were eliminated.

### 3.1 Can migration among infinitely many teeth defeat the one-step estimate?

No. Given current sampled value `x`, the next normalized sampled-OU value is

\[
Y'=rx+sZ,
\qquad Z\sim N(0,1).
\]

For an arbitrary Gaussian mean `mu=rx`, choose a nearest lattice center `j_0L`. The nearest tooth has length `2 eta`, so its Gaussian mass is at most

\[
\frac{2\eta}{s\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]

For every other tooth and every point `y` in that tooth,

\[
|y-\mu|\ge L/2-\eta.
\]

This remains true at the midpoint/tie case, where two centers are equally near. Hence the union of **all** non-nearest teeth is contained in one Gaussian tail event and has mass at most

\[
2\exp\!\left(-\frac{(L/2-\eta)^2}{2s^2}\right).
\]

Thus

\[
\sup_x\Pr(Y'\in D_{L,\eta}\mid Y=x)\le Q(P,L,\eta).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This estimate already sums over every possible tooth migration; no hidden same-tooth assumption remains.

### 3.2 Is the sampled-chain estimate valid only for a deterministic initial state?

No. The Markov bound is uniform in `x`, so for any `n>m`,

\[
\Pr(Y_m,\ldots,Y_n\in D_{L,\eta})
\le Q^{n-m}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The first sampled state costs at most one and every subsequent conditional survival factor costs at most `Q`.

### 3.3 Is the condition `Q<1` enough, making the stronger `Q<e^{-P}` suspicious?

No. This is a key audit point. The FBM witness terminal can occur in any physical unit interval `[n,n+1]`, so the fixed-terminal persistence exponent has to beat the linear count of possible physical terminal locations. Put

\[
\kappa=-\frac{\log Q}{P}.
\]

The geometric gate count between scales `R_n` and `n` is

\[
\frac{\log(n/R_n)}P+O(1),
\]

so the survival probability is at most

\[
C(n/R_n)^{-\kappa}.
\]

Summability over integer `n` requires `kappa>1`, which is exactly

\[
Q<e^{-P}.
\]

The extra `e^{-P}` is not an arbitrary safety margin; it pays for the moving-terminal union in physical time.

### 3.4 Does replacing a random terminal by the neighboring integer silently condition on it?

No. This is the main proof joint and it survives an independent reconstruction.

For standard Brownian motion `W`, define

\[
M_n=\sup\{|W_t-W_s|:0\le s,t\le n+1,\ |t-s|\le1\}.
\]

The report uses the standard bound

\[
\Pr(M_n>x)\le4(n+1)e^{-x^2/16},
\]

so `x_n=16 sqrt(log n)` is eventually summable. Choose an intermediate width

\[
\eta_0<\eta_1<\eta_2
\]

and `C_0` with

\[
32/\sqrt{C_0}\le\eta_2-\eta_1.
\]

Let `R_n=e^{m_nP}` be the first gate lag at least `C_0 log n`. If a real terminal `T in [n,n+1]` satisfies the `eta_1` comb gates and `M_n<=x_n`, then for every gate lag `u=e^{kP}` with `R_n<=u<=n`,

\[
\left|
\frac{(W_n-W_{n-u})-(W_T-W_{T-u})}{\sqrt u}
\right|
\le\frac{2M_n}{\sqrt u}
\le\eta_2-\eta_1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Both Brownian increments in the numerator are legitimate because `u<=n` and `T in[n,n+1]`. Therefore the integer-terminal reversed path satisfies the relaxed `eta_2` comb gates. Brownian time reversal makes

\[
B_u=W_n-W_{n-u},\quad 0\le u\le n,
\]

a standard Brownian motion in the lag variable. Applying (3.2) gives

\[
\Pr(\exists\hbox{ good }T\in[n,n+1])
\le C(n/R_n)^{-\kappa}+\Pr(M_n>x_n).
\]

Since `R_n=O(log n)` and `kappa>1`, this is summable. Borel--Cantelli yields the **path-only** statement that a standard Brownian path almost surely has no arbitrarily large good terminal. No random terminal is conditioned upon.

### 3.5 Can the FBM witness time depend on its Brownian forward view and invalidate the transfer?

No. Suppose T28 fails with positive probability. By countable unions, fix deterministic `K,M` and `q_0>0` so that with probability at least `q_0`,

\[
|X_0|\le M,
\qquad
X_{-e^{kP}}/e^{kP/2}\in D_{L,\eta_0}
\quad(k\ge K).
\]

After increasing `K`, the bounded `X_0` shift is less than `eta_1-eta_0` in normalized units, so by symmetry of the comb,

\[
(X_0-X_{-e^{kP}})/e^{kP/2}\in D_{L,\eta_1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Let `S_j->-infinity` be an FBM witness sequence, `T_j=-S_j`, and for each fixed deterministic index `j`,

\[
W_j(t)=X_{S_j+t}-X_{S_j}
\]

is standard Brownian motion. For `e^{kP}<=T_j`,

\[
W_j(T_j)-W_j(T_j-e^{kP})=X_0-X_{-e^{kP}}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence on the positive-probability failure event, every sufficiently large `T_j` is a good terminal for the Brownian path `W_j`. Choose deterministic `A` so the Brownian path probability of any good terminal at least `A` is `<q_0/2`; then choose a deterministic witness index `j` with `Pr(T_j<A)<q_0/2`. Equations (3.4)--(3.5) force the standard Brownian path `W_j` to have a good terminal beyond `A` with probability `>q_0/2`, contradiction.

This uses only the marginal fact that each fixed `W_j` is standard Brownian motion. No independence between `T_j` and `W_j` is assumed or needed.

The negation attacks therefore failed to break T28.

## 4. #11 DUALIZE — the theorem is an operator contraction plus a terminal-count exponent

The cleanest independent formulation is as a sub-Markov operator on the sampled OU chain. Let `Q_P(x,dy)` be the one-step OU kernel and let

\[
(K_Df)(x)=\int_D f(y)Q_P(x,dy).
\]

Then

\[
\|K_D\|_{\infty\to\infty}
=\sup_x Q_P(x,D)
\le Q(P,L,\eta).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore every `N`-gate persistence probability is bounded by `Q^N`. The nontrivial FBM step is not another spectral estimate but conversion between logarithmic gate count and physical terminal count. One unit of log time contains a multiplicative `e^P` expansion in physical time; consequently the sufficient contraction condition is exactly

\[
e^P\|K_D\|_{\infty\to\infty}<1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The periodic-comb bound is one explicit way to verify (4.2). This operator language also diagnoses the strongest natural improvement: replace the crude `L^infinity` norm by a sharper persistence spectral radius on the periodic target. Such an improvement could enlarge the obstruction regime, but it is **not** needed for the correctness of T28 and is not counted as a new P26 theorem.

## 7. #22 READ THE FAILURES, NOT THE SUCCESSES

Three failed shortcuts are more informative than another checker.

First, a **same-tooth** argument is invalid because the path is unconstrained between gates and can migrate through arbitrarily many teeth. The proof succeeds only after replacing tooth tracking by total transition mass over the entire periodic set.

Second, taking the **convex hull** destroys the problem: the convex hull of the periodic comb is all of `R`, so convexification predicts no obstruction. The theorem is inherently about disconnected target geometry under sampled Markov persistence.

Third, a **fixed-terminal** sampled-OU estimate is insufficient for FBM. The forward-Brownian witness terminal is random and can lie anywhere on the positive time axis. The proof must first establish a pathwise statement excluding *any* sufficiently large good Brownian terminal. This is exactly why the exponent condition is `kappa>1`, not merely positive.

The nearest literature I located is consistent with this diagnosis. Ehrhardt--Majumdar--Bray (Phys. Rev. E 69, 016106, 2004) studies persistence/crossing statistics for discretely sampled Gaussian stationary processes and explicitly includes Ornstein--Uhlenbeck examples. Aurzada--Kettner (arXiv:1810.09861; J. Stat. Phys. 177 (2019)) studies persistence probabilities for AR(1) chains and the associated operator eigenvalue. Those works support the sampled-OU/Markov-operator mechanism as established prior machinery. They do not, in the bounded search performed here, supply the FBM arbitrary-terminal transfer or this exact periodic-comb theorem.
