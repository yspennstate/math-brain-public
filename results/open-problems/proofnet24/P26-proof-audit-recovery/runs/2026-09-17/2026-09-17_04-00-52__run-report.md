## 4. NEW-METHOD #3 — prove a stronger Hilbert-space right-inverse theorem

The Markov statement is a special case of a finite-dimensional operator fact.

### Lemma 4.1 — minimum operator norm of a right inverse

Let `D:E->H` be a surjective linear map between finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces. If `sigma_min(D)>0` is its smallest singular value, then

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{S:\,DS=I_H}\|S\|_{op}=\frac1{\sigma_{\min}(D)}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Let `u` be a unit left singular vector for `sigma_min`. Every solution of `Dx=u` has Euclidean norm at least that of the Moore-Penrose minimum-norm solution `D^+u`, namely `1/sigma_min`. Hence every right inverse satisfies `||S||>=||Su||>=1/sigma_min`. Conversely `D^+` is a right inverse on `H` and its operator norm is exactly the reciprocal of the smallest singular value. This proves (4.1). `square`

This is stronger than the needed chain statement because it isolates the entire optimization as a right-inverse singular-value identity before any Markov-specific calculation.

## 5. NEW-METHOD #11 — dualize the Markov problem to the weighted divergence singular values

Define the weighted divergence operator

\[
D:=\Pi^{-1/2}BC^{1/2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its range is `sqrt(pi)^perp`: for a zero-sum demand `b`,

\[
y=\Pi^{-1/2}b
\]

satisfies `sqrt(pi)^T y=1^Tb=0`.

If a physical flow is written as

\[
\theta=C^{1/2}x,
\]

then `B theta=b` is exactly

\[
Dx=y.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Moreover the two norms become Euclidean:

\[
\theta^TC^{-1}\theta=\|x\|_2^2,
\qquad
b^T\Pi^{-1}b=\|y\|_2^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the infimum in (3.2) is precisely the **square** of the least possible Euclidean operator norm of a right inverse of `D` on `sqrt(pi)^perp`.

Now compute

\[
DD^T
=\Pi^{-1/2}BCB^T\Pi^{-1/2}
=\Pi^{-1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}
=I-\Pi^{1/2}P\Pi^{-1/2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Reversibility makes

\[
K:=\Pi^{1/2}P\Pi^{-1/2}
\]

symmetric. It is similar to `P`, so its eigenvalues are

\[
1=\lambda_1>\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n.
\]

Consequently the eigenvalues of `DD^T` on `sqrt(pi)^perp` are

\[
1-\lambda_2,\ldots,1-\lambda_n.
\]

The smallest positive singular value of `D` is therefore

\[
\sigma_{\min}(D)=\sqrt{1-\lambda_2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Apply Lemma 4.1 and square:

\[
\inf_{BR=I_{H_0}}
\|R\|_{\Pi^{-1}\to C^{-1}}^2
=\frac1{1-\lambda_2}
=\boxed{\tau_2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This proves the exact source-facing infimum-over-flows characterization without any minimax exchange.

### 5.1 Electrical optimizer

For a zero-sum demand `b`, let

\[
\theta_*(b)=CB^TL^+b.
\]

Because `L` is symmetric with kernel spanned by `1`,

\[
B\theta_*=BCB^TL^+b=LL^+b=b.
\]

Its energy is

\[
\theta_*^TC^{-1}\theta_*=b^TL^+b,
\]

and the Moore-Penrose argument above shows it is the minimum-energy solution of `B theta=b`. Thus (3.3) is exactly the physical-coordinate form of the optimal pseudoinverse in Lemma 4.1 and attains (5.6).

### 5.2 Continuous time

For a finite irreducible reversible generator `Q`, replace `L` by `-Pi Q` and `c_xy` by `pi_x q_xy`. Then the nonzero eigenvalues of the weighted `DD^T` are the positive eigenvalues of `-Q`; the same proof gives the reciprocal of the smallest positive eigenvalue, exactly the source continuous-time `tau_2`.

## 7. The exact formula really does recover Aldous-Fill Theorem 4.32

The source explicitly says this derivation would be desirable, so I checked it rather than treating (5.6) alone as sufficient source fit.

Start from arbitrary ordered deterministic paths `gamma_xy`. Replace them by the reversal-symmetric randomized family `Gamma_xy` which is `gamma_xy` with probability `1/2` and the reversal of `gamma_yx` with probability `1/2`, coupled so `Gamma_yx` is the reversal of `Gamma_xy`. For every directed edge, the new load is the arithmetic mean of the old load on that direction and the old load on the reverse direction. Therefore its maximum directed resistance-weighted or length-weighted load cannot increase.

Let `phi^{xy}` be the expected signed unit flow of `Gamma_xy`. Then

\[
\phi^{yx}=-\phi^{xy}.
\]

Set

\[
\psi^x=\sum_y\pi_y\phi^{xy}.
\]

This family satisfies (6.2), so it is admissible in the exact formula.

To bound its operator norm, use the adjoint. Fix an edge flow `f` and define the edge voltage/co-vector

\[
\xi_e=f_e/c_e.
\]

Let

\[
D_{xy}=E\!\left[\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{xy}}\pm\xi_e\right],
\qquad
 g_x=\sum_y\pi_yD_{xy}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Reversal symmetry gives `D_xy=-D_yx`, hence `pi(g)=0`. For independent `X,Y~pi`, direct symmetrization yields

\[
E[D_{XY}(g_X-g_Y)]=2\,\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g),
\]

and

\[
E[(g_X-g_Y)^2]=2\,\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g).
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz therefore gives the exact projection inequality

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g)\le\frac12E[D_{XY}^2].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For the first path bound, Jensen followed by pathwise Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
D_{xy}^2
\le E\left[r(\Gamma_{xy})
\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{xy}}w_e\xi_e^2\right].
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let `kappa` be the maximum **directed** resistance load in (2.1) for the symmetrized path family. For each physical edge, reversal symmetry makes the two directed loads equal. Thus the sum over the two orientations contributes a factor `2`, which cancels the `1/2` in (7.2), giving

\[
\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g)
\le
\kappa\sum_{e\,\mathrm{physical}}w_e\xi_e^2.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For the discrete weighted graph, `c_e=w_e/w`, so

\[
\sum_e w_e\xi_e^2
=w\sum_e\frac{f_e^2}{c_e}
=w\|f\|_{C^{-1}}^2.
\tag{7.5}
\]

By Hilbert-space duality, (7.4)-(7.5) say the chosen routing has squared operator norm at most `w kappa`. The exact infimum formula (5.6) then yields (2.1).

For the second bound use instead

\[
\left(\sum_{e\in\Gamma}\pm\xi_e\right)^2
\le |\Gamma|\sum_{e\in\Gamma}\xi_e^2.
\]

If

\[
\kappa'=\max_{\vec e}\frac1{w_e}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_yE[|\Gamma_{xy}|1_{\{\vec e\in\Gamma_{xy}\}}],
\]

the identical orientation cancellation gives

\[
\mathrm{Var}_\pi(g)\le
\kappa'\sum_e w_e\xi_e^2
=w\kappa'\|f\|_{C^{-1}}^2,
\]

and (5.6) yields (2.2). Thus the candidate exact formula does what the source explicitly hoped an exact characterization would do.
