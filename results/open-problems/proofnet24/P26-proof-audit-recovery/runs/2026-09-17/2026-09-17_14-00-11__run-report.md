## 2. Exact theorem audited

Let `k` be a bounded continuous probability density on `R`. Assume there exist `h>0` and

\[
\Delta:=k(0)-\max\{k(-h),k(h)\}>0,
\qquad B:=\|k\|_\infty.
\]

Assume the left-tail envelope

\[
K(r):=\sup_{x\le-r} k(x)
\]

is superpolynomially light:

\[
r^N K(r)\to0\qquad(r\to\infty)
\]

for every `N>0`. Put `k_s(x)=s^{-1}k(x/s)`.

C010 claims that there is one probability measure `mu`, independent of `s`, such that

\[
\int |x|^p\,d\mu(x)<\infty
\]

for every finite `p>0`, while

\[
\boxed{\mu*k_s\text{ is nonunimodal for every }s>0.}
\]

The right-tail version follows by reflection.

## 3. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST — proof-joint falsification

I attacked the proof at every place where a hidden quantifier or inequality reversal could invalidate the all-scales conclusion.

### 3.1 Can the recursive sequence actually be chosen?

Take

\[
0<\varepsilon\le\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\Delta}{16B}\right\},
\qquad q_n=a_n^{-n}.
\]

R19 requires, after increasing each `a_n` if necessary,

\[
a_n\ge3(a_{n-1}+nh),
\quad q_n\le\varepsilon q_{n-1},
\quad K(a_n/(3n))\le(\Delta/8)q_n.
\]

For fixed `n`, superpolynomial decay with exponent `n+1` gives

\[
r^{n+1}K(r)\to0.
\]

Putting `a=3nr`,

\[
a^n K(a/(3n))=(3n)^n r^nK(r)\to0.
\]

Therefore the tail inequality holds for all sufficiently large `a`; increasing `a` simultaneously enforces the spacing inequality and decreases `a^{-n}`, so the geometric-weight inequality can also be imposed. There is no circularity.

### 3.2 Does the measure really have every polynomial moment?

After choosing `a_1` so that `q_1/(1-\varepsilon)\le1/2`, geometric decay gives `sum q_n<=1/2`, so

\[
\mu=\left(1-\sum_nq_n\right)\delta_0+\sum_n q_n\delta_{-a_n}
\]

is a probability measure. For fixed `p>0` and every integer `n>p`,

\[
q_na_n^p=a_n^{p-n}\le2^{p-n},
\]

because `a_n>=2`. Hence every finite moment converges.

### 3.3 Is the right-hand contamination estimate in the correct direction?

Fix arbitrary `s>0` and an integer `n>=s`. Write `c_n=-a_n`. At the center and shoulders `c_n`, `c_n\pm sh`, the `n`th atom contributes a center advantage at least

\[
\frac{\Delta q_n}{s}.
\]

For the atom at zero and every `j<n`, the spacing condition gives, at all three sample points, kernel arguments no larger than `-a_n/(3s)`; in fact the available inequality is stronger than needed. Therefore their total center-versus-shoulder contamination is bounded by

\[
\frac2s K(a_n/(3s)).
\]

Since `s<=n`,

\[
\frac{a_n}{3s}\ge\frac{a_n}{3n}.
\]

The envelope `K` is nonincreasing, so the required direction is

\[
K(a_n/(3s))\le K(a_n/(3n)),
\]

and therefore this contamination is at most `Delta q_n/(4s)`.

### 3.4 Is the farther-left geometric budget sufficient?

The geometric weight condition yields

\[
\sum_{j>n}q_j\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}q_n.
\]

Their contribution to a center-versus-shoulder difference is at most

\[
\frac{2B}{s}\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}q_n.
\]

Using `epsilon<=1/4` and `epsilon<=Delta/(16B)` actually gives the sharper coefficient `Delta/6`; hence R19's stated weaker `Delta/4` allowance is safe.

Combining the two contamination budgets leaves

\[
(\mu*k_s)(c_n)-(\mu*k_s)(c_n\pm sh)
\ge\frac{\Delta q_n}{2s}>0.
\]

### 3.5 Does this geometry really contradict unimodality for every fixed scale?

For fixed `s`, the intervals

\[
I_{n,s}=[c_n-sh,c_n+sh]
\]

are pairwise disjoint for all sufficiently large `n`, and `c_n\to-\infty`. A unimodal density has a mode `m` and is nondecreasing to its left and nonincreasing to its right. For all sufficiently large `n`, `I_{n,s}` lies entirely to the left of `m`, where nondecreasing behavior would force

\[
f_s(c_n)\le f_s(c_n+sh),
\]

contradicting the strict center-over-right-shoulder inequality above. Thus the conclusion holds for the arbitrary scale `s`, hence for every `s>0`.

No counterexample to C010 survived this audit.

## 4. #11 DUALIZE — isolate the real signal-versus-contamination lemma

The proof becomes clearer when recast as a budget inequality rather than as a special stable-law construction.

For any bounded continuous density with a strict local peak of height margin `Delta`, a sparse atom at `-a_n` contributes the **signal**

\[
S_n=\Delta q_n/s.
\]

All other atoms split into two adversarial budgets:

\[
R_n\le 2K(a_n/(3n))/s
\]

from mass to the right, and

\[
L_n\le 2B\sum_{j>n}q_j/s
\]

from later atoms to the left. The entire theorem is the strict dual inequality

\[
R_n+L_n<S_n.
\]

A slightly stronger abstract sufficient condition is therefore:

- `sum q_n a_n^p<infinity` for every finite `p`;
- the spacing condition makes earlier atoms sample only the protected light tail;
- `2K(a_n/(3n)) <= Delta q_n/4`;
- `2B sum_{j>n}q_j <= Delta q_n/4`.

Then the same `mu` creates infinitely many protected bumps at every scale. R19's choice `q_n=a_n^{-n}` plus superpolynomial tail decay is one explicit way to satisfy these four budgets simultaneously.

This reformulation also explains why the hypothesis is materially stronger than merely having one fixed polynomial tail exponent: the construction asks for protected scales with moment orders tending to infinity, while retaining all moments of `mu`. The proof is not secretly obtaining all moments from a fixed finite tail order.

## 5. #22 READ THE FAILURES — source and prior-art boundary

That historical failure explains exactly what C010 changes: it does not beat a polynomial tail with the same polynomial sparse ladder.
