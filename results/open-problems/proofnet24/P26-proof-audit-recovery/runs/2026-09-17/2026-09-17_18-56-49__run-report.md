## 3. Adversarial-first diagnosis

Before accepting C11 I tried to break the rate proof at the places where an asymptotic argument most often loses uniformity:

1. **Dense-extremizer threshold:** could a maximizing sequence have `m=o(n^2)`, invalidating the uniform bridge estimate? No. The balanced-barbell lower bound gives `M_n>=n^3/54-O(n^2)`, while the universal resistance estimate gives `K(G)<=m(n-1)`. Thus every maximizer has `m>=kappa n^2` for some fixed `kappa>0` and all large `n`.

2. **Density after surgery:** could the Alev edge deletion make the retained graph too sparse for the bridge theorem? No. With `delta=n^{1/4}`, `q=O(m/delta)=O(n^{7/4})=o(n^2)`, so the retained connected spanning graph has `m_H>=kappa n^2/2` eventually.

3. **Many tiny bridge blocks:** could their total `p`-mass be non-negligible even when each has few vertices? No. With cutoff `eta=n^{-1/2}`, simplicity gives
\[
\sum_{i\notin L}(m_i+1)\le \frac{\eta n^2}{2}+n,
\]
while `m+1>=kappa n^2`, hence `1-P=O_kappa(n^{-1/2})` uniformly.

4. **Quotient-path overcount:** could a large-to-large quotient path contain more than `(1-s)n+O(eta^{-1})` edges? No. Every small bridge block consumes at least one original vertex, so there are at most `(1-s)n` of them, and there are at most `eta^{-1}` large blocks.

5. **A hidden same-block contribution:** conditioned on both samples lying in large blocks, equal blocks contribute quotient distance zero. This is why the distinct-pair factor is exactly `1-Q_2`, not `1`.

6. **Parameter constants:** Alev et al.'s theorem is stated for any sufficiently large `delta>1` with the displayed asymptotic scales; taking `delta=n^{1/4}` is within scope for all sufficiently large `n`.

None of these attacks produced a counterexample or a missing uniformity hypothesis.

## 4. Independent reconstruction of the quantitative bridge bound C11a

Let `H` be connected, simple, `n`-vertex, with `m>=kappa n^2`. Delete all bridges and let the bridge blocks be `C_i`, with internal edge counts `m_i`; contract them to a tree `Q`. Put

\[
w_i=m_i+1,
\qquad
W=\sum_i w_i=m+1,
\qquad
p_i=w_i/W.
\]

P26's earlier C8 audit independently derived the exact identity

\[
K_{\rm br}(H)
=
\frac{W^2}{m}\,E_p d_Q(X,Y)
-
\frac{(2m+1)|E(Q)|}{2m}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The negative correction may be discarded for an upper bound.

Choose `eta=n^{-1/2}` and call block `i` large if `n_i>=eta n`. Write

\[
P=\sum_{i\in L}p_i,
\quad
s=\sum_{i\in L}n_i/n,
\quad
\widetilde p_i=p_i/P,
\quad
S=\sum_{i\in L}\sqrt{\widetilde p_i},
\quad
Q_2=\sum_{i\in L}\widetilde p_i^2.
\]

### 4.1 Uniform small-block mass

For a small block, `m_i<=n_i^2/2<=eta n n_i/2`, and the number of blocks is at most `n`. Hence

\[
1-P
\le
\frac{\eta n^2/2+n}{W}
=O_\kappa(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular `P>=1/2` eventually.

### 4.2 Capacity inequality

Simplicity gives

\[
w_i=m_i+1\le n_i^2/2+1,
\]
so

\[
\sqrt{p_i}
\le
\frac{n_i}{\sqrt{2W}}+rac1{\sqrt W}.
\]

There are at most `eta^{-1}` large blocks. If `A=W/n^2`, summing and multiplying by `sqrt(2W)/n` yields

\[
\sqrt{2AP}\,S
\le
s+\epsilon_c,
\qquad
\epsilon_c=\frac{\sqrt2}{\eta n}=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
therefore

\[
A\le \frac{(s+\epsilon_c)^2}{2PS^2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.3 Quotient distance

For two distinct large blocks, the tree path meets at most all small blocks and all large blocks, hence

\[
\frac{d_Q(i,j)}n\le1-s+\epsilon_d,
\qquad
\epsilon_d=\frac1{\eta n}=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Pairs with at least one small sample have total probability at most `2(1-P)` and distance at most `n-1`. Thus

\[
\frac1n E_p d_Q(X,Y)
\le
P^2(1-Q_2)(1-s+\epsilon_d)+2(1-P).
\tag{4.4}
\]

### 4.4 Sharp scalar reduction

Divide (4.1) by `n^3`, use `(W^2/(mn^2))=(W/m)A` and `W/m=1+O_kappa(n^{-2})`, then insert (4.3)--(4.4). The leading term is at most

\[
\frac{(s+\epsilon_c)^2(1-s+\epsilon_d)}2
\frac{1-Q_2}{S^2},
\]
up to a harmless factor `P<=1`; the small-sample term is `O_kappa(n^{-1/2})`.

Hölder gives

\[
Q_2S^2\ge1,
\]
so

\[
\frac{1-Q_2}{S^2}
\le Q_2(1-Q_2)
\le\frac14.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Uniformly for `0<=s<=1`,

\[
\frac{(s+O(n^{-1/2}))^2(1-s+O(n^{-1/2}))}{8}
\le
\frac1{54}+O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
because

\[
\max_{0\le s\le1}\frac{s^2(1-s)}8=\frac1{54}
\]
at `s=2/3`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm br}(H)
\le
\frac{n^3}{54}+O_\kappa(n^{5/2}).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the new uniform quantitative ingredient C11a.

## 5. Method #5 — parameterize both cutoffs and recover the `11/4` exponent

The rate is not an arbitrary exponent inserted after the fact.

First put the bridge cutoff at `eta=n^{-b}`. The small-block mass error is `O(n^{-b})`, while the large-block-count/capacity/path error is `O(n^{b-1})`. Their maximum is minimized when

\[
-b=b-1,
\qquad b=1/2,
\]
which is exactly the cutoff used above and gives bridge error `n^3 n^{-1/2}=n^{5/2}`.

Next put the Alev scale at `delta=n^a`. For a dense maximizer, the four relevant absolute error exponents are

\[
3-a,
\qquad
3-2a,
\qquad
2+3a,
\qquad
5/2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

They come respectively from the linear C4 surgery term, the quadratic C4 surgery term, the C7 internal-resistance term, and C11a. The decreasing branch `3-a` and increasing branch `2+3a` meet at

\[
a=1/4,
\]
where the four exponents are

\[
11/4,\quad5/2,\quad11/4,\quad5/2.
\]

Thus `delta=n^{1/4}` minimizes the worst exponent in this proof architecture and forces the rate `11/4`.
