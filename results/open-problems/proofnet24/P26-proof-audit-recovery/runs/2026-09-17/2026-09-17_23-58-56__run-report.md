- whether the freeness suggestion was silently promoted into a required source hypothesis.

None produces a defect.

The only major external theorem is Paul-Henry Leemann and Mikael de la Salle, *Cayley graphs with few automorphisms: the case of infinite groups*, Annales Henri Lebesgue 5 (2022), 73–92, DOI `10.5802/ahl.118`. I inspected the primary PDF directly. Their Theorem 1.1 states that every finitely generated non-virtually-abelian group admits a finite-degree Cayley graph whose automorphism group is no larger than the group acting by left translations; since left translations are always automorphisms, this is a GRR.

The generating-set-preservation point also survives direct source inspection. Their Section 3 says that if the first orientation-rigid set is fed into the second step, the resulting Cayley graph has only translations as automorphisms. Theorem 3.1 starts from an **arbitrary finite symmetric generating set** `S_0` and takes

`S_1 = (S_0 union S_0^2 union S_0^3) \ {1}`,

so `S_0 subseteq S_1`. Proposition 3.2 then enlarges a finite generating set `S` to a finite symmetric `S_tilde` with `S subseteq S_tilde` while making old edge colors recognizable. Thus the construction can begin with P20's prescribed finite set and retain it in the final GRR. This is the precise dependency the proof needs.

## 3. Independent mathematical reconstruction

Let

`Lambda = S_3 * A_5`,

let `pi: Lambda -> S_3` be identity on the `S_3` free factor and trivial on the `A_5` factor, let

`H = <(12)> <= S_3`,

and put

`A = pi^{-1}(H)`.

Take

`S_0 = (S_3 \ {1}) union (A_5 \ {1})`.

This is a finite symmetric generating set of `Lambda`.

### 3.1 The GRR theorem applies

`Lambda` is finitely generated and infinite by the free-product normal-form theorem. It is not virtually abelian: choosing an order-two element in the `S_3` factor and an order-three element in the `A_5` factor gives a subgroup `C_2 * C_3`, which contains a nonabelian free subgroup of finite index and is not virtually abelian. A subgroup of a virtually abelian group would be virtually abelian, so `Lambda` itself is not virtually abelian.

Apply the Leemann–de la Salle relative construction starting from `S_0`. It yields a finite symmetric `S` with

`S_0 subseteq S subseteq Lambda \ {1}`

such that

`G = Cay(Lambda,S)`

has

`Aut(G) = Lambda_L`,

the left-regular translation action. Because `S` is finite, symmetric, identity-free and generating, `G` is infinite, simple, connected, locally finite and transitive. Hence it lies in the source ambient class.

Set `Gamma = Lambda_L` and identify `A` with its subgroup of left translations.

### 3.2 Non-normality and freeness

`H` has order two and index three in `S_3`. It is not normal: its conjugates are the three transposition subgroups. Since `pi` is onto, normality of `A=pi^{-1}(H)` would imply normality of `pi(A)=H`, contradiction. Thus `A` is non-normal.

The left-regular action is free. Therefore every subgroup of it, including `A`, acts freely on vertices. The construction satisfies the source's subsidiary freeness suggestion without using it as an extra assumption.

### 3.3 Exact quotient identification

The `A`-orbits of the left action are the left cosets `A g`. The map

`A g -> H pi(g)`

is a well-defined bijection from the orbit set to the three left cosets of `H` in `S_3`.

Take two distinct quotient vertices and choose representatives `r,t in S_3` from their two distinct `H`-cosets. Then `r^{-1}t != 1`, hence

`r^{-1}t in S_3 \ {1} subseteq S_0 subseteq S`.

Thus `r` and `t` are adjacent in the Cayley graph, so the corresponding two distinct `A`-orbits are adjacent in the source's simple quotient. Every pair of distinct quotient vertices is adjacent:

`G_A ~= K_3`.

Extra generators added by the GRR construction cannot spoil this: the quotient is already complete on its three vertices, and the source suppresses loops by defining adjacency only between distinct orbits.

Therefore source condition (i) holds.

### 3.4 The `Gamma'` quantifier collapses exactly

This is method **#14 FIND THE GROUP ACTING**. The GRR property says the full automorphism group is the regular group `Lambda_L`.

If `Gamma' <= Lambda_L` acts transitively on the vertex set `Lambda`, then the orbit of the identity under `Gamma'` is all of `Lambda`. But for the left-regular action that orbit is exactly the set of group elements represented by `Gamma'`. Hence

`Gamma' = Lambda_L`.

There is no hidden larger or proper transitively acting automorphism group left to check. The source's universal obstruction therefore reduces to normal subgroups of one explicit group.

### 3.5 No normal quotient can be `K_3`

Suppose `N normal Lambda_L` and `G_N ~= K_3`. Under the regular identification, `N` corresponds to a normal subgroup `N_0 normal Lambda`, and its orbits are left cosets of `N_0`.

This is method **#25 COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE**: `K_3` has three graph vertices, while the orbit count is the group index. Hence

`[Lambda:N_0]=3`.

Normality therefore gives a group quotient of order three:

`Lambda/N_0 ~= C_3`.

But

`Lambda^ab = (S_3*A_5)^ab ~= S_3^ab x A_5^ab ~= C_2 x 1 ~= C_2`.

Every homomorphism from `Lambda` to the abelian group `C_3` factors through `C_2`, so no surjection to `C_3` exists. Contradiction.

Therefore source condition (ii) holds with its full transitive-overgroup quantifier.

Combining the preceding steps gives a complete source-valid YES witness for the literal Question 4.5.

A normal three-coset subgroup would force exactly the `C_3` quotient that abelianization forbids. The non-normality is therefore the mechanism, not decoration.
