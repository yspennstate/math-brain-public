## 5. #1 CHANGE THE CATEGORY — dependence becomes one-dimensional after conditioning

The interior theorem becomes much clearer when the random-graph problem is moved into the category of order statistics plus one-shared-coordinate covariance.

Let `X` be the `(k+1)`st smallest of the root's `n-1` edge scores, and condition on `X` and the selected neighbor set. The selected root-edge scores are iid `Uniform(0,X)`. For selected neighbor `i`, let `T_i` be the `k`th smallest among its `n-2` non-root incident scores. Then

\[
T_i\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,n-k-1)
\]

and the root edge is rejected at `i` iff its score `U_i` is at least `T_i`.

For an independent `T` with that beta law, define

\[
q_n(x)=\frac1x\mathbb E[(x-T)_+].
\]

Then exactly

\[
\mathbb E[H\mid X]=kq_n(X).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For distinct selected neighbors `i,j`, their rejection indicators share exactly one non-root score, the edge `W=U_{ij}`. Given `(X,W)`, their remaining raw edge sets are disjoint, so the indicators are conditionally independent. If the root-edge score at `i` is `u`, deleting `W` leaves

\[
B_u\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-3,u),
\]

and rejection occurs iff

\[
B_u+\mathbf1_{\{W<u\}}\ge k.
\]

Therefore the conditional success function `p_X(w)` has oscillation at most

\[
\frac1X\int_0^1
\binom{n-3}{k-1}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{n-k-2}\,du
=
\frac1{(n-2)X}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The last equality is exact, not asymptotic. Popoviciu then gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X)
\le\frac1{4(n-2)^2X^2},
\]

hence

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H\mid X)
\le kq_n(X)+\frac{k(k-1)}{4(n-2)^2X^2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is the decisive structural reduction: the graph's global-looking bilateral dependence reduces, after conditioning on the root cutoff, to one shared scalar edge per pair.

On the good event

\[
G_n=\{X\ge k/(2n)\},
\]

the covariance term is `O(1)`. An exponential-Markov bound on the order statistic gives

\[
\Pr(G_n^c)\le e^{-(\log2-1/2)k}.
\]

Also on `G_n`,

\[
kq_n(X)
\le 2n\,\mathbb E(|X-T|\mid X),
\]

and the beta variance formulas yield

\[
\mathbb E[kq_n(X);G_n]=O(s_n+1).
\]

Since `s_n -> infinity` whenever both `k -> infinity` and `n-k -> infinity`, while `0<=H<=k` handles the exponentially small bad event,

\[
\frac{H-kq_n(X)}{s_n}\to0
\quad\text{in }L^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the only first-order randomness is the root cutoff itself.

## 7. #1 again at the dense boundary — endpoint geometry becomes a marked Poisson process

For fixed integer

\[
r=n-1-k\ge1,
\]

every vertex rejects exactly its `r` largest incident scores. Introduce the endpoint coordinate

\[
Y_i^{(n)}=n(1-U_{0i}).
\]

Let `A_{n,r}` be the `r`th smallest root coordinate. The root proposes edge `(0,i)` iff

\[
Y_i^{(n)}>A_{n,r}.
\]

If `Y_i^{(n)}=y`, endpoint `i` rejects the root edge exactly when at most `r-1` of its other `n-2` scores exceed it, so

\[
b_{n,r}(y)
:=
\Pr(J_i^{(n)}=1\mid Y_i^{(n)}=y)
=
\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(n-2,y/n)\le r-1\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The root coordinate process converges to a rate-one Poisson point process on `(0,\infty)`, and

\[
A_{n,r}\Rightarrow A_r,
\qquad A_r\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r,1).
\]

The only local dependence between two endpoint-rejection marks is their shared edge. On any fixed compact coordinate window `[0,L]`, delete the finitely many candidate-candidate edges. The remaining endpoint stars are disjoint. A deleted candidate-candidate edge can change a mark only if it exceeds one of the root-edge thresholds, with probability at most `L/n`. For fixed `m` candidate points, the total perturbation probability is therefore

\[
O(m^2L/n)=o(1).
\]

Hence, on compacts, the marks become asymptotically independent with retention probability

\[
p_r(y)=e^{-y}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{y^j}{j!}
=\Pr(\operatorname{Gamma}(r,1)>y).
\tag{7.2}
\]

The compact cutoff is removable because, uniformly for large `n`,

\[
b_{n,r}(y)
\le e^{-y/2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{y^j}{j!},
\]

an integrable envelope. Conditioning on the unique shared edge gives the corresponding product envelope for second factorial moments, so distribution, mean, and variance all pass to the limit.

Conditional on `A_r=a`, the post-`a` points form an independent rate-one Poisson process. Thinning by (7.2) gives

\[
H_r\mid A_r=a
\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_r(a)),
\]

where

\[
\Lambda_r(a)
=
\int_a^\infty p_r(y)\,dy
=
e^{-a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}(r-\ell)\frac{a^\ell}{\ell!}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

This establishes the fixed-complement law and the first-two-moment convergence required for standardization.
