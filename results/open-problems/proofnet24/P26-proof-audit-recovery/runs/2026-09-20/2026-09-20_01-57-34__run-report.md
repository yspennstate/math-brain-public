## 4. #20 FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS — the latent root cutoff is the real fluctuation

The obvious route would treat the `k` root-preferred edges as weakly dependent Bernoulli retention trials and try a generic dependency-graph CLT. That architecture misses the dominant dependence. All `k` indicators share the root's random `(k+1)`st order statistic `X`; this common cutoff produces fluctuations on the natural scale `s_n` and survives in the non-Gaussian limit.

Conditioning on `X` exposes the right decomposition. If `S` is the root's preferred set, the selected root-edge scores are iid `Uniform(0,X)`. For `i in S`, let `T_i` be the `k`th order statistic among the `n-2` other scores at endpoint `i`. Then

\[
T_i\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k,n-k-1),
\qquad
q_n(x)=\frac1x\mathbb E[(x-T)_+],
\]

and exactly

\[
\mathbb E[H\mid X]=kq_n(X).
\]

Two rejection indicators share exactly one non-root edge. Conditioning on that edge makes their remaining stars disjoint. Varying the shared edge can matter only at the pivotal count `k-1`, whose exact beta integral is

\[
\binom{n-3}{k-1}
B(k,n-k-1)=\frac1{n-2}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_j\mid X)
\le\frac{1}{4(n-2)^2X^2}.
\]

On `X >= k/(2n)` the entire pair-covariance contribution is `O(1)`, while the complement has exponentially small probability in `k`. The conditional Bernoulli noise is therefore `o(s_n^2)`, whereas the random conditional mean `kq_n(X)` fluctuates at order `s_n`. After standardizing the beta variables, the exact identity

\[
\frac{kq_n(X)}{s_n}
=
\frac{k}{nX}\,g_n(U_n)
\]

converges to `g(Z)` with first-two-moment convergence.

This diagnosis independently explains both the non-Gaussian limit and why a generic independent-edge or local-dependency approximation is the wrong proof engine. The dependence is not an uncontrolled global mess; after conditioning on the correct latent variable, the only residual pair interaction is one edge and is lower order.

**Outcome of #20:** PASS. The reported proof mechanism addresses the source's stated dependence obstruction rather than hiding it behind an unjustified independence approximation.

**Interior, both sides diverging.** The argument in Section 4 above survives arbitrarily slow divergence of either `k` or `n-k`; there is no hidden assumption that `k/n` tends to a number in `(0,1)`.

**Dense boundary, fixed positive `r=n-1-k`.** Put `Y=n(1-U)`. The root rejects its `r` smallest `Y` values. Conditional on a candidate coordinate `Y=y`, the opposite endpoint rejects exactly when

\[
\operatorname{Bin}(n-2,y/n)\le r-1.
\]

On bounded `Y` windows, finitely many candidate marks share only finitely many mutual edges; deleting those edges leaves disjoint endpoint stars, and the probability that a deleted edge changes a mark is `O(1/n)`. Finally the boundary really glues to the interior. With independent `A_r,G_r ~ Gamma(r,1)`,

\[
\Lambda_r(A_r)=\mathbb E[(G_r-A_r)_+\mid A_r].
\]

After centering/scaling the gamma variables, the CLT and bounded fourth moments imply

\[
\frac{\Lambda_r(A_r)}{\sqrt r}
\Longrightarrow g(-Z)\stackrel d=g(Z),
\]

while the conditional Poisson noise is negligible because

\[
\frac1r\mathbb E[(H_r-\Lambda_r(A_r))^2]
=
\frac{\mathbb E H_r}{r}
=O(r^{-1/2}).
\]

Hence `H_r/sqrt(r) => g(Z)` with first-two-moment convergence, matching `s_n^2 ~ r+1` on the dense edge of the interior regime.
