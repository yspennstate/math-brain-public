## 5. Audited theorem

Let `P=(p_xy)` be the transition matrix of a finite irreducible discrete-time Markov chain on `V`. Put

`L=I-P`, `pi P=pi`, `Pi=1 pi^T`, and

`Z=(L+Pi)^(-1)-Pi`.

Let `A` be nonempty and `U=V\A`. An `A`-rooted converging spanning forest is a directed spanning forest in which every `a in A` has outdegree zero, every `v notin A` has outdegree one, and every directed path eventually reaches `A`; its weight is the product of its transition weights. Write

`F_A = sum_{A-rooted forests F} w(F)`

and let `T=sum_r F_{ {r} }` be the total rooted-tree weight. For `i,j in U`, write `F_{A union {j}}(i->j)` for the total weight of forests rooted at `A union {j}` in which `i` lies in the tree rooted at `j`.

Define the frozen Aldous–Fill marked denominator

`M_A = [[Z_A^T, -pi_A],[1^T,0]]`,  `Delta_A=det M_A`.

The audited P02 package asserts:

1. `G_A=(I-P_UU)^(-1)` and
   
   `G_A(i,j)=F_{A union {j}}(i->j)/F_A`;

2. `Delta_A=F_A/T>0`;

3. for the inherited C3 bordered numerator `N_{A;i,j}`,
   
   `N_{A;i,j}=F_{A union {j}}(i->j)/T`;

4. for arbitrary integrable stopping data with initial law `sigma`, terminal law supported on `A`, occupation row `x`, and prescribed boundary occupations `c=x_A`, define
   
   `mu_q=sigma_q+sum_{a in A} c_a p_aq` for `q in U`.
   Then
   
   `x_j = [sum_{q in U} mu_q F_{A union {j}}(q->j)]/F_A`,
   
   and this equals the inherited C3 marked `pi,Z` bordered-determinant ratio;

5. in particular the source prototype with distinct `i,j,k,l` and `A={k,l}` is
   
   `E_i[# visits to j before min(T_k,T_l)] = F_{ {k,l,j} }(i->j)/F_{ {k,l} }`,
   
   and the same value is a ratio of two formal determinants using only the marked variables with indices in `{i,j,k,l}`.

## 6. Full independent proof

### 6.1 Conservation and the killed Green kernel

Let

`x_v = E_sigma sum_{t<T} 1_{X_t=v}`

for an integrable stopping time `T`, and let `tau` be the terminal law. Standard one-step accounting gives the exact row-vector conservation identity

`x(I-P)=sigma-tau`.                                              (6.1)

When `tau` is supported on `A`, restrict (6.1) to columns in `U`. Since the `A x U` block of `L=I-P` is `-P_AU`, and `x_A=c`,

`x_U L_UU - c P_AU = sigma_U`,

so

`x_U=(sigma_U+cP_AU)L_UU^(-1)=mu G_A`.                           (6.2)

Because the chain is finite irreducible and `A` is nonempty, every state hits `A` almost surely. Hence the killed matrix `P_UU` has spectral radius below one, so `L_UU=I-P_UU` is invertible and

`G_A=L_UU^(-1)`.

For first-hit occupation from `i`, `(G_A)_{ij}` is exactly the expected number of visits to `j` before `T_A`.

### 6.2 Forest evaluation of the inverse

For the row-Laplacian `L=I-P`, off-diagonal arc `x->y` has weight `p_xy`; self-loops do not appear as forest arcs and are already absorbed by `L_xx=sum_{y != x}p_xy`.

The directed matrix-tree/all-minors theorem gives

`det L_UU = F_A`.                                                (6.3)

By Cramer’s rule,

`(L_UU^(-1))_{ij} = C_{ji}(L_UU)/det L_UU`,

and the same all-minors theorem identifies the cofactor with the rooted-grove weight

`C_{ji}(L_UU)=F_{A union {j}}(i->j)`.                            (6.4)

Combining (6.3)–(6.4),

`G_A(i,j)=F_{A union {j}}(i->j)/F_A`.                           (6.5)

Substitution in (6.2) proves the arbitrary boundary-data forest formula

`x_j = [sum_q mu_q F_{A union {j}}(q->j)]/F_A`.                 (6.6)

Thus the intrinsic combinatorial object is established without using the bordered determinant.

### 6.3 Exact frozen-denominator bridge

The group-inverse relations are

`LZ=ZL=I-Pi`, `ZPi=PiZ=0`.

For `t>0`, put `L_t=L+tPi`. Direct multiplication gives

`L_t^(-1)=Z+t^(-1)Pi`.                                          (6.7)

Let `T_r` be the total weight of trees converging to root `r`. The Markov-chain tree theorem gives `pi_r=T_r/T`. Since the right and left nullspaces of `L` are spanned by `1` and `pi`, respectively, its adjugate is

`adj(L)=T 1 pi^T`.

The rank-one determinant identity therefore gives exactly

`det L_t = t pi^T adj(L) 1 = t T`.                              (6.8)

Apply Jacobi’s complementary principal-minor identity to the invertible matrix `L_t`:

`det((L_t^(-1))_AA) = det((L_t)_UU)/det L_t`.                   (6.9)

By (6.7), the left side is

`det(Z_A+t^(-1)1_A pi_A^T)`

`= det Z_A + t^(-1) pi_A^T adj(Z_A) 1_A`.                      (6.10)

The bordered determinant satisfies

`Delta_A = det [[Z_A^T,-pi_A],[1^T,0]]`

`= pi_A^T adj(Z_A)1_A`.                                        (6.11)

On the right side of (6.9),

`(L_t)_UU=L_UU+t1_U pi_U^T`,

so using (6.8),

`det((L_t)_UU)/det L_t = det(L_UU+t1_U pi_U^T)/(tT)`.           (6.12)

Multiply (6.9) by `t` and let `t` decrease to zero. Equations (6.10)–(6.12) and (6.3) yield

`Delta_A = det L_UU/T = F_A/T >0`.                              (6.13)

This independently proves the crucial invertibility/positivity of the frozen C3 denominator.

### 6.4 Marked-data bordered system

From (6.1), multiply on the right by `Z`:

`xLZ=(sigma-tau)Z`.

Since `LZ=I-Pi`, and if `s=x1`, then `xPi=s pi`, hence

`x = sigma Z - tau Z + s pi`.                                  (6.14)

On the boundary `A`, `x_A=c` and `tau` is supported on `A`. Thus for each `a in A`,

`sum_{b in A} tau_b Z_ba - s pi_a = (sigma Z)_a - c_a`,

and probability conservation gives `sum_{b in A}tau_b=1`. Therefore the unknown vector `(tau_A,s)` satisfies exactly

`[[Z_A^T,-pi_A],[1^T,0]] [tau_A;s] = [(sigma Z)_A-c;1]`.        (6.15)

Because (6.13) shows `Delta_A>0`, this system has a unique solution. Cramer’s rule, followed by (6.14), is precisely the inherited C3 bordered numerator/denominator formula for every non-boundary coordinate `x_j`.

But (6.2) independently determines the same occupation vector uniquely from the killed Green kernel. Therefore the C3 marked determinant ratio and the rooted-grove quotient (6.6) are equal. In the first-hit point-mass case this gives

`N_{A;i,j}/Delta_A = F_{A union {j}}(i->j)/F_A`.

Using (6.13),

`N_{A;i,j}=F_{A union {j}}(i->j)/T`.                            (6.16)

That proves the claimed normalized rooted-grove meaning of both numerator and denominator.

### 6.5 The source’s four-mark prototype

Take distinct `i,j,k,l` and `A={k,l}`. Equation (6.5) gives

`E_i[#j before min(T_k,T_l)] = F_{ {k,l,j} }(i->j)/F_{ {k,l} }`.

The forest itself is defined on the full transition graph, but its boundary type is specified by the source marks: `k,l,j` are roots and `i` belongs to the component rooted at `j`. Unmarked states are internal forest vertices and are summed over.

Equations (6.13) and (6.16) then evaluate the same quotient by the formal marked-data determinants displayed in the P02 report. Those determinants use only `p_v` and `z_vw` with indices among `{i,j,k,l}` after the substitutions `p=pi`, `z=Z`.

This meets the literal source request: an intrinsic combinatorial structure involving the marks, followed by a formal marked-variable function whose evaluation equals the occupation expression.

### 6.6 Strict returns, stationary starts and the frozen 11/11 coverage

The C3/C5 theorem is not limited to first hitting. For a strict return `T_i^+`, take `A={i}`, `sigma=e_i`, and because time 0 is included while the stopping time itself is excluded, `c_i=1`. Then (6.2) gives `mu_q=p_iq` on `U`, the exact first-step decomposition of a return-cycle occupation measure.

Stationary-start identities take `sigma=pi,c=0`; (6.14) simplifies using `pi Z=0`. Ordinary first-hit identities take point-mass `sigma` and `c=0`. Hence the frozen eleven Section 2.2.2 identities are specializations of one proved arbitrary-boundary-data theorem with the same `Delta_A`.
