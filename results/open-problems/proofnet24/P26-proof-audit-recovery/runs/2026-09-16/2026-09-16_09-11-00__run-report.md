## 4. New theorem: a deterministic Stein near-Haar bound

### Claim `P26-T4-ACTIVE-STEIN-NEAR-HAAR-22 = PROVED`

For every deterministic rank-`s` residual plane `P` as above,

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|\Sigma_P-
 \frac{2}{s(s+2)}I_{\mathrm{Sym}_s^0}
 \right\|_{\rm op}
 \le
 \frac{19}{s}\sqrt{2\left(e(P)+\frac{s^2}{d}\right)}.
 }
\]

This is an explicit deterministic comparison of the actual physical determinant-selected active covariance with the Haar coefficient-sphere covariance.

### Proof

#### 4.1 Gaussianization without changing the relevant degree-two expectation

Let `G_0~N(0,I_d)` and define

\[
 Z_i=\frac1{\sqrt2}G_0^\top A_iG_0,
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,s.
\]

Because the `A_i` are trace-free and Frobenius-orthonormal,

\[
 \mathbb EZ=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E Z_iZ_j=\delta_{ij}.
\]

Writing `G_0=RU` with `R` independent of uniform `U`, one has

\[
 Z=\rho f(U),
 \qquad
 \rho=\frac{R^2}{\sqrt{d(d+2)}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\rho^2=1.
\]

For any homogeneous function `h` of degree two,

\[
 \mathbb Eh(Z)=\mathbb Eh(f(U)).
\]

#### 4.2 Exact Stein kernel and its Casimir error

For the standard Gaussian gradient,

\[
 D Z_i=\sqrt2 A_iG_0.
\]

Since each `Z_i` lies in the second Gaussian chaos, direct Gaussian integration by parts gives, for every smooth test function `phi`,

\[
 \mathbb E[Z_i\phi(Z)]
 =\sum_j\mathbb E[\tau_{ij}\,\partial_j\phi(Z)],
\]

with

\[
 \tau_{ij}
 =\frac12\langle DZ_i,DZ_j\rangle
 =G_0^\top B_{ij}G_0,
 \qquad
 B_{ij}=\frac{A_iA_j+A_jA_i}{2}.
\]

Also `E tau_ij=delta_ij`.

Put

\[
 x_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(A_i^2A_j^2),
 \qquad
 y_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(A_iA_jA_iA_j).
\]

For a Gaussian quadratic form,

\[
 \operatorname{Var}\tau_{ij}
 =2\|B_{ij}\|_F^2
 =x_{ij}+y_{ij}
 \le2x_{ij},
\]

because `y_ij<=x_ij` by the commutator identity. Summing gives

\[
 \mathbb E\|\tau-I_s\|_F^2
 \le2\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{tr}(A_i^2A_j^2)
 =2\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2
 =2G(P).
\]

Moreover

\[
 \operatorname{tr}_s\tau-s
 =G_0^\top C(P)G_0-s,
\]

hence

\[
 \mathbb E|\operatorname{tr}(\tau-I_s)|^2
 =2\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2
 =2G(P).
\]

#### 4.3 The angular quadratic test has a uniform Hessian bound

Fix `M\in Sym_s^0` with `||M||_F=1`, and set

\[
 h_M(x)=\frac{(x^\top Mx)^2}{\|x\|^2}
\]

for `x!=0`, with `h_M(0)=0`. Let

\[
 q=x^\top Mx,\qquad r=\|x\|^2,\qquad u=Mx.
\]

For `x!=0`, direct differentiation gives

\[
 \nabla^2h_M
 =\frac{8uu^\top+4qM}{r}
 -\frac{8q(ux^\top+xu^\top)}{r^2}
 +\frac{8q^2xx^\top}{r^3}
 -\frac{2q^2I}{r^2}.
\]

Since `||M||_op<=||M||_F=1`, `|q|<=r`, and `||u||<=sqrt(r)`. Therefore for every symmetric matrix `S`, termwise Frobenius contraction yields

\[
 |S:\nabla^2h_M(x)|
 \le36\|S\|_F+2|\operatorname{tr}S|.
\]

Indeed the four non-scalar contributions are bounded respectively by `8`, `4`, `16`, and `8` times `||S||_F`; the identity term contributes at most `2|tr S|`. The function is `C^{1,1}` at the origin (the Hessian is bounded almost everywhere); the following interpolation can therefore be justified by standard mollification and dominated convergence, so no hidden smoothness assumption is used.

#### 4.4 Gaussian interpolation

Let `Y~N(0,I_s)` be independent of `Z`, and

\[
 X_t=\sqrt t\,Z+\sqrt{1-t}\,Y.
\]

Apply the above Stein identity to the `Z` term and ordinary Gaussian integration by parts to the `Y` term. The chain-rule factors cancel exactly, giving

\[
 \frac d{dt}\mathbb Eh_M(X_t)
 =\frac12\mathbb E[(\tau-I_s):\nabla^2h_M(X_t)].
\]

Using the Hessian bound and Cauchy-Schwarz with the two exact `L^2` controls,

\[
 \left|\frac d{dt}\mathbb Eh_M(X_t)\right|
 \le\frac12(36+2)\sqrt{2G(P)}
 =19\sqrt{2G(P)}.
\]

Integration over `t in [0,1]` gives

\[
 |\mathbb Eh_M(Z)-\mathbb Eh_M(Y)|
 \le19\sqrt{2G(P)}.
\]

#### 4.5 Gaussian comparator and return to determinant selection

Write `Y=R_s Theta`, with `Theta` Haar on `S^{s-1}`. Since `E R_s^2=s` and `tr M=0`, `||M||_F=1`,

\[
 \mathbb E(\Theta^\top M\Theta)^2
 =\frac{2}{s(s+2)},
\]

so

\[
 \mathbb Eh_M(Y)=\frac2{s+2}.
\]

On the other hand, determinant selection gives exactly

\[
 \mathbb E_{\rm sel}(c^\top Mc)^2
 =\frac1s\mathbb E_\mu
 \frac{(f^\top Mf)^2}{F}
 =\frac1s\mathbb Eh_M(f).
\]

Because `h_M` is homogeneous of degree two and `E rho^2=1`, `E h_M(f)=E h_M(Z)`. Hence

\[
 \left|
 s\langle M,\Sigma_PM\rangle-\frac2{s+2}
 \right|
 \le19\sqrt{2G(P)}.
\]

Taking the supremum over trace-free Frobenius-unit `M` proves the boxed operator bound. QED.

## 6. Why this still does not close Target 4

The endpoint is not obtained by simply inserting the unweighted operator estimate into the innovation recurrence. P20's exact product-sector term is

\[
 \mathrm{Mix}_s(P)
 =\operatorname{Tr}A_P\left(\frac Is-\Sigma_P\right),
\]

where `A_P` is positive semidefinite on the active trace-free coefficient sector. Since

\[
 \frac Is-\Sigma_{\rm Haar}
 =\frac1{s+2}I,
\]

the new theorem gives the deterministic lower bound

\[
 \mathrm{Mix}_s(P)
 \ge
 \frac1{s+2}\operatorname{Tr}A_P
 -\frac{19}{s}\sqrt{2G(P)}\,\operatorname{Tr}A_P.
\]

The second term is the exact remaining seam. Plain `L1` convergence of `Sigma_P` is not enough because `A_P` is built from the same residual plane and weights exceptional high-product-energy configurations. A rare set with large `G(P)` can therefore dominate the weighted error even though the unweighted covariance is close to Haar.

### Claim `P26-T4-WEIGHTED-STEIN-TENSOR-22 = OPEN`

A sufficient next theorem is a rank-summable estimate, at the scale required by the exact P20/P04 telescope, for

\[
 \mathbb E\left[
 \sqrt{G(P_s)}\,\operatorname{Tr}A_{P_s}
 \right].
\]

This is the correct weighted version of the newly proved near-Haar statement. A cruder route would combine the known deterministic product-sector trace control with a `G(P_s)^{3/2}` moment bound, but that route is recorded only as a candidate until its constants are inserted into the exact telescope. No endpoint is claimed from it here.
