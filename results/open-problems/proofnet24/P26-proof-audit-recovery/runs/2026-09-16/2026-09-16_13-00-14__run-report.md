# 3. Universal determinant-volume normalization

Let `mu` be a probability law on `R^m` satisfying

\[
\mathbb E_\mu yy^\top=I_m.
\]

For `j<=m`, let `Y_j` have `j` iid rows with law `mu`, and let

\[
G_j=Y_jY_j^\top.
\]

Gram-Schmidt gives

\[
\det G_j
=
\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}R_{m-\ell+1},
\]

where at the `ell`-th append step the residual squared norm has conditional mean equal to the current residual dimension. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Z_j:=\mathbb E\det G_j
=
\prod_{q=m-j+1}^{m}q
=
\frac{m!}{(m-j)!}.
}
\]

This uses isotropy only; no Gaussianity or spherical symmetry is required.

Define determinant-volume measure

\[
\frac{dQ_j}{d\mu^j}
=
\frac{\det G_j}{Z_j}.
\]

This includes the normalized physical quadratic-harmonic row law used by P04/P20/P26.

# 4. `P26-T4-INVERSE-MINOR-CONDITIONAL-CANCELLATION-26 = PROVED`

## 4.1 Statement

Fix `k<=m`, set

\[

\]

\[

Assume the complement is full row rank; this holds `Q_k`-almost surely on the determinant-volume support. Let `P_B` be orthogonal projection onto the complement of the row span of `B`. Its rank is

\[
q=m-(k-r)=s+r.
\]

For the rows indexed by `I`, form their projected `r x q` row matrix `X`, and let

\[
A=XX^\top.
\]

Then for every `alpha` for which the right-hand side is interpreted in `[0,infinity]`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_k}
\left[
(\det K_{I,I})^\alpha
\mid B
\right]
=
\frac{s!}{(s+r)!}
\int
(\det A)^{1-\alpha}
\,d\mu^r.
}
\]

Here the integral means: draw the `r` rows independently from `mu`, project by the fixed residual projector `P_B`, and take their residual Gram determinant.

In particular, at `alpha=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_k}
\left[
\det K_{I,I}\mid B
\right]
=
\frac{s!}{(s+r)!},
}
\]

which is independent of `B`.

## 4.2 Proof: block determinant

Permute rows so `I` comes last. The Gram matrix has block form

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
G_B & C\\
C^\top & G_I
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its Schur complement with respect to `G_B` is exactly the residual Gram matrix `A`, so

\[
\boxed{
\det G=\det G_B\,\det A.
}
\]

Conditional on `B`, the determinant-volume density of the `r` remaining iid rows is therefore proportional to `det A`.

## 4.3 Conditional normalization

The projected row has covariance `P_B`, hence is isotropic inside the `q=s+r` dimensional residual subspace. Applying the Gram-determinant normalization from Section 3 in ambient dimension `q` to `r` rows gives

\[
\int \det A\,d\mu^r
=
\frac{q!}{(q-r)!}
=
\frac{(s+r)!}{s!}.
\]

Thus the exact conditional density is

\[
\frac{s!}{(s+r)!}\det A\,d\mu^r.
\]

## 4.4 Jacobi complementary-minor identity

For invertible `G`, Jacobi's principal complementary-minor identity gives

\[
\det K_{I,I}
=
\frac{\det G_{I^c,I^c}}{\det G}
=
\frac{\det G_B}{\det G_B\det A}
=
\frac1{\det A}.
\]

Multiplying `(det A)^(-alpha)` by the conditional size-bias density `det A` gives the asserted formula.

At `alpha=1`, the integrand is exactly `1`, proving the constant conditional mean.
