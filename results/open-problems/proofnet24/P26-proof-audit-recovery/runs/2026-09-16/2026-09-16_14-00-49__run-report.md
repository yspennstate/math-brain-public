# 6. A two-quadratic Gaussian integral lemma

## Lemma 6.1

Fix `beta in (1/2,1)`, put `q=2beta-1 in (0,1)`, and fix `c_0>0`. Let

\[
F_\delta
=(XZ)^2+\bigl(X(AY+BP)-\delta(Y^2-D^2)\bigr)^2,
\]

where `X,Z,Y,P,D` are independent standard real Gaussians. Uniformly over

\[
A^2+B^2\ge c_0,
\qquad |A|,|B|\le1,
\qquad 0<|\delta|\le1,
\]

there is a finite `C(beta,c_0)` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E F_\delta^{-\beta}
\le
C(\beta,c_0)|\delta|^{1-2\beta}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Moreover, on any compact coefficient subregion with `A>=a_0>0`, there is a positive lower bound of the same order:

\[
\mathbb E F_\delta^{-\beta}
\ge
c(\beta,a_0,\text{compact region})
|\delta|^{1-2\beta}
\]

for sufficiently small nonzero `delta`.

### Proof of the upper bound

Condition on `(Z,Y,P,D)` and write

\[
L=AY+BP,
\qquad
W=Y^2-D^2.
\]

As a quadratic polynomial in `X`,

\[
F_\delta
=(Z^2+L^2)(X-X_0)^2
+
\frac{\delta^2W^2Z^2}{Z^2+L^2}
\]

for a suitable `X_0`.

The standard Gaussian density of `X` is bounded by `(2pi)^(-1/2)`. Extending the `X` integral to the full real line and using `beta>1/2`,

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
(a x^2+b)^{-\beta}dx
=C_\beta a^{-1/2}b^{1/2-\beta}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_X[F_\delta^{-\beta}\mid Z,L,W]
\le
C_\beta
|\delta|^{-q}
|WZ|^{-q}
(Z^2+L^2)^{-(1-\beta)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Put

\[
\gamma=1-\beta\in(0,1/2).
\]

By `Z^2+L^2 >= 2|ZL|`,

\[
(Z^2+L^2)^{-\gamma}
\le
2^{-\gamma}|Z|^{-\gamma}|L|^{-\gamma}.
\]

The `Z` exponent is

\[
q+\gamma=1-\gamma<1,
\]

so its Gaussian negative moment is finite.

It remains to bound uniformly

\[
\mathbb E |Y^2-D^2|^{-q}|AY+BP|^{-\gamma}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If `|B|` is bounded below, condition on `(Y,D)`: the Gaussian variable `BP` has bounded density scale `|B|`, so its shifted `gamma`-negative moment is uniformly finite, while

\[
Y^2-D^2=(Y-D)(Y+D)
\]

is the product of two independent centered Gaussians after an orthogonal linear change, and `q<1` gives a finite `q`-negative moment.

If `|B|` is small, (by `A^2+B^2>=c_0`) `|A|` is bounded below. Make the orthogonal change

\[
S=(Y-D)/\sqrt2,
\qquad
T=(Y+D)/\sqrt2.
\]

Then (6.3), up to harmless constants and Gaussian weights, is a local integral of

\[
|S|^{-q}|T|^{-q}
\left|\frac{A}{\sqrt2}(S+T)+BP\right|^{-\gamma}
\]

on `R^3`.

The only possible singular strata are hyperplanes. Individual exponents satisfy `q<1`, `gamma<1`; intersections of two independent hyperplanes have total exponent below `2`; and in the limiting case `B=0`, the three singular forms reduce in the `(S,T)` plane to `S,T,S+T`, with total exponent

\[
2q+\gamma
=2-3\gamma
<2.
\]

A finite sector decomposition around the hyperplane arrangement therefore gives a locally integrable majorant uniformly over the compact coefficient family `A^2+B^2>=c_0`. Outside a fixed ball the Gaussian density supplies exponential domination. Thus (6.3) is uniformly finite, proving (6.1).

### Proof of the lower bound

Restrict `(A,B)` to a compact set with `A>=a_0>0`. Choose a fixed positive-Gaussian-probability event on which

- `|Z|` is bounded above and below away from zero;
- `|W|=|Y^2-D^2|` is bounded above and below away from zero;
- `|L|` is bounded above;
- `Z^2+L^2` is bounded above and below.

For small `delta`, the completed-square minimizer `X_0` lies in a fixed compact interval. On an interval of width proportional to `|delta|` around `X_0`, the standard Gaussian density is bounded below and

\[
F_\delta\asymp (X-X_0)^2+\delta^2.
\]

Integrating there gives

\[
\int_{|X-X_0|\le c}
F_\delta^{-\beta}d\gamma(X)
\ge c'|\delta|^{1-2\beta}.
\]

Averaging over the fixed positive-probability event gives the lower bound.

QED.
