## 3. Exact cubic spherical contraction

### Lemma 3.1

For traceless symmetric matrices `A,B`,

\[
\int_{S^{d-1}} (u^\top A u)(u^\top B u)^2\,d\mu(u)
=\frac{8\,\operatorname{tr}(AB^2)}{d(d+2)(d+4)}.
\]

### Proof

Let `g~N(0,I_d)` and write `g=RU` with `U` uniform on the sphere and independent of `R=||g||`. Because the integrand is homogeneous of total degree six,

\[
\mathbb E[(g^\top A g)(g^\top B g)^2]
=\mathbb E[R^6]
\int (u^\top A u)(u^\top B u)^2\,d\mu(u).
\]

For a chi-square radius,

\[
\mathbb E R^6=d(d+2)(d+4).
\]

Wick contraction for Gaussian quadratic forms gives, for traceless `A,B`,

\[
\mathbb E[(g^\top A g)(g^\top B g)^2]
=8\operatorname{tr}(AB^2).
\]

All trace-product terms containing `tr A` or `tr B` vanish. Division by the radial sixth moment proves the formula. `QED`.

### Corollary 3.2: vector form

For any rank-`s` projector `P` with ONB `A_1,...,A_s`,

\[
\int q(u)\,\|Pq(u)\|^2\,d\mu(u)
=\frac{8}{d(d+2)(d+4)}J_s(P).
\]

To verify, test both sides against arbitrary traceless `C`. Since

\[
\langle A_i,q(u)\rangle=u^\top A_i u,
\]

we have

\[
\left\langle C,
\int q(u)\|Pq(u)\|^2d\mu(u)
\right\rangle
=\sum_i \int(u^\top C u)(u^\top A_i u)^2d\mu(u)
\]

\[
=\frac8{d(d+2)(d+4)}\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(C A_i^2)
=\frac8{d(d+2)(d+4)}\langle C,J_s(P)\rangle,
\]

because `C` is traceless and hence is orthogonal to `(s/d)I`. `QED`.

## 6. Exact affine update and the first-moment blindness obstruction

Let

\[
r=Pq(U_{k+1}),\qquad R=\|r\|^2,
\qquad z=\langle a,q(U_{k+1})\rangle.
\]

Appending the new row imposes one new affine constraint. Since `a` already has the old constraints and `r` is orthogonal to the old row span, the new minimum-norm affine interpolant is exactly

\[
a'=a+\frac{1-z}{R}r.
\]

Because `a\perp r`, its norm satisfies

\[
\tau'=\tau+\frac{(1-z)^2}{R}.
\]

Now average under the selected conditional density. The factor `R=||Pq||^2` cancels:

\[
\mathbb E[\tau'-\tau\mid\text{base}]
=\frac{c_d^2}{s}
\int (1-\langle a,q(u)\rangle)^2d\mu(u).
\]

Under the unselected spherical law,

\[
\int\langle a,q(u)\rangle d\mu(u)=0,
\]

and isotropy gives

\[
\int\langle a,q(u)\rangle^2d\mu(u)=c_d^{-2}\|a\|^2=c_d^{-2}\tau.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\tau'-\tau\mid\text{base}]
=\frac{c_d^2+\tau}{s}.
}
\]

This reproduces the known first-moment affine-norm recursion.

### `P26-T4-AFFINE-NORM-BLINDNESS-19 = PROVED`

The exact **conditional first-moment drift** of `tau=||a||^2` contains no selected-row bias `Ez` and no Jordan term `<a,J>`. The linear cross-term is erased because, after determinant-density cancellation, it is integrated against the original centered spherical law.

Consequently, the already-known expectation recursion for `tau`, even if iterated perfectly, does not contain the scalar datum required by `P20-AFFINE-JORDAN-O1-58`.

This is deliberately a narrow route statement: it does **not** claim that all information in the distribution of `tau`, or all nonlinear functions of `tau`, is useless. It proves only that the first-moment norm drift / first affine partition tensor is exactly Jordan-blind.

## 7. New inverse-Gram representation of the live scalar endpoint

Let `G_+` be the augmented Gram matrix after appending the selected next row, and let

\[
K=G_+^{-1}.
\]

Index the appended row by `j`. Standard block inversion with Schur complement `R` gives

\[
K_{jj}=\frac1R.
\]

The last coordinate of `K\mathbf1` is

\[
(K\mathbf1)_j=\frac{1-z}{R}.
\]

Therefore pointwise

\[
\boxed{
z=1-\frac{(K\mathbf1)_j}{K_{jj}}.
}
\]

Under the determinant-volume law, the selected rows are exchangeable, so the appended index has the same law as a uniformly chosen held-out row of the resulting `(k+1)`-row selected configuration.

### `P26-T4-LOO-INVERSE-RATIO-19 = PROVED`

At `s=2`, before append there are `m-2` selected rows and after append there are `m-1` selected rows. If `K` is the inverse Gram matrix of those `m-1` rows and `j` is a uniformly chosen row index, then

\[
\boxed{
 e_2+2\mathbb ET_2
=\frac{2(d+4)}d
\left(
\mathbb E\frac{(K\mathbf1)_j}{K_{jj}}-1
\right).
}
\]

Because the left side is nonnegative,

\[
\mathbb E\frac{(K\mathbf1)_j}{K_{jj}}\ge1.
\]

Thus the P20 sufficient condition has become a scalar normalized inverse-Gram row-sum problem.

### `P26-T4-INVERSE-RATIO-ENDPOINT-19 = OPEN`

Prove, under the `(m-1)`-row determinant-volume law,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\frac{(K\mathbf1)_j}{K_{jj}}=1+O(d^{-1}).
}
\]

This is equivalent to the P20 affine/Jordan sufficient condition and to

\[
e_2+2\mathbb ET_2=O(d^{-1}).
\]

It would in particular imply the desired `e_2=O(d^{-1})` because `T_2>=0`.

## 8. Independent narrow audit of the P20 affine/Jordan Ward bridge

### `P26-AUDIT-P20-PJ-WARD-AFFINE-19 = PASS`

This audit is intentionally limited to the exact bridge used above; it is not an audit of the full P20 report.

P04's geometric differential for a traceless symmetric perturbation direction `C` is

\[
D_C P
=-2(PL_CQ+QL_CP)
-\frac2d(PCa^\top+aC^\top P).
\]

Take the vector field `V=PJ`, where `J=J_s(P)`. Differentiating both the projector and the Jordan map and tracing over an orthonormal basis of `E` gives

\[
\operatorname{div}(PJ)
=-2e+4\|PJ\|^2-4T_s
-\frac{2(s+2)}d\langle a,J\rangle.
\]

For the determinant-volume law, the score in the same coordinates has the form

\[
R_{\rm score}=-4J+\frac4d a-(d+4)S,

with the affine vector `a` and the row-sum vector `S` lying in `Ran Q`. Hence they are orthogonal to `PJ`, and

\[
\langle R_{\rm score},PJ\rangle=-4\|PJ\|^2.

The integration-by-parts / Ward identity

\[
\mathbb E\{\operatorname{div}(PJ)+\langle R_{\rm score},PJ\rangle\}=0

therefore reduces to

\[
-2\mathbb Ee-4\mathbb ET_s
-\frac{2(s+2)}d\mathbb E\langle a,J\rangle=0,
\]

or exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb Ee+2\mathbb ET_s
=-\frac{s+2}{d}\mathbb E\langle a,J\rangle.

This matches `P20-PJ-WARD-AFFINE-BRIDGE-57`. Audit status: PASS.

## 11. Next attack

The highest-value next theorem is the OPEN scalar endpoint

\[
\mathbb E\frac{(K\mathbf1)_j}{K_{jj}}=1+O(d^{-1})
\]

for the inverse Gram matrix of an `(m-1)`-row determinant-volume sample, with `j` a uniformly chosen row.

Promising routes that genuinely use law-specific structure:

1. **Exchangeable cofactor identity.** Write `(K1)_j/K_jj` as a ratio of signed cofactors / a leave-one-out affine interpolation coefficient and exploit determinant-volume weighting before absolute values are taken.
2. **Signed row-sum cancellation.** Seek an exact identity for the mean off-diagonal contribution `sum_{l != j} K_{jl}/K_{jj}`. The target is only `O(1/d)`, so a cancellation theorem is more plausible than separate absolute bounds.
3. **One-step determinant partition derivative.** Introduce a scalar source coupled to the affine all-ones vector before taking the determinant partition expectation, so the normalized inverse ratio appears as a derivative of a log partition function rather than as a raw inverse moment.
4. **Conditional pair symmetry.** Condition on the other `m-2` rows. The final two-dimensional residual geometry may permit an exact signed integral for the normalized ratio, analogous to the cubic Jordan drift just derived.

The run should not return to first-moment `E tau` recursion as though it contained the target bias: Section 6 proves it does not.
