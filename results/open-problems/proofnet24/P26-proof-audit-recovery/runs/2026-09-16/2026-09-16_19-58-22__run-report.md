## 5. Full independent proof

### 5.1 Admissibility of the input

Under `x = exp(u)`,

`mu_L(dx) = du/(2L)` for `-L <= u <= L`.

Thus `log_* mu_L` is exactly the uniform distribution on `[-L,L]`. It is symmetric and unimodal. Symmetry of `u` under `u -> -u` is exactly multiplicative symmetry of `x` under `x -> 1/x`. Hence `mu_L` satisfies both hypotheses in Problem 4.3.

### 5.2 Exact eta inverse

For a probability measure on `R_+`, write

`psi_mu(z) = integral zx/(1-zx) dmu(x)`,  `eta_mu = psi_mu/(1+psi_mu)`.

For `mu_L`, direct integration in `u=log x` gives

`psi_L(z) = (1/(2L)) log((1-a z)/(1-a^{-1} z))`, where `a = exp(-L)`.

Set `w = eta_L(z)`. Since `psi=w/(1-w)`, exponentiation gives

`(1-a z)/(1-a^{-1} z) = E(w) := exp(2L w/(1-w))`.

Solving for `z` yields the exact inverse

`q_L(w) := eta_L^{-1}(w) = (1-E(w))/(a-a^{-1}E(w))`.

The Sigma-transform is `Sigma_mu(w)=eta_mu^{-1}(w)/w`. Therefore for `rho_L=mu_L boxtimes mu_L`, multiplicativity gives

`Q_L(w) := eta_rho^{-1}(w) = q_L(w)^2 / w`.

This derivation is independent of P20's later finite interval machinery.

### 5.3 Identify the physical boundary branch

For `-1 <= w < 0`, `E(w)` is real with `a <= E(w) < 1`. The denominator `a-a^{-1}E(w)` is nonzero and `Q_L(w)<0`. At `w=-1`, `E=a`, `q_L(-1)=-1`, and hence `Q_L(-1)=-1`. For positive measures, `eta` is strictly increasing on the negative axis because `psi'(z)=integral x/(1-xz)^2 dmu(x)>0`; thus the inverse branch through this negative-axis point is the physical branch.

Now put

`w=e^{i theta}=e^{2it}`, with `0<t<pi/2`.

The identity

`w/(1-w) = -1/2 + (i/2) cot t`

gives

`E(w)=a e^{i phi}`,  `phi=L cot t`.

Let

`B_a(v)=(v-a)/(1-a v)`.

Then

`q_L(w)=-1/B_a(e^{i phi})`,

so `|q_L(w)|=1` and `|Q_L(w)|=1`. Write `B_a(e^{i phi})=e^{i beta_a(phi)}` with the continuous argument on `0<=phi<=pi`. Its derivative is the Poisson kernel

`beta_a'(phi)=P_a(phi)=(1-a^2)/(1-2a cos(phi)+a^2)`.

Hence the boundary phase of `Q_L` is

`varphi_L(theta)=2pi-theta-2 beta_a(L cot(theta/2))`,

and

`varphi_L'(theta)=L csc^2(t) P_a(phi)-1`.

Let `t0=arctan(L/pi)`, so `phi(t0)=pi`. At `theta0=2t0`, `B_a(-1)=-1`, and the continuous phase is `varphi_L(theta0)=-2t0<0`. At `theta=pi`, the phase is `pi`.

For every `phi`,

`P_a(phi) >= (1-a)/(1+a)`.

For `L>=6`, `a=e^{-L}<1/400` (verified exactly below), so

`varphi_L'(theta) > 6*(399/401)-1 = 1993/401 > 0`.

Thus there is a unique `theta*=2t*` in `(2t0,pi)` with `varphi_L(theta*)=0`, and `Q_L(e^{i theta*})=1`. The entire arc from `w=-1` to `w*=e^{2it*}` is noncritical. By analytic continuation from the already identified negative-axis physical inverse branch, this is the physical boundary branch approaching `z=1`. No arbitrary algebraic branch is being substituted into the density formula.

### 5.4 Boundary density and curvature

The Cauchy transform satisfies

`G_rho(1/z) = z/(1-eta_rho(z))`.

Consequently the density of the logarithmic pushforward can be written on the physical boundary in terms of

`H(w)=1/(1-w)`.

Writing the boundary inverse as `w=w(y)` with `Q_L(w(y))=e^{-y}` and `w(0)=w*`, the log-density is

`g_L(y) = (1/pi) Im H(w(y))`.

Multiplicative symmetry of `rho_L` makes `g_L` even, so `g_L'(0)=0`.

Set

`A=varphi_L'(2t*)>0`,  `B=varphi_L''(2t*)`.

Differentiating `Q(w(y))=e^{-y}` at `y=0`, or equivalently differentiating the unit-circle phase first and then the inverse relation, gives

`Q'(w*)=A/w*`,

`w'(0)=-w*/A`,

`w''(0)=w*(A+iB)/A^3`.

Using

`w*/(1-w*)^2 = -(1/4)csc^2(t*)`

and

`w*^2/(1-w*)^3 = (1/8)csc^2(t*) - (i/8)csc^2(t*)cot(t*)`,

a direct substitution into `g''=(1/pi) Im(H''(w)(w')^2+H'(w)w'')` yields

`g_L''(0) = - csc^2(t*)/[4 pi A^3] * [B + A cot(t*)]`.

Therefore it suffices to prove the bracket is negative.

### 5.5 Exact sign of the bracket

Let

`D=1-2a cos(phi)+a^2`, evaluated at `phi=L cot(t*)`.

Since

`A=L csc^2(t*) (1-a^2)/D -1`,

differentiation gives

`B + A cot(t*)`
`= -cot(t*) + L^2 a(1-a^2) sin(phi)/(D^2 sin^4(t*))`.

At the phase root, `0<phi<pi`, so `sin(phi)<phi=L cot(t*)`. Also `D >= (1-a)^2`, and `t*>t0` implies

`sin(t*) > L/sqrt(L^2+pi^2)`.

Thus

`B+A cot(t*) < cot(t*)[-1 + C_L]`,

where

`C_L = a(1-a^2)(L^2+pi^2)^2/[L(1-a)^4]`.

It remains to show `C_L<1` for `L>=6`. Since `pi^2<10` and `1-a^2<1`,

`C_L < F(L):=e^{-L}(L^2+10)^2/[L(1-e^{-L})^4]`.

For `L>=6`,

`d/dL log F = -1 + 4L/(L^2+10) -1/L -4e^{-L}/(1-e^{-L})`
`< -1 + 3/L <= -1/2`.

So `F` is decreasing on `[6,infinity)`. At `L=6`, the elementary lower bound

`e > sum_{k=0}^5 1/k! = 163/60`

gives `e^6>(163/60)^6>400`, hence `e^{-6}<1/400`. Therefore

`F(6) < (1/400)*(46^2/6)*(400/399)^4`
`= 67712000000/76034875203 < 1`.

Hence `C_L<1`, so `B+A cot(t*)<0`. Since `A>0`, the curvature formula gives

`g_L''(0)>0`.

Thus the symmetric log-density has a strict local minimum at its symmetry point. An even unimodal density cannot have such a strict central minimum. Therefore `rho_L=mu_L boxtimes mu_L` is **not log-unimodal** for every `L>=6`.

This supplies an explicit counterexample with both inputs multiplicatively symmetric and log-unimodal, exactly negating Problem 4.3. QED.
