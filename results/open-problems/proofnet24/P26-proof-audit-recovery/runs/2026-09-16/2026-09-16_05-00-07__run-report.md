# 3. Re-derivation of the intrinsic random-line fourth moment

Set

\[
a_4=\operatorname{tr}(A^4),\quad
b_4=\operatorname{tr}(B^4),\quad
x=\operatorname{tr}(A^2B^2),\quad
y=\operatorname{tr}(ABAB).
\]

First,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C(P)^2)=a_4+b_4+2x.
\]

Because `A,B` are symmetric,

\[
\delta(P)=\|AB-BA\|_F^2=2x-2y.
\]

Now expand `X_theta^4` inside the trace. Odd powers of `cos(theta)` or `sin(theta)` average to zero. The six words containing two `A` and two `B` collapse by cyclicity to four copies of `x` and two copies of `y`. Since

\[
\mathbb E\cos^4\Theta=\mathbb E\sin^4\Theta=\frac38,
\qquad
\mathbb E\cos^2\Theta\sin^2\Theta=\frac18,
\]

we get

\[
\mathbb E_\Theta\operatorname{tr}(X_\Theta^4)
=\frac38(a_4+b_4)+\frac18(4x+2y).
\]

Therefore

\[
8\mathbb E_\Theta\operatorname{tr}(X_\Theta^4)
=3(a_4+b_4)+4x+2y
=3\operatorname{tr}(C(P)^2)-\delta(P).
\]

Since `tr(C(P)^2)=e(P)+4/d`, this is the exact pointwise identity

\[
\boxed{
8\mathbb E_\Theta\operatorname{tr}(X_\Theta^4)
=3\left(e(P)+\frac4d\right)-\delta(P).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the actual residual plane `P_2`, the independently audited volume-partition identity gives

\[
\mathbb E\delta(P_2)=\frac d{m-1}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Taking expectations in (3.1),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{P_2,\Theta}\operatorname{tr}(X_\Theta^4)
=\frac38e_2+rac{3}{2d}-\frac{d}{8(m-1)}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because `m-1=(d^2+d-4)/2`, the known non-`e_2` terms are `O(1/d)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{

# 7. New route-separation theorem: qualitative local Gaussianity is too weak

## `P26-T4-QUALITATIVE-LOCAL-GAUSSIANITY-NOGO-18` — PROVED

The current P20 report proves a density-level local CLT for a deterministic quadratic direction when its spectral spikes vanish. It would be tempting to regard “adaptive normal becomes Gaussian” as nearly equivalent to the P04 angular endpoint. The following explicit family proves that this is false at the needed rate.

For each large `d`, choose an even integer

\[
r=r_d=2\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt d}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Then `r~sqrt(d)`. Define a diagonal traceless matrix `X_d` by taking

- `r/2` diagonal entries equal to `+r^{-1/2}`;
- `r/2` diagonal entries equal to `-r^{-1/2}`;
- the remaining `d-r` entries equal to zero.

Then exactly

\[
\operatorname{tr}X_d=0,
\qquad
\|X_d\|_F^2=1,
\qquad
\|X_d\|_{op}=r^{-1/2}\to0,
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(X_d^4)=\frac1r\asymp d^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus the desired Haar-scale `O(1/d)` fourth moment fails by a factor of order `sqrt(d)`.

Now form

\[
Q_d=\frac{g^TX_dg}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Because the positive and negative eigenvalue multiplicities agree,

\[
Q_d=rac1{\sqrt{2r}}
\left[
\sum_{j=1}^{r/2}(g_j^2-1)
-
\sum_{j=r/2+1}^{r}(g_j^2-1)
\right].
\]

Its characteristic function can be computed exactly. One positive coordinate contributes

\[
e^{-it/\sqrt{2r}}
\left(1-i\sqrt{\frac2r}t\right)^{-1/2},
\]

and one negative coordinate contributes the conjugate factor. Pairing equal numbers of each gives

\[
\boxed{
\psi_r(t)
=\mathbb E e^{itQ_d}
=\left(1+\frac{2t^2}{r}\right)^{-r/4}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

For each fixed `t`,

\[
\psi_r(t)\to e^{-t^2/2}.
\]

Moreover, for `r>=8`, monotonicity of `(1+x/r)^r` in `r` gives

\[
\left(1+\frac{2t^2}{r}\right)^{-r/4}
\le
\left(1+\frac{t^2}{4}\right)^{-2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The right side is integrable on `R`, and after multiplication by `|t|` it remains integrable. Dominated convergence therefore yields

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
|\psi_r(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\,dt\to0,
\]

and

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
|t|\,|\psi_r(t)-e^{-t^2/2}|\,dt\to0.
\]

Fourier inversion gives densities `f_r` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\|f_r-\varphi\|_\infty\to0,
\qquad
\|f_r'-\varphi'\|_\infty\to0,
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

where `varphi` is the standard-normal density.

Consequently, for every `0<eps<=1`, uniformly in `u`,

\[
\left|
\frac{\Pr\{|Q_d-u|\le\varepsilon\}}{2\varepsilon}
-\varphi(u)
\right|
\le
\|f_r-\varphi\|_\infty+C\varepsilon.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus for **any** microscopic sequence `eps_d->0`, the normalized interval probability converges uniformly to the Gaussian density.

Yet the exact fourth moment is, by Section 4,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E Q_d^4-3
=12\operatorname{tr}(X_d^4)
=\frac{12}{r}
\asymp d^{-1/2},
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

not `O(d^-1)`.

Therefore:

> Uniform density and density-derivative convergence to the Gaussian, and even qualitative microscopic interval asymptotics, do **not** imply the `1/d` fourth-cumulant rate required by P04 Target 4.

This is a **route counterexample**, not a counterexample to the actual determinant-volume harmonic residual-plane law. It certifies only that a proof which stops after qualitative adaptive local Gaussianity has not paid the P04 angular debt.
