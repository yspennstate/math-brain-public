# 3. Normalization and determinant-selected pivot law

Let `mu` denote normalized uniform measure on `S^{d-1}`. The harmonic normalization gives isotropy in `E`:

\[
\mathbb E_\mu[h(U)\otimes h(U)]=I_E.
\]

Therefore for every rank-`r` orthogonal projector `P` on `E`,

\[
\mathbb E_\mu R(U)
=\mathbb E_\mu\|P h(U)\|_F^2
=\operatorname{Tr}P
=r.
\]

For one determinant-volume selected append step, the conditional density is therefore exactly

\[
\boxed{
\frac{dQ_P}{d\mu}(u)=\frac{R(u)}r.
}
\]

For `K=R^{-1}` and every `p>1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_P}K^p
=\frac1r\mathbb E_\mu R^{1-p}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This identity is deterministic conditional on the base and is the only selection formula needed below.

Because `r <= m-1`, the physical base contains at least one row. If its directions are `u_1,...,u_{m-r}`, then by definition of the residual projector,

\[
P h(u_i)=0
\qquad\text{for every base index }i.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the ordinary residual energy has exact zeros at every base direction.

# 4. Differential geometry of the physical harmonic map

Fix `u in S^{d-1}` and tangent vector `v perp u`. Differentiating

\[
h(u)=c_d(uu^\top-I/d)
\]

gives

\[
D h_u(v)=c_d(uv^\top+vu^\top).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since `u` and `v` are orthogonal,

\[
\|uv^\top+vu^\top\|_F^2
=2\|v\|^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\|D h_u(v)\|_F^2
=2c_d^2\|v\|^2
=d(d+2)\|v\|^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

So the differential restricted to every tangent space is a similarity with the same norm factor

\[
L_d:=\sqrt{d(d+2)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `rho(u,u_i)` be geodesic distance. Integrating (4.2) along a minimizing unit-speed geodesic and using that orthogonal projection is a contraction gives

\[
\|P h(u)-P h(u_i)\|_F
\le L_d\rho(u,u_i).
\]

By (3.2),

\[
\boxed{
R(u)\le d(d+2)\rho(u,u_i)^2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

No transversality is used. This estimate holds for every physical base, every residual rank `1 <= r <= m-1`, and every one of its base directions.

# 5. Proof of the physical inverse-moment ceiling

## Claim

`P26-T4-PHYSICAL-PIVOT-DIMENSION-CEILING-24 = PROVED`.

For every full-row-rank physical base with residual rank `1 <= r <= m-1`, under the selected one-step law `Q_P`,

\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P}K^p=\infty
\qquad\text{for every }p\ge\frac{d+1}{2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Proof

Choose one base direction `u_i`. Let

\[
0<\rho_0<\frac\pi4.
\]

On the geodesic ball `B(u_i,rho_0)`, (4.4) and `p>1` imply

\[
R(u)^{1-p}
\ge
[d(d+2)]^{1-p}\rho(u,u_i)^{-2(p-1)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Normalized spherical measure in geodesic polar coordinates about `u_i` has radial factor

\[
C_d\sin^{d-2}\rho\,d\rho
\]

with a finite positive constant `C_d`. For `0 <= rho <= pi/4`,

\[
\sin\rho\ge\frac{2}{\pi}\rho.
\]

Combining with (3.1) and (5.2), for another finite positive constant `c_{d,r,p}`,

\[
\mathbb E_{Q_P}K^p
\ge
c_{d,r,p}
\int_0^{\rho_0}
\rho^{d-2}\rho^{-2(p-1)}\,d\rho.
\]

The exponent of `rho` is

\[
d-2-2(p-1)=d-2p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\int_0^{\rho_0}\rho^{d-2p}\,d\rho=\infty
\]

exactly when

\[
d-2p\le-1,
\]

i.e.

\[
p\ge\frac{d+1}{2}.
\]

This proves (5.1). At equality the divergence is logarithmic. `QED`.

## Remarks on sharpness of what was proved

1. The theorem is a **universal upper bound on the physical critical exponent**. It does not assert that `(d+1)/2` is the exact exponent for every high-rank residual plane.
2. A lower-rank tangent image, higher-order vanishing, or additional zero geometry could make the true critical exponent smaller.
3. The theorem needs no randomness of the base beyond full row rank. It is therefore stronger than an annealed statement in its stated high-moment range.

# 6. Gaussian comparator and a scalar no-go interval

Let `Z ~ N(0,I_r)` and

\[
R_G=\|Z\|^2\sim\chi_r^2.

Size-bias by `R_G/r` gives the selected density. Since

\[
x\,f_{\chi_r^2}(x)/r=f_{\chi_{r+2}^2}(x),

the selected Gaussian energy satisfies

\[
R_G^{\mathrm{sel}}\sim\chi_{r+2}^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E (R_G^{\mathrm{sel}})^{-p}<\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
p<\frac{r+2}{2}=1+\frac r2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

If `r >= d`, then

\[
\frac{d+1}{2}<1+\frac r2.

Hence every

\[
p\in\left[\frac{d+1}{2},1+\frac r2\right)

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_P}K^p=\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E K_G^p<\infty.

At `r=d`, the mismatch window is

\left[\frac{d+1}{2},\frac{d+2}{2}\right),

This gives a **scalar radial inverse-moment obstruction** to full Gaussian pivot universality beginning precisely at the same rank where P04's vector transversality fails.

It does not imply that bounded continuous functions of `R` fail to converge, nor does it obstruct low fixed moments when their order is far below `(d+1)/2`.

# 7. Exact criticality below the transversality threshold

P04's newest theorem proves that for an actual residual plane `P_r` with

\[
1\le r\le d-1,
\]

zero is a regular value of the smooth map

\[
\Phi_P:S^{d-1}\to\mathbb R^r,
\qquad
\Phi_P(u)
=(\langle A_1,h(u)\rangle,\ldots,\langle A_r,h(u)\rangle)
\]

almost surely.

Because the physical base is nonempty, its directions lie in `Phi_P^{-1}(0)`, so the zero fiber is nonempty. Regularity on the compact zero fiber gives a finite positive coarea density at zero. Equivalently, for the ordinary law,

\[
\mu\{R\le\varepsilon\}
=C_P\varepsilon^{r/2}+o(\varepsilon^{r/2}),
\qquad
0<C_P<\infty.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For completeness, this finite-dimensional consequence does **not** require an asymptotic fixed-rank limit. It follows at each fixed `d` from the regular-value theorem and local coarea.

Using (3.1), the contribution near zero to the selected `p`-moment is locally equivalent to

\[
\int_0^{\varepsilon_0}
R^{1-p}R^{r/2-1}\,dR
=
\int_0^{\varepsilon_0}R^{r/2-p}\,dR.
\tag{7.2}
\]

This integral is finite exactly when

\[
\frac r2-p>-1,
\]

i.e.

\[
p<1+\frac r2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Away from a small neighborhood of the compact zero fiber, continuity gives `R >= c>0`, so there is no further divergence.

Thus, for every actual `P_r` with `1 <= r <= d-1`, almost surely,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_{P_r}}K^p<\infty
\iff
p<1+\frac r2.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This exactly matches (6.2).

# 9. Independent audit of P04 residual transversality

## Claim

`P26-AUDIT-P04-RESIDUAL-TRANSVERSALITY-24 = PASS`.

## 9.1 Algebraic bad-set formulation

Choose an orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_r` of a candidate residual plane and define the traceless quadratic forms

\[
q_j(u)=u^\top A_j u.
\]

At a common zero `q_1(u)=...=q_r(u)=0`, tangent gradients fail to be independent exactly when there is a nonzero coefficient vector `c=(c_j)` with

\[
\sum_j c_j A_j u=0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Thus nonregularity is an algebraic incidence condition in the plane and the point `u`.

## 9.2 Explicit full-spark diagonal witness

Take distinct real numbers `t_1,...,t_d` and barycentric weights

\[
c_i=\frac1{\prod_{\ell\ne i}(t_i-t_\ell)}.
\]

For `r <= d-1`, define vectors of diagonal coordinates

\[
v_i=c_i(1,t_i,\ldots,t_i^{r-1})\in\mathbb R^r.
\]

The barycentric identities imply

\[
\sum_{i=1}^d v_i=0,
\]

so each coordinate column defines a traceless diagonal matrix. Vandermonde nondegeneracy implies every `r` of the `v_i` are linearly independent. This is the required full-spark property.

For a common zero of the resulting diagonal quadratics, any gradient dependence (9.1) forces the corresponding coefficient functional to vanish on a support of size at most `r-1`; full spark rules this out. Hence this explicit residual plane has no bad common zero.

Therefore the algebraic bad locus is proper.

## 9.3 Physical realizability

The remaining issue is not merely to exhibit an abstract Grassmannian witness, but to realize a good residual plane as the orthogonal complement of physical rows.

For the diagonal witness, the common-zero set contains sign-flip families whose squared-coordinate vectors lie in the affine slice determined by the diagonal forms. Local variations of those squared coordinates span the annihilator of the residual diagonal space. Consequently physical features `h(u)` chosen from that slice span the full orthogonal complement `P^\perp` after taking enough points. For `r=d-1`, the witness is the full traceless diagonal space and the same conclusion follows directly from sign-flip/squared-coordinate geometry.

Thus there exists a full-row-rank physical base whose residual plane lies outside the bad algebraic locus.

## 9.4 Measure-zero transfer to the actual base law

Pulling the proper algebraic bad set back through the physical base parametrization gives a nonzero polynomial condition on the base directions. Its zero set has product spherical measure zero. The determinant-volume selected base law is absolutely continuous with respect to that product measure on its full-rank support. Therefore, for actual residual rank `r <= d-1`, zero is a regular value almost surely.

## 9.5 High-rank obstruction

For `r >= d`, every base direction `u_i` lies in the zero fiber because `P h(u_i)=0`. But

\[
D\Phi_P(u_i):T_{u_i}S^{d-1}\to\mathbb R^r
\]

has domain dimension `d-1<r`. It cannot be surjective. Hence zero is never a regular value.

This establishes the threshold exactly at `r=d`.

## Verdict

The algebraic incidence, witness, physical pullback, absolute-continuity transfer, and dimension obstruction are mutually consistent and close the stated theorem. **PASS**.

P20 and P23 independently verify the rank-two special case in their cited current reports. Those checks reinforce, but do not replace, the above general-rank audit.

The current theorem concerns the different regime where `r` reaches the physical tangent dimension.

Thus

`P26-T4-BAD-PLANE-UNTRUNCATION-23`

remains **OPEN**.

## 10.4 A route that is now ruled out

Any proof strategy claiming a Gaussian selected-pivot model uniformly over the entire deletion hierarchy **in all inverse moments up to the Gaussian critical exponent** is false from `r=d` onward. A future hierarchy argument must either:

- stay in a controlled low-moment regime;
- use scalar approximations weaker than full inverse-moment matching;
- or explicitly account for the physical base-zero singularities.

This is a rigorous route obstruction, not a heuristic warning.

# 11. Failed / incomplete route and exact obstruction

A tempting strengthening would be to claim that, for `r >= d`, the physical selected critical exponent is exactly `(d+1)/2`.

The present proof does **not** justify that. It only uses the global upper bound

\[
R(u)\le d(d+2)\rho(u,u_i)^2.
\]

To prove matching local lower behavior one would need quantitative information on the rank of

\[
P D h_{u_i}:T_{u_i}S^{d-1}\to P(E)

at one or more base zeros, plus control in tangent-kernel directions where the first derivative vanishes. P04 proves failure of surjectivity when `r >= d`, not maximal rank `d-1` of this derivative.

Therefore the stronger statement is retained as an open next lemma rather than inferred.
