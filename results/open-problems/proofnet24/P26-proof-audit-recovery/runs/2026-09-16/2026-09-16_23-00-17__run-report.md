## 3. Imported primary-source hypotheses, rechecked

### 3.1 Positive free stable normalization

Hasebe–Kuznetsov, *On free stable distributions*, Electron. Commun. Probab. 19 (2014), arXiv:1404.2981, parameterize free stable laws so that for `0<alpha<1`

\[
\phi_{\alpha,\rho}(z)=-e^{i\pi\alpha\rho}z^{1-\alpha}
\]

using the principal branch, and in their unified parameterization `rho` is the positivity parameter. At

\[
\alpha=\frac12,\qquad \rho=1,
\]

this gives

\[
\phi(z)=-i\sqrt z.
\]

Free convolution powers scale the Voiculescu transform linearly, so for the corresponding positive free strictly `1/2`-stable semigroup,

\[
\boxed{\phi_{T_t}(z)=-it\sqrt z.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is exactly the normalization used by R19. Hasebe–Kuznetsov also establish absolute continuity/smooth density for free stable laws; in particular `T_t` is atomless for `t>0`.

### 3.2 Huang–Wang global inversion and boundary classification

Hao-Wei Huang and Jiun-Chau Wang, *Regularity results for free Lévy processes*, Adv. Math. 402 (2022), arXiv:2203.00421, use for a freely infinitely divisible law `mu` and initial law `nu`

\[
H(z)=z+\phi_\mu(F_\nu(z)).
\]

Their Proposition 3.1 constructs the boundary-height function `f`, open set `V={x:f(x)>0}`, inverse homeomorphism, and a strictly increasing real homeomorphism `h`.

Their Proposition 3.2 states, among equivalent conditions for a real `alpha`, that `alpha` is a real boundary point (`f(alpha)=0`) iff the angular derivative `H'(alpha)` exists in `[0,+infty)`. In particular a real point with a finite positive derivative lies outside `V`.

Their Theorem 3.6 classifies real boundary points into mutually disjoint types `A,B,C`; Remark 3.7 gives for type A the exact condition

\[
\boxed{\nu(\{\alpha\})+\mu(\{s_\mu\})\ge1.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Their Theorem 3.9 gives a canonical density version with

\[
p_{\mu\boxplus\nu}(h(x))=0\quad(x\notin V),
\]

while it is strictly positive for `x in V`, and `h` is strictly increasing. These are exactly the source implications needed below; no stronger unverified support assertion is imported.

### 4.1 Sparse initial law and source moment

Let

\[
A_n=2^{2^n},\qquad
w_n=2^{-n}A_n^{-n}\quad(n\ge1),
\]

and put

\[
W=\sum_{n\ge1}w_n,
\qquad
w_0=1-W,
\qquad
\nu=w_0\delta_0+\sum_{n\ge1}w_n\delta_{-A_n}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

We have `w_1=1/8`. For `n>=2`, `A_n>=16`, hence

\[
w_n=2^{-n}A_n^{-n}\le 2^{-5n}=32^{-n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
W<\frac18+\sum_{n\ge2}32^{-n}
=\frac18+\frac1{992}
=\frac{125}{992}
<\frac14,
\tag{4.2}
\]

so

\[
w_0>\frac34.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For any fixed `p>0`,

\[
\int |x|^p\,d\nu(x)
=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}A_n^{p-n}.
\]

Once `n>p`, the exponent `p-n` is negative and `A_n` is doubly exponential, so the tail converges. In particular for the source exponent `p=5/2`, for `n>=3`,

\[
2^{-n}A_n^{5/2-n}
=2^{-n+(5/2-n)2^n}
\le 2^{-n-2^{n-1}},
\tag{4.4}

which is summable. Thus the source hypothesis

is satisfied, indeed with all finite polynomial moments.

### 4.2 NEW-METHOD #5: parameterize the gap point

R19 chose the midpoint `-A_n/2`. To test whether its signs or constants are an accidental midpoint phenomenon, take instead

\[
c=-\theta A,
\qquad A=A_n,
\qquad 0<\theta<\frac34.
\]

Assume `A_{n-1}=\sqrt A <= \theta A/2`, which holds for all sufficiently large `n`. Let

\[
G(z)=G_\nu(z),\qquad F(z)=1/G(z),
\qquad I_2(c)=\int\frac{d\nu(s)}{(c-s)^2}.
\]

The atom at zero contributes `-w_0/(theta A)` to `G(c)`. Every atom with index `k<n` also contributes negatively, so those terms may be discarded for an upper bound. For `k>=n`,

\[
c+A_k=A_k-\theta A\ge(1-\theta)A.
\]

Using `w_0>3/4` and `W<1/4`,

\[
G(c)
< -\frac{3}{4\theta A}
   +\frac{1}{4(1-\theta)A}
= -\frac{q(\theta)}{A},
\tag{4.5}
\]

where

\[
q(\theta)
=\frac{3}{4\theta}-\frac{1}{4(1-\theta)}
=\frac{3-4\theta}{4\theta(1-\theta)}>0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus, with `a=-G(c)`,

\[
a>\frac{q(\theta)}A.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Next bound `I_2`. The zero atom contributes at most `1/(theta^2 A^2)`. For `k<n`,

\[
|c+A_k|\ge\theta A-A_{n-1}\ge\frac{\theta A}{2},
\]

so the total earlier-atom contribution is less than

\[
\frac{4W}{\theta^2A^2}<\frac1{\theta^2A^2}.
\]

For `k>=n`, the total contribution is less than

\[
\frac{W}{(1-\theta)^2A^2}
<\frac1{4(1-\theta)^2A^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
I_2(c)<\frac{C(\theta)}{A^2},
\qquad
C(\theta)=\frac2{\theta^2}+\frac1{4(1-\theta)^2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The R19 midpoint is recovered at `theta=1/2`:

\[
q(1/2)=1,\qquad C(1/2)=9.

So the original `9t` threshold is exactly the midpoint specialization of this more general family, not an unexplained constant.

### 4.4 NEW-METHOD #29: rule out an exterior-tail artifact

An open zero-density interval is not by itself enough: it might sit outside a one-sided support and hence be compatible with unimodality. The two points at the edges of the **input** gap prevent that.

At either flanking atom

\[
\alpha\in\{-A_n,-A_{n-1}\},
\]

one has `F_\nu^*(alpha)=0`. If such an `alpha` were outside `V_t`, Huang–Wang Theorem 3.6 says it would have to be a boundary point of type A, B, or C. The condition `F_\nu^*(alpha)=0` puts this case in type A, whose exact criterion by Remark 3.7 is

\[
\nu(\{\alpha\})+T_t(\{s_{T_t}\})\ge1.
\]

But `T_t` is atomless and every sparse weight `w_j<1`. Therefore the inequality fails. Hence neither flank is a boundary point:

\[
-A_n,-A_{n-1}\in V_t.
\tag{4.19}
\]

By Theorem 3.9,

\[
p_{T_t\boxplus\nu}(h_t(-A_n))>0,
\qquad
p_{T_t\boxplus\nu}(h_t(-A_{n-1}))>0.
\tag{4.20}
\]

Since `h_t` is strictly increasing and

\[
-A_n<J<-A_{n-1},
\]

the zero-density interval `h_t(J)` lies strictly between two positive-density points. It is therefore an **interior hole**, not a tail or a missing point at infinity.

### 4.5 NEW-METHOD #18: support topology instead of derivative shape

The last step is clearer as a support-topology statement than as a derivative calculation.

A unimodal probability measure in the source definition has density nondecreasing up to some mode and nonincreasing after it, apart from a possible atom at the mode. Consequently its positive-density set cannot contain two positive points separated by a nonempty open zero-density interval: if the mode lies left of the interval, monotone decrease prevents the density from becoming positive again on the right; if it lies right, monotone increase prevents positivity on the left followed by a zero interval; and if the mode lies inside the zero interval, positivity on both sides contradicts the respective monotonicity. An atom at the mode does not change this conclusion.

Equations (4.18)–(4.20) therefore imply

\[
\boxed{\nu\boxplus T_t\text{ is not unimodal}.}
\tag{4.21}
\]

The choice of `n` was possible for every `t>0`, so

\[
\boxed{\nu\boxplus T_t\text{ is not unimodal for every }t>0.}
\tag{4.22}
\]

The initial law `nu` satisfies the source `5/2`-moment condition. This proves `R19-I0084-C002` independently.
