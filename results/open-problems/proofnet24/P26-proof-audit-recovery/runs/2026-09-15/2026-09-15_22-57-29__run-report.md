# 4. Laplace/tensor-square sign proof

The key point is not merely to formally expand `1/D`; the interchange of the infinite series, integral, and expectation must be justified despite possible singular behavior as `q^TX` approaches zero.

For `D>0`,

\[
D^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-tD}\,dt.
\]

Using

\[
D=A_1+A_2-2\langle a_1,a_2\rangle,
\]

we have

\[
e^{-tD}
=e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}
 e^{2t\langle a_1,a_2\rangle}.
\]

Expand the second exponential:

\[
e^{2t\langle a_1,a_2\rangle}
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(2t)^k}{k!}
\langle a_1,a_2\rangle^k.
\]

## 4.1 Absolute domination

The absolute sum of the integrand is

\[
|s_1s_2|
 e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}
 e^{2t|\langle a_1,a_2\rangle|}.
\]

Since

\[
\|a_1\|^2+\|a_2\|^2
-2|\langle a_1,a_2\rangle|
\ge0,
\]

we obtain

\[
A_1+A_2-2|\langle a_1,a_2\rangle|
\ge s_1^2+s_2^2.
\]

Thus

\[
|s_1s_2|
 e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}
 e^{2t|\langle a_1,a_2\rangle|}
\le
|s_1s_2|e^{-t(s_1^2+s_2^2)}.
\]

Integrating in `t`,

\[
\int_0^\infty
|s_1s_2|e^{-t(s_1^2+s_2^2)}dt
=
\frac{|s_1s_2|}{s_1^2+s_2^2}
\le\frac12.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This pointwise integrable domination is uniform in the frozen base `B` and in the two fresh rows. It justifies the necessary Fubini/Tonelli and series interchanges. In particular, no inverse moment of `q^TX` is being assumed.

## 4.2 Conditional iid factorization

For every fixed `t>0` and integer `k>=0`, the exponential factor makes the relevant tensor expectation finite. Because `X_1,X_2` are conditionally iid given `B`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
& E\left[
 s_1s_2e^{-t(A_1+A_2)}
 \langle a_1,a_2\rangle^k
 \mid B
\right]\\
&=E\left[
 \left\langle
 s_1e^{-tA_1}a_1^{\otimes k},
 s_2e^{-tA_2}a_2^{\otimes k}
 \right\rangle
 \middle|B
\right]\\
&=
\left\|
E_X\left[
 s(X)e^{-tA(X)}a(X)^{\otimes k}
 \mid B
\right]
\right\|^2\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Substituting (4.2) into (3.1) gives the exact conditional square representation

\[
\boxed{
E[K_{12}\mid B]
=-\int_0^\infty
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(2t)^k}{k!}
\left\|
E_X[s e^{-tA}a^{\otimes k}\mid B]
\right\|^2dt
\le0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Averaging over `B` proves

\[
\boxed{E K_{12}\le0.}
\]

Row exchangeability gives the same inequality for every off-diagonal pair.

## 4.3 Equality for centrally sign-symmetric rows

If `X` and `-X` have the same law, then under `X -> -X`,

\[
s\mapsto-s,
\qquad
a\mapsto a,
\qquad A\mapsto A.
\]

Hence every conditional tensor expectation in (4.3) vanishes, so

\[
E[K_{ij}\mid B]=0
\]

and thus `E K_{ij}=0` for `i!=j`.

This equality case is consistent with the familiar centrally symmetric coefficient-spherical and Gaussian benchmarks.
