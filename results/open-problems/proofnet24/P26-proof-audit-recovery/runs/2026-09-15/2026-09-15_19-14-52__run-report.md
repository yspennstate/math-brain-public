# 3. Independent dimension arithmetic

The exact multiplicity of degree-`s` spherical harmonics is

`N_{d,s} = (2s+d-2)(s+d-3)! / (s!(d-2)!)`.

For `s=o(sqrt(d))`, in particular throughout the critical range `s=k+O(R)`, the product representation gives

`N_{d,s} = d^s/s! * exp(O(s^2/d))`.

Hence

`log_d N_{d,k} = k - log_d(k!) + o(1)`.

Since `log d = R log 2`, `k=R+ell-1`, and Stirling gives

`log_d(k!) = ell - 1/log(2) + o(1)`.

Therefore

`N_{d,k}/n = d^(delta_*+o(1))`,

where

`delta_* = 1/log(2) - 1 = 0.4426950408889634...`.

Likewise, because `N_{d,s-1}/N_{d,s}=O(s/d)` uniformly in this range, the cumulative low dimension

`D_<k = sum_{s<k} N_{d,s}`

is asymptotic to its last term on the logarithmic scale, and

`D_<k/n = d^(-(1-delta_*)+o(1))`,

with

`1-delta_* = 0.5573049591110366...`.

Thus both structural risk ratios go to zero:

`D_<k/n -> 0`, and `n/N_{d,k} -> 0`.

No fixed-degree approximation is used in this arithmetic; `k` grows like `R=log_2 d`.

# 4. Exact Gaussian Mercer spectrum

Put `a=h^(-2)`. On the unit sphere,

`K_h(x,y)=e^(-a) exp(a <x,y>)`.

For normalized spherical measure, Funk-Hecke gives the degree-`s` eigenvalue

`lambda_{d,s} = e^(-a) Gamma(d/2) (2/a)^(d/2-1) I_{s+d/2-1}(a)`.

Using the positive modified-Bessel series in NIST DLMF 10.25.2 and cancelling the gamma factors yields the exact nonnegative expansion

`lambda_{d,s} = e^(-a) sum_{r>=0} a^(s+2r) / [2^r r! P_{d,s+r}]`,

where

`P_{d,t} = product_{j=0}^{t-1}(d+2j)` and `P_{d,0}=1`.

Term by term,

`lambda_{d,s+1,r}/lambda_{d,s,r} = a/(d+2(s+r)) <= a/(d+2s)`.

Therefore

`lambda_{d,s+1} <= a/(d+2s) lambda_{d,s}`

for every `s>=0`, with no asymptotic qualification.

This exact positivity is essential: no cancellation or unproved monotonicity of special functions is being used.

# 5. New theorem: factorial contraction of Mercer diagonal mass

Define the degree-`s` diagonal Mercer mass

`w_s = N_{d,s} lambda_{d,s}`.

The exact multiplicity ratio is

`N_{d,s+1}/N_{d,s} = [(2s+d)/(2s+d-2)] * [(s+d-2)/(s+1)]`.

Combining this with the exact eigenvalue ratio from Section 4 gives

`w_{s+1}/w_s <= a (s+d-2)/[(s+1)(2s+d-2)] <= a/(s+1)`.

Hence, for every `j>=0`,

`w_{k+j} <= w_k * a^j / [(k+1)(k+2)...(k+j)]`.

This proves

`P26-T2-FACTORIAL-MERCER-MASS-08 = PROVED`.

If

`tau_k = sum_{s>=k} w_s = K_T(x,x)`

is the high-tail diagonal, then for fixed `a` and growing `k`,

`w_k <= tau_k <= w_k [1+O_a(1/k)]`.

In particular the tail is genuinely dominated by its first retained harmonic shell even though the cutoff grows. This is stronger than merely saying the eigenvalues decrease: it controls multiplicity times eigenvalue, the quantity that actually enters the tail diagonal.

A second immediate consequence is

`sum_{s>=k} N_{d,s} lambda_{d,s}^2 <= lambda_{d,k} tau_k`,

so

`[sum_{s>=k} N_{d,s} lambda_{d,s}^2]/tau_k^2 <= 1/N_{d,k}`.

The last inequality uses `tau_k >= N_{d,k}lambda_{d,k}`. This is the exact high-tail effective-dimension scale needed later.

# 6. Growing-degree spherical hypercontractivity, independently re-derived

Let `Y` be a pure degree-`s` spherical harmonic and let

`H(z)=||z||^s Y(z/||z||)`.

Because `Y` is harmonic, `H` is a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree `s`. For a standard Gaussian `G=RU`, where `U` is uniform on the sphere and independent of the radius `R`, Gaussian Ornstein-Uhlenbeck hypercontractivity gives, for `p>=2`,

`||H(G)||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2) ||H(G)||_2`.

Independence of `R` and `U` turns this into

`(E R^(sp))^(1/p) ||Y||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2) (E R^(2s))^(1/2) ||Y||_2`.

Because `p>=2`, monotonicity of `L^p` norms applied to the nonnegative random variable `R^s` gives

`(E R^(sp))^(1/p) >= (E R^(2s))^(1/2)`.

Therefore the radial factor can only improve the estimate:

`||Y||_p <= (p-1)^(s/2) ||Y||_2`.

This is dimension-free and remains valid for the growing cutoff `s<=k+O(R)`. Thus the proof does not smuggle in constants valid only for fixed harmonic degree.

At `p=4` the squared fourth-moment envelope contributes the familiar `3^s` factor. At the conservative choice `p=R`, the high-moment envelope is controlled by powers of order `(2R)^s` after harmless absolute constants are absorbed.

# 7. High-tail random matrix concentration: source mechanics and scale check

Let `K_T` be the kernel consisting of degrees `s>=k`, and let `tau=tau_k`. Since `k>=1`, every retained spherical harmonic has zero mean, hence

`E_Z K_T(x,Z)=0`

for every fixed `x`. The tail is positive semidefinite because every Mercer coefficient is nonnegative, and its diagonal is the deterministic constant `tau`.

The Kaushik-Romberg-Muthukumar general kernel-matrix theorem applies to precisely this kind of centered kernel. I checked the current arXiv source rather than relying on a repository paraphrase. The proof first decouples the off-diagonal U-statistic with factor `8`; only after introducing an iid copied sample does it condition on the original sample, at which point the copied-column matrices are independent. The noncommutative Khintchine step is applied after that conditioning. The source's correlation kernel is centered in the second argument, and its displayed variance proxy contains the exact factor `1/4`.

Those dependency details matter: treating copied columns as unconditionally independent or moving the centering to the wrong argument would invalidate the reduction. Neither mistake occurs in the candidate proof.

Using the dimension-free harmonic moment bound from Section 6 and the factorial tail control from Section 5, the conservative normalized error terms used by P04/P22 are bounded by fixed `h`-dependent constants times

`d R 3^k / sqrt(n)`

and

`R d^2 (2R)^k / n`,

up to the logarithmic factors already allowed by the source theorem. On the prescribed sequence,

`3^k = d^(log_2 3 + o(1))`,

so

`d R 3^k / sqrt(n) = d^(-R/2+O(1)) -> 0`.

Also `log_d((2R)^k)=ell+1+o(1)`, hence

`R d^2 (2R)^k / n = d^(-R+ell+O(1)) -> 0`.

Any fixed power of `log n` is negligible compared with either decay. Consequently

`||K_T/tau - I_n||_op -> 0`

in probability.

The same correlation-kernel estimates control the population square-section Gram needed for high-frequency prediction; its relevant operator scale is `O(1/N_{d,k})` on the same good event. The exact diagonal bound in Section 5 already identifies that scale, and the KRM correlation-kernel control rules out an off-diagonal amplification.

# 8. Low block: exact row norm and matrix Chernoff

Let `Psi` be the `n x D_<k` matrix of all orthonormal spherical harmonics of degrees `<k`, evaluated at the sample points. The spherical addition theorem gives the deterministic row norm

`||Psi(X_i,:)||_2^2 = D_<k`.

Rotational orthonormality gives

`E[Psi(X_i,:)^T Psi(X_i,:)]=I_{D_<k}`.

The rows are iid. Matrix Chernoff therefore applies with single-summand operator bound `D_<k`. Since

`n/D_<k = d^(1-delta_*+o(1)) -> infinity`,

the failure probability is bounded by a dimension factor times `exp(-c n/D_<k)`, hence tends to zero superpolynomially on the present scale. Thus

`Psi^T Psi = n(I+o_op(1))`

with probability tending to one.

No coordinate independence inside a row is assumed or needed.

# 9. Exact low/high RKHS elimination

Write the full empirical Gram matrix as

`K = Psi Lambda Psi^T + K_T`,

where `Lambda` is diagonal with the low Mercer eigenvalues, repeated by multiplicity.

On the high-tail good event define

`W = tau K_T^(-1)`.

Then `W=I+o_op(1)`. The exact Woodbury/normal-equation elimination gives the fitted low coefficient vector

`beta_hat = G0^(-1) Psi^T W Y`,

where

`G0 = tau Lambda^(-1) + Psi^T W Psi`.

Both terms in `G0` are positive semidefinite. Sections 7-8 imply

`G0 >= c n I`

on the joint good event, so

`||G0^(-1)||_op <= C/n`.

For the true fixed-degree coefficient vector `beta_*`, eventually supported entirely inside the low block,

`beta_hat-beta_* = -G0^(-1) tau Lambda^(-1) beta_* + G0^(-1) Psi^T W epsilon`.

This identity separates signal bias and low-frequency noise without any appeal to a hard-edge limit for the full Gram matrix.

For fixed `q0`, the `r=0` Mercer term gives `lambda_{d,s} >= c_{h,q0} d^(-s)` for every `s<=q0`, while `tau<=K_h(x,x)=1`. Therefore

`||tau Lambda^(-1) beta_*|| <= C_{h,q0} d^q0 B`.

The deterministic low-frequency signal bias is consequently bounded by

`C B^2 d^(2q0)/n^2`.

Conditionally on the design, the low-frequency noise covariance and `||G0^(-1)||<=C/n` give

`E_epsilon ||beta_hat-beta_*||_low,noise^2 <= C sigma^2 D_<k/n`.

For the high-frequency fitted component, the normalized tail section has population squared norm at most `1/N_{d,k}` by Section 5, and the sampled correlation-kernel operator remains on that scale by Section 7. Applying this to the noise residual gives

`C sigma^2 n/N_{d,k}`,

while applying it to the signal residual produced by the low-block regularization gives

`C B^2 d^(2q0)/(n N_{d,k})`.

Combining low and high pieces yields the same explicit conditional expectation bound as the candidate proof:

`E_epsilon[R_ell | X] <= C [ sigma^2 (D_<k/n + n/N_{d,k}) + B^2 (d^(2q0)/n^2 + d^(2q0)/(n N_{d,k})) ]`

on a design event whose probability tends to one.

Every constant may depend on fixed `h,q0,B,sigma` but not on `ell,d,n`.

# 10. Final asymptotics and realized conditional risk

From Section 3,

`D_<k/n = d^(-0.5573049591...+o(1))`,

and

`n/N_{d,k} = d^(-0.4426950409...+o(1))`.

Since `q0` is fixed while `R->infinity`,
