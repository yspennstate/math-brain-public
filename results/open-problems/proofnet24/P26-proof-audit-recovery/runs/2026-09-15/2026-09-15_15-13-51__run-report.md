Let A and H be symmetric traceless matrices. Write `g = R u`, where g is standard Gaussian in R^d, u is uniform on the sphere, and R is independent of u. Since

`E R^6 = d(d+2)(d+4)`,

it suffices to calculate the centered Gaussian quadratic-form moment. Put

`X_A = g^T A g`, `X_H = g^T H g`.

For traceless symmetric M,

`E (g^T M g)^3 = 8 tr(M^3)`.

Polarize at `M = A + tH`. The coefficient of t on the right is `24 tr(A^2 H)`, while on the left it is `3 E[X_A^2 X_H]`. Hence

`E[X_A^2 X_H] = 8 tr(A^2 H)`.

Therefore

`∫ (u^T A u)^2 (u^T H u) dμ(u) = 8 tr(A^2 H) / (d(d+2)(d+4)).`   (2.1)

where `J(P) = C(P) - (s/d) I`.

Because `H ↦ <h(·),H>` is an isometry from `Sym_d^0` onto the degree-two spherical harmonics,

# 3. New proof of the exact intensity fourth moment

Let A and B be Frobenius-orthonormal symmetric traceless matrices:

`tr(A^2)=tr(B^2)=1`, `tr(AB)=0`.

Again write `g = R u`. Here

`E R^8 = d(d+2)(d+4)(d+6) = D_8`.

For traceless symmetric M, Wick's formula gives

`E (g^T M g)^4 = 12 (tr M^2)^2 + 48 tr(M^4).`   (3.1)

Taking M=A and dividing by D_8 yields

`∫ (u^T A u)^4 dμ = [12 + 48 tr(A^4)]/D_8.`   (3.2)

For the mixed moment, polarize (3.1) at `M=A+tB`.

Since `tr(AB)=0`,

`tr(A+tB)^2 = 1+t^2`,

so the t^2 coefficient of `12 (tr M^2)^2` is 24.

The six words with two A's and two B's in `tr(A+tB)^4` reduce cyclically to

`4 tr(A^2 B^2) + 2 tr(ABAB)`.

Thus the t^2 coefficient of `48 tr(A+tB)^4` is

`192 tr(A^2B^2) + 96 tr(ABAB)`.

The t^2 coefficient on the left is `6 E[(g^TAg)^2(g^TBg)^2]`. Therefore

`E[(g^TAg)^2(g^TBg)^2]`

`= 4 + 32 tr(A^2B^2) + 16 tr(ABAB)`.

Dividing by D_8 gives

`∫ (u^T A u)^2 (u^T B u)^2 dμ`

`= [4 + 32 tr(A^2B^2) + 16 tr(ABAB)] / D_8.`   (3.3)

Now

`F_P(u) = c_d^2 sum_a (u^T A_a u)^2`,

and

`c_d^4 / D_8 = alpha_d/4`.

Summing diagonal pairs and ordered off-diagonal pairs:

- scalar contribution: `12s + 4s(s-1) = 4s(s+2)`;
- trace contribution: `32 a(P) + 16 R(P)`.

Multiplication by `alpha_d/4` proves (0.1).

Since `∫F_P = s`, dividing by s^2 and subtracting one gives (0.2).

For symmetric A,B,

`||[A,B]||_F^2 = 2 [tr(A^2B^2) - tr(ABAB)]`.

Therefore

`delta(P) = a(P)-R(P) = (1/2) sum_{a,b} ||[A_a,A_b]||_F^2 ≥ 0`.

This confirms that the variable entering (0.2) is exactly P20's solved commutator sector.
