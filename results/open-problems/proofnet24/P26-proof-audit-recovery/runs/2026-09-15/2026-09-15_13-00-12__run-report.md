## 2. Setup and notation recovered from the source proof

Let the training Gram matrix decompose by harmonic degree as

`K = Σ_{s≥0} K_s`,  with  `K_s = λ_s Φ_s Φ_s^T`,

where degree `s` has multiplicity `N_s`, and the columns of `Φ_s` are the sampled orthonormal degree-`s` harmonics. Write

`t_s := Tr(K^{-1} K_s)`,

`q_s := Tr(K^{-1} K_s K^{-1})`.

Because `Σ_s K_s=K`,

`Σ_s t_s = Tr(K^{-1}K)=n`.

For a degree window `B`, let

`T_B := Σ_{s∈B} t_s`,   `D_B := Σ_{s∈B} N_s`.

The fixed Gaussian bandwidth is encoded by `a=h^{-2}>0`, independent of `d`.

The audit below makes explicit one hypothesis that is easy to leave implicit: for all sufficiently large `d>a`, the Gaussian Mercer eigenvalues are decreasing in harmonic degree. This is proved in §7 from the primary Bessel series, not assumed.

## 3. Step A — exact variance identity and a deterministic window inequality

For zero signal, the training labels are the noise vector `ε` with covariance `σ² I_n`, and the minimum-norm interpolation coefficients are

`α = K^{-1} ε`.

For an independent test point `x`, let `k_x` be the kernel vector against the training sample. The conditional prediction variance is

`E_ε[f_hat(x)^2 | X,x] = σ² k_x^T K^{-2} k_x`.

Averaging over the test point gives the test-kernel covariance

`C_X := E_x[k_x k_x^T]`.

Orthogonality of the Mercer eigenspaces yields

`C_X = Σ_s λ_s^2 Φ_s Φ_s^T = Σ_s λ_s K_s`.

Therefore the conditional excess risk is

`V(X) = σ² Tr(K^{-1} C_X K^{-1})`

`     = σ² Σ_s λ_s q_s`.                                      (3.1)

Next,

`t_s = λ_s ⟨K^{-1}Φ_s, Φ_s⟩_F`.

Cauchy–Schwarz in Frobenius inner product gives

`t_s^2 ≤ λ_s^2 ||K^{-1}Φ_s||_F^2 ||Φ_s||_F^2`.

Since

`q_s = λ_s ||K^{-1}Φ_s||_F^2`

and the addition theorem gives

`||Φ_s||_F^2 = n N_s`,

we get the deterministic inequality

`t_s^2 ≤ n N_s λ_s q_s`,

or

`λ_s q_s ≥ t_s^2/(nN_s)`.                                    (3.2)

Summing (3.2) over any window `B` and applying weighted Cauchy–Schwarz,

`Σ_{s∈B} λ_s q_s ≥ (1/n) Σ_{s∈B} t_s^2/N_s`

`                    ≥ T_B^2/(n D_B)`.                        (3.3)

Hence, after expectation over the training sample and Jensen,

`E_X[V(X)]/σ² ≥ E[T_B^2]/(nD_B) ≥ (E T_B)^2/(nD_B)`.          (3.4)

Thus Target 3 reduces to a lower bound on expected leverage mass in one cumulative low-degree window.

## 4. Step B — trace monotonicity reduces to a flattened low block

Fix `L`, and write

`A := K_{≤L}`,   `B := K_{>L}`,   so `K=A+B`.

Let

`G := Φ_{≤L} Φ_{≤L}^T`,   `D := D_{d,L}=Σ_{s≤L}N_s`,

and let `λ:=λ_L`. Once degree monotonicity is established, `λ_s≥λ_L` for every `s≤L`, hence

`A ≥ A_0 := λ G`.                                             (4.1)

Set

`C := A_0+B`.

Then `C≤K`, so inverse order gives `K^{-1}≤C^{-1}`. The cumulative leverage is

`T := Tr(K^{-1}A)`

`   = n - Tr(K^{-1}B)`.

Because `B≥0` and `K^{-1}≤C^{-1}`,

`Tr(K^{-1}B) ≤ Tr(C^{-1}B)`.

Therefore

`T ≥ n - Tr(C^{-1}B)`

`  = Tr(C^{-1}A_0)`.                                          (4.2)

For the Gaussian RBF kernel, distinct continuously distributed sample points give an invertible Gram matrix almost surely. Equivalently, all inverse-order steps may be regularized by adding `ηI` and then taking `η↓0`; no singular-event shortcut is needed.

## 5. Step C — the Hilbert–Schmidt lower bound

Let

`X := C^{-1/2} A_0^{1/2}`,   `Y := C^{1/2} A_0^{1/2}`.

Then

`⟨X,Y⟩_HS = Tr(A_0)`.

By Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz,

`(Tr A_0)^2 ≤ Tr(C^{-1}A_0) Tr(CA_0)`.

Combining with (4.2),

`T ≥ Tr(C^{-1}A_0)`

`  ≥ (Tr A_0)^2 / Tr(CA_0)`.                                  (5.1)

Now `Tr G=nD`, so `Tr A_0=λnD`; also

`Tr(CA_0)=Tr((λG+B)λG)`

`          = λ² Tr(G²) + λ Tr(BG)`.

Thus

`T ≥ (λ n D)^2 / [λ² Tr(G²)+λ Tr(BG)]`.                       (5.2)

This is deterministic and contains no heuristic effective-dimension replacement.

## 6. Step D — exact expectations of the denominator

The cumulative low-degree projection kernel has diagonal `D`, hence `G_{ii}=D`. For `i≠j`, the projection/reproducing identity gives

`E[G_{ij}^2]=D`.

Therefore

`E Tr(G²)=nD²+n(n-1)D`

`          = nD(D+n-1)`.                                      (6.1)

For the high block

`B = Σ_{s>L} λ_s Φ_s Φ_s^T`,

write its diagonal Mercer mass as

`τ_{>L} := Σ_{s>L} N_s λ_s`.

For `i≠j`, harmonic orthogonality between the cumulative low projection and each high-degree projection gives zero expectation to the corresponding cross term. On the diagonal, `G_{ii}=D` and `B_{ii}=τ_{>L}`. Hence

`E Tr(BG)=nD τ_{>L}`.                                         (6.2)

The numerator of (5.2) is deterministic. Since `z↦1/z` is convex on `(0,∞)`, Jensen applied to the denominator yields

`E T ≥ (λ nD)^2 / E[λ²Tr(G²)+λTr(BG)]`

`    = nD / [D+n-1+τ_{>L}/λ_L]`.                              (6.3)

No ratio of random expectations has been interchanged incorrectly.

## 7. Step E/H — primary-source verification of the Gaussian Mercer ratio

P04 uses the adjacent eigenvalue bound

`λ_{s+1}/λ_s ≤ a/(d+2s)`.                                    (7.1)

I checked this independently against NIST DLMF §10.25.2 (`https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.25.E2`), which gives for the modified Bessel function

`I_ν(z) = (z/2)^ν Σ_{k≥0} (z²/4)^k / [k! Γ(ν+k+1)]`.

For `a>0`, every term is positive and

`I_{ν+1}(a)`

` = (a/2)^{ν+1} Σ_{k≥0} (a²/4)^k / [k! Γ(ν+k+2)]`

` = (a/2) (a/2)^ν Σ_{k≥0} (a²/4)^k / [(ν+k+1) k! Γ(ν+k+1)]`

` ≤ a/[2(ν+1)] I_ν(a)`.                                      (7.2)

For the spherical Gaussian Mercer spectrum, `ν=s+(d-2)/2`, so `2(ν+1)=d+2s`, proving (7.1).

There is also a direct series check in the P04 source proof:

`λ_{d,s}=e^{-a} Σ_{r≥0}`

` a^{s+2r}/[2^r r! d(d+2)…(d+2(s+r)-2)]`.

Termwise comparison of the `s+1` and `s` series gives the same factor `a/(d+2s)`. Thus this imported spectral inequality does not rest on an uncited asymptotic.

In particular, once `d>a`, (7.1) is `<1` for every `s≥0`. Hence

`λ_0 ≥ λ_1 ≥ … ≥ λ_L`,

which proves the low-block order (4.1) used earlier.

## 8. Step F — cumulative harmonic dimension and the `1/5` window fraction

The exact cumulative harmonic dimension is

`D_{d,L} = C(d+L-1,L) + C(d+L-2,L-1)`.                       (8.1)

Its exact adjacent ratio is

`D_{d,L+1}/D_{d,L}`

` = [(d+2L+1)(d+L-1)] / [(d+2L-1)(L+1)]`.                    (8.2)

Let

`L_d := max{L : D_{d,L}≤2^d}`,

and put `n=2^d`, `D=D_{d,L_d}`, `y=D/n`.

For `L=cd`, Stirling gives the entropy exponent

`F(c) := (1+c) log(1+c) - c log c`.

At `c=2/7`,

`7F(2/7)=9log9-7log7-2log2`.

The inequality `F(2/7)<log2` is equivalent to

`9^9 < 2^9 7^7`,

which is true. The gap is strict, so Stirling implies

`L_d ≥ floor(2d/7)`

for all sufficiently large `d`.

For such `L`, the first factor in (8.2) is eventually `<10/9`. The second obeys

`(d+L-1)/(L+1) < 9/2`

exactly when

`7L > 2d-11`,

which is supplied by `L≥floor(2d/7)` for large `d`. Thus

`D_{d,L+1}/D_{d,L} < 5`                                      (8.3)

at the maximal cutoff for all sufficiently large `d`.

Maximality says

`D_{d,L_d} ≤ n < D_{d,L_d+1}`.

Using (8.3),

`n < 5D`,

hence

`y=D/n > 1/5`.                                                (8.4)

This is the exact dimension fact needed for the `5/36` constant.

## 9. Step G/I — multiplicity control and the high-degree Mercer tail

The exact degree multiplicity ratio is

`N_{s+1}/N_s`

` = [(d+2s)(d+s-2)] / [(d+2s-2)(s+1)]`.                      (9.1)

For `s≥floor(2d/7)`, the first factor is eventually `<10/9`. The second factor is `<9/2` precisely when

`7s > 2d-13`.

Therefore, for large `d` and every `s≥L_d`,

`N_{s+1}/N_s < 5`.                                           (9.2)

As a cross-check, P23's `P23-MULTIPLICITY-RATIO-22` obtains the same factor `5` already for `s≥d/4` by verifying

`8s²+(2d+4)s-d²+7d-10 ≥0`,

whose left side is increasing in `s` and at `s=d/4` equals `2(4d-5)>0`. I did not need that stronger threshold for the P04 proof.

Let

`b_s:=N_s λ_s`.

Combining (7.1) and (9.2),

`b_{s+1}/b_s ≤ 5a/(d+2s) ≤ ρ_d:=5a/d`                        (9.3)

for `s≥L_d`. For large `d`, `ρ_d≤1/2`, and therefore

`τ_{>L} = Σ_{j≥1} b_{L+j}`

`        ≤ b_L ρ_d/(1-ρ_d)`

`        ≤ D λ_L ρ_d/(1-ρ_d)`.                               (9.4)

Define

`ε_d := τ_{>L}/(nλ_L)`.

Since `D≤n`,

`0≤ε_d ≤ ρ_d/(1-ρ_d) ≤ 10a/d →0`.                            (9.5)

So the Mercer tail is negligible at exactly the scale required by (6.3).

## 10. Step J — closing the `5/36` lower bound

Divide (6.3) by `n` and use `y=D/n`:

`E T/n ≥ y/[1+y-1/n+ε_d]`.

For a lower bound we may safely enlarge the denominator:

`E T/n ≥ y/[1+y+ε_d]`.                                       (10.1)

Insert (10.1) into the window variance bound (3.4):

`E V/σ² ≥ (E T)^2/(nD)`

`        = (E T/n)^2 / y`

`        ≥ y/(1+y+ε_d)^2`.                                   (10.2)

For fixed `ε≥0`,

`g_ε(y):=y/(1+y+ε)^2`

has derivative

`g_ε'(y)=(1+ε-y)/(1+y+ε)^3 ≥0`

for `0<y≤1`. By (8.4), `y>1/5`; by (9.5), `ε_d→0`. Therefore

`liminf_{d→∞} E V/σ²`

` ≥ (1/5)/(1+1/5)^2`

` = 5/36`.                                                    (10.3)

This reproduces the exact P04 Target-3 constant from first principles. Every inequality direction used in the trace reduction, Jensen step, dimension jump, and tail estimate has been checked independently.

## 11. Independent one-window asymptotic sharpening

The crude factor `5` in §8 is not the actual phase-boundary jump. The same single cumulative window yields a slightly stronger universal constant.

Let `c_*` be the unique positive solution of

`F(c_*)=log2`,

where `F(c)=(1+c)log(1+c)-c log c`. Since

`F'(c)=log((1+c)/c)>0`,

this root is unique. Uniform Stirling asymptotics on compact `c`-intervals, together with the strict inequalities `F(2/7)<log2<F(1)`, imply from maximality of `L_d` that

`L_d/d → c_*`.                                                (11.1)

Numerically,

`c_* = 0.2938153733404154933166016533…`.                      (11.2)

Using the exact ratio (8.2),

`R_d := D_{d,L_d+1}/D_{d,L_d} → r_* := (1+c_*)/c_*`

with

`r_* = 4.403497879062293280675283113…`.                       (11.3)

Maximality gives `n<D_{L+1}=R_dD_L`, hence

`y_d=D_L/n > 1/R_d`.

Since `g_ε(y)` from §10 is increasing on `0<y≤1`, and `ε_d→0`,

`liminf E V/σ²`

` ≥ (1/r_*)/(1+1/r_*)²`

` = r_*/(1+r_*)²`

` = c_*(1+c_*)/(1+2c_*)²`

` = 0.1508161398604241208274760024…`.                         (11.4)

This strictly improves the P04 `5/36≈0.1388889` bound and also exceeds the coarse `18/121≈0.1487603` obtained by replacing `r_*` by `9/2`. It is **not** a repository-best Target-3 constant: P23 reports a stronger multi-window lower bound near `0.310607`.
