# 3. Ordered full DPP equals determinant-square tilt

Take m iid base points U_1,...,U_m from spherical measure mu and form the m by m row matrix

`H = [ h(U_1)^T ; ... ; h(U_m)^T ]`.

Because the rows are centered/isotropic in E,

`E_base det(H)^2 = m!`.

This follows directly by expanding both determinants: independence across rows forces the two permutations to agree row by row, and each surviving row covariance contributes a Kronecker delta; exactly m! permutations survive.

The rank-m projection kernel is

`K(u,v)=<h(u),h(v)>`.

Hence the ordered projection-DPP density with respect to mu^m is

`det[K(U_i,U_j)] / m!`

`= det(H H^T)/m!`

`= det(H)^2/m!`.

Thus the ordered full harmonic projection DPP is exactly the determinant-square tilted iid square row law Q used by P04/P22/P20.

This equality is finite-dimensional and exact.

# 4. New theorem: dual suffix representation of every residual plane

## `P26-T4-DUAL-SUFFIX-REPRESENTATION-13` — PROVED

Work on the almost-sure event det(H) != 0 and set

`B = H^(-1)`.

Expose the ordered DPP points U_1,...,U_(m-s). The residual coefficient plane is by definition the orthogonal complement of their coefficient-row span:

`Ran(P_s) = ker H_[1:m-s]`.

For every j>m-s,

`H (B e_j) = e_j`.

Therefore, for every k<=m-s,

`h(U_k)^T B e_j = 0`.

So each suffix inverse column B e_j lies in the residual kernel. The suffix contains exactly s columns, and they are linearly independent because B is invertible. The residual kernel also has dimension s. Hence

`Ran(P_s) = span{B e_(m-s+1),...,B e_m}`.       (4.1)

This proves the pointwise ordered statement.

The ordered DPP density det(H)^2/m! is invariant under row permutations. Relabeling rows permutes the columns of B=H^(-1) by the inverse permutation. Therefore, if S is a uniform s-subset of {1,...,m},

`P_s =_law proj span{B e_j : j in S}`             (4.2)

under determinant-square tilt Q.

In particular,

`P_2 =_law proj span{B e_I, B e_J}`               (4.3)

for a uniform unordered pair {I,J}.

### Consequence

This representation is compatible with the earlier warning that the last two raw coherent rows do not span P_2. The correct dual objects are inverse columns, not raw rows.

# 5. New theorem: exact pair Jordan quotient

## `P26-T4-DUAL-PAIR-JORDAN-QUOTIENT-13` — PROVED

Let x,y be any two linearly independent vectors in E and identify them with traceless symmetric matrices X,Y. Put

`a = <X,X>_F`,
`b = <Y,Y>_F`,
`c = <X,Y>_F`,
`Delta = a b - c^2`.

Then Delta>0.

Let U be the E-valued two-column matrix with columns x,y. The orthogonal projector onto their span is

`P = U (U^T U)^(-1) U^T`,

and

`(U^T U)^(-1) = (1/Delta) [[b,-c],[-c,a]]`.

The Jordan contraction C(P) is the basis-independent contraction of this coefficient projector with matrix multiplication. Hence

`C(P)`
` = sum_(r,t=1)^2 [(U^T U)^(-1)]_(rt) X_r X_t`
` = [ b X^2 + a Y^2 - c(XY+YX) ] / Delta`.      (5.1)

This matrix is symmetric and positive semidefinite; it has trace two because P has rank two.

Therefore

`e(P) = tr(C(P)^2) - 4/d`.                        (5.2)

By positivity of C(P) and Cauchy-Schwarz,

`tr(C(P)^2) >= tr(C(P))^2/d = 4/d`,

so e(P)>=0 pointwise.

Now apply (5.1) to the pair of inverse columns in (4.3). Write X_i for the traceless symmetric matrix corresponding to B e_i, and define

`b_i = ||X_i||_F^2`,
`c_ij = <X_i,X_j>_F`,
`Delta_ij = b_i b_j - c_ij^2`.

Since B is invertible, Delta_ij>0 for i!=j. Then

`e_2 = E_Q [ 1/C(m,2) * sum_(i<j) J_ij(B) ]`,     (5.3)

where

`J_ij(B)`
` = tr( [ b_j X_i^2 + b_i X_j^2 - c_ij(X_i X_j+X_j X_i) ]^2 ) / Delta_ij^2`
`   - 4/d`.                                       (5.4)

This is an exact finite-d identity.

### Structural meaning

J_ij is homogeneous of degree zero under independent nonzero rescalings of the two columns. Thus column norms by themselves are irrelevant. The endpoint depends on the normalized two-frame geometry produced by the pair after Gram orthonormalization.

Consequently the P22 covariance identity for raw inverse entries is useful but cannot by itself control (5.4): the denominator and fourth-order numerator must remain coupled.

## 6.1 Independent cofactor derivation

Let A be m by m with iid centered isotropic rows, nonsingular almost surely, and B=A^(-1). Let C_(ba)(A) be the signed cofactor obtained by deleting row b and column a. Then

`B_ab = C_(ba)(A) / det(A)`.

Under

`dQ/dP = det(A)^2/m!`,

we have

`E_Q[B_ab B_cd] = (1/m!) E[ C_(ba) C_(dc) ]`.    (6.1)

If b!=d, condition on row d. The cofactor C_(dc) omits row d, while C_(ba) is linear in row d. Centeredness makes the conditional expectation zero.

Now take b=d. Delete that common row and call the remaining (m-1) by m matrix R. Its signed maximal-minor vector w has components w_a=C_(ba).

Expand two minors. For permutations sigma,tau of the retained column set, row independence gives

`E product_r R_(r,sigma(r)) R_(r,tau(r))`
` = product_r delta_(sigma(r),tau(r))`.

A nonzero term requires sigma=tau. If a!=c the two minors use different retained column sets, so there is no common bijection and the expectation is zero. If a=c there are exactly (m-1)! surviving permutations, each contributing one. Hence

`E[C_(ba) C_(bc)] = (m-1)! delta_ac`.             (6.2)

Combining (6.1)-(6.2),

`E_Q[B_ab B_cd] = delta_ac delta_bd / m`.         (6.3)

The same one-row conditioning gives E_Q B_ab=0.

Only second moments are spent. Every row enters a surviving cofactor product at degree at most two, so no hidden higher moment is required.

Audit verdict: PASS.

# 7. New no-go: inverse second-order design does not control Jordan two-frame geometry

## `P26-T4-INVERSE-2DESIGN-JORDAN-NOGO-13` — PROVED

The exact identity (6.3) is strong, but it is still below the information level of (5.4). The following explicit model proves this sharply.

## 7.1 Fixed coefficient basis

Let

`E = Sym_d^0`,
`m=(d-1)(d+2)/2`.

Choose a Frobenius-orthonormal basis of E containing all off-diagonal matrices

`S_ij = (E_ij+E_ji)/sqrt(2)`,  `1<=i<j<=d`,

plus any orthonormal basis of the diagonal traceless subspace.

There are

`M = d(d-1)/2`

off-diagonal basis elements.

Let R(g) be the orthogonal representation of g in O(d) acting on E by

`X -> g X g^T`.

Take g Haar in O(d), Pi a uniform m by m permutation matrix, and

`D = diag(epsilon_1,...,epsilon_m)`

with independent Rademacher signs, all mutually independent. Define

`B = R(g) Pi D`.                                    (7.1)

Then B is orthogonal, so every singular value equals one and its condition number is exactly one.

Its columns are exchangeable, independently sign-symmetric, and the induced matrix-column law is O(d)-conjugation invariant.

## 7.2 Exact inverse 2-design covariance

For b!=d, independent signs give

`E[B_ab B_cd]=0`.

For b=d, average first over the uniform column permutation. Since R(g) is orthogonal,

`E_Pi,D[B_ab B_cb | g]`
` = (1/m) sum_k R(g)_(a k) R(g)_(c k)`
` = delta_ac/m`.

Therefore

`E[B_ab B_cd] = delta_ac delta_bd/m`,              (7.2)

exactly matching P22's determinant-tilted inverse covariance.

Thus (7.1) satisfies the full second-order design identity, with much stronger conditioning than the actual inverse matrix.

## 7.3 Uniform pair has order-one Jordan defect

Choose a uniform unordered pair of distinct columns of B. Conjugation and signs do not change the span Jordan defect, so it suffices to inspect the corresponding two basis matrices.

If the pair consists of disjoint off-diagonal edges S_ij and S_kl with {i,j} disjoint from {k,l}, then they are Frobenius orthonormal and

`S_ij^2 = (E_ii+E_jj)/2`,
`S_kl^2 = (E_kk+E_ll)/2`.

Hence for their two-plane

`C = S_ij^2 + S_kl^2`

has four diagonal entries equal to 1/2 and all others zero. Therefore

`tr(C^2)=1`

and

`e(P)=1-4/d`.                                      (7.3)

The exact probability that a uniformly selected ordered-first/unordered-second pair lands in two disjoint off-diagonal basis elements is

`p_d = [M/m] * [C(d-2,2)/(m-1)]`

`    = d(d-2)(d-3) / [(d+2)(d^2+d-4)]`.            (7.4)

This tends to one.

Since e(P)>=0 for every rank-two plane, (7.3)-(7.4) give

`E e(P_pair)`
` >= p_d (1-4/d)`
` = (d-4)(d-2)(d-3) / [(d+2)(d^2+d-4)]`
` -> 1`.                                            (7.5)

Thus even the conjunction of

- exact covariance `E[B_ab B_cd]=delta_ac delta_bd/m`;
- perfect condition number one;
- exchangeable columns;
- independent column signs;
- physical O(d)-conjugation invariance

is compatible with an order-one uniform-pair Jordan defect.

This is a strict no-go for any attempted deduction of e_2=O(1/d) from those properties alone.

### Limitation

The synthetic B in (7.1) is not claimed to be the inverse of an iid physical-harmonic square sample under determinant tilt. Therefore this theorem does NOT disprove P04 Target 4. It disproves only the proposed information reduction from second-order inverse isotropy plus the listed symmetries/conditioning.
