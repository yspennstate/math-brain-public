# 3. Independent check of the second audit

The point of this section is not to create another audit count; it is to verify that P22's audit is mathematically real and independent enough to count as the required second OTHER audit.

## 3.1 Spectral block setup

Write the kernel Gram matrix as

\[
K=\sum_{s\ge0}K_s,
\qquad
K_s=\lambda_s Z_sZ_s^\top,
\]

where the columns of `Z_s` are the degree-`s` orthonormal spherical harmonics evaluated on the sample. Let `N_s` be the degree-`s` multiplicity. For a cutoff `r`, define

\[
A:=\sum_{s\le r}K_s,
\qquad
B:=K-A,
\qquad
D:=\sum_{s\le r}N_s.
\]

For the Gaussian kernel on the sphere, the eigenvalues are decreasing over the relevant cumulative block, so

\[
A\succeq \lambda_r G,
\qquad
G:=ZZ^\top,
\]

where `Z` concatenates the cumulative features.

The integrated squared-kernel matrix is

\[
K^{(2)}=\sum_s\lambda_s K_s,
\]

and the noise variance contribution is

\[
V:=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-2}K^{(2)}).
\]

## 3.2 Deterministic block Hilbert--Schmidt inequality

Set

\[
t_s:=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}K_s),
\qquad
q_s:=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}K_sK^{-1}).
\]

Using Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz with `K_s^{1/2}` gives

\[
t_s^2
\le
q_s\,\operatorname{Tr}(K_s).
\]

By the spherical addition formula, every diagonal entry of the degree-`s` projection kernel equals `N_s`, so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_s)=nN_s\lambda_s.
\]

Therefore

\[
\lambda_s q_s\ge \frac{t_s^2}{nN_s}.
\]

Summing over `s\le r`, then applying scalar Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
V
\ge
\sum_{s\le r}\frac{t_s^2}{nN_s}
\ge
\frac{(\sum_{s\le r}t_s)^2}{nD}.
\]

With

\[
T:=\operatorname{Tr}(K^{-1}A)=\sum_{s\le r}t_s,
\]

we have the pointwise inequality

\[
\boxed{V\ge \frac{T^2}{nD}.}
\]

This is the key deterministic conversion from cumulative leverage into a variance lower bound.

## 3.3 Cumulative leverage lower bound

Since `A\succeq A_0:=\lambda_rG` and `B\succeq0`, the function

\[
A\mapsto \operatorname{Tr}((A+B)^{-1}A)
=n-\operatorname{Tr}((A+B)^{-1}B)
\]

is monotone increasing in the Loewner order after taking the trace. Hence

\[
T\ge \operatorname{Tr}((A_0+B)^{-1}A_0).
\]

For positive semidefinite `A_0` and positive definite `C:=A_0+B`, Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(C^{-1}A_0)\,\operatorname{Tr}(CA_0)
\ge (\operatorname{Tr}A_0)^2.
\]

Thus

\[
T\ge \frac{(\operatorname{Tr}A_0)^2}{\operatorname{Tr}((A_0+B)A_0)}.
\]

Now

\[
\operatorname{Tr}A_0=\lambda_r nD.
\]

The projection-kernel moment identity is exact:

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)=nD(D+n-1).
\]

Indeed, the diagonal contribution is `nD^2`, while for independent distinct sample points the expected squared cumulative projection kernel is `D`, giving `n(n-1)D` off diagonal.

Let

\[
\tau_r:=\sum_{s\ge r+1}N_s\lambda_s
\]

be the Mercer tail trace. Orthogonality between cumulative and tail harmonics gives

\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(BG)=nD\tau_r.
\]

Because `x\mapsto1/x` is convex, Jensen applied to the reciprocal denominator yields

\[
\mathbb ET
\ge
\frac{nD}{D+n-1+\tau_r/\lambda_r}.
\]

Combining with `V\ge T^2/(nD)` and Jensen for the square,

\[
\mathbb EV
\ge
\frac{nD}{(D+n-1+\tau_r/\lambda_r)^2}.
\]

Put

\[
\rho:=\frac nD,
\qquad
\varepsilon_r:=\frac{\tau_r}{n\lambda_r}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb EV
\ge
\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho-1/D+\rho\varepsilon_r)^2}.
\]

Thus if `\varepsilon_r\to0`, the asymptotic lower envelope is

\[
\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)^2}.
\]

## 3.4 Mercer-tail check and primary-source hypothesis

The only special-function input used for the Gaussian Mercer tail is a ratio bound for modified Bessel functions. The current run rechecked the primary NIST DLMF power series, equation 10.25.2:

\[
I_\nu(a)
=\left(\frac a2\right)^\nu
\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(a^2/4)^k}{k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}.
\]

For `a>0` and `\nu>-1`, all terms are positive. Therefore

\[
\frac{I_{\nu+1}(a)}{I_\nu(a)}
=
\frac a2\,
\frac{\sum_{k\ge0}c_k/(\nu+k+1)}{\sum_{k\ge0}c_k}
\le
\frac{a}{2(\nu+1)},
\]

with positive weights

\[
c_k:=\frac{(a^2/4)^k}{k!\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}.
\]

This is exactly the inequality needed in the repository's Gaussian Mercer-tail estimate. No sign cancellation or unstated asymptotic theorem is being imported here.

The repository's harmonic multiplicity comparison for the maximal cutoff `r(d)` with `N_r\le n<N_{r+1}` gives the phase range

\[
\rho=\frac nD\in[1/3,3]+o(1),
\]

and the above Bessel ratio yields

\[
\varepsilon_r=\frac{\tau_r}{n\lambda_r}\to0.
\]

Hence

\[
\liminf\mathbb EV
\ge
\min_{\rho\in[1/3,3]}\frac{\rho}{(1+\rho)^2}
=
\frac3{16}.
\]

The already-proved kernel-mean correction satisfies

\[
\text{mean correction}\le \frac1{24}+o(1).
\]

Therefore the original Target-3 lower bound follows:

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{d\to\infty}
\frac{\mathbb E\,\mathrm{ExcessRisk}_{2^d,d}}{\sigma^2}
\ge
\frac3{16}-\frac1{24}
=
\frac5{36}.
}
\]

This reconstruction matches the P04 proof and the independent P22 audit. I found no missing hypothesis in the second audit that would prevent it from counting as a genuine OTHER audit.

\[

4. keep the conditional leverage/cross term intact long enough for its cancellation with `3e_s` to occur.

This is an attack plan, not a claimed proof. `P04-T4-RANK-TWO-CANCELLATION-04b` remains **OPEN**.
