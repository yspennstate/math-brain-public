## 4. New theorem: sharpen P22's linear-DPP invisibility rate

### `P26-P22-LINEAR-DPP-RATE-SHARPEN-01` — PROVED

Keep exactly P22's pure degree-two projection-DPP hypotheses. Let
\[
f_r=c_{m,r}\det(I+R)
\]
be the \(r\)-point density relative to \(\mu^r\), where \(R\) is symmetric with zero diagonal and
\[
T_2:=\operatorname{tr}R^2.
\]
P22 proves, uniformly for \(r\le Ad\),
\[
ET_2=O_A(1),
\qquad
E|T_2-ET_2|=O_A(d^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
E\operatorname{tr}R^4=O_A(d^{-1}).
\]
It also proves
\[
\log c_{m,r}
=
\frac12 ET_2+O_A(d^{-1}).
\]

Set
\[
\mathcal G=\{\|R\|_{\rm op}\le1/2\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal G\),
\[
\log\det(I+R)
=
-\frac12T_2
+\frac13\operatorname{tr}R^3
+
\sum_{j\ge4}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\operatorname{tr}R^j.
\]

For the cubic term, if \(\lambda_i\) are the eigenvalues of \(R\),
\[
|\operatorname{tr}R^3|
\le
\sum_i|\lambda_i|^3
\le
\left(\sum_i\lambda_i^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_i\lambda_i^4\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
E|\operatorname{tr}R^3|
\le
(ET_2)^{1/2}
(E\operatorname{tr}R^4)^{1/2}
=
O_A(d^{-1/2}).
\]

For the quartic-and-higher remainder, on \(|\lambda|\le1/2\),
\[
\sum_{j\ge4}\frac{|\lambda|^j}{j}
\le C|\lambda|^4.
\]
Summing over eigenvalues,
\[
\left|
\sum_{j\ge4}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\operatorname{tr}R^j
\right|
\le
C\operatorname{tr}R^4.
\]
Hence its \(L^1\) size is \(O_A(d^{-1})\).

Combining with the exact normalization cancellation,
\[
\log f_r
=
-\frac12(T_2-ET_2)
+\frac13\operatorname{tr}R^3
+O(\operatorname{tr}R^4)
+O_A(d^{-1})
\]
on \(\mathcal G\), and thus
\[
E[|\log f_r|1_{\mathcal G}]
=
O_A(d^{-1/2}).
\]

P22's fourth-trace estimate gives
\[
P(\mathcal G^c)
\le
16E\|R\|_{\rm op}^4
\le
16E\operatorname{tr}R^4
=
O_A(d^{-1}).
\]
P22 also proves by Hadamard
\[
0\le f_r\le c_{m,r}\le C_A.
\]
Therefore, on \(\mathcal G\),
\[
|f_r-1|\le C_A'|\log f_r|,
\]
while on \(\mathcal G^c\),
\[
|f_r-1|\le C_A+1.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
E_{\mu^r}|f_r-1|
=
O_A(d^{-1/2}).
}
\]
Since \(f_r\) is uniformly bounded,
\[
(f_r-1)^2\le(C_A+1)|f_r-1|,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_m^{(r)}\|\mu^r)
=
O_A(d^{-1/2})

\qquad
\text{uniformly for }1\le r\le Ad.

## 5. A general low-block trace inequality and why the unflattened route stalls

### `P26-GENERAL-LOWBLOCK-HS-01` — PROVED

For positive semidefinite \(A,B\) and \(K=A+B\) (regularized if necessary),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}A)
\ge
\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(KA)}.
}
\]
This is the same Hilbert–Schmidt argument as in §3.2 without flattening.

If the low Mercer block has multiplicities \(N_s\), weights \(\lambda_s\), low mass
\[
\tau=\sum_{\rm low}N_s\lambda_s,
\]
quadratic mass
\[
\nu=\sum_{\rm low}N_s\lambda_s^2,
\]
and high-tail mass \(\tau_{\rm hi}\), then independence and harmonic orthogonality give
\[
E\operatorname{tr}(KA)
=
n\tau(\tau+\tau_{\rm hi})+n(n-1)\nu.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
E\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}A)
\ge
\frac{n\tau^2}
{\tau(\tau+\tau_{\rm hi})+(n-1)\nu}.
}
\]
When total kernel mass is normalized to one,
\[
E\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}A)
\ge
\frac{n\tau^2}{\tau+(n-1)\nu}.
\]

More generally, for a floor \(A_0\le A\) with spectral weights \(w_s\le\lambda_s\), put
\[
\alpha=\sum N_sw_s,\qquad
\beta=\sum N_sw_s^2.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
E\operatorname{tr}(K^{-1}A)
\ge
\frac{n\alpha^2}
{\alpha(\alpha+\tau_{\rm hi})+(n-1)\beta}.
}
\]

The naive choice \(w_s=\lambda_s\) is often too weak because \(\beta\) is dominated by stronger lower modes. P23's constant-floor choice \(w_s=\lambda_L\) is therefore not cosmetic: it suppresses the quadratic spectral penalty. The unflattened route is recorded as an **insufficient closure route**, not as a counterexample to the target.
