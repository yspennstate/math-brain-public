## 4. NEW-METHODS before bookkeeping

### B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I tried to break the implication at exactly the interfaces where ordinary mixing intuition is known to fail.

Bradley's own 2001 counterexample gives a strictly stationary countable-state Markov chain that is ordinary `rho`-mixing but not interlaced `rho*`-mixing. Therefore any proof that merely turns one-time or past/future maximal correlation into interlaced correlation is false in principle. The new argument has to exploit something genuinely stronger about `phi`.

That stronger fact is uniform essential control of transition rows: for an `n`-step Markov kernel, `phi(n)` forces all transition rows, outside a common null set, into a total-variation ball of radius `phi(n)` around the unconditional output law. Hence the essential row diameter is at most `2 phi(n)`. This uniform row statement survives arbitrary conditioning on earlier history. Bradley's `rho` counterexample does not supply such a row-TV control; that is precisely where the attempted negation is blocked.

I also attacked three local failure modes directly:

1. **Different future events might require different exceptional row sets.** A countable generating algebra plus a monotone-class extension produces one full-measure row set valid for every future Borel event, so the null sets do not leak into later prefix conditioning.
2. **Conditioning on Peyre's prefix might change the adjacent channel.** For adjacent opposite-color blocks every prefix variable lies strictly before the earlier target block, so the Markov kernel from the last state of the earlier block to the first state of the later block is unchanged; only its input law changes.
3. **A nonadjacent pair might remain correlated after prefix conditioning.** The prefix contains an entire intervening opposite-color block. A state in that block is a genuine Markov separator. Conditional on that state, past and future factor; conditioning further on coordinates belonging separately to the two factors preserves the factorization. Thus the subjective correlation is exactly zero.

No counterexample survived these tests.

### B11 — DUALIZE / pass to the correlation operator

The central combinatorial step becomes transparent on the operator side. After merging a finite interlacing into maximal chronological monochromatic blocks, put one color on the rows and the other on the columns. Peyre's local subjective-correlation bounds form a matrix `E` supported exactly on adjacent pairs of the alternating path.

If

\[
\kappa(n):=\min\{1,2\sqrt{2\phi(n)}\},
\]

then every nonzero entry of `E` is at most `kappa(n)`, and every row and column has at most two nonzero entries. Therefore

\[
\|E\|_{2\to2}
\le \sqrt{\|E\|_1\|E\|_\infty}
\le 2\kappa(n).
\]

Peyre's N-against-M tensorization theorem bounds the global maximal correlation by this operator norm. This is the exact place where arbitrarily many interlaced blocks are controlled uniformly.

For a uniform path with `m` chronological blocks the exact singular norm is

\[
2\kappa(n)\cos\frac{\pi}{m+1}<2\kappa(n).
\]

Thus the factor `2` from path accumulation is asymptotically sharp for long paths if one uses only the local edge-correlation information. This diagnoses why an argument that simply bounds each adjacent boundary and then hopes the same constant controls the full interlacing cannot work.

For source closure one can use the still shorter one-lag form. Since `phi(n)->0`, choose `n_0` with `phi(n_0)<1/32`; then

\[
\rho^*(n_0)\le4\sqrt{2\phi(n_0)}<1.
\]

Bradley Theorem 3.3(6) then forces exponential decay of `rho*`. The owner package actually proves the stronger all-`n` quantitative estimate, but the source question needs only this strict one-lag contraction.

### B22 — READ THE FAILURES, NOT THE SUCCESSES

The literature failures identify the theorem's exact missing mechanism.

- Bradley 1997 proves interlaced-rho mixing under the stronger lower-psi condition.
- Bradley 2001 constructs a stationary Markov chain that is ordinary rho-mixing but not interlaced-rho mixing.
- Bradley's 2005 survey leaves exactly the phi-to-rho-star Markov implication as Question 5.
- Later work treats reversible/spectral or special copula / lower-psi classes, but the bounded current search did not locate an explicit full-source resolution of Question 5.
- Peyre 2010 supplies the post-source tensorization tool whose minimal-prefix form is exactly what the Markov-separator argument needs.

So the printed difficulty is not obtaining decay across one temporal cut. It is preventing an arbitrarily complicated interlacing from amplifying many small local dependencies. The row-TV lemma plus the path-operator tensorization supplies exactly that missing component.

## 5. Independent proof audit in full

### 5.1 Bradley coefficients and the row-TV lemma

For `s<t`, write `g=t-s`, let `K_{s,t}(x,.)` be a regular conditional law of `X_t` given `X_s=x`, and let `mu_s,mu_t` be the one-time marginals. Fix a Borel set `B` and define

\[
h_B(x)=K_{s,t}(x,B)-\mu_t(B).
\]

For every Borel `D` with `mu_s(D)>0`, the event `{X_s in D}` is in the past sigma-field and `{X_t in B}` is in the future sigma-field separated by `g`. Hence

\[
\left|\frac1{\mu_s(D)}\int_D h_B\,d\mu_s\right|
\le \phi(g).
\]

If `h_B>phi(g)+eps` on a positive-measure set, choosing that set as `D` contradicts the displayed bound; similarly for `-h_B`. Therefore `|h_B|<=phi(g)` almost everywhere.

Because the state space in Bradley Section 3 is `R`, choose a countable algebra generating its Borel sigma-field. Intersect the corresponding full-measure sets over that algebra. For any fixed row `x` in the intersection, finite signed-measure continuity under monotone limits extends

\[
|K_{s,t}(x,B)-\mu_t(B)|\le\phi(g)
\]

from the algebra to all Borel `B`. Thus there is one full-measure set `A_{s,t}` on which

\[
\|K_{s,t}(x,.)-\mu_t\|_{TV}\le\phi(g),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|K_{s,t}(x,.)-K_{s,t}(x',.)\|_{TV}
\le2\phi(g)
}
\]

for all `x,x'` in `A_{s,t}`.

This rederivation confirms the common-null-set interface rather than merely inheriting it from the owner.

### 5.2 Arbitrary input laws and prefix robustness

Let `lambda` be any input law supported on `A_{s,t}` and sample

\[
U\sim\lambda,\qquad V\mid(U=x)\sim K_{s,t}(x,.).
\]

For every event `C` determined by `U`, the law of `V|C` and the unconditional law of `V` are convex mixtures of the same family of rows. Since that family has eventwise range at most `2phi(g)`, for every Borel `B`,

\[
|P(V\in B\mid C)-P(V\in B)|\le2\phi(g).
\]

Hence

\[
\phi(\sigma(U),\sigma(V))\le2\phi(g).
\]

Bradley's general sigma-field inequality

\[
\rho(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
\le2\sqrt{\phi(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\phi(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)}
\le2\sqrt{\phi(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho(U,V)\le2\sqrt{2\phi(g)}.
}
\]

If `lambda` itself is the conditional law of `X_s` given any earlier-history sigma-field, it is supported on `A_{s,t}` almost surely because `mu_s(A_{s,t}^c)=0`. Thus the same channel bound remains valid after arbitrary past-prefix conditioning. No reverse-phi assumption is used.

### 5.3 Alternating blocks and the minimal Peyre prefix

Take finite disjoint index sets `S,T` at mutual distance at least `n`. Order `S union T` in time and merge consecutive selected times of the same color into maximal monochromatic blocks. Let the S-blocks be `U_1,...,U_I` and the T-blocks be `V_1,...,V_J`, each indexed chronologically within its color.

For a pair `(i,j)`, Peyre's minimal-hypothesis sigma-field is

\[
\mathcal H_{ij}
=\sigma(U_{i'}:i'<i,\;V_{j'}:j'<j).
\]

If `U_i` and `V_j` are adjacent in the full chronological block order, every variable in `H_ij` is strictly earlier than the earlier of those two blocks. Let `s` be the final time in the earlier block and `t` the first time in the later block. Conditional on `H_ij`, the Markov chain factors

\[
U_i\longrightarrow X_s\longrightarrow X_t\longrightarrow V_j,
\]

with the same transition kernel `K_{s,t}` and a changed input law only. The preceding arbitrary-input lemma and maximal-correlation data processing yield subjective correlation at most

\[
\kappa(n)=\min\{1,2\sqrt{2\phi(n)}\}.
\]

If the two blocks are nonadjacent, the chronological path has an intervening block of the opposite color. That block has a smaller within-color index than the later target block, so it belongs to `H_ij`. Choose one state `X_r` inside it. Given `X_r`, the pre-`r` and post-`r` paths are independent by the Markov property. The other variables in `H_ij` condition separately on these two independent factors, so the remaining target blocks stay conditionally independent. Therefore the subjective correlation is exactly zero.

This checks both possible chronological color orientations; there is no hidden assumption that S starts first.

### 5.4 Peyre tensorization and the path norm

Peyre Theorem 3.3.1 bounds the maximal correlation between the two whole block-vectors by the operator norm of the matrix of subjective pairwise bounds. Peyre Section 3.4(a) states that for Theorems 3.3.1 and 3.3.10 it is enough to impose those pairwise decorrelation bounds relative to precisely the prefix sigma-fields generated by earlier X-blocks and earlier Y-blocks. Thus the conditioning used above matches the actual theorem, not a stronger invented variant.

The bound matrix is the bipartite incidence matrix of an alternating path with edge weights at most `kappa(n)`. Therefore

\[
\rho(\sigma(X_s:s\in S),\sigma(X_t:t\in T))
\le\min\{1,2\kappa(n)\}
\le\min\{1,4\sqrt{2\phi(n)}\}.
\]

This establishes the finite-interlacing theorem.

### 5.5 Arbitrary countable index sets

Every subset of `Z` is countable. For arbitrary admissible `S,T`, square-integrable functions measurable with respect to the full coordinate sigma-fields are approximated in `L^2` by conditional expectations onto increasing finite coordinate sub-sigma-fields. Covariances and variances converge under that approximation. Hence maximal correlation of the countable vectors is the supremum of the corresponding finite-subvector maximal correlations. The uniform finite bound passes to arbitrary `S,T`, yielding

\[
\rho^*(n)\le\min\{1,4\sqrt{2\phi(n)}\}.
\]

Taking `n->infinity` proves Bradley Question 5 affirmatively.

Equivalently, for the shortest source closure, choose `n_0` with `phi(n_0)<1/32`; then `rho*(n_0)<1`, and Bradley Theorem 3.3(6) gives exponential rho-star mixing.
