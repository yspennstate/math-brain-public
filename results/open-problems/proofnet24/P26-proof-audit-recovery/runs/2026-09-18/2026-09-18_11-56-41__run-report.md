## 2. Exact source scope and dated literature boundary

Canonical source:

Hossein Dabirian and Vijay Subramanian, *Connectivity of a family of bilateral preference random graphs*, Journal of Applied Probability 63(2) (2026), 552–579, published online 18 December 2025, DOI `10.1017/jpr.2025.10051`.

The source defines `LK(n,k)` by iid continuous edge weights on `K_n`: an edge is retained iff it is among the `k` preferred incident edges at **both** endpoints. In Section 1.1 it explicitly asks for the diameter in the **additional range of `k(n)` from Theorem 6 where connectivity holds**. Theorem 6 gives connectivity for

`k >= log n + t' sqrt(log n) log log n`, fixed `t' > 1/2`.

The nearest predecessor is La--Kabkab, *A New Random Graph Model with Self-Optimizing Nodes: Connectivity and Diameter* (Internet Mathematics 11 (2015)). Its Theorem 5.3 gives

`D(LK(n,k))/(log n/log log n) -> 1`

when `liminf k/log n > beta_*`, with `beta_* ~= 2.4626`. Thus the genuinely new source interval is the near-logarithmic band between the 2026 connected side and the older constant-factor regime.

A fresh bounded 2026-09-18 search using the exact 2026 title/authors, DOI, `LK(n,k) diameter`, and bilateral-preference diameter recovered the source, the 2015 predecessor, and classical Erdős--Rényi diameter literature, but no later primary source explicitly resolving the same near-threshold diameter problem. ## 4. Method #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER

I prove the same diameter law for every fixed **positive** surplus coefficient, not merely `t0>1/2`.

### Stronger audit theorem

Fix `eta>0` and `T>1`. Suppose

`log n + eta sqrt(log n) log log n <= k <= T log n`.

Then

`D(LK(n,k))/(log n/log log n) -> 1`

in probability.

This immediately implies the owner's source-scoped theorem by taking `eta=t0>1/2`.

### Step 1: exact threshold sandwich

Apply the monotone transform from the source's lowest Uniform weights to iid `Exp(1)` scores, with larger score preferred. Let `U_v` be the `k`th largest score among the `n-1` edges incident to `v`, and put

`L_+ = log((n-1)/k)+2`,
`L_- = log((n-1)/k)-3/2`.

For fixed `v`, the number of incident scores above `L_+` is binomial with mean `e^{-2}k`. The Chernoff exponent for having at least `k` such scores is

`k(2-1+e^{-2}) = (1+e^{-2})k`.

Since `k>=log n+omega(1)`, a union bound over all vertices gives

`max_v U_v <= L_+`

with probability `1-o(1)`. Similarly the number above `L_-` has mean `e^{3/2}k`, and the lower-tail Chernoff exponent at `k` is

`(e^{3/2}-1-3/2)k > 1.9 k`,

so

`min_v U_v >= L_-`

with probability `1-o(1)`.

Define independent threshold graphs from the same scores:

`G_+={vw:V_vw>L_+}`, `p_+=e^{-2}k/(n-1)`,

`G_-={vw:V_vw>L_-}`, `p_-=e^{3/2}k/(n-1)`.

On the threshold event,

`G_+ subset LK(n,k) subset G_-`.                                      (4.1)

Both have `np_±=Theta(log n)`, hence the Chung--Lu theorem gives

`diam(G_±)=(1+o_P(1)) log n/log log n`.                              (4.2)

The only issue is that `G_+` is generally disconnected near the lower edge of the band, so it cannot itself be used as a spanning connected subgraph.

### Step 2: pair-specific deleted cores

Let

`h_n=sqrt(log n) log log n`, `theta=eta/8`,
`delta=theta h_n/k`, `m=ceil(1/delta)`.

Then `delta->0`, `m` is polylogarithmic, and a balanced deterministic partition into blocks `B_1,...,B_m` has every block of size at most `delta n+1`.

For every unordered block pair `i,j`, delete `B_i union B_j`, call the remaining vertex set `W_ij`, and let `H_ij` be `G_+` induced on `W_ij`. Write `C_ij` for a largest component of `H_ij`.

Uniformly in `i,j`, `H_ij~G(N_ij,p_+)` with

`N_ij >= n(1-2delta)-2`,
`N_ij p_+ >= c_0 log n`

for some fixed `c_0>0` and all large `n`.

A standard component count is enough for the precise core size needed here. If an `r`-set is a component, it contains one of `r^{r-2}` spanning trees and has no cut edge, so

`P(exists an r-component)`
` <= C(N,r) r^{r-2} p_+^{r-1}(1-p_+)^{r(N-r)}`.                    (4.3)

Choose fixed `R` sufficiently large. Using `C(N,r)<=(eN/r)^r` and `lambda=Np_+>=c_0 log n`, the sum of (4.3) over `R<=r<=N/2` is polynomially small enough to union over all `O(m^2)` block pairs. For the finitely many `r<R`, multiplying (4.3) by `r` and summing gives

`E Z_{<R} <= n^{1-c_1}`

for a fixed `c_1>0`. Hence Markov plus another `O(m^2)` union bound gives, simultaneously for every block pair,

`|C_ij| >= N_ij - n/k^3`                                           (4.4)

with probability `1-o(1)`.

### Step 3: leave-one-out safe targets

Put

`q=log((n-1)/k)+delta`, `p_0=e^{-delta}k/(n-1)`.

Let `B` be the set of vertices having at least `k` incident scores greater than `q`. For a fixed vertex, the corresponding binomial mean is `e^{-delta}k`; therefore

`P(w in B) <= exp[-k I(delta)]`,
`I(x)=x-1+e^{-x} >= x^2/3`

for small positive `x`. Since

`delta^2 k = theta^2 h_n^2/k >= (theta^2/T)(log log n)^2`,

setting

`b_n=exp[-theta^2(log log n)^2/(6T)]`

gives

`P(|B|>n b_n) <= b_n ->0`,                                        (4.5)

and also `b_n=o(delta)` and `k b_n->0`.

For endpoint `v`, define the leave-one-out bad-target set

`B_v={w!=v: the kth largest score at w after deleting edge vw exceeds q}`.

Then deterministically `B_v subset B`. More importantly, `B_v` is measurable without any edge incident to `v`.

Fix `v in B_i` and a block index `j`. Condition on all non-`v` edges. Then `C_ij` and `B_v` are fixed, while the incident scores `(V_vw)_{w!=v}` remain iid exponentials. On (4.4)-(4.5), set

`A=C_ij\B_v`.

Its relative size obeys

`|A|/(n-1) >= 1-2delta-o(delta)`.                                  (4.6)

Let `R_v={w:V_vw>q}` and let `S_v` be the labels of the `k` largest incident scores of `v`. If `|R_v|<=k`, every q-high edge is proposed by `v`; if `|R_v|>k`, every member of `S_v` is q-high. Any `w in A` reached in either case also proposes `vw`, by the definition of `B_v`. Therefore

`P(v has no LK-neighbor in A | non-v edges)`
` <= (1-p_0)^{|A|} + C(n-1-|A|,k)/C(n-1,k)`
` <= exp(-p_0|A|) + (1-|A|/(n-1))^k`.                              (4.7)

Using `e^{-delta}>=1-delta` and (4.6),

`p_0|A| >= k(1-delta)(1-2delta-o(delta))`
`          >= k-3delta k-o(h_n)`
`          >= log n +(5eta/8-o(1))h_n`.                            (4.8)

The second term of (4.7) is at most `(3delta)^k` for all large `n`. There are at most `nm` endpoint/core requirements. Hence

`nm[exp(-p_0|A|)+(3delta)^k]
 <= m exp[-(5eta/8-o(1))h_n] + nm(3delta)^k ->0`.                  (4.9)

Thus, with probability `1-o(1)`, **every** vertex in block `B_i` has an `LK` neighbor in every relevant core `C_ij`.

### Step 4: diameter squeeze

Take arbitrary `u in B_i`, `v in B_j`. By the uniform attachment statement, `u` and `v` each have an `LK` edge into the same connected core `C_ij subset G_+`. Therefore

`d_LK(u,v) <= diam(G_+)+2`.

This holds for every pair, so it simultaneously proves `LK` connected on the high-probability event. Since `LK subset G_-`, the supergraph `G_-` is then connected and

`diam(G_-) <= diam(LK) <= diam(G_+)+2`.                             (4.10)

Combine (4.2) and (4.10), divide by `log n/log log n`, and the stronger audit theorem follows.

**Outcome #3:** the owner theorem is not merely reproduced; the same proof architecture survives the stronger assumption `eta>0`. I do not count this strengthening separately or claim publication novelty for it.

## 5. Method #20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS

The stronger derivation also pinpoints the exact obstruction at zero surplus. The one-edge attachment argument needs a positive reserve above the `n^{-1}` per-vertex failure scale. In (4.8) the exponent reserve is proportional to

`k-log n - 3 delta k`.

For fixed `eta>0`, choosing `delta=(eta/8)h_n/k` leaves the positive margin `(5eta/8)h_n`, and the union over all endpoints vanishes. At `k=log n` with no positive surplus, this margin disappears. One cannot choose a positive block-deletion scale and still keep `p_0|A|` above `log n` by this one-round argument; the first term of (4.7) is then only of order `1/n` per endpoint, so a union over `n` endpoints does not go to zero.

This is a proof-method obstruction, **not** a counterexample at `k=log n`. It says exactly what new ingredient would be needed to enter the critical window: a multi-round attachment/correlation argument, a sharper target-capacity mechanism, or a direct treatment of the exceptional endpoint set.

**Outcome #20:** the audited theorem's positive source-scale surplus is doing real work. No hidden claim about the unresolved critical boundary is being smuggled into the proof.
