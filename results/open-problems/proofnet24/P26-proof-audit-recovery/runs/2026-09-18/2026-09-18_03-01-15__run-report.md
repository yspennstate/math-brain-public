## 3. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I first tried to break the source-match theorem at the three places where an apparently strong joint CLT can fail: dependence, moving singular constraints, and accumulation in infinitely many coordinates.

### 3.1 Coordinate independence is false

At the first nontrivial joint state `n=4`, exact recursion gives

\[
(N_4(2),N_4(3),N_4(4))=
\begin{cases}
(1,1,1),&8/11,\\
(2,0,1),&3/11,
\end{cases}
\]

and hence

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(N_4(2),N_4(3))=-\frac{24}{121}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus no proof may use independent clade-size coordinates. The P13 proof does not: its covariance forcing is generated by the root split and propagated through the harmonic-descent Green kernel.

### 3.2 A nonsingular moving Gaussian is impossible

Every full binary `n`-leaf tree satisfies pathwise

\[
N_n(n)=1,
\qquad
\sum_{r=2}^{n}N_n(r)=n-1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For

\[
Z_n(r)=\frac{N_n(r)-E N_n(r)}{\sqrt n},
\]

this becomes

\[
Z_n(n)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{r=2}^{n-1}Z_n(r)=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

So a full-dimensional nonsingular Gaussian approximation is false. The frozen theorem correctly uses the singular Gaussian `Gamma_n=N(0,C_n)` with the exact finite covariance `C_n`; the two deterministic vectors in (3.3) lie in `ker C_n`, so the comparison Gaussian obeys the same constraints almost surely.

## 4. Exact finite joint law and covariance recursion — PASS

F_n(\mathbf z)=E\prod_{r=2}^n z_r^{N_n(r)},\qquad F_1=1.

F_n(\mathbf z)
=z_n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q(n,i)F_iF_{n-i}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the complete finite-`n` joint distribution is recursively determined.

Write

\[
\mu_r(n)=EN_n(r),
\qquad
g_r(n,i)=\mu_r(i)+\mu_r(n-i),

\[
C_{rs}(n)=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)),
\]

and

\[
\eta_{rs}(n)=\frac1n
\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q_n}
(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Total covariance, subtree independence, symmetry of `q`, and

\[
\frac{2i}{n}q(n,i)=\frac1{h_{n-1}(n-i)}

C_{rs}(n)=
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{C_{rs}(i)}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}+\eta_{rs}(n).

## 6. Uniform mean remainder and covariance kernel — PASS

Let

\[
a(r)=\frac{6h_{r-1}}{\pi^2(r-1)},
\qquad
\rho_r=\frac{a(r)}r,
\qquad
e_r(j)=\mu_r(j)-\rho_rj.
\]

Applying the explicit dyadic bound (5.1) at `2j,4j,...` gives, uniformly in `r,j`,

\[
|e_r(j)|\le D:=\frac2{\log2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `j<r`, this is immediate from `mu_r(j)=0` and `a(r)<=1`; for `j>=r`, monotonicity also gives `e_r(j)>=0`.

Since the linear part `rho_r n` is deterministic under a root split,

\[
|\eta_{rs}(n)|\le\frac{16}{(\log2)^2}\frac1n.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Define

\[
\Sigma_{rs}=\sum_{j=2}^\infty a(j)\eta_{rs}(j).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The series is absolutely convergent because `a(j)=O(log j/j)` and (6.2). Put

\[
d=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},
\qquad
\alpha=1-d=d^2=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.
\]

The owner proof combines the long-time bound

\[
0\le a(n,j)-a(j)\le A\frac jn
\]

with the short-time estimate to split the Green sum into:

1. `j<=n^d`, contributing `O(n^(d-1))=O(n^-alpha)`;
2. `j>n^d` and `n-j>=j^d`, contributing `O(sum j^(-1-d))=O(n^-d^2)`;
3. `n-j<j^d`, containing only `O(n^d)` terms with `j` of order `n`, hence `O(n^(d-1))`;
4. the limiting tail `j>n`, contributing `O(log n/n)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
sup_{r,s\ge2}|C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}|=O(n^{-\alpha}).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

I checked the moving-coordinate quantifiers in all three regions; no fixed-`r,s` constant is smuggled into (6.4).

## 7. Method #11 — dualize to the covariance operator; trace and Hilbert tail — PASS

The strongest part of the package becomes clearer on the operator side.

First,

\[
\sum_{r=2}^\infty\rho_r=1
\tag{7.1}
\]

because

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{h_k}{k(k+1)}
=\sum_{l\ge1}\frac1l\sum_{k\ge l}\frac1{k(k+1)}
=\sum_{l\ge1}\frac1{l^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]

For a size-`s` tree, the exact identity `sum_{r=2}^s N_s(r)=s-1`, positivity of `e_r(s)` for `r<=s`, and the uniform ceiling (6.1) give

\[
\sum_{r\le s}e_r(s)^2=O(1+\log s).
\]

For `r>s`, `e_r(s)=-s\rho_r`, and the square tail is also `O(1+log s)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{\|e(s)\|_{\ell^2}^2=O(1+\log(s+1)).}
\tag{7.2}
\]

If `I` is the root split and `U_j=e(I)+e(j-I)`, then

\[
T_j:=\sum_r\eta_{rr}(j)
=\frac1jE\|U_j-EU_j\|_2^2
\le C\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since the diagonal terms are nonnegative, Tonelli gives

\[
tr\,\Sigma
=\sum_j a(j)T_j<\infty.
\tag{7.4}
\]

So the kernel is a positive trace-class covariance operator on `ell^2`; this is the useful dual language for the moving-vector theorem.

For the tail projection `Q_R`, exact Green plus (7.3) gives

\[
E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
\le C\sum_{j=R+1}^n a(n,j)\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Repeating the same long/short/near-diagonal split, now with the extra logarithmic factor, yields

\[
\boxed{
sup_nE\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
=O(R^{-\alpha}\log R)\to0.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This is exactly the compact-containment estimate that product topology lacks.

Fixed-dimensional convergence follows from scalar Cramer--Wold contractions. For any finitely supported, and in fact any `ell^1`, weight vector `t`, the additive statistic

\[
X_n(t)=\sum_rt_rN_n(r)
\]

has mean error `O_t(1)`, variance error `O_t(n^(1-alpha))`, and deterministic root toll `t_n`, so current BCM Proposition 1.3 applies whenever the limiting variance is positive; if it is zero, normalized convergence to zero follows in `L^2`.

Finite-dimensional convergence plus (7.6) proves

\[
Z_n\Rightarrow G\quad\text{in }\ell^2,
\tag{7.7}
\]

where `G` is centered Gaussian with covariance `Sigma`.

Finally, if `Gamma_n=N(0,C_n)`, the Gaussian sequence has the identical tail second moment in (7.6) and matching finite-dimensional covariance limits. Hence `Gamma_n=>G` in `ell^2`. Since bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence on a separable metric space,

\[
d_{BL}^{\ell^2}(Law(Z_n),\Gamma_n)
\le d_{BL}(Law(Z_n),G)+d_{BL}(Gamma_n,G)
\to0.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The exact singular constraints from (3.3) are covariance-null directions and therefore hold under `Gamma_n` as well. This closes the full moving-vector obstruction rather than hiding it.
