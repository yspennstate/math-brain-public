## 3. Exact theorem being audited

Let `p ≡ 5 (mod 24)` be prime. Let

\[
V=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}
\]

be the two-localized Paley vertex set, and let the anharmonic group

\[
G=S_3=\langle x\mapsto 1-x,\ x\mapsto x^{-1}\rangle
\]

act on `V`. For this congruence class the action is free, so if `y_1,...,y_q` are orbit representatives then

\[
q=\frac{|V|}{6}=\frac{p-5}{24}.
\]

For each nontrivial irreducible unitary representation

\[
\rho\in\{\mathrm{sgn},\mathrm{std}\}
\]

of dimensions 1 and 2 respectively, let `A^rho` denote the multiplicity block of the adjacency operator of the localized Paley graph in the `rho`-isotypic sector. Claim 12 states

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\max}(A^\rho)\le \frac{\sqrt p-1}{2}.}
\]

Equivalently, for the baseline matrix `B_0=I+A`,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\max}(B_0^\rho)\le \frac{\sqrt p+1}{2}.}
\]

This is a structural spectral theorem for the nontrivial `S_3` sectors. It is not the source Conjecture 27 and it does not close I0101.

## 4. Method #2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

I first looked for literal-scope or normalization counterexamples rather than trying to reproduce the owner proof.

### 4.1 Small-prime boundary

At `p=5`, the localized set has

\[
|V|=\frac{p-5}{4}=0.
\]

There are no nontrivial multiplicity blocks, so the theorem is vacuous. This is not the empty-graph theta-convention issue that appears in some other packages: Claim 12 only asserts a bound on blocks that do not exist in the zero-dimensional case. The first nonvacuous prime in this residue class is `p=29`, where `q=1`.

### 4.2 Freeness cannot hide an exceptional orbit

The nonidentity stabilizers of the anharmonic `S_3` action are of orders two or three.

The three involution fixed points are

\[
\frac12,\quad -1,\quad 2.
\]

Membership in the localized set would require the relevant factor `2` to be a quadratic residue. But `p≡5 (mod 8)` implies

\[
\left(\frac2p\right)=-1,
\]

so these fixed points are excluded from `V`.

Order-three fixed points satisfy

\[
x^2-x+1=0.
\]

For `p≡5 (mod 24)` one has `p≡2 (mod 3)`, so this equation has no roots in `\mathbb F_p`. Hence the action on `V` is free.

The two generators preserve the localized Paley graph: `x\mapsto1-x` changes a difference by a sign, harmless because `\chi(-1)=1`, while inversion sends

\[
x^{-1}-y^{-1}=\frac{y-x}{xy},
\]

and `\chi(xy)=1` on `V`. Thus the group action used in the representation decomposition is genuine graph symmetry.

### 4.3 Correlation identity and factor-of-six check

For distinct `a,b\in\mathbb F_p`, the complete quadratic-character correlation is

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x-a)\chi(x-b)=-1,
\]

whereas for `a=b` it is `p-1`. Equivalently,

\[
C(a,b):=\sum_x\chi(x-a)\chi(x-b)
=-1+p\,\mathbf 1_{a=b}.
\]

This exact `-1+p\delta` form is the place where a missing factor, omitted zero, or incorrect diagonal convention would break the theorem. The calculation below keeps it explicit and produces the coefficient `6p`, not `6(p-1)` or `p`.

No boundary, stabilizer, sign, or factor-of-six counterexample survived.

## 5. Independent full proof

Fix a nontrivial unitary irrep `rho` of `S_3`, of dimension `d`. Since `rho` is nontrivial irreducible,

\[
\sum_{g\in G}\rho(g)=0.
\]

Choose orbit representatives `y_1,...,y_q`. Define the matrix-valued feature

\[
F_j(x)=\sum_{g\in G}\chi(x-g y_j)\rho(g).
\]

For indices `i,j`, expand the full-field Gram block:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}F_i(x)^*F_j(x)
&=\sum_{g,h\in G}\rho(g)^*\rho(h)
   \sum_x\chi(x-g y_i)\chi(x-h y_j)\\
&=\sum_{g,h}\rho(g)^*\rho(h)
   \bigl(-1+p\,\mathbf1_{g y_i=h y_j}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The `-1` contribution is

\[
-\left(\sum_g\rho(g)\right)^*
 \left(\sum_h\rho(h)\right)=0.
\]

If `i\ne j`, the two orbits are disjoint, so the delta term is absent and the entire block is zero. If `i=j`, freeness gives `g y_i=h y_i` if and only if `g=h`, and therefore

\[
\sum_xF_i(x)^*F_i(x)
=p\sum_{g\in G}\rho(g)^*\rho(g)
=6pI_d.
\]

Thus the complete block identity is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}F_i(x)^*F_j(x)
=6p\,\delta_{ij}I_d.
}
\]

Now let `v=(v_1,...,v_q)` with `v_j\in\mathbb C^d`, and put

\[
(\mathcal Fv)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^qF_j(x)v_j.
\]

The preceding Gram identity gives the exact Parseval relation

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\|(\mathcal Fv)(x)\|^2
=6p\sum_j\|v_j\|^2.
}
\]

The feature is equivariant under the `S_3` action (up to the harmless convention of left versus right multiplication by `rho`; either convention is unitary). Hence on each six-point orbit in `V`, all six squared norms agree. Define the quotient block operator `R` by

\[
(Rv)_i=(\mathcal Fv)(y_i).
\]

Restriction from all of `\mathbb F_p` to `V` can only decrease squared norm, so

\[
6\|Rv\|^2
=\sum_{x\in V}\|(\mathcal Fv)(x)\|^2
\le
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\|(\mathcal Fv)(x)\|^2
=6p\|v\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{R^*R\preceq pI},
\qquad
\boxed{\|R\|\le\sqrt p}.
\]

It remains to identify `R` with the adjacency multiplicity block. Its matrix blocks are

\[
R_{ij}=\sum_{g\in G}\chi(y_i-g y_j)\rho(g).
\]

For `i\ne j`, all points are distinct and

\[
\chi(y_i-g y_j)=2A(y_i,g y_j)-1.
\]

The constant `-1` vanishes after summing against `rho` because `\sum_g\rho(g)=0`, so `R_{ij}=2A^\rho_{ij}`.

For `i=j`, only `g=e` is a coincident pair. At that term `\chi(0)=0`, whereas the formal expression `2A(y_i,y_i)-1` equals `-1`; this contributes the missing `+I_d`. Hence, on the full multiplicity space,

\[
\boxed{R=2A^\rho+I},
\qquad
\boxed{A^\rho=\frac12(R-I)}.
\]

Because the graph adjacency operator is self-adjoint, `A^rho` and therefore `R` are Hermitian. The contraction bound gives every eigenvalue `mu` of `R` in `[-\sqrt p,\sqrt p]`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(A^\rho)
\subseteq
\left[-\frac{\sqrt p+1}{2},\frac{\sqrt p-1}{2}\right].
}
\]

In particular,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(A^\rho)\le\frac{\sqrt p-1}{2},
\]

which is exactly the owner claim, and

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{spec}(B_0^\rho)
\subseteq
\left[\frac{1-\sqrt p}{2},\frac{1+\sqrt p}{2}\right].
}
\]

This is a complete independent proof.

## 6. Method #11 — DUALIZE

The proof is more transparent as an analysis/synthesis operator statement. After normalization,

\[
\frac{1}{\sqrt{6p}}\mathcal F
\]

is an exact isometry from the nontrivial orbit-multiplicity coefficient space into `L^2(\mathbb F_p;\mathbb C^d)`. Passing to the localized set is composition with an orthogonal coordinate projection. Its quotient by the six-point group orbits is the operator `R/\sqrt p`. Thus the spectral theorem is simply the dual statement that compression of an isometry is a contraction.

This yields the stronger operator inequality

\[
\boxed{R^*R\preceq pI}
\]

and therefore the two-sided spectral intervals displayed above, rather than only the one-sided upper edge stated by the owner. I record this strengthening only as audit diagnosis. It is not a separate package, earns no extra metric point, and carries no novelty claim.

The dual viewpoint also identifies precisely why the proof is robust: all arithmetic is confined to the exact full-field Gram identity, while localization enters only as an orthogonal projection. There is no probabilistic approximation and no asymptotic loss in Claim 12 itself.
