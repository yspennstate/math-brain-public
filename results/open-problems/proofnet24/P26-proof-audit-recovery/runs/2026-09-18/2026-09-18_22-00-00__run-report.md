## 4. Owner theorem to be audited

Let `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)` be prime, write `s=sqrt(p)`, and let

\[
V=\{x\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\},

\]

Let the full Paley conference matrix be

\[
C_{xy}=\begin{cases}
\chi(x-y),&x\ne y,\\
0,&x=y,
\end{cases}
\]

so that, because `p ≡ 1 mod 4`,

\[
C\mathbf1=0,
\qquad
C^2=pI-J.

The standard strict Paley Gram is

\[
Q=\frac{p}{p-1}\left(I-\frac1pJ-\frac1sC\right).

Its diagonal is one; for distinct vertices its correlation is

\[
-\frac1{s-1}

on Paley edges and

\[
\frac1{s+1}

### 5.1 Two-anchor Schur algebra

Write

\[
r=-\frac1{s-1}.
\]

The two-anchor Gram is

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\r&1\end{pmatrix},

and every localized vertex has anchor cross-row `r(1,1)`. Therefore the Schur complement subtracts from every localized entry

\[
h=r^2(1,1)A^{-1}(1,1)^T
=\frac{2}{(s-1)(s-2)}.

Its diagonal is

\[
d=1-h
=\frac{s(s-3)}{(s-1)(s-2)}.

\[
q_{\rm edge}

## 6. #11 DUALIZE — exact rank-one downdate threshold

The owner proof uses the all-ones vector only as a necessary PSD test. Dualizing the rank-one subtraction gives an exact characterization and strengthens the diagnosis.

### Lemma: exact PSD rank-one downdate

Let `Q` be any finite real symmetric PSD matrix and `u` any vector. Define

\[
\delta_{\max}(Q,u)
:=\sup\{\delta\ge0:Q-\delta uu^T\succeq0\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\max}(Q,u)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u\notin\operatorname{Ran}(Q),\\[1mm]
\bigl(u^TQ^\dagger u\bigr)^{-1},&u\in\operatorname{Ran}(Q),
\end{cases}}
\]

where `Q^dagger` is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.

**Proof.** If `u` has a nonzero component in `ker Q`, choose `z in ker Q` with `u^Tz != 0`; then for every `delta>0`,

\[
z^T(Q-\delta uu^T)z=-\delta(u^Tz)^2<0.
\]

If instead `u in Ran(Q)`, work on the support of `Q`. Writing `v=Q^{\dagger/2}u`,

\[
Q-\delta uu^T
=Q^{1/2}(I-\delta vv^T)Q^{1/2}
\]

on that support. The middle factor is PSD exactly when

\[
\delta\|v\|^2\le1,
\]

which gives the formula. `QED`.

Apply the lemma with

\[
Q=Q_p^{(2)},\qquad u=\mathbf1.
\]

If `1` is not in the range, **no positive common-mode subtraction is possible at all**. If it is in the range, Cauchy–Schwarz in the positive eigenspaces gives

\[
(\mathbf1^TQ\mathbf1)
(\mathbf1^TQ^\dagger\mathbf1)
\ge
(\mathbf1^T\mathbf1)^2
=m^2.
\]

Therefore in every case

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\max}(Q_p^{(2)},\mathbf1)
\le
\frac{\mathbf1^TQ_p^{(2)}\mathbf1}{m^2}.
}
\]

Combining with the independently reconstructed character-sum estimate yields

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\max}(Q_p^{(2)},\mathbf1)
\le
\frac{3(s-1)^2}{(s-3)(s+1)(s^2-5)}
<
\delta_*
}
\]

for every `s>=11`.

This is a strict strengthening of the route diagnosis: the owner all-ones test is the **Rayleigh relaxation of the exact pseudoinverse threshold**. Even the exact optimal uniform rank-one downdate is too small to reach the source cap.

There is also a useful algebraic rewrite:

\[
Q_p^{(2)}
=
\frac{s-2}{(s+1)(s-3)}\bigl((s-1)I-2A\bigr)
+
\frac{s-5}{(s+1)(s-3)}J,
\]

where `A` is the localized adjacency matrix. Uniform deflation changes only the coefficient of `J`; it does not diagonalize or otherwise simplify the irregular localized adjacency term. This explains why an exact one-dimensional common-mode optimization cannot repair the lost circulant symmetry.

I record this exact pseudoinverse characterization only as an independent audit strengthening. It earns **no additional P26 metric point** and carries no novelty claim; rank-one PSD downdates are classical linear algebra.
