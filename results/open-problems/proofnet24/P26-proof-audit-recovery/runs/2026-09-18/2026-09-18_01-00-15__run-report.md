## 3. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I first tried to invalidate the theorem at the points where a computer-assisted continuum proof can silently lose part of its domain.

### 3.1 Normalization and the range of rho

Every row of `A` has average entry one, hence the global minimum is at most one and `rho>=0`. Entrywise nonnegativity gives `rho<=1`. Thus the claimed radial parameter really lies in `[0,1]`; no hidden sign convention is being used.

Every such matrix has the unique parametrization

\[
A(a,b,c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-a-b&1+a&1+b\\
1+a&1-a-c&1+c\\
1+b&1+c&1-b-c
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The nonnegative domain is exactly

\[
a,b,c\ge-1,\qquad a+b,a+c,b+c\le1.
\]

Moreover the nine quantities `1-A_ij` take exactly the six values

\[
-a,-b,-c,a+b,a+c,b+c,
\]

so

\[
\rho=\max\{-a,-b,-c,a+b,a+c,b+c\}.
\]

For a nonzero admissible point this maximum is strictly positive. Dividing `(a,b,c)` by `rho` preserves all homogeneous directions and puts at least one of the six inequalities at equality, so every nonconstant admissible matrix lies on a ray from `J` through a boundary face. There is therefore no missing interior region in the radial reduction.

The exact polytope vertices are

\[
L=(-1,-1,-1),\quad U=(1/2,1/2,1/2),\quad
V_1=(2,-1,-1),\ V_2=(-1,2,-1),\ V_3=(-1,-1,2).
\]

Block-label permutations reduce the six faces to two representative triangles, with charts

\[
f_-(u,v)=(-1+3u,-1+3v,-1),
\]

\[
f_+(u,v)=\frac12(1+3u-3v,\ 1-3u+3v,\ 1-3u-3v),
\]

for `u,v>=0`, `u+v<=1`. Setting `u=s`, `v=(1-s)t` covers the whole closed triangle, including the degenerate edge at `s=1`.

### 3.2 Graph orientation and the 3^10 normalization

For colors `i,j,k` define

\[
D_{ijk}=\sum_{w=0}^2A_{wi}A_{wj}A_{wk}.
\]

Summing the five right-side colors independently gives

\[

\sum_{z\in\{0,1,2\}^5}
\prod_{i=0}^4D_{z_i,z_{i+1},z_{i+2}}-3^{10}
=3^{10}(t_H(A)-1).

The subtraction is `3^10`, not `3^5`: the five triple contractions have already summed the five right-side colors. ## 5. Equality, L2 stability, and scaling checked on the page

If `rho>0`, the certified gap gives `t_H(A)>1`. If `rho=0`, then every entry of `A` is at least one while each row average is one, so every entry equals one and `A=J`. This proves the equality statement.

For the `L^2` bound, write `B=A-J` and for `rho>0` set

\[
A_*=J+B/\rho,\qquad P_*=A_*/3.
\]

The radial construction makes `A_*` nonnegative, symmetric and row-sum three, so `P_*` is symmetric stochastic. Jensen row by row yields `||P_*v||_2<=||v||_2`; hence every eigenvalue lies in `[-1,1]`. On the mean-zero subspace `J/3` vanishes, so the two eigenvalues of `B/3` have absolute value at most `rho`. Therefore

\[
\|A-1\|_{L^2}^2
=\frac19\operatorname{Tr}(B^2)
\le2\rho^2.
\]

Hence `rho^4 >= ||A-1||_{L^2}^4/4`, and the certified `rho^4/5` gap gives the stated `1/20` coefficient.

For a nonnegative equal-three-block kernel `W` whose row sums are `3d`, if `d>0` then `A=W/d` is in the normalized class and fifteen-edge homogeneity gives `t_H(W)=d^15 t_H(A)`. If `d=0`, nonnegativity and zero row sums force `W=0`. The tensor-product consequence also follows directly from Fubini. None of these operations enlarges the theorem to arbitrary graphons.
