## 3. Method #2 — attack the negation first

I attacked four possible failure points before accepting the owner theorem.

### 3.1 Could the alleged infinite-order cyclic subalgebra fail?

Fix a nontrivial free word `w in F_2`, let `a=a_e` be the identity-site lamp involution, and put

\[
g=w^{-1}a.
\]

The quotient map `G -> F_2` sends `g` to `w^{-1}`. A nontrivial element of a free group has infinite order. If `g^m=e`, its quotient would give `w^{-m}=e`, hence `m=0`. Thus `g` has infinite order and

\[
C_r^*(\langle g\rangle)\cong C(\mathbb T),
\qquad \sigma(u_g)=\mathbb T.
\]

The spectral-circle premise used by P81 is therefore sound.

### 3.2 Could the matrix completion become singular exactly where the scalar polar fails?

For `c in C`, set

\[
q_c=\frac{1+c u_g}{1+|c|}.
\]

Because the spectrum of `u_g` is the full unit circle,

\[
\|1+c u_g\|=1+|c|,
\]

so `q_c` is a normal contraction. Let

\[
D_c=(1-q_c^*q_c)^{1/2}.
\]

Normality gives `q_c q_c^*=q_c^*q_c`, and functional calculus gives

\[
[D_c,q_c]=[D_c,q_c^*]=0.
\]

Define

\[
J_c=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_c&D_c\\
D_c&-q_c^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then direct block multiplication yields

\[
J_cJ_c^*=J_c^*J_c=I_2.
\]

At the scalar wall `|c|=1`, the function `1+cz` does vanish for one `z in T`, so the scalar phase is genuinely undefined there. But at precisely that spectral point `q_c=0` and `D_c=1`, so the Julia matrix is maximally nonsingular rather than singular. The candidate counterexample to P81A therefore fails for the exact reason the owner claims: the defect channel fills the scalar zero.

### 3.3 Could complex `c` reverse the winding bookkeeping?

Off the wall,

\[
\psi_c=(1+c u_g)|1+c u_g|^{-1}
\]

belongs to `C^*(u_g) ~= C(T)`. The loop `z -> 1+cz` is the Euclidean circle centered at `1` with radius `|c|`, traversed once counterclockwise as `z` runs counterclockwise, regardless of the phase of nonzero `c`. Hence its winding about zero is exactly

\[
\operatorname{wind}(1+cz,0)=
\begin{cases}
0,&|c|<1,\\
1,&|c|>1.
\end{cases}
\]

No hidden sign reversal occurs for complex `c`.

### 3.4 Could stabilization with extra trivial channels evade P81C?

No. If `[u_w] != 0`, the subcritical scalar endpoint has K1 class `[u_w]` while the supercritical scalar endpoint has class `0`. Adding identity blocks changes neither class. Any unitary homotopy in a matrix algebra preserves K1. Therefore no stabilization by K1-trivial channels can connect those fixed scalar endpoints. P81C's no-go is exactly scoped.

If the added channel is **not** required to stay K1-trivial, the statement becomes false; the Julia continuation is itself the counterexample. This distinction is essential and is preserved in the audit verdict.

## 4. Independent full proof of P81A

With `q_c,D_c,J_c` as above, unitarity is already established. Norm continuity also holds: `c -> q_c` is norm continuous, `q_c^*q_c` is norm continuous, and positive square root is norm continuous on positive contractions.

To compute the K1 class, for `0<=s<=1` consider the Julia operator built from `s q_c`:

\[
J(sq_c)=
\begin{pmatrix}
sq_c&(1-s^2q_cq_c^*)^{1/2}\\
(1-s^2q_c^*q_c)^{1/2}&-s q_c^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here normality makes the two defect operators equal. This is a continuous path of unitaries from `J_c` to

\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The flip is null-homotopic. Writing

\[
p_-=(1-F)/2,
\]

we have `p_-^2=p_-` and

\[
t\mapsto e^{i\pi t p_-}
\]

joins the identity to `F`. Therefore

\[
[J_c]=0\in K_1(A).
\]

Now set

\[
\mathcal W_c=\operatorname{diag}(u_w,1)J_c.
\]

Multiplicativity/additivity in K1 gives

\[
\boxed{[\mathcal W_c]=[u_w]}
\]

for every `c in C`, including the entire scalar singular circle `|c|=1`.

That proves P81A end to end.

## 5. Method #11 — dualize/change category to the spectral circle

The owner proof above contracts `J_c` directly. I wanted a genuinely independent route rather than a receipt, so I moved the claim into the commutative cyclic subalgebra

\[
C^*(u_g)\cong C(\mathbb T).
\]

There `q_c` and `D_c` are scalar continuous functions of `z in T`, and

\[
J_c(z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_c(z)&D_c(z)\\
D_c(z)&-\overline{q_c(z)}
\end{pmatrix}
\in U(2).
\]

Because

\[
|q_c(z)|^2+D_c(z)^2=1,
\]

its determinant is the constant

\[
\boxed{\det J_c(z)=-1.}
\]

For a `U(2)`-valued loop, the K1 class in `K_1(C(T)) ~= Z` is the winding number of its determinant. The determinant is constant, so its winding is zero. Thus

\[
[J_c]=0\in K_1(C^*(u_g)),
\]

and functoriality under the inclusion `C^*(u_g) -> A` gives `[J_c]=0` in `K_1(A)`.

This spectral-circle/determinant proof is independent of the owner's explicit contraction and is the main #11 category change of this audit. It also makes the wall mechanism transparent: the `2 x 2` loop has constant determinant even while one scalar diagonalized channel changes winding.

## 6. Independent full proof of P81B — charge transfer

Assume `|c| != 1`. Then `1+c u_g` is invertible and

\[
q_c=\psi_c|q_c|,
\qquad
\psi_c=(1+c u_g)|1+c u_g|^{-1}.
\]

Since `q_c` is normal, `psi_c` commutes with `|q_c|`. For `0<=t<=1`, define

\[
q_{c,t}=\psi_c\big((1-t)|q_c|+tI\big).
\]

The positive factor remains invertible and bounded by one, so `q_{c,t}` is an invertible normal contraction. Its Julia matrices give a homotopy

\[
J_c\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
\psi_c&0\\
0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal W_c
\sim
\begin{pmatrix}
u_w\psi_c&0\\
0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
U_c&0\\
0&-\psi_c^*
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where `U_c=u_w psi_c` is the P80 scalar polar.

If `|c|<1`, the loop `1+cz` has winding zero, so

\[
[\psi_c]=0,
\qquad
[U_c]=[u_w],
\qquad
[-\psi_c^*]=0.
\]

If `|c|>1`, the loop has winding one, so

\[
[\psi_c]=[u_g].
\]

The lamp involution has trivial K1 class. Indeed, if `a^2=e`, then with

\[
p=(1-u_a)/2
\]

we have a projection and the path `t -> exp(i pi t p)` joins `1` to `u_a`. Hence `[u_a]=0`.

Since `g=w^{-1}a`,

\[
[u_g]=-[u_w]+[u_a]=-[u_w].
\]

Thus outside the wall

\[
[U_c]=[u_w]+[u_g]=0,
\]

while multiplication by the scalar `-1` is itself null-homotopic in the complex unitary group and therefore

\[
[-\psi_c^*]=-[\psi_c]=-[u_g]=[u_w].
\]

So the total class remains

\[
[\mathcal W_c]=[u_w]
\]

on both sides. The scalar channel loses the class and the defect/companion channel acquires it. P81B is correct.

The determinant calculation from Section 5 gives a second consistency check: the two channels must have opposite net winding because the Julia factor has constant determinant.

## 7. Independent proof of P81C and the explicit critical class

### 7.1 Stable fixed-endpoint no-go

Choose `c_-` with `|c_-|<1` and `c_+` with `|c_+|>1`, and assume `[u_w] != 0`. From the preceding winding calculation,

\[
[U_{c_-}]=[u_w],
\qquad
[U_{c_+}]=0.
\]

For every `N>=1`,

\[
[U_{c_-}\oplus I_{N-1}]=[u_w]\ne0
=[U_{c_+}\oplus I_{N-1}].
\]

Homotopic unitaries represent the same K1 class, so

\[
\boxed{
U_{c_-}\oplus I_{N-1}
\not\sim_h
U_{c_+}\oplus I_{N-1}
}
\]

for every `N`. This is exactly P81C's fixed-endpoint statement.

The proof also identifies the only escape: the added channel must itself change K1 class. That is precisely what the Julia continuation does.

### 7.2 Critical representative

Take

\[
w=s_1^5s_2^{-2},
\qquad c=1,
\qquad g=w^{-1}a.
\]

Then

\[
q=\frac{1+u_g}{2},
\]

and

\[
q^*q=\frac{2+u_g+u_g^*}{4}.
\]

Hence

\[
1-q^*q
=\frac{2-u_g-u_g^*}{4}
=\frac{(1-u_g)^*(1-u_g)}4,
\]

so

\[
D=(1-q^*q)^{1/2}=\frac12|1-u_g|.

Define

\[
\mathcal U_{\rm crit}
=\operatorname{diag}(u_w,1)J(q)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
u_wq&u_wD\\
D&-q^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Since `g=w^{-1}a`,

\[
u_wu_g=u_a,
\]

and therefore

\[
u_wq=\frac{u_w+u_a}{2},
\]

which is exactly the claimed critical scalar corner.

\[
[\mathcal U_{\rm crit}]=[u_w].

\[
\boxed{

=5[u_{s_1}]-2[u_{s_2}]
=\mu_1^G(5v_1-2v_2).

This proves the explicit P81 critical matrix assembly claim. It does not add a third K1 direction: `(5,-2)` lies in the already complete rank-two lattice.

## 10. Method #22 — read the failures and prior art, not only the successful algebra

The P81 theorem is best understood as a diagnosis of why the preceding scalar algorithm failed.

### 10.1 Scalar polar failure

The scalar phase of `1+c u_g` exists exactly when zero is not on the circle `1+cT`, i.e. `|c| != 1`. At `|c|=1` the scalar operator loses invertibility. On opposite sides of that wall the scalar K1 class differs.

A scalar proof architecture has no degree of freedom in which to store the missing topological charge. The Julia/Halmos dilation adds precisely one defect channel, and that channel is what carries the compensating class. The failure is therefore not “lack of a clever scalar formula”; it is a channel-count/topological obstruction.

### 10.2 Classical mechanism, not a novelty claim

The general Julia/Halmos unitary construction for a contraction is classical. A bounded current search recovered P. L. Robinson, `Julia operators and Halmos dilations`, arXiv:1803.09329, which gives a direct proof of unitarity and the defect intertwining identity, and the literature it cites goes back to Halmos' 1950 dilation work. ### 10.3 Fixed group already solved in the literature

Pooya, `K-theory and K-homology of the wreath products of finite with free groups`, arXiv:1707.05984 / Illinois J. Math. 63 (2019), explicitly computes the K-groups and the Baum–Connes assembly map for `F wr F_n`. In particular the K1 basis consists of the free-group unitaries and the topological generators map to them. This is prior art strong enough to rule out interpreting P81 as a new proof of Baum–Connes for the fixed wreath product.
