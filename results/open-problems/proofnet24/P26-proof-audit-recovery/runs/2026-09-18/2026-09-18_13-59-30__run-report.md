## 2. Exact claim audited

Let `rho` be the real orthogonal two-dimensional standard representation of `S_3`. Split the group into its even and odd cosets. For signs `epsilon_g in {+1,-1}`, define

\[
F_+=\sum_{g\in A_3}\epsilon_g\rho(g),\qquad
F_-=\sum_{g\notin A_3}\epsilon_g\rho(g),\qquad F=F_++F_-.
\]

Claim 44 has three parts.

**(A) Signed singular-value trichotomy.** The singular values of `F` are exactly

\[
(0,0),\qquad (2,2),\qquad (4,0),
\]

according as both parity triples are constant, exactly one is nonconstant, or both are nonconstant.

For the complement indicator `n_g=(1-epsilon_g)/2`,

\[
M_N=\sum_g n_g\rho(g)=-\frac12F,
\]

so its singular values are exactly `(0,0)`, `(1,1)`, or `(2,0)` in the same cases.

**(B) Exact nonnegative support/Pareto theorem.** For one parity triangle, if exactly `k in {1,2,3}` channels are available, nonnegative weights have total mass `m`, and `M_parity` is their standard-sector sum, then

\[
\min \|M_{\rm parity}\|_F^2=
\begin{cases}
2m^2,&k=1,\\
\frac12m^2,&k=2,\\
0,&k=3.
\end{cases}
\]

Nonzero exact standard cancellation is possible iff all three channels of the relevant parity triangle are available, and then their weights must be equal.

**(C) Uniform-mask energy consequence.** Conditional on the previously established uniform 64-mask law, if `r_+` and `r_-` are masses supported on the full-even and full-odd triangle events, `T=r_++r_-`, `S=r_+-r_-`, and `E[T]=mu`, then

\[
\mathbb E(T^2+S^2)\ge 8\mu^2.
\]

At `mu=6`, equality is realized by total mass 24, equivalently weight 8 on each channel of an available full parity triangle, and gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(T)=126,\qquad \mathbb E[S^2]=126,\qquad
\mathbb E[T^2+S^2]=288.
\]

The audit is claim-scoped: it certifies this local standard-sector theorem and the stated consequence under the already-established uniform-mask input. It does **not** certify Randomstrasse Conjecture 27 itself.

## 3. Method #1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: identify the standard representation with a complex line

Instead of working with four matrix entries and Frobenius Gram geometry, I changed category from real `2 x 2` matrices to real-linear maps of the complex line.

Let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]

After an orthogonal change of basis, the three even elements of `S_3` act as rotations

\[
z\mapsto z,\qquad z\mapsto \omega z,\qquad z\mapsto \omega^2z,
\]

and the three odd elements act as reflections

\[
z\mapsto \bar z,\qquad z\mapsto \omega\bar z,\qquad z\mapsto \omega^2\bar z.
\]

Therefore every signed six-channel sum has the real-linear form

\[
\boxed{L(z)=a z+b\bar z}
\]

with

\[
a=\epsilon_0+\epsilon_1\omega+\epsilon_2\omega^2,
\qquad
b=\epsilon_3+\epsilon_4\omega+\epsilon_5\omega^2.
\]

The singular values of the real-linear map `z -> az+b\bar z` are

\[
\boxed{|a|+|b|,\quad ||a|-|b||}.
\]

One way to see this is to absorb the arguments of `a` and `b` by left and right planar rotations; these do not change singular values, and the resulting map is `(x,y) -> ((|a|+|b|)x,(|a|-|b|)y)` up to signs.

This change of category is a genuinely different derivation from the owner's Frobenius-triangle proof and gives the singular values almost immediately once the `C_3` amplitudes are known.

## 4. Method #14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: the two cyclic `C_3` amplitudes

For one sign triple,

\[
a=\epsilon_0+\epsilon_1\omega+\epsilon_2\omega^2.
\]

Since `1+omega+omega^2=0`, if all signs agree then `a=0`. If the triple is nonconstant, exactly one sign is the minority sign and subtraction of the constant triple gives

\[
a=\pm2\omega^j
\]

for some `j`. Hence

\[
|a|\in\{0,2\},
\]

with `|a|=0` exactly for a constant even triple and `|a|=2` otherwise. The identical statement holds for the odd amplitude `b`.

Substituting these two magnitudes into the real-linear singular-value formula gives, independently:

- `|a|=|b|=0`: singular values `(0,0)`;
- exactly one of `|a|,|b|` equals 2: `(2,2)`;
- `|a|=|b|=2`: `(4,0)`.

For complement indicators `n_j=(1-epsilon_j)/2`, the corresponding cyclic amplitude is exactly `-a/2` (and `-b/2` in the odd coset), because the full three-root sum vanishes. Thus the complement singular values are `(0,0)`, `(1,1)`, `(2,0)`.

This also stress-tests an important possible failure: could the even and odd sectors cancel each other? No. If

\[
az+b\bar z=0\quad\text{for all }z,
\]

then evaluation at `z=1` gives `a+b=0`, while evaluation at `z=i` gives `a-b=0`; hence `a=b=0`. Cross-parity cancellation cannot hide a nonzero sector.

## 5. Method #30 — ONE SECTOR AT A TIME: exact weighted support and energy

Fix one parity triangle and nonnegative weights `w_0,w_1,w_2`, with unavailable positions constrained to zero. Put

\[
a=w_0+w_1\omega+w_2\omega^2,
\qquad m=w_0+w_1+w_2.
\]

The exact Eisenstein identity is

\[
|a|^2=w_0^2+w_1^2+w_2^2-w_0w_1-w_1w_2-w_2w_0,
\]

so the Frobenius norm of `z -> az` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|M_{\rm parity}\|_F^2=2|a|^2
=3\sum_i w_i^2-m^2.
}
\]

If exactly `k` positions are available, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_i w_i^2\ge \frac{m^2}{k},
\]

with equality exactly at equal weights on the available positions. Therefore

\[
\min\|M_{\rm parity}\|_F^2=\left(\frac3k-1\right)m^2,
\]

which is `2m^2`, `m^2/2`, `0` for `k=1,2,3`. In particular, positive cancellation requires all three positions and equal weights. By the previous section, the even and odd parity sectors must cancel separately, so this proves the full support criterion.

For the uniform-mask consequence, let `A_+` and `A_-` be the full-even/full-odd events. Each has probability `1/8` under the uniform six-bit law. Since `r_+` is supported on `A_+`,

\[
\mathbb E r_+^2\ge \frac{(\mathbb E r_+)^2}{\mathbb P(A_+)}
=8(\mathbb E r_+)^2,
\]

and similarly for `r_-`. Because

\[
T^2+S^2=2(r_+^2+r_-^2),
\]

writing `a=E r_+`, `b=E r_-`, `a+b=mu`, gives

\[
\mathbb E(T^2+S^2)
\ge16(a^2+b^2)
\ge8(a+b)^2
=8\mu^2.
\]

Equality requires `a=b=mu/2` and constant conditional mass on each full-triangle event. At `mu=6`, each present parity triangle receives total mass 24, i.e. channel weight 8.

A direct 64-mask calculation then gives

\[
E[T]=6,\quad E[T^2]=162,\quad \operatorname{Var}(T)=126,
\quad E[S^2]=126,
\]

hence total energy 288.

The three methods therefore certify the theorem from three complementary views: real-linear category, `C_3` group action, and one-sector weighted optimization.
