## 3. Method #4 — prove a weaker refutation completely

The cleanest attack is to complete the free `k` quantifier in the most natural universal way and test the smallest legal value.

Take
\[
k=1,\qquad f(G)=\mathbf 1_n.
\]

The map `f` is graph-independent and has polynomial degree zero, hence it lies inside the displayed degree class.

For every graph `G`, every singleton `S={v}` is a 1-clique. Also
\[
{1\choose2}=0,
\]
so every singleton satisfies the right-hand density condition exactly:
\[
|E(G[S])|=0\le(1-\varepsilon)0=0.
\]
Thus the two maximization domains are literally the same nonempty set of all singletons, for every graph and every positive `epsilon`. Moreover
\[
\langle1_{\{v\}},\mathbf1_n\rangle=1.
\]
Therefore, with no empty-max convention and no probability estimate involved,
\[
\mathbb E\max_{S:\,1\text{-clique}}\langle1_S,f\rangle
=
\mathbb E\max_{|S|=1,\,|E(G[S])|\le0}\langle1_S,f\rangle
=1.
\]
The displayed inequality would force
\[
1\le C,
\]
contradicting the stipulated `0<C<1`.

Hence any interpretation that quantifies the displayed statement universally over positive integer `k` is exactly false. No checker is appropriate: this is a finite set identity plus `1 <= C`, with no computational core.

## 4. Method #27 — swap/repair the missing quantifier and stress-test the intended scale

The exact `k=1` contradiction could in principle be dismissed as outside the author's intended logarithmic clique regime. I therefore tested a representative logarithmic-scale completion without pretending it is the uniquely intended missing quantifier.

Let
\[
k_n=\lfloor0.9\log_2 n\rfloor,\qquad f(G)=\mathbf1_n,
\]
and for concreteness set `epsilon=1/4`.

Again every `k_n`-set receives the identical score `k_n`. Thus graph structure only determines whether each feasible family is empty.

For the clique family, the classical random-graph clique-number asymptotic is approximately
\[
2\log_2n-2\log_2\log_2n+O(1),
\]
so a clique of size `0.9 log_2 n` exists with probability tending to one. One convenient modern source restating this classical fact is Das Sarma–Deshpande–Kannan, arXiv:0807.5111, whose introduction records the two-point concentration around `2 log n - 2 log log n - 1`.

For the deficient family, fix one deterministic set `S_0` of size `k_n`. Put `m={k_n choose 2}`. Then
\[
|E(G[S_0])|\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,1/2).
\]
The desired condition is `|E| <= 3m/4`. Hoeffding gives
\[
\Pr\{|E|>3m/4\}
\le \exp(-m/8)=o(1).
\]
So the right feasible family is nonempty with probability `1-o(1)` as well.

Under the natural convention that a maximum over the empty family contributes zero,
\[
L_n=k_n(1-o(1)),\qquad R_n=k_n(1-o(1)),
\]
whence
\[
\frac{L_n}{R_n}\to1.
\]
No fixed `C<1` can satisfy `L_n <= C R_n` for all large `n`.

If no empty-family convention is intended, that is a second missing definition in the displayed statement. The exact `k=1` counterexample deliberately avoids this issue entirely.

Conclusion of this stress test: merely inserting a logarithmic `k(n)` does not repair the displayed conjecture if graph-blind constant output directions remain admissible.

## 5. Method #20 — find why the proof architecture fails

The obstruction is structural. The objective class contains the degree-zero graph-blind direction
\[
f(G)=\mathbf1_n.
\]
For this direction, every `k`-set receives the same score. The purported strict constant-factor separation between cliques and edge-deficient sets can then only come from a difference in feasibility probability. At `k=1` the feasible families coincide exactly. At ordinary logarithmic scales both families are nonempty with high probability, so their expected maxima are asymptotically equal.

Therefore a plausible corrected conjecture needs, at minimum, to state all of the following explicitly:

1. the quantifier and asymptotic regime for `k=k(n)`;
2. the convention when either maximization family is empty;
3. a normalization/nondegeneracy condition on `f`;
4. a restriction, centering, quotient, or equivariance condition excluding graph-blind constant/rank-one directions if strict separation is desired;
5. the exact dependence/quantifiers of `C` and `epsilon`.

This report does not assert that any such repair is true.
