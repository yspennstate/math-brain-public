## 3. #2 ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST — try to break the threshold and strictness

I treated C14 as false until the two obvious fault lines survived: the boundary `h=1/2`, and the claim that no smaller universal threshold works.

### 3.1 Boundary `h=1/2`

At `h=1/2`, the spectrum of `Q_h` is only guaranteed nonnegative; zero eigenvalues can occur. Therefore a proof claiming strict increase at the boundary would be suspect.

`K_2` gives the exact adversarial case. Here

\[
Q_{1/2}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q_{1/2}^2=Q_{1/2}.
\]

Hence for its unique edge

\[
r_0=0,\qquad r_1=r_2=r_3=\cdots=1.
\]

So strict monotonicity is false at the boundary, exactly as the owner theorem states; the correct universal boundary assertion is non-decrease.

### 3.2 No universal threshold below `1/2`

For `K_2` and any `0<h<1/2`, direct transition arithmetic gives

\[
a_1=h,\quad b_1=1-h,
\]

and

\[
a_2=h^2+(1-h)^2,\quad b_2=2h(1-h).
\]

Thus

\[
r_1-r_2
=
\frac{(1-h)(1-2h)}{h\bigl(h^2+(1-h)^2\bigr)}>0.
\]

Therefore `r_2<r_1` for every `0<h<1/2`. This is a source-valid connected arc-transitive graph and proves that no universal threshold smaller than `1/2` is possible.

The exact interior witness `h=1/4` gives

\[
r_1=3,\qquad r_2=\frac35.
\]

So the threshold and boundary wording survive the negation-first attack, and the attack also shows that the word **universal** is essential: individual graphs may tolerate less laziness.

## 4. #11 DUALIZE — the Hankel / two-copy certificate

I did not merely repeat the owner's one-line Cauchy--Schwarz argument. The clean dual object is the `2x2` Hankel determinant of the return sequence.

Arc-transitivity gives common values

\[
a_m=(Q_h^m)_{xx},\qquad b_m=(Q_h^m)_{xy}\quad(x\sim y).
\]

Since `Q_h=hI+(1-h)S` and `Q_h` commutes with `S`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{m+1}
&=(Q_h^{m+1})_{xx}\\
&=h(Q_h^m)_{xx}+(1-h)(SQ_h^m)_{xx}\\
&=h a_m+(1-h)b_m.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 r_m^{(h)}=
 \frac{a_{m+1}/a_m-h}{1-h}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `h>=1/2`, every eigenvalue of `Q_h` lies in `[0,1]`. Let `mu_x` be the local spectral probability measure at `x`. Then

\[
a_m=\int_0^1 q^m\,d\mu_x(q).
\]

The relevant discrete derivative is controlled by

\[
D_m:=a_m a_{m+2}-a_{m+1}^2.
\]

Expanding on two independent copies of the spectral measure gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
D_m=
\frac12\iint_{[0,1]^2}
q^m r^m(q-r)^2\,d\mu_x(q)d\mu_x(r)\ge0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently, the Hankel matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a_m&a_{m+1}\\
a_{m+1}&a_{m+2}
\end{pmatrix}
\]

is positive semidefinite. This is the dual/moment-cone certificate behind the monotonicity. Since the stationary eigenvalue `1` has local mass `1/|V|`, every `a_m>0`, and `D_m>=0` is equivalent to

\[
\frac{a_{m+1}}{a_m}
\le
\frac{a_{m+2}}{a_{m+1}}.
\]

Equation (4.1) then gives

\[
r_{m+1}^{(h)}\ge r_m^{(h)}.
\]

If `h>1/2`, every spectral value is strictly positive. The local measure is not a point mass: it contains the stationary atom at `1`, while a connected nontrivial graph has nonstationary local spectral mass `1-1/|V|>0`. Hence the nonnegative integrand in (4.2) is positive on a set of positive product measure and

\[
D_m>0
\]

for every `m`. Therefore every step is strict.

This proves the theorem end to end. It also identifies exactly why the endpoint may lose strictness: when `h=1/2`, spectral mass at zero can annihilate the factor `(qr)^m` after the first step, as `K_2` demonstrates.
