## 4. Claim 40, stated precisely

Let `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)` be prime. Let

\[
V=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\},
\qquad n=|V|=\frac{p-5}{4},
\]

and let `G=G_{p,2}` be the induced Paley graph on `V`; let `K=\overline G`. For a theta-plus dual feasible state

\[
X\succeq0,\qquad \operatorname{tr}X=1,\qquad X_{ij}\le0\quad(ij\in E(K)),
\]

define

\[
T=\langle J,X\rangle,
\qquad
\Delta=-2\langle A_K,X\rangle\ge0.
\]

Claim 40 asserts

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
\ge
\frac{2[2(T-1)+\Delta]^2}{n(p-15+2\sqrt p)}.
}
\tag{C40}
\]

In particular, if `T>2√p/3`, then

\[
\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
>
\frac{8(\frac23\sqrt p-1)^2}{n(p-15+2\sqrt p)},
\]

so any asymptotic violating sequence obeys

\[
\liminf_{p\to\infty}p\operatorname{tr}(X^2)\ge\frac{128}{9},
\]

and therefore

\[
r_{\rm eff}(X)=\frac1{\operatorname{tr}(X^2)}
\le\left(\frac9{128}+o(1)\right)p
=\left(\frac9{32}+o(1)\right)n.
\]

The ordinary-theta case is included by `Delta=0`.

## 5. Independent upstream check: the exact degree bound

I did not treat the owner's localized degree estimate as a black box, because it is the only nontrivial arithmetic input controlling the constant in (C40).

For fixed `x∈V`, the degree of `x` in `G` is

\[
d_x=
\frac18
\sum_{y\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1,x\}}
(1+\chi(y))(1+\chi(y-1))(1+\chi(x-y)).
\]

Since `p≡1 (mod 4)`, `χ(-1)=1`. Over the excluded domain each of the three one-character sums equals `-2`. Each of the three two-character sums also equals `-2`: over all of `F_p` the relevant nondegenerate quadratic character sum is `-1`, and the only nonzero excluded contribution is `+1`. The triple product vanishes at `0,1,x`, so it may be summed over all of `F_p`. Hence, with

\[
S_x=\sum_{y\in\mathbb F_p}\chi\bigl(y(y-1)(x-y)\bigr),
\]

one gets the exact identity

\[
\boxed{d_x=\frac{p-15+S_x}{8}.}
\tag{1}
\]

The cubic has the three distinct roots `0,1,x`; the standard Weil bound for this quadratic-character sum gives

\[
|S_x|\le2\sqrt p.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{d_x\le\frac{p-15+2\sqrt p}{8}.}
\tag{2}
\]

Summing degrees gives

\[
\boxed{
 m:=|E(G)|\le\frac{n(p-15+2\sqrt p)}{16}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

For completeness, the same elementary indicator calculation yields

\[
|V|=\frac14[(p-2)-1-1-1]=\frac{p-5}{4}.
\]

Thus the arithmetic constant used by Claim 40 checks independently.

## 6. NEW-METHOD #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER

Let

\[
s_G=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}X_{ij},
\qquad
s_K=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(K)}X_{ij}.
\]

Because `tr X=1`,

\[
T=1+2(s_G+s_K).
\]

Also `\langle A_K,X\rangle=2s_K`, hence

\[
\Delta=-4s_K,
\qquad
\boxed{s_G=\frac{2(T-1)+\Delta}{4}.}
\tag{4}
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz on the `m` unordered `G`-edges gives

\[
s_G^2\le m\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}X_{ij}^2.
\tag{5}
\]

Now use information that the owner proof deliberately discarded. Since `tr X=1`,

\[
\sum_iX_{ii}^2\ge\frac1n.
\tag{6}
\]

Moreover

\[
\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
=\sum_iX_{ii}^2+2\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}^2.
\]

Combining (5) and (6),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
\ge
\frac1n+\frac{2s_G^2}{m}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Substitute (4) and the edge-count bound (3):

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(X^2)
\ge
\frac1n+
\frac{2[2(T-1)+\Delta]^2}{n(p-15+2\sqrt p)}.

\tag{8}
\]

This strictly strengthens Claim 40 for every nonempty localized graph. Dropping the positive `1/n` term gives exactly the owner's displayed theorem, so Claim 40 is fully proved.

## 7. NEW-METHOD #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

The same proof becomes especially transparent in Hilbert-Schmidt geometry.

Let `P_G` denote the orthogonal projection of a symmetric matrix onto its off-diagonal entries indexed by `E(G)`. Then

\[
\|A_G\|_F^2=2m,
\qquad
\langle A_G,X\rangle=T-1+\frac\Delta2,
\]

and

\[
\|P_GX\|_F^2=2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}X_{ij}^2.
\]

Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy gives

\[
\bigl(T-1+\Delta/2\bigr)^2
\le 2m\,\|P_GX\|_F^2.
\]

This is exactly the edge-energy estimate behind (5), expressed as the statement that a fixed adjacency direction cannot have large pairing with a Frobenius-diffuse state. The purity obstruction is therefore an operator-space projection theorem: a source-scale dual state must carry definite Hilbert-Schmidt mass in the `G`-edge subspace, in addition to its unavoidable diagonal mass.

This language independently confirms all factors of two: the adjacency matrix counts each unordered edge twice, while the Frobenius norm of a symmetric off-diagonal projection does the same.

First, the owner's constant `128/9` is valid but not sharp even within the same elementary argument: its proof omitted the strictly positive diagonal Frobenius mass. There is no contradiction; the theorem was only a necessary obstruction.
