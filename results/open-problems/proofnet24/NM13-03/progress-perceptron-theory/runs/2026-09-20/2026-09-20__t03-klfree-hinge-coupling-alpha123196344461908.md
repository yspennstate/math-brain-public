## 2. Exact Gaussian hinge lemma

Use the positive-gap dimensionless kernel already proved by NM13-07. To avoid overloading its scale variable `lambda`, write

`beta := xi-1 > 0`.

For fixed `t`, let

`F_beta(t)=E_H exp[-beta (t+H)_+^2/2]`,

`U(t)=log F_beta(t)`,

and let `rho_t` be the tilted law of `H` with density proportional to

`exp[-beta(t+H)_+^2/2] d gamma(H)`.

Put `Y=t+H`, `W=Y_+`,

`mu_j(t)=E_{rho_t} W^j`,

and

`K(t)=KL(rho_t || gamma_H)`.

Then, directly from the Radon--Nikodym derivative,

`K(t)=-beta mu_2(t)/2-U(t)`.                              (2.1)

### Theorem NM03-T03-HINGE-KL-CLOSURE

For every `beta>0` and every real `t`:

1. `U_t=-beta mu_1` and

   `0 <= K_t <= [beta/(1+beta)] (-U_t)`.                    (2.2)

2. Consequently

   `boxed: 0 <= K(t) <= c_beta[-U(t)]`,

   where

   `c_beta=beta/(1+beta)`.                                  (2.3)

3. For an outer Gaussian tilt

   `nu(dt) proportional exp[m U(t)] phi_d(t-a)dt`,

   with partition log `q=log Z`, define

   `D_U=m E_nu U-q`,

   `D_H=m E_nu K`,

   `h=E_nu mu_2`.

   Then the **exact closure identity** is

   `boxed: D_H=-q-D_U-(m beta/2)h`.                          (2.4)

   In particular,

   `0 <= D_H <= c_beta(-q-D_U)`.                            (2.5)

4. Holding `(a,d,beta)` fixed and varying `m`,

   `boxed: -c_beta d_m D_U <= d_m D_H`

   and

   `boxed: d_m D_H <= c_beta(-q-D_U)/m`.                    (2.6)

   Moreover

   `boxed: d_m(D_H+c_beta D_U) >= D_H/m >=0`.               (2.7)

### Proof

The formula `U_t=-beta mu_1` follows by differentiating the hinge kernel in the original `H` coordinate.

For the KL derivative, differentiating (2.1) gives the standard exponential-family identity

`K_t=(beta^2/2) Cov_{rho_t}(W^2,W)`

`   =(beta^2/2)(mu_3-mu_2 mu_1) >=0`,                       (2.8)

because `W^2` and `W` are increasing functions of the same scalar variable.

The key comparison is an exact positive-half-line integration by parts. In the `Y` coordinate the tilted density is proportional to

`exp[tY-Y^2/2-beta Y_+^2/2]`.

On `Y>0` its logarithmic derivative is `t-(1+beta)Y`. Integrating the derivative of `Y^2` times this density over the positive half-line gives

`(1+beta) mu_3=t mu_2+2mu_1`.                               (2.9)

The full score identity is

`E_{rho_t}Y=t-beta mu_1`.

Hence

`t-(1+beta)mu_1=E Y-mu_1=E[Y 1_{Y<0}] <=0`.                (2.10)

Combining (2.9)--(2.10),

`(1+beta)(mu_3-mu_2mu_1)`
` =2mu_1+mu_2[t-(1+beta)mu_1]`
` <=2mu_1`.

Insert this in (2.8):

`K_t <= beta^2 mu_1/(1+beta)`
`     =[beta/(1+beta)](-U_t)`,

which proves (2.2).

As `t->-infinity`, the hinge is inactive with probability tending to one; dominated convergence gives `U(t)->0`, `rho_t->gamma_H`, and `K(t)->0`. Integrating (2.2) from `-infinity` to `t` proves (2.3).

For (2.4), average (2.1) under `nu` and multiply by `m`:

`D_H=-(m beta/2)h-m E_nu U`.

But `D_U=m E_nu U-q`, i.e. `mE_nu U=D_U+q`. This gives (2.4) exactly. Inequality (2.5) follows by averaging (2.3), noting `-q-D_U=mE_nu(-U)`.

For the `m` derivatives, put `A=-U`. The family `nu_m` is proportional to `exp[-mA]` times a fixed Gaussian base. From (2.2), for `t>s`,

`0 <= K(t)-K(s) <= c_beta[A(t)-A(s)]`.

For iid `T,T'~nu_m`, the covariance representation therefore gives

`0 <= Cov(K,A)`
`   =1/2 E[(K(T)-K(T'))(A(T)-A(T'))]`
` <=c_beta Var(A)`.                                          (2.11)

Direct differentiation gives

`d_m D_U=m Var(A)`,

`d_m D_H=E K-m Cov(K,A)`.                                   (2.12)

The lower bound in (2.6) follows from `EK>=0` and (2.11); the upper bound follows from `Cov(K,A)>=0`, (2.3), and

`E A=(-q-D_U)/m`.

Finally, adding `c_beta d_mD_U` to (2.12) and using (2.11) gives

`d_m(D_H+c_beta D_U)`
` =E K+m[c_beta Var(A)-Cov(K,A)]`
` >=E K=D_H/m`,

proving (2.7). QED.

### Sharpness note

The constant `beta/(1+beta)` is asymptotically sharp as `t->+infinity`, where the hinge is overwhelmingly on its positive quadratic branch and the tilted law approaches the corresponding fully quadratic Gaussian tilt. The checker also verifies this numerically for several `beta`, but sharpness is not needed below.
