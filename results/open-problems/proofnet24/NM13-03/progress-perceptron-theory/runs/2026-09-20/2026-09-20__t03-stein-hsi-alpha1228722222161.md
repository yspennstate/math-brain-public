## 2. New generic theorem: a strongly-log-concave Stein–HSI cage

Let `gamma=N(0,1)` and

`d nu / d gamma = exp(phi)/Z`,

where `phi` is `C^2`, concave, and has enough decay for the integrations by parts below. Write

`mu=E_nu X`, `v=Var_nu(X)`, `delta=1-v`,

`r=phi'`, `R=Var_nu(r)`.

Let `G=N(mu,v)`. The preceding variance-deficit calculation gives `0<v<=1`, while the matched-Gaussian calculation gives the relative Fisher information

`I_G := I(nu||G) = R-delta^2/v >= 0`.

Put `psi(delta)=-delta-log(1-delta)`.

### Theorem NM03-T03-STEIN-HSI-CAGE

For every such Gaussian concave tilt,

`D(nu||gamma) = [mu^2+psi(delta)]/2 + D(nu||G)`,                         (2.1)

and, for `delta>0`,

`D(nu||G)`

` <= min { 1/2 log(1+v I_G),`

`          delta/(2v) log(1+v^2 I_G/delta) }`.                           (2.2)

When `delta=0`, `nu=G` and the second term is interpreted as zero.

The first entry in the minimum is the preceding matched-Gaussian/Stam cage. The second is new in this project. It uses no quantity beyond the same variance deficit and relative Fisher information already present in the complete-third score/curvature compiler.

### Proof, step 1: the Stein kernel is bounded by one

Write the density of `nu` as `p=e^{-W}/Z_0`, where

`W(x)=x^2/2-phi(x)`

and therefore

`W''(x)=1-phi''(x)>=1`.

The one-dimensional Stein kernel is

`tau(x)=p(x)^{-1} int_x^infinity (y-mu)p(y)dy`.                           (2.3)

It is nonnegative: if `x>=mu` this is immediate, while if `x<mu` use the equivalent formula

`tau(x)p(x)=-int_{-infinity}^x (y-mu)p(y)dy`.

We now prove `tau<=1`. Since `p'=-W'p`,

`p(x)=int_x^infinity W'(y)p(y)dy`.                                       (2.4)

Define

`g(y)=W'(y)-(y-mu)`.

Then `g'=W''-1>=0`, so `g` is increasing, and

`E_nu g(X)=E W'(X)-E(X-mu)=0`.                                           (2.5)

Subtracting (2.4) from (2.3) times `p(x)` gives

`(tau(x)-1)p(x)`
` = -int_x^infinity g(y)p(y)dy`
` =  int_{-infinity}^x g(y)p(y)dy`.                                      (2.6)

For an increasing zero-mean function, the last cumulative integral is nonpositive for every `x`: before a zero crossing the integrand is nonpositive; after it, use the negative of the nonnegative remaining tail. Hence

`0<=tau(x)<=1`.                                                          (2.7)

The Stein identity with the test function `h(x)=x-mu` gives

`E tau(X)=v`.                                                            (2.8)

Therefore, after standardizing `Y=(X-mu)/sqrt(v)`, its Stein kernel is `T(Y)=tau(X)/v`, and its Stein discrepancy satisfies

`S^2 := E[(T-1)^2]`
`     = Var(tau)/v^2`
`     <= [v-v^2]/v^2`
`     = delta/v`,                                                        (2.9)

where `tau^2<=tau` was used.

### Proof, step 2: self-contained one-dimensional HSI inequality

Let `Y` now denote any centered variance-one random variable with smooth density, relative Fisher information `I=I(Y||gamma)`, Stein kernel `T`, and `S^2=E(T-1)^2`. Let

`Y_t=aY+bZ`, `a=e^{-t}`, `b=sqrt(1-a^2)`,

with `Z` an independent standard Gaussian. Let `rho_t` be the score of `Y_t` relative to `gamma`, and `I_t=E rho_t(Y_t)^2`.

Two exact conditional-score representations give two Fisher bounds.

First, the ordinary score identity of `Y` yields

`rho_t(Y_t)=a E[rho_0(Y)|Y_t]`,

hence

`I_t<=a^2 I`.                                                            (2.10)

Second, for every smooth test `h`, the Stein identity gives

`E[rho_t(Y_t)h(Y_t)]`
` = a^2 E[(T(Y)-1) h'(Y_t)]`.

Gaussian integration by parts in `Z` turns this into

`E[rho_t(Y_t)h(Y_t)]`
` = (a^2/b) E[(T(Y)-1) Z h(Y_t)]`.

Thus in `L^2`,

`rho_t(Y_t)=(a^2/b) E[(T(Y)-1)Z | Y_t]`,

and Jensen gives

`I_t <= a^4 S^2/(1-a^2)`.                                               (2.11)

Relative de Bruijn along the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup gives

`D(Y||gamma)=int_0^infinity I_t dt`.                                     (2.12)

Using the minimum of (2.10) and (2.11), then setting `u=e^{-2t}`, gives

`D(Y||gamma)`
` <= 1/2 int_0^1 min{I, S^2 u/(1-u)} du`
` = (S^2/2) log(1+I/S^2).                                                (2.13)

(The crossing point is `u=I/(I+S^2)`.) This proves the one-dimensional HSI inequality needed here without importing an unproved black box.

### Proof, step 3: apply HSI to the matching Gaussian

For `Y=(X-mu)/sqrt(v)`,

`D(Y||gamma)=D(nu||G)`,

`I(Y||gamma)=v I_G`.

By (2.9), `S^2<=delta/v`. For fixed `I>0`, the function

`s -> s log(1+I/s)`

is increasing for `s>0` because its derivative is `log(1+x)-x/(1+x)>0`, `x=I/s`. Hence (2.13) gives

`D(nu||G)`
` <= delta/(2v) log(1+v^2 I_G/delta)`.                                  (2.14)

Taking the minimum with the already proved matched-Gaussian/Stam bound proves (2.2). If `delta=0`, then (2.7)--(2.8) force `tau=1` almost surely, so the standardized law is Gaussian and `D(nu||G)=0`. QED.

## 3. Complete-third compilation

Retain the strict complete-third notation

`0<p3<p2<1`, `0<q3<q2<1`, `c2>c3>0`,

`m=c3/c2`, `d^2=p2-p3`, `s^2=1-p2`, `B=c2/(4 gamma_sq)`.

For independent standard Gaussians `U,V,H`, set

`X=dU+sqrt(p3)V-1`,

`f(x)=E_H exp[-B(x+sH)_+^2]`, `u=log f`.

For fixed `V=v`, let `nu_v` be the `U` tilt proportional to `exp[m u(X)]`, and define

`g_v=<u_x>_v`, `t_v=Var_v(u_x)`, `c_v=< -u_xx >_v`,

`delta_v=m d^2 c_v-m^2 d^2 t_v`, `v_v=1-delta_v`,

`R_v=m^2 d^2 t_v`,

`I_v=R_v-delta_v^2/v_v`.

The exact variance-deficit lower contribution is

`L_U(v)=1/2[(m d g_v)^2+psi(delta_v)]`.

The new theorem gives the pointwise upper correction

`C_U(v)=min{`

`  1/2 log(1+v_v I_v),`

`  delta_v/(2v_v) log(1+v_v^2 I_v/delta_v)`

`}`,                                                                      (3.1)

with the second term zero at `delta_v=0`. Therefore

`E_V L_U(V) <= D_U <= E_V[L_U(V)+C_U(V)]`.                               (3.2)

No new nested integral is introduced.

For the inner `H` tilt

`d rho_x/d gamma_H proportional exp[-B(x+sH)_+^2]`,

the preceding exact identities give

`delta_H(x)=-s^2 u_xx(x)`, `v_H=1-delta_H`,

`R_H(x)=2Bs^2 P_{rho_x}(x+sH>0)-delta_H(x)`,

`I_H=R_H-delta_H^2/v_H`,

and the Gaussian-matched lower term

`L_H(x)=1/2[s^2 u_x(x)^2+psi(delta_H(x))]`.

Thus

`L_H(x) <= KL(rho_x||gamma_H) <= L_H(x)+C_H(x)`,                         (3.3)

where `C_H` is obtained from (3.1) by replacing `(delta_v,v_v,I_v)` with `(delta_H,v_H,I_H)`. Averaging under the final outer tilt and multiplying by `m` gives the c2-sector cage.

This is directly applicable to `NM13-07`'s prescribed-positive-gap four-overlap compiler: `D_U,D_H` are the expensive nested quantities in the entropy-stable residuals, and the new upper cage uses exactly the same `u_x,u_xx`, positive-tail probability and score variances already required by its overlap/Jacobian machinery.
