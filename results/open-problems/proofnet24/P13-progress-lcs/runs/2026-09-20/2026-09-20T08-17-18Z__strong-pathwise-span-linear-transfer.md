## 3. Exact true-source strip recursion

Fix strip half-width `w` and put

\[
m=2w.
\]

A formal state is

\[
s=(x,g),\qquad x=(x_1,\ldots,x_m),\quad g=(g_1,\ldots,g_m)\in\{0,1\}^m.
\]

Define the boundary profile

\[
V_0=0,\qquad V_k=\sum_{i=1}^k g_i\quad(1\le k\le m).
\]

For one fresh actual source pair `(u,v)`, let

\[
a_i={\bf1}_{\{x_{i+1}=v\}}\quad(0\le i<m),
\qquad a_m={\bf1}_{\{u=v\}}.
\]

The updated profile is

\[
Q_0=\max\{V_1,V_0+a_0\},
\]

\[
Q_k=\max\{Q_{k-1},V_k+a_k,V_{k+1}\},\qquad1\le k<m,
\]

\[
Q_m=\max\{Q_{m-1},V_m+a_m\}.
\]

Then

\[
x'=(x_2,\ldots,x_m,u),
\qquad
g'_k=Q_k-Q_{k-1},
\]

and the one-step central reward is

\[
\rho(s;u,v)=Q_w-V_w\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The same `v` occurs in every `a_i`; this is the dependence that an independent Bernoulli match matrix would destroy.

For a future source word `omega` of length `N`, write `R_N(s;omega)` for the cumulative reward generated by this exact recursion.

## 4. Unrolled-strip representation

Unroll the preceding recursion into its ordinary directed LCS strip. On the initial cross-section put boundary vertices

\[
p_k=(k,0),\qquad0\le k\le m,
\]

with initial values `V_k`. The strip is the region

\[
0\le i-j\le m,
\]

and after `N` updates the central endpoint is

\[
z_N=(w+N,N).
\]

Horizontal and vertical edges have weight zero. A diagonal edge has weight one exactly when its actual `X` and `Y` source symbols agree.

For fixed raw window `x` and fixed future source word `omega`, let

\[
M_k(x;\omega)
\]

be the maximum number of diagonal match edges on a directed path from `p_k` to `z_N`; put `M_k=-infinity` if no such path exists. Standard dynamic programming gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
R_N((x,g);\omega)
=\max_{0\le k\le m}\{V_k+M_k(x;\omega)\}-V_w.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This formula is only a restatement of the dependence-preserving recursion, not a probabilistic approximation.

## 5. New central-start squeeze

The key observation is that all initial-boundary choices can be compared to the single central start `p_w` without paying for the lifetime of a discrepancy.

### Lemma 5.1 — geometric splice inequalities

For every fixed `x`, future word `omega`, and `k` for which the path problem is feasible,

\[
M_k\le M_w+(w-k)\qquad(k<w),
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
M_k\le M_w\qquad(k>w).
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Take first `k>w`. From `p_w=(w,0)` make `k-w` horizontal zero-weight moves along the initial boundary to `p_k`, then follow an optimal path from `p_k`. This is a legal strip path because every intermediate boundary offset lies between `w` and `k<=m`. Hence `M_w>=M_k`, proving (5.2).

Now let `k<w` and take any directed path `P` from `p_k` to `z_N`. Since the endpoint has horizontal coordinate `w+N>w`, `P` crosses the vertical line `i=w`. Let `(w,r)` be its first vertex on that line. Because the path lies in the strip, `w-r>=0`, hence `0<=r<=w`. Replace the prefix of `P` from `(k,0)` to `(w,r)` by the vertical zero-weight path from `(w,0)` to `(w,r)`. The replacement remains inside the strip because its offsets run from `w` down to `w-r>=0`.

The deleted prefix increases the horizontal coordinate from `k` to `w`. Every horizontal or diagonal edge increases that coordinate by one, so the deleted prefix contains exactly `w-k` edges of those two kinds and therefore at most `w-k` diagonal match edges. The spliced path from `p_w` consequently loses at most `w-k` units of match weight. Taking an optimum over `P` proves (5.1). QED.

### Lemma 5.2 — central-start sandwich

For every formal state and every future word,

\[
\boxed{
M_w(x;\omega)
\le R_N((x,g);\omega)
\le M_w(x;\omega)+w.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

The lower bound is simply the `k=w` term of (4.1).

For `k<w`,

\[
V_k-V_w=-\sum_{i=k+1}^w g_i\le0,
\]

so Lemma 5.1 gives

\[
(V_k-V_w)+M_k\le M_w+(w-k)\le M_w+w.
\]

For `k>w`,

\[
V_k-V_w=\sum_{i=w+1}^k g_i\le k-w\le w,
\]

while Lemma 5.1 gives `M_k<=M_w`. Thus every term in (4.1) is at most `M_w+w`. QED.

### Lemma 5.3 — only the right half of the initial raw window can affect `M_w`

For two raw windows `x,y` and the same future word,

\[
\boxed{
|M_w(x;\omega)-M_w(y;\omega)|
\le d_H(x_{w+1:m},y_{w+1:m})
\le w.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

A directed path starting at `p_w` never decreases its horizontal coordinate. Hence no diagonal edge on such a path can use an initial source symbol `x_i` with `i<=w`. Only the right-half symbols `x_{w+1},...,x_m` can matter.

For a fixed path, each `X` index can appear in at most one diagonal edge. Flipping one right-half raw symbol therefore changes the weight of every fixed path by at most one. Flipping `d` such symbols changes every path weight by at most `d`, and taking maxima preserves that Lipschitz bound. QED.

## 6. Main theorem: the strong pathwise span conjecture is true

### Theorem 6.1 — sharp full-state pathwise span

For every `w>=1`, `m=2w`, every horizon `N`, every actual common future source word `omega`, and every two formal states `s=(x,g)`, `t=(y,h)`,

\[
\boxed{
|R_N(s;\omega)-R_N(t;\omega)|\le m.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

#### Proof

By Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3,

\[
R_N(s;\omega)
\le M_w(x;\omega)+w
\le M_w(y;\omega)+2w
\le R_N(t;\omega)+2w.
\]

Since `m=2w`, this is

\[
R_N(s;\omega)-R_N(t;\omega)\le m.
\]

Interchanging `s,t` proves (6.1). QED.

This closes the specific strong-span gap left open in the latest recovered R17 report. It is stronger than summing one-bit raw and gradient sensitivities separately: that route pays `m+m=2m`, whereas the central-start splice couples the two sources of boundary uncertainty geometrically and pays only `w+w=m`.

## 7. The constant `m` is pathwise sharp

The factor two improvement cannot be repeated by another state-uniform, common-word argument: the constant `m` is already exact.

Let

\[
B=(u,v)=(1,0)
\]

and take the future word `omega=B^m`. Consider

\[
s_+=(0^m;0^m)
\]

and

\[
s_-=(0^w1^w;1^w0^w).
\]

For `s_+`, all initial boundary values are zero. Starting at `p_0`, take `m` diagonal steps through the `m` initial zero `X` symbols against the `m` future zero `Y` symbols, gaining `m` matches, and then `w` horizontal zero-weight steps to the final central endpoint. Therefore `R_m(s_+;B^m)>=m`; since there are only `m` future `Y` symbols, `R_m<=m`, so

\[
R_m(s_+;B^m)=m.
\]

For `s_-`,

\[
V_k=\min(k,w),\qquad V_w=w.
\]

All `X` symbols with index greater than `w` are one: this includes the initial right half and every appended `u=1`. Since every future `Y` symbol is zero, a path starting at `p_k` can collect at most `max(w-k,0)` matches, all from the zero symbols among indices `k+1,...,w`. Hence

\[
V_k+M_k\le \min(k,w)+\max(w-k,0)=w
\]

for every `k`, while the `k=w` term attains `w`. Thus the final value equals `w` and

\[
R_m(s_-;B^m)=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_m(s_+;B^m)-R_m(s_-;B^m)=m.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

So any further universal improvement must exploit averaging, stationarity, reachability, or a source-specific restriction; it cannot improve the pathwise diameter over all formal states and all common words.

## 8. Finite-to-infinite transfer

Let

\[
F_N(s)=\mathbf E\,R_N(s),
\]

where the expectation is over the genuine iid source-pair stream. The pathwise theorem immediately gives

\[
\operatorname{span}F_N
:=\max_sF_N(s)-\min_sF_N(s)
\le m.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The inherited synchronizer theorem gives the finite strip a unique recurrent class and stationary law `pi_w`. Let `gamma_w` be its stationary mean reward. Starting from stationarity,

\[
N\gamma_w=\sum_s\pi_w(s)F_N(s).
\]

A convex combination lies between the minimum and maximum of `F_N`, so (8.1) proves the all-width transfer

\[
\boxed{
|\mathbf E_sR_N-N\gamma_w|\le m=2w
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

for every formal starting state `s` and every horizon `N`.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbf E_sR_N}{N}-\frac{2w}{N}
\le\gamma_w\le
\frac{\mathbf E_sR_N}{N}+\frac{2w}{N}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The previous recovered deterministic theorem had constant `2m=4w`, so the certified finite-horizon interval radius is cut exactly in half at every width and every horizon.

Using the inherited strip lower relation `gamma_w<=gamma_2`,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_2\ge
\frac{\mathbf E_sR_N}{N}-\frac{2w}{N}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

At width `w=6`, `m=12`, the best recovered deterministic correction therefore moves
