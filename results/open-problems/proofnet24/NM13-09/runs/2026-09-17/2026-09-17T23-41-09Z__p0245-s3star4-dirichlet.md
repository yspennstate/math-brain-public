## 4. New idea before code

The previous proof succeeded because translation invariance made every target-set hitting time collapse to a small rational expression. For the full reversible four-state problem, translation invariance is unavailable, but reversibility still gives a canonical symmetric object: the stationary conductance matrix

\[
q_{ij}=\pi_iP_{ij}=q_{ji}.
\]

Method #11 suggested replacing transition probabilities by a **Dirichlet problem in conductance coordinates**. For any target set `A`, with `B=A^c`, define the killed Dirichlet matrix

\[
L_B=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_B)-(q_{ij})_{i,j\in B}.
\]

Then the hitting vector solves `L_B h = pi_B`, hence

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\pi T_A=\pi_B^\top L_B^{-1}\pi_B.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This has two consequences.

First, Cauchy--Schwarz in the `L_B` inner product gives a nontrivial all-chain lower bound in terms of the cut capacity

\[
Q(B,A)=\sum_{i\in B,j\in A}q_{ij}:
\qquad
\mathbb E_\pi T_A
\ge
\frac{\pi(B)^2}{Q(B,A)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Second, it allows one to break the complete-graph symmetry deliberately while retaining a tractable exact Dirichlet system. I chose the most basic transverse deformation: distinguish one state from the other three, keep the three leaves exchangeable, and allow independent loop weights at the center and leaves. That family is not vertex-transitive in general and was not covered by the preceding Cayley theorem.

Both routes produced exact results before the symbolic self-check was written.

## 6. General theorem for every loopless reversible four-state chain

### Theorem `NM13-09-P0245-LOOPLESS-STAGE2-02`

Let `(X_t)` be any irreducible reversible **loopless** Markov chain on four states, started from stationarity. Let

\[
\tau_k=\min\{t:\#\{X_0,\ldots,X_t\}\ge k\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\tau_2=1,\qquad
       \mathbb E(\tau_3-\tau_2)\ge\frac32.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equality in the second inequality holds for simple random walk on `K_4`.

#### Proof

Write

\[
q_{ij}=\pi_iP_{ij}=q_{ji},\qquad q_{ii}=0.
\]

Because the chain is loopless,

\[
\pi_i=\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij},
\qquad
\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}=\frac12.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For every two-set `B`, let

\[
s_B=\pi(B),\qquad
c_B=Q(B,B^c).
\]

Taking `A=B^c` in (4.2),

\[
\mathbb E_\pi T_{B^c}\ge \frac{s_B^2}{c_B}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

There are six two-sets. Each `pi_i` appears in three of them, so

\[
\sum_{|B|=2}s_B=3.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Every undirected conductance edge `{i,j}` crosses exactly four of the six ordered-by-subset two-two cuts: choose which endpoint lies in `B` and choose one of the other two vertices to join it. Thus by (6.2)

\[
\sum_{|B|=2}c_B
=4\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}=2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Irreducibility makes every two-two cut capacity positive. Titu Andreescu/Cauchy therefore gives

\[
S_2:=
\sum_{|A|=2}\mathbb E_\pi T_A
\ge
\sum_{|B|=2}\frac{s_B^2}{c_B}
\ge
\frac{(\sum_Bs_B)^2}{\sum_Bc_B}
=\frac92.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Now define

\[
S_k=\sum_{|A|=k}\mathbb E_\pi T_A.
\]

For every sample path, if `U_t` is the number of still-unvisited states after time `t`, then

\[
\sum_{|A|=k}\mathbf 1_{\{T_A>t\}}=\binom{U_t}{k}.
\]

Summing over `t` and taking expectations gives, for four states,

\[
S_3=\mathbb E\tau_2,
\qquad
S_2=3\mathbb E\tau_2+\mathbb E(\tau_3-\tau_2).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Looplessness gives `tau_2=1` almost surely, hence `S_3=1`. Combining (6.6)--(6.7),

\[
\mathbb E(\tau_3-\tau_2)=S_2-3S_3\ge\frac92-3=\frac32.
\]

For `K_4`, after two states have been seen the chance that the next step reaches one of the two unseen states is `2/3`, so the mean second discovery increment is `3/2`. This proves (6.1). ∎

### What remains in the unrestricted loopless four-state problem

The exact four-state inclusion--exclusion identity is

\[
\mathbb E_\pi C=S_1-S_2+S_3.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For loopless chains `S_3=1`, so the source bound `E_pi C>=11/2` is exactly equivalent to

\[
\boxed{S_1-S_2\ge\frac92.}
\tag{6.9}
\]

The stronger inequality `S_1>=2S_2` would imply that the last discovery increment is at least `3`, but I have **not** proved that stronger inequality and do not count it as a result.

## 7. Exact one-state-versus-three theorem, with loops

### Theorem `NM13-09-P0245-S3STAR4-02`

Consider a reversible four-state chain with states `{0,1,2,3}` whose symmetric conductance matrix is invariant under all permutations of `{1,2,3}` fixing `0`. Up to a common positive scale, write

- center--leaf conductance `u>0`;
- leaf--leaf conductance `v>=0`;
- center self-loop conductance `r>=0`;
- each leaf self-loop conductance `s>=0`.

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\pi C\ge\frac{11}{2}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Equality holds if and only if

\[
r=s=0,\qquad u=v,
\]

i.e. the transition chain is simple random walk on `K_4`.

This family contains genuinely non-vertex-transitive chains and is therefore transverse to the preceding Cayley/vertex-transitive theorem.

### Proof

Let the conductance row sums be

\[
A=3u+r,\qquad B=u+2v+s,
\]

for the center and each leaf respectively, and let

\[
M=A+3B=r+3s+6u+6v.
\]

Thus

\[
\pi_0=A/M,\qquad \pi_1=\pi_2=\pi_3=B/M.
\]

For a target set `J`, let `K=J^c`. In unnormalized conductance coordinates define

\[
L=\operatorname{diag}(c)-C.
\]

Self-loop conductances cancel from `L`, while they remain in the row-sum vector `c`. Multiplying the standard hitting equations by the row sums gives

\[
L_K h_K=c_K.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\pi T_J
=\frac1M c_K^\top L_K^{-1}c_K.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

By the `S_3` leaf symmetry there are only six target orbits.

#### 1. One center target

For `J={0}`, the complement is the three leaves. On the constant leaf vector the killed Laplacian has eigenvalue `u`, so

\[
H_0:=\mathbb E_\pi T_{\{0\}}
=\frac{3B^2}{uM}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

#### 2. One leaf target

For `J={1}`, the complement consists of the center and two exchangeable leaves. On the symmetric two-dimensional subspace, the Dirichlet system is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
3u&-2u\\
-u&u+v
\end{pmatrix}
\binom{x}{y}
=
\binom{A}{B}.
\]

Its determinant is `u(u+3v)`. Substitution into `(Ax+2By)/M` yields

\[
H_1
=
\frac{(u+v)A^2+4uAB+6uB^2}
     {u(u+3v)M}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

#### 3. Center plus one leaf target

For `J={0,1}`, the two remaining leaves form a constant mode with killed eigenvalue `u+v`, hence

\[
H_{01}
=
\frac{2B^2}{(u+v)M}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

#### 4. Two-leaf target

For `J={1,2}`, the complement is the center plus the third leaf. The Dirichlet matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
3u&-u\\
-u&u+2v
\end{pmatrix},
\]

with determinant `2u(u+3v)`. Thus

\[
H_{12}
=
\frac{(u+2v)A^2+2uAB+3uB^2}
     {2u(u+3v)M}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

#### 5. Center plus two leaves target

For `J={0,1,2}`, only one leaf remains outside the target, giving

\[
H_{012}
=
\frac{B^2}{(u+2v)M}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

#### 6. All-leaves target

For `J={1,2,3}`, only the center remains outside, giving

\[
H_{123}
=
\frac{A^2}{3uM}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For every four-state path, target-set inclusion--exclusion gives

\[
\mathbb E_\pi C
=
\sum_{|J|=1}\mathbb E_\pi T_J
-\sum_{|J|=2}\mathbb E_\pi T_J
+\sum_{|J|=3}\mathbb E_\pi T_J.
\]

Using the orbit multiplicities,

\[
\mathbb E_\pi C
=
H_0+3H_1-3H_{01}-3H_{12}+3H_{012}+H_{123}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Substituting (7.3)--(7.8) and simplifying gives

\[
\mathbb E_\pi C-\frac{11}{2}=\frac{N}{D},
\tag{7.10}
\]

where

\[
D=
6u(u+v)(u+2v)(u+3v)(6u+6v+r+3s)>0
\tag{7.11}
\]

and the numerator has the following manifestly nonnegative decomposition:

\[
\begin{aligned}
N={}&
18(u-v)^2(u+2v)(8u^2+19uv+12v^2)\\
&+r^2(11u^3+39u^2v+40uv^2+12v^3)\\
&+rs(54u^3+162u^2v+108uv^2)\\
&+r(87u^4+306u^3v+309u^2v^2+90uv^3)\\
&+s^2(81u^3+243u^2v+198uv^2+108v^3)\\
&+s(225u^4+702u^3v+603u^2v^2+414uv^3+432v^4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.12}
\]

Every coefficient in (7.12) is nonnegative, with `u>0` and `v,r,s>=0`, so `N>=0`. This proves (7.1).

For equality, the terms linear in `r` and `s` force `r=s=0`; then the first line forces `u=v`. Conversely `u=v` and `r=s=0` is exactly simple random walk on `K_4`, whose stationary cover time is `11/2`. ∎
