## 5. New idea before code

The old search treated the four-state problem as a ten-parameter weighted-graph optimization. The new route was to ask first whether the conjectured extremizer can already be proved in a symmetry class large enough to expose the mechanism.

For a translation-invariant chain on a four-element group, stationarity is uniform and the cover time is independent of the starting vertex. With three unvisited targets, the cover time has the exact pointwise inclusion--exclusion identity

\[
C=T_x+T_y+T_z-T_{\{x,y\}}-T_{\{x,z\}}-T_{\{y,z\}}+T_{\{x,y,z\}},
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `T_A` is the first hitting time of the set `A`. The singleton sum is spectral (Kemeny's identity), while every two-target hitting time becomes a one-line geometric exit calculation. Thus the cover-time optimization collapses from a covered-set Markov chain to a rational function of two or three jump masses.

That route produced an exact sharp theorem before any checker was written.

## 7. Theorem and full proof

### Theorem `NM13-09-P0245-CAYLEY4-01`

Let `G` be a group of order four and let `(X_t)` be an irreducible reversible translation-invariant discrete-time Markov chain on `G`, with self-loops permitted. Let `C` be its cover time. Then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_\pi C\ge \frac{11}{2}}.
\]

Equality holds if and only if the chain jumps from each state uniformly to the other three states and never stays put, i.e. it is simple random walk on `K_4`.

Consequently

\[
R_{\mathrm{Cayley},4}=1.
\]

### Preliminary spectral identity

For a translation-invariant chain on a group of four elements, `pi` is uniform and translation of the whole path shows

\[
\mathbb E_\pi C=\mathbb E_e C.
\]

Let the nontrivial eigenvalues of `P` be `lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3`. The standard fundamental matrix

\[
Z=(I-P+\Pi)^{-1}-\Pi
\]

has row sums zero, constant diagonal by translation invariance, and eigenvalues `0,(1-lambda_r)^{-1}`. The hitting-time identity

\[
\mathbb E_iT_j=4(Z_{jj}-Z_{ij})
\]

therefore gives

\[
\sum_{g\ne e}\mathbb E_eT_g
 =4\,\mathrm{tr}(Z)
 =4\sum_{r=1}^3\frac1{1-\lambda_r}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

This algebraic form is valid for periodic chains as well; it does not use a convergent infinite Green-function series.

### Case 1: `G=(Z/2Z)^2`

Write the jump law as

\[
q(e)=d,\qquad q(x)=a,\quad q(y)=b,\quad q(z)=c,
\]

with

\[
s=a+b+c\le1,\qquad d=1-s.
\]

Irreducibility says the support of the positive nonidentity masses generates the group; in particular all denominators below are positive.

The three nontrivial character eigenvalues are

\[
1-2(b+c),\qquad 1-2(a+c),\qquad 1-2(a+b).
\]

Hence (7.1) yields

\[
\sum_{g\ne e}\mathbb E_eT_g
 =2\left(\frac1{a+b}+\frac1{a+c}+\frac1{b+c}\right).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For a two-point target, say `{x,y}`, the complement is `{e,z}`. From either complement state the one-step probability of exiting to `{x,y}` is exactly `a+b`; therefore

\[
\mathbb E_eT_{\{x,y\}}=\frac1{a+b}.
\]

Cyclically,

\[
\mathbb E_eT_{\{x,z\}}=\frac1{a+c},\qquad
\mathbb E_eT_{\{y,z\}}=\frac1{b+c}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The time to hit any nonidentity element is the time to leave `e`, geometric with success probability `s`, so

\[
\mathbb E_eT_{\{x,y,z\}}=\frac1s.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Substituting (7.2)--(7.4) into the exact inclusion--exclusion identity (5.1) gives

\[
\mathbb E_eC
 =\frac1{a+b}+\frac1{a+c}+\frac1{b+c}+\frac1s.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Now the three pair sums have total `2s`. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\frac1{a+b}+\frac1{a+c}+\frac1{b+c}
 \ge \frac9{2s}.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb E_eC\ge \frac{11}{2s}\ge\frac{11}{2}.
\]

Equality in the first inequality forces

`a+b=a+c=b+c`, hence `a=b=c`, and equality in the second forces `s=1`. Therefore equality occurs exactly at

\[
a=b=c=1/3,\qquad d=0.
\]

### Case 2: `G=Z/4Z`

Reversibility forces the symmetric increment law

\[
q(1)=q(3)=a,\qquad q(2)=b,\qquad q(0)=d,
\]

with

\[
s=2a+b\le1,\qquad d=1-s.
\]

Irreducibility forces `a>0`.

The nontrivial eigenvalues are

\[
1-2(a+b),\qquad 1-4a,\qquad 1-2(a+b).
\]

So (7.1) gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^3\mathbb E_0T_j
 =\frac4{a+b}+\frac1a.
\tag{7.6}
\]

The target pair `{1,3}` is exited into from its complement `{0,2}` with constant probability `2a`, hence

\[
\mathbb E_0T_{\{1,3\}}=\frac1{2a}.
\]

For the other two pairs the exit probability from the two-state complement is `a+b`, so

\[
\mathbb E_0T_{\{1,2\}}
 =\mathbb E_0T_{\{2,3\}}
 =\frac1{a+b}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Finally

\[
\mathbb E_0T_{\{1,2,3\}}=\frac1s.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Using (5.1), (7.6)--(7.8),

\[
\mathbb E_0C
 =\frac2{a+b}+\frac1{2a}+\frac1s.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Since `s-a=a+b`, direct subtraction factors exactly as

\[
\mathbb E_0C-\frac{11}{2s}
 =\frac{(s-3a)^2}{2as(s-a)}\ge0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Therefore again

\[
\mathbb E_0C\ge\frac{11}{2s}\ge\frac{11}{2}.
\]

Equality forces `s=1` and `s=3a`, hence

\[
a=b=1/3,\qquad d=0,
\]

which is exactly simple random walk on `K_4`.

This proves the theorem.

### Corollary: vertex-transitive four-state chains

Every transitive subgroup of `S_4` contains a regular subgroup isomorphic to `C_4` or `V_4` (equivalently, the transitive subgroup list is `C_4,V_4,D_8,A_4,S_4`). Hence a reversible four-state chain whose transition automorphism group is vertex-transitive is invariant under a regular group of order four and is one of the Cayley chains above. Therefore the same `11/2` lower bound and equality characterization hold for every vertex-transitive reversible four-state chain.
