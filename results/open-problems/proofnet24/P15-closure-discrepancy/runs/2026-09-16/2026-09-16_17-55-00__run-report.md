## 3. Exact source statement and current-status check

Primary source checked directly on 2026-09-16:

Antonio Agresti and Mark Veraar, *Nonlinear SPDEs and Maximal Regularity: An Extended Survey*, arXiv:2501.18561v5 / NoDEA 32 (2025), article 123.

The open-problems section says, immediately before Problem 1, that several concrete cases with nonzero `B` are known but that there is no satisfactory operator-theoretic condition covering most of them. It notes the elementary small-`B` fixed-point implication and calls that theory usually unsatisfactory. The exact problem is:

> **Problem 1 (Sufficient conditions for stochastic maximal regularity).** Find reasonable sufficient abstract conditions for stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity of `(A,B)`.

The source framework is the linear equation

\[
du+Au\,dt=f\,dt+(Bu+g)\,dW,
\]

with the source pair scale `X_1 -> X_0` and stochastic data in the half-order space.

### 3.1 Closest known abstract transformation theorem

Portal and Veraar (2019), *Stochastic maximal regularity for rough time-dependent problems*, Section 3.5, is load-bearing prior art. Its Assumption 3.17 takes finitely many group generators `B_j` and explicitly requires the groups `e^{sB_i}` and `e^{tB_j}` to **commute pairwise**. Its Theorem 3.18 uses a Doss--Sussmann transform and proves an equivalence between stochastic maximal regularity of `(A,B)` and a zero-multiplicative-noise problem with the quadratic correction

\[
\widetilde A=A+\frac12[B,B].
\]

The 2025 source itself cites this transformation route in its roadmap. Therefore the obvious idea “use a commuting group transform” is **KNOWN** and earns no metric credit here.

### 3.2 `p`-dependent coercivity is also known at the moment/variational level

Gnann--Hoogendijk--Veraar (2024), *Higher order moments for SPDE with monotone nonlinearities*, introduces a `p`-dependent coercivity condition and proves it optimal for higher moments in the variational framework. This is highly relevant to the radial-versus-skew calculation in Section 4, but it is not a general stochastic-maximal-regularity criterion for Problem 1.

## 4. Move #2 + #24: attack a naive criterion and find the extremal orientation

Before trying to prove a sufficient condition, I attacked the most tempting `p=2`-type abstraction.

Consider the exact real two-dimensional linear SDE

\[
dX_t=-aX_t\,dt+BX_t\,dW_t,
\qquad
B=sI+kJ,
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
B^*B=(s^2+k^2)I,
\qquad
\langle x,Bx\rangle=s\|x\|^2.
\]

### Claim `P15-I0066-RADIAL-SKEW-DIAGNOSTIC-01` — PROVED

For every real `p>=2`, Itô's formula gives

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\,\mathbb E\|X_t\|^p
=
 p\left[-a+\frac12\{k^2+(p-1)s^2\}\right]
 \mathbb E\|X_t\|^p.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\|X_t\|^p
=
\|X_0\|^p
\exp\left(
 p\left[-a+\frac{k^2+(p-1)s^2}{2}\right]t
\right).
}
\]

#### Proof

For `F(x)=||x||^p`,

\[
DF(x)h=p\|x\|^{p-2}\langle x,h\rangle,
\]

and

\[
D^2F(x)[h,h]
=p\|x\|^{p-2}\|h\|^2
+p(p-2)\|x\|^{p-4}\langle x,h\rangle^2.
\]

Substituting `h=Bx`, using

\[
\|Bx\|^2=(s^2+k^2)\|x\|^2,
\qquad
\langle x,Bx\rangle=s\|x\|^2,
\]

gives drift

\[
p\|x\|^p
\left[-a+\frac12(s^2+k^2)+\frac{p-2}{2}s^2\right]
=
p\|x\|^p
\left[-a+\frac12\{k^2+(p-1)s^2\}\right].
\]

The local martingale has zero expectation after the standard localization, and the resulting scalar ODE gives the formula. QED.

### Exact consequences

For fixed `B^*B=r^2 I`, the `p`-moment penalty is

\[
r^2+(p-2)s^2.
\]

It is maximal for purely radial/symmetric noise `|s|=r,k=0` and minimal for purely skew noise `s=0,|k|=r`.

Thus for `p>2`, the two matrices

\[
B_{\rm radial}=I,
\qquad
B_{\rm skew}=J
\]

have the **same** `B^*B=I` but different exact `p`-moment stability thresholds. With `a=1`:

- radial: drift coefficient is `-1` at `p=2` but `+2` at `p=4`;
- skew: drift coefficient is `-1` at `p=2` and `-2` at `p=4`.

So any proposed sharp higher-`p` criterion that sees only `A` and `B^*B` loses essential geometry. The missing quantity is the radial/numerical-range term `|<x,Bx>|^2`, exactly the kind of `p`-dependent structure that appears in the known higher-moment coercivity literature.

This is a diagnostic obstruction, **not** a refutation of Agresti--Veraar Problem 1.

## 5. Move #18: tensor-lift language and why it does not yet solve SMR

For a finite-dimensional linear homogeneous SDE

\[
dX=-AX\,dt+BX\,dW
\]

and an integer `m>=2`, the tensor process `X^{\otimes m}` gives a deterministic lifted operator after expectation.

### Claim `P15-I0066-TENSOR-LIFT-01` — PROVED

Let `A_i` denote `A` acting on tensor slot `i` and let `B_i` denote `B` acting on slot `i`. Then

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E[X_t^{\otimes m}]
=
\mathcal T_m\,\mathbb E[X_t^{\otimes m}],
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_m
=-\sum_{i=1}^m A_i
+\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}B_iB_j.
}
\]

#### Proof

Apply the Itô product rule to the `m` tensor factors. A drift contribution `-A_i` arises when the drift is taken in slot `i`. A quadratic-variation contribution `B_iB_j` arises for every unordered pair of distinct slots `i<j`. The terms with one stochastic differential sum to

\[
\left(\sum_i B_i\right)X^{\otimes m}\,dW,
\]

whose expectation is zero after localization. Taking expectations yields the displayed ODE. QED.

For a scalar real equation this recovers the exact threshold

\[
-ma+\binom m2 b^2<0
\iff
b^2<\frac{2a}{m-1}.
\]

### Why this route stops short

The lift is useful because it turns multiplicative-noise dependence into an ordinary deterministic spectral problem. But it does **not** yet solve Problem 1:

1. source SMR is an inhomogeneous adapted estimate, not only homogeneous moment stability;
2. `||X||^m` is not, in general Banach geometry, a fixed linear functional of `X^{\otimes m}`;
3. the source needs arbitrary real `p>2`, not only integer tensor powers;
4. unbounded `A,B` and the `X_1/X_{1/2}/X_0` scale must be controlled.

So I retain the tensor lift as a route to necessary spectral diagnostics, not as metric credit.

## 6. Move #14: noncommuting compact-Lie stochastic-flow criterion

The known Portal--Veraar transform is abelian at the noise-generator level: their Assumption 3.17 requires pairwise commuting groups. The natural question is whether commutation is a proof convenience or a genuine barrier.

The group action gives a clean replacement: use the **group-valued stochastic flow itself**, rather than writing one exponential of a sum of commuting generators.

### Claim `P15-I0066-NONCOMMUTING-LIE-FLOW-01` — PROVED / PARTIAL source answer

Fix a finite horizon `T`, a source-admissible `p>=2` and time weight parameter `kappa`. Let

\[
X_1\hookrightarrow X_0,
\qquad
X_{1/2}=[X_0,X_1]_{1/2}.
\]

Let `G` be a finite-dimensional compact Lie group and let

\[
\pi:G\to\mathcal L(X_0)\cap\mathcal L(X_1)
\]

be a strongly continuous representation by isometries on both `X_0` and `X_1`. Hence, by interpolation, each `pi(g)` is also an isometry on `X_{1/2}`.

Choose Lie-algebra directions `xi_1,...,xi_J` and let `B_j` be the infinitesimal generator of `t -> pi(exp(t xi_j))`. Assume:

1. `X_{1/2} subset D(B_j)` and the restriction
   \[
   B_j:X_{1/2}\to X_0
   \]
   is bounded;
2. `X_1 subset D(B_j^2)` and
   \[
   B_j:X_1\to X_{1/2}
   \]
   is bounded;
3. if `G_t` is the right-invariant Stratonovich Brownian flow generated by the chosen `xi_j` and
   \[
   S_t:=\pi(G_t),
   \]
   then for `x in X_1` the standard strong Itô identity holds in `X_0`,
   \[
   dS_t x=C S_tx\,dt+\sum_{j=1}^J B_jS_tx\,dW_t^j,
   \qquad
   C:=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^J B_j^2;
   \tag{6.1}
   \]
   equivalently, one may impose the usual invariant-core/dual-domain hypotheses that justify this identity, as in the group-transform proof of Portal--Veraar;
4. `A in L(X_1,X_0)` and
   \[
   L:=A+C
   \tag{6.2}
   \]
   commutes with the representation on `X_1`:
   \[
   L\pi(g)x=\pi(g)Lx
   \qquad(g\in G,\ x\in X_1);
   \tag{6.3}
   \]
5. the zero-multiplicative-noise pair `(L,0)` has stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity in the exact source sense on the same scale and horizon.

Define finite-dimensional multiplicative noise

\[
Bx=(B_1x,\ldots,B_Jx)
\in\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2}).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{(A,B)\in\mathrm{SMR}_{p,\kappa}(T).}
\]

No smallness assumption on `B` is used, and the generators `B_i,B_j` need **not commute**.

### Proof

Take arbitrary source-admissible progressively measurable data

\[
f\in L^p(\Omega\times(0,T),w_\kappa;X_0),
\qquad
 g=(g_1,\ldots,g_J)
\in L^p(\Omega\times(0,T),w_\kappa;\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2})).
\]

Put `T_t=S_t^{-1}`. Since `pi(G)` is isometric on `X_0`, `X_1` and `X_{1/2}`, both `S_t` and `T_t` have operator norm one on these spaces.

Define transformed data

\[
\widehat g_j(t):=T_tg_j(t),
\tag{6.4}
\]

and

\[
\widehat f(t):=T_tf(t)-\sum_{j=1}^J T_tB_jg_j(t).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The assumptions `B_j:X_{1/2}->X_0` and finite `J` give

\[
\|\widehat f\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_0)}
\le
\|f\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_0)}
+C_B\|g\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2}))},
\tag{6.6}
\]

for a finite constant `C_B` depending only on the `B_j` and `J`. By the operator ideal property of `gamma`-radonifying norms and the isometry of `T_t`,

\[
\|\widehat g\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2}))}
\le
\|g\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2}))}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

By assumption 5, the zero-multiplicative-noise equation

\[
dV+LV\,dt=\widehat f\,dt+\widehat g\,dW,
\qquad V(0)=0,
\tag{6.8}
\]

has a unique strong solution with the source SMR estimate

\[
\|V\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_1)}
\le
K_L
\left(
\|\widehat f\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_0)}
+
\|\widehat g\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;\gamma(\mathbb R^J,X_{1/2}))}
\right).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Set

\[
U_t:=S_tV_t.
\tag{6.10}
\]

We now compute the equation for `U`. On the invariant core, (6.1), (6.8), and Itô's product rule give

\[
\begin{aligned}
dU
&=(dS)V+S(dV)+(dS)(dV)\\
&=
\left[
CSV-SLV+S\widehat f
+\sum_j B_jS\widehat g_j
\right]dt\\
&\quad+
\sum_j\left[B_jSV+S\widehat g_j\right]dW^j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.11}
\]

By (6.3), `SLV=LSV=LU`. By (6.4), `S\widehat g_j=g_j`. By (6.5),

\[
S\widehat f=f-\sum_j B_jg_j.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Therefore the two `B_jg_j` drift terms in (6.11) cancel exactly. Since `L=A+C`, the remaining drift is

\[
CU-LU+f=-AU+f.
\tag{6.13}
\]

The stochastic term is

\[
\sum_j(B_jU+g_j)dW^j.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Thus `U` is a strong solution of

\[
dU+AU\,dt=f\,dt+(BU+g)\,dW.
\tag{6.15}
\]

The core calculation extends to the strong solution by approximation in the graph/half-order norms; the bounded restrictions in assumptions 1--2 make every drift and quadratic-variation term continuous in the required spaces. Equivalently, one may perform (6.11) after dual pairing with the common invariant dual core, exactly as in the standard group-transform proof.

Finally, `S_t` is an isometry on `X_1`, so

\[
\|U\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_1)}
=
\|V\|_{L^p(w_\kappa;X_1)}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

Combining (6.6)--(6.9) gives the source SMR estimate for `(A,B)`. Uniqueness follows by applying the inverse transform to the difference of two solutions, reducing it to uniqueness for (6.8). The same calculation works on the source's stopped intervals because the transform is adapted, invertible and isometric and zero initial data remains zero after transformation. QED.

### Why this is not the already-known commuting theorem

Portal--Veraar Assumption 3.17 requires

\[
e^{sB_i}e^{tB_j}=e^{tB_j}e^{sB_i}
\quad\text{for all }i,j,s,t.
\]

The theorem above imposes no such relation. Noncommutativity is absorbed into the actual `G`-valued stochastic flow. What replaces pairwise commutation is the invariant/Casimir condition (6.3) on `L=A+C`.

This is therefore a distinct sufficient-condition family. It is deliberately not claimed to cover “most” concrete cases from the survey.

### Finite exact noncommuting sanity example

For the standard `so(3)` generators `L_1,L_2,L_3`, the exact checker verifies

\[
[L_1,L_2]=L_3\ne0,
\qquad
L_1^2+L_2^2+L_3^2=-2I.
\]

Thus the quadratic correction is central although the noise directions do not commute. This is the finite-dimensional algebra behind the compact-Lie/Casimir mechanism. It is a sanity example, not the reason the abstract theorem is true.

### Metric decision

This theorem is one nonzero-`B`, non-small, abstract sufficient-condition family with a complete proof and a current prior-art comparison. Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_{\rm nonzero}:0\to1.}
\]

The metric counts a source-valid family proved in this track. It does **not** assert publication priority, does not close Problem 1, and does not imply that no earlier paper contains an equivalent noncommuting-flow theorem under different language.
