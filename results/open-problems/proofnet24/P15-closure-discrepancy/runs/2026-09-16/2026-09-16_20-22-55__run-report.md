## 2. Exact source and current literature/status check

The source problem is the linear stochastic evolution equation

\[
du+Au\,dt=f\,dt+(Bu+g)\,dW,
\]

on a Banach couple `X_1 -> X_0`, with stochastic data at the half-order level. Immediately before Problem 1, Agresti–Veraar note that several concrete nonzero-`B` cases are known but no satisfactory abstract operator-theoretic condition covers most of them. They also record the elementary perturbative implication: if `(A,0)` has stochastic maximal regularity and the critical `X_1 -> gamma(U,X_{1/2})` norm of `B` is sufficiently small, then `(A,B)` has the pair property by fixed point. They call that theory usually unsatisfactory. The exact open problem is:

> **Problem 1.** Find reasonable sufficient abstract conditions for stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity of `(A,B)`.

Primary source rechecked 2026-09-16: Agresti–Veraar, NoDEA 32 (2025), article 123, arXiv:2501.18561v5.

# 4. NEW-METHOD #3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER

The compact-Lie hypothesis was useful because compact group actions give uniform operator bounds and a clean stochastic flow. The proof mechanism itself needs less: an already-constructed invertible stochastic flow that is uniformly bounded on the source scale and whose Itô-corrected drift is invariant.

This leads to the following transfer theorem.

## Claim `P15-I0066-BOUNDED-NONCOMMUTING-FLOW-04` — PROVED

Fix `p in (2,infinity)`, a source-admissible weight exponent `kappa`, and a finite time interval `(0,T)`. Let

\[
X_1\hookrightarrow X_0,
\qquad X_{1/2}=[X_0,X_1]_{1/2},
\]

be a source Banach couple for which stochastic integration is available, and take finite-dimensional noise `H=R^m` with independent Brownian coordinates `W^1,...,W^m`.

Let

\[
B_j:X_1\to X_{1/2},\qquad B_j:X_{1/2}\to X_0
\]

be bounded for `j=1,...,m`, and put

\[
C:=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^m B_j^2\in\mathcal L(X_1,X_0).
\]

Suppose there is an operator `L in L(X_1,X_0)` and set

\[
A:=L-C.
\]

Assume:

1. `(L,0)` has the source base stochastic maximal `L^p_kappa`-regularity on `(0,T)`.
2. There is an adapted operator flow `S_t`, `S_0=I`, such that on a common invariant core and hence by closure in the strong-solution formulation,
   \[
   dS_t x=C S_t x\,dt+\sum_{j=1}^m B_jS_t x\,dW_t^j.
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
3. Each `S_t` is an isomorphism of `X_0`, `X_{1/2}`, and `X_1`, with deterministic two-sided bounds
   \[
   \sup_{t,\omega}\big(\|S_t\|_{X_r\to X_r}+\|S_t^{-1}\|_{X_r\to X_r}\big)\le M_r<\infty,
   \qquad r\in\{0,1/2,1\}.
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
4. The corrected drift is invariant under the flow:
   \[
   S_tLx=LS_tx,
   \qquad x\in X_1.
   \tag{4.3}
   \]

No commutation assumption is imposed between `B_i` and `B_j`.

Then the pair

\[
B x=(B_1x,\ldots,B_mx)
\]

has source base stochastic maximal regularity with `A`:

\[
\boxed{(A,B)\in SMR_{p,\kappa}(T).}
\]

### Proof

Take admissible progressively measurable data `f` and `g=(g_1,...,g_m)` in the source deterministic and stochastic data spaces, initially on a dense class on which the product calculation is classical. Define transformed data

\[
F_t:=S_t^{-1}\left(f_t-\sum_{j=1}^m B_jg_{j,t}\right),
\qquad
G_{j,t}:=S_t^{-1}g_{j,t}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The half-order hypothesis `B_j:X_{1/2}->X_0` makes the sum in `F` an `X_0`-valued process. The deterministic bounds (4.2) give

\[
\|F\|_{L^p_\kappa(X_0)}
\le
M_0\left(
\|f\|_{L^p_\kappa(X_0)}
+
\sum_j\|B_j\|_{X_{1/2}\to X_0}
\|g_j\|_{L^p_\kappa(X_{1/2})}
\right),
\tag{4.5}
\]

and the analogous bound for `G` in the source gamma-radonifying stochastic-data space. Adaptedness is preserved because `S_t^{-1}` is adapted.

By `(L,0)` stochastic maximal regularity there is a unique strong solution `v` of

\[
dv+Lv\,dt=F\,dt+\sum_{j=1}^mG_j\,dW^j.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Set

\[
u_t=S_tv_t.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The Itô product rule gives

\[
du=S\,dv+(dS)v+d[S,v].
\]

From (4.1) and (4.6), the quadratic-covariation term is exactly

\[
d[S,v]_t
=
\sum_{j=1}^m B_jS_tG_{j,t}\,dt
=
\sum_{j=1}^m B_jg_{j,t}\,dt.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The stochastic term is

\[
\sum_j\big(S_tG_{j,t}+B_jS_tv_t\big)dW_t^j
=
\sum_j(g_{j,t}+B_ju_t)dW_t^j.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The drift is

\[
-S_tLv_t
+f_t-\sum_jB_jg_{j,t}
+CS_tv_t
+\sum_jB_jg_{j,t}.
\]

Using (4.3) and `A=L-C`, this equals

\[
-Lu_t+Cu_t+f_t=-Au_t+f_t.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus `u` is a strong solution of

\[
du+Au\,dt=f\,dt+\sum_j(B_ju+g_j)dW^j.
\]

The solution estimate follows from the additive estimate for `v`, (4.2), and (4.5): multiplication by `S_t` costs at most the fixed scale constant `M_1` in the `L^p_kappa(X_1)` solution norm.

For uniqueness, let `w` solve the homogeneous full equation. The inverse flow `R_t=S_t^{-1}` satisfies the standard inverse Itô identity

\[
dR_t=R_tC\,dt-\sum_jR_tB_j\,dW_t^j.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Indeed this is forced by differentiating `R_tS_t=I` and using `sum_j B_j^2=2C`. Applying the product rule to `R_tw_t`, the diffusion terms cancel and the quadratic term subtracts `sum_jR_tB_j^2w_tdt`, leaving

\[
d(R_tw_t)=-R_t(A+C)w_t\,dt=-L(R_tw_t)\,dt,
\]

where (4.3) is used again. Additive uniqueness gives `R_tw_t=0`, hence `w=0`.

Density/closure in the source strong-solution spaces extends the calculation from the common core to general admissible data. This proves the claim. QED.

### Scope limitation

This theorem proves the **base** SMR property. I do **not** silently promote it to every `SMR^bullet` temporal-fractional estimate: multiplication by a Brownian stochastic flow is rough in time, so preservation of the source's stronger temporal regularity requires an additional argument not supplied here.

### Why this is strictly stronger than the old compact-Lie hypothesis

The proof uses neither compactness of an underlying group nor pairwise commutation of the generators. It only uses the actual bounded invertible flow, the source-scale mapping properties, and corrected-drift invariance.

It is also strictly different from the Portal–Veraar 2019 theorem at the hypothesis level: their Assumption 3.17 explicitly requires pairwise commuting groups. Section 6 below gives a critical-order example with a nonzero commutator that cannot be removed by rotating the Brownian coordinates.

Again, this is recorded as a **strengthening of metric family 1**, not as metric family 4.

# 5. NEW-METHOD #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED

The correct invariant is not the individual generator norm. Two algebraic quantities survive an orthogonal change of Brownian coordinates in a way that exposes exactly what the commuting theory cannot see.

Take `m=2`. For an orthogonal matrix

\[
O=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
\]

set

\[
\widetilde B_1=aB_1+bB_2,
\qquad
\widetilde B_2=cB_1+dB_2.
\]

## Claim `P15-I0066-NOISE-ROTATION-INVARIANTS-04` — PROVED

One has the exact identities

\[
\widetilde B_1^2+\widetilde B_2^2=B_1^2+B_2^2
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
[\widetilde B_1,\widetilde B_2]
=(ad-bc)[B_1,B_2]
=\det(O)[B_1,B_2].
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Expanding the ordered products,

\[
\sum_{r=1}^2\widetilde B_r^2
=
\sum_{j,k=1}^2
\left(\sum_{r=1}^2O_{rj}O_{rk}\right)B_jB_k
=
\sum_{j=1}^2B_j^2,
\]

because `O^T O=I`. For the commutator, bilinearity and `[B_j,B_j]=0` give

\[
[aB_1+bB_2,cB_1+dB_2]
=(ad-bc)[B_1,B_2].
\]

QED.

### Consequence

If `[B_1,B_2] != 0`, **no orthogonal rotation of the two Brownian directions can make the generators commute**. At the same time the Itô correction

\[
C=\frac12(B_1^2+B_2^2)
\]

is unchanged.

Therefore the tempting reduction “rotate the noise basis until Portal–Veraar's commuting hypothesis applies” fails exactly. The quadratic correction alone also forgets a real part of the geometry: it is blind to the sign/nonzero status of the commutator.

This is the conserved obstruction used in the critical example below.

# 6. NEW-METHOD #30 — ONE SPECTRAL SCALE AT A TIME: a critical Clifford-noise example

The abstract theorem would be much less useful if it only covered bounded lower-order noise, because lower-order arbitrary-size perturbations are already standard. I therefore looked for a **critical half-order** example where the two noise generators are noncommuting and their Lie closure is not finite-dimensional.

The Pauli/Clifford algebra gives one.

Let

\[
\sigma_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\sigma_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\ i&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\sigma_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then

\[
\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2^2=I,
\qquad
[\sigma_1,\sigma_2]=2i\sigma_3.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
X_0=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb C^2),
\]

and define the positive self-adjoint diagonal operator

\[
\Lambda x=(n^2x_n)_{n\ge1}.
\]

Use the invertible Hilbert scale

\[
X_1=D(\Lambda),
\qquad
X_{1/2}=D(\Lambda^{1/2}),
\]

with the equivalent homogeneous norms `||x||_{X_1}=||Lambda x||` and `||x||_{X_{1/2}}=||Lambda^{1/2}x||`.

Fix `alpha>0` and an arbitrary amplitude `tau>0`. Define

\[
B_1=i\tau\sigma_1\Lambda^{1/2},
\qquad
B_2=i\tau\sigma_2\Lambda^{1/2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because the Pauli matrices act only in the spin coordinate and commute with `Lambda`,

\[
B_1^2=B_2^2=-\tau^2\Lambda,
\qquad
C:=\frac12(B_1^2+B_2^2)=-\tau^2\Lambda.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Set

\[
A_\tau=(\alpha+\tau^2)\Lambda,
\qquad
L:=A_\tau+C=\alpha\Lambda.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The corrected drift is independent of `tau`.

## Claim `P15-I0066-CRITICAL-CLIFFORD-FLOW-04` — PROVED

For every `tau>0`, the pair

\[
\left((\alpha+\tau^2)\Lambda,
\left(i\tau\sigma_1\Lambda^{1/2},\,i\tau\sigma_2\Lambda^{1/2}\right)
\right)
\]

satisfies the base source stochastic maximal-regularity property for every exponent/weight for which the positive self-adjoint additive operator `alpha Lambda` has the corresponding source SMR property.

Moreover:

1. the noise is genuinely critical-order:
   \[
   B_j:X_1\to X_{1/2},\qquad B_j:X_{1/2}\to X_0;
   \]
2. its critical norm is not small; in the homogeneous Hilbert norms,
   \[
   \|B:X_1\to\gamma(\mathbb R^2,X_{1/2})\|=\sqrt2\,\tau\to\infty;
   \tag{6.5}
   \]
3. the generators do not commute:
   \[
   [B_1,B_2]=-2i\tau^2\sigma_3\Lambda\ne0;
   \tag{6.6}
   \]
4. no orthogonal change of Brownian basis can make them commute, by (5.2);
5. their generated Lie algebra is infinite-dimensional on the common finite-support core, so this example is not contained in the previous finite-dimensional compact-Lie hypothesis.

### Proof

#### Step 1: critical mapping norms

For `x in X_1`, using commutation with `Lambda`,

\[
\|B_jx\|_{X_{1/2}}
=
\tau\|\Lambda^{1/2}\sigma_j\Lambda^{1/2}x\|
=
\tau\|\Lambda x\|
=
\tau\|x\|_{X_1}.
\]

The same calculation one half-step lower gives `B_j:X_{1/2}->X_0`. Since the noise space is `R^2` and the target is Hilbert, the gamma norm is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, so

\[
\|Bx\|_{\gamma(\mathbb R^2,X_{1/2})}^2
=
\sum_{j=1}^2\|B_jx\|_{X_{1/2}}^2
=2\tau^2\|x\|_{X_1}^2.
\]

This proves (6.5).

#### Step 2: build the noncommuting stochastic flow mode by mode

On the `n`-th spectral block `C^2`, solve the Stratonovich matrix SDE

\[
dS_n
=i\tau n\sigma_1S_n\circ dW^1
+i\tau n\sigma_2S_n\circ dW^2,
\qquad S_n(0)=I_2.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Both coefficients are skew-Hermitian. The Stratonovich product rule gives

\[
d(S_n^*S_n)=0,
\]

hence

\[
S_n(t)^*S_n(t)=I_2
\]

for every `n,t` almost surely. Thus every block flow is unitary.

Define

\[
S_t=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}S_n(t).
\]

Because each block is unitary and `Lambda` is the scalar `n^2I_2` on that block, `S_t` is an isometry on `X_0`, `X_{1/2}`, and `X_1`, its inverse has the same norm, and

\[
S_t\Lambda=\Lambda S_t.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Converting (6.7) to Itô form gives block drift

\[
\frac12\big((i\tau n\sigma_1)^2+(i\tau n\sigma_2)^2\big)
=-\tau^2n^2I_2,
\]

which is exactly the block of `C=-tau^2 Lambda`. Hence the direct-sum flow satisfies the operator identity required in Claim 4.

#### Step 3: corrected drift and additive SMR

By (6.4),

\[
L=\alpha\Lambda.
\]

It is positive self-adjoint with bounded `H^infinity` calculus of angle zero on the Hilbert space `X_0`; it therefore has the standard additive stochastic maximal regularity in the source range. By (6.8), `L` commutes with `S_t`.

All hypotheses of `P15-I0066-BOUNDED-NONCOMMUTING-FLOW-04` are now satisfied, and the pair `(A_tau,B)` has base source SMR.

#### Step 4: noncommutation and infinite-dimensional Lie closure

From (6.1),

\[
[B_1,B_2]
=-2i\tau^2\sigma_3\Lambda\ne0.
\]

Repeated commutators with `B_1` or `B_2` generate, up to nonzero scalar factors, operators of the forms

\[
\sigma_a\Lambda^{k/2},\qquad k=1,2,3,\ldots.
\]

These are linearly independent on the common finite-support core: a finite linear dependence would give a nonzero polynomial vanishing at every spectral value `n`, which is impossible. Thus the generated Lie algebra is infinite-dimensional. In particular the example is not a representation of the finite-dimensional compact-Lie setup used by P15 in family 1.

This proves the claim. QED.

### A useful exact balance identity

Since `B_j^*B_j=tau^2 Lambda`,

\[
2A_\tau-\sum_{j=1}^2B_j^*B_j
=2(\alpha+\tau^2)\Lambda-2\tau^2\Lambda
=2\alpha\Lambda>0.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus the family keeps a fixed positive stochastic-parabolicity margin while the critical noise norm tends to infinity. The point of the example is not that Hilbert coercivity was unknown; it is that the noncommuting critical noise admits an exact stochastic-flow explanation compatible with all `p` in the additive SMR range, while neither pairwise commuting-group reduction nor small-`B` perturbation is available.

## 8. What failed / what was learned

### Failed route A: count the noncompact-flow extension as a fourth metric family

Rejected deliberately. The new theorem changes the hypothesis class substantially, but its proof is still the stochastic-flow conjugation mechanism of family 1. The metric says “mathematically distinct,” so the conservative accounting is `3 -> 3`.

### Failed route B: rotate the Brownian coordinates until the generators commute

Disproved exactly by (5.2). For two generators, an orthogonal rotation multiplies the commutator only by `det(O)=+/-1`; noncommutation survives every such change of noise basis.

### Failed route C: use only the quadratic correction `C`

Also inadequate as a classification principle. Equation (5.1) shows that `C` is invariant under orthogonal noise rotations, while (5.2) retains a separate noncommutative obstruction. The Clifford example has an especially simple scalar correction `C=-tau^2 Lambda` but a nonzero, increasingly high-order Lie commutator tower.

### Why the new theorem is not tautological

The condition is not “assume `(A,B)` has SMR.” It asks for an independently checkable operator flow with deterministic two-sided scale bounds plus additive SMR of the corrected drift. In the Clifford family all these objects are explicit: the flow is blockwise unitary, the corrected drift is `alpha Lambda`, and the critical noise norm is exactly `sqrt(2) tau`.

The remaining weakness is real: constructing such a bounded flow for a generic noncommuting critical `B` is hard. The theorem isolates that construction problem instead of solving Problem 1 in full.

or `G_*` has Fiedler-order cutwidth at most `d(2d-1)`.

### Independent audit

Let `G` be an exact `mu`-minimal connected simple `d`-regular graph and `f` a unit mean-zero Fiedler vector, ordered nonincreasingly by `pi`.

Suppose the current graph has edge-connectivity at least 3 and some Fiedler prefix has more than `d(2d-1)` crossing edges. P16's matching argument then gives two crossing matching edges

\[
u_iv_i,\qquad u_jv_j
\]

for which both same-side pairs `u_i u_j` and `v_i v_j` are nonedges. Since edge connectivity is at least 3, deleting the two crossing edges cannot disconnect the graph. Adding the two same-side edges therefore produces another connected simple `d`-regular graph `H`.

The one-dimensional Monge identity gives

\[
(f(u_i)-f(v_i))^2+(f(u_j)-f(v_j))^2
\ge
(f(u_i)-f(u_j))^2+(f(v_i)-f(v_j))^2.
\]

Hence

\[
E_H(f)\le E_G(f)=\mu(G).
\]

Because `G` is a **global** exact minimizer among connected simple `d`-regular graphs,

\[
\mu(G)\le\mu(H)\le E_H(f)\le\mu(G).
\]

Every inequality is therefore equality. In particular

\[
\mu(H)=\mu(G)=E_H(f),
\]

so `H` is another exact minimizer and the same vector `f` is still a Fiedler vector of `H`.

Now compare the integer span

\[
L_\pi(G)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}|\pi(u)-\pi(v)|.
\]

The two removed edges cross the chosen prefix and the two new edges stay on their respective sides. Writing the two left positions as `a,b` and the two right positions as `c,d`, with `a,b <= k < c,d`, the uncrossing replacement strictly lowers the sum of the two edge spans. The decrease is a positive even integer after ordering the two left and right endpoints; in particular it is at least 2. Thus

\[
L_\pi(H)<L_\pi(G).
\]

The graph remains in the finite set of labelled connected simple `d`-regular exact minimizers, while the nonnegative integer `L_pi` strictly decreases at each switch. Therefore the process terminates after finitely many steps. At termination either edge connectivity has fallen to at most 2, or no prefix violates the `d(2d-1)` matching/cutwidth bound. This is exactly the stated dichotomy.

I checked the two possible hidden gaps explicitly:

1. **Does `f` remain a Fiedler vector after a zero-energy switch?** Yes: the global-minimum/Rayleigh sandwich above forces `mu(H)=E_H(f)`, not merely `mu(H)<=E_H(f)`.
2. **Can the descent cycle?** No: `L_pi` is a nonnegative integer and strictly decreases at every performed switch while the same `f,pi` remain admissible.
