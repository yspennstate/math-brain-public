## 3. Ideas written before code: the three new routes

The previous run ended at the smallest obstruction to the acyclic theorem: a directed two-block feedback cycle. I did not add another triangular lemma. I attacked that cycle three different ways before writing any checker.

### Move #5 — ADD A PARAMETER

For a two-block cycle, rescale the two directions reciprocally:

\[
B_{12}\mapsto \tau B_{12},\qquad B_{21}\mapsto \tau^{-1}B_{21},\qquad \tau>0.
\]

The one-way feedback operators scale, but their round trip does not. This immediately suggests that a criterion phrased in terms of the round trip can be genuinely non-small: one coupling norm can tend to infinity while the criterion remains exactly unchanged.

A second parameter is a common amplitude `z`, which makes the round trip scale by `z^2`. This turns the safe-amplitude question into a resolvent problem for one operator rather than a small-`B` estimate.

### Move #20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS

The tempting next route was compactness plus the Fredholm alternative: stochastic integration looks smoothing in time, so perhaps the feedback operator is compact, and injectivity would then give invertibility. This is false even for scalar finite-dimensional state. Section 7 gives an exact proof that the scalar stochastic Volterra feedback operator is **noncompact** on its natural `L^2` process space. Thus compact-Fredholm is not the general missing theorem.

The useful replacement is not compactness but **causal/Volterra composition and exact Schur algebra**.

### Move #27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

Rather than ask only whether one fixed `(A,B)` has SMR, ask for which amplitudes `z` the pair `(A,zB)` is certified. In the two-block cycle the sufficient condition becomes

\[
I-z^2\mathcal C\mathcal D\quad\text{invertible}.
\]

Thus the guaranteed amplitude set is governed by the resolvent of one round-trip operator. This is a different object from `||zB||<epsilon` and makes explicit what repeated feedback, not one-step size, controls.

## 4. Abstract source-compatible feedback setup

Fix any source-admissible parameters `(p,kappa,T)` and whichever source SMR flavor is being asserted. For `r=1,2`, let

\[
X_1^{(r)}\hookrightarrow X_0^{(r)}
\]

be the corresponding Banach couples and assume the additive pairs `(A_r,0)` have the desired stochastic maximal regularity.

Let `E_r` denote the corresponding adapted zero-initial solution space equipped with the norm that occurs in that SMR estimate, and let

\[
G_r=L^p_\kappa(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}^{(r)}))
\]

be the stochastic-data space. Additive SMR supplies a bounded stochastic solution map

\[
\mathscr S_r^g:G_r\to E_r,
\]

where `S_r^g h` is the zero-initial solution of

\[
dv+A_rv\,dt=h\,dW.
\]

Assume bounded cross-noise maps

\[
B_{12}:X_1^{(2)}\to\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}^{(1)}),\qquad
B_{21}:X_1^{(1)}\to\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}^{(2)}).
\]

Pointwise composition gives bounded process-space maps

\[
\mathcal C:=\mathscr S_1^g\circ B_{12}:E_2\to E_1,
\qquad
\mathcal D:=\mathscr S_2^g\circ B_{21}:E_1\to E_2.
\]

Consider the block pair

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}A_1&0\\0&A_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&B_{12}\\B_{21}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The two directions are both allowed to be nonzero. This is the smallest directed cycle and is excluded by the preceding acyclic-cascade theorem.

For arbitrary admissible drift/noise/trace data, let `h_r in E_r` be the solution of the corresponding **uncoupled additive** problem. A solution of the full coupled equation must satisfy exactly

\[
\boxed{u_1=h_1+\mathcal C u_2,\qquad u_2=h_2+\mathcal D u_1.}
\]

No approximation or independence argument is being used here.

## 5. Main theorem `P15-I0066-TWOBLOCK-FEEDBACK-03` — PROVED

### Statement

Under the setup of Section 4, assume

\[
\boxed{I_{E_1}-\mathcal C\mathcal D\ \text{is boundedly invertible on }E_1.}
\]

Equivalently, assume `I_{E_2}-D C` is boundedly invertible on `E_2`.

Then the full two-block pair `(A,B)` has the same source stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity flavor as the two additive diagonal pairs.

No hypothesis of the form

\[
\|B\|<\varepsilon,
\qquad
\|\mathcal C\|<1,
\qquad
\|\mathcal D\|<1,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\|\mathcal C\mathcal D\|<1
\]

is required.

### Proof

Starting from

\[
u_1=h_1+\mathcal C u_2,\qquad u_2=h_2+\mathcal D u_1,
\]

substitute the second equation into the first:

\[
(I-\mathcal C\mathcal D)u_1=h_1+\mathcal C h_2.
\]

By the invertibility hypothesis,

\[
\boxed{u_1=(I-\mathcal C\mathcal D)^{-1}(h_1+\mathcal C h_2),}
\]

and then

\[
\boxed{u_2=h_2+\mathcal D u_1.}
\]

Both are in the adapted solution spaces because every operator in the formulas is a bounded operator between those spaces. These formulas give existence.

They also give the a priori estimate

\[
\|u_1\|_{E_1}
\le
\|(I-\mathcal C\mathcal D)^{-1}\|
\bigl(\|h_1\|_{E_1}+\|\mathcal C\|\,\|h_2\|_{E_2}\bigr),
\]

and

\[
\|u_2\|_{E_2}
\le
\|h_2\|_{E_2}+\|\mathcal D\|\,\|u_1\|_{E_1}.
\]

The additive SMR estimates for `h_1,h_2` therefore yield the required full-pair SMR estimate for all admissible source data, including the trace datum when the chosen source flavor includes it.

For uniqueness, set all external data to zero. Then

\[
(I-\mathcal C\mathcal D)u_1=0,
\]

so `u_1=0`, and then `u_2=D u_1=0`.

It remains only to justify the claimed equivalence of the two Schur conditions. This is the standard Jacobson identity, which here is one line of operator algebra. If `I-CD` is invertible, then

\[
\boxed{(I-\mathcal D\mathcal C)^{-1}
=I+\mathcal D(I-\mathcal C\mathcal D)^{-1}\mathcal C.}
\]

Multiplication verifies both left and right inverse identities. The converse follows by exchanging `C` and `D`. Therefore either round-trip Schur complement suffices. QED.

### Why this counts as a third non-small family

This theorem is not acyclic: when both `B_12` and `B_21` are nonzero, the dependency graph contains a directed 2-cycle. It is not the compact-Lie family: no group action, isometry, commutation, or stochastic conjugation is assumed.

More importantly, the criterion is genuinely non-small in the source's sense. For any `tau>0`, define

\[
B_{12}^{(\tau)}=\tau B_{12},\qquad
B_{21}^{(\tau)}=\tau^{-1}B_{21}.
\]

By linearity of the additive stochastic solution maps,

\[
\mathcal C_\tau=\tau\mathcal C,\qquad
\mathcal D_\tau=\tau^{-1}\mathcal D,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_\tau\mathcal D_\tau=\mathcal C\mathcal D.}
\]

Thus the exact hypothesis and Schur inverse are unchanged while, provided `B_12 != 0`, the full block noise norm can be made arbitrarily large as `tau -> infinity`. The theorem therefore cannot be reduced to a uniform perturbative-smallness bound on `||B||`.

This establishes the metric movement `2 -> 3` under the metric frozen in the exclusive claim.

## 6. Two useful corollaries: repeated-feedback smallness and amplitude resolvents

These are consequences of the same feedback algebra. They are not counted as additional metric families.

### Corollary `P15-I0066-POWER-FEEDBACK-03` — PROVED

Let `K` be any bounded feedback operator on a source solution space. If for some integer `m>=1`

\[
\boxed{\|K^m\|<1,}
\]

then `I-K` is boundedly invertible and therefore the corresponding full pair has the source SMR property whenever the fixed-point representation `u=h+Ku` is valid.

Indeed,

\[
(I-K)(I+K+\cdots+K^{m-1})=I-K^m,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{(I-K)^{-1}
=(I+K+\cdots+K^{m-1})(I-K^m)^{-1}.}
\]

In particular,

\[
\|(I-K)^{-1}\|
\le
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\|K\|^j}{1-\|K^m\|}.
\]

For the two-block cycle

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal C\\\mathcal D&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K^2=\operatorname{diag}(\mathcal C\mathcal D,\mathcal D\mathcal C),
\]

so small **round-trip** feedback suffices even when one-step feedback is huge.

### Common-amplitude / resolvent form

For a real amplitude `z`, replace both cross-noise blocks by `z` times themselves. Then

\[
\mathcal C_z=z\mathcal C,\qquad \mathcal D_z=z\mathcal D,
\]

so the theorem certifies SMR whenever

\[
\boxed{I-z^2\mathcal C\mathcal D\text{ is invertible}.}
\]

Equivalently, for nonzero real `z`, the sufficient condition is

\[
z^{-2}\in\rho(\mathcal C\mathcal D)
\]

(after the standard complexification if one wishes to use complex spectral notation on a real space). This is a **sufficient-certification set**, not a claimed necessary characterization of source SMR: failure of this particular fixed-point inverse condition is not proved here to imply failure of the source property.

## 7. Move #20 result: compact-Fredholm route is false in the simplest scalar case

### Claim `P15-I0066-FEEDBACK-NONCOMPACT-OBSTRUCTION-03` — PROVED

Take scalar state, `T>0`, nonzero scalar `b`, and on the adapted Hilbert process space

\[
E=L^2_{\mathcal P}(\Omega\times(0,T))
\]

define the stochastic Volterra feedback operator

\[
(Ku)(t)=b\int_0^t u(s)\,dW_s.
\]

Then `K:E->E` is bounded but **not compact**.

### Proof

Restrict the domain to deterministic functions `h,g`. Itô isometry and Fubini give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle Kh,Kg\rangle_E
&=b^2\int_0^T\int_0^t h(s)g(s)\,ds\,dt\\
&=b^2\int_0^T(T-s)h(s)g(s)\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]

On `[0,T/2]`, the weight `b^2(T-s)` is bounded above and below by positive constants. Choose an infinite sequence `(h_n)` supported in `[0,T/2]` and orthonormal for the weighted inner product

\[
\langle h,g\rangle_w=b^2\int_0^{T/2}(T-s)h(s)g(s)\,ds.
\]

Because the weight is bounded below, `(h_n)` is bounded in the ordinary deterministic `L^2(0,T)` norm. But the displayed identity gives

\[
\langle Kh_n,Kh_m\rangle_E=\delta_{nm}.
\]

Thus `(Kh_n)` has no norm-convergent subsequence. Hence `K` is not compact. QED.

So finite-dimensional state does **not** make the stochastic feedback map compact. A general Fredholm-alternative attack cannot be based on that premise.

### What survives: causal powers can still be tiny

The same scalar operator shows why temporal ordering is more relevant than compactness. Iterated Itô isometry gives, for arbitrary adapted `u`,

\[
\mathbb E|K^m u(t)|^2
=b^{2m}\int_0^t\frac{(t-s)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\,\mathbb E|u(s)|^2\,ds.
\]

Integrating in `t`,

\[
\boxed{
\|K^m u\|_E^2
\le \frac{b^{2m}T^m}{m!}\,\|u\|_E^2.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
r(K)=0
\]

although `K` is noncompact. In this scalar lower-order model, every finite noise amplitude is consequently admitted by the power-feedback sufficient condition. This is a sanity check on the mechanism, not a claim that the difficult leading-order source problem has thereby been solved.

### Independent audit

P16 chooses an exact `mu`-minimizing connected simple `d`-regular graph, a Fiedler vector, and among compatible nonincreasing Fiedler orders one minimizing the integer edge-span. Assuming edge connectivity at least 3, it claims every Fiedler-prefix cut has at most `d(2d-1)` edges.

I checked each nontrivial step independently.

1. **Connectivity after the switch.** Edge connectivity at least 3 means deleting any two edges leaves the graph connected. Therefore deleting two crossing matching edges already preserves connectedness; adding the two same-side edges cannot destroy it.

2. **Exact Monge energy identity.** For left values `a,b` and right values `c,d` with `a,b>=c,d`,

   \[
   (a-c)^2+(b-d)^2-(a-b)^2-(c-d)^2
   =2(a-d)(b-c)\ge0.
   \]

   Hence the cross-to-within 2-switch does not increase the old Fiedler Rayleigh energy.

3. **Variational sandwich.** The switched graph is connected and `d`-regular, so exact global minimality gives

   \[
   \mu_{\min}\le\mu(H)\le R_H(f)\le R_G(f)=\mu_{\min}.
   \]

   Thus equality holds throughout and the same `f` remains a Fiedler vector of the new exact minimizer.

4. **Strict secondary descent.** If the two left positions are `p_1,p_2<=k` and the right positions are `q_1,q_2>k`, then old span minus new span is

   \[
   2(q_- - p_+)>0.
   \]

   Therefore the switch contradicts span minimality whenever neither same-side pair was already an edge.

5. **Matching bound.** Fix one matching edge. Its left endpoint can be adjacent to at most `d-1` other matched left endpoints and its right endpoint to at most `d-1` other matched right endpoints. Since every other matching edge must be covered by at least one such adjacency,

   \[
   m-1\le2(d-1),\qquad m\le2d-1.

   \[
   |\partial S|\le dm\le d(2d-1).
