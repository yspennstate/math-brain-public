## 3. Move #11 — DUALIZE: replace the coupled equation by a dependency DAG

The new idea was to stop treating the full pair `(A,B)` as one monolith. Write the state as a finite direct sum of Banach couples and look at the **dependency graph** of off-diagonal blocks. If that graph is acyclic, the stochastic problem can be solved in reverse topological order. This is the dual/control-theoretic view: instead of diagonalizing the operators spectrally, diagonalize the *causal dependency relation*.

The key observation is that the usual small-`B` argument closes a simultaneous fixed point around a cycle. A DAG has no cycle, so no contraction is needed: every multiplicative-noise term entering the next component is already known when that component is solved.

This leads to the theorem below.

## 4. Main theorem `P15-I0066-ACYCLIC-CASCADE-02` — PROVED

### Statement

Fix an admissible stochastic maximal-regularity exponent/weight pair `(p,kappa)` in the sense of the source, a finite horizon `T`, and a finite number `N>=1` of Banach couples

\[
X_1^{(r)}\hookrightarrow X_0^{(r)},\qquad r=1,\ldots,N,
\]

such that the finite direct sums

\[
X_i=\bigoplus_{r=1}^N X_i^{(r)},\qquad i=0,1,
\]

are in the source class and the half-order space splits compatibly,

\[
X_{1/2}=\bigoplus_{r=1}^N X_{1/2}^{(r)}.
\]

Let `U` be the driving Hilbert space. For each `r`, let

\[
A_r\in\mathscr L(X_1^{(r)},X_0^{(r)})
\]

and assume the **additive** pair `(A_r,0)` has stochastic maximal `L^p_kappa`-regularity on `(0,T)` in the same source flavour (plain or bullet) in which we want the conclusion. Denote by `M_r<infinity` a corresponding zero-initial-data solution-operator bound.

Let the full drift and noise be block matrices

\[
A=D+R,\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,\ldots,A_N),
\]

and

\[
B=(B_{rs})_{1\le r,s\le N},
\]

with bounded off-diagonal blocks

\[
R_{rs}\in\mathscr L(X_1^{(s)},X_0^{(r)}),
\qquad
B_{rs}\in\mathscr L\bigl(X_1^{(s)},\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}^{(r)})\bigr).
\]

Assume there is an ordering of the blocks for which both couplings are **strictly upper triangular**:

\[
R_{rs}=B_{rs}=0\qquad\text{whenever }s\le r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently, the directed graph with an arrow `s -> r` whenever either `R_rs` or `B_rs` is nonzero is a finite DAG.

Then the full pair `(A,B)` has stochastic maximal `L^p_kappa`-regularity on `(0,T)`, in the same plain/bullet source flavour as the diagonal additive blocks. No smallness assumption is imposed on any off-diagonal `R_rs` or `B_rs`.

More quantitatively, put

\[
\alpha_{rs}=\|R_{rs}\|,
\qquad
\beta_{rs}=\|B_{rs}\|,
\qquad
c_{rs}=\alpha_{rs}+\beta_{rs},
\]

and define the strictly upper-triangular numerical matrix

\[
\mathcal N_{rs}=M_r c_{rs}\quad(s>r),
\qquad \mathcal N_{rs}=0\quad(s\le r).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `N` blocks are finite,

\[
\mathcal N^N=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `d_r` denotes the sum of the source norms of the `r`th deterministic and stochastic inhomogeneities and `x_r` the corresponding maximal-regularity norm of the `r`th solution component, then componentwise

\[
 x\le \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathcal N^k M d,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `M=diag(M_1,...,M_N)`. Thus the full SMR constant is finite and depends polynomially (of degree at most `N-1`) on the off-diagonal coupling norms. In particular, it need not arise from a small-coupling Neumann series.

### Proof

Topologically sort the finite dependency DAG, so (4.1) holds. Write the unknown and inhomogeneities as

\[
u=(u_1,\ldots,u_N),\qquad
f=(f_1,\ldots,f_N),\qquad
g=(g_1,\ldots,g_N).
\]

The `r`th component of

\[
\mathrm du+Au\,\mathrm dt=f\,\mathrm dt+(Bu+g)\,\mathrm dW
\]

is

\[
\mathrm du_r+A_ru_r\,\mathrm dt
=\left(f_r-\sum_{s>r}R_{rs}u_s\right)\mathrm dt
+\left(g_r+\sum_{s>r}B_{rs}u_s\right)\mathrm dW.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Start at `r=N`. The sums are empty, so `(A_N,0)` additive SMR gives a unique strong adapted `u_N` and

\[
x_N\le M_N d_N.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Now suppose `u_{r+1},...,u_N` have already been constructed, are adapted, and lie in their source `E_1` spaces. Because

\[
R_{rs}:X_1^{(s)}\to X_0^{(r)},
\]

the deterministic effective forcing

\[
F_r:=f_r-\sum_{s>r}R_{rs}u_s
\]

belongs to the source deterministic data space. Likewise, because

\[
B_{rs}:X_1^{(s)}\to\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}^{(r)}),
\]

the stochastic effective forcing

\[
G_r:=g_r+\sum_{s>r}B_{rs}u_s
\]

belongs to the source stochastic data space. Both remain progressively measurable because they are formed from already-constructed adapted components by deterministic bounded operators.

Apply additive SMR for `(A_r,0)` to (4.5). It yields a unique adapted strong solution `u_r` and

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_r
&\le M_r\left(
 d_r+\sum_{s>r}\alpha_{rs}x_s+\sum_{s>r}\beta_{rs}x_s
\right)\\
&=M_r\left(d_r+\sum_{s>r}c_{rs}x_s\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Proceed backwards to `r=1`. This proves existence.

For uniqueness, take the difference of two solutions with identical data. Equation (4.5) at `r=N` is the homogeneous additive problem, hence the difference `u_N` vanishes. Inductively, if all differences in components `s>r` vanish, the `r`th difference is again the homogeneous additive problem for `(A_r,0)`, hence it vanishes. Thus the full solution is unique.

For the global estimate, (4.7) is exactly the componentwise vector inequality

\[
x\le Md+\mathcal N x.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Iterating it `N` times and using `\mathcal N^N=0` gives

\[
x\le
\left(I+\mathcal N+\cdots+\mathcal N^{N-1}\right)Md,
\]

which is (4.4). All coefficients are nonnegative, so no cancellation argument is hidden here. Taking any fixed finite-dimensional product norm of the component estimates yields the full source SMR estimate with finite constant.

If each diagonal additive block has the stronger source `SMR^bullet` regularity, applying that same stronger additive solution estimate at every induction step gives the corresponding time/trace regularity componentwise, and finite direct sums preserve it. Hence the theorem transfers the same source flavour assumed on the diagonal blocks.

This proves `P15-I0066-ACYCLIC-CASCADE-02`. QED.

## 6. Move #15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE

The previous successful family imposed a strong symmetry: the corrected drift commuted with a compact group representation. I deliberately removed that symmetry rather than weakening it perturbatively.

The acyclic theorem permits arbitrary block-dependent `A_r`, arbitrary bounded off-diagonal drift couplings `R_rs`, arbitrary bounded off-diagonal noise couplings `B_rs`, and noncommuting noise coordinates. The price is algebraic directionality: the joint coupling graph must be acyclic.

The useful obstruction exposed by this move is equally precise. If the dependency graph contains a directed cycle, the backward recursion stops. The inequalities become schematically

\[
x\le Md+\mathcal N x
\]

with `\mathcal N` no longer nilpotent. Then the obvious proof needs either

- a contraction/spectral-radius condition on the cycle,
- a different transformation (such as the compact stochastic flow), or
- a coercive/joint-parabolicity structure capable of controlling feedback around the loop.

That is the exact place where “no smallness” ceases to be free. I did **not** promote this proof obstruction to a nonexistence theorem: cyclic systems can certainly have SMR by other mechanisms.

## 8. Attempted negation / stress tests

Although this is not the first run on I0066, I stress-tested the new candidate before counting it.

### 8.1 Noncommuting noise does not break the proof

The exact `E_12,E_23` example has nonzero commutator. The proof never permutes noise generators and therefore does not assume commutativity. It only uses the block dependency direction.

### 8.2 Arbitrarily large coupling does not break the proof

Scaling every off-diagonal block by `L>0` changes the finite polynomial constant in (4.4), but because `\mathcal N^N=0` it never creates a denominator `1-LC`. Thus this is genuinely not a perturbative-smallness result.

### 8.3 A directed cycle breaks this proof, not the theorem’s statement

If both `B_12` and `B_21` are allowed, the dependency graph is cyclic and there is no topological ordering. The recursive construction fails exactly there. I do not claim cyclic systems lack SMR. Instead, the failure isolates the missing structure for a broader theorem: one needs a way to control stochastic feedback around a cycle.

### 8.4 Off-diagonal drift and noise can be handled together

Allowing `R_rs` as well as `B_rs` was not cosmetic. Equation (4.5) shows that once `u_s` is known, the drift coupling is ordinary source forcing and the noise coupling is ordinary source stochastic forcing. The same acyclic recursion handles both at their natural source orders.

No counterexample to the stated theorem survived these tests.

### Claim being audited

For a connected graph with ordered vertices, orient every edge from the lower-index endpoint to the higher-index endpoint. Let `A` be the `(n-1) x m` matrix whose edge-column is the indicator of the consecutive prefix cuts crossed by that edge, and set

\[
K=AA^{\mathsf T}.
\]

For vertices `v_a,v_b`, `a<b`, let `s_ab` be the interval indicator of cut indices `a,...,b-1`. P16 claims

\[
R_{\rm eff}(v_a,v_b)=s_{ab}^{\mathsf T}K^{-1}s_{ab}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

### Independent audit

First, `A` has full row rank. If `A^T y=0`, define potentials

\[
p_1=0,\qquad p_k=\sum_{i<k}y_i.
\]

For an oriented edge `v_a v_b`, `a<b`,

\[
(A^Ty)_e=\sum_{i=a}^{b-1}y_i=p_b-p_a.
\]

Thus `p` is constant on every edge. Connectedness makes it constant on all vertices; because `p_1=0`, all `p_k=0`, hence all `y_i=0`. Therefore `K=AA^T` is positive definite.

Second, for any signed edge-flow vector `z`, `(Az)_i` is exactly the net oriented flow leaving the prefix `{v_1,...,v_i}`. Hence

\[
Az=s_{ab}
\]

means the cumulative divergence is zero before `a`, one from `a` through `b-1`, and zero thereafter. Taking consecutive differences gives divergence `+1` at `v_a`, `-1` at `v_b`, and zero elsewhere. Thus the feasible set is exactly the set of unit `a-to-b` flows.

By Thomson’s principle with unit edge resistances,

\[
R_{\rm eff}(v_a,v_b)=\min\{\|z\|_2^2:Az=s_{ab}\}.
\]

Since `A` has full row rank, the unique minimum-norm flow is

\[
z_*=A^T(AA^T)^{-1}s_{ab},
\]

and therefore

\[
\|z_*\|_2^2
=s_{ab}^T K^{-1}AA^TK^{-1}s_{ab}
=s_{ab}^TK^{-1}s_{ab}.
\]

This reproduces (9.1) with the same orientation convention and no missing factor.

**Verdict:** `P15-AUDIT-P16-CUTGRAM-ELECTRICAL-DUALITY-02 = PASS`.
