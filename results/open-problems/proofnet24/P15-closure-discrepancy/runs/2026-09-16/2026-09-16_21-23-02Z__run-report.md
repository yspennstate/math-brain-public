## 2. Exact source statement and current literature/status check

Primary source rechecked at the current published NoDEA version and arXiv record. Immediately before Problem 1, Agresti–Veraar say that several concrete nonzero-`B` cases are known but that no satisfactory operator-theoretic condition covers most of them. They explicitly record the easy perturbative route

\[
(A,0)\in SMR_p,
\qquad
\|B\|_{\mathscr L(X_1,\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2}))}<\varepsilon_{A,p},
\]

and say that this usually gives an unsatisfactory theory. The exact problem is:

> **Problem 1 (Sufficient conditions for stochastic maximal regularity).** Find reasonable sufficient abstract conditions for stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity of `(A,B)`.

The source Definition 3.7 quantifies over every finite horizon, every starting time, every bounded stopping time, arbitrary progressive deterministic forcing `f` in `X_0`, and arbitrary progressive stochastic forcing `g` in `gamma(U,X_{1/2})`, and asks for a uniform `L^p(X_1)` estimate. Remark 3.10 says that if `A` has deterministic maximal `L^p`-regularity then it suffices to establish the stochastic estimate for `f=0`. Theorem 3.13 gives `(A,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,0}` on Hilbert spaces for analytic semigroup generators, and Proposition 3.18 upgrades base SMR of `(A,B)` to the bullet property if an operator with the same domain has bullet SMR.

The source itself records two relevant boundaries:

1. the classical abstract coercivity theorem at `p=2` in a Gelfand triple (Theorem 3.17);
2. concrete `p>2` stochastic-parabolicity theorems for second-order differential operators, while warning that system/higher-order extensions are subtle and that large-`p` breakdown occurs in known examples.

## Claim `P15-I0066-SPECTRAL-BELLMAN-NONCOMMUTING-05` — PROVED

Let `H` be a real separable Hilbert space. Let `A` be positive self-adjoint on `H`, with compact resolvent and

\[
A\ge a_0 I\qquad(a_0>0).
\]

Put

\[
X_0=H,
\qquad
X_1=D(A),
\qquad
X_{1/2}=D(A^{1/2}),
\]

using the equivalent homogeneous norms `||Ax||`, `||A^{1/2}x||` permitted by the spectral lower bound.

Let the noise space be `U=R^m`. Let `C_1,...,C_m in L(H)` **strongly commute with `A`**, meaning that every `C_j` commutes with every spectral projection of `A`. No pairwise commutation is assumed between the `C_j` themselves. Assume the aggregate row-operator bound

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^m\|C_j y\|_H^2\le \beta^2\|y\|_H^2
\quad\text{for every }y\in H.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define

\[
B_jx=C_jA^{1/2}x,
\qquad
Bx=(B_1x,\ldots,B_mx).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Fix `p in [2,infinity)` and suppose

\[
\boxed{(p-1)\beta^2<2.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{(A,B)\in SMR_{p,0}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `A` is a positive self-adjoint analytic generator on a Hilbert space, source Theorem 3.13 gives `(A,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,0}`. Therefore source Proposition 3.18 also yields

\[
\boxed{(A,B)\in SMR^bullet_{p,0}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The theorem is unweighted (`kappa=0`) and finite-noise. Those restrictions are part of the proved scope.

### 4.1 The operator really has source-critical order

For `x in D(A)`, strong commutation gives

\[
A^{1/2}B_jx
=A^{1/2}C_jA^{1/2}x
=C_jAx.
\]

Hence

\[
\|Bx\|_{\gamma(\mathbb R^m,X_{1/2})}^2
=\sum_j\|C_jAx\|^2
\le\beta^2\|Ax\|^2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus `B:X_1 -> gamma(U,X_{1/2})` has exactly the critical half-order size required by the source. There is no lower-order compactness assumption.

# 6. NEW-METHOD #25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE

The proof hinges on computing one semigroup energy in two ways. The same quantity appears both as the Hessian cost of the multiplicative noise and as the derivative in semigroup time. The resulting exact telescope is what prevents a generic Hessian estimate from losing the source-critical constant.

## 6.1 Reduction to `f=0`

Because `A` is positive self-adjoint, it has deterministic maximal `L^p`-regularity on `H` for every `p in (1,infinity)`. By source Remark 3.10 it is enough to establish Definition 3.7 with `f=0`; the deterministic solution for `f` can then be absorbed into the stochastic inhomogeneity `Bv+g`.

Fix a finite source horizon `T`, a start `a in [0,T]`, a stopping time `tau in [a,T]`, and progressive

\[
g=(g_1,\ldots,g_m)
\in L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;\gamma(\mathbb R^m,D(A^{1/2})))).
\]

We prove a constant independent of `a` and `tau`.

## 6.2 Spectral Galerkin problem

Compact resolvent gives finite-dimensional spectral projections `P_N` increasing strongly to the identity. Because every `C_j` strongly commutes with `A`, it commutes with every `P_N`. In the finite-dimensional space `P_NH`, solve

\[
du_N+Au_N\,dt
=\sum_{j=1}^m\big(C_jA^{1/2}u_N+P_Ng_j\big)\,dW^j,
\qquad u_N(a)=0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This linear finite-dimensional SDE has a unique global adapted solution. We derive an estimate uniform in `N`.

## 6.3 Correct Bellman functional

On `P_NH` define

\[
\Phi_p(x)
:=\int_0^\infty\|Ae^{-sA}x\|^p\,ds.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The spectral lower bound makes the integral finite. On a finite spectral space it is `C^2` for `p>=2` (quadratic when `p=2`). Set

\[
z_s=Ae^{-sA}x.
\]

For the drift direction `-Ax`,

\[
D\Phi_p(x)[-Ax]
=\int_0^\infty \frac{d}{ds}\|z_s\|^p\,ds
=-\|Ax\|^p.
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is the full source `X_1` norm, not the half-order norm.

For `F(z)=||z||^p` on a real Hilbert space,

\[
D^2F(z)[h,h]
=p\|z\|^{p-2}\|h\|^2
+p(p-2)\|z\|^{p-4}\langle z,h\rangle^2
\le p(p-1)\|z\|^{p-2}\|h\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
D^2\Phi_p(x)[h,h]
\le p(p-1)\int_0^\infty
\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|Ae^{-sA}h\|^2\,ds.
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 6.4 Multiplicative noise: exact structural coefficient

Strong spectral commutation gives

\[
Ae^{-sA}B_jx
=C_jA^{1/2}z_s.
\]

Summing (4.1),

\[
\sum_j\|Ae^{-sA}B_jx\|^2
\le\beta^2\|A^{1/2}z_s\|^2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Now count the same semigroup energy through its derivative:

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\|z_s\|^p
=-p\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|A^{1/2}z_s\|^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty
\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|A^{1/2}z_s\|^2\,ds
=\frac1p\|Ax\|^p.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Putting (6.4)–(6.6) into the Itô factor `1/2` yields precisely

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p-1}{2}\beta^2\|Ax\|^p.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is the load-bearing coefficient. Condition (4.3) says it leaves positive drift coercivity.

## 6.5 Additive stochastic forcing

Fix `delta>0`. For every `s`,

\[
\sum_j\|Ae^{-sA}(B_jx+g_j)\|^2
\le(1+\delta)\sum_j\|Ae^{-sA}B_jx\|^2
 +(1+\delta^{-1})\sum_j\|Ae^{-sA}g_j\|^2.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Choose `delta` so small that

\[
\rho:=\frac{p-1}{2}(1+\delta)\beta^2<1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

For the additive part, semigroup contractivity gives `||z_s||<=||Ax||`, while the spectral theorem gives the exact square-function identity

\[
\int_0^\infty\|Ae^{-sA}h\|^2\,ds
=\frac12\|A^{1/2}h\|^2.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Thus, with

\[
G^2:=\sum_j\|A^{1/2}g_j\|^2,
\]

we have

\[
\int_0^\infty
\|z_s\|^{p-2}\sum_j\|Ae^{-sA}g_j\|^2\,ds
\le\frac12\|Ax\|^{p-2}G^2.
\tag{6.11}
\]

For `p>2`, Young's inequality gives, for every `eta>0`,

\[
\|Ax\|^{p-2}G^2
\le \eta\|Ax\|^p+C_{p,\eta}G^p.
\tag{6.12}
\]

For `p=2`, (6.11) is already purely quadratic in `G`. Choose `eta` small enough that the additional term is absorbed by `1-rho`.

## 6.6 Itô estimate, localization, and stopping times

Apply Itô's formula to `Phi_p(u_N)` up to the source stopping time `tau`, with the usual additional localization stopping times if necessary. The martingale term has zero expectation after localization. Equations (6.3), (6.7), and (6.8)–(6.12), followed by absorption, give

\[
E\int_a^\tau\|Au_N(t)\|^pdt
\le C_{p,\beta}
E\int_a^\tau
\left(\sum_j\|A^{1/2}P_Ng_j(t)\|^2\right)^{p/2}dt.
\tag{6.13}
\]

The terminal Bellman term is nonnegative and the initial term vanishes. The constant depends on `p` and the strict margin in (4.3), but not on `N`, `a`, or `tau`. Monotone/localized convergence removes the auxiliary localization.

Because `A>=a_0I`, `||x||_{D(A)}` is equivalent to `||Ax||`; similarly the source stochastic-data norm is equivalent to the displayed `A^{1/2}` norm. Therefore (6.13) is exactly the source base-SMR estimate for `f=0`.

## 6.7 Passage `N -> infinity` without a hidden approximation gap

The same estimate applies to `u_N-u_M`, because `P_M` and `P_N` commute with `A` and every `C_j`. Its stochastic inhomogeneity is `(P_N-P_M)g`. Since the spectral projections converge strongly on `D(A^{1/2})`,

\[
(P_N-P_M)g\to0
\quad\text{in }L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;\gamma(\mathbb R^m,D(A^{1/2})))),
\]

and (6.13) shows that `u_N` is Cauchy in

\[
L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;D(A))).
\]

Let the limit be `u`. Then

\[
Au_N\to Au
\]

in the corresponding `L^p(H)` space. Moreover (4.1) and `A>=a_0I` imply

\[
\|B(u_N-u)\|_{\gamma(\mathbb R^m,H)}
\lesssim\|A^{1/2}(u_N-u)\|
\lesssim\|A(u_N-u)\|,
\]

so the stochastic integrands converge in `L^2` on every finite interval (use `p>=2`). Burkholder–Davis–Gundy then gives convergence of the stochastic integrals in `L^p(\Omega;C([a,T];H))`; the drift integrals converge from the `L^p(D(A))` convergence. Passing to the limit in the integrated finite-dimensional equation proves that `u` is a source strong solution.

For uniqueness, apply (6.13) to the difference of two strong solutions with `g=0`; then `Au=0`, and `A>=a_0I` gives `u=0`.

This proves `(A,B) in SMR_{p,0}` for `f=0`. Source Remark 3.10 restores arbitrary progressive `f`, proving (4.4). Source Theorem 3.13 plus Proposition 3.18, with comparison operator `A_0=A`, gives (4.5). **QED.**

## 7. A genuinely non-pairwise-commuting finite spectral block

The theorem is not merely a diagonal scalar-noise restatement. Take

\[
H=\ell^2(\mathbb N;\mathbb R^2),
\qquad
A|_{\mathbb R^2_n}=(1+n^2)I_2.
\]

Let the same two matrices act on every two-dimensional eigenspace:

\[
C_1=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_2=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They strongly commute with `A` because `A` is scalar on each two-dimensional spectral block, but

\[
[C_1,C_2]
=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
\]

Also

\[
C_1^*C_1+C_2^*C_2=\frac12I,
\]

so `beta^2=1/2`. At `p=4`,

\[
(p-1)\beta^2=\frac32<2,
\qquad
\frac{p-1}{2}\beta^2=\frac34.
\]

Therefore the theorem supplies source SMR for a genuinely pairwise-noncommuting two-noise critical operator family.

This example also shows why the family is analytically distinct from P15's compact-group transform family: no group-action hypothesis or bounded stochastic conjugating flow is used anywhere in the proof. The mechanism is direct dissipation-versus-Hessian balance.

## 8. Routes tried but not promoted

The 90-percent research rule matters here: the successful theorem came only after rejecting broader-looking routes.

### 8.1 Drop compact resolvent immediately — not claimed

For arbitrary positive self-adjoint `A`, the formal Bellman identities still look correct, but a complete proof needs a justified infinite-dimensional Itô formula or a different approximation scheme preserving the critical noise structure. P24's just-corrected proof history is direct evidence that this is a real obligation, not clerical detail. I therefore stopped at compact resolvent instead of claiming the stronger theorem.

### 8.2 Replace spectral commutation by only an aggregate graph/form bound — failed at the key identity

If `C_j` does not commute with the spectral calculus of `A`, then

\[
Ae^{-sA}C_jA^{1/2}x
\]

cannot be rewritten as `C_j A^{1/2}z_s`. The exact derivative identity (6.6) then no longer controls the Hessian term. A future extension needs a quantitative commutator/square-function estimate; a plain form bound is insufficient for this proof.

### 8.3 Weighted `kappa>0` version — deliberately open

Inserting the source weight `(t-a)^kappa` into the direct Itô integration creates an extra time-weight bookkeeping problem near `a` and at stopping times. The source theory has powerful weighted transference tools, but I have not proved that the same sharp coefficient survives this Bellman argument. I therefore claim only `kappa=0`.

These are real remaining obligations, not renamed lemmas.
