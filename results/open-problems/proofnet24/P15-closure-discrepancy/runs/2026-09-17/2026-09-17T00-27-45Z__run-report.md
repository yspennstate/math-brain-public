## 3. Ideas before code: why take the finite DAG theorem to infinite depth

The finite acyclic theorem is striking because no off-diagonal coupling smallness is needed: after topological ordering, the numerical gain matrix is nilpotent. The previous theorem uses the identity

\[
(I-\mathcal N)^{-1}=I+\mathcal N+\cdots+\mathcal N^{N-1}.
\]

Move #7 asks what survives if the depth tends to infinity. Nilpotence then disappears. The key question is not whether each finite truncation is solvable — it is — but whether the maximal-regularity constants remain uniformly controlled as depth grows.

This led to a deliberately simple source-valid Hilbert model where every finite truncation lies in the old acyclic family, yet the countably infinite limit crosses an exact energy threshold.

## 4. The geometric shift model

Let

\[
H=\ell^2(\mathbb N_0),\qquad \lambda_n=r^n,\quad r>1,
\]

and define the positive self-adjoint operator

\[
Ae_n=\lambda_n e_n.
\]

Take

\[
X_0=H,\qquad X_1=D(A),\qquad X_{1/2}=D(A^{1/2}),
\]

with their natural graph norms. Let the driving Hilbert space be `U=R` and define the one-step multiplicative-noise shift

\[
Be_n=c\sqrt{\lambda_n}\,e_{n+1},\qquad c\in\mathbb R.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then `B:X_1 -> X_{1/2}` is bounded and in fact

\[
\|Bx\|_{X_{1/2}}^2
=c^2r\,\|Ax\|_H^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda_{n+1}c^2\lambda_n|x_n|^2
=c^2r\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda_n^2|x_n|^2.
\]

Consider the zero-initial equation

\[
du+Au\,dt=f\,dt+(Bu+g)\,dW.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The `n`th component only depends on level `n-1`, so every finite truncation is a finite directed acyclic cascade of the already-counted family 2.

Put

\[
q:=\frac{c^2r}{2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. Main route result: exact infinite-depth threshold

### Claim `P15-I0066-GEOMETRIC-SHIFT-LIMIT-01` — PROVED / ROUTE OBSTRUCTION / NO METRIC CREDIT

For the pair `(A,B)` in (4.1), on every sufficiently large finite horizon:

\[
\boxed{(A,B)\text{ has base }SMR_{2,0}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c^2r<2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

At the endpoint `c^2 r = 2`, every finite truncation has stochastic maximal regularity by finite acyclicity, but the countably infinite pair fails it. Hence the finite acyclic-cascade theorem has **no arbitrary-coupling uniform infinite-depth extension**.

### 5.1 Positive side: `c^2 r < 2`

For `N>=0`, project (4.3) onto the first `N+1` coordinates. Because the coupling is one-way, these finite systems are consistent under increasing `N`: the first `M+1` coordinates never depend on coordinates above `M`.

Apply Itô's formula to

\[
\|A^{1/2}u_N(t)\|_H^2.
\]

After expectation, the martingale drops out and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\|A^{1/2}u_N(t)\|^2
+2\mathbb E\int_0^t\|Au_N\|^2ds
&=2\mathbb E\int_0^t\langle Au_N,f_N\rangle ds\\
&\quad+\mathbb E\int_0^t\|A^{1/2}(B_Nu_N+g_N)\|^2ds.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

On every nonterminal coordinate (4.2) is exact, and after projection it becomes the inequality

\[
\|A^{1/2}B_Nx\|^2\le c^2r\|Ax\|^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Choose `eta>0` so that `(1+eta)c^2r<2`. Then

\[
\|A^{1/2}(B_Nu_N+g_N)\|^2
\le(1+\eta)c^2r\|Au_N\|^2
+(1+\eta^{-1})\|g_N\|_{X_{1/2}}^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also, for `delta>0`,

\[
2|\langle Au_N,f_N\rangle|
\le\delta\|Au_N\|^2+\delta^{-1}\|f_N\|^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Take `delta` small enough that

\[
2-(1+\eta)c^2r-\delta>0.
\]

Equations (5.2)--(5.5) give a constant independent of `N` with

\[
\mathbb E\int_0^T\|Au_N\|^2dt
\lesssim_{c,r}
\mathbb E\int_0^T\bigl(\|f\|_H^2+\|g\|_{X_{1/2}}^2\bigr)dt.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Because the truncations are coordinatewise consistent and the left side is a monotone sum over levels, monotone convergence gives an infinite sequence `u` in `L^2(Omega x (0,T);X_1)`. Every component satisfies its scalar equation, and (5.6) makes `Bu` and all source terms square-integrable in the required spaces. Passing to the limit coordinatewise in the integral equation therefore gives a strong solution. Applying (5.6) to the difference of two solutions gives uniqueness. Since `A>=I`, the graph norm on `X_1` is controlled by `||Au||`, so this is the required base `SMR_{2,0}` estimate.

This positive statement is also consistent with Agresti–Veraar Theorem 3.17: the strict inequality is exactly a stochastic-parabolicity/coercivity margin in this model.

### 5.2 Negative side, including the endpoint: `c^2 r >= 2`

Set `f=0` and use the deterministic source-admissible stochastic forcing

\[
g(t)\equiv e_0.
\tag{5.7}
\]

It has finite `L^2(0,T;X_{1/2})` norm. Let

\[
v_n(t):=\mathbb E|u_n(t)|^2.
\]

The coordinate equations and Itô isometry give exactly

\[
v_0(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}2,
\tag{5.8}
\]

and for `n>=1`,

\[
v_n(t)=c^2\lambda_{n-1}
\int_0^t e^{-2\lambda_n(t-s)}v_{n-1}(s)\,ds.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Every `v_n` is nondecreasing. Indeed, assuming `v_{n-1}` nondecreasing, (5.9) gives

\[
v_n(t)\le \frac{c^2\lambda_{n-1}}{2\lambda_n}v_{n-1}(t),
\]

which inserted into

\[
v_n'=-2\lambda_n v_n+c^2\lambda_{n-1}v_{n-1}
\]

shows `v_n'>=0`.

Define the level output energy

\[
w_n(t):=\lambda_n^2v_n(t).
\]

For

\[
\delta_n:=\frac{n}{2\lambda_n},
\]

monotonicity of `v_{n-1}` and integration only over the last `delta_n` units of time give

\[
\boxed{
 w_n(t)\ge q(1-e^{-n})\,w_{n-1}(t-\delta_n)
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

whenever `t>=delta_n`.

Now

\[
D_\infty:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\delta_n
=\frac12\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{r^n}
=\frac{r}{2(r-1)^2}<\infty.
\tag{5.11}
\]

The infinite product

\[
P_\infty:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-n})
\tag{5.12}
\]

is strictly positive, because `sum e^{-n}<infinity` and `e^{-1}<1/2`; for example `log(1-x)>=-2x` on `[0,1/2]` makes the logarithmic series bounded below.

For any

\[
t\ge 1+D_\infty,
\]

iteration of (5.10) therefore yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_n(t)
&\ge q^n\prod_{k=1}^n(1-e^{-k})
 w_0\!\left(t-\sum_{k=1}^n\delta_k\right)\\
&\ge q^n P_\infty\frac{1-e^{-2}}2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.13}
\]

If `q>=1`, then for every such `t`,

\[
\mathbb E\|Au(t)\|^2
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_n(t)
=\infty.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Thus on every horizon `T>1+D_infty`, Tonelli gives

\[
\mathbb E\int_0^T\|Au(t)\|^2dt=\infty
\]

for the finite-norm input (5.7). Base stochastic maximal regularity therefore fails for `q>=1`, including the exact endpoint `c^2r=2`.

This proves (5.1).

## 6. Move #13 — the quantity that accumulates with depth

The right depth variable is not the largest single coupling but **transported full-order energy**. At the endpoint `q=1`, the finite `N`-level stationary second-moment equations under forcing at level zero are

\[
v_0^{(\infty)}=\frac12,
\qquad
v_n^{(\infty)}=rac{c^2\lambda_{n-1}}{2\lambda_n}v_{n-1}^{(\infty)}.
\]

For `r=2,c=1`, this gives exactly

\[
v_n^{(\infty)}=\frac1{2\,4^n},
\qquad
\lambda_n^2v_n^{(\infty)}=\frac12
\quad\text{for every }n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence the cumulative stationary full-order energy of the first `N+1` levels is exactly

\[
\sum_{n=0}^N\lambda_n^2v_n^{(\infty)}=\frac{N+1}{2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is why all finite DAGs are individually legal while their constants cannot stay uniform in depth. The infinite limit does not fail because a particular finite stage is singular; it fails because every added level carries a non-decaying amount of source-scale output energy.

## 7. Move #26 — a sporadic alternating pattern shows raw local size is not the invariant

The opposite phenomenon also occurs. Let `r=2` and make the shift coefficient scale-dependent:

\[
c_n=\begin{cases}
2,&n\text{ even},\\
1/4,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

The raw local energy gains are

\[
d_n:=c_n^2r
=\begin{cases}
8,&n\text{ even},\\
1/8,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.1}
\]

so the unweighted `B:X_1->X_{1/2}` norm squared is `8`, well beyond the homogeneous coercive threshold `2`.

However, choose the bounded positive level weights

\[
\theta_n=\begin{cases}
1,&n\text{ even},\\
1/8,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

Then every transformed local gain is exactly

\[
\frac{\theta_{n+1}d_n}{\theta_n}=1<2,
\tag{7.2}
\]

and every two-step normalized product is

\[
\left(\frac{8}{2}\right)
\left(\frac{1/8}{2}\right)=\frac14.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus a diagonal Lyapunov reweighting restores a strict energy margin despite a large raw one-step norm. This is precisely the kind of scale-dependent Lyapunov mechanism already contained in P15's counted heterogeneous `05B` extension (`V_n(z)=theta_n|z|^2` here), so it receives **no new metric credit**.

The useful conclusion is structural: for infinite acyclic systems the natural invariant is a bounded reweighting/path-product cocycle, not the maximum local edge norm. That tells the next attack what must replace finite nilpotence.
