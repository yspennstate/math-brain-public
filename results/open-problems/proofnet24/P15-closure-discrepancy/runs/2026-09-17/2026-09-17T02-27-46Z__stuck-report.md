## 3. Required stuck analysis

### (a) Exact blocking statement

The obstruction is no longer “prove one more structured transfer lemma.” It is the following missing genuinely abstract mechanism:

\[
\boxed{\text{Find a checkable, nonperturbative operator condition }\mathfrak C_p(A,B)
\text{ such that }\mathfrak C_p(A,B)\Longrightarrow (A,B)\in SMR_{p,\kappa}(T),
\text{ without assuming exact flow conjugacy, acyclic block form, feedback factorization, spectral commutation, or a blockwise Lyapunov decomposition.}}
\]

A sixth metric family has to cross that boundary. Repackaging one of the five existing mechanisms is not metric movement.

### (b) Routes tried and the precise reason each one fails to cross the blocker

**Route 1 — stochastic-flow conjugation.** P15 proved a noncommuting stochastic-flow transfer family and later weakened the finite-dimensional compact-Lie presentation. It handles large critical noise when an adapted bounded invertible flow removes it and commutes with the corrected drift. Failure mode: the existence of exactly such a flow is already a strong hidden integrability hypothesis. It does not give a generic operator-theoretic condition on `(A,B)`.

**Route 2 — finite acyclic block cascades.** P15 proved arbitrary-size off-diagonal drift/noise couplings when a finite topological order makes the dependency operator nilpotent. Failure mode: nilpotence is the mechanism. Countably infinite depth can make the regularity constants diverge, and cycles destroy the finite backward recursion.

**Route 3 — exact two-way feedback / Schur complement.** P15 reduced a two-block cycle to invertibility of `I-CD` (equivalently `I-DC`) for the round-trip solution-operator feedback. Failure mode: this is exact and nonperturbative, but it is a factorization/invertibility condition expressed in terms of already-built solution operators. It does not supply the source's sought general operator criterion on leading-order `(A,B)`.

**Route 4 — spectral Bellman.** For positive self-adjoint `A`, P15 obtained a `p`-energy/Bellman family when `B_j=C_j A^{1/2}` and the `C_j` strongly commute with the spectral projections of `A`, while the `C_j` may fail to commute with each other. Failure mode: once `[C_j,A] != 0`, the clean semigroup identity produces uncontrolled commutator terms such as

`A e^{-sA} C_j A^{1/2} - C_j A^{3/2} e^{-sA}`.

No general square-function estimate obtained so far controls this at critical order without reintroducing smallness or another structural assumption.

**Route 5 — block Lyapunov / heterogeneous Lyapunov.** P15 proved weighted `ell^p` block families with scale-dependent finite-dimensional matrices and uniform local Lyapunov-generator inequalities. Failure mode: exact block diagonality/locality is doing the work. Cross-scale leakage requires a global Lyapunov or cocycle control that the local certificate does not provide.

**Route 6 — compactness / Fredholm feedback.** The tempting statement “finite-dimensional state implies compact stochastic Volterra feedback” is false. Even the scalar stochastic Volterra operator is noncompact on the natural `L^2` process space. Failure mode: Fredholm compactness is not the abstract replacement for smallness.

**Route 7 — Brownian-coordinate randomization.** Orthogonal mixing of the noise coordinates leaves the stochastic term itself unchanged after the corresponding Brownian rotation and preserves the summed Itô/Hessian burden. Failure mode: the proposed randomization has an exact invariant and therefore cannot improve the critical estimate.

**Route 8 — finite truncation followed by an infinite-depth limit.** Every finite directed cascade can be regular while the infinite geometric weighted shift fails at the endpoint. Failure mode: finite-stage solvability does not provide a depth-uniform SMR constant.

**Route 9 — density-one / typical-scale goodness.** P15 built a deterministic sparse-critical-block obstruction: the set of critical edges can have natural density zero while arbitrarily long critical blocks force the SMR constants to blow up. Failure mode: density or average local goodness ignores long products.

**Route 10 — raw local norm as the invariant.** Alternating large/small couplings can be made harmless by bounded Lyapunov reweighting even when one-step norms are large. Failure mode: the right quantity is closer to a product/cocycle or global energy metric than a supremum of raw local norms.

**Route 11 — direct “small commutator” continuation from the spectral theorem.** This was identified after the spectral-Bellman run but not closed. Failure mode: in the general UMD/interpolation setting there is no currently established P15 estimate converting a commutator bound at the right half-order into the exact `L^p(X_1)` absorption required by source SMR. Without such an estimate, calling the commutator a perturbation would only rename the missing argument.

The common pattern is now clear: every successful P15 family has an exact global organizing object — a flow, a nilpotent order, an inverse feedback map, a spectral decomposition, or a Lyapunov decomposition. Every failed extension deletes that object without replacing it by a different global quantity.

### (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A real advance needs a **coordinate-free global `p`-energy / square-function object** that simultaneously:

- sees critical-order multiplicative noise rather than treating `B` as a lower-order perturbation;
- retains the genuinely `p`-dependent Itô-Hessian correction visible in Brzeźniak–Veraar 2012;
- controls noncommutation with `A` and cross-scale feedback, not just `\sum B_j^*B_j` on individual modes;
- is stable under stopping-time localization and arbitrary progressive additive data `f,g`, as the source SMR definition requires;
- has a UMD/`\gamma`-radonifying formulation or an honest reason why Hilbert structure is indispensable;
- yields a checkable sufficient condition without secretly assuming a solution flow, a pre-existing solution operator inverse, or a hand-chosen block Lyapunov function.

The most plausible missing object is an operator-valued Bellman/square-function coercivity inequality, perhaps formulated through `R`-bounded or `\gamma`-square-function estimates for the joint pair `(A,B)` rather than for `A` alone. None of P15's current proofs produces such an object.

### (d) Simplest special case still unproved

The cleanest unresolved P15 test case is already Hilbert and autonomous:

- `H` separable Hilbert;
- `A >= a_0 I` positive self-adjoint;
- finite-dimensional noise;
- `B_j = C_j A^{1/2}` with bounded `C_j`;
- no assumption that `C_j` commute with `A`, its spectral projections, or with one another;
- no block decomposition and no prescribed stochastic-flow representation.

A natural candidate to **test, not assume true**, is whether a `p`-dependent form inequality of the schematic Itô-energy type

\[
2\|A^{1/2}x\|^2
-\sum_j\|C_jA^{1/2}x\|^2
-(p-2)\sum_j\frac{|\langle C_jA^{1/2}x,x\rangle|^2}{\|x\|^2}
\ge \delta\|A^{1/2}x\|^2
\]

(with the zero vector treated separately and with whatever domain/form correction a correct derivation requires) is sufficient for base `SMR_p`. P15 has **not proved this**. It is deliberately written here as the simplest exact question to attack or refute, not as a theorem.

### (e) Nearest solved problem in the literature and the missing ingredient

The nearest solved abstract multiplicative problem is the **Hilbert `p=2` stochastic-parabolicity / variational coercivity criterion** recorded in the Agresti–Veraar source. Its proof has a decisive simplification: for the quadratic energy `||u||^2`, the Hessian is constant, so the entire multiplicative quadratic variation enters through one quadratic form and can be absorbed by coercivity.

The nearest `p != 2` model showing what changes is Brzeźniak–Veraar (2012). Their structured gradient/spectral problem exhibits an explicitly `p`-dependent stochastic-parabolicity threshold. What that model has, and the general I0066 test case lacks, is enough spectral/commuting structure to reduce the leading-order multiplicative noise to tractable scalar/modewise calculations. In the noncommuting abstract case, the `p`-energy Hessian is direction dependent and the spectral reduction no longer diagonalizes the troublesome term.

The additive abstract neighbor, van Neerven–Veraar–Weis (2012), supplies `H^∞`-calculus, `R`-boundedness and stochastic square-function technology for `B=0`; what it lacks is the multiplicative critical-order term. A successful port must therefore combine the variational `p`-energy correction with an abstract square-function calculus rather than applying either technology alone.
