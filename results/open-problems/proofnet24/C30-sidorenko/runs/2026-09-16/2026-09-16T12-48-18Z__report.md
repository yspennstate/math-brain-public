## 3. Counterexample-first target and exact integer inequality

Let `G` be a simple host graph with `n` vertices and `m` edges. Since `v(H)=10` and `e(H)=15`, the desired inequality is

`hom(H,G)/n^10 >= (2m/n^2)^15`.

After clearing denominators this is the exact integer condition

`D(G) := hom(H,G) n^20 - (2m)^15 >= 0`.      (1)

Thus every finite candidate can be checked without floating-point arithmetic. A single `D(G)<0` would be a genuine finite counterexample to the universal conjecture for this `H`.

## 4. Exact homomorphism-count formula

For host adjacency matrix `A`, define the exact triple common-neighbor tensor

`C(a,b,c) = sum_v A[a,v] A[b,v] A[c,v] = |N(a) cap N(b) cap N(c)|`.

**Proposition.** For `H=K_{5,5}\C_{10}` with left images denoted `(a,b,c,d,e)`,

`hom(H,G) = sum_{a,b,c,d,e}
 C(c,d,e) C(d,e,a) C(e,a,b) C(a,b,c) C(b,c,d)`.      (2)

**Proof.** Fix the images of the five left-side vertices. In the chosen cyclic presentation, each of the five right-side vertices is adjacent to a triple of consecutive-complement left vertices, namely the five triples represented in (2). Once the left images are fixed, the five right images are independent choices subject only to lying in their respective triple common-neighbor sets. Their numbers of choices multiply. Summing over all left images gives (2). QED.

This formula is useful computationally because it reduces a ten-variable brute-force count to a five-index contraction over exact integer common-neighbor counts. The checker independently brute-forces all maps for every host with at most three vertices and confirms equality with (2).

## 5. Complete exact enumeration of all simple hosts through seven vertices

The finite claim in this run is stronger than a sample: every simple host graph up to isomorphism on at most seven vertices was checked.

The checker uses the NetworkX graph atlas for candidate representatives, but does not assume the atlas count as the completeness proof. For each `n<=7` it independently computes the number `U(n)` of unlabeled simple graphs by Burnside's lemma:

`U(n) = (1/n!) sum_{pi in S_n} 2^{o(pi)}`,      (3)

where `o(pi)` is the number of orbits of `pi` acting on the `binom(n,2)` unordered vertex pairs. Equation (3) is exact: an edge set fixed by `pi` is precisely a union of pair-orbits, hence there are `2^{o(pi)}` fixed labeled graphs.

The resulting exact counts are

`U(1),...,U(7) = 1,2,4,11,34,156,1044`.

For each `n`, the checker verifies that the atlas supplies exactly this many graphs and independently canonicalizes every supplied graph by minimizing its edge-bit code over all `n!` vertex permutations. All canonical codes are distinct. Therefore the atlas list contains exactly one representative of every isomorphism class for each `n<=7`.

For every representative the checker computes `hom(H,G)` using (2) and then the integer defect (1). No defect is negative.

## 7. Audit of Zhao's route before importing it

Zhao's 2026 paper gives a sufficient conjugacy-class-averaging route to Sidorenko. In particular, the relevant introductory theorem says, in substance, that if the required comparison with conjugacy-class averages holds for every finite group and every admissible nonnegative function, then the graph is strong Sidorenko and therefore Sidorenko. This is a conditional reduction, not a proof for `K_{5,5}\C_{10}`.

The paper also proves a conjugacy-averaging inequality for 1-subdivision graphs. The frozen `H` here is 3-regular on both sides and is not itself a 1-subdivision graph (a genuine subdivision side would have degree two). Therefore that theorem cannot simply be cited to settle this target.

No statement in this report labels a Zhao conditional comparison as a solved Sidorenko inequality. A future group-kernel attack must first recover Zhao's exact kernel normalization, quantifiers, and positivity assumptions and then prove them for this specific `H`.
