## 5. Exact homomorphism decomposition used by the proof

Label the five left vertices by `x_0,...,x_4` cyclically so that each right vertex is adjacent to three consecutive left vertices. For a finite host `G`, write

\[
C(a,b,c)=|N(a)\cap N(b)\cap N(c)|.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\operatorname{hom}(H,G)
=
\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_4\in V(G)}
\prod_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}
C(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative. We retain only left tuples whose image has size one or two.

### One-symbol tuples

For the constant tuple `(v,v,v,v,v)`, every factor in (5.1) is `d_v`. Hence the total one-symbol contribution is

\[
\sum_v d_v^5.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Exact two-symbol tuples

Fix an unordered pair `{a,b}` and put

\[
c=c(a,b)=|N(a)\cap N(b)|.
\]

Among the `2^5-2=30` cyclic binary words using both symbols, the five length-three cyclic windows have the following type counts:

- `(0,0,5)` -- no monochromatic `a` or `b` windows: 10 words;
- `(2,0,3)`: 5 words;
- `(1,0,4)`: 5 words;
- `(0,1,4)`: 5 words;
- `(0,2,3)`: 5 words.

Therefore the **exact** contribution of all left tuples whose image is exactly `{a,b}` equals

\[
\boxed{
10c^5+5(d_a+d_b)c^4+5(d_a^2+d_b^2)c^3.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No inequality has been used in (5.3).

## 7. Minimum-degree-four branch

Assume now

\[
\delta(G)\ge4.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a pair `{a,b}` with codegree `c`, we have

\[
d_a,d_b\ge \max\{4,c\}.
\]

Set

\[
q(c)=\max\{4,c\}
\]

and define the integer lower envelope

\[
F(c)
=
10c^5+10q(c)c^4+10q(c)^2c^3.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By (5.3),

\[
\text{two-symbol contribution for }\{a,b\}\ge F(c(a,b)).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For `0<=c<=10`,

\[
F(c)=
0,210,2240,9990,30720,93750,233280,504210,983040,1771470,3000000.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Its first differences are

\[
210,2030,7750,20730,63030,139530,270930,478830,788430,1228530,
\tag{7.5}
\]

which are strictly increasing. Thus `F` is increasing and discretely convex on every possible eleven-vertex codegree.

### 7.1 Degree fifth powers

The degree sum is

\[
\sum_v d_v=54.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Because `x^5` is convex, among eleven integers at least four with sum 54, the minimum of `sum d_v^5` occurs at the balanced multiset

\[
\{4,5,5,\ldots,5\}
\]

(one 4 and ten 5's). Hence

\[
\sum_v d_v^5
\ge
4^5+10\cdot5^5
=
\boxed{32274}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

### 7.2 Total pair codegree

Double-count length-two wedges at their center:

\[
\sum_{\{a,b\}}c(a,b)
=
\sum_v\binom{d_v}{2}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The function `binom(d,2)` is discretely convex. Under (7.6) and (7.1), the same balanced degree multiset minimizes the right side, giving

\[
\sum_{\{a,b\}}c(a,b)
\ge
\binom42+10\binom52
=
6+100
=
\boxed{106}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

### 7.3 Minimize the two-symbol envelope

There are `binom(11,2)=55` unordered vertex pairs. Since `F` is increasing, a lower bound for a total codegree at least 106 is obtained by using total exactly 106. Since `F` is discretely convex, the exchange operation

\[
(c_i,c_j)\mapsto(c_i-1,c_j+1)
\qquad(c_i\ge c_j+2)
\]

never increases `F(c_i)+F(c_j)`. Therefore the minimum occurs when all 55 codegrees differ by at most one.

The balanced representation of 106 in 55 slots is

\[
106=51\cdot2+4\cdot1.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{\{a,b\}}F(c(a,b))
\ge
51F(2)+4F(1)
=
51\cdot2240+4\cdot210
=
\boxed{115080}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

### 7.4 Finish

Keeping only the one-symbol and two-symbol tuples in the nonnegative exact sum (5.1), equations (7.7) and (7.10) give

\[
\operatorname{hom}(H,G)
\ge
32274+115080
=
\boxed{147354}.
\tag{7.11}
\]

But

\[
\left\lceil\frac{54^{15}}{11^{20}}\right\rceil=143898,

so

\[
147354>143898.

Equivalently, the exact cross-multiplication margin is

\[
147354\cdot11^{20}-54^{15}

\tag{7.12}
\]

This proves (4.1) in the minimum-degree-four branch and completes the theorem.

### Independent re-derivation

For the source BAB generator, at `theta=0` the absorbing states are exactly the nonempty independent sets. The non-independent block is a pure-death matrix triangular by particle number, with strictly negative diagonal, hence invertible. Schur complement through that fast block gives the first-order generator on independent sets

\[
M=-\operatorname{diag}(b)+RH.
\]

Starting from an independent set `I`, one birth at `v` with `k=|N(v)\cap I|` produces a star with center `v` and `k` occupied leaves. While `m` leaves remain, the center dies at rate `m` and the leaves together die at rate `m`; hence each stage chooses center versus a leaf with probability `1/2`.

A same-cardinality outcome is possible only for `k=1`: the old leaf dies first with probability `1/2`, producing the legal token slide `I-u+v`. Thus the diagonal block of `M` on independent `r`-sets is exactly rate-`1/2` simple exclusion restricted to independent sets, with Dirichlet killing for proposed moves leaving the independent-set domain.

For `r=1`, this is the ordinary one-particle generator `-L_G/2`; its nonzero spectral coefficient is `lambda_2(L_G)/2`.

For `r>=2`, every token-sliding component has access to a killed move. Indeed, choose a pair of occupied vertices at minimum graph distance and move one token along a shortest path toward the other. Until the final step, minimality of the chosen distance makes each move legal; the final proposed move creates adjacency and is killed. Therefore the Dirichlet form of every `r>=2` block is positive definite and its principal Dirichlet eigenvalue `lambda_D^{(r)}(G)` is strictly positive.

Because `M` never increases independent-set cardinality, it is block triangular by cardinality. Its spectrum is therefore the union of the singleton block spectrum and the higher killed-block spectra. The slow nonzero first-order coefficient is exactly

\[
\gamma(G)
=
\min\left\{
\frac{\lambda_2(L_G)}2,
\min_{2\le r\le\alpha(G)}\lambda_D^{(r)}(G)
\right\}.
\]

The zero eigenvalue of the `theta=0` generator is semisimple: the fast block is invertible and the absorbing block is zero. Standard finite-dimensional analytic perturbation therefore gives the slow eigenvalues as `theta` times the eigenvalues of `M`, plus `O_G(theta^2)`. The remaining fast eigenvalues stay uniformly away from zero for fixed `G`. For `theta>0` the source chain is finite irreducible reversible, so the spectral gap is the slowest nonzero real mode. Hence

\[
\operatorname{gap}_{\rm BAB}(G,\theta)
=
\theta\gamma(G)+O_G(\theta^2)
\]

and

\[
\tau_{\rm BAB}(G,\theta)
=
\frac1{\theta\gamma(G)}+O_G(1).
