## 3. B24 — find the extremal object: reduce to the regular slice

The order-nine and order-ten exact enumerations both found the complete graph as the finite minimizer of the normalized Sidorenko ratio. Rather than enumerate order eleven, I asked whether the source theorem itself permits us to freeze the candidate extremizer.

By the regular-host reduction (2.2), it is enough to consider `d`-regular graphons. If `d=0`, the inequality is trivial. For `d>0`, define

\[
A(x,y)=\frac{W(x,y)}d.
\]

Then `A` is a symmetric nonnegative kernel with

\[
\int_0^1 A(x,y)\,dy=1\qquad\text{for a.e. }x.
\tag{3.1}
\]

It need not take values in `[0,1]`; it is a bounded normalized kernel. Since `e(H)=15`, homogeneity gives

\[
t_H(W)=d^{15}t_H(A).
\]

Consequently the frozen source target is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{\quad t_H(A)\ge1\quad}
\tag{3.2}
\]

for every symmetric nonnegative **1-regular** kernel `A`. The constant kernel `A\equiv1` is therefore the exact equality candidate on this normalized slice. This does not prove it is the global minimizer, but it removes edge-density bookkeeping and makes the finite `K_n` extremizers conceptually consistent with the continuum target.

## 4. B25 — count the same thing twice: exact triple-moment reduction

Label the left vertices by `x_i` and right vertices by `u_i`, `i in Z/5Z`, so that

\[
u_i\sim x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The two missing neighbors of `u_i` are `x_{i+3},x_{i+4}`; these missing edges form one 10-cycle, so (4.1) is exactly `K_{5,5}\setminus C_{10}`.

For a normalized kernel `A`, define its third common-neighbor moment

\[
C_A(x,y,z)
:=
\int_0^1 A(u,x)A(u,y)A(u,z)\,du.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Integrating the five right-side variables `u_i` first gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 t_H(A)
 =
 \int_{[0,1]^5}
 \prod_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}
 C_A(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2})
 \,dx_0\cdots dx_4.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the first useful double count: the ten-variable graph-homomorphism integral becomes a five-variable tight-cycle contraction of a very special three-kernel.

Regularity supplies the exact normalization

\[
\int_{[0,1]^3} C_A(x,y,z)\,dxdydz
=
\int_0^1\left(\int_0^1 A(u,x)\,dx\right)^3du
=1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The source problem is therefore equivalent to proving

\[
\mathcal T_5(C_A)\ge1
\tag{4.5}
\]

for every `C_A` arising from a symmetric nonnegative 1-regular kernel through (4.2).

A crucial scope warning: I do **not** replace this by the stronger assertion for arbitrary nonnegative symmetric 3-kernels of mean one. Hypergraph analogues of Sidorenko fail in general, so the latent third-moment representation (4.2) is essential structure, not decoration.

### Written proof of (4.3)

Starting from the definition of `t_H(A)` and the adjacency description (4.1), the integrand is

\[
\prod_{i=0}^4
 A(u_i,x_i)A(u_i,x_{i+1})A(u_i,x_{i+2}).
\]

For fixed `x_0,...,x_4`, the variables `u_0,...,u_4` occur in disjoint factors. Fubini therefore factors their integral into

\[
\prod_{i=0}^4
 \int A(u_i,x_i)A(u_i,x_{i+1})A(u_i,x_{i+2})\,du_i,
\]

which is exactly the product in (4.3). No approximation or finite-host assumption is used.

## 5. B14 — find the group acting: cyclic transfer operator on finite regular hosts

The cyclic `Z/5Z` symmetry in (4.3) turns the same contraction into a closed-walk trace on pair states. This is exact for a finite uniform state space and exposes a spectral obstruction that the raw graph statement hides.

Let `A=(A_{ij})` be an `n x n` symmetric nonnegative matrix normalized by

\[
\frac1n\sum_j A_{ij}=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
C_{ijk}:=\frac1n\sum_u A_{ui}A_{uj}A_{uk}
\tag{5.2}
\]

and an `n^2 x n^2` transfer matrix `T` indexed by ordered pairs by

\[
T_{(i,j),(j,k)}=\frac1n C_{ijk},
\qquad
T_{(i,j),(p,q)}=0\ \text{if }p\ne j.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Expanding the diagonal of `T^5` gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(T^5)
=
\frac1{n^5}
\sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_4}
\prod_{r=0}^4 C_{i_r,i_{r+1},i_{r+2}}
=t_H(A),
\tag{5.4}
\]

with indices modulo five. Thus the unresolved source inequality on finite regular weighted hosts is a **fifth trace-moment inequality**.

There is additional positivity. Let `R` reverse ordered pairs,

\[
(Rf)(i,j)=f(j,i),
\]

and put `S=RT`. Then `R` is an orthogonal involution and `S` is block diagonal in the first coordinate. Its block at `i` is `(C_{ijk}/n)_{j,k}`. For every real vector `v`,

\[
\sum_{j,k}v_j\frac{C_{ijk}}n v_k
=
\frac1{n^2}\sum_u A_{ui}
\left(\sum_j A_{uj}v_j\right)^2
\ge0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{S=RT\succeq0,\qquad T=RS,\qquad t_H(A)=\operatorname{Tr}((RS)^5).}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the new structural normal form `C30-REGULAR-MOMENT-TRANSFER-01`.

It also clarifies the Lee–Schülke twisted-cycle language. The same transfer construction with a cycle of length `k` evaluates `C_k^{\bowtie}` as `Tr(T^k)`. At `k=3` this is `K_{3,3}`, and at `k=4` it is the cube, both already known Sidorenko classes; `k=5` is the frozen Möbius graph. A naive positive-operator interpolation does not close the problem because `T=RS` itself need not be positive semidefinite: the reflection `R` can create negative spectral contributions to odd traces. What remains is to exploit the stronger **block moment structure** (5.2),(5.5), not merely `S\succeq0`.

This is a structural reduction, not a proof of (3.2).
