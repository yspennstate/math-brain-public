## 4. The new theorem

### Theorem C30-RANK2-REGULAR-01

Let `H=K_{5,5}\setminus C_{10}`. Let `A:[0,1]^2 -> R_{>=0}` be a bounded measurable symmetric kernel satisfying

\[
\int_0^1 A(x,y)\,dy=1\qquad\text{for a.e. }x.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Assume the self-adjoint integral operator with kernel `A` has rank at most two. Then

\[
\boxed{t_H(A)\ge1.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover, equality holds only for `A=1` almost everywhere.

Consequently, if `W` is a `d`-regular graphon with `d>0` and `W/d` has operator rank at most two, then

\[
t_H(W)\ge d^{15}=t(K_2,W)^{15}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a **PARTIAL** source-valid theorem on a restricted host class. It does not move the frozen finite-host-order metric and does not prove the universal conjecture.

## 5. Proof: spectral normal form

Because `A` is symmetric and 1-regular, the constant function `1` is a unit eigenfunction with eigenvalue `1`. If the operator has rank one, spectral decomposition gives `A=1` almost everywhere and there is nothing to prove.

Otherwise rank is exactly two. Choose a real unit eigenfunction `f` orthogonal to constants for the remaining eigenspace. Then, in `L^2([0,1]^2)`, hence after changing the kernel on a null set if necessary,

\[
A(x,y)=1+\lambda f(x)f(y),
\qquad
\int f=0,
\qquad
\int f^2=1
\tag{5.1}
\]

for one nonzero real eigenvalue `lambda`. Put

\[
r:=\int_0^1 f^3.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The proof is now one exact polynomial inequality in `lambda` and `r`.

## 6. Exact edge-subset expansion

Use the cyclic presentation in which right vertex `R_i` is adjacent to left vertices `L_i,L_{i+1},L_{i+2}` modulo five. Expand

\[
\prod_{uv\in E(H)}\bigl(1+\lambda f(x_u)f(x_v)\bigr)
\tag{6.1}
\]

by choosing an edge subset `F`. Since `H` is cubic, each vertex degree inside `F` is `0,1,2`, or `3`. A term with a degree-one vertex vanishes because `int f=0`. Every surviving term contributes

\[
\lambda^{|F|}r^{n_3(F)},
\tag{6.2}
\]

because the second moment is one. The number `n_3(F)` of degree-three vertices is even by the handshaking lemma.

The exact `2^15` subset classification gives the following polynomial, where `x=r^2`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(\lambda,x):=t_H(A)
={}&1+5\lambda^4+15\lambda^6+5x\lambda^7
 +(30+10x)\lambda^8+60x\lambda^9\\
&+(13+70x+15x^2)\lambda^{10}
 +(95x+60x^2)\lambda^{11}\\
&+(145x^2+10x^3)\lambda^{12}
 +75x^3\lambda^{13}+15x^4\lambda^{14}+x^5\lambda^{15}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

There are exactly 625 surviving edge subsets. This finite classification is independently machine-certified in exact integer/rational arithmetic by the new checker in section 9.

If `lambda>0`, every nonconstant term in (6.3) is nonnegative and the term `5 lambda^4` is positive. Hence `P(lambda,x)>1` immediately. It remains to treat the genuinely dangerous case `lambda<0`, where the odd powers can have negative sign.

## 7. B5: follow the negative spectral ray through its full positivity interval

Write

\[
\lambda=-s,\qquad s>0.
\]

Nonnegativity of `A=1-s f\otimes f` forces an exact skewness bound.

First, if `M=s^{-1/2}`, then

\[
|f|\le M\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Indeed, if `f>M+epsilon` on a positive-measure set, then on the square of that set `1-s f(x)f(y)<0`, contradicting `A>=0`; the negative side is identical. Since `int f^2=1`, necessarily `M>=1`, i.e. `0<s<=1`.

Now use the two pointwise nonnegative cubics

\[
(M-f)(f+M^{-1})^2\ge0,
\qquad
(M+f)(f-M^{-1})^2\ge0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Taking expectations and using `E f=0`, `E f^2=1` gives

\[
-M+M^{-1}\le r\le M-M^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{x=r^2\le\frac{(1-s)^2}{s}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This moment inequality is sharp: equality is attained by an appropriate two-point distribution.

It remains to prove

\[
P(-s,x)-1\ge0
\qquad
\left(0<s\le1,\quad0\le x\le\frac{(1-s)^2}{s}\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

## 8. Exact positivity on the whole two-parameter domain

The function

\[
\phi(a)=\frac{(1-a)^2}{a}
\]

is continuous and strictly decreasing from `+infinity` to `0` on `(0,1]`. Hence every pair `(s,x)` in the domain (7.4) can be written uniquely as

\[
x=\phi(a)=\frac{(1-a)^2}{a},
\qquad
s=ay,
\qquad
0<a\le1,\quad0<y\le1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Substituting (8.1) into (6.3) gives the exact factorization

\[
P(-ay,(1-a)^2/a)-1=a^4y^4R(a,y),
\tag{8.2}
\]

where `R` is a polynomial of bidegree `(16,11)`.

Write `R` in the tensor-product Bernstein basis on the unit square:

\[
R(a,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{16}\sum_{j=0}^{11}
 b_{ij}B_i^{16}(a)B_j^{11}(y).
\tag{8.3}
\]

The exact checker computes all 204 rational coefficients and proves

\[
\boxed{5\le b_{ij}\le63\quad\text{for every }i,j.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Because every Bernstein basis function is nonnegative on `[0,1]` and the tensor-product basis sums to one,

\[
R(a,y)\ge5.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus (8.2) is strictly positive whenever `s>0`. Combined with the positive-`lambda` case, this proves (4.2) and shows that equality forces `lambda=0`, hence `A=1` almost everywhere. QED.

This is the B3 outcome: the run proves the source inequality not merely for a finite list of two-block examples, but for **every bounded symmetric nonnegative 1-regular kernel of operator rank at most two**, including non-step kernels.

The source process is Aldous–Fill Open Problem 14.31. On each oriented edge `(w,v)` with `w` occupied, an occupied `v` is emptied at rate `1`, while an empty `v` is occupied at rate `theta`; the empty configuration is excluded. Aldous–Fill's original page confirms this normalization and the conditioned Bernoulli stationary law.

Hartarsky–Martinelli–Toninelli's CBSEP resamples each nonempty edge at rate one from Bernoulli(`p`) squared conditioned nonempty. Thus for

\[
p=\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}
\]

the local rates on states `10,01,11` are exactly

\[
10\leftrightarrow01:\frac1{\theta+2},
\qquad
10\to11=01\to11:\frac\theta{\theta+2},
\qquad
11\to10=11\to01:\frac1{\theta+2}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

For the common conditioned Bernoulli law, if `r=mu(10)=mu(01)` then `mu(11)=theta r`. Hence the CBSEP branch/coalescence conductances are `theta r/(theta+2)`, while the source BAB conductances are `theta r`. Therefore

\[
D_{BAB}=(\theta+2)D_{bc}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

The only extra CBSEP edge is the exclusion chord `10<->01`, with conductance `r/(theta+2)`. The elementary triangle inequality

\[
(f_{10}-f_{01})^2
\le2(f_{10}-f_{11})^2+2(f_{01}-f_{11})^2

\[
D_{sep}\le\frac2\theta D_{bc}.
\tag{11.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\theta D_{CB}\le D_{BAB}\le(\theta+2)D_{CB},

and the Rayleigh quotient yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\tau_{CB}}{\theta+2}
\le\tau_{BAB}

\tag{11.4}
