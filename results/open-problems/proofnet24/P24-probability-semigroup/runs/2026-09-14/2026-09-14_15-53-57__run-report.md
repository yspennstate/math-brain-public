## 2. Primary theorem checked

The only non-elementary anti-concentration input in the new edge proof is the bounded-density marginal theorem for product measures.

Livshyts–Paouris–Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures* (Israel J. Math. 216 (2016), arXiv:1507.07949), prove:

> If
> \[
> f(x)=\prod_{i=1}^m f_i(x_i),
> \qquad
> \|f_i\|_\infty\le1,
> \]
> then every \(k\)-dimensional marginal density is bounded by \(2^{k/2}\).

For \(k=1\), after scaling a coordinate density bounded by \(K\), every unit linear form satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|f_{\langle X,q\rangle}\|_\infty
\le
\sqrt2\,K,
\qquad
\|q\|_2=1.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

No moment assumption enters (2.1).

Everything else in the proof below is elementary:

- Markov;
- truncation;
- Cauchy–Schwarz;
- a conditional Laplace-transform Chernoff argument;
- exact projector algebra.

## 3. Exact conditional geometry

Condition on:

1. a full-rank shared block \(A\);
2. the first private block, hence the first residual unit direction \(v\).

Let
\[
E=E_A,
\qquad
\dim E=D.
\]
Let
\[
Q_A:\mathbb R^D\to E\subset\mathbb R^m
\]
be the fixed isometry used by P14, and put
\[
q=Q_Av\in E.
\]
Then \(q\) is a fixed ambient unit vector under the conditioning.

The second private block consists of fresh original product rows
\[
X_1,\ldots,X_{D-1}
\]
independent of \((A,v)\).

Let
\[
Y_i=Q_A^\top X_i.
\]
The \(Y_i\)'s are iid as vectors, but their coordinates are generally dependent. This dependence is preserved exactly.

Let \(w\) be the unit null direction of the second residual matrix.

If
\[
1-(v\cdot w)^2\le y,
\qquad
0<y\le1/2,
\]
choose the sign of \(w\) so that
\[
v\cdot w\ge0.
\]
Then
\[
\|v-w\|^2
=
2(1-v\cdot w)
\le
2y.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Since every second-private projected row is orthogonal to \(w\),
\[
\langle Y_i,w\rangle=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
|\langle Y_i,v\rangle|
\le
\|Y_i\|\|v-w\|
\le
\sqrt{2y}\,\|Y_i\|.
\]
Returning to ambient coordinates gives the exact implication
\[
\boxed{
|\langle X_i,q\rangle|
\le
\sqrt{2y}\,
\|P_EX_i\|,
\qquad
i=1,\ldots,D-1.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since the rows are independent,
\[
\boxed{
F_D(y\mid A,v)
:=
\Pr\{
1-(v\cdot w)^2\le y
\mid A,v
\}
\le
p_{E,q}(y)^{D-1},
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
where
\[
p_{E,q}(y)
=
\Pr\left\{
|\langle X,q\rangle|
\le
\sqrt{2y}\|P_EX\|
\right\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The ultra-edge problem has therefore been reduced to one fresh row.

Under (0.1), for every deterministic \(D\)-dimensional subspace \(E\) and unit \(q\in E\),

\boxed{
p_{E,q}(y)
\le
C_K(Dy)^{1/3},
\qquad

\tag{4.1}

Consequently there is a deterministic
\[
a_0=a_0(K)\in(0,1)

such that
\[
\boxed{
F_D(y\mid A,v)
\le
\left[
C_K(Dy)^{1/3}
\right]^{D-1},
\qquad
0<y\le a_0/D,

\tag{P24-ROW-ULTRA-FV}
\]
and
\[
C_Ka_0^{1/3}\le1/2.
\tag{4.2}

Put
\[
S=\|P_EX\|^2.
\]
Isotropy gives exactly
\[
\mathbb ES=D.
\tag{4.3}

For every \(R\ge1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{E,q}(y)
&\le
\Pr\{
|\langle X,q\rangle|
\le
R\sqrt{2Dy}

+
\Pr\{S>R^2D\}.

\[
\Pr\{
|\langle X,q\rangle|
\le
R\sqrt{2Dy}

\le
4KR\sqrt{Dy}.
\tag{4.5}

By Markov and (4.3),
\[
\Pr\{S>R^2D\}
\le
R^{-2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Write
\[
z=Dy.
\]
For \(0<z\le1\), choose
\[
R=z^{-1/6}\ge1.
\]
Then
\[
4KR\sqrt z
=
4Kz^{1/3},
\qquad
R^{-2}=z^{1/3}.

Hence
\[
p_{E,q}(y)
\le
(4K+1)(Dy)^{1/3}.

Take
\[

and, for example,

=

## 5. Finite-variance uniform integrability of unit linear forms

The mesoscopic proof needs a lower probability of a nontrivial angular loss. For finite variance, the correct replacement for a \(2+\delta\) moment is **uniform integrability**, not a higher moment.

Let
\[
Z_q=(q^\top X)^2,
\qquad
\|q\|_2=1.
\]

### Lemma P24-UI-FV — PROVED

For one fixed centered variance-one coordinate law,
\[
\boxed{
\{Z_q:\ m\ge1,\ \|q\|_2=1\}
\quad\text{is uniformly integrable.}
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

More quantitatively, if
\[
\delta_L
=
\mathbb E[\xi^2;\ |\xi|>L],
\]
then for every event \(H\) with
\[
\Pr(H)\le u,
\]
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{m,\|q\|=1}
\mathbb E[Z_q1_H]
\le
\omega(u),
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
where one may take
\[
\omega(u)
=
\inf_{L\ge1}
\left\{
\sqrt{3+4L^2}\,\sqrt u
+
2\sqrt{\delta_L}
\right\},
\qquad
\omega(u)\downarrow0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
\eta
=
\xi1_{\{|\xi|\le L\}}
-
\mathbb E[\xi1_{\{|\xi|\le L\}}],
\]
and
\[
r=\xi-\eta.
\]
Then \(r\) is centered and
\[
\mathbb Er^2
\le
\delta_L.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Write
\[
Y_q=\sum_iq_i\xi_i,
\qquad
Y_q^{(L)}=\sum_iq_i\eta_i.
\]
Then
\[
\|Y_q-Y_q^{(L)}\|_2\le\sqrt{\delta_L},
\qquad
\|Y_q\|_2=1,
\qquad
\|Y_q^{(L)}\|_2\le1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E
\left|
Y_q^2-(Y_q^{(L)})^2
\right|
\le
2\sqrt{\delta_L}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The bounded centered variable \(\eta\) has
\[
\mathbb E\eta^2\le1
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E\eta^4
\le4L^2.
\]
The exact fourth-moment expansion for the independent centered sum gives
\[
\sup_{\|q\|=1}
\mathbb E|Y_q^{(L)}|^4
\le
3+4L^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence
\[
\mathbb E[(Y_q^{(L)})^2 1_H]
\le
\sqrt{3+4L^2}\,\sqrt u.
\]
Combining with (5.5) yields (5.2)–(5.3).

## 6. Uniform \(L^1\) concentration of projected energy

Let \(P\) be any deterministic rank-\(k\) orthogonal projector and
\[
S=X^\top PX.
\]

### Lemma P24-ENERGY-FV — PROVED

There exists a deterministic
\[
d_k=d_k(\operatorname{Law}\xi)\downarrow0
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{m,P:\operatorname{rank}P=k}
\frac1k\mathbb E|S-k|
\le
d_k.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

An explicit admissible choice is
\[
d_k
=
2k^{-1/4}
+
2\mathbb E[
|\xi^2-1|;
|\xi^2-1|>k^{1/4}
]
+
\sqrt{2/k}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Write
\[
S-k
=
D_P+O_P,
\]
where
\[
D_P
=
\sum_iP_{ii}(\xi_i^2-1),
\]
and
\[
O_P
=
2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\xi_i\xi_j.
\]

For the off-diagonal part,
\[
\mathbb EO_P^2
=
4\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2
\le
2\operatorname{tr}(P^2)
=
2k,
\]
so
\[
k^{-1}\mathbb E|O_P|
\le
\sqrt{2/k}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For the diagonal part, truncate and center
\[
Y=\xi^2-1
\]
at level
\[
L=k^{1/4}.
\]
Since
\[
0\le P_{ii}\le1,
\qquad
\sum_iP_{ii}=k,
\qquad
\sum_iP_{ii}^2\le k,
\]
the bounded centered contribution is \(O(k^{3/4})\) in \(L^1\), while the discarded centered tail contributes at most
\[
2k\mathbb E[
|Y|;
|Y|>k^{1/4}
].
\]
Dividing by \(k\) gives (6.2).

Only integrability of \(\xi^2\) is used.

## 7. New theorem P24-STEP-LOSS-FV

This is the main finite-variance replacement for P14's \(2+\delta\) one-step moderate-loss lemma.

Let
\[
E\subset\mathbb R^m,
\qquad
\dim E=k,
\]
and let \(q\in E\) be a deterministic unit vector. Define
\[
U_E
=
\frac{P_EX}{\|P_EX\|},
\]
and
\[
L_{E,q}
=
(q\cdot U_E)^2.
\]

### Theorem P24-STEP-LOSS-FV — PROVED

There exist constants
\[
k_0=k_0(\operatorname{Law}\xi)<\infty,
\]
\[
c_1=\frac18,
\]
and
\[
p_0=p_0(\operatorname{Law}\xi)>0
\]
such that, for every
\[
k\ge k_0,
\]
every ambient dimension \(m\), every deterministic \(k\)-dimensional subspace \(E\), and every unit \(q\in E\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{
L_{E,q}\ge\frac{1}{8k}
\right\}
\ge
p_0.
}
\tag{P24-STEP-LOSS-FV}
\]

No density hypothesis is needed for this lemma; finite variance alone suffices.

### Proof

Put
\[
Z=(q^\top X)^2,
\qquad
S=\|P_EX\|^2.
\]
Then
\[
L_{E,q}=Z/S.
\]

By P24-UI-FV, choose \(M\ge4\) so large that
\[
\omega(1/M)\le1/4.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Since
\[
\mathbb EZ=1,
\]
Markov gives
\[
\Pr\{Z>M\}\le1/M.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E[Z;Z>M]
\le
\omega(1/M)
\le
1/4.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Also
\[
\mathbb E[Z;Z<1/4]\le1/4.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[
Z;
1/4\le Z\le M
]
\ge1/2.
\]
Since \(Z\le M\) on this event,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{
1/4\le Z\le M
\}
\ge
\frac1{2M}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

By P24-ENERGY-FV,
\[
\Pr\{S>2k\}
\le
d_k.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Choose \(k_0\) so large that
\[
d_k\le\frac1{4M}
\qquad(k\ge k_0).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Combining (7.4) and (7.5),
\[
\Pr\{
1/4\le Z\le M,\ S\le2k
\}
\ge
\frac1{4M}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

On that event,
\[
\frac ZS
\ge
\frac{1/4}{2k}
=
\frac1{8k}.
\]
Thus the theorem holds with
\[
\boxed{
p_0=\frac1{4M}.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

### What is and is not uniform

The threshold \(1/(8k)\) is universal.

The success probability \(p_0\) and onset dimension \(k_0\) depend on the fixed coordinate law through its finite-variance tail.

They cannot in general be made uniform over all finite-variance laws using only a density cap \(K\) and variance one: one may put arbitrarily small mass in very distant smooth symmetric bumps and keep most mass in a narrow bounded-density central component, forcing the moderate-value probability of one coordinate to become arbitrarily small.

## 8. Sequential angular-loss product

Return to the fixed-apex edge problem and keep \(v\) fixed.

Expose the second private rows sequentially. Let
\[
E_D=E_A,
\]
and
\[
E_{k-1}
=
E_k\cap X_k^\perp,
\qquad
k=D,D-1,\ldots,2.
\]
Let \(P_k\) be the projector onto \(E_k\) and define
\[
r_k=\|P_kv\|.
\]

If
\[
r_k>0,

set
\[
q_k=P_kv/r_k,

\[
u_k=P_kX_k/\|P_kX_k\|,

\[
Z_k=(q_k\cdot u_k)^2.

P_{k-1}=P_k-u_ku_k^\top.

r_{k-1}^2
=

\tag{8.1}

\[
r_D=1

and
\[
r_1^2=(v\cdot w)^2,

Conditional on all previously exposed rows, \(E_k\) and \(q_k\) are deterministic and the next row \(X_k\) is a fresh original product vector.

## 9. New theorem P24-MIDEDGE-FV

Restrict to the top-half dimensions
\[
\lceil D/2\rceil\le k\le D.
\]
For
\[
D\ge2k_0,
\]
all these stages satisfy the one-step theorem.

Define
\[
I_k
=
1_{\{Z_k\ge1/(8k)\}}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{I_k=1\mid\mathcal F_k\}
\ge
p_0
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
for the natural reverse exposure filtration.

Let
\[
N_D
=
\sum_{k=\lceil D/2\rceil}^{D}I_k.
\]

### Adapted Chernoff bound

For every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
\mathbb E[
e^{-\lambda I_k}
\mid
\mathcal F_k
]
\le
1-p_0(1-e^{-\lambda}).
\]
Iteration yields the same lower-tail exponential form as a binomial with success probability \(p_0\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{
N_D\le\frac{p_0}{2}m_D
\right\}
\le
e^{-c_{\xi}D},
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
where
\[
m_D
=
\#\{k:\lceil D/2\rceil\le k\le D\}
\ge D/2.
\]

Now suppose
\[
1-(v\cdot w)^2\le y\le1/2.
\]
By (8.2),
\[
\prod_k(1-Z_k)\ge1-y.
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_kZ_k
\le
-\log(1-y)
\le
2y.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Every successful top-half stage contributes
\[
Z_k\ge\frac1{8k}\ge\frac1{8D}.
\]
Therefore
\[
N_D
\le
16yD.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Choose
\[
\boxed{
y_*
\le
\min\left\{
\frac12,
\frac{p_0}{128}
\right\}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]
Then for \(y\le y_*\),
\[
16yD
\le
\frac{p_0D}{8}
\le
\frac{p_0m_D}{2}.
\]
Thus the near-parallel event forces the Chernoff lower-tail event.

We have proved:

### Theorem P24-MIDEDGE-FV — PROVED

For all sufficiently large \(D\),
\[
\boxed{
F_D(y\mid A,v)
\le
e^{-c_\xi D},
\qquad
0<y\le y_*.
}
\tag{P24-MIDEDGE-FV}
\]

The theorem is uniform in the apex and first residual line.

As in P14, this bound is not integrated all the way to zero because a \(y\)-independent exponential bound is nonintegrable against \(y^{-3/2}\). The ultra-edge theorem handles that region.
