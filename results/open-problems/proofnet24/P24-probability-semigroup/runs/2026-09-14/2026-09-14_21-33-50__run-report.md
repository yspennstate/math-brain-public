# 3. Proof of `P24-DYADIC-BLOCK-CRITERION-05`

The theorem is deterministic once monotonicity of \(R_D\) is known.

For a dyadic integer

\[
q\le m,
\]

define

\[
q^+=\min(2q,m)
\]

and

\[
\mathcal B_m(q)
=
R_q-R_{q^+}.
\]

Because \(R_D\) is nonincreasing,

\[
\mathcal B_m(q)\ge0.
\]

## 3.1 Necessity

Assume

\[
R_D\le \frac C D
\qquad
(1\le D\le m).
\]

Then immediately

\[
R_m\le C/m.
\]

Also

\[
0\le
\mathcal B_m(q)
\le
R_q
\le
C/q.
\]

Thus (B1)--(B2) are necessary.

## 3.2 Sufficiency on dyadic residual dimensions

Assume

\[
R_m\le C_0/m
\]

and

\[
\mathcal B_m(q)\le C_1/q
\]

for every dyadic \(q\le m\).

Fix a dyadic \(q\le m\).

Starting at \(q_0=q\), define

\[
q_{r+1}
=
\min(2q_r,m)
\]

until the first index \(J\) with

\[
q_J=m.
\]

For \(r<J\),

\[
q_r=2^rq
\]

except that the last doubling may be truncated at \(m\).

Telescoping gives

\[
R_q
=
R_m
+
\sum_{r=0}^{J-1}
\left(
R_{q_r}-R_{q_{r+1}}
\right).
\]

Every difference is exactly

\[
\mathcal B_m(q_r).
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_q
&\le
\frac{C_0}{m}
+
\sum_{r=0}^{J-1}
\frac{C_1}{2^rq}
\\
&<
\frac{C_0}{q}
+
\frac{2C_1}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_q
\le
\frac{C_0+2C_1}{q}
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

for every dyadic \(q\le m\).

## 3.3 Extension to every D

For arbitrary

\[
1\le D\le m,
\]

choose the dyadic integer

\[
q=2^{\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor}.
\]

Then

\[
q\le D<2q.
\]

Since \(R_D\) is nonincreasing,

\[
R_D\le R_q.
\]

Using (3.1),

\[
R_D
\le
\frac{C_0+2C_1}{q}
<
\frac{
2(C_0+2C_1)
}{D}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
R_D
\le
\frac{
2(C_0+2C_1)
}{D}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This proves the equivalence up to universal constants.

QED.
