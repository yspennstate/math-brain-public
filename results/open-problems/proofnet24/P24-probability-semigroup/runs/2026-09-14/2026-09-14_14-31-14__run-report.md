## 3. The one-log shrinking-strip concentration theorem

For \(u\ge1\), define
\[
L_p(u)
=
u+\log\frac{e p}{\tau_p}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Put
\[
A_p=\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}p},
\qquad
B_p=\frac{L_p(u)}p,
\]
and
\[
q_p(\tau)
=
A_p\tau^{1/4}+B_p.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Define the relative scale
\[
\delta_p(\tau)
=
\frac{q_p(\tau)}{\sqrt\tau}
=
\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}{p\sqrt\tau}}
+
\frac{L_p(u)}{p\sqrt\tau}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The worst point is \(\tau=\tau_p\). Write
\[
\eta_p(u)
=
\frac{L_p(u)}{p\sqrt{\tau_p}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Then
\[
\delta_p(\tau_p)
=
\sqrt{\eta_p(u)}+\eta_p(u).
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem P24-XI-UNIFORM-01 — PROVED

There are constants \(c_0,C,c>0\) such that, whenever
\[
\eta_p(u)\le c_0,
\tag{3.6}
\]
with probability at least
\[
1-Ce^{-u},
\]
the following estimates hold simultaneously for all
\[
|x|\le R,
\qquad
\tau_p\le\tau\le\tau_0:
\]

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat b_p(x,\tau)-b(x,\tau)|
\le
Cq_p(\tau),
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat n_p(x,\tau)|
\le
C\sqrt\tau\,q_p(\tau),
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\frac1p\sum_{i=1}^p
\partial_xf_{x,\tau}(Y_i)
\right|
\le
Cq_p(\tau),
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

and
\[
\boxed{
|\partial_x\widehat b_p(x,\tau)-\partial_xb(x,\tau)|
\le
C\frac{q_p(\tau)}{\sqrt\tau}.
}
\tag{3.10}

Consequently,

\boxed{
|m_p(x,\tau)-m_\infty(x,\tau)|
\le
Cq_p(\tau),
}
\tag{3.12}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
|\partial_x(m_p-m_\infty)(x,\tau)|
\le
C\delta_p(\tau).

\tag{3.13}

In particular, if

\boxed{
\frac{\log(e p/\tau_p)}
     {p\sqrt{\tau_p}}
\to0,

\tag{3.14}

then the denominator self-averages uniformly on the shrinking strip and the empirical score derivative is uniformly small there.

## 4. Proof of P24-XI-UNIFORM-01

### 4.1 Dyadic peeling

Let
\[
\tau_j=2^j\tau_p
\]
and let \(j\) range until \(\tau_j\le\tau_0<2\tau_j\). On the \(j\)-th band
\[
I_j=[\tau_j,2\tau_j]\cap[\tau_p,\tau_0],
\]
the quantities
\[
\sqrt\tau,\quad
\tau^{1/4},\quad
q_p(\tau),\quad
\delta_p(\tau)
\]
are comparable, up to absolute factors, with their values at \(\tau_j\).

Set
\[
q_j=q_p(\tau_j).
\]

Under (3.6),
\[
q_j\le C\sqrt{\tau_j}\delta_p(\tau_j)
\]
is small enough that the following meshes are finer than the natural scales.

Choose an \(x\)-mesh with spacing
\[
\Delta x_j
=
c\sqrt{\tau_j}\,q_j,
\tag{4.1}
\]
and a \(\tau\)-mesh in \(I_j\) with spacing
\[
\Delta\tau_j
=
c\tau_jq_j.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The number of grid points in band \(j\) is at most
\[
N_j
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{\tau_j}q_j^2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because
\[
q_j^2
\ge
\frac{L_p(u)}p\sqrt{\tau_j},
\]
we get
\[
N_j
\le
\frac{Cp}{L_p(u)\tau_j}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Summing the geometric series,
\[
N_{\rm grid}
\le
\frac{Cp}{L_p(u)\tau_p}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Thus
\[
\log N_{\rm grid}
\le
\log\frac{Cp}{\tau_p}
\le
L_p(u)-u+C.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the source of the single logarithm.

### 4.2 Scalar Bernstein at the grid points

For each fixed parameter point, Bernstein's inequality for the empirical average gives
\[
|(P_p-P)g|
\lesssim
\sqrt{\frac{Pg^2\,L_p(u)}p}
+
\frac{\|g\|_\infty L_p(u)}p
\tag{4.7}
\]
outside probability \(2e^{-cL_p(u)}\), after harmless adjustment of constants.

Apply (4.7) to the four classes.

For \(w\), using (2.1) and (2.9),
\[
|(P_p-P)w|
\le
C\left[
\tau^{1/4}\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}p}
+
\frac{L_p(u)}p
\right]
=
Cq_p(\tau).
\tag{4.8}
\]

For \(f\), using (2.2), (2.10), and \(Pf=0\),
\[
|P_pf|
\le
C\left[
\tau^{3/4}\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}p}
+
\sqrt\tau\frac{L_p(u)}p
\right]
=
C\sqrt\tau\,q_p(\tau).
\tag{4.9}
\]

For \(d=\partial_xf\), using (2.3), (2.11), and \(Pd=0\),
\[
|P_pd|
\le
Cq_p(\tau).
\tag{4.10}
\]

For \(h=\partial_xw\), using (2.4) and (2.12),
\[
|(P_p-P)h|
\le
C\left[
\tau^{-1/4}\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}p}
+
\tau^{-1/2}\frac{L_p(u)}p
\right]
=
C\frac{q_p(\tau)}{\sqrt\tau}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The union bound over all grid points and the four classes is absorbed by (4.6), giving total failure probability \(Ce^{-u}\).

### 4.3 Passage from the grid to the whole strip

For two nearby parameter points,
\[
|(P_p-P)(g_\theta-g_{\theta'})|
\le
2\|g_\theta-g_{\theta'}\|_\infty.
\tag{4.12}
\]

For \(w\), the derivative bounds (2.5) and mesh sizes (4.1)–(4.2) give
\[
\tau_j^{-1/2}\Delta x_j
+
\tau_j^{-1}\Delta\tau_j
\le
Cq_j.
\]
This matches (4.8).

For \(f\), (2.6) gives
\[
\Delta x_j
+
\tau_j^{-1/2}\Delta\tau_j
\le
C\sqrt{\tau_j}q_j,
\]
matching (4.9).

For \(d=\partial_xf\), (2.7) gives an interpolation cost
\[
Cq_j,
\]
matching (4.10).

For \(h=\partial_xw\), (2.8) gives
\[
\tau_j^{-1}\Delta x_j
+
\tau_j^{-3/2}\Delta\tau_j
\le
C\frac{q_j}{\sqrt{\tau_j}},
\]
matching (4.11).

Thus (3.7)–(3.10) hold on the entire strip.

### 4.4 Ratio bounds

By (2.13),
\[
b(x,\tau)\ge c\sqrt\tau.
\]
Therefore (3.7) gives
\[
\left|\frac{\widehat b_p}{b}-1\right|
\le
C\delta_p(\tau).
\]
Under (3.6), the right side is less than \(1/2\) after reducing \(c_0\).

Since
\[
m_p-m_\infty
=
\frac{\widehat n_p}{\widehat b_p},
\]
(3.8) gives
\[
|m_p-m_\infty|
\le Cq_p(\tau).
\]

For the derivative,
\[
\partial_x(m_p-m_\infty)
=
\frac{P_p\partial_xf}{\widehat b_p}
-
(m_p-m_\infty)
\frac{\partial_x\widehat b_p}{\widehat b_p}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

The first term is \(O(\delta_p)\).

By (2.14) and (3.10),
\[
\frac{|\partial_x\widehat b_p|}{\widehat b_p}
\le
C+C\frac{q_p(\tau)}{\tau}.
\tag{4.14}
\]
Multiplying by the score bound \(O(q_p)\) gives
\[
O(q_p)+O\!\left(\frac{q_p^2}{\tau}\right)
=
O(q_p)+O(\delta_p^2).
\]
On the strip, \(q_p\le C\delta_p\), and under (3.6) we have \(\delta_p\le C<1\). Hence
\[
|\partial_x(m_p-m_\infty)|
\le
C\delta_p,
\]
which proves (3.13).

## 5. New nonlinear-path reduction

The uniform derivative estimate can now be inserted into the exact same-noise path equation.

Let
\[
r_p(x,\tau)=m_p(x,\tau)-m_\infty(x,\tau).
\]

Suppose that on a localization event both the population path and a candidate finite-\(p\) path remain inside \(|x|\le R\), and suppose a bootstrap gives
\[
\sup_{\tau_p\le\tau\le\tau_0}
|X_\tau^{(p)}-X_\tau^{(\infty)}|
\le
\frac{M}{\sqrt p}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By the mean value theorem and (3.13),
\[
\sqrt p\,
|r_p(X_\tau^{(p)},\tau)-r_p(X_\tau^{(\infty)},\tau)|
\le
CM\delta_p(\tau).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The path equation divides this term by \(\tau\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&
\sqrt p
\int_{\tau_p}^{\tau_0}
\frac{
|r_p(X_\tau^{(p)},\tau)-r_p(X_\tau^{(\infty)},\tau)|
}{\tau}\,d\tau
\\
&\qquad\le
CM
\int_{\tau_p}^{\tau_0}
\frac{\delta_p(\tau)}{\tau}\,d\tau.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using (3.3),
\[
\int_{\tau_p}^{\tau_0}
\frac{\delta_p(\tau)}{\tau}\,d\tau
\le
C
\left[
\sqrt{\frac{L_p(u)}{p\sqrt{\tau_p}}}
+
\frac{L_p(u)}{p\sqrt{\tau_p}}
\right].
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence:

### Corollary P24-XI-NONLINEAR-REDUCTION — PROVED

Under the localization/bootstrap hypothesis (5.1),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&
\sqrt p
\int_{\tau_p}^{\tau_0}
\frac{
|r_p(X_\tau^{(p)},\tau)-r_p(X_\tau^{(\infty)},\tau)|
}{\tau}\,d\tau
\\
&\qquad\le
CM
\{\sqrt{\eta_p(u)}+\eta_p(u)\}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the nonlinear empirical evaluation error vanishes under the same one-log condition
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log(e p/\tau_p)}
     {p\sqrt{\tau_p}}
\to0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

There is no hidden singular Gronwall factor from the population drift. Indeed
\[
\partial_xm_\infty=\frac1{g(\tau)},
\]
so
\[
\frac{\partial_xm_\infty-1}{\tau}
=
-\frac{\kappa}{g(\tau)},
\tag{5.7}
\]
which is uniformly bounded on the strip.

This is the key new consequence: after the present concentration theorem, the nonlinear path evaluation no longer appears to be the obstacle to the shrinking-strip CLT.

## 6. What remains for P11

P11's local theorem already gives the correct one-time Gaussian scale and matches the Hermite endpoint variance exactly. The present run adds simultaneous denominator/score/derivative concentration and kills the nonlinear evaluation remainder under a single logarithmic penalty.

The missing theorem is now much narrower:

> **P24/P11-LINEAR-ENDPOINT.** Conditional on the Brownian environment, prove tightness and finite-dimensional convergence of the integrated **linear** empirical influence process on a strip whose lower endpoint tends to zero, ideally all the way to the endpoint, and identify the limit with the existing common-\(B\), common-\(\xi\) Hermite process \(\Xi\).

There is strong structural evidence for this target. P11's Hermite analysis already shows that the limiting integrated influence has an almost-sure \(L^2\) endpoint, while the pointwise forcing has \(L^2\)-size \(\tau^{-3/4}\), which is time-integrable:
\[
\int_0^\varepsilon\tau^{-3/4}d\tau
=
4\varepsilon^{1/4}.
\]

The remaining issue is a functional empirical-process theorem, not denominator instability.

## 7. Route comparison for the new P11 target

### Route A — dyadic mesh + scalar Bernstein

**Succeeds in this run** for denominator, numerator and first derivative.

Advantages:

- elementary;
- exact dimension-free constants on compact spatial sets;
- keeps all pattern independence explicit;
- costs only one logarithm;
- sufficient for nonlinear-path reduction.

Limitation:

- does not by itself give the Gaussian limit of the integrated linear forcing.

### Route B — ratio-type empirical-process concentration

Giné–Koltchinskii study precisely variance-normalized empirical suprema of the form
\[
\sup \frac{|(P_p-P)f|}{\phi(\sigma_Pf)}.
\]
Their localization technology is a plausible route to reducing or removing the explicit
\[
\log(e p/\tau_p)
\]
loss in P24-XI-UNIFORM-01.

This is a refinement route, not needed for the first shrinking-strip theorem.

### Route C — Hermite / Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup

This is now the strongest candidate for the **linear endpoint process**. The integrated limit already has the exact common-\(B\), common-\(\xi\) Hermite expansion and an \(L^2\) endpoint. A useful next proof would identify the finite-\(p\) integrated influence functions as a pre-Gaussian one-parameter class whose canonical metric inherits the endpoint \(1/4\) scale.

### Route D — martingale / exchangeable-pair

Potentially useful for a maximal inequality for the integrated influence process, but less direct than the Hermite representation. There is no obvious martingale in the shrinking-time parameter before an additional representation is found.

### Route E — transportation / functional inequalities

Not needed for the Gaussian pattern model. The difficulty is a shrinking, localized empirical-process index set, not poor concentration of the base measure.

=
\left(\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}\right)^2
=
\left(\frac{\delta_0}{2+\delta_0}\right)^2.
\]

For a deterministic rank-\(k\) projector \(P\) and unit \(q\in\operatorname{ran}P\), write
\[
S=X^TPX,
\qquad
Z=(q^TX)^2.
\]

Decompose
\[
S-k
=
\sum_iP_{ii}(\xi_i^2-1)
+
2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\xi_i\xi_j.
\]

For the diagonal term, von Bahr–Esseen at exponent \(\nu\in(1,2]\) gives
\[
E\left|
\sum_iP_{ii}(\xi_i^2-1)
\right|^\nu
\le
C
\sum_iP_{ii}^\nu
E|\xi^2-1|^\nu
\le
Ck,
\]
because \(2\nu=2+\delta_0\le2+\delta\).

For the off-diagonal term,
\[
E\left(
2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\xi_i\xi_j
\right)^2
=
4\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2
\le2k.
\]
Since \(\nu\le2\),
\[
E|O_P|^\nu\le Ck^{\nu/2}\le Ck.
\]
Thus
\[
E|S-k|^\nu\le Ck.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Rosenthal's inequality at exponent
\[
2\nu=2+\delta_0>2
\]
gives
\[
\sup_{\|q\|=1}
E|q^TX|^{2\nu}
\le C.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Choose
\[
a=\frac{2\nu-1}{\nu^2}
\]
and
\[
G=\{S\le k+k^a\}.
\]
Markov plus (8.1) gives
\[
P(G^c)
\le
Ck^{1-a\nu}.
\]
Hölder with (8.2) yields
\[
E[Z1_{G^c}]
\le
C
P(G^c)^{(\nu-1)/\nu}
\le
Ck^{-\beta_*},
\]
because
\[
(\nu-1)\left(a-\frac1\nu\right)=\beta_*.
\]
Also
\[
1-a=\beta_*.
\]
Hence
\[
E\frac ZS
\ge
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta_*}}{k+k^a}
\ge
\frac{1-C'k^{-\beta_*}}k.
\]
Therefore
\[
E[U_EU_E^T]
\succeq
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta_*}}kP_E.
\]

The sequential projector recursion is exact:
\[
P_{k-1}=P_k-u_ku_k^T.
\]
Conditioning on the past leaves the next original product vector fresh. Therefore
\[
E[P_{k-1}\mid E_k]
\preceq
\left(
1-\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta_*}}k
\right)P_k.
\]
The product telescopes with defect
\[
\sum_k k^{-1-\beta_*}<\infty,
\]
giving
\[
\boxed{
E P_{\rm final}
\preceq
\frac{C_{\delta,B}}D P_E.
}
\]

I find no conditioning, exponent or moment gap.

The primary von Bahr–Esseen theorem is exactly for \(1\le r\le2\), and Rosenthal's 1970 theorem supplies the \(L^p\) estimate for \(p>2\); neither requires symmetry.

## 9. Adversarial analysis of P14-EDGE-4M

P14's next desired extension is the singular near-parallel edge under bounded density plus a fourth moment.

A natural proposal was to retain
\[
\|Vv\|_2
\le
\sqrt y\,\|V\|_{\rm op}
\]
and combine a bounded-density vector small-ball estimate with a polynomial operator-norm tail.

There is a quantitative obstruction to this **naive additive split**.

Suppose, optimistically, one had
\[
P\{\|V\|_{\rm op}>R\sqrt D\}
\le CR^{-4}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
On the good norm event, bounded scalar densities across the independent rows give
\[
P\{\|Vv\|_2\le R\sqrt{yD}\}
\le
(CR\sqrt y)^{D-1}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Also the now-proved quadratic theorem gives
\[
P\{1-\rho^2\le y\}
\le
\frac{C}{D}
\qquad(y\le1/2).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Choose
\[
R=y^{-a},
\qquad
\frac18<a<\frac12.
\]
The small-ball term in (9.2) is exponentially small in \(D\) for fixed small \(y\), while the bad-norm contribution is \(Cy^{4a}\). Thus this route gives at best
\[
F_D(y)
:=
P\{1-\rho^2\le y\}
\lesssim
\min\left(\frac CD,\ Cy^{4a}\right)
\]
up to exponentially small terms.

The crossover is
\[
y_c\asymp D^{-1/(4a)}.
\]
Then
\[
\int_0^{y_*}
y^{-3/2}F_D(y)\,dy
\lesssim
D^{-1+1/(8a)}.
\tag{9.4}
\]
Letting \(a\uparrow1/2\) only approaches
\[
D^{-3/4},
\]
not the required \(D^{-1}\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a fourth-power operator-norm tail plus an additive bad-event split is structurally insufficient for P14-EDGE-4M.}
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

A successful weak-tail edge proof needs more structure: rowwise truncation that remains inside the small-ball event, random rounding, coarea/volume weighting, or another mechanism coupling the exceptional large rows to the near-parallel geometry. Merely optimizing a polynomial norm cutoff cannot close the singular integral.
