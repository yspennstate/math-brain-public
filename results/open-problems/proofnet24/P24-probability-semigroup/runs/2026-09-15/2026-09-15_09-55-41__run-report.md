## New theorem 1 — P24-P14-COFACTOR-GRAM-TELESCOPE-17 — PROVED

Let q_1,...,q_k be one realized prefix of the sequential cofactor chain and let Gamma_k be their k by k Gram matrix. Because

`s_j = q_j^T F_{j-1} q_j`

is exactly the squared Gram-Schmidt residual length,

`det Gamma_k = product_{j=1}^k s_j`.

Since `c_j = D/(D-j+1) s_j`,

`product_{j=1}^k c_j = [D^k/(D)_k] det Gamma_k`,

where `(D)_k = D(D-1)...(D-k+1)`.

Therefore the k-step cofactor reference density relative to the iid isotropic angular reference is

`d Q_A^(k) / d tilde_nu_A^k = [D^k/(D)_k] det Gamma_k`.

Since the ordinary joint angular law has density `product_j a_A(q_j)` relative to the same iid reference, the cumulative likelihood is exactly

`L_k = [(D)_k/D^k] [product_{j<=k} a_A(q_j)] / det Gamma_k`.

Thus the apparently long multiplicative history is one inverse Gram-volume factor times the row-angular Radon factors. The P14 history problem is therefore a determinant-correlation problem, not an arbitrary martingale-product problem.

## New theorem 2 — P24-P14-Q3-ABSTRACT-HISTORY-NOGO-17 — PROVED

No generic martingale theorem can deduce the P14 bracket from local L^(4/3) plus pathwise orthogonality.

Take orthonormal e_j, iid Rademacher signs xi_j, 0<epsilon<1, and

`ell_j = 1 + epsilon xi_j`, `L_j = product_{i<=j} ell_i`, `Delta G_j = a_j xi_j e_j`.

Then L_j is a positive mean-one martingale, every ell_j is bounded in `[1-epsilon,1+epsilon]`, the Delta G_j are pathwise orthogonal and mean zero, and the local L^(4/3) budget is exactly `epsilon^(4/3) a_j^(4/3)`.

But

`E[Delta L_j Delta G_j | past] = epsilon a_j L_{j-1} e_j`,

so

`E ||sum_j E[Delta L_j Delta G_j | past]||^2 = epsilon^2 sum_j a_j^2 (1+epsilon^2)^(j-1)`.

With the exact P14 geometric profile `a_j^2 = 1/[d_j(d_j-1)]`, the local q=3 budget is summable while the global predictable square grows exponentially. Hence bounded likelihood increments, local L^(4/3), and flag orthogonality are still insufficient.

The missing theorem must use the special Gram-determinant/exchangeable-row structure.
