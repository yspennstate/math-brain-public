# 2. Exact current source statement and conventions

The current journal article is Antonio Agresti and Mark Veraar, *Nonlinear SPDEs and Maximal Regularity: An Extended Survey*, NoDEA **32** (2025), article 123, DOI `10.1007/s00030-025-01090-2`, published 1 September 2025. The corresponding arXiv source is `2501.18561`.

The current journal Problem 3 states, in substance and with the source notation:

> Suppose `(A,B) in SMR^bullet_{p,0}` for some `p in [2,infinity)`. Does one have `(A,B) in SMR^bullet_{r,0}` for all `r in (2,infinity)` (or at least some large range for `r`)?

The survey points to Bechtel--Veraar's recent self-adjoint linear variational extrapolation result as partial positive progress.

## 2.1 Definition used in the proof

The survey's standing linear setting has a dense continuous embedding `X_1 -> X_0`, UMD/type-2 spaces in the general framework, a Hilbert noise space `U`,

\[
A\in\mathcal L(X_1,X_0),
\qquad
B\in\mathcal L(X_1,\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2})),
\]

and the Itô equation

\[
du+Au\,dt=f\,dt+(Bu+g)\,dW,
\qquad u(a)=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `kappa=0`, Definition 3.7 requires, uniformly over the admissible interval/stopping-time setup, the strong solution estimate of the form

\[
\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;X_1))}
\le C
\left(
\|f\|_{L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;X_0))}
+
\|g\|_{L^p(\Omega;L^p(a,\tau;\gamma(\mathcal U,X_{1/2})))}
\right).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Definition 3.8 adds the bullet time-regularity properties. Therefore failure of the base Definition 3.7 estimate at exponent `r` already implies failure of `SMR^bullet_{r,0}`.

In a variational Gelfand triple `V -> H -> V*`, the survey's Theorem 3.17 gives `SMR^bullet_{2,0}` under the standard coercivity inequality. The candidate below satisfies it with a strict explicit margin.

# 3. Current literature/source check and prior-art boundary

1. for `B=0`, stochastic maximal regularity extrapolates much more freely;
2. for leading-order multiplicative `B`, high-`p` integrability can fail.

# 5. Main theorem: exact high-frequency pulse obstruction

## `P24-AV-P3-DIAGONAL-FAILURE-53 — PROVED`

For the fixed candidate `(4.4)`,

\[
\boxed{
(A,B)\notin\mathrm{SMR}_{r,0}
\quad\text{for every }r\ge3.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

More generally, for `(4.1)` with arbitrary `0<beta^2<2`,

\[
\boxed{
(A,B)\notin\mathrm{SMR}_{r,0}
\quad\text{whenever}
\quad
r\ge r_c(\beta):=1+\frac{2}{\beta^2}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Let `phi_n` be a real `H`-orthonormal eigenfunction of `Lambda` with

\[
\Lambda\phi_n=\lambda_n\phi_n,
\qquad
\lambda_n=1+n^2\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

Fix the source time horizon `[0,1]`. Set

\[
t_n=\lambda_n^{-1}
\]

and choose deterministic data

\[
f_n(t)=h\,\mathbf 1_{[0,t_n]}(t)\phi_n,
\qquad
g_n=0,
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `h` is any fixed nonzero real number. Since

\[
\|\phi_n\|_{V^*}=\lambda_n^{-1/2},
\]

we have exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|f_n\|_{L^r(0,1;V^*)}^r
=|h|^r\lambda_n^{-r/2-1}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The equation preserves the one-dimensional eigenspace spanned by `phi_n`. Hence its strong solution has the form

\[
u_n(t)=U_n(t)\phi_n,
\]

where

\[
dU_n+\lambda_nU_n\,dt
=h\mathbf1_{[0,t_n]}dt
+\beta\sqrt{\lambda_n}U_n\,dW_t,
\qquad U_n(0)=0.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This scalar SDE has a unique strong solution for every `n`; no global maximal-regularity theorem is being assumed in order to write it.

#### Step 1: the pulse injects a nondegenerate amount at the high-frequency scale

Define on `0<=s<=1`

\[
\widetilde W_s=\sqrt{\lambda_n}W_{s/\lambda_n},
\qquad
V_s=\frac{\lambda_n}{h}U_n(s/\lambda_n).
\]

Brownian scaling gives a standard Brownian motion `widetilde W`, and `(5.5)` becomes

\[
dV_s=-V_s\,ds+\beta V_s\,d\widetilde W_s+ds,
\qquad V_0=0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
V_1
=\int_0^1
\exp\left(
-\left(1+\frac{\beta^2}{2}\right)(1-s)
+\beta(\widetilde W_1-\widetilde W_s)
\right)ds.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The integrand is strictly positive, so `V_1>0` almost surely. On a finite time interval it has every finite positive moment. Thus for each finite `r>=1`,

\[
c_{\beta,r}:=\mathbb E[V_1^r]\in(0,\infty).
\tag{5.8}
\]

At the end of the pulse,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|U_n(t_n)|^r
=|h|^r\lambda_n^{-r}c_{\beta,r}.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

The constant depends on `(beta,r)` but crucially **not** on `n`.

#### Step 2: after the pulse the `r`-moment has an explicit Lyapunov exponent

For `t>=t_n`, the deterministic forcing vanishes, so

\[
U_n(t)
=U_n(t_n)
\exp\left(
-\left(1+\frac{\beta^2}{2}\right)\lambda_n(t-t_n)
+\beta\sqrt{\lambda_n}(W_t-W_{t_n})
\right).
\tag{5.10}
\]

The future Brownian increment is independent of `U_n(t_n)`. Consequently

\[
\mathbb E|U_n(t)|^r
=\mathbb E|U_n(t_n)|^r
\exp\left(\gamma_{\beta,r}\lambda_n(t-t_n)\right),
\tag{5.11}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{\beta,r}
=r\left[-1+\frac{\beta^2(r-1)}2\right].
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

The sign changes exactly at

\[
r_c(\beta)=1+\frac2{\beta^2}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

#### Step 3: compare the source output norm to the data norm

Since

\[
\|U_n(t)\phi_n\|_V^r
=\lambda_n^{r/2}|U_n(t)|^r,
\]

the post-pulse piece of the SMR output is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\int_{t_n}^1\|u_n(t)\|_V^r\,dt
&=
|h|^r c_{\beta,r}\lambda_n^{-r/2}
\int_{t_n}^1
 e^{\gamma_{\beta,r}\lambda_n(t-t_n)}dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.14}
\]

Divide `(5.14)` by the exact input quantity `(5.4)`. The ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_n
=c_{\beta,r}
\int_0^{\lambda_n-1}e^{\gamma_{\beta,r}s}\,ds.
}
\tag{5.15}
\]

If `gamma_{beta,r}>0`,

\[
\mathcal R_n
=\frac{c_{\beta,r}}{\gamma_{\beta,r}}
\left(e^{\gamma_{\beta,r}(\lambda_n-1)}-1\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5.16}
\]

At the critical endpoint `gamma_{beta,r}=0`,

\[
\mathcal R_n=c_{\beta,r}(\lambda_n-1)\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5.17}
\]

Thus whenever `r>=r_c(beta)`, no constant in the Definition 3.7 estimate can be uniform in the deterministic data `f_n`. Therefore `(A,B) notin SMR_{r,0}` and a fortiori `(A,B) notin SMR^bullet_{r,0}`. This proves `(5.2)`, and `beta=1` gives `r_c=3`, proving `(5.1)`. `square`

# 7. Stronger family corollary: no pair-independent fixed higher exponent

## `P24-AV-P3-NO-UNIFORM-HIGHER-P-53 — PROVED`

For every prescribed `R>2`, there exists a pair satisfying the exact same source variational assumptions and belonging to `SMR^bullet_{2,0}` but not to `SMR_{R,0}`.

Indeed choose

\[
\boxed{
\beta_R^2=1+\frac1{R-1}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `R>2`,

\[
0<\beta_R^2<2,
\]

so the coercivity margin in `(4.2)` is strictly positive and source Theorem 3.17 gives `SMR^bullet_{2,0}`. But

\[
r_c(\beta_R)

# 8. Why simpler false counterexamples do not count

The counterexample-first protocol requires checking that failure is genuinely infinite-dimensional and source-level.

## 8.1 One fixed scalar SDE is not enough

For a fixed `lambda<infinity`, the post-pulse lognormal moment on `[0,1]` is finite for every finite `r`. A Definition 3.7 constant could absorb that finite number. Therefore a single scalar equation or one bounded operator does **not** refute the source extrapolation statement.

The proof needs the unbounded spectral family `lambda_n -> infinity`; it converts a positive/critical `r`-moment Lyapunov exponent into failure of one uniform maximal-regularity constant.

## 8.2 Failure outside coercivity is irrelevant

Taking `beta^2>=2` would already lose the source `L^2` variational coercivity and would not test Problem 3. The construction deliberately uses `beta^2<2`, and the fixed candidate `beta=1` has the comfortable margin `theta=1/2`.

## 8.3 Bad initial data alone would not match Definition 3.7

The 2012 p-dependent-parabolicity mechanism is often displayed through an initial-value solution. Problem 3's SMR property is defined by zero initial data with `f,g` forcing. The short pulse `(5.3)` is what transfers the instability into that exact definition. This is why the proof is not merely a citation to the homogeneous initial-data example.

# 11. Route comparison after the counterexample

## Exact stochastic semigroup / modal Lyapunov exponent

This is decisive for falsification. It keeps all constants and produces the exact necessary threshold `r_c(beta)=1+2/beta^2` for this modal test.

## Variational martingale / Itô energy

It also sees the same p-dependent coercivity mechanism at the level of `p`-energies, but by itself it naturally controls quantities such as `||u||_H^{p-2}||u||_V^2`, not immediately the full Definition 3.7 `L^p_t(V)` norm. It is therefore a plausible route for the positive side below the threshold, not a substitute for the direct failure certificate.

## Bechtel--Veraar Stein extrapolation

This is the live positive route near `p=2` for self-adjoint variational pairs. It proves that the working set extends above two for the fixed candidate but cannot cross the explicit modal instability at three. The next question is whether its constants/argument can be sharpened all the way to the true modal threshold.

## Exchangeable pairs / non-Gaussian comparison

They are unnecessary here: the driving noise is already exactly Gaussian and a single eigenmode suffices. Replacing frequencies by independent noises would change the model and is neither needed nor source-faithful.

## Transportation / Poincare / log-Sobolev shortcuts
