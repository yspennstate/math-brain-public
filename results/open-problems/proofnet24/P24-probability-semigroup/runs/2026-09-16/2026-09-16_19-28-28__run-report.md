for every progressively measurable `H`-valued `y`, uniformly over the unbounded spectrum of `A` and compatibly with the source's stopping/translation convention.

# 5. STUCK REPORT (b): every route tried and the precise reason it fails

## Route 1 — high-frequency deterministic forcing pulse

This is the successful negative route. It exactly detects the Lyapunov threshold

\[
\gamma_p=\frac{p(p-3)}2.
\]

**Why it stops:** it supplies bad inputs for `p>=3`; for `p<3` the same normalized tail integral is finite. A failure to obtain divergence below `3` is not a positive maximal-regularity theorem for arbitrary adapted `g`.

## Route 2 — scalar / one-mode Itô energy and absorption

The archived modal checker identifies the favorable coefficient

\[
\kappa_p=\frac{p(3-p)}2>0
\qquad(2<p<3).
\]

For one scalar mode, a standard Itô/Young budget can absorb forcing terms into this positive dissipation.

**Why it stops:** after summing modes in the Hilbert norm, the natural Itô energy controls an expression of the form

\[
\|x\|^{p-2}\|A^{1/2}x\|^2,
\]

while the `SMR` output requires the full

\[
\|A^{1/2}x\|^p
\]

integrability in time. Since the spectrum is unbounded and `A\ge I`, the obvious comparison is in the wrong direction. Applying Itô directly at one higher operator level instead differentiates the inhomogeneity `g`, which is outside Definition 3.7. The scalar argument therefore does not tensorize into the source norm.

## Route 3 — treat `K_A` as a contraction using additive stochastic maximal regularity

For a single scalar frequency, the `L^2` convolution energy has a favorable constant, and the later resolvent artifact records exact scalar formulas.

**Why it stops:** the Hilbert spectral sum contains many time scales simultaneously. Bounding each scalar frequency separately and then summing produces an `ell^p`-type spectral expression rather than the required Hilbert `ell^2` square sum. Conversely, taking an operator norm in the frequency variable before time integration loses the square-function cancellation. No source-valid sharp uniform estimate

\[
\|K_A\|_{L^p_{\rm prog}(H)\to L^p_{\rm prog}(H)}<1
\]

has been established, and I do not invent one.

## Route 4 — homogeneous Gaussian solution / direct stochastic exponential

For an eigenmode, the homogeneous propagator is explicit and reproduces the same critical threshold `3`.

**Why it stops:** homogeneous initial data are `\mathcal F_0`-measurable and independent of future Brownian increments. General `g_s` is only `\mathcal F_s`-measurable. A representation that multiplies `g_s` by a stochastic exponential depending on increments before and after `s` must preserve adaptedness and stochastic integration order; the homogeneous factorization does not do this automatically.

## Route 5 — pathwise Doss/exponential conjugation

Because `A` and `A^{1/2}` commute, one can formally try to remove multiplicative noise by an exponential of `A^{1/2}W_t`.

**Why it stops:** for one sign of `W_t`, `e^{-A^{1/2}W_t}` is unbounded on the high spectrum. Moreover, the transformed stochastic inhomogeneity remains correlated with the same Brownian path, and naive two-time propagators become anticipative. The source itself lists Doss–Sussman as one possible strategy only when the resulting transformed problem can actually be controlled; that control is precisely absent here.

## Route 6 — complete the square after taking the spectral operator norm

At each fixed Brownian increment the scalar multiplier can be optimized explicitly, and its Gaussian integrability has the same `p=3` boundary.

**Why it stops:** the operator norm of `Ae^{-A\tau}` behaves like `\tau^{-1}` near `0`. Taking the spectral supremum before the maximal-regularity cancellation therefore manufactures a nonintegrable singularity. The correct proof must keep the spectral square function until after the parabolic cancellation.

## Route 7 — Bellman / martingale-energy method

The `p=3` coefficient appears naturally from Itô's formula, suggesting a nonlinear Bellman function might encode the sharp endpoint.

**Why it stops:** the straightforward Bellman choice again produces the lower Hilbert-weighted dissipation from Route 2 instead of the `L^p_t(D(A))` norm. No Bellman function has yet been found that both respects adaptedness and upgrades exactly one half derivative without asking for extra regularity of `g`.

## Route 8 — coupling with future increments

For fixed source time `s`, `g_s` is `\mathcal F_s`-measurable and the increment `W_t-W_s` is independent of `\mathcal F_s`. This is the closest structural match to the homogeneous proof.

**Why it stops:** the solution itself also appears inside the stochastic integrand, and a naive variation-of-constants formula with random future propagators is not an Itô convolution with predictable integrand. One needs a filtration-respecting rearrangement, not a formal kernel factorization.

## Route 9 — Stein, exchangeable pairs, or transportation / functional inequalities

**Why it stops as a primary route:** the driving noise is already exactly Gaussian. The obstruction is not approximation of a non-Gaussian law; it is the boundedness of an adapted stochastic resolvent with a common Brownian driver and an unbounded spectral family. Gaussian comparison would add approximation error without resolving the filtration or half-derivative issue. Functional inequalities might help once a finite-dimensional adapted representation is found, but they do not presently supply `(BLOCK)`.

# 6. STUCK REPORT (c): what a successful proof must contain that no route currently contains

A proof below `p=3` must combine **both** of the following in the same argument:

1. **The exact `p<3` Gaussian/Itô gain.** It must retain the same completing-square or moment-dissipation mechanism responsible for the scalar factor `p(3-p)/2`; using a generic BDG estimate with arbitrary loss is unlikely to reach the sharp endpoint.
2. **The analytic half-derivative / square-function cancellation.** It must control the full spectral Hilbert square sum uniformly in the unbounded eigenvalues, without first taking a spectral supremum and without differentiating the source `g`.

It must additionally be **filtration respecting**: a value `g_s` may be factored against Brownian increments after `s`, but no argument may replace an Itô integrand by an anticipative random kernel. Finally, the constants must be uniform over the source's finite intervals and compatible with the translation/stopping convention used in Definition 3.7.

No current P24 route simultaneously contains all four ingredients.

# 8. STUCK REPORT (e): nearest solved problem in the literature and the missing ingredient

The closest solved theorem I located is:

Z. Brzeźniak and M. Veraar, *Is the stochastic parabolicity condition dependent on p and q?*, Electronic Journal of Probability 17 (2012), arXiv:1104.2768v3, last revised 16 July 2012:

`https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.2768`.

Their Hilbert-space model writes

\[
dU+AU\,dt=2BU\,dW,
\]

with a skew-adjoint `C`,

\[
A=C^*C,
\qquad
B=\alpha C+\beta |C|.
\]

Their Theorem 3.6 gives homogeneous `L^p` well-posedness under the sharp stochastic-parabolicity inequality

\[
2\alpha^2+2\beta^2(p-1)<1,
\tag{8.1}
\]

and the paper proves sharpness in its model.

## 8.1 Exact identification with the fixed P24 spectral pair

Complexify the P24 Hilbert space and set

\[
C=iA^{1/2}=i\Lambda^{1/2}.
\]

Then `C` is skew-adjoint,

\[
C^*C=A,
\qquad
|C|=A^{1/2}.
\]

Take the Brzeźniak–Veraar parameters

\[
\alpha=0,
\qquad
\beta=\frac12.
\]

Then their stochastic coefficient `2B` is exactly

\[
2\left(\frac12|C|\right)=A^{1/2},
\]

which is the P24 fixed candidate. Their condition (8.1) becomes

\[
\frac12(p-1)<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{p<3}.
\]

Thus the sharp scalar endpoint discovered independently by the P24 forcing-pulse argument is not an isolated coincidence: it is the exact homogeneous threshold already present in the 2012 Hilbert spectral theorem.

This is **KNOWN prior art / exact model identification**, not a new theorem claim.

## 8.2 What their proof has that the source SMR problem lacks

Their theorem takes an `\mathcal F_0`-measurable initial datum. The spectral proof can therefore exploit independence between the initial datum and future Brownian increments when estimating the random exponential multiplier. The Agresti–Veraar Definition 3.7 asks for arbitrary progressively measurable stochastic inhomogeneity `g`. Values `g_s` are only `\mathcal F_s`-measurable, and the solution re-enters the stochastic integral.

So the nearest solved theorem supplies exactly the sharp `p<3` Gaussian spectral estimate, while the missing source-level ingredient is an **adapted inhomogeneous transference/resolvent step** that retains that estimate uniformly in the spectrum.

This is now a much sharper diagnosis than “prove SMR below 3.”

## 10.1 Independent reconstruction of the combinatorial half

Condition on one permutation realization in `\mathcal V(\mathcal B)`. Its root cycle induces a connected graph on the visited block labels, so choose any spanning tree `T` on the `k` labels. Independently color each block red/blue with fair bits.

For every tree edge `e`, let `X_e` be the indicator that its endpoints have different colors. On a tree, the map

\[
(\text{root color},(X_e)_{e\in T})
\longleftrightarrow
(\text{all vertex colors})
\]

is a bijection. Therefore the `X_e` are independent fair Bernoulli variables and

\[
Z:=\sum_{e\in T}X_e\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k-1,1/2).
\tag{10.4}
\]

By symmetry of the fair binomial law,

\[
\mathbb P_\xi\left\{
Z\ge\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil
\right\}\ge\frac12.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Every bichromatic tree edge corresponds to at least one actual root-cycle arrow crossing the block cut. A directed cycle has equally many red-to-blue and blue-to-red cut crossings, so if `N_\xi` is the number of red-to-blue root-cycle arrows,

\[
2N_\xi\ge Z.
\tag{10.6}
\]

On the event in (10.5),

\[
N_\xi
\ge
\left\lceil
\frac12\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{2}\right\rceil
\right\rceil
=
\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{4}\right\rceil
=s_k.
\tag{10.7}
\]

Averaging over block colors gives the exact factor-two reduction

\[
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}
\le
2\,\mathbb E_\xi\mathbf1_{\{N_\xi\ge s_k\}}.
\tag{10.8}
\]

This reconstructs the addendum's sharpening independently of its presentation.

## 10.2 Independent check of the probabilistic half

For a fixed color cut, color exactly the labels initially in the red union as red particles. Under interchange, the red occupied set evolves as symmetric exclusion from a deterministic initial set. The main HARD10-03 report invokes the Borcea–Brändén–Liggett strong-Rayleigh theorem, hence negative association of the final occupation indicators.

Writing

\[
N_\xi=\sum_{y\in D_\xi}\eta_\beta(y),
\qquad
\rho_y=\mathbb E\eta_\beta(y),
\qquad
\mu_\xi=\sum_{y\in D_\xi}\rho_y,
\]

negative association gives for `\theta>=0`

\[
\mathbb E e^{\theta N_\xi}
\le
\prod_y\bigl(1+(e^\theta-1)\rho_y\bigr)
\le
\exp((e^\theta-1)\mu_\xi).
\tag{10.9}
\]

Chernoff optimization gives

\[
\mathbb P(N_\xi\ge s_k)
\le
\left(\frac{e\mu_\xi}{s_k}\right)^{s_k}.
\tag{10.10}
\]

For each cut, `\mu_\xi` is precisely its directed one-particle cross heat, hence

\[
\mu_\xi\le\Lambda_\beta.
\tag{10.11}
\]

Combining (10.8)–(10.11) gives the third term of (10.1). The first-moment term `\mathcal H_\beta/k` is inherited independently from the main connected-block estimate; it is not needed for the sharpening check.

For a symmetric kernel, each unordered separated block pair contributes equally in both directions, whereas `\mathcal H_\beta` counts both directions, so (10.2) follows. If

\[
\mathcal H_\beta\le\frac{k-1}{4e},
\]

then

\[
\frac{e\Lambda_\beta}{s_k}
\le
\frac{k-1}{8s_k}
\le\frac12,
\]

because `4s_k>=k-1`. This gives (10.3).
