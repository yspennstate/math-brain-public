# 3. Counterexample-first / proposed Hermite construction and exact failure

The previous run's proposed positive route was to iterate the stochastic convolution operator

\[
(K_A h)(t)=\int_a^t C e^{-A(t-s)}h_s\,dW_s,
\qquad C=A^{1/2},
\]

and attempt to resum

\[
z=\sum_{n\ge1}K_A^n y
\]

using ordered Itô simplices / Hermite chaos. Formally, the homogeneous common-noise flow from `s` to `t` is

\[
\Phi(t,s)=\exp\!\left[-\frac32A(t-s)+C(W_t-W_s)\right]
\]

for the frozen `beta=1` case. Spectrally its operator norm has exactly the correct homogeneous moment threshold `p<3`.

The tempting algorithm is therefore

\[
X_t\stackrel{?}=\int_a^t \Phi(t,s)Y_s\,dW_s.
\]

This is **not** a valid forward Itô representation: for fixed `s<t`, the factor `W_t-W_s` is future information relative to `F_s`, so `Phi(t,s)Y_s` is not `F_s`-measurable. The construction fails exactly at adaptedness. This is `P24-AV-P3-HERMITE-COLLAPSE-NOGO-58`: a route failure, not a counterexample to SMR.

This diagnosis is important because it prevents a false proof from the otherwise correct homogeneous flow estimate.

# 4. New method: semigroup Bellman functional

The successful route keeps adaptedness throughout and moves the spectral optimization into a deterministic auxiliary semigroup variable.

Let `H` be the real Hilbert base, `A` positive self-adjoint, `C=A^{1/2}`, and `S_s=e^{-sA}`. First work after a finite spectral projection so every derivative below is classical. For `p>2` define

\[
\boxed{\Psi_p(x):=\int_0^\infty \|CS_sx\|_H^p\,ds.}
\]

For a single `A`-eigenvector of eigenvalue `lambda`,

\[
\Psi_p(x)=\frac1p\lambda^{p/2-1}|x|^p,
\]

so `Psi_p` is exactly tuned to the interpolation/trace scale and is not the raw Hilbert energy used by the earlier dimension-losing proof.

Write

\[
z_s=CS_sx,\qquad Q_sh=CS_sh.
\]

Then on a finite spectral subspace

\[
D\Psi_p(x)h
=p\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-2}\langle z_s,Q_sh\rangle\,ds,
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2\Psi_p(x)[h,h]
={}&p\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|Q_sh\|^2\,ds\\
&+p(p-2)\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-4}
\langle z_s,Q_sh\rangle^2\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]

For `p>2` this extends continuously through `x=0`, and the Hessian is positive semidefinite.

## 4.1 Exact drift telescope

Because `z_s'=-Az_s`,

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\|z_s\|^p
=-p\|z_s\|^{p-2}\langle z_s,Az_s\rangle.
\]

Also `Q_s(Ax)=Az_s`. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
D\Psi_p(x)[-Ax]
&=-p\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-2}\langle z_s,Az_s\rangle\,ds\\
&=\int_0^\infty\frac{d}{ds}\|z_s\|^p\,ds
=-\|Cx\|^p,
\end{aligned}
\]

since `S_s` decays exponentially and `z_0=Cx`.

Thus the deterministic drift produces the target `X_1` norm with coefficient exactly one.

## 4.2 Exact multiplicative-noise coefficient

Take `h=Cx`. Since `C` commutes with `S_s`,

\[
Q_s(Cx)=Cz_s.
\]

Set

\[
I_1:=\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|Cz_s\|^2\,ds.
\]

As

\[
-\frac{d}{ds}\|z_s\|^p
=p\|z_s\|^{p-2}\|Cz_s\|^2,
\]

we have

\[
I_1=\frac1p\|Cx\|^p.
\]

For the second Hessian term, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
I_2:=\int_0^\infty\|z_s\|^{p-4}\langle z_s,Cz_s\rangle^2\,ds
\le I_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{D^2\Psi_p(x)[Cx,Cx]\le(p-1)\|Cx\|^p.}
\]

For `B=beta C` this becomes

\[
D^2\Psi_p(x)[\beta Cx,\beta Cx]
\le \beta^2(p-1)\|Cx\|^p.
\]

The coefficient `beta^2(p-1)` is exactly the stochastic-parabolicity threshold appearing in the one-mode counterexample.

## 4.3 Additive-noise Hessian without dimension loss

For arbitrary `y in H`, dropping the directional refinement in the second Hessian term yields

\[
D^2\Psi_p(x)[y,y]
\le p(p-1)\int_0^\infty\|CS_sx\|^{p-2}\|CS_sy\|^2\,ds.
\]

Because `S_s` is a contraction commuting with `C`,

\[
\|CS_sx\|\le\|Cx\|.
\]

The self-adjoint square-function identity is exact:

\[
\int_0^\infty\|CS_sy\|^2\,ds
=\int_0^\infty\langle Ae^{-2sA}y,y\rangle\,ds
=\frac12\|y\|^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{D^2\Psi_p(x)[y,y]
\le\frac{p(p-1)}2\|Cx\|^{p-2}\|y\|^2.}
\]

This is the step the previous coordinatewise proof did not have: the Hilbert square function is summed **before** any modewise `l^p` comparison, so there is no factor depending on spectral dimension.

## 4.4 Cross term and the exact subcritical condition

For every positive-semidefinite quadratic form `Q` and `epsilon>0`,

\[
Q(u+v)\le(1+\epsilon)Q(u)+(1+\epsilon^{-1})Q(v).
\]

Apply this with `Q(h)=D^2\Psi_p(x)[h,h]`, `u=beta Cx`, `v=y`. We obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2\Psi_p(x)[\beta Cx+y,\beta Cx+y]
\le{}&(1+\epsilon)\beta^2(p-1)\|Cx\|^p\\
&+(1+\epsilon^{-1})\frac{p(p-1)}2
\|Cx\|^{p-2}\|y\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Now let `X` solve the source equation with `f=0`,

\[
dX_t+AX_t\,dt=(\beta CX_t+Y_t)\,dW_t,
\qquad X_a=0.
\]

For the source stopping-time formulation, first use the `p=2` variational theory to obtain the unique strong solution; `Y in L^p` lies in `L^2` on the finite probability-time measure because `p>2`.

Let `P_N=1_{[0,N]}(A)` be nested finite-rank spectral projections (on the torus these are finite Fourier-shell projections), and set

\[
X_N=P_NX,\qquad Y_N=P_NY.
\]

They solve the projected finite-dimensional equation because `P_N`, `A`, and `C` commute. Apply ordinary finite-dimensional Itô's formula to `Psi_p(X_N)` up to the source stopping time and a standard localizing sequence for the martingale; then take expectation and remove the localizer by Fatou. The terminal Bellman functional is nonnegative. If

\[
I_N:=E\int_a^\tau\|CX_N\|^pdt,
\qquad
J:=E\int_a^\tau\|Y\|^pdt,
\]

we get

\[
\left[1-\frac{(1+\epsilon)\beta^2(p-1)}2\right]I_N
\le
\frac{p(p-1)}4(1+\epsilon^{-1})
E\int_a^\tau\|CX_N\|^{p-2}\|Y_N\|^2dt.
\]

Hölder in `Omega x (a,tau)` and contractivity of `P_N` give

\[
E\int\|CX_N\|^{p-2}\|Y_N\|^2
\le I_N^{(p-2)/p}J^{2/p}.
\]

Hence whenever

\[
\boxed{\beta^2(p-1)<2},
\]

choose

\[
0<\epsilon<\frac{2}{\beta^2(p-1)}-1.
\]

Then

\[
I_N^{1/p}\le C_{p,\beta,\epsilon}J^{1/p},
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
C_{p,\beta,\epsilon}^2
=
\frac{p(p-1)(1+\epsilon^{-1})}
{2\,[2-\beta^2(p-1)(1+\epsilon)]}.
}
\]

The optimal choice in this one-parameter estimate is

\[
\epsilon_*=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta^2(p-1)}}-1,
\]

which yields the explicit dimension-free constant

\[
\boxed{
C_{p,\beta}^2
=
\frac{p(p-1)}
{2(\sqrt2-|\beta|\sqrt{p-1})^2}.
}
\]

For the frozen `beta=1`,

\[
\boxed{
C_p^2
=
\frac{p(p-1)}
{2(\sqrt2-\sqrt{p-1})^2},\qquad 2<p<3.
}
\]

The blow-up of this constant as `p` approaches the critical endpoint is consistent with the exact endpoint counterexample; no uniform endpoint estimate is being smuggled in.

## 4.5 Passage to the full spectrum

Because the projections are nested spectral projections,

\[
\|CP_NX\|^2\uparrow\|CX\|^2
\]

pointwise. Monotone convergence therefore gives

\[
E\int_a^\tau\|CX_t\|^pdt
\le C_{p,\beta}^pE\int_a^\tau\|Y_t\|^pdt.
\]

The constant is independent of the rank `N`, the starting time `a`, and the stopping time `tau` (for `tau<=T`). This is exactly the source's base SMR estimate in the `f=0` case, because in the torus scale generated by `Lambda`,

\[
\|CX\|_{L^2}=\|X\|_{H^1},
\qquad
X_{1/2}=L^2.
\]

No independent-frequency noise, coordinatewise `l^p` gluing, anticipative future kernel, or dimension-dependent embedding appears.

## 4.6 Restore `f` and upgrade to the bullet class

For the present `A=Lambda` on `X_0=H^{-1}` with domain `X_1=H^1`, `-A` generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on the Hilbert space `X_0`. Agresti--Veraar Theorem 3.13 therefore gives

\[
(A,0)\in {\mathcal {SMR}}_{p,0}^{\bullet}
\qquad(p\ge2).
\]

In particular `A` has deterministic maximal `L^p` regularity. By their Remark 3.10, proving base SMR for the pair reduces to the `f=0` estimate just established. Thus

\[
(A,\beta C)\in {\mathcal {SMR}}_{p,0}
\quad\text{for }2<p<1+2/\beta^2.
\]

Finally, Proposition 3.18 with comparison operator `A_0=A` upgrades this to

\[
\boxed{(A,\beta C)\in {\mathcal {SMR}}_{p,0}^{\bullet}}
\quad\text{for }2<p<1+2/\beta^2.
\]

At `p=2`, Theorem 3.17 supplies membership from the strict variational coercivity assumption `beta^2<2`.

This completes the positive side under the source's exact definitions.
