## 2. Idea before code

\[
V_p:=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 35

### 4.1 The localized restriction is a valid positive chain

If `x,y in V` are distinct and `a(y-x)>0`, then `chi(y-x)=-1`. Since `p=1 mod 4`, `chi(-1)=1`, so `chi(x-y)=-1` as well. Thus `x,y` are nonadjacent in the Paley graph and hence adjacent in `K_p=overline{G_{p,2}}`.

The assumptions `a>=0`, `a(c)=a(-c)`, and `a(0)=0` give nonnegativity, symmetry, and zero diagonal. The row-balance hypothesis gives `P_a 1=1`. Hence `P_a` lies exactly in the positive-Markov cone from theorem 30.

### 4.2 Uniform overlap bound for a nonresidue translation

For a nonresidue `c`, define

\[
N_c:=|V\cap(V-c)|
=\#\{x\in V:x+c\in V\}.
\]

Because `chi(+-1)=1`, a nonresidue `c` cannot be `0`, `1`, or `-1`. Therefore the four roots

\[
0,\quad1,\quad-c,\quad1-c
\]

are distinct.

Put

\[
g(x):=\frac14(1+\chi(x))(1+\chi(x-1)).
\]

For `x notin {0,1}`, `g(x)` is exactly the indicator of `V`; at `0,1` it equals `1/2`. Hence `1_V(x)<=g(x)` pointwise, so

\[
N_c\le \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}g(x)g(x+c).
\]

Expanding the right-hand side produces sixteen multiplicative-character sums from the four distinct monic linear factors

\[
x,\quad x-1,\quad x+c,\quad x+c-1.
\]

The empty subset contributes `p`. Each of the four one-factor sums is zero. Each of the six two-factor sums is exactly `-1` by the standard quadratic-character identity

\[
\sum_x\chi((x-a)(x-b))=-1\qquad(a\ne b).
\]

Each of the four three-factor sums is a squarefree cubic character sum and has absolute value at most `2 sqrt(p)` by the Weil bound. The four-factor sum is squarefree quartic and has absolute value at most `3 sqrt(p)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
N_c\le\frac{p-6+11\sqrt p}{16}.
}
\]

This is uniform over every nonresidue translation `c`.

### 4.3 Exact row balance forces total mass approximately four

Sum the stochastic row equations over `x in V`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
n
&=\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V}a(y-x)\\
&=\sum_{c:\chi(c)=-1}a(c)N_c.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since all weights are nonnegative, the overlap bound yields

\[
n\le A\frac{p-6+11\sqrt p}{16}.
\]

Using `n=(p-5)/4`,

\[
\boxed{
A\ge\frac{4(p-5)}{p-6+11\sqrt p}.
}
\]

In particular `liminf A>=4`. This is the density-four loss: a translation step drawn from the full field lands back in the two-localized set only with asymptotic density `1/4`, so exact localized row mass one needs ambient intensity at least four.

### 4.4 Square-multiplicative averaging optimizes the full Fourier lower edge

Because `C_a` is circulant, its eigenvalues are the real additive Fourier coefficients

\[
\widehat a(\xi)=\sum_{c\in\mathbb F_p}a(c)e^{2\pi i\xi c/p}.
\]

Let

\[
m(a):=\min_{\xi\in\mathbb F_p}\widehat a(\xi)=\lambda_{\min}(C_a).
\]

For every nonzero square `r`, define

\[
a_r(c):=a(r^{-1}c).
\]

Multiplication by `r` preserves the nonresidue class and permutes Fourier frequencies, so

\[
m(a_r)=m(a).
\]

The functional `m` is concave because it is the pointwise minimum of linear functionals of `a`. Average `a_r` over all nonzero squares. The square group acts transitively on the nonresidues, so the average is the uniform nonresidue weight

\[
\bar a(c)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2A}{p-1},&\chi(c)=-1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Concavity gives

\[
m(a)\le m(\bar a).
\]

For `xi != 0`, the standard quadratic Gauss-sum identity for `p=1 mod 4` gives

\[
\sum_{\chi(c)=-1}e^{2\pi i\xi c/p}
=\frac{-1-\chi(\xi)\sqrt p}{2}.
\]

Hence the smallest Fourier coefficient of `bar a` occurs at a square frequency and equals

\[
\frac{2A}{p-1}\frac{-1-\sqrt p}{2}
=-\frac{A(1+\sqrt p)}{p-1}
=-\frac{A}{\sqrt p-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}(C_a)=m(a)
\le -\frac{A}{\sqrt p-1}.
}
\]

Combining with the mass bound proves the second displayed inequality of the theorem.

### 4.5 Comparison with the P04 source-scale Markov threshold

The prior positive-Markov reduction requires

\[
\lambda_{\min}(P_a)\ge-\delta_p,
\qquad
\delta_p=\frac{8\sqrt p}{3p-8\sqrt p-15}.
\]

The ambient-Fourier/interlacing route would try to guarantee this from the stronger inequality

\[
\lambda_{\min}(C_a)\ge-\delta_p,
\]

because Cauchy interlacing gives

\[
\lambda_{\min}(P_a)\ge\lambda_{\min}(C_a).
\]

But our forced ambient lower-edge magnitude exceeds `delta_p` whenever

\[
\frac{4(p-5)}{(p-6+11\sqrt p)(\sqrt p-1)}
>
\frac{8\sqrt p}{3p-8\sqrt p-15}.
\]

Set `s=sqrt(p)`. After multiplying positive denominators, this is exactly

\[
F(s):=s^4-28s^3+4s^2+28s+75>0.
\]

At `s=28`,

\[
F(28)=3995>0,
\]

and

\[
F'(s)=4(s^3-21s^2+2s+7)>0
\qquad(s\ge28),
\]

because `s^3-21s^2=s^2(s-21)>0` there. Thus the inequality holds for every `p>=784`, completing the proof.
