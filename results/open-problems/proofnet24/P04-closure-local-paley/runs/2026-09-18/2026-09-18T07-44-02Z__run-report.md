## 3. Claim 43 — the signed `A_3` quotient refines the old `J`-parity quotient

\[
\boxed{\texttt{P04-I0101-A3-SIGNED-QUOTIENT-43 = PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL}.}
\]

Assume throughout this section that

\[
p\equiv5\pmod{24},\qquad p>5,
\]

so the anharmonic `S_3` action on the localized vertex set

\[
V_p=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}

is free.

Set

\[
A(x)=x^2-x+1,
\qquad
H(x)=(x-2)(x+1)(2x-1)=2x^3-3x^2-3x+2,
\]

and define the rational coordinate

\[
\boxed{\zeta(x)=\frac{H(x)}{x(x-1)}.}
\]

### 3.1 Alternating invariance and the degree-three quotient

For the two odd anharmonic generators one checks directly that

\[
H(1-x)=-H(x),
\qquad
H(1/x)=x^{-3}H(x).
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\zeta(gx)=\operatorname{sgn}(g)\,\zeta(x)\qquad(g\in S_3).}
\]

Thus `zeta` is invariant under `A_3` and changes sign under every odd anharmonic transformation.

The elementary identity

\[
\boxed{H(x)^2=4A(x)^3-27x^2(1-x)^2}
\]

gives, for the earlier anharmonic invariant

\[
J(x)=\frac{A(x)^3}{x^2(1-x)^2},
\]

the exact relation

\[
\boxed{\zeta(x)^2=4J(x)-27.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{J(x)=\frac{\zeta(x)^2+27}{4}.}
\]

Solving the defining equation for `x` gives

\[
\boxed{
2X^3-(\zeta+3)X^2+(\zeta-3)X+2=0.
}
\]

Its discriminant is exactly

\[
\boxed{(\zeta^2+27)^2.}
\]

On the clean family the full `S_3` orbit has size six and the `A_3` orbit has size three. Hence `zeta` identifies the `A_3` quotient, while the remaining odd involution is simply `zeta -> -zeta`. If

\[
Z_p:=\zeta(V_p),
\]

then

\[
|Z_p|=\frac{p-5}{12},
\qquad
Z_p=-Z_p,
\]

and the old `J` quotient is precisely the further identification `zeta ~ -zeta`.

The generic cyclic-cubic / anharmonic invariant theory behind this algebra is classical in spirit. The P04-specific point is that this coordinate sits directly on the localized Paley vertex set and separates the six edge channels into two signed three-channel packets.

### 3.3 Exact factorization of the six Paley channels into two signed quotient channels

Write the even anharmonic images of `y` as

\[
y,\qquad \frac{y-1}{y},\qquad \frac1{1-y},
\]

and the odd images as

\[
1-y,\qquad \frac1y,\qquad \frac{y}{y-1}.
\]

A direct multiplication gives the two exact identities

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{g\in A_3}(x-g(y))
=\frac{x(x-1)}2\,[\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)],
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{g\notin A_3}(x-g(y))
=\frac{x(x-1)}2\,[\zeta(x)+\zeta(y)].
}
\]

For the six Paley channel signs

\[
\epsilon_g(x,y)=\chi(x-g(y)),
\]

we have on `V_p`

\[
\chi(x(x-1))=1,
\qquad
\chi(2)=-1.
\]

Therefore, away from the collision cases,

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{g\in A_3}\epsilon_g(x,y)
=-\chi(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{g\notin A_3}\epsilon_g(x,y)
=-\chi(\zeta(x)+\zeta(y)).
}
\]

Multiplying the two formulas recovers the old six-channel parity quotient because

\[
(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))(\zeta(x)+\zeta(y))
=4(J(x)-J(y)).
\]

So the previous `J`-parity theorem is the product of **two** more informative Paley-type parity channels on the signed `A_3` quotient. This is a strict structural refinement: it keeps the even/odd anharmonic orientation that the full `S_3` quotient discarded.

## 4. Claim 44 — exact standard-sector hexagon and the support tradeoff

\[
\boxed{\texttt{P04-I0101-S3-STANDARD-HEXAGON-44 = PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL}.}
\]

Let `rho` be the real orthogonal standard representation of `S_3`. For one six-channel block with signs `epsilon_g in {+1,-1}`, define

\[
F_+=\sum_{g\in A_3}\epsilon_g\rho(g),
\qquad
F_-=\sum_{g\notin A_3}\epsilon_g\rho(g).
\]

The three matrices in either parity coset form an equilateral triangle in the four-dimensional Frobenius space:

\[
\sum_{g\in A_3}\rho(g)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{g\notin A_3}\rho(g)=0,
\]

and

\[
\langle\rho(g),\rho(h)\rangle_F
=\chi_{\rm std}(g^{-1}h).
\]

Within one parity coset, distinct elements differ by a 3-cycle, so their Frobenius inner product is `-1` and each has squared norm `2`. Between opposite parity cosets, the relative element is a reflection and the standard character is zero, so the two triangle spans are orthogonal.

### 4.1 Singular-value trichotomy

Inside one parity triangle, if all three signs are equal then the signed sum is zero. Otherwise exactly one sign is the minority sign, and using the zero-sum identity gives

\[
F_\pm=2\epsilon_m\rho(m)
\]

for the minority element `m`. Hence a nonzero parity component has singular values `(2,2)`.

If both parity components are nonzero, their normalized sum is a rotation plus or minus a reflection. Such a `2 x 2` matrix has singular values `(2,0)` before the overall factor two. Therefore the full standard signed sum

\[
F=F_++F_-
\]

has exactly the following possibilities:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{parity triples} & \text{singular values of }F\\ \hline
\text{both constant} & (0,0)\\
\text{exactly one nonconstant} & (2,2)\\
\text{both nonconstant} & (4,0).
\end{array}}
\]

For the unweighted complement indicator

\[
n_g=\frac{1-\epsilon_g}{2},
\]

the total standard Fourier block is

\[
M_N=\sum_g n_g\rho(g)=-\frac12F
\]

because the full group sum of the standard representation is zero. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{sv}(M_N)\in\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,0)\},
}
\]

with the same case split.

This completely classifies the local standard block of every six-bit Paley mask.

### 4.2 Exact nonnegative support criterion

Now replace the binary complement indicator by arbitrary nonnegative complement-edge weights `w_g>=0`, still supported only where the corresponding channel is a complement edge. Put

\[
M=\sum_g w_g\rho(g).
\]

Because the even and odd triangle spans are Frobenius-orthogonal, `M=0` if and only if the weighted sum vanishes separately in each parity triangle.

For one triangle, the origin lies in the nonnegative cone of a support subset precisely when either no mass is used or all three triangle vertices are available. If all three are available, the unique positive relation is equal weighting. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M=0
}
\]

is possible exactly when every parity coset carrying positive mass is **fully contained in the complement mask**, and the three weights on that coset are equal.

There is also an exact quantitative version. If `k in {1,2,3}` positions of one parity triangle are available, the total mass is `m`, and the weights are nonnegative, then

\[
\left\|\sum w_i\rho(g_i)\right\|_F^2
=3\sum_i w_i^2-m^2.
\]

At fixed `m`, the minimum is attained by equal weights on the available positions, giving

\[
\boxed{
\min \|M_{\rm parity}\|_F^2
=
\begin{cases}
2m^2,&k=1,\\
\frac12m^2,&k=2,\\
0,&k=3.
\end{cases}}
\]

This is the exact local Pareto curve between scalar mass and standard-sector leakage.

### 4.3 Why exact local standard cancellation is not yet a solution

B19 asked for an algorithm. The most literal one is now forced by the theorem: use only a parity coset whose three channels are all complement edges, and weight its three entries equally. If the average trivial Fourier coefficient is normalized to the natural source-scale value `6`, the uniform-mask optimum gives weight `8` on every available full parity triple.

Under the already-proved uniform 64-mask law, each parity triple is fully complementary with probability `1/8`. If `r_+,r_-` are the scalar masses placed on the two parity triangles, exact standard cancellation gives trivial/sign coefficients

\[
T=r_++r_-,
\qquad
S=r_+-r_-.
\]

Among all such mask-local exact-standard-cancellation rules with `E[T]=\mu`, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E(T^2+S^2)\ge8\mu^2.}
\]

Equality uses constant conditional mass on each full-triangle event. At `mu=6`, this is

\[
\mathbb E(T^2+S^2)\ge288,
\]

and the equality rule is exactly the weight-8 construction. For that rule

\[
\operatorname{Var}(T)=126,
\qquad
\mathbb E(S^2)=126.
\]

Thus killing the standard block **locally and exactly** pushes a very large amount of second-moment energy into the two scalar quotient blocks. This does not by itself prove a bad one-sided eigenvalue — Frobenius energy still forgets spectral sign — but it explains why the naive matrix-valued algorithm does not glue cleanly to the trivial/sign sectors.

This is materially sharper than saying merely that a scalar lookup table is too rigid: the standard block can actually be annihilated exactly, but doing so forces the construction onto rare full-triangle masks and creates a scalar-variance bottleneck.
