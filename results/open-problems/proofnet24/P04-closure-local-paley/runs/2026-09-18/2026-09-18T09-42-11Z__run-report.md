## 4. New structural theorem

### `P04-I0101-ANCHOR-CELL-ESCAPE-SCHUR-45 = PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL`

Let `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)` be prime. Let `Q` be the full Paley character matrix on `F_p`,

\[
Q_{xy}=\begin{cases}
\chi(x-y),&x\ne y,\\
0,&x=y,
\end{cases}
\]

where `chi` is the quadratic character. Since `chi(-1)=1`, `Q` is symmetric and obeys

\[
Q\mathbf1=0,
\qquad
Q^2=pI-J.
\]

Fix anchors `0,1`. Partition the remaining field points according to the pair

\[
(\chi(z),\chi(z-1))\in\{(+,+),(+,-),(-,+),(-,-)\}.
\]

Write

\[
V=(+,+),\qquad B=(+,-),\qquad D=(-,+),\qquad F=(-,-).
\]

(The letters `D,F` are used here only to avoid collision with the localized sign matrix `C`.) Standard quadratic-character counting gives

\[
|V|=n=\frac{p-5}{4},
\qquad
|B|=|D|=|F|=m=\frac{p-1}{4}.
\]

Let `C=Q_{V,V}` and let

\[
U_B=Q_{V,B},\qquad U_D=Q_{V,D},\qquad U_F=Q_{V,F},
\qquad U=[U_B\ U_D\ U_F].
\]

Define the anchor-cell vector

\[
\boxed{v=(C+2I)\mathbf1.}
\]

Then the following statements hold exactly.

### Theorem 45(a): exact exterior cell sums

\[
\boxed{
U_B\mathbf1=-v,\qquad
U_D\mathbf1=-v,\qquad
U_F\mathbf1=+v.
}
\]

### Theorem 45(b): rank-one coarse escape lower bound

\[
\boxed{
UU^\top\succeq \frac{12}{p-1}\,vv^\top.
}
\]

Consequently, for every positive-semidefinite trace-one matrix `X`, if

\[
T=\langle J,X\rangle,
\qquad
E=\langle UU^\top,X\rangle,
\qquad
R=\mathbf1^\top Xv,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{TE\ge \frac{12}{p-1}R^2.}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}
T&R\\
R&(p-1)E/12
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
}
\]

### Theorem 45(c): exact three-moment Gram/Schur inequality

Let

\[
S=\langle C,X\rangle,
\qquad
q=\mathbf1^\top X C\mathbf1.
\]

Then the exact moment matrix

\[
\boxed{
M_X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&S&T\\
S&p-3T-E&q\\
T&q&nT
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
}
\]

Because `R=q+2T`, its Schur complement gives

\[
\boxed{
(q-ST)^2
\le
\bigl(p-3T-E-S^2\bigr)\,T(n-T).

If `X` is theta-plus dual feasible, so that `S=T-1+Delta`, and

\[
G:=p-\Bigl(T^2+T+1+E+2(T-1)\Delta+\Delta^2\Bigr)\ge0,

\boxed{
\bigl(R-T(S+2)\bigr)^2\le G\,T(n-T).

Combining this with part (b), for `T>0`, gives the exact implicit escape bound

\[
\boxed{
E\ge
\frac{12}{(p-1)T}
\left[
T(S+2)-\sqrt{G\,T(n-T)}

## 5. Proof of Theorem 45

### 5.1 Character-cell sizes

The standard identities

\[
\sum_z\chi(z)=0,
\qquad
\sum_z\chi(z)\chi(z-1)=-1
\]

and the two exceptional points `z=0,1` give

\[
|V|=(p-5)/4,
\qquad
|B|=|D|=|F|=(p-1)/4.
\]

No asymptotic estimate is used.

### 5.2 Exact cell sums from `Q 1=0` and the two anchor columns of `Q^2`

Fix `x in V`. Since `chi(x)=chi(x-1)=1`, the two anchor entries in row `x` of `Q` are both `+1`. Restricting `Q 1=0` to the `V` rows gives

\[
U_B\mathbf1+U_D\mathbf1+U_F\mathbf1
=-2\mathbf1-C\mathbf1=-v. \tag{1}
\]

Now use the `(x,0)` entries of `Q^2=pI-J`. Since `x ne 0`, `(Q^2)_{x0}=-1`. The `y=1` term contributes `+1`; points in `V` and `B` have `chi(y)=+1`, while points in `D,F` have `chi(y)=-1`. Thus

\[
\mathbf1+C\mathbf1+U_B\mathbf1-U_D\mathbf1-U_F\mathbf1=-\mathbf1,
\]

or

\[
U_B\mathbf1-U_D\mathbf1-U_F\mathbf1=-v. \tag{2}
\]

Comparing (1) and (2) yields

\[
U_B\mathbf1=-v,
\qquad
U_D\mathbf1+U_F\mathbf1=0. \tag{3}
\]

Likewise, the `(x,1)` entries of `Q^2=pI-J` equal `-1`. The `y=0` term contributes `+1`; the second character `chi(y-1)` has signs `+,-,+,-` on `V,B,D,F`. Hence

\[
\mathbf1+C\mathbf1-U_B\mathbf1+U_D\mathbf1-U_F\mathbf1=-\mathbf1,
\]

so

\[
-U_B\mathbf1+U_D\mathbf1-U_F\mathbf1=-v. \tag{4}
\]

Insert `U_B 1=-v` from (3) and combine with `U_D 1+U_F 1=0`; this gives

\[
U_D\mathbf1=-v,
\qquad
U_F\mathbf1=+v.
\]

This proves part (a).

### 5.3 Rank-one cell projection

For any real `n x m` matrix `M`,

\[
MM^\top-\frac1m(M\mathbf1)(M\mathbf1)^\top
=M\left(I-\frac1mJ\right)M^\top\succeq0.
\]

Apply this separately to `U_B,U_D,U_F` and sum. By part (a), all three outer products are `vv^T`. Since `m=(p-1)/4`,

\[
UU^\top
\succeq
\frac3mvv^\top
=
\frac{12}{p-1}vv^\top.
\]

Pairing with `X succeq 0` gives

\[
E\ge\frac{12}{p-1}v^\top Xv.
\]

The semidefinite Cauchy inequality for the bilinear form induced by `X` gives

\[
R^2=(\mathbf1^\top Xv)^2
\le
(\mathbf1^\top X\mathbf1)(v^\top Xv)
=T(v^\top Xv).
\]

Combining the last two displays proves

\[
TE\ge\frac{12}{p-1}R^2,
\]

including the `T=0` case by continuity/directly from Cauchy. This proves part (b).

### 5.4 Three-way Hilbert-Schmidt Gram matrix

From the `V x V` principal block of `Q^2=pI-J`, the two anchors each contribute `J`; hence

\[
C^2+UU^\top=pI-3J. \tag{5}
\]

Let

\[
A=X^{1/2},\qquad B=CX^{1/2},\qquad D=JX^{1/2}.
\]

Their Hilbert-Schmidt Gram matrix is positive semidefinite. Its entries are

\[
\langle A,A\rangle=\operatorname{tr}X=1,
\]

\[
\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(CX)=S,
\qquad
\langle A,D\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(JX)=T,
\]

\[
\langle B,B\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(C^2X)=p-3T-E
\]

by (5),

\[
\langle B,D\rangle
=\operatorname{tr}(CJX)
=\mathbf1^\top XC\mathbf1=q,
\]

and, using `J^2=nJ`,

\[
\langle D,D\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(J^2X)=nT.
\]

Therefore

\[
M_X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&S&T\\
S&p-3T-E&q\\
T&q&nT
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

Taking the Schur complement of the top-left `1` gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
p-3T-E-S^2&q-ST\\
q-ST&T(n-T)
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\]

and therefore

\[
(q-ST)^2\le (p-3T-E-S^2)T(n-T). \tag{6}
\]

By definition

\[
v=(C+2I)\mathbf1,
\]

so

\[
R=\mathbf1^\top Xv
=\mathbf1^\top XC\mathbf1+2\mathbf1^\top X\mathbf1
=q+2T.
\]

For theta-plus feasible `X`, the exact edge accounting already established on P04 is `S=T-1+Delta`. Thus

\[
p-3T-E-S^2
=p-\bigl(T^2+T+1+E+2(T-1)\Delta+\Delta^2\bigr)
=G.
\]

Replacing `q=R-2T` in (6) yields

\[
(R-T(S+2))^2\le GT(n-T).
\]

Hence

\[
R\ge T(S+2)-\sqrt{GT(n-T)}.
\]

Whenever the right side is positive, part (b) lower-bounds `E` by its square; otherwise the positive-part version is the valid statement. This proves part (c).

Finally, part (d) is immediate from part (a):

\[
U(a\mathbf1_B,b\mathbf1_D,c\mathbf1_F)
=a(-v)+b(-v)+c(v)=(-a-b+c)v.
\]

Theorem 45 is proved.
