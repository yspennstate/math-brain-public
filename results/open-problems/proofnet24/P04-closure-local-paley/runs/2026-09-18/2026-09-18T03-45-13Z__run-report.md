### Theorem 36(a): the full positive complement cone is \(\vartheta^+\)

Define

\[
\Theta_+(K):=
\min\left\{
 t:\ \exists A=A^\top,\ A\ge0,\ A_{ii}=0,\ \operatorname{supp}A\subseteq E(K),\ tI-J+A\succeq0
\right\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is Szegedy's \(\vartheta^+(K)\) in the standard convention. Indeed, with

\[
Z=tI-J+A,
\]

one has \(Z_{ii}=t-1\), \(Z_{ij}=-1\) on nonedges of \(K\), and \(Z_{ij}\ge-1\) on edges of \(K\). Therefore

\[
\vartheta(K)\le \vartheta^+(K)=\Theta_+(K).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently the stronger statement

\[
\vartheta^+(\overline{G_{p,2}})\le\frac23\sqrt p
\tag{4.4}
\]

would imply the source conjecture.

The Lagrange dual of (4.2) is

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta^+(K)=
\max\left\{
\langle J,X\rangle:\ X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}X=1,\ X_{ij}\le0\quad(ij\in E(K))
\right\}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

**Proof.** Write the primal slack as

\[
M=tI-J+A\succeq0,
\]

and pair it with \(X\succeq0\). The Lagrangian is

\[
L=t-\langle X,tI-J+A\rangle
=t(1-\operatorname{tr}X)+\langle J,X\rangle-\langle A,X\rangle.
\]

The infimum over free \(t\) is finite exactly when \(\operatorname{tr}X=1\). Since each free off-diagonal coefficient of \(A\) on \(E(K)\) is constrained only by nonnegativity, the infimum over \(A\ge0\) is finite exactly when \(X_{ij}\le0\) on \(E(K)\), in which case it is attained at zero for the Lagrangian minimization. Thus the dual objective is \(\langle J,X\rangle\). Strict primal feasibility follows by taking all allowed edge weights positive and \(t\) sufficiently large, so strong SDP duality applies. This gives (4.5). ∎

### Theorem 36(c): obtuse-centroid dual

Factor a dual feasible matrix as

\[
X_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle.
\]

The trace condition becomes

\[
\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1,
\]

and the edge inequalities become

\[
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle\le0\qquad(ij\in E(K)).
\]

Finally

\[
\langle J,X\rangle
=\sum_{i,j}\langle v_i,v_j\rangle
=\left\|\sum_i v_i\right\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta^+(K)=
\max\left\{
\left\|\sum_i v_i\right\|^2:
\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1,
\ \langle v_i,v_j\rangle\le0\ \forall ij\in E(K)
\right\}.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The stronger P04 target is thus: every unit-energy vector configuration whose pairs on Paley-complement edges are nonacute has squared centroid at most \(2\sqrt p/3\).

### Theorem 36(d): exact Szegedy-plus conference defect

Let \(X\) be any feasible state in (4.5), and define

\[
T:=\langle J,X\rangle,
\qquad
S:=\langle C,X\rangle,
\qquad
E:=\langle UU^\top,X\rangle\ge0.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Let \(P_H\) be the **ordered** off-diagonal sum of \(X_{ij}\) over \(ij\in E(H)\), and let \(N_K\) be the ordered off-diagonal sum over \(ij\in E(K)\). Since \(X_{ij}\le0\) on \(E(K)\), define the nonnegative defect

\[
\Delta:=-2N_K\ge0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Because \(\operatorname{tr}X=1\),

\[
T=1+P_H+N_K,
\qquad
S=P_H-N_K.
\]

Eliminating \(P_H,N_K\) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{S=T-1+\Delta.}\tag{4.11}
\]

Now Cauchy–Schwarz in the state \(X\) gives

\[
S^2=(\operatorname{tr}CX)^2
\le \operatorname{tr}(C^2X).
\tag{4.12}
\]

Using (4.1),

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C^2X)
=p\operatorname{tr}X-3\langle J,X\rangle-\langle UU^\top,X\rangle
=p-3T-E.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Substituting (4.11) into (4.12)–(4.13) and expanding yields

\[
\boxed{
T^2+T+1+E+2(T-1)\Delta+\Delta^2\le p.}
\tag{4.14}
\]

For an ordinary-theta dual state, complement-edge entries are forced to zero, so \(\Delta=0\) and (4.14) reduces exactly to the earlier P04 conference-energy inequality

\[
T^2+T+1+E\le p.
\]

Thus (4.14) is the strict sign-sensitive extension that was missing from the ordinary-theta route: negative complement-edge Gram mass is not free; it generates a positive quadratic penalty.

Let

\[
t_p=\frac23\sqrt p,
\qquad
R_p:=p-(t_p^2+t_p+1)
=\frac59p-\frac23\sqrt p-1.
\tag{4.15}
\]

For any potential source-violating \(\vartheta^+\) state with \(T\ge t_p>1\), define

\[
D_X:=E+2(T-1)\Delta+\Delta^2\ge0.
\tag{4.16}
\]

If one can prove the uniform defect lower bound

\[
\boxed{D_X\ge R_p}
\tag{4.17}
\]

for every such feasible state, then (4.14) gives

\[
T^2+T+1\le p-R_p=t_p^2+t_p+1.
\]

Since \(x^2+x+1\) is strictly increasing for \(x\ge0\), this forces \(T\le t_p\), proving the stronger \(\vartheta^+\) version of the source bound.

There is also an exact useful dichotomy. Ignoring \(E\ge0\), the \(\Delta\)-term alone reaches the full source deficit when

\[
\Delta\ge d_p,
\]

where the positive root of

\[
\Delta^2+2(t_p-1)\Delta=R_p
\]

is

\[
\boxed{
d_p=\sqrt{p-3t_p}-(t_p-1)
=\sqrt{p-2\sqrt p}-\frac23\sqrt p+1
\sim\frac13\sqrt p.}
\tag{4.18}
\]

Therefore any \(\vartheta^+\) state with \(T>t_p\) must live in the narrow joint regime in which negative complement-edge mass is not too large and external escape energy is also too small. In particular, large negative edge mass is self-defeating rather than helpful.

This completes the proof of Claim 36. ∎
