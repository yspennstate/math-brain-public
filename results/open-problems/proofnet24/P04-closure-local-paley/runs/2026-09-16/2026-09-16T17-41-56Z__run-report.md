## 3. Exact normalization of the two-localization

Let `chi` be the quadratic character modulo `p`, with `chi(0)=0`. Because `p == 1 (mod 4)`, `chi(-1)=1`. The vertex set is

\[
V_2=\{x\in\mathbb F_p:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `x,y in V_2`, `x != y`, adjacency is

\[
x\sim y\iff \chi(x-y)=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The size is exactly

\[
|V_2|=\frac14\sum_{x\ne0,1}(1+\chi(x))(1+\chi(x-1))
=\frac{p-5}{4},
\tag{3.3}
\]

because the two linear character sums contribute `-1` each and
`sum_x chi(x(x-1))=-1`.

## 5. Move #11 — dualize

Here is the exact certificate formulation used this run.

### Lemma 5.1 — edge-fixed spectral dual certificate

Let `H` be any graph and write theta in the clique-bounding convention

\[
\vartheta(\overline H)
=\max\{\langle J,X\rangle:\operatorname{Tr}X=1,\ X\succeq0,\ X_{ij}=0\text{ for distinct nonedges }ij\notin E(H)\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If a real symmetric matrix `B` satisfies

\[
B_{ii}=1,\qquad B_{ij}=1\quad(ij\in E(H)),
\tag{5.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{\vartheta(\overline H)\le \lambda_{\max}(B).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

**Proof.** For every feasible `X`, the matrix `J-B` is supported only on distinct nonedges, where `X` vanishes. Hence

\[
\langle J,X\rangle=\langle B,X\rangle
\le\lambda_{\max}(B)\operatorname{Tr}X
=\lambda_{\max}(B).
\]

Taking the maximum proves (5.3). QED.

A particularly cheap family is

\[
B_x=I+A+x(J-I-A),
\tag{5.4}
\]

where `A` is the adjacency matrix of `G_{p,2}`. Equation (5.2) is automatic for every real `x`. This family is too rigid for the asymptotic problem, but it can produce exact finite certificates and gives a clean starting point for richer orbit-dependent duals.

## 7. Move #14 — find the group acting: a hidden S3, not merely the involution

The obvious involution `x -> 1-x` is not the full useful symmetry. There is an exact `S_3` action on every two-localized graph.

Define on `V_2`

\[
r(x)=1-x,\qquad s(x)=x^{-1}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — anharmonic S3 symmetry

For every prime `p == 1 (mod 4)`, both `r` and `s` preserve `V_2` and all graph adjacencies. They satisfy

\[
r^2=s^2=(rs)^3=1,
\tag{7.2}
\]

so they generate the usual anharmonic `S_3` action permuting the marked points `{0,1,infinity}`.

**Proof.** If `x in V_2`, then

\[
\chi(1-x)=\chi(-1)\chi(x-1)=1,
\qquad
\chi((1-x)-1)=\chi(-x)=1,
\]

so `r(x) in V_2`. Also

\[
\chi(x^{-1})=1,
\qquad
\chi(x^{-1}-1)=\chi((1-x)/x)=1,
\]

so `s(x) in V_2`.

For distinct `x,y in V_2`,

\[
r(x)-r(y)=-(x-y),
\]

and `-1` is a square. Further,

\[
s(x)-s(y)=\frac{y-x}{xy}.
\]

Both `xy` and `-1` are quadratic residues, so `chi(s(x)-s(y))=chi(x-y)`. Thus both maps preserve adjacency. The relations (7.2) are direct fractional-linear calculations. QED.

### Consequence 7.2 — exact SDP averaging

Because the theta primal and the edge-fixed dual are invariant under graph automorphisms, any feasible primal or dual matrix may be averaged over this `S_3` without changing its objective (primal) or increasing `lambda_max` (dual). Hence an optimum exists in the commutant of the `S_3` permutation representation.

At `p=149`, the exact checker proves that all `36` vertices split into six regular `S_3` orbits of size six. Thus

\[
\mathbb R^{36}\cong 6\,\mathbb R[S_3]
\cong 6\cdot\mathbf 1\ \oplus\ 6\cdot\mathrm{sgn}\ \oplus\ 12\cdot\mathrm{std}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

An `S_3`-commuting symmetric matrix therefore reduces, after a fixed real Fourier change of basis, to PSD blocks of sizes `6`, `6`, and `12` (the last tensored with the two-dimensional standard irrep). This is a materially stronger reduction than averaging only under `x -> 1-x`.

The asymptotic dimension still grows with `p`, so symmetry alone does not prove (2.1). The useful next step is to exploit the block structure to build an orbit-dependent nonedge dual whose three Fourier-sector blocks can be controlled by character sums, rather than forcing one scalar `x` on every nonedge.
