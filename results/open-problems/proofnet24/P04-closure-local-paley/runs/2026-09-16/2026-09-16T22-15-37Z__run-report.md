## 5. Move #20 — exact failure certificates for the obvious Paley routes

This section records why two natural routes cannot by themselves produce the factor `2/3`.

### 5.1 Projecting away the two clique anchors only gives `sqrt(p)-2`

Let `C` be the Paley conference matrix on `F_p`,

\[
C_{xy}=\chi(x-y)\ (x\ne y),\qquad C_{xx}=0,
\]

with `p=1 mod 4`. The standard normalized Paley Gram matrix may be written

\[
Q=\frac{I-J/p-C/\sqrt p}{1-1/p}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

It is positive semidefinite with unit diagonal. For Paley-adjacent distinct vertices the inner product is

\[
\rho=-\frac1{\sqrt p-1}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every vertex of `G_{p,2}` is adjacent to both anchors `0` and `1`, and the anchors are adjacent to one another. Project a localized vector `u_x` orthogonally off the span of `u_0,u_1`. The anchor Gram matrix is

\[
G_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\ \rho&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

and the correlation vector is `(rho,rho)`. The squared projection loss is therefore

\[
q=(\rho,\rho)G_2^{-1}(\rho,\rho)^T
=\frac{2\rho^2}{1+\rho}
=
\frac{2}{(\sqrt p-2)(\sqrt p-1)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `x,y` are adjacent inside `G_{p,2}`, their residual inner product before renormalization is `rho-q`; after renormalizing residual norms it is

\[
\rho'
=
\frac{\rho-q}{1-q}
=
-\frac1{\sqrt p-3}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The usual constant-inner-product theta argument then yields only

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,2}})\le 1-\frac1{\rho'}=\sqrt p-2.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is an exact additive improvement over the full Paley representation, but asymptotically its constant is `1`, not `2/3`. Thus simply projecting away the two known clique anchors cannot prove the source conjecture.

The same equicorrelation calculation with `k` clique anchors gives residual edge inner product `-1/(sqrt(p)-k-1)` and theta bound `sqrt(p)-k`; any fixed number of anchor projections still changes only the additive term.

### 5.2 The most direct conserved Paley projector also stops at `sqrt(p)`

Put

\[
P=I-J/p-C/\sqrt p\succeq0

=
\frac{\sqrt p+1}{p}
\left(\sqrt p\,D-X\right),
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(X).
\tag{5.7}

Hence

\[
X\preceq \sqrt p\,D.
\tag{5.8}

Testing against the all-ones vector gives

\tag{5.9}
\]

So the obvious conserved full-Paley projector recovers the classical `sqrt(p)` scale but contains no `2/3` gain. A source-level proof must use information not present in this first projector inequality.

## 6. Move #12 — conserved second moment and a new spectral-completion reduction

The preceding failures suggest not asking the full Paley projector for an immediate pointwise domination. Instead, keep the exact support constraint of theta and use a second moment.

Let `G` be an arbitrary finite simple graph with `m` vertices and adjacency matrix `A`. Define

\[
H=A-\frac12J.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus

\[
H_{ii}=-\frac12,
\qquad
H_{ij}=\begin{cases}
+\frac12,&ij\in E(G),\\
-\frac12,&ij\notin E(G),\ i\ne j.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For an edge `ij`, a direct expansion gives

\[
(H^2)_{ij}
=|N(i)\cap N(j)|-\frac{d_i+d_j}{2}+\frac m4.
\tag{6.3}
\]

On the diagonal there is a useful exact conservation law:

\[
(H^2)_{ii}
=d_i- d_i+\frac m4
=\frac m4
\quad\text{for every }i.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define the edge statistic

\[
s_{ij}
:=|N(i)\cap N(j)|-\frac{d_i+d_j}{2}+\frac m4,
\qquad ij\in E(G).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Now define the **second-moment completion constant**

\[
\boxed{
 c(G):=\min\left\{\lambda_{\max}(S):
 S=S^T,\ S_{ii}=0,\ S_{ij}=s_{ij}\ (ij\in E(G))
 \right\}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Only the nonedge entries of `S` are free.

### Theorem 6.1 — second-moment completion bound

For every finite graph `G` on `m` vertices,

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(\overline G)
\le
2\left(1+\sqrt{\frac m4+c(G)}\right).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
 c(G)=
 \max\left\{
 \operatorname{Tr}(H^2X)-\frac m4:
 X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{Tr}X=1,\ X_{ij}=0\ (ij\notin E(G),\ i\ne j)
 \right\}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus `c(G)` is not an invented unrelated statistic: it is exactly the worst excess second moment over the theta-feasible support class.

#### Proof

Let `X` be theta-primal feasible for `theta(overline G)` and write

\[
\Theta_X:=\langle J,X\rangle.
\]

Because `X` vanishes on distinct nonedges and `TrX=1`, (6.1)--(6.2) give

\[
\mu_X:=\operatorname{Tr}(HX)
=\frac12\sum_{ij\in E}^{\rm ordered}X_{ij}-\frac12\operatorname{Tr}X
=\frac{\Theta_X}{2}-1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since `X` is positive semidefinite of trace one, it defines a positive state on symmetric matrices. Positivity of the variance gives

\[
\mu_X^2
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(H^2X).
\tag{6.10}
\]

Now fix any feasible completion `S` in (6.6), and put

\[
R=\frac m4 I+S.
\tag{6.11}
\]

By (6.4)--(6.5), `R` agrees with `H^2` on the diagonal and all edge entries. Since `X` is supported exactly on the diagonal and edges,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(H^2X)=\operatorname{Tr}(RX).
\tag{6.12}
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(H^2X)
\le
\lambda_{\max}(R)\operatorname{Tr}X
=
\frac m4+\lambda_{\max}(S).
\tag{6.13}
\]

Minimizing over `S` and combining (6.9)--(6.10) yields

\[
\left(\frac{\Theta_X}{2}-1\right)^2
\le
\frac m4+c(G).
\tag{6.14}
\]

The right-hand side is nonnegative because it bounds a square. The theta objective is nonnegative, so

\[
\Theta_X
\le
2\left(1+\sqrt{\frac m4+c(G)}\right).
\]

Taking the supremum over feasible `X` proves (6.7).

For (6.8), write the primal definition of `c(G)` as minimizing `c` subject to

\[
cI-S\succeq0,
\qquad S_{ii}=0,
\qquad S_{ij}=s_{ij}\ (ij\in E).
\tag{6.15}
\]

The unconstrained nonedge entries force the dual matrix to vanish on distinct nonedges. The variable `c` forces dual trace one, and the PSD cone gives `X\succeq0`. The dual objective is the fixed diagonal/edge pairing

\[
\sum_i 0\cdot X_{ii}
+
\sum_{ij\in E}^{\rm ordered}s_{ij}X_{ij}
=
\operatorname{Tr}(H^2X)-\frac m4\operatorname{Tr}X,
\tag{6.16}
\]

where the final equality uses (6.4)--(6.5) and support. Strict primal feasibility holds after choosing arbitrary admissible fixed entries and taking `c` sufficiently large. Hence Slater strong duality applies and gives (6.8). QED.

## 9. What the algorithm is telling us, and why another finite prime is not the next goal

Three failure diagnostics now agree.

1. **Constant/very low-complexity completions are too rigid.** At `p=4001`, the best scalar-style or elementary row-potential completions found numerically remain around `0.86 sqrt(p)` or have second-moment completion ratio around `0.10`, far above the source target.
2. **Fixed-anchor orthogonal projection is additive, not multiplicative.** Section 5.1 proves it can only move `sqrt(p)` to `sqrt(p)-O(1)`.
3. **The successful full orbit completion is strongly correlated.** Both the direct `B` certificate and the second-moment candidate use many coupled free nonedge entries. This is qualitatively closer to correlated matrix-completion arguments than to a Hoffman bound or one-eigenvalue argument.

The source-level obstacle can now be stated concretely: construct, for every sufficiently large admissible `p`, a deterministic arithmetic free-nonedge completion `S_p` of the edge second moments satisfying

\[
\lambda_{\max}(S_p)
\le
\left(\frac7{144}-\delta\right)p
\tag{9.1}
\]

for some fixed `delta>0`, or otherwise prove the direct edge-fixed completion bound at the `2/3` scale. The numerical candidate (6.22) says this target is plausible, but no formula or uniform proof is yet available.

A likely useful bridge is to regard the desired free entries as a deterministic analogue of **spectral clipping**: start from the constrained second-moment matrix, correct only nonedges, and cancel its top positive modes while leaving the edge data intact. Feige--Grinberg's random-graph work reinforces that correlated rather than independent/free-entry choices are the relevant shape, but its probabilistic assumptions do not transfer automatically to this arithmetic graph.
