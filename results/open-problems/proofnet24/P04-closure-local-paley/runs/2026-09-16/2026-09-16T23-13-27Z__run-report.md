## 4. Move #10 DERANDOMIZE: exact reduction of every edge second moment to three Legendre traces

Define the Legendre-family cubic trace
\[
S_p(\lambda)
=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}
\chi\!\left(t(t-1)(t-\lambda)\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]
For x in V_2 put
\[
u(x)=\frac{x}{x-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Since x and x-1 are nonzero squares, all three coordinates
\[
x,\qquad x-1,\qquad u(x)
\tag{4.3}
\]
lie in the square subgroup Q=(F_p^\times)^2.

### Lemma 4.1 — quartic trace collapses to the same Legendre family

For distinct x,y in V_2,
\[
T(x,y):=
\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_p}
\chi\!\left(z(z-1)(z-x)(z-y)\right)
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
T(x,y)=
S_p\!\left(\frac{u(y)}{u(x)}\right)-1.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Set
\[
\lambda=\frac{x-y}{x(1-y)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
t=\frac{(x-y)z}{x(z-y)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
The inverse map is
\[
z=\frac{txy}{tx-x+y}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
A direct factorization gives
\[
z(z-1)(z-x)(z-y)
=
\frac{\kappa\,t(t-1)(t-\lambda)}
{(tx-x+y)^4},
\tag{4.7}
\]
where
\[
\kappa=x^3y^2(1-y)(x-y)^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Every factor contributing nontrivially to chi(kappa) is a square: x is a square, 1-y is a square because chi(-1)=chi(y-1)=1, and y^2,(x-y)^2 are squares. Hence chi(kappa)=1. The smooth projective quartic
\[
Y^2=z(z-1)(z-x)(z-y)
\]
is therefore birational over F_p to the untwisted Legendre cubic
\[
W^2=t(t-1)(t-\lambda).
\]
The quartic has two rational points at infinity because its leading coefficient is a square, whereas the cubic has one. Thus equality of projective point counts gives
\[
p+T(x,y)+2=p+S_p(\lambda)+1,
\]
hence
\[
T(x,y)=S_p(\lambda)-1.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Finally,
\[
1-\lambda
=
\frac{y(x-1)}{x(y-1)}
=
\frac{u(y)}{u(x)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]
The substitution t -> 1-t and chi(-1)=1 give
\[
S_p(1-\lambda)=S_p(\lambda).
\tag{4.11}
\]
Equations (4.9)--(4.11) prove (4.4). QED.

### Lemma 4.2 — exact conference-square identity

For distinct x,y in V_2,
\[
\boxed{
4(C^2)_{xy}
=
S_p\!\left(\frac yx\right)
+
S_p\!\left(\frac{y-1}{x-1}\right)
+
S_p\!\left(\frac{u(y)}{u(x)}\right)
-6.
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Consequently, on an edge xy of G_{p,2}, where C_{xy}=1,
\[
\boxed{
(H^2)_{xy}
=
\frac1{16}
\left[
S_p\!\left(\frac yx\right)
+
S_p\!\left(\frac{y-1}{x-1}\right)
+
S_p\!\left(\frac{u(y)}{u(x)}\right)
-14
\right].
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

### Proof

For z not equal to 0,1,
\[
\mathbf 1_{V_2}(z)
=\frac14(1+\chi z)(1+\chi(z-1)).
\tag{4.14}
\]
Use this in
\[
(C^2)_{xy}
=\sum_{z\in V_2}\chi(x-z)\chi(z-y).
\tag{4.15}
\]
After multiplying by four, the four expanded sums are:

- the quadratic sum
  \[
  \sum_z\chi((x-z)(z-y))=-1;
  \]
- after z=xt, the first cubic sum
  \[
  S_p(y/x);
  \]
- after z=1+(x-1)t, the second cubic sum
  \[
  S_p((y-1)/(x-1));
  \]
- the quartic sum T(x,y).

The formal weight in (4.14) equals 1/2 at each excluded endpoint z=0,1. For x,y in V_2 the character factor in (4.15) equals +1 at each endpoint, so after multiplying by four the endpoint correction is exactly -4. Therefore
\[
4(C^2)_{xy}
=-1+S_p(y/x)+S_p((y-1)/(x-1))+T(x,y)-4.
\]
Insert Lemma 4.1 to obtain (4.12).

From (3.1),
\[
(H^2)_{xy}=\frac{(C^2)_{xy}-2C_{xy}}4
\quad(x\ne y).
\]
On an edge C_{xy}=1, and (4.13) follows. QED.

This is the first useful derandomization this run: the entire edge data of the second-moment completion is a sum of **three copies of one one-parameter elliptic trace function**, transported by the anharmonic coordinates x, x-1, and x/(x-1).

## 5. Move #3 PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: exact Jacobi-square Fourier diagonalization

The natural stronger hope was that the three trace kernels might themselves give a uniform completion proving (3.4). Their Fourier structure can in fact be computed exactly.

For a multiplicative character psi of F_p^\times, extended by zero at zero, define the Jacobi sum
\[
J(\psi,\chi)=
\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}\psi(a)\chi(1-a).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — exact multiplicative transform

For every multiplicative character psi,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb F_p^\times}
S_p(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)
=
J(\psi,\chi)^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Swap the t- and lambda-sums in (4.1). The t=0 term vanishes. For t nonzero set lambda=tu. Since
\[
t(t-1)(t-tu)=t^2(t-1)(1-u)
\]
and chi(t^2)=1,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}S_p(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)
&=
\sum_{t\ne0,u\ne0}
\psi(t)\psi(u)\chi(t-1)\chi(1-u)\\
&=
\left(\sum_t\psi(t)\chi(1-t)\right)
\left(\sum_u\psi(u)\chi(1-u)\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where chi(t-1)=chi(1-t) because chi(-1)=1. This is exactly (5.2). QED.

For psi neither 1 nor chi, the standard Gauss-sum formula gives
\[
|J(\psi,\chi)|=\sqrt p.
\tag{5.3}
\]
For psi=1 or chi, one has J(psi,chi)=-1. Hence
\[
\left|
\sum_{\lambda\ne0}S_p(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)
\right|
\le p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Now restrict to the square subgroup Q. If eta is a character of Q and psi is either extension to F_p^\times, then
\[
\boxed{
\kappa_\eta
=
\sum_{q\in Q}S_p(q)\eta(q)
=
\frac12\left(
J(\psi,\chi)^2
+
J(\psi\chi,\chi)^2
\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
For generic psi,
\[
J(\psi,\chi)J(\psi\chi,\chi)=p
\tag{5.6}
\]
because the Gauss-sum factors telescope and chi(-1)=1. Thus the two generic factors have reciprocal phases and
\[
\kappa_\eta=p\cos(2\theta_\psi)
\tag{5.7}
\]
for some phase theta_psi. The two exceptional characters give kappa_eta=1. In every case
\[
\boxed{|\kappa_\eta|\le p.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Also
\[
S_p(q)=S_p(q^{-1})
\qquad(q\in Q),
\tag{5.9}
\]
from the substitution t=qu and chi(q)=1. Therefore the convolution matrix
\[
K_Q(a,b)=S_p(b/a),\qquad a,b\in Q,
\tag{5.10}
\]
is real symmetric and
\[
\boxed{\|K_Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le p.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

### Corollary 5.2 — all three transported kernels have norm at most p

Define on V_2
\[
L_1(x,y)=S_p(y/x),
\]
\[
L_2(x,y)=S_p((y-1)/(x-1)),
\]
\[
L_3(x,y)=S_p(u(y)/u(x)).
\tag{5.12}
\]
The maps x -> x, x -> x-1, and x -> u(x) are injective maps from V_2 into Q. Hence each L_j is, after relabeling, a principal compression of K_Q. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\|L_j\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le p,\qquad j=1,2,3.}
\tag{5.13}
\]

This is a fully explicit all-p arithmetic spectral statement. It does not use a Gaussian-universality or random-design ansatz.

## 7. Move #22 READ THE FAILURES: the deterministic masking problem is now explicit

Feige--Grinberg's key lesson is not a random-graph constant; it is structural: useful free entries must be correlated, and their proposed construction starts from a spectral matrix Z and then **masks it onto the free entries**. Their paper explicitly says independent free entries cannot improve the leading random-graph spectral edge, and it identifies the hard part as controlling the masked, dependent correction. In the Bernoulli case they introduce unproved spectral/eigenvector assumptions; generalized-Wigner convergence alone does not control the sum's extreme edge.

For the present arithmetic graph, that masking operation has an exact deterministic form.

Let N be the zero-one adjacency matrix of localized nonedges. Since C_xy=+1 on edges and -1 on nonedges,
\[
\boxed{
N=\frac{J-I-C}{2}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Therefore, for every symmetric matrix Z,
\[
\boxed{
N\circ Z
=
\frac12\left(
Z-\operatorname{diag}Z-C\circ Z
\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
This is the exact Paley analogue of the random free-entry mask in Feige--Grinberg.

There is an additional Schur-product identity. Let P_A be the V_2 principal block of the positive Paley projector
\[
P=I-\frac1pJ-\frac1{\sqrt p}C_{\rm full}.
\]
Then
\[
P_A\succeq0.
\]
For any Z\succeq0, Schur's theorem gives P_A\circ Z\succeq0, and entrywise expansion gives
\[
P_A\circ Z
=
\operatorname{diag}Z-\frac1p Z-\frac1{\sqrt p}(C\circ Z).
\tag{7.3}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
C\circ Z
=
\sqrt p\,\operatorname{diag}Z
-\frac1{\sqrt p}Z
-\sqrt p\,(P_A\circ Z),
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
and substituting into (7.2),
\[
\boxed{
N\circ Z
=
\frac{1+1/\sqrt p}{2}Z
-\frac{1+\sqrt p}{2}\operatorname{diag}Z
+\frac{\sqrt p}{2}(P_A\circ Z).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This identity is elementary but strategically important. If Z is chosen as a PSD spectral projector or positive spectral function of a starting completion, the desired masking operation decomposes into:

1. a controlled multiple of Z itself;
2. a diagonal term governed by eigenvector delocalization;
3. a positive Schur term P_A\circ Z whose operator interaction still needs control.

That is almost exactly the pair of obstacles isolated by Feige--Grinberg: diagonal concentration/delocalization and a spectral law for the masked correlated correction. Here the problem is no longer probabilistic: both terms are deterministic arithmetic objects.

### Why the obvious deterministic clipping did not close

I tested, only as floating route diagnostics, several low-complexity choices in the family
\[
B=I+C+N\circ f(C)
\tag{7.6}
\]
with f a low-degree polynomial, together with the simpler choice corresponding to masking a negative spectral absolute value. Such families can beat 2/3 at some small primes but do not stay below 2/3 across the diagnostic primes p=401,601,1009. No floating value is recorded as a theorem or metric movement.

The failure is coherent with (7.5): a polynomial spectral function controls Z, but without a uniform theorem for its diagonal and the Schur term P_A\circ Z, masking can regenerate a large positive edge.

## 8. Exact comparison with Kunisky's obstruction

Kunisky's Section 7 explains why weak convergence is insufficient for an extreme eigenvalue. The high-moment criterion requires moments k much larger than log p. His direct necklace-character expansion contains exponentially many terms; applying the triangle inequality leaves exponential factors that violate the edge criterion. A proof would need cancellation **before** the triangle inequality among the necklace sums.

The current trace-kernel calculation is compatible with that diagnosis:

- the one-variable Legendre trace S_p has a perfect multiplicative transform (5.2);
- localization is multiplication by the shifted-square masks x in Q, x-1 in Q;
- the three anharmonic coordinates are coupled by S_3 rather than independent;
- operator-norm control after free-entry masking asks for cancellation among these coupled character phases, not merely a Weil bound term by term.

Thus the exact missing source-level estimate can now be phrased as a deterministic harmonic-analysis problem rather than a generic SDP mystery:

> construct a correlated nonedge correction, preferably as a spectral function Z of one of the arithmetic trace operators, and prove from Jacobi/character identities that the two error terms in (7.5) stay below the fixed margin needed for lambda_max(B) <= (2/3-o(1))sqrt(p).

This is not yet a proof. It is a sharper route specification than the previous "find an arithmetic formula."
