## 4. Move #3 — a stronger exact conference-energy inequality

Let `C` be the full `p x p` Paley conference matrix

\[
C_{xy}=\chi(x-y)\quad(x\ne y),\qquad C_{xx}=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because `p=1 mod 4`, `C` is symmetric and satisfies

\[
C^2=pI-J.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `C_A` be its principal block on `A=V_2`. Let `O=F_p\setminus(A\cup\{0,1\})`, and let

\[
U=C_{A,O}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Every `x in A` is adjacent to both anchors 0 and 1, so the two anchor columns of `C` restricted to `A` are both the all-ones vector. Taking the `A x A` block of (4.2) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{C_A^2+UU^T=pI_A-3J_A.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The previous report recorded (4.4). The new point is to pair it directly with the theta state.

### Theorem `P04-I0101-CONFERENCE-ENERGY-11` — PROVED

For every admissible prime `p` and every feasible `X` in (3.1), put

\[
\Theta_X=\langle J,X\rangle,
\qquad
E_X:=\operatorname{Tr}(UU^T X)
=\|X^{1/2}U\|_F^2\ge0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\Theta_X^2+\Theta_X+1+E_X\le p.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof

On the support of a theta-feasible `X`, distinct off-diagonal entries occur only on graph edges, where `C_A(x,y)=+1`; the diagonal of `C_A` is zero. Since `Tr X=1`,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(C_A X)=\Theta_X-1.
\tag{4.7}
\]

For any real symmetric matrix `M` and positive semidefinite `X` of trace one,

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}MX)^2\le\operatorname{Tr}(M^2X),
\tag{4.8}
\]

because `Tr[X(M-mu I)^2]>=0` with `mu=Tr(MX)`. Applying (4.8) to `M=C_A` and using (4.4),

\[
(\Theta_X-1)^2
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(C_A^2X)
=p-3\Theta_X-E_X.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Expanding and moving terms gives (4.6). QED.

### Exact source-matching sufficient energy threshold

Let

\[
T_p=\frac23\sqrt p.
\]

Because the function `t -> t^2+t` is increasing for `t>=0`, (4.6) immediately implies the following stronger sufficient condition:

\[
\boxed{
E_X\ge
\frac59p-\frac23\sqrt p-1
\quad\text{for every theta-feasible }X
\Longrightarrow
\bar\vartheta(G_{p,2})\le\frac23\sqrt p.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Indeed, the right side of (4.10) is exactly

\[
p-(T_p^2+T_p+1).
\]

Thus the source problem admits a new exact reformulation **as a sufficient stronger target**: prove that the three non-anchor sign cells force at least the energy in (4.10), or, if that all-feasible statement is false, prove the corresponding lower bound only for high-objective/optimal theta states.

For a Gram realization `X_{xy}=<v_x,v_y>`, the energy has the transparent form

\[
E_X
=
\sum_{z\in O}
\left\|\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x-z)v_x\right\|^2.
\tag{4.11}
\]

So the missing `2/3` gain is a concrete **external-sign-energy** problem, not merely an unnamed SDP residual.

The unrestricted minimum eigenvalue of `UU^T` is not expected to suffice: floating diagnostics at the already studied finite primes place it far below the right side of (4.10). Those diagnostics are not a theorem and are not used as evidence; they only show that the nonedge-support constraint on `X` must be exploited.

## 5. Move #10 — derandomize the vertex set: an exact hyperbola quotient

The successful `p=1009` and `p=4001` certificates had thousands of fitted `S_3`-orbit weights. The next step should not be a larger lookup table. There is an exact cyclic parametrization of the two-localization itself.

Put

\[
q=\frac{p-1}{2},
\]

let `Q` be the subgroup of nonzero squares, and choose a generator `alpha` of `Q`. Define

\[
\phi(u)=\frac{(u+1)^2}{4u}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem `P04-I0101-HYPERBOLA-QUOTIENT-11` — PROVED

The map (5.1) induces a bijection

\[
\boxed{
(Q\setminus\{\pm1\})/(u\sim u^{-1})
\ \cong\ V_2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
V_2\cong
(\mathbb Z_q\setminus\{0,q/2\})/(k\sim-k).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `q/2=(p-1)/4=(p-5)/4+1`, the exponents

\[
k=1,2,\ldots,m,
\qquad m=\frac{p-5}{4},
\tag{5.4}
\]

are canonical representatives.

#### Proof

For `u in Q\setminus{+/-1}`, both

\[
\phi(u)=\frac{(u+1)^2}{4u},
\qquad
\phi(u)-1=\frac{(u-1)^2}{4u}
\tag{5.5}
\]

are nonzero squares, so `phi(u) in V_2`. Also

\[
\phi(u)=\frac14(u+2+u^{-1}).
\]

Hence `phi(u)=phi(v)` iff

\[
u+u^{-1}=v+v^{-1},
\]

which factors as `(u-v)(uv-1)=0`. Thus the fibers are exactly `{u,u^{-1}}`. The only inversion-fixed points in `Q` are `+/-1`, which have been removed. There are `q-2=(p-5)/2=2m` remaining elements, hence exactly `m=|V_2|` inversion pairs. This proves (5.2)--(5.4). QED.

The parametrization is compatible with the familiar anharmonic action: `x -> 1-x` and `x -> x^{-1}` become rational transformations of the hyperbola coordinate rather than mysterious graph automorphisms.

## 6. Circulant times Hankel: exact local conference factorization

For exponents in `Z_q`, define the even character sequence

\[
h_r:=\chi(\alpha^r-1).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Because

\[
\alpha^{-r}-1=-\frac{\alpha^r-1}{\alpha^r},
\]

and both `-1` and every `alpha^r` are squares,

\[
\boxed{h_{-r}=h_r.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The elementary identity

\[
\boxed{
\phi(u)-\phi(v)
=
\frac{(u-v)(uv-1)}{4uv}
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

has square denominator. Therefore, in the canonical exponent coordinates (5.4),

\[
\boxed{
(C_A)_{k\ell}
=h_{k-\ell}h_{k+\ell}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is a source-level structural gain: the noncirculant two-localized conference matrix is the entrywise product of a **difference/circulant** kernel and a **sum/Hankel** kernel.

There is an equally useful lift. Let

\[
E=\mathbb Z_q\setminus\{0,q/2\}
\]

and define on `E`

\[
M_{k\ell}=h_{k-\ell}h_{k+\ell}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

By evenness, `M` is constant on the four sign choices `(k,l),(-k,l),(k,-l),(-k,-l)`, and the within-pair value is zero. After ordering `E` in `+/-` pairs,

\[
\boxed{M=C_A\otimes J_2.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the nonzero spectrum of the lifted matrix is exactly twice the spectrum of `C_A`. This does not make the graph circulant, but it embeds its sign matrix into a rigid cyclic difference-sum operator.

## 7. Move #10 continued — Fourier/Jacobi representation

The cyclic lift has an explicit Fourier form. Let

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/q},
\qquad
\widehat h_a=\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z_q}h_r\omega^{-ar}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

First extend (6.5) to all `k,l in Z_q` and let `F` be the unitary Fourier matrix `F_{rk}=q^{-1/2}omega^{-rk}`.

### Theorem `P04-I0101-FOURIER-JACOBI-11` — PROVED

If `r,s` have opposite parity, then

\[
(FMF^*)_{rs}=0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

If they have the same parity, choose `A,B in Z_q` satisfying the **paired half equations**

\[
A+B=r,
\qquad
A-B=s.
\tag{7.3}
\]

There are exactly two paired solutions, differing by `(q/2,q/2)`, and the following expression is independent of that choice:

\[
\boxed{
(FMF^*)_{rs}
=\frac1q\left(
\widehat h_A\widehat h_B
+\widehat h_{A+q/2}\widehat h_{B+q/2}
\right).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Moreover each Fourier coefficient is an explicit pair of Jacobi sums. Let `g` be a primitive root of `F_p^*`, let `rho(g)=e^{2pi i/(p-1)}`, and put `eta_a=rho^{-a}`. Extend all multiplicative characters by zero at zero. Then

\[
\boxed{
\widehat h_a
=\frac12\Bigl(
J(\eta_a,\chi)
+J(\eta_a\chi,\chi)
\Bigr).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

#### Proof of (7.2)--(7.4)

By definition,

\[
(FMF^*)_{rs}
=\frac1q\sum_{k,l}
\omega^{-rk+sl}
h_{k-l}h_{k+l}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Set `a=k-l`, `b=k+l`. Because `q` is even, the image consists precisely of pairs `(a,b)` with the same parity, and every image point has two preimages differing by `(q/2,q/2)`. The two preimage phases differ by `(-1)^{s-r}`. Hence they cancel when `r,s` have opposite parity, proving (7.2).

For equal parity, the two phases add. With `A,B` satisfying (7.3), the phase is `omega^{-Aa-Bb}`. The indicator that `a,b` have equal parity is

\[
\frac12\bigl(1+(-1)^{a-b}\bigr).
\]

The prefactor two from the two-to-one change of variables cancels this `1/2`, and the two resulting product sums are exactly the two terms in (7.4). Replacing `(A,B)` by `(A+q/2,B+q/2)` only exchanges those two terms. QED.

#### Proof of (7.5)

As `r` ranges over `Z_q`, `u=alpha^r` ranges over the square subgroup `Q`, and `eta_a(alpha)=omega^{-a}`. Thus

\[
\widehat h_a
=\sum_{u\in Q}\eta_a(u)\chi(u-1).
\]

On `F_p^*`, the square indicator is `(1+chi(u))/2`. Since `chi(-1)=1`, `chi(u-1)=chi(1-u)`. Therefore

\[
\widehat h_a
=\frac12\sum_u\eta_a(u)\chi(1-u)
+\frac12\sum_u\eta_a(u)\chi(u)\chi(1-u),
\]

which is precisely (7.5). QED.

Equation (7.4) explains both the promise and the obstruction: this is not a one-eigenvalue circulant problem. The two-localized operator is a parity-blocked matrix built from **correlated Jacobi-sum phases**. Any uniform proof must control those correlations, not merely the individual Weil magnitudes.

## 8. Three outside cells are exact elliptic-trace coordinates

The external energy (4.11) splits naturally into the three non-anchor sign cells. For `a,b in {+1,-1}`, define

\[
Z_{ab}=\{z\ne0,1:\chi(z)=a,\ \chi(z-1)=b\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus `A=Z_{++}`. For distinct `x,y in A`, put

\[
Q_{ab}(x,y)
=
\sum_{z\in Z_{ab}}\chi(x-z)\chi(y-z).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Also define the complete character sums

\[
S_1(x,y)=\sum_z\chi(z(x-z)(y-z)),
\tag{8.3}
\]

\[
S_2(x,y)=\sum_z\chi((z-1)(x-z)(y-z)),
\tag{8.4}
\]

and

\[
S_{12}(x,y)=\sum_z\chi(z(z-1)(x-z)(y-z)).
\tag{8.5}
\]

### Theorem `P04-I0101-THREE-CELL-TRACE-11` — PROVED

For every distinct `x,y in A`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{ab}(x,y)
=\frac14\left[
-3+a(S_1-1)+b(S_2-1)+abS_{12}
\right].
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

#### Proof

Away from the endpoints `z=0,1`, the sign-cell indicator is

\[
\frac14(1+a\chi(z))(1+b\chi(z-1)).
\]

Let `K(z)=chi((x-z)(y-z))`. For `x ne y`, the standard quadratic-character identity gives

\[
\sum_zK(z)=-1.
\]

Because `x,y in A`, the endpoint values are `K(0)=K(1)=1`, so deleting 0 and 1 changes this to `-3`. In the `chi(z)K(z)` sum, the `z=0` term vanishes and the `z=1` term equals one, giving `S_1-1`; symmetrically the `chi(z-1)K(z)` contribution is `S_2-1`. The product term vanishes at both endpoints and is exactly `S_{12}`. Expanding the indicator proves (8.6). QED.

For `a=b=+1`, (8.2) is exactly `(C_A^2)_{xy}`. Summing the other three cells gives `(UU^T)_{xy}=-3-(C_A^2)_{xy}`, recovering (4.4) off the diagonal.

The three sums in (8.3)--(8.5) are elliptic Frobenius traces. The first two are squarefree monic cubics, so Hasse gives

\[
|S_1|,|S_2|\le2\sqrt p.
\tag{8.7}
\]

The quartic in (8.5) is squarefree monic with two rational points at infinity; writing its elliptic trace in the usual projective model gives

\[
|S_{12}+1|\le2\sqrt p.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Thus every external-cell correlation has an explicit `O(sqrt(p))` arithmetic description. This is a substantial compression of the `O(p^2)` free-pair data in the finite orbit certificates. It is not yet a uniform spectral bound: pointwise Hasse bounds do not control the correlated operator edge.

### 9.3 The first genuinely arithmetic low-complexity ansatz is still too small

In the hyperbola coordinates define

\[
s=h_{k-l},\qquad t=h_{k+l}
\]

for a distinct pair. The edge/nonedge decision is `st=+1/-1`. Consider a correction whose value depends only on the two signs `(s,t)` and is required to vanish on edges.

### Lemma `P04-I0101-TWO-BIT-DIMENSION-11` — PROVED

The space of such corrections has dimension exactly two, with basis

\[
N(s,t)=\frac{1-st}{2},
\qquad
D(s,t)=\frac{s-t}{2}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

#### Proof

There are four sign states. Edge states are `(1,1)` and `(-1,-1)`, where the correction must vanish. The two remaining nonedge states `(1,-1)` and `(-1,1)` may be assigned arbitrary values, hence the space has dimension two. On those two states `N=1` and `D=+1,-1`, so (9.1) is a basis. QED.

Floating discovery work, **not a theorem and not metric evidence**, indicates that this two-parameter class deteriorates with `p`; adding a few low-order local autocorrelation features does not repair the trend. Compressing each nonedge pair to the exact elliptic triple `(S_1,S_2,S_12)` is much more expressive, but exploratory convex optimization likewise shows no stable universal lookup rule yet: one sorted-triple search was around `0.61 sqrt(p)` at `p=1009` but around `0.73 sqrt(p)` at `p=4001`; an ordered-triple search at `p=4001` remained around `0.69 sqrt(p)` after extensive floating optimization. These figures are route diagnostics only: optimizer convergence was not certified and they do **not** prove impossibility of either class.

The failure is informative. The finite certificates are not merely functions of a bounded local statistic. The missing uniform object likely has to retain a genuinely operator-valued Jacobi correlation or a high-Theta state constraint.
