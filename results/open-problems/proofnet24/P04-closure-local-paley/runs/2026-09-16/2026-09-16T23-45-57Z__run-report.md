# 3. Move #29 + #1: replace the two-localization by an exact split-conic / square-torus double cover

The useful category change is not another graph symmetry. The two simultaneous square conditions defining `V_2` are the rational split conic

\[
a^2-b^2=1.
\]

That conic has a multiplicative parameter, and using it exposes an exact two-sheeted torus model of `G_{p,2}`.

Let

\[
Q=(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2,
\qquad
X=Q\setminus\{1,-1\},
\]

and define

\[
\pi(q)=\frac{(q+1)^2}{4q}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Recall

\[
V_2=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}.
\]

## Theorem `P04-I0101-SPLIT-CONIC-LIFT-11`

For every prime `p = 1 mod 4`:

1. `pi:X -> V_2` is onto and every fiber is exactly `{q,q^{-1}}`;
2. for all `q,r in X`,
   \[
   \boxed{
   \pi(q)-\pi(r)
   =\frac{(q-r)(qr-1)}{4qr};
   }
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
3. consequently
   \[
   \boxed{
   \chi(\pi(q)-\pi(r))
   =\chi((q-r)(qr-1));
   }
   \tag{3.3}
   \]
4. the graph on `X` joining `q,r` when the right side of (3.3) is `+1` is exactly the independent two-clone blowup
   \[
   G_{p,2}[\overline K_2];
   \]
5. the clique-bounding Lovasz theta value is unchanged by this blowup:
   \[
   \boxed{
   \vartheta(\overline{G_{p,2}[\overline K_2]})
   =\vartheta(\overline{G_{p,2}}).
   }
   \tag{3.4}
   \]

### Proof

For `q in Q`, write `q=r^2`. Then

\[
\pi(q)
=\left(\frac{r+r^{-1}}2\right)^2
\]

is a square, and

\[
\pi(q)-1
=\frac{(q-1)^2}{4q}
=\left(\frac{r-r^{-1}}2\right)^2
\]

is a square. Removing `q=1,-1` removes exactly the possibilities `pi(q)=1,0`, so `pi(X) subset V_2`.

Conversely let `x in V_2`. Choose `a,b` with

\[
a^2=x,\qquad b^2=x-1.
\]

Then `(a+b)(a-b)=1`. Put `r=a+b` and `q=r^2 in Q`. Since `r^{-1}=a-b`,

\[
\frac{r+r^{-1}}2=a,
\]

so `pi(q)=a^2=x`. Also `q` cannot be `1` or `-1`, since those would force respectively `b=0` or `a=0`. Hence `pi` is onto.

The equation `pi(q)=x` is

\[
q^2+(2-4x)q+1=0.
\]

Its two roots have product one. On `X` they are distinct, because a repeated root would be `q=+/-1`. Thus the fiber is exactly `{q,q^{-1}}`.

A direct subtraction gives (3.2). Since `4qr` is a nonzero square, applying the quadratic character gives (3.3). If `r=q^{-1}`, the factor `qr-1` vanishes, so the two points in one fiber are nonadjacent. For two different fibers, (3.3) says both clones have exactly the adjacency of their common image under `pi`. This proves the independent two-clone description.

It remains to prove (3.4), since this is the part that makes the lift useful rather than merely decorative.

Use the clique-normalized theta primal

\[
\vartheta(\overline G)
=\max\{\langle J,Y\rangle:
Y\succeq0,\ \operatorname{Tr}Y=1,
Y_{vw}=0\text{ for distinct nonedges }vw\text{ of }G\}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

More generally let `H=G[\overline K_s]`. Average any feasible matrix for `H` over the independent permutation group on the `s` clones in each fiber. The averaged matrix has diagonal fiber blocks

\[
a_v I_s
\]

(the within-fiber off-diagonal entries are forced to zero) and, on a base edge `vw`, a constant cross block

\[
c_{vw}J_s;
\]

cross blocks over base nonedges are zero.

In the orthogonal decomposition into the fiber-constant subspace and the fiberwise zero-sum subspaces, positivity is equivalent to

\[
a_v\ge0
\]

and positivity of the base matrix with diagonal `a_v` and off-diagonal `s c_{vw}`. Define

\[
Y_{vv}=s a_v,
\qquad
Y_{vw}=s^2c_{vw}.
\]

Then `Y/s` is exactly the constant-subspace matrix, hence `Y >= 0`; moreover `Tr Y=1`, the base nonedge zeros are preserved, and

\[
\langle J,Y\rangle
\]

is exactly the lifted objective. This maps every averaged lifted feasible point to a base feasible point with the same value.

Conversely, from a base feasible `Y`, use blocks `(Y_vv/s)I_s` on each fiber and `(Y_vw/s^2)J_s` on each base edge. The fiber-constant block is `Y/s >= 0`, and the zero-sum eigenvalues are `Y_vv/s >= 0`, so the lift is PSD and has the same trace and objective. Thus the two optima agree. Taking `s=2` proves (3.4). QED.

### Why this is useful

The original two-localization is now a quotient of a split multiplicative torus by the deck involution `q <-> q^{-1}`. This creates a genuine harmonic-analysis coordinate that was absent in the `x`-description, while preserving the exact theta value.

# 4. Move #29: the boundary points become `0` and `infinity` in a Cayley chart

The same cover has a compactified form that makes the exceptional points explicit rather than burying them in denominator casework.

For `q != -1`, set

\[
z=\frac{q-1}{q+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
q=\frac{1+z}{1-z},
\qquad
\pi(q)=\frac1{1-z^2}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\chi(q)=\chi\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)
=\chi(1-z^2),
\tag{4.3}
\]

so the square-torus condition becomes `chi(1-z^2)=1`. The deleted point `q=1` is `z=0`, while `q=-1` is the point at infinity. The deck involution becomes

\[
q\mapsto q^{-1}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
z\mapsto -z.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For two finite chart points,

\[
\pi(q(z))-\pi(q(w))
=\frac{z^2-w^2}{(1-z^2)(1-w^2)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Both denominator factors are squares on the chart domain, hence

\[
\boxed{
\chi(\pi(q(z))-\pi(q(w)))=\chi(z^2-w^2).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus `G_{p,2}` can equivalently be viewed as the `z -> -z` quotient of the finite set

\[
\{z\in\mathbb F_p^\times:\chi(1-z^2)=1\}
\]

with adjacency sign `chi(z^2-w^2)`. The previously awkward `0,1,infinity` geometry is now represented explicitly by the chart endpoints. No asymptotic claim is made here; this is an exact reparameterization.

# 5. Move #1 continued: exact Toeplitz--Hankel form on the cyclic square torus

Choose a primitive root `g`, let

\[
M=\frac{p-1}{2},
\qquad
q=g^{2a},\ r=g^{2b},
\qquad a,b\in\mathbb Z_M,
\]

and define the real even sequence

\[
f(k)=\chi(g^{2k}-1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equation (3.3) becomes

\[
\boxed{
K_{ab}=f(a-b)f(a+b).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The two forbidden torus points are `a=0` and `a=M/2`; `K` vanishes on the diagonal and on inverse-twin pairs. Thus the lifted adjacency is the sign pattern of a **Toeplitz--Hankel product** on a cyclic torus.

This is not a circulant diagonalization, but its Fourier action is exact. Put

\[
\omega=e^{2\pi i/M},
\qquad
\widehat f(k)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_M}f(a)\omega^{-ka}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the character vector `e_l(a)=omega^{la}`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
(Ke_l)(a)
&=\sum_b f(a-b)f(a+b)\omega^{lb}\\
&=\frac1M\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z_M}
\widehat f(s+l)\widehat f(s)
\omega^{(l+2s)a}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore, in the orthonormal Fourier basis,

\[
\boxed{
\langle e_k/\sqrt M,\ K(e_l/\sqrt M)\rangle
=\frac1M
\sum_{2s=k-l}
\widehat f(s+l)\widehat f(s).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because `p=1 mod 4`, the order `M` is even. Hence multiplication by two on the dual cyclic group is not invertible: the Fourier matrix splits into two parity sectors, but each sector is still of order `M/2`. This gives a precise reason the torus lift does **not** magically turn Conjecture 27 into a scalar multiplier bound.

There is also a closed Jacobi-sum formula for the coefficients. If `eta` is a character of `Q` and `psi` is either extension to `F_p^*`, then, with the usual zero extension of multiplicative characters,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q\in Q}\chi(q-1)\overline{\eta(q)}
=\frac12\left[J(\psi,\chi)+J(\psi\chi,\chi)\right].
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Indeed `1_Q=(1+chi)/2` on `F_p^*`, and `chi(q-1)=chi(1-q)` because `chi(-1)=1`. Thus (5.5) is a completely explicit parity-block matrix built from products of Jacobi sums.

### Outcome of method #1

This route succeeds in changing the category: the non-circulant localized graph becomes an exact split-conic quotient, and its lifted kernel becomes a parity-block Jacobi operator on a cyclic torus. What it does **not** yet provide is the required sharp operator estimate. The bounded literature search found split-torus Hecke algebras and strong bilinear Kloosterman estimates, but I did not establish an identity placing (5.5) inside a known Hecke algebra or an estimate with the fixed constant required by `2/3`.

That missing identification is now precise rather than rhetorical.

# 6. Move #19: the clipping iteration is exactly alternating projection between the SDP affine space and the spectral cone

The previous run wrote the free-entry mask identity. The key new observation is that the natural clipping update is not an ad hoc algorithm at all: it is **alternating Frobenius projection onto the two convex feasibility sets defining the theta dual**.

Let `G=G_{p,2}` have adjacency matrix `A`. Define the fixed-entry mask

\[
M_0=I+A,
\]

so `M_0` is one on the diagonal and graph edges, and let

\[
N=J-M_0
\]

be the nonedge/free-entry mask.

Define the affine edge-fixed space

\[
\mathcal E
=\{B=B^T:B_{ii}=1,\ B_{ij}=1\text{ on every edge }ij\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

and, for a target `t`, the closed spectral cone

\[
\mathcal C_t=\{B=B^T:B\preceq tI\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By the inherited exact edge-fixed theta dual,

\[
\vartheta(\overline G)\le t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E\cap\mathcal C_t\ne\varnothing.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For `B in E`, let

\[
Z=(B-tI)_+,
\tag{6.4}
\]

the positive spectral part. Define

\[
B^+=B-N\circ Z.
\tag{6.5}
\]

## Theorem `P04-I0101-ALTERNATING-PROJECTION-12`

The update (6.5) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
B^+=P_{\mathcal E}P_{\mathcal C_t}(B)
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for Frobenius-metric projections. If `E cap C_t` is nonempty, then iteration of (6.5) from any `B_0 in E` converges in Frobenius norm to some matrix in `E cap C_t`.

### Proof

Diagonalize `B`. On each eigenvalue `lambda`, the nearest scalar not exceeding `t` is `min(lambda,t)`. Therefore

\[
P_{\mathcal C_t}(B)=B-(B-tI)_+=B-Z.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The Frobenius projection onto `E` leaves every free nonedge coordinate unchanged and resets every diagonal/edge coordinate to one. Since `B` already has those fixed coordinates,

\[
P_{\mathcal E}(B-Z)=B-N\circ Z,
\]

which is (6.6).

Now suppose `B_* in E cap C_t`. Write

\[
Y_k=P_{\mathcal C_t}(B_k)=B_k-Z_k,
\qquad
B_{k+1}=P_{\mathcal E}(Y_k).
\]

Metric projection onto a closed convex set obeys the projection inequality, so

\[
\|Y_k-B_*\|_F^2
\le
\|B_k-B_*\|_F^2-\|Z_k\|_F^2.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Projection onto the affine space `E` is orthogonal. Hence

\[
\|B_{k+1}-B_*\|_F^2
=
\|Y_k-B_*\|_F^2
-\|Y_k-B_{k+1}\|_F^2.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Combining,

\[
\boxed{
\|B_{k+1}-B_*\|_F^2
\le
\|B_k-B_*\|_F^2
-\|Z_k\|_F^2
-\|M_0\circ Z_k\|_F^2.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Thus the sequence is Fejer monotone with respect to the nonempty intersection, bounded, and `||Z_k||_F -> 0`. Any cluster point lies in `E` because all `B_k` do, and its distance to `C_t` is zero because `||Z_k||_F` is exactly that distance. Hence every cluster point lies in the intersection. If `B_bar` is one such cluster point, Fejer monotonicity with respect to `B_bar` makes `||B_k-B_bar||_F` nonincreasing; a subsequence tends to zero, so the whole sequence tends to `B_bar`. QED.

This theorem does **not** prove the intersection is nonempty; that would be Conjecture 27 at `t=(2/3)sqrt(p)`. What it does is turn the free-entry search into a canonical deterministic feasibility algorithm with an exact Lyapunov inequality.

# 7. Refutation side of the same algorithm: a nonzero fixed point is a primal lower-bound certificate

The algorithm also has an exact opposite-side certificate, which is useful because the fleet rule says to keep trying to refute the source rather than only fitting upper-bound completions.

## Proposition `P04-I0101-CLIPPING-FIXEDPOINT-CERT-13`

Let `B in E`, `Z=(B-tI)_+`, and suppose

\[
Z\ne0,
\qquad
N\circ Z=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(\overline G)
\ge
 t+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Z^2)}{\operatorname{Tr}Z}
>t.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

Set

\[
X=\frac{Z}{\operatorname{Tr}Z}.
\]

Then `X >= 0`, `Tr X=1`, and `N circ X=0`. Thus `X` is feasible for the clique-normalized theta primal (3.5).

Because `B` equals one on every coordinate where `X` may be nonzero,

\[
\langle J,X\rangle=\langle B,X\rangle.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The matrices `B` and `Z=(B-tI)_+` commute. On an eigenvalue `lambda>t`, the corresponding eigenvalue of `Z` is `lambda-t`; elsewhere it is zero. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle B,X\rangle
&=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(BZ)}{\operatorname{Tr}Z}\\
&=t+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(Z^2)}{\operatorname{Tr}Z}
>t.
\end{aligned}
\]

The theta primal then gives (7.2). QED.

So an exact nonzero fixed point of alternating projection would not merely mean “the algorithm stalled”: it would be an exact **source-side obstruction at that t**. A finite prime obstruction still would not refute the eventual quantifier, but a uniform or infinite family of such fixed-point certificates would be a genuine refutation route.

# 8. The previous Schur-mask identity has a better sign in the alternating-projection formulation

The previous run proved, for every PSD `Z`, with

\[
P_A=I-\frac1pJ-\frac1{\sqrt p}C\succeq0
\]

the identity

\[
N\circ Z
=
\frac{1+1/\sqrt p}{2}Z
-\frac{1+\sqrt p}{2}\operatorname{diag}Z
+\frac{\sqrt p}{2}(P_A\circ Z).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Directly controlling (8.1) made the positive Schur term look like an obstacle. In the alternating-projection update, however, the relevant residual after subtracting the **full** spectral excess is the complementary fixed mask

\[
M_0\circ Z=Z-N\circ Z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
M_0\circ Z
=
\frac{1-1/\sqrt p}{2}Z
+\frac{1+\sqrt p}{2}\operatorname{diag}Z
-\frac{\sqrt p}{2}(P_A\circ Z).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Now the Schur term has the favorable negative sign. Since `P_A circ Z >= 0`, if `e=||Z||_op`, then

\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_0\circ Z)
\le
\frac{1-1/\sqrt p}{2}e
+\frac{1+\sqrt p}{2}\max_i Z_{ii}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Also, from `B-Z <= tI`,

\[
B^+
=B-Z+M_0\circ Z
\preceq tI+M_0\circ Z,
\]

so

\[
(\lambda_{\max}(B^+)-t)_+
\le\lambda_{\max}(M_0\circ Z).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Consequently, a sufficient one-step contraction condition is

\[
\max_i Z_{ii}
\le
\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\sqrt p}\,e,
\tag{8.5}
\]

because then

\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_{\max}(B^+)-t)_+
\le
\left[1-\frac\varepsilon2\left(1+\frac1{\sqrt p}\right)\right]e.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

For rank-one excess `Z=e uu^T`, (8.5) asks only

\[
\|u\|_\infty^2\le(1-\varepsilon)p^{-1/2},
\]

i.e. amplitude scale `p^{-1/4}`, much weaker than perfect `p^{-1/2}` flatness.

I do **not** claim (8.5) holds for the full excess along the required iteration; in general the positive excess may have growing rank. The source-level analytic task is therefore sharper than before:

> either exploit the favorable Schur term in (8.2) beyond the crude deletion used in (8.3), or build an explicit approximate edge-fixed dual for which the remaining spectral excess satisfies a verifiable delocalization inequality of type (8.5).

This is the main mathematical payoff of method #19.
