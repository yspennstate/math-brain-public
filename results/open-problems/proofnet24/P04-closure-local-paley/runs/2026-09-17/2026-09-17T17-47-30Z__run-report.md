# 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: a source-scale positive-definite precision homotopy

Write `s=sqrt(p)`. Let `K` be the localized Paley sign matrix indexed by the vertices of `H`:

\[
K_{ii}=0,
\qquad
K_{ij}=\chi(i-j)\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Then

\[
A=\frac12(J-I+K).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The full Paley character matrix on `F_p` satisfies the conference identity `C_p^2=pI-J`, hence has operator norm `sqrt(p)`. Since `K` is a principal compression,

\[
\boxed{\|K\|_{op}\le\sqrt p=s.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

A previously proved exact degree formula gives, for every localized vertex `x`,

\[
d_x=\frac{p-15+S_x}{8},
\qquad
S_x=\sum_y\chi(y(y-1)(y-x)),
\]

and Hasse/Weil gives `|S_x|<=2 sqrt(p)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
d_x\ge d_{\min}:=\frac{s^2-2s-15}{8}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The identity and its proof are already on the P04 page; I use them here as an explicit proof dependency rather than re-promoting them.

## Theorem `P04-I0101-SCORE-COMPATIBLE-PRECISION-HOMOTOPY-29`

**Status: PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL / PUBLICATION-NOVELTY-UNVERIFIED.**

For every admissible prime `p>=3041`, every

\[
0\le z\le z_*(p)=\frac3{2\sqrt p-3},
\qquad
0\le\lambda\le2,
\]

the sparse matrix

\[
\boxed{
P_{\lambda,p}(z)=I+\lambda zA+\lambda z^2D
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

is positive definite. It obeys the maxdet local score equation identically:

\[
\boxed{
(P_{\lambda,p})_{ii}
-z\sum_{j\sim i}(P_{\lambda,p})_{ij}=1.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

At the source endpoint, uniformly in `0<=lambda<=2`,

\[
\lambda_{\min}(P_{\lambda,p}(z_*))
\ge
1-\lambda\frac{3(5s^2+2s+33)}{8(2s-3)^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, at the worst allowed `lambda=2`,

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}(P_{2,p}(z_*))
\ge
\frac{s^2-54s-63}{4(2s-3)^2}>0
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

for `s>27+6 sqrt(22)`, hence for every prime `p>=3041`. The right-hand side tends to `1/16` as `p->infinity`.

### Proof

The support and score identity are immediate. On every edge, `(P_lambda)_{ij}=lambda z`, while the diagonal entry is `1+lambda z^2 d_i`; hence

\[
(P_\lambda)_{ii}-z\sum_{j\sim i}(P_\lambda)_{ij}
=1+\lambda z^2d_i-z(\lambda z d_i)=1.
\]

For a vector `v`, use (4.1), `J positive semidefinite`, (4.2), and (4.3):

\[
\begin{aligned}
v^TP_{\lambda,p}(z)v
&=\|v\|^2+\frac{\lambda z}{2}v^T(J-I+K)v
+\lambda z^2 v^TDv\\
&\ge
\left[
1-\frac{\lambda z}{2}(1+s)
+\lambda z^2d_{\min}
\right]\|v\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The bracket is decreasing on `0<=z<=z_*`: its stationary point is

\[
z_0=\frac{1+s}{4d_{\min}},
\]

and direct algebra gives `z_*<z_0` whenever the expressions are defined, since

\[
2(1+s)(2s-3)-3(s^2-2s-15)=s^2+4s+39>0.
\]

Thus the endpoint is worst. Substituting `z_*=3/(2s-3)` and (4.3) into (4.8) gives

\[
1-\lambda\frac{3(5s^2+2s+33)}{8(2s-3)^2},
\]

which is decreasing in `lambda`, so `lambda=2` is worst. At `lambda=2` the expression factors exactly as (4.7). Finally

\[
s^2-54s-63>0
\iff
s>27+6\sqrt{22},
\]

and `(27+6 sqrt(22))^2<3041`. This proves the theorem. QED.

### What the theorem does and does not say

This is a useful failure certificate for an obvious obstruction. At source scale, a very simple edge-supported precision family can satisfy the exact local score equations and remain uniformly positive definite all the way to `z_*`; in fact the displayed lower bound still has asymptotic margin `1/16` at `lambda=2`.

Therefore the source problem is **not** blocked merely because a score-compatible sparse precision matrix becomes singular before the target. What remains is the genuinely nonlinear requirement that its inverse have **every prescribed edge correlation exactly `-z`**.

No checker was written: (4.4)--(4.7) are symbolic statements over all sufficiently large admissible primes, and a sampled numerical certificate would add no proof value.

## 4.1 Edge-only residual equations

The preceding theorem suggests removing the diagonal equations from the nonlinear solve completely.

Let `w=(w_e)` be arbitrary edge weights, let `W(w)` be the symmetric matrix supported on edges with those off-diagonal weights, and set

\[
\boxed{
P_z(w)=I+W(w)+z\,\operatorname{Diag}(W(w)\mathbf1).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Then (3.2) holds identically. Assume `P_z(w)>0` and write `C=P_z(w)^{-1}`. Define residuals

\[
a_i=C_{ii}-1,
\qquad
e_{ij}=C_{ij}+z\quad(ij\in E).
\]

If `s_i=sum_{j~i}w_{ij}`, the `(i,i)` entry of `PC=I` gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
(1+zs_i)a_i+\sum_{j\sim i}w_{ij}e_{ij}=0.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

Because `P_{ii}=1+zs_i>0`, vanishing of all edge residuals forces every diagonal residual to vanish. Hence the full maximum-determinant completion equations can be solved by the **edge equations alone**:

\[
\boxed{(P_z(w)^{-1})_{ij}=-z\quad(ij\in E).}
\tag{4.11}
\]

The exact maxdet precision belongs to this parameterization by the already-proved score equation.

The Jacobian is explicit. For an edge perturbation `h`, with `W_h=W(h)`,

\[
D_w e[h]
=-\Pi_E\left(
C\{W_h+z\operatorname{Diag}(W_h\mathbf1)\}C
\right).
\tag{4.12}
\]

At `(z,w)=(0,0)`, this is `-I` on the edge coordinates. Thus the true branch is locally unique and analytic by the implicit-function theorem. This is the exact continuation algorithm B6 wanted; Theorem 29 identifies a large positive-definite ambient region in which one can try to control it.

\[
B=J-A_G,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `A_G` is supported on the edges of `G`, i.e. on the nonedges of `H`. Since the fixed entries of `B` are `1` on the diagonal and on `E(H)`, this is exactly a weighted adjacency of the complement. The strict negativity observed above means

\[
(A_G)_{ij}=1-B_{ij}>1

on every complement edge in those four certificates. So the successful large finite certificates already lie in the **strictly positive weighted-adjacency cone of the complement**; arbitrary signs were not needed.

This is a much smaller derandomization target than reproducing the orbit numerators.

## 5.1 Exact resolvent form

Let `A_G` be any real weighted adjacency of `G` and let `t>0`. Put

\[
M=tI+A_G.
\]

Then

\[
tI-B=tI-J+A_G=M-J.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `M-J>0`, then `M=(M-J)+J>0`. By the rank-one Schur complement / matrix determinant lemma,

\[
\boxed{
M-J>0
\iff
\mathbf1^TM^{-1}\mathbf1<1.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equivalently, for the weighted walk-generating function

\[
W_{A_G}(x)=\mathbf1^T(I-xA_G)^{-1}\mathbf1,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
B\prec tI
\iff
W_{A_G}(-1/t)<t.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The non-strict endpoint follows by closure when the inverse side is well-defined. This is a direct specialization of the weighted-walk characterization of Lovasz theta in Wolff (arXiv:2501.15277) together with the standard weighted-adjacency theta dual, so I make **no novelty claim** for (5.3)--(5.4).

What is new for the P04 route is the exact sign evidence: the current large finite certificates show that a **positive** `A_G` is compatible with beating the source threshold. This opens Perron--Frobenius, weighted Hoffman, matrix-balancing and positive-kernel tools that were unavailable in an arbitrary signed-weight search.

The asymptotic source target can therefore be attacked by constructing, for every sufficiently large admissible `p`, a positive `S_3`-invariant weighted adjacency `A_p` of `overline{G_{p,2}}` such that at

\[
t_p=\frac23\sqrt p,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf1^T(t_pI+A_p)^{-1}\mathbf1\le1.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

or, equivalently, `W_{A_p}(-1/t_p)<=t_p`.

This is a concrete derandomization target. The uniform weighting is essentially the old scalar/raw-sign route and is not enough; the finite evidence says the needed improvement can nevertheless remain inside the positive cone while being highly nonuniform.

# 6. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: positivity is not the issue; source-scale inverse matching is

The edge-only parameterization makes the first Taylor coefficients of the true maxdet precision explicit. For a fixed finite graph, write

\[
P_z=I+zA+z^2P_2+O(z^3).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
C_z=P_z^{-1}
=I-zA+z^2(A^2-P_2)+O(z^3).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The diagonal target `C_{ii}=1` forces

\[
\boxed{(P_2)_{ii}=d_i,}
\tag{6.3}
\]

and the edge target `C_{ij}=-z` forces

\[
\boxed{(P_2)_{ij}=(A^2)_{ij}\quad(ij\in E).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus, in edge-weight coordinates,

\[
\boxed{
w_{ij}(z)=z+(A^2)_{ij}z^2+O(z^3).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The important failure is already visible on the diagonal and requires no unproved codegree estimate. By (4.3), at the source scale

\[
z_*^2d_i
\ge
\frac{9(s^2-2s-15)}{8(2s-3)^2}
\longrightarrow\frac9{32}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

So the nominally 'second-order' term is **order one** at the target. Fixed-order perturbation about `z=0` is therefore nonuniform at exactly the scale Conjecture 27 asks about. This is the precise reason the straightforward continuation algorithm cannot be justified by a short Taylor truncation.

Theorem 29 simultaneously shows what is *not* going wrong: the score-compatible sparse precision skeleton remains uniformly positive definite at that same source scale. The bottleneck is the global resummation required to make **all edge entries of the inverse** equal `-z` simultaneously.

The positive-complement resolvent form says the same thing from the dual side. Uniform positive weights are too rigid, while the successful finite certificates use nonuniform positive weights. The remaining theorem has to explain that nonuniformity arithmetically; merely preserving positivity or row balance is insufficient.

**B20 outcome:** the exact obstruction is now localized to source-scale edgewise inverse matching / resolvent resummation, not to loss of PD, not to stabilizer exceptions, and not to a need for signed complement weights.
