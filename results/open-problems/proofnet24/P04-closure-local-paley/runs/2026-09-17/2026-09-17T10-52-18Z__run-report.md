## 3. Structural theorem: the unique harmonic size-three orbit

### Claim `P04-I0101-HARMONIC-ORBIT-19 = PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL`

Let `p` be a prime with `p = 17 (mod 24)`, and let
\[
V_2=\{x\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}
\]
be the two-localized Paley vertex set. Let the anharmonic group
\[
S_3=\langle r,s\rangle,\qquad r(x)=1-x,\quad s(x)=x^{-1}
\]
act on `V_2`. Then:

1. the unique non-free vertex orbit is
\[
H=\{-1,2,1/2\},
\]
and it has size three;
2. every other vertex orbit has size six;
3. `H` is an independent set in `G_{p,2}`.

### Proof

A nontrivial stabilizer in the anharmonic `S_3` is generated either by a transposition or by a 3-cycle.

The three transpositions have, after excluding the forbidden anchor points `0,1`, the fixed points
\[
1/2,\qquad -1,\qquad 2,
\]
which form one `S_3` orbit `H`. Since `p = 17 (mod 24)` implies `p = 1 (mod 8)`, quadratic reciprocity gives
\[
\chi(2)=1.
\]
Also `\chi(-1)=1` because `p=1 (mod 4)`. Hence all three points lie in `V_2`: for example `2` has `\chi(2)=\chi(1)=1`, `-1` has `\chi(-1)=\chi(-2)=1`, and `1/2` has `\chi(1/2)=\chi(-1/2)=1`.

A fixed point of a 3-cycle satisfies
\[
x^2-x+1=0.
\]
Its roots exist over `\mathbb F_p` exactly when the primitive sixth roots lie in the field, equivalently here when `p = 1 (mod 3)`. But `p = 17 (mod 24)` gives `p = 2 (mod 3)`, so there are no 3-cycle fixed points. Thus `H` is the only non-free orbit and all remaining orbits have size six.

Finally the three pairwise differences in `H`, up to factors whose quadratic character is `+1`, are
\[
3,\qquad 3/2,\qquad -3/2.
\]
For `p=17 (mod 24)` one has `p=5 (mod 12)`, so the standard quadratic-reciprocity formula gives
\[
\left(\frac3p\right)=-1.
\]
Since `\chi(-1)=\chi(2)=1`, all three differences are nonresidues. Therefore no two vertices of `H` are adjacent. This proves the claim. □

This is an infinite-family structural theorem, but it is not the source theta bound.
