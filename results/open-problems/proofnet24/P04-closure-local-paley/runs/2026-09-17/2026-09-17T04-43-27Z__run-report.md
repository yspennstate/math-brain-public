## 4. B14 — classify the unordered-pair stabilizers, not just the vertices

The previous P04 work used the anharmonic action

\[
S_3=\langle r,s:r^2=s^2=(rs)^3=1\rangle,
\qquad r(x)=1-x,\quad s(x)=x^{-1},
\]

on `V_2`. In the free part of the action, each vertex orbit is a regular `S_3`-set.

Fix two regular vertex orbits `O_i,O_j` and representatives `x_i,x_j`.

### Proposition 14.1 — pair-orbit stabilizers

1. If `i != j`, every unordered pair `{g x_i,h x_j}` has trivial stabilizer under the diagonal `S_3` action; hence its orbit has size six.
2. Inside one regular orbit `O_i`, the unordered pair `{x_i,a x_i}` has stabilizer of order two iff `a` is a transposition. In that case its unordered-pair orbit has size three. If `a` is a 3-cycle, the unordered-pair orbit has size six.
3. Consequently each regular six-vertex orbit has exactly three intrinsic size-three unordered-pair orbits, one for each transposition of `S_3`.

### Proof

For (1), the diagonal action preserves each vertex orbit. If `u` stabilizes an unordered pair meeting two distinct vertex orbits, `u` cannot swap the two vertices because they lie in different invariant orbits. It therefore fixes each vertex. Freeness of both vertex actions forces `u=e`, so the unordered-pair orbit has size `|S_3|=6`.

For (2), use the regular labeling and consider `{x,a x}` with `a != e`. If `u` stabilizes this unordered pair, freeness leaves two possibilities. Either `u=e`, or `u x=a x` and `u a x=x`. The second alternative forces `u=a` and `a^2=e`. Thus a nontrivial stabilizer exists exactly for a transposition; it then has order two and orbit size three. A 3-cycle has no such stabilizer and gives orbit size six. Distinct transpositions give distinct perfect-matching pair-orbits. QED.

This explains structurally why size-three nonedge pair-orbits occurred at `p=4013` even though the **vertex** action there was completely free. Pair-orbit stabilizers are a different layer of symmetry from vertex-orbit stabilizers.

At `p=4021`, the exact checker below finds 167 regular size-six vertex orbits plus one exceptional size-two vertex orbit. The nonedge unordered-pair census contains sizes 6, 3, and 1. Thus the next finite frontier is useful because it tests the completion mechanism in the presence of an exceptional vertex orbit as well as regular-pair stabilizers.

## 5. B11 — a sharp local Fourier leakage law

Consider one invariant off-diagonal block between two regular `S_3` vertex orbits. Such a block is a function

\[
b:S_3\to\mathbb R,
\]

where `b(g)` is the block entry at relative group element `g`. Let `E \subseteq S_3` be the set of relative positions that are graph edges and put `k=|E|`. Edge-fixed theta dual feasibility requires

\[
b(g)=1\qquad(g\in E).
\]

Let

\[
a=\sum_{g\in S_3}b(g)
\]

be the trivial Fourier coefficient. With unnormalized Fourier transforms, write

\[
\widehat b_{\rm sgn}=\sum_g b(g)\operatorname{sgn}(g),
\qquad
\widehat b_{\rm std}=\sum_g b(g)\rho_{\rm std}(g),
\]

for any orthogonal two-dimensional standard representation.

### Claim `P04-I0101-S3-BLOCK-LEAKAGE-14` — PROVED / structural

For `0<k<6`, every real edge-fixed block satisfies the sharp inequality

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
\ge
\frac{k(6-a)^2}{6-k}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equality holds iff all `6-k` free nonedge values are equal to

\[
\frac{a-k}{6-k}.
\]

### Proof

Finite-group Plancherel for the three irreducibles of `S_3` gives

\[
a^2+|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2
+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
=6\sum_{g\in S_3}b(g)^2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The `k` fixed edge entries contribute `k` to the sum of squares. The free entries have total sum `a-k`, so Cauchy-Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_g b(g)^2
\ge
k+\frac{(a-k)^2}{6-k},
\tag{5.3}
\]

with equality exactly when all free entries are equal. Substituting (5.3) into (5.2),

\[
|\widehat b_{\rm sgn}|^2+2\|\widehat b_{\rm std}\|_F^2
\ge
6k+\frac{6(a-k)^2}{6-k}-a^2
=\frac{k(6-a)^2}{6-k}.
\]

QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — pure trivial correction is impossible on a partially edged block

Let `c:S_3->R` be an edge-fixed **correction**, so `c(g)=0` on at least one edge position. If both nontrivial Fourier transforms vanish,

\[
\widehat c_{\rm sgn}=0,
\qquad
\widehat c_{\rm std}=0,
\]

then Fourier inversion says `c` is constant on `S_3`. Since it vanishes on an edge, it is identically zero.

Therefore an invariant completion cannot cancel the growing trivial quotient sector without paying some sign/standard leakage on every partially edged regular block. Only a completely nonedge block (`k=0`) can change its trivial Fourier coefficient with zero nontrivial leakage.

This is the quantitative version of the obstruction that was previously only qualitative. In the clean free-`S_3` subfamily, the prior P04 theorem placed the raw sign and standard sectors below the target at approximately the `1/2 sqrt(p)` scale, leaving about `1/6 sqrt(p)` of spectral room. Equation (5.1) identifies what any trivial-sector cancellation must spend from that room.

### Why this still does not close the source theorem

Equation (5.1) is a **blockwise Fourier-energy** lower bound. It does not by itself force a lower bound on the operator norm of the assembled sign or standard multiplicity matrices, because leakage from many orbit blocks can cancel coherently across the multiplicity space. A source proof now needs a global matrix-discrepancy/character-sum estimate controlling those cancellations, not another local inequality.

## 9. What changed conceptually

The finite metric moved again, but the more useful source-level change is the sharper description of what a uniform proof must do.

For regular `S_3` orbit blocks, cancelling the trivial quotient sector is not free. On every block containing at least one edge, any nonzero edge-fixed correction necessarily has sign or standard Fourier content. The exact lower bound (5.1) quantifies that cost. Hence an eventual proof at constant `2/3` must coordinate cancellations **globally** across orbit pairs so that the required trivial-sector correction does not create a sign/standard operator norm larger than the available slack.

A blockwise Frobenius argument cannot finish this because Fourier leakage from different orbit pairs may cancel in the multiplicity matrices. The missing theorem now has the shape of a correlated matrix discrepancy / high-order character-sum estimate over the orbit quotient.

The `p=4021` certificate is also structurally different from the clean `p≡5 (mod 24)` free-action frontier: it contains one exceptional size-two vertex orbit and still admits a source-cap certificate. That makes a proof based solely on eliminating exceptional vertex orbits less plausible.
