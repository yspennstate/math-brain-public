## 4. New exact theorem — S3 twisted-trace spectral filter

### Claim

\[
\boxed{\texttt{P04-I0101-S3-TWISTED-TRACE-FILTER-27 = PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL}.}
\]

Let G=S3 act orthogonally on a finite-dimensional real vector space H through matrices U_g, and let L=L^T be any real symmetric operator commuting with every U_g. For an irreducible real representation rho of S3, with dimension d_rho and character chi_rho, let H_rho be the rho-isotypic subspace. Since L lies in the commutant, there is a real symmetric multiplicity operator M_rho such that on H_rho

\[
L|_{H_\rho}\cong I_{d_\rho}\otimes M_\rho.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then for every polynomial f, and in fact for every function f defined by spectral functional calculus on L,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr} f(M_\rho)
=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi_\rho(g)\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(U_g f(L)\bigr).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, for every integer k>=0,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(M_\rho^k)
=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi_\rho(g)\operatorname{Tr}(U_gL^k).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the trivial representation this is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}((L^{(0)})^k)
=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\operatorname{Tr}(U_gL^k),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where L^(0) is the orbit-constant/trivial multiplicity block.

For any complex w outside the spectrum of L,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(wI-M_\rho)^{-1}
=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi_\rho(g)
\operatorname{Tr}\!\bigl(U_g(wI-L)^{-1}\bigr).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

The meromorphic function on either side has poles exactly at the eigenvalues of M_rho, with the correct multiplicities. Thus the character-weighted twisted-resolvent average is an exact sector-selective spectral filter: eigenvalue poles from other S3 sectors cancel identically.

### Proof

For every irreducible rho, the standard character projector is

\[
P_\rho=\frac{d_\rho}{6}\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi_\rho(g)U_g.
\tag{4.6}
\]

All S3 characters are real, so no complex conjugation is needed. Standard character orthogonality gives that P_rho is the orthogonal projection onto H_rho.

Because L commutes with every U_g, it commutes with P_rho. On the isotypic decomposition, Schur's lemma gives (4.1). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_\rho f(L))
=d_\rho\operatorname{tr}f(M_\rho).
\tag{4.7}
\]

On the other hand, substituting (4.6) and using linearity of trace gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_\rho f(L))
=\frac{d_\rho}{6}\sum_{g\in S_3}\chi_\rho(g)
\operatorname{Tr}(U_g f(L)).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Dividing by d_rho proves (4.2). Powers give (4.3). Taking `f(lambda)=(w-lambda)^{-1}` proves (4.5) whenever w is initially outside the spectrum; finite-dimensional spectral calculus then gives the meromorphic identity. Since the left side is the trace of the resolvent of M_rho, its poles are exactly the eigenvalues of M_rho, with algebraic/spectral multiplicity. This proves the filtering statement.

For rho=trivial, d=1 and chi(g)=1, so (4.4) is immediate. QED.

## 5. Free-action orbit basis: the same moment counted downstairs

Now impose B30 and restrict to p == 5 (mod 24), where the anharmonic S3 action on V=V(G_{p,2}) is free. Choose one representative x from each six-point orbit and define the normalized invariant vector

\[
e_{[x]}=\frac1{\sqrt6}\sum_{a\in S_3}\delta_{ax}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For any S3-equivariant symmetric L,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle e_{[x]},Le_{[y]}\rangle
&=\frac16\sum_{a,b\in S_3}L_{ax,by}\\
&=\sum_{h\in S_3}L_{x,hy}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The second equality uses equivariance: for each h=a^{-1}b there are exactly six pairs (a,b). Thus the trivial multiplicity block is exactly the six-channel orbit-sum matrix

\[
L^{(0)}_{[x],[y]}=\sum_{h\in S_3}L_{x,hy}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If one now expands `tr((L^(0))^k)` directly through (5.3), one sees 6^k channel words. Equation (4.4) proves that after the orbit sum is assembled, the same quantity is *exactly*

\[
\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\operatorname{Tr}(U_gL^k),
\tag{5.4}
\]

only six global terms. This is the B25 double count. The cancellation is performed by the group projector before any channelwise absolute values are taken.

The statement is stronger than a fixed-moment identity. It also applies to the unique maxdet completion C_z, its precision P_z=C_z^{-1}, the sparse tangent Z_z from theorem 24, and every polynomial/resolvent built from any one of those operators, because uniqueness plus graph symmetry makes the canonical object S3-equivariant. Therefore the same six-term filter is available for the source-relevant nonlinear completion path, not only for the raw adjacency matrix.

## 6. Exact level-4 specialization: six twisted character sums, not 6^k channel words

Let

\[
Q_p(x,y)=\chi(x-y)
\]

on the localized vertex set V, with Q_p(x,x)=0. Since +1/-1 records edge/nonedge and the anharmonic action is by graph automorphisms, Q_p commutes with S3.

Retain theorem 25's level-4 coordinate

\[
x(t)=\left(\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\right)^2,
\qquad
D_p=\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_p)\setminus\{0,\infty,t:t^4=1\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

where x:D_p->V is exactly four-to-one on p == 5 (mod 24). Put

\[
q(t,s)
=\chi\bigl(x(t)-x(s)\bigr)
=\chi\bigl((t-s)(t+s)(ts-1)(ts+1)\bigr).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The denominator and the factor -4 in theorem 25 are squares in this congruence class, so (6.2) is exact, with zero automatically produced on a common x-fiber.

For g in S3 choose one level-4 lift gamma_g satisfying

\[
x(\gamma_g t)=g x(t).
\tag{6.3}
\]

With the convention `U_g delta_x = delta_{gx}`, expansion of a twisted trace gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(U_gQ_p^k)
=\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1}\in V}
Q_p(x_0,x_1)\cdots Q_p(x_{k-2},x_{k-1})Q_p(x_{k-1},g x_0).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Every k-tuple downstairs has exactly 4^k lifts upstairs. Substituting (6.2)-(6.3) therefore gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(U_gQ_p^k)
=4^{-k}\!\sum_{t_0,\ldots,t_{k-1}\in D_p}
\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}q(t_j,t_{j+1})\right)
q(t_{k-1},\gamma_g t_0).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Combining (4.4) and (6.5),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}((Q_p^{(0)})^k)
=\frac1{6\cdot4^k}\sum_{g\in S_3}
\sum_{t_0,\ldots,t_{k-1}\in D_p}
\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}q(t_j,t_{j+1})\right)
q(t_{k-1},\gamma_g t_0).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the desired B30 one-family/one-sector formula. The complete k-step trivial-sector moment is a sum of **six** k-variable bounded-degree Kummer trace expressions, indexed only by the closing S3 twist. The feared 6^k channel family is not intrinsic; it was an artifact of expanding each orbit block before using the group projector.

Exactly the same construction with the sign or standard character in (4.3) yields the other two S3 sectors from these same six twisted traces.

## 7. What B3 buys: a selective resolvent route rather than an absolute-norm route

A naive use of even moments would prove the stronger sufficient condition

\[
\|L^{(0)}\|_{op}
\le \bigl(\operatorname{Tr}L^{2m}\bigr)^{1/(2m)},
\tag{7.1}
\]

because the trivial projection commutes with L. But this pays for the absolute spectral edge of the full operator, including signs and nontrivial S3 sectors that theta need not pay for. Earlier P04 work already warned that absolute-norm estimates can be too blunt.

Equation (4.5) is better. It says that the trivial-sector resolvent itself is the character/Reynolds average of six full-space twisted resolvents. In a spectral decomposition, poles from sign and standard sectors disappear from that average exactly. Thus a proof can aim directly at the source-relevant invariant-sector pole without proving a stronger bound on every sector.

For the maxdet path this suggests the concrete target

\[
R_0(w,z)
:=\operatorname{tr}(wI-M_0(z))^{-1}
=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(U_g(wI-L_z)^{-1}\bigr),
\tag{7.2}
\]

where L_z is whichever source-relevant canonical equivariant tangent/resolvent operator is being controlled and M_0(z) is its trivial multiplicity block. A source-scale theorem would follow from showing that the relevant `R_0` has no pole past the required edge throughout `0<=z<=z_*(p)`, with

\[
z_*(p)=\frac3{2\sqrt p-3}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

This is not yet proved. What theorem 27 does prove is that exponential S3 channel proliferation is **not** the reason it is hard. The remaining difficulty is arithmetic/dynamical control of only six twisted traces as the maxdet path evolves.

## 8. Why this does not close the clean p == 5 (mod 24) subfamily

The new identity eliminates one major combinatorial obstruction, but it does not itself estimate the six twisted traces at logarithmically growing moment order or control the maxdet resolvent at z about p^{-1/2}.

For the raw sign kernel Q_p, (6.6) places each fixed k in the usual fixed-complexity character-sum world. But the edge problem requires either k growing at least on the logarithmic scale or a direct resolvent estimate, and the conductor/complexity of a k-variable product grows with k. A termwise Weil/Deligne bound followed by an absolute-value sum is therefore not the desired argument.

For the source-relevant maxdet operators, there is an additional issue: their entries are generated globally by inversion/completion and are not themselves a single fixed-degree rational trace function. The level-4 formulas give the exact arithmetic data at the graph/kernel level, while theorem 24 gives the exact maxdet ODE; what is still absent is the bridge proving that the six twisted traces remain source-scale controlled under that nonlinear evolution.

So the simplest special case
