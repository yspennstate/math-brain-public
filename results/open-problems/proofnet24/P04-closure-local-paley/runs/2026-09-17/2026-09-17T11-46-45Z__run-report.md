# 3. Move #13 — a genuine monotone quantity on the max-determinant theta central path

The previous max-determinant reformulation gave a canonical positive-definite completion for every cap strictly above theta. This run asks for a quantity that can only move in one direction as the edge correlation is driven toward the critical source scale.

Let `H=(V,E)` be any finite simple graph, `n=|V|`, and put

\[
\tau(H):=\vartheta(\overline H).
\]

Assume `tau(H)>1`; the degenerate one-vertex/edgeless cases are irrelevant for the Paley application. For

\[
0\le z<z_c(H):=\frac1{\tau(H)-1},
\]

define the maximum-entropy / maximum-determinant completion value

\[
\Phi_H(z)
:=
\max\left\{
\log\det C:
C\succ0,\ C_{ii}=1,\ C_{ij}=-z\ (ij\in E)
\right\},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where distinct nonedge entries are free.

The equivalence with the edge-fixed theta dual is exact: if `t>tau(H)`, set

\[
z=\frac1{t-1}.
\]

A matrix in the strict theta completion has diagonal `t-1` and edge entries `-1`; dividing by `t-1` gives exactly (3.1). Therefore the critical correlation is `z_c=1/(tau-1)`.

For each interior `z`, strict concavity of `log det` gives a unique maximizer `C_z`. Write

\[
P_z=C_z^{-1}.
\]

The first-order equations in the free nonedge entries give the classical covariance-selection identity

\[
\boxed{(P_z)_{ij}=0\qquad(i\ne j,\ ij\notin E).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The restricted Hessian of `-log det` is positive definite, so the free entries of `C_z` depend smoothly on `z` throughout the strict-feasibility interval.

## Theorem `P04-I0101-MAXDET-DISSIPATION-MONOVARIANT-21`

For every finite graph `H` and `0<z<z_c(H)`, define

\[
\mathcal M_H(z):=-\Phi_H'(z).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_H(z)
=2\sum_{ij\in E}(P_z)_{ij}
=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}P_z-n}{z}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
H_z:=\frac{dC_z}{dz},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_H'(z)
=
\left\|P_z^{1/2}H_zP_z^{1/2}\right\|_F^2
\ge0.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In fact `H_z` is the unique minimizer of the weighted tangent energy

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_H'(z)
=
\min_{Y}
\operatorname{Tr}(P_zYP_zY),
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

where the minimum is over symmetric matrices satisfying

\[
Y_{ii}=0,
\qquad
Y_{ij}=-1\quad(ij\in E),
\tag{3.7}
\]

with the nonedge entries free.

At the origin the continuous extension satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_H(0)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal M_H'(0)=2|E|,
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

and therefore

\[
\mathcal M_H(z)=2|E|z+O(z^2),
\qquad
\Phi_H(z)=-\int_0^z\mathcal M_H(s)\,ds.
\tag{3.9}
\]

### Proof

First, `Phi_H` is concave in `z`. If `C_1,C_2` are feasible at `z_1,z_2`, then

\[
\lambda C_1+(1-\lambda)C_2
\]

is feasible at `lambda z_1+(1-lambda)z_2`, and concavity of `log det` gives the corresponding value inequality. Thus `-Phi_H'`, wherever differentiated, is nondecreasing. The calculation below gives the exact derivative and shows smoothness directly.

By the envelope theorem, only the explicitly constrained edge entries contribute to the first derivative because the free-entry derivatives pair with the first-order equations (3.2). Since both symmetric positions of every edge equal `-z`,

\[
\Phi_H'(z)
=
-2\sum_{ij\in E}(P_z)_{ij}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Now take the `i,i` entry of `P_zC_z=I`. The nonedge terms vanish by (3.2), so

\[
1
=(P_z)_{ii}
-z\sum_{j\sim i}(P_z)_{ij}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Summing over `i` gives

\[
n
=\operatorname{Tr}P_z
-2z\sum_{ij\in E}(P_z)_{ij}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Combining (3.10) and (3.12) proves (3.4).

For the dissipation identity, differentiate

\[
P_z=C_z^{-1}.
\]

Then

\[
P_z'=-P_zH_zP_z.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Differentiating `Phi'=Tr(P_zH_z)` gives

\[
\Phi_H''(z)
=
-\operatorname{Tr}(P_zH_zP_zH_z)
+\operatorname{Tr}(P_zC_z'').
\tag{3.14}
\]

The diagonal of `C_z` is constant and its edge entries are affine in `z`, so `C_z''` is supported only on distinct nonedges. By (3.2),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_zC_z'')=0.
\]

Hence

\[
-\Phi_H''(z)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(P_zH_zP_zH_z)
=
\|P_z^{1/2}H_zP_z^{1/2}\|_F^2,
\]

which proves (3.5).

It remains to prove the variational form (3.6). Differentiate the nonedge stationarity equation `(P_z)_{ij}=0`. Equation (3.13) gives

\[
(P_zH_zP_z)_{ij}=0
\qquad(i\ne j,\ ij\notin E).
\tag{3.15}
\]

Consider the strictly convex quadratic form

\[
Q_z(Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(P_zYP_zY)
\]

on the affine tangent class (3.7). An allowed variation `Z` is symmetric and supported only on distinct nonedges. Its directional derivative at `H_z` is

\[
2\operatorname{Tr}(P_zH_zP_zZ),
\]

which vanishes for every such `Z` by (3.15). Since `P_z\succ0`, `Q_z` is strictly positive on every nonzero symmetric direction, so this stationary point is the unique minimizer. This proves (3.6).

Finally, at `z=0` the unique maximum-determinant correlation completion is `C_0=I`. Thus `P_0=I`. The tangent minimization becomes ordinary Frobenius-norm minimization with edge entries fixed to `-1`, so the unique minimizer is

\[
H_0=-A_H,
\]

with all free nonedges equal to zero. Therefore

\[
\mathcal M_H'(0)=\|A_H\|_F^2=2|E|,
\]

while `Phi_H'(0)=Tr(H_0)=0`, giving `M_H(0)=0`. Since `Phi_H(0)=0`, integration yields (3.9). QED.

## Theorem `P04-I0101-EXCEPTIONAL-ORBIT-THETA-STABILITY-22`

For every finite graph `H` and every partition

\[
V(H)=S\sqcup T,
\]

put `tau(H)=theta(complement H)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\max\{\tau(H[S]),\tau(H[T])\}
\le \tau(H)
\le \tau(H[S])+\tau(H[T]).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, for the two-localized Paley graph,

\[
\boxed{
\tau(G_{p,2}[R_p])
\le
\tau(G_{p,2})
\le
\tau(G_{p,2}[R_p])+k_p
\le
\tau(G_{p,2}[R_p])+5.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{p\to\infty\atop p\equiv1(4)}
\frac{\tau(G_{p,2})}{\sqrt p}
=
\limsup_{p\to\infty\atop p\equiv1(4)}
\frac{\tau(G_{p,2}[R_p])}{\sqrt p}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The same statement holds with `liminf` whenever either side is taken along the same admissible prime sequence.

### Proof of (4.2)

Use the clique-normalized theta primal

\[
\tau(H)
=
\max\left\{
\langle J,X\rangle:
X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{Tr}X=1,
\ X_{ij}=0\text{ for distinct nonedges }ij\text{ of }H
\right\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The lower bound in (4.2) follows by embedding any feasible matrix for an induced subgraph as one diagonal block and setting all remaining rows and columns to zero.

For the upper bound, let `X` be feasible for `H` and write it in `S,T` blocks. Put

\[
a=\operatorname{Tr}X_{SS},
\qquad
b=\operatorname{Tr}X_{TT}=1-a,
\]

and

\[
u=\mathbf1_S^T X_{SS}\mathbf1_S,
\qquad
v=\mathbf1_T^T X_{TT}\mathbf1_T,
\qquad
c=\mathbf1_S^T X_{ST}\mathbf1_T.
\]

If `a>0`, then `X_SS/a` is feasible for `H[S]`, so

\[
u\le a\,\tau(H[S]).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The inequality is trivial if `a=0`; similarly

\[
v\le b\,\tau(H[T]).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Because `X\succeq0`, Cauchy--Schwarz in the Gram representation gives

\[
|c|\le\sqrt{uv}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle J,X\rangle
&=u+v+2c\\
&\le(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2\\
&\le
\left(
\sqrt{a\tau(H[S])}
+
\sqrt{b\tau(H[T])}
\right)^2\\
&\le \tau(H[S])+\tau(H[T]),
\end{aligned}
\]

where the last line is ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz applied to `(sqrt a,sqrt b)` and `(sqrt tau_S,sqrt tau_T)`. Taking the maximum over feasible `X` proves (4.2).

For (4.3), take `S=R_p`, `T=E_p`. Since every graph on `k_p` vertices has clique-normalized theta at most `k_p`,

\[
\tau(G_{p,2}[E_p])\le k_p\le5.
\]

This proves (4.3). Dividing by `sqrt p` and taking limits proves (4.4). QED.

   \[

This theorem explains why the successful finite `p=4049` certificate surviving a three-point harmonic orbit was structurally plausible: finitely many exceptional vertices are not where the source-scale constant can be generated. It does **not** turn that finite certificate into an asymptotic theorem.
