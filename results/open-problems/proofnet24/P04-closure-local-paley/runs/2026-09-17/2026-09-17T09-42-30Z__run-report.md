### Claim P04-I0101-SPLIT-KERNEL-FOURIER-SELFDUAL-18

For every prime `p = 1 mod 4`, let `F` be the normalized additive Fourier matrix

\[
F_{a,x}=p^{-1/2}\psi(-ax),\qquad a,x\in\mathbb F_p,
\]

for any fixed nontrivial additive character `psi`. Let

\[
L_{x,y}=\chi(x^2-y^2).
\]

Then:

1. **Exact Fourier self-duality:**
   \[
   \boxed{F L F=L.}
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
2. If `R` is reflection, `(Rf)(x)=f(-x)`, then `RL=L=LR`; consequently
   \[
   \boxed{FL=LF.}
   \tag{3.3}
   \]
3. `L` annihilates the odd subspace. On the even subspace, `F^2=I`, so the parent kernel splits into the `F=+1` and `F=-1` sectors.
4. Let `P_D` be the diagonal projector onto `D_p`. For every admissible `p>=13`,
   \[
   \boxed{P_DF\ne FP_D.}
   \tag{3.4}
   \]
   Hence the exact Fourier/metaplectic symmetry of the parent split kernel does **not** descend automatically to the compressed two-localized kernel `P_D L P_D`.

Status: **PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL.** This is a route theorem, not the source conjecture. No novelty claim is made for the Gauss-sum/Fourier mechanism itself.

### Proof

Because `p=1 mod 4`, `chi(-1)=1`. Let

\[
G=\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(u)\psi(u)
\]

be the quadratic Gauss sum. The standard one-line change of variables gives

\[
\sum_u\chi(u)\psi(cu)=\chi(c)G
\]

for `c!=0`, while the sum is zero for `c=0`; also

\[
G^2=\chi(-1)p=p.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Now compute an entry of `FLF`:

\[
(FLF)_{a,b}
=\frac1p\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb F_p}
\chi(x^2-y^2)\psi(-ax-by).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Make the invertible linear change

\[
u=x-y,\qquad v=x+y,
\]

so that

\[
x=\frac{u+v}{2},\qquad y=\frac{v-u}{2}.
\]

Then `x^2-y^2=uv` and

\[
-ax-by
=-\frac{a-b}{2}u-\frac{a+b}{2}v.
\]

Thus (3.6) factors into two one-dimensional Gauss sums:

\[
\begin{aligned}
(FLF)_{a,b}
&=\frac1p
\left(\sum_u\chi(u)\psi\left(-\frac{a-b}{2}u\right)\right)
\left(\sum_v\chi(v)\psi\left(-\frac{a+b}{2}v\right)\right)\\
&=\frac{G^2}{p}
\chi\left(-\frac{a-b}{2}\right)
\chi\left(-\frac{a+b}{2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The formula remains valid when either coefficient vanishes because both sides are then zero. Using (3.5), `chi(-1)=1`, and `chi(4)=1`, the last line equals

\[
\chi(a^2-b^2)=L_{a,b}.
\]

This proves (3.2).

Next, `L(-x,y)=L(x,y)=L(x,-y)`, so

\[
RL=L=LR.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The normalized finite-field Fourier transform satisfies `F^2=R`. Left-multiplying (3.2) by `F` gives

\[
F^2LF=FL.
\]

By (3.8), the left side is `RLF=LF`, proving (3.3). If `Rf=-f`, then

\[
Lf=LRf=-Lf,
\]

so `Lf=0`: the odd subspace is killed. On the even subspace `R=I`, hence `F^2=I`; therefore only the `+1` and `-1` Fourier sectors remain. This symmetry alone cannot be a bounded-size symbol reduction: their direct sum has dimension `(p+1)/2`, so at least one has dimension at least `(p+1)/4`.

Finally, the split-lift domain `D_p` has size `(p-5)/2`: it is the finite Cayley image of the square torus with the two excluded anchor points. In particular, for `p>=13` the diagonal projector `P_D` is neither zero nor the identity. Every entry of `F` is nonzero. If `P_DF=FP_D`, then for every `a,x`

\[
(P_D)_{aa}F_{a,x}=F_{a,x}(P_D)_{xx}.
\]

Since `F_{a,x}\ne0`, this forces every diagonal entry of `P_D` to be equal, contradicting nontriviality of `D_p`. Therefore (3.4) holds. QED.

### Exact relation to the lifted graph

On the split domain `D_p`, reflection `z -> -z` exchanges the two clones over one original vertex. If `R_D` denotes that clone-exchange permutation, then the lifted adjacency matrix is

\[
A^\uparrow
=\frac12\left(J+P_DLP_D-I-R_D\right)
\quad\text{on }D_p.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Indeed the diagonal and clone-pair positions are killed by `I+R_D`; elsewhere `L=+1` is exactly an edge and `L=-1` exactly a nonedge. The earlier P04 split-conic theorem proved that this is the independent two-clone blowup of `G_{p,2}` and that the relevant theta value is unchanged by the blowup.

Thus (3.2) is an exact symmetry of the correct parent operator, not of an unrelated surrogate; (3.4) pinpoints precisely where the second localization destroys the symmetry.
