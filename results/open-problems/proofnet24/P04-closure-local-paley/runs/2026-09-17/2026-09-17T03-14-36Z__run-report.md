# 4. B6 — deform to the commutative point and identify the first obstruction

For two free orbits represented by `x_i,x_j`, define the six-entry adjacency kernel

\[
e_{ij}(g)=\mathbf 1_{\{x_i\sim g x_j\}},\qquad g\in S_3.
\]

Let

\[
\bar e_{ij}=\frac16\sum_{g\in S_3}e_{ij}(g).
\]

The exact interpolation

\[
e_{ij}^{(\tau)}(g)=\bar e_{ij}+\tau\bigl(e_{ij}(g)-\bar e_{ij}\bigr),
\qquad 0\le\tau\le1,
\tag{4.1}
\]

is a diagnostic deformation, not an adjacency matrix for intermediate `tau`. At `tau=0` every six-entry block is constant; only the trivial representation of `S_3` survives. At `tau=1` we recover the true localized adjacency. In group Fourier variables, the trivial coefficient is unchanged along the deformation and every nontrivial coefficient is multiplied by `tau`.

# 5. B30 — one direction at a time: the nontrivial `S_3` sectors are uniformly source-safe

## Theorem `P04-I0101-REGULAR-S3-NONTRIVIAL-SECTORS-12` — PROVED / PARTIAL

Let `p = 5 mod 24` be prime, let `A` be the adjacency matrix of `G_{p,2}`, and let

\[
B_0=I+A.
\]

Then for each nontrivial irreducible representation `rho` of `S_3` (the sign representation or the two-dimensional standard representation), the corresponding isotypic/multiplicity block satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\max}(A^{\rho})\le \frac{\sqrt p-1}{2}
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\max}(B_0^{\rho})\le \frac{\sqrt p+1}{2}
<\frac23\sqrt p
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

for every such prime `p>=29`.

Consequently, on the regular-orbit subfamily `p = 5 mod 24`, **the naive edge-fixed dual `I+A` can violate the source cap only in the trivial/orbit-constant `S_3` sector.**

This is not a theta certificate because the trivial sector of `I+A` is large.

### Proof

Write `G=S_3` and let `rho:G->U(d)` be a nontrivial irreducible unitary representation (`d=1` for sign, `d=2` for standard). The group average vanishes:

\[
\sum_{g\in G}\rho(g)=0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `y in V` define the matrix-valued character kernel

\[
F_y^{\rho}(x)=\sum_{g\in G}\chi(x-g y)\rho(g),
\qquad x\in\mathbb F_p.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The elementary quadratic-character correlation is

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x-a)\chi(x-b)
=\begin{cases}
p-1,&a=b,\\
-1,&a\ne b.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because every `G` orbit in `V` is free, if `y,z` lie in distinct chosen orbits then `g y != h z` for every `g,h`. Expanding (5.4), using (5.5), and then (5.3) gives

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}F_y^{\rho}(x)^*F_z^{\rho}(x)=0
\qquad (Gy\ne Gz).
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `y=z`, freeness gives `g y=h y` iff `g=h`, so

\[
\sum_xF_y^{\rho}(x)^*F_y^{\rho}(x)
=p\sum_{g\in G}\rho(g)^*\rho(g)
-\Bigl(\sum_g\rho(g)\Bigr)^*\Bigl(\sum_h\rho(h)\Bigr)
=6pI_d.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Now let `mathcal F` map a coefficient vector `v=(v_1,...,v_q) in (C^d)^q` to the `C^d`-valued function

\[
(\mathcal Fv)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^qF_{x_j}^{\rho}(x)v_j.
\]

Equations (5.6)--(5.7) imply the exact full-field Gram identity

\[
\mathcal F^*\mathcal F=6pI,
\qquad \|\mathcal F\|=\sqrt{6p}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Next use the exact Paley invariance of quadratic character under the anharmonic action on `V`. It is enough to check the generators. For `r(x)=1-x`,

\[
\chi(r(x)-r(y))=\chi(-(x-y))=\chi(x-y)
\]

because `chi(-1)=1`. For `s(x)=1/x`, since every `x,y in V` is a nonzero square,

\[
\chi(s(x)-s(y))
=\chi\!\left(\frac{y-x}{xy}\right)
=\chi(x-y).
\]

Therefore this invariance holds for every `h in G`, and a change of variable `g=hk` in (5.4) gives

\[
F_y^{\rho}(h x)=\rho(h)F_y^{\rho}(x).
\tag{5.9}
\]

Let `R^rho` be the `qd x qd` block matrix

\[
R^\rho_{ij}=F_{x_j}^{\rho}(x_i).
\tag{5.10}
\]

Restrict `mathcal F` from all of `F_p` to `V`. Row restriction cannot increase operator norm. On the other hand, (5.9) and unitarity of `rho` show that the six rows in each source orbit have identical squared output norm. Hence, for every `v`,

\[
6\|R^\rho v\|^2
=\|\mathcal Fv\|_{\ell^2(V)}^2
\le 6p\|v\|^2.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\|R^\rho\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\sqrt p.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

It remains to identify the adjacency block. The standard `G`-Fourier/multiplicity block of the invariant adjacency operator is

\[
A^\rho_{ij}=\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf 1_{\{x_i\sim g x_j\}}\rho(g).
\tag{5.12}
\]

If `i!=j`, all six points are distinct, so

\[
\mathbf 1_{\{x_i\sim g x_j\}}
=\frac{1+\chi(x_i-gx_j)}2,
\]

and (5.3) yields

\[
A^\rho_{ij}=\frac12R^\rho_{ij}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

If `i=j`, the identity element gives the forbidden self-loop and contributes zero; for `g!=e` the same formula applies. Since

\[
\sum_{g\ne e}\rho(g)=-I_d
\]

and the `g=e` term of `R^rho_{ii}` is `chi(0)I_d=0`, we get

\[
A^\rho_{ii}=\frac12R^\rho_{ii}-\frac12I_d.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Combining (5.13)--(5.14),

\[
\boxed{A^\rho=\frac12(R^\rho-I).}
\tag{5.15}
\]

Because `A` is real symmetric, this Fourier block is Hermitian, hence so is `R^rho=2A^rho+I`. From (5.11),

\[
\lambda_{\max}(A^\rho)
=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(R^\rho)-1}{2}
\le\frac{\sqrt p-1}{2},
\]

which is (5.1). Adding the identity gives (5.2), and

\[
\frac{\sqrt p+1}{2}\le\frac23\sqrt p
\iff \sqrt p\ge3.
\]

Every nonvacuous prime `p = 5 mod 24` here has `p>=29`, so the inequality is strict. QED.

### What the theorem does and does not say

The theorem eliminates two entire growing representation sectors as the obstruction for the **naive** dual. It does not prove (SC), because the trivial block of `I+A` has an eigenvalue of order `p`. Nor does it say that an arbitrary nonedge correction used to repair the trivial block stays in the safe sectors. Such a correction can leak into sign and standard blocks. The useful fact is quantitative slack: before that leakage is introduced, those blocks sit at asymptotic constant `1/2`, below the source constant `2/3`.

# 6. B9 — randomize: an exact local Bernoulli law for regular six-orbit blocks

The matched random model suggests that, for a fixed target orbit, its six adjacency bits should look like independent fair signs. On `p = 5 mod 24` this can be proved uniformly with one eight-linear-factor Weil estimate.

## Theorem `P04-I0101-REGULAR-S3-BLOCK-BERNOULLI-13` — PROVED / PARTIAL

Let `p = 5 mod 24` be prime, fix `y in V`, and enumerate its free anharmonic orbit `Gy={g y:g in S_3}`. For a sign vector

\[
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_g)_{g\in G}\in\{\pm1\}^6,
\]

let `N_y(epsilon)` be the number of `x in F_p` such that

\[
\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1,
\qquad
\chi(x-g y)=\varepsilon_g\quad\forall g\in G.
\]

(The equations automatically exclude the eight distinct roots `0,1` and `Gy`.) Then, uniformly in `y` and `epsilon`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|N_y(\varepsilon)-\frac{p}{256}\right|
\le \frac{769}{256}\sqrt p+8.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently, if `k_{ij}` denotes the number of edges between one vertex of source orbit `Gx_i` and the six vertices of a distinct target orbit `Gx_j`, then for each `k=0,...,6`, uniformly in the target orbit,

\[
\#\{i\ne j:k_{ij}=k\}
=\frac{\binom6k}{64}\,q+O(\sqrt p),
\qquad q=\frac{p-5}{24},
\tag{6.2}
\]

with an explicit constant obtainable from (6.1). Thus the six-entry orbit-block degree has the `Binomial(6,1/2)` law up to `O(sqrt(p))` discrepancy.

This is local/block pseudorandomness, **not** a global theta or operator-norm theorem.

### Proof

Because the `G` action on `V` is free, the eight roots

\[
0,\quad1,\quad \{g y:g\in G\}
\]

are distinct. Away from these roots, the indicator of a prescribed eight-sign pattern is

\[
2^{-8}(1+\chi(x))(1+\chi(x-1))
\prod_{g\in G}(1+\varepsilon_g\chi(x-g y)).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Sum (6.3) over `x in F_p`. Expanding produces one empty-subset term `p`. A term involving exactly one linear factor has character sum zero. A term involving `r>=2` distinct linear factors is

\[
\sum_x\chi\!\left(\prod_{a\in S}(x-a)\right),
\]

where the polynomial is squarefree of degree `r`; the standard quadratic Weil bound gives absolute value at most

\[
(r-1)\sqrt p.
\]

Therefore the total nonconstant character-sum error is at most

\[
2^{-8}\sum_{r=2}^8\binom8r(r-1)\sqrt p.
\]

The exact binomial sum is

\[
\sum_{r=2}^8\binom8r(r-1)=769.
\]

Finally, using (6.3) at the eight roots is not a true sign indicator because `chi(0)=0`; deleting those points changes the count by at most eight. This proves (6.1).

For (6.2), sum (6.1) over the `binom(6,k)` patterns with exactly `k` positive target-orbit signs. For every source orbit different from `Gy`, simultaneous anharmonic invariance merely permutes the six target coordinates, so the number `k` is the same for all six source vertices. Divide the resulting vertex count by six and replace `p/24` by `q=(p-5)/24`; the difference is absorbed in the explicit `O(1)` term. QED.

### Why the random model still does not prove (SC)

Equation (6.2) makes the matched six-bit random-block heuristic rigorous at one target orbit at a time. It does not control correlations among `Theta(p)` target orbits simultaneously. Lovasz theta depends on the global PSD/operator geometry. This is the same local-to-global gap visible in R20/I0100: logarithmic or fixed-dimensional Bernoulli behavior is real pseudorandomness, but it is not automatically a theta theorem.

One useful exact consequence is that “complete-join” orbit pairs (`k=6`) have asymptotic density `1/64` among orbit pairs, up to `O(p^{-1/2})` rowwise discrepancy. In a `G`-invariant edge-fixed dual, the trivial Fourier coefficient of such a block is fixed at six because every one of the six relative positions is an edge; blocks having at least one nonedge possess at least one free coordinate and their trivial coefficient can be changed. Thus the trivial-sector repair problem contains a coarse complete-join graph of density about `1/64`, together with the requirement that any chosen free-coordinate correction not spend more than the `~(1/6)sqrt(p)` slack supplied by Theorem 12 in the nontrivial sectors. That is the sharper obstruction left by this run.
