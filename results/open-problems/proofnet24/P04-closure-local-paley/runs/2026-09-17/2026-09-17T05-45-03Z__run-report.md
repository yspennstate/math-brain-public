# 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: exact Bernoulli-mask Fourier identity

Let `G=S3`, `|G|=6`. A **local pattern rule** assigns to every edge mask

\[
E\subseteq G
\]

a real function

\[
b_E:G\to\mathbb R
\]

such that the edge-fixed condition holds:

\[
b_E(g)=1\qquad(g\in E).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The values on `g notin E` are the local nonedge-completion variables. This section makes no boundedness or rationality assumption on them.

For an irreducible unitary representation `rho` of `S3`, use the unnormalized Fourier transform

\[
\widehat b_E(\rho)
=\sum_{g\in G}b_E(g)\rho(g).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Write

\[
a_E=\widehat b_E(\mathbf 1)=\sum_g b_E(g),
\qquad
s_E=\widehat b_E(\operatorname{sgn}),
\qquad
U_E=\widehat b_E(\operatorname{std}).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Here `std` has dimension two.

Define the Bernoulli-mask mean trivial coefficient

\[
\mu=2^{-6}\sum_{E\subseteq G}a_E.
\tag{5.4}
\]

There are exactly

\[
64\cdot 3=192
\tag{5.5}
\]

edge incidences `(E,g)` with `g in E`, and exactly `192` free incidences `(E,g)` with `g notin E`, because every group element belongs to exactly half of the 64 masks. Therefore

\[
2^{-6}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}b_E(g)=\mu-3.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Put

\[
c=\frac{\mu-3}{3}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

## Theorem `P04-I0101-LOCAL-PATTERN-FOURIER-RIGIDITY-15` — PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL

Every real local edge-fixed pattern rule satisfies the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
2^{-6}\sum_E
\left(
 a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2
\right)
=
18+2(\mu-3)^2
+\frac6{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)-c)^2.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

In particular, if the rule is trivial-sector centered on average,

\[
\mu=0,
\tag{5.9}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
2^{-6}\sum_E
\left(
 a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2
\right)
=
36+rac6{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)+1)^2.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Thus among all centered edge-fixed six-bit local rules, the unique minimizer of total Fourier energy is the **conference-sign rule**

\[
\boxed{
b_E(g)=
\begin{cases}
1,&g\in E,\\
-1,&g\notin E.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.11}
\]

The minimum total energy is exactly `36`.

### Proof

Finite-group Plancherel on `S3`, with the unnormalized transform (5.2), is

\[
\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G}d_\rho\|\widehat b_E(\rho)\|_F^2
=|G|\sum_{g\in G}|b_E(g)|^2.
\tag{5.12}
\]

The irreducible dimensions of `S3` are `1,1,2`, so (5.12) becomes

\[
a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2
=6\sum_g b_E(g)^2.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Average (5.13) uniformly over all 64 masks. On fixed edge positions `b_E(g)^2=1`, and the average number of fixed edges is `3`. Hence

\[
\mathcal Q
:=2^{-6}\sum_E
\left(a_E^2+|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2\right)
=18+6\cdot2^{-6}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}b_E(g)^2.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Now complete the square around the global free-slot mean `c`. By (5.6)--(5.7),

\[
2^{-6}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)-c)^2
=2^{-6}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}b_E(g)^2
-\frac{(\mu-3)^2}{3}.
\tag{5.15}
\]

Substitute (5.15) into (5.14):

\[
\mathcal Q
=18+2(\mu-3)^2
+6\cdot2^{-6}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)-c)^2,
\]

which is (5.8).

When `mu=0`, equation (5.7) gives `c=-1`, yielding (5.10). The last term in (5.10) is nonnegative and vanishes if and only if every free coordinate equals `-1`. This is exactly (5.11), proving uniqueness and the minimum value `36`. QED.

### What is genuinely stronger than the previous one-block leakage bound

The previous leakage theorem gave a lower bound at each partially edged mask after fixing its trivial coefficient. Equation (5.8) instead solves the **global 64-mask quadratic variational problem exactly**: once the average trivial coefficient is specified, it identifies the unique free-coordinate profile that minimizes aggregate sign+standard+trivial Fourier energy. No numerical optimization is involved.

# 6. B3 — prove something stronger: exact simultaneous normalized-sector floor

To compare the one-dimensional trivial sector with the five scalar dimensions carried by the sign-plus-standard regular representation, define

\[
A=2^{-6}\sum_E a_E^2,
\qquad
N=2^{-6}\sum_E\left(|s_E|^2+2\|U_E\|_F^2\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus `A+N=Q`.

Under the centered condition `mu=0`, define

\[
V=\frac1{64}\sum_E\sum_{g\notin E}(b_E(g)+1)^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equation (5.10) says

\[
A+N=36+6V.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because the trivial representation contributes one scalar Fourier degree and the sign-plus-standard part contributes `1+2*2=5` scalar regular-representation degrees,

\[
\max\left\{A,\frac N5\right\}
\ge\frac{A+N}{6}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Combining (6.3)--(6.4),

\[
\boxed{
\max\left\{A,\frac N5\right\}
\ge 6+V\ge6.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The conference-sign rule attains equality in the minimax value `6`. Indeed, there `a_E=2|E|-6`. Under a uniform random mask, `|E|` is `Binomial(6,1/2)`, so

\[
A=\mathbb E(2|E|-6)^2=6.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Equation (5.10) gives `A+N=36`, hence `N=30` and `N/5=6`.

Moreover, if a centered rule differs from conference signs on even one free incidence, then `V>0`, so its normalized simultaneous floor in (6.5) is strictly larger than `6`. Thus the conference rule is the unique minimizer of this local Bernoulli second-moment minimax problem.

A robust version also follows immediately. If a sequence of local rules has `mu_p=o(1)`, then (5.8) gives total Fourier energy at least `36-o(1)`, and asymptotic equality forces the free entries to be `-1+o_{L^2}(1)` over the 192 free mask incidences.

# 7. B10 — derandomize: mask-only local completions hit an exact rigidity wall

The natural derandomization attempt suggested by the rowwise Bernoulli law is:

> choose the nonedge weight in each orbit block by one fixed deterministic formula depending only on that block's six-bit edge mask.

Theorem 15 gives an exact negative result for this particular simplification. If such a rule is asymptotically centered in the trivial sector, then no choice of mask-only free values can lower the average Fourier energy below the conference-sign rule. Any departure from the free value `-1` pays the explicit quadratic penalty `6V` in (6.3).

This does **not** say that conference signs are theta-optimal. It says something narrower and useful: a successful all-prime orbit completion cannot hope to beat the conference rule merely by a better fixed lookup table on the 64 local masks while simultaneously reducing all normalized sector second moments. It must exploit information that the six-bit mask does not contain: orbit labels, arithmetic invariants of the pair, global state of the completion, or correlations across many blocks.

That conclusion matches, but does not prove, the qualitative prior-art warning in Kunisky's degree-2 theta appendix that numerical optimal dual variables show no simple structure.

Therefore the one-target-orbit pattern law implies that their row averages converge to the uniform 64-mask averages appearing in Theorem 15, with normalized error `O(p^{-1/2})` for the fixed rule.

Consequently, an asymptotically trivial-centered mask-local completion inherits the exact local second-moment floor

\[
\max\left\{A_p,\frac{N_p}{5}\right\}
\ge6-o(1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is an infinite-family consequence, not a finite computation.

## 7.2 Absolute operator-norm corollary and its limitation

Let `T_p`, `S_p`, and `U_p` denote the trivial, sign, and standard multiplicity matrices generated by such a bounded local rule on the `q=(p-5)/24` regular orbits. The preceding rowwise law yields, after ignoring `O(q)` diagonal/intraorbit terms against `Theta(q^2)` cross-orbit terms,

\[
\frac{\|T_p\|_F^2}{q^2}\to A,
\qquad
\frac{\|S_p\|_F^2+2\|U_p\|_F^2}{q^2}\to N.
\tag{7.2}
\]

If

\[
M_p=\max\{\|T_p\|_{op},\|S_p\|_{op},\|U_p\|_{op}\},
\]

then the matrix dimensions `q,q,2q` imply

\[
\|T_p\|_F^2\le qM_p^2,
\qquad
\|S_p\|_F^2+2\|U_p\|_F^2\le5qM_p^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Using (6.5)--(7.2),

\[
\boxed{
M_p\ge(\sqrt6-o(1))\sqrt q
=\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\sqrt p.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is an **absolute operator norm** lower bound. It does not imply a lower bound on `lambda_max`; the large singular/eigenvalue magnitude could lie on the negative side. Therefore (7.4) is not a counterexample to Conjecture 27 and not even a direct obstruction to the desired positive spectral cap `2/3 sqrt(p)`.

The exact missing step is now clear: second moments and absolute norms do not remember the spectral sign. The source theorem needs one-sided control of the largest eigenvalue after a globally correlated completion.
