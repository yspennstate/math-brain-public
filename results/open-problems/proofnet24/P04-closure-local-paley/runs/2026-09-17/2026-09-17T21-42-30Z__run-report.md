### Theorem 31 (fractional-1-factor minimax dual and KKT geometry)

Let `K=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph for which the fractional perfect-matching polytope

\[
\mathcal F(K)=
\left\{w\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{E}:
\sum_{e\ni i}w_e=1\ \text{for every }i\in V\right\}
\]

is nonempty. For `w\in\mathcal F(K)`, define the symmetric zero-diagonal stochastic matrix

\[
P(w)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}w_{ij}
(e_ie_j^\top+e_je_i^\top),
\]

and define the one-sided optimum

\[
\mu(K)=\max_{w\in\mathcal F(K)}\lambda_{\min}(P(w)).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mu(K)=
\min_{\substack{X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}X=1\\ y\in\mathbb R^V}}
\left\{
\sum_{i\in V}y_i:
 y_i+y_j\ge2X_{ij}\ \text{for every }\{i,j\}\in E
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

There exist optimal `w^*`, `X^*`, `y^*`, with `\mu=\mu(K)`, satisfying the saddle/KKT relations

\[
P(w^*)-\mu I\succeq0,
\qquad
X^*\succeq0,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}X^*=1,
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{(P(w^*)-\mu I)X^*=0,}
\tag{4.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
 w^*_{ij}\bigl(y_i^*+y_j^*-2X^*_{ij}\bigr)=0
 \quad(\{i,j\}\in E),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mu=\sum_i y_i^*.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because every `P(w)` is stochastic and has zero diagonal, `\operatorname{tr}P(w)=0`, hence `\mu\le0<1` for `|V|>1`. Therefore the range of `X^*` lies in the `\mu`-eigenspace of `P(w^*)`, which is orthogonal to the stationary vector `\mathbf1`; in particular,

\[
\boxed{X^*\mathbf1=0.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Equivalently, writing `X^*` as the Gram matrix `X^*_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle`, one may choose vectors with

\[
\sum_i v_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1.
\]

If `a_i=\|v_i\|^2-y_i`, then (4.1) is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mu(K)=1-
\max
\left\{
\sum_i a_i:
\begin{array}{l}
\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1,\quad \sum_i v_i=0,\\[2mm]
\|v_i-v_j\|^2\ge a_i+a_j\quad(\{i,j\}\in E)
\end{array}
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

On every active primal edge `w^*_{ij}>0`, the distance inequality is tight:

\[
\boxed{
\|v_i-v_j\|^2=a_i+a_j.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Consequently every active 4-cycle `i-j-k-l-i` obeys the exact orthogonality relation

\[
\boxed{
\langle v_i-v_k,\ v_j-v_l\rangle=0.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Finally, for fixed `X`, the inner maximization over `\mathcal F(K)` has an extreme optimum. By the classical half-integrality theorem for the fractional 1-factor polytope, an extreme optimum is supported on a disjoint union of single edges carrying weight 1 and odd cycles carrying weight `1/2`.

### Proof

For any real symmetric matrix `P`,

\[
\lambda_{\min}(P)
=
\min_{X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}X=1}\langle X,P\rangle,
\]

because the density-matrix set is the convex hull of rank-one projectors and the minimum is attained on a minimum-eigenvalue eigenspace. Hence

\[
\mu(K)
=
\max_{w\in\mathcal F(K)}
\min_{X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}X=1}
\langle X,P(w)\rangle.
\]

Both feasible sets are compact convex sets and the displayed objective is bilinear. Sion minimax therefore allows the order to be reversed:

\[
\mu(K)
=
\min_{X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}X=1}
\max_{w\in\mathcal F(K)}
2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}X_{ij}w_{ij}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

For fixed `X`, the inner problem is the finite linear program

\[
\max\left\{
2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}X_{ij}w_{ij}:
 w\ge0,\ \sum_{e\ni i}w_e=1
\right\}.
\]

Its ordinary LP dual is

\[
\min\left\{
\sum_i y_i:
 y_i+y_j\ge2X_{ij}\quad(\{i,j\}\in E),
\ y\in\mathbb R^V
\right\},
\]

where the vertex potentials are free because the degree constraints are equalities. Strong finite-dimensional LP duality inserted into (4.10) proves (4.1).

Choose a saddle pair `(w^*,X^*)` and an LP-dual optimizer `y^*` for the inner problem at `X^*`. Since `X^*` minimizes against `P(w^*)`,

\[
P(w^*)-\mu I\succeq0
\]

and

\[
0
=\langle X^*,P(w^*)-\mu I\rangle.
\]

Both matrices in this trace pairing are positive semidefinite, so the zero trace pairing forces their product to vanish, giving (4.3). LP complementary slackness gives (4.4), and primal/dual equality gives (4.5).

The matrix `P(w^*)` is symmetric stochastic, so `P(w^*)\mathbf1=\mathbf1`. It also has zero diagonal, hence trace zero and `\mu\le0`; thus the `\mu`-eigenspace is orthogonal to the eigenvector `\mathbf1` belonging to eigenvalue 1. Equation (4.3) therefore yields (4.6).

Factor `X` as a Gram matrix. The relation `X\mathbf1=0` is equivalent to `\sum_i v_i=0`, and `\operatorname{tr}X=1` is `\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1`. Put

\[
a_i=\|v_i\|^2-y_i.
\]

Then an edge dual constraint is equivalent to

\[
\|v_i-v_j\|^2
=\|v_i\|^2+\|v_j\|^2-2\langle v_i,v_j\rangle
\ge a_i+a_j,
\]

while the objective becomes

\[
\sum_i y_i=1-\sum_i a_i.
\]

There is an optimal centered `X^*` by (4.6), so restricting the dual to centered Gram matrices does not alter the optimum. This proves (4.7). If `w^*_{ij}>0`, LP complementary slackness makes the corresponding edge inequality equality, proving (4.8).

For an active 4-cycle `i-j-k-l-i`, tightness gives

\[
2\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=y_i+y_j,\quad
2\langle v_j,v_k\rangle=y_j+y_k,
\]

\[
2\langle v_k,v_l\rangle=y_k+y_l,\quad
2\langle v_l,v_i\rangle=y_l+y_i.
\]

Therefore

\[
2\langle v_i-v_k,v_j-v_l\rangle
=(y_i+y_j)-(y_i+y_l)-(y_k+y_j)+(y_k+y_l)=0,
\]

which proves (4.9).

The half-integral description of extreme fractional 1-factors is classical: every extreme point of `\mathcal F(K)` has support components consisting of isolated edges (weight 1) and odd cycles (weight `1/2`). A linear objective admits an extreme optimum, which proves the final assertion. QED.

\[

## 7. Move #15: break symmetry at the sparse-factor level

On the clean family `p\equiv5 (mod 24)`, the graph `K_p` and the one-sided objective are invariant under the free anharmonic `S_3` action. Convex averaging therefore permits invariant optimal primal and dual matrices. But imposing invariance on each sparse half-integral factor is needlessly restrictive.

Take an invariant optimal dual pair `(X^*,y^*)`. A maximizing extreme response `F` in (6.1) will generally break `S_3` symmetry. Because `X^*` is invariant, every translate `gF` is also a maximizing response. Averaging the orbit of `F` therefore gives an invariant stochastic matrix which remains an optimizer of the **inner LP against this fixed `X^*`**.

The important failure is that inner-LP optimality is not enough. An arbitrary orbit average can still have an eigenvalue below the minimax value `\mu`; equivalently it can fail

\[
P-\mu I\succeq0
\]

and hence fail the saddle complementarity relation. Therefore the asymptotic construction problem is now precise:

> mix orbits of **dual-tight half-integral factors** so that the resulting invariant chain also satisfies the spectral lower bound and complementary slackness.

This explains why “pick one nice matching/cycle cover and average it” is not by itself a proof strategy.

The existing `S_3` block decomposition sharpens the KKT statement. For an invariant saddle,

\[
P\simeq Q\oplus S\oplus(I_2\otimes R),
\qquad
X\simeq X_Q\oplus X_S\oplus(I_2\otimes X_R).
\]

Then (4.3) splits exactly as

\[
(Q-\mu I)X_Q=0,
\qquad
(S-\mu I)X_S=0,
\qquad
(R-\mu I)X_R=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence every Fourier sector receiving nonzero dual mass must actually hit the common lower edge `\mu`; a sector whose minimum eigenvalue is strictly above `\mu` carries zero dual mass. This gives an exact KKT explanation for the previous noncertifying numerical observation of **cross-sector equioscillation**. It does not prove that all three sectors are always active, but it identifies the mechanism when they are.
