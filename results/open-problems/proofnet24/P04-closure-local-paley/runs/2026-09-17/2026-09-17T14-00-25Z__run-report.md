## 3. B1 — change category to the full level-4 / octahedral cover

Define

\[
D_p=\mathbf P^1(\mathbb F_p)\setminus\{0,\infty,t:t^4=1\}.
\]

Since `p == 1 (mod 4)`, all four roots of `t^4=1` are rational, so

\[
|D_p|=p-5.
\]

Define

\[
x(t)=\left(\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\right)^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `t in D_p`, both denominators are nonzero, `x(t)` is a square, and

\[
1-x(t)=\frac{4t^2}{(1+t^2)^2}
\]

is a square. Hence `x(t) in V`. Moreover

\[
x(t)-x(s)
=\frac{-4(s-t)(s+t)(st-1)(st+1)}{(1+s^2)^2(1+t^2)^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore a fiber of `x` consists exactly of

\[
\{t,-t,t^{-1},-t^{-1}\}.
\]

Equivalently, the deck group of `D_p -> V` is

\[
K=\langle t\mapsto-t,\ t\mapsto t^{-1}\rangle\cong V_4,
\]

and the map is exactly four-to-one. This also follows from `|D_p|=4|V|` together with the degree-four formula (3.1).

Now compose with `J`:

\[
R(t)=J(x(t))
=\frac{(t^8+14t^4+1)^3}{16t^4(t^4-1)^4}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Two simple Möbius transformations lift the standard anharmonic generators:

\[
r:t\mapsto i t,\qquad x(r t)=x(t)^{-1},
\]

where `i^2=-1` lies in `F_p`, and

\[
s:t\mapsto\frac{1-t}{1+t},\qquad x(s t)=1-x(t).

Together with `K` they generate 24 distinct Möbius transformations. Their quotient by `K` is the anharmonic `S_3`, and they all preserve `R`. Since the rational function `R` has degree 24, these transformations form its full deck group on the generic fiber; this is the classical octahedral `S_4` cover underlying `X(4) -> X(1)` in this coordinate.

Because `x:D_p->V` is four-to-one and `J:V->U_p` is six-to-one on the clean family,

\boxed{R:D_p\longrightarrow U_p\text{ is exactly 24-to-one}.}
\tag{3.4}

There is also a direct moduli interpretation. For the Legendre curve

### Proof

\[
\boxed{

:=\chi\bigl(x(t)-g x(s)\bigr)
=\chi\Big((t-h)(t+h)(th-1)(th+1)\Big),
\quad h=\gamma s,

\tag{4.1}
\]

where the right side is interpreted after clearing the fourth-power denominator of `h`. Thus each channel is a fixed-complexity Kummer trace function of degree four, independent of `p`.
