## 4. B9 RANDOMIZE: what randomization can and cannot buy

The preceding run reduced a sufficient route to construction of a symmetric, zero-diagonal, entrywise nonnegative stochastic matrix P supported on edges of the complement of G_{p,2}, with

`lambda_min(P) >= -delta_p`,

where

`delta_p = 8 sqrt(p)/(3p - 8 sqrt(p) - 15)`.

The first idea in this run was to stop assigning deterministic orbit weights and instead randomize over sparse complement-supported moves.

There is an immediate structural warning from the classical Birkhoff--von Neumann theorem. A doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of permutation matrices; when P has structural zeros, every permutation carrying positive mixture mass must respect those allowed entries. For symmetric P, one can symmetrize the representing distribution by pairing a permutation with its inverse. Therefore, randomizing over complement-supported permutation cycle covers does not enlarge the feasible positive Markov polytope. It is a parametrization of the same object. Any gain must come from an analyzable arithmetic distribution on those cycle covers, not from randomization by itself. This is standard prior art, not a new P04 theorem.

I then tested two natural positive-chain heuristics numerically, strictly as NONCERTIFYING route diagnostics:

1. symmetric Sinkhorn scaling of the raw complement adjacency;
2. on the clean p congruent to 5 mod 24 family, choose a destination S3 orbit and then choose uniformly among the allowed complement channels between the two orbits.

The first heuristic repeatedly put `-sqrt(p) lambda_min(P)` in roughly the 3.3--3.8 range on sampled clean primes, while the exact sufficient threshold `sqrt(p) delta_p` is already around 3.0--3.2 there and tends to 8/3. It therefore does not look asymptotically sharp enough.

The conditional-uniform channel scheme exposed a more informative failure. Flattening the quotient/trivial block creates large sign/standard-sector leakage; moving the quotient weights back toward the raw channel counts balances the sectors but empirically stalls again above the 8/3 target. Shallow local path-count reweightings showed the same qualitative behavior. These are exploratory numerical failures only; no theorem is inferred from them.

Outcome of B9: `FAILED AS A NAIVE CONSTRUCTION, USEFUL AS A ROUTE DIAGNOSIS`. Random cycle-cover language is not a new feasible class, and local conditional-uniform randomization trades one S3 sector against another instead of controlling the joint lower edge.

## 5. B24 FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the optimizer appears to balance all three S3 sectors

The clean family p congruent to 5 mod 24 has free S3 action, so an invariant positive chain has the exact Fourier decomposition proved in the preceding run:

`P ~= Q direct_sum S direct_sum (I_2 tensor R)`.

The natural question was whether one of Q, S, or R is the actual bad block and should be attacked in isolation.

I numerically optimized the one-sided lower-edge objective over the full S3-orbit weight variables for several small clean primes, enforcing nonnegative complement support and stochasticity. These computations are exploratory and are not accepted as finite claims. At the numerical optima, the three lower edges were essentially equal rather than one sector remaining visibly dominant. Representative values of `sqrt(p) * lambda_min` were:

- p=101: trivial about -1.98668, sign about -1.98669, standard about -1.98669;
- p=149: all three about -2.26636;
- p=173: about -2.50114, -2.50096, -2.50114;
- p=197: about -2.28165, -2.28164, -2.28165.

The important information is not those small-p constants but the KKT-like fingerprint: the positive-cone optimum seems to be a **joint sector-balancing problem**. This directly contradicts the tactical hope that one can identify a single permanently bad S3 sector and fix it without affecting the others.

No theorem is promoted from these numerics. The new working hypothesis is only:

> an asymptotically useful positive complement chain will have to coordinate all six channels so that the trivial, sign, and standard lower edges are balanced simultaneously.

Outcome of B24: `EXTREMAL-MODE DIAGNOSIS CHANGED`. The likely obstruction is cross-sector equioscillation/balancing, not a single sector.
