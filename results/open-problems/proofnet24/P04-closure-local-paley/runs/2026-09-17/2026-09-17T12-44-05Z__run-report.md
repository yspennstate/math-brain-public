# 3. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: exact sparse central-path ODE

The previous run proved `P04-I0101-MAXDET-DISSIPATION-MONOVARIANT-21`. I now turn its variational derivative into an exact constructive ODE rather than another static completion ansatz.

Let `H` be any graph with adjacency matrix `A`. For `0<=z<z_c=1/(theta(overline H)-1)`, let `C_z` be the unique maximum-determinant correlation completion with

\[
(C_z)_{ii}=1,
\qquad
(C_z)_{ij}=-z\quad(ij\in E(H)),
\]

and let

\[
P_z=C_z^{-1}.
\]

The maxdet KKT equations give `(P_z)_{ij}=0` on every distinct nonedge.

Let `S` denote the vector space of symmetric matrices supported on the diagonal and graph edges, and let `Pi_S` be orthogonal projection onto that support. Put

\[
B=-A,
\]

so `B` has zero diagonal and value `-1` on every edge. For a positive definite `C`, define

\[
\mathcal L_C: S\to S,
\qquad
\mathcal L_C(Z)=\Pi_S(CZC).
\tag{3.1}
\]

## Theorem `P04-I0101-MAXDET-SPARSE-TANGENT-ODE-24` — PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL

Along the maximum-determinant path,

\[
\boxed{
Z_z:=\mathcal L_{C_z}^{-1}(B)
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

is well-defined and the path obeys the exact first-order system

\[
\boxed{
P_z'=-Z_z,
\qquad
C_z'=C_zZ_zC_z.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Moreover, with the dissipation monovariant `M_H(z)=-d/dz log det C_z`,

\[
\boxed{
M_H'(z)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Z_zC_zZ_zC_z)
=\langle Z_z,B\rangle
=-2\sum_{ij\in E(H)}(Z_z)_{ij}
>0.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

At the origin,

\[
C_0=P_0=I,
\qquad
Z_0=-A,
\qquad
C_0'=-A,
\qquad
P_0'=A.
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof

Differentiate `P_z=C_z^{-1}` and write `H_z=C_z'`. Then

\[
P_z'=-P_zH_zP_z.
\]

Because every nonedge entry of `P_z` is identically zero on the entire maxdet path, every nonedge entry of `P_z'` is also zero. Hence

\[
Z_z:=P_zH_zP_z=-P_z'
\]

belongs to `S`. Multiplying by `C_z=P_z^{-1}` on both sides gives

\[
H_z=C_zZ_zC_z.
\]

The prescribed entries of the covariance path differentiate to

\[
(H_z)_{ii}=0,
\qquad
(H_z)_{ij}=-1\quad(ij\in E),
\]

which is exactly

\[
\Pi_S(C_zZ_zC_z)=B.
\]

This gives (3.2) provided `L_C` is invertible. On `S`, for nonzero symmetric `Z`,

\[
\langle Z,\mathcal L_C(Z)\rangle
=\operatorname{Tr}(ZCZC)
=\|C^{1/2}ZC^{1/2}\|_F^2>0.
\]

Thus `L_C` is self-adjoint positive definite and therefore invertible. This proves the ODE.

Finally claim21 already gives

\[
M_H'(z)=\operatorname{Tr}(P_zH_zP_zH_z).
\]

Using `P_zH_zP_z=Z_z` and `H_z=C_zZ_zC_z` gives the first equality in (3.4). Since `Z_z` is supported on `S`, only the prescribed part of `H_z` contributes to `<Z_z,H_z>`, so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(Z_zC_zZ_zC_z)
=\langle Z_z,H_z\rangle
=\langle Z_z,B\rangle.
\]

The last expression is `-2` times the unordered edge sum because `B` is `-1` on both symmetric edge entries. Strict positivity follows from the positive-definite quadratic form. At `z=0`, `C_0=I`, so `L_I` is the identity on `S` and `Z_0=B=-A`. QED.

### Why this is a genuine algorithm but not yet the source proof

Equation (3.2) says every infinitesimal nonedge covariance is no longer guessed: it is produced by solving one sparse positive-definite linear system. For `G_{p,2}`, exact `S_3` symmetry reduces this system to orbit variables. But the orbit dimension still grows with `p`, and (3.4) by itself supplies no uniform source-scale upper bound preventing a singularity before

\]

So B19 succeeds algorithmically and sharpens the target: the missing theorem is now an arithmetic upper bound on the inverse operator `L_{C_z}^{-1}` along the path, not another existence statement for free nonedge weights.

# 4. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: the anharmonic quotient has a canonical `j` coordinate

This is the main new route of the run.

For `x` away from `0,1`, define the unnormalized classical modular invariant

\[
\boxed{
J(x)=\frac{(x^2-x+1)^3}{x^2(1-x)^2}.
}
\tag{4.1}

Thus the usual modular `j` is `256 J`.

Let `G=S_3=<x->1-x, x->1/x>` be the anharmonic group. Direct substitution gives

\[
J(1-x)=J(x),
\qquad
J(1/x)=J(x).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The key exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&(x^2-x+1)^3 y^2(1-y)^2
-(y^2-y+1)^3 x^2(1-x)^2\\
&=(x-y)(xy-1)(x+y-1)(xy-x+1)(xy-x-y)(xy-y+1).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The six factors on the right correspond exactly to the six anharmonic transforms of `y`.

## Theorem `P04-I0101-ANHARMONIC-J-PARITY-23` — PROVED / STRUCTURAL-PARTIAL

Assume `p = 5 mod 24` is prime and let

\[
V=\{x\in F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\}.
\]

The `S_3` action on `V` is free. Define

\[
U_p=J(V)\subseteq F_p.
\]

Then:

1. `J` is constant on each anharmonic orbit and separates distinct orbits. Hence
   \[
   \boxed{|U_p|=\frac{p-5}{24}},
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
   and `V/S_3` is canonically identified with `U_p`.
2. For two distinct anharmonic orbits represented by `x,y`, let
   \[
   k(x,y)=\#\{g\in S_3:\chi(x-g y)=1\}.
   \tag{4.5}
   \]
   Then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \chi(J(x)-J(y))
   =\prod_{g\in S_3}\chi(x-g y)
   =(-1)^{k(x,y)}.
   }
   \tag{4.6}
   \]
3. Therefore the graph on orbit labels `U_p` joining two labels when the cross-orbit edge count `k` is even is **exactly the Paley graph induced on `U_p`**.

### Proof

The invariance is (4.2). For separation, clear denominators in `J(x)-J(y)` and use (4.3). If `J(x)=J(y)`, one of the six factors vanishes. Solving those six linear/fractional equations gives exactly

\[
y,\quad1-y,\quad1/y,\quad1/(1-y),\quad y/(y-1),\quad(y-1)/y.
\]

Thus `x` is in the anharmonic orbit of `y`. The converse follows from invariance. On the free family every orbit has six elements, while `|V|=(p-5)/4`, proving (4.4).

For the character identity, write the six differences explicitly:

\[
\begin{aligned}
x-y&=x-y,\\
x-(1-y)&=x+y-1,\\
x-y^{-1}&=(xy-1)/y,\\
x-(1-y)^{-1}&=-(xy-x+1)/(1-y),\\
x-y/(y-1)&=(xy-x-y)/(y-1),\\
x-(y-1)/y&=(xy-y+1)/y.
\end{aligned}
\]

On `V`, `y` and `y-1` are nonzero squares, and because `p=1 mod4`, `-1` is a square. Hence every denominator and the displayed minus sign has quadratic character `+1`. Also `x^2(1-x)^2 y^2(1-y)^2` is a square. Applying `chi` to (4.3) therefore gives the first equality in (4.6).

For distinct free orbits none of the six differences vanishes. Exactly `k` of their six signs are `+1` and `6-k` are `-1`, so their product is `(-1)^(6-k)=(-1)^k`. This proves the theorem. QED.
