\mathcal L_r

\[
a_E^2+\frac r5N_E

# 5. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: theta is a positive-definite completion threshold

The edge-fixed dual used throughout P04 can be written exactly as a matrix-completion problem.

For a graph `H` on `n` vertices, strong SDP duality gives

\[
\tau(H)=\vartheta(\overline H)
=\min\{\lambda_{\max}(B):
B=B^T,\ B_{ii}=1,\ B_{ij}=1\ (ij\in E(H))\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The primal is

\[
\tau(H)=
\max\{\langle J,X\rangle:
X\succeq0,\ \operatorname{Tr}X=1,
X_{ij}=0\text{ on distinct nonedges}\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The primal has a Slater point `I/n`, so the equality and dual attainment are standard.

Fix `t>1` and set

\[
S=tI-B.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then the fixed dual entries become

\[
S_{ii}=t-1,
\qquad
S_{ij}=-1\quad(ij\in E(H)),
\tag{5.4}
\]

while nonedge entries are free. Moreover

\[
B\preceq tI\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\succeq0,
\]

and strict inequality is equivalent to `S positive definite`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 t\ge\tau(H)
\iff
\text{the partial matrix (5.4) has a PSD completion},
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 t>\tau(H)
\iff
\text{the partial matrix (5.4) has a PD completion}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Scaling by `t-1`, this is a partial correlation matrix with

\[
R_{ii}=1,
\qquad
R_{ij}=\rho_t:=-\frac1{t-1}\quad(ij\in E(H)).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the exact Randomstrasse finite-`p` inequality at

\[
t_p=\frac23\sqrt p

is equivalent to PSD-completability of the uniform negative-edge partial correlation pattern

\rho_p=-\frac{1}{(2/3)\sqrt p-1}

\tag{5.8}

on `G_{p,2}`.

This is precisely the strict-vector-coloring language known in the graph-SDP literature. The useful change for P04 is to stop guessing the free correlations and instead use the canonical maximum-entropy completion and its sparse inverse.

# 6. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the maximum-determinant completion

We now prove claim 16.

## Theorem 6.1 — maximum-determinant sparse-precision theta path

Let `H` be a nonempty graph on `n` vertices and let `t>tau(H)`. Among all positive-definite completions of (5.4), there exists a unique maximizer

\[
S_t=\arg\max\log\det S.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let `K_t=S_t^{-1}`. Then:

1. `K_t` vanishes on every distinct nonedge;
2. `X_t=K_t/Tr(K_t)` is primal-feasible for (5.2);
3. the exact primal value is
   \[
   \langle J,X_t\rangle=t-\frac n{\operatorname{Tr}K_t};
   \tag{6.2}
   \]
4. for `F_H(t)=log det S_t`,
   \[
   F_H'(t)=\operatorname{Tr}K_t;
   \tag{6.3}
   \]
5. as `t downarrow tau(H)`,
   \[
   \operatorname{Tr}K_t\to\infty,
   \qquad
   t-\frac n{\operatorname{Tr}K_t}\to\tau(H).
   \tag{6.4}
   \]

### Proof — existence and uniqueness

By (5.6), at least one PD completion exists. Consider the PSD closure of the affine completion set. Its diagonal is fixed to `t-1`. Every PSD matrix in this closure obeys, by its `2 x 2` principal minors,

\[
|S_{ij}|\le t-1.
\]

Hence the closed feasible completion set is compact. The determinant attains a maximum there. Since a PD completion exists, the maximum determinant is strictly positive, so any maximizer is itself PD. Strict concavity of `log det` on the PD cone makes the maximizer unique.

### Proof — inverse zeros

Let `i ne j` be a nonedge. The entry `S_{ij}=S_{ji}` is a free coordinate. Perturbing it in the symmetric direction `E_{ij}+E_{ji}` and differentiating at the interior maximizer gives

\[
0=\frac{d}{ds}\log\det(S_t+s(E_{ij}+E_{ji}))\big|_{s=0}
=2(K_t)_{ij}.
\]

Therefore

\[
(K_t)_{ij}=0
\]

on every distinct nonedge. This is the classical maximum-determinant / covariance-selection condition of Grone--Johnson--Sa--Wolkowicz and Dempster, here rederived directly for the P04 normalization.

### Proof — primal witness and exact gap identity

Because `K_t` is positive definite, `X_t=K_t/Tr K_t` is PSD with trace one. The inverse-zero property makes it zero on all distinct nonedges, so it is feasible for (5.2).

Write

\[
B_t=tI-S_t.
\]

Then `B_t=1` on the diagonal and on every edge. Since `S_tK_t=I`, taking traces gives

\[
n=t\operatorname{Tr}K_t-\langle B_t,K_t\rangle.
\tag{6.5}
\]

But `K_t` is supported only on the diagonal and edges, exactly where `B_t` agrees with `J`. Hence

\[
\langle B_t,K_t\rangle=\langle J,K_t\rangle.
\]

Divide (6.5) by `Tr K_t` to obtain (6.2).

A vertexwise form of the same score equation is also exact:

\[
\boxed{
(t-1)(K_t)_{ii}-\sum_{j\sim i}(K_t)_{ij}=1
\quad\text{for every }i.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is the local moment-balance equation of the sparse precision model.

### Proof — differentiating the extremal value

Parameterize the free nonedge coordinates by a vector `z` and write the completion as `S(t,z)`. The optimizer `z_t` is unique. Danskin's envelope theorem, or direct differentiation of the unique interior optimum, gives

\[
F_H'(t)
=\left\langle S_t^{-1},\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}\right\rangle
=\langle K_t,I\rangle
=\operatorname{Tr}K_t,
\]

because only the diagonal prescribed entries vary with `t`. This proves (6.3) and therefore (1.7).

### Proof — convergence to theta

Suppose along some sequence `t_m downarrow tau(H)` that `Tr K_{t_m}` stayed bounded. Since

\[
\operatorname{Tr}K_t\ge\lambda_{\min}(S_t)^{-1},
\]

this would bound `lambda_min(S_{t_m})` away from zero. The entries of `S_{t_m}` are uniformly bounded by their fixed diagonal scale, so a subsequence would converge to a **positive-definite** completion of (5.4) at `t=tau(H)`. By (5.6), that would imply `tau(H)<tau(H)`, a contradiction. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Tr}K_t\to\infty.
\]

Equation (6.2) then gives

\[
q_H(t)=t-n/\operatorname{Tr}K_t\to\tau(H).
\]

QED.

\[
P_gS_tP_g^T=S_t,

P_gK_tP_g^T=K_t.
