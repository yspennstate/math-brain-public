## 2. Counterexample-first check

The first candidate to reject is the naive normalized clade count `N_2(n)/n`. It is not a martingale. At `n=2` every binary tree has exactly one 2-clade, hence `N_2(2)/2=1/2`; after the next growth step every 3-leaf binary tree still has exactly one 2-clade, hence `N_2(3)/3=1/3`. The exact checker below independently finds the difference `-1/6` after specializing every finite-edge survival variable to `1/2`.

So a useful object must compensate the geometry-dependent drift rather than merely normalize the raw count.

## 3. Fixed statistic family

Let `T_n=CTCS(n)` in the canonical coupled growth process, and let `F_n` be its growth filtration. For every clade `C` of `T_n`, write:

- `|C|` for its number of buds/leaves;
- `ell_C` for the length of the vertical edge on which the current subclade is exactly `C` (for singleton leaves use the source's extended representation with an infinite vertical ray);
- `Anc(C)` for the strict clade ancestors of `C` on the root-to-`C` path.

Define the survival factor

`S_T(C) = exp(- sum_{A in Anc(C)} ell_A/|A|)`.

For a non-singleton clade define `b_T(C)=1-exp(-ell_C/|C|)`, while for a singleton set `b_T(C)=1` (eventual stopping on its infinite ray). Define the explicit clade-hit probability

`p_T(C) = (|C|/n) S_T(C) b_T(C)`.

For `r>=2`, let `N_r(T)` be the number of size-`r` clades. If the new bud is inserted by stopping on the edge carrying `C`, denote the resulting tree by `T plus_C *`. Put

`d_r(T,C) = N_r(T plus_C *) - N_r(T)`.

There is a closed structural formula:

`d_r(T,C) = 1_{|C|+1=r}
            + # {A in Anc(C): |A|=r-1}
            - # {A in Anc(C): |A|=r}`.

The statistic family for this track is now frozen as the compensated clade-count family below; future runs must not swap to a different statistic merely to manufacture movement.

## 4. Proposition: explicit compensated clade-count martingale

**Proposition.** For every fixed `r>=2`, define the predictable drift

`G_r(T) = sum_{C clade of T} p_T(C) d_r(T,C)`.

Then

`M_r(N) = N_r(T_N) - sum_{n=1}^{N-1} G_r(T_n)`

is an `(F_N)` martingale (with `N_r(T_1)=0`). More generally, every finite vector `(M_2,...,M_R)` is a martingale, and its predictable quadratic covariation is explicit:

`<M_r,M_s>_N
 = sum_{n=1}^{N-1} [ sum_C p_{T_n}(C)d_r(T_n,C)d_s(T_n,C)
                     - G_r(T_n)G_s(T_n) ]`.

### Proof

Condition on `T_n`. First choose the target bud. A clade `C` contains exactly `|C|` of the `n` possible targets, so the chance that the chosen root-to-bud path enters `C` is `|C|/n`. On each strict ancestral edge `A`, the stop clock has constant rate `1/|A|`, hence survival across that edge is `exp(-ell_A/|A|)`. Multiplying over strict ancestors gives `S_T(C)`. Conditional on reaching the edge of a non-singleton `C`, the chance to stop before leaving that edge is `1-exp(-ell_C/|C|)`. For a singleton, the extended edge is infinite and the rate is 1, so eventual stopping has probability 1. Therefore the conditional probability that the unique insertion point lies on the edge carrying `C` is exactly `p_T(C)`.

These events partition the next growth step. This can also be seen leaf-by-leaf: along a fixed root-to-infinity path, successive terms are `survival-so-far times (1-next-survival)`, followed by the terminal singleton term, and telescope to 1. Averaging over the uniform target bud preserves total probability 1.

Now fix the hit clade `C`. The insertion creates exactly one new clade of size `|C|+1`. Every strict ancestor of `C` acquires the new bud and therefore has its size increased by one; all other clades keep their sizes. Because strict ancestor sizes are strictly increasing along the path, for each `r` there is at most one ancestor of size `r-1` and at most one of size `r`. This gives the displayed formula for `d_r(T,C)`.

Consequently

`E[N_r(T_{n+1})-N_r(T_n) | F_n]
 = sum_C p_{T_n}(C)d_r(T_n,C)
 = G_r(T_n)`.

Thus `M_r(N+1)-M_r(N) = d_r(T_N,C_N)-G_r(T_N)` has conditional mean zero, proving the martingale claim.

For the bracket, condition again on `T_n`. The hit clade is a single categorical outcome `C` with probabilities `p_{T_n}(C)`. Therefore

`E[(Delta M_r)(Delta M_s)|F_n]
 = sum_C p_{T_n}(C)d_r(T_n,C)d_s(T_n,C)-G_r(T_n)G_s(T_n)`,

and summing these predictable conditional covariances proves the formula. QED.

## 5. Why this is more than the generic Doob decomposition

The statement is not offered as “every adapted statistic has a martingale part.” The source-specific content is the closed clade-edge formula for the entire transition kernel `p_T(C)`, the closed structural increment `d_r(T,C)`, and hence an explicit predictable drift and bracket computable from the current realized CTCS tree.

This gives a direct route toward the neighboring Open Problem 9 without claiming to solve it: for each fixed `R`, the joint final clade-count vector satisfies

`(N_2(T_N),...,N_R(T_N)) = (sum G_2,...,sum G_R) + (M_2(N),...,M_R(N))`,

with an explicit predictable covariance matrix for the martingale noise. Thus a multivariate martingale CLT would follow once one controls (i) the asymptotics of the predictable drift vector and (ii) the normalized bracket/Lindeberg terms. This isolates the missing source-relevant analytic obligation rather than merely renaming a Doob martingale. I did not edit P13's files or claim its covariance/CLT target.

A second immediate consequence is concentration around the predictable compensator. Since `d_r(T,C)` is in `{-1,0,1}`, each centered martingale increment has absolute value at most 2. Hence standard bounded-increment martingale inequalities apply to `N_r(T_N)-sum G_r(T_n)`, and the exact bracket above permits a sharper Freedman-type bound when its realized size is controlled. This is a concrete probabilistic payoff, but by itself is not enough to declare the source's deliberately qualitative word “useful” settled.
