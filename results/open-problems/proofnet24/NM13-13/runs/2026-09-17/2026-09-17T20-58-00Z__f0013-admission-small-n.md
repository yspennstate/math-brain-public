## 4. Exact finite theorem proved in this run

### Theorem F0013-SMALL-N-01

For all Rademacher matrices of orders `n=1,2,3,4`, the complete exact factor-degree census of `det(xI-A)` over `Q` is:

| n | total matrices | irreducible | exact irreducibility probability | complete factor-degree counts |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 2 | 2 | `1` | `(1): 2` |
| 2 | 16 | 8 | `1/2` | `(1,1): 8`, `(2): 8` |
| 3 | 512 | 96 | `3/16` | `(1,1,1): 256`, `(1,2): 160`, `(3): 96` |
| 4 | 65536 | 9024 | `141/1024` | `(1,1,1,1): 10560`, `(1,1,2): 24544`, `(1,3): 19968`, `(2,2): 1440`, `(4): 9024` |

There are exactly **203 distinct characteristic polynomials** at order 4.

In particular, at `n=4` there are exactly **1440** matrices whose characteristic polynomial has factor pattern `(2,2)`, hence is reducible over `Q` but has **no rational linear factor**. Their probability is

`1440/65536 = 45/2048`.

Among all 56512 reducible order-4 matrices, their exact proportion is

`1440/56512 = 45/1766`.

Therefore even at the first nontrivial quartic order, reducibility is not equivalent to the presence of an integer/rational eigenvalue. Any asymptotic proof that controls only linear factors cannot by itself settle F0013; nonlinear factors are a genuine mechanism that must be bounded. This is a route constraint, not a refutation of the asymptotic conjecture.

### Exact proof / certification method

All arithmetic is integer arithmetic.

1. For each sign matrix `A`, traces `tr(A^k)` are computed exactly and Newton identities reconstruct the monic integer characteristic polynomial `det(xI-A)`.
2. By Gauss's lemma, a monic polynomial in `Z[x]` is reducible over `Q` exactly when it factors into monic integer polynomials.
3. Integer roots are stripped by the rational-root theorem and exact synthetic division.
4. A cubic with no integer root is irreducible over `Q`.
5. For a quartic with no integer root, the only possible reducible pattern is `(2,2)`. Writing

   `(x^2+a x+b)(x^2+c x+d)`

   gives the exact integer equations

   `a+c=A`, `ac+b+d=B`, `ad+bc=C`, `bd=D`

   for quartic coefficients `(1,A,B,C,D)`. The checker exhausts the signed divisors `b,d` of `D`, solves the quadratic for integer `a,c`, and checks the remaining coefficient equation exactly. Thus every quartic is classified without floating point or numerical factorisation.
6. The program enumerates every one of the `2^(n^2)` matrices for `n<=4` and checks internal divisibility assertions in Newton identities and exact divisions.
