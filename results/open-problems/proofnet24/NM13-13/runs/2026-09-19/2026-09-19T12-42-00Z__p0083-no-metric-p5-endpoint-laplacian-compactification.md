## 9. Method #17 — the numbers are a reversible Markov-chain / Laplacian problem in disguise

The predecessor's incidence matrix has an exact second interpretation.

Let `Q` be the vertex-edge matrix `Q_{v,e}=q_{e,v}` and `W=diag(w_e)`. Then

`C=QW^(-1/2)`.

At every support vertex, `Q 1=1`; at every support edge, the equilibrium identity gives `Q^T 1=w`. Hence

`P := C C^T = Q W^(-1) Q^T`

is symmetric and stochastic:

`P 1 = QW^(-1)w = Q1 = 1`.                                                  (9.1)

For an edge `e=uv`, define the source-valid conductance

`c_e = q_{e,u}q_{e,v}/w_e`

`    = A_u A_v/(A_u+A_v)^(p+2)`.                                           (9.2)

For a simple support graph, `P_uv=c_e` off diagonal; with parallel edges one sums their conductances. Since row sums are one,

`P = I - L_c`,                                                              (9.3)

where `L_c` is the weighted graph Laplacian with edge conductances (9.2).

This gives exact spectral translations.

### Unicyclic components

If the component is unicyclic, `m=n`. Non-instability makes `C` invertible and requires every eigenvalue of `P` to be at least `h=1/(p+1)`. By (9.3), equivalently

`lambda_max(L_c) <= 1-h = 1/alpha = p/(p+1)`.                               (9.4)

### Tree components

If the component is a tree, `m=n-1`. Let `sigma_v` be its bipartition sign and set

`y_v=sigma_v/A_v`.

For `e=uv`,

`q_{e,u}/A_u = q_{e,v}/A_v = (A_u+A_v)^(-(p+1))`,

while `sigma_u=-sigma_v`, so `C^T y=0`. Thus `Py=0`, or `L_c y=y`. Because `C` has full column rank, this kernel is one-dimensional. The nonzero eigenvalues of `P=CC^T` are exactly those of `C^TC`; therefore tree non-instability is equivalent to

`lambda_{n-1}(L_c) <= 1/alpha`,                                             (9.5)

with the final Laplacian eigenvalue equal to 1.

This is the precise place where the number `1/alpha` belongs to weighted spectral graph theory.

### Uniform odd-cycle anchor and the `4/3` number

For the uniform equilibrium on a bare odd cycle `C_k`, every `A_v=1/2`, hence every source-valid conductance is `c_e=1/4`. Therefore

`L_c=(1/4)L(C_k)`

and

`lambda_max(L_c)=cos^2(pi/(2k))`.                                          (9.6)

Condition (9.4) becomes

`alpha <= sec^2(pi/(2k))`.                                                   (9.7)

For `k=3`, the threshold is exactly `4/3`; for every odd `k>=5`, it is already strictly below `4/3`. This recovers the source's `4/3` scale from an exact spectral obstruction rather than from an isolated small-case formula.

Boyd–Diaconis–Xiao's fastest-mixing Markov-chain work shows that fixed-graph symmetric stochastic weight optimization naturally becomes an SDP, and their later symmetry methods reduce such problems substantially. That literature is genuinely useful machinery for (9.4)–(9.5). But it optimizes over arbitrary admissible Markov weights, whereas WARM conductances are constrained by (5.1) and (9.2). Dropping those nonlinear equations would enlarge the feasible set and could produce a false obstruction or false certificate. I therefore record the translation as a new route, not as a solved theorem.
