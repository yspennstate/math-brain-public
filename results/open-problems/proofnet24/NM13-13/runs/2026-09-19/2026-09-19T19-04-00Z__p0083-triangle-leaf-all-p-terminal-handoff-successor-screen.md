with strict inequality in the preceding bound, so `u>1`; similarly `v>1`.

For edge `ra`, its conductance equals its root incidence times the fraction of the endpoint potential on the `a` side:

`c_ra = x*u/(1+u) > x/2`,

and likewise `c_rb>z/2`. Therefore the total conductance incident to `r` is

`S:=d+c_ra+c_rb`

`> q/(1+q)+(x+z)/2`

`= q/(1+q)+(1-q)/2`

`= 1/2 + q(1-q)/[2(1+q)]`

`>1/2`.                                                        (7.1)

`lambda_max(L_c)>h`,

### 7.2 Regime `2 <= p <= 3`: a stronger root bound forces symmetry

`1+u > (1+q)/[q^((s-1)/s)(1-q)^(1/s)]`.                       (7.3)

Weighted AM-GM gives

`q^((s-1)/s)(1-q)^(1/s) <= [1+(s-2)q]/s`.

Hence

`1+u > s(1+q)/[1+(s-2)q] >= 2s/(s-1) >= 8/3`,

so

`u>5/3`; similarly `v>5/3`.                                   (7.4)

At vertices `a,b`, divide the equilibrium equations by `K`:

`u/(1+u)^s + u/(u+v)^s = 1/K`,

`v/(1+v)^s + v/(u+v)^s = 1/K`.                                (7.5)

Fix `S=u+v` and define

`G_S(t)=t/(1+t)^s+t/S^s`.

For `t` between `u` and `v`, both `t` and `S-t` are at least `5/3`. Therefore

`G_S'(t)=[1-(s-1)t]/(1+t)^(s+1)+S^(-s)`

`<= -[(s-1)t-1]/(1+t)^(s+1)+(t+5/3)^(-s)`.                    (7.6)

`D(t)=(27t^4+135t^3+153t^2-83t-152)/27`.

`D(5/3)=968/27>0`,

`D'(t)=(108t^3+405t^2+306t-83)/27>0`

`u=v=:t`.                                                       (7.8)

`x=z=(1-q)/2`                                                   (7.9)

`(1+t)^s = 2(1+q)^s/[q^(s-1)(1-q)]`.                          (7.10)

`1-tx = 1-t(1-q)/2`.

`t<2/(1-q)`.                                                    (7.11)

`Phi_s(q)>1`,

`Phi_s(q)=q^(s-1)(3-q)^s/[2(1+q)^s(1-q)^(s-1)]`.              (7.12)

Directly,

`Phi_s(1/3)=1`.

`(s-1)/q - s/(3-q) - s/(1+q) + (s-1)/(1-q)`.

### 7.4 A two-coordinate Rayleigh witness beats the stability ceiling

`a=t(1-q)/[2(1+t)]`,                                           (7.15)

and the pendant conductance remains `d=q/(1+q)`.

`R=(1-q)/2+2q/(1+q)`
`  - q^h(1-q)^(2-h)/[2^(2-h)(1+q)]`.                           (7.17)

`1+3/2-3/4 = 7/4>0`.                                          (7.20)
