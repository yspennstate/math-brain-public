is the weighted signless Laplacian. Put

`z_v=1/A_v`.

Using (5.1) and (5.4),

`(Qz)_v
 = sum_{u~v} c_{uv}(z_v+z_u)
 = sum_{u~v} (A_u+A_v)^(-(p+1))
 = 1/A_v
 = z_v`.                                                   (6.2)

Because `Q` is irreducible and nonnegative, `z>0` is its Perron vector with Perron eigenvalue `1`. Therefore

`K := diag(z)^(-1) Q diag(z)`                              (6.3)

is a row-stochastic reversible Markov kernel. It is reversible with respect to unnormalized stationary mass

`m_v=z_v^2=A_v^(-2)`,                                     (6.4)

because for `uv` an edge,

`m_v K_{vu}=z_v z_u c_{uv}=(A_u+A_v)^(-(p+2))=:g_{uv}`.    (6.5)

Thus the WARM tree stability problem has an exact reversible-chain dual. Since `K` is similar to `Q`, hence to `L_c`, its spectral gap is

`gamma(K)=1-lambda_2(K)=1-lambda_{n-1}(L_c)`,              (6.6)

which is exactly the smallest positive eigenvalue of `P`. Stability therefore requires

`gamma(K) >= h = 1/(p+1)`.                                (6.7)

Now take **any bridge** `e` whose removal splits the tree into vertex sets `U,V`. The two-level mean-zero test function for the reversible chain gives the exact Rayleigh upper bound

`gamma(K)

where `M_U=sum_{v in U} A_v^(-2)` and similarly for `M_V`.

This is a source-valid cut certificate, with no symmetry assumption and no numerical approximation.

`gamma(K)

`gamma(K)

Consequently

`gamma(K)

### Theorem `NM13-13-P0083-ENDPOINT-HALF-10`

Let `3/2 < alpha <= 2`, and let a finite uniform-firing graph WARM have a positive-support component that is a tree of diameter at least four. Choose any leaf-to-leaf diameter path (7.2). Let

`r_L=q_{e1,v1}`

`r_R=q_{eL,v_{L-1}}`

be the analogous parent-side incidence at the right leaf. If

`r_L >= 1/2` and `r_R >= 1/2`,                             (8.1)

then the equilibrium is linearly unstable.

Equivalently, every not-linearly-unstable non-whisker tree equilibrium in `3/2<alpha<=2` must have, on **every** leaf-to-leaf diameter path, at least one endpoint whose parent-side outer-edge incidence is strictly below `1/2`.

### Proof

Put alternating coefficients `+1,-1,+1,...` on the diameter-path edges and zero on every off-path edge. Every internal path vertex cancels exactly, including vertices with side branches because branch coordinates are zero. The signless-incidence inequality therefore gives the exact path Rayleigh quotient

`R_path
 = (w_1^(-2 alpha)+w_L^(-2 alpha))
   / sum_{i=1}^L w_i^(1-2 alpha)`.                          (8.2)

For the first two edges, (7.12)-(7.14) give

`w_1^(-2 alpha)
 < h [w_1^(1-2 alpha)+w_2^(1-2 alpha)]`.                   (8.3)

For the last two edges, the right-end analogue gives

`w_L^(-2 alpha)
 < h [w_{L-1}^(1-2 alpha)+w_L^(1-2 alpha)]`.               (8.4)

Because `L>=4`, the two edge pairs in (8.3)-(8.4) are disjoint. Adding them yields

`w_1^(-2 alpha)+w_L^(-2 alpha)
 < h [w_1^(1-2 alpha)+w_2^(1-2 alpha)
      +w_{L-1}^(1-2 alpha)+w_L^(1-2 alpha)]
 <= h sum_{i=1}^L w_i^(1-2 alpha)`.                         (8.5)

Hence `R_path<h`, contradicting the necessary stability inequality `C^T C>=hI`. Therefore the equilibrium is linearly unstable. QED.
