## 2. New theorem: exact Markov representation of the left-Radau error

Let `sigma` be a probability measure on `[0,1]`. Let `p_r` be the monic degree-`r` polynomial orthogonal with respect to `x d sigma(x)`, and let `xi_1,...,xi_r` be its roots. Let `Q_r` denote the corresponding left Gauss--Radau rule with nodes `0,xi_1,...,xi_r`; it is exact through degree `2r`.

For fixed `u in [0,1)`, define

\[
f_u(x)={x\over1-ux}.
\]

Let `H_u` be the degree-`2r` Hermite interpolant matching `f_u` at `0` and matching `f_u,f_u'` at every `xi_i`. Then

\[
\boxed{
f_u(x)-H_u(x)
={x p_r(x)^2\over(1-ux)p_r(1/u)^2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Set

\[
N_u(x)=(1-ux)(f_u(x)-H_u(x))
=x-(1-ux)H_u(x).
\]

`N_u` is a polynomial of degree at most `2r+1`. It has a simple zero at `x=0` and double zeros at all `xi_i`, so

\[
N_u(x)=C_u x p_r(x)^2.
\]

At `x=1/u`,

\[
N_u(1/u)=1/u,
\]

hence `C_u=1/p_r(1/u)^2`. This proves (2.1).

Now let

\[
N_r=E_\sigma[Xp_r(X)^2]>0,
\qquad
d\tau_r(x)={x p_r(x)^2\over N_r}\,d\sigma(x).
\]

Integrating (2.1) and using Radau exactness on `H_u` gives the **exact** error identity

\[
\boxed{
E_\sigma f_u(X)-Q_r(f_u)
={N_r\over p_r(1/u)^2}
E_{\tau_r}{1\over1-uX}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since

\[
h_K(x)=x\int_0^1{u^K\over1-ux}\,du,
\]

Tonelli's theorem and (2.2) yield

\[
E_\sigma h_K(X)-Q_r(h_K)
=N_r\int_0^1 {u^K\over p_r(1/u)^2}
E_{\tau_r}{1\over1-uX}\,du.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Writing

\[
D_r(u)=\prod_{i=1}^r(1-\xi_i u),
\qquad p_r(1/u)=u^{-r}D_r(u),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
E_\sigma h_K(X)-Q_r(h_K)
=N_r\int_0^1 {u^{K+2r}\over D_r(u)^2}
E_{\tau_r}{1\over1-uX}\,du.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is the new structural step: instead of discarding the positive Radau remainder, it converts the entire remainder into a second positive moment problem under the Christoffel-transformed law `tau_r`.

## 3. Nested Gaussian lower envelope for the exact Radau remainder

Let `(zeta_j,v_j)_{j=1}^s` be the `s`-node Gaussian quadrature rule for `tau_r`. For fixed `u in [0,1)`,

\[
g_u(x)={1\over1-ux}
\]

has nonnegative derivatives of every order on `[0,1]`. The ordinary Gaussian Hermite-remainder argument therefore gives

\[
E_{\tau_r}g_u(X)
\ge \sum_{j=1}^s {v_j\over1-u\zeta_j}.
\]

Substitution into (2.4) gives the nested lower envelope

\[
\boxed{
E_\sigma h_K(X)
\ge Q_r(h_K)
+N_r\sum_{j=1}^s v_j
\int_0^1
{u^{K+2r}\,du\over D_r(u)^2(1-u\zeta_j)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Every quantity added to the base Radau rule is positive. This is strictly stronger than simply using the base Radau quadrature whenever `N_r>0`.

For rigorous numerical lower bounds on the remaining integrals, set `u=e^{-t}`. Then

\[
G_j=\int_0^\infty
{e^{-(K+2r+1)t}\,dt\over
D_r(e^{-t})^2(1-\zeta_j e^{-t})}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The integrand is decreasing in `t`: as a function of `u`, its logarithmic derivative is

\[
{K+2r+1\over u}
+2\sum_i{\xi_i\over1-\xi_i u}
+{\zeta_j\over1-\zeta_j u}>0.
\]

Hence a right Riemann sum on `[0,T]` is a rigorous lower bound for (3.2), and the positive tail `(T,infinity)` may be discarded. This gives a completely one-sided finite certificate with no numerical integration extrapolation.
