## 2. New theorem: four-node Gaussian lower envelope for the tilted Hausdorff tail

Fix `K>=1` with `mu_K>0` and tilt the law `rho` of `Q` by `Q^K`:

\[
d\rho_K(q)=\frac{q^K}{\mu_K}\,d\rho(q),
\qquad
m_j=\mathbb E_{\rho_K}Q^j=\frac{\mu_{K+j}}{\mu_K}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Assume the order-four Hankel moment matrix is positive definite. Let `pi_4` be the monic quartic orthogonal to `1,q,q^2,q^3` under `rho_K`. Its four roots are simple and lie in `(0,1)`:

\[
0<x_1<x_2<x_3<x_4<1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Let `w_1,...,w_4>0` be the corresponding Gaussian quadrature weights. The four-node Gaussian rule is exact on every polynomial of degree at most seven:

\[
\mathbb E_{\rho_K}p(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^4 w_i p(x_i),
\qquad \deg p\le7.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define

\[
g_t=\sum_{i=1}^4w_ix_i^t.
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — eighth-derivative Gaussian tail lower bound

For every integer `t>=0`,

\[
g_t=m_t\quad(0\le t\le7),
\tag{2.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{g_t\le m_t\quad(t\ge8).}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### Proof

Exactness through degree seven gives (2.5). For `t>=8`, let `f(q)=q^t` and let `H` be the degree-at-most-seven Hermite interpolant satisfying

\[
H(x_i)=f(x_i),\qquad H'(x_i)=f'(x_i),\qquad i=1,\dots,4.
\]

The Hermite remainder at each `q in [0,1]` is

\[
f(q)-H(q)
=
\frac{f^{(8)}(\xi_q)}{8!}
\prod_{i=1}^4(q-x_i)^2
\tag{2.7}
\]

for some `xi_q in [0,1]`. Since

\[
f^{(8)}(x)=t(t-1)\cdots(t-7)x^{t-8}\ge0
\]

on `[0,1]`, the remainder is pointwise nonnegative. Thus `H(q)<=f(q)`. Integrating and using degree-seven Gaussian exactness,

\[
m_t=\mathbb E f(Q)
\ge\mathbb E H(Q)
=\sum_iw_iH(x_i)
=\sum_iw_if(x_i)=g_t.
\]

This proves (2.6). QED.

Consequently, for every finite `T`,

\[
\mu_{K+t}=\mu_Km_t\ge\mu_Kg_t
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
A(\alpha,\nu)
\le
A_K-\mu_K^2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{g_t^2}{K+t},
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

where

\[
A_K=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\mu_k^2}{k}-2\alpha L.
\tag{2.9}
\]

This is a genuine strengthening of the predecessor's three-node envelope: it uses the tilted moment data through degree seven rather than degree five and has an eighth-derivative one-sided remainder.
