\[
\boxed{E_{\sigma_K}h_K(X)\ge\sum_{i=1}^r w_i h_K(x_i).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

For `T>=1`, define

\[
h_{K,T}(x)=\sum_{t=1}^T {x^t\over K+t}.
\]

Let `H_T` be the Hermite interpolating polynomial of degree at most `2r-1` matching both `h_{K,T}` and its first derivative at the `r` Gaussian nodes. For every `x in [0,1]`, the Hermite remainder is

\[
h_{K,T}(x)-H_T(x)
={h_{K,T}^{(2r)}(\xi_x)\over(2r)!}
\prod_{i=1}^r(x-x_i)^2\ge0,
\]

because every coefficient of `h_{K,T}` is nonnegative, so its `2r`-th derivative is nonnegative on `[0,1]`. Integrating and using Gaussian exactness on `H_T`,

\[
E h_{K,T}(X)\ge E H_T(X)
=\sum_iw_iH_T(x_i)
=\sum_iw_ih_{K,T}(x_i).
\]

Now let `T->infinity`; monotone convergence applies on both sides. This proves (2.1). QED.

Thus every larger finite Hankel moment system that can be rigorously certified gives a potentially stronger one-sided complete-tail estimate without any asymptotic extrapolation.
