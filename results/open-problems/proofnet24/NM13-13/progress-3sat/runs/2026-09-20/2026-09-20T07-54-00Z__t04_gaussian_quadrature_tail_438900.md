## 2. New mathematical route: two-node Gaussian quadrature for a tilted Hausdorff measure

The new observation is that the moment tail in (1.1) is not merely log-convex. After tilting by a high power `Q^K`, the first four moments determine a two-node Gaussian quadrature rule, and positivity of the Hermite remainder gives a simultaneous lower bound on **every later moment**.

Fix `K>=1` with `mu_K>0` and define the probability measure

\[
d\rho_K(q)=\frac{q^K}{\mu_K}\,d\rho(q),
\qquad 0\le q\le1,
\tag{2.1}
\]

where `rho` is the law of `Q`. Write

\[
m_j=\mathbb E_{\rho_K}Q^j=\frac{\mu_{K+j}}{\mu_K},
\qquad j=0,1,2,3.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Assume `m_2-m_1^2>0`, as is certified below. The monic quadratic orthogonal to `1,Q` under `rho_K` is

\[
\pi(q)=q^2-sq+p,
\tag{2.3}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
 s=\frac{m_3-m_1m_2}{m_2-m_1^2},
 \qquad
 p=s m_1-m_2.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Indeed `E pi(Q)=m_2-sm_1+p=0`, and substituting (2.4) also gives `E[Q pi(Q)]=m_3-sm_2+pm_1=0`.

Let the two roots be `0<x_1<x_2<1`, and let positive weights `w_1,w_2` be chosen so that

\[
w_1+w_2=1,
\qquad
w_1x_1+w_2x_2=m_1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Because `pi` is the degree-two orthogonal polynomial of a positive measure on `[0,1]`, this is the ordinary two-node Gaussian quadrature rule; the interval certificate below directly checks root location, ordering and positive weights, so no degenerate case is hidden.

### Lemma 2.1 — Gaussian lower envelope for all later moments

For every integer `t>=0`, define

\[
g_t=w_1x_1^t+w_2x_2^t.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Then

\[
g_t=m_t\quad (0\le t\le3),
\tag{2.7}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{g_t\le m_t\quad\text{for every integer }t\ge4.}
\tag{2.8}
\]

#### Proof

Gaussian quadrature with two nodes is exact for every polynomial of degree at most three, giving (2.7). For `t>=4`, let `f(q)=q^t` and let `H` be the cubic Hermite interpolant satisfying

\[
H(x_i)=f(x_i),\qquad H'(x_i)=f'(x_i),\qquad i=1,2.
\]

For each `q in [0,1]`, the Hermite interpolation remainder has the form

\[
f(q)-H(q)
=\frac{f^{(4)}(\xi_q)}{4!}
(q-x_1)^2(q-x_2)^2
\tag{2.9}
\]

for some `xi_q in [0,1]`. Since

\[
f^{(4)}(x)=t(t-1)(t-2)(t-3)x^{t-4}\ge0,
\]

we have `H(q)<=f(q)` pointwise. Integrating against `rho_K`, and using exactness of the Gaussian rule on the cubic `H`,

\[
\mathbb E_{\rho_K}f(Q)
\ge \mathbb E_{\rho_K}H(Q)
= w_1H(x_1)+w_2H(x_2)
= w_1f(x_1)+w_2f(x_2)=g_t.
\]

This proves (2.8). QED.

Because all terms are nonnegative,

\[
\mu_{K+t}=\mu_K m_t\ge \mu_K g_t
\]

implies

\[
\frac{\mu_{K+t}^2}{K+t}
\ge
\frac{\mu_K^2g_t^2}{K+t}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Therefore, for every finite `T`,

\[
\boxed{
A(\alpha,\nu)
\le
A_K-\mu_K^2\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{g_t^2}{K+t},
}
\tag{2.11}
\]

where

\[
A_K=-\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\mu_k^2}{k}-2\alpha L.
\]

Equation (2.11) is the new variational inequality of this run. Unlike the predecessor's one-ratio geometric envelope, it uses the first three tilted moments and therefore retains a genuine second-order description of the high-`Q` tail.
