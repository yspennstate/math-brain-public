## 3. Functional and exact compound-Poisson moments

For one literal orientation of a root variable, the incident warning count is Poisson with mean

\[
\lambda=\frac{3\alpha}{2}.
\]

For independent survey variables `R_1,R_2`, let

\[
P=R_1R_2.
\]

If the incident warnings are indexed by `j`, define

\[
Z=\prod_j(1-P_j),
\qquad
Q=1-Z.
\]

Then for each integer `j>=1`, compound-Poisson independence gives

\[
\mathbb E Z^j
=
\exp\left\{
\lambda\left(\mathbb E(1-R_1R_2)^j-1\right)
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The moments

\[
\mu_k=\mathbb E Q^k
\]

are recovered exactly by binomial inversion,

\[
\mu_k=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}\mathbb EZ^j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For independent `Q_+,Q_-`, Tonelli applied to the nonnegative expansion of `-log(1-u)` yields

\[
\mathbb E\log(1-Q_+Q_-)
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mu_k^2}{k}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Writing

\[
L=\mathbb E\log(1-R_1R_2R_3),
\]

the inherited hard-field, zero-temperature, `m downarrow 0` coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
A(\alpha,\nu)
=-\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mu_k^2}{k}-2\alpha L.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The proof obligation for this run is therefore concrete: find an exact finite-support `nu` and an exact density below `4.3906` for which the one-sided bound (1.1) is strictly negative.

## 5. Transfer to the exact classical iid proper 3-SAT model

The source transfer is the same candidate-full argument already developed in the preceding interpolation reports, and the new five-support law satisfies the same hypotheses because its support is finite and lies strictly in `(0,1)`.

Strict negativity in (4.3), together with the finite-temperature K-SAT interpolation theorem and dominated convergence in the hard-field / `m downarrow 0` limit, yields finite parameters

\[
0<m_*<1,\qquad \beta_*<\infty,\qquad H_*<\infty
\]

and a constant `c>0` such that in the Poissonized with-replacement 3-SAT model,

\[
\frac1n\mathbb E\log Z_n(\beta_*)\le -c
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

Changing one clause changes `log Z_n(beta_*)` by at most `beta_*`. The Poisson bounded-difference/Poincaré estimate therefore gives `O(sqrt n)` fluctuations, so with probability tending to one,

\[
\log Z_n(\beta_*)\le -\frac c2 n.
\]

If `E_min` denotes the minimum number of violated clauses, a minimizing assignment contributes at least `exp(-beta_* E_min)` to the partition function. Hence

\[
-\beta_*E_{\min}\le \log Z_n(\beta_*)\le -\frac c2 n,
\]

and therefore

\[
E_{\min}\ge \frac{c}{2\beta_*}n
\]

with high probability. This supplies a linear unsatisfied-clause margin, not merely nonzero energy.

A with-replacement clause has a repeated variable with probability `O(1/n)`, so at fixed density the number of nonproper clauses is `O_p(1)`. Removing them changes minimum energy by at most `O_p(1)`. The remaining proper clause count differs from `floor(alpha_0 n)` by `O_p(sqrt n)`, and conditional on being proper the clauses are iid uniform over signed triples of distinct variables. Adding or deleting the `O_p(sqrt n)` count discrepancy changes minimum violated-clause energy by at most `O_p(sqrt n)`, which cannot erase the linear margin. Thus the exact fixed-count proper iid source model obeys the candidate theorem

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left[
\Phi_{n,\lfloor4.38908n\rfloor}
\text{ is satisfiable}
\right]\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Monotonicity in the number of clauses gives the same conclusion for every fixed density above `4.38908`.
