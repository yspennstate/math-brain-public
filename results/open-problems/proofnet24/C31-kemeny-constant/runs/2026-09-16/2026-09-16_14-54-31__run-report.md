# 3. Main theorem: the source ratio is unbounded

## Theorem C31-AF449-RAMANUJAN-CONE-1

For every `M>0` there exists a finite connected simple unweighted graph `G` such that

\[
\frac{F(G)}{\tau_{\rm rel}(G)D(G)}>M.
\]

Consequently no universal constant `K` can make Aldous–Fill (4.49) hold with `D` in place of `log n`.

### Proof

Fix an integer `s>=4` and put

\[
d=s^2,
\qquad
r=s.
\]

By Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava there is an infinite family of connected `d`-regular bipartite Ramanujan graphs `H`. Hence their orders are unbounded and every nontrivial adjacency eigenvalue `theta` satisfies

\[
|\theta|\le2\sqrt{d-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define the degree-`d` ball bound

\[
B(d,r)
:=1+d\sum_{j=0}^{r-2}(d-1)^j
=1+d\frac{(d-1)^{r-1}-1}{d-2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose a member `H` of the infinite family with order

\[
n\ge2B(d,r).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For independent uniform vertices `U,V` of `H`, at most `B(d,r)` vertices are within distance `<r` of any fixed `U`. Thus

\[
\Pr(d_H(U,V)\ge r)\ge\frac12,
\]

and therefore

\[
M_r:=\mathbb E\min\{d_H(U,V),r\}\ge\frac r2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now form the cone

\[
G=K_1\vee H
\]

with hub `h`. It has

\[
|E(G)|=\frac{n(d+2)}2,
\qquad
\pi_h=\frac1{d+2},
\qquad
\pi(V(H))=\frac{d+1}{d+2},
\qquad
D(G)=2.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Every base vertex has degree `d+1`. If `theta` is an adjacency eigenvalue of `H` orthogonal to constants, the corresponding random-walk eigenvalue of the cone is `theta/(d+1)`. The remaining two-dimensional constant-on-base block has eigenvalues `1` and `-1/(d+1)`. Hence

\[
\lambda_2(P_G)
\le\frac{2\sqrt{d-1}}{d+1}
<\frac{2s}{s^2+1},
\tag{3.6}
\]

so

\[
\tau_{\rm rel}(G)
<\frac1{1-2s/(s^2+1)}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Use the metric dual (1.1). Give every spoke length

\[
x:=\frac{r(d+2)}{d+r}
\]

and every base edge length

\[
y:=\frac{2(d+2)}{d+r}.
\]

Then `2x=ry`, and the dual normalization is exact:

\[
nx+\frac{nd}{2}y=n(d+2)=2|E(G)|,
\]

so `sum_e c_e ell_e=1`.

The induced metric satisfies

\[
d_\ell(h,v)=x,
\qquad
 d_\ell(u,v)=y\min\{d_H(u,v),r\}
\quad(u,v\in H).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Therefore, using (3.4),

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(G)
&\ge
2\frac1{d+2}\frac{d+1}{d+2}x
+\left(\frac{d+1}{d+2}\right)^2yM_r \\
&\ge
\boxed{
\frac{r(d+1)(d+3)}{(d+2)(d+r)}
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Since `D(G)=2`, (3.6)–(3.9) give

\[
\frac{F(G)}{\tau_{\rm rel}D}
>
\frac{r(d+3)(d+1-2s)}{2(d+2)(d+r)}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Substituting `d=s^2` and `r=s` simplifies the right side to

\[
L_s
:=
\frac{(s^2+3)(s-1)^2}{2(s^2+2)(s+1)}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Finally,

\[
L_s-\frac s8
=
\frac{3s^4-9s^3+14s^2-26s+12}
{8(s+1)(s^2+2)}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

For `s>=4`,

\[
3s^4-9s^3=3s^3(s-3)>0,
\]

and

\[
14s^2-26s+12=2(s-1)(7s-6)>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F(G)}{\tau_{\rm rel}D}>L_s>\frac s8.
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

As `s` is arbitrary, the ratio is unbounded. This disproves the existence of the universal constant asked for in Open Problem 4.49(ii). `QED`
