## 5. Move #2 — attack the negation: the exact shifted `H^infty` condition is not necessary

### Claim `P19-I0068-HINF-NOT-NECESSARY-01` — PROVED

Fix `q in (2,infinity)`. There exists a densely defined sectorial operator `A` of angle `<pi/2` on the scalar space `L^q(0,1)` such that

1. for every `p in (2,infinity)` and `kappa in [0,p/2-1)`, one has
   `(A,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,kappa}` in the exact Agresti–Veraar pair convention with `X_1=D(A)`; but
2. for **every** `lambda>=0`, the shifted operator `lambda+A` fails to have a bounded `H^infty`-calculus.

Consequently the condition in source Theorem 3.14 is sufficient but **not necessary** on the full scalar `L^q` class from Problem 2.

This disproves only that candidate characterization. **Problem 2 remains OPEN.**

### Proof

#### Step 1. Hilbert block with SMR but no shifted `H^infty` calculus

Take a Hilbert-space example `B` from Lorist Remark 5.2.4(ii) / Le Merdy's construction. Thus `-B` generates an exponentially stable analytic semigroup, so `B` is invertible and sectorial of angle `<pi/2`, and

`Q_0(x) = ( integral_0^infinity ||B^{1/2} e^{-tB}x||_H^2 dt )^{1/2} <= C||x||_H`,

while there is no `c>0` such that `c||x||_H <= Q_0(x)` for all `x`.

I first remove a possible loophole in the source condition: a positive scalar shift cannot repair this failure.

Fix `lambda>=0`. Since `B` is invertible and sectorial, the bounded operator

`R_lambda := (I + lambda B^{-1})^{1/2}`

is well defined by the bounded holomorphic functional calculus around the spectrum of the bounded sectorial operator `B^{-1}`. Fractional-power calculus gives, on `D(B^{1/2})`,

`(B+lambda)^{1/2} = R_lambda B^{1/2}`.

Also `e^{-t(B+lambda)}=e^{-lambda t}e^{-tB}`. Therefore

`Q_lambda(x)^2`
` := integral_0^infinity ||(B+lambda)^{1/2} e^{-t(B+lambda)}x||^2 dt`
` <= ||R_lambda||^2 integral_0^infinity ||B^{1/2}e^{-tB}x||^2 dt`
` = ||R_lambda||^2 Q_0(x)^2`.

If `B+lambda` had a bounded `H^infty` calculus on the Hilbert space, McIntosh's Hilbert-space square-function characterization would supply a lower quadratic estimate

`c_lambda ||x||_H <= Q_lambda(x)`

for all `x` (equivalently, the missing reverse estimate). Combining the two displays would give

`(c_lambda/||R_lambda||) ||x||_H <= Q_0(x)`,

contradicting the defining lower-square-function failure of the example. Hence

`B+lambda` has no bounded `H^infty` calculus for every `lambda>=0`.        (5.1)

On the other hand, the source Theorem 3.13 applies to `B` because `H` is Hilbert and `-B` generates an analytic semigroup. Thus

`(B,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,kappa}`

for every source-allowed `p,kappa`.                                             (5.2)

#### Step 2. Put the Hilbert obstruction inside scalar `L^q`

Let `X=L^q(0,1)` and let `(r_n)` be the Rademacher system. Set

`Y = closure span{r_n}`.

By Khintchine's inequality, `Y` is isomorphic to `ell^2`; choose an isomorphism `J:H -> Y` (using the separable Hilbert realization of the cited example). The Rademacher projection is bounded on `L^q` for `1<q<infinity`, so there is a bounded projection `P:X->Y`. Put

`Z=ker P`,

so topologically

`X = Y direct_sum Z`.                                                          (5.3)

Transfer the Hilbert operator to `Y`:

`B_Y = J B J^{-1}`.

Define

`A = B_Y direct_sum I_Z`,

with

`D(A)=J D(B) direct_sum Z`.                                                     (5.4)

The direct sum is densely defined and sectorial of angle `<pi/2`; its negative generates the direct sum of the analytic semigroup generated by `-B_Y` and the semigroup `e^{-t}I_Z`.

#### Step 3. Source stochastic maximal regularity is stable under this finite direct sum

On the `Y` block, (5.2) transfers through the Banach-space isomorphism `J`.

On the `Z` block, `I_Z` has a bounded `H^infty` calculus of angle zero, and `Z` is a closed subspace of `L^q(0,1)`. Source Theorem 3.14 therefore gives

`(I_Z,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,kappa}`

for every `p>2`, `kappa in [0,p/2-1)`.

The graph-space pair for (5.4) splits as

`X_0 = Y direct_sum Z`,
`X_1 = J D(B) direct_sum Z`.

Finite complemented sums commute, up to equivalent norms, with complex interpolation, hence

`X_theta = [Y,J D(B)]_theta direct_sum Z`.                                      (5.5)

The coordinate projections are bounded on every space in (5.5); by the ideal property they also act boundedly on the corresponding `gamma(U,X_{1/2})` spaces. Given source inhomogeneities `f,g`, split each into its `Y` and `Z` components, apply the two component SMR estimates, and recombine. Finite direct-sum norm equivalence gives the Definition 3.7 estimate. The same componentwise argument for the `H^{theta,p}` time norms gives Definition 3.8. Therefore

`(A,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,kappa}`                                                  (5.6)

for every `p>2`, `kappa in [0,p/2-1)`.

#### Step 4. No positive shift of `A` has a bounded `H^infty` calculus

For every `lambda>=0`,

`lambda+A = (lambda+B_Y) direct_sum ((lambda+1)I_Z)`.

Its resolvent and Dunford functional calculus are block diagonal. If `lambda+A` had a bounded `H^infty` calculus, restriction to the complemented invariant block `Y` would give a bounded `H^infty` calculus for `lambda+B_Y`, and conjugating by `J` would give one for `lambda+B`. This contradicts (5.1).

Combining (5.6) and Step 4 proves the claim. QED.

## 6. Move #5 — vary the shift parameter and inspect what can change

The source itself puts a scalar parameter into the sufficient condition: it asks only for *some* `lambda>=0` with bounded calculus. A counterexample at `lambda=0` would therefore be inadequate.

I treated the shift as the deformation parameter. For fixed `t`,

`d/dlambda [e^{-t(B+lambda)}] = -t e^{-t(B+lambda)}`,

so increasing `lambda` only damps the time kernel. The nontrivial domain change is isolated in

`R_lambda=(I+lambda B^{-1})^{1/2}`.

The exact inequality

`Q_lambda(x) <= ||R_lambda|| Q_0(x)`

is the useful output of this deformation: no positive shift can create a lower square-function bound that was absent at `lambda=0`. This is what upgrades the easy “unshifted `H^infty` is not necessary” observation to the exact source-quantified counterexample in Section 5.

The parameter route therefore **moved the argument materially** rather than adding another lemma after the same obstruction.
