## 5. Move #15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: positivity alone does not support the diagonal concavification identity

The preceding run left one explicit sub-obligation: determine whether the 2026 diagonal `2`-concavification mechanism can be extended to general positive analytic semigroups strongly enough to import Fackler's positive deterministic constructions.

For a positive diagonal multiplier, the mechanism contains the coordinatewise identity

\[
\left|A^{1/2}e^{-tA}x\right|^2
=Ae^{-2tA}|x|^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The most direct positive-semigroup extension would at least need the right side to remain positive, or an order domination of the same shape. A two-dimensional non-normal positive semigroup destroys this immediately.

### Claim `P19-I0068-CONCAVIFICATION-BRIDGE-FAIL-03` — PROVED

Let

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=I+N,
\qquad
N^2=0,
\]

on `ell^q_2` for any `q in [1,infinity]`. Then `-A` generates the positive bounded analytic semigroup

\[
S(t)=e^{-tA}=e^{-t}
\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\ge0,
\tag{5.2}
\]

but there is no constant `C>=0` for which the natural lattice-order domination

\[
|A^{1/2}S(t)x|^2
\le C\, A S(2t)|x|^2
\tag{5.3}
\]

holds coordinatewise for all `t>0` and `x`.

#### Proof

Because `N^2=0`, holomorphic functional calculus is finite here:

\[
A^{1/2}=I+\frac12N
=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Take

\[
x=e_2=(0,1),\qquad t=\frac14.
\]

After factoring out the positive scalar `e^{-t}`,

\[
S(t)x\sim (1/4,1),
\qquad
A^{1/2}S(t)x\sim(-1/4,1).
\]

Hence, after factoring out `e^{-2t}`,

\[
|A^{1/2}S(t)x|^2\sim(1/16,1).
\tag{5.5}
\]

On the other hand

\[
S(2t)|x|^2\sim(1/2,1),
\]

so

\[
A S(2t)|x|^2\sim(-1/2,1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

The first coordinate of the left side is positive and that of the proposed right side is negative. Thus no nonnegative `C` can make (5.3) hold. Equation (5.1) also fails outright. `square`

This is a **method falsification**, not a counterexample to SMR. Indeed at `q=2` the same matrix lies at the Hilbert endpoint where the source theorem guarantees SMR. The conclusion is exact and deliberately narrow:

> **Positivity of the analytic semigroup alone does not permit the diagonal `2`-concavification proof to be ported by the obvious pointwise square identity or its lattice-order domination.**

It does **not** rule out a different one-sided concavification theorem with additional carré-du-champ, lattice-homomorphism, dilation, or kernel assumptions.

## 6. Move #23 — DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT: reduce a scalar-Lq counterexample to finite block amplification

Rather than guess another global functional calculus condition, suppose the surviving implication (3.1) is false and ask what the **smallest concrete witness architecture** could be. There is a useful finite-dimensional reduction.

For a fixed `q>2`, fixed source temporal exponent `p>2`, and fixed finite horizon `T>0`, let `C_p(A;T)` denote the best constant in the basic stochastic maximal-regularity estimate for `A` on `(0,T)` (infinity if the estimate fails).

### Claim `P19-I0068-FINITE-BLOCK-LIFT-03` — PROVED

Fix `q in (2,infinity)` and `p in (2,infinity)`. For every `n`, let

\[
X_n=\ell^q_{d_n}
\]

and let `A_n` be injective sectorial operators with a common sectorial angle `<pi/2`. Assume their relevant `R`-sectorial families have a uniform `R`-bound `M`, while

\[
C_p(A_n;T)\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then on the scalar counting-measure space

\[
X=\Big(\bigoplus_{n\ge1}X_n\Big)_{\ell^q}\cong\ell^q,
\]

the block diagonal operator

\[
A=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}A_n,
\tag{6.2}
\]

is sectorial and `R`-sectorial of angle `<pi/2`, but **does not** have stochastic maximal `L^p`-regularity on `(0,T)`.

#### Proof — sectoriality

Give `A` its standard direct-sum domain

\[
D(A)=\{x=(x_n):x_n\in D(A_n),\ (A_nx_n)_n\in X\}.
\]

Uniform sectorial resolvent bounds imply that `A` is closed, densely defined, sectorial with the same admissible angle, and

\[
(\lambda+A)^{-1}=\bigoplus_n(\lambda+A_n)^{-1}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof — uniform R-sectoriality passes to the ell-q sum

Let `T_{j,n}` be finitely many operators from the block `R`-sectorial families and set

\[
T_j=\bigoplus_n T_{j,n}.
\]

For finitely supported vectors `x_j=(x_{j,n})`, put

\[
Y_n=\sum_j\varepsilon_jT_{j,n}x_{j,n},
\qquad
Z_n=\sum_j\varepsilon_jx_{j,n}.
\]

Because `q>=2`, monotonicity of probability `L^r` norms, Fubini for the `ell^q` direct sum, the Kahane–Khintchine inequality, and the uniform block `R`-bound give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Big(\mathbb E\big\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jT_jx_j\big\|_X^2\Big)^{1/2}
&\le
\Big(\sum_n\mathbb E\|Y_n\|_{X_n}^q\Big)^{1/q}\\
&\le K_qM
\Big(\sum_n\mathbb E\|Z_n\|_{X_n}^q\Big)^{1/q}\\
&=K_qM
\Big(\mathbb E\big\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jx_j\big\|_X^q\Big)^{1/q}\\
&\le K_q^2M
\Big(\mathbb E\big\|\sum_j\varepsilon_jx_j\big\|_X^2\Big)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the direct-sum resolvent family is `R`-bounded. Hence `A` is `R`-sectorial (with a constant depending only on `q` and the uniform block bound, not on `n`).

#### Proof — stochastic maximal regularity cannot survive the direct sum

Functional calculus and the semigroup are block diagonal:

\[
A^{1/2}=\bigoplus_n A_n^{1/2},
\qquad
e^{-tA}=\bigoplus_n e^{-tA_n}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

If `A` had basic source SMR on `(0,T)` with constant `C`, choose an admissible progressively measurable input supported in only the `n`th reducing block. The stochastic convolution stays in that block and the ambient `ell^q` norm equals the block norm. Therefore

\[
C_p(A_n;T)\le C
\qquad\text{for every }n,
\]

contradicting (6.1). Thus `A` is `R`-sectorial but fails SMR. `square`
