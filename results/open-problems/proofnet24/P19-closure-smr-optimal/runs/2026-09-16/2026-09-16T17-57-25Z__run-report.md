## 4. Move #18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: a Hilbert-block no-go theorem for every hereditary candidate

The previous H-infinity counterexample is a special case of a more structural obstruction. The important object is not one bad calculus example; it is the fact that scalar `L^q`, `q>2`, contains a complemented Hilbert block on which the source puts **no extra condition beyond analyticity**.

### Claim `P19-I0068-HILBERT-BLOCK-NOGO-02` — PROVED

Fix `q in (2,infinity)`. Let H be a separable Hilbert space and let B be any densely defined sectorial operator of angle `<pi/2` on H in the source convention (equivalently here, `-B` generates the relevant analytic semigroup). Then there exists a densely defined sectorial operator A of angle `<pi/2` on the **scalar** space `L^q(0,1)` and a complemented reducing subspace `Y subset L^q(0,1)` such that

1. `(A,0) in SMR^bullet_{p,kappa}` for every source-allowed `p,kappa`;
2. `A|_Y` is similar to B.

Consequently, let `C` be any proposed **necessary** operator property for scalar-`L^q` source SMR such that:

- `C` is invariant under Banach-space similarity; and
- `C` passes to complemented reducing restrictions.

Then `C(B)` must hold for **every separable Hilbert-space sectorial B of angle `<pi/2`**.

In particular, any such property which fails for even one analytic Hilbert-space sectorial operator is automatically disqualified as a necessary characterization of source SMR on scalar `L^q`.

### Proof

Let `(r_n)` be the Rademacher system in `L^q(0,1)` and define

\[
Y:=\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{r_n:n\ge1\}.
\]

By Khintchine's inequalities, `Y` is isomorphic to `ell^2`. The Rademacher projection is bounded on `L^q` for `1<q<infinity`, so Y is complemented. Choose a bounded projection `P:L^q -> Y` and put

\[
Z:=\ker P,
\qquad L^q(0,1)=Y\oplus Z
\]

as a topological direct sum. Choose an isomorphism `J:H -> Y` (for finite-dimensional H, use the corresponding finite Rademacher block; for infinite-dimensional separable H use `H congruent ell^2`). Transport B to Y:

\[
B_Y:=JBJ^{-1},\qquad D(B_Y)=J D(B).
\]

Now define

\[
A:=B_Y\oplus I_Z,
\qquad D(A):=D(B_Y)\oplus Z.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because the resolvent of A is block diagonal,

\[
(z-A)^{-1}=(z-B_Y)^{-1}\oplus(z-I_Z)^{-1},
\]

A is densely defined and sectorial with angle the maximum of the two block angles, hence `<pi/2`.

On the Y block, source Theorem 3.13 gives

\[
(B,0)\in SMR^\bullet_{p,\kappa}
\]

for every allowed `p,kappa`; conjugating the equation and all norms through J gives the same property for `(B_Y,0)`.

On the Z block, `I_Z` has a bounded `H^infinity` calculus of angle zero. Since Z is literally a closed subspace of scalar `L^q`, source Theorem 3.14 gives

\[
(I_Z,0)\in SMR^\bullet_{p,\kappa}
\]

for the same range.

It remains to check that the two block estimates combine in the exact source pair. The bounded projections P and `I-P` give equivalent finite-sum norms on `L^q`. They also act boundedly on the graph space

\[
D(A)=D(B_Y)\oplus Z.
\]

Complex and real interpolation are exact for bounded operators, hence every source interpolation/trace space associated to `(L^q,D(A))` splits, up to equivalent norms, into the corresponding Y and Z block spaces. The ideal property of gamma-radonifying operators gives the same decomposition for the source noise spaces `gamma(U,X_{1/2})`. Weighted `L^p` and temporal `H^{theta,p}` norms commute with a finite complemented direct sum up to equivalent norms.

Given source inhomogeneities, apply P and `I-P`, solve the two block equations using the preceding block SMR estimates, and add the solutions. The blockwise strong solutions recombine to a strong solution of (4.1); finite-sum norm equivalence combines the estimates. Thus

\[
(A,0)\in SMR^\bullet_{p,\kappa}
\]

for every allowed `p,kappa`.

Finally, Y reduces A and `A|_Y=B_Y=JBJ^{-1}`. If C were necessary for scalar-`L^q` SMR, then C(A) would hold. Heredity gives `C(B_Y)`, and similarity invariance gives `C(B)`. Since B was arbitrary, the no-go conclusion follows. `square`

### Interpretation

This theorem explains structurally why bounded `H^infinity` calculus cannot be necessary: that property is similarity invariant and inherited by reducing restrictions, but it is not automatic for analytic Hilbert-space sectorial operators. The preceding run supplied the explicit all-shifts example.

The theorem does **not** solve Problem 2. It is a filter on admissible characterizations. A viable necessary operator condition of the usual hereditary/similarity-invariant kind must be no stronger than what is automatic in the Hilbert case.

## 5. A primary-literature condition that survives the no-go filter: R-sectoriality is necessary

\[

A\text{ has deterministic maximal }L^p\text{-regularity}.
\]

Scalar `L^q`, `1<q<infinity`, is UMD. Weis' characterization therefore gives, on bounded intervals in the standard convention,

\[
A\text{ has deterministic maximal }L^p\text{-regularity}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A\text{ is R-sectorial of angle }<\pi/2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{source SMR on scalar }L^q
\Longrightarrow
R\text{-sectoriality of }A\text{ with angle }<\pi/2.

\tag{5.1}

\[
\boxed{

The September 2026 Lorist--van Neerven--Veraar--Weis paper explicitly says no general operator-theoretic analogue of Weis' characterization is currently known for stochastic maximal regularity. I therefore record (5.2) as a **concrete OPEN candidate**, not as an inferred theorem.

## 6. Move #11 — DUALIZE: exact two-time triangular formulation

The deterministic formulation of stochastic maximal regularity in arXiv:2608.20266v2, equation (2.6), exposes the stochastic part as a lower-triangular operator. Dualizing it makes the missing structure clearer.

Fix a finite interval `I=(0,T)`, `p,q in (2,infinity)`, `X=L^q(S)`, and write `S(t)=e^{-tA}`. Define on simple functions

\[
(\mathcal T_A G)(t,s)
:=\mathbf 1_{0<s<t<T}\,A^{1/2}S(t-s)G(s).
\tag{6.1}
\]

By the gamma-Fubini identification for `L^q`, equation (2.6) is equivalent, up to constants depending only on the fixed Banach exponents, to boundedness

\[
\mathcal T_A:
L^p_s(I;L^q_x)
\longrightarrow
L^p_t\big(I;L^q_x(L^2_s(I))\big).
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Claim `P19-I0068-DUAL-TRIANGULAR-02` — PROVED

Boundedness of (6.2) is equivalent to boundedness of the Banach adjoint

\[
\mathcal T_A^*:
L^{p'}_t\big(I;L^{q'}_x(L^2_s(I))\big)
\longrightarrow
L^{p'}_s(I;L^{q'}_x),
\tag{6.3}
\]

and on dense simple functions its exact formula is

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal T_A^*F)(s)
=
\int_s^T S(t-s)^*A^{*1/2}F(t,s)\,dt.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Accordingly, in the source problem the base `(A,0)` property can be read as the conjunction of deterministic maximal regularity and the triangular stochastic bound (6.2), equivalently (6.3)--(6.4). The bullet time-regularity enhancement is additional and is not silently identified with (6.2).

### Proof

For simple G and F with compact support away from the time diagonal, all integrals below are absolutely legitimate. Pair (6.1) with F in the dual mixed-norm space:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle \mathcal T_A G,F\rangle
&=\int_0^T\int_0^t
\left\langle A^{1/2}S(t-s)G(s),F(t,s)\right\rangle\,ds\,dt\\
&=\int_0^T
\left\langle G(s),
\int_s^T S(t-s)^*A^{*1/2}F(t,s)\,dt
\right\rangle\,ds,
\end{aligned}
\]

where Fubini gives the second line and sectorial functional calculus gives

\[
(A^{1/2}S(t-s))^*=S(t-s)^*A^{*1/2}.
\]

This proves (6.4) on the dense simple class. Since `1<p,q<infinity`, all mixed-norm spaces in (6.2)--(6.3) are reflexive. Ordinary Banach-space duality therefore gives

\[
\|\mathcal T_A\|=\|\mathcal T_A^*\|
\]

under the chosen equivalent gamma-Fubini realization, and (6.4) extends by density/continuity whenever either side is bounded. `square`

### What dualization teaches

R-sectoriality controls a one-parameter resolvent family. The stochastic condition instead asks for control of an arbitrary **two-time triangular field** `F(t,s)` under (6.4), with an `L^2` square-function variable nested inside `L^{q'}` and then `L^{p'}`. This is the exact place where a direct Weis-style transference is missing. The formula is an equivalent reformulation, not a source solution.

## 7. Move #20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS

I tried three apparently natural ways to turn the preceding necessary condition into a characterization. Each fails for a precise reason.

### 7.1 Discretize time

The obvious algorithm is to replace (6.1) by a lower-triangular time-step matrix and seek an R-bounded matrix criterion. The 2026 paper of Evangelopoulos-Ntemiris--Veraar shows that this does **not** simplify the classification: for its broad class of schemes, discrete stochastic maximal `ell^p`-regularity is equivalent to the original continuous SMR. In other words, discretization faithfully transports the same unknown property; it does not turn it into an easier independent resolvent criterion.

So a finite time-grid checker is useful for examples but cannot, by itself, supply the missing operator-theoretic characterization.

### 7.2 Use the 2026 concavification equivalence on an arbitrary positive semigroup

The newest necessary-conditions paper relates SMR to deterministic maximal regularity on a 2-concavification for its **specific positive diagonal multiplication/multiplier model**, and obtains exact model classifications there. It is tempting to combine that with Fackler's positive analytic semigroup counterexamples and conclude that R-sectoriality is not sufficient for SMR on scalar `L^q`.

That inference is **not justified** by the source theorem presently in hand. The concavification equivalence used in the 2026 paper is proved for the particular positive multiplication/diagonal setting (and its stated Banach-function-space extensions), not for every positive analytic semigroup on scalar `L^q`. Fackler's general positive semigroups therefore cannot be inserted into that proposition without a new theorem.

This route is explicitly **FAILED / OPEN**, not a counterexample.

### 7.3 Use only one scale or the ambient `(S_p)` condition

The September 2026 paper proves that the interval-kernel and exponential-kernel formulations of its ambient-space condition `(S_p)` are equivalent, and shows necessity using two canonical test operators. But `(S_p)` is a condition on the Banach-space geometry; on scalar `L^q` that ambient geometry is fixed. Problem 2 asks what distinguishes one sectorial operator A from another on the same scalar `L^q`.

The dual formula (6.4) shows the missing information is cross-scale/operator-specific: arbitrary values of `F(t,s)` couple many time differences `t-s` simultaneously. A one-scale test can be necessary evidence, but it does not encode this full triangular interaction.

### 7.4 Precise remaining attack suggested by the failure

The most concrete next falsification route is now:

> **Positive-semigroup concavification test.** Determine whether there is even a one-sided theorem, for a positive analytic semigroup generator A on scalar `L^q`, comparing stochastic maximal regularity of A on `L^q` with deterministic maximal regularity of a canonically induced operator on the 2-concavification `L^{q/2}`.

If such a theorem extends beyond the diagonal model, Fackler's `p`-dependent consistent semigroups become a possible source of an exact scalar-`L^q` counterexample to the conjectural R-sectorial sufficiency (5.2). If no such inequality can hold, an explicit failure will identify exactly which off-diagonal/triangular interaction is invisible to concavification.

This is the next mathematical attack; it is not yet a theorem.
