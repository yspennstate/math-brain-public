# 4. `P11-LYONS-BOUNDARY-CLOSURE-01` — PROVED

## Theorem

Let

\[
\mathcal K_3=\{K=K^*:0\le K\le I\}
\]

and let `G` be the dense subset of strict contractions `0<K<I` whose three off-diagonal entries are all nonzero. Then the following are equivalent:

1. `Phi(K)>=0` for every `K in G`.
2. The entropy of the three-site determinantal law is concave on all of `mathcal K_3`.

Consequently, no additional boundary-Hessian sign theorem is needed for the three-site milestone. The `g_ij=0`, `d=0`, singular-`K`, singular-`I-K`, and zero-atom boundary cases are genuine closure cases, not holes in X095's reduction.

## Proof

For `0<K<I`, put `L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0`. The exact atom formula

\[
p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S
\]

shows that every `p_S` is strictly positive. Hence the entropy

\[
H(K)=-\sum_S p_S(K)\log p_S(K)
\]

is `C^infinity` on the strict interior.

On `G`, all three edges are nonzero. X077 proves `M>0`, and X095 proves that `Phi>=0` is equivalent to `D^2H(K)[A,A]<=0` for every Hermitian affine velocity `A`. Thus assumption 1 gives a negative-semidefinite Hessian throughout `G`.

The set `G` is dense in the strict interior: an arbitrarily small generic Hermitian perturbation changes zero off-diagonal entries while preserving `0<K<I`, since strict positivity of `K` and `I-K` is open. The Hessian of `H` is continuous on the strict interior. Therefore its quadratic form is nonpositive at every strict interior point, including internal zero-edge or `d=0` limits.

The strict interior is convex. Hence for strict `K_0,K_1`,

\[
h(t)=H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)
\]

has `h''(t)<=0`, so `H` is concave on the strict interior.

Now take arbitrary boundary points `K_0,K_1 in mathcal K_3` and define

\[
K_i^{(\varepsilon)}=(1-\varepsilon)K_i+\varepsilon I/2.
\]

These are strict positive contractions. Interior concavity gives the desired midpoint (indeed all-`t`) inequality for `K_0^{(epsilon)},K_1^{(epsilon)}`. Every atom probability is a polynomial in the matrix entries of `K`, and `-x log x` extends continuously to `x=0` by value zero, so `H` is continuous on `mathcal K_3`. Letting `epsilon downarrow0` gives concavity on the full closed body.

Conversely, if `Phi(K)<0` at one point `K in G`, X095 supplies a Hermitian `A` with `D^2H(K)[A,A]>0`. For sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, both `K+-epsilon A` remain strict positive contractions, and Taylor's theorem gives

\[
H(K+\varepsilon A)+H(K-\varepsilon A)-2H(K)>0,
\]

contradicting midpoint concavity. This proves equivalence. ∎

### Consequence for interval optimization

A future global interval/SOS certificate may work on any compact exhaustion of the strict all-edge atom domain and prove uniform nonnegativity there; boundary boxes may be discharged by limiting estimates rather than by evaluating an ill-conditioned `C^{-1}` at `d=0`. This is a proof reduction, not permission to omit boundary analysis silently: the closure argument above is the boundary analysis.

# 6. `P11-LYONS-ADJUGATE-FEATURE-01` — PROVED

## Theorem

With `M=diag(c_1,c_2,c_3)+tau K` and `d=det M>0`, define the frozen set function `R` by `(0.1)`. Then

\[
\boxed{\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}R(S)E_S=M^{-1}.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}R(S)F_S=I.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The identity is exact for arbitrary complex Hermitian `K`; it does not assume a real loop phase.

## Proof

Write `D=diag(c_1,c_2,c_3)`. Holding `c_i` and `tau` fixed, the determinant of `M=D+tau K` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
d
={}&c_1c_2c_3
+\tau\left(c_2c_3K_{11}+c_1c_3K_{22}+c_1c_2K_{33}\right)\\
&+\tau^2\left(c_1\det K_{23}+c_2\det K_{13}+c_3\det K_{12}\right)
+\tau^3\det K.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Differentiate `(6.3)` with respect to `K`. Since

\[
D_K\det(D+\tau K)[A]
=\tau\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(M)A),
\]

we obtain

\[
\operatorname{adj}(M)
=
\operatorname{diag}(c_2c_3,c_1c_3,c_1c_2)
+\tau\left(c_1\nabla\det K_{23}+c_2\nabla\det K_{13}+c_3\nabla\det K_{12}\right)
+\tau^2\operatorname{adj}(K).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now use the DPP inclusion identities

\[
K_{ii}=\sum_{S\ni i}p_S,
\quad
\det K_{jk}=\sum_{S\supseteq\{j,k\}}p_S,
\quad
\det K=p_{123}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Differentiating them gives

\[
E_{ii}=\sum_{S\ni i}E_S,
\quad
\nabla\det K_{jk}=\sum_{S\supseteq\{j,k\}}E_S,
\quad
\operatorname{adj}(K)=E_{123}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Substitute `(6.6)` into `(6.4)` and collect the coefficient of each `E_S`. The coefficient is precisely `d R(S)`. Dividing by `d` proves `(6.1)`, and congruence by `M^{1/2}` proves `(6.2)`. ∎
