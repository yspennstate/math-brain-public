## 5.1 A zero-field face

\tag{5.1}

Among the seven nonempty atom gradients `E_S`, two types occur.

### Subsets containing site 1

\[
K-I_{S^c}=0\oplus Q_S

for a two-by-two matrix `Q_S`. Hence
\[
\operatorname{adj}(K-I_{S^c})
=\det(Q_S)E_{11}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus all four gradients with `1 in S` are scalar multiples of `E_11`. Their scalars are nonzero because the corresponding two-site atom masses of the strict block `B` are positive.

### Subsets excluding site 1

The three nonempty possibilities are `{2}`, `{3}`, and `{2,3}`. Their lower `2x2` blocks are exactly the gradients of the three nonempty atom probabilities of the strict connected two-site DPP `P_B`.

Writing the lower block of `B` as
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\\bar z&b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad z\ne0,
\]
those three gradients are generated by
\[
E_{22}-\operatorname{adj}B,
\qquad
E_{33}-\operatorname{adj}B,
\qquad
\operatorname{adj}B.
\tag{5.3}
\]
If
\[
\alpha(E_{22}-\operatorname{adj}B)
+\beta(E_{33}-\operatorname{adj}B)
+\gamma\operatorname{adj}B=0,
\]
then the off-diagonal entry, equal to `-(gamma-alpha-beta)z`, forces
\[
\gamma-\alpha-\beta=0.
\]
The two diagonal entries then give `alpha=beta=0`, hence `gamma=0`. Therefore these three lower-block gradients are linearly independent.

Combining `(5.2)` and `(5.3)`,
\[
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{span}\{E_S:S\ne\varnothing\}=4.}
\tag{5.4}

\boxed{\overline\Phi_R(0,x_2,x_3)=0.}
