# 5. P11-LYONS-RARE-SITE-KL-NORMAL-FORM-05

## 5.1 Statement

Let

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}u&c\\ \bar c&v\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<B<I_2,
\]

be a **connected** strict two-site kernel (`c!=0`). Let `a>0` and `z in C^2\setminus{0}` satisfy

\[
B^-:=B-\frac{zz^*}{a}>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `B^-<=B<I`, this is automatically a strict contraction once positive. For sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, define

\[
K_\varepsilon=
\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon a&\sqrt\varepsilon z^*\\
\sqrt\varepsilon z&B
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then `0<K_epsilon<I`. For the scaled physical direction

\[
A_\varepsilon(\alpha,w,C)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt\varepsilon\,\alpha&w^*\\
w&C
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{5.3}
\]

with real `alpha`, `w in C^2`, and Hermitian `C`, one has uniformly on compact nondegenerate parameter families

\[
\boxed{
D^2H_3(K_\varepsilon)[A_\varepsilon,A_\varepsilon]
\longrightarrow
D^2H_2(B)[C,C]
-D^2F_B(a,z)[(\alpha,w),(\alpha,w)].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Here

\[
\boxed{
F_B(a,z)=a\log a+a\,D\!\left(P^{B-zz^*/a}\middle\|P^B\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Moreover `F_B` is **strictly convex in the five real variables `(a,z)`** under `c!=0,z!=0`. Hence the limiting quadratic form in (5.4) is strictly negative definite, because the connected strict two-site entropy Hessian is negative definite on all four real Hermitian `C` directions.

Consequently, on every compact family with quantitative margins in (5.1), `0<B<I`, `|c|>0`, and `|z|>0`, there is `epsilon_0>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
D^2H_3(K_\varepsilon)[A,A]<0
\quad\text{for every nonzero Hermitian }A,
\quad0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This controls all nine real directions.

## 5.2 Exact atom split

For `T subset {2,3}` put

\[
D_T=B-I_{T^c},\qquad
s_T=(-1)^{2-|T|},\qquad
q_T=s_T\det D_T=P^B(T)>0.
\]

Set

\[
r_T=s_T\{a\det D_T-z^*\operatorname{adj}(D_T)z\}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Exact block determinant expansion in the DPP atom formula gives

\[
\boxed{
P^{K_\varepsilon}(\{1\}\cup T)=\varepsilon r_T,
\qquad
P^{K_\varepsilon}(T)=q_T-\varepsilon r_T.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

The rank-one determinant lemma gives

\[
\det\left(D_T-\frac{zz^*}{a}\right)
=\det D_T-\frac1a z^*\operatorname{adj}(D_T)z,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{r_T=aP^{B^-}(T),\qquad \sum_T r_T=a.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus, conditioned on occupation of the rare site, the remaining two coordinates have **exactly** the DPP law `P^{B^-}`.

## 5.3 Exact entropy expansion

With `h(t)=-t log t`, (5.8) gives

\[
H_3(K_\varepsilon)=\sum_T\{h(\varepsilon r_T)+h(q_T-\varepsilon r_T)\}.
\]

The rare term is exact:

\[
h(\varepsilon r_T)=-\varepsilon r_T\log\varepsilon-\varepsilon r_T\log r_T.
\]

Taylor-expand only the regular atoms. Using `sum r_T=a`,

\[
\boxed{
H_3(K_\varepsilon)
=H_2(B)+\varepsilon\{a(1-\log\varepsilon)-F_B(a,z)\}
+\varepsilon^2R_\varepsilon(a,z,B).
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

On compact families where the four `q_T` and four `P^{B^-}(T)` are bounded below, `R_epsilon` and its first two parameter derivatives are uniformly bounded. All singular `log epsilon` terms have been extracted before differentiating.

## 5.4 Affine KL extension

Define

\[
A_T=s_T\operatorname{adj}(D_T).
\tag{5.11}
\]

Then

\[
\sum_TA_T=0,
\qquad
A_T=q_TD_T^{-1}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

For Hermitian `X` in the open convex set where the following probabilities are positive, define

\[
P_T(X)=q_T-\operatorname{tr}(A_TX),
\qquad
\psi_B(X)=\sum_TP_T(X)\log\frac{P_T(X)}{q_T}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

`P(X)` is an affine probability vector because of `sum A_T=0`. It need not be a physical DPP for arbitrary rank-two `X`; this is only an affine extension. At the physical rank-one point `X=zz^*/a` it equals `P^{B^-}`. Relative entropy is convex in its first argument, so

\[
D^2\psi_B(X)[Y,Y]
=\sum_T\frac{\operatorname{tr}(A_TY)^2}{P_T(X)}\ge0.
\tag{5.14}
\]

## 5.5 The KL gradient is PSD on physical PSD points

Put

\[
\rho_T=\frac{P_T(X)}{q_T}=1-\operatorname{tr}(D_T^{-1}X),
\qquad f_T=\log\rho_T.
\]

If `j notin T`, then `D_{T union {j}}=D_T+e_je_j^*`. The matrix determinant lemma gives

\[
1+e_j^*D_T^{-1}e_j
=\frac{\det D_{T\cup\{j\}}}{\det D_T}
=-\frac{q_{T\cup\{j\}}}{q_T}<0.
\]

Sherman-Morrison therefore yields

\[
D_{T\cup\{j\}}^{-1}-D_T^{-1}
=-\frac{D_T^{-1}e_je_j^*D_T^{-1}}
{1+e_j^*D_T^{-1}e_j}\succeq0.
\tag{5.15}
\]

For `X>=0`,

\[
\rho_{T\cup\{j\}}\le\rho_T,
\qquad f_{T\cup\{j\}}\le f_T.
\tag{5.16}
\]

The gradient is

\[
G(X):=\nabla_X\psi_B(X)=-\sum_TA_Tf_T.
\tag{5.17}
\]

Write the four logs as `f_00,f_10,f_01,f_11` and put

\[
\Delta=f_{00}-f_{10}-f_{01}+f_{11}.
\]

A direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{11}&=(1-v)(f_{00}-f_{10})+v(f_{01}-f_{11}),\\
G_{22}&=(1-u)(f_{00}-f_{01})+u(f_{10}-f_{11}),\\
G_{12}&=c\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.18}
\]

All four edge differences are nonnegative. If `Delta>=0`, then

\[
G_{11}\ge(1-v)\Delta,
\qquad G_{22}\ge(1-u)\Delta,
\]

and `I-B>0` gives `|c|^2<(1-u)(1-v)`, hence `det G>=0` (strict for `Delta>0`). If `Delta<=0`, write `delta=-Delta`; then

\[
G_{11}\ge v\delta,
\qquad G_{22}\ge u\delta,
\]

and `B>0` gives `|c|^2<uv`, again `det G>=0` (strict for `delta>0`). Thus

\[
\boxed{G(X)\succeq0.}
\tag{5.19}
\]

For the physical rank-one point `X=zz^*/a`, connectedness and `z!=0` make (5.19) strict. In the only delicate case `Delta=0`, `G` is diagonal. If `G_11=0`, both likelihood-ratio drops along site 1 vanish. Equality in (5.15) at rank one gives

\[
e_1^*D_{00}^{-1}z=0,
\qquad e_1^*D_{01}^{-1}z=0.
\]

Up to nonzero scalar denominators these row vectors are `(v-1,-c)` and `(v,-c)`, linearly independent when `c!=0`; hence `z=0`, contradiction. Similarly for `G_22`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{c\ne0,\ z\ne0\Longrightarrow G(zz^*/a)>0.}
\tag{5.20}
\]

## 5.6 Perspective Hessian identity

At the physical point let `X=zz^*/a`, so

\[
F_B(a,z)=a\log a+a\psi_B(X).
\]

Along `a(t)=a+t alpha`, `z(t)=z+tw`, the identity `a(t)X(t)=z(t)z(t)^*` gives

\[
aX''+2\alpha X'=2ww^*.
\tag{5.21}
\]

Differentiating the perspective gives the exact real Hessian formula

\[
\boxed{
D^2F_B(a,z)[(\alpha,w)]
=\frac{\alpha^2}{a}
+aD^2\psi_B(X)[X',X']
+2w^*G(X)w.
}
\tag{5.22}
\]

Every term is nonnegative by (5.14) and (5.19); under `c!=0,z!=0`, the last term is positive for every nonzero `w`, while the first is positive for nonzero `alpha`. Hence

\[
\boxed{D^2F_B(a,z)>0\quad\text{on every nonzero }(\alpha,w).}
\tag{5.23}
\]

This is the decisive sign: the three formerly singular X095 modes are the Hessian of a strictly convex KL perspective.

## 5.7 Passage to the full Hessian

For fixed epsilon, `(a,z,B) -> K_epsilon` is affine after the fixed coordinate scalings `epsilon`, `sqrt(epsilon)`, `1`. Thus the physical direction (5.3) corresponds to parameter direction

\[
(\alpha/\sqrt\varepsilon,
\ w/\sqrt\varepsilon,
\ C).
\]

Apply this vector twice to (5.10). The `a(1-log epsilon)` term is affine. `F_B` cross derivatives involving `C` are multiplied by `sqrt(epsilon)` or `epsilon`; the twice-differentiated remainder is `O(epsilon)` in the scaled operator norm on compact parameter sets. This proves (5.4) uniformly.

The independent two-site audit `agents/018.md` proves full two-site entropy concavity. At a connected strict two-site base the Hessian is actually negative definite in every nonzero Hermitian direction: definite directions have an explicit strict negative curvature bound; for `det C<=0`, the exact two-site identity is strict except for the zero-score phase degeneracy at a diagonal base, excluded by `c!=0`. Thus connected strict `B` has a compact negative Hessian gap.

Together with (5.23), the limit in (5.4) has a negative gap. Uniform convergence proves (5.6). This completes the theorem-sized derivation.

# 7. B7 — the `q -> 0` shape limit is exactly the C019 one-atom rank-two boundary

At `q=0` with all `0<u_i<1`,

\[
\det R=qu_1u_2u_3=0,
\]

while every two-by-two principal minor of `R` equals `u_i>0`. Thus `R` is PSD of rank exactly two. Let `n` be a unit null vector and let the nonzero eigenvalues be `lambda_1,lambda_2`. Then

\[
\operatorname{adj}R=\lambda_1\lambda_2nn^*.
\]

The diagonal cofactors are `u_i`, so

\[
u_i=\lambda_1\lambda_2|n_i|^2.
\]

Taking the trace of the adjugate gives `lambda_1 lambda_2=u_1+u_2+u_3`, hence

\[
\boxed{|n_i|^2=\frac{u_i}{u_1+u_2+u_3}>0.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

So the null vector has all three measured coordinates nonzero.

For strict external fields, `L=D_ell^{1/2}RD_ell^{1/2}` has the same rank and null vector proportional to `D_ell^{-1/2}n`. The boundary kernel `K=L(I+L)^{-1}` has one simple zero eigenvalue and two eigenvalues strictly between zero and one. In the L-ensemble every proper principal minor is positive while `det L=0`, so **exactly the full atom vanishes** and the other seven atoms stay positive.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q=0,\ 0<u_i<1,\ 0<x_i<1
\Longrightarrow
\text{the C019 simple-zero-eigenvalue stratum with full-support null vector.}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is useful but not a universal collar proof. C019's normal form applies, and on compact `u,x` sub-strata (7.1) gives a uniform lower bound on all null-vector coordinates. Away from C019's explicitly solved near-scalar tangent family, however, the sign of the four-dimensional **rank-two tangent-face Hessian** remains the unresolved input. B7 therefore converts the `tau -> infinity` singularity into a lower-dimensional rank-two face problem; it does not declare the whole `q=0` face solved.

For global certification the boundary division is now sharper:

- external-field faces: proved one-sided full-Hessian collars on nondegenerate shape strata by Section 6;
- `q=0`: C019 normal form plus a still-needed tangent-face certificate;
