## 1. Exact model, conditioning, and theorem

Let the ambient row dimension be \(m\). After fixing the apex, choose two distinct
\((m-1)\)-subsets \(S,T\) from the remaining iid rows and define
\[
D=m-|S\cap T|,\qquad
A=S\cap T,\qquad
B=S\setminus T,\qquad
C=T\setminus S.
\]
Then
\[
|A|=m-D,\qquad |B|=|C|=D-1.
\]

For the realized shared block \(A\), define
\[
E_A=\operatorname{span}(A)^\perp,\qquad \dim E_A=D,
\]
and choose an isometry
\[
Q_A:\mathbb R^D\to E_A\subset\mathbb R^m.
\]

If \(X\) is one original iid-coordinate row, then conditional on \(A\) a projected private row is
\[
Y=Q_A^\top X.
\]
Its coordinates are generally dependent. This dependence is preserved throughout.

The two residual matrices
\[
U=BQ_A,\qquad V=CQ_A
\]
are conditionally independent \((D-1)\times D\) matrices whose rows are iid copies of \(Y\).
Let \(v,w\in S^{D-1}\) be their unit null directions. Then
\[
u_S=Q_Av,\qquad u_T=Q_Aw,\qquad
\rho_{ST}^2=(u_S\cdot u_T)^2=(v\cdot w)^2.
\]

For the active full theorem, the original coordinate law \(\xi\) is centered, variance one,
has a Lebesgue density bounded by \(K\), and is subgaussian with \(\psi_2\)-norm bounded by
\(\kappa\).

The target for every ordinary stratum \(D\ge3\) is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[(1-\rho_{ST}^2)^{-1/2}\mid D\right]
\le 1+\frac{C(K,\kappa)}{D}.
}
\tag{P14-FULL}
\]

The singular strata \(D\le2\) remain covered by the existing high-overlap fallback in the
fixed-apex second-moment argument.

## 2. What was already proved before this run

### 2.1 Shared-row conditional reduction

Conditional on \(A\), the private blocks \(B,C\) are independent, hence so are \(v,w\). If
\[
M_A=\mathbb E[vv^\top\mid A],
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[(v\cdot w)^2\mid A]=\operatorname{tr}(M_A^2).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is conditional independence only. Unconditionally the two null lines remain dependent
through the common random subspace \(E_A\).

### 2.2 High-correlation edge

P14-EDGE proved, uniformly in the realized full-rank shared block,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\{1-(v\cdot w)^2\le\varepsilon\mid A\}
\le
\big[C(K,\kappa)\sqrt{\varepsilon\log(e/\varepsilon)}\big]^{D-1},
\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le\tfrac12.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

The proof conditions on \(A\) and the first private block, making \(v\) deterministic while
the second private block stays fresh. Near-parallelity implies
\[
\|Vv\|_2\le \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|V\|_{\rm op}.

The scalar coordinates of \(Vv\) are independent projections
\[
\langle X_i,Q_Av\rangle
\]
in the original product-coordinate space, so bounded one-dimensional marginal densities
give a \((D-1)\)-dimensional Euclidean small-ball estimate. A subgaussian independent-row
operator-norm bound for \(V\) supplies the logarithmic factor.

P12 independently audited P14-EDGE in its 13:15 report and found the conditioning and
density/norm inputs valid.

Integrating (2.2) gives

## 4. New theorem P14-ANG-\(2+\delta\): one-step angular smoothing below fourth moment

Let
\[
X=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m)
\]
have iid coordinates satisfying
\[
\mathbb E\xi=0,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^2=1,\qquad
\mathbb E|\xi|^{2+\delta}\le B
\tag{4.1}
\]
for one fixed \(\delta>0\).

Set
\[
\delta_0=\min(\delta,2),\qquad
p=1+\frac{\delta_0}{2}\in(1,2],
\]
and
\[
\beta
=
\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^2
=
\left(\frac{\delta_0}{2+\delta_0}\right)^2
>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let \(E\subset\mathbb R^m\) be a deterministic \(k\)-dimensional subspace with projector
\(P=P_E\), and assume \(PX\ne0\) almost surely. Define
\[
U_E=\frac{PX}{\|PX\|},\qquad
R_E=\mathbb E[U_EU_E^\top].
\]

### Proposition P14-ANG-\(2+\delta\) — PROVED

There are \(C=C(B,\delta)<\infty\) and \(k_0(B,\delta)\) such that
\[
\boxed{
R_E
\succeq
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}\,P_E,
\qquad k\ge k_0.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The estimate is uniform in the ambient dimension and in the orientation of \(E\).

For \(\delta\ge2\), \(\beta=1/4\), recovering P24-ANG4.

### Proof

Fix a unit vector \(q\in E\) and put
\[
Z=(q^\top X)^2,\qquad
S=\|PX\|^2=X^\top PX.
\]
Then
\[
q^\top R_Eq
=
\mathbb E\frac{Z}{S},
\qquad
\mathbb EZ=1,
\qquad
\mathbb ES=k.
\tag{4.4}
\]

We first prove the moment bound
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|S-k|^p\le C_{B,\delta}\,k.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Decompose
\[
S-k
=
D_P+O_P,
\]
where
\[
D_P
=
\sum_iP_{ii}(\xi_i^2-1),
\qquad
O_P
=
2\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}\xi_i\xi_j.
\]

Because \(1<p\le2\), von Bahr--Esseen applies to the independent centered summands in
\(D_P\):
\[
\mathbb E|D_P|^p
\le
C_p\sum_iP_{ii}^p\,
\mathbb E|\xi^2-1|^p.
\]
Since \(2p=2+\delta_0\le2+\delta\),
\[
\mathbb E|\xi^2-1|^p\le C(B,\delta),
\]
and because \(0\le P_{ii}\le1\),
\[
\sum_iP_{ii}^p\le\sum_iP_{ii}=k.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E|D_P|^p\le Ck.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For the off-diagonal chaos, only second moments are needed:
\[
\mathbb E O_P^2
=
4\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2
\le
2\operatorname{tr}(P^2)
=
2k.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Indeed every mixed pair vanishes by centering, and the surviving identical-pair terms use
only \(\mathbb E\xi_i^2\xi_j^2=1\).

Since \(p\le2\),
\[
\mathbb E|O_P|^p
\le
(\mathbb E O_P^2)^{p/2}
\le Ck^{p/2}
\le Ck.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Equations (4.6)--(4.8) prove (4.5).

Next, by Rosenthal's inequality at exponent \(2p=2+\delta_0>2\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\|q\|=1}
\mathbb E|q^\top X|^{2p}
\le C(B,\delta).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
Indeed
\[
\sum_i|q_i|^{2p}\le1,\qquad
\sum_iq_i^2=1.
\]

Choose
\[
a=\frac{2p-1}{p^2},
\qquad
t=k^a.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Then
\[
\frac1p<a<1,
\]
and
\[
1-a
=
(p-1)\left(a-\frac1p\right)
=
\frac{(p-1)^2}{p^2}
=
\beta.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Let
\[
G=\{S\le k+t\}.
\]
Markov and (4.5) give
\[
\Pr(G^c)
\le
Ck\,t^{-p}.
\tag{4.12}
\]
Hölder with exponent \(p\), followed by (4.9), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_{G^c}]
&\le
(\mathbb EZ^p)^{1/p}
\Pr(G^c)^{(p-1)/p}\\
&\le
C
k^{(p-1)/p}
t^{-(p-1)}
=
Ck^{-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[Z\mathbf1_G]\ge1-Ck^{-\beta}.
\]
On \(G\), \(S\le k+k^a\), hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^\top R_Eq
&\ge
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k+k^a}\\
&=
\frac1k
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{1+k^{a-1}}\\
&\ge
\frac{1-C'k^{-\beta}}{k},
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(a-1=-\beta\). This proves (4.3).

## 5. New theorem P14-HYPERPLANE-\(2+\delta\): sequential smoothing

Let \(E_D=E\) be a deterministic \(D\)-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb R^m\).
Let \(X_D,\ldots,X_2\) be independent copies of \(X\), and recursively set
\[
E_{k-1}=E_k\cap X_k^\perp.
\]
Assume the law is such that the dimension drops by one almost surely; bounded density is more
than enough. Let \(P_k\) be the projector onto \(E_k\). Then
\[
u_k=\frac{P_kX_k}{\|P_kX_k\|},
\qquad
P_{k-1}=P_k-u_ku_k^\top.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Conditional on the past, \(E_k\) is deterministic and \(X_k\) is a fresh original product
vector. By P14-ANG-\(2+\delta\),
\[
\mathbb E[u_ku_k^\top\mid E_k]
\succeq
\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}P_k.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[P_{k-1}\mid E_k]
\preceq
\left(1-\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}\right)P_k.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Iterating down to a fixed \(k_0\),
\[
\mathbb E P_{k_0}
\preceq
\prod_{k=k_0+1}^D
\left(1-\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}\right)P_E.
\]
Now
\[
1-\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}
=
\frac{k-1}{k}
\left(
1+\frac{Ck^{-\beta}}{k-1}
\right),
\]
so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\prod_{k=k_0+1}^D
\left(1-\frac{1-Ck^{-\beta}}{k}\right)
&=
\frac{k_0}{D}
\prod_{k=k_0+1}^D
\left(
1+\frac{Ck^{-\beta}}{k-1}
\right)\\
&\le
\frac{k_0}{D}
\exp\left(
C\sum_{k>k_0}k^{-1-\beta}
\right)\\
&\le
\frac{C_{B,\delta}}{D}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since the final line lies inside \(E_{k_0}\),
\[
P_1\preceq P_{k_0}
\]
pathwise. Hence:

### Theorem P14-HYPERPLANE-\(2+\delta\) — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EP_1
\preceq
\frac{C_{B,\delta}}{D}P_E.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This subsumes P24-HYPERPLANE-SMOOTHING.

## 8. Sharpness and low-dimensional checks

### 8.1 Gaussian benchmark

If the residual lines are Haar in \(\mathbb R^D\),
\[
\mathbb E\rho^2=\frac1D.
\]
So the order \(D^{-1}\) in (6.5) is sharp.

The full Gaussian kernel benchmark is
\[
\mathbb E(1-\rho^2)^{-1/2}
=
\frac{\Gamma(D/2)\Gamma((D-2)/2)}
{\Gamma((D-1)/2)^2}
=
1+\Theta(D^{-1}).
\]
So the full theorem has the correct order.

### 8.2 Exact rotationality is false

The earlier \(D=2\) two-point example gives a nonzero off-diagonal angular covariance.
After arbitrarily small Gaussian smoothing it becomes a bounded-density subgaussian
counterexample to exact one-step isotropy.

Thus the theorem cannot be strengthened to
\[
M_A=I_D/D
\]
for arbitrary non-Gaussian product laws.

### 8.3 Why \(2+\delta\) appears naturally in the new proof

The diagonal fluctuation
\[
\sum_iP_{ii}(\xi_i^2-1)
\]
has moment \(p=1+\delta/2>1\). The positivity
\[
\beta=\left(\frac{p-1}{p}\right)^2>0
\]
is exactly what makes
\[
\sum_k\frac{k^{-\beta}}{k}<\infty.
\]

At the pure second-moment endpoint \(\delta=0\), this argument gives \(\beta=0\) and loses
summability. This is a proof obstruction, not a counterexample to the finite-variance theorem.

## 9. Literature assumptions checked

The diagonal-moment step uses the von Bahr--Esseen inequality for independent centered
summands at exponent \(1\le p\le2\), exactly the range of the 1965 von Bahr--Esseen theorem.

The directional \(2+\delta\) moment uses Rosenthal's inequality for sums of independent
mean-zero variables at exponent \(>2\).

Neither theorem requires symmetry of the coordinate law.

The edge theorem's product-marginal density input remains Livshyts--Paouris--Pivovarov,
while its matrix-norm input remains the standard independent-isotropic-subgaussian-row
operator-norm theorem.

Nguyen--Vu's fixed-vector normal approximation is still not used; its symmetry assumption
therefore causes no issue.
