## 4. Move #9 — RANDOMIZE: the physical star has a much simpler binary common-environment representation

\tag{4.1}

### Proof

\[

\]

\[
F_C(1/3)=\frac73D_C+\frac53(P_C+Q_C+S_C)+T_C.

\[
F_C(0)+3F_C(1/3)
=8D_C+6(P_C+Q_C+S_C)+4T_C

\]

QED.

Equivalently, after dividing by four, the star transfer is the expectation of the ordinary terminal-triangle transfer under a **single common random environment**

\[
W=0\quad\text{with probability }1/4,
\qquad
W=1/3\quad\text{with probability }3/4.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is not an appeal to probabilistic closure under mixtures; that closure is false in general. The value of (4.1) is that it isolates the entire remaining obstruction as one explicit environmental covariance term.

\[
\alpha=\frac{Z_0}{Z_0+3Z_1},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{3Z_1}{Z_0+3Z_1}.
\tag{4.3}

Then the physical marginal law on old edges is exactly

\[
\mu_\star=\alpha\mu_0+\beta\mu_1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For an old-edge pair `e,f`, write

\[
\Gamma_i(e,f)=p_e^{(i)}p_f^{(i)}-p_{ef}^{(i)}.

A direct expansion of (4.4) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\star(e,f)
=\alpha\Gamma_0(e,f)+\beta\Gamma_1(e,f)
-\alpha\beta
\bigl(p_e^{(0)}-p_e^{(1)}\bigr)
\bigl(p_f^{(0)}-p_f^{(1)}\bigr).

\tag{4.5}

Thus endpoint Rayleigh inequalities are not enough: the common environment makes both old-edge marginals move in the same direction and creates the positive-correlation debt in the final term.

\boxed{

The complete order-nine positive-weight dependency makes every integrand nonnegative. Therefore the unresolved old-edge/old-edge degree-three-apex problem is now equivalently a quantitative **response-product inequality**:
