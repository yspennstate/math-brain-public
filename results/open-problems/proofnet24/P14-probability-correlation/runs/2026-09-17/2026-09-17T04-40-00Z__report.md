## 4. Exact stuck blocker

Let `v` be a degree-three vertex with neighbors `x,y,z`, and let `H=G-v`. For an old-edge constraint `C` (`empty`, `e`, `f`, or `ef`), split forests of `H` into the five terminal partition states

`D=x|y|z`, `P=xy|z`, `Q=xz|y`, `S=yz|x`, `T=xyz`.

Let `F_C(u,v,w)` be the forest polynomial after adjoining the three virtual triangle edges on `x,y,z` with weights `u,v,w`. The preceding exact star--mesh calculation gives, at `a=(1/4,1/4,1/4)`,

\[
A_C:=F_C(a),\qquad K_C:=D_C,
\]

and the physical unit star polynomial, divided by the harmless positive factor four, is

\[
Q_C=A_C+\frac1{16}K_C.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For an old-edge pair `e,f`, define

\[
\mathcal C_{A,K}
=A_eK_f+K_eA_f-AK_{ef}-KA_{ef}.
\]

The exact blocking statement is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(e,f)
=16\Delta_A(e,f)+\mathcal C_{A,K}(e,f)+\frac1{16}\Delta_K(e,f)\ge0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

With the Cocks scope correction above, both diagonal terms are genuinely known nonnegative: `A` is a positive-weight forest polynomial on at most nine vertices (with harmless parallel-edge handling), and `K` is a contraction polynomial on at most seven vertices. The obstruction is therefore the **mixed polarization** `mathcal C_{A,K}`.

This is the exact formula that a degree-three elimination proof must control. It is not enough to cite Lorentzianity, complete log concavity, or endpoint Rayleigh signs.

## 7. Move #18 — say it in another language: a response-cone problem

The reverse-Cauchy inequality was too coarse because it tried to control the mixed polarization using only the two diagonal gaps. The star defect has more structure.

The terminal polynomial has degree at most two in `u,v,w`, no `uvw` term, and every mixed second derivative is exactly the separate-terminal polynomial:

\[
\partial_{uv}F_C=\partial_{uw}F_C=\partial_{vw}F_C=K_C.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `a=(1/4,1/4,1/4)` and choose any real vector `d=(d_1,d_2,d_3)` satisfying

\[
|d_i|\le\frac14,
\qquad
 d_1d_2+d_1d_3+d_2d_3=\frac1{16}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then exact quadratic Taylor cancellation gives, for every old-edge constraint `C`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_C=A_C+\frac1{16}K_C
=\frac12\bigl(F_C(a+d)+F_C(a-d)\bigr).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The endpoint terminal weights are nonnegative; zero endpoints are obtained by continuity from positive weights. By the restored Cocks baseline, each endpoint is Rayleigh.

Equation (7.3) turns the physical old-edge law into a two-environment mixture. Let `p_e^+` and `p_e^-` denote endpoint inclusion probabilities. The covariance decomposition is

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_Q(e,f)
=\lambda\operatorname{Cov}_+(e,f)
 +(1-\lambda)\operatorname{Cov}_-(e,f)
 +\lambda(1-\lambda)(p_e^+-p_e^-)(p_f^+-p_f^-),
\tag{7.4}
\]

with the natural partition-function mixture weight `lambda`. The first two terms are nonpositive. Therefore any feasible `d` for which the two marginal responses have opposite signs closes that edge pair.

The response sign is itself linear in `d`. Put `Q_0=A+K/16`, `Q_e=A_e+K_e/16`, and define

\[
R_e=Q_0\,\nabla F_e(a)-Q_e\,\nabla F(a).
\tag{7.5}
\]

A direct expansion of the two quadratic endpoint ratios shows

\[
\operatorname{sgn}(p_e^+-p_e^-)=\operatorname{sgn}(R_e\cdot d).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Hence a degree-three old-edge pair is certified whenever there exists `d` satisfying (7.2) with

\[
(R_e\cdot d)(R_f\cdot d)\le0.
\tag{7.7}
\]

After scaling `x=4d`, the feasible surface is the fixed three-dimensional set

\[
\mathcal S=\{x\in[-1,1]^3:x_1x_2+x_1x_3+x_2x_3=1\}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

So the surviving degree-three obstruction can be restated as a **response-cone geometry problem**: only pairs whose two response linear forms have the same sign on all of `mathcal S` need anything beyond endpoint Rayleigh. This is a much more specific target than the false universal reverse-Cauchy bound.

This is the route to pursue if P14 is reassigned after the mandatory handoff rule; it asks for a geometric classification of the exceptional response pairs, not another generic scalar inequality.

### Why generic real stability / Lorentzian preservation does not close (7.3)

The star defect operator contains a positive mixed second derivative. In two variables the operator

\[
T_h=1+h\partial_u\partial_v,\qquad h>0,
\]

already fails to preserve real stability: `uv` is stable, but

\[
T_h(uv)=uv+h
\]

vanishes at `u=v=i\sqrt h`, with both variables in the upper half-plane. The homogeneous Lorentzian analogue fails just as directly: `uv` is Lorentzian of degree two, whereas `uv+h z^2` has Hessian eigenvalues `1,-1,2h`, hence two positive eigenvalues.

Thus neither real stability nor Lorentzianity supplies a generic closure theorem for the actual positive mixed-derivative defect. Brändén--Huh and Anari--Liu--Oveis Gharan--Vinzant remain valuable shape machinery, but their hypotheses/conclusions do not by themselves imply the needed pairwise sign after this operator. Fang--Ma's 2026 theorem says multi-affine Gårding polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are Rayleigh, but the Gårding hypothesis for the relevant graphic independence polynomial still has to be proved; it cannot be replaced by Lorentzianity.
