# 5. P14-ALLRANK-BOUNDED-CHECKPOINT-31 — PROVED

Fix any integer `1 <= r <= d`.

Expose `d-r` of the future rows among `X_2,...,X_d`. Their orthogonal complement inside `Ran(P)` is a random rank-`r+1` projector R. Conditional on P, this checkpoint is independent of the distinguished original row `X_1`.

There remain `r-1` future original rows, denoted `Y_2,...,Y_r`.

For the distinguished row define

`q_R(U) = R U / ||R U|| = R X_1 / ||R X_1||`.                          (5.1)

The second equality is essential: because `R <= P`, the checkpoint direction is still generated by the same genuinely fresh original product row. No projected-coordinate iid claim is introduced.

Let

`Lambda_R(X_1,Y_2,...,Y_r)`

be the final rank-one projector in `Ran(R)` orthogonal to the projections of these r fresh original rows. Define the all-rank checkpoint block

`K_R^(r) = Lambda_R - R/r`.                                             (5.2)

## 5.1 Symmetry and pathwise bound

The final line depends only on the unordered span of the r projected rows, so `K_R^(r)` is symmetric in

`X_1,Y_2,...,Y_r`.

On the final line its eigenvalue is `(r-1)/r`; on the other r dimensions of `Ran(R)` the eigenvalue is `-1/r`. Therefore

`||K_R^(r)||_F^2 = ((r-1)^2+r)/r^2 = 1-1/r+1/r^2 <= 1`.                (5.3)

Also

`tr K_R^(r) = -1/r`.                                                     (5.4)

Thus **every checkpoint rank admits a bounded symmetric block observable**. The old rank-three span kernel is exactly the case `r=2`, where the squared Frobenius norm is `3/4`.

## 5.2 First-order block projection equals local raw reveal

For a fixed checkpoint R define

`M_R = E[Lambda_R | R]`,

where all r fresh rows are averaged. Given the first checkpoint direction q, define

`M_{R,q} = E[Lambda_R | R,q]`,

where only the remaining `r-1` fresh rows are averaged.

Define the first-order Hoeffding projection

`h_R^(r)(q) = M_{R,q} - M_R`.                                           (5.5)

The constant `-R/r` in (5.2) cancels in this centered first-order projection.

By the tower property and checkpoint independence,

`M_{P,U} = E_R[ M_{R,q_R(U)} | P,U ]`,

while

`M_P = E_R[ M_R | P ]`.

Hence

`M_{P,U} - M_P = E_R[ h_R^(r)(q_R(U)) | P,U ]`.                        (5.6)

This is an exact all-rank raw-reveal representation.

## 5.3 Sibling anisotropy also collapses at every checkpoint rank

Inside a rank-`r+1` checkpoint, after first direction q, the child anisotropy is

`C_r(F_q) = M_{R,q} - (R-qq^T)/r`.

Because

`M_{R,q} - R/r = E[ K_R^(r) | q,R ]`,

we have

`C_r(F_q) = E[ K_R^(r) | q,R ] + qq^T/r`.                              (5.7)

Insert this into the inherited checkpoint factorization. The checkpoint frame term cancels exactly. Define

`Psi_{d,r}^P(u) = E[ K_R^(r) | P,U=u ] + uu^T/d`.                      (5.8)

Then for two sibling directions u,u',

`E_d^P(u,u') = Psi_{d,r}^P(u) - Psi_{d,r}^P(u')`.                      (5.9)

At `r=2`, (5.8) is the previous rank-three span cancellation. At `r=d`, no earlier checkpoint rows are exposed and `Psi_{d,d}` is exactly the child anisotropy. At `r=1`, the formula reaches the rank-two full-future endpoint.

**Conclusion.** Rank three is not algebraically special for boundedness or checkpoint cancellation. It is special analytically because q=3 is the first residual rank at which the critical coupled inverse powers are subcritical after the correct centering.

# 8. P14-TRACEZERO-SUPPORT-REFINED-31 — PROVED

Let H be an n-dimensional real Hilbert space, `n>=2`. Let V be a random unit vector. Let A(V) be self-adjoint and satisfy

`A(V) V = 0`, and `tr A(V) = 0`.                                       (8.1)

Put

`K = E A(V)`, and `S = E[V V^T]`.

Then `tr K=0`. For `K != 0`, define

`Gamma_V(K)`

`= [ 2 tr(K^2 S) - ((n-2)/(n-1)) E(V^T K V)^2 ] / ||K||_F^2`.        (8.2)

## Theorem

`E ||A-K||_F^2 >= Gamma_V(K) E ||A||_F^2`.                             (8.3)

If K=0, the variance equals `E||A||_F^2`.

## Proof

Let `P_V = I - V V^T`. Since `A=P_V A P_V` and `tr A=0`, only the trace-zero part of the compression of K to `V^perp` contributes to the Frobenius pairing. Define

`B_V = P_V K P_V - [tr(P_V K P_V)/(n-1)] P_V`.                        (8.4)

Then

`||K||_F^2 = E <A,K>_F = E <A,B_V>_F`.                                (8.5)

Because `tr K=0`,

`tr(P_V K P_V) = - V^T K V`.

A direct expansion gives

`||P_V K P_V||_F^2`

`= ||K||_F^2 - 2 ||K V||^2 + (V^T K V)^2`.

Removing the scalar trace component in dimension `n-1` gives

`||B_V||_F^2`

`= ||K||_F^2 - 2 ||K V||^2`

`  + ((n-2)/(n-1)) (V^T K V)^2`.                                     (8.6)

Averaging (8.6) yields

`E||B_V||_F^2 = (1-Gamma_V(K)) ||K||_F^2`.

Cauchy-Schwarz in (8.5) gives

`||K||_F^4 <= E||A||_F^2 E||B_V||_F^2`.

Cancel `||K||_F^2` and subtract from `E||A||_F^2` to obtain (8.3).

The coefficient lies in `[0,1]`. Nonnegativity follows from `(V^T K V)^2 <= ||K V||^2`; the upper bound follows from `E||B_V||^2 >= 0`.

QED.

## 8.1 Generic frame bound

If `S >= lambda I_H` in Loewner order, then

`tr(K^2 S) >= lambda ||K||_F^2`

and

`E(V^T K V)^2 <= E||K V||^2`.

Hence

`Gamma_V(K) >= [n/(n-1)] lambda`.                                     (8.7)

This only gives one harmonic unit.

## 8.2 Haar benchmark

If V is Haar on the unit sphere in n dimensions, then

`S = I/n`,

and for trace-zero K,

`E(V^T K V)^2 = 2 ||K||_F^2 / [n(n+2)]`.

Therefore

`Gamma_Haar(n) = 2 n / [(n-1)(n+2)]`.                                 (8.8)

This is asymptotic to `2/n`, not `1/n`.

# 9. P14-ADJACENT-COERCIVE-CONTRACTION-31 — PROVED

Take the adjacent checkpoint. The parent P has rank `n=d+1`. Let V be one genuinely fresh future direction and put

`R = P - V V^T`,

`A_U(V) = h_R^(d-1)( q_R(U) )`

`       = M_{R,q_R(U)} - M_R`.                                       (9.1)

Both covariance matrices have trace one and are supported in R. Therefore

`tr A_U(V)=0`, and `A_U(V) V=0`.                                      (9.2)

Its V-mean is exactly

`K_U = E_V A_U(V) = M_{P,U} - M_P`.                                  (9.3)

Thus the refined support theorem applies inside the exact adjacent curvature identity.

## 9.1 Uniform finite-variance contraction

The inherited fresh-projection-frame theorem gives, for every frozen rank-n parent,

`E[V V^T | P] >= lambda_n P`,

with a nonasymptotic `lambda_n >= c_xi/n` and an asymptotically Haar-sharp lower bound

`lambda_n >= (1-epsilon_n(xi))/n`, with `epsilon_n(xi) -> 0`.           (9.4)

Using (8.7) in the adjacent checkpoint identity yields, pointwise in every realized parent,

`Q_n(P)`

`<= [1 - n lambda_n/(n-1)] E_V Q_{n-1}(P-VV^T)`.                     (9.5)

In particular,

`Q_n(P)`

`<= [1 - (1-epsilon_n(xi))/(n-1)] E_V Q_{n-1}(P-VV^T)`.              (9.6)

This is a source-faithful multiplicative contraction requiring only the actual fresh original row. It is not sharp enough to solve P14: its harmonic exponent is one.

## 9.2 Haar factor-of-two calibration

If the fresh direction V is Haar in the parent, (8.8) gives

`Q_n(P)`

`<= [1 - 2n/((n-1)(n+2))] E_V Q_{n-1}(P-VV^T)`.                      (9.7)

The factor is

`((n-2)(n+1))/((n-1)(n+2))`.

Its product is exactly

`product_{n=3}^N ((n-2)(n+1))/((n-1)(n+2))`

`= 4/((N-1)(N+2))`.                                                    (9.8)

Thus the refined trace-zero support mechanism alone gives the sharp `N^-2` order in the Haar angular model.

For the fully Gaussian null-line chain the exact raw reveal is

`Q_n^G = 1/[(n-1)n]`,

and the exact adjacent ratio is

`Q_n^G/Q_{n-1}^G = (n-2)/n = 1-2/n`.

So (9.7) recovers the decisive factor-two harmonic mechanism, though not the exact Gaussian constant.

## 9.3 Exact obstruction exposed

The first term in (8.2), `2 tr(K^2 S)`, is already controlled at Haar order by the inherited fresh-frame theorem. The only place the second harmonic unit can be lost is the angular quadratic leakage

`E(V^T K V)^2`.                                                        (9.9)

For Haar V this leakage is one order smaller than the frame term. Under arbitrary finite variance, a generic support theorem cannot force that. The prior sharpness construction for support coercivity achieves only order `1/n`, and generic polynomial finite-variance CLT/OU rates have already been disproved.

This isolates the missing theorem much more narrowly than “prove better concentration.”

Achintya Raya Polavarapu and Manuel Fernandez, *Intermediate Singular Values of Random Matrices under Second-Moment and Anti-Concentration Assumptions*, submitted 2026-08-31.

## 11.2 Livshyts-Paouris-Pivovarov, arXiv:1507.07949

Galyna Livshyts, Grigoris Paouris, Peter Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures*.

If a product density has one-dimensional factors bounded by one, every k-dimensional orthogonal marginal density is bounded by `2^(k/2)`. Scaling a common scalar ceiling L gives `2^(k/2) L^k`.

This is a theorem about the **joint projected density**. It does not imply independence of projected coordinates.
