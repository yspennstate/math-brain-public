Make the change of variables

# 7. `P14-TWOCAP-ENDPOINT-NOGO-44` — PROVED route obstruction

\tag{7.1}

# 9. `P14-COORD-PAIR-SUMMABILITY-44` — PROVED

## 9.1 Coordinate one-step pair likelihood

Let

\[
Y_i=\xi_i^2\ge0,
\qquad
S_q=\sum_{i=1}^qY_i,
\qquad
w_i=\frac{Y_i}{S_q}.
\]

For a continuous nondegenerate scalar law `S_q>0` almost surely. Let

\[
v=\frac{(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_q)}{\sqrt{S_q}}.
\]

Delete the direction `v` from the coordinate parent `I_q`. For the marked pair `{1,2}`, the pair-volume survival is

\[
1-T_q,
\qquad
T_q=w_1+w_2.
\]

The normalized degree-two DPP multiplier is

\[
\boxed{
G_q=\frac{q}{q-2}(1-T_q).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Exchangeability gives

\[
ET_q=\frac2q,
\qquad EG_q=1.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Define the normalized-weight concentration

\[
C_q:=E\sum_{i=1}^q w_i^2.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then

\[
Ew_1^2=\frac{C_q}{q},
\qquad
Ew_1w_2=\frac{1-C_q}{q(q-1)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
ET_q^2
=\frac{2C_q}{q}
+\frac{2(1-C_q)}{q(q-1)},
\tag{9.4}
\]

and exact simplification gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_q)
=\frac{2(q-2)(qC_q-1)}{q^2(q-1)}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
EG_q^2-1
=\frac{2(qC_q-1)}{(q-2)(q-1)}.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

For a Gaussian row, `C_q=3/(q+2)`, and `(9.6)` recovers the Haar value

\[
EG_q^2-1=\frac4{(q-2)(q+2)}.
\]

## 9.2 Laplace summability at pure finite variance

Because

\[
\frac{C_q}{q}
=E\frac{Y_1^2}{S_q^2},
\tag{9.7}
\]

it is enough to prove

\[
\sum_q E\frac{Y_1^2}{S_q^2}<\infty.
\]

Let

\[
\phi(t)=Ee^{-tY},
\qquad
\psi_2(t)=E[Y^2e^{-tY}].
\]

Using

\[
x^{-2}=\int_0^\infty t e^{-tx}\,dt,
\]

Tonelli and independence give the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}E\frac{Y_1^2}{S_q^2}
=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{t\psi_2(t)}{1-\phi(t)}\,dt.
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

Assume only

\[
0<EY=\mu<\infty.
\]

Since `(1-phi(t))/t -> mu` as `t downarrow0`, choose `t_0>0` such that

\[
1-\phi(t)\ge\frac\mu2 t,
\qquad 0<t\le t_0.
\]

Then

\[
\int_0^{t_0}
\frac{t\psi_2(t)}{1-\phi(t)}dt
\le
\frac2\mu\int_0^{t_0}\psi_2(t)dt
=
\frac2\mu E[Y(1-e^{-t_0Y})]
\le2.
\tag{9.9}
\]

For `t>=t_0`, `1-phi(t)>=1-phi(t_0)>0`, while

\[
\int_0^\infty t\psi_2(t)dt
=E\left[Y^2\int_0^\infty t e^{-tY}dt\right]
\le1
\tag{9.10}
\]

(with the `Y=0` contribution equal to zero). Hence the right side of `(9.8)` is finite.

For `q>=4`, `(9.6)` and `qC_q-1<=qC_q` give

\[
EG_q^2-1\le \frac{8C_q}{q}.
\]

The `q=3` term is finite separately. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{q=3}^{\infty}(EG_q^2-1)<\infty
}
\tag{9.11}
\]

under **only** `E xi^2<infinity`.

### Scope warning

This theorem is for a raw fresh row deleting one direction from the **coordinate parent** `I_q`. It cannot be iterated directly down the actual residual chain because after the first deletion the parent is no longer a coordinate subspace and the next normalized projected row is not an iid-coordinate direction. The theorem is a sharp model for the desired adaptive summable defect, not a closure of P14.

# 10. `P14-COORD-HAAR-RATE-NOGO-44` — PROVED counterexample

The summability theorem is genuinely weaker than pointwise Haar fourth-moment control, and that distinction is necessary at pure finite variance.

Construct a smooth symmetric bounded density which, for `|x|` sufficiently large, is proportional to

\[
\frac1{|x|^3(\log|x|)^2},
\tag{10.1}
\]

patch it smoothly and positively on a compact interval, normalize it, and finally rescale to variance one. Then

\[
E\xi^2<\infty,
\qquad E\xi^4=\infty,
\qquad \|f_\xi\|_\infty<\infty.
\]

For `Y=xi^2`, there are constants `c,t_0>0` such that

\[
P\{Y\ge t\}
\ge
\frac{c}{t(\log t)^2},
\qquad t\ge t_0.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Take the coordinate pair mass

\[
T_q=\frac{Y_1+Y_2}{Y_1+\cdots+Y_q}.
\]

On the event

\[
Y_1\ge4q,
\qquad
\sum_{k=2}^qY_k\le2q,
\]

we have `T_q>=2/3`. Since `EY=1`, Markov gives

\[
P\left\{\sum_{k=2}^qY_k\le2q\right\}>\frac12.
\]

This event is independent of `Y_1`, so for large `q`,

\[
\boxed{
ET_q^2
\ge
\frac{c'}{q(\log q)^2}.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

In particular

\[
ET_q^2\not=O(q^{-2}).
\tag{10.4}
\]

Since `(ET_q)^2=4/q^2`, the same example has

\[
EG_q^2-1
\gtrsim
\frac1{q(\log q)^2},
\]

along a tail of large `q`, while `(9.11)` remains finite.

Thus the pure-finite-variance proof cannot demand Gaussian `q^{-2}` one-step fourth-moment accuracy. A **summable but non-Haar** defect is the sharp kind of currency that survives this counterexample.

This is a route-kill for pointwise Haar-scale one-step fourth moments, not a counterexample to the full correlation theorem.
