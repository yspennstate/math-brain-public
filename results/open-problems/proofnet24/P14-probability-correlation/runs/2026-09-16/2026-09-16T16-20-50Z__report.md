## 3. New-method move #1 — change category: two-terminal effective coupling

The useful new category is not “graph with a separator” but a **two-terminal network carrying an effective connection odds**.

Let a graph `H` have terminals `x,y`. Define

- `D(H)` = number of forests in which `x,y` are disconnected;
- `C(H)` = number of forests in which `x,y` are connected;
- `r(H)=C(H)/D(H)`.

`D(H)>=1` because the empty forest disconnects the terminals.

For two edge-disjoint two-terminal networks in parallel, sharing exactly `x,y`, a global forest may connect the terminals through at most one side, so

\[
r(H_1\parallel H_2)=r(H_1)+r(H_2).
\]

For two networks in series, sharing only one articulation vertex, direct counting gives

\[
r(H_1\operatorname{series} H_2)
=\frac{r(H_1)r(H_2)}{1+r(H_1)+r(H_2)}.
\]

A single edge has `r=1`.

This is the correct state variable for a 2-separation: after one side is marginalized, the other side is not at unit weight. It sees a **single virtual separator edge with weight equal to the other side's terminal odds**. That observation identifies exactly why the source unit statement fails to feed a naive 2-cut induction and exactly what stronger invariant would make the induction close.

This is not merely heuristic; the next section proves the exact algebra.

## 4. New theorem: `P14-2CUT-CROSSPAIR-55` — PROVED

### Statement

Let `G=A union B`, with edge-disjoint `A,B` and

\[
V(A)\cap V(B)=\{x,y\}.
\]

First suppose the separator edge `xy` is absent from both sides. For side `i in {A,B}` write `D_i,C_i` for the numbers of side forests with `x,y` disconnected/connected. For an edge `e` of a side write `D_{i,e},C_{i,e}` for the corresponding counts restricted to forests containing `e`.

For `e in E(A)` and `f in E(B)`, the unit Rayleigh numerator factors as

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(e,f)
=(D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e})
 (D_B C_{B,f}-C_B D_{B,f}).
}
\]

Moreover each factor is a unit Rayleigh numerator in the corresponding side augmented by a virtual edge `g=xy`:

\[
D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e}
=\Delta_{A+g}(g,e),
\]

and likewise for `B`.

Therefore, for a 10-vertex graph with a nontrivial 2-cut, every pair lying on opposite sides is source-Rayleigh by the already complete n<=9 certificate.

If the actual graph contains the separator edge `h=xy`, remove it from both sides and treat it separately. The same cross-side factorization holds, and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(h,e)=D_B^2(D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e})\ge0
}
\]

for `e in E(A)`, again by the n<=9 source certificate on `A+xy`.

Hence, **unconditionally**, any violating edge pair in a 10-vertex graph with a 2-cut must lie wholly within one side of that 2-separation. This is a structural narrowing, not yet a proof that a minimal counterexample is 3-connected.

### Proof, separator edge absent

A union of one forest from `A` and one forest from `B` is a forest exactly when the two side forests do not both connect `x` to `y`; otherwise the two independent `x-y` paths form a cycle. Therefore

\[
Z=D_AD_B+C_AD_B+D_AC_B.
\]

For `e in A`,

\[
Z_e=D_{A,e}(D_B+C_B)+C_{A,e}D_B,
\]

and similarly

\[
Z_f=D_{B,f}(D_A+C_A)+C_{B,f}D_A.
\]

For a cross pair,

\[
Z_{ef}=D_{A,e}D_{B,f}+C_{A,e}D_{B,f}+D_{A,e}C_{B,f}.
\]

Expand `Z_e Z_f-Z Z_{ef}`. All terms cancel except

\[
(D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e})
(D_B C_{B,f}-C_B D_{B,f}),
\]

which proves the factorization.

Now add a new virtual edge `g=xy` to `A`. A connected `A`-forest cannot contain `g`, while a disconnected `A`-forest may omit or include it. Thus

\[
Z_{A+g}=C_A+2D_A,
\quad Z_g=D_A,
\]

\[
Z_e=C_{A,e}+2D_{A,e},
\quad Z_{ge}=D_{A,e}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta_{A+g}(g,e)
=Z_gZ_e-Z_{A+g}Z_{ge}
=D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e}.
\]

For a nontrivial 2-cut in a 10-vertex graph, each augmented side has at most 9 vertices. The complete n<=9 unit certificate proves each factor nonnegative, hence proves every cross-side pair nonnegative.

### Proof with actual separator edge present

Remove `h=xy` and call the remaining sides `A,B`. A global forest not using `h` has the previous admissibility rule. If it uses `h`, neither side may connect `x` to `y`, so

\[
Z=2D_AD_B+C_AD_B+D_AC_B.
\]

A direct substitution into the four counts for a cross pair again leaves exactly the same product factorization.

For `(h,e)` with `e in A`,

\[
Z_h=D_AD_B,
\qquad
Z_{he}=D_{A,e}D_B.
\]

Substituting and cancelling gives

\[
\Delta_G(h,e)
=D_B^2(D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e}).
\]

The final factor is the unit Rayleigh numerator of `(g,e)` in `A+g`, which has at most 9 vertices. QED.

## 5. New theorem: `P14-2CUT-EXTERNALFIELD-REDUCTION-55` — PROVED

The same-side case exposes the missing induction invariant exactly.

### Statement, no separator edge

For `e,f in E(A)`, after summing over forests on `B`, a disconnected `A`-forest receives multiplicity `D_B+C_B`, while a connected `A`-forest receives multiplicity `D_B`.

Let `H=A+g` where `g=xy` is a virtual edge. Give `g` weight

\[
\lambda=\frac{C_B}{D_B}>0
\]

and every old edge weight 1. Marginalizing the state of `g` gives weight `1+lambda` to disconnected `A`-forests and weight 1 to connected `A`-forests. Hence the physical marginal on `A` is exactly `D_B` times this one-edge-field forest measure, and

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(e,f)=D_B^2\Delta_H^{(g=\lambda)}(e,f).
}
\]

### Statement with actual separator edge

If `h=xy` is an actual edge of `G`, remove `h` and use the same `A,B`. A disconnected `A`-forest receives multiplicity `2D_B+C_B`, while a connected one receives `D_B`, so the equivalent virtual edge field is

\[
\lambda=1+\frac{C_B}{D_B}
\]

and again

\[
\Delta_G(e,f)=D_B^2\Delta_H^{(g=\lambda)}(e,f).
\]

### Proof

The multiplicities are the same admissibility count used in §4. Dividing the two weights by the common positive factor `D_B` gives respectively `1+C_B/D_B` versus `1`, or `2+C_B/D_B` versus `1` when the real separator edge may also be chosen. In `H=A+g`, summing over the virtual edge gives disconnected forests weight `1+lambda` and connected forests weight `1`. Matching these coefficients proves equality of the unnormalized measures up to the common factor `D_B`; every one-edge or two-edge count gets that same factor, hence each Rayleigh numerator gets `D_B^2`. QED.

### Consequence

The source unit theorem on smaller sides is **not** a closed induction invariant across a 2-cut. A same-side pair generally sees `lambda != 1`. This kills the naive route “prove every 10-vertex 2-connected case from unit n<=9 sides.” The obstruction is now exact rather than vague.

## 6. New stronger invariant: one-edge-field Rayleigh

Define `OEFR(n)`:

> For every simple graph `H` with at most `n` vertices, every distinguished edge `g`, every pair `e,f` distinct from `g`, and every real `lambda>0`, the forest polynomial with edge `g` weighted `lambda` and all other edges weighted 1 satisfies `Delta_{ef}>=0`.

Pairs involving `g` do not add an all-lambda burden: their Rayleigh sign is independent of `lambda` after the harmless positive event-weight factor is removed.

### Conditional theorem `P14-N10-THREECONNECTED-CONDITIONAL-55`

If `OEFR(9)` holds, then every 10-vertex graph with a 2-vertex cut satisfies Grimmett's source unit pairwise inequality. Combined with the previously proved block/direct-sum theorem, any 10-vertex source counterexample would then be 3-connected.

Proof: cross-side and separator-edge pairs are already handled unconditionally by §4. Same-side pairs reduce by §5 to an `OEFR(9)` instance because the augmented side has at most 9 vertices. QED.

This theorem is **CONDITIONAL**. I do not relabel the source conjecture or the 10-vertex class as proved.

The enumerative payoff is large: an exact OEFR(9) certificate would justify reducing a prospective source n=10 exhaustive class from 9,743,542 unlabeled 2-connected graphs to 5,114,079 unlabeled 3-connected graphs. The current source metric remains 9.

## 7. New exact finite certificate: `P14-OEFR-QUADRATIC-CERTIFICATE-55` — PROVED

This is the strongest reason the new invariant is computationally viable: **all positive values of the one edge field are decided by one integer quadratic, not by a rational grid.**

Fix a simple graph `H`, a distinguished edge `g`, and `e,f != g`. With every old edge weight 1 and `g` weight `lambda`, write

\[
I(\lambda)=A+\lambda B,
\]
\[
I_e(\lambda)=A_e+\lambda B_e,
\quad
I_f(\lambda)=A_f+\lambda B_f,
\quad
I_{ef}(\lambda)=A_{ef}+\lambda B_{ef}.
\]

All eight coefficients are exact nonnegative integers counting forests with `g` absent/present and the indicated old edges constrained present.

Then

\[
\Delta_{ef}(\lambda)
=I_e I_f-I I_{ef}
=q_0+q_1\lambda+q_2\lambda^2,
\]

where

\[
q_0=A_eA_f-AA_{ef},
\]

\[
q_1=A_eB_f+B_eA_f-A B_{ef}-B A_{ef},
\]

\[
q_2=B_eB_f-BB_{ef}.
\]

The polynomial is nonnegative for every `lambda>0` iff the following exact integer conditions hold:

1. `q2<0`: FAIL, because the quadratic tends to `-infinity`.
2. `q2=0`: require `q0>=0` and `q1>=0`.
3. `q2>0` and `q1>=0`: require `q0>=0`.
4. `q2>0` and `q1<0`: require

\[
q_1^2\le4q_0q_2.
\]

In the fourth case the minimum occurs at `lambda_*=-q1/(2q2)>0`, and the displayed discriminant condition is exactly the minimum nonnegativity condition. It also forces `q0>0` unless degenerate.

For a pair `(g,e)`, direct differentiation gives

\[
\Delta_{g,e}(\lambda)=B A_e-A B_e,
\]

apart from the conventional positive `lambda` event-weight factor; its sign is independent of `lambda`.

Thus `OEFR(9)` is a finite exact-arithmetic decision problem. A positive rational grid would be invalid; this coefficient/discriminant certificate is genuinely all-lambda.

## 8. A second structural sanity check: subdivision produces exactly the same external field

This theorem is not needed for the 2-cut proof, but it stress-tests the stronger invariant and explains why it is natural.

### `P14-SUBDIVISION-OEFR-55` — PROVED

Let `H` contain edge `g=xy`, and let `G` be obtained by subdividing `g` into `e1=xv`, `e2=vy`. Write `D,C` for the numbers of forests of `H-g` in which `x,y` are disconnected/connected. Then

\[
Z_G=4D+3C,
\qquad
Z_{e_1}=Z_{e_2}=2D+C,
\qquad
Z_{e_1e_2}=D,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(e_1,e_2)=C(C+D)\ge0.
}
\]

For any old edge `a`, with corresponding `D_a,C_a`, a direct count gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(e_1,a)=2(DC_a-CD_a)=2\Delta_H(g,a)
}
\]

at unit weight.

For two old edges `a,b`, marginalizing the subdivided two-edge path weights a forest of `H-g` by 4 if it disconnects `x,y` and by 3 if it connects them. Up to the factor 3 this is exactly the forest measure of `H` with `g` weight `1/3`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(a,b)=9\Delta_H^{(g=1/3)}(a,b).
}
\]

So even this elementary graph operation naturally asks for a one-edge external field. The stronger invariant is not an artificial computational convenience.

## 10. New-method move #14 — find the group acting

The useful action is the automorphism group of a side graph on the marked data `(g,{e,f})`.

An exact `OEFR(9)` checker need only test orbit representatives of distinguished edges and unordered old-edge pairs under `Aut(H)`, provided the manifest certifies complete orbit coverage. ## 11. Lorentzian, complete-log-concavity, real-stable, and Gårding audit

The requested modern machinery was checked against the actual hypotheses rather than used by slogan.

### Complete log concavity does not give the needed sign

Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant prove that the homogenization of the independent-set generating polynomial of any matroid is completely log-concave. But for a positive multiaffine polynomial `f`,

\[
\partial_{ef}\log f
=\frac{f f_{ef}-f_e f_f}{f^2}
=-\frac{\Delta_{ef}}{f^2}.
\]

Log concavity says the whole Hessian of `log f` is negative semidefinite. A negative-semidefinite matrix need not have each off-diagonal entry nonpositive. Therefore complete log concavity does **not** imply the pairwise Rayleigh inequalities one-by-one.

### Lorentzianity is not a free constant-1 Rayleigh theorem

Brändén–Huh Lorentzian theory supplies strong log-concavity/Hodge-type consequences for matroid polynomials, but Lorentzianity alone is weaker than the source-exact constant-1 Rayleigh sign required here. No current theorem found in this run says that every graphic matroid independence polynomial is Rayleigh merely because its homogenization is Lorentzian.

### Real stability of basis/spanning-tree polynomials does not transfer automatically to all forests

Graphic basis polynomials have much stronger stability properties, but the forest independence polynomial sums over all ranks. Real stability of the spanning-tree/basis polynomial does not by itself imply real stability or Rayleigh for the all-forest polynomial.

### Fang–Ma gives a genuine route only after a missing hypothesis

Fang–Ma (2026) prove that a multi-affine Gårding polynomial with nonnegative coefficients is Rayleigh. That would be sufficient if the graphic independence polynomial were known to be Gårding in the required generality. The current source does not supply such a theorem for all graphic independence polynomials; indeed the paper still points to unresolved Rayleigh directions for regular/graphic matroids. Thus the Gårding statement is a **hypothesis test / possible class theorem**, not a universal proof of Grimmett 4.2.

### Stronger weighted 2-sum closure cannot be imported from unit n<=9

Wagner/Cocks-type closure results for I-Rayleigh or related weighted matroid properties are valuable, but their hypotheses quantify over all positive edge weights. The n<=9 source certificate quantifies only over the unit point. Section 5 gives a direct mathematical reason this distinction matters: a same-side 2-cut marginal sees `lambda=C/D`, usually not 1.
