# 5. P14-FLATFLAG-LEAKAGE-NOGO-32 — PROVED

## 5.1 Statement

There exists one fixed real random variable xi with all of the source properties

- centered;
- variance one;
- `C^infty` bounded Lebesgue density;
- finite variance and no extra moment assumption;

and there exist powers of two `m_k -> infinity` and deterministic nested projector flags

`P_{k,1} <= P_{k,2} <= ... <= P_{k,m_k}=I_{m_k}`,                     (5.1)

with `rank(P_{k,n})=n`, such that the following hold.

### (a) Haar-order leverage hierarchy, pathwise

Writing `p_i=(P_{k,n})_ii`, for every fixed exponent `r>=1`,

`max_i p_i <= 2n/m_k`,

and

`sum_i p_i^r <= 2^r n^r m_k^(1-r)`.                                  (5.2)

Thus the entire deterministic flag satisfies the same dimensional leverage order as the all-rank moment hierarchy used in P24.

### (b) Large scalar leakage

For every `2<=n<=m_k` there is a symmetric matrix `K_{k,n}` satisfying

`K_{k,n}=P_{k,n}K_{k,n}P_{k,n}`,

`tr K_{k,n}=0`,

`||K_{k,n}||_F=1`,                                                     (5.3)

and

`L_{P_{k,n}}(K_{k,n}) >= c_xi c_k/n`,                                 (5.4)

where `c_k>0` is summable but chosen so that

`c_k log m_k -> infinity`.                                             (5.5)

Consequently

`sum_{n=2}^{m_k} L_{P_{k,n}}(K_{k,n}) -> infinity`.                   (5.6)

Therefore there is **no** source-dependent summable sequence `(eps_n)` such that

`L_P(K)<=eps_n`                                                        (5.7)

for every ambient dimension, every rank-n deterministic projector P satisfying Haar-order leverage bounds, and every supported trace-zero Frobenius-unit K. This remains false even when the projectors form one nested flag.

## 5.2 Construction of the fixed source law

Choose even nonnegative `C^infty` probability densities `g_0,h` supported in `[-1,1]`, with positive base variance.

Let

`c_k=2^(-k-4)`,                                                        (5.8)

and choose

`m_k = 2^(2^(2k))`.                                                    (5.9)

These are powers of two and

`c_k log m_k -> infinity`.

Fix a very large constant A, to be chosen below, and set

`L_k=sqrt(A m_k)`,

`q_k=c_k/(A m_k)`.                                                     (5.10)

The intervals around `+/-L_k` are disjoint for all large k. Put

`Q=sum_k q_k < 1`,

and define the raw symmetric density

`f_Z(x)=(1-Q)g_0(x) + sum_k (q_k/2)[h(x-L_k)+h(x+L_k)]`.              (5.11)

The sum is locally finite, hence `f_Z` is `C^infty` and bounded. Symmetry gives mean zero. Its variance is

`Var(Z)=(1-Q)Var(g_0) + sum_k q_k(L_k^2+Var(h))`

`      =(1-Q)Var(g_0) + sum_k c_k + (Var(h)/A)sum_k c_k/m_k < infinity`. (5.12)

Let `s^2=Var(Z)` and set

`xi=Z/s`.                                                             (5.13)

Then xi is centered, variance one, smooth and bounded-density. The kth spike pair has total probability q_k and center

`lambda_k=L_k/s`, with `lambda_k^2=A_* m_k`, `A_*=A/s^2`.             (5.14)

Because `s^2` stays bounded as A increases, choose A once and for all so that `A_*` is as large as needed below. This produces one fixed law xi; no triangular family is being used.

## 5.3 Nested balanced block projector flag

Fix one `m=m_k`. Start with the one-block partition of `{1,...,m}`. At each step split one largest block into two equal halves. Since m is a power of two, when

`2^q <= n < 2^(q+1)`,                                                  (5.15)

the rank-n partition consists only of blocks of sizes

`m/2^q` and `m/2^(q+1)`.                                               (5.16)

Hence every block B at rank n obeys

`m/(2n) <= |B| <= 2m/n`.                                               (5.17)

For a block B define

`u_B = |B|^(-1/2) 1_B`.

Let `P_n` be the orthogonal projector onto the space of vectors that are constant on each rank-n block:

`P_n=sum_{B in partition_n} u_B u_B^T`.                               (5.18)

Splitting a block only enlarges the block-constant space, so

`P_1 <= P_2 <= ... <= P_m=I`.                                         (5.19)

For `i in B`,

`(P_n)_ii=1/|B|`.                                                      (5.20)

By (5.17),

`(P_n)_ii <= 2n/m`.                                                    (5.21)

For every `r>=1`,

`sum_i (P_n)_ii^r <= m(2n/m)^r = 2^r n^r m^(1-r)`,                   (5.22)

proving the claimed optimal leverage hierarchy.

## 5.4 Trace-zero test matrix

For `n>=2`, choose a largest block B and any distinct block C. Define

`K_n=(u_B u_B^T-u_C u_C^T)/sqrt(2)`.                                  (5.23)

Then

`K_n=P_n K_n P_n`, `tr K_n=0`, `||K_n||_F=1`.                         (5.24)

For a fresh iid-coordinate row `X=(xi_1,...,xi_m)`, put

`V=P_n X/||P_n X||`.

Then

`L_{P_n}(K_n)=E(V^T K_n V)^2`.                                        (5.25)

We now lower-bound this by one kth rare spike inside B.

## 5.5 One-spike event

Let `b=|B|`. Because B is largest,

`b>=m/n`,                                                             (5.26)

while (5.17) gives `b<=2m/n`.

Let `H_k` be the union of the two kth spike intervals in the normalized xi law. Then

`P(xi in H_k)=q_k=c_k/(A m)`.                                         (5.27)

Let E be the event that **exactly one** coordinate in B lies in `H_k`. Since

`b q_k <= 2c_k/(A n)`,                                                 (5.28)

A may be chosen so large that `bq_k<=1/10` for every k,n. Therefore

`P(E)=b q_k (1-q_k)^(b-1) >= (1/2)bq_k >= c_k/(2A n)`.                (5.29)

Condition on the identity i of the unique spike coordinate in B. Decompose

`P_n X = (xi_i/sqrt(b)) u_B + Z_rest`.                                (5.30)

The non-spike coordinates in B remain centered by symmetry, and conditioning away from one symmetric spike pair increases their second moment by at most `(1-q_k)^(-1)<=2`. All other blocks are unconditioned and have unit second moment per block average. Thus

`E[||Z_rest||^2 | E,i] <= 2n`                                         (5.31)

for all large k. Markov gives

`P(||Z_rest||^2 <= 8n | E,i) >= 3/4`.                                 (5.32)

On `H_k`, for all large k,

`|xi_i| >= lambda_k/2`.                                                (5.33)

Using `b<=2m/n`, the spike amplitude along u_B therefore satisfies

`a^2 := xi_i^2/b >= A_* n/8`.                                         (5.34)

Choose the fixed A so large that on the event in (5.32),

`a/||Z_rest|| >= 10`.                                                  (5.35)

Let `alpha=|<V,u_B>|^2`. The triangle inequality then gives

`alpha >= ((10-1)/(10+1))^2 = 81/121 > 1/2`.                         (5.36)

Since `|<V,u_C>|^2 <= 1-alpha`,

`V^T K_n V`

` = (|<V,u_B>|^2-|<V,u_C>|^2)/sqrt(2)`

` >= (2alpha-1)/sqrt(2) = 41/(121 sqrt(2))`.                          (5.37)

Combining (5.29), (5.32) and (5.37),

`L_{P_n}(K_n) >= C_A c_k/n`,                                          (5.38)

where `C_A>0` depends only on the fixed source construction.

Summing over `2<=n<=m_k`,

`sum_n L_{P_n}(K_n) >= C_A c_k (log m_k-O(1)) -> infinity`            (5.39)

by (5.5). QED.

## 5.6 What this theorem does and does not disprove

It **does disprove** all of the following proof routes:

- a projector-uniform extension of P24's coordinate leakage summability;
- an extension based only on rank plus `max_i P_ii`;
- an extension based on the full collection of fixed leverage moments `sum_i P_ii^r`;
- the claim that adding a nested-flag hypothesis to those leverage bounds is enough.

It does **not** disprove the actual P14 theorem. The flags above are deterministic block-average subspaces, not asserted to have the law of kernels of iid original rows, and the matrices K_n are arbitrary supported trace-zero matrices, not asserted to equal the physical reveal fields K_U.

This distinction is the point: any successful P14 argument must use more of the physical construction than leverage delocalization plus support/trace zero.

# 6. P14-RAYLEIGH-ENERGY-32 — PROVED

The counterexample suggests keeping track of the relative orientation of K and the coordinate axes after projection.

Let P be any orthogonal projector and write

`p_i=P_ii=||P e_i||^2`.

Let K be self-adjoint and supported in P:

`K=PKP`.

Define

`Theta_P(K)=sum_{i:p_i>0} K_ii^2/p_i`.                                (6.1)

## Proposition

`0 <= Theta_P(K) <= ||K||_F^2`.                                       (6.2)

### Proof

Because K is supported in P,

`K_ii = <P e_i, K P e_i>`.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives

`K_ii^2 <= ||P e_i||^2 <P e_i,K^2 P e_i>`

`        = p_i <e_i,K^2 e_i>`.                                       (6.3)

Divide by p_i and sum. Since `K^2` is also supported in P,

`Theta_P(K) <= tr(K^2)=||K||_F^2`.                                   (6.4)

QED.

## 6.1 Counterexample saturates the orientation energy

For the block K_n in (5.23), on every coordinate of B,

`K_ii=1/(sqrt(2)b)`, `p_i=1/b`,

so B contributes exactly `1/2` to Theta. Block C contributes another `1/2`. Thus

`Theta_{P_n}(K_n)=1=||K_n||_F^2`.                                     (6.5)

The projector has essentially optimal leverage moments, yet K is maximally aligned with its block-coordinate frame according to Theta. This is the hidden degree of freedom that leverage-only estimates miss.

# 7. P14-REVEAL-RAYLEIGH-JENSEN-32 — PROVED

Now specialize K to the physical first-order reveal field.

Freeze a rank-n parent P. Let the terminal unit null direction be z, with rank-one projector

`Lambda=zz^T`.

Let U be the distinguished first fresh direction and define

`M=E[Lambda|P]`,

`M_U=E[Lambda|P,U]`,

`K_U=M_U-M`.                                                          (7.1)

For each coordinate i,

`E_U (K_U)_ii^2`

` = Var_U( E[Lambda_ii|P,U] )`

` <= Var(Lambda_ii|P)`

` <= E[Lambda_ii^2|P]`

` = E[z_i^4|P]`.                                                       (7.2)

Therefore

`boxed:`

`E_U Theta_P(K_U)`

` <= J(P) := E_z sum_{i:p_i>0} z_i^4/p_i`.                            (7.3)

No independence, moment assumption, or Gaussian comparison is used. If `p_i=0`, every vector in `Ran(P)` has ith coordinate zero, so the omitted term is identically zero.

## 7.1 Haar calibration

If z is Haar on the unit sphere of an n-dimensional subspace P, then

`E[z_i^4|P] = 3 p_i^2/[n(n+2)]`.                                      (7.4)

Hence

`J_Haar(P)`

` = 3/[n(n+2)] sum_i p_i`

` = 3/(n+2)`.                                                         (7.5)

Thus the natural orientation energy of the physical first-order reveal field is of order `1/n` in Haar geometry, exactly the scale that the flat-block counterexample fails by a factor n.

## 7.2 New concrete subtarget

A strong but now source-faithful intermediate theorem would be

`P14-REVEAL-RAYLEIGH-ENERGY-33:`

`E_P J(P) <= C_xi/n`                                                   (7.6)

for the actual ordinary rank-n residual projector and terminal null line.

This is **OPEN**. It is strictly more physical than a leverage-only theorem: it couples the terminal null line to the parent leverage. It also has a natural q=3/deleted-column flavor because of the numerator `z_i^4` and denominator `p_i`.

I am not claiming (7.6) alone closes the angular leakage; off-diagonal pieces of the rotated quadratic form still matter. Its value is that it identifies one exact orientation statistic on which the counterexample and Haar benchmark sharply disagree.

# 8. Why this no-go is compatible with P24's coordinate theorem

P24 fixes the coordinate projector `P=I_n` in ambient dimension n. The law of the direction is

`V=X/||X||`,

and the Laplace denominator factorizes over independent coordinates. P24 proves summability across n for that fixed source law.

Here, for a fixed large ambient `m_k`, the rank-n projector averages blocks of about `m_k/n` coordinates. The kth rare source spike has size `sqrt(A_* m_k)` and probability `c_k/(A m_k)`. After block averaging, that same physical spike has normalized projected size `Theta(sqrt(n))` simultaneously over an entire range of ranks and occurs in the selected block with probability `Theta(c_k/n)`. This creates a harmonic leakage bill over the flag.

There is no contradiction. The obstruction is exactly the adaptive/rotated-subspace issue that P24 left open.
