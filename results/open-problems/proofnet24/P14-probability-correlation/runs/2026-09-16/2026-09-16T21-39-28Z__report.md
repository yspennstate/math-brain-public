## 5. Move #11 — DUALIZE: the physical star is exactly a factor-two event tilt of a smaller weighted forest measure

The quadratic coefficients become more transparent when rewritten as an exact change of measure. This is an exploratory algebraic reduction, not promoted as a new theorem-sized claim in this stuck run.

Let `B` be `A` with the three virtual triangle edges `xy,xz,yz`, each assigned weight `mu=1/4`, and let `nu` be its normalized weighted forest measure. Fix two virtual triangle edges, say `xy` and `xz`, and let

\[
H=\{xy,xz\text{ are both present}\}.
\]

A forest in `H` necessarily has the three terminals separate in its physical `A` part, and conversely each `d`-state `A` forest contributes exactly weight `mu^2=1/16` to `H`. Hence

\[
r:=\nu(H)=\frac{(1/16)d}{F}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every physical-edge event `C`,

\[
F_C+\frac1{16}d_C
=F\left(\nu(C)+r\,\nu(C\mid H)\right).
\]

After normalization, the physical unit-star law on the `A` edges is therefore exactly

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde\nu
=\frac{\nu+r\,\nu(\cdot\mid H)}{1+r},
\qquad
\frac{d\widetilde\nu}{d\nu}
=\frac{1+\mathbf1_H}{1+r}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

So the star does not require an arbitrary two-environment mixture. It is a **factor-two tilt by one concrete two-virtual-edge event**.

The mixing parameter is uniformly small for a simple reason. The `d`-state contribution to `F` at `mu=1/4` is `(31/16)d`, while the `H` contribution is `(1/16)d`, and every other terminal state only increases `F`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{0\le r\le\frac1{31}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is suggestive but is not, by itself, a Rayleigh proof.

### 5.1 Exact probability form of the missing inequality

Write under `nu`

\[
a=\nu(e),\qquad b=\nu(f),\qquad
c=ab-\nu(ef)\ge0,
\]

and under `nu(\cdot\mid H)`

\[
a'=\nu(e\mid H),\qquad b'=\nu(f\mid H),\qquad
c'=a'b'-\nu(ef\mid H)\ge0.
\]

The two nonnegative endpoint Rayleigh gaps are exactly the normalized versions of `q0` and `q2`. Because the graph is all-weight Rayleigh in the order-nine dependency chain, increasing the two virtual edge fields cannot increase a third-edge marginal; taking both fields to infinity gives

\[
\delta_e:=a-a'\ge0,
\qquad
\delta_f:=b-b'\ge0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

A direct expansion of (5.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
(1+r)^2
\left[\widetilde\nu(e)\widetilde\nu(f)-\widetilde\nu(ef)\right]
=(1+r)(c+rc')-r\delta_e\delta_f.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
q_0=F^2c,
\qquad
q_2=d^2c',
\qquad
q_1=Fd\left(c+c'-\delta_e\delta_f\right).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the exact physical blocker is

\[
\boxed{
(1+r)(c+rc')\ge r\delta_e\delta_f,
\qquad 0\le r\le\frac1{31}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the quantitative statement missing from ordinary pairwise Rayleigh information. The endpoint signs `c,c'>=0` do not bound the positive environmental term `delta_e delta_f`. The route-kill in §4 shows that a bound strong enough to control **all** mixture strengths is false even with 3-connectivity and an automorphism swapping the tested pair. The only viable target is the actual factor-two tilt / actual `r` produced by the graph.

This formulation also explains why the two-terminal induction closes while the three-terminal one does not. A two-cut produces one positive virtual edge field, which Cocks absorbs graphwise. The singleton three-cut produces a two-edge environmental event, and covariance under positive mixing contains an additional product of marginal shifts.

### 5.2 Cone geometry of the same failure

For a constrained count vector define the `2x2` moment table

\[
M(F)=
\begin{pmatrix}
F & F_f\\
F_e & F_{ef}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The Rayleigh condition is `det M(F)<=0`. The star follows the affine line

Both endpoints `M(F)` and `M(d)` have nonpositive determinant, but the positive-entry Rayleigh region is not convex. Section 4 gives a graph-derived line that exits this region. ## 6. Move #22 — READ THE FAILURES, NOT THE SUCCESSES: what the literature machinery actually lacks

A fresh bounded literature check was done on 2026-09-16. It does not prove global openness; it records what the checked primary/current sources do and do not supply.

### 6.1 Cocks / Semple--Welsh: exactly why two terminals work

Cocks, *Correlated Matroids*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 17 (2008), 511--518, states in the abstract that the Grimmett--Winkler conjecture is equivalent to statements about correlated graphic matroids and proves that the 2-sum of correlated matroids is correlated. Semple--Welsh's final paper records the fixed-graph implication used in the current P14 chain.

That is the nearest solved structural analogue. A bounded search for an I-Rayleigh/correlated-matroid **3-sum** closure theorem did not produce one. That absence is a search result, not a theorem of nonexistence.

### 6.2 Lorentzian and complete-log-concavity machinery: conclusion gap, not just missing notation

Anari--Liu--Oveis Gharan--Vinzant prove complete log concavity for the homogenized independent-set polynomial of a matroid. That is a powerful global concavity statement, but it does not automatically give the entrywise mixed-log-Hessian sign required for Rayleigh.

Fang--Ma, *Gårding Polynomials*, arXiv:2604.27755 (2026), make the distinction particularly explicit: multi-affine Gårding polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are Rayleigh, while Lorentzian theory does not in general supply the full Rayleigh property. Their verified matroid applications cover specified classes, including series-parallel cycle matroids, not arbitrary graphic independence polynomials. P14 therefore has **not** verified the universal Gårding hypothesis needed to invoke their Rayleigh theorem.

So neither “matroid => Lorentzian/complete log concave” nor “Gårding => Rayleigh” closes Grimmett without the missing hypothesis. This route remains hypothesis-blocked, not proved.

### 6.3 Real stability / strong Rayleigh: too strong and not verified here

Real stability would imply a stronger negative-dependence package, but arbitrary graphic forest independence polynomials have not been shown in the checked sources to be real stable. The factor-two event tilt (5.2) is also not something for which a generic stability-preserver theorem may simply be assumed. Thus real-stable machinery can certify subclasses only after its hypotheses are actually proved.

### 6.4 Wheel graphs explain what symmetry buys -- and what it does not

Idriss Olivier Bado's recent paper, *Negative edge correlation for uniform random forests on wheel graphs*, Statistics & Probability Letters 241:110960, proves strict negative correlation for wheel graphs by an exact multivariate `2x2` transfer matrix and cyclic symmetry, reducing the analysis to three edge-pair types. This is an important infinite 3-connected non-series-parallel class.

But the arbitrary three-terminal separator has five terminal-connectivity states and the environmental product term in (5.5). Section 4 additionally shows that even an automorphism exchanging `e` and `f` does not make the whole-ray discriminant true. Symmetry helps only when it collapses the **transfer state**, not merely the tested pair.

### 6.5 Current restricted progress does not settle the source

Tang--Zhang, arXiv:2603.10738 (11 March 2026), treat specified spanning-subgraph families of complete graphs, including fixed-component forests for sufficiently large `n`; this is not the uniform-all-forests measure on every finite graph. Their 10 September 2026 random-cluster paper arXiv:2609.11578 says pairwise negative correlation below one remains open on general finite graphs and proves high-degree restricted results. Neither changes P14's source scope.

The checked recent literature therefore points to the same missing object as the algebra: a quantitative three-terminal mixing/polarization inequality, not another application of a theorem whose hypotheses are absent.
