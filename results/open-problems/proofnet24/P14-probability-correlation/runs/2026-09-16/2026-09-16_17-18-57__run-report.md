### 1.2 Exact source scope and statement

Section 4.2 of the source takes a finite graph `G=(V,E)` and says, “for simplicity,” that it has neither loops nor multiple edges. A set `F subset E` is a forest when `(V,F)` has no cycles. Let `mathcal F` be **all** forests of `G`, and let `Phi` be uniform on `mathcal F`.

For distinct edges `e,f`, Grimmett defines “edge-negatively associated” by the pairwise inequality

\[
\Pr(e,f\in\Phi)
\le
\Pr(e\in\Phi)\Pr(f\in\Phi).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The exact Conjecture 4.2 sentence is:

> **For all graphs `G`, the random forest `Phi` is edge-negatively associated.**

This report freezes the finite-computation scope to **finite simple graphs**, matching the source's simplifying convention and the McKay/nauty graph census. The source itself immediately notes that its edge-negative-association property is weak relative to stronger forms of negative dependence. Therefore this report does **not** silently identify (1.1) with full negative association in the standard increasing-events sense.

## 2. Three distinct properties; do not conflate them

For the forest generating polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)
=
\sum_{F\subseteq E:\,(V,F)\text{ acyclic}}
\prod_{g\in F}y_g,
\tag{2.1}
\]

and distinct `e,f`, define

\[
\Delta_G^{e,f}(\mathbf y)
=(\partial_e I_G)(\partial_f I_G)-I_G\,\partial_{ef}I_G.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The scopes tracked separately are:

1. **Source / unweighted pairwise property.** At `y_g=1` for every edge,
   \[
   \Delta_G^{e,f}(\mathbf1)=Z_eZ_f-ZZ_{ef}\ge0,
   \tag{2.3}
   \]
   where `Z` counts all forests, `Z_e` those containing `e`, and `Z_ef` those containing both. This is exactly Grimmett Conjecture 4.2.
2. **All-positive-weight I-Rayleigh property.** Require (2.2) for every `\mathbf y>0`; in particular this covers every positive rational weighting requested by the fleet prompt. This is strictly stronger than checking `\mathbf y=\mathbf1`.
3. **Full negative association.** Require negative correlation for arbitrary increasing events depending on disjoint edge sets. This is stronger again. Grimmett's Conjecture 4.2 does not state this full property.

The principal metric in this track is item 1 unless a future run explicitly changes the frozen metric under a newer user instruction.

## 4. Current literature/status check

### 4.1 Grimmett–Winkler exact finite evidence

G. R. Grimmett and S. N. Winkler, **“Negative Association in Uniform Forests and Connected Graphs”**, *Random Structures & Algorithms* 24(4) (2004), 444–460, DOI `10.1002/rsa.20012`, states in its abstract that the uniform-forest conjecture was numerically verified for

* **all graphs with at most 8 vertices**, and
* **9-vertex graphs with at most 18 edges**.

Thus, already in the published literature, any unweighted simple-graph counterexample must either have at least 10 vertices or, if it has exactly 9 vertices, have at least 19 edges. The latter is a **partial** nine-vertex bound, not complete certification of all nine-vertex graphs.

### 4.2 Contemporary open status

Pengfei Tang and Zibo Zhang, arXiv:2609.11578 (submitted 10 September 2026), explicitly state that even pairwise negative correlation for the random-cluster model with cluster parameter below one remains open on general finite graphs. Their March 2026 paper arXiv:2603.10738 proves pairwise negative correlation for several specified spanning-subgraph families of the complete graph for sufficiently large `n`, including forests with exactly `k` components; it does not settle arbitrary graphs with the uniform measure on **all** forests.

Hence no current source located in this run makes Grimmett Conjecture 4.2 KNOWN in full.

### 4.3 Weighted Rayleigh evidence through seven vertices

Alejandro Erickson's 2008 University of Waterloo thesis, **“Negative Correlation Properties for Matroids”**, defines `I`-Rayleigh by (2.2) for every positive weighting. Its abstract states that the `I`-Rayleigh difference for graphs on at most **seven** vertices is a sum of monomials times squares of polynomials, and that the same special form holds for all series-parallel graphs. This gives a literature theorem stronger than the source's unit-weight statement for every graph through seven vertices. It does **not** promote this report's unit-weight eight-vertex computation to weighted Rayleigh at eight vertices.

Series-parallel graphs of arbitrary size remain a restricted graph class and are therefore `PARTIAL`, never a solution of Conjecture 4.2 over all finite graphs.

### 4.4 Why Brändén–Huh / Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant do not close the target

I checked the hypothesis issue rather than invoking “Lorentzian” as a slogan.

Hao Fang and Biao Ma, **“Gårding Polynomials”**, arXiv:2604.27755 (2026), state that multi-affine Gårding polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are Rayleigh. But their Section 14 still lists

* Conjecture 14.15: regular matroids are `S`- and `I`-Rayleigh; and
* Conjecture 14.16: the spanning-forest generating function of every graph is Rayleigh.

More importantly, they explicitly emphasize that **Lorentzian polynomials are in general not Rayleigh**; Lorentzian theory supplies weaker `c`-Rayleigh inequalities and is naturally adapted to homogeneous polynomials. A matroid's basis generating polynomial is Lorentzian, but the forest/independent-set polynomial is nonhomogeneous, and Lorentzian/log-concavity machinery alone does not imply (2.2). Thus neither Brändén–Huh Lorentzianity nor the Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant complete-log-concavity machinery is a free proof of the desired forest Rayleigh property. One would first have to verify a genuinely sufficient hypothesis such as the appropriate Gårding property; current 2026 literature records that as conjectural in this scope.

**Killed route `P14-LORENTZIAN-FREE-PASS-NOGO-01`:** “basis polynomial is Lorentzian, therefore the all-forests polynomial is Rayleigh” is invalid.

### 5.2 Exact forest-counting identity used by the checker

Artifact source:

`proofnet24/P14-probability-correlation/artifacts/P14_forest_rayleigh_n8_checker_2026-09-16.c`

blob `26f8c039916a95a22c86fa0eb8b3bae81bf821ee`, creation commit `53cf7a9b44efd4bdcccd2cdbc06682660d0f8d7f`.

Let `tau(T)` denote the number of spanning trees of the induced graph on a nonempty vertex set `T`. For a vertex set `S`, let `F(S)` be the number of all forests of `G[S]`, with `F(emptyset)=1`. Fix the least vertex `a` of nonempty `S`. Every forest of `G[S]` has a unique connected component `T` containing `a`; that component is a spanning tree of `G[T]`, and the rest is an arbitrary forest of `G[S\T]`. Therefore

\[
F(S)=
\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq S\\a\in T}}
\tau(T)F(S\setminus T).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Conversely, a choice of `T`, a tree on `T`, and a forest on `S\T` gives one and only one forest on `S`. Thus (5.1) is an exact bijective recurrence, not a heuristic dynamic program.

The checker computes every `tau(T)` by the Matrix–Tree theorem. It also computes, for every present edge `e` whose endpoints lie in `T`, the number `tau_e(T)` of spanning trees of `G[T]` containing `e`, and for every pair `e,f`, the number `tau_ef(T)` containing both, using the one- and two-edge transfer-current determinants obtained from the reduced Laplacian inverse.

These are standard exact identities:

\[
\tau_e(T)=\tau(T)\,b_e^TL_T^{-1}b_e,
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
\tau_{ef}(T)=\tau(T)\det
\begin{pmatrix}
 b_e^TL_T^{-1}b_e & b_e^TL_T^{-1}b_f\\
 b_f^TL_T^{-1}b_e & b_f^TL_T^{-1}b_f
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Here `L_T` is a reduced Laplacian and the signed incidence columns are taken relative to the removed root. Equations (5.2)–(5.3) count actual spanning trees, so their values are integers between `0` and `tau(T)`.

For `n<=8`, Cayley's bound gives

\[
\tau(T)\le |T|^{|T|-2}\le8^6=262144.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The checker performs reduced-Laplacian inversion modulo the prime `p=1,000,000,007`. Since every integer in (5.2)–(5.4) lies in `[0,262144]`, strictly below `p`, its canonical modular representative is the actual integer count. Thus modular linear algebra here is an exact arithmetic device, not a probabilistic or residue-only certificate.

Now let `V` denote all eight vertices. For a fixed present edge `e`, every forest containing `e` has a unique component `T` containing that edge. Consequently

\[
Z_e=
\sum_{T\supseteq V(e)}
\tau_e(T)F(V\setminus T).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For two distinct edges `e,f`, there are exactly two possibilities.

* They lie in the **same** forest component `T`; this contributes
  \[
  \sum_{T\supseteq V(e)\cup V(f)}
  \tau_{ef}(T)F(V\setminus T).
  \tag{5.6}
  \]
* If their endpoint sets are disjoint, they may lie in **different** components. Then choosing disjoint vertex sets `A,B` containing `V(e),V(f)` respectively, a tree in `A` containing `e`, a tree in `B` containing `f`, and an arbitrary forest on the remainder gives the exact contribution
  \[
  \sum_{\substack{A\cap B=\varnothing\\V(e)\subseteq A,\,V(f)\subseteq B}}
  \tau_e(A)\tau_f(B)F(V\setminus(A\cup B)).
  \tag{5.7}
  \]
  If `e,f` share a vertex, this second case is impossible.

The checker implements (5.6) and (5.7) separately, so it does not make the common mistake of counting only pairs lying in one tree component.

Finally it forms, using exact integer arithmetic,

\[
\Delta=Z_eZ_f-ZZ_{ef}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For eight vertices every forest is an edge subset of at most 28 edges, so each count is at most `2^28`; 64-bit unsigned integers have ample margin. The program nevertheless evaluates the final products and difference in signed `__int128` arithmetic.
