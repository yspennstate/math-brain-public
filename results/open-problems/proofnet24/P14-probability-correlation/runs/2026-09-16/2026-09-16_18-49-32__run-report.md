### 2.2 Exact statement and fixed source scope

In §4.2 Grimmett takes a finite graph `G=(V,E)`, for simplicity without loops or multiple edges. A forest is an acyclic subset `F subseteq E`; `Phi` is chosen uniformly from the set of all such forests. The source defines edge-negative association by

\[
\Pr(e,f\in\Phi)\le \Pr(e\in\Phi)\Pr(f\in\Phi),\qquad e\ne f.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The exact Conjecture 4.2 statement is:

> “For all graphs `G`, the random forest `Phi` is edge-negatively associated.”

The source immediately notes that this pairwise property is weak relative to stronger notions of negative dependence. Therefore the tracked source statement here is **unweighted pairwise edge negative correlation**, not full negative association and not the all-positive-weight Rayleigh strengthening.

## 5. New theorem-sized reduction for the n=10 attack

### Claim `P14-FOREST-BLOCK-DIRECT-SUM-54` — PROVED

Let `G` be a finite simple graph. Partition its edge set into graph blocks: every nontrivial 2-connected block, with each bridge treated as a singleton block. Then the forest generating polynomial factors as the product of the forest generating polynomials of the blocks. Consequently, under uniform forests, the block restrictions are independent; two edges in different blocks have zero covariance; and for two edges in the same block their correlation law is exactly the law in that block. In particular, any vertex-minimal counterexample to Grimmett's pairwise forest conjecture may be taken 2-connected.

### Proof

Let the edge blocks be `B_1,...,B_t`, so

\[
E(G)=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^t E(B_j).
\]

A standard property of the block decomposition is that **every cycle of `G` is contained in a single block**. Therefore, for an arbitrary edge set `F subseteq E(G)`,

\[
F\text{ is acyclic in }G
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F\cap E(B_j)\text{ is acyclic in }B_j\text{ for every }j.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The forward implication is immediate. Conversely, if `F` contained a cycle `C`, all edges of `C` would lie in one block `B_j`, contradicting acyclicity of `F cap E(B_j)`.

For edge variables `y=(y_e:e in E(G))`, define

\[
I_G(y)=\sum_{F\text{ forest of }G}\prod_{e\in F}y_e.
\]

By the bijection in (5.1), choosing a forest of `G` is exactly the same as independently choosing one forest in each block and taking their union. Hence

\[
\boxed{I_G(y)=\prod_{j=1}^t I_{B_j}(y_{E(B_j)}).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At unit weights, (5.2) says that the uniform forest measure on `G` is the product of the uniform forest measures on the blocks. If `e` and `f` lie in different blocks, their indicators are therefore independent, so

\[
\Pr(e,f\in\Phi)=\Pr(e\in\Phi)\Pr(f\in\Phi).
\]

If `e,f` lie in the same block `B_j`, all other block choices cancel between numerator and denominator in the four relevant probabilities, so the joint and marginal probabilities are exactly those obtained from the uniform forest measure of `B_j`. Thus the Rayleigh numerator for `e,f` has the same sign as in their block.

Now suppose `G` is vertex-minimal among counterexamples. A violating pair cannot use two different blocks by independence. Hence it lies inside one nontrivial block `B`. If `G` has any other block, any articulation attachment, any bridge outside `B`, or any isolated vertex, deleting the rest leaves the smaller graph `B` with the same violating pair, contradicting vertex minimality. Thus a vertex-minimal counterexample may be taken 2-connected. QED.

## 6. Current literature / machinery check

### 6.1 Lorentzianity is still not a free Rayleigh proof

I rechecked the 2026 Fang--Ma paper *Gårding Polynomials*, arXiv:2604.27755. Their discussion explicitly states that Lorentzian polynomials are **not Rayleigh in general**; they satisfy only weaker `c`-Rayleigh inequalities. More specifically, their §14 still treats the graphic-matroid route to the unrooted spanning-forest Rayleigh property as an open problem: they ask whether graphic matroids have the needed C-Gårding property and state that an affirmative answer would imply Rayleigh for every graph's unrooted spanning-forest generating function.

Thus neither Brändén--Huh Lorentzianity nor Anari--Liu--Oveis Gharan--Vinzant complete log-concavity can be imported as a proof of Grimmett Conjecture 4.2 without an additional hypothesis that is not currently known for this generating polynomial.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27755

### 6.2 Fresh 2026 status check

Recent work remains restricted rather than resolving arbitrary finite graphs. Tang--Zhang, arXiv:2603.10738, prove pairwise negative correlation for several uniform spanning-subgraph families of the complete graph for sufficiently large `n`. Their September 2026 arXiv:2609.11578 states that even pairwise negative correlation for the broader `q<1` random-cluster setting remains open on general finite graphs and proves a high-degree regular-graph regime. Nothing located in the current source check supplies a general arbitrary-graph solution of Grimmett's uniform-forest conjecture.

Sources:
