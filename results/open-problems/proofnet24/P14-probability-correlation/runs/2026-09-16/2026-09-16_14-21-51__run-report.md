# 5. `P14-PAIR-PALM-DUALITY-45` — PROVED

\[

\tag{5.3}

&=E_{\pi_P}W^2\\
&=c_r^2E_{\pi_P}\tau^2,

\tag{5.4}

\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_3^{(2)}
&=E_{\pi_R}W\\

\tag{5.5}

\[
\boxed{

=c_r^2E_{\pi_P}[\tau^2]

\frac{d\pi_R}{d\pi_P}=c_r\tau.

E_{\pi_R}\tau

# 8. `P14-PAIR-CAP-CRITICAL-DIMENSION-45` — PROVED

The innovation representation allows a sharp absolute cap calculation and identifies its exact endpoint.

Let

\[
0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_n
\]

be the eigenvalues of `S`, and let `e_1,e_2,...` be corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors.

Fix `h<=n`, project each column of `E` onto

\[
E_h=\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_h\},
\]

and write the resulting `h x 2` matrix as `Y_h`. Put

\[
\Lambda_h=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_h).
\]

Because

\[
E^TS^{-1}E\succeq Y_h^T\Lambda_h^{-1}Y_h,
\]

monotonicity of `det(I+.)` on the positive cone gives

\[
Q
\le
\frac{\det M}
{\det(I_2+M Y_h^T\Lambda_h^{-1}Y_h)^2}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

## 8.1 Projected-density input

Conditional on `(Z,I,U)`, each column of `E` is a translate of an iid product vector in `R^n` whose one-dimensional densities are bounded by `K`.

The sharp Livshyts--Paouris--Pivovarov marginal-density theorem says that an `h`-dimensional orthogonal projection of a product density with scalar ceiling `K` has joint density at most

\[
2^{h/2}K^h.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The two marked columns are independent. Hence the joint density of `Y_h in R^{h x 2}` is bounded by

\[
2^hK^{2h}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is a statement about the **joint projected density** of each column. It does not say the `h` projected coordinates are independent.

## 8.2 Exact matrix-Cauchy integral

For positive `M` and `Lambda_h`, set

\[
Y=\Lambda_h^{1/2} Z M^{-1/2}.
\]

Then

\[
\det(I_2+M Y^T\Lambda_h^{-1}Y)
=\det(I_2+Z^TZ),
\]

and the Jacobian is

\[
dY
=\det(\Lambda_h)\det(M)^{-h/2}\,dZ.
\]

Therefore the density-ceiling integral in `(8.1)` is controlled by

\[
2^hK^{2h}
\det(\Lambda_h)
\det(M)^{1-h/2}
J_h,
\tag{8.4}
\]

where

\[
J_h:=\int_{\mathbb R^{h\times2}}
\det(I_2+Z^TZ)^{-2}\,dZ.
\tag{8.5}
\]

The standard matrix polar integral gives

\[
J_h
=\pi^h\frac{\Gamma_2(2-h/2)}{\Gamma_2(2)},
\tag{8.6}
\]

and it is finite iff

\[
2>\frac{h+1}{2},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad h<3.
\tag{8.7}
\]

In particular,

\[
J_1=\pi,
\qquad
J_2=2\pi^2,

# 9. `P14-METRIC-REMOVAL-INEQUALITY-45` — PROVED but route-losing

There is a useful universal inequality that further clarifies what is lost by an absolute reduction.

Let

\[
A_*=E^TS^{-1}E\succeq0,
\qquad 0<M\preceq I_2.
\]

Then

\[
Q=\frac{\det M}{\det(I_2+MA_*)^2}.
\]

Set

\[
N=M^{1/2}A_*M^{1/2}.
\]

Since

\[
\det M\det(I_2+A_*)
=\det(M+N),
\]

and `M<=I` implies

\[
M+N\preceq I+N,
\]

we have

\[
\det(M+N)\le\det(I+N)\le\det(I+N)^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q\le \det(I_2+E^TS^{-1}E)^{-1}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This is dimension-free algebra and uses no probability.

It is, however, unsuitable as the first sharp step. For example, with

\[
M=\varepsilon I_2,
\qquad A_*=I_2,
\]

one has

\[
Q=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{(1+\varepsilon)^4}
\sim\varepsilon^2,
\]

whereas the right side of `(9.1)` equals `1/4`. Thus `(9.1)` can lose an arbitrarily large parent-pair factor. It is a safe coarse estimate, not a path to the sharp pair theorem.
