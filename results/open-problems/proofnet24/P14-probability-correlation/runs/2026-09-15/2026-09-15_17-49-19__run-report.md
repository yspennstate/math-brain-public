# 6. Proof of the signed sibling cross identities

Continue with the frozen parent `P` of rank `d+1`.

Define

\[
S=E[UU^\top\mid P],
\qquad
\delta=S-P/(d+1).
\]

Both `S` and `P/(d+1)` have trace one, so

\[
\operatorname{tr}\delta=0.
\]

Let

\[
C=C_{d+1}(P)=M_P-P/(d+1).
\]

Because

\[
E F_U=P-S,
\]

and

\[
M_P=E C_U+E(F_U/d),
\]

we get

\[
M_P
=K+P/(d+1)-\delta/d,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{C=K-\delta/d.}
\]

This is `(E6)`.

Now use the support identity

\[
C_UU=0,
\qquad
C_{U'}U'=0.
\]

With

\[
G=UU^\top-U'U'^\top,
\]

independence of the two sibling draws gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\langle C_U-C_{U'},G\rangle_F
&=-2\langle K,S\rangle_F\\
&=-2\langle K,\delta\rangle_F\\
&=-2\left\langle C+\frac\delta d,\delta\right\rangle_F.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the other hand

\[
T=(C_U-C_{U'})-G/d.
\]

Also

\[
E\|G\|_F^2
=2\left(1-\operatorname{tr}S^2\right).
\]

Since

\[
S=P/(d+1)+\delta,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\delta=0,
\]

\[
\operatorname{tr}S^2
=\frac1{d+1}+\|\delta\|_F^2.
\]

Substitute this and `(6.1)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\langle T,G\rangle_F
&=-2\left\langle C+\frac\delta d,\delta\right\rangle_F
-\frac2d\left(1-\frac1{d+1}-\|\delta\|_F^2\right)\\
&=-2\langle C,\delta\rangle_F
-\frac{2}{d+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(E4)`.

For the Haar common-tail reference, P24 proves

\[
T_{\rm Haar}=-G/d.
\]

Therefore

\[
E\langle T_{\rm Haar},G\rangle_F
=-\frac1dE\|G\|_F^2
=-\frac2{d+1}+\frac2d\|\delta\|_F^2.
\]

Subtracting proves `(E5)`.

### Interpretation

## 8.1 Polar form of the null line

The row law is invariant under every orthogonal transformation of the final `n` Gaussian coordinates.  Choose the representative of the null line whose first coordinate is positive.  Then

\[
\boxed{v=(\sqrt A,\sqrt{1-A}\,U),}

\[
T=U\cdot V.

## 8.2 Deterministic angular gap

The vector `v` lies in the two-dimensional subspace

\[
\operatorname{span}(e_1,U).
\]

The component of `w` perpendicular to this entire two-dimensional subspace is

\[
\sqrt{1-B}\,(V-TU),

whose squared norm is

\[
(1-B)(1-T^2).

The component of `w` perpendicular to the one-dimensional line spanned by `v` can only be larger.  Therefore, pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
1-(v\cdot w)^2
\ge
(1-B)(1-T^2).
}
\tag{8.2}

Consequently

\[
\boxed{

\le
(1-B)^{-1/2}(1-T^2)^{-1/2}.

\tag{8.3}

The two factors on the right are independent.
