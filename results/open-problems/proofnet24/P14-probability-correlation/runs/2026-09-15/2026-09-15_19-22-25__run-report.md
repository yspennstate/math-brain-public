Q^\top PX
\stackrel d=

\tag{5.1}

## Uniform one-dimensional density bound

There is

\[
\boxed{
L_{k,K}
=
\max\{\pi^{-1/2},K\sqrt{2k}\}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

such that for every residual projector, every \(r\le k\), and every unit \(t\in\mathbb R^r\),

\[
\boxed{
\|f_{\langle t,\eta\rangle}\|_\infty
\le
L_{k,K}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
\eta=B\xi+g_U,
\qquad
BB^\top\le I_r,
\qquad
g_U\sim N(0,I_r-BB^\top).
\]

For unit \(t\), put

\[
c=B^\top t,
\qquad
\sigma^2=1-\|c\|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\langle t,\eta\rangle
=
c\cdot\xi+\sigma Z_0.
\]

If

\[
\sigma\ge2^{-1/2},
\]

Gaussian convolution gives

\[
\|f_{\langle t,\eta\rangle}\|_\infty
\le
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}
\le
\pi^{-1/2}.
\]

If

\[
\sigma<2^{-1/2},
\]

then

\[
\|c\|>2^{-1/2}.
\]

Hence some coordinate satisfies

\[
|c_j|
\ge
\frac{\|c\|}{\sqrt k}
>
\frac1{\sqrt{2k}}.
\]

Condition on all other variables. Convolution and translation cannot increase the density ceiling of \(c_j\xi_j\), so

\[
\|f_{\langle t,\eta\rangle}\|_\infty
\le
\frac K{|c_j|}
\le
K\sqrt{2k}.
\]

This proves `(5.3)`.

The argument is pointwise in the realized residual projector.

QED.

# 6. `P14-FIXEDK-EMPIRICAL-FRAME-26` — PROVED

This is the first new probabilistic ingredient.

Let \(\eta\in\mathbb R^r\) be centered and isotropic and suppose every one-dimensional projection has density at most \(L\):

\[
E\eta\eta^\top=I_r,
\qquad
\sup_{\|a\|=1}
\|f_{\langle a,\eta\rangle}\|_\infty
\le L.
\]

Let \(X_N\) be the \(N\times r\) matrix with iid rows distributed as \(\eta\).

For fixed \(r,L\), there are constants

\[
c_{r,L}>0,
\qquad
C_{r,L}<\infty
\]

such that for every sufficiently large \(N\),

\[
\boxed{
P\{s_{\min}(X_N)^2<c_{r,L}N\}
\le
e^{-N/C_{r,L}}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

No moment above two is used.

## Proof

Set

\[
\kappa=\frac1{16L},
\qquad
R_0=2\sqrt r.
\]

For every unit \(a\),

\[
P\{|\langle a,\eta\rangle|\le2\kappa\}
\le
4L\kappa
=
\frac14.
\]

Also isotropy gives

\[
E\|\eta\|^2=r,
\]

hence Markov gives

\[
P\{\|\eta\|>R_0\}
\le
\frac14.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P\{
|\langle a,\eta\rangle|\ge2\kappa,
\ \|\eta\|\le R_0
\}
\ge
\frac12.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Take an \(\varepsilon\)-net of \(S^{r-1}\) with

\[
\varepsilon=\kappa/R_0.
\]

Its cardinality is a finite constant depending only on \(r,L\).

Choose a fixed block length \(N_0=N_0(r,L)\) so large that, simultaneously for every net point, at least \(N_0/4\) of the \(N_0\) iid rows satisfy the event in `(6.2)` with probability at least \(1/2\). This follows by Hoeffding plus a union bound over the finite net.

On that event, for arbitrary unit \(a\), choose a net point \(a_0\) with

\[
\|a-a_0\|\le\varepsilon.
\]

For every row counted as good for \(a_0\),

\[
|\langle a,\eta_i\rangle|
\ge
2\kappa-R_0\varepsilon
=
\kappa.
\]

Hence

\[
\|X_{N_0}a\|^2
\ge
\frac{N_0}{4}\kappa^2
\]

for every unit \(a\), and therefore

\[
s_{\min}(X_{N_0})^2
\ge
\frac{\kappa^2}{4}N_0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Call such a block good. Each disjoint \(N_0\)-row block is good with probability at least \(1/2\).

For general \(N\), split the rows into

\[
M=\lfloor N/N_0\rfloor
\]

disjoint full blocks plus a remainder. Since Gram matrices add,

\[
s_{\min}(X_N)^2
=
\lambda_{\min}(X_N^\top X_N)
\ge
\sum_{b=1}^{M}
\lambda_{\min}(X_b^\top X_b).
\]

On the event that at least \(M/4\) blocks are good,

\[
s_{\min}(X_N)^2
\ge
\frac{M}{4}
\frac{\kappa^2N_0}{4}
\ge
c_{r,L}N
\]

for all sufficiently large \(N\).

A Chernoff bound gives

\[
P\{\#\text{ good blocks}<M/4\}
\le
e^{-cM}
\le
e^{-N/C_{r,L}}.
\]

This proves `(6.1)`.

QED.

### Why this lemma is source-safe

The rows are independent because they are generated by distinct **fresh original rows** after the residual geometry is frozen.

No independence between the coordinates of \(\eta\) is used.

# 7. Gaussian column-space geometry

Now fix a residual rank \(D\) and the decomposition `(5.1)`.

Let

\[
r\le k,
\qquad
p=D-r,
\qquad
n=D-1=p+r-1.
\]

Take \(n\) iid residual rows. Their matrix has block form

\[
\mathcal A=[X\mid G],
\]

where

\[
X\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}
\]

has iid rows distributed as \(\eta\), and

\[
G\in\mathbb R^{n\times p}
\]

is an independent standard Gaussian matrix.

For \(D>k\) we have \(p\ge1\). The finitely many dimensions \(D\le k\) will be absorbed by the trivial bound later.

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{col}(G)\subset\mathbb R^n.
\]

Almost surely

\[
\dim S=p,
\qquad
\dim S^\perp=r-1.
\]

Let \(H\) be any orthonormal basis of \(S^\perp\). A null vector

\[
(a,b)\in\mathbb R^r\oplus\mathbb R^p
\]

of \(\mathcal A\) satisfies

\[
Xa+Gb=0.
\]

Applying \(H^\top\),

\[
H^\top Xa=0.
\]

Almost surely \(H^\top X\) has rank \(r-1\), so its kernel is one-dimensional. Choose its unit generator \(a\). Then

\[
y=Xa\in S
\]

and \(b\) is the unique solution of

\[
Gb=-y.
\]

The unit null vector is

\[
v=\frac{(a,b)}{\sqrt{1+\|b\|^2}}.
\]

Define its total mass in the non-Gaussian \(r\)-dimensional sector by

\[
\Theta
=
\|v_{\rm sp}\|^2
=
\frac1{1+\|b\|^2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

# 8. `P14-GAUSSBULK-TOPMASS-26` — PROVED

Conditional on \(X\) and the Gaussian column space \(S\), there is a scalar

\[
Z_r\sim\chi_r^2
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\Theta
\stackrel d=
\frac{Z_r}{Z_r+\|Xa\|^2}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
E[\Theta\mid X,S]
\le
\min\left\{
1,
\frac r{s_{\min}(X)^2}
\right\}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

## Proof of the exact \(\chi_r^2\) reduction

Take a thin QR factorization

\[
G=QR.
\]

For a standard \(n\times p\) Gaussian matrix,

- \(Q\) is Haar on the Stiefel manifold \(V_{n,p}\);
- \(Q\) is independent of \(R\);
- \(R^\top R\sim W_p(n,I)\).

Conditional on the column space \(S\), write

\[
Q=Q_S O,
\]

where \(Q_S\) is a fixed orthonormal basis of \(S\) and

\[
O\in O(p)
\]

is Haar and independent of \(R\).

Put

\[
W=OR.
\]

Then

\[
G=Q_SW.
\]

The matrix \(WW^\top\) is orthogonally invariant and has the Wishart eigenvalue law, hence

\[
WW^\top\sim W_p(n,I).
\]

Let

\[
z=Q_S^\top y.
\]

Then \(\|z\|=\|y\|\) and

\[
b=-W^{-1}z.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|b\|^2
=
z^\top(WW^\top)^{-1}z.
\]

For a Wishart matrix \(Q_0\sim W_p(n,I)\) and fixed nonzero \(z\),

\[
\left[
\frac{z^\top Q_0^{-1}z}{\|z\|^2}
\right]^{-1}
\sim
\chi^2_{n-p+1}.
\]

Here

\[
n-p+1=r.
\]

For completeness, the scalar Wishart identity follows by rotating \(z\) to \(e_1\) and taking the Schur complement of the lower \((p-1)\times(p-1)\) block in a \(p\times n\) standard Gaussian Gram matrix: the reciprocal inverse diagonal entry is the squared distance of one Gaussian row to the span of \(p-1\) independent Gaussian rows, hence \(\chi^2_{n-p+1}\).

Thus

\[
\|b\|^2
\stackrel d=
\frac{\|Xa\|^2}{Z_r},
\]

which proves `(8.1)`.

Since

\[
EZ_r=r
\]

and

\[
\|Xa\|^2
\ge
s_{\min}(X)^2
\]

for unit \(a\),

\[
E[\Theta\mid X,S]
\le
\frac{r}{s_{\min}(X)^2},
\]

while trivially \(\Theta\le1\). This gives `(8.2)`.

QED.

# 10. Proof of `P14-FINITEMODE-GAUSSBULK-26`

\[
\boxed{
M_P
=
\begin{pmatrix}
B_P&0\\
0&\beta_P I_p
\end{pmatrix},
}
\tag{10.1}
