`D = m - |S ∩ T|`,

`A = S ∩ T`, `B = S \ T`, `C = T \ S`.

`|A|=m-D`, `|B|=|C|=D-1`.

The main source regime is `D>=3`; `D<=2` is separated into the high-overlap/projection-density error bucket.

`E_A = span(A)^⊥`, `dim(E_A)=D`,

and choose an isometry `Q_A : R^D -> E_A`.

For a genuinely fresh original product-law row `X`, the residual row is

`Y = Q_A^T X`.

`rho^2 = (v·w)^2`.

`M_A = E[vv^T | A]`,

# 5. Proof of `P14-RANK3-SPAN-CANCEL-30`

Fix `P` of rank `d+1` and a sibling direction `u` generated by a fresh original row `X`.

Expose the first `d-2` common-future rows. Their orthogonal complement in `Ran(P)` is a rank-three projector `R`, independent of `X` conditional on the frozen parent. Let `Y` be the **last** common-future original row, independent of both `R` and `X`.

Put

`q = q_R(u) = RX/||RX||`.

For a fixed rank-three checkpoint and unit `q`, let `L_R(q,Y)` be the line projector left after imposing the final row `Y`; equivalently it projects onto

`Ran(R) ∩ q^⊥ ∩ (RY)^⊥`.

Because `q` is the direction of `RX`, the same line can be written symmetrically as

`L_R(X,Y) = Proj( Ran(R) ∩ span(RX,RY)^⊥ )`.

The rank-three child anisotropy is

`B_R(q) = E_Y L_R(q,Y) - (R-qq^T)/2`.

Its sibling difference is exactly the inherited terminal block `mathcal E_2^R(q,q')`.

At `r=2`, the outer checkpoint term is

`H_{2|d}^P(u) = A_{2|d}^P(u)/2 - uu^T/d`,

where `A_{2|d}^P(u)=E_R qq^T`.

Now compute the one-sibling representative before taking a sibling difference:

`E_R B_R(q_R(u)) - H_{2|d}^P(u)`
` = E_{R,Y} L_R(X,Y) - (1/2)E_R R + (1/2)E_R qq^T`
`   - (1/2)E_R qq^T + uu^T/d`
` = E_{R,Y}[ L_R(X,Y)-R/2 | u,P ] + uu^T/d`.

Define

`K_R(X,Y)=L_R(X,Y)-R/2`.

Therefore

`Psi_d^P(u)=E_{R,Y}[K_R(X,Y)|U=u,P]+uu^T/d`

satisfies

`mathcal E_d^P(u,u')=Psi_d^P(u)-Psi_d^P(u')`.

This is the asserted exact cancellation.

## 5.1 Symmetry and boundedness

`L_R(X,Y)` depends only on the unordered two-plane `span(RX,RY)`. Hence

`K_R(X,Y)=K_R(Y,X)`.

Almost surely the two projected rows are linearly independent under bounded density. On the final line, `K_R` has eigenvalue `+1/2`; on their rank-two span it has eigenvalue `-1/2`. Therefore

`tr K_R = -1/2`,

`||K_R||_F^2 = 3/4`

pathwise.

Thus the combined terminal-plus-outer observable is bounded even though formulas for the individual normalized directions contain inverse projected norms and inverse angular gaps.

## 5.2 Haar/Gaussian calibration

For a fixed rank-three `R`, if the final row is Gaussian and `q` is fixed, the final line is Haar in the rank-two plane `R-q q^T`, so

`E_Y L_R(q,Y)=(R-qq^T)/2`.

Hence `B_R(q)=0`.

If in addition the checkpoint is Haar inside the parent, then

`A_{2|d}^P(u)=(2/d)uu^T + (d-2)/(d(d+1))P`.

Consequently

`Psi_d^P(u)=-(d-2)/(2d(d+1)) P`,

independent of `u`, and the sibling error vanishes pointwise.

This is the exact calibration required of any future comparison argument.

# 6. Proof of `P14-REVEAL-ANISOTROPY-COBOUNDARY-30`

Continue with a fixed rank-(d+1) parent `P`.

Let

`S=E[UU^T|P]`,

`delta=S-P/(d+1)`.

Let `M=M_P` be the parent tail covariance and

`C=M-P/(d+1)`.

For the child selected by `U`, let

`M_U=M_{P-UU^T}`,

`C_U=M_U-(P-UU^T)/d`.

Set

`K=E[C_U|P]`.

The inherited one-step identity is

`C=K-delta/d`,

hence

`K=C+delta/d`.

Define

`Q=E||M_U-M||_F^2`,

`V=E||C_U-K||_F^2`.

Since

`M_U-M=(C_U-K) - (UU^T-S)/d`,

we expand

`Q = V - (2/d) E <C_U-K, UU^T-S>`
`      + (1/d^2) E||UU^T-S||_F^2`.

The support identity `C_U U=0` gives

`E <C_U-K, UU^T-S> = -<K,S>`.

Because `K` is trace zero and `S=P/(d+1)+delta`,

`<K,S>=<K,delta>`.

Also

`E||UU^T-S||_F^2 = 1-tr(S^2)`.

Since `tr(delta)=0`,

`tr(S^2)=1/(d+1)+||delta||_F^2`.

Therefore

`Q = V + (2/d)<K,delta>`
`      + [1-1/(d+1)-||delta||_F^2]/d^2`.

Substitute `K=C+delta/d`:

`Q = V + 1/[d(d+1)] + (2/d)<C,delta> + ||delta||_F^2/d^2`.

Finally,

`||K||_F^2-||C||_F^2`
` = (2/d)<C,delta> + ||delta||_F^2/d^2`,

and `V+||K||_F^2=E||C_U||_F^2`, proving all three displayed forms in the executive summary.

## 6.1 Interpretation

The raw reveal square is **not** “anisotropy variance plus an absolute fresh-frame error.” It is the Gaussian baseline `1/[d(d+1)]` plus a signed anisotropy-energy coboundary.

The prior sufficient target `V=O(d^-2)` remains useful, but this identity shows it is not logically necessary as an isolated proof obligation: a larger `V` can in principle be offset by the negative energy drift `||K||^2-||C||^2`.

This is precisely why taking the terminal and outer checkpoint pieces separately is structurally too expensive.

The identity above makes clear that the missing theorem is control of the signed energy transport, not an absolute sum of one-step frame errors.

# 8. Proof of `P14-SYMMETRIC-CORRECTOR-HOEFFDING-30`

Fix the same parent `P`. Draw `d` iid genuinely fresh original rows and let

`U_1,...,U_d`

be their normalized projections into `Ran(P)`. Let `Lambda` be the unit null-line projector orthogonal to all `d` directions.

Define

`Z_d = Lambda + (1/d) sum_{j=1}^d U_j U_j^T`.

This is a symmetric Hilbert-space-valued statistic of the `d` iid original rows.

Let `S=E UU^T`. Conditional on `U_1=u`, the remaining rows are still iid and fresh, and

`E[Lambda | U_1=u,P]=M_u`.

Therefore

`E[Z_d | U_1=u,P]`
` = M_u + uu^T/d + (d-1)S/d`.

Since

`E Z_d = M_P+S`,

subtracting the mean gives

`E[Z_d|U_1=u,P]-E Z_d`
` = M_u-M_P + (uu^T-S)/d`
` = C_u-K`.

Hence

`V_{d+1}(P)=Var(E[Z_d|U_1,P]|P)`.

This is the exact first-order Hoeffding component.

## 8.1 Total variance formula

Pathwise, `Lambda U_j=0` for every `j`. Thus the cross term between `Lambda` and the empirical row frame vanishes, and

`||Z_d||_F^2`
` = 1 + d^(-2) || sum_j U_jU_j^T ||_F^2`.

Taking expectation and using iid rows,

`E||Z_d||_F^2`
` = 1 + 1/d + (d-1) tr(S^2)/d`.

Therefore

`Var(Z_d|P)`
` = 1 + 1/d + (d-1)tr(S^2)/d - ||M_P+S||_F^2`.

By the Hilbert-valued Hoeffding decomposition, symmetry gives the generic bound

`d V_{d+1}(P) <= Var(Z_d|P)`.

## 8.2 Gaussian calibration and no-go

For Haar/Gaussian rows in the parent,

`S=M_P=P/(d+1)`.

Then

`Var(Z_d|P)=(d-1)/(d+1)`.

But also

`M_u=(P-uu^T)/d`,

so `C_u=0` and therefore

`V_{d+1}(P)=0` exactly.

Thus the generic ANOVA inequality gives only

`V_{d+1}(P) <= (d-1)/(d(d+1)) = Theta(1/d)`

in a model where the exact answer is zero. It loses the entire sharp factor needed for P14.

### `P14-ANOVA-GAUSSIAN-NOGO-30` — PROVED

A proof based only on symmetry of the corrected d-row statistic and total-variance / Efron–Stein / generic Hoeffding contraction cannot deliver the target scale. The argument must use the **special Gaussian-degenerate first-order structure** of `Z_d` or the equivalent bounded two-row span kernel from Section 5.

If `f(x)=prod_i f_i(x_i)` and every one-dimensional factor density is bounded by one, every `k`-dimensional orthogonal marginal density is bounded by `2^(k/2)`. Scaling a common scalar ceiling `K` gives `2^(k/2)K^k`.

This is a theorem about the **joint density of a projected fresh product vector**. It never says projected coordinates are independent.

## Polavarapu–Fernandez

Rechecked the current arXiv record for Achintya Raya Polavarapu and Manuel Fernandez, *Intermediate Singular Values of Random Matrices under Second-Moment and Anti-Concentration Assumptions*, arXiv:2608.30722, submitted 2026-08-31.

For square matrices with independent centered variance-one entries satisfying a fixed one-dimensional anti-concentration condition, the theorem gives

`P{ s_{n+1-l}(A) > C t l/sqrt(n) } <= exp[-c min(tl,n)]`

for `t>=1` and `l` up to a fixed fraction of `n`, with no moment beyond variance. A scalar density ceiling `K` supplies the required anti-concentration, e.g. at radius `1/(4K)` with concentration at most `1/2`.

This validates the prior P14 sharp checkpoint-coherence theorem's imported hypothesis. The new identities in this run do not require an additional external theorem.
