# 7. New theorem: size-biased / kappa criterion

Define

`kappa(R)=sum_i ell_i^2`.

Fix `0<tau<=1`.

For `ell_i<=tau`, monotonicity of `Omega_xi` gives

`Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(ell_i)) <= Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(tau))`,

so the low-leverage contribution is at most

`3 Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(tau))`.

For `ell_i>tau`, use `Omega_xi<=2` and

`sum_{ell_i>tau} ell_i <= kappa(R)/tau`.

Hence

`sum_i ell_i Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(ell_i))`

`<= 3 Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(tau)) + 2 kappa(R)/tau`.

Define

`Phi_{xi,K}(s)` as `C_K` times the infimum over `epsilon,delta,tau` of

`epsilon^2 log(e/epsilon) + epsilon^(-3) delta`

`+ epsilon^(-2)[ Omega_xi(delta/sqrt(tau)) + s/tau ]`.

Then

`sup_{q,q'} ||E_2^R(q,q')||_F <= Phi_{xi,K}(kappa(R))`.

## Vanishing under finite variance

Take, for small `s`,

`tau=s^(1/2)`,

`R_s=tau^(-1/4)`,

`delta=sqrt(tau) R_s=tau^(1/4)`.

Then `R_s->infinity`, `delta->0`, and `s/tau=s^(1/2)->0`. Finite variance gives `Omega_xi(R_s)->0`.

Let

`b_s=Omega_xi(R_s)+s^(1/2)`

and choose

`epsilon_s = delta^(1/4) + b_s^(1/4)`

for all sufficiently small `s`. Then

`epsilon_s^(-3) delta <= delta^(1/4)->0`,

`epsilon_s^(-2) b_s <= b_s^(1/2)->0`,

and

`epsilon_s^2 log(e/epsilon_s)->0`.

Therefore

`Phi_{xi,K}(s)->0`.

This proves the deterministic criterion.

For a random checkpoint, average the pre-optimization inequality first. If

`s_m=E kappa(R_3)->0`,

then

`E sup_{q,q'} ||E_2^{R_3}(q,q')||_F <= Phi_{xi,K}(s_m)->0`.
