# 4. Proof of `P14-FRESH-PROJECTION-TWOSIDED-FRAME-22`

Let \(X\in\mathbb R^m\) have iid centered variance-one coordinates with density ceiling \(K\).

Let \(P\) be a deterministic rank-\(d\) orthogonal projector.

For a unit vector \(z\in\operatorname{Ran}P\), put

\[
N=(z^\top X)^2,
\qquad
W=X^\top PX.
\]

Then

\[
z^\top\mathcal R_\xi(P)z
=
E\frac NW.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because \(z\in\operatorname{Ran}P\),

\[
N\le W.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Also

\[
EN=1.
\]

The previous run proved two uniform facts.

### Uniform linear-form integrability

The family

\[
\{(z^\top X)^2:
m\ge1,\ \|z\|=1\}
\]

is uniformly integrable.

### Uniform projection weak law

For every fixed \(\delta>0\),

\[
\sup_{\substack{m\ge d\\\operatorname{rank}P=d}}
P\left\{
\left|\frac Wd-1\right|>\delta
\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

These use finite variance only.

## 4.1 Lower frame

The previous proof gives, uniformly,

\[
dE[N/W]\ge1-o(1).
\]

Indeed, on

\[
W\le(1+\delta)d,
\]

\[
\frac NW
\ge
\frac{N}{(1+\delta)d},
\]

and uniform integrability plus `(4.3)` shows

\[
E[
N1_{\{W>(1+\delta)d\}}
]
\to0.
\]

Then let \(\delta\downarrow0\) diagonally.

## 4.2 Extreme lower-tail estimate

The upper frame requires a bound on very small denominators.

The Livshyts–Paouris–Pivovarov product-marginal theorem says:

if all scalar coordinate densities are bounded by one, every \(d\)-dimensional orthogonal marginal density is bounded by \(2^{d/2}\).

After scaling to density ceiling \(K\), the density of \(PX\) on \(\operatorname{Ran}P\) is bounded by

\[
2^{d/2}K^d.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Use the ball-volume bound

\[
\operatorname{vol}
B_2^d(\sqrt{\alpha d})
\le
(2\pi e\alpha)^{d/2}.
\]

Let

\[
\bar K=\max\{1,K\},
\qquad
\alpha_K
=
\frac1{16\pi e\bar K^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
P\{W<\alpha_Kd\}
&=
P\{\|PX\|<\sqrt{\alpha_Kd}\}
\\
&\le
2^{d/2}K^d
(2\pi e\alpha_K)^{d/2}
\\
&\le
2^{-d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

No coordinate independence of \(PX\) is used.

## 4.3 Upper frame

Fix \(0<\delta<1-\alpha_K\) and split

\[

=
E[N/W;W\ge(1-\delta)d]
+
E[N/W;\alpha_Kd\le W<(1-\delta)d]

E[N/W;W<\alpha_Kd].

The first term is at most

\[
\frac1{(1-\delta)d}.
\]

The middle term is at most

\[
\frac1{\alpha_Kd}
E[
N1_{\{W<(1-\delta)d\}}

\[

E[
N1_{\{W<(1-\delta)d\}}

\tag{4.6}

\frac1{1-\delta}
