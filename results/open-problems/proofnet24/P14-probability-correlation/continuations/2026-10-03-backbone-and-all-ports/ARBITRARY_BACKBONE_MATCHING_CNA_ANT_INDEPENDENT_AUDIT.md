## 1. Prior theorem is the correct ordinary forest input

In the preceding audit I independently opened the full primary PDF and

\(\sum_{I\text{ independent}}y^{n-|I|}\prod_{i\in I}z_i\)

Since its constant term F is positive, the selected-edge polynomial's

log-concavity, not an assumed sign for each mixed derivative.

## 2. Hypothetical triangle and its exact Hessian

The split baseline is an ACTUAL weighted graph: delete all selected
matching edges but retain every endpoint, spoke and backbone edge. For
each split forest, let A_ij count its activity if ports i,j are distinct
components, and let r count its activity if all three ports are separate.
Adding one hypothetical triangle edge is allowed precisely in the first
case. Adding any two is allowed precisely in the all-separated case.
If two ports are already connected, those two new edges either include
an immediate cycle or form a cycle after the first joins the other port.
Adding all three is never allowed because the added triangle is a cycle.
Therefore the complete selected-variable partition is exactly

\[
Z(z)=F+A_{12}z_{12}+A_{13}z_{13}+A_{23}z_{23}
+r(z_{12}z_{13}+z_{12}z_{23}+z_{13}z_{23}).
\]

The Hessian of Z at zero has zero diagonal and off-diagonal r, so
the negative logarithmic Hessian, multiplied by F², is

\[
AA^T-rF(\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-I_3)\succeq0.
\]

The scaling is rF, not r² or F². Evaluating on
\(x=(b_1b_2,b_1b_3,b_2b_3)\) gives

\[
A^Tx=M,\qquad
\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j=Bb_1b_2b_3,\qquad
J=rb_1b_2b_3,
\]
\[
\boxed{M^2\ge2BJF.}
\]

I checked all factors and the pairing of port indices independently.
This bound need not hold for every arbitrary nonnegative five-partition
row; the fact that the split row is generated by an actual graphic
matroid is essential. The written proof correctly preserves that fact.

For the second gate, every all-separated split forest permits all three
two-spoke choices. Thus (M\ge r e_2(b)\). AMGM gives
\(Be_2(b)\ge9b_1b_2b_3\), so (BM\ge9J\).
This argument includes zero column factors and r=0 directly.

## 3. Transfer algebra and exact whole matching law

The five port partitions are sufficient states even for an arbitrary
backbone. A newly added star creates a cycle exactly when its selected
spokes hit already connected port blocks. Counting zero/one/two/three
spokes proves

\[
T(x)=(1+Bx)I+x^2P+x^3Q,\quad
P^2=2BQ,\quad PQ=QP=Q^2=0.
\]

The six ordered successful two-join products from the separated state
have summed weight (2Bb_1b_2b_3\); from a two-block state a second
join vanishes. This verifies the operator identity on all five states.
The exact exponential form follows since

\[
\gamma(x)+B\alpha(x)^2
=x^3/(1+Bx).
\]

The total split baseline is (v_0=v\prod_jT(a_j)\), and
(F=v_01,M=v_0P1,J=v_0Q1\) already include every split endpoint.
There is NO additional scalar D outside these actual-baseline quantities.

Contraction of a marked matching edge sums its endpoints' rows. Parallel
spokes aggregate exactly: two parallel choices cannot both be selected,
and summing the possible individual choices gives their total activity.
Because the terminal contractions are disjoint and the transfer algebra
commutes, replacing each split pair by its contracted pair has ratio
\(c_i\exp(\delta_iP+\epsilon_iQ)\). The needed inverse exists because
the identity coefficient d is positive and the remaining algebra is
nilpotent. No probabilistic independence of the split forest is assumed.

Expanding all marked pairs at once gives

\[
Z_{\rm mark}(\boldsymbol w)=\prod_i(1+c_iw_i)
\left[F+\sum_i h_i\delta_i\theta_i
+2BJ\sum_{i<j}\delta_i\delta_j\theta_i\theta_j\right],
\]

with the stated (h_i=M+J\epsilon_i/\delta_i+BJ\delta_i\).
In particular the within-pair second-order contribution is
(BJ\delta_i^2\), and the cross-pair coefficient is (2BJ\).
Both are retained. This is the exact multi-affine marginal partition
for the entire matching, not a pairwise-only calculation.

The OR representation also holds configuration by configuration.
Writing the forced-subset weight as (f(S)\prod_{i\in S}\theta_i\),
the conditional Bernoulli probability of a configuration containing S
divides by the same product. Their cancellation produces
\(\Pr(R=x)\sum_{S\subseteq x}f(S)\), with the bracket normalizer.
The coefficient expansion of the displayed partition gives precisely
this configuration weight. Agreement of only partition values would be
insufficient; the stronger identity used here is correct.

## 4. Negative-association lemma and its gates

I checked the full self-contained support-at-most-two lemma in Section
4, including K=0. The hit-event covariance expansion keeps all internal
pair terms. Under (h_{\min}^2\ge KF\) and
\(2h_{\min}\ge h_{\max}\), every term in (14) is nonnegative.
Weighted beta averages are within those same bounds; when s_C=0 its
beta value is multiplied by zero.

For arbitrary nontrivial increasing events on disjoint blocks, a pair-
only trigger in one block cannot co-occur with a nontrivial trigger in
the other on support size at most two. Thus the joint event is exactly
the joint hit event of the singleton-trigger sets, and each marginal
is at least its hit probability. This proves the full event inequality.
The upper-level-set expansion with a constant term handles all increasing
real functions. Averaging independently over Bernoullis on disjoint
blocks is then a direct valid NA-preservation argument for the OR law.

For positive rows and B>0, the exact d identity and superadditivity
prove

\[
0<\delta_i<1/B^2,\qquad0<\epsilon_i/\delta_i<2/B.
\]

For the second bound, \(((2/B)\alpha-\gamma)/x\)
equals \(B^{-2}(1-d(x)^{-2})\), strictly increasing. Therefore,
when M>0,

\[
M\le h_i\le M+3J/B\le4M/3.
\]

The first gate follows from the independently proved Hessian bound:
\(h_{\min}^2\ge M^2\ge2BJF=KF\).
The second follows from (4M/3<2M\le2h_{\min}\).
When J=0, all h_i=M, so the argument still applies. It does not require
the Hessian lower bound to be strict.

## 5. Zero boundaries and combined conditioning

If M=0, (BM\ge9J\) forces J=0. The bracket then reduces to F,
and the matching law is a product of independent Bernoullis with
parameters \(\theta_i=c_iw_i/(1+c_iw_i)\). If B=0 then c_i=1 and
each marked edge is an isolated independent component. If only one
column is positive, P=Q=0 and again the matching coordinates are
independent, although their parameters generally still include c_i.
The proof keeps that distinction correctly.

A zero endpoint row makes that endpoint a leaf with its sole marked
edge a bridge. Its indicator is independent of the remaining forest;
removing such coordinates avoids division by zero in epsilon/delta.
Zero columns cause no other division problem because all division by B
is confined to B>0. No strict-correlation claim is made at the boundary.

For conditioning backbone edges present, their required subset must be
acyclic under a positive-probability condition. Graphic-matroid
contraction and deletion give another ordinary weighted forest graph.
If ports merge, a hypothetical triangle edge can become a loop and
r=J=0; the linear/quadratic partition and Hessian argument still apply.
One can retain the original port labels as connectivity states, or
aggregate their column factors after contraction. Neither approach
assumes three distinct ports survive.

Conditioned-present marked edges merge disjoint terminal rows and can
be absorbed into the arbitrary backbone. Conditioned-absent marked
edges disappear; their unused endpoint stars can also be included in
the backbone. Remaining selected matching endpoints retain only their
proportional spokes and their single marked edge. Required edge
activities contribute an overall constant that cancels. Combining these
operations therefore leaves the same family, and reapplying the theorem
proves the full stated conditional property. Positive fields on
backbone and marked edges merely change permitted activities. Individual
spoke fields or spoke conditioning need not preserve the family and are
properly excluded.
