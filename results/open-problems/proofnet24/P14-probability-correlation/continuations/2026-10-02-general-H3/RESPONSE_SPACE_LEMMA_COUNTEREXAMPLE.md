# Counterexample to the all-orders linear response-space lemma

Independent calculation, 2 October 2026. This note challenges only the proposed
all-orders lemma. It does not challenge the first-order boundary response or
the already audited H2 sum-of-squares identity.

## Graph and zero-response direction

Let the interior be E={a,b}, with marked edge ab of conductance c>0.
Let S={s,r}; join both a and b to s with conductance d1>0, and both a and b
to r with conductance d2>0. Put d=d1+d2, p1=d1/d, p2=d2/d. The Dirichlet
Laplacian and residual covariance are

\[
 L_E=\begin{pmatrix}c+d&-c\\-c&c+d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=L_E^{-1},
\]

and both harmonic exit rows are p=(p1,p2). The unit-current potential is

\[
 v_a=(2c+d)^{-1},\qquad v_b=-(2c+d)^{-1},\qquad v_s=v_r=0.
\]

The marked current has balanced flux at each boundary vertex. On every spoke,
the proposed response row is a scalar multiple of (1,-1), and on ab it is
zero. Consequently h=(1,1) is in the kernel of every eta_g. The claimed
linear response-space lemma would exclude this common boundary mode from the
marked score at every order.

## Exact conditional factor

Use Grassmann variables q_x=bar(psi_x) psi_x and let

\[
 V_E=q_a+q_b-cq_aq_b-
 d_1(q_a+q_b)q_s-d_2(q_a+q_b)q_r.
\]

Conditionally on the boundary, the two interior means are the same field
m=p1 psi_s+p2 psi_r. Define

\[
 Q=(\bar\psi_s-\bar\psi_r)(\psi_s-\psi_r),\qquad
 R=q_sq_r,
\]

and

\[
 \alpha=D_{aa}=D_{bb}=\frac{c+d}{d(2c+d)},\quad
 \delta=\det D=\frac1{d(2c+d)},\quad
 \rho=D_{aa}+D_{bb}-2D_{ab}=\frac2{2c+d}.
\]

The only conditional Wick moments needed are

\[
 \langle q_a+q_b\rangle=2\alpha+2\bar m m,\qquad
 \langle q_aq_b\rangle=\delta+\rho\bar m m.
\]

Multiplication by boundary q variables commutes with conditional expectation.
Since every monomial of V_E contains q_a or q_b, V_E^3=0. Direct multiplication
gives

\[
 \frac{V_E^2}{2}=q_aq_b-2d_1q_aq_bq_s-2d_2q_aq_bq_r
                  +2d_1d_2q_aq_bq_sq_r.
\]

Writing z for the expansion parameter (to distinguish it from a boundary
vertex), the exact conditional factor F_E=<exp(z V_E)> is

\[
 F_E=f_0+f_1Q+f_2R,
\]

where, with W=c(2d+z)+(d+z)^2,

\[
 f_0=\frac{W}{d(2c+d)},\quad
 f_1=-\frac{2p_1p_2z(c+d+z)}{2c+d},\quad
 f_2=-\frac{2dp_1p_2z(2c+d+z)}{2c+d}.
\]

These formulas follow also from

\[
 \langle V_E\rangle=
 \frac{c+2d}{d(2c+d)}
 -\frac{2p_1p_2(c+d)}{2c+d}Q-2dp_1p_2R,
\]

\[
 \frac{\langle V_E^2\rangle}{2}=
 \frac1{d(2c+d)}-
 \frac{2p_1p_2}{2c+d}Q-
 \frac{2dp_1p_2}{2c+d}R.
\]

## Marked score and failure of the lemma

Because Q^2=QR=R^2=0, taking the formal logarithm and differentiating c gives

\[
 \partial_c\log F_E=
 \partial_c\log f_0+
 \frac{2d^2p_1p_2z(d+z)}{W^2}\,(Q-zR).
\]

In particular, its nonzero degree-four boundary coefficient is

\[
 -\frac{2d^2p_1p_2z^2(d+z)}{W^2}.
\]

Let u=(psi_s+psi_r)/2 and w=psi_s-psi_r, and make the same change for barred
variables. Then Q=bar(w)w and R=bar(u)u bar(w)w. Therefore the marked score
contains the common mode u corresponding to h=(1,1), despite eta_g h=0 for
every g. This refutes the claim that all nonconstant boundary dependence is
confined to the exterior algebra generated by the linear eta modes.

For c=d1=d2=1 the exact nonconstant score is especially simple:

\[
 \frac{2z(z+2)}{(z^2+5z+8)^2}\,[Q-zR].
\]

Its quartic term begins at -z^2 R/16, while its first-order bilinear is zQ/16,
consistent with the established positive semidefinite first-order response.

The example can be embedded in the global equality geometry by attaching any
positive connected core to s and r, with a grounded vertex outside E. The
Dirichlet conditional factor and all displayed calculations are unchanged.

## Independent exact audit

`response_space_lemma_counterexample_audit.py` builds the exterior algebra
from its generator multiplication signs, substitutes the conditional fields,
and performs residual Gaussian expectation directly. It verifies the exact
F_E and marked score for symbolic c with d1=d2=1, including the nonzero
common-mode quartic. Thus the conclusion does not rely only on a handwritten
Wick expansion.
