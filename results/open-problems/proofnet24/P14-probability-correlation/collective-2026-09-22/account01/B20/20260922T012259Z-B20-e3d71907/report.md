## 3. New exact graph-global compatibility theorem

For `R in A00`, both marked endpoint pairs are connected in the forest `R`. Let `P_e(R)` and `P_f(R)` be the unique paths in `R` joining the endpoints of `e` and `f`, regarded as edge sets. Define

`alpha(R)=|P_e(R)\P_f(R)|`,
`beta(R)=|P_f(R)\P_e(R)|`,
`gamma(R)=|P_e(R) cap P_f(R)|`.

For `H in A10`, let `C_e^0(H),C_e^1(H)` be the two distinct `H`-components containing the endpoints of `e`, and define

`c_e(H)=# {h in E(K): h has one endpoint in C_e^0(H) and one in C_e^1(H)}`.

For `H in A01`, define `c_f(H)` analogously.

If `H in Q`, then the two marked edges connect the **same unordered pair** of `H`-components. Indeed, if `e` joins components `A,B`, adding `e` merges only `A,B`; because `f` is individually addable but becomes cycle-closing after `e` is added, its endpoints must also lie one in `A` and one in `B`. Define

`c_q(H)=# {h in E(K): h joins these two common terminal components}`.

### Theorem `FOREST-PATH-CUT-FLUX-20260922`

For every finite simple graph `G` and every distinct pair `e,f`,

`sum_{R in A00} alpha(R) = sum_{H in A10} c_e(H)`,              (3.1)

`sum_{R in A00} beta(R)  = sum_{H in A01} c_f(H)`,              (3.2)

`sum_{R in A00} gamma(R) = sum_{H in Q}   c_q(H)`.              (3.3)

These are exact integer identities, not asymptotic or probabilistic bounds.

### Proof

For (3.1), count ordered pairs `(R,h)` with `R in A00` and `h in P_e(R)\P_f(R)`. Deleting `h` breaks the unique `e`-path but leaves the unique `f`-path intact, so `H=R-h` lies in `A10`.

Conversely take `H in A10` and an edge `h` of `K` joining the two components containing the endpoints of `e`. Since those are distinct `H`-components, `H+h` is a forest. It makes the endpoints of `e` connected, while the endpoints of `f` were already connected in `H`; hence `R=H+h` lies in `A00`. The unique `e`-path in `R` uses `h`, while the unique `f`-path lies entirely in its old `H`-component and therefore does not use `h`. Thus `h in P_e(R)\P_f(R)`. The two operations are inverse bijections, proving (3.1). Equation (3.2) is symmetric.

For (3.3), count ordered pairs `(R,h)` with `R in A00` and `h in P_e(R) cap P_f(R)`. Deleting the common path edge `h` splits the relevant tree into two components and separates both marked endpoint pairs. Thus `H=R-h` is in `A11`. Both `e` and `f` cross the same newly created component cut, so adding either alone is acyclic while adding both creates a cycle. Hence `H in Q`.

Conversely, if `H in Q`, the observation preceding the theorem gives two common terminal components `A,B`. Every `K`-edge `h` between `A,B` can be added to `H` without creating a cycle. In `R=H+h`, both marked endpoint pairs are connected, and because `h` is the unique newly added connection between `A,B`, both unique marked paths use `h`. Therefore `R in A00` and `h in P_e(R) cap P_f(R)`. Again the operations are inverse bijections. This proves (3.3). QED.

### Immediate global capacity consequences

Because `G` is simple and the marked edge itself has been deleted from `K`, every `P_e(R)` and `P_f(R)` has length at least two. The two paths cannot be the identical two-edge path unless `e=f`; therefore their union has at least three edges. Summing gives the source-valid inequalities

`2*n00 <= sum_{A10} c_e + sum_Q c_q`,                           (3.4)

`2*n00 <= sum_{A01} c_f + sum_Q c_q`,                           (3.5)

`3*n00 <= sum_{A10} c_e + sum_{A01} c_f + sum_Q c_q`.           (3.6)

The right sides depend on actual cuts between components of actual forests of the common graph `K`. They are therefore precisely the kind of global compatibility coordinates absent from the partner's abstract local three-edge mixture.

This does **not** by itself imply (2.1): the cut multiplicities can be large and must still be controlled without paying a graph-size factor.

## 4. A second new lemma: every overlap state has a multi-edge completion in the 3-connected obstruction

Suppose now `G` is 3-connected. Then it is 3-edge-connected, so deleting `e,f` leaves `K` connected.

For `H in Q`, contract each component of `H` to a vertex and retain every unused edge of `K` between the contracted components. Call this quotient graph `Gamma_H`. Since `K` is connected, `Gamma_H` is connected. Let `A,B` be the common terminal components joined by `e` and `f`. Choose any simple `A-B` path in `Gamma_H`. Adding the corresponding `K`-edges one by one to `H` only merges distinct components, so no cycle is created; after the path is added, `A` and `B` are connected and both marked endpoint pairs are connected. Thus the resulting forest belongs to `A00`.

So every `Q` state has a source-valid **multi-edge** route to `A00`, even when `c_q(H)=0` and the one-edge reverse switch in (3.3) has zero degree.

This is important because a one-edge completion is not universal, even in the smallest 3-connected example.

## 5. Source-valid counterexample to the naive direct-reconnect strengthening

`e=01`, `f=02`.

Let `H={12}` in `K=G-{01,02}`. Then `H+e` and `H+f` are forests, while `H+e+f` is the triangle on `0,1,2`; hence `H in Q`.

The common terminal components are `A={0}` and `B={1,2}`. But the remaining `K`-edges are `03,12,13,23`, so there is **no** `K`-edge directly between `A` and `B`. Therefore

`c_q(H)=0`.
