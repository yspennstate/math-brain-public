# C28 Paley ETF — first outside-source run

## 1. Source scope recovered before mathematics

The outside source is Afonso S. Bandeira’s Randomstrasse 101 post “On the clique number of the Paley Graph (problems 25–29)”, posted 15 December 2025 and updated 18 December 2025:

- https://randomstrasse101.math.ethz.ch/posts/PaleyGraph/
- canonical intake row: `probability_1000_intake_I_20260914/problems.jsonl`, I0103.

The source fixes primes `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`. Its Paley ETF has `n=p+1` unit vectors in `C^((p+1)/2)`. Conjecture 29 says there exists a fixed `epsilon>0` such that for `m ~ p^(1/2+epsilon)`, **every** choice of `m` distinct vectors in the Paley ETF has condition number bounded by a constant independent of `p`.

This quantifier structure is essential. A single poorly conditioned finite submatrix does not refute the conjecture: the source allows an unspecified p-independent constant and asks only for existence of some positive epsilon. A negative resolution would need a family satisfying the source’s asymptotic quantifiers, for example a sequence `p_j -> infinity` and admissible `m_j ~ p_j^(1/2+epsilon)` whose worst-submatrix condition numbers diverge for every candidate uniform constant at a relevant fixed epsilon, or an equivalent source-matching obstruction.

The source describes the construction as a quadratic-residue row submatrix of the DFT with a scaled canonical-basis column. The classical detailed construction is in Bandeira–Fickus–Mixon–Wong, *The Road to Deterministic Matrices with the Restricted Isometry Property*, JFAA 19 (2013), and the conditional progress is Bandeira–Mixon–Moreira, arXiv:1410.6457. The 2013 construction gives, for distinct finite Paley columns indexed by `u,v in F_p`, the exact inner product

`<phi_u,phi_v> = chi(u-v)/sqrt(p)`,

where `chi` is the Legendre symbol (for `p ≡ 1 mod 4`; the sign convention is immaterial under simultaneous Seidel switching).

## 5. Exact finite proposition

### Proposition C28-F1 — exact condition number at p=73

Let `Phi_73` be the Paley ETF and take the 14 finite columns indexed by

`S={0,1,2,3,5,32,38,41,45,46,52,54,58,61}`.

Then their Gram matrix has condition number

`kappa(Phi_S) = sqrt((sqrt(73)-1+2sqrt(13))/(sqrt(73)-1-2sqrt(13)))`

and therefore

`kappa(Phi_S) = 6.657535556520428... > 6.6575272085`.

### Proof

For finite Paley columns, define the integer symmetric Legendre-Seidel matrix

`A_S(i,j)=0` for `i=j`,
`A_S(i,j)=chi(s_i-s_j)` for `i != j`.

The Gauss-sum identity in the Paley ETF construction gives

`G_S = Phi_S^* Phi_S = I + A_S/sqrt(73)`.

Direct exact integer determinant computation gives the characteristic polynomial

`det(xI-A_S)`
`=(x-5)(x-3)^2(x-1)^3(x+1)^3(x+3)^3(x^2+2x-51)`.

The quadratic factor has roots `-1 ± 2sqrt(13)`. Comparing these exactly with `-3,-1,1,3,5` gives

`lambda_min(A_S) = -1-2sqrt(13)`,
`lambda_max(A_S) = -1+2sqrt(13)`.

Also `sqrt(73)>1+2sqrt(13)`: after the first positive squaring this is equivalent to `19>4sqrt(13)`, and after squaring again to `361>208`. Thus `G_S` is positive definite.

Its extreme eigenvalues are

`1 + (-1-2sqrt(13))/sqrt(73)` and
`1 + (-1+2sqrt(13))/sqrt(73)`.

Since the 2-norm condition number of the column matrix is the square root of the ratio of the extreme Gram eigenvalues,

`kappa(Phi_S)^2 = (sqrt(73)-1+2sqrt(13))/(sqrt(73)-1-2sqrt(13))`,

which proves the exact formula.

For an independent rational enclosure, the checker certifies

`8.544003 < sqrt(73) < 8.544004`,
`sqrt(13) > 3.605551`,

and hence

`kappa(Phi_S)^2 > 14755105/332902`,
`kappa(Phi_S) > 6.657527208587420`.

This is an exact finite claim, not a floating search result. QED.

## 8. Structural reduction exposed by the certificate

For any finite-only column set `S subset F_p`, the source normalization reduces the conditioning problem exactly to the spectrum of a principal Legendre-Seidel matrix:

`G_S = I + A_S/sqrt(p)`,

and

`kappa(Phi_S)^2 = (sqrt(p)+lambda_max(A_S))/(sqrt(p)+lambda_min(A_S))`.

Thus an asymptotic obstruction to uniform conditioning would follow from a growing admissible family whose `lambda_min(A_S)` approaches `-sqrt(p)` closely enough that the denominator tends to zero relative to the numerator. Conversely, a positive proof needs a uniform spectral exclusion away from `-sqrt(p)` for **all** sets at the target cardinality.

This reformulation points to the next non-numerical route: multiplicative subgroups/cosets `H subset F_p^*`. On such structured sets, `chi(x-y)` can be rewritten after factoring one group element as a multiplicative convolution kernel. The corresponding eigenvectors are multiplicative characters of `H`, and the eigenvalues become explicit character/Jacobi-sum expressions. That creates a route to exact algebraic spectra for an infinite family, rather than repeated finite black-box eigenvalue searches.

The next attack is therefore:

1. derive the exact subgroup/coset eigenvalue formula, including the restriction of the quadratic character to `H`;
2. identify prime/subgroup-size sequences with `|H| ~ p^(1/2+epsilon)` for a fixed epsilon;
3. use exact Jacobi/Gauss-sum identities to decide whether the minimum eigenvalue can approach `-sqrt(p)` sufficiently fast;
4. only if that route fails, return to finite search to identify another algebraically recognizable extremal family.
