## 3. Exact structure hidden in the p=73 extremal candidate

Recall the certified 14-set

\[
S=\{0,1,2,3,5,32,38,41,45,46,52,54,58,61\}\subset\mathbf F_{73}.
\]

Let `A_S` be its Legendre-Seidel matrix,

\[
(A_S)_{uv}=\begin{cases}
0,&u=v,\\
\chi(u-v),&u\ne v,
\end{cases}
\]

where `chi` is the quadratic character modulo 73. The previous exact certificate gave

\[
\det(xI-A_S)=
(x-5)(x-3)^2(x-1)^3(x+1)^3(x+3)^3(x^2+2x-51).
\tag{3.1}
\]

This run identifies a concrete switched equitable partition that explains the exceptional quadratic factor in (3.1).

Define

\[
X=\{0,5,38,46,54,61\},\qquad
Y=\{1,2,3,32,41,45,52,58\}.
\]

Switch signs by

\[
d_0=d_5=d_{38}=-1,\quad d_{46}=d_{54}=d_{61}=+1,
\]

and

\[
d_1=d_2=d_3=d_{32}=d_{41}=+1,\quad
d_{45}=d_{52}=d_{58}=-1.
\]

Let `B=D A_S D`, where `D=diag(d_u)`. Exact evaluation gives:

- every entry of the `X x Y` block is `-1`;
- inside `X`, the `+1` graph is 1-regular, so each internal Seidel row sum is `-3`;
- inside `Y`, the `+1` graph is 4-regular, so each internal Seidel row sum is `+1`.

Hence the two-dimensional subspace of vectors constant on each cell is invariant and has quotient matrix

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}-3&-8\\-6&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(xI-Q)=x^2+2x-51.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the two exceptional eigenvalues are already forced by this signed rank-one rectangle:

\[
\lambda_\pm=-1\pm2\sqrt{13}.
\]

Together with (3.1), these are the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of `A_S`.

### Proposition C28-RECT-02 — equitable signed-rectangle lemma

Let `B` be any real symmetric Seidel-type matrix indexed by a disjoint union `X union Y`, with `|X|=a`, `|Y|=b`. Suppose the cross block is exactly `-J_{a,b}`, every row of `B[X]` has sum `alpha`, and every row of `B[Y]` has sum `delta`. Then the span of the two cell indicators is invariant, and the two associated eigenvalues are

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_\pm=
\frac{\alpha+\delta\pm
\sqrt{(\alpha-\delta)^2+4ab}}{2}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

For a vector taking value `x` on `X` and `y` on `Y`, a row in `X` receives

\[
\alpha x-by,
\]

while a row in `Y` receives

\[
-ax+\delta y.
\]

Thus the cell-constant subspace is invariant with quotient

\[
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-b\\-a&\delta\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In the orthonormal basis `1_X/sqrt(a),1_Y/sqrt(b)`, this restriction is the symmetric matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&-\sqrt{ab}\\
-\sqrt{ab}&\delta
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose two eigenvalues are exactly (3.3). QED.

For the p=73 candidate, `(a,b,alpha,delta)=(6,8,-3,1)`, giving (3.2).

This lemma does not by itself resolve the source conjecture. It does, however, expose a more targeted asymptotic obstruction mechanism than generic local search: a signed Legendre rectangle with `ab` of order `p` can create an eigenvalue of order `-sqrt(p)`. At source scale `a+b ~ p^(3/5)`, the rough rank-one term `sqrt(ab) ~ sqrt(p)` suggests looking for highly unbalanced rectangles with sizes on the order of `p^(2/5)` and `p^(3/5)`. This scaling sentence is a search heuristic, not a theorem asserting such rectangles exist.

such neighbors, and every one has strictly smaller condition number than `S`.

### Proof

Use the full projective Paley conference matrix `C` on `P^1(F_73)`, as justified in the previous run. For every one-swap neighbor `T`, let `A_T=C[T,T]`.

The executed exact checker performs a rational `LDL^T` decomposition of

\[

for every one of the 840 neighbors and proves every pivot strictly positive. Therefore, exactly,

\[
\lambda_{\min}(A_T)>-8.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The smallest pivot encountered over all these exact decompositions is

\[
\frac{7258119}{33969472}>0,
\]

at the neighbor obtained by removing `1` and adding `33`.

Because `A_T` is a 14 by 14 Seidel matrix with zero diagonal and thirteen off-diagonal entries of modulus one in each row, Gershgorin gives

\[
\lambda_{\max}(A_T)\le13.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The Paley Gram matrix is `G_T=I+A_T/sqrt(73)`, so (4.1)–(4.2), together with `sqrt(73)>1068/125`, give

\[
\kappa(T)^2
=
\frac{\sqrt{73}+\lambda_{\max}(A_T)}
{\sqrt{73}+\lambda_{\min}(A_T)}
<
\frac{\sqrt{73}+13}{\sqrt{73}-8}
<
\frac{2693}{68}
<44.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the candidate `S`, the exact formula from the first run gives

\[
\kappa(S)^2=
\frac{\sqrt{73}-1+2\sqrt{13}}
{\sqrt{73}-1-2\sqrt{13}}.
\]

Using the rational enclosures

\[
\frac{1068}{125}<\sqrt{73}<\frac{1709}{200},
\qquad
\frac{721}{200}<\sqrt{13}<\frac{1803}{500},

the denominator is positive and

\[
90\sqrt{13}>43(\sqrt{73}-1),
\]

because already

\[
90\cdot\frac{721}{200}
>
43\left(\frac{1709}{200}-1\right).
\]

This is exactly equivalent to

\[
\kappa(S)^2>44.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equations (4.3)–(4.4) prove strict one-swap local maximality. QED.
