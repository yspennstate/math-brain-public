## 2. Full measured Hessian used in the searches

For `0<K<I_n`, put `B_S=K-I_(S^c)` and

    p_S=(-1)^(n-|S|) det B_S.

These are positive: equivalently `p_S=det L_S/det(I+L)`, where `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0`. For Hermitian directions A and D,

    Dp_S[A]=p_S tr(B_S^(-1) A),
    D²p_S[A,D]=p_S {tr(B_S^(-1)A) tr(B_S^(-1)D)
                                  -tr(B_S^(-1)A B_S^(-1)D)}.

Twice differentiating `H=-sum p log p`, using `sum D²p=0`, gives

    -D²H[A,D] = sum Dp_S[A] Dp_S[D]/p_S
                              +sum D²p_S[A,D] log p_S.       (2.1)

The second summand is signed and is never discarded. `search_hessian.py` evaluates the complete real Hermitian matrix in a Frobenius-orthonormal basis. A scalar finite difference independently checks the second derivative at the beginning of each run. This is a diagnostic consistency check, not a rigorous floating point error bound.

## 3. Exact obstruction: arbitrary random coordinate shuffling does not preserve parameter concavity

This was a proposed route to X088, whose Fourier channel has a special random-swap realization. It fails for arbitrary random coordinate permutations. The distinction between an arbitrary shared permutation coin and a sequence of independent transposition coins is essential.

Let `X(t)` have independent coordinates with probabilities

    (a+t, a-t, b, b),     0<a,b<1.

Independently select one of three coordinate permutations:

* identity with probability `(1-epsilon)/2`;
* exchange coordinates 1 and 2 with probability `(1-epsilon)/2`;
* exchange the two pairs, `(1 3)(2 4)`, with probability `epsilon`.

Let `Y_epsilon(t)` be the permuted vector. This is one fixed stochastic channel applied to a product Bernoulli family.

### 3.1 A general perturbative theorem

**Theorem.** For every `a != b` in `(0,1)`, the function `t -> H(Y_epsilon(t))` has positive second derivative at zero for all sufficiently small positive epsilon. More precisely,

    -d²H(Y_epsilon(t))/dt² at t=0
       = -2 epsilon (a-b)²/[a²(1-a)²] + O(epsilon²).         (3.1)

**Proof.** Write `mu_c(s)=c^s(1-c)^(1-s)` for `s in {0,1}`. At epsilon zero the channel averages a product law and its first-pair exchange. At `t=0` its probability table is

    P(y)=mu_a(y1) mu_a(y2) mu_b(y3) mu_b(y4).

Its first t-derivative is zero. Its second derivative is

    C(y)=-2(1-2y1)(1-2y2) mu_b(y3)mu_b(y4).

The pair-exchange component at `t=0` has table

    R(y)=mu_b(y1)mu_b(y2)mu_a(y3)mu_a(y4).

The complete table is `P+epsilon(R-P)` at `t=0`; its first t-derivative is `O(epsilon)`. Since P has full support, its Fisher term is `O(epsilon²)`. The additional second-derivative table has zero total mass and zero one-coordinate moments, because every coordinate marginal is affine in t. Its pairing with `log P`, a modular function, is therefore zero. Expanding the logarithm, the coefficient of epsilon in (2.1) is exactly

    sum_y C(y) (R(y)-P(y))/P(y)
      = -2 [sum_(s=0,1) (1-2s) mu_b(s)/mu_a(s)]²
      = -2 [(1-b)/(1-a)-b/a]²
      = -2 (a-b)²/[a²(1-a)²].

The term involving `sum C` vanishes. All tables are strictly positive near epsilon zero, so the remainder is genuinely `O(epsilon²)` by ordinary finite-dimensional Taylor expansion. This proves (3.1) and the asserted strict sign. End of proof.

## 4. Exact obstruction to a normalized resolvent proof

At a real symmetric strict kernel K and a real skew-symmetric T, set `A=iT`. Every atom's first derivative vanishes: the trace of a real symmetric matrix times a real skew-symmetric matrix is zero. Thus (2.1) reduces to

    Q_K(iT)=sum_S p_S'' log p_S.                          (4.1)

Let `q_S` be the product Bernoulli law with the same diagonal marginals as K and set `r_S=p_S/q_S`. Because `log q_S` is modular and every one-site marginal has zero second derivative, it may be subtracted:

    Q_K(iT)=sum_S p_S'' log r_S.

The elementary logarithm integral and `sum p_S''=0` now give the exact representation

    Q_K(iT)=integral_0^infinity I_s(K,T) ds,
    I_s(K,T)=-sum_S p_S''/(r_S+s).                        (4.2)

The integral is convergent: at infinity the `1/s` coefficient cancels, and there are finitely many strictly positive r values at zero. An appealing sufficient proof would have `I_s>=0` for every s. Even with this modular normalization, that assertion is false.

Take

    K=(1/100)[[87,-7,-8],[-7,49,-37],[-8,-37,51]],
    T=[[0,9,-8],[-9,0,0],[8,0,0]].                        (4.3)

The leading principal minors of K are

    87/100, 2107/5000, 88531/1000000;

those of `I-K` are

All are positive, so Sylvester's criterion proves `0<K<I`. The path `K+t iT` is admissible for a nonzero interval around zero by continuity of the eigenvalues.

## 5. Exact logarithm certificate

Every logarithm used by the two exact verifiers is enclosed using only Python integers and `fractions.Fraction`. Reduce a positive rational to `2^m x`, with `1<=x<=2`. Put `z=(x-1)/(x+1)`, so `0<=z<=1/3`. Then

    L_N(x)=2 sum_(k=0)^(N-1) z^(2k+1)/(2k+1),
    L_N(x) <= log x <= L_N(x)
                  +2z^(2N+1)/[(2N+1)(1-z²)].            (5.1)

This follows by integrating the geometric series for `1/(1-z²)` or by summing the atanh series and bounding each remaining denominator below by `2N+1`. Apply the same formula to `log 2` and add `m log 2`, reversing interval endpoints when m is negative. Both verifiers use N=40 and compare fractions exactly. Displayed rational decimal grids are rounded outward; no floating point sign enters either certificate.
