## 2. An exact series of two-block contractions

Use X051's notation X,Y,A_j,B_j and fair product expectation E_0. Set T=a+d, R=b+e. For each block size p and integer k>=1 put j=2k and define

    m_p,k=E[X^j], n_p,k=E[X^(j+2)],
    t_p,k=E[A_2 X^j], u_p,k=E[A_2² X^(j-2)],
    v_p,k=E[A_3 X^(j-1)], c_k=j/(j+1),
    D_p,k=u_p,k + [p/(p-1)]t_p,k/j
                     + [(p-2)/(p-1)]v_p,k/(j-1),
    ell_p,k=D_p,k-t_p,k²/(c_k² n_p,k),
    Z_p,k=T-t_p,k d/(c_k n_p,k).

At p=2, A_3=0 and the last term in D is zero. For block q replace T,d by R,e. All n,m,u are positive and n_p,k<=m_p,k. The exact expansion of the complete form is

    Q_pq,rho/4 = T²/p+R²/q
      + sum_(k>=1) rho^(2k) [G_k + m_q,k ell_p,k d²
                                     + m_p,k ell_q,k e²],              (A)
    G_k=m_q,k n_p,k Z_p,k² + m_p,k n_q,k Z_q,k²
                         +2rho c_k n_p,k n_q,k Z_p,k Z_q,k.            (B)

This is a convergent exact identity for rho<1. To verify it, expand 1/F=sum rho^h X^hY^h and -log F=sum rho^h X^hY^h/h in X051(14). Every even coefficient of order rho^(2k) is block diagonal, with the p block

    m_q,k [[n_p,k, -t_p,k/c_k],[-t_p,k/c_k,D_p,k]].

Every odd coefficient of order rho^(2k+1) has cross block

    [[c_k n_p,k n_q,k, -n_p,k t_q,k],
     [-t_p,k n_q,k, t_p,k t_q,k/c_k]].

This is the rank-one outer product

    c_k (n_p,k,-t_p,k/c_k)^T(n_q,k,-t_q,k/c_k).

The order-rho cross trace term vanishes: its count Fisher coefficient equals the full logarithmic-acceleration coefficient gamma. Completing the two diagonal squares now gives (A)-(B), with every acceleration term retained.

Each G_k is a positive local contraction, since exactly

    G_k=n_p,k n_q,k [(1-rho c_k)(Z_p,k²+Z_q,k²)

       +(m_q,k-n_q,k)n_p,k Z_p,k²
       +(m_p,k-n_p,k)n_q,k Z_q,k² >=0.                              (C)

## 3. The same-block moment lemma

The needed elementary lemma is

    D_p,k >= (1+1/k)u_p,k,  p>=2, k>=2.                            (D)

Let M_r=E[S^r], S=sum_(i=1)^p xi_i. The elementary Krawtchouk formulas are

    A_2=(pX²-1)/(p-1),
    A_3=[p²X³-(3p-2)X]/[(p-1)(p-2)]  (p>=3).

At p=2 the numerator of the second formula vanishes on the support, so its product with (p-2)/(p-1) extends as zero. Substitution shows that (D), for j=2k, is equivalent to

    I_j:=M_(j+2)+(2j-3p)M_j-2(j-1)p² M_(j-2) >=0,  j>=4 even.    (E)

Here is a coefficientwise proof of (E), requiring no imported moment theorem. Write g(z)=cosh(z)^p and

    I(z)=g''(z)-3p g(z)+2z g'(z)-2p² integral_0^z t g(t)dt.

The coefficient of z^j/j! is I_j. Differentiating and using

    g''=p²g-p(p-1)cosh(z)^(p-2)

gives

    I'(z)=p(p-1)cosh(z)^(p-3)
                    [(p-2)sinh(z)^3-2z cosh(z)].                  (F)

For p=2, this is -4z, proving (E) with equality. For p>=3 put m=p-3. The power series

    cosh(z)^m[(m+1)sinh(z)^3-2z cosh(z)]+2z

has nonnegative coefficients. Indeed decompose it as

    cosh(z)^m[sinh(z)^3-2z cosh(z)+2z]
      +m sinh(z)^3 cosh(z)^m-2z[cosh(z)^m-1].                      (G)

The first bracket has zero z and z³ coefficients and nonnegative higher coefficients: at z^(2r+1), r>=1, the required comparison is

    3^(2r+1)-3 >= 8(2r+1),

with equality at r=1 and immediate induction thereafter. For m>=1, coefficientwise

    cosh(z)^m-1 <= (m/2)z sinh(z)cosh(z)^(m-1),
    z² <= sinh(z)² cosh(z).

The first inequality follows because differentiating a nonconstant even power series multiplies every coefficient by at least two; the second follows directly from the nonnegative sinh/cosh coefficients. These inequalities make the last two terms of (G) nonnegative coefficientwise. For m=0 they are zero. Thus (F) has nonnegative coefficients at z^(j-1) for every even j>=4, proving (E) and (D).

Cauchy-Schwarz, applied to A_2 X^(k-1) and X^(k+1), gives t_p,k²<=u_p,k n_p,k. Consequently (D) implies the exact tail bound

    ell_p,k >= -u_p,k/(4k²), k>=2.                               (H)
