## Initial exact bridge: a sharp coefficient for the positive observable

For tau nonzero, put

    g(S)=prod_i (c_i+tau 1_{i in S}),  c=(c23,c13,c12).

Every factor is nonnegative, and E g=delta. C077 proves

    Cov(g,s_A)=tau delta tr B.

    H''(A) <= [2 delta/3 - tau^2 delta^2/Var(g)] (tr B)^2

Consequently the sufficient variance threshold is

    Var(g) <= (3/2) tau^2 delta.

This improves the factor 1 in the earlier sufficient certificate. It is still
only a sufficient condition; universality is not asserted. The exact
coefficient 3/2 is the Lorentzian spatial dimension calculation, not an
estimate of the determinantal law.

There is also an exact algebraic identity that may help estimate this variance:

    -log p_S = g(S)/tau^2 + a_0 + sum_i a_i 1_{i in S}.

Indeed the pair and triple Mobius coefficients of g/tau^2 are c_ij and tau.
Thus, for tau nonzero, every strict three-site DPP atom law is a product-field
tilt by exp(-g/tau^2), with this positive multiplicative observable.

The improved variance threshold is not universal. A concrete family below

## 2. New exact conditional Lorentz matrices

Hereafter `c_k` denotes the coefficient on the pair complementary to k.
Fix k, put Rk=[3] minus k, and write

    a=K_kk,  v=K_Rk,k,  d=A_kk,  w=A_Rk,k,  B=A_Rk,Rk.

For s in {0,1}, set r_s=a-(1-s), pi_1=a, pi_0=1-a. The conditional
two-site kernel and its first affine-path derivative are

    J_s=K_Rk,Rk-vv*/r_s,
    B_s=B-(wv*+vw*)/r_s+d vv*/r_s^2.

The conditional two-site frozen logarithmic coefficient is exactly
`c_k+tau s`, since dividing all four conditional atoms by pi_s does not
change their log cross ratio. Define

    eta_k=[c_k+tau(1-a)]/[a(1-a)]

and the Hermitian matrix N_k, indexed by k and Rk, by

    (N_k)_kk = c_k+tau a = M_kk,
    (N_k)_Rk,k = tau v,
    (N_k)_Rk,Rk = eta_k vv*.

Then the following is an exact identity for every Hermitian A:

    sum_s pi_s (c_k+tau s) det B_s = tr(N_k adj A).       (2.1)

To verify it, let L=2 Re(v* adj(B) w) and
`E=d v*adj(B)v-|v wedge w|^2`. Direct expansion of the two-by-two
determinant gives

    det B_s=det B-L/r_s+E/r_s^2.

The three weighted sums of `1,1/r_s,1/r_s^2` are `M_kk,tau,eta_k`.
Also `E=v*(adj A)_Rk,Rk v`, while the off-diagonal contribution of
`tau v` to `tr(N_k adj A)` is `-tau L`. This proves (2.1), including
all acceleration terms implicit in differentiating the conditional kernel.

Every N_k is positive semidefinite. Its only possible nonzero directions
lie in the span of e_k and embedded v, and its scalar Schur complement is

    eta_k - tau^2/M_kk
      = c_k(c_k+tau)/[a(1-a)M_kk] >= 0.                 (2.2)

Strict connectedness makes M_kk positive. Formula (2.2) uses exactly the
two conditional supermodularity inequalities, so no unverified matrix
positivity theorem is imported.

## 3. A universal two-site Fisher margin

For any strict two-site DPP J, any Hermitian B, and its nonnegative
frozen log cross ratio lambda, write

    Q_J(B)=2 lambda det B,
    I_J(B)=sum_x (Dp_x[B])^2/p_x.

Then the following elementary universal inequality holds:

    Q_J(B) <= (2/7) I_J(B).                            (3.1)

If det B<=0 it is immediate. If det B>0, change the sign of B to arrange
B>0. Let the atom masses be x,y,z,w in order 00,10,01,11 and put
`T=(y+z)/(2 sqrt(xw))>=1`. The empty and full scores, Cauchy--Schwarz,
and the two-by-two arithmetic/geometric mean inequality give

    I_J(B) >= 4 det(B)(1+T).

For completeness, set P=adj(J)/sqrt(w), Q=adj(I-J)/sqrt(x). Their
determinants are one and `det(P+Q)=2+2T`. Therefore

    I_J(B)>=(tr PB)^2+(tr QB)^2
           >=(tr((P+Q)B))^2/2
           >=2 det(P+Q)det B.

Also `lambda=log(yz/(xw))<=2 log T`. Finally

    log T <= (2/7)(1+T),  T>=1.

The difference has its minimum at T=7/2, and
`log(7/2)<9/7`: the first five terms of the exponential series give
`exp(9/7)>68771/19208>7/2`. This proves (3.1), with strict scalar slack
when the frozen curvature is positive. It is a strengthened Fisher
compensation bound, not merely two-site entropy concavity.

For the full three-site score, the exact chain-rule decomposition at each
pivot is

    I_K(A)=d_k^2/[K_kk(1-K_kk)]
           + sum_s pi_s I_{J_s}(B_s).                  (3.2)

Define `D_K(A)=sum_k A_kk^2/[K_kk(1-K_kk)]` and N=sum_k N_k.
Combining (2.1), (3.1), and (3.2), without dropping any conditional
acceleration, proves the new universal inequality

    2 tr(N adj A) <= (2/7) [3 I_K(A)-D_K(A)].           (3.3)

The left side may have either sign. This inequality does not assert that
N dominates M in the quadratic adjugate order.

## 4. A proved positive-curvature cone, including indefinite directions

Put `R=N-M`. Subtracting the entropy Hessian identity from (3.3) yields

    H''_K(A) <= -I_K(A)/7 -(2/7)D_K(A)-2 tr(R adj A).  (4.1)

There is a useful alternative that retains the original Lorentz form.
Rearrange (3.3) as

    I_K(A)>=(7/6) [2 tr(N adj A)] + D_K(A)/3.

Hence the exact universal sufficient bound is

    H''_K(A) <= -(1/3) tr((M+7R) adj A)-D_K(A)/3.      (4.2)

In particular every direction satisfying

    tr((M+7R) adj A)>=0                               (4.3)

has nonpositive entropy curvature. A strict inequality in (4.3), or a
nonzero diagonal of A, gives strict curvature. This is a computable
quadratic cone of directions at every connected strict base.

**Theorem (complete semidefinite cone on the tau=0 locus).** Suppose K
is connected, strict, and its atom log law has zero three-way Mobius
coefficient tau. Then every nonzero positive or negative semidefinite
Hermitian A satisfies

    H''_K(A) <= -I_K(A)/7 -(2/7)D_K(A) < 0.             (4.4)

Indeed, at tau=0,

    M=C,
    R=sum_k [c_k/(K_kk(1-K_kk))] embedded(v_k v_k*)>=0. (4.5)

For semidefinite A of either sign, adj A>=0. Thus the last term in
(4.1) is nonpositive. A nonzero semidefinite matrix has some nonzero
diagonal entry, so D_K(A)>0 and strictness follows.

This includes full-rank monotone directions, and does not rely on a
rank-one or rank-two active-support restriction.

It also gives a genuine cone beyond semidefinite directions. Let
`S=M+7R>0` at a tau=0 base, and take any unit vector u. For

    A=I-(1+epsilon)uu*, epsilon>0,

the eigenvalues are 1,1,-epsilon, and

    tr(S adj A)=u*Su-epsilon[tr S-u*Su].                (4.6)

Consequently all such full-rank indefinite directions have strictly
negative curvature whenever

    0<epsilon<lambda_min(S)/[tr S-lambda_min(S)].       (4.7)

Congruence A=P [I-(1+epsilon)uu*] P*, with P invertible, gives the
corresponding formula with S replaced by `P^-1 S P^-*` and the harmless
positive factor |det P|^2. Thus the certificate is intrinsic and not
tied to a particular Euclidean normalization of A.
