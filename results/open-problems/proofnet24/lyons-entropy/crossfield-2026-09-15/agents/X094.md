Consequently any two strict-curvature blocks admit an entire convex cylinder

## 1. Input geometry and exact conditional Fisher scores

For a strict measured kernel B, reveal the coordinates in a fixed order. A
history h before coordinate j has probability pi_h, conditional occupation
probability q_h, previously revealed set R, and absent subset J. Put

    N_h=B_R-I_J,
    (f_h)_R=-N_h^(-1) B_Rj, (f_h)_j=1,
    later coordinates zero, omega_h=pi_h/[q_h(1-q_h)].

Differentiating the conditional Schur formula gives Dq_h[E]=f_h* E f_h.
The Bernoulli conditional scores are martingale differences, so exactly

    FI_B(E)=sum_h omega_h(f_h* E f_h)^2.                    (1)

C058 proves that this frame has the complement property precisely when the
measured-coordinate graph of B is connected. Here is its relevant argument,
reproduced to make the hypothesis transparent. If nonzero x,y satisfy
(f_h*x)(f_h*y)=0 for every history, the two Hermitian directions xy*+yx* and
i(xy*-yx*) have zero Fisher information. Every atom derivative, hence every
inclusion-moment derivative in the complex direction xy*, vanishes. Singleton
moments imply x_i conjugate(y_i)=0: their supports are disjoint. Choose a
shortest graph path joining those supports. The path has no chords and has no
interior vertex in either support. Its determinant derivative is the nonzero
endpoint cofactor

    (-1)^k x_i conjugate(y_j) product_(path edges) B_(next,previous).

This contradicts the zero derivative. Conversely, for a disconnected graph,
every conditional frame vector stays inside one component, and vectors from
different components violate the property. Thus every partition of the frame
has a spanning half if and only if the graph is connected.

A full negative-definite measured entropy Hessian forces connectedness. At a
direct sum with two graph components, an intercomponent direction changes the
law only at order two, while all component marginals remain fixed. The entropy
deficit is its relative entropy to their product, hence is O(t^4). Its Hessian
in that direction is zero. Therefore connectedness is automatic for every
block in the theorem below, not an extra genericity assumption.

For a connected frame f_1,...,f_m, define the finite constants

    G_J=sum_(h in J) f_h f_h*,
    alpha=min_J max{lambda_min(G_J),lambda_min(G_Jc)}>0,
    nu=min_h omega_h>0, L_f=sum_h ||f_h||^2.                (2)

An explicit positive lower bound for alpha is sufficient. For any x,y,

    sum_h omega_h |f_h*x|^2 |f_h*y|^2
        >= nu alpha^2/m ||x||^2||y||^2.                  (3)

To prove (3), normalize x,y and partition at |f_h*x|>=|f_h*y|.
Each product dominates the fourth power of the smaller factor. One of those
two smaller-factor squared sums is at least alpha by the complement property;
Cauchy-Schwarz bounds its fourth-power sum below by alpha^2/m. This proof is
valid over C and does not assume real phases or complex phase retrieval.

## 2. Tensorization removes every two-block exceptional hyperplane

Suppose two weighted frames satisfy

    sum_h omega_h |f_h*x|^2|f_h*y|^2 >= a_A ||x||^2||y||^2,
    sum_g eta_g |g_g*x|^2|g_g*y|^2 >= a_B ||x||^2||y||^2.

For all complex rectangular matrices V,W, the new tensor inequality is

    sum_hg omega_h eta_g |f_h* V g_g|^2 |f_h* W g_g|^2
        >= a_A a_B ||V||_F^2 ||W||_F^2.                 (4)

First apply the A-frame inequality with x=Vg_g and y=Wg_g. The remaining
sum is a_A sum_g eta_g ||Vg_g||^2||Wg_g||^2. Expand both norms into squared
row evaluations, then apply the B-frame inequality to each pair of rows.
Summing their squared norms gives (4). There is no rank condition on V or W.
In particular tensorization retains a positive constant even when entire
rows or columns of the matrix of selected features vanish.

For block atoms, let R=(A-I_abs)^(-1), S=(B-I_abs)^(-1), and let mu be the
product of the block laws. The quadratic density perturbation is

    x(C)=-tr(R C S C*).

The complete conditional Fisher identity, applied successively in both
blocks, gives the exact quartic potential

    Phi(C)=1/2 E_mu x(C)^2
          =1/2 sum_hg omega_h eta_g |f_h* C g_g|^4.       (5)

Indeed for fixed R the first application is to C*RC, which is Hermitian;
the second is to the rank-one matrix Cg_g g_g*C*. All four quantities are
in the fixed measured basis. Since

    D^2(|z|^4/2)[u,u]=2|z|^2|u|^2+4 Re(conj(z)u)^2,

(4) proves

    Phi_CC(C)[D,D] >= 2 a_A a_B ||C||_F^2 ||D||_F^2.     (6)

Thus at C!=0 the complete leading quartic Hessian has no nonzero null
direction. A vanishing selected feature and a vanishing first atom score are
not a vanishing complete quartic Hessian. This is the structural reason that
higher-order singular geometry is unnecessary for the two-block target.

## 3. General two-block theorem and its analytic full-Hessian argument

**Theorem 1.** Let A_0 and B_0 be strict positive contractions of arbitrary
finite sizes. Suppose their measured entropy Hessians are negative definite
in every Hermitian direction. There exist positive delta,epsilon,g,c such
that every kernel in the full convex cylinder

    K(U,C)=[[A,C],[C*,B]], U=A direct_sum B,
    ||U-(A_0 direct_sum B_0)||_F <= delta,
    ||C||_F <= epsilon                                    (7)

is strict and satisfies, for every affine Hermitian direction
T=E direct_sum F and off-block direction [[0,D],[D*,0]],

    H''[T,D] <= -g ||T||_F^2-c ||C||_F^2||D||_F^2.       (8)

Hence the complete ambient Hessian is negative definite whenever C!=0.
Entropy is strictly concave on the entire cylinder, including chords passing
through C=0. No bridge orientation, matrix entry, rank, or phase is excluded.

**Proof.** Strict block curvature persists on a small block ball, with a
uniform positive gap. The conditional frames and their positive weights vary
continuously, so their complement constants remain uniformly positive.
Equation (6) therefore holds uniformly with one positive coefficient q.

The actual block marginals stay fixed when C changes, and exactly

    H(K)=H(A)+H(B)-I(U,C),
    I(U,C)=D(P_K || P_A tensor P_B).

All atoms are positive on a neighborhood of the independent center, so I is
real analytic. The signed determinant expansion starts in degree two in C.
Moreover the measured diagonal unitary diag(I,-I) sends C to -C without
changing any atom, so only even total degrees occur. Normalization and the
fixed marginals give the differentiated expansion

    I(U,C)=Phi_U(C)+O(||C||^6),
    I_UU=O(||C||^4), I_UC=O(||C||^3),
    I_CC=Phi_CC+O(||C||^4).                               (9)

These are uniform operator estimates on a smaller block ball; they follow by
differentiating the convergent finite-atom analytic expansion, not from a
radial asymptotic alone. Combine (6), (9), and the strict block gap, and pay
the mixed term O(r^3)||T||||D|| by one quarter of the block gap plus
O(r^6)||D||^2. A sufficiently small single r-bound absorbs both bridge errors
into q r^2||D||^2. It is independent of the bridge orientation. This proves
(8); strict finite-chord concavity follows from the Green-kernel calculation
in Section 6. All affine directions, including block-changing directions,
were retained. Section 4 makes the entire argument explicit for size 2+2.

## 4. Explicit sixteen-dimensional cylinder for two 2-by-2 blocks

Suppose the supplied block certificates are

    -H_2''(A_0)[E,E]>=gamma_A ||E||_F^2,
    -H_2''(B_0)[F,F]>=gamma_B ||F||_F^2,
    gamma=min(gamma_A,gamma_B)>0.

Set tau equal to the minimum of the four spectral margins of A_0,B_0, and
kappa=2/tau. Each block has m=3 conditional history vectors. Use its alpha,
nu,L_f from (2), and put

    delta=min{tau/16, gamma/(32 kappa^3),
              alpha_A/(6 kappa L_A),alpha_B/(6 kappa L_B)},
    a_A=nu_A alpha_A^2/24, a_B=nu_B alpha_B^2/24,
    q=2 a_A a_B,
    Acal=114 kappa^6, Bcal=62 kappa^5, Ccal=50 kappa^6,

    epsilon=min{1/(4 kappa), (gamma/(8 Acal))^(1/4),
                sqrt(q/(4 Ccal)),
                (q gamma/(32 Bcal^2))^(1/4)}.            (10)

All these constants are explicit, finite, and positive. Smaller radii are
permitted. On (7) with (10), the complete bound is

    -H''(K)[T,D] >= gamma/4 ||T||_F^2
                          + q/2 ||C||_F^2||D||_F^2.     (11)

The squared Frobenius norm of the full off-block Hermitian direction is 2||D||_F^2.
Thus (11) covers all 16 real coordinates with ordinary Hessian gap
min{gamma/4,q||C||_F^2/4} whenever C!=0.

Here are the uniform input bounds needed for (10). Inside the delta block
ball, every signed atom or prefix inverse has norm at most kappa. For a
spectral margin tau/2, this follows by splitting its present and absent
coordinates: after any real spectral shift of magnitude less than tau/2,
the present principal block is positive and the absent Schur complement is
negative, so the signed matrix has no spectrum in that interval.

A frame vector changes by at most kappa delta times its old norm, by the
exact resolvent identity. Every frame-subset Gram matrix therefore changes
by at most 3 kappa delta L_f<=alpha/2; its complement constant is at least
alpha/2. A prefix determinant has ratio between (1-delta/tau)^k and
(1+delta/tau)^k. Write omega_h=pi_h^3/(pi_h0 pi_h1); k<=2 and
delta/tau<=1/16 imply every history weight is at least half its old value.
Applying (3) gives the two constants a_A,a_B in (10).

For two-site entropy its atom probabilities are quadratic, so their third
mixed derivative is zero. Their normalized first and second derivatives have
bounds sqrt(2)kappa and 3 kappa^2. Differentiating entropy therefore bounds
its third derivative operator norm by

    3(3 kappa^2)(sqrt(2)kappa)+(sqrt(2)kappa)^3
       =11 sqrt(2) kappa^3 <16 kappa^3.

The sum of the block entropies obeys the same bound in the combined
Frobenius norm. The delta constraint therefore preserves the block gap
gamma/2. No entropy logarithm has been separately bounded at a small atom.

## 5. Exact quartic determinant correction and all differentiated bounds

For 2-by-2 blocks the signed Schur determinant identity is exactly

    P_K/mu=1+z, z=x+y,
    x=-tr(R C S C*), y=det(R)det(S)|det C|^2.              (12)

There are no further determinant terms. Neither the quartic term y nor its
ambient second derivatives are discarded, including at a rank-one bridge
where y itself vanishes but y_CC need not vanish. The entropy identity is

    I=E_mu f(z), f(z)=(1+z)log(1+z)-z,
    Phi=1/2 E_mu x^2.                                    (13)

In particular I-Phi begins in degree six, but its full differentiated bounds
must be proved. Write r=||C||_F, d=||D||_F, t=||T||_F. Direct resolvent and
determinant differentiation gives

    |x|<=kappa^2 r^2, |x_C|<=2 kappa^2 r d,
    |x_CC|<=2 kappa^2 d^2,
    |x_U|<=2 kappa^3 r^2 t,
    |x_UC|<=4 kappa^3 r d t,
    |x_UU|<=6 kappa^4 r^2 t^2,

    |y|<=kappa^4 r^4/4, |y_C|<=kappa^4 r^3 d,
    |y_CC|<=3 kappa^4 r^2 d^2,
    |y_U|<=kappa^5 r^4 t/2,
    |y_UC|<=2 kappa^5 r^3 d t,
    |y_UU|<=5 kappa^6 r^4 t^2/4.                          (14)

For example |det C|<=r^2/2, |D det C[D]|<=rd, and
|D^2 det C[D,D]|<=d^2 prove the three bridge bounds on y. The logarithmic
derivative of det(R)det(S) has magnitude at most 2 kappa t, and its second
normalized derivative at most 5 kappa^2 t^2, proving the other three.

For r<=1/(4 kappa), (14) implies the convenient uniform bounds

    |z|<=2 kappa^2 r^2<=1/8,
    |z_C|<=3 kappa^2 r d, |z_CC|<=3 kappa^2 d^2,
    |z_U|<=3 kappa^3 r^2 t, |z_UC|<=5 kappa^3 r d t,
    |z_UU|<=7 kappa^4 r^2 t^2,
    |mu_U/mu|<=2 kappa t, |mu_UU/mu|<=5 kappa^2 t^2.      (15)

Here and below mixed bounds are operator estimates in independently chosen
block and bridge directions. For |z|<=1/8, |f(z)|<=z^2,
|f'(z)|<=2|z|, and |f''(z)|<=2. Twice differentiating the varying-measure
expectation (13), using all six bounds (15), yields

    |I_UU| <=(20+48+18+28)kappa^6 r^4 t^2
            = Acal r^4 t^2,
    |I_UC| <=(24+18+20)kappa^5 r^3 td
            = Bcal r^3 td.                               (16)

For clarity, the four UU terms are respectively
(mu_UU/mu)f, 2(mu_U/mu)f'z_U, f''z_U^2, and f'z_UU.
Thus changes in the block marginals and conditional weights are retained.

The exact CC difference has five terms:

    (I-Phi)_CC=E_mu[
      (1/(1+z)-1)x_C^2 + 2x_C y_C/(1+z) + y_C^2/(1+z)
      +(log(1+z)-x)x_CC + log(1+z)y_CC].                 (17)

Use |1/(1+z)-1|<=4 kappa^2 r^2,
|log(1+z)|<=4 kappa^2 r^2, and
|log(1+z)-x|<=5 kappa^4 r^4. Equations (14) and
kappa^2 r^2<=1/16 bound the five coefficients in (17) by
16,8,1/8,10,12 times kappa^6 r^4 d^2. Their sum is less than 50, so

    |(I-Phi)_CC|<=Ccal r^4 d^2.                          (18)

This proof includes the determinant correction at arbitrary-rank base and
arbitrary ambient bridge direction. It remains valid at det C=0.

The actual full Hessian follows from (6), (16), (18):

    H'' <=(-gamma/2+Acal r^4)t^2+2 Bcal r^3td
                                      +(-q r^2+Ccal r^4)d^2.

Young's bound gives
2 Bcal r^3td<=gamma t^2/8+(8 Bcal^2/gamma)r^6d^2.
The last three constraints in (10) pay these errors and prove (11).
Every K in (7) is strict because its operator displacement from the fixed
direct sum is at most delta+r<=tau/16+tau/8<tau. The domain is convex in
the original affine kernel variables and has nonempty full-dimensional
interior. This completes the quantitative theorem.

## 7. Exact zero first response, concrete certificate, and reproducibility

bridge: take D=iC and T=0, with the Hermitian lower block -iC*. The affine
path has C(t)=(1+it)C. In (12), x(t)=(1+t^2)x and
y(t)=(1+t^2)^2 y. Thus every atom has p'_S=0 identically, although generally
p''_S!=0. In particular its full Fisher information is exactly zero and

    -H''[0,iC]=sum_S p''_S log p_S
                       >=q ||C||_F^4/2>0               (20)

in the cylinder. This is signed log-acceleration payment, with no Fisher
term left to invoke. At quartic order Phi''[iC,iC]=2 E_mu x(C)^2>0.
A nonlinear phase orbit would keep entropy constant but has a nonzero
kernel acceleration; (20) concerns the required affine tangent line.

There is an additional exact full-strength conclusion for this zero-score

At every strict physical coupling, without a smallness restriction,

    -H''[0,iC]=2 E_mu x log(1+x)>0 for C!=0.              (21)

The fixed marginal terms containing log mu cancel. Every summand is
nonnegative because x and log(1+x) have the same sign, and at least one is
positive by (5). Thus the specific first-score degeneracy is solved exactly,

## 8. Relation to genuine multi-block degeneration and remaining lemma

For three original blocks in a path A--B--C, the leading independent-block
quartic is Phi_AB+Phi_BC. It is identically zero in directions of the absent
A--C bridge. This is genuine quartic degeneration, in contrast to the
impossible two-connected-block case. Theorem 1 nevertheless gives a rigorous
higher-order recursive closure: first join A and B by any nonzero sufficiently
weak bridge; their union has strict complete curvature. Apply Theorem 1 to
that union and C with a bridge supported only on the B rows. The resulting
three-block kernel has a negative-definite Hessian in every ambient direction,
including the absent A--C directions. No generic bridge support is needed.
The new conditional histories of the merged connected block restore the
complement property; their constants encode the higher powers lost at the
original independent-block center.

Iterating gives full-Hessian gluing along every finite prescribed tree of
strict-curvature blocks, with arbitrary nonzero complex matrices on its tree
edges, provided each new edge is scaled below the radius obtained after the
preceding gluing. The earlier singleton theorem C058 is the one-site special
case. This recursive conclusion requires a hierarchy of permissible radii;
it does NOT prove a single uniform bound allowing all edge scales to tend to
zero independently, nor a same-scale weak-tree theorem.

**Exact remaining lemma for the broader assigned obstruction.** For a fixed
three-block path with both edge matrices nonzero, obtain a lower bound on the
Schur complement of the full negative entropy Hessian in the absent outer
bridge directions, with every vanishing factor of the two incident matrices
retained, uniformly under independently collapsing edge strengths. Theorem 1
supplies positivity when the second edge is sufficiently small relative to
the already glued first block, but does not control arbitrary relative rates.
Rank-deficient incident matrices may require several generations of
conditional histories. No sign is asserted for that unproved uniform
higher-order Schur comparison.
