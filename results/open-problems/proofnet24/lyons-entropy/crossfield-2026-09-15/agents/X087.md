# X087 — dimension-free Fisher allocation for real and complex Cauchy odds

Date: 2026-09-15. **COMPLETE: new bounded theorem and exact remaining gap.**

The target is the fixed-coordinate Shannon entropy of the DPP kernel K, differentiated along the actual affine path K+tA. The general Lyons conjecture and arbitrary Cauchy-shape concavity remain open. Historical novelty beyond the supplied project record has not been established.

## 1. Results and scope

This report proves a new occupation-score allocation for every DPP whose frozen log atom weights have only nonnegative pair interactions. It gives a **dimension-free interaction row condition**, a sharper spectral condition, and an additional condition using the actual measured covariance. All conclusions concern **every ambient Hermitian affine-K direction**, including noncommuting directions.

The resulting explicit families permit every positive external-field vector and every number of sites:

* Real Cauchy nodes with successive ratios at least 6. The old ROOT_PAIRWISE_CAUCHY sufficient construction required a ratio at least n+1. The new spacing does not grow with n, and the Fisher surplus is at least 1/25.
* Complex Cauchy nodes in the right half-plane, with successive real-part ratios at least 6 and completely arbitrary imaginary parts, have the same surplus.
* A different dense complex family has nodes z_i=a+i y_i, with a>0 and successive imaginary gaps at least 3a. It has Fisher surplus at least 2/9. Gaps at least 5a/2 also suffice, with surplus 1/50. This family has equal real parts, generally nonreal cycle flux, and nonuniform conditional count laws.

No odds interpolation is treated as affine in K. These are full ambient Hessian theorems at all the displayed bases; the Cauchy parameter sets are not claimed to be convex. They imply genuine local affine-K Jensen inequalities around each base by strictness and Hessian continuity. Any affine segment staying in the certified class has concave measured entropy.

## 2. The exact universal occupation-score allocation

Let 0<K<I on n≥2 measured sites (n=1 is ordinary Bernoulli entropy). Let A=A* and let s(X)=p'_X/p_X be the complete atom score along K+tA. Set

\[
v_i=A_{ii},\quad k_i=K_{ii},\quad Y_i=X_i-k_i,\quad
\chi_i=k_i(1-k_i),\quad w_{ij}=|K_{ij}|^2\ (i\ne j).
\]

The DPP inclusion identities give

\[
\mathbb E s=0,\quad \mathbb E(sY_i)=v_i,\quad
\Sigma_{ii}:=\operatorname{Var}X_i=\chi_i,\quad
\Sigma_{ij}:=\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=-w_{ij}.
\tag{1}
\]

Write I=E s² for the complete Fisher information. Positivity of K-K² gives

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\le\chi_i.
\tag{2}
\]

Define

\[
d_i=1-2\chi_i-2\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}\ge1-4\chi_i\ge0.
\]

**Lemma 1.** The following is an exact identity:

\[
\boxed{
I-2\sum_i v_i^2
=\mathbb E\left(s-2\sum_i v_iY_i\right)^2
+2\sum_i d_i v_i^2
+4\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(v_i+v_j)^2\ge0.}
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The expectation on the right equals I-4v^Tv+4v^TΣv by (1). The remaining two terms equal 2v^Tv-4v^TΣv: expand the edge squares and use the definition of d_i. This proves equality, and (2) proves every displayed coefficient is nonnegative. No independence approximation or projection of the kernel is involved. ∎

The factor 2 is a universal bound, not claimed to be optimal at any particular base. Its main benefit is that it is independent of n and of the external fields.

There is a stronger exact, base-dependent version. Put

\[
D_i=\chi_i+\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij},\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(D_i).
\tag{4}
\]

Every D_i>0 and D_i≤1/2. The covariance identity

\[
D-\Sigma=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T
\]

gives a second exact allocation:

\[
\boxed{
I-v^TD^{-1}v
=\mathbb E\left(s-\sum_i\frac{v_i}{D_i}Y_i\right)^2
+\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\left(\frac{v_i}{D_i}+\frac{v_j}{D_j}\right)^2\ge0.}
\tag{5}
\]

Indeed expanding the expectation gives I-2v^TD^{-1}v+(D^{-1}v)^TΣ(D^{-1}v), and the edge squares add precisely (D^{-1}v)^T(D-Σ)(D^{-1}v).

## 3. Pair-log theorem with a positive graph decomposition

Assume the frozen atom table has the form

\[
-\log p_S=\text{constant}+\sum_{i\in S}a_i+
\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in S}}c_{ij},\qquad c_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]

Let C be the real symmetric matrix with C_ii=0 and C_ij=c_ij. Let r_i=Σ_{j≠i}c_ij. The a_i in (6) are fixed log-table coefficients and are unrelated to the directional coordinates v_i.

Freezing (6), the modular terms have zero second derivative because E X_i=K_ii is affine. The pair inclusion probabilities are det K_{ij}, whose second derivatives are 2 det A_{ij}. The **complete** entropy Hessian is therefore

\[
H''_K(A)=-I+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(v_iv_j-|A_{ij}|^2).
\tag{7}
\]

This uses H''=-I-Σ_S p''_S log p_S and retains its acceleration term.

**Theorem 2.** If max_i r_i≤2, then H''_K(A)≤0 for every Hermitian A. More quantitatively, if

\[
r_i\le2(1-\varepsilon)\quad\text{for every }i,
\qquad 0\le\varepsilon\le1,
\tag{8}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge\varepsilon I+
\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(v_i-v_j)^2+
2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.}
\tag{9}
\]

**Exact positive decomposition.** The elementary weighted-graph identity

\[
2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}v_iv_j
=\sum_i r_iv_i^2-\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(v_i-v_j)^2
\]

turns (7) into

\[
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&\varepsilon I
+(1-\varepsilon)\left(I-2\sum_i v_i^2\right)
+\sum_i\{2(1-\varepsilon)-r_i\}v_i^2\\
&+\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(v_i-v_j)^2
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Insert (3). Every term is a nonnegative square with a nonnegative coefficient under (8), proving (9).

If ε>0 and every c_ij>0, then the Hessian is negative definite. In fact I≥2Σv_i², so

\[
-H''_K(A)\ge
\min\{2\varepsilon,\min_{i<j}c_{ij}\}\,\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus all nonzero Hermitian directions, including score-null angular directions, are strictly paid. Equation (11) also supplies a strictly positive local Euclidean Hessian margin.

## 4. Sharper weighted and spectral certificates

The row test is convenient, but it can be replaced by either of two sharper exact criteria.

**Shape-only spectral criterion.** If λ_max(C)≤2(1-ε), then

\[
-H''_K(A)\ge\varepsilon I+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\tag{12}
\]

Indeed combine I≥2v^Tv with v^TCv≤2(1-ε)v^Tv. No sign condition on v is used.

A square version permits any positive vector h. Define

\[
r_i(h)=\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}\frac{h_j}{h_i}.
\]

The exact identity

\[
v^TCv=\sum_i r_i(h)v_i^2-
\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\left(
\sqrt{h_j/h_i}\,v_i-\sqrt{h_i/h_j}\,v_j\right)^2
\tag{13}
\]

replaces the graph identity used in (10). Thus r_i(h)≤2(1-ε) is another explicit positive decomposition. With h=(1,...,1) it reduces to Theorem 2.

**Base-dependent spectral criterion.** With D from (4), if

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\max}(D^{1/2}CD^{1/2})\le1-\varepsilon,}
\tag{14}
\]

then (12) again holds. Here the exact decomposition is

\[
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&\varepsilon I+
(1-\varepsilon)(I-v^TD^{-1}v)\\
&+v^T\{(1-\varepsilon)D^{-1}-C\}v
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

The second term is the complete square sum (5), and the third is nonnegative exactly under (14). A version using only weighted rows follows from (13): it suffices that r_i(h)≤(1-ε)/D_i for all i. This allows large interaction coefficients when the corresponding measured covariance entries are sufficiently small.

These are sufficient tests. In particular a large positive eigenvalue in C-D^{-1} is not a counterexample to entropy concavity, because the score residual in (5) has not vanished.

## 5. Complex Cauchy determinants give precisely the needed log table

Let z_1,...,z_n be distinct complex numbers with x_i=Re z_i>0, and let ell_i>0. Define

\[
L_{ij}=\sqrt{\ell_i\ell_j}\,
\frac{2\sqrt{x_ix_j}}{z_i+\overline z_j},
\qquad K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\tag{16}
\]

This is Hermitian positive definite. The matrix 1/(z_i+conj z_j) is the Gram matrix of exponential functions on (0,∞), and distinct exponentials are linearly independent: a vanishing linear combination has all its first n derivatives vanish at zero, and the resulting Vandermonde matrix is invertible. Positive diagonal congruence preserves positive definiteness. Thus 0<K<I.

For every S, the Cauchy determinant identity is

\[
\boxed{
\det L_S=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i
\prod_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in S}}
\frac{|z_i-z_j|^2}{|z_i+\overline z_j|^2}.}
\tag{17}
\]

For completeness, put det(1/(u_i+v_j)) over its common denominator. The numerator is alternating separately in the u variables and the v variables, and so has their two Vandermonde factors. Its degree is exactly their combined degree; a leading coefficient or induction in the last variable fixes the constant as one. Substituting u_i=z_i, v_j=conj z_j gives a numerator product |z_i-z_j|². Its denominator is product_i(2x_i) times product_{i<j}|z_i+conj z_j|². Multiplying by the diagonal normalization in (16) proves (17). All denominators are nonzero because x_i>0.

The DPP atoms are p_S=det L_S/det(I+L), so they have exactly (6), with

\[
\boxed{
c_{ij}=\log\frac{|z_i+\overline z_j|^2}{|z_i-z_j|^2}
=\log\left(1+\frac{4x_ix_j}{|z_i-z_j|^2}\right)>0.}
\tag{18}
\]

The external fields affect only the modular coefficients in (6). Consequently every shape-only row or spectral certificate holds **uniformly over all positive ell_i**, however unequal and however close the resulting kernel is to the spectral boundary.

## 6. First explicit extension: fixed geometric spacing in any dimension

Suppose x_1<...<x_n with x_{i+1}/x_i≥R>1. The imaginary parts of the z_i are arbitrary. For i<j and d=j-i, (18) gives

\[
c_{ij}\le2\log\frac{x_j+x_i}{x_j-x_i}
\le2\log\frac{R^d+1}{R^d-1}
<\frac4{R^d-1}.
\tag{19}
\]

The last inequality is log(1+u)<u with u=2/(R^d-1). Also R^d-1≥R^{d-1}(R-1). At most two sites occur at each distance d from a given index, so

\[
r_i<8\sum_{d\ge1}\frac1{R^d-1}
\le\frac{8R}{(R-1)^2}.
\tag{20}
\]

The last expression decreases for R>1. At R=6 it is 48/25=2(1-1/25). Theorem 2 proves, for every n, every ell_i>0, and every Hermitian A,

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge\frac1{25}I+
\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(A_{ii}-A_{jj})^2
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2>0\quad(A\ne0).}
\tag{21}
\]

For real z_i this improves the prior explicit R≥n+1 construction to R≥6. Allowing arbitrary imaginary parts further enlarges its integrable base class without weakening the bound. No upper or lower field bound is imposed.

## 7. Second explicit extension: equally deep nodes with imaginary separation

Fix a>0 and set z_i=a+i y_i, with y_1<...<y_n and y_{i+1}-y_i≥h. Now all real parts agree, so the preceding geometric condition does not apply. Equation (18) becomes

\[
c_{ij}=\log\left(1+\frac{4a^2}{(y_i-y_j)^2}\right)
\le\log\left(1+\frac{4a^2}{h^2|i-j|^2}\right).
\tag{22}
\]

The elementary decreasing-integrand comparison gives

\[
\sum_{d\ge1}\frac1{d^2}
\le1+\frac14+\int_2^\infty x^{-2}\,dx=\frac74.
\]

Using log(1+u)≤u in (22) yields

\[
r_i\le\frac{8a^2}{h^2}\sum_{d\ge1}\frac1{d^2}
\le\frac{14a^2}{h^2}.
\tag{23}
\]

Thus **h≥3a** gives r_i≤14/9=2(1-2/9), proving

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge\frac29 I+
\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(A_{ii}-A_{jj})^2
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2>0\quad(A\ne0).}
\tag{24}
\]

A slightly denser explicit spacing is also certified. If h≥5a/2, separate the d=1 term and use the same integral estimate on the tail:

\[
r_i\le2\log(41/25)+\frac{32}{25}\sum_{d\ge2}\frac1{d^2}
\le2\log(41/25)+\frac{24}{25}<\frac{49}{25}.
\tag{25}
\]

For the last strict inequality, e^{1/2}>1+1/2+1/8+1/48=79/48>41/25, hence log(41/25)<1/2. Theorem 2 therefore gives Fisher surplus at least **1/50** at h≥5a/2.

These statements hold for every n and every positive field vector. They are not count-uniform or merely real-gauge constructions. For example, with y_i=(i-1)h and equal fields, the pair weights depend on |i-j|, so already n=3 has unequal pair atoms. Furthermore, writing b=2a, the triangle product L_12 L_23 L_31 is a positive real field factor times

\[
\frac{b^3}{(b-ih)^2(b+2ih)}
=\frac{b^3}{b^3+3bh^2-2ih^3},
\]

which is nonreal whenever h>0. A diagonal unitary gauge preserves this cycle product, so the kernel odds cannot be transformed into real Cauchy odds by such a gauge.

## 8. Relation to the old total-pair expenditure

The new expenditure is the largest weighted interaction row or a spectral radius, rather than a sum over all pairs. For either fixed-spacing sequence above, r_i stays bounded as n grows while Σ_{i<j}c_ij grows at least linearly, because each consecutive pair has a fixed positive interaction. Thus the old easy total-pair bound Σc_ij≤4 eventually fails for every fixed spacing, whereas the new theorem continues to hold for every n.

The prior more refined F+mv≤1 criterion and the current criteria are not claimed to be ordered at every base. Rare pair information can be more effective than occupation information for a few very strong edges. The advance here is an explicit exact allocation with a cost that does not scale with the number of uniformly separated Cauchy nodes, and its extension to a complex integrable family with equal real parts.

## 9. Constructive exact remaining gap

Arbitrary Cauchy shapes are not settled. For example, two real nodes 1 and 1+η have c_12→∞ as η↓0. The row and shape-only spectral tests then fail, although two-site entropy concavity is known. Thus the present sufficient condition is not a necessary condition.

Outside (14), the unresolved payment is precisely the following physical-score inequality:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\left(s-\sum_i\frac{v_i}{D_i}Y_i\right)^2
+\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\left(\frac{v_i}{D_i}+\frac{v_j}{D_j}\right)^2
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2\\
&\hspace{35mm}\ge v^T(C-D^{-1})v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Equations (5) and (7) show that (26) is exactly the missing compensation, with no discarded affine-K acceleration. The Cauchy determinant relation (17) constrains the score s appearing here, but no claim is made that (26) holds merely for an arbitrary score with the same occupation covariances. Rare pair or higher-count scores are plausible additional payments; their adequacy for arbitrary Cauchy shapes is not proved.
