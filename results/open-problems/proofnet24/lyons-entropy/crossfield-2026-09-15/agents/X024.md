# X024 — a full-domain quaternionic four-cycle theorem

Status: COMPLETE bounded proof. Arbitrary balanced bipartite concavity remains open.

## 1. The theorem and its exact scope

For arbitrary complex z,w define, in the fixed measured coordinate basis,

    C(z,w) = [[z, w], [-conj(w), conj(z)]],
    K(z,w) = I_4/2 + (1/2) [[0_2,C(z,w)],[C(z,w)*,0_2]].       (1)

The map (Re z,Im z,Re w,Im w) -> K is real affine. Direct multiplication gives

    C C* = C* C = (|z|^2+|w|^2) I_2.

Thus its exact closed contraction domain is the convex ball |z|^2+|w|^2<=1.

**Theorem.** On this entire ball, (z,w) -> H(P_K(z,w)) is strictly concave.
At every strict base with z*w!=0 its Hessian is negative definite on this
four-real-dimensional affine family. All boundary endpoints are included in
the strict chord statement. Fixed measured-coordinate permutations, diagonal
unitary gauges, and direct sums preserve the conclusion.

When z*w!=0, (1) has all four edges of K_(2,2). Both independent complex
parameters may change simultaneously. The gauge-invariant four-cycle product
is C11 C22 conj(C12 C21)=-|z|^2|w|^2, so its phase is fixed at pi. This is NOT
a theorem for a moving gauge-invariant phase, a general complex 2-by-2 cross
block, arbitrary diagonal perturbations, or the universal Lyons conjecture.

The proof below uses equal singular values only to identify feasibility.
It never changes the measurement basis or replaces measured entropy by a
spectral entropy.

## 2. Fixed-basis four-cycle atoms

For a general cross block C=[[a,b],[c,d]], put w_ij=|C_ij|^2 and
Delta=|det C|^2. Let the original occupation spins be s_1,...,s_4. The centered
minor identity E prod_(i in U) s_i=det((2K-I)_U) and Fourier inversion give

    16 p(s) = 1 + sum_(i=1,2; j=3,4) |C_(i,j-2)|^2 t_ij
                    + Delta s_1 s_2 s_3 s_4,
    t_ij = -s_i s_j.                                          (2)

For completeness, the centered minor identity follows by expanding both sides
as sum_(V subset U) 2^|V| (-1)^(|U|-|V|) det K_V. The only nonzero centered
minors in (2) have size two, with determinant -|C_ij|^2, or size four, with
determinant |det C|^2. Hence no other Fourier terms have been omitted.

The four edge signs have product one. Under the uniform spin reference, the
three signs eta=t_13 t_24=t_14 t_23, r=t_13, u=t_14 are independent. Therefore

    q_eta(r,u)=1+eta Delta+(w11+eta w22)r+(w12+eta w21)u,
    4 log 2-H(P_K)=(1/8) sum_(eta,r,u) q_eta log q_eta.          (3)

The fourth free spin only duplicates atoms and is already included in (3).

For (1), set x=|z|^2, y=|w|^2, s=x+y. Then Delta=s^2 and

    q_+(r,u)=1+s^2+2xr+2yu,       q_-(r,u)=1-s^2.              (4)

All densities in (4) are strictly positive whenever s<1: the smallest plus
density is (1-s)^2, and the minus density is 1-s^2.

## 3. A convex lift and the exact determinant compensation

Write psi(v)=v log v. On its open convex positivity domain define

    G(x,y,delta) = (1/8) sum_(r,u=+-1) psi(1+delta+2xr+2yu)
                         + (1/2) psi(1-delta).                 (5)

This is jointly convex, indeed strictly convex: each density is affine and
psi''(v)=1/v>0; their derivative vectors span all three coordinates. The
physical entropy deficit is the restriction F(x,y)=G(x,y,(x+y)^2).

A direct derivative of (5), with all constant terms cancelling, gives

    G_delta = (1/8) log { [((1+delta)^2-4(x+y)^2)
                          ((1+delta)^2-4(x-y)^2)]/(1-delta)^4 }.

On delta=s^2 this simplifies to the decisive sign identity

    gamma(x,y):=G_delta(x,y,s^2)
      = (1/8) log[1+16xy/(1-s^2)^2] >=0,       x,y>=0, s<1.   (6)

Consequently, for any real strength direction v=(v1,v2), the full chain rule is

    D^2 F[v,v] = Jv^T (Hess G) Jv + 2 gamma(x,y)(v1+v2)^2,
    Jv=(v1,v2,2s(v1+v2)).                                    (7)

Both terms are nonnegative and the first is strictly positive when v!=0.
In particular, F is jointly strictly convex on the nonnegative open triangle.
Equation (7) retains the complete determinant acceleration; dropping it would
not be a valid affine-kernel argument.

An explicit version, useful for checking the compensation, is

    D^2 F[v,v]
      = (1/8) sum_(r,u) [2(r+s)v1+2(u+s)v2]^2/q_+(r,u)
        + 2s^2(v1+v2)^2/(1-s^2)
        + (1/4) log[1+16xy/(1-s^2)^2] (v1+v2)^2.              (8)

This is a finite positive-square certificate rather than a Laplace integral.
It replaces the unavailable independent-edge factorization on the cycle.

## 4. Monotonicity and return to affine complex entries

At x=0 the plus densities are (1+yu)^2 and do not depend on r, so G_x=0.
Equation (6) also gives gamma(0,y)=0. The chain rule therefore gives

    F_x(0,y)=0,       F_y(x,0)=0.                              (9)

Since F_xx and F_yy are strictly positive by (7), it follows that F_x>0 for
x>0 and F_y>0 for y>0; both partial derivatives are nonnegative everywhere
on the nonnegative strict triangle. Thus F is coordinatewise nondecreasing.

For an affine complex direction (h,k), set
v=(2 Re(conj(z)h),2 Re(conj(w)k)). The exact Hessian is

    -D^2 H[(h,k),(h,k)]
       = v^T(Hess F)v + 2F_x |h|^2+2F_y |k|^2 >=0.            (10)

Formula (10), together with (8), is the complete
nonnegative decomposition for every allowed simultaneous complex motion.
It is strictly positive for nonzero (h,k) whenever z*w!=0.

For a finite chord let x_bar=(1-t)|z0|^2+t|z1|^2 and define y_bar similarly.
Then |zt|^2=x_bar-t(1-t)|z1-z0|^2<=x_bar, and likewise for w. Monotonicity
and convexity of F yield F(|zt|^2,|wt|^2)<=F(x_bar,y_bar)<=
(1-t)F(|z0|^2,|w0|^2)+tF(|z1|^2,|w1|^2), proving the claimed entropy chord.

F extends continuously to x+y<=1 by 0 log 0=0. Scaling both endpoints by
0<rho<1 and taking rho up to one proves the closed-domain weak inequality.
Strictness also holds for distinct boundary endpoints: their open chord is
inside the strict ball. If h!=0, z(t) vanishes at at most one interior time;
else k!=0 and w(t) has the same property. By (9)-(10), the deficit has strictly
positive second derivative away from at most one time along that chord.
A continuous convex function with this property has a strict endpoint chord.

## 5. A strong-coupling affine example and prior-scope comparison

Take z0=(3+i)/5, w0=(3+2i)/5 and z1=(2+3i)/5, w1=(1+3i)/5.
Both endpoint strengths are 23/25, so their kernel eigenvalues are
(1-sqrt(23)/5)/2 and (1+sqrt(23)/5)/2, each twice. The direction has eigenvalues
+-sqrt(10)/10, each twice: it is full rank with inertia (2,2). All four cycle
edges move, both complex phases change, and the edge magnitudes have opposite
radial slopes around the midpoint. The computed midpoint entropy gap is 0.2260213780589303;
the theorem, not this number, establishes its sign.

C026 and X014 concern forests. C073's star tangent theorem does not include
this full cycle. C059/C062 require a fixed coupling direction for each leaf or
one scalar per fixed reservoir cross block; the independent z,w family changes
both column directions. Choosing a measured core across the bipartition would
leave its complementary paired edge moving, violating the fixed-reservoir
hypothesis. The theorem is therefore a distinct family within the supplied
research record; no historical novelty outside that record is asserted.

The only count-uniform overlap is z=w=0. Indeed count-uniform atoms imply equal
pair-inclusion probabilities, whereas within-partition pairs here have value
1/4 and cross pairs have values (1-x)/4 or (1-y)/4. The axes z=0 or w=0 reduce
to two independent edges and are established overlap, not new scope.

## 6. Exact remaining obstruction and reproducibility

The favorable sign (6) does not hold for arbitrary determinant fibers. At
x=y=1/16, delta=0, (5) gives G_delta=(1/8)log(15/16)<0. This is a physical
balanced cross block C with all four entries equal to 1/4; its norm is 1/2.
Thus extending (7) to moving cycle phase requires a further Fisher/edge
compensation for an indefinite determinant contribution. The example only
obstructs the separate coefficient sign; it is not an entropy counterexample.

Run `python computations/X024/verify.py`. It checks all 16 fixed-basis atoms
against independent mixed-column determinants at a generic complex cycle,
and checks every atom polynomial on the complete affine example. It verifies
the product identity in (6) exactly, and compares (8)-(10) with determinant
first/second jets at five deterministic points. Results are recorded in
`computations/X024/results.json`; maximum curvature discrepancy is below
5e-16. Finite numerical checks corroborate the analytic proof and do not prove
any larger family.
