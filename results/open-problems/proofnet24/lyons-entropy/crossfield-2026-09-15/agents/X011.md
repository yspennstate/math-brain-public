# X011 — stable polarization and the exact cost of signed directions

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: COMPLETE bounded theorem.** No general proof of Lyons entropy concavity is claimed.

**Main advance.** Stable rank-one polarization gives an exact nonnegative-square decomposition for arbitrary indefinite Hermitian directions. If `r=rank A`, `J_A=FI_K(A)`, and `Delta` bounds the oscillation of `-log p` after a modular correction, then

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)\ge (1+\Delta/r)J_A-\Delta J_{|A|}.}
\tag{A}
\]

The cost of changing a positive direction into a signed direction is precisely controlled by `J_|A|-J_A`. For semidefinite directions (A) reduces to worker X005's bound; for indefinite directions it does not assume that any univariate atom polynomial is real-rooted. A more flexible positive-majorant theorem below gives a certificate using only `n`-by-`n` matrices for **every** Hermitian direction. An explicit dense complex four-site example has a certified strictly negative entropy curvature and an atom polynomial with nonreal roots.

Everything concerns the fixed measured coordinate basis and the actual affine path `K+tA`. Spectral coordinates below decompose the *direction*; they do not replace measured entropy by a spectral entropy.

## 1. Exact stable polarization, with off-diagonal motion retained

Let `0<K<I`. For an occupation set `S`, write

\[
M_S=K-I_{S^c},\qquad p_S=(-1)^{|S^c|}\det M_S>0.
\]

Strict positivity follows, for example, from the odds representation `p_S=det L_S/det(I+L)` with `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0`. In particular all `M_S` are invertible.

Let `A=U diag(a_1,...,a_r)U*` be its spectral decomposition on the nonzero eigenspaces, with columns `u_i`. Define

\[
F_S(z)=(-1)^{|S^c|}\det\left(M_S+\sum_{i=1}^r z_i u_i u_i^*\right).
\tag{1}
\]

**Polarization theorem.** Each `F_S` is a real multiaffine stable polynomial, and

\[
p_S(K+tA)=F_S(t a_1,\ldots,t a_r).
\tag{2}
\]

Here stable means nonvanishing when every variable has positive imaginary part. To prove this directly, if the matrix in (1) killed a nonzero vector `v`, taking the imaginary part of its quadratic form would give

`sum_i Im(z_i)|u_i* v|^2=0`.

Thus every `u_i* v=0`, and the same null equation would imply `M_S v=0`, a contradiction. For real variables the matrix is Hermitian, so its determinant has real coefficients. Rank-one dependence makes the polynomial multiaffine. This is the special case of Borcea–Brändén–Liggett, Proposition 3.2, where the variable coefficients are rank-one positive semidefinite matrices and the constant matrix is Hermitian; the constant matrix need not be positive semidefinite. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.2340).

Set

\[
b_{ij}(S)=u_i^*M_S^{-1}u_j,\qquad b_i(S)=b_{ii}(S).
\]

Determinant differentiation yields

\[
\partial_iF_S=p_S b_i,\qquad
\partial_{ij}F_S=p_S(b_i b_j-|b_{ij}|^2),\quad i\ne j,
\tag{3}
\]

and consequently the exact Rayleigh-square identity

\[
\boxed{(\partial_iF_S)(\partial_jF_S)-F_S\partial_{ij}F_S
=p_S^2|b_{ij}|^2.}
\tag{4}
\]

Multiaffinity also gives the same identity for `i=j`, since `partial_ii F_S=0`. Summing derivatives of the normalized atom family gives the matrix-valued Fisher identity

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_p[b_i b_j]=\mathbb E_p[|b_{ij}|^2].}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the matrix on the right of (5) is positive semidefinite after averaging, even though an individual entrywise-square matrix `(|b_ij|^2)` need not be positive semidefinite.

The entropy Hessian in this rank-one parameter span is minus the matrix

\[
\mathcal C_{ij}=
\mathbb E_p\left[b_i b_j+(b_i b_j-|b_{ij}|^2)\log p_S\right].
\tag{6}
\]

This is the exact multivariate local target. In particular, replacing a signed specialization by a positive one is not harmless: stability of (1) only ensures real-rooted specializations with slopes of one sign. The theorem below accounts for the signed loss instead of assuming it vanishes.

## 2. The signed-square theorem

Suppose `A!=0`, and put `D=|A|`. On the active range of `A`, define `E=sign A`, so that `E^2=I_r`. For each atom define

\[
B_S=D^{1/2}M_S^{-1}D^{1/2},\qquad s_S=\operatorname{tr}(EB_S),
\]

\[
C_S=\|(B_S)_{+,-}\|_F^2,\qquad
V_S=\left\|B_S-\frac{s_S}{r}E\right\|_F^2.
\tag{7}
\]

The blocks refer to the positive and negative eigenspaces of `A`. The matrix `B_S` is Hermitian, and both `C_S,V_S` are nonnegative.

A function is modular if it has the form `m(S)=a+sum_(i in S) b_i`. Suppose that

\[
f_S=-\log p_S-m(S),\qquad 0\le f_S\le\Delta.
\tag{8}
\]

Write `J_T=FI_K(T)` for the measured Fisher information in any Hermitian direction `T`.

**Theorem 1.** The following exact square decomposition holds:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&(1+\Delta/r)J_A-\Delta J_D\\
&+\mathbb E_p[f_S V_S]\\
&+\mathbb E_p\left[(\Delta-f_S)
\left(\frac{r-1}{r}s_S^2+4C_S\right)\right].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9}
\]

Every term on the last two lines is nonnegative. Thus (A) follows, and whenever `J_A>0`, the explicit sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{\Delta\le\frac{rJ_A}{rJ_D-J_A}.}
\tag{10}
\]

For rank one the denominator is zero, and the usual exact rank-one identity applies. For `r>=2`, `J_D>=J_A` makes the denominator positive whenever `J_A>0`.

**Proof.** Put `q_S=tr(EB_SEB_S)`. Ordinary determinant differentiation and normalization give

\[
p''_S=p_S(s_S^2-q_S),\qquad
\mathbb E_p q_S=\mathbb E_p s_S^2=J_A.
\tag{11}
\]

The same calculation along `D` gives `E tr B_S^2=J_D`. Hermitian block multiplication gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}B_S^2-q_S=4C_S,
\]

hence the normalized signed-polarization identity

\[
\boxed{4\mathbb E_p C_S=J_D-J_A\ge0.}
\tag{12}
\]

Equivalently, if `A=A_+-A_-`, the Fisher bilinear form obeys

`FI_K(A_+,A_-)=E C_S>=0`,

and `J_D-J_A=4 FI_K(A_+,A_-)`. Formula (12) is also obtained by summing the Rayleigh squares (4) across the two signs.

Hilbert–Schmidt projection onto the line spanned by `E` gives

\[
V_S=\operatorname{tr}B_S^2-s_S^2/r\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]

Every modular table satisfies `sum_S p''_S m(S)=0`: the total mass is constant and every one-site mean is affine along `K+tA`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)
&=J_A+\mathbb E_p[f_S(q_S-s_S^2)]\\
&=J_A+\mathbb E_p\left[f_S
\left(V_S-\frac{r-1}{r}s_S^2-4C_S\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]

Add and subtract `Delta[(r-1)J_A/r+4 E C]`, then use (12). This is exactly (9). The inequalities follow. ∎

For semidefinite `A`, `C_S=0` and `J_D=J_A`. The coefficient in (9) becomes `1-(r-1)Delta/r`, recovering X005. For indefinite `A`, the additional cost is `Delta(J_D-J_A)`. It is quantified by an actual averaged off-block square, rather than an unsupported extension of real-rootedness.

## 3. A flexible positive majorant

The special majorant `D=|A|` is not required. Work on a subspace containing the range of `A`, and let `D` be positive definite there. Assume

\[
T=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2},\qquad \|T\|_{\rm op}\le1,
\qquad \kappa=\operatorname{tr}T^2\ge1.
\tag{15}
\]

A convenient way to ensure the final condition is to scale a positive majorant until `||T||op=1`. Define

\[
B_S=D^{1/2}M_S^{-1}D^{1/2},\quad s_S=\operatorname{tr}(TB_S),
\]

\[
Z_S=\operatorname{tr}B_S^2-\operatorname{tr}(TB_STB_S),
\qquad
V_S^{(D)}=\left\|B_S-\frac{s_S}{\kappa}T\right\|_F^2.
\tag{16}
\]

Both quantities are nonnegative. Indeed, diagonalize the fixed Hermitian `T`, with eigenvalues `tau_i in [-1,1]`. Then

\[
Z_S=\sum_i(1-\tau_i^2)|(B_S)_{ii}|^2
+2\sum_{i<j}(1-\tau_i\tau_j)|(B_S)_{ij}|^2\ge0,
\tag{17}
\]

and `V_S^(D)=tr B_S^2-s_S^2/kappa>=0` by Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz.

**Theorem 2.** Under (8) and (15),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&(1+\Delta/\kappa)J_A-\Delta J_D\\
&+\mathbb E_p[f_S V_S^{(D)}]\\
&+\mathbb E_p[(\Delta-f_S)((1-1/\kappa)s_S^2+Z_S)].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{18}
\]

**Proof.** Normalization of the two affine determinant paths gives

`E Z_S=J_D-J_A`.

Moreover,

`tr(TB_STB_S)-s_S^2=V_S^(D)-(1-1/kappa)s_S^2-Z_S`.

Substitute this identity into the first line of (14), then add and subtract `Delta[(1-1/kappa)J_A+E Z_S]`. This proves (18); its last two lines are nonnegative by (15)–(17). ∎

Taking `D=|A|`, `T=sign A`, and `kappa=r` recovers (9), since then `Z_S=4C_S`.

## 4. A certificate requiring only matrices of size n

Let

\[
\Sigma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}K_{ii}-|K_{ij}|^2,
\qquad g=(A_{11},\ldots,A_{nn})^T,
\qquad \Gamma=g^T\Sigma^{-1}g.
\tag{19}
\]

Here `Sigma=Cov_K(X)` follows directly from the one- and two-site determinantal moments. It is positive definite for a strict kernel: the occupation law has full support, so a nonzero linear function of `X` cannot be constant almost surely.

Set

\[
a=\|A\|_{\rm op},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\|A\|_F^2}{a^2}\ge1,\qquad
Q=a^2\operatorname{tr}[(K(I-K))^{-1}].
\tag{20}
\]

**Corollary 3 (all Hermitian directions, conditional algebraic certificate).** Every nonzero Hermitian `A` obeys

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)\ge(1+\Delta/\kappa)\Gamma-\Delta Q.}
\tag{21}
\]

Therefore nonnegativity of the right-hand side certifies the full local entropy-concavity sign. In particular, it suffices that

\[
\Delta\le\frac{\kappa\Gamma}{\kappa Q-\Gamma}
\tag{22}
\]

whenever `Gamma>0` and the denominator is positive. The certificate uses a covariance matrix, two matrix inverses, a determinant, and norms. It does not enumerate occupation atoms. It is a sufficient certificate; its right-hand side is not asserted to be nonnegative universally.

**Proof of the Fisher lower bound.** Let `s_A=p'_S/p_S`. Differentiating the one-site expectations gives

`Cov_p(s_A,X)=g`.

The squared norm of the orthogonal projection of `s_A` onto the linear span of the centered occupations is therefore `Gamma`. Hence

\[
J_A\ge\Gamma.
\tag{23}
\]

No assertion is made that these linear statistics carry the entire Fisher information.

**Proof of the Fisher upper bound.** Apply Theorem 2 with `D=aI`, so `T=A/a` and `kappa` is exactly (20). Along `K+taI`, a fixed eigenbasis of `K` remains an eigenbasis. The spectral Bernoulli-mixture representation of a DPP samples independent mode occupations with parameters `lambda_i+ta` and then applies a fixed projection-DPP channel to obtain the measured occupation set. The conditional channel does not depend on `t`. Thus the measured score is the conditional expectation of the product-Bernoulli score. Jensen's inequality gives

\[
J_{aI}\le a^2\sum_i\frac1{\lambda_i(1-\lambda_i)}=Q.
\tag{24}
\]

For completeness, the mixture identity follows by expanding every determinantal inclusion probability in the spectral rank-one summands: repeated summands vanish under the exterior product, and each surviving product of eigenvalues is the expectation of the same product of independent Bernoulli variables. This is also Lyons, Theorem 2.11. Its hypotheses hold here because the base is a finite positive contraction and the perturbation has a fixed eigenbasis. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2707).

Combining the nonnegative-remainder bound from (18) with (23) and (24) proves (21). ∎

If one of the nonnegative lower-bound conditions holds at every interior point of a strict affine segment, integrating the local Hessian sign proves entropy concavity on that segment. Checking only the endpoints has not been shown sufficient.

There is a sharper variant when `[|A|,K]=0`: use `D=|A|` and `kappa=r`, and replace `Q` by

`tr[|A|^2(K(I-K))^-1]`.

The same fixed-common-eigenbasis argument proves its Fisher upper bound. All Hermitian involutions satisfy the required commutation of the absolute value, even when `[A,K]!=0`.

## 5. The modular spread from odds correlations

Let

\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1},\quad E_L=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{L_{ii}}),
\quad R=E_L^{-1}LE_L^{-1}.
\]

Then `R>0`, `R_ii=1`, and

\[
-\log p_S=\log\det(I+L)-\sum_{i\in S}\log L_{ii}-\log\det R_S.
\tag{25}
\]

The first two terms are modular. Hadamard's inequality gives `det R_S<=1`. The Schur complement of `R_S` is positive definite and has diagonal entries at most one, so its determinant is at most one. Hence

\[
0<\det R\le\det R_S\le1.
\tag{26}
\]

Consequently every preceding theorem holds with

\[
f_S=-\log\det R_S,\qquad \Delta=-\log\det R,
\tag{27}
\]

or with any larger certified upper bound. This spread is unchanged by positive diagonal scaling of the odds matrix. No such invariance is claimed for the Fisher ratio or covariance certificate. The formulas retain arbitrary external fields and complex cycle phases.

One may instead optimize over modular corrections. This recovers the `Delta_mod` quantity in X005 and can only improve the sufficient bounds.

## 7. Exact remaining lemma

The stable polarization is universal, and identities (9) and (18) are universal on their stated domains. Their scalar lower bounds need not be nonnegative for every direction. The exact residual assertion needed to finish the unrestricted theorem using the absolute-value polarization is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_p[f_SV_S]
+\mathbb E_p\left[(\Delta-f_S)
\left(\frac{r-1}{r}s_S^2+4C_S\right)\right]\\
&\hspace{3em}\ge\Delta J_{|A|}-(1+\Delta/r)J_A.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{34}
\]

Only the case where the right-hand side is positive remains beyond the proved certificate. Atomwise stability proves the unweighted identity (12) but does not by itself prove this log-weighted comparison.

A concrete limitation is a Fisher-null direction: `J_A=0` forces every `s_S=0`, but `J_|A|` can be strictly positive. The remaining statement then reduces exactly to

\[
\mathbb E_p[f_S\operatorname{tr}B_S^2]
\ge 4\mathbb E_p[f_SC_S].
\tag{35}
\]

The nonnegative trace-free and cross-block squares must then be compared with their actual entropy weights. No Fisher-ratio or linear-observable shortcut can settle that case. This is consistent with X002's hidden circulation: measured first derivatives can vanish while individual second-acceleration contributions remain nonzero.

Hillion–Johnson's Shepp–Olkin argument proves a sharp local derivative inequality for the one-dimensional mass function of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables (Proposition 2.4). Its variables are count probabilities and deletion currents, whereas (1) indexes full measured atoms and signed rank-one directions. Their theorem is used here as motivation, not as a black box covering (34). [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01570).
