# X015 — all three-site semidefinite directions by conditional endpoint Fisher compensation

15 September 2026. **Status: COMPLETE bounded proof.** Every pair of three-site kernels comparable in positive semidefinite order satisfies the Lyons entropy inequality. At every strict three-site base, every nonzero semidefinite Hermitian direction has an explicit strict Fisher margin. This removes X006's zero-three-way-log-interaction restriction. A computable open cone also includes full-rank indefinite directions at every base. Unrestricted three-site concavity remains open; no historical-priority claim is made.

The decisive new step is a probability-sensitive conditional Fisher bound, followed by a matrix comparison using logarithmic convexity. Replacing the conditional empty/full probabilities by the uniform 2/7 constant before this comparison loses the information needed when the three-way interaction is nonzero.

## 1. Main theorem

Write H(K)=H(P_K), with natural logarithms and the original measured basis. For 0<K<I on three coordinates and a Hermitian A, let

    I_K(A)=sum_S (Dp_S(K)[A])^2/p_S(K),
    D_K(A)=sum_i Aii^2/[Kii(1-Kii)].

**Theorem 1.** If A is positive or negative semidefinite, then

    H''_K(A) <= -I_K(A)/7 -2 D_K(A)/7.                 (1)

In particular the curvature is strictly negative whenever A is nonzero. No restriction on complex phases, sparsity, eigenvectors, diagonal thresholds, or three-way logarithmic interaction is imposed.

**Corollary 2 (all comparable three-site endpoints).** If 0<=K0<=K1<=I are three-by-three Hermitian kernels, then for 0<=t<=1,

    H((1-t)K0+tK1) >= (1-t)H(K0)+tH(K1).              (2)

There is also the quantitative bound

    H((1-t)K0+tK1)-(1-t)H(K0)-tH(K1)
      >= (6/7)t(1-t) sum_i [(K1-K0)ii]^2.             (3)

Here is the extra Fisher fact needed for the displayed coefficient. Put d_i=Aii, q_i=Kii(1-Kii), and Z=sum_i d_i(X_i-Kii)/q_i. The atom score s_A has E[s_A Z]=D_K(A). Since Cov(X_i,X_j)=-|Kij|^2 for i!=j and all d_i have the same sign,

    Var(Z)=D_K(A)-2 sum_{i<j}|Kij|^2 d_i d_j/(q_i q_j)
          <=D_K(A).

Cauchy-Schwarz gives I_K(A)>=D_K(A) (the zero case is immediate). Thus (1) implies -H''>=3D_K/7>=12 sum_i d_i^2/7. Integration gives (3). For arbitrary closed endpoints, apply the strict result to Kj,epsilon=(1-2epsilon)Kj+epsilon I and take epsilon down to zero using continuity of finite-law entropy. This also proves (2), including singular or shared deterministic coordinates.

## 2. The complete three-site ambient curvature

For every atom S, expand its frozen negative logarithm as the Boolean multilinear polynomial

    -log p_S = a0+sum_i ai Xi+sum_{i<j} cij Xi Xj+tau X1 X2 X3.

Write c_k=c_ij for the pair complementary to k, C=diag(c_1,c_2,c_3), and

    M=C+tau K.                                       (4)

The two conditional tables on the complementary pair, conditioned on Xk=s, have log cross ratios

    lambda_k0=c_k,       lambda_k1=c_k+tau.             (5)

Both numbers are nonnegative. To verify this without importing negative association, a strict two-site conditional kernel J has atoms

    x=det(I-J), y=J11-det J, z=J22-det J, w=det J,

and xw-yz=-|J12|^2<=0. Thus lambda=log(yz/(xw))>=0. The conditional kernel formulas in Section 4 show these are actual strict two-site DPPs. Dividing the four joint atoms by their common conditioning probability does not change a log cross ratio, which proves (5).

The exact entropy Hessian is

    H''_K(A)=-I_K(A)+2 tr(M adj A).                    (6)

For completeness, the first and constant terms in the frozen polynomial have zero second expectation derivative. The pair inclusion moments give D^2 det(Kij)[Aij,Aij]=2 det(Aij). In dimension three, D^2 det(K)[A,A]=2 tr(K adj A). Substitution into H''=-I-sum p'' log p gives (6). In particular, no affine-kernel acceleration is omitted.

Also M>=0. If tau>=0, this follows from C>=0 and K>0. If tau<0, write M=diag(c_k+tau)+(-tau)(I-K). The argument below does not need M to be nonsingular or the base to be connected.

## 3. Retaining actual conditional probabilities: the endpoint Fisher bound

For a strict two-site kernel J with the four atoms x,y,z,w just defined, set

    omega(J)=2+(y+z)/sqrt(xw),
    f(lambda)=2+2 exp(lambda/2).

**Lemma 3.** For every Hermitian two-by-two B,

    I_J(B)>=2 omega(J) det B,                         (7)
    omega(J)>=f(lambda(J)).                           (8)

If det B<=0, (7) is immediate. If det B>0, reverse B if necessary so B>0. Retain only the empty and full Fisher terms and put

    P=adj(J)/sqrt(w), Q=adj(I-J)/sqrt(x).

The matrices P,Q are positive definite, have determinant one, and

    det(P+Q)=2+(y+z)/sqrt(xw)=omega(J).

The last identity follows by expanding a two-by-two determinant; the cross numerator is tr(J adj(I-J))=tr J-2 det J=y+z. Hence

    I_J(B)>=[tr(PB)]^2+[tr(QB)]^2
           >=[tr((P+Q)B)]^2/2
           >=2 det(P+Q) det B.

The final inequality is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for the two positive eigenvalues of B^(1/2)(P+Q)B^(1/2). This proves (7). Finally y+z>=2sqrt(yz) gives (8).

Two elementary scalar properties will be used:

    f(x)>=kappa x+sigma for x>=0,
    sqrt(f(x)f(y))>=f((x+y)/2),                       (9)

where

    kappa=7/2,       sigma=9-7 log(7/2)>0.             (10)

The first bound follows by minimizing f(x)-kappa x: its derivative is exp(x/2)-7/2, and its minimum is exactly sigma. Positivity is elementary: the first five exponential-series terms give

    exp(9/7)>68771/19208>7/2,

so log(7/2)<9/7. For the second bound, expand both sides squared; it is precisely exp(x/2)+exp(y/2)>=2 exp((x+y)/4). Thus no external operator-convexity assertion is needed.

## 4. The conditional identity as a sum of column outer products

Fix pivot k, put a=Kkk, b=1-a, and partition the remaining two coordinates R. Write v=K_R,k, d=Akk, w=A_R,k, B=A_R,R. For s in {0,1}, put r_s=a-(1-s), pi_1=a, pi_0=b. The actual conditional kernel and its first derivative along K+tA are

    J_s=K_R,R-vv*/r_s,
    E_s=B-(wv*+vw*)/r_s+d vv*/r_s^2.                 (11)

The kernel formulas follow from the determinant Schur complement for conditioning on presence and from its complementary version for absence. K>0 and I-K>0 imply both conditionals are strict contractions. The velocities in (11) are their direct derivatives, including the changing pivot denominator.

For any nonnegative branch coefficients omega_0,omega_1, define

    N_k=(omega_1/a)(K e_k)(K e_k)*
        +(omega_0/b)((I-K)e_k)((I-K)e_k)*.             (12)

**Lemma 4.** For every Hermitian A,

    sum_s pi_s omega_s det E_s=tr(N_k adj A).         (13)

One direct proof expands the two-by-two determinant. Set

    L=2 Re(v* adj(B)w),
    E=d v*adj(B)v-|v wedge w|^2.

Then det E_s=det B-L/r_s+E/r_s^2. The branch s=1 term is the quadratic form (K e_k)*adj(A)(K e_k)/a^2, and the branch s=0 term is the analogous form for (I-K)e_k divided by b^2. Multiplication by pi_s proves (13). This is also the arbitrary-coefficient form of X006's block determinant identity, now written in the column representation that permits the comparison below.

Take omega_s=omega(J_s) from Lemma 3 and sum (12) over pivots. The resulting matrix is

    N=K diag(omega_k1/Kkk) K
      +(I-K) diag(omega_k0/(1-Kkk)) (I-K).            (14)

The complete score chain rule at each pivot is

    I_K(A)=d^2/(ab)+sum_s pi_s I_Js(E_s).             (15)

Indeed the joint score is the pivot score plus the conditional score; the conditional score has mean zero, so the cross term vanishes. Summing over k and applying (7),(13) gives

    3 I_K(A)-D_K(A)>=2 tr(N adj A).                  (16)

This is valid for every Hermitian A. The conditional entropy Hessians were not treated as affine paths. All conditional velocities and the ambient acceleration in (6) remain exact.

## 5. The new matrix compensation theorem

**Lemma 5 (column comparison).** The matrix (14) satisfies the universal inequality

    N >= (7/2)M+sigma I,                             (17)

with sigma from (10).

**Proof.** Fix any u in C^3 and write r=Ku, s=(I-K)u, so r+s=u. For each coordinate k set x=c_k+tau>=0 and y=c_k>=0. By (8), the kth summand of u*Nu is at least

    f(x)|r_k|^2/a+f(y)|s_k|^2/b.

Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz, using a+b=1, bounds this below by

    (sqrt(f(x))|r_k|+sqrt(f(y))|s_k|)^2.

Apply both inequalities in (9), including their common sigma remainder. The result is at least

    kappa[x|r_k|^2+y|s_k|^2+(x+y)|r_k||s_k|]
      +sigma(|r_k|+|s_k|)^2

and hence at least

    kappa[x|r_k|^2+y|s_k|^2+(x+y)Re(conj(r_k)s_k)]
      +sigma |u_k|^2.                               (18)

The coefficient x+y is nonnegative, so the replacement of absolute values by the real part has the stated direction. As x-y=tau,

    x|r_k|^2+y|s_k|^2+(x+y)Re(conj(r_k)s_k)
      =c_k|u_k|^2+tau Re(conj(u_k)r_k).

Sum over k. Since K is Hermitian, sum Re(conj(u_k)r_k)=u*Ku. The result is u*Nu>=kappa u*Mu+sigma||u||^2, proving (17). ∎

The scalar shift shared by the two conditional log interactions is essential to the final sum. It is exactly the three-site Boolean-logarithm identity (5).

## 6. Semidefinite directions, strictness, and a larger indefinite cone

If A is positive or negative semidefinite in dimension three, adj A is positive semidefinite. Pair (17) with adj A, then combine it with (16):

    3 I_K(A)-D_K(A)
      >=7 tr(M adj A)+2sigma tr(adj A).

Substitution into (6) proves the stronger version of Theorem 1,

    H''_K(A)<=-I_K(A)/7-2D_K(A)/7
              -(4sigma/7)tr(adj A).                 (19)

A nonzero semidefinite A has at least one nonzero diagonal, so D_K(A)>0. This proves strictness, including rank-one directions and disconnected bases, without a nonsingularity assumption on M.

There is also a direct directional certificate for indefinite directions. Put

    S=N-3M >= M/2+sigma I >0.                        (20)

From (16) and (6), for every Hermitian A,

    H''_K(A)<=-D_K(A)/3-(2/3)tr(S adj A).             (21)

Thus every direction with tr(S adj A)>=0 has nonpositive curvature. At every strict base this gives full-rank indefinite directions: for any unit u and epsilon>0, take

    A=I-(1+epsilon)uu*.

Its eigenvalues are (1,1,-epsilon), and

    tr(S adj A)=u*Su-epsilon[tr S-u*Su].

Consequently all such directions with

    0<epsilon<lambda_min(S)/(tr S-lambda_min(S))       (22)

have strictly negative curvature. Congruence by any invertible matrix gives the corresponding intrinsic family with S replaced by its inverse congruence. This extends the same cone mechanism used by X006 on tau=0 to every strict three-site base; the new mathematical content is (17) and its universal semidefinite consequence.

## 7. Exact rational example and nonperturbative scope

Take

    K=[[1/5,1/10,1/20],
       [1/10,3/5,1/10],
       [1/20,1/10,4/5]],
    A=[[1,1/5,-1/10],
       [1/5,2,1/20],
       [-1/10,1/20,1]].                              (23)

Gershgorin gives K>=I/20 and I-K>=I/20; A is positive definite. The base straddles I/2, since its first and third diagonal entries are 1/5 and 4/5. Its atom masses in mask order are (104,36,211,49,516,144,769,171)/2000. Thus its three-way log coefficient is exactly tau=log(3918824/4654449)<0, which proves it lies outside the tau=0 locus. The direction has rank three, changes all measured diagonals and three coherences, and is not of the form alpha K+diagonal: its off-diagonal entry ratios are 2,-2,1/2.

The theorem applies to the whole physical interval of K+tA, not only a sufficiently small perturbation or an old balanced conditional-mode box. For an explicit closed segment, -1/100<=t<=1/100 is strict by the same Gershgorin bounds. The endpoints are ordered and do not commute, since [K,A] is nonzero. The exact commutator entry [K,A]12=13/400 proves noncommutation. For the endpoints K±A/100, the midpoint gap is at least 9/17500 by (3). This example illustrates scope; the general proof is Sections 2–6.
