# X022 — two moving leaves, a five-path potential, and all-edge radial chords

15 September 2026. **Status: COMPLETE bounded result.** The report proves an exact two-leaf Hessian identity that retains both moving Bernoulli weights and their mixed entropy rectangle. It also deduces a full-domain balanced-forest chord theorem, with a quartic margin, when the independent edge radial slopes have a common sign at every point of the chord. All internal edges may move independently. Unrestricted five-path concavity, arbitrary moving leaf means, and full Lyons concavity remain unresolved.

The forest chord theorem is explicitly a corollary of X014's proved positive chromatic entropy series. Its additional scope is moving internal edges without a fixed incident-row shape, subject to the stated slope condition. The independent pendant theorem of X014 and the free-pair/fixed-reservoir theorems C059/C062 are inputs or prior scope, not new claims here. No historical priority outside the supplied record is asserted.

## 1. A full-strength theorem allowing every internal edge to move

Fix a forest T in the measured coordinate basis. For each edge e let

    z_e(t)=z_e^0+t h_e,     h_e=z_e^1-z_e^0,     0<=t<=1,
    K(t)=I/2+B(z(t)),

where B has zero diagonal and Hermitian complex entries supported on T. Suppose both endpoint kernels are positive contractions. Define

    r_e(t)=Re(conj(z_e(t)) h_e).

**Theorem 1 (common radial sign along the chord).** Suppose that, for every t in [0,1] and every pair of edges e,f in the same connected component,

    r_e(t) r_f(t)>=0.                                         (1)

Then

    H(P_K(t))-(1-t)H(P_K(0))-t H(P_K(1))
       >= 8 sum_e [(1-t)|z_e^0|^4+t|z_e^1|^4-|z_e(t)|^4]
       >=0.                                                  (2)

For distinct endpoint edge arrays the first entropy gap is strictly positive when 0<t<1. No bound keeping the coupling strengths away from their physical boundary is assumed. The phases and the internal-edge ratios may change independently.

At every strict point of such a line, the associated local estimate is

    -H''(t)>=64 sum_e r_e(t)^2
                    +32 sum_e |z_e(t)|^2 |h_e|^2.             (3)

**The condition is pointwise along the entire chord.** In particular, checking that all initial slopes have one common sign and all final slopes have one common sign, without comparing the signs between those times, is insufficient. For example, two radial slopes proportional to t-1/3 and t-2/3 agree in sign at both endpoints and disagree between their zeros. Condition (1) permits an interior sign change only when every nonconstant edge in that component turns at the same time.

Two simple sufficient endpoint tests are useful: all r_e(0)>=0, or all r_e(1)<=0, component by component. These work because

    r_e(t)=r_e(0)+t|h_e|^2

is nondecreasing. A common interior turning time is another permitted case. Different connected components need not share their sign or turning time, since their entropies add.

### Proof

Put w_e=4|z_e|^2 and F_T(w)=|V(T)|log 2-H(P_K). The precise input from X014, Sections 2-3, is

    F_T(w)=sum_(alpha nonzero, every alpha_e even) c_alpha w^alpha,
    c_alpha>=0,                                              (4)

with locally absolutely convergent derivatives throughout the strict physical region rho(C(w))<1, where C(w) has entries sqrt(w_e) on the tree edges. The coefficient is positive exactly for connected supported edge sets, and c_(2e)=1/2. Thus

    R_T(w):=F_T(w)-(1/2)sum_e w_e^2                           (5)

has the same convergence property and nonnegative Taylor coefficients.

Here the hypotheses of that input really apply. A diagonal phase gauge makes tree edges real and nonnegative without changing measured probabilities; the bipartite centered spectrum makes strict contraction equivalent to rho(C(w))<1. The affine kernel chord stays in the strict contraction domain when its endpoints are strict. No change of the measured basis or affine-odds substitution is used.

Along the actual complex affine entries,

    w_e'=8r_e,       w_e''=8|h_e|^2>=0.                       (6)

For any monomial w^alpha occurring in (5), its second derivative is a sum of three kinds of terms: terms proportional to w_e'', terms proportional to (w_e')^2, and mixed terms proportional to w_e'w_f'. The first two kinds are nonnegative. Every supported edge set is connected, so (1) makes the third kind nonnegative as well. This calculation uses polynomial derivatives and remains valid at zero edge strengths. The locally absolutely convergent differentiated series therefore gives

    d^2 R_T(w(t))/dt^2>=0.                                   (7)

As (1/2)sum w_e^2=8 sum |z_e|^4, (7) says precisely that

    t -> H(P_K(t))+8 sum_e |z_e(t)|^4

is concave. Its chord inequality is (2); differentiating its quartic part gives (3). Strict convexity of |z|^4 on the complex plane makes the quartic chord difference positive whenever some edge changes.

For closed-domain endpoints, replace both kernels by

    K_j^(rho)=I/2+rho(K_j-I/2),      0<rho<1.

These endpoints are strict, their radial slopes are multiplied by rho^2, and their quartic margin is multiplied by rho^4. Apply the strict result and let rho increase to one. Finite determinantal probabilities and -p log p are continuous at the boundary. This proves the entire closed-domain theorem. ∎

### A five-path chord outside the fixed incident-row shape

Take all five diagonals equal to 1/2 and the successive real edge endpoints

    z^0=(1/5, 1/8, 1/10, 1/6),
    z^1=(3/10, 1/5, 1/6, 1/4).                              (8)

All four radial slopes are positive throughout the chord. Both internal links move, and their ratio changes from 5/4 to 6/5. For the final strengths (a,b,c,d)=(9/25,4/25,1/9,1/4), exact five-path feasibility is

    b/(1-a)+c/(1-d)=43/108<1.                               (9)

The initial strengths are smaller, so they too are strict. At t=1/2, the explicit lower bound in (2) is

    1156843/82944000 = 0.01394727768132716... .                (10)

The actual entropy gap is approximately 0.015340227656662405, provided only as an illustration.

For a five-path whose four edges all change, the only two-coordinate core leaving an independent exterior is the pair {2,4}. Its central exterior row has the two internal links as its entries; their changing ratio in (8) violates the fixed-shape satellite hypothesis. Every other pair leaves a changing edge inside its exterior. Thus C059/C062 do not directly contain this chord. Likewise, neither bipartition permits fixed independent-set row amplitudes in C021: one choice ties the two internal links, and the other ties each outer link to its neighboring internal link. The ratios in (8) violate these ties. The theorem is nonetheless a straightforward additional consequence of X014, and is credited as such.

## 2. The exact balanced five-path elimination identity

Let a,b,c,d be the four successive normalized squared edge strengths, and let t_1,...,t_4 be independent uniform signs. The tree matching likelihood is

    q=1+a t_1+b t_2+c t_3+d t_4
          +ac t_1t_3+ad t_1t_4+bd t_2t_4,
    F(a,b,c,d)=5 log 2-H=E q log q.                          (11)

Only singleton edge matchings and the three displayed two-edge matchings occur. Put Q=1+a t_1 and R=1+d t_4. Then

    q=QR+b t_2 R+c t_3 Q.                                   (12)

Define

    phi(x)=E_u (1+xu)log(1+xu),
    Phi(x,y)=E_(u,v)(1+xu+yv)log(1+xu+yv).

Averaging first over t_2,t_3 gives the exact identity

    F(a,b,c,d)=phi(a)+phi(d)
       +E_(t_1,t_4) QR Phi(b/Q,c/R).                        (13)

Indeed E_(t_2,t_3)q=QR; the terms involving log Q and log R then average to phi(a)+phi(d), because E Q=E R=1. No entropy correction has been dropped.

The exact strict feasibility condition is

    a,d<1,       b/(1-a)+c/(1-d)<1.                         (14)

To prove it, order the bipartition of the normalized adjacency matrix so its cross block is

    U = [[sqrt(a),0],[sqrt(b),sqrt(c)],[0,sqrt(d)]].

Then I-U*U>0 is the two-by-two condition

    a+b<1, c+d<1,
    (1-a-b)(1-c-d)>bc.

Its determinant is (1-a)(1-d)-b(1-d)-c(1-a), giving (14); conversely (14) gives both positive diagonal entries. For every outer sign assignment, Q>=1-a and R>=1-d, so

    b/Q+c/R<=b/(1-a)+c/(1-d)<1.

Thus every conditional two-variable star potential in (13) is evaluated inside its actual positive domain.

### An exact obstruction to convexity term by term in the strengths

The core potential has an even Taylor expansion. Write

    A_m(x)=((1+x)^(-m)+(1-x)^(-m))/2.

The part of the last term of (13) homogeneous of degree six in b,c is

    G_6(a,b,c,d)
      =[b^6 A_5(a)+c^6 A_5(d)]/30
       +[b^4 c^2 A_3(a)A_1(d)+b^2 c^4 A_1(a)A_3(d)]/2.      (15)

At the strictly physical point

    (a,b,c,d)=(1/5,1/5,3/10,1/10),
    b/(1-a)+c/(1-d)=7/12,

the rational strength direction v=(-6,5,-5,3) satisfies

    v^T Hess(G_6) v
       =-10575079233625/116376761622528<0.                   (16)

This is an exact rational calculation, reproduced by the verifier. It disproves the proposed claim that every homogeneous conditional-core term is jointly convex in all four strengths. It does not disprove convexity of their full sum with phi(a)+phi(d), and it does not claim a negative affine-kernel entropy curvature. In particular the second acceleration in (6) must still be retained when returning from strengths to kernel entries.

The fixed-core pendant argument of X014 correctly avoids this problem: it holds b,c fixed and resums only the pendant variables. Equation (16) identifies the extra compensation that this proof route needs when both internal links also move.

## 3. Both leaf means moving: an exact paired identity

The following identity applies to an arbitrary core dimension. For the requested five-path the core has coordinates 2,3,4, B is its tridiagonal kernel, and U=e_1e_1*, V=e_3e_3*. More generally U=u u*, V=v v* may be any fixed rank-one attachment matrices. The two leaves have zero mutual kernel entry and respective core attachment columns z u and w v.

Along an affine full-kernel line, write the leaf means, amplitudes, and core as

    p(t)=p+t alpha,       q(t)=q+t beta,
    z(t)=z+t eta,         w(t)=w+t nu,
    B(t)=B+t C.                                             (17)

All quantities below are evaluated at t=0, at a strict full kernel. Put

    pi_1=p, pi_0=1-p,       theta_1=q, theta_0=1-q,
    r_1=1/p, r_0=-1/(1-p),
    u_1=1/q, u_0=-1/(1-q),
    s=|z|^2, ell=|w|^2,
    dot s=2 Re(conj(z)eta), dot ell=2 Re(conj(w)nu).

The leaves are independent in their marginal law: their two-by-two kernel is diagonal. Conditioning on both leaf bits x,y therefore gives

    F_xy=B-s r_x U-ell u_y V,
    H(full)=h(p)+h(q)+sum_xy pi_x theta_y H(F_xy).            (18)

This follows directly by the determinant Schur complement of the signed diagonal leaf block. Every F_xy is a strict contraction. Define the actual conditional velocity and the one-leaf conditional velocities by

    f_x=dot s r_x-s alpha r_x^2,
    g_y=dot ell u_y-ell beta u_y^2,
    Z_xy=C-f_x U-g_y V,
    C_x=C-f_x U,          C_y=C-g_y V.                       (19)

The subscript indicates which other leaf has been conditioned on; the matrices C_x,C_y must not be replaced by the unconditional core direction C.

Let the real Hermitian pairing be <A,B>=tr(AB), and set

    G_y=grad H(F_1y)-grad H(F_0y),
    J_x=grad H(F_x1)-grad H(F_x0),
    R_H=H(F_11)-H(F_10)-H(F_01)+H(F_00).                    (20)

**Theorem 2 (complete two-leaf curvature identity).**

    H''(full)
      =sum_xy pi_x theta_y D^2H(F_xy)[Z_xy,Z_xy]
       -alpha^2/[p(1-p)]-beta^2/[q(1-q)]
       +2 sum_y theta_y <G_y, alpha C_y-|eta|^2 U>
       +2 sum_x pi_x <J_x, beta C_x-|nu|^2 V>
       +2 alpha beta R_H.                                  (21)

Thus the exact correction consists of two paired gradient secants and one mixed entropy rectangle. Independence of the two unconditioned leaves does not remove the final term.

### Proof with all weight accelerations retained

Let mu_xy=pi_x theta_y. Differentiating the actual weights gives

    mu'_xy/mu_xy=alpha r_x+beta u_y,
    mu''_xy/mu_xy=2 alpha beta r_x u_y.                      (22)

Since r_x'=-alpha r_x^2 and u_y'=-beta u_y^2,

    F'_xy=Z_xy,
    F''_xy=-2r_x|eta-alpha r_x z|^2 U
            -2u_y|nu-beta u_y w|^2 V.                      (23)

The gradient terms in the second derivative of mu_xy H(F_xy) contain

    F''_xy+2(mu'_xy/mu_xy)Z_xy.

Expanding the two rank-one squares in (23) gives the exact cancellation

    F''_xy+2(alpha r_x+beta u_y)Z_xy
       =2r_x(alpha C_y-|eta|^2 U)
        +2u_y(beta C_x-|nu|^2 V).                           (24)

For each fixed y, sum_x pi_x r_x grad H(F_xy)=G_y; similarly the y-average gives J_x. Also

    sum_xy pi_x theta_y r_x u_y H(F_xy)=R_H.

Insert (22)-(24) into the twice-differentiated chain rule (18), including h''(p)alpha^2 and h''(q)beta^2. This is exactly (21). ∎

### The rectangle itself has an exact integral

Put

    D=s/[p(1-p)],       E=ell/[q(1-q)].

The four conditional kernels are the corners

    F_00, F_00-D U, F_00-E V, F_00-D U-E V.

Every point in the rectangle is a convex combination of the four strict conditional kernels. Hence the fundamental theorem of calculus gives

    R_H=int_0^D int_0^E
          D^2H(F_00-a U-b V)[U,V] db da.                    (25)

This formula justifies the domain of the integral without extrapolating a conditional formula. Even if the core entropy Hessian were known to be negative semidefinite, that fact alone would not sign its mixed entry in (25). Moreover alpha beta can have either sign. The two gradient pairings in (21) must therefore be combined with this rectangle and the full conditional Hessians; separate nodewise signs are not supplied by ordinary concavity.

## 4. The exact remaining induction gap

For the unrestricted five-path let

    Q=-sum_xy pi_x theta_y D^2H(F_xy)[Z_xy,Z_xy],
    C_pair=2 sum_y theta_y <G_y,alpha C_y-|eta|^2 U>
           +2 sum_x pi_x <J_x,beta C_x-|nu|^2 V>.

Equation (21) makes the full local target exactly equivalent to

    C_pair+2 alpha beta R_H
        <= Q+alpha^2/[p(1-p)]+beta^2/[q(1-q)].               (26)

No sign is assumed for Q in this equivalence. For a five-path its summands are three-site path curvatures at the actual, generally nonbalanced, conditional means. The supplied record does not establish all of those curvatures in arbitrary directions. Even a future proof that Q>=0 would still require compensation for the entire left side of (26).

This also answers the induction question precisely. The two-leaf identity applies unchanged to a larger tree after selecting two leaves. It preserves the core's tree support, but changes its neighboring diagonals. At the next deletion, those changed diagonals become moving Bernoulli weights, producing further terms of the form (24)-(25). There is no telescoping sign here that closes unrestricted tree induction. The explicit common-radial-sign theorem in Section 1 does extend to every balanced forest, because its proof uses the convergent whole-tree series rather than an unsupported varying-weight recursion.

The remaining mixed-slope balanced problem can instead be expressed through (13): prove the necessary joint compensation of phi(a)+phi(d) with the full averaged core potential under the actual quadratic strength accelerations. The negative value (16) shows why the individual strength-Taylor terms are not sufficient.
