# X058 — independently moving leaves and fourth-derivative compensation

15 September 2026. **Proved full joint concavity on an explicit convex domain.**
The full Lyons conjecture remains open. All entropies below are Shannon entropy in the fixed measured coordinate basis. No historical priority is asserted.

## 1. The theorem: independent means, complex amplitudes, arbitrary reservoir

Fix `m>=1`, a strict Hermitian reservoir `0<D<I_r`, and `u!=0` in `C^r`. Set

    K(p,z)=[[diag(p_1,...,p_m), z u*],[u z*,D]],  s_i=|z_i|^2,
    a=u*D^(-1)u, b=u*(I-D)^(-1)u, c=max(a,b),
    A(p,z)=sum_i |z_i|^2/p_i, B(p,z)=sum_i |z_i|^2/(1-p_i).      (1)

**Theorem 1.** The measured entropy is jointly concave in all `3m` real parameters `(p_i,Re z_i,Im z_i)` on the closed convex domain

    0<=p_i<=1,       c A(p,z)<=2/5,       c B(p,z)<=2/5.          (2)

At zero denominators use `0/0=0`, with nonzero numerators infeasible. The means, phases and radial slopes vary independently, with either sign.
The normalized constant `2/5` is dimension independent. Every nonconstant feasible chord has a strict interior Jensen gap, quantified in (17).

Indeed quadratic-over-linear functions are convex: for `p>0`, their second variation is `2|eta-alpha z/p|^2/p`. Thus (2) is convex in actual affine
kernel coordinates. Schur complements give exact physical conditions `a A<=1, b B<=1`; hence (2) lies inside the contraction domain.
For strict leaf means every physical kernel in (2) is strict.

This extends X047's synchronized moving-mean theorem on a restricted domain. C059/C062's fixed-mean argument does not supply these changing-weight terms.
Section 5 gives a less restrictive pointwise test; full-domain scope remains open.

## 2. The exact sampler, including all moving history weights

For a reservoir atom y let

    d_y=P_D(y)>0, a_y=(d/dv)P_(D+vuu*)(y)|_(v=0),
    mu_p(x)=prod_i p_i^x_i(1-p_i)^(1-x_i),
    R_i(1)=1/p_i, R_i(0)=-1/(1-p_i),
    q_y(x)=d_y-a_y sum_i s_i R_i(x_i).                          (3)

Conditioning on independent leaves gives reservoir kernel `D-(sum_i s_i R_i(x_i))uu*`. A rank-one determinant update is affine,
so its atom is exactly q_y. This proves the full measured entropy identity

    H(K)=sum_i h(p_i)-sum_y E_(mu_p)[q_y(X)log q_y(X)].           (4)

In particular `E_mu q_y=d_y`, `sum_y a_y=0`. The physical rank-one reservoir interval contains `D-U/a` and `D+U/b`, where `U=uu*`.
Their atom probabilities are nonnegative, giving the useful sharp bounds

    -b <= a_y/d_y <= a.                                        (5)

Under (2), every conditional atom therefore obeys

    (3/5)d_y <= q_y(x) <= (7/5)d_y.                             (6)

We differentiate (4), including both derivatives of mu_p. The resulting curvature is the complete `FI+sum_(x,y) P''_(x,y) log P_(x,y)`; neither
conditional kernels nor changing probabilities are silently frozen.

## 3. A compensated product lemma

Let independent centered variables Y_i take values `l_i=-t_i/(1-p_i)` and `r_i=t_i/p_i`, with respective weights `1-p_i,p_i`, where `t_i>=0`. Along affine `(p_i,t_i)` write

    p_i'=alpha_i, t_i'=sigma_i, k_i(v)=sigma_i-alpha_i v,
    L_i=r_i-l_i, V_i=k_i(r_i)^2/p_i+k_i(l_i)^2/(1-p_i).          (7)

Let D_i differentiate only the i-th pair of parameters. For any C^4 test function f on the whole support interval, exact differentiation gives

    D_i^2 E f(sum Y)=E[f''(sum Y)(Y_i')^2],
    D_i E f(sum Y)=int_(l_i)^(r_i) k_i(v) E_-i f''(v+sum_-i Y) dv,
    D_iD_j E f(sum Y)=int int k_i(v)k_j(w)
                         E_-ij f''''(v+w+sum_-ij Y) dv dw.    (8)

**Proof.** Each scalar branch is the perspective `p_i f(T+t_i/p_i)` or `(1-p_i)f(T-t_i/(1-p_i))`, with T fixed during D_i.
Its second derivative is its weight times `f''(T+Y_i)(Y_i')^2`. For the first derivative, integration by parts gives

    int_l^r (sigma-alpha v)f''(T+v)dv
    =(sigma-alpha r)f'(T+r)-(sigma-alpha l)f'(T+l)
       +alpha[f(T+r)-f(T+l)].

These are precisely the derivatives of both weights and both support points.
Apply this identity twice to obtain the cross formula. Thus (8) retains
all interactions between differently moving leaf histories. No Laplace
integrand is assigned a sign. ∎

Suppose `f''>=m2>0` and `0<=f''''<=M4` throughout the interval. Because
k_i is affine, the trapezoidal bound and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz give

    int_l^r |k_i(v)|dv <= (L_i/2)(|k_i(l_i)|+|k_i(r_i)|)
                         <= (L_i/2)sqrt(V_i).                 (9)

Let C have diagonal zero and `C_ij=L_i L_j` otherwise. With
`x_i=sqrt(V_i)`, the adverse cross contribution in (8) is at most
`(M4/4)x^T Cx`. Consequently

    D^2 E f(sum Y) >= [m2-(M4/4)lambda_max(C)]sum_i V_i,
    lambda_max(C)<=max_i L_i(sum_j L_j-L_i)
                       <=(sum_i L_i)^2/4.                    (10)

The first spectral bound follows from the row sums of a symmetric
nonnegative matrix; it is also valid when all L_i vanish. This pays opposite
signs of the affine currents directly, instead of requiring comonotonicity.

## 4. Applying the lemma and retaining amplitude acceleration

For each `a_y!=0`, apply (8)-(10) with
`t_i=|a_y|s_i`, `f(v)=(d_y-sign(a_y)v)log(d_y-sign(a_y)v)`.
Then `f''=1/q`, `f''''=2/q^3`, and (6) implies

    m2=5/(7d_y), M4=250/(27d_y^3),
    sum_i L_i=|a_y|sum_i s_i/[p_i(1-p_i)] <=(4/5)d_y.

The ratio of the possible adverse term to m2 is at most

    [(7/5)(4/5)^2]/[8(3/5)^3]=14/27.                         (11)

Thus the conditional negative entropy is convex in every `(p,s)` direction
at points of (2), with surplus `13/27`. Define the fixed measured Fisher
information and the strength perspective squares by

    J_D=sum_y a_y^2/d_y>0,
    T_i=(sigma_i-alpha_i s_i/p_i)^2/p_i
           +(sigma_i+alpha_i s_i/(1-p_i))^2/(1-p_i).           (12)

Here `sigma_i=s_i'`; zero a_y contribute zero. Positivity of J_D follows
because some reservoir inclusion mean has derivative `|u_j|^2>0`.
Summing (10)-(11) gives `sum_y (E q_y log q_y)'' >=
(65/189)J_D sum_i T_i` for affine strength parameters.

At fixed p the conditional q_y are affine in all strengths. Differentiating
in one strength, using (6) and `E R_i^2=1/[p_i(1-p_i)]`, gives

    -H_(s_i s_i)>= (5/7)J_D/[p_i(1-p_i)],
    -H_(s_i)>= (5/7)J_D s_i/[p_i(1-p_i)].                     (13)

For the second inequality integrate from s_i=0, where H_(s_i)=0 because
the i-th leaf is independent of its complement. Attenuation stays in (2).
This is also exact one-leaf reset monotonicity, with its quantitative cost.
For an actual affine complex kernel line `z_i'=eta_i`, the retained jets are

    sigma_i=2 Re(conj(z_i)eta_i),       s_i''=2|eta_i|^2.

Combining (4),(10)-(13) proves the complete bound

    -H'' >= sum_i alpha_i^2/[p_i(1-p_i)] +(65/189)J_D sum_i T_i
                   +(10/7)J_D sum_i s_i|eta_i|^2/[p_i(1-p_i)] >=0. (14)

Since `p_i(1-p_i)<=1/4` and

    (|z_i|^4)''=8[Re(conj(z_i)eta_i)]^2+4s_i|eta_i|^2
                                                    <=12s_i|eta_i|^2,

(14) proves that the function

    H(K(p,z))-sum_i h(p_i)+(10/21)J_D sum_i |z_i|^4             (15)

is concave on (2). Integrate along its actual convex kernel chords.
For boundary means use `(p,z)->(rho p+(1-rho)/2,rho z)`, `rho<1`;
convexity of (2) preserves the inequalities, and the regularized kernels are
strict. Entropy continuity and polynomial atom continuity pass to `rho=1`.

## 5. A stronger pointwise certificate and the finite gap

For any strict physical point, define for each y
`qmin_y=min_x q_y(x)`, `qmax_y=max_x q_y(x)`, and
`L_iy=|a_y|s_i/[p_i(1-p_i)]`; let C_y have these pairwise products off diagonal.
The same argument proves full joint Hessian concavity whenever

    qmax_y lambda_max(C_y)<=2 qmin_y^3   for every y.           (16)

This can certify points outside (2), particularly when one leaf dominates;
with only one active width its cross matrix is zero. It is a sufficient
pointwise test, not an assertion that its locus is convex or universal.

For endpoints in (2), put `p_theta=(1-theta)p_0+theta p_1` and likewise z.
The finite theorem from (15), including boundary endpoints, is

    Gamma_theta >= sum_i [h(p_theta,i)-(1-theta)h(p_0i)-theta h(p_1i)]
    +(10/21)J_D sum_i [(1-theta)|z_0i|^4+theta|z_1i|^4-|z_theta,i|^4]. (17)

Both sums are nonnegative, and their sum is strictly positive for distinct
parameters and `0<theta<1`. This proves every claim in Theorem 1.

    z_0=(2+i,1+2i,1-i)/20, z_1=(1+i,3+i,2-i)/20.             (18)

Here `a=240/91`, `b=c=20/7`. The two `(cA,cB)` values are
