## 2. A coupled Hellinger Gram bound on the entire edge

At s=0 put x=r², a=1-x, k_r=-(1/2)log(a), and L=R-rk_r. Then

    U0=[[2/a,2-2k_r],[2-2k_r,2+2x]],
    T0=[[R,0],[L,0]].                                     (5)

For epsilon=+/-1 let h_epsilon=1+epsilon r and
v_epsilon=(1,h_epsilon)^T. The exact logarithmic vector is

    (R,L)^T=(1/2)sum_epsilon epsilon log(h_epsilon)v_epsilon.

The scalar Hellinger divided-difference inequality

    (log h)² <= (h-1)²/h,       h>0,                      (6)

is elementary. Write h=exp(2u); it reduces to |u|<=|sinh u|,
which follows by integrating cosh u>=1. Matrix Cauchy for the two vectors,
followed by (6), gives the complete Gram comparison

    T0 T0^T
      <=(1/2)sum_epsilon log²(h_epsilon)v_epsilon v_epsilon^T
      <=x [[1/a,1],[1,1]].                               (7)

The first difference is exactly one quarter of the outer square
[log(h_+)v_+ +log(h_-)v_-][...]^T. The second difference is a positive
sum of the two outer products, by (6). Thus the cancellations in L are kept
inside a matrix square before estimating any logarithm.

Consequently

    U0-T0T0^T >= W_r,
    W_r=[[(2-x)/a, 2-x+log a],
         [2-x+log a, 2+x]].                              (8)

This is positive semidefinite for every 0<=r<1, and positive definite for
r>0. To verify the determinant, put y=-log(a)>=0. It equals

    G(y)=3exp(y)+2-exp(-y)-(1+exp(-y)-y)².                 (9)

If c0=1+exp(-y)-y>=0, then 0<=c0<=2, whereas the first three terms in
(9) are >=4, strictly for y>0. If c0<0, then |c0|<y and those terms
are >3exp(y)>=3y²/2>y². Here exp(y)>=y²/2 follows from its nonnegative
Taylor series. This proves the stated sign. At r=0, W_r=2[[1,1],[1,1]].

For the strip we need a uniform margin after the physical rational
singularity has been normalized. Write

    Z_r=D W_r D
       =[[2-x,sqrt(a)(1+a+log a)],
         [sqrt(a)(1+a+log a),2+x]].                       (10)

    D(U0-T0T0^T)D >=(1/2)I,        r>=3/5.               (11)

## 3. A perturbation that is quadratic in the small parameter

Keep r fixed and 0<=s<=1/5. Put

    Delta=k-k_r=(1/2)log(c/b).

Since c=b+a s² and log(1+t)<=t for t>=0,

    0<=Delta<=a s²/(2b)<=s²/(2b).                        (12)

The two diagonal entries of U-U0 are nonnegative. Its off-diagonal entry is

    (U-U0)12=2r²s²/c-2Delta.

The two terms being subtracted are nonnegative. Since c>=b and sqrt(a)<=1,

    |[D(U-U0)D]12|<=2s²/b.

Discarding the nonnegative diagonal therefore gives

    D(U-U0)D >=-(2s²/b)I.                                (13)

Now write DT by columns as

    DT=[w+delta,n],
    w=(sqrt(a)R,L)^T,
    delta=(sqrt(a)sP,-r Delta)^T,
    n=(sqrt(a)(S-sk),S+rP)^T.                            (14)

By the two diagonal consequences of (7), ||w||²<=2r²<=2. The elementary
integral estimate atanh(t)<=t/(1-t²) gives P<=rs/c and S<=s/b.
Using r sqrt(a)<=1/2, (12), and c>=b, we obtain

    ||delta||²<=s^4/(2b²).                               (15)

For the second column, its lower entry is bounded by

    0<=S+rP<=s/b+r²s/c<=2s/b.

The upper entry requires preserving its subtraction. Let N=S-s Delta.
Equation (12) and s<=1/5 give Delta<=1/48<1, and S>=s, so 0<=N<=S.
Furthermore (6), applied to a, gives sqrt(a)k_r<=r²/2<=1/2. Thus

    |sqrt(a)(S-sk)|
      =|sqrt(a)(N-s k_r)|
      <=max(sqrt(a)N,s sqrt(a)k_r)
      <=max(S,s/2)=S<=s/b.

Consequently

    ||n||²<=5s²/b².                                      (16)

This is the key allocation: the first column changes by order s², while
the previously absent second column contributes its outer square, also of
order s². A blanket bound on the whole perturbation of T would lose this
structure and give only an order-s estimate.

From (14)-(16), and the elementary bound
w delta^T+delta w^T<=(2||w||||delta||)I, it follows that

    D(TT^T-T0T0^T)D
      <=[2s²/b+(s^4/2+5s²)/b²]I.                        (17)

Combine (11), (13), and (17):

    D(U-TT^T)D >=[1/2-E(s²)]I,
    E(q)=4q/(1-q)+(5q+q²/2)/(1-q)².                     (18)

E is increasing on [0,1), since each numerator term and each reciprocal
denominator factor is nonnegative and increasing. At q=1/25,

    E(1/25)=443/1152,
    1/2-E(1/25)=133/1152>1/10.

This proves Theorem 1 entirely by exact inequalities. No finite search or
asymptotic sign is being used to certify the strip.
