15 September 2026. **Work in progress; universal theorem not claimed.**

The measured affine direction is always `K+t diag(v)`, with arbitrary real signs. I retain X057's exact Hessian `B=J-Lambda`, where `J` is the full diagonal-parameter Fisher matrix, `J_ii=chi_i`, `J_ij=E F_ij`, and `Lambda_ii=0`, `Lambda_ij=E lambda_ij`. ## Early new allocation identity

Write `C=diag(chi)` and suppose actual conditional tables obey `a <= lambda/F <= b` (zero edges interpreted continuously). For any `0<=c<=1`, let `E=Lambda-c(J-C)`. Then exactly

    B=(1-c)J+c C-E,
    |E_ij| <= max(c-a,b-c) J_ij, E_ii=0.

Because **the full Fisher matrix is positive semidefinite**, a global positive term `(1-c)J` can be retained, instead of bounding all compensated pairs separately in absolute value. If a compatible resolvent bound gives `sum_(j!=i)J_ij <= r chi_i`, then

    B >= (1-c)J + [c-r max(c-a,b-c)] C.

This may work even where the older criterion `r max(1-a,b-1)<1` fails. For `c=(a+b)/2<=1`, the new coefficient is `(a+b-r(b-a))/2`.

Numerical optimization (only exploratory) of determinant-feasible two-site tables suggests that at `||2K-I||<=0.85`, `a≈0.6039297`, `b≈1.3051626`, giving a positive new coefficient `0.04168`; the absolute pair criterion is already negative there.
