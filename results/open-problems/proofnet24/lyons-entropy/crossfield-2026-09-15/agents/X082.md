## Initial exact bridge (retained; the main result starts below)

Fix U and set V=U⊥, a four-dimensional Hermitian space. A unit decomposable
two-form w in Λ²V determines W; its Hodge conjugate determines Z. Hence the
two endpoint costs are quadratic forms in the same six exterior coordinates.
For harmonic q, X018 proves

`q·g(W,U) = −tr(P_W C_q(U))`.

Writing dΓ for one-body exterior second quantization, the full dual slack
therefore has the form

`a·f+h·k+c·l−q·g = <w,H_(U;f,k,l,q) w>`.

The operator H includes both endpoint diagonal costs after exterior
compression, the Hodge conjugate of the top cost, and `dΓ(C_q(U))`.
This is a six-by-six Hermitian matrix. Positivity is required only on
decomposable w, which obey one complex Pluecker quadric; full matrix
positivity is a stronger condition and will not be silently substituted.

In a Hodge-real orthonormal basis, decomposability is `z^T z=0`. Thus every
unit decomposable z equals `(x+i y)/√2` for real orthonormal x,y. If
`H=A+iB`, with A real symmetric and B real skew, its exact quadratic value is

`(x^T A x+y^T A y)/2 − x^T B y`.

This bridge is not used as a positivity assertion. It prompted the endpoint
symmetry reduction below, which gives a more explicit finite transport
geometry. Ordinary matrix positivity does not follow from the dual's
ninety pointwise inequalities, and is not claimed.

For completeness, the exterior real form is elementary: in an orthonormal
basis e₁,...,e₄ of V, use the six vectors
`(e₁₂+e₃₄)/√2`, `i(e₁₂−e₃₄)/√2`,
`(e₁₃−e₂₄)/√2`, `i(e₁₃+e₂₄)/√2`,
`(e₁₄+e₂₃)/√2`, `i(e₁₄−e₂₃)/√2`.
They are fixed by the antilinear Hodge involution and their wedge pairing
is the identity matrix times the volume form. Thus w∧w=0 is zᵀz=0.
A two-form in four dimensions is decomposable exactly when this Pfaffian
vanishes: the associated skew matrix then has rank at most two, and a
rank-two skew form is the wedge of two vectors. Writing z=(x+iy)/√2
proves the stated orthonormality and quadratic identity directly.

## 1. Main result and notation

There is an exact alternative to the five-harmonic-moment formulation:
first transport from the active pair to the swapped middle law with the
known cell caps; then only **nine symmetric endpoint moments** remain.
Every row of the remaining problem is an explicit translated triangle.
Its free mass is exactly twice the distance of the capped row from its
nearest cell boundary. Eliminating these triangles gives a weighted
absolute-value transport cost for the full endpoint dual. An explicit
minimum-norm inverse repairs all nine moments simultaneously, with a sharp
Euclidean-to-cell bound.

These are equivalences and conditional constructive certificates. Universal
existence of the first capped flow, or universal availability of the final
endpoint capacity, is **not proved**. No universal diamond or entropy
concavity theorem follows without those remaining steps.

Write E={0,...,5}, and index a diamond by the ordered pair partition
`(A,J,B)`, with J active. Let x denote its nonnegative weight. Write

`a=μ_W`, `h=μ_U`, `c=μ_Z`, `g=g(W,U)`, `r=g(U,W)`, `s=a+c`.

The name r here is a triple table, not a rank. In particular r has total
mass two. Cauchy–Binet and complementary minors give the pointwise identity

`g_M+r_M = Σ_(B⊂Mᶜ, |B|=2)c_B`.                              (1)

Indeed the left side is the three-row principal minor of P_W+P_U, while
the right side is its rank-four projection inclusion probability.

For pair data h put `h_i=Σ_(J∋i)h_J`. For triple data r put
`r_i=Σ_(M∋i)r_M`, `r_D=Σ_(M⊃D)r_M` for a pair D.

## 2. The capped flow determines the antisymmetric endpoints

Given x, define sixty cells

`y_(J,i)=Σ_(A∋i, A⊂Jᶜ) x_(A,J,Jᶜ\A)`,   i∉J.              (2)

They obey

`Σ_(i∉J)y_(J,i)=2h_J`,  `0≤y_(J,i)≤h_J`,
`Σ_(J⊂M)y_(J,M\J)=r_M`.                                    (3)

The last identity records the two swapped middles J+i with i in A.
Let Y(h,r) be the compact polytope specified by (3). This is exactly the
capped inclusion polytope studied in X020/X060/X064.

**Lemma 1 (forced endpoint difference).** Every lift of any y in Y(h,r)
has, for each pair D={i,j},

`a_D−c_D = r_i+r_j−h_i−h_j−1+h_D−r_D`.                       (4)

Its endpoint singleton sums satisfy `a_i=r_i−2h_i` and
`c_i=1+h_i−r_i`. Thus (4) agrees with the desired determinantal endpoints
whenever h,r come from the flag.

**Proof.** The swapped middle singleton count is `r_i=2h_i+a_i` on each
diamond, and the three colors partition E. For fixed D, put

`d_(J,D)=y_(J,i)+y_(J,j)−h_J` whenever J∩D=∅.

The row contribution to a_D−c_D is d_(J,D): in a random bottom pair in
Jᶜ, the probability of containing both i,j minus the probability of
containing neither equals its two singleton probabilities minus one.
Consequently

`a_D−c_D=Σ_(J∩D=∅)d_(J,D)`.

The pair marginal of r is

`r_D=2h_D+Σ_(k∉D)[y_({i,k},j)+y_({j,k},i)]`.

Subtract these intersecting-pair terms from the two site sums of y and
use `Σ_(J∩D=∅)h_J=1−h_i−h_j+h_D`. This proves (4). The same elementary
identities hold for the desired flag by (1), or by the two-row
Cauchy–Binet expansion, so the differences coincide. ∎

It follows that prescribing **s=a+c alone** supplies both endpoint laws.
Moreover every possible s satisfies

`Σ_(D∋i)s_D=1−h_i`,  i∈E.                                   (5)

The pair-to-site incidence matrix has rank six: if λ_i+λ_j=0 for all
pairs, using any three distinct sites gives λ=0. Thus these affine
constraints leave exactly `15−6=9` endpoint coordinates.

The equality constraints in (3) have rank 34: the pair–triple inclusion
graph is connected, and after changing the sign of one vertex class its
incidence matrix has rank `15+20−1`. Its connectivity follows by changing
one site of a pair at a time through a containing triple. Thus the split
into thirty-four capped-flow equations and nine additional endpoint
coordinates agrees with C038's full rank of forty-three.

## 3. Each capped row leaves a triangle, with an exact capacity

Fix J, abbreviate `H=h_J`, and label its four remaining sites i,j,k,l.
There are three unordered complementary pairings e={A,B} of Jᶜ. Define

`d_(J,A)=Σ_(i∈A)y_(J,i)−H`,   `d_(J,B)=−d_(J,A)`,
`L_(J,e)=|d_(J,A)|`.

For a lift x put `m_(J,e)=x_(A,J,B)+x_(B,J,A)`.

**Theorem 2 (exact local fiber).** The possible lifts of this capped row
are in bijection with

`m_(J,e)≥L_(J,e)`,    `Σ_(e of Jᶜ)m_(J,e)=H`.                 (6)

The inverse map is

`x_(A,J,B)=[m_(J,{A,B})+d_(J,A)]/2`.                          (7)

The free mass of this triangle is exactly

`ρ_J := H−Σ_e L_(J,e)
      =2 min_(i∉J){y_(J,i), H−y_(J,i)} ≥0`.                   (8)

Thus every capped row has a lift, including zero and saturated rows;
an explicit one is `m⁰_(J,e)=L_(J,e)+ρ_J/3`.

**Proof.** Complementarity gives `x_A−x_B=d_A`, proving (6)–(7).
Conversely (6) makes (7) nonnegative and normalized. At site i, the
three pairs containing i contribute

`(H+Σ_(j∈Jᶜ\{i})(y_i+y_j−H))/2=y_i`,

because the row sum is 2H. Thus every cell of (2) is recovered.

To prove (8), order the four cells as b₁≤b₂≤b₃≤b₄. Their sum is 2H.
Represent the three pairings by the pairs containing the smallest cell.
The first two imbalances b₁+b₂−H and b₁+b₃−H are nonpositive. Hence

`Σ_e L_e=b₄−b₁+|b₁+b₄−H|
         =max(H−2b₁,2b₄−H)
         =H−2min(b₁,H−b₄)`.

This proves (8) without division by H and therefore also at H=0. ∎

For fixed y, the possible symmetric endpoint tables are precisely

`s_D=Σ_(J∩D=∅)m_(J,{D,Jᶜ\D})`,                             (9)

with m as in (6). They form a sum of fifteen explicitly translated
triangles in the nine-dimensional affine space (5).

**Full reconstruction.** If y and m satisfy (3), (6), and (9) with the
desired s, then (7) is a full diamond. Lemma 1 recovers a,c individually.
Equation (1) then recovers the original middle law g from r and c.
Thus this construction loses no middle equations and no endpoint costs.

## 4. Exact endpoint-costed absolute-value dual

For a real pair potential v define

`c_(J,e)=v_A+v_B`,  `M_J(v)=max_(e of Jᶜ)c_(J,e)`,
`Δ_(J,e)(v)=M_J(v)−c_(J,e)≥0`.

All of these are explicit costs on the three pairings of four sites.
For fixed y, optimizing m in its triangle gives exactly

`max_m v·s
 =Σ_J[Σ_e L_(J,e)c_(J,e)+ρ_J M_J]
 =Σ_J h_J M_J−Σ_(J,e)Δ_(J,e)|d_(J,A)|`.                     (10)

No relaxation is made: put all free mass ρ_J on a maximizing pairing.

Define the weighted imbalance cost

`L_v(h,r)=min_(y∈Y(h,r))
           Σ_(J,e)Δ_(J,e)(v)|Σ_(i∈A)y_(J,i)−h_J|`.           (11)

The choice of A versus B does not matter. This is a finite convex,
piecewise-linear transport minimization; absolute values may be replaced
by two linear epigraph inequalities for each of the forty-five terms.

**Theorem 3 (complete endpoint dual).** For projection flag data, a full
diamond exists if and only if Y(h,r) is nonempty and

`v·(a+c)+L_v(h,r) ≤ Σ_J h_J M_J(v)`                           (12)

for every pair potential v. It is enough to test the nine-dimensional
space

`K₂={v:Σ_(D∋i)v_D=0 for every i}`.                           (13)

**Proof.** The attainable s in (9), while allowing all y in Y, form a
compact convex polytope: use the equivalent linear x,y,m description
(2), (6) with (7), or express m≥|d| as two inequalities. Its support
function is exactly the maximum of (10) over Y, which is the right side
of (12) minus (11). Finite convex separation proves necessity and
sufficiency. The full reconstruction above then gives the desired
diamond, not merely its symmetric endpoint image.

Adding a modular potential `v_D=λ_i+λ_j` changes every pairing cost at
fixed J by the same `Σ_(i∉J)λ_i`; thus Δ and L_v remain unchanged. By
(5), the two sides of (12) change by the same amount. Every pair table
has a unique orthogonal decomposition into a modular table and (13),
whose dimension is nine. This proves the reduction. ∎

Equation (12) is the missing endpoint cost in an explicit form.
Uncosted capped cuts assert only `Y(h,r)≠∅`; they do not bound (11).
Replacing L_v by zero would discard a nonnegative, potentially decisive
cost and would be invalid. The forty-five comparisons defining the M_J
describe the local triangle support functions; testing those comparisons
alone is not asserted to exhaust the global support inequalities.

This also identifies a broad simplification of the original dual:
when f+l is modular, all of its remaining endpoint dependence is fixed
by h,r through (4)–(5). Arbitrary antisymmetric endpoint potentials are
therefore absorbed by the swapped middle data. The additional endpoint
obstruction resides precisely in the nine nonmodular symmetric costs.

## 5. A closed simultaneous repair of all nine endpoint moments

The preceding reduction provides an explicit certificate without solving
the endpoint support problem afresh. Choose any capped y. Take the central
lift `m⁰=L+ρ/3`, let s⁰ be (9), and put `δ=(a+c)−s⁰`.
Both s and s⁰ satisfy (5), so δ belongs to K₂.

For every pairing e={A,B} of Jᶜ set

`(Rδ)_(J,e)=(3δ_A+3δ_B−δ_J)/20`.                            (14)

**Theorem 4 (endpoint right inverse and capacity).** The correction (14)
has zero row sum at every J and endpoint image δ under (9). Consequently

`m=m⁰+Rδ`, followed by (7), is a full diamond whenever

`ρ_J/3+(3δ_A+3δ_B−δ_J)/20≥0` for all forty-five pairings.      (15)

The right inverse is the unique minimum-Euclidean-norm correction with
these row and endpoint equations. Its exact norm and sharp cell bound are

`||Rδ||₂=√(3/20)||δ||₂`,
`||Rδ||∞≤(√3/10)||δ||₂`.                                    (16)

In particular the sufficient condition

`||δ||₂ ≤ (10/(3√3)) min_J ρ_J`                              (17)

gives an explicit nonnegative coupling. The criterion (15) remains useful
when some ρ_J vanish, whereas (17) then permits only δ=0.

**Proof.** For δ in K₂, total and singleton sums vanish, so

`Σ_(D⊂Jᶜ)δ_D=δ_J`,   `Σ_(D∩A=∅)δ_D=δ_A`.                   (18)

The first follows by excluding the two singleton stars of J, and the
second is the same identity with J=A. Summing (14) over the three
pairings at J therefore gives zero. At a fixed endpoint A, there are
six disjoint J, whose opposite endpoints B also run through the six
pairs disjoint from A. Hence the endpoint image is

`[18δ_A+3δ_A−δ_A]/20=δ_A`.

For the norm assertion, let S be the endpoint map (9), and Q the
orthogonal projection removing the mean of each three-cell row. Then

`(QS*δ)_(J,{A,B})=δ_A+δ_B−δ_J/3`,
`SQS*δ=(20/3)δ` on K₂.

Thus R=(3/20)QS* on K₂. It is orthogonal to every correction with zero
row and endpoint images. Pythagoras proves minimum norm and uniqueness,
and the displayed eigenvalue gives the first formula in (16).

For the sharp cell norm, the orthogonal projector Π onto K₂ has entries

`Π_(A,D)=3/5` if A=D, `−3/20` if |A∩D|=1,
`Π_(A,D)=1/10` if A∩D=∅.                                   (19)

Its columns have zero singleton sums by direct counting, and it acts as
identity on K₂ by (18). For the three mutually disjoint pairs A,B,J,

`||Π(3e_A+3e_B−e_J)/20||²
 =[19(3/5)+2(9−3−3)(1/10)]/400=3/100`.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives the second assertion of (16), and choosing δ
proportional to that projected vector attains it. Applying this bound
to the free mass ρ_J/3 proves (17); (15) itself is exactly the required
lower bound m≥L. ∎

Unlike a numerical feasible-point-and-continuity argument, (14)–(17) give
every correction coefficient, its exact optimal Euclidean norm, and all
forty-five nonnegativity conditions. They still require an actual capped
flow and its measured endpoint capacity. Automatic satisfaction for
arbitrary projection flags is the remaining mathematical gap.
