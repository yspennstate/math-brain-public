## 2. The positive angular determinant lemma

Orient the three edges as `23,31,12`. A direction is **angular at J** when

    Eii=0,   Re(conj(Jij) Eij)=0 for every pair.             (3)

When all edges are nonzero, write `E_e=i theta_e J_e`, with real theta.
This describes the affine velocity `J+tE`; it does not replace that line by
an intrinsic phase curve or discard the latter's different acceleration.

**Lemma.** If `J>0` is Hermitian three by three and (3) holds, then

    D² det(J)[E,E] <= 0.                                   (4)

If every edge of J is nonzero, the inequality is strict for `E!=0`.

**Proof.** Normalize `J=D R D`, `D=diag(sqrt(Jii))`, so `Rii=1`.
Set `x_i=|R_jk|²`, `s=sum x_i`, `Q=R12 R23 R31`, `c=2Re Q`,
`P²=4x_1x_2x_3`, and define

    T=diag(x_i)+(c/2)(11^T-I).

Positive definiteness of R gives `x_i<1`, `s-c<1`; also `c²<=P²`.
All two-by-two principal minors of T satisfy

    x_i x_j-c²/4 >= x_i x_j(1-x_k)>0,

and

    det T=[P²-c²(s-c)]/4>0.                               (5)

Together with its positive diagonal, these identities prove `T>0`.
For clarity, if `s-c>=0`, use `c²(s-c)<=P²(s-c)<P²`; if `s-c<0`
the determinant is immediately positive. Direct differentiation gives

    D²det(J)[E,E]=-2 det(D)² theta^T T theta.               (6)

Here the squared magnitudes have second derivatives `2x_i theta_i²`,
and the loop real part has second derivative `-2c sum_(i<j)theta_i theta_j`.
This proves (4) and strictness. If an edge of J vanishes, approximate that
entry by `-i epsilon Eij`, with the conjugate lower entry, so (3) still holds.
Edges where both entries vanish can be approximated arbitrarily. Strictness
of J is open; pass to the limit in its polynomial second derivative. ∎

An equivalent geometric description is a positive real phase compression.
For `alpha=Re Q/|Q|`, the real correlation matrix with entries
`Rhat_ij=alpha |Rij|` is positive: its determinant is
`1-alpha²(s-c)>0` and its two-site minors are positive. The matrix in (6)
is its diagonal congruence. This auxiliary matrix is used only to certify a
quadratic form; its occupation entropy is never substituted for H(P_K).

## 3. The complete angular entropy theorem

Use unnormalized measured entries `x_i=|K_jk|²`, `a_i=Kii`,
`Q=K12 K23 K31=q+i h`, `Theta=sum theta_i`, and

    T_K=diag(a_i x_i)+q(11^T-I),
    T_0=diag((1-a_i)x_i)-q(11^T-I).                         (7)

Both matrices are positive definite when all edges are nonzero, by (6)
applied respectively to `K` and `I-K`. The full and empty accelerations are
`p''_111=-2 theta^T T_K theta`, `p''_000=-2 theta^T T_0 theta`.
Every pair inclusion acceleration is `-2x_i theta_i²`.

For each complementary pair let `lambda_i,s` be the log odds
`log(p_10s p_01s/(p_00s p_11s))>=0`. Conditioning a strict DPP on its third
bit gives a two-site contraction J, whose four atoms satisfy
`p10 p01-p00 p11=|J12|²>=0`; this proves the sign. Boolean log differences give
`tau=lambda_i,1-lambda_i,0`, independent of i.
Only the triple inclusion has a nonzero first derivative in an angular direction:

    v:=p'_111=-2h Theta,
    p'_S=(-1)^(3-|S|) v,
    FI=4h² (sum_S 1/p_S) Theta².                           (8)

**Theorem.** Every direction (3) at every strict three-site K satisfies
`-H''_K(E)>=FI_K(E)`. For a base with all three edges present and `E!=0`,
the inequality is strict. More explicitly, put `J_p=sum_S 1/p_S`. Then

    tau>=0: -H''=4h² J_p Theta²
                 +2 sum_i lambda_i,0 x_i theta_i²
                 +2tau theta^T T_K theta;
    tau<0:  -H''=4h² J_p Theta²
                 +2 sum_i lambda_i,1 x_i theta_i²
                 +2|tau| theta^T T_0 theta.                (9)

**Proof.** Boolean inversion of all second inclusion moments, followed by
`-H''=FI+sum p''_S log p_S`, gives

    -H''=FI+2 sum_i lambda_i,0 x_i theta_i²-tau p''_111.

Insert (7), or use `p''_000+p''_111=-2 sum x_i theta_i²`, to obtain (9).
Every term is nonnegative by the lemma. Thus the entire `p''log p` term is
retained and certified, even when the Fisher term is nonzero. For strictness,
if tau is nonzero use positive definiteness in (7). If tau is zero, a nonzero
edge cannot have both conditional coherences zero: their formulas are
`Kij-Kik Kkj/Kkk` and `Kij+Kik Kkj/(1-Kkk)`. Hence each lambda_i,0 is positive.
Zero-edge cases follow by the same continuity argument as in the lemma. ∎

Equation (9) is an actual sum of squares: for any positive matrix T in (7),
with diagonal `t_i`, common off-diagonal q, `D2=t1 t2-q²`, `D3=det T`,

    theta^T T theta
      =t1(theta1+q theta2/t1+q theta3/t1)²
       +(D2/t1)(theta2+q(t1-q)theta3/D2)²+(D3/D2)theta3².  (10)

The theorem also places these directions in X029's exact negative-face cone:
all pair, empty, and full accelerations are nonpositive. Its moment argument
therefore proves the stronger frozen statement `D² E_(P_K) f[E,E]<=0` for
every supermodular f. It does not assert concavity along nonlinear phase curves.

## 4. A six-real-variable reduction of the remaining full Hessian

At a base with all edges present, write a general velocity uniquely as `R+E`,
where `Rii=delta_i`, `R_e=u_e K_e` with real u, and `E_e=i theta_e K_e`.
Let `Q_K(A,B)` be the polarization of `-H''`, and set `U=sum u_i`,

    ell(R)=sum_S (-1)^(3-|S|) Dp_S[R]/p_S.

The angular block P is explicit:

    P=diag(2x_i(lambda_i,0+tau a_i)-2tau q)
           +(2tau q+4h²J_p)11^T >0.                       (11)

No positivity of its diagonal summand alone is presumed. All mixed pair
inclusion accelerations vanish, while the mixed triple acceleration is
`-2h(U Theta-u·theta)`. Thus the **full** mixed block is

    Q_K(R,E)=b(R)·theta,
    b(R)=2h[(tau U-ell(R))1-tau u].                       (12)

Consequently the exact completion is

    -H''(R+E)=(theta+P^(-1)b)^T P(theta+P^(-1)b)
                    +Q_K(R,R)-b(R)^T P^(-1)b(R).          (13)

General three-site concavity at this base is equivalent to the remaining
six-real-variable inequality `Q_K(R,R)>=b(R)^T P^(-1)b(R)` for all R.
This eliminates the entire angular block, including its nongauge cycle mode;
X027 eliminated the two gauge modes. The six-variable inequality is unproved.
At a real loop h=0 the mixed block vanishes, recovering the known real/imaginary
splitting. Neither positivity of an optimized direction nor a positive horizontal
projection is assumed. Boundary and missing-edge extensions of (13) require
separate limiting choices; the theorem (9) itself already includes missing edges.
