## 1. Strongest explicit theorem

Each new block meets the union of earlier supports in at most one coordinate. All these later blocks use only coordinates 4 and higher, so they fix the old interface coordinate 3. Let F be the resulting frame.

Assign **arbitrary independent fixed background rates** `lambda_j in [0,1]` to every input column except 2 and 3. They may all equal 1/2, may be unequal, and may be far from every projection corner. Set

    K(M)=F [diag(lambda_0,lambda_1) direct-sum M
             direct-sum diag(lambda_4,...,lambda_(n-1))] F*,
    I2/4 <= M <= 3I2/4.                                      (1)

**Theorem.** On the entire four-real-dimensional convex domain (1),

    M -> H(P_K(M)) is concave.                                (2)

For every definite Hermitian active direction A,

    -H''_M[A,A] >= (7595/12288)(tr A)^2
                  > (3/5)(tr A)^2.                          (3)

For every indefinite or rank-one A,

    -H''_M[A,A] >= FI_M(A) >= 0.                             (4)

The theorem is uniform in the number and strength of all downstream blocks, the number of fractional occupations, and every background rate. It allows noncommuting active endpoints. For definite `Delta=M1-M0`, the Jensen gap is at least

    (7595/24576) t(1-t)(tr Delta)^2.

There is a genuinely interacting connected quadrature chain of any length: after the bridge, for each j use a pair with union `{d,d+1,d+2,d+3}` and unnormalized Householder vectors

starting with d=4 and advancing d by 3. These strong balanced blocks give dimensions `n=5+3k`; their parameters do not become small with k. Every one of the n-2 background rates remains arbitrary.

## 2. A physical one-bit joint law, proved by determinants

Start with any core covariance `Kc(M)=Jc+V M V*`, where V is an isometry onto two modes, `Jc V=0`, and `0<=Jc<=I-VV*`. Let c be one core coordinate, D the other core coordinates, and E a disjoint reservoir with any fixed positive-contraction covariance J_E. Let U act on `{c} union E` and fix D. The final physical covariance is

    Kf(M)=(I_D direct-sum U) [Kc(M) direct-sum J_E]
            (I_D direct-sum U)*.                            (5)

Let `p_M(t,b)` be the core occupation law, with `t=X_D` and `b=X_c`. Define the two physical reservoir output laws

    C_b(y)=P_(U [b direct-sum J_E] U*)(y),     b=0,1.         (6)

Then the final occupation law satisfies the **exact** identity

    Pf_M(t,y)=sum_(b=0,1) p_M(t,b) C_b(y).                  (7)

In particular

    q_M(t,b,y)=p_M(t,b) C_b(y)                              (8)

is a nonnegative normalized joint law with the correct physical marginal. It has the Markov property `T -> B -> Y`. No formal signed mixture or spectral identification with measured bits is used.

Here is an algebraic proof. Write the core block on D and c as

    Kc=[[A,v],[v*,k]],
    R=diag(z_j-1 : j in D),
    L=I+A R,
    kappa(z_D)=k-v* R L^-1 v.

Let `F_b(z_Y)` be the probability generating polynomial of (6). A block determinant Schur complement of the D block gives the full output generating polynomial

    Gf(z_D,z_Y)=det L [F_0(z_Y)
                           +kappa(z_D)(F_1(z_Y)-F_0(z_Y))]. (9)

The dependence on kappa is affine because it changes only one input covariance coordinate: the perturbation has rank one. The same Schur complement for the core alone gives

    Gc(z_D,z_c)=det L [1+kappa(z_D)(z_c-1)].                (10)

Thus the coefficient of `z_D^t` in `det L` is `p_M(t,0)+p_M(t,1)`, and its coefficient in `det L kappa` is `p_M(t,1)`. Extracting that coefficient in (9) proves (7). Invertible L is a generic formal-variable condition; both sides are polynomials, so the identity extends to all variables and singular covariance limits. This uses the standard determinantal generating identity `G_K(z)=det(I+K diag(z-1))`, itself immediate from the principal-minor expansion.

This proof also shows why a one-coordinate interface is special. For two interface coordinates the corresponding Schur-complement determinant is no longer affine in the interface covariance, and the channel entropy term below need not be affine in M.

## 3. Exact conditional mutual information and both curvature corrections

All probabilities in this section are from the actual joint law (8). Its entropy chain rule gives

    H(Pf_M)=H(p_M)+E H(C_B)-H(B|T,Y),                       (11)

or equivalently

    H(Pf_M)=H(T)+E H(C_B)+I(B;Y|T).                         (12)

The possibly very large reservoir term is

    E H(C_B)=(1-s(M))H(C_0)+s(M)H(C_1),
    s(M)=P(B=1)=(Kc(M))_cc.

It is exactly affine in M. Therefore its entire Hessian vanishes, regardless of the reservoir dimension, rates, or internal mixing. The only loss relative to the core curvature is the curvature of one posterior bit.

Along the affine path `M+tA`, on the fixed positive core support put

    s_x=p'_x/p_x,     r_x=p''_x/p_x,
    pi(b|t,y)=P(B=b|T=t,Y=y).

Subtracting the full joint and marginal Shannon Hessians yields

    H(Pf)''=H(p)''+L+R,
    L=E Var(s_(T,B)|T,Y),
    R=E[r_(T,B) log pi(B|T,Y)].                             (13)

Indeed the joint score and relative acceleration are exactly s_x and r_x because C_b is fixed. The Fisher difference is the conditional score variance; the logarithmic difference is `sum q'' log(q/Pf)`, and `q/Pf=pi`. Equation (11) identifies the same correction as

    L+R=-[H(B|T,Y)]''.                                     (14)

Neither L nor R is dropped. R can have either sign, while L is nonnegative. This is an actual conditional mutual-information construction with an explicit complete curvature cost.

## 4. A local error budget independent of reservoir size

On `aI<=M<=(1-a)I`, `0<a<1/2`, X061's conditional-flag atom bounds, averaged over fixed fractional-background labels, give

    |s_x|<=||A||_1/a,
    |r_x|<=2|det A|/a^2.                                   (15)

For clarity, every conditional flag has bottom, middle and top factors

    det(I-M) alpha,    e(M-det(M)I)e*,    det(M) beta.

The first-derivative score bounds are X061's elementary two-by-two estimates. Their second derivatives are `2det A` times `alpha,-||e||^2,beta`; all positive factors are at least a^2 times the corresponding scale. A fixed convex mixture preserves both bounds, since its score and relative acceleration are pointwise convex combinations of the conditional ones. Thus (15) does not require a projection core.

Let `bhat(T,Y)` be any deterministic decoder of B and let its error probability be epsilon. Conditional variance minimizes squared prediction error; predicting the core score by `s_(T,bhat)` gives

    L<=4 epsilon ||A||_1^2/a^2.                             (16)

Define the predictor to be zero if that core atom is impossible. On correct decoding its score error vanishes, and on an error its absolute value is at most `2||A||_1/a`, so impossible decoder atoms cause no problem.

Since each posterior logarithm is nonpositive, (13) and (15) give

    |R|<=2|det A| H(B|T,Y)/a^2
         <=2|det A| h(epsilon)/a^2.                         (17)

The second inequality is the binary error-entropy identity: if `Derr=1{B!=bhat(T,Y)}`, then B is determined by `(Derr,T,Y)` and conversely Derr is determined by `(B,T,Y)`. Hence `H(B|T,Y)=H(Derr|T,Y)<=h(epsilon)`. There is no factor involving the number of reservoir modes.

Suppose the core has the uniform definite-direction margin

    -H(p_M)''[A,A]>=g0 (tr A)^2.                            (18)

For definite A, `||A||_1=|tr A|` and `4det A<=(tr A)^2`. Equations (13), (16), and (17) prove the general bridge estimate

    -H(Pf_M)''[A,A]
       >= [g0-(4epsilon+h(epsilon)/2)/a^2](tr A)^2.          (19)

This is the new quantitative closure statement for definite directions. It holds for arbitrary U and arbitrary fixed J_E whenever its hypotheses hold. All-direction concavity additionally requires an indefinite-direction argument; Section 6 supplies it for the stated X081 trees.

## 5. Observable port retention controls the entire reservoir

Use the final port occupation itself as decoder: `bhat(T,Y)=Y_c`. Write

    rho=1-|U_cc|^2,
    nu=U_(c,E) J_E U_(c,E)*.

Since `0<=J_E<=I`, one has `0<=nu<=rho`. Under channel C_b,

    P(Y_c=1|B=b)=|U_cc|^2 b+nu.

Consequently, with `s=P(B=1)`, the **actual** decoder error is

    epsilon=s rho+(1-2s)nu
            <=rho max(s,1-s)<=rho.                          (20)

If `rho<=1/2`, (19) therefore implies the simple uniform certificate

    -H(Pf_M)''[A,A]
       >= [g0-(4rho+h(rho)/2)/a^2](tr A)^2.                  (21)

If `U=U_later Qbridge` and every later operation fixes the port c, the row `U_(c,.)` equals the corresponding bridge row. Hence rho is determined by the first bridge alone. Nothing accumulates as arbitrary reservoir blocks are subsequently joined. Their internal labels can remain highly uncertain; the proof never attempts to decode them.

## 6. Indefinite directions retain the marginal logarithmic acceleration

For a final frame assembled from X081 blocks, **every** conditional projection flag for occupied background labels has a certified unlabeled diamond. Its bottom and top laws are a_z,b_z, and its fair middle law is m_z. The complete marginal second derivative is

    Pf''=2 det A sum_z w_z (a_z+b_z-2m_z).                   (22)

For the same frozen marginal physical probability table Pf, determinantal log-submodularity says

    log Pf(S)+log Pf(S+i+j)
        <=log Pf(S+i)+log Pf(S+j).

This is the two-by-two positive Schur complement inequality for the odds matrix of a strict kernel; regularization gives the relevant boundary limits. Average it over each conditional diamond, and then over z. The bracket multiplying `2det A` in `sum Pf'' log Pf` is nonpositive. Therefore, when `det A<=0`,

    -H(Pf)''=FI+sum Pf'' log Pf>=FI.                         (23)

This proves (4) with the **marginal** logarithm, not substituted conditional logarithms. It does not assume that an arbitrary channel preserves entropy concavity. Together, a nonnegative coefficient in (21) and these final-tree diamonds yield the full Hessian sign.
