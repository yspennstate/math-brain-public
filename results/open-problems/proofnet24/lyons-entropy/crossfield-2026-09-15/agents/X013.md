## 1. Wick completion from X011, with real Gaussian squares

X011's final Section 3 proves the general positive-majorant identity, with the sharper parameter `kappa=tr E^2>=1`. The dimension-budget version recorded here follows from the same algebra and remains valid without that lower bound on kappa. Its role in this report is to make the ensuing optimization convex; it is not claimed as an advance beyond X011. Let `r=rank A>0`, work on `ran A`, and let `D` have that support and satisfy `D+A>=0`, `D-A>=0`. Such a `D` is positive definite on the active range: it is positive semidefinite as the average of `D+A` and `D-A`; if `Dx=0`, positivity forces `(D+A)x=(D-A)x=0`, hence `Ax=0`, which implies `x=0` on `ran A`. Define

    E=D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2),
    B_S=D^(1/2) Y_S D^(1/2),
    s_S=tr(E B_S),   q_S=tr(E B_S E B_S),
    N_S=tr B_S^2-q_S,
    V_S=tr B_S^2-s_S^2/r.

Here `E=E*`, `-I<=E<=I`, and `B_S=B_S*`. Consequently `s_S,q_S` are real. In a unitary eigenbasis `E=diag(e_i)`,

    N_S=sum_ij (1-e_i e_j)|B_ij(S)|^2 >=0.                 (2)

Cauchy–Schwarz and `tr E^2<=r` give `V_S>=0`. More explicitly, if `theta=tr E^2>0`,

    V_S=||B_S-(s_S/theta) E||_F^2
        +(1/theta-1/r)s_S^2.                             (3)

This makes every complex conjugation explicit. For independent standard complex Gaussian vectors `g,h`, `||C||_F^2=E|g* C h|^2`, so (2)–(3) are ordinary real nonnegative Gaussian square integrals.

Let `f_S` be any modular correction of `-log p_S` with `0<=f_S<=Delta`. As in X011, constants and modular occupation functions pair to zero with `p''`, and hence

    -H''=J_A+E_p[f_S(q_S-s_S^2)],
    E_p q_S=E_p s_S^2=J_A,
    E_p tr B_S^2=J_D,
    E_p N_S=J_D-J_A>=0.                                 (4)

The last inequality is itself a useful order property: the DPP atom Fisher form satisfies `FI(A)<=FI(D)` whenever `D>=A` and `D>=-A`.

**Lemma 1 (X011 majorant square identity, dimension-budget form).** With the above assumptions,

    -H''_K(A)=(1+Delta/r)J_A-Delta J_D
       +E_p[f_S V_S]
       +E_p[(Delta-f_S)((r-1)s_S^2/r+N_S)].              (5)

In particular, the rightmost two terms are real nonnegative Gaussian square integrals. If `(1+Delta/r)J_A>=Delta J_D`, the entire expression is real nonnegative, including its full Fisher part.

**Proof.** The pointwise identity is `q-s^2=V-((r-1)s^2/r+N)`. Multiply by `f`, add and subtract `Delta E[((r-1)s^2/r+N)]`, and use (4). This gives (5). The positivity of all displayed remainders follows from (2)–(3). No bosonic integral has been taken with an indefinite covariance, no Grassmann functional is described as positive, and all normalized atom second derivatives are retained. Choosing `D=|A|` gives `E^2=I`, `N=4||(B)_(+,-)||_F^2`, and recovers exactly X011's identity. The freedom to use a noncommuting or nonsaturated majorant `D` is already covered by X011's final theorem. ## 2. The exact logarithmic localization anomaly

Here is why the natural supersymmetric cancellation cannot discard the signed remainder in (1). Write `r=|z|^2`, `tau=r+bar eta eta`, and use the one-pair convention above. For a differentiable decaying function `F`, direct expansion gives

    integral F(tau) = -integral_0^infinity F'(r) dr = F(0). (6)

Fix `epsilon>0`. There are two different logarithmic observables:

    I_p(epsilon)=integral exp(-p tau) log(r+epsilon)
                =p integral_0^infinity exp(-pr)log(r+epsilon)dr,

    integral exp(-p tau) log(tau+epsilon)=log epsilon.      (7)

Expansion of the nilpotent logarithm and ordinary integration by parts give the exact difference

    I_p(epsilon)-log epsilon
       =integral_0^infinity exp(-pr)/(r+epsilon) dr.         (8)

Thus the ghost correction removed by replacing `log r` with its supersymmetric extension is exactly the entire `p`-dependent contribution. In the limit `epsilon downarrow0`,

    I_p(epsilon) -> -gamma-log p,
    -p[I_p(epsilon)+gamma] -> p log p.                    (9)

Differentiating (9) twice along a smooth positive atom path and summing the atoms gives the full expression `FI+sum p''log p`, because `sum p''=0`. For a finite strict kernel neighborhood, all atom probabilities have a common positive lower bound, so differentiation under the regularized integrals and then the epsilon limit is justified by exponential domination, with an integrable logarithm at zero. For example the first two p derivatives produce only polynomial factors in r times `exp(-p r)log(r+epsilon)`; these have a common integrable bound for `0<epsilon<=1`.

Equations (7)–(8) prove a specific obstruction, not a universal no-go theorem: the naive logarithmic insertion is not supersymmetric, and its supersymmetric replacement has a nonvanishing singular ghost correction. The positive certificate (5) retains that correction.

## 3. The attained convex optimization and its complementarity equation

Define the active-range majorant cost

    beta_K(A)=min { FI_K(D): D=D*, ran D subseteq ran A,
                              D+A>=0, D-A>=0 }.          (10)

**Theorem 2 (optimized majorant cost).** The objective is a positive semidefinite quadratic form, and the feasible set is convex. The minimum in (10) is attained and obeys (12). Every minimizer additionally satisfies `A D^(-1) A=D`, as proved below. It is nonempty because `D=|A|` is feasible. The minimum is attained. Indeed every feasible `D` is positive semidefinite, and the complete atom score `s_D` has mean zero and

    Cov_p(s_D, |S|)=tr D,
    Var_p(|S|)=tr[K(I-K)]>0.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

    FI_K(D)>= (tr D)^2 / tr[K(I-K)].                      (11)

On the positive cone, a trace bound gives a matrix norm bound. A minimizing sublevel set is therefore compact in the closed active-range feasible set, and the objective is continuous. The argument at the start of Section 1 shows any minimizer is positive definite on the active range, so its inverse in that theorem exists.

The order identity in (4) now proves

    FI_K(A)<=beta_K(A)<=FI_K(|A|),

    -H''_K(A)>=(1+Delta/r)FI_K(A)-Delta beta_K(A).         (12)

The second inequality is never weaker than X011's spectral-majorant bound. It is a convex quadratic optimization with two semidefinite constraints, rather than a nonconvex optimization over signed spectral frames. No external convex duality or existence theorem is needed beyond the compactness argument above.

A simple explicit subfamily gives strict improvements without solving (10). If `A=uu*-vv*` with orthonormal `u,v`, put `P=uu*+vv*`, `X=uv*+vu*`, and

    D(t)=cosh(t) P+sinh(t) X.

Then `D(t)+A>=0` and `D(t)-A>=0`, both of rank one on the active plane. If `a=FI(P)`, `b=FI(P,X)`, `c=FI(X)`, the Fisher cost is

    FI(D(t))=(a-c)/2 +(a+c)cosh(2t)/2+b sinh(2t).

When `a+c>2|b|`, its minimum is

    [a-c+sqrt((a+c)^2-4b^2)]/2,                           (13)

attained at `tanh(2t)=-2b/(a+c)`. If `b!=0`, this is strictly below `FI(|A|)=a`. Strictness follows directly from `sqrt((a+c)^2-4b^2)<a+c`. The Gram inequality `b^2<=ac` verifies the square root is real. This two-direction calculation is illustrative; (10) applies to arbitrary active rank.

### 3.1. Every optimizer has exact complementarity

The assertion just added to Theorem 2 has a short algebraic proof. For arbitrary Hermitian directions `U,V`, normalization of the polarized second determinant derivative gives

    FI_K(U,V)=E_p tr(Y_S U Y_S V).                       (14)

In particular the Fisher superoperator has the completely positive form

    Phi_K(U)=E_p[Y_S U Y_S],  FI_K(U,V)=tr[Phi_K(U)V].     (15)

The matrices `sqrt(p_S)Y_S` are Hermitian Kraus operators; they need not be positive. If `U,V>=0`, (14) becomes the positive square

    FI_K(U,V)=E_p ||U^(1/2)Y_S V^(1/2)||_F^2>=0.         (16)

Consequently the Fisher quadratic form is strictly increasing under nonzero positive-semidefinite increments on the positive cone: if `D=W+Z` with `W,Z>=0` and `Z!=0`, then

    FI(D)=FI(W)+2FI(W,Z)+FI(Z)>FI(W),

where the strict final inequality follows from (11) applied to `Z`.

Now let `D` minimize (10), and diagonalize `E=D^(-1/2) A D^(-1/2)`. Suppose an eigenvector `v` has eigenvalue `e` with `|e|<1`. For any `0<epsilon<=1-|e|`,

    D'=D^(1/2)(I-epsilon vv*)D^(1/2)

still satisfies `D'+A>=0`, `D'-A>=0`: in the E eigenbasis these inequalities are simply `I-epsilon vv* +/- E>=0`. But `D-D'>=0` is nonzero, so (16) makes `FI(D')<FI(D)`, a contradiction. Therefore every eigenvalue of E is ±1. Equivalently,

    E^2=I_r,  A D^(-1)A=D.                              (17)

Thus every convex optimizer lies on the saturated majorant surface, even though it was not necessary to impose that nonconvex equation. In particular `kappa=r` there, so X011's sharper parameter agrees with the dimension-budget parameter at any minimizer of this specific convex objective. This does not assert that minimizing (10) maximizes every possible kappa-dependent certificate over nonsaturated majorants.

Writing `P=(D+A)/2`, `N=(D-A)/2`, the complementarity is

    P D^(-1)N=0,

and the optimized cost has the equivalent positive pairing form

    beta_K(A)=FI_K(A)
       +4 min_{P,N>=0, P-N=A, supports in ran A} E_p||P^(1/2)Y_S N^(1/2)||_F^2. (18)

The cross pairing is generally positive; complementarity is in the D metric, not in the Y-weighted Fisher metric. Equations (15)–(18) give a concrete nonlinear matrix condition for the best positive decomposition of the signed tangent. They do not claim that the spectral parts `A_+,A_-` minimize it.

A useful necessary stationarity equation also follows. At an optimizer let `C=D^(1/2)Phi_K(D)D^(1/2)`. Every Hermitian off-block `Z` with `EZ+ZE=0` gives a feasible saturated curve `D(t)=D^(1/2)exp(tZ)D^(1/2)`: indeed `exp(tZ) E exp(tZ)=E`. Differentiation of the Fisher objective at its minimum implies `tr(CZ)=0` for every such Z, hence

    [C,E]=0, equivalently D Phi_K(D) A=A Phi_K(D) D.      (19)

The convex definition (10) remains the operative construction; (17) and (19) are necessary matrix equations, not a claim that any solution is globally minimizing.

## 4. An actual nonnegative bosonic integral on the certified domain

Suppose `J_A>0`, choose any feasible `D`, and assume

    lambda:=1+Delta/r-Delta J_D/J_A>=0.

Let `E=diag(e_i)` in a fixed unitary eigenbasis, `theta=tr E^2`, and define, for each atom,

    C_S=B_S-(s_S/theta)E,
    T_S,ij=sqrt(1-e_i e_j) B_S,ij.

The square root in the second definition is the nonnegative real square root, since each `e_i` lies in `[-1,1]`. Let `g,h` be independent standard complex Gaussian vectors in `C^r`, independent of the atom. Then the complete curvature has the exact representation

    -H''_K(A)=E_(S,g,h) {
        lambda s_S^2
        +f_S [ |g* C_S h|^2 +(1/theta-1/r)s_S^2 ]
        +(Delta-f_S)[ (r-1)s_S^2/r +|g* T_S h|^2 ] }.     (20)

Every summand in this ordinary integral is real and nonnegative. In particular, no contour rotation of an indefinite atom matrix is hidden inside the claim of positivity. To prove (20), use the independent Gaussian second moments to check

    E_(g,h)|g* M h|^2=sum_ij |M_ij|^2.

Equations (2), (3), and (5) then give (20). Choosing the attained minimizer in (10) gives the best certificate of the fixed-r form. At that minimizer `theta=r` by (17), so the additional scalar term in the first square bracket vanishes. If `Delta=0`, the general formula already reduces to `-H''=J_A`, including Fisher-null directions.

This is a real positive integral representation of the determinant/Fisher combination on an explicitly certified family. The role of the Grassmann calculation is to retain the connected determinant insertion and its normalization identity; the final squares use genuine positive Gaussian measures.

## 6. The bounded scope and the precise remaining sign

    -H''=2 log(559/459)>0.

       >=Delta beta_K(A)-(1+Delta/r)FI_K(A).              (23)
