# X003 — Fermionic beam splitters and measured entropy

Date: 2026-09-15. Assignment: derive an occupation-entropy inequality from fermionic mixing, retaining dephasing. Terminal report: established results are separated from open inequalities.

**Main proved result.** Every rank-one projection endpoint satisfies the midpoint Lyons inequality against every Hermitian contraction, with an explicit nonnegative gap. The key is a full measured-entropy theorem whenever one input register contains exactly one particle. Its proof appears in Section 4 and uses an elementary fourth-moment bound on a signed linear sum. Sections 5–7 give sharper special formulas and a nearby-endpoint extension. Full unrestricted concavity remains open here.

## 1. Exact two-output representation

Write `rho_K` for the gauge-invariant quasifree state with covariance `K`, and `Delta` for occupation-basis dephasing. Then

\[
H(K):=H(P_K)=S(\Delta\rho_K),\qquad
C(K):=S(\Delta\rho_K)-S(\rho_K).
\]

The one-body beam splitter with transmissivity `t` sends `K0 direct-sum K1` to

\[
B_t=\begin{pmatrix}
(1-t)K_0+tK_1&\sqrt{t(1-t)}(K_1-K_0)\\
\sqrt{t(1-t)}(K_1-K_0)&tK_0+(1-t)K_1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let `Q_t=P_{B_t}` be the full measured output law, and let `I_cl(t)` be the mutual information between its two registers. Its marginals are the desired two affine-covariance DPPs. Therefore

\[
H(K_t)+H(K_{1-t})-H(K_0)-H(K_1)
=H(Q_t)-H(K_0)-H(K_1)+I_{\rm cl}(t).
\tag{1}
\]

There is no approximation and no quantum/classical entropy identification in (1). Since the full beam splitter is unitary,

\[
H(Q_t)-H(K_0)-H(K_1)
=C(\rho_{B_t})-C(\rho_{K_0})-C(\rho_{K_1}),
\tag{2}
\]

where the first `C` uses full `2n`-mode occupation dephasing. A universal nonnegative right side in (2) would imply midpoint Lyons concavity, hence all affine parameters by continuity. This is a stronger candidate and is not presently proved.

The familiar quantum output entropy inequality controls

\[
I_{\rm q}(t)=S(\rho_{K_t})+S(\rho_{K_{1-t}})-S(\rho_{K_0})-S(\rho_{K_1})\ge0.
\]

It leaves the exact dephasing correction

\[
H(K_t)+H(K_{1-t})-H(K_0)-H(K_1)
=I_{\rm q}(t)+C(K_t)+C(K_{1-t})-C(K_0)-C(K_1).
\tag{3}
\]

No sign for the last four terms is assumed.

## 2. The measurement coupling first appears at fourth order

Fix `0<K<I` and Hermitian `A`. Take inputs `K-epsilon A` and `K+epsilon A` and a balanced splitter. Its output covariance is

\[
B_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}K&\varepsilon A\\\varepsilon A&K\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For `S subset [n]`, put `P_{S^c}=diag(1_{i notin S})`,

\[
G_S=(K-P_{S^c})^{-1},\qquad p_S=P_K(S).
\]

These inverses exist for strict contractions because the exact-atom column determinant is strictly positive and differs from `det(K-P_{S^c})` only by a sign. In particular each `G_S` is Hermitian. The block determinant identity gives

\[
Q_\varepsilon(S,T)
=p_Sp_T\det\bigl(I-\varepsilon^2G_S A G_T A\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently, if `r_{ST}=-tr(G_S A G_T A)`,

\[
Q_\varepsilon(S,T)=p_Sp_T(1+\varepsilon^2r_{ST}+O(\varepsilon^4)).
\]

The fixed marginals imply `sum_T p_T r_ST=0`, also following from the score identity `sum_T p_T G_T=0`. Expanding relative entropy against its **exact** product marginals yields

\[
I_{\rm cl}(\varepsilon)
=\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2}\sum_{S,T}p_Sp_T
\bigl[\operatorname{tr}(G_S A G_T A)\bigr]^2+O(\varepsilon^6).
\tag{5}
\]

The squared quantity is real since `A G_T A` and `G_S` are Hermitian. Hence

\[
H(Q_\varepsilon)=2H(K)
-\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2}\sum_{S,T}p_Sp_T
\bigl[\operatorname{tr}(G_S A G_T A)\bigr]^2+O(\varepsilon^6).
\tag{6}
\]

This shows precisely why measuring the full output loses essentially no second-order information across the two output registers: its correlation entropy starts at order four, although the quantum output mutual information starts at order two in general. In particular

\[
H(Q_\varepsilon)-H(K-\varepsilon A)-H(K+\varepsilon A)
=-\varepsilon^2H''(K;A)+O(\varepsilon^4).
\tag{7}
\]

Thus the proposed full-output coherence inequality is locally equivalent to Lyons. Formula (5) is a concrete correction, not an argument discarding the correction.

## 3. Primary-source checks and exact scope

The covariance and even-state beam-splitter conventions are stated explicitly in Xingjian Lyu and Kaifeng Bu, *Fermionic Gaussian Testing and Non-Gaussian Measures via Convolution*, Sections I–II and Appendix C, https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08180v2. Gauge-invariant quasifree inputs are even, so that CAR convention applies. Proposition C.6 concerns von Neumann entropy. We use only the balanced output equality, which follows directly from covariance here: at a general angle the two Gaussian output covariances differ, despite the angle notation in the printed Appendix C statement. No measured-entropy theorem is imported.

N. J. B. Aza and D. A. ## 4. Main theorem: a one-particle input and an arbitrary reservoir

**Theorem A.** Let `P=uu*` be any rank-one projection and let `0<=K<=I` be any Hermitian contraction. Then

\[
\boxed{H\!\left(\frac{P+K}{2}\right)
\ge\frac{H(P)+H(K)}2+\frac12\Gamma(P,K),}
\tag{A1}
\]

where, writing `a_i=|u_i|^2`, `a(T)=sum_{i in T}a_i`, and `r=|T|`,

\[
\Gamma(P,K)=\sum_T P_K(T)\left[
a(T)(r-1)\log2+(1-a(T))\log\frac{2^{r+1}}{3r+1}
\right]\ge0.
\tag{A2}
\]

The apparent negative factor `r-1` at `r=0` is multiplied by `a(empty)=0`. Each remaining summand is nonnegative. A simpler lower bound is

\[
\Gamma(P,K)\ge P_K(\varnothing)\log2
+P_K(|T|\ge2)\log(8/7).
\tag{A3}
\]

In particular, the midpoint inequality is strict whenever the Gaussian reservoir does not have exactly one particle almost surely. All strict contractions satisfy this condition.

### The underlying measured quantum theorem

Consider any joint `2n`-mode state supported on the subspace `N_A=1` in its first register. Independence and Gaussianity are unnecessary for this intermediate statement. A balanced beam splitter between corresponding modes does not decrease its full occupation-measurement entropy.

Let `N_i=n_{A,i}+n_{B,i}`. The splitter commutes with all `N_i`, so dephasing in their joint sectors leaves both input and output occupation laws unchanged. In a sector, let `d` be the number of sites with `N_i=2`, and let `m` be the number with `N_i=1`. Since the first register has exactly one particle, only `d=0` or `d=1` can occur.

* If `d=1`, the first-register particle is at that doubled site. Every singly occupied pair initially has its particle in the second register. The input orientation law is deterministic; the balanced splitter makes all `m` orientations uniform and independent. Its conditional entropy increases by `m log2`.

* If `d=0`, the first-register particle is somewhere among the `m>=1` singly occupied pairs. The conditional density matrix is an arbitrary positive `m by m` matrix `R`, of trace one, on the single-excitation subspace. Its input occupation entropy is at most `log m`. In interleaved Fock order the local balanced rotations are Hadamard rotations up to diagonal signs. Their output orientation law is

  \[
  q(\varepsilon)=2^{-m}\varepsilon^T R\varepsilon,
  \qquad\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^m,
  \tag{A4}
  \]

  after an immaterial diagonal-sign conjugation of `R` if needed.

Here is a direct entropy bound for (A4), including mixed `R`. Put `F(epsilon)=epsilon^T R epsilon>=0`. Since `tr R=1`, `EF=1`. Expanding the fourth-degree sign moment gives

\[
\mathbb EF^2=1+4\sum_{i<j}(\operatorname{Re}R_{ij})^2
\le1+4\sum_{i<j}R_{ii}R_{jj}
=3-2\sum_iR_{ii}^2\le3-2/m.
\tag{A5}
\]

The first inequality uses the `2 by 2` principal-minor positivity `|R_ij|^2<=R_ii R_jj`. Jensen's inequality for `log`, using probability weights `2^{-m}F(epsilon)`, yields

\[
\mathbb E[F\log F]\le\log\mathbb EF^2\le\log(3-2/m).
\]

Therefore

\[
H(q)=m\log2-\mathbb E[F\log F]
\ge m\log2-\log(3-2/m)
\ge\log m.
\tag{A6}
\]

The last inequality is precisely `2^m>=3m-2`, valid for every integer `m>=1` (check `m=1,2` and induct from `m=2`). Thus the conditional output entropy exceeds the conditional input entropy by at least `log(2^m/(3m-2))`. The preserved sector probabilities and the entropy chain rule prove the measured quantum theorem. This proof has retained the measured entropy throughout; no von Neumann replacement is involved.

### Deduction of Theorem A and its quantitative gap

Apply the measured quantum theorem to the independent Gaussian inputs `rho_P tensor rho_K`. Their input occupation entropy is `H(P)+H(K)`. Their two balanced output marginals are both `rho_{(P+K)/2}`, hence classical entropy subadditivity bounds the full output entropy by `2H((P+K)/2)`.

For the quantitative sector sum, an input measured configuration consists of the first-register particle location `i` and second-register subset `T`. If `i in T`, the sector has `d=1` and `m=r-1`. If `i notin T`, it has `d=0` and `m=r+1`. Its sector lower bound is constant over all input configurations that lead to that sector. Averaging the preceding conditional entropy gains against `a_i P_K(T)` proves (A2), and hence (A1).

For `r=0` the gain is `log2`. For `r=1` the lower bound is zero. For every `r>=2`, both `(r-1)log2` and `log(2^{r+1}/(3r+1))` are at least `log(8/7)`. This proves (A3). A Gaussian state has exactly one particle almost surely precisely when its covariance is a rank-one projection, because its particle count is a sum of independent Bernoulli eigenmode occupations. Alternatively, only the displayed probability bound is needed for the strictness statement.

This proves an arbitrary-reservoir midpoint theorem. It does not by itself justify iterating arbitrary midpoint endpoints: after one mixing step the first covariance generally ceases to be a rank-one projection. Thus full concavity has not been deduced.

## 5. A sharper rank-one/corank-one beam-splitter inequality

**Theorem.** Let `u,v` be any unit vectors in `C^n`, let `a_i=|u_i|^2`, `b_i=|v_i|^2`, and let

\[
K_0=uu^*,\qquad K_1=I-vv^*,\qquad C=(K_0+K_1)/2.
\]

For `n>=3`,

\[
\boxed{H(P_C)\ge\frac{H(a)+H(b)}2
+\frac12\left((n-2)\log2-\frac{n}{4e}\right).}
\tag{8}
\]

The constant in parentheses is strictly positive for every `n>=3`. The midpoint Lyons inequality also holds for `n=2`, by the stronger majorization statement below. No relation between `u` and `v`, their coordinate phases, or their supports is imposed.

This concerns coherent projection endpoints of opposite ranks in arbitrary dimension. When `u=v`, the midpoint is diagonal and its ordinary Jensen inequality is immediate; the statement here includes arbitrary `u,v` and supplies a stronger full-output entropy inequality. It is a midpoint theorem for these endpoint pairs, not concavity on their entire segments.

### Exact signed-sum formula

Put

\[
c_i=a_i b_i,\quad T=\sum_i c_i,\quad
Z(\varepsilon)=\sum_i\varepsilon_i u_i\overline{v_i},
\]

where `epsilon_i` are independent uniform signs. Let `Q` be the full balanced-splitter output occupation law of the two pure inputs. Then the following identity holds exactly:

\[
\boxed{H(Q)-H(a)-H(b)
=(n-2)\log2+\sum_i c_i\log(4c_i)
-\mathbb E\bigl[|Z|^2\log|Z|^2\bigr].}
\tag{9}
\]

As usual, `0 log 0=0`. This is the substantive new beam-splitter formula in this report; the general local quartic formula in Section 2 is a specialization of the prior `ROOT_BLOCK_QUARTIC.md` identity and is not claimed as a new frontier result.

### Proof of (9)

The first input has exactly one particle, with occupation location `i` of probability `a_i`. The second has exactly one hole, with location `j` of probability `b_j`. In the full quantum state the corresponding complex amplitudes are `u_i` and `conjugate(v_j)`, with the standard hole sign.

The beam splitter preserves each local total `N_k=n_{A,k}+n_{B,k}`. There are two types of mutually disjoint sectors.

* If `i != j`, then `N_i=2`, `N_j=0`, and every other `N_k=1`. This sector has probability `a_i b_j`. Within it, all `n-2` singly occupied mode pairs initially put their particle in the second register. The balanced splitter makes their output orientations uniform and independent. This sector's contribution to output entropy is therefore

  \[
  -a_i b_j\log(a_i b_j)+(n-2)a_i b_j\log2.
  \]

* If `i=j`, every site has `N_k=1`. These alternatives interfere coherently. In interleaved mode order `(a_1,b_1,...,a_n,b_n)`, the sign from the hole at `i` cancels the sign from moving `a_i` past the preceding occupied `b` modes. Thus the initial sector amplitude is `sum_i u_i conjugate(v_i)|i>`, where `|i>` has its sole first-register particle at `i`. A balanced local rotation sends an output orientation `epsilon in {−1,1}^n` to amplitude, up to an irrelevant common sign,

  \[
  2^{-n/2}\sum_i\varepsilon_i u_i\overline{v_i}.
  \]

  Hence its **unconditional** output probability is `2^{-n}|Z(epsilon)|^2`. The sector mass is `E|Z|^2=T`. Its entropy contribution is

  \[
  nT\log2-\mathbb E[|Z|^2\log|Z|^2].
  \]

The input measured entropy is `H(a)+H(b)=-sum_{i,j}a_i b_j log(a_i b_j)`. Subtract this from the two output contributions. The `i != j` terms cancel and the remaining coefficient of `log2` is `(n-2)(1-T)+nT=n-2+2T`. This is exactly (9).

### Entropy lower bound

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
|Z|\le\sum_i|u_i||v_i|\le1.
\]

Thus the final expectation in (9) is nonpositive. The elementary scalar function `x log(4x)` has minimum `−1/(4e)` on `[0,infinity)`, attained at `x=1/(4e)`. Consequently

\[
H(Q)-H(a)-H(b)
\ge (n-2)\log2-\frac n{4e}>0\quad(n\ge3).
\tag{10}
\]

Both output register marginals are `P_C`. Classical entropy subadditivity, `H(Q)<=2H(P_C)`, proves (8). This proof is entirely finite-dimensional and uses no imported specialized quantum entropy inequality.

### The n=2 case, and a separate pure-rank-one theorem

For any `n`, take instead two rank-one projection inputs `uu*` and `vv*`. For every unordered `i<j`, set `x=u_i v_j`, `y=u_j v_i`. The two input occupation atoms in this sector have probabilities `a=|x|^2`, `b=|y|^2`. The four output atoms have probabilities

\[
\left(\frac{|x-y|^2}4,\frac{|x-y|^2}4,
\frac{|x+y|^2}4,\frac{|x+y|^2}4\right).
\tag{11}
\]

This vector is majorized by `(a,b,0,0)`. Its largest entry is at most `max(a,b)` by `(|x|+|y|)^2/4<=max(a,b)`; its two largest entries sum to at most `a+b` by `|x±y|^2<=2(a+b)`; all other majorization conditions follow from total mass equality. The atoms with `i=j` remain unchanged. Direct-sum majorization therefore proves that the **whole measured output probability vector** is majorized by the input product vector. In particular, its Shannon entropy cannot decrease. For `n=2`, a corank-one projection is rank one, completing the omitted case of (8) without its positive constant.

The ordinary rank-two-active-support Jensen result was already in the starting frontier. Equation (11) is included as a stronger full-output majorization statement, not as a claim of new ordinary rank-two concavity.

## 6. Explicit extension to nearby full-rank endpoint pairs

For `n>=3` define

\[
\eta_n=\frac12\left((n-2)\log2-\frac n{4e}\right),\qquad
d_n=\left(\frac{\eta_n}{4(2+n\log2)}\right)^2,
\qquad r_n=\frac{d_n}{n2^{n-1}}.
\tag{12}
\]

If Hermitian contractions `L0,L1` satisfy

\[
\|L_0-uu^*\|_{\rm op}\le r_n,\qquad
\|L_1-(I-vv^*)\|_{\rm op}\le r_n,
\]

then their midpoint Jensen gap is at least `eta_n/2>0`.

Here is a direct continuity estimate, included to make the extension explicit. Each exact-atom matrix consists of `n` columns of Euclidean norm at most one. Telescoping its determinant one column at a time and applying Hadamard's inequality gives

\[
|P_K(S)-P_L(S)|\le n\|K-L\|_{\rm op},
\qquad
\|P_K-P_L\|_{\rm TV}\le n2^{n-1}\|K-L\|_{\rm op}.
\]

For distributions on `D=2^n` atoms with total variation distance `d`, a maximal coupling and the entropy chain rule give `|H(p)-H(q)|<=h(d)+d log(D-1)`. For our `d<=d_n<1/2`, use `h(d)<=2 sqrt(d)` to obtain

\[
|H(p)-H(q)|\le(2+n\log2)\sqrt{d_n}=\eta_n/4.
\]

The midpoint covariance also moves by at most `r_n`. Its entropy error plus the weighted endpoint entropy errors is at most `eta_n/2`, while (8) has gap at least `eta_n`. This proves the claimed uniform neighborhood. The result includes strict, noncommuting endpoint pairs; it is a Jensen statement, not a full-Hessian assertion at those kernels.

## 7. A positive two-term atom formula on the entire special segment

For `K_t=(1-t)uu*+t(I-vv*)`, `0<t<1`, put

\[
\mu_t(S)=t^{|S|}(1-t)^{n-|S|},\quad
A_S=\sum_{i\in S}a_i,\quad B_S=\sum_{i\in S}b_i,\quad
W_S=\sum_{i\in S}u_i\overline{v_i},\quad W=\langle v,u\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{P_{K_t}(S)=\frac{\mu_t(S)}{t(1-t)}
\left[A_S(1-B_S)+|W_S-tW|^2\right].}
\tag{13}
\]

To verify this, start at the diagonal kernel `tI`, whose signed atom inverse is the diagonal matrix with entries `1/t` on `S` and `−1/(1-t)` outside. Apply the two-column determinant lemma to `(1-t)uu*−t vv*`. Its determinant is

\[
1+\frac{A_S-t}{t}-\frac{B_S-t}{1-t}
-\frac{(A_S-t)(B_S-t)-|W_S-tW|^2}{t(1-t)},
\]

whose numerator simplifies to (13). Both terms in (13) are nonnegative. The first term is the output generated by distinct input particle/hole locations; the second retains all coherent equal-location alternatives.

The full-output entropy gain also has an exact biased-sign form. Let `X_i` be independent `Bernoulli(t)` variables and set

\[
Y_t=\sum_i u_i\overline{v_i}\frac{X_i-t}{\sqrt{t(1-t)}}.
\]

Then

\[
H(Q_t)-H(a)-H(b)
=(n-2)h(t)+\sum_i c_i\log\frac{c_i}{t(1-t)}
-\mathbb E[|Y_t|^2\log|Y_t|^2].
\tag{14}
\]

For the all-single sector, its output law is `mu_t(S)|Y_t(S)|^2`, and
`E|Y_t|^2=T`, `E[|Y_t|^2 sum_i X_i]=[1+(n-2)t]T`. Inserting those moments into its cross-entropy with `mu_t` proves (14); the other sectors contribute `(n-2)(1-T)h(t)`. At `t=1/2` this reduces to (9). The bound `|Z|<=1` used in the proved theorem does not extend to `Y_t`, so (14) is a reduction, not a completed weighted Jensen proof.

## 8. Two stronger shortcuts fail, already with two modes

These examples delimit possible extensions of the theorem.

**Universal output majorization fails.** Let

\[
K_0=\begin{pmatrix}7/10&-3/20\\-3/20&87/100\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}21/50&19/100\\19/100&43/50\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both are strict contractions, as is checked from their diagonal entries and the positive determinants
`det K0=1173/2000`, `det(I-K0)=33/2000`, `det K1=3251/10000`, `det(I-K1)=451/10000`. Their atom laws in order `(empty,{1},{2},{1,2})` are

\[
p_0=(33,227,567,1173)/2000,\qquad
p_1=(451,949,5349,3251)/10000.
\]

The sum of the eight largest atoms of the balanced full-output law is `928973/1000000`, exceeding the corresponding input-product sum `928373/1000000` by exactly `3/5000`. Thus the pure-rank-one majorization theorem does not extend to arbitrary mixed Gaussian inputs. Their full Shannon output entropy nevertheless increases, numerically by about `0.0808709607`; this example does not refute the open Shannon inequality.

**Monotonicity inside each local-total sector fails.** Take

\[
K_0=\frac25\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_1=I-K_0.
\]

The input atom laws are `(1,2,2,0)/5` and `(0,2,2,1)/5`. In the sector with `N_1=N_2=1`, the four **unconditional** input probabilities are `(1,4,4,0)/25`. The corresponding output probabilities are `(17,17,1,1)/100`. Both sums equal `9/25`, but the entropy contribution in this sector decreases by

\[
-\frac{34}{100}\log\frac{17}{100}
-\frac2{100}\log\frac1{100}
+\frac1{25}\log\frac1{25}
+\frac8{25}\log\frac4{25}<0.
\tag{15}
\]

In fact (15) simplifies exactly to `(17/50) log(16/17)<0`. The positive entropy gains of other local-total sectors cannot be discarded in a general proof.

### Higher-rank synthesis obstruction, realized by Gaussian inputs

Root pointed out that the uniform weight-two Dicke state on four bits loses entropy under a Hadamard rotation. The suggested Gaussian realization can be made explicit. Let `omega=exp(2 pi i/3)` and

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}
1/\sqrt2&0\\
1/\sqrt6&1/\sqrt3\\
1/\sqrt6&\omega/\sqrt3\\
1/\sqrt6&\omega^2/\sqrt3
\end{pmatrix},\qquad P=UU^*.
\tag{16}
\]

The columns of `U` are orthonormal, and every two-row minor has squared magnitude `1/6`: this is immediate for a pair containing the first row, and otherwise uses `|omega^i-omega^j|^2=3`. Take Gaussian projection inputs `P` and `I-P`. In the sector `N_i=1` for all four sites, the input amplitudes, in interleaved order, are `|det U_S|^2=1/6` for all two-element `S`, up to a common phase. Indeed, complementary-minor duality contributes the hole sign, and moving the first-register creation operators into interleaved order cancels precisely that sign. The sector mass is `6(1/6)^2=1/6`, and its normalized state is the uniform weight-two Dicke state.

The balanced output in this sector has unconditional probabilities `1/16` at the empty/full orientations and `1/144` at each of the six weight-two orientations. Normalizing gives the probabilities stated by root: `3/8` twice and `1/24` six times. Hence the conditional entropy change is exactly

\[
\left(3\log2-\tfrac12\log3\right)-\log6
=\log\frac4{3\sqrt3}<0.
\tag{17}
\]

This is an actual pair of pure Gaussian inputs, not merely an arbitrary-sector obstruction. Thus the proof of Theorem A cannot be extended to higher fixed first-register particle counts by assuming monotonicity sector by sector. A higher-rank synthesis must recover compensation between sectors. The full eight-mode determinant check is in `computations/X003/dicke_sector.py`.
