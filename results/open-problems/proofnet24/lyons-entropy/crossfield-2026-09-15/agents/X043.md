# X043 — A nonmidpoint measured beam-splitter interval

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: terminal; theorem proved and finite identities verified.

**Main result.** X003's one-particle measured beam-splitter theorem extends from the balanced splitter to every transmissivity satisfying

    t(1-t) >= 1/12,
    (1-sqrt(2/3))/2 <= t <= (1+sqrt(2/3))/2.

Thus the interval is approximately `[0.091752,0.908248]`. The input reservoir is arbitrary, and even independence and Gaussianity are unnecessary for the underlying measured theorem. For DPP kernels this proves a symmetric two-output inequality against every rank-one projection, with a strictly positive gap unless the reservoir has exactly one particle. It does not prove the individual affine Jensen inequalities away from the midpoint, or unrestricted Lyons concavity.

## 1. Finite measured theorem

Write `h(t)=-t log t-(1-t)log(1-t)`, `v=t(1-t)`, and

    delta_t = h(t)-log(1+3v).

Let `rho` be an arbitrary density matrix on two n-mode fermionic registers, supported on `N_A=1`. Let `Q_in,Q_out` be its full occupation-measurement laws before and after the sitewise beam splitter

    |B> -> sqrt(1-t)|B> + sqrt(t)|A>,
    |A> -> sqrt(1-t)|A> - sqrt(t)|B>.

Empty and doubly occupied local pairs are fixed. If `v>=1/12`, then

\[
\boxed{H(Q_{\rm out})-H(Q_{\rm in})
\ge \Pr(N_B=0)h(t)+\delta_t\,\mathbb E[(N_B-1)_+]\ge0.}
\tag{1}
\]

Throughout the displayed interval,

\[
\delta_t\ge\delta_*:=\tfrac13\log2-\log(5/4)
=\tfrac13\log(128/125)>0.
\tag{2}
\]

All entropies in this report are fixed-basis classical measurement entropies. No spectral or von Neumann entropy bound is used.

## 2. The biased one-excitation identity

The splitter commutes with each local total `N_i=n_Ai+n_Bi`. Dephasing in these totals preserves both measured laws. In a fixed sector let `d` count doubled sites and `m` count singly occupied sites. Since `N_A=1`, either `d=1` or `d=0`.

If `d=1`, all m single sites initially point to B. The conditional input orientation is deterministic; the output is a product of m Bernoulli(t) bits and gains exactly `m h(t)`.

If `d=0`, there is exactly one A orientation among the m single sites. Its conditional state is an arbitrary positive `m by m` matrix R of trace one. In interleaved Fock order the local rotations above commute, and the restricted input basis is indexed by that A location. Set

\[
\mu_t(y)=t^{|y|}(1-t)^{m-|y|},\qquad
z_i(y)=\frac{y_i-t}{\sqrt v},\qquad a_i=R_{ii}.
\]

The ratio of the output amplitude from an input A to that from an input B at site i is exactly `z_i(y)`. Consequently the output law is

\[
q_t(y)=\mu_t(y)F(y),\qquad F(y)=z(y)^*Rz(y)\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

Fixed signs from a different Fock-order convention can be absorbed into R; positivity and its diagonal are unchanged. The independent z_i have mean zero, variance one, and fourth moment

    kappa = E z_i^4 = 1/v-3.

Thus `E_mu F=1`. Directly, `E_mu[y_i F]=t+(1-2t)a_i`, so `E_q |y|=mt+1-2t`. Keeping this biased cross-entropy term gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{H(q_t)=(m-2)h(t)-\log v-\mathbb E_{\mu_t}[F\log F].}
\tag{4}
\]

Indeed its unsimplified cross entropy is `m h(t)+(1-2t)log((1-t)/t)`; the equality `2h(t)+(1-2t)log((1-t)/t)=-log v` gives (4). This is the needed measured correction away from the balanced angle.

## 3. Fourth moment with the diagonal entropy retained

Independence and centering, including the possibly nonzero third moments, give exactly

\[
\mathbb E F^2=1+(\kappa-1)\sum_i a_i^2
+4\sum_{i<j}(\operatorname{Re}R_{ij})^2.
\]

There are no third-moment terms: each such cross term also contains an independent centered variable to the first power. Positivity gives `|R_ij|^2<=a_i a_j`, hence

\[
\mathbb E F^2\le3+c\sum_i a_i^2,\qquad c=1/v-6.
\tag{5}
\]

Jensen under probability weights `mu_t(y)F(y)` yields `E[F log F]<=log E F^2`. The following elementary bound retains the input diagonal entropy and improves the crude uniform moment bound:

\[
\boxed{H(a)+\log\!\left(3+c\sum_i a_i^2\right)\le\log(3m+c)
\quad(-2\le c\le6).}
\tag{6}
\]

To prove (6), put `S_2=sum a_i^2` and `G(a)=H(a)+log(3+cS_2)`. Its logarithm argument is positive. For `a_i>a_j>0`,

    partial_i G-partial_j G
    = -log(a_i/a_j)+2c(a_i-a_j)/(3+cS_2).

For `c<=0` this is nonpositive. For `0<c<=6`, let `s=a_i+a_j<=1`. Since `S_2>=s^2/2`,

    c(s-S_2)<=c(s-s^2/2)<=c/2<=3.

Therefore `c/(3+cS_2)<=1/s`; the elementary integral inequality `log(a_i/a_j)>=2(a_i-a_j)/s` again makes the derivative difference nonpositive. Averaging any unequal coordinate pair raises G. Iterating pair averaging to the uniform vector, and using continuity at zero coordinates, proves (6), whose right side is its uniform value. This proves the finite entropy inequality directly; no majorization theorem is imported.

Combining (4)-(6), for every `m>=2` and `v>=1/12`,

\[
\boxed{H(q_t)-H(a)\ge g_{m-2}(t):=(m-2)h(t)
-\log\bigl(1+3(m-2)v\bigr).}
\tag{7}
\]

For `m=1` the exact gain is `h(t)`, since the input orientation is deterministic. At `t=1/2`, (7) becomes `log(2^m/(3m-2))`, exactly the balanced bound of X003.

## 4. Positivity and sector compensation

For every integer k>=1, Bernoulli's inequality gives

    g_k(t)>=k[h(t)-log(1+3v)]=k delta_t.

To prove (2), set `x=1-2t`. The elementary expansion

    h(t)=log2-sum_{j>=1} x^(2j)/(2j(2j-1))

has coefficient sum `log2`. Since `x^(2j)<=x^2`, it implies `h(t)>=4v log2`. Also `log(1+3v)/v` decreases for v>0. For `v>=1/12`,

    delta_t >= v[4log2-12log(5/4)]
            >= (1/3)log2-log(5/4)>0.

The final strict inequality is `128>125`. Since `g_0=0`, every sector bound is nonnegative.

Write r for the input B particle count. If `r=0`, then `d=0,m=1` and the gain is `h(t)`. If `r=1`, both possible sector types have a nonnegative gain. If `r>=2`, then either `d=1,m=r-1`, giving `(r-1)h(t)`, or `d=0,m=r+1`, giving at least `g_{r-1}(t)>=(r-1)delta_t`. The local-total sector probabilities are preserved. The Shannon entropy chain rule therefore proves (1), including arbitrary mixed and correlated input states.

The argument accounts for all sectors. In this one-particle setting every sector is nonnegative on the stated interval; no claim is made that higher-weight sectors have this property.

## 5. Gaussian/DPP consequence and sharper gap

Let `P=uu*` be any rank-one projection, `0<=K<=I` any Hermitian contraction, `a(T)=sum_{i in T}|u_i|^2`, and `p_T=P_K(T)`. Set `K_t=(1-t)P+tK`. The independent Gaussian inputs have measured product law `P_P tensor P_K`. The beam splitter's full covariance is

\[
B_t=\begin{pmatrix}K_t&\sqrt v(K-P)\\\sqrt v(K-P)&K_{1-t}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For clarity this requires only finite Slater algebra: spectrally decompose K, independently choose its eigenvectors with their Bernoulli eigenvalues, and form the corresponding Slater states. Cauchy-Binet gives inclusion probabilities `det K_S`. A one-body unitary rotates every chosen orbital, so transforms covariance by conjugation; output-register inclusion probabilities are the principal-block determinants. Thus the displayed output marginal kernels follow, including singular endpoints.

Let `I_cl(t)` denote mutual information between the measured output registers. The exact entropy chain rule is

    H(K_t)+H(K_(1-t))-H(P)-H(K)
    = H(Q_out)-H(Q_in)+I_cl(t).

Keeping the sharper sector gains proves, for every `v>=1/12`,

\[
\boxed{H(K_t)+H(K_{1-t})\ge H(P)+H(K)+\Gamma_t(P,K)+I_{\rm cl}(t),}
\tag{8}
\]

where

\[
\Gamma_t=p_\varnothing h(t)
+\sum_{|T|\ge2}p_T\left[(|T|-1)h(t)
-(1-a(T))\log\bigl(1+3(|T|-1)v\bigr)\right].
\tag{9}
\]

For a measured input `(i,T)`, membership `i in T` determines `d=1`, otherwise `d=0`; this proves the coefficient `a(T)` in (9). In particular,

    Gamma_t >= p_empty h(t)+delta_t E[(|T|-1)_+] >= 0.

This gives a strictly positive lower bound whenever the reservoir count is not identically one. Every strict contraction K has `p_empty=det(I-K)>0`. By global particle-hole complementation, the same interval theorem holds for every corank-one projection endpoint, with the reservoir particle count replaced by its hole count.

## 6. Exact scope and remaining problem

This is a new nonbalanced finite mixing family relative to X003/X012, derived entirely in the measured basis. It neither discards the dephasing correction nor assumes full-output entropy always rises for arbitrary Gaussian pairs. At the midpoint (8) recovers X003; away from the midpoint it controls the sum of the two complementary affine outputs. It does not separate the individual Jensen deficits.

The interval restriction enters exactly at (6), and its stated c range is sharp uniformly in m. For `m=2`, put `a=(1/2+epsilon,1/2-epsilon)`. The second derivative at zero of the left side minus the right side in (6) equals `4(c-6)/(c+6)`. Thus (6) itself fails for every `c>6`, not merely its proof. This does not establish a counterexample to measured entropy monotonicity outside the interval. To extend (8) to every `t in (0,1)`, one sufficient missing lemma is the exact biased one-excitation inequality `H(mu_t z^*Rz)>=H(diag R)` for every positive trace-one R. Even its proof would not resolve arbitrary higher-particle input pairs or unrestricted Lyons concavity.
