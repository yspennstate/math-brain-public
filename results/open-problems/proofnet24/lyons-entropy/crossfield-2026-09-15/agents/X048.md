## 1. An actual finite-hidden-state localization

Fix `0<K<I_n`, `p=P_K`, `L=K(I-K)^{-1}`, `T=L^{1/2}`, and `M=K(I-K)`. For every subset `S`, let `Pi_S` project onto the selected columns `T_S`. Draw `X~p` and independent standard Brownian motions `W_i`, and observe, for localization time `s≥0`,

    Y_i(s)=s X_i+W_i(s),   D_s=diag(exp(Y_i(s)−s/2)).                   (1)

Bayes' formula for the finite Gaussian mixture gives

    p_s(S)=p_S product_(i in S)(D_s)ii / Z_s,
    Z_s=det(I+T D_s T)/det(I+L).                                      (2)

Write `F_s` for the observation filtration, `m_i=E[X_i|F_s]`, and `dN_i=dY_i−m_i ds`. The `N_i` are continuous martingales with `d<N_i,N_j>=delta_ij ds`: conditional expectation removes the drift, and the Brownian quadratic variation is unchanged. Direct Itô differentiation of (2) gives, for any fixed finite table `f`,

    dp_s(S)=p_s(S) sum_i(1_(i in S)−m_i)dN_i,
    d E[f(X)|F_s]=sum_i Cov_s(f(X),X_i)dN_i.                          (3)

Thus no stochastic filtering or localization theorem with unchecked hypotheses is imported. Gaussian likelihoods, conditional expectation, and Itô's product rule suffice. All displayed stochastic identities may first be stopped where fields are bounded and then extended. The hidden state has exactly `2^n` possibilities.

There are **two different kernels**, always in the original measured basis:

    J_s=I−(I+D_s^(1/2)L D_s^(1/2))^(−1),
    Q_s=I−(I+T D_s T)^(−1).                                          (4)

The first generates the actual posterior occupation law `p_s=P_(J_s)`. The second is its recovered covariance:

    Q_s=E[Pi_X|F_s],   wedge^r Q_s=E[wedge^r Pi_X|F_s]  (0≤r≤n).       (5)

To prove (5), multiply each unnormalized Slater vector `wedge T_S` by `product_(i in S) sqrt((D_s)ii)` and sum its outer product. Cauchy–Binet gives the quasifree density `Gamma(T D_s T)/det(I+T D_s T)`. Its covariance is `Q_s`, and its compound moments are the displayed exterior powers. In general `H(P_(Q_s))` is not the posterior entropy `H(p_s)`.

## 2. Recovered martingale bracket versus physical posterior drift

Put `E_i=e_i e_i*` and

    V_i=(D_s)ii (I−Q_s) T E_i T(I−Q_s),
    R_i(J)=(E_i J+J E_i)/2−J E_i J,
    G_i(J)=i[E_i,J],   B_i(J)=[E_i,[E_i,J]].                          (6)

Differentiating (4) with respect to the log activity at site `i`, then using (3), proves

    dQ_s=sum_i V_i dN_i,                 V_i≥0, rank V_i=1,
    dJ_s=sum_i R_i(J_s)dN_i−(J_s−diag J_s)ds/4.                       (7)

For the second identity, `partial_i J=R_i`, and a direct multiplication gives

    (m_i−1/2)R_i+(1/2)D R_i[R_i]=−B_i/8,
    sum_i B_i=2(J−diag J).                                           (8)

The off-diagonal drift in (7) is essential. In particular, the posterior occupation kernel is **not** a matrix martingale; its expected off-diagonal entries equal `exp(−s/4)K_ij`. The recovered kernel is a matrix martingale, with the exact terminal bracket

    E integral_0^infinity sum_i V_i^2 ds=K(I−K).                       (9)

Indeed `Y_i(s)/s→X_i`, so (2) concentrates on the actual `X`, and `Q_s→Pi_X`. Expand the squared martingale increment, use orthogonality of the innovations, and use `Pi_X²=Pi_X`, `E Pi_X=K`. This proves (9) without treating a change of odds as an affine kernel path.

## 3. Exact likelihood martingales for an affine kernel path

Now fix the **different** parameter `t` and the actual affine path `K(t)=K+tA`, `A=A*`. Put `B=M^(−1/2) A M^(−1/2)` and `C_s=Q_s−K`. X007's determinant atom identity and (5) give the finite-parameter likelihood of the observed history:

    w_s(t):=E[p_(K+tA)(X)/p_K(X)|F_s]=det(I+t B C_s).                 (10)

At `t=0`, define `sigma_s=w_s'`, `eta_s=w_s''`, and `Zeta_s=E[log p_K(X)|F_s]`. Then

    sigma_s=tr(B C_s),   eta_s=sigma_s²−tr(B C_s B C_s),
    beta_i=tr(B V_i),    gamma_i=tr(B C_s B V_i),
    delta_i=Cov_s(log p_K(X),X_i).                                   (11)

Their stochastic differentials are exactly

    d sigma=sum beta_i dN_i,
    d eta=2 sum (sigma beta_i−gamma_i)dN_i,
    d Zeta=sum delta_i dN_i.                                         (12)

The Itô correction in `d eta` cancels because `tr(BV_iBV_i)=(tr BV_i)²` for rank-one `V_i`. The second likelihood coefficient has not been discarded. Consequently, for every finite horizon `u`,

    −H''_K[A,A]=E integral_0^u sum_i{beta_i²+
                     2(sigma beta_i−gamma_i)delta_i}ds + Rem_u,       (13)
    Rem_u=E[Var(sigma_infinity|F_u)
                     +Cov(eta_infinity,log p_K(X)|F_u)].

This is obtained by applying the martingale product rule to `sigma²+eta Zeta`. All terminal variables are bounded finite tables at the strict base, so `Rem_u→0`. Equation (13) is a continuous localization analogue of X010's complete predictable identity, with an explicit finite-horizon residual. Its covariation term is signed; no pointwise positivity is claimed.

## 4. Every correction when the localized kernel follows the affine path

For a fixed positive diagonal `D`, let `E=D−I` and

    J_D(t)=D^(1/2)(K+tA)[I+E(K+tA)]^(−1)D^(1/2),
    U=D^(1/2)(I+KE)^(−1),   F=E(I+KE)^(−1)=F*.

Differentiating this rational map gives its complete affine-origin second jet:

    A_D:=J_D'(0)=U A U*,       C_D:=J_D''(0)=−2 U A F A U*,
    sigma_D=tr(FA),            eta_D=sigma_D²−tr(FAFA).                (14)

The last two quantities are the first and second normalized derivatives of `det(I+E(K+tA))`. They equal (11) when `D=D_s`. The nonzero `C_D`, moving history weight, and its derivatives all matter.

Write `H(J)=H(P_J)`, `q_i=m_i(1−m_i)`, `a_i=(A_D)ii`, `c_i=(C_D)ii`, and define

    Xi_s=sum_i{eta_s q_i+2 sigma_s(1−2m_i)a_i
                            +(1−2m_i)c_i−2a_i²}.                    (15)

For every finite horizon `u`, the following **transport formula** holds:

    H''_K[A,A]
      =E{D²H(J_u)[A_D,A_D]+DH(J_u)[C_D]
                 +2 sigma_u DH(J_u)[A_D]+eta_u H(J_u)}
        +(1/2) E integral_0^u Xi_s ds.                               (16)

Proof: apply (3) and Itô's rule to entropy to obtain

    dH(p_s)=−sum_i Cov_s(log p_s(X),X_i)dN_i
                         −(1/2)sum_i q_i ds.                         (17)

For prior `p_(K+tA)`, its observation likelihood relative to the base is exactly `w_s(t)`. Integrating (17) under that prior gives

    H(P_(K+tA))=E[w_u(t)H(P_(J_(D_u)(t)))]
        +(1/2)integral_0^u E[w_s(t)sum_i m_i(t)(1−m_i(t))]ds.

Differentiate twice at zero. The first term yields the four terms in braces in (16), and the second yields (15). For finite `u`, differentiation is legitimate: ratios of the finitely many initial atom probabilities and their derivatives are bounded on a small `t` interval; conditional expectations preserve those bounds. Formula (16) is the requested sign-transport identity. A known sign of its localized Hessian term alone does not control the other terms.

## 5. A finite affine field/gauge identity and a proved drift sign

Here is an exact stronger atom identity behind (7). At any strict kernel and any coordinate, abbreviate `R=R_i(K)`, `G=G_i(K)`, `m=K_ii`, `q=m(1−m)`. For each atom `p_x`,

    Dp_x[R]=p_x(x_i−m),  Dp_x[G]=0,
    D²p_x[R,R]=(1/4)D²p_x[G,G],  D²p_x[R,G]=0.                       (18)

Proof: tilt one activity by `exp(v)` and conjugate by `exp(i theta E_i)`. The atom law is the Bernoulli tilt and is independent of `theta`. Its kernel derivatives are `J_v=R`, `J_theta=G`,

    J_vv=(1−2m)R−B_i/4,  J_thetatheta=−B_i,
    J_vtheta=(1/2−m)G.

Differentiating the two invariant/tilted atom formulas proves every assertion in (18). Since both `R` and `G` are supported on `span(e_i,Ke_i)`, every real combination has rank at most two. Thus (18) gives a **finite**, not merely asymptotic, affine likelihood for every physical `t`:

    p_(K+t(alpha R+beta G))(x)
      =p_x[1+alpha t(x_i−m)]
        +(t²/2)(alpha²/4+beta²)D²p_x[G,G].                            (19)

In particular, including full acceleration,

    D²H[alpha R+beta G,alpha R+beta G]
      =−alpha²q+(alpha²/4+beta²)D²H[G,G].                             (20)

The gauge term has a direct nonnegative Fisher integral in every dimension. In pivot coordinates write `K=[[m,k*],[k,B]]`, `b=1−m`, `v=k/sqrt(q)`, `D0=vv*`, and `F_r=B+rD0`, `−b≤r≤m`. Both endpoint conditional kernels, hence this whole interval, are strict. Set

    I_i(K)=integral_(−b)^m sum_T
          {Dp_T(F_r)[D0]}²/p_T(F_r) dr ≥0.                           (21)

Rank-one determinant affinity gives `d²H(F_r)/dr²=−FI_(F_r)(D0)`. On the affine gauge line, `k` is multiplied by `1−it`, so the conditional kernels become `B−b(1+t²)D0` and `B+m(1+t²)D0`. Differentiating the exact conditional entropy chain rule therefore proves

    D²H[G,G]=−2q I_i(K),
    D²H[alpha R+beta G]=−q{alpha²+(alpha²/2+2beta²)I_i(K)}.            (22)

This supplies a proved all-dimensional drift sign from an actual conditional Fisher energy; its directional sign overlaps the earlier one-row theorem and X034, as noted above. If `k≠0`, then `I_i>0`: a nonzero positive rank-one direction changes at least one marginal, so its full Fisher information is positive.

For example, the posterior kernel drift can be compensated explicitly. The process

    C_u=H(J_u)+(1/4)integral_0^u DH(J_s)[J_s−diag J_s]ds

has stochastic drift

    −(1/2)sum_i q_i−(1/4)sum_i q_i I_i(J_s) ≤0.                      (23)

Indeed its drift is `(1/2)sum_i D²H(J_s)[R_i,R_i]`, and (22) proves (23). On bounded stopped horizons it is a supermartingale. This is a concrete signed-drift theorem, with the physical kernel's compensator retained. It does not assert the corresponding sign for arbitrary affine sensitivities in (13) or (16).

## 6. Exact remaining inequality and scope

If the localized kernels at horizon `u` lie in any certified Hessian-sign family, the **remaining necessary and sufficient compensation** for the chosen `K,A,u` in this transport is

    E{DH(J_u)[C_D]+2 sigma_u DH(J_u)[A_D]+eta_u H(J_u)}
       +(1/2)E integral_0^u Xi_s ds
       ≤ −E D²H(J_u)[A_D,A_D].                                      (24)

Equivalently, after complete localization it is

    E integral_0^infinity 2 sum_i(sigma beta_i−gamma_i)delta_i ds
                ≥ −E integral_0^infinity sum_i beta_i² ds.           (25)

The right-hand magnitude is exactly the full initial atom Fisher information. These conditions retain the martingale covariance, changing normalization, physical posterior drift, and affine-path acceleration. No sign for the remaining three-site mixed-inertia cone, no stopping rule guaranteed to reach a known region with (24), and no new uniform entropy-concavity family are established here. The positive drift theorem (23) concerns its specified localization directions only.
