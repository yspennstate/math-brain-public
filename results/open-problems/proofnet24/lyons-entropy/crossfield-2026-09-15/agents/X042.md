## 1. Fixed lift and precise theorem

On three coordinates let

    v=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3),
    V0=[(1,-1,0)/sqrt(2), (1,1,-2)/sqrt(6)].

Add a physical coordinate e0, and put V=(0;V0). Fix parameters

    0<epsilon<=2^-16,  rho=sqrt(a^2+b^2)<=1/20,
    w=sqrt(1-epsilon)e0-sqrt(epsilon)(0;v),
    M(theta)=[[1/2+a,b+i theta],[b-i theta,1/2-a]],
    K(theta)=ww*/2+V M(theta)V*,       |theta|<=1/10.       (1)

These are positive contractions: the nonzero eigenvalues are 1/2 and
1/2±sqrt(rho^2+theta^2). The measured basis remains fixed. For nonzero
(a,b), any distinct M(theta0),M(theta1) do not commute.

Add a fifth coordinate f, define W=(w;1)/sqrt(2), Vhat=(V;0), and set

    Q(theta)=WW*+Vhat M(theta)Vhat*.                       (2)

Here W and Vhat are fixed orthogonal isometries. Thus (2) is a fixed
rank-one projection background with two active modes, a projection-flag
lift; Q itself is not falsely called a projection for fractional M.
The physical compression is exactly K. Measure all five occupations and
forget f. This deterministic measured channel is parameter independent.
Let C(theta)=H(X_f|X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3) for this lift.

**Theorem.** Throughout (1),

    C''(theta)>=(431/480)epsilon>0.                       (3)

Put g=log(4/3)+(2/3)log(24/25)>0. For theta_t=(1-t)theta0+t theta1,

    H(P_K(theta_t)) >= (1-t)H(P_K(theta0))+t H(P_K(theta1))
      +[g+(431/960)epsilon](theta1-theta0)^2 t(1-t).       (4)

The fixed-basis full Shannon entropy is used everywhere. In particular,
(3) supplies a genuine favorable entropy-subtraction argument for this
lift, rather than assuming that arbitrary Naimark corrections are convex.

## 2. The exact global ancilla channel

Let P0(M)=P_(V0 M V0*) and P1(M)=P_(vv*+V0 M V0*) on the final three
physical coordinates. Write Z=1-X_f. Expansion of the occupied vector
W=(w+f)/sqrt(2) gives P(Z=0)=P(Z=1)=1/2. Conditional on Z=0, physical
coordinate 0 is empty and the remaining law is P0. Conditional on Z=1,
the occupied background is w. Expanding w along e0 and v gives

    P(X_0=1,Y|Z=1)=(1-epsilon)P0(Y),
    P(X_0=0,Y|Z=1)=epsilon P1(Y).                        (5)

One can justify these expansions for every active M by first expanding
its independent spectral occupations: in each sector, multilinearity of
the Slater determinant gives the displayed two orthogonal coordinate
sectors. Averaging retains (5). No measured-coordinate rotation occurs.

Therefore X_0=1 reveals Z, and the only ambiguous histories have X_0=0.
For phi(x,y)=(x+y)log(x+y)-x log x-y log y, the exact correction is

    C(M)=(1/2)sum_Y phi(P0(M;Y),epsilon P1(M;Y)).         (6)

This is one global hidden background flag; it is not a product of binary
channels independently applied to the three measured occupations.
The lifted entropy L=H(P_Q) is exactly

    L=log2+(1/2)h(epsilon)
      +(1-epsilon/2)H(P0)+(epsilon/2)H(P1),
    H(P_K)=L-C.                                         (7)

The proof below retains C, including its full posterior Fisher loss.

## 3. Six scalar ambiguity terms

Set d=det M=1/4-rho^2-theta^2 and c=1/2-d. Define the fixed real s_i by

    (V0 Re(M)V0*)_ii=(2/3)(1/2+s_i).

The row norms of V0 are sqrt(2/3); hence |s_i|<=rho, and sum_i s_i=0.
The two-mode spectral expansion and the three-dimensional complement
identity give the following complete laws:

| Outcome | P0 | P1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Empty | d | 0 |
| Singleton i | A_i=(2/3)(c+s_i) | B=d/3 |
| Pair complementary to i | B=d/3 | A'_i=(2/3)(c-s_i) |
| Full triple | 0 | d |

For completeness, the active rank-two projection is I-vv*. Its pair
minors all equal 1/3, and its diagonal entries are 2/3. The middle active
covariance is M-dI, proving the P0 column. Particle-hole complementation
of vv*+V0 M V0* gives V0(I-M)V0*, proving the P1 column. Thus

    C=(1/2)sum_i [phi(A_i,epsilon B)+phi(B,epsilon A'_i)]. (8)

All the displayed overlapping atoms are positive. Along theta only d
moves; s_i and epsilon are fixed. In the whole theorem domain, the looser
bounds below suffice:

    1/5<=d<=1/4,  1/4<=c<=3/10,
    2/15<=A_i,A'_i<=7/30,  1/15<=B<=1/12.               (9)

## 4. A proved negative d derivative and its full Fisher term

Since phi_x=log(1+y/x) and phi_y=log(1+x/y), differentiation of (8) gives

    C_d=(1/2)sum_i {
      -(2/3)log(1+epsilon B/A_i)
      +(epsilon/3)log(1+A_i/(epsilon B))
      +(1/3)log(1+epsilon A'_i/B)
      -(2epsilon/3)log(1+B/(epsilon A'_i))}.             (10)

Use A_i/B,A'_i/B<=7/2, log(1+u)<=u, and epsilon<=1/2. Dropping only the
explicitly nonpositive first term, and bounding the other three, yields

    C_d <= epsilon[7/4+log7+(1/2)log epsilon]
        <= -(19/12)epsilon.                             (11)

Indeed log(1+7/(2epsilon))<=log(4/epsilon), while
log(1+2/(7epsilon))>=log(2/(7epsilon)). Finally log7<2 and log2>2/3:
the first follows from the exponential series through degree four plus
its positive tail, and the second from 2 atanh(1/3)>2/3. Together with
epsilon<=2^-16 these give 7/4+2-16/3=-19/12.

For affine positive x(d),y(d), direct differentiation proves

    d² phi(x,y)/dd²=-(x'y-xy')²/[xy(x+y)].

Consequently the complete second derivative, including the posterior
score-variance term, is the nonpositive expression

    C_dd=-(epsilon/162)sum_i {
      (1+2s_i)^2/[A_i B(A_i+epsilon B)]
      +(1-2s_i)^2/[B A'_i(B+epsilon A'_i)]}.             (12)

Using (9), |s_i|<=1/20, A_i+epsilon B>=A_i, and
B+epsilon A'_i>=B gives the explicit bound

    0<=-C_dd <= (3epsilon/2)(121/100)(125/12+125/6)
              =(1815/32)epsilon.                       (13)

Since d'=-2theta and d''=-2, both (11) and (13) are needed:

    C''=4theta² C_dd-2C_d
       >=epsilon[19/6-(1815/8)theta²]
       >=(431/480)epsilon.                              (14)

This proves (3). The negative conditional Fisher term in (12) has been
bounded, not omitted or assigned the opposite sign.

## 5. Direct lifted curvature and the Jensen conclusion

Write H0=H(P0). The table gives, at fixed s_i,

    (H0)_d=(2/3)sum_i log A_i-2log d+log3,
    (H0)_dd=-2/d-(4/9)sum_i 1/A_i<0.                   (15)

If sum s_i=0, |s_i|<=rho<c, then
prod_i(c+s_i)>=c(c²-rho²). To see this, take two s_i of the same sign,
write their absolute values u,v and x=u+v<=rho, and expand the product
as c(c²-x²)+(c±x)uv. Both extra factors are nonnegative.
Here rho/c<=1/5 and c/d>=1. Substitution in (15) gives

    (H0)_d >=log(4/3)+(2/3)log(24/25)=g>0.              (16)

Positivity is exact: exp(3g)=(4/3)^3(24/25)^2>1.
Thus H0''=4theta²(H0)_dd-2(H0)_d<=-2g. For H1=H(P1), replace every
s_i by -s_i; the same proof applies. Equation (7) now gives L''<=-2g.
Subtracting the convex correction (14) proves

    H(P_K)''<=-2g-(431/480)epsilon.

Integrating this scalar bound on the full interval between theta0 and
theta1 proves (4). This is the target Jensen inequality for actual affine
physical kernels, with a strictly positive quantitative gap.
