# X008 — exterior coherent-state entropy operators

Completed constructive attempt, 15 September 2026. This worker derives a concrete exterior-space logarithmic compression formula with a positive integral correction, proves a universal scalar rank-three curvature-matrix upper bound and a cone of strict concavity, and proves full active Hessian negativity for every entropy-isotropic rank-three frame, including non-equiangular families. The universal entropy conjecture is not proved. Historical novelty has not been assessed.

## 1. Scalar rank-three entropy operators

Let `V:C^3 -> C^n` be isometric, write `U_i=v_i* v_i`, `W_ij=w_ij w_ij*` with the Hodge-dual convention of X004, and put

    t_i=tr U_i,  b_ij=tr W_ij,  r_ijk=|det V_ijk|^2.

Omit zero effects in all logarithmic expressions. Define the two rank-one POVM entropy operators and the top exterior entropy

    E1=-sum_i (log t_i) U_i,       e1=tr E1,
    E2=-sum_ij (log b_ij) W_ij,    e2=tr E2,
    e3=-sum_ijk r_ijk log r_ijk.

Both effect families sum to `I_3`. Contraction in the exterior algebra gives

    sum_{j != i} W_ij=t_i I-U_i.                         (1)

Here unordered pairs are counted once in each sum with fixed `i`. One elementary proof is to complete the row vector `v_i/sqrt(t_i)` to an orthonormal basis, rotate it to the first coordinate, and use the Parseval identity for the other rows. The resulting Hodge-dual pair vectors live in its orthogonal complement and their frame operator is `t_i` times that projection.

The frozen logarithmic matrix from X004 therefore has the exact formula

    W=sum_ij log(t_i t_j/b_ij) W_ij
     =E1+E2-e1 I.                                      (2)

The rank-three projection measure has inclusion marginals

    sum_{T containing {i,j}} r_T=b_ij,
    sum_{T containing i} r_T=t_i.

Consequently its scalar cubic coefficient is

    tau=sum_ijk r_ijk log[b_ij b_ik b_jk/(t_i t_j t_k r_ijk)]
       =e1-e2+e3.                                      (3)

At `M=sI`, with `q=1-s`, the complete logarithmic polarization is thus

    T(s)=E1+E2-[q e1+s(e2-e3)]I.                        (4)

These identities are exact for arbitrary frames, including zero minors. They expose the anisotropy entirely in the two exterior POVM entropy operators. The top three-form contributes only a scalar.

Combining (4) with the exact Fisher formula from the coordinator gives

    -H''[A,A]=F_v(A)/s+F_w(A)/q
      -2 tr({E1+E2-[q e1+s(e2-e3)]I} adj A),            (5)

where `F_v=sum_i tr(U_i A)^2/t_i` and `F_w=sum_ij tr(W_ij A)^2/b_ij`.

## 2. A coherent-state compression and its exact positive integral correction

For each supported pair define

    x_ij=t_i t_j/b_ij >= 1,
    P_ij=W_ij/b_ij.

`P_ij` is the rank-one orthogonal projection perpendicular to the two normalized row columns. Let `C:C^3 -> C^m` be the isometry whose pair-indexed rows are `w_ij*`; thus `C*C=I` by exterior Parseval. Put `D=diag(x_ij)` and

    S=C* D C=sum_ij t_i t_j P_ij.

The logarithmic polarization is exactly the compressed logarithm

    W=C* (log D) C.                                    (6)

The associated measurement correction admits the finite-dimensional integral

    J=log S-W
     =integral_0^infinity [C*(D+uI)^(-1)C-(S+uI)^(-1)] du
     >=0.                                              (7)

Here the inequality holds pointwise for the **compression correction**, not for the entropy Hessian's unresolved resolvent integrand. To prove it without importing an operator-Jensen theorem, complete `C` to a unitary `[C,C_perp]`. Write the compression blocks of `D` as

\[
\begin{pmatrix} S&B\\B^*&R\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Block inversion gives the integrand in (7) as

    (S+uI)^(-1) B
      [R+uI-B*(S+uI)^(-1)B]^(-1)
      B*(S+uI)^(-1),                                  (8)

which is positive semidefinite. Every inverse exists for `u>=0` because `D>=I`. The integral converges: the integrand is bounded at zero and is `O(u^-3)` at infinity. Formula (7) then follows by applying the scalar identity

    log x=integral_0^infinity [(1+u)^(-1)-(x+u)^(-1)]du

to the finite spectral decompositions; the constant terms cancel because `C*C=I`. When `m=3`, the complementary block is absent and the correction vanishes. No Berezin-Lieb or operator-concavity theorem is assumed beyond this proved identity.

## 3. Universal bound W <= (log 2)I from exterior geometry

Write `n_i=v_i*/sqrt(t_i)` and `Q_i=I-n_i n_i*`, omitting zero rows. For a supported pair set

    d_ij=1-|n_i* n_j|^2=b_ij/(t_i t_j).

Projection inside the two-dimensional space `ran Q_i` gives

    P_ij=Q_i-Q_i n_j n_j* Q_i/d_ij.

Since `0<d_ij<=1`, and Parseval gives `sum_j t_j n_j n_j*=I`,

    sum_{j:b_ij>0} t_j Q_i n_j n_j* Q_i/d_ij >= Q_i.

Rows parallel to `n_i` contribute zero before division and are omitted. Let `m_i` be the sum of `t_j` over those parallel rows, including row `i`. Then

    sum_{j:b_ij>0} t_j P_ij <=(2-m_i)Q_i <=2Q_i.

Summing in `i` and counting each pair twice proves

    I<=S=(1/2)sum_i t_i sum_{j:b_ij>0}t_j P_ij
       <=sum_i t_i Q_i=2I.                            (9)

The lower bound follows alternatively from `x_ij>=1` and exterior Parseval. Combining (6)-(9),

    0<=W<=log S<=(log 2)I.                            (10)

This is sharper than applying scalar Jensen and only using `sum_i t_i=3`, which gives `log(9/2)`. It also respects arbitrary splitting of one row into parallel copies: the logarithmic minor ratios and their operator sums remain unchanged.

## 4. A uniform upper bound on the complete logarithmic curvature matrix

Let

    Z3=sum_{i<j<k} t_i t_j t_k <=(sum_i t_i)^3/6=9/2,
    pi_T=(product_{i in T}t_i)/Z3.

The distribution `pi` is defined on all triples of nonzero rows, including triples with zero determinant. The top projection law `r_T` has support contained in this set, so `D3=D(r||pi)` is finite. Direct expansion gives

    e3-e1=log Z3-D3,
    tau+tr W=log Z3-D3.                              (11)

Thus

    W+tau I=(log Z3-D3)I-[(tr W)I-W]
           <=log(9/2)I,                              (12)

because `W>=0` implies `(tr W)I-W>=0`. At every scalar active base,

    0<=T(s)=W+s tau I<=U_s I,
    U_s=q log 2+s log(9/2)<2.                         (13)

The lower bound is the already established X004 positivity theorem, with its finite exterior proof valid for zero minors. The upper bound is proved here. There is also an exact positive decomposition incorporating the coherent-state correction (7):

    U_s I-T(s)
     =q[log(2)I-log S+J]
      +s[(log(9/2)-log Z3+D3)I+(tr W)I-W].            (14)

Every displayed summand is positive semidefinite. In particular, the correction has not been dropped in deriving the bound.

## 5. Every semidefinite direction, and an explicit indefinite cone

**Theorem.** For every rank-three measured support `V`, every `0<s<1`, and every nonzero semidefinite Hermitian active direction `A`,

    -H''_{sI}[A,A]
      >=[1/(3sq)-2U_s/3](tr A)^2
      >=c0 (tr A)^2 >0,

    c0=[4-2log(9/2)]/3 approximately 0.330615.         (15)

Indeed, `N=|X|` has `Var(N)=3sq`, and the score identity `E[(N-3s)sigma_A]=tr A` gives `FI>=(tr A)^2/(3sq)`. Since a semidefinite `A` has `adj A>=0`, (13) gives

    2tr(T adj A)<=2U_s tr(adj A)
                 <=(2U_s/3)(tr A)^2.

The last inequality follows by writing the three same-sign eigenvalues and using the elementary symmetric inequality. This proves (15), including rank-deficient semidefinite directions. The direction is the original affine active covariance direction; no affine odds path or measured-basis rotation has been substituted.

More generally, since `0<=T<=U_s I`, for arbitrary Hermitian `A`,

    -H''[A,A]>=(tr A)^2/(3sq)-2U_s tr((adj A)_+).     (16)

Thus (16) certifies an explicit cone even for indefinite directions. In particular, if after possibly replacing `A` by `-A` its eigenvalues are `a,b,-c`, with `a,b>0` and `0<=c<=eta(a+b)`, then

    -H''[A,A]
      >={16(1-eta)^2/3-2log(9/2)}ab>0                (17)

whenever

    eta<1-sqrt(3log(9/2)/8) approximately 0.2490.

The derivation is `tr((adj A)_+)=ab`, `|tr A|>= (1-eta)(a+b)`, `(a+b)^2>=4ab`, and `sq<=1/4`. The coordinator independently proposed this useful corollary of (13); it is included with attribution rather than counted as a separate worker result. Formula (16) also covers a second large-negative-eigenvalue region when the absolute trace is sufficiently large.

## 6. Full active Hessian theorem for entropy-isotropic frames

Call a rank-three Parseval frame entropy-isotropic when

    W=sum_ij log(t_i t_j/b_ij) W_ij=cI.               (18)

This is a condition on the fixed frame, independent of the scalar base `s`. It is strictly weaker than equal-norm equiangularity. Put `t(s)=c+s tau`, and decompose any Hermitian direction as

    A=(h/3)I+B,   h=tr A,   tr B=0.

The exact two-frame Fisher form satisfies

    FI_s(A)=h^2/(3sq)+FI_s(B).                        (19)

To check that there is no cross term, observe that the polarization of either `F_v` or `F_w` at `(I,B)` equals `tr B=0`. Consequently

    -H''[A,A]
      =FI_s(B)+t(s)||B||_F^2
        +[1/(3sq)-2t(s)/3]h^2.                       (20)

By (13), `0<=t(s)<=U_s<2`, so all terms in (20) are nonnegative and the trace coefficient is at least `c0`. This proves the following new class theorem:

**Theorem.** At every scalar covariance `sI`, `0<s<1`, every entropy-isotropic rank-three frame has a nonpositive full active entropy Hessian. If `c>0`, its Hessian is negative definite in all nine real Hermitian directions, including directions that change active eigenvectors.

For strictness, X004's `W+tau I>=0` gives `tau>=-c`, hence `t(s)>=qc>0`. In particular,

    -H''[A,A]>=qc ||B||_F^2+c0 h^2.                  (21)

For each fixed `s` and frame with `c>0`, continuity therefore gives an open neighborhood of scalar active covariances where the full active Hessian remains negative definite. This neighborhood conclusion uses only the finite positive-atom formulas; the support is fixed throughout `0<M<I`.

### An explicit non-equiangular six-row example

Let `F_3` be the unitary three-dimensional Fourier matrix and take

    V=(1/sqrt(2)) [ I_3 ; F_3 ].

All rows have norm squared `1/2`. Pairs in the same basis have `b_ij=1/4` and logarithmic coefficient zero. Cross-basis pairs have `b_ij=1/6` and coefficient `log(3/2)`. Each basis alone contributes `(1/4)I` to the two-row exterior frame operator, so the cross-basis pairs contribute `(1/2)I`. Therefore

    W=(1/2)log(3/2)I,
    tau=(3/4)log(4/3).                               (22)

For the second formula, the two within-basis triples each have weight `1/8`; the eighteen mixed triples each have weight `1/24`. Thus

    e1=3log2,  e2=(3/2)log24,
    e3=(1/4)log8+(3/4)log24,

which gives (22). This frame is not equiangular, and its measured atom probabilities are not uniform within the two-particle or three-particle cardinality layers. Consequently it is outside both X004's equiangular theorem and the prior count-uniform theorem. The full active Hessian is strictly negative at every `sI` by (20).

### A continuous family from finite coherent-state orbits

Set `omega=exp(2pi i/3)`, let `X` cyclically permute the three standard basis vectors, and let `Z=diag(1,omega,omega^2)`. For any unit column `u in C^3`, form the nine rows

    v_ab=(Z^a X^b u)*/sqrt(3),       a,b=0,1,2.       (23)

The finite phase sum deletes off-diagonal entries, and the cyclic-shift sum makes the remaining diagonal constant. Hence `sum_ab v_ab* v_ab=I`: this is a Parseval frame for every fiducial `u`.

Right multiplication of all rows by `X` or `Z` permutes the rows up to phases. The pair ratios `t_i t_j/b_ij` remain unchanged. For a unitary active right action `R`, the pair column transforms as `w_ij -> det(R)R* w_ij`, so `W -> R* W R`. Therefore `W` commutes with both `X` and `Z`. Commutation with `Z` makes it diagonal, and commutation with `X` makes all diagonal entries equal. Thus (18) holds for every `u`, with no equiangular assumption and no imported representation-theoretic theorem.

Whenever this orbit has one linearly independent, nonorthogonal pair of rows, `tr W>0`, hence `c>0`. For example `u=(1,2,i)/sqrt(6)` has this property and is not equiangular; the within-orbit inner-product magnitudes take multiple values. Equation (20) proves its full active Hessian is strictly negative for every `0<s<1`. Fiducials yielding only three orthogonal rays can have `c=0`; the nonpositive conclusion still holds, but the strict bound (21) is not asserted for them.

## 7. Exact remaining obstruction and limitations

These arguments do **not** prove scalar-base concavity for every rank-three frame. The isotropy hypothesis removes the trace/traceless cross term of the logarithmic curvature; without it, a residual anisotropic coupling remains. The universal cone bound (16) does not control every indefinite direction.

For a general frame at a fixed `s`, put

    d=tr T/3,  E=T-dI,  tr E=0,
    A=(h/3)I+B,  tr B=0.

Expanding the polynomial adjugate gives the exact identity

    -H''[A,A]
     =FI_s(B)+d||B||_F^2-2tr(E B^2)
       +(2h/3)tr(E B)
       +[1/(3sq)-2d/3]h^2.                           (24)

The scalar `h^2` coefficient is strictly positive by (13), but that does not control the other two anisotropic terms. A precise sufficient and necessary remaining lemma is the positive-semidefiniteness on the eight-dimensional traceless Hermitian space of

    Q_s(B)
      =FI_s(B)+d||B||_F^2-2tr(E B^2)
        -[tr(E B)]^2/[3(1/(sq)-2d)] >=0.             (25)

Indeed, completing the scalar `h` square in (24) gives (25); the denominator is positive since `d<2<=1/(2sq)`. This is an explicit frame-measurement correction that vanishes when `E=0`. It is equivalent to the scalar arbitrary-frame Hessian problem, rather than an assumed inequality. The new bounds (10), (13), and (14) supply constraints on its entropy operator, but do not establish (25) universally. One must also avoid assuming the unresolved positive direction is semidefinite: a Lorentzian positive cone can contain indefinite matrices.

Equation (25) also gives an explicit sufficient condition that allows anisotropy. Let `e_max=lambda_max(E)`, `v_E=tr(E^2)`, and `L_s=1/(sq)-2d>0`. The inequalities

    tr(E B^2)<=e_max ||B||_F^2,
    [tr(E B)]^2<=v_E ||B||_F^2

show that the full active Hessian is nonpositive whenever

    d>=2e_max+v_E/(3L_s).                            (26)

It is negative definite if this inequality is strict. In particular this is a quantitative neighborhood certificate beyond the exactly isotropic class; it uses the Fisher information only through its nonnegativity after removing the exact trace score. For a certificate valid for every scalar `s`, it suffices to set

    d_-=min(tr W/3, tr W/3+tau),
    d_+=max(tr W/3, tr W/3+tau),

and verify

    d_->2e_max+v_E/[3(4-2d_+)].                       (27)

The universal bound gives `d_+<=log(9/2)<2`, so the denominator is positive. This stronger frame-only sufficient condition is not asserted to hold for every frame.

The non-scalar active covariance problem lies beyond the present scalar-base theorem. At a non-scalar base the log-minor ratios depend on the active odds metric, so the Parseval argument for `S<=2I` cannot be transplanted without changing its hypotheses.
