# X052 — Nonlinear endpoint Fisher information and mixed area

15 September 2026. **Proved quantitative concavity domains and an exact nonlinear
Fisher allocation theorem.** The unrestricted Lyons conjecture remains open.
The measured coordinate basis and every atom acceleration are retained.

## 1. The established statements

Fix `D=diag(d_i)`, `B:C^2→C^n`, and `K(M)=D+BMB*`, with strict `D,K`.
There is an explicit mixed-area capacity `C_end(K,B)`, defined in (8), such that

    -H''_M[E,E] >= 2 det(E)[C_end(K,B)-J(M)]  when det(E)>0.       (1)

Here `J>=0` is X040's exact face-logarithm term, reproduced below. This uses
only the empty and fully occupied atom scores to pay the positive acceleration;
all other Fisher terms remain nonnegative. Unlike the linear occupation design
of X046, this capacity can diverge at a spectral boundary.

A concrete consequence requires no restriction on the measured embedding.
Let `V:C^2→C^n` be any isometry, `n>=2`, and choose `0<epsilon<=2^(-4n)`.
Set `N_0=diag(epsilon,1/2)` and consider the entire convex matrix interval

    N_0-(epsilon/2)I_2 <= N <= N_0+(epsilon/2)I_2,
    K(N)=I_n/2+V(N-I_2/2)V*.                                    (2)

**Theorem.** `N→H(P_K(N))` is concave on (2), in all four real Hermitian
parameters, including noncommuting endpoints. All physical kernels are strict.
Every definite active direction has the quantitative bound

    -H''_N[E,E] > 2[2^(n+1)-3n-2] det(E).                        (3)

The bracket is zero at `n=2` and positive at every `n>=3`; the strict sign
in definite directions holds also at `n=2`. No strictness is asserted for all
indefinite directions. A four-site complex example below passes this nonlinear
certificate while failing X046's optimized linear occupation certificate.

## 2. Complete occupation curvature

For `z∈{0,1}^n`, put

    q_z=prod_i d_i^z_i(1-d_i)^(1-z_i),
    eta_i=z_i/d_i-(1-z_i)/(1-d_i),  T_z=B*diag(eta_i)B.

The signed-column determinant and `det(I+UV)=det(I+VU)` give

    p_z=q_z det(I_2+MT_z)
       =q_z[1+tr(MT_z)+det(M)det(T_z)],
    p''_z=2 det(E) q_z det(T_z).                                 (4)

Write `c_ij=|det B_{ij}|^2`. Cauchy–Binet gives
`det(T_z)=sum_(i<j)c_ij eta_i eta_j`. The exact centered Bernoulli identity
`E_q eta_i eta_j f=E_(q,-ij)(f_11-f_10-f_01+f_00)` then yields

    J=sum_(i<j)c_ij E_(q,-ij) log[p_10 p_01/(p_00 p_11)] >=0,
    -H''[E,E]=FI(E)-2 det(E)J,  FI(E)=sum_z (p'_z)^2/p_z.         (5)

To verify the sign, set `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0`. The atom identity
`p_S=det(I-K)det L_S` reduces each face, by eliminating its common occupied
set, to a positive matrix `[[a,z],[conj z,b]]`. Its ratio is
`ab/(ab-|z|^2)>=1`. This also proves (5) for arbitrary complex phases.
Thus all `det(E)<=0` directions are already settled by X040's identity.
No positivity of the signed `T_z`, or of the active base `M`, is assumed.

## 3. Exact mixed-area minimization

For positive Hermitian two-by-two matrices define the polarized determinant

    D_2(P,Q)=[tr(P)tr(Q)-tr(PQ)]/2,
    det(P+tQ)=detP+2tD_2(P,Q)+t^2detQ.                           (6)

The elementary matrix Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality is
`D_2(P,Q)^2>=detP detQ`: conjugate by `P^(-1/2)` and apply
`(lambda_1+lambda_2)^2>=4lambda_1 lambda_2`. This finite determinant proof
covers complex Hermitian matrices and needs no Euclidean-volume identification.

The exact Fisher form of this mixed-area inequality is

    inf_(E>0, detE=1) { [tr(EP)]^2+[tr(EQ)]^2 }
      =2{detP+detQ+sqrt[(detP-detQ)^2+4D_2(P,Q)^2]}.              (7)

**Proof.** A congruence of determinant modulus one makes both `P,Q` diagonal:
first send `P` to `sqrt(detP)I`, then diagonalize the transformed `Q` unitarily.
Write their positive diagonal entries as `p_1,p_2` and `q_1,q_2`.
For `E=[[x,z],[conj z,y]]`, the objective depends only on positive `x,y`.
Under `xy-|z|^2=1`, reducing both diagonal entries proportionally shows the
minimum has `z=0,xy=1`. It is therefore
`2sqrt[(p_1^2+q_1^2)(p_2^2+q_2^2)]+2(p_1p_2+q_1q_2)`.
The identity `(p_1^2+q_1^2)(p_2^2+q_2^2)=(detP-detQ)^2+4D_2(P,Q)^2`
proves (7). Homogeneity multiplies its right side by `detE` for general `E>0`.
These congruences act only inside the proof of an active matrix inequality;
they do not rotate the measured physical basis. ∎

## 4. Nonlinear endpoint capacity

Let `p_+=detK`, `p_-=det(I-K)`, and define

    U_+=B*K^(-1)B,                 U_-=B*(I-K)^(-1)B,
    P=sqrt(p_+) U_+,              Q=sqrt(p_-) U_-,
    C_end=detP+detQ+sqrt[(detP-detQ)^2+4D_2(P,Q)^2].              (8)

Assume first `rank B=2`, so all four matrices are positive. Empty and full
atom differentiation gives scores `-tr(EU_-)` and `tr(EU_+)`. Consequently

    FI(E) >= [tr(EP)]^2+[tr(EQ)]^2 >= 2 det(E) C_end.            (9)

The last inequality uses (7), replacing `E` by `-E` for a negative definite
direction. Combining with (5) proves (1). The complete remainder is explicitly

    -H''=sum_(z neither empty nor full)(p'_z)^2/p_z
          +{[tr(EP)]^2+[tr(EQ)]^2-2 det(E)C_end}
          +2 det(E)(C_end-J),                                  (10)

where the first two terms are nonnegative for definite `E`.
Thus `C_end>=J` certifies the entire active Hermitian Hessian. This is a
sufficient certificate, not an assertion that extreme atoms always suffice.
If `rank B<2`, every physical tangent has rank at most one, its atoms are
affine, and ordinary Shannon concavity settles the degenerate case.

A convenient finite-dimensional upper bound avoids summing the face table.
If `kappa=lambda_max(L)/lambda_min(L)>=1`, every positive two-by-two Schur
complement of `L` has eigenvalues `mu<=nu` in the same spectral interval.
For example its variational minimum formula proves the lower bound and the
choice of zero eliminated coordinates proves the upper bound. Hence

    log[p_10 p_01/(p_00 p_11)]
       <= log[(kappa+1)^2/(4kappa)] =: psi(kappa) <= log kappa,
    J <= det(B*B) psi(kappa).                                   (11)

Indeed `ab<=((mu+nu)/2)^2`, `detS=mu nu`, and
`(rho+1)^2/(4rho)` increases for `rho>=1`; finally `sum c_ij=det(B*B)`.
Thus `C_end>=det(B*B)psi(kappa)` is a complete explicit certificate over
arbitrary strict diagonal noise, including signed active bases.

## 5. Proof of the convex tube theorem

For the balanced-noise isometry model (2), let

    d=detN, d_0=det(I-N),
    T=[trN-2d]/(2sqrt(d d_0)) >=1.

The full and empty probabilities are `2^(-(n-2))d` and
`2^(-(n-2))d_0`; the response matrices are `N^(-1)` and `(I-N)^(-1)`.
Direct two-by-two determinants give

    detP=detQ=2^(2-n),  D_2(P,Q)=2^(2-n)T,
    C_end=2^(3-n)(1+T).                                        (12)

This is precisely where the endpoint mixed areas pay Fisher information.
Let `lambda_1<=lambda_2` be the eigenvalues of `N`. Matrix interval (2) gives
`epsilon/2<=lambda_1<=3epsilon/2` and
`1/2-epsilon/2<=lambda_2<=1/2+epsilon/2`. Since `epsilon<=1/16`,

    T >= (lambda_2-lambda_1)/sqrt(lambda_1)
      >= (1/2-2epsilon)/sqrt(3epsilon/2) >=1/(4sqrt(epsilon)),
    C_end >=2^(1-n)/sqrt(epsilon),    kappa<3/epsilon.             (13)

The first bound uses `lambda_2(1-lambda_2)<=1/4` in the denominator of `T`;
the last follows by bounding physical eigenvalues between `epsilon/2` and
`1/2+epsilon/2` and converting them to odds. Put
`x=2^(-2n)/sqrt(epsilon)>=1`. Equations (11)–(13), together with
`log3<2`, `log2<3/4`, and `log x<=x-1`, imply

    C_end-J > 2^(n+1)x - (3n+2x)
             >= 2^(n+1)-3n-2 >=0.                             (14)

The final elementary inequality holds at `n=2` and increases thereafter.
All preceding bounds are independent of `V`. Equations (1),(14) prove (3)
and settle definite directions; (5) settles every other direction. Integrating
the nonpositive Hessian along any segment inside the convex interval (2)
proves the theorem. No changing-zero atom limit is needed: all kernels here
remain strictly between zero and one, including the active interval boundary.

## 7. Limits and corrected exploratory route

The result imports X040's exact Bernoulli determinant transform but proves
its nonlinear endpoint compensation here. It does not claim a new proof of
the zero-background rank-two theorem, X046's linear design regions, or all
semidefinite three-site directions. The tube theorem is an explicit quantitative
family with arbitrary embeddings and fractional backgrounds. Historical
priority is not claimed. All specialized ingredients above are proved directly.

A two-ray row geometry initially suggested a separate tensor-channel theorem;
C061/X041 already contain the necessary refinement, so no novelty is claimed
for that route. The stronger universal endpoint assertion `C_end>=J` is
unproved and should not be inferred. Outside the displayed certificate, the
exact remaining inequality is still `FI(E)>=2det(E)J`. Other nonlinear events
may provide additional mixed-area Fisher terms, with their covariance losses
retained. This proof uses matrix mixed areas; ordinary mixed volumes are not
silently assigned to sign-changing shifted inverse matrices.
