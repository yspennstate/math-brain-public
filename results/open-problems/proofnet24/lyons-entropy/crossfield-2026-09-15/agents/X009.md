# X009 — forced Schrödinger bridges and a positive cube-curvature remainder

Status: COMPLETE bounded creative attempt. A convex stochastic-control representation, an exact nonnegative bridge-curvature identity, its stronger stationary subtraction, and a determinant-specific force certificate are proved. General Lyons concavity remains unresolved.

The key outcome is not an identification of an affine DPP path with an ordinary bridge: such an identification is impossible for a fixed finite reversible reference chain. Instead, the actual DPP marginals are realized by a uniquely selected positive Markov control with an explicit force. A nonnegative part of their entropy curvature is isolated exactly, and the remaining determinant lemma is stated in Section 7.

All logarithms are natural. Shannon entropy is `H(p)=-sum_x p_x log p_x`; write `F=-H`. Cube states are `x in {0,1}^n`, and `x^i` denotes flipping coordinate `i`. The fixed reference generator is

    (L u)(x)=sum_i [u(x^i)-u(x)].

Every unoriented cube edge is counted once unless explicitly indicated otherwise.

## 1. Why the force cannot be omitted

Let `L` be the generator of a finite reversible continuous-time Markov chain, reversible with respect to a strictly positive measure `m`. An ordinary Schrödinger bridge over `[0,T]` has one-time law

    p_t(x)=m(x) (exp(tL) f)(x) (exp((T-t)L) g)(x),       (1)

with fixed endpoint potentials. Because `L` is self-adjoint in `L^2(m)`, it has a real eigenbasis. Every atom in (1) is a finite real exponential sum

    sum_ab c_ab(x) exp((lambda_a-lambda_b)t).

A fixed stochastic readout from a finite latent state preserves that property.

**Theorem 1 (polynomial obstruction).** If all observed atoms of such a bridge are polynomials in `t` on a nonempty open interval, they are constant. Therefore a nonconstant affine-kernel DPP law cannot be an ordinary Schrödinger bridge for a fixed finite reversible continuous-time reference chain, even with a finite latent-state readout, in the original affine parameter.

**Proof.** Combine equal exponents in a scalar identity `sum_j c_j exp(alpha_j t)=P(t)`. Differentiate more times than the degree of `P`. The terms with nonzero distinct exponents remain linearly independent, so their coefficients vanish. Only a zero-exponent constant can remain. Each atom of `P_(K+tA)` is a determinant with affine entries and thus a polynomial of degree at most `n`. Apply the scalar fact atom by atom. ∎

The same proof excludes a fixed time-independent real killing potential added to the reversible generator: the resulting self-adjoint matrix still has a real eigenbasis. This does not exclude time-dependent reference chains or potentials, time reparametrization, zero-temperature limits, or infinite reference spaces. It also allows a changing kernel whose occupation law happens to stay constant. The construction below uses a Feynman–Kac potential and retains its full curvature contribution.

## 2. A positive Markov control for the exact DPP marginals

The construction first applies to any `C^2` strictly positive probability curve `p(t)`, including every affine DPP path on a compact interval of strict kernels. Put `v=p'`. Orient each cube edge `e=(x,y)` arbitrarily, write `Delta_e theta=theta_y-theta_x`, and let `B` be its incidence matrix, with `-1` at the tail and `+1` at the head.

Define the convex function

    Phi_p(theta)=sum_e 2 sqrt(p_x p_y) [cosh(Delta_e theta)-1].

**Theorem 2 (canonical entropic control).** There is a unique `theta`, modulo addition of a constant, minimizing

    Phi_p(theta)-sum_x v_x theta_x.                     (2)

It depends smoothly on `(p,v)` in the positive simplex. Set

    j_e=2 sqrt(p_x p_y) sinh(Delta_e theta),
    w_e=2 sqrt(p_x p_y) cosh(Delta_e theta),
    G=B diag(w_e) B^T.                                  (3)

Then `Bj=v`, and `G` is strictly positive on the subspace perpendicular to constants. The positive directed flows

    q_xy=sqrt(p_x p_y) exp(theta_y-theta_x),
    q_yx=sqrt(p_x p_y) exp(theta_x-theta_y)                (4)

realize the prescribed curve as an actual continuous-time Markov law, using rates `k_xy=q_xy/p_x`.

**Proof.** On a fixed gauge subspace, the Hessian of (2) is `G`, which is positive definite because every `w_e>0` and the cube is connected. The objective is coercive: if the range of `theta` grows, a path between its maximum and minimum contains an edge whose difference grows linearly with that range; its cosh term eventually dominates the linear term. Thus a unique minimizer exists. Its Euler equation is `Bj=v`. The implicit-function theorem gives smooth dependence because `G` is invertible on the gauge subspace. The flows in (4) have difference `j_e`, and their forward master equation is exactly `p'=Bj`. ∎

The reference rates equal one in each direction. Among all positive directed Markov flows with the same instantaneous `(p,v)`, (4) uniquely minimizes the relative-entropy rate

    A_control(p,v)=inf_(B(q_+-q_-)=v)
       sum_(directed x~y) [q_xy log(q_xy/p_x)-q_xy+p_x].   (5)

Indeed, introducing a multiplier `chi` gives `q_xy=p_x exp(chi_y-chi_x)`. The choice `chi=theta+(1/2)log p` turns this into (4). Strict convexity in the directed flows proves optimality; alternatively, subtracting the candidate value yields a sum of nonnegative scalar relative entropies. Thus the control is selected by a genuine convex action, rather than an arbitrary positive-rate repair of a signed current.

## 3. Convex symmetric action and the forced bridge equations

Define

    Hamiltonian h(p,theta)
      =sum_e [2 sqrt(p_x p_y) cosh(Delta_e theta)-p_x-p_y],

    Lagrangian L_ent(p,v)=sup_theta [v dot theta-h(p,theta)]. (6)

For fixed `theta`, `h` is concave in `p`, since the geometric mean is concave. Hence `L_ent` is jointly convex in `(p,v)`. Equations (2) and (6) have the same maximizing `theta`. Direct substitution into (5) gives

    A_control(p,v)=(1/2) v dot log p+L_ent(p,v),          (7)

    L_ent(p,v)=Phi_p^*(v)+sum_e (sqrt(p_x)-sqrt(p_y))^2.

The first term in (7) integrates to `(F(p_T)-F(p_0))/2`. Thus, with endpoints fixed, the symmetric action in (6) and the directed control action in (5) have identical minimizers.

Put

    f_x=sqrt(p_x) exp(-theta_x),
    g_x=sqrt(p_x) exp(theta_x),
    kappa_x=partial_(p_x) h
      =sum_(y~x) [sqrt(p_y/p_x) cosh(theta_y-theta_x)-1],
    V=theta'+kappa.                                     (8)

Then the factors obey the exact forced equations

    f'=Lf-Vf,                 g'=-Lg+Vg,                (9)

and `p=fg`. To check (9), note that

    v_x/(2p_x)=sum_(y~x) sqrt(p_y/p_x) sinh(theta_x-theta_y),

and add/subtract `theta'_x` in the logarithmic derivatives of `f,g`.

**Convex variational consequence.** The prescribed curve minimizes

    integral [L_ent(p,p')+sum_x V_x(t) p_x(t)] dt         (10)

among curves with the same endpoints. This is a forced Schrödinger variational problem.

**Proof.** At the constructed path, `partial_v L_ent=theta` and `partial_p L_ent=-kappa`. Convexity gives a global supporting-plane inequality for any competing `(p_bar,v_bar)`. Integrate its linear terms, use the equal endpoints, and integrate `theta dot (p_bar'-p')` by parts. The remainder is

    integral (-kappa+V-theta') dot (p_bar-p) dt=0.

This proves the minimizing property directly. The finite positive-state construction also has the usual path-space interpretation: (5) is the jump-likelihood relative entropy rate, and the linear term in (10) corresponds to a Feynman–Kac factor `exp(-integral V(t,X_t)dt)`. No external regularity theorem is needed here. Adding a spatially constant function to `V` changes only normalization/gauge, not the curve or any formula below. ∎

A convex action by itself does not prove Shannon concavity: the potential `V` in (10) may do entropy-decreasing work. The next sections calculate that work exactly.

## 4. A fully nonnegative cube bridge-curvature identity

Fix arbitrary positive `f,g` with `p=fg` normalized. At the instant under consideration, let the *unforced* bridge derivatives be

    v=g Lf-f Lg,
    a_0=g L^2 f+f L^2 g-2(Lf)(Lg),                     (11)

where products are pointwise. Define its relative-entropy curvature

    C(p,theta)=sum_x v_x^2/p_x+sum_x (a_0)_x log p_x.

For positive numbers `a,b`, put

    D(a||b)=a log(a/b)-a+b >=0.

**Theorem 3 (positive cube formula).**

    C(p,theta)=sum_x p_x sum_(ordered i,j)
       [ D(f_(x^i^j)/f_x || f_(x^i) f_(x^j)/f_x^2)
        +D(g_(x^i^j)/g_x || g_(x^i) g_(x^j)/g_x^2) ].  (12)

Here `i=j` is included, and `x^i^i=x`. In particular, `C>=0` in every dimension.

**Proof.** Write `F=sum p log f+sum p log g`. Consider the first summand `phi=sum fg log f` under `f'=Lf,g'=-Lg`. For `r_i=f_(x^i)/f_x`, integration by parts using symmetry of `L` gives

    phi'=-sum_x p_x sum_i zeta(r_i),
    zeta(r)=r log r-r+1.

For an arbitrary time-dependent function `U`, the same calculation gives

    d/dt sum_x p_x U_x
       =sum_x p_x [U'_x-sum_i r_i (U_(x^i)-U_x)].

Apply it to `U=sum_i zeta(r_i)`. Since

    r_i'=r_i [(Lf)_(x^i)/f_(x^i)-(Lf)_x/f_x],

the local second-derivative expression is

    sum_(i,j) [ r_i zeta(r_ij/r_i)-r_i zeta(r_j)
                 -log(r_i)(r_ij-r_i r_j) ],
    r_ij=f_(x^i^j)/f_x.

Cube flips commute, so `r_ij=r_ji`. Expanding `zeta` and pairing the ordered `(i,j)` and `(j,i)` terms cancels the antisymmetric expression `r_i r_j(log r_i-log r_j)` and leaves

    sum_(i,j) [r_ij log(r_ij/(r_i r_j))-r_ij+r_i r_j],

which is the `f` half of (12). Time reversal interchanges `f` and `g`; their second-derivative contributions have the same form. Adding them proves (12). ∎

This is proved directly from the commuting involutions of the cube. No continuous-space Ricci theorem or zero-temperature bridge theorem is being imported onto the DPP path.

For the actual forced curve, differentiating (3) shows

    p''=B[ j_e (v_x/p_x+v_y/p_y)/2+w_e Delta_e theta' ].

For the unforced curve, `theta'=-kappa`, so

    p''-a_0=G V.                                       (13)

Consequently the exact entropy-curvature formula is

    -H''=C(p,theta)+sum_e w_e Delta_e V Delta_e log p.    (14)

Thus the potential can also be recovered from the prescribed two-jet by solving `GV=p''-a_0`, modulo constants. This construction does not infer the force from an already assumed entropy sign.

## 5. Stronger theorem: subtract all stationary bridge curvature

The positive expression (12) admits a more informative lower bound that vanishes when `v=0`. On each square face write its four atom probabilities as `p_a,p_b,p_c,p_d`, with `a=00,b=10,c=01,d=11`, keeping outside coordinates fixed. Define

    alpha=sqrt(p_a p_d),           beta=sqrt(p_b p_c),
    u=theta_d-theta_a,             vface=theta_c-theta_b,
    hface=theta_a+theta_d-theta_b-theta_c,
    U=beta-alpha-alpha log(beta/alpha),
    W=alpha-beta+beta log(beta/alpha).                   (15)

Both `U,W` are nonnegative by `r-1-log r>=0` and `r log r-r+1>=0` for `r>0`. No log-submodularity assumption is needed for this sign.

Set

    E_edge=sum_e j_e^2 (1/p_x+1/p_y),

    R_excess=8 sum_faces {
        U [cosh(u)-1]+W [cosh(vface)-1]
       +[beta cosh(u)+alpha cosh(vface)]
                       [cosh(hface)-1] }.              (16)

Every summand in (16) is nonnegative.

**Theorem 4 (stationary-subtracted curvature).** In every dimension and for every positive `p,theta`,

    C(p,theta)-C(p,0)=E_edge+R_excess >= E_edge.         (17)

**Proof.** The `i=j` terms in (12), after pairing the endpoints of every edge, equal

    E_edge+2 sum_e (p_x-p_y) log(p_x/p_y).

The second term is independent of `theta`. For a fixed unordered pair `i<j` and fixed outside coordinates, summing the `i,j` and `j,i` contributions over its four vertices gives

    8{ U cosh(u)+W cosh(vface)
          +[beta cosh(u)+alpha cosh(vface)]
                            [cosh(hface)-1] }.

This follows by substituting (15) into the two scalar divergences in (12); terms linear in `hface sinh(u)` or `hface sinh(vface)` cancel between opposite vertices. At `theta=0`, the same face expression is `8(U+W)`. Subtracting that expression yields exactly its contribution in (16), while subtracting the diagonal terms leaves `E_edge`. ∎

An explicit expression for the subtracted value is

    C(p,0)=2 sum_e (p_x-p_y) log(p_x/p_y)
       +8 sum_faces (beta-alpha) log(beta/alpha).        (18)

Both terms in (18) are nonnegative. Importantly, the entire stationary curvature can be removed, leaving the still-nonnegative terms in (17). This avoids treating a fixed nonuniform distribution as if it carried a nonzero velocity cost.

## 6. Exact specialization to the affine DPP acceleration

Now let `p=P_K`, with `0<K<I`, and let the two-jet be taken along the actual affine Hermitian path `K+tA`. On a face `B={i,j}`, with outside configuration `T` on `R=B^c`, use the X002 quantities

    M=K_R-D_(R\T),
    q=(-1)^|R\T| det M,
    V_DPP=A_BB-A_BR M^-1 K_RB,
    Q_DPP=q Re det(V_DPP),
    Lambda=log[p_b p_c/(p_a p_d)]=2 log(beta/alpha).     (19)

The notation `V_DPP` is the generally non-Hermitian conditioned directional matrix; it is distinct from the scalar forcing potential `V`. X002 proves, by determinant row replacement,

    p''=2 sum_faces div_i div_j Q_DPP,
    Lambda>=0.                                        (20)

For the matched unforced bridge, (11) has the exact face expansion

    a_0=2 sum_i [p-p( . ^i)]
        +2 sum_faces div_i div_j Q_bridge,

    Q_bridge=f_a g_d+g_a f_d-f_b g_c-g_b f_c
       =2 alpha cosh(u)-2 beta cosh(vface).             (21)

**Proof of (21).** Write `L=sum_i L_i`, `L_i=flip_i-I`. The terms with two copies of `L_i` in (11) reduce to `2(p-p^i)`. Since the flips commute, the contribution of the pair `i<j` reduces pointwise to

    2[g f^(ij)+f g^(ij)-f^i g^j-f^j g^i].

On its square this has values `2Q_bridge` at `a,d` and `-2Q_bridge` at `b,c`. This proves (21). ∎

Remove the same bridge acceleration at zero velocity. The resulting *dynamic bridge acceleration* is

    a_dynamic=a_0(p,theta)-a_0(p,0)
       =2 sum_faces div_i div_j DeltaQ_bridge,

    DeltaQ_bridge=2 alpha[cosh(u)-1]
                    -2 beta[cosh(vface)-1].            (22)

Define the calibrated force potential `Omega`, uniquely modulo constants, by

    G Omega=p''-a_dynamic
       =2 sum_faces div_i div_j [Q_DPP-DeltaQ_bridge].   (23)

This is an explicit weighted Poisson equation on the cube. Its right side is known from the determinant two-jet and the unique convex minimizer (2). In terms of the original forcing, `Omega=V+G^dagger a_0(p,0)`, modulo constants. It subtracts the forcing needed merely to hold the same density stationary.

**Theorem 5 (DPP entropy-curvature certificate).** Every strict affine Hermitian DPP path obeys

    -H''=E_edge+R_excess
                 +sum_e w_e Delta_e Omega Delta_e log p                (24)

         =E_edge+R_excess
                 +2 sum_faces [DeltaQ_bridge-Q_DPP] Lambda.

Consequently, with `D_G(z)=sum_e w_e(Delta_e z)^2`,

    -H'' >= E_edge+R_excess
                     -sqrt(D_G(Omega) D_G(log p)).       (25)

In particular either of the explicit conditions

    sum_e w_e Delta_e Omega Delta_e log p >=0,          (26)

or

    D_G(Omega) D_G(log p) <= (E_edge+R_excess)^2         (27)

certifies `H''<=0`. The sufficient facewise condition `Q_DPP<=DeltaQ_bridge` also implies (26), because every `Lambda>=0`.

**Proof.** Subtract (11) at `theta=0` from the matched unforced bridge acceleration. The stationary bridge has zero first derivative, so its curvature is `C(p,0)=a_0(p,0) dot log p`. Combining (17), (22), and (23) gives

    F''=C(p,theta)-C(p,0)+(G Omega) dot log p,

which is (24). Twice summing a face divergence against `log p` gives `-Lambda`, proving the second equality. Inequality (25) is Cauchy–Schwarz in the positive Dirichlet form defined by `G`. The remaining assertions follow. ∎

These conditions are computable without knowing the desired entropy sign. They are sufficient certificates, not claimed universal. They involve a nonlocal optimal control and a weighted Poisson solution, so they do not have the face-local real-current limitation established in X002.

## 7. Exact remaining lemma and what the circulation example means

The strongest remaining determinant statement is now precisely

    2 sum_faces [Q_DPP-DeltaQ_bridge] Lambda
                                  <= E_edge+R_excess.   (28)

All quantities in (28) are explicitly defined above; the right side is a sum of nonnegative edge and face terms. Proving (28) for every strict `K,A` would prove the full local entropy-concavity assertion and hence the global conjecture by integration and continuity. This report does not prove (28). The norm condition (27) is stronger than necessary and is not proposed as the universal missing lemma.

At a real symmetric base and in a purely imaginary Hermitian direction, all measured scores vanish, so the minimizer (2) is `theta=0`. Then

    j=0,  E_edge=0,  R_excess=0,  DeltaQ_bridge=0,
    G Omega=p'',
    -H''=Omega dot G log p=-2 sum_faces Q_DPP Lambda.    (29)

Thus the hidden imaginary circulation is retained exactly as a nonzero force at zero kinetic action. A proof that bounds the force solely by the velocity action, or asserts that an entropic bridge can be chosen with zero force, would fail to address precisely this case.

For X002's physical three-site example

    K=I/2+(ones-I)/10,
    A=i [[0,1,10],[-1,0,0],[-10,0,0]],

the face conditioned on `X_3=1` has `Q_DPP=1/2>0`, while `DeltaQ_bridge=0`. The proposed facewise sufficient condition fails there. Nevertheless the global calibrated force work is positive, about `34.2691413774`, and it is the entire negative entropy Hessian. This confirms that the convex control retains the required cross-face compensation. It does not itself prove that compensation has the same sign for every real base in every dimension.

The Cauchy certificate is also not universally necessary: in this example it gives a negative lower bound even though the exact curvature is positive. That limitation is expected when a norm discards the favorable sign of the force work.

## 8. Primary-source hypotheses and scope

The ordinary bridge factorization (1) is the finite reversible specialization of the `(f,g)` transform in Christian Léonard, *On the convexity of the entropy along entropic interpolations*, Sections 1 and 4, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1274>. The source assumes a stationary Markov reference and suitable endpoint factors. Here the state space is finite, the reference is reversible, all factors on the working interval are strictly positive, and all derivatives are finite matrix expressions; the integrability and domain conditions are automatic. Formula (1) and its spectral obstruction use that setting only.

Giovanni Conforti's *A second order equation for Schrödinger bridges with applications to the hot gas experiment and entropic transportation cost*, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04821>, motivates tracking force rather than discarding acceleration. No manifold theorem from that source is used in this proof.

Paul-Marie Samson, *Entropic curvature on graphs along Schrödinger bridges at zero temperature*, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05179>, studies a distinguished zero-temperature bridge and its endpoint-coupling hypotheses. Its curvature theorem is not applied to the generally signed affine DPP transport. In particular the ordinary finite-temperature obstruction of Section 1 is not asserted for Samson's limiting bridges.

The DPP input (19)–(20) is the exact elementary row-replacement calculation in [X002](X002.md), for the occupation law from Russell Lyons, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2707>. Strict `0<K<I` makes every atom positive and every signed principal atom matrix in (19) invertible. The `V_DPP` nonsymmetry is retained through `Re det V_DPP`; it has not been replaced by a Hermitian matrix.

Equations (2)–(29), including the convex variational consequence and the nonnegative cube-curvature formula, are proved directly in finite dimensions above. No historical novelty claim is made; the campaign contribution is the exact forced-control reduction, its stationary subtraction, and its compatibility with the latent determinant acceleration.
