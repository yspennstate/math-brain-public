# X012 — Mixed Plücker identities and cross-sector entropy compensation

Date: 2026-09-15. Assignment: use mixed discriminants/Plücker identities to compensate the four-single-site entropy loss identified in X003. This is a creative derivation, not a proof of unrestricted Lyons concavity.

**Main established result.** Two arbitrary complex rank-two projections on four measured sites satisfy the midpoint Lyons inequality if one forbids the pair `{3,4}` and the other forbids the complementary pair `{1,2}`. More strongly, their full balanced-splitter occupation entropy increases by the explicit quantity in (7). This theorem uses an actual compensation between sectors; its hypotheses still permit a strictly negative four-single-site sector.

A second established theorem covers every rank-two projection endpoint against every Gaussian reservoir having at least seven particles almost surely, with a dimension-independent positive midpoint gap. Its proof is an elementary degree-two fourth-moment estimate.

Neither theorem covers arbitrary rank-two projection pairs. The exact remaining compensation inequality is stated at the end. No historical-priority claim is made.

## 1. Exact sector decomposition for two rank-two projections

Let `U,V` be `n by 2` isometries and let

\[
P=UU^*,\qquad Q=VV^*,\qquad
a_{ij}=\det U_{\{i,j\}},\qquad b_{ij}=\det V_{\{i,j\}}\quad(i<j).
\]

Extend the coefficients antisymmetrically. Cauchy–Binet gives

\[
\sum_{i<j}|a_{ij}|^2=\sum_{i<j}|b_{ij}|^2=1.
\]

The input occupation probabilities are `|a_ij|²` and `|b_ij|²`. A balanced beam splitter acts between corresponding sites of the two registers. Let `R` denote its full measured output law. The two output marginal kernels are both `(P+Q)/2`, and hence

\[
2H\!\left(P_{(P+Q)/2}\right)-H(P_P)-H(P_Q)
=\bigl[H(R)-H(P_P)-H(P_Q)\bigr]+I(R_A;R_B).
\tag{1}
\]

Only classical mutual information occurs in (1). Establishing a nonnegative full-output entropy gain is sufficient, though stronger than needed.

The splitter preserves every local total `N_i=n_{A,i}+n_{B,i}`. In interleaved Fock order `(A1,B1,A2,B2,...)`, its restriction to singly occupied pairs is a tensor product of two-dimensional balanced rotations. The fixed total particle number is four, so each local-total sector has one of the following forms.

* Two doubled sites: the sector contains one occupation configuration, unchanged by the splitter.
* One doubled site `i` and two single sites `j,k`: two initial amplitudes, denoted `x,y`, with magnitudes and relative phase equal to `a_ij b_ik` and `a_ik b_ij` up to fixed signs.
* Four single sites `I`: six initial amplitudes, indexed by the first-register two-set `S subset I`.

For a triple sector put

\[
M=|x|^2+|y|^2,\quad p=|x|^2/M,\quad r=2\operatorname{Re}(x\bar y)/M.
\]

Writing `h(p)=-p log p-(1-p)log(1-p)`, its exact entropy gain is

\[
G_{i;jk}=M\left[\log2+h\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)-h(p)\right]\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, the four output probabilities are `|x+y|²/4` twice and `|x-y|²/4` twice. The output conditional entropy is at least `log 2`, while the input conditional entropy is at most `log 2`. When one of `x,y` vanishes, (2) is exactly `2M log 2`.

For a four-set `I`, use its inherited order and put

\[
c_S=\operatorname{sgn}(S,I\setminus S)a_Sb_{I\setminus S},\quad
F_I(\varepsilon)=\sum_{|S|=2}c_S\prod_{i\in S}\varepsilon_i,\quad
M_I=\sum_{|S|=2}|c_S|^2.
\]

Here `epsilon_i` are independent uniform signs and the sign is the exterior shuffle sign. Its output probabilities are `2^{-4}|F_I(epsilon)|²`, and its exact entropy gain is

\[
D_I=4M_I\log2+\sum_{|S|=2}|c_S|^2\log|c_S|^2
-\mathbb E|F_I|^2\log|F_I|^2.
\tag{3}
\]

All expressions use the unnormalized sector probabilities; the sector-mass entropy terms cancel. Therefore the full identity is

\[
\boxed{H(R)-H(P_P)-H(P_Q)=\sum_{|I|=4}D_I+\sum_{i;\{j,k\}}G_{i;jk}.}
\tag{4}
\]

A zero-mass sector contributes zero. Formula (4) retains exactly the compensation that is absent from a sectorwise monotonicity argument.

## 2. Two elementary binary-entropy inequalities

For `0<=p<=1` and `-1<=r<=1`,

\[
h(p)\le2\log2\sqrt{p(1-p)},
\qquad
\log2-h\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)\le r^2\log2.
\tag{5}
\]

For completeness, the second follows from

\[
\log2-h\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{r^{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}
\le r^2\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{2k(2k-1)}=r^2\log2.
\]

To prove the first, by symmetry put `2p-1=tanh x`, `x>=0`. Then

\[
h(p)=\log(2\cosh x)-x\tanh x.
\]

Let `f(x)=x coth x-log(2 cosh x)`. Its derivative is

\[
f'(x)=\frac{\tanh x-x}{\sinh^2x}\le0,
\]

and its limit at infinity is zero. Thus `f>=0`. It follows that

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\{\cosh x\log(2\cosh x)-x\sinh x\}
=\sinh x\log(2\cosh x)-x\cosh x\le0.
\]

The expression in braces is at most its value `log 2` at zero. Dividing by `cosh x` proves the first inequality in (5), including endpoints by continuity.

These bounds provide separate estimates for loss of a classical component label and loss from interference.

## 3. A compensated four-site theorem

**Theorem A.** Let `P,Q` be arbitrary complex rank-two projections on four measured sites. Suppose, after one common permutation of the coordinates,

\[
a_{34}=0,\qquad b_{12}=0.
\tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, `P_P({3,4})=0` and `P_Q({1,2})=0`. Define

\[
\alpha=|a_{12}|^2,\qquad\beta=|b_{34}|^2.
\]

Then the balanced splitter satisfies

\[
\boxed{H(R)-H(P_P)-H(P_Q)
\ge 2\log2\left[\alpha+\beta
-2\sqrt{\alpha\beta(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}\right]\ge0.}
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{H\!\left(P_{(P+Q)/2}\right)
\ge\frac{H(P_P)+H(P_Q)}2
+\log2\left[\alpha+\beta
-2\sqrt{\alpha\beta(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}\right].}
\tag{8}
\]

The bracket is at least `2 alpha beta`. It vanishes only when `alpha=beta=0`. In particular, (8) has a positive gap whenever at least one of `a12,b34` is nonzero. Arbitrary phases are permitted. Generically the two ranges span all four dimensions and the projections do not commute.

### 3.1 The Plücker factorization

The two Plücker relations are

\[
a_{12}a_{34}-a_{13}a_{24}+a_{14}a_{23}=0,
\qquad
b_{12}b_{34}-b_{13}b_{24}+b_{14}b_{23}=0.
\]

Under (6), each cross-block matrix has rank at most one, so write

\[
a_{ij}=u_i v_j,\qquad b_{ij}=w_i z_j
\quad(i\in\{1,2\},\ j\in\{3,4\}).
\tag{9}
\]

Zero matrices cause no difficulty; choose a zero factor. The four-single-site polynomial from (3) becomes

\[
F(\varepsilon)=C\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2+L(\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2)\,M(\varepsilon_3,\varepsilon_4),
\tag{10}
\]

where

\[
C=a_{12}b_{34},\quad
L=u_1w_2\varepsilon_1-u_2w_1\varepsilon_2,\quad
M=-v_3z_4\varepsilon_3+v_4z_3\varepsilon_4.
\]

Put `a=|C|²=alpha beta` and

\[
B=\mathbb E|LM|^2
=(|u_1w_2|^2+|u_2w_1|^2)
 (|v_3z_4|^2+|v_4z_3|^2).
\]

Since the cross-block coefficient squared norms are `1-alpha` and `1-beta`,

\[
B\le(1-\alpha)(1-\beta).
\tag{11}
\]

This is the precise low-rank bridge used by the proof. It is a Plücker consequence in the measured coordinate basis; no unitary change of measurement basis is performed.

### 3.2 Bounding both kinds of entropy loss

Let `q=2^{-4}|C epsilon1 epsilon2+LM|²` be the sector output. Let

\[
q_0=2^{-4}(a+|LM|^2)
\]

be the distribution obtained by removing only the interference term. All these distributions have mass `a+B`.

The map `epsilon -> (-epsilon1,-epsilon2,epsilon3,epsilon4)` fixes the constant-modulus component `C epsilon1 epsilon2`, negates `LM`, and pairs the output probabilities. On a pair put `b=|LM|²` and

\[
r=\frac{2\operatorname{Re}(C\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2\overline{LM})}{a+b}.
\]

The second inequality in (5), averaged over these pairs, gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(q_0)-H(q)
&\le\log2\,\mathbb E\frac{4ab}{a+b}\\
&\le 2\log2\sqrt a\,\mathbb E|LM|\\
&\le 2\log2\sqrt{aB}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

In the second line we used `2 sqrt(ab)<=a+b`, and in the third Cauchy–Schwarz. The first equality underlying this estimate is the binary entropy deficit within each fixed-mass pair, so there is no unmatched cross-entropy term.

Let `D_product` be the balanced entropy gain for the four cross coefficients alone, whose Fourier polynomial is `LM`. Because this is a product of two two-variable linear polynomials,

\[
D_{\rm product}=\|M\|_2^2D_L+\|L\|_2^2D_M\ge0
\tag{13}
\]

by the two-coefficient formula (2).

The input sector coefficient list consists of the single atom of mass `a` and this cross-product list of total mass `B`. If one retains their component label, the output entropy of `q0` is `H(q_C)+H(q_product)`. Erasing that label costs at most

\[
(a+B)h\!\left(\frac{a}{a+B}\right)\le2\log2\sqrt{aB}
\]

by the first inequality in (5). The flat component `q_C` gains `a log16` relative to its single input atom. Combining with (12),

\[
\boxed{D_{\{1,2,3,4\}}
\ge D_{\rm product}+4a\log2-4\log2\sqrt{aB}.}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus this sector is allowed to lose entropy. Its possible loss has been explicitly bounded in terms of Plücker-factor masses.

### 3.3 The other sectors pay this bound

Among the one-doubled-site sectors, consider those with the double site in `{1,2}`, the other single site of that pair, and one site from `{3,4}`. Their two candidate amplitudes are `a12 b_ij` and an amplitude containing `b12=0`. Their entropy gains sum to

\[
2\log2\,\alpha(1-\beta).
\]

The analogous sectors with the doubled site in `{3,4}` have a candidate amplitude containing `a34=0`. Their gains sum to

\[
2\log2\,\beta(1-\alpha).
\]

All other triple-sector gains are nonnegative by (2). Therefore (4), (11), and (14) imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(R)-H(P_P)-H(P_Q)
&\ge4\alpha\beta\log2
 +2\log2[\alpha(1-\beta)+\beta(1-\alpha)]\\
&\hspace{12mm}-4\log2\sqrt{\alpha\beta(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}\\
&=2\log2[\alpha+\beta-2\sqrt{\alpha\beta(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}].
\end{aligned}
\]

This is (7). Nonnegativity follows more explicitly by rewriting the bracket as

\[
2\alpha\beta+
\left(\sqrt{\alpha(1-\beta)}-\sqrt{\beta(1-\alpha)}\right)^2.
\]

Classical subadditivity in (1) proves (8).

The theorem gives ordinary midpoint inequalities on a class with four-dimensional active support. By continuity, every pair with a strictly positive displayed gap has a neighborhood of endpoint pairs, including strict contractions, for which the midpoint inequality remains valid. This does not claim concavity along their whole segments.

## 4. Even real Slater inputs can require compensation

The following identity and example delimit how Theorem A can be extended.

For a four-site sector of real decomposable forms put

\[
A=c_{12}c_{34},\quad B=c_{13}c_{24},\quad C=c_{14}c_{23}.
\]

Define `X=a12 a34`, `Y=-a13 a24`, `X'=b12 b34`, `Y'=-b13 b24`. By the Plücker relations the third signed products are `-X-Y` and `-X'-Y'`. Direct expansion proves

\[
\boxed{A^2+B^2+C^2-2AB-2AC-2BC=(XY'-YX')^2\ge0.}
\tag{15}
\]

For complex forms the same polynomial identity holds, but its right side is a complex square and has no nonnegative-real interpretation. This discriminant excludes the uniform positive six-amplitude Dicke configuration from real inputs. It does **not** prove real sector monotonicity.

Here is an exact real counterfamily. Take unnormalized row matrices

\[
U_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\\0&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&0\\0&1\\t&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad 0<t\le1/32,
\tag{16}
\]

and orthonormalize their columns. Their unnormalized minors in order `(12,13,14,23,24,34)` are

\[
a=(1,1,1,1,1,0),\qquad b=(0,1,-1,1,-1,-t).
\]

The normalized forms divide these by `sqrt 5` and `sqrt(4+t²)`. They obey the hypotheses of Theorem A. The unnormalized four-single-site polynomial is

\[
F=(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2)(\varepsilon_3+\varepsilon_4)-t\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2.
\]

Its coefficients are `(-t,1,1,-1,-1,0)`. Its sixteen unnormalized output probabilities are `t²/16` twelve times and `(4+t)²/16`, `(4-t)²/16` twice each. Put

\[
g(x)=(1+x)^2\log(1+x)+(1-x)^2\log(1-x).
\]

The unnormalized sector entropy gain is exactly

\[
D(t)=\frac{t^2}{2}\log t+3t^2\log2-4g(t/4).
\tag{17}
\]

For `|x|<=1/2`, `g''(x)=6+2 log(1-x²)>=4`, so `g(x)>=2x²`. Hence for `0<t<=1/32`,

\[
D(t)\le t^2\left(\frac12\log t+3\log2-\frac12\right)
\le\frac{t^2}{2}(\log2-1)<0.
\tag{18}
\]

Dividing by `5(4+t²)` gives the actual negative sector contribution for the two normalized projection inputs. Thus the Plücker discriminant can vanish while entropy decreases inside the four-single-site sector.

For this entire family, the sum of the triple-sector gains is exactly

\[
\frac{8(1+t^2)\log2}{5(4+t^2)}.
\]

The complete full-output gain is therefore

\[
\frac{D(t)+8(1+t^2)\log2}{5(4+t^2)},
\]

which is nonnegative by Theorem A. This supplies an explicit example where the new theorem genuinely uses cross-sector compensation.

## 5. A second theorem: two particles against at least seven

**Theorem B.** Let `P` be any rank-two projection and let `0<=K<=I` be a Hermitian contraction whose Gaussian particle count is at least seven almost surely. Then

\[
\boxed{H\!\left(P_{(P+K)/2}\right)
\ge\frac{H(P_P)+H(P_K)}2+\frac12\log\frac{128}{123}.}
\tag{19}
\]

The particle-count condition is equivalent to `K` having at least seven eigenvalues equal to one. In particular, it includes every rank-at-least-seven projection, with arbitrary coherent overlap with `P`. The result is not restricted to commuting endpoints or to four-dimensional active support.

The stronger measured theorem behind (19) does not require independent or Gaussian inputs: any joint state supported on `N_A=2`, `N_B>=7` gains at least `log(128/123)` in its full occupation-measurement entropy under the balanced splitter.

### 5.1 An elementary fourth-moment bound

For complex coefficients `c_ij` on pairs of `m` signs let

\[
f(\varepsilon)=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j,\qquad
s^2=\sum_{i<j}|c_{ij}|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E|f|^4\le(15-12/m)s^4.}
\tag{20}
\]

To prove this, replace signs by independent standard real Gaussians one coordinate at a time. Conditionally on the others, `f=A x+B`. Since signs and Gaussians have identical first, second and third moments, while their fourth moments are one and three, replacing that coordinate raises the fourth absolute moment by `2|A|⁴>=0`.

For real coefficients the final Gaussian polynomial is `g^T T g`, where `T` is real symmetric and has zero diagonal, hence `tr T=0`. Standard Gaussian moments, obtained by expanding products of independent centered squares, give

\[
\sigma^2=\mathbb E(g^TTg)^2=2\operatorname{tr}T^2,\qquad
\mathbb E(g^TTg)^4=3\sigma^4+48\operatorname{tr}T^4.
\]

For each eigenvalue `lambda_i`, the trace-zero constraint and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

\[
\lambda_i^2=\left(\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j\right)^2
\le(m-1)\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j^2,
\]

so `max lambda_i² <=(m-1) tr(T²)/m`. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{tr}T^4\le\frac{m-1}{m}(\operatorname{tr}T^2)^2,
\]

which yields (20) for real coefficients. For complex coefficients write the polynomial as `X+iY`. The real bound and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[
\mathbb E(X^2+Y^2)^2
\le(15-12/m)\{\mathbb E X^2+\mathbb E Y^2\}^2.
\]

Gaussian and sign second moments both equal `sum|c_ij|²`, completing the proof. No specialized hypercontractivity theorem is imported.

The estimate also covers arbitrary mixed states on the weight-two slice. If `R` is a trace-one positive matrix on that slice, write

\[
F_R(\varepsilon)=\sum_j\lambda_j|f_j(\varepsilon)|^2
\]

using its spectral decomposition into unit coefficient vectors. Pointwise convexity gives

\[
\mathbb EF_R=1,\qquad\mathbb EF_R^2\le15-12/m.
\]

The output orientation law is `q(epsilon)=2^{-m}F_R(epsilon)`. Jensen for the logarithm under weights `2^{-m}F_R` proves

\[
H(q)=m\log2-\mathbb E[F_R\log F_R]
\ge m\log2-\log(15-12/m).
\tag{21}
\]

The input measured entropy is at most `log binom(m,2)`. For every integer `m>=9`,

\[
\frac{2^m}{\binom m2(15-12/m)}
=\frac{2^{m+1}}{(m-1)(15m-12)}\ge\frac{128}{123}>1.
\tag{22}
\]

At `m=9` this is equality. The ratio increases thereafter: the inequality comparing consecutive denominators reduces to `15m²-57m+24>0`, valid for `m>=4`.

### 5.2 Sector proof and the Gaussian consequence

Dephase the joint state in the preserved local totals. In a nonzero sector let `d` be the number of doubled sites and `m` the number of single sites. The first-register constraint gives `d<=2` and first-register single-site weight `2-d`. Moreover

\[
N_B=m+2d-2\ge7.
\]

* If `d=0`, then `m>=9`. The conditional state is an arbitrary mixed state on the weight-two slice. Its entropy gain is at least `log(128/123)` by (21)–(22).
* If `d=1`, then `m>=7`. The conditional state has one excitation. The elementary bound from X003 gives gain at least `log(2^m/(3m-2))`, which exceeds `log(128/123)` in this range. For reference, that bound follows by expanding `E(epsilon^T R epsilon)² <=3-2/m`, then applying exactly the Jensen step used in (21).
* If `d=2`, then `m>=5`. The input orientation is deterministic, and the output is uniform on all `2^m` orientations. Its gain is `m log2`, again larger.

The sector weights are preserved and sum to one. The entropy chain rule proves the full measured statement with uniform gain `log(128/123)`.

Apply this to the independent Gaussian inputs with kernels `P,K`. They satisfy the count support hypotheses, and both output marginal kernels equal `(P+K)/2`. Classical subadditivity gives (19). Finally, a finite Gaussian particle count is the sum of independent Bernoulli eigenmode occupations; its minimum possible value is exactly the number of unit eigenvalues. This proves the stated equivalent condition on `K`.

This theorem has a sufficient threshold of seven, not a claimed optimal threshold. The elementary moment estimate above does not settle the weight-two orientation entropy problem for `m=5,6,7,8`.

## 6. Sources, verification, and exact remaining gap

The target statement is Russell Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures*, Conjecture 2.6, https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2707. The task's naming assumption remains the one recorded in `TARGET.md`: this is the recovered Lyons conjecture, although the user originally called the target “Eliyahu's conjecture.”

For fermionic conventions I checked the primary source Xingjian Lyu and Kaifeng Bu, *Fermionic Gaussian Testing and Non-Gaussian Measures via Convolution*, Section II, equations II.1–II.3, https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08180v2. Its convolution is defined for even states; gauge-invariant Gaussian inputs here are even, and the fixed-count states used in the direct argument are handled in interleaved Fock order. The balanced angle is explicitly `pi/4`. Gaussian closure under the beam splitter gives the affine covariance marginals. No entropy inequality from that paper is imported, and no von Neumann entropy is substituted for measured Shannon entropy. Pure Gaussian projection inputs are included directly by their Slater amplitudes, so no strict-positivity assumption is needed. The binary bounds, Plücker algebra, and Gaussian fourth-moment estimate have all been proved above.

`computations/X012/check.py` verifies:

* the rank-two/rank-seven measured theorem on a fixed-seed complex nine-site pair, enumerating all 829 admissible local-total sectors and reproducing the midpoint DPP marginal to `1.91e-17`;
* Theorem A on six fixed-seed complex endpoint pairs, each with active support rank four and a nonzero commutator;
* the mixed-state fourth-moment bound in four selected dimensions;
* the exact real decomposable obstruction at `t=1/32` and the vanishing discriminant in (15).

Its output is saved in `computations/X012/check-results.json`. The finite checks support the algebra; neither theorem relies on them. Exploratory scripts are preserved as `probe.py`, `real_sector_opt.py`, and `weight_two_opt.py`; their optimizations are observations, not universal proofs. In particular the tentative real-sector monotonicity idea was disproved and corrected by (16)–(18).

For arbitrary rank-two projection inputs the remaining sufficient inequality is exactly

\[
\boxed{\sum_{|I|=4}D_I+\sum_{i;\{j,k\}}G_{i;jk}\ge0}
\tag{23}
\]

with the normalized decomposable forms and explicit functions in (2)–(3). On four sites, removing (6) makes the cross-block Plücker matrices have nonzero determinants. The factorization (10) then fails, and the proof does not bound the additional interfering terms. In higher dimensions, even local four-site bounds need a valid allocation of the triple-sector gains because a triple belongs to several quadruples. Equation (15) alone cannot replace either missing step.

The weaker inequality obtained by adding `I(R_A;R_B)` to (23) would already suffice for the rank-two midpoint Lyons statement. Neither unrestricted version is proved here. The original signed-mixed-discriminant Hessian problem for inertia `(2,1)` also remains open: the endpoint theorems in this report do not imply it.
