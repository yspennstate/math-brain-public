## 1. Full atom scores and a positive Fisher operator

Fix 0<K<I, set p=p_K, and let Y_S=(K-diag(1_{j notin S}))^(-1). The determinant atom formula gives

    s_A(S)=d log p_S[A]=tr(Y_S A),
    p''_S[A,A]=p_S[(tr(Y_S A))^2-tr(Y_S A Y_S A)].       (1)

Thus E_p Y=0. Polarizing (1) and differentiating sum p=1 twice gives

    J_K(U,V)=E_p[s_U s_V]=E_p tr(Y_S U Y_S V).

For nonzero A, work on the fixed active space R=ran A; all subsequent Y matrices mean their compression to R. Define

    Phi(D)=E_p[Y_S D Y_S],    J(D)=tr(D Phi(D))=E_p s_D^2,
    V_f=E_p[f_S Y_S],         E_p f=0.                  (2)

Then V_sD=Phi(D). These are full atom scores, including imaginary Hermitian velocities. The map Phi is completely positive because its Kraus matrices are sqrt(p_S)Y_S; consequently J(U,V)>=0 for U,V>=0. Compression does not diagonalize the measured law or change the affine kernel path.

## 2. Exact logsum-curvature dual, with a unique optimizer

Set

    beta_R(A)=min {J(D): D=D*, D>=A, D>=-A}.             (3)

X013 proved attainment and saturation. Here are the additional statements, with the needed existence facts included for completeness.

**Theorem 1.** The minimizer D_* is unique and positive definite on R. For centered real atom potentials,

    beta_R(A)=max_{V_f>=0}
      {2 tr|V_f^(1/2) A V_f^(1/2)|-Var_p(f)}.           (4)

The unique maximizing potential is f_*=s_D*. Its quadratic penalty is exactly a logsum Hessian:

    Var_p(f)=[d^2/dt^2 log E_p exp(tf)]_(t=0).

Equivalently, omitting zero-variance potentials,

    beta_R(A)=max_{V_f>=0}
      [tr|V_f^(1/2) A V_f^(1/2)|]^2/Var_p(f).           (5)

One may restrict (4) to potentials f=s_B with B>=0 without changing the maximum. Writing V_*=Phi(D_*)>0, the optimizer is characterized by

    D_*=V_*^(-1/2)|V_*^(1/2) A V_*^(1/2)|V_*^(-1/2),
    D_* Phi(D_*) D_*=A Phi(D_*) A.                      (6)

In fact D_* is the unique positive definite solution of the cubic equation in (6). Feasibility need not be imposed separately on a positive definite solution. It also satisfies A D_*^(-1) A=D_*.

**Proof.** Every feasible D is positive semidefinite. If Dx=0, positivity of D±A forces Ax=0, so D>0 on ran A. Since Cov_p(s_D,|S|)=tr D and Var_p(|S|)=tr[K(I-K)],

    J(D)>=(tr D)^2/tr[K(I-K)].

A minimizing positive-cone sublevel set is compact. The count identities follow by differentiating E|S|=tr K and using Cov(X_i,X_j)=-|K_ij|^2 for distinct sites. This proves attainment directly.

For any positive definite V, a change of variables proves the elementary linear-majorant formula

    min_{D>=±A} tr(VD)=tr|V^(1/2) A V^(1/2)|.           (7)

Indeed set B=V^(1/2)AV^(1/2) and Z=V^(1/2)DV^(1/2). If P_± are the spectral projections of the invertible B, then

    tr Z-tr|B|=tr[P_+(Z-B)]+tr[P_-(Z+B)]>=0.

Equality makes the positive-semidefinite compressions P_+(Z-B)P_+ and P_-(Z+B)P_- vanish. A positive-semidefinite matrix with zero compression annihilates that subspace, forcing Z=|B|; hence the linear minimizer is unique. The inequality in (7) for singular V>=0 follows by replacing V with V+epsilon I and passing to zero.

Convex first-order optimality for (3) says tr[Phi(D_*)(D-D_*)]>=0 for every feasible D. Also Phi(D_*)>0: the empty atom alone contributes a positive definite term, since its compressed score is negative definite and D_*>0. Formula (7) therefore gives the first equation in (6). Any other feasible D has

    J(D)-J(D_*)=2 tr[V_*(D-D_*)]+J(D-D_*),

whose first term is strictly positive unless D=D_*, proving uniqueness. Squaring the first equation in (6) gives the cubic equation. Conversely, for any D>0 satisfying the cubic equation, set V=Phi(D)>0; the positive matrix V^(1/2)DV^(1/2) squares to (V^(1/2)AV^(1/2))^2. It is therefore the absolute value in (6), hence feasible and a linear minimizer. The displayed quadratic expansion proves it minimizes (3). The saturation identity follows from B|B|^(-1)B=|B|.

For every feasible D and centered potential with V_f>=0, score-square completion and (7) give

    J(D)>=2 tr(V_f D)-Var_p(f)
         >=2 tr|V_f^(1/2) A V_f^(1/2)|-Var_p(f).

Taking D=D_* and f=s_D* makes both inequalities equalities, proving (4). More precisely, with a_f=tr|V_f^(1/2) A V_f^(1/2)|,

    beta_R(A)-(2a_f-Var_p f)
       =E_p(f-s_D*)^2+2[tr(V_f D_*)-a_f].              (8)

Both terms are nonnegative, proving uniqueness of the centered dual potential. Positive rescaling of f proves (5). Since D_*>0, restricting to scores of positive matrices keeps the maximizer. No minimax, SDP-duality, or matrix-sign theorem is imported. ∎

**Exact non-spectral example.** Let

## 3. The optimal entropy-potential jet

Return here to the uncompressed score matrices, allowing all Hermitian directions. For a smooth real potential field F_S(Q), define the actual variational representative

    G_F(Q)=log sum_S exp(F_S(Q))-sum_S p_Q(S)F_S(Q).

It satisfies G_F(Q)=H(p_Q)+D(p_Q||q_F(Q))>=H(p_Q). Suppose F_S(K)=log p_S, and put u_S=F'_S(K)[A]. Direct differentiation gives

    G_F''(K;A)=H''(K;A)+Var_p(u-s_A).                  (10)

To verify this without discarding a term, the logsum contributes Var_p(u)+E_p F''; differentiating the expectation contributes sum p''log p+2sum p'u+E_p F''. The two second-potential terms cancel, and E_p s_A=0 completes the square. Thus score matching, modulo constants, is the unique best first jet. The affine choice F_S(K+B)=log p_S+s_B(S) touches with the exact full entropy Hessian; its KL excess is O(||B||^4).

That affine choice need not be concave away from its touching point. Already for one site with reference k=1/10, at

the exponential surrogate is Bernoulli 1/2, and G_F''(q)=700/81>0. This is an exact obstruction to globally freezing the optimal first jet, not an entropy counterexample.

## 4. A moment-matched supporting envelope and the remaining lemma

In this section use the reference K's **full** centered atom-score space, allowing all Hermitian directions before any active-space compression. Let c_1,...,c_d be a basis of that space and c(S)=(c_j(S)). Set

    psi(theta)=log sum_S p_S exp(theta·c(S)),
    m(Q)=E_pQ c,    q_theta(S)=p_S exp(theta·c(S)-psi(theta)).

For every strict Q, there is a unique theta(Q) with E_qtheta c=m(Q). Indeed m(Q) is in the relative interior of the feature polytope because all p_Q(S)>0; the independent centered features affinely span R^d. Thus psi(theta)-theta·m is coercive and strictly convex. Its Hessian is the positive definite feature covariance, so the optimizer varies smoothly.

**Theorem 2 (adaptive supporting envelope).** The explicit function

    G_K(Q)=psi(theta(Q))-theta(Q)·m(Q)-E_pQ log p

satisfies G_K(Q)>=H(p_Q) and G_K(K)=H(p_K). Write C_Q=Cov_qtheta(c), v=m'(Q)[A], and F_Q(S)=log p_S+theta(Q)·c(S). Its exact Hessian is

    G_K''(Q;A)=-v^T C_Q^(-1)v-sum_S p''_Q(S)[A,A]F_Q(S).  (11)

**Proof.** It is the infimum of the entropy variational expression over F=log p+theta·c, so its excess is the nonnegative relative entropy to q_theta. Differentiating the matching equation gives theta'[A]=C_Q^(-1)v. Differentiating the optimized expression twice gives (11). At Q=K, every s_A is in the feature span, and the matching equation yields v^T C_K^(-1)v=J_K(A), recovering the entire Fisher curvature. ∎

This construction adapts the potential rather than asserting that a frozen logdet table is concave.     v^T C_Q^(-1)v+sum_S p''_Q(S)[A,A]F_Q(S)>=0.         (12)
