# X039 — An infinite positive-Vandermonde flag family

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: COMPLETE exact restricted-family theorem.** The full Lyons conjecture remains open. This report gives an explicit polynomial diamond for every parameter in a halfline, then a full noncommuting active-covariance concavity theorem. It does not claim full-rank measured kernels or arbitrary positive flags.

## 1. The family and completed conclusions

Fix the six measured coordinates, write
\[
x=(0,1,2,3,4,t),\quad t\ge6,\qquad B_k=(x_i^{j})_{0\le i\le5,\,0\le j<k},
\]
and let \(W=\operatorname{ran}B_2\), \(V_4=\operatorname{ran}B_4\), \(U=V_4\cap W^\perp\), \(Z=V_4^\perp\). Each of \(W,U,Z\) has dimension two. If \(R\) is increasing, put
\[
\Delta_R=\prod_{i<j\in R}(x_j-x_i),\quad s_R=\sum_{i\in R}x_i,
\quad G_k=\sum_{|R|=k}\Delta_R^2=\det(B_k^TB_k).
\tag{1}
\]
Every maximal minor of \(B_k\), for \(k=2,4\), is strictly positive. Thus both planes lie in the positive Grassmannians in the given coordinate order; orthonormalization changes all their maximal minors by one common positive factor. This is the elementary Vandermonde version of a positive flag, with no imported network theorem.

**Theorem 1.** For every \(t\ge6\), the flag \(W\subset V_4\) has the explicit nonnegative diamond coupling (6) below, supported on at most 48 of the 90 ordered pair partitions \((A,J,B)\) of the six coordinates. Its bottom law is \(\mu_W\), its top law is \(\mu_{V_4}\) on \(A\cup J=B^c\), and its aggregate middle law is \(\mu_{W+u}+\mu_{W+v}\) for any orthonormal basis \((u,v)\) of \(U\). The addition-pair law is exactly \(\mu_U\). All three pair marginals have all fifteen atoms positive.

**Theorem 2.** For every fixed \(t\ge6\) and every isometry \(V:\mathbb C^2\to U_{\mathbb C}\), the map
\[
\boxed{M\longmapsto H(P_{P_W+VMV^*})\quad\text{is concave on }0\le M\le I_2.}
\tag{2}
\]
This is an affine-convex, four-real-dimensional family in the original measured basis; \(M\) may be complex Hermitian and the endpoints may fail to commute. At strict active covariances and definite active directions \(A\),
\[
H''[A,A]\le-\bigl(8-2\log2-4\log3\bigr)\det A<0.
\tag{3}
\]
The constant is positive. For example, \(M_0=\operatorname{diag}(1/4,1/3)\) and \(M_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/2&1/16\\1/16&1/2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) are strict, do not commute, and have positive definite difference of determinant \(29/768\). The measured six-site kernels have fixed eigenvalues \(1,1,0,0\) outside the active plane; no full-rank conclusion is inferred.

The positive-coefficient certificate, rather than generic continuity of a feasible point, is the contribution. Prior C004 already establishes that continuity principle; the active-covariance implication is credited to `ROOT_DIAMOND_ACTIVE_MATRIX.md` and X017 and is restated with its proof below. Single phase frames have at most nine rank-two atoms on six sites, whereas this family has fifteen. No coordinate has zero mass in any of its three planes, so C038's zero-coordinate gluing does not apply. No classification against every possible tree-factor representation is claimed; the construction imposes no such factorization hypothesis.

## 2. A positive determinant formula for the middle marginal

Write \(a_A=\mu_W(A)\), \(c_B=\mu_Z(B)=\mu_{V_4}(B^c)\), and \(g_M=\mu_{W+u}(M)+\mu_{W+v}(M)\). Set \(D=G_2G_4>0\). Then
\[
\boxed{Da_A=\Delta_A^2G_4,\quad Dc_B=\Delta_{B^c}^2G_2,
\quad Dg_M=\Delta_M^2\sum_{|N|=3}\Delta_N^2(s_M-s_N)^2.}
\tag{4}
\]
The first two formulas are Cauchy–Binet and complementary projection minors. Here is a self-contained derivation of the third, including its normalization.

Let \(P_2,P_4\) be the projections onto \(W,V_4\). The coefficient linear in \(P_U=P_4-P_2\) in a three-by-three determinant gives
\(g_M=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}((P_2)_M)(P_U)_M)\). Since \(P_2\) has rank two, its own contribution to this trace vanishes, so \(P_U\) can be replaced by \(P_4\). Put \((r_M)_i=(-1)^{\operatorname{position}(i)-1}\Delta_{M-i}\) for \(i\in M\). Two-by-two cofactors give \(\operatorname{adj}((P_2)_M)=r_Mr_M^T/G_2\). Expansion of Vandermonde determinants gives
\[
r_M^T(B_4)_M=\pm\Delta_M(0,0,1,s_M).
\]
With \(\Gamma=B_4^TB_4\), this proves
\(Dg_M=\Delta_M^2(0,0,1,s_M)\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)(0,0,1,s_M)^T\).
The cofactor Cauchy–Binet formula writes \(\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma)=\sum_{|N|=3}q_Nq_N^T\), where \(q_N\) is the signed three-row cofactor vector. Its last two entries are \(\Delta_N(-s_N,1)\), up to a common sign: these are the last coefficients of \(\prod_{i\in N}(z-x_i)\). Substitution proves (4). The cofactor formula itself follows by expanding the coefficient of \(z\) in \(\det(\Gamma+zww^T)\) and applying ordinary Cauchy–Binet after appending the row \(\sqrt z\,w^T\).

This formula retains the measured basis. It is not a statement about affine odds matrices, eigenvalue entropy, or an arbitrary unitary rotation of the observations.

## 3. Explicit polynomial coupling on the entire halfline

Put \(q=t-6\ge0\). The denominator is the polynomial with increasing coefficient vector
\[
D(q)=(272643840,542183040,465283280,225227200,67740700,
13067200,1589800,112000,3500).
\tag{5}
\]
Here and in the coefficient table, a vector denotes its scalar polynomial in increasing powers of \(q\). For each row \((A,J)\) in the complete table below, let \(C_{A,J}(q)\) be its displayed polynomial, put \(B=(A\cup J)^c\), and set
\[
\boxed{x_{A,J,B}(t)=C_{A,J}(t-6)/D(t-6).}
\tag{6}
\]
Every unlisted partition has weight zero. All displayed coefficients are nonnegative integers, so (6) is nonnegative for every \(t\ge6\), with no parameter-by-parameter feasibility problem.

The exact polynomial identities certified by this table are
\[
\sum_{J,B}C_{A,J}=\Delta_A^2G_4,\qquad
\sum_{A,J:(A\cup J)^c=B}C_{A,J}=\Delta_{B^c}^2G_2,
\]
\[
\sum_{A,J}\sum_{j\in J}\mathbf1_{\{A+j=M\}}C_{A,J}
=\Delta_M^2\sum_N\Delta_N^2(s_M-s_N)^2.
\tag{7}
\]
They can be checked by multiplication and addition of the displayed integer coefficients. Dividing by \(D\) and using (4) proves the three required marginal equations. Summing the bottom equations gives total mass one.

The addition marginal follows without an extra assumption. For any diamond table, elementary indicator counting gives
\[
h_J=a_J+\sum_{B\cap J=\varnothing}c_B-\sum_{M\supset J}g_M.
\tag{8}
\]
For this flag, the right side is \(\det((P_U)_J)\): use the rank-three projection inclusion probabilities for the last sum, then expand the two-by-two determinants of \(P_W+uu^T\), \(P_W+vv^T\), and \(P_W+P_U\). This proves the claimed fourth marginal. At \(t=6\), its minimum is \(5/1932>0\). Since its numerator is a sum of the nonnegative polynomials in (6), all fifteen addition atoms stay positive for every \(t\ge6\). The endpoint laws are already positive by Vandermonde products.

This finishes Theorem 1. For individually equal middle labels, take a real orthonormal pair \(u,v\), replace it by \((u+iv)/\sqrt2,(u-iv)/\sqrt2\), and orient each sampled diamond fairly. Real determinant amplitudes show that each of those two projection laws is exactly \(g/2\); no measured-coordinate rotation is performed.

## 4. Why this diamond proves full active concavity

This section restates the credited active-covariance argument, rather than claiming it as new. Let \(a,b\) be the bottom/top laws, let \(e_M=(\det[W,u]_M,\det[W,v]_M)\), and put \(m_M=\|e_M\|^2/2\). Exterior orthonormality gives \(\sum_Me_M^*e_M=I_2\). For \(d=\det M\), \(d_0=\det(I-M)\), \(B=M-dI\), the three possible cardinality laws are
\[
p_S=d_0a_S,\quad p_M=e_MB e_M^*,\quad p_T=db_T.
\tag{9}
\]
Write \(\delta=\det A\), \(\mathcal I=\sum(p'_X)^2/p_X\), \(T_0=\operatorname{tr}B/(2\sqrt{dd_0})\ge1\), \(\nu_M=p_M/\operatorname{tr}B\), and \(\beta_*=2H(m)-H(a)-H(b)\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
H''=-\mathcal I+2\delta\Lambda,\qquad
\Lambda=2\log(2T_0)-\beta_*-2D(m\Vert\nu).
\tag{10}
\]
The diamond has \(\Lambda\ge0\): for strict DPP odds, the two-by-two Schur complement gives \(p_Sp_{S+i+j}\le p_{S+i}p_{S+j}\); average its negative logarithm over (6). Regularizing the full kernel and taking the finite supported limit covers its fixed projection background. Thus \(\delta\le0\) gives \(H''\le0\).

For \(\delta>0\), reverse \(A\) if necessary so \(A>0\). The two extreme cardinalities in (9), Cauchy–Schwarz, and the two-dimensional trace–determinant inequality give
\(\mathcal I\ge4(1+T_0)\delta\). Explicitly, apply them to \(\operatorname{adj}(M)/\sqrt d\) and \(\operatorname{adj}(I-M)/\sqrt{d_0}\), whose determinants equal one and whose sum has determinant \(2+2T_0\).
Fairly orient the sampled additions \(i,j\), and write \(L=S+i\). Ordinary entropy chain rules give
\(\beta_*=\log2+H(S\mid T)-2H(S\mid L)+I(i;j\mid S)\ge\log2-2\log3\), because \(S\) is one of the three pairs in \(L\). Hence \(\Lambda\le2\log T_0+\log2+2\log3\). Substitution in (10), followed by \(1+T_0-\log T_0\ge2\), proves (3). Together with the indefinite case it proves (2) on the strict active domain; continuity of finite entropy gives its boundary. This proves Theorem 2.

## 5. Certificate, provenance, and the precise remaining gap

The table has 315 nonzero coefficients and 48 potentially nonzero diamond atoms. `computations/X039/certificate.json` contains all 90 rows, including zeros. `verify_exact.py` independently checks 594 coefficient identities and 40 exact orthogonal-polynomial determinant identities, plus all 810 coefficient signs; it uses no optimization. `verified_output.json` records the passing result. Run `python computations/X039/verify_exact.py` from the campaign directory. The degree-eight identities prove an infinite family; they are not numerical tests at finitely many parameters.

`polynomial.py` preserves discovery provenance: it solved nine finite coefficient transport systems and recovered their solutions over the rationals. Every resulting coefficient is an integer. `explore.py` retains an earlier strictly positive 90-atom point at \(t=6\); that point and generic continuity are not the proof of the halfline theorem.

The coefficient method has a concrete boundary: expanding (4) about \(t=5\) gives coefficient \(-1000\) at degree two in the middle numerator for \(M=234\), so coefficientwise transport from that expansion is impossible. This does not refute a coupling below six. More generally, arbitrary positive row weights cannot be inserted while demanding coefficientwise positivity in every weight monomial: when row \(i\) is multiplied by \(\sqrt{w_i}\), at nodes \((0,1,2,3,4,6)\) the all-six-weights-once coefficient of the forced addition numerator for \(J=02\) is \(-562176\). The actual determinantal law remains nonnegative; other monomials compensate this coefficient.

The open extension is a positive path-switch or polynomial decomposition for arbitrary increasing six-node configurations, and then arbitrary positive flags. Nothing here supplies a full-rank fractional-background curvature bound. The elementary Vandermonde and cofactor arguments are self-contained. The general problem is Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures*, Conjecture 2.6, https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2707. The prior diamond calculus and limitations are credited above; no literature-wide priority claim is made. No subagents, pushes, or shared report edits were used.
