# X018 — Harmonic transport through the six-site complement

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: COMPLETE bounded theorem and exact harmonic-flow reduction. General six-site positivity remains unproved.

The assigned flag is an orthogonal decomposition \(\mathbb C^6=W\oplus U\oplus Z\) into three two-planes. A diamond is a partition into ordered pairs \((A,J,B)\), with bottom \(A\), addition pair \(J\), complementary top \(B\), and middle triples \(A+j\), \(j\in J\). The requested marginals are \(a=|\wedge^2W|^2\), \(h=|\wedge^2U|^2\), \(c=|\wedge^2Z|^2\), and the aggregate rank-three law \(g=\mu_{W+u}+\mu_{W+v}\), where \((u,v)\) is any orthonormal basis of \(U\).

q_m(\{a_s,b_t,c_v\})=(-1)^{s+t+v},
\]

and vanish on other triples. Five such vectors form a basis; each has an eight-atom signed pair-preserving lift. C038 further derives the necessary, sharp inequality

\[
|q_m\cdot g|\leq 1-\sum_{J\in m}h_J.
\tag{1}
\]

Its determinant-specific proof was left open there. C037 rules out fixed Gram sums of squares in bilinear endpoint Pluecker coordinates. This report does not reinstate that ansatz.

## Initial checkpoint (the proposed cube bound is proved below)

Let \(Q_m\) be the diagonal operator with entries \(q_m(M)\) on \(\wedge^3\mathbb C^6\). Let \(V=U^\perp\), and define the self-adjoint operator on \(\wedge^2V\)

\[
T_m(U)=\left.\bigl(\iota_u Q_m(u\wedge\cdot)+\iota_v Q_m(v\wedge\cdot)\bigr)\right|_{\wedge^2V},
\tag{2}
\]

where contraction is the adjoint of exterior multiplication. Then for the unit decomposable volume form \(w\) of \(W\),

\[
q_m\cdot g=\langle w,T_m(U)w\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

This is an exact identity, simply by expanding both middle laws in the coordinate exterior basis. It is independent of the chosen orthonormal basis of \(U\).

The proposed bounded route is to understand the spectrum, or the decomposable numerical range, of (2). A sufficient lemma for (1) is

\[
\|T_m(U)\|\leq 1-\sum_{J\in m}|(u\wedge v)_J|^2.
\tag{4?}
\]

No validity claim is made for (4) at this initial checkpoint. If it fails, the weaker exact decomposable bound will be investigated only far enough to obtain a proved finite reduction or a delimited theorem. Even all fifteen matching inequalities do not alone settle the joint five-dimensional harmonic support-function inequalities of C038.

## Completed result: scope

The following proves all fifteen determinant-specific matching-cube inequalities that C038 left open. It gives exact sums-of-squared-minors slacks for every harmonic linear combination, the exact spectral support for fixed update plane, and an explicit flow correcting all five harmonic moments while preserving the three pair marginals. The flow has an exact positivity condition and a sharp Euclidean capacity estimate. These results do not prove that arbitrary endpoint marginals supply that capacity.

Additional source read: C004's signed full right inverse. The new harmonic inverse below isolates its five-dimensional part by a direct Johnson adjacency calculation. No specialized representation-theory or spectral-variation theorem is imported without proof.

## 1. Complement-oddness with every lower-degree cancellation

For \(q\in\mathcal H\), the pair equations are
\[
\sum_{M\supset J}q_M=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Summing over all pairs containing a fixed site counts every triple containing that site twice; hence every singleton sum is zero. Summing over all pairs counts every triple three times, so the total sum is zero.

For any Hermitian matrix \(P\), put \(F_q(P)=\sum_{|M|=3}q_M\det P_M\). The expansion
\[
\det(I-P)_M
=1-\sum_{i\in M}P_{ii}
+\sum_{\substack{J\subset M\\|J|=2}}\det P_J-\det P_M
\]
and the total, singleton, and pair cancellations prove
\[
\boxed{F_q(I-P)=-F_q(P).}
\tag{6}
\]
This holds for arbitrary \(P\), not just projections.

Applying inclusion-exclusion to the sum of \(q_N\) over triples disjoint from a fixed triple \(M\), all lower-order sums vanish, leaving
\[
q_{M^c}=-q_M.
\tag{7}
\]

## 2. The five determinant moments linearize in the bottom projection

For orthogonal two-planes \(R,S\), use the notation
\[
g(R,S)_M=\sum_{s\text{ in an orthonormal basis of }S}
 |(\operatorname{vol}_R\wedge s)_M|^2.
\tag{8}
\]
It is basis-independent. Let \(U=[u,v]\), \(d_J=(u\wedge v)_J\), and \(h_J=|d_J|^2\). Define
\[
C_q(U)=\sum_{|M|=3}q_M\,\operatorname{emb}_M
 \operatorname{adj}((P_U)_M).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(M=(i_1<i_2<i_3)\), put
\[
(r_M)_{i_k}=(-1)^{k-1}\overline{d_{M-i_k}},
\qquad (r_M)_i=0\quad(i\notin M).
\]
The two-by-two cofactor formula gives
\[
\operatorname{emb}_M\operatorname{adj}((P_U)_M)=r_Mr_M^*,
\qquad C_q(U)=\sum_Mq_Mr_Mr_M^*.
\tag{10}
\]
Repeated-column determinant identities give \(r_M\perp U\), including when minors vanish. Thus \(C_q(U)P_U=0\). Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{tr}C_q(U)
=\sum_J h_J\sum_{M\supset J}q_M=0.
\tag{11}
\]
So \(C_q(U)\) is traceless Hermitian on the four-dimensional space \(V=U^\perp\).

**Theorem 1 (harmonic reciprocity).** For every complex six-site flag,
\[
\boxed{q\cdot g(W,U)=-q\cdot g(U,W)
=-\operatorname{tr}(P_W C_q(U)).}
\tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(P_Z\) has rank two, \(F_q(P_Z)=0\). Equation (6) and \(P_W+P_U=I-P_Z\) give \(F_q(P_W+P_U)=0\). Expanding the cubic determinants, terms with three \(W\) columns or three \(U\) columns vanish. By Cauchy-Binet the two remaining terms are \(g(W,U)_M\) and \(g(U,W)_M\). This proves reciprocity after summing against \(q\).

The determinant derivative at the possibly singular matrix \((P_U)_M\) is
\[
D\det(P_U)_M[(P_W)_M]
=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}((P_U)_M)(P_W)_M)
=g(U,W)_M.
\]
Summing proves the trace formula. Every step is a polynomial identity, so no nonzero-minor or interior assumption is required. \(\square\)

A useful unsigned, pointwise identity is
\[
\boxed{g(U,W)_M+g(U,Z)_M=\sum_{J\subset M}h_J.}
\tag{13}
\]
Its left side is \(\operatorname{tr}(r_Mr_M^*(P_W+P_Z))=\|r_M\|^2\), since \(r_M\perp U\); this norm is the right side.

Equivalently, the map \(D_U:x\mapsto u\wedge v\wedge x\), restricted to \(V\), is an isometry with coordinate rows \(r_M^*\). Therefore
\[
\sum_Mr_Mr_M^*=I-P_U,\qquad
C_q(U)=D_U^*\operatorname{diag}(q)D_U.
\tag{14}
\]

## 3. Exact spectral support and squared-minor slacks

Let \(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\lambda_3\ge\lambda_4\) be the eigenvalues of \(C_q(U)|_V\).

**Theorem 2 (fixed-update spectral support).**
\[
\boxed{\max_{\substack{W\subset U^\perp\\\dim W=2}}
 |q\cdot g(W,U)|=\lambda_1+\lambda_2.}
\tag{15}
\]
The signs are attained by the bottom and top two-eigenvector planes, respectively.

**Proof.** In an eigenbasis put \(p_i=(P_W)_{ii}\). These numbers satisfy \(0\le p_i\le1\), \(\sum p_i=2\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(P_W C)=\sum\lambda_i p_i\). Moving available weight to a larger eigenvalue proves that its maximum is \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2\), and minimum \(\lambda_3+\lambda_4\). These are negatives of each other by (11). Eigenplanes attain both. \(\square\)

The weaker positive-part bound \(\operatorname{tr}(C_q(U))_+\) is attained by a two-plane precisely when the positive subspace has dimension at most two and the nonnegative subspace has dimension at least two. Equality requires containing the positive subspace and avoiding the negative one. Inertia \(3+1\) or \(1+3\) can make it strictly weaker than (15).

This also settles the compressed-operator question from the initial checkpoint. For \(B\) on \(V\), write \(d\Gamma(B)(x\wedge y)=Bx\wedge y+x\wedge By\). Equation (12) gives
\[
T_q(U)=-d\Gamma(C_q(U)|_V)
\quad\text{on }\wedge^2V.
\tag{15a}
\]
Indeed their quadratic forms agree on every decomposable two-form. Such forms determine a Hermitian matrix: basis wedges determine diagonal entries; \((e_i+s e_k)\wedge e_j\) determine entries between intersecting pairs; and \((e_i+s e_k)\wedge(e_j+t e_l)\), with complex \(s,t\), separate the coefficients between disjoint pairs. Thus equality on decomposables implies operator equality. The six eigenvalues of (15a) are \(-(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)\), \(i<j\), and occur in opposite pairs because \(\sum\lambda_i=0\). Its norm is exactly \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2\). In particular, the proposed cube operator bound (4) follows from (18)-(19) below.

For general harmonic \(q\), define
\[
\kappa_q(J)=\sum_{M\supset J}(q_M)_+
=\frac12\sum_{M\supset J}|q_M|,
\qquad \beta_q(U)=\sum_Jh_J\kappa_q(J).
\tag{16}
\]
The equality follows from (5). Let \(t=q\cdot g(W,U)\). Equations (12)-(13) give
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\beta_q(U)-t
 &=\sum_{q_M>0}q_M g(U,W)_M
  +\sum_{q_M<0}(-q_M)g(U,Z)_M,\\
\beta_q(U)+t
 &=\sum_{q_M>0}q_M g(U,Z)_M
  +\sum_{q_M<0}(-q_M)g(U,W)_M.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{17}
\]
For example, use \(\beta_q=\sum_{q<0}(-q_M)\sum_{J\subset M}h_J\), insert (13), and use \(t=-q\cdot g(U,W)\) to obtain the first equality. Every term is a nonnegative coefficient times two squared three-by-three minors.

Consequently, simultaneously for every harmonic linear combination,
\[
\boxed{|q\cdot g(W,U)|
\le\lambda_1(C_q(U))+\lambda_2(C_q(U))
\le\beta_q(U).}
\tag{18}
\]
For a particular flag, equality in either signed bound is exactly the vanishing of every positively weighted squared minor in its version of (17). These are inequality slacks, not fixed-Gram endpoint formulas for coupling weights.

## 4. Closing all fifteen matching-cube inequalities

For a matching cube \(q_m\), a matched pair lies in no transversal triple; an off-matching pair lies in exactly two, of opposite sign. Thus
\[
\kappa_{q_m}(J)=1_{\{J\notin m\}}.
\]
Equation (18) proves the formerly open determinant-specific statement
\[
\boxed{|q_m\cdot g(W,U)|
\le\sum_{J\notin m}h_J=1-\sum_{J\in m}h_J.}
\tag{19}
\]
It holds for arbitrary complex flags, with all degeneracies included. Equation (17) supplies both exact squared-minor slacks.

The constant is sharp among actual projection flags. For \(m=01|23|45\), choose coordinate planes \(U=[e_0,e_2]\), \(W=[e_1,e_5]\), \(Z=[e_3,e_4]\). Its unique diamond has addition \(02\), bottom \(15\), and middle triples \(015,125\), whose cube values are \(0,1\). Both sides of (19) equal one.

There is also an explicit small coupling realizing \(h\) and this one harmonic moment. Put \(\beta=\sum_{J\notin m}h_J\), \(t=q_m\cdot g(W,U)\). For each off-matching \(J\), the four values \(q_m(J+r)\), \(r\notin J\), are \(0,0,1,-1\). Choose one fixed zero-valued site. Pair it with the \(-1\) site to obtain bottom \(A_+(J)\), or with the \(+1\) site to obtain \(A_-(J)\); the diamond costs are \(+1,-1\), respectively. Assign weights
\[
h_J\frac{\beta+t}{2\beta},\qquad
h_J\frac{\beta-t}{2\beta}.
\tag{20}
\]
Put the mass of each matched \(J\) on any partition with that addition pair. If \(\beta=0\), (19) gives \(t=0\), and only this matched-pair rule is needed. The weights are nonnegative by (17), and they have addition marginal \(h\) and cube expectation \(t\). **This auxiliary flow does not prescribe the bottom/top marginals or the other four harmonic moments.**

## 5. An explicit simultaneous harmonic flow

Let \(P\) record the three pair marginals on the ninety ordered partitions \(e=(A,J,B)\), and let \(G\) record the two middle vertices. Thus
\[
(G^*\delta)_{A,J,B}=\delta_{A+i}+\delta_{A+j},
\quad J=\{i,j\}.
\]

**Theorem 3 (canonical harmonic right inverse).** For \(\delta\in\mathcal H\), set
\[
\boxed{(\mathcal R\delta)_{A,J,B}
=\frac{\delta_{A+i}+\delta_{A+j}}6.}
\tag{21}
\]
Then \(P\mathcal R\delta=0\), \(G\mathcal R\delta=\delta\). It is the unique correction of minimum Euclidean norm among all signed tables satisfying these equations, and
\[
\|\mathcal R\delta\|_2=\|\delta\|_2/\sqrt6,\qquad
\boxed{\|\mathcal R\delta\|_\infty
\le\frac{\|\delta\|_2}{6\sqrt3}.}
\tag{22}
\]
The latter operator constant is sharp.

**Pair-sum proof.** For fixed \(A\), summing \(G^*\delta\) over its six partitions counts each \(\delta_{A+i}\) three times, giving zero by (5). Equation (7) gives the same result for fixed \(B\). Also
\[
\delta_{A+i}+\delta_{A+j}
=-\sum_{a\in A}\delta_{J+a}.
\tag{23}
\]
The four triple values inside \(A\cup J\) sum to zero: by complementation their negatives are exactly the four triples containing the complementary pair \(B\). For fixed \(J\), each site of \(J^c\) occurs in three bottoms \(A\), so (23) and (5) give zero addition marginal.

**Middle-sum proof.** A triple \(M\) has nine incident diamonds, indexed by \(a\in M,j\notin M\), with other middle \(M-a+j\). Therefore
\[
(GG^*\delta)_M
=9\delta_M+\sum_{a\in M}\sum_{j\notin M}\delta_{M-a+j}
=9\delta_M-3\delta_M=6\delta_M.
\tag{24}
\]
The inner sum is \(-\delta_M\) by (5). This is the highest-harmonic Johnson eigenvalue calculation, proved directly.

If \(z\) is another such correction, \(G(z-\mathcal R\delta)=0\), hence
\(\langle\mathcal R\delta,z-\mathcal R\delta\rangle=0\).
Pythagoras proves uniqueness and minimum norm, and (24) gives the first identity in (22).

The harmonic orthogonal projector has entries, with \(r=|M\cap N|\),
\[
(\Pi_{\mathcal H})_{M,N}=
\begin{cases}
 1/4&r=3,\\-1/12&r=2,\\1/12&r=1,\\-1/4&r=0.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]
Its columns have zero pair sums by counting. For harmonic \(\delta\), its sums over triples meeting a fixed \(M\) in \(3,2,1,0\) sites are
\(\delta_M,-3\delta_M,3\delta_M,-\delta_M\), respectively, by (5) and (7). Thus (25) acts as identity on \(\mathcal H\); symmetry proves that it is the orthogonal projector.

Two middle triples intersect in two sites. Hence
\[
\|\Pi_{\mathcal H}(e_{M_1}+e_{M_2})\|^2
=1/4+1/4-2/12=1/3.
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz in (21) proves the second bound in (22); equality holds for \(\delta\) proportional to this projected vector. \(\square\)

**Determinant-specific positivity criterion.** If a nonnegative table \(y\) has pair marginals \(a,h,c\), the forced-addition identity of C038 makes
\(\delta=g(W,U)-Gy\) harmonic. The explicit table
\[
x=y+\mathcal R\delta
\tag{26}
\]
has all required pair and middle marginals. It is nonnegative exactly when
\[
\boxed{6y_{A,J,B}+\delta_{A+i}+\delta_{A+j}\ge0
\quad\text{on every partition}.}
\tag{27}
\]
If \(y_e\ge m>0\), the sufficient condition
\[
\|\delta\|_2\le6\sqrt3\,m
\tag{28}
\]
supplies a positive repair. This is a closed flow formula, not an LP search. Its five target coordinates are directly determinantal:
\[
q\cdot\delta=-\operatorname{tr}(P_W C_q(U))
-\sum_e y_e(G^*q)_e.
\tag{29}
\]
The five C038 basis cubes reconstruct \(\delta\) from these numbers. The capacity in (27) is not claimed automatic.

## 6. Exact remaining lemmas

For general harmonic \(q\), define \(k_q(J)\) as the sum of the largest two among the four numbers \(q_{J+r}\), \(r\notin J\). They sum to zero, so this is also the maximum absolute sum of two of them. Identity (23) makes it the exact absolute diamond-cost bound at addition \(J\). One has \(k_q(J)\le\kappa_q(J)\), with possible strictness for a \(1+3\) sign split.

The sharper addition-only capacity
\[
\lambda_1(C_q(U))+\lambda_2(C_q(U))
\stackrel{?}{\le}\sum_Jh_J k_q(J)
\tag{30}
\]
is **not proved here** and has been passed to X020. A bounded preliminary eigenvalue check of 500 sampled \(q,U\) found no violation; this numerical observation is not used in any proof.

Even (30) would not establish full diamond feasibility. The exact remaining joint condition is
\[
q\cdot g(W,U)\le
\max_{\substack{y\ge0\\Py=(a,h,c)}}q\cdot Gy
\quad\text{for every }q\in\mathcal H,
\tag{31}
\]
including feasibility of the three-pair polytope. Neither (18)-(19) nor the conditional repair (27) proves (31). A full diamond circulation can succeed when (27) fails, so failure of this particular repair is not a counterexample.

The bounded route is complete: all fifteen cube inequalities, their exact SOS slacks, the harmonic one-body identity, the exact fixed-update spectral support, and the simultaneous harmonic flow with a sharp norm estimate are proved. General six-site coupling and full entropy concavity remain open.
