## 1. New four-site theorem

Let [W,V] be ANY unitary four-by-four matrix, with two columns in each block.
Fix background eigenvalues lambda_1,lambda_2 in [0,1], and set

    K(M)=W diag(lambda_1,lambda_2) W*+V M V*,
    a I_2 <= M <= (1-a) I_2,       0<a<=1/2.

Write h for binary entropy and define the exact spectral-label quantities

    H_Z=h(lambda_1)+h(lambda_2),
    tau=1-product_(j=1,2) max(lambda_j,1-lambda_j),
    g=2/3-(4 tau+H_Z/2)/a^2.                                      (1)

**Theorem.** If g>=0, measured entropy is concave on this entire convex
four-real-dimensional active domain. Every definite Hermitian E satisfies

    -H''_M[E,E] >= g (tr E)^2.                                  (2)

Every indefinite or rank-one E satisfies -H''>=FI_M(E), even without (1).
In particular, with arbitrary four-site measured embedding,

    a=1/4,  1-2^-10 <= lambda_1 <=1,  0<=lambda_2<=2^-10,

one has g>=5/12. Taking both rates strictly between zero and one gives
unequal fractional background eigenvalues and strict physical kernels.
There is no scalar-background, disjoint-guide, or diagonal-noise assumption.
Endpoints may be noncommuting. For definite Delta=M_1-M_0, the Jensen gap
is at least (g/2)t(1-t)(tr Delta)^2. No distant-endpoint conclusion outside
the stated active domain is inferred.

The new ingredient is a quantitative bound on BOTH terms introduced by
forgetting the spectral background label. Conditional projection concavity
alone would not prove this theorem. Section 6 gives an all-dimension version.

## 2. Conditional flags and the complete forgetting identity

Independently sample the two background occupations Z_j~Bernoulli(lambda_j).
Its label probabilities w_z are fixed. Conditional on Z=z the background
is a projection W_z and the measured law is a_z(M); write p=sum_z w_z a_z.
The Bernoulli spectral expansion proves that p is exactly P_K(M). This is
an occupation-law identity, not an identification with spectral entropy.

For each flag, write alpha_z,beta_z for its bottom and top projection laws,
and e_(z,U) for its two-component middle exterior rows. Put

    d=det M, d0=det(I-M), B=M-d I_2.

On the three respective cardinalities the conditional atom laws are

    a_z(S)=d0 alpha_z(S),
    a_z(U)=e_(z,U) B e_(z,U)*,
    a_z(T)=d beta_z(T).                                         (3)

Their middle rows satisfy sum_U e_U*e_U=I_2 by Cauchy--Binet. Along M+tE,
delta=det E, their second derivatives are 2delta times, respectively,
alpha_z, -||e_U||^2, beta_z. Impossible conditional atoms are identically
zero throughout the strict active domain and are omitted.

Let s_z(x)=a_z'(x)/a_z(x). Subtracting the two normalized Shannon Hessians
gives the exact identity already recorded, without a bound, in X017:

    H(p)''=sum_z w_z H(a_z)'' + L + R,
    L=sum_x p(x) Var(s_Z(x) | X=x),
    R=sum_(z,x) w_z a_z''(x) log[a_z(x)/p(x)].                    (4)

L is the full conditional score variance. R is signed and need not vanish.
Every subsequent estimate applies to these actual terms.

## 3. Two geometry-independent conditional derivative bounds

**Lemma.** On a I<=M<=(1-a)I, for every nonzero conditional atom,

    |s_z(x)| <= ||E||_1/a,
    |a_z''(x)| <= (2|det E|/a^2) a_z(x).                         (5)

Here ||E||_1 is the sum of the absolute Hermitian eigenvalues. Extreme
atoms follow from logarithmic differentiation of d,d0. For the middle,
normalize its exterior row to a unit column u and put q=u*Bu>0. Its first
derivative is tr(NE), with N=uu*-adj(M). Since adj(M) has eigenvalues
in [a,1-a], the Rayleigh quotients at u and an orthogonal unit vector
are at least a and at most -a. N has one eigenvalue of each sign and

    det N=det M-u*M u=-q.

Both eigenvalue magnitudes are at least a, so their product q implies
||N||<=q/a. Thus |tr(NE)|<=q||E||_1/a, proving the first bound.
The eigenvalues of B are m_1(1-m_2),m_2(1-m_1), both at least a^2;
using (3) proves the second bound, including its factor two.

Choose a most likely label z0. Define its score arbitrarily as zero where
its conditional law vanishes. Conditional variance minimizes the squared
error over all X-dependent predictors, so (5) gives

    L <= E[(s_Z(X)-s_z0(X))^2]
      <= 4 tau ||E||_1^2/a^2,       tau=1-w_z0.                  (6)

This remains valid on newly appearing atoms; no minimum positive measured
minor is required. For the logarithmic term, X065 supplied the sharper posterior identity
below while developing a separate near-decoding theorem. Put
pi_z(x)=w_z a_z(x)/p(x). Since sum_x a_z''(x)=0 for every z, the constant
-log w_z cancels exactly, giving

    R=sum_(z,x) w_z a_z''(x) log pi_z(x).

Every posterior logarithm is nonpositive. Thus (5) yields the complete bound

    |R| <= 2|det E| H(Z|X)/a^2 <= 2|det E| H(Z)/a^2.            (7)

The original X061 argument bounded E_(a_z)|log(a_z/p)| by
2log(1/w_z), giving twice this constant. Equation (7), credited to X065,
is used in the final theorem; no conditional entropy is omitted.

Equations (6)-(7) pay the complete mixture correction at a spectral
boundary, with vanishing error as the label approaches a point mass.
