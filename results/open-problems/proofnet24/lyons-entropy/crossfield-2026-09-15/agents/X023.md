# X023 — cavity-moment closure for internal bridges on an arbitrary balanced tree core

Status: COMPLETE bounded proof with reproducibility checks. General tree-supported affine concavity remains unresolved.

## 1. The theorem

Let T0 be any fixed finite tree, with a fixed balanced Hermitian kernel K0=I/2+B0 supported on T0. Let Tj be pairwise disjoint fixed rooted trees with roots rj and strict balanced kernels Kj=I/2+Bj. Join rj to any selected core vertex vj by the complex bridge zj. All entries except these bridges are fixed; many bridges may attach to the same core vertex. Assume the resulting full kernel K(z) is a positive contraction. A fixed coordinate gauge makes every fixed tree edge nonnegative without changing the measured occupation probabilities.

For a balanced rooted tree j, write its matching likelihood in independent uniform edge signs as

    qj(t)=Σ_(M matching Tj) ∏_(e in M) w_e t_e,   w_e=4|Bj,e|²,
    qj−(t)=q_(Tj−rj)(t),   Rj(t)=qj−(t)/qj(t),
    μj(t)=2^(−|E(Tj)|) qj(t),   κj=E_μj Rj².

The density μj is a probability measure. The root-deleted forest likelihood qj− ignores signs on deleted edges. All qj and qj− are strictly positive. Moreover E_μj Rj=1, so κj≥1.

**Theorem.** On the entire closed convex feasible domain of the complex bridges,

    z ↦ H(P_K(z)) + 8 Σ_j κj |zj|⁴

is concave. In particular every nonconstant affine segment in the bridges has strictly concave entropy. At a strict full kernel,

    −H''[h,h] ≥ Σ_j κj {64(Re(conj(zj)hj))²+32|zj|²|hj|²}.

The changing bridges may all be internal edges, and all complex directions and mixed radial signs are independent. The fixed core may have arbitrarily many measured coordinates, and every fixed branch may have arbitrary depth and size. Singleton branches give exactly X014's pendant family and κj=1; nontrivial rooted branches generally improve its constant.

This is a closure theorem over a fixed core and fixed branches. It does not allow simultaneous changes of edges inside the branches or core. It advances X014 by moving internal cut edges with arbitrarily large fixed descendants. C059/C062 already cover free measured cores of size two with fixed reservoirs; those overlaps are not claimed as new scope.

## 2. Exact cavity bounds and the strength domain

Let Cj be the nonnegative normalized adjacency matrix with entries 2|Bj,e|. Strict balance means ρ(Cj)<1. Define

    cj=[(I−Cj)^(−1)]_(rj,rj).

Then the exact uniform cavity bound is

    0<Rj(t)≤cj,   max_t Rj(t)=cj.                             (1)

To prove it, form a complex skew-symmetric tree matrix Jj(t), putting square roots of w_e t_e on one orientation and their negatives on the reverse. The determinant expansion contains only disjoint edge transpositions, hence det(I+Jj)=qj and its root cofactor is qj−. Thus Rj=[(I+Jj)^(−1)]_(rj,rj). Since |Jj|=Cj and ρ(Cj)<1, the absolutely convergent Neumann series is bounded entrywise by (I−Cj)^(−1). This proves the upper bound. Taking all t_e=−1 gives qj=det(I−Cj), and the same root cofactor; equality follows. Positivity also follows directly from the strict DPP atom and marginal laws.

Put aj=4|zj|² and let C0 be the normalized core adjacency. Eliminating the fixed branch blocks in I−C_full gives

    D(a):=diag_v(1−Σ_(j:vj=v) cj aj)−C0 ⪰0.                 (2)

Since a tree is bipartite, 0⪯K⪯I is equivalent to I−C_full⪰0 after gauge. Consequently (2) is the exact full feasible strength domain; strictness is D(a)≻0. This domain is convex and downward closed in a≥0. The Schur complements use positive definite fixed branch blocks I−Cj, including when the full kernel is on its boundary.

For independent bridge signs τj, independent of the internal branch signs, define

    Qv=1+Σ_(j:vj=v) aj τj Rj.

By (1), Qv≥1−Σ cj aj>0 in the strict domain. Also

    diag(Q)−C0 ⪰D(a)≻0.                                    (3)

Every sign-dependent effective core obtained below is therefore a strict physical balanced core. This closes the positivity issue that a purely formal entropy series would leave open.

## 3. Exact entropy recursion with fixed cavity laws

Let tj denote internal branch signs and t0 the core signs, all uniform under the reference law. Matchings of the full tree may use a bridge at most once at its core endpoint and then must delete its branch root. Grouping these choices gives the polynomial identity

    q_full=(∏_j qj)(∏_v Qv)
      q_T0(t0; w_uv/(Qu Qv)).                               (4)

Indeed each unused core vertex contributes its local Qv; a core matching edge uses both endpoints and removes those two factors. Each selected bridge supplies aj τj qj−/qj. This exhausts all full-tree matchings without overlap.

Write F=|V|log2−H and Fj=|Tj|log2−H(P_Kj). Average q_full log q_full. The branch marginals are unchanged by the bridges, so the terms log qj contribute exactly Σ Fj. Averaging the core signs kills all nonempty core matchings. The remaining factors Qv have expectation one, independently across v. Hence

    F_full=Σ_j Fj +Σ_v Ψv(a_v)
      +E_(μ,τ)[(∏_v Qv) F_T0(w_uv/(Qu Qv))],                (5)
    Ψv(a_v)=E_(μ,τ) Qv log Qv.

The branch probabilities μj are fixed and mutually independent. Equation (5) is an exact entropy identity, including the dependence of all conditional means. No conditional entropy or changing-weight correction has been discarded.

## 4. The convex cavity potential

Use X014's proved convergent chromatic entropy series on the effective tree core:

    F_T0(x)=Σ_(α≠0, even) cα x^α,   cα≥0,
    dv(α)=Σ_(core e incident v) αe.

Only connected supported edge sets contribute, and each positive dv is at least two. By (3) the series applies for every realization in (5); all expectations here are finite. Thus

    F_full=Σ_j Fj+Σ_v Ψv(a_v)
      +Σ_α cα w_core^α ∏_(v:dv>0) Av,dv−1(a_v),              (6)
    Av,m(a_v)=E_(μ,τ)(1+Σ_(j:vj=v) aj τj Rj)^(−m).

The coloring argument and zero-free convergence proof are X014, Sections 2–3. The extension here is the fixed random cavity coefficient Rj and its physical bound (1).

For m≥1 every integrand of Av,m is log-convex, because −m log Qv is convex in the strengths a_v on its positive affine domain. A finite positive sum of log-convex functions is log-convex by Hölder's inequality. Pairing τj proves coordinatewise monotonicity: conditional on all other variables, with b=1+Σ_(k≠j) akτkRk>ajRj,

    ∂aj E_τj Qv^(−m)
      =(mRj/2){(b−ajRj)^(−m−1)−(b+ajRj)^(−m−1)}≥0.

Therefore every positive term in the last sum of (6) is jointly convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing. Products remain log-convex since the factors have disjoint attachment groups. The pointwise finite limit inherits convexity and monotonicity on the convex strict domain (2).

For Ψv set Yj=τjRj. Its strength Hessian is

    ∇²Ψv=E [YYᵀ/(1+a_v·Y)]
          =E [YYᵀ/(1−(a_v·Y)²)] ⪰E YYᵀ=diag(κj).           (7)

The middle equality pairs all bridge signs simultaneously. The variables τj are independent uniform signs even under μ, so the off-diagonal entries of E YYᵀ vanish. Equation (7) proves that

    Ψv−(1/2)Σ_(j:vj=v) κj aj²

is convex. It is even in each aj, so its derivative in aj vanishes at aj=0; convexity in that coordinate makes it nondecreasing for aj≥0. Combining this with (6),

    G(a):=F_full(a)−Σ_j Fj−(1/2)Σ_j κj aj²

is jointly convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing on the full strict feasible strength domain. This is the dimension-independent potential needed for this closure.

## 5. Return to affine complex bridges and boundary

For zθ=(1−θ)z0+θz1,

    a(zθ)=(1−θ)a(z0)+θa(z1)−4θ(1−θ)(|z1j−z0j|²)_j.

The affine strength domain (2) contains the average strength, and downward closure contains a(zθ). Convexity and monotonicity of G give the theorem on strict segments, since (1/2)κj aj²=8κj|zj|⁴. Strictness for distinct endpoints follows from strict convexity of |z|⁴.

If the fixed core is strict, replace feasible bridge endpoints zj by r zj, 0<r<1. Then D(r²a)=r²D(a)+(1−r²)(I−C0)≻0; let r increase to one using entropy continuity. If the fixed core has a boundary eigenvalue, approximate all fixed and moving centered entries by r times themselves. The branch moments κj(r) converge to κj because every fixed branch is strict. The theorem for the scaled strict cores then converges to the stated inequality. All finite determinantal probabilities and x log x are continuous, including at zero. This establishes the closed-domain statement under the stated strict-branch hypothesis.

## 6. Concrete new scope and exact remaining gap

Take a four-vertex core path with fixed kernel edges (3/20,1/10,3/20). At each core vertex attach a two-vertex branch whose fixed internal kernel edge is 1/4. The four bridge endpoints are

    z0=(1/4,1/5,1/6,1/7),   z1=(1/7,1/6,1/5,1/4).

Each branch has cj=4/3 and κj=16/15. Every bridge strength is at most 1/4, so D(a)⪰(2/3)I−C0≻0: the largest row sum of C0 is 1/2<2/3. All four moving edges are internal, and their nonzero disjoint slopes give inertia (4,4). Three or more measured core coordinates are necessary to cover these changing bridges, so the structural free-pair reservoir theorem does not cover this family. The theorem supplies the enhanced correction (128/15)Σ|zj|⁴.

**Remaining gap.** When an edge inside Tj also moves, μj, Rj and κj all move. Equation (5) remains an identity pointwise, but neither the fixed-measure log-convexity argument nor (7) controls the extra derivatives. Full internal-edge induction would require a joint convexity theorem for these rooted cavity moments together with their branch entropy. This report does not claim such a theorem, full balanced-tree concavity, diagonal-changing concavity, or general Lyons concavity.
