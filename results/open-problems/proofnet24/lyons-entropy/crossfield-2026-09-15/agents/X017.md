# X017 — A sharp center test and an information bridge for two active modes

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: COMPLETE bounded constructive theorem.** General projection-entropy submodularity, arbitrary projection diamonds, and unrestricted Lyons concavity remain open. All logarithms are natural.

## Results and scope

For an arbitrary orthogonal flag consisting of a projection background W and a two-dimensional active plane V, define the invariant fair-middle entropy difference

\[
\beta_*=2H\!\left(\frac{\mu_{W+u}+\mu_{W+v}}2\right)
-H(\mu_W)-H(\mu_{W+V}),
\]

where u,v are any orthonormal basis of V. This report proves:

1. **An unconditional information inequality:** the normalized middle law \(\nu_M\) at any strict active covariance M satisfies
   \[
   D(m\Vert\nu_M)\le\log T_M.
   \]
   Consequently the entire frozen logarithmic acceleration is bounded below by the single number \(2\log2-\beta_*\). The proof uses only scalar Jensen and an exterior-power isometry.
2. **An unconditional sufficient entropy interval:** for every flag, with no diamond hypothesis,
   \[
   \boxed{2\log2-4\le\beta_*\le2\log2}
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \boxed{M\longmapsto H(P_{P_W+VMV^*})\text{ is concave on }[0,I_2].}
   \tag{A}
   \]
   This is full concavity in the four real entries of M, allowing noncommuting endpoints.
3. **An exact criterion for certified flags:** if the flag has a nonnegative unlabeled elementary-diamond coupling, then the upper inequality in (A) is automatic, and
   \[
   \boxed{\text{full active-covariance concavity}
   \iff \beta_*\ge2\log2-4
   \iff \left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{P_W+(1/2+t)P_V})\right|_{t=0}\le0.}
   \tag{B}
   \]
   In fact the scalar curvature in (B) is exactly \(-8+4\log2-2\beta_*\).

The exact threshold and the information inequality are the main constructive deliverables. They isolate a much weaker target than general projection-entropy submodularity. A general proof that \(\beta_*\) belongs to the interval in (A) is **not** supplied.

## 1. The flag, invariant middle law, and exact atom probabilities

Let \(W\in\mathbb C^{n\times r}\) and \(V=[u,v]\in\mathbb C^{n\times2}\) have orthonormal columns, with \(W^*V=0\). Write \(P_W=WW^*\). For coordinate sets of the indicated sizes define

\[
a_S=|\det W_S|^2\quad(|S|=r),\qquad
b_T=|\det[W,V]_T|^2\quad(|T|=r+2),
\]
\[
e_U=(\det[W,u]_U,\det[W,v]_U)\quad(|U|=r+1).
\tag{1}
\]

The empty determinant is one. Cauchy–Binet gives \(\sum a_S=\sum b_T=1\). The two exterior vectors \(W_1\wedge\cdots\wedge W_r\wedge u\) and the corresponding vector with v are orthonormal, so

\[
\sum_U e_U^*e_U=I_2.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular

\[
m_U=\frac{\|e_U\|^2}{2}
\tag{3}
\]

is a probability law. It is exactly the mean of the two middle projection laws. Replacing V by VR with R unitary changes \(e_U\) to \(e_UR\); hence m is invariant. The top minors change by the phase \(\det R\), while a is unchanged. Therefore

\[
\beta_*=2H(m)-H(a)-H(b)
\tag{4}
\]

is intrinsic to the subspace flag, independently of the active basis.

Let \(0<M<I_2\), and put

\[
d=\det M,\qquad d_0=\det(I-M),\qquad B=M-dI_2,
\qquad f_1=\operatorname{tr}B,
\qquad T=\frac{f_1}{2\sqrt{dd_0}}.
\tag{5}
\]

If M has eigenvalues p,q, the eigenvalues of B are \(p(1-q),q(1-p)\). Thus B is positive definite,

\[
\det B=dd_0,\qquad T\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]

The exact occupation probabilities of \(K=P_W+VMV^*\) are

\[
p_S=d_0a_S,\qquad
p_U=e_UB e_U^*,\qquad
p_T=db_T,
\tag{7}
\]

and all other cardinalities are impossible. To prove (7), diagonalize M within V and use the independent Bernoulli spectral expansion: no active mode has probability d0, both have probability d, and the one-mode weights are the two eigenvalues of B. Equation (7) is invariant under this basis change. This argument never rotates the measured coordinate system.

Set

\[
\nu_U=\frac{e_UB e_U^*}{f_1}.
\tag{8}
\]

All atoms retained in a,b,m are positive at every strict M. Atoms identically zero may be omitted throughout.

## 2. The new elementary information inequality

**Lemma 1.** For every flag and every strict M,

\[
\boxed{0\le D(m\Vert\nu)\le\log T.}
\tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** For every nonzero row e put \(z=e^*/\|e\|\), a unit vector. Scalar concavity of log, applied to the spectral decomposition of B, gives

\[
\log(z^*Bz)\ge z^*(\log B)z.
\tag{10}
\]

By (2)–(3),

\[
\sum_Um_U z_Uz_U^*=\frac{I_2}{2}.
\]

Multiplying (10) by m and summing therefore gives

\[
\sum_Um_U\log(z_U^*Bz_U)
\ge\frac12\operatorname{tr}\log B
=\frac12\log\det B.
\tag{11}
\]

Since \(\nu_U/m_U=2z_U^*Bz_U/f_1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(m\Vert\nu)
&=\log(f_1/2)-\sum_Um_U\log(z_U^*Bz_U)\\
&\le\log(f_1/2)-\tfrac12\log(dd_0)
=\log T.
\end{aligned}
\]

Nonnegativity is the ordinary log-sum inequality. All quantities are finite because B is positive definite. This proves (9). ∎

There is also an exact nonnegative remainder:

\[
\boxed{\log T-D(m\Vert\nu)
=\sum_Um_U\bigl[\log(z_U^*Bz_U)-z_U^*(\log B)z_U\bigr]\ge0.}
\tag{12}
\]

If B has two distinct eigenvalues, equality in (12) holds exactly when every nonzero row vector \(e_U^*\) belongs to one of its two eigenspaces. If B is scalar, equality holds automatically. This is the two-dimensional relative-entropy data-processing inequality in elementary form; no quantum-entropy theorem is imported.

## 3. Exact curvature and its invariant logarithmic bounds

For a Hermitian active direction A put \(\delta=\det A\). Differentiating the two-by-two determinants along M+tA gives

\[
d''=d_0''=2\delta,\qquad B''=-2\delta I_2.
\]

Thus the atom accelerations in (7) are respectively

\[
2\delta a_S,\quad-2\delta\|e_U\|^2,\quad2\delta b_T.
\tag{13}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal I=\sum_{X:p_X>0}\frac{(p'_X)^2}{p_X}.
\]

The Shannon Hessian identity gives exactly

\[
\boxed{H''=-\mathcal I+2\delta\Lambda,}
\tag{14}
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda
&=\sum_U\|e_U\|^2\log p_U-\log(dd_0)+H(a)+H(b)\\
&=\boxed{2\log(2T)-\beta_*-2D(m\Vert\nu).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

For the second equality substitute \(p_U=f_1\nu_U\), \(\|e_U\|^2=2m_U\), and the definition of relative entropy. In particular (9) proves the unconditional sandwich

\[
\boxed{2\log2-\beta_*\le\Lambda(M)
\le2\log(2T_M)-\beta_*.}
\tag{16}
\]

The lower bound is attained at every scalar M because then T=1 and \(\nu=m\). Therefore, for every flag,

\[
\boxed{\Lambda(M)\ge0\text{ for every strict }M
\iff\beta_*\le2\log2.}
\tag{16a}
\]

This is the crucial point: bounding the logarithmic acceleration at one scalar active covariance bounds its sign at every active covariance.

If \(\beta_*\le2\log2\), equations (14) and (16) immediately imply \(H''\le0\) for every \(\delta\le0\). This includes all indefinite and rank-one active directions, without a diamond hypothesis.

## 4. The sharp definite-direction bound

For \(\delta>0\), A is definite; replacing A by -A if necessary lets us assume A positive definite without changing H''. The bottom and top cardinalities in (7) alone give

\[
\mathcal I\ge
\frac{[\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}M)A]^2}{d}
+\frac{[\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(I-M))A]^2}{d_0}.
\tag{17}
\]

Define \(P=\operatorname{adj}M/\sqrt d\) and \(Q=\operatorname{adj}(I-M)/\sqrt{d_0}\). These positive matrices have determinant one. Cauchy–Schwarz and the two-dimensional trace–determinant inequality yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I
&\ge(\operatorname{tr}PA)^2+(\operatorname{tr}QA)^2\\
&\ge\tfrac12[\operatorname{tr}(P+Q)A]^2\\
&\ge2\det(P+Q)\det A
=4(1+T)\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

The trace–determinant inequality follows by applying the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality to the two positive eigenvalues of \(A^{1/2}(P+Q)A^{1/2}\). To check the final equality, diagonalize M with eigenvalues p,q. The mixed determinant term in \(\det(P+Q)\) is

\[
\frac{q(1-p)+p(1-q)}{\sqrt{pq(1-p)(1-q)}}=2T,
\]

while the two pure determinants sum to two.

Combining (14), (15), and (18) gives

\[
\boxed{
-H''\ge\delta\Bigl[
C_{\beta_*}+4(T-1-\log T)+4D(m\Vert\nu)
\Bigr],
\qquad C_{\beta_*}=8-4\log2+2\beta_*.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Since T>=1, both correction terms in brackets are nonnegative. Hence

\[
\boxed{-H''\ge C_{\beta_*}\det A\quad(\det A>0).}
\tag{20}
\]

It follows that \(\beta_*\ge2\log2-4\) controls all definite directions. Combined with Section 3, this proves Hessian nonpositivity under interval (A). The domain \(0<M<I_2\) is convex, so integration proves concavity there. Replace closed-domain endpoints by \((1-2\epsilon)M+\epsilon I_2\) and use continuity of finite Shannon entropy to obtain the full closed domain. No singular frozen log is assigned to an impossible atom.

## 5. Necessity and the exact center test

At \(M=I_2/2\) and \(A=I_2\), one has

\[
d=d_0=1/4,\quad B=I_2/4,\quad T=1,\quad\nu=m.
\]

The bottom atom derivatives are \(-a_S\), top derivatives are \(b_T\), and every middle derivative is zero. Consequently \(\mathcal I=4+4=8\), \(\delta=1\), and (14) gives

\[
\boxed{H''[I_2,I_2]_{M=I_2/2}
=-8+4\log2-2\beta_*=-C_{\beta_*}.}
\tag{21}
\]

Equations (19) and (21) also give the following unconditional exact theorem, without any upper bound or coupling:

\[
\boxed{H^{\prime\prime}_M[A,A]\le0\text{ for every strict }M\text{ and every definite }A
\iff\beta_*\ge2\log2-4.}
\tag{21a}
\]

Thus the lower endpoint of (A) is necessary for full active concavity for **every** flag. Within the class satisfying \(\beta_*\le2\log2\), it is also sufficient by the preceding proof. This proves the exact center criterion in that class, including equality at the threshold.

One can inspect the entire central Hessian. Write \(A=A_0+\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}A)I_2\), with \(\operatorname{tr}A_0=0\), and put \(L_*=2\log2-\beta_*\). Then

\[
\boxed{
-H''_{I_2/2}[A,A]
=\frac{C_{\beta_*}}4(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
+L_*\|A_0\|_F^2
+4\sum_{U:e_U\ne0}\frac{(e_UA_0e_U^*)^2}{\|e_U\|^2}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

Indeed the extreme Fisher contribution is \(2(\operatorname{tr}A)^2\), the middle contribution is the last displayed term, and \(2\det A=(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/2-\|A_0\|_F^2\). Equation (22) also explains the limit of the necessity claim: when \(\beta_*>2\log2\), the middle Fisher term can compensate a negative \(L_*\). This report does **not** prove that the upper endpoint of (A) is necessary for arbitrary flags.

## 6. Why every certified diamond meets the upper bound

An unlabeled diamond coupling consists of nonnegative weights on \((S,T)\), with \(S\subset T\), \(|T\setminus S|=2\), bottom law a, top law b, and aggregate middle law 2m. Its two middle sets are \(S+i,S+j\), where \(\{i,j\}=T\setminus S\).

For any strict determinantal law, the atom table is log-submodular:

\[
p_Sp_{S+i+j}\le p_{S+i}p_{S+j}.
\tag{23}
\]

Here is the finite determinant proof. For a strict kernel K use its positive definite odds \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}\), so \(p_X=\det(I-K)\det L_X\). After eliminating the principal block S, the remaining two-by-two positive Schur complement has determinant \(ac-|z|^2\le ac\). The common determinant factors cancel to give (23).

For our projection-background kernel, regularize the full K to \((1-2\epsilon)K+\epsilon I\), apply (23), then pass to the limit. At strict active M, every positive coupling atom has positive probabilities at all its bottom, top and middle sets by its marginal equations and (7). Thus all relevant logarithms have finite limits. Averaging the negative logarithm of (23) gives precisely \(\Lambda(M)\ge0\).

At \(M=I_2/2\), (15) reads \(\Lambda=2\log2-\beta_*\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{certified diamond}\Longrightarrow\beta_*\le2\log2.}
\tag{24}
\]

Equations (21), (24), and the sufficient interval establish (B). In particular a general diamond theorem, by itself, leaves exactly the lower entropy bound \(\beta_*\ge2\log2-4\) to prove for full active-covariance concavity. General projection-entropy submodularity \(\beta\ge0\) would be sufficient but is much stronger than this threshold.

## 8. What survives an arbitrary fractional background

Let a fixed positive contraction J vanish on V, and spectrally sample its independent background Bernoulli occupations Z with probabilities \(w_z\). For each z let W_z be the occupied projection background. Conditional on Z=z, the output law is the flag law \(p_z(M)\); the unobserved law is

\[
p(M)=\sum_z w_zp_z(M),\qquad
K(M)=J+VMV^*.
\]

The exact information identity is

\[
H(p(M))=\sum_z w_zH(p_z(M))+I(Z;X_M).
\tag{30}
\]

Thus proving (A) for every conditional flag does not suffice for arbitrary fractional backgrounds. The changing information term is a concrete remaining obligation.

More explicitly, at a strict active M retain each conditional support and write \(s_z(x)=p_z'(x)/p_z(x)\). The exact curvature loss upon forgetting Z is

\[
\boxed{
H(p)''=\sum_z w_zH(p_z)''
+\sum_xp(x)\operatorname{Var}(s_Z(x)\mid X=x)
+\sum_{z,x}w_zp_z''(x)\log\frac{p_z(x)}{p(x)}.
}
\tag{31}
\]

To prove (31), differentiate (30), or subtract the two Shannon Hessian formulas. The Fisher difference is the displayed conditional score variance by the law of total variance. The logarithmic remainder is exactly the last sum. Since \(p_z''=2\det(A)(a_z+b_z-2m_z)\), it is explicit in the flag contrasts. Impossible conditional atoms have p_z and both derivatives identically zero and are omitted. The first additional term is nonnegative; the second has no established sign here. Neither can be discarded.

If each conditional flag has a certified diamond, opposite-sign spectral slopes, and more generally every indefinite active Hermitian direction, nevertheless remain controlled: apply each diamond to the **same frozen logarithm of the marginal DPP** p, then average their nonnegative contrasts. The marginal atom acceleration is the corresponding averaged contrast times 2 det(A), so the Fisher-plus-log formula gives H''<=0 for det(A)<=0. For definite active directions, the extreme-cardinality Fisher bound used in Section 4 no longer survives unobserved background counts. Equation (31) records the precise missing compensation.
