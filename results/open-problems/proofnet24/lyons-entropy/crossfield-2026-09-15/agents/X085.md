## 1. The theorem and overlap correction

In the algebraic basis e1,e+=(e2+e3)/sqrt(2),e-=(e2-e3)/sqrt(2), write

    K=M direct-sum lambda, M=[[a,conj(r)],[r,u]], 0<M<I,
    b=1-a, q=ab, rho=|r|^2,
    x=x1=u-rho/a, y=x0=u+rho/b, d=y-x=rho/q.

Measurements remain in e1,e2,e3.

**Theorem.** If

    lambda=1/2,    0<x<y<1,                              (1)

then -D²H(K)[A,A]>0 for every nonzero Hermitian three-by-three A. In particular the active block of A may be definite and move opposite to its spectator. Arbitrary four-real-dimensional swap-odd motion is included.

The parameters a,x,y are independent: any 0<a<1 and 0<x<y<1 give a physical base through u=ax+by, |r|²=ab(y-x), since

    det M=ax>0,       det(I-M)=b(1-y)>0.                  (2)

The phase of r is arbitrary by a fixed measured-coordinate diagonal gauge. Thus this is the entire half-filled-spectator symmetric base locus with r nonzero. At r=0 the nonpositive, possibly degenerate Hessian follows by continuity from r nonzero; strictness is not asserted there.

**Overlap correction.** The initially found complementary subfamily x+y=1, lambda=1/2 is already covered by X055, as root pointed out. It is the one-coordinate activity orbit of a balanced particle-hole star. At that seed, X055's coefficients are c1=-log(1-d²), c2=c3=2atanh(d); its matrix test reduces to c2>=d c1. This follows from c2-c1=2log(1+d)>0. We credit X055 for that scope and do not claim it as new. An intermediate X085 proof covered endpoints straddling one half; the final proof below removes that restriction too. It does not need C008 or X055 as proof inputs.

## 2. Exact pivot elimination for every spectator mean

Let f(x,l) be the entropy of the four measured leaf probabilities

    ((1-x)(1-l), (x+l-2xl)/2, (x+l-2xl)/2, xl).

Set J1=-D²f(x,lambda), J0=-D²f(y,lambda), and

    g=f_x(x,lambda)-f_x(y,lambda)>=0,
    k=f_l(x,lambda)-f_l(y,lambda).

Fix r>0 by a constant coordinate phase. Write a swap-even velocity as B direct-sum delta, B=[[alpha,conj(z)],[z,beta]]. First take z real. The two physical conditional velocities e1,e0 satisfy

    2r z=q(e0-e1)+(b-a)d alpha,
    beta=a e1+b e0-d alpha.

The physical determinant therefore has the exact form

    4det B=2alpha(e1+e0)-(d/q)alpha²-(q/d)(e0-e1)².         (3)

Put Q1=(e1,delta)J1(e1,delta)^T, Q0=(e0,delta)J0(e0,delta)^T, m=(e1+e0)/2, s=1+dg/2, and h=g m+k delta. Substitution in X076's FULL physical curvature, including p_M''=2det B(1,-1,-1,1), gives

    -H''=(s/q)[alpha-q h/s]²
          +q[Q1/b+Q0/a+g(e1-e0)²/(2d)-h²/s].             (4)

For complex z add exactly 2g(Im z)². No normalization or moving-conditional term is omitted.

For fixed x,y,lambda every a is physical by (2), and inf_a(Q1/b+Q0/a)=(sqrt(Q1)+sqrt(Q0))². Therefore the FULL swap-even conjecture is equivalent to the categorical endpoint inequality

    (sqrt(Q1)+sqrt(Q0))²+g(e1-e0)²/(2d)

This all-parameter reduction is exact. The rest proves (5) under (1).

## 3. Uniformly bounded endpoint score at a half-filled spectator

For one endpoint write x=(1-t)/2, -1<t<1, and define

    sigma=1-t², L=-log(sigma), T=atanh(t),
    v=T-tL/2, phi=(T,v),
    J=-D²f(x,1/2)=[[A,B],[B,C]],
    A=2/sigma, B=2-L, C=2(1+t²).

**Scalar lemma.** With kappa=1/2, for every real two-vector W,

    [phi dot W]²<=kappa W^T J W.                         (6)

The logarithmic mixed term is included in J. Here is a self-contained proof. The assertion is invariant under t->-t, so first suppose 0<t<1. Complete the first J square and put

    R=C-B²/A=4t²+2sigma L-sigma L²/2,
    w=v-BT/A, theta=T²/A+w²/R.

The exponential inequality e^L>=1+L²/2 yields

    R>=3t²+2sigma L>=3t²>0.                              (7)

The elementary log inequality log z<=(z-1)/sqrt(z), z>=1, implies T<=t/sqrt(sigma). Its proof is differentiation: the derivative of the difference is (sqrt(z)-1)²/(2z^(3/2)). Integrating 1/(1-z²) on [0,t] also gives T<=t/sigma. Direct differentiation gives

    v'=1-L/2, w'=t(3-L)T,
    w=t²T-(L/2)(t-sigma T).

We have 0<v<=t: positivity follows from 2v=(1+t)log(1+t)-(1-t)log(1-t), and v'<=1 with v(0)=0. Also w>0: it increases from zero until L=3, then decreases to lim_(t->1)w=log 2>0. The last identity gives w<=t²T.

We bound the two parts of theta separately by 1/4. First sigma=sech²(T), and cosh(T)>=1+T²/2>=sqrt(2)T, so T²/A=sigma T²/2<=1/4.

For the second part, if 0<L<=2 then w=v-sigma(1-L/2)T<=v<=t. If 2<=L<=4, let t2=sqrt(1-exp(-2)). The function v decreases for t>=t2. Since L>=t²+t^4/2, integration of v'=1-L/2 gives v(t)<=t-t³/6-t^5/20. Also e²>7, so t2²>6/7 and

    v(t)<=v(t2)<(201/245)t2<=(201/245)t.

The bound T<=t/sqrt(sigma) gives, for L>=2,

    w<=v+t exp(-L/2)(L/2-1)<=v+t exp(-2).

The scalar factor has its maximum at L=4. Therefore 2<=L<=4 gives w<(201/245+1/7)t=(236/245)t<t. For every 0<L<=4, (7)'s exact expression gives R>=4t², so w²/R<=1/4.

It remains to handle L>=4. The exponential series through degree six proves e²>73/10, and the series through degree four at 7/10 proves exp(7/10)>2, hence log 2<7/10. Put t4=sqrt(1-exp(-4)); then t4>99/100. Since v decreases on this interval,

    v(t)<=v(t4)=log(1+t4)+2(1-t4)<18/25,
    v(t)/t<8/11,
    w/t<8/11+10/73=694/803.

Together with R>=3t², this gives

    w²/R<(694/803)²/3<1/4,

where the last exact integer comparison is 4(694)²=1926544<1934427=3(803)². Consequently

    theta=T²/A+w²/R<=1/2=kappa.                         (8)

For W=(e,delta), set X=sqrt(A)(e+B delta/A), Y=sqrt(R)delta. Then W^TJW=X²+Y² and phi dot W=(T/sqrt(A))X+(w/sqrt(R))Y. Cauchy-Schwarz and theta<=kappa prove (6). For negative t, phi changes sign and J is unchanged. At t=0, phi=0 and J=[[2,2],[2,2]], so (6) holds directly without division by R.
