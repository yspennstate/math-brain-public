# X028 — frame-channel entropy comparison and an overlap criterion

Completed bounded creative pass, 15 September 2026. The universal Lyons
conjecture and both unrestricted stronger Fisher inequalities remain open.
Established here: an exact positive pair remainder; the first stronger
inequality for frames with a weighted overlap bound; and a full scalar-base
Hessian theorem for a nonisotropic class of arbitrary rank-three frames.
No noncommutative Brascamp–Lieb theorem is imported.

## 1. Notation and the theorem

Let `sum_i t_i n_i n_i*=I_3`, with unit `n_i` and positive `t_i`. Write

    x_i=n_i* A n_i,  a_ij=n_i* A n_j,  h=tr A,
    lambda_ij=|n_i*n_j|^2,  c_ij=-log(1-lambda_ij),
    F_v=sum_i t_i x_i^2,  Q_W=tr(W adj A).

Every sum involving `c_ij` below omits parallel pairs and the diagonal.
For such pairs the cofactor is identically zero. Exact parallel-ray splitting
leaves all quantities below unchanged: cross-ray weights add, and dependent
pairs never enter. Define

    r_i=sum_{j: n_j not parallel to n_i} t_j c_ij,
    R=max_i r_i,                  delta=56/25-R.       (1)

**Theorem.** Suppose `tau>=0` and `R<=56/25`. For every scalar active base
`M=sI_3`, `0<s<1`, and every Hermitian active direction `A`,

    -D^2 H(P_{VMV*})[A,A] >= delta F_v(A) >=0.        (2)

There is no restriction on the number of measured coordinates or on
commutation, eigenvalue signs, row norms, or the anisotropy of `W`.
If `tau>0` and `R<56/25`, the Hessian is negative definite.
An explicit nonnegative remainder strengthens (2) in equation (9).
Separately, `R<=2` implies the first stronger target `F_v>=Q_W`,
without any assumption on `tau`.

## 2. The exact pair remainder

The supplied exterior determinant identity gives

    2Q_W=sum_{i,j} t_i t_j c_ij (x_i x_j-|a_ij|^2).

Consequently the following expression is an exact identity:

    G_R:=R F_v-2Q_W
      =sum_i t_i(R-r_i)x_i^2
       +(1/2)sum_{i,j}t_i t_j c_ij(x_i-x_j)^2
       +sum_{i,j}t_i t_j c_ij|a_ij|^2 >=0.           (3)

The sums in (3) are ordered. It follows by expanding the square and using
symmetry in `i,j`; every term displayed on its right is nonnegative.
In particular `Q_W<=R F_v/2`. The interference contribution is retained
in the exact remainder, even though the resulting sufficient criterion
uses only its nonnegativity.

The criterion has a simpler overlap-only sufficient version. After merging
parallel rays, let `mu=max_{i!=j} lambda_ij<1` and let `m_i` be the total
weight of the ray containing `n_i`. Since `c(lambda)/lambda` is increasing,

    r_i<=[-log(1-mu)/mu](1-m_i).                     (4)

Here `sum_j t_j lambda_ij=1` gives the final factor. At `mu=0`, use the
continuous coefficient one and note directly that `R=0`.
Equation (4) is optional: the actual theorem uses the exact row sums (1).

## 3. Exterior compensation and the proof of the theorem

Write `q=1-s`, `B=A-(h/3)I`, and use the second-exterior measurement form
`F_w` of X008/X016. Exterior contraction gives a positive remainder

    E_w=F_w-(F_v+h^2)/4 >=0.                        (5)

For completeness, if `b_ij=||w_ij||^2` and
`y_ij=w_ij* A w_ij/b_ij`, contraction says
`sum_{j!=i}b_ij=2t_i` and
`sum_{j!=i}b_ij y_ij=t_i(h-x_i)`. Expanding gives
`E_w=(1/2)sum_i sum_{j!=i}b_ij[y_ij-(h-x_i)/2]^2`.
Thus (5) includes the exact positive remainder, not a Fisher proxy.

The scalar identities from X004/X008 are

    -H''=F_v/s+F_w/q-2Q_W-2s tau tr(adj A),
    tr(adj A)=h^2/3-||B||_F^2/2.

Substituting (3) and (5) yields the exact decomposition

    -H''=C1 F_v+C2 h^2+G_R+E_w/q+s tau||B||_F^2,
    C1=1/s+1/(4q)-R,  C2=1/(4q)-2s tau/3.           (6)

The only universal entropy bound needed here is `tau<8/5`.
One can verify it directly. Put `r_T=|det V_T|^2`,
`Z=sum_{|T|=3}prod_{i in T}t_i<=9/2`, and
`pi_T=(prod_{i in T}t_i)/Z`. The projection-law marginals give

    tau+tr W=log Z-D(r||pi)<=log(9/2)<8/5.           (7)

The support of `r` is contained in that of `pi`, so this relative entropy
is finite; its nonnegativity is the ordinary finite log-sum inequality.
`W>=0` follows from its nonnegative pair coefficients. The last strict
bound follows, for example, from the exponential series through degree four.

Cauchy–Schwarz and `sum_i t_i=3` give `h^2<=3F_v`. Also

    C1 >=9/4-R,
    C1+3C2=1/(sq)-R-2s tau >=56/25-R.               (8)

For the second inequality put `z=s-1/2`. Since
`1/(sq)=4/(1-4z^2)>=4+16z^2` and `tau<8/5`, its left side
is at least `56/25-R+16(z-1/10)^2`.
If `C2>=0`, then `C1 F_v+C2 h^2>=delta F_v`.
If `C2<0`, use `h^2<=3F_v` and the second bound in (8).
Equation (6) therefore proves the sharper result

    -H''>=delta F_v+G_R+E_w/q+s tau||B||_F^2.        (9)

Under the theorem hypotheses all terms on the right are nonnegative.
If `tau>0` and `delta>0`, a nonzero traceless component is detected by
the last term, and a nonzero scalar matrix has `F_v>0`; this proves
negative definiteness. For the convenient bound `R<=2`, (9) has
`delta>=6/25`. All directions are affine in the active covariance.

## 4. An exact nonisotropic example

Let `u` have positive coordinates and

    (|u_1|^2,|u_2|^2,|u_3|^2,|u_4|^2)
       =(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8),   P=I_4-uu*.

Choose any isometry `V:C^3 -> u^perp`. Write the four displayed weights
as `p_i`. Then `t_i=1-p_i`, `b_ij=1-p_i-p_j`,
`r_{[4] minus {i}}=p_i`, and
`lambda_ij=p_i p_j/[(1-p_i)(1-p_j)]<=1/3`.
Equation (4) gives `R<=(3/2)log(3/2)<2`, while exact entropy summation gives

    tau=(1/4)log(1350000/823543)>0.                  (10)

Thus (9) applies to all nine real Hermitian directions at every scalar base.
This example is outside the condition `W` scalar. To see this
without numerical eigenvalues, take measured vectors
`a=(0,0,1,-1)/sqrt(2)` and `b=(1/sqrt(3),-sqrt(2/3),0,0)` in `u^perp`.
For `z=a,b`, the Rayleigh value is
`sum_{i<j}c_ij det(P-zz*)_{ij}`. Direct substitution gives the difference

    a*V W V*a-b*V W V*b
       =(1/12)log[2^22 3^16/(5^5 7^12)]>0,          (11)

since the numerator and denominator are respectively
`180551034077184` and `43254022503125`.

## 5. The noncommutative tensor gap that remains

The initial saved route expanded the logarithm. For integers `k>=1`, put

    Q_k=(1/2)sum_{i,j}t_i t_j lambda_ij^k
                           (x_i x_j-|a_ij|^2).

Then `Q_W=sum_{k>=1}Q_k/k`. Finite frame cardinality and ordinary
convergence on each nonparallel pair justify this signed series.
The proposed sufficient lemma is

    Q_k<=F_v/(k+1),                every k>=1.       (TP)

Its sum would establish `Q_W<=F_v`, since
`sum_{k>=1}1/[k(k+1)]=1`. The `k=1` case follows from
`sum_j t_j lambda_ij=1`, scalar `2x_i x_j<=x_i^2+x_j^2`, and the
nonnegative interference term. The higher cases are not proved here.

There is an exact symmetric-tensor formulation, also supplied independently
by root during this pass. Let `Pi_i=n_i n_i*`, `m=k+1`, and

    R_m=sum_i t_i Pi_i^tensor m <= I on Sym^m(C^3),
    T_k=sum_i t_i x_i Pi_i^tensor k,
    Abar=(1/m)sum_{ell=1}^m A_ell.

The contraction follows from `Pi_i^tensor m<=Pi_i tensor I` and Parseval.
Symmetry gives `Pi_sym A_1 Pi_sym=Abar`, and direct coherent matrix
elements give the exact identity

    2Q_k=||T_k||_HS^2-tr(R_m Abar R_m Abar).          (12)

Thus the missing assertion is the conditional-variance bound that the
right side of (12) is at most `2F_v/(k+1)` for these coherent tensors.
The contraction `R_m<=I` alone has not supplied that bound.
The separate second target `F_w>=tr((W+tau I)adj A)` is also unproved.

The proved row condition is not universal: a frame formed from two equally
weighted orthonormal bases approaching one another has matched overlaps
`lambda->1` and row sums `R->infinity`, despite finite vanishing
`(1-lambda)log(1-lambda)` contributions in `W`. The interference retained
in (12), or another equally precise mechanism, is needed in that regime.
