## 1. Exact square completion after the logarithms have been integrated

Use the root variables `0<=r,s<1`,

    a=1-r², b=1-s², c=1-r²s²,
    R=atanh r, S=atanh s, D=atanh(rs),
    k=(1/2)log[c/(ab)].

To avoid a conflict with tangent coordinates, write the root matrix as

    Q = [[U,-2T],[-2T^T,V]],
    T = [[R+sD, S-sk], [R-rk, S+rD]],                    (1)

where

    U11=2(2c-b)/(ac), U22=2(2c-a)/(bc), U12=2/c-2k,
    V11=4(1+rR+rsD), V22=4(1+sS+rsD),
    V12=2(sR+rS-D-rs k).                               (2)

These are the exact root entries, including its rank-one lost Fisher term
and its complete logarithmic acceleration. For real two-vectors `x,y`,

    (x,y)^T Q(x,y)
      = ||T^T x-2y||²+x^T(U-TT^T)x+y^T(V-4I)y.         (3)

This identity is algebraic. Crucially the square uses the **integrated**
logarithms in T. It does not presume a nonnegative resolvent integrand.

## 2. A universal positive lower block with an explicit logarithmic kernel

**Theorem 1.** Throughout the complete square `0<=r,s<1`,

    V >= 4I+2 diag(r²,s²).                              (4)

In particular V is positive definite, including at a vanishing singular value.

**Proof.** Put `x=r²,y=s²` and

    E=sR+rS-D-rs k.

When `rs>0`, the absolutely convergent scalar power series give exactly

    E/(rs)=1-sum_(n>=1)
                [x^n+y^n-(xy)^n]/[2n(2n+1)].            (5)

Indeed `atanh r/r=sum x^n/(2n+1)`, and
`k=sum_(n>=1)[x^n+y^n-(xy)^n]/(2n)`. Subtract the series.
No termwise positivity is asserted for the original entropy resolvent.
The bracket is `1-(1-x^n)(1-y^n)`, which lies in `[0,1]`.
Moreover

    sum_(n>=1) 1/[2n(2n+1)] = 1-log 2.

For example integrate the geometric series on `[0,1]` to evaluate this sum;
all terms are nonnegative, so monotone convergence is elementary here.
Consequently the sharp universal endpoint bounds are

    (log 2)rs <= E <= rs.                               (6)

The upper constant is approached as both parameters vanish; the lower
constant is approached as both tend to one. The boundary `rs=0` follows
directly from E=0. Since `rR>=r²` and `sS>=s²`, for any real `e,f`,

    (e,f)(V-4I)(e,f)^T
      >=4r²e²+4s²f²-4rs|ef|
      >=2r²e²+2s²f².

The last step is `2(rs|ef|)<=(r²e²+s²f²)`. This proves (4). ∎

One integral form of precisely the favorable remainder is

    E/(rs)=log 2 + int_0^1
       sum_(n>=1) (1-x^n)(1-y^n) t^(2n-1)(1-t) dt.       (7)

All summands are nonnegative; termwise integration gives (5). Thus (7)
is an actual integrated positive log kernel, with both endpoint parameters
coupled. Its sign is not obtained by inspecting an unrelated resolvent.

## 3. The new sufficient two-by-two target

Equations (3)-(4) prove the following conditional theorem without further
assumptions:

    U >= TT^T  implies  Q>=0.                           (8)

The exact necessary-and-sufficient condition remains
`U>=4T V^(-1)T^T`. The sufficient condition (8) is stronger, since (4)
gives `4V^(-1)<=I`. It avoids inversion of a logarithmic matrix entirely.
If (8) holds strictly for nonzero `(r,s)`, it proves strict positivity of Q.
Proving this root diagonal sector alone still requires the other one-count
sectors and the measured touching identity before any full entropy claim.

Here is an explicit hyperbolic reduction of (8) with only two scalar logs.
Assume `r>=s`, set

    t=R+S, d=R-S, z=cosh t, w=cosh d,
    l=log z, m=log w, alpha=sinh t/(z+w), beta=sinh d/(z+w).

Let `W=2^(-1/2)[[1,1],[1,-1]]`. Then `0<=d<=t`, and direct identities give

    Uhat=W U W,
    Uhat11=zw+2+z/w+w/z-1/(zw)-l-m,
    Uhat22=zw-1/(zw)+l+m,
    Uhat12=sinh t sinh d [1-1/(zw)],                    (9)

and, with `B=W T^T W`,

    B=[[t-alpha m, beta m],
       [d-beta l,  alpha l]].                          (10)

Thus (8) is exactly the two-by-two matrix inequality

    Uhat >= B^T B,        t>=d>=0.                       (11)

For verification, `rs=(z-w)/(z+w)`, `1-r²=sech² R`,
`k=(l+m)/2`, `D=(l-m)/2`, `(r+s)/2=alpha`, and
`(r-s)/2=beta`. Substitution proves (9)-(10); no bound is used.

## 4. A proved strengthened certificate on the balanced boundary

**Theorem 2.** If `r=s`, write `t=2atanh r` and `l=log cosh t`.
Then the stronger Schur remainder from (8) satisfies

    W(U-TT^T)W >= diag(13/4,6r²).                       (12)

At r=0 the second diagonal entry is zero. This is a strengthened matrix
certificate for the fallback, not a claim to first prove the already known
balanced equal-modulus entropy theorem of X044.

**Proof.** Here `d=0,w=1,m=beta=0,alpha=r`. Equations (9)-(10) make
the remainder diagonal, with entries

    Aplus=2cosh t+2-log cosh t-t²,
    Aminus=cosh t-sech t+l-r²l².                        (13)

The inequalities `cosh t>=1+t²/2+t^4/24` and
`log cosh t<=t²/2` give

    Aplus>=4-t²/2+t^4/12 >=13/4.

The logarithm inequality follows by integrating `tanh t<=t`.
For every z>=1, `log²z<=(z-1)²/z`: put `z=exp(2h)` and use
`h<=sinh h`, itself obtained by integrating `cosh h>=1`.
Since `r²=(z-1)/(z+1)`, this yields

    Aminus >= 4(z-1)/(z+1)+log z =4r²+l.

Finally `l=2atanh(r²)>=2r²`. This proves (12). ∎

There is an exact obstruction to a direct common-shift monotonicity proof
of this stronger Schur remainder. At `d=0`, its plus eigenvalue has expansion
`Aplus(t)=4-t²/2+t^4/6+O(t^6)`, so it initially **decreases** as t increases.
Thus a claim that `Uhat-B^T B` is Loewner increasing in t at fixed d is false.
The finite positive margin in (12) absorbs this initial decrease; any
extension must retain a comparable margin or use a different congruence.

## 5. What is still missing and the three-site obstruction

but (11) appears numerically sufficient throughout the domain.

modes. X054's logarithmic means and two triangle energies are all retained.

paired with an indefinite adjugate. The scalar kernel (7) exploits the
two-log product structure of the four-site fallback; an analogous common
kernel controlling the complete three-site six-mode cross block has not
been obtained. This report does not close that gap.
