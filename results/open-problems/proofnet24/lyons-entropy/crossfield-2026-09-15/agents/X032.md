## An exact warning about iterating the exterior frame

The row frame with effects `W_ij` is again Parseval in dimension three.
Call its second-exterior entropy operator `E22`. Applying the elementary
exterior contraction identity to that frame gives its pair-log operator

    W_pair=E2+E22-e2 I,
    W_pair-C=E22-E1-e3 I.                              (1)

Thus it is an additional operator identity, not a notational identification,
to replace `W_pair` by `C`. In fact its shared-index contributions can be
identified exactly. The three-dimensional cross-product identity gives

    effect({i,j},{i,k})=r_ijk U_i.

The corresponding pair-log coefficient equals

    log(b_ij b_ik/(r_ijk t_i))=c_ijk+c_jk >=0.           (2)

The disjoint-index pair effects remain present in `E22`; omitting them
does not yield (1) with zero right side. All zero effects are omitted.
These formulas are algebraic and import no external theorem.

## A nonisotropic family outside the bounded-overlap range

Fix `0<b<=1/1000`, put `a=1/2-b`, and take the four real rows

    v1=(1/sqrt(2),0,sqrt(b)),   v2=(-1/sqrt(2),0,sqrt(b)),
    v3=(0,1/sqrt(2),-sqrt(a)),  v4=(0,-1/sqrt(2),-sqrt(a)).       (3)

Their outer products sum to `I_3`. The row weights are
`t1=t2=1/2+b`, `t3=t4=1-b`; the pair weights are `b12=2b`,
`b34=1-2b`, and `bij=1/2` for the four cross-group pairs. Triple
weights are `a,a,b,b`. Define

    cA=log((1/2+b)^2/(2b)),
    cB=log((1-b)^2/(1-2b)),
    cC=log(2(1/2+b)(1-b)).

The exterior effects of pair 12, pair 34, and all four cross pairs sum
respectively to `diag(0,2b,0)`, `diag(1-2b,0,0)`, and
`diag(2b,1-2b,1)`. Consequently

    W=diag(w1,w2,w3),
    w1=(1-2b)cB+2b cC,  w2=2b cA+(1-2b)cC,  w3=cC,             (4)
    tau=-2(1/2+b)log(1/2+b)-2(1-b)log(1-b)-log2.

Since `tau(0)=0` and `tau'(b)=2log((1-b)/(1/2+b))`,

    0<=tau<=2b log2 < (7/5)b.                                  (5)

This family is not entropy-isotropic: `w2>w3`, since `cA>cC`.
The normalized overlap of rows 1 and 2 tends to one as `b` tends to zero.
For the row sum used in X028,

    R=max_i sum_{j!=i} t_j[-log(1-|<n_i,n_j>|^2)]
       >=(1/2+b)cA >=(1/2)log125 >19/8>56/25.                  (6)

Thus every frame in (3) lies beyond that overlap-row certificate. None
has exactly parallel rows; merging parallel copies does not remove it.

## Certified failure of the first stronger comparison

For the explicit active direction

    A_b=diag(3b,-1+2b,1),

direct substitution gives

    F_v(A_b)=25b^2/(1+2b),
    tr(W adj A_b)=6b^2 cA-(1-2b)[(1-2b)cB+2b cC].              (7)

Put `L=log(1/(8b))`. The elementary inequalities `log(1+x)<=x`
and the formulas above imply

    L<=cA<=L+4b,  0<=cB<=b^2/(1-2b),  0<cC<=b.                (8)

Here `L>=log125>19/4`; a rational certificate is

    log125=3log5
      >6[(2/3)+(2/3)^3/3+(2/3)^5/5]=1924/405>19/4.

This follows by integrating the positive geometric series for
`log((1+x)/(1-x))` at `x=2/3`. Equations (7)-(8) now prove

    tr(W adj A_b)-F_v(A_b)
      >=b^2[6L-3(1-2b)-25/(1+2b)] > b^2/2.                   (9)

Therefore `F_v(A)>=tr(W adj A)` is false, for every frame in the
whole interval (3). This is a counterexample only to that proposed
stronger lemma; the entropy Hessian has the opposite sign below.

## Adaptive joint comparison using the second exterior measurement

**Theorem.** For the frames (3), every `0<s<1`, and every Hermitian `A`,
writing `q=1-s` and `T=W+s tau I=diag(T1,T2,T3)`, one has

    tr(T adj A)<=F_w(A)/7,
    -H''_(sI)[A,A]>= (5/7)FI_s(A)
                     +2[T1|A23|^2+T2|A13|^2+T3|A12|^2]
                  >= b^2 ||A||_F^2.                           (10)

In particular the full active Hessian is negative definite, including
directions invisible to both real frame measurements. The first inequality
also holds at `s=1`, and hence proves the requested second comparison
`F_w>=tr((W+tau I)adj A)` with a factor of seven to spare in this family.

**Proof.** First take `D=diag(d1,d2,d3)` and use the coordinates
`x=d1`, `y=d2+d3`, `z=d2-d3`. The pair measurements give exactly

    F_w(D)=(1-2b)x^2+(b/2)(y+z)^2
                       +2[bx+(1-b)y/2-bz/2]^2.                (11)

Expand and use `2|uv|<=u^2+v^2` on its three cross terms. This gives

    F_w(D)>=(1-3b+b^2)x^2+(1/2-3b/2)y^2+(b/2-b^2)z^2
           >=(99/100)x^2+(49/100)y^2+(49/100)b z^2.             (12)

On the other hand,

    tr(T adj D)=(T1/4)(y^2-z^2)
                 +((T2+T3)/2)xy+((T3-T2)/2)xz.                (13)

All `Tj` are nonnegative. Equations (5),(8) yield uniformly in `s`

    T1/4 <=3b/8,
    (T2+T3)/2 <=b(L+5/2),
    |T3-T2|/2=b(cA-cC)<=b(L+4b).                               (14)

For completeness, `cA>=cC` follows from
`(1/2+b)-4b(1-b)=(1-2b)(1/2-2b)>=0`. The function
`b(L+5/2)` is increasing on this interval and is below `1/100`.
The derivative of `b(L+4b)^2` is
`(L+4b)(L+12b-2)>0`, so

    b(L+4b)^2 <=(log125+4/1000)^2/1000 <13/500.                (15)

Only the elementary upper bounds `log125<5` and `log2<7/10`
are used here; they follow from the positive exponential series.
Drop the negative `-T1 z^2/4` in (13), and bound
`b(L+4b)|xz| <=x^2/10+(5/2)b^2(L+4b)^2 z^2`. Then

    tr(T adj D)
      <=(21/200)x^2+(43/8000)y^2+(13/200)b z^2
      <=(1/7)[(99/100)x^2+(49/100)y^2+(49/100)b z^2]
      <=F_w(D)/7.                                              (16)

The frame is invariant under changing the sign of active coordinate 1
or active coordinate 2, by permuting rows. The pair frame has the same
invariance. Averaging these two signs removes all cross terms between
diagonal and off-diagonal entries in `F_w`; thus
`F_w(A)>=F_w(D)`. Since `T` is diagonal, determinant algebra gives

    tr(T adj A)=tr(T adj D)
                 -T1|A23|^2-T2|A13|^2-T3|A12|^2.              (17)

Use (16)-(17) in the exact X004/X008 scalar identity
`-H''=FI_s-2tr(T adj A)`, `FI_s=F_v/s+F_w/q`. Since
`F_w(D)<=F_w(A)<=FI_s`, this proves the first lower bound in (10).
Finally `cC=log(1+b-2b^2)>=b/2` by `log(1+u)>=u/(1+u)`.
Equations (4),(8) show `min_j wj>=b^2`. Equation (12) implies
`F_w(D)>=(49/50)b||D||_F^2`. Hence the first lower bound in (10)
is at least `(7/10)b||D||_F^2+2b^2 sum_{i<j}|Aij|^2`, which
is at least `b^2||A||_F^2`. This completes the full-Hermitian proof.
