## 1. An actual complementary experiment

For a strict two-site kernel `J`, put `w=det J`, `x=det(I-J)`,
`B=J-wI`, and `u=tr B=1-x-w`. The number-resolved fermionic state is

    rho_J = x |vac><vac| direct-sum B direct-sum w |full><full|.

This is positive, has trace one, and its original occupation measurement is
exactly the two-site DPP: the singleton masses are the diagonal entries of B.
It is enough to check this after diagonalizing J for the positivity assertion:
the eigenvalues of B are `lambda(1-mu)` and `mu(1-lambda)`.
The original measured basis remains fixed. Fix an eigenbasis of J at the reference point, and consider the original affine
path `J+tE`. Write `E=[[d1,h],[conj(h),d2]]` in that fixed auxiliary basis.
The one-particle block derivative is `B'=E-w'I`, so its off-diagonal derivative
is h. Measure particle number first. On the one-particle event, use either the
two Pauli X eigenvectors or the two Pauli Y eigenvectors of this fixed basis.
These are complementary, number-preserving beam-splitter settings. Let `I_X,I_Y`
be the Fisher informations of the complete number-resolved outputs,
and let `C` be the Fisher information of particle number alone. At the base,

    I_X-C=4(Re h)^2/u,     I_Y-C=4(Im h)^2/u.              (1)

Indeed the two mixed outcome masses are u/2, with derivatives `u'/2 +/- Re h`
or `u'/2 +/- Im h`. Their squared-score sum is `u'^2/u+4(Re h)^2/u`,
or the analogous imaginary formula. Empty/full outcomes are unchanged.
Thus (1) concerns actual measurements, without quantum entropy substitution.

## 2. Exact relation to the sharp count determinant square

X054 gives `C-2 eta det E=L(E)^2+2 eta |h|^2`, with its explicit sharp
count coefficient eta and its longitudinal square L. Consequently

    C-2 eta det E = L(E)^2 + (eta u/2)(I_X+I_Y-2C).        (2)

The original occupation measurement has full Fisher information

Keeping it yields the exact identity

    I_Z-2 eta det E = L(E)^2+W_Z

All terms on the right are nonnegative. This realizes the transverse
determinant remainder by two complementary measured Fisher energies, rather
than treating an unmeasured coherence norm as free available information.

## 3. The sharp measured determinant coefficient

Here are explicit formulas for all quantities; they also handle repeated eigenvalues.
Let `lambda,mu` be the eigenvalues of J and keep one eigenbasis fixed. Put

    a=(1-mu)^2/x+mu^2/w+(1-2mu)^2/u,
    c=(1-lambda)^2/x+lambda^2/w+(1-2lambda)^2/u,
    b=1/u, alpha=sqrt(a), gamma=sqrt(c), s=alpha gamma,
    eta=b+s, k=eta/s.                                      (4)

The count Fisher is `C=a d1^2+2b d1 d2+c d2^2`. The expressions follow by
differentiating the empty, full and one-particle probabilities; their cross
coefficient simplifies to `b=1/u`. These are X054's sharp count quantities.
Introduce real coordinates

    t=(alpha d1+gamma d2)/2, v=(alpha d1-gamma d2)/2,
    z1=sqrt(s) Re h, z2=sqrt(s) Im h, r=(v,z1,z2).

Then exact expansion gives

    det E=(t^2-|r|^2)/s,
    C-2 eta det E=4v^2+2k(z1^2+z2^2).                      (5)

Let `y,z` be the two original singleton masses, and let `y',z'` denote their
derivatives under E in the original measured basis. The original conditional
contrast is the square of the following real linear functional:

    Z(E)=(z y'-y z')/sqrt(u y z),   W_Z=Z(E)^2.

Define `g0,g1,g2,g3` uniquely by
`Z(E)=g0 t+g1 v+g2 z1+g3 z2`, and put `g=(g1,g2,g3)`.
Transform E into the original basis and use `B'=E-w'I` to obtain `y',z'`;
the score from a rotated basis never replaces the original score.

**Theorem.** Let epsilon be the unique nonnegative solution of

    epsilon [1+g1^2/(4+epsilon)
                +(g2^2+g3^2)/(2k+epsilon)] = g0^2.         (6)

Then the largest possible coefficient beta such that

    I_Z(E) >= 2 beta det E  for every Hermitian E

is

    beta=eta+(s/2)epsilon.                                 (7)

It is finite and independent of the auxiliary eigenbasis used in its formulas.
Moreover

    beta-eta >= s g0^2/[2(1+g1^2/4+(g2^2+g3^2)/(2k))].     (8)

Thus beta is strictly larger than eta exactly when `g0!=0`: equivalently,
the sharp count equality ray `E=diag(gamma,alpha)` changes the actual
conditional singleton probabilities. This is a test in the original basis.

**Proof.** Put `D=diag(4,2k,2k)`. From (3) and (5),

    I_Z-2 beta det E
      =r^T(D+epsilon I)r+(g0 t+g^T r)^2-epsilon t^2.        (9)

For any epsilon>=0 set `c_e=g^T(D+epsilon I)^(-1)g` and
`r_e=g0(D+epsilon I)^(-1)g/(1+c_e)`. Completing the square gives

    (9)=(r+r_e t)^T(D+epsilon I+gg^T)(r+r_e t)
                         +[g0^2/(1+c_e)-epsilon]t^2.       (10)

The first matrix is positive definite. The derivative of the left side of
(6), considered as a function of epsilon, is
`1+sum_j g_j^2 D_jj/(D_jj+epsilon)^2>0`; its value starts at zero and
tends to infinity. Thus (6) has the asserted unique solution, and (10)
proves (7)'s inequality, with an explicit positive-square remainder `R_J(E)`.
At that solution take `t=1,r=-r_e`. If epsilon>0, then

    |r_e|^2=epsilon/(1+c_e) sum_j g_j^2/(D_jj+epsilon)^2
                    <=c_e/(1+c_e)<1.

For epsilon=0, (6) forces g0=0 and r_e=0. Hence the equality direction
always has positive determinant, proving sharpness of beta. For any larger
coefficient the inequality fails on this same E. Optimality also proves the
basis independence. Finally the denominators in (6) are at least their
epsilon=0 values, which proves (8). All quantities are positive or finite
because J is strict. A small physical interval exists for every Hermitian E.

Equation (10) is an actual sum of squares: its first term equals
`sum_j(D_jj+epsilon)(r_j+(r_e)_j t)^2+[g^T(r+r_e t)]^2`.
Together with (1), this proves the precise Fisher uncertainty statement;
no general quantum entropy or uncertainty theorem is imported.

## 5. Why complementary Fisher information is not a free payment

Take `J=diag(1/5,3/5)` and `E=[[0,1],[1,0]]`. Every original atom first
derivative vanishes, so `I_Z=C=0`, whereas the actual Pauli experiment has

    I_X-C=4/u=50/7,   I_Y-C=0.

The original law is a product law and its face log ratio is zero. Its
occupation entropy Hessian is therefore also zero in this direction.
Thus there is no positive universal constant bounding either original
Fisher information or original negative entropy curvature below by the
complementary phase Fisher information. This failure is not a singular
kernel issue: J and I-J are strictly positive. Taking a product with one
more strict Bernoulli coordinate gives a three-site example. Arbitrarily
small dense complex perturbations preserve strictness, while these two
original quantities tend to zero and the phase information stays positive.
The auxiliary eigenvectors may be chosen continuously since 1/5!=3/5. In (3), the transverse information is instead paid by `-2 eta det E`;
here det E=-1. That sign is precisely why the local uncertainty identity
does not manufacture the missing indefinite three-site comparison (13).
