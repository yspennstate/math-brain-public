## Main new identity

For the physical two-mode effects of X066, set

    k_y = alpha_y + gamma_y - tr B_y,
    p_y(M) = alpha_y + tr[(B_y-alpha_y I)M] + k_y det M,
    G_y(M) = B_y-alpha_y I + k_y adj M,
    S_y(M) = G_y(M)/p_y(M).

Here G is the Hermitian matrix gradient, so p'_y=tr(G_y A). At any strict active input, omit identically zero effects. Every remaining p_y is positive. Elementary two-by-two determinant expansion gives

    det G_y = k_y p_y.                                           (1)

The **full** generating Gaussian identity gives the stronger polynomial relation

    det(sum_y z^y G_y) = (sum_y z^y p_y)(sum_y z^y k_y).           (2)

Let Y,Z be independent with law p and W=Y+Z in {0,1,2}^n, and write

    r_w(y,z) = p_y p_z / Pr(W=w),   y+z=w.

Extracting each coefficient of (2), and using (1), proves

    E[det(S_Y-S_Z) | W=w] = 0.                                  (3)

Thus global compatibility is an exact conditional cancellation of signed two-dimensional exterior areas. Local Gaussianity alone gives only E det S=0; it does not give (3).

Since sum pS=0 and sum p det S=sum k=0, an independent-copy expansion gives

    sum_y k_y log p_y
      = (1/2) E[det(S_Y-S_Z) log r_W(Y,Z)].                      (4)

Consequently the full measured Shannon curvature, with delta=det A, is exactly

    -H'' = FI + delta E[det(S_Y-S_Z) log r_W(Y,Z)],
    FI = E[tr(S_Y A)^2].                                       (5)

The equations keep the full atom Fisher information. They are valid at every active covariance, not only the central input.

## Positive-area decomposition for definite directions

In each nonempty fiber let r_w^*=max r_w(y,z), and set

    omega(y,z)=log(r_W^*/r_W(y,z)) >= 0,
    D=S_y-S_z,
    d_A=tr(A D),
    Disc_A(D)=d_A^2-4 det(A) det(D).

For a definite Hermitian A, Disc_A(D)>=0: change the sign of A if necessary, conjugate D by A^(1/2), and use the square of the difference of its two real eigenvalues. This is the elementary two-dimensional mixed-volume discriminant; no imported Brunn–Minkowski theorem is required.

The conditional cancellation (3) permits subtracting log r_w^* in (5). Also E d_A^2=2 FI. Therefore

    -H'' = (1/4) E[(2-omega)d_A^2 + omega Disc_A(D)].             (6)

This is an exact sum of a possibly signed scalar part and a nonnegative exterior-area remainder. In particular every physical active rank-two definite direction has nonpositive entropy curvature whenever

    max_w log(max_{y+z=w} p_y p_z / min_{y+z=w} p_y p_z) <= 2.   (7)

More sharply, (7) can be replaced by the full-score quadratic condition

    E[omega (tr A(S_Y-S_Z))^2] <= 4 FI(A).                      (8)

The same determinant-free condition simultaneously for all Hermitian test matrices gives a matrix certificate for all definite active directions. It is sufficient, not necessary, because (6) retains an additional positive term.

The oscillation in (7) is invariant under **every positive coordinate field tilt** p_y -> const p_y product_i t_i^(y_i), since its numerator and denominator have the same y+z. Thus the criterion measures interactions rather than ordinary atom dynamic range: every product law has oscillation zero, regardless of its marginal biases. No assumption that tilted active covariance follows an affine path is made.

## Exact remaining volume inequality

For arbitrary physical effects, the still-unproved positive-direction statement is precisely that the averaged negative part of (2-omega)d_A^2 in (6) is paid by its positive part plus omega Disc_A(D). This is a finite, explicit complementary-pair exterior-volume inequality using every coefficient identity. Merely dropping the discriminant is stronger and may fail. No universal claim is made.

For indefinite A the discriminant in (6) need not be nonnegative. Therefore (7) alone is not claimed to prove every active direction. A separate full-Hessian criterion and an exact noncommuting example follow below.

## Full-Hessian area criterion

Write each score as S_y=s_{0y}I+s_{1y}sigma_1+s_{2y}sigma_2+s_{3y}sigma_3 in the ordinary Pauli basis, and let

    C = E[s_Y s_Y^T],     Omega = max omega.

The coefficients are real and E s=0. Local Gaussianity gives tr(G_L C)=0 for G_L=diag(1,-1,-1,-1). Let

    L = E[det(S_Y-S_Z) log r_W(Y,Z)].

By (3), within each fiber subtract the midpoint of the largest and smallest log conditional masses. Because |det(t_0 I+t·sigma)|<=t_0^2+|t|^2,

    |L| <= (Omega/2) E|det(S_Y-S_Z)|
          <= (Omega/2) E||s_Y-s_Z||^2
          = Omega tr C.                                      (9)

For A=a_0 I+a·sigma, equation (5) becomes

    -H'' = 4 a^T C a + L(a_0^2-|a|^2)
          >= [4 lambda_min(C)-Omega tr C] ||a||^2.            (10)

Thus

    Omega tr C <= 4 lambda_min(C)                             (11)

certifies **all four active tangent directions**, including indefinite ones. This is a sufficient local matrix criterion. It is not claimed to hold universally. The Lorentz cancellation is essential in (9); replacing it by the unconditional local constraints does not justify subtracting a different constant in each fiber.

## An explicit physical noncommuting family with all-direction concavity

The following quantified domain demonstrates that (11) applies to physical noncommuting effects. Its limited radius is stated explicitly; this is not a proof on the entire active cube.

Let n_1,...,n_4 be the four unit tetrahedral vectors

    (0,0,1),
    (2 sqrt(2)/3,0,-1/3),
    (-sqrt(2)/3,sqrt(6)/3,-1/3),
    (-sqrt(2)/3,-sqrt(6)/3,-1/3).

Choose an isometry V:C^2->C^4 whose row effects are

    v_i v_i^* = H_i = (I+n_i·sigma)/4.

Each H_i is positive rank one with trace 1/2, and sum H_i=I; choosing any rank-one square root for each row constructs V. Set

    J=(I_4-VV^*)/2,

**Theorem.** The measured occupation entropy is concave on the entire convex domain (12), in all four real entries of M, with the quantitative bound

    -H''_M[A,A] >= ||A||_F^2/8.                              (13)

The effects do not commute. At M=I/2 the physical full kernel is I_4/2, every atom has probability 1/16, and its score is

For a singleton outcome y={i}, S_y=-I+n_i·sigma and det S_y=0. Reconstructing its fixed Gaussian effect gives

### Proof of the uniform bound

Put r=1/128 and a=1/2-r. Every covariance in (12) has K=I/2+T with T=V(M-I/2)V*, rank T<=2, ||T||<=r. Its positive odds matrix is

    L_K=K(I-K)^(-1)=I+V R V*,

where the two exceptional eigenvalues lie between b^(-1) and b, b=(1+2r)/(1-2r). The same eigenvalue interval holds for every principal submatrix. Since rank(L_K-I)<=2, at most two eigenvalues of any principal submatrix differ from one. Therefore

    |log det (L_K)_S| <= 2 log b.

The atom formula p_S=det(I-K)det(L_K)_S proves the dimension-free pair bound

    Omega <= 8 log b <= 16/63.                               (15)

The last step uses b=65/63 and log b<b-1=2/63. This estimate does not assume any alignment of M with the tetrahedral frame.

For any Hermitian A=a_0I+a·sigma, the one-coordinate mean derivatives are

    t_i=tr(H_i A)=(a_0+n_i·a)/2,
    sum_i t_i^2=a_0^2+|a|^2/3 >= ||a||^2/3.                  (16)

The determinantal occupation covariance matrix has diagonal K_ii(1-K_ii) and off-diagonal -|K_ij|^2. Its maximum absolute row sum is at most 1/4+r^2: write K=I/2+T, use sum_j |T_ij|^2<=r^2, and retain the two negative diagonal-square corrections. Hence its operator norm is at most 1/4+r^2. The scalar score covariance identity Cov(score_A,X_i)=t_i and Cauchy–Schwarz applied to sum_i t_i X_i give

    FI(A) >= sum_i t_i^2/(1/4+r^2),
    lambda_min(C) >= 1/[3(1+4r^2)].                          (17)

There is also an elementary uniform score bound for every positive Gaussian effect:

    -(I-M)^(-1) <= S_y <= M^(-1).                            (18)

For alpha_y>0 put D=B_y/alpha_y>=0 and N=M(I-M)^(-1). Then

    p_y=alpha_y det(I-M)det(D+N),
    S_y=-(I-M)^(-1)
       +(I-M)^(-1)(D+N)^(-1)(I-M)^(-1).

The lower bound is immediate; the upper bound follows from (D+N)^(-1)<=N^(-1). Boundary Gaussian effects follow by continuity. ## Details of the coefficient and entropy identities

For completeness write C_y=B_y-alpha_y I and k_y=alpha_y+gamma_y-tr B_y. Local Gaussianity gives

    det C_y = alpha_y k_y.

Because adjugation is linear in two dimensions and tr(adj C adj M)=tr(CM),

    det(C_y+k_y adj M)
      =alpha_y k_y+k_y tr(C_y M)+k_y^2 det M
      =k_y p_y.

The identical calculation applies to the generating polynomials because X066 proves det B(z)=alpha(z)gamma(z); this establishes (2). Let G(z)=sum z^y G_y and p(z),k(z) similarly. Polarization gives

    sum_{y,z} z^(y+z) p_y p_z det(S_y-S_z)
      =2p(z)k(z)-2det G(z)=0.

Every coefficient is zero separately, proving (3). This uses all fibers, including those with many distinct complementary-pair decompositions. It is stronger than merely checking adjacent effects.

To prove (4), expand

    det(S_y-S_z)=det S_y+det S_z-tr(adj S_y S_z).

Independence, E S=0, E det S=0, and linearity of adj show

    E[det(S_Y-S_Z)(log p_Y+log p_Z)]
        =2 E[det S_Y log p_Y]
        =2 sum_y k_y log p_y.

The conditional cancellation allows subtracting log Pr(W) to replace log p_Y+log p_Z by log r_W. All sums are finite. Atoms identically zero are excluded before logarithms; every remaining effect is strictly positive on a strict active Gaussian input. The same formulas apply whenever a positive normalized globally compatible effect family is supplied, whether or not sufficiency for physical realizability is known. The concrete family (12) is directly physically realized.
