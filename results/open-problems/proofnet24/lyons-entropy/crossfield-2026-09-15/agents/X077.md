## 1. A useful exact tensor identity

Index the eight original atom probabilities by subsets. For any eight entries
`t0,t1,t2,t3,t12,t13,t23,t123`, define Cayley's quartic polynomial

    Det(t)=t0²t123²+t1²t23²+t2²t13²+t3²t12²
      -2[t0t123(t1t23+t2t13+t3t12)
           +t1t23t2t13+t1t23t3t12+t2t13t3t12]
      +4[t0t12t13t23+t123t1t2t3].

For a Hermitian three-site kernel K and its **original measured** atom tensor p,

    Det(p)=-4[Im(K12 K23 K31)]².                         (1)

One direct proof uses inclusion moments mS=det KS, related to p by a local
two-by-two triangular transform of determinant one on each tensor leg. The
quartic above is invariant under those three elementary transforms (expand
the displayed polynomial). For `m0=1`, `mi=ai`,
`mij=ai aj-xk`, `m123=a1a2a3-sum ai xi+2q`, direct expansion gives
`Det(m)=4(q²-x1x2x3)`, where `xi=|Kjk|²` and
`q=Re(K12K23K31)`. This proves (1). No quantum-measurement substitution occurs.

Equivalently, with

    gij=p_i p_j-p0 p_ij,
    C=[p0² p123+2p1p2p3-p0(p1p23+p2p13+p3p12)]/2,

the purely polynomial identity is

    4(C²-g12g13g23)=p0² Det(p).                         (2)

Thus X054's exact complex-Hermitian feasibility discriminant is precisely
the negative Cayley discriminant, together with its three face conditions.
In the genuinely complex region, the loop magnitude is itself a function
of the eight measured probabilities: `|Im(K12K23K31)|=sqrt(-Det(p))/2`.
This does not discard phase or imply that real bases maximize curvature.

## 2. Stripping phases from the entire frozen quadratic form

Assume `0<K<I`, all three edges are nonzero, and use X015's coefficients

    c_i=log[p_j p_k/(p0 p_jk)],
    tau=log[p0 p12 p13 p23/(p1 p2 p3 p123)],
    M=diag(c_i)+tau K,       m_i=Mii.

Both `c_i` and `c_i+tau` are nonnegative: the two conditional two-site
tables have determinant deficit equal to the squared conditional coherence.
Thus `M>0`: use `M=diag(c)+tau K` if tau>0, or
`M=diag(c+tau)+(-tau)(I-K)` if tau<0. If tau=0, a zero c_i would force both
conditional coherences to vanish. Their formulas
`Kjk-Kji Kik/Kii` and `Kjk+Kji Kik/(1-Kii)` would then force Kjk=0,
contrary to the nonzero-edge hypothesis. Hence M is also positive then.

For a **zero-diagonal** Hermitian velocity A, orient its entries as
`z1=A23,z2=A31,z3=A12`, and similarly `k1=K23,k2=K31,k3=K12`.
Put `FI=sum p'_S²/p_S`. The complete measured curvature is

    -H''_K(A)=FI+F_K(A),
    F_K(A)=-2tr(M adj A)
      =2sum_i m_i |z_i|²-4tau Re(sum_i k_i z_j z_k).       (3)

Define two real matrices, indexed by the original three sites,

    Bminus_ii=Bplus_ii=m_i,
    Bminus_ij=-|tau| |Kij|,   Bplus_ij=|tau| |Kij|.       (4)

**Theorem 1 (exact frozen-curvature criterion).** The following are equivalent:

1. `F_K(A)>=0` for every zero-diagonal Hermitian A.
2. `Bminus>=0`.
3. `det Bminus>=0`.

Under any of these conditions, every such A satisfies the stronger bound
`-H''_K(A)>=FI`. If Bminus is positive definite, then

    -H''_K(A)>=FI+lambda_min(Bminus)||A||F².              (5)

**Proof, including the distinction from a kernel gauge.** When tau!=0,
write `alpha_i=arg(tau k_i)` and set

    phi_i=(alpha_i-alpha_j-alpha_k)/2,
    z_i=exp(i phi_i)(r_i+i s_i),    r_i,s_i real.

Then `phi_j+phi_k=-alpha_i`, so all three coefficients
`tau k_i exp(i(phi_j+phi_k))` become the positive numbers `|tau k_i|`.
These are **three independent rotations of the velocity variables**. They
are not diagonal conjugation of K, do not change K, and do not impose any
condition on the loop phase of K. The resulting exact identity is

    F_K(A)=2 r^T Bminus r+2 s^T Bplus s.                 (6)

For tau=0 the same identity holds with all phi_i=0.
Every one-by-one and two-by-two principal minor of either matrix in (4)
equals the corresponding minor of M and is positive. Moreover, writing
`q=Re(K12 K23 K31)` and `R=|K12 K23 K31|`,

    det Bplus=det M+2|tau|³[R-sign(tau)q]>0.             (7)

Hence Bplus>0 by its leading principal minors. For Bminus, the same
positive lower-order principal minors show that positive semidefiniteness
is equivalent to its determinant being nonnegative. This can also be
seen by a two-by-two Schur complement. Equation (6) proves all equivalences.
For the quantitative bound, `s^T Bplus s>=|s|^T Bminus |s|`, and
`||A||F²=2(|r|²+|s|²)`. This proves (5). No entropy term was omitted. ∎

If det Bminus<0, precisely one of its eigenvalues is negative; the other
two are positive by the positive two-by-two principal minors. Thus at most
one of the six fixed-diagonal frozen directions needs a Fisher payment.
This does **not** assert that the actual entropy curvature is negative
there; the Fisher contribution in (3) is still present.

## 3. A scalar criterion written entirely in the measured atom tensor

Every quantity in Theorem 1 can be obtained without choosing a kernel gauge
or reconstructing complex entries. Define the inclusion marginals

    a_i=sum_(S contains i) p_S,
    b_i=sum_(S contains j,k) p_S,
    x_i=a_j a_k-b_i,
    q=[p123-a1a2a3+sum_i a_i x_i]/2,
    Delta=Det(p),      R=sqrt(q²-Delta/4).

For feasible p these equal respectively `Kii`, `det K_jk`, `|Kjk|²`, the
real loop product, the Cayley discriminant, and the absolute loop product.
The entire complex phase contribution is retained through Delta. Define

    E(p)=c1c2c3+tau sum_i a_i c_j c_k
               +tau² sum_i c_i b_i+tau³ p123,
    D(p)=E(p)-2|tau|³[R+sign(tau)q].                    (8)

Use zero for the last term when tau=0. Principal-minor expansion gives
`E(p)=det M`, and determinant expansion in (4) gives

    D(p)=det Bminus.                                   (9)

Consequently `D(p)>=0` is the **necessary and sufficient** atom-tensor
criterion for the full frozen six-direction sign. It is a sufficient
condition for the actual entropy sign in all those directions. It is not
claimed necessary for entropy concavity. In particular the obstruction
depends on `sqrt(q²-Delta/4)+sign(tau)q`, which is strictly positive at
every genuinely complex base and cannot be replaced by a real-phase extreme.

On real-loop bases with `tau q<=0`, the subtraction in (8) vanishes,
so the condition follows immediately from `M>0`. X078 independently
develops that real-loop domain and extensions; the present report owns
the exact phase-stripped criterion and its tensor-discriminant form.

## 4. A monotone interval of certified complex phases

Fix the three diagonal entries a_i and three positive squared magnitudes
x_i. Put `R=sqrt(x1x2x3)`. Vary only
`q=Re(K12K23K31)` over its physical phase interval; the possible imaginary
parts are `+/-sqrt(R²-q²)`, and give the same criterion.
The fixed two-site minors of K and I-K must be positive, and the remaining
strict-contraction restrictions are

    -R<=q<=R,
    q>L:=[sum_i a_i x_i-a1a2a3]/2,
    q<U:=[product_i(1-a_i)-sum_i(1-a_i)x_i]/2.            (10)

These conditions are also sufficient: choose edge phases with the given
real product, then apply the Hermitian principal-minor criterion to K and
I-K. Thus (10) is the exact physical interval, including real-loop endpoints
when their determinants remain positive.

The eight atoms are affine in q, with derivatives

    dp_S/dq=2(-1)^(3-|S|).

In particular, throughout the physical interval,

    dc_i/dq=2(1/p_j+1/p_k+1/p0+1/p_jk)>0,
    dtau/dq=-2sum_S 1/p_S<0,
    d(c_i+tau)/dq<0.                                  (11)

**Theorem 2 (central phase interval).** On the tau>0 portion of the physical
interval, the matrix `Bminus/tau` is strictly increasing in Loewner order
as q increases. On the tau<0 portion, `Bminus/(-tau)` is strictly decreasing.
The set of phases satisfying (8) is therefore an interval, possibly empty
or all of the physical interval. If tau vanishes at an interior physical
phase, that interval contains an open neighborhood of that phase. On each
side there is at most one endpoint at which D(p)=0.

**Proof.** The off-diagonal entries of the divided matrices are fixed at
`-|Kij|`. For tau>0 their diagonal entries are `a_i+c_i/tau`, with derivative

    [c_i' tau-c_i tau']/tau²>0.

Here c_i>=0, c_i'>0 and tau'<0. For tau<0 put lambda=-tau and
d_i=c_i+tau>=0. The diagonal entries are
`1-a_i+d_i/lambda`, with derivative

    [d_i' lambda-d_i lambda']/lambda²<0.

This proves strict Loewner monotonicity. The least eigenvalue is strictly
monotone on each branch, so it has at most one zero. Since all lower-order
principal minors stay positive, this is also exactly the possible determinant
zero from (9). At tau=0 the matrix is the strictly positive diag(c_i).
If no zero of tau occurs, there is only one monotone branch; otherwise the
two certified portions join across that positive center. These facts prove
the interval assertion and include endpoint cases. ∎

This theorem concerns the **sign of the frozen quadratic form** throughout
a phase family. It does not claim that H or its full Hessian is maximized
at a real phase, and does not differentiate a nonlinear phase path as though
it were an affine kernel path. At every certified phase it covers all six
affine fixed-diagonal velocity directions, including directions that change
the squared magnitudes and leave the phase family.

## 5. The remaining Fisher payment is one explicit scalar on each phase wing

The following reduction retains every atom score, rather than replacing
them by particle count or marginal information. In the coordinates of (6),
partition

    Bminus=[[C,v],[v^T,m3]],   C>0,
    rho=m3-v^T C^(-1)v=det(Bminus)/det C.

Write `t=r3`, `y=(r_(1:2)+C^(-1)v t,s1,s2,s3)` in R^5, and
`G=diag(C,Bplus)>0`. The linear map from (y,t) to the original Hermitian A
is specified by these formulas and the fixed rotations of Section 2.
Let `U` be its 8-by-5 atom-derivative matrix and `f` its 8-vector derivative
for t=1,y=0. These are **original atom derivatives** `Dp_S(K)[A]`, computed
for example by inclusion-moment differentiation. Put W=diag(1/p_S).
Then the full fixed-diagonal entropy curvature is exactly

    -H''=y^T(2G+U^T W U)y+2t y^T U^T W f
                              +(2rho+f^T W f)t².        (12)

Completing the positive five-variable square proves that all fixed-diagonal
directions have the desired entropy sign if and only if

    2rho+f^T[W-WU(2G+U^T WU)^(-1)U^T W]f>=0.          (13)

For rho>=0 this follows from Theorem 1 (or directly by minimizing a sum of
nonnegative squares). For rho<0 it is the exact remaining Fisher payment
on the two possible uncertified phase wings. No positivity of an optimized
Hermitian direction is assumed. This is a different reduction from X053:
X053 removes all three angular modes at every base, whereas (12)-(13)
expose the single adverse frozen mode after stripping the anomalous phases.
Neither reduction by itself handles general moving diagonal entries.

## 6. Scope, overlap, and verification

The proved family is the explicit atom-tensor region (8), with the complete
six-direction curvature bound (5), and Theorem 2 proves a global monotone
phase-interval structure for that region. Its complement has exactly the
one-scalar, full-score obstruction (13). The universal sign of (13), all
moving-diagonal Schur costs, and the full three-site conjecture remain open.

The uncertified complex wings are a real obstruction to dropping Fisher
information. For example, let

    K0=(1/10)[[1,1,2],[1,2,2-i],[2,2+i,5]],
    K_epsilon=K0+epsilon I,  0<epsilon<3/10.

Writing K0=VV*/10 with rows of V equal to `(1,0),(1,1),(2,i)` proves its
spectrum is `0,1/10,7/10`: the two eigenvalues of V*V are 1 and 7.
The seven nonfull atoms of K0 are all positive, namely
`(p0,p1,p2,p3,p12,p13,p23)=(27,8,14,44,1,1,5)/100`.
Its loop is `(2-i)/500`. As epsilon decreases to zero, c_i remain finite
and tau tends to positive infinity because only p123 vanishes. Therefore

    det(Bminus(K_epsilon))/tau³
      -> -2[Re Q0+|Q0|]=-(2+sqrt(5))/250<0.

Thus the frozen criterion fails on an analytic family of strict, dense,
genuinely complex kernels with three unequal diagonals. This does not
contradict entropy concavity; the full score term in (13) is essential.
