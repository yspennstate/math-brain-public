## 1. Exact Gauss decomposition with the tangential acceleration retained

Let \(\Phi(K)=(p_x(K))_x\), with \(0<K<I\), and equip the probability simplex with the Fisher metric
\[
\langle u,v\rangle_p=\sum_x u_xv_x/p_x.
\]
For a Hermitian direction \(A\), put \(q=D\Phi_K[A]\), \(r=D^2\Phi_K[A,A]\). Let \(T=\operatorname{im}D\Phi_K\) and write the Fisher orthogonal decomposition \(r=r_T+r_N\). The elementary entropy chain rule is
\[
-D^2H(K)[A,A]=\|q\|_p^2+\langle r,p\log p\rangle_p.
\tag{1}
\]
For any chosen lift \(B\) of the tangent acceleration, meaning \(D\Phi_K[B]=r_T\), and \(\eta=\Pi_{T^\perp}(p\log p)\), this becomes exactly
\[
\boxed{-D^2H(K)[A,A]=\|D\Phi_K[A]\|_p^2-DH_K[B]+\langle r_N,\eta\rangle_p.}
\tag{2}
\]
The \(B\)-term is independent of the choice of lift because entropy is a function of \(\Phi(K)\). In particular, a bound on the normal term alone is insufficient unless the affine-kernel tangential acceleration \(B\) is also controlled. Formula (2) uses the mixture connection in the ambient simplex. For the Levi-Civita Fisher connection, replace \(r\) by \(r-q^2/(2p)+\frac12\|q\|_p^2p\) and retain the corresponding explicit connection terms; these cannot be discarded.

A sharp normal bound follows immediately:
\[
|\langle r_N,\eta\rangle_p|\le\|r_N\|_p\|\eta\|_p.
\tag{3}
\]
This is not claimed as a universal compensation estimate. It is stronger than projecting only off constants and occupations, because every tangent score is removed, but it introduces the indispensable tangent acceleration in (2).

## 2. Why three sites have no generic normal obstruction

For a Hermitian three-site kernel use the seven diagonal-unitary invariants
\[
a_i=K_{ii},\quad b_{ij}=|K_{ij}|^2,\quad c=2\Re(K_{12}K_{23}K_{31}).
\]
The seven nonempty inclusion moments are
\[
m_i=a_i,\quad m_{ij}=a_ia_j-b_{ij},\quad
m_{123}=a_1a_2a_3-a_1b_{23}-a_2b_{13}-a_3b_{12}+c.
\tag{4}
\]
Their Jacobian with respect to \((a_i,b_{ij},c)\) is triangular with nonzero diagonal \((1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,1)\). Inclusion-exclusion is an invertible affine change between these moments and the seven independent atom probabilities. If all three edges are nonzero and the cycle product has nonzero imaginary part, the seven invariants are locally independent: fix two edge phases by diagonal gauge and vary the remaining phase; \(\partial c/\partial\phi=-2|K_{12}K_{23}K_{31}|\sin\phi\ne0\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}D\Phi=7\), the full simplex dimension.

Consequently \(r_N=0\) for every direction at such a base. All the difficult three-site entropy acceleration in (1) is tangential. This does not contradict negative score-null curvature: the affine line in a gauge tangent is not the nonlinear gauge orbit and can have nonzero second atom derivative.

## 5. Erasure channels preserve principal-submatrix entropy exactly

Suppose a binary-input finite-output channel has output alphabet split into three disjoint sets `Z`, `R_0`, `R_1`, and fixed probability laws `q`, `r_0`, `r_1` on these sets, such that

    C(y|0) = e q(y) + (1-e) r_0(y),
    C(y|1) = e q(y) + (1-e) r_1(y).

Here `0<=e<=1`; zero-weight classes may be removed. Equivalently, every output row has input likelihood ratio `0`, `1`, or infinity. Write `tau=1-e`. The output identifies whether the input was erased and, if retained, its exact value. The erasure event is independent of the input.

For independent such channels indexed by `[m]`, write `R` for the random set of retained inputs and

    w_R = product_{i in R} tau_i product_{i not in R} e_i.

For **every** law `P` on `{0,1}^m`, not only a DPP, the chain rule gives the exact identity

    H((tensor_i C_i)P) = A(P_1,...,P_m) + sum_R w_R H(P_R),                 (28)

where the correction is affine in the input means `p_i=P(X_i=1)`:

    A = sum_i [h(e_i)+e_i H(q_i)
         +tau_i ((1-p_i)H(r_{i,0})+p_i H(r_{i,1}))].                    (29)

To prove this, the output reveals `R`; its entropy is `sum_i h(e_i)`. Conditional on `R`, the retained bits have law `P_R`. Each erased coordinate has a fresh law `q_i`, and each retained coordinate has a fresh law `r_{i,X_i}`. All these internal random choices are independent given the retained bits. Applying the conditional entropy chain rule proves (28)–(29). No loss of Fisher information is invoked.

For a DPP input of kernel `M`, `P_R=P_{M_R}`. Along any affine kernel line, (29) has zero second derivative. Moreover, the exact output atom scores depend only on `R` and its retained bit pattern. Thus both the full Fisher metric and the full acceleration contraction split:

    I_out(M;E) = sum_R w_R I_{M_R}(E_R),                              (30)
    sum_y p_y'' log p_y = sum_R w_R sum_x p_{R,x}'' log p_{R,x},       (31)
    -H_out''(M;E) = sum_R w_R [-H''(P_{M_R})[E_R,E_R]].               (32)

For (31), factors from internal output randomization cancel after summing the second derivative of the fixed one-site marginals, which are affine. These are complete atom-score equalities, not covariance projections.

## 6. A new arbitrary-guide fractional-background theorem

Let `V:C^2 -> C^m` be an arbitrary isometry. For every `i`, take a disjoint pair of measured coordinates and its two orthonormal vectors

    u_i=(e_{i,+}+e_{i,-})/sqrt(2),
    v_i=(e_{i,+}-e_{i,-})/sqrt(2).

Let `U:C^m -> C^{2m}` embed coordinate `i` as `u_i`, and let

    B = (1/2) sum_i v_i v_i^*.

**Theorem.** For arbitrary `m>=2`,

    T -> H(P_{B + U V T V^* U^*})

is concave on the full matrix interval `0<=T<=I_2`, allowing arbitrary noncommuting Hermitian two-by-two endpoints. This introduces `m` independently mixed background eigenmodes of eigenvalue `1/2`; it is not the zero-background active-support theorem, nor a projection-background substitution.

In fact, for every positive contraction `M` of size `m`, with no rank restriction, the following exact entropy identity holds:

    H(P_{B+UMU^*}) = (3m/2) log 2 + 2^{-m} sum_{R subseteq [m]} H(P_{M_R}).  (33)

The identity is universal. The concavity conclusion of the theorem uses the rank-two restriction `M=VTV^*`.

**Proof of the encoding law.** In one pair, the guide-absent endpoint kernel `(1/2)vv^*` has atom law

    C(00|0)=1/2,  C(10|0)=C(01|0)=1/4,  C(11|0)=0.

The guide-present endpoint `uu^*+(1/2)vv^*` has

    C(00|1)=0,  C(10|1)=C(01|1)=1/4,  C(11|1)=1/2.

Thus the local channel erases with probability `1/2`, with equiprobable singleton outputs; otherwise it reveals `0` as `00` and `1` as `11`. Its full multiblock law is

    P_{B+UMU^*} = (tensor_i C_i)P_M.                                 (34)

One may prove (34) directly by expanding all inclusion determinants in the rank-one guide columns: each block inclusion determinant is affine in its guide bit, and the coefficient for a selected set of guides is the corresponding principal minor of `M`. This is also precisely C061's general guide-channel identity; only the chosen erasure endpoints and arbitrary-guide support application are new here.

Apply (28): `h(1/2)+(1/2)H(q_i)=(3/2)log 2` per block, and the revealing laws are point masses. This proves (33).

For `M=VTV^*`, every principal submatrix has form

    M_R=V_R T V_R^*,

whose fixed combined support has dimension at most two. Let `J_R` be an isometry onto `ran V_R`, of dimension `r_R<=2`, and write `V_R=J_R C_R`. Then

    M_R=J_R (C_R T C_R^*) J_R^*.

The active matrix `C_R T C_R^*` is an affine function of `T` and a positive contraction: `||C_R||<=||V||=1`. The established root theorem `ROOT_RANK_TWO_SUPPORT.md` proves that the entropy of every such fixed-support DPP is concave in this active matrix (the `r_R<=1` case is ordinary affine-probability concavity). Hence every summand in (33) is concave in `T`; their positive average proves the theorem. Continuity handles spectral and atom boundaries.

**Measured-basis distinction.** The vectors `u_i,v_i` define an actual new kernel in a fixed `2m`-coordinate measurement basis. The theorem does not diagonalize the measured kernel or substitute quantum entropy. The physical kernel has spectrum consisting of the two eigenvalues of `T`, `m` copies of `1/2`, and `m-2` zeros. Its generic rank is `m+2`. Complementing the latent kernel yields the analogous family with `m-2` eigenvalues equal to one.

## 7. A full-score quantitative consequence

For a strict `T` and a definite Hermitian direction `E` with `delta=det E>0`, the rank-two active-support theorem gives

    -D^2 H(P_{VTV^*})[E,E] >= (8-2log 2) delta.

The all-retained term `R=[m]` alone in (32) therefore yields

    -D^2 H(P_{B+UVTV^*U^*})[E,E]
        >= 2^{-m} (8-2log 2) det E.                                 (35)

All other terms are nonnegative. There is a substantially stronger explicit bound obtained from **all two-element retained sets**, rather than using only the all-retained term. If `R={i,j}`, write `d_R=|det V_R|^2`. The ordinary two-measured-site bound is

    -D^2 H(P_{V_R T V_R^*})[E,E] >= 8 d_R det E.

For the balanced encoding, `w_R=2^{-m}`. Cauchy-Binet gives `sum_{|R|=2} d_R=1`, so

    -D^2 H(P_{B+UVTV^*U^*})[E,E] >= 2^{3-m} det E.                  (36)

When `m>2`, the all-retained subset is distinct from every two-element set, so (35) and (36) add:

    -D^2 H(P_{B+UVTV^*U^*})[E,E]
        >= 2^{-m}(16-2log 2) det E,   m>=3.                         (37)

Those initial bounds can be strengthened uniformly in the number of guides. Write `c_*=8-2log 2`. For every retained set `R` with `rank V_R=2`, the fixed-support theorem applied to its polar factorization supplies

    -D^2 H(P_{V_R T V_R^*})[E,E]
        >= c_* det(V_R^*V_R) det E.

Rank-zero and rank-one sets contribute nonnegative curvature, with zero displayed determinant. Cauchy-Binet and independent retention give the exact exterior-moment identity

    sum_R w_R det(V_R^*V_R)
      = sum_{i<j} tau_i tau_j |det V_{ij}|^2
      = det(V^* diag(tau_i) V).                                     (38)

Consequently, for arbitrary erasure probabilities,

    -H_out''(VTV^*;VEV^*)
        >= c_* det(V^*diag(tau_i)V) det E.                           (39)

This is a universal compensation theorem on the stated fixed two-dimensional active support. In the balanced physical encoding, it yields the **dimension-independent** lower bound `c_*/4`. Retained pairs have the sharper coefficient 8 instead of `c_*`, so their improvement can be added without double counting:

    -D^2 H(P_{B+UVTV^*U^*})[E,E]
        >= [2-(1/2)log 2+2^{1-m}log 2] det E.                       (40)

For general erasure probabilities, the improvement is

    2log 2 det E sum_{i<j} tau_i tau_j
                    product_{k notin {i,j}}e_k |det V_{ij}|^2.

These constants are lower bounds, not asserted sharp. The full proof retains every Fisher contribution and every logarithmic acceleration contribution via (30)–(32); merely retaining the linear occupations of the physical law does not produce these identities.

## 9. Arbitrary fractional background eigenvalues have an explicit erasure realization

The balanced pairs are not necessary. Every fractional background eigenvalue `lambda in [0,1]` has a three-coordinate realization with erasure probability `e=min(lambda,1-lambda)`.

If `lambda<=1/2`, take

    v=(1,1,0)/sqrt(2),
    u=(sqrt(lambda/[2(1-lambda)]),
       -sqrt(lambda/[2(1-lambda)]),
        sqrt((1-2lambda)/(1-lambda))).                              (41)

If `lambda>=1/2`, take

    u=(1,1,0)/sqrt(2),
    v=(sqrt((1-lambda)/(2lambda)),
       -sqrt((1-lambda)/(2lambda)),
        sqrt((2lambda-1)/lambda)).                                 (42)

The displayed vectors are orthonormal. The guide-absent and guide-present kernels are `lambda vv^*` and `uu^*+lambda vv^*`. Their atoms, for either case, have the simple formulas

    C_0(empty)=1-lambda,  C_0({j})=lambda |v_j|^2,
    C_1({j})=(1-lambda)|u_j|^2,
    C_1({j,k})=lambda |u_jv_k-u_kv_j|^2.                            (43)

All other atoms have probability zero. The two singleton outcomes `j=1,2` each have equal probability `e/2` under both inputs. Every remaining supported atom occurs under only one input. The common mass is exactly `e`, and each revealing class has mass `1-e`. Thus this is an erasure channel of the type in Section 5.

Therefore, for **arbitrary** `lambda_1,...,lambda_m in [0,1]`, build the three-site vectors (41) or (42) on disjoint blocks, let `Ue_i=u_i`, and put

    B_lambda=sum_i lambda_i v_i v_i^*,
    K_lambda(T)=B_lambda + U V T V^* U^*.                           (44)

Then `T -> H(P_{K_lambda(T)})` is concave on the full `0<=T<=I_2` interval. The nontrivial fixed background eigenvalues are the arbitrary prescribed `lambda_i`; the physical spectrum additionally contains the two eigenvalues of `T` and `2m-2` zeros. Its exact entropy is an affine function of `T` plus the weighted principal-submatrix sum in (28).

Since the retention probabilities `tau_i=max(lambda_i,1-lambda_i)` are at least `1/2`, (39) gives the uniform definite-direction bound

    -D^2H(P_{K_lambda(T)})[E,E]
          >= [2-(1/2)log 2] det E,  det E>0.                        (45)

No fractional-background limit or weak-correlation condition is imposed. Boundary `lambda_i` merely produce deterministic/no-erasure channels and are included by continuity.

Root also suggested the independent four-site realization for an arbitrary `e in [0,1/2]`, keeping background eigenvalue fixed at `1/2`:

    u=(sqrt(e),sqrt(e),sqrt(1-2e),0),
    v=(sqrt(e),-sqrt(e),0,sqrt(1-2e)).                              (46)

These vectors are orthonormal. Formula (43) at `lambda=1/2` shows that singleton outcomes 1 and 2 have common probabilities `e/2`; every other supported output reveals the input. This lets the independent erasure probabilities vary while the background spectrum remains fixed. It obeys the same weighted entropy and full Fisher identities. Equation (46) is root's contributed extension, checked here by the displayed atom formulas.
