## 1. The original one-event Fisher energy

Let `0<K<I_3`, let `A` be any Hermitian zero-diagonal affine velocity, and let `p=det K`. Write `q_S=Dp_S[A]`. Then the actual full score satisfies

    FI >= (Dp[A])^2/[p(1-p)].                                      (1)

Indeed, apply Cauchy–Schwarz to all seven nonfull atoms, whose velocities sum to `-Dp[A]`. Thus (1) is the Fisher information of the original measured indicator `1_{X={1,2,3}}`, not an auxiliary measurement. Its omitted remainder is exactly a nonnegative conditional Fisher information.

Use X015/X077's `c_i>=0`, `tau`, and `M=diag(c)+tau K`. The complete curvature in this direction is

    -H'' = FI + 2 sum_i c_i |A_jk|^2 - tau D²det(K)[A,A].            (2)

Every acceleration term is retained. When `tau<0`, complement K and A, so the event is the original empty atom and the new positive interaction is `-tau`.

## 2. Exact phase-free scalar certificate on an adverse wing

Assume `tau>0`, all edges nonzero, and use `r_i=|K_jk|`, `R=r1 r2 r3`, and `q=Re(K12 K23 K31)`. Define

    Bminus_ij=-tau |Kij|,    Bplus_ij=tau |Kij|,
    uminus_i=r_j r_k-Kii r_i,

             + (R-q)/R uplus^T Bplus^{-1} uplus <= 0.              (3)

This is an exact necessary and sufficient condition for the *stronger one-event certificate*. It is sufficient, not necessary, for entropy concavity. Every input in (3) is recoverable from the original eight atoms by X077's moment formulas; no loop angle or imaginary-part sign remains. The full Cayley geometry is retained through `R²=q²-Det(p_atoms)/4`.

**Proof.** Apply the three independent tangent rotations of X077. If `theta=arg(K12 K23 K31)`, they turn the frozen form into

    F=2 x^T Bminus x+2 y^T Bplus y.

The same rotations turn the full-atom derivative into

                -2 sin(theta/2) uplus^T y.                       (4)

To check (4), orient `k_i=K_jk`; the complex derivative coefficient is
`-Kii conjugate(k_i)+k_j k_k`. Its product with the tangent phase is
`(r_j r_k-Kii r_i)cos(theta/2)+i(r_j r_k+Kii r_i)sin(theta/2)`.

For any invertible real symmetric F with exactly one negative eigenvalue and any positive w, `F+w ll^T>=0` if and only if `l^T F^{-1}l<=-1/w`. One proof uses a positive/negative block congruence, completes the positive square, and applies its scalar Schur complement. Equivalently, the updated form still has at most one negative eigenvalue; its determinant is `det(F)(1+w l^T F^{-1}l)`, and `det(F)<0`. The zero-determinant equality leaves a positive semidefinite matrix, since the restriction to `l`-orthogonal vectors is positive definite when `l^T F^{-1}l<0`.

Apply this fact to `F=2 diag(Bminus,Bplus)`, `w=1/[p(1-p)]`, and (4). Using `2cos²(theta/2)=(R+q)/R` and `2sin²(theta/2)=(R-q)/R` gives (3). This proves the criterion including its equality case.

## 3. An explicit analytic sufficient bound

Let `tau>0`, `p=det K`, and let `0<delta<=lambda<=mu<1` be the eigenvalues of K. Set `cmin=min_i c_i` and `tplus=max(t,0)`. Then every zero-diagonal Hermitian A satisfies

    -H''_K(A) >= [cmin - p tau (tau(1-p)-3/2)_+
                                  (lambda^{-2}+mu^{-2})] ||A||_F². (5)

Thus a nonnegative coefficient in (5) certifies *all* fixed-diagonal directions, including any adverse frozen mode. The estimate uses only the full-atom event plus nonnegative frozen squares. By complementation there is an identical empty-event bound when tau<0, replacing the kernel by I-K and c by c+tau.

**Proof.** Diagonalize K only to compute determinant derivatives; the original measured entropy, c coefficients, and zero-diagonal condition remain in the original basis. In that auxiliary eigenbasis write

    K=diag(D,delta),  D=diag(lambda,mu),
    A=[[B,w],[w*,d]], H=D^{-1/2} B D^{-1/2}, T=tr H,
    v=d+delta T, P=det D, p=delta P.

Direct block determinant expansion along the original affine path gives

    Dp[A]=P v,
    D²p[A,A]=2P[vT-delta(T²-det H)-w*D^{-1}w].                    (6)

The event Fisher term is `P v²/[delta(1-p)]`. Completing its v-square in (2) yields the exact lower bound

    -H'' >= P/[delta(1-p)] [v-tau delta(1-p)T]²
          +2 sum_i c_i |A_jk|² +2tau P w*D^{-1}w
          +p[2tau(T²-det H)-tau²(1-p)T²].                        (7)

For the two real eigenvalues of Hermitian H, `det H<=(tr H)²/4`, so the last line is at least `p tau[3/2-tau(1-p)]T²`. Also

    T² <= (lambda^{-2}+mu^{-2}) ||B||_F²
       <= (lambda^{-2}+mu^{-2}) ||A||_F²,
    2 sum_i c_i |A_jk|² >= cmin ||A||_F².

The latter identity uses the original zero diagonal, not the auxiliary basis. Drop positive squares in (7) and keep the positive-part convention to obtain (5).

In particular the entire region `tau(1-p)<=3/2` needs no quantitative lower bound on cmin: its one-event payment is nonnegative in every fixed-diagonal direction.

## 6. A full nine-direction certificate in the eight-atom polynomial ring

This section extends the *known* Lorentz/Schur reduction of old-097 into an explicit atom-domain scalar. It does not claim to prove that scalar's universal sign. Unlike Sections 1–5, all moving diagonals are included.

For each nonempty original atom S, put

    E_S=(-1)^(3-|S|) adj(K-I_(S^c)),
    V_ST=p_S 1_(S=T)-p_S p_T,

    v_S=tr(M E_S),
    C=T+dV,                                                    (9)

where S,T run over the seven nonempty subsets. Each E_S is the actual kernel gradient of p_S. The covariance V is positive definite because all eight atoms are positive. The Gram matrix T is positive semidefinite; hence C>0 for connected bases, where M>0 and d>0.

    Phi(p)=v^T adj(C)v-2det C.                                  (10)

At any connected strict three-site base, the full entropy Hessian is negative semidefinite if and only if `Phi(p)>=0`. If `Phi(p)<0`, the entropy Hessian has exactly one positive eigenvalue and eight negative eigenvalues; one explicit positive-curvature witness is

    Astar=M-sum_(S nonempty) (C^(-1)v)_S M E_S M.                (11)

No unitary measurement substitution is used.

**Proof.** The seven nonempty atom jets determine the eighth by normalization, so

    -H''=FI+d||B||_F²-d(tr B)².                                (12)

## 7. A sharp finite extremal reduction for the quantitative collar

The coarse spectral factor in (5) can be replaced by a single explicitly optimized weighted off-diagonal norm. Suppose `c_i>0`, choose a unit eigenvector u for the eigenvalue delta, and define in the original measured basis

    R_delta=K^{-1}-delta^{-1}uu*,
    chi=2 sum_(i<j) |(R_delta)_ij|²/c_k,                       (16)

where k is the complementary index. Thus R_delta has eigenvalues `0,lambda^{-1},mu^{-1}`. For every zero-diagonal Hermitian A, the scalar T in (6) is exactly `tr(R_delta A)`. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives the sharp bound

    T² <= chi [2 sum_i c_i |A_jk|²].                          (17)

If chi>0, equality occurs precisely on the one real-dimensional family

    Aii=0,   Aij=t (R_delta)_ij/c_k,   t real.                 (18)

This also follows directly from the Lagrange equations for maximizing T² at fixed weighted off-diagonal norm. It is an actual extremal-direction theorem, not a numerical optimizer or an assumption about symmetric bases.

With `beta=p tau(tau(1-p)-3/2)_+`, (7) now proves

    -H''_K(A) >= (1-beta chi) 2 sum_i c_i |A_jk|².             (19)

Hence `beta chi<=1` is a stronger fully explicit one-event sufficient certificate. Equation (5) follows because `chi<=(lambda^{-2}+mu^{-2})/cmin`. The direction in (18) extremizes the *one scalar estimate (17)*; it is not asserted to extremize the entropy Hessian itself or the base kernel. For repeated smallest eigenvalues, any chosen smallest-eigenvalue unit vector gives a valid bound; taking the best such choice can strengthen it.
