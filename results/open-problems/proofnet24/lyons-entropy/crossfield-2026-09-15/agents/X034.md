## 1. Score order and a quantitative completely positive subtraction

For a strict Hermitian contraction `R` on `m` measured coordinates put

    r_T=P_R(T), Y_T=(R-I_(T^c))^-1,
    s_C(T)=tr(Y_T C), Phi_R(C)=E_r[Y_T C Y_T].                 (1)

Differentiating atom normalization once and twice gives

    E_r Y_T=0, E_r[s_C(T)Y_T]=Phi_R(C).                       (2)

For the second equality, pair with arbitrary Hermitian `D` and differentiate `sum_T r_T=1` in directions `C,D`. Thus `Phi_R` is both the full score Gram operator and a completely positive map.

If `j` is absent from `T`, the inverse update is

    Y_(T+j)-Y_T=(r_T/r_(T+j))Y_T e_j e_j*Y_T>=0.             (3)

Indeed the determinant ratio of the two signed atom matrices is `-r_(T+j)/r_T`; applying the rank-one inverse formula gives (3). Thus `s_C` is an increasing set function whenever `C>=0`.

For a fixed real set function `g`, write `F_g(R)=E_R g` and `Q_g(R)=grad F_g(R)` in the Hermitian trace pairing. The exact score formula and the established determinantal stochastic-domination theorem imply

    Q_g(R)=E_r[g(T)Y_T]>=0 whenever g is increasing.          (4)

To check the hypotheses, for every `D>=0` and sufficiently small `h>=0`, both `R` and `R+hD` are positive contractions. Lyons, Theorem 2.9, gives `F_g(R+hD)>=F_g(R)`. Differentiation proves `tr(Q_g D)>=0`. All set functions here are finite and bounded. The source was checked directly: https://arxiv.org/html/1406.2707v1, Theorem 2.9.

**Lemma 1 (compensated score covariance).** Let `C>=0`, and suppose a real function `psi` has derivative at least `c` on the interval containing all `s_C(T)`. Then

    E_r[psi(s_C)Y_T]=c Phi_R(C)+Q_(psi(s_C)-c s_C)(R),       (5)

and the final matrix is positive semidefinite.

**Proof.** Equation (2) gives the identity. Equations (3) and the derivative bound make the frozen function `psi(s_C)-c s_C` increasing, so (4) proves its gradient positive. The score field inside that frozen function is evaluated at the original `R`; it is not differentiated with the expectation. This distinction is essential. ∎

For `K=[[a,k*],[k,R]]`, let `q_T=a-k*Y_T k` be the actual conditional occupation probability of the first coordinate. Strictness gives `0<q_T<1`. Taking `C=kk*` and `psi(s)=log((1-a+s)/(a-s))` gives the explicit strengthening

    B_K:=E_r[log((1-q_T)/q_T)Y_T]>=4 Phi_R(kk*).             (6)

This follows because `psi'(s)=1/[q(1-q)]>=4` on the entire range between the conditional values. For the existing one-row direction `[[d,w*],[w,0]]`, its complete curvature consequently satisfies

    -H''>=FI+8 E_r|w*Y_T k|^2
        >=4d^2+24 E_r(Re w*Y_T k)^2+8 E_r(Im w*Y_T k)^2.    (7)

Here `FI=E_r[(d-2 Re w*Y_T k)^2/(q_T(1-q_T))]`, and `E_r w*Y_T k=0`. Equation (7) is a quantitative matrix-score refinement of that existing slice theorem; the new scope below comes from changing the exterior block as well.

For comparison, prior report 050 bounds the same positive gradient below by the diagonal matrix with entries `epsilon^(m-1) sum_T Delta_j g(T)`, for a permitted spectral noise parameter `epsilon`. Equation (6) instead retains the coherent full-score matrix `Phi_R(kk*)` with the dimension-independent coefficient four. No claim of a universal ordering between those two lower bounds is made.

## 2. The rank-one adjugate cancellation

The following algebra works for every fixed real set function, without submodularity assumptions. For `K=[[a,k*],[k,R]]`, set

    f_0(T)=f(T), g(T)=f(T+first)-f(T).

The Schur atom formula proves

    F_f(K)=F_f0(R)+a F_g(R)-k*Q_g(R)k.                     (8)

This is the bordered expectation identity of prior report 010. Also, for any vector `k`,

    Q_g(R+s kk*) is affine in s,
    (D Q_g(R)[kk*])k=0.                                  (9)

For a direct proof, expand `F_g(R)=sum_U ghat(U) det R_U`. Each gradient is an embedded adjugate. At an invertible matrix `V`,

    D adj(V)[uu*]=(u*V^-1 u)adj(V)-adj(V)uu*V^-1,

which annihilates `u`. The adjugate along `V+s uu*` is affine in `s` by determinant multilinearity (or by the same inverse formula). Polynomial continuation handles any singular principal matrix. Sum over `U` to prove (9). No matrix-gradient monotonicity under Loewner order is asserted.

## 3. A new finite-segment theorem

**Theorem 2.** Fix a measured coordinate and let

    K_t=[[a_0+t d, (k+t w)*],
         [k+t w, R_0+t F]],   F=lambda kk*,  0<=t<=1,      (10)

where `d,lambda` are real and `w` is an arbitrary complex vector. Suppose

    0<=K_0,K_1<=I,   0<=R_0+3F<=I,   lambda*d<=0.          (11)

Then the fixed-basis DPP entropy is concave on the entire affine segment (10). In particular,

    H(K_t)>=(1-t)H(K_0)+t H(K_1)+2d^2 t(1-t).              (12)

For strict inputs the exact compensated curvature representation is

    -H''(K_t)=FI_(K_t)(A)
      -2lambda*d k*Q_(g_t)(R_t)k
      +2 w*Q_(g_t)(R_0+3tF)w,                             (13)

where `A=K_1-K_0`, `R_t=R_0+tF`, and

    g_t(T)=log[p_(K_t)(T)/p_(K_t)(T+first)].

Every term on the right of (13) is nonnegative under (11).

**Proof.** Freeze `f=-log p_(K_t)` while taking the second kernel derivative at the chosen `t`. The odds representation `p_S=det(I-K)det L_S`, `L=K(I-K)^-1>0`, makes this frozen `f` supermodular. Explicitly, the positive two-by-two Schur complement of `L_T` gives `p_(T+i)p_(T+j)>=p_T p_(T+i+j)`. Therefore `g_t=Delta_first f` is increasing.

Apply (8) along the affine path, still with `f` fixed. Both `F_f0(R_s)` and `F_g(R_s)` are affine in `s` because `F` has rank one. Let `Q_s=Q_g(R_s)` and `Q'=D Q_g(R_s)[F]`. Equation (9) gives `Q_s''=0`, `Q'k=0`, and

    D²F_f(K_t)[A,A]
      =2lambda*d k*Q_t k-2w*Q_t w-4t w*Q'w
      =2lambda*d k*Q_g(R_t)k-2w*Q_g(R_0+3tF)w.            (14)

The last step is the exact affine adjugate identity `Q_t+2tQ'=Q_g(R_0+3tF)`. Hypothesis (11) makes both gradient arguments positive contractions for every `t`; (4) makes both gradients positive. Finally,

    H''=-FI+D²F_(-log p)[A,A]

proves (13), retaining both Fisher information and the complete atom acceleration. Marginalizing the score to the first occupation gives `FI>=d²/[a_t(1-a_t)]>=4d²`. Integration yields (12).

For closed endpoints, use `K_j,epsilon=(1-2epsilon)K_j+epsilon I`. The regularized data obey (10) with `k_epsilon=(1-2epsilon)k`, `w_epsilon=(1-2epsilon)w`, `d_epsilon=(1-2epsilon)d`, and `lambda_epsilon=lambda/(1-2epsilon)`. Thus the product `lambda_epsilon*d_epsilon=lambda*d` keeps its sign, and the extrapolated exterior block becomes `(1-2epsilon)(R_0+3F)+epsilon I`. Apply the strict theorem and let epsilon decrease to zero, using finite-atom entropy continuity. ∎

When `lambda!=0`, `k!=0`, and `w` is not collinear with `k`, the direction in (10) has rank exactly three. On its active space, the determinant is `-lambda ||k||² ||w_perp||²`; its inertia is `(2,1)` for `lambda>0` and `(1,2)` for `lambda<0`. This follows by using the orthonormal coordinates `first,k/||k||,w_perp/||w_perp||`; the indicated off-diagonal two-by-two block already has one eigenvalue of each sign. Thus (10) is not an arbitrary sum of already permitted rank-two directions.

In fact (14) proves concavity of `F_f(K_t)` for **every fixed supermodular** potential `f` on this family. It does not assert such a sign for arbitrary directions. The parameter can be centered at any reference time `tau`: if `F=lambda(k_0+tau w)(k_0+tau w)*`, the same conclusion holds whenever `lambda*d<=0` and `R_0+(3t-2tau)F` stays a contraction over the requested interval. Choosing the midpoint as `tau` gives symmetric exterior extrapolation conditions.

## 4. Positive square-face transport in every dimension

The preceding sign also has a finite transport representation. For a strict contraction `R` and vector `u`, define the signed atom derivative `b(R,u)=D P_R[uu*]`. Choose `epsilon>0` with `R+epsilon uu*<I`. Rank-one determinant affinity gives `P_(R+epsilon uu*)-P_R=epsilon b(R,u)` exactly. The stochastic-domination theorem supplies a monotone coupling, using the stochastic-order/coupling equivalence stated immediately before Theorem 2.7 in the same inspected Lyons source. Route each coupled transition `T subset U` along an increasing coordinate path and divide its mass by epsilon. This constructs nonnegative edge weights `c_(T,j)(R,u)` satisfying

    b(R,u)=sum_(T,j) c_(T,j)(delta_(T+j)-delta_T).          (15)

No simultaneous or diamond coupling is needed: the two laws and all routing paths are finite. For any real `g`, pairing (15) gives `u*Q_g(R)u=sum c_(T,j) Delta_j g(T)`.

At time `t` in Theorem 2, take

    z_(T,j)=-lambda*d c_(T,j)(R_t,k)
             +c_(T,j)(R_0+3tF,w)>=0.

For the square on the distinguished coordinate and `j`, with all other coordinates fixed by `T`, write `D_face=delta_00-delta_10-delta_01+delta_11`. Equations (14) and (15), paired with **every** table `f`, prove the exact full-atom acceleration identity

This extends the positive-face mechanism to the stated arbitrary-dimensional rank-three family; it makes no assertion about the entire exterior operator.
