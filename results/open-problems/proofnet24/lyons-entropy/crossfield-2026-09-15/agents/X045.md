    D_a={(u,v): u²+v²+|a|<=1},                               (2)

## 3. An exact two-clique entropy potential

Put `x=u²,y=v²,s=x+y,d=x-y,c=a²` and `psi(q)=q log q`. Define

    q_1=(1+s)²-c,       q_2=(1-s)²-c,
    q_3=1+s²+c+2d,      q_4=1+s²+c-2d,
    q_5=1-s²+c,         q_6=1-s²-c.                          (8)

All six are positive in the strict domain. The conditional `M_++` has atom
probabilities `(q_1,q_5,q_5,q_2)/4`, in order `00,10,01,11`. The conditional
`M_+-` has probabilities `(q_6,q_4,q_3,q_6)/4`. The remaining two conditionals
have the same entropies by complementation and complex conjugation. Equation (5)
therefore becomes the exact finite potential

    F_c(x,y):=4log2-H(K_a(u,v))
       =[psi(q_1)+psi(q_2)+psi(q_3)+psi(q_4)
                         +2psi(q_5)+2psi(q_6)]/8.           (9)

Its strength domain is the convex triangle `x,y>=0, x+y<1-|a|`.

## 4. The negative determinant correction is fully compensated

For any real strength direction let `alpha=dx+dy`, `beta=dx-dy`. Differentiating
(9) gives, with no omitted acceleration,

    D²F = I_q + (alpha²/4) log R,
    R=q_1 q_2 q_3 q_4/(q_5² q_6²),
    I_q=(alpha²/2){(1+s)²/q_1+(1-s)²/q_2}
         +(1/2){(s alpha+beta)²/q_3+(s alpha-beta)²/q_4}
         +s² alpha²(1/q_5+1/q_6).                           (10)

The logarithm is the full determinant acceleration correction. Its separate sign
can be negative: at `c=1/4,x=19/100,y=1/100`, an interior physical diamond,

Let `r_+=c/(1+s)²`, `r_-=c/(1-s)²`; both belong to `[0,1)`. Since `|d|<=s`,

    R >= (1-r_+²)(1-r_-²)/(1-r_+r_-)²
      >= (1-r_+²)(1-r_-²).                                 (11)

The first bound follows by minimizing `q_3q_4=(1+s²+c)²-4d²` at `|d|=s`;
the remaining product simplifies exactly to the displayed ratio. For `0<=r<1`,
`-log(1-r²)<=r²/(1-r²)<=1/(1-r)`. The first two Fisher terms in (10) are
`alpha²[1/(1-r_+)+1/(1-r_-)]/2`. They therefore dominate the potentially negative
logarithm with at least half of their own value left. Also `q_3,q_4<=4`, because
`c<(1-s)²` and `s<1`. Consequently

    D²F >= alpha²/2 + beta²/4
          >= ((dx)²+(dy)²)/2.                              (12)

Indeed the two middle Fisher terms alone contribute at least
`[(s alpha+beta)²+(s alpha-beta)²]/8 >= beta²/4`.
This proves uniform strong convexity after retaining the complete signed term.

## 5. Axis monotonicity and return to affine kernel entries

Convexity alone does not justify composing with the squares `u²,v²`. The missing
monotonicity also has an exact formula. On `x=0,y=s`, put

    Delta=atanh(r_-)-atanh(r_+)>=0.

At this axis, (11)'s first ratio equals `sech² Delta`. Direct differentiation of
(9), using the same ratio, gives

    (F_c)_x(0,s)=[Delta-s log cosh Delta]/2 >=0.              (13)

For clarity, the derivative with the variable `s²` temporarily frozen is
`Delta/2`; its full chain-rule correction is `-s log cosh Delta/2`. Both are
included in (13). The inequality follows from `log cosh Delta<=Delta` and `s<1`.
Interchanging `x,y` gives the other axis derivative.

By (12), `R_c(x,y):=F_c(x,y)-(x²+y²)/4` is convex. Its derivative in `x` at
`x=0` is nonnegative by (13), and its second `x` derivative is nonnegative;
hence it is nondecreasing in `x`. The same holds for `y`. Its triangle domain
is convex and downward closed. On an affine chord in `(u,v)`,

    u_t²=(1-t)u_0²+t u_1²-t(1-t)(u_1-u_0)²,

and likewise for `v`. Monotonicity followed by convexity of `R_c` proves (3).
Equivalently, the exact local chain rule is
