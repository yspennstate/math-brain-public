# X016 — rank-three cardinality Fisher compensation beyond scalar bases

Status: completed constructive theorem and exact identities, 15 September 2026. The universal entropy conjecture and the arbitrary-frame scalar eight-dimensional gap of X008 are not proved. This report establishes a uniform nonscalar theorem on every rank-three measured support and an exact all-layer Fisher decomposition. All paths below are affine in the active covariance `M`, never in the odds matrix.

## 1. Main theorem

Let `V:C^3 -> C^n` be any isometry and let `0<M<I_3`. Put

    L=M(I-M)^(-1),   kappa=lambda_max(L)/lambda_min(L),
    v=tr[M(I-M)],    m=lambda_max(M).

**Theorem.** If `kappa<=2`, then for every nonzero semidefinite Hermitian active direction `A`, the measured determinantal entropy satisfies

    -D^2 H(P_{VMV*})[A,A]
       >= (13/2700) FI_M(A)
       >= (13/2700) (tr A)^2 / v > 0.                 (1)

There is no restriction on the number of measured coordinates, row norms, zero minors, or relative eigenvectors of `M` and `A`. In particular, `M` can be nonscalar and `[M,A]` can be nonzero. This extends the scalar semidefinite theorem in X008 to one uniform spectral region of the full active domain. It does not establish all Hermitian directions.

A more general computable condition, without the assumption `kappa<=2`, is proved below.

**Nonperturbative corollary.** If `M0<=M1` are strict active matrices and both have odds condition number at most two, then the full Jensen inequality holds on the segment between them, for every measured embedding `V`. The endpoints can be nonscalar and noncommuting.

Here is the domain check needed for that corollary. For any `kappa>=1`, the condition `cond(M(I-M)^(-1))<=kappa` is equivalent, with `ell=lambda_min(M)` and `m=lambda_max(M)`, to

    m<=f_kappa(ell),   f_kappa(x)=kappa x/[1+(kappa-1)x].

The function `f_kappa` is increasing and concave. Rayleigh-quotient bounds for a convex matrix combination give `m_t<=(1-t)m0+t m1` and `ell_t>=(1-t)ell0+t ell1`; scalar concavity then gives `m_t<=f_kappa(ell_t)`. Thus this active region is convex. For comparable endpoints the fixed segment direction is semidefinite, so integrating (1) proves the corollary.

## 2. Frozen logarithmic geometry and its odds distortion

Write `U_i=v_i* v_i`, `t_i=tr U_i`, `W_ij=w_ij w_ij*`, `b_ij=tr W_ij`, and `r_T=|det V_T|^2`, in the Hodge convention of X004/X008. All zero effects and zero triple minors are omitted. The exterior identities are

    sum_i U_i=I,      sum_ij W_ij=I,
    sum_{T containing i} r_T=t_i,
    sum_{T containing {i,j}} r_T=b_ij,
    sum_T r_T=1.

The supported-atom logarithmic Mobius coefficients depend only on the Gram matrix `VLV*`:

    c_ij(L)=log[(v_i L v_i*)(v_j L v_j*) / det(VLV*)_ij],
    c_T(L)=log[ product_{pairs in T} det(VLV*)_pair
                / (product_{i in T} v_i L v_i*) det(VLV*)_T ].

Define

    W(L)=sum_ij c_ij(L) W_ij,
    tau(L)=sum_T r_T c_T(L),
    T(M)=W(L)+tau(L)M.

The exact X004 identity and positivity theorem give

    -H''[A,A]=FI_M(A)-2tr(T(M) adj A),   T(M)>=0.      (2)

Let `W0=W(I)` and `tau0=tau(I)`. These are independent of the scalar covariance chosen. The X008 bounds used below are

    0<=W0<=log(2) I,
    0<=W0+tau0 I<=log(9/2) I.                       (3)

### 2.1 Pair-log distortion

For a supported pair let `E_ij` be its two-dimensional row span, let `L_ij` denote the compression of `L` to this plane, and put `n_i=v_i*/sqrt(t_i)`. Then

    c_ij(L)-c_ij(I)
      =log[(n_i*L n_i)(n_j*L n_j)/det L_ij].         (4)

If the eigenvalues of `L_ij` are `mu1<=mu2`, both Rayleigh quotients in (4) lie between `mu1` and `mu2`. The displayed ratio consequently lies in `[mu1/mu2,mu2/mu1]`, which is contained in `[1/kappa,kappa]`. Thus

    |c_ij(L)-c_ij(I)|<=log kappa,
    -log(kappa)I <= W(L)-W0 <= log(kappa)I.           (5)

The matrix inequality uses the exterior Parseval sum of the positive `W_ij`. Nearly dependent pairs cause no loss: the bound is independent of their angle.

### 2.2 Averaged cubic distortion is quadratic near scalar odds

For positive `B` set

    D(B)=3log(tr B/3)-log det B >=0.

**Lemma.** For every positive `L`,

    -D(L)<=tau(L)-tau0<=D(L^(-1)).                   (6)

To prove it, put `P_ij=W_ij/b_ij`. The two-dimensional determinant identity gives

    det L_ij=det L · tr(P_ij L^(-1)).

Using the triple inclusion marginals in the difference of the cubic coefficients gives exactly

    tau(L)-tau0
      =2log det L
       +sum_ij b_ij log tr(P_ij L^(-1))
       -sum_i t_i log(n_i*L n_i).                    (7)

For either rank-one Parseval effect family, scalar concavity of the logarithm, first inside each Rayleigh quotient and then across all effects of total trace three, gives

    tr log B <= sum_alpha z_alpha log(n_alpha*B n_alpha)
              <=3log(tr B/3).                       (8)

For the lower bound in (8), expand each unit vector in a spectral basis of `B`, apply scalar Jensen, and sum with `sum z_alpha n_alpha n_alpha*=I`. The upper bound is scalar Jensen with probabilities `z_alpha/3`. Apply (8) to both sums in (7) to obtain (6). No operator-Jensen or external information inequality is imported.

There is a sharp condition-number-only bound for the defect:

    D(B)<=D_kappa:=3log((kappa+2)/3)-log kappa        (9)

whenever `cond(B)<=kappa`. Indeed, after a scalar rescaling the logarithms of its three eigenvalues lie in `[0,log kappa]`. The function `3log[(exp x1+exp x2+exp x3)/3]-sum x_i` is convex, so its maximum on this cube occurs at a vertex. The two nonzero vertex values are

    3log((kappa+2)/3)-log kappa,
    3log((2kappa+1)/3)-2log kappa.

The first is larger, since the exponentiated comparison reduces to

    kappa(kappa+2)^3-(2kappa+1)^3=(kappa-1)^3(kappa+1)>=0.

In particular, at `kappa<=2` both defects in (6) are bounded by

    D_2=log(32/27).                                 (10)

The cubic variation is therefore `O((log kappa)^2)`, whereas individual logarithmic coefficients can vary to first order. This cancellation uses all three- and two-cardinality inclusion marginals.

## 3. A nonscalar upper bound for the complete curvature matrix

Define

    u_kappa(m)=log(2kappa)+m[log(9/4)+D_kappa].

**Proposition.** Every strict rank-three support satisfies

    0<=T(M)<=u_kappa(m) I.                          (11)

For the upper bound, first observe

    W0+tau0 M <= [(1-m)log2+m log(9/2)] I.

If `tau0>=0`, use `M<=mI` and the convex combination of the two inequalities (3). If `tau0<0`, simply use `W0+tau0 M<=W0<=log2 I`. Now add (5) and the upper bound in (6), using `M<=mI`, to obtain (11). The lower bound is precisely the established X004 positivity theorem for the actual odds geometry, not a conclusion drawn from these upper estimates.

When `kappa<=2`, (11) reduces to the particularly simple universal inequality

    T(M)<=[log4+m log(8/3)] I.                      (12)

## 4. Fisher compensation and the explicit uniform gap

For any active direction, the count statistic `N=|X|` satisfies

    Var(N)=tr[M(I-M)]=v,
    Cov(N,score_A)=D E(N)[A]=tr A.

Thus Cauchy-Schwarz gives `FI_M(A)>=(tr A)^2/v`. This uses all cardinalities through the exact count score; it does not assume any lower bound from one- or two-point measured covariances.

For semidefinite `A`, its adjugate is positive semidefinite and

    tr(adj A)<= (tr A)^2/3.

Equations (2) and (11) therefore prove the more general criterion

    epsilon=1-(2/3)v u_kappa(m)>0
       ==> -H''[A,A]>=epsilon FI_M(A)
                      >=epsilon (tr A)^2/v.         (13)

It remains to show a uniform positive epsilon for `kappa<=2`. One eigenvalue of `M` is `m`, and each of the other two contributes at most `1/4` to `v`. Hence

    v<=1/2+m-m^2.

The elementary strict estimates `log4<7/5` and `log(8/3)<1` give

    v u_2(m)<(1/2+m-m^2)(7/5+m).

For completeness, put `x=m-2/3`, so `-2/3<x<1/3`. Direct expansion yields

    3/2-(1/2+m-m^2)(7/5+m)
      =1/135-x/30+(12/5+x)x^2
      >=1/135-x/30+(3/2)x^2
      =(3/2)(x-1/90)^2+13/1800.

It follows that

    (2/3)v u_2(m)<=1-13/2700.

Substitution in (13) proves (1). The two logarithmic estimates can be checked without decimal approximations: the exponential series through degree five gives `exp(7/5)>4`, and the exponential series through degree three gives `exp(1)>8/3` after retaining any additional positive term.

## 5. Exact all-layer Fisher identity at a nonscalar active base

Set `Q=I-M`, `J=Q^(-1)`, `H=M^(-1)`, `d0=det Q`, and `d3=det M`. Define weighted exterior measurement forms

    F_v^L(B)=sum_i [tr(U_i B)]^2/tr(U_i L),
    F_w^(L^-1)(B)=sum_ij [tr(W_ij B)]^2/tr(W_ij L^-1).

Choose a spectral basis of `M`, writing its eigenvalues as `lambda_i`, `q_i=1-lambda_i`, and `a_i=A_ii` in that basis. Then the full Fisher information has the exact decomposition

    FI_M(A)=d0 F_v^L(JAJ)+d3 F_w^(L^-1)(HAH)
      -sum_{i=1}^3
        [(lambda_j-lambda_i)(lambda_k-lambda_i)
         /(lambda_i^2 q_i^2)] a_i^2,                (14)

where `{j,k}` is the complementary pair of eigenvalue indices. The identity is independent of the eigenbasis chosen inside a repeated eigenspace. In particular, the correction vanishes for scalar `M`, recovering the supplied identity `FI=F_v/s+F_w/(1-s)`. It also vanishes for every direction with zero diagonal in a spectral basis of `M`.

Here is a direct all-cardinality derivation. The atom probabilities and their affine derivatives are

    p_empty=d0,             p_empty'=-d0 tr(JA),
    p_i=d0 tr(U_i L),       p_i'=d0[tr(U_i JAJ)-tr(JA)tr(U_i L)],
    p_ij=d3 tr(W_ij L^-1),  p_ij'=d3[tr(HA)tr(W_ij L^-1)-tr(W_ij HAH)],
    p_T=d3 r_T,             p_T'=d3 r_T tr(HA).

After squaring, dividing by the atoms, and summing, the non-measurement correction is

    d0{[1+tr L]j^2-2j tr(J^2 A)}
      +d3{[1+tr L^-1]h^2-2h tr(H^2 A)},

where `j=tr(JA)` and `h=tr(HA)`. The coefficient of `2a_i a_j`, for distinct `i,j`, is zero: the two cardinality contributions reduce to `(2lambda_k-1)+(1-2lambda_k)`. The diagonal coefficient is

    1/(lambda_i q_i)
       -d0/(lambda_i q_i^3)-d3/(q_i lambda_i^3)
     =-(lambda_j-lambda_i)(lambda_k-lambda_i)/(lambda_i^2 q_i^2),

giving (14). With three distinct eigenvalues, the correction has one positive and two negative diagonal coefficients; it is not a positive remainder and cannot simply be dropped for a lower Fisher bound. This is one concrete obstacle to transplanting the scalar Fisher identity unchanged.

## 6. A positive exterior Fisher remainder at scalar bases

The following exact identity gives additional structure on the still unresolved scalar Fisher term. Define

    x_i=tr(U_i A)/t_i,  y_ij=tr(W_ij A)/b_ij,  h=tr A.

The contraction identity `sum_{j!=i} W_ij=t_i I-U_i` gives

    sum_{j!=i}b_ij=2t_i,
    sum_{j!=i}b_ij y_ij=t_i(h-x_i).

Expanding each conditional variance therefore proves

    F_w(A)-(F_v(A)+h^2)/4
       =(1/2)sum_i sum_{j!=i} b_ij
          [y_ij-(h-x_i)/2]^2 >=0.                  (15)

Each unordered pair appears twice on the right, with its two possibly different conditional means. In particular, on traceless directions the second-exterior measurement supplies at least one quarter of the first-exterior Fisher form. Equality holds exactly when every supported pair has `y_ij=(h-x_i)/2=(h-x_j)/2`. This is a proved positive remainder, rather than an assumed replacement of the full Fisher information by measured one-point data.

## 7. Remaining lemma and scope

At arbitrary scalar bases, X008 equation (25) remains the exact unresolved traceless Fisher-gap inequality. The proof here does not close it. At nonscalar bases, (14) supplies an explicit replacement for the scalar Fisher form, while (6) and (11) supply frame-uniform logarithmic control.

A precise remaining statement sufficient for the full arbitrary rank-three active theorem is

    d0 F_v^L(JAJ)+d3 F_w^(L^-1)(HAH)
     -sum_i [(lambda_j-lambda_i)(lambda_k-lambda_i)
             /(lambda_i^2 q_i^2)]a_i^2
       >= 2tr([W(L)+tau(L)M] adj A)                 (16)

for every Hermitian `A`. The new theorem establishes (16) for semidefinite `A` throughout `cond(L)<=2`, and more generally in the explicit region (13). It does not establish (16) for general indefinite directions or for all positive active matrices.

All formulas hold directly on the fixed supported-atom family. When an original pair or triple minor is zero, it is zero for every strict active `M`; omit it and use the same exterior sums. No frozen infinite logarithm is introduced. The only imported specialized statements are the proved X004/X008 identities, positivity, and bounds cited explicitly above. Their reports contain the exterior-algebra arguments, and X004 also checks the optional original stochastic domination reference: Russell Lyons, *Determinantal probability measures* (2003), https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0204325. All new inequalities here have finite-dimensional proofs in the report.
