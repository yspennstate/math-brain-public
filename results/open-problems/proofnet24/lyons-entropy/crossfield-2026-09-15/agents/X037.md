# X037 — resumming the four-cycle coefficient closes its Fisher scalar

Status: COMPLETE PROOF, 2026-09-15. The unrestricted Lyons conjecture remains open.

## 1. The theorem

Let `K(Z)=I_4/2+[[0,Z],[Z*,0]]`, where `Z` is any complex `2 by 2` matrix and
`||Z||op<1/2`, in the fixed measured basis. Then the measured entropy Hessian
is nonpositive in **every zero-diagonal Hermitian direction** (twelve real
coordinates). Consequently `Z -> H(P_K(Z))` is concave on the entire closed
convex domain `||Z||op<=1/2`, allowing arbitrary simultaneous complex motion
of all four entries, all edge magnitudes, and the gauge-invariant cycle phase.
The global statement concerns the eight-dimensional cross-block family;
pointwise twelve-dimensional negativity also allows introduction of both
missing edges. Arbitrary diagonal directions are not claimed.

X025/X026 supplied the frozen comparison and its negative branch. The new
steps here are an exact phase-independent **spectral upper bound on tau** and
a two-count Fisher inequality that closes X026's positive-tau Schur scalar.
Root supplied the count-mass suggestion `p0=p4=p`, `p1=p3=m`; the proof below
uses just those four masses. No specialized external theorem is imported.

## 2. Exact atoms and a spectral bound on the complete cycle logarithm

Write `C=2Z`, let `r,s in [0,1)` be the eigenvalues of `CC*`, and put `d=rs`.
For squared entry moduli `a=|C11|^2,b=|C12|^2,c=|C21|^2,e=|C22|^2`, set

`S=a+b+c+e=r+s`, `x=a+e-b-c`, `y=a+b-c-e`, `z=a+c-b-e`.

For occupation spins `sigma_i=2X_i-1`, the centered determinant identity is
`E product_(i in U) sigma_i=det((2K-I)_U)`. It follows by expanding both
sides into inclusion moments. Fourier inversion therefore gives the eight
likelihood values, each corresponding to two atoms of probability `q/16`,

`q_eta(u,v)=1+eta*d+(a+eta*e)u+(b+eta*c)v`, `eta,u,v in {−1,1}`.     (1)

Here `eta=product_i sigma_i`, `u=−sigma_1 sigma_3`, and
`v=−sigma_1 sigma_4`; these three signs are independent under uniform spins.
The size-two centered minors are minus the entry squared moduli, the only
size-four minor is `|det C|^2=d`, and all other nonconstant moments vanish.
All atoms are positive at a strict contraction.

Freeze `f(A)=−log p_A` and define `tau=Delta_1234 f`, with each difference
occupied minus unoccupied. Products of the four densities in each eta class
resum the entire logarithmic cycle term:

`exp(tau/2) = [(1-d)^2-y^2][(1-d)^2-z^2]`
`             / {[(1+d)^2-S^2][(1+d)^2-x^2]}`.                    (2)

**Spectral bound.** For every physical complex C,

`tau <= T(r,s):=2 log[(1-rs)^2/((1-r^2)(1-s^2))] >=0`.             (3)

Here is a finite proof retaining arbitrary complex cycle products. Directly,

`y^2+4d-x^2=4|C11 conjugate(C12)−C21 conjugate(C22)|^2>=0`,
`z^2+4d-x^2=4|C11 conjugate(C21)−C12 conjugate(C22)|^2>=0`.          (4)

The diagonal differences of `CC*` and `C*C` are y and z, so their two-by-two
eigenvalue formula gives `y^2,z^2<=(r-s)^2<=(1-rs)^2`. Write `M=(1-d)^2`.
By (4), `4d+y^2+z^2-x^2>=max(y^2,z^2)`. Consequently

`M[(1+d)^2-x^2]−(M-y^2)(M-z^2)`
` =M(4d+y^2+z^2-x^2)−y^2 z^2 >=0`.                              (5)

Divide (2) by its positive denominator, use (5), and use
`(1+d)^2-S^2=(1-r^2)(1-s^2)` to obtain (3). This is an exact cycle-sign
compensation, not positivity of individual edge or cluster coefficients.
The bound is sharp: real symmetric `C=[[u,v],[v,u]]` has `y=z=0,x^2=4d`,
so equality holds in (3) whenever it is a strict contraction.

## 3. Frozen curvature, with every determinant acceleration retained

The odds matrix `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0` gives `p_A=det(I-K)det L_A`. For a set T
outside i,j, the positive two-by-two Schur complement proves
`p_Ti p_Tj>=p_T p_Tij`; hence `h_ij=Delta_i Delta_j f>=0`.
Complementation preserves this balanced law, since `I-K=DKD` for
`D=diag(I_2,−I_2)`. Expanding f in centered occupation variables therefore
has only even terms. For any Hermitian kernel V its frozen expectation is

`F_f(V)=constant+sum_(i<j) alpha_ij det(V-I/2)_{ij}+tau det(V-I/2)`, (6)

where `alpha_ij=(h00+h01+h10+h11)/4>=|tau|/4`.
The identity `E product_(i in U)(X_i−1/2)=det(V-I/2)_U` proves (6).
For a cross-block direction `A_B=[[0,B],[B*,0]]`, this gives exactly

`H''[A_B,A_B]=−I_K(A_B)+tau p''−sum_e s_e^+ |B_e|^2`,              (7)

where `p=det K=1/16−||Z||F^2/4+|det Z|^2`,
`s_e^+=h_e(0,1)+h_e(1,0)>=0`, and `I_K=sum_A (p_A')^2/p_A`.
Indeed `alpha_e−tau/4=s_e^+/2`, so subtracting `tau det K` from (6)
leaves precisely the quadratic edge remainder in (7). The entropy identity
is `H''=−sum_A(p_A')^2/p_A−sum_A p_A'' log p_A`; thus (7) includes the
full second derivatives of every atom, not just Fisher or phase terms.

For completeness the branch `tau=−lambda<=0` already has a definite sign.
Put `G(Z)=3/16−||Z||F^2/4−|det Z|^2` and `s_e^−=h00+h11>=0`. Then

`H''=−I_K+lambda G''−sum_e s_e^−|B_e|^2`,
`G''=−||B||F^2/2−2|D det_Z[B]|^2−4 Re(conjugate(det Z)det B)`
`    <=−(1/2−2|det Z|)||B||F^2−2|D det_Z[B]|^2<=0`.               (8)

The estimate uses `|det B|<=||B||F^2/2` and `|det Z|<1/4`. In particular
`(det(Z+tB))''=2det B` is retained. It remains to treat `tau>0`.

## 4. Only two real coordinates can require Fisher payment

Apply fixed block unitaries solely to calculate the scalar determinant and
count law, putting `C=diag(sqrt(r),sqrt(s))` and the C-direction `E=2B` in
that basis. Measured entropy itself is never diagonalized. Set
`h=Re E11`, `k=Re E22`. Direct differentiation of
`p=(1−||C||F^2+|det C|^2)/16` gives the complete decomposition

`p''=P(h,k)−N(E)`,
`P=[−(1-s)h^2−(1-r)k^2+4sqrt(rs)hk]/8`,
`8N=(1-s)(Im E11)^2+(1-r)(Im E22)^2+|E12|^2+|E21|^2`
`                                      +2sqrt(rs) Re(E12 E21)>=0`. (9)

The final sign follows from `sqrt(rs)<1` and
`2|E12 E21|<=|E12|^2+|E21|^2`. No other direction can add positive curvature.
The count generating function `det(I-K+wK)` is unitary invariant. Its masses
at counts 0,4 each equal `p=(1-r)(1-s)/16`, and at counts 1,3 each equal
`m=(1-rs)/4`. Their derivatives depend only on h,k. Cauchy–Schwarz within each
count fiber and deletion of the nonnegative count-two contribution give

`I_K >= J:=2p'^2/p+2m'^2/m`,
`p'=−[sqrt(r)(1-s)h+sqrt(s)(1-r)k]/8`,
`m'=−[sqrt(r)s h+sqrt(s)r k]/2`.                                  (10)

If `rs=0`, (9) already gives `p''<=0`. If `r=s`, (3) gives `tau<=0`.
Thus the remaining positive-tau case has `r,s>0` and `r!=s`.

## 5. The two-count Fisher certificate

**Lemma.** For `0<r,s<1`, the real form `J−T(r,s)P` is nonnegative.
It is positive definite when `r!=s`.

Proof. Set `X=sqrt(r)h`, `Y=sqrt(s)k`. The matrix of `8(J−TP)` is
`[[A,B],[B,D]]`, where

`A=4(1-s)/(1-r)+16s^2/(1-rs)+T(1-s)/r>0`,
`D=4(1-r)/(1-s)+16r^2/(1-rs)+T(1-r)/s>0`,
`B=4+16rs/(1-rs)−2T`.

A direct determinant expansion gives

`AD−B^2=D0+LT+T^2[(1-r)(1-s)/(rs)−4]`,
`D0=64(r-s)^2/[(1-r)(1-s)(1-rs)]`,
`L=4(1/r+1/s+2)+16(r+s+2rs)/(1-rs)>0`.                            (11)

Put `t=(r-s)^2/[(1-r^2)(1-s^2)]>=0`. Then
`T=2log(1+t)` and `D0=64t(1+r)(1+s)/(1-rs)>=64t`.
The elementary inequality `log(1+t)<=sqrt(t)` follows by differentiation:
the derivative of the difference is `(sqrt(t)−1)^2/[2sqrt(t)(1+t)]>=0`,
with zero initial difference. Consequently `T^2<=4t`, and (11) yields

`AD−B^2>=D0−4T^2>=48t>=0`.                                      (12)

Positive diagonals prove the lemma, strictly when `r!=s`. For `r=s`, T=0
and J is a sum of squares directly. This completes the finite proof. ∎

Since `0<tau<=T`, interpolation of the two nonnegative forms J and `J−TP`
gives `J−tau P>=0`. Equations (7),(9),(10) now show

`−H''[A_B,A_B] >= (J−tau P)+tau N+sum_e s_e^+|B_e|^2 >=0`.         (13)

Together with (8), this proves the entire arbitrary-phase cross-block result.
Equivalently X026's exact scalar `tau p l^T R^(-1)l<=1` now holds for every
physical positive-tau balanced four-cycle, without its former extra condition.

## 6. The missing edges, finite segments, and exact remaining scope

For clarity the X026 extension to all twelve off-diagonal coordinates has a
short self-contained argument. Split `A=A_B+A0`, where `A0=diag(U,V)` and U,V
are Hermitian with zero diagonal. The affine involution `W -> I-DWD` fixes K,
preserves entropy, fixes A_B, and negates A0. Thus `H''[A_B,A0]=0`.
With `Q=adj Z`, determinant expansion in (6) gives
`D^2 det(K-I/2)[A0,A0]=−2 tr(Q* V Q U)`. Since `||Q||op=||Z||op=sigma`,
its absolute value is at most `sigma^2(||U||F^2+||V||F^2)`.
The pair part of (6) is `−alpha_12||U||F^2−alpha_34||V||F^2`; therefore

`H''[A0,A0]<=−I_K(A0)−|tau|(1/4−sigma^2)||A0||F^2<=0`.             (14)

This proves the claimed twelve-dimensional pointwise theorem. Integrating
(13) on any strict affine cross-block segment proves concavity on the open
operator-norm ball. Scale both endpoints toward zero and use continuity of
all atom polynomials and `−p log p` at zero to include its entire closed ball.
Directions changing diagonal entries, more than four sites, and arbitrary
coupled cycles remain unresolved. No full sixteen-dimensional theorem or
unrestricted Lyons proof is asserted.
