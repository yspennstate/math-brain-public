## 1. The capped cut and its exact spectrum

Let `C^6=W⊕U⊕Z` be an orthogonal decomposition into two-planes, with unit volume form `β=u₁∧u₂` of U. Define the isometry

`T:U⊥→Λ³C⁶`, `Tf=β∧f`.

For a family F of coordinate triples, put `B_F=T*P_FT`, where P_F is the coordinate projection onto those triples, and set `h_J=|β_J|²`. If `d_F(J)` counts the triples of F containing the pair J, the capped-Hall inequality is

`g'(F):=tr(P_W B_F) ≤ C_F:=Σ_J h_J min(2,d_F(J))`.                 (1)

It is exactly X020's inclusion-capacity condition for a row of mass `2h_J` to distribute at most `h_J` to each containing triple. Since `tr B_F=Σ_Jh_Jd_F(J)`, validity for every W is equivalent to

`τ₂(B_F) ≥ S_F:=Σ_Jh_J(d_F(J)−2)_+`,                            (2)

where τ₂ is the sum of the two smallest eigenvalues. This follows by maximizing `tr(P_WB_F)` over rank-two orthogonal projections in the four-dimensional U⊥.

**Theorem 1.** Fix any coordinate three-set R and let

`F_R={M:|M∩R|≥2}`.

Let x,y be the two eigenvalues of the 2×2 matrix `U*P_RU`. They lie in `[0,1]`. Then

`spec(B_(F_R))={x,y,x+y−xy,xy}`,                                (3)

and the exact optimal capped-cut slack is

`C_(F_R)−max_W g'(F_R)=min(x,y)(1−max(x,y)) ≥0`.                 (4)

Thus this capped cut and its complement hold for every complex flag. The bound is sharp for every prescribed x,y.

**Proof of the spectrum.** Principal-angle bases give orthonormal `e₁,e₂,e₃` in the coordinate space C^R and `f₁,f₂,f₃` in C^(Rᶜ) such that

`u₁=√x e₁+√(1−x)f₁`, `u₂=√y e₂+√(1−y)f₂`.

An orthonormal basis of U⊥ is

`v₁=√(1−x)e₁−√x f₁`, `v₂=√(1−y)e₂−√y f₂`, `e₃,f₃`.

Projection onto F_R means retaining wedge components with at least two factors in C^R. It is invariant under changes of basis separately inside C^R and C^(Rᶜ). Thus these bases calculate that fixed projector; they do not replace the measured kernel by an arbitrarily diagonalized one.

The four retained images are mutually orthogonal: those of v₁,v₂ involve the respective triples `{e₁,f₁,e₂}` and `{e₁,e₂,f₂}`; the third image uses e₃, and the fourth uses f₃. Their squared norms are respectively

`y`, `x`, `xy+x(1−y)+(1−x)y=x+y−xy`, `xy`.

This proves (3), including endpoints by continuity. The three pair degrees are four for J⊂R, two for crossing J, and zero for J⊂Rᶜ. Cauchy–Binet gives

`Σ_(J⊂R)h_J=xy`, `Σ_(J⊂Rᶜ)h_J=(1−x)(1−y)`.

Consequently `S_(F_R)=2xy` and `C_(F_R)=2(x+y−xy)`. The bottom two eigenvalues in (3) sum to `xy+min(x,y)`, giving (2) and (4). The complement identity `B_(Fᶜ)=I−B_F` preserves the capped gap, or one can replace R by Rᶜ. ∎

There is an explicit nonnegative certificate retaining the actual W. Order `x≤y`, let `s=x(1−y)`, and write `w_z=||P_Wz||²` in the displayed U⊥ basis. Then

`C_(F_R)−g'(F_R)=s(2−w_(e₃))+(y−x)w_(v₂)+y(1−x)w_(f₃)`.       (5)

Every term is nonnegative, and `w_(e₃)≤1` gives the sharp lower bound s. Formula (5) follows by substituting `Σ_z w_z=2` into the spectral expansion. Taking `W=span(e₃,v₁)` attains equality in (4). This proves sharpness without a variational existence assumption.

## 2. Why this closes an actual overlapping cut

The high-degree graph of F_R is the triangle on R, with each of its three edges of degree four. X020's fractional-star condition cannot cover this cut. Its edge constraints give `t_i+t_j≥1` for the three edges, hence `Σ_(i∈R)t_i≥3/2`. Its triple-load constraint at the internal triple R gives `Σ_(i∈R)t_i≤1`, a contradiction. Coordinates outside R do not alter either conclusion.

The internal triple is exactly what distinguishes this cut from X020's proved absent-triangle class. The three full pair stars share that triple. Formula (5) pays this overlap through the actual principal-angle spectrum, with residual `min(x,y)(1−max(x,y))`; summing half of three star bounds would not pay it.

This theorem proves the 20 cuts F_R, including their complements. It does not prove arbitrary deletions from their external nine-triple layers. Adding an arbitrary triple to a cut can increase both its operator and its required excess, so mere monotonicity does not establish those extensions.

## 3. A common random flag without a diamond assumption

Now let W have rank r and let U have rank two on n sites, with `W⊥U`; write

`a=μ_W`, `b=μ_(W+U)`, `m=(μ_(W+u₁)+μ_(W+u₂))/2`,

and define `β*=2H(m)−H(a)−H(b)` as in X017. The middle law is basis independent.

**Lemma 2.** There is a nested random chain `S⊂L⊂T` of ranks `r,r+1,r+2` with marginal laws `a,m,b`.

For completeness, rank-one inclusion covers follow from an elementary exterior Hall argument. If α is a unit volume form and v is an orthogonal unit vector, set `ζ=α∧v`. For any family A of bottom sets, its inclusion neighborhood N(A) satisfies

`||P_A α||²=||P_A ι_v P_(N(A))ζ||² ≤ ||P_(N(A))ζ||²`.

These are all weighted Hall inequalities, so finite max-flow gives a cover from `|α|²` to `|ζ|²`. Mixing the two covers `a→μ_(W+u_i)` with equal weights gives a cover `c⁻` from a to m. Mixing the two corresponding covers to b gives `c⁺` from m to b. Define

`Pr(S,L,T)=c⁻(S,L)c⁺(L,T)/m_L` when `m_L>0`, and zero otherwise. (6)

Summing verifies every marginal and gives the nested chain. No opposite middle is prescribed by this construction. ∎

**Theorem 3 (unconditional entropy lower bound).** For every such complex projection flag,

`β* ≥ −log((r+1)(n−r−1))`.                                     (7)

Indeed ordinary chain rules, with no conditional-independence assumption, give exactly

`β*=H(L|S)+H(L|T)−H(S|L)−H(T|L)`.

Given L, at most r+1 deletions produce S, and at most n−r−1 additions produce T. Bound the last two conditional entropies by the logarithms of these numbers and retain the nonnegative first two. This proves (7). Determinantal structure is used to obtain the two covers; arbitrary adjacent laws need not have them.

## 4. Definite active directions through seven measured sites

**Corollary 4.** Put `P=(r+1)(n−r−1)` and `C=8−4log2−2log P`. For any strict active 2×2 covariance M and definite Hermitian direction A,

`−H''(P_W+UMU*)[UAU*,UAU*] ≥ C det A`.                         (8)

If `P≤12`, then `C>0`. In particular every projection flag on n≤7 satisfies this bound, and entropy is concave along every segment of Loewner-comparable active covariances, including noncommuting endpoints. At n=6,r=2 the constant is `8−2log36`; at n=7,r=2 or 3 it is `8−2log48`.

Here is the credited X017 calculation that makes the absence of a diamond explicit. Let

`d=det M`, `d₀=det(I−M)`, `B=M−dI`, `f₁=tr B`, `T₀=f₁/(2√(dd₀))≥1`.

With the exterior two-component rows e_L, the three atom layers are `d₀a_S`, `e_LBe_L*`, `db_T`, and `Σ_L e_L*e_L=I₂`. Let `ν_L=e_LBe_L*/f₁` and `δ=det A>0`. Direct differentiation of these atoms gives

`H''=−FI+2δΛ`, `Λ=2log(2T₀)−β*−2D(m||ν)`.                    (9)

Reverse A if needed so A is positive. The bottom and top count probabilities alone give

`FI≥[tr(adj M)A]²/d+[tr(adj(I−M))A]²/d₀ ≥4(1+T₀)δ`.

The last inequality is the 2×2 trace–determinant inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz, applied to `adj M/√d` and `adj(I−M)/√d₀`, whose determinants are one and whose sum has determinant `2+2T₀`. Substituting into (9), using (7), gives the stronger bound

`−H''≥δ[C+4(T₀−1−log T₀)+4D(m||ν)]≥Cδ`,                     (10)

proving (8). A rank-one active direction has δ=0 and `H''=−FI≤0`. Integrate along comparable segments and pass to boundary covariances by finite entropy continuity. When C>0, the Jensen gap is at least `(C/2)t(1−t)det(M₁−M₀)` for a definite comparable difference.

Since `P≤floor(n²/4)`, n≤7 implies P≤12. Also `e⁴>Σ_(j=0)^7 4^j/j!>48`, giving `8−2log48>0` without a decimal estimate. This proves strict positivity of the stated constants.

**Scope:** (7) supplies X017's lower threshold `β*≥2log2−4` in these dimensions. It supplies no upper bound `β*≤2log2`. Therefore full concavity for arbitrary, possibly incomparable active endpoints is not claimed.

## 5. The precise opposite-middle correction

For the common chain (6), put `L'=S∪(T\L)` and let q denote its law. The four sets form an elementary diamond, but q need not equal m. At active covariance I₂/2, determinantal atom log-submodularity gives

`a_S b_T ≤4m_Lm_(L')`.

This follows from the 2×2 Schur-complement minor inequality after strict regularization. On a positive chain atom it also proves `m_(L')>0`, so all following terms are finite. Define

`R=E log[4m_Lm_(L')/(a_Sb_T)]≥0`,
`κ=H(m)−H(q)−D(q||m)=Σ_L(q_L−m_L)log m_L`.

Averaging and rearranging gives the exact identity

`β*=2log2+κ−R`.                                                (11)

Thus a common random flag whose opposite middle has `κ≤0` would give X017's missing upper bound without requiring q=m. In the dimensions covered by Corollary 4, that condition would yield full active concavity. Neither κ≤0 nor its adequate compensation by R is established universally here. Equation (11) records the entropy correction; replacing q by m or dropping κ would be an invalid gluing argument.
