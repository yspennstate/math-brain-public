# X026 — arbitrary-phase four-cycle curvature and one visible scalar

Status: COMPLETE bounded result, 2026-09-15. Exact deterministic checks passed.
No unrestricted Lyons proof or unrestricted sixteen-dimensional Hessian claim.

## 1. Setting and result

Let

    K = I_4/2 + J,       J = [[0,Z],[Z*,0]],       ||Z||op < 1/2,

where Z is an arbitrary complex 2 by 2 matrix, in the fixed measured basis.
Every four-cycle phase and all four edge magnitudes are allowed. Set

    p_S = P_K(X=S),       f(S) = -log p_S,       tau = Delta_1234 f.

Write I_K(A)=sum_S (Dp_S[A])²/p_S. Let s_1,s_2 be the singular values of Z,

    p = det K > 0,
    eta = sum_(j=1,2) 2 s_j²/(1/4+s_j²) < 2.

Define c(eta)=0 for eta<=1 and c(eta)=1-1/eta for eta>1.

**Theorem.** The complete entropy Hessian is nonpositive in every Hermitian
zero-diagonal direction A (all twelve real off-diagonal coordinates), provided

    tau_+ c(eta) <= 2/(1-2p).                                    (1)

In particular this holds (a) whenever tau<=0, with no additional magnitude or
phase restriction, and (b) whenever eta<=1, with no restriction on tau. The
explicit phase-independent sufficient condition ||Z||F<=1/sqrt(6) implies
eta<=1. Thus H is concave on this entire convex eight-dimensional matrix ball.
The operator-norm ball ||Z||op<=1/sqrt(12) is another sufficient domain. There is no constraint that a direction preserve the cycle phase,
edge magnitudes, singular vectors, or even the four-cycle support.

For the eight-dimensional cross-block subspace A_B=[[0,B],[B*,0]], there is
an exact negative-square identity when tau<=0, given below. Consequently H is
concave along every affine cross-block segment whose points satisfy (1).
The pointwise theorem also includes directions introducing the two missing
within-part edges. A segment in those extra directions generally leaves the
balanced bipartite locus, so pointwise negativity is not called a global
segment theorem there. Diagonal directions remain outside this result.

## 2. Frozen polynomial and supermodular coefficients

Put h_e(u,v)=Delta_e f(u,v), with u,v the two outside occupation bits, and
alpha_e=(h00+h01+h10+h11)/4. The positive odds matrix L=K(I-K)^(-1) gives
p_S=det(I-K)det L_S. Its two-by-two Schur-complement identity proves
p_Ti p_Tj >= p_T p_Tij; hence h_e>=0. No association theorem is imported.

Because I-K=D K D for D=diag(I_2,-I_2), complementing all occupation bits
preserves the law. Thus f(S)=f(S^c). Expanding f in centered occupation
variables x_i-1/2, all odd terms vanish. For every Hermitian kernel V,

    E_(P_V) f = constant
        + sum_(i<j) alpha_ij det(V-I/2)_{ij}
        + tau det(V-I/2).                                      (2)

The identity E prod_(i in T)(X_i-1/2)=det(V-I/2)_T proves (2) directly.
Furthermore tau=h00-h01-h10+h11, so

    alpha_e >= |tau|/4,
    alpha_e-tau/4 = (h01+h10)/2              if tau>=0,
    alpha_e-(-tau)/4 = (h00+h11)/2           if tau<=0.             (3)

For V=K+tA_B, let d=det Z and d'=D det_Z[B]. Equation (2) becomes

    F_f(V)=constant-sum_(cross e) alpha_e |Z_e+tB_e|²
                      +tau |det(Z+tB)|².                       (4)

All acceleration terms are kept: (det(Z+tB))''=2 det B.
The entropy Hessian is exactly -I_K(A_B)+F_f''(K)[A_B,A_B].

## 3. Negative tau: exact squares for every complex B

Suppose tau=-lambda<=0. Define the comparison polynomial

    G(Z)=3/16-||Z||F²/4-|det Z|².

Equation (4), not a gauge-orbit argument, gives exactly

    F_f'' = lambda G'' - sum_e (h_e(0,0)+h_e(1,1)) |B_e|².        (5)

If d!=0, set theta=arg d and

    S_d(B)=|B11+exp(i theta) conjugate(B22)|²
          +|B12-exp(i theta) conjugate(B21)|².

Then

    G'' = -2|d'|² -(1/2-2|d|)||B||F² -2|d| S_d(B).              (6)

Indeed S_d=||B||F²+2 Re(exp(-i theta)det B), and differentiating |det Z|²
produces 2|d'|²+4 Re(conjugate(d)det B). When d=0 the final term in (6)
is defined as zero. Since |d|<1/4, every term on the right of

    H'' = -I_K(A_B)
           -lambda[2|d'|²+(1/2-2|d|)||B||F²+2|d|S_d(B)]
           -sum_e(h00+h11)|B_e|²                               (7)

is nonpositive. For tau<0 and B!=0 the bound is strictly negative.
For tau<0, Sections 3 and 5 also yield the uniform twelve-dimensional bound

    H''[A,A] <= -I_K(A)-(-tau)(1/4-||Z||op²)||A||F².

This proves the full eight-dimensional arbitrary-phase sector, not just its
gauge or Fisher-null tangent space. X025 supplied the comparison G and owns
the Gaussian phase-damping theorem; (5)-(7) are the full affine extension.

## 4. Positive tau: one atom-visible Lorentzian scalar

Suppose tau>0. Define

    ell(A_B)=tr(K^(-1)A_B)=Dp_full[A_B]/p,
    Q_K(A_B)=||K^(-1/2)A_B K^(-1/2)||F².

Since det K=1/16-||Z||F²/4+|det Z|², (4) gives the exact identity

    H'' = -I_K(A_B)+tau p[ell(A_B)²-Q_K(A_B)]
                    -sum_e(h00+h11- tau)|B_e|².                (8)

The last coefficient equals h01+h10>=0 by (3). Thus the only positive
candidate in the entire eight-dimensional block is the single score ell;
all score-null directions, including all three independent gauge motions,
lie in the negative hyperplane ell=0. Independent cycle-phase velocity is
retained in ell and in the full Fisher matrix.

The sharp metric estimate on this subspace is

    ell(A_B)² <= eta Q_K(A_B).                                 (9)

For proof, block-unitary conjugation puts Z=diag(s1,s2) solely to compute
this matrix inequality; no measured entropy is diagonalized. Only the real
entries b_j=Re B_jj contribute to ell. Their contributions to ell and Q are

    ell_j = -2s_j b_j/(1/4-s_j²),
    Q_j = 2(1/4+s_j²)b_j²/(1/4-s_j²)².

Every other component is orthogonal in the positive Q metric to these two.
Cauchy-Schwarz proves (9), with sharp constant eta; equality uses
b_j proportional to -s_j(1/4-s_j²)/(1/4+s_j²). Equivalently, the metric
representer is B_*=-Z(1/4 I-Z*Z)(1/4 I+Z*Z)^(-1), for which ell=Q=eta.
If Z=0 then ell=0.

For every affine cross-block path, p_empty=p_full=p, and both scores equal
ell. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the other fourteen atoms gives

    I_K(A_B) >= [2p/(1-2p)] ell(A_B)².                         (10)

Equations (8)-(10) prove (1) in the cross-block subspace. If eta<=1,
(9) already makes the frozen curvature nonpositive without Fisher payment.
Since eta is increasing in each s_j², ||Z||op<=1/sqrt(12) implies eta<=1.
More broadly, eta<=1 is equivalent to

    4||Z||F²+48|det Z|² <= 1.

Using |det Z|²<=||Z||F^4/4 proves this throughout ||Z||F<=1/sqrt(6).
That matrix ball is convex and strictly physical, so integrating its Hessian
bound gives an unconditional finite-segment concavity theorem there.
Since eta<2 and 2/(1-2p)>2, the simple condition tau<=4 also suffices in (1).

## 5. The two missing edges decouple and are always negative

Every zero-diagonal Hermitian A splits uniquely into A_B+A_0, where

    A_0=diag(U,V),       U=U*, V=V*,       diag U=diag V=0.

The affine involution K -> I-DKD fixes the base and sends A_B to A_B but
A_0 to -A_0. Entropy is invariant under this involution. Differentiating its
invariance proves H''[A_B,A_0]=0, including the Fisher terms.

Put T=adj Z and sigma=||Z||op. Differentiating the polynomial det J twice
in the A_0 direction gives

    D² det J[A_0,A_0] = -2 tr(T* V T U).                       (11)

This holds also for singular Z by polynomial continuation. Since
||T||op=sigma, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    |D² det J[A_0,A_0]| <= sigma² (||U||F²+||V||F²).

The pair part of (2) is -alpha_12||U||F²-alpha_34||V||F². Therefore

    H''[A_0,A_0] <= -I_K(A_0)
          -|tau|(1/4-sigma²)||A_0||F² <=0.                    (12)

For tau=0 use alpha_e>=0 directly. Equations (7)-(12) prove the theorem.

## 6. Exact remaining one-dimensional test

For tau>0 let R be the positive definite quadratic form

    R(B)=I_K(A_B)+tau p Q_K(A_B)+sum_e(h01+h10)|B_e|².

In any fixed real basis of complex 2 by 2 matrices, let l represent ell.
Equation (8) says the exact cross-block sign criterion is

    tau p l^T R^(-1) l <= 1.                                  (13)

This is a genuine rank-one Schur reduction: optimizing over all components
in ker ell gives the left side, so mixed visible/gauge directions have not
been discarded. Bounds (9)-(10) give the explicit sufficient domain (1).
Outside (1), (13) remains unproved; diagonal motions remain an additional
independent block. Neither gap is called a proof of unrestricted four-site
or general Lyons concavity.

Source context: Lyons's target is https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2707. C083 gives
the earlier pure-phase diagonal-plus-rank-one cycle formula. X025 contributed
the frozen-supermodular comparison polynomials and proves Gaussian damping;
this report extends its negative branch to all affine cross-block motions,
adds the sharp eta metric/Fisher bound, and controls the missing-edge block.
All displayed proofs otherwise use elementary finite determinant calculus.
