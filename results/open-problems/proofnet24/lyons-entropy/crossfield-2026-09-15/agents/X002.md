## 1. Notation and exact transport theorem

Work at a strict contraction `0<K<I`, with Hermitian direction `A`. Atoms are indexed by `x in {0,1}^n`. If `R` is a subset of coordinates and `T` is its occupied subconfiguration, set

`M_R(T)=K_R-D_(R\T)`,

`q_R(T)=(-1)^|R\T| det M_R(T)`.

This is the marginal atom on `R`; it is strictly positive. The empty matrix has determinant one. For `B=R^c`, introduce the two matrices

`C_B(T)=K_BB-K_BR M_R(T)^(-1) K_RB`,

`V_B(T)=A_BB-A_BR M_R(T)^(-1) K_RB`.                 (1)

`C` is Hermitian, while `V` generally is not, even though both `K` and `A` are Hermitian. The signs in the Schur complement matter: `M_R` is a signed atom matrix, not `K_R`.

For a real edge field `J_i` on the coordinates other than `i`, define

`(div_i J_i)(x)=(2x_i-1)J_i(x_-i)`.

For a real face field `Q_ij` on the coordinates other than `i,j`, use the corresponding iterated divergence. Define

`J_i(T)=q_R(T) Re V_{ii}(T)`,  `R=[n]\{i}`,       (2)

`Q_ij(T)=q_R(T) Re det V_{ {i,j} }(T)`, `R=[n]\{i,j}`. (3)

**Theorem 1 (exact first and second transport).** With derivatives along `K+tA`,

`p'=sum_i div_i J_i`,

`J_i'=sum_(j!=i) div_j Q_ij`,

`p''=2 sum_(i<j) div_i div_j Q_ij`.                  (4)

The second equality differentiates the exact current at the current kernel, including the conditional marginal and inverse. No acceleration is discarded.

**Proof.** The probability generating polynomial is

`F_K(z)=det[I+diag(z_i-1)K]`.

Differentiate its rows. A replacement of row `i` by the corresponding row of `A` contributes a factor `z_i-1`. Its remaining coefficient polynomial is independent of `z_i`. Taking its coefficient on a fixed configuration `T` of the remaining coordinates gives the determinant with row `i` equal to `A_i*` and the remaining signed atom rows equal to the rows of `M_R`. Schur complementation gives `q_R V_ii`. The polynomial derivative is real, so real parts may be taken in every row term. Replacing rows `i,j` gives factors `(z_i-1)(z_j-1)` and coefficient `q_R det V_B`. Every unordered pair occurs twice in the second derivative. Differentiating the one-row replacement polynomial replaces one of the other rows, which also proves the current derivative in (4). Coefficient extraction proves the claims. ∎

## 2. Exact entropy identity localized on conditioned faces

For the face `B={i,j}` and fixed outside configuration `T`, write its four full atom probabilities as `p_ab`. Let

`Lambda_ij(T)=log[p_01 p_10/(p_00 p_11)]`.            (5)

**Theorem 2.** Every strict Hermitian kernel and Hermitian direction obey

`H''(K)[A,A] = -sum_x p'_x^2/p_x + 2 sum_(i<j,T) Q_ij(T) Lambda_ij(T)`. (6)

All the coefficients `Lambda` are nonnegative.

**Proof.** Normalization gives `H''=-sum p'^2/p-sum p''log p`. Substitute (4) and sum by parts twice to obtain (6). To establish the coefficient sign without importing an association theorem, write

`C=[[a,c],[conj(c),b]]`, `d=|c|^2`.

Schur complementation of each full atom gives `p_ab=q r_ab`, where

`r_11=ab-d`, `r_10=a(1-b)+d`,

`r_01=(1-a)b+d`, `r_00=(1-a)(1-b)-d`.

All four are positive because the original atoms are positive. Direct multiplication gives

`r_01 r_10-r_00 r_11=d`.                            (7)

Thus `Lambda=log[1+d/(r_00 r_11)]>=0`. ∎

In particular, if all the explicitly computed `Q_ij(T)` are nonpositive, entropy curvature is nonpositive, with the quantitative bound `H''<=-sum p'^2/p`. This is a sufficient certificate, not a claim that its hypothesis holds generally. Positive face accelerations are precisely those which need Fisher compensation.

## 3. The local nonsymmetry defect

Decompose the effective directional matrix as `V=E+iG`, with `E=(V+V*)/2` and `G=(V-V*)/(2i)` Hermitian. A two-by-two determinant expansion gives

`Re det V = det E-det G`

`= E_11 E_22-|E_12|^2+|G_12|^2-G_11 G_22`.         (8)

More explicitly,

`E=A_BB-(A_BR M^-1 K_RB+K_BR M^-1 A_RB)/2`,

`G=(K_BR M^-1 A_RB-A_BR M^-1 K_RB)/(2i)`.            (9)

Even at real symmetric `K,A`, the matrix `G` can be nonzero: it is then purely imaginary with zero diagonal, and its contribution in (8) is the positive square `|G_12|^2`. Therefore conditioning the signed transport does not preserve a Hermitian directional matrix. Treating `V` as Hermitian would erase a term with the dangerous sign.

For a fixed face, write `h_i=Re V_ii`, `h_j=Re V_jj`, `r_i=Re(V_ij conj(c))`, and `r_j=Re(c V_ji)`. The four incident canonical currents have the exact form

`J_i(j=1,T)=q(b h_i-r_i)`,

`J_i(j=0,T)=q((1-b)h_i+r_i)`,

`J_j(i=1,T)=q(a h_j-r_j)`,

`J_j(i=0,T)=q((1-a)h_j+r_j)`.                        (10)

These follow by leaving one row of the two-by-two determinant unchanged. In particular, local real currents do not determine `Re det V`: imaginary components of `V` can be invisible to all four currents while contributing positively to the face acceleration. Establishing a determinant-compatible global cancellation, rather than facewise positivity from those four currents alone, is the remaining target.

## 4. The missing imaginary velocity is an explicit cube circulation

Keep the complex row currents before taking real parts:

`mathcal J_i(T)=q_(-i)(T) V_ii(T)`.

For an ordered pair `i,j`, define on its outside configurations

`Z_ij(T)=q_R(T) A_ij C_ji(T)`,

`Gamma_ij(T)=Im Z_ij(T)`, `R=[n]\{i,j}`.             (12)

Hermiticity gives `Z_ji=conj Z_ij`, hence `Gamma_ji=-Gamma_ij`.

**Theorem 3 (explicit Hodge decomposition).** The complex currents obey

`mathcal J_i=A_ii q_(-i)-sum_(j!=i) div_j Z_ij`,

`Im mathcal J_i=-sum_(j!=i) div_j Gamma_ij`,

`sum_i div_i Im mathcal J_i=0`.                      (13)

Consequently the probability velocity itself has the alternative expression

`p'=sum_i A_ii div_i q_(-i)-2 sum_(i<j) div_i div_j Re Z_ij`. (14)

**Proof.** Expand the row replacement giving `mathcal J_i` along row `i`. Its diagonal cofactor is the marginal generating determinant on `-i`, giving `A_ii q_(-i)`. The off-diagonal cofactor for column `j` contains a factor `z_j-1`: in the cofactor minor row `j` has lost its diagonal column. After fixing the other coordinates, the Schur-complement cofactor is `-q_R C_ji`. Thus the off-diagonal term is `-div_j(q_R A_ij C_ji)`, proving the first identity. The diagonal term is real, so taking imaginary parts gives the second. Pairing the terms indexed by `(i,j)` and `(j,i)` and using antisymmetry gives the third. Taking the real divergence and using `Re Z_ji=Re Z_ij` gives (14). ∎

Thus imaginary row currents are not numerical errors or an arbitrary complex notation: they are oriented circulations on the square faces. They are exactly invisible to the measured first derivative. Formula (8) shows why their presence can still affect the second derivative.

## 6. A complete angular reduction on bridges, in every dimension

Let `G` be the support graph of the nonzero off-diagonal entries of a strict Hermitian `K`. Let the Hermitian direction `A` be supported on that same graph (its diagonal is unrestricted). Write `F` for the bridges of `G`. For each bridge `e={i,j}`, write uniquely

`A_ij=(r_e+i theta_e) K_ij`, `r_e,theta_e real`.

Define `B` by retaining all diagonal and nonbridge entries of `A`, and putting `B_ij=r_e K_ij` on bridges. Define

`W_e(K)=sum_T q_(-{i,j})(T) Lambda_ij(T)`.           (16)

**Theorem 4 (bridge angular elimination).** The full measured-entropy Hessian obeys the exact identity

`H''(K)[A,A]=H''(K)[B,B]-2 sum_(e in F) theta_e^2 |K_ij|^2 W_e(K)`. (17)

Every `W_e` is strictly positive. Consequently an arbitrary collection of angular motions on bridges, even of large active rank and with arbitrary coherent cyclic blocks on either side, has strictly negative entropy curvature unless the direction is zero.

**Proof.** A determinant permutation traverses a bridge only in the transposition `(ij)`: any longer cycle using that edge would produce a graph cycle. Therefore every atom depends on the bridge entry only through `w_e=|K_ij|^2`. Its derivative with respect to this variable is exactly

`partial p / partial w_e = -div_i div_j q_(-{i,j})`. (18)

This follows from the negative sign of the transposition and the determinant of the complementary atom matrix. Thus

`partial H / partial w_e=-W_e`.                    (19)

Along direction `A`, the first derivative of `w_e` is `2 r_e |K_ij|^2`, the same as along `B`; their second derivatives differ by `2 theta_e^2 |K_ij|^2`. All other kernel coordinates and their first derivatives agree. Applying the ordinary multivariable chain rule to the atom entropy as a function of bridge weights and remaining coordinates therefore gives (17). This retains mixed second derivatives of the weight variables: they cancel because their first derivatives agree.

For a bridge, deleting vertices `i,j` leaves the neighbors on its two sides in different components. The signed conditioning matrix `M_R` is block diagonal across that separation; hence `K_iR M_R^-1 K_Rj=0` and `C_ij=K_ij!=0` for every outside configuration. Equation (7) makes every `Lambda_ij(T)` strictly positive, so `W_e>0`. ∎

**Forest corollary.** On an arbitrary forest-supported affine kernel family, all complex angular directions can be eliminated exactly by (17). After one fixed diagonal unitary gauge makes the base edge entries real positive, checking the full graph-supported Hermitian Hessian reduces to checking real symmetric directions; every imaginary part has the explicit negative-square remainder in (17). This does **not** prove the real symmetric radial part of forest concavity. It resolves the complex-direction obstruction on forests and on the bridges of arbitrary graphs.

## 7. Cycle expansion of the complete imaginary-direction Hessian

The bridge reduction is a special case of an exact cycle formula at a real symmetric base. Let `K` be real symmetric and strict, and let `A=iS`, where `S` is any real skew-symmetric matrix. Fix an orientation of every possible undirected edge and write `a_e=S_ij` in that orientation. Zero base entries are allowed.

For a set of vertices `U`, put

`Psi_U(K)=sum_(T on U^c) q_(U^c)(T) Delta_U log p(T)`,

where `Delta_U f=sum_(x_U) [product_(u in U)(2x_u-1)] f(x_U,T)`.

For each unoriented simple cycle `C` of length `m>=3` in the union support of `K` and `S`, choose either cyclic orientation. Let `a_e^C=S_uv` and `k_e=K_uv` with the edge oriented along that cycle, and define

`R_C(S,K)=sum_({e,f} subset C) a_e^C a_f^C product_(g in C\{e,f}) k_g`. (20)

Reversing the cycle changes both `a` signs and leaves (20) unchanged. Then

`H''(K)[iS,iS] = -2 sum_e a_e^2 W_e(K)`

`  +4 sum_(simple C, |C|>=3) (-1)^(|C|-1) R_C(S,K) Psi_(V(C))(K)`. (21)

Here `W_e` is defined by (16) for every pair, whether or not it is a bridge. All score/Fisher terms vanish at this real base because `p(K+itS)=p(K-itS)`.

**Proof.** Expand each determinant into disjoint permutation cycles. A transposition on edge `e` contributes `-(k_e^2+t^2 a_e^2)` and therefore contributes `-2 a_e^2 div_i div_j q_(-{i,j})` to `p''`. An unoriented simple cycle of length `m>=3` contributes

`2(-1)^(m-1) Re product_(e in C)(k_e+i t a_e^C)`.

Its second derivative is `-4(-1)^(m-1) R_C`. Each cycle factor has zero first derivative at zero. Thus product differentiation of disjoint cycles contributes no cross terms between distinct permutation cycles; summing the permutations on the complementary vertices gives exactly `q_(V(C)^c)`. The remaining hole sign is `product_(u in V(C))(2x_u-1)`. Multiplication by `-log p` and summation gives (21), with the transposition term reduced by `Delta_{i,j}log p=-Lambda_ij`. This accounts for every determinant permutation exactly once for each twice-differentiated cycle. ∎

This identifies where the invisible angular circulation must be compensated: **every off-diagonal coupling of two angular edge velocities is carried by a simple graph cycle containing those two edges**. If a family `F` of moving edges has no two edges lying on one simple cycle of the union support graph, the cycle remainder in (21) is zero and

`H''(K)[iS,iS]=-2 sum_(e in F) a_e^2 W_e<=0`.        (22)

This includes moving one phase edge in each of arbitrarily many cyclic blocks joined at articulation vertices, as well as all edges of a forest. The condition uses the union support so that newly introduced zero-base edges cannot silently create a two-moving-edge cycle.

For completeness, (21) also gives a direct edge-square certificate with cycle terms retained. Define symmetric off-diagonal coefficients

`B_ef=sum_(C contains e,f) (-1)^(|C|-1) epsilon_e^C epsilon_f^C`

`       * product_(g in C\{e,f}) k_g * Psi_(V(C))`,

where `epsilon_e^C` converts the fixed edge orientation to the cycle orientation, and set `B_ee=0`. Then (21) is

`H''=-2 sum_e W_e a_e^2+4 sum_(e<f) B_ef a_e a_f`.

If `W_e>=sum_(f!=e)|B_ef|` for every edge, the exact completion of squares is

`H''=-2 sum_e [W_e-sum_(f!=e)|B_ef|] a_e^2`

`     -2 sum_(e<f) |B_ef| [a_e-sign(B_ef)a_f]^2 <=0`. (23)

The diagonal dominance hypothesis is a concrete sufficient criterion. It is not asserted universally, and (23) does not yet pay for the unrestricted cyclic angular remainder.

## 8. Sources and nonclaims

The probability generating determinant is the elementary finite DPP identity associated with Russell Lyons's formulation, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2707>. The motivating discrete continuity/acceleration strategy is Hillion–Johnson, *Discrete versions of the transport equation and the Shepp–Olkin conjecture*, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3381>, and their full Bernoulli-sum theorem, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01570>. No specialized theorem from those papers is used as a black box in the proofs above; the cube construction and its signs were derived here directly. Their one-dimensional displacement hypotheses are not asserted for these generally signed currents.

The complete desired assertion would be the global inequality

`sum_x p'_x^2/p_x >= 2 sum_(i<j,T) q_R(T) Re det V_B(T) Lambda_ij(T)`. (11)

This report has not established (11) in general. The general transport reduction is exact but conditional as a route to concavity. The bridge angular theorem, the cycle-free-pair angular sign theorem (22), and the sufficient cycle diagonal-dominance theorem (23) are unconditional within their stated hypotheses.

No historical novelty claim is made for restricted entropy statements that might follow from earlier work. The specific deliverables here are the canonical signed cube two-jet, its explicit complex Hodge circulation, the bridge Hessian identity with arbitrary coherent blocks, and the all-dimensional cycle formula for the angular Hessian. The prior campaign's rank-one, diagonal-endpoint, two-site, and three-site null results are not claimed as new.
