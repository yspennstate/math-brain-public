## 1. Isolating precisely one adverse endpoint

    tau=log[p000 p110 p101 p011/(p111 p100 p010 p001)].       (1)

For a pair face with outside bit `z`, write its atoms in order `00,10,01,11` as `p_a,p_b,p_c,p_d`, and set `Lambda_ij,z=log(p_b p_c/(p_a p_d))>=0`. The difference `Lambda_ij,1-Lambda_ij,0=tau` is independent of the pair. Its sign follows directly from the odds matrix `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0`: the Schur complement of `L_T` gives `p_Ti p_Tj-p_T p_Tij=p_T² |Lij-LiT L_T^(-1)LTj|²>=0`.

Choose

    tau>=0: E=111, J=K,   B=A,  z=0;
    tau<0:  E=000, J=I-K, B=-A, z=1;
    lambda=|tau|.

**Lemma 1 (signed endpoint identity).** Without any directional sign assumption,

    -H''=FI+2 sum_(i<j) r_ij Lambda_ij,z-lambda a_E.          (2)

**Proof.** A face difference `D_ij,z=delta_00-delta_10-delta_01+delta_11` has pair inclusion moment one. Every pair inclusion acceleration is `2 det A_ij=-2r_ij`, and every singleton acceleration is zero. Put `D_123(x)=(-1)^(3-|x|)`. Boolean moment inversion gives

Since `D_123 dot log p=-tau`, this proves (2) when `tau>=0`. Also `a_000+a_111=-2 sum r_ij`; moving all pair faces to outside bit one gives (2) for `tau<0`. Finally use the actual entropy formula `-H''=FI+sum a_x log p_x`. ∎

From now through Section 4, assume **`Aii=0` for every i**. Then `r_ij=|Aij|²>=0`. Equation (2) retains the entire signed acceleration even when X029's endpoint cone fails.

## 2. The scalar, determinant-specific Stein kernel

Let `d=diag K`, `e` be the bit vector of endpoint `E`, and

    Sigma=Cov_p(X)=diag(d)-K circ conjugate(K),
    h(x)=1{x=E}-p_E-p_E(e-d)^T Sigma^(-1)(x-d),
    kappa=1-p_E-p_E(e-d)^T Sigma^(-1)(e-d).                  (3)

Strict positivity of all eight atoms makes `Sigma>0`. The covariance identity follows from `P(i,j in X)=Kii Kjj-|Kij|²`. The function `h` is the orthogonal residual of the endpoint indicator after projection onto `1,X1,X2,X3`. Therefore

    E h=0,  E[h(X)(X-d)]=0,  E h²=p_E kappa,  0<kappa<1.    (4)

The strict lower bound follows because an endpoint indicator on the full cube is not an affine function of its bits. The upper bound follows from (3).

Take independent copies `(X,X')` of the occupation sample and define the antisymmetric kernel

    F_E(x,y)=h(x)-h(y).

It is an explicit determinant-specific Stein kernel: its parameters in (3) use only `K`, a determinant atom, and a three-by-three inverse. Independence gives

    E[F_E(X,X')|X]=h(X),
    E[h(X)g(X)]=(1/2)E[F_E(X,X')(g(X)-g(X'))].               (5)

The second identity follows by swapping the two copies in one summand. Because the diagonal is fixed, `E s=0` and `E[s(X)(X-d)]=diag A=0`; hence `E[sh]=v_E`. Put

    rho=s-v_E h/(p_E kappa).

Orthogonal projection gives the exact full-score decomposition

    FI=E rho²+v_E²/(p_E kappa),
    E rho²=(1/2)E[(rho(X)-rho(X'))²].                        (6)

Thus the first term is an ordinary nonnegative reversible refresh-chain energy. No occupation-only proxy replaces full Fisher information.

## 3. Matrix projection of the endpoint determinant

Set `d_J=diag J`, `Gij=|Jij|²`, and define the Hermitian matrices

    U_i=J^(1/2) E_ii J^(1/2),
    Rmat=I-sum_i (G^(-1)d_J)_i U_i,
    nu=3-d_J^T G^(-1)d_J,   C=J^(-1/2) B J^(-1/2).          (7)

The `U_i` are linearly independent, so their Gram matrix `G` is positive definite. Their inner products with `I` are `d_J`; thus `Rmat` is the orthogonal projection of `I` onto their common orthogonal complement, and `||Rmat||_F²=nu`. The zero diagonal of `B` gives `tr(C U_i)=Bii=0`. Consequently

    tr C=tr(C Rmat),   0<=nu<2.                             (8)

For the strict upper bound, the span of the `U_i` contains `J`; the squared norm of the projection of `I` onto that span is at least `(tr J)²/tr(J²)>1`, since `J>0` has three positive eigenvalues.

In fact `nu=0` exactly when `J` is diagonal: `I` lies in the span of the `U_i` exactly when `J^(-1)` is diagonal. When `nu>0`, put `gamma=1-1/nu` and `Cperp=C-(tr C/nu)Rmat`. Orthogonal Pythagoras and the determinant derivative formula give

    ||C||²=(tr C)²/nu+||Cperp||²,
    v_E=p_E tr C,
    a_E=p_E[(tr C)²-tr(C²)]
       =gamma v_E²/p_E-p_E||Cperp||².                       (9)

This is an exact determinant second-jet identity, not a bound on the first score. If `nu=0`, then `tr C=0`, `v_E=0`, and `a_E=-p_E||C||²` instead.

## 4. Complete nonnegative energy theorem

**Theorem 1 (double Stein certificate).** If `nu>0`, every zero-diagonal Hermitian direction satisfies

    -H'' = (1/2)E[(rho(X)-rho(X'))²]
           +(1/kappa-lambda gamma) v_E²/p_E

In particular, writing `gamma_+=max(gamma,0)`, the computable condition

    lambda kappa gamma_+ <= 1                               (11)

implies nonpositive entropy curvature in **every** fixed-diagonal direction at `K`. Quantitatively,

    -H'' >= [1-lambda kappa gamma_+] FI.                     (12)

**Proof.** Substitute (6) and (9) into (2). Every displayed term in (10) is nonnegative under (11). Also `v_E²/p_E<=kappa FI` by (6), proving (12). ∎

Since `gamma<1/2` and `kappa<1`, the easily checked condition `|tau|<=2` suffices without any spectral slab. The matrix projection can improve it greatly when `nu` is close to one. If the full score vanishes, equation (10) still retains `lambda p_E||C||²+2 sum r Lambda`; its energy is not incorrectly forced to vanish with Fisher information. The already established three-site score-null sign is recovered, not claimed as new.

The last term in (10) is also an actual entropy dissipation. For each selected face set `U=p_a p_d`, `V=p_b p_c`. On two copies put rate `2r/(V-U)` in both directions on each edge `(a,d)<->(b,c)` and `(d,a)<->(c,b)`. For `V>U` the rate is nonnegative; use rate zero when `V=U`. The resulting counting-reversible generator `Q` satisfies

    (1/2) D_Q(p tensor p)=2 sum r_ij Lambda_ij,z,
    D_Q(mu)=sum_(unoriented uv) Q_uv(mu_u-mu_v)log(mu_u/mu_v)>=0.   (13)

Each of the two face edges contributes `2r log(V/U)`, proving the identity. Equality faces do zero logarithmic work. This collision generator alone does not match the entire affine acceleration: the signed endpoint component is paid exactly by the two middle terms of (10).
