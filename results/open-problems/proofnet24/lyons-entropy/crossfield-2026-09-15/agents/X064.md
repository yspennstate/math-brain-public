## 1. Exact cut problem and its remaining scope

Let `C^6=W⊕U⊕Z`, with each plane two-dimensional, and let `β=vol(U)` be unit.
Put `h_J=|β_J|²`, `a_A=|vol(W)_A|²`, and `c_B=|vol(Z)_B|²`.
The exterior map `T:U⊥→Λ³C⁶`, `Tf=β∧f`, is an isometry.
For a family F of measured coordinate triples, define

`B_F=T*P_F T`, `d_F(J)=#{M∈F:J⊂M}`,
`S_F=Σ_J h_J(d_F(J)−2)_+`, `C_F=Σ_J h_J min(2,d_F(J))`.

Here `P_F` is the fixed coordinate projection onto F. Direct cofactor expansion
from X018/X020 gives `tr B_F=Σ_J h_Jd_F(J)=C_F+S_F`, and
`g'(F):=g(U,W)(F)=tr(P_W B_F)`.
The capped inclusion cut for this flag is `g'(F)≤C_F`.
Uniform validity over W is equivalent to `τ₂(B_F)≥S_F`, with τ₂ the sum
of the two smallest eigenvalues on the four-dimensional space U⊥.

These cuts guarantee an inclusion flow of row masses `2h_J`, column masses
g', and cell caps h_J if **all** cuts hold. They do not prescribe the bottom
and complementary-top pair-color marginals. Even proving every cut would
therefore leave C038's full harmonic transport problem unresolved.

## 2. A rank-one exterior shadow inequality

Let V be any finite-dimensional coordinate space, β₀ a unit decomposable
k-form, and R₀ its underlying k-plane's orthogonal complement. For a family G
of coordinate (k+1)-sets, let ∂G be all coordinate k-sets contained in a member
of G. Define `D_G=(β₀∧·)*P_G(β₀∧·)` on R₀. Then

`0≤D_G≤(Σ_(J∈∂G)|β₀,J|²) I_R₀`.                              (1)

Indeed, for each z∈R₀, the coordinate support gives exactly
`P_G(β₀∧z)=P_G((P_(∂G)β₀)∧z)`.
Exterior multiplication by z has operator norm at most ||z||: in a unitary
basis whose first vector is z/||z||, it kills the terms already containing
that vector and multiplies every other coefficient by ||z||. Consequently
`||P_G(β₀∧z)||²≤||z||² Σ_(J∈∂G)|β₀,J|²`, proving (1).
This is a proved rank-one shadow bound, not an imported coupling theorem.

## 3. Every cut avoiding one coordinate

**Theorem 1.** Suppose every triple of F avoids a coordinate e. Put
`u_e=(P_U)ee`, `a_e=(P_W)ee`, `c_e=(P_Z)ee`, and `q=1−u_e=a_e+c_e`.
If q>0, let `g_e=P_(U⊥)e/√q` and `R₀=U⊥∩e⊥=U⊥∩g_e⊥`.
Then the following operator and actual-flag inequalities hold:

`B_F≥S_F P_R₀`,                                                (2)
`C_F−g'(F)≥(a_e/q)S_F≥0`.                                     (3)

If q=0, B_F=S_F=C_F=g'(F)=0. Thus every such cut is valid.
The coefficient in (3) is sharp even with all three coordinate masses positive.

**Proof.** First take 0<u_e<1 and select an orthonormal basis of U as
`u=√u_e e+√q u'`, `v_e=0`, where u',v are orthonormal on Q=e⊥.
Then `g_e=√q e−√u_e u'` and `R₀=[u',v]⊥∩Q` has dimension three.
For `f=αg_e+z`, direct expansion gives

`P_(Λ³Q)(u∧v∧f)=√q u'∧v∧z`.                                 (4)

Let `β'=u'∧v`, and let h' be its normalized pair law on the five coordinates Q.
Let G be the complement of F among the ten triples of Q. With D_F,D_G the
corresponding operators on R₀, the isometry `z↦β'∧z` gives

`B_F=0_[g_e] ⊕ q D_F`, `D_F=I_R₀−D_G`.                       (5)

For each pair J⊂Q, `h_J=q h'_J`. It has three possible containing triples
inside Q, so d_F(J)=3 exactly when J∉∂G. Pairs containing e have degree zero.
Therefore

`S_F=q(1−Σ_(J∈∂G)h'_J)=:q s`, with `0≤s≤1`.                  (6)

Equation (1) gives `D_G≤(1−s)I`, hence `D_F≥sI`. Equations (5)–(6)
prove (2). At u_e=0 the same proof uses g_e=e and U⊂Q. At u_e=1, U contains e,
so β∧f has no component in Λ³Q and every h_J with J⊂Q vanishes.

On U⊥, `P_W+P_Z=I`, so
`C_F−g'(F)=tr(P_Z B_F)−S_F`.
Furthermore `||P_Zg_e||²=c_e/q`, whence
`tr(P_Z P_R₀)=2−c_e/q=1+a_e/q`.
Apply (2) to obtain (3). If F is all ten triples of Q, then
`B_F=qP_R₀`, `S_F=q`, and the gap is exactly a_e. This attains (3), including
at generic complex flags with u_e,a_e,c_e all positive. ∎

Since `B_(Fᶜ)=I−B_F`, the actual gaps obey the exact symmetry
`C_(Fᶜ)−g(U,W)(Fᶜ)=C_F−g(U,Z)(F)`.
Thus the complementary cut also holds, with the refined lower bound
`(c_e/q)S_F`. The relevant excess on that right side is S_F, not S_(Fᶜ).

Equation (3) can be written `g'(F)+(S_F/q)Σ_(A∋e)a_A≤C_F`.
It retains actual endpoint mass, but its coefficient depends on U, and it
is not claimed to imply the arbitrary pair-potential inequalities of C038.

## 4. A new present-triangle class with exact spectrum

Fix a coordinate three-set R and e∉R. Define the seven-triple family
`F_(R,e)={M:e∉M, |M∩R|≥2}`.
Its high-degree graph is exactly the three edges inside R, each of degree three.
The internal triple R is present. X020's fractional-star certificate cannot
cover it: its three edge constraints require total weight on R at least 3/2,
while the internal-triple load bounds that sum by one. The complement has a
triangle together with a three-edge tail star, also outside that certificate.

**Theorem 2.** For q>0, use U'=[u',v]⊂Q from (4), and let x,y be the two
eigenvalues of `U'^*P_RU'`. They lie in [0,1]. Then

`spec(B_F)={0,qx,qy,q(x+y−xy)}`, `S_F=qxy`,                     (7)
`C_F−max_W g(U,W)(F)=q min(x,y)(1−max(x,y))`.                  (8)

To prove (7), choose principal-angle bases e₁,e₂,e₃ of C^R and f₁,f₂ of its
two-dimensional complement inside Q, so
`u'=√x e₁+√(1−x)f₁`, `v=√y e₂+√(1−y)f₂`.
The three orthogonal complement vectors in Q are
`v₁=√(1−x)e₁−√x f₁`, `v₂=√(1−y)e₂−√y f₂`, and e₃.
Retaining wedge components with at least two R factors gives mutually
orthogonal images with squared norms y,x,x+y−xy, respectively.
Equation (5) supplies the zero eigenvalue and the factor q. Cauchy–Binet gives
`Σ_(J⊂R)h_J=qxy`. The two smallest eigenvalues sum to q min(x,y), proving (8).
A W spanned by the two largest-eigenvalue vectors attains this gap, so it is
optimal for each x,y,q. At q=0 both sides vanish.

The basis changes preserve R, Q, and the cut projector. They do not change
the measured law by unrestricted diagonalization. Unlike X060's ten-triple
present triangle, this cut deletes all three external triples using e.
Theorem 1 also handles every further deletion and all other cuts inside Q;
monotonicity alone would not justify these conclusions.
