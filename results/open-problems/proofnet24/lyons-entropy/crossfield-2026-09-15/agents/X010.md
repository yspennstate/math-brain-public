## Strongest established result

The Petz–Slater ensemble has an explicit coordinate-reveal filtration of quasifree posterior states. Their one-body covariances form a matrix martingale with positive rank-one branch differences, and **every exterior power is a martingale**. Its predictable matrix bracket is exactly `K(I-K)`. The full determinantal atom score is a scalar stochastic integral against this matrix martingale, so its predictable bracket gives the entire Fisher information, including information beyond linear occupation statistics.

The second atom derivative is a second-exterior martingale. This gives a finite-parameter determinant formula for the whole likelihood-ratio martingale, an exact rational formula for every moving conditional Bernoulli weight (and all its derivatives), and a predictable decomposition of the complete entropy Hessian. The acceleration term is retained as a covariation. A whole family of three-site path kernels proves that the decomposition cannot be made nonnegative merely by taking its levels one at a time, **even after uniform averaging over all reveal orders**. The missing aggregate compensation inequality is stated precisely in Section 8.

The all-dimension identities and the analytic negative-level family, rather than a re-proof of a previously known concavity subclass, are the deliverable. The Petz projection ensemble and its unconditional compound moments are X007's input; the explicit posterior filtration, moving-weight calculus, bracket identity, and failure of levelwise positivity are developed here.

## 1. Conventions and the recovered projection ensemble

All spaces are finite-dimensional. Fix a strict Hermitian contraction `0<K<I` in the measured coordinate basis, and put

\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1},\qquad T=L^{1/2},\qquad M=K(I-K).
\tag{1}
\]

For a subset `S`, let `T_S=T E_S`, and define

\[
p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S,\qquad
\Pi_S=T_S(L_S)^{-1}T_S^*,\quad \Pi_\varnothing=0.
\tag{2}
\]

Every `p_S` is positive. The matrix `Pi_S` is the orthogonal projection onto the span of the selected columns of `T`. Let `Gamma(C)=⊕_r wedge^r C` denote exterior second quantization. The quasifree density matrix is

\[
\rho_K=\det(I-K)\Gamma(L).
\tag{3}
\]

The vector `psi_S=rho_K^(1/2)|S>/sqrt(p_S)` is the normalized exterior product of the selected columns of `T`, hence its pure state is the quasifree state of covariance `Pi_S`. Therefore

\[
\rho_K=\sum_Sp_S|\psi_S\rangle\langle\psi_S|,
\qquad \mathbb E_p\,\wedge^r\Pi_X=\wedge^rK.
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) is the X007 Petz–Slater ensemble. It also follows directly from Cauchy–Binet in each exterior degree. No rotation of the measured entropy is made: `Pi_S` is a new covariance in the original output measurement basis, and `H(P_K)` remains classical occupation entropy.

## 2. An explicit quasifree posterior at every revealed history

Choose any permutation of the measured coordinates and reveal the bits of `X~p_K` in that order. Write `F_j` for the resulting filtration. At a history let `U` be the coordinates revealed present, `V` the coordinates revealed absent, and `R` the unrevealed coordinates. Set

\[
P_U=T_U(L_U)^{-1}T_U^*,\qquad
W=(I-P_U)T_R,\qquad G=W^*W,
\tag{5}
\]

with `P_empty=0`. Because `T` is invertible, the residual columns of `W` are linearly independent and `G` is positive definite whenever `R` is nonempty.

**Theorem 1 (posterior exterior martingale).** The posterior covariance is

\[
\boxed{
Q_{U,V}=\mathbb E[\Pi_X\mid U\subset X,\ V\cap X=\varnothing]
=P_U+W(I+W^*W)^{-1}W^*.}
\tag{6}
\]

Moreover, for every exterior degree,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[\wedge^r\Pi_X\mid F_j]=\wedge^rQ_j.}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus `Q_0=K`, `Q_n=Pi_X`, and both `Q_j` and all `wedge^r Q_j` are martingales.

**Proof.** If the completion is `X=U∪Z`, `Z⊂R`, the Gram determinant Schur identity gives

\[
\det L_{U\cup Z}=\det L_U\det G_Z.
\tag{8}
\]

Summing principal minors gives the conditional law `P(Z|U,V)=det G_Z/det(I+G)`. The projection decomposes orthogonally as

\[
\Pi_{U\cup Z}=P_U+W_Z(G_Z)^{-1}W_Z^*.
\tag{9}
\]

Choose the unit Slater vector of the fixed occupied subspace `ran P_U`. Each completion's unit Slater vector factors as its exterior product with the unit Slater vector of the residual columns `W_Z`. At degree `k`, Cauchy–Binet says

\[
\sum_{|Z|=k}|\wedge W_Z\rangle\langle\wedge W_Z|
=\wedge^k(WW^*).
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently the residual density matrix is `Gamma(WW*)/det(I+W*W)`. It is quasifree with covariance `WW*(I+WW*)^(-1)=W(I+W*W)^(-1)W*`. The fixed occupied factor contributes `P_U`, on an orthogonal subspace. This proves (6). The `r`-body reduced density matrix of a quasifree state is `wedge^r` of its covariance, directly by the determinant formula for its correlations; applying this to the same conditional density matrix proves (7). Taking conditional expectation proves the martingale assertions. ∎

There is no assumption that the conditional covariances remain strict. Some eigenvalues become exactly zero or one as bits are revealed, as is required by the terminal projection. The formula (6) is well defined throughout.

## 3. Rank-one branches and the exact nonnegative matrix bracket

At one node, designate the next residual column by `w`, let `W_-` contain the other unrevealed columns, and put

\[
D=I+W_-W_-^*,\quad r=w^*D^{-1}w>0,\quad
q=\frac r{1+r},\quad
R=\frac{D^{-1}ww^*D^{-1}}r.
\tag{11}
\]

The letter `R` in this section denotes a matrix branch difference, not the earlier set of unrevealed indices.

**Theorem 2 (rank-one posterior split).** With `Q_b` the posterior after the next bit equals `b`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_0=P_U+I-D^{-1},\qquad Q_1=Q_0+R,\qquad
Q=(1-q)Q_0+qQ_1,\qquad 0\preceq R\preceq I,\quad\operatorname{rank}R=1.}
\tag{12}
\]

The probability of the next bit being one is `q`. In stochastic notation,

\[
\Delta Q_j=(X_j-q_j)R_j.
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** Give column `w` an extra positive activity `s`. The conditional normalizer changes from `det D` to `det(D+sww*)=det D(1+sr)`. Thus inclusion odds at `s=1` are `r`, giving `q`. The covariance is

\[
Q(s)=P_U+I-(D+sww^*)^{-1}
=Q_0+\frac{s}{1+sr}D^{-1}ww^*D^{-1}.
\tag{14}
\]

The limits `s↓0` and `s↑∞` condition the bit to zero and one, respectively. They give the branch formulas. The rank-one matrix `R` is positive and its nonzero eigenvalue is

\[
\operatorname{tr}R=\frac{w^*D^{-2}w}{w^*D^{-1}w}\le1,
\tag{15}
\]

because `D≥I`. This proves (12)–(13). ∎

**Corollary 3 (matrix predictable quadratic variation).**

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^nq_j(1-q_j)R_j^2=K(I-K).}
\tag{16}
\]

More generally, for every fixed matrix `B`,

\[
\mathbb E[(\Pi_X-K)B(\Pi_X-K)]
=\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)R_jBR_j.
\tag{17}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the terminal martingale increment sum on both sides of `B`. Every cross term has expectation zero by conditioning on the later step's past. The remaining diagonal terms give (17). For `B=I`, use `Pi_X²=Pi_X` and `E Pi_X=K` to obtain `E(Pi_X-K)²=K-K²`. ∎

In particular the right side of (17) is a completely positive, positive-valued expression for positive `B`. Equation (16) is an exact normalization for this bracket, independent of the reveal order.

## 4. Entropy increments that genuinely telescope

The recovered filtration supplies nonnegative entropy increments without making an affine-covariance assumption about the whole density matrix. Introduce `Y`, conditionally on `X`, with law `P_{Pi_X}` in the same measured basis. Equation (7) gives

\[
\mathcal L(Y\mid F_j)=P_{Q_j},\qquad \mathcal L(Y)=P_K.
\tag{18}
\]

At each node,

\[
\mathcal I_j
=H(P_Q)-(1-q)H(P_{Q_0})-qH(P_{Q_1})
=I(Y;X_j\mid F_{j-1}=\text{history})\ge0.
\tag{19}
\]

The equality follows from the mixture in (12): all determinant inclusion probabilities are affine along its rank-one segment, hence its measured probability law is the same mixture. The sign is the ordinary log-sum inequality. Thus

\[
\boxed{
H(P_K)-\mathbb E H(P_{\Pi_X})
=\sum_j\mathbb E\mathcal I_j=I(X;Y).}
\tag{20}
\]

Also, the ordinary entropy chain rule gives `H(P_K)=Σ_j E h(q_j)`. The two telescopes are compatible but are different: (20) tracks information in the recovered projection ensemble. Since `|Y|=|X|` almost surely, it additionally gives

\[
H(P_K)-\mathbb E H(P_{\Pi_X})\ge H(|X|).
\tag{21}
\]

These increments are nonnegative at a fixed base kernel. Their derivatives with respect to an affine kernel direction do not inherit that sign automatically. The next section supplies those derivatives exactly.

## 5. Full score and second-exterior martingales

Let `K(t)=K+tA`, with `A=A*`. All derivatives below are evaluated at zero. The random variable `X` and the recovered filtration are formed at the fixed base `K`; probabilities of a fixed history are differentiated along the actual path `p_{K(t)}`. In particular we do **not** hold a projection mixture fixed and mistake it for the moving quasifree state.

Define

\[
B=M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2},\qquad C_j=Q_j-K,
\]

\[
S_j=\operatorname{tr}(BC_j),\qquad
T_j=(\operatorname{tr}BC_j)^2-\operatorname{tr}(BC_jBC_j).
\tag{22}
\]

**Theorem 4 (score and acceleration representation).** If `w_j(t)` denotes the probability of the revealed history, then

\[
\boxed{S_j=\frac{w_j'}{w_j},\qquad T_j=\frac{w_j''}{w_j}.}
\tag{23}
\]

These are martingales at the fixed base distribution. With

\[
\beta_j=\operatorname{tr}(BR_j),\qquad
\gamma_j=\operatorname{tr}(BC_{j-1}BR_j),\qquad
\zeta_j=X_j-q_j,
\tag{24}
\]

their increments are

\[
\boxed{\Delta S_j=\zeta_j\beta_j,\qquad
\Delta T_j=2\zeta_j(S_{j-1}\beta_j-\gamma_j).}
\tag{25}
\]

The changing conditional weights satisfy the exact formulas

\[
\boxed{
q_j'=q_j(1-q_j)\beta_j,\qquad
q_j''=-2q_j(1-q_j)\gamma_j.}
\tag{26}
\]

**Proof.** Differentiating `log p_S=log det(I-K)+log det L_S` yields the matrix score coefficient

\[
G_S=-(I-K)^{-1}
 +(I-K)^{-1}E_S L_S^{-1}E_S^*(I-K)^{-1}
=M^{-1/2}(\Pi_S-K)M^{-1/2}.
\tag{27}
\]

The signed determinant atom `p_S=(-1)^{|S^c|}det(K-I_{S^c})` has inverse coefficient `G_S`, by differentiating in arbitrary Hermitian directions. Its second derivative is therefore

\[
\frac{p_S''}{p_S}
=(\operatorname{tr}G_SA)^2-\operatorname{tr}(G_SAG_SA)
=2\operatorname{Tr}_{\wedge^2}(\wedge^2(\Pi_S-K)\,\wedge^2B).
\tag{28}
\]

Conditional expectation of the score gives `S_j` by (6). Conditional expectation of (28) gives `T_j` by (7), after expanding the polarized exterior square of `Pi_S-K`. The conditional expectations are respectively `w_j'/w_j` and `w_j''/w_j`, proving (23).

Substitute `C_j=C_{j-1}+zeta_j R_j` into (22). The quadratic term in `zeta_j` is zero, because

\[
(\operatorname{tr}BR_j)^2=\operatorname{tr}(BR_jBR_j)
\tag{29}
\]

for rank-one `R_j`. This gives (25).

Finally differentiate the elementary factorization `w_j=w_{j-1} q_j^{X_j}(1-q_j)^{1-X_j}`. Its first score increment is `zeta_j q_j'/[q_j(1-q_j)]`. Its normalized second-derivative increment is

\[
\zeta_j\frac{q_j''+2S_{j-1}q_j'}{q_j(1-q_j)}.
\tag{30}
\]

Comparing (30) with (25) proves (26). This calculation keeps the changing history weights, their score, and the conditional weight's acceleration. ∎

**Finite-parameter strengthening.** The same filtration controls all moving conditional weights, not only their first two derivatives. For every `t` for which `0<K+tA<I`,

\[
\boxed{\frac{w_j(t)}{w_j(0)}=\det(I+tBC_j).}
\tag{30a}
\]

For each fixed such `t`, this is a positive mean-one martingale in the reveal index. At a node set

\[
b_j(t)=\operatorname{tr}[(I+tBC_{j-1})^{-1}BR_j].
\]

The determinant lemma gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{w_j(t)/w_j(0)}{w_{j-1}(t)/w_{j-1}(0)}
=1+(X_j-q_j)t b_j(t),\qquad
q_j(t)=q_j+q_j(1-q_j)t b_j(t).}
\tag{30b}
\]

In particular, for every integer `m≥1`,

\[
\boxed{q_j^{(m)}(0)=(-1)^{m-1}m!\,q_j(1-q_j)
\operatorname{tr}[(BC_{j-1})^{m-1}BR_j].}
\tag{30c}
\]

To prove (30a), the terminal determinant atom ratio is exactly `det(I+tB(Pi_X-K))`. Expand this determinant in exterior degrees and use (7), including its polarized consequence after subtracting the constant matrix `K`, to take conditional expectation. This is `w_j(t)/w_j(0)` and equals the displayed determinant. Its positivity supplies the inverse used in (30b). The rank-one determinant lemma then proves (30b), and the local Neumann expansion at zero proves (30c). Thus the nonlinear conditional weights are given by an exact rational transform of a fixed positive rank-one posterior tree. The Hessian compensation issue is not caused by a lack of control of these weights.

The first part of (25) yields the nonnegative predictable bracket

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{FI}_K(A)
=\mathbb E S_n^2
=\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)\beta_j^2.}
\tag{31}
\]

This is the full atom Fisher information. It is not the Fisher information of the linear occupation vector alone. As a secondary consequence of (16), rank-one Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)\le\operatorname{tr}(B^2M).
\tag{32}
\]

The upper bound (32) is not sufficient for the target and is not used to replace the equality (31).

## 6. The exact predictable entropy-curvature identity

Let `f_X=log p_X`, frozen at `K`, and let `Z_j=E[f_X|F_j]`. Define the predictable log-likelihood contrast

\[
d_j=\mathbb E[f_X\mid F_{j-1},X_j=1]
     -\mathbb E[f_X\mid F_{j-1},X_j=0].
\tag{33}
\]

Its chain-rule expression is

\[
d_j=\log\frac{q_j}{1-q_j}
+H(X_{>j}\mid F_{j-1},X_j=0)
-H(X_{>j}\mid F_{j-1},X_j=1).
\tag{34}
\]

Thus `Delta Z_j=zeta_j d_j`.

**Theorem 5 (complete curvature as bracket plus covariation).**

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
=\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)
\left\{\beta_j^2+2(S_{j-1}\beta_j-\gamma_j)d_j\right\}.}
\tag{35}
\]

**Proof.** The exact atom calculus gives `-H''=E S_n²+E T_n f_X`. Since `T_0=0`, martingale orthogonality gives

\[
\mathbb E T_n f_X
=\sum_j\mathbb E[\Delta T_j\Delta Z_j]
=2\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)(S_{j-1}\beta_j-\gamma_j)d_j.
\tag{36}
\]

Combine with (31). ∎

This is an identity in every finite dimension and every Hermitian direction. It is also valid for every deterministic reveal order, so randomizing the order independently leaves its complete sum unchanged. The first summand is a nonnegative predictable quadratic variation; the second is a predictable covariation, whose compensation remains the hard part.

## 7. Why neither nodewise positivity nor uniformly averaged level positivity closes the proof

### 7.1 A two-site nodewise obstruction with exact cancellation

Take `K=diag(3/4,3/4)` and `A=[[0,1],[1,0]]`. All first atom derivatives vanish, so `beta_j=0` and the full Fisher bracket is zero. Reveal coordinate one before two. At step two, the predictable summand in braces times `q_2(1-q_2)` is

\[
-\frac{32}{3}(X_1-3/4)\log3.
\tag{37}
\]

It is `-(8/3)log3` on `X_1=1` and `8log3` on `X_1=0`. The average is zero, which is exactly the total curvature in this off-diagonal direction at a diagonal base. Interchanging the coordinates gives the same obstruction. Positivity cannot be assigned to each revealed history.

### 7.2 An analytic three-site family with a negative entire level after order averaging

Consider any strict real path kernel

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&x&0\\x&b&y\\0&y&c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad x,y>0,
\tag{38}
\]

and the Hermitian imaginary missing-edge direction

\[
A=i\eta(e_1e_3^*-e_3e_1^*),\qquad \eta\ne0.
\tag{39}
\]

Every first atom derivative is zero: real determinants are unchanged by complex conjugation, which sends `t` to `-t`. Thus all scores and all Fisher bracket terms vanish.

The two endpoint leaves are independent at the base. Conditional on their bits, the central occupation mean is

\[
q_{uv}=q_{00}-u\alpha-v\delta,
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{x^2}{a(1-a)}>0,\quad
\delta=\frac{y^2}{c(1-c)}>0,
\tag{40}
\]

\[
q_{00}=b+\frac{x^2}{1-a}+\frac{y^2}{1-c}.
\tag{41}
\]

This is the ordinary two-leaf Schur conditional formula: the leaf block is diagonal, so the two rank-one scalar corrections add. Strictness ensures every `q_uv` lies in `(0,1)`.

If the first two revealed coordinates are the two leaves, the expected **second-level** contribution in (35) is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_2=2\eta^2\{h(q_{00})+h(q_{11})-h(q_{10})-h(q_{01})\}<0.}
\tag{42}
\]

**Proof.** The leaf-pair probabilities are independent at the base. Along (39), their second derivatives in the order `00,10,01,11` are `(-2,2,2,-2)eta²`. The first level has zero acceleration. Hence the level-two covariation is the contraction of this pair acceleration against `E[log p_X|X_1,X_3]`. The latter is `log p_leafpair-h(q_uv)`. The independent leaf-pair logarithms have zero mixed difference, leaving exactly (42).

Its sign follows without approximation from

\[
h(q_{00})+h(q_{00}-\alpha-\delta)
-h(q_{00}-\alpha)-h(q_{00}-\delta)
=\int_0^\alpha\!\int_0^\delta
h''(q_{00}-s-t)\,dt\,ds
\le-4\alpha\delta<0.
\tag{43}
\]

If the first two revealed coordinates are another pair, the restricted kernel does not change in this direction, so both its score and acceleration vanish and its level-two contribution is zero. Under a uniformly random reveal order the leaf pair occurs first with probability `1/3`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mathrm{order}}\mathcal C_2
\le-\frac83\eta^2\alpha\delta<0.}
\tag{44}
\]

This proves that uniform averaging of each predictable level cannot make every level nonnegative. ∎

For a concrete rational member, use

\[
K_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/10&0\\1/10&11/50&1/10\\0&1/10&4/5
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \eta=1.
\tag{45}
\]

Its leading principal minors are `(1/2,1/10,3/40)` and those of `I-K_*` are `(1/2,19/50,71/1000)`, so it is a strict contraction. Its four central conditional means are

\[
(q_{00},q_{10},q_{01},q_{11})=(29/100,1/4,91/400,3/16),
\quad\alpha=1/25,\quad\delta=1/16.
\tag{46}
\]

The uniformly averaged second level is at most `-1/150`; its decimal value is about `-0.00922917988`. The negative sign and bound are analytic consequences of (43), not numerical findings.

**The total curvature remains positive.** This is not a counterexample to Lyons. Let

\[
O_z=\frac{p_{0z0}\,p_{1z1}}{p_{1z0}\,p_{0z1}},\qquad z=0,1.
\tag{47}
\]

The conditional law of the leaves given the center is a two-site DPP, so `0<O_z<1` when `x,y>0`. Explicitly, its induced off-diagonal entry is `-xy/b` when the center is occupied and `xy/(1-b)` when absent, so its two-site covariance determinant is strictly smaller than the product of its means. Expanding the determinants along the imaginary missing edge gives

\[
\boxed{-H''(K;A)
=-2\eta^2\{(1-b)\log O_0+b\log O_1\}>0.}
\tag{48}
\]

Thus later predictable covariation compensates the strictly negative level in (44). Equation (48) is a verification of the obstruction's consistency with known incident-row and score-null concavity results, not a new claimed concavity class.

## 8. The precise remaining aggregate inequality

The construction reduces the universal local target exactly to the following statement for the specific Gram posterior tree of a strict contraction, its actual conditional probabilities, and the frozen log-likelihood contrasts (33):

\[
\boxed{
2\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)
(\gamma_j-S_{j-1}\beta_j)d_j
\le
\mathbb E\sum_jq_j(1-q_j)\beta_j^2.}
\tag{49}
\]

All of `Q_j,R_j,q_j,d_j,B` in (49) are explicit functions of the base kernel and direction. The left side includes every moving-weight correction through (26). The right side is the full Fisher bracket.

Neither the fact that the rank-one increments are positive, nor positivity of the entropy increments (19), nor the normalization (16), by itself proves (49). Equations (37) and (44) show why the simplest attempt to dominate every node or every uniformly averaged level separately fails. A successful martingale argument would need a potential or a predictable correction whose telescoping differences transfer curvature between levels. It must allow the score-null directions of Section 7, in which the right side is identically zero and the required sign comes entirely from global covariation cancellation.

Proving (49) for all strict contractions would prove the universal local entropy Hessian sign; integration on strict affine segments and continuity would then prove the stated closed-interval Lyons conjecture. No such inequality is asserted here.
