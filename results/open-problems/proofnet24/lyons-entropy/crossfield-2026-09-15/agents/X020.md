## 1. Exact harmonic cost identity

Let `H=ker L`, where `L` is pair-to-triple inclusion on six sites. For `q∈H`, every pair sum `Σ_(M⊃p)q_M` vanishes. Consequently its total and every one-site sum vanish. If `S` is any four-set, inclusion–exclusion over its two-site complement gives

`Σ_(M⊂S, |M|=3) q_M=0`.

For a partition into pairs `E=A ⊔ J ⊔ B`, with `A={a,b}`, `J={i,j}`, the elementary middle cost therefore satisfies the exact identity

`c_q(A,J,B)=q_(A+i)+q_(A+j)=−q_(J+a)−q_(J+b)`.

Put `v_J(r)=q_(J+r)` for the four sites outside `J`. Their sum is zero. Thus the exact addition-only pointwise dual bound is

`|c_q(A,J,B)| ≤ k_q(J)`,

where `k_q(J)` is the sum of the largest two of the four values `v_J(r)`. It is nonnegative and is also the negative of the sum of the smallest two. This can be strictly smaller than `κ_q(J)=Σ_r max(v_J(r),0)`, which X018 has proved bounds the cofactor positive part. Equality holds between these capacities when the four values have two nonnegative and two nonpositive entries, including matching cubes.

Hence every full diamond satisfies

`|q·g(W,U)| ≤ Σ_J h_J k_q(J)`.

This is still only the subfamily of C038's full costed dual with endpoint potentials zero. Its universal determinantal validity has not yet been established here.

## 2. A stronger capacitated inclusion-transport statement

Let `g'=g(U,W)`. Exterior reciprocity from X018 gives `q·g'=−q·g(W,U)` for `q∈H`. A sufficient condition for every inequality above is an inclusion table `y_(J,M)`, `J⊂M`, satisfying

`Σ_(M⊃J)y_(J,M)=2h_J`, `Σ_(J⊂M)y_(J,M)=g'_M`, and `0≤y_(J,M)≤h_J`.

Indeed, conditioned on `J` with `h_J>0`, the four ratios `y_(J,J+r)/h_J` belong to `[0,1]` and sum to two. Their `q`-weighted sum lies between the sums of the smallest and largest two entries of `v_J`. Averaging proves the displayed harmonic inequality.

This capped transport exists if and only if, for every family `F` of triples,

`g'(F) ≤ Σ_J h_J min(2,d_F(J))`,

where `d_F(J)=#{M∈F:J⊂M}`. Necessity is immediate. Sufficiency follows from the finite source–pair–triple–sink flow network with source capacities `2h_J`, pair–triple capacities `h_J`, and sink capacities `g'_M`: minimizing over which pairs are placed on either side of a cut gives exactly the right-hand side. Both total masses are two, so a flow of value two saturates every required marginal. This is an elementary finite max-flow/min-cut statement; no specialized matroid theorem is being imported.

## 3. Exact exterior form of the missing inequality

Let `β=u∧v` be a unit volume form of `U`, and let `T:U^⊥→Λ³C⁶` be `Tf=β∧f`. This is an isometry. For a triple family `F`, define the positive contraction `B_F=T*P_F T` on the four-dimensional space `U^⊥`. Then

`g'(F)=tr(P_W B_F)` and `tr B_F=Σ_J h_J d_F(J)`.

The first identity expands the two basis vectors of `W`. For the second, the cofactor vector of a triple `M` is orthogonal to `U`, and its squared norm is `Σ_(J⊂M)h_J`. Its coordinate squared norm therefore agrees with the squared row norm of `T`; summing those row norms over `F` proves the trace identity.

Consequently capped Hall for every two-plane `W⊂U^⊥` is equivalent to the exact spectral inequality

`λ₁(B_F)+λ₂(B_F) ≤ Σ_J h_J min(2,d_F(J))`.

Equivalently, with eigenvalues in decreasing order,

`λ₃(B_F)+λ₄(B_F) ≥ Σ_J h_J (d_F(J)−2)_+`.

The trace identity proves the equivalence. If `d_F(J)≤2` for every pair, the claim follows immediately from nonnegativity of the two discarded eigenvalues. Also `B_F+B_(Fᶜ)=I` on `U^⊥`, and the spectral gap is identical for complementary families, as shown below. Sections 4–7 prove complete classes of mixed high-degree cuts; arbitrary cuts remain open.

Even a proof of this lemma would establish all addition-only harmonic duals, not C038's endpoint-costed inequalities. The remaining endpoint potentials must be retained when claiming a full diamond.

## 4. Spectral preliminaries and exact partial-star spectra

Use decreasing eigenvalues and write
\[
\tau_2(B)=\lambda_3(B)+\lambda_4(B),\qquad
S_F(h)=\sum_Jh_J(d_F(J)-2)_+.
\]
The exact universal capped cut statement from Section 3 is
\[
\tau_2(B_F)\geq S_F(h). \tag{20}
\]
The variational formula
\[
\tau_2(A)=\min_{\dim R=2}\operatorname{tr}(P_RA)
\]
shows that \(\tau_2\) is monotone in the positive semidefinite order and superadditive. Since \(B_{F^c}=I-B_F\), \(d_{F^c}=4-d_F\), and \(\sum_Jh_J=1\),
\[
S_{F^c}(h)=2-\operatorname{tr}B_F+S_F(h).
\]
It follows exactly that
\[
\boxed{\tau_2(B_{F^c})-S_{F^c}(h)=\tau_2(B_F)-S_F(h).} \tag{21}
\]
Thus proving a spectral capped cut proves its complement as well.

For completeness, reciprocity from Section 2 also has a direct proof independent of the cofactor identity. Three-row Cauchy–Binet gives
\[
g(W,U)_M+g(U,W)_M=\det(P_{W+U})_M.
\]
The projection inclusion formula expresses the right side as the sum of top probabilities over four-sets containing \(M\). Its pairing with a harmonic \(q\) vanishes because the sum of \(q\) on the four triples of any four-set is zero. Hence \(q\cdot g(U,W)=-q\cdot g(W,U)\).

Let \(F_J\) be all four triples containing a fixed pair \(J\). If \(x,y\) are the eigenvalues of the compression of \(P_U\) to the coordinate plane \(\mathbb C^J\), then
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{spec}(B_{F_J})=\{x,y,xy,xy\},\qquad h_J=xy.} \tag{22}
\]
Here \(0\leq x,y\leq1\), so the bottom two eigenvalues both equal \(h_J\).

To prove this, choose principal-angle bases
\[
u_1=\sqrt{x}\,e_1+\sqrt{1-x}\,f_1,\qquad
u_2=\sqrt{y}\,e_2+\sqrt{1-y}\,f_2,
\]
where \(e_1,e_2\) span the coordinate plane \(J\), and \(f_1,\ldots,f_4\) are orthonormal in its complement. The orthogonal complements to \(u_1,u_2\) in their respective two-dimensional planes, together with \(f_3,f_4\), form a basis of \(U^\perp\). The components of \(\beta\wedge f\) containing both directions of \(J\) have squared norms \(y,x,xy,xy\), respectively, and are mutually orthogonal. This proves (22). The computation rotates only within the two fixed coordinate subspaces; projection onto all triples containing \(J\) is invariant under these rotations. It does not arbitrarily diagonalize the measured DPP kernel.

If \(F\) is any \(d\)-element subfamily of this pair star, then
\[
\boxed{\tau_2(B_F)=(d-2)_+h_J.} \tag{23}
\]
For \(d\leq2\), the operator has rank at most two. For \(d=4\), use (22). For \(d=3\), subtract one rank-one cofactor effect from (22). Rank-one interlacing forces the third eigenvalue to remain \(h_J\), while the fourth is zero because the operator has rank at most three. Degenerate cases follow directly or by continuity. Thus the capped bound is spectrally sharp for every partial pair star.

## 5. A rank-one graph bound with its transport proved

The next reduction uses a complete rank-one statement. Let \(v\) be a unit vector in \(\mathbb C^5\), and \(G\) any graph on its five coordinates. Define on \(v^\perp\)
\[
A_G=(v\wedge\cdot)^*P_G(v\wedge\cdot).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\tau_2(A_G)\geq\sum_j|v_j|^2(\deg_G(j)-2)_+.} \tag{24}
\]

Here is a self-contained derivation of the needed rank-one transport, credited to C033. Choose any two-plane \(R=[r_1,r_2]\subset v^\perp\). There is an inclusion transport from \(\mu_R\) on pairs \(B\) to \(\mu_{R+v}\) on triples \(T\): for every pair family \(\mathcal A\), exterior contraction gives
\[
\|P_{\mathcal A}(r_1\wedge r_2)\|^2
=\|P_{\mathcal A}\iota_vP_{N(\mathcal A)}(v\wedge r_1\wedge r_2)\|^2
\leq\|P_{N(\mathcal A)}(v\wedge r_1\wedge r_2)\|^2.
\]
These are all ordinary weighted Hall inequalities and provide the claimed transport.

Set \(I=T\setminus B\), a singleton. Its law is \(|v_i|^2\), by subtracting the coordinate inclusion probabilities of \(B\) from those of \(T\). If \(B=\{j,k\}\), use the two middle edges \(I+j,I+k\). For a fixed edge \(e\), its aggregate middle mass is
\[
\sum_{T\supset e}\mu_{R+v}(T)-\mu_R(e)
=\mu_{v+r_1}(e)+\mu_{v+r_2}(e),
\]
by two-row Cauchy–Binet. Every marginal is therefore established using this one transport.

Conditioned on \(I=i\), two distinct neighbors are selected, so at most \(\min(2,\deg_G(i))\) of the two middle edges belong to \(G\). Consequently
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_RA_G)
\leq\sum_i|v_i|^2\min(2,\deg_G(i)).
\]
Maximize over all two-planes \(R\), and use
\(\operatorname{tr}A_G=\sum_i|v_i|^2\deg_G(i)\). This gives (24). This step invokes only the established rank-one case; all dimensions and orthogonality hypotheses have been checked and its needed proof has been included.

## 6. A coordinate contraction proves every single-center excess bound

**Theorem 1.** For every coordinate \(e\), every complex two-plane \(U\), and every triple family \(F\),
\[
\boxed{\tau_2(B_F)\geq
\sum_{j\ne e}h_{ej}(d_F(ej)-2)_+.} \tag{25}
\]

**Proof.** Set \(p=(P_U)_{ee}\). If \(p=0\), all \(h_{ej}=0\), and positivity proves the assertion. Suppose \(0<p<1\), and choose a basis of \(U\) with
\[
u=\sqrt p\,e+\sqrt{1-p}\,u',\qquad v_e=0.
\]
On the remaining five coordinates, \(u',v\) are orthonormal. Put
\[
g=\sqrt{1-p}\,e-\sqrt p\,u',\qquad
R_0=[u',v]^\perp\subset\mathbb C^5.
\]
Every \(f\in U^\perp\) is uniquely \(f=\alpha g+z\), \(z\in R_0\). Define
\[
S(\alpha g+z)=-\alpha u'+\sqrt p\,z.
\]
It maps \(U^\perp\) onto \(v^\perp\subset\mathbb C^5\) with singular values \(1,\sqrt p,\sqrt p,\sqrt p\).

An explicit wedge expansion gives the part containing coordinate \(e\):
\[
P_{\{M:e\in M\}}(u\wedge v\wedge f)
=e\wedge v\wedge Sf. \tag{26}
\]
Let \(G\) be the link graph \(\{jk:ejk\in F\}\). Equation (26) gives the exact identity
\[
B_{F\cap\{M:e\in M\}}=S^*A_GS. \tag{27}
\]
Since \(SS^*\succeq pI\), each ordered eigenvalue of \(S^*A_GS\) is at least \(p\) times the corresponding eigenvalue of \(A_G\). Indeed its eigenvalues are those of \(A_G^{1/2}SS^*A_G^{1/2}\succeq pA_G\). Monotonicity and (24) imply
\[
\tau_2(B_F)\geq p\tau_2(A_G)
\geq p\sum_j|v_j|^2(\deg_G(j)-2)_+.
\]
Finally \(h_{ej}=p|v_j|^2\) and \(\deg_G(j)=d_F(ej)\). This proves (25). If \(p=1\), take \(u=e\); (27) holds with \(S\) unitary, and the same proof applies. ∎

This is a determinant-specific inequality for arbitrary \(F\), even when there are other high-degree pairs elsewhere. It accounts for all excess incident to one coordinate, with its full Pluecker weights.

## 7. Fractional star packing and completed capped Hall classes

Define the graph of high-degree pairs
\[
H_F=\{J:d_F(J)\geq3\}.
\]
For a coordinate \(e\), let
\[
F_e^H=\{M\in F:\text{some }ej\in H_F\text{ is contained in }M\}.
\]
Every triple in \(F_e^H\) contains \(e\). For a high-degree pair \(ej\), all its triples are retained, so \(d_{F_e^H}(ej)=d_F(ej)\).

**Theorem 2 (fractional certificate).** Suppose nonnegative numbers \(t_e\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{e:M\in F_e^H}t_e\leq1\quad(M\in F),\qquad
t_i+t_j\geq1\quad(ij\in H_F). \tag{28}
\]
Then the complete capped Hall inequality for this \(F\) holds for every complex \(U\) and every two-plane \(W\subset U^\perp\).

**Proof.** The first condition implies
\[
B_F\succeq\sum_e t_eB_{F_e^H}.
\]
Monotonicity, superadditivity, and Theorem 1 give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_2(B_F)
&\geq\sum_e t_e\sum_{j:ej\in H_F}h_{ej}(d_F(ej)-2)\\
&=\sum_{ij\in H_F}(t_i+t_j)h_{ij}(d_F(ij)-2)
\geq S_F(h).
\end{aligned}
\]
Use the exact equivalence of the spectral and capped Hall inequalities. ∎

There is also a determinant-dependent version: the edgewise second condition in (28) may be replaced by the single weighted inequality
\[
\sum_{ij\in H_F}(t_i+t_j-1)h_{ij}(d_F(ij)-2)\geq0. \tag{29}
\]
Thus the best lower bound obtained this way is a six-variable finite fractional packing problem with a Pluecker-weighted objective. This is a sufficient certificate; universal feasibility of this packing is not asserted.

**Corollary 3 (all high-degree star forests).** If every nontrivial component of \(H_F\) is a star, capped Hall holds for \(F\), and also for \(F^c\).

Choose one center of each star, assigning \(t_e=1\) there and zero elsewhere. Every high-degree edge is covered. A triple cannot contain high-degree edges from two distinct components: their disjoint vertex sets would require at least four sites. Consequently its load in (28) is at most one. Theorem 2 applies, and (21) gives the complement. Arbitrary additional triples of pair degree at most two are allowed; the cut need not itself be a union of full stars.

**Corollary 4 (additional triangular components).** A component of \(H_F\) may instead be a triangle \(R\), provided the triple \(R\) itself is absent from \(F\). Give each of its three vertices weight \(1/2\). Each edge then receives total one, and each incident triple has exactly two vertices of \(R\), hence total load one. This combines with disjoint star components and other such triangular components. Complements are again covered by (21).

For example, take the nine triples selecting exactly two sites from a fixed three-set \(R\). Its high-degree graph is exactly the triangle on \(R\), each edge of degree three. The fractional certificate proves
\[
\tau_2(B_F)\geq\sum_{J\subset R}h_J
\]
uniformly over every complex \(U\). This is a combination of three coordinate contractions and is outside the star-forest class.

## 8. The Pluecker constraint cannot be removed from the operator problem

A tempting stronger route fixed \(W\), allowed arbitrary two-forms \(\beta\in\Lambda^2W^\perp\), and attempted to prove capped Hall as a positive semidefinite quadratic form in their six coordinates. That extension is false.

Let \(Q\) be the product of the three rational Householder factors with normal vectors
\[
(1,2,3,4,5,6),\quad(2,-1,3,-2,1,4),\quad(3,1,-2,4,2,-1),
\]
in that order; each factor is \(I-2rr^T/\|r\|^2\). Let its first two columns span \(W\), and let its last four columns be a basis of \(V=W^\perp\). In the ordered exterior coordinates \(01,02,03,12,13,23\) of \(\Lambda^2V\), set
\[
z=(-842052,40754,-308860,-130625,348181,235797).
\]
Its Pluecker quadric value is
\[
z_{01}z_{23}-z_{02}z_{13}+z_{03}z_{12}=-172398266418\ne0.
\]
For the 15-triple family recorded completely in `verified_output.json`, the normalized quadratic form
\[
\sum_J|\beta_J|^2\min(2,d_F(J))
-\sum_{s=1}^2\|P_F(w_s\wedge\beta)\|^2
\]
at \(\beta=z/\|z\|\) is exactly
\[
-\frac{4296673828163490883}{12426676661571147259375}<0. \tag{30}
\]
All frame entries, minors, and the sign of (30) are checked by rational arithmetic. This is **not** a counterexample to the capped determinant conjecture: the displayed two-form is explicitly nondecomposable and is not the volume form of any two-plane. Ordinary matrix positivity therefore loses an essential Pluecker restriction.
