# X014 — a chromatic entropy series and independent leaves on an arbitrary tree core

Status: COMPLETE bounded proof. The full-domain theorem below is proved. General tree-supported affine concavity, and the general Lyons conjecture, remain unresolved.

## 1. Main theorem and exact new scope

Let T0 be any finite tree (a forest is also allowed), on a measured coordinate set V0 of arbitrary size. Fix a Hermitian matrix B0 with zero diagonal and off-diagonal support contained in T0. At each v in V0 attach any number of new pendant vertices. Give all measured coordinates diagonal 1/2. The core entries B0 stay fixed, while every individual complex pendant entry z_e varies independently:

    K(z)=I/2+B(z),
    B(z)|_(V0 x V0)=B0,
    B(z)_(v,leaf e)=z_e,  B(z)_(leaf e,v)=conj(z_e).

There are no other edges. All entropies below are measured occupation entropies with natural logarithms.

**Theorem 1.** On the entire closed convex contraction domain 0<=K(z)<=I,

    z -> H(P_K(z)) + 8 sum_(pendant e) |z_e|^4

is concave. In particular, for feasible endpoints z0,z1 and zt=(1-t)z0+t z1,

    H(P_K(zt))-(1-t)H(P_K(z0))-t H(P_K(z1))
      >= 8 sum_e [(1-t)|z0_e|^4+t|z1_e|^4-|zt_e|^4] >=0.       (1)

The first entropy inequality is strict whenever z0!=z1 and 0<t<1. Different pendant magnitudes may have arbitrary opposite radial slopes; every complex phase may also move independently. The result has no small-coupling restriction and includes feasible boundary endpoints.

At a strict base, with arbitrary complex pendant direction h, (1) gives the local bound

    -H''(K(z))[E(h),E(h)]
      >=64 sum_e (Re(conj(z_e) h_e))^2
        +32 sum_e |z_e|^2 |h_e|^2.                              (2)

**Scope crosswalk.** C059 and C062 already prove independent singleton/satellite amplitudes around a freely varying measured two-coordinate core; they include arbitrary double stars, independent complex leaf edges, and a moving central bridge. C026 and C073 also cover smaller relevant subfamilies. Those domains are not claimed as new here. The new scope in this report is an arbitrary fixed balanced tree core of three or more measured coordinates, with all pendant amplitudes independent, including mixed signs at arbitrarily many distinct attachment sites. The example in Section 8 has a four-coordinate core and direction inertia (4,4). C021's prior aligned replacement slopes do not cover its mixed radial slopes. No historical novelty outside the supplied research record is asserted.

The electrical-network assignment led to a series elimination argument for the normalized adjacency operator. The decisive entropy input is an explicit chromatic coefficient identity, proved directly below; no transfer-current or projection-substitution claim is used.

## 2. Exact tree likelihood in independent edge signs

For any balanced tree kernel K=I/2+B, define

    w_e=4|B_e|^2,  C(w)_(uv)=sqrt(w_uv) on edges,  C(w)_vv=0.

A coordinate diagonal unitary gauge makes all nonzero tree entries of 2B nonnegative. Hence strict contraction is exactly

    rho(C(w))<1.                                                (3)

This uses only gauge invariance of measured principal minors; no arbitrary change of measurement basis occurs. Since a tree is bipartite, its centered spectrum is symmetric about zero.

For occupation spins s_v=2X_v-1, the centered determinantal moment identity is

    E prod_(v in U) s_v = det(2B_U).                             (4)

It follows by expanding both sides into inclusion moments det K_W. A nonzero term of a zero-diagonal forest determinant is a collection of edge transpositions, that is, a perfect matching. Define edge signs

    t_e=-s_u s_v.

After choosing one root in each component, the map from vertex spins to root spins plus edge signs is bijective and preserves the uniform reference measure. The root spins never appear in the likelihood. Fourier inversion of (4) therefore gives

    p(s)=2^(-|V|) q_T(t;w),
    q_T(t;w)=sum_(M matching of T) prod_(e in M) w_e t_e,
    F_T(w):=|V| log 2-H(P_K)=E_t q_T log q_T.                    (5)

All edge signs in the last expectation are independent uniform signs. Strict contraction makes every likelihood positive. In particular,

    partial_e q=t_e q_(T-endpoints(e)),
    partial_e partial_f q=0 if e,f share an endpoint;

for disjoint e,f the second derivative is t_e t_f times the complementary matching polynomial. These identities explain why an arbitrary affine tree direction has a real second acceleration, even though individual squared weights enter q multiaffinely.

## 3. Positive connected entropy expansion, with exact coefficients

Write Z_T(x)=sum_M prod_(e in M) x_e. For a nonnegative multiindex alpha on E(T), form a graph G_alpha as follows: start with the line graph L(T), replace its edge-vertex e by alpha_e labeled copies forming a clique, and completely join the copies of two types when their original tree edges meet. Types with alpha_e=0 are omitted. Let chi_alpha(r) be the chromatic polynomial of this graph.

**Theorem 2 (chromatic entropy coefficients).** Throughout the full strict physical region (3),

    F_T(w)=sum_(alpha nonzero and even) c_alpha w^alpha,
    c_alpha=chi_alpha'(1)/prod_e alpha_e!.                      (6)

Here even means every alpha_e is even. The coefficient vanishes when the supported tree edges have at least two components. It is strictly positive when they form a connected subtree. Thus (6) is a convergent sum with nonnegative coefficients, supported exactly on connected edge sets. It and all derivatives converge locally absolutely in the strict physical region.

### 3.1 Coefficient identity

For an integer r>=0, Z_T(x)^r enumerates r ordered independent sets of L(T). Its coefficient of x^alpha assigns alpha_e distinct colors to each type e, forbidding shared colors between adjacent types. Labeling the copies multiplies this coefficient by prod_e alpha_e!. Consequently

    [x^alpha] Z_T(x)^r = chi_alpha(r)/prod_e alpha_e!.           (7)

Both sides are polynomials in r. For the left side this follows from the finite degree-relevant part of the binomial expansion of (1+(Z_T-1))^r. A direct inclusion-exclusion count over monochromatic graph edges proves the usual polynomial expression for chi_alpha, so no chromatic-polynomial theorem is being imported. Identity (7) for all integer r therefore holds polynomially. Differentiating at r=1 gives

    [x^alpha] Z_T(x) log Z_T(x)
       =chi_alpha'(1)/prod_e alpha_e!.

Averaging the independent signs in (5) retains exactly even alpha, proving (6) initially near zero.

### 3.2 Positivity by leaf elimination

Every supported type of a nonzero even alpha has alpha_e>=2. If the supported edge set has several connected components, G_alpha is disconnected into the same number of nontrivial components. Each component's chromatic polynomial vanishes at r=1, so the product has derivative zero there.

Suppose instead that the support is a connected subtree. Delete a leaf edge e. The remaining supported edges adjacent to e all meet at its other endpoint, and their copies form a clique of size

    a_e=sum_(remaining f adjacent to e) alpha_f >=2.

For every proper coloring of the remaining graph, the alpha_e deleted copies have exactly (r-a_e)_(alpha_e) possible ordered color choices, where (x)_k=x(x-1)...(x-k+1). Hence each leaf-edge deletion contributes this falling-factorial factor. Continue until one final edge e* remains, with factor (r)_(alpha_e*).

At r=1 the final factor has one simple zero and derivative

    [(r)_(alpha_e*)]'_(r=1)=(alpha_e*-2)! >0,

because alpha_e* is even. Every removed factor has positive value

    (1-a_e)_(alpha_e)=(a_e+alpha_e-2)!/(a_e-2)! >0.

This proves the explicit coefficient formula

    c_alpha = [(alpha_e*-2)! / prod_e alpha_e!]
              prod_(deleted e) [(a_e+alpha_e-2)!/(a_e-2)!] >0.  (8)

The value is independent of the chosen elimination order because it equals (7). A single supported edge with exponent 2k has coefficient 1/[2k(2k-1)], the ordinary binary entropy-deficit coefficient. Separated edges have zero connected coefficient.

### 3.3 Convergence throughout the physical region

This step is needed: formal coefficient positivity alone does not justify a theorem at large coupling.

Choose any positive vector R with R_e>w_e and rho(C(R))<1; it exists by strictness and continuity, including when some w_e=0. For complex x with |x_e|<R_e choose a square root of each x_e and build an oriented skew-symmetric tree matrix J(x), with entries sqrt(x_e) and -sqrt(x_e) on opposite orientations. The tree determinant expansion gives

    Z_T(x)=det(I+J(x)).

Entrywise |J(x)|<=C(R). The Neumann series is absolutely controlled by sum_k C(R)^k, so I+J(x) is invertible and Z_T(x)!=0 throughout this polydisc. The argument requires no analytic choice of the square roots: the determinant is the polynomial Z_T.

A nonvanishing holomorphic function on a polydisc with Z_T(0)=1 has the unique normalized holomorphic logarithm there. Equivalently, it follows by analytic continuation of the locally convergent logarithm with zero winding on the simply connected polydisc. Thus Z_T log Z_T is holomorphic in a polydisc strictly containing the point w. Its Taylor series and all derivatives converge absolutely on smaller compact polydiscs. Taking the finite edge-sign average proves the full-domain convergence claim in Theorem 2.

## 4. Exact pendant elimination and its affine strength domain

Return to Theorem 1. Make the fixed tree core entries of 2B0 real nonnegative by a fixed gauge, and denote that core adjacency matrix by C0. For each pendant edge put

    a_e=4|z_e|^2,
    S_v=sum_(pendant e attached to v) a_e,
    Q_v=1+sum_(pendant e attached to v) a_e t_e.

Absent pendant groups have S_v=0 and Q_v=1. Eliminating the pendant identity block in I-C_full gives the exact strength feasibility condition

    D(a):=diag_v(1-S_v)-C0 >=0.                                 (9)

Strict contraction corresponds to D(a)>0. Thus the strength domain is convex and downward closed in all a_e>=0. In the strict domain S_v<1 and Q_v>=1-S_v>0 for every pendant sign assignment.

The matching polynomial has the exact factorization

    q_T(t;w,a)=prod_v Q_v times
       q_T0(t_core; (w_uv/(Q_u Q_v))_(uv in E(T0))).             (10)

Indeed a core matching forbids pendant edges at its matched endpoints; each unmatched core vertex independently contributes either no pendant edge or one of its pendant edges, giving Q_v. Factoring prod Q_v then gives (10).

The effective core weights in (10) are physical for every pendant sign assignment. From (9),

    diag(Q)-C0 >= diag(1-S)-C0 >0.

Congruencing by diag(Q)^(-1/2) gives I-C_eff>0, where C_eff has edge values sqrt(w_uv/(Q_u Q_v)). Because C_eff is a nonnegative symmetric adjacency matrix, its spectral radius is its largest eigenvalue, which is below one. This proves exactly the hypothesis needed for Theorem 2 at every effective core, rather than extrapolating its series outside its region of convergence.

Average q log q first over core signs in (10). Since E_core q_T0=1 and independent pendant groups satisfy E Q_v=1,

    F_T(w,a)=sum_v F_star(a_v)
       + E_pendant [prod_v Q_v times F_T0(w_uv/(Q_u Q_v))],     (11)

where

    F_star(a_v)=E Q_v log Q_v.

This is an exact entropy decomposition with no discarded conditional-entropy term.

## 5. A positive series of jointly convex pendant potentials

Apply Theorem 2 to the effective core in (11). For a nonzero even core multiindex alpha, define

    d_v(alpha)=sum_(core e incident to v) alpha_e.

If v meets its support then d_v>=2. If it does not, its remaining factor Q_v has expectation one. Thus (11) becomes

    F_T(w,a)=sum_v F_star(a_v)
       +sum_alpha c_alpha w_core^alpha
          prod_(v meeting support alpha) A_(d_v(alpha)-1)(a_v), (12)

where

    A_m(a)=E_t (1+sum_j a_j t_j)^(-m),  m>=1.                   (13)

The finite pendant average and the nonnegative convergent series may be interchanged directly. Every series is evaluated at a physical effective core by Section 4.

For nonnegative a with sum a_j<1, A_m is jointly log-convex. For each sign assignment, -m log(1+a dot t) is convex; exponentiating, summing, and applying Holder's inequality proves log-convexity of the finite sum (13). It is also coordinatewise nondecreasing. Pairing the sign t_i while freezing all the others, its i-th derivative is

    (m/2)[(c-a_i)^(-m-1)-(c+a_i)^(-m-1)] >=0,
    c=1+sum_(j!=i) a_j t_j >a_i.

The factors in each term of (12) involve disjoint pendant groups. Therefore each positive term is jointly log-convex, hence convex, in all pendant squared weights and is coordinatewise nondecreasing. Partial sums have both properties; their finite pointwise limit on the convex domain (9) does as well. This proves that the second term of (12) is jointly convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing, including all mixed-sign directions in the weights.

For the star term its Hessian is

    Hess F_star(a)=E [t t^T/(1+a dot t)]
       =E [t t^T/(1-(a dot t)^2)] >= E t t^T=I.                (14)

The second equality pairs t with -t. Moreover F_star is even in each a_i. Hence

    R_star(a):=F_star(a)-(1/2)sum_i a_i^2

is convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing on the nonnegative strength simplex: its i-th derivative is zero at a_i=0 and its second i-th derivative is nonnegative.

Combining (12)-(14) yields the stronger strength statement

    G(a):=F_T(w,a)-(1/2)sum_(pendant e) a_e^2

is jointly convex and coordinatewise nondecreasing on its full strength domain. This is the exact positive compensation underlying Theorem 1.

## 6. Return to affine complex kernel entries

For two feasible complex pendant arrays, write

    abar=(1-t)a(z0)+t a(z1),
    a(zt)=abar-4t(1-t)(|z1_e-z0_e|^2)_e <=abar.                (15)

The affine LMI (9) makes abar feasible, and downward closure makes a(zt) feasible. Convexity and monotonicity from Section 5 imply

    G(a(zt))<=G(abar)<=(1-t)G(a(z0))+tG(a(z1)).

Since (1/2)sum a_e^2=8 sum |z_e|^4, this is exactly (1). Strict convexity of |z|^4 on the complex plane proves the strictness claim. Differentiating that quartic gives (2), retaining both dot a=8 Re(conj(z)h) and ddot a=8|h|^2.

For boundary endpoints, replace K by I/2+r(K-I/2), 0<r<1. For each r this is a strict family with fixed scaled core and scaled pendant entries rz. Apply (1) and let r increase to one. Finite determinantal probabilities and x log x are continuous at zero, while the quartic bound scales by r^4. Thus the complete closed-domain claim follows.

## 7. Why the positive tree series does not prove all tree directions

Formula (6) has nonnegative coefficients, but a positive mixed monomial such as a^2 b^2 need not be jointly convex. It proves convexity in logarithmic edge coordinates, not automatically in all affine edge entries. The leaf elimination (10) resums moving pendant variables into the log-convex functions A_m; that additional step is essential.

If a core edge moves, the coefficient w_core^alpha in (12) also moves. Products involving its powers and the A_m factors do not inherit joint convexity merely from the argument above. A proof for arbitrary moving internal edges would need a further compensation inequality. Likewise, varying diagonal entries destroys the balanced independent-edge-sign reduction used in (5). No unrestricted internal-edge or diagonal Hessian theorem is asserted.

The original thought that a fixed-bridge double-star theorem itself was new was corrected after comparison with C059/C062. It is now only the two-core special case of the construction; Theorem 1's distinguishing domain has arbitrarily large fixed tree cores.

## 8. A strict rank-eight affine example and reproducibility

Take a four-vertex path core 0-1-2-3 with kernel off-diagonal entries

    (K_01,K_12,K_23)=(3/10,1/5,3/10).

Attach one pendant leaf to every core vertex and take the respective endpoint arrays

    z0=(1/5,3/20,1/6,1/8),
    z1=(1/8,1/5,3/20,1/6).

All diagonals are 1/2. Every pendant strength is at most 4/25, so diag(1-S)>=(21/25)I. The normalized core has edge entries (3/5,2/5,3/5) and spectral radius

    rho(C0)=(1+sqrt(10))/5 <21/25.

The last inequality is exact: the positive root of lambda^2-(2/5)lambda-9/25 is below 21/25 since its polynomial there is 6/625>0. Hence all endpoint kernels and their full chord are strict. The four nonzero pendant slopes have mixed signs and form four disjoint moving edges; the direction has inertia (4,4). No two measured core coordinates cover those moving edges, so this affine family is outside the structural hypothesis of C059/C062.
