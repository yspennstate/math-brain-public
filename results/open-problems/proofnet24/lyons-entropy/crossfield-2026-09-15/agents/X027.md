## 1. Exact setting

Let `0<K<I` be a connected Hermitian three-site kernel. Use the frozen Boolean-log coefficients of X015:

    M=diag(c_1,c_2,c_3)+tau K>0,   delta=det M,
    H''_K(A)=-I_K(A)+2 tr(M adj A).

Here `I` is the full atom Fisher information. Write `D` for the sum of the three marginal Fisher terms and `E` for the sum of the six actual conditional empty/full Fisher contributions defined in X021. In this report `Z` denotes a real diagonal phase generator, avoiding a collision with the scalar `D_K(A)`.

The proposed sufficient inequality is

    T_K(A):=D_K(A)+E_K(A)-6 tr(M adj A)>=0.          (1)

X015 gives, with `sigma=9-7 log(7/2)>0`,

    N>=3.5 M+sigma I,
    E_K(B)>=2 tr(N adj B)   for every Hermitian B.   (2)

The second inequality follows directly from X021's endpoint identity with its nonnegative remainder. These are statements at the actual affine-kernel velocities; conditional denominators remain variable.

Define the positive real inner product

    g_K(A,B)=Re tr(M^-1 A M^-1 B),

and the two-dimensional gauge plane

    V_K={U_Z=i[Z,K]: Z real diagonal}.

Connectedness makes the kernel of `Z -> U_Z` precisely the scalar diagonals. Put `U_1=i[E11,K]`, `U_2=i[E22,K]`, `Gij=g_K(U_i,U_j)`, and `v_i=g_K(A,U_i)`. Then `G>0` and the following decomposition is explicit:

    alpha=G^-1 v,
    A_v=sum_i alpha_i U_i,   A_h=A-A_v.             (3)

The subscripts indicate the vertical and horizontal parts for this particular metric.

## 2. The exact completion theorem

**Theorem 1.** For every Hermitian `A`, decomposition (3) satisfies

    H''_K(A)=H''_K(A_h)-delta g_K(A_v,A_v),          (4)
    T_K(A)=T_K(A_h)+3delta g_K(A_v,A_v).             (5)

Both identities retain the complete ambient acceleration. Consequently (1) for every direction is equivalent to (1) on the seven-dimensional horizontal space `V_K^perp`.

**Proof.** Diagonal-unitary conjugation preserves every principal minor and therefore every atom. Differentiating at the identity gives `Dp_S[U_Z]=0` for every atom. In particular

    I_K(A+U)=I_K(A),   D_K(A+U)=D_K(A),
    E_K(A+U)=E_K(A)                   for U in V_K. (6)

For the last equality, conditional atom derivatives are determined by joint atom derivatives and pivot derivatives. Both are unchanged; all conditional endpoint scores are unchanged as well.

Also `tr(M^-1 U_Z)=0`. If `tau!=0`, use `tau U_Z=i[Z,M]` and cyclicity of trace. If `tau=0`, `M` is diagonal and `U_Z` has zero diagonal. The dimension-three determinant identity gives

    2 tr(M adj A)
      =delta[(tr(M^-1 A))^2-g_K(A,A)].             (7)

The trace in (7) is unchanged by vertical shifts, while (3) gives `g_K(A,A)=g_K(A_h,A_h)+g_K(A_v,A_v)`. Equations (6)–(7) prove (4)–(5). No comparison of entropy values is differentiated to infer a Hessian sign. ∎

This is an exact quotient by phase tangents at the original base. It does not change the cycle phase or assert C014's false canonical-phase maximum principle. The underlying score-null negative square is C077's result; the new content here is its completion with the X021 endpoint certificate and the explicit cone below.

## 3. A rigorously enlarged indefinite cone

**Theorem 2.** If `A_h` in (3) is positive or negative semidefinite, then

    T_K(A)>=D_K(A_h)+tr(M adj A_h)
             +2sigma tr(adj A_h)+3delta g_K(A_v,A_v)>=0.  (8)

Moreover,

    H''_K(A)<=-I_K(A)/7-2D_K(A)/7
              -(4sigma/7)tr(adj A_h)
              -delta g_K(A_v,A_v)<0                 (9)

for every nonzero such `A`.

**Proof.** Semidefiniteness in dimension three implies `adj A_h>=0`. Pair (2) with this adjugate and subtract `6tr(M adj A_h)` to obtain the first three terms of (8). They are nonnegative. Then use (5). X015's strict semidefinite bound applied to `A_h`, followed by (4) and (6), gives (9). If `A_h!=0`, a semidefinite matrix has a nonzero diagonal and hence `D_K(A_h)>0`; otherwise nonzero `A` has `A_v!=0`. ∎

A convenient version needs no projection. If `B` is semidefinite, `Z` is real diagonal, and

    [B,Z]=0,    A=B+i[Z,K],                         (10)

then the same bounds hold with `A_h` replaced by `B` and `A_v` by `i[Z,K]`. Indeed `g_K(B,i[Z,K])=0`: for `tau!=0` it equals `(i/tau)tr([Z,B]M^-1)`; for `tau=0` the diagonal metric makes the assertion immediate entry by entry. Thus the proof uses just the orthogonal pair, whether or not `B` is horizontal to every gauge tangent.

This includes a diagonal semidefinite velocity plus arbitrary phase rotations. When `Z` has a repeated diagonal value, `B` may also contain an arbitrary semidefinite coherent two-site block within that repeated eigenspace. The phase tangent can be arbitrarily large, so this is not merely a continuity neighborhood of semidefinite directions.

For an exact full-rank indefinite instance take

    K=[[1/5,1/10,i/20],[1/10,3/5,1/10],[-i/20,1/10,4/5]],
    B=I,   Z=diag(0,10,20),
    A=B+i[Z,K]=[[1,-i,1],[i,1,-i],[1,i,1]].        (11)

The leading principal minors of `K` are `(1/5,11/100,169/2000)` and those of `I-K` are `(4/5,31/100,53/1000)`. Thus the base is strict and connected. The eigenvalues of `A` are exactly `(-1,2,2)`. It satisfies the six-endpoint inequality and has strictly negative entropy curvature by (10). This base has unequal measured diagonals and is outside the balanced particle-hole locus.

## 4. Why the completion is not a phase-randomization channel

**Proposition 3.** The projection `A -> A_h` need not preserve positive semidefiniteness, even when `M` is scalar.

Take the exact balanced base

    K0=I/2+(i/4)[[0,1,-1],[-1,0,1],[1,-1,0]].      (12)

Its empty/full atom masses are `1/32`, its other masses are `5/32`, and `M=(log 5)I`. On real symmetric edge coordinates `(A12,A23,A31)`, the gauge plane is the plane with coordinate sum zero. For the rank-one positive matrix `P=vv^T`, `v=(1,1,0)^T`, its orthogonal horizontal projection is therefore

    P_h=[[1,1/3,1/3],[1/3,1,1/3],[1/3,1/3,0]].    (13)

The principal minor on coordinates `{1,3}` is `-1/9`; hence `P_h` is indefinite. Its determinant is `-4/27`.

Every map obtained by averaging unitary conjugations is positive, as is every positive classical mixture of such channels. It therefore cannot equal this horizontal projection. The obstruction already appears at a base whose full entropy Hessian is known to be negative; it obstructs this proof mechanism, not concavity.

There is a separate rigidity for a diagonal phase average `T(A)=E[U_theta A U_theta*]` at a fixed connected base. Suppose it preserves **all atom derivatives for every A**. Write `(TA)ij=z_ij Aij`, where `z_ij=E exp(i(theta_i-theta_j))`. Pair-inclusion derivatives imply

    Re(conj(Kij)(z_ij-1)Aij)=0 for every complex Aij.

Thus `z_ij=1` on every nonzero edge of `K`. Equality in `|E exp(i(theta_i-theta_j))|<=1` forces each corresponding phase difference to vanish almost surely. A spanning tree then makes every `U_theta` scalar almost surely, and `T` is the identity. Hence a nontrivial phase-average proof must change the score geometry and quantify that change.

## 5. The endpoint terms do admit positive coherent compressions

The failed positivity of horizontal completion should be distinguished from an exact positive representation of the endpoint frame. At pivot `k`, place that coordinate first and use the block notation of X015: `a=Kkk`, `v=K_Rk`, `d=Akk`, `w=A_Rk`, `F=A_RR`. For branch `s`, put `r=a-(1-s)`, `pi=a` or `1-a`, and

    V=[-v*/r; I_2],
    J=K_RR-vv*/r,
    E_s=F-(wv*+vw*)/r+d vv*/r^2=V* A V.            (14)

For either `F_s=J` or `F_s=I-J`, define

    Q_ks,F=sqrt(pi/det F_s) V adj(F_s) V* >=0.      (15)

Each matrix has rank two, and the complete conditional endpoint contribution is exactly

    E_K(A)=sum_(k,s,F) [tr(Q_ks,F A)]^2.            (16)

Thus `D+E` is a sum of squares of positive-matrix linear measurements. This representation includes the changing-pivot derivative in (14). It does not itself imply (1): a positive measurement frame does not make its inverse or the horizontal projection positive.
