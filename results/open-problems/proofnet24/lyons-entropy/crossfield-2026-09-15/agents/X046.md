## 1. The new concavity domains

Let `V:C^2 -> C^n` be any complex isometry with equal row norms
`||v_i||^2=2/n`, and put `F(M)=H(P_{I_n/2+VMV*})`.

**Theorem.** For every `n>=2`, `F` is concave on `0<=M<=(2/5)I_2`.
At every base in this domain and every definite Hermitian direction `E`,

    -F''_M[E,E] >= 16 det(E)/(n+32).                              (1)

For every `n>=64`, `F` is also concave on the larger signed domain
`-(2/5)I_2<=M<=(2/5)I_2`. All its physical kernels have spectrum in
`[1/10,9/10]`. No commutativity between `M` and `E`, real-frame condition,
or measured-coordinate block decomposition is assumed. General leverage
and radius criteria below extend both displayed domains.

These are full four-real-dimensional active domains. Their definite
directions supplement X040's theorem for every indefinite active direction
over any diagonal background. The new ingredient is a finite volume-design
allocation of Fisher information, followed by uniform conditional log bounds.

## 2. Exact full-occupation curvature

For the general model `K=D+BMB*`, `B` of rank two, assume strict diagonal
`D` and strict physical `K`. Set `q_z=prod d_i^z_i(1-d_i)^(1-z_i)`,
`eta_i=z_i/d_i-(1-z_i)/(1-d_i)`, and `T_z=B*diag(eta_i)B`.
The determinant lemma and the two-by-two determinant identity give

    p_z=q_z det(I+MT_z),
    p'_z=q_z[tr(E T_z)+tr(adj(M)E)det T_z],
    p''_z=2 det(E) q_z det T_z.                                  (2)

Write `c_ij=|det B_{ij}|^2`, and let `C` be the real symmetric matrix with
off-diagonal entries `c_ij` and zero diagonal. Cauchy–Binet gives
`det T_z=sum_(i<j)c_ij eta_i eta_j`. For a fixed outside occupation `R`,

    ell_ij(R)=log[p_(R+i)p_(R+j)/(p_R p_(R+i+j))] >=0,
    J=sum_(i<j)c_ij E_(q,-ij)[ell_ij(R)].                         (3)

Indeed, writing `L=K(I-K)^(-1)>0`, the four atom determinants reduce by
Schur complementation of `L_R` to `a,b,ab-|z|^2`, so the ratio is
`ab/(ab-|z|^2)>=1`. Bernoulli integration by parts is exact:
`E_q eta_i eta_j f=E_(q,-ij)[f_11-f_10-f_01+f_00]`.
Consequently `E_q det T log p=-J`, and the complete Shannon Hessian is

    -F''_M[E,E]=FI_M(E)-2 det(E)J,
    FI_M(E)=sum_z (p'_z)^2/p_z.                                 (4)

This reproduces X040's proved finite identity, including every `p''` term.
It settles `det E<=0`; all subsequent allocation concerns `det E>0`.

## 3. An optimal linear determinant-design certificate

Let `Sigma` be the actual occupation covariance:
`Sigma_ii=K_ii(1-K_ii)`, `Sigma_ij=-|K_ij|^2`. Strict atom support implies
`Sigma>0`. Define the computable generalized design eigenvalue

    Lambda=lambda_max(Sigma^(-1/2) C Sigma^(-1/2)).               (5)

**Fisher allocation theorem.** For every definite `E`,

    FI_M(E) >= 2 det(E) Lambda.                                 (6)

Hence `J<=Lambda` pays all the logarithmic acceleration in (4).

**Proof.** For nonnegative experimental weights `w_i`, put
`G_w=B*diag(w)B>=0`. The full atom score `u=p'/p` satisfies
`Cov(u,sum w_i X_i)=tr(E G_w)`. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
`FI_M(E) w^T Sigma w >=[tr(E G_w)]^2`.
Replacing `E` by `-E` if necessary makes it positive. The elementary
two-eigenvalue inequality `(tr A)^2>=4det A` applied to
`A=E^(1/2)G_w E^(1/2)` and Cauchy–Binet now yield

    FI_M(E) >= 4 det(E) det(G_w)/(w^T Sigma w)
            = 2 det(E) (w^T C w)/(w^T Sigma w).                   (7)

The largest generalized Rayleigh quotient is positive. It is attained by
a nonnegative vector: replacing any maximizing real `w` by `|w|` increases
the numerator since `C_ij>=0`, and decreases the denominator since
`Sigma_ij<=0` for `i!=j`. Thus its value is exactly (5), proving (6).
No experimental-design equivalence theorem is imported. This is the
optimal bound from (7); it is not asserted to equal the full Fisher form.

For `B=V`, the pair weights form a probability distribution:
`sum_(i<j)c_ij=1`, and their inclusion weights are
`sum_(j!=i)c_ij=||v_i||^2`. These are exact determinant-sampling identities.
At `D=I/2`, even the simple choice `w=1` gives

    Var(|X|)=tr K(I-K)=n/4-tr M^2 <=n/4,
    Lambda >=2/Var(|X|)>=8/n,
    FI_M(E)>=(tr E)^2/Var(|X|)>=16 det(E)/n.                     (8)

## 4. Conditional determinant bounds from row leverage

Fix `0<m<1/2`, set `b=2m/(1/2-m)`, and write
`tau=max_i ||v_i||^2`. The physical odds matrix is exactly

    L=I+V N V*,  N=2M( I/2-M)^(-1).                            (9)

For each occupied outside set `R`, let `U=V_R` and

    N_R=N-N U*(I+U N U*)^(-1)U N.                              (10)

The two-coordinate Schur complement has entries
`S_ii=1+v_i* N_R v_i`, `S_ij=v_i* N_R v_j`.
If `0<=M<=mI`, then `0<=N_R<=N<=bI`: factoring `N^(1/2)` in (10)
identifies the middle factor as `(I+N^(1/2)U*U N^(1/2))^(-1)`.
Put `u_i=v_i* N_R v_i<=b||v_i||^2`. Matrix Cauchy–Schwarz gives
`|S_ij|^2<=u_i u_j`, and hence

    ell_ij(R)<=log[(1+u_i)(1+u_j)/(1+u_i+u_j)]
              <=log[(1+b tau)^2/(1+2b tau)]
              <=b^2 tau^2/(1+2b tau)=:j_+.                     (11)

The middle expression increases in each `u_i>=0`; the last step uses
`log(1+x)<=x`. Thus the determinant-volume average satisfies `J<=j_+`.

For the signed domain `-mI<=M<=mI`, put `a=2m/(1/2+m)<1`.
Then `-aI<=N<=bI` and **every** update in (10) satisfies `-bI<=N_R<=bI`.
Here is a proof covering indefinite and singular `N`. Its quadratic form is

    x* N_R x=min_y {||y||^2+(x+U*y)*N(x+U*y)}.                  (12)

Choosing `y=0` gives the upper bound. Substituting `N>=-aI` into the
minimum gives `N_R>=-a(I-aU*U)^(-1)>=-a/(1-a)I=-bI`, since
`U*U<=I`. The quadratic coefficient in `y` is positive definite, so no
unjustified inverse of `N` has been used.
If `2b tau<1`, it follows that `S_ii>=1-b tau` and `|S_ij|<=b tau`.
The exact face ratio and `-log(1-r)<=r/(1-r)` imply

    ell_ij(R)<=log[(1-b tau)^2/(1-2b tau)]
              <=b^2 tau^2/(1-2b tau)=:j_-.                     (13)

Again `J<=j_-`, since the pair-volume weights sum to one. These bounds
are pointwise for every outside occupation; no conditional law is frozen
or replaced. Either bound can be combined with the optimized (5).

## 5. Proof of the domains and explicit scope

For equal row norms `tau=2/n`, (11) and (13) become

    J<=4b^2/[n(n+4b)]   if 0<=M<=mI,
    J<=4b^2/[n(n-4b)]   if -mI<=M<=mI and n>4b.                (14)

Combining these with (4),(8) proves the full Hessian sign, respectively,
whenever `n>=b^2/2-4b` or `n>=b^2/2+4b`. At `m=2/5`, `b=8`:
the first condition holds for all `n>=2`, with surplus (1); the second
holds for `n>=64`. The sharper logarithm in (13) gives, at `n=64`,
the strictly positive definite-direction surplus
`[1/4-2log(9/8)]det E>0`, since `log(9/8)<1/8`.
For `det E<=0`, (4) already has nonnegative right side. Integrating this
local Hessian sign on each segment proves concavity on the stated convex
domains. All physical kernels there are strict, even at active boundaries.

These domains permit order-one coherent spectral perturbations. They need
not satisfy X005's explicit odds-correlation condition. At `n=64`,
`M=(2/5)I` and **any** equal-norm frame, `L=I+8VV*`, so the odds
correlation determinant is `det R=81/(5/4)^64`. Thus
`-logdet R=64log(5/4)-2log9>64/5-6>2`, using `log(1+x)>=x/(1+x)`
and `log9<3`. This exceeds X005's rank-two sufficient threshold `2`.
No assertion is made about its stronger optimized modular-spread test.

The four-site complex frame with rows `(1,1),(1,-1),(1,i),(1,-i)` divided
by `2` illustrates the positive theorem. The active base
`M=[[3/8,i/40],[-i/40,1/3]]` lies strictly between `0` and `(2/5)I`;
`E=[[1,(1+i)/3],[(1-i)/3,2]]` is positive and does not commute with it.
Neither a scalar active base nor a changing-basis diagonalization is used.
