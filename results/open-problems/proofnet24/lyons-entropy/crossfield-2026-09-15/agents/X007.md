# X007 — Quantum recovery maps and the occupation-entropy defect

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: COMPLETE bounded proof attempt; no general concavity claim. The opening derivation records the initial reduction, followed by the terminal theorems below.

Let `rho_K` be the gauge-invariant quasifree state with covariance `0<K<I`, `p_K=Delta rho_K`, and let `K_s=K+sA` with `A=A*`. Set `q=dot rho_0`, `r=ddot rho_0`, `a=Delta q`, `b=Delta r`, and `p=p_K`. Because `log rho_K` is a constant plus a number-preserving quadratic observable, affine covariance gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(r\log\rho_K)=0.
\]

The measured entropy curvature is exactly

\[
-H''(K;A)=J_{\mathrm{cl}}(a;p)+\operatorname{Tr}(b\log p),
\qquad J_{\mathrm{cl}}(a;p)=\sum_S a_S^2/p_S.
\tag{1}
\]

Let `J_q(q;rho)=Tr q Dlog(rho)[q]` be the Bogoliubov–Kubo–Mori metric. The second derivative of the relative-entropy data-processing loss

\[
\mathcal L(s)=D(\rho_{K_s}\Vert\rho_K)-D(p_{K_s}\Vert p_K)
\]

at zero is

\[
\mathcal L''(0)=J_q(q;\rho_K)-J_{\mathrm{cl}}(a;p)\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the target is the exact, additional inequality

\[
\mathcal L''(0)\le J_q(q;\rho_K)+\operatorname{Tr}(b\log p).
\tag{3}
\]

Ordinary data processing supplies the *opposite side* (a lower bound zero) on the loss, so recovery lower bounds alone do not immediately prove concavity. The task below is to find an identity or a delimited family making the second-order measurement acceleration explicit, rather than treating `rho_K` as affine in K.

At the finite level, for symmetric endpoints `rho_+`, `rho_-` about covariance K and `bar p=(p_++p_-)/2`, the exact identity is

\[
H(p)-\tfrac12[H(p_+)+H(p_-)]=\tfrac12\sum_{\pm}D(p_\pm\Vert p)
+\operatorname{Tr}[(\bar p-p)\log p].
\tag{4}
\]

For the quantum states the analogous acceleration correction vanishes, since the averaged covariance equals K. Any recovery proof must therefore control the classical term in (4). This is a genuine obstruction to importing a quantum concavity statement directly, not a disproof of the measured-entropy conjecture.

## 1. Exact Petz–Slater ensemble and all compound moments

Put `L=K(I-K)^{-1}`, `M=K(I-K)`, and let `E_S` embed the measured coordinate subspace. With the convention `Pi_empty=0`, define

\[
\Pi_S=L^{1/2}E_S(L_S)^{-1}E_S^*L^{1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the orthogonal projection onto `L^{1/2} span{e_i:i in S}`. On fermionic Fock space, `rho_K=det(I-K) Gamma(L)` and its square root is `det(I-K)^{1/2} Gamma(L^{1/2})`. Consequently the occupation-dephasing Petz map is

\[
\mathcal R_K(x)=\rho_K^{1/2}\operatorname{diag}(x_S/p_S)\rho_K^{1/2}
=\sum_S x_S|\psi_S\rangle\langle\psi_S|,
\quad |\psi_S\rangle=\rho_K^{1/2}|S\rangle/\sqrt{p_S},
\tag{6}
\]

where `psi_S` is the unit Slater vector with one-body projection `Pi_S`. In particular `R_K(p)=rho_K`. Its complete reduced-density-matrix content is

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_p\,\wedge^r\Pi_S=\wedge^r K\quad(0\le r\le n).}
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, the r-body reduced density matrix of a pure Slater state is `wedge^r Pi_S`, and that of `rho_K` is `wedge^r K`; this can also be checked directly by expanding the compound minors of `Gamma(L^{1/2})` and using Cauchy–Binet.

The resolvent in X003 has the exact centered-projection form

\[
\boxed{G_S=M^{-1/2}(\Pi_S-K)M^{-1/2}.}
\tag{8}
\]

To prove (8), differentiate `log p_S=log det(I-K)+log det L_S`, use `dL=(I-K)^{-1}A(I-K)^{-1}`, and compare the resulting coefficient of arbitrary Hermitian A with `tr G_S A`. Thus, for `B=M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}` and `C_S=Pi_S-K`,

\[
a_S/p_S=\operatorname{tr}(C_SB),\qquad
b_S/p_S=2\operatorname{Tr}_{\wedge^2}(\wedge^2C_S\,\wedge^2B).
\tag{9}
\]

The second equality uses `2 Tr(wedge^2 X)=(tr X)^2-tr X^2` and multiplicativity of compound matrices.

The X005 worker independently obtained the projection (5) and its first moment; the new recovery content here is (6), all compound moments (7), and the non-Gaussian tangent representation developed next.

## 2. The missing curvature is a recovered non-Gaussian tangent

Let `f_S=log p_S-E_p log p`. Define the trace-zero recovered tangent

\[
\tau_f=\mathcal R_K(pf)=\rho_K^{1/2}\operatorname{diag}(f)\rho_K^{1/2},
\qquad V_f=\mathbb E_p[f_S\Pi_S].
\tag{10}
\]

Write `dot rho_K[V_f]` for the derivative of the quasifree state at affine covariance velocity `V_f`, and put

\[
\eta_f=\tau_f-\dot\rho_K[V_f].
\tag{11}
\]

The tangent `eta_f` has trace zero and zero one-body covariance. Its two-body reduced density matrix is exactly

\[
\boxed{(\eta_f)_2=\mathbb E_p[f_S\wedge^2(\Pi_S-K)].}
\tag{12}
\]

To check (12), expand `wedge^2(Pi_S-K)`. Its cross term is the polarized compound `Pi_S wedge K+K wedge Pi_S`, whose f-average is exactly the derivative of `wedge^2 K` in direction `V_f`; the constant term has zero f-average. Equations (1), (9), and (12) give

\[
\boxed{-H''(K;A)=\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
+2\operatorname{Tr}_{\wedge^2}[(\eta_f)_2\,\wedge^2 B].}
\tag{13}
\]

Unlike an affine-density simplification, this represents the complete acceleration through the two-body part of a specific recovered non-Gaussian tangent. Ordinary recovery controls its norm, but a sign for the pairing in (13) still requires proof.

## 3. A positive recovery-chain subtraction

This section and the next constitute the main proved recovery certificate. Set

\[
\Omega_\rho(Q)=\int_0^1\rho^uQ\rho^{1-u}\,du,
\quad \|X\|_\rho^2=\operatorname{Tr}X\Omega_\rho^{-1}(X),
\quad g_K(V,V)=\operatorname{tr}V\,D\log(K(I-K)^{-1})[V].
\tag{14}
\]

The quasifree tangent `dot rho_K[V]` has BKM norm squared `g_K(V,V)`. It is BKM-orthogonal to every trace-zero tangent with zero one-body covariance: its logarithmic derivative is a constant plus `dGamma(D logit_K[V])`, whose pairing with such a tangent vanishes. Therefore

\[
\|\tau_f\|_\rho^2=g_K(V_f,V_f)+\|\eta_f\|_\rho^2.
\tag{15}
\]

Define the stochastic Petz occupation chain, in column convention, by

\[
T_{S,T}=\frac{|(\rho^{1/2})_{S,T}|^2}{p_T}.
\tag{16}
\]

Column sums are one, `Tp=p`, and detailed balance holds because `rho^{1/2}` is Hermitian. On scalar score functions use `P=D_p^{-1}TD_p`. Its Dirichlet form is

\[
\mathcal E_T(f)=\langle f,(I-P)f\rangle_p
=\frac12\sum_{S,T}|(\rho^{1/2})_{S,T}|^2(f_S-f_T)^2\ge0.
\tag{17}
\]

Let `lambda_a` be the eigenvalues of `rho`, let `F=diag(f)` be expressed in that eigenbasis, and write `LM(x,y)=(x-y)/(log x-log y)`, continuously at equality. Direct spectral evaluation gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle f,Pf\rangle_p
&=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{1/2}F\rho^{1/2}F)
=\sum_{a,b}\sqrt{\lambda_a\lambda_b}|F_{ab}|^2,\\
\|\tau_f\|_\rho^2
&=\sum_{a,b}\frac{\lambda_a\lambda_b}{\operatorname{LM}(\lambda_a,\lambda_b)}|F_{ab}|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

Thus the additional modular remainder

\[
\mathcal R_{\rm mod}(f)=\sum_{a,b}\left[
\sqrt{\lambda_a\lambda_b}-
\frac{\lambda_a\lambda_b}{\operatorname{LM}(\lambda_a,\lambda_b)}
\right]|F_{ab}|^2\ge0
\tag{19}
\]

is nonnegative by `LM>=GM`. For example, with `x=(log lambda_a-log lambda_b)/2`, the bracket equals `sqrt(lambda_a lambda_b)[1-x/sinh x]`. Combining (15)–(19) proves the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\|\eta_f\|_\rho^2
=\operatorname{Var}_p(f)-g_K(V_f,V_f)
-\mathcal E_T(f)-\mathcal R_{\rm mod}(f).}
\tag{20}
\]

All four terms on the right are explicit, and the last two are positive subtractions. In particular the reversible chain does pay part of the recovered non-Gaussian remainder. Equation (20) is stronger than using only data processing of the preparation channel, which gives `||eta_f||^2<=Var f-g_K(V_f,V_f)`. It is still a norm identity rather than a sign for (13).

## 4. The rigorous curvature certificate

For a Hermitian operator `W` on `wedge^2 C^n`, write `dGamma_2(W)` for its two-body lift: in the ordered pair basis its entries multiply `c_i^* c_j^* c_l c_k`. Put

\[
Q_B=2d\Gamma_2(\wedge^2B),\qquad
h_B=2[(\operatorname{tr}KB)B-BKB],\qquad
m_B=(\operatorname{tr}KB)^2-\operatorname{tr}(KBKB),
\]
\[
Q_B^\perp=Q_B-d\Gamma(h_B)+m_BI,
\qquad \chi_K(B)=\operatorname{Tr}Q_B^\perp\Omega_\rho(Q_B^\perp).
\tag{21}
\]

The observable `Q_B^perp` has zero mean and is BKM-orthogonal to every quadratic observable. Indeed `E_rho Q_B=m_B`, whose covariance gradient is `h_B`; differentiating in an arbitrary natural quadratic direction proves the orthogonality, while `tr K h_B=2m_B` proves the mean-zero assertion. Since `eta_f` has zero trace and one-body covariance, (13) becomes

\[
-H''(K;A)=\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_fQ_B^\perp).
\tag{22}
\]

**Theorem 1 (Petz curvature certificate).** For every strict Hermitian positive contraction K and every Hermitian A, define the above objects with `f=log p-E log p`. Then

\[
\boxed{-H''(K;A)\ge\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
-\sqrt{\chi_K(B)\,[\operatorname{Var}f-g_K(V_f,V_f)
-\mathcal E_T(f)-\mathcal R_{\rm mod}(f)]}.}
\tag{23}
\]

Dropping either or both of the last positive subtractions yields weaker valid bounds. All radicands are nonnegative by (20). The right side uses no second atom derivatives and does not assume the desired entropy sign.

**Proof.** Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to `Tr eta_f Q_B^perp` in the dual BKM pairing:

\[
|\operatorname{Tr}(\eta_fQ_B^\perp)|^2
\le\|\eta_f\|_\rho^2\chi_K(B).
\]

Use (20) and (22). This proves (23) and all stated relaxations. No equality-case or approximate-recovery hypothesis is needed. Strictness ensures all density matrices and metrics are invertible. □

Consequently, if an affine segment `K_t=K_0+tA` stays strict and the right side of (23) is at least `gamma>=0` for every `0<=t<=1`, then

\[
H(K_t)\ge(1-t)H(K_0)+tH(K_1)+\tfrac\gamma2t(1-t).
\tag{24}
\]

This follows by integrating the certified second-derivative inequality. A positive certificate at one strict base also gives an open neighborhood of strict concavity in that fixed direction by continuity. These are rigorously delimited sufficient families, not a claim that the certificate always holds.

## 5. Modular invariance and an explicit noncommuting coefficient

The following simplifies implementation, but the broad idea of removing modular occupation terms and bounding the residual is already in prior worker C024, Theorem 2. Its residual variance and sharp measured-acceleration Cauchy bound are not claimed new here. The contribution is the Petz mechanism, the chain subtraction above, and the following explicit compound coefficient valid for noncommuting K and A.

For a centered modular function `f_D(S)=sum_{i in S}d_i-E sum_{i in S}d_i`, the recovered curve

\[
\frac{\rho^{1/2}e^{t d\Gamma(D)}\rho^{1/2}}
{\operatorname{Tr}(\rho e^{t d\Gamma(D)})}
\]

is exactly quasifree: its odds matrix is `L^{1/2}e^{tD}L^{1/2}`. Its derivative is `tau_{f_D}`. Therefore `eta_{f_D}=0`, and **eta_f is unchanged by adding any modular occupation function to f**.

Let

\[
v_{\rm int}=\min_{c,d}\mathbb E_p
\left(\log p_S-c-\sum_i d_i1_{i\in S}\right)^2.
\tag{25}
\]

This is the C024 interaction residual variance. It equals `Var(log p)-c_vec^T Gamma^{-1}c_vec`, where `c_vec_i=Cov(X_i,log p)` and `Gamma=Cov(X)` has diagonal entries `K_ii(1-K_ii)` and off-diagonal entries `-|K_ij|^2`. Full atom support makes `Gamma` positive definite. Apply the preparation-channel BKM contraction to the minimizing modular residual; since its eta is unchanged,

\[
\|\eta_f\|_\rho^2\le v_{\rm int}.
\tag{26}
\]

There is an exact Wick variance formula

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho (Q_B^\perp)^2)
=4\operatorname{Tr}_{\wedge^2}
[(\wedge^2K)(\wedge^2B)(\wedge^2(I-K))(\wedge^2B)].
\tag{27}
\]

For completeness, diagonalize K only for this **quantum trace calculation**, leaving the measured distribution and `v_int` in their original basis. Wick subtraction (21) cancels every internal contraction. The surviving ordered-pair terms are

\[
4\sum_{I,J:\,|I|=|J|=2}|(\wedge^2B)_{I,J}|^2
\prod_{i\in I}k_i\prod_{j\in J}(1-k_j),
\]

which is exactly (27). This follows directly from determinant moments of the quasifree state; the subtraction also handles pairs I,J with a common coordinate. The scalar inequality `LM<=AM` gives `chi_K(B)<=Tr rho (Q_B^perp)^2`.

As `B=M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}`, multiplicativity of compound matrices turns (27) into

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho (Q_B^\perp)^2)
=4\left\|\wedge^2[(I-K)^{-1/2}AK^{-1/2}]\right\|_F^2.
\]

**Corollary 2 (explicit recovery–Wick certificate).** Without assuming `[K,A]=0`,

\[
\boxed{-H''(K;A)\ge\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
-2\sqrt{v_{\rm int}}\,
\left\|\wedge^2[(I-K)^{-1/2}AK^{-1/2}]\right\|_F.}
\tag{28}
\]

For commuting K,A the squared coefficient is `4 sum_{i<j}a_i^2 a_j^2/[k_i(1-k_i)k_j(1-k_j)]`, precisely the explicit second-BKM coefficient in C024. Thus (28) recovers that prior commuting estimate and provides a recovery derivation and a closed noncommuting extension. It is not asserted to be sharper than C024's exact measured-acceleration norm.

\tag{30}

A small exploratory LP file `submodular_probe.py` asked for a violation at four sites and found none. **That is not evidence for a theorem in arbitrary dimension.** Root supplied the decisive prior obstruction: C041, Section 5, has an exact six-site negative full-exterior witness for the supermodular table `phi=-log det L_S` at an independently chosen rank-two projection base P. The exploratory file is retained to record the failed route, not as proof evidence.

The **self-matched** specialization remains a separate possible bridge:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_K(\log p_K)\preceq0\quad ?}
\tag{31}
\]

Here the odds defining the logarithmic table are those of the same K defining the recovery ensemble. C041 does not supply that matching, so its witness does not refute (31). Formula (31) was negative definite in the fixed four-site example and in a small targeted check, but it is unproved. If true, it would immediately settle every rank-two indefinite direction: `wedge^2B` is then negative semidefinite of rank one, so the second term in (13) is nonnegative. Even (31) would still leave positive/negative contributions from higher-rank indefinite and definite directions to be compensated by Fisher information.

The genuinely additional content of this attempt is that the matrix in (32) is exactly the zero-covariance non-Gaussian tangent of a specified physical Petz preparation, whose norm has the positive decomposition (20).
