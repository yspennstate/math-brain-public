## 1. Conditional replacement, including a moving reference

Let `mu(t)>0` be a twice differentiable law on a finite product space.
For a pivot i, define a stochastic, idempotent heat bath on probability columns:

    (T_i(t)r)(x)=mu_t(x_i | x_-i) r_-i(x_-i).                  (1)

It is reversible for `mu(t)`, because for states differing only at i,
`mu_x T_i(y,x)=mu_x mu_y / mu_-i(x_-i)` is symmetric.
Different pivots need not commute. Write

    s=mu'/mu,  u=mu''/mu,
    s_i=E_mu[s | X_-i],  u_i=E_mu[u | X_-i],
    a_i=s-s_i,  b_i=u-u_i-2s_i(s-s_i).                       (2)

The exact operator derivatives, at the displayed time, are

    T_i'=diag(a_i) T_i,   T_i''=diag(b_i) T_i.              (3)

Indeed, differentiating the ratio `mu_x / mu_-i` once gives its
logarithmic derivative `s-s_i`; differentiating the ratio twice gives
`u-u_i-2s_i(s-s_i)`. Thus for a moving input r,

    (T_i r)'=T_i r'+diag(a_i)T_i r,
    (T_i r)''=T_i r''+2diag(a_i)T_i r'+diag(b_i)T_i r.      (4)

These formulas include both conditional-kernel derivatives and changing
history weights. In particular `T_i mu=mu` and its two differentiated
versions are exact: `T_i mu'=mu s_i`, `T_i mu''=mu u_i`, so (4) gives
`mu'` and `mu''`, respectively. A heat bath based on the actual current
law does not itself move that law.

For two pivots, every term in the complete second jet is

    (T_j T_i r)''=T_j T_i r''
      +2(T_j' T_i+T_j T_i')r'
      +(T_j''T_i+2T_j'T_i'+T_j T_i'')r.                   (5)

Thus even the cross term between moving conditional kernels is retained.
For a DPP reference `mu(t)=P_(K+tA)`, insert the actual determinant scores
`s_x=p'_x/p_x`, `u_x=p''_x/p_x` into (2); no affine assumption is made
about a Schur complement.

## 2. A positive noncommuting entropy-dissipation theorem

Freeze a positive reference mu and two conditional replacements T_i,T_j.
For any positive p, disintegration gives the exact Pythagorean identity

    D(p||mu)-D(T_i p||mu)=D(p||T_i p).                    (6)

Indeed `log((T_i p)/mu)=log(p_-i/mu_-i)` is independent of i,
so its expectation agrees under p and T_i p. No commutation is used.
For `C_i(t)=(1-t)I+tT_i`, define the weighted Jensen–Shannon divergence
`JS_t(p,q)=(1-t)D(p||(1-t)p+tq)+tD(q||(1-t)p+tq)`.
Writing `Delta_i(p,t)=D(p||mu)-D(C_i(t)p||mu)` gives

    Delta_i(p,t)=t D(p||T_i p)+JS_t(p,T_i p)>=0.           (7)

Let `p_ji=C_j(t)C_i(t)p`, `p_ij=C_i(t)C_j(t)p`,
`bar p=(p_ji+p_ij)/2`, and `JS=JS_(1/2)`. Then

    D(p||mu)-D(bar p||mu)
      =[Delta_i(p,t)+Delta_j(C_i(t)p,t)
        +Delta_j(p,t)+Delta_i(C_j(t)p,t)]/2
        +JS(p_ji,p_ij).                                 (8)

This is an exact positive dissipation decomposition, with a strictly
positive final correction whenever the two replacement orders differ.
Equations (6), the definition of JS, and telescoping prove (7)–(8).
All divergences are nonnegative by `log z<=z-1`.
The corresponding *Shannon* gain retains the reference-energy term:

    H(bar p)-H(p)=[D(p||mu)-D(bar p||mu)]
                      -sum_x(bar p_x-p_x)log mu_x.       (9)

For a nonproduct DPP reference, `log mu` is not generally modular;
conservation of single-site means alone does not eliminate this term.

## 3. The exact order and its scale

With `L_i=T_i-I`, the ordered laws in Section 2 are exactly

    p_ji=p+t(L_i+L_j)p+t² L_j L_i p,
    p_ij=p+t(L_i+L_j)p+t² L_i L_j p,
    p_ji-p_ij=t² [T_j,T_i]p.                             (10)

The Shannon chain rule gives the positive, finite entropy correction

    H(bar p)-(H(p_ji)+H(p_ij))/2
       =JS(p_ji,p_ij)
       =(D(p_ji||bar p)+D(p_ij||bar p))/2 >=0.           (11)

Write `c=[T_j,T_i]p`. Taylor expansion of `-x log x` at p gives

    JS(p_ji(t),p_ij(t))
          =t⁴/8 sum_x c_x²/p_x+O(t⁵).                 (12)

The coefficient is positive if c is nonzero; its second derivative at zero
vanishes. The signed order effect appears two orders earlier:

    H''(p_ji)-H''(p_ij)=-2 sum_x c_x log p_x.            (13)

Here the Fisher terms agree, because both first derivatives are
`(L_i+L_j)p`. Formula (13) changes sign on reversing the order, while
(12) does not. Therefore the positive entropy correction cannot be used
as a second-order payment for the order-dependent acceleration.

For moving heat baths acting on their own reference,
`T_j(t)T_i(t)mu(t)=mu(t)` makes the order difference identically zero.
