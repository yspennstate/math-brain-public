## 1. A globally convergent matrix-balancing normal form

Let `0<K<I_n`, `p=P_K`, `L=K(I-K)^(-1)`, and `u in R^n`. Write `x=1_S`, `z=x-1/2`, `D=diag(exp u)`, and

    q_u(S)=p_S exp(u·x)/Z_K(u),
    B_u=D^(1/2)K[I+(D-I)K]^(-1)D^(1/2),
    Phi_K(u)=log sum_S p_S exp(u·z).                                  (1)

The principal-minor expansion of `det(I+D^(1/2)LD^(1/2))` proves `q_u=P_(B_u)`; all kernels and atom entropies remain in the original measured basis. Ordinary differentiation of the finite sum gives

    grad Phi=E_q z=diag B_u-1/2,
    Hess Phi=Sigma_u=Cov_q(X)>0.                                     (2)

Strict positivity follows because full support makes every nonconstant linear occupation statistic have positive variance. Also

    Phi_K(u)>=||u||_1/2+log(min_S p_S).                              (3)

Choose the Boolean vertex maximizing `u·z` to prove (3). Thus Phi is coercive and strictly convex, and has a unique minimizer `u_*(K)`. Every strict kernel has exactly one positive diagonal-field representative `B=B_(u_*)` with **all measured marginals equal to one half**. Equation (2) and the implicit function theorem make this balancing map smooth on the strict domain.

There is a constructive cyclic algorithm. If the current marginal is `m=B_ii`, update

    u_i <- u_i+log[(1-m)/m].                                        (4)

The new marginal is exactly one half, and the exact potential decrease is

    Phi_new-Phi_old=log[2sqrt(m(1-m))]<=-2(m-1/2)^2.                 (5)

Indeed the one-coordinate partition factor is `1-m+m exp(v)`, which proves both assertions. Iterates stay in a compact sublevel set. Equation (5) makes the deviations at updated coordinates tend to zero; their update increments tend to zero as well. Across one finite coordinate sweep, continuity on that compact set therefore makes every gradient coordinate tend to zero. Every subsequential limit is the unique stationary point, so the entire sequence converges to `u_*`. No stochastic or Sinkhorn theorem is imported.

## 2. Exactly when balancing produces complementary atoms

Fields add only `u·(2x-1)` to `log(p_x/p_(1-x))`. Hence complementary equality `q_x=q_(1-x)` is reachable **if and only if** this log ratio is linear in the centered bits. Equivalently, all its odd Walsh coefficients of degree at least three vanish. This is an invariant obstruction; balanced marginals alone do not remove higher odd interactions.

On three sites the only obstruction is

    tau=log[p_0 p_12 p_13 p_23/(p_1 p_2 p_3 p_123)]=0.               (6)

When (6) holds, the unique balancing activities and balanced atoms are explicit:

    d_i=sqrt[p_0 p_jk/(p_123 p_i)],   {i,j,k}={1,2,3},
    q_S=sqrt(p_S p_(S^c))/sum_T sqrt(p_T p_(T^c)).                    (7)

Multiplying the three activities gives `product_i d_i=p_0/p_123`, by (6). The empty/full and singleton/complement ratios then all equal one, proving (7). In particular the geometric complementary law in (7) is an actual DPP, because the diagonal odds congruence realizes it. No such DPP assertion is made for geometric complementary symmetrization when (6) fails.

For a three-site complementary law, the one-bit means imply `B_ii=1/2`. Its three-spin moment is `16 Re(B_12 B_23 B_31)`, hence the real cycle product vanishes. Diagonal gauge phases therefore put its kernel in the form

    B=I_3/2+iW,   W real skew-symmetric,
    v=(W_23,W_31,W_12),   T=||v||²<1/4.                             (8)

For a nonzero triangle, the cycle condition is exactly what permits all three edges to be purely imaginary; for a forest choose the phases successively along its edges. The spectrum of `iW` is `0,+sqrt(T),-sqrt(T)`, as follows by squaring W. Thus (8) is exactly the previously proved C008 center locus, including unequal edge strengths. Balancing to (8) is not by itself a curvature reduction: the next two sections quantify the missing terms.

## 3. Both derivatives of a moving balancing field

Consider the **affine original kernel** `K(t)=K+tA`, and let `u(t)=u_*(K(t))`; all quantities below are evaluated at zero. Put `s_S=p'_S/p_S`, `r_S=p''_S/p_S`, and evaluate these fixed tables under the balanced `q=P_B`. Define the orthogonal projection

    Pi f=f-E_q f-z^T Sigma^(-1) E_q[z f],   Sigma=E_q zz^T.           (9)

The complete balanced atom jets are

    h:=q'/q=Pi s,
    q''/q=Pi(r-s²+h²),
    u'=-Sigma^(-1)E_q[z s],
    u''=-Sigma^(-1)E_q[z(r-s²+h²)].                                 (10)

To prove them, differentiate `log q=log p+u·z-log sum p exp(u·z)`. The first mean and first-moment constraints are `E_q h=0` and `E_q zh=0`, giving the first and third formulas. Differentiating again gives `q''/q=r-s²+h²+u''·z` up to a constant. Its mean and first moments also vanish, proving the other two formulas. The term `r-s²+h²` shows exactly why deleting the occupation Fisher information does not give a valid Hessian transport.

Here is the corresponding **physical kernel acceleration**. At the balancing activity D put

    U=D^(1/2)(I+K(D-I))^(-1),   F=(D-I)(I+K(D-I))^(-1)=F*,
    A0=UAU*,   C0=-2UAFAU*,
    R_w(B)=(diag(w)B+B diag(w))/2-B diag(w)B,
    dR_w[X]=(diag(w)X+X diag(w))/2-X diag(w)B-B diag(w)X.             (11)

With `a=u'` and `b=u''`, the balanced kernel curve has

    B'=A0+R_a(B),
    B''=C0+2dR_a[A0]+R_b(B)+dR_a[R_a(B)].                          (12)

Differentiate the fixed-field rational map, then compose it with the commuting activities `exp(u(t)-u(0))`, to obtain (12). In particular every diagonal entry of both jets vanishes, as it must. If `G(B)=H(P_B)`, then the actual affine Hessian is recovered from

    D²G(B)[B',B']=-E_q h²-E_q[Pi(r-s²+h²) log q]-DG(B)[B''].        (13)

Thus even the balanced curve's entropy curvature contains a physical acceleration correction. Equation (13) is an equality, without a sign assertion for its final term.

## 4. An explicit quantitative center gap

For (8), define `t=4T`, and the four complementary single-atom masses

    q_0=(1-t)/8,   q_i=(1+t)/8-v_i²,
    c_i=log[q_j q_k/(q_0 q_i)],   m=min_i c_i,   cmax=max_i c_i.       (14)

If the graph is connected, `m>0`. The known C008 proof implies the following explicit gap, extracted here:

    -D²G(B)[A,A]>=delta ||A||F²  for every Hermitian A,
    delta=min{m, 16t²/[45(3+t²-2t³)]}>0.                            (15)

For precision, the extraction uses C008's real-sector decomposition `R=aI+u(3nn^T-I)+R0`, where `n=v/sqrt T`, `tr R0=n^T R0 n=0` and `||R||F²=3a²+6u²+||R0||F²`. Its bound is `(a,u) T0(a,u)^T+m||R0||F²`, with

    T0=[[3alpha/5-6m,3beta/5],[3beta/5,3gamma/5+6m]],
    D0=(1-t)(3+t),
    alpha=(36-12t²-8t³)/D0,   beta=-16t³/D0,
    gamma=16t²(3-2t)/D0,
    det T0>=384t²/(25D0)+(216/5)m(3+3t-2t²)/(3+t).                  (16)

C008 proves `T0>0` by its four-mass logarithmic inequality. For a positive two-by-two matrix, its least eigenvalue is at least determinant divided by trace. Applying this after normalizing by `diag(sqrt3,sqrt6)`, dropping the second positive determinant term, and using `6T0_11+3T0_22<=(18alpha+9gamma)/5` gives the second entry in (15). The imaginary sector has the direct bound `m||A||F²`. Its Hessian cross term with the real sector vanishes by complementary symmetry. This proves (15) from the credited center argument, retaining all nine Hermitian directions.

## 5. A simultaneous-field theorem with the actual affine Hessian

Start from any connected seed (8), choose **arbitrary three activities** `d_i>0`, and form `J=B_D` by (1). Put

    R=||log d||_1,   rho=||log d||_infinity.

Then the following explicit criterion proves the full nine-dimensional affine-kernel Hessian sign:

    2 cmax [exp(R+3rho)-1]<delta.                                   (17)

More quantitatively, with `U=D^(1/2)(I+B(D-I))^(-1)`,

    -D²G(J)[UAU*,UAU*]
      >=exp(-R){delta-2cmax[exp(R+3rho)-1]}||A||F².                  (18)

Because U is invertible, this covers **every** Hermitian physical direction at J, not only a field tangent or an affine odds direction.

Here is a direct proof retaining the complete affine Hessian. On three sites with tau=0 the frozen-log identity is

    -D²G(K)[A,A]=FI_K(A)-2tr(C adj A),   C=diag(c_1,c_2,c_3).         (19)

It follows by expanding `-log p_S` in Boolean monomials: the modular part has zero affine acceleration, and the second derivative of `det K_jk` is `2 det A_jk`. All c_i and tau are field invariant. The first derivative of a tilted atom in the physical direction UAU* has score `s_S(A)-E_(P_J)s(A)`, by differentiating the fixed-field map. Therefore, writing `q=P_J`,

    -D²G(J)[UAU*,UAU*]=Var_q(s)-2tr(W_D adj A),
    W_D=det(U)^2 U^(-1) C U^(-*).                                  (20)

The adjugate congruence identity proves (20). It is the actual affine Hessian; a nonlinear-field second jet has not been dropped.

The density ratio `q/p` is at least `exp(-R)`. The variational formula `Var(f)=min_a E(f-a)²` therefore gives `Var_q(s)>=exp(-R)FI_B(A)`. Next write `w=det(U)^2`. Since the seed has centered marginals,

    w=[E_p exp((log d)·z)]^(-2),   exp(-R)<=w<=1.                    (21)

Jensen gives the upper bound on w, and `|(log d)·z|<=R/2` gives its lower bound. Also

    ||U^(-1)||op<=exp(3rho/2),
    ||U^(-1)-I||op<=exp(3rho/2)-1.                                 (22)

Indeed `U^(-1)=(I+B(D-I))D^(-1/2)` and `||B||op<1`; triangle inequalities give (22). Consequently

    ||W_D-exp(-R)C||op<=cmax[exp(3rho)-exp(-R)].                    (23)

For Hermitian A, the eigenvalue inequality `sum_(i<j)|lambda_i lambda_j|<=sum_i lambda_i²` gives `||adj A||trace<=||A||F²`. Combining this with (15), (20), and (23) proves (18). This proof accounts for every frozen acceleration and uses the complete atom Fisher information.
