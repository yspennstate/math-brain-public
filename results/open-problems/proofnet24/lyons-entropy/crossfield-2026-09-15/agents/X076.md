# X076 — determinant compensation at a swap-symmetric three-site kernel

The target is Lyons's fixed-coordinate DPP Shannon-entropy conjecture; the

No universal concavity statement is asserted.

The new result is joint entropy concavity for the entire variable channel
(P,lambda)↦(Id⊗C_lambda)P, with an arbitrary retained label and an arbitrary
binary joint law P. An exact sum of squares proves it. Composing with the
physical two-site DPP law retains a determinant correction and closes every
swap-even direction whose active two-by-two velocity has nonpositive
determinant, with arbitrary spectator motion. X072 then supplies every odd
direction. The residual is the definite active block moving opposite to
the spectator; the complete five-dimensional even block remains open.

The assigned reduced target is the full five-real-dimensional swap-even
Hessian. X072 handles every odd mode, and the even hyperplanes with either
the pivot mean or the antisymmetric spectator mean fixed. This report keeps
the genuinely joint pivot/spectator motion.

Let the symmetric kernel, in the algebraic basis (e1,e+,e−), be

    K = [[a,conj(r),0],[r,u,0],[0,0,lambda]],
    0<M=[[a,conj(r)],[r,u]]<I,  0<lambda<1.

Measurements remain in the original coordinates (e1,e2,e3). Put b=1−a,
q=ab, rho=|r|², x1=u−rho/a, x0=u+rho/b. The exact measured entropy is

    F=h(a)+a f(x1,lambda)+b f(x0,lambda),

where f(x,l) is entropy of the four atoms

    ((1−x)(1−l), (x+l−2xl)/2, (x+l−2xl)/2, xl).

For w=x+l−2xl and L=log[w²/(4xl(1−x)(1−l))], the complete negative
Hessian of f is

    J(x,l) = [[(1−l)/(1−x)+l/x+(1−2l)²/w, 1/w−L],
              [1/w−L, (1−x)/(1−l)+x/l+(1−2x)²/w]].

In particular the logarithmic off-diagonal term is retained. For an affine
even velocity (alpha, beta, z, delta)=(a′,u′,r′,lambda′), define

    e1=beta−2 Re(conj(r)z)/a+alpha rho/a²,
    e0=beta+2 Re(conj(r)z)/b+alpha rho/b²,
    g=f_x(x1,lambda)−f_x(x0,lambda)>=0,
    k=f_l(x1,lambda)−f_l(x0,lambda).

Direct differentiation, using the exact two conditional accelerations,
gives

    −F′′ = alpha²/q + a (e1,delta) J(x1,lambda)(e1,delta)^T
                       + b (e0,delta) J(x0,lambda)(e0,delta)^T
            +2|z|² g −2alpha(beta g+delta k).                 (1)

This is the scalar specialization of C057's full acceleration cancellation.
It preserves all physical p′′ log p terms. The only unrestricted joint
payment is the final secant term; ordinary two-site concavity alone does
not remove it.

Put d=x0−x1=rho/q, e=beta+alpha d, and

    d′=2 Re(conj(r)z)/q + alpha(a−b)d/q.

Then e1=e−b d′ and e0=e+a d′, so (1) also reads

    −F′′ = alpha²/q+2alpha² d g−2alpha(e g+delta k)
          +a (e−b d′,delta)J(x1,lambda)(e−b d′,delta)^T
          +b (e+a d′,delta)J(x0,lambda)(e+a d′,delta)^T
          +2|z|² g.                                       (2)

The phase direction perpendicular to r contributes 2|z_perp|²g and has
zero mixed terms with the four remaining real coordinates. Hence the even
problem is exactly a four-variable real quadratic form when r!=0.

All specialized sources used here are internal credited reports X072 and

## 2. A jointly concave variable binary channel — proved

Let X be an arbitrary retained finite label and Y a binary bit, with an
arbitrary joint law P. Independently take Z~Bernoulli(l). Replace (Y,Z) by
the two measured outputs whose count is Y+Z, choosing the two singleton
labels equiprobably when Y+Z=1. Equivalently apply C_l to Y, where

    C_l(.|0)=(1−l,l/2,l/2,0),
    C_l(.|1)=(0,(1−l)/2,(1−l)/2,l).

**Theorem 1.** The function

    (P,l) ↦ H((Id_X ⊗ C_l)P)

is jointly concave on the product of the entire joint-probability simplex
and [0,1]. The label alphabet can have any finite cardinality. This is
stronger than concavity for a frozen channel; the channel parameter moves.

Here and below the theorem concerns this specific physical channel, not
arbitrary variable channels.

### 2.1 Scalar strengthened curvature

Let J be the complete negative Hessian displayed above and write

    A=J11>0, B=J12, C=J22, R=C−B²/A,
    v=f_l(x,l)−h′(l).

The key all-parameter inequality is

    R >= v².                                               (3)

It implies the matrix inequality J >= diag(0,v²). The logarithmic B term
is present on both sides of the proof.

**Proof of (3).** Put

    z=(x−l)/(x+l−2xl), h=1−2x,
    D=1+hz, E=1+2hz+z²,
    L=−log(1−z²), T=atanh(z).

Here |z|<1, |h|<1, D>0, E>0. Direct algebra gives

    l=x(1−z)/D,  w=2x(1−x)/D,
    A=E/[2x(1−x)D],  v=−T−hL/2,
    R=2D/E · [D(2z²/(1−z²)+L)−x(1−x)L²].

For the gradient identity, one may first set c=l(1−x), d=x(1−l) and use

    f(x,l)=h(x)+h(l)+c log(2c/w)+d log(2d/w).

Differentiating gives v=(1−x)log(1−z)−x log(1+z), the displayed value.

Consequently N=E(R−v²) is a polynomial of degree at most two in h:

    N=2D²(2z²/(1−z²)+L)
       −D(1−h²)L²/2−E(T+hL/2)².                           (4)

The apparent cubic terms cancel exactly. Simultaneous complementation
(x,l)↦(1−x,1−l) changes (h,z) to (−h,−z) and leaves N invariant,
so assume 0<=z<1. Put U=−log(1−z), V=log(1+z); thus L=U−V and
T=(U+V)/2. Write N=N0+N1 h+N2 h². Its three quadratic Bernstein
coefficients on [−1,1] are

    N(1)=(1+z)²[4z²/(1−z²)+2L−U²],
    N(−1)=(1−z)²[4z²/(1−z²)+2L−V²],
    N0−N2=4z²−(1−z)U²−(1+z)V²
                +(1−z²)UV+2(1−z²)(U−V).                  (5)

The elementary logarithm inequality

    |log t| <= |t−1|/sqrt(t), t>0,

follows on writing t=exp(s) from 2sinh(|s|/2)>=|s|. It yields
(1−z)U²<=z² and (1+z)V²<=z². Also U>=V>=0. Hence

    N0−N2 >=2z²,
    N(1)>=(1+z)² z²(3−z)/(1−z²),
    N(−1)>=(1−z)² z²(3+z)/(1−z²).

All three are nonnegative. Finally

    N(h)=((1+h)/2)²N(1)+((1−h)/2)²N(−1)
                       +(1−h²)(N0−N2)/2 >=0.

This proves (3). It is strict for x!=l; for x=l both R and v vanish.
No external entropy-concavity theorem is used in this scalar proof.

### 2.2 Exact sum of squares for the variable channel

For a strictly positive law write p_s=P(X=s), x_s=P(Y=1|X=s). Then

    F(P,l)=H(p)+sum_s p_s f(x_s,l).

Along an affine joint-law path P(t) and affine l(t), put

    c_s=p_s′, u_s=c_s/p_s, e_s=x_s′, delta=l′.

One has sum_s p_s u_s=0 and x_s″=−2u_s e_s. The latter is essential:
it cancels the two derivatives of the conditional weights. Direct
differentiation yields the exact formula

    −F″=sum_s p_s [u_s²+(e_s,delta)J(x_s,l)(e_s,delta)^T
                                      −2u_s delta f_l(x_s,l)].

Because sum p_s u_s=0, f_l can be replaced by v_s=f_l−h′(l).
Completing two squares gives the identity

    −F″=sum_s p_s [(u_s−v_s delta)²
                  +A_s(e_s+B_s delta/A_s)²
                  +(R_s−v_s²)delta²] >=0.                (6)

The final coefficients are nonnegative by (3). This proves Theorem 1 in
the interior. Approximation of P by strictly positive laws and of l by
interior parameters, followed by continuity of finite-law entropy,
proves it on the full closed domain.

## 3. New mixed-inertia cone at every swap-symmetric base — proved

Return to the DPP seed K and let its swap-even velocity be

    A_even = B_active ⊕ delta,
    B_active=[[alpha,conj(z)],[z,beta]],
    D_active=det(B_active)=alpha beta−|z|².

The active two-site DPP probabilities, in order (00,01,10,11), are

    P_M=(b(1−u)−rho, bu+rho, a(1−u)+rho, au−rho).

Along the affine kernel path M+t B_active, their exact acceleration is

    P_M″=2D_active (1,−1,−1,1).                           (7)

Freeze the first derivative of P_M and use it as an affine joint-law
velocity in Theorem 1. Denote the nonnegative square expression (6) for
this velocity by Q_channel. The entropy derivative in the probability
direction (1,−1,−1,1), which fixes both marginals, is exactly

    g=f_x(x1,lambda)−f_x(x0,lambda)>=0.

Indeed x1 increases at rate 1/a and x0 decreases at rate 1/b. Scalar
concavity of f gives the sign since x1<=x0. Applying the full second-order
chain rule to (7) therefore gives the exact null-Lagrangian compensation

    −H″_K(A_even)=Q_channel−2g D_active.                 (8)

**Theorem 2.** At every strict swap-symmetric three-site kernel,

    det(B_active)<=0  ==>  −H″_K(A_even)>=0,

with arbitrary spectator velocity delta. More precisely (8) is a sum
of all squares in (6) and 2g|det(B_active)|. Thus the pivot mean a and
the spectator mean lambda may move together without any relation.

X072's permutation-invariant Hessian splitting has zero even/odd cross
terms and proves every odd direction nonnegative for −H″. It follows that
Theorem 2 remains true after adding an arbitrary odd Hermitian velocity:

    A=A_even+A_odd,  det(B_active)<=0  ==>  −H″_K(A)>=0.   (9)

This includes full-rank indefinite directions with both a′ and lambda′
nonzero and arbitrary four-real-dimensional symmetry-breaking velocity.
The statement applies to the even active block, not to an arbitrarily
chosen two-dimensional compression of a nonsymmetric base.

The complete symmetric domain is convex; consequently any affine
swap-symmetric kernel segment whose constant active direction has
nonpositive determinant has the finite Shannon-entropy Jensen property,
including closed contractions by regularization. Odd-direction additions
in (9) are a local base-point assertion; a segment leaving the symmetric
locus is not automatically covered at its other points.

There is also a finite consequence with nonsymmetric endpoints. Let
Pi J=(J+PJP)/2 be measured swap averaging. If K0,K1 are contractions,
Kt=(1−t)K0+tK1 is swap symmetric at a specified t, and the active
two-by-two block of Pi(K1−K0) has nonpositive determinant, then

    H(P_Kt)>=(1−t)H(P_K0)+t H(P_K1).                     (9a)

Indeed the symmetric averaged segment has the Jensen property just proved.
X072 proves H(P_PiKj)>=H(P_Kj) by concavity in the odd swap direction.
Combining those inequalities proves (9a). Unlike X072's earlier two slice
envelopes, neither the pivot mean nor the spectator mean is fixed here.

## 4. Exact remaining obstruction

Formula (8) does not settle det(B_active)>0. In that case full concavity
requires the genuinely stronger inequality

    Q_channel >= 2g det(B_active).                       (10)

Theorem 1 provides its nonnegative left side, not the payment of its
strictly positive right side. This is the precise residual; no signed
probability acceleration has been omitted. When delta has the same sign
as the definite B_active, the whole even direction is semidefinite and
X015 applies. Thus, within the remaining even problem, the only unresolved
directions have B_active definite and a spectator velocity of the opposite
sign. The new theorem closes the entire indefinite-active sector globally
in the channel parameter, rather than merely the two previous hyperplanes.
