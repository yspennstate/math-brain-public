## 1. Results and scope

put K(M)=J+VMV*, 0<=M<=I_2, and measure the ordinary coordinate occupations.
We prove its two-mode outcome effects directly from finite determinants:

    E_y=diag(alpha_y,B_y,gamma_y),  B_y>=0,
    alpha_y,gamma_y>=0,  det B_y=alpha_y gamma_y,
    sum alpha_y=sum gamma_y=1,  sum B_y=I_2.                    (1)

More strongly, their occupation-generating effect satisfies the POLYNOMIAL
identity det B(z)=alpha(z)gamma(z), with F(z)=sum_y z^y E_y. This is a
global realizability constraint absent from (1).

**Classification theorem.** If all B_y commute, there is one fixed active
basis and a partition of the measured coordinates into two blocks such that
the entire output law equals two fixed independent binary-input channels

The coherence dependence through det M is retained throughout.

The channel-concavity closure is credited to X041/C061's balanced refinement,

## 2. Direct finite derivation of every outcome effect

For a bit pattern y let Q_y be the diagonal projector onto its zero bits and
epsilon_y=(-1)^(number of zero bits). The signed-column atom formula gives

    p_y(M)=epsilon_y det(C_y+VMV*),  C_y=J-Q_y.                 (2)

When C_y is invertible, put alpha=epsilon_y det C_y and
T=V*C_y^(-1)V, which is Hermitian. The ordinary determinant lemma yields

    p_y(M)=alpha[1+tr(TM)+det(T)det(M)].                       (3)

Set B=alpha(I+T) and gamma=alpha det(I+T). Expanding two-by-two determinants
then gives exactly

    p_y(M)=alpha det(I-M)+tr[B(M-det(M)I)]+gamma det(M),
    det B=alpha gamma.                                       (4)

These are polynomial identities in the entries of C_y,V,M. Perturb C_y
by a scalar eta I to avoid its finitely many singular values and pass to
eta=0. The coefficients alpha,B,gamma are determined by (4), so this also
proves (4) for singular atoms; no inverse or division by zero is retained.
Their Hermiticity follows by the same continuation. Physical inputs M=0,I
give alpha,gamma>=0. Input M=uu*, ||u||=1, gives p_y(uu*)=u*B_yu>=0,
so B_y>=0. Normalization at those same inputs gives all sums in (1).
Thus neither Gaussian-effect closure nor a Grassmann integration theorem
has been assumed. The proof also applies to any affine rank-two family
K_0+LML* which is physical for every M in [0,I_2].

## 3. The full polynomial compatibility and edge relations

Let z=(z_1,...,z_n), Z=diag(z), and z^y=product_(i:y_i=1) z_i.
Define alpha(z)=sum z^y alpha_y, and similarly B(z),gamma(z).
The elementary determinantal generating formula is

    sum_y z^y p_y(M)=det[I+(Z-I)(J+VMV*)].                   (5)

Apply the determinant lemma with C(z)=I+(Z-I)J. At z=1 it is invertible,
so the same rank-two calculation on an open neighborhood proves

    det B(z)=alpha(z)gamma(z)                                (6)

there. Both sides are polynomials, hence (6) holds identically. No positivity
of a nonsymmetric intermediate matrix or of the field variables is needed.
The orthogonal-background assumption also fixes the cardinality specialization:
at z_i=s, (alpha(z),B(z),gamma(z))=(phi(s),s phi(s)I,s^2 phi(s)),
where phi(s)=det(I+(s-1)J)=product_j(1-lambda_j+lambda_j s).
Thus the three coefficient layers have exactly shifted Poisson-binomial
count laws; this follows directly from JV=0 in (5).

Coefficient extraction gives further identities. If y and y+i differ in
one bit, the coefficient with doubled exponents on their common one bits
and exponent one on i has only these two ordered decompositions. Thus

    tr(adj(B_y) B_(y+i))
       =alpha_y gamma_(y+i)+alpha_(y+i) gamma_y.              (7)

In particular E_y+E_(y+i) is Gaussian. When both alpha values are positive,
write C_y=B_y/alpha_y. Equation (7) is equivalent to

    det(C_y-C_(y+i))=0.                                      (8)

Hence normalized neighboring one-particle odds differ by rank at most one.
More generally all coefficient identities of (6) retain the sums over every
decomposition of a doubled-bit exponent. These relations are necessary;
their sufficiency for physical determinantal realizability is not asserted.

## 4. Why the abstract local constraints cannot prove concavity

Take the ordinary two-mode occupation POVM and split ONLY its vacuum effect
into 64 equal effects. Every resulting effect satisfies (1), and all B_y
commute. Nevertheless, at M=tI_2 the output entropy is

    H_out(t)=2h(t)+(1-t)^2 log 64,
    H_out''(1/2)=-8+12log2>0.                                (9)

The strict sign follows from log2=2 sum_(k>=0) 1/[(2k+1)3^(2k+1)]>2/3.
This is an abstract colored-outcome counterexample, NOT a DPP counterexample.
No assignment to distinct ordinary bit outcomes can satisfy (6): b_1,b_2,
gamma would each be a single monomial, whereas alpha has 64 distinct positive
monomials, contradicting b_1 b_2=alpha gamma. Arbitrary outcome splitting adds
sum_y p_y(M) H(split_y), whose second derivative is
2det(A) sum_y (alpha_y+gamma_y-tr B_y)H(split_y). Dropping this term would
create precisely the false proof. Root independently identified (9).

## 5. Classification of all commuting globally compatible effects

Assume a positive normalized bit-outcome effect family satisfies (6) and its
Hermitian B_y commute. In ONE fixed unitary active basis let
B(z)=diag(b_1(z),b_2(z)); all four polynomials alpha,b_1,b_2,gamma have
nonnegative coefficients, are multiaffine, and take value one at z=1.
Equation (6) says b_1 b_2=alpha gamma.

In the real multivariate polynomial UFD let g=gcd(alpha,b_1), alpha=g a,
b_1=g b, with a,b coprime. Then b b_2=a gamma implies b_2=h a, gamma=h b.
Normalize g,h,a,b by their nonzero values at z=1. Because every original
product takes value one there, this gives, without changing any product,

    alpha=g a,  b_1=g b,  b_2=h a,  gamma=h b,
    g(1)=h(1)=a(1)=b(1)=1.                                   (10)

For every variable, degree in a product is the sum of degrees. Multiaffinity
of all four products therefore forces the two variable sets
I=vars(g) union vars(h), J=vars(a) union vars(b) to be disjoint.
Any entirely absent coordinate can be placed in either block. Specializing
the other block to one in alpha,b_1,b_2 shows that each of g,h,a,b has
nonnegative coefficients. They are normalized probability generating
polynomials on their respective blocks.

Let channel C_1 have endpoint laws a,b on block J, and C_2 have endpoint
laws g,h on block I. Equation (10) identifies their four product endpoint
laws with alpha,b_1,b_2,gamma. Equation (4) now proves the exact full law

    p(M)=(C_1 tensor C_2) P_M.                                (11)

In particular M_12 has never been erased or replaced by eigenvalues:
P_M has probabilities det(I-M), M_11-det M, M_22-det M, det M.
The factorization proves the stated classification, including zero effects,
constant channels, and deterministic measured coordinates.

## 6. Concavity under the classified channels, with full correction

First every two-site determinantal entropy is concave. At a strict two-site
kernel N write its atom probabilities u,v,w,x in order 00,10,01,11 and
T=(v+w)/(2sqrt(ux))>=1. Along Hermitian A, delta=det A, its exact Hessian is

    -H''=FI-2delta log(vw/(ux)).                              (12)

For delta<=0 this is >=FI because vw>=ux. For delta>0 reverse A if needed
so A>0. The empty/full score terms give FI>=(tr PA)^2+(tr QA)^2, where
P=adj(N)/sqrt(x), Q=adj(I-N)/sqrt(u), det P=det Q=1.
The trace-determinant inequality gives FI>=2det(P+Q)delta=4(1+T)delta.
Also log(vw/(ux))<=2log T. Hence -H''>=4delta(1+T-log T)>=8delta.

For completeness, refine each finite binary-input channel C(y|x)=c_y+x d_y
by pairing its positive and negative d_y rows through a nonnegative transport
r_ij with those row and column margins. Split positive i toward pair (i,j)
with probability r_ij/d_i, and negative j with r_ij/(-d_j).
Each pair label has input-independent weight
w_ij=r_ij[c_i/d_i+c_j/(-d_j)]. Conditional on that label, its positive-row
bit has probability rho_ij+sigma_ij x with 0<=rho<=rho+sigma<=1,
rho=r_ij c_i/(d_i w_ij), sigma=r_ij/w_ij. Zero-difference rows are constant
singleton labels. Conditional on both labels the two output bits therefore
have kernel D+S M S, D=diag(rho_1,rho_2), S=diag(sqrt(sigma_i)).
The label weights are fixed and independent. The entropy added by retaining
the split label is sum_i sum_y P(Y_i=y)H(split_i(.|y)), affine in M_ii.
Subtracting this exact affine correction transfers the two-site concavity
from every D+SMS to (11), proving the theorem on strict inputs. Finite
entropy continuity handles all endpoints and structural zero atoms.

Writing tau_i=(1/2)sum_y|C_i(y|1)-C_i(y|0)|, the same proof retains

    -H''>=8tau_1 tau_2 det A       if det A>0,
    -H''>=FI_output               if det A<=0.                (13)

Indeed sum w sigma=tau; refinement preserves the full score, and the affine
entropy correction has zero second derivative. No posterior loss is omitted.
