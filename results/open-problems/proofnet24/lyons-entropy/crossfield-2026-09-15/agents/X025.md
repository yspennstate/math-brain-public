## 1. The class and the exact comparison identity

Order the four sites into two parts of size two and write

`K(Z)=[[I_2/2,Z],[Z*,I_2/2]]`, `||Z||op<1/2`.                         (1)

This is exactly the strict balanced bipartite class. In cycle order it has diagonal `1/2`, possible edges `12,23,34,41`, and zero chords. Edges may vanish. Let `f:{0,1}^4 -> R` be any frozen supermodular function and set

`F_f(Z)=E_(P_K(Z)) f(X)`, `tau=Delta_1234 f`.

Here each `Delta_i` means occupied value minus unoccupied value. For an edge `e={i,j}`, let `{u,v}` be its complementary pair and put

`h_e(a,b)=Delta_i Delta_j f(X_u=a,X_v=b) >=0`,

`s_e^+=h_e(0,1)+h_e(1,0)`, `s_e^-=h_e(0,0)+h_e(1,1)`.

Thus `s_e^- - s_e^+ = tau` for every edge. Define also

`g(x)=(1/2) sum_(|T|=3) product_(i in T)x_i - product_(i=1)^4 x_i`,

`G(Z)=E g(X)=3/16-||Z||F^2/4-|det Z|^2`.                            (2)

**Lemma 1 (exact comparison).** For every complex `2 x 2` direction `B`, with the function `f` fixed, both identities hold:

`D²F_f(Z)[B,B] = tau D² det K(Z)[B,B] - sum_e s_e^+ |B_e|²`,          (3)

`D²F_f(Z)[B,B] = (-tau) D²G(Z)[B,B] - sum_e s_e^- |B_e|²`.           (4)

Here `B_e` is the corresponding cross-block entry; conjugating an entry to use cycle orientation does not change its magnitude. These are unrestricted cross-block identities, not a discarded-acceleration argument.

**Proof.** Möbius expansion gives `F_f=sum_T (Delta_T f)(0) det K_T`. Bipartiteness excludes triangles. Every proper principal minor therefore depends affinely on edge squared moduli `r_e=|Z_e|²`. The sole fourth-degree term is `tau |det Z|²`. Consequently subtracting `tau det K` removes every fourth-degree term.

More explicitly, the formal derivative in `r_e`, holding the full cycle product variable fixed, is

`partial_(r_e) F_f = -W_e`, `W_e=E_(P_K({u,v})) h_e(X_u,X_v)`.

The minus sign is the determinant transposition sign; summing permutations on the complementary pair gives precisely its marginal atom law. That law has masses `a=1/4-r_opposite` at `00,11` and `b=1/4+r_opposite` at `01,10`. Therefore

`W_e=a s_e^-+b s_e^+ = tau a+s_e^+/2 = (-tau)b+s_e^-/2`.             (5)

Since `partial_(r_e) det K=-a`, the difference `F_f-tau det K` is a constant minus `sum_e s_e^+ r_e/2`. Also `Delta_1234 g=-1` and `Delta_e g` has values `0,1/2,1/2,0`, so `partial_(r_e)G=-b`; the difference `F_f-(-tau)G` is a constant minus `sum_e s_e^- r_e/2`. Differentiating these exact quadratic remainders proves (3)–(4).

To check (2), summing the four three-site principal minors gives `1/2-||Z||F²`, while

`det K=1/16-||Z||F²/4+|det Z|²`.

Substitution into `E g=(1/2)sum_(|T|=3)det K_T-det K` proves (2). ∎

## 2. Two comparisons remove the gauge curvature obstruction

For a cross-block variation write `A=[[0,B],[B*,0]]`. The determinant differential gives

`D² det K[A,A]=det K { (tr(K^-1 A))²-tr(K^-1 A K^-1 A) }`.            (6)

In particular, on `tr(K^-1 A)=0` this is the negative square

`D² det K[A,A]=-det K ||K^-1/2 A K^-1/2||F²`.                        (7)

The other reference is strictly concave on the entire cross-block slice. With `delta=det Z`, `delta_B=tr(adj(Z) B)`, differentiating (2) gives

`D²G[B,B]=-||B||F²/2-2|delta_B|²-4 Re(conj(delta) det B)`

`          <= -(1/2-2|delta|)||B||F² <0` for `B !=0`.                (8)

Indeed `|det B|<=||B||F²/2` by its two singular values, and `|det Z|<1/4` follows from `||Z||op<1/2`.

**Theorem 2 (frozen-supermodular gauge sign).** For (1), every supermodular `f`, and every real diagonal `D`, put `A=i[D,K]=[[0,B],[B*,0]]`. Then

`D²F_f(K)[A,A] <=0`.                                                (9)

**Proof.** Diagonal-unitary conjugation leaves every principal minor invariant, hence `tr(K^-1 A)=0`. If `tau>=0`, insert (7) into (3), using `s_e^+>=0`. If `tau<0`, insert (8) into (4), using `s_e^->=0`. These two cases exhaust all real `tau`. ∎

More precisely, writing `P_f(A)=-D²F_f[A,A]`, the positive-branch identity is

`P_f(A)=tau det K ||K^-1/2 A K^-1/2||F² + sum_e s_e^+|B_e|²`         (10)

for `tau>=0`. For `tau<0`, with `lambda=-tau>0`, the exact identity is

`P_f(A)=lambda {||B||F²/2+2|delta_B|²+4 Re(conj(delta)det B)}`

`         +sum_e s_e^-|B_e|²`

`       >=lambda(1/2-2|det Z|)||B||F²+sum_e s_e^-|B_e|²`.            (11)

The reference determinants control the complete cycle interaction; neither formula requires each individual radial derivative to have the favorable sign.

## 3. Entropy, the signed Laplacian, and global Gaussian damping

Freeze `f(x)=-log p_x(K)`. It is supermodular: with `L=K(I-K)^-1>0`, the atom identity is `p_S=det(I-K)det L_S`; the positive two-site Schur complement gives `p_(Ti)p_(Tj)>=p_T p_(Tij)`, and hence `Delta_i Delta_j f(T)>=0`. This checks all hypotheses used above without importing a stronger negative-dependence assertion.

The general entropy identity is

`D²H_K[A,A]=-I_K(A)+D²F_f(K)[A,A]`, `I_K(A)=sum_x (Dp_x[A])²/p_x`.

Every gauge direction has every atom score zero. Theorem 2 therefore proves its entropy Hessian is nonpositive, with (10)–(11) as exact production identities. The same argument proves nonpositive curvature for **any** cross-block direction whose complete atom score vanishes: its all-occupied atom score makes `tr(K^-1 A)=0` in (6).

In cycle orientation put `z_e=K_(i,i+1)`, `Q=product_e z_e`, `q=Re Q`, and `x_e=d_i-d_(i+1)`, so `sum_e x_e=0`. Equations (5) and the full-cycle determinant coefficient give the equivalent signed-Laplacian formula

`-D²H_K[i[D,K],i[D,K]] = sum_e ell_e(d_i-d_(i+1))²`,

`ell_e=2(r_e W_e+tau q)`.                                           (12)

Thus the graph Laplacian with these possibly signed edge weights is positive semidefinite. This is the compensating statement needed for phase diffusion, even when an individual edge radius has positive entropy derivative as in C083.

**Theorem 3 (global four-cycle phase damping).** Let `K` be any Hermitian positive contraction of the form (1), allowing `||Z||op=1/2`. For arbitrary vectors `y_1,...,y_4 in R^m`, set

`rho_ij=||y_i-y_j||²`, `(K_t)_ij=exp(-t rho_ij)K_ij`, `t>=0`.

Then `H(P_(K_t))` is nondecreasing. If `K` is strict, it is strictly increasing exactly when some nonzero edge has positive damping rate.

**Proof.** For `D_alpha=diag(y_1alpha,...,y_4alpha)`, Gaussian averaging of diagonal unitaries gives `K_t`; in particular it preserves the spectral buffer, balanced diagonal, and bipartite zeros. Its generator is `dot K_t=-sum_alpha[D_alpha,[D_alpha,K_t]]`. Twice differentiating the constant function `H(exp(isD)K exp(-isD))` gives

`DH_K(-[D,[D,K]])=-D²H_K[i[D,K],i[D,K]]`.

Consequently

`dH(K_t)/dt=sum_alpha P_(-log p(K_t))(i[D_alpha,K_t]) >=0`.            (13)

Integrating (13), with the current data substituted in (10) or (11), gives an exact nonnegative entropy-production integral for every finite time interval.

For strictness, `tau!=0` is already strict whenever `A!=0` by (7) or (8). If `tau=0`, a supported edge has `s_e^+=s_e^->0`. To see this directly, let `z!=0` be that edge and `P` the product along its complementary three-edge path. The two conditional Schur entries on even and odd outside parity are `z+P/a` and `z-P/b`, respectively, with `a,b>0` and `a+b=1/2`. They cannot both vanish. The two-site log-odds difference is positive whenever its Schur entry is nonzero, so at least one `h_e` is positive; the equality of its even and odd sums proves the claim. Equation (10) is then positive for every nonzero gauge motion. Supported edges remain nonzero at finite times. Finally regularize a boundary base by `(1-2epsilon)K+epsilon I` and let `epsilon` decrease to zero; atom-polynomial and entropy continuity extend monotonicity to the boundary. ∎

If all four `y_i` are distinct, the limiting law is four independent fair bits, so (13) integrates to the exact deficit `4 log 2-H(K)`. Coincident vectors instead leave their corresponding principal blocks. Block-diagonal direct sums of this class and the known three-site class inherit monotonicity by entropy additivity; this observation is not a claim about coupled higher-dimensional blocks.
