## 1. Exact normalized curvature and Gaussian exterior volumes

Fix `D=diag(d_i)`, `0<d_i<1`, and a complex `n`-by-`2` matrix B with rows
`b_i`. For `M>0` assume `K=D+BMB*<I`. Put

    H1=B*(I-D)^(-1)B,       N=(M^(-1)-H1)^(-1)>0,
    x_i=b_i/sqrt(d_i(1-d_i)), gamma_i=x_i N x_i*,
    v_i=K_ii(1-K_ii),        S(M)=sum_i v_i gamma_i^2.             (1)

X040's exact determinant transform, with the original atom normalization,
is `p_z=q_z det(I+M T_z)`, where q is product Bernoulli(D),
`T_z=sum_i eta_i(z_i)b_i*b_i`, and `eta_i=z_i/d_i-(1-z_i)/(1-d_i)`.
Let `c_ij=|det[b_i;b_j]|^2` and let R be an occupied set outside i,j. Then

    ell_ij(R)=log[p_(R+i)p_(R+j)/(p_R p_(R+i+j))]>=0,
    J=sum_(i<j)c_ij E_(q,-ij) ell_ij(R),
    -F''_M[E,E]=FI_M(E)-2 det(E) J,  F(M)=H(P_K(M)).              (2)

For clarity, (2) follows by differentiating the quadratic determinant:
`p''_z=2 det(E)q_z det(T_z)`. Cauchy--Binet expands `det T` into
`sum c_ij eta_i eta_j`; centered Bernoulli integration by parts converts
its log pairing into minus the displayed face sum. Thus neither a
changing normalization nor any Fisher score is omitted.

Set `L=K(I-K)^(-1)`. Its Schur complement over R gives the positive
two-by-two matrix `Q=L_{ij}-L_{ij,R}L_R^(-1)L_{R,ij}`. Factoring the
four principal odds determinants proves `ell=log(Q_11 Q_22/det Q)`.
For a proper complex Gaussian G of covariance L this is precisely
`I(G_i;G_j | G_R)`: the density of a d-dimensional proper Gaussian is
`exp(-z*Q^(-1)z)/(pi^d det Q)`, and its differential entropy is
`log det Q+d(1+log pi)`, by direct integration. Conditional covariance
Q is independent of the conditioned values, by completing the square.

There is also a literal random exterior-volume identity. Take two
independent copies of G, and their residual vectors `g,h` on i,j after
linear regression on R. Then

    ell_ij(R)=1+E log[|g_1|^2 |h_2|^2/|g_1 h_2-g_2 h_1|^2].    (3)

Indeed, write `[g,h]=Q^(1/2)Z`. For standard complex Z, Gram--Schmidt
gives `|det Z|^2=UV`, with U of density `u exp(-u)` and V of density
`exp(-v)`, independent. If `c=int_0^infinity exp(-u)log u du`, an
integration by parts gives `E log U=c+1`, `E log V=c`. The numerator's
expected log is `log(Q_11 Q_22)+2c`, proving (3). All logarithms are
integrable. Every R can use the same two copies of the global G;
there is no independence assertion between faces. The external-set
average in (2) is still q, and no random volume normalization replaces p.

## 2. A Gaussian signal-to-noise bound that pays the full curvature

**Theorem.** At every strict positive M as above, for every Hermitian E,

    -F''_M[E,E]>=(1-S(M)) FI_M(E).                              (4)

In particular `S(M)<=1` implies the complete active Hessian is negative
semidefinite. D, the row norms, and complex phases are arbitrary.

**Proof.** Write `Lambda=D(I-D)^(-1)` and X for the rows x_i.
The elementary Woodbury identity gives

    L=Lambda^(1/2)(I+X N X*)Lambda^(1/2).                        (5)

N is only a base-point representation; derivatives remain along affine M.
Its Gaussian is the noisy factor model `Y_i=epsilon_i+x_i H`,
where independent epsilon_i are standard proper complex Gaussians and
`H` has covariance N. Completing its conditional square gives
`N_R=(N^(-1)+sum_(r in R)x_r*x_r)^(-1)<=N`. After harmless diagonal
rescaling, Q has entries `1+a,1+b,u`, where
`a=x_i N_R x_i*<=gamma_i`, `b<=gamma_j`, and `|u|^2<=ab`.
Consequently

    ell_ij(R)<=log[(1+a)(1+b)/(1+a+b)]
              <=ab/(1+a+b)<=gamma_i gamma_j/(1+gamma_i+gamma_j)
              <=gamma_i gamma_j.                              (6)

The middle fraction increases in a,b; all statements follow directly
from Cauchy--Schwarz and `log(1+x)<=x`.

If `det E>0`, reverse E's sign if necessary so `E>0`, and put
`a_i=b_i E b_i*>=0`, `A0=sum_i a_i^2/v_i`. For the full atom score u,
`Cov(u,X_i)=a_i`. The occupation covariance has diagonal v_i and
off-diagonal `-|K_ij|^2`. Therefore the statistic
`g=sum_i(a_i/v_i)(X_i-K_ii)` has `Cov(u,g)=A0` and `Var g<=A0`.
Cauchy--Schwarz proves `FI>=A0`, including the full measured score.
The two-row Gram determinant also gives `det(E)c_ij<=a_i a_j`.
Using (6), then weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, proves

    2 det(E)J<=2 sum_(i<j)a_i a_j gamma_i gamma_j
                <=(sum_i a_i gamma_i)^2
                <=A0 sum_i v_i gamma_i^2<=S(M) FI.              (7)

This proves (4) for definite E. For `det E<=0`, (2) gives `-F''>=FI`,
which is stronger. Rank-deficient B and zero row weights cause no
division by them. This completes the proof. No Gaussian comparison
theorem, quantum entropy inequality, or asymptotic assumption is used.

An optional sharper certificate is `lambda_max(W)<=1`, where
`W_ii=0`, `W_ij=sqrt(v_i v_j)gamma_i gamma_j/(1+gamma_i+gamma_j)`.
Indeed (7) with the middle bound in (6) is at most
`lambda_max(W) A0`. This real symmetric nonnegative matrix has
`lambda_max(W)>=0`; its Rayleigh bound is all that is required.

## 3. Unequal-noise domains and arbitrary finite dilution

Fix any `Mbar>0` with `Mbar^(-1)>H1`, let
`Nbar=(Mbar^(-1)-H1)^(-1)` and `gbar_i=x_i Nbar x_i*`. If

    rho=(1/4)sum_i gbar_i^2<1,                                 (8)

then F is concave on the whole convex domain `0<=M<=Mbar`, and its
interior satisfies `-F''>=(1-rho)FI`. Indeed `N(M)<=Nbar`,
`v_i<=1/4`, and (4) applies. Singular M follows by continuity from
the domain's relative interior; all physical K remain strict.

More generally replicate each row/noise pair `(b_i,d_i)` m times and
divide each replicated row by `sqrt m`. H1 and the physical domain
stay fixed, while each gamma becomes `gbar_i/m`; hence

    rho_m=(1/(4m))sum_i gbar_i^2.                               (9)

For every prescribed finite strict Mbar, sufficient replication makes
the full noncommuting active domain concave. The noise can be unequal
and arbitrarily close to either endpoint, provided it remains strict.
This is a finite quantitative theorem, not a limiting entropy analogy.

Here is an explicit rational complex family, with unequal noise and
unequal row norms. Put

    d=(9/25,7/16,5/9,3/4), c=(4/5,3/4,2/3,1/2),
    V=(1/2)[[1,1],[1,-1],[1,i],[1,-i]], B0=diag(c)V.             (10)

Then `V*V=I`, `c_i^2=1-d_i`, so `H1=I`. For every integer k>=1,
replicate each pair k^2 times, divide rows by k, and set
`t_k=2k/(2k+3)`. The entire four-real-dimensional domain
`0<=M<=t_k I` satisfies (8)-(9) with the uniform bound

    rho=(1/36)sum_i d_i^(-2)=890957/1786050<1/2.                (11)

Thus `-F''>FI/2` whenever FI is nonzero. Comparable noncommuting
endpoints are, for example,

    M0=(t_k/8)[[2,1],[1,2]], M1=(t_k/8)[[5,i],[-i,5]].         (12)

Their difference has eigenvalues `(t_k/8)(3+-sqrt 2)>0`.
The covariance bound `FI>=2||diag(BEB*)||_2^2` from X040 also gives
a strict Jensen gap at least
`(1-rho)theta(1-theta)||diag(B(M1-M0)B*)||_2^2` along this segment.

This family is not confined to an odds determinant-spread neighborhood.
At `M=t_k I`, let `R_L=diag(L)^(-1/2)L diag(L)^(-1/2)` and
`G0=sum_i d_i^(-1)V_i*V_i`. Determinant factorization of (5) gives

    det R_L=det(I+(2k/3)G0)
                 /prod_i(1+1/(3kd_i))^(k^2) ->0.               (13)

The logarithm of the denominator grows linearly in k, while the

outside X005's `det R_L>=exp(-2)` sufficient condition for rank-two
semidefinite directions. Its full physical kernels have fractional
