# X093 — second-occupation Fisher control at a noninjective projection

**15 September 2026. PROVED restricted full-Hessian theorem.** This removes a concrete degeneracy excluded by C084: a real, nontrivial four-site rank-two projection whose two adjacent-cardinality feature maps both have rank three instead of four. The empty and full atoms supply the missing score coordinates, and the singular count acceleration is controlled at arbitrarily separated endpoint rates. The universal Lyons conjecture remains open. No historical priority is asserted.

Only `agents/X093.md` and `computations/X093/` are authored here. The exact target, including the user-called “Eliyahu” naming uncertainty, is in TARGET.md. Every Hessian below is with respect to the actual affine Hermitian kernel, and entropy is measured Shannon entropy.

## 1. Concrete projection and theorem

Let

\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&-1&0\\0&1&-1&-1\\1&0&1&-1\\1&1&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad [V,W]=N/\sqrt3,
\qquad Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here `N^T N=3I`. Put `P=VV^T`, `Q=WW^T`, and `H=J=I+Y/2`. Explicitly,

\[
P=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&1&0\\-1&1&0&1\\1&0&1&1\\0&1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]

For independent positive parameters define

\[
K_{\eta,\epsilon}=V(I-\eta H)V^T+\epsilon WJW^T.
\tag{2}
\]

Since the two eigenvalues of each shape are `1/2,3/2`, (2) is a strict positive contraction for `0<eta,epsilon<2/3`. Decompose every Hermitian affine direction in the displayed orthonormal splitting as

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}B&X\\X^*&C\end{pmatrix},\quad
B=B_R+bY,\quad C=C_R+cY,
\tag{3}
\]

where `B_R,C_R` are real symmetric and `b,c` are real. Let `p_S=p_S(K)`, `\dot p_S=Dp_S(K)[A]`, and

\[
F_k(A)=\sum_{|S|=k}\frac{\dot p_S^2}{p_S},\quad
L=\log(1/\eta)+\log(1/\epsilon),
\]

\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\eta,\epsilon}(A)=F_1(A)+F_3(A)
 +(1+\epsilon L)b^2+(1+\eta L)c^2+L\|X\|_F^2.}
\tag{4}
\]

**Theorem.** There exists a fixed `delta>0`, depending only on the explicitly given matrices, such that for every `0<eta,epsilon<delta` and every Hermitian `A`,

\[
\boxed{-D^2H(P_{K_{\eta,\epsilon}})[A,A]
 \ge\tfrac12\mathcal M_{\eta,\epsilon}(A).}
\tag{5}
\]

The form (4) is positive definite on all sixteen real Hermitian dimensions when these parameters are sufficiently small. There is no comparison between the two parameters. In particular either may vanish faster than every power, or exponentially faster than the reciprocal, of the other.

The radius is effective: Section 7 gives a scalar condition involving finite polynomial bounds that selects one fixed delta. No numerical value of the guaranteed radius is asserted. The numerical samples at the end are separate from this theorem.

Unlike the adjacent-only metric, (4) keeps the exact atom-score shear. A uniform replacement of its first two terms by `||B_R||²/eta+||C_R||²/epsilon` is not claimed and does not follow from the argument.

## 2. Exactly which features fail, and which recover them

The six projection pair probabilities, in order `12,13,14,23,24,34`, are

\[
(q_{12},q_{13},q_{14},q_{23},q_{24},q_{34})
 =\tfrac19(1,1,4,1,1,1).
\]

Thus all projection atoms are positive and the law is not uniform within its count layer. The row norms of `V` are

\[
t=(2,1,1,2)/3,
\]

and those of `W` are `s=1-t`. The leading adjacent features are

\[
\ell_i^-(B)=v_i\operatorname{adj}(B)v_i^T,\qquad
\ell_i^+(C)=w_i\operatorname{adj}(C)w_i^T.
\tag{6}
\]

Because these rows are real, both maps vanish on `Y`. On real symmetric matrices they are injective: rows proportional to `(1,0),(0,1),(1,-1)` already recover the two diagonals and real off-diagonal. For either frame one may use the concrete inverse bound `||D||_F<=5||(ell_i(D))||_2` on `Sym(2)`. Hence each map has rank exactly three on `Herm(2)`. C084's hypothesis fails for both blocks, with no missing projection atom.

Every atom factors exactly as

\[
p_S=\eta^{(2-|S|)_+}\epsilon^{(|S|-2)_+}c_S(\eta,\epsilon),
\tag{7}
\]

where each `c_S` is a polynomial, positive at the origin. The leading values by layer are

\[
c_\varnothing=d,\quad c_{\{i\}}=t_i,\quad c_{\{i,j\}}=q_{ij},
\quad c_{[4]\setminus\{i\}}=s_i,\quad c_{[4]}=d,
\qquad d=\det H=\det J=3/4.
\tag{8}
\]

One elementary proof of (7) is the spectral Bernoulli expansion: a count below two requires enough missing occupied modes, and a count above two requires enough selected null modes. The leading singleton coefficient is `v_i adj(H)v_i^T=t_i`, since `v_iYv_i^T=0`; the other cases are immediate or follow by complementation. This uses the expansion of the fixed measured atom, not unitary invariance of measured entropy.

The two farther-layer atoms are especially simple:

\[
p_\varnothing=\eta^2d\det(I-\epsilon J),\qquad
p_{[4]}=\epsilon^2d\det(I-\eta H).
\tag{9}
\]

Their normalized first directional scores, on bounded directions with `B_R,C_R,X` tending to zero, satisfy

\[
\frac{\dot p_\varnothing}{\sqrt{p_\varnothing}}
 \longrightarrow-\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}H\,bY)}{\sqrt d}
 =\frac b{\sqrt d},
\quad
\frac{\dot p_{[4]}}{\sqrt{p_{[4]}}}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}J\,cY)}{\sqrt d}
 =-\frac c{\sqrt d}.
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently these atoms provide limiting Fisher information

\[
\boxed{F_0+F_4=\tfrac43(b^2+c^2)+o(\mathcal M).}
\tag{11}
\]

These are **first directional scores of second-occupation-order events**; no second derivative of a score is being substituted for Fisher information. The uniform meaning of the error is proved below.

## 3. Score shear and the sharper count-trace bounds

Here and below `C_*` denotes a finite constant depending only on (1)-(2), and may be enlarged finitely many times. All bounds hold on one sufficiently small fixed square; no constant depends on the rate ratio.

Polynomial differentiation of singleton atoms gives

\[
\dot p_i=-\ell_i^-(B_R)
 +O((\eta+\epsilon)\|B\|_F)
 +\eta O(\|C\|_F+\|X\|_F).
\tag{12}
\]

The triple formula interchanges `B,eta` and `C,epsilon`. To justify the crucial eta factor, at `eta=0` the two occupied eigenvalues are exactly one. Singleton atoms vanish identically under any null-block change or any unitary rotation. Every mixing direction is a tangent to such a rotation because `I-epsilon J` is invertible. Thus the C and X first-derivative coefficients vanish at eta zero, and polynomiality gives the factor. The complementary argument gives the epsilon factor for triples.

Since `p_i<=Pr(N_DPP=1)<=2eta`, Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the Euclidean norm of the singleton derivative vector by `sqrt(2eta F_1)`. Using the inverse bound following (6), and absorbing the small linear block errors in (12), yields

\[
\begin{split}
\|B_R\|_F\le C_*[&\sqrt\eta\,\sqrt{F_1}
 +\eta\sqrt\epsilon\,\sqrt{F_3}
 +(\eta+\epsilon)|b|+\eta|c|+\eta\|X\|_F],\\
\|C_R\|_F\le C_*[&\sqrt\epsilon\,\sqrt{F_3}
 +\epsilon\sqrt\eta\,\sqrt{F_1}
 +(\eta+\epsilon)|c|+\epsilon|b|+\epsilon\|X\|_F].
\end{split}
\tag{13}
\]

The self-blind shear `O(eta+epsilon)` need not have an individual eta or epsilon factor. This is why one must retain the exact `F_1,F_3` in the metric.

For the count acceleration, the traces have better bounds than (13). Set

\[
T=I-M=\eta H,\quad N_0=\epsilon J,\quad
u=\operatorname{tr}T,\ v=\det T,\ w=\operatorname{tr}N_0,\ z=\det N_0,
\]

and write `alpha=tr B,beta=tr C,d_B=tr(adj(T)B),d_C=tr(adj(N_0)C)`. The count-one probability is exactly

\[
a_1=u(1-w+z)+v(-2+3w-4z).
\]

Its derivative along `delta T=-B,delta N_0=C` is

\[
\dot a_1=-(1-w+z)\alpha-(-2+3w-4z)d_B
 +(-u+3v)\beta+(u-4v)d_C.
\]

Here `|d_B|<=C_*eta||B||`, `|d_C|<=C_*epsilon||C||`, and the coefficient of alpha is bounded away from zero. Grouping scores gives `|dot a_1|<=sqrt(a_1F_1)<=sqrt(2eta F_1)`. Complementation gives the analogous count-three bound. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\begin{split}
|\alpha|&\le C_*[\sqrt{\eta F_1}+\eta(\|B\|_F+\|C\|_F)],\\
|\beta|&\le C_*[\sqrt{\epsilon F_3}+\epsilon(\|B\|_F+\|C\|_F)].
\end{split}}
\tag{14}
\]

Using individual atom shear to bound these traces would lose precisely the rate factors needed below.

## 4. The exact logarithmic count acceleration

Define the spectral count polynomials `G_-=E(2-N_DPP)_+` and `G_+=E(N_DPP-2)_+`. Their difference is `2-tr K`, so their Hessians agree at every K. At a block diagonal base as above,

\[
G_-=u(1-w+z)+v(w-2z),\qquad
G_+=w(1-u+v)+z(u-2v).
\tag{15}
\]

Factoring every atom as (7) in the exact Shannon formula gives

\[
H''=-\mathrm{FI}+L D^2G_-+R_{\eta,\epsilon},\qquad
R_{\eta,\epsilon}=-\sum_S D^2p_S\log c_S.
\tag{16}
\]

This includes all sixteen atoms and both original terms `-FI-sum p'' log p`.

Differentiating (15), the diagonal-block contribution to `D²G_-` is exactly

\[
\begin{split}
2(w-2z)\det B+2(u-2v)\det C
 +2\alpha\beta-2\alpha d_C-2d_B\beta+4d_Bd_C.
\end{split}
\tag{17}
\]

There are no `BX` or `CX` terms: the base is invariant under the block sign `diag(I,-I)`, whereas X changes sign, and G is spectral. The pure-X form is

\[
D^2G_-[X,X]=-2\|X\|_F^2+O(\eta+\epsilon)\|X\|_F^2.
\tag{18}
\]

For completeness its coefficient at the projection follows from the count generating polynomial: its quadratic mixing term is `-t² z(z-1)²||X||²`; applying the weight `(2-k)_+` to its count coefficients gives `-2||X||²`. Polynomiality gives the displayed uniform error.

Crucially,

\[
\det(B_R+bY)=\det B_R-b^2,\qquad
w-2z=2\epsilon-\tfrac32\epsilon^2,
\tag{19}
\]

with the complementary identities for C. Thus the imaginary directions have the favorable singular terms

\[
-(4\epsilon-3\epsilon^2)Lb^2
 -(4\eta-3\eta^2)Lc^2.
\tag{20}
\]

These are retained even when `epsilon log(1/eta)` or `eta log(1/epsilon)` diverges.

Here is a uniform estimate for all remaining terms. Normalize `mathcal M(A)=1`. Equation (13) shows `B_R,C_R->0` uniformly, while `b,c` remain bounded and `||X||<=L^{-1/2}`. In particular B and C are uniformly bounded. From (14),

\[
|\alpha|\le C_*\sqrt\eta,\quad |\beta|\le C_*\sqrt\epsilon,
\quad |d_B|\le C_*\eta,\quad |d_C|\le C_*\epsilon.
\]

The mixed terms of (17), multiplied by L, are bounded by `C_*sqrt(eta epsilon)L`, tending to zero independently. For the potentially adverse real determinant terms, square (13) and use the actual metric weights to obtain

\[
\epsilon L\|B_R\|_F^2+\eta L\|C_R\|_F^2
\le C_*[\eta\epsilon L+(\eta+\epsilon)^2+\eta\epsilon].
\tag{21}
\]

For example, the self-blind term is bounded by
`epsilon L(eta+epsilon)²b²<=(eta+epsilon)²`; the other blind term obeys
`epsilon L eta²c²<=eta epsilon` because `eta L c²<=1`.
These estimates are valid regardless of the rate ratio. Equations (17)-(21) prove the one-sided bound

\[
L D^2G_-\le -4\epsilon Lb^2-4\eta Lc^2-2L\|X\|_F^2
 +\rho_1(\eta,\epsilon)\mathcal M(A),
\tag{22}
\]

where `rho_1->0` independently. The small coefficient differences in (20) are included in rho.

## 5. The finite blind acceleration is an exact negative form

For the pure blind direction `bVYV^T+cWYW^T`, the second atom derivatives at the projection are as follows. The middle-layer values listed are the full quadratic expressions, so their mixed signs are retained.

| Atom | `D²p_S(P)[A,A]` |
|---|---|
| empty | `-2b²` |
| singleton i | `2t_i b²` |
| 12, 13, 24, 34 | `-2(b+c)²/9` |
| 14 | `-8(b²-bc+c²)/9` |
| 23 | `-2(b²-4bc+c²)/9` |
| triple omitting i | `2s_i c²` |
| full | `-2c²` |

These identities follow directly from the column determinant for `p_S(P+tA)`. They can also be checked using the rational imaginary matrices `VYV^T` and `WYW^T`; no square roots are needed in their entries. Inserting the leading coefficients (8) gives the exact finite remainder

\[
\boxed{R_0(b,c)=2\log(3/4)(b^2+c^2)
 -\frac89\log2\,(b+c)^2.}
\tag{23}
\]

For instance, the diagonal coefficient apart from `2log d` is
`-2sum_i t_i log t_i+2sum_{i<j}q_ij log q_ij=-(8/9)log2`.
The mixed coefficient is `-(8/9)log4 bc`, arising from the different probability of pair 14. Thus (23) includes the exact signed cross-block acceleration, rather than bounding its separate atoms by absolute values.

Because `c_S` are positive analytic functions near the origin and atom Hessians are polynomial, `R_eta,epsilon` is a continuous quadratic form down to the origin after the logarithmic factors are removed. Under `mathcal M=1`, (13) and `||X||<=L^{-1/2}` imply

\[
R_{\eta,\epsilon}(A)=R_0(b,c)+o(1)
\tag{24}
\]

uniformly in the two rates. Equation (9), differentiated using block determinant derivatives, proves the same uniform assertion (11): the terms involving real blocks vanish by (13), and the opposite-block derivative is multiplied by eta or epsilon. The first derivative in X of either determinant is zero at the block diagonal base.

## 6. Closing all sixteen directions

Combining (11), (16), (22), and (24), and discarding only the nonnegative size-two Fisher information, gives

\[
\begin{split}
-H''\ge{}&F_1+F_3+\frac43(b^2+c^2)-R_0(b,c)\\
&+4\epsilon Lb^2+4\eta Lc^2+2L\|X\|_F^2
 -\rho(\eta,\epsilon)\mathcal M(A),
\end{split}
\tag{25}
\]

where `rho->0` independently. Since `R_0<=0`, the explicit main term is at least `mathcal M`. Choose one fixed delta so that `rho<=1/2` throughout its square. This proves (5).

To check definiteness of (4), if it vanishes then `b=c=X=0` and `F_1=F_3=0`. Equation (13) then gives `B_R=C_R=0`. Therefore no Hermitian direction is omitted. In particular this is a full ambient Hessian theorem at the centers (2), even though those centers form a two-parameter family. Each center also has a full-dimensional strict-concavity neighborhood by continuity of the interior Hessian.

The nonreal shapes matter concretely. If one instead chose `H=J=I`, the empty/full limiting scores on b,c would vanish, and (23) would reduce to `-(8/9)log2(b+c)²`. The mode `b=-c` would then have no gap from either of these mechanisms. Choosing `I+Y/2` recovers that mode with the two second-occupation scores while leaving the limiting projection and its noninjective adjacent features fixed.

## 7. Uniformity and effective domain

All `O` estimates above can be bounded by finitely many coefficients of explicit polynomials. One convenient combined bound, for some fixed finite `C>=1` and fixed `delta_0<=exp(-2)`, is

\[
\rho(\eta,\epsilon)\le C\left[
 \sqrt\eta+\sqrt\epsilon+L^{-1/2}
 +\sqrt{\eta\epsilon}L\right],\quad
0<\eta,\epsilon<\delta_0.
\tag{26}
\]

For the finite remainder, a coefficient bound times
`eta+epsilon+||B_R||+||C_R||+||X||` gives (26); (13) then supplies its first three terms. Equation (21) and the trace bounds supply the last term and smaller powers. This explicitly proves uniformity, rather than just convergence along each fixed-ratio path.

Here is a way to select the radius without presuming an entropy sign. On `0<=eta,epsilon<=1/3`, the spectral expansion gives `1/144<=c_S<=2`. For a middle atom retain the original rank-two spectral selection, whose weight is at least `1/16`, and use `q_S>=1/9`. For a singleton or triple retain its one-hole or one-insertion terms; the lower bound `1/48` already suffices. The endpoint determinants are at least `3/16` after their scale factors are removed. The upper bound for singleton/triple coefficients follows from the expected hole/insertion counts `2eta,2epsilon`; the other upper bounds are immediate.

Thus the logarithms and their parameter derivatives admit finite explicit bounds. For example the column-multilinearity bounds `|D^kp[A_1,...,A_k]|<=(4)_k product||A_j||_F` and `||H||_F,||J||_F<2` bound the required coefficients; division by eta in a singleton may be written as an integral of its eta derivative, and similarly for triples. Together with the inverse constant 5 after (6), these give `delta_0,C` in (26) using only finite polynomial norms and arithmetic. No lower bound on eta/epsilon enters this procedure.

For any such pair of constants, choose **any fixed**

\[
0<\delta<\delta_0\quad\text{such that}\quad
C\left[2\sqrt\delta+(2\log(1/\delta))^{-1/2}
 +2\delta\log(1/\delta)\right]\le\tfrac12.
\tag{27}
\]

Such a delta exists; for example, after taking C>=1 one may use `delta=min(delta_0/2,exp(-128C²))`. For `eta,epsilon<=delta<=exp(-2)`, monotonicity of `sqrt(x)log(1/x)` implies
`sqrt(eta epsilon)L<=2delta log(1/delta)`, and `L>=2log(1/delta)`. Hence (27) implies the theorem simultaneously at every point of the full open parameter square. This is the claimed quantified independent-rate domain; a practically useful numerical threshold remains uncomputed, not an unproved mathematical sign condition.

## 8. Verification and exact remaining scope

`computations/X093/derive.py` gives the exact projection matrices and all sixteen blind second atom derivatives. `computations/X093/verify.py` checks the orthonormal frame, all exact atom factorizations, the leading coefficients, normalization of the blind second derivatives, and identity (23). It then evaluates the full sixteen-direction affine Shannon Hessian and the exact metric (4) at 160-digit precision, with independent rates including `(10^-5,10^-60)` and its reversal. The smallest generalized eigenvalues of `-Hessian` relative to (4) range from approximately `0.9968269431` to `1` in the six samples. Those finite checks are diagnostics, not the proof of a uniform radius. Outputs are saved in `computations/X093/results.json`.

**Established:** (5), the positive sixteen-dimensional score metric (4), and its validity as both rates vanish independently at this concrete rank-three-feature projection. The new ingredients relative to C084 are farther-layer Fisher recovery, exact favorable determinant terms in the singular count acceleration, and the use of total-count scores to retain the required trace rate factors.

**Still open:** arbitrary noninjective real/complex projections, every choice of positive shapes at this same projection (including the scalar-shape joint blind mode), unrestricted approaches with moving shapes of unbounded condition number, and general Lyons concavity. There is no remaining conjectural scalar inequality for the stated family. The purely practical radius optimization in (27) is separate from these mathematical scope limitations.
