# X005 — integrated determinant barriers and Fisher compensation

Date: 2026-09-15. Worker assignment: hyperbolic barriers. **Completed bounded theorem and exact certificate.** No general proof of Lyons entropy concavity is asserted.

**Main result.** Let `R` be the unit-diagonal correlation matrix of the odds matrix `L=K(I-K)^(-1)`. If `A` is positive or negative semidefinite of rank `r>=2`, then

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge
\left[1+\frac{r-1}{r}\log\det R\right]\operatorname{FI}_K(A).
}
\tag{M}
\]

In particular, `det R >= exp(-r/(r-1))` implies the complete entropy-curvature sign in that direction. The stronger statement replaces `-log det R` by the smallest oscillation of `-log p` after subtraction of a modular table. The proof is an exact decomposition into trace-free barrier squares after modular cancellation; it does not require a pointwise resolvent sign. The condition allows arbitrary positive diagonal odds scalings, zero edges, complex phases, and noncommuting directions. It is a theorem about semidefinite directions, not the unrestricted Hermitian Hessian.

## 1. Centered inverse-barrier identity

Let `0<K<I`, let `A=A*`, and put

\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1},\quad W=(I+L)^{-1}=I-K,
\quad B=W^{-1}AW^{-1}.
\]

For a subset `S`, let `E_S` be the coordinate embedding and set

\[
R_S=E_S(L_S)^{-1}E_S^*,\qquad R_\varnothing=0.
\]

The principal-minor partition identity gives

\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det(I+L)},\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb E_p R_S=W.}
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, differentiate `sum_S det L_S=det(I+L)` in an arbitrary Hermitian direction and divide by the partition function. Along the **affine kernel path** `K+tA`, direct inverse differentiation yields

\[
L'=B,\qquad L''=2BWB.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
(\log p_S)'=\operatorname{tr}((R_S-W)B),\qquad
(\log p_S)''=-\operatorname{tr}(((R_S-W)B)^2).
}
\tag{2}
\]

For the second formula, the four matrix terms are

\[
2\operatorname{tr}(R_S BWB)-\operatorname{tr}(R_SB R_SB)
-\operatorname{tr}(WBWB),
\]

which equal the displayed centered square by cyclicity of trace. This calculation retains the odds-path acceleration; dropping `L''` would give the wrong result.

Writing `s_S=tr((R_S-W)B)` and `q_S=tr(((R_S-W)B)^2)`, normalization gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_p s_S^2=\mathbb E_p q_S=\operatorname{FI}_K(A).}
\tag{3}
\]

The random quantity `q_S` can have either sign for indefinite `B`; equation (3) only gives a sign after averaging. The full curvature is

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)
=\mathbb E_p\{s_S^2+(s_S^2-q_S)\log p_S\}.}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus a barrier argument must control an **entropy-weighted defect of an exact averaged square identity**. Ordinary pointwise self-concordance on the positive definite cone does not itself supply that control.

There is a useful projection interpretation. `P_S=L^{1/2}R_SL^{1/2}` is the orthogonal projection onto the span of `{L^{1/2}e_i:i in S}`, and (1) says `E_p P_S=K`. This follows by conjugating (1), since `L` commutes with `W`.

## 2. A product-reference expansion that integrates the barrier first

The construction below develops a compensated polynomial certificate, rather than requiring sign of the scalar or matrix resolvent density at each integration parameter. The failures in previous workers C005 and C046 therefore do not invalidate the construction.

Set `d_i=K_ii`, `v_i=d_i(1-d_i)`, let `mu` be the product law with marginals `d_i`, and let `V=diag(v_i)`. Define

\[
C=V^{-1/4}(K-\operatorname{diag}d)V^{-1/4},\qquad
D=V^{-1/4}AV^{-1/4}.
\]

In particular, `C_ii=0`. The reference `mu`, `V`, and the functions

\[
Z_i(x)=\frac{x_i-d_i}{\sqrt{v_i}},\qquad
Z_T=\prod_{i\in T} Z_i
\]

are frozen while taking derivatives in `K+tA`. The functions `Z_T` are orthonormal under `mu`. Expansion of the centered occupation moments gives the exact likelihood polynomial

\[
\frac{p_{K+tA}(x)}{\mu(x)}
=\sum_{T\subseteq[n]}\det(C_T+tD_T)Z_T(x).
\tag{5}
\]

The empty term is one; at `t=0` the singleton terms vanish. To verify (5), note that

\[
\mathbb E_{K+tA}\prod_{i\in T}(X_i-d_i)
=\det((K-\operatorname{diag}d+tA)_T),
\]

by principal-minor expansion and the determinantal inclusion moments. These are exactly the orthonormal coefficients of the likelihood ratio.

Write `y=p_K/mu-1`, `g=(p'/mu)`, and `h=(p''/mu)`. Since the one-site marginals of the kernel path are affine, `sum p'' log mu=0`, and hence

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)=\mathbb E_\mu\frac{g^2}{1+y}
+\mathbb E_\mu h\log(1+y).}
\tag{6}
\]

The scalar log-barrier remainder is the already-integrated identity

\[
\log(1+y)=y-\int_0^1\frac{s y^2}{1+s y}\,ds.
\tag{7}
\]

It holds for every `y>-1` and explicitly allows cancellation between the Fisher term, the exact polynomial term `E h y`, and the integrated remainder. Section 7 records a rigorous conditional bound from this representation. The completed sign theorem uses a stronger positive-energy structure available in semidefinite directions.

## 3. Hyperbolic directions provide positive trace-free energy

Let `Q_S=K-I_{S^c}`. The atom determinant identity and ordinary differentiation give

\[
p_S=(-1)^{|S^c|}\det Q_S,\quad
s_S=\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}A),\quad
q_S=\operatorname{tr}((Q_S^{-1}A)^2),\quad
p''_S=p_S(s_S^2-q_S).
\tag{8}
\]

These agree with Section 1. All `Q_S` are invertible at a strict contraction. For example, split present and absent coordinates: the present block is positive definite, and the absent Schur complement is negative definite because `I-K>0`.

Assume first that `A>=0` has rank `r>=1`. On the range of `A`, define the Hermitian matrix

\[
T_S=A^{1/2}Q_S^{-1}A^{1/2}.
\]

Its `r` real eigenvalues, including possible zeros, will be denoted by `lambda_{S,1},...,lambda_{S,r}`. Cyclicity of trace gives

\[
s_S=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_{S,j},\qquad
q_S=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_{S,j}^2.
\]

Hence the trace-free energy is nonnegative:

\[
\boxed{
v_S=q_S-\frac{s_S^2}{r}
=\operatorname{tr}\left(T_S-\frac{\operatorname{tr}T_S}{r}I_r\right)^2
=\frac1r\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{S,i}-\lambda_{S,j})^2\ge0.
}
\tag{9}
\]

The same result holds for `A<=0`, since replacing `A` by `-A` changes the signs of the eigenvalues and score but leaves all squared quantities unchanged.

This is the exact hyperbolic input: each atom polynomial `p_S(K+tA)/p_S(K)=det(I+t Q_S^{-1}A)` has only real zeros when it is nonconstant. Conversely, the argument below also applies to an arbitrary rank-`r` Hermitian direction whenever **all** these atom polynomials are real-rooted. Indeed, the nonzero eigenvalues of `Q_S^{-1}A` are then real, and the scalar trace identity (9) still applies. No claim is made that every Hermitian direction has this property.

Normalization and affine one-site marginals give more than the scalar identity (3):

\[
\mathbb E_p(s_S^2-q_S)=0,\qquad
\mathbb E_p[X_i(s_S^2-q_S)]=0\quad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{10}
\]

Equivalently, for every modular table `m(S)=b+sum_{i in S} a_i`,

\[
\mathbb E_p[m(S)(s_S^2-q_S)]=0.
\tag{11}
\]

Write `J=FI_K(A)=E_p s_S^2` and `c=(r-1)/r`. For `r>=2`,

\[
\mathbb E_p v_S=cJ.
\tag{12}
\]

For a nonzero semidefinite direction `J>0`: equation (3) and `q_S>=0` imply
`J>=p_[n] tr((K^(-1)A)^2)>0`. For a general real-rooted direction with `J=0`, all `q_S` and `s_S` vanish, so `H''=0` and the conclusions remain valid without division by `J`.

## 4. Exact compensated square decomposition

Let `f_0(S)=-log p_S`. Choose any modular table `m` and any constant shift such that

\[
0\le f(S):=f_0(S)-m(S)\le\Delta.
\tag{13}
\]

Such a choice always exists, for example by subtracting the minimum of `f_0`. Because of (11), the complete entropy curvature is

\[
-H''=J+\mathbb E_p[f(q-s^2)]
=J+\mathbb E_p[fv]-c\mathbb E_p[fs^2].
\]

Adding and subtracting `c Delta J` gives the promised exact identity:

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)
=(1-c\Delta)J
+\mathbb E_p[fv]
+c\,\mathbb E_p[(\Delta-f)s^2].
}
\tag{14}
\]

All terms after the first are nonnegative by (9) and (13). Therefore

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)\ge(1-c\Delta)\operatorname{FI}_K(A).}
\tag{15}
\]

This is a complete curvature certificate with the Fisher term included. It is not a frozen-log sign statement. The energy `v` is an explicit sum of real squares, and the two logarithmic weights have been combined after modular cancellation.

Define the **modular oscillation**

\[
\Delta_{\rm mod}(p)
=\inf_{a\in\mathbb R^n}
\left\{\max_S\left[-\log p_S-\sum_{i\in S}a_i\right]
-\min_S\left[-\log p_S-\sum_{i\in S}a_i\right]\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]

The minimum is attained because this is a finite-dimensional best approximation to the closed space of modular tables. Alternatively, the result follows directly by taking a minimizing sequence in (15), so attainment is not needed for the theorem. We have established:

**Modular-spread theorem.** For every strict kernel `K`, and every semidefinite rank-`r` direction with `r>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge
\left[1-\frac{r-1}{r}\Delta_{\rm mod}(p_K)\right]\operatorname{FI}_K(A).
}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus `Delta_mod<=r/(r-1)` suffices for the sign. If the inequality is strict and `A` is nonzero semidefinite, the entropy curvature is strictly negative. When `r=1`, separate affine dependence of every atom gives the familiar exact formula `-H''=FI` without a spread restriction.

### 4.1 An integrated identity between genuine probability laws

For `J>0`, define

\[
\nu_s(S)=\frac{p_Ss_S^2}{J},\qquad
\nu_v(S)=\frac{p_Sv_S}{cJ}.
\tag{18}
\]

They are nonnegative probability laws. Equations (10) and (12) show that they have exactly the same one-site marginals. Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{-H''}{J}=1+c\left(\mathbb E_{\nu_v}f_0-\mathbb E_{\nu_s}f_0\right).
}
\tag{19}
\]

Since their marginals agree, any modular table can be subtracted inside the difference. For a representative satisfying (13), the layer-cake formula gives the genuinely integrated compensation identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{-H''}{J}
=1+c\int_0^\Delta
\left[\nu_v\{f>u\}-\nu_s\{f>u\}\right]du.
}
\tag{20}
\]

There is no claim that the integrand has a fixed sign. Its integrated negative contribution is at most `Delta`, proving (15). This is distinct from the failed positive-resolvent proposals in C005 and C046.

For context, finite linear-programming duality identifies (16) with

\[
\Delta_{\rm mod}(p)
=\max_{\substack{\pi,\rho\ {m probability\ laws}\\
\mathbb E_\pi X_i=\mathbb E_\rho X_i\ \forall i}}
\left(\mathbb E_\pi f_0-\mathbb E_\rho f_0\right).
\tag{21}
\]

To verify the dual, maximize `f_0 dot pi-f_0 dot rho` subject to two unit-mass constraints and the equal-marginal constraints. Eliminating the two scalar dual variables leaves exactly `max(f_0-a dot X)-min(f_0-a dot X)`. The main theorem only needs the elementary upper bound in (21), which follows directly by subtracting `a dot X` from the two expectations.

## 5. A field-invariant determinant condition

Let

\[
D_L=\operatorname{diag}(L_{11},\ldots,L_{nn}),\qquad
R=D_L^{-1/2}LD_L^{-1/2}.
\]

Then `R>0` has diagonal one. The exact atom logarithm is

\[
-\log p_S
=\log\det(I+L)-\sum_{i\in S}\log L_{ii}
-\log\det R_S.
\tag{22}
\]

The first two terms are modular. Every principal determinant satisfies

\[
0<\det R\le\det R_S\le1.
\tag{23}
\]

The upper bound is Hadamard's inequality. For the lower bound, split `R` into `S` and its complement. Its positive Schur complement has diagonal entries at most one, so its determinant is at most one. Thus `det R=det R_S det(R/R_S)<=det R_S`. This also covers the empty and full sets by the usual empty-determinant convention.

Consequently one may take in (14)

\[
f(S)=-\log\det R_S,\qquad\Delta=-\log\det R.
\tag{24}
\]

This proves the displayed main result (M), and the exact nonnegative-square certificate is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''={}&[1+c\log\det R]J\\
&+\sum_Sp_S[-\log\det R_S]\,
\frac1r\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{S,i}-\lambda_{S,j})^2\\
&+c\sum_Sp_S\log\!\left(\frac{\det R_S}{\det R}\right)s_S^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{25}
\]

**Determinant-correlation corollary.** If

\[
\det R\ge\exp[-r/(r-1)],
\tag{26}
\]

then entropy is concave to second order in every positive or negative semidefinite direction of rank at most `r`. In dimension `n>=2`, the single condition `det R>=exp[-n/(n-1)]` therefore covers all semidefinite directions. The stronger but dimension-independent condition `det R>=e^{-1}` also suffices.

Positive diagonal odds scaling `L -> E L E`, with `E>0` diagonal, leaves `R` unchanged. It also changes `-log p` by a modular table, so `Delta_mod` itself is invariant. The condition therefore allows arbitrary external fields, including very small atom probabilities. It imposes no nonzero-edge hypothesis and no restriction on complex cycle phases. It is not an affine-odds-path claim: all derivatives above are along `K+tA`, with the odds acceleration from Section 1 included.

There is a finite-segment consequence with an explicit scope. If `K(t)=K_0+tA` is a strict affine segment, `A` is semidefinite of rank `r`, and (26) holds for its odds correlation matrix at every `t`, then `H(P_{K(t)})` is concave on that segment by integration of the Hessian inequality. Endpoint membership alone has not been shown to imply the condition at intermediate points, so no convexity of the determinant-correlation region is asserted.

## 6. Exact four-site example with complex phases and strong fields

Take

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}

1/3&1&1/5&i/6\\
-i/4&1/5&1&1/7\\
0&-i/6&1/7&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
E=\operatorname{diag}(1/5,2,5,1/3),
\]

\[
L=ERE,\qquad K=L(I+L)^{-1},\qquad
A=I+uu^*,\quad u=(1,i,2,-1)^T.
\tag{27}
\]

The absolute off-diagonal row sums of `R` are `7/12,7/10,83/140,13/42`, all below one. Thus `R>0`, `L>0`, and `0<K<I`. The matrix `A` is positive definite of rank four and does not commute with `K`.

Exact arithmetic gives

\[
\det R=\frac{520901}{705600},\qquad
-\log\det R<\frac1{\det R}-1
=\frac{184699}{520901}.
\]

Therefore (M) yields the entirely rational lower certificate

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)>
\frac{1529507}{2083604}\operatorname{FI}_K(A)>0.
}
\tag{28}
\]

## 7. A rigorous remaining lead for arbitrary Hermitian directions

The following conditional certificate is retained from Section 2 because it keeps the complete Hessian and does not assume real-rooted atoms. It is not asserted to hold with a nonnegative right-hand side universally.

Suppose `eta=||y||_infinity<1`, where the product-reference likelihood `1+y` is defined there. Let

\[
J_\mu=\mathbb E_\mu g^2,\quad
U_\mu=\mathbb E_\mu hy,\quad
Y=\|y\|_{L^2(\mu)},\quad B_2=\|h\|_{L^2(\mu)},
\]

and define `beta(0)=0` and, for `0<eta<1`,

\[
\beta(\eta)=\frac{-\log(1-\eta)-\eta}{\eta}.
\]

Since

\[
|\log(1+y)-y|\le\beta(\eta)|y|,
\]

the exact identity (6), positivity of `1+y`, and Cauchy-Schwarz give

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge\frac{J_\mu}{1+\eta}+U_\mu-\beta(\eta)B_2Y.
}
\tag{29}
\]

For completeness, the scalar bound follows from (7): for `|y|<=eta`,
`|log(1+y)-y|/|y| <= eta integral_0^1 s/(1-s eta) ds=beta(eta)`.

All the polynomial quantities in (29) can be obtained without logarithms of atoms. If

\[
c_T=\det C_T,\quad a_T=D\det(C_T)[D_T],\quad
b_T=D^2\det(C_T)[D_T,D_T],
\]

with the empty and singleton coefficients treated as in (5), orthonormality gives

\[
J_\mu=\sum_{T\ne\varnothing}a_T^2,\quad
U_\mu=\sum_{|T|\ge2}c_Tb_T,\quad
Y^2=\sum_{|T|\ge2}c_T^2,\quad
B_2^2=\sum_{|T|\ge2}b_T^2.
\tag{30}
\]

An inequality forcing the right-hand side of (29) to be nonnegative for a new broad class would complete an unrestricted-direction result on that class. The covariance-only obstruction cautions against discarding the higher determinant features in (30). No such general polynomial domination is claimed here, and no previously established generic weak-link corridor is presented as a new theorem of this worker.

## 8. Exact remaining gap and source verification

The completed theorem is (17), its exact integrated identity (20), and the explicit determinant-correlation corollary (26). Formula (25) is a full Fisher-compensated square decomposition on its stated hyperbolic-direction domain.

Two limitations remain:

1. Even for semidefinite directions, if `Delta_mod>r/(r-1)`, the proof must additionally show that the two positive terms in (14) compensate its negative first coefficient. The precise sufficient and necessary inequality within this representation is
   `E_p[fv]+c E_p[(Delta-f)s^2] >= (c Delta-1) FI`.
   This additional determinantal-energy comparison is not proved.
2. For arbitrary indefinite directions, `v_S` may be negative. At `K=I_2/2` and `A=[[0,1],[1,0]]`, a mixed occupation atom is `1/4+t^2`, which has nonreal zeros; its logarithm has positive second derivative at zero. Thus ordinary positive-cone self-concordance does not apply atomwise to all affine kernel directions. Formula (29) remains valid in that regime, but its universal sign is open.
