# X035 — A missing projection coordinate at independent endpoint rates

**Proved restricted full-Hessian theorem, 15 September 2026.** No general
Lyons claim or historical priority is asserted. All identities below have
self-contained determinant proofs; the finite algebra checks are separate.

## 1. New stratum and theorem

Fix `0<q<1`, put `r=1−q`, and use the orthonormal frame

    u=(√q,√r,0),   f=(−√r,√q,0),   g=(0,0,1).

Fix a Hermitian insertion shape `J=[[j,k],[conj(k),l]]>0` with `k≠0`.
A measured diagonal gauge on site 3 makes `k>0` real without changing any
atom probability. Set `d=jl−k²>0`, `p=1−η`, and

    Kη,ε = p uu* + ε [f,g] J [f,g]*,       η,ε>0.

The matrix converges to a rank-one projection with exactly one missing
measured coordinate. Its three spectral endpoint gaps are `η, ελ1(J),
ελ2(J)`. No relation between `η` and `ε` is imposed. Express an arbitrary
Hermitian direction in the indicated frame as

    A = [[a,conj(x),conj(y)], [x,s,z], [y,conj(z),t]],
    a,s,t real;  x,y,z complex.

This frame is only a calculation device: the law is always measured in the
original three coordinates. Put `L=log(1/η)+log(1/ε)` and

    λ = log(jl/d) > 0,
    Mη,ε(A)=a²/η+(s²+t²)/ε+L|x|²+|z|²+ε|y|².               (1)

**Theorem.** There exist `c,δ>0`, depending only on `q,J`, such that every
`0<η,ε<δ` gives a strict contraction and

    D²H(P_Kη,ε)[A,A] ≤ −c Mη,ε(A) < 0       for every A≠0.    (2)

The constants are uniform when `q,r,j,l,d,k` remain in compact subsets of
their indicated positive ranges. In particular, (2) handles arbitrary mixed
inertia and both complex directions left uncontrolled by the leading Fisher
and logarithmic terms.
It includes rates such as `η=exp(−1/ε²)` and their reversals.

Overlap is limited and explicit. ROOT_PROJECTION_BOUNDARY requires full
projection support and comparable endpoint rates. C084 requires full
projection support and injective adjacent features, both false here. C080
treats a four-site nonprojection face with a surviving active block. C058
allows weak singleton attachments near a fixed strict core and singleton
density; it supplies no uniform radius for this collapsing core and density.

## 2. All eight probabilities and the exact logarithmic grouping

Write `qi=q,r` for `i=1,2`. Direct two-by-two minors and the full determinant
give the following exact formulas:

    p∅   = η c0,                     c0=1−ε(j+l)+ε²d,
    p3   = ηε c3,                    c3=l−εd,
    p12  = ε c12,                    c12=p(j−εd),
    pi3  = ε ci3,                    ci3=p qi l−ε(qi−η)d,
    pi   = ci,                       ci=p qi(1−εl)−ε(qi−η)(j−εd),
    p123 = ε² c123,                  c123=p d.                (3)

All eight coefficients `cS` are positive at `(η,ε)=(0,0)`, and hence are
bounded above and away from zero on a sufficiently small closed rectangle.
The extra valuation `ηε` of the missing singleton must be retained.

Let `R=p∅+p3=P(Y1=Y2=0)`. The exponent of `η` in (3) sums to this probability;
the exponent of `ε` sums to `p3+Σpairs pS+2p123=tr K−1+R`.
The exact marginal determinant is

    R=det(I−K)12=η(1−εj),
    R'=−(1−εj)a−ηs,                 R''=2(as−|x|²).           (4)

Consequently the complete Hessian, including every hidden second derivative,
is exactly

    H''[A,A]=−FI(A)+2L(as−|x|²)+Cη,ε(A),
    Cη,ε(A)=−ΣS pS''[A,A] log cS.                             (5)

The form `Cη,ε` is real analytic at the closed corner. Estimating the empty
and missing-singleton logarithms separately would destroy (4) and produce
spurious uncontrolled terms. No probability-acceleration term was dropped.

## 3. Three coarse Fisher features, with no weak-mode contamination

For any event of probability `v`, Cauchy–Schwarz gives
`FI≥(v')²/[v(1−v)]`. Also `FI≥(p12')²/p12`. Taking the average of these
three valid bounds, using the events in (4) and `Y3=1`, yields

    FI ≥ (1/3){ (R')²/[R(1−R)] +(p12')²/p12
                         + t²/[εl(1−εl)] },                 (6)

because the third measured marginal is exactly `εl`, with derivative `t`.
The remaining first derivative is exactly

    p12'=p(1−εl)s−pεj t+2pεk Re z+ε(j−εd)a.                 (7)

None of these features contains `x` or `y`. Use the invertible scaling

    a=√η α, s=√ε σ, t=√ε τ,
    x=χ/√L, z=ζ, y=Y/√ε.                                   (8)

Equations (4), (6), and (7) show, as a one-sided operator estimate,

    FI ≥ (1/3)(α²+σ²/j+τ²/l)−o(1)‖P‖²,                     (9)

where `‖P‖²=α²+σ²+τ²+|χ|²+|ζ|²+|Y|²`. The error tends to zero independently
in `η,ε`. Indeed the normalized `R` score is `−α+O(η+ε)α−O(√(ηε))σ`,
the normalized `p12` score is `σ/√j+O(η+ε)σ+O(ε)τ+
O(√ε)Re ζ+O(√(ηε))α`, and the third score is `τ/√l+O(ε)τ`.

## 4. Finite compensation on the two invisible complex modes

The two crucial restrictions of the bounded form in (5) are

    C0,0(z)=−2λ|z|²,
    limε→0 C0,ε(y)/ε=−2jλ|y|².                              (10)

The second mode is weaker than a bounded gap: its correct metric is `ε|y|²`.
Here are exact jets and cancellations proving (10) and all required mixed
coefficient bounds, without inferring them from a scale grid.

For a pure `y` direction, `pS''/(2|y|²)`, in the order
`∅,3,12,13,23,1,2,123`, is

    (−(1−εj), 1−εj, εj, εj−q, εj−r,
                                  q−εj, r−εj, −εj).         (11)

Thus `C(y)=−2Bη,ε|y|²`, where

    Bη,ε=(1−εj)log(c3/c0)
          +Σi(qi−εj)log(ci/ci3)+εj log(c12/c123).             (12)

At `ε=0`, `ci/ci3=1/l`, so `Bη,0=0` for every small `η`.
Differentiating the positive coefficients in (3) gives

    ∂ε Bη,0 = jλ + η k²/[(1−η)l].                            (13)

In detail, the derivatives of the first log ratio and the two middle ratios
are respectively `l+k²/l` and `−l−(qi−η)k²/[(1−η)qi l]`;
substituting these into (12) proves (13). Hence `C(y)/ε` extends analytically
to the corner and has the claimed strictly negative limit.

For a pure `z` direction at `ε=0`, the same ordered jet vector is

    (−η, η, p, q−η, r−η, −(q−η), −(r−η), −p).               (14)

Substitution in (3) gives exactly `Cη,0(z)=−2pλ|z|²`, proving the first
part of (10). This finite logarithm is the determinant deficit
`log[jl/(jl−k²)]`, which vanishes precisely when the weak singleton disconnects.

For completeness, put `mij=det Kij`. Their second derivatives are
`mij''=2det Aij`, and every atom second derivative follows by inclusion–
exclusion from these and `D=p123''/2`, where

    D=p(st−|z|²)+εj(at−|y|²)+εl(as−|x|²)
                                      +2εk Re(x conj(y)−az). (15)

This also follows immediately from `D=tr(K adj A)` in the fixed frame.
The physical entries satisfy `Ai3=ui conj(y)+fi z` and
`Aii=qi a+(1−qi)s+2ui fi Re x`.
It follows that there are no `y`–`a,s,t` terms in `C`, and every `y`–`x`
coefficient contains `ε`. At `ε=0`, `y`–`z` terms occur only in `mi3''`;
their log coefficient is `log(ci/ci3)=−log l`, independent of `i`.
They cancel since `Σi ui fi=0`. Thus the `y`–`z` coefficients also contain
`ε`, analytically and uniformly in `η`.

All other coefficients of `C` are bounded. Applying (8), (10), and these
divisibilities therefore proves the genuine uniform operator limit

    Cη,ε(A)=−2λ|ζ|²−2jλ|Y|²+o(1)‖P‖².                     (16)

For example the mixed `y,z` term becomes `O(√ε)|Yζ|`, and the mixed `y,x`
term becomes `O(√(ε/L))|Yχ|`. The pure `y` error is `O(η+ε)|Y|²`.
This is the compensation that a leading-feature rank computation misses.

## 5. Uniform closure and precise remaining scope

Under (8), the growing term of (5) is

    −2|χ|²+2√(ηε)L ασ.

Its cross coefficient tends to zero independently because
`√(ηε)(log(1/η)+log(1/ε))≤√η log(1/η)+√ε log(1/ε)→0`.
Combining (9) and (16) gives

    H'' ≤ −α²/3−σ²/(3j)−τ²/(3l)−2|χ|²
                                      −2λ|ζ|²−2jλ|Y|²
                                      +o(1)‖P‖².            (17)

Every principal coefficient is strictly positive. Choose `δ` so the last
operator error is at most half their minimum. Since `‖P‖²=Mη,ε(A)`, this
proves (2). Positivity of the atom coefficients and their derivative bounds
on compact parameter rectangles proves the stated uniformity. More precisely,
the final operator error is bounded, for some finite shape-dependent `C0`, by

    C0[√η+√ε+L^(−1/2)+√(ηε)L] ‖P‖².                       (18)

This follows from the normalized score bounds after (9), the analytic
divisibilities after (15), and the displayed logarithmic cross coefficient.
Every term vanishes independently. One may take `c` equal to half the minimum
of `1/3,1/(3j),1/(3l),2,2λ,2jλ` after choosing `δ` to absorb (18).
For sufficiently small rates, `Mη,ε(A)≥(ε/2)‖A‖F²`, giving an ordinary
Frobenius Hessian gap of order `ε`. Complementation, `H(P_(I−K))=H(P_K)`,
gives the identical result at rank-two projections whose hole vector has
one missing coordinate. At each
strict centre, continuity also supplies a full-dimensional open Hessian-
negative neighborhood; no uniform radius for those ambient balls is claimed.

The proof covers all nine real directions: three Fisher normals, two real
components of logarithmic `x`, two of finite `z`, and two of weak `y`.
It leaves open `k→0`, a second missing projection coordinate, unbounded
condition numbers inside the insertion shape, and arbitrary interior
three-site kernels. At `k=0` the centre has a disconnected coordinate block,
and the positive-definite metric (1) cannot survive: off-block Hessian
directions have zero curvature there. Resolving simultaneous collapse of
that coupling requires a further quartic estimate, not a smaller fixed
constant in the present theorem.
