## 3. An exact support test and a new perfect-square phase inequality

The following lemma treats arbitrary linear combinations of the four phase coordinates, not only single harmonic cubes.

**Lemma 2 (exact affine support test).** For real constants \(C,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\varepsilon\), the inequality

\[
C+\alpha a+\beta b+\gamma c+\varepsilon t\ge0
\tag{19}
\]

holds for all unit triples in (6) if and only if \(C\ge|\alpha|\) and

\[
(\alpha^2+3\varepsilon^2)a^2
+2(C\alpha-\beta\gamma)a
+C^2-\beta^2-\gamma^2-3\varepsilon^2\ge0
\quad(-1\le a\le1).
\tag{20}
\]

For rational coefficients, (20) is an exact rational test: check the two endpoints and the quadratic's minimizer when it lies in the interval.

**Proof.** Fix \(n_0,n_1\) with dot product \(a\). The terms depending on \(n_2\) are its dot product with

\[
\gamma n_0+\beta n_1+\sqrt3\varepsilon(n_0\times n_1).
\]

Their minimum over unit \(n_2\) is

\[
-\sqrt{\beta^2+\gamma^2+2\beta\gamma a+3\varepsilon^2(1-a^2)}.
\]

Consequently (19) is equivalent to this square root being at most \(C+\alpha a\) for every \(a\in[-1,1]\). The right side must be nonnegative on the interval, which is exactly \(C\ge|\alpha|\). Squaring is then equivalent and gives (20). Every \(a\) in the interval occurs. ∎

Here is the inequality that closes the finite coupling construction. It is valid over the larger symbolic range \(|\lambda|\le1\), with \(\delta^2=1-\lambda^2\):

\[
\boxed{
3+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}
+\left(1+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)a
+(1+2\lambda)b-(1-2\lambda)c
\ \ge\ |\delta t|.}
\tag{21}
\]

For either sign of the last term, the quadratic (20) becomes exactly

\[
\boxed{\left(2-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)^2(a+1)^2.}
\tag{22}
\]

Also \(C-|\alpha|=2\). Thus Lemma 2 proves (21), including every equality case, with no numerical bound.

Equivalently, define

\[
v=(2\lambda-1)n_0+(1+2\lambda)n_1
\pm\sqrt3\delta(n_0\times n_1).
\]

Then (22) is the exact identity

\[
\left[3+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}
+\left(1+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)a\right]^2-\|v\|^2
=\left(2-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\right)^2(a+1)^2.
\tag{23}
\]

The coefficient in square brackets is at least two, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives (21) directly. This is the phase-sensitive positivity certificate used below.

For comparison, the transversal-middle inequality (14) has support quadratic

\[
(1-\lambda^2)(4-\lambda^2)(a-1)^2,
\tag{24}
\]

and its corresponding \(C-|\alpha|\) is again two. Thus both families of phase inequalities have exact perfect-square proofs.

## 7. Supermodular consequence and the exact remaining gap

For every supermodular set function \(f\), (31) proves

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_W}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+U}}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+u}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+v}}f
=\sum_{A,B}X_{A,B}(p)\,\Delta_{j_1}\Delta_{j_2}f(A)\ge0.
\tag{35}
\]

This is the mixed projection-diamond inequality used by the entropy strategy. In particular, the usual diamond deduction gives entropy concavity along the fixed-frame affine paths

\[
K(s)=P_W+\ell(s)uu^*+m(s)vv^*,
\qquad \ell,m\in[0,1],\qquad \ell'm'\le0.
\tag{36}
\]

Indeed the atom law is bilinear in \(\ell,m\), with second derivative along the path equal to \(2\ell'm'\) times the four-corner signed law in (35). DPP atom log-submodularity makes its pairing with \(\log p\) nonpositive, by summing the elementary diamonds in (31). For a strict kernel this follows directly from \(p_S=\det L_S/\det(I+L)\), \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\): after eliminating \(L_A\), the positive two-by-two Schur complement has determinant at most the product of its diagonal entries. Hence \(p_Ap_{Aij}\le p_{Ai}p_{Aj}\). Regularizing a boundary kernel and passing to positive atoms gives the same inequality there. Both the Fisher term and the acceleration term in the exact entropy Hessian are then nonpositive. On boundary-supported faces, the positive mixture weights make every positively weighted diamond have four positive atom probabilities; continuity handles parameter endpoints. This is the same fixed-frame consequence used in C035/C039, now for the newly certified flags.

The theorem does **not** extend to arbitrary scalar \(T\in U(3)\), although the affine support test and the perfect-square inequality (21) do have their stated wider domains. In particular, all couplings in the finite certificate use the one fixed rational scalar rotation (2); the symbolic inequalities alone are not an all-angle coupling proof.

For a general orthogonal decomposition \(\mathbb C^6=W\oplus U\oplus Z\), the row restrictions of the three planes need not have the common two-component frame structure \(\alpha_rV_r,\beta_rV_r,\gamma_rV_r\) in (1). Its full probability table therefore does not reduce to the four invariants (6). The exact remaining universal gap is still a nonnegative solution of (4) for such arbitrary flags, equivalently all five-dimensional harmonic transport support inequalities of C038. The present construction settles a fully supported complex family outside the previously certified phase-frame/Fourier classes, but not that universal gap.
