## 1. An affine physical heat flow and its full Hessian equation

Let `F(K)=H(P_K)`, `C=2K-I`, and let `L` be the generator of independent bit
flips at rate `1/2` per coordinate on probability columns. Fourier inversion of
`E prod_(i in T)(2X_i-1)=det C_T` shows exactly that

    P_(I/2+exp(-s)(K-I/2))=exp(sL)P_K.

This is an affine map of physical kernels. Both affine atom sensitivities move
by the same classical semigroup, so the full `p'' log p` term is retained.
For every unordered cube edge `{x,y}`, put

    D(p)=(1/2) sum_edges (p_x-p_y)log(p_x/p_y).

Direct differentiation gives `dH(exp(sL)p)/ds=D(exp(sL)p)`. In kernel
coordinates let `V(K)=I/2-K`, and write `Q(K)=-D²F(K)` as the full real
Hermitian Hessian tensor. Twice differentiating `V·DF=D(P_K)` gives

    V·DQ=2Q-D²_K D(P_K).                                  (A)

Consequently, for every fixed Hermitian A,

    Q(K)[A,A]=integral_0^infinity exp(-2s) M(K_s)[A,A] ds,
    M(J)=D²_J D(P_J),  K_s=I/2+exp(-s)(K-I/2).             (B)

The terminal term vanishes since F is smooth at I/2. Formula (B) is a positive
kernel representation with an as-yet unsigned complete forcing. Establishing
its nonnegative sign, or a compensating matrix bound sufficient for its
integral, would prove concavity. Classical convexity of D as a function of the
probability table alone is insufficient: its composition with the determinant
law also contains `sum_x (partial D/partial p_x) p''_x`.

Here are all second jets behind this assertion. For `K(t)=K+tA`, write
`p=P_K`, `v=Dp[A]`, `a=D²p[A,A]`. Because the kernel heat map is affine,

    p_s=exp(sL)p,   v_s=exp(sL)v,   a_s=exp(sL)a,
    v_s=Dp_(K_s)[exp(-s)A],
    a_s=D²p_(K_s)[exp(-s)A,exp(-s)A].                    (C)

Thus `exp(-2s)Q(K_s)[A,A]=sum v_s²/p_s+sum a_s log p_s` is the
full physical pulled-back curvature. Nothing replaces the second atom jet.
The Fourier proof above also proves the semigroup identity for every finite
affine parameter t: each degree-|T| principal minor of `2K(t)-I` is multiplied
by `exp(-s|T|)`, the eigenvalue of the Boolean noise channel on that character.

More generally, independent replacement of bit i at rate gamma_i by a fair
bit gives the affine physical flow

    K_s=D_s(K-I/2)D_s+I/2,
    D_s=diag(exp(-gamma_i s/2)).                          (D)

The uniform rate one case is enough for the theorem below. This is actual
coordinate noise, with neither a changing measured basis nor an odds path.

## 2. The positive square and the complete signed forcing

Let `M(K)=D²_K D(P_K)`. For each cube edge `{x,y}` put
`u=p_x`, `w=p_y`, and use the actual affine jets v,a. Direct differentiation
of `(u-w)log(u/w)` yields the exact formula

    M[A,A]=(1/2) sum_edges {
      (u+w)(v_x/u-v_y/w)²
      +a_x[log(u/w)+1-w/u]
      +a_y[log(w/u)+1-u/w] }.                            (E)

The first line is positive. Both terms on the second line are necessary.
Indeed the Hessian of the scalar two-variable function is
`(u+w)(du/u-dw/w)²`; its gradient has the two displayed logarithmic entries.
This proves (E) without appealing to an unverified composition principle.

For example, with conditional pivot probability q and marginal mass m, the
corresponding dissipation is `m g(q)`, where

    g(q)=(q-1/2)log(q/(1-q)),
    g''(q)=1/[2q²(1-q)²].                               (F)

The second derivative still includes
`m''g+2m'g'q'+mg'q''` in addition to `mg''(q')²`.
Positive conditional curvature alone therefore does not settle (E).

Set `E(K)=-V·D(P_K)`, the second entropy dissipation, and `N=D²_K E`.
The scalar E also has a complete finite-table formula:

    E=sum_x (Lp)_x²/p_x+sum_x (L²p)_x log p_x.            (G)

This follows by differentiating `D=-sum (Lp)log p` once along the heat flow.
There is no claim that the Hessian N inherits the scalar sign of E.
Differentiating the vector field, whose derivative is `-I`, gives

    Q'=2Q-M,       M'=2M-N.                              (H)

Primes in (H) mean evaluation along K_s in a **fixed physical tangent basis**.
This is a transport equation in parameter space. The probability table obeys
the forward finite-state heat equation; a backward parabolic maximum principle
in kernel coordinates does not follow merely from that fact.

## 3. Exact six-variable evolution with the moving angular optimizer

Assume every edge of the strict three-site base is nonzero. Along K_s its
phase remains constant. Choose once and for all the six-dimensional real
space consisting of real diagonal entries and entrywise radial edge motions,
and its three-dimensional angular complement. These are fixed vector spaces
along this particular noise orbit. In these fixed coordinates write

    Q=[[R,B],[B^T,P]],   Y=-P^(-1)B^T,
    X=[I_6;Y],          S=R-BP^(-1)B^T=X^TQX.            (I)

X053 proves `P>0` at every finite time. Its general three-site question is
exactly `S>=0`. The entries here can be obtained from X053's explicit blocks
by rescaling the edge-coordinate basis; no new angular sign is assumed.
Block (H) and differentiation of (I) give

    S'=2S-X^T M X,
    Y'=P^(-1) Z,   Z=M_AR+M_AA Y.                       (J)

The first equation is the envelope derivative: `QX=[S;0]`, so derivatives
of X cancel there. At second order they no longer cancel. Precisely,

    (d/ds-2)² S=X^T N X-2 Z^T P^(-1) Z.                (K)

**Proof.** Differentiate `PY=-B^T`, substitute `P'=2P-M_AA` and
`(B^T)'=2B^T-M_AR`, and obtain the second equation of (J). Next,
`d(X^TMX)/ds=X^TM'X+2Z^TP^(-1)Z`. Substitute `M'=2M-N` into
the derivative of the first equation of (J), then use that equation again.
This gives (K). Every block is a full measured-entropy Hessian block. The
square in (K) records the changing optimal angular velocity.

For a base with a real loop after diagonal gauge, real and imaginary
directions decouple by complex conjugation. Along the real noise orbit,
`B=M_AR=0`, hence `Y=Z=0`. In particular the Riccati square cannot help on
that already real six-dimensional locus. Section 4 gives a strict example
there for which both `M_RR` and `N_RR` have unfavorable directions.

These identities also give exact integrated sufficient targets:

    S(K)=integral_0^infinity exp(-2s) X_s^T M_s X_s ds,   (L)
    S(K)=integral_0^infinity s exp(-2s)
          [X_s^T N_s X_s-2Z_s^TP_s^(-1)Z_s] ds.         (M)

For completeness, the endpoint terms vanish. Near the center put
`d_i=Kii-1/2`, `z_ij=Kij`. Direct expansion of the finite entropy table gives

    3log2-H=2 sum d_i²+(4/3)sum d_i^4
                         +8 sum_(i<j)|z_ij|^4+O(||K-I/2||^6).

Particle-hole complementation removes odd total degrees. The mixed quartic
terms cancel in the Taylor expansion of `(1+u)log(1+u)`. This yields
`P_s=exp(-2s)P_*+O(exp(-4s))`, with `P_*>0` when every edge is present,
and `B_s=O(exp(-4s))` in the fixed radial/angular basis. Hence S and its
first derivative remain bounded; integration of (J) and (K) after multiplying
by `exp(-2s)` proves (L)-(M). The integrated forms allow signed forcing.
Neither is claimed positive universally.

## 4. A rational family obstructs every finite dissipation hierarchy

Consider the real symmetric matrices

    K0=[[9/40,1/10,1/40],
        [1/10,2/25,1/10],
        [1/40,1/10,9/40]],
    A =[[0,-2/5,-1],
        [-2/5,-8/25,-2/5],
        [-1,-2/5,0]],
    K_e=K0+e I,   0<e<67/100.                           (N)

The spectrum of K0 is `0,1/5,33/100`; its null vector is
`(-2/5,1,-2/5)`. The matrices commute, A has the same null vector, and its
other eigenvalues are `1,-33/25`. Thus K_e is a strict physical contraction
and A is a mixed-inertia rank-two physical direction. Every base edge is
nonzero. The full atom has exactly

    p_111=e(e+1/5)(e+33/100),
    p'_111=e[33/500-(8/25)e],
    p''_111=-(66/25)e.                                  (O)

Every other atom has a strictly positive limit at e=0; this follows either
from their exact determinant polynomials or from the positive principal
one- and two-site minors and `I-K0>0`. Thus every nonfull atom and its
entropy contribution is smooth near this boundary point.

Write `q(K)=Q(K)[A,A]`, with A fixed. More locally along the heat orbit of
K_e, let x be its null-mode eigenvalue and let f be the product of its other
two eigenvalues. Since A annihilates the same null vector throughout,

    p_111=x f,  p'_111=x g,  p''_111=x h,
    h=-66/25,
    q_111=x[g²/f+h log x+h log f].                       (P)

Here f,g and all nonfull-atom terms are smooth as x tends to zero, with
f bounded away from zero. Moreover `x'=1/2-x`. Consequently

    q(K_e)=O(1),
    M(K_e)[A,A]=(33/25)log e+O(1) -> -infinity,           (Q)
    N(K_e)[A,A]=-33/(50e)+O(|log e|) -> -infinity.        (R)

For (Q), use `M=2Q-Q'`; only the term `x h log x` has a singular first
derivative, namely `h(1/2-x)log x+O(1)`. For (R), differentiate that
expression once more and use `N=(2-d/ds)M`. The ordinary O(1) term is smooth
apart from terms `x log x`, so its derivative is at worst logarithmic.
These calculations concern the actual fixed-coordinate Shannon entropy;
commutation was used only to simplify the single determinant in (O).

In particular **there is no universal constant c such that `M+cQ>=0`**:
evaluate on A and let e tend to zero. The same conclusion holds with a
coefficient c(K) bounded on this family. Because the base and A are real,
the angular optimizer is zero, so this also disproves such a constant
zeroth-order compensation for the six-variable forcing in (J).

The failure persists through every finite iterated dissipation. Define

    D_0=-H,     D_(m+1)=-V·D_m,
    M_m=D²D_m=(2-d/ds)^m Q,   m>=0.                     (S)

Thus M_0=Q, M_1=M, M_2=N. For every fixed integer m>=2, (P) gives

    M_m(K_e)[A,A]
       =-[33(m-2)!/(25*2^(m-1))] e^(1-m)
          +o(e^(1-m)) <0                               (T)

for sufficiently small positive e. Indeed the leading singularity comes
from differentiating `h x log x` m times; terms with fewer derivatives are
lower order, `d^m(x log x)/dx^m=(-1)^m(m-2)!x^(1-m)`, and x' tends to 1/2.
The sign from `(2-d/ds)^m` cancels this alternating sign, leaving h<0.
All other atom contributions are smooth. Formula (Q) handles m=1.

Hence no finite hierarchy whose highest iterated-dissipation Hessian has
a fixed positive coefficient can become pointwise positive by adding bounded
lower-order coefficients along this family. This is a failure of a proposed
proof certificate, **not an entropy-concavity counterexample**. The curvature
is favorable at the exact strict sample below, and this direction is already
inside the established rank-two indefinite scope (e.g. X040).
