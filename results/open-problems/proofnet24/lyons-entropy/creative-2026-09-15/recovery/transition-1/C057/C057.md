# C057 — exact conditional-acceleration cancellation and a balanced site-extension theorem

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **COMPLETE bounded creative proof and exact certificates.** Full Lyons entropy concavity remains open. This work uses no subagents, external messages, pushes, or changes to other workers' files.

The main new construction is a site-induction identity that retains both nonlinear Schur-complement accelerations. Their entire contribution, including the derivatives of the conditional weights, collapses to a single gradient-secant pairing. Completing a matrix square turns this identity into a sufficient induction rule using only lower-dimensional entropy Hessians and gradients. A new positive-secant lemma makes the rule unconditional for a concrete family of three-site kernels, with curvature in **all nine real Hermitian directions**. A rational example proves that the collapsed remainder has no universal sign.

The existing two-site Hessian bound from C055 is an input, and is not claimed as new. The explicit ball below is another proved interior region, not a proof of the whole three-site problem. C031's coherent-pair endpoint theorem, C028's weak-link corridors, and the root's fixed two-dimensional-support theorem are acknowledged. The construction neither drops the conditional accelerations nor rotates measured entropy as if it were spectral entropy.

## 1. The cancellation valid in every dimension

Write a strict contraction and an arbitrary Hermitian direction as

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&z^*\\z&B\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&w^*\\w&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad b=1-a,\quad q=ab.
\tag{1}
\]

The site indexed by the first row is measured in the original occupation basis. The conditional kernels on the remaining sites are

\[
F_1=B-\frac{zz^*}{a},\qquad
F_0=B+\frac{zz^*}{b}.
\tag{2}
\]

They are strict contractions. For example, conditioning on inclusion takes a Schur complement in `K`, and conditioning on exclusion takes the complementary Schur complement in `I-K`. The entropy chain rule is

\[
H(K)=h(a)+aH(F_1)+bH(F_0).
\tag{3}
\]

Use the real Hermitian pairing `⟨U,V⟩=tr(UV)` and define

\[
G=\nabla H(F_1)-\nabla H(F_0).
\tag{4}
\]

Along the affine path `K+tA`, put

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_1=F_1'&=C-\frac{wz^*+zw^*}{a}+\frac{\alpha zz^*}{a^2},\\
E_0=F_0'&=C+\frac{wz^*+zw^*}{b}+\frac{\alpha zz^*}{b^2},\\
r_1&=w-\alpha z/a,\qquad r_0=w+\alpha z/b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

**Lemma 1 (complete acceleration cancellation).** The second derivatives and the full entropy Hessian satisfy

\[
F_1''=-\frac2a r_1r_1^*,\qquad
F_0''=\frac2b r_0r_0^*,
\tag{6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=aD^2H(F_1)[E_1,E_1]+bD^2H(F_0)[E_0,E_0]
-\frac{\alpha^2}{q}
+2\langle G,\alpha C-ww^*\rangle.}
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Direct differentiation of (2) gives (5)–(6). The four first-gradient terms obtained by differentiating (3) twice are

\[
2\alpha\langle\nabla H(F_1),E_1\rangle
-2\alpha\langle\nabla H(F_0),E_0\rangle
-2\langle\nabla H(F_1),r_1r_1^*\rangle
+2\langle\nabla H(F_0),r_0r_0^*\rangle.
\]

The two matrix identities

\[
\alpha E_1-r_1r_1^*=\alpha C-ww^*,\qquad
-\alpha E_0+r_0r_0^*=-(\alpha C-ww^*)
\tag{8}
\]

cancel every term containing `z`. The binary entropy contributes `−α²/q`, giving (7). No acceleration is discarded. ∎

In particular, when the pivot marginal is fixed (`α=0`), the entire remainder outside the two conditional Hessians is exactly

\[
\boxed{-2w^*Gw.}
\tag{9}
\]

Thus `G≽0` is necessary and sufficient for this remainder to be nonpositive for every incident velocity `w`. Ordinary concavity on the lower-dimensional kernel space does not imply this Loewner sign; Section 6 gives a rational obstruction.

## 2. A sufficient induction rule, with the remainder signed by a square

The following statement is dimension-independent. It assumes only lower-dimensional information at the two actual conditional kernels, rather than the full lower-dimensional conjecture.

**Theorem 2 (conditional gradient-secant induction).** Suppose a number `γ>0` satisfies

\[
D^2H(F_i)[T,T]\le-\gamma\|T\|_F^2
\quad(i=0,1)
\tag{10}
\]

for every Hermitian `T`. Set

\[
D=F_0-F_1=\frac{zz^*}{q},\qquad
\eta=\frac1q+2\langle G,D\rangle-\frac{\|G\|_F^2}{\gamma}.
\tag{11}
\]

If `G≽0` and `η≥0`, then the full entropy Hessian at `K` is nonpositive. If `G≻0` and `η>0`, it is negative definite. More precisely, with

\[
\dot D=\frac{wz^*+zw^*}{q}
+\frac{\alpha(a-b)}{q^2}zz^*,
\tag{12}
\]

one has the complete square bound

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le{}&
-\gamma\left\|C+\alpha D-\frac{\alpha G}{\gamma}\right\|_F^2
-\eta\alpha^2\\
&-\gamma q\|\dot D\|_F^2-2w^*Gw.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** Put `M=C+αD`. Equations (5) give the exact centering identities

\[
E_1=M-b\dot D,\qquad E_0=M+a\dot D,
\]

and therefore

\[
a\|E_1\|_F^2+b\|E_0\|_F^2
=\|M\|_F^2+q\|\dot D\|_F^2.
\tag{14}
\]

Apply (10) to (7), replace `C` by `M−αD` in the gradient pairing, and complete the square in `M`. This gives (13). Every term is nonpositive under the stated assumptions. In the strict case, equality forces `α=0` and `w=0`, after which the first square forces `C=0`. ∎

The less expensive sufficient test

\[
G\succeq0,\qquad
\|G\|_F^2\le\gamma/q,
\tag{15}
\]

works whenever `⟨G,D⟩≥0`; this last inequality in fact follows from rank-one entropy concavity, because the laws along `F1+sD` are affine probability vectors. The sharper quantity (11) retains that secant contribution. The theorem controls nonzero `α`, arbitrary `C`, and arbitrary `w`; it is not limited to a fixed pivot marginal or a common scalar cross block.

## 3. A new positive gradient-secant lemma on balanced symmetric pairs

Let

\[
v_+=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)^T,\qquad
v_-=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)^T,\qquad
P_+=v_+v_+^*,\quad P_-=v_-v_-^*,
\]

and define the actual measured two-site kernel

\[
F(u,v)=uP_++vP_-.
\tag{16}
\]

Its two eigenvectors have equal measured coordinate magnitudes. Its four occupation probabilities, rather than a unitary-invariance assertion, give

\[
p_{00}=(1-u)(1-v),\quad
p_{11}=uv,\quad
p_{10}=p_{01}=\frac{w(u,v)}2,
\quad w(u,v)=u+v-2uv.
\tag{17}
\]

Write `ℋ(u,v)=H(F(u,v))` and

\[
L(u,v)=\log\frac{w(u,v)^2}{4uv(1-u)(1-v)}\ge0.
\tag{18}
\]

Differentiating (17) gives the exact identities

\[
-\mathcal H_{uu}
=\frac{1-v}{1-u}+\frac vu+\frac{(1-2v)^2}{w},
\qquad
\mathcal H_{uv}=L-\frac1w.
\tag{19}
\]

**Lemma 3 (balanced collective-mode secant).** Suppose

\[
\frac14\le u_-<u_+\le\frac34,\qquad
\frac14\le v\le\frac34,
\qquad \delta=u_+-u_->0.
\tag{20}
\]

Then

\[
\nabla H(F(u_-,v))-\nabla H(F(u_+,v))
=g_+P_++g_-P_-,
\tag{21}
\]

where the following **uniform, dimensionless** estimates hold:

\[
\boxed{
2\delta\le g_+\le4\delta,\qquad
\frac4{15}\delta\le g_-\le\frac83\delta.}
\tag{22}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
G\succeq\frac{4\delta}{15}I_2,\qquad
\|G\|_F^2\le\frac{208}{9}\delta^2,\qquad
\langle G,\delta P_+\rangle\ge2\delta^2.}
\tag{23}
\]

**Proof.** Swap symmetry and conjugation symmetry at the real kernel (16) force its Hermitian entropy gradient to be a real linear combination of `P+` and `P−`. Its coefficients are exactly `ℋu` and `ℋv`, by differentiating along those two projectors. Thus

\[
g_+=\int_{u_-}^{u_+}-\mathcal H_{uu}(s,v)\,ds,
\qquad
g_-=\int_{u_-}^{u_+}-\mathcal H_{uv}(s,v)\,ds.
\tag{24}
\]

Throughout the square `[1/4,3/4]²`,

\[
\frac38\le w\le\frac58,\quad
uv(1-u)(1-v)\ge\frac9{256},\quad
0\le L\le2\log(5/3)\le\frac43.
\tag{25}
\]

The bounds for `w` follow by evaluating its bilinear expression at the four corners. The logarithm bound follows from a ratio at most `25/9` and `log(1+x)≤x`. Therefore

\[
\frac4{15}=\frac85-\frac43
\le-\mathcal H_{uv}=\frac1w-L\le\frac83.
\tag{26}
\]

For `−ℋuu`, the first two terms of (19) sum to at most `10/3`: the expression is affine in `v` and convex in `u`, so its maximum on the square occurs at a corner. The last term is at most `(1/4)/(3/8)=2/3`. This proves the upper bound `−ℋuu≤4`.

For the lower bound, the law (17) is affine in `u`, so `−ℋuu` is its Fisher information. The count has derivative of its mean equal to one and variance `u(1−u)+v(1−v)≤1/2`. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the count score therefore gives `−ℋuu≥2`. Equivalently this inequality follows directly from (19). Integrating proves (22). The two orthogonal projectors have Frobenius norm one, so (23) follows. ∎

The sign in (26) is a measured entropy statement. It is stronger than ordinary concavity along the moving rank-one mode because it also controls the entropy gradient in the transverse collective mode. It is precisely the extra sign needed by (9) and (13).

## 4. An unconditional three-site extension family

Consider kernels of the form

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
a&\sqrt\rho\,v_+^*\\
\sqrt\rho\,v_+&uP_++vP_-
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
0<a<1,\quad \rho>0.
\tag{27}
\]

Put

\[
q=a(1-a),\quad
u_-=u-\rho/a,\quad
u_+=u+\rho/(1-a),\quad
\delta=\rho/q=u_+-u_-,
\]

\[
\gamma=\min\{(u_--v)^2,(u_+-v)^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]

**Theorem 4 (balanced symmetric site extension).** Assume (20), `γ>0`, and

\[
\boxed{208\rho^2\le9\gamma q.}
\tag{29}
\]

Then `K` is a strict contraction and its entropy Hessian is negative definite in **every complex Hermitian direction**, without restrictions on the changing pivot marginal, either incident cross entry, or the lower principal block. A quantitative bound is (13), with

\[
G\succeq\frac{4\delta}{15}I_2,\qquad
\eta\ge\frac1q+4\delta^2-\frac{208\delta^2}{9\gamma}
\ge4\delta^2>0.
\tag{30}
\]

**Proof.** The conditional kernels are exactly `F1=F(u−,v)` and `F0=F(u+,v)`. Their strict contraction bounds, together with `0<a<1`, imply strict positivity of both `K` and `I−K` by Schur complementation.

The proved two-site Hessian estimate C055, equation (7), is

\[
-D^2H_2(F)[T,T]\ge4|F_{12}|^2\|T\|_F^2
\tag{31}
\]

for every Hermitian `T`. Since the two conditional off-diagonal entries are `(u−−v)/2` and `(u+−v)/2`, this supplies exactly (10) with (28). Lemma 3 supplies (23), and (29) is equivalent to `208δ²/9≤γ/q`. Equations (11) and (23) give (30); Theorem 2 proves the claim. ∎

Coordinate permutations and diagonal unitary changes preserve the statement, as they preserve the measured occupation law. The perturbation direction need not preserve the symmetry of the base kernel. Negative definiteness at each base point supplies a full-dimensional neighborhood by continuity; the next section makes one such neighborhood explicit.

## 5. A rational center and an explicit full-dimensional convex ball

Take

\[
\boxed{
K_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/10&1/10\\
1/10&1/2&1/5\\

\tag{32}

The parameters are

\[
a=1/2,\quad\rho=1/50,\quad u=7/10,\quad v=3/10,

\[
q=1/4,\quad\delta=2/25,\quad\gamma=81/625,
\quad 9\gamma q-208\rho^2=\frac{521}{2500}>0.
\tag{33}
\]

All entries of the kernel and its conditional kernels are rational. The gradient-secant eigenvalues are approximately `0.244182462196515` and `0.0860607019130302`; these displays are not the proof of their signs.

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K_*)[A,A]\le-\frac1{50}\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{34}
\]

Furthermore, the entire nine-real-dimensional closed ball

\[
\boxed{\mathcal B=\{K=K^*: \|K-K_*\|_F\le10^{-7}\}}
\tag{35}
\]

consists of strict contractions and satisfies

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\frac1{100}\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{36}
\]

Consequently, for any `K,J∈𝓑` and `t∈[0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
H((1-t)K+tJ)\ge(1-t)H(K)+tH(J)
+\frac{t(1-t)}{200}\|J-K\|_F^2.}
\tag{37}
\]

**Point-gap proof.** With `E=D−G/γ`, (23) gives

\[

because `sqrt(208)/3<5`. The rational lower bound in (30) is

\tag{38}

For `X=C+αE`,

\[

Since `γ≥1/25` and `η≥33/50`, the first two negative terms of (13) dominate `(‖C‖F²+α²)/50`. Also

\[
2w^*Gw\ge\frac{8\delta}{15}\|w\|^2
=\frac{16}{375}\|w\|^2\ge\frac1{25}\|w\|^2.

Discarding the remaining nonnegative square proves (34), since `‖A‖F²=α²+2‖w‖²+‖C‖F²`.

**Ball proof.** Gershgorin gives `K*≽I/5` and `I−K*≽I/5`. Its eight exact atom probabilities have minimum `91/1000`. For a strict contraction with spectral margins at least `τ`, every signed atom matrix `Q=K−I_{Sᶜ}` has inverse norm at most `1/τ`. One proof splits `Q−λI` into its positive present block and negative absent Schur complement; for `|λ|<τ`, this shows that no eigenvalue can lie in `(-τ,τ)`.

For `‖K−K*‖F≤10⁻⁷`, the margins exceed `1/10`, so every such inverse has operator norm at most `10`. The determinant-ratio estimate from the center gives

p_S(K)\ge\frac{91}{1000}(1-5\cdot10^{-7})^3>\frac1{20}.

For three arbitrary Hermitian unit directions, the normalized absolute first, second, and third atom derivatives are bounded respectively by

These follow directly from the differentiated determinant and trace Cauchy–Schwarz; for example, the second bound is `(3+1)10²`, and the third is `(3^{3/2}+3sqrt(3)+2)10³`.

The variation of a unit-direction Hessian from the center is therefore less than `74000·10⁻⁷=0.0074`. Combining with (34) proves (36), with room to spare. Integration along a segment in the convex ball gives (37). ∎

## 6. A rational obstruction to an unconditional remainder sign

The assumptions involving `G` cannot simply be removed. Take

\[
\widetilde K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/10&0\\
1/10&11/50&1/10\\
0&1/10&4/5
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\widetilde A=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{42}
\]

The leading principal minors of `K̃` are `(1/2,1/10,3/40)`, and those of `I−K̃` are `(1/2,19/50,71/1000)`. Thus it is a strict contraction and small affine perturbations in either sign are feasible. Here `α=0`, `C=0`, `z=(1/10,0)ᵀ`, `w=(0,1)ᵀ`, and

\[
F_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&1/10\\1/10&4/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
F_0=\begin{pmatrix}6/25&1/10\\1/10&4/5\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{43}
\]

The relevant gradient entry is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{22}={}&\frac15\log(1/3)+\frac45\log(3/13)\\
&-\frac6{25}\log(29/91)-\frac{19}{25}\log(71/309)\\
={}&\frac1{25}\log\frac ND,
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]

where

\[
N=3^{34}7^6 103^{19},\qquad
D=13^{14}29^6 71^{19}.
\]

The exact integer difference is

\[
D-N=
5498050420673646735604190142408463302595378867725283418312>0.
\tag{45}
\]

Therefore `G22<0`, and the entire collapsed remainder in (7) is

\[
\boxed{-2w^*Gw=\frac2{25}\log(D/N)>0.}
\tag{46}
\]

It is approximately `0.0012683330203728`. The positive sign is proved by (45), not by this decimal value. In this example the conditional weight derivatives vanish; the offending contribution comes entirely from the two opposite nonlinear conditional accelerations.

This is **not** an entropy-concavity counterexample. The two conditional Hessians supply additional negative curvature, and the direction is inside the previously proved incident-row/column family. The example specifically refutes dropping the acceleration remainder, asserting that it always helps, or inferring `G≽0` from lower-dimensional entropy concavity alone.
