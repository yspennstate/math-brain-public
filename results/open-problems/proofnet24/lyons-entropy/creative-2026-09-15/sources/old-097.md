## 1. The reconstructed theorem

Let \(K=K^*\) satisfy \(0<K<I_3\), and let \(P_K\) be its determinantal subset law on the three original point coordinates. Write
\[
H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq[3]}p_S(K)\log p_S(K),
\qquad L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0.
\]
Logarithms are natural, and every Hessian is along an affine Hermitian direction in \(K\), not in \(L\).

Put \(\ell_i=L_{ii}\), and normalize the edges as
\[
a=\frac{L_{12}}{\sqrt{\ell_1\ell_2}},\qquad
b=\frac{L_{23}}{\sqrt{\ell_2\ell_3}},\qquad
c=\frac{L_{31}}{\sqrt{\ell_3\ell_1}}.
\]
If
\[
\frac1{10}\le |a|,|b|,|c|\le\frac{11}{100},
\tag{1}
\]
then every Hermitian \(A\) satisfies the retained bound
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le
-\frac{3173}{2400}(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
-\frac3{4000}\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
In particular the Hessian is negative definite throughout this region. Concavity follows on every convex subset of the region; no convexity of the entire normalized-edge region is asserted.

A concrete full-dimensional convex subset is
\[
\mathcal B=\left\{K=K^*:
\|K-K_0\|_F<\frac1{200000}\right\},
\tag{3}
\]
where
\[
K_0=\frac1{7933846}
\begin{pmatrix}
3945082&208000+11130i&-11024-210000i\\
208000-11130i&3944871&212000+10920i\\
-11024+210000i&212000-10920i&3944662
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a ball in all nine real coordinates of Hermitian \(3\times3\) matrices. Every point of its closed ball also remains an interior contraction and satisfies (1). The center is genuinely complex in the point-coordinate sense: its associated \(L\) has a nonreal cycle product, invariant under diagonal unitary changes of coordinate phases.

Consequently, for \(K,J\in\mathcal B\) and \(0<t<1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
H((1-t)K+tJ)\ge{}&(1-t)H(K)+tH(J)\\
&+\frac{t(1-t)}2
\left[
\frac{3173}{2400}\{\operatorname{tr}(J-K)\}^2
+\frac3{4000}\|J-K\|_F^2
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

## 2. Exact atom, Fisher, and frozen-log identities

For \(S\subseteq[3]\), set
\[
Q_S=K-I_{S^c},\qquad
p_S(K)=(-1)^{3-|S|}\det Q_S
=\det(I-K)\det L_S>0.
\tag{6}
\]
These atom identities follow by expanding the probability generating determinant
\(\det(I-K+KZ)\), with \(Z\) diagonal. All atoms are positive in the interior; all displayed atom inverses therefore exist.

The score in direction \(A=A^*\) is
\[
s_S[A]=D\log p_S(K)[A]=\operatorname{tr}(Q_S^{-1}A).
\tag{7}
\]
It is real. Normalization gives \(\mathbb E s_X[A]=0\), and
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)=\sum_Sp_Ss_S[A]^2
=\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

Freeze \(f(S)=-\log p_S(K)\) at the base \(K\), and let
\[
f(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}c_S,\qquad
F_f(J)=\mathbb E_{P_J}f(X)=\sum_Sc_S\det J_S.
\tag{9}
\]
Twice differentiating entropy, using \(\sum_S D^2p_S[A,A]=0\), gives
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\mathrm{FI}_K(A)+D^2F_f(K)[A,A].
\tag{10}
\]

The determinant inequality
\[
\det L_{T\cup\{i,j\}}\det L_T
\le
\det L_{T\cup\{i\}}\det L_{T\cup\{j\}}
\]
follows from the positive \(2\times2\) Schur complement of \(L_T\).
Taking negative logarithms proves that \(f\) is supermodular.
On three sites this says exactly
\[
c_{ij}\ge0,\qquad c_{ij}+c_{123}\ge0
\quad (ij=12,13,23).
\tag{11}
\]

Set
\[
D_0=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12}),\qquad
\tau=c_{123},\qquad M=D_0+\tau K.
\tag{12}
\]
Then \(M\ge0\). For \(\tau\ge0\) this is immediate from (11) and \(K\ge0\). For \(\tau<0\),
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23}+\tau,c_{13}+\tau,c_{12}+\tau)
+(-\tau)(I-K)\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]

For arbitrary Hermitian \(A\), not only a gauge direction,
\[
D^2\det K_{ij}[A_{ij},A_{ij}]=2\det A_{ij},\qquad
D^2\det K[A,A]=2\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}A).
\]
The second identity is the coefficient of \(t^2\) in the cubic
\(\det(K+tA)\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\mathrm{FI}_K(A)
+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).}
\tag{14}
\]
The paired coefficient \(c_{ij}\) is placed on the complementary diagonal entry of \(M\); this indexing is essential.

## 3. The spectral estimate and the count Fisher bound

Suppose \(mI\le M\le uI\). Put
\[
\alpha=\frac{m+u}{2},\qquad
\eta=\frac{3m-u}{2}.
\]
Writing \(M=\alpha I+E\), we have
\(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le(u-m)/2\).
For eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) of the Hermitian matrix \(A\),
\[
\|\operatorname{adj}A\|_*
=|\lambda_1\lambda_2|+|\lambda_1\lambda_3|+|\lambda_2\lambda_3|
\le\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2+\lambda_3^2=\|A\|_F^2.
\]
The inequality is the sum of the three inequalities
\(2|\lambda_i\lambda_j|\le\lambda_i^2+\lambda_j^2\).
Also \(2\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}A)
=(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\|A\|_F^2\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
&\le \alpha\bigl((\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\|A\|_F^2\bigr)
 +(u-m)\|A\|_F^2\\
&=\alpha(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\eta\|A\|_F^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
This estimate itself does not require \(u<3m\); that condition makes \(\eta>0\).

The DPP count has
\[
\mathbb E|X|=\operatorname{tr}K,\qquad
v:=\operatorname{Var}|X|=\operatorname{tr}(K-K^2)\le\frac34.
\tag{16}
\]
For completeness, its covariance formula is
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf1_{i\in X})=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}),\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf1_{i\in X},\mathbf1_{j\in X})=-|K_{ij}|^2.
\]
Summing these identities gives (16), and the spectral upper bound uses
\(\lambda(1-\lambda)\le1/4\).

Differentiation of the count mean gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}A
=\mathbb E\bigl[(|X|-\mathbb E|X|)s_X[A]\bigr].
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and \(v>0\) yield
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2}{v}
\ge\frac43(\operatorname{tr}A)^2.
\tag{17}
\]
Combining (14), (15), and (17),
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le
-\left(\frac43-\alpha\right)(\operatorname{tr}A)^2
-\eta\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{18}
\]

Therefore

\frac{789}{1789}I\le K_0=L_0(I+L_0)^{-1}

## 6. Which interior cases have \(M>0\)?

If \(\tau\ne0\), (12) or (13) immediately gives \(M>0\), since \(K\) and \(I-K\) are positive definite.

If \(\tau=0\), then \(M=D_0\). A zero \(c_{ij}\) is exactly a zero normalized edge of \(L\). Equation \(\tau=0\) in (19) is
\[
2\operatorname{Re}(abc)=xy+xz+yz-xyz.
\tag{30}
\]
If, for example, \(a=0\), then (30) forces \(yz=0\). A zero edge forces another zero edge. Hence either all three edges are nonzero and \(M>0\), or \(L\), and therefore \(K\), is a block sum of a two-site kernel and a singleton, or is diagonal.

It follows that
\[
\boxed{M>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the three-site kernel is connected in the point coordinates}.}
\tag{31}
\]
Here connected means that no nontrivial coordinate partition makes \(K\) block diagonal. This is equivalent for \(K\) and \(L\), since each is a matrix function of the other.

The singular cases are already nonpositive-curvature cases. For a \(2+1\) block sum, (14) and the block atom score show that the Hessian is the sum of the two-site entropy Hessian and the singleton Bernoulli entropy Hessian, with the four real cross-block directions in its radical. For a diagonal kernel, it is the sum of the three Bernoulli Hessians, with all off-diagonal directions in its radical.

For completeness, the needed two-site statement can be checked as follows. Let the four positive atoms be \(x,y,u,w\) in the order \(00,10,01,11\), for a connected interior two-site kernel \(J\). Then \(yu-xw=|J_{12}|^2>0\). Set
\[
\Lambda=\log\frac{yu}{xw}>0,\qquad d=\det A.
\]
The two-site formula is \(H''=-\mathrm{FI}+2d\Lambda\).
If \(d<0\), it is negative. If \(d=0\) and \(A\ne0\), the rank-one Hermitian direction has a nonzero diagonal entry, whose Bernoulli marginal gives strictly positive Fisher information.

If \(d>0\), replace \(A\) by \(-A\) to arrange \(A>0\), and put
\[
P=\frac{\operatorname{adj}J}{\sqrt w},\quad
Q=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-J)}{\sqrt x},\quad
T=\frac{y+u}{2\sqrt{xw}}\ge1.
\]
Then \(\det(P+Q)=2+2T\), and the empty and full atom scores give
\[
\mathrm{FI}\ge(\operatorname{tr}PA)^2+(\operatorname{tr}QA)^2
\ge\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}(P+Q)A)^2
\ge4d(1+T).
\]
The last inequality is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for the two eigenvalues of \((P+Q)^{1/2}A(P+Q)^{1/2}\).
Since \(\Lambda\le2\log T\),
\(H''\le-4d(1+T-\log T)\le-8d<0\).
Thus the connected two-site Hessian is negative definite, establishing the assertion about the singular three-site cases.

## 7. Exact scalar Schur criterion for every connected interior base

Assume now \(M>0\), and define
\[
S=M^{1/2},\qquad \delta=\det M>0,\qquad A=SBS,\quad B=B^*.
\]
Congruence of adjugates gives
\[
\operatorname{adj}(SBS)
=\delta S^{-1}(\operatorname{adj}B)S^{-1}.
\]
The identity first follows for invertible \(B\) and extends to every \(B\) polynomially. Therefore
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
=\delta\bigl((\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\|B\|_F^2\bigr).
\tag{32}
\]

On the nine-dimensional real Hilbert space \(\operatorname{Herm}_3\), with
\(\langle B,C\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(BC)\), define the positive semidefinite operator \(\mathcal F\) by
\[
\langle B,\mathcal F B\rangle=\mathrm{FI}_K(SBS).
\]
Then
\[
D^2H(K)[SBS,SBS]
=\delta\langle I,B\rangle^2
-\langle B,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})B\rangle.
\tag{33}
\]
The operator \(\mathcal T=\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id}\) is positive definite, even when Fisher information is singular.

Cauchy--Schwarz in the \(\mathcal T\) inner product, with equality at \(B=\mathcal T^{-1}I\), proves the exact equivalence
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0\ \text{for all }A=A^*
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\delta\langle I,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})^{-1}I\rangle\le1.}
\tag{34}
\]
This is the fully reconstructed scalar criterion.

There is also an exact inertia statement:

- If the scalar in (34) is less than \(1\), the entropy Hessian is negative definite.
- If it equals \(1\), the Hessian has a one-dimensional radical and is negative on a complementary eight-dimensional subspace. Its null direction is \(A_*=S\mathcal T^{-1}I\,S\).
- If it exceeds \(1\), the Hessian has exactly one positive eigenvalue and eight negative eigenvalues. The same \(A_*\) is a positive-curvature witness.

These claims follow by congruence from
\(-\operatorname{Id}+\delta(\mathcal T^{-1/2}I)\otimes(\mathcal T^{-1/2}I)\).
They describe the criterion; no base violating it is asserted.

## 8. The seven conditional Fisher features and a square-root-free criterion

Fix the reveal order \(1,2,3\).
At stage \(i\), let \(F=\{1,\ldots,i-1\}\), and let \(h\subseteq F\) be the included history. Put
\[
Q_h=K_{FF}-I_{F\setminus h},\qquad
\pi_h=P_K(X\cap F=h)>0,
\]
\[
q_h=K_{ii}-K_{iF}Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi}\in(0,1),
\qquad
(w_h)_F=-Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi},\quad
(w_h)_i=1,\quad(w_h)_j=0\ (j>i).
\tag{35}
\]
The Schur complement gives the conditional inclusion probability \(q_h\). Differentiating the inverse in (35) gives
\[
Dq_h[A]=w_h^*Aw_h.
\]
The conditional Bernoulli score increment is
\[
\frac{\mathbf1_{i\in X}-q_h}{q_h(1-q_h)}w_h^*Aw_h.
\]
Different stages are orthogonal martingale differences. Thus, with
\[
\omega_h=\frac{\pi_h}{q_h(1-q_h)}>0,
\]
the exact identity is
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)=\sum_{h=1}^7\omega_h(w_h^*Aw_h)^2.
\tag{36}
\]
There are \(1+2+4=7\) stage-history pairs. Histories are retained separately even if their vectors coincide. The positive rank-one representation (36) is the relevant input from Source081, now rederived.

Let \(V_h=Sw_hw_h^*S\). Then
\(\mathcal F=\sum_h\omega_h V_h\otimes V_h\).
Define real numbers and a real symmetric matrix
\[
b_h=w_h^*Mw_h,\qquad
G_{hg}=|w_h^*Mw_g|^2,\qquad
C=G+\delta\operatorname{diag}(\omega_h^{-1}).
\tag{37}
\]
Here \(G\) is the Gram matrix of \(V_h\) in the Hermitian trace inner product, and \(C>0\).
Woodbury's identity gives
\[
\delta\langle I,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})^{-1}I\rangle
=3-b^\mathsf T C^{-1}b.
\tag{38}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)\le0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b^\mathsf TC^{-1}b\ge2,}
\tag{39}
\]
at every connected interior three-site base. Equations (35)--(39) use no square root or inverse square root of \(M\). Only seven real linear equations remain after obtaining \(M\) and the conditional histories.

An exact variational form is
\[
b^\mathsf TC^{-1}b
=\max_{r\in\mathbb R^7}
\{2b^\mathsf Tr-r^\mathsf TCr\}.
\tag{40}
\]
Any explicit \(r\) making the right side at least \(2\) is therefore a sufficient certificate. The maximizer is \(r=C^{-1}b\). Equivalently,
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}C&b\\b^\mathsf T&2\end{pmatrix}\le0,
\tag{41}
\]
since the determinant equals
\(\det C\,(2-b^\mathsf TC^{-1}b)\).

The precise remaining universal inequality is (39), for every connected interior three-site \(K\), with the logarithmic \(M\) matched to that same \(K\). A proof for arbitrary positive \(M\), or for arbitrary positive conditional feature weights, is neither supplied nor implied.

## 9. A structural fact about Fisher null directions

The Fisher operator can be singular. However, at these connected three-site bases,
\[
\boxed{I\in\operatorname{range}\mathcal F.}
\tag{42}
\]
To prove this, suppose \(B\in\ker\mathcal F\), and put \(A=SBS\).
Then every atom derivative \(Dp_T[A]\) is zero by positivity of the atoms and (8). Hence
\(D\det K_T[A_T]=0\) for every principal set \(T\), since
\(\det K_T=P_K(T\subseteq X)\).

If \(\tau\ne0\), expand the determinant with the coefficients \(D_0,\tau\) frozen:
\[
\det(D_0+\tau K)
=\sum_{T\subseteq[3]}
\tau^{|T|}\det K_T\prod_{j\notin T}(D_0)_{jj}.
\]
All directional derivatives on the right vanish. On the left, the derivative is
\[
\tau\det M\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A).
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{tr}B=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A)=0\).
If \(\tau=0\), \(M\) is a positive diagonal matrix and the same conclusion follows from \(A_{ii}=0\).
Therefore \(I\) is orthogonal to \(\ker\mathcal F\), proving (42).

In particular, every nonzero Fisher-null direction at a connected interior three-site base has
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\delta\|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2<0.
\tag{43}
\]
Fisher singularity is consequently not the remaining obstruction. The unresolved inequality concerns the one possible positive mode in (33).

## 10. Bounded additional attack: two simpler sufficient scalar tests

The full seven-feature test (39) is exact. Two smaller tests give proved sufficient conditions, which can be useful before solving all seven equations.

First retain only the count statistic. Its Fisher bound is
\(\mathrm{FI}_K(SBS)\ge\langle M,B\rangle^2/v\).
Applying the rank-one inverse formula to this lower bound in (34) gives
\[
\boxed{
(\operatorname{tr}M)^2
\ge2\operatorname{tr}(M^2)+2v\det M
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D^2H(K)\le0.}
\tag{44}
\]
A strict inequality gives a negative definite Hessian. Indeed, the rank-one lower bound gives the scalar
\[
3-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}M)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(M^2)+v\det M},
\]
which must be at most \(1\). This test is more precise than replacing \(M\) only by the interval \([m,u]\); it remains only sufficient.

A stronger statistic uses the three occupation indicators. Let
\[
\Sigma_{ii}=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}),\qquad
\Sigma_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Full support makes the covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) positive definite: a zero-variance real linear combination of the indicators must have equal values on the empty atom and on every singleton, forcing all its coefficients to vanish.

Projection of the score on the centered occupation vector gives
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)\ge
\operatorname{diag}(A)^\mathsf T\Sigma^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(A).
\tag{45}
\]
To see this directly, the covariance of the score with that vector is
\(D\mathbb E(\mathbf1_{i\in X})[A]=(A_{ii})_i\), and the variance of the residual after the best linear prediction is nonnegative.

Put
\[
d=(M_{11},M_{22},M_{33})^\mathsf T,\qquad
C_3=(|M_{ij}|^2)_{i,j=1}^3+\delta\Sigma.
\]
The matrix \(C_3\) is positive definite. Applying Woodbury to the three feature matrices
\(Se_ie_i^*S\) yields
\[
\boxed{
d^\mathsf TC_3^{-1}d\ge2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D^2H(K)\le0.}
\tag{46}
\]
Again, a strict inequality gives negative definiteness. The three-indicator Fisher bound contains the count bound, so (46) is at least as strong as (44). Both are weaker tests than the exact seven-feature condition.
