## Results and scope

The full Lyons conjecture is not proved here. The main positive result is the following all-dimensional restricted theorem, with a nonnegative decomposition of the **actual classical Shannon entropy Hessian**.

**Theorem A (arbitrary diagonal endpoint).** Let (0\le K\le I) be any complex Hermitian contraction, and let (D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)), (0\le d_i\le1), be any diagonal contraction. Then

\[
\eta\longmapsto H(P_{D+\eta(K-D)})
\tag{A}
\]

is concave on ([0,1]). In particular,

\[
H(P_{(1-\eta)D+\eta K})
\ge(1-\eta)\sum_i h(d_i)+\eta H(P_K).
\tag{A1}
\]

If (0<D<I) and (K\ne D), this function is strictly concave on every nontrivial subinterval of ([0,1]).

The proof does not require negative dependence. It follows from a universal entropy theorem for independent coordinate replacement on finite product spaces. The replacement theorem also has a quantum version, proved below. The fermionic occupation measurement intertwines the diagonal-reservoir Gaussian channel with this classical replacement channel exactly.

Other completed results:

1. An exact expression for the measured Hessian as a sum of Pearson terms and relative-entropy contraction rates, all nonnegative. This proves Theorem A directly and gives a quantitative curvature bound.
2. The precise extra acceleration term that separates the Gaussian quantum entropy Hessian from the occupation entropy Hessian.
3. An exact two-mode counterexample to the promising stronger claim that Gaussianizing a mixture of two Gaussian states increases its occupation-measurement entropy. At the same example, occupation measurement loses **zero** quantum Fisher information in the chosen direction, while the missing acceleration term is strictly nonzero.
4. Every strictly positive, nondiagonal gauge-invariant Gaussian state is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum of occupation-measurement entropy among states with its full one-body covariance. Thus a Gaussian measured-entropy extremality principle cannot replace the channel argument.

The diagonal endpoint theorem goes beyond the prior packet's fixed-diagonal resampling **monotonicity** and local diagonal-kernel expansion. Its reference diagonal (D) need not equal \(\operatorname{diag}K\), and its conclusion is global concavity in the interpolation parameter. Scalar and zero endpoints are included, not counted as new cases. No claim of bibliographic priority is made.

## 1. Universal coordinate-replacement entropy concavity

Let \(\Omega=\prod_{i=1}^n\Omega_i\) be a finite product space. Fix strictly positive probability laws \(\mu_i\) and put \(\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i\). For a law (p) on \(\Omega\), define

\[
(R_i p)(x)=\mu_i(x_i)\sum_{y_i\in\Omega_i}p(x_1,\ldots,y_i,\ldots,x_n).
\]

Thus (R_i) forgets coordinate (i) and samples it afresh from \(\mu_i\), independently of the other coordinates. The maps commute and satisfy (R_i^2=R_i). Set

\[
C_{i,\eta}=\eta I+(1-\eta)R_i,
\qquad T_\eta=\prod_{i=1}^n C_{i,\eta}.
\tag{1}
\]

The semigroup identity is (T_\eta T_\theta=T_{\eta\theta}).

**Theorem B.** For every input law (p_0), \(\eta\mapsto H(T_\eta p_0)\) is concave on ([0,1]). This remains true when the reference laws have zeros.

### 1.1 Entropy dissipation equals a sum of Jeffreys divergences

Initially take positive reference and input laws. Use semigroup time \(\tau=-\log\eta\), write (p_\tau=T_{e^{-\tau}}p_0), and set

\[
F(\tau)=D(p_\tau\Vert\mu),\qquad
J(p,q)=D(p\Vert q)+D(q\Vert p).
\]

The generator is

\[
\dot p_\tau=\sum_i(R_i p_\tau-p_\tau).
\tag{2}
\]

For each (i), \(\log((R_i p)/\mu)\) depends only on the coordinates other than (i). Hence

\[
\sum_x(p-R_i p)(x)\log\frac{(R_i p)(x)}{\mu(x)}=0.
\]

It follows that

\[
F'(\tau)=-I(p_\tau),
\qquad I(p)=\sum_iJ(p,R_i p).
\tag{3}
\]

This identity is exact; no log-Sobolev estimate is being assumed.

### 1.2 The elementary contraction that supplies the second derivative

Fix (i). For any (j\ne i), commutativity and data processing for both relative entropies give

\[
J(C_{j,\theta}p,R_i C_{j,\theta}p)
=J(C_{j,\theta}p,C_{j,\theta}R_i p)
\le J(p,R_i p).
\tag{4}
\]

For the own-coordinate map, (R_i C_{i,\theta}p=R_i p). Joint convexity of relative entropy gives

\[
J(C_{i,\theta}p,R_i p)
\le\theta J(p,R_i p).
\tag{5}
\]

Applying (4) to all the other coordinates and then (5) proves

\[
I(T_\theta p)\le\theta I(p).
\tag{6}
\]

The finite-space facts used here follow directly from the log-sum inequality: applying log-sum to each output atom proves data processing, and applying it to the two mixture components proves joint convexity.

Putting \(\theta=e^{-s}\) in (6) and differentiating at (s=0) yields

\[
\frac{d}{d\tau}I(p_\tau)\le-I(p_\tau),
\qquad F''(\tau)+F'(\tau)\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]

The change of variables \(\tau=-\log\eta\) therefore gives

\[
\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}D(T_\eta p_0\Vert\mu)
=\frac{F''(\tau)+F'(\tau)}{\eta^2}\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]

Finally, each one-coordinate marginal of (T_\eta p_0) is

\[
\eta(p_0)_i+(1-\eta)\mu_i.
\]

Since \(\log\mu(x)=\sum_i\log\mu_i(x_i)\), its cross-entropy against \(\mu\) is affine in \(\eta\). Thus

\[
H(T_\eta p_0)=-D(T_\eta p_0\Vert\mu)
-\sum_x(T_\eta p_0)(x)\log\mu(x)
\]

is concave, proving Theorem B for positive laws. Arbitrary input and reference laws follow by approximating them by positive laws. The channels and output probabilities depend continuously on these laws, and finite Shannon entropy is continuous including at zero.

## 2. An exact nonnegative measured-Hessian decomposition

The preceding proof has a sharper form. For positive (p,q), let

\[
\mathscr E_j(p,q)
:=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}
J(C_{j,e^{-s}}p,C_{j,e^{-s}}q)\right|_{s=0}.
\tag{9}
\]

Data processing shows \(\mathscr E_j(p,q)\ge0\). It is an explicit finite expression. With

\[
u=R_jp-p,\quad v=R_jq-q,\quad r=\log(p/q)
\]

interpreted entrywise, it is

\[
\mathscr E_j(p,q)
=-\sum_x\left[
u_x\left(r_x+1-\frac{q_x}{p_x}\right)
+v_x\left(-r_x+1-\frac{p_x}{q_x}\right)
\right].
\tag{10}
\]

For (q=R_i p), differentiation in the own coordinate is particularly simple:

\[
\mathscr E_i(p,R_i p)
=J(p,R_i p)+\chi_i(p),\qquad
\chi_i(p):=\sum_x\frac{(p_x-(R_i p)_x)^2}{p_x}.
\tag{11}
\]

Indeed (C_{i,e^{-s}}R_i p=R_i p), and the first argument has derivative \(-(p-R_i p)\). Substitution into (10) gives (11).

The full generator is the sum of the coordinate generators. Combining (3), (9), and (11) yields the exact identity

\[
\frac{d}{d\tau}I(p_\tau)
=-I(p_\tau)-\sum_i\chi_i(p_\tau)
-\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p_\tau,R_i p_\tau).
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently, with (p_\eta=T_\eta p_0),

\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}H(p_\eta)
=\sum_i\chi_i(p_\eta)
+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p_\eta,R_i p_\eta).
}
\tag{13}
\]

Every term on the right is nonnegative. This formula controls the actual entropy of the measured distribution; it is not a comparison to the entropy of an unmeasured Gaussian state. In particular,

\[
H''(p_\eta)\le-\eta^{-2}\sum_i\chi_i(p_\eta).
\tag{14}
\]

Here and below (H''(p_\eta)) means differentiation of (H(p_\eta)) in \(\eta\).

If \(\mu\) has full support, (p_\eta) has full support for (0<\eta<1). The right side of (13) can vanish only if (R_i p_\eta=p_\eta) for every (i), which implies (p_\eta=\mu). Each (C_{i,\eta}) is invertible for \(\eta>0\), with eigenvalues (1,\eta), so this is equivalent to (p_0=\mu). Thus Theorem B is strictly concave when the reference has full support and (p_0\ne\mu).

## 3. Fermionic Gaussian channels and the determinantal interpolation

For a Hermitian contraction (K), let \(\rho_K\) be its gauge-invariant fermionic Gaussian state. In the interior,

\[
\rho_K=\det(I-K)\exp(d\Gamma(\log K-\log(I-K))).
\tag{15}
\]

The covariance convention is chosen so that \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_K\prod_{i\in S}n_i)=\det K_S\). Occupation dephasing \(\Delta\) therefore gives

\[
\Delta\rho_K=\sum_S P_K(S)|S\rangle\langle S|,
\qquad S(\Delta\rho_K)=H(P_K).
\tag{16}
\]

Couple the system to a fresh Gaussian reservoir \(\rho_D\), where (D) is diagonal, and mix the matching system and reservoir modes by the fermionic beam splitter with one-body matrix

\[
W_\eta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt\eta I&\sqrt{1-\eta}I\\
-\sqrt{1-\eta}I&\sqrt\eta I
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Tracing the reservoir gives the Gaussian state with covariance

\[
K_\eta=\eta K+(1-\eta)D.
\tag{17}
\]

Because \(\rho_D\) is an occupation-basis product state, its measured channel is exactly independent coordinate replacement with reference \(\mu=P_D\). The following direct calculation establishes that statement without needing any convention about fermionic tensor signs.

Given (X\sim P_K), retain each (X_i) with probability \(\eta\), otherwise replace it by a fresh independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_i)\). For its output (Y),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}Y_i
&=\sum_{J\subseteq S}\eta^{|J|}(1-\eta)^{|S\setminus J|}
\det K_J\prod_{i\in S\setminus J}d_i\\
&=\det(\eta K_S+(1-\eta)D_S).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

The last equality is the determinant expansion selecting the diagonal columns contributed by (D_S). Inclusion probabilities determine the law, so

\[
\boxed{P_{K_\eta}=T_\eta P_K.}
\tag{19}
\]

Theorem B and (13) now prove Theorem A and its exact Hessian formula. Strictness follows because (P_K=P_D) implies (K=D): first moments fix (K_{ii}=d_i), and

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbf 1_{i\in X},\mathbf 1_{j\in X})=-|K_{ij}|^2
\]

forces every off-diagonal entry to vanish when (P_K) is product.

The diagonal reference is in the **actual measured coordinate basis**. It cannot be replaced by “diagonal after an arbitrary unitary change of basis,” since that changes occupation entropy.

The resampling identity itself appears in the prior packet, agent058, §2.1, for (D=\operatorname{diag}K), where it was used for divergence monotonicity. The new step here is the Jeffreys-dissipation differentiation (12) and the resulting global Shannon entropy concavity for arbitrary diagonal (D).

## 4. Quantum local-replacement version

For clarity, the entropy argument has a genuinely quantum extension independent of Gaussianity. Let \(\mathcal H=\bigotimes_i\mathcal H_i\), fix full-rank local states \(\omega_i\), and put \(\omega=\bigotimes_i\omega_i\). Let

\[
R_i(\rho)=\omega_i\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_i\rho
\]

with factors placed in the original order, and let \(\mathcal T_\eta=\prod_i[\eta\mathrm{id}+(1-\eta)R_i]\).

**Theorem C.** For every finite-dimensional state \(\rho\),

\[
\eta\longmapsto S(\mathcal T_\eta\rho)
\]

is concave on ([0,1]).

**Proof.** The maps (R_i) are commuting idempotent channels. For (q=R_i\rho\),

\[
\log q-\log\omega
=I_i\otimes(\log\rho_{-i}-\log\omega_{-i}),
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho-q)(\log q-\log\omega)=0.
\]

Therefore the derivative of (D(\rho_\tau\Vert\omega)) equals minus \(\sum_i[D(\rho_\tau\Vert R_i\rho_\tau)+D(R_i\rho_\tau\Vert\rho_\tau)]\). Quantum relative entropy obeys both channel data processing and joint convexity. The proofs (4)–(8) apply without a change. Finally, \(\operatorname{Tr}\rho_\eta\log\omega\) is affine in \(\eta\), because each local marginal is affine. Continuity removes full-rank assumptions on the input, and approximation removes them on the reference. \(\square\)

The exact quantum analogue of (13) has

\[
\chi_i(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(\rho-R_i\rho)
\mathcal L_\rho(\rho-R_i\rho)\bigr],
\qquad
\mathcal L_\rho(X)=\int_0^\infty(\rho+sI)^{-1}X(\rho+sI)^{-1}\,ds,
\tag{20}
\]

in place of the classical Pearson term. This is nonnegative, and the cross-coordinate terms are the quantum Jeffreys contraction derivatives. The own-coordinate identity follows by differentiating \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho-q)(\log\rho-\log q)\) with (q) fixed and \(\dot\rho=-(\rho-q)\).

The ordinary quantum replacement channel \(\mathcal T_\eta\) is **not** being identified with the Gaussian attenuation channel of §3. They generally act differently on coherences. What is equal is their occupation-diagonal output law, as established by (18)–(19). Theorem A can be proved entirely by the classical theorem, or by applying Theorem C to \(\Delta\rho_K\).

## 5. The exact obstruction in a general Gaussian covariance direction

Let (K(t)=K+tA), with (0<K<I), (A=A^*\), and write

\[
\rho(t)=\rho_{K(t)},\quad p(t)=\operatorname{diag}\rho(t),\quad
X=\rho'(0),\quad Y=\rho''(0).
\]

Define the quantum BKM metric and the measured Fisher information by

\[
g_Q=\operatorname{Tr}X\mathcal L_\rho(X),\qquad
g_M=\sum_S\frac{p'_S(0)^2}{p_S(0)}.
\]

The second derivative (Y) has zero trace and zero one-body covariance, since covariance is affine in (t). Equation (15) therefore gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}Y\log\rho=0.
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}S(\rho(t))\Big|_0&=-g_Q,\\
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}S(\Delta\rho(t))\Big|_0
&=-g_M-\operatorname{Tr}Y(\log\Delta\rho-\log\rho).
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]

Writing (C(\rho)=D(\rho\Vert\Delta\rho)=S(\Delta\rho)-S(\rho)), one obtains

\[
C''=g_Q-g_M-\operatorname{Tr}Y(\log\Delta\rho-\log\rho).
\tag{23}
\]

Measurement data processing gives (g_M\le g_Q). It does not control the sign of the acceleration pairing in (22). In eigenbasis coordinates of (K), the quantum metric has the explicit covariance expression

\[
g_Q=\operatorname{tr}A\,D[\log K-\log(I-K)][A]
=\sum_{i,j}b^{[1]}(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)|\widetilde A_{ij}|^2,
\tag{24}
\]

where (b(x)=\log(x/(1-x))) and (b^{[1]}(x,x)=1/[x(1-x)]). Thus even complete knowledge of the Gaussian metric leaves exactly the second term in (22).

For the diagonal-endpoint directions, formula (13) supplies the required control of the **sum** of measured Fisher information and acceleration. No universal sign for the acceleration alone is asserted.

## 6. Exact two-mode Gaussianization obstruction and Fisher saturation

Take

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=I_2.
\tag{25}
\]

The center atom law in the order \(00,10,01,11\) is

\[
p=(3,5,5,3)/16.
\]

Along (K+tI),

\[
p'=(-1,0,0,1),\qquad p''=(2,-2,-2,2).
\]

The eigenvalues of (K) are (1/4,3/4). Therefore

\[
g_Q=g_M=32/3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}Y\log\Delta\rho=4\log(3/5)<0.
\tag{26}
\]

The actual entropy curvature and coherence curvature are

\[
H''=-32/3+4\log(5/3)<0,
\qquad C''=4\log(5/3)>0.
\tag{27}
\]

This is exact saturation of Fisher data processing with a strictly positive coherence second derivative. Any proof discarding the Gaussian acceleration because the quantum and measured Fisher metrics agree fails even here.

Now put (K_\pm=K\pm I/8) and \(\sigma=(\rho_{K_-}+\rho_{K_+})/2\). Its covariance is (K), so Gaussianization sends it to \(\rho_K\). The measured laws are

\[
p_-=(21,19,19,5)/64,\qquad
p_+=(5,19,19,21)/64,
\]

and hence

\[
q:=\operatorname{diag}\sigma=(13,19,19,13)/64.
\tag{28}
\]

A law of the form \((r,1/2-r,1/2-r,r)\) has entropy

\[
\varphi(r)=-2r\log r-2(1/2-r)\log(1/2-r),
\qquad
\varphi'(r)=2\log\frac{1/2-r}{r}>0
\]

for (0<r<1/4). Since \(3/16<13/64<1/4\),

\[
S(\Delta\sigma)>S(\Delta\rho_K).
\tag{29}
\]

Thus Gaussianization of a mixture of two Gaussian states can **decrease measured entropy**. Its numerical decrease here is approximately (0.0139013443). In contrast, the Gaussian maximum-von-Neumann-entropy property gives (S(\rho_K)>S(\sigma)); the actual increase is approximately (0.0036263045). These signs can also be read from their exact eigenvalues:

\[
\operatorname{spec}\rho_K=(3,3,9,1)/16,
\qquad
\operatorname{spec}\sigma=(13,13,35,3)/64.
\]

This does not refute Lyons concavity: the true midpoint entropy gap is positive, approximately (0.0688092410). It refutes the stronger Gaussianization step that would combine ordinary mixture entropy concavity with measured-entropy maximization at fixed covariance.

## 7. Why measured-entropy Gaussian extremality fails locally in every dimension

There is a broader exact obstruction. Let (0<K<I) be nondiagonal, put \(\rho=\rho_K\), (p=\operatorname{diag}\rho\), and let \(\mu\) be the product law with the same one-coordinate marginals as (p). Define the diagonal matrix

\[
Z=\operatorname{diag}(\mu-p).
\]

It has zero trace and zero covariance: its diagonal one-body moments vanish because the marginals agree, and every off-diagonal quadratic fermionic observable has zero trace against a diagonal matrix. Since \(\rho>0\), both \(\rho+sZ\) and \(\rho-sZ\) are states with exactly the same covariance for all sufficiently small (s>0).

The law (p) is not product, because some \(K_{ij}\ne0\) gives a nonzero occupation covariance. Product entropy maximization with fixed marginals gives (H(\mu)>H(p)), or directly

\[
H(\mu)-H(p)=D(p\Vert\mu)>0.
\]

The derivative at zero of \(H((1-s)p+s\mu)\) is positive: by concavity it is at least (H(\mu)-H(p)>0). Thus the (+Z) perturbation raises measured entropy to first order and the (-Z) perturbation lowers it. This proves the stated absence of either local extremum at a nondiagonal strict Gaussian state.

For a concrete complete two-mode description, write

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every number-preserving state with covariance (K) has occupation law

\[
(1-a-b+r,\ a-r,\ b-r,\ r),
\]

and its one-particle block has off-diagonal entry (c). Positivity is equivalent to

\[
\max(0,a+b-1)\le r\le\lambda_{\min}(K).
\tag{30}
\]

The entropy is maximized at

\[
r_*=\min(ab,\lambda_{\min}(K)).
\tag{31}
\]

The Gaussian value is (r_G=ab-|c|^2=\det K). For a strict contraction and (c\ne0\),

\[
r_G<ab,\qquad r_G<\lambda_{\min}(K),
\]

so (r_G<r_*) and the measured entropy can strictly increase at fixed covariance. This explicitly describes the failed variational envelope in two modes.
