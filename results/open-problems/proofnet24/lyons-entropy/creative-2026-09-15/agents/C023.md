## 1. Arbitrary common invariant laws

Let \(\Omega\) be finite, let \(\mu>0\) be any probability law, and let \(R_1,\ldots,R_m\) be pairwise commuting stochastic maps on probability columns satisfying
\[
R_i^2=R_i,\qquad R_i\mu=\mu.
\tag{1}
\]
Neither a product reference nor coordinate replacement is assumed. Define
\[
C_{i,s}=sI+(1-s)R_i,\qquad
p_s=\prod_i C_{i,s_i}p_0,\qquad
F(s)=D(p_s\Vert\mu).
\tag{2}
\]

**Projection lemma.** Each \(R_i\) resamples within the cells of a partition using the conditional law of \(\mu\) on that cell. Its dual \(E_i=R_i^*\) is therefore a conditional expectation and an orthogonal projection in \(L^2(\mu)\); furthermore
\[
R_i(\mu f)=\mu E_i f.
\tag{3}
\]
To see this, a finite Markov chain with a strictly positive stationary law has no transient states. Its closed irreducible classes partition the state space. On each class, an idempotent transition matrix has range equal to its harmonic functions, which are constant by the maximum principle. Thus each class has identical transition rows, necessarily the stationary conditional law. This also proves reversibility and (3).

For \(p=p_s\), put \(q_i=R_ip\),
\[
J_i=J(p,q_i),\qquad
\chi_i=\sum_x\frac{(p_x-q_{i,x})^2}{p_x},
\]
and, for \(i\ne j\), let
\[
\mathcal E_{ij}=
-\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}
J(C_{j,e^{-\tau}}p,C_{j,e^{-\tau}}q_i)\right|_{\tau=0}.
\tag{4}
\]
Classical data processing gives \(\mathcal E_{ij}\ge0\).

**Theorem 1 (common-invariant Hessian).** At positive laws and positive parameters,
\[
\boxed{
F_i=\frac{J_i}{s_i},\qquad
F_{ii}=\frac{\chi_i}{s_i^2},\qquad
F_{ij}=\frac{\mathcal E_{ij}}{s_is_j}\quad(i\ne j).
}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal E_{ij}=\mathcal E_{ji}\), and all displayed derivatives are nonnegative.

**Proof.** Commutativity and idempotence give \(\partial_i p=(p-q_i)/s_i\). The ratio \(q_i/\mu\) is constant on every cell of the partition for \(R_i\), and \(p-q_i\) has zero mass on each cell. Hence
\[
\sum_x(p_x-q_{i,x})\log(q_{i,x}/\mu_x)=0.
\tag{6}
\]
This converts differentiation of relative entropy into \(J_i/s_i\). The output law is affine in each separate \(s_i\), so the second derivative in that parameter is \(\chi_i/s_i^2\). Finally, applying \(C_{j,e^{-\tau}}\) changes \(s_j\) to \(e^{-\tau}s_j\) in both arguments of \(J_i\); this gives the mixed identity. This is C021's unequal-rate calculation with (6) replacing its product-coordinate argument. \(\square\)

For synchronized parameters \(s_i=\eta\), (5) implies
\[
\boxed{
\eta^2\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}D(p_\eta\Vert\mu)
=Q(p_\eta):=\sum_i\chi_i+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathcal E_{ij}\ge0.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus relative-entropy convexity survives for every common positive invariant law. Shannon concavity requires one further condition, characterized next.

## 2. Exact cross-entropy conditions, including necessity

Set \(L=\log\mu\), \(f=p_0/\mu\), and define the simultaneous orthogonal projections
\[
\Pi_A=\prod_{i\in A}(I-E_i)\prod_{j\notin A}E_j,
\qquad A\subseteq[m].
\tag{8}
\]
They are pairwise orthogonal and sum to the identity, including when some projections vanish. Put
\[
a_A=\langle f,\Pi_A L\rangle_\mu,\qquad
c_k=\sum_{|A|=k}a_A.
\]
The exact cross-entropy formula is
\[
\sum_xp_s(x)\log\mu(x)
=\sum_A a_A\prod_{i\in A}s_i;
\qquad
\sum_xp_\eta(x)\log\mu(x)=\sum_{k=0}^m c_k\eta^k.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently:

* For a **fixed input and synchronized parameter**, cross-entropy is affine exactly when \(c_k=0\) for all \(k\ge2\). Cancellation among sets of the same size is allowed.
* It is concave exactly when \(\sum_{k\ge2}k(k-1)c_k\eta^{k-2}\ge0\) throughout the parameter interval. Nonnegative individual \(c_k\) are sufficient, but unnecessary.
* For a **fixed input and independent parameters**, cross-entropy is jointly affine exactly when \(a_A=0\) for \(|A|\ge2\), and constant exactly when \(a_A=0\) for every nonempty \(A\).

Here cross-entropy means \(-\sum p\log\mu\), explaining the signs above. The actual Shannon curvature is
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2H''(p_\eta)
=Q(p_\eta)+\sum_{k\ge2}k(k-1)c_k\eta^k.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Equation (10), rather than cross-entropy concavity alone, is the exact condition for a particular input: positive relative-entropy curvature can offset an adverse cross-entropy term.

**Theorem 2 (sharp universal characterization).** Under (1),
\[
\boxed{
H\left(\prod_i C_{i,\eta}p_0\right)
\text{ is concave in }\eta\text{ for every }p_0
\iff
\Pi_A\log\mu=0\quad\text{for every }|A|\ge2.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Equivalently, \((I-E_i)(I-E_j)\log\mu=0\) for every distinct pair of indices.

**Proof.** The stated projection condition makes (9) affine for every input, so (7) proves sufficiency. For necessity let \(T_\eta=\prod_i[\eta I+(1-\eta)E_i]\) act on densities. If a higher-degree component of \(L\) is nonzero, then at any \(0<\eta_0<1\),
\[
\phi=T_{\eta_0}''L
=\sum_{|A|\ge2}|A|(|A|-1)\eta_0^{|A|-2}\Pi_A L\ne0.
\]
Orthogonality prevents cancellation, and \(\phi\) has mean zero. For sufficiently small signed \(\varepsilon\), take \(p_0^{\varepsilon}=\mu(1+\varepsilon\phi)\). Relative-entropy curvature is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), whereas the cross-entropy curvature at \(\eta_0\) is
\[
-\varepsilon\langle\phi,T_{\eta_0}''L\rangle_\mu
=-\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2.
\]
Choosing negative \(\varepsilon\) makes the Shannon second derivative positive. This proves necessity. \(\square\)

The condition genuinely admits nonproduct references. For example,
\[
\mu(z,x_1,\ldots,x_m)=\nu(z)\prod_i\mu_i(x_i\mid z)
\tag{12}
\]
may correlate all physical variables. Conditional replacement of \(x_i\), retaining \(z\), gives commuting projections. Each summand in \(\log\mu\) is affected by at most one projection, so (11) holds. No determinant-compatible realization of **every** law (12) is asserted.

Conversely, a common nonproduct invariant law alone is insufficient. Let
\[
\mu=(3,5,5,3)/16,
\quad p_0=(1,1,1,1)/4,
\quad R_1=R_2=R,\quad Rp=\mu\sum_xp_x.
\]
The invariant law itself is the two-site DPP with diagonal \(1/2\) and off-diagonal \(1/4\). Nevertheless
\[
p_\eta=\mu+\eta^2(p_0-\mu),\qquad
H''(p_\eta)|_{\eta=0}=\tfrac14\log(5/3)>0.
\tag{13}
\]
This is a counterexample to the unrestricted channel claim, not an affine-kernel counterexample to Lyons.

## 3. A new affine family: coherent blocks against a coherent exterior

Partition the physical sites into measured blocks \(U_1,\ldots,U_m\) and an exterior \(O\). Let
\[
K_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_1&0&\cdots&u_1v_1^*\\
0&D_2&\cdots&u_2v_2^*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
v_1u_1^*&v_2u_2^*&\cdots&C
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le K_*\le I.
\tag{14}
\]
Each \(D_i\) can be fully coherent on an arbitrarily large block. The exterior \(C\) is arbitrary subject to feasibility. No eigenvector condition on \(D_iu_i\) is imposed. Different active blocks have zero mutual kernel entries; their couplings to the exterior each have rank one. The combined exterior coupling can have arbitrary rank.

For \(f\in[0,1]^m\), define \(K(f)\) by replacing every \(u_iv_i^*\) with \(f_i u_iv_i^*\), retaining all diagonal blocks. These are contractions: for \(S=\operatorname{diag}(f_iI_{U_i},I_O)\),
\[
K(f)=SK_*S+\bigoplus_i(1-f_i^2)D_i\oplus0,
\tag{15}
\]
and the analogous expression for \(I-K(f)\) is positive.

Let \(R_i\) replace the entire occupation configuration on \(U_i\) by an independent sample from \(P_{D_i}\), keeping its complement. These channels share the physically nonproduct invariant law
\[
\mu=\bigotimes_i P_{D_i}\otimes P_C.
\tag{16}
\]
The exact intertwining is
\[
\boxed{P_{K(f)}=\prod_i C_{i,f_i^2}P_{K_*}.}
\tag{17}
\]

Here is the determinant argument, using C022's conditional rank-one mechanism. First assume strict kernels. Fix the configuration \(Y\) on the complement of \(U_i\). Its probability is independent of \(f_i\), and its conditional kernel on \(U_i\) has the form
\[
D_i-f_i^2\,\gamma_Y u_i u_i^*,\qquad \gamma_Y\in\mathbb R,
\tag{18}
\]
by the conditional Schur-complement formula. The coefficient \(\gamma_Y\) may have either sign. Every inclusion determinant, hence every atom, is affine in a rank-one matrix displacement. Thus changing \(f_i^2\) from one to \(s\) replaces the conditional atom law by \(s\) times its original value plus \((1-s)P_{D_i}\). This is precisely \(C_{i,s}\). Doing so successively proves (17), since the block channels commute. Approximation by \((1-\epsilon)K_*+\epsilon I/2\) proves the boundary case while preserving the rank-one cross-block structure.

All block marginals and the entire exterior marginal remain fixed. Therefore
\[
H(P_{K(f)})=\sum_iH(P_{D_i})+H(P_C)
-D(P_{K(f)}\Vert\mu).
\tag{19}
\]
This verifies the exact constant-cross-entropy condition; it is not an inference from commutation alone.

**Theorem 3 (coherent-block affine concavity).** If
\[
f_i(t)=a_i+b_it\in[0,1],\qquad b_ib_j\ge0\quad\forall i,j,
\tag{20}
\]
then \(K(f(t))\) is an affine kernel segment and its DPP entropy is concave. At strict kernels it is strictly concave if some varying amplitude has a nonzero coupling.

Indeed, substitute \(s_i=f_i^2\) into (5) and (19), and put \(r_i=b_i/f_i\). The exact curvature is
\[
\boxed{
-H''=
\sum_i(4\chi_i+2J_i)r_i^2
+8\sum_{i<j}\mathcal E_{ij}r_ir_j\ge0.
}
\tag{21}
\]
Zero amplitudes are handled by continuity. Strictness follows because a nonzero cross entry gives a strictly negative pair covariance across that block boundary whenever \(f_i>0\), so \(p\ne R_ip\) and \(J_i>0\).

## 4. Explicit rank-six segment and reproducibility

Take three two-site active blocks and a three-site exterior. Set
\[
D_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&1/20\\1/20&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
D_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/25\\1/25&3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
D_3=\begin{pmatrix}9/20&-3/100\\-3/100&11/20\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
C=\frac1{100}\begin{pmatrix}50&2&-1\\2&48&3\\-1&3&52\end{pmatrix},
\quad
u_1=(1,2)^T,\quad u_2=(2,1)^T,\quad u_3=(1,-1)^T,
\]
and let the rows \(v_i^*\) be those of
\[
\frac1{100}\begin{pmatrix}1&2&-1\\2&-1&1\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{22}
\]
Use \(f(t)=(1/5,2/5,3/5)+t(4/5,3/5,2/5)\).

Exact Gershgorin bounds put \(K_*\)'s spectrum inside \([31/100,17/25]\). The integer matrix in (22) has determinant \(-12\), so the affine direction has inertia \((3,3,3)\) and rank six. The endpoints are connected, coherent kernels with a nonzero commutator: in one-based indexing,
\[
[K(f(0)),K(f(1))]_{1,7}=-1/2500.
\]
The invariant law is nonproduct on physical sites; for instance its first active pair has covariance \(-1/400\).

Run `python computations/C023/verify.py`. The saved `results.json` contains the exact rational kernel, positivity bounds, and numerical checks on eleven segment parameters. Maximum atom-intertwining error is \(5.64\times10^{-18}\), and maximum discrepancy between the direct determinant Hessian and (21) is \(5.05\times10^{-18}\). All sampled curvatures are negative. These checks support the formulas; the proofs do not rely on floating-point signs.

## 5. Scope and remaining gap

C006 supplies the product-coordinate entropy method. C021 supplies its unequal-rate/amplitude calculation and the singleton version of (14). C022 supplies the conditional rank-one replacement mechanism. The new DPP statement here permits coherent active blocks of arbitrary size, an arbitrary coherent exterior, and unbounded direction rank. C022's all-block affine replacement theorem does not cover this construction when the exterior coupling rank exceeds one; C021's independent physical sites do not cover coherent active blocks.

The realization (16) is nonproduct at the physical-site level, but factors over the resampled blocks and the frozen exterior. It does not establish a determinant realization of an arbitrary interacting conditional-product reference such as (12), nor entropy concavity for arbitrary coherent endpoints. The obstruction from C007 remains: commuting coherent Gaussian operations need not induce probability channels. Equations (17)–(18) verify that missing property for the present family, while (9)–(11) identify exactly when the common-invariant argument can be used more generally.
