## 2. General partial-reveal identity

For any differentiable full-support finite law, let \(s\) be its mean-zero score. If \(T\) is a fixed statistic, its score is \(\mathbb E(s\mid T)\). Orthogonal projection in \(L^2(p)\) gives

\[
\boxed{
I(A)=I_T(A)+\mathbb E\{s-\mathbb E(s\mid T)\}^2.
}
\tag{7}
\]

If an independent random variable chooses one of several statistics and its value is observed, both terms average over that choice. Thus, for (3),

\[
I(A)=I_{\rm ad}(A)+\mathcal R_{\rm ad}(A)
\tag{8}
\]

exactly. For a two-atom leaf, the conditional-variance identity is

\[
p_Ss_S^2+p_Ts_T^2
-{(p_Ss_S+p_Ts_T)^2\over p_S+p_T}
={p_Sp_T\over p_S+p_T}(s_S-s_T)^2,
\]

which proves the residual formula without a hidden completion of the reveal.

For a DPP, the retained information also has a local reveal formula. At an internal tree node let \(F\) be the already observed sites, \(h\subseteq F\) the sites observed occupied, and \(i\notin F\) the next site. Set

\[
Q_h=K_{FF}-I_{F\setminus h},\qquad
\theta_h=K_{ii}-K_{iF}Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi},
\]

\[
(w_h)_F=-Q_h^{-1}K_{Fi},\quad (w_h)_i=1,
\quad(w_h)_j=0\quad(j\notin F\cup\{i\}).
\tag{9}
\]

The Schur complement gives the conditional success probability \(\theta_h\), and direct differentiation gives \(D\theta_h[A]=w_h^*Aw_h\). Martingale orthogonality of the successive Bernoulli score increments gives

\[
I_{\rm tree}(A)
=\sum_{h\text{ internal}}
{P(X\cap F=h)\over\theta_h(1-\theta_h)}(w_h^*Aw_h)^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Formula (10) remains exact for adaptive site choices and for a tree stopped before full observation. The omitted conditional score is the residual in (7). A complete reveal has zero residual and the same full Fisher information for every order; averaging complete orders would create no new bound.

The entropy identity retains the original atom accelerations:

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(K)[A,A]
=I_{\rm ad}(A)+\sum_Sr_S\log p_S+\mathcal R_{\rm ad}(A).
}
\tag{11}
\]

Only the first term has been coarsened. No second derivative of a conditional kernel or a conditional Bernoulli probability is assumed zero.

## 3. Full frozen acceleration and all Hermitian coordinates

\[

\end{aligned}

\[

\]

\[
\sum_Sr_S\log p_S=\sum_Sr_S\log w_S.
\tag{14}
\]

This only removes a frozen constant. All derivatives in (12) are along the original affine kernel path; the odds matrix specifies the base and its frozen log table.

Use coordinates

\[

\Re A_{12},\Re A_{13},\Re A_{14},\Re A_{23},\Re A_{24},\Re A_{34})^T

and \(y=(\Im A_{12},\Im A_{13},\Im A_{14},\Im A_{23},\Im A_{24},\Im A_{34})^T\).

\[

(H_S^R)_{ab}&=p_S\left\{
\operatorname{tr}(V_SE_a)\operatorname{tr}(V_SE_b)

These are rational. At the real base, every imaginary score is zero. The real-imaginary mixed acceleration also vanishes: \(V_SE_aV_S\) is real symmetric and has zero trace pairing with a real skew-symmetric matrix. Thus

\[

\tag{16}

\[

\[

\tag{17}
\]

Then

\[
-D^2H(K_*)[A,A]=v^TM_Rv+y^TM_Iy+\mathcal R_{\rm ad}(A).
\tag{18}
\]

It suffices to certify

\[
M_R\succeq {1\over20}D_R,\quad M_I\succeq {1\over20}D_I,
\qquad
D_R=\operatorname{Diag}(1,1,1,1,2,2,2,2,2,2),\quad D_I=2I_6.
\tag{19}
\]

No favorable or unfavorable entry of the frozen-log table is removed in (17).
