# C028 — an explicit full-rank block corridor for entropy concavity

Date: 2026-09-15. Restricted theorem; no claim about arbitrary kernels or historical priority. This extends `ROOT_BLOCK_QUARTIC.md` quantitatively. It does not assume rank-one block rows or rank-one links. Only this report and `computations/C028/` were written.

**Result.** Strict blocks with certified full-Hessian gaps can be joined by arbitrary-rank weak links inside an explicitly computable, full-dimensional convex set. The entropy is strictly concave on that entire set. Its block radius, coupling radius, and full-Hessian coercivity are given below using block atom minima, block gaps, and selected conditional Fisher features. A new cancellation improves the mutual-information remainder from degree five to degree six for **all** block partitions.

## 1. Input data and the corridor

Partition the sites into \(m\ge2\) blocks of sizes \(d_a\), let \(n=\sum_a d_a\), \(d_*=\max_a d_a\), and fix
\[
D=\bigoplus_a D_a,\qquad 0<D_a<I.
\]
All matrix norms below are Frobenius unless labeled otherwise. Assume certified numbers \(\gamma_a>0\) satisfy
\[
D^2H(D_a)[T_a,T_a]\le-\gamma_a\|T_a\|_F^2
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }T_a.
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
\gamma=\min_a\gamma_a,\quad
p_*=\min_{a,S}P_{D_a}(S),\quad
\tau=\min_a\{\lambda_{\min}(D_a),\lambda_{\min}(I-D_a)\},
\quad\kappa=2/\tau.
\tag{2}
\]
One may replace \(\tau\) by the smaller number \(p_*\) if only atom minima are available: \(\det D_a\le\lambda_{\min}(D_a)\) and \(\det(I-D_a)\le\lambda_{\min}(I-D_a)\).

In each block select one reveal history at every depth, as in the root report. Let their conditional Fisher vectors and weights be \(w_{a,r}\), \(\omega_{a,r}>0\). The matrix
\[
F_a=(w_{a,1},\ldots,w_{a,d_a}),\qquad
s_a=\sigma_{\min}(F_a)>0,\qquad f_a=\|F_a\|_F
\tag{3}
\]
is triangular with diagonal entries one. Choose a Hermitian off-block matrix \(V\), \(\|V\|_F=1\), such that every selected feature is nonzero. Index selected features by \(j=(a,b,r,s)\), \(a<b\), and put
\[
v_j=|w_{a,r}^*V_{ab}w_{b,s}|,
\quad l_j={\|w_{a,r}\|\,\|w_{b,s}\|\over\sqrt2},
\quad\eta={1\over2}\min_j{v_j\over l_j},
\quad a_j={v_j-\eta l_j\over1+\eta}>0.
\tag{4}
\]
The \(\sqrt2\) accounts for both rectangular copies in a Hermitian off-block matrix. In particular \(0<\eta\le1/2\).

Here is an explicit block radius. For a block dimension \(d\), define
\[
t_1(d)=\sqrt d\,\kappa,\qquad
t_2(d)=(d+1)\kappa^2,\qquad
t_3(d)=(d^{3/2}+3\sqrt d+2)\kappa^3,
\]
\[
J=\max_a\left\{
\log(2/p_*)t_3(d_a)+3t_2(d_a)t_1(d_a)+t_1(d_a)^3
\right\},
\tag{5}
\]
and choose
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\delta=\min\left\{
{\tau\over8d_*},\quad {\gamma\over2J},\quad
\min_a{s_a\over2\kappa f_a},\quad
\min_j{a_j\over6\kappa l_j}
\right\}>0.}
\tag{6}
\]
Smaller positive \(\delta\) are also allowed. Put
\[
\zeta={(1-\delta/\tau)^{3d_*}\over(1+\delta/\tau)^{2d_*}},
\quad s'_a=s_a-\kappa\delta f_a,
\quad a'_j=a_j-\kappa\delta(2+\kappa\delta)l_j,
\]
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
c=\zeta^2\min_{a<b}\left[
(s'_a)^2(s'_b)^2
\min_{r,s}\omega_{a,r}\omega_{b,s}(a'_{abrs})^2
\right]>0.}
\tag{7}
\]
No optimization or unstated continuity modulus is required in these formulas. In particular \(s'_a\ge s_a/2\), \(a'_j\ge a_j/2\), and \(\zeta\ge(7/8)^5>1/2\). Relative to the corresponding frozen-feature constant \(c_D=\min_{a<b}s_a^2s_b^2\min_{r,s}\omega_{a,r}\omega_{b,s}a_{abrs}^2\), this gives the coarse lower bound \(c\ge c_D/256\); formula (7) is usually much better.

The entirely explicit positive constants \(A,B,C\) in Section 3 depend only on \(n,\kappa\). Define
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\varepsilon=\min\left\{
{1\over4n\kappa},\quad
\left({\gamma\over8A}\right)^{1/4},\quad
\sqrt{c\over4C},\quad
\left({c\gamma\over32B^2}\right)^{1/4}
\right\}.}
\tag{8}
\]
The promised corridor is
\[
\mathcal K=\left\{U+sV+E:
\begin{array}{l}
U=\bigoplus_a U_a,\quad\|U-D\|_F\le\delta,\\
E=E^*\text{ off-block},\quad\langle E,V\rangle_F=0,\\
0\le s\le\varepsilon/(1+\eta),\quad\|E\|_F\le\eta s
\end{array}\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

**Quantitative composition theorem.** Every point of \(\mathcal K\) is a strict contraction. For \(K=U+Z\in\mathcal K\), \(r=\|Z\|_F>0\), and every Hermitian direction \(T+W\), with \(T\) block diagonal and \(W\) off-block,
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
D^2H(K)[T+W,T+W]
\le-{\gamma\over4}\|T\|_F^2
-{c\over2}r^2\|W\|_F^2.}
\tag{10}
\]
The set \(\mathcal K\) is convex and has nonempty interior in the full real vector space of Hermitian matrices. Entropy is strictly concave on \(\mathcal K\), including finite segments with different diagonal blocks and different links. At its block-diagonal points the off-block Hessian is zero; (10) is asserted to be definite only when \(r>0\).

Full-rank centers always exist. Given the selected invertible \(F_a\), choose rectangular matrices \(J_{ab}\) with every entry nonzero and rank \(\min(d_a,d_b)\), for example an all-ones matrix plus its rectangular identity. Set \(\widetilde V_{ab}=(F_a^*)^{-1}J_{ab}F_b^{-1}\), complete Hermitian symmetry, and normalize. All selected features are then nonzero, and every rectangular link has its maximal possible rank. A sufficiently narrow version of the same cone retains those ranks. Thus this is not covered by a one-dimensional row-space assumption per block.

## 2. Uniform control of the blocks and Fisher features

We record the quantitative facts needed for (6)–(7).

**Signed atom inverse bound.** If \(\tau I\le D_a\le(1-\tau)I\), then every signed atom matrix \(N=D_a-I_J\), including those of principal restrictions, satisfies \(\|N^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1/\tau\). To see this, split present and absent coordinates. For every real \(|\lambda|<\tau\), the present diagonal block of \(N-\lambda I\) is positive definite and its absent Schur complement is negative definite. Thus \(N\) has no eigenvalue in \((-\tau,\tau)\). The all-present and all-absent cases are immediate.

For \(\|U-D\|_F\le\delta\), all such inverses at \(U\) have norm at most \(\kappa\). A prefix history on \(k\) coordinates has probability ratio, between \(U\) and \(D\), in
\[
[(1-\delta/\tau)^k,(1+\delta/\tau)^k].
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed its ratio is the positive signed-determinant ratio \(\det(I+N_D^{-1}\Delta)\), whose absolute determinant has these singular-value bounds. Hence all block atoms at \(U\) exceed \(p_*/2\).

For a block atom probability \(p\), its normalized derivatives in arbitrary Hermitian unit directions have respective bounds \(t_1(d),t_2(d),t_3(d)\). These follow from the exact determinant derivative formulas and
\[
|\operatorname{tr}RA|\le\sqrt d\,\kappa\|A\|_F,
\quad |\operatorname{tr}RARB|\le\kappa^2\|A\|_F\|B\|_F,
\quad |\operatorname{tr}RARBRC|\le\kappa^3\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\|C\|_F.
\]
Differentiating \(-\sum p\log p\) three times and using \(\sum p'''=0\) gives the bound (5). Summing over blocks yields
\[
\left|D^2\sum_aH(U_a)[T,T]-D^2\sum_aH(D_a)[T,T]\right|
\le J\delta\|T\|_F^2.
\]
Consequently (6) implies
\[
D^2\sum_aH(U_a)[T,T]\le-\gamma\|T\|_F^2/2.
\tag{12}
\]

For a fixed selected history, let \(R\) be the previously revealed sites and \(j\) the next site. Its feature has \(w_R=-N_R^{-1}D_{Rj}\), \(w_j=1\). The exact inverse identity gives
\[
w(U)_R-w(D)_R=-N_R(U)^{-1}\Delta_{R,R\cup\{j\}}w(D).
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(\|w(U)-w(D)\|\le\kappa\delta\|w(D)\|\), and
\[
\sigma_{\min}(F_a(U))\ge s'_a.
\tag{14}
\]
Writing a history probability as \(\pi\), and its two one-step extension probabilities as \(\pi_0,\pi_1\), its weight is \(\omega=\pi^3/(\pi_0\pi_1)\). Equation (11) gives
\[
\omega_{a,r}(U)\ge\zeta\omega_{a,r}(D).
\tag{15}
\]
For \(Z=sV+E\) in (9), the triangle inequality first gives
\[
|w_{a,r}(D)^*Z_{ab}w_{b,s}(D)|\ge a_j\|Z\|_F.
\]
Using (13) on both vectors then gives
\[
|w_{a,r}(U)^*Z_{ab}w_{b,s}(U)|\ge a'_j\|Z\|_F.
\tag{16}
\]
Here the vector perturbation error is bounded by \(\kappa\delta(2+\kappa\delta)l_j\|Z\|_F\).

Let \(\Phi(U,Z)=Q_U(Z)/2\), where \(Q\) is the root's exact Fisher-feature quartic. The elementary complex identity
\[
D^2(|z|^4/2)[u,u]=2|z|^2|u|^2+4(\operatorname{Re}\bar zu)^2
\]
together with (14)–(16), and
\(\|F_a(U)^*W_{ab}F_b(U)\|_F\ge s'_as'_b\|W_{ab}\|_F\), proves
\[
\Phi_{ZZ}(U,Z)[W,W]\ge c\,\|Z\|_F^2\|W\|_F^2.
\tag{17}
\]
This is uniform over the full corridor, with the explicit constant (7).

## 3. Explicit sixth-order remainder constants

Everything in this section is uniform over \(\|U-D\|_F\le\delta\). Let \(\mu_U\) be the product of the block laws, and \(R(S)=\bigoplus_a(U_a-I_{E_a\setminus S_a})^{-1}\). The exact density ratio is
\[
{p_{U+Z}(S)\over\mu_U(S)}=\det(I+R(S)Z)=1+q(S),
\qquad q=\sum_{k=2}^n q_k,
\tag{18}
\]
where \(q_k\) is homogeneous of degree \(k\) in \(Z\). There is no degree-one term because \(Z\) is off-block.

**Fifth-degree cancellation.** One has
\[
q_2=-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}((RZ)^2),\qquad
q_3=\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}((RZ)^3),\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mu_U}q_2q_3=0.}
\tag{19}
\]
The first expression is a sum over unordered block pairs. Every summand of the second visits three distinct blocks, since consecutive equal blocks would use a zero diagonal block of \(Z\). In any product of one pair summand and one triangle summand there is a triangle block absent from the pair. Its inverse appears exactly once, and its expectation is zero by differentiation of that block's total atom probability. Independence then kills that product. This proves (19) for all \(U\), so its derivatives in \(U\) also vanish.

Since the block marginals remain fixed,
\[
H(U+Z)=\sum_aH(U_a)-I(U,Z),\qquad
I=\mathbb E_{\mu_U}\{(1+q)\log(1+q)-q\}.
\]
Let \(\psi(q)=(1+q)\log(1+q)-q-q^2/2\). Then
\[
\boxed{I=\Phi+\mathcal R,\qquad
\mathcal R=\frac12\sum_{\substack{k,l\ge2\\k+l\ge6}}
\mathbb E_{\mu_U}q_kq_l+\mathbb E_{\mu_U}\psi(q).}
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal R\) starts in degree six, without any unspecified analytic tail.

The following finite sums make all differentiated bounds explicit. Put
\[
r_0={1\over4n\kappa},\quad L=\sqrt n\,\kappa,
\quad M=(n+1)\kappa^2,
\quad\beta_k={n\choose k}\kappa^k\quad(2\le k\le n),
\]
and set \(\beta_k=0\) outside this range. Define
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
b_0=\sum_k\beta_kr_0^{k-2},&
b_1=\sum_k k\beta_kr_0^{k-2},&
b_2=\sum_k k(k-1)\beta_kr_0^{k-2},\\
e_0=\kappa b_1,&
e_1=\kappa\sum_k k^2\beta_kr_0^{k-2},&
e_2=\kappa^2\sum_k k(k+1)\beta_kr_0^{k-2},
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]
\[
c_d=\sum_k\beta_k\beta_{d-k},\qquad
\begin{aligned}
D_2&=\tfrac12\sum_{d=6}^{2n}d(d-1)c_dr_0^{d-6},\\
D_1&=\tfrac12\sum_{d=6}^{2n}d(L+d\kappa)c_dr_0^{d-6},\\
D_0&=\tfrac12\sum_{d=6}^{2n}
\{M+2Ld\kappa+d(d+1)\kappa^2\}c_dr_0^{d-6}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Empty sums are zero. Finally put
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_2&=D_2+2b_0b_1^2+b_0^2b_2,\\
R_1&=D_1+Lb_0^2b_1+2b_0e_0b_1+b_0^2e_1,\\
R_0&=D_0+\tfrac M3b_0^3+2Lb_0^2e_0+2b_0e_0^2+b_0^2e_2,\\
P_0&=(M/2+4L\kappa+10\kappa^2)\beta_2^2,\\
P_1&=(2L+8\kappa)\beta_2^2,\\
\boxed{A=P_0+R_0r_0^2,\qquad B=P_1+R_1r_0^2,\qquad C=R_2.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
These are positive expressions involving at most \(2n\) terms; the reproduction script evaluates them directly.

Here is a derivation of their error bounds. Write \(r=\|Z\|_F\le r_0\), \(t=\|T\|_F\), \(w=\|W\|_F\). Columnwise determinant differentiation gives
\[
|q_k|\le\beta_kr^k,\quad
|(q_k)_Z|\le k\beta_kr^{k-1}w,\quad
|(q_k)_{ZZ}|\le k(k-1)\beta_kr^{k-2}w^2.
\tag{24}
\]
An internal-block derivative replaces a factor \(R\) by \(-RTR\), and a second derivative replaces it by \(2RTRTR\) or differentiates two factors. The additional multipliers for first \(U\), mixed \(UZ\), and second \(U\) derivatives are respectively \(k\kappa\), \(k^2\kappa\), and \(k(k+1)\kappa^2\), with the appropriate powers of \(r\) and direction norms. Consequently
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
|q|\le b_0r^2,&|q_Z|\le b_1rw,&|q_{ZZ}|\le b_2w^2,\\
|q_U|\le e_0r^2t,&|q_{UZ}|\le e_1rtw,&|q_{UU}|\le e_2r^2t^2.
\end{array}
\tag{25}
\]
The product measure satisfies \(|\mu_U'/\mu_U|\le Lt\) and \(|\mu_U''/\mu_U|\le Mt^2\). Also
\[
|q|\le e^{n\kappa r}-1-n\kappa r<1/2,
\quad |\psi(q)|\le|q|^3/3,
\quad |\psi'(q)|\le q^2,
\quad |\psi''(q)|\le2|q|.
\tag{26}
\]
For the polynomial part of (20), a product of total degree \(d\) has the same derivative multipliers as a degree-\(d\) product of inverse factors. Differentiating its measure as well gives precisely (22). Applying (25)–(26) and differentiating the measure gives the remaining terms of \(R_i\). Hence
\[
|\mathcal R_{ZZ}|\le R_2r^4w^2,\qquad
|\mathcal R_{UZ}|\le R_1r^5tw,\qquad
|\mathcal R_{UU}|\le R_0r^6t^2.
\tag{27}
\]
The same calculation applied just to \(\Phi=\frac12\mathbb E q_2^2\) gives
\[
|\Phi_{UZ}|\le P_1r^3tw,\qquad
|\Phi_{UU}|\le P_0r^4t^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Every derivative above permits arbitrary complex Hermitian directions, and includes differentiation of the product measure. No radial estimate is being promoted to a matrix Hessian estimate.

## 4. Complete proof of the corridor theorem

Combine (12), (17), and (23), (27)–(28):
\[
D^2H(U+Z)[T+W,T+W]
\le(-\gamma/2+Ar^4)t^2+2Br^3tw+(-cr^2+Cr^4)w^2.
\tag{29}
\]
Young's inequality gives
\[
2Br^3tw\le\gamma t^2/8+(8B^2/\gamma)r^6w^2.
\]
The four conditions in (8) imply
\[
Ar^4\le\gamma/8,\quad Cr^2\le c/4,
\quad(8B^2/\gamma)r^4\le c/4.
\]
Substitution proves (10).

All corridor kernels are strict because
\(\|U-D+Z\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta+r_0<\tau\).
The constraints in (9) are a Euclidean ball, linear subspaces, an interval, and a norm cone. Their affine image is therefore convex. The orthogonality constraint makes the decomposition \(Z=sV+E\) unique; the cone has interior in the whole off-block space, while the block ball has interior in the whole block-diagonal space. This proves the stated full dimension.

At \(Z=0\), (18)–(20) show that the mixed and off-block entropy Hessians vanish, and (12) controls its block part. Thus the Hessian is nonpositive everywhere on \(\mathcal K\). Integrate along any affine segment in the convex set. A nonconstant segment with nonzero link in its interior has strictly negative curvature there by (10); the norm cone has \(s\ge0\), so any segment with at least one linked endpoint has positive \(s\) throughout its open interior. A wholly block-diagonal nonconstant segment is strictly concave by (12). This proves strict concavity on the entire corridor, including its apex face.

## 5. Concrete full-rank example and reproduction

Take two identical coherent two-site blocks
\[
D_1=D_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/10\\1/10&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
V_{12}={1\over\sqrt{14}}
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{30}
\]
The Hermitian completion of \(V\) has norm one and its rectangular link has determinant \(1/14\), hence rank two. Here \(p_*=6/25\), \(\tau=2/5\). Selecting the first reveal and then the history in which the first site is absent gives
\[
F_1=F_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/5\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (\omega_1,\omega_2)=(4,625/312).
\]
An entirely elementary gap certificate is \(\gamma=2/13\). In the orthonormal identity, diagonal-difference, real-off-diagonal, and imaginary-off-diagonal directions, the four negative-Hessian eigenvalues of a block are
\[
\frac{25}{6}-2\log(13/12),\quad
\frac{50}{13}+2\log(13/12),\quad
\frac1{25}\left(\frac{25}{6}+\frac{50}{13}\right)+2\log(13/12),\quad
2\log(13/12).
\tag{31}
\]
The bound follows from \(1/13\le\log(13/12)\le1/12\). It covers every complex Hermitian block direction.

`computations/C028/verify.py` evaluates the displayed formulas and writes `results.json`. It also checks (19) exactly with rational arithmetic for **three** coherent two-site blocks and three rank-two complex links, verifies the root's quartic identity exactly, and checks that \(\mathbb E q_3^2>0\). The last condition confirms that the cancellation example is not merely bipartite, where \(q_3\) would vanish pointwise. Finally it computes the complete 16-dimensional real Hermitian Hessian at three points of (30)'s corridor with 80-decimal-digit arithmetic and checks (10).

The executed example gives
\[
\eta=0.1889822365\ldots,\qquad
\delta=1.7688852163\cdot10^{-5},\qquad
\varepsilon=2.6228278774\cdot10^{-5},\qquad
c=0.12893104668\ldots.
\]
All three numerical Hessians satisfy the full bound (10); the smallest eigenvalue of the positive slack matrix is greater than \(4.05\cdot10^{-12}\) across these examples. The exact three-block calculation gives \(\mathbb E q_2q_3=0\), zero error in the Fisher quartic identity, and \(\mathbb E q_3^2=6308459100985923/6112537306362500000>0\).

The proof is analytic and applies in every finite dimension. The finite exact identities and numerical Hessian examples corroborate it; they are not substitutes for the uniform bounds. The constants are conservative and no optimal radius is claimed. Generic selected-feature nonvanishing is still needed; the result quantitatively solves the arbitrary-rank weak-link composition task rather than a degenerate coupling stratum.
