## 1. Gauge coordinates, physical coverage, and frozen coefficients

A diagonal unitary does not change any occupation probability and carries arbitrary Hermitian directions bijectively to arbitrary Hermitian directions. Thus it suffices to write
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\rho_3&\rho_2\\
\rho_3&1&\rho_1e^{i\theta}\\
\rho_2&\rho_1e^{-i\theta}&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i})R
\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i}),\quad z=\cos\theta\in[-1,1].\tag{4}
\]
Set
\[
u_i=1-\rho_i^2,\qquad P=\rho_1\rho_2\rho_3,\qquad
\Delta=\det R=1-\sum_i\rho_i^2+2Pz.\tag{5}
\]
Throughout (1), Gershgorin's theorem gives
\[
\frac4{25}I\preceq R\preceq\frac{46}{25}I,
\qquad \frac2{25}I\preceq L\preceq\frac{92}{25}I.
\]
Applying \(x\mapsto x/(1+x)\) spectrally proves (3). Thus every independent parameter choice in the specified box and every phase is physical. An additional useful exact bound is
\[
\Delta\ge1-3(21/50)^2-2(21/50)^3
=\frac{5041}{15625}>0.\tag{6}
\]

The exact atoms are \(p_S=\det L_S/\det(I+L)\). Their nonmodular frozen negative-log coefficients are
\[
c_i=-\log u_i\quad\text{(the coefficient on the pair complementary to \(i\))},
\qquad \tau=\log\frac{u_1u_2u_3}{\Delta}.
\]
Define
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,c_2,c_3)+\tau K,
\quad d_i=M_{ii},\quad \delta=\det M.\tag{7}
\]
All logarithms are natural. The conditional pair coefficients have the cancellation-free formula
\[
\boxed{c_i+\tau
=\log\left(1+\frac{(\rho_i-\rho_j\rho_k)^2+2P(1-z)}{\Delta}\right)\ge0,}
\quad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.\tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(u_ju_k-\Delta=\rho_i^2+\rho_j^2\rho_k^2-2Pz\). This identity is used directly in the interval certificate.

The matrix \(M\) is positive definite. For \(\tau\ge0\), its positive diagonal part suffices. For \(\tau<0\),
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_i+\tau)+(-\tau)(I-K)>0.
\]
In particular \(d_i,\delta>0\) throughout the chart.

## 2. A single explicit dual witness controls all nine directions

Let \(\mathcal I_K(A)=\sum_S(Dp_S[A])^2/p_S\) be complete atom Fisher information. The exact three-site identity from old-097 is
\[
-D^2H(P_K)[A,A]=\mathcal I_K(A)-2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).\tag{9}
\]
It follows by expressing the frozen negative logarithm in its subset-Möbius coefficients and differentiating the inclusion moments. Only the three pair coefficients and triple coefficient contribute second derivatives.

Let \(\Sigma\) be the covariance matrix of the three occupation indicators:
\[
\Sigma_{ii}=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii}),\qquad
\Sigma_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2\quad(i\ne j).
\]
It is positive definite because every atom is positive. Projection of the atom score onto the centered indicators gives
\[
\mathcal I_K(A)\ge \operatorname{diag}(A)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(A).\tag{10}
\]
Put
\[
C_3=(|M_{ij}|^2)_{i,j}+\delta\Sigma>0.
\]
The three-indicator criterion of old-097 is
\(d^{\mathsf T}C_3^{-1}d\ge2\), sufficient for nonpositive full Hermitian curvature. Here an explicit one-dimensional choice inside its dual variational formula is enough. Take
\[
r_i=1/d_i,
\]
and define the exact scalar
\[
\boxed{T=\delta\sum_i\frac{K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})}{d_i^2}
+2(\tau^2-\delta)\sum_{i<j}\frac{|K_{ij}|^2}{d_id_j}.}\tag{11}
\]
Since \(M_{ij}=\tau K_{ij}\) for distinct indices,
\[
d^{\mathsf T}r=3,\qquad r^{\mathsf T}C_3r=3+T>0.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz, equivalently optimization over all scalar multiples of \(r\), proves
\[
\boxed{d^{\mathsf T}C_3^{-1}d\ge\frac9{3+T}.}\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(T\le3/2\) suffices for full entropy concavity; \(T\le7/5\) gives the strict bound
\[
d^{\mathsf T}C_3^{-1}d\ge\frac{45}{22}.\tag{13}
\]

For clarity, (13) gives a quantitative curvature gap without any loss of unmeasured Hermitian directions. Write \(S=M^{1/2}\), \(A=SBS\), and let \(\mathcal F_3\) denote the quadratic form on \(B\) supplied by the right side of (10). The adjugate congruence identity gives
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}(SBS))
=\delta\{(\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\|B\|_F^2\}.
\]
Set \(\mathcal P=\mathcal F_3+\delta\operatorname{Id}>0\). With feature matrices \(V_i=Se_ie_i^*S\), their Gram matrix is \((|M_{ij}|^2)\), and \(\langle I,V_i\rangle=d_i\). Woodbury's identity gives
\[
\delta\langle I,\mathcal P^{-1}I\rangle
=3-d^{\mathsf T}C_3^{-1}d\le\frac{21}{22}.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz in the \(\mathcal P\) inner product therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
-D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
&\ge\langle B,\mathcal PB\rangle-\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2\\
&\ge\frac1{22}\langle B,\mathcal PB\rangle
\ge\frac\delta{22}\|B\|_F^2
\ge\frac\delta{22\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}\|A\|_F^2.\tag{14}
\end{aligned}
\]
No invertibility of the complete Fisher operator is needed.

## 3. A positive scalar envelope and elimination of the three fields

Hadamard's determinant inequality gives \(\delta\le d_1d_2d_3\). Dropping the negative \(-\delta\) part of the second term in (11), and using \(K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})\le1/4\), gives
\[
T\le U:=\frac14\sum_i\frac{d_jd_k}{d_i}
+2\tau^2\sum_{i<j}\frac{W_{ij}}{d_id_j},\tag{15}
\]
provided \(W_{ij}\ge|K_{ij}|^2\). The following choices enclose all fields \(\ell_i\in[1/2,2]\) through rational functions of just \((\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3,z)\).

First, under variation of the odds fields, the DPP law has weights
\(\det R_S\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i\). Differentiation gives
\[
\frac{\partial K_{ii}}{\partial\ell_i}
=\frac{K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})}{\ell_i}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial K_{ii}}{\partial\ell_j}
=-\frac{|K_{ij}|^2}{\ell_j}\le0\quad(j\ne i).\tag{16}
\]
Thus the minimum of \(K_{ii}\) over the entire field box occurs at
\(\ell_i=1/2,\ell_j=\ell_k=2\), and the maximum at the opposite corner.

These corner values are evaluated by the exact effective odds formula
\[
K_{ii}=\frac{q_i}{1+q_i},\qquad
q_i=\frac{\ell_i(1+\ell_ju_k+\ell_ku_j+\ell_j\ell_k\Delta)}
{1+\ell_j+\ell_k+\ell_j\ell_ku_i}.\tag{17}
\]
The numerator of \(K_{ii}\) is the total weight of atoms containing \(i\); its complementary denominator in odds form is the total weight of atoms omitting \(i\). Schur complementation also gives \(q_i\le\ell_i\), which supplies a harmless additional upper bound. Write the resulting interval as \([k_i^-,k_i^+]\).

For any fixed shape,
\[
\boxed{d_i=(1-K_{ii})c_i+K_{ii}(c_i+\tau).}\tag{18}
\]
This is a convex interpolation between two nonnegative log coefficients. The certificate intersects two rigorous enclosures: direct evaluation of \(c_i+\tau[k_i^-,k_i^+]\), and evaluation of the two endpoints of the linear interpolation (18), with (8) used for its second log coefficient. This intersection is particularly useful when a conditional correlation is close to zero. It retains the entire continuous field box.

For \(W_{jk}\), eliminate site \(i\) from \(I+L\). Put
\[
s=\frac{\ell_i}{1+\ell_i}\in[1/3,2/3].
\]
The effective two-site odds matrix has diagonal terms
\(\ell_j(1-s\rho_k^2)\), \(\ell_k(1-s\rho_j^2)\) and normalized squared correlation
\[
\boxed{b_{jk}(s)=
\frac{\rho_i^2-2sPz+s^2\rho_j^2\rho_k^2}
{(1-s\rho_j^2)(1-s\rho_k^2)}\in[0,1).}\tag{19}
\]
An arbitrary positive two-site odds matrix with diagonal terms \(a,b\) and normalized correlation magnitude \(v\) has
\[
|K_{12}|=
\frac{v\sqrt{ab}}{1+a+b+(1-v^2)ab}
\le\frac{v}{2(1+\sqrt{1-v^2})}.
\]
Indeed \(a+b\ge2\sqrt{ab}\), and the resulting one-variable rational function attains its maximum at \(\sqrt{ab}=1/\sqrt{1-v^2}\). Since \(\sqrt{1-x}\ge1-x\) on \([0,1]\),
\[
|K_{jk}|^2\le\frac{b_{jk}(s)}{4(2-b_{jk}(s))^2}.\tag{20}
\]
The right side is increasing in \(b_{jk}\). Evaluate (19) by interval arithmetic with the whole interval \(s\in[1/3,2/3]\). If its upper endpoint is \(\beta<1\), use
\[
W_{jk}=\frac{\max(\beta,0)}{4(2-\max(\beta,0))^2}.
\]
If the enclosure reaches one, use the universal contraction bound \(W_{jk}=1/4\). This follows from \(|K_{jk}|^2\le\min(K_{jj}K_{kk},(1-K_{jj})(1-K_{kk}))\le1/4\).

Equations (15)–(20) define a fully explicit four-variable interval upper bound for \(T\). All three external fields have been eliminated by proved inequalities. The interval certificate checks this upper bound, including variation of all fields, rather than checking only field corners of the original Hessian.

## 5. An explicit uniform Frobenius gap

We finish by deriving the second inequality in (2) analytically. Since \(-\log(1-x)\ge x\),
\[
c_*:=\min_i c_i\ge1/100.
\]
For \(\tau\ge0\), \(M\succeq c_*I\). For \(\tau<0\), (8) implies \(-\tau\le c_*\), while (3) gives
\[
M\succeq\left(c_*+\frac{92}{117}\tau\right)I
\succeq\frac{25}{117}c_*I
\succeq\frac1{468}I.\tag{23}
\]
Hence (23) holds in either case.

Also
\[
\max_i c_i\le\frac{441}{2059}<\frac3{10},\qquad
\tau\le-\log\Delta\le\log\frac{15625}{5041}<\frac65.
\]
The last strict comparison is exact: the first five terms of the exponential series give
\[
e^{6/5}>1+6/5+(6/5)^2/2+(6/5)^3/6+(6/5)^4/24
=2059/625>15625/5041.
\]
For positive \(\tau\), \(M\preceq(\max c_i+\tau)I\prec(3/2)I\). For negative \(\tau\), the upper bound \(M\preceq(\max c_i)I\) is stronger. Thus
\[
\delta\ge468^{-3},\qquad \|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le3/2.
\]
Substituting into (14) yields
\[
\frac\delta{22\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}
\ge\frac{2}{99\cdot468^3}>0,
\]
which completes (2).
