# C084 — independent-rate tubes at complex rank-two projections

**Status: proved restricted full-Hessian theorem; no counterexample found or claimed.**

This construction treats a four-site rank-two projection limit, distinct from the completed three-site projection strata and four-site `(0,p,q,1)` stratum. The argument gives an abstract all-dimension criterion and an explicit non-count-uniform complex four-site example. Historical priority is not asserted. It does not settle Lyons' general conjecture.

## 1. The theorem

Let `[V,W]` be unitary on `C^n`, with `V` having `r` columns and `W` having `s=n-r` columns, where `1<=r<n`. Write

\[
P=VV^*,\qquad Q=WW^*.
\]

Assume every rank-`r` projection atom is positive:

\[
|\det V_T|^2>0\qquad(|T|=r).\tag{1}
\]

For `|S|=r-1` and `|T|=r+1`, define the real linear maps on Hermitian matrices

\[
\ell^-_S(B)=-Dp_S(P)[VBV^*],\qquad
\ell^+_T(C)=Dp_T(P)[WCW^*].\tag{2}
\]

These are positive rank-one matrix functionals. Assume both maps are injective:

\[
[\ell^-_S(B)=0\ \forall S]\Longrightarrow B=0,
\qquad
[\ell^+_T(C)=0\ \forall T]\Longrightarrow C=0.\tag{3}
\]

Fix positive definite `H` on `C^r` and `J` on `C^s`. Then at

\[
\boxed{K_{\eta,\epsilon}
=V(I_r-\eta H)V^*+\epsilon WJW^*,}\tag{4}
\]

the **full real `n^2`-dimensional Hermitian entropy Hessian is negative definite** whenever `eta,epsilon>0` are both sufficiently small. Their ratio is unrestricted. More precisely, write an arbitrary direction in this splitting as

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}B&X\\X^*&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad
L_\eta=\log(1/\eta),\quad L_\epsilon=\log(1/\epsilon),\quad
S_0=L_\eta+L_\epsilon.
\]

There are constants `c,delta>0` such that for `0<eta,epsilon<delta`,

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(P_{K_{\eta,\epsilon}})[A,A]
\le-c\left(\frac{\|B\|_F^2}{\eta}
+\frac{\|C\|_F^2}{\epsilon}
+S_0\|X\|_F^2\right).}\tag{5}
\]

Here and below, `D^2H(P_K)` denotes the entropy Hessian with respect to the marginal kernel `K`. The notation does not indicate differentiation of the projection `P`.

The constants can be uniform on compact sets of frames satisfying (1)-(3) and shapes with strictly positive eigenvalues. Thus the theorem allows varying nearby eigenspaces and shapes. It permits arbitrary separation of the two endpoint scales; it does not permit unbounded condition numbers within `H` or within `J` in the uniform statement.

## 2. Positive atom factorizations at two independent rates

For the proof, diagonalize `H,J` within their fixed subspaces. The DPP is the spectral Bernoulli mixture of projection laws. If an atom has size `k<r`, every spectral selection producing it removes at least `r-k` occupied eigenmodes. If it selects `a` null modes, it removes `r-k+a` occupied modes. Therefore the exact atom polynomial has the factorization

\[
p_S(K_{\eta,\epsilon})=
\begin{cases}
\eta^{r-|S|}c_S(\eta,\epsilon),&|S|<r,\\
c_S(\eta,\epsilon),&|S|=r,\\
\epsilon^{|S|-r}c_S(\eta,\epsilon),&|S|>r.
\end{cases}\tag{6}
\]

Every `c_S` is analytic and positive at `(0,0)`.

To justify strict positivity, for `k<r` the leading coefficient is a positive weighted sum of squares of the `k`-minors of `V_S`. Condition (1) implies that these rows have rank `k`: extend them to any `r` rows. Thus at least one leading minor is nonzero. For `k>r`, apply the same argument to holes and use the complementary-minor identity for `[V,W]`. For `k=r`, the coefficient is exactly the positive atom in (1). Consequently all the `c_S` stay bounded above and away from zero in a sufficiently small closed rectangle.

For adjacent cardinalities the leading coefficients are

\[
c_S(0,0)=\ell^-_S(H)\quad(|S|=r-1),\qquad
c_T(0,0)=\ell^+_T(J)\quad(|T|=r+1).\tag{7}
\]

The positive rank-one interpretation in (2) is explicit: take the cofactor vector of the `(r-1)`-row matrix `V_S`. If it is denoted `u_S`, then

\[
\ell^-_S(B)=u_S^*Bu_S.
\]

The analogous cofactor vector of `W_{T^c}` gives `ell^+_T`. Condition (1) makes each such vector nonzero. This also follows by taking the first hole/insertion coefficient in the spectral mixture.

## 3. Fisher information separates the two spectral blocks

Define the positive definite forms

\[
\mathcal F^-_H(B)=\sum_{|S|=r-1}
\frac{\ell^-_S(B)^2}{\ell^-_S(H)},\qquad
\mathcal F^+_J(C)=\sum_{|T|=r+1}
\frac{\ell^+_T(C)^2}{\ell^+_T(J)}.\tag{8}
\]

Their definiteness is exactly (3). The first derivatives of the adjacent atoms have the uniform forms

\[
Dp_S[A]= -\ell^-_S(B)
+O((\eta+\epsilon)\|B\|_F)
+\eta O(\|C\|_F+\|X\|_F),\tag{9}
\]

\[
Dp_T[A]= \ell^+_T(C)
+O((\eta+\epsilon)\|C\|_F)
+\epsilon O(\|B\|_F+\|X\|_F).\tag{10}
\]

The displayed factors `eta` and `epsilon` are essential; an undifferentiated `O(eta+epsilon)||A||` bound would not justify independent rates.

Here is a proof of those factors. At `eta=0`, the kernel has `r` eigenvalues equal to one, so all atoms of size `r-1` vanish identically under every null-block perturbation and every unitary rotation. Arbitrary mixing directions are tangents to those rotations, since `I_s-epsilon J` is invertible. Therefore their first derivatives vanish at `eta=0`. Polynomiality makes their coefficients divisible by `eta`. The occupied-block coefficients are analytic and have the limiting value in (2). This proves (9). At `epsilon=0`, the kernel has rank `r`; the same reasoning with rank-preserving rotations and occupied-block perturbations proves (10).

Set

\[
B=\sqrt\eta\,b,\qquad C=\sqrt\epsilon\,c,\qquad
X=\chi/\sqrt{S_0}.\tag{11}
\]

Using (6)-(10), the sum of the adjacent-atom Fisher terms is

\[
\sum_{|S|=r-1\text{ or }r+1}\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}
=\mathcal F^-_H(b)+\mathcal F^+_J(c)
+o(1)(\|b\|_F^2+\|c\|_F^2+\|\chi\|_F^2),\tag{12}
\]

uniformly as the two rates tend independently to zero. For example, after division by `sqrt(p_S)`, the error in a lower-cardinality score is bounded by a constant times

\[
(\eta+\epsilon)\|b\|_F+
\sqrt{\eta\epsilon}\|c\|_F+
\sqrt{\eta/S_0}\|\chi\|_F.
\]

All other Fisher terms are nonnegative and may be discarded in an upper bound on the entropy Hessian.

## 4. Exact cancellation of all singular logarithms

Let `N=|X_DPP|` be the occupation count, and define the spectral polynomials

\[
G_-(K)=\mathbb E_K(r-N)_+,
\qquad G_+(K)=\mathbb E_K(N-r)_+.
\]

Equation (6) gives the **exact** entropy-Hessian decomposition

\[
H''[A,A]=-\mathrm{FI}(A)
+L_\eta D^2G_-(K)[A,A]
+L_\epsilon D^2G_+(K)[A,A]
+R_{\eta,\epsilon}(A),\tag{13}
\]

where

\[
R_{\eta,\epsilon}(A)=-\sum_S D^2p_S(K)[A,A]\log c_S(\eta,\epsilon)
\]

is a uniformly bounded quadratic form. No logarithmic atom is omitted, including counts farther than one from `r`.

At the projection, the count generating polynomial gives an exact second-order identity. With `e_2(B)=((tr B)^2-tr(B^2))/2`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{P+tA}z^N
={}&z^r+t(z-1)[z^{r-1}\operatorname{tr}B+z^r\operatorname{tr}C]\\
&+t^2(z-1)^2\bigl[
 z^{r-2}e_2(B)
+z^{r-1}(\operatorname{tr}B\operatorname{tr}C-\|X\|_F^2)
+z^r e_2(C)\bigr]+O(t^3).
\end{aligned}\tag{14}
\]

Terms involving `e_2` vanish when their block has dimension one. Applying either count weight `(r-N)_+` or `(N-r)_+` to (14) cancels both `e_2` terms and yields

\[
D^2G_\pm(P)[A,A]
=2(\operatorname{tr}B\operatorname{tr}C-\|X\|_F^2).\tag{15}
\]

The mixing coefficient is therefore universally negative.

The next bound is what makes two arbitrary rates safe. For both signs, analyticity and the block symmetries give

\[
\left|D^2G_\pm(K)[A,A]+2\|X\|_F^2\right|
\le C\left[
\epsilon\|B\|_F^2+\eta\|C\|_F^2
+\|B\|_F\|C\|_F
+(\eta+\epsilon)\|X\|_F^2\right].\tag{16}
\]

To prove (16), observe:

- At `epsilon=0`, every supported-block line has `N<=r`, so `G_-=r-tr K` and `G_+=0`; both `BB` Hessians vanish. Their coefficients are therefore `O(epsilon)`.
- At `eta=0`, every null-block line has `N>=r`, so `G_-=0` and `G_+=tr K-r`; both `CC` Hessians vanish. Their coefficients are `O(eta)`.
- Conjugation by `diag(I_r,-I_s)` leaves the base fixed and changes `X` to `-X`. Since these count polynomials are unitarily invariant, there are no `BX` or `CX` Hessian terms.
- The `BC` coefficients are bounded; the `XX` coefficients converge to (15) with error `O(eta+epsilon)`.

Thus (16) follows from finite-dimensional polynomial coefficient bounds, without an assumption relating the rates.

After (11), the two logarithmic terms in (13) are

\[
-2\|\chi\|_F^2+
O(\eta+\epsilon)\|\chi\|_F^2
+O\bigl(\eta\epsilon S_0\bigr)(\|b\|_F^2+\|c\|_F^2)
+O\bigl(\sqrt{\eta\epsilon}S_0\bigr)\|b\|_F\|c\|_F.\tag{17}
\]

Every error tends to zero independently. For example,

\[
\sqrt{\eta\epsilon}(L_\eta+L_\epsilon)
\le\sqrt\eta L_\eta+\sqrt\epsilon L_\epsilon\longrightarrow0.
\]

This handles even regimes where `epsilon log(1/eta)` is unbounded. The bounded remainder in (13) is `o(1)` after scaling because

\[
\|A\|_F^2=\eta\|b\|_F^2+\epsilon\|c\|_F^2+2S_0^{-1}\|\chi\|_F^2.
\]

## 5. Closing the full-Hessian sign

Combining (12), (13), and (17) gives the one-sided operator asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
H''[A,A]\le
-\mathcal F^-_H(b)-\mathcal F^+_J(c)-2\|\chi\|_F^2
+\rho(\eta,\epsilon)(\|b\|_F^2+\|c\|_F^2+\|\chi\|_F^2),}\tag{18}
\]

where `rho -> 0` independently in the two rates. One admissible form of the error bound, up to a finite parameter-dependent constant, is

\[
\rho\le C\left[
\eta+\epsilon+S_0^{-1}+
\sqrt{\eta\epsilon}S_0+
\sqrt{(\eta+\epsilon)/S_0}\right].\tag{19}
\]

By injectivity, the first two forms have positive minimum eigenvalues. Choose `delta` so that `rho` is less than half the minimum of those eigenvalues and `2`. This proves (5). There are `r^2+s^2` Fisher-controlled modes and `2rs` logarithmically controlled mixing modes, totaling `n^2`.

Equation (18) is an upper bound; discarded farther-cardinality Fisher terms need not be assigned a limiting value. The proof does not infer a global sign from numerical Hessians.

## 6. Four sites: an explicit geometric criterion

When `n=4,r=2`, write `v_i,w_i` for the rows of `V,W`. The maps reduce to

\[
\ell^-_i(B)=v_i\operatorname{adj}(B)v_i^*,\qquad
\ell^+_{[4]\setminus i}(C)=w_i\operatorname{adj}(C)w_i^*.\tag{20}
\]

The two-by-two adjugate is a linear invertible map on Hermitian matrices. Thus injectivity is precisely that the four row projectors span `Herm(2)`.

In fact the single invariant condition

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}(P_{12}P_{23}P_{31})\ne0}\tag{21}
\]

implies both injectivity assumptions and all six positive projection atoms.

For a proof, write `R_i=v_i^*v_i=(t_i I+r_i dot sigma)/2` in Pauli coordinates. Since `sum_i R_i=I`, the four real vectors `r_i` sum to zero. The `R_i` span `Herm(2)` exactly when `r_1,r_2,r_3` are linearly independent. The identity

\[
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{tr}(R_1R_2R_3)
=\tfrac14 r_1\mathbin\cdot(r_2\mathbin\times r_3)
=\operatorname{Im}(P_{12}P_{23}P_{31})
\]

proves the criterion. For the complementary row frame, the off-diagonal entries are `Q_ij=-P_ij`, so its triple product has the opposite imaginary part and is also nonzero. If two rows of `V` were proportional (or a row were zero), the four row projectors could not be independent. Hence all two-row minors are nonzero. This proves all the abstract hypotheses.

Consequently every four-site rank-two projection satisfying (21) has a full sixteen-dimensional strict-concavity tube (4), with arbitrarily separated insertion and hole rates. Real row frames, and complex frames failing this invariant test, are outside this criterion; their unmeasured imaginary block modes need a separate finite or logarithmic argument.

## 7. Concrete rational complex example and diagnostics

Take

\[
Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\\1&i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-1&i\\1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad P=Z(Z^*Z)^{-1}Z^*.
\]

Here `Z*Y=0`, and `det(Z*Z)=7`. The six pair probabilities in order `12,13,14,23,24,34` are

\[
(1,1,1,1,1,2)/7.
\]

Thus the projection law is not count-uniform. The four row projectors of `Z` directly measure the two diagonal entries and the real and imaginary off-diagonal entries of a Hermitian two-by-two matrix, so they span. Normalization by `(Z*Z)^(-1/2)` is an invertible congruence and preserves spanning. The same elementary argument applies to `Y`.

The reproducible diagnostic uses the rational complex affine family

\[
K_{\eta,\epsilon}=P-\eta ZDZ^*+\epsilon YEY^*,
\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\quad
E=\begin{pmatrix}2&1/2+i/3\\1/2-i/3&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both shapes are strictly positive: `det D=2` and `det E=59/36`. Their ambient traces are `tr(ZDZ*)=9` and `tr(YEY*)=28/3`, so the trace bound proves strict physical feasibility whenever `0<eta<1/9` and `0<epsilon<3/28`. The imaginary triangle invariant is exactly `-1/49`; the unnormalized row-feature determinants are `4` and `-4`.

The script computes all sixteen exact atom polynomials by symbolic determinants, checks the two-rate factors and their positive leading coefficients, and evaluates the analytic full-Hessian identity at 75-digit precision in all sixteen rescaled Hermitian modes. All seven sampled scaled Hessians are strictly negative numerically. Their largest eigenvalues range from approximately `-0.0331004` to `-0.0314935`; the limiting Fisher/mixing form has largest eigenvalue `-0.03177902406748`. The samples include `(eta,epsilon)=(10^-6,10^-30)` and its reversal. These are bounded diagnostics, separate from the theorem.
