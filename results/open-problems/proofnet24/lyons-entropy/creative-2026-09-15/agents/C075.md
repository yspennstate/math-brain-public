## 1. The theorem

For every integer \(n\ge2\), let
\[
P=\frac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^*,\qquad
K=a(I-P)+cP,\qquad 0<a,c<1.
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{D^2H(P_K)[A,A]\le0\quad\text{for every complex Hermitian }A.}
\tag{2}
\]
If \(a\ne c\), the full Hermitian Hessian is negative definite. If \(a=c\),
\[
D^2H(P_{aI})[A,A]=-\frac1{a(1-a)}\sum_i A_{ii}^2,
\tag{3}
\]
so the nullspace is exactly the zero-diagonal Hermitian directions.

Consequently the two-parameter occupation entropy is concave throughout the closed square \(0\le a,c\le1\). The assertion is pointwise in the full ambient Hermitian space: arbitrary symmetry-breaking directions, including imaginary ones, are retained. Every strict base with \(a\ne c\) has an ambient neighborhood of negative-definite Hessians by continuity.

Diagonal unitary conjugation preserves all occupation probabilities. Thus \(P\) can be replaced by \(vv^*\) for every unit vector with \(|v_i|=1/\sqrt n\). No arbitrary unitary-conjugation assertion is made. For \(n=1\), the kernel is simply the scalar \(c\) and the ordinary Bernoulli entropy handles the assertion.

The central new step is a binomially weighted version of the local Shepp–Olkin argument. It proves the stronger scalar statement
\[
\boxed{
(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\longmapsto
H\!\left(\sum_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)\right)
+\mathbb E\log {n\choose \sum_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)}
\quad\text{is concave}.}
\tag{4}
\]
The Bernoulli variables in this display are independent. This is an entropy statement about the uniform shuffle of their occupation vector; it is not an assertion that arbitrary such shuffled laws are determinantal.

## 2. The exact external local inequality

Let \(p_i(t)=p_i+t v_i\) be affine Bernoulli parameters, with \(0<p_i<1\) at the base. Let
\[
f_j=\Pr\!\left(\sum_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)=j\right),
\]
and let \(f_j^{(i)}\) and \(f_j^{(i,\ell)}\) denote the count probabilities after deleting the indicated trials. Define
\[
g_j=\sum_i v_i f_j^{(i)},\qquad
h_j=\sum_{i\ne\ell}v_i v_\ell f_j^{(i,\ell)}.
\tag{5}
\]
All sequences are zero off their natural supports. Generating functions give
\[
f_j'=g_{j-1}-g_j,\qquad
f_j''=h_j-2h_{j-1}+h_{j-2}.
\tag{6}
\]

**Hillion–Johnson, Proposition 2.4, equation (6).** For arbitrary real slopes \(v_i\), and \(0\le j\le n-2\),
\[
h_j(f_{j+1}^2-f_jf_{j+2})
\le
2g_jg_{j+1}f_{j+1}
-g_j^2f_{j+2}
-g_{j+1}^2f_j.
\tag{7}
\]
There is no same-sign restriction on the slopes. This finite local inequality is the sole non-elementary external input below.

For completeness, its implication needed here admits a short algebraic proof. Set
\[
\rho=\frac{\sqrt{f_jf_{j+2}}}{f_{j+1}},\qquad
x=\frac{g_j}{\sqrt{f_j}},\qquad
y=\frac{g_{j+1}}{\sqrt{f_{j+2}}},
\]
and
\[
Q_j=x^2+y^2-2\rho xy
=\frac{g_j^2}{f_j}
-\frac{2g_jg_{j+1}}{f_{j+1}}
+\frac{g_{j+1}^2}{f_{j+2}}.
\tag{8}
\]
For the strict Bernoulli count law, \(0<\rho<1\). In particular \(Q_j\ge0\). Dividing (7) by \(f_{j+1}^2\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_j(1-\rho^2)
&\le2\rho xy-\rho^2(x^2+y^2)\\
&=\rho Q_j-\rho(1+\rho)(x-y)^2\\
&\le\rho Q_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The elementary inequality
\[
2\rho\log(1/\rho)\le1-\rho^2
\tag{10}
\]
is equivalent, under \(\rho=e^{-s}\), to \(s\le\sinh s\).
If \(h_j\ge0\), (9) and (10) therefore imply
\[
\boxed{
Q_j+h_j\log\frac{f_jf_{j+2}}{f_{j+1}^2}\ge0.}
\tag{11}
\]
For \(h_j\le0\), (11) also follows directly from log-concavity and \(Q_j\ge0\). This recovers the local Shannon inequality established in the source's proof, while keeping its all-sign hypotheses explicit.

## 3. Adding the binomial weights, with the sign checked locally

Write
\[
w_j={n\choose j},\qquad q_j=\frac{f_j}{w_j}.
\]
Newton's inequalities applied to the positive odds \(p_i/(1-p_i)\) give
\[
q_{j+1}^2\ge q_jq_{j+2}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is exactly ultra-log-concavity of the Bernoulli count law. Indeed,
\[
f_j=\left(\prod_i(1-p_i)\right)e_j\!\left(
\frac{p_1}{1-p_1},\ldots,\frac{p_n}{1-p_n}\right),
\]
and (12) is the normalized elementary-symmetric-polynomial form of Newton's inequalities. Also \(w_{j+1}^2>w_jw_{j+2}\), so ordinary count log-concavity is strict.

Consider the weighted entropy
\[
\mathcal E_n(p)=-\sum_{j=0}^n f_j\log q_j.
\tag{13}
\]
Normalization eliminates the constant term in its second derivative:
\[
-\mathcal E_n''
=\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{(f_j')^2}{f_j}
+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}h_j
\log\frac{q_jq_{j+2}}{q_{j+1}^2}.
\tag{14}
\]
The Fisher term has the **exact** rearrangement
\[
\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{(f_j')^2}{f_j}
=\frac{g_0^2}{f_1}
+\frac{g_{n-1}^2}{f_{n-1}}
+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}Q_j.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(n=2\), the two displayed boundary denominators are both \(f_1\), and the two numerators remain distinct; the identity is still exact.

Define the weighted local quantity
\[
\widetilde u_j
=Q_j+h_j\log\frac{q_jq_{j+2}}{q_{j+1}^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
It is nonnegative in both possible sign cases:

* If \(h_j\ge0\), then
  \[
  \widetilde u_j
  =\left[Q_j+h_j\log
  \frac{f_jf_{j+2}}{f_{j+1}^2}\right]
  +h_j\log\frac{w_{j+1}^2}{w_jw_{j+2}}
  \ge0
  \tag{17}
  \]
  by (11) and binomial log-concavity.
* If \(h_j\le0\), (12) makes the logarithm in (16) nonpositive. Its product with \(h_j\) is nonnegative, and \(Q_j\ge0\).

Combining (14)–(17) proves the stronger inequality
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_n''\le
-\frac{g_0^2}{f_1}
-\frac{g_{n-1}^2}{f_{n-1}}
\le0.}
\tag{18}
\]
This proves (4) in the open parameter cube. Approximate boundary parameters by \((1-2\epsilon)p_i+\epsilon\) and use continuity of finite-law entropy to get the closed cube.

The correction \(\mathbb E\log {n\choose N}\) need not be concave on its own. The proof compensates it locally according to the sign of each \(h_j\); it does not infer (4) merely by adding it to the ordinary Shepp–Olkin theorem.

Equivalently, for every fixed \(r\in(0,1)\),
\[
p\longmapsto
D\!\left(\mathcal L\!\left(\sum_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)\right)
\middle\|\operatorname{Bin}(n,r)\right)
\quad\text{is convex}.
\tag{19}
\]
The difference from \(-\mathcal E_n\) is affine in \(\sum_i p_i\).

## 4. Strict invariant concavity and an explicit certificate

At (1), permutation invariance makes the occupation law uniform conditional on the count, and the determinantal count generating function gives
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,a)+\operatorname{Bern}(c),
\qquad m=n-1.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus its occupation entropy equals \(\mathcal E_n(a,\ldots,a,c)\), so (18) already proves invariant-direction concavity in every dimension.

For a direction \(A_0=u(I-P)+vP\), the corresponding Bernoulli slopes are \(m\) copies of \(u\) and one \(v\). Let \(f_j=\Pr(N=j)\) for this count law. The two endpoint derivatives are
\[
g_0=-f_0'
=f_0\left(\frac{mu}{1-a}+\frac v{1-c}\right),
\]
\[
g_{n-1}=f_n'
=f_n\left(\frac{mu}{a}+\frac vc\right).
\tag{21}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A_0,A_0]\le-B_{a,c,n}(u,v),}
\tag{22}
\]
where the entirely explicit quadratic form is
\[
B_{a,c,n}(u,v)
=\frac{f_0^2}{f_1}
\left(\frac{mu}{1-a}+\frac v{1-c}\right)^2
+\frac{f_n^2}{f_{n-1}}
\left(\frac{mu}{a}+\frac vc\right)^2.
\tag{23}
\]
All count masses in (23) are positive. The determinant of its two linear forms is
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
m/(1-a)&1/(1-c)\\
m/a&1/c
\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{m(a-c)}{a(1-a)c(1-c)}.
\tag{24}
\]
Therefore \(B_{a,c,n}>0\) whenever \(a\ne c\). This proves negative definiteness of the invariant Hessian without any equality characterization for the general Bernoulli-sum theorem.

The endpoint masses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=(1-a)^m(1-c),&
f_n&=a^m c,\\
f_1&=ma(1-a)^{m-1}(1-c)+c(1-a)^m,&
f_{n-1}&=ma^{m-1}(1-a)c+(1-c)a^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Thus (23) is also a usable rational lower bound, though it need not be sharp when \(n\) is large.

## 5. All symmetry-breaking Hermitian directions

This section reproduces the C005/C017 reduction to make the final theorem complete. Assume first \(c>a\). Put
\[
x=\frac a{1-a},\qquad
r=\frac{c/(1-c)}{a/(1-a)}>1,\qquad
w=\frac{r-1}{n}>0.
\]
The odds matrix is
\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1}=x\{I+(r-1)P\}.
\]
For every subset \(S\),
\[
\det L_S=x^{|S|}(1+w|S|).
\tag{26}
\]
The frozen atom table \(-\log p_S(K)\), after deletion of its constant and modular parts, is therefore
\[
\phi(S)=-\log(1+w|S|).
\]
The determinantal expectation of a modular table is affine, so these deletions do not change its frozen Hessian.

For \(t>0\), define
\[
b_t=1-e^{-tw},\qquad
M_t=I-b_tK,\qquad
R_t=b_t M_t^{-1}>0.
\]
The scalar identity for \(-\log(1+z)\), followed by the determinantal generating function and two derivatives, gives
\[
D^2F_\phi(K)[A,A]
=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t\det M_t
\left\{(\operatorname{tr}R_tA)^2
-\operatorname{tr}(R_tAR_tA)\right\}\,dt.
\tag{27}
\]
The finite-atom derivation and convergence are given in C005. Near zero the differentiated integrand is \(O(t)\), and at infinity the strict contraction supplies a finite denominator and the factor \(e^{-t}\).

Let \(\Pi\) be averaging under coordinate permutations and write
\[
A=A_0+A_\perp,\qquad
A_0=\Pi A=u(I-P)+vP,\qquad \Pi A_\perp=0.
\tag{28}
\]
This is the orthogonal decomposition of the entire real vector space of complex Hermitian matrices. Its perpendicular part satisfies
\[
\operatorname{tr}A_\perp=\operatorname{tr}(PA_\perp)=0.
\]
Every \(R_t\) belongs to the span of \(I,P\), so \(\operatorname{tr}(R_tA_\perp)=0\). Therefore
\[
D^2F_\phi(K)[A_\perp,A_\perp]
=-\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t\det M_t
\|R_t^{1/2}A_\perp R_t^{1/2}\|_F^2\,dt.
\tag{29}
\]
It is strictly negative for every \(A_\perp\ne0\). The entropy Hessian equals this frozen Hessian minus the nonnegative atom Fisher information.

At the permutation-invariant base \(K\), the full entropy Hessian is permutation invariant. Averaging either slot shows its cross term between \(A_0\) and \(A_\perp\) is zero. Combining (22) and (29) proves (2), including strictness.

An explicit combined bound is
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-B_{a,c,n}(u,v)
-\gamma_{a,c,n}\|A_\perp\|_F^2,}
\tag{30}
\]
where
\[
\gamma_{a,c,n}
=\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-t}}t
b_t^2(1-b_ta)^{n-3}(1-b_tc)\,dt>0.
\tag{31}
\]
Indeed, the smaller eigenvalue of \(R_t\) is \(b_t/(1-b_ta)\). The exponent \(n-3=-1\) when \(n=2\) causes no problem because \(1-b_ta\ge1-a>0\).

When \(c<a\), use occupation complementation \(H(I-K)=H(K)\): the complementary eigenvalues are \(1-a<1-c\). The direction changes to \(-A\), preserving the quadratic conclusion. At \(a=c\), the frozen table is modular and the product-law score immediately yields (3).

For a segment whose endpoints belong to the closed exchangeable square, contract it towards \(I/2\), integrate the interior Hessian inequality, and then let the contraction tend to zero. This proves the closed-square concavity asserted in Section 1. It does not assert a boundary Hessian exists or a concavity theorem for arbitrary lines leaving the exchangeable class.

## 6. A sharp range for the binomial correction

For every \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
H(N)+\theta\,\mathbb E\log{n\choose N}
\tag{32}
\]
is concave in all independent Bernoulli parameters: it is a convex combination of ordinary Shepp–Olkin entropy and (4). The endpoint \(\theta=1\) is sharp as a universal upper range. For \(n=2\), take \(p_1=1/2+t\), \(p_2=1/2-t\). Its three count masses are
\[
f_0=f_2=\tfrac14-t^2,\qquad f_1=\tfrac12+2t^2.
\]
The second derivative of (32) at zero is
\[
4(\theta-1)\log2,
\]
which is positive when \(\theta>1\). This example concerns the weighted-entropy extension and is not a DPP entropy counterexample.
