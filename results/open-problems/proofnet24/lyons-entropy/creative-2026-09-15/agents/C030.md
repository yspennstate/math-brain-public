# C030 — Constructive projection dilations, noisy diamond couplings, and the exact conditional defect

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **complete bounded contribution**. Full Lyons entropy concavity remains open.

This report proves that a certified projection-flag diamond coupling survives an explicit dilation with one occupied and one empty auxiliary mode per site. The enlarged flags have an explicit positive coupling, and their physical compression implements every independent monotone binary channel. Consequently the opposite-slope entropy theorem transfers to generally nonorthogonal rank-one updates at strict, noncommuting contraction kernels.

The report also identifies the exact conditional entropy correction and gives a minimal-dimensional obstruction: for the canonical pure projection dilation of a two-site affine kernel, that correction has both signs of curvature. Subtracting a supposedly convex conditional entropy is therefore unavailable. A coupling-level transfer supplies the replacement.

## 1. Definitions

For a positive contraction (K) on (mathbb C^E), write (mu_K) for its determinantal subset law:

\[
\Pr(S\subseteq X)=\det K_S.
\]

For (S\subseteq E) and distinct (i,j\notin S), define the signed elementary diamond

\[
\mathsf D_{S;ij}=\delta_S+\delta_{Sij}-\delta_{Si}-\delta_{Sj}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here (Si=S\cup\{i\}), and similarly for other suffixes. A nonnegative diamond representation is a finite identity (b=\sum w_{S;ij}\mathsf D_{S;ij}) with (w\ge0). Pairing it with a supermodular set function is nonnegative.

A projection flag consists of a subspace (W), and orthonormal vectors (u,v\perp W). Its contrast is

\[
b=\mu_{P_W}+\mu_{P_W+uu^*+vv^*}
-\mu_{P_W+uu^*}-\mu_{P_W+vv^*}.
\tag{2}
\]

A certified flag means that (2) has a nonnegative representation (1) supported on (|S|=\dim W). The coefficients sum to one. Their bottom, top, and aggregate middle marginals are the four projection laws in (2), as in old-107.

For more general data

\[
0\le K_0,\qquad B=xx^*,\qquad C=yy^*,\qquad K_0+B+C\le I,
\tag{3}
\]

use the same four-corner contrast

\[
b=\mu_{K_0}+\mu_{K_0+B+C}-\mu_{K_0+B}-\mu_{K_0+C}.
\tag{4}
\]

Its representation need not have one base rank, and its total coefficient mass need not be one. Every atom of (mu_{K_0+\lambda B+\mu C}) is separately affine in (lambda,mu): inclusion moments are principal determinants with two rank-one updates, and inclusion-exclusion preserves separate affinity. Therefore

\[
p_{\lambda,\mu}=(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)\mu_{K_0}
+\lambda(1-\mu)\mu_{K_0+B}
+(1-\lambda)\mu\mu_{K_0+C}
+\lambda\mu\mu_{K_0+B+C}.
\tag{5}
\]

## 2. Restriction and monotone binary noise preserve the diamond cone

**Theorem 1.** A nonnegative representation (1) remains nonnegative under coordinate restriction and under every product channel

\[
\Pr(X_i=1\mid Y_i=0)=a_i,\qquad
\Pr(X_i=1\mid Y_i=1)=a_i+s_i,
\quad a_i,s_i\ge0,\quad a_i+s_i\le1.
\tag{6}
\]

The channel sends a DPP kernel (K) to

\[
\mathcal T(K)=A+DKD,
\quad A=\operatorname{diag}(a_i),\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{s_i}).
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Sample, independently for each coordinate, a map (\phi_i:\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}): constant one with probability (a_i), identity with probability (s_i), and constant zero with probability (1-a_i-s_i). Use the same sampled map at every vertex of a diamond. If both addition coordinates use identity, its image is an elementary diamond. If either is constant, its image as a signed measure is zero. Averaging proves cone preservation. Restriction has the same proof: a diamond survives exactly when both added sites are retained.

For the kernel formula, for every (S\subseteq E),

\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}X_i
=\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}(a_i+s_iY_i)
=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\Bigl(\prod_{i\in S\setminus T}a_i\Bigr)
\Bigl(\prod_{i\in T}s_i\Bigr)\det K_T
=\det(A+DKD)_S.
\]

Also (0\le A+DKD\le A+D^2\le I). This proves (7). ∎

The theorem is constructive. For a fixed added pair ({i,j}), its output weights are

\[
\boxed{
w'_{T;ij}=s_i s_j\sum_{S\subseteq E\setminus\{i,j\}}
w_{S;ij}\prod_{k\ne i,j}
\begin{cases}
a_k+s_k\mathbf1_{k\in S},&k\in T,\\
1-a_k-s_k\mathbf1_{k\in S},&k\notin T.
\end{cases}}
\tag{8}
\]

Zero input weights at unused base ranks are understood. This is a finite positive formula, not an optimization problem.

**Fixed addition-pair marginal.** For any representation of (4),

\[
\boxed{\sum_S w_{S;ij}=|x_i y_j-x_j y_i|^2.}
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, pair (4) with (\mathbf1_{\{i,j\}\subseteq X}). A diamond contributes one precisely when its added pair is ({i,j}). The other side is the mixed coefficient in the two-by-two determinant, namely

\[
B_{ii}C_{jj}+C_{ii}B_{jj}-B_{ij}C_{ji}-C_{ij}B_{ji}
=|x_i y_j-x_j y_i|^2.
\]

Consequently the exact total coefficient mass is

\[
\boxed{\sum_{S;ij}w_{S;ij}=\|x\|^2\|y\|^2-|x^*y|^2.}
\tag{10}
\]

After (7), each pair mass in (9) is multiplied by (s_i s_j); the additive noise parameters (a_i) do not affect it. Formula (10) also explains why restriction can lose all coupling mass when the two compressed update vectors become collinear.

## 3. An explicit projection dilation that preserves the coupling

The preceding stochastic construction has a pure projection realization at every corner, with an explicit positive coupling on the enlarged ground set.

**Theorem 2.** Suppose a flag ((W,u,v)) on (n) sites is certified. Choose arbitrary channel parameters (6). There is an explicit certified flag on (3n) sites, with ranks

\[
\dim W+n,\quad \dim W+n+1,\quad \dim W+n+1,\quad \dim W+n+2,
\tag{11}
\]

whose restriction to one designated site in each triple gives the four kernels obtained from the original flag by (7).

**Local construction.** For one site, put (a=a_i), (s=s_i), (r=1-a-s), and (q=\sqrt{a+s}). The three input modes, in order, are the original mode, an occupied auxiliary mode, and an empty auxiliary mode. If (a+s>0), apply the real orthogonal matrix

\[
U=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt s&\sqrt a&\sqrt r\\
-\sqrt a/q&\sqrt s/q&0\\
-\sqrt{rs}/q&-\sqrt{ra}/q&q
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]

Row zero is the designated physical output. Direct inner products give (UU^*=I).

If the original mode is absent, the local occupied subspace is the second column of (U). Its singleton law is

\[
\kappa_0(\{0\})=a,\qquad
\kappa_0(\{1\})=s/(a+s),\qquad
\kappa_0(\{2\})=ar/(a+s).
\tag{13}
\]

If it is present, the occupied subspace is the first two columns. Complementary minors of an orthogonal matrix give

\[
\kappa_1(\{0,1\})=a+s,\qquad
\kappa_1(\{1,2\})=r,\qquad
\kappa_1(\{0,2\})=0.
\tag{14}
\]

Here is an explicit coupling (L\subset H), (|L|=1,|H|=2), of (13) and (14):

| (L) | (H) | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| ({0}) | ({0,1}) | (a) |
| ({1}) | ({0,1}) | (s) |
| ({1}) | ({1,2}) | (sr/(a+s)) |
| ({2}) | ({1,2}) | (ar/(a+s)) |

Its rows and columns sum to (13) and (14). Looking only at output zero, the random map is constant one with probability (a), identity with probability (s), and constant zero with probability (r).

If (a=s=0), take (U) with rows ((0,0,1),(1,0,0),(0,1,0)), and use the single coupling atom (L=\{2\},H=\{1,2\}). This covers the only omitted denominator case.

**Global projection law.** Adjoin one filled and one empty mode at each site. Let (mathcal U=\bigoplus_iU_i), with the evident regrouping of coordinates into triples. Put

\[
\widetilde P_W=\mathcal U(P_W\oplus I_n\oplus0_n)\mathcal U^*,
\quad \widetilde u=\mathcal U(u\oplus0\oplus0),
\quad \widetilde v=\mathcal U(v\oplus0\oplus0).
\tag{15}
\]

The displayed objects form the required orthogonal flag. Restricting (15) to the designated output in each triple gives (A+DP_WD), (Du), and (Dv), directly from the first row of (12).

For clarity, the enlarged projection law is exactly a classical local channel applied to the original projection law; no decoherence assumption is needed. If (T) is an enlarged occupation set, write (T_i) for its part in triple (i). In the Cauchy–Binet expansion of an occupied-frame minor, an input minor can be nonzero only if it contains every filled auxiliary mode, no empty mode, and an original occupation set (Y). Since (mathcal U) is block diagonal, its minor can be nonzero only when

\[
|T_i|=1+\mathbf1_{i\in Y}.
\]

Thus at most one original set (Y) contributes: it is determined by the local cardinalities. The squared minor consequently factors as

\[
\mu_{\widetilde P_H}(T)
=\mu_{P_H}(Y)\prod_i\kappa_{i,\mathbf1_{i\in Y}}(T_i),
\tag{16}
\]

for every one of the four source subspaces (H). Sets with a local cardinality other than one or two have probability zero.

**Positive diamond lift.** Draw the source diamond, and independently draw the four-atom local coupling above at every site. Send an input set (Y) to

\[
\Psi(Y)=\bigcup_i
\begin{cases}H_i,&i\in Y,\\L_i,&i\notin Y.\end{cases}
\tag{17}
\]

A source diamond with additions (i,j) becomes a genuine enlarged diamond with additions (H_i\setminus L_i) and (H_j\setminus L_j). They are distinct because they lie in distinct triples. Its base rank increases by (n). Formula (16) verifies every corner marginal; linearity verifies the aggregate middle law for an unlabeled source coupling. This proves Theorem 2. ∎

This construction proves a closure theorem for projection couplings themselves, and not just for compressed signed measures. Applied to C004, it gives explicit eighteen-site flags of ranks (8,9,9,10). It also applies without alteration to every flag certified by C012.

## 4. A minimal simultaneous projection dilation for arbitrary contraction corners

There is also a universal dilation, without a claim that its flag is certified.

**Theorem 3.** Given (3), put (R=I-K_0-B-C). The four kernels have a common projection-flag dilation in dimension

\[
N=\operatorname{rank}K_0+\operatorname{rank}R+2\le2n+2.
\tag{18}
\]

This dimension is minimal among dilations with two orthogonal rank-one projection increments and orthogonal residual subspace.

**Proof.** On

\[
\mathcal H=\operatorname{ran}K_0\oplus\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C\oplus\operatorname{ran}R,
\]

define

\[
Vz=(K_0^{1/2}z,x^*z,y^*z,R^{1/2}z).
\tag{19}
\]

Then (V^*V=K_0+B+C+R=I). The first summand is the bottom projection subspace, the next two one-dimensional summands are its orthogonal increments, and the fourth is the complement of the top. Complete the columns of (V) to a unitary to identify its image with the first (n) physical coordinates. Compression gives exactly the four kernels in (3).

For minimality, the bottom projection has rank at least (operatorname{rank}K_0), the complement of the top has rank at least (operatorname{rank}R), and the two mutually orthogonal increments each have rank one. Their ranks sum to the ambient dimension. ∎

Combining Theorems 1 and 3 gives a precise reduction: the universal projection-flag diamond statement is equivalent to diamond-cone positivity of (4) for all finite-dimensional contraction rectangles (3). The reverse implication is the projection special case. The forward implication uses (19) and restriction.

For a strict kernel and any direction (xx^*-yy^*), a sufficiently small rectangle centered at that kernel meets (3). Thus a universal projection coupling theorem would settle all such opposite rank-one directions, including directions not aligned with eigenvectors of the kernel. Neither existence of the universal coupling nor full Lyons concavity is proved here.

## 5. Entropy transfer and the exact missing conditional term

**Theorem 4.** Suppose the contrast (4) has a nonnegative diamond representation. Then

\[
t\longmapsto H(\mu_{K_0+\lambda(t)B+\mu(t)C})
\]

is concave whenever (lambda,mu\in[0,1]) are affine and their slopes (alpha,eta) satisfy (alpha\beta\le0).

**Proof for strict kernels.** Put (p=p_{\lambda,\mu}). Equation (5) gives (p''=2\alpha\beta b). With atom Fisher information (I_p=\sum(p'_S)^2/p_S),

\[
H''=-I_p-2\alpha\beta\sum_Sb_S\log p_S.
\tag{20}
\]

Let (L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0), so (p_S=\det L_S/\det(I+L)). The two-by-two Schur complement over (S) gives

\[
g_{S;ij}:=\log\frac{p_{Si}p_{Sj}}{p_Sp_{Sij}}
=-\log\left(1-
\frac{|L_{ij\mid S}|^2}{L_{ii\mid S}L_{jj\mid S}}\right)\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]

Consequently

\[
G_p:=-\sum_Sb_S\log p_S=\sum_{S;ij}w_{S;ij}g_{S;ij}\ge0,
\]

and (20) becomes the stronger certificate

\[
\boxed{H''=-I_p-2|\alpha\beta|G_p\le-I_p\le0.}
\tag{22}
\]

For boundary kernels, apply the product channel (a_i=\epsilon,s_i=1-2\epsilon). It preserves the nonnegative representation by Theorem 1 and makes every kernel strict. Pass to the limit using continuity of finite-law Shannon entropy. ∎

This proves the entropy consequence of the constructive dilation in Section 3 without assuming a sign for conditional entropy.

To make the missing term precise, let (Y) be the source DPP along an opposite-slope spectral segment, and let (X) be its coordinate restriction or its output under (6). Write (I_Y,I_X) for their Fisher informations, and (G_Y,G_X) for their frozen diamond quantities in (22). Set (c=2|\alpha\beta|).

For restriction, the chain rule is (H(X)=H(Y)-H(Y\mid X)). For (6), (H(X\mid Y)) is a sum of single-coordinate terms whose expectations are affine in the kernel diagonal. Its second derivative is zero. Hence in both cases the exact correction (C=H(Y\mid X)) satisfies

\[
\boxed{C''=-(I_Y-I_X)-c(G_Y-G_X).}
\tag{23}
\]

The Fisher difference has the conditional-score representation

\[
I_Y-I_X
=\mathbb E\left[\operatorname{Var}\left(
\frac{p_Y'(Y)}{p_Y(Y)}\,\middle|\,X\right)\right]\ge0.
\tag{24}
\]

Indeed the output score is the conditional expectation of the input score. For the actual projection dilation (15), (16) also gives

\[
H(\widetilde Y)=H(Y)+\sum_i\mathbb E H(\kappa_{i,Y_i});
\]

the last term has zero second derivative. Thus the auxiliary conditional entropy (H(\widetilde Y\setminus X\mid X)) has exactly the same second derivative (23).

The useful proved lower bound is

\[
C''\ge-(I_Y-I_X)-cG_Y,
\tag{25}
\]

because the transferred coupling proves (G_X\ge0). Substituting (23), rather than guessing convexity of (C), yields (H(X)''=-I_X-cG_X\le0).

**Sharp cancellation example.** Take two orthonormal Hadamard vectors, (W=0), and spectral parameters (1/2+t,1/2-t). The two-site atom masses are

\[
p_{00}=p_{11}=1/4-t^2,\qquad
p_{10}=p_{01}=1/4+t^2.
\]

Keep only the first site. Its law is the constant Bernoulli (1/2), so (I_X=G_X=H(X)''=0). The conditional entropy is (C(t)=h(1/2+2t^2)), and at (t=1/4),

\[
C''=-64/15+4\log(3/5)<0.
\tag{26}
\]

There is a perfect projection diamond coupling: the single diamond on two sites. Bound (25) is attained, and the conditional correction cancels all source curvature. No positive uniform fraction of the lifted entropy curvature can survive every restriction.

## 6. The canonical pure dilation has a correction with both curvature signs

For a general positive contraction, its usual pure projection dilation is

\[
\mathcal P(K)=
\begin{pmatrix}
K&\sqrt{K(I-K)}\\
\sqrt{K(I-K)}&I-K
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{27}
\]

It is a rank-(n) projection, as direct multiplication shows. If its physical and auxiliary occupation sets are (X,Z), then

\[
H(\mu_K)=H(\mu_{\mathcal P(K)})-H(Z\mid X).
\tag{28}
\]

**Theorem 5.** The conditional term in (28) is neither convex nor concave on affine kernel lines, already for (n=2).

**Proof.** Take

\[
K(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&t\\t&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<t<1/2.
\tag{29}
\]

Then (K(I-K)=(1/4-t^2)I). Label the physical sites (1,2), and their auxiliary copies (\bar1,\bar2). The six rank-two atom masses of (27) are obtained directly from its two-by-two principal minors:

| Occupied pair | Probability |
|---|---:|
| (12), (\bar1\bar2) | (1/4-t^2), each |
| (1\bar1), (2\bar2) | (t^2), each |
| (1\bar2), (2\bar1) | (1/4), each |

Writing (b=1/4+t^2), the exact conditional entropy is

\[
C(t)=H(Z\mid X)
=2b\log b-2t^2\log(t^2)-\tfrac12\log(1/4),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{C''(t)=4\log\left(1+\frac1{4t^2}\right)
-\frac2{1/4+t^2}.}
\tag{30}
\]

At (t=1/4), it is (4\log5-32/5>0). An exact elementary lower bound is

\[
\log5>2\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{(2/3)^{2k+1}}{2k+1}
=\frac{122492}{76545}>\frac85.
\]

At (t=1/3), it is (4\log(13/4)-72/13<-7/13<0). Indeed

\[
e^{5/4}>\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{(5/4)^k}{k!}
=\frac{1289}{384}>\frac{13}4,
\]

so (log(13/4)<5/4). This proves both signs at strict interior kernels. ∎

This is minimal in the number of physical sites: when (n=1), the rank-one dilation has exactly one occupied site, and the auxiliary occupation is determined by the physical occupation, so (C\equiv0). Theorem 5 is an obstruction to this entropy-subtraction method, not to Lyons concavity. In particular, (K\mapsto\mathcal P(K)) is nonlinear, and no affine-line entropy theorem applies to its image automatically.

## 7. An explicit strict, noncommuting six-site replacement family

Let (zeta=e^{\pi i/3}), (f_k(j)=6^{-1/2}\zeta^{jk}), and use C004's certified Fourier flag

Now choose

and define the two-parameter contraction family

\[
\boxed{K(\lambda,\mu)=A+D(P_W+\lambda uu^*+\mu vv^*)D,

\tag{31}
\]

This is the channel (a_i=(1-s_i)/2), (s_i=D_{ii}^2). Uniformly throughout the closed square,

\[
\frac7{32}I\le K(\lambda,\mu)\le\frac{25}{32}I.

The update vectors (x=Du,y=Dv) have exact Gram matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}x^*x&x^*y\\y^*x&y^*y\end{pmatrix}
=\frac1{96}\begin{pmatrix}49&-5\\-5&49\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{33}
\]

To see this, (D^2=(9/16)I-(5/16)e_0e_0^*), and (u_0=v_0=1/\sqrt6). In particular (x,y) are independent and not orthogonal. Their rank-one matrices do not commute, since

\[

\]

Thus this family cannot be put into one fixed eigenbasis.

Formula (8) supplies all 240 physical diamond weights, including every base (T\subseteq E\setminus\{i,j\}). Every one is positive. A uniform analytic lower bound is

\[
\boxed{w'_{T;ij}\ge\eta:=\frac{2401}{268435456}.}

\]

For each pair there are six source bottom pairs. Every source weight is at least (1/216), every factor at a non-addition coordinate is at least (7/32), and (s_i s_j\ge9/64). Thus the lower bound is (6(1/216)(9/64)(7/32)^4=eta). Its total mass is, by (10) and (33),

\[
\sum w'=\frac{49^2-5^2}{96^2}=\frac{33}{128}.
\tag{35}

**Corollary 6.** Entropy in (31) is strictly concave along every nonconstant segment with affine slopes (alpha\beta\le0).

For (alpha\beta<0), Theorem 4 and the empty-base terms alone give

\[
H''\le-I_p-2|\alpha\beta|\eta
\sum_{i<j}-\log\left(1-\frac{|L_{ij}|^2}{L_{ii}L_{jj}}\right)<0.
\tag{36}
\]

The strict inequality follows because every kernel (31) is non-diagonal. Indeed the undamped Fourier kernel is circulant with eigenvalues (1,1,\lambda,\mu,0,0); a diagonal circulant matrix is scalar, which these eigenvalues preclude. Multiplication by invertible diagonal (D) preserves the existence of an off-diagonal entry. Also (L) is diagonal if and only if (K) is diagonal. If precisely one slope is nonzero, a single-site inclusion probability has a nonzero derivative, so (I_p>0). This proves the corollary including all parameter boundary points, since (32) keeps the physical kernel strict.

In particular, the segment (\lambda=t,\mu=1-t) has constant diagonal (K_{ii}=1/2) and a nonzero, zero-diagonal Hermitian direction. Its strict concavity follows for its entire length. This is a contraction result at generally nonexchangeable bases, not a restatement of C075.
