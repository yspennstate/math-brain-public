Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: complete bounded method obstruction.**

## 1. The general six-site flag and the proposed SOS

Let (E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}), and let

\[
Q=(w_1,w_2,u,v,z_1,z_2)
\]

be an arbitrary complex unitary matrix. Put (W=[w_1,w_2]), (Z=[z_1,z_2]), and use oriented Plücker coordinates

\[
p_{ij}=w_{1i}w_{2j}-w_{1j}w_{2i},\qquad
z_{ij}=z_{1i}z_{2j}-z_{1j}z_{2i}.
\tag{1}
\]

They are antisymmetric in their indices. An unordered pair subscript is always sorted. The two endpoint laws, with the top indexed by its complementary pair, are

\[
a_A=|p_A|^2,\qquad c_B=|z_B|^2
       =\mu_{W+[u,v]}(E\setminus B).
\tag{2}
\]

For disjoint pairs (A,B), put (J=E\setminus(A\cup B)=\{i,j\}). The corresponding diamond is

\[
A,\quad A+i,\quad A+j,\quad E\setminus B.
\]

Its weight (x_{A,B}) must satisfy

\[
\sum_{B:B\cap A=\varnothing}x_{A,B}=|p_A|^2,\qquad
\sum_{A:A\cap B=\varnothing}x_{A,B}=|z_B|^2,
\tag{3}
\]

and, for each triple (M),

\[
\sum_{\substack{A\subset M,\ |A|=2\\B\subset M^c,\ |B|=2}}
x_{A,B}
=g_M:=\mu_{W+[u]}(M)+\mu_{W+[v]}(M).
\tag{4}
\]

There are 90 atoms and 225 ordered bilinear endpoint products. Consider either feature vector

\[
\Phi=(p_Cz_D)_{C,D},\qquad
\Psi=(p_C\overline z_D)_{C,D},
\tag{5}
\]

where (C,D) independently range over all 15 pairs, including intersecting pairs. The proposed universal lift is

\[
x_{A,B}=\Phi^*H_{A,B}\Phi
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
x_{A,B}=\Psi^*H_{A,B}\Psi,
\qquad H_{A,B}\succeq0,
\tag{6}
\]

with matrices fixed independently of (Q). Equivalently, every atom is an arbitrary finite sum of squared absolute values of fixed linear combinations of the products in (5).

**Theorem 1.** No collection (6) satisfies even the two endpoint equalities (3) for every complex six-site unitary flag. The same conclusion holds if each square amplitude is any bilinear expression in the four sectors

\[
p_Cz_D,\quad p_C\overline z_D,\quad
\overline p_Cz_D,\quad\overline p_C\,\overline z_D.
\tag{7}
\]

This is a restriction on the allowed amplitude space and on the coefficients being fixed. It does not rule out higher-degree amplitudes, frame-dependent Gram matrices, rational or piecewise formulas, or unrestricted nonnegative diamond couplings.

## 2. Endpoint-zero rigidity

An orthonormal pair of two-frames (W,Z) with (W^*Z=0) always extends to a unitary (Q), so it suffices to work with those two frames.

**Lemma 2.** Fix disjoint pairs (A,B). Suppose a bilinear form (L) in either of the feature spaces in (5) vanishes on every such frame with (p_A=0), and on every such frame with (z_B=0). Then, as a function on all orthogonal two-frame pairs,

\[
L=\kappa p_Az_B
\quad\hbox{or respectively}\quad
L=\kappa p_A\overline z_B
\tag{8}
\]

for a constant (kappa\in\mathbb C).

The sesquilinear assertion allows every identity among its 225 features. It is an assertion about their represented functions, not about uniqueness of the original coefficient vector.

We first prove a one-endpoint version: vanishing whenever (p_A=0) forces

\[
L=p_A\sum_D t_Dz_D
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
L=p_A\sum_D t_D\overline z_D.
\tag{9}
\]

### 2.1 Coordinate phases separate the relevant sectors

Multiplying coordinate (i) of both frames by a unit complex number (\tau_i) preserves orthogonality and all zero conditions under consideration. The product (p_Cz_D) has phase character

\[
\tau^{\mathbf1_C+\mathbf1_D},
\]

while (p_C\overline z_D) has character

\[
\tau^{\mathbf1_C-\mathbf1_D}.
\tag{10}
\]

If (L) vanishes throughout a coordinate-phase orbit, each of its character components vanishes. This is elementary finite Fourier projection: average against the desired character over (\tau_i\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}), independently for all six coordinates. The exponents in either separate convention do not alias modulo four.

All frame constructions below may use the fixed rational numbers

\[
c=3/5,\qquad s=4/5,\qquad c^2+s^2=1.
\tag{11}
\]

### 2.2 Holomorphic products (p_Cz_D)

Write (L=\sum_{C,D}h_{C,D}p_Cz_D), and fix (C\ne A). We show (h_{C,D}=0) for every (D).

If (C\cap D=\varnothing), take (W) and (Z) to be the coordinate frames on (C) and (D). Only (p_Cz_D) is nonzero. Also (p_A=0), so its coefficient vanishes.

If (C=\{i,j\}), (D=\{i,k\}) have one common coordinate, choose

\[
\ell\notin A\cup C\cup D.
\]

Such a coordinate exists in six sites. Set

\[
W=(ce_i+se_\ell,\ e_j),\qquad
Z=(se_i-ce_\ell,\ e_k).
\tag{12}
\]

The four columns are orthonormal. The support of (W) omits (A\setminus C\ne\varnothing), hence (p_A=0). The character with exponent (2e_i+e_j+e_k) contains only the two possible products (p_Cz_D) and (p_Dz_C). The second is zero in this frame, and the first is nonzero. Fourier projection forces (h_{C,D}=0).

Finally, if (C=D=\{i,j\}\ne A), choose distinct (k,\ell\notin A\cup C), possible because this union has at most four elements. Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=(ce_i+se_k,\ ce_j+se_\ell),\\
Z&=(se_i-ce_k,\ se_j-ce_\ell).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

Again (p_A=0). The character (2e_i+2e_j) contains only (p_Cz_C), which is nonzero. Its coefficient must vanish.

These cases eliminate all 210 coefficients with (C\ne A). This proves the first formula in (9), without invoking any unproved algebraic-divisibility statement.

### 2.3 Sesquilinear products and all bilinear incidence identities

Orthogonality gives the 36 exact contractions

\[
R_{ij}:=\sum_{k=0}^5p_{ik}\overline z_{jk}=0,
\qquad 0\le i,j\le5.
\tag{14}
\]

For example, expansion of (1) reduces each sum to linear combinations of the four inner products (\langle z_a,w_b\rangle=0). These are the incidence relations relevant to the bilinear degree. We now use them explicitly rather than treating the products as independent.

The phase sectors of (p_C\overline z_D) have three types.

**Disjoint pairs.** If (C\cap D=\varnothing), its character identifies ((C,D)) uniquely. Coordinate frames show that its coefficient vanishes whenever (C\ne A).

**One common coordinate.** For each ordered (i\ne j), the sector has the form

\[
\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}h_kp_{ik}\overline z_{jk}.
\tag{15}
\]

Here the antisymmetric convention absorbs all sorting signs. Choose distinct (k,\ell\notin\{i,j\}) such that neither (\{i,k\}) nor (\{i,\ell\}) equals (A), and take

\[
W=(e_i,\ ce_k+se_\ell),\qquad
Z=(e_j,\ se_k-ce_\ell).
\tag{16}
\]

This frame has (p_A=0). In the selected sector its only nonzero products are (cs) and (-cs), so (h_k=h_\ell). Consequently all coefficients in (15) are equal except possibly the unique index (k_0) with (\{i,k_0\}=A), when such an index exists. Subtracting the common multiple of (R_{ij}=0) leaves either zero or a single multiple of (p_A\overline z_D).

**Equal pairs.** The zero-character sector is

\[
\sum_{i<j}h_{ij}p_{ij}\overline z_{ij}.
\tag{17}
\]

For distinct (i,j,k,\ell), the frame (13) has diagonal-sector products proportional to

\[
\delta_{ij}+\delta_{k\ell}-\delta_{i\ell}-\delta_{jk}.
\tag{18}
\]

Whenever none of these four pairs is (A), the frame has (p_A=0). Thus the corresponding alternating four-cycle sum of the coefficients (h) is zero. This implies

\[
h_{ij}=\lambda_i+\lambda_j
\qquad\text{for every }ij\ne A.
\tag{19}
\]

Here is a direct proof for (A=01); relabeling gives the general case. On the complete five-vertex graph (\{1,2,3,4,5\}), the four-point identities imply additive vertex coefficients. Fix three vertices (a,b,c), put

\[
\lambda_a=(h_{ab}+h_{ac}-h_{bc})/2,\qquad
\lambda_j=h_{aj}-\lambda_a\quad(j\ne a),
\]

and use the four-point identities first for pairs involving (b,c), then for every remaining pair. They give (h_{ij}=\lambda_i+\lambda_j) on that graph. For distinct (j,k,\ell\in\{2,3,4,5\}), the permitted rectangle

\[
h_{0j}+h_{k\ell}=h_{0k}+h_{j\ell}
\]

shows that (h_{0j}-\lambda_j) is independent of (j\ge2). Call it (\lambda_0). This proves (19).

Subtracting (\sum_i\lambda_iR_{ii}=0) from (17) leaves only a multiple of (p_A\overline z_A). Combining all three character types proves the second formula in (9).

For precision, the 36 relations (14) are independent: the 30 off-diagonal ones occupy distinct phase sectors, and independence of the six diagonal ones is the fact that (\lambda_i+\lambda_j=0) for every edge of a complete graph forces all (\lambda_i=0). They are also all linear identities in this bilinear feature space. The exact data in Section 6 exhibit feature-span rank (189=225-36). Thus no additional hidden linear Plücker cancellation at this degree is being excluded from the ansatz.

### 2.4 Imposing the other endpoint

It remains to show that (9), together with vanishing when (z_B=0), leaves only (t_B). The following argument works in either conjugation convention.

First take a coordinate frame (Z=e_D), where (D\cap A=\varnothing) and (D\ne B), and (W=e_A). Then (p_A\ne0), (z_B=0), and only (z_D\ne0). Hence (t_D=0) for every such (D).

Next let (D=\{a,d\}) have exactly one element (a\in A), and write (A=\{a,a'\}). Choose (b\in B\setminus D), and choose (k\notin A\cup\{d,b\}). Put

\[
Z=(ce_a+se_k,\ e_d),\qquad
W=(se_a-ce_k,\ e_{a'}).
\tag{20}
\]

These frames are orthogonal, (p_A\ne0), and (z_B=0) because coordinate (b) is absent. The only nonzero (Z)-minors are at (D) and at (\{k,d\}). The latter coefficient was already eliminated, so (t_D=0).

Finally take (D=A=\{a,a'\}). Choose (b\in B) and distinct (k,\ell\notin A\cup\{b\}), and put

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z&=(ce_a+se_k,\ ce_{a'}+se_\ell),\\
W&=(se_a-ce_k,\ se_{a'}-ce_\ell).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

Again (p_A\ne0) and (z_B=0). Every other nonzero (Z)-minor has a coefficient already eliminated. Thus (t_A=0). This proves Lemma 2 completely.

## 3. The only possible fixed SOS weights, and their contradiction

Factor each positive-semidefinite matrix in (6), so an atom is a sum of squares (sum_\nu|L_{A,B,\nu}|^2).

If (p_A=0), its entire nonnegative row in (3) has sum zero. Every square in every atom of that row is therefore zero. If (z_B=0), the same argument applies to its column. Lemma 2 consequently gives, in either convention,

\[
x_{A,B}=k_{A,B}|p_A|^2|z_B|^2,
\qquad k_{A,B}\ge0\text{ constant}.
\tag{22}
\]

For every disjoint (A,B), choose the coordinate flag (W=e_A), (Z=e_B), and let (U) use the remaining two coordinates. At that flag the endpoint laws are both point masses, so their only possible atom has weight one. Thus

\[
k_{A,B}=1\qquad\text{for all 90 atoms}.
\tag{23}
\]

The ansatz has been forced to the product table

\[
x_{A,B}=a_Ac_B\,\mathbf1_{\{A\cap B=\varnothing\}}.
\tag{24}
\]

Take the exact rational flag

\[
\begin{aligned}
w_1&=(3e_0+4e_2)/5,&w_2&=e_1,\\
u&=e_4,&v&=e_5,\\
z_1&=(4e_0-3e_2)/5,&z_2&=e_3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

Its only positive endpoint probabilities are

\[
a_{01}=c_{23}=9/25,\qquad
a_{12}=c_{03}=16/25.
\tag{26}
\]

Of the positive top-complement pairs, only (23) is disjoint from bottom (01). The forced product row is therefore

\[
\sum_{B\cap01=\varnothing}x_{01,B}
=\frac9{25}\frac9{25}
=\frac{81}{625}
<\frac9{25}.
\tag{27}
\]

The exact shortfall is (144/625). This contradicts (3) and proves Theorem 1 for each convention.

For the larger amplitude space (7), independently multiply one basis column of (W) by a phase and one basis column of (Z) by a phase. This does not change any projection marginal. Average the hypothesized weight formulas over fourth roots of unity for those two phases. The four sectors in (7) have distinct characters, so the averaging removes all cross terms between them and gives a sum of separate SOS blocks. Conjugating an entire amplitude does not change its square. The averaged formula therefore reduces to sums of the two already treated conventions, and the same argument (22)–(27) applies. A universal formula before averaging would have produced a universal formula after averaging. This completes the theorem.

The proof uses boundary flags because nonnegative marginals force exact amplitude zeros there. A fixed Gram formula asserted on every generic full-support flag would extend to these flags by continuity, so restricting the assertion to that dense set does not avoid the obstruction.

## 4. Actual top Plücker coordinates are covered

Write

\[
t_T=\det[w_1\ w_2\ u\ v]_T\qquad(|T|=4).
\]

The complementary-minor identity for the unitary matrix (Q) gives

\[
t_T=\varepsilon_T\det(Q)\,\overline z_{E\setminus T},
\qquad\varepsilon_T\in\{-1,1\}.
\tag{28}
\]

Consequently any square of a fixed linear combination of the natural products (p_Ct_T) is, after absorbing the fixed signs into its coefficients,

\[
\left|\sum_{C,T}h_{C,T}p_Ct_T\right|^2
=\left|\sum_{C,D}\widetilde h_{C,D}p_C\overline z_D\right|^2,
\tag{29}
\]

because (|\det Q|=1). These are precisely the sesquilinear squares ruled out above. The result therefore obstructs a universal SOS built from products of the bottom and top Plücker coordinates even when all flag-incidence relations are used.

This differs from C012's obstruction to affine lifts on a particular Fourier ellipsoid. Here the candidate weights can be nonlinear functions of the flag, and the proof treats all bilinear endpoint-product Gram matrices, including all 36 linear relations among those products. Neither result implies nonexistence of the piecewise or frame-dependent couplings constructed in the other reports.
