## 1. The proved family and explicit curvature bound

Let
\[
P=\frac13\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},\qquad
K_t=\frac{1+t}{2}I-\frac t2P
=\frac{1+t}{2}I-\frac t6\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T},
\qquad -1<t<1.
\tag{1}
\]
Its eigenvalues are
\[
\frac12,\quad \frac{1+t}{2},\quad\frac{1+t}{2},
\]
so every base is a strict positive contraction. Write
\(H(K)=-\sum_Xp_X(K)\log p_X(K)\), with natural logarithms.

**Theorem.** For every Hermitian \(A\),
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K_t)[A,A]
\le -\frac{4t^2}{9-t^2}\,\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
For \(t\ne0\), this proves negative definiteness of the entire Hessian. For \(t=0\), the exact Hessian is
\[
D^2H(I/2)[A,A]=-4\sum_i A_{ii}^2\le0.
\tag{3}
\]
The conclusion and constant persist under diagonal unitary conjugation, coordinate permutations, and complex conjugation. Every nonzero base has an ambient open neighborhood on which the Hessian remains negative definite, by continuity. No uniform neighborhood radius is asserted.

For example,
\[
K_{1/2}=\frac1{12}
\begin{pmatrix}8&-1&-1\\-1&8&-1\\-1&-1&8\end{pmatrix}
\]
has
\[
D^2H(K_{1/2})[A,A]\le-\frac4{35}\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{4}
\]
The family approaches the spectra \((1/2,0,0)\) and \((1/2,1,1)\). It is outside C008's particle–hole symmetric locus whenever \(t\ne0\), since its point-coordinate diagonals are \(1/2+t/3\). It is also outside C010's small-odds family, even after particle–hole complementation: its odds matrix has
\[
\operatorname{tr}L_t=\frac{3+t}{1-t}>1,
\]
whereas C010's sufficient condition entails \(\operatorname{tr}L\le\alpha<1\).

## 2. Symmetry eliminates seven real directions exactly

First consider the full exchangeable locus
\[
K(u,v)=uP+v(I-P),\qquad 0<u,v<1,\quad u\ne v.
\tag{5}
\]
These kernels have equal diagonal entries and equal real off-diagonal entries. Their graphs are connected.

The source's exact curvature identity is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A),
\tag{6}
\]
where
\[
M=D_0+\tau K>0.
\]
Exchangeability makes the three entries of \(D_0\) equal, say \(c\), so
\[
M=\mu P+\nu(I-P),\qquad
\mu=c+\tau u>0,\quad \nu=c+\tau v>0.
\tag{7}
\]
The source also gives, for \(A=M^{1/2}BM^{1/2}\) and \(\delta=\det M\),
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)-\delta\|B\|_F^2.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, curvature is strictly negative on the hyperplane
\(\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A)=0\).

Let \(\Pi\) average conjugation by the six coordinate permutation matrices. Its fixed subspace on Hermitian matrices is exactly
\[
\operatorname{range}\Pi
=\{xP+y(I-P):x,y\in\mathbb R\}.
\tag{9}
\]
This follows directly: invariant diagonal entries are equal, and invariant off-diagonal entries are equal and real. The averaging map is the orthogonal projection for the Frobenius inner product.

Decompose
\[
A=A_\parallel+A_\perp,
\qquad A_\parallel=\Pi A=xP+y(I-P),\qquad \Pi A_\perp=0.
\]
Because \(K\) is fixed by every permutation, the entropy Hessian is permutation invariant. Averaging its polarized cross term therefore gives
\[
D^2H(K)[A_\parallel,A_\perp]=0.
\tag{10}
\]
Also \(M^{-1}\) is fixed, so
\[
\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A_\perp)
=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}\Pi A_\perp)=0.
\]
Equation (8) consequently makes the entire seven-dimensional perpendicular subspace strictly negative. No Fisher features have been discarded in the remaining two-dimensional problem.

When \(\nu\ge\mu\), the useful quantitative version is
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A_\perp,A_\perp]\le-\mu\|A_\perp\|_F^2.}
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, \(\delta=\mu\nu^2\), \(\|M\|_{\rm op}=\nu\), and
\[
\|A_\perp\|_F
\le\nu\|M^{-1/2}A_\perp M^{-1/2}\|_F.
\]
Substitute this in (8), whose trace term vanishes.

## 3. Exact conditional variance on the two-parameter locus

The four per-atom probabilities, indexed by cardinality, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=(1-u)(1-v)^2,\qquad &d&=uv^2,\\
b&=\frac{(1-v)(u+2v-3uv)}3,\qquad
&c_2&=\frac{v(2u+v-3uv)}3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
There is one atom of mass \(a\), three of mass \(b\), three of mass \(c_2\), and one of mass \(d\). The formulas follow from the count-generating polynomial
\[
(1-u+uz)(1-v+vz)^2
\]
and exchangeability.

Equivalently, draw independent variables
\[
U\sim\operatorname{Bern}(u),\qquad
V_1,V_2\sim\operatorname{Bern}(v),\qquad N=U+V_1+V_2,
\]
then uniformly shuffle their three occupations. The resulting subset law is exactly the law in (12). Conditional on \(N\), the random shuffle does not depend on the parameters. Thus its observed Fisher information is precisely the Fisher information of \(N\).

Put
\[
\alpha=u(1-u),\qquad \beta=v(1-v),
\]
\[
A_1=u+2v-3uv>0,\qquad A_2=2u+v-3uv>0,
\]
and let the genuine conditional-variance quantity be
\[
w:=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(U\mid N).
\]
The two nontrivial posterior probabilities are
\[
P(U=1\mid N=1)=\frac{u(1-v)}{A_1},\qquad
P(U=1\mid N=2)=\frac{2u(1-v)}{A_2}.
\]
Multiplying their Bernoulli variances by the corresponding count masses gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
w=2\alpha\beta\left(\frac{1-v}{A_1}+\frac{v}{A_2}\right).}
\tag{13}
\]

The complete-data scores are
\[
S_u^{\rm comp}=\frac{U-u}{\alpha},\qquad
S_v^{\rm comp}=\frac{V_1+V_2-2v}{\beta}.
\]
Their covariance matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(1/\alpha,2/\beta)\). Given \(N\), their random parts are respectively \(U/\alpha\) and \(-U/\beta\). Applying the law of total covariance proves the **exact**, rather than lower-bound, observed Fisher matrix
\[
\boxed{
F=
\begin{pmatrix}1/\alpha&0\\0&2/\beta\end{pmatrix}
-w\begin{pmatrix}1/\alpha\\-1/\beta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1/\alpha&-1/\beta\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{14}
\]
Writing \(\kappa=w/(\alpha\beta)\), its entries are
\[
F_{uu}=\frac{1-\beta\kappa}{\alpha},\qquad
F_{uv}=\kappa,\qquad
F_{vv}=\frac{2-\alpha\kappa}{\beta}.
\tag{15}
\]
This conditional-variance identity retains all information lost by observing only the shuffled subset. In particular it does not replace the Fisher matrix by the covariance bound from the particle count mean alone.

The frozen logarithmic coefficients in (7) are explicitly
\[
c=\log\frac{b^2}{a c_2},\qquad
\tau=\log\frac{a c_2^3}{d b^3},\qquad
\mu=c+\tau u,\quad \nu=c+\tau v.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(A_\parallel=xP+y(I-P)\), its eigenvalues are \((x,y,y)\), and (6) becomes
\[
D^2H(K)[A_\parallel,A_\parallel]
=-\begin{pmatrix}x&y\end{pmatrix}Q
\binom xy,
\]
\[
\boxed{
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
F_{uu}&F_{uv}-2\nu\\
F_{uv}-2\nu&F_{vv}-2\mu
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{17}
\]
The upper-left entry is strictly positive: the full atom has derivative \(\partial_u d=v^2>0\). It follows that the **entire nine-coordinate Hessian is nonpositive if and only if**
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S(u,v):=
F_{vv}-2\mu-\frac{(F_{uv}-2\nu)^2}{F_{uu}}\ge0.}
\tag{18}
\]
This is the promised exact elimination of the unique potentially positive mode on the exchangeable locus. With (13)–(16), it is an explicit scalar conditional-variance inequality. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\det Q=\det F+4F_{uv}\nu-2F_{uu}\mu-4\nu^2\ge0.}
\tag{19}
\]
No universal claim for (18) is made yet. The next sections prove it uniformly when \(u=1/2\).

## 4. Explicit data on the half-eigenvalue slice

Set
\[
u=\frac12,\qquad v=\frac{1+t}{2},\qquad
s=t^2,\qquad e=1-s,\qquad D=9-s.
\tag{20}
\]
Particle–hole complementation sends \(t\) to \(-t\) and preserves the Hessian quadratic form, so it suffices to use \(0<t<1\).

The per-atom masses specialize to
\[
a=\frac{(1-t)^2}{8},\qquad
b=\frac{(1-t)(3+t)}{24},\qquad
c_2=\frac{(1+t)(3-t)}{24},\qquad
d=\frac{(1+t)^2}{8}.
\tag{21}
\]
Equations (16) give
\[
\boxed{
\mu=\frac12\log\frac{9-t^2}{9(1-t^2)},\qquad
\nu=\mu+t\left(\operatorname{atanh}t
                  -3\operatorname{atanh}(t/3)\right).}
\tag{22}
\]
The difference inside parentheses is positive for \(t>0\): it vanishes at zero and has derivative
\[
\frac1{1-t^2}-\frac1{1-t^2/9}>0.
\]
Thus
\[
0<\mu\le\nu.
\tag{23}
\]

Substituting (20) in the conditional-variance formula (14), or differentiating (12) directly, gives
\[
\boxed{
F_{uu}=\frac{4(3+3s+2s^2)}D,
\qquad F_{uv}=\frac{8(3+s)}D,
\qquad F_{vv}=\frac{16(3-s)}{eD}.}
\tag{24}
\]
Their determinant and the needed linear difference simplify to
\[
\boxed{
\det F=\frac{64s(1+s)}{eD},\qquad
4F_{uv}-2F_{uu}=\frac{8(9+s-2s^2)}D>0.}
\tag{25}
\]
All these rational identities are independently regenerated in the accompanying verification program.

## 5. A uniform Schur-complement inequality

For every \(z\ge1\),
\[
\frac{z-1}{z}\le\log z\le\frac{z-1}{\sqrt z}.
\tag{26}
\]
The first follows by integrating \(1/x\ge1/z\) over \([1,z]\). For the second, the derivative of its right side minus \(\log z\) is
\((\sqrt z-1)^2/(2z^{3/2})\ge0\), and the difference vanishes at one.

Using the upper bound first at \(z=D/(9e)\), then at \(z=(1+t)/(1-t)\), gives
\[
\mu\le\frac{4s}{3\sqrt{eD}},\qquad
\operatorname{atanh}t\le\frac{t}{\sqrt e}.
\]
Dropping the nonpositive term \(-3t\operatorname{atanh}(t/3)\) in (22) and using \(\sqrt D\le3\),
\[
\boxed{
\nu\le\frac{s(4/3+\sqrt D)}{\sqrt{eD}}
\le\frac{13s}{3\sqrt{eD}}.}
\tag{27}
\]

Equations (23) and (25) show that the linear logarithmic terms in (19) are nonnegative:
\[
4F_{uv}\nu-2F_{uu}\mu
=(4F_{uv}-2F_{uu})\mu+4F_{uv}(\nu-\mu)\ge0.
\]
Consequently (25) and (27) prove
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det Q
&\ge \det F-4\nu^2\\
&\ge \frac{64s(1+s)-(676/9)s^2}{eD}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{4s(144-25s)}{9eD}>0.}
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
This proves the missing Schur inequality (18) on the entire displayed family. More explicitly, since \(F_{uu}>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S(1/2,(1+t)/2)
\ge\frac{s(144-25s)}{9(1-s)(3+3s+2s^2)}>0.}
\tag{29}
\]
The genuine conditional variance in (13)–(14) is used exactly; the proof's only inequalities are the transparent logarithm bounds and the nonnegative linear-term deletion above.

## 6. Quantitative bound in every Hermitian direction

Since \(Q>0\), its smallest eigenvalue is at least \(\det Q/\operatorname{tr}Q\). Also
\[
\operatorname{tr}Q\le F_{uu}+F_{vv}
=\frac{4(15-4s-s^2-2s^3)}{eD}.
\]
The denominator polynomial is at least 8 for \(0\le s\le1\). Thus (28) implies
\[
Q\succeq\eta(s)I_2,
\qquad
\eta(s)=\frac{s(144-25s)}{9(15-4s-s^2-2s^3)}.
\tag{30}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\frac{\eta(s)}2\ge\frac{4s}{9-s}.}
\tag{31}
\]
After clearing positive denominators, (31) is exactly
\[
(144-25s)(9-s)-72(15-4s-s^2-2s^3)
=216-81s+97s^2+144s^3>0.
\]
Positivity is immediate from \(216-81s\ge135\).

Because \(\|xP+y(I-P)\|_F^2=x^2+2y^2\), (30)–(31) give
\[
D^2H(K_t)[A_\parallel,A_\parallel]
\le-\frac{4s}{9-s}\|A_\parallel\|_F^2.
\tag{32}
\]
The lower logarithm bound in (26) gives
\[
\mu=\frac12\log\frac D{9e}\ge\frac{4s}D.
\tag{33}
\]
Apply (11), then combine (10), (32), and the Frobenius orthogonality of \(A_\parallel,A_\perp\). This proves (2) for \(t\ne0\). At \(t=0\), every atom derivative depends only on the diagonal of \(A\), yielding (3) directly.

## 7. What is proved, and what remains open

Proved statements:

- The exact symmetry reduction (9)–(10) for the entire exchangeable locus, retaining the complete ambient Hermitian Hessian.
- The exact missing-information formula (13)–(15), using the actual posterior conditional variance \(\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(U\mid N)\).
- The exact scalar Schur criterion (18) for all exchangeable strict three-site kernels.
- The uniform Schur lower bound (29) on \(u=1/2\), and the full-Hermitian quantitative theorem (2) throughout \(-1<t<1\).

Limitations of the present proof:

- This report does not prove (18) for unrestricted \(u,v\); the coordinator reports that C017 supplies an independent proof on that broader locus. The argument here uses two special properties of the half-eigenvalue slice: the favorable linear Fisher comparison in (25) and the simple even logarithms in (22). Neither is assumed outside this slice.
- Eliminating the symmetry assumption. There is no proposed universal scalar atom statistic here, and C011's parity-span obstruction is not bypassed by claiming otherwise.
- Proving the unrestricted three-site or all-dimensional Lyons conjecture.

This is a theorem for base kernels with a repeated eigenvalue and a flat distinguished eigenvector, together with their stated coordinate symmetries. Arbitrary unitary conjugation is not allowed: occupation entropy is not invariant under it.
