# C044 — A positive-coordinate contraction bridge and exact corank-two induction

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **COMPLETE bounded induction theorem, exact criterion, and certificates.**

This report proves a dimension-extension rule for projection diamonds whose top has corank two. It applies when **every coordinate has positive mass in each of the bottom, update, and complementary spaces**. The rule uses a certified contraction face, one ordinary inclusion cover, and a modified deletion-face transport. The required modification is derived exactly; separately chosen conditional faces are never assumed to glue.

The deletion transport has an exact necessary-and-sufficient Hall criterion for this specified bridge. A simpler positive bound follows from C033's strict Hall theorem. Consequently there are nonempty open families of six-site rank-2/2/2 flags covered by this construction, and the extension can be iterated to arbitrarily large bottom rank with top corank two. All update-plane pair minors can remain nonzero throughout the iteration.

The exact six-site example has 57 positive diamond atoms, all 18 coordinate masses positive, and all 15 bottom, top, and update-plane pair probabilities positive. Its bridge parameter is

\[
\lambda=\frac{225}{10426}<\frac{2401}{41704}=\Lambda.
\]

Here \(\Lambda\) is the exact feasibility threshold for the conditional transport at this frame. Another parameter in the same family has a negative forced residual pair probability, proving that the bridge recipe does not solve arbitrary flags.

C038 proves coordinate gluing when a coordinate is missing one of the three colors. C040 proves projection-block substitution, and C036 proves specified unitary composition rules. The new result here is the conditional signed transport repair, its exact threshold, and a positive-coordinate dimension-extension rule. The entropy consequence uses the established diamond argument. ## 1. The generic conditional laws and their mismatch

Let

\[
\mathbb C^E=W\oplus U\oplus Z,
\qquad \dim U=\dim Z=2,
\]

and put \(\dim W=r=|E|-4\). Write \(\mu_H\) for the projection DPP of a subspace \(H\). The contrast is

\[
\mathcal B(W,U)=\mu_W+\mu_{W+U}-\mu_{W+u}-\mu_{W+v},
\tag{1}
\]

where \(u,v\) is any orthonormal basis of \(U\). Its aggregate middle measure is basis independent: for each coordinate set, the two middle amplitudes transform by a unitary \(2\times2\) matrix, preserving their sum of squared moduli.

Fix a coordinate \(e\), and assume all three masses at this coordinate are positive:

\[
a=(P_W)_{ee}>0,\qquad b=(P_U)_{ee}>0,
\qquad c=(P_Z)_{ee}>0,\qquad a+b+c=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Put \(F=E\setminus\{e\}\), \(s=a+b=1-c\), and define

\[
w=P_We/\sqrt a,\quad u=P_Ue/\sqrt b,\quad z=P_Ze/\sqrt c,
\]

\[
A=W\cap e^\perp,\qquad
[v]=U\cap e^\perp,\qquad [z_0]=Z\cap e^\perp.
\]

Take \(v,z_0\) unit. There are orthonormal \(f,k\in\mathbb C^F\), orthogonal to \(A,v,z_0\), for which

\[
\begin{aligned}
L&=\sqrt s\,e+\sqrt c\,k,\\
w&=\sqrt{a/s}\,L+\sqrt{b/s}\,f,\\
u&=\sqrt{b/s}\,L-\sqrt{a/s}\,f,\\
z&=\sqrt c\,e-\sqrt s\,k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For a direct derivation, set \(L=(\sqrt a\,w+\sqrt b\,u)/\sqrt s\), \(f=(\sqrt b\,w-\sqrt a\,u)/\sqrt s\), and \(k=\sqrt c\,L-\sqrt s\,z\). The identity \(e=\sqrt a\,w+\sqrt b\,u+\sqrt c\,z\) proves (3) and all stated orthogonality properties. Thus (3) describes every flag satisfying (2), rather than imposing a real-frame assumption. The vectors may be complex.

Define another orthonormal basis of \([f,k]\) by

\[
x=\frac{\sqrt b\,f+\sqrt{ac}\,k}{\sqrt{s(1-a)}},
\qquad
g=\frac{\sqrt{ac}\,f-\sqrt b\,k}{\sqrt{s(1-a)}}.
\tag{4}
\]

In particular \(w=\sqrt a\,e+\sqrt{1-a}\,x\). Notation such as \(A+x+v\) means the orthogonal direct sum of \(A\) and the displayed lines. The complete conditional table is:

| Projection space | Law on sets avoiding \(e\) | Law after removing \(e\) from sets containing it |
|---|---|---|
| \(W\) | \((1-a)\mu_{A+x}\) | \(a\mu_A\) |
| \(W+u\) | \(c\mu_{A+f+k}\) | \(s\mu_{A+f}\) |
| \(W+v\) | \((1-a)\mu_{A+x+v}\) | \(a\mu_{A+v}\) |
| \(W+u+v\) | \(c\mu_{A+f+k+v}\) | \(s\mu_{A+f+v}\) |

These are subprobability laws, including the branch weights. Expand the normalized exterior form according to whether it contains \(e\). For \(W+u\), the two moving vectors span \([L,f]\); the \(e\)-coefficient has squared norm \(s\) and the absent coefficient has squared norm \(c\). The other rows follow by adjoining the fixed vectors of \(A\) and, where applicable, \(v\). This proves every entry of the table.

Let \(\iota_e\) insert \(e\) into a law on \(F\), and write the contrast as \(B_0+\iota_eB_1\). Define the two ordinary conditional projection contrasts

\[
D_{\rm del}=\mathcal B(A+x,[g,v]),
\qquad D_{\rm con}=\mathcal B(A,[f,v]).
\]

The table gives the exact identities

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
B_0&=cD_{\rm del}+b(\mu_{A+x}-\mu_{A+x+v}),\\
B_1&=aD_{\rm con}+b(\mu_{A+f+v}-\mu_{A+f}).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5}
\]

They expose the gluing problem. A bridge diamond crossing coordinate \(e\) has the form

\[
S,\quad M,\quad S+e,\quad M+e,
\qquad S\subset M,\quad |M\setminus S|=1.
\tag{6}
\]

Its absent contribution is \(\delta_S-\delta_M\), and its present contribution is the negative of that same measure. The leftover terms in (5) therefore match directly only when

\[
\mu_{A+x}-\mu_{A+x+v}
=\mu_{A+f}-\mu_{A+f+v}.
\tag{7}
\]

Since the two ranks are distinct, this requires both endpoint equalities separately. In the exact example below neither equality holds. Independently coupling \(D_{\rm del}\) and \(D_{\rm con}\) leaves a genuine discrepancy.

## 2. Fix one bridge, and compute the required deletion repair

Take **one** inclusion coupling

\[
S\subset M,\qquad
S\sim\mu_{A+f},\quad M\sim\mu_{A+f+v}.
\tag{8}
\]

This always exists. If \(\alpha\) is a unit exterior form of \(A+f\), then \(\zeta=v\wedge\alpha\) is a unit form for \(A+f+v\). For a family \(\mathcal A\) of lower-rank sets and its inclusion shadow \(N(\mathcal A)\),

\[
\|P_{\mathcal A}\alpha\|^2
=\|P_{\mathcal A}\iota_vP_{N(\mathcal A)}\zeta\|^2
\le\|P_{N(\mathcal A)}\zeta\|^2.
\tag{9}
\]

The norm of contraction by a unit vector is at most one. These are all weighted Hall inequalities, so exact inclusion max flow gives (8). This proves the only one-step cover used in the bridge.

Use (6) with this same draw, with total weight \(b\). Use any certified coupling of \(D_{\rm con}\), with \(e\) inserted at all vertices, with total weight \(a\). Equation (5) forces the remaining absent-face signed measure to be

\[
\boxed{
R_0=cD_{\rm del}
+b(\mu_{A+x}-\mu_{A+f}-\mu_{A+x+v}+\mu_{A+f+v}).}
\tag{10}
\]

Its required marginal tables are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{bottom: }&(b+c)\mu_{A+x}-b\mu_{A+f},\\
\text{top: }&c\mu_{A+f+k+v},\\
\text{aggregate middle: }&c\mu_{A+f+k}
+(b+c)\mu_{A+x+v}-b\mu_{A+f+v}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

The total diamond mass here is \(c\). The signed notation does not assert positivity of any table in (11). Positivity is the exact obligation addressed next.

Complement all sets inside \(F\). Because \(Z\) has dimension two, the bottom of the complemented face has rank one, namely \([z_0]\). Define probability laws on pairs and triples of \(F\):

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
h_g=\mu_{[g,v]},&C_g=\mu_{[z_0,g,v]},\\
h_k=\mu_{[k,v]},&C_k=\mu_{[z_0,k,v]}.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]

The two tables which an inclusion transport must realize are

\[
\boxed{h=(b+c)h_g-bh_k,\qquad C=(b+c)C_g-bC_k.}
\tag{13}
\]

Both have mass \(c\), but are not automatically nonnegative.

**Lemma 1 (the repaired face is exactly one inclusion transport).** There is a nonnegative diamond decomposition of \(R_0\) if and only if there is a nonnegative transport

\[
y_{B,T}\ge0,\quad B\subset T,\quad |B|=2,\quad |T|=3,
\qquad
\sum_{T\supset B}y_{B,T}=h_B,\quad
\sum_{B\subset T}y_{B,T}=C_T.
\tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** For any pair-inside-triple transport, use the rank-one diamond with bottom \(T\setminus B\), top \(T\), and addition pair \(B\). Its bottom singleton masses and aggregate middle pair masses are respectively

\[
C_i-h_i,\qquad C^{(2)}_B-h_B,
\tag{15}
\]

where subscripts \(i\) and superscript \((2)\) denote inclusion marginals. These are combinatorial identities: a coordinate in \(T\) lies either in \(B\) or in its singleton complement; a pair inside \(T\) is a middle precisely when it is different from \(B\).

For the tables (13), the projection inclusion identities give

\[
C_i-h_i=c|z_{0,i}|^2,
\tag{16}
\]

\[
C^{(2)}-h
=c\mu_{[z_0,v]}+(b+c)\mu_{[z_0,g]}-b\mu_{[z_0,k]}.
\tag{17}
\]

For example \(C_g^{(2)}=\mu_{[z_0,g]}+\mu_{[z_0,v]}+h_g\) by two-row Cauchy–Binet. Thus (15) gives exactly the complement of (11). Complement each diamond back to obtain \(R_0\). The complement identity for projection laws follows from complementary minors of a unitary frame.

Conversely, complement any decomposition of \(R_0\). Its top marginal is \(C\), and counting its middles as in (15) forces its addition-pair marginal to be \(C^{(2)}-\text{middle}=h\). It therefore produces (14). This proves both directions, including all boundary zero entries. ∎

## 3. The exact criterion and a simpler strict bound

For every family \(\mathcal A\subseteq\binom F2\), let \(N(\mathcal A)\) be its triple inclusion shadow and put

\[
\sigma_g(\mathcal A)=C_g(N(\mathcal A))-h_g(\mathcal A),
\quad
\sigma_k(\mathcal A)=C_k(N(\mathcal A))-h_k(\mathcal A).
\tag{18}
\]

Both are nonnegative by (9), using contraction by \(z_0\). Introduce

\[
\lambda=\frac b{b+c}=\frac b{1-a}\in(0,1).
\tag{19}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda=
\min\left\{
1,
\min_{B:h_k(B)>0}\frac{h_g(B)}{h_k(B)},
\min_{T:C_k(T)>0}\frac{C_g(T)}{C_k(T)},
\min_{\mathcal A:\sigma_k(\mathcal A)>0}
\frac{\sigma_g(\mathcal A)}{\sigma_k(\mathcal A)}
\right\}.}
\tag{20}
\]

An inner minimum over an empty collection is omitted. The quantities in (20) are evaluated at the actual conditional vectors (4); the notation does not claim that \(\Lambda\) stays constant as the original frame varies.

**Theorem 2 (exact bridge induction).** Suppose the contraction flag \((A,[f,v])\) has an unlabeled projection-diamond coupling. Then the construction in Sections 1–2 extends it to the full flag whenever

\[
\lambda\le\Lambda.
\tag{21}
\]

For the recipe which keeps the contraction branch at weight \(a\) and uses the bridge endpoints (8) at weight \(b\), condition (21) is also necessary. It places no restriction on the internal choice of either coupling.

**Proof.** Nonnegativity of both tables in (13) is exactly the pair and triple inequalities in (20). Their weighted Hall inequalities are exactly

\[
C(N(\mathcal A))-h(\mathcal A)
=(b+c)\bigl(\sigma_g(\mathcal A)-\lambda\sigma_k(\mathcal A)\bigr)\ge0.
\tag{22}
\]

Terms with zero denominator impose no condition because their numerator is nonnegative. Hence (21) is necessary and sufficient for nonnegative marginals of equal mass and all Hall inequalities.

For completeness, a source/pair/triple/sink network with source capacities \(h\), sink capacities \(C\), and inclusion-edge capacities \(2c\) has a flow of mass \(c\). A cut crossing an inclusion edge has capacity at least \(2c\). A cut crossing none has capacity at least \(c-h(\mathcal A)+C(N(\mathcal A))\ge c\). Thus max flow has value \(c\). Exact shortest-augmenting-path max flow terminates and supplies (14), including real or rational capacities. Lemma 1 completes the absent branch.

The three branches have masses \(a,b,c\). Their complete marginals can be checked without any implicit gluing assertion:

| Location | Contribution after adding the three branches |
|---|---|
| Bottom avoiding \(e\) | \(((b+c)\mu_{A+x}-b\mu_{A+f})+b\mu_{A+f}=(1-a)\mu_{A+x}\) |
| Bottom containing \(e\) | \(a\mu_A\) after removing \(e\) |
| Top avoiding \(e\) | \(c\mu_{A+f+k+v}\) |
| Top containing \(e\) | \((a+b)\mu_{A+f+v}\) after removing \(e\) |
| Middles avoiding \(e\) | \(c\mu_{A+f+k}+(b+c)\mu_{A+x+v}\) |
| Middles containing \(e\) | \((a+b)\mu_{A+f}+a\mu_{A+v}\) after removing \(e\) |

These are the conditional table in Section 1. Every sample is one elementary diamond. Necessity for the specified recipe follows because its remaining face is forced to be (10), and Lemma 1 and Hall give (20). ∎

This is a constructive induction: it requires an already certified contraction coupling, followed by two one-rank inclusion transports with proved feasibility. It never asks an unrestricted higher-rank diamond LP to certify its own conclusion.

**Corollary 3 (a positive, explicitly computable parameter range).** Suppose

\[
z_*:=\min_{i\in F}|z_{0,i}|^2>0,
\quad h_*:=\min_{B\in\binom F2}h_g(B)>0,
\quad C_*:=\min_{T\in\binom F3}C_g(T)>0.
\tag{23}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\lambda\le\eta:=\min\{C_*,z_*h_*\}}
\tag{24}
\]

implies (21). If the inequality is strict, both residual tables and every nonempty proper Hall slack are strictly positive.

**Proof.** C033, Theorem 4, applies to the orthonormal triple \((z_0,g,v)\) and gives

\[
\sigma_g(\mathcal A)\ge z_*h_*
\quad\text{for every nonempty proper pair family.}
\tag{25}
\]

Each entry of \(h_k,C_k\) and each slack \(\sigma_k\) is at most one. Also \(z_*\le1\), so \(\eta\le h_*\). Consequently all pair, triple, and proper-family inequalities in (20) follow from (24). The empty and full families have zero slack for both laws. Strict inequalities give the last assertion. ∎

The use of C033 is its previously proved quantitative rank-one theorem, not an assumption about higher-rank diamonds. The exact criterion (20) can be much stronger than the convenient bound (24), as the example demonstrates.

## 4. Exact conditional coherence amplification

The signed residual is not generally another projection law. This can be seen exactly, without a failed numerical induction.

For a pair \(B\), write \(F_B=\det[f\ v]_B\), \(K_B=\det[k\ v]_B\). Substituting (4) in (13) gives

\[
\frac{h_B}{c}
=\frac a{s}|F_B|^2+\frac b{s}|K_B|^2
-\frac{2\sqrt{ab}}{s\sqrt c}\operatorname{Re}(F_B\overline{K_B}).
\tag{26}
\]

The same formula gives \(C_T/c\) with amplitudes \(\det[z_0\ f\ v]_T\) and \(\det[z_0\ k\ v]_T\). Thus both measured tables use the trace-one matrix

\[
R=\frac1s
\begin{pmatrix}
a&-\sqrt{ab/c}\\
-\sqrt{ab/c}&b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\boxed{\det R=-\frac{ab}{c(a+b)}<0.}
\tag{27}
\]

Relative to a pure two-component superposition with those diagonal weights, the coherence has been multiplied by \(1/\sqrt c>1\). The eigenvalues are

\[
\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4ab/[c(a+b)]}}2.
\tag{28}
\]

One is strictly negative whenever all three coordinate masses are positive. Therefore a universal repair which interprets this residual **operator** as a positive projection mixture is impossible. Positivity of its finitely many measured tables and Hall slacks is a separate, weaker possibility. Theorem 2 exploits exactly that possibility. Equations (26)–(28) identify the conditional acceleration and show why deleting the mismatch term would be incorrect.

## 5. Positive-coordinate open families and arbitrarily many extensions

For six sites, the contraction flag lives on five sites with bottom rank one. Its coupling is always available from prior rank-one existence. Theorem 2 therefore gives an unconditional sufficient criterion for every six-site \(2/2/2\) flag satisfying it.

**Corollary 4 (an open six-site interior class).** The strict version of (24), and more generally strict positivity of the residual entries and proper Hall slacks in (20), describes a nonempty open set of six-site flags admitting an unlabeled diamond coupling. It contains flags for which every coordinate has positive mass in \(W,U,Z\) and every pair minor of each two-plane is nonzero.

**Proof.** At positive \(a,b,c\), all conditional projection probabilities and Hall slacks are continuous functions of the flag. There are finitely many entries and pair families. Strict inequalities and nonzero minors therefore persist on a neighborhood. Nonemptiness, including all the stated nonvanishing properties, follows from either exact certificate in Section 6. ∎

The same argument yields a genuine dimension-extension procedure. Start on a ground set \(F\) of size \(m\) with a certified flag

\[
\mathbb C^F=A\oplus[f,v]\oplus[k,z_0],
\qquad \dim A=m-4,
\tag{29}
\]

and assume every update-plane pair minor, every coordinate of \(z_0\), and every triple minor of \([z_0,f,v]\) is nonzero. Add a new coordinate \(e\), choose a fixed \(c\in(0,1)\), and choose \(b>0\) sufficiently small, with \(a=1-c-b\). Construct \(w,u,z\) by (3), and output

\[
W^+=A+[w],\qquad U^+=[u,v],\qquad Z^+=[z,z_0].
\tag{30}
\]

Its contraction face is exactly the source flag (29). As \(b\downarrow0\), \(g\to f\), the quantity \(\eta\) in (24) tends to a strictly positive limit, while \(\lambda=b/(b+c)\to0\). Thus (24) holds for all sufficiently small positive \(b\), proving the extension without a feasibility guess.

**Proposition 5 (iteration with full update-pair support).** The nonvanishing conditions in (29) can be maintained for arbitrarily many applications of (30). If the initial bottom has positive mass at every coordinate, all three spaces can have positive mass at every coordinate at every stage.

**Proof.** Before an extension, choose the orthonormal basis \(f,v\) of the source update plane so that every coordinate of \(v\) is nonzero. This is possible because nonzero pair minors imply nonzero coordinate evaluations, and a finite union of proper linear subspaces cannot exhaust the plane. Rotating its basis preserves all pair and triple nonvanishing conditions.

For an old pair, the new update-plane determinant tends, as \(b\to0\), to the corresponding nonzero determinant of \([-f,v]\). For a pair \(\{e,i\}\), it is, up to ordering, \(\sqrt b\,v_i\), which is nonzero for every \(b>0\). Hence all new update-plane pair minors are nonzero for sufficiently small positive \(b\).

For the next complementary distinguished line, choose

\[
z_0^+=\alpha z_0+\beta z,
\qquad \alpha^2+\beta^2=1,
\quad \alpha\beta\ne0,
\tag{31}
\]

with real \(\beta\) sufficiently small. At \(b=0\), its new-coordinate entry is \(\beta\sqrt c\ne0\), and its old-coordinate entries are \(\alpha z_{0,i}-\beta\sqrt{1-c}\,k_i\), all nonzero for small enough \(\beta\). On old triples, its determinant with \([-f,v]\) is a small perturbation of the nonzero determinant with \(z_0\). On triples \(\{e,i,j\}\), that determinant is, up to sign,

\[
\beta\sqrt c\,\det[f\ v]_{ij}\ne0.
\tag{32}
\]

Choose one such nonzero \(\beta\), then choose \(b>0\) small enough that all these finitely many nonvanishing conditions persist and (24) holds. Complete \(z_0^+\) to an orthonormal basis of the new complementary plane. This proves the induction of (29).

Every old bottom coordinate retains its original positive mass because the old bottom \(A\) is included in the new bottom. The new coordinate has the positive masses \(a,b,c\). Old update coordinates stay positive by the nonzero minors; old complementary coordinates stay positive because their plane contains the nonzero old entries of \(z_0\). This proves the last assertion. ∎

Beginning with the rational five-site rank-one seed in Section 6 gives certified families in every size \(n\ge6\), of bottom rank \(n-4\) and top corank two. Complementation gives analogous bottom-rank-two families with arbitrarily large top corank.

Full update-pair support also distinguishes these extensions from a nontrivial C040 projection-block substitution from a smaller ground set. Such a substitution restricts the entire update plane to a one-dimensional space on each local block. A block containing two coordinates therefore forces its internal update-plane pair minor to vanish. No nontrivial block is possible when every pair minor is nonzero. This is a precise comparison with that closure, not a classification of all alternative unitary factorizations in C036.

## 7. Supermodular and entropy consequences

For each constructed flag, averaging elementary supermodular inequalities against its positive coupling gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_W}F+\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+U}}F
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+u}}F-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+v}}F\ge0
\tag{41}
\]

for every supermodular set function \(F\). This holds for every orthonormal basis \(u,v\) of the same update plane because the aggregate middle law is basis independent.

Consequently, for

\[
K(t)=P_W+\rho(t)uu^*+\tau(t)vv^*,
\quad \rho,\tau\in[0,1]\text{ affine},
\quad \rho'\tau'\le0,
\tag{42}
\]

the entropy is concave. In the spectral-square interior, the atom second derivative is \(2\rho'\tau'\mathcal B(W,U)\). DPP atom log-submodularity pairs nonpositively with every elementary diamond, so both terms in

\[
H''=-\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
-2\rho'\tau'\sum_S\mathcal B(W,U)(S)\log p_S
\]
