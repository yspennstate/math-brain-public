# C024 — second-acceleration BKM control and a global coherent spectral corridor

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a constructive correction estimate for C006 (22), with an explicit new restricted family. No subagents, pushes, external messages, or bibliographic-priority claims. The full Lyons conjecture remains open.

## Proved scope

Four results are proved below.

1. **Exact equality classification for first-order Fisher data processing.** At a strict kernel whose nonzero-entry graph is connected, the entire equality space is the one-dimensional space
   \(A=cK(I-K)\). Thus first-order information loss vanishes at every coherent kernel in a nonzero, usually full-rank direction.
2. **A sharp measured correction bound using second-order information.** Project \(\log p\) off additive occupation observables. Its residual variance, multiplied by the measured Fisher norm of the acceleration, bounds the square of the missing pairing. The coefficient is sharp: this inequality is equality for every two-site covariance line. A second BKM contraction evaluates its quantum upper bound explicitly when \([K,A]=0\).
3. **A global concavity corridor with arbitrary eigenvectors.** One eigenvalue may traverse \([1/4,3/4]\), while every other eigenvalue changes simultaneously and stays in an explicit small interval. Both endpoints may be dense, and the direction may have full rank. The proof gives \(H''\le-1/2\) throughout the segment. No weak-block expansion is used.
4. **A strongly coherent four-mode family.** A second application certifies scalar shifts of connected kernels with eigenvalues near \(1/10\) and \(9/10\), uniformly over a continuum of mixing matrices. This application uses an exact rational logarithm certificate.

The corridor is quantitatively narrow near a rank-one spectrum. Its coherence need not be weak: if the leading eigenvector has no zero entries, normalized correlations tend to one as the small eigenvalues tend to zero.

## 1. What exact BKM recovery does and does not control

Use C006 notation
\[
\rho=\rho_K,\quad X=\rho'_K[A],\quad Y=\rho''_K[A,A],\quad
p=\operatorname{diag}\rho,
\]
and let \(g_\rho(Z,Z)=\operatorname{Tr}Z\,D\log_\rho[Z]\). Put
\[
b(x)=\log\frac{x}{1-x},\qquad B=Db_K[A].
\]

**Theorem 1.** For strict \(K\),
\[
g_\rho(X,X)=\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B\text{ is diagonal and }[B,K]=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
A=K(I-K)B,
\tag{2}
\]
where the diagonal entries of \(B\) are constant on each connected component of the graph with edges \(K_{ij}\ne0\).

**Proof.** Write
\[
\Omega_\rho(L)=\int_0^1\rho^sL\rho^{1-s}\,ds.
\]
The inverse of \(\Omega_\rho\) is \(D\log_\rho\), and
\[
g_\rho(X,X)=\max_{L=L^*}\{2\operatorname{Tr}XL-
\operatorname{Tr}L\Omega_\rho(L)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For Hermitian \(L\), the scalar logarithmic-mean inequality gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}L\Omega_\rho(L)\le\operatorname{Tr}\rho L^2,
\tag{4}
\]
with equality exactly when \([\rho,L]=0\). Indeed, in an eigenbasis of \(\rho\), compare the logarithmic mean of each pair of eigenvalues with their arithmetic mean; equality requires the corresponding off-diagonal entry of \(L\) to vanish whenever the eigenvalues differ.

The measured Fisher metric is the maximum of
\(2\operatorname{Tr}XL-\operatorname{Tr}\rho L^2\) over diagonal \(L\). Its unique maximizer is \(L_* =\operatorname{diag}(p'/p)\). Thus equality of the two metrics forces both equality in (4) at \(L_*\) and equality in the strictly concave maximization (3). Equivalently, \(D\log_\rho[X]\) must be diagonal and commute with \(\rho\). These conditions are also sufficient.

The Gaussian logarithm gives
\[
D\log_\rho[X]=cI+d\Gamma(B).
\]
It is occupation-diagonal exactly when \(B\) is diagonal. It commutes with \(\rho\) exactly when \([B,b(K)]=0\), equivalently \([B,K]=0\). In this case inversion of \(Db_K\) yields (2). Finally, \([B,K]_{ij}=(B_{ii}-B_{jj})K_{ij}\), proving the graph description. \(\square\)

For a connected kernel take \(A=K(I-K)\), and write
\(N=\sum_i n_i\), \(m=\operatorname{tr}K\). Then
\[
X=\rho(N-m),\qquad p'_S=p_S(|S|-m),
\qquad g_M=g_Q=\operatorname{tr}K(I-K).
\tag{5}
\]
This is exact first-order recoverability, not just a metric coincidence. The Petz map for occupation dephasing and reference \(\rho\) sends a diagonal operator \(D\) to
\[
\mathcal R_\rho(D)=\rho^{1/2}(\Delta\rho)^{-1/2}
D(\Delta\rho)^{-1/2}\rho^{1/2}.
\]
It recovers both \(\Delta\rho\) and \(\Delta X\), because \(N\) commutes with \(\rho\). The acceleration need not be recovered. C006's example already shows its pairing can be negative at (5); Theorem 1 identifies the full equality locus rather than assuming that defect is exceptional.

## 2. The sharp residual correction inequality

Let \(\xi_i(S)=\mathbf1_{i\in S}\). Define the interaction residual variance
\[
V_\perp(p)=\min_{c\in\mathbb R,\,d\in\mathbb R^n}
\sum_Sp_S\left(\log p_S-c-\sum_i d_i\xi_i(S)\right)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
This is explicit. If \(C=\operatorname{Cov}_p(\xi)\) and
\(r=\operatorname{Cov}_p(\xi,\log p)\), then
\[
V_\perp(p)=\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p)-r^TC^{-1}r.
\tag{7}
\]
Strictness gives full support, so \(C>0\). Its entries are
\(C_{ii}=K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})\) and \(C_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2\) for \(i\ne j\).

Define the measured acceleration norm
\[
J_M(Y)=\sum_S\frac{(p''_S)^2}{p_S}.
\]

**Theorem 2.** In every affine covariance direction,
\[
\boxed{
\left|\operatorname{Tr}Y(\log\Delta\rho-\log\rho)\right|^2
\le J_M(Y)V_\perp(p)
\le g_\rho(Y,Y)V_\perp(p).
}
\tag{8}
\]
The first inequality has sharp coefficient one.

**Proof.** C006 (21) gives \(\operatorname{Tr}Y\log\rho=0\). Also
\[
\sum_Sp''_S=0,\qquad \sum_Sp''_S\xi_i(S)=0,
\]
because total mass is constant and every occupation marginal is affine in the covariance. Thus the pairing against \(\log p\) equals its pairing against the minimizing residual in (6). Cauchy–Schwarz in \(L^2(p)\) gives the first bound. BKM contraction under dephasing, applied to the tangent \(Y\), gives the second. \(\square\)

Sharpness has a concrete Gaussian realization. For two sites order the probabilities as \((x,y,z,w)\) and put
\(L=\log(yz/(xw))\). The orthogonal complement of \(1,\xi_1,\xi_2\) is spanned by \((1/x,-1/y,-1/z,1/w)\). Hence
\[
V_\perp=\frac{L^2}{1/x+1/y+1/z+1/w},\qquad
J_M(Y)=4(\det A)^2(1/x+1/y+1/z+1/w).
\]
The square root in (8) is exactly \(2|\det A|L\), the absolute value of the actual acceleration pairing. The subsequent quantum upper bound in (8) need not be equality.

### Explicit second BKM norm for commuting paths

Suppose \(K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^*\) and
\(A=U\operatorname{diag}(a_i)U^*\). Set
\[
v_i=\lambda_i(1-\lambda_i),\quad
G_i=\frac{a_i^2}{v_i},\quad G=\sum_iG_i.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{g_\rho(Y,Y)=2\left(G^2-\sum_iG_i^2\right).}
\tag{9}
\]
To prove this, the eigenmode occupation variables \(Z_i\) are independent Bernoulli \(\lambda_i\). Put \(s_i=a_i(Z_i-\lambda_i)/v_i\). The product eigenvalue law \(q\) has
\[
\frac{q''}{q}=2\sum_{i<j}s_is_j.
\]
Distinct unordered pairs are orthogonal in \(L^2(q)\), and
\(\mathbb E s_i^2=G_i\). Since \(Y\) commutes with \(\rho\), its BKM norm is \(\sum(q'')^2/q\), proving (9). The same calculation may be viewed through the number-preserving measurement channel
\(W_{S,T}=|\det U_{S,T}|^2\) for \(|S|=|T|\), with \(p=Wq\).

Combining (8)–(9) with the variance bound for the statistic \(|S|\) gives the usable estimate
\[
\boxed{
H''(K;A)\le
-\frac{(\sum_i a_i)^2}{\sum_i v_i}
+\sqrt{2\left(G^2-\sum_iG_i^2\right)V_\perp(p)}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed \(\mathbb E|S|=\operatorname{tr}K\),
\(\operatorname{Var}|S|=\sum_i v_i\), and Cauchy–Schwarz against the measured score gives
\(g_M\ge(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/\sum_i v_i\).

Equation (10) is a correction estimate, not a claim that its right side is always negative. It is sharpest when the exact measured quantities in (8) are retained. At the equality directions (5), the Fisher lower bound in (10) is equality.

## 3. A spectral criterion and a global full-rank family

An elementary dimension bound removes all eigenvector dependence. For a positive law on \(d\) points,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\log p)\le(\log d)^2+2.
\tag{11}
\]
For completeness put \(L=-\log p(X)\), \(a=\log d\). Counting atoms gives
\(\Pr(L>a+t)\le e^{-t}\). Consequently
\(\mathbb E(L-a)_+^2\le2\), while
\((L-a)_-^2\le a^2\); finally
\(\operatorname{Var}(L)\le\mathbb E(L-a)^2\). This proves (11).

Set \(B_n=(n\log2)^2+2\). Every commuting direction satisfying
\[
\frac{(\sum_i a_i)^2}{\sum_i v_i}
>\sqrt{2B_n\left(G^2-\sum_iG_i^2\right)}
\tag{12}
\]
has strictly negative entropy curvature, **for every unitary eigenbasis**. This is already a spectral sufficient criterion; (6) often gives a much better certificate.

**Theorem 3 (global coherent corridor).** Let \(n\ge2\), fix any unitary \(U\), and suppose
\[
0<\varepsilon\le\frac1{64(n-1)B_n}.
\tag{13}
\]
Choose arbitrary \(\lambda_{i,0},\lambda_{i,1}\in[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]\) for \(i\ge2\), and define
\[
K_t=U\operatorname{diag}\left(
\frac14+\frac t2,
(1-t)\lambda_{2,0}+t\lambda_{2,1},\ldots,
(1-t)\lambda_{n,0}+t\lambda_{n,1}\right)U^*.
\tag{14}
\]
Then throughout \([0,1]\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K_t})\le-\frac12,}
\qquad
H(P_{K_t})\ge(1-t)H(P_{K_0})+tH(P_{K_1})
+\frac14t(1-t).
\tag{15}
\]
All small eigenvalues may change, with arbitrary signs of their changes.

**Proof.** Put \(\eta=(n-1)\varepsilon\), so \(\eta\le1/(64B_n)\le1/128\). Along (14), \(a_1=1/2\), \(|a_i|\le\varepsilon\), and
\[
\sum_i v_i\le\frac14+2\eta,
\quad \sum_i a_i\ge\frac12-\eta,
\quad G_1\le\frac43,
\quad R:=\sum_{i\ge2}G_i\le\frac{\eta}{1-2\varepsilon}\le\frac43\eta.
\]
Therefore the negative term in (10) has magnitude at least
\[
\frac{(1/2-\eta)^2}{1/4+2\eta}
\ge\frac{3969}{4352}>\frac78.
\]
The square of its positive term, using \(V_\perp\le B_n\), is at most
\[
B_n\left(\frac{16}3R+2R^2\right)
\le B_n\left(\frac{64}9\eta+\frac{32}9\eta^2\right)
\le\frac19+\frac1{2304}<\frac9{64}.
\]
The positive term is consequently less than \(3/8\), proving \(H''\le-1/2\). Integration gives the chord bound. \(\square\)

This is not an extrapolation of C006 to an unphysical diagonal reservoir. It permits distinct small eigenvalues at both endpoints and arbitrary eigenvectors; there need be no scalar or diagonal point on the affine line.

## 4. Strong coherence without a small eigenvalue parameter

The second-order estimate also gives an explicit finite segment at substantial coherence. Let \(O\) be any complex \(2\times2\) unitary with
\[
\frac9{25}\le |O_{ij}|^2\le\frac{16}{25}
\quad\text{for all }i,j,
\]
and put
\[
K_t=\left(\frac12+t\right)I_4+
\frac25\begin{pmatrix}0&O\\O^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{16}
\]
Its nonzero-entry graph is connected; each cross entry has modulus between \(6/25\) and \(8/25\).

**Theorem 4.** For every such \(O\) and \(|t|\le1/2000\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K_t})\le-\frac{100}{63}.}
\tag{17}
\]
This is a full-rank scalar direction at a strongly coherent four-mode kernel. The interval is deliberately conservative.

**Proof.** At zero let \(v=9/100\), \(w=41/100\), and \(r=|O_{11}|^2\). The atom values and multiplicities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{value}&v^2&vw&w^2&w^2-(4/25)r&w^2-(4/25)(1-r)\\
\text{multiplicity}&2&8&2&2&2.
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]
For example \(K_0(I-K_0)=vI\), so its \(L\)-ensemble matrix is \((wI+(2/5)S)/v\), where \(S\) is the off-diagonal block matrix in (16). The one- and two-site principal minors give (18), and complementation gives the triple atoms.

Put \(f(x)=x(\log x+5/2)^2\). On
\([657/10000,1105/10000]\),
\(f''(x)=2(\log x+7/2)/x>0\). Thus the second moment
\(\mathbb E_{p_0}(\log p_0+5/2)^2\) is a convex symmetric function of \(r\), and is maximized at \(r=9/25\) or \(16/25\). At either endpoint the five atom values in (18) are
\[
(81,369,1681,1105,657)/10000.
\]
The exact rational certificate described below proves
\[
\mathbb E_{p_0}(\log p_0+5/2)^2<\frac{19}{40}<\frac12.
\tag{19}
\]

The eigenvalues along (16) are \(1/10+t\) and \(9/10+t\), each twice. For \(|t|\le h=1/2000\), every eigenmode atom has logarithmic derivative of absolute value at most \(4/(1/10-h)\). The fixed eigenmode-to-occupation channel therefore gives
\[
e^{-\theta}p_0\le p_t\le e^\theta p_0,
\qquad |\log p_t-\log p_0|\le\theta,
\quad \theta=\frac4{199}<\frac1{49}.
\]
Consequently, by (19), Minkowski's inequality, and \(e^\theta\le1/(1-\theta)\),
\[
V_\perp(p_t)\le\mathbb E_{p_t}(\log p_t+5/2)^2
\le\frac{49}{48}\left(\frac57+\frac1{49}\right)^2
=\frac{27}{49}<\frac9{16}.
\tag{20}
\]
Here \(\sqrt{1/2}<5/7\).

For \(A=I_4\), each \(G_i=1/v_i\) is at most \(80/7\), since
\(v_i\ge9/100-(4/5)h-h^2>7/80\). Hence (9) gives
\(\sqrt{g_\rho(Y,Y)}\le\sqrt{24}(80/7)<400/7\).
The measured Fisher lower bound is
\[
g_M\ge\frac{16}{4(9/100-t^2)}\ge\frac{400}{9}.
\]
Equations (8) and (20) now give
\(H''\le-400/9+(400/7)(3/4)=-100/63\), proving (17). \(\square\)

The certificate for (19) uses only rational arithmetic. For a rational \(x\), scale it by a power of two into \([1,2]\), and use
\[
\log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R,
\quad z=\frac{x-1}{x+1},\quad
0\le R\le\frac{2z^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)(1-z^2)}.
\]
Both the scaled logarithm and \(\log2\) have \(z\le1/3\). Twelve terms bound every squared logarithm in (19), giving a rational upper bound approximately \(0.474383619454<19/40\). `computations/C024/strong_certificate.py` verifies the exact inequalities; `strong_certificate.json` records all rational endpoints. The same certificate verifies \(\log(657/10000)>-3\), sufficient for the convexity used above.

## 5. Explicit dense example, computation, and limits

Take
\[
U=\frac13\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\2&-2&1\\2&1&-2\end{pmatrix},
\quad\varepsilon=\frac1{1024},
\]
with endpoint spectra
\[
(1/4,\varepsilon,3\varepsilon/2),
\qquad (3/4,3\varepsilon/2,2\varepsilon).
\tag{21}
\]
Condition (13) follows, for example, from \(\log2<3/4\), which implies \(B_3<8\). Every off-diagonal entry of both kernels is positive. The direction has eigenvalues \((1/2,\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)\), so it has rank three. The three eigenvalues of each endpoint are distinct, and its diagonal entries are unequal. In particular this example does not use an exchangeable eigenbasis or a two-coordinate variation. The theorem proves the entire segment, not a finite grid.

Reproducible files are `computations/C024/verify.py` and `results.json`. On 48 random complex commuting examples in dimensions two through five, the maximum absolute error in (9) was \(1.94\cdot10^{-12}\); first-Fisher saturation (5) had error below \(1.56\cdot10^{-15}\). The two-site sharpness identity had error below \(1.61\cdot10^{-15}\). Roundoff produced a minimum Cauchy slack of \(-3.11\cdot10^{-15}\).

A 201-point grid for (21) gave maximum actual \(H''\) about \(-0.991876\); even the universal spectral certificate was at most \(-0.900159\) on that grid. These are numerical checks of formulas. The proof of (15) is the uniform analytic estimate above.

The exploratory `explore.py` and `explore.json` additionally show why a two-site relative constant should not be generalized without proof: sampled ratios \(-\operatorname{Tr}Y\log\Delta\rho/g_M\) on the saturated locus reached approximately \(0.278,0.321,0.398,0.417,0.469\) in dimensions two through six, respectively. They do not establish optimal constants or asymptotics.

**Remaining obligations.** The general contraction bound (8) holds without commutativity, but (9) and the spectral corridor use \([K,A]=0\). A tractable covariance formula for \(g_\rho(Y,Y)\) in arbitrary directions, or a universal way to absorb (8) into first-order measured Fisher information, is still missing. The sufficient corridor constant is not claimed optimal. These results control the total acceleration pairing; they do not assert positivity of C007's individual coherent cross-dissipation terms.
