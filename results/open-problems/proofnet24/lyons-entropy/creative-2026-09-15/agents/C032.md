## 1. Results

Write
\[
h(r)=-r\log r-(1-r)\log(1-r),
\qquad
v(r)=r(1-r)\log^2\frac{1-r}{r}.
\]
Let \(z_*>0\) be the unique solution of \(z_*\tanh z_*=1\), and define
\[
\boxed{v_*:=z_*^2-1
=\max_{0\le r\le1}v(r)
=0.4392288398906451507\ldots .}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(1/v_*=2.2767175312280725973\ldots\).

**Theorem A — sharp measured binary entropy power.** On \(n\) binary coordinates, fix any product reference \(\mu=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\). Let \(R_i\) replace coordinate \(i\) by a fresh sample from its reference marginal, and set
\[
T_\eta=\prod_{i=1}^n[\eta I+(1-\eta)R_i].
\]
For every input probability law \(p\), the function
\[
\boxed{\eta\longmapsto
\exp\!\left(\frac{H(T_\eta p)}{n v_*}\right)}
\tag{2}
\]
is concave on \([0,1]\). The coefficient \(1/(n v_*)\) is the **largest possible universal exponential coefficient**. The input need not be determinantal or negatively dependent.

In particular, for every Hermitian positive contraction \(K\) and every diagonal positive contraction \(D\),
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{(1-\eta)D+\eta K})
\ge n v_*\log\!\left[
(1-\eta)e^{H(P_D)/(n v_*)}
+\eta e^{H(P_K)/(n v_*)}
\right].}
\tag{3}
\]
The right side is at least the ordinary Shannon chord, strictly greater when the endpoint entropies differ and \(0<\eta<1\). This proves a genuine stronger conclusion than C006. The entropy-power function is concave on the **whole physical interval of any affine line through a diagonal contraction**, not only between that diagonal point and one chosen endpoint.

**Theorem B — sharp reciprocal measured Stam inequality.** Take the fair reference \(\mu=\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)^{\otimes n}\), and define
\[
\mathcal I(p)=\sum_i\left[D(p\Vert R_i p)+D(R_i p\Vert p)\right].
\]
For positive nonuniform \(p\) and \(0<\theta\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{\mathcal I(T_\theta p)}
\ge \frac1{\theta\mathcal I(p)}
+\frac{\theta^{-1}-1}{n v_*}.}
\tag{4}
\]
Its differential form is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal I(T_{e^{-\tau}}p)
\le-\mathcal I(T_{e^{-\tau}}p)
-\frac{\mathcal I(T_{e^{-\tau}}p)^2}{n v_*}.}
\tag{5}
\]
The constant in the quadratic term is optimal. Equation (5), with \(\eta=e^{-\tau}\), converts exactly into entropy-power concavity in the affine covariance parameter. In contrast, the weaker decay \(\mathcal I'\le-\mathcal I\) supplies only Shannon concavity.

**Theorem C — quantum comparison.** For gauge-invariant fermionic Gaussian states,
\[
S(\rho_K)=\operatorname{tr}h(K).
\]
Both functions
\[
\boxed{K\longmapsto e^{\operatorname{tr}h(K)/(n v_*)}},
\qquad
\boxed{K\longmapsto h^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}h(K)}n\right)}
\tag{6}
\]
are concave on the full Hermitian contraction set, where \(h^{-1}\) is the branch taking values in \([0,1/2]\). The exponential coefficient is sharp. No assertion that the second function remains concave after replacing quantum entropy by occupation entropy is made.

**Exact obstruction.** Even for two modes, the natural first-Fisher inequality
\[
(H'(P_{K+tA})|_{t=0})^2\le n v_*\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
\tag{7}
\]
is false. Section 6 gives a rational physical \((K,A)\) for which the ratio is
\[
\frac{(H')^2}{g_M}=\frac{51}{400}\log^2 17
>\frac{68}{75}>2v_*.
\tag{8}
\]
The actual sharp entropy power still has negative second derivative at this example. Thus (8) obstructs a proposed proof step, not the entropy-power conjecture or Lyons concavity.

## 2. The sharp scalar constant

For \(0<r\le1/2\), put \(z=\tfrac12\log((1-r)/r)\). Then
\[
v(r)=z^2\operatorname{sech}^2z.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d}{dz}[z^2\operatorname{sech}^2z]
=2z\operatorname{sech}^2z\,[1-z\tanh z].
\]
The function \(z\tanh z\) is strictly increasing from zero to infinity, so there is exactly one maximizer. At that point \(\tanh z_*=1/z_*\), and the maximum is \(z_*^2-1\), proving (1). Symmetry handles \(r\ge1/2\). The maximizing smaller probability is
\[
r_*=(1-1/z_*)/2=0.08322172019951765078\ldots .
\tag{9}
\]
This is the maximal varentropy of a single Bernoulli variable. In particular,
\[
\frac{d^2}{dr^2}e^{\alpha h(r)}
=\alpha e^{\alpha h(r)}
\left[-\frac1{r(1-r)}+
\alpha\log^2\frac{1-r}{r}\right]
\]
is nonpositive for all \(r\) exactly when \(\alpha\le1/v_*\).

An elementary rational enclosure needed later is
\[
v_*<\frac{11}{25}.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed \(e^{12/5}>11\), certified by summing its terms through degree ten. This implies \((6/5)\tanh(6/5)>1\), hence \(z_*<6/5\) and (10). The exact positive difference between that partial sum and 11 is recorded in `results.json`.

## 3. A measured conditional-score inequality

Assume temporarily that \(p\) and all reference marginals are positive. For a configuration \(y\) of all coordinates other than \(i\), write
\[
r_i(y)=p(X_i=1\mid X_{-i}=y),
\qquad p_{-i}(y)=p(X_{-i}=y).
\]
Define
\[
\chi_i(p):=\sum_x\frac{(p(x)-R_i p(x))^2}{p(x)},
\quad
 a_i(p):=\sum_x[p(x)-R_i p(x)]\log p(x).
\]
Summing over the two possible values of \(X_i\) gives the exact expressions
\[
\chi_i(p)=\mathbb E_{p_{-i}}
\frac{(r_i-d_i)^2}{r_i(1-r_i)},
\qquad
a_i(p)=\mathbb E_{p_{-i}}(r_i-d_i)
\log\frac{r_i}{1-r_i}.
\tag{11}
\]
The terms \(\log p_{-i}\) cancel in the second formula.

Set
\[
\chi(p)=\sum_i\chi_i(p),\qquad
B(p)=\sum_i\mathbb E_{p_{-i}}v(r_i).
\]
One Cauchy--Schwarz inequality over the disjoint union of all pairs \((i,y)\), with weights \(p_{-i}(y)\), proves
\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_i a_i(p)\right)^2
\le B(p)\chi(p)\le n v_*\chi(p).}
\tag{12}
\]
This is the new finite-mode estimate. It uses the varentropy of **binary conditional distributions**, each bounded by the sharp one-bit constant. It does not assert that the varentropy of the full \(2^n\)-atom law is at most \(n v_*\); Section 6 disproves that assertion for a two-mode DPP.

Along \(p_\eta=T_\eta p_0\), the semigroup identity \(T_\eta T_\theta=T_{\eta\theta}\) gives
\[
\eta p_\eta'=\sum_i(p_\eta-R_i p_\eta).
\]
Consequently
\[
\eta H'(p_\eta)=-\sum_i a_i(p_\eta).
\tag{13}
\]
Combining (12)--(13) yields
\[
\boxed{\eta^2(H')^2\le B(p_\eta)\chi(p_\eta)
\le n v_*\chi(p_\eta).}
\tag{14}
\]
The Fisher quantity here is the sum of coordinate-replacement Pearson terms. It is not the first-order Fisher information of the entire parameterized law.

## 4. Curvature and the affine parameter conversion

For self-containedness, the needed C006 curvature identity is derived here and attributed to C006. Let \(J(p,q)=D(p\Vert q)+D(q\Vert p)\), and let
\[
C_{j,\theta}=\theta I+(1-\theta)R_j,
\quad
\mathscr E_j(p,q)=-\left.\frac{d}{ds}
J(C_{j,e^{-s}}p,C_{j,e^{-s}}q)\right|_{s=0}.
\tag{15}
\]
Data processing for finite probability distributions gives \(\mathscr E_j(p,q)\ge0\). It follows from the log-sum inequality and applies because both arguments undergo the same stochastic map.

Put \(\tau=-\log\eta\), \(F(\tau)=D(p_\eta\Vert\mu)\). Since \(\log((R_i p)/\mu)\) is independent of coordinate \(i\), its pairing with \(p-R_i p\) is zero. Therefore
\[
F'=-\mathcal I,\qquad
\mathcal I=\sum_iJ(p_\eta,R_i p_\eta).
\tag{16}
\]
The maps commute. Differentiating every summand of \(\mathcal I\) under each coordinate generator gives
\[
\mathcal I'=-\sum_{i,j}\mathscr E_j(p_\eta,R_i p_\eta).
\]
For \(j=i\), the second argument \(R_i p\) stays fixed under its own replacement, and direct differentiation gives
\[
\mathscr E_i(p,R_i p)=J(p,R_i p)+\chi_i(p).
\]
Hence
\[
F''+F'=\chi(p_\eta)+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p_\eta,R_i p_\eta).
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, \(\log\mu\) is additive in the coordinates, and every one-coordinate marginal of \(p_\eta\) is affine in \(\eta\). Its cross-entropy against \(\mu\) is therefore affine. This proves the exact measured identity
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2H''(p_\eta)=\chi(p_\eta)
+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p_\eta,R_i p_\eta).}
\tag{18}
\]

Now let \(c=n v_*\) and \(E(\eta)=e^{H(p_\eta)/c}\). Equations (14) and (18) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{c\eta^2E''(\eta)}{E(\eta)}
&=\chi+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j
-\frac{\eta^2(H')^2}{c}\\
&\ge\left(1-\frac{B(p_\eta)}{c}\right)\chi
+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j
\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
This proves Theorem A for positive data. Approximate arbitrary input and reference laws by positive ones. The channel and finite entropy are continuous, including at zero probabilities, so (2) holds without positivity assumptions.

### Strictness for coherent inputs

When all reference marginals are positive and \(p_\eta\) is positive and nonproduct, the cross sum in (18)--(19) is strictly positive. Here is an elementary justification, avoiding any unproved strict data-processing assertion. For a finite Markov generator with source-to-destination rates \(Q_{xy}\), and positive \(p,q\), let \(r_x=p_x/q_x\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
-\left.\frac d{ds}D(p_s\Vert q_s)\right|_{s=0}
=\sum_{x\ne y}q_xQ_{xy}
\left[r_x\log\frac{r_x}{r_y}-r_x+r_y\right].
\tag{20}
\]
Each bracket is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when \(r_x=r_y\). Under replacement of coordinate \(j\), positive rates connect its two values in every fiber. For \(q=R_i p\), the ratio \(p/q\) is \(p(X_i\mid X_{-i})/\mu_i(X_i)\). Thus \(\mathscr E_j(p,R_i p)=0\) for \(i\ne j\) forces the conditional law of coordinate \(i\) to be independent of coordinate \(j\), with all other coordinates fixed. If every cross term vanishes, every single-coordinate conditional law is independent of all other coordinates, which forces the full positive law to be product. This proves the strictness claim.

For a strict DPP kernel, a nonzero off-diagonal entry gives occupation covariance \(-|K_{ij}|^2<0\), so its law is nonproduct. Therefore the sharp entropy power has **strictly negative second derivative** at every strict coherent point of a diagonal-reference line. No equality classification for every diagonal product path is needed for this conclusion.

### The reciprocal Stam form

For the uniform reference, cross-entropy is constant, and (16) gives \(dH/d\tau=\mathcal I\). Equations (18)--(19) imply (5). If \(J=1/\mathcal I\), then
\[
J'=-\mathcal I'/\mathcal I^2\ge J+1/c.
\]
Integrating this scalar inequality for a duration \(s=-\log\theta\) gives
\[
J(s)\ge e^sJ(0)+(e^s-1)/c,
\]
which is (4). Uniform input has \(\mathcal I=0\) and can be handled separately. Initial zero atoms can be treated by positive approximation or by beginning the flow at positive time.

The parameter conversion is explicit:
\[
\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}e^{H(p_\eta)/c}
=\frac{e^{H/c}}{c\eta^2}
\left[\mathcal I'+\mathcal I+\frac{\mathcal I^2}{c}\right],
\tag{21}
\]
where the prime on \(\mathcal I\) is in semigroup time \(\tau\). The linear \(+\mathcal I\) term is essential; it is the acceleration of the change \(\eta=e^{-\tau}\).

### Optimality

Take iid Bernoulli input with \(K=I\), diagonal reference \(D=0\), and \(K_\eta=\eta I\). Then \(H(P_{K_\eta})=nh(\eta)\). Section 2 proves that any exponential coefficient larger than \(1/(n v_*)\) gives a positive second derivative at \(\eta=r_*\). Thus the theorem's coefficient cannot be increased even in the diagonal subclass.

The differential coefficient in (5) is sharp even with the fair reference. At the positive iid law \(p=\operatorname{Bern}(r_*)^{\otimes n}\), all cross terms vanish and
\[
\chi=n\frac{(r_*-1/2)^2}{r_*(1-r_*)},
\quad
\mathcal I=n(r_*-1/2)\log\frac{r_*}{1-r_*},
\quad
\chi=\frac{\mathcal I^2}{n v_*}.
\]
Thus equality holds in (5) at that state. This is sharpness of the differential constant; it is not a claim of finite-time equality in (4) along a nonconstant interval.

## 5. Determinantal realization and an explicit stronger bound

Let \(X\sim P_K\). Retain each bit with probability \(\eta\), and otherwise replace it independently by \(\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\). For every coordinate set \(S\),
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}Y_i
=\sum_{J\subseteq S}\eta^{|J|}(1-\eta)^{|S\setminus J|}
\det K_J\prod_{i\in S\setminus J}d_i
=\det(\eta K_S+(1-\eta)D_S).
\tag{22}
\]
The last equality is the determinant expansion selecting diagonal columns from \(D\). Inclusion probabilities determine the full law, proving
\[
P_{D+\eta(K-D)}=T_\eta P_K.
\tag{23}
\]
This exact measured intertwining, established in C006, is what permits use of the conditional-score bound; an unmeasured quantum entropy inequality by itself does not do so.

For the whole physical affine line \(K_\eta=D+\eta A\), one has locally at **any** physical point
\[
T_\theta P_{K_\eta}=P_{K_{\theta\eta}},\qquad0\le\theta\le1.
\]
Thus (13)--(19) apply at every nonzero parameter where the kernel is strict, including negative parameters. At zero the entropy power is smooth if \(D\) is strict. This gives concavity on the full physical interval. For nonstrict \(D\), apply the argument to \((1-2\varepsilon)K_\eta+\varepsilon I\) and pass to the limit. This full-line Shannon scope belongs to C006; the conclusion here is the sharp entropy-power strengthening.

An exact four-mode illustration uses
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(1/5,2/5,3/5,4/5),
\]
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
2/5&1/10&i/20&1/30\\
1/10&3/5&1/40&(1+i)/50\\
-i/20&1/40&9/20&-1/25\\
1/30&(1-i)/50&-1/25&11/20
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
\]
Both \(K\) and \(I-K\) are strictly diagonally dominant. Rational upper bounds for the four off-diagonal row sums are \(11/60,33/200,23/200,17/150\), all smaller than both relevant diagonal entries. Hence \(K\) is a strict contraction. It is dense, complex, and does not commute with \(D\).

At the midpoint, the numerical values are
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_D)&=2.3468281810948886\ldots,\\
H(P_K)&=2.7213390730652659\ldots,\\
H(P_{(D+K)/2})&=2.6264230048962074\ldots .
\end{aligned}
\]
The Shannon chord is \(2.5340836270800773\ldots\), while the rigorously valid sharp-power formula (3) gives \(2.5440438152559207\ldots\), an improvement of \(0.0099601881758434\ldots\). The decimal evaluations illustrate the proved formula; the verifier additionally checks all sixteen atom-polynomial identities in (23) exactly over the Gaussian rationals.

## 6. Exact obstruction to the first measured-Fisher shortcut

Take the rational strict kernel and direction
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}9/10&1/5\\1/5&1/10\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-3/2\\-3/2&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{25}
\]
Both \(\det K\) and \(\det(I-K)\) equal \(1/20\), and all relevant diagonal entries are positive, so the affine line is physical on a nontrivial interval around zero. In the order \(00,10,01,11\), exact determinant differentiation gives
\[
p=(1,17,1,1)/20,\quad
p'=(-1,3,-1,-1),\quad
p''=(-25,25,25,-25)/2.
\tag{26}
\]
Consequently
\[
g_M=\sum\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}=\frac{1200}{17},
\qquad H'=-3\log17,
\]
\[
\boxed{\frac{(H')^2}{g_M}
=\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p)
=\frac{51}{400}\log^217.}
\tag{27}
\]
The first equality with varentropy holds because the score \(p'/p\) is proportional to the centered \(\log p\): both take one value on the heavy atom and another common value on the three light atoms.

The failure of (7) has a short rational proof. The identity
\(\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}(1/3)^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\) gives \(\log2>2/3\). Thus
\[
\frac{51}{400}\log^217
>\frac{51}{400}(4\log2)^2
>\frac{68}{75}
>\frac{22}{25}>2v_*,
\tag{28}
\]
where the final inequality uses (10). Numerically the ratio in (27) is \(1.0234549762496213\ldots\), while \(2v_*=0.8784576797812903\ldots\).

The actual entropy Hessian is
\[
H''=-\frac{1200}{17}-\frac{25}{2}\log17.
\tag{29}
\]
Here the acceleration pairing helps concavity. Indeed the sharp-power curvature bracket \(H''+(H')^2/(2v_*)\) is strictly negative. This also has an elementary rational certificate: \(\log3>13/12\) implies
\[
v_*\ge v(1/10)=\frac9{100}\log^29>\frac{169}{400},
\]
and the degree-five exponential series gives \(e^3>17\), so \(\log17<3\). Together with \(\log17>8/3\),
\[
H''+\frac{(H')^2}{2v_*}
<-\frac{1200}{17}-\frac{100}{3}+\frac{16200}{169}<0.
\tag{30}
\]
This example therefore does not refute any entropy-power concavity claim. It proves that the sharp quantum binary constant cannot be transferred by simply bounding the first measured Fisher term, or by asserting a full-law varentropy bound \(\operatorname{Var}_p(\log p)\le n v_*\). The conditional replacement quantity in (12), and the acceleration terms in (18), are essential.

## 7. A stronger quantum entropy-number theorem, proved independently

For \(0<K<I\), let \(K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^*\), \(\widetilde A=U^*AU\), and \(b(x)=\log(x/(1-x))\). Along the affine covariance direction \(A\),
\[
S'=-\operatorname{tr}A b(K),
\qquad
-S''=G_Q:=\sum_{i,j}b^{[1]}(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)
|\widetilde A_{ij}|^2.
\tag{31}
\]
The diagonal divided difference is \(b'(\lambda_i)=1/[\lambda_i(1-\lambda_i)]\). Every divided difference is positive. Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz on the diagonal entries therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
(S')^2\le V_Q(K)G_Q,
\qquad
V_Q(K)=\sum_i v(\lambda_i)\le n v_*.}
\tag{32}
\]
This proves the first concavity assertion in (6), with sharpness inherited from \(K=rI\). Formula (31) follows by differentiating the spectral trace function \(\operatorname{tr}h(K)\); it includes arbitrary noncommuting directions, not just fixed eigenvectors.

There is a state-dependent refinement. Put
\[
\psi(s)=v(h^{-1}(s)),\qquad0\le s\le\log2.
\]
For \(r=h^{-1}(s)\in(0,1/2)\), set \(\ell=\log((1-r)/r)>0\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
\psi'(s)=(1-2r)\ell-2,
\qquad
\boxed{\psi''(s)=-2-
\frac{1-2r}{r(1-r)\ell}<0.}
\tag{33}
\]
By continuity \(\psi\) is concave on the closed entropy interval. If \(q=h^{-1}(S/n)\), Jensen's inequality gives
\[
V_Q(K)=\sum_i\psi(h(\lambda_i))\le n\psi(S/n)=nv(q).
\tag{34}
\]
Differentiating \(N_Q(K)=h^{-1}(S(K)/n)\) along any affine line yields, when \(0<S<n\log2\),
\[
N_Q''=\frac1{n h'(q)}
\left[S''+\frac{(S')^2}{nv(q)}\right]\le0
\tag{35}
\]
by (32)--(34). The only strict interior kernel with \(S=n\log2\) is \(I/2\). Along a line through it,
\[
N_Q(I/2+tA)=\frac12-
\frac{\|A\|_F}{\sqrt n}|t|+O(|t|^3),
\]
so the one-sided derivative decreases across this possible cusp. Concavity therefore holds along the entire line. Continuity extends it to the boundary contraction set, completing Theorem C.

Occupation measurement satisfies \(H(P_K)=S(\Delta\rho_K)\), while \(S(\rho_K)=\operatorname{tr}h(K)\). Theorems (6) alone do not give the measured assertion. A pointwise larger function need not inherit concavity, and Section 6 shows why substituting the measured first-Fisher metric into (32) is invalid. The proof of Theorem A supplies an independent measured argument instead.
