## 1. The coefficient inequality from polynomial duality

Let

\[
g(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}p_S z^S,\qquad
p_S>0,\quad g(\mathbf1)=1,
\]

be a real-stable multi-affine probability generating polynomial. Put

\[
g^c(z)=z_1\cdots z_n\,g(z_1^{-1},\ldots,z_n^{-1})
=\sum_Sp_S z^{S^c}.
\tag{2}
\]

This is the generating polynomial of the complemented occupation vector. It is real stable: when all \(z_i\) lie in the upper half-plane, all \(1/z_i\) lie in the lower half-plane; a real-coefficient stable polynomial has no zero there, by complex conjugation. The prefactor is nonzero.

The cited Corollary 2.10 states that a nonzero real-stable polynomial with nonnegative coefficients has concave logarithm on the positive orthant. The proof uses stable homogenization and logarithmic concavity of homogeneous hyperbolic polynomials. Apply that fact separately to \(g\) and \(g^c\).

Let \(X\in\{0,1\}^n\) have law \(p\), let \(\mu=\mathbb E X\), and let \(\Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(X)\). Differentiating the coefficient sums at \(\mathbf1\) gives

\[
\nabla^2\log g(\mathbf1)=\Sigma-\operatorname{Diag}(\mu),
\qquad
\nabla^2\log g^c(\mathbf1)=\Sigma-\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf1-\mu).
\tag{3}
\]

The diagonal entries follow from multi-affinity; the off-diagonal entries are the usual inclusion covariances. Adding the two negative-semidefinite matrices yields

\[
\boxed{\quad 0\preceq\Sigma\preceq\tfrac12 I.\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

To turn this into coefficient Fisher information, take any real \(q\) with \(\sum_Sq_S=0\), and define

\[
s(S)=q_S/p_S,\qquad
a_i=\sum_{S\ni i}q_S=\mathbb E[s(X)(X_i-\mu_i)],
\qquad I_p(q)=\mathbb E s^2.
\tag{5}
\]

There is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\quad
I_p(q)-2\|a\|_2^2
=\mathbb E\left[s(X)-2a^T(X-\mu)\right]^2
+4a^T(\tfrac12 I-\Sigma)a\ \ge0.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, the square expands to \(I_p(q)-4\|a\|^2+4a^T\Sigma a\). This proves (1). No interpolation of the coefficients was introduced: \(q\) can be the actual first derivative of any specified smooth law, including the affine-kernel DPP law below.

Both polynomial logarithms are used. Log-concavity of \(g\) alone gives only \(\Sigma\preceq\operatorname{Diag}(\mu)\); complementing supplies the second constraint whose sum has the fixed scalar right side.

## 2. A direct, exact DPP version

For a Hermitian contraction \(0<K<I\), let \(p_S=P_K(X=S)\). Its generating polynomial is

\[
g_K(z)=\det\bigl(I-K+K^{1/2}\operatorname{Diag}(z)K^{1/2}\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]

The determinant is positive for positive real \(z\). Its logarithm is concave there because its matrix argument is affine and positive definite. Equivalently, differentiating at \(\mathbf1\) gives

\[
-\nabla^2\log g_K(\mathbf1)=K\circ\overline K\succeq0,
\tag{8}
\]

where \(\circ\) is the entrywise product. Positivity in (8) also follows directly from the Schur product theorem. Applying the same statement to \(I-K\) proves (4) without using any external stability result:

\[
\Sigma=\operatorname{Diag}(K_{ii})-K\circ\overline K,
\quad
I-2\Sigma=K\circ\overline K+(I-K)\circ\overline{(I-K)}\succeq0.
\tag{9}
\]

Now set \(K(t)=K+tA\), where \(A=A^*\) is arbitrary. Strictness of the base guarantees a two-sided admissible interval around zero. Put

\[
q_S=p'_S(0),\quad r_S=p''_S(0),\quad
\mathcal I_K(A)=\sum_Sq_S^2/p_S,\quad a_i=A_{ii}.
\tag{10}
\]

The DPP first inclusion identity gives exactly

\[
\sum_{S\ni i}q_S=A_{ii},\qquad \sum_{S\ni i}r_S=0.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus (6) becomes the exact affine-kernel Fisher certificate

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I_K(A)-2\|\operatorname{diag} A\|_2^2
={}&\sum_Sp_S\left[\frac{q_S}{p_S}
-2\sum_i A_{ii}(\mathbf1_{i\in S}-K_{ii})\right]^2\\
&+2a^T\left[K\circ\overline K+(I-K)\circ\overline{(I-K)}\right]a
\ \ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The universal coefficient 2 cannot be increased, even for these genuine affine-kernel tangents. Take

\[
K_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2-\varepsilon\\1/2-\varepsilon&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\qquad0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\]

The extreme atoms both have mass \(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2\), and the two singleton atoms both have mass \(1/2-\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\). Their first derivatives are \(0,1,-1,0\). Consequently

\[
\frac{\mathcal I_{K_\varepsilon}(A)}{\|\operatorname{diag}A\|^2}
=\frac1{1/2-\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2}\longrightarrow2.
\tag{13}
\]

This sharpness concerns the universal Fisher step, not sharpness of the Cauchy spacing threshold.

## 3. A full-Hermitian entropy theorem for weak pairwise interactions

**Theorem 1.** Suppose \(0<K<I\) and its frozen log-atom table is pairwise:

\[
-\log p_S=b+\sum_{i\in S}b_i+\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}c_{ij},
\qquad c_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]

Let \(C\) be the real symmetric matrix with zero diagonal and \(C_{ij}=c_{ij}\) for \(i\ne j\). If

\[
\lambda_{\max}(C)\le r\le2,
\tag{15}
\]

then for every complex Hermitian \(A\),

\[
\boxed{\quad
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
\le -(1-r/2)\mathcal I_K(A)
-2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2\le0.
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]

If \(r<2\) and all \(c_{ij}>0\), the full Hessian is negative definite. More explicitly, with \(m=\min_{i<j}c_{ij}\),

\[
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
\le-\min\{2-r,m\}\,\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{17}
\]

For \(n=1\), ordinary Bernoulli entropy supplies the conclusion and no minimum over pairs is used.

**Proof.** The exact Shannon differentiation formula is

\[
H''=-\mathcal I_K(A)-\sum_Sr_S\log p_S.
\tag{18}
\]

Normalization and (11) remove the constant and modular terms in (14). For every pair,

\[
\sum_{S\supseteq\{i,j\}}r_S
=\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\det(K+tA)_{\{i,j\}}\right|_{t=0}
=2\bigl(A_{ii}A_{jj}-|A_{ij}|^2\bigr).
\tag{19}
\]

Therefore the **actual** acceleration contributes

\[
H''=-\mathcal I_K(A)+a^TCa-2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\tag{20}
\]

There is now the exact decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
-H''={}&(1-r/2)\mathcal I_K(A)
+\frac r2\left[\mathcal I_K(A)-2\|a\|^2\right]\\
&+a^T(rI-C)a+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

All terms are nonnegative: use (12), (15), and \(r\ge0\), the last following from the nonnegative symmetric matrix \(C\). Dropping the middle two terms gives (16). Applying (12) once more gives

\[
-H''\ge(2-r)\|a\|^2+2m\sum_{i<j}|A_{ij}|^2,
\]

which proves (17). The argument keeps every diagonal, real coherent, and imaginary coherent direction; it does not restrict \(A\) to a tangent of the pairwise-table family. \(\square\)

A particularly elementary sufficient test is \(\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}\le r\), by the symmetric-matrix row-sum bound. Unlike a total interaction budget, this test does not grow with the number of sites when the interactions decay summably with distance.

## 4. Cauchy odds at fixed spacing, with arbitrary external fields

**Theorem 2.** Let \(n\ge2\), let \(0<x_1<\cdots<x_n\), and assume

\[
x_{i+1}/x_i\ge R\ge6\qquad(1\le i<n).
\tag{22}
\]

For arbitrary \(\ell_i>0\), form

\[
L_{ij}=\sqrt{\ell_i\ell_j}\,\frac{2\sqrt{x_ix_j}}{x_i+x_j},
\qquad K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\tag{23}
\]

Put

\[
c_{ij}=2\log\frac{x_i+x_j}{|x_i-x_j|},\qquad
\alpha_R=1-\frac{4R}{(R-1)^2}>0.
\tag{24}
\]

Then for every complex Hermitian \(A\),

\[
\boxed{\quad
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]
\le-\alpha_R\mathcal I_K(A)
-2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\quad}
\tag{25}
\]

The full Hessian is negative definite. At \(R=6\), \(\alpha_R=1/25\). The formula also applies whenever \(R>3+2\sqrt2\); 6 is a convenient integer threshold, and no optimal-spacing assertion is made.

**Proof.** The matrix \((1/(x_i+x_j))\) is the Gram matrix of the linearly independent functions \(e^{-x_it}\) in \(L^2(0,\infty)\). Hence \(L>0\), so \(0<K<I\).

The Cauchy determinant identity gives

\[
\det L_S=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i
\prod_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}\left(\frac{x_i-x_j}{x_i+x_j}\right)^2.
\tag{26}
\]

For completeness, this identity follows by placing the Cauchy determinant over its common denominator: the numerator alternates in both sets of nodes, so has their two Vandermonde factors; degrees and a one-variable leading coefficient determine the remaining constant. Specializing both node sets to \(x\) and multiplying the row and column normalizations gives (26).

The L-ensemble atom formula \(p_S=\det L_S/\det(I+L)\) now gives exactly (14), with the \(c_{ij}\) in (24). The external fields occur only in the modular coefficients. In particular, they can be arbitrarily unequal and arbitrarily large or small.

If \(j-i=d\), then \(x_j/x_i\ge R^d\), and the function \(u\mapsto2\log((u+1)/(u-1))\) decreases for \(u>1\). Since \(\log(1+v)<v\) for \(v>0\),

\[
0<c_{ij}\le2\log\frac{R^d+1}{R^d-1}<\frac4{R^d-1}.
\tag{27}
\]

There are at most two nodes at each positive index distance from a fixed node. Also \(R^d-1\ge R^{d-1}(R-1)\). Thus

\[
\lambda_{\max}(C)
\le\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ij}
<8\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac1{R^d-1}
\le\frac{8R}{(R-1)^2}=:r_R.
\tag{28}
\]

For \(R\ge6\), \(r_R\le48/25<2\). Apply Theorem 1 with \(r=r_R\). This is (25). All pair coefficients are strictly positive, proving negative definiteness by (17). \(\square\)

This is a pointwise statement about the **entire ambient Hermitian Hessian**. By continuity, each displayed strict base has an open convex neighborhood on which occupation entropy is concave along all affine kernel segments. Neither arbitrary chords between two displayed Cauchy kernels nor concavity in the nonlinear external-field parameters is asserted. No coefficient interpolation, odds interpolation, or arbitrary unitary change of occupation basis substitutes for affine kernel interpolation anywhere in the proof.

The lower bound is about 1.08081424; that decimal is only a display. Positivity in the first line follows from \(m,v>0\). The lower logarithm inequality follows by integrating \(2/(1-s^2)>2\) from zero to \(1/u\). All remaining comparisons in (33)–(34) use rational arithmetic. Thus the old sufficient condition \(F+mv\le1\) does not certify this kernel, whereas (31) proves its full-Hermitian Hessian strictly negative.
