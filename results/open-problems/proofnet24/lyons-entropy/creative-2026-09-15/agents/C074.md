## 1. The full-Hermitian theorem

Let \(0<K<I\) be an \(n\)-site Hermitian kernel, and write
\[
p_S=p_S(K),\qquad f_k=\Pr_K(|X|=k),\qquad w_k={n\choose k}.
\]
Call its law **uniform given the count** when
\[
p_S=\frac{f_{|S|}}{w_{|S|}}\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq[n].
\tag{1}
\]
This is equivalent to all principal minors of a fixed order being equal. Indeed, the inclusion probabilities are sums of the atom probabilities, and Boolean inclusion–exclusion recovers the atoms from the inclusion probabilities. Both operations preserve dependence only on cardinality.

**Theorem 1.** At every strict kernel satisfying (1),
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0\quad\text{for every complex Hermitian }A.}
\tag{2}
\]
If \(K\) is nonscalar, the inequality is strict for every nonzero \(A\). At the remaining scalar bases,
\[
D^2H(aI)[A,A]
=-\frac{1}{a(1-a)}\sum_i A_{ii}^2,
\qquad 0<a<1.
\tag{3}
\]

The theorem is about a symmetry of the **occupation law**, which does not require the kernel itself to be permutation invariant. C075 proves the exchangeable-kernel case \(a(I-P)+cP\). The circulant examples in Section 5 have \(n\) distinct eigenvalues for nonzero coupling; for \(n\ge3\) they cannot be unitarily equivalent to those two-eigenvalue kernels.

The theorem also yields an exact curvature decomposition. For a unitary eigenbasis \(K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)U^*\), put
\[
B=U^*AU,\qquad v_i=B_{ii},\qquad q_k=f_k/w_k.
\]
Let \(\mathcal E_n\) be the weighted count entropy in Section 2, let \(G=\nabla^2\mathcal E_n(\lambda)\), and let
\[
R_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}f_k^{(i,j)}
 \log\frac{q_{k+1}^2}{q_kq_{k+2}},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(f^{(i,j)}\) is the independent Bernoulli-count law after deleting spectral parameters \(\lambda_i,\lambda_j\). Then
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=v^T Gv-2\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}|B_{ij}|^2
-\sum_{k=0}^n\frac1{q_k}
 \sum_{|S|=k}\left(Dp_S[A]-\frac{Df_k[A]}{w_k}\right)^2.}
\tag{5}
\]
For a nonscalar base, \(G\prec0\) and every \(R_{ij}>0\). Thus all Hermitian directions are controlled before even using the final nonpositive term. Formula (5) also holds at repeated eigenvalues by continuity; (4) supplies the continuous divided-difference value.

## 2. The weighted count entropy, with strictness

For \(p=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\in(0,1)^n\), let
\[
f_k(p)=\Pr\!\left(\sum_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)=k\right),
\qquad q_k(p)=\frac{f_k(p)}{{n\choose k}},
\]
with independent Bernoulli variables, and define
\[
\mathcal E_n(p)=-\sum_{k=0}^n f_k(p)\log q_k(p).
\tag{6}
\]
C075 already establishes concavity of (6). The refinement needed here is
\[
\boxed{\nabla^2\mathcal E_n(p)\prec0
\quad\text{if the parameters }p_i\text{ are not all equal}.}
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, fix arbitrary real slopes \(v_i\), and set
\[
g_k=\sum_i v_i f_k^{(i)},\qquad
h_k=\sum_{i\ne j}v_i v_j f_k^{(i,j)}.
\tag{8}
\]
Sequences vanish outside their support. The count generating polynomial gives
\[
f'_k=g_{k-1}-g_k,\qquad
f''_k=h_k-2h_{k-1}+h_{k-2}.
\tag{9}
\]
The exact external local inequality of Hillion–Johnson is
\[
h_k(f_{k+1}^2-f_kf_{k+2})
\le2g_kg_{k+1}f_{k+1}-g_k^2f_{k+2}-g_{k+1}^2f_k,
\quad 0\le k\le n-2.
\tag{10}
\]
The slopes in (10) can have either sign. This is the same precise input used by C075.

Set
\[
Q_k=\frac{g_k^2}{f_k}
-\frac{2g_kg_{k+1}}{f_{k+1}}
+\frac{g_{k+1}^2}{f_{k+2}},
\]
\[
\ell_k=\log\frac{f_{k+1}^2}{f_kf_{k+2}},\qquad
b_k=\log\frac{w_{k+1}^2}{w_kw_{k+2}},\qquad
L_k=\ell_k-b_k=\log\frac{q_{k+1}^2}{q_kq_{k+2}}.
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(Q_k\ge0\), \(\ell_k>0\), \(b_k>0\), and \(L_k\ge0\). The last inequality is Newton's inequality for the positive odds \(p_i/(1-p_i)\). Its equality case says that
\[
L_k>0\text{ for every }k\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}
\quad\text{whenever the }p_i\text{ are not all equal}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is the strict equality characterization of Newton's inequalities for positive variables.

The elementary passage from (10) to the local Shannon bound is useful for keeping the proof explicit. Put
\[
\rho=\frac{\sqrt{f_kf_{k+2}}}{f_{k+1}}\in(0,1),\quad
x=\frac{g_k}{\sqrt{f_k}},\quad y=\frac{g_{k+1}}{\sqrt{f_{k+2}}}.
\]
Then \(Q_k=x^2+y^2-2\rho xy\), and (10) gives
\[
h_k(1-\rho^2)
\le\rho Q_k-\rho(1+\rho)(x-y)^2\le\rho Q_k.
\]
Since \(2\rho\log(1/\rho)\le1-\rho^2\), this implies
\[
Q_k-h_k\ell_k\ge0\quad\text{when }h_k\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
The elementary scalar inequality follows by writing \(\rho=e^{-s}\) and using \(s\le\sinh s\).

Differentiating (6), summing (9) by parts, and expanding the count Fisher information gives the exact identity
\[
-\mathcal E_n''
=\frac{g_0^2}{f_1}+\frac{g_{n-1}^2}{f_{n-1}}
+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(Q_k-h_k L_k).
\tag{14}
\]
For \(n=2\), both boundary denominators happen to equal \(f_1\); their numerators remain separate. The local terms obey
\[
Q_k-h_kL_k\ge
\begin{cases}
h_k b_k,&h_k\ge0,\\
|h_k|L_k,&h_k\le0.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]
For the first case, use \(L_k=\ell_k-b_k\) and (13); for the second, use \(Q_k\ge0\). This proves the previously established concavity of (6).

Now assume the parameters are not all equal. By (12), every nonzero \(h_k\) gives a strictly positive term in (15). Hence equality in (14) can hold only if all \(h_k=0\). In that case \(f''=0\), so equality also forces the count Fisher information to vanish, hence \(f'_k=0\) for every \(k\). Differentiating the mean gives
\[
\sum_i v_i=\sum_k k f'_k=0.
\]
But summing the second sequence in (8) gives
\[
0=\sum_kh_k
=\sum_{i\ne j}v_i v_j
=\left(\sum_i v_i\right)^2-\sum_i v_i^2
=-\sum_i v_i^2.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(v=0\), proving (7). No strictness or equality result from the ordinary Shepp–Olkin theorem has been assumed.

At the completely equal vector \(p_i=a\), \(L_k=0\) for all \(k\), and direct differentiation instead gives
\[
D^2\mathcal E_n(a\mathbf1)[v,v]
=-\frac{(\sum_i v_i)^2}{n a(1-a)}.
\tag{17}
\]
The case \(n=1\) is ordinary strict Bernoulli entropy concavity and does not require (10).

## 3. A concave spectral envelope for measured entropy

For every strict Hermitian contraction define
\[
\mathcal F_n(K)=\mathcal E_n(\lambda_1(K),\ldots,\lambda_n(K)).
\tag{18}
\]
The determinantal count identity
\[
\mathbb E_K z^{|X|}=\det(I-K+zK)
=\prod_i(1-\lambda_i+z\lambda_i)
\tag{19}
\]
identifies the count law with the independent Bernoulli sum in (6).

Here is a direct Hessian proof that (18) is matrix-concave; it also supplies the strict transverse coefficients. Hold all spectral parameters except \(p_i,p_j\) fixed. The count polynomial is affine in the sum \(p_i+p_j\) and their product. Differentiation, or expansion of the two factors, yields
\[
\partial_i\mathcal E_n-\partial_j\mathcal E_n
=(p_j-p_i)\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}f_k^{(i,j)}L_k.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus the spectral divided difference is exactly
\[
\frac{\partial_i\mathcal E_n-\partial_j\mathcal E_n}{p_i-p_j}
=-R_{ij}\le0.
\tag{21}
\]
If the parameter vector is nonconstant, (12) makes every \(R_{ij}>0\), including its value when \(p_i=p_j\).

For a simple-spectrum \(K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)U^*\) and \(B=U^*AU\), differentiating its eigenvalues gives
\[
\lambda_i'=B_{ii},\qquad
\lambda_i''=2\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{|B_{ij}|^2}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
\]
Consequently
\[
D^2\mathcal F_n(K)[A,A]
=v^T Gv-2\sum_{i<j}R_{ij}|B_{ij}|^2.
\tag{22}
\]
The expression extends continuously to repeated eigenvalues using (20). Smoothness can also be seen directly: (18) equals \(-\sum_k f_k(K)\log(f_k(K)/w_k)\), and every \(f_k(K)\) is a positive polynomial coefficient of (19). Equations (7), (21), and (22) prove
\[
D^2\mathcal F_n(K)\preceq0\text{ always},\qquad
D^2\mathcal F_n(K)\prec0\text{ whenever }K\text{ is nonscalar}.
\tag{23}
\]
This strict spectral-envelope statement is global in the strict contraction domain. It is not yet a statement about measured entropy at arbitrary kernels.

The information-theoretic comparison is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_n(K)-H(K)
&=\sum_{k=0}^n f_k\,
D\!\left(\mathcal L_K(X\mid |X|=k)
\middle\|\operatorname{Unif}\{S:|S|=k\}\right)\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Equality holds exactly under (1). At an equality base, differentiate twice. The terms containing second atom derivatives cancel because \(\log p_S=\log q_{|S|}\). What remains is exactly
\[
D^2(\mathcal F_n-H)(K)[A,A]
=\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}
-\sum_k\frac{(Df_k[A])^2}{f_k}
\]
\[
=\sum_k\frac1{q_k}\sum_{|S|=k}
\left(Dp_S[A]-\frac{Df_k[A]}{w_k}\right)^2\ge0.
\tag{25}
\]
Combining (22) and (25) proves (5), hence Theorem 1 and its strictness. Equation (3) follows directly at a product kernel: its frozen log-atom table is modular, and its Fisher information is \(\sum_i A_{ii}^2/[a(1-a)]\).

There is no inference from an upper bound at a point without equality: the touching condition (1) is essential to the Hessian comparison.

## 4. All three-site complex circulants

Every strict Hermitian three-site circulant
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
a&z&\overline z\\
\overline z&a&z\\
z&\overline z&a
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{26}
\]
has a law uniform given the count. Cyclic permutations act transitively on subsets of each size. Equivalently, every one-site principal minor is \(a\) and every two-site principal minor is \(a^2-|z|^2\).

The precise physical domain is
\[
0<a+2\operatorname{Re}(z\omega^j)<1,
\qquad j=0,1,2,\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\tag{27}
\]
Throughout this domain Theorem 1 gives the full nine-real-dimensional Hessian inequality, strictly when \(z\ne0\). This includes arbitrary triangle phase and arbitrary allowed \(a\). Thus it is not limited to the particle–hole locus of C008 or the gauge-exchangeable kernels of C075.

For example, \(a=9/20\), \(z=1/10+4i/25\) gives eigenvalues
\[
\frac{13}{20},\qquad \frac7{20}\pm\frac{4\sqrt3}{25},
\]
all strictly in \((0,1)\). Here \(a\ne1/2\) and the triangle product has nonzero real and imaginary parts. The theorem at this kernel controls every Hermitian perturbation, not only the three-dimensional circulant subspace.

## 5. An explicit strong-coupling circulant family in every dimension

For \(n\ge2\), index coordinates by \(0,\ldots,n-1\) and define
\[
(S_n)_{jk}=\begin{cases}
1,&k>j,\\
0,&k=j,\\
-1,&k<j.
\end{cases}
\qquad
K_n(a,b)=aI+i bS_n,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R.
\tag{28}
\]
Every principal restriction on \(m\) coordinates, listed in increasing order, is exactly \(aI_m+i bS_m\). Therefore the condition of Theorem 1 holds in every dimension.

These are genuinely circulant kernels after an allowed diagonal gauge. Put
\[
D_{jj}=e^{\pi i j/n},\qquad C_n(a,b)=D^*K_n(a,b)D.
\tag{29}
\]
The first row of \(C_n\) is
\[
\boxed{c_0=a,\qquad c_d=i b e^{\pi i d/n},\quad 1\le d\le n-1.}
\tag{30}
\]
One has \(c_{n-d}=\overline{c_d}\), and \((C_n)_{jk}=c_{k-j\pmod n}\), proving that it is Hermitian circulant. Diagonal gauge leaves every determinantal atom unchanged and transports the full Hermitian tangent space bijectively.

For completeness, its Fourier spectrum can be derived without invoking a table of skew-matrix eigenvalues. If \(z^n=-1\) and \(w_j=z^j\), summing the geometric series gives
\[
S_nw=\frac{1+z}{1-z}w.
\]
For \(z=e^{(2k+1)\pi i/n}\), this yields
\[
\boxed{\lambda_k=a-b\cot\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2n},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.}
\tag{31}
\]
Under (29), these eigenvectors become the ordinary discrete Fourier vectors. The cotangents in (31) are all distinct and their extreme values are \(\pm c_n\), where \(c_n=\cot(\pi/(2n))\).

It follows that the **entire** strict physical domain is
\[
\boxed{0<a<1,\qquad |b|\cot\frac\pi{2n}<\min(a,1-a).}
\tag{32}
\]
On all of (32), the full Hermitian entropy Hessian is nonpositive. For every \(b\ne0\), it is negative definite. This includes arbitrarily close approaches to either spectral boundary from the interior; no finite boundary Hessian is asserted.

There is also an explicit single-atom formula. For \(m\ge0\),
\[
\det(aI_m+i bS_m)
=\frac{(a+i b)^m+(a-i b)^m}{2}
=\operatorname{Re}(a+i b)^m.
\tag{33}
\]
For \(b\ne0\), both sides are monic degree-\(m\) polynomials in \(a\) with roots \(b\cot((2k+1)\pi/(2m))\), by the preceding eigenvector calculation. This proves the identity; \(b=0\) and \(m=0\) follow immediately. Inclusion–exclusion now gives, for \(|S|=k\),
\[
\boxed{p_S(K_n(a,b))
=\operatorname{Re}\left[(a+i b)^k(1-a-i b)^{n-k}\right].}
\tag{34}
\]
Indeed, summing (33) with coefficients \((-1)^j{n-k\choose j}\) gives (34). Positivity also follows from (32): the arguments of \(a+i b\) and \(1-a-i b\) each have magnitude less than \(\pi/(2n)\), so the argument of the displayed product lies strictly between \(-\pi/2\) and \(\pi/2\).

At \(n=4\), the analytic parameter interval is
\[
a=\frac12,\qquad |b|<\frac1{2(1+\sqrt2)}.
\tag{35}
\]
Its exact rational point \(b=1/5\) has eigenvalues
\[
\frac12\pm\frac{1+\sqrt2}{5},\qquad
\frac12\pm\frac{\sqrt2-1}{5}.
\tag{36}
\]
The extremes are approximately \(0.0171573\) and \(0.9828427\); this is about \(96.57\%\) of the coupling allowed in (35). Its complete exact atom table is

| Subset size | Number of subsets | Probability of each subset |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 | \(41/10000\) |
| 1 | 4 | \(609/10000\) |
| 2 | 6 | \(841/10000\) |
| 3 | 4 | \(609/10000\) |
| 4 | 1 | \(41/10000\) |

This point is illustrative; the proof covers the whole interval (35) and the full domain (32) for every \(n\).

## 6. Fourier tangent representations and exact scope

At \(C_n(a,b)\) with \(b\ne0\), take \(U\) to be the discrete Fourier matrix in (5). A Hermitian tangent \(A\) becomes \(B=U^*AU\).

- The real diagonal entries of \(B\) are the \(n\) circulant tangent coordinates. Their complete quadratic contribution from the spectral envelope is \(v^TGv<0\) for nonzero \(v\).
- Every off-diagonal Fourier coordinate \(B_{ij}\) contributes the explicit negative term \(-2R_{ij}|B_{ij}|^2\), with \(R_{ij}>0\). These are the symmetry-breaking directions.
- The last term of (5) is a further nonnegative Fisher-information loss with a minus sign; it cannot spoil the conclusion.

More explicitly, cyclic conjugation acts on the Fourier matrix entry \(B_{ij}\) through the character indexed by \(i-j\pmod n\). Distinct character pairs are orthogonal for every cyclically invariant real quadratic form, including the Fisher loss in (5). The real isotypic blocks have dimensions \(n\) for character zero, \(2n\) for each pair \(q,-q\) with \(q\ne -q\), and, when \(n\) is even, \(n\) for \(q=n/2\). Their dimensions sum to \(n^2\). Formula (5) controls every block without discarding any imaginary or symmetry-breaking coordinate.

The new scope is the full ambient Hessian at the displayed nonexchangeable bases and, more generally, at every strict count-uniform determinantal law. Concavity on individual affine lines through a diagonal kernel was already proved by C006; no new-scope claim is made for that narrower consequence. The all-three-site-circulant corollary overlaps C008 on its particle–hole sublocus and C075 on its gauge-exchangeable sublocus.

On any convex affine subfamily that remains count-uniform, ordinary entropy concavity also follows by integration and continuity to feasible endpoints. The pointwise theorem does **not** establish entropy concavity along an arbitrary segment merely because both endpoints are count-uniform: its interior kernels need not satisfy (1). Arbitrary circulants in dimensions \(n\ge4\) need not satisfy (1), and they are not all covered here.
