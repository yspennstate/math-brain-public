# C001 — a constructive three-history trial and a new full-dimensional three-site region

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: a bounded creative proof development is complete.

The universal three-site inequality is **not** proved. This report proves a new explicit full-dimensional complex region, including kernels arbitrarily close to a singular boundary. The proof uses only three of the seven conditional Fisher features. It also proves a longer one-parameter subfamily and explains analytically why the older three-occupation-indicator certificate eventually fails there. No numerical claim is used as a proof.

Source read: `TARGET.md` and `sources/old-097.md`. The conditional Fisher identity, the exact seven-feature criterion, and its relation to the entropy Hessian are prior results from that source. The construction and bounds below are the present contribution; no historical novelty claim is made.

## 1. The new region and theorem

Let
\[
L=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_1},\sqrt{\ell_2},\sqrt{\ell_3})
R\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_1},\sqrt{\ell_2},\sqrt{\ell_3}),
\]
where, after a diagonal unitary gauge change,
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\sqrt{1-t}&\gamma\sqrt t\,e^{-i\phi}\\
\sqrt{1-t}&1&\beta\sqrt t\\
\gamma\sqrt t\,e^{i\phi}&\beta\sqrt t&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume
\[
\boxed{
0<t\le10^{-4},\qquad
\frac9{10}\le\ell_i\le\frac{11}{10},\qquad
\frac{49}{100}\le\beta,\gamma\le\frac12,\qquad
|\cos\phi|\le\frac1{100}.}
\tag{2}
\]
Put \(K=L(I+L)^{-1}\), and form \(M,\delta,b,C\) as in source old-097, with reveal order \(1,2,3\). Then
\[
\boxed{b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b\ge2+\frac1{25\log(1/t)}.}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently every nonzero Hermitian direction \(A\) has strictly negative entropy curvature. More quantitatively,
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le
-\frac{\det M}{25\log(1/t)}
\|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2.}
\tag{4}
\]
The family has nonempty interior in all nine real coordinates of \(\operatorname{Herm}_3\): its parameters are three positive diagonals, \(t\), two small-edge amplitudes, the cycle phase, and two gauge phases. Taking all interval inequalities strictly gives an open set. Every allowed cycle is nonreal. Its strong normalized edge has magnitude at least \(\sqrt{1-10^{-4}}\), so this region is disjoint from the earlier region with all three normalized edge magnitudes between \(0.10\) and \(0.11\). Adjoining the present region strictly enlarges that earlier proved domain.

This is a pointwise Hessian theorem. Entropy is concave on every convex subset of the region; convexity of the entire parameter region or concavity along segments leaving it is not claimed.

## 2. A general explicit three-feature trial

For any connected interior three-site base with \(M>0\), select the following three histories from the seven-feature representation:

1. The first-site feature, \(w_1=e_1\).
2. The second-site feature after site 1 was included.
3. The third-site feature after site 1 was included and site 2 was excluded.

Write their weights as \(\omega_j\), and put
\[
W=(w_1,w_2,w_3),\quad H=W^*MW,\quad b_j=H_{jj},\quad\delta=\det M,
\]
\[
\gamma_{ij}=\frac{|H_{ij}|^2}{b_i b_j},\qquad
\kappa_j=\frac{\delta}{\omega_j b_j^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(W\) is upper triangular with diagonal entries 1, so
\[
\det H=\delta,\qquad \delta\le b_1b_2b_3.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
E=\kappa_1+\kappa_3+2\gamma_{13},\qquad
R_*=1-\gamma_{12}-\gamma_{23}.
\tag{7}
\]
An explicit trial vector in the original seven-dimensional variational problem is zero at the other four histories and is
\[
\boxed{
r_1=\frac1{b_1},\qquad
r_2=\frac{R_*}{(1+\kappa_2)b_2},\qquad
r_3=\frac1{b_3}.}
\tag{8}
\]
Its value is exactly
\[
\boxed{
2b^{\mathsf T}r-r^{\mathsf T}Cr
=2-E+\frac{R_*^2}{1+\kappa_2}.}
\tag{9}
\]
To check this, put \(x_j=b_jr_j\). The selected normalized quadratic matrix has diagonal \(1+\kappa_j\) and off-diagonal \(\gamma_{ij}\). At \(x=(1,s,1)\), the objective is
\[
2-E+2s(1-\gamma_{12}-\gamma_{23})-(1+\kappa_2)s^2.
\]
Complete the square in \(s\). This gives (8)–(9), with no sign restriction on \(R_*\).

Thus the following general condition is a proved, explicit sufficient certificate:
\[
\boxed{E(1+\kappa_2)\le R_*^2.}
\tag{10}
\]
Unlike the exact seven-by-seven inverse criterion, this needs no linear solve. The rest of the report proves (10), with a quantitative margin, throughout (2).

## 3. Logarithmic coefficients and positivity

Write \(a=\sqrt{1-t}\), \(u=\log(1/t)\), and
\[
h=1-\beta^2-\gamma^2+2a\beta\gamma\cos\phi.
\]
Then
\[
\det R=t h,\qquad \frac{99}{200}\le h\le\frac{328}{625}.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, each squared small-edge amplitude lies between \(2401/10000\) and \(1/4\), while the absolute cycle contribution is at most \(1/200\).

The first two leading principal minors of \(R\) are \(1\) and \(t\), and its determinant is positive by (11). Sylvester's criterion proves \(L>0\), hence \(0<K<I\).

The complementary pair coefficients and triple coefficient are
\[
d_1=-\log(1-\beta^2t),\quad d_2=-\log(1-\gamma^2t),\quad d_3=u,
\]
\[
\tau=\log\frac{(1-\beta^2t)(1-\gamma^2t)}{h},\qquad
M=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,u)+\tau K.
\tag{12}
\]
Elementary integral logarithm bounds give
\[
\boxed{
\frac6{25}t\le d_1,d_2\le\frac{13}{50}t,
\qquad \frac35\le\tau\le\frac34.}
\tag{13}
\]
For the pair bounds use \(x\le-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\). For the lower triple bound, the inequality
\(\log x\ge2(x-1)/(x+1)\), \(x\ge1\), gives
\[
\tau\ge\frac{594}{953}-\frac{2t}{3}>\frac35.
\]
For the upper bound, \(\tau\le\log(200/99)<3/4\), since
\[
e^{3/4}\ge1+\frac34+\frac9{32}+\frac9{128}
=\frac{269}{128}>\frac{200}{99}.
\]
In particular \(M>0\).

We shall also use
\[
u>8,\qquad t u^2<\frac1{100}.
\tag{14}
\]
The first follows from \(e<3\), so \(e^8<6561<10000\). For the second, \(t\log^2(1/t)\) increases on \((0,10^{-4}]\), and \(\log10000<10\). The latter is equivalent to \(\log100<5\), which follows, for example, by summing the first seven nonnegative terms of the series for \(e^5\).

## 4. Conditional probabilities and explicit vectors

Let \(Z=\det(I+L)\), and set
\[
B_0=1+\ell_3(1-\gamma^2t),\qquad
N=\ell_2t(1+\ell_3h).
\]
The eight unnormalized atoms are
\[
1,\quad \ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3,\quad
\ell_1\ell_2t,\quad
\ell_1\ell_3(1-\gamma^2t),\quad
\ell_2\ell_3(1-\beta^2t),\quad
\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3 t h.
\tag{15}
\]
Their sum is \(Z<7\). The bounds in (2) imply
\[
\frac{15}{8}\le B_0\le\frac{21}{10},\qquad
\frac9{10}t\le N\le\frac74t,\qquad B_0+N\le\frac94.
\tag{16}
\]
The selected history probabilities are
\[
p=K_{11}=\frac{\ell_1(B_0+N)}Z,
\quad q_2=P(2\in X\mid1\in X)=\frac{N}{B_0+N},
\]
\[
\pi=P(1\in X,2\notin X)=\frac{\ell_1B_0}Z,
\quad q_3=P(3\in X\mid1\in X,2\notin X)
=\frac{\ell_3(1-\gamma^2t)}{B_0}.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\frac15\le p\le\frac35,\quad
\frac25t\le q_2\le t,\quad
\pi\ge\frac15,\quad q_3\le\frac35.}
\tag{18}
\]
For the upper bound on \(p\), the diagonal Schur-complement bound
\((I+L)^{-1}_{11}\ge(1+\ell_1)^{-1}\) gives
\(p\le\ell_1/(1+\ell_1)\le11/21<3/5\). All other assertions follow directly from (16)–(17).

The selected vectors are
\[
w_1=e_1,\qquad w_2=(-\alpha,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\quad
\alpha=K_{12}/K_{11},\qquad w_3=(v_1,v_2,1)^{\mathsf T},
\]
\[
\boxed{
v_1=-\frac{L_{13}}{\ell_1B_0},\qquad
v_2=\frac{L_{23}-L_{21}L_{13}/\ell_1}{B_0}.}
\tag{19}
\]
One derivation of (19) is to put \(Q=K-I_{\{2\}}\). The defining equations are \(Qw_3=q_3e_3\). Multiplication by \(I+L\) changes the left side to
\([L_{:1},-e_2,L_{:3}]w_3\). Its first and second rows give the displayed expressions, with \(1-q_3=B_0^{-1}\).

The following estimates hold:
\[
\boxed{
|\alpha|\le\frac65,\quad \|w_2\|^2\le\frac52,\quad
|v_1|\le\frac13\sqrt t,\quad |v_2|\le\frac35\sqrt t,
\quad |v_1|^2+|v_2|^2\le\frac t2.}
\tag{20}
\]
For the first, marginalize site 3. The effective positive \(L\)-matrix on sites 1,2 is
\[
L'=L_{FF}-\frac{L_{F3}L_{3F}}{1+\ell_3}.
\]
The two-by-two formula gives
\[
|\alpha|\le\sqrt{\frac{L'_{22}}{L'_{11}}}
\le\sqrt{\frac{11/9}{1-t/4}}<\frac65.
\]
For the other bounds, use (19), \(B_0\ge15/8\),
\(\sqrt{\ell_3/\ell_1}<6/5\), and \(\sqrt{\ell_2\ell_3}\le11/10\).

The inverse feature weights satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\omega_1^{-1}=p(1-p)\le\frac14,\quad
\omega_2^{-1}=\frac{q_2(1-q_2)}p\le5t,\quad
\omega_3^{-1}=\frac{q_3(1-q_3)}\pi\le\frac54.}
\tag{21}
\]

## 5. Bounding the trial value

The conditional vectors have the useful cancellation identities
\[
e_1^*Kw_2=e_1^*Kw_3=0,\qquad
w_2^*Kw_2=q_2,\quad w_2^*Kw_3=v_2,
\quad w_3^*Kw_3=q_3+|v_2|^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_1&=d_1+\tau p,\\
b_2&=d_1|\alpha|^2+d_2+\tau q_2,\\
b_3&=u+d_1|v_1|^2+d_2|v_2|^2
       +\tau(q_3+|v_2|^2),\\
H_{12}&=-d_1\alpha,\quad H_{13}=d_1v_1,\\
H_{23}&=-d_1\overline\alpha v_1+(d_2+\tau)v_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
Equations (13), (18), and (20) give
\[
\boxed{
\frac3{25}\le b_1\le\frac12,\quad
\frac25t\le b_2\le\frac75t,\quad
u\le b_3\le u+1\le\frac54u.}
\tag{24}
\]
For example, the lower bound on \(b_2\) already follows from
\(d_2+\tau q_2\ge(6/25+6/25)t\), while its upper bound is
\((13/50)(5/2)t+(3/4)t=(7/5)t\).

Also
\[
|H_{23}|\le\left(\frac9{20}+\frac{13}{50}t\right)\sqrt t
\le\frac12\sqrt t.
\]
The definitions in (5), (6), and bounds (21), (24) now give the entirely explicit table
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c@{\qquad}c|c}
\gamma_{12}&3t&\kappa_1&4tu\\
\gamma_{13}&t^3/(15u)&\kappa_2&8u\\
\gamma_{23}&5/(8u)&\kappa_3&t/u
\end{array}}
\tag{25}
\]
where each entry on the right of a symbol is an upper bound.

For clarity, the unrounded bounds for the three penalties are
\[
\kappa_1\le\frac{175}{48}tu,\qquad
\kappa_2\le\frac{125}{16}u,\qquad
\kappa_3\le\frac78\frac tu.
\]
They follow by replacing \(\delta\) with \(b_1b_2b_3\). For the small overlaps,
\[
\gamma_{12}\le\frac{6084}{3000}t<3t,
\qquad \gamma_{13}\le\frac{169}{2700}\frac{t^3}{u}
<\frac{t^3}{15u}.
\]

It follows from (14), (25) that
\[
R_*\ge1-3t-\frac5{8u}>\frac9{10},\qquad
E\le4tu+\frac tu+\frac{2t^3}{15u}\le5tu,
\]
\[
1+\kappa_2\le9u,
\qquad E(1+\kappa_2)\le45tu^2<\frac9{20}.
\]
Thus
\[
R_*^2-E(1+\kappa_2)\ge\frac{81}{100}-\frac9{20}=\frac9{25}.
\]
Substitution in the exact trial identity (9) yields
\[
2b^{\mathsf T}r-r^{\mathsf T}Cr
\ge2+\frac{9/25}{1+\kappa_2}
\ge2+\frac1{25u}.
\]
This proves (3).

To obtain (4), use source old-097's notation
\(\mathcal T=\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id}\). Its Woodbury identity gives
\[
\delta\langle I,\mathcal T^{-1}I\rangle
=3-b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b\le1-\varepsilon,
\quad \varepsilon=\frac1{25u}.
\]
For every Hermitian \(B\), Cauchy–Schwarz in the \(\mathcal T\) inner product gives
\[
\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2\le(1-\varepsilon)\langle B,\mathcal TB\rangle.
\]
The exact entropy identity therefore gives
\[
D^2H(K)[M^{1/2}BM^{1/2},M^{1/2}BM^{1/2}]
\le-\varepsilon\langle B,\mathcal TB\rangle
\le-\varepsilon\delta\|B\|_F^2.
\]

## 6. A longer central curve

For the particular curve
\[
L(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\sqrt{1-t}&-i\sqrt t/2\\
\sqrt{1-t}&1&\sqrt t/2\\
i\sqrt t/2&\sqrt t/2&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<t\le\frac1{100},
\tag{26}
\]
the same trial proves the stronger bound
\[
\boxed{b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b\ge2+\frac1{20\log(1/t)}.}
\tag{27}
\]
Here \(\det L=t/2\), \(Z=6+t\),
\[
d_1=d_2=d=-\log(1-t/4),\quad u=-\log t,
\quad\tau=\log2-2d.
\]
The selected quantities are explicitly
\[
p=\frac{8+5t}{4(6+t)},\quad q_2=\frac{6t}{8+5t},\quad
\pi=\frac{8-t}{4(6+t)},\quad q_3=\frac{4-t}{8-t},
\]
\[
\alpha=\frac{8\sqrt{1-t}+it}{8+5t},\quad
v_1=\frac{2i\sqrt t}{8-t},\quad
v_2=\frac{2\sqrt t(1+i\sqrt{1-t})}{8-t}.
\tag{28}
\]
The proof is the same as above, using these sharper bounds:
\[
\frac t4\le d\le\frac t3,\quad \frac23\le\tau\le\frac7{10},
\quad \frac13\le p\le\frac38,\quad
\frac23t\le q_2\le\frac34t,
\]
\[
|\alpha|\le1,\quad |v_1|^2\le t/12,\quad
|v_2|^2\le t/6,\quad |v_1|^2+|v_2|^2\le t/4.
\]
These imply
\[
\frac29\le b_1\le\frac13,\quad
\frac23t\le b_2\le\frac65t,\quad u\le b_3\le u+1,
\]
\[
\gamma_{12}\le\frac34t,\quad \gamma_{13}\le\frac{t^3}{24u},
\quad \gamma_{23}\le\frac{27}{128u},
\]
\[
\kappa_1\le\frac{27}{16}tu,\quad
\kappa_2\le\frac{45}{32}u,\quad
\kappa_3\le\frac25\frac tu.
\tag{29}
\]
For instance, \(H_{23}\) has magnitude at most \((d+7/20)\sqrt t\le(3/8)\sqrt t\), yielding its displayed overlap bound. The weight reciprocals used here are \(1/4,9t/4,1\), respectively.

On this larger interval \(u>4\), \(tu^2<1/4\). Hence
\[
R_*\ge\frac9{10},\quad E\le\frac74tu,
\quad 1+\kappa_2\le\frac{53}{32}u,
\]
\[
R_*^2-E(1+\kappa_2)
\ge\frac{81}{100}-\frac{371}{512}
=\frac{1093}{12800}.
\]
The trial surplus is at least
\[
\frac{1093}{21200u}>\frac1{20u},
\]
proving (27). The logarithm bounds are elementary: the series
\(\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}(1/3)^{2j+1}/(2j+1)\) places \(\log2\) between \(56/81\) and \(25/36\), which suffices for the asserted bounds on \(\tau\).

## 7. Useful failures and why this adds information

### 7.1 Omitting the correction feature never proves the claim here

Keeping only \(r_1=1/b_1,r_3=1/b_3\) gives value exactly \(2-E<2\), because \(\kappa_1,\kappa_3>0\). The correction feature in (8) supplies the positive term \(R_*^2/(1+\kappa_2)\). The main proof compares its order \(1/u\) with the loss of order \(tu\). This is the mechanism behind the region extending to \(t\downarrow0\).

### 7.2 The three-occupation-indicator certificate actually fails eventually

Let \(J(t)\) denote source old-097's three-indicator value
\[
J(t)=d_M^{\mathsf T}\bigl((|M_{ij}|^2)_{ij}+\delta\Sigma\bigr)^{-1}d_M,
\quad d_M=\operatorname{diag}M,
\]
along the central curve (26). Put \(\lambda=\log2\), \(u=-\log t\). Analytically,
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{J(t)-2}{tu}
=-\frac{2+3\lambda}{24\lambda}<0.}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(J(t)<2\) for all sufficiently small positive \(t\), whereas (27) holds for every \(t\le1/100\). No explicit numerical threshold for the failure is asserted.

Here is a derivation. Expanding the determinant using the diagonal part of \(M\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta={}&d^2u+\tau(2dup+3d^2/Z)\\
&+\tau^2\left(\frac{3tu}{2Z}+\frac{2d(1+t/4)}Z\right)
+\frac{\tau^3t}{2Z}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\delta=t(Au+B)+O(t^2u),\quad
A=\frac{\lambda(2+3\lambda)}{12},\quad
B=\frac{\lambda^2(1+\lambda)}{12}.
\tag{31}
\]
The indicator quadratic matrix and target are symmetric under exchanging coordinates 1 and 2 after taking entry magnitudes. Restrict to the orthonormal basis \((e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2,e_3\). Write \(m_1=M_{11}=M_{22}\), \(m_3=M_{33}\), \(z=K_{12}\), \(p=K_{11}\). The reduced target is \((\sqrt2m_1,m_3)\), and its first diagonal matrix entry is
\[
a_0=m_1^2+\tau^2|z|^2+\delta\{p(1-p)-|z|^2\}.
\]
Using \(m_1=\lambda/3+O(t)\), \(p=1/3+O(t)\), and
\(p^2-|z|^2=3t/(2Z)\), one obtains
\[
a_0=2m_1^2+\frac{A}{9}tu+O(t).
\]
The second diagonal entry is \(m_3^2+O(tu)\), where \(m_3=u+O(1)\). The off-diagonal entry is \(O(t)\). Inverting this two-by-two matrix consequently gives
\[
J(t)=2-\frac{A}{2\lambda^2}tu+O(t),
\]
which is (30). The remainder claims use \(tu^2\to0\), so the inverse expansion error \(O(t^2u^2)\) is \(o(t)\).

This establishes an analytic obstruction to relying on occupation features alone in the present region. It does not obstruct the seven-feature method.
