## 1. Full-domain two-reservoir theorem

Let `B_1,B_2` be two nonempty measured-coordinate blocks, let
\[
0<D_i<I_{B_i},\qquad u_i\in\mathbb C^{B_i}\setminus\{0\},\quad i=1,2,
\]
and fix all this data. Neither block need be diagonal, and `u_i` need not be an eigenvector of `D_i`. Define
\[
\alpha_i=(u_i^*D_i^{-1}u_i)^{-1},\qquad
\beta_i=(u_i^*(I-D_i)^{-1}u_i)^{-1},\qquad
\ell_i=\alpha_i+\beta_i.
\tag{1}
\]
For real `a,b` and complex `z`, put
\[
K(a,b,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_1+a u_1u_1^*&z u_1u_2^*\\
\bar z u_2u_1^*&D_2+b u_2u_2^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1.** The function `H(P_K)` is jointly concave in the four real variables `(a,b,Re z,Im z)` on its entire positive-contraction domain. That domain is exactly
\[
0\le M\le I_2,\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}
(a+\alpha_1)/\ell_1&z/\sqrt{\ell_1\ell_2}\\
\bar z/\sqrt{\ell_1\ell_2}&(b+\alpha_2)/\ell_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every chord inside this domain is covered, including chords between noncommuting full-rank coherent kernels. No fixed eigenvector condition on the reservoirs is imposed.

Define the two binary-input block-output channels
\[
C_i(Y\mid0)=P_{D_i-\alpha_i u_iu_i^*}(Y),\qquad
C_i(Y\mid1)=P_{D_i+\beta_i u_iu_i^*}(Y),
\tag{4}
\]
and set
\[
\tau_i=\operatorname{TV}(C_i(\cdot\mid0),C_i(\cdot\mid1))
=\frac12\sum_Y|C_i(Y\mid1)-C_i(Y\mid0)|>0.
\tag{5}
\]
There is an exact law identity
\[
\boxed{P_{K(M)}=(C_1\otimes C_2)P_M.}
\tag{6}
\]
For every strict `0<M<I_2` and Hermitian direction `E` with `det E>0`, the theorem has the quantitative curvature form
\[
\boxed{-D^2H(P_{K(M)})[E,E]\ge8\tau_1\tau_2\det E.}
\tag{7}
\]
Here derivatives use the affine `M` coordinates in (3). Equivalently, a physical direction `(\dot a,\dot b,\dot z)` with `\dot a\dot b>|\dot z|^2` has lower bound
\[
8\tau_1\tau_2\frac{\dot a\dot b-|\dot z|^2}{\ell_1\ell_2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Integrating (7) gives a finite-chord lower bound
`4 t(1-t) tau_1 tau_2 det(M_1-M_0)` whenever this determinant is positive; continuity includes boundary endpoints.

The new input is the following finite-alphabet lemma. An arbitrary stochastic channel cannot in general be invoked as preserving entropy concavity merely by data processing; its special exact refinement is what makes the present assertion valid.

## 2. Balanced-pair refinement of a binary-input channel

Let `C(y|x)`, `x in {0,1}`, be any stochastic channel with finite output alphabet. Delete rows zero under both inputs. Put
\[
c_y=C(y\mid0),\qquad d_y=C(y\mid1)-C(y\mid0),\qquad \sum_y d_y=0.
\]
Partition the rows into `P={d_y>0}`, `N={d_y<0}`, and `Z={d_y=0}`. Choose any nonnegative transport `r_{ij}`, `i in P,j in N`, satisfying
\[
\sum_{j\in N}r_{ij}=d_i,\qquad
\sum_{i\in P}r_{ij}=-d_j.
\tag{9}
\]
Such a transport exists because the two total masses agree. Retain only its positive entries. If all `d_y=0`, there are only singleton groups below.

For each retained pair `g=(i,j)`, refine output row `i` into this group with probability `r_{ij}/d_i`, and row `j` with probability `r_{ij}/(-d_j)`. These splitting probabilities are independent of the input and sum to one within each original row. Label the refined pair by `(g,B)`, with `B=1` for the positive row `i` and `B=0` for the negative row `j`. Its two probabilities given input `x` are
\[
\widetilde C(g,1\mid x)=\frac{r_{ij}}{d_i}(c_i+d_i x),\qquad
\widetilde C(g,0\mid x)=\frac{r_{ij}}{-d_j}(c_j+d_j x).
\tag{10}
\]
The group probability is
\[
w_g=\frac{r_{ij}}{d_i}c_i+
\frac{r_{ij}}{-d_j}c_j>0,
\tag{11}
\]
independent of `x`. Conditional on the group, the positive bit satisfies
\[
\Pr(B=1\mid G=g,X=x)=\rho_g+s_g x,
\quad
\rho_g=\frac{r_{ij}c_i}{d_iw_g},\quad
s_g=\frac{r_{ij}}{w_g}>0.
\tag{12}
\]
The channel inequalities give `0<=rho_g<=rho_g+s_g<=1`. Every zero-difference row `y in Z` is a singleton group of weight `w_g=c_y`, with `B=0` and `rho_g=s_g=0`. These weights sum to one, and
\[
\boxed{\sum_g w_gs_g=\sum_{i\in P}d_i
=\operatorname{TV}(C(\cdot\mid0),C(\cdot\mid1)).}
\tag{13}
\]

Let `lambda(g|y)` denote the splitting law of the original output row. For any binary input of mean `m`, writing `Y` for the original output and `\widetilde Y` for its refinement, the chain rule gives exactly
\[
H(\widetilde Y)=H(Y)+A_C(m),\qquad
A_C(m)=\sum_y(c_y+d_ym)H(\lambda(\cdot\mid y)).
\tag{14}
\]
The correction is affine in `m`. The original row is recoverable from the refined label, so (14) involves no lost conditional entropy or unsupported equality case of data processing.

The refinement also preserves Fisher information. For a differentiable family `p_y(t)`, each refined probability is `p_y(t)lambda(g|y)`, with fixed `lambda`, and hence
\[
\sum_{y,g}\frac{[p_y'(t)\lambda(g\mid y)]^2}
{p_y(t)\lambda(g\mid y)}
=\sum_y\frac{p_y'(t)^2}{p_y(t)}.
\tag{15}
\]
Fixed zero entries are omitted from both sides.

## 3. Product channels become an affine correction plus a fixed mixture of DPP entropies

This algebraic identity holds for any finite number `m` of binary-input channels, although the unconditional concavity conclusion below is used only when `m=2`.

Apply independent refinements to channels `C_i`, and let `X~P_M` for `0<=M<=I_m`. Because each `G_i` has an input-independent law, the labels `G_i` are mutually independent and independent of the entire input `X`. Given a label vector `g`, the refined bits arise by independent binary channels
\[
\Pr(B_i=1\mid X_i=x_i,G=g)=\rho_{i,g_i}+s_{i,g_i}x_i.
\]
Set
\[
R_g=\operatorname{diag}(\rho_{i,g_i}),\qquad
S_g=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{s_{i,g_i}}),\qquad
F_g(M)=R_g+S_gMS_g,
\quad W_g=\prod_iw_{i,g_i}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then `0<=F_g(M)<=I_m`: the lower bound is immediate, while
\[
I-F_g=\operatorname{diag}(1-\rho_{i,g_i}-s_{i,g_i})
+S_g(I-M)S_g\ge0.
\]
The conditional bit law is precisely `P_{F_g(M)}`. Indeed, for every coordinate set `A`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(A\subseteq B\mid G=g)
&=\mathbb E\prod_{i\in A}(\rho_{i,g_i}+s_{i,g_i}X_i)\\
&=\sum_{T\subseteq A}\prod_{i\in A\setminus T}\rho_{i,g_i}
\prod_{i\in T}s_{i,g_i}\det M_T
=\det(F_g(M)_A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Inclusion moments determine the entire finite law.

The output entropy is consequently
\[
\boxed{
H\big((\bigotimes_iC_i)P_M\big)
=\sum_iH(w_i)+\sum_gW_gH(P_{F_g(M)})
-\sum_iA_{C_i}(M_{ii}).
}
\tag{18}
\]
Every weight and affine correction is fixed by the channels. Thus product binary-input channels preserve any DPP entropy-concavity theorem valid for all the displayed affine images. No concavity of an arbitrary higher-site DPP is assumed.

For a line `M+tE` in the strict domain, (15) and the fixed group weights give the exact identities
\[
\boxed{\mathcal I_{\rm out}(M;E)
=\sum_gW_g\mathcal I_{F_g(M)}(S_gES_g),}
\tag{19}
\]
\[
\boxed{D^2H_{\rm out}(M)[E,E]
=\sum_gW_gD^2H(P_{F_g(M)})[S_gES_g,S_gES_g].}
\tag{20}
\]
These derivatives are on the fixed positive support of each law. Singleton groups produce deterministic zero bits, which can be omitted before differentiation. In particular, the formulas do not differentiate a logarithm at a structurally zero atom.

## 4. Self-contained two-site curvature theorem

For a strict two-site kernel
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}a&c\\\bar c&b\end{pmatrix},
\]
write its probabilities, in order `00,10,01,11`, as
\[
x=(1-a)(1-b)-|c|^2,\quad
y=a(1-b)+|c|^2,\quad
z=b(1-a)+|c|^2,\quad
w=ab-|c|^2.
\]
They satisfy `yz-xw=|c|^2`. If `E=E*` and `delta=det E`, their second derivative vector is `2 delta (1,-1,-1,1)`. Therefore
\[
-H''=\mathcal I-2\delta L,\qquad
L=\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0,
\quad \mathcal I=\sum_\omega\frac{(p_\omega')^2}{p_\omega}.
\tag{21}
\]
This is nonnegative when `delta<=0`.

For `delta>0`, reverse `E` if needed to make it positive definite. Set
\[
T=\frac{y+z}{2\sqrt{xw}}\ge1,
\quad P=\frac{\operatorname{adj}F}{\sqrt w},
\quad Q=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-F)}{\sqrt x}.
\]
Both `P,Q` are positive definite with determinant one, and elementary two-dimensional determinant expansion gives `det(P+Q)=2+2T`. The empty and full atoms alone imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I
&\ge(\operatorname{tr}PE)^2+(\operatorname{tr}QE)^2\\
&\ge\tfrac12[\operatorname{tr}((P+Q)E)]^2
\ge2\det(P+Q)\det E=4(1+T)\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The last inequality is `tr(AE)^2>=4det(A)det(E)` for positive `2x2` matrices; apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the eigenvalues of `E^{1/2}AE^{1/2}`. Also `yz<=((y+z)/2)^2` yields `L<=2 log T`. Thus
\[
\boxed{-H''\ge4\delta(1+T-\log T)\ge8\delta.}
\tag{23}
\]
This proves entropy concavity on the strict two-site domain in all Hermitian directions, with the definite-direction constant used in (7). Integration and the regularization `F -> (1-2epsilon)F+epsilon I` prove concavity on the closed domain. This two-site result is prior mathematics; it is included to make the new channel argument self-contained.

For two arbitrary finite-output channels, (18) is a fixed positive mixture of these concave functions plus an affine term. Consequently:

**Theorem 2 (balanced-pair channel theorem).** For every pair of finite binary-input channels, the map
\[
M\longmapsto H\big((C_1\otimes C_2)P_M\big)
\]
is concave on `0<=M<=I_2`.

For the stronger curvature statement, a group pair has transformed direction determinant `s_{1,g}s_{2,h}det E`. Sum (23) over the groups and use (13) to obtain
\[
-D^2H_{\rm out}(M)[E,E]
\ge8\det E\left(\sum_gw_{1,g}s_{1,g}\right)
\left(\sum_hw_{2,h}s_{2,h}\right)
=8\tau_1\tau_2\det E
\tag{24}
\]
when `det E>0`. Groups with a zero slope have a deterministic coordinate and contribute nonnegative ordinary binary-entropy curvature.

For completeness, the full signed-determinant identity is
\[
\boxed{-D^2H_{\rm out}(M)[E,E]
=\mathcal I_{\rm out}(M;E)-2\det E\,\Lambda_C(M),}
\tag{25}
\]
\[
\Lambda_C(M)=\sum_{g,h}w_{1,g}w_{2,h}s_{1,g}s_{2,h}
\log\frac{P_{F_{gh}}(10)P_{F_{gh}}(01)}
{P_{F_{gh}}(00)P_{F_{gh}}(11)}\ge0.
\tag{26}
\]
Only group pairs with both slopes positive occur in (26); their kernels are strict at strict `M`. This explicitly identifies the logarithmic correction and its compensating Fisher decomposition. In particular, when `det E<=0`, the output curvature is at least its entire Fisher information.

## 5. Exact feasibility normalization and block-channel law

Embed the vectors into their disjoint blocks and let `U` have these two columns. Write `D=D_1 direct-sum D_2`, `T=[[a,z],[conj(z),b]]`, `A=diag(alpha_i)`, and `L=diag(ell_i)`. Then `K=D+UTU*` and
\[
U^*D^{-1}U=A^{-1}.
\]
The two columns of `D^{-1/2}U` are orthogonal. Decompose the ambient space into their range and its orthogonal complement. On the complement, `I+D^{-1/2}UTU*D^{-1/2}` is the identity; on the range, its positivity is equivalent to `I+A^{-1/2}TA^{-1/2}>=0`. Hence
\[
K\ge0\iff T+A\ge0.
\tag{27}
\]
Applying the same calculation to `I-K` gives
\[
I-K\ge0\iff\operatorname{diag}(\beta_i)-T\ge0.
\tag{28}
\]
Combining (27)-(28) and conjugating by `L^{-1/2}` proves (3), including strictness equivalence. The channel endpoint matrices in (4) are therefore positive contractions.

To prove (6) without any invertibility assumption at those endpoints, fix inclusion sets `A_i subseteq B_i`. Write
\[
h_i(x)=\det\big((D_i-\alpha_i u_iu_i^*
+x\ell_i u_iu_i^*)_{A_i}\big),\qquad x\in\{0,1\}.
\]
A rank-one determinant expansion shows `h_i(x)=h_i(0)+x Delta_i`, where `Delta_i=h_i(1)-h_i(0)`. Put `Q_i=D_i-alpha_i u_iu_i*`. Block determinant expansion for
\[
K(M)_{A_1\cup A_2}
=\operatorname{diag}((Q_1)_{A_1},(Q_2)_{A_2})
+U_A L^{1/2} M L^{1/2}U_A^*
\]
gives
\[
\det K(M)_{A_1\cup A_2}
=h_1(0)h_2(0)
+M_{11}\Delta_1h_2(0)+M_{22}h_1(0)\Delta_2
+\det M\,\Delta_1\Delta_2.
\tag{29}
\]
For example, first prove (29) when both baseline principal blocks are invertible by the rank-two determinant lemma. Its two diagonal transfer factors are `Delta_i/h_i(0)`. Multiplying out removes the denominators, and the resulting polynomial identity holds also for singular baselines. Empty inclusion sets have determinant one and `Delta_i=0`, so are included.

If `X~P_M` is sent through the two independent channels (4), the probability that both output blocks contain their respective `A_i` is
\[
\mathbb E[(h_1(0)+X_1\Delta_1)(h_2(0)+X_2\Delta_2)].
\]
Using `E X_i=M_ii` and `E X_1X_2=det M` gives (29). Equality of all inclusion moments proves the entire output law identity (6). Theorem 2 now proves Theorem 1. Finally, `tau_i>0`: the singleton inclusion probabilities of the channel columns differ by `ell_i|u_i(j)|^2`, which is nonzero for at least one coordinate.

## 6. Rational coherent example and reproducibility

Take two two-coordinate blocks and
\[
D_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&1/10\\1/10&3/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_2=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-1/10\\-1/10&2/5\end{pmatrix},
\quad u_1=\binom11,\quad u_2=\binom1{-1}.
\tag{30}
\]
Both reservoirs have eigenvalues `1/2 +/- sqrt(2)/10`. Moreover,
`D_1u_1=(1/2,7/10)^T` and `D_2u_2=(7/10,-1/2)^T`, so neither coupling vector is a reservoir eigenvector. The exact normalization constants are
\[
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\frac{23}{80},\qquad
\beta_1=\beta_2=\frac{23}{120},\qquad
\ell_1=\ell_2=\frac{23}{48}.
\tag{31}
\]
For the first block the channel endpoint matrices are
\[
Q_{1,0}=\frac1{80}\begin{pmatrix}9&-15\\-15&25\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q_{1,1}=\frac1{120}\begin{pmatrix}71&35\\35&95\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{32}
\]
Their eigenvalues are respectively `{0,17/40}` and `{23/60,1}`. The second block has the same endpoint eigenvalues, with the two singleton output probabilities exchanged. The exact first-block channel is

| Output subset | `C_1(Y|0)` | `C_1(Y|1)` | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Empty | `23/40` | `0` | `-23/40` |
| First coordinate | `9/80` | `5/24` | `23/240` |
| Second coordinate | `5/16` | `49/120` | `23/240` |
| Both coordinates | `0` | `23/60` | `23/60` |

Thus `tau_1=tau_2=23/40`. Pair each positive-difference row with the empty row. The empty row is split with probabilities `(1/6,1/6,2/3)`; each positive row is unsplit. The three first-block groups have the following parameters:

| Positive row in the pair | Group weight `w` | Intercept `rho` | Slope `s` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First coordinate | `5/24` | `27/50` | `23/50` |
| Second coordinate | `49/120` | `75/98` | `23/98` |
| Both coordinates | `23/60` | `0` | `1` |

After exchanging the singleton labels, the second block has the identical table. In this example, (18) is an explicit nine-term mixture, with group-label constant
`2 H(5/24,49/120,23/60)` and affine correction
\[
\frac{23}{40}(2-\operatorname{tr}M)H(1/6,1/6,2/3).
\tag{33}
\]

Choose the rational complex latent endpoints
\[
M_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&(1+i)/12\\(1-i)/12&2/5\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_1=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&(2-i)/15\\(2+i)/15&2/3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{34}
\]
Their determinants and complementary determinants are
\[
\det M_0=43/360,\quad \det(I-M_0)=139/360,
\quad \det M_1=17/45,\quad \det(I-M_1)=1/9.
\]
Their diagonal entries are strictly between zero and one, so these exact signs certify both strict contractions. By (27)-(28), their four-site images `K_0,K_1` are strict contractions as well. Explicitly, the physical parameters in (2) are
\[
(a_0,b_0,z_0)=\left(-\frac{23}{180},-\frac{23}{240},
\frac{23(1+i)}{576}\right),
\quad
(a_1,b_1,z_1)=\left(0,\frac{23}{720},

\]
The four-site endpoints do not commute: their commutator has first diagonal entry
\[
\boxed{(K_0K_1-K_1K_0)_{11}=\frac{529}{34560}i\ne0.}
\tag{36}
\]
The latent direction is
\[
E=M_1-M_0=\begin{pmatrix}4/15&(1-3i)/20\\(1+3i)/20&4/15\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det E=\frac{83}{1800}>0.

Consequently the proved midpoint curvature and finite-chord bounds are the exact rationals

H(P_{K_{1/2}})-\frac{H(P_{K_0})+H(P_{K_1})}{2}

## 7. Scope and relation to the existing frontier

C022 proves the reservoir-to-endpoint radial line for any number of coherent blocks under its rank-one block-row condition. The present theorem covers the **entire four-real-dimensional contraction domain** for two such blocks, including arbitrary internal subsegments. Its decisive new lemma allows completely arbitrary finite-output binary channels, with an exact affine-entropy correction and Fisher-preserving refinement.

The zero-background fixed-two-dimensional-support theorem in `ROOT_RANK_TWO_SUPPORT.md` does not itself cover these generally full-rank kernels. Conversely, (18) alone does not prove entropy concavity for three or more latent sites; it supplies a reduction to the corresponding transformed DPP theorem. No assertion of general Lyons concavity, historical priority, arbitrary higher-rank interblock updates, or arbitrary within-block changes is made.

The prior two-site Fisher argument is the one reproduced in C055 and `ROOT_RANK_TWO_SUPPORT.md`; the proof above retains its stronger measured-two-site constant eight. The determinant identity for rank-one coherent block updates is consistent with C022, but the endpoint-channel normalization and balanced-pair refinement yield the new full-domain result.
