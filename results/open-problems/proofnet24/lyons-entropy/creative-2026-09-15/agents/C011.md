# C011 — determinant-product atom statistics, a sharp parity repair, and exact trial-space obstructions

Date: 2026-09-15. Assignment: construct a scalar atom statistic for C002's exact three-site variational criterion. This report gives a universal **identity-covariance statistic**, a closed-form successful parity repair on a substantial symmetric family, and two exact obstructions to extending small trial spaces universally. It does **not** refute entropy concavity or prove the universal three-site criterion.

## 1. Results and their scope

1. For every connected interior three-site kernel, there is a canonical explicit scalar statistic `g0` with
   \[
   \operatorname{Cov}(g_0,T_X)=I.
   \]
   Its atom values are a centered product of three affine functions of the inclusion indicators; the coefficients come directly from the frozen entropy's Möbius coefficients. No seven-by-seven inverse is needed.
2. The best variational value in the two-dimensional space spanned by this statistic and the centered three-site parity has an exact two-by-two formula. **This entire trial space fails** at the rational real kernel (12): its optimized objective is strictly greater than \(1.095\). Thus neither rescaling nor the parity correction makes this particular construction universal.
3. On C002's equal-edge particle–hole family, the explicit statistic (22) has objective strictly below one at every nonzero interior base. It supplies a short scalar certificate, an explicit full-Hessian negative bound, and a representation as a log-probability-weighted centered count. C002 already proved the negative-definite Hessian on this family; the new result here is the closed-form atom witness and quantitative certificate, not a claim to have first proved that family.
4. At the particularly simple rational edge \(r=15/52\), **every scalar statistic using only Walsh monomials of degree at most two has objective greater than \(1017/1015>1\)**. Coefficients may depend arbitrarily on the kernel. This rules out a universal first-six-Walsh construction on an open set of kernels. In the boundary limit, its best objective tends to three, while the parity witness remains below one.

The two obstructions are proved statements. The general unequal-edge scalar inequality recorded in Section 7 remains open in this report. C008's separate full particle–hole proof should not be conflated with a proof of this particular trial statistic's bound.

## 2. A universal identity-covariance atom statistic

Use C002's notation, with every coefficient frozen at the base kernel:
\[
K=K^*,\quad 0<K<I_3,\quad
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)
=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12}),
\]
\[
\tau=c_{123},\qquad M=D+\tau K>0,
\qquad \delta=\det M,\qquad S=M^{1/2}.
\]
For an outcome \(X\), write \(x_i=\mathbf1_{i\in X}\),
\[
Q_X=K-I_{X^c},\qquad T_X=SQ_X^{-1}S.
\]
The exact criterion is the existence of a centered real statistic \(g\) for which
\[
\mathcal Q_K(g):=\delta\operatorname{Var}(g)
+\|I-\operatorname{Cov}(g,T_X)\|_F^2\le1.
\tag{1}
\]

For arbitrary diagonal \(D\), scalar \(\tau\), and three-site DPP kernel \(J\), the generating determinant gives
\[
\mathbb E_J\prod_{i=1}^3(d_i+\tau x_i)
=\det(D+\tau J).
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, expansion of the product gives
\(\sum_R\tau^{|R|}\prod_{i\notin R}d_i\det J_R\), the principal-minor expansion of the determinant on the right.

If \(\tau\ne0\), define
\[
W_X=\prod_i(d_i+\tau x_i),\qquad
\boxed{\quad g_0(X)=\frac{W_X-\delta}{\tau\delta}.\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
Equation (2) shows \(\mathbb E_Kg_0=0\). Treating the atom values in (3) as frozen while differentiating with respect to \(J\),
\[
D_J\mathbb E_Jg_0\big|_{J=K}[A]
=\frac1\delta\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}M\,A)
=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A).
\tag{4}
\]
The score identity therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E[g_0Q_X^{-1}]=M^{-1},\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(g_0,T_X)=SM^{-1}S=I.}
\tag{5}
\]
This is an exact identity for every Hermitian direction, including the nonreal directions.

The formula has a nonsingular polynomial version valid also at \(\tau=0\). Center the statistic
\[
\widetilde g_0(X)=\frac1\delta\left[
\sum_i d_jd_kx_i
+\tau\sum_{i<j}d_kx_ix_j
+\tau^2x_1x_2x_3
\right],
\tag{6}
\]
where the unused index is \(k\) in the pair sum, and \(\{i,j,k\}=[3]\) in the first sum. For \(\tau\ne0\), (6) differs from (3) only by its centering constant. At \(\tau=0\), it becomes
\[
g_0=\sum_i\frac{x_i-K_{ii}}{d_i}.
\tag{7}
\]
The connected assumption makes all \(d_i>0\) when \(\tau=0\). Formula (6) is a linear combination of the seven Walsh features after the change \(x_i=(1+\sigma_i)/2\).

For the entropy coefficients, supermodularity gives \(d_i\ge0\) and \(d_i+\tau\ge0\). Thus \(W_X\ge0\); in particular, \(W_X/\delta\) is a probability-density ratio with respect to \(P_K\). This interpretation is not needed for (5), but identifies the statistic as a centered multiplicative tilt rather than an arbitrary matrix fit.

### Closed determinant moments

For \(\tau\ne0\), let
\[
B_2=\det\bigl(D^2+\tau(2D+\tau I)K\bigr),
\quad \mu=\det(2K-I),
\]
\[
B_P=\det\bigl((2D+\tau I)K-D\bigr).
\]
The generating determinant applied to the product of the corresponding one-site functions gives
\[
\mathbb EW_X^2=B_2,
\qquad
\mathbb E[W_X\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3]=B_P.
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Var}(g_0)=\frac{B_2-\delta^2}{\tau^2\delta^2},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(g_0,\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3)
=\frac{B_P-\delta\mu}{\tau\delta}.
\tag{8}
\]
These are polynomial determinants with logarithmic frozen coefficients. Formula (6) gives their finite limits at \(\tau=0\).

## 3. The optimally scaled product-plus-parity trial

Put
\[
h=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3-\mu,
\quad V_0=\operatorname{Var}(g_0),
\quad c=\operatorname{Cov}(g_0,h),
\quad v_h=1-\mu^2.
\]
Since \(\mu=\det(2K-I)\), its gradient is
\[
G=2\operatorname{adj}(2K-I).
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(h,T_X)=SGS=:C,
\qquad \ell=\operatorname{tr}(MG)=\operatorname{tr}C,
\quad d_C=\operatorname{tr}(MGMG)=\|C\|_F^2.
\]
For arbitrary real \(a,b\), the objective of \(g=ag_0+bh\) is exactly
\[
\mathcal Q_K(ag_0+bh)
=3-2u^T\binom ab+\begin{pmatrix}a&b\end{pmatrix}
J\binom ab,
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
u=\binom3\ell,
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}
3+\delta V_0&\ell+\delta c\\
\ell+\delta c&d_C+\delta v_h
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
Whenever \(J>0\), the best value in this entire trial space is
\[
\boxed{\min_{a,b}\mathcal Q_K(ag_0+bh)=3-u^TJ^{-1}u.}
\tag{11}
\]
This optimizes the scale of \(g_0\) as well as the parity coefficient. In particular, failure of (11) is stronger than failure of the fixed-scale correction \(g_0+bh\).

## 4. An exact obstruction to that two-dimensional construction

Take the real symmetric rational kernel
\[
\boxed{
K_*=\frac1{1000}
\begin{pmatrix}
997&25&-29\\
25&600&465\\
-29&465&455
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{12}
\]
Its leading principal minors are
\[
\frac{997}{1000},\quad \frac{23903}{40000},\quad
\frac{1102829}{20000000},
\]
and those of \(I-K_*\) are
\[
\frac3{1000},\quad \frac{23}{40000},\quad
\frac{51}{20000000}.
\]
All are positive, so Sylvester's criterion proves \(0<K_*<I\).

The exact atom probabilities, in indicator order, are:

| \(x_1x_2x_3\) | \(20{,}000{,}000\,p_X\) |
|---|---:|
| 000 | 51 |
| 001 | 11449 |
| 010 | 15829 |
| 011 | 32671 |
| 100 | 35449 |
| 101 | 7953051 |
| 110 | 10848671 |
| 111 | 1102829 |

They follow directly from \(p_X=(-1)^{3-|X|}\det(K_*-I_{X^c})\). The frozen log coefficients are exactly
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3)=\left(
\log\frac{181226221}{1666221},
\log\frac{405855601}{405605601},
\log\frac{561122221}{553282221}
\right),
\]
\[
\tau=\log\frac{143761604106995879841}{7084891450675879841}.
\tag{13}
\]
In particular every \(d_i\) and \(\tau\) is positive, so \(M=D+\tau K_*>0\).

The following enclosing intervals have rational decimal endpoints. They are produced using only integer and fraction arithmetic in `certify_obstruction.py`:
\[
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The same exact arithmetic gives

\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(J>0\) and \(2J-uu^T>0\). Congruence by \(J^{-1/2}\) proves
\(u^TJ^{-1}u<2\). Substitution into (11) proves the strict obstruction
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_{K_*}(ag_0+bh)>1
\quad\text{for every }a,b\in\mathbb R.}
\tag{16}
\]
More precisely, the exact interval calculation encloses the minimum in
\[
[1.095219439472,1.095219439473].
\tag{17}
\]
The fixed-scale family \(g_0+bh\) is much worse: its minimum lies in
\([6.862350173540,6.862350173541]\).

### Why the logarithmic enclosures are a proof

For \(1\le y\le2\), put \(z=(y-1)/(y+1)\). Then
\[
\log y=2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_N,
\qquad
0\le R_N\le\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z^2)}.
\tag{18}
\]
This follows by integrating the geometric series for \((1-z^2)^{-1}\), or by bounding the positive tail term by term. For each rational logarithm argument, repeatedly multiply or divide by two to write it as \(2^ky\), \(1\le y<2\). Apply (18) with \(N=32\) to \(y\) and to two, then combine \(k\log2+\log y\). All endpoints are rational. The implementation rounds these endpoints outwards to denominator \(10^{20}\), and all subsequent matrix and moment operations use `fractions.Fraction` interval arithmetic. No floating-point operation enters (15)–(17).

This refutes a proposed **trial statistic construction**, not the DPP entropy conjecture. The minimum over all seven Walsh statistics can be smaller. By continuity of the finite-dimensional minimum, the obstruction persists on a full-dimensional open neighborhood of \(K_*\).

## 5. A successful explicit count-plus-parity witness

Consider the equal-edge family already proved in C002,
\[
K_r=\frac12I+ir
\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad 0<r^2<\frac1{12}.
\]
Set
\[
t=12r^2\in(0,1),\quad
m=\log\frac{3+t}{3(1-t)},\quad
q_0=\frac{1-t}{8},\quad q=\frac{3+t}{24}.
\tag{19}
\]
Then the empty/full atoms have probability \(q_0\), the other six atoms have probability \(q\), and \(M=mI\), \(\delta=m^3\).

Let
\[
N=|X|,\quad n=N-\frac32,\quad P=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3,
\quad e=\frac1{\sqrt3}(1,1,1)^T.
\]
The needed moments and gradient covariances are
\[
\mathbb En=\mathbb EP=0,
\quad \operatorname{Var}(n)=\frac{3-2t}{4},
\quad \operatorname{Cov}(n,P)=-\frac t2,
\quad \operatorname{Var}(P)=1,
\tag{20}
\]
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(n,T_X)=mI,
\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(P,T_X)=-2mt\,ee^T.
\tag{21}
\]
For (20), use \(\mathbb E\sigma_i\sigma_j=-t/3\) and the identity \(\sigma_iP=\sigma_j\sigma_k\). For (21), differentiate \(\mathbb EN=\operatorname{tr}K\) and \(\mathbb EP=\det(2K-I)\); here \(2\operatorname{adj}(2K-I)=-2t\,ee^T\).

The proposed statistic is
\[
\boxed{
 g_t(X)=\frac{n}{m}+\frac{t}{2(m+4t^2)}P.}
\tag{22}
\]
Its covariance matches the identity exactly on the two-dimensional subspace orthogonal to \(e\):
\[
I-\operatorname{Cov}(g_t,T_X)
=\frac{mt^2}{m+4t^2}ee^T.
\tag{23}
\]
More generally, for \(g=n/m+bP\), substitution of (20)–(21) gives
\[
\mathcal Q(g)=\frac{m(3-2t)}4-m^2tb+m^2(m+4t^2)b^2.
\]
The minimizing \(b\) with the count coefficient fixed is exactly (22). Its objective is
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_t:=\mathcal Q(g_t)
&=\frac{m(3-2t)}4-\frac{m^2t^2}{4(m+4t^2)}\\
&=\frac{m(1-t)(3+t)}4+\frac{mt^4}{m+4t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

### A short closing inequality

For \(x\ge1\),
\[
\log x\le\frac{x-1}{\sqrt x}.
\]
The derivative of the right side minus the left side is
\((\sqrt x-1)^2/(2x^{3/2})\ge0\), and the difference is zero at one. Substituting \(x=(3+t)/(3(1-t))\) gives
\[
m^2(1-t)(3+t)\le\frac{16t^2}{3}.
\tag{25}
\]
Direct rearrangement of (24) gives
\[
1-F_t=
\frac{16t^2+4m(1-t)^2(1+2t)-m^2(1-t)(3+t)}{4(m+4t^2)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
1-F_t\ge
\varepsilon_t:=
\frac{\frac83t^2+m(1-t)^2(1+2t)}{m+4t^2}>0.}
\tag{26}
\]
This proves the scalar criterion everywhere on the displayed nonzero interior family.

It also gives a quantitative ambient Hessian bound. In C002's normalized direction \(A=mB\), let \(\mathcal R=\mathcal F+\delta I\). The exact minimum in (1) is \(\delta\langle I,\mathcal R^{-1}I\rangle\le F_t\). Matrix Cauchy–Schwarz yields
\[
\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2\le F_t\langle B,\mathcal RB\rangle.
\]
Using the exact curvature identity then proves
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K_r)[A,A]\le-m\varepsilon_t\|A\|_F^2
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.}
\tag{27}
\]
No direction has been discarded.

### The same witness using log probabilities

On this family,
\(\log(q/p_X)=m\) at the two extreme atoms and zero elsewhere. Pointwise,
\[
P=n\left[-2+\frac8{3m}\log\frac q{p_X}\right].
\]
Hence (22) is equivalently
\[
\boxed{
g_t(X)=n\left[
\frac1m-\frac{t}{m+4t^2}
+\frac{4t}{3m(m+4t^2)}\log\frac q{p_X}
\right].}
\tag{28}
\]
The useful log-probability contrast is multiplied by the centered count, which changes sign under complementation.

For reference, allowing both coefficients in \(an+bP\) to vary gives the exact full seven-Walsh minimum on this symmetric family:
\[
\mathcal Q_{\min}=
\frac{m\{8t^2(3-2t)+3m(1-t)(3+t)\}}
{32t^2+4m(3+t^2-2t^3)+m^2(1-t)(3+t)}.
\tag{29}
\]
To derive it, use \(x=ma\), \(y=2mtb\). The two-variable matrix and linear vector are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
3+\frac{m(3-2t)}4&-1-\frac m4\\
-1-\frac m4&1+\frac m{4t^2}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \binom3{-1},
\]
and inversion gives (29). The symmetry reduction proving that other features cannot improve this minimum is the same orthogonal decomposition used next, with the parity term retained.

## 6. Every degree-at-most-two Walsh statistic can fail

A statistic of degree at most two, after centering, has the form
\[
g=\sum_i a_i\sigma_i+\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}
\left(\sigma_i\sigma_j-\mathbb E\sigma_i\sigma_j\right).
\tag{30}
\]
At a particle–hole symmetric base, odd and even functions are orthogonal in \(L^2(P_K)\). Also
\(Q_{X^c}=-\overline{Q_X}\), so their score covariances are respectively real symmetric and purely imaginary Hermitian after whitening by real diagonal \(S\). These matrices are Frobenius orthogonal. The identity is real, so the even part in (30) only adds nonnegative terms to (1). Its optimal coefficient is zero.

For the equal-edge base, write \(a_i=\bar a+a_i^\perp\), \(\sum_i a_i^\perp=0\). The singleton covariance matrix has diagonal one and off-diagonal \(-t/3\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_i a_i\sigma_i\right)
=(3-2t)\bar a^2+\left(1+\frac t3\right)\sum_i(a_i^\perp)^2.
\]
Its matrix covariance is \(2m\operatorname{diag}(a_i)\), so
\[
\left\|I-2m\operatorname{diag}(a_i)\right\|_F^2
=3(1-2m\bar a)^2+4m^2\sum_i(a_i^\perp)^2.
\]
Every nonsymmetric coefficient again contributes a strictly positive amount. Consequently the minimum over **all six** degree-one/two Walsh features equals the minimum over the single centered count:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_{\le2,\min}
=\frac{3A_t}{3+A_t},\qquad
A_t=\frac{m(3-2t)}4.}
\tag{31}
\]
It is at most one exactly when
\[
m(3-2t)\le6.
\tag{32}
\]

Now take the exact rational edge
\[
r=\frac{15}{52},\qquad t=\frac{675}{676},\qquad
q_0=\frac1{5408},\quad q=\frac{901}{5408},\quad m=\log901.
\tag{33}
\]
The kernel is interior because \(12r^2=675/676<1\). Since \(e<3\),
\(e^6<729<901\), whence \(m>6\). Also
\[
\frac{3-2t}{4}=\frac{339}{1352}.
\]
Equation (31), increasing in \(m\), gives the entirely exact obstruction
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_{\le2,\min}
=\frac{1017\log901}{4056+339\log901}
>\frac{1017}{1015}>1.}
\tag{34}
\]
Numerically the exact minimum is about \(1.08751\), while the explicit parity witness (22) has objective about \(0.63678\). These decimals are supplementary; inequality (34) needs only \(e<3\).

As \(t\uparrow1\), \(m\to\infty\), so
\[
\mathcal Q_{\le2,\min}\longrightarrow3,
\qquad
F_t=1-\frac4m+o(m^{-1})<1.
\tag{35}
\]
The omitted cubic feature is therefore essential even asymptotically; the deficit of a degree-two trial does not disappear close to the spectral boundary. The strict failure in (34), combined with continuity of the optimized six-feature problem, persists throughout a full-dimensional open neighborhood of the displayed kernel.

Finally, every statistic \(g(X)=\phi_K(p_X)\) is even at a particle–hole symmetric base, regardless of the scalar function \(\phi_K\). Its covariance with \(T_X\) is purely imaginary and has trace zero. Thus, after centering,
\[
\mathcal Q_K(g)=3+\delta\operatorname{Var}(g)
+\|\operatorname{Cov}(g,T_X)\|_F^2\ge3.
\tag{36}
\]
This rules out a bare function of atom probability, including the frozen surprisal, on that entire locus. Adding such an even function to weighted counts does not bypass (34).

## 7. The remaining explicit unequal-edge inequality

The product statistic at a general connected particle–hole base is
\[
g_0=\frac12\sum_i\frac{\sigma_i}{m_i},
\]
using C002's \(r_i=v_i^2\) and
\(m_i=\log(q_jq_k/(q_0q_i))\). Put
\[
R=\sum_i m_ir_i,\qquad U=\sum_i\frac{r_i}{m_i},
\qquad \delta=m_1m_2m_3.
\]
Because \(\operatorname{Cov}(\sigma_i,P)=-4r_i\) and
\(\operatorname{Cov}(P,T_X)=-8(Sv)(Sv)^T\), the best fixed-scale parity repair is
\[
g=g_0+\frac{2\delta U}{\delta+64R^2}P.
\tag{37}
\]
Its exact objective simplifies to
\[
\boxed{
F(r_1,r_2,r_3)=
\frac14\sum_i\frac{m_jm_k}{m_i}
-2R-
\frac{4\left(\sum_i m_jm_k r_i\right)^2}{\delta+64R^2}.}
\tag{38}
\]
To obtain this, use
\[
\delta\operatorname{Var}(g_0)
=\frac14\sum_i\frac{m_jm_k}{m_i}-2R,
\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(g_0,P)=-2U,
\qquad \|\operatorname{Cov}(P,T_X)\|_F^2=64R^2,
\]
and complete the scalar square. Equation (22) is precisely (37) when the three \(r_i\) agree.

A seeded floating-point search of 200,000 anisotropic inputs with \(r_i\ge0\), \(\sum r_i<1/4\) did not find \(F>1.001\); the reported maximum was below one. This is exploratory evidence only, particularly near small logarithmic coefficients where numerical cancellation can occur. A proof of \(F\le1\) for the entire unequal-edge locus is **not** supplied here. C008 proves that some statistic works throughout that locus, which does not automatically establish (38) for this specified statistic.
