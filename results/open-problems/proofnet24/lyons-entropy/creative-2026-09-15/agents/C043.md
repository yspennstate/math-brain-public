## 1. Definitions and the canonical operator

For a real set table f on a finite coordinate set E, put

\[
F_f(K)=\mathbb E_{P_K}f(X),\qquad
q_f(K;u,v)=D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*].
\tag{1}
\]

If c_S are the Boolean Möbius coefficients of f, the DPP inclusion identity gives

\[
F_f(K)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}c_S\det K_S.
\tag{2}
\]

The second cofactor expansion of each determinant defines its canonical Hermitian operator T_f(K) on exterior two-forms, with

\[
q_f(K;u,v)=\langle u\wedge v,T_f(K)(u\wedge v)\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

For a principal determinant with K_S>0, its operator is the embedding of

\[
\det K_S\,\bigwedge^2K_S^{-1}\succeq0.
\tag{4}
\]

The cofactor polynomial extends this identity to singular positive semidefinite K_S. Constants and modular tables have zero operator. All assertions of full operator positivity below are proved from these canonical operators; none is inferred merely from their values on real decomposable forms.

## 2. A positive grouped operator with a negative fourth Boolean coefficient

On four sites define the particle-count hinge

\[
h(S)=(|S|-2)_+.
\tag{5}
\]

Its exact Boolean expansion is

\[
h(S)=\sum_{|T|=3}\mathbf1_{T\subseteq S}
-2\mathbf1_{E\subseteq S}.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus

\[
F_h(K)=e_3(K)-2\det K,
\tag{7}
\]

where e_3 is the sum of the four principal triple determinants. In particular, the fourth Boolean coefficient of h is -2.

**Lemma 1.** For every Hermitian contraction 0<=K<=I on four sites, T_h(K) is positive semidefinite. It is positive definite when 0<K<I.

**Proof.** Write K=U diag(lambda_1,...,lambda_4)U*, where U is unitary and 0<=lambda_i<=1. Both polynomials in (7) are invariant under unitary conjugation, and their canonical cofactor operators transform by exterior-square conjugation. At a diagonal K, the second cofactor formula makes T_h diagonal in the coordinate exterior basis. Its entry for the pair ij is

\[
w_{ij}=\lambda_k+\lambda_l-2\lambda_k\lambda_l
=\lambda_k(1-\lambda_l)+\lambda_l(1-\lambda_k),
\qquad\{k,l\}=E\setminus\{i,j\}.
\tag{8}
\]

Consequently the full operator identity is

\[
T_h(K)=(\bigwedge^2U)\operatorname{diag}(w_{ij})
(\bigwedge^2U)^*\succeq0.
\tag{9}
\]

Every w_ij is positive for an interior contraction. The identity is polynomial and also holds at repeated or boundary eigenvalues. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Equation (8) exhibits the cancellation directly. The negative contribution -2 lambda_k lambda_l from the quadruple term is compensated by the two triple contributions lambda_k+lambda_l. It is not necessary, or true, that the separate Möbius coefficients are all nonnegative.

## 3. The equimodular four-cycle log-determinant theorem

Let rho>=0 and phi be real. Let A_phi be the Hermitian four-cycle adjacency matrix with

\[
(A_\phi)_{12}=(A_\phi)_{23}=(A_\phi)_{34}=1,
\qquad (A_\phi)_{41}=e^{i\phi},
\tag{10}
\]

their conjugate reverse edges, and zero other entries. Set

\[
L_0=I+\rho A_\phi,\quad x=\rho^2,
\quad d=1-4x+2x^2(1-\cos\phi).
\tag{11}
\]

Assume

\[
0\le x<\tfrac12,\qquad d>0.
\tag{12}
\]

These conditions are equivalent to L_0>0. In fact, its leading principal minors in the displayed vertex order are 1, 1-x, 1-2x, d; Sylvester's criterion gives sufficiency, and all principal triple minors equal 1-2x, giving necessity.

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
a&=-\log(1-x),\\
b&=\log\frac{(1-x)^2}{1-2x},\\
c&=\log\frac{(1-2x)^2}{d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

All three coefficients are nonnegative: (1-x)^2=(1-2x)+x^2, and

\[
d=(1-2x)^2-2x^2(1+\cos\phi)\le(1-2x)^2.
\tag{14}
\]

**Theorem 2.** Let D>0 be arbitrary diagonal and L=D^(1/2)L_0D^(1/2). For the table f_L(S)=-log det L_S, the canonical operator T_f_L(K) is positive semidefinite at every Hermitian positive contraction K. If rho>0 and K is interior, that operator is positive definite on exterior two-forms.

**Proof.** Write C_4 for the four cycle edges and m(S)=sum_{i in S}(-log D_ii). Direct principal minors give the exact table identity

\[
\boxed{
f_L(S)=m(S)
+a\sum_{ij\in C_4}\mathbf1_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}
+b(|S|-2)_+
+c\mathbf1_{E\subseteq S}.}
\tag{15}
\]

To verify it on all sixteen subsets, empty/singleton minors of L_0 are 1; edge-pair minors are 1-x; opposite-pair minors are 1; triple minors are 1-2x; and the full determinant is d. Every triple contains two cycle edges and the full set contains four. Thus (13) gives 2a+b=-log(1-2x) and 4a+2b+c=-log d, which establishes every case of (15). The full determinant formula in (11) follows by enumerating the two perfect matchings and the two oriented four-cycles; the verifier also proves it as a Laurent-polynomial identity in rho and e^(i phi).

Let P_ij denote the rank-one projector onto e_i wedge e_j in exterior space. Applying canonical cofactor operators to (15) yields

\[
\boxed{
T_{f_L}(K)=a\sum_{ij\in C_4}P_{ij}
+bT_h(K)+cT_{\mathbf1_{E\subseteq\bullet}}(K)\succeq0.}
\tag{16}
\]

The first summand is positive semidefinite, the second is covered by Lemma 1, and the third is the positive cofactor operator (4) for det K. The modular term disappears. If rho>0, then b>0; Lemma 1 therefore gives positive definiteness at every strict K. This proves the theorem for complex phases and complex Hermitian external bases. \(\square\)

Direct sums of these odds blocks, arbitrary positive singleton blocks, and their arbitrary positive diagonal scalings give an all-dimensional additive extension. The external K need not respect the block decomposition: F_f depends on the appropriate principal restrictions K_B, and the corresponding positive exterior operators are embedded and added. This is a constructive block extension, not a new solution of the genuinely coupled six-site problem.

At an entropy-linked strict K=L(I+L)^(-1), the frozen log-atom table is

\[
-\log p_S(K)=\log\det(I+L)-\log\det L_S.
\tag{17}
\]

The constant in (17) has no canonical Hessian. For A=alpha uu*-beta vv*, alpha,beta>=0, rank-one affinity of each atom gives

\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}
-2\alpha\beta q_{f_L}(K;u,v)\le0.
\tag{18}
\]

This is the precise entropy consequence. General same-sign eigenvalue pairs in a higher-rank direction are not controlled by (18), and arbitrary affine segments can leave the odds class. No full-Hessian theorem follows merely from (16). As already stated, the four-site entropy scope itself was known in the supplied prior work.

## 4. Exact obstruction to positive one-direction scalar Laplace certificates

For n coordinates define a multiplicative table

\[
g_z(S)=\prod_{i\in S}z_i.
\tag{19}
\]

Let C_n be the closure, in the finite-dimensional real space of tables, of the set

\[
m(S)+\sum_{r=1}^N a_r g_{z^{(r)}}(S),\qquad a_r\ge0,
\tag{20}
\]

where m is arbitrary modular (including a constant), and each z^(r) obeys one of

\[
0\le z_i^{(r)}\le1\quad\hbox{for every i},
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
z_i^{(r)}\ge1\quad\hbox{for every i}.
\tag{21}
\]

The choice of orthant may vary between summands. This permits all finite positive mixtures, convergent integrals after modular renormalization, and their finite-table limits. It includes the positive exponential mechanisms of C005, the absent-coordinate gate z_0=0 in C018, and the occupied-coordinate gate obtained by dividing by z_0 and sending z_0 to infinity in the upper orthant. No uniform bound on the parameters is imposed.

These are precisely a broad class of scalar certificates for which the determinantal expectation of each multiplicative table has a Gram-positive exterior Hessian:

\[
\mathbb E_Kg_z(X)=\det(I-K+KZ),\qquad Z=\operatorname{diag}(z).
\tag{22}
\]

For Z<=I, write the determinant as det(I-B^(1/2)KB^(1/2)), B=I-Z>=0. For Z>=I, write it as det(I+C^(1/2)KC^(1/2)), C=Z-I>=0. The second rank-one derivatives in each case have the nonnegative Gram expression from C005. Boundary parameters follow by the cofactor formula and continuity. Thus every table in C_n has positive canonical exterior operator at every contraction.

**Proposition 3.** Every f in C_n has

\[
\Delta_T f(S)\ge0\quad
\text{whenever }T\cap S=\varnothing\text{ and }|T|\ge2\text{ is even}.
\tag{23}
\]

**Proof.** For each generator,

\[
\Delta_Tg_z(S)
=\left(\prod_{i\in S}z_i\right)
\left(\prod_{j\in T}(z_j-1)\right)\ge0.
\tag{24}
\]

There are an even number of factors of one sign in the second product. A modular table contributes zero; nonnegative sums preserve the inequality; and this finite linear functional is continuous under the closure defining C_n. \(\square\)

Now choose phi=pi and rho=1/2. The exact positive definite odds and linked marginal matrices are

L=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2&0&-1/2\\
1/2&1&1/2&0\\
0&1/2&1&1/2\\
-1/2&0&1/2&1

K=\frac17\begin{pmatrix}
3&1&0&-1\\
1&3&1&0\\
0&1&3&1\\
-1&0&1&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{25}
\]

Writing A=2(L-I), one has A^2=2I. Hence L has eigenvalues 1 plus/minus 1/sqrt(2), each twice, and K has eigenvalues (3 plus/minus sqrt(2))/7, each twice. Both required strict inequalities are therefore explicit.

The complete atom table is rational:

Here det(I+L)=49/4. Formula (15) becomes

\[
f_L(S)=\log(4/3)\sum_{ij\in C_4}\mathbf1_{ij\subseteq S}
+\log(9/8)(|S|-2)_+.
\tag{26}
\]

Therefore

\boxed{\Delta_1\Delta_2\Delta_3\Delta_4 f_L(\varnothing)

Equations (23) and (27) prove f_L is not in C_4. The same is true of the exact log-atom weight (17), of all positive diagonal scalings of L, and of its embedding as a principal block with independent extra sites. Constants and modular scalings cannot change the separating functional.

## 5. A fractional determinant-power Laplace obstruction

A related proposal would start from positive Gaussian integral representations of inverse determinant powers and then continue the exponent down to zero. For the matrix L in (25), that continuation cannot preserve an arbitrary positive measure on the nonnegative orthant.

**Proposition 4.** With L from (25), the analytic function

\[
G(t)=\det(I+\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)L)^{-1/4},
\qquad t_i\ge0,
\tag{28}
\]

is not a Laplace transform of a positive measure on [0,infinity)^4.

**Proof.** More generally set G_alpha(t)=det(I+diag(t)L)^(-alpha). The squarefree fourth Taylor coefficient is

\[
\partial_{1234}G_\alpha(0)
=\sum_{\pi\in S_4}\alpha^{\#\operatorname{cycles}(\pi)}
\prod_{i=1}^4L_{i,\pi(i)}.
\tag{29}
\]

For completeness, expand -alpha log det(I+TL) into traces and then exponentiate. A squarefree trace monomial corresponds to a directed cycle; the factor 1/k removes its k choices of starting point. Exponentiating joins disjoint cycles into permutations. Each cycle contributes one alpha, and the combined trace-series sign is (-1)^4=1. This proves (29) directly in a neighborhood of zero.

For L=I+rho A_pi, the nonzero permutations in (29) are the identity, four adjacent transpositions, two disjoint adjacent-transposition matchings, and two oriented four-cycles. The latter each have product -rho^4. Hence

\[
\partial_{1234}G_\alpha(0)
=\alpha^4+4\rho^2\alpha^3+2\rho^4\alpha^2-2\rho^4\alpha.
\tag{30}
\]

At rho=1/2 and alpha=1/4 this is

\[
\boxed{\frac1{256}+\frac1{64}+\frac1{128}-\frac1{32}
=-\frac1{256}.}
\tag{31}
\]

If G were a positive Laplace transform, every fourth mixed derivative at strictly positive t would equal

\[
\int x_1x_2x_3x_4e^{-\langle t,x\rangle}\,\mu(dx)\ge0.
\tag{32}
\]

Here mu has finite mass G(0)=1; differentiation at strictly positive t is justified since the polynomial factor times the exponential is bounded. The analytic derivative in (31) is continuous, so it stays negative at sufficiently small strictly positive t. This contradicts (32) without any assumption that mu has finite moments at the boundary. \(\square\)

There is also a direct finite-table obstruction at the same exponent. Let r(S)=(det L_S)^(-1/4). From the exact minor table,

\[
\Delta_{1234}r(\varnothing)
=\sqrt2-4\sqrt[4]2+4\sqrt[4]{4/3}-1<-\frac1{28}<0.
\tag{33}
\]

The strict bound is elementary:

\[
\sqrt2<\frac{99}{70},\quad
\sqrt[4]2>\frac{19}{16},\quad
\sqrt[4]{4/3}<\frac{43}{40},
\tag{34}
\]

as verified by raising the positive rational bounds to the relevant powers. Substituting them in (33) gives 99/70-19/4+43/10-1=-1/28. Thus the inverse-principal-determinant table at exponent 1/4 is also outside the cone C_4.

Proposition 4 is about the continuous determinant generating polynomial, while (27) and (33) concern tables of principal determinants. They are distinct exact obstructions; neither is being substituted for the other.
