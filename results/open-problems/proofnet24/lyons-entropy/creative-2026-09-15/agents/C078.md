## 1. Results and relation to the current frontier

Write a three-site kernel using its diagonal `d`, squared edge magnitudes `r`, and real cycle coordinate `q`. The following statements are proved below.

1. With **arbitrary fixed diagonal and fixed real cycle coordinate**, the exact feasible `r` set is convex. Every chord in it realizes the ordinary classical mixture of its endpoint DPP laws. Consequently the entropy is strictly concave on this fiber, and its negative Hessian is a **matrix-convex Fisher matrix** in `r`.
2. On that same fiber, entropy is nonincreasing in each `r_i`. Radially shrinking the squared magnitudes while adjusting the phase to keep `q` fixed remains feasible until the cycle product becomes real, and increases entropy. This gives an exact real-cycle comparison with arbitrary, unequal diagonal entries; it does not hold the edge magnitudes fixed as in C014.
3. With equal diagonal entries, every doubly stochastic redistribution `r -> D r` at fixed `q` is feasible and isospectral. It acts by one fixed classical channel on the singleton atoms and by the same channel on the complementary doubleton atoms. Entropy increases, and the complete intrinsic Fisher metric obeys the exact contraction and score-variance identity in (16)–(17).
4. For a single pair equalization, the entropy Hessian in the unscaled pair-imbalance coordinate becomes less negative as the imbalance shrinks; formula (20) gives its exact monotonicity and excess Fisher information.

C074 already proves the full ambient Hessian theorem at the balanced, count-uniform endpoint, and its spectral envelope already gives the corresponding global maximum there. The new comparison is **every partial magnitude redistribution**, its mixture realization and Fisher identity, rather than another proof of that endpoint theorem. The construction is not C051's linear average of coordinate-permuted kernels: phases are adjusted to retain the cycle invariant and the original spectrum.

## 2. Exact feasibility and classical-mixture closure

For a Hermitian three-site kernel put
\[
d_i=K_{ii},\qquad (e_1,e_2,e_3)=(K_{23},K_{31},K_{12}),
\qquad r_i=|e_i|^2,\qquad q=2\operatorname{Re}(e_1e_2e_3).
\]
Fix `0<d_i<1` and a real number `q`. For `i,j,k` all distinct, define the set
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal R_{d,q}=\{r\in[0,\infty)^3:\;&q^2\le4r_1r_2r_3,\\
&r_i<d_jd_k,\quad r_i<(1-d_j)(1-d_k),\\
&d_1d_2d_3-\sum_i d_i r_i+q>0,\\
&(1-d_1)(1-d_2)(1-d_3)-\sum_i(1-d_i)r_i-q>0\}.
\end{split}\tag{1}
\]

**Lemma 1.** The set (1) is exactly the squared-magnitude image of the strict contractions with these `d,q`, and it is convex.

**Proof.** The last two lines are `det K>0` and `det(I-K)>0`; the preceding line gives their order-two principal-minor conditions. The order-one conditions were fixed in advance. The phase condition is necessary because `|Re(e_1e_2e_3)|<=sqrt(r_1r_2r_3)`.

Conversely, if `r_1 r_2>0`, choose
\[
e_1=\sqrt{r_1},\qquad e_2=\sqrt{r_2},\qquad
e_3=\frac{q}{2\sqrt{r_1r_2}}
i\sqrt{r_3-\frac{q^2}{4r_1r_2}}.\tag{2}
\]
This realizes `r,q`. If an edge vanishes, `q=0`; choose phases of the remaining edges arbitrarily. All principal minors of the resulting `K` and `I-K` are positive by (1), so both matrices are positive definite.

All conditions except the first line in (1) are affine strict inequalities in `r`. For `q=0`, its first line adds no restriction beyond nonnegativity. For `q!=0`, it is the convex superlevel set
\[
\sum_i\log r_i\ge\log(q^2/4)
\]
of a concave function. Intersecting these sets proves convexity. \(\square\)

For an atom `S`, set
\[
a_i(S)=d_i-\mathbf1_{i\notin S},\qquad
\epsilon_S=(-1)^{3-|S|}.
\]
The exact atom formula is
\[
p_S(d,r;q)=\epsilon_S\left[\prod_i a_i(S)-\sum_i a_i(S)r_i+q\right].\tag{3}
\]
It is affine in `r`. Thus if `r^0,r^1` belong to (1) and
`r^t=(1-t)r^0+t r^1`, Lemma 1 produces a strict kernel `K^t` satisfying
\[
\boxed{P_{K^t}=(1-t)P_{K^0}+tP_{K^1}.}\tag{4}
\]
This is actual mixture closure within a specified feasible DPP family. No assertion that an arbitrary mixture of DPP laws is determinantal is used.

Write `h_{d,q}(r)=H(P_{K(r)})`; the value is independent of the choice of realizing phases. For
\[
B_{Si}=-\epsilon_S a_i(S),\qquad
\mathsf G(r)=B^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(p_S(r)^{-1})B,
\]
one has the exact intrinsic Hessian
\[
\boxed{\nabla_r^2 h_{d,q}(r)=-\mathsf G(r).}\tag{5}
\]
The matrix `B` has rank three: if every atom derivative `Bx` vanishes, every pair-inclusion derivative vanishes, while
`D det K_{jk}=-x_i`. Thus `x=0`. All atoms are positive in (1), so `G(r)>0`.

In particular, `h` is strictly concave on every nontrivial feasible chord. Its exact Jensen gap is
\[
\begin{split}
h(r^t)-(1-t)h(r^0)-th(r^1)
&=(1-t)D(p^0\Vert p^t)+tD(p^1\Vert p^t)\\
&=\int_0^1\bigl(\min(t,s)-ts\bigr)
(\Delta r)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf G(r^s)\Delta r\,ds,
\end{split}\tag{6}
\]
where `Delta r=r^1-r^0`.

There is a further Hessian comparison, not merely the sign in (5). Convexity of the reciprocal on positive numbers gives
\[
\boxed{\mathsf G(r^t)\preceq(1-t)\mathsf G(r^0)+t\mathsf G(r^1).}\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently, the entropy Hessian in the fixed `r` coordinates is matrix-concave. Equations (4)–(7) allow arbitrary unequal diagonal entries.

## 3. Coordinatewise monotonicity and a feasible real-cycle comparison

Freeze `f(S)=-log p_S`, and define its pair and triple Möbius coefficients by
\[
c_{jk}=f(\{j,k\})-f(\{j\})-f(\{k\})+f(\varnothing),
\qquad
\tau=\sum_S(-1)^{3-|S|}f(S).
\]
For completeness, with `L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0`, the atoms satisfy
`p_S=det(I-K) det L_S`. The determinant inequality for a positive two-by-two Schur complement gives
\[
c_{jk}\ge0,\qquad c_{jk}+\tau\ge0.\tag{8}
\]
The second inequality applies after conditioning on the remaining site. These are the standard three-site frozen-log inequalities used in old-097.

Only the pair minor `det K_{jk}=d_jd_k-r_i` and the full minor
`det K=d_1d_2d_3-sum d_i r_i+q` vary when `r_i` varies. Differentiating the frozen expectation therefore gives
\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial h}{\partial r_i}=-m_i,\qquad
m_i=c_{jk}+d_i\tau
=(1-d_i)c_{jk}+d_i(c_{jk}+\tau)\ge0.}\tag{9}
\]
The derivative with respect to `q`, wherever the affine atom formula is considered, is
\[
\frac{\partial h}{\partial q}=\tau.\tag{10}
\]

If the kernel graph is connected, every `m_i` is positive. Indeed `c_{jk}=0` is equivalent to `L_{jk}=0`, and `c_{jk}+tau=0` additionally forces the conditional edge
`L_{jk}-L_{ji}L_{ik}/L_{ii}` to vanish. Both equalities would give
`L_{jk}=0` and `L_{ji}L_{ik}=0`, disconnecting the three-site graph. The connectivity of `L` and `K` is equivalent, since either is a rational function of the other and block decomposition is preserved.

It follows immediately from (9) and the feasible chord that
\[
r^-\le r^+\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(r^-)\ge h(r^+)
\qquad(r^\pm\in\mathcal R_{d,q}).\tag{11}
\]

There is an explicit useful endpoint. For `r_1r_2r_3>0`, put
\[
s_* = \left(\frac{q^2}{4r_1r_2r_3}\right)^{1/3}\in[0,1].
\]
Every point `s r`, `s_*<=s<=1`, is feasible: the phase condition is exactly `s>=s_*`, and decreasing `s` improves **every** order-two principal minor and both order-three determinants in (1). Thus
\[
\boxed{h(s_*r)-h(r)=\int_{s_*}^1\sum_i r_i m_i(sr)\,ds\ge0.}\tag{12}
\]
For `q!=0`, the final cycle product has zero imaginary part, and its sign is the sign of `q`. If the original cycle product was nonreal, then `s_*<1` and the gain is strict. In terms of the original cycle angle `theta`, `s_*=|cos theta|^{2/3}`. For `q=0`, the endpoint is the diagonal kernel; this last special case overlaps prior diagonal-ray scope.

This compares a nonreal-cycle kernel to a real-cycle kernel while **changing magnitudes**. It does not contradict C014's obstruction, which fixes those magnitudes and concerns the full ambient Hessian scalar.

## 4. Equal-diagonal isospectral balancing is a fixed atom channel

Now assume `d_1=d_2=d_3=d`, and put `T=r_1+r_2+r_3`. Define
\[
\alpha=d(1-d)^2+(1-d)T+q,\qquad
\beta=d^2(1-d)+dT-q.
\]
The complete atom table is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{atom}&\text{probability}\\\hline
\varnothing&(1-d)^3-(1-d)T-q\\
\{1,2,3\}&d^3-dT+q\\
\{i\}&\alpha-r_i\\
\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{i\}&\beta-r_i.
\end{array}\tag{13}
\]

Let `D` be any three-by-three doubly stochastic matrix and set `r^D=D r`. This point is feasible. One proof is that (1) is convex and permutation invariant under equal `d`, and a doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of permutations. Directly, each new coordinate lies between the extreme old coordinates, `T` is retained, and Jensen's inequality for `log` gives `prod (Dr)_i>=prod r_i` when all entries are positive; zero entries follow by continuity. These observations verify all conditions in (1).

The characteristic polynomial is
\[
\det(xI-K)=x^3-3dx^2+(3d^2-T)x-(d^3-dT+q).\tag{14}
\]
It is unchanged by `r -> Dr`, so every such balancing is isospectral.

Let `T_D` be the eight-by-eight classical channel that fixes the empty and full atoms, applies `D` to the singleton triple, and applies `D` to the doubleton triple indexed by their omitted sites. From (13),
\[
\boxed{p(Dr)=T_Dp(r),\qquad H(p(Dr))\ge H(p(r)).}\tag{15}
\]
Both assertions hold for every feasible `r` in this fixed `d,q` fiber; `T_D` is independent of `r`. The entropy inequality follows from concavity of `-x log x` in each row and the column sums of `D`. Its gap is the sum over the two triples of
`sum_{ij}D_{ij}a_j log(a_j/(Da)_i)`, with `a_i=alpha-r_i` or `beta-r_i`. Equality holds precisely when each row of `D` mixes only equal `r_j` values. Full equalization is strictly improving unless `r` is already constant.

Thus the entropy is Schur-concave in `r` on each fixed-`T,q` fiber. This is a comparison along every feasible magnitude majorization, not only the fully balanced maximum already supplied by C074.

## 5. Exact Fisher contraction under the same redistribution

Use the three-dimensional intrinsic Fisher matrix `G` of (5). Since `d,q` remain fixed and `D` is fixed, differentiation of the channel identity in (15) gives
`B D=T_D B`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{D^{\mathsf T}\mathsf G(Dr)D\preceq\mathsf G(r).}\tag{16}
\]
This controls all three `r` tangent coordinates, including changes of `T`; no count-preserving restriction on the tangent is needed.

Here is the exact nonnegative remainder. For a tangent `x`, write
`u=Bx`, `\widehat p=T_Dp`, and `\widehat u=T_Du`. Then
\[
\boxed{
x^{\mathsf T}\mathsf G(r)x-(Dx)^{\mathsf T}\mathsf G(Dr)(Dx)
=\sum_{S,R}(T_D)_{SR}p_R
\left(\frac{u_R}{p_R}-\frac{\widehat u_S}{\widehat p_S}\right)^2.
}\tag{17}
\]
Expanding the square proves the identity: its first term uses the channel's column sums, and its mixed and last terms combine using `widehat u=T_Du` and `widehat p=T_Dp`. In particular, this is the usual Fisher data-processing inequality with the exact DPP realization and tangent map supplied, not an unsupported comparison between two matrices that merely share some minors.

At full equalization, the channel forgets the site label conditional on the count, and (17) is precisely the intrinsic count-Fisher loss on this family. C074 controls the full ambient Hessian at that endpoint independently; (17) does not transport its conclusion backwards to arbitrary ambient directions.

## 6. A pair-imbalance Hessian has genuine scalar monotonicity

Fix `r_3`, `u=(r_1+r_2)/2`, and write
\[
r_1=u+h,\qquad r_2=u-h.
\]
Holding `d,T,q` fixed, every replacement `h -> theta h`, `|theta|<=1`, is feasible by the preceding theorem. Put
`a=alpha-u`, `b=beta-u`. Positivity of all atoms gives `a>|h|`, `b>|h|`. Apart from terms independent of `h`, entropy is
\[
\psi(h)=-(a-h)\log(a-h)-(a+h)\log(a+h)
-(b-h)\log(b-h)-(b+h)\log(b+h).\tag{18}
\]
Consequently
\[
\psi'(h)=-2\left[\operatorname{artanh}(h/a)+\operatorname{artanh}(h/b)\right],
\]
\[
\boxed{-\psi''(h)=J(h)=\frac{2a}{a^2-h^2}+\frac{2b}{b^2-h^2}.}\tag{19}
\]
The Fisher information `J(h)` increases strictly with `|h|`, and
\[
\boxed{
J(h)-J(0)=\frac{2h^2}{a(a^2-h^2)}+\frac{2h^2}{b(b^2-h^2)}.
}\tag{20}
\]
Thus `psi''(theta h)>=psi''(h)` with strict inequality for `h!=0` and `|theta|<1`. This comparison uses the **same unscaled imbalance tangent** at both bases; it is stronger in this direction than (16), which also contracts the tangent by `theta`.

Integration of `artanh z>=z` for `z>=0` gives the quantitative entropy improvement
\[
\psi(\theta h)-\psi(h)
\ge(1-\theta^2)h^2\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right)
\quad(0\le\theta\le1).\tag{21}
\]
No Loewner comparison of the entire ambient Hermitian Hessians is inferred from (20).

## 7. The precise ambient correction and the remaining obstruction

Let `A` be an arbitrary Hermitian direction with zero diagonal, and write
`b=(A_23,A_31,A_12)`. Along the **affine kernel line** `K+tA`,
\[
r_i'=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline e_i b_i),\qquad
r_i''=2|b_i|^2,
\]
\[
q'=2\operatorname{Re}(b_1e_2e_3+e_1b_2e_3+e_1e_2b_3),
\]
\[
q''=4\operatorname{Re}(b_1b_2e_3+b_1e_2b_3+e_1b_2b_3).
\]
The atom formula is affine jointly in `(r,q)` at fixed `d`, so its intrinsic Hessian is minus the corresponding Fisher form. Equations (9)–(10) therefore give the exact ambient formula
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)-2\sum_i m_i|b_i|^2+\tau q''.
}\tag{22}
\]
If a smooth lift of an affine fixed-`q` chord is used instead, and `C=K''(0)` is its acceleration, then
\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(K(t))
=D^2H(K)[A,A]+DH(K)[C]
=-x^{\mathsf T}\mathsf G(r)x.\tag{23}
\]
Its acceleration satisfies
`D r_i[C]=-2|b_i|^2` and `D q[C]=-q''`, so
`DH[C]=2 sum_i m_i|b_i|^2-tau q''`, exactly accounting for (22).

Smooth lifts are supplied by (2) where all `r_i>0` and `q^2<4prod r_i`. At a real-cycle phase face one should use feasible one-sided intrinsic limits; a smooth lift in every invariant direction is not claimed.

Thus the matrix-convex Fisher theorem, (16), and the scalar monotonicity (20) are complete statements about their specified principal-minor coordinates. Their ambient extension would require comparing the remaining `tau q''` term together with the magnitude penalties and, for nonzero diagonal tangents, the additional diagonal/cycle terms. This term is retained rather than assumed to improve under majorization. In particular, fixing the atom law or spectrum does not fix the tangent Fisher geometry.

A direct limited ambient consequence of (22) is that all zero-diagonal directions satisfying `tau q''<=0` have nonpositive curvature; connected bases give strictness for every such nonzero direction. This is an elementary consequence of the existing frozen-log identity, not the main new-scope claim of this report.
