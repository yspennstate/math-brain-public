# C047 — An inertia-sensitive Fisher budget for signed-rank-one odds

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: COMPLETE new restricted theorem, with exact full-curvature certificates.**

This report proves full-Hermitian entropy concavity at a dimension-uniform, quantitatively bounded class of nonexchangeable signed-rank-one odds matrices. A sharper version allows substantially larger correlations in directions with several eigenvalues of each sign, including every inertia-(2,1) direction under an explicit larger bound. A convex family in the marginal kernel gives actual finite concavity segments whose directions can have arbitrarily high rank.

The proof keeps the complete frozen logarithmic curvature and pays for its positive part using the complete atom Fisher information. It does not discard same-sign eigenvalue pairs or infer entropy concavity from positivity of an exterior operator. In four retained six-site examples the frozen curvature is strictly positive; the Fisher term is necessary to the displayed sign conclusion.

Inputs: TARGET.md, PRIOR_FRONTIER.md, C005, C013, C018, C075, ROOT_RANK_TWO_SPECTRAL.md, C056, and the completed C043. C005 supplies the signed weighted-count Laplace representation, reproduced below. The new step is an inertia-refined determinant inequality combined with an exact variance-range and score-square allocation. It is an elementary mixed-determinant/trace argument; no unquoted Alexandrov–Fenchel theorem is assumed. C043's exact scalar-Laplace obstruction explains why this argument is not asserted for arbitrary odds tables; its separate particle-count hinge certificate is not needed for this theorem.

This does not prove general Lyons concavity, unrestricted signed-rank-one odds concavity, or the nonpositive-diagonal-background case of C018. Historical priority outside the supplied project sources has not been checked. No subagents, audits, pushes, or external messages were used.

## 1. The theorem and explicit ranges

Let \(n\ge2\) (the one-site case is binary entropy), and let
\[
L=D+\sigma aa^*>0,\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)>0,\qquad
\sigma\in\{+1,-1\},
\qquad K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]
Put
\[
w_i=\frac{|a_i|^2}{d_i},\qquad M=\sum_iw_i,
\qquad
\kappa=
\begin{cases}
1+M,&\sigma=+1,\\
(1-M)^{-1},&\sigma=-1.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
The negative case has \(M<1\), by positivity of \(L\). In either case, \(\kappa\) is exactly the spectral condition number of \(D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2}\). This is a specified background normalization; it is not asserted to equal normalization by the diagonal of \(L\).

For a nonzero Hermitian direction \(A\), let \(p\) and \(q\) be the numbers of its positive and negative eigenvalues, and set
\[
m=\max(p,q)\ge1,\qquad
\gamma_m=1-\frac1m,
\qquad
\mathcal B(\kappa)=\log\frac{(\kappa+1)^2}{4\kappa}.
\tag{3}
\]
The Fisher information below is the complete atom Fisher information,
\[
I_K(A)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}
\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}.
\tag{4}
\]

**Theorem 1.** For every matrix and direction just specified,
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-\left[1-\left(1-\frac1m\right)
\log\frac{(\kappa+1)^2}{4\kappa}\right]I_K(A).
}
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, entropy curvature is nonpositive whenever
\[
\gamma_m\mathcal B(\kappa)\le1.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(m=1\), (5) reduces to the previously known rank-one/rank-two-indefinite conclusion, without any condition on \(\kappa\). The new content concerns \(m\ge2\).

For \(m\ge2\), the exact allowable condition number in (6) is
\[
\boxed{
\kappa\le\kappa_m:=
\left(\sqrt{e^{m/(m-1)}}+
\sqrt{e^{m/(m-1)}-1}\right)^2.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Some consequences are:

| Direction or dimension | Sufficient range |
|---|---:|
| Any inertia-(2,1), (1,2), or (2,2) direction | \(\kappa\le\kappa_2\approx27.519887\) |
| Any direction with at most three eigenvalues of each sign | \(\kappa\le\kappa_3\approx15.863719\) |
| Every Hermitian direction in dimension six | \(\kappa\le\kappa_6\approx11.191111\) |
| Every Hermitian direction, uniformly in dimension | \(\kappa\le\kappa_\infty=(\sqrt e+\sqrt{e-1})^2\approx8.758958\) |

For the last row, \(\mathcal B(\kappa)\le1\), so the coefficient in (5) is at least \(1/m\). The simpler entirely rational range
\[
\boxed{\quad \kappa\le8\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
therefore gives all-dimensional full-Hermitian concavity. Indeed,
\(\mathcal B(8)=\log(81/32)<1\), since
\(e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3>81/32\).

If every \(a_i\ne0\), the full Hessian is strictly negative definite throughout the dimension-uniform range \(\mathcal B(\kappa)\le1\). The proof of this strictness, including possible zero Fisher information, is in Section 5.

## 2. The full frozen logarithm as positive determinant integrals

For the strict kernel in (1), all atoms are positive and
\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det(I+L)}.
\]
The determinant lemma gives
\[
\det L_S=\left(\prod_{i\in S}d_i\right)
\left(1+\sigma W(S)\right),
\qquad W(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w_i.
\tag{9}
\]
After deleting the constant and modular parts of the frozen table \(-\log p_S\), its remaining part is
\[
\phi_\sigma(S)=-\log(1+\sigma W(S)).
\tag{10}
\]
The deleted terms have affine determinantal expectations, so their second derivatives vanish. Consequently the exact, complete entropy Hessian is
\[
H''=-I_K(A)+D^2F_{\phi_\sigma}(K)[A,A],
\quad F_{\phi_\sigma}(J)=\mathbb E_J\phi_\sigma(X).
\tag{11}
\]
The table, including its \(w_i\)'s, is frozen at the base; (11) does not differentiate the odds parametrization along the direction.

For both signs, C005's scalar identity reads
\[
\phi_\sigma(S)=
\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t
\left(e^{-\sigma tW(S)}-1\right)\,dt.
\tag{12}
\]
The negative sign requires \(M<1\), as already supplied by (1). Define
\[
Y_t(S)=e^{-\sigma tW(S)},\quad
G_t=\mathbb E_KY_t,
\quad C_t=\sigma\left(I-e^{-\sigma t\operatorname{diag}(w)}\right)\ge0,
\]
\[
M_t=I-\sigma C_t^{1/2}KC_t^{1/2}>0,
\qquad
R_t=C_t^{1/2}M_t^{-1}C_t^{1/2}\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
The DPP generating determinant gives \(G_t=\det M_t\). For an arbitrary complex Hermitian \(A\), let
\[
V_t=R_t^{1/2}AR_t^{1/2}=V_t^*.
\]
Ordinary determinant differentiation, in the full direction \(A\), gives
\[
G_t'=-\sigma G_t\operatorname{tr}V_t,
\qquad
G_t''=G_t\left[(\operatorname{tr}V_t)^2
-\operatorname{tr}(V_t^2)\right].
\tag{14}
\]
Thus
\[
D^2F_{\phi_\sigma}(K)[A,A]
=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t G_t
\left[(\operatorname{tr}V_t)^2-\operatorname{tr}(V_t^2)\right]dt.
\tag{15}
\]
This expression includes every same-sign and opposite-sign pair of directional eigenvalues.

All differentiations are justified by finite sums of atoms. Near zero, the constant and modular terms in \(Y_t\) have zero second derivative, leaving \(O(t^2)\) in (14). At infinity, the positive-odds integrals have the factor \(e^{-t}\), and the negative-odds integrals are bounded by a constant times \(e^{-(1-M)t}\). Equivalently, one may differentiate the finite polynomial atom table coefficient by coefficient before integrating. There is no infinite-support limiting obligation.

## 3. The mixed-determinant estimate remembers inertia

**Lemma 2.** If a Hermitian matrix \(V\) has at most \(m\) positive and at most \(m\) negative eigenvalues, then
\[
\operatorname{tr}(V^2)\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V)^2}{m}.
\tag{16}
\]
This is stronger than the ordinary rank bound for an indefinite matrix.

**Proof.** Let the positive eigenvalues be \(x_i\), the absolute values of the negative eigenvalues be \(y_j\), and put \(P=\sum x_i\), \(N=\sum y_j\). Pad each list with zeros to length \(m\). Then the exact identity is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(V^2)-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V)^2}{m}
={}&\sum_{i=1}^m(x_i-P/m)^2
+\sum_{j=1}^m(y_j-N/m)^2
+\frac{2PN}{m}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Congruence or compression by a positive semidefinite matrix cannot increase either positive or negative inertia. Hence (16) applies to every \(V_t\) in (14), with the original direction's \(m=\max(p,q)\). This proves the lemma, including singular \(R_t\).

Combining (14) and (16) gives the determinant bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
G_t''\le\gamma_m\frac{(G_t')^2}{G_t}.
\quad}
\tag{18}
\]
For a positive semidefinite direction of rank \(r\), it is the familiar rank-\(r\) trace estimate for the second mixed determinant. For inertia \((2,1)\), its coefficient is \(1/2\), rather than the rank-three coefficient \(2/3\). This improvement is what gives the larger threshold in the first row of Section 1.

The left side in (18) can be positive. The next step compensates it with Fisher information; positivity of the exterior operator alone cannot do that.

## 4. An exact score and variance allocation

Let \(s(S)=Dp_S(K)[A]/p_S(K)\) be the full atom score. Then
\[
\mathbb E s=0,\qquad \mathbb E s^2=I_K(A)=:I,
\qquad G_t'=\mathbb E(sY_t).
\tag{19}
\]
Consequently Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\frac{(G_t')^2}{G_t}
\le I\,\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_t)}{G_t}.
\tag{20}
\]
This uses the complete score, including all rare atoms, rather than only linear occupation statistics or the count score.

The following exact range estimate is useful. For any random variable \(Y\in[a,b]\), where \(0<a\le b\), write \(\mu=\mathbb EY\). Then
\[
\boxed{
(\sqrt b-\sqrt a)^2-
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}\mu
=\frac{\mathbb E[(Y-a)(b-Y)]+(\mu-\sqrt{ab})^2}{\mu}
\ge0.
}
\tag{21}
\]
The identity follows by expanding both numerators. It retains a square and a pointwise nonnegative term, and is sharp for endpoint-supported variables with mean \(\sqrt{ab}\).

For the specific \(Y_t\) in (13), take
\[
(a_t,b_t)=
\begin{cases}
(e^{-tM},1),&\sigma=+1,\\
(1,e^{tM}),&\sigma=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
c_t=(\sqrt{b_t}-\sqrt{a_t})^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (18)–(22) imply
\[
D^2F_{\phi_\sigma}(K)[A,A]
\le\gamma_m I\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t c_t\,dt.
\tag{23}
\]
The integral evaluates exactly:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_+(M)
&=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t(1-e^{-tM/2})^2dt
=2\log(1+M/2)-\log(1+M),\\
\mathcal B_-(M)
&=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t(e^{tM/2}-1)^2dt
=2\log(1-M/2)-\log(1-M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
For example, the first line follows by writing the numerator as
\(2(e^{-t}-e^{-(1+M/2)t})-(e^{-t}-e^{-(1+M)t})\)
and using the elementary logarithm integral. In terms of (2), both answers equal
\[
\mathcal B_\sigma(M)=\log\frac{(\kappa+1)^2}{4\kappa}.
\tag{25}
\]
Substituting (23) and (25) in (11) proves Theorem 1.

### An exact nonnegative decomposition of the full entropy Hessian

For clarity, the argument provides more than a chain of upper bounds. Put
\(v_t=\operatorname{Var}(Y_t)\) and
\[
E_t=\operatorname{tr}(V_t^2)-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V_t)^2}{m}\ge0.
\]
Then the full exact decomposition is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''={}&[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B(\kappa)]I\\
&+\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t
\left\{
\gamma_m I\left(c_t-\frac{v_t}{G_t}\right)
+\gamma_m\left(\frac{Iv_t-(G_t')^2}{G_t}\right)
+G_tE_t
\right\}dt.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{26}
\]
Expansion using \((G_t')^2/G_t=G_t(\operatorname{tr}V_t)^2\) verifies (26). Every term after its first line is nonnegative, with no sign restriction on \(A\):

* The first bracket is the exact expression in (21).
* If \(v_t>0\), the second bracket is
  \[
  \frac{v_t}{G_t}\,
  \mathbb E\left[
  s-\frac{G_t'}{v_t}(Y_t-G_t)
  \right]^2.
  \tag{27}
  \]
  If \(v_t=0\), it is zero, since then \(G_t'=0\).
* The last bracket is the inertia-square identity (17), multiplied by \(G_t>0\).

Thus (26), under the single scalar budget condition (6), is an actual Fisher-compensated certificate for the complete frozen logarithm. It does not allocate the same Fisher information repeatedly without accounting for its total cost: that cost is exactly \(\gamma_m\mathcal B(\kappa)\).

## 5. Strictness and a convex family with unrestricted diagonal motion

Suppose every \(a_i\ne0\), so every \(w_i>0\). Then \(R_t>0\) for every \(t>0\). If \(\mathcal B(\kappa)\le1\) and \(I>0\), the coefficient in (5) is at least \(1/m\), proving \(H''<0\). If \(I=0\), all atom first derivatives vanish, so \(G_t'=0\), and therefore \(\operatorname{tr}V_t=0\). Equation (15) becomes
\[
H''=-\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}tG_t
\operatorname{tr}(V_t^2)\,dt<0
\qquad(A\ne0),
\tag{28}
\]
because invertible congruence preserves nonzeroness. This proves the strict full-Hessian assertion, without assuming that the atom Fisher form itself is positive definite.

The theorem also gives a finite-segment result substantially beyond a fixed-diagonal rank-one line. Fix a nonzero vector \(u\in\mathbb C^n\) and \(0<\delta\le1/2\), and define
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{u,\delta}
=\left\{
\operatorname{diag}(c)+\tau uu^*:
\delta\le c_i\le1-\delta,\quad
|\tau|\|u\|^2\le\frac{7\delta}{9}
\right\}.
}
\tag{29}
\]

**Corollary 3.** Every member of (29) is a strict contraction; its complete ambient Hermitian entropy Hessian is negative semidefinite. Consequently \(K\mapsto H(P_K)\) is concave on the entire convex set \(\mathcal C_{u,\delta}\).

**Proof.** The eigenvalues of every kernel in (29) lie between \(2\delta/9\) and \(1-2\delta/9\). For its odds transformation put
\[
r=\sum_i\frac{|u_i|^2}{c_i},\qquad
s=\sum_i\frac{|u_i|^2}{1-c_i},
\qquad b_i=\frac{u_i}{1-c_i}.
\]
Sherman–Morrison gives exactly
\[
L=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{c_i}{1-c_i}\right)
+\frac{\tau}{1-\tau s}bb^*.
\tag{30}
\]
The normalized nonunit eigenvalue of this rank-one update is
\[
\zeta=\frac{1+\tau r}{1-\tau s}>0,
\qquad \kappa=\max(\zeta,\zeta^{-1}).
\tag{31}
\]
Since \(r,s\le\|u\|^2/\delta\) and
\(|\tau|\|u\|^2/\delta\le7/9\), equations (31) give
\[
\kappa\le\frac{1+7/9}{1-7/9}=8.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(\tau\ne0\), apply Theorem 1. At \(\tau=0\), the product-kernel Hessian is
\(-\sum_i A_{ii}^2/[c_i(1-c_i)]\le0\).
The parameter box in (29) is convex and its map to \(K\) is affine, so integrate this nonpositive Hessian along every contained segment. This proves the corollary.

No eigenvector is held fixed along a general segment in (29). Its direction is
\(A=\operatorname{diag}(\dot c)+\dot\tau uu^*\), and it can have several positive and several negative eigenvalues, up to full rank. The pointwise assertion is stronger still: at each such base the direction \(A\) may be any Hermitian matrix, not merely a tangent to (29).

### An exact rank-six indefinite segment

Take
\[
u=(1,2,1,1,2,1)^T,\qquad \delta=\frac14,
\qquad t_*:=\frac{7}{432},
\]
\[
c^{(0)}=\left(\frac14,\frac13,\frac25,
\frac35,\frac23,\frac34\right),\qquad
c^{(1)}=\left(\frac34,\frac23,\frac35,
\frac25,\frac13,\frac14\right),
\]
\[
K_0=\operatorname{diag}(c^{(0)})+t_*uu^*,\qquad
K_1=\operatorname{diag}(c^{(1)})-t_*uu^*.
\tag{33}
\]
Both endpoints and the entire segment belong to (29). The difference is
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac12,\frac13,\frac15,
-\frac15,-\frac13,-\frac12\right)-\frac{7}{216}uu^*.
\tag{34}
\]
It has inertia exactly \((3,3)\). Indeed, calling the displayed diagonal matrix \(D_A\), symmetry gives \(u^*D_A^{-1}u=0\). Hence the determinant lemma shows that \(D_A-\theta uu^*\) has the same nonzero determinant for every real \(\theta\). Its inertia cannot change along this continuous nonsingular path; at \(\theta=0\) it is \((3,3)\). This supplies an explicit high-rank finite segment, not a rank-two spectral square or a fixed-coordinate principal-block update.
