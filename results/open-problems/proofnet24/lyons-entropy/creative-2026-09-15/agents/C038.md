## 1. The exact costed Hall characterization in arbitrary dimension

Write the bottom, aggregate middle, and top masses as

\[
a_S,\qquad g_M,\qquad c_T,
\]

on ranks \(r,r+1,r+2\), respectively. The totals are \(1,2,1\). A possible diamond is an incidence \(S\subset T\), \(|T\setminus S|=2\). If \(T\setminus S=\{i,j\}\), define its middle cost for a real table \(q\) by

\[
C_q(S,T)=q(Si)+q(Sj).
\tag{1}
\]

Here \(Si=S\cup\{i\}\). Let

\[
\mathcal T(a,c)=\left\{x\geq0:
\sum_{T\supset S}x_{S,T}=a_S,
\quad \sum_{S\subset T}x_{S,T}=c_T\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]

This is just the bottom-to-top inclusion-transport polytope. It can be tested by ordinary weighted Hall inequalities. It does not yet prescribe the middles.

**Theorem 1 (full costed Hall criterion).** A diamond coupling with all three prescribed marginal tables exists if and only if, for every real \(q,\alpha,\beta\) satisfying

\[
\alpha_S+\beta_T\geq C_q(S,T)
\quad(S\subset T),
\tag{3}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\quad
\sum_S\alpha_Sa_S+\sum_T\beta_Tc_T
\geq\sum_Mq_Mg_M.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, if \(\mathcal T(a,c)\ne\varnothing\), the additional necessary and sufficient inequalities are

\[
q\cdot g\leq
\max_{x\in\mathcal T(a,c)}\sum_{S,T}x_{S,T}C_q(S,T)
\quad\text{for every real }q.
\tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** Necessity follows by multiplying (3) by a coupling's nonnegative weights and summing. For sufficiency, apply finite Farkas separation to the system consisting of the bottom equations, top equations, and middle equations. A separating linear functional has coefficients \((\alpha,\beta,-q)\); its nonnegative evaluation on each column is precisely (3), and its negative evaluation on the requested right-hand side contradicts (4). This is the finite duality already justified in old-107, rearranged to isolate the middle cost.

Alternatively, the middle images of \(\mathcal T(a,c)\) form a compact convex polytope. Separation from that polytope gives (5). Ordinary finite transport duality identifies its maximum with the minimum left side of (4) subject to (3). ∎

Taking \(q=0\) recovers only endpoint feasibility. Indicator or single-middle tests are particular choices of \(q\); the theorem requires arbitrary, possibly signed middle potentials. One cannot replace (4) by independently constructed covers on the four sides of a labeled square.

## 2. Six-site coordinates and the forced third pair marginal

Let \(E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). For a compatible flag write

\[
\mathbb C^6=W\oplus U\oplus Z,
\qquad \dim W=\dim U=\dim Z=2,
\]

and choose any orthonormal basis \((u,v)\) of \(U\). Put

\[
a_A=\mu_W(A),\quad h_J=\mu_U(J),\quad c_B=\mu_Z(B),
\qquad g_M=\mu_{W+u}(M)+\mu_{W+v}(M).
\tag{6}
\]

The aggregate \(g\) depends on the plane \(U\), not its chosen orthonormal basis. Complementary minors identify the top law as \(\mu_{W+U}(E\setminus B)=c_B\).

Index a diamond by ordered disjoint pairs \((A,B)\), and put

\[
J=E\setminus(A\cup B),\qquad
M_1=A\cup\{j_1\},\quad M_2=A\cup\{j_2\}.
\tag{7}
\]

There are 90 such atoms. In addition to the bottom and complement-top marginals, **every** valid coupling must have addition-pair law \(h\):

\[
\sum_{(A,B):E\setminus(A\cup B)=J}x_{A,B}=h_J.
\tag{8}
\]

Here is an identity that proves this and is useful even for nondeterminantal data. Define the 15-by-20 pair-triple inclusion matrix

\[
L_{p,M}=\mathbf1_{\{p\subset M\}}.
\]

For every diamond table with three marginal tables \((a,g,c)\), its addition-pair marginal satisfies

\[
\boxed{\quad
h_p=a_p+\sum_{B\cap p=\varnothing}c_B-\sum_{M\supset p}g_M.
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]

For an individual diamond, its alternating evaluation on the pair-inclusion function \(\mathbf1_{\{p\subset X\}}\) is one precisely when \(p=J\), and zero otherwise. Summing proves (9).

For the projection data (6), the right side is exactly \(\mu_U(p)\). Indeed the two-row Cauchy–Binet expansions give

\[
\det(P_W)_p+\det(P_{W+U})_p
-\det(P_{W+u})_p-\det(P_{W+v})_p
=|u\wedge v|_p^2.
\tag{10}
\]

The projection-DPP inclusion formula converts (10) into (9). Consequently the third pair marginal is forced by the original diamond equations. Its nonnegativity is a genuine rank-two determinantal constraint that arbitrary three-layer tables can violate.

## 3. Exactly five middle moments remain

Let \(P\) be the 45-by-90 nonnegative matrix recording the three pair marginals \((A,J,B)\), and let \(G\) be the 20-by-90 matrix recording the two middle vertices. Set

\[
\mathcal H=\ker L\subset\mathbb R^{\binom E3}.
\tag{11}
\]

**Theorem 2 (five-dimensional residual).** The pair-color marginal map has rank 38, \(L\) has rank 15, and the full diamond map has rank 43. If two tables have the same three pair marginals, their middle difference belongs to the five-dimensional space \(\mathcal H\). Conversely, every vector of \(\mathcal H\) is the middle difference of a signed table preserving all three pair marginals.

**Proof of the rank of \(L\).** For a pair table \(y\), let \(s_i=\sum_{j\ne i}y_{ij}\). Counting triples gives

\[
(LL^Ty)_{ij}=2y_{ij}+s_i+s_j,
\]

and hence

\[
y^TLL^Ty=2\sum_{i<j}y_{ij}^2+\sum_i s_i^2.
\tag{12}
\]

This is positive for every nonzero \(y\), so \(\operatorname{rank}L=15\) and \(\dim\mathcal H=20-15=5\).

**Proof of the rank of \(P\).** A left-null vector consists of three pair functions \(f,k,l\) with

\[
f(A)+k(J)+l(B)=0
\quad\text{for every partition }E=A\sqcup J\sqcup B.
\tag{13}
\]

Interchanging the first two pairs shows that \(f-k\) takes the same value on disjoint pairs. The graph of disjoint pairs is connected: intersecting pairs have a common disjoint pair among their three remaining sites. Thus \(f-k\) is constant, and similarly \(f-l\) is constant.

Holding one pair fixed in (13) and exchanging the other four sites gives the four-point equalities

\[
f(ij)+f(kl)=f(ik)+f(jl)=f(il)+f(jk).
\]

They imply \(f(ij)=\lambda_i+\lambda_j\), up to a common constant that can be absorbed into the \(\lambda_i\). For example choose
\(\lambda_0=(f(01)+f(02)-f(12))/2\), then \(\lambda_j=f(0j)-\lambda_0\); the displayed equalities verify all remaining pairs.

Thus all left-null vectors have the form

\[
f(p)=\sum_{i\in p}\lambda_i+c_f,\quad
k(p)=\sum_{i\in p}\lambda_i+c_k,\quad
l(p)=\sum_{i\in p}\lambda_i+c_l,
\tag{14}
\]

where \(\sum_i\lambda_i+c_f+c_k+c_l=0\). There are nine parameters, one constraint, and one redundant simultaneous shift of the \(\lambda_i\) and the constants. The nullspace dimension is seven, so the rank is \(45-7=38\).

Equation (9) proves that equal pair marginals force equal \(LGx\). It remains to realize every harmonic difference; the following explicit trades do this and complete the rank proof. ∎

### 3.1 A unimodular harmonic basis and eight-atom trades

Fix a perfect matching

\[
\mathfrak m=((a_0,a_1),(b_0,b_1),(c_0,c_1)).
\]

A triple is transversal if it takes one site from each matched pair. Define the parity cube

\[
q_{\mathfrak m}(M)=
\begin{cases}
(-1)^{s+t+v},&M=\{a_s,b_t,c_v\},\\
0,&M\text{ is not transversal}.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]

Every pair is either contained in no transversal or in two transversals with opposite signs. Therefore \(Lq_{\mathfrak m}=0\).

The following five matchings give a basis of \(\mathcal H\):

\[
01|23|45,\quad01|24|35,\quad02|13|45,\quad
02|14|35,\quad03|14|25.
\tag{16}
\]

Restricting their five cube columns to rows \(012,013,014,023,024\) gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&0&1\\
0&0&0&1&0\\
0&0&1&0&0\\
0&1&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{17}
\]

whose determinant is one. This proves independence and supplies an integer coordinate system for integral harmonic tables.

For the same matching define a signed table with eight atoms:

\[
F_{\mathfrak m}
=\sum_{s,t,v\in\{0,1\}}-(-1)^{s+t+v}
\,\delta_{\{a_s,b_t\},\{a_{1-s},c_v\}}.
\tag{18}
\]

Holding the bottom pair fixes \(s,t\), and summation over \(v\) cancels. Holding the complement-top fixes \(s,v\), and summation over \(t\) cancels. Holding the addition pair
\(\{b_{1-t},c_{1-v}\}\) fixes \(t,v\), and summation over \(s\) cancels. Thus

\[
PF_{\mathfrak m}=0.
\]

Each atom has one nontransversal middle \(\{a_s,b_0,b_1\}\), whose total signed contribution cancels, and one transversal middle \(\{a_s,b_t,c_{1-v}\}\), with exactly its parity sign. Hence

\[
\boxed{\quad GF_{\mathfrak m}=q_{\mathfrak m}.\quad}
\tag{19}
\]

The five trades corresponding to (16) realize all harmonic middle differences. Therefore adding the middle equations to \(P\) increases its rank by exactly five, to 43. Identity (9) shows that the original diamond equations already contain all rows of \(P\); hence their rank is also 43.

These eight-atom trades are different from C004's marginal-preserving diamond circulations: they deliberately change a specified harmonic middle moment while preserving all three pair-color marginals. They give an exact signed correction, not an automatic nonnegative repair.

Every \(q\in\mathcal H\) is antisymmetric under complementation:

\[
q(M^c)=-q(M).
\tag{20}
\]

Indeed its total, one-coordinate sums, and pair-coordinate sums all vanish. Inclusion–exclusion over the three sites of \(M\) then says that its only triple disjoint from \(M\) has value \(-q(M)\).

### 3.2 The reduced exact Hall test

Let

\[
\mathcal P(a,h,c)=\{x\geq0:Px=(a,h,c)\}.
\]

Assume the algebraic compatibility (9). The full diamond problem is equivalent to the following exact condition: for every \(q\in\mathcal H\) and pair potentials \(f,k,l\) satisfying

\[
f(A)+k(J)+l(B)\geq q(M_1)+q(M_2)
\tag{21}
\]

on all 90 partitions, one has

\[
\boxed{\quad
\sum_Aa_Af(A)+\sum_Jh_Jk(J)+\sum_Bc_Bl(B)
\geq\sum_Mg_Mq(M).
\quad}
\tag{22}
\]

This includes feasibility of \(\mathcal P(a,h,c)\): set \(q=0\) and use Farkas. If that polytope is nonempty, equivalently

\[
q\cdot g\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal P(a,h,c)}q\cdot Gx
\quad(q\in\mathcal H).
\tag{23}
\]

To prove sufficiency, separate the desired harmonic projection of \(g\) from the compact middle image of \(\mathcal P\). All points in that image have the same \(L\)-image by (9); a separating potential can therefore be projected onto \(\ker L\). Conversely, agreement of the \(L\)-image and of all harmonic moments forces full agreement, since
\(\mathbb R^{20}=\ker L\oplus\operatorname{im}L^T\). Ordinary finite LP duality gives (21)–(22).

Thus the residual middle obstruction has exactly five dimensions, but still requires the support function in every direction in those dimensions. Testing just five basis inequalities would not test every linear combination, and is not asserted sufficient.

## 4. A sharp harmonic Hall inequality

Fix a perfect matching \(\mathfrak m\), and use its cube \(q=q_{\mathfrak m}\).

**Theorem 3 (sharp cube inequality).** Every diamond coupling obeys

\[
\boxed{\quad
\left|\sum_Mq(M)g_M\right|
\leq\sum_{J\notin\mathfrak m}h_J.
\quad}
\tag{24}
\]

The constant one on the right cannot be reduced for general graded laws.

**Proof.** Consider a single diamond. If its addition pair \(J\) is a matched pair, its two middle triples are either both nontransversal, or both transversal with opposite parity. Therefore their total cube cost is zero.

If \(J\) is not a matched pair, at most one middle can be transversal. If both were transversal, the two added sites would have to come from the same third matched pair, contradicting the assumption on \(J\). Thus the cost belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The pointwise inequality

\[
|q(M_1)+q(M_2)|\leq\mathbf1_{\{J\notin\mathfrak m\}}
\]

and averaging prove (24).

For sharpness take \(\mathfrak m=01|23|45\), bottom \(A=02\), complement-top \(B=35\), and addition pair \(J=14\). The middles are \(012\), of cube value zero, and \(024\), of cube value one. A unit mass on this diamond attains equality with both sides one. ∎

Equation (24) is a particular exact dual in (21): use \(f=l=0\), \(k(J)=\mathbf1_{\{J\notin\mathfrak m\}}\), and either sign of \(q\). Its determinantal specialization is a necessary inequality involving \(\mu_U\) and the two rank-three projection laws. A proof of that specialization for every compatible flag is **not** supplied here; it is one concrete low-dimensional Hall target for further determinant identities.

## 5. A sharp counterexample to all the weaker marginal tests

Use \(\mathfrak m=01|23|45\) and let \(q\) be its cube. Prescribe uniform bottom and complement-top pair laws,

\[
a_A=c_A=\frac1{15},
\qquad
h_J=\begin{cases}1/3,&J\in\mathfrak m,\\0,&J\notin\mathfrak m.\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]

For \(-1/5\leq t\leq1/5\), prescribe

\[
g_t(M)=
\begin{cases}
1/5+tq(M),&M\text{ transversal},\\
1/30,&M\text{ not transversal}.
\end{cases}
\tag{26}
\]

Let both individually named middle laws be \(\nu_t=g_t/2\).

**Theorem 4 (weaker Hall tests can all pass).** For every \(t\) in that interval:

- \(a,c,\nu_t\) are probability laws and \(g_t\) has mass two;
- the modular balances \(a_i+(\text{top})_i=(g_t)_i\) hold;
- the forced addition law (9) is exactly the nonnegative \(h\) in (25);
- the three pair-color marginals \((a,h,c)\) have a single simultaneous nonnegative partition coupling;
- all four labeled side covers \(a\to\nu_t\) and \(\nu_t\to\text{top}\), repeated for the two equal middle labels, exist.

Nevertheless the full unlabeled diamond coupling exists **if and only if \(t=0\)**.

**Proof of the moments.** There are eight transversal triples and twelve others. The cube has four positive and four negative entries, and has zero pair sums. Hence the total in (26) is \(8/5+12/30=2\). Every coordinate appears in four transversals and six nontransversals, with balanced cube signs, giving \((g_t)_i=4/5+6/30=1\). Uniform bottom and top coordinate marginals are \(1/3\) and \(2/3\), respectively.

A matched pair is contained in four nontransversals, so its middle pair sum is \(2/15\). A cross pair is contained in two transversals of opposite sign and two nontransversals, so its middle pair sum is \(2/5+1/15=7/15\). Since
\(a_p+\sum_{B\cap p=\varnothing}c_B=7/15\), equation (9) gives (25) for every \(t\).

**Simultaneous three-pair coupling.** Define the following 18-atom table:

\[
x^0_{A,B}=
\begin{cases}
1/30,&J\in\mathfrak m\text{ and }A\in\mathfrak m,\\
1/15,&J\in\mathfrak m\text{ and }A\notin\mathfrak m,\\
0,&J\notin\mathfrak m.
\end{cases}
\tag{27}
\]

A matched bottom pair admits two other matched addition pairs, giving row sum \(2/30=1/15\). A cross bottom pair has a unique disjoint matched addition pair, giving row sum \(1/15\). The formula is symmetric between bottom and complement-top, proving the column sums. Each matched addition pair sees four cross bottoms and two matched bottoms, giving \(4/15+2/30=1/3\). Thus (27) realizes \((a,h,c)\) simultaneously.

Its middle law is \(g_0\): a transversal has three cross-bottom contributors, giving \(1/5\); a nontransversal has its unique full matched pair as bottom, giving \(1/30\). Therefore a full diamond exists at \(t=0\).

**All adjacent covers.** It suffices to construct them at \(t=\pm1/5\), since convex mixtures give all intermediate \(t\). At an endpoint of sign \(\sigma\), each cross pair \(A\) is contained in exactly one transversal of cube sign \(\sigma\). Send its entire mass \(1/15\) there. Each matched pair sends \(1/60\) to each of the four triples containing it.

Every positive-sign transversal receives three cross-pair masses, totaling \(1/5=\nu_{\sigma/5}(M)\). Opposite-sign transversals receive zero. Every nontransversal has a unique matched pair and receives \(1/60\). Thus this is a lower inclusion cover with the prescribed marginals.

Complementation sends \(\nu_{-\sigma/5}\) to \(\nu_{\sigma/5}\), because \(q(M^c)=-q(M)\). Complementing the lower cover of the opposite sign therefore gives the upper inclusion cover. Reuse these covers for both equal middle labels. Ordinary endpoint transport also exists, for example (27).

**Failure of the simultaneous diamond.** The right side of (24) is zero, whereas

\[
q\cdot g_t=t\sum_Mq(M)^2=8t.
\tag{28}
\]

Thus a diamond requires \(t=0\). Together with (27), this is the claimed if-and-only-if statement. ∎

This is stronger than an example where only ordinary endpoint Hall fails to see an incompatible middle. Even knowledge of a full simultaneous coloring with the correct three pair laws does not repair the missing harmonic moments.

**Why it is not a determinantal counterexample.** The pair law \(h\) cannot be a rank-two projection law. Its Pluecker coordinates would have nonzero \(\beta_{01}\) and \(\beta_{23}\), while \(\beta_{02},\beta_{03},\beta_{12},\beta_{13}\) all vanish. This contradicts

\[
\beta_{01}\beta_{23}-\beta_{02}\beta_{13}+\beta_{03}\beta_{12}=0.
\tag{29}
\]

Determinantal rank constraints exclude the example. The point is to identify exactly what they must accomplish beyond the weaker Hall tests.

## 6. A constructive one-coordinate recursion for projection flags

For this section the dimension and bottom rank are arbitrary. Let \(W\perp U\), \(U=[u,v]\) have dimension two, and write the contrast measure

\[
b(W,U)=\mu_W+\mu_{W+U}-\mu_{W+u}-\mu_{W+v}.
\tag{30}
\]

Changing the orthonormal basis of \(U\) does not change this measure. If \(e\) is a coordinate, write \(\iota_e\nu\) for the measure obtained by adjoining \(e\) to every set in a law on the other coordinates. This notation denotes a set insertion, not exterior contraction.

**Lemma 5 (coordinate gluing).** Each of the following is an exact nonnegative construction of a full unlabeled diamond coupling, assuming only the lower-dimensional couplings indicated.

### 6.1 No added-plane mass at the coordinate

Suppose \((P_U)_{ee}=0\), and set \(p=(P_W)_{ee}\). For \(0<p<1\), choose

\[
w_0=P_We/\sqrt p,
\qquad W_+=W\cap e^\perp,
\qquad w_0=\sqrt p\,e+\sqrt{1-p}\,w'_0,
\]

and put \(W_-=W_+\oplus[w'_0]\), a subspace on \(E\setminus\{e\}\). The spaces \(W_+,W_-\) have ranks \(r-1,r\) and are both orthogonal to the unchanged plane \(U\). One has the exact contrast identity

\[
\boxed{\quad
b(W,U)=(1-p)b(W_-,U)+p\,\iota_e b(W_+,U).
\quad}
\tag{31}
\]

Thus sample a lower-dimensional diamond of the first type with probability \(1-p\), or of the second type with probability \(p\) and adjoin \(e\) to all four vertices. This gives the requested full coupling.

**Proof.** Every vector of \(U\) vanishes at \(e\). The displayed orthogonal expansion of \(w_0\) shows that for every spectral subset \(J\subseteq\{u,v\}\), the projection law of \(W+[J]\) is

\[
(1-p)\mu_{W_-+[J]}+p\,\iota_e\mu_{W_++[J]}.
\tag{32}
\]

This follows directly by expanding its normalized exterior volume form according to absence or presence of the coordinate \(e\). The two coefficients have squared norms \(1-p,p\), independently of \(J\). Apply the four signs in (30). ∎

At \(p=0\), only the absence branch exists. At \(p=1\), only the presence branch exists. No division by a zero branch weight is needed.

### 6.2 No bottom-plane mass at the coordinate

Suppose \((P_W)_{ee}=0\), and put \(q=(P_U)_{ee}\). If \(0<q<1\), rotate the orthonormal basis of \(U\) so that

\[
u=\sqrt q\,e+\sqrt{1-q}\,u',\qquad v_e=0.
\tag{33}
\]

The unit vectors \(u',v\) are orthogonal to each other and to \(W\), on the remaining coordinates. Then

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
b(W,U)={}&(1-q)b(W,[u',v])\\
&+q\bigl(\mu_W-\mu_{W+v}
 +\iota_e\mu_{W+v}-\iota_e\mu_W\bigr).
\end{aligned}\quad}
\tag{34}
\]

The first term uses a lower-dimensional rank-\(r\) diamond. The second is always a positive mixture of diamonds: take **one** inclusion coupling

\[
S\subset M,\qquad S\sim\mu_W,\quad M\sim\mu_{W+v}.
\tag{35}
\]

If \(M\setminus S=\{j\}\), use the diamond with bottom \(S\), top \(M\cup\{e\}\), and middles \(M,S\cup\{e\}\). Mix these two constructions with probabilities \(1-q,q\).

**Proof.** Expanding (33) gives

\[
\mu_{W+u}=(1-q)\mu_{W+u'}+q\,\iota_e\mu_W,
\]

\[
\mu_{W+u+v}=(1-q)\mu_{W+u'+v}+q\,\iota_e\mu_{W+v}.
\]

The other two laws are supported away from \(e\). Rearrangement is precisely (34). The one coupling (35) gives every marginal of the second term simultaneously, as seen from its four explicit vertices. ∎

For \(q=0\), only the lower-dimensional term is used. For \(q=1\), take \(u=e\) and use only (35).

For completeness, the cover (35) can be constructed without an unproved coupling assertion. Let \(\alpha\) be a unit exterior volume form for \(W\), and \(\zeta=v\wedge\alpha\). For any family \(\mathcal A\) of rank-\(r\) coordinate sets, let \(N(\mathcal A)\) be its rank-\(r+1\) inclusion neighborhood. Exterior contraction by the unit vector \(v\) gives

\[
\|P_{\mathcal A}\alpha\|^2
=\|P_{\mathcal A}\iota_vP_{N(\mathcal A)}\zeta\|^2
\leq\|P_{N(\mathcal A)}\zeta\|^2.
\tag{36}
\]

These are all weighted Hall inequalities. Exact max flow, or C033's sum/min construction, supplies (35). This is one cover inside a proved gluing identity, not an unsupported gluing of separately specified side covers.

### 6.3 The complementary endpoint case

Let \(Z=(W+U)^\perp\). If \((P_Z)_{ee}=0\), complement every set. The four projection laws become the flag with bottom \(Z\), added plane \(U\), and top \(W^\perp\), with the two middle labels interchanged. Apply Section 6.2 there and complement each resulting diamond back. Complements preserve the elementary-diamond property.

## 7. The closed six-site boundary theorem

**Theorem 6 (one missing color in six sites).** For every complex orthogonal decomposition

\[
\mathbb C^6=W\oplus U\oplus Z,
\qquad \dim W=\dim U=\dim Z=2,
\]

if some coordinate \(e\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{\quad
(P_W)_{ee}(P_U)_{ee}(P_Z)_{ee}=0,
\quad}
\tag{37}
\]

then the four compatible projection laws have a full unlabeled diamond coupling. Consequently their alternating expectation is nonnegative for every supermodular set function.

**Proof.** If the zero factor is \((P_U)_{ee}\), (31) uses five-site flags of bottom ranks one and two. Bottom-rank-one coupling existence is established prior work; bottom rank two on five sites is its complement. C033 gives exact constructive transport in either case.

If the zero factor is \((P_W)_{ee}\), (34) uses a five-site bottom-rank-two flag, again the complement of a rank-one flag, and the single covering construction (36). If the zero factor is \((P_Z)_{ee}\), use Section 6.3. All branch weights are nonnegative, and every branch prescribes all diamond marginals simultaneously. Pairing the resulting positive diamond measure with a supermodular function gives the final assertion. ∎

This family has no smallness assumption and allows arbitrary complex phases. It includes flags whose bottom and top laws have all 15 atoms positive. It is not asserted to contain a neighborhood of any frame satisfying (37): a perturbation that makes all three coordinate masses positive is outside this theorem.

The usual entropy consequence is immediate by the same argument as C012, Section 6. For any flag covered by Theorem 6, if

\[
K(t)=P_W+\lambda(t)uu^*+\mu(t)vv^*,
\quad \lambda,\mu\in[0,1]\text{ affine},
\quad \lambda'\mu'\leq0,
\tag{38}
\]

then \(H(\mathbb P_{K(t)})\) is concave. Indeed \(p''=2\lambda'\mu'b\); a positive diamond decomposition gives \(\sum b_S\log p_S\leq0\) by the determinantal atom inequality \(p_{Si}p_{Sj}\geq p_Sp_{Sij}\). Both terms in
\(H''=-\sum(p'_S)^2/p_S-2\lambda'\mu'\sum b_S\log p_S\)
are nonpositive. Positive spectral coefficients ensure every positive coupling atom uses positive law atoms; continuity handles the spectral-square boundary. This is a restricted fixed-flag theorem, not unrestricted Lyons concavity.

## 8. An exact non-block example and reproducible certificate

Take the real orthogonal frame
