# C059 — Independent leaf amplitudes around a free two-dimensional core

Creative proof, 15 September 2026. Recovered from C059's saved exact computation after the session transition. This is one completed C059 task, not an additional agent execution. The general Lyons conjecture remains open; historical priority is not asserted.

**Proved result.** Fix independent leaf marginals and one coupling direction per leaf. Determinantal entropy is jointly concave in every complex leaf amplitude and a freely varying Hermitian two-coordinate core. The amplitudes need not change proportionally, have the same phase, or have radial derivatives of the same sign. Consequently, the result covers arbitrary-size double-star trees with fixed leaf diagonals and every other permitted entry free. The same proof allows the measured core to occupy any fixed two-dimensional support, using the support entropy theorem from `ROOT_RANK_TWO_SUPPORT.md`, reproduced below in the form needed here.

The proof has two ingredients: conditioning makes the core affine in the squared amplitudes, and classical resampling of one leaf makes entropy nonincreasing in each squared amplitude. Their composition controls all mixed directions.

## 1. Family and theorem

Let there be \(m\) leaves and a two-coordinate core. Fix
\[
0<d_i<1,\qquad w_i\in\mathbb C^2,\qquad Q_i=w_iw_i^*,
\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
For \(A=A^*\in\mathbb C^{2\times2}\) and \(z\in\mathbb C^m\), set
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m),\qquad
B(z)_{i,:}=z_iw_i^*,\qquad
K(A,z)=\begin{pmatrix}D&B(z)\\B(z)^*&A\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]
Write \(\mathcal H(K)=H(P_K)\) for natural-log Shannon entropy of the determinantal occupation law.

**Theorem 1.** The function
\[
\boxed{(A,z)\longmapsto\mathcal H(K(A,z))}
\tag{2}
\]
is jointly concave on the convex domain \(0\le K(A,z)\le I\), treating each complex amplitude as two real variables. In particular, for any feasible endpoints and \(0\le t\le1\),
\[
\mathcal H\bigl(K((1-t)A_0+tA_1,(1-t)z^0+tz^1)\bigr)
\ge(1-t)\mathcal H(K(A_0,z^0))+t\mathcal H(K(A_1,z^1)).
\tag{3}
\]
If some \(w_i\ne0\) and \(z_i^0\ne z_i^1\), this inequality is strict for every \(0<t<1\), including feasible boundary endpoints.

The fixed leaf diagonals may also lie in \([0,1]\): positivity of \(K\) forces the incident row of a leaf with \(d_i=0\) to vanish, and positivity of \(I-K\) does the same when \(d_i=1\). Delete these deterministic, decoupled coordinates and apply the theorem.

## 2. Exact conditioning and the strength domain

Put \(s_i=|z_i|^2\). Since \(D\) and \(I-D\) are positive definite, the two Schur complements give the exact feasibility conditions
\[
L(A,s):=A-\sum_i\frac{s_i}{d_i}Q_i\ge0,
\qquad
U(A,s):=I_2-A-\sum_i\frac{s_i}{1-d_i}Q_i\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the strength domain
\[
\Omega=\{(A,s):s_i\ge0,\ L(A,s)\ge0,\ U(A,s)\ge0\}
\tag{5}
\]
is convex. It is downward closed in the strengths: if \((A,s)\in\Omega\) and \(0\le\widetilde s\le s\) coordinatewise, then \((A,\widetilde s)\in\Omega\). Conversely, every point of \(\Omega\) is realized by (1), for example with \(z_i=\sqrt{s_i}\).

The leaf occupation vector \(x\in\{0,1\}^m\) has the fixed product law
\[
\mu(x)=\prod_i d_i^{x_i}(1-d_i)^{1-x_i}.
\]
Define
\[
r_d(1)=\frac1d,\qquad r_d(0)=-\frac1{1-d},\qquad
M_x(A,s)=A-\sum_i s_i r_{d_i}(x_i)Q_i.
\tag{6}
\]
Conditioned on the complete leaf configuration \(x\), the core has determinantal kernel \(M_x\).

For completeness, if \(T\) is a set of core coordinates and \(O=\{i:x_i=0\}\), inclusion-exclusion on the absent leaves gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(T\subseteq X_{\rm core},X_{\rm leaves}=x)
&=(-1)^{|O|}\det\begin{pmatrix}
D-I_O&B_{:,T}\\B_{:,T}^*&A_T
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\mu(x)\det(M_x)_T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The inverse of the diagonal matrix \(D-I_O\) has entries \(r_{d_i}(x_i)\), proving the formula. No inverse of \(A\) is used.

Every conditional matrix is a positive contraction. Indeed,
\[
M_x=L(A,s)+\sum_{i:x_i=0}\frac{s_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}Q_i\ge0,
\]
\[
I_2-M_x=U(A,s)+\sum_{i:x_i=1}\frac{s_i}{d_i(1-d_i)}Q_i\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(H_2(M)=H(P_M)\) for a two-site kernel, and let \(h(d)=-d\log d-(1-d)\log(1-d)\). The entropy chain rule now reads
\[
F(A,s):=\mathcal H(K(A,z))
=\sum_i h(d_i)+\sum_x\mu(x)H_2(M_x(A,s)).
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, the law and its entropy depend on the amplitudes only through \(s\). The phases disappear from the actual conditional occupation kernels, not just from an entropy bound.

The two-site entropy theorem says that \(H_2\) is concave on \([0,I_2]\). A self-contained proof, also establishing the stronger fixed-support input, appears in Section 7. Since each \(M_x\) is affine in \((A,s)\), equation (9) proves:
\[
\boxed{F\text{ is jointly concave on }\Omega.}
\tag{10}
\]

## 3. A single leaf is an exact classical reset channel

For a full occupation law \(P\), define the reset of leaf \(i\) by
\[
(R_iP)(x_i,y)=\mu_i(x_i)P_{-i}(y),\qquad
\mu_i(1)=d_i,\quad\mu_i(0)=1-d_i,
\tag{11}
\]
where \(y\) contains every coordinate except that leaf. This operation redraws the leaf independently with its original marginal and preserves the joint law of all remaining coordinates.

Fix \(A\) and all strengths except \(s_i\). Every atom of the full determinantal law is affine in \(s_i\). One proof uses (6): for fixed \(x\), the conditional matrix changes by a rank-one matrix \(-s_i r_{d_i}(x_i)Q_i\); every principal determinant is affine in a rank-one update, and exact atom probabilities are linear combinations of these determinants. Equivalently, expand each full inclusion determinant along the scaled leaf row and column.

At \(s_i=0\), the leaf is a decoupled Bernoulli coordinate. Its complementary principal kernel, and hence \(P_{-i}\), did not change. Therefore this zero-strength law is exactly \(R_iP\). Consequently, for \(0\le\lambda\le1\),
\[
\boxed{P_{(A,s_1,\ldots,\lambda s_i,\ldots,s_m)}
=\lambda P+(1-\lambda)R_iP.}
\tag{12}
\]

Set \(Q=R_iP\). Because \(Q\) is the product of the two marginals of \(P\),
\[
H(Q)-H(P)=D(P\Vert Q)
=I_P(X_i;X_{-i})\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
Ordinary Shannon concavity applied to (12) gives
\[
H(\lambda P+(1-\lambda)Q)-H(P)
\ge(1-\lambda)D(P\Vert Q)\ge0.
\tag{14}
\]
This proves that \(F(A,s)\) is nonincreasing in each strength throughout its physical domain. Repeatedly applying (14) proves
\[
0\le\widetilde s\le s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(A,\widetilde s)\ge F(A,s).
\tag{15}
\]
The conclusion uses a literal stochastic reset and holds at boundary kernels as well.

At a strict physical point, this monotonicity has an exact differential form. Define the Jeffreys divergence with no factor of one half:
\[
J(P,Q):=D(P\Vert Q)+D(Q\Vert P)
=\sum_\omega(P_\omega-Q_\omega)\log\frac{P_\omega}{Q_\omega}.
\tag{16}
\]
Affineness and (12) imply \(s_i\partial_{s_i}P=P-R_iP\). Furthermore,
\[
\sum_\omega(P_\omega-Q_\omega)\log Q_\omega=0,
\]
since \(\log Q(x_i,y)=\log\mu_i(x_i)+\log P_{-i}(y)\) and both corresponding marginals agree. Hence
\[
\boxed{\partial_{s_i}F(A,s)=-\frac{J(P,R_iP)}{s_i}\le0
\qquad(s_i>0).}
\tag{17}
\]

## 4. Joint complex-amplitude concavity and strictness

For the two endpoints in Theorem 1, write
\[
A_t=(1-t)A_0+tA_1,\quad z_t=(1-t)z^0+tz^1,
\]
\[
\overline s_t=(1-t)|z^0|^2+t|z^1|^2,\qquad
s_t=|z_t|^2,
\]
with coordinatewise squared moduli. The elementary identity
\[
\overline s_{t,i}-s_{t,i}=t(1-t)|z_i^1-z_i^0|^2\ge0
\tag{18}
\]
is the entire composition step. Convexity of \(\Omega\) makes \((A_t,\overline s_t)\) feasible; downward closure makes \((A_t,s_t)\) feasible. Equations (10) and (15) then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H(K(A_t,z_t))
&=F(A_t,s_t)\\
&\ge F(A_t,\overline s_t)\\
&\ge(1-t)F(A_0,|z^0|^2)+tF(A_1,|z^1|^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
This proves the theorem for all closed-domain endpoints directly. It places no restriction on signs of \(d|z_i(t)|^2/dt\), on phases, or on the simultaneous change in \(A\).

There is also a useful strict chord bound. Choose any \(i\) with \(\overline s_{t,i}>0\). Let \(\overline P_t\) be the law at \((A_t,\overline s_t)\). Reduce its \(i\)-th strength to \(s_{t,i}\) first and then reduce the others. Equation (14) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal H(K(A_t,z_t))
 -(1-t)\mathcal H(K(A_0,z^0))-t\mathcal H(K(A_1,z^1))\\
&\qquad\ge
\frac{t(1-t)|z_i^1-z_i^0|^2}{\overline s_{t,i}}
I_{\overline P_t}(X_i;X_{-i}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
If \(w_i\ne0\) and \(\overline s_{t,i}>0\), the leaf is dependent on at least one core coordinate: the determinantal covariance identity gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_{m+a})=-\overline s_{t,i}|(w_i)_a|^2
\]
for some nonzero component of \(w_i\). Its mutual information with the rest is therefore strictly positive. When \(z_i^0\ne z_i^1\) and \(0<t<1\), equation (18) gives \(\overline s_{t,i}>0\) and a positive coefficient in (20), proving the stated strictness even at boundary endpoints.

## 5. Exact curvature decomposition for every mixed slope

Consider an affine path \(A(t)=A+tE\), \(z_i(t)=z_i+t\xi_i\), with \(E=E^*\) arbitrary and \(\xi_i\in\mathbb C\) arbitrary. At a point with the two Schur matrices in (4) positive definite,
\[
M_x'=E-\sum_i2\operatorname{Re}(\overline z_i\xi_i)
r_{d_i}(x_i)Q_i,
\qquad
M_x''=-2\sum_i|\xi_i|^2r_{d_i}(x_i)Q_i.
\tag{21}
\]
Differentiate (9) twice and use (17). With \(P=P_{K(A,z)}\), this gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
-\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathcal H(K(A(t),z(t)))
=\mathbb E_{x\sim\mu}\!left[-D^2H_2(M_x)[M_x',M_x']\right]
 +2\sum_i|\xi_i|^2\frac{J(P,R_iP)}{|z_i|^2}.
}
\tag{22}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative. The first term includes all interactions between the free core direction and the radial amplitude slopes; it is the complete conditional entropy Hessian, without discarding mixed terms. The second term is the exact entropy cost of the quadratic acceleration of the squared amplitudes.

The formula extends through \(z_i=0\) at a strict physical point by assigning the corresponding quotient its limit, which is zero. Indeed, with the other parameters fixed, write \(P(s_i)=Q+s_iV\), where \(Q=R_iP\) has strictly positive atoms and \(V\) has zero relevant marginals. Then
\[
J(Q+s_iV,Q)=s_i^2\sum_\omega\frac{V_\omega^2}{Q_\omega}+O(s_i^3),
\]
so \(J/s_i\to0\). Equivalently, \(\partial_{s_i}F(A,0)=0\).

If \(z_i\ne0\), \(w_i\ne0\), and \(\xi_i\ne0\) for some leaf, its Jeffreys term is strictly positive by the covariance argument above. Thus (22) gives strict local curvature whenever a nonzero coupling amplitude changes, regardless of the other slopes. At a boundary where entropy is not twice differentiable, the finite chord proof (19)–(20) supplies the theorem without assigning values to divergent Hessian terms.

## 6. Arbitrary-size double-star trees

A double-star has two adjacent centers, and every other vertex is adjacent to exactly one of them. Take these centers as the two core coordinates, set \(w_i=e_1\) for leaves at the first center and \(w_i=e_2\) for leaves at the second, and allow the core matrix \(A\) to vary freely.

**Corollary 2.** In every dimension, determinantal entropy is jointly concave on the contraction slice consisting of kernels supported on a fixed double-star, with fixed leaf diagonal entries. Both center diagonals, the complex center-to-center edge, and every complex leaf edge may vary independently and simultaneously. Zero edges and double-star subgraphs are included.

This is not restricted to a single common scalar multiplying the cross matrix. In particular, with at least one changing leaf at each center, the cross direction can have rank two. The complete Hermitian direction then has rank four and inertia \((2,2,m-2)\): split off the kernel of the cross matrix and use a congruence to reduce the remaining block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&C\\C^*&E\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with \(C\) invertible, to \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I_2\\I_2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Thus the corollary includes directions beyond the earlier inertia-\((1,1)\) theorem.

It does not prove arbitrary tree support, variable leaf diagonals, or leaf rows whose directions \(w_i\) change independently of their scalar amplitudes.

## 7. The core may occupy any fixed two-dimensional support

Here is the exact extension and its concavity input. Let \(V:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb C^q\) be any fixed isometry in the measured coordinate basis. Replace (1) by
\[
K_V(A,z)=\begin{pmatrix}
D&B(z)V^*\\VB(z)^*&VAV^*
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus the measured core has arbitrary size, its kernel stays on \(\operatorname{ran}V\), and every core coupling vector lies in that same fixed support. This is an exact support restriction; an arbitrary fixed background on its orthogonal complement is not part of this theorem.

**Corollary 3.** Theorems (2)–(3), their strictness statement, and the reset formulas remain valid for \(K_V\). In (9) and (22), replace \(H_2(M)\) by
\[
\Gamma_V(M):=H(P_{VMV^*}).
\tag{24}
\]
No invariance of occupation entropy under unitary changes of coordinates is assumed.

The Schur feasibility conditions are still exactly (4); this is a statement about operator positivity on \(\mathbb C^m\oplus\operatorname{ran}V\), with a zero kernel on the remaining support. Conditioning gives the actual measured-core kernel \(VM_xV^*\). The reset proof still applies because \(VQ_iV^*\) has rank at most one. The only additional input is concavity of \(\Gamma_V\), which is the already established root support theorem. The following proof records it here to make the present argument self-contained.

**Rank-two support lemma.** For every fixed isometry \(V\), \(\Gamma_V\) is concave on \(0\le M\le I_2\).

Write \(v_a\) for row \(a\) of \(V\), and set
\[
t_a=\|v_a\|^2,\qquad \sigma_a=t_a/2,\qquad
\rho_{ab}=|\det V_{\{a,b\}}|^2.
\]
Cauchy–Binet and the isometry identity give
\[
\sum_a t_a=2,\qquad\sum_{a<b}\rho_{ab}=1,
\qquad\sum_{b\ne a}\rho_{ab}=t_a.
\tag{25}
\]
At \(0<M<I_2\), put
\[
d=\det M,\qquad d_0=\det(I_2-M),\qquad C=M-dI_2.
\]
The only possibly nonzero atoms are
\[
p_\varnothing=d_0,\qquad p_a=v_aCv_a^*,\qquad
p_{ab}=d\rho_{ab}.
\tag{26}
\]
These follow directly from the inclusion determinants: rank two forbids three occupied core coordinates, and subtracting the pair inclusion probabilities from each one-point inclusion probability uses (25). If the eigenvalues of \(M\) are \(p,q\in(0,1)\), the eigenvalues of \(C\) are \(p(1-q),q(1-p)\), so \(C>0\) and \(\det C=dd_0\). Rows with \(t_a=0\) and pairs with \(\rho_{ab}=0\) are fixed zero atoms and are omitted below.

For a Hermitian direction \(E\), let \(\delta=\det E\). Equation (26) yields
\[
p_\varnothing''=2\delta,\qquad p_a''=-2\delta t_a,
\qquad p_{ab}''=2\delta\rho_{ab}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Gamma_V''=-\mathcal I+2\delta\Lambda,
\quad \mathcal I=\sum_S\frac{(p_S')^2}{p_S},
\quad
\Lambda=\sum_{a<b:\rho_{ab}>0}\rho_{ab}
\log\frac{p_ap_b}{d_0d\rho_{ab}}.
\tag{27}
\]
The two-row Gram determinant gives
\[
p_ap_b-dd_0\rho_{ab}=|v_aCv_b^*|^2\ge0,
\tag{28}
\]
so \(\Lambda\ge0\), proving \(\Gamma_V''\le0\) whenever \(\delta\le0\).

For \(\delta>0\), reverse \(E\) if necessary so that \(E>0\). Put
\[
f=\operatorname{tr}C,\qquad \nu_a=p_a/f,\qquad
T=\frac{f}{2\sqrt{dd_0}}\ge1.
\]
The inequality follows from \(f^2-4dd_0=(p-q)^2\). Give the pair law \(\rho\) a fair random ordering. Both ordered marginals equal \(\sigma\), and its entropy is \(H(\rho)+\log2\). Shannon subadditivity gives
\[
\beta:=2H(\sigma)-H(\rho)\ge\log2.
\]
Rearranging (27) using (25),
\[
\Lambda=2\log(2T)-\beta-2D(\sigma\Vert\nu)
\le2\log T+\log2.
\tag{29}
\]
The empty atom and all pair atoms alone supply
\[
\mathcal I\ge
\frac{\bigl(\operatorname{tr}((\operatorname{adj}M)E)\bigr)^2}{d}
+\frac{\bigl(\operatorname{tr}((\operatorname{adj}(I_2-M))E)\bigr)^2}{d_0}.
\]
Set \(P=\operatorname{adj}M/\sqrt d\) and \(Q=\operatorname{adj}(I_2-M)/\sqrt{d_0}\). They are positive definite with determinant one, and the two-dimensional determinant identity gives \(\det(P+Q)=2+2T\). Cauchy–Schwarz and the trace–determinant inequality for the positive matrix \((P+Q)^{1/2}E(P+Q)^{1/2}\) imply
\[
\mathcal I\ge\tfrac12[\operatorname{tr}((P+Q)E)]^2
\ge2\det(P+Q)\det E=4(1+T)\delta.
\tag{30}
\]
Combining (27), (29), and (30),
\[
\Gamma_V''\le-4\delta\left(1+T-\log T-\tfrac12\log2\right)
\le-(8-2\log2)\delta<0.
\tag{31}
\]
This proves the Hessian sign for every direction at every strict \(M\). Integrating and then replacing endpoints by \((1-2\epsilon)M+\epsilon I_2\) proves concavity on the closed domain, since every atom is polynomial and \(-p\log p\) is continuous at zero. For the ordinary two-site case \(V=I_2\), one has \(\beta=2\log2\), improving (29) to \(\Lambda\le2\log T\) and (31) to the familiar \(-8\det E\) bound.

This completes the input needed for Corollary 3. In its relative interior, all retained atoms in (26) are positive, so (22) with \(\Gamma_V\) holds after omitting the fixed zero atoms. If \(w_i\ne0\), then \(Vw_i\ne0\), so the measured-coordinate covariance argument also proves the same strict chord statement.

## 9. Scope relative to the prior frontier

Prior section 6 already proves a free two-coordinate core with its entire cross matrix multiplied by one common complex scalar and an arbitrary fixed complementary block. C059 instead fixes a diagonal complementary block and permits one independently varying complex amplitude per leaf, with a fixed row direction. These restrictions are different: this result extends the scalar-amplitude freedom for independent leaves, while retaining that structural restriction on the complementary block. The displayed six-site endpoints have unequal complex row-scaling ratios and are not a common-scalar cross interpolation.

The two-site core theorem is prior work. The arbitrary measured two-dimensional support theorem is the established root result, not a C059 novelty claim. C059's completed construction is their combination with independent leaf conditioning, strength-domain concavity, exact leaf reset monotonicity, and the resulting simultaneous-amplitude theorem and curvature identity.

No general positive-contraction entropy theorem, arbitrary coherent satellite-block theorem, or proof for all trees is claimed. No remaining lemma is required for Theorem 1 or its stated corollaries.
