## 2. Pair score projectors and the matrix budget

Let \(0<K<I_n\), and suppose the frozen atom log weight has the form
\[
-\log p_S=\text{constant}+\sum_{i\in S}a_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}c_{ij},\qquad c_{ij}\ge0.
\tag{4}
\]
There are no interactions of degree three or higher in (4). This condition is required only at the base, not along the direction.

For each pair \(e=ij\), write its four marginal masses as \(x_e,y_e,z_e,w_e\) in the order \(00,10,01,11\), and set
\[
T_e=\frac{y_e+z_e}{2\sqrt{x_ew_e}},\qquad
D_e=2(1+T_e),\qquad C_e=2D_e=4(1+T_e).
\tag{5}
\]
The pair DPP inequality gives \(T_e\ge1\). For the marginal-pair Fisher information \(I_e(A)\), C013's exact square identity supplies
\[
I_e(A)-C_e\det A_e\ge0
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A_e.
\tag{6}
\]
This is valid for indefinite pair directions as well.

Let \(u\in\mathbb R^{2^n}\) have entries \(u_S=\sqrt{p_S}\). For a pair configuration \(h\subseteq e\), put
\(p_e(h)=\sum_{S:S\cap e=h}p_S\). Define the symmetric matrix
\[
(P_e)_{ST}
=\frac{\sqrt{p_Sp_T}}{p_e(S\cap e)}
  \mathbf1_{S\cap e=T\cap e}-\sqrt{p_Sp_T}.
\tag{7}
\]
This is the orthogonal projector onto the centered functions of \(X\cap e\), under the identification \(g\mapsto(\sqrt{p_S}g(S))_S\). In particular,
\[
P_e^2=P_e,\qquad P_e\ge0,\qquad P_eu=0,\qquad\|P_e\|=1.
\tag{8}
\]
These assertions follow directly by conditioning twice and subtracting the constant projection.

Put \(N(S)=|S|\), \(\bar N=\mathbb EN\), and
\[
\nu_S=\sqrt{p_S}(N(S)-\bar N),\qquad
v=\|\nu\|^2=\operatorname{tr}(K-K^2).
\tag{9}
\]
For any \(t\) in \(0\le t\le m:=\min_e c_e\), define
\[
f_e(t)=\frac{c_e-t}{D_e}\ge0,
\qquad
\boxed{B_t=t\nu\nu^{\mathsf T}+\sum_e f_e(t)P_e.}
\tag{10}
\]
The matrix is positive semidefinite and annihilates \(u\). All its inputs are atom probabilities and their pair marginals.

### Theorem 1: overlap-aware pair allocation

If
\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\max}(B_t)\le\beta\le1}
\tag{11}
\]
for some \(t\in[0,m]\), then, for every Hermitian \(A\),
\[
\boxed{-D^2H(K)[A,A]\ge(1-\beta)\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+t\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{12}
\]
In particular \(t>0\) gives a negative definite full Hessian.

**Proof.** Write \(q_S=Dp_S[A]\) and \(z_S=q_S/\sqrt{p_S}\). Normalization gives \(z\perp u\), and
\[
\|z\|^2=I:=\operatorname{FI}_K(A),\quad
I_e=z^{\mathsf T}P_ez,\quad
\operatorname{tr}A=\nu^{\mathsf T}z.
\tag{13}
\]
The marginal-score identity in (13) is just differentiation of each sum defining \(p_e(h)\). The last identity differentiates \(\mathbb E|X|=\operatorname{tr}K\).

From the frozen-log formula and (4),
\[
-D^2H=I-2\sum_e c_e\det A_e.
\tag{14}
\]
The identity \(2\sum_{i<j}\det A_{ij}=(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\|A\|_F^2\) gives the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
-D^2H
=z^{\mathsf T}(\mathrm{Id}-B_t)z+t\|A\|_F^2
+\sum_e f_e(t)\{I_e-C_e\det A_e\}.}
\tag{15}
\]
Every final bracket is nonnegative by (6). Condition (11) now proves (12). This proof retains the complete pair Fisher quadratic forms, including mixed diagonal/off-diagonal directions. \(\square\)

### Why this is stronger than the scalar budget

Since \(\|\nu\nu^{\mathsf T}\|=v\), the previous scalar estimate is
\[
\lambda_{\max}(B_t)\le tv+\sum_e f_e(t)=:S(t).
\tag{16}
\]
C013 uses \(t=m\). Merely allowing all \(t\in[0,m]\) improves it; since \(S(t)\) is affine, its best scalar value is at one of the endpoints. Theorem 1 uses the actual matrix sum, and the example in Section 5 separates it from **both** endpoints. It does not simply adjust the former scalar constants.

Moreover \(B_t\) is affine in \(t\), so its largest eigenvalue is a convex function of one real variable. Thus finding the best shared coefficient is a one-dimensional convex optimization, with a rigorous final certificate obtainable by rational upper bounds as below. No optimization is needed to verify a proposed \(t\).

### The logarithmic means are explicit

For \(\mathcal L(a,b)=(a-b)/(\log a-\log b)\), continuously extended on the diagonal, put \(u_e=e^{-c_e}\) and \(a=e^{-t}\). Then
\[
f_e(t)=\frac{a-u_e}{D_e\mathcal L(a,u_e)},\qquad
 t=\frac{1-a}{\mathcal L(1,a)}.
\tag{17}
\]
Under (4), \(u_e=p_\varnothing p_{ij}/(p_ip_j)\), equally using any fixed outside atom fiber. Equation (10) is therefore a positive matrix combination of exact divided differences between paired DPP atom products. At equality of the two inputs, the coefficient is interpreted continuously as zero.

### A diagonal-weighted version

Choose numbers \(h_i\ge0\) with \(h_ih_j\le c_{ij}\), and let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(h_i)\). Replace \(\nu\) by
\[
(\nu_h)_S=\sqrt{p_S}\left(\sum_{i\in S}h_i-\mathbb E\sum_{i\in X}h_i\right),
\]
and set
\[
B_h=\nu_h\nu_h^{\mathsf T}
 +\sum_{i<j}\frac{c_{ij}-h_ih_j}{D_{ij}}P_{ij}.
\tag{18}
\]
Then
\[
\lambda_{\max}(B_h)\le\beta\le1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-D^2H\ge(1-\beta)I+\operatorname{tr}(DADA).
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed,
\(2\sum_{i<j}h_ih_j\det A_{ij}=(\operatorname{tr}DA)^2-\operatorname{tr}(DADA)\), and \(\nu_h^{\mathsf T}z=\operatorname{tr}DA\). The last term in (19) is \(\|D^{1/2}AD^{1/2}\|_F^2\ge0\). This proves (19) by exactly the identity (15), with the stated replacements. The shared choice \(h_i=\sqrt t\) recovers Theorem 1.

## 3. A nonzero-triple extension with a sharp residual constant

Now take any interior three-site kernel. Use the standard coefficients
\[
c_{ij}=\log\frac{p_ip_j}{p_\varnothing p_{ij}},\qquad
\tau=\log\frac{p_\varnothing p_{12}p_{13}p_{23}}{p_1p_2p_3p_{123}}.
\]
As in old-097, \(c_{ij}\ge0\) and \(c_{ij}+\tau\ge0\). Put
\[
\bar c_{ij}=c_{ij}+\tau/2\ge0,
\qquad E=\tau(K-I/2).
\tag{20}
\]
The exact frozen identity is
\[
-D^2H=I-2\sum_{i<j}\bar c_{ij}\det A_{ij}
               -2\operatorname{tr}(E\operatorname{adj}A).
\tag{21}
\]
Construct \(B_t\) by (10), now replacing \(c_{ij}\) by \(\bar c_{ij}\), and requiring \(0\le t\le\min\bar c_{ij}\).

Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) be the eigenvalues of \(E\), and define
\[
R(e)=
\begin{pmatrix}0&e_3&e_2\\e_3&0&e_1\\e_2&e_1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r(E)=\lambda_{\max}(R(e)).
\tag{22}
\]
This is independent of the eigenvalue order. Equivalently, it is the largest root of
\[
r^3-(e_1^2+e_2^2+e_3^2)r-2e_1e_2e_3=0.
\tag{23}
\]

### Lemma: exact adjugate residual norm

For any Hermitian \(E\),
\[
\boxed{\sup_{A\ne0}\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(E\operatorname{adj}A)}{\|A\|_F^2}=r(E).}
\tag{24}
\]
To prove it, diagonalize \(E\); adjugates respect unitary conjugation and the Frobenius norm is invariant. On the real diagonal entries of \(A\), the quadratic matrix is \(R(e)\). On the two real coordinates of each complex off-diagonal entry \(A_{jk}\), its eigenvalue relative to the Frobenius norm is \(-e_i\). Each \(e_i\) occurs as an off-diagonal entry of a two-by-two principal submatrix of \(R(e)\), so the largest eigenvalue of \(R(e)\) is at least \(|e_i|\). It therefore dominates every off-diagonal eigenvalue. Equality is attained by a diagonal eigenvector of \(R(e)\), proving (24).

In particular,
\[
r(E)\le2\|E\|_{\rm op}=2|\tau|\|K-I/2\|_{\rm op},
\tag{25}
\]
by the row-sum bound. Thus (24) is never worse than the residual estimate in C013.

### Theorem 2: general three-site sufficient certificate

If
\[
\lambda_{\max}(B_t)\le\beta\le1,
\qquad t\ge r\bigl(\tau(K-I/2)\bigr),
\tag{26}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{-D^2H(K)[A,A]\ge(1-\beta)I+
\left(t-r\bigl(\tau(K-I/2)\bigr)\right)\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{27}
\]
Apply decomposition (15) to the first three terms of (21), then (24) to its final term. This proves the theorem.

The coefficients remain exact logarithmic-mean coefficients: \(e^{-\bar c_{ij}}\) is the geometric mean of the inverse pair odds ratios in the two outside fibers, and (17) applies unchanged. The theorem allows arbitrary complex phases. Strict inequalities persist in an ambient neighborhood, so (1) and (26) also certify a full-dimensional neighborhood containing nonzero-triple bases. No explicit radius is claimed.

## 4. A uniform large-coefficient family

### Theorem 3

For three sites assume \(\tau=0\), \(0<\ell_i:=p_i/p_\varnothing\le1\), and \(c_{ij}\ge8\) for all pairs. Then (3) holds.

**Proof.** Put \(u_{ij}=e^{-c_{ij}}\). The zero-triple atom weights are
\[
\frac{p_S}{p_\varnothing}
=\prod_{i\in S}\ell_i\prod_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}u_{ij}.
\tag{28}
\]
For pair \(ij\), with remaining site \(k\), its four unnormalized marginal weights are
\[
1+\ell_k,\quad
\ell_i(1+\ell_k u_{ik}),\quad
\ell_j(1+\ell_k u_{jk}),\quad
\ell_i\ell_j u_{ij}(1+\ell_k u_{ik}u_{jk}).
\tag{29}
\]
Their common normalization cancels in \(T_{ij}\). Using \(\ell_k\le1\) and \(u\le1\), the first and last entries in (29) have product at most \(4\ell_i\ell_j u_{ij}\); the two middle entries have sum at least \(2\sqrt{\ell_i\ell_j}\). Hence
\[
T_{ij}\ge\frac{e^{c_{ij}/2}}2,
\qquad
f_{ij}(0)\le c_{ij}e^{-c_{ij}/2}\le8e^{-4}.
\tag{30}
\]
The last function decreases for \(c\ge2\). Moreover,
\[
e^4>\sum_{k=0}^{6}\frac{4^k}{k!}=\frac{437}{9}>48,
\]
so \(\sum f_{ij}(0)<1/2\). Choose \(t=1/2\). Since \(v\le3/4\),
\[
\lambda_{\max}(B_{1/2})\le\sum f_{ij}(1/2)+\tfrac12v
<\tfrac12+\tfrac38=\tfrac78.
\]
Theorem 1 proves (3). \(\square\)

This is a regime of **large**, mutually comparable log coefficients, rather than small minimum coefficient. For a concrete example take the normalized Cauchy matrix with nodes \((100,101,102)\) and \(\ell_1=\ell_2=\ell_3=1\). Its \(u_{ij}=((x_i-x_j)/(x_i+x_j))^2\) obey \(\max u_{ij}=1/101^2\), so \(m=2\log101>8\), using \(e<3\). Its normalization is \(Z=4+\sum u_{ij}+\prod u_{ij}<9/2\), and
\[
v=\sum_{a<b}P(N=a)P(N=b)(a-b)^2
\ge P(N=0)P(N=1)=3/Z^2>1/8.
\]
Consequently the previous fixed-minimum budget has \(mv>1\) and fails. Theorem 3 proves the full Hessian bound anyway. The Cauchy determinant factorization and positivity used here are the established elementary construction in ROOT_PAIRWISE_CAUCHY.

## 6. Exact obstruction to universality of the new matrix budget

The matrix improvement does not remove all losses in (15). Consider, for \(0<u<1\), the eight-atom law
\[
p_\varnothing=p_{123}=\frac{u}{2(u+3)},\qquad
p_S=\frac1{2(u+3)}\quad(1\le|S|\le2).
\tag{41}
\]
It is a full-support three-site DPP. One construction has \(\ell_i=1/u\), normalized edge magnitude \(a=\sqrt{1-u}\), two real normalized edges, and third edge phase \(\theta\) satisfying
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{3a-a^3}{2}.
\]
The phase exists since
\(1-\cos^2\theta=u^2(3+u)/4>0\). The normalized pair principal determinants are \(u>0\), and the full determinant is \(u^3>0\), so the constructed Hermitian \(L\) is positive definite and gives exactly (41).

For this law, \(\tau=0\), every \(c_e=c=-\log u\), and
\[
T_e=\frac2{1+u},\quad D_e=\frac{2(u+3)}{u+1},\quad
v=\frac{3(3u+1)}{4(u+3)}.
\tag{42}
\]
Use the Rayleigh vector \(\nu\) of the particle count. For each missing coordinate, its expected conditional variance given the other two is
\[
\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(N\mid X\cap e)
=\frac{3u+1}{2(u+3)(u+1)}.
\]
By conditional variance decomposition,
\[
\frac{\nu^{\mathsf T}(\sum_eP_e)\nu}{v}
=\frac{3u+1}{u+1}.
\]
Therefore, for every shared coefficient \(0\le t\le c\),
\[
\frac{\nu^{\mathsf T}B_t\nu}{v}
=\frac{3u+1}{4(u+3)}(2c+t)
\ge\frac c6.
\tag{43}
\]
Take \(u=1/1024\). Then \(c=10\log2>20/3\), so (43) exceeds \(10/9>1\). **No shared coefficient makes Theorem 1's matrix budget pass at this exact admissible base.**

The same obstruction applies to the arbitrary diagonal weights in (18). By permutation symmetry,
\(\operatorname{Cov}(N,X_i)=v/3\), so
\[
\frac{\nu^{\mathsf T}B_h\nu}{v}
=\frac{v}{9}\left(\sum_i h_i\right)^2
 +\frac{3u+1}{6(u+3)}\left(3c-\sum_{i<j}h_ih_j\right)
=\frac{3u+1}{u+3}\left(\frac c2+\frac{\sum_i h_i^2}{12}\right)
\ge\frac c6>1.
\tag{44}
\]
Thus nonuniform diagonal weights cannot repair this example either.

This is already a known particle–hole-symmetric entropy-concavity case; it is not a counterexample to entropy concavity. The obstruction identifies the remaining methodological loss: the final pair square remainders in (15) can be indispensable. Bounding the first quadratic form independently by positivity discards their compensation. Retaining those remainders jointly with the score matrix, or using the full conditional Fisher features in old-097, is a viable next step. No pointwise-false entrywise Fisher comparison is used to make that step.
