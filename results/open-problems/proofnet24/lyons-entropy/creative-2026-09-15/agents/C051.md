## 1. The completed theorem

Let `n >= 2`, let `K` be a Hermitian positive contraction on the measured coordinates, and put

\[
u=\frac1{\sqrt n}(1,\ldots,1)^T,\qquad
\lambda=u^*Ku,\qquad
a=\frac{\operatorname{tr}K-\lambda}{n-1},\qquad
b=\frac{\lambda-a}{n}.
\tag{1}
\]

Define

\[
\overline K=aI+b\mathbf1\mathbf1^*
=a(I-uu^*)+\lambda uu^*.
\tag{2}
\]

Its two eigenvalues are `a` and `lambda`, and both belong to `[0,1]`. Direct coordinate averaging gives

\[
\overline K=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n}P_\pi KP_\pi^*.
\tag{3}
\]

**Theorem 1 (permutation-average entropy inequality).**

\[
\boxed{H(P_{\overline K})\ge H(P_K).}
\tag{4}
\]

If `0<a,lambda<1`, equality holds if and only if `K=Kbar`.

Equivalently, among all positive contractions with fixed `tr K` and fixed `u*Ku`, the exchangeable kernel (2) maximizes determinantal Shannon entropy. When (2) is strict, it is the unique maximizer. Since coordinate permutations preserve measured entropy, (4) is the precise many-endpoint Jensen inequality

\[
H\!\left(P_{\frac1{n!}\sum_\pi P_\pi KP_\pi^*}\right)
\ge\frac1{n!}\sum_\pi H(P_{P_\pi KP_\pi^*}).
\tag{5}
\]

The proof supplies an exact nonnegative gap decomposition, not only its sign.

## 2. The exact variational formula and the exchangeable supporting potential

For any finite law `p`, the classical Gibbs dual is

\[
\boxed{
H(p)=\inf_{\phi\in\mathbb R^{2^n}}
\left\{\log\sum_S e^{\phi(S)}-\sum_Sp(S)\phi(S)\right\}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed the objective minus `H(p)` is `D(p||q_phi)`, where `q_phi(S)=e^{phi(S)}/sum e^phi`. A positive `p` attains the infimum at `phi=log p`; zero probabilities are handled by limits. Consequently this is an exact variational representation of `H(P_K)`. It is not a claim that each frozen objective is concave in `K`.

Initially assume `0<a,lambda<1`, and write `q=P_Kbar`. For a configuration of cardinality `k`, the signed-column determinant and the matrix determinant lemma give

\[
\begin{aligned}
q(S)=q_k
&=a^k(1-a)^{n-k}
\left[1+b\left(\frac{k}{a}-\frac{n-k}{1-a}\right)\right]\\
&=a^k(1-a)^{n-k}\frac{c+bk}{a(1-a)},
\qquad c=a(1-\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

These are probabilities **per configuration**, not the count probabilities `binom(n,k)q_k`. The affine factor is positive for every `k`: its endpoint values are

\[
c=a(1-\lambda)>0,\qquad c+bn=(1-a)\lambda>0.
\tag{8}
\]

The optimal potential at `Kbar` therefore has the special form

\[
\log q(S)
=|S|\log a+(n-|S|)\log(1-a)
-\log(a(1-a))+\log(c+b|S|).
\tag{9}
\]

The first three terms have the same expectation under `P_K` and `q`, since the two kernels have equal trace. The nonlinear term is a logarithm of an affine function of the **measured count**. That is the feature making the covariance curvature usable here.

## 3. The exact determinant-integral gap

Assume first that `b>0`, so `c>0`. For each nonnegative integer `k`,

\[
\log(c+bk)-\log c
=\int_0^\infty e^{-ct}(1-e^{-btk})\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\tag{10}
\]

For `N=|X|` under `P_K`, the determinantal inclusion expansion gives

\[
\mathbb E_K z^N=\det(I+(z-1)K).
\tag{11}
\]

Put `eta(t)=1-e^{-bt}`. Subtracting the expectations of (10), then using (9) and (11), yields

\[
\mathbb E_K\log q(X)-\mathbb E_{\overline K}\log q(X)
=\mathcal T_b(K),
\tag{12}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_b(K)=\int_0^\infty e^{-ct}
\left[\det(I-\eta(t)\overline K)-\det(I-\eta(t)K)\right]\frac{dt}{t}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Every expectation in (10) is finite. Near `t=0`, the determinant difference is `O(t^2)` because the traces agree. At infinity the factor `e^{-ct}` gives integrability. Thus the displayed manipulations of finite sums and integrals are justified, including when `K` itself has boundary eigenvalues.

The definition of relative entropy now proves the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
H(q)-H(P_K)=D(P_K\Vert q)+\mathcal T_b(K).
}
\tag{14}
\]

For computation, an equivalent integral over a finite interval is

\[
\mathcal T_b(K)
=\int_0^1
\frac{(1-\eta)^{c/b-1}}{-\log(1-\eta)}
\left[\det(I-\eta\overline K)-\det(I-\eta K)\right],d\eta.
\tag{15}
\]

### Why every determinant gap is nonnegative

The following elementary covariance inequality supplies the sign:

\[
\boxed{
\det(I-\eta K)
\le(1-\eta\lambda)(1-\eta a)^{n-1}
=\det(I-\eta\overline K),\quad 0<\eta<1.
}
\tag{16}
\]

One proof is strict concavity of `log det` applied to the permutation average (3). Here is a direct proof requiring only a Schur complement and scalar arithmetic-geometric mean.

In `Cu direct-sum u-perp`, write

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&w^*\\w&B\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{tr}B=(n-1)a.
\]

Since `I-eta K` is positive definite,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(I-\eta K)
&=(1-\eta\lambda)
\det\left(I-\eta B-\frac{\eta^2}{1-\eta\lambda}ww^*\right)\\
&\le(1-\eta\lambda)\det(I-\eta B)\\
&\le(1-\eta\lambda)
\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}(I-\eta B)}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}\\
&=(1-\eta\lambda)(1-\eta a)^{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

The first inequality removes a positive rank-one subtraction, and the second applies arithmetic-geometric mean to the positive eigenvalues of `I-eta B`. Equality throughout is equivalent to `w=0` and `B=aI`, hence to `K=Kbar`. Thus if `K!=Kbar`, the integrand of (13) is strictly positive for every `t>0`.

Equations (14) and (16) prove Theorem 1 for positive `b` and strict `Kbar`, including its strictness.

### Negative and zero `b`; boundary kernels

If `b<0`, complement every occupation bit and replace `K` by `I-K`. Its exchangeable parameters are

\[
a'=1-a,\qquad \lambda'=1-\lambda,\qquad b'=-b>0.
\tag{18}
\]

Complementation preserves Shannon entropy and relative entropy. Apply (14) to these complemented kernels. This gives the same nonnegative decomposition with the determinant integral evaluated for `I-K` and `I-Kbar`.

If `b=0`, then `q` is the product of `n` Bernoulli variables of common mean `a`. Its logarithm is affine in the count, so

\[
\boxed{H(q)-H(P_K)=D(P_K\Vert q).}
\tag{19}
\]

Equality in (19) implies that `P_K` is this product law. Singleton moments then give `K_ii=a`, and pair moments give `|K_ij|^2=0`; hence `K=aI=Kbar`.

For arbitrary closed contractions, apply the proved inequality to

\[
K^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I,
\qquad
\overline K^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)\overline K+\varepsilon I.
\tag{20}
\]

The permutation average commutes with this replacement. Both exchangeable eigenvalues become strict, and every finite-law entropy is continuous in the kernel. Taking `epsilon down to 0` proves (4) on the full closed contraction domain. The exact integral formula above is stated only for strict `Kbar`; no frozen logarithm is assigned to an impossible atom.

## 4. A strict covariance curvature certificate in every dimension

Fix a strict exchangeable base `Kbar`, with `b!=0`, and a Hermitian direction `A` satisfying

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}A=0,\qquad u^*Au=0.}
\tag{21}
\]

The entire physical line `Kbar+tau A` has the same exchangeable average. This is a real vector space of dimension `n^2-2`; it includes full-rank directions in every even dimension. Let

\[
\mathcal I(A)=\sum_S\frac{(p'_S(0))^2}{q_S}
\]

be its measured Fisher norm.

For `b>0`, define `R_eta=(I-eta Kbar)^(-1)`. Since `R_eta` belongs to the span of `I` and `uu*`, (21) gives `tr(R_eta A)=0`. Differentiating the exact identity (14) at the base therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(P_{\overline K})[A,A]
=\mathcal I(A)
+\int_0^\infty e^{-ct}\det(I-\eta\overline K)\eta^2
\operatorname{tr}(R_\eta A R_\eta A)\,\frac{dt}{t}.
}
\tag{22}
\]

In detail, the second derivative of `D(p_tau||q)` is `I(A)`, and

\[
\frac{d^2}{d\tau^2}\det(I-\eta(\overline K+\tau A))\Big|_0
=-\eta^2\det(I-\eta\overline K)
\operatorname{tr}(R_\eta A R_\eta A).
\]

The integrand in (22) is positive for nonzero `A`, because
`tr(R A R A)=||R^(1/2) A R^(1/2)||_F^2`. Differentiation under the integral is justified on a small strict neighborhood of the base. The complementary version proves the same strict sign for negative `b`.

This is an exact covariance-curvature consequence of the Gibbs representation. In particular it does not discard an uncontrolled atom-acceleration term or assume a measured quantum maximum-entropy principle.

### A finite explicit lower bound

Put

\[
R_{\max}=\max_{1\le k\le n-1}(c+bk),\qquad
\kappa=-\log\left(1-\frac{b^2}{R_{\max}^2}\right)>0.
\tag{23}
\]

All factors in (8) are positive, so `R_max>|b|`. Then

\[
\boxed{-D^2H(P_{\overline K})[A,A]
\ge\mathcal I(A)+\kappa\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{24}
\]

Here is a finite derivation, also valid for either sign of `b`. In the decomposition `Cu direct-sum u-perp`, write

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&v^*\\v&B\end{pmatrix},\qquad\operatorname{tr}B=0.
\]

Let `f(k)=log q_k`. Its second difference is

\[
\Delta^2 f(k)
=\log\frac{(c+bk)(c+b(k+2))}{(c+b(k+1))^2}
=\log\left(1-\frac{b^2}{(c+b(k+1))^2}\right)
\le-\kappa.
\tag{25}
\]

For `n>=3`, differentiating the count generating determinant gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2H[A,A]
={}&-\mathcal I(A)
+2\|v\|^2\,\mathbb E\Delta^2f(Z_1)
+\|B\|_F^2\,\mathbb E\Delta^2f(Z_2),\\
Z_1&\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-2,a),\\
Z_2&\sim\operatorname{Bern}(\lambda)+\operatorname{Bin}(n-3,a),
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

with independent summands in `Z_2`. To verify it directly, the second count generating polynomial is

\[
-(z-1)^2\left[
2\|v\|^2(1-a+az)^{n-2}
+\|B\|_F^2(1-\lambda+\lambda z)(1-a+az)^{n-3}
\right].
\tag{27}
\]

Pairing its coefficients with `f` produces (26). For `n=2`, `B=0`, and the first term uses the deterministic variable `Z_1=0`; this gives the same conclusion. Since `||A||_F^2=2||v||^2+||B||_F^2`, (25) proves (24).

For a real exchangeable base and a purely imaginary Hermitian `A`, all first atom derivatives vanish: conjugation changes `tau` to `-tau` and preserves every real determinant probability. Thus `I(A)=0`, while (24) is still strictly negative. C024's absolute residual bound alone has no negative Fisher term in these examples; the sign of the optimized count potential is the additional information used here.

## 5. A concrete full-rank six-site concavity interval

Take

\[

A=\operatorname{diag}(J,J,J),\quad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\-i&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{28}
\]

Here `A^2=I`, `rank A=6`, `tr A=u*Au=0`, and `||A||_F^2=6`. The base eigenvalues are `1/4` five times and `3/4` once. In (23),

\[
c=\frac1{16},\qquad R_{\max}=\frac{23}{48},\qquad
\kappa=\log\frac{529}{513}.
\]

Therefore

\[
H''(0)\le-6\log(529/513)\le-\frac{96}{529}<0.
\tag{29}
\]

The exact executed atom calculation gives approximately `H''(0)=-0.936555865942586`; the analytic bound (29), not that decimal, controls the interval below.

For completeness, an explicit conservative interval is

\[
\boxed{
H''(\tau)\le-\frac{48}{529}
\quad\text{for every }|\tau|\le\frac1{20\,000\,000}.
}
\tag{30}
\]

This is a full affine concavity interval with a changing rank-six direction. Its small radius is a proof constant, not an estimate of the actual maximal interval.

To verify (30), first note that for `|tau|<=1/8`, Weyl's elementary norm bound gives
`(1/8)I <= Kbar+tau A <= (7/8)I`. For any strict kernel in `[rI,(1-r)I]`, its signed Hermitian atom matrix
`Q_S=K-I_(S^c)` has no eigenvalue in `(-r,r)`: its `S` diagonal block is at least `rI`, its complementary block is at most `-rI`, and the Schur complement excludes such an eigenvalue. Hence `||Q_S^(-1)||<=1/r`.

When `||A||_op<=1`, logarithmic determinant differentiation now gives

\[
\frac{|p'_S|}{p_S}\le\frac nr,\quad
\frac{|p''_S|}{p_S}\le\frac{n(n+1)}{r^2},\quad
\frac{|p'''_S|}{p_S}\le\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{r^3}.
\tag{31}
\]

Using

\[
H'''=-\sum_Sp'''_S\log p_S
-3\sum_S\frac{p'_Sp''_S}{p_S}
+\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^3}{p_S^2}
\]

and `H(p)<=n log 2` yields

\[
|H'''|\le
\frac{4n^3+3n^2+n^2(n+1)(n+2)\log2}{r^3}.
\tag{32}
\]

For `n=6,r=1/8`, replace `log2` by `1` to obtain the rational bound `1,529,856`. Finally,

\[
\frac{1\,529\,856}{20\,000\,000}<\frac{48}{529}.
\]

The mean-value bound for `H''`, combined with (29), proves (30). All rational inequalities were checked exactly by the retained script.

## 7. Why this is not measured-Gaussian extremality

Only the exact classical Gibbs identity (6) was used. The following small obstruction makes the distinction concrete. Fix

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/8\\1/8&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In the ordered occupation basis `(empty,{1},{2},{1,2})`, take density matrices with off-diagonal entries `rho_{1,2}=rho_{2,1}=1/8` in the one-particle sector and all other off-diagonal entries zero. The following three diagonal vectors all give positive semidefinite states with exactly this one-body covariance:

\[
p_{\rm lower}=(3,1,1,3)/8,\quad
p_{\rm Gaussian}=(15,17,17,15)/64,\quad
p_{\rm upper}=(1,1,1,1)/4.
\tag{35}
\]

Their measured entropies satisfy

\[
H(p_{\rm lower})<H(p_{\rm Gaussian})<H(p_{\rm upper}).
\tag{36}
\]

Positivity reduces to the displayed two-dimensional one-particle blocks. Both occupation marginals equal `1/2`, and the common off-diagonal block entry fixes the covariance coherence. The upper entropy is strictly larger because it is uniform. For the lower comparison, the two entropies have the forms `log2+h(3/4)` and `log2+h(17/32)`, and binary entropy strictly decreases on `[1/2,1]`.

Thus the Gaussian state is neither a measured-entropy maximum nor a minimum under its quantum covariance constraint, already in this strict two-site example. The exchangeable theorem instead compares **determinantal laws of different kernels with two specified linear kernel constraints**, using the exact frozen optimal potential (9). It is compatible with this obstruction.
