## 1. Result and scope correction

Let \(n\ge6\), let \(v\in\mathbb C^n\) be a unit vector, and put
\[
K=\frac12I+\frac\theta2vv^*,\qquad
0<|\theta|\le1-2^{-n},\qquad
\max_i\bigl|n|v_i|^2-1\bigr|\le\epsilon.
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
d=\frac{4\theta^2}{[n+(n-2)|\theta|]^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
For a Hermitian matrix \(A\), write
\[
\mathcal I_K(A)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}
\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)},\qquad
\|A\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(A^2).
\]

**Theorem.** If
\[
\epsilon\le\frac{d}{16(n-1)},
\tag{3}
\]
then every complex Hermitian direction satisfies
\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(K)[A,A]\ge
\frac{67}{320}\mathcal I_K(A)+\frac d2\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular the full ambient Hermitian Hessian is negative definite. All atoms and eigenvalues in (1) are strictly interior, so no boundary interpretation of the derivatives is needed.

## 2. Actual atom law, count law, and the residual table

Complementation sends \((K,A,\theta)\) to \((I-K,-A,-\theta)\), preserves entropy and atom Fisher information, and permutes the atom labels. We may therefore assume \(0<\theta<1\). Put \(P=vv^*\), \(Q=I-P\), and \(w_S=\sum_{i\in S}|v_i|^2\). The signed-determinant atom formula and the rank-one determinant lemma give
\[
p_S(K)=2^{-n}[1+\theta(2w_S-1)].
\tag{5}
\]
For the flat vector, the probability per individual subset of size \(k\) is
\[
q_k=2^{-n}\left[1+\theta\frac{2k-n}{n}\right].
\tag{6}
\]
These \(q_k\) are per-atom weights, not count masses. Regardless of the magnitudes or phases of \(v\),
\[
\Pr_K(N=k)=\binom nkq_k,
\qquad
N\overset d=\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,1/2)
+\operatorname{Bern}((1+\theta)/2),
\tag{7}
\]
with independent summands. This follows either by summing (5), using
\(\sum_{|S|=k}w_S=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\), or from
\(\mathbb E z^N=\det(I+(z-1)K)\).
Its mean and variance are
\[
\mu=\frac n2+\frac\theta2,
\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{n-\theta^2}{4}.
\tag{8}
\]

Freeze at the actual base \(K\) the two tables
\[
f(S)=-\log p_S(K),\qquad
f_0(S)=-\log q_{|S|},\qquad g=f-f_0.
\tag{9}
\]
Here and below a frozen expectation means
\(F_h(L)=\mathbb E_{P_L}h(X)\), with the table \(h\) held fixed while \(L\) varies. Entropy differentiation gives exactly
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=D^2F_{f_0}(K)[A,A]
+D^2F_g(K)[A,A]-\mathcal I_K(A).
\tag{10}
\]

To bound \(g\), write \(w_S=k/n+\delta_S\), with
\(|\delta_S|\le\epsilon k/n\). For \(k>0\),
\[
\left|\frac{p_S}{q_k}-1\right|
=\frac{2\theta|\delta_S|}{1-\theta+2\theta k/n}
\le\epsilon.
\]
For \(k=0\), the ratio is exactly one. Consequently
\[
-\log(1+\epsilon)\le g(S)\le-\log(1-\epsilon),
\qquad
\operatorname{osc}(g)\le
\log\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}.
\tag{11}
\]
This estimate avoids the small eigenvalue \((1-\theta)/2\): the perturbation to an atom is controlled relative to the same count contribution that produces it.

## 3. Exact curvature of the frozen count potential

Decompose a general complex Hermitian direction with respect to
\(\mathbb Cv\oplus v^\perp\). Set
\[
u=v^*Av,\quad y=\operatorname{tr}(QAQ),\quad
B=QAQ,\quad \rho=\|QAP\|_F^2,\quad t=\operatorname{tr}A=u+y.
\]
Then \(u,y\) are real and
\[
\|A\|_F^2=u^2+\|B\|_F^2+2\rho.
\tag{12}
\]
Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta&=\mathbb E\Delta^2f_0\bigl(
\operatorname{Bin}(n-3,1/2)+\operatorname{Bern}((1+\theta)/2)\bigr),\\
\gamma&=\mathbb E\Delta^2f_0\bigl(\operatorname{Bin}(n-2,1/2)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
where \(f_0(k)=-\log q_k\), and the summands in \(\beta\) are independent. The exact identity is
\[
D^2F_{f_0}(K)[A,A]
=\beta(y^2-\|B\|_F^2)+2\gamma(uy-\rho).
\tag{14}
\]

For completeness, put \(h(z)=(1+z)/2\) and
\(j(z)=1-b+bz\), \(b=(1+\theta)/2\). Two derivatives of the count generating determinant give the polynomial identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2\det(I+(z-1)K)[A,A]
=(z-1)^2\bigl[&(y^2-\|B\|_F^2)j(z)h(z)^{n-3}\\
&+2(uy-\rho)h(z)^{n-2}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
One may first use the inverse-matrix derivative formula away from the finitely many singular values of \(z\); polynomial continuation proves it for all \(z\). Pairing the coefficients of \((z-1)^2z^k\) with \(f_0\) yields \(\Delta^2f_0(k)\), proving (14). The argument uses the spectrum of \(K\) and the actual projection \(P=vv^*\). It requires no coordinate-permutation symmetry of the actual law and retains all complex Hermitian directions.

Set
\[
r=\frac{n(1-\theta)}{2\theta}>0,
\qquad
\ell_r(k)=\Delta^2f_0(k)
=\log\frac{(k+r+1)^2}{(k+r)(k+r+2)}
=-\log\left(1-\frac1{(k+r+1)^2}\right).
\tag{16}
\]
The sequence \(\ell_r(k)\) is positive and decreasing. Coupling the last Bernoulli trial in (13) gives
\(0<\beta\le\gamma\), since \(b\ge1/2\). Both random arguments range from zero to \(n-2\), so
\[
\beta\ge\ell_r(n-2)
\ge\frac1{(r+n-1)^2}
=\frac{4\theta^2}{[n+(n-2)\theta]^2}=d.
\tag{17}
\]
In fact the first logarithmic lower bound is strict when \(\theta>0\), but non-strict bounds suffice.

Writing \(c=(\beta+\gamma)/2\), the complete-square form of (14) is
\[
\boxed{
D^2F_{f_0}(K)[A,A]
=ct^2-\beta\|A\|_F^2
-\frac{\gamma-\beta}{2}(u-y)^2
-2(\gamma-\beta)\rho.}
\tag{18}
\]
In particular it is at most \(ct^2-d\|A\|_F^2\).

## 4. The uniform Fisher fraction, with all constants proved

The score \(s_A(S)=Dp_S(K)[A]/p_S(K)\) satisfies
\(\mathbb E s_A=0\) and
\(\mathbb E[(N-\mu)s_A]=D\operatorname{tr}K[A]=t\).
Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
\[
t^2\le\sigma^2\mathcal I_K(A).
\tag{19}
\]
Equivalently, this follows from count Fisher information followed by data processing; no flat conditional law is assumed. We next prove
\[
\sigma^2c\le\frac{253}{320}.
\tag{20}
\]

Let \(m=n-2\). The \(k=0\) term of \(\gamma\) obeys
\[
\ell_r(0)
=\log(1+1/r)+\log\frac{r+1}{r+2}
\le\log(1+1/r).
\]
For \(k\ge1\),
\[
\ell_r(k)
=\log\left(1+\frac1{(k+r)(k+r+2)}\right)
\le\frac1{k(k+2)}
\le\frac2{(k+1)(k+2)}.
\]
The exact binomial identity
\[
\sum_{k=0}^m\frac{\binom mk}{(k+1)(k+2)}
=\frac{2^{m+2}-m-3}{(m+1)(m+2)}
\tag{21}
\]
follows by replacing each summand with
\(\binom{m+2}{k+2}/[(m+1)(m+2)]\). Including the extra nonnegative \(k=0\) term in this rational upper bound yields
\[
\gamma\le\frac8{n(n-1)}
+2^{2-n}\log(1+1/r).
\tag{22}
\]
By the restriction on \(\theta\),
\[
\frac1r=\frac{2\theta}{n(1-\theta)}
\le\frac{2(2^n-1)}n,
\qquad 1+\frac1r\le2^n
\tag{23}
\]
for \(n\ge2\). Since \(c\le\gamma\) and \(\sigma^2\le n/4\),
\[
\sigma^2c
\le\frac2{n-1}+n^2 2^{-n}\log2.
\tag{24}
\]
The sequence \(n^2/2^n\) decreases for integer \(n\ge3\), because
\((n+1)^2\le2n^2\). Finally the absolutely convergent series
\[
\log2=2\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{3^{-(2j+1)}}{2j+1}
<\frac23+\frac23\sum_{j=1}^\infty3^{-(2j+1)}
=\frac23+\frac1{36}=\frac{25}{36}
\tag{25}
\]
gives, for \(n\ge6\),
\[
\sigma^2c\le\frac25+\frac{36}{64}\frac{25}{36}
=\frac25+\frac{25}{64}=\frac{253}{320}.
\tag{26}
\]
Combining (18), (19), and (26) proves the base estimate at every \(P=vv^*\):
\[
\mathcal I_K(A)-D^2F_{f_0}(K)[A,A]
\ge\frac{67}{320}\mathcal I_K(A)+d\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{27}
\]
For nonflat \(v\), (27) is explicitly a bound for the frozen count reference plus the actual Fisher information. The entropy Hessian still contains the residual in (10).

## 5. A sharp oscillation bound for any frozen table

**Lemma.** For any real table \(g:2^{[n]}\to\mathbb R\), any positive contraction \(L\), and any Hermitian \(A\),
\[
\boxed{
|D^2F_g(L)[A,A]|
\le2\operatorname{osc}(g)
\bigl[(\operatorname{tr}|A|)^2-\|A\|_F^2\bigr]
\le2(n-1)\operatorname{osc}(g)\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{28}
\]
At boundary \(L\), derivatives mean derivatives of the atom polynomials; the estimate follows directly there as well. The factor \(2(n-1)\) is sharp, even if one asks for a bound uniform only over strict contractions.

The rank-two compression identity used next is established in prior section 4; a proof is included to specify its unrestricted-table applicability. For orthonormal \(u,v\), put \(R=I-uu^*-vv^*\) and \(B=RLR\). Then
\[
D^2F_g(L)[uu^*,vv^*]=D^2F_g(B)[uu^*,vv^*].
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed, expand
\(F_g(L)=\sum_T\widehat g(T)\det L_T\), where
\(\widehat g(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T\setminus S|}g(S)\).
For each \(T\), the coefficient of \(st\) in
\(\det(L_T+s u_Tu_T^*+t v_Tv_T^*)\) is zero if \(u_T,v_T\) are dependent. Otherwise it is their Gram determinant multiplied by the determinant of the compression of \(L_T\) to their orthogonal complement. This follows by choosing a unitary basis with their span first and selecting the two perturbation factors in the determinant. Extending complement vectors by zero puts them in the global span of \(u,v\) orthogonal complement. The same compressed matrix is therefore obtained from \(B_T\), proving (29).

Rank-one determinant affinity also makes the frozen expectation affine in each of \(s,t\) separately. Thus the right side of (29) equals the four-corner difference
\[
F_g(B+uu^*+vv^*)-F_g(B+uu^*)-F_g(B+vv^*)+F_g(B).
\tag{30}
\]
All four arguments are positive contractions: \(B\) is supported on the orthogonal complement of \(u,v\). Each expectation lies between \(\min g\) and \(\max g\), so the absolute value of (30) is at most \(2\operatorname{osc}(g)\).

Take an orthonormal spectral decomposition
\(A=\sum_i\lambda_i u_i u_i^*\), with real \(\lambda_i\). Pure rank-one second derivatives vanish. Summing the mixed bounds gives
\[
|D^2F_g(L)[A,A]|
\le2\operatorname{osc}(g)\sum_{i\ne j}|\lambda_i\lambda_j|
=2\operatorname{osc}(g)\left[
\left(\sum_i|\lambda_i|\right)^2-\sum_i\lambda_i^2\right].
\]
The final inequality in (28) is Cauchy–Schwarz. This argument is independent of the eigenvalue gaps of \(L\).

For sharpness, let \(g(S)=\mathbf1_{\{|S|\text{ even}\}}\), \(L=0\), and \(A=I\). Then
\[
F_g(tI)=\frac{1+(1-2t)^n}{2},\qquad
\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}F_g(tI)\right|_{t=0}=2n(n-1).
\tag{31}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{osc}(g)=1\) and \(\|I\|_F^2=n\), giving equality in both bounds. At \(L=\delta I\), \(0<\delta<1/2\), the derivative is
\(2n(n-1)(1-2\delta)^{n-2}\), approaching equality as \(\delta\downarrow0\).

## 6. Absorbing the nonflat residual

The function \(2\theta/[n+(n-2)\theta]\) increases on \([0,1]\), so
\[
d\le\frac1{(n-1)^2},\qquad
\epsilon\le\frac1{16(n-1)^3}<\frac12.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(0\le\epsilon\le1/2\),
\[
\log\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}
=2\int_0^\epsilon\frac{dx}{1-x^2}
\le4\epsilon.
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (11), (28), and (3) now imply
\[
|D^2F_g(K)[A,A]|
\le8(n-1)\epsilon\|A\|_F^2
\le\frac d2\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{34}
\]
Substitute (27) and (34) into (10) to obtain (4). This completes the theorem for positive \(\theta\), and complementation completes both signs.
