## 1. The affine DPP family and exact density modes

For positive real parameters \(a,b\), set
\[
K_\eta=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&b&a\eta&0\\
b&1/2&0&-a\eta\\
a\eta&0&1/2&b\\
0&-a\eta&b&1/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad |\eta|\le1.
\tag{1}
\]
Because
\[
(K_\eta-I/2)^2=(b^2+a^2\eta^2)I,
\tag{2}
\]
the strict physical condition is \(b^2+a^2<1/4\). The eigenvalues are \(1/2\pm\sqrt{b^2+a^2\eta^2}\), each twice. The affine direction has rank four and inertia \((2,2)\), and the cross block has rank two. The coherent measured blocks \(12\) and \(34\) stay fixed.

Use spins \(y_i=1-2X_i\), and write
\[
z=y_1y_2,\quad w=y_3y_4,\quad s=y_1y_3,\qquad
\alpha=4b^2,\quad\gamma=4a^2,\quad
\zeta=32b^2a^2,\quad\chi=\gamma^2,
\]
\[
q=(1-\alpha z)(1-\alpha w),\quad
\mu(y)=\frac{q}{16},\quad
h_0(y)=-\frac{\gamma s(1+zw)}q,\quad
v(y)=\frac{zw}q.
\tag{3}
\]
The actual determinantal atoms satisfy
\[
\boxed{\quad p_\eta(y)=\mu(y)f_\eta(y),\qquad
f_\eta=1+\eta^2h_0+(\zeta\eta^2+\chi\eta^4)v.\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
For a direct derivation, the spin identity is
\(\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}y_i=\det(I-2K_\eta)_S\).
All odd spin moments vanish. The nonzero pair moments are \(-\alpha\) on the two internal edges and \(-\gamma\eta^2\) on the cross edges. The full determinant equals
\(\alpha^2+\zeta\eta^2+\chi\eta^4\), by (2). Fourier inversion gives (4). Thus the atoms, their first derivatives, and their accelerations below belong to the stated affine Hermitian line; they are not a freely chosen probability interpolation.

Under \(\mu\), the sign \(s\) is fair conditional on \((z,w)\). Exact summation gives
\[
\langle1,h_0\rangle_\mu=\langle1,v\rangle_\mu
=\langle h_0,v\rangle_\mu=0,
\]
\[
N_h=\|h_0\|_\mu^2
=\frac{2\gamma^2(1+\alpha^2)}{(1-\alpha^2)^2},\qquad
N_v=\|v\|_\mu^2=\frac1{(1-\alpha^2)^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, both modes have zero marginal on each measured block. Since
\(\mu=P_{K_0}=\mu_{12}\otimes\mu_{34}\), this proves that
\[
\sum_y p_\eta(y)\log\mu(y)
\quad\text{is constant in }\eta.
\tag{6}
\]
Equation (6) is the exact compensation needed to pass from relative entropy to Shannon entropy.

## 2. An adaptive reversible generator and its derivative

On functions of the sixteen configurations, define the mutually orthogonal projections
\[
Rf=\langle1,f\rangle_\mu1,\qquad
\Pi_hf=h_0\frac{\langle h_0,f\rangle_\mu}{N_h},\qquad
\Pi_vf=v\frac{\langle v,f\rangle_\mu}{N_v}.
\]
For \(\eta=e^{-\tau}\in(0,1]\), set
\[
\theta_\eta=\frac{\zeta}{\zeta+\chi\eta^2}
=\frac{2b^2}{2b^2+a^2\eta^2},\qquad
\boxed{\quad L_\eta=4(R-I)+2\Pi_h+2\theta_\eta\Pi_v.\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
The eigenvalue of \(L_\eta\) on \(h_0\) is \(-2\). Its eigenvalue on \(v\) is
\(-4+2\theta_\eta\), exactly the logarithmic derivative of
\(\zeta\eta^2+\chi\eta^4\) in the clock \(\tau\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{\quad \dot f_\eta=L_\eta f_\eta=-\eta\partial_\eta f_\eta.\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
This is a time-dependent occupation generator, with a fixed stationary reference law \(\mu\).

The derivative must be retained:
\[
\dot\theta=2\theta(1-\theta),\qquad
\dot L=4\theta(1-\theta)\Pi_v.
\tag{9}
\]
Using the orthogonal projection algebra in (7), it follows exactly that
\[
\boxed{\quad
M_\eta:=-(\dot L_\eta+L_\eta^2+L_\eta)
=12(R-I)+10\Pi_h+10\theta_\eta\Pi_v.
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular,
\[
M_\eta f_\eta
=-2\eta^2h_0-(2\zeta\eta^2+12\chi\eta^4)v
=-\eta^2\partial_\eta^2 f_\eta.
\tag{11}
\]
Omitting \(\dot L\) would change the \(v\)-coefficient in (10) and would fail to reproduce the actual affine acceleration.

## 3. Exact positive-conductance criterion

Let
\[
T_\theta(x,y)=\frac{h_0(x)h_0(y)}{N_h}
+\theta\frac{v(x)v(y)}{N_v}.
\]
For distinct configurations,
\[
(L_\eta)_{xy}=\mu_y\{4+2T_{\theta_\eta}(x,y)\},\qquad
(M_\eta)_{xy}=\mu_y\{12+10T_{\theta_\eta}(x,y)\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Their rows sum to zero, and the conductances \(\mu_xL_{xy}\) and \(\mu_xM_{xy}\) are symmetric. Hence nonnegative off-diagonal entries are the only remaining Markov conditions.

Put \(\rho=(1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha)\). The normalized mode pairs have only the following forms:
\[
\left(\frac{h_0}{\sqrt{N_h}},\frac{v}{\sqrt{N_v}}\right)
=\begin{cases}
(0,-1),&zw=-1,\\[2pt]
\left(-s\sqrt{\frac2{1+\alpha^2}}\,t,t\right),&z=w,\quad
t\in\{\rho,\rho^{-1}\}.
\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
If both configurations have \(zw=-1\), their projection kernel is \(\theta\ge0\). If exactly one does, its smallest possible value is \(-\theta\rho\). If both have \(zw=1\), the minimum occurs at opposite \(s\) and \(t_x=t_y=\rho\). Therefore, for \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
-\min_{x\ne y}T_\theta(x,y)
=\max\left\{\rho^2\left(\frac2{1+\alpha^2}-\theta\right),\theta\rho\right\}
=\rho^2\left(\frac2{1+\alpha^2}-\theta\right).
\tag{14}
\]
For the last equality, \(2\rho/((\rho+1)(1+\alpha^2))=(1+\alpha)/(1+\alpha^2)>1\), so the first branch dominates throughout this range. Opposite signs with \(z=w=1\) occur at distinct configurations, so this is the exact off-diagonal minimum.

Let
\[
\theta_*:=\frac{2b^2}{2b^2+a^2},\qquad
\boxed{\quad\kappa_*:=\rho^2\left(\frac2{1+\alpha^2}-\theta_*\right).\quad}
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(\theta_\eta\in[\theta_*,1]\), the full family \(L_\eta\) is Markov if and only if \(\kappa_*\le2\), and the full family \(M_\eta\) is Markov if and only if \(\kappa_*\le6/5\). Strict inequalities make every off-diagonal conductance positive. This is a sharp matrix-sign criterion for the prescribed construction; it is not a necessary condition for entropy concavity or for another possible transport.

Whenever \(\kappa_*<6/5\), write
\[
\beta_*:=12-10\kappa_*>0,\qquad
M_\eta=\beta_*(R-I)+\widetilde M_\eta.
\tag{16}
\]
The remainder \(\widetilde M_\eta\) is another \(\mu\)-reversible Markov generator: each of its off-diagonal entries is \(10\mu_y(T_{\theta_\eta}(x,y)+\kappa_*)\ge0\).

## 4. Actual transport in an affine kernel clock

Fix \(\eta_0\in(0,1]\), and decrease the kernel parameter by the affine clock \(u\), so \(\eta=\eta_0-u>0\). Define
\[
G_u=\frac{L_\eta}{\eta}.
\tag{17}
\]
Then \(\partial_u f_\eta=G_uf_\eta\). In particular, the actual DPP atom velocity is the positive-conductance continuity equation
\[
\boxed{\quad
\partial_up_\eta(x)=\sum_{y\ne x}c_u(x,y)
\left(\frac{p_\eta(y)}{\mu_y}-\frac{p_\eta(x)}{\mu_x}\right),
\qquad
c_u(x,y)=\frac{\mu_x\mu_y}{\eta}
\{4+2T_{\theta_\eta}(x,y)\}>0.
\quad}
\tag{18}
\]
The clock is affine in the Hermitian kernel, not only in the probability density. The acceleration identity also holds as an operator identity:
\[
\boxed{\quad
-(\partial_uG_u+G_u^2)=\frac{M_\eta}{\eta^2}.
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed \(\partial_u=-\partial_\eta\) and \(\dot L=-\eta\partial_\eta L\), so (19) is precisely (10). Thus both the velocity and its affine-curvature compensation have positive symmetric conductances. These are jumps between full occupation configurations; no restriction to one-site flips or swaps is asserted. Also \(G_u\) diverges as \(\eta\to0\); the finite affine generator is claimed on positive-parameter intervals. The density and entropy themselves extend smoothly to zero.

## 5. Entropy identity and quantitative gap

For a \(\mu\)-reversible Markov generator \(B\), define
\[
\mathcal E_B(f,\log f)
=\frac12\sum_{x,y}\mu_xB_{xy}
(f_x-f_y)(\log f_x-\log f_y)\ge0.
\]
Differentiating finite entropy, using the constant cross entropy (6), and substituting (11) gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\quad
-\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}H(p_\eta)
=\underbrace{\sum_x\mu_x\frac{(\partial_\eta f_\eta(x))^2}{f_\eta(x)}}_{\mathcal I_{\rm aff}(\eta)}
+\frac1{\eta^2}\mathcal E_{M_\eta}(f_\eta,\log f_\eta).
\quad}
\tag{20}
\]
Equivalently, \(-\eta^2H''\) equals the Fisher term formed from \(L_\eta f_\eta\) plus the \(M_\eta\) Dirichlet term. All terms use the exact DPP atoms.

By (16), and since \(Rf_\eta=1\),
\[
\mathcal E_{R-I}(f_\eta,\log f_\eta)
=\langle f_\eta-1,\log f_\eta\rangle_\mu
=D(p_\eta\Vert\mu)+D(\mu\Vert p_\eta).
\]
Consequently the following quantitative bound is proved throughout the strict physical interval, for \(\eta\ne0\):
\[
\boxed{\quad
-H''(p_\eta)\ge
\mathcal I_{\rm aff}(\eta)
+\frac{\beta_*}{\eta^2}
\{D(p_\eta\Vert\mu)+D(\mu\Vert p_\eta)\}.
\quad}
\tag{21}
\]
The leftover term is exactly \(\eta^{-2}\mathcal E_{\widetilde M_\eta}(f_\eta,\log f_\eta)\), so no uncontrolled remainder is hidden in this inequality. Orthogonality in (5) makes \(\partial_\eta f_\eta\ne0\) for \(\eta\ne0\). Thus the entropy second derivative is strictly negative there. Equation (4) makes entropy even and smooth, with \(H''(0)=0\). Concavity extends to the whole interval \([-1,1]\).
