## 1. Physical family and statement

Use coordinates

\[
E=L\sqcup R,\qquad L=\{0,1,2\},\quad R=\{3,4,5\}.
\]

Let \(X,Y\in\mathbb C^{3\times2}\) satisfy

\[
X^*X=Y^*Y=I_2,
\qquad \|X_{i,:}\|^2=\|Y_{j,:}\|^2=\frac23.
\tag{1}
\]

Choose unit vectors \(u_0\perp\operatorname{ran}X\) and \(v_0\perp\operatorname{ran}Y\). Their coordinates have squared moduli \(1/3\). Define the three orthogonal planes by frames

\[
W=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}X\\Y\end{pmatrix},
\qquad U=\operatorname{span}\left\{
\begin{pmatrix}u_0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\begin{pmatrix}0\\v_0\end{pmatrix}\right\},
\qquad Z=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}X\\-Y\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]

Frames and their column spaces are denoted by the same letters. Direct multiplication verifies that these planes are orthogonal and exhaust \(\mathbb C^6\). Every one of their six coordinate projection diagonals is \(1/3\).

After coordinate phases make the two complements flat, a common bottom basis change sets \(X=X_0\), and \(Y=X_0R_0\) for arbitrary \(R_0\in U(2)\). Thus (1) includes all relative complex two-by-two unitaries, not only real rotations or a small parameter neighborhood.

For pairs \(A,B,J\) and triples \(M\), write

\[
a_A=\mu_W(A),\quad c_B=\mu_Z(B),\quad h_J=\mu_U(J),
\quad g_M=\mu_{W+[u]}(M)+\mu_{W+[v]}(M),
\tag{3}
\]

where \(u,v\) is **any** orthonormal basis of \(U\). The aggregate \(g\) is basis independent: for each \(M\), its two determinant amplitudes transform by a unitary matrix, preserving their squared norm.

Index a diamond by ordered disjoint pairs \((A,B)\). Its bottom is \(A\), top is \(E\setminus B\), and added pair is \(J=E\setminus(A\cup B)\). Its two middles are \(A+i,A+j\) for \(J=\{i,j\}\).

**Theorem 1.** Every flag (1)–(2), with any orthonormal update basis, has an explicitly constructible nonnegative probability table \(x_{A,B}\) with

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_Bx_{A,B}&=a_A,&
\sum_Ax_{A,B}&=c_B,\\
\sum_{A,B:\,E\setminus(A\cup B)=J}x_{A,B}&=h_J,&
\sum_{A\subset M,\ B\subset M^c}x_{A,B}&=g_M.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The construction is symmetric: \(x_{A,B}=x_{B,A}\). Every supermodular set function therefore satisfies

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_W}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+U}}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[u]}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[v]}}f\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]

No individually prescribed middle labeling is needed or asserted for arbitrary complex frames. When the frame is real, the usual complex equalization and fair ordering supply the separately correct equalized middle labels.

## 2. Hodge symmetry removes the missing middle conditions

The diagonal unitary

\[
D_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)
\]

maps \(W\) to \(Z\) and preserves the two distinguished update lines in (2). Coordinate phases do not change projection probabilities, so

\[
a=c.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(u=(u_0,0)\), \(v=(0,v_0)\) for this argument. Complementary unitary minors give

\[
\mu_{W+[u]}(M)=\mu_{Z+[v]}(M^c)
=\mu_{W+[v]}(M^c).
\]

The analogous equality exchanges \(u,v\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{g_M=g_{M^c}.}
\tag{7}
\]

Basis independence extends (7) to every update basis.

The following exact finite fact explains why (7) is sufficient once the three pair-color marginals are correct.

**Lemma 2.** Suppose \(x_{A,B}=x_{B,A}\) has pair-color marginals \(a,c,h\). Suppose a desired middle table \(g\) is complement even and satisfies

\[
\sum_{M\supset p}g_M
=a_p+\sum_{B\cap p=\varnothing}c_B-h_p
\quad\text{for every pair }p.
\tag{8}
\]

Then the table of middles induced by \(x\) is exactly \(g\).

**Proof.** For one diamond, the number of its two middles containing a fixed pair \(p\) is exactly

\[
\mathbf1_{\{p=A\}}+\mathbf1_{\{p\cap B=\varnothing\}}
-\mathbf1_{\{p=J\}}.
\tag{9}
\]

This is checked by whether \(p\) contains two, one, or zero bottom sites. Averaging proves (8) for the induced table \(g_x\). Interchanging \(A,B\) complements the two middle sets, so the symmetry of \(x\) makes \(g_x\) complement even.

Let \(r=g_x-g\). It is complement even and its pair upper sums vanish. More generally, for every complement-even triple table, writing \(R_p=\sum_{T\supset p}r_T\), one has

\[
\sum_{p\subset M}R_p
=2r_M+\frac12\sum_Tr_T.
\tag{10}
\]

To see this, group the twenty triples into ten complementary pairs. The pair \(\{M,M^c\}\) contributes \(3r_M\). Every other complementary pair contributes its common value once, since its two intersection sizes with \(M\) are one and two. This proves (10). If all \(R_p=0\), summing them gives \(3\sum_Tr_T=0\), and (10) then gives \(r_M=0\) for all \(M\). ∎

For the physical tables, (8) follows independently from two-row Cauchy–Binet:

\[
\det(P_W+uu^*)_p+\det(P_W+vv^*)_p
=a_p+\det(P_W+P_U)_p-h_p.
\tag{11}
\]

The rank-four inclusion probability is
\(\det(P_W+P_U)_p=\sum_{B\cap p=\varnothing}c_B\).
Thus Lemma 2 applies. This is where Hodge symmetry genuinely changes the general six-site problem: no unresolved triple harmonic remains in this family.

### 3.1 The capped polytope has only two vertex types

Scale \(V=3C\). Its entries lie in \([0,2]\), and every row and column sums to three. At a vertex, the bipartite graph of **free** entries \(0<V_{ij}<2\) has no cycle: an alternating perturbation on a free cycle would remain feasible in both directions.

Conversely, a table whose free-entry graph is a forest is a vertex. Any feasible homogeneous perturbation vanishes on boundary entries and has zero row and column sums; deleting leaves of the forest forces every remaining perturbation entry to vanish.

At such a vertex every entry is an integer. Boundary entries are zero or two. In a free forest, a leaf equation expresses its unique free entry as an integer row or column total minus already fixed integer entries. Leaf deletion proves integrality for all entries. Thus free entries equal one.

Every row is therefore a permutation of \((0,1,2)\) or is \((1,1,1)\). If one row is all ones, the other two rows either are also all ones, which is not a vertex, or are complementary permutations of \((0,1,2)\); they have their ones in the same column. Up to row and column permutations this is the first matrix below. If no row is all ones, no column can be all ones either, by the transposed argument. The zeros, ones, and twos are then three disjoint permutation matrices, yielding the second type.

Consequently the complete list, up to independent row and column permutations, is

\[
V_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&1&1\\2&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
V_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&2&0\\2&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{16}
\]

Both free-entry graphs are forests. Their orbits have sizes 18 and 12, respectively. For \(V_1\), choose its all-one row and column (nine choices), then choose one of two corner orientations. For \(V_2\), choose its zero permutation (six choices), then one of the two derangements for its two permutation. Thus there are exactly thirty vertices.

### 3.3 A finite constructive decomposition for every matrix

The convex decomposition into vertices can be made explicit using only comparisons, additions, and minima. At the current matrix \(V\), choose the first free simple cycle in a fixed ordering. Give alternating cycle edges signs \(s_{ij}=\pm1\). Define

\[
\epsilon_+=\min\{2-V_{ij}:s_{ij}=1;\ V_{ij}:s_{ij}=-1\},
\]
\[
\epsilon_-=\min\{V_{ij}:s_{ij}=1;\ 2-V_{ij}:s_{ij}=-1\}.
\tag{18}
\]

Both are strictly positive. Let \(V^+=V+\epsilon_+s\) and \(V^-=V-\epsilon_-s\). Then

\[
V=\frac{\epsilon_-}{\epsilon_++\epsilon_-}V^+
+\frac{\epsilon_+}{\epsilon_++\epsilon_-}V^-.
\tag{19}
\]

Each child remains feasible and has strictly fewer free entries; no already fixed boundary entry changes. Recursing terminates at forest tables, which are exactly the vertices just classified. There are at most nine strict decreases on a branch. On three-by-three tables it is enough to order the nine four-cycles and six six-cycles; the implementation fixes that order.

Write the resulting convex decomposition as \(C=\sum_t\lambda_t C^{(t)}\). Translate the appropriate seed table by the row and column permutations producing each vertex. Then

\[
\boxed{x_{A,B}=\sum_t\lambda_t x^{(t)}_{A,B}.}
\tag{20}
\]

Every coefficient and every seed weight is nonnegative. The marginals (17) are affine in \(C\), so (20) gives the required three pair-color marginals, symmetry, and total mass one. Lemma 2 supplies the fourth marginal for (1)–(2). Averaging elementary supermodular inequalities proves (5). This completes Theorem 1. ∎

## 6. The universal alternating Hodge invariant

The family above has zero middle harmonic because of (7). It is useful to identify exactly what can fail outside it.

Let \(\mathcal H\) consist of real triple tables \(q\) satisfying

\[
\sum_{M\supset p}q_M=0\qquad\text{for every pair }p.
\tag{27}
\]

This space has dimension five. Indeed the pair-to-triple incidence matrix \(L\) has full row rank: for a real pair table \(z\), writing \(r_i=\sum_{j\ne i}z_{ij}\),

\[
\|L^Tz\|^2=2\sum_pz_p^2+\sum_i r_i^2\ge2\|z\|^2.
\tag{28}
\]

There are fifteen pairs and twenty triples. Every \(q\in\mathcal H\) is complement odd. To see this, its complement also satisfies (27), by inclusion-exclusion and the vanishing lower-order sums implied by (27). Its even part has zero pair sums, so Lemma 2's identity (10) makes that even part zero. C038's matching-parity cubes give an explicit basis of this same space.

For a Hermitian six-by-six matrix \(K\), define

\[
F_q(K)=\sum_{|M|=3}q_M\det K_M.
\tag{29}
\]

Let \(P_W,P_U,P_Z\) be **any** mutually orthogonal rank-two projections summing to \(I\), with no assumption (1)–(2). Let \(g^{WU}\) be the aggregate middle law for bottom \(W\) and update plane \(U\), and put

\[
\Gamma_q(W,U,Z)=\sum_Mq_M g^{WU}_M.
\tag{30}
\]

**Theorem 3 (Vandermonde factorization).** For all real \(a,b,c\),

\[
\boxed{
F_q(aP_W+bP_U+cP_Z)
=\Gamma_q(W,U,Z)(a-b)(a-c)(b-c).
}
\tag{31}
\]

Consequently \(\Gamma_q\) changes sign under every exchange of two plane labels and is invariant under cyclic permutation. In particular,

\[
q\cdot g^{WU}=q\cdot g^{UZ}=q\cdot g^{ZW}
=-q\cdot g^{UW}.
\tag{32}
\]

**Proof.** Expanding the three-by-three principal determinants after adding a scalar multiple of the identity gives

\[
\det(K_M+tI)=\det K_M
+t\sum_{p\subset M}\det K_p
+t^2\sum_{i\in M}K_{ii}+t^3.
\]

Every coefficient after the first vanishes upon summation against \(q\), by (27) and its singleton and constant consequences. Thus

\[
F_q(K+tI)=F_q(K).
\tag{33}
\]

Because the projections have rank two, \(F_q(P_W)=F_q(P_U)=F_q(P_Z)=0\). Homogeneity therefore gives

\[
F_q(sP_W+tP_U)=A s^2t+Bst^2.
\]

Cauchy–Binet identifies \(A=q\cdot g^{WU}=\Gamma_q\). Equation (33) and \(P_W+P_U=I-P_Z\) give

\[
A+B=F_q(P_W+P_U)=F_q(-P_Z)=0.
\]

Finally subtract \(cI\) and apply this two-variable identity:

\[
F_q(aP_W+bP_U+cP_Z)
=\Gamma_q(a-c)(b-c)((a-c)-(b-c)),
\]

which is (31). Permuting the three eigenvalue labels gives (32). ∎

This is an exact alternating invariant, not a positivity assertion. Its nonvanishing is the precise obstruction to removing the five missing middle coordinates using only complement-even data.

For an explicit rational nonvanishing example, let

\[

\quad Q=\left(I-\frac{2vv^T}{6}\right)
\left(I-\frac{2ww^T}{91}\right),
\tag{34}
\]

and take its consecutive two-column planes. For the matching \((0,1)|(2,4)|(3,5)\), let \(q\) vanish except on its eight transversals and assign the sign \((-1)^{s+t+r}\) to the transversal choosing endpoints \(s,t,r\). Then exact minors give

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_q=352/8281\ne0.}
\tag{35}
\]

The verifier checks (31) at three distinct eigenvalue triples for five independent matching cubes. The proof above establishes it for every parameter. Thus arbitrary balanced rank-two flags cannot simply be declared complement even. ## 7. Entropy consequence and exact completion status

For every flag in Theorem 1 and every fixed orthonormal basis \(u,v\) of its update plane, consider

\[
K(t)=P_W+\lambda(t)uu^*+\mu(t)vv^*,
\qquad 0\le\lambda,\mu\le1,
\qquad \lambda'\mu'\le0,
\tag{36}
\]

where \(\lambda,\mu\) are affine. The entropy of its occupation law is concave.

For completeness, let
\(b=\mu_W+\mu_{W+U}-\mu_{W+[u]}-\mu_{W+[v]}\).
Separate affinity gives \(p''=2\lambda'\mu'b\). DPP atom probabilities are log-submodular, and the positive diamond representation gives \(\sum_Sb_S\log p_S\le0\). Hence

\[
H''=-\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
-2\lambda'\mu'\sum_Sb_S\log p_S\le0.
\tag{37}
\]

For parameters in the open square, every atom in a positive coupling diamond has positive mixture probability. Identically zero atoms can be omitted. Continuity handles the parameter endpoints; a constant boundary eigenvalue gives an ordinary affine mixture. No joint concavity for arbitrary slopes is inferred.
