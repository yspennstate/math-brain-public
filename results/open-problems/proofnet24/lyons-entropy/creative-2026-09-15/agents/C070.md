# C070 — a diagonal threshold theorem for nonphysical diagonal intersections

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: complete restricted theorem in every dimension, with an exact nonnegative Hessian decomposition.** This is not a proof of the full Lyons entropy-concavity conjecture. Historical priority is not asserted.

The assignment was to obtain a same-sign spectral-slope result for an arbitrary fixed measured projection, beyond C006's physical scalar intersections and C075's exchangeable bases. The theorem below does so, and also permits a nonconstant diagonal part in the direction. All comparisons use the measured coordinate basis.

## 1. Main theorem and quantitative interpolation

Let \(0<K<I\) be any complex Hermitian kernel on \(n\) measured coordinates. Fix a real number \(\alpha\) and a real diagonal matrix \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_i)\), and suppose
\[
r_i:=\beta_i>0,\qquad s_i:=\alpha+\beta_i>0
\quad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{1}
\]
Set
\[
A=\alpha K+D,\qquad
T=\operatorname{diag}(\tau_i),\qquad
\tau_i=\frac{\beta_i}{\alpha+2\beta_i}
=\frac{r_i}{r_i+s_i}\in(0,1).
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem A.** If either \(K\preceq T\) or \(K\succeq T\), then
\[
\boxed{
D^2 H(P_K)[A,A]
\le-\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{A_{ii}^2}{K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
Reversing both \(\alpha\) and \(D\), hence replacing \(A\) by \(-A\), leaves the conclusion unchanged. No rank, dimension, exchangeability, sparsity, or odds condition-number restriction is imposed.

The result integrates on the affine line
\[
K(t)=K+tA=(1+\alpha t)K+tD.
\tag{4}
\]
More precisely, let \(J\) be its physical interval through zero. On every subinterval where either \(K(t)\preceq T\) for all \(t\), or \(K(t)\succeq T\) for all \(t\), the occupation entropy is concave. The endpoint comparison suffices, since each of these two matrix regions is convex.

There is a stronger interpolation conclusion. For any two endpoints \(K_0,K_1\) on one such certified subinterval, write \(K_\theta=(1-\theta)K_0+\theta K_1\) and \(h(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&H(P_{K_\theta})-(1-\theta)H(P_{K_0})-\theta H(P_{K_1})\\
&\quad\ge\sum_i\bigl[h((K_\theta)_{ii})
-(1-\theta)h((K_0)_{ii})-\theta h((K_1)_{ii})\bigr]\\
&\quad\ge2\theta(1-\theta)
\sum_i((K_1-K_0)_{ii})^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5}
\]
Equivalently, total correlation
\[
\sum_i h(K_{ii})-H(P_K)
=D\!\left(P_K\middle\|\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bern}(K_{ii})\right)
\tag{6}
\]
is convex along each certified interval.

The proof is elementary: the exact measured Hessian is expressed as own-coordinate Fisher terms plus nonnegative contributions from full two-coordinate conditional tables.

## 2. The requested arbitrary-projection theorem

Fix **any** orthogonal projection \(P\), and let
\[
K(t)=a(t)P+b(t)(I-P),\qquad a'(t)=u,\quad b'(t)=v.
\tag{7}
\]
Both multiplicities are arbitrary. If \(u\ne v\), the full affine line meets a scalar matrix at value
\[
d=\frac{u b-v a}{u-v}.
\tag{8}
\]
The numerator and the resulting scalar intersection are constant along the line. C006 already proves concavity on the entire physical interval when \(d\in[0,1]\); in particular all opposite-sign slopes are already covered.

**Corollary B (nonphysical scalar intersection).** If \(d\notin[0,1]\), put
\[
\tau=\frac{d}{2d-1}\in(0,1).
\tag{9}
\]
Then the occupation entropy in (7) is concave on every subinterval where either
\[
\max(a(t),b(t))\le\tau\quad\text{for all }t,
\tag{10a}
\]
or
\[
\min(a(t),b(t))\ge\tau\quad\text{for all }t.
\tag{10b}
\]
The quantitative bound (5) holds as well.

If \(u=v\ne0\), the same statement holds with \(\tau=1/2\). Consequently common scalar shifts are concave throughout each region \(K\preceq I/2\) and \(K\succeq I/2\), for arbitrary kernels, not just two-value kernels.

To derive the corollary, at a nonscalar base write
\[
A=uP+v(I-P)=\alpha K+\beta I,
\quad
\alpha=\frac{u-v}{a-b},\quad
\beta=\frac{av-bu}{a-b}=-\alpha d.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(d<0\), orient the direction so \(\alpha>0\); for \(d>1\), orient it so \(\alpha<0\). In both cases \(r=\beta>0\) and \(s=\alpha+\beta>0\). Formula (2) becomes (9). Equal nonzero slopes give \(\alpha=0\), \(\beta=u\), and \(\tau=1/2\), after reversing the direction if necessary.

A further immediate corollary of Theorem A is:
\[
\boxed{K\preceq I/2\text{ or }K\succeq I/2,quad
D\text{ diagonal with all entries of one sign}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H''(K)[D,D]\le-\sum_i\frac{D_{ii}^2}{K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})}.}
\tag{12}
\]
Zero diagonal slopes follow by a limit. This does not assert the analogous conclusion for arbitrary mixed-sign diagonal directions.

## 3. Exact affine occupation operators

For each coordinate use the mass-preserving two-state linear operator
\[
G_i=\begin{pmatrix}-r_i&-s_i\\ r_i&s_i\end{pmatrix},
\tag{13}
\]
acting on that coordinate and as the identity on the others. The operators on distinct coordinates commute. Let \(p=P_K\). The following identity holds as an identity of polynomial probability tables near \(t=0\):
\[
\boxed{P_{K+tA}=\prod_i(I+tG_i)p.}
\tag{14}
\]
The factors in (14) can have negative entries; this is an algebraic identity, and no stochastic-channel inequality is applied to them. The left side is a positive table for all sufficiently small \(t\), since the base kernel is strict.

Here is a direct verification. Each factor preserves the sum of a two-state vector and sends its first moment \(x\) to
\[
t r_i+[1+t(s_i-r_i)]x=t\beta_i+(1+\alpha t)x.
\]
For a coordinate set \(S\), the transformed inclusion moment is therefore
\[
\mathbb E_p\prod_{i\in S}\bigl(t\beta_i+(1+\alpha t)X_i\bigr)
=\det\bigl((1+\alpha t)K_S+tD_S\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]
The equality follows by expanding the determinant in its diagonal columns and using \(\mathbb E\prod_{i\in U}X_i=\det K_U\). Inclusion moments determine the full table by finite inclusion-exclusion, proving (14).

In particular,
\[
p'=\sum_iG_i p,\qquad
p''=2\sum_{i<j}G_iG_jp.
\tag{16}
\]
Normalization eliminates the constant part in differentiating entropy:
\[
-H''=
\sum_x\frac{(p'_x)^2}{p_x}+\sum_x p''_x\log p_x.
\tag{17}
\]
Define
\[
\chi_i=\sum_x\frac{(G_i p)_x^2}{p_x}.
\]
Expanding the square in (17) gives the exact identity
\[
-H''=\sum_i\chi_i+2\sum_{i<j}\left[
\sum_x\frac{(G_i p)_x(G_j p)_x}{p_x}
+\sum_x(G_iG_jp)_x\log p_x\right].
\tag{18}
\]

## 4. The full conditional two-coordinate factorization

Fix \(i<j\), condition on an exact occupation configuration \(y\) of all remaining coordinates, and denote its probability by \(m_y>0\). Write the normalized conditional table, in the order \(00,01,10,11\), as
\[
(a,b,c,d),\qquad a+b+c+d=1.
\tag{19}
\]
All four numbers are positive. For now assume only its negative conditional odds property
\[
R:=\frac{ad}{bc}\le1.
\tag{20}
\]
Put
\[
X=r_i r_j a+s_i s_j d,\qquad
Y=r_i s_j b+s_i r_j c.
\tag{21}
\]
The contribution inside square brackets in (18), restricted to this context and divided by \(m_y\), is
\[
\boxed{Q_{ij,y}=X F(R)+Y G(R)
=(X-Y)F(R)+Y J(R),}
\tag{22}
\]
where
\[
F(R)=R^{-1}-1+\log R,\qquad
G(R)=1-R+\log R,
\]
\[
J(R)=F(R)+G(R)=R^{-1}-R+2\log R.
\tag{23}
\]

For completeness the finite algebra is as follows. On the normalized table,
\[
G_i p=(-r_i a-s_i c,-r_i b-s_i d,r_i a+s_i c,r_i b+s_i d),
\]
\[
G_j p=(-r_j a-s_j b,r_j a+s_j b,-r_j c-s_j d,r_j c+s_j d).
\]
Their Fisher cross is
\[
-(ad-bc)\left(
\frac{s_i s_j}{a}+\frac{s_i r_j}{b}
+\frac{r_i s_j}{c}+\frac{r_i r_j}{d}\right).
\tag{24}
\]
The coefficient of \((1,-1,-1,1)\) in \(G_iG_jp\) is \(X+Y\), so its log term is \((X+Y)\log R\). Collecting the coefficients of \(r_i r_j,s_i s_j,r_i s_j,s_i r_j\) in (24) gives (22). The \(\log m_y\) terms cancel because the four signs sum to zero.

The two functions needed for positivity satisfy
\[
F(R)\ge0,\qquad J(R)\ge0\quad(0<R\le1).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(F\), use \(z-1\ge\log z\) with \(z=1/R\). For \(J\), write \(R=e^{-x}\), \(x\ge0\): then \(J(R)=2(\sinh x-x)\ge0\). Both vanish at \(R=1\), and are strictly positive when \(R<1\).

Thus it remains to prove \(Y\ge0\) and \(X-Y\ge0\) for every conditional table. The first is immediate from (1) and positivity of the table. The second is precisely where the diagonal threshold condition enters.

## 5. Determinantal conditioning preserves diagonal comparison bounds

Conditioning a strict DPP on one present coordinate \(k\) gives the kernel
\[
K^{(1)}=K_{\bar k,\bar k}
-\frac{K_{\bar k,k}K_{k,\bar k}}{K_{kk}}.
\tag{26}
\]
Conditioning on that coordinate being absent gives
\[
K^{(0)}=K_{\bar k,\bar k}
+\frac{K_{\bar k,k}K_{k,\bar k}}{1-K_{kk}}.
\tag{27}
\]
These formulas follow from the determinant formula for conditional inclusion moments; (26) is a Schur complement, and (27) follows either by the rank-one determinant lemma or by complementing (26).

We need the following comparison property:
\[
K\preceq T\Longrightarrow K^{(e)}\preceq T_{\bar k},
\qquad
K\succeq T\Longrightarrow K^{(e)}\succeq T_{\bar k},
\quad e\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{28}
\]
To verify it, recall that if \(B\succeq\Delta>0\) with \(\Delta\) diagonal, then the Schur complement after eliminating one coordinate satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Schur}_k(B)\succeq\Delta_{\bar k}.
\tag{29}
\]
Indeed, its quadratic form at \(z\) is the minimum over the eliminated coordinate \(w\) of \((w,z)^*B(w,z)\), which is at least \(z^*\Delta_{\bar k}z\).

If \(K\succeq T\), apply (29) to (26); (27) is at least the principal restriction of \(K\). If \(K\preceq T\), (26) is at most that restriction; apply (29) to \(I-K\succeq I-T\) to handle (27). This proves (28). Iterating proves the same comparison for any full conditioning. The same argument with scalar bounds also shows that strict kernels have strict conditional kernels.

The two-coordinate conditional table in (19) therefore has a strict DPP kernel
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}x&z\\\overline z&y\end{pmatrix},
\]
which lies on the same side of \(T_{ij}=\operatorname{diag}(\tau_i,\tau_j)\) as \(K\) lies of \(T\). Its four atoms are
\[
a=(1-x)(1-y)-|z|^2,\quad
b=(1-x)y+|z|^2,
\]
\[
c=x(1-y)+|z|^2,\quad
d=xy-|z|^2.
\tag{30}
\]
In particular \(ad-bc=-|z|^2\le0\), proving (20).

Substituting (30) into (21) gives the crucial exact identity
\[
\boxed{
X-Y=(r_i+s_i)(r_j+s_j)\det(T_{ij}-C).
}
\tag{31}
\]
For instance the right side expands to
\[
[r_i-(r_i+s_i)x][r_j-(r_j+s_j)y]
-(r_i+s_i)(r_j+s_j)|z|^2,
\]
which is exactly the alternating weighted table on the left.

If \(C\preceq T_{ij}\), the matrix in (31) is positive semidefinite. If \(C\succeq T_{ij}\), it is negative semidefinite of dimension two. In either case its determinant is nonnegative. Consequently every \(Q_{ij,y}\) in (22) is nonnegative.

Combining the preceding identities yields a complete nonnegative decomposition:
\[
\boxed{
-H''=\sum_i\chi_i+
2\sum_{i<j}\sum_y m_y
\left[
(r_i+s_i)(r_j+s_j)\det(T_{ij}-C_{ij\mid y})F(R_{ij\mid y})
+Y_{ij\mid y}J(R_{ij\mid y})
\right].}
\tag{32}
\]
Every factor whose sign is used has been identified explicitly.

## 6. Retaining the marginal curvature and integrating

Let \(q_i=\Pr(X_i=1\mid X_{\bar i})\), regarded as a random conditional probability. Then
\[
\chi_i=\mathbb E\frac{[r_i(1-q_i)+s_iq_i]^2}{q_i(1-q_i)}.
\tag{33}
\]
The function inside this expectation is convex in \(q\), because
\[
\frac{[r_i(1-q)+s_iq]^2}{q(1-q)}
=r_i^2\frac{1-q}{q}+2r_i s_i+s_i^2\frac q{1-q},
\tag{34}
\]
whose second derivative is \(2r_i^2/q^3+2s_i^2/(1-q)^3\ge0\). Also \(\mathbb E q_i=K_{ii}\) and \(r_i(1-K_{ii})+s_iK_{ii}=A_{ii}\). Conditional Jensen therefore gives
\[
\chi_i\ge\frac{A_{ii}^2}{K_{ii}(1-K_{ii})}.
\tag{35}
\]
Equations (32) and (35) prove Theorem A.

To integrate, observe first that \(A>0\). For \(\alpha\ge0\), this follows from \(D>0\). For \(\alpha<0\), write
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(s_i)+(-\alpha)(I-K)>0.
\]
On the physical interval through zero, \(1+\alpha t>0\). If it vanished, (4) would equal the diagonal matrix \(-D/\alpha\), whose entries are all negative when \(\alpha>0\), or all greater than one when \(\alpha<0\). Neither matrix is physical.

At another point of the line,
\[
A=\frac\alpha{1+\alpha t}K(t)+\frac D{1+\alpha t}.
\tag{36}
\]
The rates in (1) are therefore scaled by the same positive number, so \(T\) is unchanged. Theorem A applies at every strict point on either certified side. Since \(A>0\), all interior points of a nontrivial physical segment are strict: a zero eigenvalue would forbid moving backwards, and an eigenvalue one would forbid moving forwards. Finite-law entropy continuity covers possible boundary endpoints.

The right side of (3) is exactly the second derivative of \(\sum_i h(K(t)_{ii})\). Thus \(H(P_{K(t)})-\sum_i h(K(t)_{ii})\) is concave, proving the first bound in (5). The second follows from \(-h''(x)=1/[x(1-x)]\ge4\), including boundary values by continuity.

## 7. A high-contrast, full-rank spectral interval

For every projection \(P\), choose
\[
a(t)=\frac25+t,\qquad
b(t)=\frac1{100}+\frac65t,
\qquad 0\le t\le\frac15.
\tag{37}
\]
Here the two slopes have the same sign and are unequal. The scalar intersection and threshold are
\[
d=\frac{47}{20}>1,\qquad
\tau=\frac{47}{74}.
\tag{38}
\]
Both eigenvalues stay below \(\tau\): their maxima on the interval are \(3/5\) and \(1/4\). Hence Corollary B proves concavity and (5) on the entire interval, in every dimension and for all \(P\).

At \(t=0\), the odds eigenvalue ratio is
\[
\frac{(2/5)/(3/5)}{(1/100)/(99/100)}=66.
\tag{39}
\]
The direction \(A=P+(6/5)(I-P)\) is full rank and generally nondiagonal. Thus the construction is outside the physical-scalar-intersection criterion, has no exchangeability requirement, and is not a fixed rank-two-support direction. It also does not rely on the prior odds-condition-number-three diagonal-direction certificate.

The exact verifier uses the rational rank-two projection in six coordinates
\[
P=V(V^*V)^{-1}V^*,\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\1&1\\0&1\\1&-1\\2&1\\1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{40}
\]
The proof of (37) is not specific to that example.

## 8. The remaining straddling region and a precise method boundary

For a nonphysical scalar intersection, the unresolved part of the requested family is the region in which the two spectral values straddle \(\tau\). Neither (31) nor the proof above gives a sign there. The full arbitrary-two-value theorem is therefore **not** claimed.

The need for compensation in that region is real. Take the exact conditional table
\[
(a,b,c,d)=\frac1{1000}(1,1,997,1),\qquad r_i=s_i=r_j=s_j=1.
\tag{41}
\]
It is a strict two-site DPP table: (30) is obtained from diagonal entries \(x=499/500\), \(y=1/500\), and \(|z|^2=249/250000\). Its kernel straddles \(I/2\), and \(R=1/997\). The cross contribution is
\[
Q=\frac{372504}{124625}-\log997<0.
\tag{42}
\]
The exact rational-log verifier encloses this strictly below zero. Thus the stronger proposed argument that negative conditional odds alone makes every cross term nonnegative is false. This is a counterexample only to that pairwise sign claim; the complete two-site entropy Hessian still has its own-coordinate Fisher terms.
