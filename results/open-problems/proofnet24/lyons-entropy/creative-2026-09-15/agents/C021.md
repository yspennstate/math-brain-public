# C021 — unequal replacement rates and high-rank affine directions

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a new restricted proof contribution extending C006. It does **not** prove the full Lyons conjecture. No subagents, pushes, or external messages were used. No bibliographic-priority claim is made.

## 1. Completed theorem

Let the ground set be partitioned into a set of selected sites \(I=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and its complement \(O\). Suppose

\[
K_* = \begin{pmatrix}D&B\\B^*&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad 0\le K_*\le I,
\qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m).
\tag{1}
\]

Thus the selected sites form an independent set in the support graph of the kernel. The outside block \(C\) is an **arbitrary Hermitian contraction compatible with (1)**; it may be fully coherent and have arbitrary rank. The rectangular matrix \(B\) may also have arbitrary rank.

For \(f\in[0,1]^m\), put

\[
K(f)=\begin{pmatrix}D&\operatorname{diag}(f)B\\
B^*\operatorname{diag}(f)&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \mathcal H(f)=H(P_{K(f)}).
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem A (independent-set affine directions).** Every \(K(f)\) is a positive contraction. The function \(\mathcal H\) is nonincreasing in each coordinate, separately concave, and has nonpositive mixed second derivatives wherever these are evaluated in the strict interior. In particular, if

\[
f_i(t)=a_i+b_it\in[0,1],\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\qquad b_ib_j\ge0\quad\hbox{for every }i,j,
\tag{3}
\]

then \(K(f(t))\) is an **affine kernel segment** and

\[
H(P_{K(f(t))})\ge
(1-t)H(P_{K(f(0))})+tH(P_{K(f(1))}).
\tag{4}
\]

If \(0<K_*<I\), this concavity is strict whenever some \(b_i\ne0\) has \(B_{i,O}\ne0\).

Equivalently, suppose two positive contractions have identical diagonal selected blocks and identical outside blocks, and each selected-to-outside row of the first is \(r_i\in[0,1]\) times the corresponding row of the second. Their affine interpolation obeys Lyons concavity. No diagonal endpoint is required.

The direction in (3) has the form

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\operatorname{diag}(b)B\\
B^*\operatorname{diag}(b)&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]

It can have arbitrarily many positive and negative eigenvalues. Thus the theorem is not confined to rank-one directions or inertia \((1,1)\). Section 6 supplies an exact rank-six example outside the diagonal-plus-rank-one kernel class.

## 2. Unequal replacement parameters: an exact Hessian identity

The following finite-probability statement does not require determinantal structure, negative dependence, or independence of the selected input coordinates.

Let \(p_0\) be a law on

\[
\Omega_1\times\cdots\times\Omega_m\times\mathcal Y.
\]

Choose positive reference laws \(\mu_i\) and let \(\nu=(p_0)_{\mathcal Y}\), restricting \(\mathcal Y\) to the support of \(\nu\). Write

\[
\mu=\mu_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_m\otimes\nu.
\]

The map \(R_i\) replaces coordinate \(i\) by an independent sample from \(\mu_i\), retaining every other coordinate. These maps commute and are idempotent. Define

\[
C_{i,\eta_i}=\eta_i I+(1-\eta_i)R_i,
\quad
p_\eta=\prod_i C_{i,\eta_i}p_0,
\quad
F(\eta)=D(p_\eta\Vert\mu).
\tag{6}
\]

For a positive output law \(p=p_\eta\), abbreviate

\[
q_i=R_ip,\quad \delta_i=p-q_i,
\quad J_i=\sum_x(p_x-q_{i,x})\log\frac{p_x}{q_{i,x}},
\quad \chi_i=\sum_x\frac{\delta_{i,x}^2}{p_x}.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus \(J_i=J(p,R_ip)\) is Jeffreys divergence. For \(i\ne j\), define the infinitesimal Jeffreys contraction

\[
E_{ij}(p)=
-\left.\frac{d}{ds}
J(C_{j,e^{-s}}p,C_{j,e^{-s}}R_ip)\right|_{s=0}.
\tag{8}
\]

Data processing for both relative entropies gives \(E_{ij}\ge0\). It is completely explicit: with \(q=R_ip\), \(r=\log(p/q)\), \(u=R_jp-p\), and \(v=R_jq-q\),

\[
E_{ij}=-\sum_x\left[
u_x\left(r_x+1-\frac{q_x}{p_x}\right)
+v_x\left(-r_x+1-\frac{p_x}{q_x}\right)
\right].
\tag{9}
\]

**Theorem B (unequal-rate replacement Hessian).** For \(0<\eta_i<1\),

\[
\boxed{
F_i=\frac{J_i}{\eta_i}\ge0,
\qquad
F_{ii}=\frac{\chi_i}{\eta_i^2}\ge0,
\qquad
F_{ij}=\frac{E_{ij}}{\eta_i\eta_j}\ge0\quad(i\ne j).
}
\tag{10}
\]

In particular, \(E_{ij}=E_{ji}\). The first- and second-derivative identities do **not** require \((p_0)_i=\mu_i\).

**Proof.** Idempotence and commutation give

\[
\partial_i p_\eta=\frac{p_\eta-R_ip_\eta}{\eta_i}.
\tag{11}
\]

The function \(\log(q_i/\mu)\) is independent of coordinate \(i\), and \(p\) and \(q_i\) have the same marginal on its complement. Therefore

\[
\sum_x(p_x-q_{i,x})\log\frac{q_{i,x}}{\mu_x}=0,
\]

which turns differentiation of relative entropy into the first formula in (10). The law is affine in each individual \(\eta_i\), so direct second differentiation gives the diagonal formula. For \(j\ne i\), applying \(C_{j,e^{-s}}\) to the current output is exactly the change \(\eta_j\mapsto e^{-s}\eta_j\); commutation gives the same change to \(R_ip\). Hence

\[
\partial_jJ_i=E_{ij}/\eta_j,
\]

proving the mixed formula. Equality of mixed derivatives proves symmetry. All nonnegativity statements now follow from (7) and data processing. This proves the theorem.

The cross-entropy \(-\sum_xp_\eta(x)\log\mu(x)\) is affine in the vector \(\eta\), since the \(i\)-th marginal is \(\eta_i(p_0)_i+(1-\eta_i)\mu_i\), and the outside marginal is fixed. Consequently the entropy Hessian in \(\eta\) is exactly \(-\nabla^2F\), even for unmatched reference marginals. In particular, arbitrary reference marginals still permit concavity along affine schedules whose slopes have one sign.

## 3. Convex schedules with matched marginals

Now assume \((p_0)_i=\mu_i\) for the coordinates being replaced. Their marginals remain fixed, and

\[
H(p_\eta)=\sum_i H(\mu_i)+H(\nu)-F(\eta).
\tag{12}
\]

For any twice differentiable schedule in the parameter cube, (10) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
-\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(p_{\eta(t)})
=\sum_i\chi_i\left(\frac{\eta_i'}{\eta_i}\right)^2
+\sum_{i\ne j}E_{ij}
\frac{\eta_i'}{\eta_i}\frac{\eta_j'}{\eta_j}
+\sum_iJ_i\frac{\eta_i''}{\eta_i}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Thus entropy is concave whenever, pointwise along the schedule,

\[
\eta_i''\ge0,
\qquad \eta_i'\eta_j'\ge0\quad\hbox{for every }i,j.
\tag{14}
\]

For \(\eta_i=f_i^2\), with nonnegative affine \(f_i=a_i+b_it\), put \(u_i=b_i/f_i\). Then (13) specializes to

\[
\boxed{
-H''=
\sum_i(4\chi_i+2J_i)u_i^2
+8\sum_{i<j}E_{ij}u_iu_j.
}
\tag{15}
\]

This is a nonnegative decomposition whenever the slopes have one sign. In individual amplitude coordinates,

\[
\partial_{f_i}H=-\frac{2J_i}{f_i},\qquad
\partial_{f_i}^2H=-\frac{4\chi_i+2J_i}{f_i^2},\qquad
\partial_{f_i}\partial_{f_j}H=-\frac{4E_{ij}}{f_if_j}.
\tag{16}
\]

Positive input laws justify every differentiation immediately. For arbitrary input laws, positive reference laws give full support on the retained outside support when all selected \(\eta_i<1\). Boundary parameters and references with zeros follow by positive approximation and continuity of finite Shannon entropy. Expressions with vanishing denominators are interpreted through the entropy function or one-sided limits, not by literal substitution.

## 4. Determinantal realization and proof of Theorem A

There is an unequal-coordinate version of C006's determinant identity. If \(X\sim P_K\), retain \(X_i\) with probability \(\eta_i\), otherwise replace it by an independent Bernoulli \(d_i\). For \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\eta_i})\), the output law is

\[
P_{QKQ+(I-Q^2)\operatorname{diag}(d)}.
\tag{17}
\]

Indeed, the inclusion moment on a set \(S\) is

\[
\sum_{J\subseteq S}
\left(\prod_{i\in J}\eta_i\right)\det K_J
\prod_{i\in S\setminus J}(1-\eta_i)d_i.
\tag{18}
\]

Expanding the diagonal contribution in the determinant of the matrix in (17) gives exactly (18). Inclusion moments determine a finite subset law. Parameters equal to one simply omit replacement on the corresponding sites.

Apply (17) to \(K_*\), retaining every site in \(O\), replacing selected site \(i\) toward Bernoulli \(d_i\), and setting \(\eta_i=f_i^2\). Since the selected block \(D\) is diagonal, the resulting kernel is exactly (2). In particular,

\[
P_{K(f)}=\prod_{i\in I}C_{i,f_i^2}P_{K_*}.
\tag{19}
\]

The selected reference marginals match the original marginals. Their outside marginal is \(P_C\), with no independence or diagonality requirement on that outside DPP. Thus (12) becomes

\[
H(P_{K(f)})=\sum_{i\in I}h(d_i)+H(P_C)
-D\left(P_{K(f)}\,\middle\Vert\,
\bigotimes_{i\in I}\operatorname{Bern}(d_i)\otimes P_C\right).
\tag{20}
\]

Equations (15) and (16) prove the entropy assertions.

For completeness, positivity of all the kernels in (2) is also immediate at matrix level. With \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(f_1,\ldots,f_m,1,\ldots,1)\),

\[
K(f)=QK_*Q+\operatorname{diag}((1-f_i^2)d_i,0_O),
\]

and

\[
I-K(f)=Q(I-K_*)Q+
\operatorname{diag}((1-f_i^2)(1-d_i),0_O).
\tag{21}
\]

Both are positive semidefinite.

If \(0<K_*<I\), the intermediate kernels and all their atoms are strict. A nonzero row \(f_iB_{i,O}\) implies \(R_ip\ne p\), because some pair occupation covariance is \(-|f_iB_{ij}|^2<0\). Hence \(J_i>0\). An active nonnegative affine \(f_i\) is positive in the open time interval; therefore an active nonzero row makes the corresponding diagonal summand in (15) strictly positive. This proves strictness. Approximating \(K_*\) by \((1-\varepsilon)K_*+\varepsilon I/2\) preserves the required block pattern and extends concavity to every boundary contraction.

## 5. A valid complex-phase extension

The same theorem allows selected rows \(z_i(t)B_{i,O}\), where \(z_i(t)=\alpha_i+\beta_it\) is complex affine and \(|z_i(t)|\le1\). The associated Hermitian kernel is still affine in real \(t\). A diagonal unitary on the selected sites changes \(z_i\) to \(|z_i|\) without changing any determinantal inclusion probability. Hence (19) holds with

\[
\eta_i(t)=|z_i(t)|^2,
\quad
\eta_i''=2|\beta_i|^2,
\quad
\eta_i'=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{z_i}\beta_i).
\tag{22}
\]

Concavity follows whenever the radial derivatives in (22) have one sign at each time. Their common sign may change during the segment. With only one selected varying site the pairwise sign requirement is empty, so **every complex affine scalar multiple of that row** is covered, including segments passing through zero.

If two endpoints have such complex row multiples and one endpoint maximizes all row magnitudes, it can serve as \(K_*\) after rowwise normalization. For one selected site some endpoint always does, because the norm of a complex affine scalar is convex. No claim is made here about arbitrary changes in a row's direction within \(\mathbb C^{|O|}\).

## 6. Exact higher-rank example with a coherent outside block

Take three selected and three outside sites and set

\[
D=\tfrac12I_3,
\qquad
B=\frac1{40}\begin{pmatrix}3&1&0\\0&2&1\\1&0&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C=\tfrac12I_3+
\frac1{40}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&2\\1&0&-1\\2&-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{23}
\]

The largest absolute off-diagonal row sum of \(K_*\) is \(7/40\). Thus its spectrum lies in \([13/40,27/40]\), so it is a strict positive contraction. Set

\[
f_1(t)=\tfrac14+\tfrac34t,
\qquad f_2(t)=\tfrac12+\tfrac12t,
\qquad f_3(t)=\tfrac34+\tfrac14t.
\tag{24}
\]

Both endpoints are nondiagonal; the outside block remains coherent. The integer matrix in \(40B\) has determinant \(13\), so (5) has rank six and inertia \((3,3)\). The theorem proves strict entropy concavity on this entire segment.

No kernel on this segment is diagonal plus a signed rank-one matrix: the three selected off-diagonal entries vanish, which in such a representation would force at most one selected rank-one vector coordinate to be nonzero, whereas all three selected-to-outside rows are nonzero. This also distinguishes the example from C018's diagonal-plus-signed-rank-one base theorem. The proof places no smallness requirement beyond contraction; the modest numbers in (23) make feasibility transparent.

## 7. Exact obstruction to an unrestricted mixed-sign channel lemma

The same-sign condition cannot simply be removed from the universal replacement theorem, even after assuming that the replaced input coordinates are mutually independent.

Let \((X_1,X_2,Y)\in\{0,1\}^3\) have the strictly positive law

\[
p_\varepsilon(x_1,x_2,y)
=\frac18\left[1+\varepsilon(-1)^{x_1+x_2+y}\right],
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.
\tag{25}
\]

Every pair of coordinates is independent uniform. Replace only \(X_1,X_2\), toward their uniform marginals, using

\[
f_1(t)=\frac{1+t}{2},\quad
f_2(t)=\frac{1-t}{2},\quad
\eta_i=f_i^2,
\qquad -1<t<1.
\]

The output again has form (25), with parity coefficient

\[
r(t)=\frac{\varepsilon}{16}(1-t^2)^2.
\]

Define

\[
\phi(r)=\frac12\big[(1+r)\log(1+r)+(1-r)\log(1-r)\big].
\]

Then \(H(t)=3\log2-\phi(r(t))\), \(\phi'(r)=\operatorname{arctanh}(r)\), \(r'(0)=0\), and \(r''(0)=-\varepsilon/4\). Consequently

\[
\boxed{H''(0)=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
\operatorname{arctanh}(\varepsilon/16)>0.}
\tag{26}
\]

This is an exact channel obstruction, **not a determinantal counterexample**. A DPP with all its pair covariances zero has a diagonal kernel and is product; (25) is nonproduct when \(\varepsilon\ne0\).

The exact remaining mixed-sign obligation for the independent-set DPP family is particularly small to state. At a positive law in (19), form the symmetric matrix

\[
M_{ii}=4\chi_i+2J_i,
\qquad M_{ij}=4E_{ij}\ (i\ne j).
\tag{27}
\]

Equation (15) is precisely \(-H''=u^TMu\). Every entry of \(M\) is nonnegative, proving the completed same-sign theorem. Arbitrary real slope signs would follow from the additional lemma \(M\succeq0\) for these determinantal laws. Formula (26) proves that this lemma is false for general laws with independent selected marginals. It therefore needs genuinely determinantal input; no such proof is asserted here. If the original law factors into disjoint independent components, cross-component dissipations vanish and signs can be chosen independently between components, a direct additive extension.
