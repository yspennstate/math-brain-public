# C007 — one coherent endpoint block: exact Hessian reduction, negative cross dissipation, and a local theorem

Date: 2026-09-15. Assignment: extend C006's resampling/Jeffreys method to an endpoint with one genuinely non-diagonal two-coordinate block and otherwise diagonal entries, with arbitrary coupling and kernel on the other coordinates. No subagents, external messages, or repository pushes were used.

## Results and exact scope

Let \(U=\{1,2\}\), \(O=E\setminus U\), and
\[
D=B\oplus D_O,\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}d_1&c\\\bar c&d_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D_O=\operatorname{diag}(d_j:j\in O).
\]

This report does **not** prove concavity along the entire segment from an arbitrary \(K\) to \(D\). It gives three complete bounded results:

1. **An exact one-block Hessian reduction.** The measured entropy Hessian is the sum of the nonnegative exterior-resampling curvature, the nonnegative average conditional two-site curvature, and twice a collection of measured block/exterior Jeffreys dissipation terms. The partial Gaussian attenuation operations commute exactly. The only missing sign in this decomposition is stated explicitly.
2. **A strict three-site DPP counterexample to the missing termwise sign.** For rational strict contractions \(K,D\), one block/exterior Jeffreys dissipation is rigorously between \(-5\cdot10^{-6}\) and \(-4\cdot10^{-6}\). Thus the natural proposal to apply measured data processing to each commuting block channel fails **inside the Gaussian/DPP class**, even when the two conditional kernels differ by rank one. The same example increases measured mutual information under attenuation. This does not refute entropy concavity.
3. **A local positive theorem with arbitrary external direction.** If \(D\) is strict and \(c\ne0\), then every fixed nonzero Hermitian line through \(D\) has strictly concave occupation entropy on a sufficiently small interval around \(D\). For directions that only connect different blocks, the entropy loss starts at order four, with an explicit strictly positive coefficient in terms of the measured rank-one Fisher metric of \(B\).

The first result identifies a precise replacement for the all-nonnegative decomposition in C006. The second proves that its new cross terms cannot individually be declared nonnegative. The third gives a positive endpoint result without imposing any restriction on the external entries of the direction.

## 1. Two commuting attenuation operations

Write an arbitrary strict contraction as
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}A&V\\V^*&C\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_s^B(K)
&=\begin{pmatrix}
B+s(A-B)&\sqrt{s}\,V\\
\sqrt{s}\,V^*&C
\end{pmatrix},\\
\mathcal R_t(K)
&=\begin{pmatrix}
A&\sqrt{t}\,V\\
\sqrt{t}\,V^*&D_O+t(C-D_O)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\mathcal R_t\) attenuates all the outside singleton modes, and \(\mathcal A_s^B\) attenuates the pair against the coherent reservoir \(B\).

Both operations preserve positive contractions. For example, with
\(S_s=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{s}I_U,I_O)\),
\[
\mathcal A_s^B(K)=S_sKS_s+(1-s)(B\oplus0),
\]
and
\[
I-\mathcal A_s^B(K)
=S_s(I-K)S_s+(1-s)((I_U-B)\oplus0).
\]
The outside operation has the analogous representation.

The operations commute, have multiplicative semigroup parameters, and their composition is
\[
K_{s,t}=
\begin{pmatrix}
B+s(A-B)&\sqrt{st}\,V\\
\sqrt{st}\,V^*&D_O+t(C-D_O)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular,
\[
K_{\eta,\eta}=D+\eta(K-D).
\tag{3}
\]

The measured action of \(\mathcal R_t\) is the product of the usual singleton replacement maps from C006. To see this without quantum notation, expand every inclusion determinant after factoring \(\sqrt t\) from rows and columns indexed by retained outside coordinates. Selecting the diagonal \(D_O\) contributions gives exactly the independent replacement expansion. It also follows that these replacement maps commute with the measured action of \(\mathcal A_s^B\) whenever the latter is evaluated with its required coherence information.

The measured block operation alone has a useful conditional description. For an outside atom \(Y\subset O\), put
\[
Q_Y=C-I_{O\setminus Y},\qquad
q_Y=(-1)^{|O\setminus Y|}\det Q_Y,\qquad
C_Y=A-VQ_Y^{-1}V^*.
\tag{4}
\]
Strictness gives \(q_Y>0\), and the conditional kernel \(C_Y\) is a strict two-site contraction. Under \(\mathcal A_s^B\), the outside law stays fixed and
\[
C_Y(s)=B+s(C_Y-B).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the measured entropy is separately concave in \(s\) and \(t\): fixed \(t\) uses the established two-site theorem in every conditional fiber, and fixed \(s\) uses C006's exterior replacement theorem. Separate concavity alone does not control the diagonal \(s=t\).

## 2. The minimal coherence extension of the occupation dynamics

For each outside atom define
\[
z_Y=q_Y(C_Y)_{12}.
\tag{6}
\]
The probability vector in that fiber is ordered \(00,10,01,11\), and
\[
|z_Y|^2=p_{10,Y}p_{01,Y}-p_{00,Y}p_{11,Y}.
\tag{7}
\]
The phase of \(z_Y\), which (7) does not determine, is the additional information required by a coherent reservoir.

Let \(R_j\) replace coordinate \(j\) by \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_j)\), and let
\[
L_0=\sum_{j\in E}(R_j-I),\qquad
L_O=\sum_{j\in O}(R_j-I).
\]
The same outside replacement map acts on the complex vector \(z=(z_Y)_Y\) by linearity. In semigroup time \(\tau=-\log\eta\), the full interpolation (3) satisfies the closed system
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\dot p_{\cdot,Y}
&=(L_0p)_{\cdot,Y}
-2\operatorname{Re}(\bar c z_Y)(1,-1,-1,1),\\
\dot z&=L_Oz-z+cq.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(q=(q_Y)\) is the outside marginal.

For the block part of (8), write \(a_i=q_Y(C_Y)_{ii}\) and
\(r=p_{11,Y}=q_Y\det C_Y\). Formula (5) immediately gives
\[
\dot a_i=d_iq_Y-a_i,\qquad
\dot z_Y=cq_Y-z_Y,
\]
and
\[
\dot r=-2r+d_2a_1+d_1a_2-2\operatorname{Re}(\bar c z_Y).
\tag{9}
\]
The first three terms in (9) are exactly the two diagonal replacement generators. This proves the first equation of (8) for the block part.

For the exterior part, the marked moments of \(z\) are
\[
\sum_{Y\supset S}z_Y
=\det\begin{pmatrix}K_{12}&K_{1S}\\K_{S2}&K_{SS}\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{10}
\]
Schur complements and inclusion-exclusion give (10) from (4). The same diagonal determinant expansion used for ordinary inclusion moments proves that exterior attenuation replaces coordinates in these marked moments too. This proves the second equation of (8) and supplies a purely determinantal justification of the closed system.

There cannot be a universal map on occupation probabilities alone that implements coherent attenuation. For an exact example, take
\[
B=K_+=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_-=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&-1/4\\-1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both input occupation laws are \((3,5,5,3)/16\). At \(\eta=1/2\), interpolation with \(B\) sends \(K_+\) to \(B\), but sends \(K_-\) to \(I/2\), whose occupation law is uniform. Therefore no input-independent Markov map, or even a deterministic function of the input probability vector, can implement the interpolation for all kernels. The closed system (8) identifies exactly what this elementary obstruction omits.

## 3. An exact measured Hessian decomposition with one coherent block

All formulas in this section hold at strict \(K,D\). Set
\[
\mu_0=P_{\operatorname{diag}D}
\]
and let \(F(p)=D(p\Vert\mu_0)\). Since every singleton marginal of \(P_{D+\eta(K-D)}\) is affine in \(\eta\), its cross entropy against \(\mu_0\) is affine. Consequently
\[
-\eta^2H''(\eta)=F_{\tau\tau}+F_\tau,\qquad \tau=-\log\eta.
\tag{11}
\]

For positive laws define the Jeffreys divergence
\[
J(p,q)=D(p\Vert q)+D(q\Vert p).
\]
For each outside coordinate write \(I_j(p)=J(p,R_jp)\).

### 3.1 The exterior curvature

Define
\[
\chi_j(p)=\sum_x\frac{(p_x-(R_jp)_x)^2}{p_x},
\]
and, for \(k\ne j\),
\[
\mathscr E_k(p,R_jp)=
-\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}
J(C_{k,e^{-\tau}}p,C_{k,e^{-\tau}}R_jp)\right|_{\tau=0},
\tag{12}
\]
where \(C_{k,s}=sI+(1-s)R_k\). Set
\[
Q_O(p)=
\sum_{j\in O}\chi_j(p)+
\sum_{\substack{j,k\in O\\j\ne k}}\mathscr E_k(p,R_jp).
\tag{13}
\]
Each term is nonnegative by the same own-coordinate identity and classical data processing used in C006. Empty sums are zero.

### 3.2 The block curvature

For a strict two-site kernel \(M\), write \(p_M=(x,y,z,w)\), and for a Hermitian direction \(E\) define
\[
g_2(M;E)
=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{(Dp_M[E]_\alpha)^2}{p_M(\alpha)}
-2\det(E)\log\frac{yz}{xw}
=-D^2H(P_M)[E,E].
\tag{14}
\]
The established full two-site entropy theorem gives \(g_2(M;E)\ge0\). The contribution of the coherent block is
\[
G_B(K)=\sum_Y q_Y\,g_2(C_Y;C_Y-B)\ge0.
\tag{15}
\]

### 3.3 The cross terms and the identity

Let \(p_s=P_{\mathcal A_s^B(K)}\). Define
\[
\boxed{
E_{B,j}(K)=
\left.\frac{d}{ds}J(p_s,R_jp_s)\right|_{s=1}
=
-\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}J(p_{e^{-\tau}},R_jp_{e^{-\tau}})\right|_{\tau=0}.
}
\tag{16}
\]
These terms are computed from actual measured laws. The outside replacement of the full conditional state commutes with block attenuation, so \(R_jp_s\) is the measured block attenuation of the correspondingly replaced input.

If \(u=\partial_sp_s|_1\) and \(r=R_jp\), an explicit finite formula is
\[
E_{B,j}
=\sum_x\left[
u_x\left(\log\frac{p_x}{r_x}+1-\frac{r_x}{p_x}\right)
+(R_ju)_x\left(-\log\frac{p_x}{r_x}+1-\frac{p_x}{r_x}\right)
\right],
\tag{17}
\]
where the derivative in each outside fiber is
\[
u_{\cdot,Y}=q_Y\,Dp_{C_Y}[C_Y-B].
\tag{18}
\]
Thus no unmeasured relative entropy occurs in (16)–(18).

**Theorem 1 (exact one-block reduction).** At
\(K_\eta=D+\eta(K-D)\), \(0<\eta\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}H(P_{K_\eta})
=
Q_O(P_{K_\eta})+G_B(K_\eta)
+2\sum_{j\in O}E_{B,j}(K_\eta).
}
\tag{19}
\]

**Proof.** In separate semigroup times for the commuting operations of §1, let \(V_O\) and \(V_B\) denote their generators on the extended state \((p,z)\). The exterior derivative of \(F\) is
\[
D_{V_O}F=-I_O,\qquad I_O=\sum_{j\in O}I_j.
\]
Put \(I_B=-D_{V_B}F\). The total derivative is \(-I_O-I_B\). The pure exterior part of \(F_{\tau\tau}+F_\tau\) is \(Q_O\), by C006's dissipation identity restricted to the outside coordinates. The pure block part is \(G_B\), since (5) gives an affine conditional two-site kernel in \(s\), while the cross entropy against \(\mu_0\) is affine in \(s\).

Commutation gives
\[
D_{V_B}I_O=D_{V_O}I_B.
\]
Thus the two mixed contributions in the second derivative add to
\[
-2D_{V_B}I_O=2\sum_{j\in O}E_{B,j}.
\]
Combining the three parts with (11) proves (19). Equivalently, this follows by twice differentiating the smooth two-parameter function \(F(P_{K_{s,t}})\); the equality of mixed derivatives is then literal scalar calculus. \(\square\)

When \(B\) is diagonal, its block generator is the sum of two measured singleton replacement generators, so each \(E_{B,j}\) is a sum of nonnegative classical dissipation terms. In that case (19) recovers C006. For a coherent \(B\), the sign of \(E_{B,j}\) is genuinely new.

A global one-block endpoint proof through this identity must establish the combined inequality
\[
2\sum_jE_{B,j}\ge-Q_O-G_B.
\tag{20}
\]
The next section proves that the stronger proposed inequality \(E_{B,j}\ge0\) is false, even in dimension three.

## 4. Exact negative cross dissipation for a strict three-site DPP

Let
\[
A=\frac1{20}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&5\end{pmatrix},
\quad
B=\frac1{5000}\begin{pmatrix}1003&1996\\1996&3997\end{pmatrix},
\quad
Q=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\-2&4\end{pmatrix},
\quad \delta=\frac1{1000}.
\tag{21}
\]
The matrix \(Q\) is a rank-one projection. The reservoir eigenvalues are
\(1/1000,999/1000\), so \(B\) is a strict coherent contraction.

Take the strict three-site kernel
\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/20&1/10&1/100\\
1/10&1/4&-1/50\\
1/100&-1/50&1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D=B\oplus(1/2).
\tag{22}
\]
Positivity is certified by the leading principal minors:
\[
K:\quad \frac1{20},\ \frac1{400},\ \frac{233}{200000};
\qquad
I-K:\quad \frac{19}{20},\ \frac{281}{400},\
\frac{70167}{200000}.
\tag{23}
\]
All are positive.

The outside bit has probability \(1/2\). Its two conditional pair kernels are
\[
C_0=A+\delta Q,\qquad C_1=A-\delta Q.
\tag{24}
\]
Under block attenuation put
\[
A_s=B+s(A-B),\qquad
p_s=P_{A_s},\qquad v_s=Dp_{A_s}[Q].
\]
Because \(\det Q=0\), every two-site atom is affine in a displacement along \(Q\). Hence the two conditional laws are exactly
\[
p_s^+=p_s+\delta s\,v_s,\qquad
p_s^-=p_s-\delta s\,v_s.
\tag{25}
\]
Use \(R_3\) with reference \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). The full three-site law is the equiprobable pair of conditionals in (25), while its replacement is the product with pair marginal \(p_s\).

Let \(r_\alpha(s)=\delta s\,v_{s,\alpha}/p_{s,\alpha}\). Directly from the definition of \(J\),
\[
J(P_{\mathcal A_s^B(K)},R_3P_{\mathcal A_s^B(K)})
=\delta s\sum_\alpha v_{s,\alpha}\operatorname{atanh}r_\alpha(s).
\tag{26}
\]
This is also one quarter of \(J(p_s^+,p_s^-)\).

At \(s=1\), let \(p=p_1\), \(v=v_1\),
\(p'=\partial_sp_s|_1\), and \(w=\partial_s(sv_s)|_1\).
All required data are rational:

| vector | \(00\) | \(10\) | \(01\) | \(11\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(p\) | \(281/400\) | \(19/400\) | \(99/400\) | \(1/400\) |
| \(v\) | \(-83/100\) | \(3/100\) | \(63/100\) | \(17/100\) |
| \(p'\) | \(17368/25000\) | \(-3633/25000\) | \(-13603/25000\) | \(-132/25000\) |
| \(w\) | \(-32493/25000\) | \(12493/25000\) | \(27493/25000\) | \(-7493/25000\) |
| \(r=\delta v/p\) | \(-83/70250\) | \(3/4750\) | \(7/2750\) | \(17/250\) |

Differentiating (26) gives the exact expression
\[
E_{B,3}(K)
=\delta\sum_\alpha\left[
w_\alpha\operatorname{atanh}r_\alpha
+\frac{v_\alpha(\delta w_\alpha-r_\alpha p'_\alpha)}
{p_\alpha(1-r_\alpha^2)}
\right].
\tag{27}
\]

**Theorem 2 (strict failure of measured cross data processing).**
For (21)–(22),
\[
\boxed{
-\frac5{10^6}<E_{B,3}(K)<-\frac4{10^6}.
}
\tag{28}
\]

**Exact sign certificate.** For each rational \(r\), use
\[
\operatorname{atanh}r
=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\frac{r^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+\varepsilon_r,
\qquad
|\varepsilon_r|
\le\frac{|r|^{11}}{11(1-r^2)}.
\tag{29}
\]
Substituting these rational lower and upper bounds in (27), reversing the endpoints when their coefficient is negative, proves (28) using rational arithmetic only. The complete fractions are saved in computations/C007/exact_obstruction.json. The interval is approximately
\[
[-4.445164932444666,\,-4.445164932436796]\cdot10^{-6}.
\]
The displayed decimal endpoints merely summarize the exact rational bounds; the program asserts the simpler rational inequalities in (28).

Since attenuation time is \(\tau=-\log s\), (28) means the measured Jeffreys divergence **increases** for a sufficiently short positive attenuation time. Both compared extended inputs are Gaussian: one is the strict DPP (22), and the other is obtained from it by complete replacement of its outside mode. Commuting the two attenuation channels therefore does not repair measured data processing.

The obstruction is already infinitesimal in the rank-one conditional displacement. Let
\[
\mathcal F_A(Q)=\sum_\alpha \frac{Dp_A[Q]_\alpha^2}{p_A(\alpha)}.
\]
The same rational data give
\[
\mathcal F_A(Q)=\frac{831792}{58729},
\]
and
\[
\left.\frac{d}{ds}\big[s^2\mathcal F_{A_s}(Q)\big]\right|_{s=1}
=-\frac{382198673104}{86227386025}<0.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus measured rank-one Fisher information can increase under coherent block attenuation too. The sign is not an artifact of large conditional separation or non-Gaussian inputs.

For completeness, the measured mutual information satisfies
\[
I_s=\sum_\alpha p_{s,\alpha}\,
\frac{(1+r_\alpha)\log(1+r_\alpha)
+(1-r_\alpha)\log(1-r_\alpha)}2,
\]
and
\[
I_s'\big|_1=
\frac12\sum_\alpha p'_\alpha\log(1-r_\alpha^2)
+\delta\sum_\alpha w_\alpha\operatorname{atanh}r_\alpha.
\tag{31}
\]
Rational Taylor bounds certify
\[
-\frac{223}{10^8}<I_s'\big|_1<-\frac{221}{10^8}<0.
\]
Hence measured mutual information with the outside bit increases under a short attenuation step as well.

This example does **not** give positive entropy curvature. Independently evaluated terms in (19) at \(\eta=1\) are
\[
Q_O\approx1.42169378254\cdot10^{-5},\quad
G_B\approx2.36478321136,\quad
2E_{B,3}\approx-8.89032986480\cdot10^{-6}.
\]
Their sum is approximately \(2.36478853797>0\), so the actual entropy Hessian there is strictly negative. What fails is the proposed termwise Jeffreys contraction step.

## 5. Local concavity at a coherent block endpoint with arbitrary external direction

This section proves a positive result that does not assume any sign for the cross terms of (19).

**Theorem 3 (every fixed line is locally strictly concave).**
Suppose \(0<B<I_2\), \(B_{12}\ne0\), and \(0<d_j<1\) for \(j\in O\).
For every nonzero Hermitian \(A\) on the full coordinate set, there is
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
t\longmapsto H(P_{D+tA})
\]
is strictly concave on \([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\), with
\(0<D+tA<I\) throughout that interval.

In particular, for every other kernel \(K\ne D\), the full segment
\(D+\eta(K-D)\) is strictly concave on a sufficiently short initial interval.
The neighborhood size is allowed to depend on the direction.

### 5.1 Directions that vary a block marginal

Let \(\mu_t\) be the product of the pair marginal and all singleton outside marginals of \(P_{D+tA}\). Cross-block kernel entries are of order \(t\), and their first contribution to a principal determinant requires at least two such entries. Therefore
\[
P_{D+tA}-\mu_t=O(t^2)
\]
atomwise. Since all reference atoms are positive near zero,
\[
D(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu_t)=O(t^4).
\tag{32}
\]
The entropy chain identity for total correlation gives
\[
H(P_{D+tA})
=H_2(B+tA_{UU})+\sum_{j\in O}h(d_j+tA_{jj})
-D(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu_t).
\tag{33}
\]
Consequently,
\[
H''(0)=
D^2H_2(B)[A_{UU},A_{UU}]
-\sum_{j\in O}\frac{A_{jj}^2}{d_j(1-d_j)}.
\tag{34}
\]

The two-site Hessian is strictly negative in every nonzero direction at a strict \(B\) with \(B_{12}\ne0\). Here is a short deduction from the established two-site formula. With \(E\ne0\), let \(\Delta=\det E\), \(\mathcal I\) be the measured Fisher term, and
\(L=\log(p_{10}p_{01}/(p_{00}p_{11}))>0\). Then
\[
D^2H_2(B)[E,E]=-\mathcal I+2\Delta L.
\]
If \(\Delta<0\), this is strictly negative. If \(\Delta=0\), a nonzero Hermitian rank-one \(E\) has a nonzero diagonal entry, so some marginal derivative is nonzero and \(\mathcal I>0\). If \(\Delta>0\), the established two-site bound is at most \(-8\Delta<0\).

It follows from (34) and continuity that every direction with
\(A_{UU}\ne0\) or some \(A_{jj}\ne0\), \(j\in O\), has \(H''(t)<0\) on a sufficiently small interval.

### 5.2 Pure inter-block directions and their exact fourth-order coefficient

It remains to consider
\[
A_{UU}=0,\qquad A_{jj}=0\quad(j\in O).
\tag{35}
\]
Now every block marginal is fixed. Put
\[
\mu=P_B\otimes\bigotimes_{j\in O}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(d_j),
\qquad v_j=d_j(1-d_j),
\qquad Z_j(y_j)=\frac{y_j-d_j}{v_j}.
\]
For \(j\in O\), let \(a_j=A_{Uj}\in\mathbb C^2\), and define the pair score
\[
S_j(x)=\frac{Dp_B[a_ja_j^*](x)}{p_B(x)}.
\tag{36}
\]
Its \(P_B\)-mean is zero.

**Theorem 4 (explicit quartic loss).** Under (35),
\[
\boxed{
H(P_{D+tA})=H(P_D)-\frac{t^4}{2}\,\mathcal C_D(A)+O(t^6),
}
\tag{37}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_D(A)=
\sum_{j\in O}\frac{\mathcal F_B(a_ja_j^*)}{v_j}
+\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\in O}}
\frac{|A_{jk}|^4}{v_jv_k},
\qquad
\mathcal F_B(Q)=\sum_x\frac{Dp_B[Q](x)^2}{p_B(x)}.
}
\tag{38}
\]
For every nonzero direction satisfying (35), \(\mathcal C_D(A)>0\).

**Proof of the coefficient.** Write
\[
\frac{P_{D+tA}(x,y)}{\mu(x,y)}
=1+t^2F_2(x,y)+t^3F_3(x,y)+O(t^4).
\tag{39}
\]
The order-two term is exactly
\[
F_2(x,y)=
-\sum_{j\in O}S_j(x)Z_j(y_j)
-\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\in O}}
|A_{jk}|^2Z_j(y_j)Z_k(y_k).
\tag{40}
\]

For the first sum, the exact joint marginal on \(U\cup\{j\}\) gives
\[
P(X_U=x,X_j=1)
=d_jp_B(x)-t^2Dp_B[a_ja_j^*](x).
\tag{41}
\]
This is the bordered determinant identity, followed by inclusion-exclusion on the absent pair coordinates. The \(X_j=0\) atom has the opposite correction, which proves the first term in (40). The second sum follows from
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_j,X_k)=-t^2|A_{jk}|^2.
\]
At order two, no term can involve two external coordinates and the pair block simultaneously: a determinant cycle that visits all these blocks needs at least three inter-block entries. Equivalently, expand in the orthogonal exterior contrasts \(Z_S\); for \(|S|=2\), the leading determinant term is
\(-t^2|A_{jk}|^2p_B(x)\), and for \(|S|\ge3\) there is no order-two term. This proves (40) completely.

Independence under \(\mu\) and the zero means of the \(Z_j\) and \(S_j\) make all the displayed components in (40) orthogonal. Since
\(\mathbb E Z_j^2=v_j^{-1}\),
\[
\mathbb E_\mu F_2^2=\mathcal C_D(A).
\tag{42}
\]

We also have
\[
\mathbb E_\mu F_2F_3=0.
\tag{43}
\]
Indeed, in the exterior contrast expansion there is no order-three term with zero exterior coordinates, since the pair marginal is fixed. There is none with exactly one exterior coordinate, since (41) is exactly quadratic. For exactly two exterior coordinates, the order-three coefficient has zero \(P_B\)-mean: summing out the pair leaves a two-site outside marginal whose density has only its quadratic correction. Those components are therefore orthogonal to the \(x\)-independent exterior-pair terms of \(F_2\). Every component involving at least three exterior coordinates is orthogonal to all of \(F_2\). This proves (43).

The block marginals are fixed, so
\[
H(P_D)-H(P_{D+tA})=D(P_{D+tA}\Vert\mu).
\]
Use
\[
(1+u)\log(1+u)=u+\frac{u^2}{2}-\frac{u^3}{6}+O(u^4)
\]
in (39), together with normalization, (42), and (43). The fourth-order term is \(\mathcal C_D(A)/2\), the fifth-order term vanishes, and all remaining terms begin at order six. This proves (37).

If some \(A_{jk}\ne0\) between outside coordinates, its contribution in (38) is positive. Otherwise a nonzero \(A\) satisfying (35) has some \(a_j\ne0\). The rank-one matrix \(a_ja_j^*\) has a strictly positive diagonal entry, so its measured probability derivative has a nonzero marginal derivative. Hence \(\mathcal F_B(a_ja_j^*)>0\). This proves positivity. \(\square\)

Analyticity of the finite positive atom law allows differentiation of (37):
\[
H''(t)=-6\mathcal C_D(A)t^2+O(t^4)<0
\]
for all sufficiently small nonzero \(t\). At \(t=0\) the second derivative is zero. Thus \(H\) is strictly concave on a sufficiently small interval across zero. Together with §5.1 this proves Theorem 3.

The local theorem allows arbitrary complex external entries and arbitrary rank of \(A\). It is not a uniform neighborhood theorem for all directions simultaneously, and it does not imply global concavity along the segment.
