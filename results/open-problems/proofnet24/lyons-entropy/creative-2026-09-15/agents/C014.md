## 3. The full-Hessian scalar, without matrix square roots

This section restates the exact source criterion in the form used by the certificate. It is a computational realization of old-097, not a separate claimed discovery.

Freeze \(f(S)=-\log p_S\), and let
\[
\tau=\sum_{S\subseteq[3]}(-1)^{3-|S|}f(S),
\qquad
c_{jk}=f(\{j,k\})-f(\{j\})-f(\{k\})+f(\varnothing).
\]
Put
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12})+\tau K,
\qquad \delta=\det M.
\]
The connected interior case has \(M>0\), as proved in old-097. The certificate also checks positive diagonal entries, all principal minors of order two, and positive determinant of \(M\) for every evaluated phase interval.

Use any real coordinate basis of \(\operatorname{Herm}_3\). Let \(F\) be its Fisher matrix, \(g\) the coordinate gradient of the ordinary determinant evaluated at \(M\), and \(G\) the coordinate Hessian of that determinant evaluated at \(M\). Define
\[
N=F+\frac{gg^{\mathsf T}}\delta-G,
\qquad
\boxed{\Phi(K)=\frac{g^{\mathsf T}N^{-1}g}{\delta}.}
\tag{11}
\]
These formulas have no square roots and retain all nine real Hermitian coordinates.

Indeed, the source frozen-log identity and the three-dimensional determinant identity give
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+D^2\det(M)[A,A].
\]
Also,
\[
D^2\det(M)[A,A]
=\delta\left\{\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A)^2-
\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}AM^{-1}A)\right\}.
\]
Consequently \(N\) is positive definite and the full entropy Hessian matrix is exactly
\[
\mathscr H=-N+\frac{gg^{\mathsf T}}\delta.
\tag{12}
\]
After congruence by \(N^{-1/2}\), its spectrum is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{spec}(N^{-1/2}\mathscr H N^{-1/2})
=\{-1\text{ (multiplicity eight)},\ \Phi(K)-1\}.}
\tag{13}
\]
This proves both the equivalence (1) and the claimed full-Hessian interpretation of the counterexample. The scalar is invariant under real changes of Hermitian coordinate basis. In particular, using unnormalized real and imaginary edge coordinates in the certificate introduces no normalization error.

\[

\[
