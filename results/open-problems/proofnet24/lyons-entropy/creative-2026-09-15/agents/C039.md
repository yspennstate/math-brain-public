## 1. Exact four-coordinate reduction

Label the sites by \((r,\sigma)\), where \(r=0,1,2\) and \(\sigma=+1,-1\); their integer labels are \(2r\) and \(2r+1\). Let the two rows of \(V_r\) be \(v_{r,+}\) and \(v_{r,-}\). Define the Bloch vectors \(n_r\in\mathbb R^3\) by

\[
v_{r,\sigma}^*v_{r,\sigma}
=\frac{I+\sigma n_r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2},
\qquad \|n_r\|=1.
\tag{2}
\]

The Pauli matrices in (2) fix the orientation convention. Put

\[
a=n_0\cdot n_1,\quad b=n_1\cdot n_2,\quad c=n_0\cdot n_2,
\qquad t=\sqrt3\,n_0\cdot(n_1\times n_2).
\tag{3}
\]

Write \(d_{01}=a,d_{12}=b,d_{02}=c\), and let \(p=(a,b,c,t)\). The physical parameters satisfy

\[
t^2=3(1+2abc-a^2-b^2-c^2),\qquad |a|,|b|,|c|\leq1.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(W,U,Z\) be the three two-column spaces in (1), and choose the displayed columns \(u,v\) of \(U\). The frame is unitary because each \(V_r\) is unitary and the scalar Fourier transform is unitary. Its block rows are

\[
\frac1{\sqrt3}\,[V_r,\omega^rV_r,\omega^{2r}V_r].
\tag{5}
\]

For a two-set \(A\), write \(q_A=\mu_W(A)\). Since multiplying row block \(r\) by \(\omega^r\) does not change absolute minors,

\[
\mu_W=\mu_U=\mu_Z=q.
\tag{6}
\]

The bottom and complementary-top marginals are therefore equal. Their complete formulas are

\[
q_{\{(r,+),(r,-)\}}=\frac19,
\qquad
q_{\{(r,\sigma),(s,\tau)\}}
=\frac{1-\sigma\tau d_{rs}}{18}\quad(r\ne s).
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed a within-block determinant has modulus \(1/3\). For two normalized rows \(x,y\),
\(|\det[x;y]|^2=1-|xy^*|^2\), while (2) gives
\(|v_{r,\sigma}v_{s,\tau}^*|^2=(1+\sigma\tau d_{rs})/2\). This proves (7).

Let

\[
g_M=\mu_{W+[u]}(M)+\mu_{W+[v]}(M).
\]

A triple occupying both sites in one row block and one site in another has

\[
g_M=\frac19.
\tag{8}
\]

For a transversal triple \(M_\sigma=\{(0,\sigma_0),(1,\sigma_1),(2,\sigma_2)\}\), the formula is

\[
g_{M_\sigma}
=\frac{3-\sigma_0\sigma_1a-\sigma_1\sigma_2b-\sigma_0\sigma_2c
              +\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_2t}{36}.
\tag{9}
\]

Here is a derivation that also checks the orientation of \(t\). Write \(P=P_W\) and \(P_U=\Lambda P\Lambda^*\), with \(\Lambda_{(r,\sigma)}=\omega^r\). By Cauchy–Binet, \(g_M\) is the coefficient of \(x^2y\) in
\(\det(xP_M+y(P_U)_M)\). For a transversal triple, all diagonal entries of both projections are \(1/3\). The diagonal determinant term contributes \(1/9\). Every pair term vanishes in this coefficient because \(1+2\Re\omega=0\). The two directed three-cycle terms contribute

\[
\frac29\Re(\omega^2 B_\sigma),\quad
B_\sigma=(v_{0,\sigma_0}v_{1,\sigma_1}^*)
         (v_{1,\sigma_1}v_{2,\sigma_2}^*)
         (v_{2,\sigma_2}v_{0,\sigma_0}^*).
\]

The Pauli trace identity yields

\[
B_\sigma=\frac{1+\sigma_0\sigma_1a+\sigma_1\sigma_2b+\sigma_0\sigma_2c
       +i\sigma_0\sigma_1\sigma_2t/\sqrt3}{4}.
\]

Substitution gives (9). For (8), subtract \(\omega^r\) times the appropriate bottom column from the added column in a minor whose doubled row block is \(r\). The last column vanishes on those two rows, leaving a determinant proportional to \((\omega^s-\omega^r)v_{s,\tau}\). Summing its two squared column contributions gives \(|\omega^s-\omega^r|^2/27=1/9\).

Thus every bottom, middle, and top coordinate is affine in \(p\). Define

\[
B(p)=(q,-g,q_{\mathrm{complement}})\in\mathbb R^{50}
\tag{10}
\]

by (7)–(9) even for nonphysical real \(p\). These affine tables have the required grading, total masses, and modular balance identically; this follows directly from the displayed coefficients, or from the vertex transports below because their parameter points affinely span \(\mathbb R^4\).

To identify the Fourier inclusion explicitly, order the original Fourier rows as \(0,3,1,4,2,5\). In (5), its blocks are

\[
V_r^{\rm F}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}1&\zeta^r\\1&-\zeta^r\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \zeta=e^{\pi i/3}.
\tag{11}
\]

Independent left multiplication by arbitrary two-by-two unitaries changes these three blocks into arbitrary \(V_r\). C012 changes only the first block. Here all three change independently; in particular the dot product \(n_1\cdot n_2\), fixed at \(1/2\) in C012's coordinates, can vary throughout \([-1,1]\).

## 2. The sharp affine transport polytope

Use C004's 90 ordered disjoint pair indices \((A,B)\), in lexicographic order. The associated diamond has bottom \(A\), top \(E\setminus B\), and added pair \(J=E\setminus(A\cup B)\). Let \(D\) be its 50-by-90 signed incidence matrix. A coupling is \(x\geq0\) with \(Dx=B(p)\).

Let \(\mathcal E=\{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^3:\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3=1\}\). Define the rational polytope

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P=\left\{(d,t):
\begin{array}{l}
|d_i|\leq1\quad(i=1,2,3),\\
|t|\leq3-\epsilon\cdot d\quad(\epsilon\in\mathcal E),\\
2|t|\leq9+3\epsilon\cdot d\quad(\epsilon\in\mathcal E)
\end{array}\right\}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

**Theorem 1 (exact transport region).** For an arbitrary real parameter \(p\), the affine table \(B(p)\) has a nonnegative diamond decomposition if and only if \(p\in\mathcal P\). Every point in \(\mathcal P\) has the explicit coupling in Sections 3–4.

### Necessity, including a small explicit dual

The first six inequalities in (12) follow from nonnegative bottom marginals in (7). The next eight are precisely nonnegative middle marginals in (9).

For the last eight, define a table \(\phi\) on ranks two, three, and four as follows:

- On a pair, \(\phi=1\) when its sites belong to different row blocks and have opposite signs; otherwise \(\phi=0\).
- On a four-set, apply the preceding rule to its complementary pair.
- On a transversal triple, \(\phi=1\) when exactly two signs are minus, \(\phi=-1\) when all three are minus, and \(\phi=0\) otherwise. On nontransversal triples, \(\phi=0\).

This table satisfies every three-rank diamond inequality. Here is a direct check without an LP duality assumption. If neither middle is transversal, their \(\phi\)-sum is zero and both endpoint values are nonnegative. If both middles are transversal, the added pair is a full row block. The two endpoint values agree: they are zero when the two signs of the bottom pair agree, and one when those signs differ. In the former case the two middle values sum to zero; in the latter they sum to one. Finally suppose exactly one middle is transversal. Only its value \(+1\) could cause a problem. If the bottom pair has opposite signs, its endpoint value is already one. Otherwise the bottom consists of the two minus sites of that transversal; the complementary-top pair then has opposite signs in different blocks, so its endpoint value is one. Thus all diamond values are nonnegative. In fact they belong to \(\{0,1,2\}\).

Direct substitution of (7)–(9) gives

\[
\langle B(p),\phi\rangle
=\frac{9+3(a+b+c)-2t}{18}.
\tag{13}
\]

For example, the sum of the pair probabilities selected by \(\phi\) is \((3+a+b+c)/9\); the corresponding signed middle sum is \((3+a+b+c+2t)/18\). Including both endpoint ranks gives (13). Therefore every positive transport satisfies \(2t\leq9+3(a+b+c)\). Independently swapping the two sites of each row block gives all eight last inequalities in (12). The triple-sign product changes the sign of \(t\), while the pair-sign products range over \(\mathcal E\). This proves necessity.

The dual is only a three-rank table. The rank-barrier extension from `sources/old-107.md` makes it a globally supermodular table if one is wanted; no extension is needed for the finite transport assertion.

## 4. An evaluation formula with at most four radial steps

Fix one vertex table \(X_v\) for each \(v\in\operatorname{vert}\mathcal P\) by (16)–(17). Write the 22 inequalities of (12) as \(h_i\cdot p\leq c_i\). All \(h_i,c_i\) are rational.

For a face \(F\), let \(V_F\) be its vertices and define

\[
q_F=\frac1{|V_F|}\sum_{v\in V_F}v,\qquad
\overline X_F=\frac1{|V_F|}\sum_{v\in V_F}X_v.
\]

Start with \(F=\mathcal P\) and the target \(p\in F\). If \(p=q_F\), return \(\overline X_F\). Otherwise put \(u=p-q_F\) and

\[
\rho=\min_{i:h_i\cdot u>0}
\frac{c_i-h_i\cdot q_F}{h_i\cdot u}\ \geq1,
\qquad p'=q_F+\rho u.
\tag{18}
\]

Choose a minimizing inequality, let \(F'\) be its intersection with \(F\), and use the recurrence

\[
\boxed{\quad
X_F(p)=(1-\rho^{-1})\overline X_F+\rho^{-1}X_{F'}(p').
\quad}
\tag{19}
\]

The barycenter \(q_F\) lies in the relative interior of \(F\). A positive denominator in (18) therefore belongs to an inequality not constant on that face, so \(F'\) is a strictly smaller face. Boundedness guarantees at least one positive denominator. Consequently (19) terminates after at most four steps. All coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one; its parameter barycenter is \(p\). Affineness of \(B\) and (16) prove

\[
X(p):=X_{\mathcal P}(p)\geq0,\qquad DX(p)=B(p),\qquad \sum_jX_j(p)=1.
\tag{20}
\]

Ties in (18) may be resolved by taking the first inequality in the fixed list in `core.py`; either choice is valid. There is no LP or feasibility oracle in (18)–(20). Rational inputs use rational arithmetic exactly; the same finite real formulas define the construction at irrational physical parameters. Boundary cases are included, since \(\rho=1\) simply passes to a smaller face.

## 5. Every physical three-block flag lies in the polytope

**Theorem 2 (global complex six-site family).** For every choice of the three unitaries in (1), \(X(p)\) is an unlabeled diamond coupling for

\[
\mu_W,\quad \mu_{W+[u]},\quad\mu_{W+[v]},\quad\mu_{W+U}.
\tag{21}
\]

**Proof.** Pairwise dot products of unit vectors satisfy \(|d_i|\leq1\). The eight middle inequalities in (12) follow from the nonnegative sums of squares in (9).

For any \(\epsilon\in\mathcal E\), choose signs \(\sigma_r\) with pair products \(\epsilon\). Then

\[
0\leq\|\sigma_0n_0+\sigma_1n_1+\sigma_2n_2\|^2
=3+2\epsilon\cdot d.
\]

Hence \(\epsilon\cdot d\geq-3/2\). Also \(|t|\leq\sqrt3\), since the scalar triple product of three unit vectors has absolute value at most one. It follows that

\[
9+3\epsilon\cdot d\geq\frac92>2\sqrt3\geq2|t|.
\tag{22}
\]

Thus every remaining transport inequality holds, with a uniform strict slack of at least \(9/2-2\sqrt3\). Therefore \(p\in\mathcal P\), and (20), together with the determinant formulas, gives all four required marginal equations. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

No limiting argument or strictly positive-minor assumption is used. The family includes real coplanar Bloch configurations \(t=0\), both complex orientations, aligned vectors, and every boundary degeneration allowed by (1). When all three \(|d_{rs}|<1\), every bottom and complementary-top pair probability is positive. Such a rank-two projection on six sites cannot split over nontrivial disjoint coordinate blocks: any block of rank zero or any rank-one block containing two sites forces a zero pair minor, while two rank-one singleton blocks cannot cover six positive coordinates.

## 7. Supermodular and entropy consequences

For every supermodular set function \(f\), Theorem 2 gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_W}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+U}}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[u]}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[v]}}f
=\sum_{A,B}X_{A,B}(p)\,\Delta_i\Delta_jf(A)\geq0,
\tag{30}
\]

where \(\{i,j\}=E\setminus(A\cup B)\). The aggregate middle law is independent of the orthonormal basis of \(U\), so (30) holds after any basis change in that update plane.

In particular, fix a frame (1), an arbitrary orthonormal basis \(u,v\) of \(U\), and affine functions \(\lambda(s),\nu(s)\in[0,1]\) with opposite slopes. Then

\[
K(s)=P_W+\lambda(s)uu^*+\nu(s)vv^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s\longmapsto H(P_{K(s)})\text{ is concave.}
\tag{31}
\]

Here is the standard deduction, with the boundary addressed. The spectral-mixture atom law is bilinear in \(\lambda,\nu\), and its mixed derivative is exactly \(B(p)\). Along the line, \(p_X''=2\lambda'\nu'B_X\). On an interval with \(0<\lambda,\nu<1\), all atoms in the union of the four projection supports have positive probability; atoms outside it vanish identically. Every positive-weight diamond in (20) has all four vertices in that support, because its marginals are nonnegative. The DPP log-submodularity inequality gives \(\langle B,\log p\rangle\leq0\), by summing over those diamonds. This inequality also holds for projection-boundary kernels: regularize the kernel to a strict contraction, use the positive \(L\)-ensemble determinant inequality, and pass to the four positive atom probabilities in question. Consequently

\[
H''=-\sum_{X:p_X>0}\frac{(p'_X)^2}{p_X}
-2\lambda'\nu'\langle B,\log p\rangle\leq0.
\]

Continuity extends this conclusion to closed parameter intervals. If either varying eigenvalue is constant at an endpoint, its slope is zero and the atom law is affine in the other eigenvalue, so ordinary entropy concavity applies directly. This proves (31), including the support-degenerate cases. The theorem does not assert concavity for arbitrary varying eigenvectors or unrestricted spectral slopes.
