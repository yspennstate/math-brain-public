# C019 — A simple-eigenvalue boundary normal form and a uniform three-site tube

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: complete restricted theorem. No general three-site or general Lyons concavity claim is made. Historical priority is not claimed. The computation below is supplementary evidence; the theorem is proved analytically.

## 1. Results and the stratum selected

Work in the original three occupation coordinates. Write

\[
H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq[3]}p_S(K)\log p_S(K).
\]

Choose a unit vector \(u\in\mathbb C^3\) with all three coordinates nonzero, put \(Q=uu^*\), \(P=I-Q\), and let \(J=PJP\) be strictly between zero and identity on the two-dimensional space \(u^\perp\). Set

\[
K_\varepsilon=J+\varepsilon Q,
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\lambda_{\min}(J|_{u^\perp}).
\tag{1}
\]

Thus precisely one eigenvalue approaches zero. At the boundary, precisely the full atom vanishes. The other seven atoms remain strictly positive.

There are two results.

1. An exact singular expansion of the full nine-real-dimensional affine Hessian, including its four bounded tangent modes. After scaling the normal direction by \(\sqrt\varepsilon\), the four eigenvector-mixing directions by \(\log(1/\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\), and leaving the four tangent directions unscaled, the Hessian converges to an explicit direct sum. In particular the bounded face Hessian, not the singular terms alone, determines whether this argument proves negativity.
2. For every \(0<\eta<1/3\), \(0<\sigma<1/2\), there are explicit positive constants \(\rho,\varepsilon_0\), uniform in \(u\), such that the full Hessian is negative definite whenever

\[
|u_i|^2\ge\eta,\qquad
\|J-sP\|_F\le\rho
\quad\text{for some }s\in[\sigma,1-\sigma],
\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0.
\tag{2}
\]

The strict versions of these inequalities give a full-dimensional boundary neighborhood in the space of Hermitian contractions. The family includes unequal \(|u_i|\), arbitrary coordinate phases, and independent perturbations of the two surviving eigenvalues and their eigenvectors. It is not restricted to the exchangeable locus, the particle-hole locus, or a scalar approach of all eigenvalues to zero.

Complementation gives the corresponding simple-eigenvalue-one family near \(I-J\).

## 2. Atom geometry at the boundary

Write \(q_i=|u_i|^2\), \(r_i=1-q_i\), and let \(d=\det(J|_{u^\perp})\). The cofactor identity for a rank-two positive matrix is

\[
\operatorname{adj}J=d\,uu^*.
\]

Consequently its seven positive atom probabilities are

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_\varnothing(J)&=1-\operatorname{tr}J+d,\\
p_{[3]\setminus\{i\}}(J)&=dq_i,\\
p_{\{i\}}(J)&=J_{ii}-dr_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

For example, the pair inclusion probability is its principal minor \(dq_i\); the full atom is zero; and singleton atoms follow by inclusion-exclusion.

If the two eigenvalues of \(J\) are \(\lambda,\mu\in(0,1)\), then

\[
p_\varnothing=(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)>0.
\]

On \(u^\perp\), the eigenvalues of \(J-dP\) are \(\lambda(1-\mu)\) and \(\mu(1-\lambda)\). Thus \(p_{\{i\}}=e_i^*(J-dP)e_i>0\), since \(r_i>0\). Every pair atom is positive because \(q_i>0\).

For \(\varepsilon>0\), the full atom is exactly

\[
p_{123}(K_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon d.
\tag{4}
\]

## 3. The full-Hessian normal form

Decompose an arbitrary Hermitian affine direction as

\[
A=aQ+uv^*+vu^*+B,
\qquad a\in\mathbb R,\quad v\in u^\perp,\quad B=PBP=B^*.
\tag{5}
\]

Here \(\|A\|_F^2=a^2+2\|v\|^2+\|B\|_F^2\). In all inverse and determinant expressions involving \(J\), the inverse and determinant are on \(u^\perp\). Put

\[
t=\operatorname{tr}(J^{-1}B),\qquad L=\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]

Block determinant expansion in the decomposition \(\mathbb Cu\oplus u^\perp\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
Dp_{123}[A]&=da+\varepsilon dt,\\
D^2p_{123}[A,A]
 &=2d\{at-v^*J^{-1}v\}+2\varepsilon\det B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

The usual entropy identity, with the normalization term canceled over all eight atoms, is

\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S[A])^2}{p_S}
 -\sum_S D^2p_S[A,A]\log p_S.
\tag{7}
\]

Define the regular quadratic form at the rank-two base by

\[
\mathcal R_J(A)=
-\sum_{S\ne[3]}
\left\{\frac{(Dp_S(J)[A])^2}{p_S(J)}
 +D^2p_S(J)[A,A]\log p_S(J)\right\},
\tag{8}
\]

and its finite correction by

\[
\mathcal Q_J(A)=\mathcal R_J(A)
-2d(1+\log d)at+2d\log d\,v^*J^{-1}v.
\tag{9}
\]

Substituting (4)–(6) into (7) proves

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K_\varepsilon)[A,A]
=-\frac d\varepsilon a^2
 +2dL\,at-2dL\,v^*J^{-1}v
 +\mathcal Q_J(A)
 +O\bigl(\varepsilon(1+L)\|A\|_F^2\bigr).}
\tag{10}
\]

The error is uniform on compact subsets of the stratum \(q_i>0\), \(0<J|_{u^\perp}<I\). This follows directly from the positive lower bounds on the seven regular atoms. Explicit uniform bounds are supplied in Section 6.

Let \(T_{u,J}(B)\) denote the Hessian of the boundary entropy along the fixed-nullspace affine face \(J+xB\). Its seven atoms stay positive and its full atom is identically zero. Thus

\[
\mathcal Q_J(B)=\mathcal R_J(B)=T_{u,J}(B).
\tag{11}
\]

In particular, with

\[
A_\varepsilon=
\sqrt\varepsilon\,\alpha Q
 +L^{-1/2}(uw^*+wu^*)+B,
\]

equation (10) gives the full scaled normal form

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K_\varepsilon)[A_\varepsilon,A_\varepsilon]
\longrightarrow
-d\alpha^2-2d\,w^*J^{-1}w+T_{u,J}(B).}
\tag{12}
\]

Convergence is in quadratic-form operator norm and uniform on compact boundary strata. Indeed, the growing mixed term becomes \(2d\sqrt\varepsilon L\,\alpha t\to0\); all bounded mixed terms vanish under the scaling; and the displayed error tends to zero uniformly. This retains all nine real modes: one normal, four mixing, and four tangent.

It follows, without any universal face sign assumption, that a strictly negative face Hessian gives a negative full Hessian for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), uniformly on compact families with a uniform face gap. Conversely, a positive face direction persists for small \(\varepsilon\). The five divergent negative modes by themselves do not decide the remaining four modes.

## 4. Exact bounded tangent form on the selected family

Now take \(J=sP\), \(0<s<1\). For a supported Hermitian \(B\), put

\[
\tau=\operatorname{tr}B,\qquad b_i=B_{ii},\qquad
\Delta=\det(B|_{u^\perp})
=\frac{\tau^2-\|B\|_F^2}{2}.
\]

Define

\[
\Lambda(q)=\sum_i(1-q_i)\log(1-q_i)
-\sum_iq_i\log q_i.
\tag{13}
\]

At this base, the probabilities in (3) become

\[
p_\varnothing=(1-s)^2,\qquad
p_{[3]\setminus\{i\}}=s^2q_i,\qquad
p_{\{i\}}=s(1-s)r_i.
\tag{14}
\]

Their first derivatives in direction \(B\) are respectively

\[
-(1-s)\tau,\qquad s\tau q_i,\qquad b_i-s\tau r_i.
\tag{15}
\]

Their second derivatives are respectively

\[
2\Delta,\qquad 2\Delta q_i,\qquad-2\Delta r_i.
\tag{16}
\]

The factors involving \(\log s\) and \(\log(1-s)\) cancel in the frozen-log sum in (7). Its remaining contribution is \(2\Lambda(q)\Delta\). The Fisher term simplifies exactly, using \(\sum_i b_i=\tau\) and \(\sum_i r_i=2\), to

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{FI}_{sP}(B)
 &=2\tau^2+
 \frac1{s(1-s)}\sum_i\frac{(b_i-s\tau r_i)^2}{r_i}\\
 &=\frac1{s(1-s)}\sum_i\frac{b_i^2}{r_i}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore the retained tangent form is the explicit formula

\[
\boxed{
T_{u,sP}(B)
=-\frac1{s(1-s)}\sum_i\frac{b_i^2}{1-q_i}
 +\Lambda(q)\{\tau^2-\|B\|_F^2\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

This formula includes tangent directions invisible to the diagonal Fisher statistics; they are controlled by the strictly positive coefficient \(\Lambda\).

## 5. Strict positivity and bounds for the entropy coefficient

For \(x,y,z>0\), \(x+y+z=1\), the identity

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(x,y,z)=
\int_0^x\int_0^y\int_0^z
\frac{dc\,db\,da}{(a+b+c)^2}}
\tag{18}
\]

follows by three finite differences of \(g(t)=t\log t\), since \(g'''(t)=-t^{-2}\). One may apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to \(g(t+\delta)\), \(\delta>0\), and take \(\delta\downarrow0\) by monotone convergence. The finite difference tends to the expression (13), with \(0\log0=0\). The singularity is integrable in three variables.

Because \(a+b+c\le1\), (18) gives

\[
\Lambda(q)\ge q_1q_2q_3>0.
\tag{19}
\]

Also

\[
\Lambda(q)\le\log(4/3)<2.
\tag{20}
\]

Here is an elementary proof of the sharper upper bound. The continuous function in (13) is zero on the boundary of the probability simplex. At an interior stationary point, differentiating \(f(x)=(1-x)\log(1-x)-x\log x\) shows that all \(q_i(1-q_i)\) are equal. The two roots of \(x(1-x)=c\) are \(x\) and \(1-x\). An interior triple summing to one cannot mix these two values unless they coincide; otherwise the multiplicities force one root to be zero or one. Hence the sole interior critical point is the uniform triple. Positivity in (19) puts the maximum in the interior, where its value is \(\log(4/3)\).

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_i\frac{b_i^2}{r_i}\ge\frac{\tau^2}{2}.
\]

Combining this with (17), \(s(1-s)\le1/4\), and (20), yields

\[
\boxed{
T_{u,sP}(B)
\le-\Lambda(q)\|B\|_F^2
-\left\{\frac1{2s(1-s)}-\Lambda(q)\right\}\tau^2
\le-q_1q_2q_3\|B\|_F^2.}
\tag{21}
\]

In particular, if \(q_i\ge\eta\), the tangent gap is at least \(\eta^3\), uniformly in both \(u\) and \(s\).

## 6. An explicit uniform tube

The following constants are deliberately conservative. They make the family (2) a completely specified set rather than an unspecified pointwise continuity statement.

Given \(0<\eta<1/3\), \(0<\sigma<1/2\), define

\[
\begin{gathered}
\kappa=\sigma/2,\qquad m=\eta\kappa^2/2,\qquad
\ell=2\log(1/\kappa),\qquad \gamma=\eta^3/2,\\
W=\frac{6\sqrt3}{m}+\frac{3\sqrt3}{m^2}+2\log(1/m),\\
\rho=\min\left\{\kappa,\frac{\eta^3}{14W}\right\},\\
C=7\left\{\frac3m+2\log(1/m)\right\},\qquad
D=C+\frac{\sqrt2(1+\ell)}\kappa,\\
R=7W+\frac2{\kappa^2}+\ell+1,\qquad
E=D+\frac{2D^2}{\gamma},\qquad F=E+2R,
\end{gathered}
\tag{22}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_0=\min\left\{
\frac\kappa2, e^{-9},
\frac{\kappa^2}{4F}, e^{-2F/\kappa},
\left[\frac{\gamma}{4(8/\kappa^2+2R)}\right]^2
\right\}>0.}
\tag{23}
\]

For every kernel in (2), with these constants, the quantitative conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K_\varepsilon)[A,A]
\le-\frac{\kappa^2}{2\varepsilon}a^2
-\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon)\|v\|^2
-\frac\gamma4\|B\|_F^2.}
\tag{24}
\]

### 6.1 Uniform atom and derivative bounds

The choice \(\rho\le\kappa\) implies that the eigenvalues of \(J|_{u^\perp}\) lie in \([\kappa,1-\kappa]\). Formula (3) then bounds every regular boundary atom below by \(\eta\kappa^2=2m\). This also holds on the segment from \(sP\) to \(J\).

Each atom of \(J+\varepsilon Q\) is affine in \(\varepsilon\), because the update has rank one. Both \(J\) and \(J+Q\) are contractions, so for \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/2\),

\[
p_S(J+\varepsilon Q)
=(1-\varepsilon)p_S(J)+\varepsilon p_S(J+Q)
\ge\tfrac12p_S(J)\ge m
\quad(S\ne[3]).
\tag{25}
\]

For completeness, use the polynomial atom formula

\[
p_S(K)=(-1)^{3-|S|}\det(K-I_{S^c}).
\]

If \(0\le K\le I\), the Hermitian matrix \(V=K-I_{S^c}\) has operator norm at most one. Hence its atom gradient, Hessian, and third derivative obey

\[
\begin{aligned}
|Dp_S[A]|&\le\sqrt3\|A\|_F,\\
|D^2p_S[A,B]|&\le2\|A\|_F\|B\|_F,\\
|D^3p_S[A,A,G]|&\le2\|G\|_F\|A\|_F^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Indeed, the gradient is \(\pm\operatorname{adj}V\), whose Frobenius norm is at most \(\sqrt3\). The identity \(D^2\det V[A,A]=2\operatorname{tr}(V\operatorname{adj}A)\) and \(\|\operatorname{adj}A\|_*\le\|A\|_F^2\) give the diagonal Hessian bound and hence its bilinear operator bound. Differentiating this identity gives the third derivative bound.

For one regular atom, the quadratic contribution in (8) has operator norm at most \(3/m+2\log(1/m)\). Differentiation in a unit Frobenius base direction, followed by (26), bounds its change by

\[
\frac{4\sqrt3}{m}+\frac{3\sqrt3}{m^2}
+2\log(1/m)+\frac{2\sqrt3}{m}=W.
\tag{27}
\]

Thus the seven-atom regular form has operator norm at most \(C\) and Lipschitz constant at most \(7W\) along any segment on which the regular atoms are at least \(m\).

### 6.2 Retaining a uniform tangent gap and controlling the finite form

By (21), (22), and (27),

\[
T_{u,J}(B)
\le-\eta^3\|B\|_F^2+7W\rho\|B\|_F^2
\le-\gamma\|B\|_F^2.
\tag{28}
\]

Since \(d\in[\kappa^2,1]\),

\[
|t|\le\frac{\sqrt2}{\kappa}\|B\|_F,\qquad
v^*J^{-1}v\le\frac1\kappa\|v\|^2,\qquad
|\log d|\le\ell.
\]

These inequalities and (9) imply \(\|\mathcal Q_J\|_{\rm op}\le D\). Let \(n^2=a^2+2\|v\|^2\), \(b=\|B\|_F\). Since the restriction to \(B\) satisfies (28), polarization and Young's inequality give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_J(A)
&\le-\gamma b^2+Dn^2+2Dnb\\
&\le-\frac\gamma2b^2+En^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]

The regular-form error between \(J\) and \(J+\varepsilon Q\) is at most \(7W\varepsilon\|A\|_F^2\). The full-atom terms omitted from (10) are exactly

\[
-\varepsilon dt^2+2\varepsilon L\det B
-2\varepsilon\log d\det B.
\]

Using \(|\det B|\le\|B\|_F^2/2\) proves the explicit remainder bound

\[
|\text{error in (10)}|
\le R\varepsilon(1+L)\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{30}
\]

### 6.3 Absorbing the mixed singular term

The growing mixed term is controlled without discarding the tangent modes:

\[
2dL|at|
\le\frac d{4\varepsilon}a^2+4d\varepsilon L^2t^2
\le\frac d{4\varepsilon}a^2
+\frac{8\varepsilon L^2}{\kappa^2}b^2.
\tag{31}
\]

Moreover, \(dJ^{-1}\ge\kappa I\). Combining (10), (29)–(31) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2H(K_\varepsilon)[A,A]
\le{}&\left[-\frac{3\kappa^2}{4\varepsilon}
 +E+R\varepsilon(1+L)\right]a^2\\
&+\left[-2\kappa L+2E+2R\varepsilon(1+L)\right]\|v\|^2\\
&+\left[-\frac\gamma2+
\frac{8\varepsilon L^2}{\kappa^2}
 +R\varepsilon(1+L)\right]b^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]

For \(L\ge9\), \(\varepsilon L^2\le\sqrt\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon(1+L)\le2\sqrt\varepsilon\). The five restrictions in (23) now imply, respectively, a separated simple eigenvalue and \(L\ge9\),

\[
F\le\frac{\kappa^2}{4\varepsilon},\qquad
2F\le\kappa L,\qquad
(8/\kappa^2+2R)\sqrt\varepsilon\le\gamma/4.
\]

Substituting these bounds in (32) proves (24). All constants are independent of \(u\), \(s\), \(J\), and \(\varepsilon\) within the displayed family.

## 7. Scope, geometry, and complementation

The spectral gap \(\varepsilon\le\kappa/2<\lambda_{\min}(J)\) makes \(u\) the unique small-eigenvalue eigenspace. Near any point satisfying the strict versions of (2), the spectral projection onto this eigenspace varies continuously; the remaining restrictions are open. Thus the union of these points is an ambient open subset of the nine-dimensional real space of Hermitian matrices. It approaches the rank-two boundary uniformly while allowing all Hermitian directions in its Hessian bound. No convexity of this entire tube is asserted; entropy is strictly concave along every nonconstant affine segment contained in it.

The kernel \(I-K\) has the complementary occupation law, so \(H(I-K)=H(K)\). Applying the result to \(I-K\) proves the matching full-dimensional neighborhood near a simple eigenvalue one.

The analysis leaves the following precise issue open: for general positive \(J|_{u^\perp}<I\), the normal form (12) reduces eventual full-Hessian negativity to the four-dimensional face form \(T_{u,J}\), but this report proves its strict negativity only in the uniform neighborhoods (2) of \(J=sP\). In particular no universal three-site result follows.

## 8. Reproducible evidence, separate from the proof

`computations/C019/check.py` constructs full affine Hessians using polynomial determinant derivatives, retaining the full atom analytically to avoid small-determinant cancellation. It verifies the tangent formula (17) and evaluates the scaled normal-form error for a fixed nonexchangeable complex vector with \(q=(1/2,1/3,1/6)\), first at \(J=0.37P\) and then with unequal surviving eigenvalues.

The recorded results are in `computations/C019/results.json`. These finite floating-point checks are implementation and asymptotic evidence only. The explicit radius in (23) is intentionally too conservative for ordinary floating-point exponentiation; numerical examples do not certify that radius and are not used in the proof.


The numerical identity check passed, with operator discrepancy \(2.47\times10^{-15}\) between (17) and the seven-atom tangent Hessian. For the isotropic example, \(\Lambda(q)=0.2425859717\), and the largest tangent eigenvalue is \(-0.2425859717\). For the second example, the surviving eigenvalues are approximately \(0.341459,0.408541\), and the largest tangent eigenvalue is \(-0.2675784581\).

In both examples the scaled full Hessian was negative definite at each of \(\varepsilon=10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-8},10^{-16},10^{-32}\). Its operator-norm distance from the limit decreased respectively from \(1.4086\) to \(0.2525\) and from \(1.3847\) to \(0.2496\). The slow decay is consistent with the retained bounded mixing-tangent terms becoming \(O(L^{-1/2})\) after scaling. Neither these samples nor their unequal-eigenvalue perturbation are claimed to lie within the extremely conservative explicit radius (22).
