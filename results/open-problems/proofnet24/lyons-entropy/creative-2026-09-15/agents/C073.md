# C073 — a dimension-free diagonal certificate at every balanced star

Date: 2026-09-15. Completed creative proof. This report proves a new restricted sign theorem and an exact positive matrix integral; it does not resolve the general Lyons conjecture. No numerical sample is used as a universal proof. No subagents, publishing, or external messaging were used.

## 1. Completed theorem and exact scope

Index a star by its center \(0\) and leaves \(1,\ldots,m\), and let
\[
K(z)=\frac12I_{m+1}+
\begin{pmatrix}0&z^*\\z&0_m\end{pmatrix},
\qquad z\in\mathbb C^m,\qquad
4\sum_{i=1}^m|z_i|^2<1.
\tag{1}
\]
The edge magnitudes and phases are completely independent. The strict contraction condition in (1) is exact: the centered eigenvalues are \(0\) and \(\pm\|z\|_2\).

**Theorem 1.** For every real diagonal direction \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_0,\ldots,a_m)\),
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K(z))[D,D]\le -4\sum_{i=0}^m a_i^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
If every \(z_i\ne0\), the inequality is strict for every nonzero \(D\). More generally, when at least one edge is nonzero, the excess over \(4I\) is positive definite on the center and the nonzero-edge leaves, and vanishes on isolated leaves. If every edge is zero, the bound is equality.

The sign also holds for every Hermitian direction supported on the star together with its diagonal:
\[
A=D+E(h),\qquad
E(h)=\begin{pmatrix}0&h^*\\h&0_m\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
\]
An exact nonnegative decomposition for this entire tangent space is given in (25). When every edge is nonzero, the entropy Hessian is negative definite on this tangent space.

These are **pointwise Hessian theorems at the balanced bases (1)**. They do not assert concavity along every diagonal-changing segment with the same star support, because other points on such a segment generally cease to be balanced. They do not include arbitrary leaf-to-leaf perturbations.

The new scope relative to the supplied frontier is unrestricted-strength, arbitrary-size, independently weighted stars in all diagonal directions, and their joint diagonal/edge tangent space. C003 proved the equal two-leaf diagonal case; no equal-edge assumption remains here. Pure edge interpolation alone was already inside the prior incident-row theorem and C006, and is not counted as a new theorem. The diagonal-changing lines in (2) do not pass through a diagonal kernel when \(z\ne0\). Arbitrary rank diagonal directions are outside the fixed two-dimensional-support theorem. This result is neither exchangeable nor a low-coupling neighborhood theorem. Historical novelty outside the supplied project is not asserted.

## 2. Particle-hole structure for every balanced bipartite kernel

This exact reduction applies more broadly than the proved star sign.

For an arbitrary fixed bipartition \(L\sqcup R\), write
\[
K=\frac12I+B,\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\X^*&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(I_L,-I_R).
\]
Then \(\Gamma B\Gamma=-B\), so
\[
I-K=\Gamma K\Gamma.
\tag{4}
\]
Coordinate phases preserve the occupation law, and replacing \(K\) by \(I-K\) complements the occupied set. Hence the law at every such base is invariant under complementation.

There is also a complete Walsh description. For occupation spins \(s_i=2X_i-1\),
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in T}s_i=\det(2B_T).
\tag{5}
\]
This follows by expanding the determinant in the identity
\(\mathbb E\prod_{i\in T}(2X_i-1)
=\sum_{U\subseteq T}2^{|U|}(-1)^{|T|-|U|}\det K_U\).
The moment in (5) vanishes unless \(|T\cap L|=|T\cap R|=r\), in which case it equals
\[
(-1)^r4^r\left|\det X_{T\cap L,T\cap R}\right|^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Flipping all spins on one side removes the sign \((-1)^r\). Thus the transformed atom density has nonnegative squared-minor Walsh coefficients. No general Hessian sign follows solely from their nonnegativity.

For every physical kernel, entropy obeys
\[
H(K)=H(I-\Gamma K\Gamma).
\tag{7}
\]
At the balanced bipartite base, split a Hermitian direction into its across-partition part \(A_{\rm cross}\) and its within-partition part \(A_{\rm within}\). The derivative of the involution in (7) preserves the former and negates the latter. Differentiating twice gives the exact sector decoupling
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A_{\rm cross},A_{\rm within}]=0.}
\tag{8}
\]
All diagonal directions lie in the second sector. This is the general particle-hole reduction used later for the star.

## 3. Star atoms, diagonal Fisher information, and conditional log odds

Set
\[
w_i=4|z_i|^2,\qquad \sum_i w_i<1.
\]
Let \(r=s_0\) and \(t_i=-s_0s_i\). Under the uniform reference measure, \(r,t_1,\ldots,t_m\) are independent uniform signs. All centered star minors of order greater than two vanish, so (5) yields
\[
\boxed{p(s)=2^{-(m+1)}q(t),\qquad q(t)=1+\sum_i w_it_i.}
\tag{9}
\]
The full physical range gives \(q\ge1-\sum_iw_i>0\).

Write \(\mathbb E_0\) for uniform expectation over \(t\), and define
\[
\mathcal A=\mathbb E_0\frac1q,\qquad
L_i=\mathbb E_0[t_i\log q],\qquad
G_{ij}=\mathbb E_0[t_it_jq\log q]\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{10}
\]
For the diagonal direction in (2), put
\[
c=a_0+\sum_iw_ia_i,\qquad Z=\sum_i a_it_i.
\]
The diagonal derivative identity
\(\partial_{K_{ii}}p(s)=s_i[p(s)+p(s^{\{i\}})]\)
gives the exact density derivative
\[
q'_D(s)=2r(c-qZ).
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(\mathbb E_0Z=0\) and \(\mathbb E_0(qZ^2)=\sum_i a_i^2\), the Fisher quadratic form is
\[
\operatorname{FI}(D)=4\left\{\sum_{i=1}^m a_i^2+\mathcal A c^2\right\}.
\]
Equivalently, in center/leaf block order,
\[
\boxed{\frac14J=\operatorname{diag}(0,I_m)+
\mathcal A\begin{pmatrix}1\\w\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&w^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{12}
\]

For completeness, the general diagonal entropy Hessian is
\[
\nabla^2_{\rm diag}H=-J+C,\qquad C_{ii}=0,
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
C_{ij}=\sum_{y}p_{-ij}(y)
\log\frac{p_{10}(y)p_{01}(y)}{p_{00}(y)p_{11}(y)}.
\tag{14}
\]
This follows directly by grouping
\(-\sum_s\partial_i\partial_jp(s)\log p(s)\)
into four-atom squares, using
\(\partial_i\partial_jp=s_is_jp_{-ij}\).

For a uniform-reference density \(q\), the same formula is
\[
C_{ij}=-4\mathbb E_{\rm unif}
[s_is_jq_{-ij}\log q],
\]
where \(q_{-ij}\) is the uniform density of the outside marginal. Removing the star center leaves density \(1\); removing two leaves deletes their two linear terms. Therefore (9) gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_{0i}/4&=L_i,\\
C_{ij}/4&=w_iL_j+w_jL_i-G_{ij}\quad(i\ne j,\ i,j\ge1).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{15}
\]

## 4. The exact Laplace-integral certificate

For \(u>0\), define
\[
M(u)=e^{-u}\prod_{i=1}^m\cosh(w_iu),\qquad
v_i(u)=\frac{\tanh(w_iu)}u,\qquad
d_i(u)=w_i-v_i(u),
\]
\[
e_i(u)=2w_iv_i(u)-v_i(u)^2.
\tag{16}
\]
The resolvent and logarithm Laplace formulas give
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A=\int_0^\infty M(u)\,du,\qquad
L_i=\int_0^\infty M(u)v_i(u)\,du,\qquad
G_{ij}=\int_0^\infty M(u)v_i(u)v_j(u)\,du.}
\tag{17}
\]
For the middle formula, use
\[
\log q=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-u}-e^{-qu}}u\,du
\]
and
\(\mathbb E_0[t_ie^{-qu}]=-M(u)\tanh(w_iu)\).
For the last formula, use
\[
q\log q-q+1=
\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-qu}-e^{-u}+(q-1)ue^{-u}}{u^2}\,du.
\]
Its constant and affine terms have zero \(t_it_j\) coefficient for \(i\ne j\).

Substituting (17) into (12) and (15), including the zero diagonal of \(C\), proves the exact matrix identity
\[
\boxed{\frac{J-C-4I}{4}=\int_0^\infty\mathcal R(u)\,du,}
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R(u)=
\begin{pmatrix}
M(u)-e^{-u}&M(u)d(u)^{\mathsf T}\\
M(u)d(u)&M(u)\{d(u)d(u)^{\mathsf T}+\operatorname{diag}(e_i(u))\}
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{19}
\]
For example its leaf diagonal is \(M(d_i^2+e_i)=Mw_i^2\), exactly as required by (12). Its distinct-leaf entry integrates to
\(\mathcal A w_iw_j-w_iL_j-w_jL_i+G_{ij}\).
Thus no log-odds term has been dropped.

All integrals converge absolutely. Near zero, \(v_i\to w_i\), \(d_i=O(u^2)\), and \(M-e^{-u}=O(u^2)\). At infinity,
\(M(u)\le e^{-(1-\sum_iw_i)u}\), while \(v_i,d_i,e_i\) remain bounded. These estimates also justify the displayed finite-dimensional manipulations.

## 5. Pointwise positivity of the integrand

This is the universal analytic sign step.

Delete zero-weight leaves temporarily; their row and column in \(\mathcal R(u)\) are zero. For the remaining indices, set
\[
x_i=w_iu>0,\qquad T_i=\tanh x_i,\qquad
\rho_i=\frac{(x_i-T_i)^2}{2x_iT_i-T_i^2},\qquad
\rho=\sum_i\rho_i,\qquad
P=\prod_i\cosh x_i.
\]
The denominator is positive. Moreover
\[
1+\rho_i=\frac{x_i^2}{2x_iT_i-T_i^2}<\cosh x_i.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed \(0<T_i<x_i\) and \(\sinh x_i>x_i\) imply
\[
2x_iT_i-T_i^2>x_iT_i>
\frac{x_i^2}{\cosh x_i}.
\]
At zero, (20) extends weakly with \(\rho_i=0\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{1+\rho\le1+\sum_i(\cosh x_i-1)\le
\prod_i\cosh x_i=P,}
\tag{21}
\]
strictly when at least one weight is nonzero.

Let \(E=\operatorname{diag}(e_i(u))\), which is positive definite on active leaves. By the rank-one inverse formula,
\[
d^{\mathsf T}(E+dd^{\mathsf T})^{-1}d
=\frac{d^{\mathsf T}E^{-1}d}{1+d^{\mathsf T}E^{-1}d}
=\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}.
\]
The Schur complement of the positive lower-right block of (19) is therefore
\[
M-e^{-u}-M\frac{\rho}{1+\rho}
=M\frac{P-1-\rho}{P(1+\rho)}\ge0.
\tag{22}
\]
It is strictly positive when any edge is present. Thus \(\mathcal R(u)\succeq0\) for every \(u>0\), and is positive definite on the center plus active leaves. Integrating (18) gives
\[
J-C\succeq4I,
\]
with the stated strictness. Together with (13), this proves Theorem 1.

This proof does not rely on a condition number, a small-parameter expansion, a sign restriction on \(a\), or a finite collection of samples.

## 6. Simultaneous independent edge and diagonal directions

For the star, the across-partition directions are exactly the edge directions \(E(h)\), while all diagonal directions are within-partition. The general parity identity (8) proves
\[
D^2H(K)[D,E(h)]=0.
\tag{23}
\]

To state the edge contribution explicitly, let
\[
F(w)=(m+1)\log2-H(K(z))=\mathbb E_0[q\log q].
\]
The density \(q\) is affine in every \(w_i\), hence
\[
F_i=L_i\ge0,\qquad F_{ij}=\mathbb E_0\frac{t_it_j}{q}.
\tag{24}
\]
The Hessian in (24) is a Gram matrix and is positive definite. Also \(L_i>0\) exactly when \(w_i>0\), as follows either from (17) or by conditioning on the other signs.

Along the affine edge direction \(h\),
\(\dot w_i=8\operatorname{Re}(\overline z_i h_i)\) and
\(\ddot w_i=8|h_i|^2\).
Combining (18), (23), and (24) yields
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-D^2H(K)[A,A]
={}&4\|a\|_2^2+
4\int_0^\infty
\begin{pmatrix}a_0\\a_{\rm leaf}\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf T}
\mathcal R(u)
\begin{pmatrix}a_0\\a_{\rm leaf}\end{pmatrix}\,du\\
&+\mathbb E_0\frac{(\sum_i\dot w_it_i)^2}{q}
+8\sum_iL_i|h_i|^2
\ge0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{25}
\]
All terms are explicitly nonnegative, including when different edge magnitudes have opposite radial slopes. If every \(z_i\ne0\), this proves negative definiteness for every nonzero star-supported Hermitian \(A\).

The pure-edge conclusion in (24) was already within prior entropy scope. Its purpose here is to complete the new joint tangent statement after the diagonal certificate and parity decoupling.

## 7. Strength, example, and limitations

For arbitrary positive \(c_i\) summing to one, the entire independent ratio family
\[
w_i=(1-\varepsilon)c_i,\qquad0<\varepsilon<1,
\]
satisfies the theorem. Its extreme eigenvalues are
\((1\pm\sqrt{1-\varepsilon})/2\), so the bases approach both spectral boundaries at arbitrary strength.

A concrete four-site base has
\[
z=\frac18(3,2,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
w=\frac1{16}(9,4,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
\sum_iw_i=\frac78.
\]
Its eigenvalues are
\(1/2-\sqrt{14}/8,1/2,1/2,1/2+\sqrt{14}/8\).
The theorem applies, for example, to
\(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-2,3,-4)\), a rank-four indefinite direction, and to any simultaneous independent edge slopes. This is a star base, whereas C026 treats a four-site path and fixes all diagonals.

Block-diagonal direct sums of the proved stars and independent Bernoulli coordinates inherit the diagonal bound by entropy additivity; for an arbitrary independent Bernoulli coordinate, \(h''(p)=-1/[p(1-p)]\le-4\). Star-supported directions within those blocks inherit (25).

The report does not assert a full ambient Hermitian sign when arbitrary leaf-to-leaf edges are introduced. It does not prove the sign for every bipartite graph, every rank-one rectangular cross block, or arbitrary centrally symmetric cube laws. The exact reduction (4)–(8) survives in those broader bipartite settings; the linear Rademacher density (9), which enables this integral factorization, need not survive. Boundary kernels with \(\sum_iw_i=1\) can have zero atoms, so an ordinary finite ambient Hessian there is not asserted.
