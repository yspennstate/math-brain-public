## 1. The complete rank-one polytope is an ordinary inclusion-transport polytope

Let $E$ be any finite set of size $n\geq3$. Let $w,u,v\in\mathbb C^E$ be orthonormal, and put

\[
W=[w],\qquad U=[u,v],\qquad V=[w,u,v].
\]

Write $\mu_H$ for the projection DPP of a subspace $H$. For pairs $B$, triples $T$, and singletons $i$, set

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_i&=|w_i|^2,\\
h_{ij}&=|u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2=\mu_U(ij),\\
c_{ijk}&=|\det[w\ u\ v]_{ijk}|^2=\mu_V(ijk),\\
g_{ij}&=|w_iu_j-w_ju_i|^2+|w_iv_j-w_jv_i|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus $g=\mu_{[w,u]}+\mu_{[w,v]}$, with total mass two. A rank-one diamond is specified by a pair $B\subset T$, with center $k=T\setminus B$. Its bottom, top, and middles are

\[
\{k\},\quad T,\quad\{k,i\},\quad\{k,j\},\qquad B=\{i,j\}.
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem 1 (exact polytope identification).** Nonnegative arrays $x_{B,T}$, indexed by $B\subset T$, give the prescribed rank-one unlabeled diamond couplings if and only if

\[
\sum_{T\supset B}x_{B,T}=h_B,\qquad
\sum_{B\subset T}x_{B,T}=c_T.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, the full coupling polytope is exactly the transportation polytope between $\mu_U$ and $\mu_V$, with the inclusion restriction $B\subset T$. There are no further bottom or middle constraints hidden in this identification.

**Proof.** Suppose (3) holds, and sample ((B,T)) with this joint law. Since $B\subset T$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(T\setminus B=\{k\})
&=\Pr(k\in T)-\Pr(k\in B)\\
&=(|w_k|^2+|u_k|^2+|v_k|^2)
 -( |u_k|^2+|v_k|^2)=a_k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The one-point projection-DPP inclusion formula proves the middle equality. For a fixed pair $e$, a diamond with top $T\supset e$ has $e$ as one of its two middles exactly when its addition pair $B$ is different from $e$. Therefore its aggregate middle mass is

\[
\sum_{T\supset e}c_T-\sum_{T\supset e}x_{e,T}
=\det(P_V)_e-h_e=g_e.
\tag{5}
\]

The last identity is two-row Cauchy–Binet for the three orthonormal columns $w,u,v$: the three squared two-column minors are the two terms of $g_e$ and the term $h_e$.

Conversely, start with any valid diamond coupling. The same counting in (5), now using its prescribed top and middle marginals, forces

\[
\sum_{T\supset e}x_{e,T}=\det(P_V)_e-g_e=h_e.
\]

Its top marginal already gives the other equations in (3). This proves both directions. ∎

This uses **one** inclusion transport. It does not infer a diamond by simultaneously selecting several independently supplied stochastic coverings. Equations (4)–(5) are the reason a single transport suffices at bottom rank one.

## 2. Every Hall slack has an exact exterior SOS formula

Give each exterior power of $\mathbb C^E$ its standard orthonormal coordinate basis. Define

\[
\beta=u\wedge v,\qquad \zeta=w\wedge u\wedge v.
\]

Let $\iota_w$ be contraction by $w$, using the Hermitian inner product. Since $w,u,v$ are orthonormal,

\[
\iota_w\zeta=\beta.
\tag{6}
\]

For any pair family $A\subseteq\binom E2$, let

\[
N(A)=\{T\in\tbinom E3:\text{some }B\in A\text{ satisfies }B\subset T\}.
\]

Let $P_A,P_{N(A)}$ be the corresponding coordinate projections, and set $z=P_{N(A)}\zeta$. Contraction can produce a pair in $A$ only from a triple in (N(A)), so

\[
P_A\iota_w z=P_A\beta.
\tag{7}
\]

For a unit vector $w$, every exterior vector $z$ satisfies

\[
\|\iota_w z\|^2+\|w\wedge z\|^2=\|z\|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

One direct proof completes $w$ to an orthonormal basis. Each exterior basis monomial either contains $w$, when contraction preserves its norm and exterior multiplication kills it, or does not, when the roles are reversed.

**Theorem 2 (Hall SOS and equality).** Every pair family satisfies two exact identities:

\[
\boxed{
c(N(A))-h(A)
=\|w\wedge P_{N(A)}\zeta\|^2
 +\|P_{A^c}\iota_wP_{N(A)}\zeta\|^2\geq0.
}
\tag{9}
\]

and, writing $\beta_A=P_A\beta$,

\[
\boxed{
c(N(A))-h(A)
=\|\iota_w\beta_A\|^2
+\|P_{N(A)}(w\wedge\beta_{A^c})\|^2.
}
\tag{9a}
\]

Here $c(N)=\sum_{T\in N}c_T$ and $h(A)=\sum_{B\in A}h_B$. Equality holds precisely when both displayed vectors vanish.

**Proof.** Subtract $\|P_A\iota_w z\|^2$ from (8) and use (7) to obtain (9). For (9a), $w\wedge\beta_A$ is supported on (N(A)), so

\[
z-w\wedge\beta_A=P_{N(A)}(w\wedge\beta_{A^c}).
\]

Moreover

\[
\langle z,w\wedge\beta_A\rangle
=\langle\iota_w z,\beta_A\rangle=\|\beta_A\|^2,
\quad
\|w\wedge\beta_A\|^2=\|\beta_A\|^2-\|\iota_w\beta_A\|^2.
\]

Expanding $\|z-w\wedge\beta_A\|^2$ therefore gives $c(N(A))-h(A)-\|\iota_w\beta_A\|^2$, as claimed. ∎

For later use, equality in (9) implies, writing $\beta_A=P_A\beta$,

\[
\iota_w\beta_A=0,\qquad
P_{N(A)}\zeta=w\wedge\beta_A,\qquad
w\wedge\beta_{A^c}=0\text{ on }N(A).
\tag{10}
\]

Indeed equality gives $\iota_w z=\beta_A$ and $w\wedge z=0$. The identity

\[
\iota_w(w\wedge z)+w\wedge\iota_w z=z
\]

then gives $z=w\wedge\beta_A$, while $\iota_w^2=0$ gives the first assertion.

Formula (9) proves all weighted Hall inequalities directly. No stochastic-domination theorem is needed for the construction below, and there is no unproved transport feasibility condition.

## 3. Explicit probabilities using only sums and minima

The following finite formula applies to any finite bipartite transport graph, not just the inclusion graph. Let its vertices be $L,R$, its edge set $G$, and its nonnegative marginal masses be $p_l,q_r$, with equal totals. Assume

\[
p(A)\leq q(N_G(A))\quad(A\subseteq L).
\tag{11}
\]

Fix an ordering of all edges. Process them in that order, deleting one edge at every step, including steps that assign zero. At a current edge $e=(l,r)$, let $p,q$ denote the residual masses and $G$ the remaining graph. For $A\subseteq L$, put

\[
s_G(A)=q(N_G(A))-p(A).
\]

Assign the following exact weight:

\[
\boxed{
x_e=\min\left\{
p_l,\ q_r,\
\min_{\substack{A\subseteq L:\ l\notin A,\ r\in N_G(A)}}s_G(A)
\right\}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

The inner minimum is $+\infty$ if its index set is empty. Replace $p_l,q_r$ by $p_l-x_e,q_r-x_e$, then delete $e$. All neighbor sets in (12) come from the fixed suffix of the original edge ordering, so this is an unambiguous finite formula in the input masses.

**Theorem 3 (canonical exact selection).** Formula (12) terminates with every residual mass zero and gives a nonnegative transport with the requested marginals. For the chosen edge order it is the lexicographically maximal transport. As a function of the marginal masses on the feasible Hall cone, its output is continuous and piecewise linear.

**Proof of feasibility.** Subtracting $\delta=x_e$ from the two endpoint masses changes a Hall slack by

\[
s_G(A)\longmapsto s_G(A)
-\delta\mathbf1_{\{r\in N_G(A)\}}
+\delta\mathbf1_{\{l\in A\}}.
\tag{13}
\]

If $l\in A$, then $r\in N_G(A)$, so the last two terms cancel. Otherwise (12) is exactly the bound needed whenever the first indicator is one. Thus all Hall inequalities survive the mass subtraction.

It remains to justify deleting the edge. If an endpoint mass becomes zero, deletion preserves Hall: a zero right mass cannot change a neighbor capacity, and a zero left mass can be omitted from any tested family. Otherwise the inner minimum in (12) is attained at a family $A$ with

\[
l\notin A,\quad r\in N_G(A),\quad p(A)=q(N_G(A))
\tag{14}
\]

for the updated masses. A tight family as in (14) permits deletion of the crossing edge ((l,r)). To prove this directly, the only families $X$ whose neighbor capacity can decrease are those for which deleting this edge removes the sole neighbor $r$ contributed by $X$. Such an $X$ contains $l$; since $l\notin A$,

\[
r\notin N_G(A\cap X).
\]

Using Hall for $A\cup X$ and $A\cap X$,

\[
\begin{aligned}
p(X)+p(A)
&=p(X\cup A)+p(X\cap A)\\
&\leq q(N_G(X)\cup N_G(A))+q(N_G(X\cap A))\\
&\leq q(N_G(X))+q(N_G(A))-q_r.
\end{aligned}
\]

Cancel the tight equality (14). The result is exactly the Hall inequality for $X$ after deleting $r$ from its neighbor set. All other families are unchanged.

Therefore the invariant (11) holds after each step. After the last edge is deleted, Hall says the remaining total left mass is at most zero. Nonnegativity and equality of the remaining totals force both residual tables to vanish. This proves the construction.

For any feasible transport on the current graph and any family tested in (12), the slack $s_G(A)$ is exactly the mass sent from $L\setminus A$ into $N_G(A)$. It therefore bounds the weight of ((l,r)) from above. Endpoint masses give the other two upper bounds. The construction attains their minimum and permits a completion, so it maximizes the current edge. Induction proves lexicographic maximality.

Finally, the graph and edge order are fixed. Each step consists of finitely many linear combinations and a finite minimum of continuous piecewise-linear functions. Induction proves the asserted continuity and piecewise linearity. ∎

For rank-one diamonds, use

\[
L=\tbinom E2,\quad R=\tbinom E3,\quad G=\{(B,T):B\subset T\},\quad p=h,\ q=c.
\]

Theorem 2 supplies (11), formula (12) supplies the probabilities, and Theorem 1 supplies all four diamond marginals. This handles arbitrary complex phases and all vanishing-coordinate/minor boundary cases.

Formula (12) is exponential if implemented by enumerating pair families, but it is an explicit formula and uses no optimizer. For larger inputs, `maxflow_transport` provides the same type of exact transport by deterministic Edmonds–Karp: source-to-pair capacities $h_B$, pair-to-triple capacities twice the total mass, and triple-to-sink capacities $c_T$. An augmenting path has its bottleneck amount added, using exact fractions. Hall implies every source-sink cut has capacity at least the total mass; the standard shortest-augmenting-path algorithm terminates in finitely many augmentations independently of the arithmetic values. This produces another valid coupling, not necessarily the lexicographic one.

## 4. Exact support criterion, and strict Hall for nonzero bottom coordinates and added-plane minors

Formula (12), with a chosen edge first, immediately gives the following support criterion for any feasible instance:

\[
\begin{split}
x_{B,T}>0\text{ in some coupling}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
&h_B>0,\quad c_T>0,\\
&c(N(A))-h(A)>0\text{ for every }A\text{ with }B\notin A,\ T\in N(A).
\end{split}
\tag{15}
\]

Thus the SOS equality conditions (10) describe every additional forced-zero edge. Merely checking that the bottom and top probabilities are positive does not, for arbitrary transport data, settle this support question.

For compatible rank-one flags there is a strong sufficient condition eliminating every nontrivial tight family.

**Theorem 4 (uniform strict Hall).** Suppose

\[
w_i\ne0\quad(i\in E),\qquad
u_iv_j-u_jv_i\ne0\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{16}
\]

Put

\[
a_*=\min_i|w_i|^2>0,\qquad h_*=\min_{i<j}|u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2>0.
\]

Then, for every nonempty proper family of pairs,

\[
\boxed{\quad c(N(A))-h(A)\geq a_*h_*>0.\quad}
\tag{17}
\]

No nonvanishing assumption on the rank-three minors is required.

**Proof.** Regard $A$ as the edge set of a graph on $E$. If a triangle has exactly two edges in $A$, its coefficient of $P_{N(A)}(w\wedge\beta_{A^c})$ has exactly one term, $\pm w_k\beta_{ij}$. By (9a), its squared modulus alone gives

\[
c(N(A))-h(A)\geq |w_k|^2|\beta_{ij}|^2\geq a_*h_*.
\]

Otherwise the graph of $A$ has no induced path on three vertices. Each connected component is a clique: along a shortest path, any two successive edges force the shortcut edge, so a shortest path within a component cannot have length greater than one.

Since $A$ is nonempty and proper, there is a clique component $C\subsetneq E$ with at least two vertices. Choose $k\notin C$. Put

\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\operatorname{span}(u_C,v_C),\qquad
H=\|u_C\wedge v_C\|^2,\\
a_C=\|w_C\|^2,\qquad
t=\|P_Sw_C\|^2,\qquad
z_k=v_ku_C-u_kv_C,\qquad m=\|z_k\|^2.
\end{gathered}
\]

The internal pair minors are nonzero, so $S$ is two dimensional and $H>0$. A normalized simple bivector on $S$ contracts with squared norm equal to the squared norm of the projected contracting vector. Consequently the contribution of coordinates in $C$ to the first square in (9a) is

\[
\|\iota_{w_C}(u_C\wedge v_C)\|^2=Ht.
\]

For each $i,j\in C$, the coefficient on the triple $ijk$ in the second square in (9a) is, up to orientation,

\[
w_i(u_jv_k-u_kv_j)-w_j(u_iv_k-u_kv_i).
\]

Thus the squared norm of these coefficients is $\|w_C\wedge z_k\|^2$. Since $z_k\in S$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\|w_C\wedge z_k\|^2
=a_Cm-|\langle w_C,z_k\rangle|^2
\geq(a_C-t)m.
\]

Keeping just these two parts of (9a),

\[
c(N(A))-h(A)\geq Ht+(a_C-t)m
\geq a_C\min(H,m).
\tag{18}
\]

Here $0\leq t\leq a_C$, $a_C\geq2a_*$, $H=\sum_{i<j\in C}|\beta_{ij}|^2\geq h_*$, and $m=\sum_{i\in C}|\beta_{ik}|^2\geq2h_*$. Hence this case even gives the stronger bound $2a_*h_*$. The two cases prove (17). ∎

In particular, under (16), every incidence $B\subset T$ with $c_T>0$ can receive positive weight. The next section gives one coupling making all these weights positive simultaneously, together with an explicit lower bound.

## 5. A computable common positive floor

Assume (16). Let

\[
G_+=\{(B,T):B\subset T,\ c_T>0\},
\quad d_B=\#\{T:(B,T)\in G_+\},
\quad d_T=\#\{B:(B,T)\in G_+\}=3.
\]

Use neighbor sets in $G_+$, which have the same $c$-mass as the full inclusion neighbor sets. Every $d_B>0$, by Hall and $h_B>0$. Define

\[
s_A=c(N(A))-h(A),\qquad
m_A=\#\{(B,T)\in G_+:B\notin A,\ T\in N(A)\}.
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon=\frac12\min\left(
\left\{\frac{h_B}{d_B}:B\in\tbinom E2\right\}
\cup\left\{\frac{c_T}{3}:c_T>0\right\}
\cup\left\{\frac{s_A}{m_A}:m_A>0\right\}
\right)>0.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Empty and full pair families have $m_A=0$; every included slack is strictly positive by Theorem 4. Subtract a provisional mass $\varepsilon$ on every edge of $G_+$, leaving

\[
h'_B=h_B-\varepsilon d_B,\qquad
c'_T=c_T-\varepsilon d_T.
\]

The endpoint bounds in (19) leave nonnegative masses. Every residual Hall slack is

\[
c'(N(A))-h'(A)=s_A-\varepsilon m_A\geq0.
\tag{20}
\]

The identity for $m_A$ follows by counting all edges entering (N(A)) and subtracting those leaving $A$. Apply the construction of Section 3 to these residual masses and add $\varepsilon$ to every edge of $G_+$. This proves:

**Corollary 5.** Under (16), there is an explicitly constructed coupling with

\[
x_{B,T}\geq\varepsilon\quad(B\subset T,\ c_T>0),
\tag{21}
\]

and $x_{B,T}=0$ when $c_T=0$. If every rank-three minor is also nonzero, every possible rank-one diamond receives at least the positive mass (19).

This is an all-dimension quantitative interior theorem, not a perturbation theorem around one symmetric example. It also specifies how the guarantee deteriorates as a coordinate, minor, or Hall slack approaches zero.

The uniform estimate (17) eliminates the need to enumerate Hall families for a valid positive floor. Let $c_*$ be the smallest **positive** top probability. Since $d_B\leq n-2$, $d_T=3$, and $m_A\leq3\binom n3$, the following simpler closed expression also works:

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_0=\frac12\min\left\{
\frac{h_*}{n-2},\quad\frac{c_*}{3},\quad
\frac{a_*h_*}{3\binom n3}
\right\}>0.
}
\tag{21a}
\]

Subtract $\varepsilon_0$ on every supported incidence and run the exact transport algorithm on the residual tables. The endpoint residuals stay nonnegative; each nontrivial residual Hall slack is at least $s_A-a_*h_*/2\geq s_A/2$. Thus the resulting coupling has every supported weight at least $\varepsilon_0$. The function `closed_floor_transport` implements this construction with no subset enumeration or numerical optimization.

The nonvanishing class exists in every dimension: for $\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}$, take $w_j=n^{-1/2}$, $u_j=n^{-1/2}\omega^j$, and $v_j=n^{-1/2}\omega^{2j}$. The added-plane pair minors are nonzero because the roots are distinct, and the top minors are nonzero Vandermonde determinants. Arbitrary frames satisfying (16), far beyond this example, satisfy the same uniform-gap theorem.

## 6. Polytope geometry and strict supermodular positivity

When the hypotheses of Corollary 5 hold and all $c_T>0$, the inclusion graph is connected and the coupling polytope has dimension

\[
3\binom n3-\binom n2-\binom n3+1
=2\binom n3-\binom n2+1.
\tag{22}
\]

Indeed an incidence graph's row-and-column sum equations have rank equal to the number of vertices minus one when the graph is connected. Any two pairs sharing a coordinate are adjacent through their union triple; any two disjoint pairs are connected through a pair meeting each. Thus this inclusion graph is connected, including the three-site star. The strictly positive point from Corollary 5 makes the full affine dimension available.

A feasible transport is a vertex exactly when its positive support graph is a forest. An even cycle admits a small alternating signed circulation in both directions, so a cyclic support is not extreme. Conversely, a homogeneous row-and-column-sum perturbation supported on a forest vanishes by deleting leaves. Thus the canonical lexicographic transport is a vertex and has at most

\[
\binom n2+\binom n3-1
\tag{23}
\]

positive weights. The general sparse-cone bound from old-107 specializes to this same number; the new identification explains its transportation meaning here.

For any supermodular $f$, the usual contrast is exactly

\[
q_f=\mathbb E_{\mu_W}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_V}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{[w,u]}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{[w,v]}}f
=\sum_{B=\{i,j\}\subset T}x_{B,T}\Delta_i\Delta_jf(T\setminus B).
\tag{24}
\]

When all three nonvanishing conditions hold, (21) gives the quantitative refinement

\[
q_f\geq\varepsilon\sum_{B=\{i,j\}\subset T}\Delta_i\Delta_jf(T\setminus B).
\tag{25}
\]

Hence $q_f=0$ precisely when every rank-one elementary diamond difference vanishes. This means $f$ is modular on the three relevant levels: there exist $\alpha,\beta_i\in\mathbb R$ such that

\[
f(S)=\alpha+\sum_{i\in S}\beta_i\qquad(1\leq |S|\leq3).
\tag{26}
\]

For completeness, comparing the diamond equalities at two centers of a triple shows that all numbers (f(ij)-f(i)-f(j)) agree, say with common value $d$. Then the triple values are (f(i)+f(j)+f(k)+2d); choose $\alpha=-d$, $\beta_i=f(i)+d$. The converse is immediate. Thus the prior nonnegative mixed sign is strictly positive throughout this nonvanishing family for every supermodular $f$ whose three-level restriction is not modular.
