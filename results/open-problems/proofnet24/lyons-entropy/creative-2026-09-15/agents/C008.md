# C008 — the full particle–hole symmetric three-site locus

Date: 2026-09-15. This report extends C002's four-by-four criterion to **all unequal edge magnitudes**. It proves an all-Hermitian-direction theorem, not merely concavity along the particle–hole symmetric subspace. Numerical computations are supplementary checks only.

## 1. The proved theorem

Let
\[
K=\frac12I_3+iB,\qquad B^\mathsf T=-B\text{ real},\qquad
\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
For the determinantal subset law of \(K\), let
\(H(K)=-\sum_{S\subseteq[3]}p_S(K)\log p_S(K)\).
Then
\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.}
\tag{2}
\]
If the graph of nonzero off-diagonal entries of \(K\) is connected, then the inequality is strict for every nonzero \(A\). Equivalently in dimension three, strictness holds when at least two of the three edges of \(B\) are nonzero. All zero-edge cases in the interior are included in (2).

Thus C002's exact four-by-four inequality (15) holds strictly on its entire connected particle–hole symmetric locus. The conclusion is preserved by coordinate permutations, diagonal unitary conjugation, and complex conjugation. Every connected base in (1) has a full-dimensional ambient neighborhood with a negative definite entropy Hessian.

The proof spends at most **two-fifths of the Fisher information** on the unequal part of the three frozen log coefficients. The remaining **three-fifths** controls their common part through the particle-count statistic. Both constants are uniform in the edge magnitudes and in the distance to the spectral boundary.

## 2. Complementary masses and the exact curvature to be controlled

Put
\[
v=(B_{23},B_{31},B_{12}),\qquad r_i=v_i^2,\qquad T=r_1+r_2+r_3.
\]
Since \(B^2=vv^\mathsf T-TI\), condition (1) is exactly \(T<1/4\). The four complementary pairs have common single-atom probabilities
\[
q_0=p_\varnothing=p_{123}=\frac18-\frac T2,
\qquad
q_i=p_i=p_{[3]\setminus\{i\}}=\frac18+\frac T2-r_i.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
\sum_{\alpha=0}^3q_\alpha=\frac12,
\qquad q_i\ge q_0>0,
\qquad q_0+q_i=\frac14-r_i\le\frac14.
\tag{4}
\]
Relabel the sites so that
\(q_1\le q_2\le q_3\). Define
\[
m_i=\log\frac{q_jq_k}{q_0q_i}\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).
\tag{5}
\]
Then \(m_1\ge m_2\ge m_3\ge0\). For the last inequality use the exact identity
\[
q_jq_k-q_0q_i=\frac{r_i}{4}+r_jr_k\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
Every \(m_i\) is positive if at least two \(r_i\) are positive.

The frozen-log identity in C002, with its vanishing triple coefficient and diagonal \(M\), is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
  +2\sum_i m_i\bigl(A_{jj}A_{kk}-|A_{jk}|^2\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{FI}_K(A)=\sum_S(Dp_S[A])^2/p_S\). This is the same exact Hessian underlying C002's four-by-four Walsh reduction.

Write \(A=R+iC\), with \(R\) real symmetric and \(C\) real skew-symmetric. The involution \(J\mapsto I-\overline J\) fixes \(K\), reverses a real symmetric direction, and fixes a purely imaginary direction. Consequently the atom derivatives in direction \(R\) are opposite on complementary pairs, while those in direction \(iC\) are equal. Pairwise cancellation gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(R+iC)=\operatorname{FI}_K(R)+\operatorname{FI}_K(iC).
\tag{8}
\]
The frozen term also has zero cross term. Therefore it suffices to handle \(R\): the imaginary sector is
\[
D^2H(K)[iC,iC]
=-\operatorname{FI}_K(iC)-2\sum_i m_iC_{jk}^2\le0,
\tag{9}
\]
strictly negative for nonzero \(C\) when all \(m_i>0\).

For the real sector set
\[
d_i=R_{ii},\qquad s=\operatorname{tr}R,\qquad z=v^\mathsf TRv,
\qquad d=\frac s4-z,\qquad g_i=\frac{d_i}{2}-\frac s4-z.
\tag{10}
\]
The full-atom derivative is \(d\), the singleton derivatives are \(g_i\), and complementary derivatives have opposite signs. These formulas follow either by differentiating the principal minors or directly from
\(\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{adj}K)=I/4-vv^\mathsf T\).
Thus the exact real-sector Fisher information is
\[
\mathcal I(R):=\operatorname{FI}_K(R)
=2\left(\frac{d^2}{q_0}+\sum_i\frac{g_i^2}{q_i}\right).
\tag{11}
\]

## 3. A four-mass logarithmic inequality

**Lemma 1.** If positive masses satisfy
\[
q_0\le q_1\le q_2\le q_3,\qquad
\sum q_\alpha=\frac12,\qquad q_0+q_3\le\frac14,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
F:=2(q_0+q_1)\log\frac{q_3}{q_1}
  +2(q_0+q_2)\log\frac{q_3}{q_2}<\frac25.}
\tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Write \(a=q_0\), \(h=q_3\), and \(S=q_1+q_2=1/2-a-h\).
The function
\[
\phi(x)=(a+x)\log(h/x),\qquad
\phi''(x)=\frac{a-x}{x^2},
\]
is concave for \(x\ge a\). Hence
\[
F=2\phi(q_1)+2\phi(q_2)
\le 2(2a+S)\log\frac{2h}{S}.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(w=2h/S\). Ordering gives \(w\ge1\). The constraint \(a+h\le1/4\) implies
\[
S=\frac{1-2a}{w+2},\qquad
a\le\frac{2-w}{8},\qquad 1\le w\le2.
\]
Because \(\log w\ge0\), (13) gives
\[
F\le
\frac{2\{1+2a(w+1)\}}{w+2}\log w
\le\frac{3-w}{2}\log w.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(w\ge1\),
\[
\log w\le\frac{w^2-1}{2w}.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, the derivative of the right side minus the left side is
\((w-1)^2/(2w^2)\ge0\), and the difference vanishes at one.
Put \(u=w-1\in[0,1]\). Then
\[
\frac{3-w}{2}\log w
\le\frac{u(4-u^2)}{4(1+u)}<\frac25,
\tag{16}
\]
where the final strict inequality follows from the polynomial identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac25-\frac{u(4-u^2)}{4(1+u)}
&=\frac{8-12u+5u^3}{20(1+u)}\\
&=\frac{8(1-u)^3+12u(1-u)^2+u^3}{20(1+u)}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The numerator is positive throughout \([0,1]\), including both endpoints. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

The physical restriction \(q_0+q_3\le1/4\) is an essential input to this argument; (12) is not asserted for arbitrary ordered four-mass distributions.

## 4. Paying for the unequal log coefficients

Set \(m=m_3\). The exact ordered decomposition is
\[
(m_1,m_2,m_3)=(m,m,m)+(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,0),
\qquad
\Delta_i=2\log\frac{q_3}{q_i}\ge0\quad(i=1,2).
\tag{18}
\]
This keeps the off-diagonal penalties attached to their coefficients; it does not make an invalid independent replacement of coefficients multiplying indefinite minors.

For every real symmetric \(R\), Cauchy–Schwarz in two of the four atom terms gives
\[
\mathcal I(R)
\ge2\left(\frac{d^2}{q_0}+\frac{g_i^2}{q_i}\right)
\ge\frac{2(d-g_i)^2}{q_0+q_i}
=\frac{(d_j+d_k)^2}{2(q_0+q_i)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Also
\[
d_jd_k-R_{jk}^2\le\frac{(d_j+d_k)^2}{4}.
\]
Consequently, for \(i=1,2\),
\[
2\Delta_i(d_jd_k-R_{jk}^2)
\le\frac{\Delta_i}{2}(d_j+d_k)^2
\le\Delta_i(q_0+q_i)\mathcal I(R).
\tag{20}
\]
Summing (20) and applying Lemma 1 yields
\[
2\sum_i m_i(d_jd_k-R_{jk}^2)
\le m\{s^2-\|R\|_F^2\}+\frac25\mathcal I(R).
\tag{21}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[R,R]
\le-\frac35\mathcal I(R)+m\{s^2-\|R\|_F^2\}.}
\tag{22}
\]

There is also a uniform upper bound for the retained common coefficient. Write \(q_\Sigma=q_1+q_2+q_3\). Since \(q_3\) is largest,
\[
3q_1q_2\le q_3q_1+q_3q_2+q_3^2=q_3q_\Sigma.
\]
Thus
\[
0\le m=\log\frac{q_1q_2}{q_0q_3}
\le\log\frac{q_\Sigma}{3q_0}
=\log\frac{3+t}{3(1-t)}=:M(t),
\qquad t=4T\in[0,1).
\tag{23}
\]

## 5. Three-fifths of the count Fisher information suffices

Aggregating by the particle count, or applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the three singleton terms in (11), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I(R)&\ge\mathcal I_N(R),\\
\mathcal I_N(R)
&:=2\left\{
\frac{(s/4-z)^2}{q_0}
+\frac{(-s/4-3z)^2}{q_\Sigma}
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Unlike the full Fisher information, this expression depends on \(v\) only through \(T\) and \(z=v^\mathsf TRv\).

**Lemma 2.** For \(0<t=4T<1\), \(0\le m\le M(t)\), and any real symmetric \(R\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac35\mathcal I_N(R)-m\{(\operatorname{tr}R)^2-\|R\|_F^2\}\ge0.}
\tag{25}
\]
It is strictly positive for \(R\ne0\) if \(m>0\).

**Proof.** Put \(n=v/\sqrt T\), and decompose orthogonally
\[
R=aI+u(3nn^\mathsf T-I)+R_0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}R_0=0,\quad n^\mathsf TR_0n=0.
\tag{26}
\]
Then
\[
s=3a,\qquad z=T(a+2u),\qquad
s^2-\|R\|_F^2=6(a^2-u^2)-\|R_0\|_F^2.
\tag{27}
\]
Since \(q_0=(1-t)/8\) and \(q_\Sigma=(3+t)/8\), (24) becomes
\[
\mathcal I_N(R)
=\frac{\{(3-t)a-2tu\}^2}{1-t}
+\frac{9\{(1+t)a+2tu\}^2}{3+t}
=\alpha a^2+2\beta au+\gamma u^2.
\tag{28}
\]
Writing \(D=(1-t)(3+t)>0\), direct expansion gives
\[
\alpha=\frac{36-12t^2-8t^3}{D},\qquad
\beta=-\frac{16t^3}{D},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{16t^2(3-2t)}{D},
\tag{29}
\]
and
\[
\alpha\gamma-\beta^2=\frac{576t^2}{D},\qquad
\alpha-\gamma=12L,
\qquad L=\frac{3+3t-2t^2}{3+t}>0.
\tag{30}
\]
Take \(\kappa=3/5\). The two-dimensional quadratic form in (25) has matrix
\[
\mathcal T_\kappa=
\begin{pmatrix}
\kappa\alpha-6m&\kappa\beta\\
\kappa\beta&\kappa\gamma+6m
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{31}
\]
Its lower-right entry is positive, and
\[
\det\mathcal T_\kappa
=\frac{576\kappa^2t^2}{D}+72\kappa mL-36m^2.
\tag{32}
\]
The elementary inequality
\(\log x\le(x-1)/\sqrt{x}\) for \(x\ge1\)
follows by differentiation: the derivative of the difference is
\((\sqrt x-1)^2/(2x^{3/2})\ge0\).
Applying it to \(x=(3+t)/(3(1-t))\) and using \(0\le m\le M(t)\) gives
\[
m^2\le M(t)^2\le\frac{16t^2}{3D}.
\tag{33}
\]
Inserting this into (32) yields the explicit allocation margin
\[
\boxed{
\det\mathcal T_{3/5}
\ge\frac{384}{25}\frac{t^2}{D}+\frac{216}{5}mL>0.}
\tag{34}
\]
Indeed, \(576(3/5)^2-192=384/25\). Thus \(\mathcal T_{3/5}\) is positive definite. The remaining term in (25) is \(m\|R_0\|_F^2\), proving nonnegativity and the claimed strictness. \(\square\)

This strengthens the count-Fisher step needed here: the coefficient need not equal the equal-edge log coefficient, and only three-fifths of the count Fisher information is required.

## 6. Completion, degeneracies, and spectral boundary

For \(T>0\), combine (22), (24), and Lemma 2:
\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2H(K)[R,R]
&\le-\frac35\mathcal I(R)+m(s^2-\|R\|_F^2)\\
&\le-\frac35\mathcal I_N(R)+m(s^2-\|R\|_F^2)
\le0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
If at least two edges are nonzero, (6) gives \(m=m_3>0\), so Lemma 2 makes (35) strict for every nonzero real symmetric \(R\). Equations (8)–(9) then give strict negativity for every nonzero Hermitian direction.

The remaining cases are explicit:

* **Exactly one edge is nonzero.** After relabeling, \(r_1>0\), \(r_2=r_3=0\). Then \(m_1>0\), \(m_2=m_3=0\), and (22) directly gives \(D^2H(K)[R,R]\le-(3/5)\mathcal I(R)\le0\). Equation (9) handles the imaginary sector. The radical consists exactly of the four real cross-block directions between site 1 and the block \(\{2,3\}\): all diagonal entries and \(A_{23}\) vanish. To check exactness, vanishing Fisher information forces the diagonal derivatives \(A_{ii}\) to vanish; the only penalized off-diagonal entry is \(A_{23}\). With those entries zero, all first atom derivatives and the frozen quadratic vanish.
* **All edges vanish.** At \(K=I/2\), every \(m_i=0\), and direct differentiation gives \(\operatorname{FI}_K(A)=4\sum_iA_{ii}^2\). Thus \(D^2H(K)[A,A]=-4\sum_iA_{ii}^2\); the six real off-diagonal directions form its radical. This handles \(t=0\) without dividing by \(T\) in (26).
* **Approaching the spectral boundary.** All arguments hold for every \(t<1\), with no separation from one assumed. At \(t=1\), \(q_0=0\), so the interior atom/Fisher Hessian formulas used here do not apply. No finite all-direction boundary Hessian is claimed. The theorem's strict interior norm hypothesis is exactly the one in the assigned problem.

For connection to the assigned four-by-four criterion, when the base is connected, \(\delta=m_1m_2m_3>0\) and C002's exact equivalence now yields
\[
\boxed{u_o^\mathsf T(D_o+\delta V_o)^{-1}u_o>2.}
\tag{36}
\]
No direction has been removed: the real sector was controlled using sufficient Fisher lower bounds and the complete frozen quadratic; the imaginary sector was handled exactly.
