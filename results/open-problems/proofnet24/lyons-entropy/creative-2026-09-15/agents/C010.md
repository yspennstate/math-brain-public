# C010 — optimized diagonal scaling, an all-phase family, and a sharp three-feature obstruction

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **bounded proof development complete**.

This report proves a new explicit family of three-site kernels having negative entropy Hessian in every Hermitian direction. The normalized positive matrix may have **arbitrary nonzero edge magnitudes and arbitrary cycle phase**. An explicit weighted simplex controls its diagonal scaling, and the determinant-maximizing diagonal scaling in that simplex is obtained exactly. In particular this removes both C001's small-
`t` and cycle-phase restrictions for a newly scaled version of its family.

There is also an exact obstruction: at one rational, permutation-symmetric kernel, **every selection of at most three of the seven conditional Fisher features fails**, even after optimizing all trial coefficients and allowing every reveal order. A rational seven-feature trial succeeds at that same kernel. Thus no universal theorem can be obtained merely by selecting and optimizing three histories from a single reveal-order decomposition.

The unrestricted three-site inequality and the general Lyons conjecture remain open here. Diagonal scaling is not proved to preserve Hessian negativity.

Sources read: `TARGET.md`, the C010 entry of `ASSIGNMENTS.json`, `agents/C001.md`, and the definitions of the conditional Fisher criterion in `sources/old-097.md`. The exact Hessian and seven-feature identities used below are prior inputs, not new claims. The work below is a constructive extension, not an audit of those reports.

## 1. New scaling theorem

Let \(R\) be a positive definite Hermitian \(3\times3\) matrix with diagonal entries 1 and all three off-diagonal entries nonzero. Define
\[
d_i=-\log(1-|R_{jk}|^2),\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\},
\]
\[
d_\times=d_1d_2d_3,\qquad
\tau=\log\frac{(1-|R_{12}|^2)(1-|R_{13}|^2)(1-|R_{23}|^2)}{\det R},
\]
\[
\boxed{c_i=1+\frac{|\tau|}{d_i}+\frac{d_\times}{d_i^2}.}
\tag{1}
\]
All these quantities depend on the normalized shape \(R\), not its diagonal scaling. Put
\[
L=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i})R\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i}),
\qquad K=L(I+L)^{-1},\qquad \ell_i>0.
\]
Let
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)+\tau K,
\qquad \delta=\det M.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(M>0\). Let \(\alpha\) be the unique positive root of
\[
\boxed{27\alpha^5+207\alpha^4+645\alpha^3+850\alpha^2-625=0.}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular
\[
\frac23<\alpha<\frac{27}{40},\qquad
\alpha\approx0.6717846249665378.
\]

**Theorem.** If
\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^3 c_i\ell_i\le\alpha,}
\tag{4}
\]
then, in the exact seven-feature criterion of old-097,
\[
b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b\ge2+\frac1{2000}.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, for every Hermitian direction \(A\),
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-\frac{\delta}{2000}
\|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2.}
\tag{6}
\]
Two simpler rational subregions have larger quantitative margins:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{Scaling condition}&\text{Lower bound on }b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b\\ \hline
\sum c_i\ell_i\le2/3&2+7/375\\
\sum c_i\ell_i\le1/2&2+1/2.
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
The same larger surplus replaces \(1/2000\) in (6).

The region has nonempty interior in all nine real kernel coordinates: start from any normalized positive shape with nonzero edges and take sufficiently small positive diagonal scales with strict inequality in (4). The theorem is pointwise. Concavity follows along affine kernel segments contained in the region, or on any convex subset of it; no convexity of the entire region is claimed.

## 2. Exact diagonal-scaling optimization

For a fixed shape \(R\), the costs \(c_i\) are fixed. Within
\(\sum c_i\ell_i\le s\), where \(s\) is any of the radii above,
\[
\det L=\det R\,\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3
\le\frac{s^3\det R}{27c_1c_2c_3}.
\tag{8}
\]
The unique maximizing scaling is
\[
\boxed{\ell_i=\frac{s}{3c_i},\qquad i=1,2,3.}
\tag{9}
\]
This follows from AM--GM applied to the three positive numbers
\(c_i\ell_i\); equality requires all three to equal \(s/3\).

Equivalently, if a target determinant \(\det L=\Delta\) is prescribed, the smallest possible cost is
\[
\boxed{
\min\sum_i c_i\ell_i
=3\left(\frac{c_1c_2c_3\Delta}{\det R}\right)^{1/3},}
\tag{10}
\]
attained exactly when the three \(c_i\ell_i\) are equal. Thus the optimization over diagonal scales is symbolic and exact for the explicit family (4), not a numerical search.

No claim is made that (9) maximizes the true entropy-curvature region. Changing \(\ell\) changes the base kernel; one cannot transfer (6) to other scales without a separate theorem.

## 3. Explicit all-`t`, all-phase extension of C001's curve

Take
\[
R(t,\phi)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&\sqrt{1-t}&\tfrac12\sqrt t\,e^{-i\phi}\\
\sqrt{1-t}&1&\tfrac12\sqrt t\\
\tfrac12\sqrt t\,e^{i\phi}&\tfrac12\sqrt t&1
\end{pmatrix},
\quad 0<t<1,\quad\phi\in\mathbb R.
\tag{11}
\]
Here
\[
\det R=th,\qquad
h=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-t}\cos\phi}{2}
\ge\frac{1-\sqrt{1-t}}2>0.
\]
Together with the first two leading principal minors \(1,t\), this proves \(R>0\) for **every** displayed parameter pair.

Write
\[
d=-\log(1-t/4),\qquad u=-\log t,\qquad
\tau=\log\frac{(1-t/4)^2}{h}.
\]
The optimal scales from (9) specialize to
\[
\boxed{
\ell_1=\ell_2=\frac{s}{3(1+|\tau|/d+u)},\qquad
\ell_3=\frac{s}{3(1+|\tau|/u+d^2/u)}.}
\tag{12}
\]
For \(s=\alpha\), the resulting kernel satisfies (6) for every \(0<t<1\) and every \(\phi\). For \(s=2/3\), its variational surplus is at least \(7/375\). Positive scales with \(\sum c_i\ell_i\le s\) also work; equality and equality of \(\ell_1,\ell_2\) are only needed to maximize the determinant.

This includes the real positive and real negative cycle phases, as well as all nonreal phases. It does not extend C001's original fixed scaling \(\ell_i=1\) to every \(t,\phi\); it supplies the requested explicit optimized diagonal scaling instead.

## 4. A new exact three-feature trial

For any reveal order, the prior conditional Fisher identity is
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A)=\sum_{h}\omega_h(w_h^*Aw_h)^2,
\qquad
\omega_h=\frac{\pi_h}{q_h(1-q_h)},
\tag{13}
\]
where \(h\) runs through the seven histories, \(\pi_h\) is its probability, and \(q_h\) is the next conditional inclusion probability. The vectors have last nonzero coordinate 1 and earlier entries determined by conditional differentiation.

The exact variational matrix is
\[
b_h=w_h^*Mw_h,\qquad
C_{hg}=|w_h^*Mw_g|^2+\delta\,\omega_h^{-1}\mathbf1_{h=g}.
\tag{14}
\]
It is positive definite. In particular
\[
b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b
=\sup_r\{2b^{\mathsf T}r-r^{\mathsf T}Cr\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Relabel the sites so that \(d_1\le d_2\le d_3\). Select the following three histories:

1. The first site, before any revelation.
2. The second site, after the first was excluded.
3. The third site, after the first two were excluded.

Let their vectors be the columns of \(W\), and write
\(\eta_i=\omega_i^{-1}\),
\(D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)\). Use
\[
\boxed{r_i=1/d_i\quad\text{on the selected histories, and }r_h=0\text{ elsewhere}.}
\tag{16}
\]
Put
\[
J=D^{-1/2}W^*MWD^{-1/2}.
\]
The value of this trial has the exact form
\[
\boxed{
V=2b^{\mathsf T}r-r^{\mathsf T}Cr
=3-\|J-I\|_F^2-\delta\sum_i\frac{\eta_i}{d_i^2}.}
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed, the first term of the objective is \(2\operatorname{tr}J\), its Gram-quadratic term is \(\operatorname{tr}J^2\), and
\(2\operatorname{tr}J-\operatorname{tr}J^2=3-\|J-I\|_F^2\).
No optimization or sign estimate has yet been used.

This trial differs from C001's selected inclusion--exclusion path. Its diagonal reciprocals refer to the shape coefficients \(d_i\), and the matrix error in (17) allows the diagonal scales to be controlled uniformly over shape and phase.

## 5. Bounds valid for every normalized positive shape

Define three nonnegative scale parameters
\[
x=\sum_i\ell_i,\qquad
y=|\tau|\sum_i\frac{\ell_i}{d_i},\qquad
z=d_\times\sum_i\frac{\ell_i}{d_i^2}.
\tag{18}
\]
Then \(x+y+z=\sum c_i\ell_i\).

### 5.1 The triangular feature change is small in the correct diagonal metric

For the last selected feature,
\[
w_3=\left(\frac{L_{13}}{1+\ell_3},
\frac{L_{23}}{1+\ell_3},1\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{19}
\]
This follows from \((I-K)w_3=e_3/(1+\ell_3)\).
For the second feature, let
\[
\widehat L=L_{\{1,2\},\{1,2\}}
-\frac{L_{\{1,2\},3}L_{3,\{1,2\}}}{1+\ell_3}>0.
\]
Then
\[
w_2=\left(\frac{\widehat L_{12}}{1+\widehat L_{22}},1,0\right)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad w_1=e_1.
\tag{20}
\]
The \(2\times2\) positive matrix \(\widehat L\) has
\(\widehat L_{ii}\le\ell_i\). Thus all upper off-diagonal entries of \(W\) satisfy
\[
|W_{ij}|\le\sqrt{\ell_i\ell_j},\qquad i<j.
\tag{21}
\]
Set \(T=D^{1/2}WD^{-1/2}\) and \(a=\|T-I\|_F\).
The reveal order was chosen so that \(d_i/d_j\le1\) for \(i<j\), hence
\[
a^2\le\sum_{i<j}\frac{d_i}{d_j}\ell_i\ell_j
\le\sum_{i<j}\ell_i\ell_j
\le\frac{x^2}{3}.
\]
In particular
\[
\boxed{a\le x/\sqrt3\le3x/5.}
\tag{22}
\]
The final rational inequality uses \(25\le27\).

### 5.2 The normalized matrix error

Let \(U=D^{-1/2}KD^{-1/2}\ge0\). Because \(K=L(I+L)^{-1}\le L\),
\[
|\tau|\operatorname{tr}U
\le |\tau|\sum_i\ell_i/d_i=y.
\tag{23}
\]
Using \(M=D+\tau K\),
\[
J=T^*T+\tau T^*UT.
\]
For \(E=T-I\),
\[
\|T^*T-I\|_F\le2a+a^2,
\qquad
\|T^*UT\|_F\le\operatorname{tr}(T^*UT)
\le(1+a)^2\operatorname{tr}U.
\]
These inequalities hold because \(U\ge0\) and \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1+a\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\|J-I\|_F\le2a+a^2+y(1+a)^2
\le(1+y)(1+3x/5)^2-1.}
\tag{24}
\]
The sign of \(\tau\) has disappeared without being restricted.

### 5.3 The conditional Fisher penalties

Conditioning an \(L\)-ensemble on exclusions retains the principal submatrix of \(L\) on the unexcluded sites. The next conditional inclusion probability is therefore at most \(\ell_i/(1+\ell_i)\), and in particular at most \(\ell_i\).

The probability \(\pi_i\) that all earlier sites were excluded is
\[
\pi_i=\frac{\det(I+L_{\{i,\ldots,3\}})}{\det(I+L)}
\ge\prod_{j<i}(1+\ell_j)^{-1}.
\tag{25}
\]
To see the inequality, take the Schur complement of the retained block in \(I+L\). Its diagonal entries are at most \(1+\ell_j\), and apply Hadamard's determinant inequality. Consequently
\[
\eta_i=\frac{q_i(1-q_i)}{\pi_i}
\le\ell_i\prod_{j<i}(1+\ell_j)
\le\ell_i(1+x)^2.
\tag{26}
\]

Also,
\[
\frac{\delta}{d_\times}=\det(I+\tau U)
\le\det(I+|\tau|U)
\le\left(1+\frac{|\tau|\operatorname{tr}U}{3}\right)^3
\le(1+y/3)^3.
\tag{27}
\]
For negative \(\tau\), the first inequality follows eigenvalue by eigenvalue; \(M>0\) guarantees all factors on the left are positive. The next inequality is AM--GM for the three positive eigenvalues.

Combining (26)--(27),
\[
\boxed{
\delta\sum_i\frac{\eta_i}{d_i^2}
\le(1+y/3)^3(1+x)^2z.}
\tag{28}
\]

Equations (17), (24), and (28) give the universal estimate
\[
\boxed{V\ge3-P(x,y,z),}
\tag{29}
\]
where the explicit degree-six polynomial is
\[
\boxed{
P(x,y,z)=\big((1+y)(1+3x/5)^2-1\big)^2
+(1+y/3)^3(1+x)^2z.}
\tag{30}
\]
Every coefficient of \(P\) is nonnegative. Notice that (29) gives an additional explicit non-simplex region whenever \(P(x,y,z)<1\); the simplex bounds below are convenient uniform certificates that can be optimized over diagonal scaling in closed form.

## 6. Exact polynomial certificate and the radius alpha

The following are exact rational inequalities:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}

s=1/2&1/2\\
s=2/3&368/375\\
s=\alpha&1-1/2000.
\end{array}
\tag{31}
\]
Here is a reproducible finite certificate, including its algebraic derivation.

Write \(P=\sum p_{abc}x^ay^bz^c\). Since its coefficients are nonnegative, it suffices to bound the face \(x+y+z=s\). Substitute \((x,y,z)=s(X,Y,Z)\), where \(X+Y+Z=1\), and homogenize every monomial to degree 6 by multiplying it by \((X+Y+Z)^{6-a-b-c}\). The resulting Bernstein representation is
\[
P(sX,sY,sZ)=
\sum_{i+j+k=6}\beta_{ijk}(s)\binom{6}{i,j,k}X^iY^jZ^k,
\tag{32}
\]
with
\[
\beta_{ijk}(s)=\binom{6}{i,j,k}^{-1}
\sum_{\substack{a\le i,b\le j,c\le k}}
p_{abc}s^{a+b+c}
\binom{6-a-b-c}{i-a,j-b,k-c}.
\tag{33}
\]
The Bernstein weights are nonnegative and sum to 1. Every \(\beta\) is a polynomial in \(s\) with nonnegative coefficients.

## 7. From the trial surplus to the Hessian bound

For completeness, the prior exact curvature identity is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]= -\mathrm{FI}_K(A)+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).
\tag{37}
\]
The shape coefficients satisfy the supermodularity inequalities
\(d_i\ge0\) and \(d_i+\tau\ge0\). If \(\tau\ge0\), then \(M\ge D>0\). If \(\tau<0\),
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(d_i+\tau)+(-\tau)(I-K)>0.
\]
Thus every inverse used in this report exists.

Write \(A=M^{1/2}BM^{1/2}\), and let \(\mathcal F\) be the Fisher quadratic-form operator on Hermitian \(B\). The identity becomes
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2-
\langle B,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})B\rangle.
\]
The Woodbury identity for (14) says
\[
\delta\langle I,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})^{-1}I\rangle
=3-b^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}b.
\tag{38}
\]
If the trial value is at least \(2+\varepsilon\), Cauchy--Schwarz in the positive quadratic form \(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id}\) gives
\[
\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2
\le(1-\varepsilon)
\langle B,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})B\rangle.
\]
It follows that
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\varepsilon\delta\|B\|_F^2.
\]
Apply (29)--(31). This proves (5)--(7).
