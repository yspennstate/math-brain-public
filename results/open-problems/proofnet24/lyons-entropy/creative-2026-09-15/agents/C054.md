## 1. An operator logarithm for neighboring Schur complements

Let \(L>0\) be Hermitian. For disjoint coordinate sets \(S,J\), write
\[
L_{J\mid S}=L_J-L_{JS}L_S^{-1}L_{SJ}>0.
\]
The convention for empty \(S\) is \(L_{J\mid\varnothing}=L_J\). Fix a further coordinate \(i\notin S\cup J\), and put
\[
C=L_{J\mid S},\qquad
b=L_{Ji}-L_{JS}L_S^{-1}L_{Si},\qquad
c=L_{ii}-L_{iS}L_S^{-1}L_{Si}>0.
\]
Successive Schur complementation gives
\[
D:=L_{J\mid S\cup\{i\}}=C-\frac{bb^*}{c}>0.
\tag{1}
\]

**Lemma 1 (neighboring operator-log identity).** The following is an exact positive operator integral:
\[
\boxed{
\log C-\log D
=\int_0^\infty
\frac{(C+tI)^{-1}bb^*(C+tI)^{-1}}
{c-b^*(C+tI)^{-1}b}\,dt\succeq0.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Its trace is the neighboring-minor logarithm
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\log C-\log D)
=\log\frac{\det L_{S\cup J}\det L_{S\cup\{i\}}}
{\det L_S\det L_{S\cup J\cup\{i\}}}\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** The scalar spectral logarithm integral gives
\[
\log C-\log D
=\int_0^\infty[(D+tI)^{-1}-(C+tI)^{-1}]\,dt.
\]
Sherman–Morrison applied to (1) gives precisely the integrand in (2). Its denominator is positive because \(D+tI>0\), and its numerator is positive semidefinite. The difference is bounded near zero and is \(O(t^{-2})\) at infinity. Taking traces and using the Schur determinant formula proves (3). No simultaneous diagonalization is assumed. \(\square\)

Normalize by \(D_L=\operatorname{diag}(L_{ii})\), and define
\[
f(S)=-\log\det\bigl((D_L^{-1/2}LD_L^{-1/2})_S\bigr),
\qquad f(\varnothing)=0.
\tag{4}
\]
For one coordinate, the increment is
\[
f(S\cup\{i\})-f(S)
=\log\frac{L_{ii}}{L_{ii}-L_{iS}L_S^{-1}L_{Si}}\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]
The Schur complement decreases as conditioning coordinates are added; this follows from (1), and (2) gives its operator-log form. Hence the increment in (5) is at most the terminal increment
\[
\boxed{
\delta_i:=f(E)-f(E\setminus\{i\})
=\log\bigl(L_{ii}(L^{-1})_{ii}\bigr)\ge0.
}
\tag{6}
\]
Adding omitted coordinates one at a time proves the finite-table inequality
\[
\boxed{
0\le f(E)-f(S)\le\sum_{i\notin S}\delta_i.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Statements (1)–(7) hold for every positive definite \(L\). Their use below still requires the separate Stieltjes positivity of the closed-walk coefficients. General neighboring-minor inequalities alone do not establish the entropy sign.

## 2. The positive coefficients and the complete Fisher allocation

Assume first that \(L\) is real symmetric positive definite with nonpositive off-diagonal entries. Write
\[
L=D_L^{1/2}(I-C)D_L^{1/2},\qquad C\ge0\text{ entrywise},\quad C_{ii}=0.
\]
Positive definiteness implies \(\rho(C)<1\): for real \(v\),
\(|v^TCv|\le |v|^TC|v|\), so every eigenvalue has absolute value at most the largest one, which is below one. Thus
\[
f(S)=\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(C_S^k)}k
=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq S\\|T|\ge2}}w_T,
\qquad w_T\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(w_T\) is the sum of the nonnegative weights of closed walks whose exact visited set is \(T\), including the factor \(1/k\). Absolute convergence follows from \(\rho(C)<1\); there are only finitely many sets \(T\). This is the closed-walk representation already proved in `ROOT_STIELTJES.md`.

Let
\[
K=L(I+L)^{-1},\qquad 0<K<I,
\]
and take any Hermitian direction \(A\). All DPP atoms are strictly positive. Write
\[
p_S=P_K(X=S),\quad s_S=\frac{Dp_S(K)[A]}{p_S},\quad
I:=I_K(A)=\mathbb E s_X^2,\quad\mathbb E s_X=0.
\tag{9}
\]
For the inclusion event \(Y_T=\mathbf1_{T\subseteq X}\), set
\[
G_T=\mathbb EY_T=\det K_T\in(0,1),\qquad
g_T=DG_T(K)[A]=\mathbb E(s_XY_T),
\]
\[
V_T=K_T^{-1/2}A_TK_T^{-1/2}=V_T^*.
\tag{10}
\]
Ordinary determinant differentiation gives
\[
g_T=G_T\operatorname{tr}V_T,\qquad
G_T''=G_T[(\operatorname{tr}V_T)^2-\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)].
\tag{11}
\]

The frozen log atom is
\[
-\log p_S=\log\det(I+L)-\sum_{i\in S}\log L_{ii}+f(S).
\]
Its constant and modular parts have affine determinantal expectations. Consequently the **entire** entropy Hessian is
\[
\boxed{H''_K(A)=-I+\sum_Tw_TG_T''.}
\tag{12}
\]
The odds matrix and the table \(f\) are frozen in this identity; the odds parametrization is not differentiated along \(A\).

Let \(m\ge1\) bound both the positive and negative inertia of \(A\), and put \(\gamma_m=1-1/m\). Congruence and principal compression cannot increase either inertia. If the positive eigenvalues of \(V_T\) sum to \(P\) and the absolute negative eigenvalues sum to \(N\), padding each list with zeroes to length \(m\) proves
\[
\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V_T)^2}{m}
=\sum_{j=1}^m(x_j-P/m)^2+
\sum_{j=1}^m(y_j-N/m)^2+\frac{2PN}{m}\ge0.
\tag{13}
\]
This is the C047 inertia refinement, now applied to every principal inclusion determinant.

Define the exact occupation budget
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B_K=\sum_Tw_T(1-G_T)=f(E)-\mathbb E_Kf(X).
}
\tag{14}
\]
The complete score obeys
\[
g_T^2\le I\operatorname{Var}(Y_T)=IG_T(1-G_T).
\tag{15}
\]
This is one Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for each event. Its **total** expenditure is accounted for in (14), rather than treating the same Fisher information as free at every event.

**Theorem 2 (Stieltjes Fisher budget).** For every matrix and direction above,
\[
\boxed{
H''_K(A)\le-[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B_K]I_K(A),
\qquad
\mathcal B_K\le\min\left\{
f(E),\ \sum_i(1-K_{ii})\delta_i\right\}.
}
\tag{16}
\]
In particular, the complete entropy Hessian is nonpositive in that direction whenever either displayed upper budget is at most \(1/\gamma_m\); the convention for \(m=1\) is that no budget restriction is needed.

**Proof.** The exact nonnegative-remainder identity is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B_K]I\\
&+\sum_Tw_T\left\{
\gamma_m\frac{IG_T(1-G_T)-g_T^2}{G_T}
 +G_T\left[\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)
-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V_T)^2}{m}\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{17}
\]
Expanding (17), using \(g_T=G_T\operatorname{tr}V_T\), gives exactly (12). Both braces are nonnegative by (13) and (15). For example, the first numerator divided by \(G_T\) equals
\[
(1-G_T)\mathbb E\left[
s_X-\frac{g_T}{G_T(1-G_T)}(Y_T-G_T)\right]^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Finally, (8) gives \(0\le f(X)\le f(E)\), while taking expectations in the neighboring-minor inequality (7) gives
\[
f(E)-\mathbb E_Kf(X)\le\sum_i(1-K_{ii})\delta_i.
\]
This proves every assertion. \(\square\)

All statements also hold when a fixed diagonal unitary gauge turns \(L\) into a real Stieltjes matrix: principal minors and DPP entropy are invariant under that gauge, and the direction is conjugated along with the base.

## 3. Saturation with unrestricted normalized correlation

For \(\kappa\ge1\), put
\[
\mathfrak b(\kappa)=\log\frac{(\kappa+1)^2}{4\kappa}.
\tag{19}
\]
If \(aI\preceq L\preceq bI\), functional calculus gives the elementary operator inequality
\[
L+abL^{-1}\preceq(a+b)I.
\]
Apply it to \(e_i\), then use the scalar arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, to obtain
\[
1\le L_{ii}(L^{-1})_{ii}\le\frac{(a+b)^2}{4ab}.
\tag{20}
\]
The lower bound is Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(L^{1/2}e_i,L^{-1/2}e_i\). Thus if the spectral condition number of \(L\) is at most \(\kappa\),
\(0\le\delta_i\le\mathfrak b(\kappa)\).

If also \(L\succeq\nu I\), then
\[
I-K=(I+L)^{-1}\preceq\frac{I}{1+\nu}.
\]
Combining this with (16) yields an entirely explicit full-Hessian consequence:
\[
\boxed{
H''_K(A)\le-
\left[1-\frac{(n-1)\mathfrak b(\kappa)}{1+\nu}\right]I_K(A)
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.
}
\tag{21}
\]
Here \(\gamma_m\le1-1/n\) was used. This is useful whenever
\((n-1)\mathfrak b(\kappa)\le1+\nu\).

For fixed Stieltjes \(L_0\), scaling \(L=sL_0\) leaves every \(\delta_i\) and every normalized closed-walk coefficient unchanged. Nevertheless,
\[
\mathcal B_{K_s}\le
\frac{\sum_i\log((L_0)_{ii}(L_0^{-1})_{ii})}
{1+s\lambda_{\min}(L_0)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus arbitrarily ill-conditioned normalized Stieltjes odds eventually have a nonpositive full Hermitian entropy Hessian as their occupation approaches saturation. This is not a normalized-condition-number restriction like C047's signed-rank-one bound. Formula (22) is also more precise than the condition-number estimate (21).

If the graph of nonzero off-diagonal entries of \(L\) is connected, some closed walk visits all vertices, so \(w_E>0\). Under a strict budget inequality, the full Hessian is strictly negative for every nonzero direction. Indeed, positive Fisher gives strictness in (16). If \(I=0\), all event first derivatives vanish, so \(\operatorname{tr}V_E=0\); the \(T=E\) term of (12) is then
\(-w_E\det K\operatorname{tr}(V_E^2)<0\) for \(A\ne0\), and all other determinant terms are nonpositive. No positive-definiteness assumption on the Fisher form is needed.

## 4. Real-part inertia and an unrestricted high-rank direction theorem

Return to a real Stieltjes \(L\), hence a real symmetric \(K\). Decompose any Hermitian direction uniquely as
\[
A=X+iB,\qquad X=X^T\text{ real},\qquad B=-B^T\text{ real}.
\tag{23}
\]
Every signed atom matrix \(K-I_{S^c}\) is real symmetric and invertible. The trace of the product of a real symmetric matrix with \(B\) is zero. Therefore
\[
Dp_S(K)[iB]=0\quad\text{for every }S,
\qquad I_K(A)=I_K(X).
\tag{24}
\]
For a principal inclusion determinant, write
\[
U_T=K_T^{-1/2}X_TK_T^{-1/2},\qquad
Z_T=K_T^{-1/2}B_TK_T^{-1/2}.
\]
Then \(U_T\) is real symmetric and \(Z_T\) is real skew. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{tr}(U_T+iZ_T)=\operatorname{tr}U_T,
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}((U_T+iZ_T)^2)
=\operatorname{tr}(U_T^2)+\|Z_T\|_F^2.
\tag{25}
\]
The cross trace vanishes exactly. Define
\[
\mathcal J_K(B)=\sum_Tw_T\det K_T
\|K_T^{-1/2}B_TK_T^{-1/2}\|_F^2\ge0.
\tag{26}
\]

**Theorem 3 (real-part refinement).** If \(m\ge1\) bounds both inertias of the **real part \(X\)**, then
\[
\boxed{
H''_K(X+iB)
\le-[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B_K]I_K(X)-\mathcal J_K(B).
}
\tag{27}
\]
There is no restriction on the rank or inertia of the entire matrix \(X+iB\). When \(X=0\), take \(m=1\).

**Proof.** Use (24)–(25) in the exact allocation (17). Replace \(V_T\) there by \(U_T\) and add the nonnegative term \(\det K_T\|Z_T\|_F^2\) inside the sum. The inertia identity (13) now needs only the inertia of \(X\). This gives an exact identity with the same nonnegative score-regression remainder and the additional sum (26), proving (27). \(\square\)

In particular, let
\[
X=\alpha uu^T-\beta vv^T,
\qquad\alpha,\beta\ge0,\qquad u,v\in\mathbb R^n,
\]
and let \(B\) be **any** real skew-symmetric matrix. The real part has at most one eigenvalue of either sign. There is no budget restriction, and the complete exact identity is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(X+iB)={}&I_K(X)+\mathcal J_K(B)\\
&+2\alpha\beta\sum_Tw_T\det K_T\left[
(u_T^TK_T^{-1}u_T)(v_T^TK_T^{-1}v_T)
-(u_T^TK_T^{-1}v_T)^2\right]\ge0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{28}
\]
Each bracket is a real Gram determinant. Formula (28) treats all same-sign and opposite-sign eigenvalue pairs of the full direction through an exact real/imaginary decomposition; it does not discard its high-rank part.

For connected Stieltjes odds, (28) is strict for every nonzero such direction. If the Fisher information is zero, the full-set term has zero trace and a strictly positive squared Hermitian norm. If the Fisher information is positive, its first term already gives strictness.

This theorem is a global pointwise direction statement: it applies at every strict real Stieltjes base, with no size or correlation restriction. An arbitrary imaginary affine line need not keep its bases real or gauge-Stieltjes. A claim of concavity along every such whole line does not follow. The next section supplies a separate actual finite-segment result.

## 5. A finite connected direction class from affine Stieltjes odds

The saturation class can contain an entire affine segment even though the odds transformation is nonlinear. The following elementary resolvent identity supplies a checkable way to keep the odds Stieltjes along that segment.

Let \(T>0\) be real Stieltjes, with spectral endpoints \(\mu,M\), condition number \(\kappa=M/\mu\), and let
\[
L_j=a_jT+b_jI,\qquad a_j>0,\quad b_j\ge0,\quad j=0,1.
\]
Put \(K_j=L_j(I+L_j)^{-1}\) and \(K_t=(1-t)K_0+tK_1\). Write \(c_j=1+b_j\). The hole kernel is
\[
I-K_t=(1-t)(a_0T+c_0I)^{-1}+t(a_1T+c_1I)^{-1}.
\tag{29}
\]
For \(x>0\), its reciprocal scalar function is
\[
h_t(x)=\frac{(a_0x+c_0)(a_1x+c_1)}{\bar a x+\bar c},
\qquad
\bar a=(1-t)a_1+ta_0,\quad
\bar c=(1-t)c_1+tc_0.
\]
Define
\[
r=\bar c/\bar a>0,\qquad
\alpha=a_0a_1/\bar a>0,\qquad
\gamma=\frac{t(1-t)(a_0c_1-a_1c_0)^2}{\bar a^3}\ge0.
\]
Polynomial division gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\ell_t(x):=h_t(x)-1
=\ell_t(0)+\alpha x+\frac\gamma r\frac{x}{x+r},
\qquad
\ell_t(0)=\frac{c_0c_1}{\bar c}-1\ge0.
}
\tag{30}
\]
The nonnegative constant follows because \(1/h_t(0)=(1-t)/c_0+t/c_1\le1\).

The odds of \(K_t\) are exactly \(L_t=\ell_t(T)\). A positive definite Stieltjes matrix has an entrywise nonnegative inverse, as is seen by its convergent normalized nonnegative Neumann series. Hence
\[
T(T+rI)^{-1}=I-r(T+rI)^{-1}
\]
has nonpositive off-diagonal entries. Formula (30) proves that every \(L_t\) is positive definite Stieltjes, with connected graph whenever \(T\) is connected. Also, \(\ell_t\) is increasing and \(\ell_t(x)/x\) is decreasing on \((0,\infty)\); therefore
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(L_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(L_t)}
=\frac{\ell_t(M)}{\ell_t(\mu)}\le\frac M\mu=\kappa.
\tag{31}
\]
If both endpoint odds are at least \(\nu I\), (29) implies the same lower bound \(L_t\succeq\nu I\).

**Corollary 4 (finite chords).** If
\[
\min(a_0\mu+b_0,a_1\mu+b_1)\ge\nu,
\qquad (n-1)\mathfrak b(\kappa)\le1+\nu,
\tag{32}
\]
then entropy is concave on the whole affine segment \([K_0,K_1]\). Every base on it has the full ambient Hermitian Hessian bound (21). The matrices \(L_1-L_0\) and \(K_1-K_0\) may be indefinite and have full rank. This is not an assumption that entropy is invariant under arbitrary unitary changes of the occupation basis; \(T\) is explicitly Stieltjes in that basis.

### An exact six-site indefinite segment

Let
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&0&0&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&0&0&0\\
0&-1&2&-1&0&0\\
0&0&-1&2&-1&0\\
0&0&0&-1&2&-1\\
0&0&0&0&-1&2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad L_0=120T,\qquad L_1=60T+120I.
\tag{33}
\]
The exact inverse formula
\((T^{-1})_{ij}=\min(i,j)(7-\max(i,j))/7\), with one-based indices, gives maximum inverse row sum \(6\). Since \(\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le4\),
\[
\lambda_{\min}(T)\ge1/6,\qquad\kappa(T)\le24.
\]
Both endpoint odds have minimum eigenvalue at least \(20\). Furthermore
\[
\mathfrak b(24)=\log(625/96)<2,
\]
because \(e^2>1+2+2+8/6+16/24=7>625/96\). Thus (21) yields, at every point of the segment,
\[
\boxed{
H''_{K_t}(A)\le-\frac{11}{21}I_{K_t}(A)
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.
}
\tag{34}
\]
The direction of this segment is
\[
\boxed{
A_{\mathrm{seg}}=K_1-K_0
=60(2I-T)(I+120T)^{-1}(I+60T+120I)^{-1}.
}
\tag{35}
\]
All factors commute, and the last two are positive definite, so its inertia is the inertia of the path adjacency \(2I-T\). Alternating coordinate signs conjugate that adjacency to its negative, while its determinant is \(-1\). Its spectrum has three positive and three negative eigenvalues and no zero. Thus (35) has rank six and inertia \((3,3)\).

The base is connected throughout. This direction is not confined to one row or a fixed two-coordinate principal block. The full-Hessian estimate holds throughout the segment, not just at the retained midpoint.

## 6. Exact computation and active Fisher compensation

For the path matrix \(T\) in (33), the closed-walk table has a particularly short exact form. If \(I\) ranges over contiguous coordinate intervals, then
\[
\boxed{
-\log\det((T/2)_S)
=\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq S\text{ contiguous}\\|I|\ge2}}
\log\frac{|I|^2}{|I|^2-1}.
}
\tag{36}
\]
Indeed, a contiguous component of length \(r\) has determinant \(r+1\) in \(T\). Summing the second differences
\(\log(k^2/(k^2-1))\) with multiplicities \(r-k+1\) gives
\(r\log2-\log(r+1)\), and disconnected components multiply. Formula (36) holds unchanged for every scalar multiple of \(T\) after its modular diagonal contribution is removed.

The first example uses \(L=T\),
\[
X=\tfrac1{10}\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0,0,0,-1),
\qquad B_{ij}=1\ (i<j),\quad B_{ji}=-1.
\tag{37}
\]
The full direction is \(A=X+iB\). Here \(\det B=1\) and
\(\|B^{-1}\|_F^2=30\). Hence the eigenvalues of \(iB\) stay outside
\((-1/\sqrt{30},1/\sqrt{30})\), while \(\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1/10<1/\sqrt{30}\). The direction has inertia \((3,3)\), by a nonsingular homotopy from \(iB\), whose eigenvalues occur in opposite pairs. The real part has inertia \((1,1)\), so (28) applies without a budget.

In this example the exact event budget is approximately \(1.69544016>3/2\). Applying a rank-six inertia-(3,3) budget directly would not prove the sign: \((2/3)\mathcal B_K>1\). The real-part refinement removes that obstruction exactly.

The second example uses \(L=8T\). Let \(V\) be unit lower-bidiagonal with subdiagonal \(1/5\), and put
\[
A=V\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3,4,5,6)V^T>0.
\tag{38}
\]
The complete normalized walk mass is \(f(E)=\log(64/7)>2\), so the unweighted total-mass budget fails even for real-part inertia two. In contrast, exact neighboring-minor bounds give
\[
\sum_i(1-K_{ii})\delta_i
\in(0.72082363926848,0.72082363926850)<3/4.
\tag{39}
\]
Consequently (16), with \(m\le6\), proves the full-Hessian bound
\(H''\le-(3/8)I\) at this base. Its frozen curvature in direction (38) is strictly positive.

The last example evaluates the actual rank-six indefinite segment direction (35) at its midpoint. Its frozen curvature is also strictly positive.
