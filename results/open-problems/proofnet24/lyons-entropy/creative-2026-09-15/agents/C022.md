# C022 — sharp conditional block replacement at coherent endpoints

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a new restricted affine-segment theorem, not a proof of the full Lyons conjecture. C006 supplies the universal product-replacement entropy theorem; C007 supplies the coherent block attenuation and conditional-kernel formulas. The new content is an exact classification of when that coherent attenuation becomes measured block replacement, its simultaneous realization for arbitrary coherent blocks, and a structural obstruction to substituting commuting endpoint heat-bath updates. No subagents, external messages, or pushes were used. Mathematical coordination with C023 avoided duplicate proofs.

## 1. The affine-segment theorem

Partition the measured coordinates into nonempty blocks $B_1,\ldots,B_m$. Let
\[
D=\bigoplus_{i=1}^m D_i,\qquad 0\le D\le I,
\]
where the individual $D_i$ may be arbitrary nondiagonal Hermitian contractions. Let $A=A^*$, assume $0\le K:=D+A\le I$, and impose
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank} A_{B_i,E}\le1\quad\text{for every }i.}
\tag{1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
A=UMU^*,
\tag{2}
\]
where the nonzero $i$-th column $u_i$ of $U$ is supported on $B_i$, and $M$ is an arbitrary Hermitian $m\times m$ matrix. Neither $M\ge0$ nor a common eigenvector condition $D_i u_i\propto u_i$ is required.

**Theorem A.** Under (1),
\[
\eta\longmapsto H(P_{D+\eta A})
\]
is concave on $[0,1]$. If $0<D<I$ and $A\ne0$, it is strictly concave. More precisely, writing $\mu_i=P_{D_i}$, and letting $R_i$ replace the entire measured block $B_i$ independently with law $\mu_i$,
\[
\boxed{
P_{D+\eta A}=\prod_i[\eta I+(1-\eta)R_i]P_K.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This permits arbitrarily many coherent blocks and unrestricted rank of $A$ up to the number of blocks. For C007's endpoint $D=B\oplus\operatorname{diag}(d_j)$, the only restriction beyond validity of the kernels is
\[
\operatorname{rank}[K_{UU}-B\ \ K_{UO}]\le1;
\tag{4}
\]
the entire outside kernel remains arbitrary. The result concerns affine covariance segments, rather than C021's affine amplitude scaling against a fixed coherent exterior.

## 2. Conditional replacement: an exact criterion

First work with strict kernels. Write one block and its complement as
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}A_0&V\\V^*&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0<B<I.
\]
The coherent attenuation toward $B$ is
\[
\mathcal A_s^B(K)=
\begin{pmatrix}B+s(A_0-B)&\sqrt{s}V\\\sqrt{s}V^*&C\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]
It preserves positive contractions. For an outside atom $Y\subset O$, its probability $q_Y>0$ stays fixed, while the conditional block kernel is
\[
C_Y(s)=B+s(C_Y-B),\qquad
C_Y=A_0-V(C-I_{O\setminus Y})^{-1}V^*.
\tag{6}
\]

**Theorem B (sharp block-replacement criterion).** The following are equivalent:

1. For every $s\in[0,1]$,
   \[
   P_{\mathcal A_s^B(K)}=sP_K+(1-s)(P_B\otimes P_C).
   \tag{7}
   \]
2. Every outside atom satisfies $\operatorname{rank}(C_Y-B)\le1$.
3. $\operatorname{rank}[A_0-B\ \ V]\le1$.

Thus (4) is the exact condition for the literal coherent block-replacement step, not merely an eigenspace-based sufficient condition.

**Proof of $1\Longleftrightarrow2$.** A Hermitian line $F+sE$ has an affine DPP law precisely when $\operatorname{rank}E\le1$. Sufficiency follows from determinant multilinearity or the rank-one determinant lemma. Conversely, affineness of every inclusion probability forces $\det E_S=0$ for every $|S|\ge2$, by taking the highest-degree coefficient in $\det(F_S+sE_S)$. Hence
\[
\det(I+zE)=1+z\operatorname{tr}E,
\]
so the Hermitian matrix $E$ has at most one nonzero eigenvalue. Apply this observation separately to every positive conditional fiber (6).

**Proof of $3\Longrightarrow2$.** All columns of $A_0-B$ and $V$ lie in one common line, so (6) makes every $C_Y-B$ supported on that line.

**Proof of $2\Longrightarrow3$.** This is the substantive necessity step. Set
\[
L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0.
\]
Conditioning the outside coordinates to equal $Y$ gives the block $L$-kernel
\[
L^Y=L_{BB}-L_{BY}L_{YY}^{-1}L_{YB},\qquad
C_Y=I-(I+L^Y)^{-1}.
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, $C_{Y\cup\{j\}}\le C_Y$. This follows from the Schur-complement update for $L^Y$ and order reversal under inversion.

Put $E_Y=C_Y-B$, all of rank at most one by hypothesis. For an outside singleton $j$, Sherman–Morrison gives
\[
C_\varnothing-C_{\{j\}}=\frac{w_jw_j^*}{\delta_j},\quad
w_j=(I+L_{BB})^{-1}L_{Bj},\quad
\delta_j=L_{jj}-L_{jB}(I+L_{BB})^{-1}L_{Bj}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Block inversion of $I+L$ shows
\[
\operatorname{span}\{w_j:j\in O\}=\operatorname{range}V.
\tag{10}
\]

If $E_\varnothing\ne0$, two nonzero rank-one Hermitian matrices with nonparallel ranges have a rank-two difference. Since $E_\varnothing$, $E_{\{j\}}$, and their difference all have rank at most one, every nonzero $w_j$ therefore lies in the range of $E_\varnothing$.

If $E_\varnothing=0$, then $E_{\{j\}}=-w_jw_j^*/\delta_j$. For any distinct $j,k$, the positive semidefinite matrix $-E_{\{j,k\}}$, of rank at most one, dominates both $w_jw_j^*/\delta_j$ and $w_kw_k^*/\delta_k$. Their ranges must coincide whenever both are nonzero. Thus (10) again has dimension at most one. The cases of zero or one outside coordinate are immediate.

Finally,
\[
C_\varnothing=A_0+V(I-C)^{-1}V^*.
\tag{11}
\]
In both cases above, (11) places the columns of $A_0-B$ in the same line as $V$, or gives $\operatorname{rank}(A_0-B)\le1$ if $V=0$. This proves 3. $\square$

## 3. Simultaneous realization and the Jeffreys bound

Return to (2). Attenuate each block $i$ toward $D_i$, with its own parameter $s_i$. The block operations commute and give exactly
\[
K_{\mathbf s}=D+U S^{1/2}MS^{1/2}U^*,\qquad
S=\operatorname{diag}(s_i).
\tag{12}
\]
Every intermediate kernel remains a positive contraction: one block step is a compression of $K$, plus $(1-s_i)D_i$ on that block, and the same statement applied to $I-K$ proves the upper bound.

For every block of every intermediate kernel, the row condition in Theorem B remains valid. Hence its measured action is exactly $s_i I+(1-s_i)R_i$, proving
\[
P_{K_{\mathbf s}}=\prod_i[s_iI+(1-s_i)R_i]P_K.
\tag{13}
\]
Taking all $s_i=\eta$ proves (3).

For completeness, the resulting curvature is the actual measured Shannon curvature with no unbounded coherent correction. Put $p_\eta=P_{D+\eta A}$, $\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i$, and
\[
J(p,q)=D(p\Vert q)+D(q\Vert p),\quad
I(p)=\sum_iJ(p,R_ip),\quad
\chi_i(p)=\sum_x\frac{(p_x-(R_ip)_x)^2}{p_x}.
\]
For $i\ne j$, define the ordinary classical contraction rate
\[
\mathscr E_j(p,R_ip)=
-\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}J(C_{j,e^{-\tau}}p,C_{j,e^{-\tau}}R_ip)\right|_{\tau=0},
\quad C_{j,s}=sI+(1-s)R_j.
\tag{14}
\]
Data processing gives $\mathscr E_j\ge0$. In time $\tau=-\log\eta$, the product-replacement identity yields exactly
\[
\frac{d}{d\tau}D(p\Vert\mu)=-I(p),
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{d}{d\tau}I(p)
=-I(p)-\sum_i\chi_i(p)-\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p,R_ip).}
\tag{15}
\]
These are C006's finite-alphabet Jeffreys identities, now rigorously applicable to the coherent measured blocks. The own-block contribution is $J(p,R_ip)+\chi_i(p)$; the other contributions are (14).

Every measured block marginal is affine in $\eta$, so cross entropy against $\mu$ is affine. Changing time in (15) therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2 H''(p_\eta)
=\sum_i\chi_i(p_\eta)
+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(p_\eta,R_ip_\eta)
\ge\sum_i\chi_i(p_\eta)\ge0.
}
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the otherwise uncontrolled coherent correction is identically zero in this family. In particular, C007's potentially negative block/exterior cross terms become genuine classical contraction rates under (4).

When $D$ is strict, equality in the final bound at $0<\eta<1$ requires $p_\eta=\mu$. Each replacement factor is invertible for $\eta>0$, so this forces $P_K=P_D$. The block marginal singleton moments then force every $M_{ii}=0$; cross-block covariances $-|K_{ab}|^2$, which vanish for $D$, force every $M_{ij}=0$. Hence $A=0$, proving strictness. General boundary kernels follow by replacing $D,K$ with $(1-2\epsilon)D+\epsilon I,(1-2\epsilon)K+\epsilon I$ and taking $\epsilon\downarrow0$. This preserves (1), and finite Shannon entropy is continuous.

## 4. Why singleton endpoint heat baths cannot replace this construction

There is also an exact obstruction to extending C006 merely by replacing product resets with the endpoint's conditional coordinate distributions.

**Theorem C.** Let $0<D<I$, $\mu=P_D$, and let
\[
(R_i^\mu p)(x)=\mu(x_i\mid x_{-i})p_{-i}(x_{-i}).
\]
For distinct $i,j$, the maps $R_i^\mu,R_j^\mu$ commute if and only if $i,j$ belong to different connected components of the graph of nonzero off-diagonal entries of $D$. Consequently all singleton heat-bath maps commute if and only if $D$ is diagonal.

**Proof.** On densities relative to $\mu$, the maps are conditional-expectation projections. Full support implies that two such projections commute precisely when $X_i$ and $X_j$ are conditionally independent given all remaining coordinates: their intersection consists of functions of the remaining coordinates, and commuting orthogonal projections project onto that intersection.

For the positive definite $L=D(I-D)^{-1}$, this conditional independence is equivalent to
\[
\det L_{Sij}\det L_S=\det L_{Si}\det L_{Sj}
\quad\text{for every }S\subset E\setminus\{i,j\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Schur complements make the difference between the two sides
\[
-(\det L_S)^2\left|L_{ij}-L_{iS}L_S^{-1}L_{Sj}\right|^2.
\tag{18}
\]
If $i,j$ are connected in the graph of $L$, choose a shortest path and let $S$ be its internal vertices. The path is induced, so $L_S$ is Hermitian tridiagonal along that path; its endpoint inverse entry is a nonzero product of path edges divided by $\det L_S$. Consequently the expression inside the absolute value in (18) is nonzero. A direct edge uses $S=\varnothing$. Thus (17) fails.

Different graph components factor the entire law, so their heat baths do commute. Finally $D$ and $L$ have exactly the same finest block-diagonal partition, because each is an invertible rational matrix function of the other. This proves the claim for the graph of $D$. $\square$

This rules out a commuting-singleton tensorization at any connected coherent full-support endpoint. It does not rule out noncommuting conditional generators or corrected dissipation inequalities. Theorem A instead uses an entire coherent component as the update unit, with Theorem B identifying exactly when that larger update has no measured correction.

## 5. Exact example, numerical verification, and limits

A rational four-site example has two coherent blocks:
\[
D_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&1/10\\1/10&1/2\end{pmatrix},\quad
D_2=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-1/12\\-1/12&2/5\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&0\\1/2&0\\0&1/3\\0&-2/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&1/4\\1/4&-1/6\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{19}
\]
Here $\det M=-23/240<0$, $D_i u_i$ is not proportional to $u_i$, and $D$ and $K=D+UMU^*$ do not commute. Positivity of $K$ and $I-K$ is certified by their respective leading principal minors
\[
(9/20,9/40,3733/28800,8459/216000),
\]
\[
(11/20,9/40,2647/28800,24743/432000).
\]
The exact symbolic calculation verifies (3) for all $\eta$, atom by atom. Its numerically evaluated midpoint entropy gap is approximately $0.00676839879445$.

The independent random check evaluates determinant derivatives and the right side of (16) on 54 strict complex examples in dimensions four through six, with unequal block sizes. Maximum atom-identity error: $6.94\cdot10^{-17}$. Maximum Hessian-identity error: $1.53\cdot10^{-16}$. Every sampled cross contraction rate was positive. These checks are supporting evidence; the theorems above do not depend on numerics.
