# C013 — an eight-atom logarithmic-mean certificate beyond particle–hole symmetry

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a new creative extension using the frozen-log identity in old-097 and the Fisher-allocation idea in C008. The general three-site conjecture remains open. All Hessians below are in **every Hermitian affine direction of K**, not only directions tangent to a displayed family.

## 1. Proved results

Write the eight positive atoms as

\[
p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3,p_{12},p_{13},p_{23},p_{123},\qquad \sum_Sp_S=1.
\]

For complementary indices \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), define

\[
c_i=\log\frac{p_jp_k}{p_0p_{jk}},\qquad
\tau=\log\frac{p_0p_{12}p_{13}p_{23}}{p_1p_2p_3p_{123}}.
\tag{1}
\]

The new results are:

1. An exact nonnegative logarithmic-mean and Fisher-square decomposition whenever the explicit atom certificate in Section 5 holds. There is a version with \(\tau\ne0\), so the certificate applies to a full-dimensional part of the interior kernel space.
2. On **every** connected three-site kernel with \(\tau=0\) and \(m:=\min_i c_i\le1/2\),
   \[
   \boxed{H''_K(A)\le-\frac1{40}\operatorname{FI}_K(A)-m\|A\|_F^2<0\quad(A\ne0).}
   \tag{2}
   \]
   This allows arbitrary unequal edges and arbitrary positive external fields.
3. If \(\tau=0\), the two smallest \(c_i\)'s agree, and \(m\le3/4\), the stronger allocation gives
   \[
   \boxed{H''_K(A)\le-\frac{11}{80}\operatorname{FI}_K(A)-m\|A\|_F^2.}
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Section 7 gives an explicit connected family with arbitrary three external fields and a normalized edge tending to one. In particular this is not merely an ambient neighborhood obtained by continuity from C008.
4. An exact obstruction to replacing the full Fisher information by **all linear occupation information**, even on \(\tau=0\). The obstruction occurs inside the family proved in item 3: the true Hessian is strictly negative, but the optimized occupation-only surrogate has a positive-curvature direction. A rare two-site atom is essential to the successful decomposition.

Historical novelty outside the supplied project sources has not been investigated. The elementary two-site determinant estimate is an input already proved in old-097; its exact square decomposition and its use in the new three-site allocation are supplied below. The new theorems do not assert the complete \(\tau=0\) case, complete three-site concavity, or the general Lyons conjecture.

## 2. Exact admissibility of eight atom variables

The following is a complete algebraic characterization for **complex Hermitian** three-site DPPs with full support. It is useful because arbitrary positive eight-tuples are not admissible determinantal laws.

Set

\[
g_{ij}=p_ip_j-p_0p_{ij},
\qquad
C=\frac{p_0^2p_{123}+2p_1p_2p_3-p_0(p_1p_{23}+p_2p_{13}+p_3p_{12})}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]

Then the positive normalized atom vector is the law of an interior Hermitian contraction if and only if

\[
\boxed{g_{12},g_{13},g_{23}\ge0,\qquad C^2\le g_{12}g_{13}g_{23}.}
\tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** Let \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\). Since \(p_S=p_0\det L_S\),

\[
L_{ii}=p_i/p_0,\quad |L_{ij}|^2=g_{ij}/p_0^2,\quad
\operatorname{Re}(L_{12}L_{23}L_{31})=C/p_0^3.
\]

This proves necessity. Conversely, when \(g_{12}g_{13}>0\), choose the gauge

\[
L_{12}=\frac{\sqrt{g_{12}}}{p_0},\quad
L_{13}=\frac{\sqrt{g_{13}}}{p_0},\quad
L_{23}=\frac{C+i\sqrt{g_{12}g_{13}g_{23}-C^2}}
 {p_0\sqrt{g_{12}g_{13}}},\quad L_{ii}=p_i/p_0.
\tag{6}
\]

Take the conjugate entries below the diagonal. If a relevant \(g\) vanishes, (5) forces \(C=0\), and choose the nonzero edges with the specified magnitudes and any compatible phases. The principal determinants of this Hermitian matrix are exactly \(p_S/p_0>0\); hence \(L>0\). Also
\(\det(I+L)=\sum_S\det L_S=1/p_0\). Therefore \(K=L(I+L)^{-1}\) is an interior contraction with exactly the prescribed atoms. This proves sufficiency.

The six conditional determinant identities are

\[
p_ip_j-p_0p_{ij}=p_0^2|L_{ij}|^2,
\tag{7}
\]

\[
p_{ij}p_{ik}-p_ip_{123}
=p_0^2|L_{ii}L_{jk}-L_{ji}L_{ik}|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

In particular, (1) obeys

\[
c_i\ge0,\qquad c_i+\tau
=\log\frac{p_{ij}p_{ik}}{p_ip_{123}}\ge0.
\tag{9}
\]

These are not extra assumptions placed on an arbitrary probability vector: they follow from the admissibility conditions above.

## 3. The complete Hessian in the atom chart

Reconstruct \(K\) from (6). The two choices of the square root are conjugate and give equivalent all-direction Hessian statements. No direction is lost by choosing a diagonal unitary gauge at the base.

For a Hermitian direction \(A\), write

\[
a_i=A_{ii},\qquad
d_i=D\det K_{jk}[A]=K_{jj}a_k+K_{kk}a_j
 -2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{K_{jk}}A_{jk}),
\qquad f=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}K\,A).
\tag{10}
\]

All eight first atom derivatives \(q_S=Dp_S[A]\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
S&q_S\\ \hline
123&f\\
jk&d_i-f\\
i&a_i-d_j-d_k+f\\
0&-\sum_i a_i+\sum_i d_i-f.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]

Set

\[
b_i=\det A_{jk}=a_ja_k-|A_{jk}|^2,
\qquad \eta=\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}A).
\]

The complete second atom derivatives are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
S&\tfrac12D^2p_S[A,A]\\ \hline
123&\eta\\
jk&b_i-\eta\\
i&\eta-b_j-b_k\\
0&\sum_i b_i-\eta.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]

These formulas follow by inclusion–exclusion from the seven principal minors and the identities \(D^2\det K_{jk}=2\det A_{jk}\), \(D^2\det K=2\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}A)\). Therefore, with

\[
I:=\operatorname{FI}_K(A)=\sum_S q_S^2/p_S,
\]

the full nine-real-coordinate quadratic form is

\[
\boxed{-H''_K(A)=I-2\sum_i c_i b_i-2\tau\eta.}
\tag{13}
\]

Equations (4)–(13) express the full Hessian using the eight admissible atom values and a nine-coordinate Hermitian direction. The phase information necessary to evaluate the directional derivatives is reconstructed by (6); it cannot simply be discarded and replaced by arbitrary atom tangents.

For positive \(x,y\), let the logarithmic mean be

\[
\mathcal L(x,y)=\frac{x-y}{\log x-\log y},\qquad \mathcal L(x,x)=x.
\tag{14}
\]

The coefficients in (13) are exact divided differences. For example,

\[
c_i=\frac{p_jp_k-p_0p_{jk}}{\mathcal L(p_jp_k,p_0p_{jk})},
\quad c_i+\tau
=\frac{p_{ij}p_{ik}-p_ip_{123}}
 {\mathcal L(p_{ij}p_{ik},p_ip_{123})}.
\tag{15}
\]

The numerators in (15) are the admissible squared determinants (7)–(8). The remaining issue is that they multiply indefinite principal minors of \(A\). The following allocation retains those minors inside positive quadratic forms.

## 4. A two-site Fisher form with an exact sum of squares

Consider the marginal pair \(\{j,k\}\). Its four atom probabilities, expressed in the eight original atoms, are

\[
x_i=p_0+p_i,\quad
y_i=p_j+p_{ij},\quad
z_i=p_k+p_{ik},\quad
w_i=p_{jk}+p_{123}.
\tag{16}
\]

Let \(J=K_{jk}\), \(B=A_{jk}\), and let \(I_i\) be the Fisher information of that marginal in direction \(B\). Define

\[
T_i=\frac{y_i+z_i}{2\sqrt{x_iw_i}}\ge1,
\qquad C_i=4(1+T_i).
\tag{17}
\]

The inequality \(T_i\ge1\) follows from \(y_iz_i\ge x_iw_i\), the two-site determinantal identity.

**Pair lemma.** For every Hermitian \(B\), including indefinite \(B\),

\[
\boxed{I_i-C_i\det B\ge0.}
\tag{18}
\]

Here is an exact square decomposition proving it. Put

\[
P=\frac{\operatorname{adj}J}{\sqrt{w_i}},\quad
Q=\frac{\operatorname{adj}(I-J)}{\sqrt{x_i}},\quad
W=P+Q>0,
\]

\[
\alpha=\operatorname{tr}(PB),\quad
\beta=\operatorname{tr}(QB),\quad
V=W^{1/2}BW^{1/2}.
\]

One has \(\det P=\det Q=1\) and
\(\det W=2+(y_i+z_i)/\sqrt{x_iw_i}=2+2T_i\). Let \(\dot y_i,\dot z_i\) denote the derivatives of the two mixed atoms. Then

\[
\boxed{
I_i-C_i\det B
=\frac{\dot y_i^2}{y_i}+\frac{\dot z_i^2}{z_i}
+\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^2}{2}
+\frac{(V_{11}-V_{22})^2+4|V_{12}|^2}{2}.}
\tag{19}
\]

Indeed the extreme atoms contribute \(\alpha^2+\beta^2\); expand this as \((\alpha-\beta)^2/2+(\alpha+\beta)^2/2\), use \(\alpha+\beta=\operatorname{tr}V\), and use \(\det V=(\det W)\det B\). This proves (19) and (18) without a definiteness case split.

The full score \(s_S=q_S/p_S\) gives two other exact nonnegative square forms:

\[
I-I_i=\sum_Sp_S\{s_S-\mathbb E[s_X\mid X\cap\{j,k\}]\}^2\ge0,
\tag{20}
\]

and, with \(N=|X|\), \(v=\operatorname{Var}N=\operatorname{tr}(K-K^2)>0\), \(s=\operatorname{tr}A\),

\[
I-\frac{s^2}{v}
=\sum_Sp_S\left\{s_S-\frac{s}{v}(N(S)-\mathbb EN)\right\}^2\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]

The identity in (21) uses \(\mathbb E s_X=0\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(s_X,N)=s\). Also \(v\le3/4\).

## 5. The logarithmic-mean allocation certificate

First suppose \(\tau=0\). Set

\[
m=\min_i c_i,\quad \Delta_i=c_i-m,\quad
f_i=\frac{\Delta_i}{2(1+T_i)},\quad F=\sum_i f_i.
\tag{22}
\]

Because \(C_i f_i=2\Delta_i\), (13) has the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&(1-F-mv)I
+mv\left(I-\frac{s^2}{v}\right)+m\|A\|_F^2\\
&+\sum_i f_i\left\{(I-I_i)+(I_i-C_i b_i)\right\}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{23}
\]

Expansion verifies (23), using \(2\sum_i b_i=s^2-\|A\|_F^2\). Every bracket has been explicitly written as squares in (19)–(21). Therefore

\[
\boxed{F+mv\le1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H''_K(A)\le-(1-F-mv)I-m\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{24}
\]

The coefficients really are nonnegative logarithmic-mean terms. Let \(u_i=e^{-c_i}=p_0p_{jk}/(p_jp_k)\), and \(u=\max_i u_i=e^{-m}\). Then

\[
m=\frac{1-u}{\mathcal L(1,u)},\qquad
f_i=\frac{u-u_i}{2(1+T_i)\mathcal L(u,u_i)}.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus (23), under its explicit scalar budget condition, is a sum of nonnegative logarithmic-mean-weighted square forms valid beyond particle–hole symmetry. It retains the marginal-pair Fisher information that a count-only or occupation-only argument can lose.

### A full-dimensional version allowing nonzero triple interaction

For arbitrary \(\tau\), put

\[
\bar c_i=c_i+\tau/2\ge0,\quad
\mu=\min_i\bar c_i,\quad
\Delta_i=\bar c_i-\mu=c_i-\min_jc_j,
\quad f_i=\frac{\Delta_i}{2(1+T_i)},\quad F=\sum_i f_i.
\tag{26}
\]

The same exact decomposition holds with \(m\) replaced by \(\mu\), except that its Frobenius term becomes

\[
\mathcal R_K(A)=\mu\|A\|_F^2
-2\tau\operatorname{tr}\bigl((K-I/2)\operatorname{adj}A\bigr).
\tag{27}
\]

Let \(\kappa=\|K-I/2\|_{\rm op}<1/2\). If \(\lambda_i\) are the eigenvalues of \(A\),

\[
\|\operatorname{adj}A\|_*
=\sum_{i<j}|\lambda_i\lambda_j|
\le\sum_i\lambda_i^2=\|A\|_F^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal R_K(A)\ge(\mu-2|\tau|\kappa)\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{28}
\]

This proves the fully general sufficient test

\[
\boxed{
F+\mu v\le1,\quad \mu\ge2|\tau|\kappa
\ \Longrightarrow\ 
H''_K(A)\le-(1-F-\mu v)I
 -(\mu-2|\tau|\kappa)\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{29}
\]

The slightly stronger hypothesis \(\mu\ge|\tau|\) makes the test **entirely atom-based**, without reconstructing a spectral norm. The \(\bar c_i\)'s are averages of the two nonnegative logarithmic means in (15). Thus the residual form (27) is the precise remaining correction when the complementary log ratios do not balance. This does not assert that (29) holds at every three-site base.

For an explicit \(\tau\ne0\) example, take

\[
L_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&1\\
1&1&(19+9i)/25\\
1&(19-9i)/25&1
\end{pmatrix}>0,\qquad K_*=L_*(I+L_*)^{-1}.
\tag{30}
\]

Its principal pair determinants are \(1,1,183/625\), and its determinant is \(66/625>0\). Its coefficients are

\[
(c_1,c_2,c_3)=\bigl(\log(625/183),\log2,\log2\bigr),
\quad \tau=\log(61/44)>0,
\quad \mu=\log2+\tfrac12\log(61/44).
\]

Only one \(\Delta_i\) is nonzero and
\(\Delta_1=\log(625/366)<\log2<3/4\). Since \(T_i\ge1\), \(F<3/16\). Also \(\tau<17/44<1/2\), so \(\mu<1\), giving \(1-F-\mu v>1/16\). Finally,

\[
\mu-\tau=\tfrac12\log(176/61)>115/237,
\]

using \(\log x>2(x-1)/(x+1)\) for \(x>1\). Thus

\[
H''_{K_*}(A)\le-\frac1{16}I-\frac{115}{237}\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{31}
\]

The strict certificate inequalities persist on an ambient open neighborhood. The main extension of this report, however, is the explicit boundary-approaching family in Section 7, for which a bounded normalized-edge or bounded-condition-number restriction would not suffice.

## 6. Uniform bounds on the zero-triple-interaction locus

Suppose \(\tau=0\). Let \(\ell_i=p_i/p_0>0\), and retain \(u_i=e^{-c_i}\in(0,1]\). The atoms have the exact odds form

\[
\frac{p_i}{p_0}=\ell_i,\qquad
\frac{p_{jk}}{p_0}=\ell_j\ell_k u_i,\qquad
\frac{p_{123}}{p_0}=\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3 u_1u_2u_3.
\tag{32}
\]

For a pair \(jk\), its four marginal weights are

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_i/p_0&=1+\ell_i,\\
y_i/p_0&=\ell_j(1+\ell_i u_k),\\
z_i/p_0&=\ell_k(1+\ell_i u_j),\\
w_i/p_0&=\ell_j\ell_k u_i(1+\ell_i u_ju_k).
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]

Let \(u=\max_i u_i\). Whenever \(\Delta_i>0\), one of the other two \(u\)'s equals \(u\); call the remaining value \(a\), and put \(h=\ell_i\). Arithmetic–geometric mean in \(y_i+z_i\) gives

\[
T_i^2\ge
\frac{(1+ha)(1+hu)}{u_i(1+h)(1+hau)}\ge\frac{u}{u_i}.
\tag{34}
\]

The second inequality follows from the exact positive polynomial

\[
(1+ha)(1+hu)-u(1+h)(1+hau)
=(1-u)+ha(1-u^2)+h^2au(1-u)\ge0.
\tag{35}
\]

In particular, if \(z=\sqrt{u/u_i}\ge1\),

\[
f_i=\frac{\log z}{1+T_i}\le\frac{\log z}{1+z}<\frac3{10}.
\tag{36}
\]

Here is a rationally bounded proof of the last uniform constant. The minimum over \(z>0\) of \((3/10)(1+z)-\log z\) is at \(z=10/3\). The elementary inequality
\(\log x\le(x-1)/\sqrt x\), proved by differentiation, yields

\[
\log(10/3)\le\frac7{\sqrt{30}}<\frac{13}{10},
\]

where the final comparison is \(49/30<169/100\). Thus the minimum is positive. No numerical optimization is used in (36).

At most two \(\Delta_i\)'s are nonzero, so \(F<3/5\). If \(m\le1/2\), then \(mv\le3/8\), and hence

\[
1-F-mv\ge1-\frac35-\frac38=\frac1{40}.
\]

This proves (2) through the exact decomposition (23). If the two smallest \(c_i\)'s agree, at most one \(\Delta_i\) is nonzero. For \(m\le3/4\),

\[
1-F-mv\ge1-\frac3{10}-\frac9{16}=\frac{11}{80},
\]

which proves (3).

For strictness, connectedness on \(\tau=0\) implies \(m>0\). To see this directly, let the normalized squared edges of \(L\) be \(r_i=1-u_i\). If, say, \(r_1=0\), the real cycle product is zero. The identity \(\tau=0\) says that the normalized determinant is \(\prod_i(1-r_i)\); comparing this with \(1-r_2-r_3\) forces \(r_2r_3=0\). Thus a zero edge forces a second zero edge, so the kernel is disconnected. For disconnected cases the same nonpositive bounds hold with \(m=0\), without asserting strictness on cross-block directions.

## 7. A concrete family with arbitrary external fields, reaching the spectral boundary

Choose

\[
e^{-3/4}\le u<1,\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le u,
\qquad \ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3>0,
\]

and set

\[
z=1-\frac{1+u}{2}\varepsilon
+i\sqrt{u\varepsilon-\frac{(1+u)^2}{4}\varepsilon^2},
\]

\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\sqrt{1-u}&\sqrt{1-u}\\
\sqrt{1-u}&1&z\\
\sqrt{1-u}&\overline z&1
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
L=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i})\,N\,
\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\ell_i}),
\quad K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\tag{37}
\]

The radical is nonnegative: \(\varepsilon\le u\le4u/(1+u)^2\). Direct determinant identities give

\[
|z|^2=1-\varepsilon,\quad
\det N_{12}=\det N_{13}=u,\quad
\det N_{23}=\varepsilon,\quad
\det N=u^2\varepsilon>0.
\tag{38}
\]

Hence \(L>0\), every \(K\) is interior, and its frozen coefficients are

\[
(c_1,c_2,c_3)=(-\log\varepsilon,-\log u,-\log u),\quad \tau=0.
\tag{39}
\]

Since \(\varepsilon\le u\), the two smallest coefficients agree and \(m=-\log u\le3/4\). Equation (3) therefore applies to **all Hermitian directions** at every point of (37), uniformly over all three positive external fields. Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives a normalized edge tending to one and \(\det L\downarrow0\), while the theorem continues to hold at every interior point. No finite Hessian at the endpoint is asserted.

For a rational complex sequence, take \(u=1/2\), \((\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)=(2,1,1)\), and integers \(n\ge1\):

\[
\varepsilon_n=\frac8{16n^2+9},\qquad
L_n=\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&1\\
1&1&\dfrac{16n^2+3+8in}{16n^2+9}\\
1&\dfrac{16n^2+3-8in}{16n^2+9}&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{40}
\]

Its eight unnormalized atom weights in mask order \(0,1,2,12,3,13,23,123\) are

\[
(1,2,1,1,1,1,\varepsilon_n,\varepsilon_n/2).
\tag{41}
\]

These laws are not particle–hole symmetric. For example their empty and full atoms are unequal. The theorem gives

\[
H''_{K_n}(A)\le-\frac{11}{80}I-(\log2)\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{42}
\]

## 8. Exact obstruction to an occupation-only logarithmic-mean closure

Let \(\Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,X_2,X_3)\). The best lower bound on \(I\) available from **all linear combinations of the occupation variables** is

\[
I\ge a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}a,\qquad a=(A_{11},A_{22},A_{33})^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{43}
\]

On \(\tau=0\), this would prove the Hessian sign if

\[
\Sigma^{-1}-\begin{pmatrix}
0&c_3&c_2\\c_3&0&c_1\\c_2&c_1&0
\end{pmatrix}\ge0.
\tag{44}
\]

However, (44) fails exactly inside the proved family (40). Take \(n=100\), so \(\varepsilon=8/160009\), and \(a=(0,1,1)\). Using the rational atoms (41), exact fraction arithmetic gives

\[
a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}a
=\frac{13765345467524200}{819428370733277}<17.
\tag{45}
\]

But \(1/\varepsilon=160009/8>3^9\), and
\(\log3>2(3-1)/(3+1)=1\). Therefore

\[
a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}a-2c_1
<17-2\log(1/\varepsilon)<17-18=-1.
\tag{46}
\]

Choosing \(A=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,1)\) makes every off-diagonal penalty vanish, so (46) is a genuine negative direction of the surrogate for \(-H''\). It is **not** a positive direction of the actual entropy Hessian: (42) already proves that actual Hessian is strictly negative.

The mechanism persists along the sequence. As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the occupation covariance tends to the positive definite matrix

\[
\Sigma_0=\frac1{49}
\begin{pmatrix}
12&-1&-1\\-1&10&-4\\-1&-4&10
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_0^{-1}a=84/5,
\]

while \(c_1=-\log\varepsilon\to\infty\). In contrast, the marginal-pair determinant budget in (22) costs only \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))\), because \(T_1\) diverges as \(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\). This identifies exactly which information repairs the failed method: the rare two-site atom information in (19), which is absent from the linear occupation projection.
