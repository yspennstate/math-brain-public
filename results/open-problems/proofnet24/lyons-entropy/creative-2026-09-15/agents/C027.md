## 1. Result and scope

There is a nonexchangeable four-site affine family with two coherent endpoint blocks, perturbation block rows of rank two, and a fixed reversible Markov generator realizing its entire determinantal interpolation. Its entropy curvature is controlled by a second, explicitly positive Markov generator. Neither conditional-replacement intertwining nor commutativity of endpoint heat baths is assumed.

Set
\[
K_\eta=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&c&\eta a&0\\
c&1/2&0&i\eta b\\
\eta a&0&1/2&d\\
0&-i\eta b&d&1/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad -1\le\eta\le1,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(0<c,d<1/2\), \(a,b>0\), and \(0<K_1<I\). Its endpoint at zero is
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&c\\c&1/2\end{pmatrix}
\oplus
\begin{pmatrix}1/2&d\\d&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define
\[
\alpha=4c^2,\quad\beta=4d^2,\quad
\gamma=4a^2,\quad\delta=4b^2
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\Theta=
\frac{(\gamma+\delta)^2}
{\gamma^2+\delta^2+2\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
\frac{(1+\alpha)(1+\beta)}{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

**Theorem A.** If \(\Theta\le6/5\), then
\[
\eta\longmapsto H(P_{K_\eta})
\]
is concave on \([-1,1]\), with strictly negative second derivative whenever \(\eta\ne0\).

The direction \(A=K_1-D\) has rank four and inertia \((2,2)\). Both block rows associated with \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\) have rank two. Thus C022's exact rank-one block replacement criterion fails. The two nonzero coherent entries \(c,d\) remain constant along the line, so the family has no diagonal endpoint. The graph of \(D\) is two disjoint edges; the graph of every \(K_\eta\), \(\eta\ne0\), is a cycle. These kernels are not exchangeable, even after diagonal unitary conjugation.

Condition (2) is independent of a common scaling of \(a,b\). It allows substantial perturbations whenever the scaled kernels remain contractions. It is sufficient for entropy concavity; no necessity for entropy concavity is claimed.

## 2. A curvature criterion with the generator derivative retained

Let \(\mu\) be a strictly positive law on a finite space. Write
\[
\langle u,v\rangle_\mu=\sum_x\mu_xu_xv_x.
\]
Let \(L_\tau\) be differentiable Markov generators acting on functions, reversible with respect to the same fixed \(\mu\). Thus their off-diagonal entries are nonnegative, rows sum to zero, and \(\mu_xL_{xy}=\mu_yL_{yx}\). For a positive probability density \(f_\tau\) relative to \(\mu\), suppose
\[
\dot f_\tau=L_\tau f_\tau.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
F(\tau)=\langle f_\tau,\log f_\tau\rangle_\mu,
\qquad
I(\tau)=-\langle L_\tau f_\tau,\log f_\tau\rangle_\mu.
\]
Then \(F'=-I\). Direct differentiation, including the variation of the generator, gives
\[
I'=-\langle (\dot L_\tau+L_\tau^2)f_\tau,\log f_\tau\rangle_\mu
-\sum_x\mu_x\frac{(L_\tau f_\tau)_x^2}{f_\tau(x)}.
\tag{4}
\]
Put
\[
M_\tau=-\bigl(\dot L_\tau+L_\tau^2+L_\tau\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
If \(M_\tau\) is also a Markov generator, it is reversible automatically. Equations (4)–(5) give the **exact** decomposition
\[
\boxed{
-I'-I=
\sum_x\mu_x\frac{(L_\tau f_\tau)_x^2}{f_\tau(x)}
\;+
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\mu_x(M_\tau)_{xy}
(f_\tau(x)-f_\tau(y))
(\log f_\tau(x)-\log f_\tau(y)).
}
\tag{6}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative. This proves \(I'\le-I\) without decomposing \(L_\tau\) into commuting projections.

To identify exactly what is needed for Shannon entropy, set \(\eta=e^{-\tau}\), \(p_\eta=\mu f_\tau\), and
\[
C(\eta)=\sum_xp_\eta(x)\log\mu_x.
\]
Since \(H(p_\eta)=-D(p_\eta\Vert\mu)-C(\eta)\),
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2H''(p_\eta)=-I'-I+\eta^2C''(\eta).
}
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, (6) proves Shannon concavity when \(C\) is affine. The cross-entropy term cannot be dropped for arbitrary reference laws. In the DPP construction below it is exactly constant. Also \(\dot L=0\) there because the generator is fixed, rather than because a derivative term has been omitted.

## 3. A two-mode generator and sharp matrix-sign thresholds

Fix a nonzero real function \(h\) with \(\mathbb E_\mu h=0\), and put
\[
V=\mathbb E_\mu h^2,
\qquad
Rf=(\mathbb E_\mu f)\mathbf1,
\qquad
\Pi_hf=\frac{\mathbb E_\mu(hf)}Vh.
\]
Consider
\[
\boxed{L=4(R-I)+2\Pi_h.}
\tag{8}
\]
The spaces of constants, multiples of \(h\), and their orthogonal complement have eigenvalues \(0,-2,-4\), respectively. Its off-diagonal rates are
\[
L_{xy}=\mu_y\left(4+\frac{2h_xh_y}{V}\right),\qquad x\ne y.
\tag{9}
\]
They obey detailed balance. Define
\[
\kappa=-\min_{x\ne y}\frac{h_xh_y}{V}.
\]
Since \(h\) has both signs, this is positive. Formula (9) shows that \(L\) is Markov **if and only if** \(\kappa\le2\).

The second generator has the explicit form
\[
\boxed{M=-(L^2+L)=12(R-I)+10\Pi_h,}
\tag{10}
\]
with off-diagonal rates
\[
M_{xy}=\mu_y\left(12+\frac{10h_xh_y}{V}\right).
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(M\) is Markov **if and only if** \(\kappa\le6/5\). This is a sharp criterion for the matrix sign in (5) for the prescribed generator (8). It is not asserted to be necessary for \(I'\le-I\) or for the existence of some other generator.

If \(g\perp\mathbf1,h\), the probability curve
\[
p_\eta=\mu(1+\eta^2h+\eta^4g)
\tag{12}
\]
is exactly the flow of (8):
\[
e^{\tau L}(1+h+g)=1+e^{-2\tau}h+e^{-4\tau}g.
\tag{13}
\]
When \(\kappa<6/5\), both \(L\) and \(M\) have strictly positive rates between every two distinct states.

Although \(\Pi_h\) is an orthogonal projection, it is not a stochastic replacement operator. In the example below \(\kappa>1\), so even \(R+\Pi_h\) has a negative matrix entry. In particular the formal decomposition
\[
L=2(R-I)+2\{(R+\Pi_h)-I\}
\]
does not decompose the flow into two Markov replacement channels. Positivity is established for the full generators (8) and (10), and the proof uses (6).

## 4. The determinantal two-mode identity

Use spins \(y_i=1-2X_i\in\{-1,1\}\), and define
\[
z=y_1y_2,\qquad w=y_3y_4,\qquad s=y_1y_3,
\qquad q(z,w)=(1-\alpha z)(1-\beta w).
\]
The endpoint law and two modes are
\[
\boxed{
\mu(y)=\frac{q(z,w)}{16},\qquad
h(y)=-\frac{s(\gamma+\delta zw)}{q(z,w)},\qquad
g(y)=\frac{\gamma\delta zw}{q(z,w)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

**Exact identity.** For the kernels (1),
\[
\boxed{P_{K_\eta}(y)=\mu(y)\{1+\eta^2h(y)+\eta^4g(y)\}.}
\tag{15}
\]

To prove it, use the determinantal spin identity
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}(1-2X_i)=\det(I-2K)_S
\]
and Fourier inversion on the discrete cube. The diagonal of \(I-2K_\eta\) is zero. Its only nonzero two-site determinants are
\[
-\alpha,\quad-\beta,\quad-\eta^2\gamma,\quad-\eta^2\delta
\]
on the edges \(12,34,13,24\). There are no triangles. The four-cycle contribution to the full determinant is zero because its edge product is purely imaginary. The full determinant is therefore
\[
\alpha\beta+\eta^4\gamma\delta.
\]
Fourier inversion yields
\[
16P_{K_\eta}(y)=
(1-\alpha z)(1-\beta w)
-\eta^2s(\gamma+\delta zw)+\eta^4\gamma\delta zw,
\]
which proves (15).

Under \(\mu\), conditional on \((z,w)\), \(s\) is a fair sign. Consequently \(h\perp\mathbf1,g\). Also \(\mathbb E_\mu g=0\), by summing \(zw\) over the four equally weighted macro configurations after cancellation of \(q\).

Straight summation gives
\[
V=\mathbb E_\mu h^2
=\frac{\gamma^2+\delta^2+2\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
{(1-\alpha^2)(1-\beta^2)},
\qquad
\|h\|_\infty=\frac{\gamma+\delta}{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Both signs of the extremal value occur, so
\[
\kappa=\frac{\|h\|_\infty^2}{V}=\Theta.
\tag{17}
\]
Theorem A's hypothesis therefore makes both (8) and (10) Markov.

Finally, \(\mu=\mu_{12}\otimes\mu_{34}\), and the two measured block marginals of \(P_{K_\eta}\) remain \(\mu_{12},\mu_{34}\), because their kernel principal blocks are constant. It follows that
\[
\sum_yP_{K_\eta}(y)\log\mu(y)
=\sum\mu_{12}\log\mu_{12}+\sum\mu_{34}\log\mu_{34}
\]
is constant. Apply (6)–(7) to obtain
\[
\boxed{
-\eta^2\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}H(P_{K_\eta})
=\sum_y\mu(y)\frac{(Lf_\eta(y))^2}{f_\eta(y)}
\;+
\frac12\sum_{x,y}\mu_xM_{xy}
(f_\eta(x)-f_\eta(y))
(\log f_\eta(x)-\log f_\eta(y))\ge0.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Here \(f_\eta=1+\eta^2h+\eta^4g\) and \(Lf_\eta=-2\eta^2h-4\eta^4g\). Orthogonality and \(h\ne0\) make the first term strictly positive for \(\eta>0\). The identity (15) makes entropy even in \(\eta\), smooth at zero, and with derivative zero there. This extends concavity to \([-1,1]\), proving Theorem A. Positive contraction of \(K_{-1}\) follows by conjugating \(K_1\) by \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)\).

The physical update is a jump on entire occupation configurations with rates (9). These rates depend on the current configuration and connect changes across both coherent blocks. The block-rank-two interaction is present in the determinantal line itself.

## 5. Exact example and a sharp phase obstruction

Take
\[
c=\frac1{20},\qquad d=\frac1{16},\qquad
a=\frac1{10},\qquad b=\frac1{40}.
\tag{19}
\]
The largest off-diagonal absolute row sum for \(|\eta|\le1\) is \(13/80\). Hence all eigenvalues lie in \([27/80,53/80]\). The exact mode data are
\[
V=\frac{234976}{146235375},\qquad
\|h\|_\infty=\frac{272}{6237},\qquad
\Theta=\frac{379457000}{320588037}<\frac65,
\tag{20}
\]
with strict gap \(26243222/1602940185\). In the explicit sixteen-state matrices,
\[
\min_{x\ne y}L_{xy}=\frac{130859537}{1315865600}>0,
\qquad
\min_{x\ne y}M_{xy}=\frac{13121611}{1315865600}>0.
\]
Thus the generator and its curvature generator are both genuinely irreducible. Multiplying \(a,b\) simultaneously by any \(r\in(0,4]\) leaves \(\Theta\) and the entire generator \(L\) unchanged. At \(r=4\), Gershgorin still gives the strict interval \([3/80,77/80]\). This supplies a family extending to sizeable off-block coherences.

There is also an exact obstruction explaining the quarter-turn phase. Replace \(ib\) in (1) by \(be^{i\phi}\), and its conjugate accordingly. The law still has only degrees \(0,2,4\), but its degree-two density becomes
\[
h_\phi=h+\frac{\zeta zw}{q(z,w)},\qquad
\zeta=-32cdab\cos\phi,
\]
while \(g\) is unchanged. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\langle h_\phi,g\rangle_\mu
=\frac{\zeta\gamma\delta}{(1-\alpha^2)(1-\beta^2)}.
}
\tag{21}
\]
If a fixed \(\mu\)-reversible generator realizes this curve under \(\eta=e^{-\tau}\), comparison of polynomial coefficients forces
\[
Lh_\phi=-2h_\phi,\qquad Lg=-4g.
\]
Self-adjointness forces their inner product to vanish. Since all four parameters are positive, this is possible only when \(\cos\phi=0\). Conversely, under (2), the construction above supplies the reversible generator at exactly those phases. Thus, within this parameter region and whenever the kernels are valid, **a time-homogeneous reversible realization exists if and only if the cycle phase has zero real part**. This obstruction does not exclude time-dependent reversible generators or nonreversible ones.

For (19) with real phase \(\phi=0\), the exact nonzero inner product is \(-128/5118238125\). The phase obstruction is independent of any entropy-sign claim and is not a DPP counterexample.
