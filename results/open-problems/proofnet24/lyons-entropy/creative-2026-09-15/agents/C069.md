## 1. Physical theorem in every dimension

Let \(u,v\in\mathbb C^n\) be fixed orthonormal vectors satisfying the point-coordinate condition
\[
|u_i|^2=|v_i|^2=:m_i\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{(p,q)\longmapsto H(P_{p uu^*+q vv^*})
\quad\text{is concave on }[0,1]^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every two kernels in this square commute, and their entire affine segment satisfies Lyons's entropy inequality in the fixed occupation basis. Both eigenvalues may change independently. The result is not restricted to rank-one directions, proportional endpoints, or a zero/scalar endpoint.

The complementary family
\[
(p,q)\longmapsto I-p uu^*-q vv^*
\tag{3}
\]
has the same conclusion by occupation complementation. Direct sums with independent coordinate blocks inherit concavity by entropy additivity.

The hypothesis is broad: if \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\) are any real orthonormal pair, then
\[
u=\frac{x+iy}{\sqrt2},\qquad v=\frac{x-iy}{\sqrt2}
\tag{4}
\]
satisfies (1). Consequently **every real rank-two subspace** gives a physical two-parameter family, generally with nonuniform occupation marginals and complex kernels. Explicitly,
\[
p uu^*+q vv^*
=\frac{p+q}{2}(xx^T+yy^T)
+\frac{i(p-q)}2(yx^T-xy^T).
\tag{5}
\]
This uses fixed point coordinates; it makes no arbitrary-unitary invariance assertion for occupation entropy.

## 2. Exact projection-mixture reduction

Let
\[
r_{ij}=|u_iv_j-u_jv_i|^2\quad(i<j)
\tag{6}
\]
be the two-particle projection DPP law. Cauchy--Binet gives \(\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}=1\). If \(N\) is the number of occupied eigenmodes, then
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Bern}(p)+\operatorname{Bern}(q),
\qquad
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=((1-p)(1-q),p(1-q)+q(1-p),pq).
\tag{7}
\]
The empty projection is deterministic. The one-particle projection law is the same \(m\) whichever of \(u,v\) was selected, by (1). The two-particle law is \(r\). Therefore every atom is exactly
\[
P(\varnothing)=f_0,\qquad
P(\{i\})=f_1m_i,\qquad
P(\{i,j\})=f_2r_{ij},
\tag{8}
\]
with no atoms of larger size. The count is observable from the occupation set, so the full occupation entropy, including the projection-mixture term, is
\[
\boxed{H(P_K)=H(N)+f_1H(m)+f_2H(r).}
\tag{9}
\]
Ordinary count entropy alone is not used to conclude concavity.

## 3. The pair-entropy bound

Set
\[
\beta=2H(m)-H(r).
\]
The crucial entirely elementary bound is
\[
\boxed{\log2\le\beta\le2\log2.}
\tag{10}
\]

To prove it, sample an unordered pair with law \(r\), and independently order its two elements fairly. Its ordered law is
\[
Q_{ij}=\frac{r_{ij}}2\quad(i\ne j),\qquad Q_{ii}=0.
\]
Here \(r_{ji}=r_{ij}\) when convenient. Orthonormality gives
\[
\sum_{j\ne i}r_{ij}=|u_i|^2+|v_i|^2=2m_i,
\tag{11}
\]
so both marginals of \(Q\) are \(m\). Also
\[
r_{ij}\le(|u_i||v_j|+|u_j||v_i|)^2=4m_im_j.
\tag{12}
\]
On its support, \(Q_{ij}/(m_im_j)\le2\). It follows that
\[
0\le D(Q\Vert m\otimes m)\le\log2.
\tag{13}
\]
The lower bound is Gibbs's inequality; the upper bound follows by averaging the pointwise logarithmic bound. Coordinates with \(m_i=0\) carry no mass and can be omitted.

Since \(H(Q)=H(r)+\log2\) and both marginals are \(m\),
\[
D(Q\Vert m\otimes m)=2H(m)-H(r)-\log2=\beta-\log2.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) prove (10). The upper endpoint is attained for the two-site balanced pair, so that endpoint cannot be reduced universally.

## 4. Completion using the weighted two-trial theorem

C075, Section 6, proves that for every \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
E_\theta(p,q)
=H(N)+\theta\,\mathbb E\log {2\choose N}
=H(N)+\theta f_1\log2
\tag{15}
\]
is jointly concave. This is the convex-combination consequence of ordinary count-entropy concavity and C075's uniform-shuffle entropy theorem, with the correction controlled in that proof.

Because \(f_1+2f_2=p+q\), equation (9) becomes
\[
H(P_K)
=H(N)+\frac\beta2 f_1+\frac{H(r)}2(p+q)
=E_\theta(p,q)+\frac{H(r)}2(p+q),
\tag{16}
\]
where
\[
\theta=\frac{\beta}{2\log2}\in[1/2,1].
\tag{17}
\]
The second term in (16) is affine, and the first is concave by (15). This proves (2), including the entire closed square by continuity. No sign claim is made for the projection-mixture correction on its own.

A slightly more general useful criterion follows from the same algebra. Suppose a two-mode spectral face has fixed conditional laws \(\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2\) at three distinct consecutive ranks, with the two one-mode middle laws equal to \(\mu_1\). Write \(h_k=H(\mu_k)\). If
\[
0\le2h_1-h_0-h_2\le2\log2,
\tag{18}
\]
then its occupation entropy is concave in both mode parameters: the entropy equals \(E_\theta\) plus an affine function, with \(\theta=(2h_1-h_0-h_2)/(2\log2)\). The proof above establishes (18) for every equal-modulus rank-two face and its complement. **It does not establish (18) for arbitrary intermediate-rank projection backgrounds.**

## 5. A nonuniform three-site shuffle consequence

Let \(w=(w_1,w_2,w_3)\) be any probability vector, and define
\[
m_i=\frac{1-w_i}{2},\qquad B=[\,m\ \ m\ \ w\,].
\tag{19}
\]
Every such doubly stochastic matrix is unistochastic. Indeed, take the real unit vector \(z_i=\sqrt{w_i}\), choose any real orthonormal basis \(x,y\) of \(z^\perp\), and use (4). Then \(U=[u\ v\ z]\) is unitary and has squared-modulus matrix (19). Its rank-two pair law is
\[
r_{ij}=w_k\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).
\tag{20}
\]

The root's fixed-shuffle representation therefore has concave full occupation entropy on both complete spectral faces
\[
(p_1,p_2,p_3)=(p,q,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(p,q,1)
\tag{21}
\]
for every \(B\) in (19), including arbitrary nonuniform \(w\). Formula (16) has
\[
\theta(w)=\frac{2H((1-w)/2)-H(w)}{2\log2}.
\]
This is a two-dimensional nonuniform physical result. The three-dimensional cube with an arbitrary third parameter remains open here.

For an exact fully nonuniform example, take
\[
x=\frac1{7}(6,-2,-3)^T,
\qquad y=\frac1{7}(-2,3,-6)^T,
\qquad z=\frac1{7}(-3,-6,-2)^T.
\tag{22}
\]
These vectors are orthonormal. The construction gives
\[
m=\frac1{98}(40,13,45),\qquad
w=\frac1{49}(9,36,4),
\tag{23}
\]
and the exact unordered-pair masses are \(r_{12}=4/49\), \(r_{13}=36/49\), \(r_{23}=9/49\). The coefficient is
\[
\beta\approx1.23990136481630658,
\qquad\theta\approx0.89439977510597148.
\]
All coordinates of \(m\) and \(w\) are positive and their entries are not equal.

## 6. Exact obstruction to unrestricted fixed shuffles in four sites

The arbitrary-fixed-shuffle extension of C075 is false in dimension four. This is **not a determinantal counterexample**, and it does not refute the three-site shuffle conjecture.

Let the random permutation be uniform on the Klein four group
\[
G=\{\mathrm{id},(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Independently generate Bernoulli bits with parameter vector
\[
p(t)=\left(\frac34+t,\frac34-t,\frac14+t,\frac14-t\right),
\tag{25}
\]
and apply the random permutation. Denote the output occupation entropy by \(\mathcal H(t)\). All atoms are positive near zero and every atom is an even polynomial in \(t\). Hence the atom Fisher term at zero is zero.

The exact atom types at zero and their second derivatives are:

| Atoms, written as bit strings | Multiplicity | Mass at zero | Second derivative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0000, 0110, 1001, 1111 | 4 | \(9/256\) | \(-5/4\) |
| All one-bit and all three-bit strings | 8 | \(15/256\) | \(1/4\) |
| 0011, 1100 | 2 | \(41/256\) | \(-5/4\) |
| 0101, 1010 | 2 | \(9/256\) | \(11/4\) |

Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H''(0)
&=-\frac12\log\frac9{256}
-2\log\frac{15}{256}
+\frac52\log\frac{41}{256}\\
&=\boxed{\frac12\log\frac{41^5}{9\cdot15^4}}
>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The sign is exact since \(41^5>9\cdot15^4\). Numerically it is \(2.769217475888239686\ldots\).

There is also an explicit rational finite Jensen violation. Take
\[
p_-=\frac18(5,7,1,3),\quad
p_0=\frac14(3,3,1,1),\quad
p_+=\frac18(7,5,3,1),\qquad p_0=\frac{p_-+p_+}2.
\tag{27}
\]
The shuffled output laws at \(p_-\) and \(p_+\) agree. Their four atom types, in the table's order, are
\[
\frac{105}{4096},\quad\frac{247}{4096},
\quad\frac{617}{4096},\quad\frac{233}{4096}.
\tag{28}
\]
Exact rational logarithm enclosures prove
\[
0.00412631436548458994
<\frac{\mathcal H(p_-)+\mathcal H(p_+)}2-\mathcal H(p_0)
<0.00412631436548458995.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus the requested concavity inequality has the wrong sign for this shuffle.

For comparison, the count entropy's curvature at the same point is strictly negative, approximately \(-5.080969993336485293\). The positive full-entropy curvature is entirely compatible with ordinary Shepp--Olkin count concavity; the conditional occupation entropy matters.

### Physical exclusion, twice

First, at \(p_0\), all four output marginals equal \(1/2\), whereas
\[
\mathbb P(X_1=X_2=1)=\frac5{16},\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X_1,X_2)=\frac1{16}>0.
\tag{30}
\]
A Hermitian DPP has covariance \(-|K_{12}|^2\le0\). The particular output law is therefore not determinantal.

Second, the *whole fixed shuffle channel* cannot be the spectral projection channel of a four-site unitary. Uniform Klein-group shuffling sends every singleton uniformly to all four sites, which would force \(|U_{ij}|^2=1/4\). But it sends an input pair \(\{1,2\}\) to \(\{1,2\}\) and \(\{3,4\}\) with probability \(1/2\) each. Every two-by-two minor of a unitary with flat entry moduli obeys
\[
|\det U_{T,S}|^2\le(1/4+1/4)^2=1/4,
\tag{31}
\]
contradicting the required probability \(1/2\). The one-site matrix is itself uniform and unistochastic; the incompatible exterior-minor data are exactly what the root's three-site representation warns about in higher dimension.
