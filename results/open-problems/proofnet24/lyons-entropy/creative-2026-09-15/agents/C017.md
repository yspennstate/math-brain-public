# C017 — full Hermitian Hessian at every three-site exchangeable kernel

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a restricted theorem, not a proof of the full Lyons conjecture. Historical priority is not assessed. No subagents, external writes, pushes, or email were used.

## 1. Completed theorem and scope

Let \(P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/3\), and put
\[
K=a(I-P)+cP,\qquad 0<a,c<1.
\tag{1}
\]
Then the entropy of the three-site occupation DPP has a nonpositive Hessian in **every complex Hermitian direction**. If \(a\ne c\), the Hessian is negative definite. If \(a=c\), the kernel is a product kernel and
\[
D^2H(aI)[A,A]=-{1\over a(1-a)}\sum_i A_{ii}^2;
\tag{2}
\]
its nullspace consists of the zero-diagonal Hermitian directions.

There is no restriction on how close either eigenvalue in (1) is to 0 or 1. On the closed square \(0\le a,c\le1\), entropy is concave as a function of the two affine kernel parameters \((a,c)\). A boundary Hessian is not asserted to exist. Every strict member with \(a\ne c\) also has an ambient neighborhood of strict full-Hermitian concavity, by continuity of the interior Hessian.

Diagonal unitary conjugation preserves the occupation law. Thus \(P\) in (1) can be replaced by \(vv^*\) for any unit vector with \(|v_1|=|v_2|=|v_3|=1/\sqrt3\), allowing arbitrary phases.

For the assignment's positive rank-one class, (1) covers \(D=aI\) and \(|u_1|=|u_2|=|u_3|\), at every admissible scaling. Arbitrary diagonal backgrounds or unequal coordinate magnitudes are **not** proved here. The proof is stronger than the half-eigenvalue subfamily developed independently by C016: both eigenvalues \(a,c\) are free. The essential new compensation is a two-statistic Fisher bound using the count and the empty-set event.

Two auxiliary results are given below:

1. In every dimension, full-Hermitian concavity at an exchangeable kernel is exactly equivalent to its two-parameter invariant Hessian. All remaining directions have strictly negative frozen curvature when the eigenvalues differ.
2. The three-occupation-indicator sufficient test can fail at a rational positive-diagonal-plus-rank-one three-site kernel. This is certified by rational logarithm enclosures, and explains why an extra Fisher statistic is material.

## 2. An all-dimensional symmetry reduction

Let now \(n\ge2\), \(P=\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/n\), and
\[
K=a(I-P)+cP,\qquad 0<a<c<1.
\]
Write
\[
x={a\over1-a},\qquad
r={c/(1-c)\over a/(1-a)}>1.
\]
The odds matrix is
\[
L=x\{I+(r-1)P\},
\]
so its principal determinants are
\[
\det L_S=x^{|S|}\left(1+{r-1\over n}|S|\right).
\tag{3}
\]
The constant and modular parts of the frozen atom logarithm have affine determinantal expectations. Its nonzero frozen curvature therefore comes from
\[
f(S)=-\log(1+w|S|),\qquad w=(r-1)/n>0.
\]
The scalar Laplace identity proved in C005 gives, with
\[
b_t=1-e^{-tw},\quad Q_t=I-b_tK>0,\quad R_t=b_tQ_t^{-1}>0,
\]
the full quadratic identity
\[
D^2F_f(K)[A,A]
=\int_0^\infty {e^{-t}\over t}\det Q_t
\left\{(\operatorname{tr}R_tA)^2-
\operatorname{tr}(R_tAR_tA)\right\}\,dt.
\tag{4}
\]
This follows directly by differentiating \(\det(I-b_tK)\) twice. It uses a finite set of atom identities; the integral converges as in C005. Every \(R_t\) belongs to the real span of \(I,P\).

Average an arbitrary Hermitian \(A\) over coordinate permutations, and decompose it as
\[
A=A_0+A_\perp,\qquad
A_0=h(I-P)+kP.
\tag{5}
\]
The average of \(A_\perp\) is zero, equivalently
\[
\operatorname{tr}A_\perp=0,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(PA_\perp)=0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{tr}(R_tA_\perp)=0\) for every \(t\). Formula (4) implies
\[
D^2F_f(K)[A_\perp,A_\perp]
=-\int_0^\infty {e^{-t}\over t}\det Q_t
\|R_t^{1/2}A_\perp R_t^{1/2}\|_F^2\,dt<0
\tag{7}
\]
when \(A_\perp\ne0\).

The entropy Hessian is permutation invariant at this base. Averaging in either slot proves that its mixed term between \(A_0\) and \(A_\perp\) is zero. Consequently
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]
=D^2H(K)[A_0,A_0]+D^2H(K)[A_\perp,A_\perp].
\tag{8}
\]
Since \(D^2H=-\mathrm{FI}+D^2F_f\), the second term is strictly negative for nonzero \(A_\perp\).

This proves the announced all-dimensional equivalence: only the \((a,c)\) Hessian can obstruct full concavity at an exchangeable kernel. The reduction includes imaginary Hermitian directions; it does not assume that \(A\) is real. When \(c<a\), apply the same result to \(I-K\), whose two eigenvalues have the reverse order. The product case is separate.

The next sections solve the remaining two-dimensional inequality completely when \(n=3\).

## 3. Three-site atoms and frozen invariant curvature

Assume \(0<a<c<1\). Retain \(x,r\) above, so
\[
a={x\over1+x},\qquad c={xr\over1+xr},\qquad x>0,\ r>1.
\]
Every atom of size \(j\) has the same mass \(p_j\). With
\(Z=(1+x)^2(1+xr)\), these masses are
\[
p_0={1\over Z},\quad
p_1={x(r+2)\over3Z},\quad
p_2={x^2(2r+1)\over3Z},\quad
p_3={x^3r\over Z}.
\tag{9}
\]
Define the two positive discrete log curvatures
\[
U=\log{(r+2)^2\over3(2r+1)},\qquad
V=\log{(2r+1)^2\over3r(r+2)}.
\tag{10}
\]
They are \(\log(p_1^2/(p_0p_2))\) and
\(\log(p_2^2/(p_1p_3))\), respectively.

For the invariant direction \(A_0=h(I-P)+kP\), put
\[
m=(1-c)U+cV={U+xrV\over1+xr},\qquad
t=(1-a)U+aV={U+xV\over1+x}.
\tag{11}
\]
Then the exact frozen curvature is
\[
D^2F_f(K)[A_0,A_0]=2mh^2+4thk.
\tag{12}
\]

One direct derivation uses the three-site identity from old-097:
\(D^2F_f=2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)\).
Here every pair coefficient is \(U\), the triple coefficient is \(V-U\), and
\(M=UI+(V-U)K\). Its eigenvalue on \(P\) is \(m\), and its two eigenvalues on \(I-P\) are \(t\). Since \(\operatorname{adj}A_0\) has eigenvalues \(h^2\) on \(P\) and \(hk\) on \(I-P\), (12) follows. Alternatively, differentiate the count law \(\operatorname{Bin}(2,a)+\operatorname{Bern}(c)\) twice.

## 4. Fisher compensation from the count and the empty event

Let \(N=|X|\), \(E=\mathbf1_{X=\varnothing}\), and put
\[
\mu=\mathbb EN=2a+c,\qquad
v=\operatorname{Var}N=2a(1-a)+c(1-c),\qquad
p=\mathbb EE=(1-a)^2(1-c).
\]
Their covariance matrix is
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
v&-p\mu\\-p\mu&p(1-p)
\end{pmatrix}>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, full support on counts \(0,1,2,3\) precludes an affine relation between \(N\) and \(E\).

For any mean-zero score \(s\), its squared norm is at least the squared norm of its projection onto the centered pair \((N,E)\). The derivatives in direction \((h,k)\) are
\[
D\mu=2h+k,\qquad
Dp=-p\left({2h\over1-a}+{k\over1-c}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
\mathrm{FI}_K(A_0)\ge
\begin{pmatrix}h&k\end{pmatrix}F\binom hk,
\qquad
F=J^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}J,
\quad
J=\begin{pmatrix}
2&1\\-2p/(1-a)&-p/(1-c)
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is a Fisher lower bound, not an equality claim. It retains an event that becomes particularly informative near the spectral boundary \(c=1\).

Define
\[
G=F-\begin{pmatrix}2m&2t\\2t&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (12)--(14) give
\[
D^2H(K)[A_0,A_0]\le-\begin{pmatrix}h&k\end{pmatrix}G\binom hk.
\tag{16}
\]
We will prove \(G>0\) throughout \(x>0,r>1\).

## 5. Exact determinant and its positive coefficients

Set
\[
Q=2r^2x^2+4r^2x+2r^2+rx^2+8rx+5r+2>0.
\]
Elementary inversion of (13) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{11}&={4(x+1)^2\{r^3x^3+2r^3x^2+r^3x+3r^2x^2+2r^2x+4rx+2r+1\}
\over x(rx+1)Q},\\
F_{12}&={2(x+1)^2(rx+1)(rx+2r+1)\over xQ},\\
F_{22}&={(rx+1)^2(2r+x^2+4x+1)\over xQ}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
In particular \(G_{22}=F_{22}>0\). Its determinant is
\[
\det G={\sum_{j=0}^6 q_j x^j\over x(x+1)^2Q}.
\tag{18}
\]
For \(d=r-1\), the seven coefficients are as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0/2={}&(6r+3)U+2d^2,\\
q_1/2={}&-(4r^2+10r+4)U^2+(6r^2+27r+6)U\\
&+(-2r^2+7r+4)V+2d^2(r+4),\\
q_2/2={}&-(8r^2+16r)U^2-(8r^2+20r+8)UV\\
&+(24r^2+40r+2)U+(-2r^3+3r^2+26r+12)V
+4d^2(2r+3),\\
q_3/4={}&-(2r^2+r)U^2-(8r^2+16r)UV+(17r^2+11r-1)U\\
&-(2r^2+5r+2)V^2+(-2r^3+10r^2+19r+6)V
+2d^2(3r+2),\\
q_4/2={}&-(8r^2+4r)UV+(20r^2+2r-1)U
-(8r^2+16r)V^2\\
&+(-2r^3+26r^2+26r+4)V+2d^2(4r+1),\\
q_5/(2r)={}&(4r-1)U-(4r+2)V^2+(14r+7)V+2d^2,\\
q_6={}&6Vr^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
These are finite polynomial identities in the formal variables \(x,r,U,V\). The verifier checks (17)--(19) exactly with rational symbolic algebra, treating the logarithms as independent formal variables.

The following three scalar bounds are enough:
\[
U\ge0,\qquad U^2\le{d^2\over6r},\qquad
0\le V\le{d^2\over3r(r+2)}\le{1\over3}.
\tag{20}
\]
For the \(V\) bound, apply \(\log y\le y-1\) to its ratio in (10). For \(U\), the inequality
\(\log y\le(y-1)/\sqrt y\) for \(y\ge1\) follows from
\(2\sinh(z/2)\ge z\) with \(z=\log y\). Consequently
\[
U^2\le {d^4\over3(2r+1)(r+2)^2}\le {d^2\over6r}.
\]
The last comparison is exactly
\[
(2r+1)(r+2)^2-2r(r-1)^2=13r^2+10r+4>0.
\]

Here is a coefficient-by-coefficient proof of positivity, retaining explicit slack:

* \(q_0/2\ge2d^2\).
* In \(q_1/2\), drop the positive \(U\) term and the positive part \((7r+4)V\). The remaining two negative terms are at most
  \[
  d^2\left({2r\over3}+{5\over3}+{2\over3r}
  +{2r\over3(r+2)}\right)
  \le d^2\left({2r\over3}+3\right).
  \]
  Hence \(q_1/2\ge d^2(4r/3+5)\).
* In \(q_2/2\), absorb its negative \(UV\) term into its positive \(U\) term using \(V\le1/3\). The remaining \(U\) coefficient is
  \((64r^2+100r-2)/3>0\), so it can be dropped. Also drop the positive part of the \(V\) coefficient. Bound the two surviving negative terms by
  \(d^2\{4(r+2)/3+2r/3\}\). It follows that
  \(q_2/2\ge d^2(6r+28/3)\).
* In \(q_3/4\), the same absorption leaves the positive \(U\) coefficient
  \((43r^2+17r-3)/3\). Drop it and the positive \(V\) terms. The three remaining negative terms are bounded by
  \[
  d^2\left({2r+1\over6}+{2r+1\over9r}+{2r\over3}\right)
  \le d^2(r+1/2).
  \]
  Here \(V^2\le V/3\) was used. Thus
  \(q_3/4\ge d^2(5r+7/2)\).
* In \(q_4/2\), absorption leaves the positive \(U\) coefficient
  \((52r^2+2r-3)/3\). After dropping positive terms, the two negative terms are bounded by
  \(d^2(8/9+2r/3)\). Hence
  \(q_4/2\ge d^2(22r/3+10/9)\).
* In \(q_5/(2r)\), the \(U\) term is nonnegative, and
  \((14r+7)V-(4r+2)V^2\ge0\), using \(V\le1/3\). Thus
  \(q_5/(2r)\ge2d^2\).
* Finally \(q_6=6Vr^2>0\) when \(r>1\).

All coefficients in (18) are therefore strictly positive for \(r>1\). Since its denominator is positive, \(\det G>0\). Together with \(G_{22}>0\), this proves \(G>0\).

Combining (16) with the symmetry reduction proves strict negativity of the full Hermitian Hessian for \(0<a<c<1\). Complementation, \(H(I-K)=H(K)\), gives \(c<a\). At \(a=c\), direct differentiation of the product law gives (2).

To include boundary endpoints of an exchangeable segment, replace every point \(K\) by \((1-2\epsilon)K+\epsilon I\), apply the interior Hessian result along the segment, and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). Finite-law entropy is continuous. This completes the theorem.

## 6. Exact score form for an arbitrary positive diagonal background

The following identity is also useful for the original, more general target. Let
\[
K=D+uu^*,\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i),\quad
0<d_i<1,\quad 0<K<I.
\]
Let \(Q\) be the product Bernoulli law with means \(d_i\), and set
\[
y_i={X_i-d_i\over d_i(1-d_i)},\quad r_i=|u_i|^2,
\quad z=1+\sum_i r_i y_i>0.
\]
The determinant lemma gives \(p_K(X)=Q(X)z(X)\). In a Hermitian direction \(A\), define
\[
N_A=\sum_i A_{ii}y_i+
\sum_{i<j}\{r_jA_{ii}+r_iA_{jj}
-2\operatorname{Re}(\overline u_i A_{ij}u_j)\}y_iy_j.
\tag{21}
\]
Then the full Fisher information, in every dimension, is exactly
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{FI}_K(A)=\mathbb E_Q{N_A^2\over z}.}
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed, Sherman--Morrison gives the score numerator
\(z\sum_i A_{ii}y_i-u^*\operatorname{diag}(y)A\operatorname{diag}(y)u\).
The diagonal quadratic terms cancel, giving (21).

The second atom derivative has the equally finite form
\[
{D^2p_K(X)[A,A]\over Q(X)}=R_A(X),
\]
\[
R_A=2\sum_{i<j}\det A_{ij}\,y_iy_j
+2\sum_{i<j<k}u_{ijk}^*\operatorname{adj}(A_{ijk})u_{ijk}\,y_iy_jy_k.
\tag{23}
\]
This follows by expanding the determinant around the diagonal background: a rank-one update can contribute to the second derivative only through principal sets of sizes two or three. Normalization and the affine occupation means annihilate the constant and modular part of \(\log Q\), so
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-mathbb E_Q{N_A^2\over z}
-\mathbb E_Q[R_A\log z].}
\tag{24}
\]
The formula does not discard any Fisher modes and allows arbitrary complex Hermitian \(A\). For unequal \(d_i\) and unequal \(|u_i|\), a general sign proof of (24) is still missing. When some diagonal background entries are negative, \(Q\) is no longer a probability law, so this particular positive-product representation does not apply as stated.

## 7. A certified obstruction to the occupation-only Fisher shortcut

The three-indicator sufficient criterion from old-097 is
\[
\operatorname{diag}(M)^{\mathsf T}
\left(M\circ\overline M+\det(M)\Sigma\right)^{-1}
\operatorname{diag}(M)\ge2,
\tag{25}
\]
where \(M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12})+c_{123}K\) is its frozen matrix and
\(\Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(X)\). This is sufficient, not necessary, for entropy concavity.

It can fail within the positive-diagonal-plus-rank-one class. Take the entirely rational data
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}\left({18\over25},
{12934613807\over30843413807},{1\over1600}\right),
\quad u=\left({9\over20},{2\over5},{7\over200}\right)^{\mathsf T},
\quad K=D+uu^*.
\tag{26}
\]
Every \(d_i\) belongs to \((0,1)\), and exact rational arithmetic gives
\[
1-\sum_i {|u_i|^2\over1-d_i}={1\over10^7}>0.
\]
Thus \(0<K<I\). The rational-enclosure verifier proves that the left side of (25) is strictly between
\[
\boxed{{1947\over1000}\quad\text{and}\quad{1948\over1000},}
\tag{27}
\]
and hence is below 2. The logarithms are enclosed by an explicit positive atanh series with a rational tail bound, followed by outward-rounded rational interval arithmetic. This is an exact certificate for failure of this proposed sufficient test. It is **not** an entropy counterexample; no positive entropy curvature is claimed at (26).

For an exact all-dimensional remaining target, let
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,a)+\operatorname{Bern}(c),\qquad
p_j={\Pr(N=j)\over\binom nj}.
\]
Let \(R_n\) be the two-by-two frozen Hessian of
\(-\sum_j\Pr(N=j)\log p_j\), with the logarithms frozen, and let
\[
C_n=\begin{pmatrix}v_n&-p_0\mu_n\\-p_0\mu_n&p_0(1-p_0)\end{pmatrix},
\quad
J_n=\begin{pmatrix}n-1&1\\-(n-1)p_0/(1-a)&-p_0/(1-c)\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(\mu_n=(n-1)a+c\) and
\(v_n=(n-1)a(1-a)+c(1-c)\). The natural sufficient extension is
\[
J_n^{\mathsf T}C_n^{-1}J_n-R_n\ge0.
\tag{28}
\]
The symmetry reduction proves that (28) would establish the full-Hermitian theorem at every exchangeable kernel in that dimension. Sections 3--5 prove it for \(n=3\). No proof of (28) for all \(n\), and no full-Hessian proof for arbitrary diagonal-plus-rank-one three-site kernels, is supplied here.
