# C015 — finite transport under positive diagonal odds scaling

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **bounded proof development complete**.

This report proves a quantitative transport theorem for the full three-site
entropy Hessian under positive diagonal scaling of the odds matrix. It does
not prove unrestricted preservation under arbitrarily large scaling.

The main consequences are:

1. An explicit finite matrix test transports any established strict Hessian
   margin to prescribed new diagonal scales. The test uses matrices of size
   three and does not recompute the seven-feature inverse.
2. Every normalized shape has an explicit nonuniform scaling box that
   preserves a controlled fraction of a supplied margin.
3. When the three normalized edge magnitudes are equal, a sharper theorem
   depends only on the product of the three scaling ratios. In particular,
   C010's difficult rational kernel admits independent odds factors in
   \([30/31,31/30]\), with a new explicit full-Hessian margin.
4. C001's full-dimensional near-boundary region is enlarged explicitly in
   all three diagonal scales. C010's shape-dependent weighted simplex also
   receives an explicit finite enlargement.

These results concern finite scaling around already certified kernels,
including a high-odds example and a region approaching a singular normalized
shape. They are not another small-density theorem.

Sources read: `TARGET.md`, `agents/C001.md`, `agents/C010.md`, the relevant
three-site identities in `sources/old-097.md`, and `agents/C008.md`. The
identity for the entropy Hessian, positivity of the shape matrix, and the
source curvature margins below are prior campaign inputs. No historical
novelty claim is made.

## 1. Notation and the source margin

Let \(L>0\) be Hermitian of size three, let
\[
K=L(I+L)^{-1},\qquad
R_{ij}=L_{ij}/\sqrt{L_{ii}L_{jj}},
\]
and suppose all three off-diagonal entries of \(R\) are nonzero. Write
\[
d_i=-\log(1-|R_{jk}|^2),\quad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\},
\qquad D_0=\operatorname{diag}(d_i),
\]
\[
\tau=\log\frac{\prod_{i<j}(1-|R_{ij}|^2)}{\det R},
\qquad M=D_0+\tau K,\qquad \delta=\det M.
\tag{1}
\]
The prior logarithmic inequalities give \(d_i\ge0\) and \(d_i+\tau\ge0\).
Here \(M>0\). The exact identity is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]= -\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
                  +2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).
\tag{2}
\]

Assume a source theorem has proved, for some \(0<\varepsilon\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\varepsilon\delta
 \|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2\quad(A=A^*).}
\tag{3}
\]
The new results require the quantitative margin, rather than merely the
sign at a single base. Available source values include
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{Source region}&\varepsilon\\ \hline
\text{C001 full-dimensional region, }u=\log(1/t)&1/(25u)\\
\text{C001 longer central curve}&1/(20u)\\
\text{C010 cost radius }\alpha&1/2000\\
\text{C010 cost radius }2/3&7/375\\
\text{C010 cost radius }1/2&1/2\\
\text{C010 rational kernel }K_*&23/100.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

## 2. The exact kernel and Fisher transformations

Give each odds coordinate a positive factor \(a_i\), and set
\[
F=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{a_i}),\quad
\mathsf A=F^2,\quad E_0=\mathsf A-I,\quad
L'=FLF,\quad K'=L'(I+L')^{-1}.
\]
Define
\[
Z=\det(I+E_0K),\qquad
T=F(I+KE_0)^{-1},
\]
\[
w_- =\prod_i\min(1,a_i),\qquad
w_+ =\prod_i\max(1,a_i),\qquad
r=w_-/Z,\quad s=w_+/Z.
\tag{5}
\]
All inverses exist. In particular, \(Z\) is positive. Direct multiplication
gives
\[
\boxed{
K'=FK(I+E_0K)^{-1}F,\qquad
D K'[A]=TAT^*,\qquad
\det(TT^*)=rs.}
\tag{6}
\]
For reference, the acceleration along an affine source line is
\[
D^2K'[A,A]=-2TANAT^*,\qquad
N=E_0(I+KE_0)^{-1}=N^*.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus this kernel change is not affine.

For \(p_S=P_K(X=S)\), put
\(w_S=\prod_{i\in S}a_i\). The odds atom formula gives
\[
p'_S:=P_{K'}(X=S)=\frac{w_Sp_S}{Z},\qquad
Z=\sum_Sw_Sp_S.
\tag{8}
\]
The prime in \(p'_S\) here labels the new base, not a derivative.
For a source tangent \(A\), let
\(u_S=Dp_S[A]/p_S\). Differentiating (8) and using (6) shows
\[
\operatorname{FI}_{K'}(TAT^*)
 =\operatorname{Var}_{p'}(u).
\]
For every real number \(c\), \(p'_S\ge r p_S\); therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}_{p'}(u)
&=\min_c\sum_Sp'_S(u_S-c)^2\\
&\ge r\min_c\sum_Sp_S(u_S-c)^2
=r\operatorname{FI}_K(A).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{FI}_{K'}(TAT^*)\ge r\operatorname{FI}_K(A).}
\tag{9}
\]
This Fisher comparison and (6)--(8) are valid in every dimension. The
subsequent frozen-log argument uses dimension three.

Positive diagonal odds scaling leaves \(R\), \(d_i\), and \(\tau\)
unchanged. Thus the new frozen-log matrix is simply
\[
M'=D_0+\tau K',\qquad\delta'=\det M'.
\tag{10}
\]

## 3. A finite matrix transport theorem

Define the Hermitian mismatch matrix
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E=sM^{-1/2}T^{-1}M'T^{-*}M^{-1/2}-I.}
\tag{11}
\]
Here \(I+\mathcal E>0\). No entropy differentiation at the new base is
needed to calculate this matrix.

If \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) are its eigenvalues, define
\[
\Lambda(\mathcal E)=\max\left\{
\lambda_{\max}\begin{pmatrix}
0&e_3&e_2\\e_3&0&e_1\\e_2&e_1&0
\end{pmatrix},\ -e_1,-e_2,-e_3\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is the exact best constant in
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal E\operatorname{adj}X)
 \le\Lambda(\mathcal E)\|X\|_F^2\quad(X=X^*).
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed, diagonalize \(\mathcal E\). On the three real diagonal entries of
\(X\), the quadratic matrix is the displayed matrix in (12). Each complex
off-diagonal entry contributes \(-2e_i|X_{jk}|^2\), while its contribution
to \(\|X\|_F^2\) is \(2|X_{jk}|^2\). This proves (13) and its optimality.
In particular,
\[
\Lambda(\mathcal E)\le2\|\mathcal E\|_{\rm op}.
\tag{14}
\]

**Theorem 1.** Under (3), if \(\Lambda(\mathcal E)<\varepsilon\), the entropy
Hessian at \(K'\) is negative definite in all Hermitian directions. More
precisely,
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K')[TAT^*,TAT^*]\le
-r\delta\{\varepsilon-\Lambda(\mathcal E)\}
  \|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2.}
\tag{15}
\]

**Proof.** The adjugate congruence identity and (6) give
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M'\operatorname{adj}(TAT^*))
=2rs\operatorname{tr}(T^{-1}M'T^{-*}\operatorname{adj}A).
\]
Combine this with (2), (9), and (11). For
\(X=M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\), the result is
\[
D^2H(K')[TAT^*,TAT^*]
\le rD^2H(K)[A,A]
 +2r\delta\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal E\operatorname{adj}X).
\]
Now apply (3) and (13). Since \(T\) is invertible, every target Hermitian
direction has the form \(TAT^*\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

A simpler sufficient test, involving only two three-by-three positive
semidefinite checks, is
\[
(1-\eta)M\preceq sT^{-1}M'T^{-*}\preceq(1+\eta)M,
\qquad 2\eta<\varepsilon.
\tag{16}
\]
It is weaker than the exact quadratic mismatch test (12).

The standardized target margin can also be read from this calculation:
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm new}=
\frac{\{\varepsilon-\Lambda(\mathcal E)\}
 \lambda_{\min}(I+\mathcal E)^2}{\det(I+\mathcal E)}.}
\tag{17}
\]
Namely,
\[
D^2H(K')[B,B]\le-\varepsilon_{\rm new}\delta'
 \|M'^{-1/2}BM'^{-1/2}\|_F^2.
\tag{18}
\]
To verify the change of normalization, put \(Y=I+\mathcal E\). Equation
(11) implies
\[
M'=s^{-1}TM^{1/2}YM^{1/2}T^*,\qquad
\delta'=r\delta\det(Y)/s^2.
\]
If \(B=TAT^*\), the two normalized directions satisfy
\(|M'^{-1/2}BM'^{-1/2}\|_F
\le s\|X\|_F/\lambda_{\min}(Y)\).
Substitution in (15) proves (17)--(18).

At the identity scaling, \(\mathcal E=0\). Thus this theorem always gives
a nonempty finite neighborhood of the identity for every strict source
margin. It also permits testing larger or strongly nonuniform scales
directly, when the simple boxes below are conservative.

## 4. An explicit nonuniform scaling box for every shape

The following bound removes the matrix test from the description of a
guaranteed small scaling box. Set
\[
(Q,\Delta,\sigma)=
\begin{cases}
(K,D_0,\tau),&\tau\ge0,\\
(I-K,D_0+\tau I,-\tau),&\tau<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
\]
Then \(0<Q<I\), \(\Delta\ge0\), \(\sigma\ge0\), and
\(M=\Delta+\sigma Q\). Let \(c\) be any upper bound on
\[
\|M^{-1/2}Q\Delta^{1/2}\|_{\rm op}.
\tag{20}
\]
For example, a bound depending only on normalized shape is
\[
\boxed{
\widehat c(R)=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{d_{\max}/\tau},&\tau>0,\\
\sqrt{(d_{\max}+\tau)/(-\tau)},&\tau<0,\\
\sqrt{d_{\max}/d_{\min}},&\tau=0.
\end{cases}}
\tag{21}
\]
The last case is well defined under the nonzero-edge hypothesis.
For \(\sigma>0\), the first two bounds follow from
\(QM^{-1}Q\preceq Q/\sigma\preceq I/\sigma\). For \(\sigma=0\), use
\(M=\Delta=D_0\) and \(\|K\|_{\rm op}\le1\).

**Theorem 2.** Define
\[
\boxed{h=\frac{\varepsilon}{4(c+3)^2}.}
\tag{22}
\]
Every independent choice
\[
\boxed{\frac1{1+h}\le a_i\le1+h\quad(i=1,2,3)}
\tag{23}
\]
satisfies the target bound (18) with
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_{\rm new}\ge\varepsilon/8.}
\tag{24}
\]

**Proof.** First suppose \(\tau\ge0\), so \(Q=K\). Write
\(\rho=\max_i\max(a_i,a_i^{-1})\) and \(v=\rho-1\).
For \(\widetilde M=T^{-1}M'T^{-*}\), the identities
\[
T^{-1}Q'T^{-*}=Q+Q(\mathsf A-I)Q,
\]
\[
T^{-1}\Delta^{1/2}
=\Delta^{1/2}\mathsf A^{-1/2}
 +Q\Delta^{1/2}(\mathsf A^{1/2}-\mathsf A^{-1/2})
\tag{25}
\]
give a useful weighted estimate. In the \(M\) metric, the two terms in the
second line have norms at most \(\sqrt\rho\) and \(cv\), respectively.
Here \(\Delta\preceq M\), diagonal commutation is used, and
\(\sqrt\rho-1/\sqrt\rho\le v\).
The change in the first term's square has norm at most \(v\). The change
in the \(\sigma Q\) term has norm at most \(v\), because
\(\sigma\|M^{-1/2}Q\|_{\rm op}^2\le1\), with the assertion immediate if
\(\sigma=0\). Consequently
\[
\|M^{-1/2}(\widetilde M-M)M^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
\le2v+2\sqrt\rho\,cv+c^2v^2.
\tag{26}
\]
Also \(1\le s\le w_+/w_-\le\rho^3\). For \(0\le v\le1/4\), this implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal E\|_{\rm op}
&\le\rho^3-1+\rho^3(2v+2\sqrt\rho\,cv+c^2v^2)\\
&\le v\left(\frac{247}{32}+\frac{1125}{256}c
                         +\frac{125}{256}c^2\right)\\
&\le v(c+3)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
The rational bounds used in the middle line are
\(\rho^3-1\le61v/16\), \(\rho^3\le125/64\), and
\(\sqrt\rho\le9/8\). Each coefficient in the last comparison is strictly
smaller than its counterpart in \((c+3)^2\).

If \(\tau<0\), apply the same argument to the complementary kernel
\(Q=I-K\), whose odds are \(L^{-1}\), and use odds factors \(a_i^{-1}\).
Complementation changes the shape coefficients to
\(d_i+\tau,-\tau\) and leaves \(M\) unchanged. The tangent matrix \(T\),
and the numbers \(r,s,\rho\), are also unchanged. For example,
\[
F^{-1}[I+(I-K)(\mathsf A^{-1}-I)]^{-1}
=F(I+K(\mathsf A-I))^{-1}=T.
\]
Thus (27) applies to the original mismatch as well.

Since \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), (22) gives \(h\le1/36<1/4\). Under (23),
(27) yields \(\|\mathcal E\|_{\rm op}\le\varepsilon/4\), and hence
\(\Lambda(\mathcal E)\le\varepsilon/2\). By (17),
\[
\varepsilon_{\rm new}
\ge\frac\varepsilon2
 \frac{(1-\varepsilon/4)^2}{(1+\varepsilon/4)^3}
\ge\frac{18}{125}\varepsilon>\frac\varepsilon8.
\]
This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

The radius (22) is intentionally explicit rather than optimized. In
particular (11)--(12) can certify scales outside (23).

## 5. A sharper finite theorem for equal normalized edge magnitudes

Assume \(d_1=d_2=d_3=d>0\). This is equivalent to equality of the three
nonzero normalized edge magnitudes. No equality of the odds diagonals is
required, and the cycle phase is arbitrary subject to \(R>0\). Put
\[
P=\prod_{i=1}^3\max(a_i,a_i^{-1})=w_+/w_-.
\tag{28}
\]

**Theorem 3.** In this case
\[
\boxed{0\preceq\mathcal E\preceq(P-1)I.}
\tag{29}
\]
Consequently (3) is transported whenever
\[
\boxed{2(P-1)<\varepsilon,}
\tag{30}
\]
and the target standardized margin is at least
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_{\rm new}\ge
       \frac{\varepsilon-2(P-1)}{P^3}.}
\tag{31}
\]

**Proof of the sandwich.** First consider
\[
J=I-K+K^{1/2}\mathsf A K^{1/2}>0,\qquad\det J=Z.
\]
It is the compression of \(\operatorname{diag}(I,\mathsf A)\) by the
isometry \(x\mapsto((I-K)^{1/2}x,K^{1/2}x)\). The min--max principle
therefore bounds any product of two eigenvalues of \(J\) between \(w_-\)
and \(w_+\): the largest such product is at most the product of the two
largest eigenvalues of \(\operatorname{diag}(I,\mathsf A)\), which is at
most \(w_+\); the lower bound follows from its two smallest eigenvalues.
For each eigenvalue \(j_i\) of \(J\),
\[
1\le\frac{w_+j_i}{\det J}
       =\frac{w_+}{\prod_{k\ne i}j_k}\le P.
\]
Hence
\[
K\preceq s\{K+K(\mathsf A-I)K\}\preceq PK.
\tag{32}
\]
This argument and conclusion actually work in any dimension, replacing
the product of two eigenvalues by the product of all but one.

Apply the same argument to \(I-K\) with \(\mathsf A^{-1}\). Its determinant
is \(Z/\det\mathsf A\), and its corresponding normalization constant is
again \(s\). It follows that
\[
I-K\preceq s\{(I-K)\mathsf A^{-1}(I-K)+K(I-K)\}
       \preceq P(I-K).
\tag{33}
\]
On the other hand direct algebra gives
\[
T^{-1}K'T^{-*}=K+K(\mathsf A-I)K,
\]
\[
T^{-1}(I-K')T^{-*}
=(I-K)\mathsf A^{-1}(I-K)+K(I-K).
\tag{34}
\]
Now
\[
M=d(I-K)+(d+\tau)K,
\qquad M'=d(I-K')+(d+\tau)K',
\]
and both coefficients are nonnegative. Multiply (32)--(33) by these
coefficients and add. This proves
\(M\preceq sT^{-1}M'T^{-*}\preceq PM\), which is exactly (29).
Equations (14), (17), and (29) prove (30)--(31). \(\square\)

The argument uses equality of the three \(d_i\) in its last positive
linear combination. Arbitrary unequal \(d_i\) cannot be inserted into
that step as scalar coefficients; Theorem 2 handles them instead.

## 6. Concrete transported regions

### 6.1 An explicit nonuniform box around C010's difficult rational base

Take the prior certified matrix
\[
L_* =\begin{pmatrix}
100&-499/10&-499/10\\
-499/10&100&-499/10\\
-499/10&-499/10&100
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K_* =\frac1{9054}
\begin{pmatrix}
6499&-2495&-2495\\
-2495&6499&-2495\\
-2495&-2495&6499
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{35}
\]
C010 proves the source margin \(\varepsilon=23/100\). Its normalized
edges all have magnitude \(499/1000\), so Theorem 3 applies. For every
independent choice
\[
\boxed{\frac{30}{31}\le a_i\le\frac{31}{30},\qquad
L'=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{a_i})L_*
          \operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{a_i}),}
\tag{36}
\]
we have
\[
P\le\left(\frac{31}{30}\right)^3=\frac{29791}{27000},
\quad
\frac{23}{100}-2(P-1)\ge\frac{157}{6750},
\]
\[
\frac{157}{6750}\left(\frac{30}{31}\right)^9
=\frac{457812000000}{26439622160671}>\frac1{60}.
\tag{37}
\]
Thus the entire three-parameter scaling box (36) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K')[B,B]\le-\frac{\det M'}{60}
 \|M'^{-1/2}BM'^{-1/2}\|_F^2.}
\tag{38}
\]
The source odds diagonals are 100, and the target ones independently
range from \(3000/31\) to \(310/3\). This is a finite nonuniform
transport near the high-odds example, not a low-density conclusion.
The fixed-shape box itself has three scale parameters; no claim that it
alone is full-dimensional in all nine kernel coordinates is intended.

### 6.2 A strictly larger C001 full-dimensional near-boundary region

Use C001's shape parameters
\[
0<t\le10^{-4},\quad 49/100\le\beta,\gamma\le1/2,
\quad|\cos\phi|\le1/100,
\]
and the normalized matrix
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}
1&\sqrt{1-t}&\gamma\sqrt t\,e^{-i\phi}\\
\sqrt{1-t}&1&\beta\sqrt t\\
\gamma\sqrt t\,e^{i\phi}&\beta\sqrt t&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Put \(u=\log(1/t)\) and
\[
\boxed{h(t)=\frac1{625u^2}.}
\tag{39}
\]
The new enlarged diagonal range is
\[
\boxed{
\frac9{10(1+h(t))}\le\ell_i\le
\frac{11}{10}(1+h(t)),\qquad i=1,2,3.}
\tag{40}
\]
Every corresponding kernel satisfies
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\frac{\det M}{200u}
  \|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2.}
\tag{41}
\]

To prove this, choose source scales in \([9/10,11/10]\) by clipping each
target scale to that interval. The target/source odds factors then lie
in \([(1+h)^{-1},1+h]\). Uniformly over the original C001 region,
\(d_{\max}=u\), \(\tau\ge3/5\), and \(u>8\). Thus
\[
c\le\sqrt{5u/3},\qquad
c+3<\left(\frac43+\frac98\right)\sqrt u
       <\frac52\sqrt u.
\]
With source margin \(1/(25u)\), the guaranteed radius (22) is larger
than \(1/(625u^2)\). Theorem 2 gives (41).

The scale enlargement is small but explicit, independent in the three
coordinates, and strictly positive for every allowed \(t\). It shrinks
only as \(1/\log^2(1/t)\), rather than as a power of the smallest atom
probability. Together with the original shape and gauge parameters,
strict inequalities give a genuinely full-dimensional open region.

For the longer central curve of C001, the same \(h(t)\) works on
\(0<t\le1/100\), now with all scales in
\([(1+h(t))^{-1},1+h(t)]\) and target margin \(1/(160u)\).
Indeed \(\tau\ge2/3\), \(u>4\), and
\(c+3\le(11/4)\sqrt u\), so (22) is at least
\(1/(605u^2)>h(t)\).

### 6.3 Transporting C010's all-shape weighted simplex

For any C010 shape let its costs be
\[
\mathfrak c_i=1+|\tau|/d_i+(d_1d_2d_3)/d_i^2.
\]
Choose a row \((s_0,\varepsilon)\) from
\[
(\alpha,1/2000),\qquad(2/3,7/375),\qquad(1/2,1/2),
\]
and let
\[
h_R=\frac{\varepsilon}{4(3+\widehat c(R))^2}.
\tag{42}
\]
Then the enlarged explicit simplex
\[
\boxed{\sum_i\mathfrak c_i\ell_i\le(1+h_R)s_0}
\tag{43}
\]
has target Hessian margin at least \(\varepsilon/8\). To see this, divide
all target scales by \(1+h_R\) and apply C010 at the source. Theorem 2
then transports by the uniform odds factor \(1+h_R\).

For \(s_0=\alpha\), this strictly enlarges that specified weighted
simplex for each shape. It is not claimed to exceed every point already
certified by C010's separate non-simplex polynomial test; that test has
additional slack. Equations (11)--(12) also provide the more flexible
nonuniform transport from any particular source in these simplices.

## 7. Exact limitations and the remaining obligation

There is no pure affine-coordinate Hessian invariance: the kernel map
has the nonzero acceleration (7), and the Fisher information is
reweighted by (8). Even at an independent diagonal kernel, the natural
tangent congruence scales the curvature of its \(i\)-th diagonal
direction by
\[
\frac{a_i}{[1+(a_i-1)K_{ii}]^2},
\]
which depends on the coordinate. Thus a common scalar congruence law
for the full Hessian cannot simply be assumed. This observation does
not refute sign preservation for the particular determinantal entropy.

The supplied results do give a sharper exact obstruction to transporting
the *three-feature certificate* through all scales. For the normalized
shape \(R_*\) of (35), take equal source scales
\(\ell_0=1/(6\mathfrak c)\), where the three C010 costs equal
\(\mathfrak c\). C010's cost-radius \(1/2\) theorem supplies a trial on
three histories with value at least \(5/2\). Uniform scaling by
\(100/\ell_0=600\mathfrak c\) takes that source to \(L_*\). At the target,
C010's exact obstruction says every choice of at most three histories,
in every reveal order and with arbitrary optimized real coefficients,
has value below \(199/100\). Therefore such sparse certificates are not
preserved by unrestricted positive scalar odds scaling. Both endpoint
entropy Hessians are nonetheless strictly negative. The obstruction
does not contradict Theorems 1--3, which transport the curvature margin
using all available source information.

What remains open here is whether a negative full entropy Hessian at
one scale forces negativity at every positive diagonal scale of the
same normalized shape. Neither the Fisher lower comparison nor the
positive sandwich in Theorem 3 makes the finite mismatch cost vanish.
Iterating the finite theorem loses quantitative margin and does not,
without another argument, cover arbitrary scale factors. In particular,
this report does not turn C010's one certified scale for each shape
into the full three-site theorem, and does not resolve the general
Lyons conjecture.
