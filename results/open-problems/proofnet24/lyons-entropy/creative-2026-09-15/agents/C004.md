# C004 — A positive six-site Fourier diamond coupling and explicit exchange flows

**Status: complete bounded partial result.** This report constructs an exact unlabeled diamond coupling for a fully supported, non-block six-site complex flag, proves a quantitative neighborhood theorem in the full flag space, and gives an additional fully supported real rational seed with its own neighborhood. It also proves that all six-site marginal-preserving signed corrections are generated by explicit four-site triangular exchange flows. **The universal six-site coupling statement and Lyons entropy concavity remain open.**

## 1. The six-site coordinates

Let \(E=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). Let \(Q=(w_1,w_2,u,v,z_1,z_2)\) be a unitary matrix, with
\[
W=\operatorname{span}(w_1,w_2),\quad
U=\operatorname{span}(u,v),\quad
Z=\operatorname{span}(z_1,z_2).
\]
Write
\[
a_A=\mu_W(A),\quad
c_B=\mu_Z(B),\quad
g_M=\mu_{W+[u]}(M)+\mu_{W+[v]}(M).
\]
Here \(A,B\) are pairs and \(M\) is a triple. Complementary minors give
\[
\mu_{W+U}(T)=c_{E\setminus T}.
\]

Index the 90 possible diamonds by **ordered disjoint pairs** \((A,B)\). Their bottom, top, and two middle sets are
\[
A,\quad E\setminus B,\quad A\cup\{i\},\quad A\cup\{j\},
\qquad \{i,j\}=E\setminus(A\cup B).
\]
Thus a coupling is a nonnegative table \(x_{A,B}\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{B\cap A=\varnothing}x_{A,B}=a_A,\qquad
\sum_{A\cap B=\varnothing}x_{A,B}=c_B,
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\substack{A\subset M,\ |A|=2\\
B\subset M^c,\ |B|=2}}x_{A,B}=g_M.
\tag{2}
\]
There are nine terms in (2).

Equivalently let \(D\) be the 50 by 90 signed diamond matrix, with row levels \(2,3,4\), and set
\[
b=(a,-g,\mu_{W+U}).
\]
Then \(Dx=b\). The matrices satisfy the modular balance conditions
\[
\sum_X b_X=0,\qquad \sum_{X\ni i}b_X=0\quad(i\in E).
\tag{3}
\]
The second identity follows from
\(P_W+P_{W+U}=P_{W+[u]}+P_{W+[v]}\).
These conditions will be exactly the compatibility conditions for the signed right inverse below.

## 2. An exact positive Fourier coupling

Put \(\zeta=e^{\pi i/3}\), and let
\[
f_k(j)=6^{-1/2}\zeta^{jk},\qquad 0\le j,k\le5.
\]
Take
\[
W=\operatorname{span}(f_0,f_1),\quad
U=\operatorname{span}(f_2,f_3),\quad
Z=\operatorname{span}(f_4,f_5).
\tag{4}
\]
The six Fourier columns are orthonormal.

The pair laws \(a\) and \(c\) agree. If \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\) is the circular distance between the two sites, then
\[
a_A=c_A=
\begin{cases}
1/36&d=1,\\
1/12&d=2,\\
1/9&d=3.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed their minors differ only by row phases, and
\[
|\det[f_0\ f_1]_{\{i,j\}}|^2
=\frac{|\,\zeta^j-\zeta^i\,|^2}{36}.
\]

For a triple \(M\), let its circular gaps be the three positive integers summing to six. Then
\[
g_M=
\begin{cases}
5/72&\text{gaps }(1,1,4),\\
1/9&\text{gaps }(1,2,3),\\
1/8&\text{gaps }(2,2,2).
\end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
This is an exact determinant identity. For \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) the roots at the selected sites,
\[
\det[1,z,z^2]=\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i),\qquad
\det[1,z,z^3]=\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)(z_1+z_2+z_3).
\]
Consequently
\[
g_M=\frac{\prod_{i<j}|z_i-z_j|^2}{216}
\left(1+|z_1+z_2+z_3|^2\right).
\]
For the three gap types, the two factors before division by 216 are respectively
\((3,5),(12,2),(27,1)\), proving (6).

Define \(x_{A,B}\) by the following eight orbits. The acting group consists of the twelve maps \(j\mapsto \pm j+s\pmod6\), together with interchange of \(A,B\). An orbit's listed numerator is the weight of **each** atom in that orbit.

| Representative \(A\) | Representative \(B\) | Orbit size | \(216x_{A,B}\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 01 | 23 | 12 | 1 |
| 01 | 24 | 24 | 1 |
| 01 | 34 | 6 | 1 |
| 01 | 25 | 12 | 1 |
| 02 | 35 | 6 | 7 |
| 02 | 13 | 12 | 1 |
| 02 | 14 | 12 | 7 |
| 03 | 14 | 6 | 4 |

The eight orbits are disjoint and have total size 90.

**Theorem 1.** These weights form an unlabeled diamond coupling for (4), and every one of the 90 weights is at least \(1/216\).

**Proof.** A bottom pair at distance one sees six weights with numerator one. A bottom pair at distance two sees four numerators one and two numerators seven. A bottom pair at distance three sees two numerators one, two numerators seven, and two numerators four. The row sums are therefore \(6,18,24\), divided by 216, exactly (5). Interchange symmetry proves the top/complement column sums.

For a consecutive triple, the nine terms in (2) have eight numerators one and one numerator seven. For a triple with gaps \((1,2,3)\), they have six numerators one, two numerators seven, and one numerator four. For an alternating triple, they have six numerators one and three numerators seven. The respective sums \(15,24,27\), divided by 216, are (6). This verifies every marginal by symmetry. The total mass is one by summing bottom marginals. All weights are positive. ∎

The lower bound \(1/216\) is the **largest possible common lower bound** on all 90 weights for this instance: any bottom pair of probability \(1/36\) has six incident atoms.

This is not a coordinate-block construction. In particular \(W\) has a strictly positive minor at every pair. More directly, its projection has
\[
(P_W)_{ij}=(1+\zeta^{i-j})/6;
\]
the graph of nonzero off-diagonal entries is connected. It has no nontrivial decomposition over disjoint coordinate blocks.

## 3. An explicit universal signed right inverse

The following linear map is useful for constructing actual couplings near the seed. It is defined for any real table on the three ranks, not only determinantal tables.

For a diamond \((A,B)\), write \(J=E\setminus(A\cup B)\). For a row set \(X\), put
\[
(\alpha,\eta,\beta)=(|X\cap A|,|X\cap J|,|X\cap B|).
\]
Define the 90 by 50 rational matrix \(R\) by the following tables, divided throughout by 12600.

For \(|X|=2\), use the function \(p(\alpha,\eta,\beta)\):

| Counts | Numerator \(p\) |
|---|---:|
| (2,0,0) | 1788 |
| (1,1,0) | −702 |
| (1,0,1) | 378 |
| (0,2,0) | 588 |
| (0,1,1) | −222 |
| (0,0,2) | 228 |

For \(|X|=3\), use \(q(\alpha,\eta,\beta)\):

| Counts | Numerator \(q\) |
|---|---:|
| (2,1,0) | −1222 |
| (2,0,1) | 838 |
| (1,2,0) | 838 |
| (1,1,1) | −357 |
| (1,0,2) | 338 |
| (0,2,1) | 338 |
| (0,1,2) | −122 |

For \(|X|=4\), use \(p(2-\beta,2-\eta,2-\alpha)\).

**Lemma 2.** If \(b\) satisfies (3), then \(DRb=b\). Moreover
\[
\|Rb\|_\infty\le\frac{154}{75}\|b\|_\infty.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** In fact an exact matrix identity holds:
\[
DR=I-\Pi,
\tag{8}
\]
where
\[
\Pi_{X,Y}=
\frac1{50}
+\frac{(|X|-3)(|Y|-3)}{30}
+\frac{|X\cap Y|-|X||Y|/6}{14}.
\tag{9}
\]
Here \(\Pi\) is the orthogonal projection onto the seven-dimensional space of modular tables restricted to the three ranks.

To verify the last assertion, the constant function has squared norm 50, the function \(|X|-3\) has squared norm 30 and is orthogonal to the constant, and the six functions
\[
v_i(X)=\mathbf1_{\{i\in X\}}-|X|/6
\]
have Gram matrix \(14(I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/6)\). They are orthogonal to the first two functions and sum to zero. This gives (9). Condition (3) makes \(\Pi b=0\).

Identity (8) is a finite coefficient identity for the displayed tables. An integer-only version, useful both as a direct verification formula and in the accompanying verifier, is
\[
\sum_{A,B}D_{X,(A,B)}(12600R_{(A,B),Y})
=6\left(2100\mathbf1_{\{X=Y\}}
-42-70(|X|-3)(|Y|-3)-150|X\cap Y|+25|X||Y|\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The verifier enumerates the 50 choices of \(X\), the 50 choices of \(Y\), and the 90 ordered disjoint pairs and checks all 2,500 equalities over integers. Formula (10), with the two small tables above and the stated indexing, is also a complete exact certificate of (8); no numerical inverse is used in its verification.

Each row of \(12600R\) has absolute entry sum 25872, as follows by multiplying each count-type coefficient by
\(\binom2\alpha\binom2\eta\binom2\beta\)
and summing over the three ranks. Thus its row norm is
\(25872/12600=154/75\), proving (7). ∎

The signed formula \(Rb\) is not itself a universal nonnegative coupling. Already at the Fourier seed it has minimum \(-1/288\). The exchange-flow correction below repairs exactly this defect.

## 4. Four-site triangular exchange flows

Fix distinct \(h,e\in E\), and distinct \(a,b,c\in E\setminus\{h,e\}\). Define the signed table
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{h,e;(a,b,c)}
={}&\delta_{ha,eb}+\delta_{hb,ec}+\delta_{hc,ea}\\
&-\delta_{hb,ea}-\delta_{hc,eb}-\delta_{ha,ec}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Pairs in each subscript are sorted. The fourth element of
\(E\setminus\{h,e\}\) is determined. Thus only four coordinates vary; every bottom contains \(h\), and every top excludes \(e\).

**Lemma 3.** \(DF_{h,e;(a,b,c)}=0\).

**Proof.** Bottom row sums and complement-top column sums cancel term by term. A middle set that can occur contains \(h\), excludes \(e\), and chooses two of the four varying sites. Its signed marginal is the net directed flow across the resulting two-versus-two cut of the oriented three-cycle \(a\to b\to c\to a\). A circulation has zero net flow across every cut. This proves the middle cancellation as well. ∎

There are 120 flows when \(h,e\) are ordered and \((a,b,c)\) ranges over increasing triples in their four-element complement.

**Theorem 4.** These flows span the entire real kernel of \(D\). Indeed 47 of them form an integer basis of
\(\ker D\cap\mathbb Z^{90}\).

Here “integer basis” concerns signed vectors. It does not assert that a sequence of individual moves can connect any two nonnegative couplings while every intermediate table stays nonnegative.

**Proof.** First \(\operatorname{rank}D=43\). To see this, suppose a table \(\phi\) annihilates every diamond. Write its values on pairs, triples, quadruples as \(s,t,v\). Summing two diamond equalities inside a fixed four-set gives
\[
s_{ij}+s_{kl}=s_{ik}+s_{jl}=s_{il}+s_{jk}
\]
for every four distinct sites. These four-point identities imply \(s_{ij}=\lambda_i+\lambda_j\). One explicit construction is
\(\lambda_0=(s_{01}+s_{02}-s_{12})/2\), followed by
\(\lambda_j=s_{0j}-\lambda_0\); the four-point identities verify every other pair.

Subtract these coordinate contributions from the triple and quadruple tables. Inside a fixed four-set, the sum of any two distinct remaining triple values is the same. Hence all four remaining triple values agree. The graph of triples joined when their union has size four is connected, so the remaining triple value is a global constant \(t_0\); each remaining quadruple value is \(2t_0\). The original table is therefore the restriction of the modular function
\[
X\longmapsto \sum_{i\in X}(\lambda_i+t_0)-2t_0.
\]
There are precisely seven independent such functions. Thus the rank is \(50-7=43\), and the kernel has dimension 47.

For completeness, the integer basis is certified by a square minor equal to **−1**, not merely by floating-point rank. Order the 90 rows \((A,B)\) lexicographically; order the 120 flows by \(h,e\), then the increasing triple. All indices below are zero-based. Take flow columns
\[
\begin{gathered}
0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10,13,14,20,21,22,24,25,26,28,29,30,33,34,\\
40,41,42,44,45,46,48,49,50,53,54,60,61,62,64,65,66,70,73,74,\\
81,82,85,86,90
\end{gathered}
\]
and the following ordered rows:
\[
\begin{gathered}
6,7,13,0,1,15,3,4,9,8,10,30,31,37,16,18,39,17,19,33,32,34,\\
36,42,55,25,26,57,29,12,45,20,40,43,48,61,22,49,63,46,28,52,\\
50,67,21,69,51.
\end{gathered}
\]
The minor obtained by (11) has determinant \(-1\); exact rational elimination in the verifier checks this. The 47 columns are independent and hence span the kernel. Their selected row minor has integer inverse. Therefore an integer vector in the kernel has integer coordinates in this basis. ∎

### The Fourier correction uses only three averaged flows

Let \(\mathcal S F\) denote the **sum**, without normalization, of the 24 images under the dihedral coordinate group and interchange of \(A,B\). Put
\[
F_0=F_{0,1;(2,3,4)},\quad
F_1=F_{0,1;(2,3,5)},\quad
F_2=F_{0,2;(1,3,5)}.
\]
The Fourier coupling has the constructive form
\[
x^{\rm F}=Rb^{\rm F}
+\frac{-5\mathcal SF_0+5\mathcal SF_1+\mathcal SF_2}{1728}.
\tag{12}
\]
Every correction term preserves every marginal by Lemma 3.

For a short exact verification, the three symmetrized flow values on the eight representatives in the order of Section 2 are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal SF_0&=(0,1,0,-2,-4,2,-2,4),\\
\mathcal SF_1&=(2,-1,0,-2,0,0,2,0),\\
\mathcal SF_2&=(4,0,-4,-4,0,0,0,4).
\end{aligned}
\]
On those representatives, \(864Rb^{\rm F}\) equals
\[
(-3,9,6,6,18,9,18,24).
\]
Substitution in (12) gives
\(864x^{\rm F}=(4,4,4,4,28,4,28,16)\),
exactly the coupling table. Thus the construction combines explicit determinant identities with ordinary local exchange flows.

## 5. A quantitative neighborhood with no symmetry assumption

**Theorem 5.** For any compatible six-site flag whose contrast table satisfies
\[
\|b-b^{\rm F}\|_\infty\le\frac{25}{11088},
\tag{13}
\]
the explicit formula
\[
x=x^{\rm F}+R(b-b^{\rm F})
\tag{14}
\]
is an unlabeled diamond coupling.

**Proof.** Both contrast tables satisfy (3), so Lemma 2 gives \(Dx=b\). Every entry is at least
\[
\frac1{216}-\frac{154}{75}\|b-b^{\rm F}\|_\infty\ge0.
\]
Its bottom marginals sum to one. This proves the assertion. ∎

All 50 atom differences are included in the norm: bottom probabilities, the aggregate middle probabilities, and top probabilities. There is no cyclic, real, or block assumption on the perturbed flag.

For an explicit sufficient condition directly on frames, if \(Q_0,Q\) are unitary and
\(\|Q-Q_0\|_{\rm op}\le\varepsilon\), each \(d\)-column maximal-minor amplitude changes by at most \(d\varepsilon\): telescope the determinant over its columns and use that each restricted column has norm at most one. Squared amplitudes consequently change by at most \(2d\varepsilon\). The two middle laws give
\[
\|b(Q)-b(Q_0)\|_\infty\le12\varepsilon.
\]
Thus the Fourier theorem applies, for example, whenever
\[
\|Q-Q_{\rm F}\|_{\rm op}\le\frac{25}{133056}.
\tag{15}
\]
This proves a neighborhood in the full unitary flag space, including perturbations breaking all seed symmetries. The quantitative radius is a sufficient bound, not an optimal radius.

## 8. The remaining universal obstruction

The linear freedom is completely resolved in six sites: every signed candidate coupling is
\[
x=Rb+F_*t,\qquad t\in\mathbb R^{47},
\]
where \(F_*\) is the explicit integer flow basis in Theorem 4. Therefore the unresolved task is specifically to choose \(t\) so that all 90 entries are nonnegative for **every** compatible determinantal contrast \(b\).

The Pluecker/determinant identities prove the Fourier marginal formulas and the modular compatibility, and the local flows repair the signed inverse at the two certified seeds. This report does not prove a general capacity inequality that makes the same repair possible for every flag. Spanning all signed corrections does not supply such an inequality, and the displayed fixed Fourier correction is not asserted to work outside the proved neighborhood.

Thus the new proved contribution is a constructive positive non-block six-site family with an explicit full-dimensional neighborhood, a corresponding real family, and an exact local exchange-flow basis. Neither universal six-site mixed supermodular positivity nor the full entropy conjecture is claimed.
