## 1. Atom monotonicity and a finite entropy comparison

For a strict Hermitian contraction \(0\prec F\prec I\), let \(p_F(S)\) be its exact occupation-atom probability and put
\[
L_F=F(I-F)^{-1}=(I-F)^{-1}-I.
\tag{1}
\]
Every atom is positive and \(p_F(S)=\det(I-F)\det(L_F)_S\). If \(i\notin S\), the principal Schur complement gives
\[
\frac{p_F(S\cup\{i\})}{p_F(S)}
=(L_F)_{ii}-(L_F)_{iS}(L_F)_S^{-1}(L_F)_{Si}
\le (L_F)_{ii}.
\tag{2}
\]
The empty-set ratio is exactly \((L_F)_{ii}\). Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_F\text{ is decreasing under inclusion}
&\iff (L_F)_{ii}\le1\quad\hbox{for all }i,\\
p_F\text{ is increasing under inclusion}
&\iff ((I-F)F^{-1})_{ii}\le1\quad\hbox{for all }i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The second equivalence applies the first one to the complementary DPP with kernel \(I-F\). Equivalently, the inverse diagonal bounds are \(((I-F)^{-1})_{ii}\le2\) in the first case and \((F^{-1})_{ii}\le2\) in the second. These are statements about measured atoms, not spectral entropy.

For two positive probability laws \(P_M,P_F\), direct expansion gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
H(P_F)-H(P_M)
=D(P_M\Vert P_F)
+\mathbb E_M\log p_F-\mathbb E_F\log p_F.}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, the relative entropy has orientation \(M\Vert F\). Replacing it by the reverse orientation would not give (4).

**Lemma 1 (a target atom law signs its own finite correction).** Suppose \(M,F\) are strict Hermitian contractions.

* If \(M\preceq F\) and \(p_F\) is decreasing under inclusion, the expectation difference in (4) is nonnegative.
* If \(F\preceq M\) and \(p_F\) is increasing under inclusion, the same expectation difference is nonnegative.

Thus \(H(P_F)\ge H(P_M)+D(P_M\Vert P_F)\) in either case. Only the target \(F\) needs the indicated atom monotonicity.

**Proof.** Established DPP stochastic domination says that increasing a positive-contraction kernel in Loewner order increases the expectation of every increasing set function. Apply it to \(-\log p_F\) in the first case and \(\log p_F\) in the second. Equation (4) completes the proof. Equivalently, finite stochastic domination admits a coupling supported on nested sets, and the pointwise signs follow on those nested pairs. The input is Lyons, *Determinantal Probability: Basic Properties and Conjectures*, Theorem 2.9, as recorded in the prior source. \(\square\)

There is a useful strict version. If \((L_F)_{ii}\le r<1\), (2) shows that
\[
S\subseteq T\implies \log p_F(S)-\log p_F(T)
\ge\log(1/r)(|T|-|S|).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the first order cost in Lemma 1 is at least
\(\log(1/r)\operatorname{tr}(F-M)\). In the second case, the complementary odds bound \(((I-F)F^{-1})_{ii}\le r<1\) gives a cost at least
\(\log(1/r)\operatorname{tr}(M-F)\). This follows either from a nested coupling or by applying stochastic domination to the increasing function \(-\log p_F(S)-\log(1/r)|S|\), respectively \(\log p_F(S)-\log(1/r)|S|\).

## 2. The varying-pivot matrix covariance identity

Let two strict endpoint kernels on \(n\ge2\) measured sites be
\[
K_j=\begin{pmatrix}a_j&z_j^*\\z_j&B_j\end{pmatrix},
\qquad j=0,1,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(z_j\in\mathbb C^{n-1}\), and let \(K_t=(1-t)K_0+tK_1\), \(0<t<1\). Set
\[
a=(1-t)a_0+ta_1,\quad b=1-a,\quad
z=(1-t)z_0+tz_1,\quad B=(1-t)B_0+tB_1.
\]
The endpoint conditional kernels and actual target conditional kernels are
\[
G_{j,1}=B_j-\frac{z_jz_j^*}{a_j},\qquad
G_{j,0}=B_j+\frac{z_jz_j^*}{1-a_j},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
F_1=B-\frac{zz^*}{a},\qquad
F_0=B+\frac{zz^*}{b}.
\tag{8}
\]
They are strict contractions by the conditional Schur-complement formulas. The correct branch probabilities for averaging the endpoint conditional kernels are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\omega_{0,1}=(1-t)a_0/a,&\omega_{1,1}=ta_1/a,\\
\omega_{0,0}=(1-t)(1-a_0)/b,&\omega_{1,0}=t(1-a_1)/b.
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]
Define their kernel barycenters
\[
M_i=\omega_{0,i}G_{0,i}+\omega_{1,i}G_{1,i},\qquad i=0,1.
\tag{10}
\]
These are kernel barycenters; their DPP laws need not be the corresponding mixture of laws.

Write
\[
\alpha=a_1-a_0,\qquad w=z_1-z_0,\qquad C=B_1-B_0,
\]
\[
R=ww^*-\alpha C,\qquad V=t(1-t)R.
\tag{11}
\]
Then the exact identities are
\[
\boxed{F_1=M_1+V/a,\qquad F_0=M_0-V/b.}
\tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Multiplying the first difference by \(a\) gives
\[
a(F_1-M_1)
=aB-[(1-t)a_0B_0+ta_1B_1]
+[(1-t)z_0z_0^*+tz_1z_1^*-zz^*].
\]
The first covariance difference is \(-t(1-t)\alpha C\); the outer-product covariance is \(t(1-t)ww^*\). For the absent branch replace \(a_j\) by \(1-a_j\) and change the sign of the outer-product term, which gives \(b(F_0-M_0)=-V\). \(\square\)

In particular \(R\succeq0\) whenever \(\alpha C\preceq0\). This allows \(\alpha>0,C\prec0\), with arbitrary changing orientation in \(z\). It also includes a fixed pivot (\(\alpha=0\)) with an unrestricted changing block \(C\), provided the later atom hypotheses hold. No conditional acceleration or change of branch weight has been discarded.

## 3. Exact nonnegative finite recursion

Abbreviate \(H(P_Q)\) as \(H(Q)\). Let
\[
\Gamma_t=H(K_t)-(1-t)H(K_0)-tH(K_1)
\]
and define
\[
J_h=h(a)-(1-t)h(a_0)-th(a_1),
\tag{13}
\]
\[
J_i=H(M_i)-\omega_{0,i}H(G_{0,i})-\omega_{1,i}H(G_{1,i}),
\quad i=0,1,
\tag{14}
\]
\[
D_i=D(P_{M_i}\Vert P_{F_i}),\qquad
O_i=\mathbb E_{M_i}\log p_{F_i}-\mathbb E_{F_i}\log p_{F_i}.
\tag{15}
\]
The entropy chain rule at each endpoint and at the target, followed by (4), gives the identity
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t=J_h+aJ_1+bJ_0+aD_1+bD_0+aO_1+bO_0.
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
This identity holds without any order or concavity assumptions.

**Theorem 2 (finite conditional-order induction).** Suppose:

1. \(R\succeq0\).
2. The actual target included kernel satisfies \((F_1(I-F_1)^{-1})_{ii}\le1\) for every remaining measured coordinate.
3. The actual target excluded kernel satisfies \(((I-F_0)F_0^{-1})_{ii}\le1\) for every remaining measured coordinate.
4. The two lower-dimensional conditional Jensen gaps \(J_1,J_0\) are nonnegative.

Then every term on the right of (16) is nonnegative, so \(\Gamma_t\ge0\). The condition on the \(J_i\) may be supplied by full lower-dimensional concavity or only by concavity on these two conditional chords.

**Proof.** The binary Jensen gap is nonnegative. Equations (11)–(12) give \(M_1\preceq F_1\) and \(F_0\preceq M_0\). Conditions 2–3 and Lemma 1 give \(O_1,O_0\ge0\). Relative entropies are nonnegative. Condition 4 supplies the two remaining signs in (16). \(\square\)

If the two odds bounds improve to \(r_1,r_0<1\), the trace bounds after (5) yield
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t\ge J_h+aJ_1+bJ_0+aD_1+bD_0
+\log\frac1{r_1r_0}\operatorname{tr}V.
\quad}
\tag{17}
\]
The factors \(a,b\) cancel the denominators in (12). This gives an order improvement that is quadratic in the endpoint separation. In particular, if \(R\ne0\) and both odds bounds are strict, the gap is strict for \(0<t<1\), even when \(a_0=a_1\).

There is also a simple divergence-only bound. Put \(v_*=\max_i V_{ii}\ge0\). The marginal probability difference for coordinate \(i\) is \(V_{ii}/a\), respectively \(V_{ii}/b\). Total variation dominates every marginal difference, so Pinsker's inequality gives
\[
aD_1+bD_0\ge \frac{2v_*^2}{ab}.
\tag{18}
\]
All logarithms are natural, explaining the factor two.

For three sites, condition 4 is automatic by the established full two-site entropy theorem. If an affine three-site segment has \(R\succeq0\) and conditions 2–3 hold at every parameter value, then entropy is concave on **every subsegment**, not merely above its original endpoint chord. Indeed, for endpoints at \(s,u\), the covariance matrix in (11) is \((u-s)^2R\), and the intermediate kernel is another point of the original segment.

## 4. A uniform elementary inverse-diagonal certificate

Here is a check that avoids computing conditional odds along an entire interval. Suppose throughout the segment, for numbers \(d,A,Q,\mu\) with \(0<d<1/2\),
\[
dI\preceq B\preceq(1-d)I,\qquad
a,b\le A,\qquad \|z\|^2\le Q,\qquad |z_i|^2\ge\mu>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
I-F_1=I-B+zz^*/a\succeq dI+zz^*/a,
\]
\[
F_0=B+zz^*/b\succeq dI+zz^*/b.
\]
Matrix inversion reverses Loewner order. The Sherman–Morrison formula therefore gives, for \(c=a\) or \(c=b\),
\[
\left[(dI+zz^*/c)^{-1}\right]_{ii}
=\frac1d-\frac{|z_i|^2}{d(cd+\|z\|^2)}
\le \frac1d-\frac{\mu}{d(Ad+Q)}.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus both conditional odds diagonals are bounded above by
\[
r=\frac1d-\frac{\mu}{d(Ad+Q)}-1.
\tag{21}
\]
When \(r<1\), this certifies both strict monotonicity conditions. The bound follows directly for each \(t\); no grid, polynomial interpolation, or numerical eigenvalue claim is involved. Feasibility of the actual kernels remains a separate hypothesis of the theorem.

## 5. An exact three-site segment with every block changing

Take
\[
K_0=\begin{pmatrix}
2/5&1/5&3/20\\
1/5&51/100&1/200\\
3/20&1/200&51/100
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}
3/5&3/20&1/5\\
3/20&49/100&-1/200\\
1/5&-1/200&49/100
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{22}
\]
Their common affine parametrization has
\[
a(t)=\frac25+\frac t5,\qquad
z(t)=\frac1{20}\binom{4-t}{3+t},
\]
\[
B(t)=\left(\frac{51}{100}-\frac{t}{50}\right)I_2
+\left(\frac1{200}-\frac{t}{100}\right)
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{23}
\]
Exact row-sum bounds give
\[
K_0\succeq\frac1{20}I,\quad I-K_0\succeq\frac14 I,
\qquad
K_1\succeq\frac14 I,\quad I-K_1\succeq\frac1{20}I.
\tag{24}
\]
Convexity gives \(I/20\preceq K_t\preceq19I/20\) for every \(0\le t\le1\), so every endpoint, target, and conditional kernel used is strict.

The block direction is
\[
\alpha=\frac15>0,\quad
w=\frac1{20}\binom{-1}{1},\quad
C=-\frac1{100}\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}\prec0,
\tag{25}
\]
with eigenvalues \(-3/100,-1/100\). In fact
\[
R=ww^*-\alpha C
=\frac1{2000}\begin{pmatrix}13&-1\\-1&13\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\operatorname{spec}R=\left\{\frac3{500},\frac7{1000}\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Thus \(R\succ0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}R=13/1000\).

To verify the target conditions uniformly, use
\[
d=\frac{97}{200},\quad A=\frac35,\quad
Q=\frac1{16},\quad \mu=\frac9{400}.
\tag{27}
\]
Indeed \(\|B(t)-I/2\|\le3/200\), since the two eigenvalues of that deviation are \(3(1-2t)/200\) and \((1-2t)/200\). Also \(a(t),1-a(t)\le3/5\), both components of \(z(t)\) are at least \(3/20\), and convexity of squared norm gives \(\|z(t)\|^2\le\max\{\|z_0\|^2,\|z_1\|^2\}=1/16\). Substitution in (20) gives
\[
\left((I-F_1(t))^{-1}\right)_{ii},\
\left(F_0(t)^{-1}\right)_{ii}
\le\frac{132400}{68579}<\frac{31}{16}.
\tag{28}
\]
The last exact inequality is \(2118400<2125949\). Both conditional odds diagonals are therefore at most \(15/16<1\), for **all** \(t\in[0,1]\).

Theorem 2 now proves full entropy concavity on every subsegment of (22). In fact, for \(0\le s<u\le1\) and \(0<\lambda<1\), let \(v=(1-\lambda)s+\lambda u\). Binary entropy has \(h''(x)\le-4\), so (17) yields
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
H(K_v)&-(1-\lambda)H(K_s)-\lambda H(K_u)\\
&\ge\lambda(1-\lambda)(u-s)^2
\left[\frac2{25}+\frac{13}{500}\log\frac{16}{15}\right]>0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{29}
\]
The lower-dimensional Jensen and relative entropy terms can be retained in addition. Equation (18), using \(V_{ii}=13\lambda(1-\lambda)(u-s)^2/2000\) and \(ab\le1/4\), improves (29) further by
\[
\frac{169}{500000}\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2(u-s)^4.
\tag{30}
\]
The constants in these inequalities are exact consequences of the displayed bounds, not fitted curvature values.

## 6. Scope beyond fixed exterior data

The two endpoint cross vectors are not parallel:
\[
\det(z_0,z_1)=\frac7{400}\ne0.
\tag{31}
\]
The entire Hermitian direction \(K_1-K_0\) is nonsingular. Since \(C\prec0\), block congruence gives its inertia from \(C\) and
\[
\alpha-w^*C^{-1}w=\frac7{10}>0.
\]
Hence its inertia is \((1,2)\), with
\[
\det(K_1-K_0)=\det C\cdot\frac7{10}
=\frac{21}{100000}\ne0.
\tag{32}
\]
It therefore cannot be represented by a direction supported on a fixed two-dimensional subspace. Every measured diagonal entry changes, which excludes the C059/C062 fixed-exterior families for every choice of a two-site core in three sites. Both cross entries and the complementary off-diagonal entry change. Nonparallel \(z_0,z_1\) also shows that the affine line never passes through a diagonal kernel: its two incident off-diagonal entries cannot vanish simultaneously. Thus the line-through-a-diagonal theorem C006 does not contain this segment.

All these distinctions concern the theorem scopes stated in the archive. They do not claim that the exact example is disjoint from every previously certified local region. The new construction is the finite conditioning recursion (16)–(17) and its uniform moving-data application.

The hypotheses are strict with uniform margins on the compact interval: the endpoints are strict contractions, \(R\succ0\), and both odds bounds lie below one. Therefore a sufficiently small open neighborhood of the pair \((K_0,K_1)\), in the full real vector space of Hermitian endpoint pairs, satisfies the same all-subsegment theorem. This follows from continuity of conditional kernels and inverse diagonals, uniformly for \(t\in[0,1]\). Small complex perturbations are included. The proved family is consequently more than a single real rational line.
