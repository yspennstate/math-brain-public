# C060 — Positive Boolean coefficients for frustrated triangle trees

The matrix support is a rooted tree of edge and triangle blocks, and the condition permits positive, gauge-frustrated triangle products. ## 1. Rooted triangle trees and the local condition

Start from a root vertex. Repeatedly attach either an edge, adding one new child vertex, or a triangle, adding two new child vertices, at a single existing parent vertex. No other edges are present. This is a tree of edge and triangle clique blocks: different blocks can meet only at articulation vertices. The construction may branch, and each child can support any number of later child blocks. A forest is handled componentwise.

Let \(L>0\) be a Hermitian matrix supported on this graph, and write \(d_v=L_{vv}>0\). For each oriented triangle \((r;a,b)\), where \(r\) is its parent and \(a,b\) its new children, put
\[
u=L_{ra},\qquad v=L_{rb},\qquad c=L_{ab},\qquad
\alpha=|u|^2,\quad\beta=|v|^2,\quad h=|c|^2,
\quad t=2\operatorname{Re}(uc\overline v).
\]
Assume on every such triangle that
\[
\boxed{\delta_{r;a,b}:=
h\left(\frac{\alpha}{d_a}+\frac{\beta}{d_b}\right)-t\ge0.}
\tag{1}
\]
The diagonal entries in (1) are the original diagonal entries of \(L\), before any descendants are eliminated. Edge blocks have no additional condition. The orientation matters, and the theorem needs one root orientation satisfying (1).

The condition is invariant under diagonal unitary gauge. It is also invariant under positive diagonal congruence: replacing \(L\) by \(DLD\), with \(D_{ii}=s_i>0\), multiplies the margin on \((r;a,b)\) by \(s_r^2s_a^2s_b^2\).

**Theorem 1.** Under these hypotheses, the normalized table
\[
f_L(S)=-\log\det L_S+\sum_{v\in S}\log d_v,
\qquad \det L_\varnothing=1,
\]
has an exact Boolean expansion
\[
\boxed{f_L(S)=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq S\\|T|\ge2}}w_T,
\qquad w_T\ge0.}
\tag{2}
\]
This holds for complex edge entries satisfying (1), without any gauge assumption.

## 2. The positive Boolean algebra and convergence

Use variables \(x_v=\mathbf1_{v\in S}\), with \(x_v^2=x_v\). Every set function has a unique expansion \(F(x)=\sum_T a_Tx_T\), where \(x_T=\prod_{v\in T}x_v\). Let \(\mathcal C\) be the cone with \(a_T\ge0\) for every \(T\). It is closed under addition, multiplication, and coefficientwise limits. Products use \(x_Ax_B=x_{A\cup B}\), so they preserve nonnegative coefficients even when the factors involve overlapping variables.

On this finite-dimensional algebra use \(\|F\|_1=\sum_T|a_T|\). It is submultiplicative, and for \(F\in\mathcal C\), \(\|F\|_1=F(\mathbf1)\). Therefore, whenever \(F\in\mathcal C\) and \(F(\mathbf1)<1\), both
\[
(1-F)^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}F^k,
\qquad -\log(1-F)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{F^k}{k}
\tag{3}
\]
converge in coefficient norm and belong to \(\mathcal C\). They equal the displayed scalar functions at every Boolean selection. This provides convergence before regrouping any coefficients.

## 3. Subtree responses and the triangle identity

Let \(T_r\) be the subtree consisting of \(r\) and all its descendants; the two children of a triangle have disjoint descendant subtrees. For a selected subset, eliminate all selected strict descendants of \(r\) inside \(T_r\). Let \(R_r\) be the total correction to the diagonal at \(r\), and put
\[
g_r=\frac{x_r}{d_r-R_r}.
\tag{4}
\]
The response \(R_r\) depends only on strict descendants, even when \(r\) is absent from the selected subset. If \(r\) is selected, \(g_r\) is the \(r,r\) entry of the inverse selected subtree matrix; if \(r\) is absent, it is zero. The empty response at a leaf is \(R_r=0\).

All these formulas are well-defined at every selection. Indeed, the matrix formed from the selected strict descendants together with \(r\) is a positive definite principal submatrix of \(L\). Its scalar Schur complement is \(d_r-R_r>0\). For a triangle whose children are both included, eliminating their respective strict descendants gives the positive definite matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}d_a-R_a&c\\\overline c&d_b-R_b\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(1-hg_ag_b>0\). If either child is absent, that factor equals one. In particular these strict inequalities hold at the all-selected configuration.

An edge block \((r;a)\) contributes
\[
Q_{r;a}=|L_{ra}|^2g_a
\tag{5}
\]
to \(R_r\). A triangle block \((r;a,b)\) contributes
\[
\boxed{Q_{r;a,b}=
\frac{\alpha g_a+\beta g_b-tg_ag_b}{1-hg_ag_b}.}
\tag{6}
\]
When both children are selected this is the quadratic Schur-complement response of the displayed two-by-two matrix to the row \((u,v)\); when either is absent, the indicator factors reduce it to the appropriate one-child response. Responses of different child blocks add to \(R_r\).

We prove inductively that every \(R_r\) and \(g_r\) belongs to \(\mathcal C\), and that
\[
g_r^2-\frac{g_r}{d_r}\in\mathcal C.
\tag{7}
\]
Assume the result below \(r\). The exact algebraic identity
\[
\boxed{
Q_{r;a,b}=\alpha g_a+\beta g_b+
\frac{
\delta_{r;a,b}g_ag_b
+h\alpha(g_a^2-g_a/d_a)g_b
+h\beta g_a(g_b^2-g_b/d_b)
}{1-hg_ag_b}}
\tag{8}
\]
has a nonnegative numerator in the Boolean coefficient cone. Its inverse denominator belongs to the same cone by (3), because \(hg_ag_b\in\mathcal C\) and its all-selected value is below one. Thus (5), (8), and addition give \(R_r\in\mathcal C\). The all-selected Schur complement gives \(R_r(\mathbf1)/d_r<1\), so
\[
g_r=\frac{x_r}{d_r}\sum_{k\ge0}(R_r/d_r)^k\in\mathcal C.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(R_r\) is independent of \(x_r\), the idempotence \(x_r^2=x_r\) also gives
\[
\boxed{
g_r^2-\frac{g_r}{d_r}
=\frac{x_rR_r}{d_r(d_r-R_r)^2}
=\frac{x_r}{d_r^2}\sum_{k\ge1}k(R_r/d_r)^k\in\mathcal C.}
\tag{10}
\]
The series converges in coefficient norm. This completes the induction, including leaves.

The terms in (8) explain why descendants do not spoil the local criterion. The original-diagonal term is exactly \(\delta g_ag_b\); every increase of inverse response caused by descendants appears in the additional positive terms (10).

## 4. Exact determinant factorization for every selected subset

The selection indicators in (4) are essential: a sibling edge in a triangle remains present when its parent is absent. Eliminating subtrees gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\det L_S=
\prod_v(d_v-R_v)^{x_v}
\prod_{(r;a,b)\text{ triangle}}(1-|L_{ab}|^2g_ag_b).}
\tag{11}
\]
Here all responses are evaluated on \(S\). In particular a triangle factor is included regardless of whether its parent belongs to \(S\).

To prove (11) formally, use induction on the rooted construction. For a child edge, its selected subtree determinant factors out, and its inverse diagonal response gives (5). For a child triangle, the selected matrices on \(T_a,T_b\) are coupled only by the entry \(c\) between their roots. Their combined determinant is their separate determinant product times \(1-hg_ag_b\); this follows either from the two-by-two Schur determinant formula or the rank-two determinant lemma. This remains true if either child root is absent. If \(r\) is selected, the last Schur pivot is \(d_r-R_r\), with the responses (5), (6) added over child blocks. If \(r\) is absent, there is no last pivot, and the child-block determinant product remains. These are exactly the two cases of (11).

Taking logarithms of positive factors in (11) yields
\[
\boxed{
f_L(S)=
\sum_vx_v\left[-\log(1-R_v/d_v)\right]
+\sum_{(r;a,b)\text{ triangle}}\left[-\log(1-hg_ag_b)\right].}
\tag{12}
\]
Each summand has nonnegative Boolean coefficients by (3) and the induction above. There are finitely many summands, all series converge in coefficient norm, and their total coefficient mass is the finite number \(f_L(E)\). Thus regrouping is legitimate. The normalized table vanishes on the empty set and all singletons, so its constant and singleton coefficients are zero. This proves Theorem 1.

## 5. Canonical exterior positivity and restricted entropy consequences

For any external positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix \(K\), the DPP inclusion polynomial associated with the unnormalized table is
\[
F_L(K)=-\sum_i K_{ii}\log d_i+
\sum_{|T|\ge2}w_T\det K_T.
\tag{13}
\]
On a contraction this is \(\mathbb E_K[-\log\det L_X]\). The canonical exterior Hessian of each principal determinant is the embedding of
\[
\det K_T\,\bigwedge^2K_T^{-1}\succeq0
\tag{14}
\]
for \(K_T>0\), and its cofactor-polynomial continuation is positive semidefinite on the boundary. The nonnegative finite sum in (13) therefore has positive semidefinite canonical exterior Hessian at every external positive contraction, including complex bases and boundary bases. This is an operator statement, not an inference from tests only on decomposable forms.

At the entropy-linked strict base \(K=L(I+L)^{-1}\), freeze the log-atom table. Its difference from \(-\log\det L_S\) is a constant. Let \(I_K(A)=\sum_S(Dp_S(K)[A])^2/p_S(K)\). The exact entropy identity is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-I_K(A)+\sum_Tw_TD^2\det K_T[A_T,A_T].
\tag{15}
\]
For \(A=\alpha uu^*-\beta vv^*\), \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), rank-one affinity and (14) give
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-I_K(A)-2\alpha\beta q_{f_L}(K;u,v)\le0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every direction with at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue is covered, in every dimension, for the complex class of Theorem 1.

There is also a high-rank consequence for real \(L\). Write a Hermitian direction as \(A=X+iB\), with \(X=X^T\) real and \(B=-B^T\) real. At a real base, conjugation invariance of every atom gives \(Dp_S(K)[iB]=0\), so \(I_K(A)=I_K(X)\). Put
\[
U_T=K_T^{-1/2}X_TK_T^{-1/2},\qquad
Z_T=K_T^{-1/2}B_TK_T^{-1/2}.
\]
The symmetric/skew trace identities give the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-I_K(X)
-\sum_Tw_T\det K_T
\left\{\operatorname{tr}(U_T^2)-(\operatorname{tr}U_T)^2+\|Z_T\|_F^2\right\}.}
\tag{17}
\]
If \(X\) has at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue, so does each congruent principal compression \(U_T\). Its two potentially nonzero eigenvalues are \(p\ge0\) and \(-q\le0\), so the real-part expression in braces is \(2pq+\|Z_T\|_F^2\ge0\). There is no rank or inertia restriction on \(B\), and the full Hermitian direction \(A\) can have arbitrarily many eigenvalues of both signs. This is precisely C054's real/imaginary argument, now valid at the new real frustrated triangle-tree bases. A fixed diagonal-unitary gauge to a real matrix gives the corresponding gauge extension.

These are pointwise entropy curvature assertions. They integrate to concavity along an affine segment only if the required odds and direction hypotheses hold at every interior point. Positive Boolean coefficients do not by themselves control arbitrary higher-rank real directions, and no full entropy theorem is asserted here.

## 6. Explicit frustrated family and seven-site certificate

For every attached triangle choose
\[
d_r=d_a=d_b=4,\qquad L_{ra}=1,\quad L_{ab}=1,\quad L_{rb}=1/10,
\tag{18}
\]
with real symmetric reverse entries. Thus the weak edge joins the parent to one child; it is not the sibling edge. Every local margin is
\[
\boxed{\delta=1\left(\frac14+\frac1{400}\right)-\frac15=\frac{21}{400}>0.}
\tag{19}
\]
Every triangle cycle product is \(1/10>0\), invariant under diagonal unitary gauge. A real Stieltjes triangle with all three edges nonzero has negative cycle product. Therefore these matrices cannot be made Stieltjes by diagonal unitary conjugation.

Any connected rooted triangle tree with these entries is covered whenever the resulting matrix is positive definite. The chain obtained by attaching each new triangle at the preceding weak-edge child \(b\) has maximum absolute off-diagonal row sum \(11/5<4\), in every length. Thus these chains are strictly diagonally dominant and positive definite.

There is an explicit construction on **every finite rooted tree of edge and triangle blocks**, regardless of branching. Let \(\Delta\ge1\) bound its graph degree, choose
\[
0<s\le\min\{1,2/\Delta\},\qquad d_i=4,
\tag{20}
\]
and assign on each oriented triangle
\[
L_{ra}=L_{ab}=s,\qquad L_{rb}=s^2/10.
\tag{21}
\]
Any edge-block entry may be chosen real, nonzero, and of absolute value at most \(s\). Each row has off-diagonal absolute sum at most \(\Delta s\le2<4\), proving positive definiteness. Every triangle has
\[
\boxed{\delta=\frac{s^4}{20}+\frac{s^6}{400}>0,
\qquad L_{ra}L_{ab}L_{br}=\frac{s^4}{10}>0.}
\tag{22}
\]
Thus (21) gives connected, arbitrarily large, gauge-frustrated examples. The weak edge scales quadratically with \(s\). Uniformly shrinking all three edge entries, or merely increasing the diagonals while fixing the edges, would not in general preserve (1).
