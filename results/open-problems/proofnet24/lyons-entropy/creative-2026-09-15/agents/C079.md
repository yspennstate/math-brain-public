## 3. A general centered four-site curvature inequality

This section applies to every positive definite complex Hermitian odds matrix `L`, without equal edge magnitudes. Write its frozen log table, up to constant and modular terms, as
\[
f(S)=\sum_{e\subseteq S}a_e+\sum_{T\subseteq S,\,|T|=3}b_T
c_4\mathbf1_{[4]\subseteq S}.
\]
At `K=L(I+L)^{-1}`, set `R=K-I/2` and
\[
\alpha_e=a_e+\frac12\sum_{T\supset e}b_T+\frac14c_4,
\qquad \eta_T=b_T+\frac12c_4.
\tag{10}
\]

**The centered pair coefficients are always nonnegative.** Indeed, with `Y` ranging over the four subsets of `[4]\e`,
\[
\alpha_{ij}=\frac14\sum_Y\Delta_i\Delta_j f(Y)\ge0.
\tag{11}
\]
After conditioning on `Y`, the two-site Schur complement has diagonal entries `x,z>0`, off-diagonal entry `y`, and determinant `xz-|y|^2>0`. Its contribution to the difference in (11) is
`log(xz/(xz-|y|^2))>=0`. This proves (11) for arbitrary complex phases.

The exact complete entropy identity is
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(P_K)[A,A]=-I_K(A)
 +2\sum_e\alpha_e\det A_e
 +2\sum_{|T|=3}\eta_T\operatorname{tr}(R_T\operatorname{adj}A_T)
 +c_4D^2\det(R)[A,A].}
\tag{12}
\]
To prove it, freeze `f=-log det L_S`; the additional `log det(I+L)` in `-log p_S` is constant. Inclusion moments give `E_J f(X)=sum_T w_T det J_T`. The principal-minor identity
\[
\det J_T=\sum_{U\subseteq T}2^{-|T\setminus U|}\det(J-I/2)_U
\]
gives precisely (10). In dimension three,
`D^2 det(R_T)[A_T,A_T]=2 tr(R_T adj A_T)`.
The entropy chain rule contributes the whole `-I_K(A)`, proving (12).

Here are exact spectral constants for the two remainders. For a Hermitian three-by-three `E` with eigenvalues `e_1,e_2,e_3`, let
\[
\theta(E)=\lambda_{\max}\begin{pmatrix}
0&e_3&e_2\\e_3&0&e_1\\e_2&e_1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(E\operatorname{adj}B)\le\theta(E)\|B\|_F^2,
\quad
|\theta(E)-\theta(E')|\le2\|E-E'\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{14}
\]
The first constant is sharp. Diagonalizing `E`, the diagonal coordinates of `B` have the quadratic matrix in (13); each complex off-diagonal coordinate has normalized eigenvalue `-e_i`. The largest eigenvalue of (13) is at least every `|e_i|`, by its two-by-two principal restrictions. This proves sharpness. For the Lipschitz bound, the eigenvalues of `adj B` have absolute sum at most `||B||_F^2`, since `sum_{i<j}|mu_i mu_j|<=sum_i mu_i^2` in dimension three. Apply this to the difference of the two quadratic forms. This is the C045 spectral lemma, with its explicit perturbation consequence.

There is an analogous exact four-dimensional constant. If `lambda_1,...,lambda_4` are the eigenvalues of `R`, define `M` by
\[
M_{ii}=0,\qquad M_{ij}=c_4\lambda_k\lambda_l,
\quad\{k,l\}=[4]\setminus\{i,j\},
\]
\[
\psi(c_4,R)=\max\left\{\lambda_{\max}(M),\,
\max_{i<j}(-c_4\lambda_k\lambda_l)\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then `c_4 D^2 det(R)[A,A]<=psi(c_4,R)||A||_F^2`, and this constant is sharp. At diagonal `R`, the determinant Hessian is
\[
2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_k\lambda_l(A_{ii}A_{jj}-|A_{ij}|^2),
\]
so (15) lists every diagonal and off-diagonal spectral block. A convenient uniform consequence is
\[
|D^2\det(R)[A,A]|\le3\|R\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\|A\|_F^2.
\tag{16}
\]
For the diagonal block, use `(sum |A_ii|)^2<=4 sum A_ii^2`; the off-diagonal block has the smaller constant `||R||^2`.

**Theorem 2 (centered Fisher allocation).** Choose `0<=t<=min_e alpha_e`, put
\[
v_K=\operatorname{tr}(K-K^2)\le1,\qquad
\beta=t v_K+\frac14\sum_e(\alpha_e-t),
\]
\[
r_T=\theta(\eta_T R_T),\qquad
\mathcal R_3=\sum_T r_T-\min_T r_T.
\tag{17}
\]
Then, for every Hermitian direction,
\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(P_K)[A,A]\ge(1-\beta)I_K(A)
 +[t-\mathcal R_3-\psi(c_4,R)]\|A\|_F^2.}
\tag{18}
\]
One may replace the exact last constant by `3|c_4| ||R||_op^2`.

**Proof.** The full atom score is centered and has variance `I=I_K(A)`. Its covariance with the particle count is `tr A`, so `(tr A)^2<=v_K I`. On a pair, the marginal count variance is at most `1/2`. Its marginal Fisher information `I_e` therefore obeys
\[
I_e\ge2(\operatorname{tr}A_e)^2\ge8\det A_e,
\qquad I_e\le I.
\tag{19}
\]
The second inequality in the first chain follows from
`(tr A_e)^2-4 det A_e=(A_ii-A_jj)^2+4|A_ij|^2>=0`; it does not presume a definite direction. Thus
\[
2\sum_e\alpha_e\det A_e
\le\left[tv_K+\tfrac14\sum_e(\alpha_e-t)\right]I-t\|A\|_F^2,
\tag{20}
\]
using `2 sum_e det A_e=(tr A)^2-||A||_F^2`.

Every `r_T` is nonnegative. Each diagonal coordinate of `A` lies in three triples and each edge coordinate in two; hence
\[
\sum_T r_T\|A_T\|_F^2\le
\left(\sum_T r_T-\min_T r_T\right)\|A\|_F^2.
\]
Apply (14)–(16) to (12) and then (20). This proves (18) with no omitted atom term. ∎

The criterion (18) is sufficient, not universal. Its merit here is that its signed cubic and quartic terms can be bounded on a whole independent-phase family.
