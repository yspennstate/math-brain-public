## 1. Exact cancellation theorem

Let \(0<K<I_3\), write \(p_S=P_K(X=S)>0\), and freeze \(f(S)=-\log p_S\). Define
\[
c_{ij}=f(ij)-f(i)-f(j)+f(\varnothing),\qquad
\tau=f(123)-\sum_{i<j}f(ij)+\sum_i f(i)-f(\varnothing),
\tag{1}
\]
\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12}),\qquad
M=C+\tau K,\qquad \delta=\det M.
\tag{2}
\]
All matrix norms without a subscript are Frobenius. Put
\[
\mathcal I_K(A)=\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S},\qquad
\mathcal N_K=\{A=A^*:Dp_S(K)[A]=0\text{ for every }S\}.
\tag{3}
\]

**Theorem 1.** If \(K\) is connected in the measured coordinates, then \(M>0\), and every \(A\in\mathcal N_K\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-\det M\,
\|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F^2.
}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular it is strictly negative for \(A\ne0\). If
\[
u=\lambda_{\max}(M),\qquad \gamma=\frac{\det M}{u^2}>0,
\]
then
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]\le-\gamma\|A\|_F^2
\qquad(A\in\mathcal N_K).
\tag{5}
\]
For every real diagonal \(D\), \(A_D=i[D,K]\) belongs to \(\mathcal N_K\), so (4) applies. At a connected three-site kernel these tangents span a two-dimensional real plane. They need not have rank two.

### Proof

The odds matrix \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\) gives \(p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S\). The positive two-by-two Schur complement of \(L_T\) implies
\[
p_{Ti}p_{Tj}\ge p_Tp_{Tij}.
\]
Thus \(f\) is supermodular; on three sites this is exactly
\[
c_{ij}\ge0,\qquad c_{ij}+\tau\ge0.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(\tau\ge0\), \(M=C+\tau K\ge0\). For \(\tau<0\),
\[
M=(C+\tau I)+(-\tau)(I-K)\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\tau\ne0\), strict contraction gives \(M>0\).

If \(\tau=0\), a zero \(c_{ij}\) implies \(L_{ij}=0\). The equality \(c_{ij}+\tau=0\) also says that the conditional Schur-complement entry after the remaining coordinate \(k\) is zero:
\[
L_{ij}-L_{ik}L_{kk}^{-1}L_{kj}=0.
\]
It follows that \(L_{ik}L_{kj}=0\). Thus \(L\), and hence \(K\), is disconnected into measured-coordinate blocks. Conversely a disconnected three-site kernel has \(\tau=0\) and at least one zero \(c_{ij}\). Therefore \(M>0\) exactly when \(K\) is connected.

For the frozen expectation \(F_f(J)=\mathbb E_{P_J}f(X)\), Möbius expansion gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
D^2H(K)[A,A]
&=-\mathcal I_K(A)+D^2F_f(K)[A,A]\\
&=-\mathcal I_K(A)+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\det A_{ij}
  +2\tau\operatorname{tr}(K\operatorname{adj}A)\\
&=-\mathcal I_K(A)+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This is the previously established Lorentzian formula. With \(B=M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\), congruence of the adjugate gives
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
=\delta\big((\operatorname{tr}B)^2-\|B\|_F^2\big).
\tag{9}
\]

The additional exact cancellation is
\[
\boxed{A\in\mathcal N_K\ \Longrightarrow\
\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A)=0.}
\tag{10}
\]
Write \(c_1=c_{23},c_2=c_{13},c_3=c_{12}\), and define
\[
g(S)=\prod_{i=1}^3(c_i+\tau\mathbf1_{i\in S})\ge0.
\tag{11}
\]
Nonnegativity follows from (6). Product expansion and the inclusion-minor identity yield
\[
\mathbb E_{P_J}g(X)=\det(C+\tau J).
\tag{12}
\]
All \(c_i,\tau\) remain frozen at \(K\). Differentiation gives the exact score representation
\[
\sum_S g(S)Dp_S(K)[A]
=\tau\delta\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(A\in\mathcal N_K\) and \(\tau\ne0\), this proves (10). If \(\tau=0\), \(M=C>0\) is diagonal, while \(A\in\mathcal N_K\) implies
\[
A_{ii}=D\mathbb P_K(i\in X)[A]=0.
\]
This again proves (10). Equations (8)–(10), together with \(\mathcal I_K(A)=0\), prove (4). The inequality
\(\|M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\|_F\ge\|A\|_F/u\) proves (5).

For a gauge tangent, diagonal-unitary conjugation leaves every principal minor invariant, so every atom derivative vanishes. A separate direct check of (10) is
\[
[D,M]=\tau[D,K],\qquad
\tau\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}A_D)
=i\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}[D,M])=0.
\tag{14}
\]
For \(\tau=0\), \(M\) is diagonal and \(A_D\) has zero diagonal. This distinguishes the strictly negative affine curvature from the zero second derivative along the nonlinear gauge orbit.

## 2. Global Gaussian diagonal phase damping

Let \(x_1,x_2,x_3\in\mathbb R^m\) be arbitrary, and set
\[
\rho_{ij}=\|x_i-x_j\|^2,\qquad
(K_t)_{ij}=e^{-t\rho_{ij}}K_{ij},\qquad t\ge0.
\tag{15}
\]
For coordinate \(\alpha\), let
\[
D_\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(x_{1\alpha},x_{2\alpha},x_{3\alpha}).
\]
The Gaussian characteristic-function representation shows that \(K_t\) is an average of diagonal-unitary conjugates of \(K\). It is therefore a positive contraction, and every initial spectral buffer
\(\varepsilon I\le K\le(1-\varepsilon)I\) is retained. Its generator is
\[
\dot K_t=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^m[D_\alpha,[D_\alpha,K_t]].
\tag{16}
\]

**Theorem 2.** For every three-site Hermitian positive contraction \(K\),
\[
H(K_t)\ge H(K_s)\qquad(t\ge s\ge0).
\tag{17}
\]
If \(K\) is strict, the inequality is strict for \(t>s\) exactly when the flow changes \(K\).

If \(K\) is also connected, define \(M_t,\delta_t\) from (1)–(2) using the current atoms of \(K_t\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}H(K_t)
=\det M_t\sum_{\alpha=1}^m
\|M_t^{-1/2}i[D_\alpha,K_t]M_t^{-1/2}\|_F^2.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus for \(0\le s<t<\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
H(K_t)-H(K_s)
=\int_s^t\det M_v\sum_\alpha
\|M_v^{-1/2}i[D_\alpha,K_v]M_v^{-1/2}\|_F^2\,dv.
}
\tag{19}
\]
This is an exact finite positive entropy gap without a Fisher term.

### Proof

Differentiating the invariant function \(H(e^{izD}Je^{-izD})\) twice at \(z=0\) gives
\[
D^2H(J)[i[D,J],i[D,J]]
=DH(J)[[D,[D,J]]].
\tag{20}
\]
Equation (20) alone supplies no sign. Combining it with (4) and (16) proves (18). A connected graph remains connected at every finite time because the factors in (15) are positive. Thus every \(M_t>0\), and (18) is strictly positive precisely when some \([D_\alpha,K_t]\ne0\).

For a disconnected strict three-site kernel, entropy adds over its measured-coordinate blocks. Its only nonsingleton block has size two. If its off-diagonal square magnitude is \(q(t)=q(0)e^{-2\rho t}\), its four masses are
\[
x=(1-a)(1-b)-q,\quad y=a(1-b)+q,\quad
z=(1-a)b+q,\quad w=ab-q.
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}H=2\rho q\log\frac{yz}{xw}\ge0,
\qquad yz-xw=q,
\]
strictly when \(\rho q>0\). This proves (17) and its strictness assertion for every strict kernel. Continuity of atom polynomials and entropy extends (17) to boundary contractions.

If the \(x_i\) are pairwise distinct, \(K_t\to\operatorname{diag}(K)\). Consequently, for connected strict \(K\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^3h(K_{ii})-H(K)
=\int_0^\infty\det M_v\sum_\alpha
\|M_v^{-1/2}i[D_\alpha,K_v]M_v^{-1/2}\|_F^2\,dv.
\tag{21}
\]
For coincident \(x_i\), the limit is instead the block-diagonal kernel for the equivalence classes \(x_i=x_j\), and its entropy replaces the Bernoulli sum.

### A finite lower bound from the starting kernel

Let
\[
m_0=\lambda_{\min}(M),\quad u_0=\lambda_{\max}(M),\quad
R=\max_{i,j}\rho_{ij},\quad
B=\|K-\operatorname{diag}(K)\|_F,
\]
and choose an initial spectral buffer \(\varepsilon>0\). Put
\[
\mathcal L_M=\frac{12\sqrt3}{\varepsilon}+|\tau|,
\qquad T_0=\frac{m_0}{2\mathcal L_M RB}.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(RB=0\), the flow is constant. Otherwise, for \(0\le T\le T_0\),
\[
\boxed{
H(K_T)-H(K)\ge
\frac{m_0^2}{6u_0}
\sum_{i<j}|K_{ij}|^2(1-e^{-2\rho_{ij}T}).
}
\tag{23}
\]

To prove this, \(\|K_t-K\|_F\le RBt\). For kernels with buffer \(\varepsilon\), every atom matrix
\(Q_S=K-I_{S^c}\) has eigenvalues of magnitude at least \(\varepsilon\), by the Loewner eigenvalue inequalities for
\(-I_{S^c}+\varepsilon I\le Q_S\le-I_{S^c}+(1-\varepsilon)I\).
Thus each \(\log p_S\) has gradient norm at most \(\sqrt3/\varepsilon\). Formula (1) bounds the changes of \(c_{ij}\) and \(\tau\) by
\(4\sqrt3\|E\|_F/\varepsilon\) and \(8\sqrt3\|E\|_F/\varepsilon\), respectively. From \(M=C+\tau K\),
\[
\|M_t-M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\mathcal L_M\|K_t-K\|_F.
\]
For \(t\le T_0\), \(M_t\ge(m_0/2)I\) and
\(\lambda_{\max}(M_t)\le3u_0/2\). Hence
\[
\frac{\det M_t}{\lambda_{\max}(M_t)^2}
\ge\frac{m_0^2}{6u_0}.
\]
Finally
\[
\sum_\alpha\|i[D_\alpha,K_t]\|_F^2
=2\sum_{i<j}\rho_{ij}|K_{ij}|^2e^{-2t\rho_{ij}}.
\]
Integrating the resulting lower bound in (18) gives (23).

## 3. A finite affine strong-concavity neighborhood

This consequence makes the negative-square identity concrete on a fixed affine plane rather than on an orbit. Retain \(\gamma\) from (5), choose a spectral buffer \(\varepsilon\), and define
\[
\mathcal L_\varepsilon=
\left(\frac2\varepsilon\right)^3
\left[38\log\frac2\varepsilon+26\right],
\qquad
r=\min\left\{\frac\varepsilon2,\frac{\gamma}{2\mathcal L_\varepsilon}\right\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Then the convex affine ball
\[
\mathcal C_K=\{K+E:E\in\mathcal N_K,\ \|E\|_F\le r\}
\tag{25}
\]
consists of strict contractions, and
\[
D^2H(J)[A,A]\le-\frac\gamma2\|A\|_F^2
\qquad(J\in\mathcal C_K,\ A\in\mathcal N_K).
\tag{26}
\]
For \(J_0,J_1\in\mathcal C_K\),
\[
\boxed{
H((1-t)J_0+tJ_1)\ge(1-t)H(J_0)+tH(J_1)
+\frac\gamma4t(1-t)\|J_1-J_0\|_F^2.
}
\tag{27}
\]
The same result holds on the fixed gauge plane \(\{i[D,K]:D\text{ real diagonal}\}\), which is contained in \(\mathcal N_K\).

For completeness, the required third-derivative bound is elementary. On the radius-\(\varepsilon/2\) ball around \(K\), put \(a=\varepsilon/2\). The atom matrices have inverse operator norm at most \(a^{-1}\), and \(p_S\ge a^3\). For arbitrary Hermitian directions,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|Dp_S[A]|&\le p_S\sqrt3a^{-1}\|A\|_F,\\
|D^2p_S[A,B]|&\le4p_Sa^{-2}\|A\|_F\|B\|_F,\\
|D^3p_S[A,B,E]|&\le
(6\sqrt3+2)p_Sa^{-3}\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\|E\|_F.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
The third bound comprises one product of three scores, three score-times-trace terms, and two cyclic triple traces. Differentiating entropy three times and summing \(p_S=1\) gives
\[
|D^3H(J)[A,B,E]|
\le a^{-3}\big[(18\sqrt3+6)\log(1/a)+15\sqrt3\big]
\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\|E\|_F.
\tag{29}
\]
This is at most \(\mathcal L_\varepsilon\) times the three norms. Integrating (29) from \(K\) to \(J\) gives (26), and integrating on a chord gives (27). The radius is deliberately conservative.

## 5. Scope and the remaining obstruction

The input negative Lorentzian hyperplane was known; the new cancellation identifies the entire atom-score nullspace inside it and turns that fact into an exact coercive identity. The finite affine result holds at every connected strict complex three-site base. It does not establish nonpositivity of the full nine-dimensional Hessian. The one potentially positive direction in old-097 still requires its Fisher-compensation criterion.

The global theorem covers Gaussian diagonal phase damping with squared-Euclidean rates. It does not claim arbitrary Schur-multiplier monotonicity, arbitrary diagonal-unitary orbit-average inequalities, or general unitary invariance.

A supplementary creative LP search in explore.py asked whether every supermodular frozen expectation has nonpositive gauge curvature in arbitrary dimension. No positive example was found among 120 random strict kernels in each dimension 3–6, with elementary supermodular differences normalized between zero and one. This is numerical evidence only; the all-dimensional statement remains unproved.

The positive observable (11) also suggests the sufficient full-Hessian certificate
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{P_K}(g(X))\le\tau^2\det M\qquad(\tau\ne0),
\]
by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to (13) and then (8)–(9). This simple bound is not universal: certificate_explore.py found ratios above one. It is not used in any theorem. It records why the exact null-mode cancellation does not itself settle the remaining Lorentzian direction.
