# C005 — positive scalar Laplace kernels and an exact resolvent obstruction

Date: 2026-09-15. Task: a direct Hessian attempt using integral representations and positivity on decomposable exterior two-forms, starting at six sites.

**Scope.** The general Lyons conjecture and general six-site indefinite rank-two concavity are not proved here. Two bounded results are complete:

1. If a positive definite matrix has the form (L=D+\sigma aa^*), where (D>0) is diagonal and (\sigma\in\{1,-1\}), the frozen function (f_L(S)=-\log\det L_S) has a positive semidefinite canonical exterior Hessian operator at **every** positive-contraction kernel. This follows from an explicit positive scalar Laplace integral. Consequently, DPP entropy has nonpositive Hessian in every indefinite rank-two direction at any kernel whose odds matrix has this form. This includes every strict kernel that is a diagonal strict contraction plus a signed rank-one matrix. A precise family of finite concavity segments is given below.
2. The more direct matrix-resolvent integrand is **not** nonnegative on decomposable two-forms, even when (L=K(I-K)^{-1}) is linked to the base kernel. An explicit rational strict kernel gives the exact mixed derivative (-1/308). Adding three independent sites yields the same obstruction in six sites.

These results do not assume full exterior positivity in general. In fact, the second result shows why one natural attempt to obtain even decomposable positivity term by term fails. Historical novelty of the restricted theorem is not assessed.

## 1. Setup and the exact Hessian target

For a fixed real table (f) on subsets, write
\[
 F_f(K)=\sum_Sp_S(K)f(S),\qquad
 q_f(K;u,v)=D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*].
\]
Each exact atom is separately affine along a rank-one matrix direction. Therefore, for
\[
 A=\lambda uu^*-\mu vv^*,\qquad\lambda,\mu\ge0,
\]
and (f(S)=-\log p_S(K)) frozen at a strict base kernel,
\[
 D^2H(K)[A,A]
 =-\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S(K)}
   -2\lambda\mu q_f(K;u,v).
 \tag{1}
\]
No normalization or orthogonality of (u,v) is needed for this identity. If the direction is specified by its positive and negative eigenspaces, orthonormal vectors may of course be used.

The odds matrix (L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0) satisfies
\[
 p_S(K)=\det(I-K)\det L_S.
\]
The constant (-\log\det(I-K)) contributes zero to (q_f), so the frozen table in (1) may be replaced by (f_L(S)=-\log\det L_S).

For reference, let (Q_S=K-I_{E\setminus S}), so
\[
 p_S=(-1)^{|E\setminus S|}\det Q_S.
\]
At a strict kernel all these matrices are invertible. The canonical exterior operator used here is
\[
 T_f(K)=\sum_S p_S f(S)\,\bigwedge^2 Q_S^{-1}.
 \tag{2}
\]
It is Hermitian, and
\[
 q_f(K;u,v)
 =\langle u\wedge v,T_f(K)(u\wedge v)\rangle.
 \tag{3}
\]
Equation (2), or equivalently the two-row/two-column cofactor expansion of the determinant, specifies the operator; this is not an arbitrary extension from real sectional values.

The available dimension-five result proves (3) nonnegative for arbitrary supermodular (f) in that dimension range. The first unresolved general projection case in the supplied sources is compressed rank two on six sites. The old-107 diamond criterion was read as an obstruction/coupling reference; no universal diamond coupling is assumed here.

## 2. A scalar Laplace positivity lemma

Let (w_i\ge0) and (w(S)=\sum_{i\in S}w_i). The following two set functions have positive semidefinite canonical exterior operators at every positive contraction (K):
\[
 f_+(S)=-\log(1+w(S)),
 \tag{4}
\]
and, provided (\sum_iw_i<1),
\[
 f_-(S)=-\log(1-w(S)).
 \tag{5}
\]

### 2.1 The plus case

The scalar identity
\[
 -\log(1+x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t(e^{-tx}-1)\,dt
 \qquad(x\ge0)
 \tag{6}
\]
and the DPP generating function give
\[
 \mathbb E_K e^{-tw(X)}
 =\det(I-K+Ke^{-tW})
 =\det M_t,
 \tag{7}
\]
where
\[
 W=\operatorname{diag}(w_i),\quad
 B_t=I-e^{-tW}\ge0,\quad
 M_t=I-B_t^{1/2}KB_t^{1/2}>0.
\]
The last strict inequality holds for every finite (t), including when (K) is a boundary contraction, because (B_t<I).

Put
\[
 R_t=B_t^{1/2}M_t^{-1}B_t^{1/2}\ge0.
\]
Differentiating the determinant in two rank-one directions gives
\[
 D^2\det M_t[uu^*,vv^*]
 =\det M_t\bigl[
 (u^*R_tu)(v^*R_tv)-|u^*R_tv|^2
 \bigr].
 \tag{8}
\]
The two minus signs in the derivatives of (M_t) cancel. The bracket is a Gram determinant, so it is nonnegative. At the canonical exterior-operator level,
\[
 \boxed{
 T_{f_+}(K)=\int_0^\infty
 \frac{e^{-t}}t\det M_t\,\bigwedge^2R_t\,dt\ \ge0.
 }
 \tag{9}
\]
This operator identity follows directly from the determinant cofactor identity underlying (8); it does not infer full exterior positivity from real decomposable tests.

### 2.2 The minus case

Use instead
\[
 -\log(1-x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t(e^{tx}-1)\,dt,
 \qquad 0\le x<1.
 \tag{10}
\]
Define
\[
 C_t=e^{tW}-I\ge0,\quad
 N_t=I+C_t^{1/2}KC_t^{1/2}>0,\quad
 S_t=C_t^{1/2}N_t^{-1}C_t^{1/2}\ge0.
\]
The generating function is now (\mathbb E_K e^{tw(X)}=\det N_t), and the same determinant differentiation proves
\[
 \boxed{
 T_{f_-}(K)=\int_0^\infty
 \frac{e^{-t}}t\det N_t\,\bigwedge^2S_t\,dt\ \ge0.
 }
 \tag{11}
\]

### 2.3 Convergence and differentiation

There are finitely many atoms, and their matrix derivatives are polynomial. Each mixed derivative annihilates both the constant table and the modular table (w(S)). Thus the mixed differentiated exponential generating function is (O(t^2)) as (t\downarrow0), and the displayed integrands are (O(t)) there.

For (6), the original finite scalar sums are exponentially integrable at infinity. For (10), every exponent is bounded by (e^{t\sum_iw_i}), and the hypothesis (\sum_iw_i<1) supplies exponential decay after multiplication by (e^{-t}). These bounds also justify differentiating the finite polynomial in (K) coefficient by coefficient under the integral. The operator formulas extend to boundary contractions by those same cofactor identities or continuity. No unproved interchange involving an infinite atom space occurs.

## 3. The diagonal-plus-rank-one log-determinant theorem

**Theorem.** Suppose
\[
 L=D+\sigma aa^*>0,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)>0,
 \qquad\sigma\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
For every positive contraction (K), the canonical exterior operator of (f_L(S)=-\log\det L_S) is positive semidefinite.

**Proof.** The determinant lemma gives
\[
 \det L_S=\left(\prod_{i\in S}d_i\right)
 \left(1+\sigma\sum_{i\in S}\frac{|a_i|^2}{d_i}\right).
 \tag{13}
\]
The logarithm of the first factor is modular and its determinantal expectation is affine in (K). It therefore contributes zero to the exterior operator. With (w_i=|a_i|^2/d_i), the remaining table is precisely (4) or (5). In the negative case, (L>0) is equivalent to (\sum_iw_i<1), by congruence with (D^{-1/2}). Apply (9) or (11). This proof covers arbitrary complex phases of (a) and arbitrary complex Hermitian (K). \(\square\)

The theorem is also valid for a block diagonal (L) whose blocks separately have the form (12), because the table is a sum of the corresponding block tables. This extension uses additivity and restriction of (K) to each block; no assumption that (K) itself is block diagonal is needed for the frozen-table statement.

### 3.1 Consequence for entropy

If the odds matrix of a strict kernel has the form (12), substituting its positive (q_f) into (1) gives
\[
 D^2H(K)[A,A]\le0
\]
for every direction with at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Pure rank-one directions are included.

There is a useful equivalent sufficient description in terms of the kernel itself. Suppose
\[
 K=C+\tau bb^*,\qquad
 C=\operatorname{diag}(c_i),\quad0<c_i<1,\quad0<K<I,
 \tag{14}
\]
where (\tau\in\mathbb R). Let
\[
 d=(I-C)^{-1}b,\qquad r=b^*(I-C)^{-1}b.
\]
Sherman–Morrison gives
\[
 L=K(I-K)^{-1}
 =C(I-C)^{-1}+\frac{\tau}{1-\tau r}\,dd^*.
 \tag{15}
\]
The denominator is positive: this follows from (I-K>0) when (\tau\ge0), and is immediate when (\tau<0). The first term in (15) is positive diagonal; absorb the magnitude of the second coefficient into (d) to obtain (12). Thus (14) is covered in every dimension.

This is a **pointwise Hessian result at the specified kernels**. It is not a claim about every line through such a kernel after the line leaves the class.

### 3.2 A finite-segment corollary that stays within the class

Fix a diagonal (C), an original coordinate (i), and a vector (b). Let (x(t),y(t)) be affine on ([0,1]), and suppose
\[
 K(t)=C+x(t)e_ie_i^*+y(t)bb^*,
 \tag{16}
\]
is a positive contraction and its diagonal background (C+x(t)e_ie_i^*) is itself a diagonal positive contraction. If
\[
 x'(t)y'(t)\le0,
 \tag{17}
\]
then (H(P_{K(t)})) is concave in (t).

For strict kernels and strict diagonal backgrounds, every point of the segment satisfies (14). The direction is
\(A=x'e_ie_i^*+y'bb^*\), and direct separate affinity gives
\[
 H''(t)=-\mathcal I(t)+2x'y'\,q_{f_t}(K(t);e_i,b)\le0.
\]
For closed contractions/backgrounds, replace (K(t)) by ((1-2\varepsilon)K(t)+\varepsilon I). Its background becomes strict, its rank-one coefficient is multiplied by (1-2\varepsilon), and (17) is preserved. Integrate and pass to the limit using continuity of finite-law entropy. This proves a genuine all-dimensional segment statement, while retaining the structural restrictions in (16).

## 4. Why the direct matrix-resolvent integrand fails

The matrix logarithm identity gives
\[
 -\log\det L_S
 =\int_0^\infty\left[
 \operatorname{tr}(L_S+sI_S)^{-1}-\frac{|S|}{1+s}
 \right]ds.
 \tag{18}
\]
Since (|S|) is modular, a tempting sufficient lemma is
\[
 q_{g_s}(K;u,v)\ge0,
 \qquad g_s(S)=\operatorname{tr}(L_S+sI_S)^{-1},
 \tag{19}
\]
with (L=K(I-K)^{-1}). That lemma is false even for decomposable (u\wedge v), as follows.

### 4.1 Exact strict three-site example

Set
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}
 6&-5&5\\
 -5&6&-5\\
 5&-5&10
 \end{pmatrix}
 =\operatorname{diag}(1,1,5)
   +5(1,-1,1)^T(1,-1,1)>0.
 \tag{20}
\]
The linked strict kernel is
\[
 K=L(I+L)^{-1}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 28/41&-15/82&5/82\\
 -15/82&28/41&-5/82\\
 5/82&-5/82&35/41
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Take
\[
 u=(1,1,0)^T,\qquad v=(0,0,1)^T.
 \tag{22}
\]
These vectors are orthogonal, and their exterior product in the ordered coordinates ((12,13,23)) is ((0,1,1)), explicitly decomposable.

At (s=0), the table (g_0(S)=\operatorname{tr}L_S^{-1}) is

| (S) | (g_0(S)) |
|---|---:|
| (arnothing) | (0) |
| (1,2) | (1/6) |
| (3) | (1/10) |
| (12) | (12/11) |
| (13,23) | (16/35) |
| (123) | (27/20) |

Consequently its relevant Möbius coefficients are
\[
 c_{13}=c_{23}=\frac4{21},\qquad
 c_{123}=-\frac{205}{924}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Put (z=(1,-1,0)^T/\sqrt2), the unit vector orthogonal to (u,v). From (21),
\[
 z^*Kz=\frac{71}{82}.
\]
The pair minors of (uu^*,vv^*) contribute one on (13) and (23). The mixed derivative of the full determinant is (2z^*Kz), since (|u\wedge v|^2=2). Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{g_0}(K;u,v)
 &=2\left[\frac4{21}
      +\frac{71}{82}\left(-\frac{205}{924}\right)\right]\\
 &=\boxed{-\frac1{308}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{24}
\]
All entries and operations in this verification are rational. Since (L>0), the integrand is continuous in (s\ge0). It is therefore negative on a nonempty interval of **positive** (s), not merely at an endpoint of integration.

### 4.2 Exact six-site embedding

Take
\[
 L_6=L\oplus I_3,\quad K_6=K\oplus\tfrac12 I_3,
\]
and append three zeros to (u,v). The trace-resolvent table splits additively between the blocks. Its mixed derivative in these directions is exactly the three-site value, so
\[
 q_{g_0}(K_6;u,v)=-1/308.
\]
The verifier independently enumerates all (64) atoms to check this equality; it does not merely reuse the block argument.

### 4.3 This is not an entropy counterexample

The integrated frozen log-determinant mixed derivative in this example is
\[
 q_{f_L}(K;u,v)
 =2\left[
 \log\frac{12}{7}
 +\frac{71}{82}\log\frac{539}{864}
 \right]
 \approx0.2608744392568578>0.
 \tag{25}
\]
It is also covered by the positive integral theorem, since (20) is diagonal plus a positive rank-one matrix. The negative contribution in (24) must be compensated elsewhere in the integral (18). Thus one must either retain cancellations between resolvent parameters or change the representation. Equations (9)–(11) accomplish the latter for the restricted class.

## 5. Two additional failed termwise routes

These observations explain choices made during the attempt, rather than adding claims about general entropy.

First, the scalar atom formula
\[
 -\log p=\int_0^\infty[(p+s)^{-1}-(1+s)^{-1}],ds
\]
does not yield pointwise nonnegative mixed derivatives even for an independent two-site law. For (K=\operatorname{diag}(1/10,1/10)), the four atoms are (81/100,9/100,9/100,1/100). In the (e_1,e_2) mixed direction, the integrand at (s=1) is
\[
 \frac{100}{181}+\frac{100}{101}-\frac{200}{109}<0,
\]
while the full integral is zero because the log-atom table is modular.

Second, a trace-power expansion of (-\log\det(I-A_S)) need not be positive term by term. For example, take a positive definite real matrix with diagonal one and entries (A_{13}=A_{23}=1/2), (A_{12}=-1/10). For (g(S)=\operatorname{tr}A_S^3),
\[
 \Delta_1\Delta_2g(\{3\})
 =3|A_{12}|^2(A_{11}+A_{22})
  +6A_{12}A_{23}A_{31}
 =-9/100<0.
\]
Scale (A) by a positive constant if a strict contraction is needed for a convergent logarithm series. The sign persists. This is not a counterexample to the summed logarithm.

## 7. Remaining obligation

For arbitrary (L>0), no replacement of (18) by a positive mixed-discriminant integral was established. The rank-one update in (13) is essential to the scalar generating-function reduction: a general principal determinant is not a function of a single nonnegative weighted count. The general decomposable inequality
\[
 q_{-\log\det L_{\bullet}}(K;u,v)\ge0
\]
is therefore unresolved here, both in its independent-(K,L) form and at the entropy-linked choice (L=K(I-K)^{-1}). Full Lyons concavity is stronger still and also requires controlling directions with several eigenvalues of the same sign.

The completed contribution is the positive integral theorem for the stated structural class, its carefully restricted entropy consequences, and an exact decomposable counterexample to the direct matrix-resolvent sign lemma.
