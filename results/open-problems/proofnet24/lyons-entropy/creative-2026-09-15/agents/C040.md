## 1. General projection-block substitution

Let the old ground set be \(E\). For each \(e\in E\), choose a disjoint finite coordinate block \(F_e\), a subspace \(L_e\subseteq\mathbb C^{F_e}\) of dimension \(\ell_e\), and a unit vector \(z_e\perp L_e\). A site that is to remain unchanged uses a singleton block, \(L_e=0\), and \(z_e=1\).

Put

\[
F=\bigsqcup_e F_e,\qquad L=\bigoplus_eL_e,\qquad
J:\mathbb C^E\longrightarrow\mathbb C^F,\quad Je_e=z_e.
\tag{1}
\]

Disjoint supports make \(J\) an isometry, and \(L\perp\operatorname{ran}J\). For every positive contraction \(K\), define

\[
K^\#=P_L+JKJ^*.
\tag{2}
\]

This is a positive contraction: its spectrum is that of \(K\), together with \(\sum_e\ell_e\) additional ones and \(|F|-|E|-\sum_e\ell_e\) additional zeros. For a projection subspace \(V\), the new projection subspace is

\[
V^\#=L\oplus JV.
\tag{3}
\]

Let

\[
\kappa_{e,0}=\mu_{L_e},\qquad
\kappa_{e,1}=\mu_{L_e+[z_e]}.
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\mu_V\) denotes the projection DPP law in the indicated coordinates.

**Theorem 1 (exact substitution law and diamond gluing).**

1. For \(B\subseteq F\), its new atom probability is zero unless every local cardinality is \(\ell_e\) or \(\ell_e+1\). For an allowed \(B\), put
   \[
   A(B)=\{e:|B\cap F_e|=\ell_e+1\}.
   \]
   Then, for every positive contraction \(K\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mu_{K^\#}(B)=\mu_K(A(B))
   \prod_e\kappa_{e,\mathbf1_{e\in A(B)}}(B\cap F_e).
   }
   \tag{5}
   \]
2. For each \(e\), there is a constructible nonnegative coupling
   \[
   (Y_{e,0},Y_{e,1}),\qquad
   Y_{e,0}\subset Y_{e,1},\quad
   |Y_{e,1}\setminus Y_{e,0}|=1,
   \tag{6}
   \]
   with laws (4).
3. If \((W,u,v)\) is a certified projection flag, where \(u,v\perp W\) are orthonormal, then
   \[
   (L\oplus JW,Ju,Jv)
   \tag{7}
   \]
   is a certified flag. Its bottom rank increases by \(\sum_e\ell_e\). An individually labeled source coupling stays individually labeled; an unlabeled source coupling retains its bottom law, top law, and correct aggregate middle law.

### Proof of the atom formula

First let \(K=P_V\), with \(\dim V=k\), and use orthonormal frames for \(V\) and all \(L_e\). In the exterior expansion of an orthonormal frame for \(L\oplus JV\), an old occupation set \(A\), of size \(k\), contributes

\[
\det(V_A)\;\bigwedge_e
\begin{cases}
a_e,&e\notin A,\\
a_e\wedge z_e,&e\in A,
\end{cases}
\tag{8}
\]

up to an irrelevant ordering sign; \(a_e\) is the unit exterior form of \(L_e\). The local exterior degrees are \(\ell_e+\mathbf1_{e\in A}\). For a fixed output set \(B\), those degrees identify **at most one** old set \(A=A(B)\). There is consequently no sum of amplitudes from different old sets to square. Squaring the unique coefficient gives exactly (5).

For a general positive contraction, choose an orthonormal eigenbasis and retain eigenvector \(j\) independently with probability equal to its eigenvalue. The resulting mixture of projection laws is \(\mu_K\): Cauchy–Binet gives its inclusion probabilities \(\det K_D\) for every \(D\). Applying the already proved formula to each projection, with the same local factors, and averaging proves (5) for all \(K\).

### A full feasibility proof for the one-rank local cover

Suppress the index \(e\). Let \(a\) be a unit exterior form for \(L\), and let \(c=a\wedge z\), a unit form for \(L+[z]\). Contraction by the unit vector \(z\), denoted \(\iota_z\), satisfies \(\iota_zc=\pm a\) and has operator norm at most one.

For a family \(\mathcal A\) of \(\ell\)-subsets, let \(N(\mathcal A)\) be its inclusion shadow among \((\ell+1)\)-subsets. Coordinate projections obey

\[
P_{\mathcal A}\iota_zc
=P_{\mathcal A}\iota_zP_{N(\mathcal A)}c.
\]

Indeed only a set in the shadow can contract to a member of \(\mathcal A\). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_0(\mathcal A)=\|P_{\mathcal A}a\|^2
\le\|P_{N(\mathcal A)}c\|^2
=\kappa_1(N(\mathcal A)).
}
\tag{9}
\]

These are every weighted Hall inequality for (6), including zero-probability sets and the endpoint ranks.

For an explicit finite construction, create a flow network with a source, the \(\ell\)-subsets, the \((\ell+1)\)-subsets, and a sink. Source capacities are \(\kappa_0\); sink capacities are \(\kappa_1\); inclusion edges have capacity two. Every cut crossing an inclusion edge has capacity at least two. For a cut crossing none, if its left vertices on the source side are \(\mathcal A\), its capacity is at least

\[
1-\kappa_0(\mathcal A)+\kappa_1(N(\mathcal A))\ge1.
\]

A maximum flow exists by compactness. If its value were below one, the vertices reachable from the source in its residual graph would give a cut of that same value, contradicting the displayed bound. Thus a unit flow exists, and its inclusion-edge weights are the desired \(\eta(Y_0,Y_1)\).

Deterministic shortest-augmenting-path max flow constructs these weights in finitely many operations, even for real capacities. With rational probability tables all operations and output weights are rational. `verify.py` includes the exact rational implementation. This is a one-rank transport whose feasibility has just been proved, not a four-marginal diamond feasibility oracle. Alternatively C033's finite sums-and-minima formula applies to the same Hall data.

### The common random map and all marginals

Independently draw the local pairs (6), once per block, and use the **same** draws at all source vertices. Define

\[
\Phi(A)=\bigcup_eY_{e,\mathbf1_{e\in A}}.
\tag{10}
\]

For a source elementary diamond with bottom \(S\) and additions \(i,j\), write \(d_e\) for the unique site in \(Y_{e,1}\setminus Y_{e,0}\). Its image is exactly

\[
\Phi(S),\quad \Phi(S)+d_i,\quad
\Phi(S)+d_j,\quad \Phi(S)+d_i+d_j.
\tag{11}
\]

The two additions are distinct because their blocks are disjoint. Equation (5) verifies every individually prescribed marginal. For an unlabeled source law, applying the same linear Markov kernel to its aggregate middle measure verifies precisely that aggregate, which is all that is required.

If \(x_{S,T}\) are the source diamond weights, the output weights have the explicit finite formula

\[
x^\#_{B,C}
=\sum_{S,T}x_{S,T}
\sum_{(Y_{e,0},Y_{e,1})_e}
\left(\prod_e\eta_e(Y_{e,0},Y_{e,1})\right)
\mathbf1_{\{\Phi(S)=B,\ \Phi(T)=C\}}.
\tag{12}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative; total mass is one. Orthogonality in (7) follows directly from (1). This proves Theorem 1. ∎

## 2. The exact entropy scope

**Theorem 2 (entropy differs by an affine term).** For every positive contraction \(K\),

\[
\boxed{
H(\mu_{K^\#})=H(\mu_K)
+\sum_e\left[(1-K_{ee})H(\kappa_{e,0})
+K_{ee}H(\kappa_{e,1})\right].
}
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** In the sampling representation (5), the old set \(A\) is recovered exactly from the output's local cardinalities. Consequently \(H(B)=H(A)+H(B\mid A)\). Conditional independence of the blocks gives the displayed sum, and \(\Pr(e\in A)=K_{ee}\). The argument includes zero probabilities and every boundary kernel. ∎

Thus for an affine path \(K(t)\), wherever ordinary derivatives exist,

\[
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(\mu_{K(t)^\#})
=\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(\mu_{K(t)}).
\tag{14}
\]

Without derivatives, the two paths have exactly the same chord entropy deficit. Concavity and strict chord inequalities are preserved in both directions on this specified image slice. This is an exact reduction, not a claim that arbitrary high-dimensional kernels belong to the slice.

For a certified flag, the familiar consequence is concavity along

\[
K(t)=P_W+\lambda(t)uu^*+\mu(t)vv^*,\qquad
\lambda,\mu\in[0,1],\quad\lambda'\mu'\le0.
\tag{15}
\]

For completeness, let \(b\) be its bottom plus top minus the two middle laws. Separate affinity gives \(p''=2\lambda'\mu'b\). Each positive coupling atom is a diamond. DPP atom probabilities are log-submodular, so \(\sum b_S\log p_S\le0\). Therefore

\[
H''=-\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
-2\lambda'\mu'\sum_Sb_S\log p_S\le0.
\tag{16}
\]

At parameters in \((0,1)^2\), every corner atom used by a positive diamond has positive mixture probability; log-submodularity there follows by limiting strict contractions. Identically absent atoms can be omitted. Continuity gives parameter endpoints. Constant boundary parameters give an ordinary affine mixture. This proves (15), and (13) transfers it with no additional conditional-entropy obligation.

## 3. Arbitrary weighted two-terminal networks realize the substitution

Let \(G\) be a finite connected loopless graph, allowing parallel edges, with positive conductances \(c_e\). Fix orientations. A vertex potential \(\phi\) has weighted edge gradient

\[
\nabla_c\phi(e)=\sqrt{c_e}\,[\phi(a_e)-\phi(b_e)].
\tag{17}
\]

Write \(H_G\) for the full gradient space. For three distinct fixed roots \(R\), write

\[
W_{G,R}=\{\nabla_c\phi:\phi|_R=0\}.
\tag{18}
\]

Their dimensions are \(|V(G)|-1\) and \(|V(G)|-3\). The orthogonal complement of \(W_{G,R}\) inside \(H_G\) is a real two-plane. Choose any real orthonormal basis \(u,v\) of that plane.

Replace an edge \(e=(a,b)\) by a finite connected network \(N\) with terminals \(a,b\) and new internal vertices. Its edge coordinates are \(F_e\). The internal vertices are disjoint from the old vertices, and none becomes a root. Let \(\psi\) have boundary values \(1,0\) at \(a,b\) and be harmonic internally. Put

\[
L_e=\{\nabla_d\theta:\theta(a)=\theta(b)=0\},\qquad
g_e=\nabla_d\psi,\qquad C_e=\|g_e\|^2.
\tag{19}
\]

The Dirichlet energy minimizer is unique. Its Euler equations give \(g_e\perp L_e\). The number \(C_e>0\) is the effective conductance. Assume exactly

\[
\boxed{C_e=c_e,\qquad z_e=g_e/\sqrt{c_e}.}
\tag{20}
\]

An arbitrary positively weighted connected network can always meet (20): multiply all its conductances by \(c_e/C_e\). This leaves its harmonic voltage function unchanged and scales its energy by the same factor.

**Theorem 3 (graphic edge-substitution closure).** Under (20), with all unreplaced coordinates left unchanged,

\[
H_{G^\#}=L_e\oplus JH_G,
\qquad W_{G^\#,R}=L_e\oplus JW_{G,R}.
\tag{21}
\]

Hence a certified flag \((W_{G,R},u,v)\) produces the certified graphical flag

\[
(W_{G^\#,R},Ju,Jv).
\tag{22}
\]

**Proof.** Extend an old potential harmonically to the network. If its terminal difference is \(\delta\), its old edge coordinate is \(\sqrt{c_e}\delta\), and its network gradient is

\[
\delta g_e=\sqrt{c_e}\delta z_e,
\]

exactly the action of \(J\) on that coordinate. Every new potential is uniquely this harmonic extension plus a potential vanishing at both terminals. The latter gradients span \(L_e\) and are orthogonal to the former ones. Imposing zero values on the unchanged roots gives the second identity as well. Theorem 1 now applies. ∎

The conductance equality is necessary for this particular isometric proof. Without it, the corresponding old coordinate is rescaled, and originally orthogonal updates need not stay orthogonal. Nested graphical supports alone do not replace (20)–(21).

### The correct weighted forest and tree laws

For the local network, let

\[
Z_0=\sum_{F\text{ spanning two-forest separating }a,b}\prod_{f\in F}d_f,
\qquad
Z_1=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\prod_{f\in T}d_f.
\tag{23}
\]

Then the two local projection laws in Theorem 1 are exactly

\[
\kappa_0(F)=\frac{\prod_{f\in F}d_f}{Z_0},\qquad
\kappa_1(T)=\frac{\prod_{f\in T}d_f}{Z_1},\qquad
C_e=\frac{Z_1}{Z_0}.
\tag{24}
\]

Here is a determinant proof, fixing every normalization. A basis for \(L_e\) is the weighted incidence rows of the internal vertices. Its maximal incidence minors are \(\pm1\) precisely on spanning forests with two components, each containing one terminal, and zero otherwise. This follows by successively removing a leaf in each rooted component; a cycle or a root-free component instead gives dependent columns or rows. Cauchy–Binet makes its Gram determinant equal \(Z_0\). The same argument for incidence rows with only terminal \(b\) removed gives \(Z_1\), and its row span is \(L_e+[z_e]\).

Finally, in the grounded Laplacian, eliminate the internal-vertex block. Its determinant is \(Z_0\), and the remaining scalar Schur complement is the energy of the harmonic voltage \(1,0\), namely \(C_e\). Thus \(Z_1=Z_0C_e\). This proves (24) directly.

The old three-root bottom law is likewise the positive-conductance law on spanning three-forests with one root in every component, and the old top law is the spanning-tree law. Under (20), formula (5) gives their exact new weights: an occupied old edge contributes \(c_e/Z_1=1/Z_0\), and an absent old edge contributes the same normalization \(1/Z_0\). Consequently both global partition functions multiply by \(Z_0\) per replacement. This is a determinant-probability statement, not merely a matroid support statement.

The two middle laws in (22) are the actual projection laws of the harmonic update vectors. They need not be the laws obtained by independently merging two different pairs of roots; those update directions generally are not orthogonal. No such identification is used here.

## 4. The resulting unconditional graphical family

Start with **any** positively weighted connected graph on four vertices, choose three roots, and allow parallel edges. Its pinned-root space has rank one. Every orthonormal basis of its two-dimensional update plane therefore has an unlabeled diamond coupling by the prior rank-one theorem. A self-contained construction of that seed is recalled below.

Replace any edges by arbitrary two-terminal networks satisfying (20), and repeat finitely many times. Theorem 3 proves a genuine projection diamond for every resulting three-root graphical flag and every orthonormal basis of its update plane. The latter assertion holds because (21) identifies the whole update plane isometrically with the original one; any final basis pulls back to an original basis.

The bottom rank and top rank become \(|V|-3\) and \(|V|-1\), with no dimension bound. Networks can have arbitrarily many internal vertices and cycles. For all these flags, (15) is concave, and its exact entropy differs from that of the four-vertex seed by an affine term. This adds a precisely characterized family of high-rank graphic kernels; it does not settle graphs that do not admit the specified substitution construction.

Complementation gives the analogous weighted cographic family. Algebraically, on each block,

\[
I-K^\#=P_{L^\dagger}+J(I-K)J^*,\qquad
L_e^\dagger=(L_e+[z_e])^\perp.
\tag{25}
\]

Thus the same theorem applies on the dual side. Set complementation sends each positive diamond to a positive diamond with bottom and top reversed. The cographic bottom and top are \(H_G^\perp\) and \(W_{G,R}^\perp\). Their subset laws are complements of the corresponding weighted graphic laws; equivalently their matroid basis weights use reciprocal conductances, up to the common product of all conductances. Entropy is unchanged by complementation.

For a real graphical flag, the standard equalization

\[
u'=(u+iv)/\sqrt2,\qquad v'=(u-iv)/\sqrt2
\tag{26}
\]

makes the two middle laws equal to their original average. A fair ordering of the two added sites therefore gives all four individually correct marginals for this rotated update basis. Arbitrary original real middle labels are not asserted.

### Why the rank-one seed has a constructive coupling

Let \(w,u,v\) be real or complex orthonormal vectors, with bottom \([w]\), added plane \(U=[u,v]\), and top \(V=[w,u,v]\). Apply the one-rank Hall construction in (9) to obtain a coupling \(B\subset T\) of \(\mu_U\) on pairs and \(\mu_V\) on triples. Make the diamond bottom \(T\setminus B\) and added pair \(B\).

Its bottom singleton marginal is

\[
\Pr(T\setminus B=\{k\})
=\Pr(k\in T)-\Pr(k\in B)=|w_k|^2.
\]

For a fixed pair \(M\), its aggregate middle mass is

\[
\Pr(M\subset T)-\Pr(B=M)
=\det(P_V)_M-\det(P_U)_M
=\mu_{[w,u]}(M)+\mu_{[w,v]}(M),
\]

where the last equality is two-row Cauchy–Binet. This proves every seed marginal. It is the established rank-one mechanism, included only to make the graphical construction fully executable.

## 5. An exact eight-edge weighted graphic certificate

Take the unit-conductance complete graph \(K_4\), with roots \(0,1,2\). Order its edges as

\[
(01,02,03,12,13,23).
\]

Use vertex potentials

\[
p_w=(0,0,0,1),\quad
p_u=(1,-1,0,0),\quad
p_v=(1,1,-2,0).
\tag{27}
\]

Their gradient Gram matrix is \(\operatorname{diag}(3,8,24)\). Thus their normalized gradients \(w,u,v\) form the required orthonormal frame, with \(W=[w]\). The top law is uniform on the sixteen spanning trees of \(K_4\).

Replace edge \(03\) by the triangle with edges \(03,04,43\), of conductances

\[
1/2,\quad1,\quad1.
\tag{28}
\]

Its direct branch has conductance \(1/2\), and its two-edge series branch has conductance \(1/2\), so its effective conductance is one. In this local edge order,

\[
z=(1/\sqrt2,1/2,1/2),\qquad
L=[(0,-1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)].
\tag{29}
\]

The local separating-forest and tree laws are

\[
\kappa_0(04)=\kappa_0(43)=1/2,
\]
\[
\kappa_1(\{03,04\})=\kappa_1(\{03,43\})=1/4,
\qquad\kappa_1(\{04,43\})=1/2.
\tag{30}
\]

A local cover has these four atoms, all with probability \(1/4\):

| Lower forest | Upper tree |
|---|---|
| \(\{04\}\) | \(\{03,04\}\) |
| \(\{43\}\) | \(\{03,43\}\) |
| \(\{04\}\) | \(\{04,43\}\) |
| \(\{43\}\) | \(\{04,43\}\) |

The expanded graph has edge order

\[
(01,02,12,13,23,03,04,43)
\tag{31}
\]

and conductances \((1,1,1,1,1,1/2,1,1)\). For its bottom, use the ordinary incidence potentials \(\delta_3,\delta_4\). The two unnormalized update potentials are the harmonic extensions

\[
\widehat p_u=(1,-1,0,0,1/2),\qquad
\widehat p_v=(1,1,-2,0,1/2).
\tag{32}
\]

The Gram matrix of these four weighted gradients is exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
7/2&-1&0&0\\
-1&2&0&0\\
0&0&8&0\\
0&0&0&24
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{33}
\]

This verifies actual orthogonality to the entire rank-two bottom, as well as preservation of both update norms. The four projection ranks are \(2,3,3,4\).

The exact max-flow seed has 24 positive diamonds. Formula (12), using (30), aggregates to **68 positive output diamonds**, with minimum weight **\(1/192\)** and total mass one. Independent weighted incidence-minor computation gives the following support sizes:

| Law | Seed support | Expanded support |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bottom | 3 | 7 |
| Original \(u\)-middle | 12 | 29 |

All bottom atoms, all top atoms, every aggregate middle atom, both equalized individual middle laws, and the cographic complementary diamond are checked by exact fractions. The full certificate retains every probability, all 68 output atoms, the original 24-atom seed, the local coupling, graph data, and potential frames.

This example cannot come from any coordinate flag under a single rank-one phase unitary, even after coordinate relabeling and phases. In such a frame every rank-\(k\) projection has at most \(1+k(n-k)\) positive maximal-minor atoms, since only zero or one coordinate exchange is possible. Here the rank-four top on eight sites has \(40>1+4\cdot4=17\) atoms. This is a sharp support obstruction to identifying the example with the already proved C034/C035 family, not an obstruction to any diamond coupling.

There is also a short exact entropy formula for this example. At the replaced seed edge,

\[
K_{03,03}=\frac13+\frac\lambda8+\frac\mu{24}.
\]

The local entropies are \(H(\kappa_0)=\log2\) and \(H(\kappa_1)=\tfrac32\log2\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H(K^\#(\lambda,\mu))
=H(K(\lambda,\mu))
+\left(\frac76+\frac\lambda{16}+\frac\mu{48}\right)\log2.
}
\tag{34}
\]

For example, on \(\lambda=t,\mu=1-t\), the added term is \((19/16+t/24)\log2\), and the eight-site entropy is concave by the rank-one seed theorem. Formula (34) holds on the entire parameter square; it does not assert joint concavity on that square without a corresponding seed result.
