# C058 — every weak singleton border, with no excluded coupling directions

Date: 2026-09-15. Completed bounded constructive theorem. This strengthens the
generic-link condition of C028 for a singleton border. It does not establish
full Lyons concavity, and historical priority has not been investigated.

## 1. Result and distinction from the prior frontier

Let `0<A0<I` be an `n`-site kernel whose occupation-entropy Hessian has a
certified strict gap on **all complex Hermitian directions**. Fix `q0 in (0,1)`.
There are explicit positive radii `delta, epsilon` such that entropy is strictly
concave on the entire full-dimensional convex cylinder

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C=\left\{
K(B,q,b)=\begin{pmatrix}B&b\\b^*&q\end{pmatrix}:
\|(B-A_0)\oplus(q-q_0)\|_F\le\delta,\quad
\sqrt2\|b\|\le\varepsilon\right\}.}\tag{1}
\]

**Every border vector is permitted**, including vectors on any number of the
conditional-feature hyperplanes excluded by the generic construction. At every
point with `b != 0`, the complete `(n+1)^2`-dimensional real Hermitian Hessian is
negative definite. At `b=0`, its necessary border-direction degeneracy remains;
the finite-segment strictness conclusion still holds across that locus.

The new ingredient is an exact structural fact: the conditional Fisher frame of
a strict kernel has the complement property if and only if the kernel's
coordinate graph is connected. A strict entropy Hessian forces that graph to be
connected. This makes the singleton coupling quartic uniformly coercive in
**all** link directions at **every** nonzero link, replacing C028's cone by a
ball. The singleton Schur complement also makes the density perturbation
exactly quadratic and supplies simpler explicit remainder constants.

The already known incident-row theorem holds with `B` fixed. The present
statement permits arbitrary simultaneous variations of `B`, `q`, and `b` on
the full cylinder. The prior fixed-support theorem does not supply those
variations either. C055's endpoint class and gap are not claimed as new here.

## 2. Connected coordinate graphs and the Fisher complement property

For a strict `n`-site kernel `A`, fix any order of the measured sites. Reveal
the occupation bits in that order. For each history `h` before revealing site
`j`, let `pi_h` be its probability, `q_h` the conditional probability of that
site, and `R={1,...,j-1}`. If `J` contains the absent sites of the history, put

\[
N_h=A_R-I_J,\qquad
(w_h)_R=-N_h^{-1}A_{Rj},\quad (w_h)_j=1,
\qquad \omega_h={\pi_h\over q_h(1-q_h)}>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Later entries of `w_h` are zero. There are `m=2^n-1` histories. Empty prefixes
have probability one. Write `p_S=P_A(S)` and define the Fisher quadratic form
by `I_A(E)=sum_S (Dp_S[E])^2/p_S`. The exact conditional-score identity from
ROOT_BLOCK_QUARTIC is

\[
\mathcal I_A(E)=\sum_h\omega_h(w_h^*Ew_h)^2
\quad(E=E^*).\tag{3}
\]

For a self-contained derivation, the conditional Schur formula is
`q_h=A_jj-A_jR N_h^{-1}A_Rj`, whose derivative is `Dq_h[E]=w_h^*Ew_h`.
The joint log-density score is the sum of the successive Bernoulli scores
`(Y_j-q_h)Dq_h[E]/[q_h(1-q_h)]`. These are martingale differences under the
unperturbed law. Orthogonality and conditional variance give (3). Strictness
ensures every atom and every history probability is positive, and each
`q_h` lies in `(0,1)`.

The coordinate graph of `A` has edge `ij` precisely when `Aij != 0`.

**Complement-property lemma.** The following are equivalent:

1. The coordinate graph of `A` is connected, with a one-vertex graph regarded
   as connected.
2. For every nonzero `b,u in C^n`, some history satisfies
   `(w_h^*b)(w_h^*u) != 0`.
3. For every partition of the histories into `J` and `J^c`, at least one of
   the two subfamilies spans `C^n`.

Here is a proof accounting for complex directions. Suppose condition 2 fails
and let `C=bu*`. For the two Hermitian matrices

\[
E=C+C^*,\qquad F=i(C-C^*),
\]

equation (3) gives

\[
\mathcal I_A(E)+\mathcal I_A(F)
=4\sum_h\omega_h|w_h^*b|^2|w_h^*u|^2=0.\tag{4}
\]

Every atom has positive probability, so its derivatives in both directions
vanish. The same is true for every inclusion moment `det A_T`. Its complex
linear derivative in the rank-one matrix `C` therefore vanishes:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A_T)C_T)=0
\quad\text{for every nonempty }T.\tag{5}
\]

For singleton `T`, this says `b_i conjugate(u_i)=0`. Thus the nonempty supports
of `b` and `u` are disjoint. In a connected graph choose a shortest path
`i0,...,ik` from one support to the other. Its interior vertices are in neither
support, and the path has no chord; either defect would shorten the path
between the supports. In the order of this path, `A_T` is tridiagonal, and the
only nonzero entry of `C_T` is its first-row, last-column entry. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{adj}(A_T)C_T)
=(-1)^k b_{i_0}\overline{u_{i_k}}
\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1} A_{i_{\ell+1},i_\ell}\ne0.\tag{6}
\]

This contradicts (5). Notice that diagonal entries do not occur in the endpoint
cofactor (6), so no spectral or diagonal cancellation can invalidate it.

If the graph is disconnected, every feature `w_h` is supported on the connected
component of its revealed site. Taking `b,u` in two different components
violates condition 2. Conditions 2 and 3 are equivalent by taking nonzero
vectors perpendicular to two nonspanning subfamilies, in one direction, and
partitioning features according to which vector they annihilate in the other.
This proves the lemma.

**Why the graph hypothesis is automatic.** If `A` has at least two graph
components, it is a direct sum after a coordinate permutation. For a nonzero
direction joining two components, all component marginals stay fixed, while
the atom perturbation begins at degree two. The entropy deficit from the
product law is its relative entropy, hence is `O(t^4)`. The entropy Hessian is
zero in that direction. Thus a negative-definite full entropy Hessian implies
connectedness. This argument uses measured-coordinate components, not arbitrary
unitary rotations.

## 3. A finite spectral constant with no genericity condition

For the frame (2) at `A0`, define

\[
G_J=\sum_{h\in J}w_hw_h^*,\qquad
\alpha=\min_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}}
\max\{\lambda_{\min}(G_J),\lambda_{\min}(G_{J^c})\}>0,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\nu=\min_h\omega_h>0,\qquad L_f=\sum_h\|w_h\|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

The complement-property lemma proves strict positivity of (7). This is a
finite expression involving eigenvalues of explicit `n by n` matrices, not an
unspecified continuity modulus. Direct enumeration is expensive in large
dimension; any certified positive lower bound for (7) may be used instead.

For any frame with complement constant `alpha`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_h|w_h^*b|^2|w_h^*u|^2
\ge {\alpha^2\over m}\|b\|^2\|u\|^2.}\tag{9}
\]

To see this, normalize `b,u` and set
`J={h: |w_h^*b| >= |w_h^*u|}`. The left side is at least

\[
\sum_{h\in J}|w_h^*u|^4+
\sum_{h\notin J}|w_h^*b|^4.
\]

One of the corresponding squared-norm sums is at least `alpha` by (7).
Cauchy-Schwarz on that subfamily bounds its fourth-power sum below by
`alpha^2/m`. The cases `b=0` or `u=0` are immediate.

In particular, the Hessian of the positive quartic
`sum_h omega_h |w_h^*b|^4` is positive definite at every `b != 0`. For a
disconnected graph it has genuine nonzero degeneracies, exactly as described
in Section 2. This is an exact obstruction to removing connectedness from this
quartic argument.

## 4. Explicit cylinder radii and full affine-direction estimate

Assume the input certificate is

\[
D^2H(A_0)[E,E]\le-\gamma_A\|E\|_F^2
\quad\text{for all Hermitian }E,\qquad\gamma_A>0.
\]

Use the following data:

\[
\begin{split}
\gamma&=\min(\gamma_A,4),\qquad N=n+1,\qquad m=2^n-1,\\
p_*&=\min\{\min_SP_{A_0}(S),q_0,1-q_0\},\\
\tau&=\min\{\lambda_{\min}(A_0),\lambda_{\min}(I-A_0),q_0,1-q_0\},
\qquad\kappa=2/\tau.
\end{split}\tag{10}
\]

The scalar Bernoulli entropy has curvature at most `-4`, explaining the input
gap `gamma`. Define, for `d in {n,1}`,

\[
t_1(d)=\sqrt d\,\kappa,\quad
t_2(d)=(d+1)\kappa^2,\quad
t_3(d)=(d^{3/2}+3\sqrt d+2)\kappa^3,
\]
\[
J_0=\max_{d\in\{n,1\}}\left[
\log(2/p_*)t_3(d)+3t_2(d)t_1(d)+t_1(d)^3\right].\tag{11}
\]

The block radius and uniform quartic coefficient are

\[
\boxed{\delta=\min\left\{{\tau\over8n},
{\gamma\over2J_0},\ {\alpha\over6\kappa L_f}\right\},\qquad
c={\nu\alpha^2\over4m}>0.}\tag{12}
\]

Smaller positive radii are allowed. To specify the link radius, put

\[
L=\sqrt N\kappa,\quad M=(N+1)\kappa^2,
\quad\beta=\kappa^2/2,\quad r_0=1/(4\kappa),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=(M/2+4L\kappa+10\kappa^2)\beta^2,&
P_1&=(2L+8\kappa)\beta^2,\\
R_0&=(M/3+4L\kappa+14\kappa^2)\beta^3,&
R_1&=(2L+12\kappa)\beta^3,\\
\mathsf A&=P_0+R_0r_0^2,&
\mathsf B&=P_1+R_1r_0^2,\qquad
\mathsf C=10\beta^3.
\end{aligned}\tag{13}
\]

Every constant is positive. Finally take

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon=\min\left\{r_0,
\left({\gamma\over8\mathsf A}\right)^{1/4},
\sqrt{{c\over4\mathsf C}},
\left({c\gamma\over32\mathsf B^2}\right)^{1/4}\right\}.}\tag{14}
\]

For `K(B,q,b)` in (1), write

\[
Z=\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\b^*&0\end{pmatrix},\quad r=\|Z\|_F,
\qquad T=E\oplus a,\quad
W=\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\u^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then **every Hermitian direction** is uniquely `T+W`, and the promised bound is

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[T+W,T+W]
\le-{\gamma\over4}\|T\|_F^2
-{c\over2}r^2\|W\|_F^2.}\tag{15}
\]

It holds also at `b=0`, where the second term is zero. In particular, it
controls mixed variations of the old block, singleton diagonal, and all
complex entries of the new column.

## 5. Uniform block and frame bounds

The signed atom inverse estimate used in C028 is elementary: if a strict
contraction has spectral margin `tau`, every signed principal atom matrix
`A_R-I_J` has inverse norm at most `1/tau`. Indeed, for real `|lambda|<tau`,
split present and absent coordinates of `A_R-I_J-lambda I`. Its present block
is positive definite and its absent Schur complement is negative definite.
There is no eigenvalue in that interval. All-present and all-absent matrices
are immediate.

Inside the block ball (12), every such inverse for `B` or the singleton has
norm at most `kappa`. A prefix probability on `k` sites has ratio to its value
at `A0` in

\[
[(1-\delta/\tau)^k,(1+\delta/\tau)^k].\tag{16}
\]

This follows by applying singular-value determinant bounds to
`det(I+N_A0^{-1}(B-A0))`. All block atoms are at least `p_*/2`.
Normalized first, second and third determinant derivatives are bounded by the
three `t_i(d)` in (11), for arbitrary independent unit Hermitian directions.
For example, the trace bounds are
`|tr(RE)|<=sqrt(d) kappa ||E||_F`,
`|tr(RERF)|<=kappa^2 ||E||_F ||F||_F`, and
`|tr(RERFRG)|<=kappa^3 ||E||_F ||F||_F ||G||_F`.
For `p_E=Dp[E]` and the analogous mixed derivatives, differentiation gives

\[
D^3H[E,F,G]=-
\sum_S\left[p_{EFG}\log p+
{p_{EF}p_G+p_{EG}p_F+p_{FG}p_E\over p}
-{p_Ep_Fp_G\over p^2}\right].
\]

Here `sum p_EFG=0` removed the constant term. The absolute mixed derivative
is bounded by `J0 ||E||_F ||F||_F ||G||_F`. This also holds for the sum of the
two block entropies, since
`sum_a ||E_a||_F ||F_a||_F ||G_a||_F`
is at most the product of the three combined norms. Integrating this bound
along the block displacement, whose norm is at most `delta`, proves

\[
D^2[H(B)+h(q)][T,T]\le-\gamma\|T\|_F^2/2.\tag{17}
\]

For each fixed history the exact inverse identity gives

\[
\|w_h(B)-w_h(A_0)\|\le\kappa\delta\|w_h(A_0)\|.\tag{18}
\]

Thus every frame-subset Gram matrix changes by operator norm at most
`kappa delta (2+kappa delta)L_f <= 3 kappa delta L_f <= alpha/2`.
Its complement constant remains at least `alpha/2`.

Writing a history weight as `pi^3/(pi0 pi1)` and using (16) gives

\[
\omega_h(B)\ge
{(1-\delta/\tau)^{3n}\over(1+\delta/\tau)^{2n}}\omega_h(A_0)
\ge (7/8)^5\omega_h(A_0)>\omega_h(A_0)/2.\tag{19}
\]

For the last estimate, put `x=delta/tau<=1/(8n)` and use
`(1-x)^n>=1-nx>=7/8` and `(1+x)^{-n}>=(1-x)^n`.

Consequently (9) yields the uniform bound

\[
\sum_h\omega_h(B)|w_h(B)^*b|^2|w_h(B)^*u|^2
\ge {\nu\alpha^2\over8m}\|b\|^2\|u\|^2.\tag{20}
\]

## 6. Exact singleton density and differentiated remainder

Let `mu=P_B tensor Bern(q)` be the product of the two block marginals. For an
old-block atom `S` and the singleton bit `y`, put

\[
R_S=(B-I_{S^c})^{-1},\qquad
\rho_y=(q-\mathbf1_{y=0})^{-1}.
\]

The singleton Schur complement gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\frac{P_K(S,y)}{\mu(S,y)}=1+z(S,y),\qquad
z=-\rho_y b^*R_Sb.}\tag{21}
\]

There are no cubic or higher determinant terms in `b`. Since both block
marginals remain fixed, the entropy decomposition is

\[
H(K)=H(B)+h(q)-I(B,q,Z),\qquad
I=\mathbb E_\mu\{(1+z)\log(1+z)-z\}.
\tag{22}
\]

Set

\[
\Phi=\tfrac12\mathbb E_\mu z^2,\qquad
\mathcal R=\mathbb E_\mu\psi(z),\qquad
\psi(z)=(1+z)\log(1+z)-z-z^2/2.
\tag{23}
\]

The Fisher identity makes the leading term

\[
\Phi(B,q,Z)={1\over2q(1-q)}
\sum_h\omega_h(B)|w_h(B)^*b|^4.\tag{24}
\]

Use
`D^2(|z|^4/2)[v,v]=2|z|^2|v|^2+4(Re(conjugate(z)v))^2`,
equation (20), and `1/[q(1-q)]>=4`. Remembering
`r^2||W||_F^2=4||b||^2||u||^2`, one gets

\[
\Phi_{ZZ}[W,W]\ge c r^2\|W\|_F^2,\tag{25}
\]

with exactly the constant (12).

For completeness the error estimates below include all block derivatives and
derivatives of the varying measure. Write `t=||T||_F`, `w=||W||_F`. Direct
differentiation of (21), using the inverse bound, gives

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
|z|\le\beta r^2,&|z_Z|\le2\beta r w,&|z_{ZZ}|\le2\beta w^2,\\
|z_U|\le2\kappa\beta r^2t,&
|z_{UZ}|\le4\kappa\beta rtw,&
|z_{UU}|\le6\kappa^2\beta r^2t^2.
\end{array}\tag{26}
\]

Here `U=B direct_sum q`. For example, differentiating either of the two inverse
factors supplies at most `kappa`, and differentiating twice gives at most
`6 kappa^2`; the constants in (26) also follow immediately by differentiating
the scalar singleton inverse and the quadratic form separately. The two
rectangular copies of a link are already accounted for by `beta=kappa^2/2`.

The product atom measure satisfies

\[
|\mu_U'/\mu|\le Lt,\qquad
|\mu_U''/\mu|\le Mt^2.\tag{27}
\]

These are the signed-determinant score and second-derivative bounds in total
dimension `N`. If `r<=r0`, then `|z|<=1/32<1/2`, so

\[
|\psi(z)|\le|z|^3/3,\quad |\psi'(z)|\le z^2,
\quad |\psi''(z)|\le2|z|.\tag{28}
\]

Applying the product rule to (23), with (26)-(28), yields

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
|\Phi_{UU}|\le P_0r^4t^2,&|\Phi_{UZ}|\le P_1r^3tw,\\
|\mathcal R_{UU}|\le R_0r^6t^2,&
|\mathcal R_{UZ}|\le R_1r^5tw,\\
|\mathcal R_{ZZ}|\le10\beta^3r^4w^2.&
\end{array}\tag{29}
\]

For instance, the last coefficient is
`2 beta(2 beta)^2 + beta^2(2 beta)=10 beta^3`.
The `UU` remainder coefficient is
`M beta^3/3 + 4L kappa beta^3 + 8kappa^2 beta^3 + 6kappa^2 beta^3`,
which is precisely `R0` in (13). Thus these are differentiated analytic bounds,
not a radial Taylor estimate used in place of a full Hessian bound.

Combining (17), (25) and (29) gives

\[
D^2H(K)[T+W,T+W]
\le(-\gamma/2+\mathsf A r^4)t^2
+2\mathsf B r^3tw+(-cr^2+\mathsf C r^4)w^2.\tag{30}
\]

Use Young's inequality

\[
2\mathsf B r^3tw\le\gamma t^2/8+
(8\mathsf B^2/\gamma)r^6w^2.
\]

The three nontrivial constraints in (14) give respectively
`mathsf A r^4<=gamma/8`, `mathsf C r^2<=c/4`, and
`(8mathsf B^2/gamma)r^4<=c/4`. Equation (15) follows. At `r=0` it follows
directly from the exact quadratic density formula and (17).

All kernels in the cylinder are strict: their operator-norm displacement from
`A0 direct_sum q0` is at most `delta+epsilon<=tau/(8n)+tau/8<tau`.
The cylinder is a product of two Euclidean balls in complementary real linear
spaces, hence is convex with nonempty interior.

## 7. A full-chord modulus, including opposite border vectors

Take any `K0,K1 in C`, let `Delta T` and `Delta Z` be the block and link parts
of their difference, and let `0<t<1`. The Green-kernel representation of the
Jensen gap and (15) imply

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
H((1-t)K_0+tK_1)&-(1-t)H(K_0)-tH(K_1)\\
&\ge t(1-t)\left[
{\gamma\over8}\|\Delta T\|_F^2+
{c(1-t+t^2)\over72}\|\Delta Z\|_F^4\right]\\
&\ge t(1-t)\left[
{\gamma\over8}\|\Delta T\|_F^2+
{c\over96}\|\Delta Z\|_F^4\right].
\end{aligned}}\tag{31}
\]

To check the quartic coefficient, put `g_t(s)=min(s,t)-st`. The exact moments
give

\[
\begin{split}
{1\over t(1-t)}\int_0^1g_t(s)\|Z_0+s\Delta Z\|_F^2ds
={}&\tfrac12\left\|Z_0+{1+t\over3}\Delta Z\right\|_F^2\\
&+{1-t+t^2\over36}\|\Delta Z\|_F^2.
\end{split}\tag{32}
\]

Multiplying the second term by `(c/2)||Delta Z||_F^2` gives the first bound
in (31). Since `1-t+t^2>=3/4`, its second bound follows. This proves strict
concavity for every distinct pair, including a segment whose link passes
through zero. No one-sided cone or fixed coupling phase is imposed.

One constructive consequence is recursive singleton attachment. Starting with
any certified strict block, attach any nonzero sufficiently small column,
including a column supported on a single old site; the resulting kernel is
again a certified strict block. Its full gap is at least
`min(gamma/4, c||Z||_F^2/2)`. The procedure may be iterated with the displayed
constants. Beginning from one Bernoulli site allows prescribed connected zero
patterns and prescribed edge phases: order vertices so each new vertex has an
earlier neighbor, prescribe its nonzero earlier-neighbor column, and scale each
such column below its current certified radius. This is a constructive
corollary; no uniform scale independent of the previous attachments is claimed.
