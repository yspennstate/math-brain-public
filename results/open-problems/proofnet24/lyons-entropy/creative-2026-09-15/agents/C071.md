## 1. Statement and an easy entrywise sufficient condition

Let `L>0` be an `n`-by-`n` Hermitian matrix and set
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(L_{11},\ldots,L_{nn}),\qquad
C=I-D^{-1/2}LD^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus `C=C*`, `C_ii=0`, and `I-C>0`. A simple cycle is a cyclic sequence of distinct vertices of length at least three. Reversing its orientation conjugates its edge product. Assume
\[
\boxed{\quad
\operatorname{Re}\!\left(C_{i_1i_2}C_{i_2i_3}\cdots C_{i_ki_1}\right)\ge0
\quad\text{for every simple cycle.}\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Cycles containing a zero edge contribute zero. Diagonal unitary gauge and positive diagonal congruence of `L` leave this condition unchanged.

**Theorem 1.** Under (1)–(2), the normalized principal-logarithm table has the exact finite Boolean expansion
\[
f(S):=-\log\det(I-C_S)
=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq S\\|T|\ge2}}w_T,
\qquad w_T\ge0.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently the canonical exterior Hessian of
\[
J\longmapsto\mathbb E_J[-\log\det L_X]
\]
is positive semidefinite at every positive contraction `J`, real or complex, including boundary contractions.

At the actual entropy-linked strict base
\[
K=L(I+L)^{-1},
\tag{4}
\]
every Hermitian direction with at most one positive and at most one negative eigenvalue has nonpositive entropy curvature. If every off-diagonal entry of `L` is nonzero, every genuinely rank-two-indefinite direction has strictly negative curvature.

An inexpensive sufficient condition for (2), after a fixed diagonal unitary gauge if desired, is
\[
\operatorname{Re}C_{ij}>0,\qquad
|\operatorname{Im}C_{ij}|\le\frac1n\operatorname{Re}C_{ij}
\quad\text{at every nonzero edge.}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, each edge argument has absolute value at most `arctan(1/n)`, so the absolute sum of arguments on a simple cycle is at most
`n arctan(1/n)<1<pi/2`. Its product has positive real part. This criterion permits arbitrary support graphs and nonzero complex cycle flux. In particular it is not a coordinate-tree condition.

The size of the entries is constrained only by `L>0`. There is no normalized condition-number bound in the rank-two conclusion.

## 2. A self-contained connected-cluster sign lemma

Take any finite collection of objects, called polymers, with a symmetric incompatibility relation. Every polymer is declared incompatible with itself. A compatible family therefore consists of distinct mutually compatible polymers. Write its independence polynomial with negative activities as
\[
Z(y)=\sum_{I\text{ compatible}}(-1)^{|I|}\prod_{p\in I}y_p.
\tag{6}
\]
The empty term is one. All statements in this section are identities of formal power series near `y=0`.

For a tuple of polymer occurrences `p_1,...,p_m`, let `G` be its incompatibility graph on the labeled occurrence set `[m]`. Two different occurrences are adjacent precisely when their polymers are incompatible, including two occurrences of the same polymer. Define
\[
c(G)=\sum_{\substack{F\subseteq E(G)\\([m],F)\text{ connected}}}(-1)^{|F|},
\qquad
\kappa(G)=(-1)^{m-1}c(G).
\tag{7}
\]
The single-vertex value is `c=kappa=1`; a disconnected graph has `c=kappa=0`.

**Lemma 2.** For every such graph, `kappa(G)` is a nonnegative integer, and
\[
\boxed{
-\log Z(y)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{m!}
\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_m}
\kappa(G(p_1,\ldots,p_m))\prod_{j=1}^m y_{p_j}.}
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every coefficient of this logarithm is nonnegative.

**Proof of the graph sign.** Choose an edge `e` in a connected graph with at least two vertices. Partition its connected spanning edge sets according to whether they use `e`. Contracting `e` in the second class gives
\[
c(G)=c(G-e)-c(G/e).
\tag{9}
\]
Contraction can create parallel edges. They can be replaced by one edge without changing `c`: the total weight of all nonempty choices from `r` parallel edges is
`sum_{j=1}^r binom(r,j)(-1)^j=-1`, exactly the single-edge weight. There are no loops when a single edge of a simple graph is contracted and that edge is removed. Since contraction reduces the vertex count by one, (9) becomes
\[
\kappa(G)=\kappa(G-e)+\kappa(G/e),
\tag{10}
\]
with zero for a disconnected deletion graph. Induction on the number of edges, with the single-vertex and disconnected base cases, proves nonnegativity. In fact `kappa(G)>0` for connected `G`.

**Proof of the expansion.** First use positive formal activities `z_p`. Put
`f(p,q)=-1` for incompatible pairs and zero for compatible pairs. The positive-activity independence polynomial is
\[
Z_+(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}\frac1{m!}\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_m}
\prod_j z_{p_j}\prod_{i<j}(1+f(p_i,p_j)).
\tag{11}
\]
The product is one exactly on tuples of distinct compatible polymers and otherwise zero, so (11) counts each compatible family once. Expand its product as a sum over graphs on the occurrence labels. Every graph decomposes uniquely into connected components, and its edge weight and activity monomial factor over those components. Comparing coefficients after grouping the labels into their connected-component partition gives
\[
\log Z_+(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{m!}\sum_{p_1,\ldots,p_m}
c(G(p_1,\ldots,p_m))\prod_jz_{p_j}.
\tag{12}
\]
This coefficient comparison is also a direct proof of the exponential formula used here: the `1/k!` from the exponential removes the ordering of the `k` components, and each component carries its own occurrence-label factorial.

Now substitute `z=-y`. A tuple of length `m` contributes sign `(-1)^m`; negating the logarithm changes this to `(-1)^(m-1)`. Equations (7) and (12) give (8). Each fixed total degree contains finitely many terms, so these substitutions need no convergence assumption. ∎

## 3. The determinant is the required independence polynomial

Associate a polymer to every nonzero undirected edge and to every unoriented simple cycle of the support graph of `C`. Its support is its vertex set. Two polymers are incompatible exactly when their supports intersect. Their activities are
\[
a_{\{i,j\}}=|C_{ij}|^2,
\qquad
a_{(i_1\cdots i_k)}
=2\operatorname{Re}(C_{i_1i_2}\cdots C_{i_ki_1})\quad(k\ge3).
\tag{13}
\]
All are nonnegative by (2); zero activities can be omitted.

Let `X=diag(x_1,...,x_n)` and write `x_T=prod_{i in T}x_i`, with the variables in this section ordinary commuting indeterminates. The permutation expansion of the determinant gives exactly
\[
\det(I-XC)
=\sum_{I\text{ vertex-disjoint polymers}}(-1)^{|I|}
\prod_{p\in I}(a_p x_{\operatorname{supp}p}).
\tag{14}
\]
To check its sign, a permutation cycle of length `k>=2` has permutation sign `(-1)^(k-1)` and contributes `k` factors from `-XC`; the resulting sign is always minus. A two-cycle contributes `-|C_ij|^2 x_i x_j`. For a longer cycle, the two orientations pair into `-2 Re(product C) x_support`. Disjoint permutation cycles have disjoint vertex supports, and their orientation sums factor. Fixed points contribute one because `C_ii=0`. This proves (14), including all overlapping-cycle exclusions.

Substitute `y_p=a_p x_supp(p)` in Lemma 2. Every substituted monomial has degree at least two, so fixed-degree coefficients remain finite. We obtain
\[
-\log\det(I-XC)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb N^n}b_\nu x^\nu,
\qquad b_\nu\ge0,
\tag{15}
\]
as a formal power series. Its degree-zero and degree-one terms vanish. Multiple different cycles on the same support remain different polymers; (14) and (15) do not identify or discard them.

## 4. A phase version of the Perron bound resolves convergence

**Lemma 3.** If `C=C*` has zero diagonal and satisfies (2), then
\[
\rho(C)=\lambda_{\max}(C).
\tag{16}
\]

**Proof.** Set every `x_i=t` in (15). Formal determinant differentiation, or the geometric series for its resolvent near zero, gives
\[
-\log\det(I-tC)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(C^m)}m t^m.
\tag{17}
\]
Equation (15) makes every coefficient in (17) nonnegative. In particular
`tr(C^(2k+1))>=0` for all `k`.

If `C=0`, (16) is immediate. Otherwise its real eigenvalues have sum zero, so some are positive and some negative. Were
`|lambda_min(C)|>lambda_max(C)`, the eigenvalues of largest absolute value would all be negative. Their contributions would make
`sum_j lambda_j^(2k+1)<0` for all sufficiently large `k`, contradicting (17). Therefore the spectral radius equals the largest eigenvalue. ∎

Since `I-C>0`, (16) now supplies `||C||=lambda_max(C)<1`. This norm conclusion was **derived**, not silently added as an extra hypothesis. The sum of all nonnegative coefficients in (15) is consequently finite:
\[
\sum_\nu b_\nu
=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(C^m)}m
=-\log\det(I-C)<\infty.
\tag{18}
\]
The scalar series converges absolutely because `|tr(C^m)|<=n||C||^m`. For each degree `m`, its coefficient equals the finite sum of the `b_nu` of that degree. Thus (18) also justifies the summation over all multidegrees.

Now group (15) by the exact support of `nu`:
\[
w_T=\sum_{\operatorname{supp}\nu=T}b_\nu\ge0.
\tag{19}
\]
There are finitely many vertex sets, and (18) gives finite total mass. Substituting `x_i=1_{i in S}` is legitimate by this summability. Alternatively, apply (17) to the principal compression `C_S`, whose norm is at most `||C||<1`. The result is precisely (3). This completes the logarithm-table part of Theorem 1.

Notice what the proof does not require: an individual closed walk can have a negative real weight after it winds repeatedly around a cycle. The signs are established after the connected-cluster regrouping, not by asserting that all those individual walk weights are nonnegative.

## 5. Exterior positivity and the complete rank-two entropy identity

For any external positive contraction `J`, inclusion moments give
\[
F_L(J):=\mathbb E_J[-\log\det L_X]
=-\sum_iJ_{ii}\log D_{ii}+\sum_{|T|\ge2}w_T\det J_T.
\tag{20}
\]
The first term is affine. At `J_T>0`, the canonical exterior Hessian of its principal determinant is the embedded operator
\[
\det J_T\,\bigwedge^2(J_T^{-1})\succeq0.
\tag{21}
\]
Equivalently, on a decomposable vector it is the Gram determinant
\[
D^2\det J_T[uu^*,vv^*]
=\det J_T\left[(u_T^*J_T^{-1}u_T)(v_T^*J_T^{-1}v_T)
-|u_T^*J_T^{-1}v_T|^2\right]\ge0.
\tag{22}
\]
The operator formula (21), rather than only its decomposable tests, proves positive semidefiniteness. Its determinant-cofactor continuation is polynomial in `J_T`, so approximation proves the same assertion at singular positive semidefinite compressions. The nonnegative finite sum in (20) proves the exterior assertion in Theorem 1.

At (4), every atom is positive and
\[
p_S=\frac{\det L_S}{\det(I+L)}.
\]
Freeze this table at the base. For an arbitrary Hermitian direction `A`, let
\[
I_K(A)=\sum_S\frac{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2}{p_S}.
\]
The **complete** entropy identity is
\[
H''_K(A)=-I_K(A)+D^2F_L(K)[A,A].
\tag{23}
\]
No odds derivative is taken inside the frozen table. If
`A=alpha uu*−beta vv*`, with `alpha,beta>=0`, rank-one affinity of every principal determinant removes its two repeated rank-one derivatives. Equations (20)–(23) give
\[
\boxed{
H''_K(A)=-I_K(A)-2\alpha\beta\sum_Tw_T\det K_T
\left[(u_T^*K_T^{-1}u_T)(v_T^*K_T^{-1}v_T)
-|u_T^*K_T^{-1}v_T|^2\right]\le0.}
\tag{24}
\]
Every Hermitian direction of the stated inertia has such a representation. Rank-one and zero directions are included.

For pairs,
\[
w_{\{i,j\}}=-\log(1-|C_{ij}|^2),
\]
and the determinant factor in (22) is
`|u_i v_j-u_j v_i|^2`, independent of `K`. In particular,
\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge I_K(A)+2\alpha\beta\sum_{i<j}
[-\log(1-|C_{ij}|^2)]|u_i v_j-u_j v_i|^2.}
\tag{25}
\]
If every off-diagonal entry is nonzero and `u,v` are independent, some wedge coordinate is nonzero and every pair coefficient is positive. This proves strictness for every genuinely rank-two-indefinite direction. The argument does not sign arbitrary same-sign directional pairs and does not by itself prove a full-Hessian theorem.

## 6. The inherited Fisher budget now applies to complex phase bases

This section extends the **base scope** of C054's Fisher argument using the new coefficients (3). The score and inertia inequalities themselves are prior constructions, included here with their full allocation so the consequence is unambiguous.

Let `m>=1` bound both the positive and negative inertia of an arbitrary Hermitian `A`, and put `gamma_m=1-1/m`. Define
\[
\mathcal B_K=\sum_Tw_T(1-\det K_T)=f(E)-\mathbb E_K f(X).
\tag{26}
\]
Then, for every base in Theorem 1,
\[
\boxed{
H''_K(A)\le-[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B_K]I_K(A),\qquad
\mathcal B_K\le\sum_i(1-K_{ii})\log\bigl(L_{ii}(L^{-1})_{ii}\bigr).}
\tag{27}
\]
This remains valid for complex `L` satisfying (2); no real/imaginary decomposition of the direction is assumed.

Here is the complete budget proof. Set
`G_T=det K_T`, `g_T=DG_T[A]`, `V_T=K_T^(-1/2)A_TK_T^(-1/2)`. Compression and congruence cannot increase either inertia. If the positive eigenvalues of `V_T` sum to `P` and the absolute negative ones sum to `N`, Cauchy–Schwarz separately on their lists gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)\ge(P^2+N^2)/m\ge(P-N)^2/m.
\]
The full atom score has mean zero and variance `I_K(A)`, while
`g_T` is its covariance with the inclusion-event indicator. Therefore
\[
g_T^2\le I_K(A)G_T(1-G_T),\qquad
G_T''=G_T[(\operatorname{tr}V_T)^2-\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)].
\]
The exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
-H''_K(A)={}&[1-\gamma_m\mathcal B_K]I_K(A)\\
&+\sum_Tw_T\left\{
\gamma_m\frac{I_K(A)G_T(1-G_T)-g_T^2}{G_T}
+G_T\left[\operatorname{tr}(V_T^2)-\frac{(\operatorname{tr}V_T)^2}{m}\right]
\right\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
proves the first inequality in (27). Its braces are nonnegative and its total Fisher expenditure is precisely `gamma_m B_K`.

For the second inequality, adding a coordinate `i` to the normalized log table changes it by the logarithm of `L_ii` divided by its Schur-complement pivot. The pivot decreases as conditioning coordinates are added: one additional coordinate subtracts a positive semidefinite rank-one matrix from the Schur complement. Thus every such increment is at most its terminal increment
`delta_i=log(L_ii (L^(-1))_ii)`. Adding the missing coordinates to `S` gives
`f(E)-f(S)<=sum_{i notin S}delta_i`; taking expectations proves (27).

The unrestricted-rank consequence needs the explicit budget condition `gamma_m B_K<=1`. The new phase theorem alone does not remove that condition.

## 7. A dense complex six-site interval with a uniform rational gap

Let `n=6` and define
\[
C_{ii}=0,\qquad C_{ij}=\frac{12+i}{72}\quad(i<j),\qquad
C_{ji}=\overline{C_{ij}},\quad L=I-C,\quad K=L(I+L)^{-1}.
\tag{29}
\]
Here the `i` in `12+i` denotes the imaginary unit. Let
\[
u=(1,1,1,1,1,1)^T,\qquad
v=(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1)^T,\qquad A=uu^*-vv^*.
\tag{30}
\]
The two vectors are orthogonal with squared norms six. Hence `A` has exactly two nonzero eigenvalues, `6` and `−6`, and `||A||=6`. Define the actual affine interval
\[
\boxed{K(t)=K+tA,\qquad |t|\le1/6000.}
\tag{31}
\]

**Proposition 4.** Every kernel in (31) is a strict contraction with odds satisfying (2), and throughout the whole interval,
\[
\boxed{\quad -\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H(P_{K(t)})\ge\frac{16200}{9409}>0.\quad}
\tag{32}
\]
In particular the endpoint midpoint gap is at least
\[
\boxed{
H(P_K)-\tfrac12H(P_{K-A/6000})-\tfrac12H(P_{K+A/6000})
\ge\frac9{376360000}.}
\tag{33}
\]

**Proof.** The row-sum norm estimate gives
\[
\|C\|\le5\sqrt{145}/72<65/72.
\]
Consequently the eigenvalues of `L` lie between `7/72` and `137/72`, and those of `K` between `7/79` and `137/209`. The perturbation norm is at most `1/1000`, so every kernel in (31) remains a strict contraction.

Write `R=(I-K)^(-1)=I+L`. Then `||R||<3`, and the resolvent identity gives, for the odds `L(t)=(I-K-tA)^(-1)-I`,
\[
\|L(t)-L\|
\le\frac{|t|\|R\|^2\|A\|}{1-|t|\|R\|\|A\|}
\le\frac{54|t|}{1-18|t|}
\le\frac9{997}<\frac1{96}.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus every off-diagonal entry `z=-L_ij(t)` satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Re}z\ge\frac16-\frac1{96}=\frac5{32},\qquad
|\operatorname{Im}z|\le\frac1{72}+\frac1{96}=\frac7{288},
\]
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{Im}z|}{\operatorname{Re}z}\le\frac7{45}<\frac16.
\tag{35}
\]
Normalizing by the positive diagonal of `L(t)` multiplies each off-diagonal entry by a positive real number and therefore preserves its argument. Criterion (5) applies at every `t`.

Moreover `L_ii(t)<=97/96`. For the normalized matrix `C(t)` in (1),
\[
|C_{ij}(t)|\ge\frac{5/32}{97/96}=\frac{15}{97}.
\tag{36}
\]
Every pair weight is therefore at least
`-log(1-(15/97)^2)>=(15/97)^2=225/9409`. There are exactly nine pairs of opposite parity in (30); on each their wedge coordinate has squared modulus four, and on the others it is zero. Equation (25), with the unscaled representation (30), gives
\[
-H''\ge2\cdot9\cdot4\cdot\frac{225}{9409}
=\frac{16200}{9409}.
\]
This proves (32). Integrating the uniform second-derivative bound on the interval of radius `1/6000` gives (33), because the midpoint gap for curvature `-c` is at least `c/(2\cdot6000^2)`. ∎

This example is genuinely complex: the product `L_12 L_23 L_31` has nonzero imaginary part. No diagonal unitary gauge can make `L` Stieltjes, since such a gauge preserves this product. For the same reason it cannot be a diagonal plus a signed rank-one matrix, whose nonzero triangle products are real. Its support graph is the complete graph on six vertices, so it is not a triangle tree. The verifier also certifies a nonreal triangle product of `L^(-1)`, ruling out a reduction to gauge-Stieltjes odds by complementation.

The line never passes through a measured diagonal kernel: `A_13=0` but `K_13!=0`. The commutator `[K,A]` is nonzero. Thus this is neither the already proved diagonal-through-line scope nor a commuting two-eigenvalue interpolation. The actual base and both endpoints have full rank, with no projection background.

### A full-Hermitian phase family near saturation

There is an additional exact consequence of (27) at the same dense phase shape. Put
\[
K_s=sL(I+sL)^{-1},\qquad s\ge360,
\tag{37}
\]
with `L` from (29). Positive scaling leaves its normalized log table and cycle phases unchanged. Since `lambda_min(L)>7/72`,
\[
1-(K_s)_{ii}\le\|(I+sL)^{-1}\|<\frac1{36}.
\]
Also `L_ii=1` and `(L^(-1))_ii<72/7`, so every terminal logarithm is below three. One elementary check is `e^3>1+3+9/2+27/6=13>72/7`. Hence
\[
\mathcal B_{K_s}<\frac{6\cdot3}{36}=\frac12.
\tag{38}
\]
For every ambient Hermitian direction,
\[
H''_{K_s}(A)\le-[1-\gamma_m/2]I_{K_s}(A)\le-\tfrac12 I_{K_s}(A).
\tag{39}
\]
The full Hessian is in fact strictly negative for every nonzero `A`. If `I>0`, (39) proves this. If `I=0`, all atom derivatives and therefore all inclusion derivatives vanish. Then each `tr V_T=0`, so every frozen term is `-w_T G_T tr(V_T^2)<=0`. The singleton derivatives give `A_ii=0`. A nonzero direction has some `A_ij!=0`; its pair determinant second derivative equals `-2|A_ij|^2`, and that pair has strictly positive weight. Thus the full curvature is negative also in this case.

This is a new complex-base application of the existing Fisher allocation, not a new claim that all directions are controlled without a budget. No global concavity of the nonlinear parametrization `s -> K_s` is inferred from (39).
