# C026 — a global mixed-sign theorem on a balanced four-site path

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a completed restricted proof, not a proof of the general Lyons conjecture. It builds on C021's replacement Hessian but supplies a genuinely determinantal bound for its mixed terms. No subagents, pushes, or external messages were used. No bibliographic-priority claim is made.

## 1. Completed result and its scope

Keep the complex middle edge (w) fixed and consider

\[
K(u,v;w)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&0&u&0\\
0&1/2&0&v\\
\bar u&0&1/2&w\\
0&\bar v&\bar w&1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]

The selected sites are (1,2), their kernel block is diagonal, their two outside rows are independent whenever (uv\ne0), and the outside block on (3,4) is coherent whenever (w\ne0). The support graph is the path (1-3-4-2).

**Theorem 1.** For every two positive contractions of the form (1) with the same (w), occupation entropy is concave along their affine interpolation. The complex outer edges may move independently, with arbitrary and opposite radial derivatives. If the two kernels differ, the entropy is strictly concave along the segment.

The theorem permits arbitrary middle-edge strength compatible with contraction. It is neither a weak-coupling theorem nor an inertia-((1,1)) direction theorem. Section 7 gives an exact segment whose direction has inertia ((2,2)), with a strongly coherent outside block, and whose kernels are not diagonal plus a signed rank-one matrix.

A stronger statement will be proved. Set

\[
a=4|u|^2,\qquad b=4|w|^2,\qquad c=4|v|^2.
\tag{2}
\]

For fixed (b), entropy is jointly concave in the two **squared** outer-edge weights (a,c), and is nonincreasing in each one. More precisely, if (F=4\log2-H(P_K)), then

\[
\boxed{\nabla^2_{a,c}\{F-\phi(a)-\phi(c)\}\succeq0,}
\qquad
\phi(x)=\frac{(1+x)\log(1+x)+(1-x)\log(1-x)}2.
\tag{3}
\]

This yields the following strong form of C021's missing cross-rate inequality. In the notation of Section 5,

\[
\boxed{
0\le E_{12}\le
\sqrt{\left(\chi_1-\frac{a^2}{1-a^2}\right)
       \left(\chi_2-\frac{c^2}{1-c^2}\right)}
\le\sqrt{\chi_1\chi_2}.}
\tag{4}
\]

Both factors under the first square root are nonnegative. Thus the two-coordinate replacement Hessian itself is positive semidefinite, before adding the favorable Jeffreys terms from squaring affine row amplitudes.

The restrictions matter: all four marginals are (1/2), the two selected-to-outside rows have the disjoint supports in (1), and the middle edge is fixed. No reduction of a general coherent outside block or a dense two-by-two cross block to this form is asserted.

## 2. Feasibility and the exact atom law

A bipartite permutation writes (K-I/2) as an off-diagonal block matrix with a triangular two-by-two block. Its nonzero spectral magnitudes are the singular values of that block. Consequently

\[
0<K<I
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
0\le a,c<1,\qquad 0\le b<(1-a)(1-c).
\tag{5}
\]

For example, after harmless phase changes the normalized triangular block can be written

\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt a&0\\\sqrt b&\sqrt c\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(I-NN^*)=(1-a)(1-c)-b.
\]

The same conditions with weak inequalities and (a,c\le1) characterize (0\le K\le I). The domain in ((a,c)) is convex: for (b>0), it is the superlevel set

\[
\log(1-a)+\log(1-c)\ge\log b
\]

of a concave function. The case (b=0) is a square.

Let (s_i=2X_i-1\in\{-1,1\}). The DPP atom law is

\[
\boxed{
p(s)=\frac1{16}
\{1-a s_1s_3-b s_3s_4-c s_2s_4+ac s_1s_2s_3s_4\}.}
\tag{6}
\]

One direct proof uses the centered inclusion moments. For a kernel with diagonal (1/2), the spin coefficient indexed by (S) is (2^{|S|}\det(K-I/2)_S). In the path there are three two-vertex minors, no nonzero three-vertex minors, and the four-vertex determinant is (|uv|^2). This gives (6), including its coefficient (ac). In particular the formula uses the determinantal perfect-matching coefficient, rather than a free four-spin interaction.

Put

\[
U=s_1s_3,\qquad V=s_3s_4,\qquad W=s_2s_4,
\qquad q(U,W)=(1-aU)(1-cW).
\]

Under the uniform reference law, (U,V,W) and (s_3) are independent uniform signs; the transformation between these four signs and (s) is bijective. Formula (6) becomes

\[
p(s)=\frac1{16}\{q(U,W)-bV\}.
\tag{7}
\]

The positivity condition (q>b) for all (U,W) is exactly the strict condition (5). Under (p), the signs (U,W) are independent with means (-a,-c), while the conditional mean of (V) is (-b/q(U,W)). The unused sign (s_3) stays uniform independent of (U,V,W).

## 3. The entropy series and its positive Hessian

The conditional description in (7) gives

\[
F(a,c;b)=4\log2-H(P_K)
=\phi(a)+\phi(c)+\frac14\sum_{U,W=\pm1}
q(U,W)\phi\!\left(\frac b{q(U,W)}\right).
\tag{8}
\]

Define, for every positive integer (m),

\[
A_m(x)=\frac{(1-x)^{-m}+(1+x)^{-m}}2
=\frac{\cosh(m\operatorname{atanh}x)}{(1-x^2)^{m/2}},
\qquad
\kappa_k=\frac1{2k(2k-1)}.
\tag{9}
\]

Using

\[
\phi(r)=\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k r^{2k},\qquad |r|<1,
\]

the sum in (8) factorizes exactly:

\[
\boxed{
F(a,c;b)=\phi(a)+\phi(c)
+\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k b^{2k}A_{2k-1}(a)A_{2k-1}(c).}
\tag{10}
\]

On every compact subset of (5), (b/[(1-a)(1-c)]<1) uniformly. The series and every derivative used below therefore converge uniformly, since differentiation adds polynomial factors in (k) to a geometrically convergent series. At (b=0) the sum is zero.

Here is the decisive structural fact. Write (ell_m=\log A_m), (T=\tanh(m\operatorname{atanh}x)). Direct differentiation gives

\[
\ell_m'(x)=\frac{m(x+T)}{1-x^2}\ge0,
\tag{11}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\ell_m''(x)=
\frac{m\{1+x^2+2xT+m(1-T^2)\}}{(1-x^2)^2}>0,
\qquad 0\le x<1.}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus each (A_m) is increasing and log-convex. For

\[
T_k(a,c)=\kappa_k b^{2k}A_m(a)A_m(c),\qquad m=2k-1,
\]

its exact Hessian is

\[
\nabla^2 T_k
=T_k\left[
\begin{pmatrix}\ell_m''(a)&0\\0&\ell_m''(c)\end{pmatrix}
+
\begin{pmatrix}\ell_m'(a)\\\ell_m'(c)\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\ell_m'(a)&\ell_m'(c)\end{pmatrix}
\right]\succeq0.
\tag{13}
\]

Every coefficient is nonnegative. Summing proves (3), as well as joint convexity and coordinatewise monotonicity of (F). This establishes the stronger squared-weight theorem.

### 3.1 Exact nonnegative decomposition for affine complex edges

Let (u(t),v(t)) be complex affine functions, with derivatives (dot u,dot v). Then

\[
\dot a=8\operatorname{Re}(\bar u\dot u),\qquad
\ddot a=8|\dot u|^2\ge0,
\]

and analogously for (c). Put (L_{k,1}(t)=\ell_{2k-1}(a(t))) and (L_{k,2}(t)=\ell_{2k-1}(c(t))). Equations (11)–(12) imply

\[
\ddot L_{k,1}=\ell_m''(a)\dot a^2+\ell_m'(a)\ddot a\ge0,
\]

and similarly for the second edge. Twice differentiating (10) gives the promised sum of squares and nonnegative diagonal terms:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''(t)
={}&\frac{\dot a^2}{1-a^2}+\operatorname{atanh}(a)\ddot a
  +\frac{\dot c^2}{1-c^2}+\operatorname{atanh}(c)\ddot c\\
&+\sum_{k\ge1}T_k\left[
(\dot L_{k,1}+\dot L_{k,2})^2
+\ddot L_{k,1}+\ddot L_{k,2}\right]\ge0.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{14}
\]

The derivatives (dot a,dot c) can have opposite signs. No termwise sign of their product is used.

This proves the interior assertion of Theorem 1. Strictness follows already from the two scalar functions (\phi(4|u|^2)), (\phi(4|v|^2)): each is strictly convex in its complex argument, viewed as two real coordinates. For boundary contractions, replace both endpoints by ((1-\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I/2). This preserves the common-middle-edge path form, gives strict contractions, and converges back to the given segment. Entropy continuity proves concavity on the boundary too. The positive-series remainder has a convex continuous boundary extension; adding the strictly convex outer-pair terms proves strictness for distinct endpoints there as well.

The same calculation also covers nonaffine schedules (a(t),c(t)\ge0) with (ddot a,ddot c\ge0) that stay in the feasible domain. The main application here is the genuinely affine kernel segment.

## 4. Conditional mutual information and the exact combined inequality

Let (Y=(X_3,X_4)), and put

\[
R_1=I(X_1;Y),\qquad R_2=I(X_2;Y),\qquad
\mathcal C=I(X_1;X_2\mid Y).
\]

Because the two selected coordinates are independent uniform and the outside marginal is fixed,

\[
2\log2+H(Y)-H(X_1,X_2,Y)=R_1+R_2+\mathcal C.
\tag{15}
\]

Deleting one leaf in (6), or setting its weight to zero in (10), gives

\[
R_1=\phi(a)+\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k b^{2k}\{A_m(a)-1\},
\tag{16}
\]

with (m=2k-1), and the analogous formula with (c) for (R_2). Subtracting these from (10) minus (\phi(b)) gives an exact DPP-specific CMI expansion:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(a,c;b)=
\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k b^{2k}
\{A_m(a)-1\}\{A_m(c)-1\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

The individual products in (17) need not be convex. What works is their sum with the extra information in (16):

\[
\mathcal C+\{R_1-\phi(a)\}+\{R_2-\phi(c)\}
=\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k b^{2k}\{A_m(a)A_m(c)-1\}.
\tag{18}
\]

The right side has the positive Hessian (13). Equation (18) is the precise combined mechanism: the two missing linear terms restore the log-convex product.

There is also a direct conditional-Fisher formulation. The outside atom with signs (s_3,s_4) has probability

\[
q_Y=\frac{1-b s_3s_4}4,
\]

and the conditional selected means are

\[
\Pr(X_1=1\mid Y)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{a s_3}{1-b s_3s_4}\right),
\quad
\Pr(X_2=1\mid Y)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{c s_4}{1-b s_3s_4}\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Consequently, if (R(x;b)) denotes the common scalar form of (R_1,R_2),

\[
R(x;b)=\frac12\sum_{\epsilon=\pm1}
(1-\epsilon b)\phi\!\left(\frac{x}{1-\epsilon b}\right).
\tag{20}
\]

Define its first derivative and conditional Fisher information by

\[
\mathcal U_b(x)=\frac12\sum_{\epsilon=\pm1}
\operatorname{atanh}\!\left(\frac{x}{1-\epsilon b}\right),
\tag{21}
\]

\[
\mathcal V_b(x)=\frac12\sum_{\epsilon=\pm1}
\frac{1-\epsilon b}{(1-\epsilon b)^2-x^2}.
\tag{22}
\]

For instance (\mathcal V_b(a)\dot a^2) equals the average, over (Y), of the Bernoulli Fisher information (dot m_1(Y)^2/[m_1(Y)(1-m_1(Y))]). Twice differentiating (15) yields the exact Hessian decomposition

\[
\boxed{
-H''=\mathcal V_b(a)\dot a^2+\mathcal U_b(a)\ddot a
     +\mathcal V_b(c)\dot c^2+\mathcal U_b(c)\ddot c
     +\mathcal C''.}
\tag{23}
\]

Equations (13) and (18) prove the stronger combined CMI inequality

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C''
&+\left(\mathcal V_b(a)-\frac1{1-a^2}\right)\dot a^2
 +\{\mathcal U_b(a)-\operatorname{atanh}a\}\ddot a\\
&+\left(\mathcal V_b(c)-\frac1{1-c^2}\right)\dot c^2
 +\{\mathcal U_b(c)-\operatorname{atanh}c\}\ddot c
\ge0
\end{aligned}}
\tag{24}
\]

whenever (ddot a,ddot c\ge0). Every coefficient accompanying a square or an acceleration in (24) is nonnegative, by (16) and the monotonicity and convexity of (A_m). Combining (23)–(24) recovers at least the four explicitly nonnegative outer-pair terms on the first line of (14). Thus the CMI term is controlled by a specified amount of excess conditional Fisher information and its amplitude drift.

## 5. The stronger Jeffreys cross-rate bound

Here is the connection to the exact obstruction in C021. For the positive law (p=P_K), let (R_i p) replace selected coordinate (i) by an independent uniform bit, retaining the other three coordinates. Define

\[
\delta_i=p-R_ip,\quad
\chi_i=\sum_x\frac{\delta_i(x)^2}{p(x)},\quad
J_i=\sum_x\delta_i(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{(R_ip)(x)}.
\tag{25}
\]

For (C_{i,\eta}=\eta I+(1-\eta)R_i), define

\[
E_{12}=-\left.\frac d{ds}
J(C_{2,e^{-s}}p,C_{2,e^{-s}}R_1p)\right|_{s=0}.
\tag{26}
\]

Replacement of leaf 1 multiplies (a) by (eta); replacement of leaf 2 multiplies (c) by (eta). This is immediate from (6), including the product (ac). Let

\[
\widetilde F
=D\big(p\,\Vert\,\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)^{\otimes2}\otimes P_{K_{\{3,4\}}}\big)
=F-\phi(b).
\]

The reference law has no (a,c) dependence. The C021 identities, or direct differentiation of the affine individual-coordinate dependence in (6), give

\[
\widetilde F_a=\frac{J_1}{a},\quad
\widetilde F_c=\frac{J_2}{c},\quad
\widetilde F_{aa}=\frac{\chi_1}{a^2},\quad
\widetilde F_{cc}=\frac{\chi_2}{c^2},\quad
\widetilde F_{ac}=\frac{E_{12}}{ac}.
\tag{27}
\]

For the mixed identity, differentiating (J_1/a) in (c) is exactly the infinitesimal replacement derivative in (26), since (R_1,R_2) commute. Thus (27) involves measured classical probabilities throughout.

Apply (3) to the two-by-two Hessian in (27). It gives

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\chi_1/a^2-1/(1-a^2)&E_{12}/(ac)\\
E_{12}/(ac)&\chi_2/c^2-1/(1-c^2)
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\tag{28}
\]

Its diagonal signs and determinant prove (4). The sign (E_{12}\ge0) also follows directly from (10), since (A_m'\ge0). Equations involving (a=0) or (c=0) are interpreted by the continuous law or limits; no literal division by zero is intended.

There is a useful quantitative version in exactly C021's amplitude notation. If (u=f_1u_*,v=f_2v_*), with real positive current (f_i), put (r_i=\dot f_i/f_i). Its Hessian matrix satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}4\chi_1+2J_1&4E_{12}\\4E_{12}&4\chi_2+2J_2\end{pmatrix}
\succeq
\operatorname{diag}\left(
\frac{4a^2}{1-a^2}+2a\operatorname{atanh}a,
\frac{4c^2}{1-c^2}+2c\operatorname{atanh}c
\right).}
\tag{29}
\]

Indeed, (28) controls the excess (chi) matrix by a diagonal congruence, while (10)–(11) imply (J_1\ge a\operatorname{atanh}a), (J_2\ge c\operatorname{atanh}c). Pairing (29) with ((r_1,r_2)) proves the sign for every real slope pair, including opposite signs. This is the desired DPP-specific replacement for an unsupported sign argument on the mixed product.

## 6. CMI curvature alone really has the wrong sign inside this family

The preceding combined control cannot be replaced by the assertion that (mathcal C) is convex along affine kernel paths. This fails even for the same balanced four-site DPPs.

From (9),

\[
A_m(x)=1+\frac{m(m+1)}2x^2+O(x^4).
\]

Equation (17) therefore gives, for fixed (0<b<1),

\[
\mathcal C(a,c;b)=S(b)a^2c^2
+O(a^4c^2+a^2c^4),
\qquad
S(b)=\frac{b^2(1+3b^2)}{2(1-b^2)^3}>0.
\tag{30}
\]

The coefficient follows by summing

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}\kappa_k b^{2k}\{k(2k-1)\}^2
=\frac12\sum_{k\ge1}k(2k-1)b^{2k}.
\]

Choose opposite affine outer edges with

\[
a(t)=s(1+t)^2,\qquad c(t)=s(1-t)^2.
\]

For every fixed (0<b<1), these are strict DPPs near (t=0) when (s>0) is sufficiently small. Their conditional MI obeys

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C''(0)=-8S(b)s^4+O(s^6)<0.}
\tag{31}
\]

Thus the obstruction is determinantal, not merely the nondeterminantal parity channel in C021.

There is an explicit rational certificate. In (1), set

\[
u(t)=\frac{1+t}8,\quad v(t)=\frac{1-t}8,\quad w=\frac25.
\tag{32}
\]

At zero, (a=c=1/16,b=16/25), and the strict feasibility inequality holds. Rational arithmetic with bounded logarithms proves

\[
\boxed{-\frac3{10000}<\mathcal C''(0)<-\frac2{10000}.}
\tag{33}
\]

The value is approximately (-0.000260463075397), while the actual entropy curvature is approximately (-0.0807219408350). This is an obstruction to the separate CMI sign, not an entropy counterexample.

For the exact certificate, (6) gives rational (p,p',p''). Sum these arrays for the two three-site marginals and apply

\[
H''=-\sum_x\frac{p_x'^2}{p_x}-\sum_xp_x''\log p_x,
\qquad
\mathcal C''=H''(X_1,Y)+H''(X_2,Y)-H''(X_1,X_2,Y).
\]

The outside entropy is constant. Each logarithm is range-reduced to (log y+e\log2), (1\le y<2), and bounded using 24 terms of

\[
\log y=2\sum_{j=0}^{23}\frac{r^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+\varepsilon,
\quad r=\frac{y-1}{y+1},\quad
0\le\varepsilon\le\frac{2r^{49}}{49(1-r^2)}.
\tag{34}
\]

The script preserves the full rational lower and upper bounds and asserts (33), independently of floating-point approximations.

## 7. An exact strong-coupling, rank-four affine segment

For (0\le t\le1), take

\[
u(t)=\frac{1+t}8,\qquad
v(t)=\frac{2-t}8,\qquad
w=\frac25.
\tag{35}
\]

At either endpoint the squared outer weights are (1/16,1/4), in opposite order, and

\[
(1-a)(1-c)-b
=\frac{45}{64}-\frac{16}{25}
=\frac{101}{1600}>0.
\tag{36}
\]

Both endpoints are strict contractions; their affine segment stays strict by matrix convexity. The outside block has eigenvalues (1/10,9/10). The full endpoint kernels have eigenvalues approximately

\[
0.0163154,\quad0.435392,\quad0.564608,\quad0.983685.
\]

The direction has the two nonzero selected-to-outside rows (1/8,-1/8), so its eigenvalues are (-1/8,-1/8,1/8,1/8). This is a rank-four, inertia-((2,2)) direction with opposite replacement-amplitude slopes.

None of these kernels is diagonal plus a signed rank-one matrix: the zero selected entry (K_{12}) would force at least one selected rank-one coordinate to vanish, but both selected-to-outside rows are nonzero. Thus the theorem does not follow from C018's diagonal-plus-rank-one base result. Nor is there a small-coupling assumption; (35) keeps a middle edge of magnitude (2/5).

Theorem 1 proves strict concavity on the entire segment. The computed midpoint gap is

\[
H(K(1/2))-\tfrac12H(K(0))-\tfrac12H(K(1))
\approx0.0263219515403.
\]

This number illustrates the result; the proof is (10)–(14).
