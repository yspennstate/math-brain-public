## 1. Atom derivatives and the full target

Let \(0<K<I\) be a Hermitian contraction on \(n\) measured coordinates, and let \(A=A^*\). At the affine line \(K+tA\), write

\[
p_S=p_S(K),\qquad g_S=\left.\frac{d}{dt}p_S(K+tA)\right|_0,
\qquad h_S=\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}p_S(K+tA)\right|_0.
\]

Every atom is strictly positive. All sums below run over the \(2^n\) subsets. Normalization and affine one-site marginals imply

\[
\sum_Sg_S=\sum_Sh_S=0,
\qquad \sum_{S\ni i}h_S=0.
\tag{1}
\]

Fix a strictly positive **product** law \(\mu\), frozen while \(K\) moves. Set \(r_S=p_S/\mu_S\). Since \(\log\mu_S\) is a modular table, (1) gives

\[
\sum_Sh_S\log\mu_S=0.
\]

The exact full curvature is therefore

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)=\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}+\sum_Sh_S\log r_S.}
\tag{2}
\]

Both Fisher information and atom acceleration occur in every subsequent identity. The product reference is used to make the reference cross-entropy affine, not to remove the acceleration term.

## 2. Integer replicas are explicit determinant integrals

The occupation generating polynomial is

\[
F_K(z)=\det(I-K+K\operatorname{diag}z)
=\sum_Sp_S(K)\prod_{i\in S}z_i.
\tag{3}
\]

For an integer \(m\ge2\), define the mixed relative replica functional

\[
\mathcal R_m^\mu(K_1,\ldots,K_m)
=\sum_S\mu_S^{1-m}\prod_{j=1}^mp_S(K_j).
\tag{4}
\]

Write the product reference as

\[
\mu_S=\mu_\varnothing\prod_{i\in S}\omega_i,
\qquad \omega_i>0,
\qquad \eta_i=\omega_i^{1-m}.
\]

Let \(z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m-1)}\) be independent Haar points on \(\mathbb T^n\), and use coordinatewise multiplication. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_m^\mu(K_1,\ldots,K_m)
=\mu_\varnothing^{1-m}
\int
\left[\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}F_{K_j}(z^{(j)})\right]
F_{K_m}\!\left(\eta\,\overline{z^{(1)}\cdots z^{(m-1)}}\right)
\,d\theta.}
\tag{5}
\]

The Haar measure is normalized. This is an integral of \(m\) explicit \(n\)-by-\(n\) determinants.

**Proof.** Expand every factor using (3). Integration over \(z_i^{(j)}\) forces the indicator of membership of site \(i\) in subset \(S_j\) to equal its indicator in \(S_m\). All \(m\) subsets must therefore coincide. The remaining coefficient is
\(\mu_\varnothing^{1-m}\eta^S\prod_jp_S(K_j)=\mu_S^{1-m}\prod_jp_S(K_j)\). This proves (5).

There is also an exactly equivalent **finite Rademacher integral**. Replace each \(z_i^{(j)}\) by an independent sign \(\sigma_i^{(j)}\in\{-1,1\}\), replace conjugation by the identity, and average over the \(2^{n(m-1)}\) sign choices. The same proof works: an exponent difference belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\), and its sign average is nonzero exactly when it is zero. No quadrature error is present.

For the ordinary power sum \(Z_m(K)=\sum_Sp_S^m\), omit the reference weights in (5). In particular,

\[
Z_2(K)=\int_{\mathbb T^n}|F_K(z)|^2\,d\theta
=2^{-n}\sum_{T\subseteq[n]}\det(I-2K_T)^2.
\tag{6}
\]

The last equality uses the Rademacher version and the identity
\(F_K(\sigma)=\det(I-2K_T)\) when the negative signs occupy \(T\). The determinant values in (5) need not individually be positive.

### Marked replicas preserve the complete kernel derivative

Each argument of \(\mathcal R_m^\mu\) can be varied independently along \(K+t_jA\). A derivative in one argument marks the corresponding determinant factor. Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_j&=\sum_Sh_Sr_S^j,
&\mathcal A_j&=\left.\partial_{t_1}^2
\mathcal R_{j+1}^\mu(K+t_1A,K,\ldots,K)\right|_0,\\
\mathcal B_j&=\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{\mu_S}r_S^j,
&\mathcal B_j&=\left.\partial_{t_1}\partial_{t_2}
\mathcal R_{j+2}^\mu(K+t_1A,K+t_2A,K,\ldots,K)\right|_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Here \(j\ge0\), and \(\mathcal A_0=0\). Thus acceleration and Fisher-weighted powers are both exact replicated determinant integrals. In particular a common-direction derivative of the ordinary relative moment
\(R_m^\mu(K)=\mathcal R_m^\mu(K,\ldots,K)\) is

\[
(R_m^\mu)''
=m\sum_S\mu_S^{1-m}p_S^{m-1}h_S
+m(m-1)\sum_S\mu_S^{1-m}p_S^{m-2}g_S^2.
\tag{8}
\]

The two marked terms cannot be identified with one another or discard one another.

## 3. A convergent continuation through order one

Choose a fixed \(c>\max_Sr_S\) and set

\[
x_S=r_S/c\in(0,1),\qquad u_S=1-x_S\in(0,1),\qquad q=\max_Su_S<1.
\]

For \(k\ge0\), define the corrected relative replica combination

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_k^{\mu,c}(K)
&=\sum_Sp_Su_S^k\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}c^{-j}R_{j+1}^\mu(K).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

In particular \(C_0=1\). For real, or complex, \(\alpha\), the binomial expansion gives the exact continuation

\[
\boxed{
R_\alpha^\mu(K):=\sum_Sp_S^\alpha\mu_S^{1-\alpha}
=c^{\alpha-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
(-1)^k\binom{\alpha-1}{k}C_k^{\mu,c}(K).}
\tag{10}
\]

This is not an inference from an arbitrary interpolation of integer replica data. It is the scalar binomial identity \(r^{\alpha-1}=c^{\alpha-1}(1-u)^{\alpha-1}\), summed over the actual atoms. Since \(|u|\le q<1\), the series converges uniformly for \(\alpha\) in compact sets. After shrinking a neighborhood of the base kernel, \(|u_S(K+tA)|\le q'<1\) uniformly. Taking two kernel derivatives adds only polynomial factors in \(k\), so the series and its first order derivative in \(\alpha\), together with two kernel derivatives, still converge uniformly there.

At \(\alpha=1\),

\[
\left.\partial_\alpha\left[(-1)^k\binom{\alpha-1}{k}\right]\right|_1
=-\frac1k\qquad(k\ge1).
\]

Consequently

\[
D(P_K\Vert\mu)=\log c-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_k^{\mu,c}(K)}k,
\]

\[
\boxed{
H(P_K)=-\sum_Sp_S\log\mu_S-\log c
+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_k^{\mu,c}(K)}k.}
\tag{11}
\]

The first term in (11) is affine in \(K\): if the reference marginals are \(d_i\), it equals
\(-\sum_i\log(1-d_i)-\sum_iK_{ii}\log(d_i/(1-d_i))\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{H''_K(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(C_k^{\mu,c})''(K)[A,A]}k.}
\tag{12}
\]

The interchange in (12) is justified by the uniform convergence above. Formula (10) also gives the requested continuation of the ordinary \(Z_\alpha\): for the uniform product reference, \(R_\alpha^\mu=2^{n(\alpha-1)}Z_\alpha\).

## 4. Why finite additive replica entropies cannot be the universal answer

**Theorem 1, four-site separable rigidity.** Suppose \(\phi\) is continuous on \([0,1]\) and twice continuously differentiable on \((0,1)\). If

\[
K\longmapsto\sum_{S\subseteq[4]}\phi(p_S(K))
\tag{13}
\]

is concave on the strict four-site contractions, then

\[
\boxed{\phi(x)=-a x\log x+b x+d\quad(0<x\le1),\qquad a\ge0.}
\tag{14}
\]

Continuity assigns the corresponding value at zero. This theorem is a necessary condition only. It does not assert the presently open concavity of the nonzero Shannon multiples in (14).

**Proof.** Continuity extends the hypothesized concavity to closed contractions. Put \(f=\phi'\). Consider

\[
K_{a,b}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}a&t\\t&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad0<a,b<1,
\]

whose atom table is

\[
(q_{00}-t^2,q_{10}+t^2,q_{01}+t^2,q_{11}-t^2),
\]

where \(q_{00}=(1-a)(1-b),q_{10}=a(1-b),q_{01}=(1-a)b,q_{11}=ab\).
Define

\[
\Delta_f(s)=f(sq_{00})+f(sq_{11})-f(sq_{10})-f(sq_{01}),
\qquad G(s)=s\Delta_f(s).
\]

Every probability triple \((w_0,w_1,w_2)\) is the occupation law, on its three possible atoms, of the two-site rank-one contraction

\[
J_w=\begin{pmatrix}w_1&\sqrt{w_1w_2}\\
\sqrt{w_1w_2}&w_2\end{pmatrix}:
\quad P(\varnothing)=w_0,\ P(\{1\})=w_1,\ P(\{2\})=w_2.
\]

Use the four-site block sum \(K_{a,b}(t)\oplus J_w\). Its positive atoms are \(w_jp_S(K_{a,b}(t))\). At zero, their first derivatives vanish. The second derivative of (13) is

\[
-2\sum_{j:w_j>0}G(w_j).
\]

Replacing \(b\) by \(1-b\) changes every \(\Delta_f(s)\) to its negative. Concavity for both base kernels forces

\[
\sum_{j:w_j>0}G(w_j)=0
\tag{15}
\]

for every probability triple, including triples with zero entries. No derivative of \(\phi\) at zero is used: those atom probabilities remain identically zero on the entire line.

The two-positive-entry and three-positive-entry cases of (15) imply, for \(x,y>0\), \(x+y<1\),

\[
G(x)+G(y)=G(x+y).
\]

Also \(G(1)=0\). Continuity on \((0,1]\) and the restricted Cauchy equation give \(G(s)=sG(1)=0\). Since \(a,b\) were arbitrary, we have the scaled rectangle identity

\[
f(cab)+f(c(1-a)(1-b))
=f(ca(1-b))+f(c(1-a)b)
\tag{16}
\]

for all \(0<c\le1\) and \(0<a,b<1\).

Differentiate (16) in \(b\) at \(b=1/2\). With \(x=ca/2\) and \(y=c(1-a)/2\), this gives

\[
xf'(x)=yf'(y)\qquad(x,y>0,\ x+y\le1/2).
\]

Any two \(x,y\in(0,1/2)\) can be compared through a sufficiently small positive third number, so \(xf'(x)=\kappa\) on that interval. Thus \(f(x)=\kappa\log x+\beta\) there.

For arbitrary \(x\in(0,1)\), choose \(\epsilon>0\) small enough that \(x+2\epsilon+\epsilon^2/x\le1\) and both \(\epsilon,\epsilon^2/x<1/2\). The four positive numbers \((x,\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon^2/x)\) form a scaled product rectangle. Equation (16) gives
\(f(x)+f(\epsilon^2/x)=2f(\epsilon)\), extending \(f(x)=\kappa\log x+\beta\) to all \(x\in(0,1)\). Integrating proves \(\phi(x)=\kappa x\log x+bx+d\).

Finally, restrict to \(K=\operatorname{diag}(t,0,0,0)\). Its only positive atoms are \(t,1-t\); the second derivative of (13) is
\(\kappa(1/t+1/(1-t))\). Hence \(\kappa\le0\), proving (14). \(\square\)

**Corollary.** No nonconstant finite additive combination

\[
\sum_{m=0}^Mc_m\sum_Sp_S^m
\tag{17}
\]

is universally concave on four-site kernels. Here the \(m=0,1\) contributions are constant on strict kernels. Indeed its separable \(\phi\) is a polynomial; (14) forces the Shannon coefficient to vanish and the polynomial to be affine. In particular every nontrivial finite truncation of the ordinary Shannon survival series \(\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_Sp_S(1-p_S)^k/k\) fails such universal concavity.

This extends the obstruction beyond an individual Rényi order. It does not prohibit reference-dependent corrections, marked derivative certificates, or an infinite resummation. Those are precisely what is used next.

## 5. Differentiated replicas give a complete series, but their individual signs still fail

Expand the reciprocal and logarithm in (2), retaining the different coefficients:

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_k,
\qquad
\Gamma_k=
\frac1c\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{\mu_S}u_S^k
-\frac1{k+1}\sum_Sh_Su_S^{k+1}.}
\tag{18}
\]

This series converges absolutely. By expanding the powers of \(u=1-r/c\), (7) expresses \(\Gamma_k\) as a finite combination of marked replicas of orders at most \(k+2\).

Unlike ordinary Rényi curvatures, this grouping is exactly nonnegative at product kernels when \(\mu=p\): all the \(u_S\) coincide and \(\sum_Sh_S=0\), so

\[
\Gamma_k=\frac1c(1-1/c)^k\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}\ge0.
\tag{19}
\]

Nevertheless, **the terms in (18) are not universally nonnegative**. Already

\[
\Gamma_0=\frac1c\left[\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{\mu_S}
+\sum_Sh_Sr_S\right]=\frac{(R_2^\mu)''}{2c}.
\tag{20}
\]

For the C046 kernel and direction used in Section 7, with its product reference and \(c=2\), exact arithmetic gives

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_0=-\frac{1447050}{753571}<0.}
\tag{21}
\]

The kernel, direction, and negative relative-square curvature are previously known C046 data. Equation (21) identifies their consequence for this newly constructed marked-replica series. It rules out a termwise nonnegative proof of (18). The replacement is to keep a finite sum and control its remaining tail, rather than assigning signs to its individual orders.

## 6. Finite rational bounds for the full Hessian

Put

\[
S_N(u)=\sum_{k=1}^N\frac{u^k}{k},\qquad
e_N(u)=\frac{u^{N+1}}{(N+1)(1-u)}.
\]

For \(0\le u<1\), the logarithmic tail obeys the elementary bound

\[
0\le-\log(1-u)-S_N(u)\le e_N(u).
\tag{22}
\]

The first \(N\) terms of (18) are

\[
\Phi_N(K,A)=
\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}(1-u_S^N)
-\sum_Sh_SS_N(u_S).
\tag{23}
\]

Despite the quotient notation in (23), its Fisher part is a finite polynomial in the replica weights:

\[
\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}(1-u_S^N)
=\frac{g_S^2}{c\mu_S}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}u_S^j.
\]

Thus \(\Phi_N\) uses only the marked determinant replicas of orders at most \(N+1\). The exact remainder is

\[
-H''-\Phi_N
=\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}u_S^N
-\sum_Sh_S\big[-\log(1-u_S)-S_N(u_S)\big].
\]

The first term on the right is nonnegative. Applying (22) only to the dangerous positive accelerations proves the finite certificate

\[
\boxed{
-H''_K(A)\ge
\Phi_N(K,A)-\sum_S(h_S)_+e_N(u_S).}
\tag{24}
\]

All terms on the right are rational if the kernel, direction, product reference, and \(c\) have rational real and imaginary parts. No logarithm needs evaluation.

### A matrix certificate for every Hermitian direction simultaneously

Fix any real coordinate basis \(E_1,\ldots,E_d\) of the Hermitian matrices, and write \(A=\sum_a a_aE_a\). Let

\[
g_S=v_S^Ta,\qquad h_S=a^TB_Sa,
\]

where \(B_S\) is real symmetric. Define its rational diagonal majorant

\[
D_S=\operatorname{diag}\left(\sum_b|(B_S)_{ab}|\right)_a.
\]

The inequality \(2|a_aa_b|\le a_a^2+a_b^2\) gives \(-D_S\preceq B_S\preceq D_S\). Let \(\mathcal H\) denote the matrix of \(-D^2H(P_K)\) in these coordinates. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H\succeq L_N:=
\sum_S\frac{v_Sv_S^T}{p_S}(1-u_S^N)
-\sum_SB_SS_N(u_S)
-\sum_Se_N(u_S)D_S.}
\tag{25}
\]

**Proof.** The omitted Fisher tail is a sum of positive semidefinite matrices. For each logarithmic remainder \(T_S\in[0,e_N(u_S)]\),
\(-T_SB_S\succeq-e_N(u_S)D_S\). Sum these inequalities. This proves (25), including all off-diagonal entries of the kernel Hessian.

Moreover,

\[
L_N\longrightarrow\mathcal H\quad\text{in operator norm}.
\tag{26}
\]

Indeed there are finitely many atoms, \(u_S\le q<1\), the Fisher tail is bounded by a constant times \(q^N\), and (22) is bounded by a constant times \(q^{N+1}/(N+1)\). In particular, if \(\mathcal H\) is positive definite, then some finite \(L_N\) is positive definite. The finite marked-replica certificates are therefore **complete for strict local entropy concavity**. This is an equivalence of local certificate existence, not a theorem that \(L_N\) is positive for every kernel, or monotone in \(N\).

The matrix jets in (25) are also explicit determinants. If \(M_S\) is the signed-column atom matrix and \(D_{S,a}\) is its columnwise derivative for \(E_a\), set \(R_{S,a}=M_S^{-1}D_{S,a}\). Then

\[
(v_S)_a=p_S\operatorname{tr}R_{S,a},\quad
(B_S)_{ab}=p_S\big[
\operatorname{tr}R_{S,a}\operatorname{tr}R_{S,b}
-\operatorname{tr}(R_{S,a}R_{S,b})\big].
\tag{27}
\]

Thus (25) is a specified finite algebraic construction, not an existential optimization over unknown Hessians.
