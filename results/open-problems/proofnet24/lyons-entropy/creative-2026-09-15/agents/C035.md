# C035 — Explicit diamonds for every Householder flag, in arbitrary rank

Date: 2026-09-15. Status: **complete new restricted family, with exact joint laws**. No universal real-frame diamond theorem or full Lyons entropy theorem is claimed. Historical priority is unchecked.

The main result gives an explicit nonnegative diamond coupling for every flag obtained by applying one real Householder reflection to a coordinate flag, in every dimension and at every bottom rank. The construction also works for every complex rank-one phase rotation, with the phase parameter throughout its physical range. For real reflections, the real-frame complex rotation followed by an independent fair swap gives a joint law with the **four individually correct rotated projection marginals**.

The input sources were `TARGET.md`, `sources/old-107.md`, `agents/C004.md`, `agents/C012.md`, and `agents/C069.md`. The cone interpretation and real-frame equalization are prior work. C004's triangle-circulation idea is used in an arbitrary-rank embedding. The one-negative-entry lift, its explicit repair, and the resulting global arbitrary-rank family are the contribution here. The root suggested the phase extension after receiving the real-reflection construction. C034 independently obtains a common-permutation, simultaneously labeled construction for a smaller universal phase range; its scope and the distinction are recorded below. No subagents were spawned or external messages sent.

## 1. The theorem and its scope

Let (E) be a finite coordinate set, with a partition

\[
E=R\sqcup P\sqcup C,
\qquad |R|=r,\quad P=\{p,q\},\quad |C|=n-r-2.
\tag{1}
\]

Let (h\in\mathbb C^E) be a unit vector and let (z\in\mathbb C) satisfy (|z|=1). Define

\[
Q=I+(z-1)hh^*,\qquad
s_i=|h_i|^2,\qquad
\gamma=|z-1|^2\in[0,4].
\tag{2}
\]

The operator (Q) acts by (z) on the line spanned by (h) and by the identity on its orthogonal complement, so it is unitary. Write

\[
W=Q\mathbb C^R,\quad u=Qe_p,\quad v=Qe_q,
\quad H=R\cup P.
\tag{3}
\]

For a subspace (V), (mu_V) denotes its projection DPP law in the fixed coordinate ground set.

**Theorem 1.** For every choice in (1)–(3), there is an explicit nonnegative probability distribution on pairs ((S,T)) with

\[
S\subset T,\quad |S|=r,\quad |T|=r+2
\]

whose marginals satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{law}(S)&=\mu_W,\\
\operatorname{law}(T)&=\mu_{W+[u,v]},\\
\mathbb E\left[\delta_{S\cup\{i\}}+\delta_{S\cup\{j\}}\right]
&=\mu_{W+[u]}+\mu_{W+[v]},
\qquad \{i,j\}=T\setminus S.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The closed formulas are in Section 4. They require no optimization and no choice of an unspecified stochastic cover.

**Theorem 2.** If (h) is real and (z=-1), so (Q=I-2hh^T) is a real Householder reflection, set

\[
u'=\frac{u+iv}{\sqrt2},\qquad
v'=\frac{u-iv}{\sqrt2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Draw ((S,T)) from Theorem 1 and independently order its two added sites fairly as ((i,j)). Then

\[
\left(S,S\cup\{i\},S\cup\{j\},T\right)
\tag{6}
\]

is a genuine joint coupling with the four individual marginals

\[
\mu_W,\quad\mu_{W+[u']},\quad\mu_{W+[v']},\quad\mu_{W+[u',v']}.
\tag{7}
\]

This family includes (r\ge2) and (n-r-2\ge2) in arbitrary dimension. It is not merely a rank-one-bottom or codimension-one-top result. Its kernels generally have no coordinate-block decomposition, although maximal-minor support is sparse. Products of unrelated Householder reflections, arbitrary real frames, and independently prescribed original real middle labels are not asserted by this construction.

## 2. All projection laws of a rank-one phase rotation

For (I,J\subseteq E) with (|I|=|J|=k), the maximal minor of (Q) has the following squared modulus:

\[
|\det Q_{J,I}|^2=
\begin{cases}
1-\gamma s_I(1-s_I),&J=I,\\
\gamma s_a s_b,&J=I\setminus\{a\}\cup\{b\},\ a\in I,\ b\notin I,\\
0,&|J\setminus I|\ge2,
\end{cases}
\qquad s_I=\sum_{i\in I}s_i.
\tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** For (J=I), the determinant lemma gives

\[
\det Q_{I,I}=1+(z-1)s_I.
\]

Since (2\Re(z-1)=-|z-1|^2=-\gamma), its squared modulus is the first line of (8). If (J=I-a+b), the determinant's only nonzero rank-one-update term is, up to the ordering sign, ((z-1)h_b\overline{h_a}). All terms involving two update columns vanish because those columns are proportional. If at least two rows of (J) are outside (I), the identity submatrix has rank at most (k-2), and adding a rank-one matrix cannot give full rank. This proves (8), including zero entries of (h) and the empty/full-rank endpoints. ∎

There is a useful **signed**, generally not probabilistic, representation of all these laws at once. Put mass

\[
w_{ab}=\gamma s_a s_b\quad(a<b)
\]

on transposition ((a\ b)), and put

\[
w_{\mathrm{id}}=1-\gamma\sum_{a<b}s_as_b
\tag{9}
\]

on the identity. The total mass is one, but (9) can be negative. For each input set (I), the pushforward of these signed weights under (pi\mapsto\pi(I)) is exactly (8): a single exchanged output has its unique crossing transposition, while the unchanged output has mass

\[
1-\gamma\sum_{a\in I,b\notin I}s_as_b
=1-\gamma s_I(1-s_I).
\]

The signed representation is used only to establish identities. The completed coupling below is nonnegative.

## 3. A signed diamond table with only one possible negative entry

Apply the signed permutations (9) to the coordinate diamond

\[
(R,R+p,R+q,H),\qquad H=R+p+q,
\]

and forget the ordering of the two middles. A transposition within (R), within (P), or within (C) fixes this unlabeled diamond. Cross-block transpositions give the following table:

\[
\alpha=\sum_{a\in R}s_a,\qquad
\beta=s_p+s_q,\qquad
\chi=\sum_{c\in C}s_c,\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\chi=1.
\tag{10}
\]

| Bottom (S) | Top (T) | Indices | Signed weight |
|---|---|---|---:|
| (R) | (H) | — | (c_0=1-\gamma(\alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi)) |
| (R-a+k) | (H) | (a\in R,k\in P) | (\gamma s_as_k) |
| (R) | (H-k+c) | (k\in P,c\in C) | (\gamma s_ks_c) |
| (R-a+c) | (H-a+c) | (a\in R,c\in C) | (\gamma s_as_c) |

Here (R-a+k) abbreviates ((R\setminus\{a\})\cup\{k\}). These are distinct diamond types. Formula (8) proves that this signed table already has all the marginals in (4), and the total mass is one. Its **only** possibly negative entry is the central diamond's weight (c_0).

Set

\[
\tau=(-c_0)_+=\left[\gamma(\alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi)-1\right]_+.
\tag{11}
\]

The capacity inequalities needed below are

\[
\boxed{\tau\le\gamma\alpha\beta,\qquad
\tau\le\gamma\alpha\chi,\qquad
\tau\le\gamma\beta\chi.}
\tag{12}
\]

For example, if (	au>0), then

\[
\tau-\gamma\alpha\beta
=\gamma\chi(1-\chi)-1
\le\gamma/4-1\le0.
\]

The other two inequalities follow by the same calculation with the other block masses. If (	au=0), (12) is immediate. If any of (alpha,\beta,\chi) is zero, the other two masses have product at most (1/4), so (c_0\ge0) and (	au=0). Thus no division by a zero block mass is needed.

For (gamma\le3), even the signed table after unlabeled aggregation is nonnegative, because (alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi\le1/3). The repair is useful in the remaining range (3<\gamma\le4), including all nontrivial real reflections.

## 4. The explicit nonnegative coupling

If (	au=0), use the table in Section 3 unchanged. If (	au>0), all three block masses are positive. Use the following completed table:

| Bottom (S) | Top (T) | Indices | Nonnegative weight |
|---|---|---|---:|
| (R) | (H) | — | (c_0+\tau=\max(c_0,0)) |
| (R-a+k) | (H) | (a\in R,k\in P) | (s_as_k\left(\gamma-\dfrac{\tau}{\alpha\beta}\right)) |
| (R) | (H-k+c) | (k\in P,c\in C) | (s_ks_c\left(\gamma-\dfrac{\tau}{\beta\chi}\right)) |
| (R-a+c) | (H-a+c) | (a\in R,c\in C) | (s_as_c\left(\gamma-\dfrac{\tau}{\alpha\chi}\right)) |
| (R-a+c) | (H-k+c) | (a\in R,k\in P,c\in C) | (\dfrac{\tau s_as_ks_c}{\alpha\beta\chi}) |
| (R-a+k) | (H-a+c) | (a\in R,k\in P,c\in C) | (\dfrac{\tau s_as_ks_c}{\alpha\beta\chi}) |

Every unspecified diamond has weight zero. The types and their indexed atoms are distinct. This is an actual finite joint law once the marginals and positivity are proved; it is not a specification of separate pairwise covers.

Equivalently, there is a short direct sampling algorithm. Draw one of the six table rows with probabilities

\[
c_0+\tau,\quad \gamma\alpha\beta-\tau,\quad
\gamma\beta\chi-\tau,\quad\gamma\alpha\chi-\tau,
\quad\tau,\quad\tau.
\tag{12a}
\]

For each block needed by the chosen row, independently draw its site with probabilities (s_a/\alpha), (s_k/\beta), or (s_c/\chi), and form the indicated bottom/top pair. A row of probability zero is never selected; this convention handles all zero block masses. Thus no state-dependent transport problem is hidden in the formula. When (	au>0), the coupling has at most (2r+2|C|+5r|C|) positive atoms; its potentially exponential ambient Boolean lattice does not affect the size of the construction.

### 4.1 Each local repair preserves all four diamond coordinates

For a bottom/top pair write ([S,T]) for its formal diamond column, namely

\[
\delta_S+\delta_T-\delta_{S+i}-\delta_{S+j},
\quad \{i,j\}=T\setminus S.
\]

For (a\in R,k\in P,c\in C), the exact identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&[R,H]
+[R-a+c,H-k+c]
+[R-a+k,H-a+c]\\
&-[R-a+c,H-a+c]
-[R-a+k,H]
-[R,H-k+c]
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

holds as an equality of signed measures on **all** subsets.

To check it without an implicit transport argument, put (B=R-a) and let (ell) be the other member of (P). The three positive diamonds have middle pairs

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{diamond}&\text{its two middle sets, with the common }B\text{ omitted}\\\hline
[R,H]&\{a,k\},\{a,\ell\}\\
[R-a+c,H-k+c]&\{a,c\},\{\ell,c\}\\
[R-a+k,H-a+c]&\{k,\ell\},\{k,c\}.
\end{array}
\]

The three negative diamonds have the same six middle sets, once each. Their bottom sets (B+a,B+c,B+k) and their top sets also agree as multisets. Thus every bottom, top, and aggregate middle coordinate cancels in (13).

Multiply (13) by

\[
t_{akc}=\frac{\tau s_as_ks_c}{\alpha\beta\chi}\ge0
\tag{14}
\]

and sum over (R\times P\times C). The central diamond gains (sum t_{akc}=\tau). An (R\)-to-(P) transposition loses (sum_c t_{akc}=\tau s_as_k/(\alpha\beta)). The other two transposition types lose the corresponding terms in the displayed completed table. The two new types gain exactly (14). This derives the table and proves that it retains all marginals (4).

### 4.2 Positivity, total mass, and all degenerate cases

The first and the last two rows of the completed table are nonnegative by definition. The three remaining factors are nonnegative by (12). Therefore all weights are nonnegative.

Its total mass is

\[
(c_0+\tau)
+\gamma(\alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi)-3\tau+2\tau
=1.
\tag{15}
\]

If (	au=0), Section 3 is already nonnegative and has mass one, including zero block masses, zero entries of (h), (r=0), (r=n-2), and (z=1). This proves Theorem 1 on its full stated domain. ∎

## 5. Real equalization gives the individually correct four-marginal law

Under Theorem 2, use the real columns (Qe_a), (a\in R), as an orthonormal basis of (W). For each ((r+1))-set (M), put

\[
A_M=\det[Q_R\ u]_M\in\mathbb R,\qquad
B_M=\det[Q_R\ v]_M\in\mathbb R.
\]

The vectors in (5) are orthonormal, perpendicular to (W), and span the same complex plane as (u,v). Linearity in the last determinant column gives

\[
\mu_{W+[u']}(M)=\left|\frac{A_M+iB_M}{\sqrt2}\right|^2
=\frac{A_M^2+B_M^2}{2}
=\mu_{W+[v']}(M).
\tag{16}
\]

Conditional on any sampled ((S,T)), the fair ordering in (6) gives each middle label half the sum of its two middle indicators. By (4) and (16), each labeled marginal is exactly the correct projection law in (7). The bottom and top marginals are already correct, and the top subspace is unchanged by (5). This proves the complete joint-coupling assertion of Theorem 2. ∎

The same argument applies to (z=-1) with complex (h) after harmless row and column phases make (h) real. Specifically, write (h_i=e^{i\phi_i}|h_i|); first replace (u,v) by (e^{i\phi_p}u,e^{i\phi_q}v) in (5). Coordinate row phases do not change the projection laws. The completely real version is the main assigned target.

## 7. A precise limitation of fair equalization for the complex extension

The phase extension in Theorem 1 is unlabeled. It does not justify applying (5) to every complex frame. Indeed, equalization can fail even in this family.

For a fixed middle set (M), let ((A_M,B_M)) be its two amplitudes in an orthonormal update basis. Define its real vector

\[
v_M=\left(2\Re(A_M\overline{B_M}),\;2\Im(A_M\overline{B_M}),\;|A_M|^2-|B_M|^2\right).
\tag{20}
\]

If one (U(2)) basis change makes the two middle laws equal for every (M), there is a nonzero real vector (n) with (n\cdot v_M=0) for every (M). To see this, conjugate (operatorname{diag}(1,-1)) by that basis change: its traceless Hermitian coefficient vector is a unit vector in the three-dimensional space of traceless Hermitian (2\times2) matrices, and each equality of squared amplitudes says its pairing with the corresponding amplitude matrix is zero. The possible sign convention on the second coordinate of (20) does not affect linear independence.

For the six-site example with uniform (h), (R=01), (P=23), and (z=(-3+4i)/5), three exact middle vectors are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
M&v_x&v_y&v_z\\\hline
024&0&0&4/45\\
014&8/45&0&0\\
012&0&-4/15&1/9.
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]

Their determinant is (-128/30375\ne0). Consequently **no orthonormal basis of this update plane equalizes all its middle probabilities**. Theorem 1 nevertheless gives its explicit nonnegative unlabeled diamond coupling. This is an exact obstruction to extending the real fair-equalization step unchanged to the whole phase family; it is not an obstruction to the unlabeled theorem and not a counterexample to Lyons concavity. `equalization_obstruction.json` verifies the amplitudes and determinant exactly.

## 8. A universal signed exterior lift: the remaining general obstruction

There is also a compact signed solution for an arbitrary real or complex orthonormal frame, with no Householder assumption. This identifies exactly what the special construction repairs.

Let (w_1,\ldots,w_r,u,v) be orthonormal and write

\[
a=w_1\wedge\cdots\wedge w_r,\quad
d=u\wedge v,\quad c=a\wedge d.
\]

For (|S|=r), (J=\{i,j\}\subseteq E\setminus S), and (T=S\cup J), let (epsilon(S,J)) be the sign that sorts the concatenated increasing lists (S,J). Define the real number

\[
\boxed{x^{\mathrm{ext}}_{S,T}
=\Re\!\left(a_S\overline{c_T}\,\epsilon(S,J)d_J\right).}
\tag{22}
\]

**Proposition 3.** The signed table (22) has the correct bottom and top laws, the correct aggregate middle law, and total mass one. Its added-pair marginal is also exactly (|d_J|^2).

**Proof.** The wedge expansion (c=a\wedge d) gives the top marginal:

\[
\sum_{S\subset T}x^{\mathrm{ext}}_{S,T}
=\Re(c_T\overline{c_T})=|c_T|^2.
\]

Because (a) and (d) are unit decomposable forms on orthogonal subspaces, contracting (c) against (d) gives (a), and contracting (c) against (a) gives (d), with the sorting signs in (22). The conjugate of each contraction identity therefore gives, respectively,

\[
\sum_{T\supset S}x^{\mathrm{ext}}_{S,T}=|a_S|^2,
\qquad
\sum_{S\cap J=\varnothing}x^{\mathrm{ext}}_{S,S\cup J}=|d_J|^2.
\tag{23}
\]

For completeness, the middle identity follows by writing out one contraction. For (|M|=r+1), let (A_M=(a\wedge u)_M) and (B_M=(a\wedge v)_M). The exterior expansions and right contractions yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_M&=\sum_{i\in M}\epsilon(M-i,i)a_{M-i}u_i,
&A_M&=\sum_{j\notin M}\epsilon(M,j)c_{M+j}\overline{v_j},\\
B_M&=\sum_{i\in M}\epsilon(M-i,i)a_{M-i}v_i,
&B_M&=-\sum_{j\notin M}\epsilon(M,j)c_{M+j}\overline{u_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

Multiply each left expansion by the conjugate of its right expansion and add. The result is

\[
|A_M|^2+|B_M|^2
=\sum_{i\in M,j\notin M}
\epsilon(M-i,i)\epsilon(M,j)
a_{M-i}\overline{c_{M+j}}(u_iv_j-v_iu_j).
\tag{25}
\]

The orientation factor on each term is exactly the one in (22), whether (i<j) or (i>j). Taking real parts identifies the right side with the sum of all diamond weights incident to (M), proving the aggregate middle marginal. Summing the bottom marginals gives mass one. ∎

The real part in (22) is unnecessary for a real frame. This formula does **not** solve the positivity problem: already (17) has minimum signed exterior weight (-1/27). An exact six-site real frame formed by the product of two rational Householder reflections has minimum (-273/20000), with every signed marginal in Proposition 3 nevertheless exact. Its full matrix and signed table are saved in `signed_exterior_example.json`.

Thus, for a general frame, the exact unresolved step remains finding a kernel correction to (22) that makes every weight nonnegative. Equalizing real middles supplies the labels only after that nonnegative correction exists. For the rank-one phase family, Sections 3–4 supply such a correction explicitly by first choosing the particularly sparse signed transposition lift. No universal capacity inequality for arbitrary frames is established here.

## 9. Entropy implications and relation to the other workers

Theorem 1 implies

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_W}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[u,v]}}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[u]}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{W+[v]}}f\ge0
\tag{26}
\]

for every supermodular (f), simply by averaging its elementary diamond inequalities. This is the old-107 interpretation applied to the new family.

For any fixed frame in Theorem 1, the argument in C012, Section 6, consequently proves entropy concavity along

\[
K(t)=P_W+\lambda(t)uu^*+\mu(t)vv^*,
\quad \lambda'\mu'\le0,
\tag{27}
\]

where (lambda,\mu) are affine and take values in ([0,1]). Indeed, with (b=\mu_W+\mu_{W+[u,v]}-\mu_{W+[u]}-\mu_{W+[v]}), one has (p''=2\lambda'\mu'b). The DPP atom law is log-submodular, so the explicit coupling gives (sum b_S\log p_S\le0), and both terms of

\[
H''=-\sum\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
-2\lambda'\mu'\sum b_S\log p_S
\]

are nonpositive. On boundary-supported faces, the positive mixture coefficients ensure that every positively weighted diamond has four positive atoms; then continuity handles parameter endpoints exactly as in C012. This is a restricted physical entropy theorem, not general joint concavity for arbitrary slopes.

There is also a useful upper half of C069's equal-middle entropy criterion. For any real frame that has a diamond coupling, let (h_0,h_1,h_2) be the entropies of its bottom, equalized middle, and top laws. At (lambda=\mu=1/2), pairing the log-submodular mixture atom law with the coupling gives

\[
2h_1-h_0-h_2\le2\log2.
\tag{28}
\]

Indeed, the rank coefficients are (1/4,1/2,1/4), and the alternating log expectation is (2h_1-h_0-h_2-2\log2\le0). No lower bound (2h_1-h_0-h_2\ge0) is proved for arbitrary intermediate ranks; therefore C069's full-square sufficient criterion is not asserted to follow here.

C034 independently gives a stronger **simultaneously labeled common-permutation** realization of all projection laws of a rank-one phase rotation when (gamma\le3). That theorem has greater simultaneous scope. The present theorem reaches all (gamma\le4) for each fixed unlabeled diamond and gives the real-reflection fair-label consequence. These are distinct assertions. For example, at the uniform six-site reflection (17), no one random permutation realizes every projection law: the zero minors in (8) force any supported permutation to be an identity, a transposition, or one three-cycle, hence to move at most three coordinates, but the singleton laws require an expected (6(1-4/9)=10/3>3) moved coordinates. The fixed-diamond coupling above is still positive. The underlying general permutation obstruction is C034's result, acknowledged here rather than counted again.

C030's separately proved restriction transfer can be applied to these nonnegative projection diamonds. C036 is assigned products and multiphase constructions. Those extensions are not included in the theorem proved here.
