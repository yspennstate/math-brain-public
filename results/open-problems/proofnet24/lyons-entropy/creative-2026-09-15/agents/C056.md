## 1. The quantitative theorem

Let E be a finite coordinate set, let h in C^E be a unit vector, and let |z|=1. Put
\[
Q=I+(z-1)hh^*,\qquad
s_i=|h_i|^2,\qquad
\gamma=|z-1|^2\in[0,4].
\tag{1}
\]
For I contained in E, let mu_I be the projection DPP of Q C^I, and define
\[
\mathcal H(I)=H(\mu_I).
\]
Fix R contained in E and distinct p,q outside R. Write
\[
x=s(R),\qquad a=s_p,\qquad b=s_q,\qquad
a+b+x\le1,
\]
and set
\[
B_R=\mathcal H(R+p)+\mathcal H(R+q)
       -\mathcal H(R)-\mathcal H(R+p+q).
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
X=1-\frac{\gamma}{4}+\frac{\gamma(a+b)^2}{4},
\qquad
Y=1-\frac{\gamma}{4}+\frac{\gamma(a-b)^2}{4},
\qquad D=\gamma ab=X-Y.
\tag{3}
\]
All three quantities are nonnegative. With 0 log 0=0:

**Theorem 1.**
\[
\boxed{\quad
B_R\ \ge\
2\bigl[X\log X-Y\log Y-D\log D\bigr]
=2X\,h(D/X)\ \ge0.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
Here h is binary Shannon entropy, and the expression involving h is defined to be zero when X=0.

Consequently, I maps to H(mu_I) is a submodular set function on the whole Boolean lattice. In particular the requested projection-entropy inequality holds for
\[
W=Q\mathbb C^R,\qquad u=Qe_p,\qquad v=Qe_q
\tag{5}
\]
with every bottom rank and every physical phase angle.

The lower bound in (4) is attained whenever
\[
s(R)=s(E\setminus(R+p+q))=\frac{1-a-b}{2}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus it is sharp whenever the prescribed positive remaining mass can be split between nonempty bottom and complementary coordinate blocks. For gamma<4 and gamma ab>0, the lower bound is strictly positive. At gamma=4, it vanishes when a=b; condition (6) then gives actual zero entropy difference.

## 2. Exact entropy reduction

The exact projection law, proved directly by the rank-one determinant lemma in C034/C035, is
\[
\mu_I(J)=
\begin{cases}
1-\gamma s(I)(1-s(I)),&J=I,\\
\gamma s_i s_j,&J=I-i+j,\quad i\in I,\ j\notin I,\\
0,&|I\setminus J|\ge2.
\end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, the unchanged minor is
\[
\det Q[I,I]=1+(z-1)s(I).
\]
Its squared modulus gives the first line. A one-exchange minor has amplitude, up to sorting sign, (z-1)h_j conjugate(h_i), giving the second line. Two exchanged rows are proportional in the rank-one update, so their minor vanishes. This proves (7), including empty or full I and zero coordinates.

Assume first 0<gamma<4, so all unchanged probabilities are positive. Put
\[
L_I=\sum_{i\in I}s_i\log s_i,\qquad
L=\sum_{i\in E}s_i\log s_i,
\]
\[
k(s)=1-\gamma s(1-s),\qquad
f(s)=-k(s)\log k(s)-\gamma s(1-s)\log\gamma.
\tag{8}
\]
Summing the one-exchange entropies in (7) gives exactly
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal H(I)=f(s(I))
 -\gamma\bigl[(1-2s(I))L_I+s(I)L\bigr].
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed their logarithms are log gamma+log s_i+log s_j, and their total mass is gamma s(I)(1-s(I)).

Let l_a=a log a and l_b=b log b. In the alternating difference (2), the coefficient of L_R and the linear term s(I)L cancel. The remaining alternating contribution of (1-2s(I))L_I is
\[
2b l_a+2a l_b=2ab\log(ab).
\]
For a,b>0 this proves
\[
\boxed{\quad
B_R=f(x+a)+f(x+b)-f(x)-f(x+a+b)
     -2\gamma ab\log(ab).
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular the entire dependence on the background and complementary masses is through the single number x. No distribution of weights within those blocks remains.

## 3. Centering minimizes the entropy difference

Here is the elementary calculus fact used in the proof. If V is a differentiable even function nondecreasing on [0,infinity), then
\[
(\mathcal A_\ell V)(c)=\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2}V(c+t)\,dt
\]
is even and nondecreasing on [0,infinity). Evenness follows by changing t to -t; for c>=0,
\[
\frac{d}{dc}\mathcal A_\ell V(c)
=V(c+\ell/2)-V(c-\ell/2)\ge0,
\tag{11}
\]
because |c+ell/2|>=|c-ell/2|. Applying this statement twice shows that the integral of V over any pair of centered intervals is minimized when their common center is zero.

For 0<gamma<4 define
\[
\delta=\frac1\gamma-\frac14>0,\qquad
V_\delta(t)=\log(t^2+\delta)+1+\frac{2t^2}{t^2+\delta}.
\tag{12}
\]
A direct differentiation of (8) gives
\[
-f''(s)=2\gamma V_\delta(s-1/2).
\tag{13}
\]
This identity uses k(s)=gamma[(s-1/2)^2+delta]. The function V_delta is even and
\[
V_\delta'(t)=
\frac{2t(t^2+3\delta)}{(t^2+\delta)^2}>0
\quad(t>0).
\tag{14}
\]
The fundamental theorem of calculus applied twice to (10) therefore gives
\[
B_R=
2\gamma\int_0^a\int_0^b
\bigl[V_\delta(x+y+z-1/2)-\log(ab)\bigr]\,dz\,dy.
\tag{15}
\]
After centering the integration variables, the argument of V_delta is
\[
c+y'+z',\qquad
c=x+\frac{a+b}{2}-\frac12,\quad
y'\in[-a/2,a/2],\quad z'\in[-b/2,b/2].
\]
Equations (11) and (14) show that (15) is minimized at c=0. Hence
\[
B_R\ge B_{\mathrm{center}},
\qquad x_{\mathrm{center}}=\frac{1-a-b}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]
This center is feasible because a+b<=1. The proof is about the explicit scalar function; it does not require physically moving an existing coordinate block or changing its dimension.

## 4. The centered value and every boundary case

At the center in (16), the two outside arguments x and x+a+b of f have unchanged probability X from (3), and the two middle arguments x+a and x+b have unchanged probability Y. Also
\[
(x+a)(1-x-a)+(x+b)(1-x-b)
-x(1-x)-(x+a+b)(1-x-a-b)=2ab.
\]
Substituting into (10) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{\mathrm{center}}
&=2X\log X-2Y\log Y
   -2\gamma ab\log\gamma-2\gamma ab\log(ab)\\
&=2\bigl[X\log X-Y\log Y-D\log D\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Since X=Y+D, this equals 2X h(D/X) and is nonnegative. Equations (16)–(17) prove (4) for 0<gamma<4 and a,b>0, including its asserted sharpness.

The remaining cases have no hidden logarithmic obligation:

* If a=0, then h_p=0 and Qe_p=e_p. Adding p is a deterministic coordinate addition to every law under consideration, so H(R+p)=H(R) and H(R+p+q)=H(R+q). Thus B_R=0. The case b=0 is identical, and the right side of (4) is also zero.
* If gamma=0, then Q=I and every projection law is deterministic. Both sides of (4) vanish.
* For gamma=4, apply the already proved inequality with the same weights and gamma tending upward to 4. Formula (7) is continuous in gamma, and finite Shannon entropy is continuous, including at zero atoms. The right side of (4) is also continuous under 0 log 0=0. This proves the endpoint without integrating a singular function.
* Empty R, empty complementary block, zero masses inside either block, and arbitrary zero coordinates are all covered by (7) and the preceding argument. No positivity assumption on a whole projection support is used.

Every elementary entropy difference is now nonnegative. The usual finite telescoping of elementary differences proves the set-function assertion for arbitrary A,B:
\[
\mathcal H(A)+\mathcal H(B)
\ge \mathcal H(A\cap B)+\mathcal H(A\cup B).
\tag{18}
\]
This telescoping uses the proved projection inequality; it is not an invocation of ordinary random-variable entropy submodularity. This completes Theorem 1.

## 5. The full spectral-concavity consequence

**Theorem 2.** For every Q,R,p,q in (1)–(5), the function
\[
(\lambda,\mu)\longmapsto
H\!\left(P_{\,P_W+\lambda uu^*+\mu vv^*}\right)
\tag{19}
\]
is jointly concave on the entire square [0,1]^2.

The two varying eigenvectors are fixed. All other eigenvalues are the fixed zero or one eigenvalues prescribed by W. The theorem includes arbitrary nonzero bottom rank and arbitrary top codimension; it does not assert concavity for general varying eigenvectors.

**Proof with its inputs stated explicitly.** Write H_j for the four projection entropies, with H_0 at W, H_1 at W+[u], H_2 at W+[v], and H_3 at W+[u,v]. Let beta=H_1+H_2-H_0-H_3. Theorem 1 proves beta>=0.

The spectral Bernoulli expansion separates the bottom and top cardinalities and mixes only the two middle laws. Define
\[
A=\lambda(1-\mu),\qquad B=\mu(1-\lambda),\qquad
F=H(\operatorname{Bern}(\lambda)+\operatorname{Bern}(\mu)),
\]
\[
G(A,B)=(A+B)H\!\left(\frac{A\mu_1+B\mu_2}{A+B}\right)
-A H_1-B H_2.
\]
The independent Bernoulli variables in F are count variables. Conditioning on cardinality gives the root's exact identity
\[
H(P_K)=F+H_0+\lambda(H_1-H_0)+\mu(H_2-H_0)
-\beta\lambda\mu+G(A,B).
\tag{20}
\]
The perspective G is concave in its nonnegative arguments. On the interior,
\[
G_A=D\!\left(\mu_1\middle\Vert
        \frac{A\mu_1+B\mu_2}{A+B}\right)\ge0,
\quad
G_B=D\!\left(\mu_2\middle\Vert
        \frac{A\mu_1+B\mu_2}{A+B}\right)\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]
Along an affine direction with slopes alpha,zeta and alpha zeta>=0,
\[
A''=B''=-2\alpha\zeta,
\]
so G''<=0, and (-beta lambda mu)''=-2 beta alpha zeta<=0. The two-Bernoulli count entropy F is jointly concave by the theta=0 result of C075. Therefore (20) has nonpositive curvature in every same-sign direction.

For opposite signs, use precisely Theorem 1 of C035: it constructs nonnegative weights t_d on actual elementary diamonds d=(S,T), with |T\setminus S|=2, such that
\[
\sum_d t_d
(\delta_S+\delta_T-\delta_{M_1}-\delta_{M_2})
=\mu_0+\mu_3-\mu_1-\mu_2=:\nu.
\tag{22}
\]
Here M_1,M_2 are the two middle sets of the same diamond, and the weights sum to one. This input holds throughout gamma in [0,4], without equal middle laws or a common-permutation hypothesis.

At 0<lambda,mu<1 the mixture atom law p has fixed support equal to the union of the four projection supports. A positive weight in (22) forces positive bottom, top, and aggregate-middle projection mass, so all four corresponding mixture atoms are positive. DPP atom log-submodularity gives
\[
\log p_S+\log p_T-\log p_{M_1}-\log p_{M_2}\le0.
\tag{23}
\]
One way to justify (23) at a projection boundary is to approximate K by epsilon I+(1-2 epsilon)K. For the strict contractions, the L-ensemble formula and the Hadamard–Fischer determinant inequality prove (23); continuity preserves the inequality at the positive atoms just identified. Averaging it with (22) yields
\[
\sum_J\nu_J\log p_J\le0.
\]
The atom acceleration is p''=2 alpha zeta nu, and the exact entropy Hessian is
\[
H''=-\sum_{J:p_J>0}\frac{(p'_J)^2}{p_J}
-2\alpha\zeta\sum_J\nu_J\log p_J\le0
\quad\text{when }\alpha\zeta\le0.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus every direction has nonpositive curvature on the open square. Integrating along segments proves concavity there, and continuity of finite Shannon entropy extends it to the closed square. The proof has no remaining projection-entropy or transport obligation for the stated family.

## 6. Sharp and noncentered exact examples

### 6.1 A nonzero-background equality with a dense bottom projection

Let n=6, h=(1,1,1,1,1,1)/sqrt(6), z=-1, R={0,1}, p=2, q=3. Then
\[
Q=I-\frac13\mathbf1\mathbf1^T,\quad
\gamma=4,\quad x=\frac13,\quad a=b=\frac16.
\]
All four projection laws are uniform on nine sets. For ranks two and four these are the unchanged set and eight exchanges; for rank three the unchanged atom is zero and the nine exchanges have mass 1/9. Hence
\[
H_0=H_1=H_2=H_3=\log9,\qquad \beta=0.
\tag{25}
\]
The bottom projection has every off-diagonal entry nonzero, so this is not a coordinate direct sum. It is C035's reflection example, now with a proved sharp entropy identity and full spectral concavity.

### 6.2 A genuinely complex phase attaining the positive lower bound

Keep the same six-site h,R,p,q and put z=(-3+4i)/5. Then
\[
\gamma=\frac{16}{5},\quad
X=\frac{13}{45},\quad Y=\frac15,\quad D=\frac4{45},
\]
and exact equality holds in (4):
\[
\beta=
2\left[
\frac{13}{45}\log\frac{13}{45}
-\frac15\log\frac15
-\frac4{45}\log\frac4{45}\right]>0.
\tag{26}
\]
C035 proved that this complex update plane cannot be rotated to equalize all middle probabilities. Theorem 2 needs no such equalization.
The certified value is approximately 0.35662857806641958437.

### 6.3 A nonuniform rank-three reflection with strict centering gap

Let
\[
h=\frac1{\sqrt{20}}(1,2,1,1,2,2,1,2)^T,\quad z=-1,
\quad R=\{0,1,2\},\ p=3,\ q=4.
\]
Then
\[
x=\frac3{10},\quad a=\frac1{20},\quad b=\frac15,\quad
X=\frac1{16},\quad Y=\frac9{400},\quad D=\frac1{25}.
\]
The actual entropy difference is exactly
\[
\beta=
\frac4{25}\log\frac4{25}
+\frac1{100}\log\frac1{100}
-\frac9{100}\log\frac9{100}
-\frac2{25}\log\frac1{25},
\tag{27}
\]
whereas the lower bound is
\[
2\left[
\frac1{16}\log\frac1{16}
-\frac9{400}\log\frac9{400}
-\frac1{25}\log\frac1{25}
\right].
\tag{28}
\]
The exact certificate encloses the actual beta near 0.13496043470851400407, the lower bound near 0.08167727434921272241, and their strictly positive difference near 0.05328316035930128166. These are display approximations to rationally certified logarithm intervals. This example has nonzero rank-three background and nonzero codimension-three complement; its bottom projection has no zero off-diagonal entries.
