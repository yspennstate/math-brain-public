# C080 — One missing endpoint coordinate: exact entropy clustering

Complete restricted theorem, 15 September 2026. The target is a four-site boundary point
\(K_0=ww^*+VMV^*\), with \([z,w,V]\) unitary, \(0<M<I_2\), every coordinate of \(z\) nonzero, and \(w_4=0\), \(w_1w_2w_3\ne0\). This stratum is outside ROOT_FOUR_SITE_BOUNDARY. No global Lyons claim or historical priority claim is made.

**Result.** For every such boundary point, the full sixteen-dimensional Hermitian entropy Hessian is negative definite at
\[
K_{e,h}=ezz^*+(1-h)ww^*+VMV^*
\]
whenever \(e,h>0\) are sufficiently small, with **no restriction on their ratio or the ratio of their logarithms**. Explicit uniform constants and a positive threshold are given below. Every admitted center has an explicitly specified full-dimensional convex Frobenius ball on which the full Hessian stays strictly negative. Their union is an ambient open set approaching the new boundary stratum. Permuting occupation coordinates and complementing the kernel gives the analogous statement for any one missing coordinate of either endpoint vector.

The proof uses the existing rank-one-background active-matrix concavity theorem from ROOT_DIAMOND_ACTIVE_MATRIX, with a new strictness certificate suited to the missing-coordinate stratum. It does not assert an extension to several missing endpoint coordinates, arbitrary rates at which endpoint-vector coordinates vanish outside the explicit balls, or all four-site kernels. No subagents, push, or messaging outside this proof campaign were used.

## Exact new construction

Put \(K_{e,h}=ezz^*+(1-h)ww^*+VMV^*\), \(N=I-M\), \(d=\det M\), \(d_0=\det N\). All atoms except the empty atom, the full atom, and the singleton \(\{4\}\) are positive at \(K_0\). The three singular atoms are denoted \(q_0,q,q_4\).

The decisive identity, valid for every kernel, is

\[
R:=q_0+q_4=\det(I-K)_{\{1,2,3\}}.
\]

Choose a four-by-two isometry \(T\) spanning \(\{w,e_4\}^{\perp}\), and put
\[
F_e=T^*[(1-e)zz^*+VNV^*]T,\qquad D_e=\det F_e.
\]
Then \(R=hD_e\), \(q_0=h(1-e)d_0\), and \(q_4=h[D_e-(1-e)d_0]\). Writing \(s=|z_4|^2\), \(v=V_{4,:}\), and \(g=vN^{-1}Mv^*>0\), cofactor expansion gives
\[
D_e=d_0[(1-e)(1+g)+es],\qquad
q_4=hd_0[(1-e)g+es].
\]

Write an arbitrary affine Hermitian direction in the \([z,w,V]\) frame as
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&u&x^*\\\bar u&b&y^*\\x&y&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The logarithmic singularity from \(q_0,q_4\) together is exactly the one belonging to \(R\). Its mixing term involves only
\[
f=T^*Aw=T^*V r,\qquad r=y-\frac{v^*}{\bar z_4}u.
\]
The cancellation uses \(zu+V(v^*/\bar z_4)u=e_4u/\bar z_4\). Consequently its two real null mixing modes are precisely the Hermitian directions generated by \(e_4w^*\); the full atom controls them because \(z_4\ne0\).

The exact grouped entropy Hessian is
\[
H''=Q_{13}-\frac{(q')^2}{q}-q''\log q
-\frac{(R')^2}{R}-R''\log R+\mathcal S,
\]
where \(Q_{13}\) is the contribution of the thirteen regular atoms. If \(\theta=q_0/R\),
\[
\mathcal S=-R\frac{(\theta')^2}{\theta(1-\theta)}
-q_0''\log\theta-q_4''\log(1-\theta).
\]
For this family, \(\theta=(1-e)d_0/D_e\) stays strictly between zero and one on compact parameter families. The singular terms \(-b/h\) cancel in the quotient derivative \(\theta'\), leaving it bounded; hence \(\mathcal S\) is a bounded quadratic form. Its restriction to \(B\) tends to zero.

## Strict active face gap

ROOT_DIAMOND_ACTIVE_MATRIX gives \(T(B)=-\mathrm{FI}(B)+2\det(B)\Lambda\), \(\Lambda\ge0\), for every rank-one background. Here strictness has a particularly direct proof.

Hodge duality gives the active-plane pair masses
\(h_{ij}=|z_4|^2|w_k|^2\) for \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\). In every C033 inclusion transport the bottom mass at site 4 is zero. Thus the entire mass of addition pair \(ij\subset123\) is forced into top \(123\), with center \(k\). It follows that
\[
\Lambda\ge s\sum_{k=1}^3|w_k|^2
\log\frac{p_{ki}p_{kj}}{p_kp_{123}}.
\]

Condition on site 4 being absent and complement the remaining three occupations. Its kernel is the Schur complement of \((I-K_0)_{44}\) in \(I-K_0\), hence has rank two. Its empty atom and every singleton are positive, so its finite odds matrix \(L\) is positive semidefinite, has rank two, and has positive diagonal. The three displayed gaps are
\(-\log(1-|L_{ij}|^2/(L_{ii}L_{jj}))\).

Normalize \(L\) to diagonal one. Since its rank is two and its trace is three, its squared trace norm gives
\(\sum_{i<j}|L_{ij}|^2/(L_{ii}L_{jj})\ge3/4\). Jensen's inequality therefore proves
\[
\boxed{\Lambda\ge3s\min_{k\le3}|w_k|^2\log(4/3)>0.}
\]
With \(C_1=8-6\log2\), the active theorem supplies
\[
T(B)\le-\min\{\Lambda,2C_1/(C_1+2\Lambda)\}\|B\|_F^2.
\]

## Complete independent-scale normal form

Put \(L_e=\log(1/e)\), \(L_h=\log(1/h)\), and rescale all sixteen real modes by
\[
a=\sqrt e\,\alpha,\quad b=\sqrt h\,\beta,\quad
u=\chi/\sqrt{L_e},\quad x=\xi/\sqrt{L_e},\quad
r=\upsilon/\sqrt{L_h},
\]
leaving \(B\) unscaled. The grouped determinant jets yield
\[
H''[A,A]=
-d\alpha^2-D_0\beta^2-2d|\chi|^2
-2d\xi^*M^{-1}\xi
-2D_0(T^*V\upsilon)^*F_0^{-1}(T^*V\upsilon)
+T(B)+o(1).
\]
Here \(T^*V\) is invertible, with squared singular values \(1,s\). All displayed blocks are strictly negative. The grouped determinant avoids errors such as \(eL_h/L_e\) that would invalidate a termwise independent-scale argument. The convergence is in quadratic-form operator norm, uniformly on compact parameter families. The rest of this report supplies the detailed proofs, explicit error constants, and constructive convex balls.

## 1. Atom positivity and uniform parameter bounds

Fix parameters
\[
0<\kappa<1/2,\qquad 0<\zeta\le1/4,\qquad 0<\omega\le1/3,
\]
and impose
\[
\kappa I_2\le M\le(1-\kappa)I_2,\qquad
|z_i|^2\ge\zeta\ (1\le i\le4),\qquad
|w_i|^2\ge\omega\ (1\le i\le3),\qquad w_4=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Every fixed boundary point in the theorem belongs to a family of this form. Endpoint eigenspaces and the two-dimensional active covariance are otherwise arbitrary, including complex phases and vanishing active-plane minors.

Let \(a_i=|w_i|^2\), \(q_i=|z_i|^2\), \(s=q_4\), and
\[
e_{ij}=w_iV_{j,:}-w_jV_{i,:},\qquad g_{ij}=\|e_{ij}\|^2.
\]
The boundary atoms are
\[
p_i=d_0a_i,\qquad
p_{[4]\setminus\{j\}}=dq_j,\qquad
p_{ij}=e_{ij}(M-dI_2)e_{ij}^*.
\tag{2}
\]
The eigenvalues of \(M-dI_2\) are \(\lambda(1-\mu)\) and \(\mu(1-\lambda)\), so this matrix is at least \(\kappa^2I_2\).

For \(i\le3\), \(g_{i4}=a_i(1-s)\). If \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\), Hodge duality gives the active minor square \(h_{ij}=sa_k\). Two-row Cauchy--Binet in the rank-three projection \(I-zz^*\) then gives
\[
g_{ij}=1-q_i-q_j-h_{ij}=q_k+s(1-a_k).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus every pair atom is positive. The only vanishing atoms at the boundary are \(\varnothing,\{4\},[4]\). Every other atom is at least \(\kappa^2\omega\zeta\).

For \(0\le e,h\le1/2\), the independent spectral Bernoulli mixture has weight \((1-e)(1-h)\ge1/4\) on the boundary law. Hence all thirteen regular atoms of \(K_{e,h}\) are at least
\[
m:=\frac{\kappa^2\omega\zeta}{4}.
\tag{4}
\]
This bound also holds on the segment from \(K_0\) to \(K_{e,h}\).

## 2. Proof of the quantitative active strictness claim

The inclusion transport of C033 has addition-pair mass \(h_{ij}=sa_k\) whenever \(\{i,j,k\}=123\). Its bottom marginal at site 4 is \(a_4=0\). Thus no positive weight can have center 4, and the only remaining containing triple for the addition pair \(ij\subset123\) is \(123\). These three forced weights are exactly
\[
x_{\{i,j\},123}=sa_k>0.
\tag{5}
\]
No strict-Hall assertion is needed; other transport edges can be forced to zero. The nonnegative frozen logarithmic diamond gaps therefore give
\[
\Lambda\ge s\sum_{k=1}^3a_k G_k,\qquad
G_k=\log\frac{p_{ki}p_{kj}}{p_kp_{123}},\quad\{i,j,k\}=123.
\tag{6}
\]

For completeness, the rank argument behind the quantitative lower bound can be written entirely in terms of conditional kernels. Put \(H_0=I-K_0\) and \(I=\{1,2,3\}\). The scalar \((H_0)_{44}\) is positive because
\((H_0)_{44}=s+vNv^*>0\). Condition the hole process on occupation of site 4. Its remaining marginal kernel is
\[
G=(H_0)_{I,I}-(H_0)_{I,4}(H_0)_{44}^{-1}(H_0)_{4,I}.
\tag{7}
\]
The matrix \(H_0\) has rank three, and the pivot is invertible, so Schur-complement rank additivity gives \(\operatorname{rank}G=2\). Its empty atom equals \(p_{123}/(H_0)_{44}>0\), implying \(I-G>0\). Thus
\[
L=G(I-G)^{-1}\ge0,\qquad \operatorname{rank}L=2.
\]
Exact conditional atom ratios give, for \(\{i,j,k\}=123\),
\[
L_{ii}=\frac{p_{123\setminus\{i\}}}{p_{123}}>0,
\qquad
\det L_{\{i,j\}}=\frac{p_k}{p_{123}}>0.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently, if \(c_{ij}=|L_{ij}|^2/(L_{ii}L_{jj})\), then
\[
G_k=-\log(1-c_{ij}),\qquad 0\le c_{ij}<1.
\]
Normalize \(L\) to diagonal one, calling the resulting rank-two matrix \(C\). Its two nonzero eigenvalues sum to three. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
3+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}C^2
\ge\frac{(\operatorname{tr}C)^2}{2}=\frac92.
\]
Since \(-\log(1-x)\) is increasing and convex on \([0,1)\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^3G_k\ge3\log(4/3).
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (6)--(9) prove
\[
\boxed{\Lambda\ge\lambda_*:=3\zeta\omega\log(4/3)>0.}
\tag{10}
\]

An upper bound useful for uniform constants follows from the active-matrix identity:
\[
\Lambda=\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}\log p_{ij}-\log(dd_0)+H(a)+H(q)
\le4\log(1/\kappa)+\log12=:\Lambda_*.
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed the first term is nonpositive, \(dd_0\ge\kappa^4\), and the bottom and top entropies are at most \(\log3\) and \(\log4\). With \(C_1=8-6\log2\), the existing active theorem now gives the uniform face gap
\[
\boxed{
T(B)\le-\gamma\|B\|_F^2,\qquad
\gamma=\min\left\{\lambda_*,\frac{2C_1}{C_1+2\Lambda_*}\right\}>0.
}
\tag{12}
\]

## 3. Exact jets of all singular pieces

For a Hermitian direction written in the frame \([z,w,V]\) as above, put
\[
t=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}B),\qquad
t_0=\operatorname{tr}(N^{-1}B).
\]
The full and empty atom jets are
\[
\begin{aligned}
q&=e(1-h)d,\\
q'&=d[(1-h)a+eb+e(1-h)t],\\
q''&=2d[ab+(1-h)at+ebt-|u|^2
 -(1-h)x^*M^{-1}x-ey^*M^{-1}y]
 +2e(1-h)\det B,
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0&=h(1-e)d_0,\\
q_0'&=-d_0[ha+(1-e)b+h(1-e)t_0],\\
q_0''&=2d_0[ab+hat_0+(1-e)bt_0-|u|^2
 -hx^*N^{-1}x-(1-e)y^*N^{-1}y]
 +2h(1-e)\det B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
These are block determinant expansions; they retain both endpoint normals and every eigenvector mixing direction.

To express the marginal determinant jets, let \(A_{\rm phys}\) denote the direction in the measured basis, and define
\[
C=T^*A_{\rm phys}T,\qquad f=T^*A_{\rm phys}w,
\qquad t_F=\operatorname{tr}(F_e^{-1}C).
\]
In the orthonormal basis \([w,T]\) of the first three measured coordinates, the hole principal block is \(\operatorname{diag}(h,F_e)\), and its affine direction is the corresponding compression of \(-A_{\rm phys}\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
R=hD_e,\quad
R'=-D_e(b+ht_F),\quad
R''=2D_e(bt_F-f^*F_e^{-1}f)+2h\det C.
}
\tag{15}
\]
The singleton jets are exactly \(q_4^{(j)}=R^{(j)}-q_0^{(j)}\), \(j=0,1,2\). Equations (13)--(15) are therefore a complete jet formula for all three singular atoms.

The cofactor expression in the opening construction also follows directly. Since \(e_4\perp w\),
\[
\det(I-K)_{123}
=\det(I-K)\,[(I-K)^{-1}]_{44}
=hd_0[s+(1-e)vN^{-1}v^*].
\]
Using \(vN^{-1}v^*=(1-s)+g\) yields the stated \(D_e\) and \(q_4\) formulas.

## 4. Exact binary clustering and the canceled modes

For positive \(q_0,q_4\), let \(\theta=q_0/R\). The elementary Fisher identity is
\[
\frac{(q_0')^2}{q_0}+\frac{(q_4')^2}{q_4}
=\frac{(R')^2}{R}
+R\frac{(\theta')^2}{\theta(1-\theta)}.
\]
Separating \(\log q_0=\log R+\log\theta\) and
\(\log q_4=\log R+\log(1-\theta)\) proves the grouped Hessian identity in the opening construction, with no approximation.

At the specified spectral centers,
\[
\theta=\frac{(1-e)d_0}{D_e},\qquad
\boxed{\theta'=\theta\left[-\frac{a}{1-e}-t_0+t_F\right].}
\tag{16}
\]
The \(-b/h\) terms in \(q_0'/q_0\) and \(R'/R\) cancel exactly. Thus the binary split contributes a bounded quadratic form even when \(h\) is much smaller than every power of \(e\).

The orthogonal resolution
\(e_4=z\bar z_4+Vv^*\) gives
\[
f=T^*V\left(y-\frac{v^*}{\bar z_4}u\right)=T^*Vr.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular the \(\log(1/h)\) mixing form vanishes exactly when the physical mixing vector \(A_{\rm phys}w\) is a multiple of \(e_4\). This is a two-real-dimensional kernel, not an omitted direction. Since \(z_4\ne0\), the full atom has the strictly negative term \(-2d\log(1/e)|u|^2\) on this kernel.

The map \(T^*V\) has squared singular values \(1,s\), because
\[
V^*TT^*V=V^*(I-ww^*-e_4e_4^*)V=I_2-v^*v.
\tag{18}
\]
This proves its invertibility and the nondegeneracy of the two complex \(r\) modes. Exact grouping is essential: estimating the empty and singleton logarithmic terms separately would lose their common kernel and could introduce a false error proportional to \(eL_h/L_e\).

## 5. Explicit uniform normal-form remainder

The following intentionally conservative constants make the boundary neighborhood fully specified. They are mathematical positive constants and need not be numerically representable in ordinary floating point.

Write
\[
\ell=\log(1/\kappa),\qquad
\tau=\kappa^3\zeta,\qquad J=2+3/\kappa,
\]
and, with \(m\) as in (4), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_R&=13\{16/m+12\log(1/m)\},\\
W_R&=13\{144/m+64/m^2+24\log(1/m)\},\\
C_S&=J^2/\tau^2+24\log(1/\tau),\\
C_F&=(2+12\ell)/\kappa,\\
C_E&=\frac{16(1+3\ell)}{\kappa^2\zeta},\\
\mathcal C&=10+\frac{20}{\kappa}+C_E+C_S
+\frac{24C_F+12C_R+12C_S+4W_R}{\zeta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Define the real parameter norm
\[
\|P\|^2=\alpha^2+\beta^2+|\chi|^2+\|\xi\|^2+\|\upsilon\|^2+\|B\|_F^2,
\]
and let \(A_{e,h}(P)\) be the scaled direction from the opening normal form. Define
\[
\mathcal P(P)=
-d\alpha^2-D_0\beta^2-2d|\chi|^2
-2d\xi^*M^{-1}\xi
-2D_0(T^*V\upsilon)^*F_0^{-1}(T^*V\upsilon)
+T(B).
\tag{20}
\]
For \(0<e,h\le\min(\kappa/2,\exp(-9))\), the precise uniform bound is
\[
\boxed{
|H''(K_{e,h})[A_{e,h}(P),A_{e,h}(P)]-\mathcal P(P)|
\le\mathcal C\,\rho(e,h)\,\|P\|^2,
}
\tag{21}
\]
where
\[
\rho(e,h)=\sqrt e(1+L_e)+\sqrt h(1+L_h)+L_e^{-1/2}+L_h^{-1/2}.
\tag{22}
\]
Every term tends to zero independently in the two endpoint gaps.

### 5.1 Regular and split form bounds

For any four-site contraction and any atom polynomial
\(p_S(K)=(-1)^{4-|S|}\det(K-I_{S^c})\), column multilinearity gives
\[
|Dp_S[A]|\le4\|A\|_F,\quad
|D^2p_S[A,B]|\le12\|A\|_F\|B\|_F,\quad
|D^3p_S[A,B,C]|\le24\|A\|_F\|B\|_F\|C\|_F.
\tag{23}
\]
Here \(\|K-I_{S^c}\|_{\rm op}\le1\); the constants count ordered replaced columns. Therefore the thirteen-atom regular quadratic form has norm at most \(C_R\) and Lipschitz constant at most \(W_R\) on the segment in Section 1. The single-atom Lipschitz estimate is
\[
\frac{3(4)(12)}m+\frac{4^3}{m^2}+24\log(1/m).
\]

On the compact families (1), \(F_e\) has its two eigenvalues in \([\kappa,1]\). Also \(1-s\ge3\zeta\) and \(g\ge\kappa(1-s)\). The exact formulas imply
\[
\theta\ge\kappa^2/2\ge\tau,\qquad
1-\theta\ge\tfrac12\kappa^3(1-s)\ge\tau.
\tag{24}
\]
Equation (16) gives \(|\theta'|\le J\|A\|_F\). Combining (23)--(24) with the split formula proves \(\|\mathcal S\|_{\rm op}\le C_S\). On active directions alone, the stronger bound is
\[
|\mathcal S(B)|\le hC_S\|B\|_F^2.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed \(|q_0''|\le h\|B\|_F^2\), \(|R''|\le h\|B\|_F^2\), so \(|q_4''|\le2h\|B\|_F^2\); the remaining Fisher term has the factor \(R=hD_e\le h\).

### 5.2 A determinant identity retaining all finite terms

Consider a block determinant \(p=\varepsilon\det J_0\) with positive block \(J_0\), scalar normal direction \(a\), mixing vector \(v\), and tangent block \(C_0\). Put
\[
D=\det J_0,\quad t=\operatorname{tr}(J_0^{-1}C_0),\quad
f_2=t^2-\operatorname{tr}(J_0^{-1}C_0J_0^{-1}C_0),\quad L=\log(1/\varepsilon).
\]
Direct determinant differentiation gives the exact entropy contribution
\[
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{(p')^2}{p}-p''\log p
={}&-\frac D\varepsilon a^2+2DL\,at-2DL\,v^*J_0^{-1}v\\
&-2D(1+\log D)at+2D\log D\,v^*J_0^{-1}v\\
&+\varepsilon D\{-t^2+(L-\log D)f_2\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
For the full atom use \(J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1-h,M)\),
\(C_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&y^*\\y&B\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
and \(v=(\bar u,x)^T\). For the marginal determinant \(R\), use
\(\varepsilon=h\), \(J_0=F_e\), and the normal, mixing, and tangent directions \(-b,-f,-C\). Both signs reverse in the product \(at\), yielding \(+bt_F\) in (15).

In either application, the middle line of (26) is a bounded quadratic form of norm at most \(C_F\), and vanishes on pure active \(B\). To see the bound, the positive blocks have eigenvalues at least \(\kappa\), dimension at most three, and \(|\log D|\le3\ell\). Hence
\(|t|\le\sqrt3\|C_0\|_F/\kappa\) and
\(v^*J_0^{-1}v\le\|v\|^2/\kappa\); Young's inequality gives the stated conservative \(C_F\).

### 5.3 Collecting the error without a scale-ratio assumption

Let
\(r_0=\max\{\sqrt e,\sqrt h,L_e^{-1/2},L_h^{-1/2}\}\).
From \(\|v^*/\bar z_4\|^2=(1-s)/s\),
\[
\|A_{e,h}(P)\|_F\le\frac2{\sqrt\zeta}\|P\|,
\qquad
\|A_{e,h}(P)-VBV^*\|_F\le\frac{2r_0}{\sqrt\zeta}\|P\|.
\tag{27}
\]
Here the second equation is in the measured basis. Thus a bounded quadratic form of norm \(C\) that vanishes on active directions contributes at most \(12Cr_0\|P\|^2/\zeta\). Apply this to both middle lines of (26), to the cross terms of the boundary regular form, and to the split form after subtracting its active restriction (25).

The regular-form base change contributes at most
\(4W_R(e+h)\|P\|^2/\zeta\). The principal coefficients differ from those in (20) by at most
\[
\{4e+(1+2/\kappa)h\}\|P\|^2.
\tag{28}
\]
For (28), \(|D_e-D_0|\le2e\) and
\(\|\operatorname{adj}F_e-\operatorname{adj}F_0\|\le e\), since adjugation is linear on two-by-two matrices. The full-atom \(u\) coefficient is exactly \(-2dL_e|u|^2\), with no \(h\) error.

The two leading mixed terms of (26) are bounded by
\[
\left\{(4+4/\kappa)\sqrt eL_e
+(12/\kappa)\sqrt hL_h\right\}\|P\|^2.
\tag{29}
\]
For the second bound, the compression \(C\) depends only on \(a,x,B\); it has norm at most \(\sqrt6\|P\|<3\|P\|\). Thus it carries no potentially large \(y\) coefficient.

The last lines of (26) together contribute at most
\[
C_E\{e(1+L_e)+h(1+L_h)\}\|P\|^2.
\tag{30}
\]
For the full atom, use \(|f_2|\le4\|C_0\|_F^2/\kappa^2\),
\(|t|^2\le3\|C_0\|_F^2/\kappa^2\), and (27). For the marginal determinant, dimension two improves these constants to three and two, respectively, and is covered by the same \(C_E\).

Finally, \(e+h\), \(r_0\), each term in (29), and the bracket in (30) are bounded by the corresponding sums in \(\rho(e,h)\). Adding these bounds and the split active error \(hC_S\) gives exactly the conservative coefficient \(\mathcal C\) in (19), proving (21). The grouping in Section 4 is why no term contains an uncontrolled ratio such as \(eL_h/L_e\).

## 6. An explicit threshold and full-Hessian gap

Let
\[
\mu=\min\{\kappa^2,\,2\kappa\zeta,\,\gamma\}>0.
\tag{31}
\]
The principal form satisfies
\[
\mathcal P(P)\le-\mu\|P\|^2.
\]
Indeed \(d,D_0\ge\kappa^2\), \(dM^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}M\ge\kappa I_2\),
\(D_0F_0^{-1}=\operatorname{adj}F_0\ge\kappa I_2\), and (18) bounds the remaining compression below by \(\zeta I_2\). The active block uses (12).

The explicit choice
\[
\boxed{
\delta=\min\left\{
\kappa/2,\ \exp(-9),\
\left(\frac{\mu}{16\mathcal C}\right)^4,\
\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{8\mathcal C}{\mu}\right)^2\right]
\right\}>0
}
\tag{32}
\]
ensures \(\mathcal C\rho(e,h)\le\mu/2\) whenever \(0<e,h\le\delta\). To verify it, for \(L\ge9\) the elementary inequality \(L\le e^{L/4}\) gives
\(\sqrt\varepsilon(1+\log(1/\varepsilon))\le2\varepsilon^{1/4}\).
Thus
\[
\rho(e,h)\le4\delta^{1/4}+\frac2{\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}};
\]
the last two bounds in (32) make the two terms contribute at most \(\mu/(4\mathcal C)\) each.

Consequently all sixteen real modes satisfy
\[
\boxed{
H''(K_{e,h})[A,A]
\le-\frac\mu2\left[
\frac{a^2}{e}+\frac{b^2}{h}
+L_e(|u|^2+\|x\|^2)
+L_h\left\|y-\frac{v^*}{\bar z_4}u\right\|^2
+\|B\|_F^2\right]
\le-g_*\|A\|_F^2,
\qquad g_*:=\frac{\mu\zeta}{8}.
}
\tag{33}
\]
The transformation is invertible for every \(e,h>0\), so this is a full Hermitian Hessian statement. Unlike a spectral-face-only argument, it controls directions that change both endpoint eigenspaces, including the two logarithmically canceled modes identified in Section 4.

## 7. Explicit convex ambient balls

The spectral centers in (33) satisfy \(w_4=0\), a constraint that is not open. The following quantitative construction supplies actual full-dimensional convex regions.

For any admitted center \(K=K_{e,h}\), put
\[
\ell_K=\min(e,h,\kappa),\qquad m_K=(\ell_K/2)^4,
\]
and define
\[
W_K=16\{144/m_K+64/m_K^2+24\log(1/m_K)\},
\qquad
\boxed{r_K=\min\{\ell_K/2,\ g_*/(2W_K)\}>0.}
\tag{34}
\]
Then the Frobenius ball
\[
\boxed{\mathcal B_K=\{K+E:E=E^*,\ \|E\|_F<r_K\}}
\tag{35}
\]
is a convex open subset of the full sixteen-real-dimensional space of strict four-site contractions, and
\[
H''(K+E)[A,A]\le-\frac{g_*}{2}\|A\|_F^2
\quad\text{for every }K+E\in\mathcal B_K.
\tag{36}
\]

Here is a direct proof of the uniform probability lower bound used in (34). Every matrix in the larger ball of radius \(\ell_K/2\) has all eigenvalues in \([\ell_K/2,1-\ell_K/2]\), by Weyl's inequality. In the spectral projection mixture, each eigen-subset weight is therefore at least \((\ell_K/2)^4\). For a fixed occupation set \(S\), the squared minors of the unitary eigenvector matrix over all eigen-subsets of size \(|S|\) sum to one by Cauchy--Binet. Hence every atom is at least \(m_K\). Applying (23) and the same Hessian Lipschitz calculation as before to all sixteen atoms gives the constant \(W_K\). The Hessian changes by at most \(W_K\|E\|_F\le g_*/2\), which proves (36).

Every nonconstant affine segment within one of these balls has strictly concave entropy. Their union is an ambient open subset approaching every boundary point in the theorem. Each ball includes perturbations for which the previously missing endpoint coordinate becomes nonzero, with no need to select eigenvectors for those perturbations. The radius is explicit; the neighborhood conclusion does not rely solely on an unspecified continuity argument. No claim is made that the union of the balls is convex.

Complementation uses \(H(I-K)=H(K)\). It exchanges the endpoint roles and produces the same type of full-dimensional balls near a point whose zero-eigenvector has exactly one missing coordinate while the one-eigenvector is fully supported.
