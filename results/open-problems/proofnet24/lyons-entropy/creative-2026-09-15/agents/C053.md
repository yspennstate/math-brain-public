## 2. Exact degree-two projection and the reduced Schur theorem

Suppose \(0<K<I_n\), and at this base its complete frozen log-atom table is pairwise:

\[
-\log p_S=b+\sum_{i\in S}b_i+
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}c_{ij},\qquad c_{ij}>0.
\tag{5}
\]

Initially assume \(K_{ij}\ne0\) for every pair. This assumption is convenient for the Schur coordinates, not needed for the direct polynomial proof of Theorem A.

For the genuine affine law \(K+tA\), write

\[
s(S)=\frac{Dp_S(K)[A]}{p_S},\quad I=\mathbb E s^2,
\quad a_i=A_{ii}.
\]

Let \(\mu_i=K_{ii}\), and introduce centered statistics

\[
Y_i=X_i-\mu_i,\qquad
Q_{ij}=Y_iY_j-\mathbb E(Y_iY_j).
\]

All centers are fixed at the base when taking derivatives. Define their exact Gram blocks

\[
\Sigma=\mathbb E YY^T,\quad
T=\mathbb E YQ^T,\quad V=\mathbb E QQ^T,
\qquad R=V-T^T\Sigma^{-1}T.
\tag{6}
\]

Full support implies that the centered degree-one and degree-two monomials are linearly independent; hence \(\Sigma>0\), their combined Gram matrix is positive definite, and \(R>0\).

The determinant inclusion identities give the exact moment derivatives

\[
\mathbb E(sY_i)=a_i,
\qquad
b_{ij}:=\mathbb E(sQ_{ij})=-2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{K_{ij}}A_{ij}).
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, diagonal changes do not appear directly in the second expression: the two linear terms created by differentiating \(\det K_{ij}\) cancel against the fixed centering terms.

Put \(M=T^T\Sigma^{-1}\). Orthogonally residualizing \(Q\) against \(Y\) gives the **exact** identity

\[
\boxed{
I=a^T\Sigma^{-1}a+(b-Ma)^TR^{-1}(b-Ma)
+\mathbb E(s-\Pi_{\le2}s)^2.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Here \(\Pi_{\le2}\) is the orthogonal projection onto the full space of occupation polynomials of degree at most two. Its centered expression is

\[
\Pi_{\le2}s=(\Sigma^{-1}a)^TY+
\bigl[R^{-1}(b-Ma)\bigr]^T(Q-T^T\Sigma^{-1}Y).
\]

Equation (8) is an orthogonal projection identity, not a covariance-only approximation to the score.

Write

\[
\frac{\overline{K_{ij}}A_{ij}}{|K_{ij}|}=u_{ij}+iv_{ij},
\qquad W_{ij,ij}=\frac{c_{ij}}{2|K_{ij}|^2}>0.
\tag{9}
\]

Then \(b_{ij}=-2|K_{ij}|u_{ij}\), and the coherent penalty is

\[
2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2=b^TWb+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}v_{ij}^2.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(C\) have zero diagonal and off-diagonal entries \(c_{ij}\). Because the normalization and first inclusion probabilities have zero second derivative, the complete frozen entropy identity is

\[
-H''=I-a^TCa+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}|A_{ij}|^2.
\tag{11}
\]

No acceleration term has been deleted except the constant and modular terms whose actual affine-kernel accelerations vanish.

Define

\[
\boxed{\quad
E=\Sigma-T(V+W^{-1})^{-1}T^T,
\qquad \mathcal S=E^{-1}-C.
\quad}
\tag{12}
\]

The matrix \(E\) is positive definite: it is a Schur complement of the positive matrix obtained by adding \(W^{-1}\) to the pair block of the Gram matrix.

**Theorem B — exact degree-two Schur certificate.** Under the assumptions above, \(\mathcal S\succeq0\) implies the full Hermitian entropy Hessian is negative semidefinite. If \(\mathcal S>0\), it is negative definite. More precisely, with

\[
b_*=(R^{-1}+W)^{-1}R^{-1}Ma,
\]

there is the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
-H''={}&\mathbb E(s-\Pi_{\le2}s)^2+a^T\mathcal S a\\
&+(b-b_*)^T(R^{-1}+W)(b-b_*)
+2\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}v_{ij}^2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** Complete the square in
\((b-Ma)^TR^{-1}(b-Ma)+b^TWb\). Its minimum over \(b\) is

\[
a^TM^T(R+W^{-1})^{-1}Ma.
\]

The Woodbury identity, using \(R=V-T^T\Sigma^{-1}T\), reads

\[
E^{-1}=\Sigma^{-1}
+\Sigma^{-1}T(R+W^{-1})^{-1}T^T\Sigma^{-1}.
\]

Substituting this square completion into (8), (10), and (11) proves (13). Strictness follows because a nonzero direction must have a nonzero diagonal vector, a nonzero phase-aligned coherent coordinate, or a nonzero phase-perpendicular coordinate. \(\square\)

The degree-one test is only \(\Sigma^{-1}-C\succeq0\). The positive addition in (12) is the information in the quadratic occupations after paying for coherent cancellation. Dropping this addition is precisely what fails in (3).

## 3. A useful analytic Gram bound near laws supported on at most two particles

The following lemma supplies an explicit, inexpensive proof of the interval family, avoiding symbolic inversion of its moving Gram matrix.

For a four-site occupation vector, let \(N=\sum_iX_i\), and define eleven degree-two polynomials indexed by subsets \(S\) of size at most two:

\[
g_\varnothing=1-N+\binom N2,\qquad
 g_i=X_i(2-N)=X_i-\sum_{j\ne i}X_iX_j,
\qquad g_{ij}=X_iX_j.
\tag{14}
\]

On atoms of size at most two, these are the coordinate delta functions:
\(g_S(T)=\mathbf1_{S=T}\). They form a basis for all polynomials of degree at most two; their sum is the constant polynomial 1.

For a full-support law \(p\), write

\[
G=\mathbb E[gg^T],\qquad m=\mathbb E[sg].
\]

Again the projection identity is exact:

\[
I=m^TG^{-1}m+\mathbb E(s-g^TG^{-1}m)^2.
\tag{15}
\]

**Lemma C — low-count Lagrange Gram domination.** Define

\[
d_S=p_S+\sum_{|T|\ge3}p_T|g_S(T)|\sum_{|U|\le2}|g_U(T)|.
\tag{16}
\]

Then

\[
G\preceq\operatorname{Diag}(d_S),
\qquad
I\ge m^TG^{-1}m\ge\sum_{|S|\le2}\frac{m_S^2}{d_S}.
\tag{17}
\]

**Proof.** For every real vector \(v\), weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\(vv^T\preceq\|v\|_1\operatorname{Diag}(|v|)\). Apply it to the evaluation vector \(g(T)\) at each high-count atom. At each low-count atom the evaluation vector is a coordinate vector, so its contribution is exactly the corresponding diagonal entry \(p_S\). Summing proves the Gram inequality. Inversion reverses the positive-definite order, and (15) gives the Fisher assertion. \(\square\)

For a triple atom, the nonzero evaluations are \(1\), three copies of \(-1\), and three copies of \(1\), so their absolute sum is 7. For the full atom the evaluations are \(3\), four copies of \(-2\), and six copies of \(1\), so the absolute sum is 17. Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_\varnothing&=p_\varnothing+7\sum_{|T|=3}p_T+51p_{1234},\\
d_i&=p_i+7\sum_{\substack{|T|=3\\i\in T}}p_T+34p_{1234},\\
d_{ij}&=p_{ij}+7\sum_{\substack{|T|=3\\ij\subset T}}p_T+17p_{1234}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]

This bound is a theorem about degree-two score projection with an explicit high-count correction; it is not an assumption that higher-order score components vanish.
