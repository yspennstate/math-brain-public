## 1. Precisely which Doob contribution is being studied

Let `r_t` be a twice differentiable family of positive laws on a finite set, with base law `r=r_0`. All logarithms are natural. Define

\[
f=-\log r,\qquad s=\frac{\dot r}{r},\qquad
u=\frac{\ddot r}{r},\qquad
\mathcal I=\mathbb E_r[s^2].
\tag{1}
\]

Here `u` is the normalized second derivative of the probability, not the second derivative of its logarithm. For any complete reveal filtration

\[
\mathcal F_0\subset\mathcal F_1\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal F_N,
\]

with trivial `F0`, freeze at `r` the two Doob martingales

\[
M_i=\mathbb E_r[f\mid\mathcal F_i],\qquad
U_i=\mathbb E_r[u\mid\mathcal F_i].
\]

The frozen curvature assigned to stage `i` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_i
&=\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\mathbb E_{r_t}[M_i-M_{i-1}]\right|_{t=0}\\
&=\mathbb E_r[u(M_i-M_{i-1})]\\
&=\mathbb E_r[(U_i-U_{i-1})(M_i-M_{i-1})].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

These are frozen functions: no derivative is taken through the conditional expectations defining `Mi`. The last equality follows from conditional orthogonality. Since `sum ddot r=0`,

\[
\boxed{H''(r_t)|_{0}=-\mathcal I+\sum_{i=1}^N C_i.}
\tag{3}
\]

Consecutive stages may be aggregated by summing their increments. In particular, the reservoir contribution below is the sum over all reservoir sites, in any order after the leaves. It is not asserted that its individual site increments have a sign.

## 2. A finite-law recovery identity

Let `p=(p_y)` be a positive finite probability vector and `d=(d_y)` satisfy

\[
\sum_y d_y=0,\qquad p_y+d_y>0,\qquad p_y-d_y>0.
\tag{4}
\]

On two bits `X=(X1,X2)` and a finite reservoir state `Y`, define, for sufficiently small real `t`,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
x&r_t(x,y)\\\hline
00&(\tfrac14-t^2)p_y+\tfrac14d_y\\
01&(\tfrac14+t^2)p_y\\
10&(\tfrac14+t^2)p_y\\
11&(\tfrac14-t^2)p_y-\tfrac14d_y.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]

This is a positive probability law on a neighborhood of zero. At zero the four leaf histories have probability `1/4`, with conditional reservoir laws

\[
p+d,\quad p,\quad p,\quad p-d.
\tag{6}
\]

Write

\[
\begin{aligned}
J&=D(p+d\Vert p)+D(p-d\Vert p),\\
L&=D(p\Vert p+d)+D(p\Vert p-d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

**Theorem 1 (exact recovery cancellation).** Reveal `X1`, then `X2`, then all of `Y`. At `t=0`, every first atom derivative is zero, and

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I=0,\qquad C_1=0,\qquad
C_{\rm leaves}=C_2=2J,\qquad
C_{\rm reservoir}=-2(J+L),\qquad H''=-2L.}
\tag{8}
\]

If `d` is nonzero, the leaf contribution is strictly positive, while the reservoir contribution is strictly negative.

**Proof.** Equation (5) gives `dot r_0=0` and

\[
\ddot r_0(00,y)=\ddot r_0(11,y)=-2p_y,\qquad
\ddot r_0(01,y)=\ddot r_0(10,y)=2p_y.
\tag{9}
\]

Both individual leaf marginals are constant `Bernoulli(1/2)` along the path, so a frozen function of the first revealed bit has zero curvature. Thus `C1=0`.

At the pair reveal,

\[
M_2(x)=\log4+H(r_0(Y\mid X=x)).
\]

The leaf-history second derivatives are `(-2,2,2,-2)`, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\rm leaves}
&=4H(p)-2H(p+d)-2H(p-d)\\
&=2J.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

The last identity uses `(p+d)+(p-d)=2p`. Direct summation of the complete frozen curvature gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x,y}\ddot r_0(x,y)[-\log r_0(x,y)]
&=2\sum_y p_y\log\frac{(p_y+d_y)(p_y-d_y)}{p_y^2}\\
&=-2L.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]

Since the Fisher term vanishes, this is also the entropy Hessian. Subtracting (10) proves the reservoir identity.

The cancellation can also be read directly as a second-score martingale residual. On leaf histories `00` and `11`, respectively,

\[
u-U_2=\frac{8d_y}{p_y+d_y},\qquad
u-U_2=-\frac{8d_y}{p_y-d_y};
\]

on histories `01,10` the residual is zero. Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_{\rm reservoir}
=-2\sum_y d_y\log\frac{p_y+d_y}{p_y-d_y}
=-2(J+L).}
\tag{12}
\]

The conditional entropy constants disappear because `sum d=0`. This also identifies the late contribution with minus the symmetric relative entropy between `p+d` and `p-d`. Strict positivity of `J` when `d` is nonzero proves the asserted signs. ∎

## 3. An arbitrary-dimensional DPP realization

Let the reservoir have `m≥1` measured sites, let `D=D*` be `m×m`, and let `w` be a complex column vector such that

\[
D-4ww^*\succ0,\qquad I-D-4ww^*\succ0.
\tag{13}
\]

Consider the Hermitian affine kernel path

\[
\boxed{K(t)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\tfrac12&it&w^*\\
-it&\tfrac12&w^*\\
w&w&D
\end{pmatrix}.}
\tag{14}
\]

The two leaves are independent at zero **as a marginal law**; they need not be independent after conditioning on the reservoir. Schur complements in `K(0)` and `I-K(0)` show that (13) is exactly their strict positivity condition. Thus (14) is a strict contraction for all sufficiently small `|t|`.

Put

\[
p_y=P_D(Y=y),\qquad
d_y=P_{D+4ww^*}(Y=y)-p_y.
\tag{15}
\]

For every atom, a rank-one change of its kernel makes its exact atom determinant affine in the change parameter. Consequently

\[
P_{D+\lambda ww^*}(Y=y)=p_y+\frac\lambda4d_y,
\qquad
P_{D-4ww^*}(Y=y)=p_y-d_y.
\tag{16}
\]

The probability notation in (16) is used wherever the kernel is a contraction; the determinant identity itself holds for every `λ`. For `Q_y=D-I_{y^c}`, the atom probability is `(-1)^{|y^c|}det Q_y`, and `det(Q_y+λww*)` is affine in `λ` by determinant multilinearity. This does not require commuting matrices or a change of measurement basis. Conditions (13) make `D` and `D±4ww*` strict contractions, so their atoms are positive and (4) holds. To see strict atom positivity directly, partition `Q_y` into present and absent coordinates: the present block is positive definite and its absent Schur complement is negative definite, giving precisely the determinant sign `(-1)^{|y^c|}`. The empty-block cases follow directly.

**Theorem 2 (DPP recovery theorem).** The exact law of (14) has precisely form (5), with (15). Hence all of Theorem 1 holds for any coherent reservoir `D` and any complex `w` satisfying (13).

**Proof.** Let `xi∈{0,1}` and `σi=2xi-1`. In the signed atom matrix, its leaf block is

\[
B_x(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_1/2&it\\-it&\sigma_2/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det B_x=\sigma_1\sigma_2/4-t^2.
\]

For the two identical links, its Schur complement coefficient is

\[
(1,1)B_x(t)^{-1}(1,1)^T
=\frac{\sigma_1+\sigma_2}{2\det B_x(t)}.
\tag{17}
\]

The imaginary terms cancel. Write `a(t)=1/4-t²` and `b(t)=1/4+t²`. The marginal leaf probabilities are `a,b,b,a`, and the reservoir conditional kernels are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
x&P(X=x)&K_{Y\mid X=x}\\\hline
00&a&D+ww^*/a\\
01&b&D\\
10&b&D\\
11&a&D-ww^*/a.
\end{array}
\tag{18}
\]

These formulas follow from the signed determinant Schur complement, including the absent-leaf signs. Multiplying the affine conditional atom probabilities (16) by their history probabilities gives exactly (5).

If `w≠0`, then `d≠0`: the inclusion marginal of reservoir coordinate `j` changes by `4|wj|²`, so

\[
\sum_{y:j\in y}d_y=4|w_j|^2.
\tag{19}
\]

Thus every nonzero permitted link gives strict positive early curvature and strict negative total curvature. ∎

## 4. Sharp factor two, with an exact quartic improvement

Under (4), let `zy=dy/py`, so `|zy|<1`. The absolutely convergent scalar expansions are

\[
\begin{aligned}
L&=\sum_y p_y\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{z_y^{2k}}k,\\
J&=\sum_y p_y\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{z_y^{2k}}{k(2k-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

For the second expansion, differentiate `(1+z)log(1+z)+(1-z)log(1-z)` twice to obtain `2/(1-z²)`, then integrate twice from zero. The first is the expansion of `-log(1-z²)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{L-J
=\sum_y p_y\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{2(k-1)}{k(2k-1)}z_y^{2k}
\ge\frac13\sum_y\frac{d_y^4}{p_y^3}.}
\tag{21}
\]

Combining this with (8) proves

\[
\boxed{C_{\rm reservoir}
\le-2C_{\rm leaves}-\frac23\sum_y\frac{d_y^4}{p_y^3},
\qquad
H''\le-C_{\rm leaves}-\frac23\sum_y\frac{d_y^4}{p_y^3}.}
\tag{22}
\]

In particular, the late reveal cancels at least twice the early positive contribution. Both improvements are strict for nonzero `d`.

The factor two is optimal even within the DPP construction. Fix a strict reservoir `D` and a nonzero link vector `v`, and use `w=εv` for sufficiently small positive `ε`. By (16), `d=ε²d0` for a fixed nonzero vector `d0`. Set

\[
B=\sum_y\frac{d_{0,y}^2}{p_y}>0.
\]

Since the alphabet is finite, (20) gives

\[
J=\varepsilon^4B+O(\varepsilon^8),\qquad
L=\varepsilon^4B+O(\varepsilon^8),
\quad
\frac{-C_{\rm reservoir}}{C_{\rm leaves}}
=1+\frac LJ\longrightarrow2.
\tag{23}
\]

Thus no uniform factor larger than two can replace the first factor in (22). The coefficient `1/3` in (21) is likewise the exact leading quartic coefficient.

## 5. Exact rational three-site example

Take

\[
K(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&it&1/4\\
-it&1/2&1/4\\
1/4&1/4&1/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=K'(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i&0\\-i&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{24}
\]

Its eigenvalues are `1/2` and `1/2±sqrt(t²+1/8)`. Hence it is a strict contraction throughout

\[
|t|<\frac1{2\sqrt2}.
\tag{25}
\]

In the atom order `X1 X2 Y`, its probabilities are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
000,111&1/16-t^2/2\\
001,110&3/16-t^2/2\\
010,011,100,101&1/8+t^2/2.
\end{array}
\tag{26}
\]

Here `p=(1/2,1/2)` and `d=(-1/4,1/4)`. Exact evaluation gives

\[
J=\tfrac32\log3-2\log2,\qquad
L=2\log2-\log3,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I(0)=0,\quad
C_{\rm leaves}(0)=\log(27/16)>0,\quad
C_{\rm reservoir}(0)=-\log3,\quad
H''(0)=-2\log(4/3)<0.}
\tag{27}
\]

These signs follow from the indicated rational logarithm arguments, without a floating-point sign test.

## 6. The obstruction persists with nonzero Fisher information

At a nonzero base parameter `t`, use the same velocity `A`, but freeze the Doob functions at `r_t`. Each first atom derivative in (26) is `±t`. Consequently the **complete atom Fisher information** is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I(t)=32t^2\left(
\frac1{1-8t^2}+\frac1{3-8t^2}+\frac1{1+4t^2}
\right)>0}
\tag{28}
\]

for `0<|t|<1/(2sqrt2)`, with

\[
\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\mathcal I(t)}{t^2}=\frac{224}{3}.
\tag{29}
\]

For clarity, the early frozen curvature at this moving base also has a direct formula. Put

\[
a_t=1/4-t^2,\quad b_t=1/4+t^2,\quad
\delta_t=\frac{d}{4a_t},\quad
J_t=D(p+\delta_t\Vert p)+D(p-\delta_t\Vert p).
\]

The two conditional laws for equal leaf bits are `p±δt`, and the pair marginal is `at,bt,bt,at`. Since the leaf marginal second derivatives are still `(-2,2,2,-2)`,

\[
\boxed{C_{\rm leaves}(t)=4\log(a_t/b_t)+2J_t
\longrightarrow\log(27/16)>0.}
\tag{30}
\]

The first revealed bit still contributes zero. Positivity of the probabilities in (25) makes the displayed functions continuous. Thus

\[
\boxed{\frac{C_{\rm leaves}(t)}{\mathcal I(t)}\longrightarrow+\infty,
\qquad
t^2\frac{C_{\rm leaves}(t)}{\mathcal I(t)}
\longrightarrow\frac3{224}\log(27/16).}
\tag{31}
\]

This is an exact obstruction: **there is no finite universal constant `C` such that the early frozen Doob contribution is at most `C` times the entire Fisher information**, even restricting to strict three-site kernels, this fixed reveal order, and directions with strictly positive Fisher information. The base kernels converge to an interior point, so the obstruction is not a spectral-boundary effect.

The conclusion concerns this particular decomposition and this proposed Fisher-only payment for its early positive part. The late frozen terms are indispensable here. The example has negative full entropy curvature and does not refute Lyons entropy concavity.
