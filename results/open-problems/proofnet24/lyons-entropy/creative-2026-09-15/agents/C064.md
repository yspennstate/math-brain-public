## 1. Projection flags and the precise diamond hypothesis

Let \(W\subset\mathbb C^n\) have dimension \(r\), and let \(u,v\) be orthonormal
and perpendicular to \(W\). Denote the four projection DPP laws by
\[
\mu_0=P_W,\quad \mu_1=P_{W+[u]},\quad
\mu_2=P_{W+[v]},\quad \mu_3=P_{W+[u,v]}.
\]
Write \(H_j=H(\mu_j)\), with natural logarithms throughout, and set
\[
\beta=H_1+H_2-H_0-H_3,\qquad
m=\frac{\mu_1+\mu_2}{2},\qquad
\beta_*=2H(m)-H_0-H_3.
\]

An **unlabeled diamond** is a probability law on pairs \((S,T)\) such that
\(S\subset T\), \(|S|=r\), \(|T|=r+2\), the bottom and top laws are
\(\mu_0,\mu_3\), and the sum of the laws of its two possible middle sets is
\(\mu_1+\mu_2\). The two middle sets need not have the individually prescribed
laws \(\mu_1,\mu_2\).

Orient the two distinct additions by a fair coin conditional on \((S,T)\).
Call the ordered additions \(i,j\), and put
\[
M=S\cup\{i\},\qquad N=S\cup\{j\}.
\]
Both \(M\) and \(N\) have law \(m\).

## 2. Exact fair-middle information identity

**Lemma 1.** Every such finite diamond satisfies
\[
\boxed{\beta_*=
\log2+H(S\mid T)-2H(S\mid M)+I(i;j\mid S).}\tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** The pair \((S,i)\) is equivalent to \((S,M)\). Fair orientation gives
the same joint entropy for \((S,i)\) and \((S,j)\), while
\((S,i,j)\) is equivalent to \((S,T)\) together with its independent fair
orientation bit. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
I(i;j\mid S)
&=H(S,i)+H(S,j)-H(S)-H(S,i,j)\\
&=2H(m)+2H(S\mid M)-H(S)-H(S,T)-\log2\\
&=\beta_*+2H(S\mid M)-H(S\mid T)-\log2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Rearranging proves (1). No middle-label realization is used. \(\square\)

Given \(M\), the set \(S\) is obtained by deleting one of its \(r+1\) elements,
so \(H(S\mid M)\le\log(r+1)\). The other two information terms in (1) are
nonnegative. Hence
\[
\beta_*\ge\log2-2\log(r+1).\tag{2}
\]
Complementing every set replaces \(r\) by \(n-r-2\), exchanges bottom and top,
and preserves the middle entropy. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\beta_*\ge\log2-2\log(k+1),\qquad
k=\min(r,n-r-2).}\tag{3}
\]

The equal-weight Jensen–Shannon divergence satisfies
\[
J=H(m)-\frac{H_1+H_2}{2}\in[0,\log2].
\]
Indeed it is the mutual information between a fair binary label and an
observation sampled from the corresponding middle law. Thus
\[
\beta=\beta_*-2J
\]
and the exact identity and useful bound are
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\beta&=\log2+H(S\mid T)-2H(S\mid M)+I(i;j\mid S)-2J,\\
\beta&\ge-2\log(k+1)-\log2.
\end{aligned}}\tag{4}
\]
For a rank-one bottom, \(\beta\ge-3\log2\). These are lower bounds, not a
claim that \(\beta\ge0\), and their constants are not claimed sharp.

## 3. Sharp same-sign curvature of the two-Bernoulli count

Let
\[
F(p,q)=H(\operatorname{Bern}(p)+\operatorname{Bern}(q)),\qquad 0<p,q<1,
\]
with independent Bernoulli variables. Along an affine line let
\(p'=\alpha,\ q'=\zeta\). Define
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=(1-p)(1-q),\quad b=p(1-q)+q(1-p),\quad c=pq,\\
v=p(1-p),\quad w=q(1-q),\quad
z=\frac b{\sqrt{ac}}=\frac b{\sqrt{vw}}\ge2,\\
b'=\alpha(1-2q)+\zeta(1-2p).
\end{gathered}
\]
The inequality \(z\ge2\) is AM–GM on \(p(1-q)\) and \(q(1-p)\).

**Theorem 2 (sharp constant).** If \(\alpha\zeta\ge0\), then
\[
\boxed{F''\le-2(4-2\log2)\alpha\zeta.}\tag{5}
\]
For \(\alpha\zeta>0\), equality holds exactly when
\(p=q=1/2\) and \(\alpha=\zeta\). In particular the constant cannot increase.

**Proof.** The count Fisher information is
\[
\mathcal I=\frac{(a')^2}{a}+\frac{(b')^2}{b}+\frac{(c')^2}{c}.
\]
Expanding just the two extreme terms gives
\[
\frac{(a')^2}{a}+\frac{(c')^2}{c}
=b\frac{\alpha^2}{v}+b\frac{\zeta^2}{w}+4\alpha\zeta.
\]
For example, the coefficient of \(\alpha^2\) is
\((1-q)/(1-p)+q/p=b/v\); the other coefficients follow directly.
Completing one square yields the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I
=2\alpha\zeta(z+2)
+b\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt v}-\frac{\zeta}{\sqrt w}\right)^2
+\frac{(b')^2}{b}.}\tag{6}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal I/(2\alpha\zeta)\ge z+2\) for positive slope product.
This Fisher bound is attained at every possible \(z\ge2\) by suitable
\(p+q=1,\ \alpha=\zeta\).

Since \((a'',b'',c'')=2\alpha\zeta(1,-2,1)\), direct differentiation of entropy
gives
\[
F''=-\mathcal I+2\alpha\zeta\log\frac{b^2}{ac}
=-\mathcal I+4\alpha\zeta\log z.\tag{7}
\]
Combining (6) and (7), with \(C=4-2\log2\), gives a sharper exact form:
\[
\boxed{
F''+2C\alpha\zeta
=-b\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt v}-\frac{\zeta}{\sqrt w}\right)^2
-\frac{(b')^2}{b}
-2\alpha\zeta\left[z-2-2\log\frac z2\right].}\tag{8}
\]
For \(z\ge2\), the last bracket is nonnegative: it vanishes at \(z=2\) and its
derivative is \(1-2/z\ge0\). This proves (5).

If \(\alpha\zeta>0\), equality in (8) forces \(z=2\), hence \(p=q\).
The first square then forces \(\alpha=\zeta\); the second forces
\(p=q=1/2\). These choices do attain equality. \(\square\)

Equation (6) shortens the saved checkpoint proof, which obtained the same
Fisher bound through the full Fisher matrix and a monotonicity calculation.
It is a scalar count identity; no active-matrix extension is claimed here.

## 4. Compensation in the exact spectral entropy

Consider
\[
K_{p,q}=P_W+p\,uu^*+q\,vv^*.
\]
The Bernoulli/projection expansion and disjoint cardinality levels give
\[
P_{K_{p,q}}
=a\mu_0+x\mu_1+y\mu_2+c\mu_3,\qquad
x=p(1-q),\ y=q(1-p),\ x+y=b.
\]
Define the homogeneous Jensen–Shannon perspective
\[
G(x,y)=(x+y)H\left(\frac{x\mu_1+y\mu_2}{x+y}\right)-xH_1-yH_2.
\]
Conditioning on cardinality gives exactly
\[
H(P_{K_{p,q}})
=F(p,q)+H_0+p(H_1-H_0)+q(H_2-H_0)-\beta pq+G(x,y).\tag{9}
\]

The function \(G\) is concave in \((x,y)\). On the union of the two middle
supports,
\[
G_x=D\left(\mu_1\middle\Vert\frac{x\mu_1+y\mu_2}{x+y}\right)\ge0,\quad
G_y=D\left(\mu_2\middle\Vert\frac{x\mu_1+y\mu_2}{x+y}\right)\ge0.
\]
Because \(x''=y''=-2\alpha\zeta\),
\[
G''=(x',y')\nabla^2G(x',y')^{\mathsf T}
-2\alpha\zeta(G_x+G_y)\le0
\quad\text{when }\alpha\zeta\ge0.\tag{10}
\]
Thus Theorem 2 proves the quantitative bound
\[
\boxed{H''\le-2(C+\beta)\alpha\zeta,\qquad
C=4-2\log2,\quad \alpha\zeta\ge0.}\tag{11}
\]
In particular **a certified diamond and \(\beta\ge-C\) suffice for full
spectral-square concavity**, once the opposite-sign argument below is added.
Projection-entropy nonnegativity is stronger than this sufficient condition.

For \(k\le1\), (4) and (11) give, in positive-product directions,
\[
\boxed{H''\le-2(4-5\log2)\alpha\zeta<0.}\tag{12}
\]
Here \(4-5\log2>0\). For instance
\(\log2=2\sum_{j\ge0}(1/3)^{2j+1}/(2j+1)<3/4<4/5\).

## 5. Opposite signs and the entire closed square

The diamond supplies the signed atom identity
\[
\mu_0+\mu_3-\mu_1-\mu_2
=\mathbb E(\delta_S+\delta_T-\delta_M-\delta_N).
\]
At \(0<p,q<1\), every vertex of every positive-weight diamond has positive
probability \(\pi_X=P_{K_{p,q}}(X)\). The determinantal atom inequality is
\[
\pi_M\pi_N\ge\pi_S\pi_T.\tag{13}
\]
To justify it even when \(K\) has zero and unit eigenvalues, replace \(K\) by
\((1-2\varepsilon)K+\varepsilon I\). Its positive definite L-ensemble obeys
(13) by the principal-minor Hadamard–Fisher inequality. Let
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\); the four relevant limiting probabilities are
positive.

The exact atom Hessian formula now reads
\[
H''=-\sum_X\frac{(\pi_X')^2}{\pi_X}
+2\alpha\zeta\,\mathbb E\log\frac{\pi_M\pi_N}{\pi_S\pi_T}\le0
\quad\text{when }\alpha\zeta\le0.\tag{14}
\]
All terms are finite on the fixed positive support. This includes a zero
slope product directly.

For rank-one \(W=[w]\), prior rank-one covering and C033 provide a diamond
for every orthonormal complex triple \(w,u,v\). Equations (12) and (14)
therefore prove
\[
\boxed{(p,q)\longmapsto H(P_{ww^*+p\,uu^*+q\,vv^*})
\text{ is jointly concave on }[0,1]^2.}\tag{15}
\]
The same conclusion holds for flags with top codimension one by
complementation. Nonpositive Hessian on the open square gives concavity on
interior line segments. Approximating both endpoints inside the square and
using continuity of finite Shannon entropy gives the whole closed square,
including all vanishing-coordinate and vanishing-minor cases.

As stated at the start, ROOT_DIAMOND_ACTIVE_MATRIX.md now proves a stronger
noncommuting theorem that includes (15). The completed C064 work supplies
the exact fair-middle identity and the sharp count calculation, together
with this original spectral route.
