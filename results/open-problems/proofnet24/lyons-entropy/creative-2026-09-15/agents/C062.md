## 1. The concavity theorem and its scope

Partition the measured coordinates as
\[
E=U\sqcup O_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup O_m,\qquad |U|=2.
\]
Fix arbitrary Hermitian contractions \(C_j\) on \(O_j\) and arbitrary complex matrices \(B_j\in\mathbb C^{2\times |O_j|}\). Each \(B_j\) may have rank two. Consider the affine kernel family
\[
K(A,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
A&z_1B_1&\cdots&z_mB_m\\
\bar z_1 B_1^*&C_1&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
\bar z_m B_m^*&0&&C_m
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A=A^*,\quad z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\in\mathbb C^m.
\tag{1}
\]
The reservoir blocks have no cross entries between them. Their occupation vectors \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\) are consequently mutually independent in every member of this family, although each block may have arbitrary internal coherence.

**Theorem 1 (satellite-block concavity).** The function
\[
\mathcal H(A,z)=H(P_{K(A,z)})
\]
is jointly concave on the closed convex domain \(0\preceq K(A,z)\preceq I\). The core \(A\) varies freely over Hermitian two-by-two matrices, and every complex scalar \(z_j\) varies independently. Thus every admissible affine line
\[
A(u)=A_0+uE,\qquad z_j(u)=a_j+u b_j
\]
has concave measured Shannon entropy, with arbitrary Hermitian \(E\) and arbitrary complex slopes \(b_j\).

Theorem 2 below supplies an exact sum of three nonnegative finite gaps, an additive mutual-information improvement, and the explicit bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t\ge
8t(1-t)\sum_{j=1}^m
|z_{1j}-z_{0j}|^2\,\bar s_j\,
\max_{i\in U,\ k\in O_j}|(B_j)_{ik}|^4,
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
where \(\Gamma_t\) is the actual entropy Jensen gap and
\(\bar s_j=(1-t)|z_{0j}|^2+t|z_{1j}|^2\). An empty or identically zero block contributes zero. In particular the entropy inequality is strict for \(0<t<1\) whenever some nonzero \(B_j\) has \(z_{0j}\ne z_{1j}\), including boundary kernels.

The prior source, Section 6, proves the case in which one scalar multiplies the entire cross matrix, with an arbitrary fixed exterior. For \(m=1\), the present theorem has exactly that scope. The extension is the collection of independent complex strengths for mutually independent coherent reservoir blocks. Singleton reservoirs are contained in C059's simultaneous-singleton result; the distinguishing case here has several coherent reservoirs and unrestricted rank-two \(B_j\). C031 and C055 are endpoint theorems with one structured endpoint and an arbitrary other endpoint; their results do not automatically give concavity on all interior subsegments of (1).

All coordinates are measured coordinates. No arbitrary unitary change of measurement basis is used. Historical priority outside this campaign has not been investigated, and full Lyons concavity remains open.

## 2. Conditional kernels and the convex strength domain

Initially assume \(0\prec C_j\prec I\). For an atom \(y_j\subseteq O_j\), set
\[
Q_{j,y_j}=C_j-P_{O_j\setminus y_j},\qquad
q_j(y_j)=(-1)^{|O_j\setminus y_j|}\det Q_{j,y_j}>0,
\]
\[
D_{j,y_j}=B_jQ_{j,y_j}^{-1}B_j^*,\qquad
q(y)=\prod_j q_j(y_j).
\tag{3}
\]
The matrices \(D_{j,y_j}\) are Hermitian, with no assumed sign. If
\(s_j=|z_j|^2\), conditioning on all reservoir atoms gives the core DPP kernel
\[
M_y(A,s)=A-\sum_j s_jD_{j,y_j}.
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness, inclusion-exclusion on absent exterior coordinates gives, for \(W\subseteq U\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{K(A,z)}(W\subseteq X,\ Y=y)
&=(-1)^{|O\setminus y|}
\det\begin{pmatrix}
A_{WW}&(z_jB_j)_{W,O}\\
(\bar z_jB_j^*)_{O,W}&\operatorname{diag}_j Q_{j,y_j}
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=q(y)\det(M_y(A,s))_{WW}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Successive included-site and excluded-site Schur complements show
\(0\preceq M_y\preceq I\). Strict full kernels give strict conditional kernels. Formula (5) proves the assertion for the entire conditional law, since its inclusion probabilities determine that finite law.

Put
\[
L(A,s)=K(A,\sqrt{s_1},\ldots,\sqrt{s_m}),\qquad s_j\ge0.
\]
The two Schur complements of the fixed exterior and its complement show that its exact closed feasibility domain is
\[
\mathcal D=\left\{(A,s):
\begin{array}{l}
s_j\ge0,\\
A-\sum_j s_j B_jC_j^{-1}B_j^*\succeq0,\\
I-A-\sum_j s_j B_j(I-C_j)^{-1}B_j^*\succeq0
\end{array}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Both matrix inequalities are affine in \((A,s)\). Therefore \(\mathcal D\) is convex. At any fixed \(A\), it is downward closed: if \((A,s)\in\mathcal D\) and \(0\le s'\le s\) coordinatewise, then \((A,s')\in\mathcal D\). This uses the positive semidefiniteness of the two coefficient matrices for each \(s_j\), not a sign for the conditional matrices in (3).

Different choices of the phase of \(z_j\) are coordinate diagonal unitary conjugations of \(L(A,s)\); all measured principal minors and atom probabilities are unchanged. Equivalently this phase invariance follows directly from (5). Thus define
\[
F(A,s)=H(P_{L(A,s)})
=\sum_j H(P_{C_j})+\sum_yq(y)H_2(M_y(A,s)).
\tag{7}
\]
The established full two-site entropy theorem, proved in the prior source Section 1, implies that \(F\) is jointly concave on \(\mathcal D\): the weights and reservoir entropies are fixed and every conditional kernel in (4) is affine in \((A,s)\).

## 3. Decoupling gives coordinatewise monotonicity

Fix a feasible \((A,s)\), and let \(s^{\setminus j}\) agree with \(s\) except for its zero \(j\)-th entry. Under \(P_{L(A,s)}\), let \(X\) denote the measured core occupation and \(Y_j\) the entire \(j\)-th reservoir occupation. The principal marginal on \(U\cup O_{-j}\) is unchanged when block \(j\) is decoupled. The decoupled kernel is block diagonal across \(O_j\) and its complement. Hence
\[
F(A,s^{\setminus j})-F(A,s)
=I_{L(A,s)}(Y_j;X,Y_{-j})\ge0.
\tag{8}
\]
This identifies zero strength as a maximum along every feasible single-strength interval, with the other strengths fixed.

A concave function on \([0,b]\) whose value at zero is maximal is nonincreasing. Indeed for \(0\le a\le b\),
\[
f(a)\ge(1-a/b)f(0)+(a/b)f(b)\ge f(b).
\]
Use the downward closure of (6) and joint concavity of (7) to apply this fact one coordinate at a time. Consequently
\[
0\le s'\le s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(A,s')\ge F(A,s).
\tag{9}
\]

For endpoints \((A_0,z_0),(A_1,z_1)\), write
\[
A_t=(1-t)A_0+tA_1,\quad z_t=(1-t)z_0+tz_1,
\]
\[
s_{aj}=|z_{aj}|^2,\quad
\bar s_j=(1-t)s_{0j}+t s_{1j},\quad
\delta_j=t(1-t)|z_{1j}-z_{0j}|^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The scalar identity \(|z_{tj}|^2=\bar s_j-\delta_j\) and (6) show that both \((A_t,\bar s)\) and \((A_t,|z_t|^2)\) are feasible. By (9), followed by joint concavity in the strength variables,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H(A_t,z_t)
&=F(A_t,\bar s-\delta)\\
&\ge F(A_t,\bar s)\\
&\ge (1-t)F(A_0,s_0)+tF(A_1,s_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
This proves Theorem 1 for strict reservoir blocks, including feasible full kernels on the spectral boundary. Section 6 removes the reservoir strictness assumption.

## 4. An exact finite Jensen gap with independent block removal

Assume \(0<t<1\). Put
\[
L=L(A_t,\bar s),\qquad
\lambda_j=\begin{cases}\delta_j/\bar s_j,&\bar s_j>0,\\0,&\bar s_j=0.\end{cases}
\tag{12}
\]
Then \(0\le\lambda_j\le1\). Independently select a random set \(R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), including block \(j\) with probability \(\lambda_j\). For a selected set, define
\[
s_j^R=\bar s_j\mathbf1_{\{j\notin R\}},\qquad
L^R=L(A_t,s^R).
\tag{13}
\]
All these kernels are feasible by downward closure, and
\(\mathbb E_Rs^R=|z_t|^2\).

Use the conditional kernels
\[
M_y^a=M_y(A_a,s_a),\quad
\bar M_y=M_y(A_t,\bar s)=(1-t)M_y^0+tM_y^1,
\]
\[
M_y^R=M_y(A_t,s^R),\quad
M_y^t=M_y(A_t,|z_t|^2)=\mathbb E_R M_y^R.
\tag{14}
\]
Define two conditional two-site Jensen gaps,
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\rm end}
&=\sum_yq(y)\big[H_2(\bar M_y)-(1-t)H_2(M_y^0)-tH_2(M_y^1)\big],\\
J_{\rm remove}
&=\sum_yq(y)\big[H_2(M_y^t)-\mathbb E_RH_2(M_y^R)\big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Each is nonnegative by full two-site concavity, including its finite multiway form.

**Theorem 2 (exact nonnegative decomposition).** With all information quantities on the right computed under the single auxiliary law \(P_L\),
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t=J_{\rm end}+J_{\rm remove}
+\mathbb E_R I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c}).
\quad}
\tag{16}
\]
Here an empty occupation vector is a constant and contributes zero information.

**Proof.** Equation (7) gives
\[
J_{\rm end}=H(P_L)-(1-t)H(P_{K_0})-tH(P_{K_1}),
\]
\[
J_{\rm remove}=H(P_{K_t})-\mathbb E_RH(P_{L^R}).
\tag{17}
\]
The kernel \(L^R\) has the original principal marginal on \(U\cup O_{R^c}\), independently accompanied by all original reservoir laws on \(O_R\). Since the reservoir blocks were already mutually independent under \(P_L\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(P_{L^R})-H(P_L)
&=H_L(Y_R)+H_L(X,Y_{R^c})-H_L(X,Y)\\
&=I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Adding (17) and the expectation of (18) proves (16). \(\square\)

The independent random set \(R\) averages the conditional **kernels** in (14). The second Jensen gap accounts for the nonlinear passage from those kernels to their two-site laws. For rank-two \(B_j\), an atom law generally has a nonzero quadratic term in \(s_j\); it need not be a literal classical reset mixture. Formula (16) uses no reset assertion and no signed Jeffreys identity.

The prior two-site determinant bound also gives
\[
J_{\rm end}\ge4t(1-t)
\sum_yq(y)\,[\det(M_y^1-M_y^0)]_+.
\tag{19}
\]
One may retain this in addition to the information bounds below. C055's coherent two-site bound separately yields
\[
J_{\rm end}\ge\frac29t(1-t)
\sum_yq(y)|(M_y^0)_{12}|^2\|M_y^1-M_y^0\|_F^2.
\tag{20}
\]
These are uses of established conditional two-site estimates, not new claims about their constants.

## 5. Additive information and an explicit quartic kernel bound

Independence of the reservoir vectors provides an additive lower bound, even though their conditional laws given \(X\) are generally dependent. Order the selected blocks in \(R\), and let \(R_{<j}\) be the selected blocks preceding \(j\). The entropy chain rule gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_L(Y_R;X,Y_{R^c})
&=\sum_{j\in R}\big[H(Y_j)-H(Y_j\mid X,Y_{R^c},Y_{R_{<j}})\big]\\
&=\sum_{j\in R}\Big[I_L(Y_j;X)
+I_L(Y_j;Y_{R^c},Y_{R_{<j}}\mid X)\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
In the first line, \(H(Y_j\mid Y_{R_{<j}})=H(Y_j)\) uses mutual independence. Thus (16) proves
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t\ge J_{\rm end}+J_{\rm remove}
+\sum_j\lambda_j I_L(Y_j;X)
\ge\sum_j\lambda_j I_L(Y_j;X).
\quad}
\tag{22}
\]

For a core coordinate \(i\) and a coordinate \(k\in O_j\), the two Bernoulli occupations under \(P_L\) have covariance
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_L(X_i,(Y_j)_k)=-\bar s_j|(B_j)_{ik}|^2.
\tag{23}
\]
A binary joint table of covariance \(c\) differs from the product table by \((c,-c,-c,c)\). Its total variation distance from that product is \(2|c|\). Pinsker's inequality with natural logarithms, followed by data processing under the coordinate projections, therefore gives
\[
I_L(Y_j;X)\ge I_L((Y_j)_k;X_i)
\ge8\bar s_j^2|(B_j)_{ik}|^4.
\tag{24}
\]
Take the maximum over \((i,k)\), multiply by \(\lambda_j\), and sum. Since
\(\lambda_j\bar s_j^2=\delta_j\bar s_j\), this proves (2). A block with \(\bar s_j=0\) contributes zero on both sides. For a changing scalar and a nonzero block, \(\delta_j>0\), \(\bar s_j>0\), and the corresponding contribution in (2) is strictly positive.

The lower bound is quartic in cross entries, as required by the entropy loss near a product of fixed marginals. For a local differentiable line through an interior point, (2) also implies the directional consequence
\[
-\frac{d^2}{du^2}H(P_{K(A(u),z(u))})\bigg|_{u=0}
\ge16\sum_j |z'_j(0)|^2|z_j(0)|^2
\max_{i,k}|(B_j)_{ik}|^4.
\tag{25}
\]
To obtain the constant, use endpoints at \(u=\pm h\), \(t=1/2\), and divide the resulting gap by \(h^2/2\) before sending \(h\) to zero. The primary theorem is the finite inequality; (25) asserts no strict Hessian control when the relevant base strength vanishes.

## 6. Zero strengths and boundary reservoir blocks

If \(\bar s_j=0\) and \(0<t<1\), then \(z_{0j}=z_{1j}=z_{tj}=0\), so \(\delta_j=0\) and this block is already decoupled in every kernel in (14). Our convention \(\lambda_j=0\) is therefore exact. If \(t=0\) or \(t=1\), the chord gap is zero and the finite inequality is immediate; (16) has the evident zero-gap interpretation with \(R=\varnothing\). Blocks with \(\lambda_j=1\) cause no problem: they are selected deterministically and the target cross scalar is zero.

For arbitrary closed reservoir contractions and closed endpoint kernels, use the common regularization
\[
K_a^\varepsilon=(1-2\varepsilon)K_a+\varepsilon I,
\qquad0<\varepsilon<1/2.
\tag{26}
\]
Write \(c_\varepsilon=1-2\varepsilon\). This has the same form (1), with
\[
A_a^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon A_a+\varepsilon I_2,
\quad C_j^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon C_j+\varepsilon I_{O_j},
\quad z_{aj}^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon z_{aj},
\quad B_j^\varepsilon=B_j.
\tag{27}
\]
Consequently \(\bar s_j^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon^2\bar s_j\),
\(\delta_j^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon^2\delta_j\), and all \(\lambda_j\) remain unchanged. The auxiliary and partially decoupled kernels satisfy exactly
\[
L^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon L+\varepsilon I,
\qquad (L^R)^\varepsilon=c_\varepsilon L^R+\varepsilon I.
\tag{28}
\]
In particular this proves feasibility of the unregularized auxiliary kernels by a limit, without inverses of singular \(C_j\).

Finite DPP atom probabilities are polynomials in the entries. Finite entropy and mutual information are continuous, using \(0\log0=0\). Thus (11), (16), (22), and (2) pass to the limit. The explicit right side of (2) is multiplied by \(c_\varepsilon^4\) before the limit and converges to the stated bound.

For a boundary atom \(y\) with \(q(y)>0\), the block diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}_j Q_{j,y_j}\) remains invertible because its determinant is, up to sign, \(q(y)\); formulas (3)–(5) therefore still apply. Omit atoms with zero weight. In (15), each entropy is bounded by \(\log4\); hence all terms from atoms tending to zero weight vanish. The two conditional gaps have well-defined limits, equivalently the unconditional entropy differences (17). This establishes the exact identity on the boundary as well as its inequalities.

## 7. Independent finite-output channels preserve the theorem

There is an immediate extension using the balanced-pair refinement constructed in C061, Sections 2–3. Its general identity applies to any number of input sites; the specific concavity input here is Theorem 1.

At each measured coordinate \(i\in E\), fix an arbitrary binary-input channel \(T_i(o\mid x)\) with a finite output alphabet. Apply these channels independently to the occupation bits under \(P_{K(A,z)}\), and let \(Z\) be the output vector. Define the channel distinction
\[
\tau_i=\operatorname{TV}\big(T_i(\cdot\mid0),T_i(\cdot\mid1)\big)\in[0,1].
\tag{29}
\]

**Theorem 3 (finite local observations).** The output entropy \(H(Z)\) is jointly concave in \((A,z)\) on the same satellite-kernel domain. Its chord gap satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma_t^{\rm out}\ge8t(1-t)\sum_j
|z_{1j}-z_{0j}|^2\bar s_j
\max_{i\in U,\ k\in O_j}
\big[\tau_i^2\tau_k^2|(B_j)_{ik}|^4\big].
\quad}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus a changing satellite scalar still gives strict observed entropy concavity whenever an affected nonzero entry joins two locally distinguishable input bits.

Here are the precise refinement facts used from C061. Every channel has a refinement into groups \(g\) with input-independent probabilities \(w_{i,g}\). Conditional on a group, its remaining bit is a monotone binary channel
\[
P(\widetilde Z_i=1\mid X_i=x,G_i=g)=r_{i,g}+a_{i,g}x,
\quad0\le r_{i,g}\le r_{i,g}+a_{i,g}\le1,
\]
\[
\sum_gw_{i,g}a_{i,g}=\tau_i.
\tag{31}
\]
Singleton groups have \(r_{i,g}=a_{i,g}=0\). Refinement recovers the original output together with a label split independently of the input. Consequently its entropy increase \(\mathcal A_i(m_i)\) is affine in the input mean \(m_i\).

For clarity, this refinement can be constructed directly. Put
\(d_o=T_i(o\mid1)-T_i(o\mid0)\). Match the positive and negative \(d_o\)'s by any nonnegative transport \(u_{pq}\) with row sums \(d_p>0\) and column sums \(-d_q>0\). Split row \(p\) toward group \((p,q)\) with probability \(u_{pq}/d_p\), and row \(q\) toward the same group with probability \(u_{pq}/(-d_q)\). Within that group the positive-row probability has slope \(a=u_{pq}/w\), while the group probability \(w\) is input independent. Summing \(wa\) gives \(\sum_{d_p>0}d_p=\tau_i\). Rows with zero difference give singleton groups. For an original output row \(o\), the conditional splitting entropy is a fixed number; its expected value is affine in \(m_i\), proving the stated correction property.

The labels \(G_i\) are independent of the entire input vector and independent across sites. For a label vector \(g\), put
\[
W_g=\prod_iw_{i,g_i},\qquad
D_g=\operatorname{diag}_i\sqrt{a_{i,g_i}},\qquad
R_g=\operatorname{diag}_i r_{i,g_i}.
\]
The conditional refined bit law is the DPP with kernel
\[
K_g=R_g+D_g K(A,z)D_g.
\tag{32}
\]
This is a positive contraction: its complement is
\(\operatorname{diag}(1-r_i-a_i)+D_g(I-K)D_g\succeq0\).
The inclusion probabilities follow by expanding
\(\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}(r_i+a_i X_i)\), which is the corresponding principal determinant of (32).

The satellite form is preserved exactly. Its fixed data and free core are
\[
A_g=R_{g,U}+D_{g,U}AD_{g,U},\qquad
C_{j,g}=R_{g,O_j}+D_{g,O_j}C_jD_{g,O_j},
\]
\[
B_{j,g}=D_{g,U}B_jD_{g,O_j},
\qquad\hbox{cross scalar }z_j\hbox{ unchanged}.
\tag{33}
\]
Theorem 1 covers every such transformed family, including deterministic coordinates. The refinement chain rule gives the exact identity
\[
H(Z)=\sum_i H(w_i)+\sum_gW_g H(P_{K_g})
-\sum_i\mathcal A_i(K_{ii}).
\tag{34}
\]
All diagonal means \(K_{ii}\) are affine in the parameter \(A\) or fixed. The last term therefore has zero chord gap, while the first term is constant. This proves concavity and the exact averaged-gap formula
\(\Gamma_t^{\rm out}=\sum_gW_g\Gamma_{t,g}\).

To derive (30), apply (2) in each group. The transformed entry satisfies
\[
|(B_{j,g})_{ik}|^4=a_{i,g_i}^2a_{k,g_k}^2|(B_j)_{ik}|^4.
\]
Let \(\mu_i=\sum_gw_{i,g}a_{i,g}^2\). Group independence and
\(\mathbb E\max\ge\max\mathbb E\) give the stronger bound with
\(\mu_i\mu_k\) in place of \(\tau_i^2\tau_k^2\) in (30). Jensen's inequality and (31) give \(\mu_i\ge\tau_i^2\), proving the stated channel-only bound. All arguments are finite, and the boundary passage in Section 6 applies without change.

## 8. Exact example and reproducibility

Take a two-site core and two two-site reservoirs, with
\[
A_0=\begin{pmatrix}
2/5&1/25+i/50\\1/25-i/50&1/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}
11/20&(1-i)/30\\(1+i)/30&9/20
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
C_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&1/12\\1/12&3/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_2=\begin{pmatrix}9/20&(1+2i)/40\\(1-2i)/40&11/20\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/20&i/30\\(1+i)/40&1/25\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_2=\begin{pmatrix}1/30&(1+i)/50\\i/40&-1/20\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
z_0=\left(1,\frac{1+i}{2}\right),\qquad
z_1=\left(\frac i2,1-\frac i2\right),\qquad t=\frac25.
\tag{35}
\]
Both reservoirs are coherent. The cross determinants are
\[
\det B_1=\frac{17-5i}{6000},\qquad
\det B_2=\frac{-7-3i}{6000},
\tag{36}
\]
so both have rank two. The scalar ratios between the two endpoints are \(i/2\) and \((1-3i)/2\), which differ. Thus the two endpoint cross matrices are not proportional, and the prior one-common-scalar theorem does not contain this segment. The endpoint direction has inertia \((2,2)\) with two zero eigenvalues: its core-to-exterior block has row rank two and its entire exterior direction is zero. Indeed, after splitting off the two-dimensional exterior nullspace, this direction is congruent to \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}E&I_2\\I_2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), whose inertia is \((2,2)\) by eliminating \(E\) with a triangular congruence. In particular it is outside a fixed two-dimensional-support direction theorem.

Exact rational Gershgorin bounds, using \(|a+ib|\le|a|+|b|\), give the full endpoint margins
\[
\frac{61}{300}I\preceq K_0\preceq
\left(1-\frac{61}{300}\right)I,
\qquad
\frac{271}{1200}I\preceq K_1\preceq
\left(1-\frac{271}{1200}\right)I.
\tag{37}
\]
For the auxiliary kernel \(L\), both amplitude square roots are at most one; bounding them by one in all row radii gives the uniform margin \(13/60\). Thus every kernel used in the example is a strict contraction by explicit rational bounds.

The strengths, phase losses, and removal probabilities are exactly
\[
\bar s=(7/10,4/5),\quad |z_t|^2=(2/5,1/2),\quad
\delta=(3/10,3/10),\quad\lambda=(3/7,3/8).
\tag{38}
\]
The probabilities of \(R=\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\) are respectively
\(5/14,15/56,3/14,9/56\). The quartic right side of (2) is the exact rational
\[
\frac9{400000}=0.0000225.
\tag{39}
\]
