# C002 — complementary outcomes, an order-free covariance criterion, and a full-Hessian family

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a new creative derivation from `sources/old-097.md`, rather than a re-audit of that source. The general three-site conjecture remains open. No numerical evidence is used as proof.

## Results and scope

1. The exact seven-feature criterion in old-097 has an **order-free covariance realization** using the seven nonconstant Walsh spin moments. Its entries require polynomial principal-minor derivatives and a covariance matrix, rather than conditional Schur complements. The exact inequality is (6) below.
2. Averaging all six reveal orders gives a canonical 27-feature representation. It is particle–hole invariant term by term after pairing complementary histories. Since every order represents the same Fisher operator, averaging itself does not strengthen that operator.
3. On the entire particle–hole symmetric three-site locus, the exact criterion reduces to an explicit **four-by-four covariance matrix**, given in (13)–(15). This reduction retains all directions of the original nine-real-dimensional Hermitian Hessian.
4. The four-by-four obstruction is proved on the entire equal-edge-magnitude subfamily. In particular, for
   \[
   K_r=\frac12I_3+ir
   \begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},
   \qquad 0<|r|<\frac1{\sqrt{12}},
   \tag{1}
   \]
   **the entropy Hessian is negative definite in every Hermitian direction**. This is not merely concavity along the displayed one-parameter curve. The conclusion also holds after coordinate permutations, diagonal unitary conjugation, and complex conjugation. Every base in (1) consequently has an ambient convex neighborhood with strictly concave entropy. The family approaches the spectral boundary arbitrarily closely.

The novel proved family is item 4; items 1–3 are exact structural reformulations. Historical novelty outside the supplied project sources is not asserted.

## 1. Source identity and notation

For an interior connected three-site kernel, take the notation established in old-097:
\[
H''_K(A)=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A),
\quad M>0,
\quad \delta=\det M,
\quad S=M^{1/2}.
\]
Here
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12})+\tau K,
\]
where the coefficients are the degree-two and degree-three Möbius coefficients of the frozen function \(-\log p_X(K)\). All directions below are affine directions in \(K\), not in an odds kernel.

Writing \(A=SBS\), define \(\mathcal F\) by
\(\langle B,\mathcal F B\rangle=\operatorname{FI}_K(SBS)\). The supplied identity gives
\[
H''_K(SBS)=\delta(\operatorname{tr}B)^2-
\langle B,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})B\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus nonpositive curvature in every direction is equivalent to
\(\delta\langle I,(\mathcal F+\delta\operatorname{Id})^{-1}I\rangle\le1\).

## 2. An exact order-free covariance inequality

Let \(\sigma_i=2\mathbf1_{i\in X}-1\). Index the seven nonempty subsets \(T\subseteq[3]\), and put
\[
Z_T=\prod_{i\in T}\sigma_i,
\qquad
\mu_T=\mathbb E Z_T
=\sum_{R\subseteq T}2^{|R|}(-1)^{|T|-|R|}\det K_R.
\]
Use \(\mu_\varnothing=1\). Their covariance matrix is
\[
V_{TU}=\mu_{T\triangle U}-\mu_T\mu_U.
\tag{3}
\]
Full support makes \(V\) positive definite: the seven nonconstant Walsh functions, together with the constant function, are a basis of all functions on the eight atoms.

Let \(G_T=\nabla_K\mu_T\), meaning
\(D\mu_T[A]=\operatorname{tr}(G_TA)\). Explicitly,
\[
G_T=\sum_{\varnothing\ne R\subseteq T}
2^{|R|}(-1)^{|T|-|R|}
\operatorname{emb}_R(\operatorname{adj}K_R).
\tag{4}
\]
These are Hermitian polynomial matrices. No conditional inverse appears.

The score has mean zero, and
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Z_T,s_X[A])=D\mu_T[A]\). Because the centered Walsh functions span all zero-mean functions, the score is exactly its linear prediction from these seven functions. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(A)=j(A)^\mathsf T V^{-1}j(A),
\qquad j_T(A)=\operatorname{tr}(G_TA).
\tag{5}
\]
This is an equality, not a data-processing lower bound.

Define
\[
u_T=\operatorname{tr}(MG_T),
\qquad
D_{TU}=\operatorname{tr}(MG_TMG_U),
\qquad
C_W=D+\delta V>0.
\]
Woodbury, applied to the feature matrices \(SG_TS\), yields
\[
\boxed{\quad
H''_K\le0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u^\mathsf T(D+\delta V)^{-1}u\ge2.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed the left scalar in the source criterion becomes
\(3-u^\mathsf TC_W^{-1}u\). Strict inequality in (6) is equivalent to negative definiteness. The inequality is invariant under every invertible change of the seven centered statistics.

Equation (6) answers the order-free part of the assignment. It does not prove a universal covariance bound; it replaces the reveal-order-dependent seven-feature formula by an exact, permutation-covariant polynomial-moment formula.

### A direct covariance variational form

For completeness, let \(Q_X=K-I_{X^c}\) and
\(T_X=SQ_X^{-1}S\). Then \(\mathbb ET_X=0\) and
\(\mathcal F=\mathbb E(T_X\otimes T_X)\). Finite-dimensional least squares gives
\[
\delta\langle I,(\mathcal F+\delta I)^{-1}I\rangle
=
\min_{g:\,\mathbb Eg=0}
\left\{
\delta\operatorname{Var}(g)
+\|I-\operatorname{Cov}(g,T_X)\|_F^2
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the remaining universal conjecture can also be phrased as the existence of a scalar atom statistic with the objective in (7) at most one. This identity is exact, but no universal explicit choice of \(g\) is supplied.

## 3. All six reveal orders and particle–hole symmetry

For a site \(i\), a revealed set \(F\subseteq[3]\setminus\{i\}\), and an included history \(h\subseteq F\), define the source's Schur-complement feature \(w_{i,F,h}\), conditional probability \(q_{i,F,h}\), and history probability \(\pi_{F,h}\). Averaging the six exact chain-rule Fisher decompositions gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
=
\sum_i\sum_{F\subseteq[3]\setminus\{i\}}
\frac1{3\binom2{|F|}}
\sum_{h\subseteq F}
\frac{\pi_{F,h}}{q_{i,F,h}(1-q_{i,F,h})}
(w_{i,F,h}^*Aw_{i,F,h})^2.
\tag{8}
\]
There are 3 first-stage, 12 second-stage, and 12 third-stage features, after identical occurrences from different orders are combined. The coefficients are respectively \(1/3,1/6,1/3\).

Under \(K\mapsto I-K\), match \(h\mapsto F\setminus h\). Then
\[
Q_{F\setminus h}(I-K)=-Q_h(K),
\quad w'_{i,F,F\setminus h}=w_{i,F,h},
\quad q'=1-q,
\quad \pi'=\pi.
\]
So every matched summand in (8) is invariant. Also the frozen coefficients transform by
\(\tau'=-\tau\), \(c'_{ij}=c_{ij}+\tau\), giving \(M'=M\). This proves particle–hole invariance directly at the level of the averaged positive rank-one features.

For the covariance criterion, if \(P_{TT}=(-1)^{|T|}\), then
\[
V(I-K)=PVP,
\qquad G_T(I-K)=(-1)^{|T|+1}G_T(K),
\]
so \(u'=-Pu\), \(D'=PDP\), and (6) is unchanged.

Averaging is useful for symmetric trial certificates, but cannot by itself add Fisher information: the six summands being averaged are identical operators.

## 4. Complementary outcome pairs at a particle–hole symmetric base

A three-site law is unchanged under \(X\mapsto X^c\) if and only if
\[
K_{11}=K_{22}=K_{33}=\frac12,
\qquad \operatorname{Re}(K_{12}K_{23}K_{31})=0.
\tag{9}
\]
To verify sufficiency and necessity, the odd Walsh moments are the three means \(2K_{ii}-1\) and, once these vanish, the triple moment \(16\operatorname{Re}(K_{12}K_{23}K_{31})\). Vanishing of all odd moments is exactly complementary-pair symmetry.

After a diagonal unitary gauge, every kernel in (9) has the form
\[
K=\frac12I+iB,
\qquad B^\mathsf T=-B\text{ real}.
\]
Let \(v=(B_{23},B_{31},B_{12})\), \(r_i=v_i^2\), and \(T=r_1+r_2+r_3\). Then
\(B^2=vv^\mathsf T-TI\), so the interior condition is exactly \(T<1/4\). The four complementary atom pairs have probabilities
\[
q_0=p_\varnothing=p_{123}=\frac{1-4T}{8},
\qquad
q_i=p_{\{i\}}=p_{[3]\setminus\{i\}}
=q_0+T-r_i.
\tag{10}
\]
Each pair has total mass \(2q_\alpha\).

The frozen triple coefficient is zero and
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(m_1,m_2,m_3),
\qquad m_i=\log\frac{q_jq_k}{q_0q_i}.
\tag{11}
\]
The exact identity
\(q_jq_k-q_0q_i=r_i/4+r_jr_k\)
shows \(m_i\ge0\). If at least two edges are present, the kernel is connected and every \(m_i>0\).

### The entire obstruction is four-dimensional

Order the odd Walsh statistics as
\((\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3,\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3)\).
Their covariance is
\[
V_o=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-4r_3&-4r_2&-4r_1\\
-4r_3&1&-4r_1&-4r_2\\
-4r_2&-4r_1&1&-4r_3\\
-4r_1&-4r_2&-4r_3&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{12}
\]
It is diagonalized by the four complementary-pair sign vectors, with eigenvalues \(8q_0,8q_1,8q_2,8q_3\).

The gradient matrices are especially simple:
\[
G_i=2E_{ii}\quad(1\le i\le3),
\qquad G_4=-8vv^\mathsf T.
\tag{13}
\]
For the last formula, use
\(\operatorname{Re}(\operatorname{adj}K)=I/4-vv^\mathsf T\)
in the derivative of
\(\mu_{123}=8\det K-4\sum_{i<j}\det K_{ij}+2\operatorname{tr}K-1\).

Put \(R_m=\sum_i m_ir_i\), \(\delta=m_1m_2m_3\), and define
\[
u_o=(2m_1,2m_2,2m_3,-8R_m)^\mathsf T,
\]
\[
(D_o)_{ij}=4m_i^2\mathbf1_{i=j}\ (i,j\le3),
\quad(D_o)_{i4}=(D_o)_{4i}=-16m_i^2r_i,
\quad(D_o)_{44}=64R_m^2.
\tag{14}
\]
The exact all-direction condition on this entire locus is
\[
\boxed{\quad u_o^\mathsf T(D_o+\delta V_o)^{-1}u_o\ge2.\quad}
\tag{15}
\]
To justify dropping the other three Walsh features, odd and even statistics have zero covariance at a complementary-pair symmetric law. The odd gradients are real symmetric; the even gradients are purely imaginary Hermitian matrices (i times real skew-symmetric matrices). Since \(M\) is real diagonal, their Gram cross terms vanish, and \(u_T=0\) for the even statistics. Consequently (6) splits and only (15) remains. No Fisher lower bound or omission of an adverse direction is involved.

## 5. Proof of the equal-magnitude family

Assume \(r_1=r_2=r_3=r^2\). Signs and gauge can be reduced to the displayed kernel (1). Introduce
\[
t=12r^2\in(0,1),
\quad q_0=\frac{1-t}{8},
\quad q_1=q_2=q_3=q=\frac{3+t}{24},
\quad m=\log\frac q{q_0}=\log\frac{3+t}{3(1-t)}.
\tag{16}
\]
Then \(M=mI\), so for every Hermitian \(A\),
\[
H''_K(A)=-\operatorname{FI}_K(A)+m\{(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-\|A\|_F^2\}.
\tag{17}
\]

### 5.1 Reduction to two real variables

Write \(A=R+iC\), with \(R\) real symmetric and \(C\) real skew-symmetric. Complementary outcomes give odd atom derivatives for \(R\), and even atom derivatives for \(iC\). Their Fisher cross term therefore vanishes. The frozen quadratic in (17) also has zero cross term. Since \(\operatorname{tr}(iC)=0\), the imaginary sector is strictly negative for every nonzero \(C\).

For real symmetric \(R\), let \(s=\operatorname{tr}R\) and \(z=v^\mathsf TRv\). The derivatives of the full atom and of the three singleton atoms are
\[
d=\frac s4-z,
\qquad g_i=\frac{R_{ii}}2-\frac s4-z.
\tag{18}
\]
The complementary atoms have the opposite derivatives. Hence
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(R)=2\left(\frac{d^2}{q_0}+\sum_i\frac{g_i^2}{q}\right).
\tag{19}
\]

Decompose orthogonally
\[
R=aI+u(J-I)+R_0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}R_0=0,
\quad\sum_{i<j}(R_0)_{ij}=0.
\]
Since \(v=r(1,1,1)\), \(v^\mathsf TR_0v=0\). The Fisher cross term between the first two-dimensional summand and \(R_0\) vanishes in (19), because \(\sum_i(R_0)_{ii}=0\). The frozen cross term vanishes by Frobenius orthogonality. The residual has curvature
\[
-\frac1{2q}\sum_i(R_0)_{ii}^2-m\|R_0\|_F^2<0
\quad\text{if }R_0\ne0.
\]
It remains only to prove strict negativity for \(R=aI+u(J-I)\).

### 5.2 The two-by-two inequality

For this invariant component,
\[
d=\frac{3-t}{4}a-\frac t2u,
\qquad
 g_i=-\frac{1+t}{4}a-\frac t2u.
\]
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(R)
=\frac{((3-t)a-2tu)^2}{1-t}
+\frac{9((1+t)a+2tu)^2}{3+t}.
\tag{20}
\]
Let \(D=(1-t)(3+t)>0\). Write the right side as
\(\alpha a^2+2\beta au+\gamma u^2\), where
\[
\alpha=\frac{36-12t^2-8t^3}{D},
\qquad \beta=-\frac{16t^3}{D},
\qquad \gamma=\frac{16t^2(3-2t)}D.
\]
The desired matrix is
\[
\mathcal T=
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha-6m&\beta\\\beta&\gamma+6m\end{pmatrix},
\tag{21}
\]
since the frozen term is \(6m(a^2-u^2)\). Exact polynomial expansion gives
\[
\alpha\gamma-\beta^2=\frac{576t^2}{D},
\qquad
\alpha-\gamma=12L,
\qquad
L=\frac{3+3t-2t^2}{3+t}>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus
\[
\det\mathcal T=\frac{576t^2}{D}+72mL-36m^2.
\tag{23}
\]

For every \(x\ge1\),
\[
\log x\le\frac{x-1}{\sqrt x}.
\tag{24}
\]
A self-contained proof is to differentiate the difference: its derivative is
\((\sqrt x-1)^2/(2x^{3/2})\ge0\), and the difference vanishes at one. Substituting \(x=(3+t)/(3(1-t))\) gives
\[
m^2\le\frac{16t^2}{3D}.
\tag{25}
\]
Combining (23) and (25),
\[
\det\mathcal T\ge\frac{384t^2}{D}+72mL>0.
\]
Also \(\gamma+6m>0\). Therefore \(\mathcal T\) is positive definite. Together with the orthogonal negative sectors above, this proves negative definiteness of the complete nine-coordinate entropy Hessian.

### Concrete rational base and consequences

For example,
\[
K=\frac12I+\frac{2i}{7}
\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has complementary extreme atoms \(q_0=1/392\), the other six atoms \(q=65/392\), and \(M=(\log65)I\). It has a strictly negative entropy Hessian in every Hermitian direction. Its eigenvalues are \(1/2\) and \(1/2\pm2\sqrt3/7\).

As \(t\to1\), the family approaches eigenvalues \(0,1/2,1\); the proof holds at every interior point, without a distance-from-boundary hypothesis. Continuity of the interior Hessian gives a full-dimensional open neighborhood of each nonzero base on which the Hessian stays negative definite. No explicit common radius is claimed. At \(r=0\), the kernel is diagonal and the known nonpositive Hessian has off-diagonal null directions.

## 6. What remains open and exploratory calculations

The unresolved obligation on the particle–hole symmetric locus is to prove (15) for arbitrary \(r_i\ge0\), \(\sum r_i<1/4\), with at least two positive \(r_i\). Outside this locus, the unresolved obligation is (6) or the original criterion.

A tempting inequality is
\[
m_i\le\frac{r_i/4+r_jr_k}{\sqrt{q_0q_1q_2q_3}},
\]
which follows correctly from (24). However, replacing the three \(m_i\) independently by these upper bounds is not a valid monotonicity step for the frozen quadratic: the coefficients multiply indefinite forms \(A_{jj}A_{kk}-|A_{jk}|^2\). Exploratory floating-point computations also find that the matrix bound produced by simultaneously making this replacement can fail. This is a diagnostic obstruction to that particular proof strategy, not a theorem counterexample.

Similarly, retaining only the Fisher information of the particle count can lose needed anisotropic directions at unequal \(r_i\). The symmetric proof succeeds because permutation symmetry forces the relevant sector to two dimensions; this simplification does not persist automatically for unequal magnitudes.
