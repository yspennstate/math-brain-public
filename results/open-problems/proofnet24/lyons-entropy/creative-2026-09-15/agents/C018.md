# C018 — gated rank-one odds and a quantitative convex concavity corridor

Date: 2026-09-15. This is a restricted proof contribution. It does not prove the full Lyons conjecture or unrestricted full-Hessian concavity at every diagonal-plus-rank-one kernel. Historical priority has not been assessed.

Two results are proved here.

1. C005's canonical exterior positivity theorem extends to **every positive definite odds matrix that is diagonal plus a signed rank-one matrix**, even when the diagonal background is indefinite. The new case is a positive rank-one update repairing one nonpositive diagonal entry. A gated Laplace representation proves the extension. Its entropy consequence covers every direction with at most one positive and one negative eigenvalue, not arbitrary directions.
2. An explicit **full-Hessian concavity region near arbitrary interior product kernels** is proved, uniformly over diagonals in a compact box and dense weak off-diagonal shapes. A convex cone construction turns this into a genuine finite-segment interpolation theorem around either sign of a rank-one family. The cone contains non-rank-one kernels and segments between distinct rank-one families. This result is independent of particle-hole rewriting and is not confined to kernels near zero or near the identity.

The root subsequently derived the broader qualitative weak-block theorem in `ROOT_BLOCK_QUARTIC.md`. The product expansion below is its singleton specialization, with an additional vanishing fifth-order term, explicit estimates, and a convex corridor. The nonpositive-diagonal odds extension is separate from that result.

## 1. The gated Laplace positivity lemma

Use C005's notation. For a frozen real set table f, let

\[
F_f(K)=\mathbb E_K f(X),\qquad
T_f(K)=\sum_S p_S(K) f(S)\,\bigwedge^2 Q_S^{-1},
\quad Q_S=K-I_{E\setminus S}.
\]

At an interior kernel this canonical Hermitian operator satisfies

\[
D^2F_f(K)[uu^*,vv^*]
=\langle u\wedge v,T_f(K)(u\wedge v)\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

The cofactor version extends it to boundary contractions. Constants and modular tables have zero operator.

Distinguish a coordinate 0. Let w_i>=0 for i!=0, W(S)=sum_{i in S,i!=0}w_i, and x_0=1_{0 in S}. The following two tables have positive semidefinite canonical exterior operators at **every** positive contraction K:

\[
g_0(S)=(1-x_0)[-\log(1+W(S))],
\tag{2}
\]

and, when c>=0 and c sum_i w_i<1,

\[
g_1(S)=x_0[-\log(1-cW(S))].
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof for (2).** The scalar Laplace identity gives

\[
F_{g_0}(K)=\int_0^\infty {e^{-t}\over t}
\left\{\mathbb E_K[(1-X_0)e^{-tW(X)}]-(1-K_{00})\right\}dt.
\tag{4}
\]

Set z_0=0 and z_i=e^{-tw_i} for i!=0. The expectation in braces before the affine subtraction is the DPP generating determinant

\[
G_t(K)=\det(I-K+KZ)=\det(I-B_t^{1/2}KB_t^{1/2}),
\quad B_t=I-Z\ge0.
\]

For strict K, M_t=I-B_t^{1/2}KB_t^{1/2}>0, even though (B_t)_{00}=1. Its canonical exterior Hessian is

\[
\det M_t\,\bigwedge^2
\left(B_t^{1/2}M_t^{-1}B_t^{1/2}\right)\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the determinant cofactor identity, not an inference from testing only decomposable two-vectors. Integrating (5) with the positive weight in (4) proves the claim.

**Proof for (3).** Similarly,

\[
F_{g_1}(K)=\int_0^\infty {e^{-t}\over t}
\left\{\mathbb E_K[X_0e^{tcW(X)}]-K_{00}\right\}dt.
\tag{6}
\]

Fix t and let C_{s,t} be diagonal with entry s>=0 at coordinate 0 and entries e^{tcw_i}-1 elsewhere. All its entries are nonnegative. Therefore

\[
G_{s,t}(K)=\det(I+C_{s,t}^{1/2}KC_{s,t}^{1/2})
\]

has positive semidefinite canonical exterior Hessian. Since X_0 is binary, the generating identity gives exactly

\[
G_{s,t}(K)=\mathbb E_K e^{tcW(X)}
+s\,\mathbb E_K[X_0e^{tcW(X)}].
\tag{7}
\]

Divide the operator by s and let s tend to infinity. The finite-dimensional closed cone of positive semidefinite operators contains the resulting exterior Hessian of the second expectation. Integrate it in (6).

All differentiations and limits here concern finite tables. Near t=0 the twice-differentiated gated integrands are O(t), giving bounded integrands after division by t. At infinity the first case has the e^{-t} factor, and the second has exponential decay because c sum_i w_i<1. This justifies the integrals coefficient by coefficient. Boundary K follows by continuity. If c=0, the second table is zero. This completes both proofs.

## 2. Indefinite diagonal odds are covered by the gates

**Theorem 1.** Let

\[
L=D+\sigma aa^*>0,
\qquad D\text{ real diagonal},\quad\sigma\in\{1,-1\}.
\tag{8}
\]

No positivity assumption on D is needed. Then T_{-log det L_bullet}(K)>=0 at every positive contraction K.

**Proof.** For sigma=-1, D=L+aa*>0, so C005 applies. For sigma=+1, positivity of L allows at most one nonpositive diagonal entry of D: if two existed, there would be a nonzero vector supported on those two coordinates and orthogonal to a, and its L quadratic form would be nonpositive. The case D>0 is C005. Relabel the remaining case so d_0<=0 and d_i>0 for i!=0.

Put

\[
\ell=d_0+|a_0|^2>0,\qquad
c=-d_0/\ell\ge0,\qquad
w_i=|a_i|^2/d_i\quad(i\ne0).
\tag{9}
\]

The Schur complement at coordinate 0 is

\[
D_R-c a_Ra_R^*>0,
\]

which is equivalent to c sum_i w_i<1. The matrix determinant lemma, applied to this Schur complement or to the submatrix without coordinate 0, gives all principal minors explicitly:

\[
\det L_S=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\prod_{i\in S}d_i\,[1+W(S)],&0\notin S,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle\ell\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{0\}}d_i\,[1-cW(S)],&0\in S.
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
\]

Consequently

\[
-\log\det L_S
=-\sum_{i\in S\setminus\{0\}}\log d_i-x_0\log\ell
+g_0(S)+g_1(S).
\tag{11}
\]

The first two terms are modular. Apply the two gated positivity results to the others. This includes d_0=0 without a limiting argument. The proof is complete.

At an entropy-linked strict kernel L=K(I-K)^{-1}, and for

\[
A=\lambda uu^*-\mu vv^*,\quad\lambda,\mu\ge0,
\]

the exact entropy Hessian is

\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]=-
\sum_S{(Dp_S(K)[A])^2\over p_S(K)}
-2\lambda\mu\,
\langle u\wedge v,T_{-\log\det L_\bullet}(K)(u\wedge v)\rangle
\le0.
\tag{12}
\]

This is the precise entropy consequence of Theorem 1. Several positive or negative eigenvalues in A introduce same-sign pairs, so (12) does not become a full-Hessian theorem merely because the entire exterior operator is positive.

There is also a marginal-kernel formulation without constraints on the diagonal background. Suppose

\[
K=C_0+\tau bb^*,\qquad C_0\text{ real diagonal},\quad0<K<I.
\tag{12a}
\]

If I-C_0 is invertible, put d=(I-C_0)^{-1}b and r_0=b^*(I-C_0)^{-1}b. Sherman--Morrison, valid even when I-C_0 is indefinite, gives

\[
L=C_0(I-C_0)^{-1}+{\tau\over1-\tau r_0}dd^*.
\tag{12b}
\]

The denominator is nonzero because I-K is invertible. The diagonal and rank-one coefficient in (12b) may have either sign; Theorem 1 covers precisely this situation. If some diagonal entry of C_0 equals 1, approximate K by K+epsilon I with sufficiently small epsilon avoiding these finitely many singularities, and pass to the limit in the finite log-principal-minor tables. Thus (12) holds at every strict kernel of form (12a), including backgrounds with an entry outside [0,1]. This removes the strict diagonal-background assumption in C005's marginal-kernel corollary, while retaining the stated restriction on the inertia of A.

## 3. Exact expansion near a product law: no degree-five term

Write an arbitrary strict kernel near a diagonal kernel as

\[
K=\operatorname{diag}(c)+B,
\quad B=B^*,\quad B_{ii}=0,
\quad v_i=c_i(1-c_i).
\]

Let mu_c be the product Bernoulli law with means c_i, and define

\[
z_i(x)={x_i-c_i\over v_i},\qquad
q(c,B;x)=\sum_{|S|\ge2}\det B_S\prod_{i\in S}z_i(x).
\tag{13}
\]

There is an exact identity

\[
{p_K(x)\over\mu_c(x)}=1+q(c,B;x).
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed, determinant expansion gives
E_K product_{i in S}(X_i-c_i)=det B_S. The centered product functions form an orthogonal basis for functions on the finite Boolean cube, with squared norms product_{i in S}v_i. Their expansion coefficients give (14).

Since the two laws have identical marginals,

\[
H(K)=\sum_i h(c_i)-D(p_K\Vert\mu_c).
\tag{15}
\]

Let phi(q)=(1+q)log(1+q)-q and psi(q)=phi(q)-q^2/2. Orthogonality gives the exact expression

\[
D(p_K\Vert\mu_c)=\Phi(c,B)+R(c,B),
\tag{16}
\]

where

\[
\Phi(c,B)=\sum_{i<j}{|B_{ij}|^4\over2v_iv_j},
\tag{17}
\]

and

\[
R(c,B)=\frac12\sum_{|S|\ge3}
{(\det B_S)^2\over\prod_{i\in S}v_i}
+\mathbb E_{\mu_c}\psi(q(c,B;X)).
\tag{18}
\]

Every term in R has off-diagonal degree at least six. In particular, the potentially fifth-degree term E(q_2q_3) vanishes exactly by product orthogonality. Thus

\[
H(\operatorname{diag}(c)+B)
=\sum_i h(c_i)-\sum_{i<j}{|B_{ij}|^4\over2v_iv_j}
+O(\|B\|_F^6),
\tag{19}
\]

with the corresponding differentiated remainder bounds. The proof below supplies explicit uniform bounds rather than relying on an unspecified analytic remainder.

## 4. Explicit full-Hessian weak-correlation theorem

Fix n>=2, 0<delta<=1/2, and 0<alpha<=1. Define

\[
\boxed{C=64n^7\delta^{-8},\qquad
\varepsilon_0=\min\left\{{\delta\over4n},
{\alpha\over\sqrt C}\right\}.}
\tag{20}
\]

**Theorem 2.** Suppose c_i in [delta,1-delta], B is zero-diagonal Hermitian, and

\[
0<r:=\|B\|_F\le\varepsilon_0,
\qquad |B_{ij}|\ge\alpha r\quad(i<j).
\tag{21}
\]

Then K=diag(c)+B is a strict contraction, and for every Hermitian direction A=diag(a)+Z with Z_ii=0,

\[
\boxed{D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-2\|a\|_2^2-8\alpha^2r^2\|Z\|_F^2.}
\tag{22}
\]

Thus the Hessian is negative definite on the entire real vector space of Hermitian matrices. The bound holds for arbitrary complex phases, not only real symmetric kernels.

### 4.1 Uniform remainder estimates

Here are details sufficient to verify the deliberately conservative constant in (20). In this subsection assume r<=delta/(4n). Put z=||Z||_F, a=||a||_2 when only scalar norms occur, and Q=2n^2 delta^{-2}. Hadamard's inequality and columnwise determinant differentiation give

\[
|\det B_S|\le r^{|S|},\quad
|D\det B_S[Z]|\le |S|r^{|S|-1}z,\quad
|D^2\det B_S[Z,Z]|\le |S|(|S|-1)r^{|S|-2}z^2.
\tag{23}
\]

Since |z_i(x)|<=delta^{-1}, binomial summation gives

\[
|q|\le e^{nr/\delta}-1-nr/\delta<1/2,
\quad |q|\le Qr^2,\quad
|q_B[Z]|\le Qrz,\quad |q_{BB}[Z,Z]|\le Qz^2.
\tag{24}
\]

For diagonal derivatives use L=sqrt(n)/delta and U=(n+1)/delta^2. The absolute logarithmic derivative of a product of the z_i(x)'s is at most La, and its normalized second derivative at most Ua^2. The same bounds hold for mu_c. Consequently

\[
|q_c[a]|\le LQr^2a,\quad
|q_{cc}[a,a]|\le UQr^2a^2,\quad
|q_{cB}[a,Z]|\le LQra z.
\tag{25}
\]

For |q|<=1/2,

\[
|\psi(q)|\le |q|^3/3,\quad
|\psi'(q)|\le q^2,\quad
|\psi''(q)|\le2|q|.
\tag{26}
\]

Applying (24)--(26) and differentiating the measure as well gives, for G=E_mu psi(q),

\[
|G_{BB}|\le24n^6\delta^{-6}r^4z^2,\quad
|G_{cB}|\le32n^{13/2}\delta^{-7}r^5az,\quad
|G_{cc}|\le56n^7\delta^{-8}r^6a^2.
\tag{27}
\]

For the first term T of (18), use (23), v_i>=delta^2, and x=r^2/delta^2. The elementary tail estimates

\[
\sum_{k\ge3}{n\choose k}x^k\le n^3x^3/3,
\quad
\sum_{k\ge3}k{n\choose k}x^k\le n^3x^3,
\quad
\sum_{k\ge3}k^2{n\choose k}x^k\le3n^3x^3
\]

hold here because nx<=1/(16n), and yield

\[
|T_{BB}|\le6n^3\delta^{-6}r^4z^2,\quad
|T_{cB}|\le2n^{7/2}\delta^{-7}r^5az,\quad
|T_{cc}|\le n^4\delta^{-8}r^6a^2.
\tag{28}
\]

The logarithmic derivatives of product_{i in S}v_i^{-1} used in (28) have bounds 2sqrt(n)/delta and (4n+2)/delta^2. Finally, direct differentiation of (17) gives

\[
|\Phi_{cc}|\le n\delta^{-6}r^4a^2,
\qquad |\Phi_{cB}|\le\sqrt n\delta^{-5}r^3az.
\tag{29}
\]

The common constant C in (20) dominates the sum of the needed coefficients in (27)--(29), using r<=1. Thus all error terms in the full entropy Hessian are bounded by

\[
Cr^4a^2+2Cr^3az+Cr^4z^2.
\tag{30}
\]

These are derivative bounds in arbitrary diagonal and complex off-diagonal directions; no radial-only inference is used.

### 4.2 Dominate the error by the quartic curvature

For b,z in C,

\[
D^2(|b|^4/2)[z,z]
=2|b|^2|z|^2+4\{\operatorname{Re}(\bar b z)\}^2.
\tag{31}
\]

Since v_i<=1/4 and ||Z||_F^2=2 sum_{i<j}|Z_ij|^2, condition (21) implies

\[
\Phi_{BB}[Z,Z]\ge16\alpha^2r^2z^2.
\tag{32}
\]

Also h''(c_i)=-1/v_i<=-4. Equations (15)--(18) and (30)--(32) give

\[
H''\le(-4+Cr^4)a^2+2Cr^3az
-(16\alpha^2r^2-Cr^4)z^2.
\]

Apply 2Cr^3az<=a^2+C^2r^6z^2. The radius choice ensures Cr^2<=alpha^2, Cr^4<=1, and C^2r^4<=alpha^4<=alpha^2. Hence the diagonal coefficient is at most -2 and the off-diagonal coefficient is at most -14alpha^2r^2; the weaker bound (22) follows. Strict contraction follows from ||B||_op<=r<delta. This proves Theorem 2.

At B=0 the Hessian is exactly -sum_i a_i^2/v_i and has off-diagonal null directions. The theorem therefore does not follow from strictness at the product law by continuity.

## 5. A convex corridor and a valid gluing statement

Choose a zero-diagonal Hermitian B_0 with ||B_0||_F=1 and every off-diagonal entry nonzero. Put

\[
m=\min_{i<j}|(B_0)_{ij}|>0,
\quad\eta=m/2,
\quad\alpha={m\over2(1+\eta)}.
\tag{33}
\]

Use epsilon_0 from (20) with this alpha. Define the following set of kernels:

\[
\mathcal C=\left\{
\operatorname{diag}(c)+sB_0+E:
\begin{array}{l}
c_i\in[\delta,1-\delta],\quad
0\le s\le\varepsilon_0/(1+\eta),\\
E=E^*,\ E_{ii}=0,\ \langle E,B_0\rangle_F=0,\quad
\|E\|_F\le\eta s
\end{array}\right\}.
\tag{34}
\]

This set is convex: its parameter constraints are a box, an interval, a linear subspace, and a norm cone. When delta<1/2, it has nonempty interior in the full Hermitian matrix space. For s>0, B=sB_0+E satisfies

\[
\|B\|_F\le(1+\eta)s\le\varepsilon_0,
\quad |B_{ij}|\ge(m-\eta)s={m\over2}s
\ge\alpha\|B\|_F.
\]

Theorem 2 therefore applies at every non-diagonal point of C. At its diagonal points the entropy Hessian is nonpositive by the preceding exact formula. Restricting H to any affine segment in C and integrating its nonpositive second derivative proves:

\[
\boxed{H\text{ is concave on the entire convex set }\mathcal C.}
\tag{35}
\]

In fact it is strictly concave there: a nonconstant segment that is not wholly diagonal has negative curvature at its non-diagonal interior points, and a nonconstant wholly diagonal segment is strictly concave because sum_i h(c_i) is.

To make the center a rank-one-base family, take a vector u with no zero entries and

\[
B_0=\pm{uu^*-\operatorname{diag}(|u_i|^2)
\over\|uu^*-\operatorname{diag}(|u_i|^2)\|_F}.
\tag{36}
\]

For the minus sign, a center point can be written exactly as

\[
\operatorname{diag}(c)-t[uu^*-\operatorname{diag}(|u_i|^2)]
=I-D-tu u^*,
\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(1-c_i-t|u_i|^2).
\tag{37}
\]

Thus the construction contains a full-Hessian family of the requested complementary rank-one form and enlarges it to a full-dimensional **convex** region. It is the uniform quartic control and the convex cone constraints that justify the enlargement. Concavity at isolated rank-one bases alone would not justify it.

### 5.1 Exact higher-rank midpoint inside such a corridor

For n=4 let u=(1,1,1,1)^T and v=(1,1+s,1+2s,1+3s)^T with 0<s<=1/100. Set

\[
K_0=\tfrac12I-t[uu^*-\operatorname{diag}(u_i^2)],\qquad
K_1=\tfrac12I-t[vv^*-\operatorname{diag}(v_i^2)].
\tag{38}
\]

Take B_0=-(J-I)/sqrt(12), delta=1/4, and t small enough for the radial cap in (34). Both endpoints lie in this cone. Indeed, the relative orthogonal deviation of the second off-diagonal shape from the first is at most

\[
5s+6s^2\le0.0506<\eta={1\over2\sqrt{12}}.
\]

Here one uses ||off(vv*)-off(uu*)||_F<=sqrt(12)(5s+6s^2), while the projection of off(vv*) on (J-I)/sqrt(12) is at least sqrt(12). Therefore the whole segment is covered by (35).

Every diagonal-plus-signed-rank-one Hermitian matrix satisfies the off-diagonal tetrad equality K_12 K_34=K_14 K_32. But the midpoint M=(K_0+K_1)/2 obeys exactly

\[
M_{12}M_{34}-M_{14}M_{32}=t^2s^2>0.
\tag{39}
\]

This proves the interpolation really leaves the diagonal-plus-rank-one class. It also isolates the elementary structural obstruction to gluing arbitrary rank-one endpoint theorems without additional control of the intermediate kernels.

The remaining obligations are clear. The gated exterior proof does not control directions with several same-sign eigenvalues. The weak-correlation theorem does not extend along a whole rank-one ray to large correlations. No unproved assertion of closure under arbitrary affine mixing, and no global entropy counterexample, is made here. The completed gluing result is the convex corridor (34)--(35), and its full-Hessian proof is independent of any uncompleted rank-one-base argument.
