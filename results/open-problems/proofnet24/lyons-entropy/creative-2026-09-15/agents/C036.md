# C036 — Multiphase projection diamonds and the sharp overlap boundary

Date: 2026-09-15. **Status: COMPLETE bounded constructive result.**

This report gives explicit projection-diamond couplings in every dimension and every bottom rank for products of arbitrarily many rank-one phase unitaries on coordinate supports joined at at most one coordinate. Every factor's phase is allowed throughout its physical range. In the smaller common-permutation range, a single random permutation simultaneously realizes every projection law of the product frame, with the prescribed middle labels. The report also proves an exact extension to two-coordinate overlaps: two Householder factors whose relative overlap phases are in quadrature. It proves the sharp obstruction for all larger overlaps in this two-factor composition method.

Two exact examples are retained: a six-site, genuinely complex two-phase frame with a rank-two bottom, 70 positive labeled diamond atoms and a 24-atom unlabeled coupling; and an eight-site, genuinely complex quadrature pair with a rank-three bottom and 228 positive unlabeled atoms. Their full occupation channels are checked by 924 and 12,870 exact unitary-minor identities, respectively. The first bottom law cannot arise from **any** single rank-one phase applied to any coordinate rank-two subspace: it has 13 positive pairs, whereas such a single-phase law has at most nine.

## 1. Occupation channels and elementary diamonds

Let \(E=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). For a unitary \(U\), define the channel on the entire power set by
\[
 W_U(S,T)=
 \begin{cases}|\det U[S,T]|^2,&|S|=|T|,\\0,&|S|\ne|T|.\end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]
All row and column subsets use increasing order. By Cauchy–Binet, each column sums to one. For the coordinate subspace \(\mathbb C^T\), the projection DPP of \(U\mathbb C^T\) is exactly \(W_U(\cdot,T)\).

An elementary diamond of bottom rank \(r\) is a pair \(d=(R,H)\) with \(R\subset H\), \(|R|=r\), and \(|H|=r+2\). Write \(P=H\setminus R=\{p,q\}\). Its signed column is
\[
 D_d=\delta_R+\delta_H-\delta_{R+p}-\delta_{R+q}.
 \tag{2}
\]
For a frame \(U\), an **unlabeled diamond coupling** for the input diamond \(d_0\) is a probability distribution \(x_d\) on elementary diamonds such that
\[
 \sum_d x_dD_d=W_UD_{d_0}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Since the three ranks are distinct, this specifies the bottom law, the top law, and the sum of the two middle laws separately. A labeled version specifies each middle law in a prescribed order. No arbitrary relabeling or equalization of the complex middle subspace is assumed below.

The rank-one factors are
\[
 Q_j=I+(\omega_j-1)h_jh_j^*,\qquad
 \|h_j\|=1,\quad |\omega_j|=1,\quad
 s_{ja}=|(h_j)_a|^2,\quad \gamma_j=|\omega_j-1|^2\in[0,4].
 \tag{4}
\]
Their active supports are \(A_j=\{a:s_{ja}>0\}\). A factor acts as the identity on the complement of its active support.

The elementary minor formula proved by C034/C035 is
\[
 W_{Q_j}(S,T)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-\gamma_js_j(T)(1-s_j(T)),&S=T,\\
 \gamma_js_{ja}s_{jb},&S=T-a+b,\ a\in T,\ b\notin T,\\
 0,&|S\setminus T|\ge2,
 \end{cases}
 \quad s_j(T)=\sum_{a\in T}s_{ja}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed a principal minor is \(1+(\omega_j-1)s_j(T)\); a one-exchange minor is, up to its ordering sign, \((\omega_j-1)(h_j)_b\overline{(h_j)_a}\); two exchanged rows make the rank-one update insufficient to recover full rank. This holds at all ranks and at zero masses.

## 2. Explicit local probability kernels

This section restates the two completed local inputs so that the global algorithm is fully specified.

### 2.1 Full-angle unlabeled kernel

Fix a factor \(Q\), write \(s_a=|h_a|^2\), \(\gamma=|\omega-1|^2\), and take an arbitrary input diamond \((R,H)\). Set
\[
 P=H\setminus R,\quad C=E\setminus H,\qquad
 \alpha=s(R),\quad\beta=s(P),\quad\chi=s(C),
 \tag{6}
\]
so \(\alpha+\beta+\chi=1\). Define
\[
 c_0=1-\gamma(\alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi),\qquad
 \tau=(-c_0)_+.
 \tag{7}
\]
C035's conditional probabilities \(L_Q((S,T)\mid(R,H))\) are below. When \(\tau=0\), omit all terms involving division and replace the three parenthesized factors by \(\gamma\).

| Output bottom \(S\) | Output top \(T\) | Indices | Probability |
|---|---|---|---:|
| \(R\) | \(H\) | — | \(c_0+\tau\) |
| \(R-a+k\) | \(H\) | \(a\in R,\ k\in P\) | \(s_as_k(\gamma-\tau/(\alpha\beta))\) |
| \(R\) | \(H-k+c\) | \(k\in P,\ c\in C\) | \(s_ks_c(\gamma-\tau/(\beta\chi))\) |
| \(R-a+c\) | \(H-a+c\) | \(a\in R,\ c\in C\) | \(s_as_c(\gamma-\tau/(\alpha\chi))\) |
| \(R-a+c\) | \(H-k+c\) | \(a\in R,\ k\in P,\ c\in C\) | \(\tau s_as_ks_c/(\alpha\beta\chi)\) |
| \(R-a+k\) | \(H-a+c\) | \(a\in R,\ k\in P,\ c\in C\) | \(\tau s_as_ks_c/(\alpha\beta\chi)\) |

Every other output has probability zero. Here \(R-a+k=(R\setminus\{a\})\cup\{k\}\). If a block mass is zero, \(\tau=0\), so the no-division branch covers every degenerate case.

For completeness, nonnegativity follows from
\[
 \tau\le\gamma\alpha\beta,\qquad
 \tau\le\gamma\alpha\chi,\qquad
 \tau\le\gamma\beta\chi.
 \tag{8}
\]
For example, if \(\tau>0\),
\[
 \tau-\gamma\alpha\beta=\gamma\chi(1-\chi)-1\le\gamma/4-1\le0.
\]
The other inequalities follow by permuting the block masses. The probabilities sum to
\[
 c_0+\tau+\gamma(\alpha\beta+\alpha\chi+\beta\chi)-3\tau+2\tau=1.
 \tag{9}
\]

The signed common-transposition representation of (5) gives the first four rows with \(\tau=0\), with only \(c_0\) possibly negative. For each \(a\in R,k\in P,c\in C\), the six-diamond identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&D_{(R,H)}+D_{(R-a+c,H-k+c)}+D_{(R-a+k,H-a+c)}\\
 &-D_{(R-a+c,H-a+c)}-D_{(R-a+k,H)}-D_{(R,H-k+c)}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
holds because its positive and negative sides have identical multisets of bottom, top, and middle vertices. Multiplying by \(\tau s_as_ks_c/(\alpha\beta\chi)\) and summing gives exactly the displayed probability table. Therefore
\[
 \sum_e L_Q(e\mid d)D_e=W_QD_d.
 \tag{11}
\]

There is also the added-pair identity, present in C035's exact joint laws:
\[
 \sum_{e=(S,T)}L_Q(e\mid(R,H))\delta_{T\setminus S}
 =W_Q\delta_{H\setminus R}.
 \tag{12}
\]
To see directly that the repair retains it, write \(P=\{k,\ell\}\). The three positive diamonds in (10) have added pairs \(\{k,\ell\},\{a,\ell\},\{\ell,c\}\); the negative diamonds have the same three pairs. The original signed permutation lift has (12), so its repair does as well.

### 2.2 Common-permutation kernel with prescribed labels

For a factor \(Q\), C034's explicit random permutation has probabilities
\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_{\mathrm{id}}&=1-\frac\gamma3\left(1-\sum_as_a^3\right),\\
 a_{(ab)}&=\gamma s_as_b(s_a+s_b),\qquad a<b,\\
 a_{(abc)}=a_{(acb)}&=\gamma s_as_bs_c,\qquad a<b<c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The table is nonnegative whenever \(a_{\mathrm{id}}\ge0\), in particular for every \(\gamma\le3\). It realizes \(W_Q(\cdot,T)\) as the law of \(\Pi T\), simultaneously for all subsets. Its elementary identity on subsets is
\[
 P_{(ab)}+P_{(bc)}+P_{(ac)}=I+P_{(abc)}+P_{(acb)}.
 \tag{14}
\]
For \(\gamma\le3\), sample as follows: with probability \(1-\gamma/3\) do nothing; otherwise draw three independent indices with law \(s\), use the identity for one distinct value, transpose two distinct values, and choose either three-cycle orientation fairly for three distinct values.

C034 also supplies larger admissible local tables. For a flat vector on \(k\ge3\) active sites, the exact common-permutation range is
\[
 \gamma\le\frac{3k}{k-1},
 \tag{15}
\]
with identity probability \(1-\gamma(k-1)/(3k)\), probability \(\gamma/[k^2(k-2)]\) on each oriented three-cycle, and no transpositions. The global construction can use any of these completed local tables. The full-angle unlabeled theorem uses (6)–(12) and needs none of the extra conditions in (13)–(15).

## 3. Complete multiphase family on supports joined at one coordinate

**Theorem 1.** Let \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\) be (4), and suppose
\[
 \left|A_j\cap\bigcup_{\ell<j}A_\ell\right|\le1
 \qquad(2\le j\le m).
 \tag{16}
\]
Put \(U=Q_m\cdots Q_1\). Then:

1. For every \(0\le r\le n-2\) and every coordinate diamond \((R_0,H_0)\) of bottom rank \(r\), start at that diamond and, for \(j=1,\ldots,m\), apply the explicit conditional table \(L_{Q_j}\) from Section 2.1 to the current diamond. The final diamond is an unlabeled coupling of the projection diamond of \(U\).
2. The added pair \(H_m\setminus R_m\) has the projection law of \(U\mathbb C^{H_0\setminus R_0}\). Thus the sample partitions \(E\) into three blocks having the projection marginals of \(U\mathbb C^{R_0}\), \(U\mathbb C^{H_0\setminus R_0}\), and \(U\mathbb C^{E\setminus H_0}\), with the middle block of size two.
3. If every factor has a nonnegative common-permutation table, independently draw \(\Pi_j\) from it and set \(\Pi=\Pi_m\cdots\Pi_1\). Then \(\operatorname{law}(\Pi T)=W_U(\cdot,T)\) for **every** subset \(T\), all with the same draw. Every diamond has the individually prescribed two middle marginals, and all unions and intersections of input subsets are preserved pathwise.

The exact unlabeled probability of a terminal diamond, without sampling, is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 x(d_m)=\sum_{d_1,\ldots,d_{m-1}}\prod_{j=1}^m L_{Q_j}(d_j\mid d_{j-1}).
 \quad}
 \tag{17}
\]
All sums are finite over elementary diamonds of the fixed bottom rank. Equation (17) and the local table are complete probabilities, requiring no optimization or unspecified coupling selection. Sparse iteration evaluates them exactly. The prescribed-label probability is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 x(S;i,j)=\sum_{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_m}
 \left(\prod_{\ell=1}^m a^{(\ell)}_{\pi_\ell}\right)
 \mathbf1\{\pi R_0=S,\ \pi p=i,\ \pi q=j\},
 \quad \pi=\pi_m\cdots\pi_1.
 \quad}
 \tag{18}
\]
Here \(H_0\setminus R_0=\{p,q\}\) is ordered in the desired original basis.

**Proof.** The root's unique-intermediate lemma states that if unitaries \(V,Q\) are supported on \(A,B\) with \(|A\cap B|\le1\), then
\[
 W_{QV}=W_QW_V.
 \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, in Cauchy–Binet
\[
 \det(QV)[S,T]=\sum_F\det Q[S,F]\det V[F,T],
\]
a nonzero first factor forces \(F\setminus B=S\setminus B\), while a nonzero second factor forces \(F\setminus A=T\setminus A\). These conditions determine every coordinate of \(F\) except possibly the unique coordinate in \(A\cap B\); its cardinality determines that last coordinate. At most one term survives. Squaring produces (19), including the zero-term case. The preceding product is supported on the union of its supports, so (16) gives
\[
 W_U=W_{Q_m}\cdots W_{Q_1}.
 \tag{20}
\]

Let \(D\) have columns (2). Equation (11) reads \(DL_{Q_j}=W_{Q_j}D\). Thus
\[
 DL_{Q_m}\cdots L_{Q_1}\delta_{d_0}
 =W_{Q_m}\cdots W_{Q_1}D_{d_0}=W_UD_{d_0}.
 \tag{21}
\]
The coefficients are the nonnegative probability distribution (17), proving the first assertion. Apply the same calculation to (12) for the added-pair law. The top law gives the complement-block law by complementary unitary minors. Independent permutation composition has channel \(W_{Q_m}\cdots W_{Q_1}=W_U\), proving every simultaneously prescribed marginal in (18). ∎

The full range \(0\le\gamma_j\le4\) is allowed independently for every factor in the **unlabeled** family, with no bound on their number. The simpler sufficient range \(0\le\gamma_j\le3\) gives the stronger simultaneous labeled conclusion. Larger local ranges in (13)–(15) can be used factor by factor.

## 4. Rank growth and phase covariance

**Proposition 2.** If the nontrivial phase factors have linearly independent vectors \(h_1,\ldots,h_m\), then
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(U-I)=m.
 \tag{22}
\]
This holds under (16) whenever each new active support contains a coordinate absent from all earlier supports. The rank of \(U-I\) can therefore be arbitrarily large within Theorem 1.

**Proof.** Put \(\rho_j=\omega_j-1\ne0\) and \(H=[h_1\ \cdots\ h_m]\). Telescoping gives
\[
 U-I=\sum_{j=1}^m\rho_jh_jh_j^*Q_{j-1}\cdots Q_1.
\]
The row \(h_j^*Q_{j-1}\cdots Q_1\) equals \(h_j^*\) plus a linear combination of \(h_1^*,\ldots,h_{j-1}^*\). Hence \(U-I=HCH^*\), with \(C\) lower triangular and nonzero diagonal \(\rho_j\). If \(H\) has full column rank, \(H^*\) is surjective, \(C\) invertible, and \(H\) injective, proving (22). One newly appearing coordinate from each support gives a triangular nonzero minor of \(H\). ∎

For example, supports \(\{0,1,2\},\{2,3,4\},\{4,5,6\},\ldots\), arbitrary nonzero supported vectors, and nontrivial phases give connected families with arbitrarily large rank in (22). Theorem 1 imposes no restriction on the choice of bottom set or pivot pair. These sparse product frames are not claimed to exhaust the unitary group.

**Proposition 3 (phase covariance).** Under (16), the entire channel \(W_U\), and the coupling algorithms (17)–(18) where applicable, depend only on the masses \(s_{ja}\) and parameters \(\gamma_j\). They are invariant under independent changes of the coordinate phases of every \(h_j\), and under independently replacing any \(\omega_j\) by \(\overline{\omega_j}\).

This follows from (5), (20), and the local probability tables. It holds for all column subsets at once. The measured DPP law for
\[
 K=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_a)U^*,\qquad 0\le\lambda_a\le1
 \tag{23}
\]
has the same invariance, by its spectral mixture. Arbitrary diagonal row or column phases of \(U\) also preserve \(W_U\) directly. These invariances do not imply that the projection kernels can be made real by coordinate phases; the next example proves otherwise.

## 6. Exact composition across a two-coordinate phase cancellation

The unique-intermediate rule is not the only possible composition mechanism.

**Lemma 4 (quadrature composition for phase-conjugated real matrices).** Let
\[
 V_j=D_jO_jD_j^*,\qquad j=1,2,
 \tag{29}
\]
where \(O_j\) is real orthogonal, \(D_j\) diagonal unitary, and \(V_j\) is supported on \(A_j\). Suppose \(A_1\cap A_2=\{c,d\}\) and
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\left(
 (D_1)_{cc}\overline{(D_2)_{cc}}\,
 \overline{(D_1)_{dd}}(D_2)_{dd}\right)=0.
 \tag{30}
\]
Then \(W_{V_2V_1}=W_{V_2}W_{V_1}\) at every rank.

**Proof.** Nonzero Cauchy–Binet terms have their intermediate subset fixed outside \(\{c,d\}\), as in (19). There is one possible term when the required overlap occupancy is zero or two, and at most two when it is one. Let \(d_j(F)=\prod_{a\in F}(D_j)_{aa}\) and \(\eta_a=(D_1)_{aa}\overline{(D_2)_{aa}}\). The term indexed by \(F\) is
\[
 d_2(S)\overline{d_1(T)}
 \left(\prod_{a\in F}\eta_a\right)
 \det O_2[S,F]\det O_1[F,T].
\]
The final two determinants are real. If there are two terms, their intermediate sets exchange \(c,d\), so their cross product is a real multiple of \(\eta_c\overline{\eta_d}\). Condition (30) makes its real part zero. The squared modulus of their sum is the sum of squared moduli. This proves the channel identity for every \(S,T\). ∎

**Corollary 5 (two complex Householders).** Let
\[
 H_1=I-2hh^*,\qquad H_2=I-2gg^*,
 \tag{31}
\]
with active supports intersecting exactly in \(\{c,d\}\), and suppose
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\left[
 (\overline{g_c}h_c)\overline{(\overline{g_d}h_d)}\right]=0.
 \tag{32}
\]
Then \(W_{H_2H_1}=W_{H_2}W_{H_1}\). The two full-angle kernels in Section 2.1 therefore compose to a projection-diamond coupling in every bottom rank.

Indeed write \(h=D_1|h|\), \(g=D_2|g|\). The associated real matrices \(I-2|h||h|^T\), \(I-2|g||g|^T\) are orthogonal. The factors in (32) have positive moduli and the phase in (30), so Lemma 4 applies. No common-permutation representation of either Householder is required.

### 6.1 Arbitrarily many factors including quadrature pairs

Mix the two mechanisms as follows. Make an ordered list of **blocks**, each either one rank-one phase factor or a quadrature pair as in Corollary 5. Suppose each block's coordinate support meets the union of earlier block supports in at most one coordinate. Within a quadrature block use Lemma 4; between blocks use (19). The complete occupation channel is the product of its local rank-one channels in their original order. Hence the same formula (17) supplies all-rank diamonds for the complete product, with every \(\gamma\le4\). Whenever every factor also has a common-permutation table, (18) supplies the stronger simultaneous conclusion.

If every single-factor block has at least two active sites, every quadrature pair has an exclusive active site in each support, and all phases are nontrivial, its phase vectors are independent. For the only extra point, restrict a new quadrature pair to coordinates absent from all earlier block supports. If the possible attachment coordinate lies outside its two-site overlap, both overlap coordinates remain new, and their \(2\times2\) vector minor is nonzero by quadrature. If the attachment coordinate lies in the overlap, an exclusive coordinate from each support remains new, giving a diagonal nonzero vector minor. Thus each block adds one or two independent vectors on new coordinates. Proposition 2 gives rank equal to the total number of rank-one factors.

## 7. Sharp obstruction for two rank-one factors with larger overlaps

The next theorem exactly identifies the boundary of channel composition for two genuinely supported rank-one factors with exclusive coordinates. It is an obstruction to the channel identity, not to diamond couplings or entropy concavity.

**Theorem 6 (complete two-factor classification).** Let
\[
 Q_1=I+\rho hh^*,\quad Q_2=I+\sigma gg^*,\qquad
 \rho=\omega_1-1\ne0,\quad\sigma=\omega_2-1\ne0,
 \tag{35}
\]
with unit \(h,g\) and unit-modulus \(\omega_1,\omega_2\). Set \(A=\operatorname{supp}h\), \(B=\operatorname{supp}g\), \(I=A\cap B\), and assume \(A\setminus B\ne\varnothing\), \(B\setminus A\ne\varnothing\). Then
\[
 W_{Q_2Q_1}=W_{Q_2}W_{Q_1}
 \tag{36}
\]
holds if and only if:

- \(|I|\le1\); or
- \(|I|=2\), both \(\omega_1=\omega_2=-1\), and (32) holds on the two overlap coordinates.

In particular (36) is impossible when \(|I|\ge3\).

**Proof.** Sufficiency is (19) and Corollary 5. For necessity choose exclusive coordinates \(a\in A\setminus B\), \(b\in B\setminus A\), and write
\[
 z_k=\overline{g_k}h_k\ne0\qquad(k\in I).
 \tag{37}
\]
For distinct \(c,d\in I\), put \(J=I\setminus\{c,d\}\), take input \(T=J+a\), and output \(S=J+b\). Formula (5) shows that the **only** possible intermediates are \(J+c\) and \(J+d\): \(Q_2\) fixes \(a\), so \(Q_1\) must remove it; \(Q_1\) makes at most one exchange and cannot add \(b\). The coordinate it adds must lie in the overlap and be unoccupied in \(J\), leaving precisely \(c,d\).

Move the common ordered coordinates \(J\) to the beginning of each row and column list. The one-exchange determinant formula makes the product amplitudes, up to the same overall ordering sign,
\[
 \rho\sigma g_b\overline{h_a}\,z_c,\qquad
 \rho\sigma g_b\overline{h_a}\,z_d.
 \tag{38}
\]
Intermediate ordering signs cancel between the two minors in each product, making the external sign common. Equality (36) for this minor therefore forces
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(z_c\overline{z_d})=0
 \qquad\text{for every distinct }c,d\in I.
 \tag{39}
\]
Three nonzero complex numbers cannot be pairwise orthogonal under the real inner product \(\operatorname{Re}(z\overline w)\) on the two-dimensional real space \(\mathbb C\). Thus \(|I|\ge3\) is impossible.

Now suppose \(I=\{c,d\}\). The outside-to-outside singleton equation gives \(z_c\overline{z_d}=iy\) with real \(y\ne0\). For the singleton transition \(a\mapsto c\), the two path amplitudes through \(c,d\) have cross product
\[
 |\rho|^2|h_a|^2
 \bigl(\overline\sigma+|\sigma|^2|g_c|^2\bigr)
 z_c\overline{z_d}.
 \tag{40}
\]
Its real part must vanish; substituting \(iy\) makes that real part
\[
 |\rho|^2|h_a|^2\,y\operatorname{Im}\sigma.
\]
Every preceding factor is nonzero, so \(\operatorname{Im}\sigma=0\). Since \(|\omega_2|=1\) and \(\omega_2\ne1\), we obtain \(\omega_2=-1\). The singleton transition \(c\mapsto b\) has cross product
\[
 |\sigma|^2|g_b|^2
 \bigl(\overline\rho+|\rho|^2|h_c|^2\bigr)
 z_c\overline{z_d},
 \tag{41}
\]
forcing \(\operatorname{Im}\rho=0\), hence \(\omega_1=-1\). Along with (39), these are exactly the stated conditions. ∎

The exclusive-support and nontrivial-phase assumptions matter. The theorem does not classify nested or equal supports, identity factors, or algebraic degeneracies outside the stated setting. It does not say that every general unitary factorization has an irreducible obstruction.

## 8. Supermodular and spectral entropy consequences

For every frame in Theorem 1 or Section 6.1, every bottom column set \(R\), every \(p,q\notin R\), and every supermodular function \(f\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{U\mathbb C^R}f+\mathbb E_{U\mathbb C^{R+p+q}}f
 -\mathbb E_{U\mathbb C^{R+p}}f-\mathbb E_{U\mathbb C^{R+q}}f\ge0.
 \tag{42}
\]
This follows by averaging elementary supermodular inequalities against (17). It holds in every bottom rank using only the unlabeled construction.

Consequently fix such a frame \(U\), fix arbitrary eigenvalues in \([0,1]\) except for two, and let \(\lambda_p(t),\lambda_q(t)\) be affine with slopes \(a,b\) satisfying \(ab\le0\). Then
\[
 t\longmapsto H\!\left(P_{U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda(t))U^*}\right)
 \quad\text{is concave on its contraction interval.}
 \tag{43}
\]
The frozen eigenvalues may be arbitrary, not merely zero or one. Here is the exact argument. In the spectral mixture, condition on the Bernoulli background column set \(R\subseteq E\setminus\{p,q\}\). The mixed atom derivative is the convex combination of signed projection diamonds in (42), hence has a nonnegative elementary-diamond decomposition \(d\). Along the line, \(p''=2ab\,d\). In the strict contraction interior every atom is positive, and DPP log-submodularity gives
\[
 p_{S+i}p_{S+j}\ge p_Sp_{S+i+j}.
\]
This follows by writing \(p_S=\det L_S/\det(I+L)\), \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\), and using the \(2\times2\) Schur complement over \(S\). Pairing every positive diamond with \(\log p\) gives \(\sum_Sd_S\log p_S\le0\). Thus
\[
 H''=-\sum_S\frac{(p'_S)^2}{p_S}
       -2ab\sum_Sd_S\log p_S\le0.
 \tag{44}
\]
Regularize every eigenvalue by \(\lambda\mapsto\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)\lambda\); this preserves the fixed frame, affine line, and slope condition. Continuity of finite Shannon entropy at boundary atom laws proves (43) on the closed interval.

This is the prior diamond-to-entropy mechanism applied to the constructed frames. It proves neither unrestricted simultaneous spectral slopes nor varying eigenvectors along the segment. Full Lyons concavity remains open.
