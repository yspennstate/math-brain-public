# C055 — finite Bregman compensation and a uniform coherent-endpoint gap

Date: 2026-09-15. This is one completed quantitative theorem. It strengthens the C031 endpoint theorem; it does **not** prove concavity on arbitrary interior subsegments or full Lyons concavity. No subagents, external messages, pushes, or edits to other workers' files were used.

## 1. The theorem

Partition the measured coordinates into a pair and an exterior of size `m>=1`. Let

\[
D=B\oplus\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_m),\qquad
K=\begin{pmatrix}A&V\\V^*&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\preceq D,K\preceq I,
\]

and put

\[
r=|B_{12}|^2,\quad a_2=\|A-B\|_F^2,\quad
d_2=\sum_j(C_{jj}-d_j)^2,\quad
e_4=\sum_{j<k}|C_{jk}|^4,\quad v_4=\sum_{i=1}^2\sum_j|V_{ij}|^4.
\]

Write `Gamma_t=H(P_{(1-t)D+tK})-(1-t)H(P_D)-tH(P_K)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t\ge t(1-t)\left[
\frac{2r}{9}a_2+2d_2+
\frac{8}{3(m-1)}e_4+\frac{8r}{9m^3}v_4
\right].}
\tag{1}
\]

The exterior-pair term is omitted for `m=1`. For no exterior, the bound is simply `Gamma_t >= (2r/9)t(1-t)||A-B||_F²`.

In particular, if `B12 != 0`, the endpoint inequality is strict for **every** `K != D` and `0<t<1`, including coherent endpoint kernels `D` on the spectral boundary. There are no spectral-margin or minimum-atom constants in (1).

A stronger version, used in the proof, retains the parameter dependence:

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_t\ge t(1-t)\bigg[&\frac{2r}{9}a_2+2d_2
+\frac{8(1+t+t^2)}{3(m-1)}e_4\\
&+\frac1m\left(4(1-t)^{m-1}+\frac89rt^2\right)v_4\bigg].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2}
\]

Again omit the `e4` term when `m=1`. These bounds detect all changes within the coherent pair quadratically. New cross and exterior coherences are detected quartically. That distinction is necessary: with the principal marginals fixed at a strict block-diagonal kernel, the first atom displacement caused by a new cross entry is quadratic, and its entropy deficit starts at fourth order.

What is new here is the explicit modulus, its strictness conclusion for coherent boundary reservoirs, and an exact **sum of positive KL/Bregman terms minus a signed determinant correction**. The endpoint class of C031 is acknowledged rather than recounted as a new concavity class. Its previous positive-determinant improvement can vanish on every conditional fiber; (1) still controls every nonzero pair displacement when `r>0`. ROOT_RANK_TWO_SUPPORT is not used to rotate the measured pair or to assert a larger support theorem.

## 2. The finite Bregman identity

For any positive interpolation law `p_t`, with endpoint laws `p_0,p_1`, let `q_t=(1-t)p_0+tp_1`. Direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
H(p_t)-(1-t)H(p_0)-tH(p_1)
=(1-t)D(p_0\Vert p_t)+tD(p_1\Vert p_t)
+\sum_x(q_t(x)-p_t(x))\log p_t(x).}
\tag{3}
\]

The first two terms are Bregman remainders for `sum p log p`. For DPPs the last term is the determinant interpolation defect; no general sign is presumed.

For a two-site covariance `F` and direction `E`, set `delta=det E`. In atom order `00,10,01,11`, the quadratic coefficient of `P_{F+sE}` is `delta(1,-1,-1,1)`. Thus, for `G=(1-t)B+tF`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_t(B,F)&=(1-t)D(P_B\Vert P_G)+tD(P_F\Vert P_G),\\
L(G)&=\log\frac{P_G(10)P_G(01)}{P_G(00)P_G(11)},\\
\boxed{\mathcal J_t(B,F)&=\mathcal R_t(B,F)
-t(1-t)\det(F-B)L(G).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Here `J_t` is the actual two-site entropy Jensen gap. The correction helps when the displacement is indefinite and costs entropy when it is definite. The following estimate compensates that cost uniformly:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_t(B,F)\ge t(1-t)\det(F-B)L(G)
+\frac29t(1-t)|B_{12}|^2\|F-B\|_F^2.}
\tag{5}
\]

This holds for arbitrary two-site contractions. It is first proved for strict kernels, where all logarithms are finite; the entropy-gap interpretation extends to the boundary by continuity.

## 3. A coherent two-site Hessian bound

This section proves (5), rather than assuming that endpoint strictness supplies a uniform constant.

At a strict two-site kernel with diagonal `a,b` and off-diagonal `c`, write its four probabilities as `x,y,z,w`, in the order used above. Along a Hermitian direction

\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\\bar v&s\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\delta=us-|v|^2,
\]

the established elementary two-site calculation gives

\[
-H''=\mathcal I-2\delta L,\quad
\mathcal I=\sum_\alpha\frac{(p'_\alpha)^2}{p_\alpha},\quad
L=\log(yz/(xw))\ge0,
\quad yz-xw=|c|^2.
\tag{6}
\]

We claim the stronger coercivity estimate

\[
\boxed{-H''\ge\min(1,L)\|E\|_F^2
\ge4|c|^2\|E\|_F^2.}
\tag{7}
\]

**Indefinite and rank-one directions.** Marginal Fisher information gives `I>=4u²` and `I>=4s²`, hence `I>=2Q`, where `Q=u²+s²`. If `delta<=0`, set `zeta=|v|²-us>=0`. Then

\[
-H''\ge2Q+2L\zeta,
\qquad \|E\|_F^2=Q+2us+2\zeta\le2Q+2\zeta.
\]

This proves the first inequality of (7) in this case.

**Definite directions.** Negate `E` if necessary so it is positive. Set

\[
T=\frac{y+z}{2\sqrt{xw}}\ge1.
\]

The count variance is `tr F(I-F)<=1/2`, so the count score gives
`I>=2(tr E)²`. The extreme-atom Fisher argument reproduced in C031, Section 8, gives

\[
\mathcal I\ge4(1+T)\delta,\qquad L\le2\log T.
\tag{8}
\]

For completeness, set `P=adj(F)/sqrt(w)` and `Q0=adj(I-F)/sqrt(x)`. Both have determinant one. The extreme atom terms imply
`I >= (tr PE)²+(tr Q0 E)² >= (tr(P+Q0)E)²/2 >= 2 det(P+Q0)det E`, and `det(P+Q0)=2+2T`.

Take the convex combination of the two Fisher lower bounds with weight `L/[2(1+T)]` on (8). It gives

\[
-H''\ge\left(2-\frac{L}{1+T}\right)(\operatorname{tr}E)^2
\ge(\operatorname{tr}E)^2\ge\|E\|_F^2.
\tag{9}
\]

The middle inequality follows from `2 log T <= 1+T` for `T>=1`; the function `1+T-2 log T` has its minimum at `T=2`, with positive value `3-2 log 2`. The last inequality uses `det E>0`.

**The scalar coherence bound.** Put `alpha=sqrt(xw)` and `beta=sqrt(yz)`. Normalization and arithmetic-geometric mean give `alpha+beta<=1/2`. Since `beta²-alpha²=|c|²`,

\[
L=4\operatorname{artanh}\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\beta+\alpha}
\ge\frac{4|c|^2}{(\alpha+\beta)^2}\ge16|c|^2.
\]

Also `|c|²<=1/4` for a contraction. Therefore `min(1,L)>=4|c|²`, proving (7). The same argument also gives the useful separate bound `-H''>=u²+s²`.

### Exact integration and its positive square

For a twice differentiable scalar function, its Jensen gap is the integral of `-H''` against
`g_t(s)=min(s,t)-st`. Write `b=B12`, `v=F12-B12`, and `d(t)=1+t+t²`. The exact Green moments give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{t(1-t)}\int_0^1g_t(s)|b+sv|^2\,ds
&=\frac{|b|^2}{2}+\frac{1+t}{3}\Re(\bar b v)+\frac{d(t)}{12}|v|^2\\
&=\left[\frac1{18}+\frac{(1-t)^2}{9d(t)}\right]|b|^2
+\frac{d(t)}{12}\left|v+\frac{2(1+t)}{d(t)}b\right|^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

Both terms on the second line are nonnegative. Integrating (7) and discarding only its positive square proves

\[
\mathcal J_t(B,F)\ge\frac29t(1-t)|B_{12}|^2\|F-B\|_F^2,
\]

and (4) proves (5). Formula (10) retains a stronger explicit remainder if desired. Approximation by `(1-2epsilon)F+epsilon I` and the same operation on `B` proves the finite inequality for all closed contractions. No frozen logarithm is assigned to a zero atom.

## 4. An exact global sum of KL terms and one determinant correction

The conditional construction here is C031's construction, explicitly retained as the prior input. Let `(X,Y)~P_K`, with `X` the pair. Replace each exterior bit independently from its reservoir mean `d_j` with probability `1-t`; let `W` be the resulting exterior and `S` the independent retained subset. Define

\[
C_t=\operatorname{diag}(d)+t(C-\operatorname{diag}(d)),\qquad
M_t=\begin{pmatrix}A&\sqrt tV\\\sqrt tV^*&C_t\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then `(X,W)~P_{M_t}` by the determinant expansion for coordinate replacement. Let `q_t(y)=P_{C_t}(y)`, and, for strict data, define

\[
Q_y=C_t-I_{y^c},\qquad F_y=A-tVQ_y^{-1}V^*,\qquad
G_y=(1-t)B+tF_y.
\tag{11}
\]

The conditional pair kernels of `M_t` and `(1-t)D+tK`, given their common exterior atom `y`, are `F_y` and `G_y`. They are genuine contractions. Put `delta_y=det(F_y-B)` and `L_y=L(G_y)`.

Here is the promised exact positive Bregman decomposition:

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_t=\mathcal B_t-t(1-t)\sum_yq_t(y)\delta_y L_y,}
\tag{12}
\]

where every term in

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal B_t={}&I(W;S)\\
&+\mathbb E_S\sum_{j\in S}
I\!\left(Y_j;Y_{\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\setminus S}\mid
Y_{\{1,\ldots,j-1\}\cap S}\right)\\
&+t I(X;Y\mid W)\\
&+\sum_yq_t(y)\big[(1-t)D(P_B\Vert P_{G_y})
+tD(P_{F_y}\Vert P_{G_y})\big]
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

is a KL divergence or an average of KL divergences, and hence nonnegative. The retained-subset weights are `t^{|S|}(1-t)^{m-|S|}`. Conditional mutual information has its ordinary expected-KL meaning.

To verify (12), C031's entropy chain rule gives

\[
\Gamma_t=\mathcal E_t+t\mathcal L_t+\sum_yq_t(y)\mathcal J_t(B,F_y),
\tag{14}
\]

where `E_t=H(W)-(1-t)H(P_diag(d))-tH(Y)` and `L_t=I(X;Y)-I(X;W)`. The Markov chain `X -> Y -> W` gives `L_t=I(X;Y|W)`. Conditioning on `S` gives

\[
\mathcal E_t=I(W;S)+\mathbb E_SH(Y_S)-tH(Y).
\]

The chain-rule expansion of the second term is exactly the second line of (13). Finally use (4) in each conditional pair. Thus (12) is an identity, including the precise determinant correction. Substituting (5) controls that correction and gives

\[
\Gamma_t\ge\mathcal E_t+t\mathcal L_t+
\frac29rt(1-t)\mathbb E\|F_W-B\|_F^2.
\tag{15}
\]

## 5. Eliminating every conditional kernel from the lower bound

### Pair covariance fluctuation detects the cross block

Write `v_j` for column `j` of `V` and `c_j=(C_t)jj`. Differentiating the polynomial identities
`sum_y q_t(y)=1` and `sum_{y:j in y}q_t(y)=c_j` with respect to the entries of `C_t` gives

\[
\sum_yq_t(y)Q_y^{-1}=0,\qquad
\sum_{y:j\in y}q_t(y)Q_y^{-1}=e_je_j^*.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\mathbb EF_W=A,\qquad
\mathbb E[(F_W-A)(W_j-c_j)]=-t v_jv_j^*.
\tag{16}
\]

Hilbert-space Cauchy-Schwarz, applied to matrices with the Frobenius norm, yields

\[
\mathbb E\|F_W-A\|_F^2
\ge\frac{t^2\|v_jv_j^*\|_F^2}{c_j(1-c_j)}
\ge4t^2\|v_j\|^4.
\]

Average these `m` bounds and use `||v_j||⁴ >= sum_i|Vij|⁴`. Orthogonality of the mean and fluctuation gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|F_W-B\|_F^2
\ge a_2+\frac{4t^2}{m}v_4.}
\tag{17}
\]

### Classical information loss detects the cross block near the other endpoint

The all-replaced event has probability `(1-t)^m`. Revealing it and using data processing gives, as in C031,

\[
\mathcal L_t\ge(1-t)^m I(X;Y).
\]

For each cross pair the occupation covariance is `-|Vij|²`. Its joint binary table differs from the product of its marginals by four entries of absolute value `|Vij|²`, so its total variation distance is `2|Vij|²`. Pinsker and data processing give
`I(X;Y)>=I(X_i;Y_j)>=8|Vij|⁴`. Average over the `2m` pairs:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_t\ge\frac4m(1-t)^m v_4.}
\tag{18}
\]

Pinsker here can be proved by log-sum onto the event where `p>q`, followed by the binary relative-entropy bound `D(Bern(a)||Bern(b))>=2(a-b)²`; the latter follows by integrating the second derivative `1/[a(1-a)]>=4`.

### Exterior replacement supplies the remaining terms

Use the exact nonnegative C006 curvature formula for the exterior replacement path `q_s=P_{diag(d)+s(C-diag(d))}`:

\[
-H(q_s)''\ge s^{-2}\sum_j\chi_j(q_s),\qquad
\chi_j=\mathbb E\frac{(\rho_j-d_j)^2}{\rho_j(1-\rho_j)},
\]

where `rho_j=P(Y_j=1|Y_{-j})` under `q_s`. Since the denominator is at most `1/4`,

\[
\chi_j\ge4s^2(C_{jj}-d_j)^2+4\operatorname{Var}(\rho_j).
\]

For every `k != j`, conditional expectation and scalar Cauchy-Schwarz give

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\rho_j)\ge
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y_j,Y_k)^2}{\operatorname{Var}(Y_k)}
\ge4s^4|C_{jk}|^4.
\]

Averaging over `k != j` and summing over `j` proves

\[
-H(q_s)''\ge4d_2+\frac{32s^2}{m-1}e_4.
\]

Integrate against the same Green kernel as in (10):

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_t\ge t(1-t)
\left[2d_2+\frac{8(1+t+t^2)}{3(m-1)}e_4\right].}
\tag{19}
\]

For `m=1` omit the variance and exterior-pair terms; the diagonal bound remains valid. These estimates are derived from C006's full replacement curvature, not from an assumed first-Fisher curvature bound in an arbitrary coherent direction.

Equations (15), (17), (18), and (19) prove (2). To obtain (1), first drop `t+t²` in (19). If `t>=1/m`, the `rt²` part of (2) is at least `8r/(9m³)`. If `t<=1/m`, Bernoulli's inequality gives `(1-t)^{m-1}>=1/m`, and `4/m²>=8r/(9m³)` since `r<=1/4`. This proves (1), including `m=1`.

### Boundary passage and strictness

Apply the strict proof to `D^epsilon=(1-2epsilon)D+epsilon I` and `K^epsilon=(1-2epsilon)K+epsilon I`. All structural assumptions are preserved. Finite entropy and every expression in (1)-(2) are continuous, so the bounds survive with no positive-atom hypothesis. Conditional formulas are only used before taking this limit.

If `r>0`, vanishing of the bracket in (1) forces `A=B`, `V=0`, every exterior off-diagonal entry to vanish, and `Cjj=d_j`. Thus it forces `K=D`. This proves the stated strictness even when the coherent reservoir has an eigenvalue zero or one or some exterior reservoir bits are deterministic.

## 7. Terminal scope

The completed result is a quantitative finite-chord compensation theorem on C031's coherent-pair endpoint family. The exact positive remainder (13) consists entirely of measured relative entropies; the only signed part is the explicit conditional determinant correction. Equations (5), (17), and (19) turn this into a nonconditional, dimension-explicit kernel gap, with no margin assumptions.

The report does not assert a full-line Hessian sign with coherent endpoints, arbitrary coherent reservoirs of larger measured size, or full Lyons concavity. It does not count the already known diagonal-reference line theorem, two-site concavity, or arbitrary fixed-two-dimensional-support theorem as a new scope. No further verification or unbounded search is required for this bounded task.
