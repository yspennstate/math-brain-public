# C020 — an all-direction theorem on a uniform equal-diagonal weak-edge cone

Date: 2026-09-15. **Completed analytic restricted theorem.** The proof allows arbitrary complex phases, arbitrarily unequal edge magnitudes, and missing edges. It does not solve the unrestricted equal-diagonal three-site problem or the general Lyons conjecture. Historical priority beyond the supplied campaign sources is not assessed.

## 1. The theorem

Let \(0<d<1\), put \(\mu=\min(d,1-d)\), and let \(K\) be Hermitian with
\[
K_{11}=K_{22}=K_{33}=d,\qquad
\boxed{\max_{i\ne j}|K_{ij}|\le {2\over7}\mu.}
\tag{1}
\]
Every such matrix is automatically a strict positive contraction. For its occupation-DPP entropy, in **every complex Hermitian direction** \(A\),
\[
\boxed{
D^2H(K)[A,A]
\le-{2\over19}\sum_i A_{ii}^2
   -{3\over7}\sum_{\{i,j,k\}=[3]}m_i|A_{jk}|^2\le0.}
\tag{2}
\]
The last sum has three terms, one for each complementary edge, and \(m_i=M_{ii}\), where \(M\) is the standard three-site frozen-log matrix defined below. If the coordinate graph of \(K\) is connected, every \(m_i>0\), so (2) is negative for every nonzero \(A\).

There is no lower bound on any edge, no comparability condition, and no restriction on the cycle phase. In particular, the theorem covers bases with nonzero real cycle product and nonzero frozen triple coefficient. The common diagonal can approach either endpoint arbitrarily closely; the coefficient \(2/19\) is uniform.

The closed set obtained by allowing \(0\le d\le1\) in (1) is convex, and entropy is concave on that entire set. The pointwise Hessian conclusion is stronger: it includes directions that split the three diagonal entries and leave the set. Each connected interior base consequently also has an ambient neighborhood of strict full-Hermitian concavity.

The new closing device is a quantitative completion of all off-diagonal squares. It uses the fact that the frozen matrix inherits small normalized correlations from \(K\), even when its diagonal entries have arbitrarily bad ratios. It does not use phase extremality, the \(\tau=0\) hypothesis, or permutation symmetry.

## 2. Frozen coefficients and the occupation Fisher bound

Write \(p_S=P_K(X=S)\), freeze \(f(S)=-\log p_S\), and define its nonmodular coefficients by
\[
c_{ij}=f(ij)-f(i)-f(j)+f(\varnothing),\qquad
\tau=\sum_{S\subseteq[3]}(-1)^{3-|S|}f(S).
\]
Set
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23},c_{13},c_{12})+\tau K,
\qquad m_i=M_{ii}.
\tag{3}
\]
The exact identity from old-097 is
\[
D^2H(K)[A,A]= -\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
                       +2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A).
\tag{4}
\]
It follows by differentiating entropy and expanding the expectation of the frozen function in principal minors. All indices in (3) are complementary indices.

For completeness, \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\) gives
\(p_S=\det(I-K)\det L_S\). The determinant inequality for the positive two-by-two Schur complements of \(L\) implies
\[
c_{ij}\ge0,\qquad c_{ij}+\tau\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]
These are exactly the two conditional pair curvatures for each pair of sites. Thus \(M\ge0\): for \(\tau\ge0\), use (3), while for \(\tau<0\), use
\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(c_{23}+\tau,c_{13}+\tau,c_{12}+\tau)
       +|\tau|(I-K).
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(a_i=A_{ii}\), and let \(\Sigma\) be the covariance matrix of the three occupation indicators. Its entries are
\[
\Sigma_{ii}=d(1-d),\qquad \Sigma_{ij}=-|K_{ij}|^2.
\]
Projection of the atom score onto the centered occupation indicators gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(A)\ge a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{-1}a.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the covariances of the score with those indicators are precisely their mean derivatives \(a_i\). Full atom support makes \(\Sigma\) positive definite.

More generally, suppose \(|K_{ij}|\le\eta\mu\) with \(0\le\eta<1/2\). The Hermitian row-sum bound gives
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(A)
\ge {\sum_i a_i^2\over d(1-d)+2\eta^2\mu^2}.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(\eta=2/7\), this is at least
\[
\operatorname{FI}_K(A)\ge {196\over57}\sum_i a_i^2,
\tag{9}
\]
using \(d(1-d)\le1/4\) and \(\mu\le1/2\).

## 3. The new off-diagonal completion lemma

Suppose the six nonnegative numbers in (5) are all at most \(C\). Equal diagonal entries give
\[
m_i=(1-d)c_{jk}+d(c_{jk}+\tau)\le C,
\qquad |\tau|\le C.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (3) and (6) also imply the crucial lower bound
\[
m_i\ge |\tau|\mu.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(M_{jk}=\tau K_{jk}\), this yields both
\[
|M_{jk}|\le\eta\sqrt{m_jm_k},
\qquad
{|M_{jk}|^2\over m_i}\le |\tau|\eta^2\mu.
\tag{12}
\]
When a denominator vanishes, \(\tau=0\) and the corresponding numerator is zero; that term is simply omitted. The first inequality handles quadratic edge interactions. The second handles their coupling to the diagonal direction. Neither requires a positive lower bound on the individual \(m_i\).

Put \(x_i=A_{jk}\), with an arbitrary consistent orientation of the three edges, and \(y_i=\sqrt{m_i}|x_i|\). Expanding the adjugate, then taking absolute values only on the mixed terms, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
\le{}&2\sum_i m_i a_ja_k-2\sum_i y_i^2\\
&+4\sum_i |M_{jk}|\{|x_jx_k|+|a_ix_i|\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The first mixed sum is at most \(4\eta\sum_i y_i^2\), because
\(\sum_i y_jy_k\le\sum_i y_i^2\). Also
\(2\sum_i m_i a_ja_k\le2C\sum_i a_i^2\).
With \(b=1-2\eta>0\), (13) becomes
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
\le2C\sum_i a_i^2-2b\sum_i y_i^2
       +4\sum_i|M_{jk}|\,|a_ix_i|.
\]
Keep half the negative edge quadratic, and complete the other half:
\[
-b m_i|x_i|^2+4|M_{jk}|\,|a_ix_i|
\le {4|M_{jk}|^2\over b m_i}a_i^2.
\]
Using (12) proves the closing lemma
\[
\boxed{
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
\le C\left(2+{4\eta^2\mu\over1-2\eta}\right)\sum_i a_i^2
 -(1-2\eta)\sum_i m_i|A_{jk}|^2.}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus (8) and (14) give a general explicit sufficient condition, valid beyond the particular constant in (1): full concavity follows whenever
\[
{1\over d(1-d)+2\eta^2\mu^2}
\ge C\left(2+{4\eta^2\mu\over1-2\eta}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Here \(C\) may be the actual maximum of the six conditional pair coefficients. The next step supplies a uniform bound using only the edge radius.

## 4. Uniform logarithm control from the product law

Let \(p_S^0=d^{|S|}(1-d)^{3-|S|}\). In the atom determinant, set
\(q_i=d-\mathbf1_{i\notin S}\), and orient the edges as
\((e_1,e_2,e_3)=(K_{23},K_{31},K_{12})\). Direct determinant expansion gives
\[
{p_S\over p_S^0}
=1-\sum_i{|e_i|^2\over q_jq_k}
      +{2\operatorname{Re}(e_1e_2e_3)\over q_1q_2q_3}.
\tag{16}
\]
Because \(|q_i|\ge\mu\),
\[
\left|{p_S\over p_S^0}-1\right|
\le3\eta^2+2\eta^3=:z<1.
\tag{17}
\]
Therefore every atom is positive and
\[
|f(S)+\log p_S^0|\le-\log(1-z)=:\ell.
\tag{18}
\]
The function \(-\log p_S^0\) is modular. Each of the six conditional pair coefficients is a four-term alternating sum of the corrections in (18). Together with (5), this gives the uniform choice
\[
\boxed{C=4\ell=-4\log(1-3\eta^2-2\eta^3).}
\tag{19}
\]

At \(\eta=2/7\), we have \(z=100/343\) and
\[
\ell=\log{343\over243}<{7\over20},\qquad C<{7\over5}.
\tag{20}
\]
An elementary rational proof of the logarithm bound uses \(t=50/293\) and the positive odd series:
\[
\log{343\over243}
=2\sum_{r\ge0}{t^{2r+1}\over2r+1}
\le2\left(t+{t^3\over3(1-t^2)}\right)
={25254700\over73263771}<{7\over20}.
\tag{21}
\]
No numerical logarithm is needed.

Substitution of \(\mu\le1/2\), \(\eta=2/7\), and \(C\le7/5\) into (14) gives
\[
2\operatorname{tr}(M\operatorname{adj}A)
\le {10\over3}\sum_i a_i^2-{3\over7}\sum_i m_i|A_{jk}|^2,
\tag{22}
\]
because \((7/5)(2+8/21)=10/3\). Subtracting (9) proves (2), since
\(196/57-10/3=2/19\).

## 5. Strictness, convexity, and an explicit new-family example

For \(\eta<1/2\), the off-diagonal operator norm is at most \(2\eta\mu<\mu\). Hence \(0<K<I\), as asserted.

At a connected base, old-097's characterization gives \(M>0\). One can also see the relevant diagonal positivity directly. If \(\tau\ne0\), (11) suffices. If \(\tau=0\) and some \(c_{ij}=0\), then both unconditional and conditional pair determinant inequalities are equalities. The first forces \(L_{ij}=0\); the second forces \(L_{ik}L_{kj}=0\). Thus \(L\), and consequently its matrix function \(K\), is disconnected. Therefore connected bases have every \(m_i>0\), proving strictness in (2).

Convexity of the stated kernel set follows by expressing its edge constraints as both \(|K_{ij}|\le(2/7)d\) and \(|K_{ij}|\le(2/7)(1-d)\). Integrating the interior Hessian along segments proves concavity. Entropy continuity handles \(d=0,1\); alternatively regularize by \(K\mapsto(1-2\epsilon)K+\epsilon I\), which preserves the constraints. No boundary Hessian is asserted.

A rational example outside the completed families listed in the assignment is
\[
K_*=
\begin{pmatrix}
2/5&1/10&(3-4i)/100\\
1/10&2/5&2/25\\
(3+4i)/100&2/25&2/5
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{23}
\]
Its edge magnitudes are \(1/10,2/25,1/20\), its real cycle product is \(3/12500\ne0\), and its common diagonal is not \(1/2\). Its parity-product ratio is exactly
\[
\exp(\tau)={288760558504899\over297104721804899}\ne1.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus it is outside C008's particle–hole locus, C013's \(\tau=0\) family, and C017's equal-magnitude exchangeable family. The theorem includes an entire unequal-edge, arbitrary-phase region around it within the common-diagonal subspace, together with an ambient strict-concavity neighborhood.
