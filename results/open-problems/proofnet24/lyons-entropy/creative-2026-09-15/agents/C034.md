## 1. A dense phase family and its projection laws

Let \(E=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), let \(h\in\mathbb C^E\) have norm one, and let \(|\omega|=1\). Put
\[
Q=I+(\omega-1)hh^*,\qquad s_i=|h_i|^2,\qquad
\gamma=|\omega-1|^2\in[0,4].
\tag{1}
\]
The matrix \(Q\) acts by \(\omega\) on \([h]\) and by the identity on its orthogonal complement, so it is unitary. For a coordinate subset \(A\), write
\[
H_A=Q\operatorname{span}\{e_i:i\in A\},\qquad
\mu_A(B)=|\det Q_{B,A}|^2\quad(|B|=|A|).
\]
These are projection DPP laws. Let \(\tau_{ij}\) be the coordinate transposition of \(i,j\). The exact probability table is
\[
\mu_A(B)=
\begin{cases}
1-\gamma\alpha_A(1-\alpha_A),& B=A,\\
\gamma s_i s_j,& B=A\setminus\{i\}\cup\{j\},\ i\in A,\ j\notin A,\\
0,&|A\setminus B|\geq2,
\end{cases}
\qquad \alpha_A=\sum_{i\in A}s_i.
\tag{2}
\]

For completeness, the unchanged minor is
\(\det(I_A+(\omega-1)h_Ah_A^*)=1+(\omega-1)\alpha_A\).
Its squared modulus is the first line because
\(\omega+\overline\omega-2=-\gamma\).
A one-exchange minor has determinant
\(\pm(\omega-1)h_j\overline h_i\): multilinearity leaves precisely the term using the outer-product column in the missing coordinate. If two coordinates must be exchanged, at least two selected rows outside \(A\) come from the same rank-one matrix and are proportional, so the determinant is zero. This proves every case of (2), including zero coordinates and empty or full \(A\).

For a permutation \(\pi\), let \(P_\pi\delta_A=\delta_{\pi A}\). Equation (2) is the following identity of transition operators on the **whole power set**:
\[
\boxed{\quad M_Q=I+\gamma\sum_{i<j}s_i s_j(P_{\tau_{ij}}-I),\quad
M_Q\delta_A=\mu_A.\quad}
\tag{3}
\]
The right side is initially a signed permutation decomposition. Its identity coefficient can be negative. Sections 2 and 4 give nonnegative replacements.

When all \(h_i\ne0\), \(\omega\notin\mathbb R\), and \(A\) is a proper nonempty subset, \(P_{H_A}\) has **no zero off-diagonal entries**. Indeed, for \(i\ne j\), its entry is
\[
h_i\overline h_j
\left[(\omega-1)\mathbf1_{j\in A}
 +(\overline\omega-1)\mathbf1_{i\in A}+\gamma\alpha_A\right].
\tag{4}
\]
The bracket is \(-\gamma(1-\alpha_A)\) inside \(A\), \(\gamma\alpha_A\) outside \(A\), and has nonzero imaginary part across the cut. Thus these projection kernels cannot split into nontrivial disjoint coordinate blocks. Their maximal-minor tables can nevertheless have zero entries, as (2) explicitly records.

## 2. Explicit common-permutation probabilities

Write \(c_{ijk}=(i\ j\ k)\) for an oriented three-cycle on distinct coordinates. The following operator identity holds on all subsets:
\[
P_{\tau_{ij}}+P_{\tau_{jk}}+P_{\tau_{ik}}
=I+P_{c_{ijk}}+P_{c_{ijk}^{-1}}.
\tag{5}
\]
To prove it, freeze the coordinates outside the triple. On subsets containing zero or all three of its coordinates, both sides give three copies of the unchanged set. On a singleton of the triple, both sides give each of the three singleton positions once. Complementing within the triple proves the two-coordinate case. These exhaust the possibilities.

**Theorem 1 (explicit simultaneous coupling).** Suppose
\[
a_0:=1-\frac\gamma3\left(1-\sum_i s_i^3\right)\geq0.
\tag{6}
\]
The following probabilities define a random coordinate permutation \(\Pi\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(\Pi=\mathrm{id})&=a_0,\\
\Pr(\Pi=\tau_{ij})&=\gamma s_i s_j(s_i+s_j), &&i<j,\\
\Pr(\Pi=c_{ijk})=\Pr(\Pi=c_{ijk}^{-1})
 &=\gamma s_i s_j s_k, &&i<j<k.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
All other permutations have probability zero. A **single** draw satisfies
\[
\boxed{\quad\operatorname{law}(\Pi A)=\mu_A
\quad\text{for every }A\subseteq E.\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
Condition (6) holds for every \(h\) whenever \(0\leq\gamma\leq3\). It also includes some phases with \(\gamma>3\). If \(\sum_i s_i^3=1\), the permutation is simply the identity and every physical phase is allowed.

**Proof.** Let \(e_k\) denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(k\) in \(s\). Apply (5) to each equally weighted pair of oriented cycles in (7). The total coefficient of \(P_{\tau_{ij}}\) becomes
\[
\gamma s_i s_j(s_i+s_j)+\gamma s_i s_j\sum_{k\ne i,j}s_k
=\gamma s_i s_j.
\]
The coefficient of the identity becomes \(a_0-\gamma e_3\). Since
\[
\sum_i s_i^3=1-3e_2+3e_3,
\]
this is \(1-\gamma e_2\). The resulting operator is exactly (3). All displayed probabilities are nonnegative, and their sum is one because the columns of (3) sum to one. This proves (8). The formulas also give the direct mass identity
\(a_0+\gamma\sum_{i<j}s_i s_j(s_i+s_j)+2\gamma e_3=1\). ∎

For \(\gamma\leq3\), a particularly short sampling algorithm implements (7):

1. With probability \(1-\gamma/3\), use the identity.
2. Otherwise draw three independent indices, each with law \(s\).
3. If there is one distinct index, use the identity. If there are two, transpose them. If there are three, use either orientation of their three-cycle with probability \(1/2\).

There are three ordered draws with multiset \(\{i,i,j\}\), and six with three distinct indices, so these instructions give precisely (7). For the extra range allowed by (6), use the nonnegative table (7) directly.

This is a simultaneous theorem: after drawing \(\Pi\), define \(X_A=\Pi A\) for all \(A\). Then
\[
X_{A\cap B}=X_A\cap X_B,\qquad X_{A\cup B}=X_A\cup X_B
\tag{9}
\]
for every pair of subsets, with all projection marginals correct. No separately chosen covering couplings occur in this construction.

## 3. Prescribed labeled diamonds, including top codimension one

Fix \(A\subseteq E\) and distinct \(p,q\notin A\). Let
\[
W=H_A,\qquad u=Qe_p,\qquad v=Qe_q.
\]
The four laws in the question are \(\mu_A,\mu_{A+p},\mu_{A+q},\mu_{A+p+q}\). Draw \(\Pi\) by Theorem 1, and set
\[
S=\Pi A,\qquad i=\Pi p,\qquad j=\Pi q.
\tag{10}
\]
Then
\[
(S,S+i,S+j,S+i+j)
=(\Pi A,\Pi(A+p),\Pi(A+q),\Pi(A+p+q)).
\]
By (8), these four vertices have the four individually prescribed laws, in the displayed order. They form an elementary diamond deterministically. In particular the construction remains correct when the two middle laws differ.

To state every transport probability explicitly, let \(a_\pi\) be the finite table (7). The labeled weight is
\[
\boxed{\quad
x_{S;i,j}=\sum_{\pi}a_\pi\,
\mathbf1_{\{\pi A=S,\ \pi p=i,\ \pi q=j\}}.
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
The sum contains only the identity, transpositions, and oriented three-cycles, so it has at most \(1+\binom n2+2\binom n3\) explicitly known terms. Formula (11) is an evaluation formula, with no transport optimization or feasibility oracle. Forgetting the order of \(i,j\) gives the requested unlabeled weights.

For the assigned **top-codimension-one case**, choose distinct \(p,q,z\) and put
\[
A=E\setminus\{p,q,z\}.
\]
Thus \(\dim W=n-3\) and \(W+[u,v]=(Qe_z)^\perp\). An atom of (11) has
\[
S=E\setminus\{\Pi p,\Pi q,\Pi z\},\qquad
T=E\setminus\{\Pi z\},
\tag{12}
\]
with first addition \(\Pi p\) and second addition \(\Pi q\). Equations (7), (11), and (12) are explicit complementary low-rank transport probabilities in every ambient dimension. The same draw also couples all the other ranks, which is strictly more simultaneous structure than the already known corank-one unlabeled existence statement.

By (9), for any supermodular \(f\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_A}f+\mathbb E_{\mu_{A+p+q}}f
-\mathbb E_{\mu_{A+p}}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_{A+q}}f
=\mathbb E\,\Delta_{\Pi p}\Delta_{\Pi q}f(\Pi A)\geq0.
\tag{13}
\]

## 4. A sharp larger range for flat vectors

**Theorem 2 (exact flat-vector threshold).** Suppose \(n\geq3\) and \(s_i=1/n\) for all \(i\). A distribution on coordinate permutations realizes (8) for every subset if and only if
\[
\boxed{\quad \gamma\leq\frac{3n}{n-1}.\quad}
\tag{14}
\]
The physical parameter still satisfies \(\gamma\leq4\). Whenever (14) holds, the explicit distribution has no transpositions and is
\[
\Pr(\Pi=\mathrm{id})=1-\frac{\gamma(n-1)}{3n},\qquad
\Pr(\Pi=c_{ijk})=\Pr(\Pi=c_{ijk}^{-1})
=\frac{\gamma}{n^2(n-2)}.
\tag{15}
\]

**Sufficiency.** These weights are nonnegative and sum to one. Each pair belongs to \(n-2\) triples. Applying (5), its transposition coefficient is \(\gamma/n^2\), while the identity coefficient is
\(1-\gamma(n-1)/(2n)\). These are exactly (3) for the flat vector.

**Necessity.** More generally, suppose any permutation distribution realizes (8). By (2), every permutation \(\pi\) assigned positive probability must satisfy
\[
|\pi A\setminus A|\leq1\qquad\text{for every }A\subseteq E.
\tag{16}
\]
Such a permutation is the identity, a single transposition, or a single three-cycle. Indeed, if it has two disjoint nontrivial cycles, take one point from each cycle. Both selected points move outside the chosen pair. If it has a cycle of length at least four, select its first and third points in cyclic order; again both images lie outside the pair. These contradict (16). The remaining listed permutations all satisfy (16), by direct inspection.

Every permitted permutation therefore moves at most three coordinates. But the singleton marginals in (2) force
\[
\mathbb E\#\{i:\Pi i\ne i\}
=\sum_i\gamma s_i(1-s_i)
=\gamma\left(1-\sum_i s_i^2\right)\leq3.
\tag{17}
\]
For flat \(s\), this is exactly (14). ∎

For \(n=3,4\), every physical phase is covered by (15). At \(n=4,\gamma=4\), the distribution is uniform on the eight oriented three-cycles. At \(n=5,\gamma=4\), no common permutation exists: the required expected number of moved coordinates is \(16/5>3\).

For each \(\gamma>3\), taking \(n>\gamma/(\gamma-3)\) violates (14). Thus **three is the sharp phase bound valid uniformly over all dimensions and all vectors for this common-permutation method**. This is not an obstruction to unlabeled diamonds, to a coupling for one particular diamond, or to entropy concavity. C035's larger unlabeled family illustrates why the distinction matters.

For \(n=2\), use the identity with probability \(1-\gamma/4\) and its one transposition with probability \(\gamma/4\) in the flat case. All physical phases are allowed. Dimension one is trivial.

## 5. Exact general obstruction and reduction by triangle packing

The previous classification also resolves exactly what must be added to improve the common-permutation construction for a nonflat vector.

**Theorem 3 (complete finite criterion for a common permutation).** Put
\(c_{ij}=\gamma s_i s_j\). There is a random coordinate permutation realizing every \(\mu_A\) if and only if there are numbers \(t_{ijk}\geq0\), one for each unordered triple, such that
\[
\sum_{k\ne i,j}t_{ijk}\leq c_{ij}\quad(i<j),\qquad
\sum_{i<j<k}t_{ijk}\geq\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}-1.
\tag{18}
\]
Here the triple subscript is unordered. Given these numbers, all probabilities are explicit:
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathrm{id}}&=1-\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}+\sum_{i<j<k}t_{ijk},\\
a_{\tau_{ij}}&=c_{ij}-\sum_{k\ne i,j}t_{ijk},\\
a_{c_{ijk}}=a_{c_{ijk}^{-1}}&=t_{ijk}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

**Proof.** Conditions (18) make (19) nonnegative. Their total mass is
\(1-C+T+C-3T+2T=1\), and (5) gives (3), proving sufficiency.

Conversely, the classification in the proof of Theorem 2 excludes every other permutation from the support. The operator \(M_Q\) in (3) is symmetric. Transposing a permutation operator replaces the permutation by its inverse, so averaging the proposed distribution with its inverse distribution preserves \(M_Q\). The two orientations of each three-cycle now have the same weight \(t_{ijk}\). Expanding them by (5), the off-diagonal singleton transition from \(i\) to \(j\) is
\(a_{\tau_{ij}}+\sum_k t_{ijk}\). Equation (2) forces this to equal \(c_{ij}\). The identity weight is then forced by normalization to be the first line of (19). Its nonnegativity and the transposition weights' nonnegativity are exactly (18). ∎

For an equivalent sharp scalar reduction, let
\[
\nu(s)=\max\left\{\sum_{i<j<k}z_{ijk}:
z\geq0,\ \sum_{k\ne i,j}z_{ijk}\leq s_i s_j\right\}.
\]
Then the criterion is
\[
\gamma\bigl(e_2(s)-\nu(s)\bigr)\leq1.
\tag{20}
\]
No closed formula for this weighted triangle-packing maximum is claimed. Theorem 1 uses the explicit feasible choice \(t_{ijk}=\gamma s_i s_j s_k\). The flat construction saturates every edge capacity with \(t_{ijk}=\gamma/[n^2(n-2)]\). The reduction is to a sharply identified *common-permutation* problem, not to the general diamond problem.

## 6. Sampling general kernels and an entropy consequence

Let \(\lambda_i\in[0,1]\) and
\[
K=Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)Q^*.
\]
In any parameter range covered by a nonnegative table above, draw independent Bernoulli variables \(B_i\) with means \(\lambda_i\), independently draw \(\Pi\), and return
\[
X=\Pi\{i:B_i=1\}.
\tag{21}
\]
This is an exact sampler for \(P_K\). Indeed the spectral mixture first selects the column subset \(A=\{i:B_i=1\}\) and then uses its projection law \(\mu_A\); (8) supplies precisely that conditional law.

Fix every eigenvalue except \(\lambda_p(t),\lambda_q(t)\), let these two be affine, and suppose their slopes \(a,b\) satisfy \(ab\leq0\). Then
\[
t\longmapsto H\!\left(P_{Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda(t))Q^*}\right)
\quad\text{is concave.}
\tag{22}
\]
This allows arbitrary fixed eigenvalues on the other coordinates, rather than only frozen values zero or one.

Here is a complete short proof. First put all eigenvalues strictly between zero and one, so every atom \(p_X\) is positive. The mixed derivative in \(\lambda_p,\lambda_q\) is a convex combination, over all Bernoulli background column subsets \(A\subseteq E\setminus\{p,q\}\), of the signed diamond laws in (13). The weights of this combination are nonnegative and fixed along the segment. Hence the mixed signed atom table has a nonnegative diamond decomposition, denoted \(d\). Along the affine two-eigenvalue line, \(p''=2ab\,d\).

For a positive DPP atom law, writing \(L=K(I-K)^{-1}>0\) and taking the two-by-two Schur complement over \(S\) gives
\[
p_{S+i}p_{S+j}\geq p_Sp_{S+i+j}.
\]
Consequently each diamond pairs nonpositively with \(\log p\), so \(\sum_Xd_X\log p_X\leq0\). The entropy Hessian is
\[
H''=-\sum_X\frac{(p'_X)^2}{p_X}
      -2ab\sum_Xd_X\log p_X\leq0.
\tag{23}
\]
Regularizing every eigenvalue by \(\lambda\mapsto\varepsilon+(1-2\varepsilon)\lambda\) preserves the slope condition and the fixed frame. Finite Shannon entropy is continuous at zero probabilities. Passing to the limit proves (22) on closed contraction segments. This argument does not prove concavity for unrestricted spectral slopes or varying eigenvectors.
