## 1. Exact order-dependent kernel curvature

For a finite probability law `p`, write

\[
H_\alpha(p)=\frac{\log Z_\alpha(p)}{1-\alpha},
\qquad Z_\alpha(p)=\sum_Sp_S^\alpha,
\qquad \alpha>0,\quad\alpha\ne1.
\tag{1}
\]

This is the entropy of the measured occupation law. It is not the Rényi entropy of a fermionic Gaussian density matrix.

Fix a strict determinantal kernel `K`, a Hermitian direction `A`, and the exact affine parameter `K+tA`. Let `p,g,h` be its atom values, first derivatives, and second derivatives at zero. These are obtained from the signed-column determinant in `TARGET.md`; in particular `sum g=sum h=0`. Direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
H_\alpha''=
\frac1{1-\alpha}\left[
\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\sum_Sp_S^{\alpha-2}g_S^2
+\alpha\sum_Sp_S^{\alpha-1}h_S}{Z_\alpha}
-\frac{\alpha^2(\sum_Sp_S^{\alpha-1}g_S)^2}{Z_\alpha^2}
\right].}
\tag{2}
\]

The last term is the contribution from the logarithmic normalization; no escort derivative or atom acceleration is omitted. For `epsilon=alpha−1`, normalization rewrites (2) as

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{1+\varepsilon}''={}&
-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{Z_{1+\varepsilon}}
\sum_Sp_S^{\varepsilon-1}g_S^2\\
&-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{Z_{1+\varepsilon}}
\sum_S\frac{p_S^\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon}\,h_S\\
&+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^2\varepsilon}{Z_{1+\varepsilon}^2}
\left(\sum_S\frac{p_S^\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon}\,g_S\right)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently the exact Shannon limit is

\[
H_1''=-\sum_S\frac{g_S^2}{p_S}-\sum_Sh_S\log p_S.
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (3) shows what a genuine continuation argument must control. The sign at nearby orders is not automatic from the existence of the limit.

## 2. A universal ordinary-Rényi obstruction, including both sides of one

**Theorem 1.** For every `alpha>0` with `alpha≠1`, the map

\[
K\longmapsto H_\alpha(P_K)
\]

fails concavity on the strict two-site Hermitian contractions. For every `alpha>1`, it already fails on a rank-two commuting square with fixed eigenvectors. Direct sums extend the obstruction to every dimension at least two.

**Proof.** Consider the exact physical kernel path

\[
K_{a,b}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}a&t\\t&b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad 0<a,b<1.
\tag{5}
\]

Its four atoms, ordered as empty, first singleton, second singleton, and full, are exactly

\[
\begin{split}
p_\varnothing&=(1-a)(1-b)-t^2,\qquad
p_1=a(1-b)+t^2,\\
p_2&=(1-a)b+t^2,\qquad
p_{12}=ab-t^2.
\end{split}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus every first atom derivative vanishes at zero, while the second derivatives are `(-2,2,2,-2)`. Put

\[
z_\alpha(x)=x^\alpha+(1-x)^\alpha,
\qquad d_\alpha(x)=x^{\alpha-1}-(1-x)^{\alpha-1}.
\]

Substituting (6) into (2) gives the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}H_\alpha(P_{K_{a,b}(t)})\right|_{t=0}
=\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}
\frac{d_\alpha(a)d_\alpha(b)}{z_\alpha(a)z_\alpha(b)}.}
\tag{7}
\]

For `alpha>1`, choose `a=b=1/4`. The numerator in the ratio is a nonzero square, so (7) is strictly positive. The eigenvectors of this entire line are fixed:

\[
K_{1/4,1/4}(t)
=(1/4+t)uu^*+(1/4-t)vv^*,
\quad u=(1,1)/\sqrt2,\quad v=(1,-1)/\sqrt2.
\tag{8}
\]

Thus the obstruction occurs inside the fixed-eigenvector spectral family whose Shannon concavity is proved in `ROOT_RANK_TWO_SPECTRAL.md`.

For `0<alpha<1`, choose `a=1/4,b=3/4`. Now `d_alpha(b)=−d_alpha(a)`, so the ratio in (7) is strictly negative, as is `alpha−1`; the curvature is again strictly positive. Both displayed paths are strict contractions throughout `|t|≤1/8`. For the first path this follows from the eigenvalues `1/4±t`. For the second, both `K` and `I−K` have positive diagonal entries and determinant `3/16−t²>0`.

In each case the entropy is an even smooth function of `t`. Positive second derivative at zero gives sufficiently small nonzero `t` for which its value at the midpoint is less than its equal endpoint values, contradicting concavity. A block direct sum with any fixed diagonal positive contraction makes the occupation law a product; Rényi entropy is additive for products, so the same failure persists. This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

The same determinant expansion yields a complete formula on every off-diagonal tangent space at a product kernel. If `D=diag(d_i)` is strict and `A=A*` has zero diagonal, then

\[
\boxed{
D^2H_\alpha(P_D)[A,A]
=\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}
\sum_{i<j}|A_{ij}|^2
\frac{d_\alpha(d_i)d_\alpha(d_j)}
{z_\alpha(d_i)z_\alpha(d_j)}.}
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed the second atom derivative is the sum of its two-cycle terms. Each pair gives (6), multiplied by the independent spectator atoms; their power sums cancel the corresponding factors of `Z_alpha`. Cross terms between distinct off-diagonal entries cannot occur in a determinant's second derivative at a diagonal matrix. The formula handles complex Hermitian directions through `|A_ij|²`.

For `alpha=1+epsilon`, (9) has the local expansion

\[
D^2H_{1+\varepsilon}(P_D)[A,A]

\log\frac{d_i}{1-d_i}\log\frac{d_j}{1-d_j}

\tag{10}

Shannon's entire off-diagonal quadratic form at a strict product kernel is zero. At every other order, the leading correction has either sign, depending on the marginals.

## 3. A positive compensated family at every order from one to two

The obstruction above does not prohibit a correlation-dependent correction. The following correction uses a frozen product reference, with the nonlinear relative-divergence term retained before the order limit.

Fix a strict diagonal contraction

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n),\qquad
\mu=P_D=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bern}(d_i).
\]

For a law `p`, let `r=p/mu` and define

\[
\Phi_\alpha(x)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{x^\alpha-\alpha x+\alpha-1}
{\alpha(\alpha-1)},&1<\alpha\le2,\\[6pt]
x\log x-x+1,&\alpha=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]

The value at zero is given by continuity. In every case `Phi_alpha(1)=Phi_alpha'(1)=0` and `Phi_alpha''(x)=x^(alpha−2)` for `x>0`. Put

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_{\alpha,\mu}(K)
=-\sum_Sp_S(K)\log\mu_S
-\mathbb E_\mu\Phi_\alpha(p_S(K)/\mu_S).}
\tag{16}
\]

For `alpha>1`, this has an exact expression using classical *relative* Rényi divergence:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_{\alpha,\mu}(K)
=-\mathbb E_{P_K}\log\mu
-\frac{\exp[(\alpha-1)D_\alpha(P_K\Vert\mu)]-1}
{\alpha(\alpha-1)}.}
\tag{17}
\]

Here `D_alpha(p||mu)=(alpha−1)^(−1) log sum_S p_S^alpha mu_S^(1−alpha)`. The exponential and its normalization in (17) are part of the functional. Neither relative Rényi divergence alone nor ordinary Rényi entropy is being asserted concave. At order one there is exact cancellation:

\[
\mathcal G_{1,\mu}(K)
=-\mathbb E_{P_K}\log\mu-D(P_K\Vert\mu)
=H(P_K).
\tag{18}
\]

**Theorem 2.** For every `alpha in [1,2]`, every strict diagonal `D`, and every Hermitian direction `A`, the function

\eta\longmapsto\mathcal G_{\alpha,P_D}(D+\eta A)

is concave on the whole physical interval `{eta: 0<=D+eta A<=I}`. In particular it is concave on every segment from `D` to a positive contraction `K`. The reference `P_D` is frozen along the line.

### Proof of Theorem 2

**Step 1: an elementary jointly convex kernel.** For positive `x,y`, let

\[
J_\alpha(x,y)
=(x-y)(\Phi_\alpha'(x)-\Phi_\alpha'(y)).
\]

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives

\[
\boxed{
J_\alpha(x,y)
=(x-y)^2\int_0^1[(1-u)y+ux]^{\alpha-2}\,du.}
\tag{19}
\]

For `0<=q<=1`, the function `z²w^(−q)` on `z real, w>0` is jointly convex. Indeed its Hessian has first diagonal entry `2w^(−q)>0` and determinant

\[
2q(1-q)z^2w^{-2q-2}\ge0.
\tag{20}
\]

Take `q=2−alpha`. In each integrand of (19), `z=x−y` and `w=(1−u)y+ux` are affine functions of `(x,y)`. Therefore every integrand, and its integral, is jointly convex. This proof includes both endpoints `alpha=1,2`.

**Step 2: coordinate replacement gives convex power divergence in the affine retention parameter.** This step holds for any input law, with no determinantal assumption. Let `E_i` average a density with respect to the reference bit `mu_i`, keeping the other coordinates fixed. These are commuting conditional-expectation projections. On densities put

\[
T_\eta=\prod_i[\eta I+(1-\eta)E_i],\qquad r_\eta=T_\eta r_0.
\tag{21}
\]

Assume first that `r_0>0`, and set `tau=−log eta`. A dot denotes differentiation in `tau`. Then

\[
\dot r=\sum_i(E_i-I)r.
\]

Let `F=E_mu Phi_alpha(r)` and define

\[
I_i=\mathbb E_\mu J_\alpha(r,E_i r),\qquad
I=\sum_iI_i,
\qquad
\chi_i=\mathbb E_\mu\Phi_\alpha''(r)(r-E_i r)^2.
\]

Because `Phi_alpha'(E_i r)` is independent of coordinate `i`, conditional expectation gives

\[
\dot F=-\sum_i\mathbb E_\mu\Phi_\alpha'(r)(r-E_i r)
=-I.
\tag{22}
\]

For any positive pair of densities `r,s`, define the dissipation under coordinate `j` by

\[
\mathscr E_j(r,s)
=-\left.\frac d{dv}\mathbb E_\mu
J_\alpha(C_{j,e^{-v}}r,C_{j,e^{-v}}s)\right|_{v=0},
\quad C_{j,\theta}=\theta I+(1-\theta)E_j.
\]

Joint convexity from Step 1 and Jensen's inequality imply

\[
\mathbb E_\mu J_\alpha(C_{j,\theta}r,C_{j,\theta}s)
\le\mathbb E_\mu J_\alpha(r,s).
\]

Thus `mathscr E_j>=0`. Commutation gives

\[
\ddot F=\sum_{i,j}\mathscr E_j(r,E_i r).
\]

For `j=i`, the second argument stays fixed under its own replacement. Differentiating `J_alpha` directly yields

\[
\mathscr E_i(r,E_i r)=I_i+\chi_i.
\]

Hence the complete curvature identity, with the parameter conversion retained, is

\[
\boxed{
\eta^2\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}\mathbb E_\mu\Phi_\alpha(r_\eta)
=\ddot F+\dot F
=\sum_i\chi_i+\sum_{i\ne j}\mathscr E_j(r,E_i r)\ge0.}
\tag{23}
\]

This is the power-divergence counterpart of C032's order-one conditional-replacement identity. Its positive scalar kernel (19) supplies the additional orders. In particular it is not the arbitrary-direction scalar atom-resolvent density refuted by C046.

Approximate an input with zero atoms by mixing it with `mu`. The channel, finite sum in (15), and endpoint values are continuous; the convexity inequality passes to the limit. The same argument handles `eta=0`.

**Step 3: the determinantal parameter is exactly affine.** Let `X~P_K`. Retain each bit independently with probability `eta`, and otherwise replace it by a fresh bit from `mu_i`. For any coordinate set `S`, its new inclusion probability is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in S}Y_i
&=\sum_{J\subseteq S}\eta^{|J|}(1-\eta)^{|S\setminus J|}
\det K_J\prod_{i\in S\setminus J}d_i\\
&=\det\bigl(\eta K_S+(1-\eta)D_S\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The last equality expands the determinant along the selected diagonal columns of `D`. Inclusion probabilities determine the entire finite law. Thus

\[
P_{D+\eta(K-D)}=T_\eta P_K,
\tag{25}
\]

where on the right the channel acts on laws, or equivalently on densities as in (21). This is the exact C006/C032 intertwining, explicitly retained here.

The cross entropy in (16) is affine in the kernel diagonal:

\[
-\mathbb E_{P_K}\log\mu
=-\sum_i[K_{ii}\log d_i+(1-K_{ii})\log(1-d_i)].
\tag{26}
\]

Equations (23), (25), and (26) prove concavity on each segment from `D` to `K`. For an arbitrary physical line through `D`, apply this result separately to its two endpoint segments. At `D`, the derivative of the divergence term is zero because `Phi_alpha'(1)=0`; the affine cross-entropy derivative agrees from both sides. Therefore the two concave pieces join with no upward jump in derivative and give concavity on the entire physical interval. This proves Theorem 2. \(\square\)

## 4. The limiting statements are uniform and do not assume a continuation theorem

### Uniform ordinary-Rényi convergence does not imply nearby concavity

Let `N` be the number of atoms and allow zero probabilities. For `|epsilon|<=1/2`, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
H_{1+\varepsilon}(p)
=-\int_0^1
\frac{\sum_Sp_S^{1+u\varepsilon}\log p_S}
{\sum_Sp_S^{1+u\varepsilon}}\,du,}
\tag{28}
\]

with `0^beta log 0=0` for `beta>0`. The integrand is jointly continuous on the compact set consisting of the finite simplex, `u in [0,1]`, and `epsilon in [−1/2,1/2]`. Its denominator has a strictly positive lower bound, for example `N^(−1/2)`. Therefore (28) gives uniform convergence of `H_alpha` to `H` over the entire simplex.

Nevertheless, Theorem 1 supplies nonconcavity at every nonunit order. Its shrinking gaps (14) are completely compatible with uniform convergence. A limit of concave functions would be concave even under pointwise convergence, but the required concave ordinary-Rényi sequence does not exist here.

### An explicit uniform bound for the positive compensated family

For the frozen positive reference `mu`, let

\[
m=\min_S\mu_S>0,\qquad L=\log(1/m),\qquad N=2^n.
\]

For every law `p` and `0<epsilon<=1`, put

\[
M(\varepsilon)=\sum_Sp_S(p_S/\mu_S)^\varepsilon,
\qquad D=\sum_Sp_S\log(p_S/\mu_S).
\]

One has `0<=D<=L`. Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives

\[
M(\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon D+R,
\quad 0\le R\le\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}B,
\quad B=\frac{8N/e^2+2L^2}{m}.
\tag{29}
\]

To check the bound without any lower bound on `p`, note that for `0<=u<=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
M''(u)
&=\sum_Sp_S(p_S/\mu_S)^u\log^2(p_S/\mu_S)\\
&\le\frac1m\left[2\sum_Sp_S\log^2p_S
+2\sum_Sp_S\log^2\mu_S\right]\\
&\le\frac{8N/e^2+2L^2}{m}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The scalar maximum `max_{0<=x<=1} x log²x=4/e²` proves the last line. All expressions extend continuously over zero atoms.

Since the power-divergence term in (16) is

\[
\mathbb E_\mu\Phi_{1+\varepsilon}(p/\mu)
=\frac{D+R/\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon},
\]

(29) proves the explicit uniform error estimate

\[
\boxed{
\sup_p\left|\mathcal G_{1+\varepsilon,\mu}(p)-H(p)\right|
\le\varepsilon\left[L+\frac{4N/e^2+L^2}{m}\right].}
\tag{30}
\]

The supremum is over all laws on the finite configuration space, hence in particular over all determinantal laws. Applying the concavity inequality of Theorem 2 and taking any sequence `alpha down to 1` gives the Shannon chord inequality on each diagonal-reference line. This is a valid compensated limit with a proved premise.

### Interior curvature convergence and its precise limitation

Both (3) and (28), and the analogous finite formulas for (16), are smooth through `alpha=1` wherever all atoms are bounded below. If

\[
\kappa I\preceq K\preceq(1-\kappa)I,
\qquad 0<\kappa\le1/2,
\]

then every atom has the useful bound `p_S(K)>=kappa^n`. For `kappa<1/2`, write `K=kappa I+(1−2kappa)B`, with `0<=B<=I`, and use (25) with fair replacement. The event that all bits are replaced has probability `(2kappa)^n` and assigns each configuration conditional probability `2^(−n)`. The case `kappa=1/2` is immediate.

Since exact atoms are polynomials in the real Hermitian coordinates, their derivatives are bounded on this compact domain. Differentiating the preceding finite smooth expressions therefore gives uniform `C²` convergence in the kernel coordinates, with error `O(|alpha−1|)`. This justifies limiting the Hessian on strict compact sets. It does **not** preserve the sign of a zero eigenvalue of the limiting Hessian: (10) is the exact obstruction at its off-diagonal null space.
