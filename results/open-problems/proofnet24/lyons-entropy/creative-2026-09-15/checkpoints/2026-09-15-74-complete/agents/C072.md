## 2. The exact three-score theorem

Let \(0<K<I_n\), let \(u_1,u_2,u_3\in\mathbb C^n\) be linearly independent, and put \(B_i=u_i u_i^*\). Write \(p_S=P_K(X=S)>0\), and define

\[
s_i(S)=\frac{Dp_S(K)[B_i]}{p_S},\qquad
G_{ij}=\sum_Sp_Ss_i(S)s_j(S),
\]
\[
C_{ij}=-\sum_SD^2p_S(K)[B_i,B_j]\log p_S.
\tag{6}
\]

Each atom is affine along a rank-one direction, so \(C_{ii}=0\). The matrix \(G\) is the complete score Gram matrix; its off-diagonal entries are retained. Moreover \(G_{ii}>0\): otherwise the first derivative of every atom would vanish, contradicting
\(D\mathbb E|X|[B_i]=\operatorname{tr}B_i>0\).

Put \(H=G-C\), and abbreviate

\[
\alpha=G_{11},\quad \delta=G_{22},\quad \gamma=G_{33},\quad
b=H_{12},\quad c=H_{13},\quad d=H_{23}.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(A=xB_1+yB_2-zB_3\), the exact entropy curvature is

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(K)[A,A]
=\alpha x^2+2bxy+\delta y^2+\gamma z^2-2(cx+dy)z.
}
\tag{8}
\]

For positive \(x,y,z\), the direction has rank three and inertia (2,1), by congruence through the full-column-rank matrix \((u_1,u_2,u_3)\). Formula (8) includes all mixed frozen terms, including the same-sign pair \(B_1,B_2\), and all Fisher cross terms.

**Theorem B — exact scalar threshold when \(c,d\ge0\).** Suppose \(c,d\ge0\). Define

\[
a=\alpha-\frac{c^2}{\gamma},\qquad
e=\delta-\frac{d^2}{\gamma},\qquad
f=b-\frac{cd}{\gamma}.
\tag{9}
\]

Then every \(x,y,z\ge0\) has nonpositive entropy curvature if and only if

\[
\boxed{\quad a\ge0,\qquad e\ge0,\qquad f+\sqrt{ae}\ge0.\quad}
\tag{10}
\]

When (10) holds, the complete quadratic form has the constructive decomposition

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H[A,A]
=\gamma\left(z-\frac{cx+dy}{\gamma}\right)^2
 +(\sqrt a\,x-\sqrt e\,y)^2
 +2(f+\sqrt{ae})xy.
}
\tag{11}
\]

**Proof.** Complete the square in \(z\) in (8). Since \(c,d,x,y\ge0\), its unconstrained minimizer \((cx+dy)/\gamma\) is also allowed under \(z\ge0\). The remaining form is \(ax^2+2fxy+ey^2\). Its nonnegativity on \(x,y\ge0\) is equivalent to (10), by the last two terms in (11). Necessity of \(a,e\ge0\) follows on the axes. If \(a,e>0\) and \(f< -\sqrt{ae}\), use \((x,y)=(\sqrt e,\sqrt a)\). If either diagonal vanishes, a sufficiently unbalanced positive pair proves the same necessity by a limit. This establishes the equivalence and the identity. \(\square\)

For example, if

\[
0\le C_{13}\le G_{13},\qquad 0\le C_{23}\le G_{23},
\tag{12}
\]

then \(c,d\ge0\), and the first two inequalities in (10) follow from score Cauchy–Schwarz. The only remaining condition is the explicit mixed-curvature threshold

\[
\boxed{
C_{12}\le G_{12}-\frac{(G_{13}-C_{13})(G_{23}-C_{23})}{G_{33}}
+\sqrt{
\left(G_{11}-\frac{(G_{13}-C_{13})^2}{G_{33}}\right)
\left(G_{22}-\frac{(G_{23}-C_{23})^2}{G_{33}}\right)}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Condition (12) is an explicit hypothesis. It is not inferred from conditional negative association; ROOT_FISHER_IDENTITY gives an obstruction to such an entrywise inference.

The cross-score correction has a useful interpretation. Write
\(g=(G_{13},G_{23})^T\) and \(v=(C_{13},C_{23})^T\), and let \(G_2,C_2\) be the top-left two-by-two blocks. The matrix in (9) is exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&f\\f&e\end{pmatrix}
=\underbrace{G_2-\frac{gg^T}{G_{33}}}_{\text{Gram matrix of scores residualized against }s_3}
-C_2+\frac{gv^T+vg^T-vv^T}{G_{33}}.
\tag{14}
\]

Thus (13) retains the information shared with the oppositely signed score and the way both opposite-sign mixed curvatures modify that cancellation.

### A universal finite test without the signs in (12)

No assumption on \(c,d\) is necessary for the exact reduction. Minimizing (8) over \(z\ge0\) gives

\[
\min_{z\ge0}[-D^2H[A,A]]
=\alpha x^2+2bxy+\delta y^2-\frac{(cx+dy)_+^2}{\gamma}.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(y>0\), put \(t=x/y\). Split \([0,\infty)\) at the possible nonnegative root of \(ct+d\). On the portion where \(ct+d\le0\), test

\[
q_-(t)=\alpha t^2+2bt+\delta;
\]

on the portion where \(ct+d\ge0\), test

\[
q_+(t)=\left(\alpha-\frac{c^2}{\gamma}\right)t^2
+2\left(b-\frac{cd}{\gamma}\right)t
+\delta-\frac{d^2}{\gamma}.
\tag{16}
\]

These two polynomials being nonnegative on their assigned intervals is **necessary and sufficient** for all \(x,y,z\ge0\). This is a finite exact test: a quadratic \(At^2+2Bt+D\) on an interval needs only its finite endpoints and its vertex \(-B/A\) when \(A>0\) and that vertex belongs to the interval. On an unbounded interval, also require \(A\ge0\), and \(B\ge0\) when \(A=0\). These tail conditions include the missing case \(y=0\). The claim follows immediately from (15) and elementary quadratic minimization. `verify.py:check_cone` implements this finite test with rational inputs.

## 4. A full-score interval bound with only three Bernstein matrices

Put \(s_0=2^{32},s_1=2^{33}\), let \(\rho_j=\rho(s_j)\), and parameterize the whole interval by

\[
t=\frac{\rho-\rho_0}{\rho_1-\rho_0}\in[0,1].
\]

Use subscripts 0 and 1 for endpoint values of the atom data in (24), and set \(p_S^{\max}=\max(p_{S,0},p_{S,1})\). Because all data are affine in \(\rho\),

\[
p_S(t)\le p_S^{\max},\qquad
q_S(t)=(1-t)q_{S,0}+tq_{S,1}.
\]

Consequently the **complete** atom Fisher matrix obeys

\[
\boxed{
G(t):=\sum_S\frac{q_S(t)^Tq_S(t)}{p_S(t)}
\succeq (1-t)^2G_0+2t(1-t)G_1+t^2G_2,
}
\tag{25}
\]

where

\[
G_0=\sum_S\frac{q_{S,0}^Tq_{S,0}}{p_S^{\max}},\qquad
G_2=\sum_S\frac{q_{S,1}^Tq_{S,1}}{p_S^{\max}},
\]
\[
G_1=\sum_S\frac{q_{S,0}^Tq_{S,1}+q_{S,1}^Tq_{S,0}}{2p_S^{\max}}.
\tag{26}
\]

The right side is the Bernstein expansion of the sum of the actual score-row squares with their denominators replaced by interval upper bounds. In particular, the rare empty-atom contribution is retained as its complete correlated rank-one matrix. It is not replaced by six separate diagonal budgets.

The complete frozen curvature matrix is

\[
C(t)=-\sum_Sr_S(t)\log p_S(t).
\tag{27}
\]

## 5. Imaginary directions and the full nine-dimensional theorem

This section supplies an analytic bound, avoiding any inference from a real-symmetric numerical calculation. Let \(J=iB\), where \(B\) is any real skew-symmetric six-by-six matrix. At the real base \(K_s\), conjugation symmetry gives \(Dp_S[J]=0\) for every atom, and therefore \(I_{K_s}(J)=0\).

After deleting constant and modular terms from the frozen log atom in (19), its complete remaining table is

\[
\phi(S)=-\log\left(1+s\sum_{i\in S}i\right)
=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t
\left(e^{-st\sum_{i\in S}i}-1\right)dt.
\tag{31}
\]

Define

\[
Y_t(S)=e^{-st\sum_{i\in S}i},\quad G_t=\mathbb E_{K_s}Y_t,
\quad C_t=I-\operatorname{diag}(e^{-sti}),
\]
\[
M_t=I-C_t^{1/2}K_sC_t^{1/2}>0,
\qquad R_t=C_t^{1/2}M_t^{-1}C_t^{1/2}.
\tag{32}
\]

The DPP generating determinant gives \(G_t=\det M_t\). All these matrices are real. Ordinary determinant differentiation gives, for a Hermitian direction \(A\),

\[
D^2G_t[A,A]=G_t\{(\operatorname{tr}R_tA)^2
-\operatorname{tr}[(R_t^{1/2}AR_t^{1/2})^2]\}.
\]

For the imaginary direction \(J\), \(\operatorname{tr}R_tJ=0\), since a real symmetric matrix is trace-orthogonal to a real skew-symmetric matrix. Hence the full entropy curvature has the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(K_s)[J,J]
=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}tG_t
\operatorname{tr}[(R_t^{1/2}JR_t^{1/2})^2]dt.
}
\tag{33}
\]

All differentiations in (31) are justified by a finite atom sum. Near zero the constant and modular terms have zero second derivative, leaving an integrable \(O(t)\) term; at infinity the positive-odds integral has its \(e^{-t}\) factor.

For \(s\ge2\), restrict (33) to \(1/s\le t\le2/s\). The elementary inequalities \(2<e<3\) give

\[
R_t\succeq C_t\succeq\tfrac12 I,
\qquad
\int_{1/s}^{2/s}\frac{e^{-t}}t\,dt\ge\frac16.
\tag{34}
\]

Indeed \(M_t\preceq I\), so \(M_t^{-1}\succeq I\); also \(sti\ge1\), \(t\le1\), and \(1/t\ge s/2\).

The singleton atom \(S=\{1\}\) has the exact uniform lower bound

\[
p_{\{1\}}(s)=\frac{1+s}{7(1+sh)}>\frac1{7h}=\frac{20}{617}.
\]

On the chosen integration interval, \(Y_t(\{1\})\ge e^{-2}>1/9\), so
\(G_t\ge20/(9\cdot617)\). In an eigenbasis of \(R_t\),
\(\operatorname{tr}[(R_t^{1/2}JR_t^{1/2})^2]
=\sum_{a,b}\lambda_a\lambda_b|J_{ab}|^2\ge\|J\|_F^2/4\).
Thus (33)–(34) prove the explicit bound

\[
\boxed{
-D^2H(K_s)[J,J]\ge\frac5{33318}\|J\|_F^2
>\frac1{10000}\|J\|_F^2
\qquad(s\ge2).
}
\tag{35}
\]

Now write \(A=UMU^T=R+J\), with \(R\) real symmetric and \(J\) imaginary Hermitian. The real part belongs to the six-dimensional span (20). Entropy is invariant under entrywise conjugation, so its mixed real/imaginary Hessian vanishes at a real base. Also \(\|A\|_F^2=\|R\|_F^2+\|J\|_F^2\). Combining (3) and (35) proves Theorem A in all nine Hermitian coordinates. No restriction on the rank or orientation of the imaginary part was used in (35).
