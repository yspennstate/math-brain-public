## 1. A convex family with two moving reservoir modes

On a measured pair put
\[
v_+=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom11,\quad
v_-=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom1{-1},\quad
P_\pm=v_\pm v_\pm^*.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), real \(x_*,y_*\), and \(c_-,c_+\ge0\). Write
\[
x(p)=x_*-c_-(p-\tfrac12),\qquad
y(p)=y_*-c_+(p-\tfrac12),
\]
\[
B(p)=y(p)P_++x(p)P_-,\qquad
T=c_+P_++c_-P_-\succeq0.
\tag{2}
\]
The seed is the three-site kernel
\[
N(p,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&\bar z\,v_+^*\\
zv_+&B(p)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad p\in\mathbb R,\ z\in\mathbb C.
\tag{3}
\]
Define the parameter domain \(\Omega\) by
\[
\epsilon\le p\le1-\epsilon,\qquad
\frac14\le x(p)\le\frac34,
\tag{4}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\begin{pmatrix}p&\bar z\\z&y(p)-1/4\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1-p&\bar z\\z&3/4-y(p)\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.}
\tag{5}
\]
This is a convex domain in the three real variables \(p,\Re z,\Im z\): all displayed matrices and scalar bounds are affine. We assume it is nonempty.

The conditional pair kernels given the pivot are
\[
F_1=x(p)P_-+\left(y(p)-\frac{|z|^2}{p}\right)P_+,
\quad
F_0=x(p)P_-+\left(y(p)+\frac{|z|^2}{1-p}\right)P_+.
\tag{6}
\]
Every mode eigenvalue of both conditionals lies in \([1/4,3/4]\). Indeed, (5) gives the lower bound for the included guide mode and the upper bound for the excluded guide mode; their reverse bounds follow from \(1/4\le y(p)\le3/4\), which (5) also implies. The transverse bounds are (4). Thus every seed kernel is a strict contraction, including boundary points of the displayed domain, by the two conditional Schur complements and \(p\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon]\).

**Theorem 1.** The map \(H(P_{N(p,z)})\) is jointly concave on \(\Omega\). More strongly, for every fixed finite binary-input channel \(C(o\mid b)\), the entropy of the vector consisting of the channel output at the pivot and the unmodified two pair occupations is jointly concave on the same domain. Sections 3–5 give exact and quantitative finite-gap formulas.

The theorem does not claim concavity on every physical parameter value outside (4)–(5). Both moving modes are allowed throughout the entire stated convex domain.

## 2. The balanced pair has an entrywise negative mode Hessian

For \(u,v\in[1/4,3/4]\), define
\[
\mathcal H(u,v)=H(P_{uP_++vP_-}).
\]
Its measured atom probabilities are
\[
p_{00}=(1-u)(1-v),\quad p_{11}=uv,\quad
p_{10}=p_{01}=\frac{w}{2},\qquad w=u+v-2uv.
\tag{7}
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal H\) is the entropy in the original pair coordinates. It is not being identified with spectral Bernoulli entropy.

Let
\[
L=\log\frac{w^2}{4uv(1-u)(1-v)}.
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
-\mathcal H_{uu}
=\frac{1-v}{1-u}+\frac vu+\frac{(1-2v)^2}{w},
\qquad
\mathcal H_{uv}=L-\frac1w.
\tag{8}
\]
On the balanced square,
\[
\frac38\le w\le\frac58,\quad
uv(1-u)(1-v)\ge\frac9{256},\quad
0\le L\le2\log(5/3)\le\frac43.
\]
Consequently
\[
-\mathcal H_{uv}\ge\frac85-\frac43=\frac4{15}.
\tag{9}
\]
Also \(-\mathcal H_{uu}\ge2\): the four-atom law is affine in \(u\), so this second derivative is its Fisher information. The total count has mean derivative one and variance \(u(1-u)+v(1-v)\le1/2\), and score Cauchy–Schwarz gives the bound. The same argument gives \(-\mathcal H_{vv}\ge2\). Thus every entry of the negative two-mode Hessian satisfies
\[
\boxed{[-D^2\mathcal H(u,v)]_{ij}\ge\kappa,\qquad
\kappa=\frac4{15},\quad i,j\in\{+,-\}.}
\tag{10}
\]
These bounds are the relevant C057 balanced-pair calculation, reproduced to make the new finite construction complete.

If \(Q_1\preceq Q_0\) are both of the form \(uP_++vP_-\) in this square, write their nonnegative mode difference as \(\mathbf d\). Integrating (10) along the segment between them shows that each mode component of
\(\nabla\mathcal H(Q_1)-\nabla\mathcal H(Q_0)\)
is at least \(\kappa\,\operatorname{tr}(Q_0-Q_1)\). Therefore, for every \(W=w_+P_++w_-P_-\succeq0\),
\[
\left\langle\nabla H(Q_1)-\nabla H(Q_0),W\right\rangle
\ge\kappa\,\operatorname{tr}W\,\operatorname{tr}(Q_0-Q_1).
\tag{11}
\]
Only the mode derivatives are needed in this pairing. It is enough to read the gradients as derivatives within the two-dimensional span of \(P_+,P_-\).

## 3. A new positive finite correction

Take any two points \((p_0,z_0),(p_1,z_1)\in\Omega\), let \(0<t<1\), and put
\[
(p,z)=(1-t)(p_0,z_0)+t(p_1,z_1),\qquad a=p,\quad b=1-p,\quad q=ab.
\]
Write \(\Delta p=p_1-p_0\), \(\Delta z=z_1-z_0\). Let \(G_{j,i}\) be the endpoint conditional pair kernels, and define the branch weights and barycenters
\[
\omega_{0,1}=(1-t)p_0/a,\quad\omega_{1,1}=tp_1/a,
\]
\[
\omega_{0,0}=(1-t)(1-p_0)/b,\quad
\omega_{1,0}=t(1-p_1)/b,\qquad
M_i=\omega_{0,i}G_{0,i}+\omega_{1,i}G_{1,i}.
\tag{12}
\]
Every \(G_{j,i},M_i,F_i\) belongs to the same balanced mode square.

Since \(B(p_1)-B(p_0)=-(\Delta p)T\), the C067 covariance identity gives
\[
V=t(1-t)\big(|\Delta z|^2P_++(\Delta p)^2T\big)\succeq0,
\]
\[
\boxed{F_1=M_1+V/a,\qquad F_0=M_0-V/b.}
\tag{13}
\]
For clarity, the general varying-pivot expression is
\(V=t(1-t)(\Delta z_{\rm vec}\Delta z_{\rm vec}^*-
\Delta p\,\Delta B)\); here \(\Delta z_{\rm vec}=\Delta z\,v_+\).

Define
\[
J_h=h(a)-(1-t)h(p_0)-th(p_1),
\quad
J_i=H(M_i)-\sum_{j=0}^1\omega_{j,i}H(G_{j,i}).
\tag{14}
\]
The entropy chain rule is the exact identity
\[
\Gamma_t^{\rm seed}
=J_h+aJ_1+bJ_0+\mathcal C_t,
\quad
\mathcal C_t=a[H(F_1)-H(M_1)]+b[H(F_0)-H(M_0)].
\tag{15}
\]
The two individual differences in \(\mathcal C_t\) need not have known signs.

Set, for \(0\le s\le1\),
\[
Q_1(s)=M_1+sV/a,\qquad Q_0(s)=M_0-sV/b.
\tag{16}
\]
These are segments between balanced pair kernels. They are ordered, since
\[
D(s):=Q_0(s)-Q_1(s)
=\frac{|z|^2P_++(1-s)V}{q}\succeq0.
\tag{17}
\]
Differentiating the two entropy differences along (16) gives the exact finite correction
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_t
=\int_0^1
\left\langle\nabla H(Q_1(s))-\nabla H(Q_0(s)),V\right\rangle\,ds.}
\tag{18}
\]
This is a paired correction: the two gradients are compared along an ordered path, rather than signing either entropy difference separately.

For an explicitly positive integrand, write \(\mathbf v\) for the two mode coefficients of \(V\), and \(\mathbf q_1(s),\mathbf d(s)\) for those of \(Q_1(s),D(s)\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_t
=\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1
\mathbf v^{T}\left[-D^2\mathcal H\big(
\mathbf q_1(s)+r\mathbf d(s)\big)\right]\mathbf d(s)\,dr\,ds\ge0.}
\tag{19}
\]
Both vectors have nonnegative entries, and every entry of the matrix is nonnegative by (10). Every evaluation point is in the balanced square by convexity. This is not an inference from negative semidefiniteness alone; the entrywise sign and the two nonnegative vectors are essential.

Applying (11) to (18) gives
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_t\ge
\frac4{15q}\operatorname{tr}V\,|z|^2
+\frac2{15q}(\operatorname{tr}V)^2.}
\tag{20}
\]
The three Jensen terms in (15) are nonnegative: binary concavity gives \(J_h\ge0\), and established full two-site kernel concavity gives \(J_0,J_1\ge0\). This proves the seed assertion of Theorem 1, with the exact nonnegative decomposition (15),(19). It applies to every chord in \(\Omega\).

## 4. Every fixed channel at the pivot preserves the construction

Apply a finite binary-input channel \(C\) only to the pivot. C061's balanced-pair refinement provides finitely many input-independent labels \(g\), weights \(w_g\), and conditional monotone binary channels
\[
\Pr(\widetilde X=1\mid X=x,g)=r_g+s_gx,\quad
0\le r_g\le r_g+s_g\le1,
\]
\[
\sum_gw_g=1,\qquad
\sum_gw_gs_g=\tau_C:=
\operatorname{TV}(C(\cdot\mid0),C(\cdot\mid1)).
\tag{21}
\]
The refinement entropy correction \(A_C(p)\) is affine in the pivot mean. The refined joint entropy therefore satisfies
\[
H_{\rm out}(p,z)=H(w)+\sum_gw_g H(P_{N_g(p,z)})-A_C(p),
\tag{22}
\]
where
\[
N_g(p,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
p_g&\sqrt{s_g}\,\bar z\,v_+^*\\
\sqrt{s_g}\,zv_+&B(p)
\end{pmatrix},\qquad p_g=r_g+s_gp.
\tag{23}
\]
This is an exact fixed mixture of DPP entropies plus an affine term, not a generic data-processing claim about entropy concavity.

For \(s_g>0\), all conditional pair modes of (23) are still balanced. One direct check is
\[
y-\frac{s_g|z|^2}{p_g}
=y-\theta_1\frac{|z|^2}{p},\qquad
\theta_1=\frac{s_gp}{r_g+s_gp}\in[0,1],
\]
\[
y+\frac{s_g|z|^2}{1-p_g}
=y+\theta_0\frac{|z|^2}{1-p},\qquad
\theta_0=\frac{s_g(1-p)}{1-r_g-s_gp}\in[0,1].
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the transformed included guide mode lies between \(y\) and the original included guide mode, and similarly for the excluded mode. The transverse mode stays \(x\). Since the original \(p\) is interior, \(s_g>0\) also gives \(0<p_g<1\).

The covariance for any transformed chord is exactly
\[
V_g=s_gV\succeq0.
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, the pivot difference is \(s_g\Delta p\), the coupling difference is \(\sqrt{s_g}\Delta z\), and the complementary-block difference is still \(-\Delta p\,T\). The proof in Section 3 needs only this positive covariance and balanced conditional modes; it therefore applies to every group. For \(s_g=0\), the kernel is a direct sum of a fixed Bernoulli bit and \(B(p)\); its entropy is concave by two-site concavity, including a deterministic fixed bit.

Since the label entropy is constant and \(A_C(p)\) is affine, their chord gaps vanish. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_t^{\rm out}
=\sum_gw_g\big[J_{h,g}+p_gJ_{1,g}
+(1-p_g)J_{0,g}+\mathcal C_{t,g}\big]\ge0,}
\tag{26}
\]
with the evident direct-sum interpretation for zero-slope groups. Every \(\mathcal C_{t,g}\) has the positive double-integral formula (19). This completes the channel assertion of Theorem 1.

Let \(\mu_C=\sum_gw_gs_g^2\ge\tau_C^2\). Using
\(h''\le-4\), \(p_g(1-p_g)\le1/4\), \(V_g=s_gV\), and
\(|z_g|^2=s_g|z|^2\) in (20),(26) yields
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_t^{\rm out}\ge\mu_C\bigg[
&2t(1-t)(\Delta p)^2\\
&+\frac{16}{15}\operatorname{tr}V\,|z|^2
+\frac8{15}(\operatorname{tr}V)^2\bigg].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{27}
\]
The same inequality holds with \(\tau_C^2\) in place of \(\mu_C\). If \(\tau_C>0\), every nonconstant chord in the parameter pair \((p,z)\) has strictly positive gap: a nonzero \(\Delta p\) makes the first term positive; if \(\Delta p=0,\Delta z\ne0\), then \(\operatorname{tr}V=t(1-t)|\Delta z|^2>0\), making the last term positive even when the target \(z\) vanishes.

## 5. Replace the pivot by any coherent reservoir

Let \(D\) be any strict Hermitian contraction on a nonempty measured block \(E\), and let \(u\ne0\) be any vector on that block. It need not be an eigenvector of \(D\). Fix
\[
\alpha=(u^*D^{-1}u)^{-1},\qquad
\beta=(u^*(I-D)^{-1}u)^{-1},\qquad
\ell=\alpha+\beta.
\tag{28}
\]
Consider the physical family on \(E\sqcup\{+{\rm pair}\}\):
\[
\boxed{
K(p,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D+(\ell p-\alpha)uu^*&\sqrt\ell\,\bar z\,uv_+^*\\
\sqrt\ell\,zv_+u^*&y(p)P_++x(p)P_-
\end{pmatrix},\qquad (p,z)\in\Omega.}
\tag{29}
\]
The cross block has rank one whenever \(z\ne0\). The first reservoir changes in its guide direction; the second changes in both orthogonal collective modes if \(c_-,c_+>0\).

**Theorem 2 (moving coherent reservoirs across a rank-one bridge).**
The measured entropy \(H(P_{K(p,z)})\) is jointly concave on the whole convex domain \(\Omega\), with exact gap (26) and bound (27) for the channel
\[
C(Y\mid0)=P_{D-\alpha uu^*}(Y),\qquad
C(Y\mid1)=P_{D+\beta uu^*}(Y).
\tag{30}
\]
This channel has \(\tau_C>0\), so every nonconstant parameter chord is strictly concave.

Here are the needed block-substitution facts. Rank-one Schur complements give
\(D+suu^*\succeq0\iff s\ge-\alpha\), and
\(I-D-suu^*\succeq0\iff s\le\beta\).
For \(B=yP_++xP_-\), its inverse on the guide is \(1/y\); its complementary inverse on the guide is \(1/(1-y)\). Hence positivity and complementary positivity of (29) are exactly the corresponding seed conditions
\[
p-|z|^2/y\ge0,\qquad
1-p-|z|^2/(1-y)\ge0,
\tag{31}
\]
in addition to \(0<x,y<1\). They are strict on \(\Omega\) by Section 1. Thus (29) is a strict positive contraction and the endpoint kernels in (30) are closed positive contractions.

The exact law identity is
\[
\boxed{P_{K(p,z)}
=(C\otimes {\rm Id}_{\{0,1\}^2})P_{N(p,z)}.}
\tag{32}
\]
To see it at the level of inclusion moments, fix \(A\subseteq E\) and a subset \(J\) of the pair. Put
\[
h_b(A)=\det(D-\alpha uu^*+b\ell uu^*)_A,\quad b=0,1,
\quad \Delta h=h_1-h_0.
\]
A rank-one block determinant expansion gives
\[
\det K_{A\cup J}
=h_0(A)\det B_J+
\Delta h(A)\det N_{\{\mathrm{pivot}\}\cup J}.
\tag{33}
\]
One may first use invertible baseline principal blocks and then extend the resulting polynomial identity to singular ones; empty inclusion sets have determinant one. The right side is exactly
\(\mathbb E[(h_0(A)+X_{\rm pivot}\Delta h(A))
1_{\{J\subseteq X_{\rm pair}\}}]\).
Thus all inclusion moments agree, proving (32). Theorem 1 applied to (30) proves Theorem 2. Finally, at least one singleton inclusion probability changes by
\(\ell|u_i|^2>0\), so the channel is informative.

The normalization and block-channel identity are the established C061 mechanism, now used with a new three-site family that remains concave under every pivot channel. The moving transverse pair mode is absent from C061's two-guide theorem.

## 6. Why the finite correction enlarges the usable domain

For the concrete constants
\[
\epsilon=\frac14,\quad x_*=\frac25,\quad y_*=\frac35,
\quad c_-=\frac15,\quad c_+=\frac1{10},
\]
\[
x(p)=\frac12-\frac p5,\qquad
y(p)=\frac{13}{20}-\frac p{10},
\tag{34}
\]
the point \(p=1/2,z=0\) belongs to the interior of \(\Omega\). Its two actual conditionals coincide:
\[
F_1=F_0=\frac35P_++\frac25P_-.
\]
Both their ordinary and complementary odds diagonals are
\[
\frac12\left(\frac{3/5}{2/5}+\frac{2/5}{3/5}\right)
=\frac{13}{12}>1.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus neither of C067's opposite target atom-monotonicity tests holds at this point. In fact its atoms are \((6/25,13/50,13/50,6/25)\), increasing from empty to singletons and decreasing from singletons to full. The new theorem covers the entire domain containing this point because it signs the paired correction (18), not either correction separately.

The point (35) is only a transparent obstruction to the older atom criterion. The example below has nonzero rank-one coupling at both endpoints, noncommuting physical endpoints, and a rank-three direction.

## 7. Exact rational four-site example

Use (34) and choose
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/3\\1/3&1/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u=\binom10,\qquad
\alpha=\frac16,\quad\beta=\frac13,\quad\ell=\frac12.
\tag{36}
\]
The guide is not an eigenvector of \(D\). The channel endpoints are
\[
Q_0=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&1/3\\1/3&1/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q_1=\begin{pmatrix}5/6&1/3\\1/3&1/3\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{37}
\]
Their eigenvalues are \(\{0,2/3\}\) and \(\{1/6,1\}\), respectively. Their atom channel, in order \(\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\), is
\[
C(\cdot\mid0)=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0),\qquad
C(\cdot\mid1)=(0,2/3,1/6,1/6).
\tag{38}
\]
Thus \(\tau_C=1/2\). It has a particularly simple refinement with no row splitting:

| Group | Original output atoms | Weight | Intercept \(r_g\) | Slope \(s_g\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | \(\varnothing,\{1\}\) | \(2/3\) | \(1/2\) | \(1/2\) |
| B | \(\{2\},\{1,2\}\) | \(1/3\) | \(0\) | \(1/2\) |

Here \(\mu_C=\tau_C^2=1/4\) and \(A_C(p)=0\). Formula (22) is exactly a two-term entropy mixture plus \(h(1/3)\).

Choose
\[
p_0=\frac13,\quad z_0=\frac{1+i}{10},\qquad
p_1=\frac23,\quad z_1=\frac{2-i}{12}.
\tag{39}
\]
The resulting physical matrices have only rational complex entries:
\[
K_0=\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/3&(1-i)/20&(1-i)/20\\
1/3&1/3&0&0\\
(1+i)/20&0&21/40&11/120\\
(1+i)/20&0&11/120&21/40
\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
K_1=\begin{pmatrix}
2/3&1/3&(2+i)/24&(2+i)/24\\
1/3&1/3&0&0\\
(2-i)/24&0&19/40&13/120\\
(2-i)/24&0&13/120&19/40
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{40}
\]
Their LMI determinant margins in (5) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & p(y-1/4)-|z|^2 &(1-p)(3/4-y)-|z|^2\\ \hline
0&23/225&31/450\\
1&3/16&1/48
\end{array}
\tag{41}
\]
and \(x_0=13/30,x_1=11/30\) are in the balanced range. These positive rational margins prove endpoint membership in \(\Omega\); convexity proves membership of the full segment. Strict feasibility of (40) follows from (31).

The direction has zero second row and column. On coordinates \(\{1,3,4\}\) its determinant is
\[
\det(K_1-K_0)_{\{1,3,4\}}=\frac{59}{36000}>0.
\tag{42}
\]
Its two pair-mode eigenvalues in the lower block are negative and its scalar Schur complement is positive, so the full direction has inertia \((1,2,1\text{ zero})\) and rank three. Both coherent reservoirs move, and all three active measured diagonal entries change. The cross coupling changes complex phase.

For the midpoint,
\[
\Delta p=\frac13,\quad
\Delta z=\frac{4-11i}{60},\quad
\operatorname{tr}V=\frac{257}{14400}
=\left|\frac{z_0+z_1}{2}\right|^2.
\tag{43}
\]
Using \(\mu_C=1/4\) in (27), the exact rational lower bound is
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{1/2}^{\rm phys}\ge
\frac1{72}+\frac{66049}{518400000}
=\frac{7266049}{518400000}>0.}
\tag{44}
\]
The full double-integral gap and the conditional Jensen terms can be retained as additional exact positive contributions.
