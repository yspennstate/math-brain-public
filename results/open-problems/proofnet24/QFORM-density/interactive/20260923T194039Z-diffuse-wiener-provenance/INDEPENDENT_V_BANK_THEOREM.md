# Independent square bank closes a rank-two leakage model

**Status:** proved scoped consequence of the archive's exact-rank-two half-potential theorem and unconditional two-eigenvalue half-potential theorem. The remaining case where the square direction shares original inputs with the leakage directions is not covered.

## Set-up and the convolution step

Let \(X_R=(H,X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) consist of independent real coordinates with densities at most one, and let \(V\) be independent of **all** of \(X_R\), with scalar density \(f_V\le C\). For real \(\lambda\ne0,\gamma,c\) and any real random variable \(R=R(X_R)\), put
\[
 Q=R+\lambda V^2+\gamma V+c.
\]
Completing the square gives \(s_0=c-\gamma^2/(4\lambda)\), and the change-of-variables formula yields
\[
 p_{\lambda V^2+\gamma V+c}(s)
 \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|\,|s-s_0|}}
\]
for almost every \(s\), with its density zero on the prohibited side of \(s_0\). Consequently, whenever the displayed half-potential is finite,
\[
 \boxed{\|p_Q\|_\infty
 \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}\,
       \sup_z\mathbb E |R-z|^{-1/2}.}                       \tag{1}
\]
This follows by convolution, using only the independence of the **two original-coordinate banks**. A unit linear projection \(V=v\cdot X_J\) of another independent cap-one bank has \(C\le\sqrt2\) by the scalar Rogozin–Ball projection bound. One may instead retain any known cap \(C\).

Affine terms in \(X_R\), including those generated by conditioning **other original coordinates**, are permitted below. The only term allowed from the \(V\)-bank beyond \(\lambda V^2\) is \(\gamma V+c\); an arbitrary affine functional of that bank need not be a function of \(V\).

## Exact-rank-two hub and leakage: arbitrary \(\beta,\varepsilon\)

Let \(L=\ell\cdot X\), \(P=p\cdot X\), with \(\|\ell\|=1\) and arbitrary \(p\) supported on the light input bank, disjoint from the scalar original coordinate \(H\). No orthogonality between \(\ell\) and \(p\) is needed. Consider
\[
 R=2HL+\beta L^2+2\varepsilon LP+b_R\cdot X_R+c_R,         \tag{2}
\]
where \(b_R\) is **arbitrary**. In physical-coordinate coefficient space put
\[
 u=(0,\ell),\qquad
 w=e_H+\tfrac{\beta}{2}u+\varepsilon(0,p).
\]
The quadratic part of (2) is \(2(u\cdot X_R)(w\cdot X_R)\), so its symmetric Hessian matrix (in the \(x^\mathsf T A x\) convention) is \(A_R=uw^\mathsf T+wu^\mathsf T\). It has rank exactly two. Since \(\|u\|=1\), its two nonzero eigenvalues are \(u\cdot w\pm\|w\|\), and the absolute product is
\[
 D=|\det\Lambda_R|
   =\|w\|^2-(u\cdot w)^2
   =1+\varepsilon^2\bigl(\|p\|^2-(\ell\cdot p)^2\bigr)
   \ge1.                                                     \tag{3}
\]
The hub \(e_H\perp u,p\) supplies the final \(1\); \(\beta\) cancels from \(D\). In the usual orthonormal \(P,L\) frame, \(D=1+\varepsilon^2\).

Theorem 6.1 in the archived [rank-two affine half-Riesz proof](https://github.com/yspennstate/math-open-problems/blob/master/proofnet24/QFORM-density/interactive/20260922T161801Z-rank-two-fusion/REPORT.md) applies to an exact-rank-two quadratic plus **any original-coordinate affine term**:
\[
 \sup_z\mathbb E|R-z|^{-1/2}\le128D^{-1/4}.                \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (3), and (4) proves, uniformly in \(\beta,\varepsilon,b_R,c_R,\gamma\),
\[
 \boxed{\|p_{\,2HL+\beta L^2+2\varepsilon LP+
                   b_R\cdot X_R+c_R+\lambda V^2+\gamma V}\|_\infty
       \le \frac{128C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}\,D^{1/4}}
       \le\frac{128C}{\sqrt{|\lambda|}}.}                   \tag{5}
\]
For \(\lambda=1\) and \(V\) a unit independent-bank projection, this is at most \(128\sqrt2\). If the full block-diagonal quadratic matrix \(A_R\oplus\lambda vv^\mathsf T\) has third singular value at least \(\sigma>0\), then \(|\lambda|\ge\sigma\) and both nonzero singular values of \(A_R\) are at least \(\sigma\), so \(D\ge\sigma^2\) and (5) is at most \(128C/\sigma\).

## Arbitrary rank in the remainder, including \(aH^2\)

The exact-rank-two formula above ceases to apply to
\[
 R_a=aH^2+2HL+\beta L^2+2\varepsilon LP+b_R\cdot X_R+c_R
\]
when \(a\varepsilon\ne0\): its physical hub/light matrix can have rank three. An unconditional, weaker estimate follows from the **second-level** results in the archived assembled manuscript: [lower_levels.tex](https://github.com/yspennstate/math-open-problems/blob/master/proofnet24/QFORM-density/interactive/20260923T164118Z-full-proof-paper/manuscript/lower_levels.tex) and [structure.tex](https://github.com/yspennstate/math-open-problems/blob/master/proofnet24/QFORM-density/interactive/20260923T164118Z-full-proof-paper/manuscript/structure.tex). Their statements, with \(c_*=10^{-15}\), are:

- If a real symmetric remainder matrix \(M\) has \(s_2(M)\ge1\) and **no** \(c_*\)-pivot, Theorem `lower:two` gives the truncated half-potential
  \(\mathcal H(R)\le10^6\), uniformly in every affine term.
- If \(s_2(M)\ge1\) and **has** a \(c_*\)-pivot, Lemma `pivot-half` gives \(\mathcal H(R)\le44c_*^{-1/2}\), again with arbitrary affine terms.

Both inputs are unconditional; in particular the pivot lemma uses the previously established one-eigenvalue logarithmic estimate. The manuscript's **later** displayed \(G_2\) inside its reduction to a hypothetical third-level bound should not be used here, since that displayed formula also offers a route through the unproved third-level hypothesis.

Let \(G_*= \max\{10^6,44\cdot10^{7.5}\}=44\sqrt{10^{15}}<1.392\cdot10^9\). Scaling a quadratic with \(s_2(M)\ge\sigma\) by \(\sigma^{-1}\), then bounding the contribution from \(|R-z|>1\) by \(1\), proves
\[
 \boxed{\sup_z\mathbb E|R-z|^{-1/2}
           \le \sigma^{-1/2}(G_*+1).}                       \tag{6}
\]
This remains valid for **arbitrary-rank** \(M\) and any affine perturbation.

For a general-rank remainder \(M\), assume **separately** that \(s_2(M)\ge\sigma\) and \(|\lambda|\ge\sigma\). The full block-diagonal matrix having \(s_3\ge\sigma\) alone is insufficient to force \(|\lambda|\ge\sigma\): \(M\) may supply all three large singular values while the square coefficient is arbitrarily small. Under the two stated quantitative assumptions, (1) and (6) give
\[
 \boxed{\|p_{R+\lambda V^2+\gamma V+c}\|_\infty
       \le \frac{C(G_*+1)}{\sigma}.}                         \tag{7}
\]
Thus the independent-square-bank receiver handles \(aH^2\), arbitrary extra rank in \(R\), and arbitrary affine terms in the \(R\)-bank. Use (5) when exact rank two is available; it is much sharper.

## A heavy physical coefficient permits arbitrary square-bank affine terms

Suppose \(V=v\cdot X_J\) for an independent original-coordinate bank and some \(j\in J\) has \(|v_j|\ge\delta>0\). Allow **any** affine term \(b_J\cdot X_J\) in addition to \(\lambda V^2\). Condition all \(X_{J\setminus\{j\}}\). The remaining function of \(X_j\) is
\(\lambda v_j^2 X_j^2+\eta X_j+\kappa\), and its conditional density is bounded by
\[
 p(s\mid X_{J\setminus\{j\}})
 \le\frac{1}{|v_j|\sqrt{|\lambda|}\sqrt{|s-s_0(X_{J\setminus\{j\}})|}}
\]
on the permitted side of its shifted vertex. The \(R\)-bank is independent of the entire \(V\)-bank, so its half-potential bound applies uniformly to every such random center. Consequently (1) holds with \(C\) replaced by \(\delta^{-1}\), and **no alignment requirement on \(b_J\)**. This costs a factor \(\delta^{-1}\) and does not control a completely diffuse \(V\)-bank as \(\delta\to0\).

## Conditional external-coordinate version

Let \(W\) be further independent original coordinates. Suppose conditioning \(W=w\) leaves a decomposition
\[
 Q\mid W=w=R_w(X_R)+\lambda V^2+\gamma(w)V+c(w),
\]
where the quadratic matrix of \(R_w\) does not depend on \(w\), while its affine terms may depend arbitrarily on \(w\). Then (5) or (7) holds conditionally with the **same constant** for every \(w\), and hence unconditionally after averaging, provided (7)'s two spectral inequalities hold for the conditional two-bank block \(M\oplus\lambda vv^\mathsf T\). This includes an independent square bank consisting of one physical coordinate \(V=X_j\), with arbitrary cross couplings to the conditioned \(W\) coordinates, provided the \(X_R\)--\(X_j\) cross block vanishes. Large singular values of the **unconditioned full matrix including \(W\)** do not by themselves imply those conditional block inequalities. It does not treat cross quadratic couplings directly between \(X_R\) and the \(V\)-bank.

The genuinely diffuse near-coloop obstruction has \(V,P,L\) all supported on the same light original coordinates. It therefore lies outside this independent-bank theorem, even though its formal three-dimensional spectral variables are orthogonal.
