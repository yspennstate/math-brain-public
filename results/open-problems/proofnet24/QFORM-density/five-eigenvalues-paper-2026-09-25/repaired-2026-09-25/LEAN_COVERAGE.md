Lemma 2.6 has a shorter proof than the paper's. Take the N = N_A(t) orthonormal eigenvectors Phi of A with eigenvalues of
modulus at least t. For a coefficient vector a with (A Phi a)_top = Bx + Cy = 0, where Phi a = (x, y), one has
x = -B^{-1} C y and A Phi a = (0, S y), so abs(S y) = abs(A Phi a) >= t abs(a) >= t abs(y), and y = 0 forces a = 0. The
vectors a satisfying these k conditions and R y = 0, for any R of rank less than N - k, still include a nonzero one, so
s_{N-k}(S) >= t; for symmetric S that is N_S(t) >= N - k. Only B invertible is used.

Lemma 2.7(a) is proved without Cauchy-Binet from Mathlib, which does not have it. The Leibniz formula gives the
principal-minor expansion det(diag(d) + M) = sum_T prod_{i not in T} d_i det(M_TT): a permutation contributes to the
T-term only if it fixes every point outside T, and those permutations are the permutations of T. Hence
det(1 + X K) = sum_T det(K_TT) X^{abs T} in R[X], while the spectral theorem gives det(1 + X G) = prod_i (1 + lambda_i X)
for symmetric G; comparing coefficients of X^m gives the first equality. For the second, Sylvester's identity
det(1 + X A A^T) = det(1 + X A^T A) (Mathlib's `det_one_add_mul_comm`) with A = M_T, compared at X^{abs T}, gives
Theta(T) = sum over abs(S) = abs(T) of det(M_{T,S}^T M_{T,S}), and each term is det(M_{T,S})^2 by reindexing.

The partition step of Lemma 5.4 is proved without operator norms. If S v = lambda v with abs(v) = 1 and abs(lambda) = L,
the off-diagonal part O has (O v)_i = (lambda - S_ii) v_i, so norm(O)_F^2 >= norm(O v)^2 >= (L - max abs(S_ii))^2 >= L^2/4.
For i != j exactly a quarter of the 2^n labellings put i in I and j in J (flipping the label of i or of j permutes the
four classes), so the labellings average norm(S_{I,J})_F^2 = norm(O)_F^2/4, and one of them reaches the average.
