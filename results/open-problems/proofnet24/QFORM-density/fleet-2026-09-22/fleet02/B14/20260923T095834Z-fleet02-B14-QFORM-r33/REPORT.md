## 3. Random-slope subprobability lemma

Let `X` be a real random variable with probability density at most one. Let `mu` be any finite positive measure on a bank space, of total mass `m in (0,1]`. Let measurable functions `S,R` on that bank define the output submeasure generated by

`Y = X S(V) + R(V)`,                                               (3.1)

with `X` independent of `V`. No independence whatsoever is required between `S(V)` and `R(V)`.

Assume only that the slope pushforward `S_# mu` has a density `g` satisfying

`0 <= g <= L`, `integral g = m`.                                  (3.2)

Set

`H=L/m`.                                                           (3.3)

### Theorem 3.1 — `QFORM-RANDOM-SLOPE-SUBPROBABILITY-v1`

For every center `t` and radius `rho>0`,

`nu_Y{|Y-t|<=rho} <= m F(4H rho)`,                                (3.4)

where

`F(q)=q(1+log(1/q))` for `0<q<=1`, and `F(q)=1` for `q>=1`.         (3.5)

Consequently, for every `A>0`,

`integral log_+(A/|t-y|) dnu_Y(y) <= m Omega_0(4HA)`,              (3.6)

uniformly in `t`, where

`Omega_0(y)= y[2+log(1/y)]`, `0<y<=1`,
`Omega_0(y)= log y+2`, `y>=1`.                                    (3.7)

### Proof

Condition on the bank variable. For a fixed slope `s`, the set of `x` for which

`|x s + R(v)-t|<=rho`

is an interval of length `2rho/|s|`; because the density of `X` is at most one, its probability is at most

`min{1,2rho/|s|}`.                                                 (3.8)

Therefore

`nu_Y{|Y-t|<=rho} <= integral min{1,2rho/|s|} g(s) ds`.            (3.9)

Normalize `h=g/m`, so `h<=H` and `integral h=1`. To avoid any hidden attainment assumption in a bathtub argument, use layer cake directly. Put `u=2rho` and

`k(s)=min{1,u/|s|}`.

For `0<v<1`, the set `{k>v}` is the interval `|s|<u/v`, of measure `2u/v`. Hence

`integral k(s)h(s)ds`
` = integral_0^1 integral_{k>v} h(s) ds dv`
` <= integral_0^1 min{1,2Hu/v} dv`.                               (3.10)

Writing `q=2Hu=4Hrho`, the last integral equals `q(1+log(1/q))` when `q<=1` and equals one when `q>=1`. This proves (3.4), with no appeal to compact support or existence of a bathtub maximizer.

For the logarithmic potential,

`integral log_+(A/|t-y|)dnu_Y(y)`
` = integral_0^infinity nu_Y{|Y-t|<Ae^{-s}} ds`.                  (3.11)

If `y=4HA<=1`, substitute (3.4) for all `s` and integrate

`y e^{-s}[1+log(1/y)+s]`,

giving `m y[2+log(1/y)]`. If `y>=1`, use the trivial mass bound `m` until `s=log y`, and after that write `s=log y+u`; the remaining integral is

`m integral_0^infinity e^{-u}(1+u)du = 2m`.

This proves (3.6)-(3.7). QED.

The crucial feature is that `R(V)` is arbitrary and may be maximally correlated with `S(V)`. Only the genuinely independent hub coordinate `X` is conditioned last.

## 5. Why this is genuinely stronger than a remainder-density bound

The random-slope lemma never asks `R(V)` to possess a density. It does not ask for a conditional density of `R` given the slope, and it does not ask `S` and `R` to be independent. The only conditional density estimate is the one-dimensional interval estimate for the original independent hub `X`.

This matters in the heavy-star/nonseparable Schur states: the same bank variables may generate both the hub slope and arbitrary dense quadratic curvature. A route through `sup_s ||p_{R|S=s}||_infinity` would reintroduce exactly the false conditional-fiber interfaces recorded in the correction inbox. Equation (3.8) avoids them.

Physical and conditioning-generated translations are also free. An affine hub term merely translates the slope `S`; an affine bank term is absorbed into `R`; and the estimates depend only on the slope density cap, not on its center.
