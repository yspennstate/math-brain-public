The theorem requires neither moments, identical laws, support restrictions, centering, symmetry, log-concavity, Gaussianity nor smoothness.

That one-dimensional theorem yields the scalar negative-half-moment estimate

\[
\sup_c \mathbb E|b^T Y-c|^{-1/2}
\le 2^{7/4}\|b\|_2^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed the scalar density cap is `sqrt(2)/||b||`; among probability densities capped by `K`, the decreasing kernel `|x|^{-1/2}` is maximized by filling a centered interval at height `K`, giving `2 sqrt(2K)`.

A standard auxiliary-Gaussian randomization upgrades (1) to

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\|MY+\alpha\|_2^{-1/2}
\le 2^{7/4}\|M\|_{HS}^{-1/2}.}
\tag{2}
\]
