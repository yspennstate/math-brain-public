## 3. Schur completion keeps a cap-one conditional square

Fix an original coordinate, relabel it `0`, and assume

`d=A_00 != 0`.

Let `K={1,...,n-1}` and let `a=A[K,0]`. Completing the square gives the exact identity

`Q = d V^2 + R`,

where

`V = X_0 + d^{-1} a^T X_K`,

`R = X_K^T H X_K`,

`H = A[K,K] - d^{-1} a a^T`.                         (3.1)

Although `V` and `R` need not be independent, the conditional density cap required by the sharp square-smoothing theorem survives.

Indeed, conditional on `X_K=x`, `V` is a translate of `X_0`, so its density is at most one. Conditional on the coarser sigma-field generated by `R`, the law of `V` is a mixture of those translates. A convex mixture of densities bounded by one is again bounded by one. Therefore

`||p_{V|R=r}||_infinity <= 1` for almost every `r`.     (3.2)

This is the dependence-safe interface that allows arbitrary couplings from the pivot coordinate to every residual coordinate.

## 4. Fixed Schur cross packet: an explicit coupled theorem

Choose a physical pair `I subset K`, `|I|=2`, and a disjoint physical pair `J_0 subset K\I`, `|J_0|=2`. Put

`C_0 = H[I,J_0]`.

Assume `det C_0 !=0`. Condition all residual coordinates outside `I union J_0`. The two coordinates in `I` retain their original independent cap-one densities, while the random linear coefficient created by `X_{J_0}` has a two-dimensional density bounded by

`1/(4 |det C_0|)`.

Applying the exact rank-two random-slope Plancherel identity therefore gives

`|phi_R(t)| <= min(1, pi |det C_0|^{-1/2} |t|^{-1}).`  (4.1)

Set `L=pi |det C_0|^{-1/2}`. Then

`int_R |phi_R(t)|^{3/2} dt`
` <= 2[int_0^L 1 dt + int_L^infinity (L/t)^{3/2} dt]`
` = 6L`.                                                (4.2)

With Fourier convention `p(x)=(2pi)^{-1}int e^{-itx}phi(t)dt`, Hausdorff--Young gives

`||p_R||_3 <= (2pi)^(-2/3) ||phi_R||_(3/2)`
`          <= 3^(2/3) |det C_0|^(-1/3).`               (4.3)

Combining (4.3) with (3.2) and the sharp dependent-square theorem yields

`boxed(||p_Q||_infinity`
` <= 2 sqrt(pi) 3^(1/4) |d|^(-1/2) |det C_0|^(-1/4))`. (4.4)

The numerical leading constant is

`2 sqrt(pi) 3^(1/4) = 4.665360904668642...`.            (4.5)

This theorem permits arbitrary self-curvature in every active block and arbitrary outside couplings. The scalar pivot may couple to the packet; those couplings are paid exactly by the Schur update in (3.1), rather than being set to zero.

## 5. Main result: aggregate all rank-two Schur cross minors before reciprocal payment

The fixed-packet theorem still pays a single determinant. The following version aggregates the whole residual bank.

Choose a physical pair `I subset K`, `|I|=2`, and set `J=K\I`. Let

`C = H[I,J]`

and assume `rank C=2`. Define its two-dimensional cross-volume

`s_I = sqrt(det(C C^T)) >0`.                            (5.1)

### Theorem 5.1 — `QFORM-SCHUR-RANK2-CROSSVOLUME-v1`

Under the cap-one product assumptions and `d=A_00!=0`, if (5.1) holds, then `Q` has an essentially bounded density and

`boxed(||p_Q||_infinity`
` <= 2 sqrt(pi) 3^(1/4) C_RV^(1/2)`
`    |d|^(-1/2) s_I^(-1/4)).`                          (5.2)

Equivalently, the assigned partner's exact signed Fejer averages obey

`boxed(sup_{T>0,y} D_T law(Q)(y)`
` <= 2 sqrt(pi) 3^(1/4) C_RV^(1/2)`
`    |d|^(-1/2) s_I^(-1/4)).`                          (5.3)

No continuous-density conclusion is claimed from this argument.

### Proof

Write the residual form in the coordinates `(x,y)=(X_I,X_J)` as

`R=x^T Bx + 2x^T Cy + y^T Dy`.

For fixed nonzero `t` define

`F_t(beta)=int_R2 exp(i t[x^T Bx+beta^T x]) prod_{i in I} f_i(x_i) dx`.

By the random-slope Plancherel identity,

`int_R2 |F_t(beta)|^2 d beta <= (2pi)^2 |t|^(-2).`      (5.4)

Factor `C=S U`, with `S=(CC^T)^(1/2)` and `U U^T=I_2`. The bounded-density projection theorem gives

`||p_{U X_J}||_infinity <= C_RV^2`.

Since `det S=s_I`,

`||p_{C X_J}||_infinity <= C_RV^2/s_I`,

and therefore the random slope `beta=2 C X_J` has density cap

`M_beta <= C_RV^2/(4 s_I).`                            (5.5)

The outside factor `exp(i t y^T Dy)` has modulus one. Cauchy--Schwarz followed by (5.4)-(5.5) gives

`|phi_R(t)|^2`
` <= E |F_t(2 C X_J)|^2`
` <= [C_RV^2/(4s_I)] (2pi)^2 |t|^(-2)`
` = pi^2 C_RV^2 s_I^(-1)|t|^(-2).`                    (5.6)

Thus

`|phi_R(t)| <= min(1, pi C_RV s_I^(-1/2)|t|^(-1)).`    (5.7)

Exactly as in (4.2),

`||phi_R||_(3/2)^(3/2) <= 6 pi C_RV s_I^(-1/2)`.

Hausdorff--Young yields

`||p_R||_3 <= (3 C_RV)^(2/3) s_I^(-1/3).`              (5.8)

Finally apply the sharp dependence-safe square theorem using (3.2):

`||p_Q||_infinity`
` <= [2sqrt(pi)/3^(1/4)] |d|^(-1/2)`
`    [(3C_RV)^(2/3)s_I^(-1/3)]^(3/4)`
` = 2sqrt(pi)3^(1/4) C_RV^(1/2)`
`   |d|^(-1/2)s_I^(-1/4),`

which is (5.2). Applying the completed account04/B01 exact Fejer equivalence gives (5.3) immediately. This is an actual use of the assigned partner theorem in the new inequality. QED.

### Cauchy--Binet interpretation

`s_I^2 = det(C C^T)`
