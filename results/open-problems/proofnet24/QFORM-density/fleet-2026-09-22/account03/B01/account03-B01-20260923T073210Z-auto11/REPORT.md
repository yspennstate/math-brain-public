## 1. Exact target and correction boundary

Target: for every `n`, independent real coordinates with densities at most one, and every real symmetric `A` with at least three eigenvalues of modulus at least one, prove a universal density bound for `X^T A X`, or give a valid counterexample.

Preserved correction: for any prescribed frequency `s>0` and any small `delta>0`, the set

`{x : dist(s x^2, 2 pi Z) <= delta}`

has infinite Lebesgue measure. Choose a finite union of intervals `E` inside it with `|E|=1`, and let `f=1_E`. Then

`Re int exp(i s x^2) f(x) dx >= cos(delta)`.

Hence no uniform positive power pointwise decay in `s` can hold over all cap-one densities. The theorem below instead uses a rank-two physical bilinear block to gain a `1/|t|` factor only inside a time integral, and closes the logarithmic endpoint using an independent scalar-square `L^3(dt)` estimate.

## 2. New theorem

### Theorem QFORM-RANK2-CROSS-PLUS-SQUARE-L3-v1

Partition the original independent coordinates into four disjoint banks `I,J,D,W`. Write `X_I,X_J,X_D,X_W` for those blocks. Let

* `K` be a real `|I| x |J|` matrix of **exact rank two**, with nonzero singular values `kappa_1 >= kappa_2 > 0`;
* `a in R^D`, `sigma=|a|_2>0`;
* `lambda != 0`;
* for each value `w` of `X_W`, let `F_w(X_I)` and `G_w(X_J)` be arbitrary real measurable functions, `h(w),c(w)` arbitrary real numbers.

Define

`Q = F_{X_W}(X_I) + G_{X_W}(X_J) + 2 X_I^T K X_J + lambda (a^T X_D + h(X_W))^2 + c(X_W)`.

Then `Q` has a continuous density in `C_0(R)` and

`||p_Q||_infinity <= C_* (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-3/8) (|lambda| sigma^2)^(-1/4)`,

where

`C_* = (16/3) 12^(1/4) = 9.926451830422394... < 9.927`.

Equivalently, setting `rho=|lambda| sigma^2`,

`||p_Q||_infinity < 9.927 (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-3/8) rho^(-1/4)`.

The functions `F_w,G_w` may contain arbitrary same-bank quadratic self-curvature and all affine terms generated by conditioning `W`. No moment, support, connectedness, log-concavity, Gaussianity, or post-rotation independence assumption is used.

The only structural restriction is that the scalar-square bank `D` is source-disjoint from the two active cross banks, and that after conditioning `W` no further term couples `D` to `I` or `J` except through the shift `h(w)`.

## 3. Rank-two physical-gradient estimate with arbitrary curvature on both banks

Fix `w`. Let a thin SVD be

`K = P diag(kappa_1,kappa_2) R^T`,

where `P` and `R` have orthonormal columns. Put

`U=P^T X_I in R^2`, `V=R^T X_J in R^2`.

Because `I` and `J` are disjoint original coordinate banks, `U` and `V` are independent. No independence of coordinates *inside* `U` or `V` is asserted.

The source-valid product-projection theorem gives

`||p_U||_infinity <= 2`, `||p_V||_infinity <= 2`.

For `t` real, define the complex fiber amplitudes

`A_{t,w}(u) = p_U(u) E[ exp(i t F_w(X_I)) | U=u ]`,

`B_{t,w}(v) = p_V(v) E[ exp(i t G_w(X_J)) | V=v ]`.

Then pointwise

`|A_{t,w}(u)| <= p_U(u)`, `|B_{t,w}(v)| <= p_V(v)`.

Therefore

`||A_{t,w}||_2^2 <= ||p_U||_infinity int p_U <= 2`,

and similarly `||B_{t,w}||_2 <= sqrt(2)`.

The conditional characteristic function of the two-bank core is

`psi_w(t)=int int A_{t,w}(u) B_{t,w}(v) exp(2 i t u^T D v) du dv`,

with `D=diag(kappa_1,kappa_2)`.

Use the nonunitary Fourier transform

`hat B(zeta)=int exp(i zeta dot v) B(v) dv`.

Then

`psi_w(t)=int A_{t,w}(u) hat B_{t,w}(2 t D u) du`.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, the change of variables `zeta=2 t D u`, and Plancherel in two dimensions,

`|psi_w(t)|`

`<= ||A_{t,w}||_2 [int |hat B_{t,w}(2tDu)|^2 du]^(1/2)`

`= ||A_{t,w}||_2 ( 1/(4 t^2 kappa_1 kappa_2) )^(1/2) ||hat B_{t,w}||_2`

`= [pi/(|t| sqrt(kappa_1 kappa_2))] ||A_{t,w}||_2 ||B_{t,w}||_2`

`<= 2 pi / (|t| sqrt(kappa_1 kappa_2))`.

Thus, uniformly in all same-bank self-curvature and all conditioned affine terms,

`|psi_w(t)| <= min(1, A/|t|)`,

where

`A = 2 pi (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-1/2)`.

This is the first key point: **simultaneous arbitrary curvature on both active cross banks costs nothing in this L2 argument**, because those phases have modulus one before the cross-bank Plancherel step.

## 4. Translation-uniform scalar-square L3 theorem

Let `Y` be any real random variable with a probability density bounded by `m`, and let `b` be arbitrary. Define

`g(t)=E exp(i t lambda (Y+b)^2)`.

Then

`||g||_{L^3(R)} <= 2 (2 pi)^(1/3) m^(2/3) |lambda|^(-1/3)`.

### Proof

Set `Z=m(Y+b)`. Its density `r` is bounded by one. Let `S=Z^2`, with density `q`. For `s>0`,

`q(s)=[r(sqrt(s))+r(-sqrt(s))]/(2 sqrt(s)) <= s^(-1/2)`.

Hence

`int q(s)^(3/2) ds <= int q(s) s^(-1/4) ds = E |Z|^(-1/2)`.

Among probability densities bounded by one, the radially decreasing kernel `|z|^(-1/2)` is maximized by filling the unit-length interval centered at zero. Therefore

`E |Z|^(-1/2) <= int_{-1/2}^{1/2} |z|^(-1/2) dz = 2 sqrt(2)`.

Consequently

`||q||_(3/2) <= (2 sqrt(2))^(2/3) = 2`.

Hausdorff-Young for the above nonunitary Fourier convention gives

`||hat q||_3 <= (2 pi)^(1/3) ||q||_(3/2) <= 2(2 pi)^(1/3)`.

Finally

`lambda(Y+b)^2 = (lambda/m^2) S`,

so a change of variables in frequency gives

`||g||_3 <= 2(2 pi)^(1/3) (m^2/|lambda|)^(1/3)`.

This estimate is uniform in the translation `b` and requires no pointwise decay in frequency.

## 5. Source-disjoint linear projection input

For `Y=a^T X_D`, the source-valid one-dimensional product-marginal theorem used by the designated partner gives

`||p_Y||_infinity <= sqrt(2)/sigma`,

where `sigma=|a|_2`.

Therefore, uniformly in `w`, for

`g_w(t)=E exp(i t lambda(a^T X_D+h(w))^2)`,

we have

`G := sup_w ||g_w||_3`

`<= 2(2 pi)^(1/3) (sqrt(2)/sigma)^(2/3) |lambda|^(-1/3)`.

## 6. Time-integrated closure

Given `w`, the `D` bank is independent of the `I,J` core, so

`phi_{Q|w}(t)=exp(i t c(w)) psi_w(t) g_w(t)`.

For any `T>0`,

`int_R |psi_w(t) g_w(t)| dt`

`<= 2T + 2 A int_T^infinity t^(-1)|g_w(t)| dt`.

Use Holder with exponents `3` and `3/2`:

`int_T^infinity t^(-1)|g_w(t)| dt`

`<= ||g_w||_3 (int_T^infinity t^(-3/2) dt)^(2/3)`

`= 2^(2/3) G T^(-1/3)`.

Thus

`int_R |psi_w g_w| <= 2T + B T^(-1/3)`,

where

`B = 2^(5/3) A G`.

The minimizing choice is

`T=(B/6)^(3/4)`,

and the minimum equals

`(4/3) 6^(1/4) B^(3/4)`.

Before inserting the one-dimensional projection cap, the scalar-square theorem gives

`B = 16 pi^(4/3) m^(2/3) |lambda|^(-1/3) (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-1/2)`.

Therefore

`int_R |phi_{Q|w}(t)| dt`

`<= (32/3) 6^(1/4) pi m^(1/2) |lambda|^(-1/4) (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-3/8)`.

Averaging over `W` and using triangle/Tonelli preserves this same uniform bound for `phi_Q`. Since `phi_Q in L^1(R)`, Fourier inversion yields a continuous `C_0` density and

`||p_Q||_infinity`

`<= (16/3) 6^(1/4) m^(1/2) |lambda|^(-1/4) (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-3/8)`.

Now `m <= sqrt(2)/sigma`, so

`m^(1/2)|lambda|^(-1/4)`

`<= 2^(1/4) sigma^(-1/2)|lambda|^(-1/4)`

`=2^(1/4)(|lambda|sigma^2)^(-1/4)`.

Thus

`||p_Q||_infinity`

`<= (16/3) 12^(1/4) (kappa_1 kappa_2)^(-3/8)(|lambda|sigma^2)^(-1/4)`.

Numerically the constant is `9.926451830422394...`.

## 9. What this pays and what remains

Paid analytic obstruction: the earlier determinant-area bilinear receiver controlled arbitrary curvature on only the conditioned side. The present theorem allows arbitrary self-curvature **simultaneously on both active rank-two cross banks**, provided there is one source-disjoint scalar-square innovation. It also avoids any reciprocal individual physical minor; the cross currency is the product of the two nonzero singular values.

Unpaid structural implication: the bare hypothesis of three large eigenvalues does not automatically expose such a disjoint `rank-two cross + square` packet with dimension-free lower bounds on

`(kappa_1 kappa_2)` and `rho=|lambda|sigma^2`.

The remaining routing problem is to prove that a still-unpaid N3 state either

1. exposes this packet with quantitative currencies;
2. falls into the existing grouped-square / diffuse-ridge / pair-log / hub / null-coloop branches; or
3. requires a receiver in which the third curvature direction overlaps one or both cross banks.

This report does **not** claim the full QFORM conjecture, and it does not claim a universal pointwise characteristic-function decay.
