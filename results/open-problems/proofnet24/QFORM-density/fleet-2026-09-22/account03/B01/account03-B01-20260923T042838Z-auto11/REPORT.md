### Proof

`(u,z)=O^T X_I`, with `u in R^2`, `z in R`.                        (4.3)

`0<=rho<=1`, `int rho=1`.                                         (4.4)

We do **not** claim independence of `u` and `z`.

Since `v spans ker(K^T)`, in these coordinates

`O^T K = [[M],[0]]`,                                              (4.5)

where `M` is `2 x |J|`, rank two, and its two nonzero singular values are exactly those of `K`. In particular

`sqrt(det(MM^T))=Delta`.                                          (4.6)

The center quadratic becomes

`u^T H u + 2z a^T u + s_w^T u + ell_w z + epsilon d z^2`,        (4.7)

where `epsilon in {+1,-1}` and `|epsilon d|=d`; the precise `H,a,s_w,ell_w` are irrelevant.

For fixed `t`, define the partial oscillatory integral

`h_(t,w)(u)
 = int rho(u,z)
   exp{it[u^T H u+2z a^T u+s_w^T u+ell_w z+epsilon d z^2]} dz`. (4.8)

`A_w(t)=||h_(t,w)||_(L^2(du))`.                                  (4.9)

For each fixed `u`, the slice `q_u(z)=rho(u,z)` is a subprobability density with cap one and mass

`m(u)=int rho(u,z)dz`.                                            (4.10)

`||h_(.,w)(u)||_(L^4(dt)) <= C4 m(u)^(1/2)d^(-1/4)`.             (4.11)

`||A_w||_4

because `int m=1`. This is exactly why the critical exponent `p=4` survives an arbitrary orientation of the cross-kernel despite the loss of independence between `u` and `z`.
