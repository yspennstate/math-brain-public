## 2. General two-sided subprobability shear estimate

Let `mu_3(dx)=g_3(x)dx` and `mu_4(dx)=g_4(x)dx` be finite positive subprobability measures with

`0<=g_j<=1`, `m_j=mu_j(R) in (0,1]`.

Let `X1,X2` remain independent probability variables with densities at most one. Fix nonzero `c,d`, real `a,b,beta,ell1,ell2,gamma`, and define the subprobability output measure generated by

`Q=2c X1 X2 +(d/2)(a X3+b X4-beta)^2 +ell1 X1+ell2 X2+gamma`.

Put

`M=m_3 m_4`,

`L=min( m_4/|a| , m_3/|b| )`,

with the convention that a quotient by zero is `+infinity`, and

`vartheta=(|c|/|d|) (L/M)^2`.

### Theorem 2.1 — QFORM-SHEAR-SUBPROB-HOMOGENEITY-v1

The output submeasure has an `L^infinity` density and

`boxed( ||p_Q^(sub)||_infinity <= M |c|^(-1) H(vartheta). )`     (2.1)

The bound is uniform in `beta,ell1,ell2,gamma` and in all support locations and fragment geometry.

### Proof

The pushforward `W=aX3+bX4-beta` is a subprobability measure of mass `M`. Its density is a convolution of scaled subdensities. Young's `L^1-L^infinity` inequality gives both bounds

`||p_W||_infinity <= m_4/|a|`,
`||p_W||_infinity <= m_3/|b|`,

hence

`||p_W||_infinity <= L`.                                          (2.2)

Normalize the output by `c` and reflect one physical pair variable if necessary, so the pair is `2X1X2`. Write

`R=(d/(2c)) W^2`.

For every center `t` and radius `rho>0`, the inverse image under `w -> (d/(2c))w^2` of `(t-rho,t+rho)` has total Lebesgue length at most

`4 sqrt(|c|/|d|) sqrt(rho)`.

Therefore (2.2) implies the **unnormalized** small-ball estimate

`nu_R{|R-t|<rho}`
` <= min{ M, 4 L sqrt(|c|/|d|) sqrt(rho) }`
` = M min{1,4 sqrt(vartheta) sqrt(rho)}.`                         (2.3)

Now use exactly the partner's physical-pair logarithmic envelope, but integrate it against the subprobability remainder measure `nu_R` instead of a probability law. Linearity gives, for every `r>0`,

`p_(Q/c)^(sub)(t)`
` <= 2 sqrt(2) M/r`
`    + int log_+(2r^2/|t-s|) dnu_R(s).`                           (2.4)

(The arbitrary pair-affine terms only move the logarithmic critical value; the partner proof is uniform in them.)

Layer cake and (2.3) yield

`int log_+(2r^2/|t-s|) dnu_R(s)`
` = int_0^infinity nu_R{|t-s|<2r^2 e^(-u)} du`
` <= M int_0^infinity min{1,4 sqrt(2) r sqrt(vartheta)e^(-u/2)} du`
` = 2M Psi(4 sqrt(2) r sqrt(vartheta)),`                           (2.5)

where `Psi(z)=z` for `0<=z<=1` and `Psi(z)=1+log z` for `z>=1`.

Combining (2.4)-(2.5),

`||p_(Q/c)^(sub)||_infinity`
` <= M [ 2 sqrt(2)/r +2 Psi(4 sqrt(2) r sqrt(vartheta)) ].`        (2.6)

The bracket is exactly the scalar function optimized in account04/B14 r7. Its infimum is `H(vartheta)`. Rescaling the output by `c` proves (2.1). QED.

The important point is that no normalized fragment density ever appears: the mass `M` stays outside the entire smoothing calculation.

## 3. Exact one-sided fragment localization

Assume now `|b|>=|a|`, let `mu_4` be a full probability law (`m_4=1`), and let `mu_3` be an arbitrary subprobability fragment of mass `m`.

`L <= m/|b|`,

`boxed(

### Theorem 3.1 — QFORM-ONE-SIDED-SHEAR-FRAGMENT-LOCALIZATION-v1

Let a cap-one probability density `f_3` be decomposed into **any finite or countable sum of positive subdensities**

`f_3 = sum_j g_j`, `m_j=int g_j`, `sum_j m_j=1`,

with no restriction on the locations, diameters, number, separation or moments of the pieces. Leave the dominant-coefficient coordinate `X4` unfragmented. Then the corresponding output law decomposes as a positive sum of output submeasures `eta_j`, and each satisfies

`||d eta_j/dt||_infinity <= m_j |c|^(-1) H(theta_b).`              (3.2)

Hence, by monotone convergence of the positive measures,

`boxed(
 ||p_Q||_infinity <= |c|^(-1) H(theta_b)

This is a genuine before-normalization localization statement. It remains valid for fragments that are arbitrarily remote or unbounded and for countably many pieces. ## 5. Why two-sided fragment-first localization is different

The same calculation also isolates a real proof-order obstruction. If **both** shear coordinates are first split independently into subdensities of masses `m,n`, Theorem 2.1 contains

`vartheta=(|c|/|d|)[L/(mn)]^2`,
`L=min(n/|a|,m/|b|)`.

Even for the symmetric target-valid control `a=b=c=d=1`, splitting each coordinate into `N` equal-mass pieces gives, on every cell,

`M=N^(-2)`, `L=N^(-1)`, `vartheta=N^2`.

Therefore the **cellwise analytic majorant** is

`N^(-2) H(N^2)`,

and summing the `N^2` cells yields

`H(N^2)=4+2log(8N)`,                                               (5.1)

which grows like `2log N` although the unsplit partner theorem gives `H(1)=4+2log8`.

This is **not** a lower bound on the true density and is not a counterexample to QFORM. It proves only that independent two-sided fragment-first summation of this particular smoother is not a zero-loss localization mechanism. The safe localization order is asymmetric: keep at least the dominant shear coordinate whole, aggregate its `L^infinity` smoothing effect, and only then fragment the other coordinate or the outside environment.

This directly parallels the programme's broader aggregate-before-inversion rule: normalize/split only after a whole-bank cap has been extracted.
