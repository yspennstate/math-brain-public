Let C=[c1 c2] be a real 3 by 2 matrix of rank two and B=B^T a real 3 by 3 matrix. Put u=c1 cross c2, Delta=det(C^T C)=||u||^2, and v=u/sqrt(Delta), so v spans ker(C^T). For h=1,2,3 define d_h=det[c1 c2 B e_h]. Then d=B u, hence sum_h d_h^2=Delta ||Bv||^2. Also v^T d=sqrt(Delta) v^T Bv, so Delta(v^T Bv)^2=(v^T d)^2.

Therefore the total shared bordered-star mass controls ||Bv||, but the quadratic coefficient in the one-dimensional crossing kernel controls only its component parallel to v. The exact missing factor is alpha=|v^T d|/||d||. There is no universal positive lower bound on alpha.

This is a supporting structural identity, not a density counterexample and not a fixed-multiplicity-seven theorem.
