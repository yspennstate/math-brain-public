# Addendum: a precise submass criterion for upgrading paid height to rough C0 regularity

Status: `QFORM-SUBMASS-C0-COMPILER-v1 PROVED`.

This is a regularity theorem, not a universal QFORM height theorem.

## 1. Interval-height notation

For a finite positive measure `nu` on `R`, define

\[
H(\nu):=\sup_{I:\,|I|>0}\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|},
\]

where `I` ranges over bounded intervals. If `H(nu)<infinity`, then `nu<=H(nu) dx` and its density has essential supremum at most `H(nu)`.

Fix a measurable output map `T:R^n->R`; for QFORM take `T(x)=x^TAx`. Fix a class of cap-one product probability inputs. For coordinate `j`, replace its probability density by a cap-one subdensity `h` of mass

\[
m=\int h\in[0,1],
\]

while leaving all other coordinate factors as cap-one probability densities. Denote the resulting positive output submeasure by `nu_{j,h}`.

## 2. The compiler

### Theorem — `QFORM-SUBMASS-C0-COMPILER-v1`

Assume there is a function

\[
\Psi:[0,1]\to[0,\infty),
\qquad
\Psi(m)\to0\quad(m\downarrow0),
\]

such that for every coordinate `j`, every admissible choice of the other probability marginals, and every cap-one subdensity `h` of mass `m`,

\[
\boxed{H(\nu_{j,h})\le\Psi(m).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Assume also that smooth compact cap-one product inputs for this fixed output map have `C_0(R)` output densities. Then **every rough cap-one product input has a canonical `C_0(R)` output density**.

More quantitatively, for any two cap-one product probability families `f=(f_j)` and `g=(g_j)`, whenever

\[
\delta_j:=\|f_j-g_j\|_1,
\]

their output densities satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\|p_f-p_g\|_\infty
\le
2\sum_{j=1}^n \Psi(\delta_j/2).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

If, separately, a probability-output height bound `||p||_infinity<=C` has already been paid on the smooth core, then the rough `C_0` representative inherits the **same** height `C`; continuity costs no additional height.

### Proof

Use the original-coordinate product telescoping identity

\[
\prod_i f_i-\prod_i g_i
=
\sum_{j=1}^n
(f_j-g_j)
\prod_{i<j}f_i
\prod_{i>j}g_i.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Write

\[
f_j-g_j=h_j^+-h_j^-.
\]

Because `f_j` and `g_j` are probability densities,

\[
\int h_j^+=\int h_j^-={\delta_j\over2},
\tag{2.4}
\]

and since both inputs are cap one,

\[
0\le h_j^\pm\le1.
\]

By (2.1), the positive output submeasures generated by `h_j^+` and `h_j^-` each have density height at most `Psi(delta_j/2)`. Therefore the signed density of the `j`-th telescoping contribution has `L^infinity` norm at most

\[
2\Psi(\delta_j/2).
\]

Summing over `j` proves (2.2).

Now fix an arbitrary rough product input `f`. Choose the cap-preserving smooth compact approximants `g^{(k)}` from Lemma 3.1 of the main report so that every marginal `L1` error tends to zero. For fixed finite `n`, (2.2) gives

\[
\|p_{g^{(k)}}-p_f\|_\infty\to0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The smooth outputs lie in `C_0(R)` by the smooth-core rank-at-least-three theorem in the QFORM target. A uniform limit of `C_0` functions lies in `C_0`; hence `p_f in C_0(R)`.

Finally, if `||p_{g^{(k)}}||_infinity<=C` was paid separately, uniform convergence gives `||p_f||_infinity<=C`. Thus the submass hypothesis is a **continuity compiler**, not the source of the central probability-height constant.

\[
