## 1. Pair phase-space budget recalled

For an iid pair `X,X'` with density `0<=f<=1`, put

`D=X-X'`, `S=X+X'`,

and define the unnormalised conditional Fourier factor

\[
F(d,u)=\int f(x)f(x-d)e^{iu(2x-d)}\,dx.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This packages the full dependence of `(D,S)` rather than pretending that `S|D=d` has a uniform density cap. Plancherel gives the exact Moyal identity

\[
\boxed{\iint_{\mathbb R^2}|F(d,u)|^2\,dd\,du
=\pi\left(\int f^2\right)^2\le\pi.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

For a real quadratic form `Q(x)=x^T A x+ell^T x+c` with symmetric `A`, independent-copy variables satisfy

\[
Q(X)-Q(X')=D^TAS+\ell^TD.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus every use below retains each conjugate sum/difference pair jointly inside its own `F_i`.

## 2. First essential check: every symmetric 3-by-3 phase-space BL datum is singular

The previous B03 run introduced maps

\[
L_i^A d=(d_i,(Ad)_i),\qquad i=1,2,3,
\tag{2.1}
\]

with Brascamp--Lieb exponents `p_i=1/2`. A tempting route was to seek a uniformly finite BL constant on a three-coordinate physical block. This cannot work: in fact the BL constant is infinite for **every** real symmetric `3x3` matrix.

### Proposition 2.1 — `QFORM-SYMMETRIC-3D-PHASESPACE-BL-INFINITE-v1`

For every real symmetric `A in R^{3x3}`,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{BL}((L_i^A)_{i=1}^3;(1/2,1/2,1/2))=+\infty.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Postcompose each target map by the determinant-one shear

\[
(r,s)\mapsto(r,s-A_{ii}r).
\]

BL finiteness and its constant are unchanged, so it suffices to take zero diagonal,

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
0&a&b\\
a&0&c\\
b&c&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

If some off-diagonal row is zero, the corresponding `L_i` has rank one, while the other two maps have rank at most two. Taking `V=R^3` in the BL dimension condition gives

\[
3=\dim V>\frac12(1+2+2),
\]

so the datum is not finite.

Assume now all three maps have rank two. Their kernel lines are generated by

\[
k_1=(0,-b,a),\qquad
k_2=(c,0,-a),\qquad
k_3=(-c,b,0).
\tag{2.4}
\]

They satisfy the exact identity

\[
\boxed{k_1+k_2+k_3=0.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let `V=span{k_1,k_2,k_3}`. If `dim V=1`, every `L_i` vanishes on `V`, hence

\[
1=\dim V>\frac12\sum_i\dim L_iV=0.
\]

If `dim V=2`, each `ker L_i` is a line contained in `V`, so `dim L_iV<=1`. Therefore

\[
2=\dim V>\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3\dim L_iV\le\frac32.
\]

Again the BL dimension condition fails. This proves (2.2). `square`

**Interpretation.** The phase-space Moyal/BL theorem from r35 remains correct, but it cannot possibly turn a physical three-coordinate symmetric block into the desired `L^1_t` estimate. The first new mechanism must therefore aggregate coordinates differently. This is a structural obstruction, not a counterexample to QFORM.

## 3. A dependence-safe exact bipartite receiver

The obstruction above suggests pairing two physical banks before invoking any geometric inequality.

Let `k>=1`. Let

\[
X=(X_1,\ldots,X_k),\qquad Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_k)
\]

consist of `2k` independent scalar variables with densities bounded by one. Consider

\[
R=X^TCX+2X^TBY+Y^TDY+\ell_X^TX+\ell_Y^TY+c,
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `C,D` are arbitrary real symmetric `k x k` matrices and `B` is an invertible real `k x k` matrix. No sign assumption is made.

### Theorem 3.1 — `QFORM-BIPARTITE-MOYAL-CS-v1`

For every `t!=0`,

\[
\boxed{
|\phi_R(t)|^2
\le
\pi^k |\det B|^{-1}|t|^{-k}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
|\phi_R(t)|
\le
\min\{1,\pi^{k/2}|\det B|^{-1/2}|t|^{-k/2}\}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The estimate is uniform in `C,D,ell_X,ell_Y,c`.

### Proof

Take an independent copy and write `(d_X,s_X)` and `(d_Y,s_Y)` for the coordinatewise difference/sum vectors. The exact difference identity is

\[
R-R'
=d_X^TCs_X+d_X^TBs_Y+s_X^TBd_Y+d_Y^TDs_Y
+\ell_X^Td_X+\ell_Y^Td_Y.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Let `F_i^X,F_j^Y` be the pair factors (1.1). The affine terms contribute only a unimodular factor. Hence

\[
|\phi_R(t)|^2
\le
\int_{\mathbb R^{2k}}
\prod_{i=1}^k
\left|F_i^X\!\left(d_{X,i},t[(Cd_X)_i+(Bd_Y)_i]\right)\right|
\prod_{j=1}^k
\left|F_j^Y\!\left(d_{Y,j},t[(B^Td_X)_j+(Dd_Y)_j]\right)\right|
\,dd_Xdd_Y.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Define `A_t` to be the product of the squares of the `X`-factors and `H_t` the corresponding product of the squares of the `Y`-factors. Cauchy--Schwarz in the entire `(d_X,d_Y)` space gives

\[
|\phi_R(t)|^2
\le
\left(\int A_t\right)^{1/2}
\left(\int H_t\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

For fixed `d_X`, make the genuine change of variables

\[
u=tBd_Y,
\qquad dd_Y=|t|^{-k}|\det B|^{-1}du.
\]

The shift `u -> u+tCd_X` does not change Lebesgue measure, and the product separates coordinatewise in the `u_i`. Therefore

\[
\int A_t\,dd_Xdd_Y
=|t|^{-k}|\det B|^{-1}
\prod_{i=1}^k\iint |F_i^X(d,u)|^2\,dd\,du
\le |t|^{-k}|\det B|^{-1}\pi^k.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Likewise, for fixed `d_Y`, use `v=tB^Td_X`; the arbitrary `Dd_Y` term is only a translation in `v`. Thus

\[
\int H_t\,dd_Xdd_Y
\le |t|^{-k}|\det B|^{-1}\pi^k.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Substituting (3.7)--(3.8) into (3.6) proves (3.2). No sum/difference pair was split or conditionally re-capped. `square`
