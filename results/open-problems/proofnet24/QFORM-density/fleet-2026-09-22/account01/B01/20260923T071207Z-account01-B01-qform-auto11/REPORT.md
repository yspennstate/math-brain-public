## 3. New scalar lemma: a quadratic kernel is uniformly weak-L2 away from the double root

### Lemma 3.1 — `QFORM-SCALAR-QUADRATIC-WEAKL2-v1`

Let f be any probability density on R satisfying `0<=f<=1`. For every real h, every nonzero d, and every nonzero r,

`boxed:
 int f(x) | d(x-h)^2+r |^(-1/2) dx
 <= 2^(7/4) |d|^(-1/4)|r|^(-1/4).`                               (3.1)

No support, moment, connectedness or regularity assumption is imposed on f.

### Proof

After translating x and factoring out |d|, it is enough to bound

`I_q(f)=int f(x)|x^2-q|^(-1/2)dx`, `q!=0`.                       (3.2)

Put `k_q(x)=|x^2-q|^(-1/2)` and let

`m_q(epsilon)=|{x: |x^2-q|<epsilon}|`.

If `q>0`, then for `0<epsilon<=q`,

`m_q(epsilon)=2[sqrt(q+epsilon)-sqrt(q-epsilon)]`,

so

`m_q(epsilon)/epsilon
 =4/[sqrt(q+epsilon)+sqrt(q-epsilon)]
 <=2sqrt(2) q^(-1/2).`                                           (3.3)

For `epsilon>=q`,

`m_q(epsilon)=2sqrt(q+epsilon)`,

and `m_q(epsilon)/epsilon` decreases from the same endpoint value `2sqrt(2)q^(-1/2)`.

If `q<0`, write `q=-rho`, `rho>0`. Then `m_q(epsilon)=0` for `epsilon<=rho`, while for `epsilon>rho`,

`m_q(epsilon)=2sqrt(epsilon-rho)`.

The ratio `2sqrt(epsilon-rho)/epsilon` is maximized at `epsilon=2rho` and equals `rho^(-1/2)`. Thus in both signs

`sup_(t>0) t |{k_q>t}|^(1/2)
 <=2^(3/4)|q|^(-1/4).`                                           (3.4)

Call the right side K. Layer cake and the two constraints on f give

`int f k_q
 =int_0^infinity int_{k_q>t} f(x)dx dt
 <=int_0^infinity min(1, |{k_q>t}|)dt
 <=int_0^K 1 dt + int_K^infinity K^2 t^(-2)dt
 =2K.`                                                            (3.5)

Therefore `I_q(f)<=2^(7/4)|q|^(-1/4)`. Restoring d and r gives (3.1). QED.

The omitted case r=0 is exactly the double-root singularity: the kernel becomes proportional to `|x-h|^(-1)`, which need not be integrable. In the application below the bad equality occurs only on a zero set of a nonzero quadratic polynomial and therefore has probability zero.
