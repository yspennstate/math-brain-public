## 2. New theorem: cross-singular-tail reserve

### Theorem (QFORM-CROSS-SINGULAR-TAIL-RESERVE-v1)

Let `A_0,E` be real symmetric `n x n` matrices and set

`A=A_0+E`.

Let `I,J` be disjoint original-coordinate sets and put

`M_0=A_0[I,J]`, `D=E[I,J]`.

Write the singular values in nonincreasing order. For any integer `q>=0` such that `q+5<=min(|I|,|J|)`, define

`eta_q = s_{q+1}(D)`

and

`b_k(q) = ( s_{q+k}(M_0) - eta_q )_+`,  `k=1,...,5`.

If `b_5(q)>0`, then the law of `Q=X^TAX` has a **continuous** density and

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 5 sqrt(2e) [ prod_{k=1}^5 b_k(q) ]^(-1/5).`   (2.1)

Equivalently, define the deterministic reserve functional

`G_5(M_0,D) = max_q [ prod_{k=1}^5 (s_{q+k}(M_0)-s_{q+1}(D))_+ ]^(1/5)`,

the maximum taken over admissible `q`. Whenever `G_5>0`,

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 5 sqrt(2e) / G_5(M_0,D).`   (2.2)

The first `q` cross singular directions of the added matrix are completely **free in amplitude**. Only the `(q+1)`-st singular value of the added physical cross block is charged, at the price of consuming `q` extra cross directions from the baseline.

### Proof

The key point is the standard singular-value Weyl inequality, here used in the direction that is useful for hostile perturbations. For matrices of the same shape,

`s_{i+j-1}(X+Y) <= s_i(X)+s_j(Y)`.

Apply this with

`X=M_0+D`, `Y=-D`, `i=k`, `j=q+1`.

Then

`s_{q+k}(M_0) <= s_k(M_0+D)+s_{q+1}(D)`

and therefore

`s_k(A[I,J]) = s_k(M_0+D) >= s_{q+k}(M_0)-eta_q >= b_k(q)`   (2.3)

for `k=1,...,5`.

Now

`Z_5(A[I,J]) = e_5((A[I,J])^T A[I,J])`.

If `sigma_1>=sigma_2>=...` are the singular values of the actual final cross block, then one summand in the elementary symmetric polynomial is the product of the five largest squared singular values, hence

`Z_5(A[I,J]) >= prod_{k=1}^5 sigma_k^2 >= prod_{k=1}^5 b_k(q)^2`.   (2.4)

Applying the completed five-volume theorem to the **actual final matrix A** gives

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 5 sqrt(2e) Z_5(A[I,J])^(-1/10)`

`<= 5 sqrt(2e) [prod_{k=1}^5 b_k(q)]^(-1/5)`.

The five-volume proof is Fourier-integrable, so the density is continuous. This proves (2.1). Optimizing over `q` proves (2.2). QED.

## 4. Global spectral corollary: arbitrary large and small added eigenvalues

Because `E` is symmetric, let its eigenvalues be ordered by absolute value

`|theta_1| >= |theta_2| >= ... >= |theta_n|`.

Left and right multiplication by coordinate projections are contractions, so singular values satisfy

`s_{q+1}(E[I,J]) <= s_{q+1}(E)=|theta_{q+1}|`.   (4.1)

Therefore the stronger but easier-to-check sufficient quantities

`c_k(q)=(s_{q+k}(M_0)-|theta_{q+1}|)_+`

give

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 5 sqrt(2e) [prod_{k=1}^5 c_k(q)]^(-1/5)`   (4.2)

whenever `c_5(q)>0`.

Interpretation: the first `q` added eigenvalues may have **arbitrarily large magnitudes, arbitrary signs, and arbitrary eigenvectors**. All remaining added eigenvalues, also of arbitrary signs and multiplicity, are paid only through the largest remaining magnitude `|theta_{q+1}|`. No rotation of the random variables occurs.

A flexible variant chooses any subset `L` of spectral modes to be treated as free. Put

`R_L=sum_{j in L} theta_j v_j v_j^T`, `T_L=E-R_L`.

Then one may use the local cross rank

`q_L=rank(R_L[I,J]) <= min(rank((v_j)_I:j in L), rank((v_j)_J:j in L), |L|)`

and the tail norm `||T_L||op=max_{j notin L}|theta_j|` to obtain

`s_k(A[I,J]) >= s_{q_L+k}(M_0)-||T_L||op`.   (4.3)

Thus even **many** arbitrarily large eigenvalue changes are free when their physical cross footprint has low rank.

## 5. Explicit target-class family

Fix any integer `q>=0`, let `m=q+5`, and set on `R^{2m}`

`A_0 = [[0, 2 I_m],[2 I_m,0]]`.

Use the first `m` coordinates as `I` and the last `m` as `J`. Then

`M_0=2 I_m`, so `s_j(M_0)=2` for every `j<=m`.

Now add **any** real symmetric matrix `E` satisfying

`s_{q+1}(E) <= 1/4`.

Equivalently, `E` may have `q` eigenvalues of completely arbitrary magnitude and sign, with arbitrary eigenvectors, while every further eigenvalue lies in `[-1/4,1/4]`.

By (4.1), `eta_q<=1/4`, and therefore

`b_1=...=b_5 >= 2-1/4 = 7/4`.

Hence

`||p_{X^T(A_0+E)X}||_infinity <= 5 sqrt(2e)/(7/4)`

`= 20 sqrt(2e)/7 = 6.661839947420355...`.   (5.1)

This is uniform in `q`, in the magnitudes/signs/orientations of the first `q` eigenvalues of `E`, and in the number and signs of all remaining small eigenvalues.

These matrices remain inside the exact target class. Indeed split `E=R+T` by spectral truncation with `rank R<=q`, `||T||op<=1/4`. Since all `2m` singular values of `A_0` equal `2`, the same rank-reserve inequality gives at least `2m-q=q+10` singular values of `A_0+R` at least `2`; Weyl for `T` leaves at least `q+10` singular values of `A_0+E` at least `7/4>1`. In particular at least three eigenvalues have modulus at least one.

## 6. Why this is different from the previous footprint theorem

The earlier B12 footprint bound charged a possibly high-rank perturbation through a localized operator norm such as `||E[I,J]||op` or a spectral-footprint surrogate. The present theorem gives a complementary **rank-vs-amplitude tradeoff**:

- the first `q` singular directions of `E[I,J]` are not charged at all, no matter how huge;
- only the residual cross tail `s_{q+1}(E[I,J])` is charged;
- the price is that the baseline physical cross block must carry `q+5` robust singular directions rather than five.

This is strictly stronger when `E[I,J]` has a few enormous singular values and a small tail.

## 7. What remains open

This does **not** prove the full three-large-eigenvalue conjecture. The bare condition `s_3(A)>=1` does not force any physical cross block with `q+5` reserve singular directions for a useful `q`; known corrections already rule out a universal rank-three cross-cut reduction. The remaining structural task is to show that failure of every reserve/volume certificate forces one of the other paid exits (localized physical core, hub/grouped-bank, ridge, scalar-Schur, or aggregate product-preserving branch).

The theorem does remove a real additional-eigenvalue obstruction once such a cross-volume certificate exists: extra eigenvalues need not be discarded by a false convolution argument; they can be absorbed deterministically through their physical cross singular tail.
