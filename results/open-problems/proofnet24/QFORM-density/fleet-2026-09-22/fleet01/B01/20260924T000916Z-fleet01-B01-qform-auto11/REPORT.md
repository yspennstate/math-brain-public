## 2. A one-square subcritical potential bound with arbitrary adapted shift

For a real random variable `Y`, write

`H_beta(Y)=sup_s E |Y-s|^(-beta)`.

### Theorem 2.1 — `QFORM-ONE-SQUARE-SUBCRITICAL-POTENTIAL-v1`

Let `X` be an original scalar coordinate, independent of a sigma-field `G`, with density bounded by `M`. Let `m,T` be arbitrary `G`-measurable real variables and let `c!=0`. For every `0<beta<1/2`,

`boxed:
 H_beta(c(X+m)^2+T)
 <= (8 M^2/|c|)^beta /(1-2 beta).`                              (2.1)

The estimate is uniform in both the shift `m` and the additive term `T`.

### Proof

Fix the conditioning on `G`, a center `s`, and `epsilon>0`. Put

`delta=epsilon/|c|`,
`r=(s-T)/c`

(with the harmless sign change absorbed into `r`). The relevant set of values of `y=X+m` is

`E(r,delta)={y: |y^2-r|<delta}`.

Its Lebesgue length is always at most

`|E(r,delta)| <= 2 sqrt(2 delta)`.                              (2.2)

Indeed, if `r<=-delta`, it is empty. If `-delta<r<delta`, it is contained in
`[-sqrt(r+delta),sqrt(r+delta)]`, whose length is at most `2sqrt(2delta)`. If `r>=delta`, it consists of the two symmetric intervals between `sqrt(r-delta)` and `sqrt(r+delta)`, with total length

`2[sqrt(r+delta)-sqrt(r-delta)] <=2sqrt(2delta)`,

because the last expression is maximal at `r=delta`.

Therefore, conditionally and hence unconditionally,

`P(|c(X+m)^2+T-s|<epsilon)
 <= min{1,K sqrt(epsilon)},`

where

`K=2 sqrt(2) M |c|^(-1/2)`.                                    (2.3)

The layer-cake identity for a negative power gives

`E|Y-s|^(-beta)
 = beta int_0^infinity epsilon^(-beta-1)
       P(|Y-s|<epsilon) d epsilon.`                             (2.4)

Split (2.4) at `epsilon=K^(-2)`. The two elementary integrals are

`beta K int_0^(K^-2) epsilon^(-beta-1/2)d epsilon`

and

`beta int_(K^-2)^infinity epsilon^(-beta-1)d epsilon`.

Their sum is

`K^(2beta)/(1-2beta)
 = (8M^2/|c|)^beta/(1-2beta)`.                                  (2.5)

Taking the supremum in `s` proves (2.1). QED.

This theorem is deliberately subcritical. At `beta=1/2` the integral in (2.4) is logarithmically critical, exactly matching the rank-two/one-square obstructions already present in the source corrections.
