## 2. Three-dimensional spectral coordinates and an exact inverse

Put

`beta=b^T z`,

and define the unit vector

`q=(b-beta z)/sqrt(E)`.

Then `q perpendicular z` and

`b=beta z+sqrt(E) q`.

On the three-dimensional range, in the orthonormal basis `(hub,z,q)`, the quadratic matrix is

```
C = [ a      beta     sqrt(E) ]
    [ beta   lambda   0       ]
    [ sqrt(E) 0       0       ].
```

Hence

`det C = -lambda E`,

and direct multiplication gives the exact inverse

```
C^{-1} = [ 0            0                 E^{-1/2} ]
         [ 0            lambda^{-1}       -beta/(sqrt(E) lambda) ]
         [ E^{-1/2}     -beta/(sqrt(E)lambda)
           (beta^2-a lambda)/(E lambda) ].
```

After scaling the matrix by `sigma^{-1}`, we may prove the theorem under `sigma=1`. Then `||C^{-1}||_op<=1`, so entrywise

`E>=1`, `|lambda|>=1`,

and

`|beta^2-a lambda| <= E |lambda|`,

hence

`beta^2 <= |lambda|(E+|a|)`.                                     (2.1)

Also `|lambda|E=|det C|>=1`. All currency estimates below are homogeneous of degree four in the matrix, so scaling back restores a factor `sigma^{-1}` in the final density bound.

## 3. The natural two-frame law

For `i<j`, define

`theta_ij=b_i z_j-b_j z_i`.

Since the `beta z` component cancels,

`theta_ij=sqrt(E)(q_i z_j-q_j z_i)`.

Cauchy--Binet gives

`sum_{i<j} theta_ij^2 = E`.

Thus

`p_ij=theta_ij^2/E`

is a probability law on physical leaf pairs. Its vertex marginals are

`ell_i=sum_{j != i} p_ij = z_i^2+q_i^2`,                         (3.1)

and `sum_i ell_i=2`.

The earlier B01 overlap-one theorem gives, for two independent pairs `P,Q` sampled from `p`,

`W_1 = lambda^2 E^2 P(P cap Q = empty)`                           (3.2)

and, whenever `max_i ell_i<=1/2`,

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 3*2^(4/3) W_1^(-1/6)`,                     (3.3)

with an `L^1` characteristic function, hence a continuous `C_0` density in this branch. That theorem keeps arbitrary hub self-curvature, affine leakage and outside conditioning.

## 4. New diffuse matching lemma

### Lemma

Let `p_ij` be any probability law on two-element subsets and let

`ell_i=sum_{j!=i}p_ij <=1/2`.

Then for independent `P,Q~p`,

`P(P cap Q=empty) >= 1/100`.                                     (4.1)

### Proof

The exact inclusion--exclusion identity is

`D:=P(P cap Q=empty)=1-sum_i ell_i^2+sum_{i<j}p_ij^2`.

Because `sum_i ell_i=2`,

`A:=1-sum_i ell_i^2=sum_i ell_i(1/2-ell_i)`.

If `A>=1/100`, then `D>=1/100`.

Assume instead `A<1/100`. Let

`H={i: ell_i>=1/4}`.

For `i notin H`,

`ell_i(1/2-ell_i)>=ell_i/4`,

so

`sum_{i notin H} ell_i < 1/25`.

Therefore the probability that a random edge touches a vertex outside `H` is at most `1/25`, by the union bound. Hence more than `24/25` of the edge mass lies entirely in `H`.

Since every vertex in `H` has marginal at least `1/4` and the marginals sum to two, `|H|<=8`; therefore there are at most `28` edges internal to `H`. Cauchy--Schwarz yields

`sum_{i<j} p_ij^2 >= (24/25)^2/28 > 1/100`.

Thus again `D>=1/100`. QED.

Combining (3.2), (4.1), and `|lambda|E>=1` in the normalized `sigma=1` setting gives

`W_1 >= lambda^2 E^2/100 >=1/100`,

and therefore

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 3*2^(4/3)*100^(1/6) <16.287`.              (4.2)

So the entire diffuse-leverage regime is paid at the spectral scale without choosing an individual physical `3x3` minor.

## 5. Two exact half-Riesz compilers

Suppose now some row has `ell_i>1/2`.

### 5.1 Hub completion

If `a!=0`, completing only the ORIGINAL hub square leaves the leaf residual matrix

Arbitrary affine terms are retained by the rank-two theorem.

### 5.2 One leaf completion

Fix `i` with `z_i!=0` and complete only the ORIGINAL leaf square with coefficient `lambda z_i^2`. On the other leaves put

`w_i=b_{-i}-(b_i/z_i)z_{-i}`.

All remaining leaf quadratic terms cancel exactly, and the residual quadratic form is

`-b_i^2/(lambda z_i^2) X_0^2 + 2 X_0 w_i^T Y_{-i}`.

Its two nonzero eigenvalues have product `-||w_i||_2^2`, while

`||w_i||_2^2

`||p_Q||_infinity

This estimate remains valid when the hub scalar `a` is zero or arbitrarily small.

## 6. Exact pair Schur identity

`B_i=[[a,b_i],[b_i,lambda z_i^2]]`,
