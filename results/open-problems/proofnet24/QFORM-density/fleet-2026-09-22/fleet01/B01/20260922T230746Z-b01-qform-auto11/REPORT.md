## 1. Scope and corrections

The full target remains open: for every real symmetric `A` having at least three eigenvalues of absolute value at least one, and every vector of independent real coordinates whose probability densities are bounded by one, prove a universal bound for the density of `X^T A X`, or give a target-valid counterexample. Continuity is a separate desired conclusion.

This run preserves the live correction that no law-uniform pointwise estimate

`|E exp(i s X^2)| <= C |s|^(-1/2)`

can hold for all one-dimensional densities bounded by one. Indeed, for any fixed `s>0` and small `delta>0`, choose a finite union `E` of intervals, of total length one, inside

`{x : dist(s x^2, 2 pi Z) <= delta}`.

Then `f=1_E` is an admissible probability density and

`Re E exp(i s X^2) >= cos(delta)`.

Thus the replacement below is explicitly integrated/averaged in frequency and, in the main application, aggregates whole coordinate banks before any reciprocal coefficient is paid.

The bounded-density projection input used below is the sharp product-marginal theorem of Livshyts--Paouris--Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures*, Israel J. Math. 216 (2016), 877--889, Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:1507.07949): for a product probability density with one-dimensional factors bounded by one, every `k`-dimensional orthogonal marginal has density at most `2^(k/2)`. In particular, for every unit vector `u`, `u^T X` has density at most `sqrt(2)`. Scaling gives

`||p_(c^T X)||_infinity <= sqrt(2)/||c||_2`                                      (1.1)

for every nonzero coefficient vector `c` supported on original independent coordinates.

No rotation is used to assert independence: independence below comes only from disjoint original-coordinate blocks.

## 2. A translation-uniform scalar L^3 Fourier gate

Let `Y` have probability density `f` with `||f||_infinity<=M`, let `a!=0`, and let `b` be arbitrary. Put

`Z=a(Y+b)^2`.

After reflection if `a<0`, its density `h` obeys for `z>0`

`h(z) <= M / sqrt(|a| z)`.                                                       (2.1)

Set `c=M/sqrt(|a|)`. Since `int h=1`, for every `lambda>0`,

`|{h>lambda}| <= min(lambda^(-1), c^2 lambda^(-2))`.                            (2.2)

Layer cake at exponent `3/2` gives

`int h^(3/2)
 <= (3/2)[int_0^(c^2) lambda^(-1/2) d lambda
          + c^2 int_(c^2)^infinity lambda^(-3/2) d lambda]
 = 6c`.                                                                          (2.3)

With Fourier convention `hat h(t)=int e^(itx) h(x) dx`, Hausdorff--Young by interpolation between `L^1 -> L^infinity` and Plancherel gives

`||hat h||_3 <= (2 pi)^(1/3) ||h||_(3/2)`.

Therefore, uniformly in the translation `b`,

`boxed: int_R |E exp(i t a(Y+b)^2)|^3 dt <= 72 pi M^2/|a|.`                    (2.4)

This is a genuine integrated-frequency replacement for the false pointwise Fresnel estimate. Fragmented phase-aligned supports may create a large value at a selected frequency, but their total cubic Fourier budget is still uniformly finite.

## 3. Three disjoint grouped squares

### Theorem 3.1 — grouped-square smoothing

Let `B_1,B_2,B_3` be pairwise disjoint sets of original independent coordinates. For `j=1,2,3`, let `c_j` be a nonzero vector supported on `B_j` and put

`Y_j=c_j^T X_(B_j)`, `s_j=||c_j||_2`.

Let `Z` denote all original coordinates outside `B_1 union B_2 union B_3`. Suppose the full quadratic form admits the exact representation

`Q = sum_(j=1)^3 a_j (Y_j + beta_j(Z))^2 + R(Z)`,                              (3.1)

where every `a_j!=0` and `beta_j,R` are arbitrary measurable functions generated by the remaining quadratic/affine structure. Then `Q` has a continuous density. Moreover, if `r,s` are any two indices for which `a_r,a_s` have the same sign,

`boxed: ||p_Q||_infinity <= 2 pi /(s_r s_s sqrt(|a_r a_s|)).`                  (3.2)

Since two of three nonzero real `a_j` always share a sign, (3.2) always supplies a bound.

The characteristic function also satisfies the explicit integrated estimate

`boxed: int_R |phi_Q(t)| dt
 <= 72 pi * 2 * (s_1 s_2 s_3)^(-2/3) |a_1 a_2 a_3|^(-1/3),`                  (3.3)

that is,

`int |phi_Q| <= 144 pi (s_1 s_2 s_3)^(-2/3)|a_1 a_2 a_3|^(-1/3)`.             (3.4)

Consequently Fourier inversion alone gives

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 72 (s_1 s_2 s_3)^(-2/3)|a_1 a_2 a_3|^(-1/3)`.            (3.5)

#### Proof

By (1.1), each `Y_j` has density cap

`M_j <= sqrt(2)/s_j`.                                                           (3.6)

The three variables are independent because their supports are disjoint original-coordinate blocks; they are also independent of `Z`.

Condition on `Z=z`. The shifts `beta_j(z)` and `R(z)` are deterministic. For a same-sign pair `r,s`, (2.1) gives their two square-density envelopes

`M_r / sqrt(|a_r|u)`, `M_s / sqrt(|a_s|v)`.

Their convolution is bounded by the beta integral

`(M_r M_s/sqrt(|a_r a_s|)) int_0^x [u(x-u)]^(-1/2) du
 = pi M_r M_s/sqrt(|a_r a_s|).`                                                (3.7)

Convolving with the third conditional square law, and translating by `R(z)`, cannot increase `L^infinity`. Thus the conditional density is bounded uniformly in `z` by the right side of (3.7). Averaging in `z` and using (3.6) proves (3.2).

For the Fourier statement, condition again on `Z=z`. The conditional characteristic function is, up to the unimodular factor `exp(itR(z))`, the product of the three shifted-square characteristic functions. By (2.4) and Holder with exponents `(3,3,3)`,

`int |phi_(Q|Z=z)(t)| dt
 <= product_j (72 pi M_j^2/|a_j|)^(1/3)
 =72 pi (M_1 M_2 M_3)^(2/3)|a_1a_2a_3|^(-1/3).`                               (3.8)

Using (3.6) turns the factor `(M_1M_2M_3)^(2/3)` into at most

`2 (s_1s_2s_3)^(-2/3)`,

which proves (3.3)--(3.4). Finally

`|phi_Q(t)| <= E_Z |phi_(Q|Z)(t)|`,

so Tonelli transfers the same `L^1_t` bound to `phi_Q`. Fourier inversion gives a bounded continuous density vanishing at infinity, proving the continuity assertion and (3.5). QED.

### Interpretation

The result spends each entire coefficient vector through its Euclidean norm `s_j` *before* any coordinate is selected. It is therefore stable on diffuse banks for which every individual physical coefficient tends to zero.
