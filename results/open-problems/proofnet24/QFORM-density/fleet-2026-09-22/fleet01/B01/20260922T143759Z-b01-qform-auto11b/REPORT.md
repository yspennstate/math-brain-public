# QFORM B01 — aggregated rank-two random-slope smoothing with a transverse quadratic gate

Status: `ESSENTIAL_STEP_APPLIED / SUPPORTING_THEOREM / FULL_TARGET_OPEN`.

## 1. Exact target and corrections retained

The programme target is unchanged: for independent real `X_i` with densities `0<=f_i<=1` and real symmetric `A` having at least three eigenvalues with absolute value at least one, prove a universal bound for the density of `Q=X^T A X`, or give a valid counterexample. No moment, compact-support, log-concavity, spectral-coordinate independence, or diagonal-basis assumptions are used below.

The phase-aligned fragmented-support construction remains a decisive correction: bounded input density alone does **not** imply any law-uniform pointwise `|t|^{-1/2}` decay for a scalar quadratic phase. The present argument instead combines (i) a sharp bounded-density theorem for a two-dimensional **linear projection** of the untouched product law, (ii) Plancherel in the corresponding random slope, and (iii) the completed averaged scalar `L^3` quadratic-gate theorem of fleet02/B01. Thus no rejected pointwise Fresnel estimate is used.

## 2. Partner result actually applied

The completed fleet02/B01 scalar theorem (run `20260922T120355Z-fleet02-B01-qform-r19`, report commit `fedbec07e3e8b2f9fcd2b1a65b0bb2a0137c212f`) states that if `0<=h<=1` has mass `m` and

`F(t)=int exp(i t (a w^2+bw)) h(w) dw`, `a!=0`,

then uniformly in the linear tilt `b`,

`int_R |F(t)|^3 dt <= 16*pi*m/|a|`.                                      (2.1)

I apply (2.1) below to every physical three-coordinate slice after an orthogonal change of variables **inside that three-coordinate integration block only**. This is an actual use of the partner theorem, not merely a citation.

A second completed partner theorem, fleet02/B01 r21 (report commit `949dd0672776cb2f4e100fe917faaee1644b80fc`), proves the same `2+1` smoothing mechanism when the randomizing bank consists of exactly two original variables. The new result below removes that two-column restriction and replaces a single `2x2` determinant by an aggregate over an arbitrary coordinate bank.

## 3. External linear-projection input

Livshyts--Paouris--Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures*, arXiv:1507.07949 / Israel J. Math. 216 (2016), prove that if a product density on `R^m` has coordinate densities bounded by one, then every `k`-dimensional orthogonal marginal has density bounded by `2^(k/2)`. For `k=2`, the cap is therefore exactly `2`.

Consequently, if `M:R^m->E` has rank two, with nonzero singular values `s_1,s_2` and `Delta=s_1 s_2`, then the density of `M Y` with respect to Lebesgue measure on `E` is at most `2/Delta`; the density of `2 M Y` is at most `1/(2 Delta)`.

## 4. New theorem: arbitrary-width rank-two bank plus one transverse curvature direction

Let `I` be any set of three original coordinates and put `K=I^c`. Set

`B=A[I,I]`,   `M=A[I,K]`.

Assume `rank(M)=2`. Let `nu` be a unit vector spanning `ker(M^T)` and define

`a = nu^T B nu`,
`Delta = product of the two nonzero singular values of M`.

Assume `a!=0`. Then `Q=X^T A X` has a bounded **continuous** density vanishing at infinity. More precisely, with

`Theta_I = |a| Delta^2`,

one has the dimension-free bound

`||p_Q||_infinity <= C_bank Theta_I^(-1/5)`,                           (4.1)

where

`C_bank = 5 * 2^(-2/5) * 3^(3/5) = 7.325390128958803...`.              (4.2)

Thus the relevant `2+1` gate is controlled by one aggregate fifth-root invariant rather than by the largest individual physical minor.

## 5. Proof

Choose an orthogonal basis of `R^I` in which `nu` is the third basis vector. Write the three physical variables as

`x = P u + nu w`,   `u in R^2`, `w in R`,

where the columns of `P` span `E=range(M)=nu^perp`. Orthogonal change of variables preserves Lebesgue measure. The transformed joint density

`G(u,w)=prod_{i in I} f_i((P u+nu w)_i)`

satisfies `0<=G<=1` and `int G=1`; no independence after this rotation is asserted or needed.

Write the remaining original-coordinate vector as `Y=X_K`. Since `nu^T M=0`, the random linear slope generated by `Y` lies entirely in the `u` variables. After discarding the unit-modulus phase depending only on `Y`, the characteristic function is bounded by an average of

`Psi_t(z)=int exp(i t [u^T H u + 2 c^T u w + a w^2 + 2 u^T z]) G(u,w) du dw`,

at the random point `z=M Y` (constant affine shifts can be retained and do not change any estimate).

For fixed `u`, define

`F_u(t)=int exp(i t [a w^2+b_u w]) G(u,w) dw`,

where `b_u` is the induced real linear coefficient. Let

`H_0(t) = (int_R2 |F_u(t)|^2 du)^(1/2)`.

### 5.1 Random-slope Plancherel estimate

The density of `2 M Y` on `E` is at most `1/(2 Delta)` by the sharp two-dimensional marginal theorem. Cauchy--Schwarz against that probability density and two-dimensional Plancherel give, for every `t!=0`,

`|phi_Q(t)| <= sqrt(2)*pi*Delta^(-1/2)*|t|^(-1)*H_0(t)`.              (5.1)

Indeed, Plancherel with Fourier convention `hat g(xi)=int exp(i xi.u)g(u)du` gives

`int_E |Psi_t(z)|^2 dz = (2*pi)^2 |t|^(-2) int |F_u(t)|^2 du`,

and the square root of the slope-density cap `1/(2 Delta)` supplies the remaining factor.

### 5.2 An `L^4_t` budget for the residual slice amplitude

For each `u`, let `m(u)=int G(u,w)dw`. Then `int m(u)du=1` and `|F_u(t)|<=m(u)`. Applying the partner scalar theorem (2.1) to the non-normalized slice gives

`int_R |F_u(t)|^3 dt <= 16*pi*m(u)/|a|`.                              (5.2)

At each fixed `t`, interpolation between `L^1(du)` and `L^3(du)` yields

`||F_.(t)||_2 <= ||F_.(t)||_1^(1/4) ||F_.(t)||_3^(3/4)`.

Since `||F_.(t)||_1 <= int m=1`, raising to the fourth power gives

`H_0(t)^4 <= int |F_u(t)|^3 du`.

Integrating in `t` and using (5.2),

`int_R H_0(t)^4 dt <= 16*pi/|a|`.                                    (5.3)

This is the key new averaging step: unlike the r21 logarithmic slice estimate, it requires no pointwise upper bound on the line-slice mass `m(u)`.

### 5.3 Tail integration and optimization

For `T>0`, use `|phi_Q(t)|<=1` on `|t|<=T`. On the tail, combine (5.1), Holder with exponents `4/3` and `4`, and (5.3):

`int_|t|>T |phi_Q(t)| dt`
` <= 2*sqrt(2)*pi*Delta^(-1/2) * (int_T^infty t^(-4/3)dt)^(3/4) * (16*pi/|a|)^(1/4)`
` = 4*sqrt(2)*3^(3/4)*pi^(5/4)*Delta^(-1/2)*|a|^(-1/4)*T^(-1/4)`.     (5.4)

Hence

`||phi_Q||_1 <= 2T + B T^(-1/4)`,

with

`B=4*sqrt(2)*3^(3/4)*pi^(5/4)*Delta^(-1/2)*|a|^(-1/4)`.

The minimum occurs at `T=(B/8)^(4/5)`, where the two terms have ratio `2T : B T^(-1/4) = 1:4`. Therefore

`||phi_Q||_1 <= 10 (B/8)^(4/5)`.

Fourier inversion gives

`||p_Q||_infinity <= (2*pi)^(-1)||phi_Q||_1`
` <= 5*2^(-2/5)*3^(3/5) * Delta^(-2/5)*|a|^(-1/5)`
` = C_bank * (|a|Delta^2)^(-1/5)`.

Because `phi_Q in L^1`, the inverse Fourier transform is a bounded continuous density vanishing at infinity. This proves (4.1).

## 6. Exact structural identities for the aggregate gate

Put

`S_I = M M^T = A[I,I^c] A[I,I^c]^T`.

Since `rank(S_I)=2`, its two positive eigenvalues are `s_1^2,s_2^2`, so

`det_+(S_I)=Delta^2`.

Moreover,

`adj(S_I)=Delta^2 nu nu^T`,

and therefore

`Theta_I = |a|Delta^2 = |tr(adj(S_I) B)|`.                            (6.1)

Thus the gate is basis-free inside the chosen physical triple. Cauchy--Binet gives the fully aggregated minor identity

`Delta^2 = sum_{R subset I, |R|=2} sum_{C subset I^c, |C|=2} det(A[R,C])^2`.  (6.2)

Equations (6.1)-(6.2) show that the proof pays for **all** two-column minors attached to the triple before taking a reciprocal power; it does not select a single largest `2x2` minor.

## 7. Comparison with the partner r21 theorem

Replacing the exact two-variable change-of-variables step in fleet02/B01 r21 by the sharp two-dimensional marginal theorem immediately gives the arbitrary-width extension

`||p_Q||_infinity <= 1/pi + 4*sqrt(2)*pi^(1/3)*Delta^(-1/2)*max(3^(1/4),|a|^(-1/4)).`  (7.1)

The new bound (4.1) is complementary. It is weaker in some numerical regimes, but it has the structurally cleaner dependence

`(|a| Delta^2)^(-1/5) = Theta_I^(-1/5)`,

which is the quantity that can plausibly be targeted by a spectral-to-coordinate aggregation argument.

## 8. Mandatory bank-family control and remaining gap

The known stress-test family

`A_m = [1] direct_sum J_m/m direct_sum (-J_m/m)`

has spectrum `{1,1,-1}`. Choose `I` to consist of the singleton coordinate, one coordinate from the positive bank, and one from the negative bank. Then

`a=1`,
`Delta=(m-1)/m^2`,
`Theta_I=(m-1)^2/m^4 ~ m^(-2)`.

Therefore (4.1) grows like `m^(2/5)` and does **not** close this family uniformly. If `I` omits the singleton and contains two coordinates from one rank-one bank, then the transverse curvature `a` vanishes. This confirms that the theorem is a real supporting bridge but not the missing full structural theorem.

A compact conditional end-to-end route is now:

1. the already supplied diffuse/full-spectrum theorem closes the branch where the entire range projection has all coordinate leverages `<=1/32`;
2. the theorem above closes any heavy branch with a physical triple satisfying `rank S_I=2` and `Theta_I>=theta_0>0`;
3. what remains is to show that every matrix with three eigenvalues `|lambda|>=1` outside the diffuse branch either has such a triple, **or** admits a second aggregate mechanism that captures rank-one coordinate banks such as `A_m` without paying for one triple.

The third item is still unpaid. Full QFORM remains OPEN.
