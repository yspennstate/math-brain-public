## 3. The standard scalar-pivot coarea certificate has a logarithmic singularity

Complete the square in the literal original coordinate `H`:

`Q_{eps,sigma}
 = sigma eps (H + b/(sigma eps) V)^2 + R_{eps,sigma}`,               (3.1)

where

`R_{eps,sigma}=W^2 - b^2/(sigma eps) V^2`.

Set

`kappa=b^2/eps=(1+eps)/eps`.                                        (3.2)

For a translated cap-one scalar `H`, the pushforward of `sigma eps(H+s)^2` has the standard one-sided density majorant

`p_{sigma eps(H+s)^2}(z)
 <= eps^(-1/2)|z|^(-1/2) 1_{sigma z>0}`.                            (3.3)

Thus the usual physical-pivot peel at output `y=0` produces the certificate

`B_{eps,+}
 := eps^(-1/2) E[(kappa V^2-W^2)_+^(-1/2)]`,                        (3.4)

and

`B_{eps,-}
 := eps^(-1/2) E[(W^2+kappa V^2)^(-1/2)]`.                          (3.5)

These are upper-bound *certificates* generated by the method, not assertions about the true value of `p_Q(0)`. The point is that the certificates themselves diverge.

### Theorem 3.1 — `QFORM-SCALAR-PIVOT-HALFRIESZ-LOG-BARRIER-v1`

For all `0<eps<=1/16`,

`B_{eps,+} >= (1/sqrt(1+eps)) log((1+eps)/eps)`,                    (3.6)

and

`B_{eps,-} >= (1/sqrt(2(1+eps))) log((1+eps)/eps)`.                 (3.7)

In particular both are at least a universal positive constant times `log(1/eps)`.

### Proof for the positive pivot

By symmetry of `V,W`,

`I_+ := E[(kappa V^2-W^2)_+^(-1/2)]`

`=4 int_0^(1/2) int_0^(min(1/2,sqrt(kappa)v))
       (kappa v^2-w^2)^(-1/2) dw dv`.                               (3.8)

For `v>=1/(2sqrt(kappa))`, the inner integration reaches `w=1/2` and equals

`arcsin(1/(2sqrt(kappa)v))`.

Using `arcsin x>=x` for `0<=x<=1`,

`I_+
 >=4 int_{1/(2sqrt(kappa))}^{1/2}
       [1/(2sqrt(kappa)v)] dv
 =(1/sqrt(kappa)) log kappa`.                                      (3.9)

Multiplying by `eps^(-1/2)` and using `eps kappa=1+eps` proves (3.6).

### Proof for the negative pivot

Again by symmetry,

`I_- := E[(W^2+kappa V^2)^(-1/2)]`

`=4 int_0^(1/2) int_0^(1/2)
       (w^2+kappa v^2)^(-1/2) dw dv`.                               (3.10)

For `v>=1/(2sqrt(kappa))` and `0<=w<=1/2`, one has `w<=sqrt(kappa)v`, hence

`sqrt(w^2+kappa v^2)<=sqrt(2kappa)v`.

Therefore

`I_-
 >=4 int_{1/(2sqrt(kappa))}^{1/2}
       [1/(2sqrt(2kappa)v)] dv
 =(1/sqrt(2kappa)) log kappa`.                                     (3.11)

Multiplication by `eps^(-1/2)` proves (3.7). QED.
