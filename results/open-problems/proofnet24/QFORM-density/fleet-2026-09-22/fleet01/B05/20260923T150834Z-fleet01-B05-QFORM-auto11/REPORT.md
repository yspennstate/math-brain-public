## 2. Exact Schur anti-parabolic identity

### Lemma 2.1 — `QFORM-NEARNULL-SCHUR-ANTI-PARABOLIC-v1`

Let `M` be a real symmetric invertible `3x3` matrix and let `e` be a unit vector. Write `M` in the deterministic orthogonal decomposition `R e direct_sum e^perp` as

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix} d & g^T\\ g & C\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad d=e^TMe\ne0,
\]

where `g in R^2` and `C=C^T in R^{2x2}`. Define the Schur complement

\[
 S=C-d^{-1}gg^T.
\]

Then for every scalar `s` and `y in R^2`,

\[
\boxed{
 d s^2+2s g^T y+y^TCy
 =d\bigl(s+d^{-1}g^Ty\bigr)^2+y^TSy,
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\det M=d\det S.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently, if all three eigenvalues of `M` have modulus at least one, then

\[
\boxed{|\det S|\ge |d|^{-1}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

In particular the transverse Schur curvature becomes *stronger*, not weaker, as a nonzero pivot `d` tends to zero.

#### Proof

Expanding the square in (2.1) gives

`d s^2+2s g^T y+d^{-1}(g^Ty)^2+y^T(C-d^{-1}gg^T)y`,

and the two rank-one terms cancel. Equation (2.2) is the scalar-pivot Schur determinant identity. If the eigenvalues satisfy `|lambda_k(M)|>=1`, then `|det M|>=1`; divide (2.2) by `|d|`. QED.

## 3. Square-root slices alone do not close a quadratic hub

### Theorem 3.1 — `QFORM-QUADRATIC-HUB-SQRT-SLICE-ONLY-FALSE-v1`

\[

\boxed{\mu_l(E)\le 2|E|^{1/2}}
\tag{3.1}

Nevertheless

\[

\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{\|p_{Q_0}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{ess}}=\infty.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

More precisely, writing

\[

\qquad Q_0=S^2-Z^2,

\tag{3.4}

\begin{pmatrix}
 a&1&0\\

\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof of the uniform slice estimate

Condition on `L=l`. Translation by `a^{-1}l^2` does not affect interval/measure size, so the slice is the pushforward of Lebesgue measure on `[-1/2,1/2]` by `z -> -z^2`. The density of `z^2` on `(0,1/4)` is `t^{-1/2}`. Therefore, for any measurable set of Lebesgue measure `delta`, decreasing rearrangement gives

\[
\mu_l(E)\le\int_0^{\delta}t^{-1/2}dt=2\sqrt\delta.
\]

This proves (3.1), including fragmented sets, not just intervals.

#### Proof of logarithmic divergence

The two summands in

`S=sqrt(a)X+U/sqrt(a)`

are centred uniforms of widths `sqrt(a)` and `1/sqrt(a)`. Put

\[
m_a=\min(\sqrt a,a^{-1/2})>0,
\qquad r_a=m_a/4\le1/4.
\]

Their convolution density `g_S` is the overlap length of the two supporting intervals divided by the product of their widths (which is one). At zero the overlap length is `m_a`, and shifting by `s` can reduce overlap by at most `|s|`. Hence

\[
g_S(s)\ge m_a/2\qquad (|s|\le r_a).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Now set

\[
P=S-Z,\qquad R=S+Z.
\]

The map `(p,r)->(s,z)=((p+r)/2,(r-p)/2)` has Jacobian `1/2`. Thus the joint density `h` of `(P,R)` satisfies

\[
h(p,r)={1\over2}g_S\!\left({p+r\over2}\right)
 1_{\{|r-p|\le1\}}.
\]

If `|p|,|r|<=r_a`, then `|(p+r)/2|<=r_a` and `|(r-p)/2|<=r_a<=1/4`, so by (3.6)

\[
 h(p,r)\ge m_a/4.
\tag{3.7}
\]

But `Q_0=S^2-Z^2=PR`. For `0<|q|<r_a^2/4`, the product-density formula and the interval

\[
{2|q|\over r_a}\le p\le {r_a\over2}
\]

give `|q/p|<=r_a/2`, hence both coordinates lie in the square covered by (3.7). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{Q_0}(q)
&=\int_{\mathbb R} h(p,q/p){dp\over|p|}\\
&\ge {m_a\over4}
   \int_{2|q|/r_a}^{r_a/2}{dp\over p}\\
&={m_a\over4}\log{r_a^2\over4|q|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

This proves essential-supremum divergence. Since `{PR=0}` has zero two-dimensional Lebesgue measure, there is no atom at zero. QED.
