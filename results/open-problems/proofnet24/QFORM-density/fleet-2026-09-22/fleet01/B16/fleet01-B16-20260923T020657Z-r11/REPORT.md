## 3. Main theorem: scalar-plus-two-dimensional product factor

### Theorem 3.1 — `QFORM-SCALAR-PAIR-FACTOR-BRIDGE-v1`

Partition the independent original coordinates into three disjoint sets

`{0} union L union O`.

Let `U` be an `|L| x 2` matrix with orthonormal columns and put

`Y=U^T X_L`.

Assume that, after conditioning on `X_O=z`, the whole remaining dependence of the quadratic form on `(X_0,X_L)` factors through `(X_0,Y)` as

`Q_z = [X_0;Y]^T B [X_0;Y]
       + eta_z^T [X_0;Y] + c_z`,                                  (3.1)

where `B` is a fixed real symmetric `3x3` matrix. The functions `eta_z,c_z` may be arbitrary measurable functions of the conditioned outside coordinates.

Assume every eigenvalue of `B` has modulus at least `s>0`. Then the full unconditional law has an essentially bounded density and

`boxed:
 ||p_Q||_infinity
 <= C_0/s,
 C_0=16 sqrt(30) 2^(1/4)
     =104.21688998922089... .`                                     (3.2)

No sign restriction is imposed on `B`. No moments, bounded supports, identical laws, or independence of the coordinates of `Y` are assumed. All outside quadratic terms and couplings are allowed provided the factorization (3.1) holds after conditioning. Continuity is not asserted by this theorem.

### Proof

Fix an outside value `z`; all affine coefficients below are therefore deterministic. Write

`B=[[a,b^T],[b,C]]`,                                                (3.3)

and

`D=|det B|`.

The spectral assumption gives

`D>=s^3`.                                                           (3.4)

The scalar `X_0` remains genuinely independent of the complete pair `Y`, because the conditioning used only the disjoint original coordinates `O`.

We split according to the size of the physical scalar coefficient `a`.

### Case A: `|a|>=s/2`

Complete the scalar square. Writing the affine coefficient as `(eta_0,eta_Y)`,

`Q_z
 =a [X_0+(b^T Y+eta_0/2)/a]^2
  +Y^T K Y + ell^T Y + c`,                                         (3.5)

where

`K=C-b b^T/a`,                                                     (3.6)

and `ell,c` are deterministic after conditioning. Since `B` is invertible and `a!=0`, `K` is invertible and

`det B=a det K`.                                                    (3.7)

For every fixed value of `Y`, the translated scalar `X_0+(b^TY+eta_0/2)/a` still has density cap one as a function of its scalar argument. Hence the density of its scaled square is bounded by the standard square kernel, and after averaging over `Y`,

`p_{Q_z}(t)
 <=|a|^(-1/2)
   E |Y^T K Y+ell^T Y+c-t|^(-1/2).`                                (3.8)

Now apply the genuine rank-two product-projection half-Riesz theorem (2.1). The affine term in (3.8) is in the two-dimensional projected range, so

`||p_{Q_z}||_infinity
 <=16 sqrt(30)|a|^(-1/2)|det K|^(-1/4)`

`=16 sqrt(30)|a|^(-1/4)D^(-1/4)`.                                 (3.9)

Using `|a|>=s/2` and `D>=s^3`,

`||p_{Q_z}||_infinity
 <=16 sqrt(30) 2^(1/4) s^(-1).`                                   (3.10)

This step never declares the two coordinates of `Y` independent.

### Case B: `|a|<s/2`

We first prove that the missing cross-transverse resource in (2.2) is forced by the spectral gap.

Rotate only in the two-dimensional receiver plane so that

`b=(beta,0)`, `beta>=0`,

and write

`C=[[c,d],[d,e]]`.

Set

`P=beta^2|e|`.                                                      (3.11)

Because every eigenvalue of `B` has modulus at least `s`,

`||B^(-1)||_op<=1/s`.                                               (3.12)

If `b=0`, then `a` itself is an eigenvalue of the block-diagonal matrix `B`, contradicting `|a|<s/2`. Hence `b!=0`.

If `det C=0`, the determinant identity

`det B=a det C-beta^2 e`                                            (3.13)

gives immediately

`P=D`.                                                              (3.14)

Suppose next that `det C!=0`. Let

`r=a-b^T C^(-1)b`

be the scalar Schur complement. Then

`(B^(-1))_00=r^(-1)`.                                               (3.15)

By (3.12), `|r|>=s`. Since `det B=(det C)r`,

`|det C|<=D/s`.                                                     (3.16)

Using (3.13) and the reverse triangle inequality,

`P=|beta^2 e|
 =|a det C-det B|
 >=D-|a||det C|
 >=(1-|a|/s)D

Thus in both subcases

`P>=D/2`.                                                           (3.18)

The product-projection theorem gives `||p_Y||_infinity<=2`. Apply the cross-transverse theorem (2.2), including the deterministic affine terms generated by conditioning:

`<=C_* 2^(5/6)s^(-1)`

`=83.70601351695441... s^(-1).`                                    (3.19)

This is strictly smaller than the Case-A constant (3.10).

The conditional bounds are uniform in `z`. Averaging the conditional densities over `X_O` proves (3.2). QED.
