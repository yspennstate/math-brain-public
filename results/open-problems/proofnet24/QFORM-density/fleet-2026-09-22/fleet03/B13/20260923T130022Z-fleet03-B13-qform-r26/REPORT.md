## 2. General slab-conditioned core transfer

The previous r25 transfers used the essential radii of the entire finite-union sets. Those inequalities were correct, but a tiny remote fragment can make an essential radius enormous while changing the slab probability negligibly. The right quantity is the geometry of a set carrying a prescribed fraction of the **slab mass itself**.

Let `Q` be the actual polynomial, `J` an interval of length `L>0`,

`S={Q in J}`, `m=P(S)>0`, `rho=m/L`.

For a finite coordinate bank `I`, let `K_i` be measurable coordinate cores and put

`C=cap_(i in I) {X_i in K_i}`.

`P(S cap C^c) <= eta m`, `0<=eta<1`.                            (2.1)

Choose centers `c_i` and radii `r_i` with `|x-c_i|<=r_i` on `K_i`.

Suppose that after rewriting both polynomials around the same chosen centers,

`Q-Q0 = y_I^T E y_I`, `y_i=X_i-c_i`,

and `||E||_F<=beta`. Then

`P(Q0 in J^{+Delta}) >= (1-eta)m`,

with

`Delta=beta sum_(i in I) r_i^2`.                                (2.2)

Hence, whenever `Q0` has a density cap `M0` uniformly in all affine terms generated by the chosen center,

`boxed:
 rho <= [M0/(1-eta)] [1+2 beta R2/L],

`                                                                    (2.3)

If instead `||E||_max<=gamma`, then on the core

`|y^T E y| <= gamma (sum_i r_i)^2`,

`boxed:

#### Proof

`|Q-Q0|=|<E,yy^T>_F|<=||E||_F ||y||_2^2<=beta R2`.

`S cap C subset {Q0 in J^{+Delta}}`.

`(1-eta)m <= M0(L+2Delta)`.

## 4. Tiny-fragment poisoning of ambient essential radius

### Proposition 4.1 — `QFORM-AMBIENT-ESSENTIAL-RADIUS-NONCOERCIVE-v1`

Fix any bounded finite-union set `E` of measure one and any `0<epsilon<1`. There are bounded finite-union unit sets `E_R`, with `rad(E_R)->infinity`, such that

`TV(1_E dx,1_(E_R) dx)=epsilon`.                                (4.1)

Consequently, if only one marginal in any product law is changed from `1_E` to `1_(E_R)`, the probability of **every** measurable event changes by at most `epsilon`, uniformly in `R`.

#### Proof

Choose a finite-union subset `F subset E` of measure `epsilon`. For large `R`, choose an interval `G_R` of length `epsilon` disjoint from `E` and at distance tending to infinity. Set

`E_R=(E\F) union G_R`.

Then `|E triangle E_R|=2epsilon`, so the total variation distance of the two unit-density laws is `epsilon`. Tensoring with unchanged marginals cannot increase total variation. Meanwhile the essential convex hull of `E_R` has diameter tending to infinity, hence so does `rad(E_R)`. QED.

This does **not** refute the r25 inequalities involving ambient radii. It refutes only the idea that a forced lower bound on such a radius can by itself be a closing mechanism. A divergent ambient radius may be carried by an arbitrarily small amount of probability.

The slab-conditioned core transfer removes exactly this defect: a remote fragment that carries at most an `eta` fraction of the actual slab mass is charged to (2.1) and never enters the core radii.
