## 5. Exact separated support geometry

For the `j`th component of `E_2`, the variable

`s=y^2+z^2`, `y in [u_j,u_j+h]`, `z in [0,1]`,

ranges over the entire interval

`[u_j^2,(u_j+h)^2+1]`.

Indeed, for fixed `y`, the possible values form `[y^2,y^2+1]`; as `y` varies continuously these intervals form a connected union from the smallest lower endpoint to the largest upper endpoint.

Consequently the positive `x`-support generated by component `j` is the interval

`B_j^+=[a_j,b_j]`,

where

`a_j=sqrt(R^2-(u_j+h)^2-1)`,

`b_j=sqrt(R^2+delta-u_j^2)`.

The negative support is `B_j^-=-B_j^+`. All radicals are real because `u_j+h<30M` while `R=100M`.

Thus

`supp(g) subset union_{j=1}^M (B_j^+ union B_j^-)`.

In fact every one of these `2M` bands contains an open subinterval on which `g>0`, as shown below.

### 5.1 Total support length is strictly below one

For every `j`,

`b_j-a_j = [2u_j h+h^2+1+delta]/(a_j+b_j)`.

Since `u_j+h<30M`,

`a_j^2 > 10000M^2-900M^2-1 = 9100M^2-1 > (95M)^2`.

Hence `a_j+b_j>190M`. Also

`2u_jh+h^2+1+delta < 60+1+5/4 = 249/4`.

Therefore

`|B_j^+| < 249/(760M)`

and

`|supp(g)| <= 2 sum_j |B_j^+| < 249/380 <1`.                 (5.1)

This estimate is independent of `M`.

### 5.2 Distinct support bands are separated by more than one

For adjacent positive bands, with `j<M`,

`a_j^2-b_{j+1}^2`

` = u_{j+1}^2-(u_j+h)^2-(1+delta)`

` = (20-h)(2u_j+20+h)-5/4`.

Using `h<=1` and `u_j>=10M`,

`a_j^2-b_{j+1}^2 >= 19(20M+20)-5/4 >380M`.

Since `a_j+b_{j+1}<2R=200M`,

`a_j-b_{j+1}>1.9>1`.                                        (5.2)

The same holds for adjacent negative bands by symmetry. The gap between the positive and negative families is at least `2a_M>190M>1`. Hence **every pair of distinct bands among the `2M` bands is separated by more than one**.

### 5.3 Every band has positive conditional mass

Fix `j` and choose interior points

`y_j=u_j+h/2`, `z_j=1/2`.

Because `R` is much larger than `u_j`, the number

`x_j=sqrt(R^2+delta/2-y_j^2-z_j^2)`

is positive and belongs to the interior of `B_j^+`. At `(x_j,y_j,z_j)`,

`x_j^2+y_j^2+z_j^2=R^2+delta/2`,

strictly inside `J`. Continuity therefore gives an open product neighborhood of `(x_j,y_j,z_j)` still lying in the event and with `(y,z)` inside the chosen component and `[0,1]`. Thus `g(x)>0` on a nonempty open interval inside `B_j^+`. By evenness, the same holds on `B_j^-`.

Define

`m_* = min_{1<=j<=M, eps in {+,-}} int_{B_j^eps} g(x) dx`.

There are finitely many bands and every displayed integral is positive, so

`m_*>0`.                                                     (5.3)

## 6. Exact optimizer versus every at-most-M-interval marginal

Because `g>=0` and the total support length is below one by (5.1), choose a compact finite union `E_1` of total measure exactly one that contains all `2M` support bands; fill the remaining measure in a bounded interval disjoint from `supp(g)`. Then

`int_{E_1} g = int_R g`.                                     (6.1)

For any admissible density `0<=f_1<=1`,

`int f_1 g <= int g`,                                       (6.2)

so (6.1) is the exact unrestricted optimum. This is the partner bathtub theorem realized explicitly.

Now let `F` be any measurable union of at most `M` intervals with `|F|=1`. Replace overlapping intervals by its connected interval components; there are still at most `M`, and because the total union length is one, every component has length at most one.

By (5.2), an interval of length at most one can intersect **at most one** of the `2M` support bands. Hence `F` intersects at most `M` bands and misses at least `M` bands completely. By (5.3),

`int_F g <= int_R g - m_* < int_R g`.                        (6.3)

Combining (6.1)-(6.3) gives the strict inequality

`sup_{0<=f<=1, int f=1} L(f)`

`  > sup_{F: |F|=1, F union of <=M intervals} L(1_F)`.       (6.4)

This holds for every finite `M`.

## 7. Theorem and precise scope

### Theorem — `QFORM-NO-UNIFORM-FRAGMENT-COUNT-BATHTUB-v1`

For every finite integer `M>=1`, there is an exact QFORM target instance with `A=I_3`, a bounded interval `J`, and fixed independent second and third marginals whose densities are indicators of unions of at most `M` intervals, such that optimizing the first marginal over all probability densities bounded by one gives a strictly larger small-ball probability than optimizing the first marginal over indicators of unions of at most `M` intervals.

Therefore the completed exact coordinatewise bathtub localization cannot be strengthened to a universal fixed fragment count while preserving each fixed-fiber optimum and the exact constant.

### What this theorem does **not** say

It does not prove that the **global** supremum over all product laws is smaller when every marginal is restricted to at most `M` intervals; other marginals could in principle reorganize. Thus no global fixed-`M` equivalence is claimed or refuted here. What is decisively refuted is the coordinate-by-coordinate fixed-count compiler that would try to strengthen the partner theorem while holding the other marginals fixed.

It also does not refute QFORM. Here `A=I_3`, whose output is already covered by the proven diagonal `C=pi` theorem. The construction concerns the geometry of exact localization, not failure of the density bound.
