## 2. A translation-uniform definite physical-pair theorem

### Lemma 2.1 — `QFORM-DEFINITE-PHYSICAL-PAIR-v1`

Let `i,j` be two ORIGINAL coordinates. Write their symmetric principal block as `B=A_{ {i,j},{i,j} }`. If `B` is positive or negative definite, then for arbitrary independent cap-one input densities in every coordinate, arbitrary remaining spectrum/couplings, and arbitrary dimension,

`boxed: ||p_{X^T A X}||_infinity <= pi / sqrt(|det B|).`          (2.1)

The estimate is uniform over all affine terms generated by conditioning the outside coordinates.

#### Proof

Condition on all original coordinates except `(X_i,X_j)`. Independence is genuine at this step. The conditional polynomial has the form

`q(x)=x^T Bx + ell^T x + c`,                                     (2.2)

where `ell,c` are deterministic under the conditioning and the joint density of `x=(X_i,X_j)` is at most one.

Assume first `B>0`. Complete the square,

`q(x)=(x-x_0)^T B(x-x_0)+c_0`,                                  (2.3)

and set `z=B^(1/2)(x-x_0)`. The density `rho` of `z` satisfies

`0<=rho(z)<=1/sqrt(det B)`.                                      (2.4)

For `R=|z|^2`, polar coordinates give, for almost every `s>0`,

`p_R(s)=(1/2) integral_0^(2pi) rho(sqrt(s)(cos theta,sin theta)) dtheta`
`      <= pi/sqrt(det B)`.                                      (2.5)

Translation by `c_0` does not change the essential supremum. If `B<0`, apply the same argument to `-q`; `|det(-B)|=|det B|`. The bound is uniform in the conditioned outside variables, so averaging the conditional interval probabilities proves (2.1) for the full ambient form. QED.

This lemma is deliberately physical-coordinate based. It cannot be reproduced by diagonalizing an arbitrary spectral pair and pretending the new coordinates stay independent.
