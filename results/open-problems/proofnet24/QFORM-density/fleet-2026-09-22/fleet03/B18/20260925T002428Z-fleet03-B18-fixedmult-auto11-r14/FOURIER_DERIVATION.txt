Detailed derivation. Let V=2CY+beta and Psi_t(v)=E_U exp(it(U^TBU+v.U)). Then phi_Q(t)=E_Y[e^{itH(Y)} Psi_t(V)], so Cauchy-Schwarz gives |phi_Q(t)|^2<=E|Psi_t(V)|^2. LPP in dimension two gives density(CY)<=2K^2/sqrt(Delta), Delta=det(CC^T), hence density(V)<=M=K^2/(2sqrt(Delta)). Therefore E|Psi_t(V)|^2<=M integral|Psi_t(v)|^2dv. With f_U=f_1 f_2 and g_t(u)=f_U(u)e^{it u^TBu}, Psi_t(v)=hat(g_t)(tv). Two-dimensional Plancherel gives integral|Psi_t(v)|^2dv=(2pi)^2|t|^-2 integral f_U^2 <=(2pi)^2 K^2|t|^-2. Thus |phi_Q(t)|<=min(1,A/|t|), A=sqrt(2)pi K^2 Delta^-1/4. For q=r/(r-1), 2<=r<infinity, integral|phi|^q<=2A q/(q-1)=2Ar. At q=2, Gaussian regularization phi(t)e^{-eps t^2} and Plancherel produce densities converging in L2; weak convergence identifies the limit with the original law, proving absolute continuity. Interpolating inverse Fourier L1->Linfinity norm 1/(2pi) and L2->L2 norm 1/sqrt(2pi) gives ||p||_r<=(2pi)^(-1/q)||phi||_q <=(Ar/pi)^(1/q)=(sqrt(2)rK^2Delta^-1/4)^(1-1/r).
