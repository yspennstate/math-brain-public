### Proof

A pairwise-intersecting graph of 2-subsets is either a star or a triangle: choose intersecting edges `{a,b}` and `{a,c}`. If every edge contains `a`, it is a star. Otherwise an edge avoiding `a` must meet both selected edges and hence is `{b,c}`; then any further edge involving a fourth vertex would be disjoint from one of the triangle edges.

In the star case, `det(u_j,u_k)=0` for all `j,k!=s`, hence all outside nonzero rows are collinear, say along a unit vector `q`. Write

`sum_(j!=s)u_j u_j^T=alpha q q^T`.                             (3.5)

`u_su_s^T=I_2-alpha q q^T`.                                   (3.6)

The left side has rank one. Therefore the determinant of the right side is zero, forcing `alpha=1`. Equation (3.6) becomes the orthogonal projection onto `q^perp`, which proves (3.3)-(3.4).

In the non-star case the support graph is a triangle and its three supporting rows are pairwise nonparallel. If an outside row `u_l` were nonzero, it could be parallel to at most one triangle row, so it would form a positive determinant edge with another triangle vertex.
