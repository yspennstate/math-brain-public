## 2. Conditional end-to-end coarea argument

Among any three large eigenvalues two have the same sign.  Let `E` be the corresponding two-dimensional eigenspace.  On each affine fiber `w+E`, the restriction of `Q` in the `E` coordinates is a positive- or negative-definite quadratic form with both eigenvalues of modulus at least one; its Lebesgue coarea density is therefore at most `pi` times the fiber-amplitude ceiling.

A tempting completion is consequently

`p_Q(y) <= pi int_{E^perp} sup_{z in E} prod_i f_i((w+z)_i) dw`.                (2.1)

If the mixed norm on the right were universally bounded, the full conjecture would follow immediately.  The first essential question for this B05 card is therefore whether (2.1) can be dimension-free.

## 3. Exact counterexample to the fiber-supremum completion

### Proposition `QFORM-FIBER-SUP-PROJECTION-v1`

For every even `n`, there is an orthonormal two-plane `E subset R^n` and a legitimate product density `F=prod_i f_i`, with every `f_i<=1`, such that

`int_{E^perp} sup_{z in E} F(w+z) dw = n/2`.                                  (3.1)

Thus (2.1), even with the sharp planar coarea constant `pi`, can lose a factor growing linearly in dimension.

### Proof

Take the independent-coordinate density

`F(x)=1_[0,1]^n(x)=prod_i 1_[0,1](x_i)`.

Let

`r_1=(1,...,1)/sqrt(n)`

and let `r_2` have its first `n/2` coordinates `+1/sqrt(n)` and its last `n/2` coordinates `-1/sqrt(n)`.  Then `r_1,r_2` are orthonormal; set `E=span(r_1,r_2)`.

For an indicator, `sup_{z in E} F(w+z)` equals one exactly when the affine fiber `w+E` meets the cube.  Therefore the left side of (3.1) is the `(n-2)`-dimensional volume of the orthogonal projection `P_{E^perp}[0,1]^n`.

The projected cube is the zonotope generated by `P_{E^perp}e_1,...,P_{E^perp}e_n`.  The zonotope volume formula, followed by Hodge duality for the complementary two coordinates, gives

`Vol_{n-2}(P_{E^perp}[0,1]^n)
 = sum_{1<=i<j<=n} |r_{1i}r_{2j}-r_{1j}r_{2i}|`.                              (3.2)

If `i,j` belong to the same half, the determinant in (3.2) is zero.  If they lie in opposite halves, its absolute value is `2/n`.  There are `(n/2)^2=n^2/4` cross-half pairs.  Hence

`Vol_{n-2}(P_{E^perp}[0,1]^n)=(n^2/4)(2/n)=n/2`,

proving (3.1).  QED.

### Interpretation

This is a proof-mechanism counterexample, not a counterexample to the conjecture.  It identifies the missing factor exactly: **taking the fiber amplitude supremum discards the third large direction and converts product normalization into a projected-cube volume, whose correct scale can be `Theta(n)`.**

It also source-validly matches the independent account01/B05 Walsh-plane obstruction.  The current shared `CURRENT_RESEARCH` now records the same dimension-loss phenomenon, so no novelty beyond the exact proof above is asserted.

## 4. A quantitative replacement resource that retains product normalization

The preceding failure suggests that any successful coarea estimate must keep an averaged quantity from each coordinate rather than replacing the product amplitude by a fiberwise supremum.  The cleanest exact representation I found comes from account03/B16's unnormalized-pair suggestion.

Let `X'` be an independent copy, `phi_A(t)=E exp(i t X^TAX)`, and define

`K_i(u,eta)=(1/2) int exp(i eta v) f_i((v+u)/2) f_i((v-u)/2) dv`

`          =exp(-i eta u) int exp(i2 eta x) f_i(x)f_i(x-u) dx`.                (4.1)

Then, with `u=x-x'`, `v=x+x'`, Jacobian `2^{-n}`, and
`x^TAx-x'^TAx'=u^TAv`, the `v_i` integrations separate exactly:

`|phi_A(t)|^2 = int_{R^n} prod_i K_i(u_i,t(Au)_i) du`.                         (4.2)

Moreover, writing `h_i(u)=int f_i(x)f_i(x-u)dx`,

`|K_i(u,eta)|<=h_i(u)`, `0<=h_i<=1`, `int h_i=1`,                             (4.3)

and Plancherel at frequency `2 eta` gives the exact Moyal-type budget

`int int |K_i(u,eta)|^2 du deta
 = pi (int f_i^2)^2 <= pi`.                                                    (4.4)

Unlike (2.1), (4.2)--(4.4) retain both the unit `u`-marginal mass and an oscillatory phase-space budget for every original independent coordinate.

### Why bare `L^2` is still insufficient

In the ideal three-coordinate transverse Brascamp--Lieb scaling, using only (4.4) after taking absolute values gives one factor `|t|^{-1/2}` per selected kernel, hence at best

`|phi_A(t)|^2 <= C |t|^{-3/2}`,

or `|phi_A(t)| <= C^{1/2}|t|^{-3/4}`, which is not integrable.  The same square-root loss occurs in small-ball language: a linear `eps` estimate for `Q-Q'` yields only an `O(sqrt(eps))` estimate for `Q` by `P(Q in I)^2 <= P(Q-Q' in I-I)`.

Thus a successful repair must couple (4.3) and (4.4) (or use another genuinely three-direction geometric average), rather than apply a bare `L^2` Brascamp--Lieb inequality after one squaring.

## 5. Supporting one-variable averaged estimate

For later use, the `p=3/2` result of account03/B16 extends uniformly to every `1<p<2` and to linear shifts.  If `0<=f<=K` is a probability density and

`Z=lambda X^2+bX+c`, `lambda!=0`,

then

`||p_Z||_p <= [2^{p-1}/(2-p)]^{1/p} K^{2-2/p}|lambda|^{1/p-1}`.                (5.1)

Proof: complete the square, let `g(r)=f(a+r)+f(a-r)`, so `0<=g<=2K`, `int g=1`, and

`int p_Z^p=(2|lambda|)^{1-p} int_0^infinity g(r)^p r^{1-p}dr`.

The decreasing-weight bathtub extremizer is `g=2K 1_[0,1/(2K)]`, giving

`int g^p r^{1-p}dr <= (2K)^{2p-2}/(2-p)`

and hence (5.1).  At `p=3/2` this recovers `2K^{2/3}|lambda|^{-1/3}`.  This is an averaged-frequency resource, not a pointwise decay assertion.

## 6. External source check

Livshyts--Paouris--Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures* (arXiv:1507.07949; Israel J. Math. 216 (2016)), prove that every `k`-dimensional orthogonal marginal of a product density with one-dimensional factors bounded by one has density at most `2^{k/2}`.  In particular each unit eigen-coordinate `<X,r>` has density at most `sqrt(2)`.  Combining this with (5.1) gives uniform `L^{3/2}` control on each individual squared large eigen-coordinate, but those eigen-coordinates are dependent after rotation; the estimates cannot simply be multiplied.

Kwan--Sah--Sauermann--Sawhney, arXiv:2208.02874, Theorem 1.6, prove the optimal *linear* small-ball scale for Gaussian quadratic polynomials under a robust-rank-three hypothesis and note the rank-two `Z_1^2-Z_2^2` logarithmic obstruction.  Their Gaussian theorem supports the threshold but does not supply the arbitrary bounded-density product result here.
