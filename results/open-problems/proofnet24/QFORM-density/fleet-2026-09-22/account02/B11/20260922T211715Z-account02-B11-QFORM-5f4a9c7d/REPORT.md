## Setup

Let

`A = R M R^T`, `R^T R = I_3`,

where `M` is real symmetric of inertia `(2,1)` or `(1,2)` and `sigma_min(M) >= s`. In the heavy-null normalization choose one original coordinate `0` with row

`r_0 = (sqrt(c),0,0)`,  `c >= 1/2`.

For each residual original coordinate `j`, write

`r_j = (a_j,b_j,d_j)` and `z_j=(b_j,d_j) in R^2`.

Because the last two columns of `R` are orthonormal and row `0` has zero transverse coordinates,

`sum_j z_j z_j^T = I_2`.                                                (1)

The partner account03/B11 heavy-null normal form may be taken as

## Theorem: diffuse transverse two-bank wedge compiler

Assume in addition

`max_j ||z_j||_2^2 <= 1/16`.                                             (2)

Then the residual original coordinates admit a disjoint partition `S union T` such that, with

`G_S=sum_{j in S} z_j z_j^T`,  `G_T=sum_{j in T} z_j z_j^T`,

one has

`(1/4) I_2 <= G_S <= (3/4) I_2`,
`(1/4) I_2 <= G_T <= (3/4) I_2`,                                        (3)

and the cross-bank transverse wedge energy obeys

`W_ST := sum_{i in S, j in T} det(z_i,z_j)^2 >= 3/8`.                   (4)

Consequently the physical three-coordinate principal minors containing the heavy coordinate have a **dimension-free aggregate mass**:

`sum_{i in S, j in T} |det A[{0,i,j},{0,i,j}]|`
` = c |det M| W_ST`
` >= (3/8)c |det M|`
` >= (3/8)c s^3`
` >= 3 s^3/16`.                                                        (5)

The partition is across ORIGINAL coordinate sets, so the input sigma-fields generated by `{X_i:i in S}` and `{X_j:j in T}` remain genuinely independent. No rotated-variable independence or conditional-density cap is asserted.

### Proof of (3)

Choose independent Bernoulli `xi_j in {0,1}` with mean `1/2` and put

`Y_j=(xi_j-1/2) z_j z_j^T`.

Then `E Y_j=0`, `||Y_j|| <= delta/2` with `delta=1/16`, and

`sum_j E Y_j^2`
` = (1/4) sum_j ||z_j||^2 z_j z_j^T`
` <= (delta/4) I_2`                                                    (6)

by (1). The self-adjoint matrix Bernstein inequality in dimension two gives, for `t=1/4`,

`P(||sum_j Y_j|| >= 1/4)`
` <= 4 exp(- t^2/(2 sigma^2 + (2/3) R t))`
` <= 4 exp(-3/(28 delta))`
` = 4 exp(-12/7)`
` < 1`.                                                               (7)

Thus there exists a deterministic realization with `||G_S-I_2/2||<=1/4`; since `G_T=I_2-G_S`, (3) follows for both banks.

Source for the matrix Bernstein form used in (7): J. A. Tropp, *User-Friendly Tail Bounds for Sums of Random Matrices*, Foundations of Computational Mathematics 12 (2012), 389–434, DOI `10.1007/s10208-011-9099-z`, arXiv `1004.4389`.

### Proof of (4)

For any two vectors `u,v in R^2`,

`det(u,v)^2 = ||u||^2 ||v||^2 - (u dot v)^2`.

Hence

`W_ST = tr(G_S) tr(G_T) - tr(G_S G_T)`.                                (8)

Because `G_T=I-G_S`, the two matrices commute. If the eigenvalues of `G_S` are `lambda_1,lambda_2`, then by (3), each lies in `[1/4,3/4]`, and

`W_ST = lambda_1(1-lambda_2)+lambda_2(1-lambda_1)`.

This bilinear expression attains its minimum over the square `[1/4,3/4]^2` at a corner, where the minimum is `3/8`. This proves (4).

### Proof of (5)

For `I={0,i,j}`, the exact-rank-three factorization gives

`A[I,I]=R_I M R_I^T`,
`det A[I,I] = det(M) det(R_I)^2`.                                      (9)

Since the heavy row is `(sqrt(c),0,0)`,

`det(R_I)=sqrt(c) det(z_i,z_j)`.                                       (10)

Taking absolute values and summing (9)–(10) over `i in S,j in T` yields the identity in (5). Finally `|det M|>=s^3`.

`M_0=[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,1]]`.

` <= 3/m^2 -> 0`.                                                      (11)

`sum_{i<j} |det A[{0,i,j},{0,i,j}]| = 3/4`,                            (12)
