## 5. KKS theorem package with exact hypotheses

Primary source: Andrzej Kisielewicz and Marek Szykuła, *Generating Synchronizing Automata with Large Reset Lengths*, arXiv:1404.3311v4 (28 Mar 2018).

An extension `B` of a parent `A` has the same state set and contains all letters of `A` plus possibly more. For a set `P` of compressible state pairs, write `h(P)` for its synchronizing height and `p(P,r)` for the maximum length of an r-subset Frankl–Pin sequence over `P`.

The imported results are:

**KKS Theorem 2.** Every compressible r-subset can be compressed by a word of length at most

\[
\binom{n-r+2}{2}-p(P,r)+h(P).
\]

**Pin/KKS Theorem 3.** If a word `u` has rank r and some word of rank at most r-1 exists, then there is such a word of length at most

\[
2|u|+n-r+1.
\]

KKS explicitly applies these successively to a nonsynchronizing parent of minimal rank m, choosing the smaller bound at each rank, and Algorithm 1 discards a parent once the resulting extension bound is below the target threshold. This is the technique ported here; no unpublished strengthening is assumed.

## 6. Claim `P16-KKS-T23-EXTENSION-SAFE-54` — PROVED / source-ported

Let `A` be a nonsynchronizing n-state parent of minimal rank `m>=2`. Let `L_m` be the length of a shortest parent word of rank m. Let `P` be any set of pairs compressible in A, with parent synchronizing height `h_A(P)`. Define

\[
U_m=L_m
\]

and, for `r=m,m-1,...,2`,

\[
\boxed{
U_{r-1}=\min\left\{
2U_r+n-r+1,\quad
U_r+\binom{n-r+2}{2}-p(P,r)+h_A(P)
\right\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Then **every synchronizing extension B of A has a reset word of length at most `U_1`**.

### Proof

Because B contains every parent letter, the parent rank-m word remains available, so B has a word of rank m and length `U_m`.

Assume B has a word `u` of rank at most r and length at most `U_r`. If its rank is already below r, skip that compression stage; both candidate increments in (1) are nonnegative, so retaining the same word is no worse than the recorded upper bound. Thus consider the worst case `rank(u)=r`.

Because B is synchronizing and `r>1`, a word of rank at most r-1 exists. Pin/KKS Theorem 3 therefore supplies such a word with length at most

\[
2U_r+n-r+1.
\]

For the second alternative let `M=Qu`, so `|M|=r`. B is synchronizing, hence M is compressible in B. Every pair in P was already compressible in A and remains so in B by the same parent words, whence

\[
h_B(P)\le h_A(P).
\]

KKS Theorem 2 inside B gives a word compressing M of length at most

\[
\binom{n-r+2}{2}-p(P,r)+h_B(P)
\le
\binom{n-r+2}{2}-p(P,r)+h_A(P).
\]

Appending this word to u yields rank at most r-1 with length at most the second entry in (1). Taking the smaller valid construction proves the induction step. Iterating to rank one gives a reset word of length at most `U_1`. QED.

### P16 consequence

At n=8, every nonsynchronizing ternary parent with computed `U_1<=49` is **extension-safe**: no fourth letter can turn it into a counterexample. Such a parent can be omitted from all fourth-letter shards without changing coverage.
