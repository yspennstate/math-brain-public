# 3. Exact target this run

The previous theorem used deterministic conditional `psi_1` envelopes `L_ij` for every deletion/replacement prediction difference. That produced exponential confidence but can be conservative because it first applies triangle inequalities across all affected deletion learners.

The concrete target is:

> Can the same leave-one-out hard-pruning risk theorem use the actual coordinate-replacement conditional variance/scale process, retaining cancellations and cross-covariances, while preserving exponential confidence and without assuming the selected pruning support is independent of the trained matrix?

Answer: **yes**, at the exact martingale level. A second-moment-only version necessarily retains a Bernstein scale or a stronger MGF assumption; the counterexample in Section 9 shows this is unavoidable.

# 4. Setup and exact Doob replacement identity

Let

```text
S=(Z_1,...,Z_n)
```

be iid, with any auxiliary training randomness independent of `S` and held common across coupled retrains. Let `H_T=H_T(S)` be the clipped leave-one-out generalization gap from the preceding reports:

```text
H_T = Rbar_loo,T - Rhat_loo,T.
```

Because both terms lie in `[0,T]`,

```text
-T <= H_T <= T,
E H_T = 0.
```

Let

```text
F_j = sigma(Z_1,...,Z_j),
M_j = E[H_T | F_j],
D_j = M_j-M_(j-1).
```

Let `Z_j'` be an independent copy and `S^(j)` the sample with coordinate `j` replaced. Define

```text
Delta_j H_T = H_T(S)-H_T(S^(j)).
```

The exact identity is

```text
D_j = E[Delta_j H_T | F_j].                              (4.1)
```

Indeed, conditional on `F_j`, integrating the independent replacement `Z_j'` and the unrevealed future coordinates makes `H_T(S^(j))` have conditional mean `E[H_T|F_(j-1)]`.

This identity is the bridge from direct ghost-replacement moments to the Doob martingale.

# 5. `P19-LOO-PREDICTABLE-EFRONSTEIN-34` — PROVED

Define the predictable direct-replacement second moment

```text
q_j^(2)
 := E[ (Delta_j H_T)^2 | F_(j-1) ].                      (5.1)
```

Then

```text
E[D_j^2 | F_(j-1)] <= q_j^(2).                           (5.2)
```

Proof: by `(4.1)` and conditional Jensen,

```text
|D_j|^2
<= E[(Delta_j H_T)^2 | F_j].
```

Take conditional expectation given `F_(j-1)`.

A sharper Efron–Stein normalization is also valid. Let `g_j(z)=E[H_T|F_(j-1),Z_j=z]`. Then

```text
E[D_j^2 | F_(j-1)]
 = Var(g_j(Z_j)|F_(j-1))
 = (1/2) E[(g_j(Z_j)-g_j(Z_j'))^2 | F_(j-1)]
 <= (1/2) q_j^(2).                                      (5.3)
```

The last inequality is Jensen over the common unrevealed future coordinates.

Thus the predictable quadratic variation of the Doob martingale is controlled by an exact coordinate-replacement process. No `L_ij`, iid pruning mask, or pathwise raw-feature bound is needed for `(5.2)`–`(5.3)`.

# 6. `P19-LOO-PREDICTABLE-BERNSTEIN-34` — PROVED

Assume there exist nonnegative `F_(j-1)`-measurable random variables `q_j,c_j` such that for every integer `m>=2`, almost surely,

```text
E[ |Delta_j H_T|^m | F_(j-1) ]
<= (m!/2) q_j c_j^(m-2).                                (6.1)
```

By `(4.1)` and conditional Jensen,

```text
E[ |D_j|^m | F_(j-1) ]
<= (m!/2) q_j c_j^(m-2).                                (6.2)
```

Since `E[D_j|F_(j-1)]=0`, expansion of the conditional MGF gives for `0<=lambda<1/c_j`,

```text
E[exp(lambda D_j)|F_(j-1)]
<= exp( lambda^2 q_j / [2(1-lambda c_j)] ).              (6.3)
```

Put

```text
Q_k=sum_(j<=k) q_j,
C_k=max_(j<=k) c_j.
```

For deterministic `v,c,t>0`, stopping before either `Q_k` exceeds `v` or the next predictable scale exceeds `c`, the exponential supermartingale from `(6.3)` gives

```text
P{ H_T >= sqrt(2 v t)+c t,
   Q_n<=v,
   C_n<=c }
<= exp(-t).                                               (6.4)
```

This is the direct predictable replacement analogue of Freedman/Bernstein. The important point is that `q_j,c_j` may depend on the revealed training history; they are not coupling-wide deterministic constants.

There is always a coarse admissible pair because `|Delta_j H_T|<=2T`:

```text
q_j = E[(Delta_j H_T)^2|F_(j-1)] <= 4T^2,
c_j = 2T.                                                 (6.5)
```

Indeed `|Delta|^m <= (2T)^(m-2) Delta^2` and `m!/2>=1`. This universal choice may be too coarse in the linear Bernstein term, but it proves the theorem is never vacuous as a formal concentration statement.

# 7. `P19-LOO-SELFNORM-STITCH-34` — PROVED

Assume deterministic caps

```text
Q_n<=Q_max,
C_n<=C_max
```

for the chosen predictable Bernstein process. Choose floors `q0,c0>0` and put

```text
R_Q = max{0, ceil(log_2(Q_max/q0))},
R_C = max{0, ceil(log_2(C_max/c0))},
Ncell=(R_Q+1)(R_C+1),
L=log(Ncell/eta).                                        (7.1)
```

Partition the possible values of `(Q_n,C_n)` into dyadic cells. On a cell containing the realized pair, the deterministic upper edges satisfy

```text
v <= 2 (Q_n vee q0),
c <= 2 (C_n vee c0).                                    (7.2)
```

Apply `(6.4)` to each cell with failure probability `eta/Ncell` and union bound. With probability at least `1-eta`,

```text
boxed:
H_T
<= 2 sqrt( (Q_n vee q0) L )
   + 2 (C_n vee c0) L.                                  (7.3)
```

This is the promised self-normalized theorem. Its confidence cost over an oracle deterministic `(Q,C)` bound is only the logarithm of the number of dyadic variance/scale cells, i.e. a log-log-type overhead when the floors are polynomially smaller than the natural caps.

For the universal clipped caps from `(6.5)`, one may take

```text
Q_max=4 n T^2,
C_max=2T.
```

A useful theorem therefore comes from making the **actual** predictable `C_n` small by a boundary-influence tail argument, not from pretending the replacement variance itself controls rare large jumps.

# 8. `P19-LOO-GAUSSIAN-MIXTURE-34` — exact no-peeling theorem

There is a cleaner theorem under a stronger, explicit conditional sub-Gaussian hypothesis. Suppose `q_j` is predictable and for every real `lambda`,

```text
E[exp(lambda D_j)|F_(j-1)]
<= exp(lambda^2 q_j/2).                                  (8.1)
```

Let

```text
Q_n=sum_j q_j.
```

For each fixed `lambda`,

```text
exp(lambda M_n - lambda^2 Q_n/2)
```

is a nonnegative supermartingale of mean at most one. Mix `lambda` against a centered Gaussian density with precision `rho>0`. Fubini and the Gaussian integral give

```text
E[ sqrt(rho/(Q_n+rho))
   exp( H_T^2 / [2(Q_n+rho)] ) ]
<=1.                                                      (8.2)
```

Markov therefore yields, with probability at least `1-eta`,

```text
boxed:
|H_T|
<= sqrt( (Q_n+rho)
         [2 log(1/eta)+log(1+Q_n/rho)] ).                (8.3)
```

This completely removes deterministic replacement envelopes, but it requires a genuine conditional sub-Gaussian MGF statement. Section 9 proves that second moments alone cannot imply `(8.1)`.

# 9. `P19-VARONLY-SELFNORM-CE-34` — PROVED counterexample

Let `0<p<=1/2` and let a one-step martingale difference be

```text
X_p = 1-p   with probability p,
      -p    with probability 1-p.
```

Then

```text
E X_p=0,
Var(X_p)=p(1-p),
P{X_p>=1/2}=p.                                           (9.1)
```

Suppose a universal variance-only sub-Gaussian inequality of the form

```text
P{X>=x} <= exp( -x^2/[C Var(X)] )                       (9.2)
```

held for all centered bounded increments with a universal constant `C`. At `x=1/2`, `(9.2)` would give

```text
p
<= exp( -1/[4 C p(1-p)] ),                              (9.3)
```

which fails for sufficiently small `p`.

Therefore predictable variance alone cannot yield a Bernstein/sub-Gaussian exponential tail. One must retain either

- a scale/jump term such as `c_j` in `(6.1)`;
- a stronger conditional MGF hypothesis such as `(8.1)`;
- or another explicit tail structure.

This is the exact obstruction to a theorem that would try to replace the previous `psi_1` hypothesis by `L^2` influence only.

# 10. `P19-BOUNDARY-INFLUENCE-COVPROCESS-34` — exact hard-pruning form

The preceding report proved for the clipped LOO gap the exact coordinate decomposition

```text
Delta_j H_T
= n^(-1) [ A_j + sum_(i != j) xi_ij ],                  (10.1)
```

where `A_j` is the direct held-out evaluation replacement and

```text
xi_ij
= E_Z d_ij(Z) - d_ij(Z_i),

d_ij(z)
= ell_T(f_-i;z)-ell_T(f_-i^(j);z).                       (10.2)
```

Hence the direct predictable second moment in `(5.1)` is exactly

```text
q_j^(2)
= n^(-2)
  E[ (A_j + sum_(i != j) xi_ij)^2 | F_(j-1) ].          (10.3)
```

Writing `xi^(j)` for the vector `(xi_ij)_(i!=j)` and `Gamma_j` for its conditional second-moment matrix,

```text
Gamma_j
= E[ xi^(j) (xi^(j))* | F_(j-1) ],                      (10.4)
```

one has

```text
E|sum_(i!=j) xi_ij|^2
= 1* Gamma_j 1.                                          (10.5)
```

No off-diagonal term is declared zero. The deletion learners overlap heavily, and conditioning on a response-trained support does not restore independence.

For squared loss clipped at `T`, the map from prediction to clipped loss is `2 sqrt(T)`-Lipschitz, so

```text
E|xi_ij|^2
<= 4T E||f_-i(X)-f_-i^(j)(X)||^2.                       (10.6)
```

The already-proved hard-boundary prediction theorem can upper-bound the diagonal prediction energies by quantities of the form

```text
rho_ij sqrt(b_k(G_ij))
+ Psi_(G_ij,k)(rho_ij;C_-i)
+ representation debts.                                 (10.7)
```

But `(10.5)` shows the new exact source obligation: either estimate the whole aggregate coordinate variance directly, or control the cross-deletion covariances. A triangle inequality over all `i` recovers the previous deterministic envelope theorem, but the self-normalized route can be strictly sharper when the conditional cross-covariances cancel.

This is the precise sense in which the new theorem uses the **trained boundary influence covariance process** rather than an iid-mask approximation.

# 11. `P19-SELFNORM-DYAD-CERT-34` — PROVED composition

The spectral layers remain separate. Suppose an application of Sections 6–8 to the prediction-discrepancy loss between two legally aligned hard-pruned predictors yields a high-probability population bound

```text
E ||(C_1-C_2) psi||^2 <= Rbar_Delta.                    (11.1)
```

Assume their column-support union `J` has size at most `2k` and the honest feature Gram satisfies

```text
G_JJ >= a I,
a>0.                                                       (11.2)
```

Then deterministically

```text
||C_1-C_2||_op
<= sqrt(Rbar_Delta/a).                                    (11.3)
```

If a positive singular cluster of the deployed readout has protected gap `Delta_K` and

```text
sqrt(Rbar_Delta/a) <= Delta_K/2,                         (11.4)
```

only then Wedin/Hermitian dilation gives

```text
||Delta Pi_K^L||_op,
||Delta Pi_K^R||_op,
||Delta F_K||_op
<= 4 sqrt(Rbar_Delta/a) / Delta_K.                       (11.5)
```

Thus the new predictable-variance confidence theorem can feed a genuine singular-vector certificate, but only after a sparse restricted-Gram lower edge and an actual singular-value gap are supplied. The July `alpha_L,alpha_R` BBP overlaps remain compressed-resolvent residue statements and are not inferred from Wedin.

Under an honest simultaneous Gaussian sparse-RIP event, `(11.2)` may hold even when `r/n>1` and the global Gram is singular with a Marchenko–Pastur zero atom. Global MP ESD, sparse extreme eigenvalues, singular-vector overlap, prediction risk, and finite-width confidence are therefore different layers.

# 12. Method comparison and dependence audit

## Resolvents

Still the correct source tool for July outlier locations, cluster residues, and BBP singular-vector overlap. Nothing in the present LOO theorem proves the non-Haar growing-frame compressed-resolvent hypothesis.

## Davis–Kahan / Wedin

Second-stage only, as in Section 11. No gap, no projector theorem.

## Leave-one-out / ghost

Essential. The previously proved memorization counterexample shows that tiny fresh full-sample replacement RMS plus zero reused training error need not control population risk. Deletion restores the held-out independence used by the coordinate replacement identity.

## Predictable Bernstein / Freedman

This run moves from deterministic coupling-wide influence envelopes to the direct replacement moment process. Freedman's classical result controls a martingale by predictable quadratic variation plus an increment scale. The present proof derives the required conditional MGF directly from `(6.1)` and then stitches over the random predictable process.

## Deterministic equivalents

A source-relevant deterministic equivalent should target `(10.3)`–`(10.5)`, local restricted Gram edges, and boundary covariance; an ambient ESD alone does not determine these quantities.

## Free probability

No free-probability reduction is used. A response-trained representation and a support selected from it are not automatically asymptotically free from the feature/noise Gram. Any future free-probability step must verify its hypotheses explicitly.

## Sparsification concentration

The valid MP/null route remains simultaneous sparse-RIP or another support-uniform event. Such an event survives adaptive support selection because it is uniform over supports. This is not iid masking.

## Same trained matrix

Hard top-`k` support may be an arbitrary deterministic function of the trained matrix throughout this report. Its instability is paid through the actual boundary prediction/influence process. No Bernoulli mask is substituted.

# 13. Adversarial audit of the new theorem

Three possible overreaches were checked.

### 13.1 Random optimization of the Bernstein parameter

One cannot simply plug the realized random `Q_n,C_n` into a fixed-parameter Chernoff bound and optimize after seeing the data. Section 7 avoids that invalid step by dyadic stitching and a union bound over deterministic variance/scale cells.

### 13.2 Variance-only exponential confidence

Section 9 proves this is false even for a one-step bounded martingale. The linear scale/tail term is not a technical artifact.

### 13.3 Cross-deletion independence

Equation `(10.5)` keeps every conditional covariance. No claim is made that the deletion influences are conditionally independent merely because the original sample was iid.

The Gaussian-mixture theorem in Section 8 is separately labeled with its stronger conditional sub-Gaussian hypothesis and is not inferred from `(5.1)` alone.

```text

```

with the same replacement copies used for `B` and `G`, and

```text
C_r(B,G)
= sum_(|I|=r) E <Delta_I B, Delta_I G>.
```

Let

```text
B=EB+sum_A B_A,
G=EG+sum_A G_A
```

be Hilbert-valued Hoeffding decompositions. If `I` is not a subset of `A`, `Delta_I B_A=0`. If `I subseteq A` and `|I|=r`, expanding the `2^r` replacement terms shows that only matching replacement patterns survive after conditional centering, so

```text
E<Delta_I B_A,Delta_I G_A>

```

```text
C_r(B,G)

```

