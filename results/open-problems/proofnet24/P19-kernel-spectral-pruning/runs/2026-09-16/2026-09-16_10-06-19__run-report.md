# 4. Primary concentration input audited this run

Freshly read:

**David Janz, “Self-normalised Bennett inequalities for Hilbert-valued martingales,” arXiv:2608.15874v1, 16 Aug 2026.**

Primary HTML: `https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15874`.

The source theorem applies to a separable real Hilbert-space martingale `M_j` with increments `X_j`, `||X_j||<=1`, and predictable covariance

\[
V_j=\sum_{\ell\le j}
E[X_\ell\otimes X_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{\ell-1}].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Its fixed-regularisation Bernstein relaxation states that, for every `rho>0` and `y>=0`, with probability at least `1-e^{-y}` simultaneously over time,

\[
\left\|(V_j+\rho I)^{-1/2}M_j\right\|
\le
\sqrt{2(y+\ell_\rho(V_j))}
+
\frac{y+\ell_\rho(V_j)}{3\sqrt\rho},
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
\ell_\rho(V)
=
\frac12\log\det(I+\rho^{-1}V).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The source also records the Gaussian analogue: if for every Hilbert vector `theta`

\[
E\!\left[
\exp\{\langle\theta,X_j\rangle\}
\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}
\right]
\le
\exp\left\{
\frac12\langle\theta,W_j\theta\rangle
\right\},
\tag{4.4}
\]

with predictable positive trace-class `W_j`, then the quadratic/log-determinant Gaussian-mixture potential is a nonnegative supermartingale.

The paper explicitly warns that merely having scalar directional sub-Bennett/sub-gamma inequalities does **not** suffice to replace the bounded-increment Hilbert Bennett potential. P19 therefore keeps the two routes separate: bounded Hilbert increments, or the stronger common-operator full-vector sub-Gaussian MGF `(4.4)`.

# 5. `P19-REPLACEMENT-DOOB-HILBERT-42` — PROVED

## 5.1 Setup

Work in the real Hilbert space of signal matrices with Frobenius inner product. Complex matrices, if needed, are regarded as a real Hilbert space; no argument uses complex scalar order.

Let

\[
Z=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)
\tag{5.1}
\]

have independent coordinates, and let

\[
F=F(Z)\in\mathcal H,
\qquad EF=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Set

\[
\mathcal F_j=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_j),
\qquad
M_j=E[F\mid\mathcal F_j],
\qquad
D_j=M_j-M_{j-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For an independent replacement `Z_j'`, let `Z^{(j)}` replace only coordinate `j`, and define

\[
R_j=F(Z)-F(Z^{(j)}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_j=E[R_j\mid\mathcal F_j].
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Proof of `(5.5)`

Fix the prefix through `j-1`. Write the remaining future coordinates as `U` and

\[
g(z)=E_U F(Z_{<j},z,U).
\]

Then

\[
M_j=g(Z_j),
\qquad
M_{j-1}=E_{Z_j'}g(Z_j'),
\]

while conditioning `R_j` on `\mathcal F_j` averages both the common future `U` and the independent replacement `Z_j'`. Hence

\[
E[R_j\mid\mathcal F_j]
=g(Z_j)-E_{Z_j'}g(Z_j')=D_j.
\]

## 5.2 Predictable covariance reduction

Define

\[
q_j
=
E[\|R_j\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}].
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
E[\|D_j\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
\le\frac12q_j.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the prefix. With the notation above and an iid copy `Z_j'`, the Hilbert variance identity gives

\[
E\|g(Z_j)-Eg(Z_j)\|^2
=
\frac12E\|g(Z_j)-g(Z_j')\|^2.
\tag{5.8}
\]

By Jensen over the common future,

\[
\|g(Z_j)-g(Z_j')\|^2
\le
E_U\|F(Z_{<j},Z_j,U)-F(Z_{<j},Z_j',U)\|^2.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Taking the remaining conditional expectation proves `(5.7)`.

If, pathwise,

\[
\|R_j\|\le c_j,
\tag{5.10}
\]

then `(5.5)` and conditional Jensen give

\[
\boxed{\|D_j\|\le c_j.}
\tag{5.11}
\]

Finally, if the replacement difference has the full-vector conditional sub-Gaussian MGF

\[
E[e^{\langle H,R_j\rangle}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
\le
\exp\left\{
\frac12\langle H,W_jH\rangle
\right\}
\quad\forall H\in\mathcal H,
\tag{5.12}
\]

then Jensen applied to `(5.5)` yields

\[
\boxed{
E[e^{\langle H,D_j\rangle}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
\le
\exp\left\{
\frac12\langle H,W_jH\rangle
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

Thus both the second-moment and the strong vector-MGF hypotheses transfer legally from coordinate replacements to the Doob martingale.

# 6. `P19-GHOST-HILBERT-BERNSTEIN-42` — PROVED

## 6.1 Ghost construction

Let

\[
P=A(S,\Theta),
\qquad
P_0=A(S',\Theta),
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

- `S=(Z_1,...,Z_N)` has iid coordinates;
- `S'` is an independent copy;
- `Theta` is exogenous procedure randomness;
- conditional on `Theta`, both applications use the **same** procedure randomness;
- the source legality condition is handled separately: when `S'` and `Theta` are independent of the RMT noise object, `P_0` is a candidate source-legal proxy.

Conditional on `Theta`, define

\[
F=P-P_0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because `P` and `P_0` are iid conditional on `Theta`,

\[
E[F\mid\Theta]=0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Reveal the `2N` coordinates of `(S,S')`. Apply Section 5 to the terminal martingale `M_{2N}=F`.

Suppose every coordinate replacement obeys

\[
\|R_j\|_F\le c
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

and define `q_j` by `(5.6)`. Let

\[
V_{2N}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{2N}
E[D_j\otimes D_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}].
\tag{6.5}
\]

By `(5.7)` and the identity `tr(x\otimes x)=||x||^2`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}V_{2N}
\le
\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{2N}q_j.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Assume first that

\[
\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{2N}q_j\le Q
\tag{6.7}
\]

almost surely for deterministic `Q>0`.

## 6.2 Application of the Hilbert Bernstein theorem

Normalize `D_j` by `c`. The normalized increments have norm at most one and normalized predictable covariance

\[
V'=V_{2N}/c^2.
\]

Choose

\[
\rho=Q/c^2.
\tag{6.8}
\]

On `(6.7)`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}V'\le\rho,
\qquad
\|V'\|_{\rm op}\le\rho.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Because `log(1+x)<=x`, the trace-class spectral expansion gives

\[
\ell_\rho(V')
=\frac12\sum_k\log(1+\lambda_k(V')/\rho)
\le
\frac{\operatorname{tr}V'}{2\rho}
\le\frac12.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Set

\[
y=\log(1/\delta),
\qquad
L=y+\frac12.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Janz's Bernstein boundary yields, with probability at least `1-delta`,

\[
\|(V'+\rho I)^{-1/2}(F/c)\|
\le
\sqrt{2L}+\frac{L}{3\sqrt\rho}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

But

\[
V'+\rho I
=\frac1{c^2}(V_{2N}+QI),
\]

so the left side of `(6.12)` is exactly

\[
\|(V_{2N}+QI)^{-1/2}F\|.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Moreover

\[
\|V_{2N}+QI\|_{\rm op}\le2Q.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Multiplying `(6.12)` by `sqrt(2Q)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\|P-P_0\|_F
\le
2\sqrt{QL}
+
\frac{\sqrt2}{3}cL.
}
\tag{6.15}
\]

Since operator norm is bounded by Frobenius norm,

\[
\boxed{
\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}
\le
2\sqrt{QL}
+
\frac{\sqrt2}{3}cL.
}
\tag{6.16}
\]

This proves the theorem.

## 6.3 Random predictable variance upper bound

Suppose instead the cumulative predictable variance is certified only with

\[
P\left\{
\frac12\sum_jq_j>Q
\right\}\le\delta_Q.
\tag{6.17}
\]

Because each predictable covariance increment is `F_{j-1}`-measurable, stop before the first increment that would push `tr V_j` above `Q`. Janz's theorem is time-uniform, so the bound applies to the stopped martingale. On the event in which the terminal predictable trace never exceeds `Q`, the stopped and unstopped terminal martingales agree. Therefore `(6.15)`–`(6.16)` hold with probability at least

\[
1-\delta-\delta_Q.
\tag{6.18}
\]

This is the legal way to use a predictable variance UCB; one does not condition on a future-dependent good event and then pretend the martingale law is unchanged.

## 8.1 Why same-trained-matrix pruning remains legal

If each trained/pruned row is `k`-sparse, the difference of two independently support-selected retrainings is supported on the union of two sets of size at most `k`, hence is `2k`-sparse. Therefore a simultaneous `2k` restricted Gram floor supplies `(8.2)` **after** the support was adaptively selected from the same trained object. No independence between support and `G_psi` is needed if the floor is simultaneous over the class.

If an exact score-margin theorem locks the support under the relevant replacement, the difference support stays inside one `k`-set and the required restricted order drops from `2k` to `k`.

A fixed-support concentration theorem evaluated only after selecting the support from the same data remains invalid unless a selection/dependence cost is paid. The inherited argmin example and adaptive BBP counterexample remain active.

# 10. `P19-MEAN-SQUARE-NOT-EXPONENTIAL-CE-42` — PROVED

The new exponential theorem genuinely needs a jump/MGF hypothesis beyond mean-square ghost closeness.

Let

\[
B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p),
\qquad
B'\ \text{an iid copy},
\tag{10.1}
\]

and define rank-one `2 x 2` signal matrices

\[
P(B)=u_Be_1^*,
\qquad
u_0=e_1,
\quad
u_1=e_2.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Every realization has

\[
\sigma_1(P(B))=1.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Thus there is no vanishing singular-floor pathology.

If `B=B'`, the ghost difference is zero. If `B!=B'`,

\[
\|P(B)-P(B')\|_{\rm op}
=
\|P(B)-P(B')\|_F
=\sqrt2.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
P\{\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}=\sqrt2\}
=2p(1-p),
\tag{10.5}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
E\|P-P_0\|_F^2
=4p(1-p)\to0.
}
\tag{10.6}
\]

Suppose one tried to infer from the variance scale alone a universal constant-threshold sub-Gaussian bound of the form

\[
P\{\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}\ge1\}
\le
\exp\{-c/Q_p\},
\qquad
Q_p\asymp p.
\tag{10.7}
\]

The left side is asymptotic to `2p`, whereas the right side is `exp(-Theta(1/p))`, so `(10.7)` fails for all sufficiently small `p`.

Numerical sanity checks used only to test the algebra, not as proof: for `p=0.005`, the tail is `0.00995` and `Q=4p(1-p)=0.0199`; the illustrative `exp[-1/(8Q)]` is about `0.00187`. For `p=0.002`, the tail is `0.003992` while the same illustrative exponential is about `1.59e-7`.

Thus the entering Markov theorem cannot be upgraded to exponential confidence from second moments alone. A bounded jump scale, a common-operator conditional MGF, or another explicit tail mechanism is structurally necessary.

# 11. Separation of spectral and statistical layers

This run preserves the required distinctions.

## 11.1 Eigenvalue / singular-value distribution

Global Marchenko–Pastur ESD, including a possible zero atom when the ambient Gram is rectangular/singular, is a statement about the empirical singular-value distribution. It does not certify a sparse restricted floor and does not imply singular-vector isotropy after adaptive training/pruning.

## 11.2 Extreme and restricted singular values

A simultaneous sparse-RIP/restricted-Gram event is an extreme-singular-value statement on the structured difference class. It is exactly what converts prediction-scale replacement energy into coefficient/matrix energy in `(8.3)`.

A global hard edge at zero can coexist with a strong `2k` restricted lower edge when `k log(d/k)=o(M)`.

## 11.3 Singular-vector overlap

The August source overlap/projector formulas come from compressed resolvents/subspace local laws and contour residues. They are not Davis–Kahan or Wedin consequences. The ghost theorem only supplies a legal bridge from an adaptive frame to a source-legal frame on which that resolvent theorem applies.

## 11.4 Prediction risk

Prediction/operator-learning stability enters through the fresh-probe influence `beta_j^2` in `(8.5)`. It becomes matrix/operator control only after a restricted feature floor.

## 11.5 Finite-width / finite-sample confidence

`Q_n`, `c_n` or the MGF proxy `W_j`, restricted floor failure, ghost singular-floor failure, representation debt, raw-risk tails and score-margin failures are finite-sample debts. None is absorbed into the asymptotic MP overlap constants.

# 12. Method comparison and rejected shortcuts

## 12.1 Resolvent methods

For singular-vector overlap, resolvent compression remains the primary source method. The correct adaptive-frame strategy is:

\[
\text{source-legal proxy SLL}
\ +\

## 12.2 Davis–Kahan / Wedin

Wedin/Hermitian dilation is used only when a **real protected singular gap** is already available and the perturbation is below that gap scale. It can stabilize a deployed cluster after identifiability has been established. It cannot generate BBP overlap constants or substitute for the source SLL.

## 12.3 Leave-one-out / ghost / predictable martingales

The new proof is the matrix-valued high-probability counterpart of the entering Efron–Stein ghost theorem. The crucial legal identity is `(5.5)`, which preserves the actual replacement dependence and permits Hilbert martingale concentration without treating retraining effects as independent summands.

## 12.4 Free probability / deterministic equivalents

A global MP/free-probability deterministic equivalent describes eigenvalue distributions and, in fixed-feature benchmarks, prediction-risk integrals. It does not by itself control a same-noise adaptive signal frame. The inherited adaptive rank-one counterexample remains decisive: one can preserve the global MP ESD while forcing a planted direction to coincide with the top noise singular vector.

## 12.5 Sparsification concentration

Fixed-support concentration after support selection is not enough. The legal route is a simultaneous restricted event over the full admissible support family, or a separate support-lock theorem. This is why trained-matrix pruning does not invalidate `(8.3)` but does invalidate an iid-mask shortcut.

# 14. Tests and falsification checks

1. **Hilbert constant check.** On `tr V<=Q`, choosing normalized regularisation `rho=Q/c^2` gives `ell<=1/2` and `||V+QI||op<=2Q`. The Janz Bernstein ellipsoid therefore gives exactly the constants in `(6.15)`; no hidden dimension factor appears.
2. **Zero-variance check.** If `Q=0`, every Doob increment has zero conditional second moment and hence is zero a.s.; the ghost displacement is zero. The theorem is interpreted separately in this degenerate case rather than dividing by `Q`.
3. **Two-copy factor check.** The Doob covariance trace has the `1/2` Efron–Stein factor. With symmetric original/ghost coordinate prediction envelopes `l_i^2`, the two copies cancel that factor, yielding `Q<=a^{-1}sum_i l_i^2`, not an erroneous extra factor of two.
4. **Rare-jump falsification.** The rank-one Bernoulli example keeps `sigma_1(P0)=1`, so its failure of exponential-from-variance is genuinely a tail obstruction rather than loss of identifiability.
5. **Adaptive-support legality.** The simultaneous `2k` Gram event is quantified before support selection and therefore remains valid for supports selected from the same matrix. No conditioning-on-selected-support step is used.
6. **Source legality check.** The actual adaptive `P` is never declared independent of the RMT noise. Only the independent-data ghost `P0`, when its exogenous randomness is also noise-independent, is proposed as a source-legal proxy.
