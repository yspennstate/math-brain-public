# 4. Exact target and structured feature notation

Let `P_0,P in F^{p x d}` (`F=R` or `C`) be rank-`r` matrices in the **same physical input/output coordinates**. `P_0` is the source-legal proxy and `P` the actual trained/pruned signal whose singular frame is to be certified.

The theorem below does not guess how the July manuscript identifies a trained neural layer with `P_n`; that source-specific identification remains an explicit obligation. It states the exact interface once such an identification is legal.

Let `\psi in F^d` be a fresh feature/probe vector with

\[
G=E[\psi\psi^*].
\tag{4.1}
\]

For each output row `i`, assume the support of row `i` of

\[
\Delta=P-P_0
\tag{4.2}
\]

belongs to a prescribed difference-support family `\mathfrak D`. Define

\[
a_{\mathfrak D}(G)
:=
\inf_{J\in\mathfrak D}\lambda_{\min}(G_{JJ}).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Examples:

- if `P,P_0` are ordinary rowwise `k`-sparse, every difference row is supported on at most `2k` coordinates, so `\mathfrak D={J:|J|\le2k}`;
- if a deterministic score-margin certificate locks the same rowwise support in `P` and `P_0`, one may use `|J|\le k`;
- for block / N:M / group sparsity, `\mathfrak D` is the union/difference family of the permitted physical supports. No Bernoulli mask model is needed.

Define the operator-learning prediction distance

\[
d_G(P,P_0)^2
:=
E\|\Delta\psi\|^2
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta G\Delta^*).
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a distance between deployed operators on fresh features. It is distinct from the target-risk difference `R(P)-R(P_0)`: equal scalar risks do not in general determine `d_G`.

# 5. Main theorem: structured prediction stability implies relative signal stability

## `P19-RISK-TO-RELATIVE-SLL-40` — PROVED

Assume

\[
a:=a_{\mathfrak D}(G)>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}
\le
\frac{d_G(P,P_0)}{\sqrt a}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If, in addition,

\[
g=\sigma_r(P_0)>0,
\qquad
q:=\frac{d_G(P,P_0)}{g\sqrt a}<1,
\tag{5.3}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\|UU^*-U_0U_0^*\|_{\rm op},
\ \|VV^*-V_0V_0^*\|_{\rm op}
\le q,
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and the aligned actual signal frame satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm actual}(K)
\le
\varepsilon_0(K)+2R_Kh(q).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular, if

\[
\varepsilon_0(K)\to0,
\qquad
\frac{d_G(P,P_0)^2}{a g^2}\to0,
\tag{5.6}
\]

then the actual adaptive frame obeys the same SLL. Under the remaining source hypotheses (weighted compatibility and a stable outlier contour), Theorem 3.7 applies to the actual signal and transfers the left/right/cross singular-projector conclusions.

### Proof

Write the `i`th row of `\Delta` as `\delta_i^*`, with support `J_i in \mathfrak D`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_G(P,P_0)^2
&=\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta G\Delta^*)\\
&=\sum_i\delta_i^*G\delta_i\\
&=\sum_i\delta_{i,J_i}^*G_{J_iJ_i}\delta_{i,J_i}\\
&\ge a\sum_i\|\delta_i\|^2\\
&=a\|\Delta\|_F^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\|\Delta\|_{\rm op}
\le\|\Delta\|_F
\le d_G(P,P_0)/\sqrt a,
\]

which proves (5.2). If (5.3) holds, the entering theorem `P19-RANKR-PROJECTOR-GAP-39` applies with

\[
e/g\le q<1,
\]

giving (5.4). `P19-BASISFREE-PROCRUSTES-39` then gives an aligned frame distance at most `h(q)`, and `P19-RELATIVE-SIGNAL-SLL-TRANSFER-39` gives (5.5). This proves the theorem. `\square`

### Singular-value floor preservation

Weyl's singular-value inequality also gives

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_r(P)\ge g-\|P-P_0\|_{op}\ge g(1-q).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Thus the same certificate simultaneously prevents the retained signal floor from collapsing. If a proxy spike is separated from the BBP threshold by an explicit nonzero strength margin, (5.2) also supplies the needed singular-strength perturbation bound; this is a separate scalar-strength condition from the SLL frame condition.

### Kernel/operator-learning interpretation

For deployed predictors

\[
f_P(x)=P\psi(x),
\qquad
f_{P_0}(x)=P_0\psi(x),
\]

one has exactly

\[
d_G(P,P_0)=\|f_P-f_{P_0}\|_{L^2(P_X)}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Hence any legal leave-one-out, cross-fit, independent-half, or fresh-probe theorem that upper-bounds the **prediction distance itself** can feed (5.3). A mere comparison of their risks against labels does not suffice without another argument controlling their prediction difference.

# 6. Finite structured-sparsity certificate with adaptive support

## `P19-SPARSE-PROBE-RELATIVE-SLL-40` — PROVED

Let

\[
Z\in\mathbb F^{M\times d},
\qquad
\widehat G=M^{-1}Z^*Z,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and define the observed probe discrepancy

\[
\widehat d^2
:=
M^{-1}\|Z\Delta^*\|_F^2
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\Delta\widehat G\Delta^*).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Suppose the realized Gram satisfies the simultaneous structured floor

\[
\widehat a
:=
\inf_{J\in\mathfrak D}
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G_{JJ})
>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then, **pathwise**, even if `P`, `P_0`, all coefficients and all selected supports are measurable functions of this same `Z`,

\[
\boxed{
\|P-P_0\|_{op}
\le
\widehat d/\sqrt{\widehat a}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently, with

\[
\widehat q
=
\frac{\widehat d}{g\sqrt{\widehat a}},
\tag{6.5}
\]

`\widehat q<1` implies the projector and SLL bounds (5.4)–(5.5) with `q` replaced by `\widehat q`.

### Proof

Repeat (5.7) with `G` replaced by the realized `\widehat G`. The inequality is simultaneous over every admissible support and every coefficient vector. Therefore no conditioning or independence between `\Delta` and `Z` is needed once (6.3) holds. `\square`

### Why this addresses trained-matrix pruning

A fixed-support concentration statement is **not** enough when pruning chooses its support from the same realized matrix. Conditioning on a data-selected support changes its law. By contrast, (6.3) is a uniform event over the entire structured support class, so it remains valid after arbitrary deterministic or random trained-matrix selection.

This is exactly why an iid Bernoulli pruning mask is not introduced anywhere in the proof.

# 7. Gaussian / MP-null certificate

## `P19-GAUSSIAN-RIP-RELATIVE-SLL-40` — PROVED

Take a real iid Gaussian probe

\[
Z_{ab}\stackrel{iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\qquad Z\in\mathbb R^{M\times d}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For ordinary rowwise `k`-sparsity let

\[
s=2k
\tag{7.2}
\]

or `s=k` under exact support lock. For any `0<\delta<1`,

\[
\Pr\left\{
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\|\widehat G_{JJ}-I\|_{op}>\delta
\right\}
\le
2\exp\left[
 s\log\frac{9ed}{s}
 -\frac{M\delta^2}{32}
\right].
\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore if

\[
\boxed{
M
\ge
\frac{32}{\delta^2}
\left[
 s\log\frac{9ed}{s}
 +\log\frac2\eta
\right],
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

then with probability at least `1-\eta`, simultaneously over every selected support,

\[
(1-\delta)I\preceq\widehat G_{JJ}\preceq(1+\delta)I,
\qquad |J|\le s.
\tag{7.5}
\]

On this event,

\[
\boxed{
\|P-P_0\|_{op}
\le
\frac{\widehat d}{\sqrt{1-\delta}},
\qquad
\widehat q
\le
\frac{\widehat d}{g\sqrt{1-\delta}}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

### Proof of (7.3)

Fix one support `J` of size `s` and one unit vector `x`. Then

\[
M^{-1}\|Z_Jx\|^2
\stackrel d=\chi_M^2/M.
\]

For `0<t<1`, the elementary chi-square Chernoff bound gives

\[
P\left\{
\left|M^{-1}\|Z_Jx\|^2-1\right|>t
\right\}
\le2e^{-Mt^2/8}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Let `\mathcal N_J` be a `1/4`-net of the unit sphere in `\mathbb R^s`, with

\[
|\mathcal N_J|\le9^s.
\]

For a symmetric matrix `A`, the standard net inequality gives

\[
\|A\|_{op}
\le2\sup_{x\in\mathcal N_J}|x^*Ax|.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Apply (7.7) with `t=\delta/2` and union-bound over the net and over

\[
\binom ds\le(ed/s)^s
\]

supports. This gives (7.3). Smaller supports are contained in an `s`-support, so their principal submatrices inherit the same bound. `\square`

## `P19-MP-HARDEDGE-SPARSE-SEPARATION-40` — PROVED

If

\[
d/M\to\gamma>1,
\tag{7.9}
\]

then `\widehat G=Z^*Z/M` has at least `d-M` exact zero eigenvalues at every finite dimension and its global ESD converges to the MP law with zero atom `1-1/\gamma`.

Nevertheless, if

\[
s\log(ed/s)=o(M),
\tag{7.10}
\]

one may take `\delta=\delta_M\downarrow0` slowly enough in (7.3), obtaining

\[
\inf_{|J|\le s}\lambda_{min}(\widehat G_{JJ})
\to1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Thus a global MP hard edge / zero atom does **not** imply failure of the pruning-relevant sparse identifiability floor.

This separates five layers that must not be conflated:

1. **global eigenvalue distribution:** MP ESD and its zero atom;
2. **restricted extreme singular values:** sparse-RIP / restricted Gram floors;
3. **singular-vector overlap:** source holomorphic SLL plus contour residues;
4. **prediction/operator distance:** `d_G` or `\widehat d`;
5. **finite-width probability:** RIP failure, SLL rate, LOO/cross-fit and tail debts.

\]

Then both matrices have rank one and

\[
g=\sigma_1(P_0)=1,
\qquad
\|P-P_0\|_{op}=\epsilon<g.
\tag{9.3}
\]

But

\[
(P-P_0)\psi=0
\quad\text{almost surely},
\tag{9.4}
\]

so

\[
d_G(P,P_0)=0.
\tag{9.5}
\]

The right singular line of `P_0` is `span(e_1)`, while the right singular line of `P` is

\[
\operatorname{span}\big((1,\epsilon)^T\big).
\]

Their projector distance is

\[
\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2}}>0.
\tag{9.6}
\]

Thus even **identical fresh predictions** do not imply singular-vector stability when the probe Gram is singular on a pruning-relevant direction. The restricted floor in Section 5 is not proof slack.

This is the same structural phenomenon as the inherited duplicate-feature counterexample, now written directly at the source-signal/SLL interface.

# 10. Why same-trained-matrix pruning invalidates iid-mask shortcuts

The present theorem does not assume an iid mask.

A fixed deterministic support `J` may satisfy a concentration theorem. After selecting

\[
J=J(Z)
\]

from the same matrix, one may not condition on that selected `J` and reuse the fixed-support law as though selection were independent.

A one-line exact illustration is already enough. If `T_1,...,T_d` are iid continuous column scores with CDF `F` and the learned rule chooses

\[
J=\arg\min_jT_j,
\]

then

\[
P\{T_J\le t\}
=1-(1-F(t))^d,
\tag{10.1}
\]

not `F(t)`. For Gaussian probe columns, taking `T_j=M^{-1}\|Z_{:j}\|^2` gives the same selection bias in a concrete Gram statistic.

The simultaneous event (7.5) is precisely what survives adaptive selection. It is proved before and uniformly over every admissible support, so the trained matrix may select the support afterwards.

The inherited rank-one adaptive BBP counterexample remains decisive at the resolvent layer: aligning a spike to the top singular pair of the same Gaussian noise preserves the global MP ESD while changing the extreme singular value for every positive spike and making the planted overlap identically one. Therefore an MP-looking bulk is not evidence that the trained signal frame behaves like an independent spike.

# 12. Method comparison and route discipline

## 12.1 Resolvent/SLL methods

**Primary route for source BBP singular-vector overlap.** The August theorem is explicitly a compressed resolvent/Riesz-projector theorem. Once the actual adaptive frame is aligned to a legal proxy frame at `o(1)` cost, the SLL transfers pathwise. This is where the present prediction-to-relative-signal theorem plugs in.

## 12.2 Davis–Kahan / Wedin

Use only after a genuine identification gap exists. The direct rank-`r` projector bound (3.1) uses the proxy's nonzero singular floor `g`; the inherited deployed-matrix Wedin/Hermitian-dilation theorem uses a protected observed singular-cluster gap only after an operator perturbation has been certified.

Neither theorem produces the MP overlap coefficients `alpha_L,alpha_R`. Those coefficients remain resolvent residues.

## 12.3 Leave-one-out / cross-fit

LOO and cross-fit remain the strongest current route to a legal **prediction-distance** certificate for a learned representation. The inherited P19 chain supplies clipped/raw LOO risk bounds, predictable-variance refinements, ghost calibration and score-margin support-lock theorems. The new result identifies exactly what such work must prove to reach the SLL:

\[
d_G(P,P_0)=o(g\sqrt a).
\tag{12.1}
\]

A small absolute prediction debt is insufficient if either the feature restricted floor `a` or retained signal floor `g` vanishes.

## 12.4 Free probability / global deterministic equivalents

A global MP/free-probability description of the eigenvalue distribution is not enough for the adaptive frame. The inherited same-noise rank-one counterexample preserves the bulk law while destroying the independent-spike BBP vector law.

A deterministic equivalent for `d_G`, by contrast, is useful if its hypotheses genuinely cover the proxy/actual learned representation. P15's current report makes clear that same-sample learned features and half/full sample movement cannot simply be replaced by fixed random features.

## 12.5 Sparsification concentration

This run provides the correct concentration interface: **simultaneous restricted Gram control**, not a typical fixed support and not an iid pruning mask. Under Gaussian probes the entropy price is exactly the support/net term in (7.3).

## 12.6 Counterexamples

Retained route-kills include:

- MP ESD does not imply vector isotropy;
- same-noise adaptive rank-one signal can preserve MP bulk and violate BBP overlap;
- prediction equality does not imply coefficient/vector stability without restricted identifiability;
- absolute signal closeness does not imply SLL transfer when `sigma_r(P_0)` vanishes;
- pre-pruning stability does not imply post-hard-pruning stability without score-boundary control;
- small RMS replacement plus low reused training error does not imply low population risk;
- clipped covariance alone does not upper-control unbounded raw risk;
- fixed-support concentration cannot be silently conditioned through trained support selection.
