## Main theorem

### `P19-RELATIVE-SIGNAL-SLL-TRANSFER-39` — **PROVED**

Let

\[
P_0=U_0\Theta_0V_0^*,\qquad
P=U\Theta V^*
\]

be two rectangular matrices of the same rank `r`, with orthonormal thin SVD frames. Put

\[
g:=\sigma_r(P_0)>0,
\qquad
e:=\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}.
\]

Assume `e<g`. Then the left and right signal subspaces obey

\[
\boxed{
\|UU^*-U_0U_0^*\|_{\rm op}
\le {e\over g},
\qquad
\|VV^*-V_0V_0^*\|_{\rm op}
\le {e\over g}.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Let

\[
h(s):=\sqrt{2-2\sqrt{1-s^2}},\qquad 0\le s<1.
\]

There exist unitary/orthogonal matrices `Q_L,Q_R` such that

\[
\boxed{
\max\{\|U-U_0Q_L\|_{\rm op},
       \|V-V_0Q_R\|_{\rm op}\}
\le h(e/g)
\le \sqrt2\,e/g.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Now use the same noise matrix `X_n` and its Hermitian linearization `mathcal X_n`. Suppose the proxy block frame

\[
\mathcal F=\operatorname{diag}(U_0,V_0)
\]

satisfies on a compact

\[
K\Subset\mathbb C\setminus[-b,b]
\]

the source holomorphic SLL

\[
\sup_{\zeta\in K}
\|\mathcal F^*R_X(\zeta)\mathcal F-\mathcal M(\zeta)\|_{\rm op}
\le\varepsilon_0,
\]

where

\[
R_X(\zeta)=(\zeta I-\mathcal X_n)^{-1},
\qquad
R_K:=\sup_{\zeta\in K}\|R_X(\zeta)\|_{\rm op}.
\]

Then for the **actual, possibly same-noise adaptive** frame

\[
\mathcal E=\operatorname{diag}(U,V)
\]

one has pathwise

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\zeta\in K}
\|\mathcal E^*R_X(\zeta)\mathcal E-\mathcal M(\zeta)\|_{\rm op}
\le
\varepsilon_0+2R_Kh(e/g)
\le
\varepsilon_0+2\sqrt2 R_K{e\over g}.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

No independence assumption on `P` is used in (0.1)--(0.3). Thus a legal proxy SLL plus the **relative signal perturbation certificate**

\[
\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}=o_{\mathbb P}(\sigma_r(P_0))
\tag{0.4}
\]

is sufficient to put the same-matrix trained/pruned frame inside the source singular-projector theorem.

For bounded actual spike strengths, the source weighted compatibility follows from the SLL as in the predecessor. Hence every stable outlier window inherits the August v1 left projector, right projector, and cross-projector conclusions.

## Hard-pruning corollary

If a score-margin/support-lock certificate shows that actual and proxy hard-pruned matrices use the same deterministic support `S`, and

\]

If both have rank `r` and

\[
\rho=o(\sigma_r(P_0)),
\]

then (0.3) transfers the proxy SLL to the actual trained/pruned frame. This gives the explicit chain

\[
\boxed{
\text{score margin/support lock}
\Rightarrow
\text{signal-matrix closeness}
\Rightarrow
\text{subspace alignment}
\Rightarrow
\text{SLL}
\Rightarrow
\text{source singular projectors}.

\tag{0.5}

The support may have been chosen from the trained matrix. No iid mask is introduced.

## Sharp obstruction

### `P19-ABSOLUTE-CLOSE-NOT-SLL-CE-39` — **PROVED**

Absolute signal-matrix closeness is not enough. Let `X_n` be Gaussian MP noise with top singular pair `(u_1,v_1)`, let `a_n,b_n` be any fixed deterministic unit vectors, and let `theta_n -> 0`. Put

\[
P_{0,n}=\theta_n a_nb_n^*,
\qquad
P_n=\theta_n u_1v_1^*.
\]

Then

\[
\|P_n-P_{0,n}\|_{\rm op}\le2\theta_n\to0,
\]

and the proxy rank-one frame is source-legal, but the actual frame is the top noise singular pair. Its compressed resolvent has the order-one off-diagonal term

\[
{s_1\over \zeta^2-s_1^2},
\]

so the scalar MP SLL fails exactly as in `P19-ADAPTIVE-BBP-COLLAPSE-CE-38`. Here the proxy singular floor equals `theta_n`, so the perturbation is not small **relative** to the identifying signal scale.

Therefore the relative denominator in (0.4) is structural, not a cosmetic proof artifact.

# 4. Signal perturbation to left/right projector control

## `P19-RANKR-PROJECTOR-GAP-39` — PROVED

Let `P_0,P` have the same finite rank `r`. Denote their left range projectors by

\[
\Pi_0=U_0U_0^*,
\qquad
\Pi=UU^*.
\]

Put

\[
g=\sigma_r(P_0),
\qquad e=\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}.
\]

Take any unit vector `y` in `Ran(P_0)`. Let

\[
z=P_0^\dagger y.
\]

Then

\[
P_0z=y,
\qquad
\|z\|\le {1\over g}.
\]

Because `Pz` lies in `Ran(P)`,

\[
(I-\Pi)Pz=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(I-\Pi)y\|
&=\|(I-\Pi)P_0z\|\\
&=\|(I-\Pi)(P_0-P)z\|\\
&\le {e\over g}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Taking the supremum over unit `y` in `Ran(P_0)` gives

\[
\|(I-\Pi)\Pi_0\|_{\rm op}\le e/g.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For equal-rank orthogonal projectors, the nonzero singular values of `(I-Pi)Pi_0` are the sines of the principal angles, and

\[
\|\Pi-\Pi_0\|_{\rm op}
=\|(I-\Pi)\Pi_0\|_{\rm op}
=\sin\theta_{\max}.
\]

Thus

\[
\|UU^*-U_0U_0^*\|_{\rm op}\le e/g.
\]

Apply the identical argument to `P^*` and `P_0^*`. Their nonzero singular values are the same, and their ranges are the right signal subspaces. Hence

\[
\|VV^*-V_0V_0^*\|_{\rm op}\le e/g.
\]

This proves (0.1).

### Scope

The same-rank hypothesis matters. The statement is a subspace-identification result across the rank-`r` gap from `sigma_r(P_0)` to the zero singular spectrum. It is an elementary Wedin-type sin-theta argument, proved here directly so no hidden gap or constant is imported.

# 5. Projector control to optimal block-Procrustes control

## `P19-BASISFREE-PROCRUSTES-39` — PROVED

Let `U,U_0` be `N x r` orthonormal frames spanning equal-rank subspaces. Let principal angles be

\[
0\le\theta_1\le\cdots\le\theta_r\le\pi/2.
\]

Then

\[
\|UU^*-U_0U_0^*\|_{\rm op}
=\sin\theta_r=:s.
\]

Write a singular-value decomposition

\[
U_0^*U=A\,\operatorname{diag}(\cos\theta_i)B^*.
\]

Choose

\[
Q=AB^*.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
(U-U_0Q)^*(U-U_0Q)
=B\,\operatorname{diag}(2-2\cos\theta_i)B^*.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|U-U_0Q\|_{\rm op}
=2\sin(\theta_r/2)
=h(s).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Moreover

\[
h(s)^2
=2(1-\sqrt{1-s^2})
={2s^2\over1+\sqrt{1-s^2}}
\le2s^2,
\]

so

\[
h(s)\le\sqrt2\,s.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Apply this separately on the left and right using (0.1). If `e<g`, then `s<1`, and there exist `Q_L,Q_R` with

\[
\delta
:=
\left\|
\operatorname{diag}(U,V)
-
\operatorname{diag}(U_0,V_0)
\operatorname{diag}(Q_L,Q_R)
\right\|_{\rm op}
\le h(e/g).
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is basis-invariant. Repeated singular values and arbitrary within-signal rotations are harmless.

# 8. Hard pruning and structured sparsity

Suppose the source signal matrix is concretely the deployed hard-pruned readout, or another explicitly identified structured low-rank matrix. Do **not** assume this architecture if the source does not expose it.

On a support-lock event with a common coordinate support `S`,

\[
P=H_S(C),
\qquad
P_0=H_S(C_0).
\]

Then

\[
\|P-P_0\|_{\rm op}
\le\|P-P_0\|_F
\le\|C-C_0\|_F.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Therefore an inherited score-margin theorem plus a retraining/readout perturbation certificate `rho` gives

\[
{e\over g}
\le
{\rho\over\sigma_r(P_0)}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

This identifies a new necessary diagnostic for pruning experiments:

\[
\boxed{
\text{not only support margin, but also the smallest retained nonzero signal singular value.}
}
\]

A support can be perfectly locked while its retained rank-`r` signal subspace is ill-conditioned because `sigma_r(P_0)` is tiny. In that regime singular-vector stability is not certified by small absolute weight movement.

If support is **not** locked, one may still apply the theorem whenever a direct operator certificate for `||P-P_0||` is available. Same support is sufficient, not necessary.

### Why iid masking remains invalid

The theorem conditions on the actual deterministic hard-pruned matrices and their matrix difference. It never samples an independent Bernoulli mask. A support selected from the trained matrix may have arbitrarily complicated dependence on the same noise; that dependence is paid through `e/g` and the proxy SLL rather than deleted by modeling fiat.

# 9. `P19-ABSOLUTE-CLOSE-NOT-SLL-CE-39` — full proof

Let

\[
X_n=\sum_j s_j u_jv_j^*
\]

be Gaussian MP noise. Let `a_n,b_n` be deterministic unit vectors and choose any positive `theta_n -> 0`.

Define

\[
P_{0,n}=\theta_na_nb_n^*,
\qquad
P_n=\theta_nu_1v_1^*.
\]

Both matrices have rank one and operator norm `theta_n`. Hence

\[
\|P_n-P_{0,n}\|_{\rm op}
\le\|P_n\|+\|P_{0,n}\|
=2\theta_n\to0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The proxy frame `(a_n,b_n)` is deterministic and therefore source-legal in the Gaussian/LSI model.

But the actual signal frame equals the top noise singular pair. In the Hermitian linearization, its two-dimensional subspace is invariant and

\[
\mathcal E_n^*(\zeta I-\mathcal X_n)^{-1}\mathcal E_n
=
{1\over\zeta^2-s_1^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
\zeta&s_1\\
s_1&\zeta
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The MP scalar deterministic equivalent has zero off-diagonal blocks. Therefore for fixed real `zeta>b` separated from the edge,

\[
\|\mathcal G_n(\zeta)-\mathcal M(\zeta)\|_{\rm op}
\ge
{s_1\over|\zeta^2-s_1^2|}
\to
{1+\sqrt c\over
 \zeta^2-(1+\sqrt c)^2}>0.
\tag{9.3}
\]

So the actual-frame SLL fails despite (9.1).

The proxy singular floor is

\[
g_n=\sigma_1(P_{0,n})=\theta_n,
\]

and the absolute perturbation is only known to be of the same order as `g_n`. Thus this example is exactly excluded by the relative condition `e/g -> 0`.

### Consequence

The phrase “the trained/pruned signal matrix is close to a legal proxy in operator norm” is not sufficient unless the comparison scale is specified. A vanishing absolute perturbation can rotate a vanishing-strength signal subspace arbitrarily and can expose maximally noise-adaptive directions.

# 10. Method comparison and adversarial proof audit

## Resolvent / Riesz projector

This remains the source-exact route to BBP-type singular-subspace limits. The present theorem supplies an explicit route from structured-pruning stability to the source holomorphic SLL.

## Davis--Kahan / Wedin

The new projector bound is precisely a rank-gap-to-zero sin-theta phenomenon, but it was derived directly. Its sufficient gap is explicit:

\[
\sigma_r(P_0)>\|P-P_0\|.
\]

No Davis--Kahan/Wedin theorem is invoked without such a gap. Later Wedin comparisons between **observed deployed matrices** still require their own protected singular-value gap and remain distinct from the source BBP overlap law.

## Leave-one-out / ghost replacement

These remain useful to prove score/support stability and matrix perturbation bounds such as `rho`. They do not by themselves make a learned frame independent of the full noise matrix. Their legitimate role in the present chain is to prove `e/g -> 0`.

## Deterministic equivalents / free probability

A scalar/global deterministic equivalent does not repair same-noise frame bias; the predecessor's adaptive top-singular-pair counterexample proves this. A matrix-valued equivalent may be appropriate when the true model is not scalar on the relevant subspace, but it still needs control on the actual selected frame.

## Sparsification concentration

A simultaneous coordinate-sparse RIP theorem can cover a support chosen from the same realized feature matrix because it is uniform over all supports in the class. That is different from an SLL on an arbitrary data-dependent spectral frame. The present relative-alignment theorem handles the latter geometrically.

## Adversarial audit of the new proof

The proof uses four hypotheses that cannot be silently weakened:

1. **same rank:** otherwise one-sided range control does not identify equal-dimensional projectors;
2. **positive proxy singular floor:** otherwise the pseudoinverse step has no finite identifying scale;
3. **relative error below one:** needed for nontrivial principal-angle/Procrustes control;

### Independent derivation

The Mehler operator

\[
(P_tg)(Z)=E[g(Z_t)\mid Z]
\]

acts on chaos degree `k` by multiplication by `t^k`. Orthogonality of different Wiener chaoses immediately gives (11.1).

Expand `R_t`. Since `Z_t` has the same Gaussian marginal law for every `t`,

\[
E\|g_{Z_t}\|^2
\]

is constant. Since `T` is independent of training,

\[
E\langle T,g_{Z_t}\rangle
=\langle ET,Eg\rangle
\]

is also constant. Thus the only `t`-dependent term is

\[
2E\langle b_Z,g_{Z_t}\rangle,

and (11.1) proves (11.2).

\[
A=E\langle b_Z,g_Z\rangle,
\qquad
C=E\langle b_Z,g_{Z_t}\rangle,

\[
E\langle b_Z-b_{Z_t},g_Z-g_{Z_t}\rangle
=A-C-C+A=2(A-C).
\]

The same quantity equals `R_same-R_t` by the preceding risk expansion, proving (11.3). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives P15's transfer bound.
