`Growing_rank_spiked_singular_vectors_and_singular_values__with_applications_to_pruning_deep_neural_networks.pdf`.

The source model is

W_n = X_n + P_n,
P_n = U_n Theta_n V_n^*,
rank(P_n)=r_n -> infinity,
r_n=o(n),
n/m -> c in (0,1].

Assumption 3.1 requires bounded spikes and, if P_n is random, independence of P_n from X_n. Assumption 3.2 requires an empirical singular-value law with compact right edge b, ||X_n||_op -> b, and a nondegenerate D-transform edge. For z>b, the key compressed blocks are

A_n(z)=U_n^* z(z^2-X_nX_n^*)^{-1}U_n,
D_n(z)=V_n^* z(z^2-X_n^*X_n)^{-1}V_n,
B_n(z)=U_n^* X_n(z^2-X_n^*X_n)^{-1}V_n.

Assumption 3.3 asks that these be close in operator norm to scalar transforms phi_n(z)I, phitilde_n(z)I, and 0 uniformly off the bulk. Section 4 upgrades this to complex contours. Theorem 4.3 then identifies cluster projector overlaps, and Theorem 4.4 identifies the mixed dyad overlap. Repeated or tightly clustered spikes are handled only through subspaces/projectors, not individually named singular vectors.

The manuscript explicitly states that the contour hypothesis is proved for rotationally invariant/Haar singular-vector noise, while the deterministic-frame independent-entry case is left as a local-law route (Theorem 4.9), not as a proved theorem. Its limitations section repeats that this is the missing random-matrix input.

The August editorial state confirms that this is the right bottleneck. Gmail `1a0179eae5a54e95`, RMTA manuscript `Mesoscopic additive spikes for rectangular random matrices`, reports that one referee's central objection was precisely that the random-matrix difficulty had been put into the abstract resolvent-compression assumption and had only been checked mainly in the Haar class; the second referee separately asked why singular vectors had not been treated. The July singular-vector manuscript is therefore directly aligned with the publication-level gap.

The current pruning paper, arXiv:2606.02608, also correctly distinguishes model and certificate: its Supposition 1 writes a trained layer as independent random perturbation plus structured signal, but explicitly calls this a modeling device rather than an assertion that trained ViT weights globally have that law. Its practical claims are backed by deterministic data-path certificates. This distinction is essential below.

## 2. Target chosen this run

### P19-TARGET-01 — independent-entry growing-block contour compression

Let X_n have independent centered entries normalized by E|X_ij|^2=1/m, n/m->c in (0,1], and let U_n,V_n be deterministic orthonormal spike frames of rank r_n. Prove the source Assumption 4.2 uniformly on any fixed contour family a positive distance from the Marchenko-Pastur singular support, ideally with the optimal block scale

beta_n^cc = O_P(sqrt(r_n/n))

for the full source range r_n=o(n).

This would discharge the model-dependent hypothesis behind the growing-rank singular-vector/projector theorems for ordinary iid rectangular noise. The source itself suggested a net over exp(O(r_n)) test vectors, but a standard anisotropic local law only gives polynomially high probability for each deterministic bilinear form; that is not enough for an exponential net once r_n grows faster than O(log n).

The main result of this run proves a substantial subrange without any exponential net.

## 3. New theorem: iid contour compression for r << sqrt(n)

### P19-IID-BLOCK-01

Fix kappa>0. Let X_n be n x m with n/m->c in (0,1], independent entries satisfying the Knowles-Yin sample-covariance hypotheses in the identity-population case:

E X_ij=0,
E|X_ij|^2=1/m,
and uniformly bounded moments of sqrt(m) X_ij of all orders (or, as Knowles-Yin note, sufficiently many fixed high moments after minor adjustments).

In the complex case also impose the usual zero second complex moment condition. Let U_n in F^{n x r_n}, V_n in F^{m x r_n} be deterministic orthonormal frames, independent of X_n, with

r_n <= n^{1/2-kappa}.

Let Gamma be a fixed finite union of rectifiable contours contained in {Re z>b+delta, |z|<=C}, or more generally a compact contour family whose squared image stays a fixed positive distance from the limiting sample-covariance support. Then, uniformly on Gamma,

||A_n(z)-phi_n(z)I||_op
+||D_n(z)-phitilde_n(z)I||_op
+||B_n(z)||_op
= O_prec(r_n/sqrt(n)).

In particular this tends to zero in probability for r_n<=n^{1/2-kappa}. Hence source Assumption 4.2 holds in this iid deterministic-frame regime. Consequently, every audited spike cluster whose deterministic outlier gap satisfies

Delta_n >> r_n/sqrt(n)

(up to the usual n^epsilon interpretation of stochastic domination and the source transform error) obeys the source cluster-projector and mixed-dyad conclusions.

### Proof

Use the Hermitian/sample-covariance linearization. With w=z^2, Knowles-Yin's block matrix is

G_KY(w) = [ -I   X ; X^*   -w I ]^{-1}.

Its blocks satisfy, by Schur complement,

(G_KY)_{11}=w(XX^*-wI)^{-1},
(G_KY)_{22}=(X^*X-wI)^{-1},
(G_KY)_{12}=X(X^*X-wI)^{-1}.

Therefore, with the source convention

A(z)=z(z^2-XX^*)^{-1},
D(z)=z(z^2-X^*X)^{-1},
B(z)=X(z^2-X^*X)^{-1},

we have

A(z)=-z^{-1}(G_KY)_{11}(z^2),
D(z)=-z(G_KY)_{22}(z^2),
B(z)=-(G_KY)_{12}(z^2).

Knowles-Yin's anisotropic local law controls every deterministic generalized entry of G_KY minus its deterministic equivalent. Off the limiting spectrum, Theorem 3.7/Remark 3.17 extends the estimate to the real axis, and on a fixed positive-distance contour the scalar error is O_prec(n^{-1/2}). Their averaged local law gives the scalar empirical-transform error at least as strongly, O_prec(n^{-1}) on such a fixed domain.

Let u_1,...,u_r and v_1,...,v_r denote the columns of U,V. Apply the scalar anisotropic bound to the deterministic embedded pairs (u_a,u_b), (v_a,v_b), and (u_a,v_b). Stochastic domination is stable under a polynomial union, and there are only O(r_n^2)<=O(n) such pairs in the stated range. Thus, uniformly on Gamma,

max_{a,b}|[U^*AU-phi I]_{ab}| = O_prec(n^{-1/2}),
max_{a,b}|[V^*DV-phitilde I]_{ab}| = O_prec(n^{-1/2}),
max_{a,b}|[U^*BV]_{ab}| = O_prec(n^{-1/2}).

For any r x r matrix M,

||M||_op <= ||M||_F <= r max_{a,b}|M_ab|.

Hence each compressed block is O_prec(r_n/sqrt(n)) in operator norm. Replacing the deterministic limiting scalar transforms by the empirical transforms costs only the averaged local-law error, which is lower order here. This proves the claim.

The key point is methodological: no exp(O(r_n)) sphere net is needed. The price is a nonoptimal extra sqrt(r_n) in the block norm, which limits this direct proof to r_n=o(sqrt n) rather than the desired r_n=o(n).

### Sharpness of the proof method, not of the theorem

The expected true block scale is sqrt(r_n/n), as in the rotationally invariant theorem. Entrywise anisotropic control plus Frobenius loses a factor sqrt(r_n). To reach all r_n=o(n), one needs a rank-uniform matrix-valued local law, concentration of the compressed resolvent as an r_n x r_n operator, or another argument exploiting correlation among the r_n^2 bilinear forms. A plain union bound over an epsilon-net requires exponentially strong tail bounds and is not supplied by the usual O_prec formulation.

## 4. Source correction: Appendix D contains a false fluctuation rate as written

Theorem 4.8 states the plausible Haar envelope

sqrt(r_n/n)+sqrt((log N_Gamma,n+s_n)/n).

But Appendix D says that a fixed-z Haar compression has operator-norm deviation

lesssim (r_n+t)/n

with failure probability e^{-t}. That sentence cannot be correct as written.

Already at r=1, let q be uniform on S^{n-1} and compress the deterministic diagonal matrix

D=diag(1,...,1,0,...,0)

with n/2 ones and n/2 zeros. Then

q^T D q ~ Beta(n/4,n/4),
Var(q^T D q)=1/(2n+4).

Equivalently, by the chi-square CLT,

sqrt(n)(q^TDq-1/2)

converges to a nondegenerate Gaussian. Thus a typical fluctuation is order n^{-1/2}, not n^{-1}. For fixed t=1, no constant multiple of (1+t)/n can contain the fluctuation with probability bounded away from zero as n grows.

So:

P19-SRC-FIX-01: the Appendix-D display/rate `(r_n+t)/n` is DISPROVED as stated.

This does not disprove Theorem 4.8. On the contrary, its stated square-root envelope is consistent with the correct Haar scale and is the rate the proof should establish. The appendix needs a corrected concentration calculation, not a change to the theorem statement.

## 5. Data-dependent pruning can preserve the MP bulk while destroying spike/vector laws

### P19-ADAPT-01 — exact counterexample

Let X have SVD

X=sum_j s_j u_j v_j^*,

and define a rank-one perturbation using the *observed noise singular vectors*

P(X)=theta u_1(X)v_1(X)^*, theta>0.

Then

W=X+P(X)

has exactly the same singular vectors as X and

s_1(W)=s_1(X)+theta.

For iid MP noise, s_1(X)->b=1+sqrt(c), so

s_1(W)->b+theta.

This is completely different from the independent-spike law. Under the independent rectangular spike model, the BBP threshold is theta_*=c^{1/4}; subcritical theta does not make a separated outlier, while a supercritical spike has location

rho(theta)=sqrt((1+theta^2)(c+theta^2))/theta,

not b+theta in general. At c=1, theta=1/2 is subcritical: the independent model leaves the top singular value at 2, whereas the adapted perturbation gives 2.5. At theta=2, the independent formula gives 2.5 while the adapted construction gives 4.

Yet rank(P)=1, so the empirical singular-value distribution is asymptotically unchanged by the perturbation. Thus one can have a perfect MP bulk null and maximally wrong extreme-singular-value/eigenvector conclusions at the same time.

This rigorously separates:

- ESD/bulk stability: rank control alone can suffice and does not require independence.
- Extreme singular values: independence or a replacement condition is essential.
- Singular-vector overlaps: dependence can change them maximally.
- Prediction/pruning safety: cannot follow from the MP ESD alone.

This is exactly why the current arXiv pruning paper is right to call independence a modeling device and to rely on deterministic data-path certificates for realized networks.

## 6. A dependence-transfer lemma for learned/adaptive frames

The counterexample says independence cannot simply be dropped. But a quantitative proxy condition is enough.

### P19-PROXY-01

Let R(z) be any operator family with sup_{z in Gamma}||R(z)||<=M. Let U and U0 be two orthonormal r-frames with

||U-U0||_op<=epsilon.

Then

sup_z ||U^*R(z)U-U0^*R(z)U0||_op <= 2M epsilon.

For rectangular blocks R_{12}(z), if also ||V-V0||_op<=epsilon_V, then

sup_z ||U^*R_{12}(z)V-U0^*R_{12}(z)V0||_op
<= M(epsilon_U+epsilon_V).

Proof: expand once in the left frame and once in the right frame and use submultiplicativity.

Consequently, if a trained/data-dependent spike frame admits a leave-one-out, sample-split, or other independent proxy frame whose operator-frame distance is o(Delta_n), then any contour-compression theorem for the proxy transfers to the learned frame at an additive O(epsilon_n) cost. This gives a mathematically exact way to turn the source independence assumption into a stability obligation rather than simply deleting it.

This is the natural interface with P15's same-sample kernel program: sample dependence is not fatal when there is a quantitative independent/cross-fitted proxy, but it invalidates naive population or independent-null substitution without such control.

## 7. Relation to current local-law literature

Knowles-Yin (arXiv:1410.3516, revised 2016) gives the required deterministic-vector anisotropic local law for sample covariance matrices with independent centered entries, variance normalization N^{-1}, and uniformly bounded moments of sqrt(N)X_ij of every order; the paper explicitly notes that a sufficiently large fixed number of moments can replace all moments after minor modifications. Its outside-spectrum theorem reaches the real axis and is exactly the primary input used in P19-IID-BLOCK-01.

A very recent extension by Ma and Misiakiewicz, arXiv:2609.09440 (8 September 2026), proves an optimal anisotropic local law for sample covariance matrices with independent columns whose coordinates may be strongly dependent, assuming optimal quadratic-form concentration, polynomial norm moments, and nondegenerate covariance. They explicitly list deep random features among the applications. This is highly relevant to the neural-feature side of P19, but it does not by itself prove the source rectangular additive-spike contour hypothesis at growing rank: one still needs the correct linearized/cross-block control and a growing-block upgrade. It is a promising route beyond iid entries.

The classical Benaych-Georges-Nadakuditi singular-vector/outlier theorem is finite-rank. It validates the D-transform architecture, but not the growing-rank block estimates that are the issue here.

Q=E[q(U)q(U)^T],

Q <= kappa^2 K

in Loewner order.

Independent proof: for a in R^n, define f_a=sum_i a_i k(x_i,.) in the RKHS. Then

a^T Q a=E f_a(U)^2.

|f_a(U)|^2 <= ||f_a||_H^2 k(U,U) <= kappa^2 ||f_a||_H^2.

A^{-1}QA^{-1} <= kappa^2 A^{-1}KA^{-1}

## 10. Next attack

The highest-value next step is the optimal block local law. Instead of an epsilon-net over the r-dimensional sphere, study the compressed resolvent

U^*(G-Pi)U

as a random r x r matrix and seek a Schatten/matrix-martingale or resolvent-cumulant estimate of order sqrt(r/n) directly. The desired statement is

E Tr[(U^*(G-Pi)U)^*(U^*(G-Pi)U)] = O(r^2/n)

plus enough noncommutative concentration to upgrade operator norm from the Frobenius scale r/sqrt(n) to sqrt(r/n). A second route is to exploit modern isotropic laws with Hilbert-Schmidt test observables, but one must check whether they control unnormalized rank-r compressions rather than only normalized traces.

For trained/adaptive pruning, attack a separate leave-one-out proxy lemma rather than pretending independence: if removing one minibatch or using an independent calibration split changes the learned singular subspace by epsilon_n=o(Delta_n), P19-PROXY-01 transports the independent-noise theorem. This is the cleanest bridge from the RMT manuscript to the actual DNN setting.
