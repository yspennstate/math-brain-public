## 3. New theorem P19-HAAR-REPAIR-01

Let \(\mathbb F=\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\). Let \(X_n\in\mathbb F^{n\times m}\), \(n\le m\), and suppose that on a good singular-value event
\[
X_n=Q_n\Sigma_nR_n^*
\]
where, conditional on \(\Sigma_n\),

* \(Q_n\in O(n)\) or \(U(n)\) is Haar,
* \(R_n\in O(m)\) or \(U(m)\) is Haar,
* \(Q_n,R_n\) are independent,
* both are independent of deterministic orthonormal spike frames
  \(U_n\in\mathbb F^{n\times r}\), \(V_n\in\mathbb F^{m\times r}\).

Let \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb C\) be a finite union of rectifiable contours satisfying
\[
0<z_{\min}\le |z|\le z_{\max}<\infty
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{dist}\bigl(\Gamma,\{\pm s_j(X_n):1\le j\le n\}\bigr)\ge\delta>0
\]
on the good event.

Choose an \(a_n\)-net \(\Gamma^\sharp\subset\Gamma\) with
\[
|\Gamma^\sharp|=N_{\Gamma,n}.
\]

Define
\[
L_{0,\Gamma}
=
\sup_{\substack{z\in\Gamma\\0\le s\le s_{\max}}}
\max\left\{
\left|\frac{z}{z^2-s^2}\right|,
\left|\frac{s}{z^2-s^2}\right|,
\frac1{|z|}
\right\},
\]
and
\[
L_{1,\Gamma}
=
\sup_{\substack{z\in\Gamma\\0\le s\le s_{\max}}}
\max\left\{
\left|\frac{z^2+s^2}{(z^2-s^2)^2}\right|,
\left|\frac{2zs}{(z^2-s^2)^2}\right|,
\frac1{|z|^2}
\right\},
\]
where \(s_{\max}\) is any deterministic bound valid on the good event. These constants are finite by the contour separation.

Let
\[
\phi_n(z)=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{z}{z^2-s_j^2},
\]
and
\[
\widetilde\phi_n(z)
=
\frac1m\left[
\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{z}{z^2-s_j^2}
+\frac{m-n}{z}
\right].
\]

Then there are universal constants \(C,c>0\), depending only on whether the field is real or complex, such that for every \(s\ge1\) with
\[
r+\log N_{\Gamma,n}+s\le c n,
\]
conditionally on \(\Sigma_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\Bigg(
\sup_{z\in\Gamma}
\Big[
&\|A_n(z)-\phi_n(z)I_r\|_{\rm op}\\
&+\|D_n(z)-\widetilde\phi_n(z)I_r\|_{\rm op}\\
&+\|B_n(z)\|_{\rm op}
\Big]\\
>
&C L_{0,\Gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{r+\log N_{\Gamma,n}+s}{n}}
+
C L_{1,\Gamma}a_n
\;\Big|\;\Sigma_n
\Bigg)
\le
C e^{-s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\beta_n^{cc}(\Gamma)
=
O_{\mathbb P}\!\left(
\sqrt{\frac{r_n}{n}}
+
\sqrt{\frac{\log N_{\Gamma,n}+s_n}{n}}

\right)

\tag{3.2}
\]
uniformly for
\[
r_n+\log N_{\Gamma,n}+s_n=o(n).
\]

\[

## 4. Proof of P19-HAAR-REPAIR-01

### 4.1 Exact conditional diagonalization

Conditional on \(\Sigma_n\), write its nonzero singular values as
\[
s_1,\ldots,s_n.
\]

For the left block,
\[
z(z^2-X_nX_n^*)^{-1}
=
Q_n H_L(z)Q_n^*,
\]
where
\[
H_L(z)=
\operatorname{diag}
\left(
\frac{z}{z^2-s_1^2},
\ldots,
\frac{z}{z^2-s_n^2}
\right).
\]
Thus
\[
A_n(z)=U_n^*Q_nH_L(z)Q_n^*U_n,
\qquad
\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}H_L(z)=\phi_n(z).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the right block,
\[
z(z^2-X_n^*X_n)^{-1}
=
R_nH_R(z)R_n^*,
\]
where
\[
H_R(z)=
\operatorname{diag}
\left(
\frac{z}{z^2-s_1^2},\ldots,
\frac{z}{z^2-s_n^2},
\underbrace{\frac1z,\ldots,\frac1z}_{m-n}
\right),
\]
hence
\[
D_n(z)=V_n^*R_nH_R(z)R_n^*V_n,
\qquad
\frac1m\operatorname{Tr}H_R(z)=\widetilde\phi_n(z).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Finally
\[
X_n(z^2-X_n^*X_n)^{-1}
=
Q_n C(z)R_n^*,
\]
where \(C(z)\) is rectangular diagonal with entries
\[
C_{jj}(z)=\frac{s_j}{z^2-s_j^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
B_n(z)=U_n^*Q_nC(z)R_n^*V_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]

On the good contour event,
\[
\|H_L(z)\|,\ \|H_R(z)\|,\ \|C(z)\|
\le L_{0,\Gamma}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### 4.2 Primary concentration input

The only probabilistic input is concentration of Lipschitz functions on a high-dimensional sphere.

For a uniform \(Y\in S^{N-1}\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz real-valued function \(f\), the standard Lévy concentration estimate around the mean gives
\[
\Pr\{|f(Y)-\mathbb Ef(Y)|\ge Lt\}
\le
e^\pi e^{-Nt^2/4}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For the complex sphere one identifies \(\mathbb C^N\) with \(\mathbb R^{2N}\), yielding the same estimate with real dimension \(2N\), so the real bound is conservative for both fields.

If \(H=H^*\) and
\[
f(y)=y^*Hy,
\]
then \(f\) is \(2\|H\|\)-Lipschitz and
\[
\mathbb Ef(Y)=\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}H.
\]
Consequently
\[
\Pr\left\{
\left|Y^*HY-\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}H\right|\ge t
\right\}
\le
e^\pi
\exp\left(
-\frac{Nt^2}{16\|H\|^2}
\right).
\tag{4.6}
\]

For complex \(H\), apply (4.6) separately to its Hermitian real and imaginary parts.

### 4.3 Left self-compression

Fix a contour point \(z\) and a unit \(x\in\mathbb F^r\). Since \(Q_n\) is Haar and \(U_nx\) is a fixed unit vector,
\[
Y=Q_n^*U_nx
\]
is uniform on the unit sphere.

Therefore
\[
x^*
\left[
A_n(z)-\phi_n(z)I
\right]
x
=
Y^*H_L(z)Y-\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}H_L(z).
\]

Let \(\mathcal N_r\) be a \(1/4\)-net of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb F^r\). We may take
\[
|\mathcal N_r|
\le
9^r
\quad(\mathbb R),
\qquad
|\mathcal N_r|
\le
9^{2r}
\quad(\mathbb C).
\tag{4.7}
\]

For a Hermitian \(r\times r\) matrix \(M\),
\[
\|M\|_{\rm op}
\le
2\max_{x\in\mathcal N_r}|x^*Mx|.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Applying (4.6), then (4.7)–(4.8), and splitting real/imaginary parts when needed, gives
\[
\Pr\left\{
\|A_n(z)-\phi_n(z)I\|_{\rm op}
>
C L_{0,\Gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{r+s}{n}}
\;\middle|\;\Sigma_n
\right\}
\le
C e^{-s}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The crucial point is that the net entropy is \(O(r)\), while the scalar Haar tail is exponentially strong in \(n\). This pays the exponential net and gives the optimal block scale \(\sqrt{r/n}\).

### 4.4 Right self-compression

Exactly the same argument with \(R_n\), \(H_R(z)\), ambient dimension \(m\), and frame \(V_n\) gives
\[
\Pr\left\{
\|D_n(z)-\widetilde\phi_n(z)I\|_{\rm op}
>
C L_{0,\Gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{r+s}{m}}
\;\middle|\;\Sigma_n
\right\}
\le
C e^{-s}.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Since \(n/m\to c\in(0,1]\), this is at most a constant multiple of the \(n\)-scale in (4.9).

### 4.5 Cross block

Fix unit \(x,y\in\mathbb F^r\). Let
\[
a=Q_n^*U_nx\in\mathbb F^n,
\qquad
b=R_n^*V_ny\in\mathbb F^m.
\]
Conditional on \(\Sigma_n\), the vectors \(a,b\) are independent Haar sphere vectors.

Then
\[
x^*B_n(z)y=a^*C(z)b.
\]

Condition on \(a\). As a function of \(b\),
\[
b\mapsto a^*C(z)b
\]
is a linear functional of Lipschitz constant at most \(\|C(z)\|\le L_{0,\Gamma}\), and its mean is exactly zero by spherical symmetry. Applying concentration to real and imaginary parts gives
\[
\Pr\{|a^*C(z)b|>t\mid a,\Sigma_n\}
\le
C\exp\left(
-\frac{cmt^2}{L_{0,\Gamma}^2}
\right).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Use \(1/4\)-nets for both \(x\) and \(y\). For every rectangular \(r\times r\) matrix \(M\),
\[
\|M\|_{\rm op}
\le
(1-1/4)^{-2}
\max_{x,y\in\mathcal N_r}|x^*My|.
\tag{4.12}
\]
The two-net entropy is still only \(O(r)\). Thus
\[
\Pr\left\{
\|B_n(z)\|_{\rm op}
>
C L_{0,\Gamma}
\sqrt{\frac{r+s}{m}}
\;\middle|\;\Sigma_n
\right\}
\le
C e^{-s}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Again, because \(m\asymp n\), this has the desired \(\sqrt{r/n}\) scale.

### 4.6 Uniformity on the complex contour

Apply the fixed-\(z\) estimates at every \(z\in\Gamma^\sharp\). The union bound simply replaces \(s\) by
\[
s+\log N_{\Gamma,n}
\]
inside the square root.

It remains to pass from the mesh to the full contour. The scalar factors have derivatives
\[
\frac{d}{dz}
\frac{z}{z^2-s^2}
=
-\frac{z^2+s^2}{(z^2-s^2)^2},
\]
\[
\frac{d}{dz}
\frac{s}{z^2-s^2}
=
-\frac{2zs}{(z^2-s^2)^2},
\]
\[
\frac{d}{dz}\frac1z=-\frac1{z^2}.
\]
Thus the compressed blocks and their scalar centers are uniformly Lipschitz in \(z\), with constants bounded by \(C L_{1,\Gamma}\). Moving from a contour point to its nearest mesh point costs at most
\[
C L_{1,\Gamma}a_n.
\]

This proves (3.1).

For fixed rectifiable contours, \(a_n=n^{-2}\) gives \(N_{\Gamma,n}=O(n^2)\), so
\[
\log N_{\Gamma,n}=O(\log n).
\]
That proves (3.3).

## 6. New spectrum-only counterexample P19-SPECONLY-02

The previous P19 run already showed that adaptive low-rank perturbations can preserve the MP empirical spectral distribution while completely changing outlier/eigenvector behavior.

There is an even simpler obstruction to treating an MP singular-value fit as a singular-vector theorem.

Take any deterministic singular values
\[
s_1,\ldots,s_n
\]
whose empirical squared law is arbitrarily close to Marchenko–Pastur and whose top edge is correct. Form a deterministic diagonal rectangular matrix
\[
X_n=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_n)
\]
and take the spike frame
\[
U_n=e_1.
\]
Then
\[
A_n(z)
=
\frac{z}{z^2-s_1^2},
\]
whereas
\[
\phi_n(z)
=
\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{z}{z^2-s_j^2}.
\]
Unless \(s_1\) happens to equal the appropriate average in this nonlinear transform,
\[
|A_n(z)-\phi_n(z)|
\]
is order one on fixed off-spectrum \(z\).

Thus one may have:

* exact/near-exact MP eigenvalue distribution;
* correct spectral edge;
* no compressed-resolvent isotropy whatsoever;
* correspondingly no justification for Haar/independent-spike singular-vector overlaps.

So an MP bulk fit is an eigenvalue-distribution diagnostic only. It says nothing by itself about spike-frame orientation.

This is distinct from the adaptive perturbation counterexample: here the failure occurs already in the noise singular vectors, with no data-dependent spike added.

## 7. iid and neural-feature regimes remain separate

The repaired Haar proof does **not** solve the full iid-entry target. In an iid matrix the singular-vector frames are not conditionally product Haar given the singular values, so the above conditional sphere argument cannot be imported.

The current iid result remains P19-IID-BLOCK-01:
\[
\beta_n^{cc}
=
O_\prec(r_n/\sqrt n)
\]
for
\[
r_n\le n^{1/2-\kappa},
\]
obtained by applying the scalar anisotropic local law to \(r_n^2\) frame entries and paying Frobenius norm.

Knowles–Yin supplies deterministic-vector anisotropic local laws for ordinary independent-entry sample covariance matrices, but the standard stochastic-domination statement does not directly pay an \(\exp(O(r_n))\) sphere net when \(r_n\) is an arbitrary \(o(n)\) sequence.

The September 8, 2026 Ma–Misiakiewicz theorem is relevant for neural/random-feature nulls: it proves an optimal anisotropic local law for sample covariance matrices with independent columns whose coordinates may be strongly dependent, under optimal quadratic-form concentration, polynomial norm moments, and nondegenerate covariance, and explicitly includes deep random features among its applications. But it is still a **bilinear-form** theorem. To close P19 for growing spike rank one still needs a rank-uniform/matrix-valued upgrade for the linearized cross blocks, or a concentration theorem strong enough to pay the \(r\)-dimensional net.

Therefore the hierarchy is now clean:

1. product-Haar singular-vector noise: full \(r=o(n)\) contour compression is PROVED by P19-HAAR-REPAIR-01;
2. iid independent entries: only \(r=o(\sqrt n)\) is currently proved by P19-IID-BLOCK-01;
3. dependent-coordinate/random-feature columns: modern anisotropic local laws exist, but the growing-rank block upgrade and the rectangular cross block remain OPEN;
4. trained/adaptive pruning frames: independence is false in general; one needs a proxy/stability theorem.
