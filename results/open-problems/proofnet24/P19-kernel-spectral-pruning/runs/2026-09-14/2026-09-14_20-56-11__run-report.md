# 5. Weighted Haar compression lemma

The main new ingredient is a weighted version of the earlier product-Haar block theorem.

## `P19-HAAR-WEIGHT-05` — PROVED

Let

\[
O\in O(n)
\]

be Haar and let

\[
U\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}
\]

have orthonormal columns, deterministic or independent of \(O\).

Let

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_n)
\]

be deterministic conditional data, possibly complex diagonal.

Put

\[
\bar d
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n d_i,
\]

\[
L_2(D)
=
\left[
\frac1n
\sum_{i=1}^n
|d_i-\bar d|^2
\right]^{1/2},
\]

\[
L_\infty(D)
=
\max_i|d_i-\bar d|.
\]

For

\[
q=r+s,
\qquad
q=o(n),
\]

there are absolute constants \(C,c>0\) such that

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\left\|
U^*ODO^*U-\bar d I_r
\right\|_{\rm op}
>
C
\left[
L_2(D)\sqrt{\frac qn}
+
L_\infty(D)\frac qn
\right]
\right\}
\le
Ce^{-cs}.
}
\tag{H1}
\]

The same conclusion holds for the independent left/right Haar bilinear block. If

\[
O_L\in O(n),
\qquad
O_R\in O(m)
\]

are independent Haar frames, \(n/m\asymp1\), \(U,V\) are orthonormal rank-\(r\) test frames and \(D\) is a rectangular diagonal singular-value weight, then

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
U^*O_LDO_R^*V
\right\|_{\rm op}
\le
C
\left[
L_2(D)\sqrt{\frac qn}
+
L_\infty(D)\frac qn
\right]
}
\tag{H2}
\]

with the same exponential tail, after harmless aspect-ratio constants.

### Proof

Represent the first \(r\) rows of a Haar/Stiefel matrix by a normalized Gaussian matrix.

Let

\[
G\in\mathbb R^{r\times n}
\]

have iid \(N(0,1)\) entries and

\[
S=\frac1nGG^*.
\]

Then

\[
Q
=
S^{-1/2}\frac G{\sqrt n}
\]

has Haar Stiefel row law.

On

\[
\|S-I_r\|\le1/2,
\]

which has failure probability

\[
\le
2e^{-c(n-r)},
\]

the normalization factors have uniformly bounded operator norm.

For a fixed unit

\[
x\in S^{r-1},
\]

\[
x^*
\frac{G(D-\bar dI)G^*}{n}
x
=
\frac1n
g^*(D-\bar dI)g,
\]

where

\[
g\sim N(0,I_n).
\]

The Gaussian quadratic-form inequality gives

\[
P\left\{
\left|
\frac1n
g^*(D-\bar dI)g
\right|>t
\right\}
\le
2\exp
\left[
-c
\min
\left(
\frac{nt^2}{L_2(D)^2},
\frac{nt}{L_\infty(D)}
\right)
\right].
\]

A \(1/4\)-net of \(S^{r-1}\) has size at most \(9^r\). Therefore the net entropy is paid by choosing

\[
t
=
C
\left[
L_2(D)\sqrt{\frac{r+s}{n}}
+
L_\infty(D)\frac{r+s}{n}
\right].
\]

The standard quadratic-form net inequality converts this to operator norm.

The Stiefel normalization contributes only universal multiplicative constants on the Wishart-good event.

For complex diagonal \(D\), apply the argument separately to real and imaginary parts.

For the mixed block, use independent Gaussian Stiefel representations on the left and right. For fixed unit \(x,y\), the relevant scalar is a Gaussian bilinear form. Its tail has the same variance and operator-envelope structure:

\[
\operatorname{Var}
\asymp
\frac{\|D\|_F^2}{nm},
\]

with subexponential scale controlled by

\[
\frac{\|D\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt{nm}}.
\]

Two sphere nets give (H2).

QED.

### Why this is the right edge quantity

The previous global-Lipschitz proof paid only

\[
\|D\|_\infty
\asymp
\kappa^{-1}
\]

inside a derivative, which generated a \(\kappa^{-2}\) fluctuation loss.

The Haar theorem instead charges the actual second moment of the spectral weights:

\[
L_2(D).
\]

At a square-root spectral edge this is much smaller.

\qquad

\qquad

Write the singular values as

\[
s_1,\ldots,s_n.

Let

\[
z=w^2

\[
\operatorname{dist}
\left(

[\lambda_{-,n},\lambda_{+,n}]
\right)
\ge\kappa_n.

On the usual soft-edge rigidity event, assume

\[
\kappa_n
\gg
n^{-2/3+\tau}

for some fixed \(\tau>0\).

For the left self-resolvent the diagonal spectral weights are

\[
d_j^{(L)}
=
\frac{w}{w^2-s_j^2}.
\]

For the mixed block they are

\[
d_j^{(C)}
=
\frac{s_j}{w^2-s_j^2}.
\]

For the right self-resolvent there are the same \(n\) nonzero weights plus \(m-n\) zero-singular-value directions with bounded weight \(1/w\).

Because

\[
0<w_0\le|w|\le w_1,
\]

the zero-singular-value contribution is harmless.

At a regular square-root edge,

\[
\rho_{\rm MP}(\lambda_+-x)
\asymp
\sqrt x.
\]

Therefore

\[
\int
\frac{\rho_{\rm MP}(x)\,dx}
{|z-x|^2}
\asymp
\kappa^{-1/2}
\]

for \(z\) a distance \(\kappa\) outside the edge.

Soft-edge rigidity/local MP control transfers this to the empirical spectrum:

\[
\boxed{
\frac1n
\sum_j
\frac1{|z-s_j^2|^2}
\le
C\kappa_n^{-1/2}
}
\tag{E-HS}
\]

with overwhelming probability, uniformly on a polynomial-size contour mesh.

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
L_2(D_L)
+
L_2(D_R)
+
L_2(D_C)
\le
C\kappa_n^{-1/4},

\tag{E1}

while trivially

\[
\boxed{
L_\infty(D_L)
+
L_\infty(D_R)
+
L_\infty(D_C)
\le
C\kappa_n^{-1}.
}
\tag{E2}
\]

The centering by the empirical trace can only decrease the \(L^2\) norm of the self-block weights.

# 7. Main new theorem: shrinking-edge product-Haar/Gaussian compression

## `P19-HAAR-EDGE-WEIGHTED-05` — PROVED

Assume a rectangular noise matrix has conditional product-Haar left/right singular-vector frames, independent of its singular values.

Let

\[
\Gamma_n
\]

be a finite union of rectifiable contours in the singular parameter \(w\), with uniformly bounded total length and

\[
0<w_0\le |w|\le w_1<\infty.
\]

Let

\[
\kappa_n
=
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
\Gamma_n^2,
[\lambda_{-,n},\lambda_{+,n}]
\right).
\]

Assume:

1. regular square-root MP edge geometry;
2. soft-edge rigidity strong enough to give (E-HS) uniformly on the contour;
3. \(\kappa_n\gg n^{-2/3+\tau}\);
4. \(r_n+s_n+\log n=o(n)\).

Then, with probability at least

\[
1-Ce^{-cs_n}-n^{-D}
\]

for every fixed \(D\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{w\in\Gamma_n}
\Big[
&
\|A_n(w)-\phi_n(w)I_{r_n}\|_{\rm op}\\
&+
\|D_n(w)-\widetilde\phi_n(w)I_{r_n}\|_{\rm op}
+
\|B_n(w)\|_{\rm op}
\Big]
\\
&\le
C
\left[
\kappa_n^{-1/4}
\sqrt{
\frac{
r_n+\log n+s_n
}{
n
}
}
+
\kappa_n^{-1}
\frac{
r_n+\log n+s_n
}{
n
}
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{P19-HAAR-EDGE-WEIGHTED-05}
\]

Here

\[
\phi_n(w)
=
\frac1n
\operatorname{Tr}
\left[
w(w^2-X_nX_n^*)^{-1}
\right]
\]

and \(\widetilde\phi_n\) is the right empirical companion transform.

### Proof

Condition on the singular values.

By the product-Haar hypothesis, each compressed block is exactly a Haar compression of a diagonal spectral-weight matrix.

For the left self-block,

\[
A_n(w)
=
U_n^*
O_L
D_L(w)
O_L^*
U_n.
\]

Its conditional center is exactly

\[
\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}D_L(w)
=
\phi_n(w).
\]

Apply `P19-HAAR-WEIGHT-05` with (E1) and (E2):

\[
\|A_n(w)-\phi_n(w)I\|
\le
C
\left[
\kappa_n^{-1/4}
\sqrt{\frac{r_n+s_n}{n}}
+
\kappa_n^{-1}
\frac{r_n+s_n}{n}
\right].
\]

The right self-block is identical after the harmless rectangular zero-singular-value contribution.

For the mixed block,

\[
B_n(w)
=
U_n^*
O_L
D_C(w)
O_R^*
V_n.
\]

Its conditional mean is zero and (H2) gives the same bound.

For contour uniformity, discretize \(\Gamma_n\).

Resolvent derivatives satisfy

\[
\|\partial_w R(w)\|
\le
C\kappa_n^{-2}
\]

on the spectral-safety event.

Choose the mesh spacing small enough that interpolation contributes less than the right-hand side. Because

\[
\kappa_n\gg n^{-2/3+\tau},
\]

the number of mesh points is polynomial in \(n\), so its entropy is

\[
O(\log n).
\]

Absorb this into

\[
r_n+\log n+s_n.
\]

Finally uncondition the singular values and intersect with the rigidity/(E-HS) event.

QED.
