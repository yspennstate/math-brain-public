# 0. Executive result

P19 remains **OPEN**, but this run closes a concrete gap in the same-observation singular-vector certificate: the preceding reused-data theorem required **bounded raw squared losses**. That hypothesis is unnatural for Gaussian/ReLU/random-feature models.

This run proves a robust replacement based on **supportwise radial clipping** and **clipped-loss stability**.

The new package is:

- `P19-CLIPPED-REUSED-LOWER-RE-30` — a same-observation finite-sample lower restricted-eigenvalue certificate for an arbitrarily unbounded raw feature map; no tail assumption is needed for this one-sided lower certificate;
- `P19-CLIPPED-FEATURE-STABILITY-30` — supportwise feature replacement drift implies the clipped squared-loss stability required by the first theorem;
- `P19-CLIPPED-SCALAR-UPPER-30` — a same-observation upper population certificate for an unbounded nonnegative loss from clipped-loss stability plus an explicit conditional exponential tail envelope;
- `P19-UNBOUNDED-REUSED-DYAD-30` — combining the two preceding modules gives a reusable-data coefficient/operator and singular-projector/mixed-dyad certificate for unbounded raw features/losses under explicit clipped-stability and tail obligations;
- `P19-GAUSS-CLIP-CALIBRATION-30` — for an honest Gaussian feature null on a fixed support, supportwise radial clipping has an exact scalar population covariance and, above a chi-square radius, coincides with the ordinary Wishart/MP sample covariance with explicit probability;
- `P19-CLIP-NOT-UPPER-CE-30` — clipped covariance alone cannot upper-bound raw covariance or prediction risk: a rare large coordinate can leave the clipped second moment fixed while sending the raw second moment to infinity;
- `P19-AUDIT-P15-HYBRID-LOO-30` — independent **PASS** of P15's exact fresh-input hybrid replacement bridge and its `rho=n lambda` normalization.

The main source-facing consequence is:

> unbounded Gaussian/ReLU features no longer force a bounded-raw-feature assumption in the **lower identifiability denominator** of the sparse singular-vector certificate. What remains genuinely necessary is post-selection clipped stability for reused data, plus a tail envelope for the **upper prediction-error numerator**.

Hard pruning based on the trained matrix is still not an iid mask. The preceding `P19-HARD-TOPK-REPLACEONE-CE-29` remains active: an `O(1/n)` pre-pruning perturbation can become `Theta(1)` after hard top-`k` at a score boundary. Clipping removes an unbounded-loss obstacle; it does **not** remove the boundary-selection obstacle.

# 5. Supportwise radial clipping

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)
\]

be iid observations and let the trained algorithm return a measurable feature map

\[
\psi_S(x)\in\mathbb R^r.
\]

No boundedness assumption is made on `psi_S(x)`.

For a coordinate support `J subset [r]`, write `P_J psi_S(x)` for the coordinate restriction. For `R>0`, define Euclidean radial projection onto the radius-`R` ball by

\[
T_R(y)
=
\begin{cases}
y,&\|y\|\le R,\\
R y/\|y\|,&\|y\|>R.
\end{cases}
\]

For every support `J`, define the **supportwise clipped feature**

\[
\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(x)
=
T_R(P_J\psi_S(x)).
\]

Define the raw population restricted Gram

\[
G_{S,J}
=
E_X[P_J\psi_S(X)\psi_S(X)^*P_J\mid S],
\]

the clipped population Gram

\[
G^{[R]}_{S,J}
=
E_X[
\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(X)
\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(X)^*
\mid S],
\]

and the same-observation clipped empirical Gram

\[
\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(X_i)
\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(X_i)^*.
\]

For each `J` and unit `v in R^J`, define

\[
\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}(S,z)
=
|v^*\psi^{[R]}_{S,J}(x)|^2.
\]

Always

\[
0\le\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}\le R^2.
\]

# 6. `P19-CLIPPED-REUSED-LOWER-RE-30` — PROVED

Assume that for every neighboring samples `S~S'`, every evaluation point `z`, every support `|J|<=s`, and every unit `v in R^J`,

\[
\boxed{
|\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}(S,z)-
\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}(S',z)|
\le\beta_{s,R}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define the support/net count

\[
\mathfrak N_{r,s}
=
\sum_{d=1}^s {r\choose d}9^d
\]

and

\[
L_{r,s}(\eta)
=
\log\frac{2\mathfrak N_{r,s}}{\eta}.
\]

Put

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{s,R}(\eta)
=
2\beta_{s,R}
+
2\bigl[(2n-1)\beta_{s,R}+R^2\bigr]
\sqrt{\frac{L_{r,s}(\eta)}{2n}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then with probability at least `1-eta`, simultaneously for every `J` with `|J|<=s`,

\[
\boxed{
\|G^{[R]}_{S,J}-\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J}\|_{op}
\le
\tau_{s,R}(\eta).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Moreover the **raw** population Gram satisfies the one-sided lower certificate

\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}(G_{S,J})
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J})
-
\tau_{s,R}(\eta)
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

simultaneously for every `|J|<=s`.

Equivalently, with

\[
\widehat a_{s,R}
=
\inf_{|J|\le s}
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J}),
\]

and

\[
a_s(G_S)
=
\inf_{|J|\le s}
\lambda_{\min}(G_{S,J}),
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
a_s(G_S)
\ge
\widehat a_{s,R}-\tau_{s,R}(\eta).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

No moment or tail bound on the raw feature vector is required.

## Proof

### Step 1: fixed directional same-observation generalization gap

Fix `J,v` and put

\[
g_{J,v}(S)
=
E_Z\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}(S,Z)
-
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\ell^{[R]}_{J,v}(S,Z_i).
\]

The standard replace-one exchangeability argument, carried out directly, gives

\[
|E g_{J,v}(S)|\le\beta_{s,R}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Indeed, replace `Z_i` by an independent copy `Z_i'`, compare the fresh loss under the two neighboring learners, and use exchangeability for the term in which the held-out coordinate is swapped.

### Step 2: bounded differences

If one training observation is replaced, the population term changes by at most `beta_{s,R}`.

In the empirical term, the `n-1` unchanged evaluation points each move by at most `beta_{s,R}`, while the replaced evaluation-point term is between `0` and `R^2`. Therefore one sample replacement changes `g_{J,v}` by at most

\[
\frac{(2n-1)\beta_{s,R}+R^2}{n}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

McDiarmid therefore gives, for every fixed `J,v`,

\[
P\left\{
|g_{J,v}(S)|>
\beta_{s,R}+t
\right\}
\le
2\exp\left(
-\frac{2n t^2}
{[(2n-1)\beta_{s,R}+R^2]^2}
\right).
\tag{6.8}
\]

### Step 3: support nets

For each `d`-dimensional support choose a `1/4`-net of the unit sphere with cardinality at most `9^d`. Across all supports of size at most `s`, the total number of net points is at most `mathfrak N_{r,s}`.

Using `(6.8)` with

\[
t=
[(2n-1)\beta_{s,R}+R^2]
\sqrt{\frac{L_{r,s}(\eta)}{2n}}
\]

and union-bounding gives, with probability at least `1-eta`, the directional bound simultaneously on every net point.

For a symmetric matrix `A` and a `1/4`-net `N`,

\[
\|A\|_{op}
\le
2\sup_{v\in N}|v^*Av|.
\]

Apply this to

\[
A=G^{[R]}_{S,J}-\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J}
\]

for every support. This proves `(6.3)` with the exact factor in `(6.2)`.

### Step 4: clipping is Loewner-lower than the raw covariance

For one raw restricted feature vector `y=P_J psi_S(X)`, write

\[
T_R(y)=a(y)y,
\qquad 0\le a(y)\le1.
\]

Then pointwise

\[
yy^*-T_R(y)T_R(y)^*
=
[1-a(y)^2]yy^*
\succeq0.
\]

Taking fresh-query conditional expectation yields

\[
\boxed{
G_{S,J}\succeq G^{[R]}_{S,J}.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Hence

\[
\lambda_{\min}(G_{S,J})
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(G^{[R]}_{S,J})
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G^{[R]}_{S,J})
-
\tau_{s,R}(\eta).
\]

This proves `(6.4)` and `(6.5)`.

QED.

### Interpretation

This theorem is deliberately one-sided. It certifies the lower feature identifiability needed to turn prediction agreement into coefficient/operator agreement. It does **not** upper-bound raw covariance or raw prediction risk; Section 10 proves that no such upper bound can follow from clipping alone.

# 7. `P19-CLIPPED-FEATURE-STABILITY-30` — PROVED

The stability hypothesis `(6.1)` can be obtained from a supportwise feature-map replacement bound.

Euclidean projection onto a closed ball is nonexpansive. Therefore

\[
\|T_R(y)-T_R(y')\|
\le\|y-y'\|.
\]

Suppose

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{S\sim S'}
\sup_x
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\|P_J(\psi_S(x)-\psi_{S'}(x))\|
\le a_s.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then for clipped vectors `z,z'`, both of norm at most `R`, and any unit `v`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\bigl||v^*z|^2-|v^*z'|^2\bigr|
&\le
(|v^*z|+|v^*z'|)
|v^*(z-z')|\\
&\le
2R\|z-z'\|\\
&\le
2R a_s.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{s,R}\le2Ra_s.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The same conclusion holds if `(7.1)` is assumed directly for the **clipped** support vectors rather than for the raw vectors. This distinction matters for unbounded inputs: no raw pathwise bound is silently assumed when only a clipped one is available.

# 8. `P19-CLIPPED-SCALAR-UPPER-30` — PROVED

Lower restricted eigenvalues need no tail assumption, but upper population errors do.

Let `L(S,z)>=0` be an arbitrary sample-dependent nonnegative loss. No raw boundedness is assumed. For `T>0`, define

\[
L_T(S,z)=\min\{L(S,z),T\}.
\]

Assume:

1. **clipped replace-one stability**
   \[
   \sup_{S\sim S'}\sup_z
   |L_T(S,z)-L_T(S',z)|
   \le\beta_T;
   \tag{8.1}
   \]

2. **fresh conditional exponential tail envelope**: for every realized training sample `S`,
   \[
   P_Z\{L(S,Z)>t\mid S\}
   \le
   A e^{-t/K},
   \qquad t\ge0.
   \tag{8.2}
   \]

Define

\[
q(S)=E_Z[L(S,Z)\mid S],
\]

\[
\widehat q_T(S)
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n L_T(S,Z_i).
\]

Then with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
q(S)
\le
\widehat q_T(S)
+
\omega_T(\eta)
+
AKe^{-T/K},
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\omega_T(\eta)
=
\beta_T
+
[(2n-1)\beta_T+T]
\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\eta)}{2n}}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

## Proof

Put

\[
q_T(S)=E_Z[L_T(S,Z)\mid S].
\]

Exactly as in Section 6, exchangeability gives

\[
|E[q_T-\widehat q_T]|\le\beta_T.
\]

One training replacement changes `q_T-hat q_T` by at most

\[
\frac{(2n-1)\beta_T+T}{n}.
\]

McDiarmid gives the one-sided bound

\[
q_T(S)-\widehat q_T(S)
\le
\omega_T(\eta)
\]

with the stated confidence; the `log(2/eta)` form is a safe two-sided version.

Finally,

\[
\begin{aligned}
q-q_T
&=
E[(L-T)_+\mid S]\\
&=
\int_T^\infty
P\{L>t\mid S\}\,dt\\
&\le
AKe^{-T/K}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Combining the two inequalities proves `(8.3)`.

QED.

### Scope

The theorem is exact under `(8.1)`–`(8.2)`. It does not assert that the source DNN losses have a particular `A,K`; those source constants are currently unavailable. A subexponential or subgamma source theorem must first be proved or measured.

# 9. `P19-UNBOUNDED-REUSED-DYAD-30` — PROVED under explicit clipped-stability/tail hypotheses

Consider two legally aligned deployed predictors

\[
f_1=C_1\phi_1,
\qquad
f_2=C_2\phi_2,
\]

with each readout having at most `k` nonzero feature columns. Let

\[
J=
\operatorname{supp}_{col}(C_1)
\cup
\operatorname{supp}_{col}(C_2),
\qquad |J|\le2k.
\]

Let `psi_S` be a common legally aligned proxy feature map.

Define the three raw fresh-query losses

\[
L_0(S,z)=\|f_1(x)-f_2(x)\|^2,
\]

\[
L_1(S,z)=\|C_1(\phi_1(x)-\psi_S(x))\|^2,
\]

\[
L_2(S,z)=\|C_2(\phi_2(x)-\psi_S(x))\|^2.
\]

Let

\[
q_j=E_Z L_j(S,Z)\mid S.
\]

## Denominator certificate

Apply `P19-CLIPPED-REUSED-LOWER-RE-30` to the proxy with `s=2k` and radius `R`. Put

\[
\underline a_{2k,R}
=
\widehat a_{2k,R}
-
\tau_{2k,R}(\eta_{RE}).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Assume

\[
\boxed{\underline a_{2k,R}>0.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Because the theorem is uniform over **all** supports of size at most `2k`, the actual support union `J` may be selected from the same training data. No selector independence is used.

## Numerator certificates

For each `j=0,1,2`, choose a clipping threshold `T_j` and assume the hypotheses of `P19-CLIPPED-SCALAR-UPPER-30` with constants

\[
\beta_{j,T_j},
\qquad
A_j,
\qquad
K_j.
\]

Define

\[
\widehat q_{j,T_j}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\min\{L_j(S,Z_i),T_j\},
\]

\[
\overline q_j
=
\widehat q_{j,T_j}
+
\omega_{j,T_j}(\eta_j)
+
A_jK_j e^{-T_j/K_j}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then, on an event of probability at least

\[
1-\eta_{RE}-\eta_0-\eta_1-\eta_2,
\]

all three raw population energies obey

\[
q_j\le\overline q_j.
\tag{9.4}
\]

## Operator consequence

The raw proxy Gram on `J` obeys

\[
E[\psi_{S,J}\psi_{S,J}^*\mid S]
\succeq
\underline a_{2k,R}I.
\]

For any unit output vector `y`, put

\[
w=(C_1-C_2)^*y.
\]

Then `w` is supported on `J` and

\[
\sqrt{\underline a_{2k,R}}\,\|w\|
\le
\|w^*\psi_S\|_{L^2}.
\]

Using

\[
(C_1-C_2)\psi_S
=
(f_1-f_2)
-
C_1(\phi_1-\psi_S)
+
C_2(\phi_2-\psi_S),
\]

Minkowski in fresh-query `L^2` gives

\[
\|w^*\psi_S\|_{L^2}
\le
\sqrt{q_0}+\sqrt{q_1}+\sqrt{q_2}.
\]

Taking the supremum over unit `y` and inserting `(9.4)` yields

\[
\boxed{
\|C_1-C_2\|_{op}
\le
\overline{\mathcal E}_{clip}
:=
\frac{
\sqrt{\overline q_0}
+
\sqrt{\overline q_1}
+
\sqrt{\overline q_2}
}{
\sqrt{\underline a_{2k,R}}
}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

## Singular-vector consequence

Let a positive singular cluster of the deployed readout have an observed protected gap `Delta_K>0`. Only if

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal E}_{clip}
\le
\Delta_K/2
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

is Wedin/Hermitian dilation invoked. Then

\[
\boxed{
\|\Delta\Pi_K^L\|_{op},
\quad
\|\Delta\Pi_K^R\|_{op},
\quad
\|\Delta F_K\|_{op}
\le
\frac{4\overline{\mathcal E}_{clip}}
{\Delta_K}.
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

The constant `4` is the inherited conservative mixed-dyad transfer constant. The source July BBP overlap factors `alpha_L,alpha_R` are **not** obtained from Wedin; they remain resolvent-residue quantities from the independent-spike compressed-resolvent theorem.

## What this theorem does and does not close

It removes bounded raw Gaussian/ReLU features from the denominator and allows unbounded raw prediction losses in the numerator when an explicit tail envelope is supplied.

It does **not** prove the needed clipped-loss stability for a hard pruning rule. The previous hard-top-`k` counterexample shows that this post-selection stability can fail at a trained score boundary. The correct next bridge remains the boundary operator profile or a source-valid post-selection stability theorem.

# 10. `P19-CLIP-NOT-UPPER-CE-30` — PROVED falsification

Clipping gives a valid lower covariance certificate because raw covariance dominates clipped covariance in Loewner order. The reverse direction is impossible without a tail hypothesis.

Take a scalar feature `Xi` with

\[
\Xi=
\begin{cases}
M,&\text{with probability }p,\\
0,&\text{with probability }1-p,
\end{cases}
\]

and fix a clipping radius `R<M`.

Then

\[
E[\min\{\Xi^2,R^2\}]
=pR^2,
\]

while

\[
E\Xi^2=pM^2.
\]

For fixed `p,R`, the clipped second moment is constant while the raw second moment diverges as `M->infinity`.

A same-sample version is even sharper. Let `p=1/(2n)`. With probability

\[
(1-p)^n
\to e^{-1/2},
\]

the entire reused sample contains no nonzero observation, so the empirical clipped covariance is exactly zero while the population raw second moment `pM^2` is arbitrarily large.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{clipped/reused covariance alone does not upper-bound raw covariance or prediction risk.}
}
\]

The explicit tail remainder in `P19-CLIPPED-SCALAR-UPPER-30` is mathematically necessary, not cosmetic.

# 11. `P19-GAUSS-CLIP-CALIBRATION-30` — PROVED

The clipping certificate has a clean calibration under an **honest** Gaussian feature null.

Let

\[
g\sim N(0,I_d)
\]

on a fixed `d`-coordinate support and put

\[
z=T_R(g).
\]

By rotational invariance,

\[
E[zz^*]
=a_{d,R}I_d,
\]

with the exact scalar

\[
\boxed{
a_{d,R}
=
\frac1d
E\min\{\chi_d^2,R^2\}.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

Hence clipping preserves isotropy exactly; it changes only the scalar variance.

Let

\[
R^2=(1+\delta)d,
\qquad \delta>0,
\]

and define

\[
h(\delta)=\delta-\log(1+\delta)>0.
\]

Then

\[
1-a_{d,R}
=
\frac1d E[(\chi_d^2-R^2)_+].
\]

For `lambda=delta/[2(1+delta)]`, the chi-square MGF gives

\[
\frac1d E[(\chi_d^2-R^2)_+]
\le
(1+\delta)
\exp\left[-\frac d2h(\delta)\right].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
a_{d,R}
\ge
\left[
1-(1+\delta)e^{-d h(\delta)/2}
\right]_+.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

For `m` iid honest Gaussian rows on this **fixed** support, the standard chi-square Chernoff bound gives

\[
P\{\|g_i\|^2>R^2\}
\le
e^{-d h(\delta)/2}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
P\{\text{no row is clipped}\}
\ge
1-m e^{-d h(\delta)/2}.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

On this event the clipped empirical Gram is **exactly** the ordinary fixed-support Wishart Gram. Therefore every valid fixed-support Wishart/MP extreme-eigenvalue theorem applies without alteration on the no-clipping event.

### Dependence warning

Equation `(11.3)` is a fixed-support statement. If the support is selected from the **same** Gaussian matrix, one cannot simply condition on that support and reuse `(11.3)` as though it were independent. A uniform theorem must pay the support entropy explicitly, or use the already-proved uniform sparse-Wishart/RIP route.

This is the same dependence principle as throughout P19: same-score or same-matrix selection is legitimate only when the relevant conditional symmetry or simultaneous event has actually been proved.

# 15. Route comparison

## Compressed resolvents / local laws

**Still indispensable** for the July growing-rank spike-overlap theorem, non-Haar edge fluctuations, and critical BBP coupling. They are not needed for the deterministic/stability clipping argument.

## Davis–Kahan / Wedin

**Second stage only.** They are invoked only after `(9.5)` gives an honest operator perturbation and `(9.6)` verifies a positive protected singular gap. They do not produce source overlap constants.

## Leave-one-out / sample splitting

**Cleaner when available.** A deletion learner makes the held-out observation independent and removes the expectation bias in the reused covariance gap. The clipped reused theorem is for the harder setting where the same observations are reused and stability must be paid.

## Deterministic equivalents

Potentially useful for estimating feature-tail constants, leverage derivatives, conditional covariance, and prediction energies in a specified random-feature model. They do not transfer to an arbitrary response-trained representation without a stability/comparison theorem.

P15's new leverage derivative identity reinforces this point: a deterministic equivalent for a ridge leverage itself is not enough when the target involves its ridge derivative.

## Free probability

A valid free-probability reduction would require a model with the needed asymptotic freeness or a proved comparison to one. Nothing in the same-trained-matrix pruning problem supplies that automatically. No heuristic free convolution is promoted to a theorem here.

## Sparsification concentration

The new theorem uses a simultaneous support/net event on the **actual clipped feature map**. This is legitimate for data-selected supports because the event is uniform. It is fundamentally different from iid masking.

## Counterexamples

The inherited hard-selection counterexample and the new clipping upper-bound counterexample isolate different failures:

- hard top-`k` can destroy replacement stability at a boundary;
- clipping alone cannot upper-bound an unbounded raw risk.

Both debts must be handled separately.
