# 6. Proof of `P19-PROXY-BOUNDARY-TRANSFER-19`

## 6.1 Score and order-statistic stability

For every column \(j\),

\[
|s_j(C)-s_j(D)|
\le
\|(C-D)e_j\|_2
\le
e.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence every order statistic is \(e\)-Lipschitz:

\[
\boxed{
|s_{(\ell)}(C)-s_{(\ell)}(D)|
\le
e
\qquad
(1\le\ell\le r).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This follows either from the variational characterization of order statistics or from the elementary fact that coordinatewise perturbation by at most \(e\) moves every sorted coordinate by at most \(e\).

## 6.2 Boundary-set inclusion

Take \(j\in J_\rho(C)\).

### Case A: \(j\in S_k(C)\)

Then

\[
s_j(C)
\le
s_{(k+1)}(C)+2\rho.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If \(j\in S_k(D)\), then by `(6.1)`--`(6.2)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_j(D)
&\le
s_j(C)+e
\\
&\le
s_{(k+1)}(C)+2\rho+e
\\
&\le
s_{(k+1)}(D)+2\rho+2e
\\
&=
s_{(k+1)}(D)+2(\rho+e),
\end{aligned}
\]

so \(j\in J_{\rho+e}(D)\).

If instead \(j\notin S_k(D)\), then \(j\in S_k(C)\) gives

\[
s_j(C)\ge s_{(k)}(C),
\]

and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_j(D)
&\ge
s_j(C)-e
\\
&\ge
s_{(k)}(C)-e
\\
&\ge
s_{(k)}(D)-2e
\\
&\ge
s_{(k)}(D)-2(\rho+e).
\end{aligned}
\]

Again \(j\in J_{\rho+e}(D)\).

### Case B: \(j\notin S_k(C)\)

Then

\[
s_j(C)
\ge
s_{(k)}(C)-2\rho.
\tag{6.4}
\]

If \(j\notin S_k(D)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_j(D)
&\ge
s_j(C)-e
\\
&\ge
s_{(k)}(C)-2\rho-e
\\
&\ge
s_{(k)}(D)-2\rho-2e
\\
&=
s_{(k)}(D)-2(\rho+e),
\end{aligned}
\]

so \(j\in J_{\rho+e}(D)\).

If instead \(j\in S_k(D)\), then \(j\notin S_k(C)\) implies

\[
s_j(C)\le s_{(k+1)}(C),
\]

hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_j(D)
&\le
s_j(C)+e
\\
&\le
s_{(k+1)}(C)+e
\\
&\le
s_{(k+1)}(D)+2e
\\
&\le
s_{(k+1)}(D)+2(\rho+e).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus `(5.2)` holds in all cases.

## 6.3 Operator-profile transfer

Put

\[
J=J_\rho(C),
\qquad
K=J_{\rho+e}(D).
\]

By `(5.2)`,

\[
J\subseteq K.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_C(\rho)
&=
\|CP_J\|
\\
&\le
\|CP_K\|
\\
&\le
\|DP_K\|
+
\|(C-D)P_K\|
\\
&\le
\Omega_D(\rho+e)+e.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(5.3)`.

Apply `(5.3)` with \(C,D\) interchanged and radius \(\rho-e\) to obtain the lower side of `(5.4)`.

## 6.4 Löwner frame certificate

For every \(x\in\mathbb R^q\),

\[
x^*\mathcal Q_C(\rho)x
=
\|P_JC^*x\|^2
\le
\|P_KC^*x\|^2.
\]

Now

\[
P_KC^*x
=
P_KD^*x
+
P_K(C-D)^*x.
\]

Using

\[
\|a+b\|^2
\le
(1+\eta)\|a\|^2
+
(1+\eta^{-1})\|b\|^2

and

\[
\|P_K(C-D)^*x\|
\le

\[
x^*\mathcal Q_C(\rho)x
\le
(1+\eta)

+
(1+\eta^{-1})e^2\|x\|^2.
\]

This is `(5.5)`.

# 7. Boundary-width enlargement is genuinely necessary

## `P19-BOUNDARY-WIDTH-CE-19` — NEW / PROVED

A same-width comparison

\[
\Omega_C(\rho)
\lesssim
\Omega_D(\rho)+\|C-D\|
\]

is false near a score-boundary transition.

Fix \(\rho>0\) and let \(e>0\) be small. Take \(q=r=2\), \(k=1\), and diagonal matrices whose column scores are

\[
D:
\qquad
d_1=1+\rho+e-\varepsilon/2,
\qquad
d_2=1-\rho-e+\varepsilon/2,
\]

with \(0<\varepsilon\ll e\), so

\[
d_1-d_2
=
2\rho+2e-\varepsilon
>
2\rho.
\]

Hence

\[
J_\rho(D)=\varnothing,
\qquad
\Omega_D(\rho)=0.
\]

Now perturb the first score down by \(e\) and the second up by \(e\):

\[
C:
\qquad
c_1=d_1-e,
\qquad
c_2=d_2+e.
\]

Then

\[
\|C-D\|_{\rm op}=e,
\]

but

\[
c_1-c_2
=
2\rho-\varepsilon
<
2\rho.
\]

Therefore both columns lie in \(J_\rho(C)\), and

\[
\Omega_C(\rho)
\ge
c_1
=
1+\rho-\varepsilon/2,
\]

an order-one value even though \(e\to0\).

On the other hand,

\[
2(\rho+e)
>
d_1-d_2,
\]

so both columns are correctly captured by the enlarged proxy boundary

\[
J_{\rho+e}(D).
\]

Thus the boundary-width enlargement in `(5.2)` is not bookkeeping; it is a necessary selection-dependence debt, with the \(+e\) scale sharp up to arbitrarily small slack.
