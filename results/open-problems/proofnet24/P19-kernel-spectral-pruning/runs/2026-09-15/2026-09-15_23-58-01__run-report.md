`R(f)=E ||Y-f(X)||^2`.

For a clipping threshold `T`, define the raw leave-one-out empirical loss

`Rhat_loo = n^{-1} sum_i ||Y_i-f_{-i}(X_i)||^2`.

If pairwise deletion-replacement predictors have fresh RMS debts `B_ij`, the raw losses have explicit conditional tail envelopes, and

`D_i = ||f_S-f_{-i}||_{L^2(P_X)}`,

then with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{R(f_S)}
\le
\sqrt{
\widehat R_{\rm loo}
+\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
+\tau_T
}
+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n D_i,
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
\le
\frac1{n\sqrt{2\eta}}
\left[
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left(
T+2\sqrt T\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}
\right)^2
\right]^{1/2}
}
\]

and `tau_T` is the explicit raw-loss tail remainder. The hard top-`k` supports may be deterministic functions of the same trained matrices. The theorem uses no iid Bernoulli mask.

# 4. Leave-one-out setup

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n),
\qquad Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)
\]

be iid. Any auxiliary training randomness is taken independent of `S` and held common across the replacement/deletion couplings below. If coefficient-level comparisons require a neural symmetry alignment, only a legally defined alignment may be used; prediction outputs themselves require no hidden-unit alignment.

For each `i`, let

\[
f_{-i}=A(S_{-i})
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the learner retrained after deleting observation `i`. For `j\ne i`, let

\[
f_{-i}^{(j)}
=A(S_{-i}^{(j)})
\tag{4.2}
\]

where the `j`th retained observation is replaced by an independent copy `Z_j'` and all common training randomness is reused.

For squared loss

\[
\ell(f;z)=\|y-f(x)\|^2,
\tag{4.3}
\]

define the analytic clipping

\[
\ell_T(f;z)=\min\{\ell(f;z),T\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This clipping is used only in the proof. The deployed predictor remains raw.

Define

\[
R_{i,T}=E_Z\ell_T(f_{-i};Z),
\qquad
\widehat R_{\rm loo,T}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_T(f_{-i};Z_i),
\tag{4.5}
\]

\[
\overline R_{\rm loo,T}
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n R_{i,T}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For `i\ne j`, define the **fresh clipped-loss replacement RMS**

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{ij,T}^2
=E\left[
\ell_T(f_{-i};Z)
-
\ell_T(f_{-i}^{(j)};Z)
\right]^2,
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

where `Z` is an additional fresh draw independent of every training/replacement observation.

# 5. `P19-LOO-RMS-CLIPPED-32` — PROVED

Define

\[
H_T(S)
=\overline R_{\rm loo,T}
-
\widehat R_{\rm loo,T}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{EH_T=0.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(H_T)
\le
\frac1{2n^2}
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left(
T+\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_{ij,T}
\right)^2.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, for every `eta in (0,1)`, with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\overline R_{\rm loo,T}
\le
\widehat R_{\rm loo,T}
+arepsilon_{T,\eta},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
=
\frac1{n\sqrt{2\eta}}
\left[
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left(
T+\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_{ij,T}
\right)^2
\right]^{1/2}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

## Proof

Because `Z_i` is independent of its deletion learner `f_{-i}`,

\[
E\ell_T(f_{-i};Z_i)=ER_{i,T}
\]

for every `i`. Averaging proves `(5.2)`.

Now replace the single training observation `Z_j` by an iid copy `Z_j'`, and write `H_T^{(j)}` for the resulting value.

### The `i=j` summand

Deleting `j` removes that coordinate entirely, so `f_{-j}` itself is unchanged by the replacement. Only the evaluation point changes from `Z_j` to `Z_j'`. Since `0<=ell_T<=T`, the corresponding contribution to

\[
\|H_T-H_T^{(j)}\|_{L^2}
\]

is at most `T/n`.

### The `i\ne j` summands

Put

\[
d_{ij}(z)
=\ell_T(f_{-i};z)
-
\ell_T(f_{-i}^{(j)};z).
\tag{5.6}
\]

The change of the `i`th centered LOO summand is exactly

\[
E_Zd_{ij}(Z)-d_{ij}(Z_i).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Crucially, `Z_i` is excluded from both learners `f_{-i}` and `f_{-i}^{(j)}`. Hence conditional on those two learners, `Z_i` is an honest iid test draw. Therefore

\[
E\left[
(E_Zd_{ij}(Z)-d_{ij}(Z_i))^2
\mid f_{-i},f_{-i}^{(j)}
\right]
\le
E_Zd_{ij}(Z)^2.
\tag{5.8}
\]

After expectation,

\[
\left\|
E_Zd_{ij}(Z)-d_{ij}(Z_i)
\right\|_{L^2}
\le
\beta_{ij,T}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Minkowski now gives

\[
\|H_T-H_T^{(j)}\|_{L^2}
\le
\frac1n
\left(
T+\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_{ij,T}
\right).
\tag{5.10}
\]

For completeness, the Efron–Stein step can be derived by the same coordinate martingale argument used in the current P15 peer report: decompose `H_T-EH_T` into Doob increments along `Z_1,...,Z_n`; the `j`th increment energy is at most one half of the squared `L^2` difference under an independent replacement of `Z_j`. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H_T)
\le
\frac12\sum_j E(H_T-H_T^{(j)})^2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Combining `(5.10)` and `(5.11)` proves `(5.3)`. Finally Chebyshev gives `(5.4)`--`(5.5)`.

QED.

### What was gained

The theorem never assumes

\[
\sup_z|\ell(f_{-i};z)-\ell(f_{-i}^{(j)};z)|\ll1.
\]

Only a **fresh RMS** replacement quantity appears. This is exactly the type of debt furnished by the covariance-boundary theorem.

The price is polynomial confidence `1/sqrt(eta)`. Exponential confidence would require a stronger influence-moment hypothesis; that is left explicit rather than smuggled in.

# 6. Prediction-RMS specialization

The clipped squared loss is globally `2 sqrt(T)`-Lipschitz in the prediction. Indeed, the scalar map

\[
r\mapsto\min\{r^2,T\}
\]

is `2 sqrt(T)`-Lipschitz on `[0,infinity)`, and the norm is 1-Lipschitz. Hence

\[
\boxed{
|\ell_T(f;(x,y))-\ell_T(g;(x,y))|
\le
2\sqrt T\,\|f(x)-g(x)\|.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
B_{ij}^2
=E\|f_{-i}(X)-f_{-i}^{(j)}(X)\|^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{ij,T}
\le
2\sqrt T\,B_{ij}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore `P19-LOO-RMS-CLIPPED-32` yields the explicit bound

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
\le
\frac1{n\sqrt{2\eta}}
\left[
\sum_{j=1}^n
\left(
T+2\sqrt T\sum_{i\ne j}B_{ij}
\right)^2
\right]^{1/2}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Homogeneous stability corollary

If

\[
B_{ij}\le B/n
\qquad(i\ne j),
\tag{6.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
\le
\frac{T+2B\sqrt T}{\sqrt{2\eta n}}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus an `O(1/n)` fresh predictor replacement scale is enough for vanishing same-observation LOO generalization despite the absence of a pathwise loss-stability constant.

# 7. `P19-LOO-RMS-RAW-RISK-32` — PROVED

The clipping in Section 5 can be removed by an explicit tail integral.

Let

\[
R_i=E_Z\ell(f_{-i};Z)
\tag{7.1}
\]

be the raw population risk of the `i`th deletion learner. Assume an explicit conditional tail envelope, valid almost surely in the deletion training sample,

\[
P\{\ell(f_{-i};Z)>t\mid S_{-i}\}
\le
A_i e^{-t/K_i}
\qquad(t\ge T).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Here `A_i,K_i` may be deterministic certified envelopes; if data-dependent envelopes are used, their validity must itself be certified. No such source values are guessed.

Since

\[
R_i-R_{i,T}
=E(\ell-T)_+
=\int_T^\infty P\{\ell>t\}\,dt,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
R_i-R_{i,T}
\le
A_iK_i e^{-T/K_i}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Put

\[
\tau_T
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n A_iK_i e^{-T/K_i}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Because the clipped empirical LOO risk is bounded above by the raw empirical LOO risk

\[
\widehat R_{\rm loo}^{raw}
=\frac1n\sum_i\ell(f_{-i};Z_i),
\tag{7.5}
\]

Section 5 gives, with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\overline R_{\rm loo}^{raw}
:=\frac1n\sum_iR_i
\le
\widehat R_{\rm loo}^{raw}
+\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
+\tau_T.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This is a **raw-loss** result. The deployed model has not been clipped.

If `A_i<=A`, `K_i<=K`, `B_ij<=B/n`, and one chooses `T=K log n`, then at fixed confidence

\[
\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
=O\left(\frac{\log n+\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt n}\right),
\qquad
\tau_T\le AK/n.
\tag{7.7}
\]

No Gaussian/ReLU tail law is assumed for the inaccessible source pipeline; `(7.2)` remains an explicit conditional obligation there.

# 8. `P19-FULL-FROM-LOO-RMS-32` — PROVED

The preceding theorem controls average deletion risk. To return to the actual full-data trained predictor, define

\[
D_i
=\|f_S-f_{-i}\|_{L^2(P_X)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For every `i`, the reverse triangle inequality in the residual Hilbert space gives

\[
\sqrt{R(f_S)}
\le
\sqrt{R_i}+D_i.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Average over `i` and use concavity of the square root:

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{R(f_S)}
\le
\sqrt{\overline R_{\rm loo}^{raw}}
+\overline D,
\qquad
\overline D=\frac1n\sum_iD_i.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Combining with `(7.6)` yields the main one-run certificate:

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{R(f_S)}
\le
\sqrt{
\widehat R_{\rm loo}^{raw}
+\varepsilon_{T,\eta}
+\tau_T
}
+\overline D
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

with probability at least `1-eta`.

This separates four debts cleanly:

1. observed raw LOO error;
2. RMS replacement concentration `epsilon_(T,eta)`;
3. raw-loss tail remainder `tau_T`;
4. full-vs-deletion drift `Dbar`.

None is replaced by an iid pruning mask.

# 9. `P19-FRESH-RMS-NOT-REUSE-CE-32` — PROVED counterexample

A tempting shortcut would be to apply the fresh RMS replacement debt of the **full-sample learner** directly to its reused training loss. That is false even for bounded losses.

Let the input space be

\[
\mathcal X=\{1,\ldots,M\},
\]

with `X` uniform and deterministic target `Y=0`. Given training inputs `S=(X_1,...,X_n)`, define

\[
\boxed{
f_S(x)=1\{x\notin\{X_1,\ldots,X_n\}\}.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The squared training loss is exactly zero, because every training point belongs to the observed set:

\[
\widehat R_{train}(f_S)=0.
\tag{9.2}
\]

But conditional on the sample,

\[
R(f_S)
=1-rac{|\{X_1,\ldots,X_n\}|}{M}
\ge1-rac nM.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus for `M>>n`, population risk is arbitrarily close to one.

Now replace one training input. The observed-set indicator can change on at most two atoms, so for a fresh uniform query

\[
\boxed{
E_X|f_S(X)-f_{S^{(j)}}(X)|^2
\le\frac2M.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Hence the fresh predictor replacement RMS is at most

\[
\sqrt{2/M}\to0,
\]

while the same-sample generalization gap remains order one.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{small fresh RMS replacement}
+\text{reused empirical agreement}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{population agreement}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

The LOO construction catches this memorizer: the held-out point is unobserved by `f_{-i}` unless another training point duplicates it, and

\[
E\widehat R_{\rm loo}
=P\{X_i\notin\{X_\ell:\ell\ne i\}\}
=(1-1/M)^{n-1},
\tag{9.6}
\]

which is close to one for `M>>n`.

This counterexample is the rigorous reason Section 5 uses deletion/ghost independence rather than a direct full-sample RMS-stability shortcut.

# 10. `P19-BOUNDARY-LOO-RAW-32` — hard-pruning specialization

The preceding run's boundary theorem now plugs into the only place where fresh RMS replacement is needed.

For deletion learner `i` write its hard-pruned predictor as

\[
f_{-i}(x)=H_k(C_{-i})\phi_{-i}(x),
\tag{10.1}
\]

and under replacement `j` as

\[
f_{-i}^{(j)}(x)
=H_k(C_{-i}^{(j)})\phi_{-i}^{(j)}(x).
\tag{10.2}
\]
