# 8. Exact Gaussian spectral-ridge boundary null

The cleanest calibration is spectral pruning in the empirical feature-eigenbasis of the honest proxy.

Let \(M\) be the proxy sample size. Let

\[
Z\in\mathbb R^{M\times p}
\]

be fixed and response-independent. Define the empirical feature Gram matrix

\[
G
=
\frac1MZ^*Z
=
V\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_p)V^*,
\qquad x_j\ge0.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Assume a pure Gaussian output-noise null

\[
Y=E,
\qquad
E_{ia}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\sigma^2).
\tag{8.2}
\]

The ridge readout is

\[
W
=
(G+\lambda I)^{-1}
\frac1MZ^*E.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Define spectral coordinates and the corresponding canonical readout

\[
\widetilde W=V^*W,
\qquad
C_{\rm spec}=\widetilde W^*
=W^*V
\in\mathbb R^{q\times p}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Its \(j\)-th column is the transpose of the \(j\)-th spectral row of \(\widetilde W\).

## `P19-GAUSSIAN-SPECTRAL-RIDGE-NULL-20` — NEW / PROVED

Conditionally on \(Z\), the columns \(c_j\) of \(C_{\rm spec}\) are mutually independent centered isotropic Gaussian vectors:

\[
\boxed{
c_j
\sim
N(0,\nu_j^2I_q),
\qquad
\nu_j^2
=
\frac{
\sigma^2x_j
}{
M(x_j+\lambda)^2
}.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

If \(x_j=0\), then \(c_j=0\) deterministically.

For every nonzero column, write

\[
c_j=R_jU_j.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Then, conditionally on \(Z\):

1. \(U_j\) are iid Haar on \(S^{q-1}\);
2. the full direction family \((U_j)\) is independent of the full radius family \((R_j)\);
3. any boundary selected from the **same realized spectral column norms** \(R_j\) is therefore a legitimate score-conditioned radial boundary.

This is an exact same-score selection theorem, not an iid-mask theorem.

### Proof

Let

\[
\widetilde Z=ZV.
\]

Then

\[
\widetilde Z^*\widetilde Z
=M\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_p),
\tag{8.7}
\]

so the spectral feature columns are pairwise orthogonal.

From `(8.3)`,

\[
\widetilde W_{j,:}
=
\frac1{x_j+\lambda}
\frac1M
\widetilde z_j^*E.
\tag{8.8}
\]

For each output coordinate, this is a centered Gaussian linear functional. Its variance is

\[
\frac1{(x_j+\lambda)^2}
\frac{\sigma^2}{M^2}
\|\widetilde z_j\|^2
=
\frac{\sigma^2x_j}{M(x_j+\lambda)^2}.
\tag{8.9}
\]

For \(j\ne\ell\), the cross-covariance vanishes because

\[
\widetilde z_j^*\widetilde z_\ell=0.
\]

The whole family is jointly Gaussian, hence the spectral rows are independent. Isotropy across the \(q\) output coordinates yields `(8.5)`. The radius/direction factorization of an isotropic Gaussian vector gives the remaining claims.

QED.
