# 4. Exact hard top-`k` setup

Let

\[
C=(c_1,\ldots,c_r),
\qquad
C'=(c'_1,\ldots,c'_r)
\in\mathbb R^{q\times r}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Use channel scores

\[
s_j(C)=\|c_j\|_2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `S=S_k(C)` and `S'=S_k(C')` be the deterministic exact-cardinality top-`k` supports under the inherited fixed tie rule. Put

\[
H_k(C)=CP_S,
\qquad
H_k(C')=C'P_{S'}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Assume

\[
\boxed{\|C-C'\|_F\le\rho.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then for every channel

\[
|s_j(C)-s_j(C')|
\le\|c_j-c'_j\|
\le\rho.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let `s_(1)>=...>=s_(r)` denote the ordered scores of `C`. Define the explicit boundary

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_\rho(C)
={}&
\{j\in S:
 s_j(C)\le s_{(k+1)}(C)+2\rho\}
\\
&\cup
\{j\notin S:
 s_j(C)\ge s_{(k)}(C)-2\rho\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

When `k=r`, interpret the first threshold as `-infinity` and the support never changes; the nontrivial case below is `1<=k<r`.

Define the ordinary boundary operator profile

\[
\Omega_k(\rho;C)
=
\|CP_{J_\rho(C)}\|_{op}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Now let `psi(X) in R^r` be any common fresh-query proxy feature vector with finite second moment

\[
G=E[\psi(X)\psi(X)^*].
\tag{4.8}
\]

No centering is needed for the exact second-moment identity below. Define restricted upper and lower Gram edges

\[
b_s(G)=
\sup_{|I|\le s}
\lambda_{max}(G_{II}),
\tag{4.9}
\]

\[
a_s(G)=
\inf_{|I|\le s}
\lambda_{min}(G_{II}).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Finally define the **covariance-weighted boundary-swap profile**

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C)
=
\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q_{k,\rho}(C)}
\|CQG^{1/2}\|_F,
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

where `Q` ranges over diagonal matrices with entries in `{-1,0,1}` such that

- `supp(Q) subset J_rho(C)`;
- the number of `+1` entries equals the number of `-1` entries;
- each number is at most `k`.

This class contains every cardinality-preserving support-swap matrix `P_S-P_S'` whose disagreements lie in the boundary.

# 5. `P19-HARD-BOUNDARY-SYMDIFF-31` — PROVED

Under `(4.4)`,

\[
\boxed{
S_k(C)\triangle S_k(C')
\subseteq
J_\rho(C).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Proof

Take `j in S\S'`. Because both supports have cardinality `k`, choose `h in S'\S`. Since `h` is selected by `C'` while `j` is not,

\[
s_h(C')\ge s_j(C').
\]

By `(4.5)`,

\[
s_j(C)
\le s_j(C')+\rho
\le s_h(C')+\rho
\le s_h(C)+2\rho.
\]

Since `h notin S`,

\[
s_h(C)\le s_{(k+1)}(C).
\]

Therefore

\[
s_j(C)
\le s_{(k+1)}(C)+2\rho,
\]

so `j` lies in the selected side of `J_rho(C)`.

Conversely, take `h in S'\S` and choose `j in S\S'`. Then

\[
s_h(C)
\ge s_h(C')-\rho
\ge s_j(C')-\rho
\ge s_j(C)-2\rho.
\]

Since `j in S`,

\[
s_j(C)\ge s_{(k)}(C),
\]

hence

\[
s_h(C)
\ge s_{(k)}(C)-2\rho.
\]

So `h` lies in the unselected side of the boundary. This proves `(5.1)`.

QED.

### Immediate operator corollary

The exact decomposition

\[
H_k(C)-H_k(C')
=
(C-C')P_{S'}
+
C(P_S-P_{S'})
\tag{5.2}
\]

and `(5.1)` give

\[
\boxed{
\|H_k(C)-H_k(C')\|_{op}
\le
\rho+\Omega_k(\rho;C).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, the first term has operator norm at most `rho`, while `Q=P_S-P_S'` is supported in `J_rho(C)` and `||CQ||_op<=||CP_J||_op`.

This reproduces the inherited hard-boundary mechanism by a self-contained proof and fixes the exact boundary used below.

# 6. `P19-COVBOUNDARY-PREDICTION-31` — PROVED

Under the setup of Section 4,

\[
\boxed{
\left(
E\|[H_k(C)-H_k(C')]\psi(X)\|_2^2
\right)^{1/2}
\le
\rho\sqrt{b_k(G)}
+
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is the central theorem of the run.

## Proof

For any deterministic matrix `M`,

\[
E\|M\psi\|^2
=
\operatorname{tr}(MGM^*)
=
\|MG^{1/2}\|_F^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Use the exact decomposition `(5.2)` and Minkowski in Frobenius norm:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(H_k(C)-H_k(C'))G^{1/2}\|_F
&\le
\|(C-C')P_{S'}G^{1/2}\|_F
\\
&\quad+
\|C(P_S-P_{S'})G^{1/2}\|_F.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For the smooth retraining term,

\[
\|(C-C')P_{S'}G^{1/2}\|_F
\le
\|C-C'\|_F
\|P_{S'}G^{1/2}\|_{op}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because `|S'|=k`,

\[
\|P_{S'}G^{1/2}\|_{op}^2
=
\lambda_{max}(G_{S'S'})
\le b_k(G).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus the first term is at most `rho sqrt(b_k(G))`.

For the support-swap term put

\[
Q=P_S-P_{S'}.
\]

Its diagonal entries lie in `{-1,0,1}`, it has equally many `+1` and `-1` entries, each count is at most `k`, and by `(5.1)` its support is contained in `J_rho(C)`. Hence

\[
\|CQG^{1/2}\|_F
\le
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Combining `(6.2)`--`(6.6)` proves `(6.1)`.

QED.

## Why the covariance-weighted profile is the correct prediction object

The ordinary operator boundary `Omega_k` is worst-case over every feature direction. Prediction under a given fresh-query feature law sees instead

\[
CQG^{1/2}.
\]

If the ambiguous boundary columns are large but the feature covariance cancels or suppresses the actual support-swap direction, `Psi` can be much smaller than `Omega`. Conversely, coherent feature covariance can make a small score boundary prediction-relevant.

Nothing in `(6.1)` assumes that the support was sampled independently. `S,S'` may be arbitrary deterministic functions of the two trained matrices.

# 7. `P19-BOUNDARY-RISK-31` — PROVED

Let

\[
f(X)=H_k(C)\psi(X),
\qquad
f'(X)=H_k(C')\psi(X),
\]

and let `Y` be any square-integrable target. For squared prediction risks

\[
R(f)=E\|Y-f(X)\|^2,
\qquad
R(f')=E\|Y-f'(X)\|^2,
\]

the reverse triangle inequality in `L^2` gives

\[
\boxed{
|\sqrt{R(f)}-\sqrt{R(f')}|
\le
\|f-f'\|_{L^2}
\le
\rho\sqrt{b_k(G)}
+
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus the boundary theorem is already a kernel/operator-learning **performance-stability** theorem. It says nothing about the sign of the risk change: a small pruning perturbation preserves performance, but does not prove that pruning improves it.

For two different legally aligned feature maps `phi,phi'` and a common proxy `psi`, define

\[
\zeta
=
\|H_k(C)(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^2},
\qquad
\zeta'
=
\|H_k(C')(\phi'-\psi)\|_{L^2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then by Minkowski,

\[
\boxed{
\|H_k(C)\phi-H_k(C')\phi'\|_{L^2}
\le
\rho\sqrt{b_k(G)}
+
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C)
+
\zeta+\zeta'.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This is the source-faithful nonlinear interface: representation drift is paid explicitly rather than silently absorbed into an iid feature null.

# 8. `P19-COVBOUNDARY-DYAD-31` — PROVED

Prediction stability becomes coefficient/singular-vector stability only with restricted feature identifiability.

Assume

\[
\boxed{a_{2k}(G)>0.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Put

\[
\delta_{pred}
=
\rho\sqrt{b_k(G)}
+
\Psi_{G,k}(\rho;C).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|H_k(C)-H_k(C')\|_{op}
\le
\frac{\delta_{pred}}{\sqrt{a_{2k}(G)}}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

## Proof

Let

\[
M=H_k(C)-H_k(C').
\]

Every nonzero column of `M` lies in

\[
S\cup S',
\]

whose size is at most `2k`.

For a unit output vector `y`, let

\[
w=M^*y.
\]

Then `w` is supported on at most `2k` coordinates. Hence

\[
a_{2k}(G)\|w\|^2
\le
w^*Gw
=
E|y^*M\psi|^2
\le
E\|M\psi\|^2.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Take square roots and the supremum over unit `y`, then use `(6.1)`. This proves `(8.3)`.

QED.

## Singular-projector / mixed-dyad consequence

Let a positive singular cluster of the deployed readout be separated from the rest of the singular spectrum by an **observed genuine gap** `Delta_K>0`. Only if

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta_{pred}}{\sqrt{a_{2k}(G)}}
\le
\Delta_K/2
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

do we invoke Wedin/Hermitian dilation. With the inherited conservative mixed-dyad constant,

\[
\boxed{
\|\Delta\Pi_K^L\|_{op},
\quad
\|\Delta\Pi_K^R\|_{op},
\quad
\|\Delta F_K\|_{op}
\le
\frac{4\,\delta_{pred}}
{\Delta_K\sqrt{a_{2k}(G)}}.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

This is **stability between two deployed matrices**. It is not the July BBP overlap theorem. The July factors `alpha_L,alpha_R` remain resolvent-residue quantities under the independent-spike compressed-resolvent hypotheses.

# 9. `P19-SPARSE-RIP-BOUNDARY-CERT-31` — PROVED

The previous theorem has a clean Marchenko–Pastur-type honest-null calibration.

Assume the feature Gram satisfies the simultaneous `2k` restricted covariance event

\[
(1-\delta)I
\preceq
G_{II}
\preceq
(1+\delta)I
\qquad
\text{for every }|I|\le2k,
\tag{9.1}
\]

with `0<=delta<1`.

Then

\[
a_{2k}(G)\ge1-\delta,
\qquad
b_k(G)\le1+\delta.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Define the measurable boundary Frobenius budget

\[
\boxed{
B_{2k,\rho}(C)
=
\max_{\substack{D\subseteq J_\rho(C)\\|D|\le2k}}
\|CP_D\|_F.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

For every admissible swap matrix `Q`, the nonzero support `D` has size at most `2k`, and conjugating `G_D` by the diagonal sign matrix in `Q` does not change its eigenvalues. Therefore

\[
\|CQG^{1/2}\|_F
\le
\sqrt{1+\delta}\,\|CQ\|_F
\le
\sqrt{1+\delta}\,B_{2k,\rho}(C).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{pred}
\le
\sqrt{1+\delta}
\bigl[\rho+B_{2k,\rho}(C)\bigr].
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|H_k(C)-H_k(C')\|_{op}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}}
\bigl[\rho+B_{2k,\rho}(C)\bigr].
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

With a singular gap, `(8.6)` applies using this explicit debt.

## Honest Gaussian finite-width null

For an honest feature-sample matrix `X in R^{n x r}` with iid `N(0,1)` entries and empirical Gram

\[
G_n=\frac1nX^*X,
\tag{9.7}
\]

a standard chi-square Chernoff plus `1/4`-net argument gives, for `0<delta<1`,

\[
P\left\{
\sup_{|I|\le s}
\|(G_n)_{II}-I\|_{op}>\delta
\right\}
\le
2\mathfrak N_{r,s}
\exp\left(-\frac{n\delta^2}{32}\right),
\tag{9.8}
\]

where

\[
\mathfrak N_{r,s}
=
\sum_{d=1}^s{r\choose d}9^d.
\tag{9.9}
\]

Taking `s=2k` therefore supplies `(9.1)` whenever

\[
k\log(er/k)+\log(1/\eta)=o(n)
\tag{9.10}
\]

at asymptotically vanishing `delta`.

This is a **restricted extreme-eigenvalue** theorem, not a consequence of the MP empirical spectral distribution alone. It can remain valid when `r/n>1`, where the global empirical Gram has an exact nullspace and the global MP law has a zero atom.

Thus the MP hard edge and sparse pruning identifiability answer different questions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{global MP singularity}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{failure of sparse boundary/vector certificates}.
}
\tag{9.11}
\]

Conversely, an MP-looking bulk cannot guarantee `(9.1)`: the inherited duplicate-column counterexample changes only finite rank in the ambient ESD while killing a local restricted eigenvalue.

# 10. `P19-CLIPPED-BOUNDARY-STABILITY-31` — PROVED under an explicit clipped feature interface

The previous run needed a **uniform clipped-loss replacement constant** to obtain a high-probability reused-data certificate. The hard selector itself was the missing discontinuous step.

The boundary theorem supplies that step when the pruning-relevant deployed feature interface is uniformly clipped, or when an equivalent deterministic bounded feature certificate is available.

Let neighboring samples produce `C,C'` satisfying `(4.4)`, and let `J=S union S'`, so `|J|<=2k`. For radius `R`, radially clip the restricted aligned feature vectors:

\[
z_S(x)=T_R(P_J\phi_S(x)),
\qquad
z_{S'}(x)=T_R(P_J\phi_{S'}(x)),
\tag{10.1}
\]

embedded back into `R^r` on support `J`.

Assume the uniform clipped feature replacement debt

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x
\|z_S(x)-z_{S'}(x)\|
\le a_{2k,R}
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

and a pruned-readout bound

\[
\|H_k(C')\|_{op}\le M_k.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Define the clipped-interface predictors

\[
f_S^{[R]}(x)=H_k(C)z_S(x),
\qquad
f_{S'}^{[R]}(x)=H_k(C')z_{S'}(x).
\tag{10.4}
\]

Then by `(5.3)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|f_S^{[R]}(x)-f_{S'}^{[R]}(x)\|
&\le
\|(H_k(C)-H_k(C'))z_S(x)\|
\\
&\quad+
\|H_k(C')[z_S(x)-z_{S'}(x)]\|
\\
&\le
R[\rho+\Omega_k(\rho;C)]
+M_k a_{2k,R}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.5}
\]

For squared loss clipped at level `T`,

\[
\ell_T(f;y)=\min\{\|y-f\|^2,T\},
\tag{10.6}
\]

the map `f -> ell_T(f;y)` is globally `2 sqrt(T)`-Lipschitz. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|\ell_T(f_S^{[R]}(x);y)
-
\ell_T(f_{S'}^{[R]}(x);y)|
\le
2\sqrt T
\left[
R(\rho+\Omega_k(\rho;C))
+M_k a_{2k,R}
\right].
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

Thus a valid uniform replacement constant is

\[
\boxed{
\beta^{post}_{T,R}
\le
2\sqrt T
\left[
R(\rho+\Omega_k(\rho;C))
+M_k a_{2k,R}
\right].
}
\tag{10.8}
\]

This is the precise analytic form sought by the previous run:

\[
\beta^{post}
\lesssim
\text{pre-pruning drift}
+
\text{boundary operator debt}
+
\text{aligned feature drift}.
\]

### Important scope boundary

`(10.8)` is exact for the radially clipped pruning-relevant feature interface, or for a raw interface already known to satisfy the corresponding radius bound. It is **not** silently asserted for an unbounded raw DNN feature vector. To transfer it back to raw prediction loss one must pay the clipping/tail remainder from the preceding P19 theorem or prove a source-specific feature envelope.

That remaining source obligation is real: clipping bounds losses but does not make the actual unbounded source feature map bounded unless the source pipeline uses or is compared to that clipping.

# 11. `P19-HARDPRUNE-ZERORISK-VECTOR-CE-31` — PROVED counterexample

Restricted feature identifiability in Section 8 is necessary.

Let `q=1`, `r=2`, `k=1`, and take any `epsilon>0`. Define

\[
C=(1+\varepsilon,\ 1),
\qquad
C'=(1,\ 1+\varepsilon).
\tag{11.1}
\]

Then

\[
\|C-C'\|_F
=\sqrt2\,\varepsilon
\to0.
\tag{11.2}
\]

Hard top-1 pruning selects channel 1 for `C` and channel 2 for `C'`:

\[
H_1(C)=(1+\varepsilon,0),
\qquad
H_1(C')=(0,1+\varepsilon).
\tag{11.3}
\]

Now let the two proxy features be exact duplicates,

\[
\psi(X)=(Z,Z),
\qquad
EZ^2=1.
\tag{11.4}
\]

Then

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a_2(G)=0.
\tag{11.5}
\]

The two **pruned predictions agree pointwise**:

\[
H_1(C)\psi
=(1+\varepsilon)Z
=H_1(C')\psi.
\tag{11.6}
\]

Hence every fresh-query prediction discrepancy is zero, and every clipped prediction loss against the same target is identical.

But the coefficient operators differ by

\[
\|H_1(C)-H_1(C')\|_{op}
=\sqrt2(1+\varepsilon),
\tag{11.7}
\]

and their one-dimensional right singular subspaces are

\[
\operatorname{span}(e_1)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{span}(e_2),
\tag{11.8}
\]

which are orthogonal.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{arbitrarily small pre-pruning drift}
+
\text{zero post-pruning prediction drift}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{singular-vector stability}.
}
\tag{11.9}
\]

The missing hypothesis is exactly a positive restricted feature-Gram floor such as `a_(2k)(G)>0`.

This counterexample is distinct from the inherited hard-top-`k` jump example: here the discontinuous support change is completely invisible in prediction space because the features are nonidentifiable.

# 13. Spectral quantities kept separate
