# 3. `P19-STABLE-REUSED-SPARSE-COV-29` — PROVED

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\sim\mathcal D^n
\]

and, after conditioning on a common algorithmic random seed if necessary, let the learner output a sample-dependent vector map

\[
\xi_S(x)\in\mathbb R^r.
\]

For

\[
\mathcal V_s=\{v:\|v\|_2=1,\ \|v\|_0\le s\},
\]

define

\[
\ell_v(S,z)=|v^*\xi_S(x)|^2.
\]

Let

\[
H_S=E_Z[\xi_S(X)\xi_S(X)^*],
\qquad
\widehat H_S=\frac1n\sum_i\xi_S(X_i)\xi_S(X_i)^*.
\]

Assume the **actual sample-dependent sparse squared loss** satisfies

\[
0\le\ell_v(S,z)\le M_s
\tag{3.1}
\]

for all `S,z,v`, and same-size replace-one stability

\[
\sup_{S\sim S'}\sup_z\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}
|\ell_v(S,z)-\ell_v(S',z)|\le\beta_s.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Set

\[
L_s(\eta)=s\log\frac{9er}{s}+\log\frac2\eta,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta)
=
2\beta_s+2[(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s]
\sqrt{\frac{L_s(\eta)}{2n}}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|J|\le s}\|(H_S-\widehat H_S)_{JJ}\|_{\rm op}
\le\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently, for

\[
\varepsilon_s^2(S)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}v^*H_Sv,
\qquad
\widehat\varepsilon_s^2(S)=\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}v^*\widehat H_Sv,
\]

\[
\boxed{|\varepsilon_s^2-\widehat\varepsilon_s^2|\le\tau_{\rm stab}}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_s\le\sqrt{\widehat\varepsilon_s^2+\tau_{\rm stab}}.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `\beta_s=O(1/n)`, `M_s=O(1)`, and `s\log(er/s)=o(n)`, the debt vanishes.

## Full proof

Fix `v` and define

\[
R_v(S)=E_Z\ell_v(S,Z),
\qquad
\widehat R_v(S)=n^{-1}\sum_i\ell_v(S,Z_i),
\]

\[
g_v(S)=R_v(S)-\widehat R_v(S).
\]

Let `Z_i'` be independent and let `S^{(i)}` replace `Z_i` by `Z_i'`. Exchangeability gives

\[
ER_v(S)=E\ell_v(S^{(i)},Z_i).
\]

Hence

\[
|Eg_v|
\le E|\ell_v(S^{(i)},Z_i)-\ell_v(S,Z_i)|
\le\beta_s.
\tag{3.7}
\]

If `S,S'` differ only at position `i`, the population term changes by at most `\beta_s`. In the reused empirical average, each unchanged evaluation point contributes at most `\beta_s`, and the changed evaluation term itself changes by at most `M_s`. Thus

\[
|\widehat R_v(S)-\widehat R_v(S')|
\le\frac{(n-1)\beta_s+M_s}{n},
\]

so

\[
|g_v(S)-g_v(S')|
\le
\frac{(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s}{n}=:c_s.
\tag{3.8}
\]

McDiarmid gives

\[
P\{|g_v-Eg_v|>c_s\sqrt{nt/2}\}\le2e^{-t}.
\]

Together with `(3.7)`,

\[
|g_v|
\le
\beta_s+[(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s]\sqrt{t/(2n)}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For each support of size `s`, choose a `1/4`-net of its unit sphere of cardinality at most `9^s`. Smaller supports may be enlarged, so the total net cardinality is at most

\[
{r\choose s}9^s\le(9er/s)^s.
\]

Take `t=L_s(eta)` and union bound. For every self-adjoint matrix `A` on an `s`-coordinate support, the standard quadratic-form net inequality gives

\[
\|A\|_{\rm op}\le2\max_{v\in\mathcal N}|v^*Av|.
\]

Apply this to `A=(H_S-\widehat H_S)_{JJ}`. The factor two converts `(3.9)` exactly into `(3.3)`, proving `(3.4)`. Taking suprema over sparse quadratic forms proves `(3.5)`–`(3.6)`. QED.

# 5. `P19-LOO-SPARSE-COV-29` — PROVED

For each `i`, retrain on `S_{-i}`. Let

\[
H_{-i}=E[\xi_{S_{-i}}(X)\xi_{S_{-i}}(X)^*],
\]

\[
\widehat H_{\rm loo}=n^{-1}\sum_i\xi_{S_{-i}}(X_i)\xi_{S_{-i}}(X_i)^*.
\]

Assume the size-`n-1` sparse loss is bounded by `M_s`, has replace-one stability `\beta_s^-`, and the full-vs-deletion loss bridge obeys

\[
\sup_i\sup_z\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}
|\ell_v(S,z)-\ell_v(S_{-i},z)|\le\delta_s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
\tau_{\rm loo}(s,\eta)
=
2[M_s+2(n-1)\beta_s^-]\sqrt{\frac{L_s(\eta)}{2n}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then with probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|J|\le s}\|(H_S-\widehat H_{\rm loo})_{JJ}\|_{\rm op}
\le\delta_s+\tau_{\rm loo}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

## Proof

For fixed `v`, define

\[
h_v(S)=n^{-1}\sum_i[R_v(S_{-i})-\ell_v(S_{-i},Z_i)].
\]

Because `Z_i` is independent of `S_{-i}`, `Eh_v=0`. Replacing base observation `Z_j` changes the `i=j` term by at most `M_s/n` because its deletion learner is unchanged and only the held-out evaluation point changes. For every `i\ne j`, the deletion learner changes by one training observation, so its population term changes by at most `\beta_s^-` and its loss on unchanged `Z_i` by at most `\beta_s^-`. Hence

\[
|h_v(S)-h_v(S')|
\le\frac{M_s+2(n-1)\beta_s^-}{n}.
\]

McDiarmid plus the same sparse net gives

\[
\sup_{|J|\le s}\left\|\left(n^{-1}\sum_iH_{-i}-\widehat H_{\rm loo}\right)_{JJ}\right\|_{\rm op}
\le\tau_{\rm loo}.
\]

Assumption `(5.1)` already holds for every sparse unit vector, so

\[
\sup_{|J|\le s}\left\|\left(H_S-n^{-1}\sum_iH_{-i}\right)_{JJ}\right\|_{\rm op}\le\delta_s.
\]

Triangle inequality proves `(5.3)`. QED.

This is the cleanest no-fresh-data route if actual deletion retrains are available.

# 8. `P19-HARD-TOPK-REPLACEONE-CE-29` — PROVED

Take two neighboring samples with scalar summaries

\[
t_S=1/n,\qquad t_{S'}=-1/n,
\]

and one-row trained matrix

\[
C(t)=(1+t,1-t).
\]

Then

\[
\|C(1/n)-C(-1/n)\|_{\rm op}=2\sqrt2/n.
\]

Exact hard top-1 magnitude pruning keeps coordinate 1 for `t>0` and coordinate 2 for `t<0`, so

\[
H_1(C(1/n))=(1+1/n,0),
\]
\[
H_1(C(-1/n))=(0,1+1/n),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|H_1(C(1/n))-H_1(C(-1/n))\|_{\rm op}
=\sqrt2(1+1/n).
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{pre-pruning replacement stability}\not\Rightarrow\text{post-hard-pruning replacement stability}.}
\]

This directly answers the dependence question: a trained-matrix pruning mask is a discontinuous deterministic function of trained scores near a boundary and cannot be replaced by an iid Bernoulli mask.

This counterexample does not contradict the inherited boundary-operator theorem. Both competing boundary columns have order-one norm, so its boundary operator profile is also order one. If the boundary profile is small, support flips may still be operator-stable; the valid repair is boundary localization, not iid-mask substitution.
