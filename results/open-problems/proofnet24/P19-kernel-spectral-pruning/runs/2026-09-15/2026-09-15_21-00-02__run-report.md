# 5. Stable reused sparse covariance theorem

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\sim\mathcal D^n
\]

be iid. After conditioning on any algorithmic seed coupled identically across neighboring samples, let a deterministic learning procedure output a feature-like vector map

\[
\xi_S(x)\in\mathbb R^r.
\]

The map may encode a trained feature difference, a trained proxy feature map, or another sample-dependent vector observable.

For

\[
\mathcal V_s
=
\{v\in\mathbb R^r:\|v\|_2=1,\ \|v\|_0\le s\},
\]

define the squared directional loss

\[
\ell_v(S,z)
=
|v^*\xi_S(x)|^2,
\qquad z=(x,y)\ \text{or simply }x.
\]

Define the population and reused empirical covariance matrices

\[
H_S
=
E_Z[\xi_S(X)\xi_S(X)^*],
\]

\[
\widehat H_S
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\xi_S(X_i)\xi_S(X_i)^*.
\]

Assume:

### Bounded sparse loss

\[
\boxed{
0\le \ell_v(S,z)\le M_s
\quad\text{for all }S,z,v\in\mathcal V_s.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Uniform replace-one stability

Whenever `S,S'` have the same size and differ in one observation,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_z\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}
|\ell_v(S,z)-\ell_v(S',z)|
\le \beta_s.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Set

\[
L_s(\eta)
=
s\log\frac{9er}{s}
+
\log\frac2\eta,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta)
=
2\beta_s
+
2\bigl[(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s\bigr]
\sqrt{\frac{L_s(\eta)}{2n}}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

## `P19-STABLE-REUSED-SPARSE-COV-29` — NEW / PROVED

With probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\|(H_S-\widehat H_S)_{JJ}\|_{\rm op}
\le
\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
\varepsilon_s^2(S)
=
\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}v^*H_Sv,
\qquad
\widehat\varepsilon_s^2(S)
=
\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}v^*\widehat H_Sv,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
|\varepsilon_s^2(S)-\widehat\varepsilon_s^2(S)|
\le
\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_s(S)
\le
\sqrt{
\widehat\varepsilon_s^2(S)
+
\tau_{\rm stab}(s,\eta)
}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

When

\[
\beta_s=O(n^{-1}),
\qquad
M_s=O(1),
\qquad
s\log(er/s)=o(n),
\]

the audit debt tends to zero.

## Proof

Fix `v` and write

\[
R_v(S)=E_Z\ell_v(S,Z),
\qquad
\widehat R_v(S)=\frac1n\sum_i\ell_v(S,Z_i),
\]

\[
g_v(S)=R_v(S)-\widehat R_v(S).
\]

### Step 1: expectation bias

Let `Z_i'` be an independent copy and `S^{(i)}` be `S` with observation `i` replaced by `Z_i'`. By iid exchangeability,

\[
E R_v(S)
=
E\ell_v(S^{(i)},Z_i).
\]

Therefore

\[
|Eg_v(S)|
\le
E|\ell_v(S^{(i)},Z_i)-\ell_v(S,Z_i)|
\le
\beta_s.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the exact place where replace-one stability pays for the reused-query dependence.

### Step 2: bounded difference of the generalization gap

Let `S,S'` differ only in observation `i`.

The population risk changes by at most `beta_s`.

For the empirical risk, each term `j != i` changes by at most `beta_s`. The changed term itself is a difference of two quantities in `[0,M_s]`, so it changes by at most `M_s`. Hence

\[
|\widehat R_v(S)-\widehat R_v(S')|
\le
\frac{(n-1)\beta_s+M_s}{n}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
|g_v(S)-g_v(S')|
\le
c_s
:=
\frac{(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s}{n}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

McDiarmid gives

\[
P\left\{
|g_v-Eg_v|
>
c_s\sqrt{\frac{nt}{2}}
\right\}
\le
2e^{-t}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Combining `(5.7)` and `(5.9)`,

\[
|g_v|
\le
\beta_s
+
\bigl[(2n-1)\beta_s+M_s\bigr]
\sqrt{\frac{t}{2n}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

### Step 3: sparse net

For every support `J` of size exactly `s`, take a `1/4`-net of its unit sphere of cardinality at most `9^s`. Supports of smaller size may be enlarged to size `s`, so the total net cardinality is at most

\[
{r\choose s}9^s
\le
\left(\frac{9er}{s}\right)^s.
\]

Use `(5.10)` with

\[
t=L_s(\eta).
\]

A union bound controls every net vector simultaneously with failure at most `eta`.

For any self-adjoint matrix `A` on an `s`-coordinate support, the standard quadratic-form net inequality at radius `1/4` gives

\[
\|A\|_{\rm op}
\le
2\max_{v\in\mathcal N}|v^*Av|.
\]

Apply this to

\[
A=(H_S-\widehat H_S)_{JJ}.
\]

The resulting factor `2` turns `(5.10)` exactly into `(5.3)` and proves `(5.4)`.

Taking suprema of the quadratic forms proves `(5.5)`–`(5.6)`.

QED.

# 6. A checkable feature-level sufficient condition

The squared-loss stability assumptions above can themselves be reduced to a directional feature stability condition.

Suppose

\[
\sup_{S,z,v\in\mathcal V_s}
|v^*\xi_S(x)|
\le B_s,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and for neighboring samples

\[
\sup_{S\sim S'}\sup_{z,v\in\mathcal V_s}
|v^*(\xi_S(x)-\xi_{S'}(x))|
\le a_s.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
M_s=B_s^2,
\]

and from

\[
|a^2-b^2|
\le
|a-b|(|a|+|b|),
\]

\[
\boxed{
\beta_s\le2B_sa_s.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus `a_s=O(1/n)` with `B_s=O(1)` is sufficient for the useful stability regime.

If a legally aligned parameterization obeys

\[
\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s,x}
|v^*(\xi_\theta(x)-\xi_{\theta'}(x))|
\le
L_{s,\theta}\|\theta-\theta'\|,
\]

and same-randomness neighboring retraining gives

\[
\|\theta_S-\theta_{S'}\|
\le
A/n,
\]

then

\[
a_s\le L_{s,\theta}A/n.
\]

This final parameter-stability premise is **not** asserted for the inaccessible P19 source pipeline. It is an explicit conditional obligation.

For unbounded Gaussian/ReLU features, `(5.1)` is not automatic. One must either truncate/clip with an explicit tail debt, prove an unbounded-loss stability concentration theorem, or use the preceding fresh-query subgaussian theorem. The bounded theorem is not silently applied outside its hypotheses.

# 7. Leave-one-out sparse covariance theorem

The same-sample theorem can be sharpened when actual deletion retrains are available.

For each `i`, train the size-`n-1` map

\[
\xi_{S_{-i}}.
\]

Define

\[
H_{-i}
=
E_Z[\xi_{S_{-i}}(X)\xi_{S_{-i}}(X)^*],
\]

and the honest deletion-evaluation covariance

\[
\widehat H_{\rm loo}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\xi_{S_{-i}}(X_i)\xi_{S_{-i}}(X_i)^*.
\]

Assume the same sparse loss bound `M_s` for size-`n-1` learners and replace-one stability

\[
\beta^-_s
\]

between any two size-`n-1` training samples differing in one observation.

Also assume the explicit full-vs-deletion bridge

\[
\boxed{
\sup_i\sup_z\sup_{v\in\mathcal V_s}
|\ell_v(S,z)-\ell_v(S_{-i},z)|
\le
\delta_s.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm loo}(s,\eta)
=
2\bigl[M_s+2(n-1)\beta^-_s\bigr]
\sqrt{\frac{L_s(\eta)}{2n}}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

## `P19-LOO-SPARSE-COV-29` — NEW / PROVED

With probability at least `1-eta`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\|(H_S-\widehat H_{\rm loo})_{JJ}\|_{\rm op}
\le
\delta_s+\tau_{\rm loo}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
|\varepsilon_s^2(S)-\widehat\varepsilon_{\rm loo,s}^2|
\le
\delta_s+\tau_{\rm loo}(s,\eta).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

## Proof

For fixed `v`, define

\[
h_v(S)
=
\frac1n\sum_i
\left[
R_v(S_{-i})
-
\ell_v(S_{-i},Z_i)
\right].
\]

Because `Z_i` is independent of `S_{-i}`,

\[
Eh_v=0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Replace one base observation `Z_j`.

- For term `i=j`, the deletion training set `S_{-j}` is unchanged; only the held-out evaluation point changes. That term changes by at most `M_s/n`.
- For each `i != j`, the size-`n-1` training set changes by one observation. Its population risk changes by at most `beta_s^-`, and its loss on the unchanged held-out `Z_i` changes by at most `beta_s^-`. Each such bracket changes by at most `2 beta_s^-/n`.

Hence

\[
|h_v(S)-h_v(S')|
\le
\frac{M_s+2(n-1)\beta_s^-}{n}.
\]

McDiarmid, the same support net, and the same quadratic-form factor `2` give

\[
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\left\|
\left(
\frac1n\sum_iH_{-i}
-
\widehat H_{\rm loo}
\right)_{JJ}
\right\|_{\rm op}
\le
\tau_{\rm loo}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Finally `(7.1)` implies, for every sparse unit `v`,

\[
\left|
v^*H_Sv
-
\frac1n\sum_i v^*H_{-i}v
\right|
\le
\delta_s.
\]

Because this bound already holds for **all** sparse unit vectors, no extra net factor is needed for this deterministic bridge. Combining it with `(7.6)` proves `(7.3)`–`(7.4)`.

QED.

### Interpretation

The leave-one-out estimator uses only the original dataset but evaluates each observation under a learner that did not see it. Its remaining debt is explicit whole-pipeline deletion/replacement stability, not fictitious evaluation independence.

\[

=
\min_{|J|\le s}
\lambda_{\min}
((\widehat G_{\psi,S})_{JJ}),
\]

\[
\widehat b_s
=
\max_{|J|\le s}
\lambda_{\max}
((\widehat G_{\psi,S})_{JJ}).
\]

Let `tau_psi` be the Section-5 audit debt for `psi_S`, and `tau_Delta` the audit debt for `Delta_S`, with failure probabilities allocated by a union bound. Put

\[
\underline a_s
=
\widehat a_s-\tau_\psi,
\qquad
\overline b_s
=
\widehat b_s+\tau_\psi,
\]

\[
\overline\varepsilon_s
=
\sqrt{
\widehat\varepsilon_s^2+\tau_\Delta
},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\overline d_s
=
2\sqrt{\overline b_s}\,\overline\varepsilon_s
+
\overline\varepsilon_s^2.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The inherited deterministic trained-to-proxy Gram comparison is

\[
\sup_{|J|\le s}
\|(G_{\phi,S}-G_{\psi,S})_{JJ}\|_{\rm op}
\le
2\sqrt{b_s^\psi}\,\varepsilon_s+arepsilon_s^2.

## `P19-STABLE-REUSED-TRAINED-RIP-29` — NEW / PROVED

On the joint audit event, for every `|J|<=s`,

\[
\boxed{
(\underline a_s-\overline d_s)I
\preceq
(G_{\phi,S})_{JJ}
\preceq
(\overline b_s+\overline d_s)I.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus if

\[
\boxed{\underline a_s>\overline d_s,}
\tag{8.3}
\]

the trained representation has a certified positive sparse population lower eigenvalue although the same observations were used both to train and to form the empirical audit.

For block pruning with maximum block width `b`, take

\[
s=2bk.
\]

The inherited deterministic Schur/RIP theorem then gives the adaptive prune-and-refit weak-submodularity ratio

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k
\ge
\frac{\underline a_s-\overline d_s}
{\overline b_s+\overline d_s}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Hence greedy prune-and-refit retains the inherited guarantee

\[
F(S_g)
\ge

# 10. Hard trained-matrix pruning can destroy replacement stability

The preceding theorem deliberately assumes stability of the observable **after** all data-dependent operations represented in `Z_j`. That is necessary for hard pruning.

Take `n` odd and two neighboring samples whose scalar summary is

\[
t_S=1/n,
\qquad
t_{S'}=-1/n.
\]

Define a one-row trained matrix

\[
C(t)=(1+t,\ 1-t).
\]

Then

\[
\|C(1/n)-C(-1/n)\|_{\rm op}
=
\frac{2\sqrt2}{n}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Let `H_1` be exact hard top-1 magnitude pruning. For `t>0` it keeps coordinate 1; for `t<0` it keeps coordinate 2. Therefore

\[
H_1(C(1/n))
=(1+1/n,0),
\]

\[
H_1(C(-1/n))
=(0,1+1/n),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|H_1(C(1/n))-H_1(C(-1/n))\|_{\rm op}
=
\sqrt2(1+1/n).
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

## `P19-HARD-TOPK-REPLACEONE-CE-29` — NEW / PROVED

An `O(1/n)` neighboring-sample perturbation of the **unpruned** trained matrix can produce an `O(1)` change after hard top-`k` pruning at a score boundary.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{pre-pruning replacement stability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{post-hard-pruning replacement stability}.
}
\]

This is exactly why pruning based on the trained matrix cannot be replaced by an iid Bernoulli mask.

The counterexample does **not** contradict the inherited boundary-operator theorem. Here both competing boundary columns have `Theta(1)` norm, so the boundary operator profile is also `Theta(1)` and correctly warns that the hard selector is unstable. If the trained score boundary has small operator profile, the previous deterministic boundary-transfer theorem can be used to establish post-pruning stability without requiring the support itself to remain unchanged.

# 11. Relation to Marchenko–Pastur, deterministic equivalents, and free probability

The new theorem concerns a **sample-dependent local restricted covariance**, not a global empirical spectral distribution.

The spectral layers remain distinct:

1. **Global ESD:** an honest iid proxy may have a Marchenko–Pastur ESD.
2. **Extreme global eigenvalues:** the MP/Bai–Yin edges control global conditioning only when the aspect ratio permits it.
3. **Sparse local restricted eigenvalues:** these can remain near one under
   \[

   even when the global Gram is singular and the MP law has a zero atom.
4. **Singular-vector overlap:** the July `alpha_L,alpha_R` laws come from compressed resolvent residues and spike/noise independence, not from an ESD fit.
5. **Prediction risk/stability:** structured prune-and-refit performance depends on covariance/Schur geometry and actual prediction debts, not only eigenvalue histograms.
6. **Finite-width effects:** Sections 5–9 retain explicit finite `n,r,s` complexity and stability terms.

No free-probability heuristic is needed for Sections 5–10. If a proved free-probability reduction or deterministic equivalent supplies the honest proxy covariance law, it can be inserted at the proxy stage, but it cannot erase the trained-to-proxy or same-sample stability debts.

Useful for proxy spectra and prediction risk under explicit random-feature models. They remain insufficient for a response-trained representation unless training dependence is visible in the theorem or paid by stability.

Potentially useful for a verified proxy law under explicit asymptotic freeness hypotheses. No unproved freeness is inserted for the same-sample trained feature map.

A fresh iid mask or query block supports ordinary concentration. Deterministic magnitude pruning selected from the trained matrix does not. The valid same-sample substitute here is stability plus an all-support sparse net, not iid-mask replacement.
