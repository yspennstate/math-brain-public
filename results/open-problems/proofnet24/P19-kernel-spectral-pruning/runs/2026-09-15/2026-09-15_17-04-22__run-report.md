# 5. Exact incremental Schur-gain identity

Fix disjoint block sets

\[
A,L\subset[p].
\]

Write `Z_A=\xi_{U(A)}` and `Z_L=\xi_{U(L)}`. Let

\[
e_A
=
Y-\operatorname{Proj}_{\rm lin}(Y\mid Z_A)
\tag{5.1}
\]

be the optimal linear residual after fitting on `A`, and let

\[
u_L
=
Z_L-\Sigma_{LA}\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}Z_A
\tag{5.2}
\]

be the candidate features residualized against `A`. Here covariance subscripts denote the corresponding coordinate unions.

Define

\[
M_{L\mid A}
=
E[u_Lu_L^*]
=
\Sigma_{LL}-\Sigma_{LA}\Sigma_{AA}^{-1}\Sigma_{AL},
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
K_{L\mid A}
=
E[e_Au_L^*].
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `\Sigma\succ0`, every such Schur complement is positive definite.

## `P19-REFIT-BLOCK-MARGINAL-25` — NEW / PROVED

The exact joint gain from adding all blocks in `L` after `A` is

\[
\boxed{
F(A\cup L)-F(A)
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
K_{L\mid A}M_{L\mid A}^{-1}K_{L\mid A}^*
\right].
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For a single block `b\notin A`,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_b(A)
:=F(A\cup\{b\})-F(A)
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
K_{b\mid A}M_{bb\mid A}^{-1}K_{b\mid A}^*
\right].
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Proof

Once the optimal predictor based on `A` has been removed, adding `L` is exactly the least-squares problem

\[
\min_B E\|e_A-Bu_L\|^2.
\]

Its normal equation is

\[
B^*=K_{L\mid A}M_{L\mid A}^{-1}.
\]

Completing the square gives the risk reduction `(5.5)`. Taking `L={b}` gives `(5.6)`. No Gaussianity is used. QED.

# 6. Conditional block-correlation geometry

For disjoint `A,L`, let

\[
D_{L\mid A}
=
\bigoplus_{b\in L}M_{bb\mid A}
\tag{6.1}
\]

be the block-diagonal part of the conditional covariance. Define the normalized conditional block-correlation operator

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_{L\mid A}
=
D_{L\mid A}^{-1/2}
M_{L\mid A}
D_{L\mid A}^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

For a block budget `k`, define

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k
=
\inf\left\{
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal R_{L\mid A}):
A\cap L=\varnothing,
\ |A|\le k-1,
\ 1\le |L|\le k
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since `\Sigma\succ0` and the block family is finite, `\gamma_k>0`, though it may be arbitrarily small as the covariance approaches a singular correlated configuration.

## `P19-BLOCK-WEAK-SUBMOD-25` — NEW / PROVED

For every admissible disjoint `A,L`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{b\in L}\Delta_b(A)
\ge
\gamma_k
\left[
F(A\cup L)-F(A)
\right].
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus `F` has submodularity ratio at least `\gamma_k` for the equal-cost block budget `k`.

### Proof

Write

\[
M=M_{L\mid A},
\qquad
D=D_{L\mid A},
\qquad
K=K_{L\mid A},
\qquad
\mathcal R=D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2}.
\]

By `(5.6)`, summing blockwise singleton gains gives

\[
\sum_{b\in L}\Delta_b(A)
=
\operatorname{tr}[KD^{-1}K^*].
\tag{6.5}
\]

By `(5.5)`, the joint gain is

\[
F(A\cup L)-F(A)
=
\operatorname{tr}[KM^{-1}K^*].
\tag{6.6}
\]

Since

\[
M^{-1}
=D^{-1/2}\mathcal R^{-1}D^{-1/2}
\]

and

\[
\mathcal R\succeq\gamma_kI,
\]

we have

\[
\mathcal R^{-1}\preceq\gamma_k^{-1}I.
\]

Therefore

\[
M^{-1}\preceq\gamma_k^{-1}D^{-1},
\]

and congruence by `K` followed by trace gives

\[
F(A\cup L)-F(A)
\le
\gamma_k^{-1}
\sum_{b\in L}\Delta_b(A).
\]

This is `(6.4)`. QED.

# 7. Two explicit lower bounds on the submodularity ratio

## 7.1 Global spectral-condition bound

Put

\[
m=\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma),
\qquad
M=\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma).
\tag{7.1}
\]

## `P19-WEAKSUBMOD-CONDITION-25` — NEW / PROVED

For every block partition and every budget,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k\ge\frac mM.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

Every conditional covariance `M_{L|A}` is a Schur complement of a principal submatrix of `\Sigma`. For every vector `x` on the `L` coordinates,

\[
x^*M_{L\mid A}x
=
\min_y
\begin{pmatrix}y\\x\end{pmatrix}^*
\Sigma_{A\cup L,A\cup L}
\begin{pmatrix}y\\x\end{pmatrix}
\ge
m\|x\|^2.
\]

Hence

\[
M_{L\mid A}\succeq mI.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Each conditional diagonal block obeys

\[
M_{bb\mid A}\preceq\Sigma_{bb}\preceq MI.
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{L\mid A}\preceq MI.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Combining `(7.3)` and `(7.4)`,

\[
M_{L\mid A}
\succeq
\frac mM D_{L\mid A}.
\]

Congruence by `D^{-1/2}` proves `(7.2)`. QED.

## 7.2 Conditional block-coherence bound

Define

\[
\mu_k
=
\sup
\left\{
\left\|
M_{aa\mid A}^{-1/2}
M_{ab\mid A}
M_{bb\mid A}^{-1/2}
\right\|_{\rm op}:
\begin{array}{l}
A\cap L=\varnothing,\ |A|\le k-1,\\
a\ne b\in L,\ |L|\le k
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

## `P19-WEAKSUBMOD-COHERENCE-25` — NEW / PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k
\ge
\max\{0,\,1-(k-1)\mu_k\}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

### Proof

The normalized conditional correlation matrix `\mathcal R_{L|A}` has identity diagonal blocks and off-diagonal block operator norms at most `\mu_k`. For a block vector `x=(x_b)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x^*\mathcal R x
&\ge
\sum_b\|x_b\|^2
-2\mu_k\sum_{a<b}\|x_a\|\|x_b\|\\
&\ge
\left[1-(|L|-1)\mu_k\right]
\sum_b\|x_b\|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]

because

\[
2\sum_{a<b}u_au_b
\le
(|L|-1)\sum_bu_b^2.
\]

Since `|L|\le k`, `(7.6)` follows. QED.

The two bounds can be combined:

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k
\ge
\max\left\{
\frac mM,
\,1-(k-1)\mu_k
\right\}
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

whenever the second term is positive.

# 8. Greedy prune-and-refit algorithm

Assume equal block cost and a budget of `k` retained blocks. Starting from

\[
S_0=\varnothing,
\]

choose recursively

\[
b_t\in
\arg\max_{b\notin S_t}\Delta_b(S_t),
\qquad
S_{t+1}=S_t\cup\{b_t\},
\qquad
0\le t<k.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Let

\[
S_g=S_k
\]

be the greedy support, and let `S_*` maximize `F` over all `k`-block supports, equivalently minimize the exact refit loss `R`.

## `P19-REFIT-GREEDY-25` — NEW / PROVED

Define

\[
\beta_k(\gamma_k)
=\left(1-\frac{\gamma_k}{k}\right)^k.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
F(S_g)
\ge
\left[1-\beta_k(\gamma_k)\right]F(S_*)
\ge
(1-e^{-\gamma_k})F(S_*).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Equivalently, in exact refit risk,

\[
\boxed{
R(S_g)-R(S_*)
\le
\beta_k(\gamma_k)
\left[R_0-R(S_*)\right]
\le
 e^{-\gamma_k}
\left[R_0-R(S_*)\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Proof

At step `t`, let

\[
L=S_*\setminus S_t.
\]

Then `|L|\le k`. By `(6.4)`,

\[
\sum_{b\in L}\Delta_b(S_t)
\ge
\gamma_k
\left[
F(S_t\cup S_*)-F(S_t)
\right].
\]

By monotonicity,

\[
F(S_t\cup S_*)\ge F(S_*).
\]

Hence some block in `L` has marginal gain at least

\[
\frac{\gamma_k}{k}
\left[F(S_*)-F(S_t)\right].
\]

Greedy chooses a marginal at least this large. Therefore the unexplained optimal gain

\[
G_t=F(S_*)-F(S_t)
\]

obeys

\[
G_{t+1}
\le
\left(1-\frac{\gamma_k}{k}\right)G_t.
\]

Iterating for `k` steps proves the first inequality in `(8.3)`. Since

\[
\left(1-\frac{\gamma_k}{k}\right)^k
\le e^{-\gamma_k},
\]

the second follows. Substituting `F=R_0-R` gives `(8.4)`. QED.

### Interpretation

The previous P19 counterexample showed that **static** singleton conditional deletion scores can fail by a large factor. The present theorem does not resurrect that failed route. Greedy recomputes the exact conditional marginal after every selected block. The quality of that adaptive procedure is controlled by the conditional block-correlation ratio `\gamma_k`.

## `P19-WEAKSUBMOD-SHARP-CE-25` — NEW / PROVED

Take two scalar feature blocks with

\[
\Sigma_ho
=
\begin{pmatrix}

\end{pmatrix},
\qquad

\tag{9.1}

Let

\qquad

so `v` is the minimum-eigenvalue direction of `\Sigma_\rho`. Choose scalar output

\[

\qquad

Then

\[

\[
F(L)-F(\varnothing)
=\operatorname{Var}(Y)
