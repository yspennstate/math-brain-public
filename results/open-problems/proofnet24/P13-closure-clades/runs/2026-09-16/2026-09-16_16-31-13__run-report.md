# 4. A summation identity for the limiting clade frequencies

We first record a fact needed to control all coordinates simultaneously.

## Claim 4.1

\[
\boxed{\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\rho_r=1.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Using (3.7) and writing `k=r-1`,

\[
\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\rho_r
=\frac6{\pi^2}
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_k}{k(k+1)}.
\]

All terms are nonnegative, so Tonelli is legitimate. Since

\[
h_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^k\frac1\ell,

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{h_k}{k(k+1)}
&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac1\ell
  \sum_{k=\ell}^{\infty}\frac1{k(k+1)}\\
&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac1\ell\cdot\frac1\ell
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Multiplication by `6/pi^2` proves (4.1). QED.

We will also use the elementary consequences

and

These follow by comparison with the corresponding integrals.

# 5. New logarithmic `ell^2` control of the entire mean-remainder vector

For `s>=1` set

\[
e(s):=(e_r(s))_{r\ge2}\in\mathbb R^{\{2,3,\ldots\}}.
\]

For `r>s`, `N_s(r)=0` identically, hence

\[
e_r(s)=-s\rho_r.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Claim 5.1

There is an absolute constant `C` such that for all `s>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|e(s)\|_2^2\le C(1+\log(s+1)).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Handle `s=1` by (5.1), (4.4), and enlargement of `C`. Assume `s>=2`.

A rooted full binary tree with `s` leaves has exactly `s-1` internal clades, so pathwise

\[
\sum_{r=2}^sN_s(r)=s-1
\]

and therefore

\[
\sum_{r=2}^s\mu_r(s)=s-1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using (4.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=2}^se_r(s)
&=(s-1)-s\sum_{r=2}^s\rho_r\\
&=s\sum_{r>s}\rho_r-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Every summand on the left is nonnegative by (3.9), and every one is at most `D` by (3.8). Hence

\[
\sum_{r=2}^se_r(s)^2
\le D\sum_{r=2}^se_r(s)
\le Ds\sum_{r>s}\rho_r
\le C(1+\log(s+1))
\tag{5.5}
\]

by (4.3).

For the remaining coordinates, (5.1) and (4.4) give

\[
\sum_{r>s}e_r(s)^2
=s^2\sum_{r>s}\rho_r^2
\le C\frac{(1+\log(s+1))^2}{s}
\le C'(1+\log(s+1)).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.5) and (5.6) proves (5.2). QED.

The point is that the fixed-coordinate `O(1)` mean remainder does not accumulate like `O(s)` in square norm; the exact internal-clade count and positivity force only logarithmic total square mass.

# 6. `P13-I0051-TRACE-FORCING-03`: trace of the covariance forcing

\[
g_r(j,I)=\rho_rj+e_r(I)+e_r(j-I),
\tag{6.1}

put

\[
U_j:=e(I)+e(j-I).
\tag{6.2}
\]

By the definition (3.3), for every coordinate `r`,

\[
\eta_{rr}(j)
=\frac1j\operatorname{Var}(U_j(r)).
\]

All these terms are nonnegative. Tonelli and (5.2) therefore give

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_j:=\sum_{r\ge2}\eta_{rr}(j)
&=\frac1j\mathbb E\|U_j-\mathbb EU_j\|_2^2\\
&\le\frac1j\mathbb E\|U_j\|_2^2\\
&\le\frac2j\mathbb E\bigl(\|e(I)\|_2^2+\|e(j-I)\|_2^2\bigr)\\
&\le C\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{T_j\le C\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This proves `P13-I0051-TRACE-FORCING-03`.

Because

\[
a(j)=O\left(\frac{\log(j+1)}j\right),
\]

we may sum the nonnegative diagonal of (3.11):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r\ge2}\Sigma_{rr}
&=\sum_{r\ge2}\sum_{j\ge2}a(j)\eta_{rr}(j)\\
&=\sum_{j\ge2}a(j)T_j\\
&\le C\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{(1+\log(j+1))^2}{j^2}

# 7. `P13-I0051-HILBERT-TAIL-03`: a uniform tail bound for the moving vector

Let `Q_R` be orthogonal projection onto coordinates `r>R`. Since `eta_rr(j)=0` whenever `r>j`, the exact Green formula and nonnegativity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
&=\sum_{r>R}C_{rr}(n)\\
&=\sum_{j=R+1}^{n}a(n,j)
  \sum_{r>R}\eta_{rr}(j)\\
&\le\sum_{j=R+1}^{n}a(n,j)T_j\\
&\le C\sum_{j=R+1}^{n}a(n,j)\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

We now bound the final sum uniformly in `n`.

Set

\[
d:=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},
\qquad
\alpha:=1-d=d^2=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The preceding P13 report, from the current Brandenberger--Chin--Mossel harmonic-descent estimates, established two uniform tools:

\[
0\le a(n,j)-a(j)\le A\frac jn,
\qquad
A=\frac2{\log2},
\tag{7.3}
\]

and, for all sufficiently large `j`, all `1<=m<=j^d`,

\[
a(j+m,j)\le\frac{c^2}{m h_j}
\tag{7.4}
\]

for one fixed admissible constant `c`.

If `n<=R`, (7.1) is zero. Assume `n>R` and split `j>R` into three regions.

### Region I: `j<=n^d`

By (7.3), the contribution from the limiting term `a(j)` is

\[
C\sum_{j>R}\frac{(1+\log j)^2}{j^2}
=O\left(\frac{(1+\log R)^2}{R}\right).
\tag{7.5}
\]

The error term `Aj/n` contributes at most

\[
\frac Cn\sum_{j\le n^d}(1+\log j)
=O(n^{d-1}\log n).
\tag{7.6}
\]

If this region contains an index `j>R`, then `n^d>R`, hence `n>R^{1/d}`. For large `R`, the function `x^{d-1}\log x` is decreasing, so (7.6) is

\[
O(R^{-\alpha/d}\log R)=O(R^{-d}\log R),
\tag{7.7}
\]

because `alpha=d^2`.

### Region II: `j>n^d` and `n-j>=j^d`

Put `m=floor(j^d)`. Eventually `m>=j^d/2`; monotonicity in the starting state and (7.4) yield

\[
a(n,j)\le a(j+m,j)
\le\frac{2c^2}{j^dh_j}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Since `h_j` is comparable to `1+log j`, the Region-II summand in (7.1) is at most `Cj^{-1-d}`. Therefore the whole region is

\[
O\left(\sum_{j>R}j^{-1-d}\right)=O(R^{-d}).
\tag{7.9}
\]

### Region III: `n-j<j^d`

Here `j>n^d` by the partition. If `j<n/2`, then `n-j>j>j^d`, a contradiction, so `j>=n/2`. There are at most `n^d+1` such integers, `a(n,j)<=1`, and

\[
\frac{1+\log(j+1)}j\le C\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]

Thus the contribution is

\[
O(n^{d-1}\log n).
\tag{7.10}
\]

Because this part of (7.1) has `j>R`, necessarily `n>R`, and for large `R` the right side is bounded by

\[
O(R^{d-1}\log R)
=O(R^{-\alpha}\log R).
\tag{7.11}
\]

Combining (7.5), (7.7), (7.9), and (7.11), enlarging the constant for finitely many small `R`, proves

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{n\ge1}\mathbb E\|Q_RZ_n\|_2^2
\le C R^{-\alpha}(1+\log R).
}
\tag{7.12}
\]

This is `P13-I0051-HILBERT-TAIL-03`.

# 8. `P13-I0051-HILBERT-FULL-CLT-03`: the full `ell^2` CLT

The preceding run already proved convergence of every fixed finite-dimensional marginal to the corresponding Gaussian marginal of `G`. It remains only to upgrade to tightness in `H`.

## Tightness

Fix `epsilon>0`. By (7.12), choose increasing integers

\[
R_1<R_2<\cdots
\]

so large that

\[
\sup_n\mathbb E\|Q_{R_k}Z_n\|_2^2
\le\epsilon 2^{-3k}
\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{8.1}
\]

Markov gives

\[
\sup_n
\mathbb P\{\|Q_{R_k}Z_n\|_2>2^{-k}\}
\le\epsilon2^{-k}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The finite-dimensional head `P_{R_1}Z_n` has uniformly bounded second moment: this follows either from (7.12) plus the total trace bound implicit in (7.1), or directly from the finitely many diagonal covariance coordinates. Hence choose `K<infinity` so that

\[
\sup_n\mathbb P\{\|P_{R_1}Z_n\|_2>K\}<\epsilon.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Define

\[
\mathcal K_\epsilon:=
\left\{
 x\in H:
 \|P_{R_1}x\|_2\le K,
 \ \|Q_{R_k}x\|_2\le2^{-k}\ \forall k\ge1
\right\}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This set is compact in `H`. Indeed it is closed; it is uniformly bounded; and its tails vanish uniformly because for every `k`, every `x` in the set satisfies `||Q_{R_k}x||<=2^{-k}`. A standard finite-dimensional-net argument then gives total boundedness: for any `delta>0`, choose `k` with `2^{-k}<delta/3`, cover the bounded projection `P_{R_k}K_epsilon` by finitely many `delta/3`-balls, and lift those centers to `H`.

By (8.2), (8.3), and the union bound,

\[
\inf_n\mathbb P\{Z_n\in\mathcal K_\epsilon\}
\ge1-\epsilon-\sum_{k\ge1}\epsilon2^{-k}
\ge1-2\epsilon.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus `(Z_n)` is tight in the separable Hilbert space `H`.

## Identification of every subsequential limit

Every subsequential weak limit has the same finite coordinate marginals as `G`, because those marginals already converge. Coordinate maps generate the Borel sigma-field of separable `ell^2`, so these finite-dimensional distributions determine the probability law. Hence every subsequential limit is `G`, and tightness implies

\[
\boxed{Z_n\Longrightarrow G\quad\text{in }H.}
\tag{8.6}
\]

This proves `P13-I0051-HILBERT-FULL-CLT-03`.

This is genuinely stronger than the previous countable-product CLT. In product topology, large moving coordinates are invisible to every fixed neighborhood; (7.12) is precisely the uniform tail control missing there.

which is exactly the same quantity bounded in (7.12). The compact-containment argument in Section 8 therefore applies verbatim to `(Y_n)`. Hence `(Y_n)` is tight; all finite-dimensional limits agree with `G`; and (9.1) follows.

On a separable metric space, the bounded-Lipschitz metric metrizes weak convergence. Therefore

\[
d_{BL}^H(\mathcal L(Z_n),\mathcal L(G))\to0
\tag{9.3}
\]

and

\[

\tag{9.4}
\]

The triangle inequality yields the moving-target CLT

\[
\boxed{

}
\tag{9.5}
\]

No nonsingularity is asserted. In fact the exact deterministic constraints are automatically inherited by `Gamma_n`.

From (3.2), after centering,

\[
Z_n(n)=0,
\qquad
\sum_{r=2}^{n-1}Z_n(r)=0
\tag{9.6}
\]

almost surely. Let `e_n` be the `n`th coordinate vector and let

\[
u_n:=\sum_{r=2}^{n-1}e_r.

Then

\[
\operatorname{Var}\langle Z_n,e_n\rangle
=\operatorname{Var}\langle Z_n,u_n\rangle=0,
\]

so

\[
C_ne_n=0,
\qquad C_nu_n=0.
\tag{9.7}
\]

A centered Gaussian has zero projection almost surely along every covariance-null vector. Hence

\[
\langle Y_n,e_n\rangle=0,
\qquad
\langle Y_n,u_n\rangle=0
\tag{9.8}
\]

almost surely. Thus the comparison Gaussian is supported on the same exact centered hyperplane as the clade-count vector.

This resolves the strongest objection recorded in the previous run: the moving deterministic hyperplane is no longer merely ignored by a product-topology statement.
