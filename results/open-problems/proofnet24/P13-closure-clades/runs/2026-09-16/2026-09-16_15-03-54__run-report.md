## 2. Current source and prior art

The current source is arXiv:2303.02529v3, last revised 18 April 2025:

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02529v3
- https://arxiv.org/html/2303.02529v3

Open Problem 9 asks to study the joint distribution of `N_m^(n)`, `2<=m<=n`, in a way that permits covariance calculations and CLTs. For a clade of size `n`, the exact ordered split law is

\[
q_n(i)=\frac{n}{2h_{n-1}}\frac1{i(n-i)},\qquad 1\le i\le n-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let `a(n,k)` be the harmonic-descent occupation probability of `k` starting from `n`. The source records

\[
\mathbf E N_n(k)=\frac n k a(n,k),
\qquad
a(k):=\lim_{n\to\infty}a(n,k)=\frac{6h_{k-1}}{\pi^2(k-1)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\rho_k:=\frac{a(k)}k=\frac{6h_{k-1}}{\pi^2k(k-1)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The key prior art is Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel, arXiv:2505.24821v2 (8 July 2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24821v2. Their Theorem 1.1(i)–(iii) gives monotonicity and quantitative harmonic-descent bounds; Lemma 4.1 gives, for fixed `k`,

\[
\mathbf E N_n(k)=\rho_k n+O_k(1);
\tag{2.4}
\]

Proposition 1.3 is a scalar contraction CLT; Corollary 1.4 proves a **univariate** CLT for every fixed `N_n(k)`. Their introduction says they answer Open Problems 5 and 6 and partially Open Problem 8, not Open Problem 9.

A boundary convention matters: their displayed (4.1) suppresses the root toll in `N_n(k)=N_L(k)+N_{n-L}(k)`, but at `n=k` the root itself is a size-`k` clade. Their later base condition treats this separately. Below I use the globally exact recurrence with the deterministic root toll `1_{n=k}`.

Fresh literature check: Heng Ma, arXiv:2609.14325 (13 September 2026), studies chronological size-`q` clade-count dynamics in an extreme-time window for fragmentation trees, not the static terminal covariance matrix or fixed-dimensional joint CLT here. Searches on 16 September 2026 found no later primary source stating the fixed-block covariance formula and multivariate CLT below. This is a negative search result, not a proof of global novelty.

No result from excluded Open Problem 8 / Ansatz 20 is used below.

## 3. Counterexample-first exact small-tree search

Before extending the known univariate CLTs, I tested the tempting independence/product-law simplification. For `n=4`,

\[
q_4(1)=q_4(3)=\frac4{11},\qquad q_4(2)=\frac3{11}.
\]

A `1+3` or `3+1` split gives `(N_2,N_3)=(1,1)` with total probability `8/11`; a `2+2` split gives `(2,0)` with probability `3/11`. Hence

\[
\mathbf EN_2=\frac{14}{11},\qquad \mathbf EN_3=\frac8{11},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(N_2,N_3)=-\frac{24}{121}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus cross-size dependence is already present at the smallest nontrivial joint state. Any independence/product-Poisson Ansatz is false. This does not refute Open Problem 9; it refutes an overstrong route. Claim `P13-N4-DEPENDENCE-01`: **PROVED**.

## 4. Exact covariance recursion

Fix `r,s>=2` and write `mu_r(n)=E N_n(r)`. Conditional on ordered root split `I=i`, the child trees are independent and

\[
N_n(r)\stackrel d=\mathbf1_{\{n=r\}}+N_i^{(1)}(r)+N_{n-i}^{(2)}(r).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define

\[
g_r(n,i):=\mu_r(i)+\mu_r(n-i),
\qquad
\eta_{rs}(n):=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q_n}(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\eta_{rs}(n)=\frac1n\left[\sum_iq_n(i)g_r(n,i)g_s(n,i)-\Bigl(\sum_iq_n(i)g_r(n,i)\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_iq_n(i)g_s(n,i)\Bigr)\right].
\tag{4.3}
\]

Put

\[
C_{rs}(n):=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)).
\]

**Lemma `P13-COV-REC-01`.** For every `n>=2`,

\[
\boxed{C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}C_{rs}(i)+\eta_{rs}(n).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

**Proof.** Conditional on `I=i`, independence gives covariance `K_{rs}(i)+K_{rs}(n-i)` with `K_{rs}(j)=jC_{rs}(j)`. Total covariance adds `n eta_{rs}(n)`. Divide by `n`. Using (2.1), the contribution of `iC_{rs}(i)` is half the displayed HD sum. The contribution of `(n-i)C_{rs}(n-i)` becomes the same half after `i -> n-i`. The deterministic root toll has no covariance. QED.

By (2.4), uniformly in `i` for fixed `r`,

\[
g_r(n,i)=\rho_r n+O_r(1),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|\eta_{rs}(n)|\le C_{r,s}/n.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 5. Green representation and explicit covariance constant

The harmonic-descent kernel is

\[
p_n(i)=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}.
\]

Its occupation probability satisfies `a(j,j)=1` and, for `n>j`,

\[
a(n,j)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p_n(i)a(i,j).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Comparing (4.4) and (5.1) and inducting on `n` yields the exact finite-`n` Green identity

\[
\boxed{C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{j=2}^{n}a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

I do **not** invoke Ansatz 20 to pass to the limit. Instead apply Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel Theorem 1.1(i),(iii) directly to `x_n^(j)=a(n,j)/j`, whose initial value is `1/j`. Let `m_j=floor(j^{1/6})`. Their `c=2, ell=1` short-time estimate and monotonicity imply, for all sufficiently large `j` and every `n>=j+m_j`,

\[
a(n,j)\le \frac{C}{j^{1/6}h_j}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The same holds for `a(j)=lim_n a(n,j)`. Together with (4.5),

\[
\sum_{j=2}^\infty a(j)|\eta_{rs}(j)|<\infty.
\tag{5.4}
\]

To justify passage in (5.2), fix `K`. For `j>K` with `n-j>=j^{1/6}`, (5.3) bounds the tail by `C sum_{j>K} j^{-7/6}/h_j`, which tends to zero as `K->infinity`. In the complementary near-diagonal range, `j>n-n^{1/6}`; there are `O(n^{1/6})` terms, and `a(n,j)<=1`, (4.5) give total `O(n^{-5/6})`. For `j<=K`, termwise convergence `a(n,j)->a(j)` applies. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\Sigma_{rs}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)\eta_{rs}(j),}
\tag{5.5}
\]

with absolute convergence, where

\[
a(j)=\frac{6h_{j-1}}{\pi^2(j-1)}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Each `eta_{rs}(j)` is a finite exact rational quantity recursively determined by the finite DTCS split law and means. This is a source-matching computable covariance formula. Claim `P13-FIXEDBLOCK-COV-01`: **PROVED**.

## 6. Polynomial variance rate for every fixed linear combination

Fix finite `M` and `t=(t_2,...,t_M)`. Set

\[
X_n(t)=\sum_{r=2}^{M}t_rN_n(r).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Its exact recursion is

\[
X_n(t)\stackrel d=X_{I_n}^{(1)}(t)+X_{n-I_n}^{(2)}(t)+b_n(t),
\quad
b_n(t)=t_n\mathbf1_{\{2\le n\le M\}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By (2.4),

\[
\mathbf EX_n(t)=\rho_tn+O_t(1),\qquad \rho_t=\sum_{r=2}^{M}t_r\rho_r.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let `V_n(t)=Var X_n(t)/n`. The same total-variance calculation gives

\[
V_n(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p_n(i)V_i(t)+\epsilon_n(t),
\tag{6.4}
\]

where

\[
\epsilon_n(t)=\frac1n\operatorname{Var}_{I\sim q_n}\left(\sum_{r=2}^{M}t_rg_r(n,I)\right),
\qquad 0\le\epsilon_n(t)\le C_t/n.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For each `m>=2`, define `u_n^(m)` to be zero for `n<m`, equal to `epsilon_m(t)` at `n=m`, and thereafter to evolve by the homogeneous HD recursion. Then

\[
V_n(t)=\sum_{m=2}^{n}u_n^{(m)}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Let `lambda_m=lim_n u_n^(m)>=0`. Theorem 1.1(ii) and (6.5) give, for `m<sqrt(n)`,

\[
|u_n^{(m)}-\lambda_m|\le C_t/(n-m).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Theorem 1.1(iii) with `c=2, ell=1`, followed by monotonicity, gives for large `m` and `n-m>=m^{1/6}`

\[
0\le u_n^{(m)},\lambda_m\le C_tm^{-7/6}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For the near-diagonal indices use the trivial `u_n^(m),lambda_m<=C_t/m`. Split the difference between (6.6) and `sum_m lambda_m` into: `m<sqrt(n)`, contributing `O(n^{-1/2})` by (6.7); `sqrt(n)<=m<=n-n^{1/6}`, contributing

\[
O\left(\sum_{m\ge\sqrt n}m^{-7/6}\right)=O(n^{-1/12});
\]

and `n-n^{1/6}<m<=n`, contributing `O(n^{-5/6})`. Also (6.8) implies `sum_{m>n}lambda_m=O(n^{-1/6})`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{V_n(t)=\sigma_t^2+O_t(n^{-1/12}),\qquad \sigma_t^2:=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\lambda_m.}
\tag{6.9}
\]

By §5, `sigma_t^2=t^T Sigma^(M)t`. By polarization,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))=n\Sigma_{rs}+O_{r,s}(n^{11/12}).}
\tag{6.10}
\]

I deliberately use the weaker directly checked `1/12` exponent; it is sufficient for contraction. Claim `P13-GENVAR-RATE-01`: **PROVED**.

## 7. Fixed-dimensional multivariate CLT

**Theorem `P13-FIXEDBLOCK-MVCLT-01`.** For every fixed `M>=2`, with

\[
Y_n^{(M)}=(N_n(2),...,N_n(M)),
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{\frac{Y_n^{(M)}-\mathbf EY_n^{(M)}}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow d\mathcal N_{M-1}(0,\Sigma^{(M)}),}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where `Sigma^(M)=(Sigma_rs)_{2<=r,s<=M}` and each entry is given by (5.5). The Gaussian may be singular. Since the mean error in (2.4) is `O(1)`, exact centering may equivalently be replaced by `n(rho_2,...,rho_M)`.

**Proof.** Fix `t`. If `sigma_t^2>0`, then (6.2), (6.3), and (6.9) satisfy Brandenberger–Chin–Mossel Proposition 1.3 with any sufficiently small fixed positive epsilon, e.g. `<1/12`. The toll is zero for every `n>M`, so its third-moment condition holds eventually; finitely many initial recurrences are absorbed into the initial conditions. Hence

\[
\frac{X_n(t)-\mathbf EX_n(t)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)}}\to_dN(0,1).
\]

Since `Var X_n(t)/n -> sigma_t^2`, Slutsky yields convergence after `sqrt(n)` scaling to `N(0,sigma_t^2)`. If `sigma_t^2=0`, then (6.9) gives `Var X_n(t)/n->0`, so the normalized projection converges to zero in `L^2`. Thus every fixed projection has the Gaussian limit with variance `t^T Sigma^(M)t`; Cramér–Wold proves (7.1). QED.

This proves the full covariance block and joint CLT for **every fixed finite `M`**, but gives no uniformity for `M=M(n)->infinity` and no full-vector limit through size `n`. Source-problem status remains **PARTIAL**.
