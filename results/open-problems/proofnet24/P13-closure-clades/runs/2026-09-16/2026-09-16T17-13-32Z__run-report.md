x_{n/2}-x_n<\frac{1}{\log 2}\frac{k}{n-k}x_k,

\mathbf E N_n(k)=\frac{n\,a(n,k)}{k},

## 4. Idea before code: the three prescribed new-method moves

No new DTCS checker was written before the route was clear.

### Move #25 — count the same clade event twice

Introduce the leaf--clade incidence set

\[
\mathcal I_r(T)=\{(\ell,C): C\text{ is a size-}r\text{ clade of }T,\ \ell\in C\}.
\]

Every size-`r` clade contributes exactly `r` incidences. On the other hand, a uniformly sampled leaf lies in a size-`r` ancestral clade exactly when the harmonic-descent chain visits `r`, an event of probability `a(n,r)`. Taking expectations gives the same count in two ways:

\[
\boxed{r\,\mathbf E N_n(r)=n\,a(n,r).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The identity itself is known in the current primary paper; the new use is to feed the explicit dyadic constant uniformly in the parameter `r`.

### Move #12 — find what is conserved

Every rooted full binary tree with `n` leaves has exactly `n-1` internal vertices, hence exactly `n-1` internal clades of sizes at least two. Therefore, deterministically,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{r=2}^n N_n(r)=n-1.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{r=2}^n (N_n(r)-\mathbf EN_n(r))=0
\tag{4.3}
\]

and, for every `s`,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{r=2}^n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))=0.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus every finite full covariance block has an exact singular direction. This is structural bookkeeping, not a new CLT.

### Move #5 — add a parameter and differentiate

For `t>0` define the harmonic-descent weighted transform

\[
F_n(t):=\sum_{j=2}^n a(n,j)j^{-t},
\qquad
F(t):=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)j^{-t}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Published convergence (3.4) gives `F_n(t)->F(t)` for every fixed `t>0`. At the covariance-relevant point `t=1`, the finite derivative is

\[
F_n'(1)=-\sum_{j=2}^n a(n,j)\frac{\log j}{j}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Because `log j/j=O(j^{-1/2})`, (3.4) also applies directly to this nonnegative weight and gives

\[
F_n'(1)\longrightarrow
-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)\frac{\log j}{j}=F'(1).
\tag{4.7}
\]

So `t=1` has one logarithmic derivative of summability slack. This is the parameterized route used to understand why a uniform covariance sum should close without a fixed-`r` cutoff.

## 5. New theorem `P13-I0051-UNIFORM-MEAN-REMAINDER-04` — PROVED

Define

\[
\mu_r(m):=\mathbf E N_m(r),
\qquad
\rho_r:=\frac{a(r)}r,
\qquad
e_r(m):=\mu_r(m)-\rho_r m.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then for **every** `r>=2` and `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{|e_r(m)|\le D:=\frac{2}{\log2}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Fix `r` and set

\[
x_m:=\frac{a(m,r)}r=\frac{\mu_r(m)}m,\qquad m\ge r.
\]

The harmonic-descent recursion has initial value `x_r=1/r`, and its limit is `rho_r`. Apply the explicit inequality (3.2) with `k=r` and `n=2m` (`m>=r`):

\[
x_m-x_{2m}<\frac{1}{\log2}\frac{r}{2m-r}x_r
=\frac{1}{\log2}\frac{1}{2m-r}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Monotonicity permits dyadic telescoping:

\[
0\le x_m-\rho_r
=\sum_{\ell\ge0}(x_{2^\ell m}-x_{2^{\ell+1}m}).
\]

Since `r<=m`,

\[
2^{\ell+1}m-r\ge 2^\ell m,
\]

and therefore

\[
0\le x_m-\rho_r
\le\frac1{\log2}\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac1{2^\ell m}
=\frac{2}{m\log2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Multiplying by `m` gives

\[
0\le e_r(m)\le\frac2{\log2}
\qquad(m\ge r).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `m<r`, `N_m(r)=0`, so

\[
|e_r(m)|=\rho_r m=\frac{a(r)}r m\le a(r)\le1<\frac2{\log2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This proves (5.2), with a numerical constant independent of both `r` and `m`. QED.

The point is not just a better fixed-size error term. The earlier fixed-block proof used `O_r(1)`; (5.2) removes the dependence on the clade-size coordinate entirely.

## 6. Uniform root-split covariance forcing

For the ordered root split `I~q_n`, retain the exact P13 notation

\[
g_r(n,I)=\mu_r(I)+\mu_r(n-I),
\]

\[
\eta_{rs}(n):=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q_n}
(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)).
\tag{6.1}
\]

From (5.1),

\[
g_r(n,I)=\rho_r n+e_r(I)+e_r(n-I).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The deterministic linear term `rho_r n` disappears under covariance. Put

\[
R_r=e_r(I)+e_r(n-I).
\]

By (5.2), `|R_r|<=2D` for every `r,n,I`. Hence

\[
\operatorname{Var}(R_r)\le4D^2
\]

and Cauchy--Schwarz gives the **coordinate-uniform** forcing estimate

\[
\boxed{
|\eta_{rs}(n)|
\le\frac{4D^2}{n}
=\frac{16}{(\log2)^2}\frac1n
=: \frac K n
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for all `r,s>=2` and all `n>=2`.

No fixed-`r` constant remains.

## 7. New theorem `P13-I0051-UNIFORM-COV-KERNEL-04` — PROVED

Pad the clade counts by `N_n(r)=0` for `r>n`. Define the normalized covariance

\[
C_{rs}(n):=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The earlier exact root decomposition and harmonic-descent Green representation are finite identities valid for every coordinate:

\[
C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{j=2}^n a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For every `r,s>=2` define

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_{rs}:=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)\eta_{rs}(j).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The series converges absolutely and uniformly in `r,s`, because (6.3) gives

\[
|a(j)\eta_{rs}(j)|\le K\frac{a(j)}j,
\]

while

\[
a(j)=\frac{6h_{j-1}}{\pi^2(j-1)}=O\!\left(\frac{\log j}{j}\right),
\]

so `sum_j a(j)/j<infinity`.

For fixed `j`, harmonic-descent monotonicity gives `a(n,j)>=a(j)` whenever `n>=j`. Subtract (7.3) from (7.2) and use (6.3):

\[
\begin{aligned}
|C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}|
&\le
K\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{a(n,j)-a(j)}j
+K\sum_{j>n}\frac{a(j)}j\\
&=:K\Delta_n.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Crucially, `Delta_n` is independent of `r,s`. Apply the published weighted-convergence theorem (3.4) with `f(j)=1/j`:

\[
\sum_{j=2}^n\frac{a(n,j)}j
\longrightarrow
A:=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{a(j)}j.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Also the ordinary partial sums of the absolutely convergent series tend to `A`. Hence

\[
\Delta_n
=
\left(\sum_{j=2}^n\frac{a(n,j)}j-
      \sum_{j=2}^n\frac{a(j)}j\right)
+
\sum_{j>n}\frac{a(j)}j
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Taking the supremum in (7.4) therefore proves

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{r,s\ge2}
\left|
\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))-
\Sigma_{rs}
\right|\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This is stronger than the inherited entrywise fixed-`r,s` covariance limit and is genuinely uniform over the coordinate index. In particular, for **every deterministic moving cutoff** `M(n)<=n`, including `M(n)=n`,

\[
\boxed{
\max_{2\le r,s\le M(n)}
\left|
\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))-
\Sigma_{rs}
\right|\to0.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

QED.
