# 4. Exact joint-law and covariance recursions

For `n>=1` define the full joint probability generating function

\[
F_n(z_2,\ldots,z_n)
:=\mathbb E\prod_{r=2}^n z_r^{N_n(r)},
\qquad F_1:=1.
\]

Conditioning on the root split and using independence of the two recursive subtrees gives the exact full-law recursion

\[
\boxed{
F_n(\mathbf z)
=z_n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}q(n,i)
F_i(z_2,\ldots,z_i)
F_{n-i}(z_2,\ldots,z_{n-i}).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the whole finite-`n` law and every mixed moment are recursively computable. This formula alone is not an asymptotic solution of Open Problem 9.

Write

\[
\mu_r(n):=\mathbb E N_n(r),
\qquad
g_r(n,i):=\mu_r(i)+\mu_r(n-i),
\]

with `mu_r(j)=0` for `j<r`. For `r,s>=2` define

\[
V_{rs}(n):=\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s)),
\qquad C_{rs}(n):=\frac1nV_{rs}(n),
\]

and

\[
\eta_{rs}(n)
:=\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}_{I\sim q(n,\cdot)}
\bigl(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I)\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The deterministic root toll `1_{n=r}` drops out of covariance. Conditional independence of the two subtrees gives

\[
V_{rs}(n)
=\sum_iq(n,i)\{V_{rs}(i)+V_{rs}(n-i)\}
+n\eta_{rs}(n).
\]

By symmetry of `q`, division by `n` yields the harmonic-descent recursion

\[
\boxed{
C_{rs}(n)=
\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}
\frac{C_{rs}(i)}{h_{n-1}(n-i)}+\eta_{rs}(n).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `a(n,j)` be the harmonic-descent occupation probability, with `a(j,j)=1`. The Green representation for a strictly descending chain then gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
C_{rs}(n)=\sum_{j=2}^{n}a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and in fact `eta_rs(j)=0` for `j<max(r,s)`. Therefore for the whole finite block

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}(N_n(r),N_n(s))
=n\sum_{j=\max(r,s)}^{n}a(n,j)\eta_{rs}(j),
\qquad 2\le r,s\le n.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

All quantities in (4.5) are finite rational recursions, since the split probabilities are rational harmonic expressions. This proves the exact covariance formula at the maximal possible block size `m=n`.

# 5. New uniform mean-remainder theorem

## Claim `P13-I0051-MEAN-REMAINDER-02` — PROVED

Put

\[
\rho_r:=\frac{a(r)}r,
\qquad e_r(j):=\mu_r(j)-\rho_r j.
\]

Then for every `r>=2` and every `j>=1`,

\[
\boxed{|e_r(j)|\le D:=\frac2{\log2}.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Consequently, uniformly in all `r,s,n`,

\[
\boxed{|\eta_{rs}(n)|\le\frac{Q}{n},
\qquad Q:=4D^2=\frac{16}{(\log2)^2}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

For fixed `r`, set

\[
x_j:=\frac{\mu_r(j)}j=\frac{a(j,r)}r.
\]

This is exactly the harmonic-descent recursion of Brandenberger--Chin--Mossel, with initial index `k=r`, `x_r=1/r`, and limit `rho_r`.

Their proof of Theorem 1.1(ii) contains the explicit one-dyadic-step inequality

\[
x_{N/2}-x_N<\frac1{\log2}\frac{r}{N-r}x_r,
\qquad N/2\ge r.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Apply (5.3) with `N=2j,4j,8j,...` and sum. Since `j>=r`,

\[
0\le x_j-\rho_r
=\sum_{u\ge0}(x_{2^uj}-x_{2^{u+1}j})
\le\frac{r x_r}{\log2}
\sum_{u\ge0}\frac1{2^u j}
=\frac2{j\log2}.
\]

Because `r x_r=1`, multiplication by `j` gives

\[
0\le e_r(j)\le\frac2{\log2},\qquad j\ge r.
\]

If `j<r`, then `mu_r(j)=0`, while `a(r)<=1` because it is an occupation probability; hence

\[
|e_r(j)|=j\rho_r=\frac{j}{r}a(r)<1<D.
\]

This proves (5.1).

Now

\[
g_r(n,i)=\rho_r n+e_r(i)+e_r(n-i).
\]

The constant `rho_r n` does not affect covariance. The remaining random term has absolute value at most `2D`, so by Cauchy--Schwarz

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(g_r(n,I),g_s(n,I))|
\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g_r)\operatorname{Var}(g_s)}
\le4D^2.
\]

Divide by `n` to obtain (5.2). QED.

This uniform bound is the key new input: unlike the preceding fixed-`r,s` argument, its constants do not deteriorate when the clade sizes grow with `n`.

# 6. New full covariance-kernel theorem

## Claim `P13-I0051-FULL-COV-KERNEL-02` — PROVED

For all `r,s>=2`, define

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_{rs}:=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a(j)\eta_{rs}(j),
\qquad
a(j)=\frac{6h_{j-1}}{\pi^2(j-1)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The series is absolutely convergent. Extend `C_rs(n)=0` whenever `r>n` or `s>n`. Then, with

\[
\alpha:=\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}=0.381966\ldots,
\]

one has the **uniform entrywise estimate**

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{r,s\ge2}|C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}|
=O(n^{-\alpha}).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

In particular, the entire normalized covariance block of dimensions `(n-1)x(n-1)` is approximated entrywise by the single infinite kernel `Sigma`, uniformly even when `r` and `s` depend on `n`.

### Proof

Absolute convergence follows immediately from (5.2) and

\[
a(j)=O\!\left(\frac{\log j}{j}\right),
\]

because

\[
\sum_{j\ge2}|a(j)\eta_{rs}(j)|
\le Q\sum_{j\ge2}\frac{a(j)}j<\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]

We next need two bounds for `a(n,j)`, uniform in `j`. Apply the dyadic argument in Section 5 directly to the harmonic-descent sequence `x_N=a(N,j)`, whose initial value is `x_j=1`. It gives

\[
0\le a(n,j)-a(j)\le A\frac jn,
\qquad A:=\frac2{\log2},
\qquad n\ge j.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For the short-time bound use Brandenberger--Chin--Mossel Theorem 1.1(iii). Choose `ell=2` and

\[
c=3+\frac{8\sqrt5}{5}.
\]

Then

\[
d:=1-\frac1{c+1}-\frac14=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2},
\qquad d^2=1-d=\alpha.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For all sufficiently large `j` and every `1<=m<=j^d`,

\[
a(j+m,j)\le\frac{c^2}{m h_j}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Using the Green formula (4.4),

\[
C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}
=\sum_{j=2}^n(a(n,j)-a(j))\eta_{rs}(j)
-\sum_{j>n}a(j)\eta_{rs}(j).
\tag{6.7}
\]

We bound the first sum in three regions, uniformly in `r,s`.

**Region 1: `j<=n^d`.** By (6.4) and (5.2), every summand is at most `AQ/n`, hence

\[
\sum_{j\le n^d}|a(n,j)-a(j)|\,|\eta_{rs}(j)|
=O(n^{d-1})=O(n^{-\alpha}).
\tag{6.8}
\]

**Region 2: `j>n^d` and `n-j>=j^d`.** Put `m=floor(j^d)`. For large `j`, `m>=j^d/2`. Monotonicity in the starting state and (6.6) give

\[
a(n,j)\le a(j+m,j)\le \frac{2c^2}{j^d h_j}.
\]

Since `a(n,j)>=a(j)`, (5.2) yields

\[
\sum_{\text{Region 2}}
|a(n,j)-a(j)|\,|\eta_{rs}(j)|
\le C\sum_{j>n^d}j^{-1-d}
=O(n^{-d^2})=O(n^{-\alpha}).
\tag{6.9}
\]

**Region 3: `n-j<j^d`.** This region contains at most `n^d+1` indices, all with `j>=n-n^d`; eventually `j>=n/2`. Using only `0<=a(n,j)-a(j)<=1` and (5.2),

\[
\sum_{\text{Region 3}}
|a(n,j)-a(j)|\,|\eta_{rs}(j)|
=O(n^d/n)=O(n^{-\alpha}).
\tag{6.10}
\]

Finally, the tail in (6.7) obeys

\[
\sum_{j>n}a(j)|\eta_{rs}(j)|
\le C\sum_{j>n}\frac{\log j}{j^2}
=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)
=O(n^{-\alpha}).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Combining (6.8)--(6.11) proves (6.2). The constants depend only on the fixed choices in the harmonic-descent estimates, not on `r` or `s`. QED.

# 7. Infinite absolutely-summable linear-statistic CLT

The uniform covariance theorem allows a strictly stronger joint statement than the preceding fixed-block CLT.

## Claim `P13-I0051-L1-CLT-02` — PROVED

Let `t=(t_r)_{r>=2}` be any real absolutely summable sequence,

\[
\|t\|_1:=\sum_{r\ge2}|t_r|<\infty,
\]

and define the additive clade-count statistic

\[
X_n(t):=\sum_{r=2}^n t_r N_n(r).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then

\[
\mu_t:=\sum_{r\ge2}t_r\rho_r
\]

is absolutely convergent, as is

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_t^2:=\sum_{r,s\ge2}t_rt_s\Sigma_{rs}\ge0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\mathbb E X_n(t)=\mu_t n+O_t(1),
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)=\sigma_t^2n+O_t(n^{1-\alpha}),
\tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{X_n(t)-\mathbb E X_n(t)}{\sqrt n}
\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_t^2),
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

where the Gaussian is understood as degenerate at zero if `sigma_t^2=0`.

### Proof

Since `rho_r=a(r)/r<=1/r`, the mean coefficient is absolutely convergent. From (5.1),

\[
\mathbb E X_n(t)-n\mu_t
=\sum_{r\le n}t_re_r(n)-n\sum_{r>n}t_r\rho_r.
\]

The first term is bounded by `D||t||_1`. The second is bounded by

\[
n\sum_{r>n}|t_r|/r\le\sum_{r>n}|t_r|\le\|t\|_1.
\]

This proves (7.3).

Pad `C_rs(n)=0` when either coordinate exceeds `n`. Then

\[
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)
=\sum_{r,s\ge2}t_rt_sC_{rs}(n).
\]

The kernel `Sigma` is uniformly bounded by (6.3), so (7.2) is absolutely convergent. The uniform estimate (6.2) gives

\[
\left|
\frac1n\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)-\sigma_t^2
\right|
\le\|t\|_1^2\sup_{r,s}|C_{rs}(n)-\Sigma_{rs}|
=O_t(n^{-\alpha}),
\]

which is (7.4). Non-negativity of `sigma_t^2` follows because it is a limit of variances divided by `n`.

Finally the root split gives exactly

\[
X_n(t)\stackrel d=X_{I_n}(t)+X'_{n-I_n}(t)+t_n,
\tag{7.6}
\]

with the two subtree copies conditionally independent. Choose any

\[
0<\varepsilon<\min\{1/2,\alpha\}.
\]

The mean error (7.3) is `O(1)=O(n^{1/2-epsilon})`; the variance error (7.4) is `O(n^{1-alpha})=O(n^{1-epsilon})`; and the deterministic toll satisfies

\[
|t_n|^3\le\|t\|_1^3=O(n^{3/2-\varepsilon}).
\]

If `sigma_t^2>0`, Brandenberger--Chin--Mossel Proposition 1.3 applies to (7.6), giving the Gaussian limit. If `sigma_t^2=0`, (7.4) gives

\[
\mathbb E\left|
\frac{X_n(t)-\mathbb E X_n(t)}{\sqrt n}
\right|^2=\frac{\operatorname{Var}X_n(t)}n\to0,
\]

so (7.5) holds with the degenerate Gaussian. QED.

This theorem includes every fixed finite linear combination but also genuinely infinite additive combinations with absolutely summable size weights.

# 8. Countable-product Gaussian-process CLT

## Claim `P13-I0051-PRODUCT-CLT-02` — PROVED

Pad `Z_n(r)=0` for `r>n`. In the countable product space

\[
E:=\mathbb R^{\{2,3,\ldots\}}
\]

with the product topology,

\[
\boxed{
Z_n=(Z_n(2),Z_n(3),\ldots)
\Longrightarrow G=(G_2,G_3,\ldots),
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

where `G` is a centered Gaussian process with covariance

\[
\mathbb E[G_rG_s]=\Sigma_{rs}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

### Proof

Every finite principal block of `Sigma` is positive semidefinite because it is the entrywise limit of covariance matrices `C^(n)`. Hence the consistent centered finite-dimensional Gaussian laws with covariance blocks from `Sigma` exist; by the standard countable Kolmogorov extension theorem they define a Gaussian process `G` on `E`.

For any finitely supported vector `t`, Claim `P13-I0051-L1-CLT-02` gives convergence of the scalar projection `sum_r t_rZ_n(r)` to the corresponding Gaussian projection. Cramer--Wold therefore gives convergence of every finite-dimensional marginal of `Z_n` to the matching marginal of `G`.

It remains to prove tightness in the product topology. Claim (6.2), absolute boundedness of `Sigma`, and the finitely many small values of `n` imply a constant `V<infinity` with

\[
\sup_{n\ge1}\sup_{r\ge2}\mathbb E[Z_n(r)^2]\le V.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Given `epsilon>0`, choose positive radii `R_r` so that

\[
\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{V}{R_r^2}<\varepsilon;
\]

for example `R_r^2=V2^r/epsilon` after harmless adjustment of the geometric constant. Then

\[
K:=\prod_{r=2}^{\infty}[-R_r,R_r]
\]

is compact in the countable product topology by Tychonoff, and Chebyshev plus the union bound gives

\[
\sup_n\mathbb P(Z_n\notin K)
\le\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{V}{R_r^2}<\varepsilon.
\]

Thus `(Z_n)` is tight. Every subsequential weak limit has the same finite-dimensional marginals as `G`; on the countable product Borel sigma-field those marginals determine the law uniquely. Therefore the whole sequence converges to `G`. QED.

This is a genuine countably infinite joint CLT in the weak product topology, but it is **not** a uniform Gaussian approximation for the moving Euclidean block `(Z_n(2),...,Z_n(n))`. In particular the moving deterministic hyperplane (3.4) is not visible to every fixed product-continuous test. I therefore do not call Open Problem 9 closed.

# 12. What is and is not solved

**PROVED / source-matching progress.** The covariance part has now reached the maximal finite block: every entry of the full sizes-`2,...,n` covariance matrix has the exact Green formula (4.5), and the entire normalized matrix converges entrywise, uniformly over moving coordinates, to one explicit infinite kernel `Sigma` at a polynomial rate. There is also a CLT for every absolutely summable infinite clade-size weighting and a Gaussian-process CLT in countable product topology.

**PARTIAL, not closure.** Open Problem 9 asks for the joint distribution of the full moving vector in a way that supports covariances and CLTs. Product-topology convergence tests only finitely many coordinates at a time (or, via Section 7, summable linear weights). It does not provide a quantitative approximation for the Euclidean block of dimension `n-1`, nor a growing window `M(n)` in a strong metric. The deterministic relations (3.3)--(3.4) also show that any such high-dimensional Gaussian statement must be singular and correctly projected. I therefore retain **PARTIAL** and do not create a paper/closure package.

No source statement was refuted. The only refutations are proof shortcuts: coordinate independence/product law, and any nonsingular full-vector Gaussian formulation.

# 14. Remaining obligation and next attack

The exact remaining source-facing obligation is a growing-dimensional CLT, not another fixed covariance calculation. A useful next target is:

with an error tending to zero, after projecting away any deterministic relations present in the chosen window. The uniform mean bound and covariance rate already show why a polynomial window is plausible: for an `l2`-unit coefficient vector supported on `M` coordinates, the uniform entrywise covariance error costs at most `M n^{-alpha}`, with `alpha=(3-sqrt5)/2`. The next run should inspect their Zolotarev-3 proof constants rather than re-prove any fixed-dimensional CLT.
