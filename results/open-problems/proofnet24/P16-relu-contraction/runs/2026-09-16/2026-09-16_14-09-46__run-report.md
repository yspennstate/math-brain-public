# 4. Exact joint radial / support / l1 Cramer transform

Fix a deterministic unit source `u` independent of `W`. Then

`F_W(u)=sqrt(alpha/N) Z_+`,

where `Z_1,...,Z_N` are iid standard Gaussians.

For one coordinate define

`B=1_{Z>0}`,  `Y=Z_+`,  `X=(Z_+)^2`.

The previous run used only the MGF of `X`. Here we keep all three coordinates.

For `t<1/2` and real `s,r`, direct Gaussian integration gives

`Lambda(t,s,r)`

`= log E exp(tX+sY+rB)`

`= log[ 1/2`
` + exp(r) (1-2t)^(-1/2)`
`   exp( s^2/[2(1-2t)] )`
`   Phi( s/sqrt(1-2t) ) ].`   `(4.1)`

At `s=r=0`, this reduces exactly to the preceding radial MGF

`M(t)=[1+(1-2t)^(-1/2)]/2`.

Let

`sigma_N=N^(-1) sum_i B_i`,

`m_N=N^(-1) sum_i Y_i`,

`q_N=N^(-1) sum_i X_i`.

Because `(4.1)` is finite in an open neighborhood of the origin, the finite-dimensional Cramer theorem applies to the iid vectors `(B_i,Y_i,X_i)`.

## `P16-RADIAL-JOINT-CRAMER-50 — PROVED`

The triple `(sigma_N,m_N,q_N)` satisfies a full large-deviation principle with good convex rate

`I(sigma,m,q)`

`= sup_(t<1/2, s,r in R)`
`  { t q + s m + r sigma - Lambda(t,s,r) }.`   `(4.2)`

For a radial threshold `a>0`, put

`beta=alpha/a^2`.

The radial event is

`G_a={q_N >= 1/beta}`.

On `q_N>0`, the normalized output direction

`V=Z_+/||Z_+||`

has normalized `l1` statistic

`b(V)=||V||_1/sqrt(N)=m_N/sqrt(q_N).`   `(4.3)`

For `beta in [1/2,2]`, the preceding exact radial rate is `J(beta)`. Therefore the conditional joint support/`l1` profile has variational rate

`I_beta(sigma,b)`

`= inf_(q>=1/beta) I(sigma, b sqrt(q), q) - J(beta).`   `(4.4)`

Equation `(4.4)` is the exact large-deviation object requested by the first half of `P16-L1-RADIAL-JOINT-RD-50`. It does **not** by itself solve angular rate distortion; it supplies the missing joint radial/support/`l1` geometry that any such theorem must use.

### Proof details

The only nontrivial calculation is `(4.1)`. On `Z<=0`, `(B,Y,X)=(0,0,0)`, contributing `1/2`. On `Z>0`, completing the square gives

`integral_0^infinity exp(tz^2+sz) phi(z) dz`

`= (1-2t)^(-1/2)`
`  exp(s^2/[2(1-2t)])`
`  Phi(s/sqrt(1-2t)).`

This proves the log-MGF. Cramer's theorem then gives `(4.2)`. The contraction principle under `(4.3)`, followed by conditioning on `q>=1/beta` and subtracting the radial denominator rate `J(beta)`, gives `(4.4)`.

No fresh-layer substitution is involved; this is intentionally a one-step fixed-source reference law that will later be entered through exact KL change of measure when the source is adaptive.

# 5. Typical support and l1 geometry under radial conditioning

For `beta<2`, let `z=z_beta in (0,1)` solve

`beta=z^2(1+z)`.

The preceding radial tilt uses

`t_beta=(1-z^2)/2`.

Under this tilted one-coordinate law, the negative half-line retains mass proportional to `1/2`, while the positive half-line has total mass proportional to `1/(2z)`. Hence

`p_beta := E_t B = 1/(1+z).`   `(5.1)`

Conditional on positivity, the magnitude is half-normal with scale `1/z`. Therefore

`E_t Y = sqrt(2/pi)/[z(1+z)]`,   `(5.2)`

`E_t X = 1/[z^2(1+z)] = 1/beta`.   `(5.3)`

The corresponding normalized `l1` level is

`b_beta := (E_t Y)/sqrt(E_t X)`

`= sqrt( 2/[pi(1+z)] )`

`= sqrt(2 p_beta/pi).`   `(5.4)`

At `beta=2`, `z=1`, `t_beta=0`, so `p_beta=1/2` and

`b_beta=1/sqrt(pi)`.

## `P16-RADIAL-L1-TYPICAL-50 — PROVED`

For every fixed `beta in [1/2,2]` and every `epsilon>0`, there exists `c=c(beta,epsilon)>0` such that

`P( |sigma_N-p_beta|>epsilon | G_a ) <= exp[-cN+o(N)]`,   `(5.5)`

and

`P( |b(V)-b_beta|>epsilon | G_a ) <= exp[-cN+o(N)].`   `(5.6)`

### Proof

For `beta<2`, the unique minimizer of the Cramer rate over `q>=1/beta` is the exponential-tilt mean `(p_beta,E_tY,1/beta)`. Strict convexity of the log-MGF on its nondegenerate support gives a strictly positive rate gap outside every fixed neighborhood of that point. Divide the resulting joint large-deviation upper bound by

`P(G_a)=exp[-N J(beta)+o(N)]`.

This proves `(5.5)--(5.6)`. At `beta=2`, the radial threshold equals the unconditioned mean `EX=1/2`; the denominator is not exponentially small, while ordinary Chernoff concentration of the empirical triple gives the same exponential conditional concentration about the unconditioned mean.

### Dead-neuron interpretation

The typical active fraction under radial conditioning is at least `1/2`, and increases above `1/2` for `beta<2`. A sparse high-gain target is therefore atypical under the decoupled radial reference. Any tied bad event that systematically uses sparse high-gain states must pay for that selection in the same change-of-measure ledger.

# 6. Exact typical-l1 angular gap

The preceding fixed-target radial-conditioned alignment theorem gives, for deterministic nonnegative unit target `v` with

`b=||v||_1/sqrt(N)`,

`C(beta,b)=[1-sqrt(beta/(2pi)) b]_+^2/(2beta)`,   `(6.1)`

and conditional exponent at least

`C(beta,b)-J(beta)`.

Substitute the radial-typical value `(5.4)`. Since

`sqrt(beta/(2pi)) b_beta = z/pi`,   `(6.2)`

we obtain

`D_typ(beta)`

`= [1-z/pi]^2/[2 z^2(1+z)] - J(beta).`   `(6.3)`

## `P16-RADIAL-TYPICAL-L1-GAP-50 — PROVED`

For every `beta in [1/2,2]`,

`D_typ(beta) >= (1/4)(1-1/pi)^2`

`             = 0.11617535281868908... .`   `(6.4)`

The minimum occurs at the critical edge `beta=2`.

### Proof

Differentiate `(6.3)` using

`J(beta)=(1-z)/(2z^2)-log((1+z)/(2z))`.

Direct simplification gives

`dD_typ/dz`

`= - P(z) / [2 pi^2 z^2(1+z)^2]`,   `(6.5)`

where

`P(z)=(1+3pi^2)z^2 + (2pi^2-4pi)z - 2pi`.

The range `beta>=1/2` implies `z>1/2`. The polynomial `P` is increasing there, and

`P(1/2)=(1+7pi^2)/4 - 4pi >0`.

Hence `(6.5)` is strictly negative throughout the interval. At `z=1`, equivalently `beta=2`, `J(2)=0` and `(6.4)` follows.

### What this does and does not buy

The universal target bound used only `b<=1` and gave edge coefficient

`(1/4)(1-1/sqrt(pi))^2 = 0.047482679772069526...`.

The typical radial target geometry more than doubles that fixed-target exponent. However the preceding run's explicit **generic radial-conditioned target rate-distortion** certificate at the inherited edge distortion scale is

`0.15839073592924388... N - o(N)`,

still larger than `0.1161753528... N` even before allowing distortion to weaken the alignment event. Thus substituting only the typical `l1` value does not repair the generic one-state coding route at the edge. This is a method comparison, not a counterexample to the contraction theorem and not a complete joint rate-distortion solution.

# 7. Closing balancing mass forces radial gain somewhere

Return now to the **actual tied trajectory**. Let a live normalized segment satisfy

`F_W(u_t)=g_t u_(t+1)`,  `t=0,...,k-1`,

and let

`kappa=(product_t g_t)^(1/k)`.

Define balancing coefficients

`a_0=1`,

`a_(t+1)=a_t g_t/kappa`,

`lambda_t=a_t/sum_(s=0)^(k-1) a_s`.

Because `a_k=a_0`, extend cyclically by `lambda_k=lambda_0`. Then the exact identity is

`g_t = kappa lambda_(t+1)/lambda_t`.   `(7.1)`

Suppose `kappa>=1` and let `m=lambda_0` be the balancing mass of the chosen starting/closing phase.

## `P16-CLOSING-MASS-RADIAL-BUDGET-50 — PROVED`

For every such segment,

`sum_t (log g_t)_+ >= [ log(1/(k m)) ]_+.`   `(7.2)`

Consequently,

`sum_t (g_t-1)_+ >= [ log(1/(k m)) ]_+`,   `(7.3)`

and

`sum_t (g_t-1)_+^2 >= [log(1/(k m))]_+^2 / k.`   `(7.4)`

If additionally `g_t<=L` for every `t`, fix `eta>0` with `1+eta<L`, and put

`q_eta = #{t: g_t>=1+eta}`.

Then

`q_eta >=`
` [ log(1/(km)) - k log(1+eta) ]_+`
` / log(L/(1+eta)).`   `(7.5)`

### Proof

Some balancing weight is at least `1/k`; choose `j` with `lambda_j>=1/k`. From `(7.1)`,

`lambda_j/lambda_0`

`= kappa^(-j) product_(t<j) g_t`

`<= product_(t<j) max(1,g_t)`,

because `kappa>=1`. Hence

`product_t max(1,g_t) >= 1/(km)`.

Taking logarithms gives `(7.2)`. Since `log x<=x-1` for `x>=1`, `(7.3)` follows, and Cauchy--Schwarz gives `(7.4)`. Finally,

`sum_t (log g_t)_+`

`<= q_eta log L + (k-q_eta)log(1+eta)`,

which rearranges to `(7.5)`.

### Interpretation

Tiny closing balancing mass cannot hide for free: it forces compensating positive radial log-gain elsewhere. But this alone does **not** force many comparable transitions. Section 9 gives an exact deterministic counterexample to that stronger inference.

# 8. Exact cycles: phase rotation removes tiny closing mass

Suppose now the segment is an **exact projective cycle**:

`u_k=u_0`.

Let `j_*` maximize the balancing weights `lambda_j`.

Cyclically rotate the same exact cycle to start at phase `u_(j_*)`. The balancing weights of the rotated description are simply the cyclic shift of the original weights.

## `P16-EXACT-CYCLE-MAXWEIGHT-CLOSING-50 — PROVED`

After this phase rotation,

`lambda_0^(rot)=lambda_(j_*) >= 1/k`,   `(8.1)`

and the incoming closing gain satisfies

`g_(j_*-1) = kappa lambda_(j_*)/lambda_(j_*-1) >= kappa`.   `(8.2)`

Indices are cyclic.

### Proof

Equation `(8.1)` is maximality plus `sum lambda=1`. Equation `(8.2)` is `(7.1)` and maximality `lambda_(j_*)>=lambda_(j_*-1)`.

### Consequence

For exact cycles, “the designated closing edge has tiny balancing mass” is not an invariant obstruction. Choose the phase of maximal balancing weight and the closing edge has both mass at least `1/k` and gain at least the cycle geometric mean. The genuine difficulty is approximate recurrence: rotating a near-cycle requires transporting its small return error through part of the dynamics.

# 9. Near-cycle phase rotation and the exact sensitivity obstruction

Let `u_0,...,u_k` be a live realized normalized segment as in Section 7, not necessarily exact, and put

`delta = ||u_k-u_0||`,

`L=||W||_op`,

`G_j=product_(t=0)^(j-1) g_t`, with `G_0=1`.

Thus

`F_W^j(u_0)=G_j u_j`.

Extend the actual trajectory after time `k`, and define

`G'_j = ||F_W^j(u_k)||`,

so when this future segment remains live,

`F_W^j(u_k)=G'_j u_(k+j)`.

Because one ReLU step is `L`-Lipschitz, `F_W^j` is `L^j`-Lipschitz. Hence

`||F_W^j(u_k)-F_W^j(u_0)|| <= L^j delta.`   `(9.1)`

Define the dimensionless return-transport factor

`Xi_j := L^j/G_j`

`      = (L/kappa)^j lambda_0/lambda_j.`   `(9.2)`

## `P16-NEARCYCLE-ROTATION-STABILITY-50 — PROVED`

If `delta Xi_j<1`, then

`|G'_j/G_j - 1| <= delta Xi_j`,   `(9.3)`

and

`||u_(k+j)-u_j|| <= 2 delta Xi_j`.   `(9.4)`

Moreover the geometric-mean gain of the shifted length-`k` segment from `u_j` to `u_(k+j)` is

`kappa_shift`

`= kappa (G'_j/G_j)^(1/k)`

`>= kappa (1-delta Xi_j)^(1/k).`   `(9.5)`

### Proof

The norm inequality in `(9.3)` follows directly from `(9.1)` after division by `G_j`. If `delta Xi_j<1`, the future vector is nonzero. The standard normalization inequality

`||x/||x|| - y/||y|||| <= 2||x-y||/||y||`

gives `(9.4)`. The product of gains along the shifted length-`k` segment is

`(kappa^k/G_j) G'_j`,

which gives `(9.5)`.

## `P16-ROTATED-CLOSURE-OR-SENSITIVITY-50 — PROVED`

Let `j_*` maximize `lambda_j`.

- If `j_*=0`, then the original final edge has gain
  `g_(k-1)>=kappa`, and its normalized output `u_k` is within `delta` of the historical target `u_0`.

- If `j_*>0`, put `r=j_*-1`. Then the historical incoming edge has
  `g_r>=kappa`. For every `Q>=1`, either

  `Xi_r>Q`,   `(9.6)`

  or, provided `2LQ delta<kappa`, the future edge from `u_(k+r)` has gain `h` satisfying

  `h >= kappa - 2LQ delta`,   `(9.7)`

  and its normalized output direction obeys

  `||u_(k+j_*) - u_(j_*)|| <= 4LQ delta/kappa`.   `(9.8)`

### Proof

If `Xi_r<=Q`, `(9.4)` gives

`||u_(k+r)-u_r|| <= 2Q delta`.

Apply one more `L`-Lipschitz ReLU step. The old output has norm `g_r>=kappa`, so the future output norm differs by at most `2LQdelta`, proving `(9.7)`. Normalizing against the old output gives `(9.8)`.

### Global bad-orbit consequence

The retained compactness closing lemma gives arbitrarily accurate near-neutral near-cycles along any orbit with nonnegative upper radial Lyapunov exponent. Apply `(9.6)--(9.8)` with, for example, `Q=delta^(-1/2)`. Along a sequence with `delta->0` and `kappa->1`, every witness has one of two forms:

1. **recurrent old-target branch:** a future transition has gain tending to at least one and points within `O(sqrt(delta))` of a previously visited state;
2. **return-transport sensitivity branch:** the factor `Xi_r=L^r/G_r` diverges at least like `delta^(-1/2)`.

This is a deterministic dichotomy. It does not call the second branch “nonnormal” as a theorem: `Xi_r` uses the coarse global Lipschitz bound `L^r`, so large `Xi_r` is an exact sensitivity/transient certificate but may include more than spectral nonnormality.

# 10. Small closing mass does not imply delocalization

A tempting continuation of Section 7 would be:

> If the closing balancing mass is tiny, then the balancing weights must spread over many phases, so the weighted effective rank or multi-transition information debt becomes large.

This is false deterministically.

Fix `k>=2`, `L>1`, and dimension `N>=k`. On basis vectors define a nonnegative weighted permutation matrix by

`W e_t = L e_(t+1)` for `t=0,...,k-2`,

`W e_(k-1) = L^(-(k-1)) e_0`,

and let `W` vanish on the remaining basis directions.

All listed coordinates are nonnegative, so ReLU acts as the identity on this orbit. The basis vectors form an exact period-`k` projective cycle with gains

`g_0=...=g_(k-2)=L`,

`g_(k-1)=L^(-(k-1))`,

and geometric mean

`kappa=1`.

Also `||W||_op=L`.

The balancing coefficients are

`a_t=L^t`,

so

`lambda_0=(L-1)/(L^k-1)`,   `(10.1)`

which is exponentially small in `k`. Yet the balancing participation ratio is

`r_lambda = 1/sum_t lambda_t^2`

`= (sum_t a_t)^2 / sum_t a_t^2`

`= [(L+1)/(L-1)] * [(L^k-1)/(L^k+1)]`

`-> (L+1)/(L-1)`   `(10.2)`

as `k->infinity`.

## `P16-NOGO-CLOSINGMASS-DELOCALIZATION-50 — DISPROVED route`

Small designated closing mass, even exponentially small mass, does **not** force balancing-weight delocalization or large weighted effective rank under a bounded operator norm.

This is not a Gaussian counterexample and not a counterexample to P16. By Section 8, the exact cycle can be rotated to its maximal-weight phase, after which its closing mass is at least `1/k` and its closing gain is at least one. The example is a certificate against a proof step, not against the conjecture.
