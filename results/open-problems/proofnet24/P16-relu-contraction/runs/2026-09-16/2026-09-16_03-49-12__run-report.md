# 0. Executive result

P16 remains **OPEN** for the original tied-weight model

$$
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N),\qquad
F_W(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx),\qquad
x_{t+1}=F_W(x_t),
$$

with the **same matrix `W` reused forever**, throughout

$$
\frac12\le \alpha<2.
$$

The strongest target is unchanged: for every fixed `alpha<2`, with probability tending to one as `N -> infinity`, there are random constants `C(W)<infinity` and `rho(W)<1` such that

$$
\|F_W^t(x)\|\le C(W)\rho(W)^t\|x\|
$$

for **every** input `x` and every `t>=0`. Equivalently, the maximal radial Lyapunov exponent is negative. Positive homogeneity gives, for `W=sqrt(alpha) A`,

$$
\lambda_{\max}(W)=\lambda_{\max}(A)+\frac12\log\alpha,
$$

so the conjectural edge statement is

$$
P\left\{\lambda_{\max}(A_N)<-\frac12\log 2+\varepsilon\right\}\to1
$$

for every `eps>0`.

## Main advance of this run

The preceding run proved that noncontracting **period-one** ReLU eigenrays are exponentially unlikely for every `alpha<2`. This run treats a genuine tied multi-step class and proves an exact finite-width theorem:

> **Every projective cycle of period at least two whose phase supports are pairwise disjoint is exponentially unlikely, uniformly over every input and every period.**

This is a restriction of the original tied model, not a replacement by independent layers. The independence used below is **derived inside the one tied matrix** from the fact that pairwise-disjoint supports select pairwise-disjoint entry blocks.

Fix a projective period `k>=2`. A phase-marked cycle is a tuple of nonzero states `x_0,...,x_{k-1}` and positive gains `g_0,...,g_{k-1}` satisfying, cyclically,

$$
F_W(x_j)=g_jx_{j+1}.
$$

Because the output of ReLU is nonnegative, every phase of such a cycle is coordinatewise nonnegative. Put

$$
S_j=\operatorname{supp}(x_j),\qquad s_j=|S_j|.
$$

This run assumes only for the new theorem that the `S_j` are nonempty and **pairwise disjoint**.

For a fixed ordered support tuple define

$$
A_j=W_{S_{j+1},S_j},
$$

with indices modulo `k`, and let

$$
P=A_{k-1}\cdots A_0\in\mathbb R^{s_0\times s_0}.
$$

Let `R^+_{s_0,...,s_{k-1}}` be the number of positive nonzero real eigenvalues of `P`. Then the exact first-moment identity is

$$
\boxed{
E Z_{S_0,...,S_{k-1}}
=2^{-(kN-1)}E R^+_{s_0,...,s_{k-1}},
}
\tag{0.1}
$$

where `Z_{S_0,...,S_{k-1}}` is the number of phase-marked projective ReLU cycles with exactly those supports. This is `P16-DISJOINT-CYCLE-FIRST-MOMENT-39 — PROVED`.

Changing the sign of one Gaussian block leaves its law unchanged and sends `P` to `-P`. Hence positive and negative nonzero real eigenvalues have equal expected counts, while the total number of nonzero real eigenvalues is at most `min_j s_j`. Therefore

$$
E R^+_{s_0,...,s_{k-1}}
\le \frac12\min_j s_j,
$$

and consequently

$$
\boxed{
E Z_{S_0,...,S_{k-1}}
\le 2^{-kN}\min_j s_j.
}
\tag{0.2}
$$

Summing `(0.2)` over **all ordered pairwise-disjoint support tuples** gives

$$
\boxed{
P\{\exists\text{ pairwise-disjoint-support projective }k\text{-cycle}\}
\le
\frac{N}{k+1}\left(\frac{k+1}{2^k}\right)^N.
}
\tag{0.3}
$$

No gain restriction is needed. Thus `(0.3)` excludes even contracting exact cycles in this structural class. For period two,

$$
\boxed{
P\{\exists\text{ projective 2-cycle with }S_0\cap S_1=\varnothing\}
\le
\frac N3\left(\frac34\right)^N.
}
\tag{0.4}
$$

Since nonempty pairwise-disjoint supports force `k<=N`, summing `(0.3)` over all periods gives

$$
\boxed{
P\{\exists\text{ any pairwise-disjoint-support projective cycle of period }k\ge2\}
\le
\frac N3\left(\frac34\right)^N
+
\frac{N^2}{4}2^{-N}.
}
\tag{0.5}
$$

This is `P16-DISJOINT-CYCLE-EXCLUSION-39 — PROVED` and the `k=2` case is `P16-PERIOD2-DISJOINT-EXCLUSION-39 — PROVED`.

The bound is summable in `N`. Therefore, under **any coupling across widths having the correct Gaussian marginal at each width**, Borel--Cantelli gives eventual almost-sure absence of pairwise-disjoint-support projective cycles of every period at least two. This is an almost-sure-across-widths statement about the restricted cycle event, **not** all-time contraction of the original iteration.

The remaining obstruction becomes sharper. For a period-two candidate with supports `S,T`, let

$$
I=S\cap T,\qquad U=S\cup T,\qquad R=U^c,
$$

and let `u>0` on `S`, `v>0` on `T` be the two internal phase vectors. Conditional on the internal rows indexed by `U`, every outside row `i in R` imposes two centered Gaussian inequalities. Their correlation is exactly

$$
\rho=\frac{\langle u,v\rangle}{\|u\|\|v\|}
=\frac{\langle u_I,v_I\rangle}{\|u\|\|v\|}\in[0,1],
$$

and therefore

$$
\boxed{
P\{w_i^Tu\le0,\ w_i^Tv\le0\mid u,v,\text{ internal data}\}
=
\frac14+\frac{\arcsin\rho}{2\pi}.
}
\tag{0.6}
$$

The outside rows are conditionally independent, so the exact outside-union factor is the right side of `(0.6)` to the power `N-|U|`. This is `P16-OVERLAP-EXTERNAL-ORTHANT-39 — PROVED`.

At `rho=0` the per-row cost is `1/4`; at `rho=1` it is `1/2`. Thus overlap can recycle one full bit of sign entropy per outside row. Crucially, **overlap cardinality alone does not control `rho`**: positive vectors can put almost all of their mass on one common coordinate even when `|S cap T|=1`. The next attack must therefore control **overlap energy / cosine**, not merely the number of reused active coordinates.

Combining the previous period-one theorem with `(0.5)`, the typical exact-periodic obstruction below `alpha=2` has now been forced into a much narrower class:

$$
\boxed{
\text{period }k\ge2\text{ with active-coordinate reuse across phases,}
}
$$

or else a nonperiodic recurrent invariant set / indefinitely renewed nonnormal transient mechanism. This does not yet prove `(MAIN)`.

# 3. Projective cycles and the pairwise-disjoint restriction

Fix `k>=2`. A phase-marked projective cycle consists of nonzero vectors `x_j` and gains `g_j>0`, indices modulo `k`, with

$$
F_W(x_j)=g_jx_{j+1}.
\tag{3.1}
$$

Since the left side is nonnegative, every `x_j` is coordinatewise nonnegative. Put

$$
S_j=\{i:(x_j)_i>0\},\qquad s_j=|S_j|,
$$

and write `u_j=(x_j)_{S_j}>0`.

For the new theorem only, assume

$$
S_i\cap S_j=\varnothing\qquad(i\ne j).
\tag{3.2}
$$

This restriction is explicit. It is **not** assumed in `(MAIN)` and it is precisely what the rest of the run identifies as the next obstruction.

On the active target rows, `(3.1)` is equivalent to

$$
A_ju_j=g_ju_{j+1},
\qquad
A_j:=W_{S_{j+1},S_j}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

On all inactive rows it requires

$$
W_{S_{j+1}^c,S_j}u_j\le0
\quad\text{coordinatewise}.
\tag{3.4}
$$

Because the support sets are pairwise disjoint, the entry sets defining `A_0,...,A_{k-1}` are pairwise disjoint. Hence, **inside the original one tied matrix**, the blocks are independent rectangular Gaussian matrices with iid `N(0,alpha/N)` entries. No fresh-layer model has been introduced.

# 4. Internal product representation

Define positive scalars and rescaled phase vectors by

$$
c_0=1,
\qquad
c_j=\prod_{h=0}^{j-1}g_h\quad(j\ge1),
\qquad
v_j=c_ju_j.
$$

Then `(3.3)` gives

$$
A_jv_j=v_{j+1}\quad(0\le j\le k-2),
$$

while

$$
A_{k-1}v_{k-1}=\Lambda v_0,
\qquad
\Lambda:=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}g_j>0.
$$

Thus

$$
P v_0=\Lambda v_0,
\qquad
P=A_{k-1}\cdots A_0.
\tag{4.1}
$$

Moreover every intermediate vector

$$
v_j=A_{j-1}\cdots A_0v_0
$$

is strictly positive coordinatewise.

Conversely, a positive real eigenpair of `P` whose intermediate vectors are all sign-definite in one common orientation, together with the inactive-row inequalities `(3.4)`, produces a projective ReLU cycle after normalizing each phase. Therefore cycle counting reduces exactly to a positive-eigenvalue/sign problem plus inactive-row signs.

If the dimensions vary, `P` can have a deterministic zero eigenspace. We count only **positive nonzero** eigenvalues. For Gaussian blocks, nonzero eigenvalues are simple and all relevant intermediate coordinates are nonzero outside a finite union of algebraic null sets. This is enough to assign a unique projective sign class almost surely.

# 5. Internal sign symmetry

For each phase choose a diagonal sign matrix

$$
D_j\in\{\operatorname{diag}(\pm1,\ldots,\pm1)\}\subset\mathbb R^{s_j\times s_j}.
$$

Transform the internal blocks cyclically by

$$
A_j\mapsto A'_j=D_{j+1}A_jD_j,
\qquad D_k:=D_0.
\tag{5.1}
$$

Because each block has independent centered Gaussian entries and the blocks are entry-disjoint, the joint law of `(A_0,...,A_{k-1})` is invariant under `(5.1)`.

The product transforms by similarity:

$$
P'=D_0PD_0,
$$

so every eigenvalue is unchanged. If `v_0` is an eigenvector and

$$
v_j=A_{j-1}\cdots A_0v_0,
$$

then under `(5.1)` the entire phase tuple transforms as

$$
v_j\mapsto D_jv_j.
$$

Modulo the unavoidable simultaneous global sign, a tuple of nonzero coordinate-sign patterns has exactly

$$
2^{s_0+\cdots+s_{k-1}-1}
$$

classes. The sign group in `(5.1)` acts transitively on them. Therefore, if `R^+` denotes the total number of positive nonzero real eigenvalues of `P`, the expected number whose phase vectors are all positive after one global orientation is exactly

$$
2^{-(s_0+\cdots+s_{k-1}-1)}E R^+.
\tag{5.2}
$$

This step is exact and finite-dimensional.

# 6. Inactive-row signs and the exact first-moment identity

Conditional on all internal blocks and one internally positive candidate phase tuple, consider `(3.4)`.

At phase `j`, there are `N-s_{j+1}` inactive target rows. Because the source-column sets `S_j` are pairwise disjoint, every matrix entry used by these inactive-row linear forms is disjoint from:

1. every internal block `A_l`; and
2. every inactive-row linear form belonging to a different phase.

For each such row, the scalar product against `v_j` is therefore an independent centered nondegenerate Gaussian. Its probability of being nonpositive is exactly `1/2`.

The total number of inactive scalar inequalities is

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(N-s_{j+1})
=kN-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s_j.
$$

Hence the conditional probability that all inactive inequalities hold is exactly

$$
2^{-\left(kN-\sum_j s_j\right)}.
\tag{6.1}
$$

Multiplying `(5.2)` and `(6.1)` proves

$$
\boxed{
E Z_{S_0,...,S_{k-1}}
=2^{-(kN-1)}E R^+_{s_0,...,s_{k-1}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
$$

This is `P16-DISJOINT-CYCLE-FIRST-MOMENT-39`.

Notice the cancellation: every fixed support tuple pays exactly `kN-1` sign bits, **independent of the individual support sizes**. This is the multi-phase analogue of the support/sign cancellation in the period-one theorem, but it requires pairwise-disjoint phase supports.

# 7. No product-spectrum formula is needed

The random-matrix product in `(4.1)` is a product of independent rectangular real Gaussian blocks. There is substantial literature on such products, but the present theorem does not need an explicit eigenvalue density.

Indeed, changing only the sign of `A_0` leaves the joint block law unchanged and sends

$$
P\mapsto-P.
$$

Thus

$$
E R^+=E R^-.
$$

The number of nonzero eigenvalues of `P`, counted algebraically, is at most

$$
\operatorname{rank}(P)\le\min_j s_j.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
E R^+\le\frac12\min_j s_j.
}
\tag{7.1}
$$

Combining `(6.2)` and `(7.1)` gives `(0.2)`.

This bound is deliberately elementary: it avoids importing a real-eigenvalue large-deviation theorem for rectangular Ginibre products whose hypotheses would have to be checked separately.

For context, current primary literature was checked: Ipsen--Kieburg, *Weak Commutation Relations and Eigenvalue Statistics for Products of Rectangular Random Matrices* (arXiv:1310.4154), develops finite-size eigenvalue statistics for products including rectangular real Ginibre ensembles; Forrester--Ipsen, *Real eigenvalue statistics for products of asymmetric real Gaussian matrices* (2016), gives explicit real-eigenvalue statistics for square products. Neither result is used in the proof above.

The preceding period-one theorem retains its separate primary-source input: Edelman--Kostlan--Shub, *How many eigenvalues of a random matrix are real?*, JAMS 7 (1994), 247--267, DOI `10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1231689-0`.

# 8. Summing all disjoint supports

For each coordinate, a pairwise-disjoint ordered support tuple is equivalent to assigning that coordinate one of `k+1` labels:

- unused;
- active in phase `0`;
- ...;
- active in phase `k-1`.

Thus there are `(k+1)^N` labelings if empty supports are temporarily allowed. Empty supports contribute zero because `min_j s_j=0`.

For every labeling,

$$
\min_j s_j\le\frac1k\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s_j.
$$

Across all labelings, the sum of the total active population is

$$
Nk(k+1)^{N-1},
$$

because for each of the `N` coordinates there are `k` active labels and `(k+1)^{N-1}` arbitrary labels for the others. Hence

$$
\sum_{\text{disjoint support tuples}}\min_j s_j
\le N(k+1)^{N-1}.
\tag{8.1}
$$

Summing `(0.2)` and using Markov's inequality gives

$$
P\{Z_k^{\rm disj}\ge1\}
\le
2^{-kN}N(k+1)^{N-1}
=
\frac N{k+1}\left(\frac{k+1}{2^k}\right)^N.
\tag{8.2}
$$

This proves `P16-DISJOINT-CYCLE-EXCLUSION-39`.

The base

$$
b_k=\frac{k+1}{2^k}
$$

is decreasing for `k>=2`; `b_2=3/4` and `b_k<=1/2` for `k>=3`. Since nonempty disjoint supports force `k<=N`, summing `(8.2)` yields

$$
P\{\exists k\ge2:\ Z_k^{\rm disj}\ge1\}
\le
\frac N3\left(\frac34\right)^N
+
\frac{N^2}{4}2^{-N}.
\tag{8.3}
$$

The series over `N` of the right side converges. Thus the Borel--Cantelli corollary stated in the executive summary holds under any coupling of the width-indexed matrices.

# 9. Period two with overlap: the exact external-row law

The disjoint theorem identifies support overlap as the first unresolved exact-cycle mechanism. The dependence created by overlap can be quantified exactly at the rows outside the union.

Let a period-two internal candidate have positive phase vectors `u` on support `S` and `v` on support `T`. Put

$$
U=S\cup T,
\qquad I=S\cap T,
\qquad R=U^c.
$$

The internal equations use only rows indexed by `U`. Therefore the row vectors indexed by `R` remain independent Gaussian rows after conditioning on the complete internal candidate.

For `i in R`, the two inactive constraints are

$$
X_i=w_{iS}^Tu\le0,
\qquad
Y_i=w_{iT}^Tv\le0.
$$

Their variances and covariance are

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\frac\alpha N\|u\|^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)=\frac\alpha N\|v\|^2,
$$

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,Y_i)
=\frac\alpha N\langle u_I,v_I\rangle.
$$

Thus the correlation is

$$
\rho=\frac{\langle u_I,v_I\rangle}{\|u\|\|v\|}\in[0,1].
\tag{9.1}
$$

For a centered standard bivariate Gaussian pair of correlation `rho`, the classical Gaussian orthant formula gives

$$
P(X\le0,Y\le0)
=
\frac14+\frac{\arcsin\rho}{2\pi}.
\tag{9.2}
$$

Rows in `R` are independent, so the exact conditional factor contributed by them is

$$
\boxed{
\left(\frac14+\frac{\arcsin\rho}{2\pi}\right)^{N-|U|}.
}
\tag{9.3}
$$

This is `P16-OVERLAP-EXTERNAL-ORTHANT-39`.

Equation `(9.3)` explains precisely why overlap is not a superficial combinatorial nuisance. For disjoint phases `rho=0`, one pays `1/4` for the two inequalities in each external row. If the two phase vectors align strongly on their overlap, `rho` approaches one and the cost degrades toward `1/2` per row.

A cardinality-only argument cannot repair this. Even if `|I|=1`, abstract positive unit vectors on `S,T` can put `1-o(1)` of their norm on the common coordinate and hence have `rho=1-o(1)`. Whether the **dynamics** permits such localization is a substantive unresolved question. Any successful overlap theorem must therefore control at least one of:

- overlap cosine / shared energy;
- delocalization of recurrent phase vectors;
- the internal cost of concentrating both phases on the same coordinates;
- reset/synchronization or recycled-energy debt created by such concentration.

# 10. What the new theorem proves — and what it does not

## Uniformity

The support sum searches all pairwise-disjoint support tuples and all corresponding internal eigenvectors. The input/cycle may be chosen after observing `W`. Thus `(8.2)` is genuinely **uniform in input within the stated structural class**.

## Tied dependence

There is still only one random matrix `W`. Independent rectangular blocks appear solely because pairwise-disjoint supports select disjoint matrix entries. When supports overlap, the relevant blocks share entries and the proof explicitly stops; there is no independent-layer substitution.

## Gains and Lyapunov rate

The bound excludes every exact disjoint-support cycle, regardless of whether its cycle gain

$$
\prod_jg_j
$$

is less than, equal to, or greater than one. It therefore removes noncontracting disjoint cycles automatically, but gives no direct rate for a nonperiodic trajectory.

## Global conjecture

Excluding fixed rays and disjoint-support cycles does **not** imply negative maximal Lyapunov exponent. Remaining possibilities include:

- exact periodic cycles with coordinate reuse across phases;
- non-atomic recurrent invariant measures;
- approximate cycles whose stitching errors never close exactly;
- repeatedly renewed stable nonnormal transients.

The previous mask-cycle, block-pseudospectrum, numerical-range, information-projection, and lossy-witness machinery remains active for these branches.

# 11. Route comparison after the new theorem

## Lyapunov exponents

The maximal radial Lyapunov exponent is still the exact global object. The period-one theorem removes atomic invariant measures supported on one projective point; the present theorem removes all exact finite atomic cycles whose phase supports are pairwise disjoint. It does not control invariant measures supported on overlapping cycles or non-atomic recurrent sets.

## Random-matrix products

For disjoint supports the tied dynamics genuinely generates an independent rectangular block product because those blocks are disjoint subsets of the one matrix. This is an exact property of the original model. Overlap destroys that independence through shared entries, especially the shared core on `S cap T` in period two.

## Mean field

The new cycle exclusion is essentially sign-combinatorial and is independent of `alpha>0`; it is not a mean-field contraction statement. The previous period-one threshold `sqrt(alpha/2)` remains the place where the fresh one-step RMS factor reappears quantitatively.

## Concentration and large deviations

No concentration theorem is needed for `(8.2)`: exact sign symmetry, rank, and support entropy already win. This is stronger than a directionwise `exp[-cN(2-alpha)^2]` estimate for this structural class.

## Coupling / correlations

Equation `(9.3)` is the exact correlation law at outside rows for overlapping period-two candidates. It supplies a concrete scalar parameter `rho` for the next overlap attack.

## Deterministic Lipschitz / monotonicity

No one-step global Lipschitz contraction is asserted. The known low-`alpha` uniform theorem remains separate. Expanding one-step directions can exist even though disjoint exact cycles are absent.

# 12. Stress tests

## Dead neurons

A dead phase `F_W(x)=0` cannot belong to a projective cycle with positive gains. Dead trajectories remain harmless for maximal growth.

## Exceptional directions

The cycle may depend arbitrarily on `W`. The theorem sums over all support choices and internal spectral candidates; it does not impose ordinary-start independence.

## Correlations across time

No fresh temporal resampling is used. The disjoint theorem derives independence from entry-disjointness. The overlap lemma keeps the exact shared-coordinate covariance rather than declaring the two steps independent.

## Finite width

Equations `(6.2)`, `(8.2)`, `(8.3)`, and `(9.3)` are finite-dimensional exact statements/bounds. No `N -> infinity` approximation is needed for them.

## Rectangular rank deficiency

The internal cyclic product may have deterministic zero eigenvalues when phase dimensions differ. They are irrelevant because a projective cycle has positive total gain. The proof uses only positive nonzero eigenvalues and the rank bound.

## Period larger than width

Pairwise-disjoint nonempty phase supports imply `k<=N`; the event is empty for `k>N`. This is why the all-period sum is finite at each width.

## Activation and weight law

The primary theorem is stated for the intended iid Gaussian zero-bias ReLU model. The sign argument itself extends, as a separately labeled observation, to iid absolutely continuous sign-symmetric entries: sign transformations preserve block laws, outside linear forms are symmetric with no atom at zero, and genericity removes degenerate eigenpairs. No such universality extension is used for `(MAIN)`.

where `c_k` is the total order-`k` Hoeffding interaction covariance. It then claims the inverse

c_k

\sum_{m=0}^k(-1)^{k-m}\binom km Q_m.

$$
a_k=\frac{c_k}{\binom nk},

Q_m=\sum_{k=0}^m\binom mk a_k.

a_k=\sum_{m=0}^k(-1)^{k-m}\binom km Q_m,

exactly as P15 states.

# 18. Probability / quantifier / normalization classification
