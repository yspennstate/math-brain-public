Define the full two-parameter Legendre transform

\[
\mathcal R(s,a)
:=
\sup_{\theta<1/2,\,\eta\in\mathbb R}
\left\{

\tag{5.2}

# 11. Mask distortion requires a margin theorem

Output-space compression and mask compression are not the same thing.

Let

\[
z_i(U)=\sqrt{N/\alpha}\,w_i^TU,
\qquad
z_i(V)=\sqrt{N/\alpha}\,w_i^TV.
\]

If the two sign indicators differ, then for every `t>0` either

\[
|z_i(U)|\le t
\]

or

\[
|z_i(U)-z_i(V)|>t.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1N d_H(M_W(U),M_W(V))
&\le
\frac1N\#\{i:|z_i(U)|\le t\}\\
&\quad+
\frac1{Nt^2}\sum_i|z_i(U)-z_i(V)|^2\\
&=
\frac1N\#\{i:|z_i(U)|\le t\}
+
\frac{\|W(U-V)\|^2}{\alpha t^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.1}
\]

This proves `(0.20)` and, after conditional expectation, `(0.21)`.

## 11.1 Why no fresh Gaussian margin law may be inserted

For a deterministic direction independent of `W`, normalized preactivations are iid `N(0,1)` and the small-margin fraction is `O(t)` with high probability.

For an adaptive direction this is false as an automatic statement. A selector can be chosen orthogonal to many observed rows whenever the available dimension permits it, creating exact zero margins. Thus an adaptive dynamic margin theorem is a real missing hypothesis, not a technical detail.

## 11.2 Same-mask representations

If a future representation `V` is guaranteed to remain in the same activation cell as `U`, then the Hamming term is exactly zero and the gate transfers without any margin estimate. This is why the inherited small-cell geometry remains potentially useful.

However a same-matrix cell representative can itself contain arbitrarily large information about `W`. The representation must simultaneously satisfy:

- small `W`-output distortion;
- controlled conditional information;
- and either same-mask or controlled margin/Hamming distortion.

No one of these three requirements implies the other two.
