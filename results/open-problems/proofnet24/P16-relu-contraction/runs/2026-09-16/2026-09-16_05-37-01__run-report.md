# 2. Exact model, normalization, and probability levels

Throughout the new P16 derivation,

\[
W\in\mathbb R^{N\times N},
\qquad
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N),
\qquad
F_W(x)=(Wx)_+.
\tag{2.1}
\]

There is no bias. ReLU is coordinatewise. The matrix is tied across every iteration.

For a live state \(x\ne0\), write

\[
g_W(x)=\frac{\|F_W(x)\|}{\|x\|},
\qquad
T_W(x)=\frac{F_W(x)}{\|F_W(x)\|}
\tag{2.2}
\]

when \(F_W(x)\ne0\). Once one ReLU step has occurred, every live normalized state lies in

\[
S_+^{N-1}
=
\{x\ge0:\|x\|=1\}.
\]

Dead states are harmless: if \(F_W(x)=0\), every later state is zero.

The probability levels must not be conflated:

1. **Expectation / annealed:** e.g. a one-step fresh Gaussian norm or information inequality.
2. **High probability over one width:** a random \(W_N\) satisfies a uniform property with probability \(1-o(1)\), or with an explicit exponential bound.
3. **Almost surely across widths:** after placing \(W_N\) on a product probability space, a random-matrix statistic converges almost surely.
4. **Uniform in input:** after drawing one \(W\), a statement holds for every candidate input/orbit/cycle, including one selected as a function of \(W\).
5. **All time:** a single matrix controls an infinite trajectory or every trajectory forever.

The new exact two-cycle inequalities are deterministic and uniform in candidate cycles. Their Gaussian corollaries are high-probability (and the underlying spectral statistics have almost-sure across-width limits). They are **not** an all-time contraction theorem.

# 7. `P16-PERIOD2-COPOSITIVE-POLARIZATION-41` — PROVED

This is the main deterministic derivation.

Let \(W\) be any real square matrix. Suppose \(u,v\in S_+^{N-1}\) are a live exact projective two-cycle:

\[
F_W(u)=gv,
\qquad
F_W(v)=hu,
\qquad
g,h>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The phases may be chosen after observing \(W\). Put

\[
\rho=\langle u,v\rangle\in[0,1],
\qquad
\kappa=\sqrt{gh}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

## 7.1 Exact bilinear identities

Because \(v\ge0\) is supported exactly on coordinates where the positive output of \(Wu\) lives,

\[
v^\top Wu
=v^\top F_W(u)
=g\|v\|^2
=g.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Similarly,

\[
u^\top Wv=h.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore the skew-symmetric part cancels **exactly over the reversed two-step pair**:

\[
\boxed{
 u^\top Sv
=
\frac12(u^\top Wv+v^\top Wu)
=
\frac{g+h}{2}
\ge\sqrt{gh}=\kappa.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This cancellation is special to a two-cycle. It is unavailable for a generic longer support program.

## 7.2 Polarization bound

Polarization gives

\[
4u^\top Sv
=
(u+v)^\top S(u+v)
-
(u-v)^\top S(u-v).
\tag{7.6}
\]

The vector \(u+v\) is coordinatewise nonnegative, so by the definition of \(\Lambda_+\),

\[
(u+v)^\top S(u+v)
\le
\Lambda_+(W)\|u+v\|^2
=
2\Lambda_+(W)(1+\rho).
\tag{7.7}
\]

For the difference vector, the full minimum eigenvalue gives

\[
(u-v)^\top S(u-v)
\ge
\lambda_{\min}(S)\|u-v\|^2
=
-2B(W)(1-\rho).

## 7.3 Why this is stronger than the previous high-cosine Lipschitz bound

The preceding run proved, for a noncontracting two-cycle,

Equation (7.10) instead uses the exact reversal of the two phases to cancel the skew-symmetric part before bounding anything. The mismatch cost enters as \(1-\rho\), not \(\sqrt{1-\rho}\). It is therefore the sharper object for exact period two.

# 10. `P16-BADORBIT-PROJECTIVE-MOTION-41` — PROVED

This byproduct applies to arbitrary live orbits and helps compare the Lyapunov and deterministic-monotonicity viewpoints.

Let \(W\) be deterministic and let

\[
u_{t+1}=\frac{F_W(u_t)}{g_t},
\qquad
u_t\in S_+^{N-1},
\qquad
g_t=\|F_W(u_t)\|>0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

For notational simplicity write \(u_t=\nu_t\). Since \(u_{t+1}\) is the normalized positive output,

\[
g_t
=u_{t+1}^\top Wu_t.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Add and subtract \(u_t\):

\[
g_t
=u_t^\top Wu_t
+(u_{t+1}-u_t)^\top Wu_t.
\]

Because \(u_t\ge0\),

\[
u_t^\top Wu_t=u_t^\top Su_t\le\Lambda_+(W).
\]

Putting \(L=\|W\|_{2\to2}\) and

\[
d_t=\|u_{t+1}-u_t\|,
\]

we obtain the exact deterministic bound

\[
\boxed{
g_t\le\Lambda_+(W)+Ld_t.}
\tag{10.3}
\]

For a block of length \(T\), arithmetic-geometric mean and Jensen give

\[
\exp\left(
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}\log g_t
\right)
\le
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}g_t
\le
\Lambda_+(W)+L\bar d_T,
\tag{10.4}
\]

where

\[
\bar d_T=\frac1T\sum_{t<T}d_t.
\]

Hence, if the block mean log gain is at least \(\ell\),

\[
\boxed{
\bar d_T
\ge
\frac{[e^\ell-\Lambda_+(W)]_+}{L}.
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

In particular, if

\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}\log g_t\ge0
\]

and \(\Lambda_+(W)<1\), then along an appropriate subsequence

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\bar d_T
\ge
\frac{1-\Lambda_+(W)}{\|W\|}>0.
}
\tag{10.6}
\]

Thus below the Gaussian symmetric-cone threshold \(\alpha=1\), a bad orbit cannot generate its nonnegative Lyapunov exponent while becoming projectively stationary. It must continually traverse activation geometry. This complements, but does not replace, the mask-cycle/information machinery.

The estimate deliberately keeps \(\|W\|\) explicit. Standard Gaussian operator-norm asymptotics could turn (10.6) into a numeric high-dimensional constant, but no such asymptotic is needed for the deterministic conclusion.

# 12. Stress tests and route comparison

## 12.1 Dead neurons

If an orbit hits zero, it stays zero. No exact nonzero projective cycle contains a dead phase. The present theorem concerns only live phases \(g,h>0\).

## 12.2 Exceptional directions / adversarial input

The source's fixed-horizon theorem does not control \(x_0(W)\). In contrast, (7.10) is deterministic after \(W\) is fixed and applies to **every** exact two-cycle simultaneously. The Gaussian spectral good event likewise contains no reference to a preselected input. Thus the new period-two theorem genuinely handles the adversarial-input quantifier for that obstruction class.

## 12.3 Correlation across tied steps

No fresh-layer independence is used. Equations (7.3)--(7.5) exploit the fact that the *same* \(W\) maps \(u\) to \(v\) and \(v\) back to \(u\). The skew part cancels because the two bilinear forms are reversals of one another. Replacing the steps by independent matrices would destroy this identity.

## 12.4 Random-matrix products

The two-cycle theorem does not approximate \(W_{T,S}W_{S,T}\) by a product of independent Ginibre blocks. Overlap entries remain reused. The proof bypasses that dependence by collapsing the two gain equations into the symmetric bilinear form.

## 12.5 Mean-field recurrence

The ordinary mean-field one-step radial value \(\sqrt{\alpha/2}\) predicts the threshold \(2\) for a fresh direction. The new symmetric-cone quantity has limit \(\sqrt\alpha\), creating a separate threshold \(1\) for this *proof mechanism*. The mismatch is informative: a maximizer of \(x^\top Wx\) over the positive cone is much easier to find than a self-consistent ReLU recurrent orbit. The missing complementarity constraints are exactly where a proof beyond \(\alpha=1\) must gain entropy or energy.

## 12.6 Deterministic Lipschitz / monotonicity

A crude one-step norm argument gives the old uniform threshold \(\alpha<1/2\). The present polarization argument is not a global Lipschitz theorem; it uses two-step recurrence identities to cancel the skew part and therefore reaches \(12-8\sqrt2>1/2\) for excluding noncontracting exact period-two recurrence.

## 12.7 Nonnormal transients

Even eliminating exact periods one and two does not imply negative maximal Lyapunov exponent. Prior P16 Jordan examples show that near-unit pseudospectral witnesses can coexist with strictly stable asymptotics. Conversely, a bad ReLU orbit might continually renew transient growth without converging to a finite cycle. That route remains live.

## 12.8 Finite-width errors

The deterministic inequality (7.10) has no finite-width error. The only asymptotic inputs are \(\Lambda_+(W)\to\sqrt\alpha\) and \(B(W)\to\sqrt{2\alpha}\). Fixed-gap deviations are exponentially concentrated by Section 6. No Tracy--Widom-scale or optimal finite-size edge claim is used.

# 13. Sharp obstruction exposed by the new theorem

The new route splits naturally at \(\alpha=1\).

For \(\alpha<1\),

\[
\Lambda_+(W)\to\sqrt\alpha<1.
\]

Thus positive directions have a strict symmetric-quadratic deficit. Exact two-step recurrence can overcome it only by separating the phases enough that the negative lower edge of \(S\) contributes through the difference vector. This creates the cosine debt (9.5).

At \(\alpha=1\),

\[
\Lambda_+(W)\to1.
\]

The symmetric positive-cone margin closes exactly.

For \(\alpha>1\),

\[
\Lambda_+(W)\to\sqrt\alpha>1.
\]

There are positive vectors with symmetric quadratic value above one with overwhelming probability, so **any proof that only bounds a recurrent gain by the unconstrained positive-cone quadratic maximum cannot establish contraction** in this regime.

This does not construct a ReLU eigenray or cycle. The variational optimizer \(x\) need not satisfy

\[
(Wx)_+=q x
\]

or any small-residual analogue; it may have large off-support positive leakage, active-coordinate misalignment, or the wrong next mask. The sharp missing structure is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive-cone large quadratic value}
\quad+
\text{ReLU complementarity/self-consistency}.
}
\tag{13.1}
\]

The next proof must price the second requirement rather than hoping the first stays below one up to \(\alpha=2\).

This is `P16-NOGO-SYMMETRIC-COPOSITIVE-BEYOND1-41` — **DISPROVED ROUTE**.
