# 3. Probability-level and model bookkeeping before the new proof

These levels are not interchangeable.

## 3.1 Expectation / mean-field one step

For deterministic `x`, or more generally `x` independent of a fresh Gaussian `W`,

\[
E\|F_W(x)\|^2=\frac\alpha2\|x\|^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is an annealed one-step statement. It does **not** hold after maximizing over `x=x(W)`.

## 3.2 Fixed-horizon tied dynamics

The reviewed theorem gives exponentially high-probability concentration through each fixed horizon for a prescribed deterministic input, and extends to random starts / input measures only under independence from `W`.

This is not a uniform-in-input theorem.

## 3.3 Uniform every-input/all-time tied theorem already proved below `1/2`

Inherited `P16-UNIF-LOW` is for the original tied model. Let

\[
M_+(W)=\sup_{x\ge0,\ \|x\|=1}\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)\|.
\]

The validated Gaussian-process positive-cone bound is

\[
M_+(W)\le\sqrt{2\alpha}+o_P(1).
\]

Hence for every fixed `alpha<1/2`, with probability tending to one there is a deterministic `rho(alpha)<1` such that

\[
\|F_W(x)\|\le\rho(\alpha)\|x\|
\qquad\text{for every }x,
\]

and therefore

\[
\|F_W^t(x)\|\le\rho(\alpha)^t\|x\|
\qquad\text{for every }x,t.
\]

This remains the only complete whole-state-space infinite-time tied theorem.

## 3.4 Almost-sure statements

Whenever a width-indexed failure bound is summable, Borel--Cantelli gives eventual almost-sure validity under any chosen coupling having the correct Gaussian marginal at each width. This is an across-width statement and is not the same as an infinite-time result for one fixed matrix.

## 3.5 Dead neurons and exceptional directions

Dead directions exist: if `W` is invertible, `x=-W^{-1}1` is killed in one step. Therefore no uniform lower radial law can be true. They do not obstruct contraction.

The actual difficulty is the opposite quantifier: an expanding or noncontracting direction may be selected after `W` is known. The source explicitly leaves that possibility open. The new theorem prices one precise form of such adaptivity.

# 4. Gaussian symmetric/skew decomposition

Write

\[
W=S+A,
\qquad
S=\frac{W+W^T}{2},
\qquad
A=\frac{W-W^T}{2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because the entries of `W` are jointly Gaussian and, for each `i<j`, the sum and difference of `W_ij,W_ji` are uncorrelated, `S` and `A` are independent.

For `i<j`,

\[
A_{ij}\sim N\!\left(0,\frac\alpha{2N}\right),
\qquad
A_{ji}=-A_{ij},
\qquad A_{ii}=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every deterministic unit `u`,

\[
Au\sim N\!\left(0,
\frac\alpha{2N}(I-uu^T)
\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed, with `sigma^2=alpha/(2N)`,

\[
\operatorname{Var}((Au)_i)=\sigma^2(1-u_i^2),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}((Au)_i,(Au)_j)=-\sigma^2u_i u_j.
\]

This split is a decomposition of **one tied matrix**, not a fresh-layer replacement. Temporal correlations remain fully present.

# 5. Compressible expanding pseudorays are uniformly absent

The previous run's deterministic complementarity inequality admits a useful local form.

Let `x>=0`, `||x||=1`, `q>=1`, and

\[
e=\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)-qx\|,
\qquad L=\|W\|_{\rm op}.
\]

Writing `B={i:(Wx)_i<0}`, on `B` the residual equals `-q x_i`, hence `||x_B||<=e/q`. Also

\[
x^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)\ge q-e.
\]

The negative-preactivation leakage satisfies

\[
0\le D:=\sum_{i\in B}x_i[-(Wx)_i]
\le \frac{Le}{q}.
\]

Since `x^T W x=x^T Sx`,

\[
\boxed{
q-x^TSx\le e\left(1+\frac Lq\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Unlike a global `Lambda_+` bound, `(5.1)` allows us to localize the symmetric Rayleigh value to low-complexity vectors.

## 5.1 Uniform sparse symmetric-Rayleigh bound

Fix throughout this section

\[
\beta=\frac1{250},
\qquad
\tau=\frac1{800\sqrt\alpha},
\qquad
r=\frac1{40\sqrt\alpha},
\qquad
L_0=8\sqrt\alpha.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a fixed deterministic unit vector `y`,

\[
y^TSy=y^TWy\sim N(0,\alpha/N).
\]

Therefore

\[
P\{y^TSy\ge1/2\}
\le \exp[-N/(8\alpha)].
\tag{5.3}
\]

For each support of size at most `beta N`, take a `tau`-net of its unit sphere of size at most `(1+2/tau)^{beta N}`. The number of supports is at most `(N+1)e^{NH(beta)}`. Hence

\[
P\{\exists\text{ sparse net point }y:\ y^TSy\ge1/2\}
\le
(N+1)e^{-c_{\rm sp}(\alpha)N},
\tag{5.4}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
c_{\rm sp}(\alpha)
=\frac1{8\alpha}
-H(1/250)
-\frac1{250}\log(1+1600\sqrt\alpha).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `alpha in [1/2,2)`, this is minimized at `alpha=2`, and

\[
c_{\rm sp}(2)
=0.005523069646127948\ldots>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

On the operator-norm event `L<=L_0`, net interpolation costs at most

\[
2L_0\tau=\frac1{50}.
\]

Thus every unit vector supported on at most `beta N` coordinates satisfies

\[
y^TSy\le\frac12+\frac1{50}=\frac{13}{25}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

## 5.2 Extension to compressible vectors

Define

\[
\operatorname{Comp}(\beta,r)
=
\left\{x\in S^{N-1}:
\operatorname{dist}(x,
\{y\in S^{N-1}:|\operatorname{supp}y|\le\beta N\})\le r
\right\}.
\]

If `x` is within `r` of a sparse unit `y`, then on `L<=L_0`,

\[
x^TSx
\le y^TSy+2L_0r
\le\frac{13}{25}+\frac25
=\frac{23}{25}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Insert `(5.8)` into `(5.1)`. For every nonnegative compressible unit `x` and every `q>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)-qx\|
\ge
\varepsilon_{\rm comp}(\alpha)
:=
\frac{2}{25(1+8\sqrt\alpha)}.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Therefore:

## `P16-COMPRESSIBLE-PSEUDORAY-EXCLUSION-43` — PROVED

For every fixed `alpha in [1/2,2)`, for all sufficiently large `N`, except on an event of probability at most

\[
2e^{-(8-\log81)N}
+(N+1)e^{-c_{\rm sp}(\alpha)N},
\tag{5.10}
\]

every nonnegative unit vector `x` satisfying

\[
\inf_{q\ge1}
\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)-qx\|
<\varepsilon_{\rm comp}(\alpha)
\]

is **incompressible**: `x notin Comp(beta,r)`.

This is finite-width and uniform over `x=x(W)`. It covers the entire unresolved interval `1/2<=alpha<2`, but only for the approximate-expanding-fixed-ray mechanism; it is not an all-time contraction theorem.

# 6. Incompressibility produces many usable coordinates

## `P16-INCOMPRESSIBLE-SPREAD-43` — PROVED

Let `u>=0`, `||u||=1`, and suppose

\[
u\notin\operatorname{Comp}(\beta,r)
\]

with `(beta,r)` from `(5.2)`. Then for all sufficiently large `N` there exists a set `J` such that

\[
|J|\ge c_*N,
\qquad
c_*:=\frac{\beta r^2}{32}
=\frac1{12,800,000\,\alpha},
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
u_i\ge\frac{r}{2\sqrt N}
\quad(i\in J),
\qquad
\|u_J\|^2\le\frac18.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Let `I` be the indices of the largest `floor(beta N)` coordinates of `u` in magnitude, and put `a=||u_I||`. The nearest unit vector supported on `I` is `u_I/a` (when `a>0`) and has squared distance

\[
2(1-a).
\]

Incompressibility gives

\[
a<1-r^2/2,
\]

hence the tail mass obeys

\[
\|u_{I^c}\|^2
=1-a^2
>r^2-r^4/4
\ge3r^2/4.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Coordinates outside `I` have squared magnitude at most `2/(\beta N)` for all large `N`. Coordinates with magnitude below `r/(2sqrt N)` contribute at most `r^2/4` in total. Consequently the set

\[
J_0=
\left\{i\in I^c:u_i\ge\frac{r}{2\sqrt N}\right\}
\]

carries squared mass at least `r^2/2`, so

\[
|J_0|\ge\frac{\beta r^2}{4}N.
\]

Choose a subset `J` of size between `beta r^2 N/32` and `beta r^2 N/16`. Since every coordinate in `I^c` has squared magnitude at most `2/(beta N)`,

\[
\|u_J\|^2\le r^2/8\le1/8.
\]

This proves `(6.1)`--`(6.2)`.

The only role of this lemma is geometric: a low-residual expanding pseudoray cannot concentrate all its mass in a tiny support, so the independent skew Gaussian must satisfy many simultaneous coordinate constraints.

# 7. Conditional skew small ball for a fixed query

Fix `S`, a nonnegative unit vector `u` satisfying the spread conclusion above, and `q>=1`. Under the independent skew law, set

\[
Z=Au,
\qquad
\sigma^2=\frac\alpha{2N}.
\]

For any `L subseteq J`, `(4.3)` gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_L)
=\sigma^2(I_L-u_Lu_L^T).
\]

By the matrix determinant lemma,

\[
\det\operatorname{Cov}(Z_L)
=\sigma^{2|L|}(1-\|u_L\|^2)
\ge\frac78\sigma^{2|L|}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Define

\[
\delta:=\frac r8=\frac1{320\sqrt\alpha},
\qquad
\varepsilon_0:=\frac{\delta\sqrt{c_*}}2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For `alpha>=1/2`,

\[
\frac{2\delta}{\sqrt{\pi\alpha}}
=\frac1{160\sqrt\pi\,\alpha}
<\frac1{16}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Suppose

\[
\|\operatorname{ReLU}((S+A)u)-qu\|\le\varepsilon_0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

At least half of the coordinates in `J` then have scalar residual at most `delta/sqrt N`; otherwise the squared residual would exceed `c_*delta^2/2`, contradicting `(7.2)`.

For such a coordinate,

\[
qu_i\ge\frac r{2\sqrt N}
>\frac\delta{\sqrt N},
\]

so the ReLU output is strictly positive and the preactivation itself must lie within `delta/sqrt N` of `qu_i`. Equivalently,

\[
(Au)_i\in
\left[qu_i-(Su)_i-\frac\delta{\sqrt N},
qu_i-(Su)_i+\frac\delta{\sqrt N}\right].
\tag{7.5}
\]

For a prescribed subset `L` of at least `|J|/2` coordinates, the Gaussian density bound from `(7.1)` gives

\[
P_A\{(7.5)\text{ for all }i\in L\}
\le
\sqrt{\frac87}
\left(\frac{2\delta}{\sqrt{\pi\alpha}}\right)^{|L|}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Union over the possible subsets `L`. By `(7.3)`,

\[
\boxed{
P_A\left\{
\|\operatorname{ReLU}((S+A)u)-qu\|\le\varepsilon_0
\right\}
\le
\sqrt{\frac87}\,2^{-|J|}
\le
\sqrt{\frac87}\,e^{-c_*N\log2}.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This proves:

## `P16-SKEW-FIXEDQUERY-SMALLBALL-43` — PROVED

For every fixed `S`, every fixed nonnegative incompressible unit `u`, and every fixed `q>=1`, the independent skew component pays an exponentially small probability to make `u` an `epsilon_0`-accurate expanding ReLU pseudoray.

The bound is uniform in the centers `(Su)_i`; no distributional assumption on those centers is used.

# 8. Remove the multiplier and prove the adaptive information debt

## 8.1 Uniformity over `q` without charging information for `q`

On

\[
\|W\|\le L_0,
\]

if

\[
\|F_W(u)-qu\|\le1,
\]

then

\[
q\le\|F_W(u)\|+1\le L_0+1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Set

\[
\varepsilon_*=
\min\left\{
\frac12\varepsilon_0,
\varepsilon_{\rm comp}(\alpha),
\frac12
\right\}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Cover `[1,L_0+1]` by a grid of spacing at most `epsilon_*`. Its size is a finite constant depending only on `alpha`, for instance

\[
K_\alpha
\le
2+\frac{L_0+1}{\varepsilon_*}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

If there exists `q>=1` with residual at most `epsilon_*`, then a grid point has residual at most `2epsilon_*<=epsilon_0`. Thus `(7.7)` yields, uniformly in fixed `S` and fixed incompressible `u`,

\[
P_A\left\{
\|S+A\|\le L_0,
\ \inf_{q\ge1}\|F_{S+A}(u)-qu\|\le\varepsilon_*
\right\}
\le
K_\alpha\sqrt{\frac87}\,
e^{-c_*N\log2}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is `P16-SKEW-INDEPENDENT-QUERY-SMALLBALL-43`.

## 8.2 Change of measure for a fully adaptive selector

Let `E` be a `W`-measurable event of probability `p>0`. Let `U` be a possibly randomized nonnegative unit selector, allowed to depend on the full `(S,A)`, such that on `E`:

1. `||W||<=L_0`;
2. `U notin Comp(beta,r)`;
3. there exists `q>=1` with
   \[
   \|F_W(U)-qU\|\le\varepsilon_*.
   \tag{8.5}
   \]

Let `P_E` denote the conditional law of `(S,A,U)` given `E`.

Construct the reference law

\[
Q(dS\,dA\,dU)
=P_S(dS)P_A(dA)K_S(dU),
\tag{8.6}
\]

where `K_S` is the conditional law `P_E(U in du | S)` on the `P_{S|E}`-support, extended arbitrarily outside that support using any fixed incompressible nonnegative unit vector. Under `Q`, `U` preserves its conditional distribution given `S` from the bad event but is **decoupled from the skew matrix `A`**.

The KL chain rule gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
D(P_E\|Q)
=
\log\frac1p
+I(A;U\mid S,E).
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

Indeed,

\[
D(P_{S,A|E}\|P_SP_A)=D(P_{W|E}\|P_W)=\log(1/p)
\]

because `E` is `W`-measurable, and the conditional `U` term is precisely the conditional mutual information with `A` after `S` is given.

Let `G` be the event in `(8.5)` together with the norm and incompressibility conditions. Then `P_E(G)=1`. Under `Q`, `(8.4)` integrates over `S,U` and yields

\[
Q(G)
\le
K_\alpha\sqrt{\frac87}\,e^{-c_*N\log2}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Binary data processing applied to `1_G` gives

\[
D(P_E\|Q)
\ge\log\frac1{Q(G)}.
\]

Combining with `(8.7)`--`(8.8)` proves the central new theorem.

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{P(E)}
+I(A;U\mid S,E)
\ge
c_*N\log2
-\log\!\left(K_\alpha\sqrt{\frac87}\right).
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Here

\[
\boxed{
c_*=\frac1{12,800,000\,\alpha}>0.}
\tag{8.10}
\]

Thus for all sufficiently large `N`, a simpler asymptotic form is

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{P(E)}
+I(A;U\mid S,E)
\ge
c_{43}(\alpha)N,
\qquad
c_{43}(\alpha)=\frac{\log2}{25,600,000\,\alpha}.
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

## 9.1 S-measurable selectors are exponentially unlikely to work

If a selector is conditionally independent of `A` given `S,E`, then

\[

\le H(U\mid S,E)
\le\log M_N.

## 9.3 Why this still does not prove the global theorem

A deterministic continuous selector `U=U(W)` can have order-`N` or infinite exact mutual information with `A`; earlier P16 work already rejected any general bound of exact mutual information by a finite row-replacement `L2` sensitivity. Therefore `(8.9)` cannot be inverted into an unconditional probability bound for the existence of a pseudoray unless one first constructs a low-information representation or establishes a dynamic upper bound on skew information.

A naive ambient Euclidean net does not help. ReLU is 1-Lipschitz, so for fixed `q`,

\[

\|F_W(u)-qu\|

## 9.4 Fixed rays are not the only possible obstruction

Even complete exclusion of low-residual expanding fixed rays would not imply a negative maximal Lyapunov exponent. A bad tied orbit can in principle use:

- primitive projective cycles of period at least two;
- non-atomic recurrent invariant measures;
- support/mask switching with no single near-fixed direction;
- repeatedly renewed nonnormal transient amplification.

Thus `P16-SKEW-ADAPTIVE-INFO-COST-43` closes one important mechanism but not the global Lyapunov problem.
