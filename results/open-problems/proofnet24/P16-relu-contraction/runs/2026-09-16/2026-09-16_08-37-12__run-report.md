# 0. Executive result

P16 remains **OPEN** for the original tied-weight Gaussian ReLU iteration

\[
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N),\qquad
F_W(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx),\qquad
x_{t+1}=F_W(x_t),
\]

with the **same square matrix `W` reused forever**, zero bias, and coordinatewise ReLU, throughout

\[
\boxed{\tfrac12\le\alpha<2}.
\]

The strongest requested statement remains:

> for every fixed `alpha<2`, with probability tending to one as `N->infinity`, there exist random `C(W)<infinity` and `rho(W)<1` such that
> \[
> \|F_W^t(x)\|\le C(W)\rho(W)^t\|x\|
> \]
> for **every** input `x` and **every** `t>=0`.

Equivalently, the maximal radial Lyapunov exponent is negative. No counterexample to this strongest statement is found in this run.

This run makes four theorem-sized advances.

1. **Fixed-query directional alignment has an order-`N` large-deviation cost all the way to `alpha=2`.** For every fixed unit query independent of `W`, the scalar
   \[
   H_v(W):=v^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wv)
   \]
   has mean at most
   \[
   \mu_\alpha:=\sqrt{\alpha/(2\pi)}
   \]
   and Gaussian subgaussian parameter `alpha/N`. Hence
   \[
   P\{H_v\ge1-\varepsilon\}
   \le
   \exp\left[-N\frac{[1-\varepsilon-\mu_\alpha]_+^2}{2\alpha}\right].
   \]
   At the edge `alpha=2`, `epsilon=0`, the rate tends to
   \[
   \boxed{0.047482679772069526\ldots}
   \]
   nats per coordinate. Unlike the earlier energy-only debt `~N(2-alpha)^2/40`, this directional-alignment cost does **not** collapse as `alpha↑2`.

2. **A lossy adaptive witness theorem closes the exact-MI gap at the level of a representation.** On any `W`-measurable bad event `E`, if an adaptive unit selector `U` is an `epsilon` expanding pseudoray, `||W||<=L_0`, and a unit representation `V` has conditional mean distortion `E[||U-V|| | E]<=delta`, then
   \[
   \boxed{
   \log\frac1{P(E)}+I(W;V\mid E)
   \ge
   \frac{N}{2\alpha}
   \left[1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta-\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}\right]_+^2.
   }
   \]
   This is genuinely lossy: `V` need not itself be a pseudoray. It is also fully adaptive: `U` and `V` may depend on the entire tied matrix. The remaining obstruction is now an **upper** information budget for a dynamically natural low-distortion representation.

3. **A nonnegative-Lyapunov orbit has a pseudoray-or-motion dichotomy.** If no one-step expanding/neutral pseudoray of residual at most `epsilon` occurs on a long block, then any block whose mean log gain is close to nonnegative must spend a fixed positive fraction of its steps moving by at least `epsilon/(2||W||)` in projective space. Thus bad growth cannot simultaneously avoid pseudorays and remain nearly stationary.

4. **Eventually periodic activation masks force an exact noncontracting projective cycle.** If the activation-mask sequence becomes periodic with period `d` and the radial Lyapunov exponent of that live tail is nonnegative, then finite-dimensional cone Perron--Frobenius/Krein--Rutman theory produces an exact projective cycle of period dividing `d` with product gain at least one. Hence the inherited exact fixed-ray / period-two / disjoint-cycle exclusion theorems already rule out several entire classes of eventually periodic tails. A surviving bad orbit must either use a still-uncontrolled primitive cycle or genuinely **aperiodic mask switching**.

A fifth deterministic compactness lemma shows that any nonnegative-Lyapunov orbit produces arbitrarily accurate finite **approximate** projective cycles with near-nonnegative mean log gain. This gives a precise robust-closing formulation of the remaining all-time problem.

## 3.1 Mean-field expectation

For a deterministic vector independent of a fresh Gaussian matrix,

\[
E\|F_W(x)\|^2=\frac\alpha2\|x\|^2.
\]

This is annealed and pointwise. It cannot be substituted after choosing `x` from `W`.

## 3.2 Fixed-horizon high probability

The source theorem controls one prescribed deterministic start for fixed `T`; its random-start and input-measure variants require independence from `W`.

## 3.3 Uniform-in-input, all-time regime already proved

Inherited `P16-UNIF-LOW` gives

\[
M_+(W):=
\sup_{\substack{x\ge0\\\|x\|=1}}
\|F_W(x)\|
\le\sqrt{2\alpha}+o_P(1).
\]

For every fixed `alpha<1/2`, choose `sqrt(2alpha)<rho<1`. With high probability `M_+(W)<=rho`. For an **arbitrary signed** start, only the first step needs a prefactor:

\[
\|x_t\|\le\|W\|\rho^{t-1}\|x_0\|,\qquad t\ge1,
\]

hence

\[
\|F_W^t(x_0)\|
\le C(W)\rho^t\|x_0\|,
\qquad
C(W)=\max\{1,\|W\|/\rho\}.
\]

No one-step contraction for arbitrary signed inputs is asserted.

Summable failure probabilities over width can yield eventual almost-sure statements under a specified coupling by Borel--Cantelli. That is different from all-time contraction for one fixed matrix.

## 3.5 Dead directions

Dead neurons/directions help contraction. The hard event is the existence of a `W`-dependent recurrent or expanding direction; uniform lower radial laws are neither needed nor true.

# 4. `P16-FIXEDQUERY-ALIGNMENT-TAIL-44` — PROVED

The earlier information theory priced **energy**. Here we price the stronger event of **directional self-alignment**.

Fix a deterministic unit vector `v` independent of `W`, and define

\[
H_v(W):=v^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wv).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write

\[
W=\sqrt{\alpha/N}\,G,
\]

with `G` having iid standard normal entries. For fixed `v`,

\[
Gv\sim N(0,I_N)
\]

with independent coordinates. Therefore

\[
E H_v
=
\sqrt{\frac\alpha N}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\sum_{i=1}^Nv_i
\le
\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}
=:\mu_\alpha,
\tag{4.2}
\]

where the last inequality uses `sum_i v_i <= ||v||_1<=sqrt(N)`; no nonnegativity of `v` is needed for the upper bound.

As a function of the Gaussian matrix `G`,

\[
|H_v(G)-H_v(G')|
\le
\sqrt{\frac\alpha N}\,\|(G-G')v\|
\le
\sqrt{\frac\alpha N}\,\|G-G'\|_F,
\tag{4.3}
\]

because ReLU is 1-Lipschitz and `||v||=1`. Gaussian concentration / Herbst therefore gives

\[
\log E\exp\{\lambda(H_v-EH_v)\}
\le
\frac{\lambda^2\alpha}{2N}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining `(4.2)` and `(4.4)`, for every real `a`,

\[
\boxed{
P\{H_v\ge a\}
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2\alpha}[a-\mu_\alpha]_+^2\right].
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

If `v` is an `epsilon` expanding/neutral pseudoray, i.e. for some `q>=1`,

\[
\|F_W(v)-qv\|\le\varepsilon,
\]

then

\[
H_v
\ge q-\varepsilon
\ge1-\varepsilon.
\]

Thus for every fixed query independent of `W`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\inf_{q\ge1}\|F_W(v)-qv\|\le\varepsilon
\right\}
\le
\exp[-N c_{\rm al}(\alpha,\varepsilon)],
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
 c_{\rm al}(\alpha,\varepsilon)
 =
 \frac1{2\alpha}
 \left[1-\varepsilon-\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}\right]_+^2.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a finite-width theorem with exact normalization.

# 6. `P16-LOSSY-ALIGNMENT-KL-44` — PROVED

This theorem addresses the previous run's explicit missing bridge: exact selectors may have huge/infinite mutual information, so pass to a **lossy representation**.

Let `E in sigma(W)` be a bad matrix event with

\[
p=P(E)>0.
\]

On `E`, let `U` be a possibly randomized unit selector, allowed to depend on the full matrix `W` and independent auxiliary randomness, satisfying

\[
\inf_{q\ge1}\|F_W(U)-qU\|\le\varepsilon
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
\|W\|\le L_0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let `V` be **any** unit-vector representation, also allowed to depend on `W`, with conditional mean Euclidean distortion

\[
E[\|U-V\|\mid E]\le\delta.
\tag{6.3}
\]

No claim is made that `V` itself is a pseudoray.

For unit `u,v`, ReLU's 1-Lipschitz property gives the deterministic alignment transfer

\[
\begin{aligned}
|v^TF_W(v)-u^TF_W(u)|
&\le |(v-u)^TF_W(v)|
   +|u^T(F_W(v)-F_W(u))|\\
&\le \|W\|\|u-v\|+\|W\|\|u-v\|\\
&=2\|W\|\|u-v\|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

From `(6.1)`,

\[
U^TF_W(U)\ge1-\varepsilon.
\]

Thus under `P(.|E)`,

\[
E[V^TF_W(V)\mid E]
\ge
1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Now define the product reference law

\[
Q(dW\,dV)=P_W(dW)P_{V|E}(dV).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Because `E` is `W`-measurable,

\[
\boxed{
D(P_{W,V|E}\|Q)
=
\log\frac1p+I(W;V\mid E).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Under `Q`, conditional on `V=v`, the vector is fixed and independent of `W`. Equations `(4.2)`--`(4.4)` therefore imply the **uniform** exponential-moment bound

\[
\log E_Q e^{\lambda V^TF_W(V)}
\le
\lambda\mu_\alpha
+\frac{\lambda^2\alpha}{2N}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The entropy variational inequality gives, for every `lambda>0`,

\[
D(P_{W,V|E}\|Q)
\ge
\lambda(1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta-\mu_\alpha)
-\frac{\lambda^2\alpha}{2N}.
\]

Optimizing in `lambda` proves

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{P(E)}+I(W;V\mid E)
\ge
\frac{N}{2\alpha}
\left[
1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta
-\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}
\right]_+^2.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This is the desired lossy adaptive information theorem.

### Explicit source-norm specialization

Taking the source-certified high-probability bound `L_0=8sqrt(alpha)` gives the bracket

\[
1-\varepsilon-16\sqrt\alpha\,\delta
-\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

At the worst edge `alpha=2` and `epsilon=0`, a strictly positive rate remains whenever

\[
\delta<
\frac{1-1/\sqrt\pi}{16\sqrt2}
=0.019260281299069676\ldots.
\tag{6.11}
\]

The constants are not claimed optimal; the qualitative gain is that the permitted distortion is a fixed positive constant even at `alpha=2`.

### Codebook / information-rate corollary

If

\[
I(W;V\mid E)\le rN,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
P(E)
\le
\exp\left\{-N\left(
\frac{[1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta-\mu_\alpha]_+^2}{2\alpha}
-r\right)\right\}.
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

In particular an `M_N`-point deterministic codebook has `r<=N^{-1}log M_N`.

This gives an exact quantitative target for `P16-DYNAMIC-WITNESS-RATEDISTORTION-45`.

# 7. `P16-ENTROPY-PSEUDORAY-44` — PROVED, and a route obstruction

Let `K` be a deterministic `W`-independent subset of the unit sphere, and let `N(K,delta)` be its Euclidean covering number. On `||W||<=L_0`, if some `u in K` satisfies `(6.1)` and `v` is a net point with `||u-v||<=delta`, then by `(6.4)`

\[
v^TF_W(v)
\ge1-\varepsilon-2L_0\delta.
\]

A union bound with `(4.5)` yields

\[
\boxed{
P\{\exists u\in K:\inf_{q\ge1}\|F_W(u)-qu\|\le\varepsilon\}
\le
N(K,\delta)
\exp\left[-N c_{\rm al}(\alpha,\varepsilon+2L_0\delta)\right]
+P\{\|W\|>L_0\}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is useful for low-entropy dynamically reachable classes.

It also rigorously explains why a full ambient sphere net is still not a closure. At edge-scale distortion `delta<0.0193`, the ambient covering entropy per dimension is of order `log(C/delta)`, several nats per coordinate, whereas the alignment rate at `alpha=2` is only `0.04748...`. The mismatch is enormous. This rejects the **ambient-net route**, not the original contraction conjecture.

A generic isotropic Gaussian rate-distortion smoothing has the same problem. If one releases `U+sigma Z` with `Z~N(0,I_N)` and chooses `sigma` of order `delta/sqrt(N)` to keep Euclidean distortion of order `delta`, the universal entropy bound is of order

\[
\frac N2\log(1+\delta^{-2}),
\]

again much larger than `(5.1)` at the distortion needed by `(6.11)`. Thus the required compression must exploit **dynamical structure**, not generic sphere compression.

# 8. `P16-BLOCK-PSEUDORAY-OR-MOTION-44` — PROVED

Consider any live normalized tied orbit

\[
u_t=\frac{x_t}{\|x_t\|}\ge0,
\qquad
g_t=\|F_W(u_t)\|>0,
\qquad
u_{t+1}=\frac{F_W(u_t)}{g_t}.
\]

Let

\[
d_t=\|u_{t+1}-u_t\|,
\qquad
L=\|W\|.
\]

Then `g_t<=L`. Define the expanding/neutral pseudoray residual

\[
r_t^+
=
\inf_{q\ge1}\|F_W(u_t)-qu_t\|.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Fix `0<epsilon<1` and suppose throughout a block `0<=t<T` that

\[
r_t^+>\varepsilon.
\tag{8.2}
\]

If

\[
d_t\le\frac\varepsilon{2L}
\tag{8.3}
\]

and `g_t>=1`, then choosing `q=g_t` gives

\[
r_t^+\le g_td_t\le\varepsilon/2,
\]

contradicting `(8.2)`. Hence under `(8.3)` one has `g_t<1`. Choosing `q=1`,

\[
r_t^+
\le
\|g_tu_{t+1}-u_t\|
\le
(1-g_t)+g_td_t
\le
(1-g_t)+Ld_t,
\]

so `(8.2)` and `(8.3)` imply

\[
\boxed{g_t<1-\varepsilon/2.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Put

\[
a=-\log(1-\varepsilon/2)>0.
\]

Let `m` be the number of large-movement steps violating `(8.3)`. On the other `T-m` steps, `log g_t<=-a`; on large-movement steps, `log g_t<=log L`. Therefore if

\[
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}\log g_t\ge-\eta,
\qquad 0\le\eta<a,
\tag{8.5}
\]

then

\[
-\eta T
\le
m\log L-(T-m)a,
\]

and hence, for `L>1`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac mT
\ge
\frac{a-\eta}{\log L+a}.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}d_t
\ge
\frac\varepsilon{2L}
\frac{a-\eta}{\log L+a}.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

If `L<=1`, the global map is already nonexpansive and the interesting strict-growth case disappears; the source event `(1.1)` gives a convenient deterministic upper bound `L_0>1` in the random model.

# 9. `P16-NONNEG-LYAP-DICHOTOMY-44` — PROVED

Suppose a live orbit has upper radial Lyapunov exponent

\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}\log g_t\ge0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Fix `epsilon in (0,1)` and a deterministic bound `L<=L_0`, `L_0>1`. Set

\[
a=-\log(1-\varepsilon/2),
\qquad
\eta=a/2.
\]

Then along infinitely many arbitrarily long prefixes the mean log gain is at least `-eta`. For each such prefix, either some time has

\[
r_t^+\le\varepsilon,
\]

or `(8.7)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac1T\sum_{t<T}\|u_{t+1}-u_t\|
\ge
\delta_{\rm mov}(\varepsilon,L_0)
:=
\frac{\varepsilon a}{4L_0(\log L_0+a)}
>0.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus a noncontracting orbit must choose between two concrete mechanisms:

- **near-ray branch:** produce an expanding/neutral pseudoray, which Sections 4--7 price by directional large deviations / information; or
- **switching branch:** sustain macroscopic projective motion along infinitely many long high-growth blocks.

This is an exact deterministic dichotomy, not a probabilistic heuristic.

# 10. `P16-EVENTUAL-MASK-PERIODIC-CYCLE-44` — PROVED

This theorem converts a broad recurrent class into an exact finite cycle without any random-matrix approximation.

Let

\[
D_t=\operatorname{diag}(1_{\{(Wx_t)_i>0\}})
\]

be the activation mask of a live orbit. Suppose that after some time `t_0` the masks are periodic with period `d>=1`:

\[
D_{t+d}=D_t
\qquad(t\ge t_0).
\tag{10.1}
\]

Relabel one period `D_0,...,D_{d-1}` and put

\[
A_j=D_jW,
\qquad
C_0=I,
\qquad
C_j=A_{j-1}\cdots A_0,
\qquad
B=C_d=A_{d-1}\cdots A_0.
\tag{10.2}
\]

The phase-zero subsequence satisfies

\[
y_n:=x_{t_0+nd}=B^n y_0.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Every `y_n` realizes the same infinite periodic itinerary. Let

\[
\mathcal C
=
\overline{\operatorname{cone}\{y_n:n\ge0\}}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Because all orbit points obey the same linear sign inequalities at every phase, nonnegative linear combinations and limits obey them as weak inequalities. Hence for every `v in C`,

\[
F_W(C_jv)=C_{j+1}v\quad(0\le j<d-1),
\qquad
F_W(C_{d-1}v)=Bv.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Also

\[
B\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal C.
\tag{10.6}
\]

Let

\[
H=\operatorname{span}\mathcal C.
\]

The cone `C` is closed, convex, pointed (it lies in the nonnegative orthant), and generating in `H` by construction. The finite-dimensional positive operator `B|_H` therefore satisfies the cone Perron--Frobenius/Krein--Rutman theorem: if its spectral radius `r>0`, there exists

\[
v\in\mathcal C\setminus\{0\}
\]

such that

\[
Bv=rv.
\tag{10.7}
\]

Because `H` is the cyclic span of `y_0`,

\[
r
=
\rho(B|_H)
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|B^ny_0\|^{1/n}.
\tag{10.8}
\]

If the eventual tail has nonnegative radial Lyapunov exponent, bounded within-period factors imply

\[
r\ge1.
\tag{10.9}
\]

Define phase vectors

\[
v_j=C_jv.
\]

None is zero, since otherwise `Bv=0`, contradicting `r>=1`. Equations `(10.5)` and `(10.7)` give the exact tied ReLU cycle

\[
F_W(v_j)=v_{j+1}\quad(0\le j<d-1),
\qquad
F_W(v_{d-1})=rv_0.
\tag{10.10}
\]

Thus the projective period divides `d` and the product gain is `r>=1`.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Eventually periodic masks + nonnegative Lyapunov growth}
\Longrightarrow
\text{an exact noncontracting projective cycle.}
}
\tag{10.11}
\]

# 11. `P16-COMPACTNESS-APPROX-CYCLE-44` — PROVED

Exact finite-cycle exclusion is not enough unless nonperiodic recurrence can be robustly closed. The following deterministic lemma isolates the required robust object.

Let a live normalized orbit `u_t` have gains `g_t>0` and assume

\[
g_t\le L
\]

for all `t`. Fix `delta>0` and cover the nonnegative unit sphere by `M=M_N(delta)<infinity` sets of diameter at most `delta`. Let `c_t` denote the cell containing `u_t` and let

\[
a_t=\log g_t,
\qquad
S_T=\sum_{t<T}a_t.
\]

Fix `eta>0`. Suppose for some sufficiently large `T`,

\[
\frac{S_T}{T}\ge-\eta/2,
\tag{11.1}
\]

and

\[
T>
\frac{2M(\eta+\log_+L)}{\eta}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

Consider the finite directed walk

\[
c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_T.
\]

Erase repeated-cell loops successively. The edges decompose into directed cycles plus a residual simple path of length at most `M`. If every erased cycle segment had mean weight `<-eta`, then, since every residual edge has weight at most `log_+ L`,

\[
S_T
<
-\eta(T-M)+M\log_+L
=
-\eta T+M(\eta+\log_+L)
<-\eta T/2,
\]

contradicting `(11.1)`.

Therefore some pair `0<=s<t<=T` has the same cell and

\[
\|u_t-u_s\|\le\delta,
\qquad
\frac1{t-s}\sum_{j=s}^{t-1}\log g_j\ge-\eta.
\tag{11.3}
\]

By homogeneity,

\[
F_W^{t-s}(u_s)
=G_{s,t}u_t,
\qquad
G_{s,t}:=\prod_{j=s}^{t-1}g_j,
\]

so

\[
G_{s,t}^{1/(t-s)}\ge e^{-\eta}
\tag{11.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|F_W^{t-s}(u_s)-G_{s,t}u_s\|
\le
G_{s,t}\delta.
}
\tag{11.5}
\]

If the orbit has nonnegative upper Lyapunov exponent, then for **every** fixed `delta,eta>0` there are arbitrarily long prefixes satisfying `(11.1)`, hence at least one realized finite word satisfying `(11.3)`--`(11.5)`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\lambda^+_{\rm rad}\ge0
\Longrightarrow
\text{arbitrarily accurate realized approximate projective cycles
with mean gain arbitrarily close to one.}
}
\tag{11.6}
\]

This does not claim an exact periodic orbit. The period may be enormous, and the covering number is exponential in `N`. It gives the exact robust-closing obligation needed for a full theorem: rule out **realized approximate cycles uniformly over length**, not merely exact short cycles.

# 14. Failed routes and sharp obstruction after this run

## 14.1 Exact-selector information is still the wrong upper-bound object

The previous run proved a linear information **lower** bound for a fully adaptive pseudoray. Exact continuous selectors can nevertheless have linear or infinite mutual information. No generic upper bound from row-replacement `L^2` sensitivity exists. The new theorem therefore moves to a lossy `V`.

## 14.2 Generic sphere rate-distortion is far too expensive

The edge directional rate `0.04748...` is substantial but still much smaller than the per-coordinate entropy of a fine ambient sphere net. The required representation must exploit trajectory structure, masks, causal span, or another low-complexity object.

## 14.3 Fixed rays and short exact cycles are not enough

Section 11 proves that nonnegative Lyapunov growth forces **approximate** realized cycles, not necessarily exact cycles. A proof based only on exact algebraic cycles can miss non-atomic recurrence or arbitrarily long near-cycles.

## 14.4 Eventual periodic masks are no longer a surviving obstruction

Section 10 collapses eventual periodic mask tails to exact projective cycles. Therefore a genuinely new counterexample mechanism cannot simply be “the masks eventually repeat” unless it also evades the existing finite-cycle exclusions in an uncontrolled primitive period.

## 14.5 Sharp surviving obstruction

After this run the main obstruction can be stated as

\[
\boxed{
\text{aperiodic, high-complexity mask switching / renewed nonnormal recurrence}
}
\]

with the following forced structure:

- if it spends long high-growth intervals near one projective direction, the alignment theorem imposes an order-`N` information/probability debt;
- if it avoids such pseudorays, it must sustain positive average projective motion;
- if its masks become periodic, it creates an exact noncontracting projective cycle;
- if it remains nonperiodic with nonnegative Lyapunov exponent, compactness produces arbitrarily accurate realized approximate cycles.

The missing theorem is therefore a **uniform robust approximate-cycle gap or a dynamical rate-distortion theorem** for those aperiodic recurrent words.
