# 4. Fixed-source radial law and exact Cramer cost

Fix a deterministic unit vector `u` independent of `W`. Row rotational invariance gives

`F_W(u) = sqrt(alpha/N) Z_+`,

where `Z_1,...,Z_N` are iid standard real Gaussians and `Z_+` means coordinatewise positive part.

Write

`X_i=(Z_i)_+^2`.

Equivalently `X=B R^2`, where `B~Bernoulli(1/2)` and `R=|Z|` are independent. For `t<1/2`,

`M(t):=E exp(tX) = (1 + (1-2t)^(-1/2))/2.`   `(4.1)`

Fix a radial level `a>0` and define

`beta = alpha/a^2`.

The radial event

`G_a(u) := { ||F_W(u)|| >= a }`

is exactly

`(1/N) sum_i X_i >= 1/beta.`

The range relevant to near-neutral recurrence is

`1/2 <= beta <= 2`,   `(4.2)`

which is equivalent to `a<=sqrt(2alpha)` and `a>=sqrt(alpha/2)`. In particular, for each fixed `alpha<2`, a level `a` sufficiently close to `1` lies in this range.

For `beta<2`, let `z=z_beta in (0,1)` be the unique root

`beta = z^2 (1+z)`.   `(4.3)`

Set

`t_beta=(1-z^2)/2`.

Differentiating `(4.1)` shows

`M'(t_beta)/M(t_beta)=1/beta`,

so the exact Cramer rate is

`J(beta) = t_beta/beta - log M(t_beta)`

`        = (1-z)/(2z^2) - log((1+z)/(2z)).`   `(4.4)`

At `beta=2`, define `z=1`, `t_beta=0`, `J(2)=0` by continuity.

## `P16-RADIAL-CRAMER-49 — PROVED`

For every fixed `beta in [1/2,2]`,

`lim_(N->infinity) (1/N) log P(G_a(u)) = -J(beta).`   `(4.5)`

### Proof

The Chernoff upper bound with `(4.1)` gives the `limsup` immediately and optimizing in `t` gives `(4.4)`.

For the lower bound when `beta<2`, tilt the iid coordinates by

`dP_t/dP = exp(t_beta sum_i X_i) / M(t_beta)^N`.

Under `P_t`, the coordinates remain iid and have mean exactly `1/beta` and finite variance. For any fixed `h>0`, the central limit theorem gives a nonexponentially small probability that

`N/beta <= sum_i X_i <= N(1/beta+h)`.

On this interval the inverse likelihood ratio is at least

`M(t_beta)^N exp[-t_beta N(1/beta+h)]`.

Therefore

`liminf (1/N)log P(G_a) >= -J(beta)-t_beta h`.

Let `h downarrow 0`. At `beta=2`, the threshold equals the mean `EX=1/2`, so the central limit theorem directly gives a nonexponentially small upper-tail probability and exponent zero.

No external large-deviation theorem is needed for the displayed exponent.

# 5. Radial-conditioned prescribed-target alignment

Let `v>=0` be any deterministic unit vector and define

`b(v)=||v||_1/sqrt(N) <= 1`.

For the same fixed source `u`, put

`H_(u,v)(W)=v^T F_W(u)`.

Exactly,

`E H_(u,v) = sqrt(alpha/(2pi)) b(v) =: mu_alpha b(v).`   `(5.1)`

As a function of the underlying standard Gaussian matrix, `H_(u,v)` is `sqrt(alpha/N)`-Lipschitz. Gaussian concentration therefore gives

`P{H_(u,v)>=a}`

`<= exp{-N C(beta,b(v))}`,   `(5.2)`

where

`C(beta,b) = [1-sqrt(beta/(2pi)) b]_+^2/(2 beta).`   `(5.3)`

Combining `(4.5)` and `(5.2)` yields

`limsup (1/N) log P{ H_(u,v)>=a | G_a(u) }`

`<= -[ C(beta,b(v))-J(beta) ].`   `(5.4)`

The worst nonnegative target has `b(v)=1`. Define

`D(beta)=C(beta,1)-J(beta).`

The next lemma is the useful point.

## `P16-RADIAL-COND-ANGULAR-GAP-49 — PROVED`

For every `beta in [1/2,2]`,

`D(beta) >= c_edge`,   `(5.5)`

where

`c_edge=(1/4)(1-1/sqrt(pi))^2`

`      =0.047482679772069526...`.

Thus for every fixed `alpha,a` satisfying `(4.2)` and every deterministic nonnegative unit `u,v`,

`limsup (1/N)log P{v^T F_W(u)>=a | ||F_W(u)||>=a}`

`<= -c_edge.`   `(5.6)`

Targets with `b(v)<1` only improve the exponent.

### Proof of the uniform gap

Parameterize by `beta=z^2(1+z)`. Since `beta>=1/2`, one has `z>1/2`; at `beta=2`, `z=1`.

Expanding `(5.3)` at `b=1` gives

`C = 1/[2z^2(1+z)] - 1/[z sqrt(2pi(1+z))] + 1/(4pi).`

Subtract `(4.4)` and differentiate. A direct simplification gives

`dD/dz`

`= (3z+2) [-2 sqrt(pi) z sqrt(z+1) + sqrt(2)(z+1)]`

`  / [4 sqrt(pi) z^2 (z+1)^(5/2)].`   `(5.7)`

For `z>=1/2`, the bracket is negative because

`z+1 < 2 pi z^2`.

Indeed it holds strictly at `z=1/2` since `3/2 < pi/2`, and the right side minus the left side has derivative `4pi z-1>0` thereafter. Hence `D` decreases with `z`, so its minimum occurs at `z=1`, equivalently `beta=2`. There `J(2)=0` and

`C(2,1)=(1/4)(1-1/sqrt(pi))^2`.

This proves `(5.5)`.

### Interpretation

Near the critical edge, radial expansion itself becomes cheap: at `beta=2`, its exponential cost is zero. Yet **directional alignment with one prescribed nonnegative target retains a strictly positive order-N cost**. This separates radial Lyapunov growth from recurrent directional self-consistency.

# 6. Exact support tilt and radial-conditioned angular small balls

The previous theorem prices a linear alignment. We now price an actual ball around the normalized target direction.

On `G_a(u)` define

`V = F_W(u)/||F_W(u)|| = Z_+/||Z_+||.`

Let

`S={i:Z_i>0}`,   `K=|S|`.

## 6.1 Support under the radial exponential tilt

Under the tilt `P_t` from Section 4, one coordinate is positive with probability

`p_beta = 1/(1+z_beta).`   `(6.1)`

Indeed the negative half-line contributes mass `1/2`, while the positive half-line contributes `(2z)^(-1)` before normalization. For `beta in [1/2,2]`,

`p_beta >= 1/2.`

Let `rho<p_beta`. On the event `G_a`, the likelihood-ratio argument gives

`P{K<=rho N, G_a}`

`<= exp[-N J(beta)] P_t{K<=rho N}.`

Using `(4.5)` in the denominator and Bernoulli Chernoff under `P_t`,

`limsup (1/N)log P{K<=rho N | G_a}`

`<= -D_Ber(rho || p_beta).`   `(6.2)`

This is `P16-RADIAL-SUPPORT-TILT-49 — PROVED`.

It is a useful dead-neuron stress test: under the decoupled reference, conditioning on unusually large radial output does **not** create a sparse mask. It tilts the active probability upward from `1/2`. A recurrent bad event may still select sparse states, but then it pays a change-of-measure cost.

## 6.2 Exact conditional direction given the support

Condition on a support set `S` of size `s>=1`. The active magnitudes are iid half-normal. Their joint density in the positive orthant is radial:

`const * exp(-||r||^2/2).`

Therefore radius and direction are independent, and the direction is uniform on the positive orthant of the sphere in `R^S`.

The radial event `G_a` depends only on the active radius. Hence:

> Conditional on `S` and on `G_a`, the direction `V` is still exactly uniform on the positive orthant sphere `S_+^(s-1)`.

This is an exact finite-N statement.

## 6.3 Spherical cap bound

Fix an arbitrary deterministic ambient unit vector `vhat`. If `V` is supported on `S` and

`||V-vhat|| <= delta`,

then, writing `r=||P_S vhat||` and `q=P_S vhat/r` when `r>0`,

`<V,q> >= 1-delta^2/2 =: c_delta.`   `(6.3)`

The implication holds because `<V,vhat>=r<V,q>` and `r<=1`.

For a full uniform sphere in dimension `s`, the standard one-coordinate density gives the exponential cap estimate

`limsup_(s->infinity) (1/s) log P{<U,q> >= c_delta}`

`<= (1/2) log(1-c_delta^2)`

uniformly in the unit cap center `q`.

Conditioning on one specified orthant multiplies probability by at most `2^s`. Hence define

`r_cap(delta)`

`:= -log 2 - (1/2) log(delta^2-delta^4/4)`

` = log[1/(2 delta sqrt(1-delta^2/4))].`   `(6.4)`

Whenever `r_cap(delta)>0`, uniformly in `S` and `vhat`,

`P{||V-vhat||<=delta | S,G_a}`

`<= exp[-s r_cap(delta)+o(s)].`   `(6.5)`

## `P16-CONDITIONAL-TARGET-SMALLBALL-49 — PROVED`

For every fixed `beta in [1/2,2]`, every fixed `delta` with `r_cap(delta)>0`, every deterministic unit source `u`, and every deterministic ambient unit codeword `vhat`,

`limsup (1/N) log P{||V-vhat||<=delta | G_a(u)}`

`<= -R_beta(delta),`   `(6.6)`

where for every `rho in (0,p_beta)`,

`R_beta(delta) >= min{ D_Ber(rho||p_beta), rho r_cap(delta) }.`   `(6.7)`

In particular, because `p_beta>=1/2`, any fixed `rho<1/2` gives the uniform lower bound

`R_beta(delta) >= min{ D_Ber(rho||1/2), rho r_cap(delta) }.`   `(6.8)`

### Proof

Split according to `K<rho N` and `K>=rho N`.

The first branch is bounded by `(6.2)`. On the second branch, condition on the exact support. Then `(6.5)` gives at most

`exp[-rho N r_cap(delta)+o(N)]`.

Sum the two branches. The exponent is the minimum in `(6.7)`.

### Scope

This is a **fixed-source theorem under the radial-conditioned Gaussian reference law**. It is not yet a statement about an adaptive tied trajectory. Section 8 performs the exact adaptive change of measure; no adaptive query is treated as fresh Gaussian.

# 7. Conditional rate-distortion lower bound

Let `V` have the radial-conditioned fixed-source law from Section 6 and let `Vhat` be any randomized representation.

Suppose

`P{||V-Vhat||<=delta | G_a} >= 1-epsilon`,

with `0<epsilon<1/2`.

Let `P` denote the joint conditional law of `(V,Vhat)` and let

`Q=P_(V|G_a) tensor P_(Vhat|G_a)`.

By `(6.6)`, uniformly in a realized codeword,

`Q{||V-Vhat||<=delta}`

`<= exp[-N R_beta(delta)+o(N)].`

Data processing of relative entropy through this binary event gives

`I(V;Vhat | G_a)`

`>= (1-epsilon) N R_beta(delta) - h(epsilon) - o(N).`   `(7.1)`

Since `V` is a deterministic function of `W` for fixed `u`,

`I(W;Vhat | G_a) >= I(V;Vhat | G_a).`   `(7.2)`

If only expected Euclidean distortion is known,

`E[||V-Vhat|| | G_a] <= d`,

then Markov gives `(7.1)` with any `epsilon in (0,1/2)` and

`delta=d/epsilon`.   `(7.3)`

## `P16-RADIAL-TARGET-RD-49 — PROVED`

Equations `(7.1)--(7.3)` are the theorem.

### Explicit edge-budget certificate

Use the inherited universal one-transition edge distortion scale

`d_*=(1-1/sqrt(pi))/(8sqrt(2))`

`   =0.03852056259813935...`.

Choose the exact constants

`epsilon=3/8`,

`rho=4/25`.

Then

`delta=d_*/epsilon=(8/3)d_*`

`     =0.10272150026170494...`,

`r_cap(delta)=1.5839073592924389...`,

`D_Ber(4/25 || 1/2)=0.25347730115860234...`,

`(4/25) r_cap(delta)=0.2534251774867902...`.

Therefore the uniform conditional small-ball exponent is at least

`R_* = 0.2534251774867902...`,

and `(7.1)` yields

`liminf (1/N) I(W;Vhat | G_a)`

`>= (5/8) R_*`

` = 0.15839073592924388...`   `(7.4)`

whenever the expected conditional distortion is at most `d_*`.

This constant is deliberately not optimized.

For comparison, the preceding universal `b0=1` alignment debt at the edge is only

`c_edge=0.047482679772069526...`.

Even the typical radial-conditioned edge target has normalized `l1` near `1/sqrt(pi)`, whose zero-error fixed-pair alignment coefficient is

`(1/4)(1-1/pi)^2 = 0.11617535281868908...`,

still below the explicit generic-direction coding coefficient `(7.4)`. This comparison is only a benchmark; atypically smaller `l1` targets strengthen the alignment rate and must be treated jointly rather than replaced by the typical value.

# 8. Exact adaptive radial-baseline KL theorem

The preceding sections used a deterministic source. We now let the source and target representation depend arbitrarily on the same matrix and pay that dependence exactly.

Let `E in sigma(W)` be an event with `P(E)>0`. On `E`, let `U` be any possibly randomized unit-vector selector and assume

`||F_W(U)|| >= a`   `(8.1)`

almost surely. Let `Vhat` be any possibly randomized unit-vector representation. Define the actual normalized output direction

`V_out(W,U)=F_W(U)/||F_W(U)||`.

For a deterministic unit source `u`, rotational invariance makes

`p_a := P_W{||F_W(u)||>=a}`

independent of `u`.

Define the reference law

`Q(dW,du,dvhat)`

`= P_(U,Vhat | E)(du,dvhat) P_W(dW | G_a(u)).`   `(8.2)`

Let

`R=P_W tensor P_(U,Vhat|E)`.

Because `E` is `W`-measurable,

`D(P_(W,U,Vhat|E) || R)`

`= log(1/P(E)) + I(W;U,Vhat | E).`   `(8.3)`

Moreover, on the support of the conditional bad-event law `(8.1)` holds, while

`dQ/dR = 1_(G_a(U))/p_a`.

Therefore the exact identity is

`D(P_(W,U,Vhat|E) || Q)`

`= log(p_a/P(E)) + I(W;U,Vhat | E).`   `(8.4)`

Under `Q`, conditional on `(U=u,Vhat=vhat)`, the matrix has exactly the fixed-source radial-conditioned law of Section 6. Hence `(6.6)` applies uniformly.

## `P16-ADAPTIVE-RADIAL-BASELINE-KL-49 — PROVED`

If, on `E`,

`E[||V_out(W,U)-Vhat|| | E] <= d`,   `(8.5)`

then for every `epsilon in (0,1/2)` with `r_cap(d/epsilon)>0` and every `rho<1/2`,

`log(p_a/P(E)) + I(W;U,Vhat | E)`

`>= (1-epsilon) N`

`   * min{D_Ber(rho||1/2), rho r_cap(d/epsilon)}`

`   - h(epsilon) - o(N).`   `(8.6)`

At `d<=d_*`, choosing `epsilon=3/8`, `rho=4/25` gives

`log(p_a/P(E)) + I(W;U,Vhat | E)`

`>= 0.15839073592924388... N - o(N).`   `(8.7)`

### Proof

The KL identity is `(8.4)`. Under `Q`, condition on `(u,vhat)` and use the fixed-source small-ball theorem. Markov under the actual conditional law turns `(8.5)` into a binary event of probability at least `1-epsilon`. Data processing of `(8.4)` through that event gives `(8.6)` exactly as in Section 7.

No adaptive query is declared fresh. The radial conditioning is built into the reference measure itself.

### Recurrence-specific corollary

Suppose `Vhat` is not a code for the generic next state but an **old historical direction** selected from a recurrent witness, and the selected transition has gain at least `a` while its normalized output returns within distance `d` of that old direction. Then `(8.6)` says that recurrent directional matching carries an order-`N` debt **relative to the generic radial-gain probability `p_a`**.

The theorem still does not close P16 because the old source/target pair can itself contain order-`N` information about `W`. The next missing object is therefore an upper bound on the **incremental information of a recurrent historical target given the already revealed trajectory**, not a rate-distortion code for an ordinary new output direction.

# 9. Consequences for the global contraction program

## 9.1 Relation to high-product finite blocks

By the retained positive-cone block equivalence, failure of uniform exponential contraction means

`L_n^+(W)>=1`

for every `n`. The retained measurable-witness theorem produces canonical finite high-product blocks, and compactness produces near-neutral near-returns.

If a realized block has geometric mean gain `kappa>=a`, then at least one one-step gain is at least `a`. Thus the adaptive radial-baseline theorem can always be attached to a selected transition of a high-product block.

However, that by itself is not enough: Section 7 shows that merely describing the ordinary high-gain next direction can already cost substantially more information than the universal one-transition alignment theorem earns.

## 9.2 Closing-transition mass remains the recurrence bottleneck

The retained cyclic gain-balancing theorem places recurrence-specific information in the transition that closes back toward an old state. If its balancing weight is large, `(8.6)` offers a strong directional-matching debt relative to radial gain. If its balancing weight is tiny, severe gain imbalance can hide the recurrence constraint on a low-mass edge while nonclosing transitions carry most of the geometric-mean budget.

Therefore `P16-CLOSING-MASS-DICHOTOMY-49` remains genuinely open and is now sharper:

- **large closing mass:** exploit radial-conditioned small-ball / old-target matching;
- **small closing mass:** prove that several nonclosing transitions, the weighted Gram functional, or gain delocalization forces a larger multi-transition debt.

## 9.3 `l1` / support coupling

The alignment exponent improves as

`b(v)=||v||_1/sqrt(N)`

decreases. Section 6 simultaneously shows that, under the generic radial reference, very sparse supports are exponentially costly. These are two sides of the same tradeoff, but the present report does not derive the exact joint rate function for support, `l1`, radius and angular coding.

That is the next natural quantitative attack.

## `P16-NOGO-RADIAL-GENERIC-TARGET-49 — DISPROVED route`

Rejected proof substitution:

> Condition on one selected high-gain transition, encode its ordinary normalized ReLU output direction at the full inherited edge distortion budget, and use the universal `b0=1` one-transition alignment exponent to beat that code rate.

The conditional rate-distortion lower bound `(7.4)` shows the generic radial-conditioned target already costs at least

`0.15839... N`

nats at that distortion, while the universal edge alignment debt is only

`0.04748... N`.

This is a **method obstruction**, not a counterexample to the contraction conjecture. Conditioning on a rare recurrent event may produce atypical low-`l1`, low-support, or information-reusing targets whose recurrence-specific coding behaves differently.

# 12. Comparison of mechanisms

## Lyapunov / submultiplicativity

The exact deterministic reduction remains: one positive-cone finite block with norm below one is equivalent to uniform exponential contraction. Nonnegative maximal growth forces arbitrarily long high-product blocks and recurrent near-cycles.

## Random-matrix products

Independent-product Lyapunov exponents do not directly control the tied matrix. The present proof avoids importing them.

## Mean field

The scalar radial law for one fixed source is exact and gives the Cramer cost `J(beta)`. It is not extrapolated to an adaptive tied orbit.

## Concentration

Gaussian concentration prices prescribed-target alignment. Spherical geometry prices normalized output direction after radial conditioning. These are separate from radial norm concentration.

## Coupling / information

The new identity `(8.4)` is the key coupling statement: the adaptive bad-event law is compared with a source-by-source Gaussian law already conditioned on the required radial gain. Thus the KL debt measures **angular/recurrent excess beyond radial expansion**, plus information in the adaptive selector.

## Deterministic Lipschitz / monotonicity

ReLU positive homogeneity underlies the retained block equivalence and gain balancing. No unjustified global monotonicity of the tied dynamics is used.

## Bottom line

P16 is not solved. This run removes an important ambiguity from the information program. The expensive part of an ordinary high-gain ReLU transition is not only its radial norm: after conditioning on the radial event, its direction remains exponentially diffuse and has a large intrinsic rate-distortion cost. An exact adaptive change of measure preserves that statement for a matrix-dependent selected source without pretending the query is fresh. Therefore the remaining proof must exploit **recurrent reuse** — pointing back to already revealed states, closing-transition mass, joint `l1`/support geometry, or multi-transition structure — rather than trying to compress a generic high-gain next state below its natural information rate.
