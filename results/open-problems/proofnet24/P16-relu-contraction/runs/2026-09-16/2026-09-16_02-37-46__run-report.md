## Main advance of this run

This run closes one concrete adversarial-input obstruction **uniformly over all inputs**, without ordinary-start independence:

> **Expanding projective fixed rays are exponentially unlikely for every `alpha<2`.**

More generally, if

\[
q>\sqrt{\alpha/2},
\]

then with exponentially high probability there is **no** nonzero `x` and no `lambda>=q` satisfying

\[
F_W(x)=\lambda x.
\]

Thus the largest multiplier of any period-one ReLU eigenray is asymptotically at most the fresh mean-field radial factor `sqrt(alpha/2)`, up to any fixed positive slack. In particular, for `alpha<2`, noncontracting fixed rays `lambda>=1` are not the sharp obstruction to the global conjecture.

Let `G_s` be an `s x s` standard real Ginibre matrix and

\[
R_s(T)=\#\{\hbox{real eigenvalues of }G_s\hbox{ in }[T,\infty)\}.
\]

If `Z_q(W)` denotes the number of ReLU eigenrays with multiplier at least `q`, then the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
E Z_q(W)
=2^{-(N-1)}\sum_{s=1}^N {N\choose s}
E R_s\!\left(q\sqrt{N/\alpha}\right).
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

For any

\[
\frac12<\delta_0<\min\left\{1,\frac{q^2}{\alpha}\right\},

put

\[
r_0=\frac{q^2}{\alpha\delta_0}>1,
\qquad
I_0=\frac12(r_0-1-\log r_0),

\[
c_<(\alpha,q,\delta_0)
=\log\frac{2}{1+e^{-I_0}},

and

\[
c_>(\delta_0)
=D_{\rm Ber}(\delta_0\|1/2)

\]

Then there is an explicit finite constant `C=C(alpha,q,delta_0)` such that

\[
\boxed{
P\{\exists x\ne0,\ \exists\lambda\ge q:\ F_W(x)=\lambda x\}
\le
2C e^{-c_< N}+2N e^{-c_>N}.

\tag{0.2}

This is `P16-RELU-EIGENRAY-EXCLUSION-38 — PROVED`.

# 3. Period-one ReLU eigenrays: deterministic support reduction

Fix `q>0`. Define

\[
Z_q(W)
=\#\{\hbox{projective rays }[x]:x\ne0,\ F_W(x)=\lambda x\hbox{ for some }\lambda\ge q\}.
\]

Because `q>0`, any such eigenvector automatically satisfies `x>=0`: the left side `F_W(x)` is coordinatewise nonnegative and equals a positive scalar times `x`.

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(x)=\{i:x_i>0\},\qquad s=|S|.
\]

Then `x_S>0`, and the eigenray equation is equivalent to

\[
W_{SS}x_S=\lambda x_S,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
W_{S^c,S}x_S\le0
\quad\hbox{coordinatewise}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Indeed, on `S` the ReLU output is positive and therefore the preactivation must equal `lambda x_S`; off `S` the ReLU output is zero and therefore the preactivation must be nonpositive.

For a Gaussian matrix, simultaneously over the finitely many principal submatrices, with probability one:

- every principal submatrix has simple spectrum;
- every real eigenvector has no zero coordinate.

Both are complements of finite unions of nontrivial algebraic zero sets. Thus sign classes of real eigenvectors are well-defined almost surely.

This reduction is exact and does not assume the input is independent of `W`.

# 4. Exact support/sign first-moment identity

## `P16-EIGENRAY-FIRST-MOMENT-38` — PROVED

Let `G_s` be standard real Ginibre and

\[
T_N=q\sqrt{N/\alpha}.
\]

For every support `S` of size `s`,

\[
\boxed{
E Z_{q,S}(W)
=2^{-(N-1)}E R_s(T_N),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `Z_(q,S)` counts eigenrays with support exactly `S`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
E Z_q(W)
=2^{-(N-1)}\sum_{s=1}^N{N\choose s}E R_s(T_N).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Scale the principal block as

\[
W_{SS}=\sqrt{\alpha/N}\,G_s.
\]

Thus a multiplier `lambda>=q` corresponds to a real eigenvalue of `G_s` at least `T_N`.

For each sign vector `sigma in {+1,-1}^s`, let `D_sigma` be the diagonal sign matrix. Standard real Ginibre is invariant under diagonal sign conjugation:

\[
G_s\stackrel d= D_\sigma G_sD_\sigma.
\]

This conjugation leaves every eigenvalue unchanged and sends a right eigenvector `v` to `D_sigma v`. Modulo the unavoidable global sign, the group acts transitively on the `2^(s-1)` coordinate-sign classes.

Let `C_tau(G_s)` count real eigenvalues at least `T_N` whose right eigenvector belongs to sign class `tau`. Since the spectrum is simple and eigenvectors have no zero coordinates almost surely,

\[
\sum_{\tau}C_\tau(G_s)=R_s(T_N).
\]

Distributional sign-conjugation symmetry implies that all `E C_tau` are equal. Therefore the expected number whose eigenvector is sign-definite (all coordinates equal in sign, modulo global sign) is exactly

\[
2^{-(s-1)}E R_s(T_N).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Choose the positive orientation `v>0`. Conditional on the entire principal block and on this eigenvector, each outside row dot product

\[
w_{iS}^Tv,\qquad i\notin S,
\]

is an independent centered nondegenerate Gaussian. Hence

\[
P\{W_{S^c,S}v\le0\mid W_{SS},v\}=2^{-(N-s)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Multiplying `(4.3)` and `(4.4)` and using linearity of expectation gives

\[
2^{-(s-1)}2^{-(N-s)}=2^{-(N-1)},
\]

proving `(4.1)`. Summing over the `{N choose s}` supports proves `(4.2)`. QED.

### Interpretation

The exact sign cost is strikingly support-independent: every candidate real eigenvalue pays precisely `N-1` unbiased sign constraints in expectation—`s-1` to make its principal-block eigenvector positive, and `N-s` to make all outside preactivations nonpositive.

This identity is already uniform over adversarial inputs; no Fubini exceptional-set step is present.

# 5. Real-Ginibre right-tail lemma from the exact density

The only specialized external input in the new proof is the exact finite-dimensional real-eigenvalue density from:

A. Edelman, E. Kostlan and M. Shub, **How many eigenvalues of a random matrix are real?**, *J. Amer. Math. Soc.* 7 (1994), 247--267, DOI `10.1090/S0894-0347-1994-1231689-0`.

I checked the original paper directly. Corollary 4.2 states that the expected number of real eigenvalues in `[a,b]` is the integral of

\[
\rho_s(x)=
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\frac{\Gamma(s-1,x^2)}{\Gamma(s-1)}
+
\frac{|x|^{s-1}e^{-x^2/2}}{\Gamma(s/2)2^{s/2}}
\frac{\gamma((s-1)/2,x^2/2)}{\Gamma((s-1)/2)}
\tag{5.1}
\]

(for the usual interpretation at the smallest dimensions). The same paper proves the asymptotic real-eigenvalue count `~sqrt(2s/pi)` and normalized real-eigenvalue support on `[-1,1]`; only the exact density `(5.1)` is used here.

## `P16-REAL-GINIBRE-RIGHTTAIL-38` — PROVED

Fix `r_0>1` and define

\[
I_0=\frac12(r_0-1-\log r_0)>0.
\]

There is a finite explicit constant `C_0=C_0(r_0,T_{min})` such that whenever

\[
T^2\ge r_0s,
\qquad T\ge T_{min}>0,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
E R_s(T)\le C_0e^{-I_0s}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Split the density `(5.1)` as `rho_s=A_s+B_s`.

For the second term, the lower-incomplete-gamma ratio is at most one, so

\[
\int_T^\infty B_s(x)dx
\le
\frac12P\{\chi_s\ge T\}.
\]

The standard chi-square Chernoff bound gives, for `T^2/s>=r_0`,

\[
P\{\chi_s^2\ge T^2\}
\le
\exp\left[-\frac s2
\left(r_0-1-\log r_0\right)\right]
=e^{-I_0s}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the first term and `s>=2`, if `Y~Gamma(s-1,1)`, then

\[
\frac{\Gamma(s-1,x^2)}{\Gamma(s-1)}=P\{Y\ge x^2\}.
\]

For `0<theta<1`, Chernoff gives

\[
P\{Y\ge x^2\}\le e^{-\theta x^2}(1-\theta)^{-(s-1)}.
\]

Choose

\[
\theta=1-\frac{s-1}{T^2}.
\]

Since `T^2>=r_0s`, this satisfies

\[
\theta\ge\theta_0:=1-r_0^{-1}>0.
\]

Using

\[
\int_T^\infty e^{-\theta x^2}dx
\le\frac{e^{-\theta T^2}}{2\theta T},
\]

we obtain

\[
\int_T^\infty A_s(x)dx
\le
\frac{1}{2\theta_0T_{min}\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\left[-(s-1)\phi\left(\frac{T^2}{s-1}\right)\right],
\tag{5.4}
\]

where

\[
\phi(r)=r-1-\log r.
\]

Since `phi` is increasing on `(1,infinity)` and `T^2/(s-1)>r_0`, `(5.4)` is at most a constant times

\[
e^{-(s-1)\phi(r_0)}
\le e^{\phi(r_0)}e^{-I_0s},
\]

because `phi(r_0)=2I_0`. The case `s=1` is a single standard Gaussian eigenvalue and is absorbed by increasing the finite constant. Combining with `(5.3)` proves `(5.2)`. QED.

One explicit admissible choice is

\[
C_0=
\max\left\{
\frac{e^{2I_0}}{2(1-r_0^{-1})T_{min}\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac12,
\frac12e^{I_0}
\right\}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

No asymptotic circular-law theorem is needed.

# 6. Exponential exclusion of high-gain fixed rays

## `P16-RELU-EIGENRAY-EXCLUSION-38` — PROVED

Assume

\[
q>\sqrt{\alpha/2}.
\]

Choose any

\[
\frac12<\delta_0<\min\left\{1,\frac{q^2}{\alpha}\right\}.
\]

Set

\[
r_0=\frac{q^2}{\alpha\delta_0}>1,
\qquad
I_0=\frac12(r_0-1-\log r_0),
\]

\[
c_<=\log\frac{2}{1+e^{-I_0}},
\qquad
c_>=D_{\rm Ber}(\delta_0\|1/2).
\]

Then, with `C_0` from `(5.5)` using `T_min=q/sqrt(alpha)`,

\[
\boxed{
P\{Z_q(W)\ge1\}
\le
2C_0e^{-c_<N}+2Ne^{-c_>N}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

Start from the exact first-moment identity `(4.2)` and split supports at `s=delta_0N`.

For `s<=delta_0N`,

\[
T_N^2=\frac{q^2N}{\alpha}
\ge
\frac{q^2}{\alpha\delta_0}s
=r_0s,
\]

so `(5.2)` applies:

\[
E R_s(T_N)\le C_0e^{-I_0s}.
\]

Therefore

\[
2^{-(N-1)}
\sum_{s\le\delta_0N}{N\choose s}ER_s(T_N)
\le
2C_0\left(\frac{1+e^{-I_0}}2\right)^N
=2C_0e^{-c_<N}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For `s>delta_0N`, use only the deterministic bound

\[
R_s(T_N)\le s.
\]

If `B~Bin(N,1/2)`, then

\[
2^{-(N-1)}\sum_{s>\delta_0N}{N\choose s}s
=2E[B1_{\{B>\delta_0N\}}]
\le2NP\{B>\delta_0N\}.
\]

The binomial Chernoff bound yields

\[
P\{B>\delta_0N\}
\le e^{-ND_{\rm Ber}(\delta_0\|1/2)},
\]

which gives the second term in `(6.1)`. Finally Markov's inequality gives

\[
P\{Z_q\ge1\}\le EZ_q.
\]

QED.

### Corollary: asymptotic period-one multiplier bound

Define

\[
\rho_{\rm ray}(W)
=\sup\{\lambda>0:\exists x\ne0,\ F_W(x)=\lambda x\},
\]

with the supremum of the empty set interpreted as zero. Then for every fixed `eta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{\rho_{\rm ray}(W)>
\sqrt{\alpha/2}+\eta\right\}
\to0
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

exponentially in `N`.

This is an **every-input** high-probability statement. It does not use a deterministic or `W`-independent start.

# 7. Near-edge rate for excluding noncontracting fixed rays

## `P16-NEAREDGE-EIGENRAY-RATE-38` — PROVED

Set

\[
\alpha=2-\Delta,
\qquad q=1,
\qquad
\delta_0=\frac12+\frac{\Delta}{12}.
\]

The choice is admissible for every `0<Delta<2` because

\[
(2-\Delta)\left(\frac12+\frac{\Delta}{12}\right)
=1-\frac{\Delta}{3}-\frac{\Delta^2}{12}<1.
\]

Thus

\[
r_0
=\frac1{1-\Delta/3-\Delta^2/12}.
\]

Taylor expansion gives

\[
c_<
=\log\frac{2}{1+\exp[-(r_0-1-\log r_0)/2]}
=\frac{\Delta^2}{72}
+\frac{17\Delta^3}{1296}
+O(\Delta^4),
\tag{7.1}
\]

while

\[
c_>
=D_{\rm Ber}\left(\frac12+\frac{\Delta}{12}\middle\|\frac12\right)
=\frac{\Delta^2}{72}
+\frac{\Delta^4}{15552}
+O(\Delta^6).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore for fixed small `Delta>0`,

\[
P\{\exists x\ne0,\ F_W(x)=\lambda x,\ \lambda\ge1\}
\le
2C_\Delta e^{-c_<(\Delta)N}
+2Ne^{-c_>(\Delta)N}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The leading certified exponent is

\[
\boxed{\frac1{72}N(2-\alpha)^2.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The constant `C_Delta` in `(5.5)` grows only polynomially in `1/Delta` here (in fact `O(Delta^{-1})`). Hence if `Delta=Delta_N ->0` and

\[
N\Delta_N^2\gg \log N+\log(1/\Delta_N),
\]

then the probability in `(7.3)` tends to zero.

The choice `Delta/12` balances, to leading order, the small-support Ginibre right-tail exponent against the large-support binomial sign-entropy exponent. Indeed, if one writes `delta_0=1/2+theta Delta`, the two leading exponents are

\[
\frac{(4\theta-1)^2}{32}\Delta^2
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
2\theta^2\Delta^2,
\]

and equality occurs at `theta=1/12`, giving `1/72`.

Again, `1/72` is the optimized leading coefficient of this two-piece first-moment proof, not a claim of sharp large-deviation optimality.

# 8. What this proves, and what it does not

The result is stronger in quantifier than the ordinary-start fixed-horizon theorem in one very specific direction: it excludes **all** high-gain fixed rays simultaneously, including rays selected after seeing `W`.

It does **not** prove the full tied contraction conjecture. A positively homogeneous nonlinear map can have negative gain on every invariant ray and still sustain growth along a projective cycle or more complicated recurrent set. The earlier mask-cycle, block-pseudospectrum, numerical-range and Jordan-transient analyses remain relevant.

The new sharp obstruction is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{period }\ge2\text{ projective cycles / nonperiodic recurrence / renewed nonnormal transients},
}
\]

plus the quantitative problem of converting these mechanisms into an all-time Lyapunov upper bound.

A tempting but invalid inference is

`rho_ray(W)<1  =>  lambda_max(W)<0`.

This is false for general nonlinear homogeneous maps and is **not** used.

# 9. Route comparison after the new theorem

## Lyapunov exponents

The maximal radial Lyapunov exponent remains the exact global object. `P16-RELU-EIGENRAY-EXCLUSION-38` removes only invariant probability measures supported on one projective point with nonnegative gain. It does not remove invariant measures on longer cycles or non-atomic recurrent sets.

## Random-matrix products

The new proof does not replace the tied matrix by independent layers. The only random-matrix input is a principal submatrix of the same `W`; support conditioning is handled by exact sign symmetry and independent outside rows.

## Mean field

The threshold in `(6.3)` is `sqrt(alpha/2)`, exactly the fresh one-step RMS factor. This is a nontrivial agreement: even an adversarial fixed ray cannot asymptotically beat that threshold by a fixed amount without paying an exponential support/eigenvalue cost. This does **not** imply that an adaptive time-varying orbit obeys fresh mean field.

## Concentration / large deviations

The proof separates two mechanisms:

1. for support fraction below `delta_0`, a real Ginibre eigenvalue must lie beyond its natural circular edge, priced by the exact real-eigenvalue density;
2. for support fraction above `delta_0>1/2`, eigenvalue information can be discarded entirely, because sign-pattern entropy alone is exponentially unfavorable.

The near-edge `1/72` comes from balancing these two costs.

## Coupling / information

The preceding information-projection machinery remains the leading route for arbitrary time-varying adversarial witnesses. The new fixed-ray theorem is complementary: it obtains a direct every-input certificate for one structurally important class without lossy compression.

## Deterministic Lipschitz / monotonicity

No global one-step Lipschitz contraction is asserted. The map can have expanding directions that are not invariant rays. The result concerns projective fixed points, not the full operator norm or full positive cone.

# 10. Stress tests

## Dead neurons

If `F_W(x)=0`, the trajectory dies and is harmless. The eigenray theorem assumes `lambda>=q>0`; dead rays never enter the count.

## Exceptional directions

The direction may depend arbitrarily on `W`. The proof sums over all supports and all real eigenvectors of all principal blocks. This directly addresses the source's warning about `W`-dependent starts for the period-one mechanism.

## Correlations across layers / time

There are no fresh layers. The theorem makes no temporal independence assumption. Its limitation is structural—period one—not probabilistic resampling.

## Finite width

Equation `(6.1)` is a finite-`N` bound. The only asymptotic statement is the interpretation of its exponents and the edge expansion.

## Zero coordinates and repeated eigenvalues

For Gaussian principal submatrices, zero eigenvector coordinates and repeated eigenvalues occur with probability zero. The theorem is unaffected by redefining the count arbitrarily on this null event.

## Support size one

The EKS density formula is written naturally for `s>=2`; `s=1` is a single standard Gaussian eigenvalue and is handled separately in the finite constant. No dimension-one singularity is hidden.

## Weight law and activation dependence

The exact sign-conjugation and real-Ginibre-density proof uses iid **centered Gaussian** entries, variance `alpha/N`, zero bias and coordinatewise ReLU. The support/sign identity itself extends to iid continuous sign-symmetric entries insofar as sign-conjugation symmetry and outside-row symmetry persist, but the quantitative eigenvalue tail `(5.2)` is Gaussian-specific. No universality claim is made.

## Structured models

The user also asked about structured multilayer DNN models. This run does not replace the intended Gaussian tied model by a structured surrogate. **AUDIT_FOR:** P15  
**Source path:** `proofnet24/P15-kernel-mean-correction/runs/2026-09-16/2026-09-16_02-25-33__run-report.md`

**Claim ID:** `P15-BERNOULLI-REPLACEMENT-GENERATOR-35`  
**Verdict:** **PASS**.

P15 takes an independent product sample

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)
\]

and constructs `S^[p]` by independently replacing each coordinate by an iid copy with probability `p`; write `q=1-p`. For square-integrable Hilbert-valued maps `B,G`, let

\[
B-EB=\sum_{A\ne\emptyset}B_A,
\qquad
G-EG=\sum_{A\ne\emptyset}G_A
\]

be their Hoeffding decompositions and

\[
c_A=E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle.
\]

The key overlap identity is immediate from Hoeffding degeneracy:

\[
E\langle B_A(S),G_A(S^{[p]})\rangle=q^{|A|}c_A.
\tag{13.1}
\]

Indeed, the contribution survives only if **every** coordinate in `A` is retained; if any coordinate in `A` is independently refreshed, conditioning on the other coordinates kills the cross term by degeneracy.

Therefore the paired movement

\[
H_p(B,G)
=E\langle B_S-B_{S^{[p]}},\,G_S-G_{S^{[p]}}\rangle
\]

satisfies

\[
H_p(B,G)=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})c_A.
\tag{13.2}
\]

The previous P15 replacement tensor is

\[
C_r(B,G)=2^r\sum_A { |A|\choose r}c_A.
\tag{13.3}
\]

Substituting `(13.3)` into the claimed generating formula gives

\[
\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}p^rC_r
=2\sum_Ac_A
\sum_{r=1}^{|A|}(-1)^{r+1}{|A|\choose r}p^r.
\]

The inner sum is

\[
1-(1-p)^{|A|}=1-q^{|A|},
\]

so the expression is exactly `(13.2)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
H_p(B,G)
=\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}p^rC_r(B,G).
}
\]

Differentiating the polynomial at `p=0` also gives exactly

\[
H_p^{(r)}(0)
=(-1)^{r+1}r!2^{1-r}C_r(B,G).
\]

The self-energy formula follows by setting `B=G=F`, replacing `c_A` by `e_A(F)=E||F_A||^2`. Every factor of two and every sign checks.

Essential hypotheses: independent product coordinates; independent Bernoulli refresh decisions; fresh iid coordinate copies; square-integrability in a Hilbert space; the same refreshed sample used in both factors of the paired movement. No Gaussianity, kernel linearity, differentiability, or fixed representation is needed.
