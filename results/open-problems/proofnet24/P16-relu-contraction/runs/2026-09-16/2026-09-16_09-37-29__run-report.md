with the **same square matrix `W` reused forever**, zero bias, and coordinatewise ReLU, throughout

The strongest requested statement remains:

> for every fixed `alpha<2`, with probability tending to one as `N->infinity`, there exist random `C(W)<infinity` and `rho(W)<1` such that
> \[
> \|F_W^t(x)\|\le C(W)\rho(W)^t\|x\|
> \]
> for **every** input `x` and **every** `t>=0`.

Equivalently, the maximal radial Lyapunov exponent / subadditive uniform growth rate is negative. No counterexample to the strongest statement is found here.

This run makes three linked theorem-sized advances.

1. **A realized near-cycle can be aggregated into a one-step pseudoray, with one exact obstruction: signed ReLU cancellation.** For any live normalized segment, balance its step gains to a common geometric mean `kappa`; if `z` is the sum of the balanced phases, then
   \[
   F_W(z)=\kappa z+\kappa(y_k-y_0)-c,
   \]
   where `c>=0` is an exact rowwise cancellation vector. Its coordinates are
   \[
   c_i=\min\left\{\sum_t (w_i^Ty_t)_+,\ \sum_t(-w_i^Ty_t)_+\right\}.
   \]
   Thus a near-returning near-neutral cycle either yields an expanding/neutral one-step pseudoray or must pay substantial signed mask-switch cancellation.

2. **Below `alpha=1`, every bad recurrent orbit has a quantitative cancellation floor.** Combining the new identity with the inherited uniform pseudoray gap for `alpha<1` and the previous compactness closing lemma shows that a nonnegative-Lyapunov orbit must generate arbitrarily accurate near-neutral recurrent segments whose normalized cancellation is at least
   \[
   \gamma_\alpha-o(1),\qquad
   \gamma_\alpha=\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{2(1+8\sqrt\alpha)}>0.
   \]
   This is a new structural obstruction: below one, a bad orbit cannot survive merely by long approximate cycles with negligible ReLU cancellation.

3. **Whole recurrent paths have a Gram-effective-rank large-deviation / information cost.** For a deterministic nonnegative cyclic path `V=(v_0,...,v_{k-1})`, define
   \[
   A_V(W)=\frac1k\sum_t v_{t+1}^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wv_t)
   \]
   (cyclic indices), Gram matrix `K_{st}=<v_s,v_t>`, and
   \[
   \Gamma(V)=\frac1{k^2}\sum_{s,t}K_{st}K_{s+1,t+1}.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   P\{A_V\ge a\}
   \le
   \exp\left[-\frac{N}{2\alpha\Gamma(V)}[a-\mu_\alpha]_+^2\right],
   \qquad
   \mu_\alpha=\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}.
   \]
   With effective rank
   \[
   r_{\rm eff}(V)=\frac{k^2}{\sum_{s,t}K_{st}^2},
   \]
   one has `Gamma(V)<=1/r_eff(V)`, so the exponent is amplified by `r_eff(V)`. A fully adaptive lossy version is proved by the same exact KL decoupling used in the preceding one-ray theorem.

At `alpha=2`, the one-ray alignment rate is

\[

\]

A path with `r_eff>=15` has the corresponding ideal fixed-query exponent at least

# 4. `P16-BALANCED-SEGMENT-CANCELLATION-45` — PROVED

This is deterministic and holds for **every real matrix `W`**, not only Gaussian matrices.

Consider any live normalized segment of the tied ReLU dynamics

\[
u_t\ge0,\qquad \|u_t\|=1,
\qquad
F_W(u_t)=g_tu_{t+1},\qquad g_t>0,
\qquad 0\le t<k.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define its geometric mean gain

\[
\kappa=\left(\prod_{t=0}^{k-1}g_t\right)^{1/k}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Balance the phases by

\[
a_0=1,
\qquad
a_{t+1}=a_t\frac{g_t}{\kappa},
\qquad
y_t=a_tu_t.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because the product of the ratios in `(4.3)` is one,

\[
a_k=1,
\qquad
y_0=u_0,
\qquad y_k=u_k.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Positive homogeneity gives the equal-gain chain

\[
F_W(y_t)=\kappa y_{t+1}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now put

\[
z=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}y_t.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Coordinatewise ReLU is subadditive in the form

\[
\operatorname{ReLU}(a+b)
\le
\operatorname{ReLU}(a)+\operatorname{ReLU}(b),
\]

so define the nonnegative cancellation vector

\[
c
:=
\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}F_W(y_t)-F_W(z)
\ge0
\quad\text{coordinatewise}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Summing `(4.5)` gives

\[
\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}F_W(y_t)
=\kappa\sum_{t=1}^{k}y_t
=\kappa(z-y_0+y_k).
\]

Hence the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
F_W(z)
=\kappa z+\kappa(y_k-y_0)-c.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

No independence, probability, fixed period, or mask assumption appears in `(4.8)`.

## 4.1 Exact rowwise meaning of `c`

For row `i`, write

\[
b_{it}=w_i^Ty_t,
\qquad
P_i=\sum_t(b_{it})_+,
\qquad
N_i=\sum_t(-b_{it})_+.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_tb_{it}=P_i-N_i
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
c_i
=P_i-(P_i-N_i)_+
=\min(P_i,N_i).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Thus `c_i>0` exactly when the balanced phases produce both positive and negative preactivation mass in row `i`. It is a quantitative **signed mask-switch cancellation** statistic, not merely a Hamming count of mask changes.

If every row has one fixed weak sign across the whole segment, then `c=0`. In particular, the old same-mask/support-word compression mechanism is recovered as the zero-cancellation endpoint.

# 5. `P16-CYCLE-TO-PSEUDORAY-OR-CANCELLATION-45` — PROVED

Normalize the aggregate

\[
v=\frac z{\|z\|}.
\]

Because every `y_t>=0`, one has `z>=y_0` coordinatewise and hence

\[
\|z\|\ge\|y_0\|=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
\delta_{\rm ret}=\|u_k-u_0\|,
\qquad
\chi=\frac{\|c\|}{\|z\|},
\qquad
L=\|W\|.
\]

From `(4.8)` and `y_k-y_0=u_k-u_0`, positive homogeneity gives

\[
\boxed{
\|F_W(v)-\kappa v\|
\le
\kappa\delta_{\rm ret}+\chi
\le
L\delta_{\rm ret}+\chi.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For an exact projective cycle, `delta_ret=0`, and in fact

\[
\boxed{
\|F_W(v)-\kappa v\|=\chi.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Suppose only that

\[
\kappa\ge e^{-\eta}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Choose `q=max{1,kappa}`. If `kappa>=1`, `(5.2)` already uses an allowed expanding/neutral multiplier. If `kappa<1`, then `1-kappa<=1-e^{-eta}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{q\ge1}\|F_W(v)-qv\|
\le
L\delta_{\rm ret}+\chi+(1-e^{-\eta}).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the promised robust-closing interface. A near-returning segment with near-neutral geometric mean gain produces a one-step expanding/neutral pseudoray **unless** its normalized signed cancellation `chi` is large.

The restriction is not an easier model: `(5.5)` applies to an actual segment of the original tied dynamics. It merely exposes the exact residue that prevents cycle aggregation from becoming a one-ray theorem.

# 6. `P16-BADORBIT-CANCELLATION-FLOOR-BELOW1-45` — PROVED

The preceding run retained/proved, for every fixed `alpha<1`, a uniform expanding-pseudoray gap: with exponentially high probability,

\[
\inf_{\substack{x\ge0,\ \|x\|=1\\q\ge1}}
\|F_W(x)-qx\|
\ge
\gamma_\alpha,
\qquad
\gamma_\alpha
=
\frac{1-\sqrt\alpha}{2(1+8\sqrt\alpha)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The constant is deliberately nonoptimized. Intersect this event with any deterministic norm event `||W||<=L` (for example the source-certified `L=8sqrt(alpha)` event).

If a realized segment satisfies `(5.4)`, then combining `(5.5)` with `(6.1)` forces

\[
\boxed{
\chi
\ge
\gamma_\alpha
-L\delta_{\rm ret}
-(1-e^{-\eta}).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now use inherited `P16-COMPACTNESS-APPROX-CYCLE-44`: if a live orbit has nonnegative upper radial Lyapunov exponent, then for every fixed `delta,eta>0` it contains a finite segment with

\[
\delta_{\rm ret}\le\delta,
\qquad
\kappa\ge e^{-\eta}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore, on the high-probability event `(6.1)` plus `||W||<=L`, every nonnegative-Lyapunov orbit produces, for every sufficiently small `delta,eta`, a recurrent segment satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\chi
\ge
\gamma_\alpha-L\delta-(1-e^{-\eta}).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Choosing a sequence `delta_j,eta_j downarrow0` yields recurrent segments with

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_j\chi_j\ge\gamma_\alpha>0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Meaning and limitation

This is a real theorem-sized restriction on the unresolved interval `1/2<=alpha<1`: a bad orbit cannot be built from increasingly accurate near-neutral returns whose ReLU cancellation vanishes.

It does **not** prove contraction. The balancing coefficients `a_t` can fluctuate violently when individual gains alternate between very small and very large values, and `chi` is normalized by the aggregate `||z||`. Such gain imbalance is a form of renewed nonnormal transient behavior. Pricing `(6.5)` probabilistically, uniformly over a matrix-adaptive recurrent word, remains open.

# 7. `P16-FIXEDPATH-GRAM-ALIGNMENT-45` — PROVED

The previous fixed-query theorem priced one direction. Here the same Gaussian concentration mechanism prices an entire prescribed cyclic path and automatically accounts for correlations between its phases.

Let

\[
V=(v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}),
\qquad
v_t\ge0,
\qquad
\|v_t\|=1,
\]

be deterministic and independent of `W`, with cyclic convention `v_k=v_0`. Define

\[
A_V(W)
=
\frac1k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}
 v_{t+1}^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wv_t).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let

\[
K_{st}=\langle v_s,v_t\rangle
\tag{7.2}
\]

be the Gram matrix and set

\[
\Gamma(V)
=
\frac1{k^2}
\sum_{s,t=0}^{k-1}
K_{st}K_{s+1,t+1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

All cyclic indices are modulo `k`.

## 7.1 Exact mean

For fixed `v_t`, the coordinates of `Wv_t` are iid `N(0,alpha/N)`. Thus

\[
E\,v_{t+1}^T\operatorname{ReLU}(Wv_t)
=
\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi N}}\,\|v_{t+1}\|_1.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
E A_V
=\mu(V)
:=
\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi N}}
\frac1k\sum_t\|v_t\|_1
\le
\sqrt{\frac\alpha{2\pi}}
=: \mu_\alpha.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

## 7.2 Exact path-dependent Lipschitz coefficient

Write `W=sqrt(alpha/N) G`, with `G` iid standard Gaussian. For two matrices `G,G'`, the scalar ReLU secant on each coordinate can be represented by a diagonal matrix `D_t` with diagonal entries in `[0,1]` so that

\[
\operatorname{ReLU}(Gv_t)-\operatorname{ReLU}(G'v_t)
=D_t(G-G')v_t.
\]

Therefore the increment of `A_V` is the Frobenius inner product of `G-G'` with

\[
\frac1k\sum_t D_tv_{t+1}v_t^T,
\]

up to the outer factor `sqrt(alpha/N)`. Because every `v_t` is nonnegative,

\[
\langle D_sv_{s+1},D_tv_{t+1}\rangle
\le
\langle v_{s+1},v_{t+1}\rangle.
\]

Consequently

\[
\left\|
\frac1k\sum_tD_tv_{t+1}v_t^T
\right\|_F^2
\le
\Gamma(V).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus `A_V` is `sqrt(alpha Gamma(V)/N)`-Lipschitz as a function of the standard Gaussian matrix `G`. Gaussian concentration yields, for every `a`,

\[
\boxed{
P\{A_V\ge a\}
\le
\exp\left[
-\frac{N}{2\alpha\Gamma(V)}
[a-\mu(V)]_+^2
\right]
\le
\exp\left[
-\frac{N}{2\alpha\Gamma(V)}
[a-\mu_\alpha]_+^2
\right].
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Every constant in `(7.6)` is finite-width and explicit.

# 8. `P16-EFFECTIVE-RANK-ALIGNMENT-45` — PROVED

Define the Gram effective rank

\[
\boxed{
 r_{\rm eff}(V)
 =
 \frac{(\operatorname{tr}K)^2}{\|K\|_F^2}
 =
 \frac{k^2}{\sum_{s,t}K_{st}^2}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Since `K` is a Gram matrix of `k` unit vectors,

\[
1\le r_{\rm eff}(V)\le k.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz and cyclic relabeling give

\[
\sum_{s,t}K_{st}K_{s+1,t+1}
\le
\left(\sum_{s,t}K_{st}^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_{s,t}K_{s+1,t+1}^2\right)^{1/2}
=
\sum_{s,t}K_{st}^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma(V)\le\frac1{r_{\rm eff}(V)}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Substituting into `(7.6)` yields

\[
\boxed{
P\{A_V\ge a\}
\le
\exp\left[
-\frac{N r_{\rm eff}(V)}{2\alpha}
[a-\mu_\alpha]_+^2
\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is the exact path analogue of the preceding one-ray directional-alignment theorem.

### Interpretation

- If all phases are almost parallel, `r_eff` is close to one and `(8.4)` reduces to the one-ray scale.
- If the phases explore many nearly orthogonal directions, `r_eff` approaches `k`, and the same persistent high alignment is exponentially more expensive.
- Positive average projective motion by itself does **not** force large `r_eff`: a path may move repeatedly inside a low-dimensional recurrent set. This is a precise remaining geometric obligation, not something silently assumed.

# 9. `P16-NEARCYCLE-FIXEDQUERY-RATE-45` — PROVED

Take an actual normalized orbit segment `u_0,...,u_k`, but for the moment regard the tuple `u_0,...,u_{k-1}` as a **prescribed deterministic path** independent of `W`. Its cyclic path statistic closes the last phase back to `u_0`:

\[
A_{\rm cyc}
=\frac1k\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{k-2}u_{t+1}^TF_W(u_t)
+u_0^TF_W(u_{k-1})
\right].
\tag{9.1}
\]

Along a realized orbit,

\[
u_{t+1}^TF_W(u_t)=g_t
\quad(t<k-1),
\]

and

\[
u_0^TF_W(u_{k-1})
=g_{k-1}\langle u_0,u_k\rangle.
\]

For unit vectors,

\[
\langle u_0,u_k\rangle
=1-\frac12\|u_k-u_0\|^2.
\]

Therefore, if `delta_ret=||u_k-u_0||` and `||W||<=L`,

\[
A_{\rm cyc}
=
\frac1k\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}g_t
-
\frac{g_{k-1}}{2k}\delta_{\rm ret}^2
\ge
\kappa-
\frac{L}{2k}\delta_{\rm ret}^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Arithmetic mean dominates geometric mean. If `kappa>=e^{-eta}` then

\[
\boxed{
A_{\rm cyc}
\ge
 e^{-\eta}-\frac{L\delta_{\rm ret}^2}{2k}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Combining `(9.3)` with `(8.4)` gives the fixed-query cost of a prescribed near-returning high-growth path. This is **not** applied directly to a matrix-adaptive orbit; Section 10 pays that dependence correctly through KL.

# 10. `P16-LOSSY-PATH-GRAM-KL-45` — PROVED

\[

=

\tag{10.7}

\[

# 14. Failed routes and sharpened obstruction

## 14.1 Exact-cycle counting alone remains insufficient

A nonnegative-Lyapunov orbit need only produce arbitrarily accurate near-cycles, not an exact finite cycle. The new cancellation identity shows exactly why summing phases of an approximate cycle may fail to create a fixed ray: positive and negative preactivation contributions can cancel before ReLU.

## 14.2 Projective motion alone does not imply large path effective rank

The preceding pseudoray-or-motion theorem forces motion when near-rays are absent. But motion may recur in a low-dimensional set, leaving `r_eff` bounded. Therefore no claim of the form “positive average motion => path exponent multiplied by time length” is valid without an additional effective-dimension theorem.

## 14.3 Large effective rank is not free closure

For a deterministic path, large `r_eff` multiplies the large-deviation exponent. A matrix-adaptive orbit can encode substantial information about `W`; an ambient path/mask union bound can erase the gain. Section 10 pays adaptivity by `I(W;V|E)`, leaving an explicit upper-information obligation.

## 14.4 Small cancellation is useful; large cancellation is the new hard branch

Below `alpha=1`, a bad orbit must have `chi>=gamma_alpha-o(1)` on increasingly accurate recurrent segments. The theorem does not yet show that such cancellation is probabilistically expensive for a fully adaptive path. The row formula `(4.9)` suggests the correct bridge to the inherited adaptive-margin information theorem: large `c_i` requires both positive and negative preactivation mass in the same row across the recurrent word. But a rigorous time-correlated information bound is still missing.

## 14.5 Gain imbalance / renewed nonnormal transients

The balancing factors `a_t` can be highly nonuniform even when the geometric mean gain is near one. This is not a technical nuisance to suppress: it is a concrete manifestation of the previously identified renewed-nonnormal-transient obstruction. A successful proof must either control these factors or charge their fluctuation to a negative radial/information budget.

### Sharp surviving obstruction after this run

A bad orbit in the original tied model must now evade **both** of the following:

1. **pseudoray / high-effective-rank alignment cost:** if a recurrent path is well represented with low information and nontrivial effective rank, `(10.9)` gives an order-`N r_eff` debt;
2. **cycle aggregation:** if a near-neutral recurrent segment has small signed cancellation, `(5.5)` turns it into an expanding/neutral pseudoray.

Thus the unresolved mechanism is

\[
\boxed{
\text{matrix-adaptive, low-effective-rank recurrent switching with substantial signed cancellation}
\\
\text{and/or severe gain imbalance sufficient to renew nonnormal amplification.}
}
\]

This is a proof obstruction, not an exhibited Gaussian counterexample.
