# 0. Executive result

P16 remains **OPEN** for the original tied-weight model on

`1/2 ≤ α < 2`.

The model is exactly

`W_ij iid~ N(0, α/N),     F_W(x) = ReLU(Wx),     x_(t+1) = F_W(x_t),`

with **the same matrix W reused forever**.

The strongest target remains: for every fixed `α < 2`, with probability tending to one as `N -> infinity`, there exist random constants `C(W) < infinity` and `rho(W) < 1` such that

`||F_W^t(x)|| ≤ C(W) rho(W)^t ||x||`

for **every** input `x` and every `t ≥ 0`. Equivalently, the maximal radial Lyapunov exponent is negative. By positive homogeneity, if `W = sqrt(α) A`, then

`lambda_max(W) = lambda_max(A) + (1/2) log α`,

so the conjectural edge statement is

`P{ lambda_max(A_N) < -(1/2) log 2 + eps } -> 1`

for every `eps > 0`.

This run proves a new exact **information projection / residual-KL theorem** for a direction chosen after observing the same Gaussian matrix. It strictly strengthens the preceding mask-energy theorem and closes one precise part of the dynamic-margin obstruction.

For an arbitrary measurable unit selector `U = U(W,xi)`, with `xi` independent of `W`, define normalized row preactivations

`Z_i = sqrt(N/α) w_i^T U`.

Put

`a = (1/N) sum_i P{Z_i > 0}`

and

`s = (1/N) sum_i E (Z_i)_+^2 = E||ReLU(WU)||^2 / α`.

For `0<a<1` and `s>0`, set `v=s/a` and define the one-dimensional law `nu_(s,a)` with density

`2(1-a) phi(z)` on `z≤0`,

`(2a/sqrt(v)) phi(z/sqrt(v))` on `z>0`,

where `phi` is the standard Gaussian density. This law has exactly

`P_nu{Z>0}=a,       E_nu (Z_+)^2=s`.

Define

`R(s,a) = D_Ber(a || 1/2) + (1/2)[s-a-a log(s/a)].`

Let `P_Z` be the actual law of the normalized preactivation vector `Z=(Z_1,...,Z_N)`, and let `gamma` denote the one-dimensional standard Gaussian law. Then, in the extended-value sense,

`I(W;U) ≥ D(P_Z || gamma^N)`

and the latter has the exact Pythagorean decomposition

`D(P_Z || gamma^N)`

` = N R(s,a) + D(P_Z || nu_(s,a)^N).`                    (0.1)

Therefore

`I(W;U) ≥ N R(s,a) + D(P_Z || nu_(s,a)^N).`             (0.2)

Unlike the preceding theorem, `(0.2)` does **not** require `s≥a`; it is valid for every finite `s>0`. In the noncontraction problem `s≥1/α>1/2` anyway, so the relevant branch lies inside the upper-tail regime.

The new remainder is not an abstract slack. It measures exactly how far the actual same-matrix row-preactivation law is from the least-informative law having the observed gate fraction and positive energy. It also admits the exact row-shape/correlation split

`D(P_Z || nu^N)`

` = D(P_Z || tensor_i P_(Z_i)) + sum_i D(P_(Z_i) || nu).`       (0.3)

Thus low residual information forces both low aggregate row dependence and small average one-row shape mismatch.

For `s≥1/2`, let the inherited energy-only rate be

`J_+(s)=min_(0<a<1) R(s,a)`.

Its unique minimizer `q_*(s)` satisfies

`q_*^3 = s(1-q_*)^2`.

Then `(0.2)` becomes a three-debt decomposition:

`I(W;U) ≥ N J_+(s)`

`          + N[R(s,a)-J_+(s)]`

`          + D(P_Z || nu_(s,a)^N).`                              (0.4)

The three nonnegative terms are respectively:

1. radial-energy large-deviation debt;
2. gate-choice debt away from the information-optimal gate;
3. residual shape/correlation debt.

Near `α=2-Delta` at the noncontracting energy level `s=1/α`, the inherited expansion remains

So a bad adaptive direction can be information-cheap only if its gate, magnitude law, near-zero margins, and cross-row dependence all lie close to one explicit tilted half-Gaussian reference.

and define the reference small-margin probability

`p_(s,a)(t)`

`   + 2a[Phi(t sqrt(a/s))-1/2].`                                  (0.5)

The residual in `(0.2)` satisfies

`D(P_Z || nu_(s,a)^N) ≥ N D_Ber(b_t || p_(s,a)(t))`              (0.6)

for every `t>0`.

`B_E ≥ N R(s,a) + D(P_(Z|E) || nu_(s,a)^N).`                    (0.7)

The third theorem turns `(0.8)` into mask transfer. For any two unit vectors `U,V`, define their ReLU masks with the same `W`. Deterministically,

`d_H(M_W(U),M_W(V))/N`

`h_E ≤ A(s,a)t + sqrt(kappa_E/2) + eps_E^2/(α t^2).`             (0.10)

# 5. P16-GATEENERGY-IPROJ-37 — exact theorem and proof

Let the rows of `W` be `w_1,...,w_N`, with entries iid `N(0,α/N)`. Let `U=U(W,xi)` be any measurable unit vector, where auxiliary randomness `xi` is independent of `W`.

Set

`Z_i=sqrt(N/α) w_i^T U`.

Let `P` denote the actual joint law of `(W,U)`, and let

`Q=P_W tensor P_U`.

Under `Q`, the selector and matrix are independent. Conditional on any realized unit `U=u`, the variables `Z_i` are iid `N(0,1)`. Therefore the `Q`-law of `Z` is exactly `gamma^N`.

KL data processing for the measurable map `(W,U)->Z` gives

`I(W;U)=D(P||Q) ≥ D(P_Z||gamma^N).`                              (5.1)

This step is where the same-matrix adaptivity is paid. The proof never asserts that `Z` is Gaussian under the actual law `P`.

Define

`a=(1/N)sum_i P(Z_i>0)`,

`s=(1/N)sum_i E(Z_i)_+^2`,

and assume `0<a<1`, `0<s<infinity`.

Put `v=s/a`. Define `nu=nu_(s,a)` by

`dnu/dz = 2(1-a) phi(z),                    z≤0,`

`dnu/dz = (2a/sqrt(v)) phi(z/sqrt(v)),      z>0.`                 (5.2)

The density integrates to one. Its negative mass is `1-a`, its positive mass is `a`, and conditional on positivity its squared magnitude has mean `v`; hence `E_nu(Z_+)^2=s`.

Relative to standard Gaussian measure,

`log(dnu/dgamma)(z) = log(2(1-a))`, for `z≤0`,

and for `z>0`,

`log(dnu/dgamma)(z)`

` = log(2a) - (1/2)log v + (1/2)(1-1/v)z^2.`                    (5.3)

The logarithm is affine in the two sufficient statistics `1_{z>0}` and `z_+^2`. Therefore the aggregate constraints `a,s` determine its expectation under the actual `P_Z` exactly:

`(1/N) E_P log(dnu^N/dgamma^N)(Z)`

` = (1-a)log(2(1-a)) + a log(2a)`

`   -(a/2)log(s/a) +(s-a)/2`

` = R(s,a).`                                                     (5.4)

Since `nu` and `gamma` have everywhere positive mutually absolutely continuous densities, the KL chain identity gives, whenever the terms are finite,

`D(P_Z||gamma^N)`

` = D(P_Z||nu^N) + E_P log(dnu^N/dgamma^N)`

` = D(P_Z||nu^N) + N R(s,a).`                                   (5.5)

If the left side is infinite, `(5.5)` is interpreted by truncation/extended-value continuity; the moment term `(5.4)` is finite when `s<infinity`, and the desired inequality is immediate. Thus `(0.1)` and `(0.2)` hold in full generality stated above.

This proves `P16-GATEENERGY-IPROJ-37`.

## 5.1 Exact relation to the previous mask-entropy theorem

Data processing the residual term through the sign vector `M=(1_{Z_i>0})` gives

`D(P_Z||nu^N) ≥ D(P_M||Ber(a)^N).`                               (5.6)

Because the aggregate expected active fraction is `a`, the cross entropy against `Ber(a)^N` is exactly `N h(a)`. Hence

`D(P_M||Ber(a)^N)=N h(a)-H(M).`                                  (5.7)

Substituting `(5.7)` into `(0.2)` gives

`I(W;U) ≥ N R(s,a)+N h(a)-H(M)`

` = N log2-H(M)+(N/2)[s-a-a log(s/a)],`                          (5.8)

which is exactly the previous bound. Thus the old theorem is recovered as a data-processed corollary, while the new residual retains the magnitude and correlation information that the sign-only theorem discards.

## 5.2 Row-shape/correlation split

Let `P_i` be the actual marginal law of `Z_i`. The standard product-reference identity gives

`D(P_Z||nu^N)`

` = D(P_Z || tensor_i P_i) + sum_i D(P_i||nu).`                  (5.9)

The first term is total correlation/multi-information among the normalized rows; the second is the aggregate one-row shape mismatch. A near-minimal information selector must therefore be simultaneously close to the reference in both senses. This directly stress-tests the possibility that the selector obtains its gain solely through strong across-row correlations.

`B_E=log(1/p)+I(W;V|E).`

# 9. Representative-side deterministic mask transfer
