# 12. Convergence for generic matrices

Assume every coordinate submatrix of \(A\) has simple nonzero singular values.

This property holds almost surely for iid Gaussian \(A\), because every singular-value collision is contained in the zero set of a nonzero polynomial and there are only finitely many coordinate submatrices.

For each support pair

\[
(S,T),
\]

there are then only finitely many nonzero singular-vector pairs.

The positivity and off-support inequalities can only remove pairs.

Therefore the complete stationary set of the alternating best-response map is finite.

We already proved:

- every cluster point is stationary;
- successive increments tend to zero.

A sequence in a compact metric space whose increments tend to zero and whose cluster set is contained in a finite set must converge to one element of that set.

Indeed, if it had two distinct cluster points, then once the increments are smaller than one-third their separation, every passage from a neighborhood of one to a neighborhood of the other would pass through a compact region separated from the finite stationary set. Those crossing points would have another cluster point, contradiction.

Hence:

### P16-BR-CONV-06 — PROVED

For generic \(A\), every nondead alternating positive best-response trajectory converges to one sign-feasible positive singular-vector pair of one coordinate submatrix.

This turns the “best response may stall” alternative into an explicit finite-dimensional object.

# 14. Exact first moment of stationary pairs

Let \(A_{ij}\sim N(0,1/N)\).

For fixed supports

\[
|S|=s,
\qquad
|T|=t,
\]

condition on the submatrix

\[
A_{S,T}.
\]

Almost surely:

- its nonzero singular values are simple;
- the associated left/right singular vectors have no zero coordinates.

Fix one singular-vector pair.

## 14.1 Positivity inside the supports

The Gaussian law of \(A_{S,T}\) is invariant under independent sign flips of its rows and columns:

\[
A_{S,T}
\mapsto
D_SA_{S,T}D_T.
\]

For a singular pair \((u,v)\), the transformed pair is

\[
(D_Tu,D_Sv).
\]

The pair is defined up to simultaneous sign.

Out of the

\[
2^{s+t}
\]

row/column sign flips, exactly two put the pair into the positive orthants modulo that simultaneous sign.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P\{u>0,\ v>0\}
=
2^{1-s-t}
}
\tag{14.1}
\]

after sign symmetrization.

## 14.2 Off-support inequalities

Conditional on the submatrix and its positive singular vectors,

\[
A_{S^c,T}u
\]

has \(N-s\) independent centered Gaussian coordinates.

Hence

\[
P\{
A_{S^c,T}u<0
\}
=
2^{-(N-s)}.
\tag{14.2}
\]

Likewise,

\[
P\{
A_{S,T^c}^\top v<0
\}
=
2^{-(N-t)}.
\tag{14.3}
\]

These two off-support blocks are disjoint and independent of each other and of \(A_{S,T}\).

Multiplying (14.1)–(14.3),

\[
\boxed{
2^{1-s-t}
2^{-(N-s)}
2^{-(N-t)}
=
2^{1-2N}.
}
\tag{14.4}
\]

The striking point is that this factor is **independent of \(s,t\)**.

If

\[
R_{s,t}(c)
\]

counts singular values of \(A_{S,T}\) at least \(c\), linearity of expectation yields

\[
E[
\#\text{ stationary pairs on }(S,T)
\text{ with value}\ge c
]
=
2^{1-2N}
ER_{s,t}(c).
\]

Summing over support pairs proves

\[
\boxed{
E\mathcal N_N(c)
=
2^{1-2N}
\sum_{s,t}
\binom Ns\binom Nt
ER_{s,t}(c).
}
\]

Equivalently, for independent

\[
S,T\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2),
\]

\[
\boxed{
E\mathcal N_N(c)
=
2E R_{S,T}(c).
}
\]

This is `P16-CONE-COUNT-06`.
