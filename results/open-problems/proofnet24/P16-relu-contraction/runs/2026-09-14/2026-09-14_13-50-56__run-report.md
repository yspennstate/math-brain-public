# ProofNet24 P16 — ReLU contraction

**Run timestamp:** 2026-09-14 13:50:56 Asia/Jerusalem  
**Status:** OPEN for the strongest tied-weight all-input infinite-time theorem. This run proves a new explicit one-step *uniform obstruction* for W-dependent inputs, sharpens the proven global-cone bracket, gives an explicit expanding 2-cycle showing why ray exclusion cannot close the problem, and independently audits P15-STAB-02.

## 0. Executive result

The primary P16 model is the tied-weight ReLU iteration
\[
W_{ij}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N),\qquad
F_W(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx),\qquad
x_{t+1}=F_W(x_t),
\]
with the **same** matrix reused at every time.

The strongest desired statement remains:

> For every fixed \(\alpha<2\), with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\), every starting vector \(x_0\) has \(F_W^t(x_0)\to0\) exponentially fast, ideally with asymptotic radial exponent \(\frac12\log(\alpha/2)\), or at least with some deterministic \(\rho(\alpha)<1\).

This is still OPEN for \(1/2\le\alpha<2\), including the original \(\alpha=1\) case.

The previous P16 run proved a growing-horizon theorem for every deterministic/W-independent start: for \(T\) up to a small constant times \(N\), the per-step squared radial gain is uniformly \(\alpha/2+o(1)\), with exponentially high probability. It also inherited the older truly uniform infinite-time theorem for \(\alpha<1/2\), obtained from a one-step positive-cone operator bound.

The main new result here shows that the latter one-step strategy cannot be pushed anywhere near \(\alpha=1\):

\[
M_+(W):=\sup_{\substack{u\ge0\\ \|u\|=1}}\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wu)\|
\]
satisfies, for \(W=\sqrt{\alpha}\,A\) and \(A_{ij}\sim N(0,1/N)\),
\[
M_+(W)\ge
\sqrt{\alpha\,\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}-o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
where
\[
\Psi(m):=\mathbb E(Z+m)_+^2
=(1+m^2)\Phi(m)+m\phi(m).
\]
Numerically,
\[
\Psi(1/\sqrt2)=1.360070553093646\ldots,
\]
so
\[
\sqrt{\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}
=1.1662206279661007\ldots,
\qquad
\alpha_{\rm obs}:=\Psi(1/\sqrt2)^{-1}
=0.7352559745708619\ldots.
\]

Therefore, for every fixed
\[
\boxed{\alpha>0.73525597457\ldots}
\]
there is, with probability tending to one, a **W-dependent nonnegative unit input that expands on its very next step**. At \(\alpha=1\), the worst cone direction has one-step gain at least \(1.16622-o(1)\) with high probability, even though every fixed W-independent input has typical one-step gain \(2^{-1/2}+o(1)\).

Thus the statement
\[
\|F_W(x)\|\le \rho\|x\|\quad\forall x\ge0
\quad\text{for some }\rho<1
\]
is false w.h.p. throughout \(\alpha>0.73526\). The desired \(\alpha=1\) theorem, if true, must exploit **multi-step dynamics after transient expansion**, not a one-step cone contraction.

Combining the old Sudakov–Fernique upper bound with the new lower bound brackets the asymptotic one-step cone-contraction threshold:
\[
0.5\ \lesssim\ \alpha_{\rm cone}\ \lesssim\ 0.735256.
\]
This does **not** disprove eventual all-input collapse for \(\alpha<2\); a trajectory may expand initially and then decay forever.

A second new result is the explicit deterministic matrix
\[
W_0=\begin{pmatrix}-M&d\\ c&-M\end{pmatrix},
\qquad c,d>1,\quad M>\sqrt{cd}.
\]
For \(F(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_0x)\),
\[
F(e_1)=c e_2,\qquad F(e_2)=d e_1,
\]
so \(F^2(e_1)=cd\,e_1\) is expanding, while \(F\) has no nonzero positive ReLU eigenray at all. This makes the prior logical warning exact: **excluding expanding rays is not enough; expanding cycles are a genuine separate obstruction.**

### 1.3 Independent-layer / global-Lipschitz project

This is a different model:
\[
x_\ell=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_\ell x_{\ell-1}),
\qquad
(W_\ell)_{ij}\sim N(0,\alpha/n),
\]
with the \(W_\ell\)'s independent across layers.

The reviewed manuscript `2 - Global stability of deep Gaussian ReLU networks (reviewed v2, 6 Sep)(1).pdf` defines the global Euclidean Lipschitz constant \(K_{n,L}(\alpha)\). Its finite-width theorem is
\[
\mathbb P\{K_{n,L}(\alpha)>1\}
\le
\mathbb E K_{n,L}(\alpha)^{2\theta n}
\le
2e^{nB_L(\alpha,\theta)},
\]
where
\[
B_L(\alpha,\theta)
=
L H(1/L)+\log5+\theta\log4+L g_\alpha(\theta)
\]
for \(L\ge2\).

Its global threshold theorem states:

- for every \(\alpha<2\), there is \(L_0(\alpha)\) such that every fixed \(L\ge L_0(\alpha)\) has
  \[
  \mathbb P\{K_{n,L}(\alpha)>1\}\le2e^{-c_{\alpha,L}n};

  \alpha_L\ge

  \]

This is a true all-input *pairwise Lipschitz* result for independent layers. It does not transfer to tied weights, because the layer-by-layer sign gauge used in that proof is lost when the same \(W\) is reused.

## 2. Previous P16 theorem retained: growing horizon for a fixed input

The previous run proved the following and I found no new defect in its logic.

Let
\[
W_{ij}\sim N(0,\alpha/N),
\qquad x_{t+1}=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx_t),
\]
and let \(x_0\ne0\) be deterministic or independent of \(W\). There exist universal \(c,C>0\) such that if \(T<N\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\), and
\[
(T+1)\log(C/\varepsilon)
\le c\varepsilon^2N,
\]
then, with probability at least
\[
1-2\exp\{-c\varepsilon^2N+C(T+1)\log(C/\varepsilon)\}
-2e^{-cN},
\]
simultaneously for \(0\le t<T\),
\[
\alpha(1/2-\varepsilon)
\le
\frac{\|x_{t+1}\|^2}{\|x_t\|^2}
\le
\alpha(1/2+\varepsilon).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The proof uniformly controls
\[
Q_G(u)
=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N(G_i\cdot u)_+^2
\]
over the full coefficient sphere generated by at most \(T+1\) adaptive Gaussian innovations.

Consequences:

- if \(\alpha<2\), each fixed start contracts geometrically through \(c_\alpha N\) steps w.h.p.;
- if \(T_N\log N=o(N)\), one can choose \(\varepsilon_N\to0\) and retain the exact per-step radial rate \(\sqrt{\alpha/2}+o_{\mathbb P}(1)\);
- if \(\alpha>2\), every fixed start instead grows geometrically through a linear-in-\(N\) horizon w.h.p.

This is a **fixed/W-independent-input theorem**. It says nothing about a worst input chosen after observing \(W\).

## 3. Previous uniform theorem retained: \(\alpha<1/2\)

After one ReLU step, every nonzero state lies in the nonnegative cone. Define
\[
M_+(W)
=
\sup_{\substack{u\ge0\\\|u\|=1}}
\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wu)\|
=
\sup_{u,v\in S_+^{N-1}}v^\top Wu.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(W=\sqrt\alpha A\), \(A_{ij}\sim N(0,1/N)\), the Gaussian-process comparison from the previous run gives
\[
\mathbb E M_+(W)
\le
\sqrt{2\alpha}+o(1),
\]
and Gaussian concentration gives
\[
M_+(W)\le\sqrt{2\alpha}+o_{\mathbb P}(1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore if \(\alpha<1/2\), there is a deterministic \(\rho(\alpha)<1\) such that w.h.p.
\[
\|F_W(u)\|\le\rho(\alpha)\|u\|
\qquad\forall u\ge0.
\]
Every trajectory enters the cone after one step, hence all trajectories decay geometrically forever.

This is **radial contraction toward zero**. It should not be called a pairwise Lipschitz contraction: a bound on \(\|F(x)\|\) relative to zero does not by itself bound \(\|F(x)-F(y)\|\) by the same constant.

## 4. New theorem P16-CONE-LB — explicit W-dependent expanding directions

### Statement

Let
\[
A_{ij}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1/N),
\qquad
M_+(A)
=
\sup_{u\in S_+^{N-1}}\|(Au)_+\|.
\]
Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), and define
\[
\Psi(m)
=
\mathbb E(Z+m)_+^2
=
(1+m^2)\Phi(m)+m\phi(m).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}M_+(A)
\ge
\sqrt{\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}
=
1.1662206279661007\ldots
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
in probability. More explicitly, for every fixed \(\delta>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
M_+(A)
<
\sqrt{\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}-\delta
\right\}\to0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The proof below in fact gives exponential concentration for each fixed \(\delta\), although only convergence to one is needed for the obstruction.

Since \(M_+(\sqrt\alpha A)=\sqrt\alpha\,M_+(A)\),
\[
\boxed{
M_+(W)
\ge
\sqrt{\alpha\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}-o_{\mathbb P}(1).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently, if
\[
\alpha>
\alpha_{\rm obs}
:=
\frac1{\Psi(1/\sqrt2)}
=
0.7352559745708619\ldots,
\tag{4.5}
\]
then
\[
\mathbb P\{M_+(W)>1\}\to1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Put
\[
u_0=N^{-1/2}\mathbf 1.
\]
Define
\[
q_i
:=
\sqrt N\,(Au_0)_i
=
\sum_{j=1}^N A_{ij}.
\]
The \(q_i\)'s are iid \(N(0,1)\). The first ReLU output is
\[
(Au_0)_+
=
N^{-1/2}q_+.
\]
Let
\[
v
=
\frac{q_+}{\|q_+\|}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Then \(v\ge0\), \(\|v\|=1\), and
\[
\mu_N
:=
\frac{\|q_+\|}{\sqrt N}
=
\left[
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N(q_i)_+^2
\right]^{1/2}
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Now condition on the row sums \(q=(q_1,\dots,q_N)\). For each row \(A_i\), Gaussian regression gives
\[
A_i
=
\frac{q_i}{N}\mathbf1^\top+r_i,
\]
where, conditionally on \(q\),
\[
r_i\sim
N\!\left(
0,\,
\frac1N
\left(I-\frac1N\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\right)
\right),
\]
independently across rows.

Set
\[
z=A^\top v.
\]
Its conditional mean is
\[
\mathbb E[z\mid q]
=
\frac1N\left(\sum_i v_iq_i\right)\mathbf1
=
\frac{\|q_+\|}{N}\mathbf1.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Because \(\sum_i v_i^2=1\), its conditional covariance is
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(z\mid q)
=
\frac1N
\left(I-\frac1N\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\right).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Thus, on an extension with iid \(g_j\sim N(0,1)\) independent of \(q\),
\[
\sqrt N\,z_j
=
\mu_N+g_j-\bar g,
\qquad
\bar g=N^{-1}\sum_jg_j.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Now take the best nonnegative response to \(v\):
\[
u
=
\frac{z_+}{\|z_+\|}
\]
whenever \(z_+\ne0\). Since \(u,v\in S_+^{N-1}\),
\[
M_+(A)
\ge
v^\top Au
=
z^\top u
=
\|z_+\|.
\tag{4.12}
\]
By (4.11),
\[
\|z_+\|^2
=
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N
(\mu_N+g_j-\bar g)_+^2.
\tag{4.13}
\]

The law of large numbers, \(\mu_N\to1/\sqrt2\), and \(\bar g\to0\) imply
\[
\|z_+\|^2
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
\mathbb E(Z+1/\sqrt2)_+^2
=
\Psi(1/\sqrt2).
\tag{4.14}
\]
For completeness, the closed form follows by integrating the first two truncated Gaussian moments:
\[
\mathbb E(Z+m)_+^2
=
\mathbb E[(Z+m)^2\mathbf1_{\{Z>-m\}}]
=
(1+m^2)\Phi(m)+m\phi(m).
\]

This proves (4.2)–(4.4).

For a fixed quantitative tolerance, exponential concentration follows directly: the empirical average in (4.8) is an average of iid subexponential variables; \(\bar g\) is Gaussian; and for \(m\) in a compact interval the variables \((m+g)_+^2\) have uniformly bounded subexponential norms. Bernstein plus a fixed one-dimensional net in \(m\) controls (4.13). No external random-matrix theorem is needed.

### Interpretation

At \(\alpha=1\), every fixed W-independent direction satisfies
\[
\|F_W(x)\|/\|x\|
\approx 2^{-1/2}=0.7071
\]
at finite/growing horizons, yet w.h.p. there is a W-dependent cone direction with one-step gain at least
\[
1.16622-o(1).
\]
This is a concrete and substantial quantifier gap.

The theorem disproves any attempt to prove the \(\alpha=1\) target through a single uniform inequality
\[
\|F_W(u)\|\le\rho\|u\|
\quad\forall u\ge0,\ \rho<1.
\]
It does **not** disprove eventual exponential collapse after a transient.

## 6. New deterministic counterexample P16-CYCLE-CE — ray exclusion is not enough

The July source proves a strong no-expanding-ray theorem at \(\alpha=1\). The previous P16 report correctly warned that this alone cannot exclude cycles. Here is an exact two-dimensional witness.

Choose
\[
c,d>1,\qquad M>\sqrt{cd},
\]
and set
\[
W_0
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-M&d\\
c&-M
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then for \(F(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_0x)\),
\[
F(e_1)=c e_2,
\qquad
F(e_2)=d e_1,
\]
and therefore
\[
F^2(e_1)=cd\,e_1,
\qquad
F^{2k}(e_1)=(cd)^k e_1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
This is an exponentially expanding period-two projective orbit.

Yet \(F\) has no nonzero eigenray \(F(x)=\lambda x\) with \(\lambda>0\).

Indeed, neither axis is invariant: the axes are interchanged. If \(x_1,x_2>0\) and \(F(x)=\lambda x\) with \(\lambda>0\), both preactivation coordinates must be positive:
\[
d x_2>Mx_1,
\qquad
c x_1>Mx_2.
\]
Multiplying gives
\[
cd>M^2,
\]
contradicting \(M>\sqrt{cd}\).

For example, \(c=d=1.1\), \(M=2\) gives an expanding two-cycle of per-step geometric rate \(1.1\) and no positive eigenray.

This is not a random-Gaussian counterexample: the entries are order one, not \(N^{-1/2}\). Its purpose is logical. Any proof that uses only an expanding-ray count is incomplete unless it also controls periodic and nonperiodic invariant projective dynamics.

## 7. Expectation / high probability / almost sure / uniform quantifiers

The current P16 state can now be stated cleanly.

### Expectation

For every deterministic \(x\) independent of \(W\),
\[
\mathbb E\|F_W(x)\|^2
=
\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x\|^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]
This is only a one-step first moment and does not survive adaptive maximization over \(x=x(W)\).

### High probability, fixed input

The growing-horizon theorem (2.1) gives
\[
\|x_t\|
=
(\sqrt{\alpha/2}+o(1))^t\|x_0\|
\]
uniformly through \(T_N\log N=o(N)\) for W-independent \(x_0\), with the precise probability bound in Section 2.

### Almost sure across widths

If one fixes a sequence of deterministic starts and chooses parameters with summable width-\(N\) failure probabilities, Borel–Cantelli upgrades the growing-horizon conclusion to eventual almost sure validity **as \(N\to\infty\)**. This is not an infinite-time theorem at one fixed width.

### Uniform over all inputs, infinite time

Proved for
\[
\alpha<1/2
\]
through the cone bound (3.2).

Open for
\[
1/2\le\alpha<2.
\]

### Uniform one-step contraction

The new theorem proves it is impossible w.h.p. for
\[
\alpha>0.73525597457\ldots.
\]

### Uniform lower radial rate

Impossible for a more elementary reason: for an invertible \(W\),
\[
x=-W^{-1}\mathbf1
\]
satisfies \(Wx=-\mathbf1\), hence \(F_W(x)=0\). Thus no all-input two-sided radial law can hold. Dead directions are harmless for collapse but rule out uniform lower bounds.

## 8. Why the strongest target remains unresolved

### 8.1 The innovation budget ends

The exact fresh-Gaussian argument can generate at most \(N\) orthogonal probes. Once the trajectory has exposed all input directions there is no untouched Gaussian subspace. A proof based on endlessly multiplying independent-looking radial gains is therefore structurally impossible.

### 8.2 W-dependent exceptional directions already exist at one step

P16-CONE-LB shows that optimizing the input after observing \(W\) changes the radial behavior qualitatively. At \(\alpha=1\), fixed inputs contract at rate about \(0.707\), but some W-dependent cone direction expands by at least \(1.166\).

Thus a uniform theorem must prove that such exceptional directions are dynamically transient or cannot chain together for long times.

### 8.3 Ray exclusion does not control invariant measures

P16-CYCLE-CE shows this exactly. Even perfect exclusion of expanding fixed rays leaves periodic orbits. In the random problem, one must control the maximal invariant average
\[
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal I(T_W)}
\int
\log\|F_W(u)\|\,d\nu(u),
\tag{8.1}
\]
where
\[
T_W(u)
=
\frac{F_W(u)}{\|F_W(u)\|}
\]
on nondead directions.

Uniform exponential collapse is equivalent, modulo dead points, to strict negativity of the maximal long-run radial exponent
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}
\frac1t
\log
\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|F_W^t(x)\|.
\tag{8.2}
\]
The fixed-input state evolution controls neither supremum.

### 8.4 Tiny norm after \(cN\) steps is not enough

Positive homogeneity means absolute smallness carries no future safety margin:
\[
F_W^s(\varepsilon u)
=
\varepsilon F_W^s(u).
\]
Only future relative gains matter.

## 9. Route comparison

### Lyapunov / ergodic optimization

This is the conceptually exact infinite-time formulation: prove the maximal invariant mean log-gain is negative. The obstacle is not defining the Lyapunov exponent but controlling invariant measures generated by a W-dependent piecewise-projective map.

### Mean-field / state evolution

Excellent for fixed starts and now for growing horizons below the innovation budget. It cannot by itself handle the W-dependent all-input supremum.

### Concentration / reachable-class width

Still the most promising bridge. The previous report proposed proving that after a burn-in the **reachable coefficient class** has Gaussian width \(o(\sqrt N)\), rather than paying for the entire \(K\)-dimensional sphere. P16-CONE-LB makes this more urgent: the full cone contains genuine expanding W-dependent directions, so the class must exploit dynamical reachability, not just positivity.

### Deterministic one-step Lipschitz or monotonicity

Now rigorously blocked at \(\alpha=1\) by P16-CONE-LB. A one-step radial theorem cannot close the target.

### Periodic-orbit counting

Stronger than ray counting and worth exploring, but even exclusion of every finite period would not automatically exclude an aperiodic maximizing invariant measure without an additional closing/specification theorem. P16-CYCLE-CE shows at least period two must be addressed.

### Independent-layer interpolation

The independent-layer global-Lipschitz theorem provides the right critical number \(2\), but its proof uses layerwise sign-gauge freedom. Any interpolation from independent layers to tied weights must quantify the loss when the Gaussian matrices become correlated; simply identifying one-layer marginals is invalid.

\frac1{2\sqrt{n\lambda'}}.
\]
This gives the second term in (10.1). The same calculation with \(R-R'\) gives the third. Minkowski completes the proof.

No independence between the trained kernel/mean and sample is required; the inequality is deterministic after conditioning on the realized systems.
