# 5. Proof of P16-CONE-LB2

The proof is a finite-step Gaussian regression calculation. No asymptotic independence is guessed.

## 5.1 First positive response

Start with

\[
u_0=N^{-1/2}\mathbf1.
\]

Put

\[
a=Au_0,
\qquad
q_i=\sqrt N\,a_i.
\]

Then

\[
q_i\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1).
\]

Set

\[
s_N
=
\|a_+\|
=
\left[
\frac1N\sum_i(q_i)_+^2
\right]^{1/2},
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
v_0
=
\frac{a_+}{\|a_+\|}
=
\frac{q_+}{\sqrt N\,s_N}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

By the law of large numbers,

\[
s_N\to s=1/\sqrt2
\]

in probability.

Now define

\[
b=A^\top v_0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Conditional on \(a\), the vector \(v_0\) is deterministic. Exact Gaussian regression gives

\[
A
=
a u_0^\top
+
\widetilde A P_{u_0^\perp},
\tag{5.4}
\]

where \(\widetilde A\) is an independent copy of \(A\).

Hence, on an extension with iid \(g_j\sim N(0,1)\),

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt N\,b_j
=
s_N+g_j-\bar g,
\qquad
\bar g=N^{-1}\sum_jg_j.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let

\[
d_N=\|b_+\|,
\qquad
u_1=\frac{b_+}{d_N}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then \(u_1\ge0\), \(\|u_1\|=1\).

From (5.5), the law of large numbers gives

\[
d_N^2
=
\frac1N
\sum_j
(s_N+g_j-\bar g)_+^2
\to
\Psi(s)
=
d^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Also

\[
c_N
:=
u_0^\top u_1
=
\frac{
N^{-1}
\sum_j
(s_N+g_j-\bar g)_+
}{
d_N
}
\to
c
=
\frac{m_1(s)}d.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Finally,

\[
v_0^\top a=s_N,
\qquad
b^\top u_1=d_N.
\tag{5.9}
\]

The previous P16-CONE-LB theorem stopped here and used

\[
M_+(A)\ge v_0^\top Au_1=d_N\to d.
\]

We now perform one more response.

## 5.2 Exact two-sided Gaussian conditioning

Condition on both linear observations

\[
Au_0=a,
\qquad
A^\top v_0=b.
\]

They satisfy the compatibility identity

\[
v_0^\top a
=
u_0^\top b
=
s_N.
\]

For a Gaussian matrix, the exact conditional decomposition is

\[
\boxed{
A
=
a u_0^\top
+
v_0 b^\top
-
s_Nv_0u_0^\top
+
P_{v_0^\perp}\widehat A P_{u_0^\perp},
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

where \(\widehat A\) is an independent copy of \(A\), independent of the conditioned data.

This identity is the matrix Gaussian-regression formula for two compatible row/column linear observations.

Applying (5.10) to \(u_1\) gives

\[
A u_1
=
c_Na
+
(d_N-s_Nc_N)v_0
+
\xi_N,
\tag{5.11}
\]

where, conditionally,

\[
\xi_N
\sim
N\!\left(
0,
\frac{1-c_N^2}{N}P_{v_0^\perp}
\right).
\tag{5.12}
\]

Set

\[
\sigma_N=\sqrt{1-c_N^2},
\]

and

\[
\kappa_N
=
\frac{d_N-s_Nc_N}{s_N}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Since

\[
\sqrt N\,a_i=q_i,
\qquad
\sqrt N\,(v_0)_i=\frac{(q_i)_+}{s_N},
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt N\,(Au_1)_i
=
c_Nq_i
+
\kappa_N(q_i)_+
+
\sigma_N(P_{v_0^\perp}h)_i,
}
\tag{5.14}
\]

where \(h\sim N(0,I_N)\) is independent of all previously exposed variables.

---

## 5.3 Removing the rank-one projection in the innovation

Because

\[
P_{v_0^\perp}h
=
h-v_0(v_0^\top h),
\]

the difference between the scaled vectors in (5.14) with \(P_{v_0^\perp}h\) and with \(h\) is

\[
\sigma_Nv_0(v_0^\top h).
\]

Its Euclidean norm is

\[
\sigma_N|v_0^\top h|
=
O_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]

The actual ReLU norm is the scaled positive-part norm

\[
\|(Au_1)_+\|
=
\frac1{\sqrt N}
\left\|
\left(
\sqrt N\,Au_1
\right)_+
\right\|_2.
\]

Since coordinatewise ReLU is \(1\)-Lipschitz in Euclidean norm,

\[
\left|
\|(Au_1)_+\|
-
\frac1{\sqrt N}
\left\|
\left(
c_Nq+\kappa_Nq_++\sigma_Nh
\right)_+
\right\|_2
\right|
=
O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{5.15}
\]

Thus the left-projection correction is asymptotically negligible for this scalar norm.

## 5.4 Limit of the second response

We already have

\[
s_N\to s,
\qquad
d_N\to d,
\qquad
c_N\to c,
\qquad
\kappa_N\to\kappa,
\qquad
\sigma_N\to\sigma
\]

in probability.

The \(q_i\)'s are iid \(N(0,1)\), and the new \(h_i\)'s are iid \(N(0,1)\), independent of the previously exposed variables.

Therefore, by the law of large numbers plus Slutsky,

\[
\frac1N
\sum_{i=1}^N
\left(
c_Nq_i+\kappa_N(q_i)_+ +\sigma_Nh_i
\right)_+^2
\to
\mathbb E
\left[
cQ+\kappa Q_+ +\sigma H

C_2^2.

\[
\boxed{
\|(Au_1)_+\|
\to
C_2

\tag{5.17}

in probability.

Since \(u_1\ge0\) and \(\|u_1\|=1\),

\[
M_+(A)
\ge
\|(Au_1)_+\|.
\]

Therefore

\[
M_+(A)
\ge
C_2-o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]

which proves P16-CONE-LB2.
