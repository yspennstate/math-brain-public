## 0. Executive result

The primary P16 model is the tied-weight ReLU iteration
\[
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N),\qquad
F_W(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx),\qquad
x_{t+1}=F_W(x_t),
\]
with the **same** matrix reused at every time.

The strongest desired statement remains:

> For every fixed \(\alpha<2\), with probability tending to one as \(N\to\infty\), every starting vector \(x_0\) has \(F_W^t(x_0)\to0\) exponentially fast, ideally with asymptotic radial exponent \(\frac12\log(\alpha/2)\), or at least with some deterministic \(\rho(\alpha)<1\).

This is still OPEN for \(1/2\le\alpha<2\), including \(\alpha=1\).

The previous run proved two sharp obstructions to naive proofs:

1. the full nonnegative cone already contains \(W\)-dependent one-step expanding directions for every
   \[
   \alpha>0.7352559745708619\ldots;
   \]
2. deterministic ReLU maps can have expanding period-two projective cycles even when they have no expanding eigenray.

The new theorem below attacks the second obstruction in the **random Gaussian** model.

Let
\[
H(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)
\]
be the binary entropy in nats. For \(0<a,b\le1/2\) and \(\eta>0\), define
\[
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)
=
\sqrt{\alpha}\left[
\sqrt a+\sqrt b+
\sqrt{2(H(a)+H(b)+\eta)}
\right].
\tag{0.1}
\]

Then with probability at least
\[
1-(N+1)^2e^{-\eta N},
\]
simultaneously for **every** pair of coordinate supports
\[
|S|\le aN,\qquad |T|\le bN,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\|W_{T,S}\|_{\rm op}\le L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b).
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently, for every \(W\)-dependent state \(x\) with
\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(x),\qquad
T=\operatorname{supp}(F_W(x)),
\]
if \(|S|\le aN\) and \(|T|\le bN\), then
\[
\boxed{
\|F_W(x)\|
\le
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)\|x\|.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

This is a **uniform over inputs** statement, not a fixed-input state-evolution theorem.

In the symmetric case \(a=b=\delta\),
\[
L_{\alpha,\eta}(\delta,\delta)
=
\sqrt\alpha
\left[
2\sqrt\delta+
\sqrt{4H(\delta)+2\eta}
\right].
\tag{0.4}
\]

Define \(\delta_{\rm sp}(\alpha)\in(0,1/2)\) as the unique solution of
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt\alpha\left[
2\sqrt{\delta_{\rm sp}}
+
2\sqrt{H(\delta_{\rm sp})}
\right]
=1.
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Then for every fixed
\[
0<\delta<\delta_{\rm sp}(\alpha)
\]
there are deterministic \(\rho(\alpha,\delta)<1\) and \(c(\alpha,\delta)>0\) such that, with probability at least \(1-e^{-cN}\),

> every ReLU trajectory whose active support stays at size at most \(\delta N\) at every step contracts geometrically at every step:
> \[
> \|x_t\|\le \rho^t\|x_0\|.
> \]

Numerically,
\[
\delta_{\rm sp}(1)=0.024996441459399\ldots,
\]
so at the original \(\alpha=1\),

\[
\boxed{
\text{any noncontracting invariant dynamics must at some time activate more than about \(2.50\%\) of the neurons.}
}
\]

For comparison,
\[
\delta_{\rm sp}(1.5)=0.0155851960\ldots,
\qquad
\delta_{\rm sp}(2)=0.0111884479\ldots.
\]

This does **not** solve the main theorem: the typical fixed-input ReLU support is macroscopic, near \(N/2\), and the previous one-step expanding direction is also dense. But it removes an entire potential failure mechanism: a counterexample cannot hide in a permanently sparse/dead-neuron subsystem.

## 4. New theorem P16-SPARSE-TRANSITION

### 4.1 Statement

Let
\[
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N).
\]

Fix
\[
0<a,b\le1/2,\qquad \eta>0,
\]
and define
\[
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)
=
\sqrt{\alpha}
\left[
\sqrt a+\sqrt b+
\sqrt{2(H(a)+H(b)+\eta)}
\right].
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left\{
\sup_{\substack{|S|\le aN\\|T|\le bN}}
\|W_{T,S}\|_{\rm op}
>
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)
\right\}
\le
(N+1)^2e^{-\eta N}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently, on this event, for **every** \(x\in\mathbb R^N\), including \(x=x(W)\), if
\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(x),\qquad
T=\operatorname{supp}(F_W(x)),
\]
with
\[
|S|\le aN,\qquad
|T|\le bN,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|F_W(x)\|}{\|x\|}
\le
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No independence between \(x\) and \(W\) is needed.

### 4.2 Fixed-submatrix Gaussian norm estimate

Let \(G\) be a \(t\times s\) standard real Gaussian matrix.

The sharp classical Gaussian estimate is
\[
\mathbb E\|G\|_{\rm op}\le\sqrt s+\sqrt t
\]
and, because \(G\mapsto\|G\|_{\rm op}\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm,
\[
\Pr\left\{
\|G\|_{\rm op}\ge\sqrt s+\sqrt t+u
\right\}
\le e^{-u^2/2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For completeness, the expectation bound follows directly from Gaussian comparison. Index
\[
X_{p,q}=p^\top Gq
\]
by \(p\in S^{t-1},q\in S^{s-1}\), and compare it to
\[
Y_{p,q}=g^\top p+h^\top q
\]
with independent standard Gaussian \(g,h\). If
\[
r=p^\top p',\qquad s_0=q^\top q',
\]
then
\[
\mathbb E(X_{p,q}-X_{p',q'})^2
=
2-2rs_0,
\]
while
\[
\mathbb E(Y_{p,q}-Y_{p',q'})^2
=
4-2r-2s_0.
\]
Their difference is
\[
2(1-r)(1-s_0)\ge0.
\]
Sudakov--Fernique therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\sup X_{p,q}
\le
\mathbb E\|g\|+\mathbb E\|h\|
\le
\sqrt t+\sqrt s.
\]

The tail estimate is the standard Gaussian concentration inequality.

This exact Gaussian norm estimate is recorded in Davidson--Szarek,
*Local operator theory, random matrices and Banach spaces*,
Handbook of the Geometry of Banach Spaces 1 (2001),
DOI 10.1016/S1874-5849(01)80010-3.

### 4.3 Union bound over all sparse supports

For a fixed \(S,T\), with
\[
s=|S|,\qquad t=|T|,
\]
the block
\[
W_{T,S}
\]
has the same distribution as
\[
\sqrt{\alpha/N}\,G_{t\times s}.
\]

Therefore, by (4.3),
\[
\Pr\left\{
\|W_{T,S}\|
>
\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{N}}
(\sqrt s+\sqrt t+u)
\right\}
\le e^{-u^2/2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For \(a,b\le1/2\),
\[
\sum_{s\le aN}\binom Ns
\le
(N+1)e^{NH(a)},
\]
and likewise for \(b\).

Choose
\[
u
=
\sqrt{2N(H(a)+H(b)+\eta)}.
\]

Then a union bound over all ordered support pairs gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\{\exists S,T:\ &|S|\le aN,\ |T|\le bN,\\
&\|W_{T,S}\|>
\sqrt\alpha[
\sqrt a+\sqrt b+
\sqrt{2(H(a)+H(b)+\eta)}
]\}
\\
&\le
(N+1)^2
 e^{N(H(a)+H(b))}
 e^{-N(H(a)+H(b)+\eta)}\\
&=
(N+1)^2e^{-\eta N}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (4.1).

### 4.4 Transfer from block norm to ReLU gain

Let
\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(x),\qquad
T=\operatorname{supp}(F_W(x)).
\]

Because \(x\) vanishes off \(S\),
\[
(Wx)_T=W_{T,S}x_S.
\]
On \(T\) these coordinates are positive, so
\[
F_W(x)_T=(W_{T,S}x_S)_+,
\]
and by definition \(F_W(x)\) vanishes off \(T\).

Hence
\[
\|F_W(x)\|
=
\|(W_{T,S}x_S)_+\|
\le
\|W_{T,S}x_S\|
\le
\|W_{T,S}\|\,\|x\|.
\]

Combining with (4.1) proves (4.2).

The key point is that the support pair is allowed to be chosen **after seeing \(W\)**. The union bound already paid for all choices.

## 6. Corollary for period-two cycles

Suppose
\[
x\ge0,\qquad y=F_W(x),
\qquad
F_W(y)=\lambda x,
\qquad
\lambda>0.
\]

Let
\[
S=\operatorname{supp}(x),
\qquad
T=\operatorname{supp}(y).
\]

Then
\[
y_T=(W_{T,S}x_S)_+,
\qquad
\lambda x_S=(W_{S,T}y_T)_+.
\]

Therefore
\[
\|y\|
\le
\|W_{T,S}\|\|x\|,
\]
and
\[
\lambda\|x\|
\le
\|W_{S,T}\|\|y\|.
\]

Hence
\[
\boxed{
\lambda
\le
\|W_{S,T}\|\,\|W_{T,S}\|.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the uniform sparse-block event, if
\[
|S|\le aN,\qquad |T|\le bN,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\lambda
\le
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)^2.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus whenever
\[
L_{\alpha,\eta}(a,b)<1,
\]
no noncontracting projective two-cycle with those support-size bounds exists.

The same argument applies to any finite period: if every active support along the cycle is \(\delta N\)-sparse and \(L_{\alpha,\eta}(\delta)<1\), the product of one-step gains is \(<1\).

This removes sparse expanding cycles as a possible obstruction.

## 7. Interpretation: what obstruction remains?

### 7.1 Dead neurons are not enough to make a bad invariant set

A natural concern was that an exceptional orbit might progressively kill most neurons and then live in a small unstable subsystem.

P16-SPARSE-DYN rules this out quantitatively. In the Gaussian model, after paying the entropy of **all** sparse support choices, every sufficiently small active subnetwork is contractive.

Thus a bad invariant set, if it exists, must be dense enough that support entropy defeats the simple Gaussian submatrix norm estimate.

### 7.2 The previous one-step obstruction is dense

The P16-CONE-LB construction is compatible with the new theorem. Its optimized positive input has support fraction approaching
\[
\Phi(1/\sqrt2)\approx0.76025.
\]

Therefore the known \(W\)-dependent one-step expanding direction lies far outside the sparse regime.

### 7.3 This does not prove eventual sparsification

The theorem is conditional on support sizes. It does not show that a generic or worst-case tied trajectory becomes sparse.

For a fixed \(W\)-independent input, the first ReLU mask has density near \(1/2\), and state evolution predicts macroscopic masks at fixed times. Therefore the main \(\alpha=1\) theorem still requires control of **dense** reachable directions.
