# 3. Exact target, scaling, and probability levels

For fixed `W`, define

\[
K_t(W)
=
\sup_{x\ne0}
\frac{\|F_W^t(x)\|}{\|x\|}.
\]

Positive homogeneity gives submultiplicativity

\[
K_{s+t}(W)\le K_s(W)K_t(W).
\]

Hence

\[
\lambda_{\max}(W)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac1t\log K_t(W)
=
\inf_{t\ge1}\frac1t\log K_t(W).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Therefore:

- `lambda_max(W)<0` iff some finite block has `K_L(W)<1`;
- `lambda_max(W)>=0` implies `K_L(W)>=1` for every fixed `L`.

This is the exact deterministic finite-block form of the uniform problem.

The probability levels remain distinct:

1. **Expectation / annealed:** `E log gain`, `E gain^2`, or expected exceptional-set mass.
2. **High probability for one ordinary start:** source Theorem 7.1 and its growing-horizon descendants.
3. **Almost-sure in a matrix-sequence limit:** e.g. sharp rectangular Gaussian span limits retained from prior work.
4. **Uniform in input for one matrix:** `(0.1)`, the actual target.

The new mutual-information theorem first gives an annealed arbitrary-selector statement and then, through `(0.8)`, a **quenched bad-event reduction**. It still does not by itself provide the required compression of the bad selector.

# 5. Exact scalar Gaussian-ReLU transform

The scalar transform is elementary but the normalization is crucial.

Let

\[
X=(Z_+)^2,
\qquad Z\sim N(0,1).
\]

Then for `theta<1/2`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_+(\theta)
&=E e^{\theta X}\\
&=P\{Z\le0\}
+\int_0^\infty
\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\left[-\left(\frac12-\theta\right)z^2\right]dz\\
&=\frac12+
\frac1{2\sqrt{1-2\theta}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Put

\[
\Lambda(\theta)=\log M_+(\theta).
\]

Then

\[
\Lambda'(0)=EX=\frac12,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=E(Z_+)^4-\frac14
=\frac32-\frac14
=\frac54.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The upper-tail Legendre transform is `(0.4)`.

## `P16-RELU-RATE-EXPLICIT-35` — PROVED

For `s>=1/2`, let `r>=1` be the unique solution of

\[
\boxed{r^3=s(1+r).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then the optimizer satisfies

\[
\theta_s=\frac12(1-r^{-2}),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
J_+(s)
=
\frac{s}{2}(1-r^{-2})
-\log\frac{1+r}{2}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
r=(1-2\theta)^{-1/2}\ge1.
\]

From `(5.1)`,

\[
M_+(\theta)=\frac{1+r}{2},
\qquad
M_+'(\theta)=\frac{r^3}{2},
\]

so

\[
\Lambda'(\theta)=\frac{r^3}{1+r}.
\]

This is strictly increasing from `1/2` to infinity. Therefore the unique optimizer for `s>=1/2` obeys `(5.3)`. Substitution gives `(5.4)`.

No large-deviation theorem is needed for the identities; ordinary Chernoff optimization later uses this transform.

# 6. `P16-ADAPTIVE-MI-ENERGY-35` — arbitrary adaptive selector

## 6.1 Statement

Let

\[
W_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\alpha/N)
\]

and let `xi` be independent auxiliary randomness. Let

\[
U=U(W,\xi)\in S^{N-1}
\]

be any measurable unit vector. Then for every `0<=theta<1/2`,

\[
\boxed{
I(W;U)
\ge
N\left[
\theta\frac{E g_W(U)^2}{\alpha}
-\Lambda(\theta)
\right].
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Taking the supremum gives `(0.5)`.

## 6.2 Proof

Let `P` be the actual joint law of `(W,U)` and let

\[
Q=P_W\otimes P_U
\]

be the product of the same marginals.

Then

\[
D(P\|Q)=I(W;U).
\]

If `P` is not absolutely continuous with respect to `Q`, the mutual information is infinite and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise use the elementary entropy variational inequality

\[
D(P\|Q)
\ge E_P f-\log E_Q e^f.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For completeness, `(6.2)` follows by defining the tilted probability

\[
dQ_f=\frac{e^f}{E_Qe^f}\,dQ
\]

and writing

\[
D(P\|Q)
=D(P\|Q_f)+E_Pf-\log E_Qe^f
\ge E_Pf-\log E_Qe^f.
\]

Choose

\[
f(W,U)
=\frac{N\theta}{\alpha}
\|\operatorname{ReLU}(WU)\|^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Under `Q`, condition on `U=u`. Since `u` is then independent of `W`, the rows satisfy

\[
w_i^Tu\sim N(0,\alpha/N)
\]

independently. Hence

\[
\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wu)\|^2
=
\frac\alpha N
\sum_{i=1}^N (Z_i^+)^2
\]

with iid standard Gaussians `Z_i`, and therefore

\[
E_Q e^f
=M_+(\theta)^N.
\tag{6.4}
\]

On the actual joint law,

\[
E_Pf
=\frac{N\theta}{\alpha}E g_W(U)^2.
\]

Substitute these two identities into `(6.2)` to obtain `(6.1)`.

### Dependence discipline

The proof never says that `WU` is Gaussian under the actual law. It is generally not. Gaussianity is used only under the product reference law `Q`, and the exact KL cost of replacing the actual adaptive joint law by that product is `I(W;U)`.

That is the entire point of the theorem.

# 7. Log-gain, tail, and finite-description corollaries

## 7.1 `P16-ADAPTIVE-MI-LOGGAIN-35`

If

\[
E\log g_W(U)\ge\ell,
\]

then

\[
\log E g_W(U)^2
\ge E\log g_W(U)^2
=2E\log g_W(U)
\ge2\ell.
\]

Hence

\[
E g_W(U)^2\ge e^{2\ell}.
\]

Whenever `e^{2ell}/alpha>=1/2`, `(6.1)` yields

\[
\boxed{
I(W;U)
\ge
N J_+(e^{2\ell}/\alpha).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

At `ell=0` this is `(0.6)`.

## 7.2 `P16-ADAPTIVE-MI-TAIL-35` — probability form

Fix `q` with

\[
q^2/\alpha\ge1/2,
\]

and set

\[
p=P\{g_W(U)\ge q\}.
\]

Under the independent reference law `Q`, the fixed-direction Chernoff bound gives

\[
Q\{g_W(U)\ge q\}
\le
\exp[-N J_+(q^2/\alpha)].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Data processing of relative entropy to the indicator of this event gives

\[
I(W;U)
\ge
D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\|
\operatorname{Ber}(p_0)),
\]

where `p_0` is the `Q` probability. Since

\[
D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\|
\operatorname{Ber}(p_0))
\ge
p\log(1/p_0)-h_2(p),
\]

with binary entropy in nats,

\[
\boxed{
p\,N J_+(q^2/\alpha)
\le
I(W;U)+h_2(p)
\le I(W;U)+\log2.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus if

\[
I(W;U)=o(NJ_+(q^2/\alpha)),
\]

the probability that the adaptive selector attains gain at least `q` tends to zero.

This is weaker than a raw union bound for a known fixed finite codebook, but it applies to arbitrary randomized adaptive selectors with a directly controlled information budget.

## 7.3 Fixed codebook obstruction

If `U` itself takes values in a deterministic codebook of size at most `M`, then

\[
I(W;U)\le H(U)\le\log M.
\]

Therefore nonnegative expected log gain requires

\[
\boxed{
\log M
\ge
N J_+(1/\alpha).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Near the edge this becomes

\[
\log M
\ge
\left(\frac1{40}+o(1)\right)N\Delta^2.
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is not a theorem that a bad orbit needs a finite codebook. It is a lower bound on the size of any **fixed, matrix-independent discrete witness family** that hopes to support a selector with nonnegative average log gain.

Define

\[
\varepsilon_E
=
\left(E_E\|W(U-V)\|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

ReLU is 1-Lipschitz, so pointwise

\[
|g_W(U)-g_W(V)|
\le\|W(U-V)\|.
\]

Taking `L^2(P_E)` norms and using the reverse triangle inequality,

\[
\sqrt{E_E g_W(V)^2}
\ge
\sqrt{E_E g_W(U)^2}-\varepsilon_E.
\tag{8.3}
\]

By Jensen and `(8.1)`,

\[
\sqrt{E_E g_W(U)^2}\ge e^\ell.
\]

Hence

\[
E_Eg_W(V)^2
\ge
(e^\ell-\varepsilon_E)^2.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Now let `P` denote the conditional joint law of `(W,V)` and compare it to

\[
Q=P_W\otimes P_{V|E},
\]

where the `W` marginal in `Q` is the **original unconditioned Gaussian law**.

The KL cost splits exactly:

\[
\boxed{
D(P\|Q)
=
\log\frac1p
+I(W;V\mid E).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Indeed

\[
\frac{dP_{W|E}}{dP_W}
=\frac{1_E}{p},
\]

so the marginal conditioning costs `log(1/p)`, while the remaining joint-to-product cost is conditional mutual information.

Apply the same variational argument as Section 6 to `P,Q`. Under `Q`, `V` is independent of an **unconditioned Gaussian `W`**, so the exact MGF `(6.4)` is restored. Thus

\[
\log\frac1p+I(W;V|E)
\ge
N J_+\left(
\frac{E_Eg_W(V)^2}{\alpha}
\right).
\]

Use `(8.4)` and monotonicity of `J_+` above `1/2` to obtain `(0.8)`.

No hidden resampling of the dynamics occurs. The product `Q` is only a comparison law in a KL inequality.

## 8.2 Deterministic codebook corollary

If `V` belongs to a deterministic codebook `C_N` with

\[
|C_N|\le M,
\]

then

\[
I(W;V|E)\le H(V|E)\le\log M.
\]

Therefore `(0.9)` follows.

## 8.3 Why the `W`-output distortion is the right metric

An ordinary Euclidean approximation `||U-V||` is not enough unless one separately controls the relevant action of `W`. The proof naturally requires

\[
\|W(U-V)\|,
\]

which is exactly the metric in which ReLU outputs are stable.

In inherited causal-span regimes, the revealed-frame theorem can convert Euclidean error into this metric on the revealed span. Outside those regimes, an ambient `||W||` replacement would reintroduce the already rejected global operator-norm debt.

# 14. Stress tests and exact limitations

## 14.1 Dead neurons

If `F_W(U)=0` with positive probability, `log g=-infinity` there. Such a selector cannot satisfy a finite nonnegative expected log-gain hypothesis. Dead trajectories remain contractive and do not create a bad witness.

## 14.2 Exceptional directions

The theorem explicitly allows `U=U(W)`. It does not pretend exceptional directions are fresh. Their advantage is charged by KL/information.

## 14.3 Infinite mutual information

A deterministic continuous selector `U=f(W)` will often have

\[
I(W;U)=\infty
\]

because the joint law is singular relative to the product of its marginals. Therefore `(0.6)` by itself does **not** solve the worst-input problem.

This is why the lossy representation `V` and conditional theorem `(0.8)` are essential.

## 14.4 Random codebooks depending on `W`

The fixed-codebook corollary cannot be applied to a codebook whose representatives are themselves arbitrary functions of `W`. In that case the actual vector `V` can carry large or infinite mutual information even when its **label** uses only a few bits.

This explicitly blocks a circular use of same-matrix activation-cell representatives.

## 14.5 Correlations across layers / times

No time-step independence is assumed. The finite-block selector in Section 9 is extracted from an actual tied trajectory under one `W`.

## 14.6 Finite width

Equations `(6.1)`, `(7.3)`, `(8.8)`, and `(9.4)` are finite-`N` inequalities. The only asymptotic expansion is Section 10.

## 14.7 Nonnormal transients

The Jordan/pseudospectral counterexample from the previous run remains live. A nonnegative one-step selected gain or numerical range need not imply nonnegative asymptotic Lyapunov exponent. Section 9 avoids this mistake by starting from the actual finite-block consequence `K_L>=1` of `lambda_max>=0`.
