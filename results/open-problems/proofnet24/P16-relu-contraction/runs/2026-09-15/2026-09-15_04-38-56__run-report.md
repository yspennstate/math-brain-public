## Main new theorem: exact multi-start tied Gaussian innovations

### `P16-MULTISTART-INNOVATION-16` — PROVED

Let \(G\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}\) have iid \(N(0,1)\) entries and \(W=\sqrt{\alpha/N}\,G\).  Let \(\mathcal F_0\) be independent of \(G\).  Consider any finite adaptive query sequence \(v_1,\dots,v_R\) such that
\[
v_r\ \text{is }\mathcal F_{r-1}\text{-measurable},\qquad
\mathcal F_{r-1}:=\mathcal F_0\vee\sigma\{v_s,Gv_s:s<r\}.
\]
Set \(S_{r-1}=\operatorname{span}(v_1,\dots,v_{r-1})\),
\[
p_r=P_{S_{r-1}^\perp}v_r,\qquad \delta_r=\|p_r\|,
\]
and when \(\delta_r>0\), \(q_r=p_r/\delta_r\).

There are iid innovations
\[
z_1,z_2,\dots\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,I_N)
\]
introduced only at rank increases, with each new \(z_r\) independent of the entire past at its reveal time, such that
\[
\boxed{Gv_r=\sum_{s<r}\langle v_r,q_s\rangle z_s+\delta_r z_r}
\tag{I1}
\]
when \(\delta_r>0\).  If \(\delta_r=0\), the fresh term is absent:
\[
\boxed{Gv_r=\sum_{s<r}\langle v_r,q_s\rangle z_s.}
\tag{I2}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
Wv_r\mid\mathcal F_{r-1}\sim
N\!\left(\sqrt{\frac\alpha N}\sum_{s<r}\langle v_r,q_s\rangle z_s,\,
\frac{\alpha\delta_r^2}{N}I_N\right).}
\tag{I3}
\]
Thus the exact fresh-variance parameter is
\[
\boxed{\delta_r^2=\operatorname{dist}(v_r,S_{r-1})^2.}
\tag{I4}
\]

**Proof.**  Inductively, conditionally on the past, write
\[
G=\sum_{s<r}z_sq_s^\top+H_{r-1}P_{S_{r-1}^\perp},
\]
where \(H_{r-1}\) is an independent standard Gaussian matrix.  The new \(q_r\) is fixed conditional on the past, so \(z_r:=H_{r-1}q_r\sim N(0,I_N)\) and is independent of the past.  Rowwise Gaussian orthogonal decomposition makes the restriction of \(H_{r-1}\) to \(S_r^\perp\) independent of \(z_r\), closing the induction.  Linearity gives (I1).  If \(\delta_r=0\), the query lies in the old span, yielding (I2).  QED.

For \(k\) initial states independent of \(W\) and fixed depth \(L\), enumerate all queries \(v_{t,a}=x_t^{(a)}\), \(0\le t<L\), \(1\le a\le k\), in causal order.  The theorem orthogonalizes the **union** of all query histories.  It therefore gives an exact joint representation of all \(k\) tied ReLU trajectories.  No independent-layer substitution and no inter-trajectory independence is introduced.

### `P16-PREDICTABLE-CHOLESKY-16` — PROVED

Assume the first \(R\) adaptive queries are linearly independent.  Let
\[
K_R=(\langle v_r,v_s\rangle)_{r,s\le R}
\]
and let \(L_R\) be the lower-triangular Gram–Schmidt coefficient matrix,
\[
(L_R)_{rs}=
\begin{cases}
\langle v_r,q_s\rangle,&s<r,\\
\delta_r,&s=r,\\
0,&s>r.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{K_R=L_RL_R^\top.}
\tag{C1}
\]
If \(Z_R=[z_1|\cdots|z_R]\) and \(Y_R=[Gv_1|\cdots|Gv_R]\), then
\[
\boxed{Y_R=Z_RL_R^\top,\qquad
[Wv_1|\cdots|Wv_R]=\sqrt{\alpha/N}\,Z_RL_R^\top.}
\tag{C2}
\]
Here \(Z_R\) has iid \(N(0,1)\) entries, but \(L_R\) is adapted to and generally dependent on \(Z_R\).  The \(r\)-th coefficient row is measurable before \(z_r\) is revealed.  This is a predictable triangular Gaussian system, not an independent-layer product.

### `P16-GRAM-INNOVATION-RATIO-16` — PROVED

Let \(K_r\) be the Gram matrix of the first \(r\) queries and
\[
k_r=(\langle v_r,v_1\rangle,\dots,\langle v_r,v_{r-1}\rangle)^\top.
\]
Whenever \(K_{r-1}\succ0\),
\[
\boxed{\delta_r^2=\|v_r\|^2-k_r^\top K_{r-1}^{-1}k_r
=\frac{\det K_r}{\det K_{r-1}}.}
\tag{G1}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(Wv_r\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
=\frac{\alpha}{N}\frac{\det K_r}{\det K_{r-1}}\,I_N.}
\tag{G2}
\]
The exact tied-depth dependence parameter is therefore the Gram Schur complement of the new query against the old query span.

### `P16-FINITEPATH-IID-BASE-16` — PROVED

If the \(k\) initial starts are iid uniform on the sphere, write
\[
x_0^{(a)}=\frac{g_a}{\|g_a\|},\qquad g_a\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,I_N),
\]
independently of \(W\).  For fixed \(k,L\), the innovation construction can be realized with mutually independent Gaussian \(N\)-vectors
\[
g_1,\dots,g_k,z_1,\dots,z_R,\qquad R\le kL.
\]
Thus the coordinate tuples
\[
\eta_i=(g_{1i},\dots,g_{ki},z_{1i},\dots,z_{Ri})
\]
are iid \(N(0,I_{k+R})\).  The complete finite collection of tied ReLU paths is a measurable permutation-equivariant recursion driven by these iid base rows, with coefficients formed from empirical inner products and ReLU transforms.

So the remaining finite-depth LDP debt is not absence of an iid base.  It is the nonlinear self-consistency of the predictable Cholesky coefficients, especially near Gram-rank loss.

## Interpretation: innovation collapse is the exact tied-depth obstruction

For a ReLU query \(v_r=x_t^{(a)}\),
\[
Wv_r=
\sqrt{\frac\alpha N}
\left(
\underbrace{\sum_{s<r}\langle v_r,q_s\rangle z_s}_{\text{recycled tied-matrix information}}
+
\underbrace{\delta_r z_r}_{\text{fresh isotropic innovation}}
\right).
\]
Thus matrix reuse is not an amorphous dependence problem.  The fresh component is exactly controlled by
\[
\iota_r:=\frac{\delta_r}{\|v_r\|}.
\]

A useful exact dichotomy is:

- **high innovation:** \(\iota_r\ge\eta\).  There is a nondegenerate fresh isotropic Gaussian convolution, but one must still control the recycled mean;
- **low innovation:** \(\iota_r<\eta\).  The new state lies close to the span of old queried states, i.e. an approximate low-dimensional recurrence/compressibility event.

This is the first exact bridge between the July innovation/state-evolution approach and the later ray/cycle/projective-recurrence program.

If \(\delta_r=0\), there is no fresh randomness at all: the tied output is completely determined by previously revealed query outputs.  If \(\delta_r\ll\|v_r\|\), fresh variance is suppressed by \(\delta_r^2/\|v_r\|^2\).

The theorem requires the initial sigma-field to be independent of \(W\).  This is essential.  It applies to deterministic or \(W\)-independent random starts, including many starts jointly, but not to a maximizing direction chosen after observing \(W\).  A top singular vector is an immediate counterexample to any “fresh first query” statement for a \(W\)-dependent start.

### `P16-AUDIT-P13-GAUSS-LEVERAGE-16` — PASS

**Claim:** `P13-GAUSS-LEVERAGE-TAIL-16`

Let \(E\) be a Haar \(k\)-plane in \(\mathbb R^{m+k-1}\), \(k\log m=o(m)\), and \(0<\tau<1\).  P13 claims
\[
-\frac1m\log P\{\max_i(P_E)_{ii}>\tau\}\to-\frac12\log(1-\tau).
\tag{A1}
\]

For one coordinate,
\[
\ell_1=(P_E)_{11}\stackrel d=
\frac{\chi_k^2}{\chi_k^2+\chi_{m-1}^2}
\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k/2,(m-1)/2).
\]
Write \(a_m=k/2\), \(b_m=(m-1)/2\).  Stirling gives
\[
\log B(a_m,b_m)=O(k\log m+\log m)=o(m).
\]
For the numerator of the Beta tail, the upper bound
\[
\int_\tau^1x^{a_m-1}(1-x)^{b_m-1}dx
\le C_\tau\frac{(1-\tau)^{b_m}}{b_m}
\]
gives the correct upper exponential rate.  For the lower bound, integrate only over
\[
[\tau,\tau+1/b_m].
\]
The interval length costs \(o(m)\), the \(x^{a_m-1}\) factor costs \(O_\tau(k)=o(m)\), and
\[
(1-x)^{b_m-1}\ge(1-\tau-1/b_m)^{b_m-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
-\frac1m\log P\{\ell_1>\tau\}\to-\frac12\log(1-\tau).
\]
Finally,
\[
P\{\ell_1>\tau\}\le P\{\max_i\ell_i>\tau\}\le(m+k-1)P\{\ell_1>\tau\},
\]
and \(\log(m+k-1)=o(m)\).  The maximum has the same exponent.  **PASS.**  No independence among coordinate leverages is assumed.

## Bottom line

The main tied ReLU contraction conjecture remains unresolved, but the finite-depth dependence mechanism is now exact.

For any finite causal query family of one reused Gaussian matrix,
\[
\boxed{
Gv_r=\sum_{s<r}\langle v_r,q_s\rangle z_s+\delta_rz_r,
}
\]
with fresh iid \(z_r\sim N(0,I_N)\) and
\[
\boxed{

\[
\boxed{
\delta_r^2=\frac{\det K_r}{\det K_{r-1}}.
}
\]
So the sharp finite-depth obstruction is **innovation collapse**: either a trajectory keeps receiving quantitatively fresh isotropic Gaussian noise, or its queries become nearly trapped in their previous span.  This is the new exact bridge between the July finite-horizon state-evolution argument and the later Lyapunov/recurrent-orbit program.
