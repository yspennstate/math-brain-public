# 5. `P16-CYCLE-BALANCING-33` — exact gain balancing

Define the geometric mean gain

\[
G
=
\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}g_j\right)^{1/\ell}>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Choose \(s_0=1\) and recursively

\[
\boxed{
s_{j+1}=s_j\frac{g_j}{G}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because \(G^\ell=\prod_jg_j\),

\[
s_\ell=s_0,
\]

so the scaling is cyclic.

Set

\[
w_j=s_jv_j.
\]

Then

\[
A_jw_j
=s_jg_j\widehat v_{j+1}
=Gs_{j+1}\widehat v_{j+1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
A_jw_j-Gw_{j+1}
=
Gs_{j+1}(\widehat v_{j+1}-v_{j+1}).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every edge of the rescaled pseudoorbit has the **same target multiplier \(G\)**. No lower bound on the individual gains \(g_j\) is needed.

This balancing is important because an unbalanced product expansion contains products of ratios involving individual \(g_j\), which may be tiny even when their geometric mean is near one.

# 6. `P16-BLOCK-PSEUDOSPECTRUM-33` — length-free singular-value certificate

Let

\[
\mathcal H=(\mathbb R^N)^\ell
\]

with the Euclidean direct-sum norm. Define the cyclic block operator \(\mathcal B:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) by

\[
(\mathcal Bz)_{j+1}=A_jz_j
\]

with indices modulo \(\ell\). Explicitly,

\[
\mathcal B=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\cdots&0&A_{\ell-1}\\
A_0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&A_1&\ddots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A_{\ell-2}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
w=(w_0,\ldots,w_{\ell-1}).
\]

By `(5.3)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(\mathcal B-GI)w\|^2
&=
G^2\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
 s_{j+1}^2
\|\widehat v_{j+1}-v_{j+1}\|^2\\
&\le
G^2D^2\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}s_{j+1}^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Cyclically,

\[
\sum_js_{j+1}^2=\sum_js_j^2=\|w\|^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|(\mathcal B-GI)w\|}{\|w\|}
\le GD.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Taking the infimum over unit vectors gives

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\min}(\mathcal B-GI)\le GD.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\min}(G^{-1}\mathcal B-I)\le D.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Weighted RMS sharpening

The proof actually yields

\[
\frac{\|(\mathcal B-GI)w\|}{G\|w\|}
=
\left[
\frac{\sum_j s_{j+1}^2\varepsilon_j^2}
{\sum_j s_j^2}
\right]^{1/2},
\qquad
\varepsilon_j=\|\widehat v_{j+1}-v_{j+1}\|.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus a weighted RMS stitching error can replace the maximum \(D\). The maximum form is retained for a clean uniform theorem.

### Exact cycle case

If every stitching error is zero, then `(6.2)` is equality with zero residual:

\[
\mathcal Bw=Gw.
\]

So an exact projective mask cycle gives an exact block eigenvalue \(G\). The new theorem is the exact approximate-cycle analogue.

### Block-power relation

The \(\ell\)-th power of \(\mathcal B\) is block diagonal:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal B^\ell
=
\operatorname{diag}(P_0,\ldots,P_{\ell-1}),
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

where each \(P_j\) is the cyclic product of the \(A\)'s beginning at phase \(j\). Hence exact block spectral information is equivalent to exact spectral information for the repeated mask-cycle products. The approximate certificate `(6.3)` is therefore the correct pseudospectral relaxation of a true repeated mask cycle.

# 7. Why the block formulation is stronger than naive sequential shadowing

Write

\[
c_{k+1}=\prod_{j=0}^{k}g_j,
\qquad
P=A_{\ell-1}\cdots A_0,

and

\[
d_{k+1}=\widehat v_{k+1}-v_{k+1}.
\]

Iterating

\[

gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
Pv_0-c_\ell v_0
=
\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}

1\\\beta a/M

\tag{10.6}
\]

Both coordinates are strictly positive, so \(v_M\) is in the full-positive activation cell. Its normalized image is also strictly positive, and applying \(W_M\) to that image again gives strictly positive coordinates. Thus the pseudocycle remains in one genuine ReLU activation cell; no zero-coordinate or dead-neuron artifact is being used.

Let

\[
u_M=v_M/\|v_M\|,
\qquad
g_M=\|W_Mu_M\|.
\]

Exactly,

\[
\boxed{
g_M^2
=
\frac{1+\beta^2a^2/M^2}
{1+a^2/M^2}.
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

Hence

\[
1-g_M
=
\Theta(M^{-2}),
\qquad
\log g_M=-\Theta(M^{-2}).
\tag{10.8}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
(W_M-I)v_M
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\-a^2/M
\end{pmatrix},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\|(W_M-I)u_M\|
=
\frac{a^2}{M\sqrt{1+a^2/M^2}}
=\Theta(M^{-1}).
}
\tag{10.9}
\]

Normalizing `(10.6)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\|T_M(u_M)-u_M\|
=
\frac{a^2}{M}+O(M^{-2})
=\Theta(M^{-1}).
}
\tag{10.10}
\]

Thus the projective stitching error tends to zero while the gain defect is even smaller, quadratic in that error.

For the one-block cycle,

\[
\sigma_{\min}(W_M-g_MI)
\le
g_M\|T_M(u_M)-u_M\|
=O(M^{-1}),
\tag{10.11}
\]

although

\[
\operatorname{spec}(W_M)=\{\beta,\beta\}
\]

stays a fixed distance inside the unit circle.

+
\text{tiny mask-cycle stitching}

is **DISPROVED** without a nonnormality control.
