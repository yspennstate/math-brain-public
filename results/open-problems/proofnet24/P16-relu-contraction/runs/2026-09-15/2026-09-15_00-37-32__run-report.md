# 4. Proof of `P16-SPHERICAL-CAP-RATE-12`

By rotational invariance of surface measure, fix the cap center at the north pole.

Write

\[
r=2\sin(\theta/2),
\qquad
0<\theta<\pi/2.
\]

The chord cap is the geodesic cap of polar angle \(\theta\).

Its normalized surface measure is

\[
\mu_N(\theta)
=
\frac{
\int_0^\theta
\sin^{N-2}\phi\,d\phi
}{
\int_0^\pi
\sin^{N-2}\phi\,d\phi
}.
\]

For the numerator,

\[
\frac{d}{d\phi}
\sin^{N-1}\phi
=
(N-1)
\sin^{N-2}\phi
\cos\phi
\le
(N-1)
\sin^{N-2}\phi.
\]

Integrating from \(0\) to \(\theta\),

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\theta
\sin^{N-2}\phi\,d\phi
\ge
\frac{
\sin^{N-1}\theta
}{
N-1
}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The denominator is trivially at most \(\pi\).

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mu_N(\theta)
\ge
\frac{
\sin^{N-1}\theta
}{
\pi(N-1)
}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now

\[
\sin\theta
=
2\sin(\theta/2)\cos(\theta/2)
=
r\sqrt{1-r^2/4}
=
s(r).
\]

This proves `(0.3)`.

For the matching exponential upper bound at fixed \(r\), since \(\sin\phi\) is increasing on \([0,\theta]\),

\[
\int_0^\theta
\sin^{N-2}\phi\,d\phi
\le
\theta\sin^{N-2}\theta.
\]

Also the denominator has only a polynomial loss.

For example, on

\[
\left[
\frac\pi2-\frac1{\sqrt N},
\frac\pi2+\frac1{\sqrt N}
\right],

\[
\sin\phi
=
\cos(\phi-\pi/2)

Therefore

\[

C_r\sqrt N\,

The lower and upper bounds differ only by polynomial factors.

Thus

\[
