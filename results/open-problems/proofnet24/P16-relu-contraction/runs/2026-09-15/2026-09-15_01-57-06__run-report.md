# New theorem 3

## `P16-ONESTEP-MULTISTART-GRAM-13` — PROVED

Let

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_k\in S^{N-1}
\]

be arbitrary deterministic starts, and define their Gram matrix

\[
G_U
=
(\langle U_j,U_\ell\rangle)_{j,\ell\le k}.
\]

For the same tied Gaussian matrix \(W\), let \(w_i^\top\) denote row \(i\).

Conditional on the starts,

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\frac N\alpha}
\left(
w_i^\top U_1,\ldots,w_i^\top U_k
\right)
\stackrel{\rm iid\ in\ i}{\sim}
N(0,G_U).
}
\tag{M1}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
Z_i=(Z_{i1},\ldots,Z_{ik})
\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}
N(0,G_U),
\]

then jointly

\[
\boxed{
\left(
\|F_W(U_j)\|^2
\right)_{j=1}^k
\stackrel d=
\left(
\frac{\alpha}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^N
(Z_{ij})_+^2
\right)_{j=1}^k.
}
\tag{M2}
\]

Thus at one step the entire shared-matrix multi-start dependence is encoded exactly by the **start Gram matrix**.

For random iid uniform starts and fixed \(k\), \(G_U\to I_k\) in probability, but this cannot simply be substituted inside an **exponential-rate** calculation: atypically correlated starts themselves have exponentially small probabilities and can compete with the radial large-deviation cost.

Therefore a fixed-\(k\) multistart analysis must preserve a Gram-overlap variational problem. Treating different starts as though they used independent matrices is invalid even at depth one when exponential rates are the target.

# 4. Proof of `P16-ONESTEP-CRAMER-13`

Fix a deterministic unit \(u\).

Every row \(w_i^\top\) of \(W\) satisfies

\[
w_i^\top u
\sim
N(0,\alpha/N),
\]

independently over \(i\).

Thus

\[
\|F_W(u)\|^2
=
\frac{\alpha}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^N
(Z_i)_+^2.
\]

Put

\[
Y=(Z)_+^2.
\]

For \(\theta<1/2\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
E e^{\theta Y}
&=
P\{Z\le0\}
+
E[
e^{\theta Z^2}1_{\{Z>0\}}
]\\
&=
\frac12
+
\frac12
E e^{\theta Z^2}\\
&=
\frac12
\left[
1+(1-2\theta)^{-1/2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(C1)`.

The mean is

\[
EY=\frac12.
\]

For an upper-tail level

\[
y>1/2,
\]

the exponential rate is the Legendre transform

\[
I(y)
=
\sup_{0<\theta<1/2}
\left\{
\theta y-\log M_Y(\theta)
\right\}.
\]

This can be established directly by exponential Chernoff for the upper bound and standard exponential tilting for the matching lower bound; no special random-matrix theorem is involved.

Now write

\[
s=(1-2\theta)^{-1/2}>1.
\]

Then

\[
\theta=\frac12(1-s^{-2}),
\]

and

\[
\log M_Y(\theta)
=
\log\frac{1+s}{2}.
\]

Differentiating gives

\[
\frac{d}{d\theta}\log M_Y(\theta)
=
\frac{s^3}{1+s}.
\]

Hence the optimizer is characterized uniquely by

\[
y=\frac{s^3}{1+s}.
\]

The function on the right is strictly increasing for \(s>0\).

At the optimizer,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta y
&=
\frac12
\left(1-\frac1{s^2}\right)
\frac{s^3}{1+s}\\
&=
\frac{s(s^2-1)}{2s(1+s)}\\
&=
\frac{s(s-1)}2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
I(y)
=
\frac{s(s-1)}2
-
\log\frac{1+s}{2}.
\]

If

\[
y\le1/2,
\]

the law of large numbers implies that the upper-tail event does not have a positive exponential decay rate, so \(I_+(y)=0\).

This proves `(C4)`.

# 6. Exact one-step multi-start dependence

Take deterministic starts

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_k.
\]

For each row \(w_i\),

\[
\left(
w_i^\top U_1,\ldots,w_i^\top U_k
\right)
\]

is jointly Gaussian.

Its covariance is

\[
E[
(w_i^\top U_j)
(w_i^\top U_\ell)

=
\frac{\alpha}{N}
\langle U_j,U_\ell\rangle.
\]

Different rows of \(W\) are independent.

Therefore `(M1)` follows.

Applying ReLU coordinatewise and summing squares gives `(M2)`.

This is the exact one-step starting point for `P16-MULTISTART-LD-13`.

### Fixed-\(k\) warning

For iid random sphere starts and fixed \(k\),

\[
G_U\to I_k
\]

in probability.

That does **not** justify replacing \(G_U\) by \(I_k\) inside an exponential large-deviation calculation.

Already for \(k=2\), the overlap

\[
\rho=\langle U_1,U_2\rangle
\]

has density proportional to

\[
(1-\rho^2)^{(N-3)/2}.
\]

Thus a fixed nonzero overlap carries its own \(e^{-cN}\) cost.

The conditional radial event under that overlap is a bivariate Gaussian empirical large-deviation problem.

The correct two-start exponential rate must therefore optimize a sum of:

1. start-overlap entropy;
2. conditional shared-row radial deviation cost.

This is a concrete reason why “many random starts behave like independent matrices” is wrong at the rate level.
