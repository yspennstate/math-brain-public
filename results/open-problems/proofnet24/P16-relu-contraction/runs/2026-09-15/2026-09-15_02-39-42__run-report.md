# 6. Proof of `P16-FIXEDK-GRAM-LDP-14`

Take

\[
U_1,\ldots,U_k
\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}
\operatorname{Unif}(S^{N-1}).
\]

Proceed sequentially.

After \(U_1,\ldots,U_{j-1}\) are exposed, choose an orthonormal frame whose first \(j-1\) vectors span their span.

Let

\[
v_j\in\mathbb R^{j-1}
\]

be the first \(j-1\) coordinates of \(U_j\) in that frame.

Because \(U_j\) is independent and spherical, conditionally on all preceding starts,

\[
v_j
\]

has density on the unit ball

\[
\boxed{
c_{N,j}
(1-\|v\|^2)^{(N-j-1)/2}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The conditional density does not depend on the preceding realized frame.

Thus the \(v_j\)'s are independent in these adapted coordinates.

The normalizing constants satisfy

\[
\log c_{N,j}=O(\log N)
\]

for fixed \(j\).

The Gram–Schmidt/Cholesky relation gives

\[
\boxed{
\det G_N
=
\prod_{j=2}^k
(1-\|v_j\|^2).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The map from

\[
(v_2,\ldots,v_k)
\]

to the positive-definite correlation matrix \(G_N\) is a fixed-dimensional triangular smooth change of coordinates on the interior.

Its Jacobian contributes only \(O(1)\) to the log density at speed \(N\).

Therefore the joint density has exponential part

\[
\exp\left\{
\frac N2
\sum_{j=2}^k
\log(1-\|v_j\|^2)
+
o(N)
\right\}.
\]

Using `(6.2)`,

\[
\frac1N\log f_N(G)
=
\frac12\log\det G
+
o(1)
\]

locally uniformly on compact subsets of

\[
\mathcal C_k^\circ.
\]

Hadamard's inequality gives

\[
\det G\le1,
\]

with equality only at

\[
I_k.
\]

As \(G\) approaches singularity,

\[
-\log\det G\to+\infty.
\]

Elementary finite-dimensional Laplace upper and lower bounds therefore give the LDP

\[
\boxed{
J_k(G)=-\frac12\log\det G.
}
\]

QED.

# 7. Proof of the fixed-\(k\) mixture rate

\[

\[
\theta\ge0,

-N
\left(

\right)
\right].

\[
\boxed{
P_G\{\text{all bad}\}

e^{-N\Psi_G(y)}.

\tag{7.1}

On compact subsets of the positive-definite correlation matrices,

\[
G\mapsto\Psi_G(y)

is continuous.

\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac1N
\log
P\{\text{all bad}\}
\le
-
\inf_G
[J_k(G)+\Psi_G(y)].

The singular boundary costs infinite Gram entropy and does not contribute.

## Lower bound

Choose

\[
x^{(\varepsilon)}
\in(y,\infty)^k

whose Cramér cost is within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\Psi_{G_0}(y)\).

Under that tilted law the empirical mean lies in a small box inside

\[
(y,\infty)^k

with probability tending to one.

Thus

\[
P_G\{\text{all bad}\}
\ge
\exp[
-N(\Psi_{G_0}(y)+2\varepsilon)

\]

for

\[
G\in\mathcal U
\]

\[
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\frac1N
\log
P\{\text{all bad}\}
\ge
-
J_k(G_0)

\Psi_{G_0}(y)

Now optimize \(G_0\) and send

\[
\varepsilon\downarrow0.

This proves

\[
\boxed{
