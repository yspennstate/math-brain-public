# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**. The fixed-population identity is preserved exactly in its restricted scope:

\[
B(\lambda)=\sum_j\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2,
\qquad r=f^\star-m.
\]

This is population spectral calculus for a **fixed mean** and a **fixed population kernel / representation**. The recovered kernel-learning shortlist calls this identity “elementary, not a new conjecture” and separately identifies the harder regime in which the mean, representation and correction are learned from the same observations. Nothing in this run promotes the identity to a same-sample learning theorem.

The strongest new result is a deterministic **feature-space perturbation theorem** for the controlled same-response double-KRR benchmark, followed by a high-dimensional translation of sub-`sqrt(N)` low-rank feature-learning spikes. It complements the preceding run's ridge-whitened theorem by working in primal feature coordinates and exposing exactly when a response-trained representation can be transferred to a frozen/random-feature reference.

Let

\[
\phi_W(x)=N^{-1/2}\sigma(Wx),
\qquad
\Phi\in\mathbb R^{n\times N},
\qquad
C=\frac1n\Phi^\top\Phi,
\qquad
g=\frac1n\Phi^\top Y.
\]

For a query feature vector `z=phi_W(u)`, zero-mean KRR with source-normalized ridge `lambda` is exactly

\[
b(u)=z^\top(C+\lambda I)^{-1}g.
\]

If a KRR mean with ridge `mu` is learned from the **same responses** and residual KRR with ridge `lambda` is then fitted to those same responses, the complete two-stage predictor is exactly

\[
f(u)=z^\top A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)g,
\qquad
A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)
=(C+\mu I)^{-1}
+\mu(C+\lambda I)^{-1}(C+\mu I)^{-1}.
\]

Now let a second, possibly response-trained representation have feature matrix `Phi_tilde=Phi+E` and query feature `z_tilde=z+e_z`, on the **same response matrix `Y`**. Put

\[
s=\frac{\|E\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n},
\quad
B=\frac{\|\Phi\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n},
\quad
\widetilde B=\frac{\|\widetilde\Phi\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n},
\quad
y=\frac{\|Y\|_F}{\sqrt n},
\]

\[
\eta_C=2Bs+s^2,
\qquad e=\|e_z\|_2,
\qquad M=\|z\|_2.
\]

This run proves pathwise

\[
\|\widetilde C-C\|_{\rm op}\le\eta_C,
\qquad
\|\widetilde g-g\|_F\le s y,
\qquad
\|g\|_F\le By,
\qquad
\|\widetilde g\|_F\le\widetilde B y.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde b(u)-b(u)\|_2
\le y\left[
\frac{e\widetilde B}{\lambda}
+M\left(
\frac{\eta_C\widetilde B}{\lambda^2}
+\frac{s}{\lambda}
\right)
\right].
}
\]

For the same-response KRR-mean-plus-residual-KRR learner,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde f(u)-f(u)\|_2
\le y\left[
 e\widetilde B\left(\frac1\mu+\frac1\lambda\right)
+M\left\{
\eta_C\widetilde B
\left(\frac1{\mu^2}+\frac1{\lambda^2}+\frac1{\lambda\mu}\right)
+s\left(\frac1\mu+\frac1\lambda\right)
\right\}
\right].
}
\]

No independence from `Y` is used in either bound. The second representation may be trained on exactly the responses that are later reused by its mean and residual correction. This is

**`P15-PRIMAL-DOUBLEKRR-PERTURB-47 — PROVED`.**

The corresponding root-`L2` gain transfers by the reverse triangle inequality, and the manuscript's unsquared mean-relative gain has the deterministic finite-test analogue obtained by dividing the two pointwise movement terms by the target norm and averaging over the common test rows. Thus a positive frozen-reference same-response margin survives whenever the representation-movement debt is smaller than that margin.

The high-dimensional theorem then starts from an actual response-trained weight perturbation. Suppose `n,d,N` are proportional, the activation is Lipschitz on the realized train/query line segments, and

\[
\widetilde W-W=P+R,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(P)\le r_0,
\qquad
\|P\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^\gamma),
\quad \gamma<\tfrac12,
\qquad
\|R\|_{\rm op}=o_P(1).
\]

If `||X||_op=O_P(sqrt(N))`, `||X||_F=O_P(N)` and a fresh isotropic query is independent of the fitted training sample, then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|\widetilde\Phi-\Phi\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n}
=O_P(N^{\gamma-1/2})+o_P(1),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde\phi(U)-\phi(U)\|_2
=O_P(N^{\gamma-1/2})+o_P(1).
}
\]

The low-rank spike may diverge in weight operator norm, yet it is perturbative at fixed positive KRR ridge because its rank is fixed and its strength is `o(sqrt(N))`. This is

**`P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-FEATURE-47 — PROVED`** under the stated pathwise/local-Lipschitz hypotheses.

A quantitative rate theorem follows. If `||R||_op=O_P(N^{-b})`, put

\[
c=\min\{\tfrac12-\gamma,b\}>0.
\]

If the reference feature matrix has

\[
\|\Phi\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^\rho),
\qquad 0\le\rho\le\tfrac12,
\]

then

\[
s=O_P(N^{-c}),
\qquad
\eta_C=O_P(N^{-c_C}),
\qquad
c_C=\min\{\tfrac12+c-\rho,\,2c\}.
\]

For comparable ridges `lambda_N,mu_N \asymp N^{-a}`, bounded response RMS and bounded query-feature norms, the same-response double-KRR prediction debt vanishes whenever

\[
\boxed{2a<c_C.}
\]

Because `c_C<=2c`, this condition also implies `a<c`, controlling the direct query/label-vector terms. In the fully coherent worst case `rho=1/2`, one has `c_C=c` and needs `a<c/2`. In a centered/random-like feature regime with `rho=0`, one has `c_C=2c` and the condition improves to `a<c`. This is

**`P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-GAIN-TRANSFER-47 — PROVED`** as a conditional high-dimensional implication under the explicit quantitative assumptions above.

The theorem gives a clean reading of the current two-step feature-learning literature. Moniri--Hassani (arXiv:2605.17767v2, 21 May 2026) work in the proportional linear-width regime with step sizes `eta_i asymp N^{alpha_i}`, `alpha_i<1/2`, prove a low-rank spiked description of the two-step weight matrix, and show a real reused-batch versus fresh-batch difference: reuse can reveal target directions unavailable to the independent-batch update. Their source model has Gaussian covariates and additive response noise, and their detailed assumptions/feature expansion are not the July deterministic-PDE pipeline. The paper's reported weight remainder is `o_P(1)` in operator norm rather than a source-ready polynomial rate. Therefore:

- for a **fixed positive ridge**, their sub-`sqrt(N)` spiked weight geometry is compatible with the new P15 perturbative transfer **provided** the induced feature map satisfies the local train/query regularity used above;
- for a **shrinking ridge**, a rate such as `||R||_op=O_P(N^{-b})` and corresponding local feature control are additionally needed before one may invoke `2a<c_C`;
- their reuse theorem is evidence that same-batch feature learning can have a genuine sign-relevant effect, not a theorem that reuse is always a penalty.

This is recorded as **`P15-MONIRI-SPIKE-TRANSLATION-47 — CONDITIONAL`**, not as an imported source solution.

The run also proves a sharp obstruction showing why the ridge scale cannot be omitted. Take `n=d=N`, linear activation, deterministic training matrix `X=sqrt(N) I`, reference `W_0=0`, and

\[
W_1=N^\gamma a b^\top,
\qquad 0<\gamma<\tfrac12,
\]

for unit vectors `a,b`. Then

\[
\Phi_1=N^\gamma b a^\top,
\qquad
K_1=\frac1N\Phi_1\Phi_1^\top
=\kappa_N bb^\top,
\qquad
\kappa_N=N^{2\gamma-1}\to0.
\]

Choose a query with `b^T u=1`, response vector `Y=sqrt(N) theta b`, and ridge

\[
\lambda_N=\kappa_N.
\]

The reference KRR predictor is zero, while the learned predictor is exactly

\[
\boxed{b_1(u)=\theta/2.}
\]

Yet

\[
\|W_1-W_0\|_{\rm op}=N^\gamma=o(\sqrt N),
\quad
\|K_1-K_0\|_{\rm op}=\kappa_N\to0,
\quad
\|k_1(u)-k_0(u)\|=\kappa_N/\sqrt N\to0.

The ridge-relative perturbation stays exactly order one:

\[
\|(K_0+\lambda_N I)^{-1/2}K_1(K_0+\lambda_N I)^{-1/2}\|=1.

Thus sub-`sqrt(N)` weight movement plus absolute Gram/query convergence does **not** imply prediction convergence at the critical shrinking-ridge scale. This is

**`P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-CRITICALRIDGE-CE-47 — DISPROVED route`.**

# 4. Exact primal form of the same-response double-KRR learner

Let `Phi in R^{n x N}` be any realized feature matrix and let `z in R^N` be a query feature. Define

\[
K=\frac1n\Phi\Phi^\top,
\qquad
k(u)=\frac1n\Phi z,
\qquad
C=\frac1n\Phi^\top\Phi,
\qquad
g=\frac1n\Phi^\top Y.
\]

The source-normalized dual KRR predictor with ridge `lambda>0` is

\[
b(u)=k(u)^\top(K+\lambda I_n)^{-1}Y.
\]

The standard push-through identity

\[
\frac1n\Phi^\top(K+\lambda I_n)^{-1}
=(C+\lambda I_N)^{-1}\frac1n\Phi^\top
\]

gives the exact primal form

\[
\boxed{b(u)=z^\top(C+\lambda I)^{-1}g.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now learn a KRR mean from the **same response matrix `Y`** with ridge `mu>0`. Its training predictions are

\[
M=K(K+\mu I)^{-1}Y,
\]

so the residual labels are exactly

\[
Y-M=\mu(K+\mu I)^{-1}Y.
\]

Residual KRR with ridge `lambda` followed by query evaluation yields

\[
f(u)
=k(u)^\top(K+\mu I)^{-1}Y
+\mu k(u)^\top(K+\lambda I)^{-1}(K+\mu I)^{-1}Y.
\]

Pushing through to feature coordinates gives

\[
\boxed{
f(u)=z^\top A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)g,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)
=(C+\mu I)^{-1}
+\mu(C+\lambda I)^{-1}(C+\mu I)^{-1}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equations `(4.1)--(4.3)` are exact and make no independence assertion. They are the feature-coordinate version of the previously proved double-KRR rational filter.

# 5. Deterministic feature-matrix perturbation theorem

Let a second representation, possibly trained using exactly the same responses `Y`, have

\[
\widetilde\Phi=\Phi+E,
\qquad
\widetilde z=z+e_z.
\]

Write

\[
D=\|E\|_{\rm op},
\qquad
s=D/\sqrt n,
\qquad
B=\|\Phi\|_{\rm op}/\sqrt n,
\qquad
\widetilde B=\|\widetilde\Phi\|_{\rm op}/\sqrt n,
\]

\[
y=\|Y\|_F/\sqrt n,
\qquad
e=\|e_z\|_2,
\qquad M=\|z\|_2.
\]

Let

\[
\widetilde C=\frac1n\widetilde\Phi^\top\widetilde\Phi,
\qquad
\widetilde g=\frac1n\widetilde\Phi^\top Y.
\]

## `P15-PRIMAL-COVARIANCE-PERTURB-47 — PROVED`

Pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde C-C\|_{\rm op}
\le 2Bs+s^2=:\eta_C,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde g-g\|_F\le s y,
\qquad
\|g\|_F\le By,
\qquad
\|\widetilde g\|_F\le\widetilde B y.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Expand

\[
\widetilde C-C
=\frac1n(\Phi^\top E+E^\top\Phi+E^\top E).
\]

The three operator norms are bounded by `||Phi|| D/n`, the same quantity, and `D^2/n`, which is `(5.1)`. Also

\[
\widetilde g-g=E^\top Y/n,
\]

and Frobenius/operator submultiplicativity gives

\[
\|E^\top Y/n\|_F
\le D\|Y\|_F/n=s y.
\]

The remaining two inequalities in `(5.2)` are identical.

No probabilistic assumption and no response independence appears.

# 6. Same-response baseline and double-KRR prediction movement

Let

\[
R_a(C)=(C+aI)^{-1}.
\]

For PSD `C, C_tilde`,

\[
\|R_a(\widetilde C)-R_a(C)\|_{\rm op}
\le\frac{\eta_C}{a^2}
\tag{6.1}
\]

by the resolvent identity and `||R_a||<=1/a`.

## `P15-PRIMAL-DOUBLEKRR-PERTURB-47 — PROVED`

The zero-mean KRR predictors satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde b(u)-b(u)\|_2
\le
 y\left[
\frac{e\widetilde B}{\lambda}
+M\left(
\frac{\eta_C\widetilde B}{\lambda^2}
+\frac{s}{\lambda}
\right)
\right].
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The same-response KRR-mean-plus-residual-KRR predictors satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|\widetilde f(u)-f(u)\|_2
\le y\Bigg[&
e\widetilde B\left(\frac1\mu+\frac1\lambda\right)\\
&+M\Bigg\{
\eta_C\widetilde B
\left(\frac1{\mu^2}+\frac1{\lambda^2}+\frac1{\lambda\mu}\right)
+s\left(\frac1\mu+\frac1\lambda\right)
\Bigg\}\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof of `(6.2)`

From `(4.1)`, telescope

\[
\widetilde b-b
=e_z^\top R_\lambda(\widetilde C)\widetilde g
+z^\top[R_\lambda(\widetilde C)-R_\lambda(C)]\widetilde g
+z^\top R_\lambda(C)(\widetilde g-g).
\]

Use `||R_lambda||<=1/lambda`, `(5.2)` and `(6.1)`.

### Proof of `(6.3)`

First,

\[
\|A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)\|_{\rm op}
\le \frac1\mu+\frac1\lambda.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For the operator difference, the first resolvent contributes `eta_C/mu^2`. For the product term,

\[
\widetilde R_\lambda\widetilde R_\mu-R_\lambda R_\mu
=(\widetilde R_\lambda-R_\lambda)\widetilde R_\mu
+R_\lambda(\widetilde R_\mu-R_\mu).
\]

After multiplying by `mu`, this is at most

\[
\eta_C\left(\frac1{\lambda^2}+\frac1{\lambda\mu}\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{\lambda,\mu}(\widetilde C)-A_{\lambda,\mu}(C)\|_{\rm op}
\le
\eta_C\left(\frac1{\mu^2}+\frac1{\lambda^2}+\frac1{\lambda\mu}\right).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Telescope

\[
\widetilde f-f
=e_z^\top\widetilde A\widetilde g
+z^\top(\widetilde A-A)\widetilde g
+z^\top A(\widetilde g-g)
\]

and apply `(5.2)`, `(6.4)` and `(6.5)`.

### Exact-dependence scope

The proof conditions on every realized object. `Phi_tilde` may have been trained on `Y`; the KRR mean and the residual correction may then reuse that same `Y`. This is therefore a same-observation perturbation theorem, not an independent-feature statement.

\ge
\Gamma_{\rm rel}
-E\frac{P_b(U)+P_f(U)}{\|G(U)\|_2}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is no inserted inverse-square target norm condition.

### Deterministic finite-test certificate

On a fixed common test table `(u_j,v_j)`, `(7.2)` holds exactly after replacing expectation by the empirical average

\[
\frac1{N_{\rm test}}\sum_j
\frac{P_b(u_j)+P_f(u_j)}{\|v_j\|_2}.
\]

This is a realized-table theorem; it is not a population confidence statement unless a separate evaluation-sampling argument is supplied.

# 8. From low-rank weight spikes to feature/kernel movement

We now connect `(6.2)--(7.2)` to a response-trained weight matrix.

Let

\[
\phi_W(x)=N^{-1/2}\sigma(Wx).
\]

Assume `sigma` is `L`-Lipschitz on every line segment between the relevant preactivations `Wx` and `W_tilde x` for the training rows and evaluation queries. A globally Lipschitz activation is a sufficient special case.

Suppose

\[
\widetilde W-W=P+R,
\tag{8.1}
\]

with

\[
\operatorname{rank}(P)\le r_0,
\qquad
\|P\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^\gamma),
\quad \gamma<1/2,
\tag{8.2}
\]

and `||R||_op=o_P(1)`. Assume the proportional regime and input norm controls

\[
n\asymp d\asymp N,
\qquad
\|X\|_{\rm op}=O_P(\sqrt N),
\qquad
\|X\|_F=O_P(N).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Because the spike rank is fixed,

\[
\|P\|_F\le\sqrt{r_0}\|P\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^\gamma).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Let `E=Phi_tilde-Phi`. Entrywise Lipschitzness yields

\[
\|E\|_F
\le \frac{L}{\sqrt N}\|X(P+R)^\top\|_F.
\]

For the spike,

\[
\frac{L}{\sqrt N}\|XP^\top\|_F
\le
\frac{L}{\sqrt N}\|X\|_{\rm op}\|P\|_F
=O_P(N^\gamma).
\tag{8.5}
\]

For the remainder,

\[
\frac{L}{\sqrt N}\|XR^\top\|_F
\le
\frac{L}{\sqrt N}\|X\|_F\|R\|_{\rm op}
=O_P(\sqrt N\,\|R\|_{\rm op}).
\tag{8.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|E\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt n}
\le\frac{\|E\|_F}{\sqrt n}
=O_P(N^{\gamma-1/2})+O_P(\|R\|_{\rm op})
=o_P(1).
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

For a fresh isotropic query `U`, condition on the fitted training objects. Then

\[
E[\|PU\|^2\mid P]=\|P\|_F^2=O_P(N^{2\gamma}),
\]

so

\[
\frac1{\sqrt N}\|PU\|=O_P(N^{\gamma-1/2}).
\]

Also `||RU||/sqrt(N)<=||R||_op ||U||/sqrt(N)=o_P(1)` when `||U||=O_P(sqrt(N))`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\phi_{\widetilde W}(U)-\phi_W(U)\|_2
=O_P(N^{\gamma-1/2})+o_P(1).
}
\tag{8.8}
\]

## `P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-FEATURE-47 — PROVED`

Equations `(8.7)--(8.8)` are the theorem. The spike is permitted to diverge in weight operator norm; finite rank and `gamma<1/2` are what make its train/query feature effect perturbative after the source-compatible normalization.

### Quantitative covariance rate

If additionally

\[
\|R\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^{-b}),
\qquad b>0,
\]

put

\[
c=\min\{1/2-\gamma,b\}.
\]

Then `s=O_P(N^{-c})`, and the same holds for fresh-query feature movement. If

\[
\|\Phi\|_{\rm op}=O_P(N^\rho),
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1/2,
\]

then

\[
B=O_P(N^{\rho-1/2})
\]

and `(5.1)` gives

\[
\eta_C
=O_P(N^{-(1/2+c-\rho)})+O_P(N^{-2c}).
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
c_C=\min\{1/2+c-\rho,\,2c\}.}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\eta_C=O_P(N^{-c_C}).}
\tag{8.10}
\]

The parameter `rho` quantifies feature coherence. The deterministic worst case from bounded row norms is `rho=1/2`; centered/random-like feature matrices often have much smaller operator norm, but such a source-specific estimate must be checked rather than assumed.

### Shrinking ridge

Suppose `lambda_N` and `mu_N` are comparable and bounded below by a constant times `N^{-a}`. Assume bounded response RMS, bounded query feature norms and the corresponding integrability for the desired risk metric. From `(6.2)--(6.3)`, the direct query/response-vector terms are

\[
O_P(N^{-c+a}),
\]

while the covariance/resolvent term is

\[
O_P(N^{-c_C+2a}).
\]

Since `c_C<=2c`, the single strict condition

\[
\boxed{2a<c_C}
\tag{8.11}
\]

implies both vanish.

## `P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-GAIN-TRANSFER-47 — PROVED`

If a frozen-reference same-response double-KRR gain converges to a positive margin and `(8.1)--(8.11)` hold, then the corresponding learned-representation gain has the same positive limit up to `o_P(1)`.

Special cases:

- worst-case coherent features, `rho=1/2`: `c_C=c`, so `a<c/2` is sufficient;
- centered/random-like features, `rho=0`: because `c<=1/2`, `c_C=2c`, so `a<c` is sufficient;
- fixed positive ridge, `a=0`: the quantitative remainder exponent is unnecessary; `||R||_op=o_P(1)` and `gamma<1/2` already make the feature movement vanish under the local regularity assumptions.

These are sufficient transfer conditions, not claimed sharp universal phase boundaries.

# 9. Exact critical-ridge counterexample

## `P15-SUBSQRT-SPIKE-CRITICALRIDGE-CE-47 — DISPROVED route`

A tempting route is:

> If the response-trained first-layer perturbation is `o(sqrt(N))` in operator norm and the induced normalized training Gram/query vector converge absolutely, then KRR predictions must converge even under a shrinking ridge.

This is false.

Take `n=d=N`, linear activation `sigma(t)=t`, and deterministic design

\[
X=\sqrt N I_N.
\]

Let the reference weight be `W_0=0`. For fixed unit vectors `a,b` and any `gamma in (0,1/2)`, define

\[
W_1=N^\gamma a b^\top.
\]

With `phi_W(x)=N^{-1/2}Wx`, the training feature matrices are

\[
\Phi_0=0,
\qquad
\Phi_1=N^\gamma b a^\top.
\]

Therefore the normalized training Gram is

\[
K_1=\frac1N\Phi_1\Phi_1^\top
=N^{2\gamma-1}bb^\top
=:\kappa_N bb^\top,
\]

with `kappa_N -> 0`.

Choose a query `u` with `b^T u=1`. Its feature is

\[
z_1=N^{\gamma-1/2}a,
\]

and its normalized query kernel vector is

\[
k_1(u)=\frac1N\Phi_1 z_1
=\frac{\kappa_N}{\sqrt N}b.
\]

Choose the response vector

\[
Y=\sqrt N\,\theta b
\]

and ridge

\[
\lambda_N=\kappa_N.
\]

The reference predictor is exactly zero. For the learned representation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_1(u)
&=\frac{\kappa_N}{\sqrt N}
 b^\top
 (\kappa_N bb^\top+\kappa_N I)^{-1}
 (\sqrt N\theta b)\\
&=\frac{\kappa_N}{\sqrt N}
 \frac1{2\kappa_N}
 \sqrt N\theta
=\frac\theta2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the prediction movement is order one for every `N`, even though

\[
\|W_1-W_0\|_{\rm op}=N^\gamma=o(\sqrt N),
\]

\[
\|K_1-K_0\|_{\rm op}=\kappa_N\to0,
\]

and

\[
\|k_1(u)-k_0(u)\|=\kappa_N/\sqrt N\to0.
\]

The correct relative diagnostic is

\[
\|(K_0+\lambda_N I)^{-1/2}K_1(K_0+\lambda_N I)^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}
=\kappa_N/\lambda_N=1.
\]

So absolute convergence and a sub-`sqrt(N)` weight spike do not control a ridge tuned on the same vanishing spectral scale. This is exactly why the preceding ridge-whitened theorem and the new rate condition `(8.11)` retain the ridge scale.

This counterexample is algebraic and deterministic. It is not presented as a structural-mechanics data model.
