# 4. New theorem: finite-step same-observation feature movement

## `P15-FINITEGD-FEATURE-MOVEMENT-45 — PROVED`

Let the training sample be arbitrary fixed pairs `(x_i,y_i)`, `i=1,...,n`, with

\[
\|x_i\|_2\le R.
\]

Let `m` be the hidden width, `sigma` act coordinatewise, and assume

\[
|\sigma'(z)|\le L
\]

wherever the path is evaluated. Define the feature map

\[
\phi_t(x)=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sigma(W_tx)\in\mathbb R^m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

At step `t`, allow an output head

\[
A_t\in\mathbb R^{m\times q}
\]

which may itself depend arbitrarily on the same labeled observations and on the previous optimization history, subject only to

\[
\|A_t\|_{\rm op}\le A_0\sqrt m.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The current network prediction is

\[
z_t(x)=\frac1{\sqrt m}A_t^T\sigma(W_tx).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `B_t` be any minibatch of size `b_t`; it may be chosen by exogenous procedure randomness and is not assumed independent of the same training pool. Put

\[
r_{t,i}=z_t(x_i)-y_i,
\qquad
s_t^2=\frac1{b_t}\sum_{i\in B_t}\|r_{t,i}\|_2^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Update the first-layer weights by an ordinary gradient step on the minibatch square loss:

\[
W_{t+1}=W_t-\eta_t\nabla_W
\left[\frac1{2b_t}\sum_{i\in B_t}\|z_t(x_i)-y_i\|_2^2\right].
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
\|\nabla_W L_t\|_F\le LA_0R\,s_t,
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\|W_T-W_0\|_F
\le LA_0R\sum_{t<T}\eta_ts_t.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

For every evaluation point with `||x||<=R`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\phi_T(x)-\phi_0(x)\|_2
\le
D_T:=\frac{L^2A_0R^2}{\sqrt m}
\sum_{t<T}\eta_ts_t.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof

For one row `i`, differentiation of `(4.3)` gives

\[
\nabla_W\frac12\|r_{t,i}\|_2^2
=\frac1{\sqrt m}
\operatorname{diag}(\sigma'(W_tx_i))A_tr_{t,i}x_i^T.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\left\|
\nabla_W\frac12\|r_{t,i}\|_2^2
\right\|_F
\le
\frac{L\|A_t\|_{\rm op}}{\sqrt m}
\|r_{t,i}\|_2\|x_i\|_2
\le LA_0R\|r_{t,i}\|_2.
\]

Average over the minibatch and apply Cauchy--Schwarz:

\[
\|\nabla_WL_t\|_F
\le
LA_0R\frac1{b_t}\sum_{i\in B_t}\|r_{t,i}\|_2
\le LA_0R s_t.
\]

This proves `(4.6)`. Summing the update norms gives `(4.7)`. Finally, coordinatewise `L`-Lipschitzness of `sigma` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\phi_T(x)-\phi_0(x)\|_2
&\le\frac L{\sqrt m}\|(W_T-W_0)x\|_2\\
&\le\frac{LR}{\sqrt m}\|W_T-W_0\|_F,
\end{aligned}
\]

and `(4.8)` follows.

### Dependence scope

The theorem is deterministic after the realized training/optimization path is fixed. In particular:

- `A_t` may have been trained on the same responses;
- minibatches may reuse the same observations many times;
- `W_t` is response-trained by construction;
- no response-noise model is used;
- no train/test independence beyond the usual fresh evaluation point is used in the later risk statement.

What matters is the explicit cumulative path budget, not a fictitious independence assumption.

# 5. From feature movement to Gram and fresh-query movement

Let `Phi_t in R^{n x m}` have rows `phi_t(x_i)^T`. Define the raw feature Gram and query vector

\[
K_t=\Phi_t\Phi_t^T,
\qquad
k_t(u)=\Phi_t\phi_t(u).
\]

To match the source normalization exactly, define

\[
\bar K_t=\frac1nK_t,
\qquad
\bar k_t(u)=\frac1n k_t(u).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then

\[
k_t(u)^T(K_t+n\lambda I)^{-1}Y
=
\bar k_t(u)^T(\bar K_t+\lambda I)^{-1}Y.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume the reference feature norms satisfy

\[
\|\phi_0(x_i)\|_2\le M
\]

on all training rows and

\[
\|\phi_0(u)\|_2\le M
\]

on the evaluation points under consideration. By `(4.8)`, every feature row moves by at most `D_T`. Thus

\[
\|\Phi_T-\Phi_0\|_{\rm op}
\le
\|\Phi_T-\Phi_0\|_F
\le\sqrt n D_T,
\]

while

\[
\|\Phi_0\|_{\rm op}\le\sqrt n M.
\]

Expanding the Gram difference yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\bar K_T-\bar K_0\|_{\rm op}
&\le\frac1n\left[
2\|\Phi_0\|_{\rm op}\|\Phi_T-\Phi_0\|_{\rm op}
+\|\Phi_T-\Phi_0\|_{\rm op}^2
\right]\\
&\le 2MD_T+D_T^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For a fresh/evaluation query `u`, a three-term telescope gives

\[
\sqrt n\,\|\bar k_T(u)-\bar k_0(u)\|_2
\le 2MD_T+D_T^2.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
\boxed{E_T:=2MD_T+D_T^2,}
\tag{5.3}
\]

we have the exact deterministic bounds

\[
\boxed{
\|\bar K_T-\bar K_0\|_{\rm op}\le E_T,
\qquad
\|\bar k_T(u)-\bar k_0(u)\|_2\le\frac{E_T}{\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also

\[
\|\bar k_0(u)\|_2
\le\frac{M^2}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This simultaneously controls the two quantities that the preceding run proved are both necessary: the training Gram **and** the fresh-query representation.

# 7. High-dimensional same-observation corollary

## `P15-FINITEGD-HIGHDIM-SIGN-45 — PROVED`

Consider a sequence with

\[
\frac mn\to\beta\in(0,\infty).
\]

Suppose along the sequence:

- `L,A_0,R,M,y_rms` are bounded;
- `lambda,mu` are bounded below by a positive constant;
- the reference same-response double-KRR root-risk gain satisfies `Gamma_0^(2) -> gamma>0` (or the mean-relative analogue has a positive limiting margin and bounded first inverse-target moment);
- the response-trained feature path satisfies
  \[
  S_T:=\sum_{t<T}\eta_ts_t=o_P(\sqrt m).
  \]

Then

\[
D_T=o_P(1),
\qquad
E_T=o_P(1),
\]

and `(6.8)` gives

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_T^{(2)}\ge\gamma-o_P(1).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

In particular the learned same-observation correction has positive gain with probability tending to one.

If `S_T=O_P(1)`, then

\[
D_T=O_P(m^{-1/2}),
\qquad
E_T=O_P(m^{-1/2}),
\]

and at linear width the transfer debt is `O_P(n^{-1/2})`.

This is a real high-dimensional same-observation guarantee, but only in an explicitly perturbative training regime. It does not cover a feature-learning regime in which cumulative first-layer movement is order one or larger after the `1/sqrt(m)` feature scaling.

# 9. Exact obstruction: linear width is not enough

## `P15-LINEARWIDTH-STEPSIZE-CE-45 — DISPROVED route`

The statement

> `m` proportional to `n` by itself forces response-trained feature movement to vanish

is false.

Take one deterministic observation

\[
x=1,\qquad y=1,
\]

one-dimensional input/output, width `m`, linear activation `sigma(z)=z`, initial first-layer weights

\[
W_0=0\in\mathbb R^{m\times1},
\]

and output head

\[
A=\mathbf 1_m,
\qquad
\|A\|_2=\sqrt m.
\]

The network output is

\[
z_W(1)=\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{j=1}^mW_j.
\]

At initialization `z=0`, so the gradient is

\[
\nabla_W\frac12(z-1)^2
=-\frac1{\sqrt m}\mathbf1_m.
\]

Take one step with

\[
\eta=\sqrt m.
\]

Then

\[
W_1=\mathbf1_m.
\]

The normalized feature vector moves from

\[
\phi_0(1)=0
\]

to

\[
\phi_1(1)=\frac1{\sqrt m}\mathbf1_m,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\|\phi_1(1)-\phi_0(1)\|_2=1}
\]

for every width. With `n=1`, the normalized feature Gram moves from `0` to `1` and the fresh-query kernel moves by the same order-one amount.

The bound `(4.8)` is attained at the level of scaling: here `s_0=1`, `L=A_0=R=1`, and

\[
D_1=\eta/\sqrt m=1.
\]

Therefore width growth alone gives no same-sample safety theorem. A cumulative update/risk budget such as `(0.7)` is essential.

This does not replace the stronger inherited sign-flip counterexamples; it explains why a naive high-dimensional perturbative route can fail before risk comparison even begins.
