# 0. Exact target and immutable boundary

The population identity remains

\[
\boxed{
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j
\left[
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}
\right]^2 r_j^2,
\qquad
r=f^\star-m.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

It is an exact population ridge-bias identity for a **fixed** mean `m` and a **fixed** population kernel operator.

It is **not** a theorem that a mean, representation, stack, selected hyperparameters and correction learned from the **same observations** improve fresh prediction.

The original accessible shortlist `Kernel_Learning_Conjectures 2.pdf` states on page 5 that `(0.1)` is “elementary, not a new conjecture” and then explicitly names the harder follow-up: comparable prediction guarantees when the **mean, representation, and correction are trained on the same observations**. That is the P15 target.

This run attacks a concrete source-facing obstacle left by the overlap/ghost theory from the preceding runs:

> Fresh iid solver replicas are not currently available, but complete **deletion retraining** can in principle be done using the already existing labeled simulator sample. Can deletion be converted rigorously into refresh/ghost stability and, more directly, into a certificate in the manuscript’s actual unsquared mean-relative metric?

The answer is yes, with an exact Hilbert-space decomposition and a direct loss-level theorem.

# 4. New exact theorem: deletion is refresh variance plus sample-size bias

## `P15-DELETION-REFRESH-PYTHAGORAS-37` — PROVED

This is the main new bridge.

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)
\]

be iid observations and let `R` be any deletion block of size `r`; the theorem works for a fixed block or for a random block independent of the data. Write `T=R^c`.

Let

\[
F_n(S)\in\mathcal H
\]

be an arbitrary square-integrable Hilbert-valued complete learner trained on all `n` observations. It may contain a neural mean, response-trained representation, optimizer trajectory, validation-selected checkpoint, stack, kernel/ridge choice and residual KRR.

Let

\[
F_{n-r}(S_T)
\]

be the corresponding reduced-sample training procedure. No differentiability, convexity or fixed-representation assumption is imposed.

Let `Z'_R` be an iid fresh copy of the deleted block, independent of everything else, and define the refreshed full-size sample

\[
S^{\langle R\rangle}=(S_T,Z'_R).
\]

Define

\[
D_R^{\rm ref}(F)
:=
E\|F_n(S)-F_n(S^{\langle R\rangle})\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and the deletion distance

\[
\Delta_R^{\rm del}(F)
:=
E\|F_n(S)-F_{n-r}(S_T)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Conditional on `S_T`, define the conditional full-size projection

\[
\mu_T
:=
E[F_n(S)\mid S_T],
\tag{4.3}
\]

and the **sample-size / algorithmic deletion bias**

\[
B_R^{\rm size}(F)
:=
E\|\mu_T-F_{n-r}(S_T)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_R^{\rm del}(F)
=
\frac12 D_R^{\rm ref}(F)
+
B_R^{\rm size}(F).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
D_R^{\rm ref}(F)
=
2\big(\Delta_R^{\rm del}(F)-B_R^{\rm size}(F)\big)
\le
2\Delta_R^{\rm del}(F).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the retained observations `S_T`. Put

\[
X=F_n(S_T,Z_R),
\qquad
X'=F_n(S_T,Z'_R).
\]

Conditional on `S_T`, `X` and `X'` are iid Hilbert-valued random variables with common conditional mean `mu_T`. Therefore

\[
E[\|X-X'\|^2\mid S_T]
=
2E[\|X-\mu_T\|^2\mid S_T].
\tag{4.7}
\]

Because `F_{n-r}(S_T)` is `S_T`-measurable, conditional Pythagoras gives

\[
E[\|X-F_{n-r}(S_T)\|^2\mid S_T]
=
E[\|X-\mu_T\|^2\mid S_T]
+
\|\mu_T-F_{n-r}(S_T)\|^2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Average `(4.7)`--`(4.8)` to obtain `(4.5)`.

No theorem about KRR or neural networks was imported: this is a probability/Hilbert identity for the complete learning algorithm.

### Interpretation

Deletion is conservative for refresh because it mixes two effects:

1. sensitivity to the deleted observations at fixed full sample size;
2. the change in the training rule caused by reducing the sample size from `n` to `n-r`.

The second effect is exactly `B_R^size`.

Equality

\[
D_R^{\rm ref}=2\Delta_R^{\rm del}
\]

holds iff

\[
F_{n-r}(S_T)=E[F_n(S)\mid S_T]
\]

in `L2`. That conditional-projection condition is the exact hypothesis under which deletion is an unbiased proxy for refresh variance.

# 5. Deletion-only full-ghost certificate

## `P15-DELETION-GHOST-BRIDGE-37` — PROVED

Combine `(4.6)` with the fixed-cardinality overlap theorem from the preceding run.

For a uniformly chosen exact-`r` block, write

\[
\Delta_r^{\rm del}(F)=E_R\Delta_R^{\rm del}(F),
\qquad
B_r^{\rm size}(F)=E_R B_R^{\rm size}(F).
\]

The inherited fixed-overlap concavity theorem gives

\[
V(F)
:=
E\|F_n(S)-F_n(S')\|^2
\le
\frac nr D_r^{\rm ref}(F).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
V(F)
\le
\frac{2n}{r}
\big(\Delta_r^{\rm del}(F)-B_r^{\rm size}(F)\big)
\le
\frac{2n}{r}\Delta_r^{\rm del}(F).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a fresh-sample stability certificate using only **deletion retraining**.

For the source-sized block `n=19000`, `r=4750`,

\[
\frac{2n}{r}=8,
\tag{5.2}
\]

so a random 4,750-row deletion experiment gives

\[
\boxed{V(F)\le8\Delta_{4750}^{\rm del}(F).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If the exact sample-size bias `(4.4)` is separately bounded or estimated, `(5.1)` is sharper.

### Squared-risk same-observation reuse debt

For the arbitrary complete baseline/correction decomposition

\[
F_S=B_S+G_S,
\]

prior P15 proved

\[
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=
E\langle B_S-B_{S'},G_S-G_{S'}\rangle.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz plus `(5.1)` yields

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}|
\le
\frac{2n}{r}
\sqrt{
\Delta_r^{\rm del}(B)
\Delta_r^{\rm del}(G)
}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

At `n/r=4`, the coefficient is exactly `8`.

This is a true same-observation theorem with arbitrary learned representation and hard selection inside the learners. It is not yet a numerical mechanics certificate because the required complete-pipeline deletion distances have not been released or computed.

# 6. New source-metric theorem without inverse-square target-norm assumptions

The preceding mean-relative ghost theorems used the weighted Hilbert norm

\[
E\frac{\|h(U)\|^2}{\|Y\|^2},
\]

which requires an inverse-square target-norm moment. The manuscript does not supply that assumption.

Deletion gives a more direct route that only needs the same first-moment integrability as the reported relative risk.

## `P15-MEANREL-DELETION-HYBRID-37` — PROVED

Let the full-data uncorrected learner be `B_n`, and write the full corrected learner as

\[
F_n=B_n+G_n.
\]

For a deletion block `R`, retrain whatever reduced pipeline is desired and construct a correction increment `G_{-R}`. Define the **hybrid deletion benchmark**

\[
H_R=B_n+G_{-R}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The full baseline is kept fixed; only the learned correction increment is taken from the deleted-data fit.

For the source loss

\[
\ell_{\rm rel}(f;u,y)
=
\frac{\|y-f(u)\|}{\|y\|},
\tag{6.2}
\]

the reverse triangle inequality gives pointwise

\[
\left|
\ell_{\rm rel}(F_n;u,y)
-
\ell_{\rm rel}(H_R;u,y)
\right|
\le
\frac{\|G_n(u)-G_{-R}(u)\|}{\|y\|}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore, whenever the displayed expectations are finite,

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
:=
R_{\rm rel}(B_n)-R_{\rm rel}(F_n)
\ge
\Gamma_{R,\rm hyb}^{\rm rel}
-
\delta_R,
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

where

\[
\Gamma_{R,\rm hyb}^{\rm rel}
=
R_{\rm rel}(B_n)-R_{\rm rel}(H_R),
\tag{6.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\delta_R
=
E\frac{\|G_n(U)-G_{-R}(U)\|}{\|Y\|}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{R,\rm hyb}^{\rm rel}>\delta_R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{\rm rel}(F_n)<R_{\rm rel}(B_n).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is a direct theorem in the **exact unsquared mean-relative metric used by the manuscript**.

No inverse-square moment is required. No Gaussian noise model is required. No independence between mean, representation and correction is required. Hard checkpoint/kernel/ridge selection is allowed because `G_n` and `G_{-R}` are treated as complete realized algorithm outputs.

### Multiple deletions

For deletion blocks `R_1,...,R_K`, `(6.4)` holds for each. Averaging gives

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\frac1K\sum_{\ell=1}^K
\Gamma_{\ell,\rm hyb}^{\rm rel}
-
\frac1K\sum_{\ell=1}^K\delta_\ell.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

If weighted second moments happen to be available, then

\[
\frac1K\sum_\ell\delta_\ell
\le
\left[
\frac1K\sum_\ell
E\frac{\|G_n(U)-G_{-\ell}(U)\|^2}{\|Y\|^2}
\right]^{1/2},
\tag{6.9}
\]

but `(6.8)` itself does not require them.

### An even simpler comparator form

The hybrid construction is useful for isolating the correction. More generally, for **any** deleted-data predictor `F_{-R}`,

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm rel}(B_n)-R_{\rm rel}(F_n)
\ge
R_{\rm rel}(B_n)-R_{\rm rel}(F_{-R})
-
E\frac{\|F_n(U)-F_{-R}(U)\|}{\|Y\|}.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

So a reduced-data corrected pipeline that already beats the full-data uncorrected baseline by more than its prediction movement from the full corrected fit certifies the desired sign.

This is elementary but source-facing: it converts deletion retraining directly into the requested corrected-versus-uncorrected comparison without passing through a squared-risk surrogate.

# 7. Deterministic certificate on the fixed public test block

## `P15-FINITE-TEST-DELETION-CERT-37` — PROVED

Let the evaluation block be any fixed collection

\[
\{(u_j,y_j)\}_{j=1}^M
\]

with `y_j != 0`. Define empirical mean-relative risk

\[
\widehat R_{\rm rel}(f)
=
\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M
\frac{\|y_j-f(u_j)\|}{\|y_j\|}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For the hybrid `(6.1)` define

\[
\widehat\delta_R
=
\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M
\frac{\|G_n(u_j)-G_{-R}(u_j)\|}{\|y_j\|}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then the finite-table inequality is deterministic:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat R(B_n)-\widehat R(F_n)
\ge
\widehat R(B_n)-\widehat R(H_R)
-
\widehat\delta_R.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

For `K` deletion retrains,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Gamma_{\rm same}
\ge
\frac1K\sum_{\ell=1}^K\widehat\Gamma_{\ell,\rm hyb}
-
\frac1K\sum_{\ell=1}^K\widehat\delta_\ell.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This requires no probability theorem whatsoever.

For the mechanics source, `M=20000` is the fixed public test block. Equation `(7.4)` would be an exact certificate for the **realized public-test comparison** provided the test labels remain evaluation-only.

It is not by itself a population generalization theorem. Turning `(7.4)` into a population confidence statement requires an honest evaluation-sample assumption plus a valid concentration condition (bounded relative loss, finite variance with a robust mean, or another explicit tail hypothesis). I do not invent such a source hypothesis.

# 8. First-moment deletion-to-ghost transfer

The Hilbert theorem `(5.1)` is useful for squared risk, but the source metric is an `L1`-type norm ratio. A pure metric argument gives a ghost bridge without inverse-square moments.

## `P15-MEANREL-DELETION-GHOST-L1-37` — PROVED

Let

\[
d_w(f,g)
:=
E_{U,Y}
\frac{\|f(U)-g(U)\|}{\|Y\|},
\tag{8.1}
\]

assuming the first moment is finite.

For a block `R`, conditional symmetry and the triangle inequality through the reduced learner give

\[
\boxed{
E\,d_w(F_n(S),F_n(S^{\langle R\rangle}))
\le
2E\,d_w(F_n(S),F_{n-r}(S_T)).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Now partition the data coordinates into disjoint blocks `R_1,...,R_K` and replace them one block at a time by iid copies. The triangle inequality along this path gives

\[
\boxed{
E\,d_w(F_n(S),F_n(S'))
\le
2\sum_{\ell=1}^K
E\,d_w(F_n(S),F_{n-|R_\ell|}(S_{-R_\ell})).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

No permutation invariance is needed for `(8.3)` if the blocks are fixed independently of the iid observations; each intermediate sample is again iid in the coordinate positions.

For the correction increment `G`, the mean-relative same-versus-ghost risk difference obeys

\[
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,ghost}|
\le
E\,d_w(G_S,G_{S'}),
\tag{8.4}
\]

so `(8.3)` yields a full **first-moment** deletion certificate.

For four equal fitting blocks this becomes

\[
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,fit\text{-}ghost}|
\le
2\sum_{\ell=1}^4\delta_\ell^{\rm del,1}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Important scope: if the 1,000-row validation block remains shared, `(8.5)` ghosts only the fitting-sample dependence conditional on the validation data. A full pipeline ghost over validation selection also requires refreshing/deleting the validation role. This is exactly consistent with `P15-STRATIFIED-FIXEDOVERLAP-36`.

The direct hybrid theorem `(6.4)` is usually preferable for the source because it certifies the desired sign without needing a full ghost interpretation.

\[

\qquad
D_r^{\rm ref}=2r,
\qquad

\tag{9.1}
\]

The full ghost distance is

\[

\tag{9.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
V(F)
=
\frac nrD_r^{\rm ref}
=
\frac{2n}{r}\Delta_r^{\rm del}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The universal constant in `(5.1)` cannot be improved.

## 9.2 Nonlinear sample-size bias is real

Let `n=4`, delete one coordinate, `Z_i` iid Rademacher, and

\[

\]

Exact enumeration gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_1^{\rm del}=13,
\qquad
D_1^{\rm ref}=24,
\qquad
B_1^{\rm size}=1.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Indeed, if `T` is the sum of the three retained signs, then

\[

## Independent derivation

Fix an ordered disjoint support tuple

\[
S_0,\ldots,S_{k-1},
\qquad
s_j=|S_j|\ge1.
\]

For a phase-marked projective cycle, the active equations use the Gaussian blocks

\[
A_j=W_{S_{j+1},S_j}
\]

and close through the cyclic product. Almost surely the relevant real spectral candidates are simple and have no zero coordinates.

Diagonal sign conjugations of the internal Gaussian blocks preserve their joint law and act transitively on the coordinate sign patterns of all active phase vectors modulo one global sign. Therefore a real candidate has all active phases sign-definite with expected factor

\[
2^{-(\sum_j s_j-1)}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

The inactive inequalities use

\[
kN-\sum_j s_j
\]

Gaussian row-dot-products. Pairwise disjointness of the supports makes the corresponding matrix entries disjoint across phases and disjoint from the internal blocks. After conditioning on the internal cycle, each inactive sign constraint contributes exactly `1/2`, for total factor

\[
2^{-(kN-\sum_j s_j)}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Multiplying `(10.3)` and `(10.4)` gives the support-independent orthant factor

\[
2^{-(kN-1)}.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Now flip the sign of one Gaussian internal block. The cyclic product changes sign in law, so positive and negative nonzero real eigenvalue counts have the same expectation. Since the product rank is at most

\[
\min_j s_j,
\]

the expected positive-real count is at most

\[
\frac12\min_j s_j.
\tag{10.6}
\]

Thus a fixed support tuple contributes at most

\[
2^{-kN}\min_j s_j.
\tag{10.7}
\]

Sum over ordered pairwise-disjoint supports. Assign every coordinate one of `k+1` labels: unused or one of the `k` phases. Using

\[
\min_j s_j\le \frac1k\sum_j s_j,
\]

and symmetry among the `k` active labels,

\[
\sum_{\text{ordered disjoint tuples}}\min_j s_j
\le
\frac1k N k (k+1)^{N-1}
=
N(k+1)^{N-1}.
\tag{10.8}
\]

Combining `(10.7)`--`(10.8)` proves `(10.1)`.

\frac N3(3/4)^N.

Pratik Patil and Jin-Hong Du, **Generalized equivalences between subsampling and ridge regularization**, arXiv:2305.18496.
