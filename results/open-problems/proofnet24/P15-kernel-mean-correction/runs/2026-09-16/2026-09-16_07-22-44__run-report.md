# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**, but this run proves a practical no-new-solver bridge from a genuinely independent-data correction benchmark to the deployed full-sample same-observation learner. The bridge is stated both for squared population risk and for the manuscript's actual unsquared mean-relative metric.

The fixed-population identity remains exactly

\[
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j
\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2,
\qquad r=f^\star-m.
\tag{0.1}
\]

It is retained only for a **fixed mean** `m` and a **fixed population kernel operator**. The recovered shortlist says this is “elementary, not a new conjecture” and separately identifies the harder problem in which the mean, representation and correction are trained on the same observations. No theorem below relabels `(0.1)` as a same-sample learning theorem.

The principal new construction is a **role-preserving disjoint split** of the already available labeled sample. It differs from the preceding Gaussian-OU benchmark in a useful way: it needs no new PDE/solver evaluations.

Let the full training/model-selection sample be `S`. For the structural-mechanics high-data protocol, condition on the realized role assignment and split each role by predetermined indices into disjoint halves:

- fitting role: `19000 = 9500 + 9500`;
- validation role: `1000 = 500 + 500`.

Call the resulting complete role-preserving samples `S_A` and `S_B`. Because the original observations are iid and the split is fixed independently of their values, `S_A` and `S_B` are independent. Every normalization, learned mean, learned representation, checkpoint, stack, kernel/ridge selection and residual correction in an `A`-learner must use **only A**, and similarly for `B`. Algorithmic randomness must be fixed or independently generated between the two halves; shared random seeds treated as random variables would otherwise create an avoidable coupling.

For any complete learner define

\[
b_S=\text{uncorrected KRR comparator trained on }S,
\qquad
f_S=\text{corrected learner trained on }S,
\]

and

\[
g_S=f_S-b_S.
\]

Likewise form `b_A,f_A,g_A` and `b_B,f_B,g_B`. The genuinely independent split hybrids are

\[
h_{AB}=b_A+g_B,
\qquad
h_{BA}=b_B+g_A.
\tag{0.2}
\]

The correction increment `g_B` may internally be an arbitrarily complicated same-B learned object. What matters is that it is independent of the data used to train `b_A`. Thus `(0.2)` is a clean benchmark for separating the comparator/correction training samples without pretending that every internal stage of the correction is itself cross-fitted.

The source-metric theorem is:

### `P15-DISJOINT-SPLIT-MEANREL-40` — PROVED

For

\[
\ell_{\rm rel}(f;u,y)
=
\frac{\|y-f(u)\|_2}{\|y\|_2},
\qquad
R_{\rm rel}(f)=E\,\ell_{\rm rel}(f;U,Y),
\]

define

\[
\delta_b^A
=
E\frac{\|b_S(U)-b_A(U)\|_2}{\|Y\|_2},
\qquad
\delta_g^B
=
E\frac{\|g_S(U)-g_B(U)\|_2}{\|Y\|_2}.
\tag{0.3}
\]

Let

\[
\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}
=
R_{\rm rel}(b_A)-R_{\rm rel}(h_{AB})
\]

be the genuinely independent-half benchmark gain, and

\[
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
=
R_{\rm rel}(b_S)-R_{\rm rel}(f_S)
\]

the desired full-sample same-observation gain. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}
-2\delta_b^A
-\delta_g^B.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

The reversed orientation satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\Gamma_{BA}^{\rm rel}
-2\delta_b^B
-\delta_g^A.
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Averaging the two valid lower bounds gives

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\frac{\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}+\Gamma_{BA}^{\rm rel}}2
-
(\delta_b^A+\delta_b^B)
-
\frac{\delta_g^A+\delta_g^B}{2}.
}
\tag{0.6}
\]

No response-noise model, Gaussianity, differentiability, RKHS-membership assumption, or global lower bound on `||Y||` is used. The only integrability requirement is finiteness of the displayed first moments.

The squared-risk version is best stated in root-risk form. Let

\[
\mathcal R_2(f)=\big(E\|Y-f(U)\|_2^2\big)^{1/2},
\qquad
d_2(f,h)=\big(E\|f(U)-h(U)\|_2^2\big)^{1/2}.
\]

Define

\[
M_{AB}=\mathcal R_2(b_A)-\mathcal R_2(h_{AB}),
\quad
\Delta_b^A=d_2(b_S,b_A),
\quad
\Delta_g^B=d_2(g_S,g_B).
\]

Then:

### `P15-DISJOINT-SPLIT-L2-40` — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_2(b_S)-\mathcal R_2(f_S)
\ge
M_{AB}-2\Delta_b^A-\Delta_g^B.
}
\tag{0.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
M_{AB}>2\Delta_b^A+\Delta_g^B
\tag{0.8}
\]

is a rigorous finite-sample sufficient condition that the full same-observation corrected learner has **strictly smaller fresh squared risk** than full-sample uncorrected KRR. The analogous `BA` and averaged bounds hold as above.

The proof of both theorems is only the reverse triangle inequality in the appropriate prediction space, but the result is useful because it moves all dependence difficulty into directly measurable nested-sample prediction movements.

For the fixed public mechanics test block, the exact same inequality holds deterministically with expectations replaced by test averages. In particular, writing hats for empirical mean-relative quantities,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\widehat\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}
-2\widehat\delta_b^A
-\widehat\delta_g^B.
}
\tag{0.9}
\]

This is `P15-HALFSPLIT-FINITE-TEST-40 = PROVED`. It is a statement about the realized 20,000-case public test table, not a new population confidence theorem.

Crucially, no separately trained “hybrid network” is required. From complete prediction arrays only,

\[
g_S=f_S-b_S,
\qquad
g_A=f_A-b_A,
\qquad
g_B=f_B-b_B,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
h_{AB}=b_A+f_B-b_B,\qquad h_{BA}=b_B+f_A-b_A.}
\tag{0.10}
\]

Thus two complete role-preserving half-pipeline retrains plus the existing full-pipeline predictions are enough to compute every term of `(0.6)` or `(0.9)`. This is `P15-HALFSPLIT-ARRAY-CONTRACT-40 = PROVED`.

A sharp counterexample shows why the movement debt in `(0.4)` cannot be dropped. Let the target be the constant `Y=1` and let every comparator be zero. Suppose the half-sample correction algorithm outputs `g_A=g_B=1`, so the independent split hybrid is perfect and

\[
\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}=1.
\]

Let the full-sample correction algorithm output `g_S=3`. Then

\[
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}=1-2=-1,
\qquad
\delta_b^A=0,
\qquad
\delta_g^B=2,
\]

and `(0.4)` is attained with equality:

\[
-1=1-2.
\]

Therefore an independent half-split improvement does **not** automatically transfer to the full learner even in a problem with no subtle stochastic dependence at all; sample-size/algorithm movement is an essential term. This is

`P15-HALFSPLIT-NOSTABILITY-CE-40 = PROVED COUNTEREXAMPLE`.

This matters for the source because the KRR objective itself uses `K+n lambda I`. Halving the fitting sample while keeping numerical `lambda` fixed changes the matrix shift from `19000 lambda` to `9500 lambda`, before any neural or selection effect is considered. The previous source-normalization theorem already showed that sample-size movement cannot be silently identified with a fixed-`rho` update.

The new result gives a practical benchmark hierarchy:

1. **full same-observation learner:** desired object;
2. **role-preserving disjoint split hybrid:** genuinely independent comparator/correction training without new solver evaluations;
3. **full independent ghost / Gaussian OU replica:** theoretically cleaner, but requires fresh solver labels;
4. **leave-fold deletion:** no-new-solver stability route, but not itself an independent-data correction benchmark.

The disjoint-split theorem is complementary to, not a replacement for, the stronger deletion and OU identities retained from prior runs.

# 3. Exact theorem: disjoint-split mean-relative transfer

We now give the full proof of `(0.4)`.

Let

\[
L(f;u,y)=\frac{\|y-f(u)\|_2}{\|y\|_2}.
\]

For any two predictions `p,q`, the reverse triangle inequality gives pointwise

\[
|L(p;u,y)-L(q;u,y)|
\le
\frac{\|p(u)-q(u)\|_2}{\|y\|_2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

First compare full and half baselines:

\[
R_{\rm rel}(b_S)
\ge
R_{\rm rel}(b_A)-\delta_b^A.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Next note

\[
f_S-h_{AB}
=(b_S-b_A)+(g_S-g_B).
\]

Applying `(3.1)` and then the ordinary triangle inequality,

\[
R_{\rm rel}(f_S)
\le
R_{\rm rel}(h_{AB})
+\delta_b^A+\delta_g^B.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Subtract `(3.3)` from `(3.2)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
&=R_{\rm rel}(b_S)-R_{\rm rel}(f_S)\\
&\ge
R_{\rm rel}(b_A)-R_{\rm rel}(h_{AB})
-2\delta_b^A-\delta_g^B\\
&=
\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}-2\delta_b^A-\delta_g^B.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(0.4)`. Exchanging `A,B` proves `(0.5)` and averaging proves `(0.6)`.

### Independence scope

The inequality itself is pathwise/metric and does not require independence. Independence is needed only for the **interpretation** of `h_AB=b_A+g_B` as a clean independent-data benchmark. That interpretation is valid when:

1. `S_A` and `S_B` are disjoint subsets chosen by a deterministic/data-independent role-preserving split of iid observations;
2. each learner sees only its own half, including its standardization statistics, OOF construction, validation choices and residual labels;
3. any algorithmic randomness is fixed in advance or independently generated across halves.

If a hyperparameter, centering mean, normalization statistic, checkpoint decision or shared random variable is computed from the full 20,000-case pool and then reused in both halves, the benchmark is no longer independent in the intended sense. The metric inequality remains true, but the “independent correction” interpretation must be withdrawn.

### Source fidelity

For the source, the clean split should preserve roles rather than simply split the 20,000 rows and reuse the full validation decisions. A concrete protocol is:

- fix the existing 19,000 fitting positions and 1,000 validation positions;
- partition the fitting positions into `A_fit,B_fit`, each 9,500;
- partition validation positions into `A_val,B_val`, each 500;
- recompute standardization from each half's fitting data;
- rebuild OOF kernel fields inside each half using fold assignments internal to that half;
- train/refit all neural members used in the half, including the kernel-conditioned refiner;
- perform stacking and all accepted/per-pixel decisions only on that half's 500 validation rows;
- select kernel/ridge scales only from that half's available data and use the source normalization with that half's fitting size;
- evaluate both half pipelines only on the unchanged public test block.

This is more expensive than a fixed-layer K-fold algebra update, but it is exactly what is required if the goal is an independence benchmark for the whole learned system rather than only the last KRR layer.

# 4. Exact theorem: squared-risk / Hilbert transfer

Let

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(P_{U,Y};\mathbb R^q)
\]

for the error functions. Then

\[
\mathcal R_2(f)=\|Y-f(U)\|_{\mathcal H}.
\]

The reverse triangle inequality in `mathcal H` gives

\[
|\mathcal R_2(f)-\mathcal R_2(h)|
\le
d_2(f,h).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal R_2(b_S)
\ge
\mathcal R_2(b_A)-\Delta_b^A,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and, since

\[
f_S-h_{AB}=(b_S-b_A)+(g_S-g_B),
\]

Minkowski gives

\[
d_2(f_S,h_{AB})
\le
\Delta_b^A+\Delta_g^B.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal R_2(f_S)
\le
\mathcal R_2(h_{AB})+\Delta_b^A+\Delta_g^B.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Subtracting proves `(0.7)`.

If the right side of `(0.7)` is positive, then

\[
\mathcal R_2(f_S)<\mathcal R_2(b_S),
\]

and monotonicity of squaring on nonnegative numbers gives

\[
E\|Y-f_S(U)\|^2
<
E\|Y-b_S(U)\|^2.
\]

Thus `(0.8)` is a genuine squared-risk sign theorem, despite being expressed through root-risk quantities.

### Why root risk is used

A direct expansion of squared risks introduces signed inner products with the half-sample residual and with both baseline/correction sample-size movements. Those terms can be useful if their signs are controlled, but there is no universal sign in the source-level arbitrary learner. The Hilbert root-risk form isolates only prediction movements and therefore preserves a theorem without bounded-response or covariance-sign assumptions.

# 6. Sharp obstruction: split gain without size stability does not transfer

The counterexample in the executive section is deliberately minimal because it isolates the logical obstruction.

Let the query space consist of one point and target `Y=1`. Let all baselines be the zero predictor. Let the algorithm family depend on training sample size:

- at half size `m`, output correction increment `g_m=1`;
- at full size `n`, output correction increment `g_n=3`.

Then for any disjoint halves `A,B`,

\[
h_{AB}=1,
\]

so

\[
R_{\rm rel}(b_A)=1,
\qquad
R_{\rm rel}(h_{AB})=0,
\qquad
\Gamma_{AB}^{\rm rel}=1.
\]

But the deployed full corrected predictor is `3`, giving

\[
R_{\rm rel}(f_S)=2,
\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}=1-2=-1.
\]

The correction movement is

\[
\delta_g^B=|3-1|=2,
\]

and the theorem is exactly tight.

Therefore the route

> “train the correction on an independent half, observe improvement, then conclude the full same-sample correction improves”

is **DISPROVED** without an explicit sample-size/algorithm-stability bridge.

The example does not claim that the mechanics pipeline behaves this way. It proves that the missing movement term is logically necessary. In the source, a real size perturbation is already present at the last KRR stage because the normalization is `K+n lambda I`, so no source-faithful argument may simply identify the half-size and full-size estimators.

# 7. Relation to inherited deletion, overlap and Gaussian-replica theory

The new split theorem fits the prior P15 machinery as follows.

## 7.1 Relation to deletion stability

`b_A` is a nested-sample deletion retrain relative to `b_S`; likewise `g_A` versus `g_S`. Thus the quantities

\[
\Delta_b^A,
\quad
\Delta_g^A
\]

are exactly the kind of full-to-reduced prediction movements controlled abstractly by the inherited deletion theorems.

The preceding deletion/refresh Pythagoras theorem gave, for an arbitrary learner `F_n` and deleted block `R`,

\[
\Delta_R^{\rm del}
=
\frac12D_R^{\rm ref}+B_R^{\rm size},
\]

so deletion movement automatically contains both true observation-refresh sensitivity and sample-size algorithm drift. That is precisely the debt the half-split transfer theorem needs rather than ignores.

## 7.2 Relation to OU/Hermite replicas

The prior Gaussian-OU theorem creates an exactly correlated Gaussian training replica with the correct source marginal law and diagonalizes Wiener-chaos interaction orders. It is theoretically sharper about reuse covariance, but its numerical use requires new solver labels at correlated loads.

The disjoint split loses half the fitting and validation sample in each arm but uses only existing labeled observations. It is therefore a practical benchmark for the current archive.

## 7.3 Relation to ordinary K-fold cross-fitting

The clean independent-half hybrid is not ordinary K-fold prediction. In particular, source OOF kernel fields do not make the entire refiner/pipeline independent of held-out rows because:

- historical target centering used the pooled fitting mean;
- the refiner itself is trained on all fitting rows;
- stacking and kernel/ridge selection use validation information;
- the deployed query-time kernel channel differs from the training OOF channel.

The half-split theorem requires all those stages to be recomputed within the half if independence is claimed.

Set

\sigma^2=\frac{\alpha}{2N}.
\]

Conditional on `S,x`, the vector `Ax` is Gaussian. Direct covariance calculation gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}((Ax)_i)
=
\sigma^2\sum_{j\ne i}x_j^2
=
\sigma^2(1-x_i^2),
\]

and for `i\ne j`, the only shared skew entry contributes

\[
\operatorname{Cov}((Ax)_i,(Ax)_j)
=-\sigma^2x_ix_j.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Ax)
=
\sigma^2(I-xx^T).
\tag{8.2}
\]

For active `i in I`, the positivity constraint is locally inactive. The KKT stationarity equation on the unit sphere is

\[
(Sx)_i=qx_i.
\tag{8.3}
\]

It remains to prove the scalar Gaussian inequality. For `Z~N(0,1)` and `a>=0`, define

\[
h(a)=E[((a+Z)_+-a)^2].
\]

Splitting at `Z=-a` gives

\[
h(a)=\Phi(a)-a\phi(a)+a^2\Phi(-a).
\]

Differentiation yields

\[
h'(a)=2a\Phi(-a)\ge0,
\]

so

\[
h(a)\ge h(0)=\frac12.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Hence, if `Y~N(0,tau^2)` and `c>=0`,

\[
E[((c+Y)_+-c)^2]\ge\frac{\tau^2}{2}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

On the active support, `(Wx)_i=qx_i+(Ax)_i`; the hypothesis `q>=0` ensures `qx_i>=0`. Apply `(8.5)` with

\[
\tau_i^2=\sigma^2(1-x_i^2).
\]

Summing active coordinates and discarding nonnegative off-support residuals,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_A\|\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)-qx\|^2
&\ge
\frac{\sigma^2}{2}
\sum_{i\in I}(1-x_i^2)\\
&=
\frac{\sigma^2}{2}(k-1)\\
&=
\frac{\alpha(k-1)}{4N}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is `(8.1)`.

## Audit verdict

\[
\boxed{\texttt{PASS}.}
\]

The `q>=0` condition is genuinely used in the scalar ReLU step and is present in the peer proof immediately before the lemma. The subsequent high-probability P16 theorem works on `q>1`, so the scope is satisfied there. No independence is assumed between an adaptive `x` and the full `W`; rather, `x` is explicitly required to be measurable with respect to `S`, and the remaining skew matrix `A` is independent of `S`. This is a valid component audit, not one of the two complete end-to-end OTHER-worker audits required for P15 closure.

# 10. High-dimensional reduction sharpened

The new finite-sample theorem suggests a different deterministic-equivalent target that is more operational than reconstructing the entire same-sample law directly.

Fix a proportional half/sample ratio

\[
\theta=m/n\in(0,1),
\]

with source benchmark `theta=1/2` for the role-preserving split. Let

\[
M_{\rm split,n}
=
\mathcal R_2(b_A)-\mathcal R_2(b_A+g_B),
\]

and nested-sample movements

\[
\Delta_{b,n}=d_2(b_S,b_A),
\qquad
\Delta_{g,n}=d_2(g_S,g_B).
\]

Then the finite theorem gives

\[
\mathcal R_2(b_S)-\mathcal R_2(f_S)
\ge
M_{\rm split,n}-2\Delta_{b,n}-\Delta_{g,n}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Therefore:

### `P15-RF-DISJOINT-SPLIT-DE-40` — CONDITIONAL REDUCTION

If a high-dimensional model proves deterministic equivalents

\[
M_{\rm split,n}\to M_\infty,
\qquad
\Delta_{b,n}\to D_{b,\infty},
\qquad
\Delta_{g,n}\to D_{g,\infty},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
M_\infty>2D_{b,\infty}+D_{g,\infty},
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

then the full same-observation corrected learner eventually beats the full uncorrected learner in squared fresh risk.

For fixed-feature linear/KRR baselines, existing subsampling/ridge and KRR deterministic-equivalent machinery may attack `M_split` and `Delta_b`. The unresolved source-specific object is `Delta_g`, because the correction increment includes the jointly trained neural mean, learned representation, hard selection and residual correction. Moniri–Hassani shows why that learned-feature nested-sample law is not safely replaceable by a pretrained/random-feature law.

For the manuscript's mean-relative metric one can make the identical reduction with the first-moment movements in `(0.3)`; no squared inverse-target norm is required.

This target complements the entering `P15-RF-GAUSSIAN-REPLICA-DE-40`: the Gaussian two-replica law is theoretically natural, while the disjoint nested-sample law is experimentally accessible from the existing labeled archive.
