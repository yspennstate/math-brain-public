# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**, but this run gives a new exact finite-sample interpolation between full same-observation reuse and the independent-sample ghost benchmark. It packages the all-orders Hoeffding/replacement hierarchy from the preceding run into a directly measurable one-parameter **Bernoulli independent-refresh path**.

This is useful for the user's central target because the theorem permits the mean, representation, optimizer/checkpoint, kernel family, ridge parameter, stacking rule, and correction to depend on the **same observations**. It does not require a fixed representation, Gaussian response noise, or differentiability.

The fixed-population identity remains strictly in its original scope:

\[
\boxed{
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2,
\qquad r=f^\star-m.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

It is an elementary fixed-`m`, fixed-`K` population ridge-bias identity and is **not** relabeled as a same-sample learning theorem.

Let the training sample be the independent product sample

\[
S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n),
\]

where for the deterministic mechanics source the correct observation is the **complete pair**

\[
Z_i=(U_i,G(U_i)).
\]

Let `B_S` denote the uncorrected learner and write the corrected learner as

\[
F_S=B_S+G_S,
\]

where `G_S` is the complete learned correction increment relative to `B_S`.

For `p in [0,1]`, independently for every coordinate replace `Z_i` by an iid copy `Z_i'` with probability `p`; otherwise retain it. Call the resulting sample `S^[p]`, and put

\[
q=1-p.
\]

This is **not** ordinary K-fold cross-fitting. It is a mathematically exact independent-refresh/overlap coupling. `p=0` is full same-sample reuse and `p=1` is the independent-sample ghost.

If

\[
B-EB=\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}B_A,
\qquad
G-EG=\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}G_A
\]

are their Hilbert-valued Hoeffding decompositions, write

\[
c_A=E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle,
\qquad
e_A(F)=E\|F_A\|^2.
\]

Then the coupling diagonalizes **exactly** by interaction order:

\[
\boxed{
E\langle B_S-EB,\,G_{S^{[p]}}-EG\rangle
=
\sum_A q^{|A|}c_A.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
D_p(F)
:=E\|F_S-F_{S^{[p]}}\|^2
=
2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})e_A(F).
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Define the signed paired movement

\[
H_p(B,G)
:=E\langle B_S-B_{S^{[p]}},\,G_S-G_{S^{[p]}}\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
H_p(B,G)=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})c_A.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

A role-preserving extension is also exact. If coordinate `i` is independently refreshed with its own probability `p_i`, let `q_i=1-p_i`. Then every interaction `A` is attenuated by

Q_A=\prod_{i\in A}q_i,

D_{\mathbf p}(F)=2\sum_A(1-Q_A)e_A(F),
\qquad
H_{\mathbf p}(B,G)=2\sum_A(1-Q_A)c_A.

\[
\boxed{
E\|F_S-F_{S'}\|^2
\le
\frac{D_{\mathbf p}(F)}{p_*}.

\tag{0.18}

This is useful for the source because train/validation/fold **roles can remain fixed by index** while fresh iid pairs are inserted at different positive rates by role. If some role has refresh probability zero, interactions supported entirely inside that role are invisible and no full ghost certificate follows.

and let

\[
B=Z_1+Z_1Z_2,
\qquad
G=t+Z_1-2Z_1Z_2.
\]

The uncorrected risk is

\[
R(B)=t^2+E B^2
=\frac{71}{10}=7.1.
\]

For the `p`-refresh correction, with `q=1-p`,

\[
\boxed{
R_p=7+2q-4q^2.
}
\tag{0.19}

Therefore

\[
R_0=5<7.1,
\qquad
R_1=7<7.1,

\boxed{

\tag{0.20}

Thus making the correction “more cross-fitted” is **not a monotone improvement path**. Mixed-sign first- and second-order baseline/correction interactions can make an intermediate overlap strictly worse than both endpoints. # 4. Bernoulli independent-refresh coupling

Let `(Z_i,Z_i')`, `i=1,...,n`, be iid pairs, with `Z_i'` an independent copy of `Z_i`. Let `delta_i iid Bernoulli(p)`, independent of everything, and define

\[
Z_i^{[p]}
=
\begin{cases}
Z_i,&\delta_i=0,\\
Z_i',&\delta_i=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
S^{[p]}=(Z_1^{[p]},\ldots,Z_n^{[p]})
\]

has exactly the same product marginal distribution as `S`, but shares each original coordinate with probability `q=1-p`.

The coupling is exchangeable:

\[
(S,S^{[p]})\stackrel d=(S^{[p]},S).
\]

This exchangeability is used below only to identify the two cross terms in paired differences.

Let `H` be any real Hilbert space. In the squared-risk theorem we take

\[
H=L^2(P_U;\mathbb R^q).
\]

For the source metric we take the weighted Hilbert space when the inverse-square weighted moment is finite.

# 5. Interaction diagonalization

Let

\[
F-EF=\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}F_A,
\qquad
G-EG=\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}G_A
\]

be Hoeffding decompositions on the product sample.

## 5.1 Matching components

Fix `A`. Conditional on the Bernoulli refresh indicators, if **any** coordinate of `A` is refreshed, then the corresponding original and refreshed Hoeffding components are orthogonal: centering in the refreshed coordinate makes the cross expectation zero. If no coordinate of `A` is refreshed, the two components are identical.

Therefore

\[
E\langle F_A(S),G_A(S^{[p]})\rangle
=q^{|A|}E\langle F_A,G_A\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `A != C`, choose a coordinate belonging to one subset but not the other; Hoeffding centering in that coordinate gives

\[
E\langle F_A(S),G_C(S^{[p]})\rangle=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Summing `(5.1)--(5.2)` proves

\[
E\langle F_S-EF,G_{S^{[p]}}-EG\rangle
=
\sum_Aq^{|A|}E\langle F_A,G_A\rangle.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This proves `(0.2)`.

## 5.2 Self-distance

Using equal marginals and `(5.3)` with `F=G`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_p(F)
&=E\|F_S-F_{S^{[p]}}\|^2\\
&=2E\|F-EF\|^2
-2E\langle F_S-EF,F_{S^{[p]}}-EF\rangle\\
&=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})e_A(F).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(0.3)`.

## 5.3 Signed paired movement

Likewise, exchangeability gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_p(B,G)
&=E\langle B_S-B_{S^{[p]}},G_S-G_{S^{[p]}}\rangle\\
&=2E\langle B-EB,G-EG\rangle
-2E\langle B_S-EB,G_{S^{[p]}}-EG\rangle\\
&=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})c_A.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(0.4)`.

# 6. `P15-PARTIAL-GHOST-RISK-IDENTITY-35`

\[
R_p
=E\|Y-B_S(U)-G_{S^{[p]}}(U)\|^2.
\]

\[
R_p-R_1
=2E\langle B_S-EB,G_{S^{[p]}}-EG\rangle
=2\sum_Aq^{|A|}c_A.
\tag{6.1}
\]

At `p=0`,

\[
R_0-R_p
=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})c_A
=H_p(B,G).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Equations `(0.5)--(0.6)` follow.

Finally,

\[
|H_p(B,G)|
\le
\sqrt{
E\|B_S-B_{S^{[p]}}\|^2
}
\sqrt{
E\|G_S-G_{S^{[p]}}\|^2
}
\]

proves `(0.7)--(0.8)`.

# 7. `P15-BERNOULLI-REPLACEMENT-GENERATOR-35`

The preceding run proved

\[
C_r(B,G)
=2^r\sum_A\binom{|A|}{r}c_A
\]

and

\[
D_r(F)
=2^r\sum_A\binom{|A|}{r}e_A(F).
\]

Use

\[
1-(1-p)^k
=
\sum_{r=1}^k(-1)^{r+1}\binom{k}{r}p^r.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_p(B,G)
&=2\sum_A[1-(1-p)^{|A|}]c_A\\
&=\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}p^rC_r(B,G),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is `(0.9)`. The self-pair calculation gives `(0.10)`.

Differentiating at `p=0` gives `(0.11)`. Thus the all-orders replacement tensors are the Taylor coefficients of a single overlap path.

At `p=1`, `(0.9)` becomes

exactly the preceding same-vs-independent-ghost squared-risk reuse debt.

### Fixed-design KRR specialization

If the design, kernel, and ridge parameter are fixed and zero-mean KRR is viewed only as a function of independent response rows, then the baseline is additive and has Hoeffding degree one. Hence only `|A|=1` can contribute to matching baseline/correction covariance and

Therefore any single `p>0` identifies the full reuse debt in that restricted benchmark. This exact linear interpolation generally fails when the representation/design/kernel is learned from the same complete pairs.

# 8. `P15-SELF-REFRESH-CONCAVITY-35` and source-metric transfer

From `(0.3)`, for an interaction of size `k`, the coefficient is

\[
2[1-(1-p)^k].
\]

Its first derivative is

\[
2k(1-p)^{k-1}\ge0
\]

and its second derivative is

\[
-2k(k-1)(1-p)^{k-2}\le0.
\]

Since every self-energy `e_A(F)` is nonnegative, `D_p(F)` is nondecreasing and concave.

For any nonnegative concave `f` with `f(0)=0`, `f(p)/p` is nonincreasing, so

\[
D_1(F)\le D_p(F)/p.
\]

Also

\[
\frac{1-(1-p)^k}{p}
=1+q+\cdots+q^{k-1}
\le k.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{D_p(F)}p
\le2\sum_A|A|e_A(F)
=
\sum_iE\|F_S-F_{S^{(i)}}\|^2,
\]

proving `(0.12)`.

This gives a useful **implementation check**: an empirical paired-retraining estimate of a self-distance path should be nondecreasing and concave in `p` up to sampling error. The signed squared-risk path need not be monotone; Section 10 gives an exact counterexample.

## 8.1 Mean-relative risk

The manuscript loss is

\[
\ell_{\rm rel}(f;u,y)
=\frac{\|y-f(u)\|}{\|y\|}.
\]

Pointwise reverse triangle inequality gives

\[
|\ell_{\rm rel}(B_S+G_S)-\ell_{\rm rel}(B_S+G_{S^{[p]}})|
\le
\frac{\|G_S-G_{S^{[p]}}\|}{\|Y\|}.
\]

Expectation and Cauchy--Schwarz prove `(0.13)` and the same-observation certificate `(0.14)`.

## 8.2 Full ghost from one partial-refresh experiment

Let

\[
V_G^w=2\sum_Ae_A^w(G).
\]

For every nonempty interaction size `k`,

\[
1-q^k\ge1-q=p,
\]

so

\[
D_p^w(G)
=2\sum_A(1-q^{|A|})e_A^w
\ge

The exact overpayment is

\frac{D_p^w(G)}p-V_G^w

# 9. Role-preserving heterogeneous refresh

Let coordinate `i` be refreshed independently with probability `p_i`. For interaction `A`, the event that every coordinate is retained has probability

\[
Q_A=\prod_{i\in A}(1-p_i).
\]

Repeating the proof of Section 5 gives exactly

\[
E\langle F_A(S),G_A(S^{[\mathbf p]})\rangle
=Q_AE\langle F_A,G_A\rangle.
\]

Hence `(0.17)` follows.

If

\[
p_*:=\min_i p_i>0,
\]

then for every nonempty `A`,

\[
1-Q_A\ge p_*.
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{\mathbf p}(F)
=2\sum_A(1-Q_A)e_A
\ge
p_*\,2\sum_Ae_A,
\]

which is `(0.18)`.

### Source interpretation

This does not license data leakage. It gives a theoretical way to preserve deterministic **roles** while refreshing observations:

- fitting-row positions remain fitting positions;
- validation positions remain validation positions;
- fold labels remain attached to positions;
- each refreshed position receives a fresh iid complete pair from the source distribution.

That preserves the algorithmic role map while perturbing data. Numerically applying it to the source requires fresh simulator pairs or an otherwise valid independent pool. The fixed public test set should not be repurposed as training data merely to estimate this theorem.

## 11.2 Current primary literature boundary

The current literature was rechecked against that obligation.

1. **Misiakiewicz--Saeed, arXiv:2403.08938**, *A non-asymptotic theory of Kernel Ridge Regression: deterministic equivalents, test error, and GCV estimator*. It gives non-asymptotic KRR deterministic equivalents under spectral/concentration conditions on the kernel eigendecomposition and target alignment. ### Claim

For an iid-entry `N x m` matrix, put `n=m+r` and let one iid product row be `R`. Define

\[
\mathcal A_m(a)
=
\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}P\{|Rx|\le a\}.
\]

\[
P\{M_{m,r}(A)\le\sqrt n\,a\}
\ge
P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,\mathcal A_m(a))\ge n\}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

### Independent derivation

Fix any deterministic unit vector `x` and write

\[
q_x=P\{|Rx|\le a\}.
\]

The row indicators

\[
I_i=1_{\{|R_i x|\le a\}}
\]

are iid Bernoulli(`q_x`). If at least `n` indicators equal one, choose any `n` of those rows as `T`. Then

\[
\|A_Tx\|_2^2
=\sum_{i\in T}|R_i x|^2
\le na^2,
\]

and therefore

\[
\sigma_m(A_T)
\le\|A_Tx\|_2
\le\sqrt n\,a.
\]

Since `M_(m,r)` is the minimum over all admissible `T`,

\[
P\{M_{m,r}\le\sqrt n a\}
\ge
P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,q_x)\ge n\}.
\]

The binomial upper-tail probability is a continuous nondecreasing polynomial in `q`. Taking a sequence of unit vectors with `q_x -> A_m(a)` therefore gives `(12.1)`.

### Audit nuance

No continuity of the map `x -> q_x` is required; atoms could make such continuity false. The only continuity needed to pass to the supremum is continuity of the finite binomial-tail polynomial in its scalar success probability. This is a proof-wording clarification, not a defect in the theorem.
