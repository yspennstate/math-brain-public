# 5. Same-response double-KRR setup

The previous P15 checkpoint solved the frozen-representation spectral risk of a KRR mean followed by residual KRR. This run asks how robust that same-response conclusion is when the **representation itself is changed or learned using the same labels**.

Fix `n` training observations and a matrix-valued response

\[
Y\in\mathbb R^{n\times q}.
\]

No distributional assumption on `Y` is used in Sections 5--9.

For a PSD Gram matrix `K` and mass `a>0`, write

\[
R_a(K)=(K+aI)^{-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `rho>0` be the correction ridge mass and `nu>0` the mean ridge mass. The source normalization corresponds to `rho=n lambda`; if the mean is itself source-normalized KRR, `nu=n lambda_mean`.

For a query `u`, let `k(u)` be the training-query kernel vector.

The zero-mean KRR comparator is

\[
b_K(u)=k(u)^TR_\rho(K)Y.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The KRR mean is

\[
m_K(u)=k(u)^TR_\nu(K)Y,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and its training-row mean vector is

\[
M_K=KR_\nu(K)Y.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Residual KRR on the **same response matrix** uses

\[
Y-M_K
=(I-KR_\nu(K))Y
=\nu R_\nu(K)Y.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_K(u)
&=m_K(u)+k(u)^TR_\rho(K)(Y-M_K)\\
&=k(u)^T\left[R_\nu(K)+\nu R_\rho(K)R_\nu(K)\right]Y.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Define

\[
A_{\rho,\nu}(K)
:=R_\nu(K)+\nu R_\rho(K)R_\nu(K).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Because both resolvents are functions of the same PSD matrix, they commute, but the perturbation proof below does not assume that `K` commutes with its perturbed counterpart.

The training-row transfer is

\[
KA_{\rho,\nu}(K)
=I-\rho\nu R_\rho(K)R_\nu(K),
\tag{5.8}
\]

the inherited double-KRR rational filter.

# 6. `P15-DOUBLEKRR-LEARNEDREP-MARGIN-44` — PROVED

## 6.1 Statement

Let `K` and `K_tilde` be arbitrary PSD `n x n` matrices on the same response rows, and let `k(u)`, `k_tilde(u)` be arbitrary corresponding query vectors. They may be random and may be learned using `Y`; this theorem is pathwise after they are realized.

Fix `rho,nu>0`. Put

\[
\varepsilon=\|\widetilde K-K\|_{op},
\quad
\delta_k(u)=\|\widetilde k(u)-k(u)\|_2,
\quad
q(u)=\min\{\|k(u)\|_2,\|\widetilde k(u)\|_2\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then:

### Resolvents

\[
\boxed{
\|R_a(\widetilde K)-R_a(K)\|_{op}
\le \frac{\varepsilon}{a^2}
\qquad(a>0).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Double-KRR coefficient operator

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{\rho,\nu}(K)\|_{op}
\le \frac1\nu+\frac1\rho,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|A_{\rho,\nu}(\widetilde K)-A_{\rho,\nu}(K)\|_{op}
\le C_{\rho,\nu}\varepsilon,
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
C_{\rho,\nu}
=\frac1{\nu^2}+\frac1{\rho^2}+\frac1{\rho\nu}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Prediction movement

\[
\boxed{
\|b_{\widetilde K}(u)-b_K(u)\|_2
\le
\|Y\|_F
\left[
\frac{\delta_k(u)}\rho
+
\frac{q(u)\varepsilon}{\rho^2}
\right].
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|f_{\widetilde K}(u)-f_K(u)\|_2
\le
\|Y\|_F
\left[
\delta_k(u)\left(\frac1\nu+\frac1\rho\right)
+
q(u)C_{\rho,\nu}\varepsilon
\right].
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

No response-independence assumption appears.

## 6.2 Proof of the resolvent bound

The resolvent identity gives

\[
R_a(\widetilde K)-R_a(K)
=R_a(\widetilde K)(K-\widetilde K)R_a(K).
\tag{6.8}
\]

Because both matrices are PSD,

\[
\|R_a(K)\|_{op},\|R_a(\widetilde K)\|_{op}\le \frac1a.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus `(6.2)` follows immediately.

## 6.3 Proof of the double-KRR coefficient bound

First,

\[
\|A_{\rho,\nu}(K)\|_{op}
\le
\|R_\nu(K)\|+
\nu\|R_\rho(K)\|\|R_\nu(K)\|
\le
\frac1\nu+\frac1\rho,
\]

which proves `(6.3)`.

For the perturbation, abbreviate

\[
R_a=R_a(K),
\qquad
\widetilde R_a=R_a(\widetilde K).
\]

Then

\[
\widetilde A-A
=(\widetilde R_\nu-R_\nu)
+\nu\left(
\widetilde R_\rho\widetilde R_\nu-R_\rho R_\nu
\right).
\tag{6.10}
\]

Telescope the product:

\[
\widetilde R_\rho\widetilde R_\nu-R_\rho R_\nu
=(\widetilde R_\rho-R_\rho)\widetilde R_\nu
+R_\rho(\widetilde R_\nu-R_\nu).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Apply `(6.2)` and `(6.9)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\widetilde A-A\|
&\le \frac\varepsilon{\nu^2}
+\nu\left[
\frac\varepsilon{\rho^2}\frac1\nu
+\frac1\rho\frac\varepsilon{\nu^2}
\right]\\
&=
\varepsilon\left(
\frac1{\nu^2}+\frac1{\rho^2}+\frac1{\rho\nu}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

No commutativity between `K` and `K_tilde` was used.

## 6.4 Proof of prediction movement

For the baseline,

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{\widetilde K}-b_K
&=(\widetilde k-k)^T\widetilde R_\rho Y
+k^T(\widetilde R_\rho-R_\rho)Y.
\end{aligned}
\]

This gives

\[
\|b_{\widetilde K}-b_K\|
\le
\|Y\|_F
\left[
\frac{\delta_k}\rho+
\frac{\|k\|\varepsilon}{\rho^2}
\right].
\]

The alternate decomposition

\[
(\widetilde k-k)^TR_\rho Y
+\widetilde k^T(\widetilde R_\rho-R_\rho)Y
\]

gives the same first term with `||k_tilde||` in the second. Taking the better bound gives `q(u)` and proves `(6.6)`.

The identical argument with `A` in place of `R_rho`, using `(6.3)--(6.5)`, proves `(6.7)`.

## 6.5 Why this is a same-observation theorem

The proof is deterministic conditional on all realized objects. Therefore `K_tilde` may be the Gram matrix of features learned from exactly the same response matrix `Y` later used by the KRR mean and correction. The theorem pays the resulting dependence through observed/controlled representation movement rather than pretending that the learned kernel is independent.

What it does **not** prove is that a response-trained representation is close to a response-independent reference. That is the remaining learned-feature problem.

# 7. `P15-DOUBLEKRR-RIDGE-DRIFT-44` — PROVED

A source-faithful perturbation theorem must not assume that ridge masses stay fixed if sample size or the selected hyperparameter changes.

Let the reference use `(K,rho,nu)` and the perturbed branch use `(K_tilde,rho_tilde,nu_tilde)`, all masses strictly positive. Define

\[
r=\min\{\rho,\widetilde\rho\},
\qquad
v=\min\{\nu,\widetilde\nu\},
\qquad
\bar\nu=\max\{\nu,\widetilde\nu\},
\tag{7.1}
\]

\[
\delta_\rho=|\widetilde\rho-\rho|,
\qquad
\delta_\nu=|\widetilde\nu-\nu|,
\tag{7.2}
\]

and

\[
d_\rho=\frac{\varepsilon+\delta_\rho}{r^2},
\qquad
d_\nu=\frac{\varepsilon+\delta_\nu}{v^2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The mixed resolvent identity

\[
R_{\widetilde a}(\widetilde K)-R_a(K)
=R_{\widetilde a}(\widetilde K)
\big[(K+aI)-(\widetilde K+\widetilde a I)\big]
R_a(K)
\]

gives

\[
\|R_{\widetilde\rho}(\widetilde K)-R_\rho(K)\|\le d_\rho,
\qquad
\|R_{\widetilde\nu}(\widetilde K)-R_\nu(K)\|\le d_\nu.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For

\[
\widetilde A
=R_{\widetilde\nu}(\widetilde K)
+\widetilde\nu
R_{\widetilde\rho}(\widetilde K)
R_{\widetilde\nu}(\widetilde K),
\]

one has

\[
\|\widetilde A\|
\le \frac1v+\frac1r.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Add and subtract `nu R_{rho_tilde}(K_tilde)R_{nu_tilde}(K_tilde)` and telescope the product. This gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde A-A\|
\le
C_A^{\rm drift},
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
C_A^{\rm drift}
=
 d_\nu
+\frac{\delta_\nu}{rv}
+\bar\nu\left(
\frac{d_\rho}{v}+\frac{d_\nu}{r}
\right).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde b-b\|
\le
\|Y\|_F\left[
\frac{\delta_k}{r}+q\,d_\rho
\right],
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde f-f\|
\le
\|Y\|_F\left[
\delta_k\left(\frac1v+\frac1r\right)
+q\,C_A^{\rm drift}
\right].
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

When the branch is fixed, `delta_rho=delta_nu=0`, `(7.7)` reduces exactly to the coefficient in `(6.4)`.

This repairs two common shortcuts simultaneously:

1. learned representations need both Gram and query-vector control;
2. changing sample size or a selected `lambda` needs ridge-mass control as well.

## 8.1 `P15-DOUBLEKRR-MEANREL-TRANSFER-44` — PROVED

# 10. `P15-DOUBLEKRR-GRAMONLY-QUERY-CE-44` — exact counterexample

The query-vector term in `(6.6)--(6.7)` is not a proof artifact.

Take one training point and scalar output:

\[
K=[1],\qquad Y=[1],\qquad \rho=\nu=1.
\]

Then

\[
R_\rho(K)=R_\nu(K)=\frac12,
\qquad
A_{1,1}(K)
=\frac12+\frac14
=\frac34.
\]

Construct two legitimate one-dimensional feature maps with the same training feature `phi(x_1)=1` and query features

\[
\phi_+(u)=1,
\qquad
\phi_-(u)=-1.
\]

Both have the identical training Gram `K=[1]`; the extended train/query Gram matrices are respectively

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

both PSD. Thus

\[
k_+(u)=1,
\qquad
k_-(u)=-1.
\]

Let the deterministic query target be `G(u)=1`.

For the `+` representation,

\[
b_+=\frac12,
\qquad
f_+=\frac34,
\]

so the unsquared relative gain is

\[
\Gamma_+^{\rm rel}
=\left|1-\frac12\right|-
\left|1-\frac34\right|
=\frac14>0.
\]

For the `-` representation,

\[
b_-=-\frac12,
\qquad
f_-=-\frac34,
\]

and

\[
\Gamma_-^{\rm rel}
=\left|1+\frac12\right|-
\left|1+\frac34\right|
=-\frac14<0.
\]

Thus:

- training Gram perturbation is exactly zero;
- every training eigenvalue and every training spectral functional is identical;
- the same-response mean/correction algebra on the training sample is identical;
- the generalization gain changes sign solely because the learned fresh-query representation changed.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{training-Gram / ESD control alone cannot certify learned-representation gain transfer.}
}
\]

This is a source-relevant obstruction because the source's feature-space correction evaluates the kernel on response-trained neural features at fresh queries.

# 15. Adversarial audit of the new theorem

The strongest potential failure points were checked explicitly.

## 15.1 Noncommuting matrices

The proof of `(6.4)` uses only the resolvent identity and a product telescope. It never assumes `K K_tilde = K_tilde K`. Hence the theorem is valid for arbitrary learned PSD perturbations, not only eigenvalue perturbations in a shared basis.

## 15.2 Same-response dependence

No expectation is taken before the deterministic operator inequalities. `K_tilde`, `k_tilde`, `rho_tilde`, `nu_tilde` may be arbitrary measurable functions of the entire response matrix and the same observations. Dependence is not removed; its effect must appear through the realized movement budget.

## 15.3 Query movement cannot be removed

Section 10 gives an exact sign-flip example with `epsilon=0`. Therefore any attempted simplification of `(0.7)` that drops `delta_k` is false.

## 15.4 Ridge movement cannot be removed

The inherited `P15-FIXED-RHO-DELETION-CE-38` already shows that source-normalized deletion changes `n lambda`; holding the old Gram ridge mass fixed can report zero movement when the correctly normalized deleted fit moves. Section 7 explicitly pays ridge drift.

## 15.5 `||Y||_F` scaling cannot be ignored

The bound is dimensionally honest. In high dimension, an operator perturbation `epsilon=o(1)` alone is not enough if `||Y||_F`, query norms, or inverse ridge masses grow. The correct sufficient condition is `Xi_n=o_P(1)`, not unscaled Gram convergence.

## 15.6 The source mean is not silently replaced

The July source uses neural/stacked means, not a KRR mean. The double-KRR theorem is a controlled benchmark and a perturbative bridge for kernel-learned structured means. Application to the exact neural source requires either a separate movement theorem for the neural mean or the already-proved general mean/delete/seed certificates. This report labels that obligation OPEN/CONDITIONAL rather than inventing equivalence.
