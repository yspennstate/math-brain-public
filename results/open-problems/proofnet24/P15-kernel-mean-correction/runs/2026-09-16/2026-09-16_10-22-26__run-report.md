# 6. `P15-DOUBLEKRR-MODEWISE-ORACLE-43` — PROVED

Specialize the mean itself to KRR with ridge mass `nu>=0`:

\[
s_\nu(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\nu}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The full two-stage same-response transfer is the inherited rational filter

\[
\boxed{
 t_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)
 =
 \frac{\kappa(\kappa+\rho+\nu)}
 {(
 \kappa+\rho)(\kappa+\nu)}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

## 6.1 Exact per-mode gain

Substitute `(6.1)` into `(5.4)`. For one mode,

\[
2s-s^2
=
\frac{\kappa(\kappa+2\nu)}{(\kappa+\nu)^2},
\]

and

\[
2\rho s+\frac{\rho^2s^2}{\kappa}
=
\frac{\rho\kappa(2\kappa+2\nu+\rho)}{(\kappa+\nu)^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\kappa(\rho,\nu)
=
\frac{\rho\kappa}{d(\kappa+\rho)^2(\kappa+\nu)^2}
\left[
\rho(\kappa+2\nu)\theta^2
-
\omega(2\kappa+2\nu+\rho)
\right].
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Every prefactor outside the square bracket is nonnegative. For `kappa>0`, `rho>0`, finite `nu`, it is strictly positive.

Hence the exact fixed-ridge sign threshold is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\kappa(\rho,\nu)>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\theta^2}{\omega}
>
q_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)
:=
\frac{2\kappa+2\nu+\rho}
{\rho(\kappa+2\nu)}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

when `omega>0`; the zero-noise limit is interpreted directly from `(6.3)`.

## 6.2 Spectral shape of the threshold

Differentiate:

\[
\boxed{
q'_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)
=
\frac{2\nu-\rho}
{\rho(\kappa+2\nu)^2}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus:

- if `nu>rho/2`, high-eigenvalue modes require larger SNR;
- if `nu<rho/2`, low-eigenvalue modes require larger SNR;
- if `nu=rho/2`, the threshold is spectrally flat.

The endpoints are

\[
q(0)=\frac1\rho+\frac1{2\nu},
\qquad
q(\infty)=\frac2\rho.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Therefore a sufficient condition making **every** represented mode beneficial is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\theta_j^2}{\omega}
>
\max\left\{
\frac2\rho,
\frac1\rho+\frac1{2\nu}
\right\}
\quad\text{for every active mode }j.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

For equal mean/correction ridge masses `nu=rho`,

\[
q_{\rho,\rho}(\kappa)
=
\frac{2\kappa+3\rho}{\rho(\kappa+2\rho)},
\qquad
\frac{3}{2\rho}
\le q_{\rho,\rho}(\kappa)<\frac2\rho.
\tag{6.8}
\]

## 6.3 Optimize the mean ridge on one mode

As `nu` ranges from `infinity` down to `0`, the smoother coefficient

\[
s_\nu(\kappa)=\frac\kappa{\kappa+\nu}
\]

ranges continuously from `0` to `1`. Thus optimizing over `nu` is equivalent to optimizing `(5.4)` over `s in [0,1]` on that mode.

From `(5.4)`, ignoring the positive common factor `1/[d(\kappa+\rho)^2]`, the mode gain is

\[
2\rho(\rho\theta^2-\omega)s
-
\rho^2\left(\theta^2+\frac\omega\kappa\right)s^2.
\tag{6.9}
\]

It is a strictly concave quadratic unless signal and residual are both zero. Therefore a nonzero mean is beneficial **iff** the derivative at zero is positive:

\[
\boxed{
\rho\theta^2>\omega.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

When `(6.10)` holds, the exact optimal smoother strength is

\[
s^\star
=
\frac{\kappa(\rho\theta^2-\omega)}
{\rho(\kappa\theta^2+\omega)}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

It lies in `(0,1)`. Solving `s^*=kappa/(kappa+nu^*)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\nu^\star
=
\frac{\omega(\kappa+\rho)}
{\rho\theta^2-\omega}.
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

Substitution into the quadratic gives

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\kappa^\star
=
\frac{\kappa(\rho\theta^2-\omega)^2}
{d(\kappa+\rho)^2(\kappa\theta^2+\omega)}.
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

This completes the proof.

### Relation to the earlier spectral oracle

`P15-SAMEOBS-SPECTRAL-ORACLE-24` optimized an arbitrary commuting smoother strength in response space. The present theorem translates that oracle exactly into:

It is therefore a specialization/refinement, not a replacement of the earlier theorem.

# 8. `P15-DETERMINISTIC-CONDITIONAL-PROJECTION-43` — PROVED under explicit fixed-representation hypotheses

The structural-mechanics labels are deterministic solver values. A source-facing bridge should therefore avoid pretending that `omega` is physical response noise.

Let

\[
U_i\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}P_U,
\qquad
Y_i=G(U_i)\in\mathbb R^q
\]

with deterministic `G`. Fix a representation

\[
Z=\phi(U)\in\mathbb R^d
\]

that is chosen independently of the current response sample, and assume

\[
E[ZZ^\top]=\frac1d I_d.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Assume there exists `Theta` such that

\[
E[Y\mid Z]=Z^\top\Theta
\tag{8.2}
\]

and define the representation residual

\[
\eta=Y-Z^\top\Theta.
\]

Assume the explicit conditional second-moment bound

\[
\boxed{
E[\|\eta\|^2\mid Z]\le\omega
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

No stochastic solver is present: conditional randomness in `eta` is simply information in `U` discarded by the fixed representation `phi`.

Train on iid rows `Z_i`, forming `X` and `K=XX^T`, and use the same KRR-mean/KRR-correction system of Section 6.

## Claim

Conditional on `X`, the expected fresh-risk gain of the same-response double-KRR estimator over zero-mean KRR obeys

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm det}(X)
\ge
\frac1d\sum_{j:\kappa_j>0}
\frac{\rho\kappa_j}
{(\kappa_j+\rho)^2(\kappa_j+\nu)^2}
\left[
\rho(\kappa_j+2\nu)\theta_j^2
-
\omega(2\kappa_j+2\nu+\rho)
\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Here `theta_j^2` is the represented signal energy in the corresponding right-singular direction of `X`.

Consequently, the modewise threshold `(6.7)` is a rigorous sufficient condition for improvement even though the original labels are deterministic.

## Proof

Because the observations are iid and `(8.2)` holds, conditional on the design rows `Z_i`, the residual rows `eta_i` are independent and centered. For any fixed spectral coefficient map `q(K)`, the fitted feature coefficient noise is

\[
X^\top q(K)\eta.
\]

Fresh-query isotropy `(8.1)` gives its conditional prediction second moment

\[
\frac1d E\|X^\top q(K)\eta\|_F^2.
\]

Let `q_0` be zero-mean KRR and `q_1` the double-KRR coefficient map. The difference of their noise quadratic forms is

\[
D
=
q_1(K)Kq_1(K)-q_0(K)Kq_0(K).
\tag{8.5}
\]

In a `K` eigenmode its eigenvalue is

\[
\frac{t_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)^2-h_\rho(\kappa)^2}{\kappa}
\ge0,
\]

because `t_{rho,nu}(kappa)>=h_rho(kappa)>=0`. Hence

\[
D\succeq0.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Conditional independence/centering gives

\[
E\,\operatorname{tr}(\eta^\top D\eta\mid X)
=
\sum_iD_{ii}E[\|\eta_i\|^2\mid Z_i]
\le
\omega\operatorname{tr}D,
\tag{8.7}
\]

using `(8.3)` and `D_{ii}>=0`.

The fresh irreducible residual `eta_*` contributes the same term to both predictors and its cross term vanishes by `(8.2)`, so it cancels in the risk difference. The represented bias calculation is deterministic and identical to Section 5. Combining it with `(8.7)` gives `(8.4)`.

QED.

with iid standard Gaussian entries. The limiting ESD of `X^T X` is the free-Poisson / Marchenko–Pastur law `mu_alpha`, supported on

\[

with atom `(1-alpha)_+` at zero.

The preceding run proved `(0.8)`. We now evaluate its sign threshold in closed Stieltjes form.

## 9.1 Stieltjes transform

For `a>0`, define

\[
M_\alpha(a)
=
\int\frac{1}{x+a}\,d\mu_\alpha(x).
\tag{9.1}
\]

The free-Poisson quadratic is

\[
\boxed{
aM^2+(a+\alpha-1)M-1=0.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha(a)
=
\frac{
\sqrt{a^2+2(1+\alpha)a+(1-\alpha)^2}
-a+1-\alpha
}{2a}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Writing

\[
D_\alpha(a)
=
\sqrt{a^2+2(1+\alpha)a+(1-\alpha)^2},
\]

implicit differentiation of `(9.2)` gives the convenient exact identity

\[
\boxed{
M_\alpha'(a)
=-\frac{M_\alpha(a)(M_\alpha(a)+1)}{D_\alpha(a)}.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

## 9.2 Three rational integrals

Assume first `rho!=nu` and write

\[
\delta=\nu-\rho.
\]

Define

\[
C
=
\int\frac{1}{(x+\rho)^2(x+\nu)^2}\,d\mu_\alpha(x),
\]

\[
A
=
\int\frac{x}{(x+\rho)^2(x+\nu)^2}\,d\mu_\alpha(x),
\]

\[
B
=
\int\frac{x^2}{(x+\rho)^2(x+\nu)^2}\,d\mu_\alpha(x).
\tag{9.5}
\]

Partial fractions / differentiation of

\[
J
=
\int\frac{1}{(x+\rho)(x+\nu)}d\mu_\alpha
=
\frac{M_\alpha(\rho)-M_\alpha(\nu)}{\nu-\rho}
\]

give

\[
\boxed{
C
=
\frac{-M_\alpha'(\rho)-M_\alpha'(\nu)}{\delta^2}
+
\frac{2[M_\alpha(\nu)-M_\alpha(\rho)]}{\delta^3},
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
A
=
\frac{\delta M_\alpha'(\nu)+M_\alpha(\rho)-M_\alpha(\nu)}{\delta^2}
-\rho C,
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
B
=
\frac{M_\alpha(\rho)-M_\alpha(\nu)}{\delta}
-(\rho+\nu)A-\rho\nu C.
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

The equal-ridge case follows continuously by taking `nu->rho` (or by differentiating `M` to order three); no singularity is present in the integrals themselves.

## 9.3 Exact critical SNR

Using `(9.5)`, the MP gain becomes

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\infty(\rho,\nu)
=
\rho\Big[
\rho\tau^2(B+2\nu A)
-
\omega\{2B+(2\nu+\rho)A\}
\Big].
}
\tag{9.9}
\]

Therefore, for `omega>0`, the exact critical represented SNR is

\[
\boxed{
Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu)
=
\frac{2B+(2\nu+\rho)A}
{\rho(B+2\nu A)},
}
\tag{9.10}
\]

and

\[
\Gamma_\infty>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\tau^2}{\omega}>Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu).
\tag{9.11}
\]

This is a closed radical/Stieltjes expression through `(9.3)--(9.8)`; no numerical quadrature is needed.

## 9.4 Distribution-free bracket for the threshold

Let

\[
w_{\rho,\nu}(x)
=
\frac{x}{(x+\rho)^2(x+\nu)^2}.
\]

Then `(9.10)` is exactly the weighted average ratio

\[
Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu)
=
\frac{
\int w(x)(2x+2\nu+\rho)d\mu_\alpha(x)
}{
\rho\int w(x)(x+2\nu)d\mu_\alpha(x)
}.
\tag{9.12}
\]

Equivalently it is a positive weighted average of the modewise threshold `(6.4)`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\min\left\{
\frac2\rho,
\frac1\rho+\frac1{2\nu}
\right\}
\le
Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu)
\le
\max\left\{
\frac2\rho,
\frac1\rho+\frac1{2\nu}
\right\}.
}
\tag{9.13}
\]

The argument does not use the explicit MP density; `(9.13)` holds for any nonnegative spectral law with positive mass away from zero.

# 10. `P15-MP-KRRMEAN-EXISTENCE-43` — PROVED

The fixed-`nu` critical threshold `(9.10)` itself depends on `alpha`. But the existence of **some** beneficial KRR mean has a simpler exact boundary.

For every `x>=0`,

\[

=
\frac{x+\rho}{\rho(x+2\nu)}>0
\tag{10.1}
\]

for finite `nu>0`. Therefore

\[
Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu)>\frac1\rho.
\tag{10.2}
\]

As `nu->infinity`, the MP support is compact and `(10.1)` tends to zero uniformly on that support, so

\[
\boxed{
Q_\alpha(\rho,\nu)\downarrow\frac1\rho
\quad\text{in the limit }\nu\to\infty
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

(the convergence statement, not monotonicity in `nu`, is all that is needed).

- if `tau^2/omega<=1/rho`, every modewise bracket in `(0.8)` is nonpositive for every finite `nu`, so `Gamma_infty<=0`;
- if `tau^2/omega>1/rho`, choose `nu` sufficiently large that `Q_alpha(rho,nu)<tau^2/omega`, giving `Gamma_infty>0`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\exists\nu>0:\Gamma_\infty(\rho,\nu)>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho\tau^2>\omega.

\tag{10.4}

The gain tends to zero as `nu->infinity`; `(10.4)` is an existence statement, not a claim that an infinitely strong ridge is optimal.
