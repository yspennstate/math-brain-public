# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**, but this run closes a real gap left by the preceding Hoeffding/Efron theorem and gives a source-faithful theorem for the manuscript's actual unsquared mean-relative metric.

The previous run proved that the squared-risk same-observation reuse debt

\[
D_{\rm reuse}=R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=2\sum_{A\neq\varnothing}E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle
\]

is the sum of matching Hoeffding interactions of the uncorrected baseline `B` and correction increment `G`, and that the signed one-row replacement sum overcounts higher-order interactions. It bounded the error by Efron--Stein interaction slack.

This run derives the **exact all-orders replacement inversion** and an explicit truncation hierarchy.

For a nonempty coordinate subset `I`, define the mixed replacement difference

\[
\Delta_I F
:=
\prod_{i\in I}(I-R_i)F
=
\sum_{J\subseteq I}(-1)^{|J|}F(S^{(J)}),
\]

where `S^(J)` replaces the observations in `J` by independent copies and the same replacement copies are used when comparing two learners. Put, for `r>=1`,

\[
C_r(B,G)
:=
\sum_{|I|=r}E\langle\Delta_I B,\Delta_I G\rangle .
\]

Then:

## `P15-HIGHERORDER-REUSE-INVERSION-34` — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm reuse}
=
\sum_{r=1}^n
(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}C_r(B,G).
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Thus the preceding signed one-row sum is only the first term of an exact inclusion--exclusion series. If either learner has Hoeffding degree at most `d`, the series stops after `d`.

More usefully, let

\[
P_d(B,G)
:=
\sum_{r=1}^d(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}C_r(B,G),
\]

and let

\[
D_r(F)
:=
\sum_{|I|=r}E\|\Delta_I F\|^2.
\]

Then:

## `P15-HIGHERORDER-REUSE-TRUNCATION-34` — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
|D_{\rm reuse}-P_d(B,G)|
\le
2^{-d}
\sqrt{D_{d+1}(B)D_{d+1}(G)}.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

For `d=1`, `(0.2)` is exactly the preceding second-order replacement certificate. The new theorem is the complete hierarchy behind it.

Therefore, with ghost improvement

\[
\Gamma_{\rm ghost}=R(B)-R_{\rm ghost},
\]

we obtain:

## `P15-HIGHERORDER-GHOST-IMPROVEMENT-34` — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}
\ge
\Gamma_{\rm ghost}
-P_d(B,G)
-2^{-d}\sqrt{D_{d+1}(B)D_{d+1}(G)}.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

A positive right side certifies that the fully same-observation corrected learner beats the uncorrected learner in expected fresh squared risk. No fixed representation, Gaussian noise, linear neural mean, or differentiability is assumed; only an independent product training sample and square integrability are needed.

The manuscript, however, is scored by the **unsquared mean-relative metric**. This run derives an exact replacement hierarchy for that metric's ghost-transfer budget.

Let

\[
\|h\|_{w,2}^2
:=
E_{U,Y}\frac{\|h(U)\|^2}{\|Y\|^2},

assuming this weighted second moment is finite, and let `G_S` be the full correction increment relative to the uncorrected baseline. Define `D_r^w(G)` by replacing the ordinary Hilbert norm in `D_r` by `||.||_(w,2)`.

The pointwise reverse triangle inequality gives

\[
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,ghost}|
\le
\left(E\|G_S-G_{S'}\|_{w,2}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{0.4}
\]

The squared ghost prediction distance has the exact all-orders inversion

\[
\boxed{
V_G^{(w)}
:=E\|G_S-G_{S'}\|_{w,2}^2
=
\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}D_r^w(G).
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
T_d^w(G)
:=
\sum_{r=1}^d(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}D_r^w(G),
\]

then the remainder has a **known sign**:

\[
V_G^{(w)}-T_d^w(G)
=
2(-1)^d
\sum_A\binom{|A|-1}{d}\|G_A\|_{w,2}^2.
\tag{0.6}
\]

Hence odd `d` gives an upper bound and even `d` a lower bound. Quantitatively,

\[
\boxed{
|V_G^{(w)}-T_d^w(G)|
\le
2^{-d}D_{d+1}^w(G).
}
\tag{0.7}
\]

In particular, every odd `d` gives the source-metric certificate

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,ghost}|
\le
\sqrt{T_d^w(G)}.
}
\tag{0.8}
\]

The `d=1` case is exactly the previous `sqrt(n) beta_(G,w)` bound, while `(0.8)` shows how certified third-, fifth-, ... order replacement experiments can tighten it without assuming smooth loss or a lower bound on `||Y||`.

Thus:

## `P15-MEANREL-REPLACEMENT-INVERSION-34` — PROVED

If the independent/ghost corrected learner improves the uncorrected learner by mean-relative margin `Gamma_rel,ghost`, then the actual same-observation learner improves whenever, for any odd `d`,

# 5. `P15-HIGHERORDER-REUSE-INVERSION-34` — exact proof

## 5.1 Mixed replacement operator

For each coordinate `i`, let `R_i` replace `Z_i` by an independent copy `Z_i'`. For a nonempty subset `I`, define

\[
\Delta_I
=\prod_{i\in I}(I-R_i).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The replacement copies are independent of the original sample and of each other. When forming a cross-effect between `B` and `G`, the **same replacement copies** are used on both sides.

Let

\[
B=EB+\sum_{A\neq\varnothing}B_A,
\qquad
G=EG+\sum_{A\neq\varnothing}G_A
\tag{5.2}
\]

be their Hilbert-valued Hoeffding decompositions.

A component `F_A` is killed by `Delta_I` unless `I subseteq A`.

If `I subseteq A` and `|I|=r`, expansion of `(5.1)` has `2^r` terms. Distinct terms differ in at least one replaced/original coordinate, and Hoeffding centering in that coordinate makes them orthogonal. Therefore, for matching components,

\[
E\langle\Delta_I B_A,\Delta_I G_A\rangle
=2^rE\langle B_A,G_A\rangle.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Different Hoeffding subsets remain orthogonal after the replacement difference. Hence

\[
\boxed{
E\langle\Delta_I B,\Delta_I G\rangle
=2^{|I|}
\sum_{A\supseteq I}E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Summing `(5.4)` over all `I` with size `r` gives

\[
C_r(B,G)
=2^r
\sum_A\binom{|A|}{r}E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle.
\tag{5.5}
\]

## 5.2 Binomial inversion

Use

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{k}(-1)^{r+1}\binom{k}{r}=1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}C_r(B,G)
&=2\sum_AE\langle B_A,G_A\rangle
\sum_{r=1}^{|A|}(-1)^{r+1}\binom{|A|}{r}\\
&=2\sum_AE\langle B_A,G_A\rangle\\
&=D_{\rm reuse}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(0.1)`.

## 5.3 Degree truncation

If either `B_A=0` or `G_A=0` whenever `|A|>d`, then all matching interaction covariances vanish above order `d`, and the series stops exactly at `d`.

The fixed-design, fixed-kernel KRR baseline linear in independent response rows has degree one, so `(0.1)` reduces to the preceding exact signed one-row formula. A response-trained representation generally destroys that degree-one structure.

# 6. `P15-HIGHERORDER-REUSE-TRUNCATION-34` — remainder bound

For `d>=1`, the partial binomial identity is

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{d}(-1)^{r+1}\binom{k}{r}
=
1-(-1)^d\binom{k-1}{d}
\quad(k>d).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
D_{\rm reuse}-P_d(B,G)
=2(-1)^d
\sum_A\binom{|A|-1}{d}
E\langle B_A,G_A\rangle.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Define the order-`d` interaction tail energy

\[
\tau_d(F)
:=
\sum_A\binom{|A|-1}{d}\,E\|F_A\|^2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz in `(6.2)` gives

\[
|D_{\rm reuse}-P_d|
\le2\sqrt{\tau_d(B)\tau_d(G)}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
D_{d+1}(F)
=2^{d+1}
\sum_A\binom{|A|}{d+1}E\|F_A\|^2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since

\[
\binom{k-1}{d}
=\frac{d+1}{k}\binom{k}{d+1}
\le\binom{k}{d+1},
\]

we have

\[
\tau_d(F)
\le2^{-(d+1)}D_{d+1}(F).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Combining `(6.4)` and `(6.6)` proves

\[
\boxed{
|D_{\rm reuse}-P_d(B,G)|
\le
2^{-d}\sqrt{D_{d+1}(B)D_{d+1}(G)}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

For `d=1`, `tau_1(F)` is exactly the Efron--Stein interaction slack from the preceding run, and `(6.7)` recovers its second-order replacement upper bound.

This identifies the entire higher-order hierarchy rather than a one-off second-order trick.

# 7. Source-metric theorem: exact ghost prediction-distance inversion

For the manuscript metric

\[
\ell_{\rm rel}(f;u,y)
=\frac{\|y-f(u)\|}{\|y\|},
\]

the reverse triangle inequality gives pointwise

\[
|\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G_S)-\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G_{S'})|
\le
\frac{\|G_S-G_{S'}\|}{\|Y\|}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

After expectation and Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,ghost}|
\le
\sqrt{V_G^{(w)}},
\tag{7.2}
\]

where

\[
V_G^{(w)}
=E\|G_S-G_{S'}\|_{w,2}^2.
\]

Since `G_S,G_S'` are independent copies in the weighted Hilbert space,

\[
V_G^{(w)}
=2E\|G-EG\|_{w,2}^2
=2\sum_A\|G_A\|_{w,2}^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Apply the self-pair version of `(5.5)`:

\[
D_r^w(G)
=2^r\sum_A\binom{|A|}{r}\|G_A\|_{w,2}^2.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Binomial inversion immediately yields

\[
\boxed{
V_G^{(w)}
=\sum_{r=1}^n(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}D_r^w(G).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The partial-sum identity `(6.1)` now has a fixed sign because all self-energies are nonnegative:

\[
V_G^{(w)}-T_d^w(G)
=2(-1)^d
\sum_A\binom{|A|-1}{d}\|G_A\|_{w,2}^2.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Hence:

- odd `d`: `T_d^w >= V_G^(w)`;
- even `d`: `T_d^w <= V_G^(w)`.

Using the same comparison to the `(d+1)`st replacement energy gives

\[
\boxed{
|V_G^{(w)}-T_d^w(G)|
\le2^{-d}D_{d+1}^w(G).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This proves `P15-MEANREL-REPLACEMENT-INVERSION-34`.

# 8. New counterexample: pair subtraction is not a safe upper bound

Let `Z_1,Z_2,Z_3` be iid Rademacher and take scalar

\[
G=Z_1Z_2Z_3.
\]

This is a pure third-order Hoeffding component with `E G^2=1`.

For any coordinate `i`, replacing it independently yields

\[
E|\Delta_iG|^2=2,
\]

so

\[
D_1(G)=6.
\]

For every pair `i<j`,

\[
E|\Delta_{ij}G|^2=4,
\]

hence

\[
D_2(G)=12.
\]

For the triple,

\[
E|\Delta_{123}G|^2=8,
\]

so

\[
D_3(G)=8.
\]

The true ghost distance is

\[
V_G=E(G-G')^2=2.
\]

But the naive pair-subtracted estimate is

\[
D_1-\frac12D_2=6-6=0.
\]

Thus the statement

> subtracting half the total pair-replacement energy from the first-order Efron budget gives an upper bound on the independent-ghost prediction distance

is **DISPROVED**.

The third-order term repairs it exactly:

\[
6-6+\frac14\cdot8=2.
\]

This matters for neural/selected pipelines because optimizer and representation learning naturally induce interaction orders above two.

# 9. Consequences for KRR, learned representations, and random features

## 9.1 Fixed-design / fixed-kernel KRR

Conditional on fixed `X,K,lambda`, zero-mean KRR is linear in response rows:

\[
B(u)=k(u,X)(K+n\lambda I)^{-1}Y.
\]

Therefore its response-Hoeffding degree is one and all mixed replacement energies of order at least two vanish on the `B` side. The exact reuse debt is first order, consistent with the previous run.

This remains a stochastic fixed-design benchmark. In the deterministic mechanics source, replacing a complete pair changes the design itself and therefore changes the learned representation, Gram matrix, fresh kernel vector, optimizer path, and selected branch.

## 9.2 Learned representation

Prior P15 work proved the exact finite resolvent decomposition

\[
B'-B
=\Delta k_u^TA'^{-1}
-k_u^TA'^{-1}\Delta A A^{-1},
\]

with corresponding norm bounds, and gave a counterexample showing identical training Gram matrices can coexist with `O(1)` fresh kernel-vector movement.

The present hierarchy adds a second axis: even if each one-row representation perturbation is small, multi-row interactions can make the signed first-order reuse accounting wrong. A learned-feature theorem must therefore control both perturbation magnitude and interaction order/tail.

## 9.3 Random-feature deterministic equivalents

A sufficient high-dimensional theorem need not model every interaction order if it proves a finite-order closure. For fixed `d`, suppose

\[
C_r(B_n,G_n)\to c_r\quad(1\le r\le d)
\]

and

\[
2^{-d}\sqrt{D_{d+1}(B_n)D_{d+1}(G_n)}\to0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_{{\rm reuse},n}
\to
\sum_{r=1}^d(-1)^{r+1}2^{1-r}c_r.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

For the source metric, if `D_r^w(G_n)` converge through odd order `d` and `D_(d+1)^w(G_n)->0`, the mean-relative same-vs-ghost gap is controlled by the limiting odd partial sum.

This is the current `P15-RF-HOEFFDING-DE-REDUCTION-34` target.

Current primary literature remains narrower:

- Misiakiewicz--Saeed, arXiv:2403.08938, prove non-asymptotic KRR deterministic equivalents under spectral/concentration assumptions on the kernel eigendecomposition and target alignment;
- Defilippis--Loureiro--Misiakiewicz, arXiv:2405.15699 / NeurIPS 2024, prove a dimension-free multiplicative deterministic equivalent for random-feature ridge under a feature-concentration hypothesis;
- Cui et al., arXiv:2402.04980, derive a spiked-random-feature equivalent after one specified gradient step in a two-layer model;
- Dandi et al., arXiv:2410.18938, rigorously analyze the learned-feature spectrum/generalization after one aggressive gradient step in a controlled two-layer setting.

Those results provide strong fixed/random-feature or tightly controlled learned-feature machinery. None supplies the arbitrary multi-epoch, same-observation neural mean/representation/selection replacement tensors `C_r,D_r` needed here. No existing Gram spectral law is promoted into a theorem about the source pipeline.

Let

\[
P=P_{W,U},
\qquad
Q=P_W\otimes P_U.

\[
D(P\|Q)=I(W;U).

Under `Q`, conditional on any realized unit `U=u`,

\[
Z_i=\sqrt{N/\alpha}\,w_i^Tu

are iid standard normal. Therefore the sign mask is uniform on `{0,1}^N`, and conditional on the mask, each active `X_i=(Z_i^+)^2` is chi-square with one degree of freedom.

\[
I(W;U)
\ge
D(P_M\|U_{\{0,1\}^N})
+E_M D(P_{X|M}\|Q_{X|M}).

\]

\[
E_Q\exp\left(\theta\sum_iX_i\right)
=(1-2\theta)^{-k/2}.
\]

The entropy variational inequality gives, after averaging over the actual mask,

\[
E_M D(P_{X|M}\|Q_{X|M})
\ge
N\left[\theta s+\frac a2\log(1-2\theta)\right].
\]

When `s>=a`, the optimizer is

\[
\theta=\frac12\left(1-\frac a s\right),
\]

which yields

\[
\frac N2\left[s-a-a\log(s/a)\right].
\]

Adding the mask term proves `(11.1)` exactly.

# 14. Adversarial checks of the new theorem

## 14.1 Pure order-one component

If `G` is additive, only `|A|=1` components occur. Then

\[

\]

and every higher `D_r` vanishes. This recovers the old first-order bound exactly.

## 14.2 Pure order-two component

For one normalized pure pair component, `V_G=2`, `D_1=4`, `D_2=4`. Then

\[

\]

so the degree-two truncation is exact.

## 14.3 Pure order-three component

The cubic Rademacher example gives

\[
V_G=2,
\quad
T_1=6,
\quad
T_2=0,
\quad
T_3=2.
\]

Thus odd partial sums are upper bounds and even partial sums lower bounds exactly as `(7.6)` predicts.

## 14.4 Remainder constant

For a pure interaction of size `k>d`, the exact truncation remainder coefficient in `V_G-T_d` is

\[
2(-1)^d\binom{k-1}{d}.
\]

The next-order aggregate replacement energy contributes

\[
2^{d+1}\binom{k}{d+1}.
\]

Since

\[
2\binom{k-1}{d}
\le
2^{-d}\,2^{d+1}\binom{k}{d+1},
\]

the constant `2^-d` in `(0.7)` checks termwise.
