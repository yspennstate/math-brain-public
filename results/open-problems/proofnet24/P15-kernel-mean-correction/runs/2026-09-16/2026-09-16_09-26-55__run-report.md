# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**, but this run proves an exact finite-sample and high-dimensional theorem for a nontrivial same-observation structured-mean-plus-residual-KRR model, and it produces a sharp deterministic-label counterexample showing that even a mean which is better by itself, and even a cross-fitted corrected learner which is better, can become worse than uncorrected KRR when the **same observation** is reused by both the mean and the correction.

The source fixed-population identity remains exactly

\[
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right)^2r_j^2,
\qquad
r=f^\star-m.
\]

It is used only as the elementary population squared-bias identity for a **fixed** population mean and a **fixed** population kernel operator. It is not promoted to a theorem for a learned mean, learned representation, selected correction, or same-observation training.

The principal positive theorem of this run is a controlled finite-design model in which the structured mean itself is a spectral linear smoother of the **same response matrix** subsequently used by residual KRR. For a fixed feature matrix `X`, Gram matrix `K=XX^T`, source-normalized ridge mass `rho=n lambda`, and structured mean

\[
m_b(u)=k_u^\top b(K)Y,
\]

with training smoother

\[
S_b=Kb(K),
\qquad s(\kappa)=\kappa b(\kappa),
\]

the same-response residual correction is

\[
f_b(u)
=m_b(u)+k_u^\top(K+\rho I)^{-1}\{Y-Kb(K)Y\}.
\]

It collapses exactly to one spectral transfer function

\[
\boxed{
 t_b(\kappa)
=
\frac{\kappa[1+\rho b(\kappa)]}{\kappa+\rho}
=
\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\rho}
+
\frac{\rho}{\kappa+\rho}s(\kappa).
}
\]

For a linear teacher plus independent row perturbation,

\[
Y=X\Theta+E,
\qquad E[E_i]=0,
\qquad E[E_iE_i^\top]=\Omega,
\]

the conditional fresh squared risk is obtained exactly mode by mode. If `kappa_j>0` are the nonzero eigenvalues of `K`, `v_j` are the corresponding right singular vectors of `X`,

\[
\theta_j^2=\|v_j^\top\Theta\|_2^2,
\qquad
h_j=\frac{\kappa_j}{\kappa_j+\rho},
\qquad
t_j=t_b(\kappa_j),
\]
then

\[
\boxed{
R_b(X)
=
\frac1d\|P_{\ker X}\Theta\|_F^2
+
\frac1d\sum_j(1-t_j)^2\theta_j^2
+
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Omega}{d}
\sum_j\frac{t_j^2}{\kappa_j}.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

The exact improvement over uncorrected zero-mean KRR is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_b(X)=R_0(X)-R_b(X)
=
\frac1d\sum_j
\frac{\rho^2(2s_j-s_j^2)\theta_j^2
-
\operatorname{tr}\Omega\,[2\rho s_j+\rho^2s_j^2/\kappa_j]}
{(\kappa_j+\rho)^2}.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

This is a genuine same-response finite-sample theorem: the structured mean and residual correction use the same `Y` and the same realized design. It is sharply restricted to the stated linear fixed-representation model; no claim is made that the released multi-epoch neural pipeline satisfies these hypotheses.

A useful positive corollary is immediate. In the **noiseless, well-specified, fixed-representation** case `Omega=0`, if

\[
0\le s(\kappa_j)\le2
\]

on every nonzero design mode, then

\[
\Gamma_b(X)\ge0
\]

for every finite design `X`, with strict gain whenever a signal-bearing mode has `0<s_j<2`. In particular, if the structured mean is another KRR smoother with ridge mass `nu>0`,

\[
b_\nu(\kappa)=\frac1{\kappa+\nu},
\qquad
s_\nu(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\nu}\in[0,1),
\]
then same-response KRR-mean-plus-residual-KRR cannot worsen zero-mean KRR under those **restricted noiseless linear-teacher hypotheses**.

The corresponding double-KRR filter is exact:

\[
\boxed{
 t_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)
=
\frac{\kappa(\kappa+\rho+\nu)}
{(\kappa+\rho)(\kappa+\nu)}.
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

This positive theorem does not survive arbitrary deterministic-label misspecification. The run gives the following exact counterexample. Let the input and training points be iid Rademacher, let the deterministic target be

\[
G(u)=c+\beta u,
\qquad c=\sqrt3,
\qquad\beta=1,
\]
use the linear kernel `k(u,v)=uv`, one training observation, correction ridge mass `rho=1`, and mean ridge mass `nu=3`. Then

\[
h=\frac12,
\qquad
a=\frac14,
\qquad
t=h+(1-h)a=\frac58.
\]

The exact fresh risks are

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm KRR}=4,
\qquad
R_{\rm mean}=\frac{15}{4},
\qquad
R_{\rm crossfit}=\frac{63}{16},
\qquad
R_{\rm same}=\frac{69}{16}.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

Thus the KRR mean alone improves the uncorrected KRR by `1/4`; the independently trained/cross-fitted mean followed by the correction improves it by `1/16`; but using the **same training observation for both mean and residual correction worsens the baseline by `5/16`**. The exact same-versus-crossfit reuse debt is

\[
\boxed{R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm crossfit}=\frac38.}
\tag{0.5}
\]

No response noise is present. The failure is created by deterministic misspecification (the linear kernel cannot represent the constant component) plus same-observation reuse.

The finite-design theorem also has an explicit proportional Gaussian-design limit. For

\[
X=d^{-1/2}Z,
\qquad Z_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\qquad n/d\to\alpha\in(0,\infty),
\]
with fixed `rho>0`, fixed output dimension, bounded continuous `b`, deterministic signals satisfying

\[
\|\Theta_d\|_F^2/d\to\tau^2,
\]
and row covariance `Omega`, the conditional expected fresh risk converges to

\[
\boxed{
R_b
\longrightarrow
\tau^2\int(1-t_b(x))^2\,d\mu_\alpha(x)
+
\operatorname{tr}\Omega
\int\frac{t_b(x)^2}{x}\,d\mu_\alpha(x),
}
\tag{0.6}
\]

where the second integrand is defined as `0` at `x=0`, and `mu_alpha` is the free-Poisson/Marchenko--Pastur law for `X^TX`, with atom `(1-alpha)_+` at zero and support endpoints

\[
x_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt\alpha)^2.
\]

The exact limiting gain is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\infty(b)
=
\tau^2\int
\frac{\rho^2(2s-s^2)}{(x+\rho)^2}
\,d\mu_\alpha(x)
-
\operatorname{tr}\Omega\int
\frac{2\rho s+\rho^2s^2/x}{(x+\rho)^2}
\,d\mu_\alpha(x).
}
\tag{0.7}
\]

For a KRR mean with ridge `nu`, this simplifies to

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_\infty(\rho,\nu)
=
\rho\int
\frac{x}{(x+\rho)^2(x+\nu)^2}
\Big[
\rho\tau^2(x+2\nu)
-
\omega(2x+2\nu+\rho)
\Big]
\,d\mu_\alpha(x),
}
\tag{0.8}
\]

where `omega=tr Omega`. Hence the sign is explicit through a one-dimensional spectral integral. This is a solved high-dimensional benchmark, not yet the arbitrary response-trained representation problem.

A deterministic-label version removes any need to interpret `E` as physical response noise. Let the full simulator input be `(x,z)` with independent Gaussian blocks and deterministic target

\[
G(x,z)=x^\top\Theta+z^\top\Gamma,
\]
while the learner/kernel sees only `x`. Then the omitted coordinate contribution `z^T Gamma` is deterministic given the full simulator input, but from the learner's restricted representation it has row covariance

\[
\Omega=m^{-1}\Gamma^\top\Gamma.
\]

Equations (0.1)--(0.8) apply to the learnable component, with the same irreducible omitted-coordinate test term added to both estimators. This is an exact deterministic-label misspecification benchmark. It is **not** asserted as a hidden hypothesis of the mechanics manuscript.

# 3. `P15-SPECTRAL-SAMEOBS-FINITEN-42` — PROVED

## 3.1 Model and exact same-response estimator

Let

\[
X\in\mathbb R^{n\times d},
\qquad
K=XX^\top,
\]

and let a fresh query satisfy

\[
E[uu^\top]=d^{-1}I_d.
\]

For the clean fixed-representation theorem take

\[
Y=X\Theta+E,
\]

where `Theta in R^{d x q}` is deterministic and the rows `E_i in R^q` are independent of `X`, centered, mutually independent, with common covariance `Omega`.

Fix

\[
\rho>0.
\]

For the source normalization one has exactly

\[
\rho=n\lambda.
\]

The zero-mean KRR baseline is

\[
f_0(u)=k_u^\top(K+\rho I)^{-1}Y,
\qquad
k_u=Xu.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let `b` be a bounded real function on the spectrum of `K`, and define a structured mean from the **same response matrix**:

\[
m_b(u)=k_u^\top b(K)Y.
\tag{3.2}
\]

On the training rows its fitted values are

\[
M_b=Kb(K)Y.
\]

Now fit residual KRR to the **same** `Y`:

\[
f_b(u)
=
m_b(u)
+k_u^\top(K+\rho I)^{-1}
\left[Y-Kb(K)Y\right].
\tag{3.3}
\]

No response-copy independence is used in (3.3).

## 3.2 Exact filter collapse

Because `b(K)` commutes with `K`, the dual coefficient operator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_b(K)
&=b(K)+(K+\rho I)^{-1}[I-Kb(K)]\\
&=\frac{I+\rho b(K)}{K+\rho I}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus

\[
f_b(u)=u^\top X^\top a_b(K)Y.
\]

Take an SVD

\[
X=U\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\kappa_1},\ldots,\sqrt{\kappa_r})V^\top,
\]

with `kappa_j>0`. Put

\[
s_j=s(\kappa_j)=\kappa_jb(\kappa_j).
\]

The induced primal transfer on the `j`th row-space mode is

\[
\boxed{
 t_j
=
\kappa_j a_b(\kappa_j)
=
\frac{\kappa_j[1+\rho b(\kappa_j)]}{\kappa_j+\rho}
=
h_j+(1-h_j)s_j,
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

where

\[
h_j=\frac{\kappa_j}{\kappa_j+\rho}.
\]

This identity is exact at finite `n,d`.

## 3.3 Exact signal term

Write

\[
\theta_j^2=\|v_j^\top\Theta\|_2^2.
\]

The clean signal prediction operator is

\[
T_b=X^\top a_b(K)X
=V\operatorname{diag}(t_j)V^\top.
\]

Hence, averaging over a fresh isotropic query,

\[
E_u\|u^\top(\Theta-T_b\Theta)\|_2^2
=
\frac1d\|P_{\ker X}\Theta\|_F^2
+
\frac1d\sum_j(1-t_j)^2\theta_j^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]

## 3.4 Exact same-response variance term

The fitted perturbation contribution is

\[
u^\top X^\top a_b(K)E.
\]

Conditioning on `X`, averaging over the independent rows of `E` and the fresh query gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E\|u^\top X^\top a_b(K)E\|_2^2
&=
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Omega}{d}
\operatorname{tr}[a_b(K)Ka_b(K)]\\
&=
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Omega}{d}
\sum_j\frac{t_j^2}{\kappa_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equations (3.6)--(3.7) prove (0.1).

## 3.5 Exact gain against uncorrected KRR

For the baseline, `t_j=h_j`. Since

\[
1-t_j=(1-h_j)(1-s_j),
\]

the signal gain on mode `j` is

\[
(1-h_j)^2\left[1-(1-s_j)^2\right]\theta_j^2
=
\frac{\rho^2}{(\kappa_j+\rho)^2}(2s_j-s_j^2)\theta_j^2.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Also

\[
\frac{t_j^2-h_j^2}{\kappa_j}
=
\frac{2\rho s_j+\rho^2s_j^2/\kappa_j}
{(\kappa_j+\rho)^2}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Subtracting corrected risk from baseline risk proves (0.2).

The formula makes the same-observation tradeoff explicit: the structured mean can reduce shrinkage bias through `2s-s^2`, but because that mean was fitted to the same response matrix, its perturbation component is also propagated through the residual correction. That same-response variance/misspecification debt is the second term of (0.2).

# 4. `P15-SPECTRAL-NOISELESS-MONOTONE-42` — PROVED under explicit restricted hypotheses

Set

\[
\Omega=0.
\]

Then every term of (0.2) has sign determined by

\[
2s_j-s_j^2=s_j(2-s_j).
\]

Therefore if

\[
0\le s_j\le2
\quad\text{for every nonzero design mode},
\tag{4.1}
\]
then

\[
\boxed{R_b(X)\le R_0(X)}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for **every finite design** `X` and every deterministic linear teacher `Theta`.

Strict inequality holds if at least one mode has

\[
\theta_j^2>0
\quad\text{and}\quad
0<s_j<2.
\]

This is a real same-response theorem. Its restrictions are essential:

1. the representation `X` is fixed rather than learned from the responses;
2. the target is exactly linear in that representation;
3. there is no omitted component in the training response;
4. the mean is a spectral linear smoother of the same `Y`;
5. the same kernel representation is used by the mean and correction.

It must not be generalized to the manuscript's neural/refiner/stack pipeline without separately controlling representation learning, misspecification and selection.

For a KRR mean

\[
b_\nu(\kappa)=\frac1{\kappa+\nu},
\]
condition (4.1) is automatic because

\[
s_\nu(\kappa)=\frac\kappa{\kappa+\nu}\in[0,1).
\]

This proves the restricted same-data monotonicity statement for KRR-mean-plus-residual-KRR in the noiseless well-specified fixed-feature model.

# 5. `P15-DOUBLEKRR-FILTER-42` — PROVED

For a KRR mean with ridge mass `nu>0`, substitute

\[
b_\nu(\kappa)=\frac1{\kappa+\nu}
\]
into (3.5). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
t_{\rho,\nu}(\kappa)
&=
\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\rho}
+
\frac\rho{\kappa+\rho}
\frac\kappa{\kappa+\nu}\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa(\kappa+\rho+\nu)}
{(\kappa+\rho)(\kappa+\nu)}
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The finite-design modewise gain becomes

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rho,\nu}(X)
=
\frac1d\sum_j
\frac{\rho\kappa_j}
{(\kappa_j+\rho)^2(\kappa_j+\nu)^2}
\left[
\rho\theta_j^2(\kappa_j+2\nu)
-
\omega(2\kappa_j+2\nu+\rho)
\right],
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
\omega=\operatorname{tr}\Omega.
\]

This gives an exact finite-sample spectral sign criterion in the controlled fixed-feature model.

# 6. `P15-SPECTRAL-MP-DE-42` — PROVED for proportional Gaussian fixed features

## 6.1 Assumptions

\[

\qquad (Z_d)_{ij}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1),
\qquad

\[

\[

\]

\[

## 6.2 Marchenko--Pastur/free-Poisson law

# 8. `P15-KRR-MEAN-CROSSFIT-SIGNFLIP-42` — PROVED exact deterministic-label counterexample

This counterexample directly addresses the requested monotonicity failure.

Let

\[

\]

and let the deterministic target be

\[
G(u)=c+\beta u,
\qquad
c=\sqrt3,
\qquad
\beta=1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Use the linear kernel

\[

\]

There is one training observation. Set correction ridge mass

\[
\rho=1
\]

and mean ridge mass

\[
\nu=3.
\]

Thus

\[
h=\frac1{1+\rho}=\frac12,
\qquad
a=\frac1{1+\nu}=\frac14.
\]

## 8.1 Uncorrected KRR

The baseline trained on `X` is

\[
b_X(u)=h\,uX(c+\beta X)
=u[h\beta+h cX].
\]

Averaging over the fresh query and training point,

\[
R_0
=c^2+\beta^2(1-h)^2+c^2h^2
=3+\frac14+\frac34
=\boxed4.
\tag{8.2}
\]

## 8.2 Mean alone

The KRR mean with ridge `nu=3` is

\[
m_X(u)=a\,uX(c+\beta X).
\]

Its risk is

\[
R_{\rm mean}
=c^2+\beta^2(1-a)^2+c^2a^2
=3+\frac9{16}+\frac3{16}
=\boxed{\frac{15}{4}}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

So the structured learned mean alone is genuinely better than the uncorrected KRR.

## 8.3 Mean and residual correction on the same observation

The training-row fitted mean is `aY`, hence the residual is `(1-a)Y`. The final coefficient is

\[
t=a+h(1-a)=h+(1-h)a=\frac58.
\]

Therefore

\[
f_{\rm same}(u)=t\,uX(c+\beta X),
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm same}
&=c^2+\beta^2(1-t)^2+c^2t^2\\
&=3+\frac9{64}+3\frac{25}{64}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{69}{16}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is **worse** than the uncorrected risk `4` by `5/16`.

## 8.4 Independent/cross-fitted mean benchmark

Train the mean on the independent observation `X'` but fit the residual correction on `X`. Then

\[
m_{X'}(u)=a\beta u+ac\,uX'.
\]

Evaluated at the correction point `X`, the correction residual is

\[
Y_X-m_{X'}(X)
=c+(1-a)\beta X-acXX'.
\]

The resulting corrected predictor simplifies to

\[
f_{\rm cf}(u)
=u\left[
 t\beta+c\{hX+a(1-h)X'\}
\right].
\]

The fresh risk is

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm cf}
&=c^2+\beta^2(1-t)^2
+c^2\left[h^2+a^2(1-h)^2\right]\\
&=3+\frac9{64}+3\left(\frac14+\frac1{64}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{63}{16}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus

\[
R_{\rm mean}<R_0,
\qquad
R_{\rm cf}<R_0,
\qquad
R_{\rm same}>R_0.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The exact reuse debt is

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm cf}
=
2c^2ha(1-h)
=
\frac38.
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

This counterexample has all of the requested qualitative features:

- deterministic labels;
- the mean and correction use the same kernel family;
- the mean alone improves the baseline;
- a genuinely independent/cross-fitted mean correction improves the baseline;
- reusing the same observation in the mean and residual correction reverses the sign.

The obstruction is representation misspecification: the linear kernel cannot represent the constant component `c`. The same observation imprints that unrepresented component into both fitted stages, creating an aligned reuse term.

Therefore the following route is **DISPROVED** even in a one-dimensional KRR/KRR system:

> “If the learned structured mean is individually better and the cross-fitted residual correction helps, then fitting both stages on the same observations cannot hurt.”

`A non-asymptotic theory of Kernel Ridge Regression: deterministic equivalents, test error, and GCV estimator`, arXiv:2403.08938.

`Generalized equivalences between subsampling and ridge regularization`, arXiv:2305.18496.

They establish proportional-asymptotic structural/risk equivalences for linear ridge estimators along joint subsampling/ridge paths under moment assumptions. They give a controlled high-dimensional spectral characterization of the second gradient step and explicitly find a qualitative independent-batch versus reused-batch gap: independent batches restrict learned directions more strongly, while reuse can expose directions with information exponent above one.
