# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**, but this run isolates a dependence term that the released ten-seed campaign cannot see.

The fixed-population identity

\[
B(\lambda)=\sum_j\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right)^2r_j^2,
\qquad r=f^\star-m,
\]

is retained exactly as a spectral squared-bias identity for a **fixed** population mean `m` and a **fixed** population kernel operator. It is not used as a theorem about a learned mean, learned representation, selected correction, or same-observation training.

The principal new theorem is an exact Hilbert-space decomposition of the same-observation squared-risk debt into

1. a **labeled-pool covariance** that remains even after averaging over all training seeds; and
2. a **procedure/seed covariance conditional on that pool**.

This distinction is source-critical. The final manuscript's structural-mechanics campaign repeats the procedure at ten seeds, but the public 20,000-case training/model-selection pool and the public 20,000-case test block remain fixed. A seed changes the validation permutation, member initialization and batch order, the stacking half-split, kernel-tuning subsamples and the conformal draw. Thus the reported seed spread probes procedural randomness on one realized labeled pool; it does **not** resample an independent labeled pool.

The exact squared-risk decomposition is

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}
=
D_{\rm data}+D_{\rm proc},
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm data}
=2E\langle \mu_B(P)-EB,\mu_G(P)-EG\rangle,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm proc}
=2E\langle B-\mu_B(P),G-\mu_G(P)\rangle,
}
\]

with `P` the complete iid labeled pool, `B` the uncorrected learner, `G` the correction increment, and

\[
\mu_B(P)=E[B\mid P],\qquad \mu_G(P)=E[G\mid P].
\]

No fixed representation, differentiability, Gaussian response noise, linear mean, or soft selection rule is assumed.

A conditional independent-seed ghost identifies `D_proc` exactly, while a genuinely independent-pool ghost adds `D_data`. Consequently, a small across-seed standard deviation, or even **zero** seed variability, does not certify that same-observation reuse is harmless.

A finite-sample hierarchical bound is also proved. If the procedure randomness is exogenous and `P^{(i)}` replaces complete observation `i` independently, define synchronized replacement RMS quantities

\[
A_i^2=E\|B(P,\Theta)-B(P^{(i)},\Theta)\|_{L^2(P_U)}^2,
\]

\[
C_i^2=E\|G(P,\Theta)-G(P^{(i)},\Theta)\|_{L^2(P_U)}^2.
\]

Let `B^\circ,G^\circ` be conditionally iid procedure copies given `P` and put

\[
V_{\rm proc}(B)=E\|B-B^\circ\|^2,
\qquad
V_{\rm proc}(G)=E\|G-G^\circ\|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}|
\le
\sqrt{V_{\rm proc}(B)V_{\rm proc}(G)}
+
\sum_{i=1}^n A_iC_i.
}
\]

The first term is a seed/procedure debt. The second is an aligned labeled-data debt. Vanishing per-row influence by itself is insufficient: an exact Rademacher example has `A_i=C_i=sqrt(2/n)->0` for every coordinate while the total reuse debt stays equal to `2`.

For the manuscript's actual unsquared mean-relative metric there is a first-moment transfer theorem requiring no inverse-square output-norm hypothesis:

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,full\,ghost}|
\le
E\frac{\|G-G^\circ\|}{\|Y\|}
+
E\frac{\|G^\circ-G'\|}{\|Y\|}.
}
\]

The first term is procedural; the second is labeled-data reuse. Existing ten-seed prediction arrays, if complete comparator/correction arrays can be recovered, can measure the first component. They cannot identify the second component without an independent-data, half-split, deletion, refresh, or equivalent stability bridge.

A sharp mean-relative counterexample makes the point exact: all seeds can be identical, an independent-data correction can improve the uncorrected learner, and the same-data correction can still worsen it.

\[

\[
\boxed{
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j
\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2.

This is elementary spectral calculus. It does not compare the generalization of two random algorithms that learn their mean, representation, stacking weights, ridge/kernel scale and correction from overlapping observations.

# 4. `P15-SEED-DATA-COVARIANCE-41` — PROVED

## 4.1 Setup

Let

\[
P=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)
\]

be the complete iid labeled training/model-selection pool, where for the mechanics source one may take `Z_i=(U_i,G(U_i))`. Let `Theta` be exogenous procedure randomness, independent of `P`. It may encode role assignment, validation permutation, network initialization, minibatch order, tuning subsamples, checkpoint choice and any other randomized implementation step.

Let

\[
B=B(P,\Theta_B)
\]

be the uncorrected learner and write the corrected learner as

\[
F=B+G,
\qquad
G=G(P,\Theta_G).
\]

`B` and `G` are random elements of

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(P_U;\mathbb R^q).
\]

Within one procedure run, `Theta_B` and `Theta_G` may be arbitrarily dependent: for example they may share a seed and learned representation. Across a ghost copy, the joint procedure is redrawn according to the same law.

Let `(U,Y)` be a fresh test pair independent of all training randomness. Define

\[
R_{\rm same}
=E\|Y-B(U)-G(U)\|^2.
\]

Let `P'` be an independent iid labeled pool and `Theta_G'` an independent procedure draw. Put

\[
G'=G(P',\Theta_G')
\]

and

\[
R_{\rm full\,ghost}
=E\|Y-B(U)-G'(U)\|^2.
\]

Define conditional procedure means

\[
\mu_B(P)=E[B\mid P],
\qquad
\mu_G(P)=E[G\mid P].
\]

## 4.2 Exact identity

Because `G` and `G'` have the same marginal law, their squared-norm terms and their fresh-target cross terms agree. Since the full ghost is independent of `B` as a random element of `H`,

\[
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}
=
2\left(E\langle B,G\rangle
-
\langle EB,EG\rangle\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Now decompose

\[
B-EB
=
\big(\mu_B(P)-EB\big)
+
\big(B-\mu_B(P)\big),
\]

and similarly for `G`. Conditional centering kills the two cross-level terms. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}
=
D_{\rm data}+D_{\rm proc},
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm data}
=2E\left\langle
\mu_B(P)-EB,
\mu_G(P)-EG
\right\rangle,
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm proc}
=2E\left\langle
B-\mu_B(P),
G-\mu_G(P)
\right\rangle.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is simply the Hilbert-space law of total covariance, but here it gives an exact operational separation of the same-observation debt.

### Interpretation

`D_data` is the covariance generated by using the same labeled pool after averaging over all procedural randomness. `D_proc` is the extra covariance from sharing role assignment / initialization / minibatch / selection randomness conditional on that pool.

Neither term has a universal sign. In particular, seed variability is not synonymous with data-reuse dependence.

# 5. `P15-SEED-GHOST-IDENTITY-41` — PROVED

Construct a **conditional seed ghost** `G^circ` as follows. It uses the same labeled pool `P` as `B`, but conditional on `P` it is generated from a fresh procedure draw and is independent of `B` given `P`. It has the same conditional law as `G` given `P`.

Define

\[
R_{\rm seed\,ghost}
=E\|Y-B(U)-G^\circ(U)\|^2.
\]

Conditional independence gives

\[
E[\langle B,G^\circ\rangle\mid P]
=
\langle\mu_B(P),\mu_G(P)\rangle.
\]

The same marginal cancellation as in Section 4 yields

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm seed\,ghost}
=D_{\rm proc},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm seed\,ghost}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}
=D_{\rm data}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus independent reseeding at fixed labeled data removes only the procedural component. It does not manufacture an independent-data experiment.

This distinction is exact even if the procedure randomness includes the validation carve and tuning subsamples, as in the released campaign.

# 6. `P15-HIERARCHICAL-REUSE-BOUND-41` — PROVED

The exact decomposition gives a finite-sample bound with two independently interpretable pieces.

## 6.1 Procedure part

Let `(B^circ,G^circ)` denote a conditionally iid copy of the complete joint procedure given `P`. Define

\[
V_{\rm proc}(B)
=E\|B-B^\circ\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\qquad
V_{\rm proc}(G)
=E\|G-G^\circ\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\]

Conditional iid symmetry gives

\[
V_{\rm proc}(B)
=2E\|B-\mu_B(P)\|^2,
\]

and the analogous identity for `G`. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
|D_{\rm proc}|
\le
\sqrt{V_{\rm proc}(B)V_{\rm proc}(G)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

## 6.2 Data part

Let `P^{(i)}` replace full observation `Z_i` by an independent iid copy, while the same exogenous procedure seed `Theta` is used on the two datasets. Define

\[
A_i^2
=
E\|B(P,\Theta)-B(P^{(i)},\Theta)\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\]

\[
C_i^2
=
E\|G(P,\Theta)-G(P^{(i)},\Theta)\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\]

For the conditional mean maps,

\[
\bar A_i^2
:=E\|\mu_B(P)-\mu_B(P^{(i)})\|^2
\le A_i^2
\]

by Jensen, and similarly `bar C_i <= C_i`.

Apply the Hilbert-valued Doob covariance decomposition to `mu_B(P),mu_G(P)` along the iid coordinates. If `D_i^B,D_i^G` are their martingale increments, then

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(\mu_B,\mu_G)
=
\sum_i E\langle D_i^B,D_i^G\rangle.
\]

The standard replacement comparison gives

\[
E\|D_i^B\|^2\le\frac12\bar A_i^2,
\qquad
E\|D_i^G\|^2\le\frac12\bar C_i^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
|D_{\rm data}|
\le
\sum_{i=1}^n \bar A_i\bar C_i
\le
\sum_{i=1}^n A_iC_i.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining `(6.1)` and `(6.2)` proves

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}|
\le
\sqrt{V_{\rm proc}(B)V_{\rm proc}(G)}
+
\sum_{i=1}^n A_iC_i.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This theorem permits the mean, representation, validation branch, ridge/kernel choice and correction to be learned from the same observations.

### Exchangeable simplification

For a permutation-equivariant algorithm with common replacement scales `A_i=A` and `C_i=C`,

\[
|D_{\rm data}|\le nAC.
\]

Therefore `A,C -> 0` is not by itself enough. The correct requirement is an aggregate rate such as `nAC -> 0`, or a sharper aligned-coordinate cancellation theorem.

# 7. `P15-THREELEVEL-SEED-ANOVA-41` — PROVED

The final source's single seed mixes role assignment with optimizer randomness. The exact covariance law can be refined accordingly.

Let `R` denote randomized role assignment / validation split / tuning subsampling conditional on the pool `P`, and let `Xi` denote the remaining optimizer randomness conditional on `(P,R)`. Put

\[
\mu_B^P=E[B\mid P],
\qquad
\mu_B^{PR}=E[B\mid P,R],
\]

and analogously for `G`.

Then

\[
B-EB
=
(\mu_B^P-EB)
+(\mu_B^{PR}-\mu_B^P)
+(B-\mu_B^{PR}),
\]

with the three summands orthogonal in `L^2`; the same is true for `G`. All cross-level pairings vanish by conditional centering. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm full\,ghost}
={}&2E\langle\mu_B^P-EB,\mu_G^P-EG\rangle\\
&+2E\langle\mu_B^{PR}-\mu_B^P,\mu_G^{PR}-\mu_G^P\rangle\\
&+2E\langle B-\mu_B^{PR},G-\mu_G^{PR}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The three terms are, respectively:

1. labeled-pool reuse;
2. role/split/selection randomness on that pool;
3. residual optimizer/training randomness on fixed pool and roles.

The released ten-seed standard deviation mixes terms 2 and 3 in a marginal variability statistic; it still contains no direct measurement of term 1.

# 8. `P15-SEED-PAIR-USTAT-41` — PROVED

The procedural covariance can be estimated from paired prediction arrays without fitting a new hybrid.

Conditional on a fixed pool `P`, let

\[
(B_a,G_a),\qquad a=1,\ldots,M,
\]

be iid complete procedure runs, where within each pair `B_a,G_a` retain their actual same-seed dependence. For `a != b`, independence across seeds gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[\langle B_a-B_b,G_a-G_b\rangle\mid P]
&=2\Big(
E[\langle B,G\rangle\mid P]
-
\langle\mu_B(P),\mu_G(P)\rangle
\Big).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\widehat D_{\rm proc}(P)
=
\binom M2^{-1}
\sum_{a<b}
\langle B_a-B_b,G_a-G_b\rangle_{\mathcal H}
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

is conditionally unbiased for the procedural contribution

\[
D_{\rm proc}(P)
=2\,\operatorname{Cov}(B,G\mid P).
\]

Similarly,

\[
\binom M2^{-1}\sum_{a<b}\|F_a-F_b\|^2
\]

is conditionally unbiased for `V_proc(F)`.

On the fixed public test table, one can replace the `H` inner product by the empirical test average to obtain an exact finite-table seed-pair statistic. That statistic describes the realized table and seed distribution; population confidence would require the appropriate evaluation-sampling assumptions.

# 9. `P15-SEED-BAGGING-LIMIT-41` — PROVED

Suppose `(B_a,G_a)` are conditionally iid joint procedure runs on the same labeled pool and define averaged predictors

\[
\bar B_M=\frac1M\sum_{a=1}^M B_a,
\qquad
\bar G_M=\frac1M\sum_{a=1}^M G_a.
\]

Their conditional means remain `mu_B(P),mu_G(P)`, while conditional cross-covariance scales by `1/M`. Therefore the same-versus-full-ghost squared-risk debt of the averaged pair is

\[
\boxed{
D_M
=D_{\rm data}+\frac1M D_{\rm proc}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Thus actual predictor bagging can average away procedural covariance but cannot remove the labeled-pool reuse term. In particular,

\[
D_M\to D_{\rm data}
\qquad(M\to\infty).
\]

The manuscript reports the distribution of separately trained seed runs rather than deploying this exact averaged predictor, so `(9.1)` is not a reinterpretation of the reported mean ± standard deviation. Its role is structural: even infinite seed averaging cannot certify away the data-overlap term.

# 10. `P15-MEANREL-SEED-DATA-TRANSFER-41` — PROVED

Now use the source metric

\[
\ell_{\rm rel}(f;u,y)
=\frac{\|y-f(u)\|}{\|y\|}.
\]

Let `G^circ` be the same-pool conditional seed ghost and `G'` the independent-pool full ghost. Pointwise reverse triangle inequality gives

\[
\left|
\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G;U,Y)
-
\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G^\circ;U,Y)
\right|
\le
\frac{\|G(U)-G^\circ(U)\|}{\|Y\|},
\]

and similarly

\[
\left|
\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G^\circ;U,Y)
-
\ell_{\rm rel}(B+G';U,Y)
\right|
\le
\frac{\|G^\circ(U)-G'(U)\|}{\|Y\|}.
\]

After expectation,

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,full\,ghost}|
\le a_{\rm proc}+a_{\rm data},
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

where

\[
a_{\rm proc}
=E\frac{\|G-G^\circ\|}{\|Y\|},
\qquad
 a_{\rm data}
=E\frac{\|G^\circ-G'\|}{\|Y\|}.
\]

If the full ghost beats the uncorrected comparator by

\[
\Gamma_{\rm ghost}^{\rm rel}
=R_{\rm rel}(B)-R_{\rm rel}(B+G'),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm same}^{\rm rel}
\ge
\Gamma_{\rm ghost}^{\rm rel}
-a_{\rm proc}-a_{\rm data}.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

This theorem requires only finiteness of the displayed first moments. It does not assume `E||Y||^{-2}<infinity`, a deterministic positive output-norm floor, Gaussian response noise, RKHS membership, or differentiability.

The existing seed campaign can at most inform `a_proc` on the fixed public pool. The data term still needs an independent split, deletion, refresh, correlated replica, or another genuine data-stability argument. This is why the preceding half-split and deletion theorems remain necessary rather than superseded.

# 11. Exact counterexamples and falsification tests

## 11.1 `P15-SEEDZERO-MEANREL-SIGNFLIP-CE-41` — PROVED

Let the complete training pool contain one Rademacher datum

\[
Z\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad P\{Z=1\}=P\{Z=-1\}=1/2,
\]

and suppose there is **no procedure randomness at all**. Thus every seed returns exactly the same predictors and

\[
V_{\rm proc}(B)=V_{\rm proc}(G)=0,
\qquad D_{\rm proc}=0.
\]

Take a constant fresh target

\[
Y=t=\frac65,
\]

an uncorrected predictor

\[
B_Z=Z,
\]

and correction increment

\[
G_Z=t+\frac12 Z.
\]

All predictors may be viewed as constant functions of the fresh query.

The uncorrected source-metric risk is

\[
R_{\rm rel}(B)
=E\frac{|t-Z|}{t}
=1.
\]

For an independent training copy `Z'`, the full-ghost corrected predictor is

\[
B_Z+G_{Z'}=Z+t+\frac12Z',
\]

so

\[
R_{\rm rel,ghost}
=E\frac{|Z+\frac12Z'|}{t}
=\frac1t
=\frac56.
\]

Thus the independent-data correction improves by `1/6`.

With same-data reuse,

\[
B_Z+G_Z=t+\frac32Z,
\]

hence

\[
R_{\rm rel,same}
=\frac{3/2}{6/5}
=\frac54.
\]

The same-data correction worsens the uncorrected learner by `1/4`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
V_{\rm proc}=0
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{safe same-observation correction}.
}
\]

This counterexample has the exact qualitative form relevant to the source metric: independent-data correction helps while same-data correction hurts, even though repeated seeds are identical.

## 11.2 `P15-VANISHING-PERROW-NOTENOUGH-CE-41` — PROVED

Let

\[
P=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n),
\qquad Z_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Rad}(1),
\]

with no procedure randomness, and define

\[
B_P=G_P=rac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i.
\]

Take squared fresh target `Y=0`. Since `Var(B)=1`,

\[
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=2\operatorname{Cov}(B,G)=2
\]

for every `n`.

Replacing one coordinate by an independent copy gives

\[
B(P)-B(P^{(i)})
=\frac{Z_i-Z_i'}{\sqrt n},
\]

so

\[
A_i^2=C_i^2=\frac2n,
\qquad
A_i=C_i=\sqrt{\frac2n}\to0.
\]

Nevertheless

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nA_iC_i=2
}
\]

exactly. The hierarchical bound is attained at the correct order.

Hence the route

> “every single observation has vanishing influence, therefore same-sample dependence vanishes”

is **DISPROVED** without an aggregate rate.

# 13. High-dimensional / random-feature reduction and current literature

## `P15-RF-HIERARCHICAL-DE-41` — CONDITIONAL reduction

For a high-dimensional sequence indexed by `n`, write

\[
D_n=D_{{\rm data},n}+D_{{\rm proc},n}.
\]

If deterministic equivalents can be proved for the two pieces separately,

\[
D_{{\rm data},n}\to d,
\qquad
D_{{\rm proc},n}\to a,
\]

then the full same-observation debt converges to `d+a`. If one actually averages `M_n` conditionally iid jointly trained seed predictors, then

\[
D_{n,M_n}
=D_{{\rm data},n}+\frac1{M_n}D_{{\rm proc},n}.
\]

Thus `M_n -> infinity` removes the procedural term under boundedness but leaves the data-overlap limit `d`. A deterministic equivalent based only on seed variance is structurally incomplete.

Three current primary results were rechecked against this target:

1. **Misiakiewicz--Saeed, arXiv:2403.08938.** Their non-asymptotic KRR deterministic equivalent assumes spectral/concentration structure on a kernel eigendecomposition and controls test error through eigenvalues and target alignment. It is a powerful fixed-kernel ingredient, not an arbitrary same-response learned-representation theorem.

2. **Defilippis--Loureiro--Misiakiewicz, arXiv:2405.15699.** Their dimension-free random-feature ridge equivalent holds under feature concentration and depends on feature-map eigenvalues. Again, the random feature map is governed by explicit concentration hypotheses; it does not supply the joint labeled-data covariance of a multi-epoch response-trained representation and its residual correction.

3. **Moniri--Hassani, arXiv:2605.17767, submitted 18 May 2026.** Their linear-width two-layer analysis characterizes the second gradient step and proves a qualitative gap between independent and reused batches: batch reuse can learn directions inaccessible to the independent-batch update. This is direct evidence in a controlled learned-feature model that sample reuse is not intrinsically a penalty-only perturbation.

The live high-dimensional target is therefore not just a Marchenko--Pastur law for one Gram matrix. It is a two-level deterministic equivalent for

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P(\mu_B(P),\mu_G(P))
\]

and for the conditional procedure covariance, or an equivalent overlap/replacement representation with all hypotheses explicit.

### Claim audited

For fixed points `z_1,...,z_M in R^j`, consider the centered slab class

\[
S_c=\{z:|\langle z,c\rangle|\le\rho\},
\qquad \|c\|_2=1.
\]

P12 claims that the number `Pi_j(M)` of distinct membership patterns

\[
(1_{S_c}(z_1),\ldots,1_{S_c}(z_M))
\]

satisfies, for `1<=j<=M`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pi_j(M)
\le
\sum_{t=0}^j2^t\binom{2M}{t}
\le
\left(\frac{8eM}{j}\right)^j.
}
\tag{15.1}
\]

### Independent derivation

For each data point `z_i`, membership changes only when `c` crosses one of the two affine hyperplanes

\[
\langle z_i,c\rangle=\rho,
\qquad
\langle z_i,c\rangle=-\rho
\]

in coefficient space `R^j`. Therefore the membership pattern is constant on every face, including boundary faces, of an arrangement of `H=2M` affine hyperplanes. Restricting `c` to the unit sphere can only decrease the number of patterns.

Let `F(H,j)` be the maximal total number of faces of all dimensions in an arrangement of `H` affine hyperplanes in `R^j`. Adding the last hyperplane preserves old faces not cut and, on each face of the induced `(j-1)`-dimensional arrangement, creates at most one new on-hyperplane face and one additional split piece. Hence

\[
F(H,j)
\le
F(H-1,j)+2F(H-1,j-1).
\]

With the usual boundary values, induction gives

\[
F(H,j)
\le
\sum_{t=0}^j2^t\binom Ht.
\]

Setting `H=2M` proves the first inequality in `(15.1)`.

For `j<=M`, the terms `2^t C(2M,t)` are increasing for `0<=t<=j`, so

\[
\sum_{t=0}^j2^t\binom{2M}{t}
\le
(j+1)2^j\binom{2M}{j}.
\]

Now

\[
\binom{2M}{j}
\le
\left(\frac{2eM}{j}\right)^j
\]

and `j+1<=2^j`, yielding

\[
(j+1)2^j\binom{2M}{j}
\le
\left(\frac{8eM}{j}\right)^j.
\]

Every constant in the peer claim checks. No Warren theorem, density hypothesis, or asymptotic approximation is required for this lemma.

This is a concrete component audit. It is **not** one of the two still-required complete end-to-end OTHER-worker audits of P15 itself.
