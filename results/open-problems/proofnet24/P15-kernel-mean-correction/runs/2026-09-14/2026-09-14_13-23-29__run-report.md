# ProofNet24 P15 — Kernel mean correction

Run timestamp: 2026-09-14 13:23:29 Asia/Jerusalem

## Executive result

**STATUS: OPEN, with a new finite-sample learned-representation stability theorem.**

The previous P15 run already established three facts: (i) the exact conditional risk-difference identity for a residual KRR correction when the mean and kernel may be trained on the same sample; (ii) an exact integrated bound for the current pipeline's training-row/query-time mean mismatch; and (iii) a counterexample showing that same-sample residual correction is not universally monotone for population squared risk.

This run attacks the main remaining theorem-sized gap identified there: **how to transfer an improvement theorem from an honest/cross-fitted proxy to the actual same-sample system when the learned representation, Gram matrix, query vector, mean and residual labels all change.**

The main new theorem is an integrated perturbation inequality for two arbitrary realized kernel-ridge corrections. It is substantially sharper than the previous pointwise resolvent bound because it uses the fresh-query covariance operator and the RKHS inequality `Q <= kappa^2 K`, rather than bounding query vectors pointwise. A feature-map corollary reduces the learned-representation problem to directly interpretable train/test RMS feature stability quantities. Combining this with the previous label-mismatch theorem gives an explicit sufficient condition under which a positive gain of a cross-fitted/independent proxy survives in the deployed same-sample pipeline.

I also strengthen the same-sample counterexample: for a scalar target of constant norm one, the residual correction can worsen the **mean relative L2 error used by the manuscript for every finite ridge parameter**, not merely squared risk for a small-ridge range.

No independence between the full-sample mean, representation, correction and the training sample is assumed in the new transfer theorem. Dependence is isolated into measurable stability defects.

For a *fixed* mean and fixed kernel, if `r=f*-m=sum_j r_j psi_j` in the population kernel eigenbasis, the population ridge-bias identity is

### 1.5 Risk normalization caveat

The theorems below concern squared `L2(mu;R^q)` prediction risk, which is the natural risk behind the population spectral identity and the exact quadratic KRR algebra. The structural-mechanics tables report the **mean relative L2 norm**, `E ||error(U)||/||G(U)||`, which is not the same functional.

A weighted squared-risk version is immediate: replace `mu` by the finite measure `w(u)mu(du)` for bounded `0<=w<=W`; all `Q`-domination constants acquire a factor `sqrt(W)`. Thus squared relative risk is covered if `||G(U)||` is bounded away from zero. The sign of the manuscript's unsquared mean-relative metric is not automatically determined by the squared-risk theorem. The counterexample in Section 5 addresses that metric directly.

## 2. New theorem P15-STAB-02 — integrated stability under a changing learned representation

Let the output space be `R^q` with Euclidean norm. Fix the same `n` training input locations `X=(x_1,...,x_n)`. Consider two arbitrary realized correction systems; they may both have been learned from the same labels.

System 1:

`K in R^{n x n}`, `q(u)=(k(x_1,u),...,k(x_n,u))^T`,

`A=K+n lambda I`, `R in R^{n x q}`,

`c(u)=q(u)^T A^{-1} R`.

System 2:

`K'`, `q'(u)`, `A'=K'+n lambda' I`, `R'`,

`c'(u)=q'(u)^T A'^{-1}R'`.

Assume `lambda,lambda'>0` and `k'(u,u)<=kappa'^2` for every query `u`. Define

`D=E_U[(q(U)-q'(U))(q(U)-q'(U))^T]`,

`Q'=E_U[q'(U)q'(U)^T]`,

and

`E_A=A'-A=(K'-K)+n(lambda'-lambda)I`.

Then

**P15-STAB-02 (sharp integrated form).**

`||c-c'||_{L2(mu)}` is at most

`[tr(R^T A^{-1} D A^{-1} R)]^{1/2}`

`+ [kappa'/(2 sqrt(n lambda'))] || E_A A^{-1}R ||_F`

`+ [kappa'/(2 sqrt(n lambda'))] ||R-R'||_F`.                     (2.1)

Consequently, if

`r_rms=||R||_F/sqrt(n)`,

`rho_rms=||R-R'||_F/sqrt(n)`,

`d_q=sqrt(||D||_op/n)`,

`delta_A=||E_A||_op/n`,

then

`||c-c'||_{L2(mu)}`
`<= d_q r_rms/lambda`
` + kappa' delta_A r_rms/(2 lambda sqrt(lambda'))`
` + kappa' rho_rms/(2 sqrt(lambda'))`.                           (2.2)

For a common ridge `lambda'=lambda`, define `delta_K=||K-K'||_op/n`; then

`||c-c'||_{L2(mu)}`
`<= d_q r_rms/lambda`
` + kappa' delta_K r_rms/(2 lambda^{3/2})`
` + kappa' rho_rms/(2 sqrt(lambda))`.                            (2.3)

### Proof

The exact algebra is

`c-c'`
`=(q-q')^T A^{-1}R`
` + q'^T(A^{-1}-A'^{-1})R`
` + q'^T A'^{-1}(R-R')`.                                        (2.4)

The first term has exact integrated square

`E ||(q-q')^T A^{-1}R||^2`
`=tr(R^T A^{-1} D A^{-1} R)`.                                  (2.5)

For the second term, the resolvent identity gives

`A^{-1}-A'^{-1}=A'^{-1} E_A A^{-1}`.                            (2.6)

The RKHS covariance inequality from the previous P15 run is

`Q' <= kappa'^2 K'`.                                            (2.7)

Therefore

`E ||q'^T A'^{-1} E_A A^{-1}R||^2`
`<= kappa'^2 ||K'^{1/2} A'^{-1} E_A A^{-1}R||_F^2`.             (2.8)

Because `K'` commutes with `A'=K'+n lambda'I`,

`||K'^{1/2}A'^{-1}||_op`
`=max_{t>=0} sqrt(t)/(t+n lambda')`
`=1/(2 sqrt(n lambda'))`.                                       (2.9)

This gives the middle term of (2.1). The last term is the same computation with `R-R'`:

`E ||q'^T A'^{-1}(R-R')||^2`
`<= [kappa'^2/(4n lambda')] ||R-R'||_F^2`.                       (2.10)

Minkowski proves (2.1).

For (2.2), use

`tr(R^T A^{-1}D A^{-1}R)^{1/2}`
`<= sqrt(||D||_op) ||R||_F/(n lambda)`,

and

`||E_A A^{-1}R||_F <= ||E_A||_op ||R||_F/(n lambda)`.

No probabilistic independence enters the proof. After conditioning on all training randomness, this is a deterministic inequality.

### Why this is better than the previous pointwise resolvent bound

The previous P15 bound contained a factor `||q'(u)||`, which is typically of order `sqrt(n)` and was therefore poorly matched to population risk. Here the query vector is integrated before being bounded. The RKHS covariance relation converts the dangerous factor into the sharp ridge filter

`max_t sqrt(t)/(t+n lambda)=1/(2 sqrt(n lambda))`.

The middle term in (2.1),

`[kappa'/(2 sqrt(n lambda'))] ||E_A A^{-1}R||_F`,

is also directly computable once the two fitted systems are available and can be much smaller than the worst-case `lambda^{-3/2}` corollary.

The theorem also exposes a separate hyperparameter-selection obstruction: if the two systems select different ridge values, `n(lambda'-lambda)I` enters exactly like a Gram perturbation. A finite grid does not make this vanish automatically.

## 3. New corollary P15-FEAT-01 — learned feature stability in RMS norms

Suppose both kernels are pullbacks of the same base kernel `bar k` through learned representations `phi,phi'`:

`k_phi(x,u)=bar k(phi(x),phi(u))`,

`k_phi'(x,u)=bar k(phi'(x),phi'(u))`.

Assume the base kernel is jointly Lipschitz:

`|bar k(a,b)-bar k(a',b')| <= L (||a-a'||+||b-b'||)`.           (3.1)

Define feature-stability defects

`delta_phi,X^2=(1/n) sum_i ||phi(x_i)-phi'(x_i)||^2`,

`delta_phi,mu^2=E_U ||phi(U)-phi'(U)||^2`.                       (3.2)

Then

`delta_K=||K-K'||_op/n <= 2 L delta_phi,X`,                     (3.3)

and

`d_q <= sqrt(2) L sqrt(delta_phi,X^2+delta_phi,mu^2)`.          (3.4)

Proof of (3.3): if `e_i=||phi(x_i)-phi'(x_i)||`, then

`|(K-K')_{ij}|<=L(e_i+e_j)`.

Hence

`||K-K'||_op<=||K-K'||_F`
`<= [sum_{ij} L^2(e_i+e_j)^2]^{1/2}`
`<=2Ln delta_phi,X`.

Proof of (3.4): for `v(u)=q(u)-q'(u)`,

`|v_i(u)|<=L(e_i+e(u))`,

so

`tr D=E||v(U)||^2`
`<=2L^2[sum_i e_i^2+nE e(U)^2]`
`=2nL^2(delta_phi,X^2+delta_phi,mu^2)`.

Since `||D||_op<=tr D`, (3.4) follows.

If the correction labels are consistent with two means `m,m'` on the same labeled training inputs,

`R=Y-m(X)`, `R'=Y-m'(X)`,

then

`rho_rms=delta_m,X := n^{-1/2}||m(X)-m'(X)||_F`.

For the same ridge `lambda`, (2.3) therefore becomes

`||c-c'||_{L2(mu)}`
`<= [sqrt(2)L r_rms/lambda] sqrt(delta_phi,X^2+delta_phi,mu^2)`
` + [kappa' L r_rms/lambda^{3/2}] delta_phi,X`
` + [kappa'/(2 sqrt(lambda))] delta_m,X`.                        (3.5)

This is the first P15 theorem that directly converts **learned-representation stability** into **population correction stability** without requiring the learned representation to be independent of the training labels.

For fixed positive ridge, `delta_phi,X`, `delta_phi,mu`, and `delta_m,X` tending to zero imply correction equivalence. If `lambda=lambda_n->0`, the theorem makes the required rates explicit and shows why the nearly interpolating regime is much harder.

## 4. New theorem P15-GAIN-02 — transfer an honest/cross-fitted gain to the deployed same-sample predictor

Let `m',c'` be any proxy system for which a positive population root-risk gain has been proved or independently certified:

`gamma' = ||G-m'||_{L2(mu)} - ||G-m'-c'||_{L2(mu)} > 0`.        (4.1)

Let `m,c` be the full-sample **consistent-label** mean/correction pair, and let `c_act` be the actual deployed correction made from the manuscript's mixed training-row labels.

Define

`delta_m,mu=||m-m'||_{L2(mu)}`,

`delta_c=||c-c'||_{L2(mu)}`,

`eta_Delta=||c_act-c||_{L2(mu)}`.

By two applications of the triangle inequality,

`||G-m|| >= ||G-m'||-delta_m,mu`,

`||G-m-c_act||`
`<=||G-m'-c'||+delta_m,mu+delta_c+eta_Delta`.

Therefore

`[root-risk gain of deployed system]`
`>= gamma' - 2 delta_m,mu - delta_c - eta_Delta`.                (4.2)

Using the already-proved mixed-label theorem,

`eta_Delta <= kappa delta_Delta,rms/(2 sqrt(lambda))`,

`delta_Delta,rms=n^{-1/2}||m_full(X)-M_tr||_F`.                  (4.3)

Hence a sufficient condition for strict improvement of the actual same-sample deployed predictor is

`gamma' > 2 delta_m,mu + delta_c`
`         + kappa delta_Delta,rms/(2 sqrt(lambda))`,             (4.4)

where `delta_c` may be bounded by P15-STAB-02 or, for learned pullback kernels, P15-FEAT-01.

Combining (3.5) and (4.3), one fully explicit fixed-ridge sufficient condition is

`gamma' > 2 delta_m,mu`
` + [sqrt(2)L r_rms/lambda] sqrt(delta_phi,X^2+delta_phi,mu^2)`
` + [kappa L r_rms/lambda^{3/2}] delta_phi,X`
` + [kappa/(2 sqrt(lambda))](delta_m,X+delta_Delta,rms)`.        (4.5)

This statement is deterministic conditional on the realized training procedures. Consequently, if a stochastic learner satisfies the right-hand stability inequalities with probability at least `1-delta`, then the same improvement conclusion holds on that event. There is no assumption that `m`, `phi`, `K` or the residual correction are independent of the training sample.

This is the clean cross-fitting benchmark requested by the P15 program:

1. obtain/prove a positive gain `gamma'` for an honest or cross-fitted proxy;
2. control mean stability on training and fresh inputs;
3. control representation stability on training and fresh inputs;
4. control the historical/query-time label defect `Delta`;
5. check (4.4) or (4.5).

For a fixed positive ridge and a nonvanishing proxy margin, `o(1)` stability of all four defects is enough. With vanishing ridge, the explicit powers of `lambda` are real obligations, not notation.

## 5. Stronger counterexample in the manuscript's mean-relative metric

The previous run disproved universal monotonicity for squared population risk over a positive-ridge range. The same construction can be shifted so that the target norm is identically one; it then disproves monotonic improvement of the **mean relative L2 error for every finite ridge parameter**.

Let the input space be `{a,b}` with

`P(U=a)=epsilon`, `P(U=b)=1-epsilon`, `0<epsilon<1/2`.

Let the scalar target be

`G(a)=G(b)=1`.

Take one training observation, `n=1`, and the constant PSD kernel `k=1`. Define a data-dependent mean algorithm:

- if `X_1=a`, set `m_S(a)=2`, `m_S(b)=1`;
- if `X_1=b`, set `m_S` identically equal to 1.

On the event `X_1=a`, the training residual is `-1`; KRR with ridge `lambda>=0` produces the constant correction

`c(u)=-1/(1+lambda)`.

The uncorrected mean-relative error is exactly

`L0=epsilon`,

because `|G|=1` at both points. The corrected mean-relative error is

`L1=[epsilon lambda+(1-epsilon)]/(1+lambda)`.

Therefore

`L1-L0=(1-2epsilon)/(1+lambda)>0`                                (5.1)

for **every finite lambda>=0**.

On `X_1=b`, both predictors are exact. Hence the expected-over-training-sample mean-relative risk is also strictly worsened for every finite ridge:

`E_S L1 - E_S L0 = epsilon(1-2epsilon)/(1+lambda)>0`.            (5.2)

For squared risk the corrected conditional value remains

`[(1-epsilon)+epsilon lambda^2]/(1+lambda)^2`,

which exceeds the baseline `epsilon` exactly when

`lambda < (1-2epsilon)/(2epsilon)`.

So ridge regularization can eventually protect squared risk in this toy example, but it does **not** restore a universal monotonicity theorem for the manuscript's mean-relative metric.

This counterexample keeps labels deterministic, noise-free and the kernel positive semidefinite. The only pathology is the one the P15 program is meant to control: the learned mean uses the same observation that generates its correction label and can place the fitted residual in a direction whose kernel extrapolation is harmful on the high-mass unseen point.

## 6. Independent certification benchmark

If all model fitting, feature learning and hyperparameter selection are complete before an untouched certification sample is drawn, the sign of the final gain can be certified without analyzing internal dependence.

Let `Z_j=ell_0(U_j)-ell_1(U_j)` be paired loss differences on `N_c` independent certification points and suppose `|Z_j|<=M` almost surely. Hoeffding's inequality gives, with probability at least `1-delta`,

`E Z >= (1/N_c)sum_j Z_j - M sqrt(2 log(1/delta)/N_c)`.          (6.1)

Thus a paired empirical gain above that margin certifies a positive population gain.

This is a benchmark, not a theorem about the manuscript's existing validation block: that block is already used for checkpoints, stacking and kernel selection. A theorem-backed post-selection sign certificate requires a genuinely untouched split or nested cross-fitting.

`C=M_e^{-1/2} L M_e^{-1/2}`,

`B=M_e^{-1/2} A M_e^{-1/2}`.

Because `0<=A<=M<=M_e`, one has `0<=B<=I`. Also

`K_e=M_e^{1/2}(I+C)M_e^{1/2}`.

`tr(M_e^{-1}A)-tr(K_e^{-1}A)`
`=tr([I-(I+C)^{-1}]B)`.                                        (7.1)

`D_C=I-(I+C)^{-1}=C(I+C)^{-1}`.

## 9. Failed routes and sharp obstructions

1. **Population spectral bias alone is insufficient.** The identity `B(lambda)=sum [lambda/(lambda_j+lambda)]^2 r_j^2` assumes a fixed population residual and operator. It contains no control of the adaptive empirical cross-moment that decides same-sample improvement.

2. **Training residual reduction is insufficient.** The counterexample in Section 5 fits a nonzero observed residual but transports it to a higher-mass unseen point and worsens population error.

3. **Cross-fitting is not by itself an improvement theorem.** It creates an honest proxy on which a gain may be analyzable or certifiable, but the gain must still be positive and the full-sample system must be close enough for (4.4).

4. **A pointwise query-vector bound is unnecessarily lossy.** It pays `||q(u)||=Theta(sqrt n)` in common bounded-kernel designs. The integrated theorem uses `Q<=kappa^2K` and recovers the ridge filter `1/(2 sqrt(n lambda))`.

5. **Changing selected hyperparameters can break naive leave-one-out continuity.** If a fold or a nearby representation switches from one ridge-grid value to another, the perturbation contains `n(lambda'-lambda)I`. Stability of the representation alone does not imply stability of the selected KRR estimator.

6. **Vanishing ridge is genuinely delicate.** Even the integrated theorem carries `lambda^{-1}`, `lambda^{-1/2}` and, in its crude Gram perturbation corollary, `lambda^{-3/2}`. Fixed-`lambda` stability does not automatically extend to interpolation.

7. **The manuscript's reported mean-relative metric is not the squared risk of the spectral identity.** A sign theorem must either work directly with that loss, use a weighted squared-risk formulation under additional target-norm assumptions, or certify the actual paired metric on untouched data.

### DISPROVED / REJECTED

1. **Universal same-sample monotonicity:** false, for squared risk and, more strongly, for mean-relative error.

2. **Population eigenbasis bias => empirical same-sample gain:** false without alignment/stability.

3. **Small training residual => smaller fresh-input error:** false.

6. **One may ignore the current `M_tr` versus `m_full(X)` distinction:** false for a theorem; the manuscript explicitly documents this mismatch.
