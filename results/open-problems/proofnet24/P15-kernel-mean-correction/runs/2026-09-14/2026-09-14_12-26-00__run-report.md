## SOURCE RECOVERY AND EXACT EMPIRICAL SETUP

I recovered the July 2026 manuscript from Gmail message `19f42944fc201768` (subject “Some interesting results”, attachment `main (10).pdf`), the current JMLR repository `yspennstate/neural-means-kernel-corrections`, and the kernel-learning shortlist from Gmail message `1a09b043481ea995`.

The July structural-mechanics setup is: 40,000 input/output pairs; the first 20,000 are the available training block and the last 20,000 the test block. Within the 20,000 training budget, 1,000 are held out for validation and 19,000 are used for fitting. Neural means are trained on the fitting rows; a kernel-conditioned refiner uses four-fold out-of-fold kernel predictions during training and the full-data kernel predictor at test. Global stack weights are fit on validation, and a per-pixel affine stack is fit on one half of validation and accepted on the other half.

The current implementation then performs an exact Matérn-5/2 residual kernel correction. If `X=(u_1,...,u_n)` are the fitting inputs, `K` is their Gram matrix and `R` is the training residual matrix, the correction is

\[
\widehat r_\lambda(u)=k(u,X)(K+n\lambda I)^{-1}R.
\]

The code uses `n=19,000` in the high-data regime, searches scale multipliers and `lambda in {1e-6,1e-5,1e-3}` on validation using an 8,000-row fitting subsample, then refits the selected pair on all fitting rows.

A crucial implementation detail is that the correction labels are not necessarily residuals of a single query-time mean. The current manuscript explicitly defines

\[
\Delta=m_{\rm full}(X)-M_{\rm tr},\qquad R_{\rm tr}=G(X)-M_{\rm tr},
\]

where `M_tr` is the matrix of training-row predictions used to form correction labels and `m_full` is the deployed query-time mean. The kernel member contributes foldwise/out-of-fold training predictions while neural channels can contribute in-sample training predictions. The manuscript already proves the pointwise bound

\[
\|\text{error}(u)\|\le \|G-m_{\rm full}\|_K\widetilde P_\lambda(u)+\|c_\lambda(u)^T\Delta\|,
\]

but this does not yet give a population comparison between the corrected and uncorrected same-sample estimators.

The kernel-learning shortlist gives the population-eigenbasis identity for a fixed mean `m` and residual `r=f^*-m`,

\[
B(\lambda)=\sum_j\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2.
\]

That identity is useful intuition, but it does not imply empirical same-sample improvement because the empirical correction operator, the learned mean, and possibly the representation all depend on the training sample.

## NEW RESULT 1 — EXACT CONDITIONAL IMPROVEMENT IDENTITY

Fix an arbitrary realized training sample `S`. The mean `m_S` and kernel `k_S` may have been learned from `S` in any way; after conditioning on `S` they are simply fixed functions. Let

\[
r_S(u)=G(u)-m_S(u),\quad A=K+n\lambda I,\quad q(u)=k_S(X,u)\in\mathbb R^n,
\]

\[
R=r_S(X),\qquad \alpha=A^{-1}R.
\]

Assume for this theorem that the correction labels are consistent with the deployed mean, i.e. `R=r_S(X)`. Define the conditional fresh-input squared risks

\[
\mathcal R_0(S)=\mathbb E_U\|r_S(U)\|_2^2,
\]

\[
\mathcal R_1(S)=\mathbb E_U\|r_S(U)-q(U)^T\alpha\|_2^2,
\]

where `U~mu` is independent of `S`. Put

\[
Q_S=\mathbb E_U[q(U)q(U)^T]\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},
\]

\[
B_S=\mathbb E_U[q(U)r_S(U)^T]\in\mathbb R^{n\times q}.
\]

Then, exactly,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_1(S)-\mathcal R_0(S)=-2\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TB_S)+\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TQ_S\alpha).}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the squared norm inside the fresh-input expectation. The cross term is

\[
\mathbb E\,r_S(U)^Tq(U)^T\alpha=\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TB_S),
\]

and the correction-energy term is

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TQ_S\alpha).
\]

No independence between `m_S`, `k_S` and `S` is used.

Therefore the consistent empirical residual correction improves the conditional population squared risk if and only if

\[
\boxed{2\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TB_S)>\operatorname{tr}(\alpha^TQ_S\alpha).}
\]

This is the finite-sample same-sample analogue of the favorable spectral-filter picture behind the population identity. It exposes the exact missing quantity: alignment of the fitted correction with the fresh-input residual must dominate the fresh-input energy of the correction. Same-sample dependence does not invalidate the identity; it makes the sign nonautomatic.

Interpretation: the population eigenbasis formula effectively builds in the correct population covariance/alignment. In the empirical adaptive problem, `B_S` and `Q_S` are random and coupled to `alpha` through `S`. A theorem that forces the sign must control this adaptive cross-moment, not merely show that the empirical residual on `X` is reduced.

## NEW RESULT 2 — EXACT L2 MISMATCH FORMULA AND A RIDGE-STABILITY BOUND

Now return to the actual mixed-label setup of the manuscript. Freeze the deployed mean `m_full` and kernel `K`. Let

\[
A=K+n\lambda I,\qquad \Delta=m_{\rm full}(X)-M_{\rm tr}.
\]

Let `Ghat_con` be the correction using the consistent residual labels `G(X)-m_full(X)`, and `Ghat_act` the actual correction using `G(X)-M_tr`. Then for every query `u`,

\[
\widehat G_{\rm act}(u)-\widehat G_{\rm con}(u)=q(u)^TA^{-1}\Delta.
\]

Let

\[
Q=\mathbb E_U[q(U)q(U)^T].
\]

Then the integrated discrepancy is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat G_{\rm act}-\widehat G_{\rm con}\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2
=\operatorname{tr}(\Delta^TA^{-1}QA^{-1}\Delta).
}
\]

This identity is already useful because it converts the implementation mismatch into a measurable matrix quadratic form.

If the kernel satisfies `k(u,u)<=kappa^2` for all `u` and `lambda>0`, then

\[
Q\preceq \kappa^2K
\]

in Loewner order. Indeed, for any `a in R^n`, `f_a=sum_i a_i k(u_i,.)` has

\[
a^TQa=\|f_a\|_{L^2(\mu)}^2=\langle f_a,Tf_a\rangle_H
\le \|T\|\|f_a\|_H^2\le \kappa^2 a^TKa,
\]

where `T` is the RKHS covariance operator and `||T||<=E k(U,U)<=kappa^2`.

Since `A` commutes with `K`, the eigenvalues of `A^{-1}KA^{-1}` are

\[
\frac{t}{(t+n\lambda)^2}\le \frac1{4n\lambda}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat G_{\rm act}-\widehat G_{\rm con}\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\le \frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{n\lambda}}\|\Delta\|_F.
}
\]

Writing

\[
\delta_{\rm rms}^2=\frac1n\|\Delta\|_F^2,
\]

we obtain the clean dimension-free form

\[
\boxed{
\eta:=\|\widehat G_{\rm act}-\widehat G_{\rm con}\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\le \frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt\lambda}\delta_{\rm rms}.
}
\]

For the normalized Matérn kernel used in the implementation, `kappa=1`.

This gives an immediate transfer theorem. Let `e0=sqrt(R0)` be the root population risk of `m_full` and `econ` the root risk of the consistent corrected predictor. By the reverse triangle inequality in `L^2(mu)`,

\[
|e_{\rm act}-e_{\rm con}|\le\eta.
\]

Therefore if the consistent correction has a root-risk improvement margin

\[
\gamma=e_0-e_{\rm con}>\eta,
\]

then the actual mixed-label predictor still strictly improves on `m_full`. Equivalently, at the squared-risk level,

\[
|\mathcal R_{\rm act}-\mathcal R_{\rm con}|\le2\sqrt{\mathcal R_{\rm con}}\eta+\eta^2.
\]

This is a precise same-sample stability condition tied directly to the implementation. It also shows the role of ridge: the tolerance to train/query mean mismatch degrades like `lambda^{-1/2}`. A very small nugget can amplify a small training-row inconsistency.

## CROSS-FITTING INTERPRETATION

The preceding theorem gives a rigorous reason to use out-of-fold/cross-fitted training-row means. If `M_tr` consists of predictions from fits that exclude the corresponding rows while `m_full` is the deployed fit, then `delta_rms` is a direct empirical leave-one-out stability defect. No generic deep-network stability theorem is assumed here. The transfer condition is simply

\[
\delta_{\rm rms}=o(\sqrt\lambda\,\gamma)
\]

for a nonvanishing consistent-correction gain `gamma`, or `delta_rms=o(sqrt(lambda))` when only asymptotic equivalence of actual and consistent corrections is needed.

For a strongly stable learner, this can be a route to a theorem. For the current neural stack, however, the relevant stability rate has not been proved. The present result cleanly separates that missing learner-stability problem from the kernel correction algebra.

## NEW RESULT 3 — UNIVERSAL MONOTONE IMPROVEMENT IS FALSE

A concrete noiseless counterexample shows that training the mean and correction on the same observation can make population risk strictly worse.

Let the input space be `{a,b}`, with `mu(a)=epsilon in (0,1/2)`, `mu(b)=1-epsilon`, and let the deterministic target be `G=0`. Take one training observation `n=1` and the constant positive-definite kernel `k=1`. Define the data-dependent mean algorithm:

- if `X_1=a`: `m_S(a)=1` and `m_S(b)=0`;
- if `X_1=b`: `m_S` is identically zero.

On the event `X_1=a`, the training residual is `-1`. With ridge parameter `lambda>=0`, the residual correction is the constant `-1/(1+lambda)`. The uncorrected conditional population risk is `epsilon`. The corrected conditional population risk is

\[
\frac{(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon\lambda^2}{(1+\lambda)^2}.
\]

This exceeds `epsilon` exactly when

\[
\lambda<\frac{1-2\epsilon}{2\epsilon}.
\]

Thus the failure occurs not only for interpolation but also for a positive ridge range. For example `epsilon=1/4` and `lambda=1/2` gives baseline conditional risk `1/4` and corrected conditional risk `13/36>1/4`.

On the event `X_1=b`, both predictors are zero. Hence the expected-over-training-sample risks satisfy

\[
\mathbb E_S\mathcal R(m_S)=\epsilon^2,
\]

whereas for sufficiently small positive `lambda`,

\[
\mathbb E_S\mathcal R(\text{corrected})
=\epsilon\frac{(1-\epsilon)+\epsilon\lambda^2}{(1+\lambda)^2}>\epsilon^2.
\]

So the statement “a residual KRR stage trained on the same labels cannot hurt population generalization” is **DISPROVED**, even with deterministic noiseless labels and a PSD kernel. Any monotone-improvement theorem needs an alignment/stability/cross-fitting/independent-certification hypothesis.

## REPRESENTATION DEPENDENCE — WHAT REMAINS

The two theorems above allow `m_S` and `k_S` to be arbitrary sample-dependent objects, but the mismatch transfer theorem freezes the realized kernel. To compare a leave-one-out or cross-fitted representation `phi_{S\i}` with the full representation `phi_S`, one needs a second perturbation layer.

For two realized systems `(K,q,R)` and `(K',q',R')`, the resolvent identity gives

\[
A^{-1}-A'^{-1}=A^{-1}(K'-K)A'^{-1},
\]

and therefore, for `lambda>0`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|q^TA^{-1}R-q'^TA'^{-1}R'\|
&\le \frac{\|q-q'\|\|R\|_F}{n\lambda}
 +\frac{\|q'\|\|K-K'\|_{\rm op}\|R\|_F}{(n\lambda)^2}\\
&\quad+\frac{\|q'\|\|R-R'\|_F}{n\lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This deterministic inequality is immediate, but making it useful requires a nontrivial leave-one-out stability theorem for the learned features or direct empirical control of the Gram/query perturbations. This is the main theorem-sized open step for the same-sample learned-representation case.

## INDEPENDENT VALIDATION AS A CERTIFICATE

The exact conditional improvement identity also clarifies the statistical role of validation. Once all fitting and hyperparameter selection is complete, a genuinely independent certification set can estimate the paired loss difference between `m_S` and `m_S+c_S` directly, without any independence between the components of the fitted predictor. The current pipeline already uses its validation split for checkpoints, stacking and kernel selection, so that same validation set is not an untouched post-selection certificate. A nested tune/certify split, or cross-fitting across the full training block, is the clean finite-sample route if one wants a theorem-backed empirical sign certificate rather than a descriptive held-out comparison.

## LEDGER

### PROVED

1. Exact reconstruction of the July and current neural-mean/kernel-correction empirical setup and the current ridge normalization `K+n lambda I`.
2. Exact conditional population-risk difference identity for a consistent empirical residual correction, allowing mean and kernel to depend arbitrarily on the same training sample.
3. Exact `L2(mu)` formula for the training-row/query-time mean mismatch.
4. Operator inequality `Q <= kappa^2 K` and the bound
   \[
   \|\widehat G_{\rm act}-\widehat G_{\rm con}\|_{L^2(\mu)}
   \le \kappa\|\Delta\|_F/(2\sqrt{n\lambda}).
   \]
5. Root-risk and squared-risk transfer criteria from a consistent correction to the actual mixed-label correction.
6. Deterministic resolvent perturbation inequality for a changing learned representation/kernel.
7. Explicit positive-ridge counterexample showing same-sample residual correction can worsen expected population risk.
8. Independent proof of P04's leverage Cauchy inequality.

### CONDITIONAL

1. The actual pipeline is guaranteed to retain an improvement if its consistent-correction root-risk margin exceeds `kappa delta_rms/(2 sqrt(lambda))`.
2. Cross-fitting yields asymptotic equivalence if the full-vs-OOF mean defect obeys `delta_rms=o(sqrt(lambda))`; a rate for the actual deep stack is not yet proved.
3. A learned-feature version will follow from sufficiently strong leave-one-out control of `K`, `q` and residual labels, plugged into the resolvent perturbation bound.
4. A post-selection finite-sample sign certificate follows from an independent certification split plus a concentration bound for paired losses; this requires an additional untouched split or nested cross-fitting.

### DISPROVED / REJECTED SHORTCUTS

1. “Residual KRR on the same sample cannot hurt population risk.” False by the two-point counterexample.
2. “The population eigenbasis bias identity implies finite-sample same-sample improvement.” False without an empirical alignment/stability argument.
3. “Small training residual automatically means smaller fresh-input error.” False; the counterexample interpolates the observed residual while moving error to the high-mass unseen point.
4. “A fitted RKHS norm of mixed correction labels lower-bounds the native-space norm of the true deployed residual.” The current manuscript correctly rejects this because the labels need not come from one compatible deployed residual function.

### OPEN

1. Prove a high-probability leave-one-out stability rate for the actual neural mean/stack, or a realistic abstract learner class covering it.
2. Extend the mismatch theorem to jointly changing learned representations with sharp integrated rather than crude pointwise operator perturbation constants.
3. Derive a finite-sample concentration theorem for the empirical paired risk-gain statistic after nested tuning/certification.
4. Connect the exact conditional identity to a high-dimensional random-feature/deterministic-equivalent limit in which the sign can be computed from spectra and residual-feature alignment.
5. Decide how much of the current in-sample neural training prediction channel should be replaced by OOF predictions if the objective is theorem-backed rather than only empirical performance.
