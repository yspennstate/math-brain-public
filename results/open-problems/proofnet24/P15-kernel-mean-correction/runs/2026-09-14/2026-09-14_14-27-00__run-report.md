## 3. New theorem P15-STAB-03 — ridge-preconditioned spectral stability

Consider the same \(n\) training input locations and two arbitrary realized systems:

\[
c(u)=q(u)^\top A^{-1}R,
\qquad
A=K+n\lambda I,
\]

and

\[
c'(u)=q'(u)^\top A'^{-1}R',
\qquad
A'=K'+n\lambda'I,
\]

with \(\lambda,\lambda'>0\). No independence is assumed between either system and the training labels.

Assume only

\[
k'(u,u)\le \kappa'^2
\]

for every query \(u\). Define

\[
D
=
\mathbb E[
(q(U)-q'(U))(q(U)-q'(U))^\top
],
\]

\[
E_A=A'-A,
\]

and the **relative ridge perturbation**

\[
F=A^{-1/2}E_AA^{-1/2}.
\]

Since \(A'\succ0\),

\[
I+F=A^{-1/2}A'A^{-1/2}\succ0.
\]

Define three data-dependent spectral energies:

\[
\delta_q^2
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
R^\top A^{-1}DA^{-1}R
\right),
\]

\[
\delta_A^2
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
R^\top A^{-1/2}
F^2(I+F)^{-1}
A^{-1/2}R
\right],
\]

and

\[
\delta_R^2
=
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
(R-R')^\top A'^{-1}(R-R')
\right].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|c-c'\|_{L^2(\mu)}
\le
\delta_q
+
\kappa'\delta_A
+
\kappa'\delta_R.
}
\tag{P15-STAB-03}
\]

Every term is finite-sample and conditional on the realized learned systems. There is no sample-splitting assumption in the inequality itself.

### Proof

Use the exact decomposition

\[
c-c'
=
(q-q')^\top A^{-1}R
+
q'^\top(A^{-1}-A'^{-1})R
+
q'^\top A'^{-1}(R-R').
\tag{3.1}
\]

The first term has integrated squared norm exactly

\[
\delta_q^2.
\]

For the other two terms, let

\[
Q'=\mathbb E[q'(U)q'(U)^\top].
\]

The standard RKHS covariance inequality is

\[
Q'\preceq\kappa'^2K'
\preceq\kappa'^2A'.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For the middle term put

\[
M=A^{-1}-A'^{-1}.
\]

Because

\[
A'
=
A^{1/2}(I+F)A^{1/2},
\]

we have

\[
A'^{-1}
=
A^{-1/2}(I+F)^{-1}A^{-1/2}
\]

and hence

\[
M
=
A^{-1/2}
\left[
I-(I+F)^{-1}
\right]
A^{-1/2}.
\]

Since \(F\) commutes with \(I+F\),

\[
I-(I+F)^{-1}
=
F(I+F)^{-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
M A' M
=
A^{-1/2}
F^2(I+F)^{-1}
A^{-1/2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Using (3.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E
\|q'(U)^\top M R\|^2
&=
\operatorname{tr}(R^\top M Q'MR)\\
&\le
\kappa'^2
\operatorname{tr}(R^\top M A'MR)\\
&=
\kappa'^2\delta_A^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the label term,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E
\|q'(U)^\top A'^{-1}(R-R')\|^2
&=
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
(R-R')^\top
A'^{-1}Q'A'^{-1}
(R-R')
\right]\\
&\le
\kappa'^2
\operatorname{tr}
\left[
(R-R')^\top A'^{-1}(R-R')
\right]\\
&=
\kappa'^2\delta_R^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Minkowski proves P15-STAB-03.

### A simpler relative-form corollary

Let

\[
\rho=\|F\|_{\rm op},
\qquad
\eta=\lambda_{\min}(I+F)>0.
\]

Since every eigenvalue \(f\) of \(F\) obeys

\[
\frac{f^2}{1+f}
\le
\frac{\rho^2}{\eta},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\delta_A
\le
\frac{\rho}{\sqrt\eta}
\left[
\operatorname{tr}(R^\top A^{-1}R)
\right]^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If \(\rho<1\), then \(\eta\ge1-\rho\), so

\[
\boxed{
\delta_A
\le
\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}
\left[
\operatorname{tr}(R^\top A^{-1}R)
\right]^{1/2}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This exposes the correct conditioning variable: not the absolute Gram difference alone, but the perturbation after preconditioning by the fitted ridge system.

### Simultaneously diagonalizable corollary

If \(A\) and \(A'\) share an orthonormal eigenbasis and have eigenvalues \(a_j,a'_j>0\), while \(R_j\in\mathbb R^q\) denotes the residual-label coefficient in that basis, then

\[
\boxed{
\delta_A^2
=
\sum_j
\frac{(a'_j-a_j)^2}{a_j^2a'_j}
\|R_j\|^2.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the desired spectral-average replacement for the previous worst-case \(\lambda^{-3/2}\) bound. A large Gram perturbation in a direction with negligible residual energy costs essentially nothing, while a perturbation in a poorly regularized residual-carrying direction is correctly penalized.

The theorem does not claim that vanishing ridge is harmless. It identifies the exact ridge-preconditioned quantity that must remain controlled.

## 5. New theorem P15-GAIN-03 — same-sample gain transfer with spectral defects

Let \((m',c')\) be an honest/cross-fitted or otherwise independently analyzable proxy for which a positive population **root-\(L^2\)** gain is known:

\[
\gamma'
=
\|G-m'\|_{L^2(\mu)}
-
\|G-m'-c'\|_{L^2(\mu)}
>0.
\]

Let \((m,c)\) be the full-sample consistent-label system and \(c_{\rm act}\) the actually deployed correction using the manuscript's mixed training-row labels.

Define

\[
\delta_{m,\mu}
=
\|m-m'\|_{L^2(\mu)},
\]

and let \(\delta_q,\delta_A,\delta_R\) be the P15-STAB-03 quantities comparing \(c\) to \(c'\).

Let

\[
\eta_\Delta
=
\|c_{\rm act}-c\|_{L^2(\mu)}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\|G-m\|_{L^2}
-
\|G-m-c_{\rm act}\|_{L^2}\\
&\qquad\ge
\gamma'
-
2\delta_{m,\mu}
-
\delta_q
-
\kappa'\delta_A
-
\kappa'\delta_R
-
\eta_\Delta.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{P15-GAIN-03}
\]

For the manuscript mismatch,

\[
\eta_\Delta
\le
\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{n\lambda}}
\|\Delta\|_F.
\]

Therefore the actual same-sample deployed correction is rigorously better in root-\(L^2\) risk whenever

\[
\boxed{
\gamma'
>
2\delta_{m,\mu}
+
\delta_q
+
\kappa'\delta_A
+
\kappa'\delta_R
+
\frac{\kappa}{2\sqrt{n\lambda}}
\|\Delta\|_F.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a finite-sample theorem with explicit dependence. It does not pretend the same-sample learner is independent; all dependence is packaged into realized measurable stability energies.

## 6. New finite-grid selection lemma P15-SEL-01

The manuscript selects scale/ridge from a finite validation grid, so representation stability alone does not guarantee that the selected estimator is stable.

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a finite candidate set. Let \(V_h\) be the actual validation score and \(V'_h\) a proxy validation score. Suppose the proxy has a unique winner

\[
h_\star=\arg\min_h V'_h
\]

with margin

\[
g_{\rm sel}
=
\min_{h\ne h_\star}
(V'_h-V'_{h_\star})>0.
\]

If

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{h\in\mathcal H}|V_h-V'_h|
<
\frac{g_{\rm sel}}2,
}
\tag{P15-SEL-01}
\]

then the actual system selects the same \(h_\star\).

For empirical squared validation error, with normalized validation norm \(\|\cdot\|_V\),

\[
V_h=\|Y-f_h\|_V^2,
\qquad
V'_h=\|Y-f'_h\|_V^2,
\]

one has exactly

\[
|V_h-V'_h|
\le
\left(
\|Y-f_h\|_V+
\|Y-f'_h\|_V
\right)
\|f_h-f'_h\|_V.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus a finite-grid selection theorem reduces to prediction stability plus an observed validation margin. No independence of the validation candidates is needed for this deterministic argmin statement.

This closes the purely deterministic part of the previous P15 “hyperparameter jump” obstruction. What remains probabilistic is proving or certifying the required margin and candidate-wise prediction closeness without reusing the same validation block for an invalid post-selection population claim.

## 7. Why this is stronger than the population eigenbasis identity

The elementary identity

\[
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right)^2r_j^2
\]

only describes shrinkage bias for a fixed population operator and fixed residual.

P15-STAB-03 instead controls how a **realized learned correction itself changes** when:

- the learned representation changes;
- the Gram matrix changes;
- the ridge parameter changes;
- the query map changes;
- the residual labels change.

The crucial new quantity

\[
F=A^{-1/2}(A'-A)A^{-1/2}
\]

is a finite-sample ridge-preconditioned perturbation. Equation (3.6) shows the exact analogy with spectral bias: perturbations are weighted by the residual energy that actually occupies the affected regularized directions.

This is the correct theorem-level bridge between the population spectral intuition and the same-observation empirical pipeline.

## 8. Counterexamples / failed routes retained

### Universal same-sample monotonicity is false

The previous P15 two-point construction remains decisive. A data-dependent mean can fit the observed point in such a way that the KRR residual is extrapolated with the wrong sign onto a higher-mass unseen point. Even with deterministic noiseless labels and a PSD constant kernel, the manuscript's mean-relative error can be worsened for every finite ridge parameter.

Thus no theorem of the form “residual KRR always improves a learned mean” can be true without alignment/stability assumptions.

### Small absolute Gram error is not the right invariant

If \(\lambda\) is tiny, a small absolute operator perturbation can be large relative to \(A=K+n\lambda I\). P15-STAB-03 shows that the right object is the preconditioned perturbation \(F\), or more sharply the residual-weighted spectral energy \(\delta_A\).

### Cross-fitting is not itself a gain theorem

Cross-fitting supplies an honest proxy. One still needs a positive proxy margin \(\gamma'>0\) and a transfer inequality such as P15-GAIN-03.

### A finite hyperparameter grid does not remove selection instability

A nearby learned representation can switch the selected ridge/scale when the validation margin is small. P15-SEL-01 states the exact deterministic condition under which this cannot happen.

### The manuscript's mean-relative metric is still separate

P15-GAIN-03 is a root-\(L^2\) theorem. The reported metric
\[
\mathbb E\frac{\|G(U)-\widehat G(U)\|}{\|G(U)\|}
\]
is not automatically ordered by root-\(L^2\) risk. A direct theorem requires output-norm assumptions or an untouched paired certification sample.

**Claim:** after replacing a proportional clean ghost block row-by-row, the summed first-order discrepancy is
\[
O\!\left(
|\tau|\varepsilon_d+\frac{\tau^2}{\sqrt d}
\right),
\]
rather than the previously overclaimed \(O(|\tau|\varepsilon_d+\tau^2/M)\).

**Verdict: PROVED / PASS.**

P21's exact rank-one update is

\[
\Phi_r(\tau)-\Phi_{-r}(\tau)
=
\frac{\tau}{2M}
\frac{e^2}{1+\tau Q_r}.
\]

On its good event,

\[
1+\tau Q_r\ge\frac12
\]

uniformly for \(|\tau|\le h_0\).

Freeze \(Q_r\) at its conditional mean. The map
\[
q\mapsto \frac{e^2}{1+\tau q}
\]
has derivative bounded by \(C|\tau|e^2\). With
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Q_r\mid\mathcal F_-)=O(d^{-1})
\]
and the stated conditional fourth-moment bound on \(e\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives an **inside-the-bracket** freezing error
\[
O(|\tau|d^{-1/2}).
\]
Multiplying by the exact outer factor \(|\tau|/(2M)\) yields
\[
O\!\left(\frac{\tau^2}{M\sqrt d}\right)
\]
per row.

After freezing, the numerator is quadratic in the teacher/logit pair, while the actual and Gaussian-surrogate second moments and mean leverage differ by \(O(\varepsilon_d)\). This gives
\[
O\!\left(\frac{|\tau|\varepsilon_d}{M}\right)
\]
per row.

Summing the \(M\asymp d\) ghost rows yields exactly
\[
O\!\left(
|\tau|\varepsilon_d+\frac{\tau^2}{\sqrt d}
\right).
\]

The former \(O(\tau^2/M)\) term would require stronger leverage control than the displayed variance estimate supplies. P21 was correct to repair it; the weaker rate still vanishes and is enough for its finite-difference clean-risk argument.
