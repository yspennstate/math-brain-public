## `P15-THREELEVEL-MEAN-HIERARCHY-25` — NEW / PROVED

Condition on a fixed design and let

\[
Y=\mu+\varepsilon,
\qquad
E\varepsilon=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon)=\sigma^2 I_N.
\]

Let a fixed symmetric KRR smoother satisfy

\[
0\preceq H\prec I,
\qquad
A=I-H.
\]

For any deterministic symmetric PSD linear mean smoother `S`, compare:

1. fixed oracle mean `S mu`;
2. cross-fit mean `S Y'`, where `Y'=mu+epsilon'` has independent noise;
3. same-response mean `S Y`.

All three are followed by the same residual KRR smoother `H`.

If positive gain means zero-mean-KRR risk minus hybrid risk, then exactly

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm det}
=
\|A\mu\|^2-\|A(I-S)\mu\|^2,
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm cf}
=
G_{\rm det}
-
\sigma^2\|AS\|_F^2,
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm same}
=
G_{\rm cf}
-
2\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(AHS).
}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Since `AH` and `S` are PSD,

\[
\boxed{
G_{\rm det}\ge G_{\rm cf}\ge G_{\rm same}.
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

This gives the exact bridge from the elementary fixed-mean identity to a learned same-response mean:

- cross-fitting pays the mean-estimation variance `sigma^2 ||AS||_F^2`;
- same-response fitting pays the additional covariance tax `2 sigma^2 tr(AHS)`.

No commutation between `S` and `H` is required.

## `P15-NONCOMMUTING-SUBSPACE-ORACLE-25` — NEW / PROVED

Take a structured mean

\[
m_s(Y)=s P Y,
\qquad
0\le s\le 1,
\]

where `P` is any orthogonal projector and need not commute with `H`.

The zero-mean KRR baseline and hybrid are

\[
f_0=HY,
\qquad
f_s=sPY+H(Y-sPY)=HY+sAPY.
\]

Define

\[
G_P(s)=E\|f_0-\mu\|^2-E\|f_s-\mu\|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
G_P(s)=2sL(P)-s^2D(P),
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
L(P)=\mu^\top A^2P\mu-\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(AHP),
}
\tag{0.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
D(P)=\mu^\top PA^2P\mu+\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(PA^2)
=\|AP\mu\|^2+\sigma^2\|AP\|_F^2.
}
\tag{0.7}
\]

For nonzero `P`, `D(P)>0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 s_P^*=
 \min\left\{1,\frac{[L(P)]_+}{D(P)}\right\},
}
\tag{0.8}
\]

and a nonzero amount of the same-response mean is beneficial iff

\[
\boxed{L(P)>0.}
\tag{0.9}
\]

Define the same-response gain-score matrix

\[
\boxed{
Q=
\frac12\left[
\mu(A^2\mu)^\top+(A^2\mu)\mu^\top
\right]
-
\sigma^2AH.
}
\tag{0.10}
\]

Then

\[
L(P)=\operatorname{tr}(P Q).
\]

Hence a beneficial rank-one same-response mean exists iff

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{\max}(Q)>0.}
\tag{0.11}
\]

For rank `r`, the largest infinitesimal gain over orthogonal rank-`r` subspaces is governed by the top `r` eigenvalues of `Q` via Ky Fan.

\[
\boxed{
G_v^{\rm dep}(s)
=2sL_v^{\rm dep}-s^2D_v^{\rm dep},

\tag{0.13}

where

\[
\boxed{
L_v^{\rm dep}=-E\langle e_0(Y),Z_v(Y)\rangle,
\qquad
D_v^{\rm dep}=E\|Z_v(Y)\|^2.
}
\tag{0.14}

\boxed{

# 5. Proof of the three-level hierarchy

Let `A=I-H` and `S=S^T >= 0`.

Zero-mean KRR has error

\[
HY-\mu=-A\mu+H\varepsilon.
\]

Thus

\[
R_0=\|A\mu\|^2+\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(H^2).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the deterministic oracle mean `S mu`,

\[
f_{\rm det}=HY+AS\mu,
\]

so

\[
R_{\rm det}
=\|A(I-S)\mu\|^2+\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(H^2).
\]

Subtracting proves `(0.1)`.

For the cross-fit mean `S Y'`,

\[
f_{\rm cf}=HY+ASY'.
\]

The independent noises contribute

\[
\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
+
\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(ASS^\top A).
\]

Hence `(0.2)`.

For the same-response mean,

\[
f_{\rm same}=HY+ASY,
\]

and the noise matrix is

\[
H+AS.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}[(H+AS)(H+AS)^\top]
={}&\operatorname{tr}(H^2)
\\
&+2\operatorname{tr}(HS^\top A)
+\operatorname{tr}(ASS^\top A).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves `(0.3)`.

Finally, because `A` is a polynomial in `H`, `AH` is PSD. For `S` PSD,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(AHS)
=
\operatorname{tr}[(AH)^{1/2}S(AH)^{1/2}]
\ge0.
\]

Thus `(0.4)` follows without simultaneous diagonalization.

# 6. Proof of the noncommuting projector oracle

Set `S=sP`, with `P=P^T=P^2`.

The deterministic signal gain is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\|A\mu\|^2-\|A(I-sP)\mu\|^2
\\
&=2s\mu^\top A^2P\mu
-s^2\mu^\top PA^2P\mu.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The cross-fit mean-estimation variance is

\[
\sigma^2s^2\|AP\|_F^2
=\sigma^2s^2\operatorname{tr}(PA^2).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The additional same-response covariance tax is

\[
2\sigma^2s\operatorname{tr}(AHP).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining `(6.1)`--`(6.3)` gives `(0.5)`--`(0.7)`.

The function

\[
2sL-s^2D
\]

is concave in `s`. For nonzero `P`, `A` is positive definite under `H<I`, so `D>0`. Its unconstrained maximizer is `[L]_+/D`; clipping to `[0,1]` proves `(0.8)`.

The gain derivative at zero is `2L`, so a positive `s` can improve risk iff `L>0`.

# 7. Gain-score matrix and exact generalized threshold

Let

\[
b=A^2\mu
\]

and define `Q` by `(0.10)`.

For every orthogonal projector `P`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(PQ)
=\mu^\top PA^2\mu-
\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(PAH)
=L(P).
\]

Thus a rank-one projector `P=vv^T` is beneficial at small positive scale iff

\[
v^\top Qv>0.
\]

This proves `(0.11)`.

For a fixed rank `r`, Ky Fan gives

\[
\max_{\operatorname{rank}(P)=r}\operatorname{tr}(PQ)
=
\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j(Q).
\]

The best infinitesimal rank-`r` mean subspace is therefore the leading eigenspace of the **gain score** `Q`, not necessarily a kernel eigenspace.

For the generalized threshold, write

\[
J=AH,
\qquad
C=\frac12\left[\mu b^\top+b\mu^\top\right],
\qquad
Q=C-\sigma^2J.
\]

If `J>0`, congruence by `J^{-1/2}` preserves inertia:

\[
J^{-1/2}QJ^{-1/2}
=
\frac12(pq^\top+qp^\top)-\sigma^2I,
\]

where

\[
p=J^{-1/2}\mu,
\qquad
q=J^{-1/2}b.
\]

The two possibly nonzero eigenvalues of the symmetric rank-two matrix `(pq^T+qp^T)/2` are

\[
\frac{
\langle p,q\rangle\pm\|p\|\|q\|
}{2}.
\]

Therefore a positive direction exists exactly under `(0.12)`.

If `J` is only semidefinite, the correct statement is split:

- a direction in `ker(J)` with positive signal score is automatically beneficial at first order;
- on `range(J)`, use the generalized Rayleigh quotient of `(C,J)`.

No unjustified global inverse or pseudoinverse is used.

# 10. Response-dependent representation and partial cross-fitting

Let `H=H(Y)` and `A(Y)=I-H(Y)`.

The squared-risk difference is pathwise quadratic:

-2s\langle f_0-\mu,Z_v\rangle

# 12. High-dimensional / random-feature reduction

The new theorem identifies a sharper high-dimensional object than an ordinary kernel spectral law.

Let `U_n` be a candidate structured-mean subspace with orthonormal basis `V_n`. Define

\[
C_n=
\frac12\left[
\mu_n(A_n^2\mu_n)^\top
+(A_n^2\mu_n)\mu_n^\top
\right],

\[
J_n=A_nH_n.
\]

Restrict to `U_n`:

\[
C_{U,n}=V_n^\top C_nV_n,
\qquad
J_{U,n}=V_n^\top J_nV_n.
\]

When `J_{U,n}>0`, define

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_n=
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
J_{U,n}^{-1/2}
C_{U,n}
J_{U,n}^{-1/2}
\right).
}
\tag{12.1}
\]

Then a beneficial rank-one same-response direction inside `U_n` exists iff

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_n>\sigma^2.}
\tag{12.2}
\]
