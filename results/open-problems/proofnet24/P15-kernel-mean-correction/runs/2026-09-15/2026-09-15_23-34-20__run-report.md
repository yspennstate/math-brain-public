# 2. Exact kernel-learning shortlist boundary

The accessible original shortlist is `Kernel_Learning_Conjectures 2.pdf`.

Its neural-mean paragraph states, in substance and notation, that for a fixed mean `m` and kernel `K`, the corrected model learns

\[
r=f^\star-m,
\]

and in a population eigenbasis `(\psi_j,\lambda_j)` of `K`, writing

\[
r=\sum_j r_j\psi_j,
\]

the squared population ridge bias is

\[
\boxed{
B(\lambda)
=
\sum_j\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The source then says explicitly that this identity is **elementary, not a new conjecture**, and that the harder follow-up is to obtain comparable prediction guarantees when the **mean, representation, and correction are trained on the same observations**.

That is exactly the boundary maintained here:

- `(2.1)` remains a fixed-population/fixed-representation bias identity;
- the same-observation results below are separate finite-sample learning statements;
- none is presented as if it followed automatically from `(2.1)`.

# 5. New theorem: exact coordinate decomposition of the reuse debt

## 5.1 Setup

Let

\[
S=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)
\]

be independent training observations, let `S'` be an independent copy, and let `U` be a fresh test input independent of the training sample. Let

\[
\mathcal H=L^2(P_U;\mathbb R^q).
\]

Let

- `b_S in H` be any learned uncorrected baseline;
- `f_S in H` be the complete corrected learner;
- `g_S=f_S-b_S` be the learned correction increment.

Every object may depend on all of `S`; this includes a neural mean, response-trained representation, optimizer trajectory, checkpoint, stack, kernel family, ridge/scale selection, and final residual solve.

The inherited exact ghost identity is

\[
\Delta_{\rm reuse}
:=R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=
2\,\mathrm{Cov}_{\mathcal H}(b_S,g_S).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\Delta_{\rm reuse}
=
E\langle b_S-b_{S'},g_S-g_{S'}\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Positive `Delta_reuse` means same-sample reuse is worse than the full independent-correction ghost benchmark; negative means reuse helps.

## 5.2 `P15-SAMEOBS-DOOB-COV-32` — PROVED

Let

\[
\mathcal F_i=\sigma(Z_1,\dots,Z_i),
\qquad \mathcal F_0=\{\varnothing,\Omega\},
\]

and define the Hilbert-valued Doob increments

\[
D_i^b=E[b_S\mid\mathcal F_i]-E[b_S\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}],
\]

\[
D_i^g=E[g_S\mid\mathcal F_i]-E[g_S\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\rm reuse}
=
2\sum_{i=1}^nE\langle D_i^b,D_i^g\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Doob decomposition gives

\[
b_S-Eb_S=\sum_iD_i^b,
\qquad
g_S-Eg_S=\sum_iD_i^g.
\]

If `i<j`, then `D_i^b` is `F_{j-1}`-measurable while

\[
E[D_j^g\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]=0,
\]

so

\[
E\langle D_i^b,D_j^g\rangle=0.
\]

The same holds for `j<i`. Hence

\[
\mathrm{Cov}_{\mathcal H}(b_S,g_S)
=
\sum_iE\langle D_i^b,D_i^g\rangle.
\]

Insert `(5.1)`.

No linearity, Gaussianity, fixed representation, or fixed kernel is assumed.

## 5.3 `P15-COORD-GHOST-TENSOR-32` — PROVED

For each `i`, let `S^{(i)}` replace only `Z_i` by an independent copy and define

\[
a_i^2=E\|b_S-b_{S^{(i)}}\|_{\mathcal H}^2,
\qquad
c_i^2=E\|g_S-g_{S^{(i)}}\|_{\mathcal H}^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}|
\le
\sum_{i=1}^n a_i c_i
\le
\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}\sqrt{\sum_i c_i^2}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The first inequality is the new sharpening; the second is ordinary Cauchy-Schwarz and recovers the previous run’s bound.

### Proof of the coordinate bound

Fix `i` and the prefix `(Z_1,...,Z_{i-1})`. Write

\[
h_i(z_i)=E[b_S\mid Z_1,\dots,Z_{i-1},Z_i=z_i]
\]

where the future coordinates are averaged out. The conditional pair identity gives

\[
E\|D_i^b\|^2
=\frac12E\|h_i(Z_i)-h_i(Z_i')\|^2.
\]

Using the same future coordinates in the two copies and Jensen,

\[
E\|D_i^b\|^2
\le
\frac12E\|b_S-b_{S^{(i)}}\|^2
=rac{a_i^2}{2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Likewise

\[
E\|D_i^g\|^2\le c_i^2/2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore `(5.3)` and Cauchy-Schwarz **within each coordinate** give

\[
|\Delta_{\rm reuse}|
\le
2\sum_i
\sqrt{E\|D_i^b\|^2E\|D_i^g\|^2}
\le
\sum_i a_i c_i.
\]

This proves `(5.5)`.

### Why this is materially stronger

The old global product only knows total baseline instability and total correction instability. The new quantity pays only where the two learners are sensitive to the **same training coordinate**.

If

\[
a_i c_i=0\quad\text{for every }i,
\]

then

\[
R_{\rm same}=R_{\rm ghost}
\]

exactly, even if both learners are individually unstable on disjoint coordinate sets.

## 5.4 `P15-COORD-GHOST-ORTHOG-CERT-32` — exact strictness example

Let `n=2`, `Z_1,Z_2` be independent Rademachers, and use scalar constant predictors

\[
b_S=Z_1,
\qquad
g_S=Z_2,
\qquad Y^{\rm new}=0.
\]

Then

\[
R_{\rm same}=E(Z_1+Z_2)^2=2,
\]

and the independent-correction ghost also has risk 2, so the true reuse debt is zero.

But replacement scales are

\[
a_1^2=2,\quad a_2=0,
\qquad
c_1=0,\quad c_2^2=2.
\]

The preceding global Cauchy bound is

\[
\sqrt{(2)(2)}=2,
\]

while the new coordinate tensor bound is

\[
\sum_i a_ic_i=0,
\]

which is exact.

This certifies that coordinate alignment is not cosmetic; it can remove an entire spurious dependence budget.

# 6. `P15-REP-AWARE-GHOST-32` — exact response-dependent KRR decomposition

This section resolves the representation-aware target left open in the previous run.

## 6.1 Fully learned KRR notation

For each training sample `S`, allow the representation/kernel and ridge value to be learned or selected from `S`.

Let

\[
K_S\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},
\qquad
\rho_S=n\lambda_S>0,
\qquad
A_S=K_S+\rho_SI,
\]

and for a fresh query `u` let

\[
k_S(u)=k_S(X_S,u)\in\mathbb R^n,
\qquad
B_S(u)=k_S(u,X_S)A_S^{-1}\in\mathbb R^{1\times n}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let the response matrix be `Y_S in R^{n x q}`. Let `m_S` be any learned structured mean and

\[
M_S=m_S(X_S)\in\mathbb R^{n\times q}.
\]

Define zero-mean KRR using the **same learned representation/kernel** by

\[
b_S(u)=B_S(u)Y_S.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Define the learned-mean residual correction by

\[
f_S(u)=m_S(u)+B_S(u)(Y_S-M_S).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The correction increment over uncorrected KRR is therefore exactly

\[
\boxed{
g_S(u)=f_S(u)-b_S(u)=m_S(u)-B_S(u)M_S.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

No independence is used in `(6.4)`.

## 6.2 One-observation replacement identities

Let `S_i=S^{(i)}` and abbreviate

\[
B=B_S,
\quad B_i=B_{S_i},
\quad Y=Y_S,
\quad Y_i=Y_{S_i},
\quad M=M_S,
\quad M_i=M_{S_i},
\]

\[
d_i(u)=m_S(u)-m_{S_i}(u).
\]

Then the uncorrected KRR replacement is exactly

\[
\boxed{
b_S-b_{S_i}
=
B(Y-Y_i)+(B-B_i)Y_i.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The correction-increment replacement is exactly

\[
\boxed{
g_S-g_{S_i}
=
d_i
-B(M-M_i)
-(B-B_i)M_i.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

### Proof

For `(6.5)`, add and subtract `BY_i`:

\[
BY-B_iY_i=B(Y-Y_i)+(B-B_i)Y_i.
\]

For `(6.6)`, use `(6.4)` and add/subtract `BM_i`:

\[
(m-m_i)-(BM-B_iM_i)
=d_i-B(M-M_i)-(B-B_i)M_i.
\]

These are pathwise algebraic identities. In particular, they remain valid when a row replacement retrains the neural mean, changes the learned feature map, changes the Gram matrix, changes the selected ridge/scale, and changes the fresh query kernel vector.

## 6.3 Five concrete replacement debts

Define the following fresh-input RMS quantities:

\[
L_i
=
\left(E\|B_S(U)(Y_S-Y_{S_i})\|_2^2\right)^{1/2},
\tag{6.7}
\]

\[
J_i
=
\left(E\|(B_S(U)-B_{S_i}(U))Y_{S_i}\|_2^2\right)^{1/2},
\tag{6.8}
\]

\[
M_i^{\rm out}
=
\left(E\|m_S(U)-m_{S_i}(U)\|_2^2\right)^{1/2},
\tag{6.9}
\]

\[
T_i
=
\left(E\|B_S(U)(M_S-M_{S_i})\|_2^2\right)^{1/2},
\tag{6.10}
\]

\[
Q_i
=
\left(E\|(B_S(U)-B_{S_i}(U))M_{S_i}\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

By Minkowski and `(6.5)`--`(6.6)`,

\[
a_i\le L_i+J_i,
\qquad
c_i\le M_i^{\rm out}+T_i+Q_i.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Combining with the coordinate ghost theorem yields the representation-aware same-observation certificate

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}|
\le
\sum_{i=1}^n
(L_i+J_i)
(M_i^{\rm out}+T_i+Q_i).
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

This is `P15-REP-AWARE-GHOST-32`.

### Interpretation

- `L_i`: direct response/design-row KRR movement using the old operator;
- `J_i`: movement of the uncorrected KRR operator itself, including fresh kernel vector, training Gram, and selected ridge/scale;
- `M_i^{out}`: fresh-output movement of the learned mean;
- `T_i`: movement of the mean evaluated on fitting rows, filtered by the old KRR operator;
- `Q_i`: interaction of learned-operator movement with the replacement-sample mean.

Unlike the fixed-kernel specialization from earlier runs, `(6.13)` remains correct for a response-trained representation. It also shows exactly what cross-fitting buys: it can eliminate some coordinate overlaps, but no claim is made that “cross-fitting the mean” alone removes `J_i` or `Q_i` when the representation/kernel remains response-trained.

# 7. `P15-REP-RESOLVENT-REPLACEMENT-32` — fresh kernel vector, Gram, ridge

The operator term in `(6.8)` and `(6.11)` can be decomposed without differentiability.

For two realized systems define

\[
A=K+\rho I,
\qquad
A'=K'+\rho'I,
\]

\[
B(u)=k(u)^TA^{-1},
\qquad
B'(u)=k'(u)^TA'^{-1},
\]

and

\[
\Delta k=k-k',
\qquad
\Delta A=A-A'=\Delta K+(\rho-\rho')I.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
B-B'
=
\Delta k^TA^{-1}
-k'^TA^{-1}\Delta A\,A'^{-1}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `K,K'` are positive semidefinite and `rho,rho'>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\|B-B'\|_2
\le
\frac{\|\Delta k\|_2}{\rho}
+
\frac{\|k'\|_2\,\|\Delta K+(\rho-\rho')I\|_{op}}
{\rho\rho'}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

Add and subtract `k'^TA^{-1}` and use the resolvent identity

\[
A^{-1}-A'^{-1}
=-A^{-1}(A-A')A'^{-1}.
\]

Since `lambda_min(A)>=rho` and `lambda_min(A')>=rho'`, `(7.2)` follows.

### Source normalization

For the mechanics residual correction,

\[
\rho=n\lambda.
\]

Thus a selected-ridge change contributes through

\[
|\rho-\rho'|=n|\lambda-\lambda'|.
\]

This is not interchangeable with a bare `|lambda-lambda'|` inside the Gram inverse.

### Why the fresh vector term cannot be dropped

The inherited counterexample with one training point and one fresh point has identical training Gram matrices but different cross-kernel values. Therefore `Delta K=0` can coexist with an `O(1)` fresh prediction change. Formula `(7.2)` retains the necessary `Delta k` term and does not repeat that failed route.

# 8. `P15-SELECTION-MARGIN-STAB-32` — hard finite-grid selection debt

The source selects kernel/ridge hyperparameters on validation. A hard argmin is not globally differentiable, but its replacement stability can still be localized exactly by a validation margin.

Let `Theta` be a finite candidate set, let `V_S(theta)` be the validation score, and let `theta_S` be the unique selected minimizer under a deterministic tie convention. Define the realized margin

\[
m_S
=
\min_{\theta\ne\theta_S}
\{V_S(\theta)-V_S(\theta_S)\}>0
\tag{8.1}
\]

and under one-row replacement define

\[
q_i(S,S_i)
=
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
|V_S(\theta)-V_{S_i}(\theta)|.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
q_i<m_S/2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta_{S_i}=\theta_S.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf 1\{\theta_{S_i}\ne\theta_S\}
\le
\mathbf 1\{m_S\le2q_i\}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Proof

For any `theta != theta_S`,

\[
V_{S_i}(\theta)-V_{S_i}(\theta_S)
\ge
m_S-2q_i.
\]

If `q_i<m_S/2`, this is positive for every competitor.

### Consequence for operator stability

Suppose a selected operator is `B_{S,theta_S}`. Add and subtract the same branch under the replacement sample:

\[
B_{S,\theta_S}-B_{S_i,\theta_{S_i}}
=
[B_{S,\theta_S}-B_{S_i,\theta_S}]
+
[B_{S_i,\theta_S}-B_{S_i,\theta_{S_i}}].
\tag{8.5}
\]

The second term vanishes on `{m_S>2q_i}`. Hence any branch-jump envelope `J_i^{jump}` gives

\[
\|B_{S,\theta_S}-B_{S_i,\theta_{S_i}}\|_{L^2}
\le
\|B_{S,\theta_S}-B_{S_i,\theta_S}\|_{L^2}
+
\left(E[(J_i^{jump})^2\mathbf1\{m_S\le2q_i\}]\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Thus hard kernel/ridge selection does not require pretending the selector is smooth. It requires either a stable branch or an explicit near-tie probability/debt.

For the source, numerical use of `(8.6)` remains **CONDITIONAL** because the per-replacement validation margins and branch jumps have not been released.

# 9. Mean-relative source metric with response-dependent representation

The mechanics loss is

\[
\ell_{rel}(f;U,Y)
=
\frac{\|Y-f(U)\|_2}{\|Y\|_2}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Let the weighted Hilbert norm be

\[
\|h\|_{w,2}^2
=
E\frac{\|h(U)\|_2^2}{\|Y\|_2^2},
\tag{9.2}
\]

whenever finite. Define replacement correction scales

\[
c_{i,w}^2
=
E\|g_S-g_{S_i}\|_{w,2}^2.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The previous ghost argument plus Efron-Stein gives

\[
|R_{rel,same}-R_{rel,ghost}|
\le
\left(\sum_i c_{i,w}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Now apply the exact representation decomposition `(6.6)` inside the weighted space. Define

\[
M_{i,w}^{out}
=
\left(E\frac{\|d_i(U)\|^2}{\|Y\|^2}\right)^{1/2},
\]

\[
T_{i,w}
=
\left(E\frac{\|B_S(U)(M_S-M_{S_i})\|^2}{\|Y\|^2}\right)^{1/2},
\]

\[
Q_{i,w}
=
\left(E\frac{\|(B_S(U)-B_{S_i}(U))M_{S_i}\|^2}{\|Y\|^2}\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Then

\[
c_{i,w}
\le
M_{i,w}^{out}+T_{i,w}+Q_{i,w},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
|R_{rel,same}-R_{rel,ghost}|
\le
\left[
\sum_{i=1}^n
(M_{i,w}^{out}+T_{i,w}+Q_{i,w})^2
\right]^{1/2}.

\tag{9.5}
\]

This is `P15-MEANREL-REP-GHOST-32`.

No lower bound on `||Y||` is invented. If `(9.2)` is not finite or cannot be verified, the inherited truncated/small-output theorem remains the valid fallback.

# 12. Primary-literature hypothesis check

Current primary literature was checked against the actual P15 dependence structure.

## 12.1 Fixed/random-feature deterministic equivalents

**Defilippis, Loureiro, Misiakiewicz (2024), arXiv:2405.15699, _Dimension-free deterministic equivalents and scaling laws for random feature regression_.** Their main object is random-feature ridge regression. Under a feature concentration property they obtain a non-asymptotic multiplicative deterministic equivalent for test error depending on feature-map eigenvalues, with dimension-independent approximation control.

This is useful for a fixed/random-feature branch but does not automatically model a multi-epoch feature map trained on the same response labels later used by the correction.

**Misiakiewicz, Saeed (2024), arXiv:2403.08938, _A non-asymptotic theory of Kernel Ridge Regression: deterministic equivalents, test error, and GCV estimator_.** Their KRR deterministic equivalent assumes explicit spectral and concentration properties of the kernel eigendecomposition and models scalar noisy labels `y_i=f_*(u_i)+epsilon_i`. It gives strong fixed-kernel/KRR machinery and GCV control, not the arbitrary response-trained representation/mean covariance term of P15.

## 12.2 Learned-feature asymptotics

**Cui, Pesce, Dandi, Krzakala, Lu, Zdeborova, Loureiro (2024), arXiv:2402.04980, _Asymptotics of feature learning in two-layer networks after one gradient-step_.** This treats a specified one-gradient-step feature-learning regime via a spiked random-feature surrogate and proportional asymptotics. It is rigorous and directly relevant as a model of controlled response-dependent feature learning, but its hypotheses do not cover the source’s arbitrary multi-epoch AdamW/refiner/selection pipeline.

The same caution applies to the related single-aggressive-step spiked-feature random-matrix analysis of Dandi et al., arXiv:2410.18938.

## 12.3 2026 weak-to-strong RFRR

**Wu, Chen, Misiakiewicz, Mondelli (2026), arXiv:2603.05691, _Improved Scaling Laws via Weak-to-Strong Generalization in Random Feature Ridge Regression_.** This derives a deterministic equivalent for a strong RFRR student trained on labels produced by a weak RFRR teacher and identifies regimes where the student improves its scaling law.

This is highly relevant as a rigorous learned-label/two-stage benchmark. But the teacher/student random-feature construction is specified independently of the arbitrary same-observation multi-stage neural mean, response-trained representation, hard validation selection, and residual KRR of the mechanics source.

## 12.4 Consequence for P15

The correct high-dimensional target is therefore still a **joint** deterministic equivalent or stability theorem that controls, in one probability space,

- fresh-query kernel-vector changes;
- training-Gram changes;
- selected ridge/scale changes;
- learned-mean output replacement;
- learned-mean fitting-row replacement;
- and their coordinate-aligned covariance with the uncorrected KRR baseline.

A bulk Marchenko-Pastur law, a fixed-feature KRR test-error equivalent, or a single-step learned-feature equivalent cannot by itself close this gap.

# 13. High-dimensional reduction after the new theorem

Let the sample size be `n` and suppose the per-observation replacement scales in `(6.7)`--`(6.11)` admit asymptotic descriptions. Define

\[
A_i=L_i+J_i,
\qquad
C_i=M_i^{out}+T_i+Q_i.
\]

Then

\[
|R_{same}-R_{ghost}|
\le
\sum_iA_iC_i.
\tag{13.1}
\]

This gives a clean scaling criterion:

- if exchangeability gives `A_i=O(n^{-1})` and `C_i=O(n^{-1})`, then reuse debt is `O(n^{-1})`;
- if each is only `O(n^{-1/2})`, the generic reuse debt can remain `O(1)`;
- if the two sensitivity profiles occupy mostly different coordinates, `(13.1)` can be much smaller than either global product even when their total energies are large.

For a cross-fit/ghost gain margin `gamma_n`, a sufficient transfer condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_iA_iC_i=o(\gamma_n)
}
\tag{13.2}
\]

or, for a finite nonvanishing margin, strict inequality against that margin.

This is a more precise high-dimensional target than asking whether the mean “usually helps.” One must compare the gain scale to the **aligned replacement profile**.
