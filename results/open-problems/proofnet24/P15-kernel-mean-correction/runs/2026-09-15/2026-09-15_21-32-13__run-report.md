The new fixed-kernel theorem is particularly explicit. Let

\[
A_\rho=K+\rho I,
\qquad
\rho=n\lambda>0,
\qquad
c_\rho(u)=A_\rho^{-1}k(X,u),
\]

and let `m` be any realized mean, possibly learned from the **same responses**. Then

\[
\widehat f_m(u)
=
m(u)+k(u,X)A_\rho^{-1}(Y-m(X))
\]

and zero-mean KRR with the same kernel is

\[
\widehat f_0(u)=k(u,X)A_\rho^{-1}Y.
\]

Pathwise,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f_m(u)-\widehat f_0(u)
=
\mathcal R_{\rho,X}m(u)
:=
m(u)-c_\rho(u)^Tm(X).
}
\]

No independence is used. This identity is a same-observation comparison theorem, but it is **not** a monotonic-improvement theorem: the population sign still depends on the alignment of this residualized learned mean with the zero-mean KRR error.

If a response row `i` is replaced by an increment `delta_i` and the learned mean changes by `d_i`, while the kernel is held fixed, then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_i\widehat f(u)
=
c_{\rho,i}(u)\delta_i
+
\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d_i(u).
}
\]

If `d_i` belongs to the same RKHS coordinatewise, the second term has the sharp bound

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d_i(u)\|_2
\le
\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K}\widetilde P_\lambda(u),
}
\]

where the final manuscript's exact regularized power function is

\[
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2
=
P_\lambda(u)^2
-
\rho\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\rho\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2
=
P_\lambda(u)^2-\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2.
}
\]

This identity is the precise duality between direct response sensitivity and native-space mean residualization. It also shows why the source normalization matters: `rho=n lambda`, not `lambda` by itself.

For one-row replacements with `||delta_i||<=D`, define

\[
\beta_{\mathcal K}^2
=
\frac1n\sum_i\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K}^2,
\qquad
\overline P^2
=E_U\widetilde P_\lambda(U)^2.
\]

Then the fresh-input RMS replacement of the actual residual-KRR hybrid obeys

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\left[
\frac1n\sum_i
E_U\|\Delta_i\widehat f(U)\|_2^2
\right]^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{D}{n\sqrt\lambda}
\left[
E_U(P_\lambda(U)^2-\widetilde P_\lambda(U)^2)
\right]^{1/2}
+
\overline P\,\beta_{\mathcal K}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

At fixed positive `lambda`, the direct response term is `O(1/n)` if the power gap remains bounded; if `lambda` scales like `1/n`, the same term is only `O(n^{-1/2})`. This normalization-sensitive distinction is exact and is important for any high-dimensional theorem.

The result is source-facing but not yet a numerical source certificate, because the manuscript does **not** establish the required RKHS replacement stability of the trained DNN/refiner mean, and the learned representation/hyperparameter choice can also move under response replacement. Those missing hypotheses are kept explicit rather than invented.

# 3. Exact same-observation comparison with zero-mean KRR

## `P15-SAMEOBS-HYBRID-DIFFERENCE-30` — PROVED

Fix a design `X`, a positive-semidefinite kernel Gram matrix `K`, positive ridge `rho=n lambda`, and the corresponding query vector

\[
k_u=k(X,u).
\]

Set

\[
A_\rho=K+\rho I,
\qquad
c_\rho(u)=A_\rho^{-1}k_u,
\qquad
B_\rho(u)=k_u^TA_\rho^{-1}.
\]

Let `Y in R^{n x q}` be the observed response matrix. Let `m` be **any realized map**, allowed to depend arbitrarily on `Y`, on `X`, on a learned neural fit, and on any other part of the same training procedure. Conditional on the realized kernel, define

\[
\widehat f_m(u)
=
m(u)+B_\rho(u)(Y-m(X)).
\]

The zero-mean KRR predictor using the **same** realized kernel is

\[
\widehat f_0(u)=B_\rho(u)Y.
\]

Subtracting gives the exact pathwise identity

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f_m(u)-\widehat f_0(u)
=
m(u)-B_\rho(u)m(X)
=:\mathcal R_{\rho,X}m(u).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

No statistical independence is used.

This is the correct same-observation comparison object. It is deliberately not labeled as an improvement theorem. For a fresh response `Y^new` at `U`, conditional on the fitted sample `S`, put

\[
g_S(U)=\mathcal R_{\rho,X}m_S(U),
\qquad
b_S(U)=\widehat f_{0,S}(U).
\]

Then the conditional corrected-minus-zero-mean squared-risk difference is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_S
=
E\left[
\|g_S(U)\|^2
-2\langle Y^{new}-b_S(U),g_S(U)\rangle
\mid S
\right].
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the sign requires an alignment term. Equation (3.1) does not turn the fixed-population spectral bias identity into a same-sample theorem.

# 4. Sharp RKHS norm of the mean-residualization operator

## `P15-RKHS-MEAN-RESIDUALIZATION-30` — PROVED

Let `d=(d_1,...,d_q)` with every coordinate `d_j in H_k` and

\[
\|d\|_{\mathcal K}^2
=
\sum_j\|d_j\|_{H_k}^2.
\]

Define

\[
g_u(\cdot)
=
k(u,\cdot)-\sum_i c_{\rho,i}(u)k(x_i,\cdot).
\]

For each coordinate, the reproducing property gives

\[
\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d_j(u)
=
\langle d_j,g_u\rangle_{H_k}.
\]

Hence

\[
\|\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d(u)\|_2
\le
\|d\|_{\mathcal K}\|g_u\|_{H_k}.
\]

The representer norm is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g_u\|_{H_k}^2
&=
k(u,u)-2k_u^Tc_\rho+c_\rho^TKc_\rho\\
&=
k(u,u)-k_u^TA_\rho^{-1}k_u
-\rho\,k_u^TA_\rho^{-2}k_u\\
&=
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d(u)\|_2
\le
\|d\|_{\mathcal K}\widetilde P_\lambda(u).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The constant is sharp. If `g_u !=0`, choose one output coordinate proportional to `g_u`; equality holds. This is exactly the sharpness mechanism in the manuscript's Theorem 6.9, now applied to a **difference of learned means**.

The theorem makes the preceding run's obstruction precise:

- training-row attenuation alone does not control `d(u)`;
- an explicit off-design function-space hypothesis does;
- the optimal native-space operator norm is the source's `widetilde P_lambda(u)`.

### Source scope

The manuscript does **not** prove that the neural/refiner mean itself, or a one-row difference between two such fitted means, belongs to the Matérn RKHS with a controlled native-space norm. Applying (4.1) to the real DNN pipeline therefore requires an explicit additional hypothesis or a measured substitute. That application remains **CONDITIONAL**.

# 5. Exact one-row same-observation replacement decomposition

## `P15-SAMEOBS-REPLACE-DECOMP-30` — PROVED

Keep `X,K,rho` fixed. Compare two observed-response/learned-mean pairs

\[
Y'=Y+E,
\qquad
m'=m+d.
\]

Their residual-KRR hybrids satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f_{Y',m'}(u)-\widehat f_{Y,m}(u)
&=
d(u)+B_\rho(u)(E-d(X))\\
&=
\boxed{
B_\rho(u)E+
\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d(u)
}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is pathwise and remains true when `d` is the data-dependent response of an arbitrary learning algorithm to the changed observation.

For a single-row response increment

\[
E=e_i\delta_i^T,
\]

(5.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_i\widehat f(u)
=
c_{\rho,i}(u)\delta_i
+
\mathcal R_{\rho,X}d_i(u).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `d_i in H_k^q`, then

\[
\boxed{
\|\Delta_i\widehat f(u)\|_2
\le
|c_{\rho,i}(u)|\,\|\delta_i\|_2
+
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This isolates two exact dependence channels:

1. a direct KRR label-leverage term;
2. an off-design learned-mean replacement term.

Cross-fitting removes the first channel only when the evaluated prediction was genuinely fit without that response and the learned mean/representation path also excludes it. Same-observation reuse must pay both channels.

# 6. Power-function / response-sensitivity duality

## `P15-POWER-SENSITIVITY-DUALITY-30` — PROVED

From the exact source formula,

\[
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2
=
P_\lambda(u)^2
-
\rho\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2,
\qquad
\rho=n\lambda.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\rho\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2
=
P_\lambda(u)^2-
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

So the gap between the ordinary posterior-variance factor `P_lambda` and the sharp native-space ridge factor `widetilde P_lambda` is **exactly** the aggregate squared response sensitivity of the query to the training labels.

This gives a direct normalization check. With the source convention `K+n lambda I`, the coefficient energy is

\[
\boxed{
\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2
=
\frac{P_\lambda(u)^2-\widetilde P_\lambda(u)^2}{n\lambda}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Replacing `n lambda` by `lambda` would be a factor-`n` error.

# 7. Finite-sample fresh-input replacement bridge

## `P15-HYBRID-LOO-RKHS-BRIDGE-30` — PROVED

Let `S^{(i)}` be a one-row replacement of the response block at fixed design/kernel. Write

\[
\delta_i=Y_i^{(i)}-Y_i,
\qquad
 d_i=m_{S^{(i)}}-m_S.
\]

Assume every `d_i in H_k^q`. Let `U` be a fresh query independent of the replacement index, and define

\[
\beta_{\mathcal K}^2
=
\frac1n\sum_i\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K}^2,
\]

\[
D_c^2
=
\frac1n\sum_i
E_U\left[c_{\rho,i}(U)^2\|\delta_i\|_2^2\right],
\]

\[
\overline P^2
=
E_U\widetilde P_\lambda(U)^2.
\]

Minkowski's inequality in the product space of `(i,U)` and (5.3) give

\[
\boxed{
\left[
\frac1n\sum_i
E_U\|\Delta_i\widehat f(U)\|_2^2
\right]^{1/2}
\le
D_c+
\overline P\,\beta_{\mathcal K}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

If additionally

\[
\|\delta_i\|_2\le D
\quad\text{for every }i,
\]

then

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_c^2
&\le
\frac{D^2}{n}
E_U\|c_\rho(U)\|_2^2\\
&=
\frac{D^2}{n\rho}
E_U\left[
P_\lambda(U)^2-\widetilde P_\lambda(U)^2
\right]\\
&=
\frac{D^2}{n^2\lambda}
E_U\left[
P_\lambda(U)^2-\widetilde P_\lambda(U)^2
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\left[
\frac1n\sum_i
E_U\|\Delta_i\widehat f(U)\|_2^2
\right]^{1/2}\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{D}{n\sqrt\lambda}
\left[
E_U(P_\lambda^2-\widetilde P_\lambda^2)
\right]^{1/2}
+
\overline P\,\beta_{\mathcal K}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

No Gaussian response assumption is used.

### Rate reading

If `lambda` is held fixed, the direct response term has an explicit `1/n` prefactor. If instead `lambda~1/n`, its generic scale is only `1/sqrt(n)`. If `lambda` shrinks faster, the direct-label stability can deteriorate further unless the power gap shrinks correspondingly.

This is an exact reason not to drop the source normalization when importing generic KRR stability heuristics.

### Interface with inherited LOO theorems

The preceding P15 full-pipeline LOO theorem requires prediction/loss replacement and deletion stability. Equation (7.1) supplies the fixed-kernel residual-KRR part of that stability from two quantities:

- learned-mean RKHS replacement stability;
- the computable power-function sensitivity gap.

For the real source, the representation, kernel selection, stack, and refiner optimizer can also move, so (7.1) is a component theorem, not the whole-pipeline certificate.

# 8. Exact paired-loss stability: learned mean correction versus zero-mean KRR

The user target is the **comparison**, not just the stability of one predictor. The common baseline-label motion can be kept exactly.

Let at a fresh point `z=(u,y)`

\[
b=\widehat f_0(u),
\qquad
g=\widehat f_m(u)-\widehat f_0(u)=\mathcal Rm(u).
\]

Under a training replacement write

\[
b'=b+s,
\qquad
g'=g+t.
\]

For the fixed-kernel one-row replacement,

\[
s=c_{\rho,i}(u)\delta_i,
\qquad
t=\mathcal R d_i(u).
\]

## `P15-HYBRID-PAIRLOSS-REPLACE-30` — PROVED

For squared loss define corrected-minus-baseline paired loss

\[
d_{sq}
=
\|y-b-g\|^2-
\|y-b\|^2.
\]

Let

\[
q=y-b-g.
\]

Direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
d_{sq}'-d_{sq}
={}&
-2\langle q,t\rangle
+2\langle g,s\rangle\\
&+2\langle s,t\rangle
+\|t\|^2.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Consequently, if `Q=||q||`, `G=||g||`, `S=||s||`, and `T=||t||`,

\[
\boxed{
|d_{sq}'-d_{sq}|
\le
2QT+2GS+2ST+T^2.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Together with

\[
S=|c_{\rho,i}(u)|\|\delta_i\|,
\qquad
T\le\widetilde P_\lambda(u)\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K},
\]

this is an explicit same-observation pair-comparison stability theorem.

The direct response perturbation does not disappear: it moves both predictors together and therefore enters only through the interaction `2<g,s>`, while the learned-mean differential enters through `t`.

## `P15-MEANREL-ZEROKRR-REPLACE-30` — PROVED

For the manuscript's actual unsquared mean-relative paired difference,

\[
d_{rel}
=
\frac{
\|y-b-g\|-
\|y-b\|
}{\|y\|},
\]

the triangle inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
|d_{rel}'-d_{rel}|
\le
\frac{2\|s\|+\|t\|}{\|y\|}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus under the fixed-kernel one-row theorem,

\[
\boxed{
|d_{rel}'-d_{rel}|
\le
\frac{
2|c_{\rho,i}(u)|\|\delta_i\|
+
\widetilde P_\lambda(u)\|d_i\|_{\mathcal K}
}{\|y\|}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This plugs directly into the inherited weighted/truncated LOO theorem without inventing a positive lower bound on the source output norm.

# 9. Learned representation / kernel perturbation

The fixed-kernel theorem is not enough when the representation itself is trained from the same observations. The finite perturbation can nevertheless be kept exact.

## `P15-JOINT-MEAN-REP-RESOLVENT-30` — PROVED

Consider two realized systems

\[
(K,k_u,\rho,Y,m)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(K',k_u',\rho',Y',m'),
\]

with `rho,rho'>0`, positive-semidefinite `K,K'`,

\[
A=K+\rho I,
\qquad
A'=K'+\rho'I,
\]

\[
B=k_u^TA^{-1},
\qquad
B'=k_u'^TA'^{-1}.
\]

Put

\[
E=Y'-Y,
\qquad
d=m'-m,
\qquad
r=Y-m(X).
\]

Then the two residual-KRR hybrids satisfy the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f'-\widehat f
=
\underbrace{d(u)-B'd(X)}_{\mathcal R'd(u)}
+
\underbrace{B'E}_{\text{direct response}}
+
\underbrace{(B'-B)r}_{\text{representation/ridge}}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

No differentiability and no branch-stability assumption is needed for the algebra.

Let

\[
\Delta k_u=k_u'-k_u,
\qquad
\Delta A=A'-A
=(K'-K)+(\rho'-\rho)I.
\]

The resolvent identity gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
B'-B
=
\Delta k_u^TA'^{-1}
-
k_u^TA'^{-1}\Delta A A^{-1}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Since

\[
\|A^{-1}\|_{op}\le\rho^{-1},
\qquad
\|A'^{-1}\|_{op}\le(\rho')^{-1},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\|B'-B\|_2
\le
\frac{\|\Delta k_u\|_2}{\rho'}
+
\frac{\|k_u\|_2\|\Delta A\|_{op}}{\rho'\rho}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

If in addition `d in H_{k'}^q`, then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|\widehat f'(u)-\widehat f(u)\|_2
\le{}&
\widetilde P_{\lambda'}'(u)\|d\|_{\mathcal K'}
+
\|B'E\|_2\\
&+
\|r\|_F
\left[
\frac{\|\Delta k_u\|_2}{\rho'}
+
\frac{\|k_u\|_2\|\Delta A\|_{op}}{\rho'\rho}
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

This theorem handles hard representation/kernel/ridge changes as a **finite** perturbation. A branch derivative or selection margin is unnecessary once the finite differences are known.

### Exact missing source quantities

For the real DNN/kernel pipeline, the source does not archive or prove one-row bounds for:

- `||m'-m||_{H_{k'}}`;
- `||K'-K||op` after representation or kernel-selection changes;
- `||k_u'-k_u||` on fresh inputs;
- checkpoint/stack/accept-reject selection jumps.

The theorem therefore supplies the correct conditional interface rather than a fabricated numerical certificate.

# 10. Counterexample: training-Gram stability is not representation stability

## `P15-GRAMONLY-REP-CE-30` — DISPROVED overstrong route

A fresh-input representation theorem must control the query kernel vector, not only the training Gram matrix.

Take a domain with one training point `x` and one fresh point `u`, scalar response `Y=1`, zero mean, and any `rho>0`. Define two positive-semidefinite kernels on `{x,u}` by the full two-point Gram matrices

\[
\mathcal K
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal K'
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&a\\
a&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
0<a<1.
\]

On the training design both kernels have exactly the same Gram matrix

\[
K=K'=[1],
\]

so

\[
\|K'-K\|=0.
\]

But their fresh kernel vectors are `0` and `a`. The zero-mean KRR predictions at `u` are

\[
\widehat f(u)=0,
\qquad
\widehat f'(u)=\frac{a}{1+\rho}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{zero training-Gram perturbation can coexist with nonzero fresh-input prediction perturbation.}
}
\]

For example `a=0.8`, `rho=0.2` gives a fresh-prediction change exactly `2/3`.

This falsifies the route

> “small learned-representation Gram perturbation on the training sample alone implies fresh-input representation stability.”

The missing quantity is precisely the `Delta k_u` term in (9.3).

# 11. High-dimensional / random-feature reduction

## `P15-LEVERAGE-DERIVATIVE-30` — PROVED

For fixed finite `K` and query vector `k_u`, define the ridge leverage function

\[
q_\rho(u)
=
k_u^T(K+\rho I)^{-1}k_u.
\]

Differentiating the resolvent gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\rho q_\rho(u)
=-k_u^T(K+\rho I)^{-2}k_u
=-\|c_\rho(u)\|_2^2.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
P_\rho(u)^2-
\widetilde P_\rho(u)^2
=-\rho\,\partial_\rho q_\rho(u),
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde P_\rho(u)^2
=
k(u,u)-q_\rho(u)+\rho\,\partial_\rho q_\rho(u).
}
\tag{11.3}
\]

This is the correct random-feature deterministic-equivalent target for the fixed-feature part of same-observation response sensitivity: one needs control not only of the leverage `q_rho`, but also of its ridge derivative.

## `P15-RF-DERIVATIVE-DE-30` — CONDITIONAL

Suppose in a high-dimensional random-feature model there is a deterministic equivalent

\[
q_{n,\rho}(U)\to q_\infty(\rho,U)
\]

locally uniformly in `rho` on a positive interval, with enough regularity to pass to the derivative. Then (11.1)--(11.3) transfer the deterministic equivalent directly to:

- aggregate response replacement sensitivity `||c_rho(U)||^2`;
- the sharp residualization power `widetilde P_rho(U)`;
- the fixed-kernel term in the LOO replacement bound (7.2).

A pointwise deterministic equivalent or bulk spectral limit without derivative control is insufficient.

This asymptotic claim is therefore **CONDITIONAL** on a derivative-stable deterministic equivalent.

### Audited statement

Let `G` be an `N x r` iid standard Gaussian matrix. For `0<epsilon,eta<1/2`, P16 claims a universal `C_HW` such that

\[
N
\ge
\frac{C_{HW}}\epsilon
\left[

### Independent audit

For unit directions `a,b`, the spherical wedge where their homogeneous-halfspace signs differ has uniform spherical measure exactly

\[
\mu(R_{a,b})=\theta(a,b)/\pi.
\]

Normalized iid Gaussian rows are iid uniform on the sphere.

Cover's homogeneous dichotomy count gives

\[
H_r(m)
\le
2\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\binom{m-1}{j}.
\]

A wedge trace is the symmetric difference of two homogeneous-halfspace traces, so the wedge shatter coefficient is at most `H_r(m)^2`. At `m=16r`, the standard binomial estimate used by P16 gives

\[
H_r(16r)^2
\le
4(16e)^{2r}
<2^{16r}
\]

for every `r>=1`; equivalently the exponent gap is

\[
2+2r\log_2(16e)-16r
=2-5.1146\ldots r<0.
\]

Thus the wedge class has VC dimension below `16r`.

The Haussler--Welzl epsilon-net theorem for a VC class then gives a universal constant with sample size

\[
O\left(
\epsilon^{-1}
[r\log(1/\epsilon)+\log(1/\eta)]
\right).
\]

If two directions have the same sampled activation mask, no sampled row direction lies in their disagreement wedge. On the epsilon-net event, that wedge must have measure below `epsilon`; hence the angle is below `pi epsilon`.

The strict-mask boundary convention causes no hidden issue: the range itself is defined by the same strict sign predicate, and spherical boundaries have zero measure. The theorem is uniform over all coefficient directions once the epsilon-net event holds.

# 18. Bottom line

The same-observation kernel/mean problem is now separated into exact finite-dimensional channels.

For a fixed realized kernel, the hybrid differs from zero-mean KRR by the residualized learned mean

\[
\mathcal Rm(u)
=
m(u)-k(u,X)(K+n\lambda I)^{-1}m(X).
\]

A one-row response replacement changes the hybrid by exactly

\[
c_i(u)\delta_i+
\mathcal R d_i(u).
\]

The sharp RKHS norm of `mathcal R` is the manuscript's own `widetilde P_lambda(u)`, and the direct-label coefficient energy is exactly the complementary power-function gap divided by `n lambda`.

When the representation/kernel changes, a third finite perturbation appears, and controlling the training Gram matrix alone is provably insufficient because the fresh kernel vector can move independently.

Thus a rigorous same-sample generalization theorem cannot be obtained by rebranding the fixed-population bias identity, by looking only at training residualization, or by controlling only a training Gram perturbation. The correct finite-sample obligations are now explicit:

- native/off-design mean stability;
- direct response leverage;
- fresh query-kernel-vector stability;
- representation/ridge Gram stability;
- optimizer/selection stability;
- and the inherited paired LOO/generalization bridge in the source's actual mean-relative metric.

No closure criterion is met.
