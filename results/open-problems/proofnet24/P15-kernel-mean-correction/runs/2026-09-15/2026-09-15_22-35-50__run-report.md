# 0. Executive result

P15 remains **OPEN**.

The fixed-population identity remains exactly

\[
B(\lambda)=\sum_j\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_j+\lambda}\right]^2r_j^2,
\qquad r=f^\star-m,
\]

for a **fixed** mean and a **fixed** population kernel operator. It is not promoted to a theorem about a mean, representation, selection rule and correction trained on the same observations.

The main advance is a full-dataset ghost/cross-fit theorem that permits the baseline and correction increment to be arbitrary measurable learning procedures. Thus mean fitting, feature/representation learning, validation selection, stacking, kernel/ridge selection and residual correction may all depend on the same sample.

Let `S=(Z_1,...,Z_n)` and `S'=(Z'_1,...,Z'_n)` be independent iid training samples. Let `b_S` be the uncorrected baseline predictor, let `f_S` be the corrected predictor and put `g_S=f_S-b_S`. For a fresh independent test pair `Z=(U,Y)`, compare

\[
f_{\rm same}=b_S+g_S,
\qquad
f_{\rm ghost}=b_S+g_{S'}.
\]

For squared loss,

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=E\left\langle b_S(U)-b_{S'}(U),\,g_S(U)-g_{S'}(U)\right\rangle.
}
\tag{0.1}
\]

This is `P15-SAMEOBS-GHOST-COVARIANCE-31 — PROVED`. It is finite-sample, non-Gaussian and representation-agnostic.

If `S^{(i)}` replaces one observation by an independent copy and

\[
\beta_b^2=\frac1n\sum_iE\|b_S-b_{S^{(i)}}\|_{L^2(P_U)}^2,
\qquad
\beta_g^2=\frac1n\sum_iE\|g_S-g_{S^{(i)}}\|_{L^2(P_U)}^2,
\]

then Hilbert-space Efron--Stein gives

\[
\boxed{|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}|\le n\beta_b\beta_g.}
\tag{0.2}
\]

Hence a ghost/cross-fit squared-risk gain `G_ghost >= gamma > 0` transfers to the actual same-sample learner whenever `n beta_b beta_g < gamma`.

For the manuscript's actual unsquared mean-relative loss, define the weighted prediction norm

\[
\|h\|_{w,2}^2=E_Z\frac{\|h(U)\|_2^2}{\|Y\|_2^2}
\]

whenever finite, and

\[
\beta_{g,w}^2=\frac1n\sum_iE\|g_S-g_{S^{(i)}}\|_{w,2}^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{|R_{\rm rel,same}-R_{\rm rel,ghost}|\le\sqrt n\,\beta_{g,w}.}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Thus an honest/cross-fitted mean-relative gain larger than `sqrt(n) beta_{g,w}` transfers to the fully reused learner. This theorem allows learned representations and hard selected stages inside `g_S`; numerical application remains conditional on measuring the weighted replacement energy or invoking the inherited truncation/small-output theorem.

The run also gives a sharper fixed-kernel stochastic-response specialization in which the dependence debt factors into KRR leverage and mean-output stability, and an exact fresh-input counterexample where the same structured mean **helps** the independent-response benchmark but **hurts** same-response KRR.

# 3. Exact full-dataset ghost covariance theorem

## `P15-SAMEOBS-GHOST-COVARIANCE-31` — PROVED

Let `S,S'` be independent iid training samples of size `n`, and let fresh `Z=(U,Y)` be independent. Let `b_S` be any learned baseline and `f_S=b_S+g_S` any corrected learner. All same-sample neural fitting, representation learning, validation selection, stacking, kernel/ridge selection and residual fitting may be hidden inside these maps.

Define

\[
R_{\rm same}=E\|Y-b_S(U)-g_S(U)\|^2,
\]

\[
R_{\rm ghost}=E\|Y-b_S(U)-g_{S'}(U)\|^2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=E\left\langle b_S(U)-b_{S'}(U),\,g_S(U)-g_{S'}(U)\right\rangle.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Expand the two risks. Because `g_S` and `g_{S'}` have the same marginal law, their quadratic terms cancel. Because the fresh target is independent of both training samples and the two increments have identical marginals,

\[
E\langle Y,g_S(U)\rangle=E\langle Y,g_{S'}(U)\rangle.
\]

Thus

\[
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}
=2\{E\langle b_S,g_S\rangle-E\langle b_S,g_{S'}\rangle\}.
\]

Exchangeability of `S,S'` turns the right side into

\[
E\langle b_S-b_{S'},g_S-g_{S'}\rangle.
\]

No distributional or architectural assumption beyond finite second moments is used.

The exact same-sample/ghost difference is therefore a **training-sample covariance between the baseline and learned correction increment**. Its sign is not fixed.

# 4. Full-pipeline replacement transfer

## `P15-GHOST-REPLACEMENT-TRANSFER-31` — PROVED

Let `S^{(i)}` replace observation `i` by an independent copy and work in `H=L^2(P_U;R^q)`. Define

\[
a_i^2=E\|b_S-b_{S^{(i)}}\|_H^2,
\qquad
c_i^2=E\|g_S-g_{S^{(i)}}\|_H^2.
\]

Hilbert Efron--Stein gives

\[
E\|b_S-b_{S'}\|_H^2\le\sum_i a_i^2,
\qquad
E\|g_S-g_{S'}\|_H^2\le\sum_i c_i^2.
\]

Combining with `(3.1)` and Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\boxed{
|R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm ghost}|
\le\sqrt{\sum_i a_i^2}\sqrt{\sum_i c_i^2}
\le n\beta_b\beta_g.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `beta_b^2=n^{-1} sum a_i^2` and `beta_g^2=n^{-1} sum c_i^2`.

## `P15-CROSSFIT-TO-SAME-CERT-31` — PROVED

Let

\[
G_{\rm ghost}=R(b_S)-R(b_S+g_{S'}),
\qquad
G_{\rm same}=R(b_S)-R(b_S+g_S).
\]

If

\[
G_{\rm ghost}\ge\gamma>0
\]

and

\[
n\beta_b\beta_g<\gamma,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm same}\ge\gamma-n\beta_b\beta_g>0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is a finite-sample expected-population same-observation theorem, not a relabeling of the fixed-population spectral bias identity.

\[

\[
\boxed{

\tag{6.2}

\[
\beta_{\rm out}^2=\frac1n\sum_iE\|g_Y-g_{Y^{(i)}}\|_{L^2(P_U)}^2.

\[

# 8. Exact fresh-input sign-flip counterexample

## `P15-FRESH-CROSSFIT-SIGNFLIP-CE-31` — PROVED / counterexample

Take one training point `x`, one distinct fresh point `u`, scalar output and the strictly positive-definite kernel

\[
\mathcal K=\begin{pmatrix}1&4/5\\4/5&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\mathcal K=9/25>0.
\]

Let

\[
\rho=\lambda=1/15.
\]

Then the fresh KRR leverage coefficient is

\[
c(u)=\frac{4/5}{1+1/15}=3/4.
\]

Let the true conditional mean at `x` and `u` be one and observe

\[
Y=1+\varepsilon,
\qquad E\varepsilon=0,
\qquad E\varepsilon^2=1.
\]

Use the structured constant learned mean

\[
m_Y(x)=m_Y(u)=\frac25Y.
\]

The residualized fresh mean increment is

\[
g_Y(u)=\frac25(1-c(u))Y=\frac1{10}Y.
\]

Thus

\[
f_0(u)=\frac34Y,
\qquad
f_{\rm same}(u)=\frac{17}{20}Y.
\]

For an independent response copy `Y^circ=1+epsilon^circ`, the ghost/cross-fit benchmark is

\[
f_{\rm cf}(u)=\frac34Y+\frac1{10}Y^\circ.
\]

Against the fresh conditional mean one,

\[
R_0=\left(1-\frac34\right)^2+\left(\frac34\right)^2=\frac58,

\[
R_{\rm cf}
=\left(1-\frac{17}{20}\right)^2+\left(\frac34\right)^2+\left(\frac1{10}\right)^2
=\frac{119}{200},
\]

and

\[
R_{\rm same}
=\left(1-\frac{17}{20}\right)^2+\left(\frac{17}{20}\right)^2

Therefore

\[
\boxed{R_0-R_{\rm cf}=3/100>0,}

but

\[
\boxed{R_0-R_{\rm same}=-3/25<0.}

The same structured mean therefore improves fresh-input KRR when its response noise is independent of the correction response, yet worsens fresh-input KRR under same-response reuse.

Moreover

\[
R_{\rm same}-R_{\rm cf}=3/20,

exactly matching `(6.1)`:

\[
2\cdot(3/4)\cdot(1/10)\cdot E\varepsilon^2=3/20.

This is a strict fresh-generalization counterexample, not a training-point interpolation artifact.

# 9. Learned representation boundary and next exact decomposition

The general dataset theorem `(3.1)` already permits a response-trained representation because `b_S` and `g_S` are arbitrary learned predictors. Its output-level replacement transfer therefore remains valid without conditioning on the representation.

The sharper formulas `(6.1)`--`(7.3)`, however, use a deterministic fixed-kernel leverage vector `c(U)`. They cannot be silently applied after conditioning on a response-dependent representation. Conditioning on a learned kernel generally destroys the row-noise independence used in those formulas.

The inherited exact finite perturbation identity remains the representation interface. For two realized systems,

\[
\widehat f'-\widehat f=[d(u)-B'd(X)]+B'E+(B'-B)r,
\]

with

\[
B'-B=\Delta k_u^TA'^{-1}-k_u^TA'^{-1}\Delta A A^{-1}.
\]

Therefore source representation stability must control both training-Gram/regularization movement and the fresh kernel-vector movement. The inherited counterexample already proves that `Delta K=0` alone does not control fresh prediction changes.

The next theorem target `P15-REP-AWARE-GHOST-32` is to add/subtract a leave-one-out leverage vector `c_{-i}` and separate the same-observation debt into:

1. mean-output deletion debt weighted by leave-one-out leverage;
2. fresh representation/leverage deletion debt;
3. selection/ridge-change debt.

This run does not label that sketch as proved because the correction residualization operator itself also changes with the learned representation.
