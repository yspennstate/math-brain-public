## 2. Exact conditional Walsh packets

Fix every bias except coordinate `j`.  In particular, one arbitrary “core” bias may already be nonuniform.  Write

\[
u=1-2p_j=\mathbb E X_j,
\qquad p_j={1-u\over2},
\]

so `u=0` is the unbiased tail point.

Let `g_+` and `g_-` be the two restrictions of `G` obtained by fixing `X_j=+1` and `X_j=-1`.  Expand these two children in the biased Walsh basis of all remaining coordinates (which already includes the fixed core bias).  For every `S` not containing `j`, define

\[
a_S={\widehat g_+(S)+\widehat g_-(S)\over2},
\qquad
b_S={\widehat g_+(S)-\widehat g_-(S)\over2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

A one-coordinate basis change gives the **exact** two-member packet at arbitrary `u`:

\[
\widehat G_u(S)=a_S+u b_S,
\qquad
\widehat G_u(S\cup\{j\})=\sqrt{1-u^2}\,b_S.
\tag{2.2}
\]

No approximation has occurred.  Squaring gives

\[
x_S(u)=(a_S+ub_S)^2,
\qquad
y_S(u)=(1-u^2)b_S^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since the total spectral mass is one for every `u`, (2.3) also yields

\[
\sum_S(a_S^2+b_S^2)=1,
\qquad
\sum_S a_Sb_S=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Put

\[
\beta=\sum_S b_S^2,
\qquad
\beta_0=\sum_{S:a_S=0}b_S^2,
\qquad
\beta_1=\sum_{S:a_S\ne0}b_S^2=\beta-\beta_0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Here `beta_0` is the “dark-packet” mass: tail energy whose tail-absent partner is exactly zero.  `beta_1` is the “bright-packet” mass.

## 3. The tail multiplier is constant

From (2.3),

\[
\operatorname{It}_j(G;u)=\sum_S y_S(u)
=(1-u^2)\beta.
\]

But `V_{p_j}=1-u^2`.  Hence the physical sensitivity multiplier in (1.1) is **exactly constant** along this tail line:

\[
{\operatorname{It}_j(G;u)\over V_{p_j}}=\beta.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The entropy part of the tail payment is therefore

\[
\beta h_2(V_{p_j})
=\beta h_2(1-u^2)
=\beta h_2(u^2).
\]

As `u->0`,

\[
\beta h_2(u^2)
=\beta u^2\log_2{1\over u^2}
+\beta(\log_2 e)u^2+O(u^4).
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 4. Spectral-entropy expansion and the sign theorem

Let `h(x)=-x log_2 x`, with `h(0)=0`.  If `a_S\ne0`, both packet terms in

\[
h(x_S(u))+h(y_S(u))
\]

are ordinary smooth functions at `u=0` (a zero `b_S` is harmless).  If instead `a_S=0` and `b_S\ne0`, then

\[
x_S(u)=u^2b_S^2,
\]

and therefore

\[
h(x_S(u))
=b_S^2u^2\log_2{1\over u^2}
+b_S^2u^2\log_2{1\over b_S^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The companion `h((1-u^2)b_S^2)` is regular.  Summing all packets gives

\[
\widetilde H_G(u)
=\widetilde H_G(0)+H_1u
+\beta_0u^2\log_2{1\over u^2}
+O(u^2),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where the regular derivative can be written exactly as

\[
H_1=-2\sum_{S:a_S\ne0}a_Sb_S\log_2(a_S^2).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The constant `-log_2 e` in `h'` cancels by `sum a_S b_S=0` from (2.4).

For the present `Gamma_*`, every remaining term in (1.1) is one-sided differentiable at `u=0`: the source `Gamma_0` is piecewise log-affine, the quadratic part is polynomial, the shaped correction is polynomial, the other biased influences are polynomial in `u`, and `p_out` is affine in `u`.  Knots may change the **one-sided linear coefficient**, but none creates a `u^2 log(1/u^2)` term.

Combining (3.2) and (4.2) therefore proves the requested result.
