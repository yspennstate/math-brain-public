### 5.2 Selected--selected support graph

Now isolate the potentially extremal `q_SS` part. Form the finite graph `H_SS` whose vertices are the retained selected labels touched by selected--selected charged support edges and whose edges are those `q_SS` raw variables.

Let

`v=|V(H_SS)|`,

`sum_a v_a=v <= 2q_SS`.                                      (5.2)

A selected--selected raw edge can contaminate only the retained deadline at each of its two selected endpoints. Therefore the total number of SS-caused omissions satisfies

`d_SS <= v <= 2q_SS`.                                        (5.3)

For a fixed abstract graph shape and fixed incidence orientations, choosing the actual retained proposal labels costs at most

`C_r k^v`:                                                    (5.4)

there are only `rk` proposal positions and `r,q_SS` are fixed. Root-owner assignments contribute only constants. Distinct charged SS edges are distinct Uniform raw variables and, on the Claim-315 guard, each positive charge costs at most `C_r k/n`. Thus the charged-edge codimension contributes

`(C_r k/n)^{q_SS}`.                                          (5.5)

Finally the residual omission relief contributed by these SS incidences is at most

`k^{d_SS} <= k^v`.                                           (5.6)

Multiplying (5.4)--(5.6), the entire selected--selected factor is bounded by

`C_r^{q_SS} k^{v+d_SS+q_SS}/n^{q_SS}`

This is rootwise and does not assume symmetry among roots.

If charged SS edges share a selected endpoint, then `v<2q_SS`; the candidate-label multiplicity and the possible omission relief both decrease. If an edge is lighter than only one endpoint deadline, then `d_SS<v`; again the factor decreases. Therefore no root-concentrated or endpoint-sharing SS skeleton beats (5.7).
