# 5. `P03-QUADRATIC-PIVOT-SELECTOR-45` — PROVED

## 5.1 Every affine four-block is first separated in degree two

Identify the truth table with `V=F_2^n`. Every nonzero affine four-block can be written
\[
P=x+W,
\qquad
W=\{0,u,v,u+v\},
\qquad
\dim W=2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a Boolean polynomial gate `g=(-1)^q`, its product on `P` is
\[
g_P=(-1)^{\sum_{y\in P}q(y)}.
\]
Constants and linear monomials have even parity on every affine two-plane, so they never separate `P`.

For a quadratic monomial
\[
q_{ij}(y)=y_i y_j,
\]
a direct four-point expansion in `F_2` gives
\[
\sum_{y\in P}y_i y_j
=
u_i v_j+u_jv_i.
\tag{5.2}
\]
The right side is the Plücker coordinate
\[
p_{ij}(W).
\]
Because `u,v` are independent, their wedge `u wedge v` is nonzero. Hence at least one minor `p_ij(W)` equals one.

Therefore, for any compiler order in which all affine monomials precede all quadratics,
\[
\boxed{
\text{the first separator of every affine four-block is a quadratic gate.}
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
No cubic or higher monomial can ever be the first separator of a weight-four affine relation.

This is an actual compiler statement, not an affine change of the physical influence coordinates.

## 5.2 Lexicographic order = Plücker pivot / Schubert cell

Fix the allowed compiler order in which quadratic pairs `(i,j)`, `1<=i<j<=n`, are lexicographically ordered.

Then the first separator of `W` is the first pair `(i,j)` with
\[
p_{ij}(W)=1.
\]
Equivalently, `(i,j)` is the pivot-column pair of the unique reduced row-echelon basis matrix of `W`.

A reduced row-echelon basis has

\tag{5.4}

Summing the cells gives the Gaussian binomial identity

# 8. `P03-TERMINAL-AFFINE-COSET-SEPARATION-47` — PROVED

\[

\[

\]
But `P triangle Q` is nonzero and has weight at most eight, contradicting the minimum distance `Delta_d>8`.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D_d+P\ne D_d+Q.

\tag{8.1}
\]

Equivalently, for every outside relation `A notin D_d`, there is **at most one** affine block `P` for which
