# 5. `P03-QUADRATIC-PIVOT-SELECTOR-45` — PROVED

## 5.1 Every affine four-block is first separated in degree two

Identify the truth table with `V=F_2^n`. Every nonzero affine four-block has the form
\[
P=x+W,
\qquad
W=\{0,u,v,u+v\},
\qquad
\dim W=2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
For a polynomial gate `g=(-1)^q`, its product on `P` is `(-1)^{sum_{y in P}q(y)}`. Constants and linear monomials have even parity on every affine two-plane.

For the quadratic monomial
\[
q_{ij}(y)=y_i y_j,
\]
a direct four-point expansion in `F_2` gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{y\in P}y_i y_j=u_i v_j+u_jv_i.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
The right side is the Plücker coordinate `p_{ij}(W)`. Because `u,v` are independent, `u wedge v` is nonzero, so at least one `p_{ij}(W)=1`.

Therefore, for any compiler order in which all affine monomials precede all quadratics,
\[
\boxed{\text{the first separator of every affine four-block is a quadratic gate.}}
\tag{5.3}
\]
No cubic or higher monomial can be the first separator of a weight-four affine relation.

## 5.2 Lexicographic order = Plücker pivot / Schubert cell

Fix lexicographic order on quadratic pairs `(i,j)`, `1<=i<j<=n`. The first separator of `W` is the first pair with `p_{ij}(W)=1`, equivalently the pivot-column pair of the unique reduced row-echelon basis matrix of `W`.

Let
\[
\mathcal W_{ij}=\{W\le F_2^n:\dim W=2,\ \operatorname{pivot}(W)=(i,j)\}.
\]
The RREF basis has `j-i-1` free entries in row one between the pivots and `2(n-j)` free entries after the second pivot. Hence
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal W_{ij}|=2^{2n-i-j-1}.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Summing the cells gives
\[
\sum_{i<j}|\mathcal W_{ij}|={n\brack2}_2=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}6.
\tag{5.5}
\]

## 5.3 Exact translation-covariant selector formula

For pivot `(i,j)`, define
\[
m_{ij}(x)=\#\{W\in\mathcal W_{ij}:f_{x+W}=-1\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
For each direction `W`, the unique affine plane through `x` is `x+W`, so
\[
\boxed{
m_{ij}(x)f(x)=\frac12\sum_{W\in\mathcal W_{ij}}\left[f(x)-\prod_{0\ne w\in W}f(x+w)\right].
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Let
\[
A_{ij,S}=\sum_xm_{ij}(x)f(x)\chi_S(x).
\]
For a basis `W=span{u,v}`, set
\[
g_W(x)=f(x+u)f(x+v)f(x+u+v).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{A_{ij,S}=\frac N2\sum_{W\in\mathcal W_{ij}}\left[\widehat f(S)-\widehat g_W(S)\right].}
\tag{5.8}
\]
Moreover
\[
\widehat g_W(S)=\sum_{A\triangle B\triangle C=S}\widehat f(A)\widehat f(B)\widehat f(C)\chi_A(u)\chi_B(v)\chi_C(u+v).
\tag{5.9}
\]
Thus the gate-resolved selector measure has the explicit quadratic-pivot form
\[
\boxed{
\mu_{ij}=\frac{12}{N^2}\sum_{W\in\mathcal W_{ij}}\sum_S\widehat f(S)\left[\widehat f(S)-\widehat g_W(S)\right]\delta_{\widehat f(S)^2}.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]
There are no higher-degree first-separator selector measures:
\[
\boxed{\mu_k=0\quad\text{for every first-separator gate of degree }\ge3.}
\tag{5.11}
\]
Consequently the signed aggregate identities (4.4)–(4.7) are generated entirely in the quadratic layer. The selector/correlation problem is reduced from an exponentially long compiler to at most `binom(n,2)` explicit Plücker pivot cells.

## 5.4 Exact first-separation masses

# 7. `P03-TERMINAL-MOMENT-SATURATION-46` — PROVED

# 8. `P03-TERMINAL-AFFINE-COSET-SEPARATION-47` — PROVED

\[

\boxed{P\ne Q\implies D_d+P\ne D_d+Q.}

Equivalently, for every `A notin D_d`, there is at most one affine block `P` with

So one-step affine terminal re-entry has source multiplicity one.
