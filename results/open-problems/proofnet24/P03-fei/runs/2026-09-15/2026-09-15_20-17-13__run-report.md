# 4. `P03-POLARIZED-CELL-GAP-68` — **PROVED**

This is the first new theorem.

Fix an actual degree-`q` monomial gate

\[
g_S(x)=\prod_{i\in S}x_i,
\qquad |S|=q\ge2,
\]

and let `M` be the actual mask code immediately before this gate. Since every lower-degree monomial has already been appended,

\[
RM(q-1,n)\subseteq M.
\]

Hence

\[
D=M^\perp\subseteq RM(n-q,n)
\]

has minimum distance at least

\[
L=2^q.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For any truth-table subset `C` and `sigma in {1,f}`, define the normalized masked cell correlation

\[
c_C^\sigma(m)
=\frac1N\sum_{x\in C}\sigma(x)(-1)^{m(x)},
\qquad m\in M.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then for **arbitrary** cells `C_1,...,C_s` with

\[
s<L,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{m\in M}\prod_{j=1}^s c_{C_j}^f(m)
=
\mathbb E_{m\in M}\prod_{j=1}^s c_{C_j}^1(m).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

## Proof

Expand the left-minus-right side:

\[
\frac1{N^s}
\sum_{x_j\in C_j}
\left(\prod_{j=1}^sf(x_j)-1\right)
\mathbb E_{m\in M}(-1)^{\sum_jm(x_j)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The mask average is the exact relation filter

\[
\mathbb E_{m\in M}(-1)^{\sum_jm(x_j)}
=1_{\{A\in D\}},
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `A` is the parity support of the tuple: a vertex lies in `A` iff it occurs an odd number of times.

If the source/comparator factor in (4.4) is nonzero, then

\[
\prod_j f(x_j)=-1.
\]

Repeated vertices of even multiplicity cancel from this product, so

\[
f_A=-1,
\]

and in particular `A` is nonzero. But

\[
|A|\le s<L,
\]

contradicting the dual-distance bound (4.1). Therefore every term in (4.4) vanishes.

QED.

### Interpretation

This is stronger than the scalar statement `C_{M,r}=0` below the collision threshold. It remains true after **arbitrary cell polarization** and is therefore exactly adapted to the parent-Walsh recurrence from `P03-PARENT-WALSH-GATE-66`.

No absolute value has been introduced.

# 5. `P03-MINIMAL-QFLAT-DEPOSIT-69` — **PROVED**

Let

\[

\qquad
R=2^{q-1}.
\]

The R16 deposit at order `R` is

\right),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `x_1,...,x_{L-1}` are iid uniform and `x_L=x_1+...+x_{L-1}`.

## 5.1 Every contributing support has exactly `L` distinct vertices

Let `A` be the tuple's parity support. The pre-gate relation implies

\[
A\in D=M^\perp.
\]

The sign condition gives

\[
f_A=-1,
\]

so `A` is nonzero. Also

\[
|A|\le L.
\]

By the minimum distance (4.1),

\[
|A|=L.
\]

Since the tuple itself has exactly `L` positions, every position is distinct and `A` is the complete tuple support.

## 5.2 The current gate coordinates occur in every pattern exactly once

For `y in F_2^q`, let

\[
n_y=|\{x\in A:x_S=y\}|.
\]

Because every degree-`<q` monomial lies in the pre-gate code, for every proper subset `U subsetneq S`,

\[
\sum_{y\in\mathbb F_2^q} n_y\prod_{i\in U}y_i=0\pmod2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The current gate is odd on `A`, so

\[
\sum_y n_y\prod_{i\in S}y_i=1\pmod2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Over `F_2`, the square zeta matrix

\[
Z_{U,y}=1_{\{U\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(y)\}}
\]

is invertible. The all-ones vector `(n_y mod 2)=1` has transform zero on every proper `U` and one on `U=S`, because

\[
\sum_y\prod_{i\in U}y_i
=2^{q-|U|}
\]

is even for `U subsetneq S` and equals one for `U=S`. Hence (5.2)–(5.3) force

\[
n_y\equiv1\pmod2
\qquad\text{for every }y.
\]

There are `L` cells and exactly `L` points, so

\[
\boxed{n_y=1\quad\forall y\in\mathbb F_2^q.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus projection onto the current gate coordinates is a bijection.

## 5.3 The support is an affine `q`-flat

By (5.4), write the support as the graph

\[
A=\{(y,F(y)):y\in\mathbb F_2^q\}
\]

after placing the gate coordinates first.

Fix an outside coordinate `j` and write its graph component in ANF:

\[
F_j(y)=\sum_{V\subseteq S}a_V y_V.
\]

For every `U subseteq S` with `|U|<=q-2`, the monomial

\[
x_j\prod_{i\in U}x_i
\]

has degree at most `q-1` and belongs to the pre-gate code. Hence

\[
0
=\sum_yF_j(y)y_U
=\sum_{V:V\cup U=S}a_V.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Put `W=S\setminus U`. Then for every `|W|>=2`,

\[
\sum_{V\supseteq W}a_V=0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Descending Möbius inversion on the subset lattice gives

\[
a_V=0
\qquad(|V|\ge2).
\]

Therefore every outside coordinate `F_j` is affine linear. The support `A` is an affine `q`-flat.

This proof uses the actual monomial constraints and does not import an unseen Reed–Muller minimum-word classification.

## 5.4 The current gate is its first Plücker separator

For an affine `q`-flat, the parity of a degree-`q` monomial is the corresponding `q x q` Plücker minor, as proved in `P03-QFLAT-PLUCKER-FIRSTSEP-59`. All earlier degree-`q` gates vanish on `A` because `A in D`; the current gate is one by (5.3). Hence the current gate is precisely the first nonzero Plücker coordinate in the actual compiler order.

Let `M_g(f)` be the number of frustrated affine `q`-flats first separated by the current gate. Every such flat has XOR zero for `q>=2` and contributes exactly

\[

\]

ordered tuples to (5.1). The R16 XOR-zero sampling has denominator `N^{L-1}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{

=\frac{2L!}{N^{L-1}}M_g(f).

\tag{5.7}
\]

This proves (0.2).

## 5.6 Complete degree-`q` hierarchy

Every affine `q`-flat survives all lower-degree monomials and is first separated at degree `q`; the Plücker pivot makes that separator unique. After all degree-`q` monomials have been appended, the dual minimum distance doubles to `2^{q+1}`, so the order-`R` reservoir is zero.

\tag{5.8}
