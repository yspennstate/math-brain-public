# 4. New theorem: exact good q-flip drift

## `P03-GOOD-QFLIP-DRIFT-93` — **PROVED**

Let `A in C_0\D_h` be a frustrated debit:

\[
f_A=-1,
\qquad
w=|A|\equiv0\pmod4,
\qquad
w\le N/4-2.
\]

Let

- `U_all(A)=sum_i E_{1,i}E_{0,i}` be all upward q-flips;
- `D_all(A)=sum_i E_{1,i}E_{2,i}` be all downward q-flips;
- `U_+(A)` and `D_-(A)` be the frustration-preserving subsets, i.e. the pairs on which the two `f`-derivative signs agree;
- `U_bad=U_all-U_+`, `D_bad=D_all-D_-`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
U_+(A)-D_-(A)
\ge
\frac N4\Big[(N-2w)J_A-(N-w)J(f)\Big].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

From (3.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{\rm all}-D_{\rm all}
&=\sum_i E_{1,i}(E_{0,i}-E_{2,i})\\
&=(N/2-w)\sum_iE_{1,i}\\
&=\frac{N(N-2w)}4J_A,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]

using (3.1).

After the minimizing-character gauge, let `m_i` be the number of original `f`-disagreement undirected `i`-edges. Then

\[
m_i=\frac N2j_i(f),
\qquad
\sum_i m_i=\frac N2J(f).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Exactly as in the preceding upper-flow proof, because `w<=N/4` implies `E_{0,i}>=E_{1,i}`, the sign-destroying upward pairs in direction `i` are at most `E_{0,i}m_i`. Since

\[
E_{0,i}\le (N-w)/2,
\]

we have

\[
U_{\rm bad}
\le\frac{N(N-w)}4J(f).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_+-D_-
&=(U_{\rm all}-U_{\rm bad})-(D_{\rm all}-D_{\rm bad})\\
&=(U_{\rm all}-D_{\rm all})-U_{\rm bad}+D_{\rm bad}\\
&\ge(U_{\rm all}-D_{\rm all})-U_{\rm bad}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Insert (4.2) and (4.4), proving (4.1). No absolute value, independence assumption, or ambient-coordinate change has been used. `QED`.

### Normalized form

Define

\[
p_A=\frac{4D_-(A)}{N J_A},
\qquad
q_A=\frac{4U_+(A)}{N J_A}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
q_A-p_A
\ge
K_A:=(N-2w)-(N-w)\frac{J}{J_A}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Using edge isoperimetry

\[
J_A\ge \ell_w,
\qquad
\ell_w:=\frac{2w}{N}\log_2\frac Nw,
\tag{4.7}
\]

we also obtain the source-independent but weaker estimate

\[
q_A-p_A
\ge
(N-2w)-(N-w)\frac{J}{\ell_w}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The value of (4.6) is that downward availability `p_A` is not thrown away: whenever it is large, the same source also has at least as much upward availability plus the deterministic drift.

# 5. New theorem: bidirectional unit-capacity Borel flow

## `P03-BIDIRECTIONAL-BOREL-FLOW-94` — **PROVED**

Fix a debit shell

\[
w\equiv0\pmod4,
\qquad
8\le w\le N/4-2,
\]

and a Borel time in the first quadrant

\[
x=s/N\in(0,\pi/2),
\qquad
y=\tan^2x>0.
\]

Put

\[
\rho_u(x)=\sin^u x\cos^{N-u}x.
\]

Then

\[
\rho_{w-2}=\rho_w/y,
\qquad
\rho_{w+2}=\rho_w y.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
\varepsilon_n=\frac{8n}{N}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

A four-point q-flip changes the folded relation influence by at most `epsilon_n`, because multiplying by its four-point phase changes total physical influence by at most the physical boundary of four vertices:

\[
|J_{A\triangle P}-J_A|\le \varepsilon_n.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For the adjacent credit shells define

\[
\ell_-:=\ell_{w-2},
\qquad
\ell_+:=\ell_{w+2},
\qquad
\beta_-:=\varepsilon_n/\ell_-,
\qquad
\beta_+:=\varepsilon_n/\ell_+.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Assume `beta_-<1` and `beta_+<1`.

For a particular frustrated debit source `A`, define `p_A,q_A` by (4.5). If

\[
\boxed{
F_A(y):=
(1-\beta_-)
\frac{p_A}{(N-w+2)y}
+
(1-\beta_+)
\frac{q_A y}{w+2}
\ge1,
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

then the entire Borel debit capacity `rho_w(x)` of that source can be routed, with target load at most one, into actual frustrated `2 mod 4` credits of weights `w-2` and `w+2`.

### 5.1 Lower-credit target degree

Let `C` be a lower target of weight `w-2`. Every reverse channel from `C` back to a weight-`w` debit is an upward boundary-empty q-flip of `C`. Therefore its number of incident candidate source edges is at most

\[
\begin{aligned}
\deg_-(C)
&\le \sum_iE_{1,i}(C)E_{0,i}(C)\\
&\le \frac{N-w+2}{2}\sum_iE_{1,i}(C)\\
&=\frac{N(N-w+2)}4J_C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Because `J_C>=ell_-` and `|J_A-J_C|<=epsilon_n`, every incident source obeys

\[
J_A\ge J_C-\varepsilon_n
\ge(1-\beta_-)J_C.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Assign provisionally to every good downward edge from `A` the mass

\[
\mu_-(A\to C)
=\rho_w
\frac{4(1-\beta_-)}{N(N-w+2)yJ_A}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Using (5.6)-(5.7), the total provisional load at `C` is at most

\[
\rho_w/y=\rho_{w-2}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Thus the lower negative-credit radial capacity is respected exactly.

### 5.2 Upper-credit target degree

Let `B` be an upper target of weight `w+2`. Every reverse channel is a downward boundary-full q-flip of `B`, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
\deg_+(B)
&\le \sum_iE_{1,i}(B)E_{2,i}(B)\\
&\le \frac{w+2}{2}\sum_iE_{1,i}(B)\\
&=\frac{N(w+2)}4J_B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Similarly

\[
J_A\ge(1-\beta_+)J_B.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Assign provisionally to every good upward edge

\[
\mu_+(A\to B)
=\rho_w
\frac{4(1-\beta_+)y}{N(w+2)J_A}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Then every upper target receives total load at most

\[
\rho_w y=\rho_{w+2}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

### 5.3 Source saturation

The provisional total mass leaving source `A` is

\[
\begin{aligned}
& D_-(A)\mu_-+U_+(A)\mu_+\\
&\qquad=\rho_w\left[
(1-\beta_-)
\frac{p_A}{(N-w+2)y}
+
(1-\beta_+)
\frac{q_Ay}{w+2}
\right]\\
&\qquad=\rho_wF_A(y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.14}
\]

If `F_A(y)>=1`, scale every outgoing provisional mass of this source by `1/F_A(y)<=1`. The source then emits exactly `rho_w`, while every target load can only decrease from the already-valid bounds (5.9), (5.13).

Both target supports have weight `2 mod 4`, so the half-band dual `D_h`, being doubly even, cannot absorb them. Since every used q-flip has `f_P=+1`, they remain frustrated. Hence they are actual deposited negative R32 credits, not synthetic capacity.

This proves the theorem.

### 5.4 Exact unpaid interval for one source

Set

\[
a_A=(1-\beta_-)\frac{p_A}{N-w+2},
\qquad
b_A=(1-\beta_+)\frac{q_A}{w+2}.
\tag{5.15}
\]

Then the payment condition is simply

\[
\boxed{a_A/y+b_Ay\ge1.}
\tag{5.16}
\]

If `a_A b_A>=1/4`, the source is paid for **every** `y>0`.

If `0<a_Ab_A<1/4`, define

\[
y_\pm(A)
=
\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-4a_Ab_A}}{2b_A}.
\tag{5.17}
\]

Then payment holds precisely outside the middle interval:

\[
y\le y_-(A)
\quad\text{or}\quad
y\ge y_+(A).
\tag{5.18}
\]

If `p_A=0`, then `a_A=0`; the theorem reduces to the preceding upper-only transport and has no early-time lower-credit payment.

### 5.5 Drift-based all-time sufficient condition

From (4.6), `q_A>=p_A+K_A`. Therefore a sufficient source-adaptive all-time condition is

\[
\boxed{
4(1-\beta_-)(1-\beta_+)
\,p_A\,(p_A+K_A)
\ge
(N-w+2)(w+2),
}
\tag{5.19}
\]

whenever the right-hand lower bound `p_A+K_A` is nonnegative.

This is the first retained criterion in the compiler that can certify a debit source **before and after its radial mode using actual lower and upper negative credits with unit target capacity**.

### 5.6 Simultaneous shell use and dual-code multiplicity

A credit shell of weight `u=2 mod 4` is adjacent to at most two debit shells, `u-2` and `u+2`. Therefore unrestricted simultaneous application of the two-sided theorem to every debit shell could double-use a credit shell.

There are two rigorous safe forms:

1. apply the theorem to one fixed debit shell at a time, keeping all other credits reserved;
2. apply it simultaneously to all debit shells in one fixed congruence class modulo eight (`w=0 mod 8` or `w=4 mod 8`). For one such class, the adjacent credit-shell sets are disjoint.

No hidden multiplicity factor is suppressed. A proof that combines both modulo-eight debit classes must explicitly split or otherwise account for shared credit capacity.

# 7. Tensor/product stress test of the shell-uniform paid cone

## `P03-TENSOR-PAIDCONE-BARRIER-96` — **PROVED AS A METHOD LIMITATION**

The preceding shell-uniform theorem `P03-SIGNED-BOREL-PAID-CONE-91` used the sufficient condition

\[
w\log_2(N/w)\ge3NJ(f).
\tag{7.1}
\]

This condition is not tensor-stable and therefore cannot be the whole FEI proof.

Let `g` be any fixed noncharacter Boolean phase on `m` variables with `J(g)>0`, and form the product/tensor phase on `k` disjoint blocks

\[
f_k(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(k)})
=\prod_{a=1}^k g(x^{(a)}).
\]

Fourier squared mass tensorizes, hence

\[
H(f_k)=kH(g).
\]

For a coordinate in one block, multiplication by the other blocks cancels in the flip comparison, so each coordinate influence is unchanged and

\[
J(f_k)=kJ(g).
\tag{7.2}
\]
