\boxed{q_{k,1}=\mu_k([0,1])=2\delta_{k,2}.}

## 4.3 Aggregate selector measure is positive

Let `\mathcal P_-` denote all frustrated affine four-blocks. Since every such block is eventually first-separated,
\[
\sum_kA_{k,S}
=
\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_-}\sum_{x\in P}f(x)\chi_S(x).
\tag{4.10}
\]
Put
\[
g(x)=f(x)\chi_S(x),
\qquad
h=\mathbb Eg=h_S.
\]
For an affine block, `\prod_{x\in P}\chi_S(x)=1`, hence `\prod_{x\in P}g(x)=f_P`.

The number of affine four-blocks through one vertex is
\[
r_0=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}6.
\]
For a fixed vertex `x`, let
\[
B_x=\sum_{P\ni x}\prod_{y\in P\setminus\{x\}}g(y).
\]
The unnormalized triple convolution satisfies
\[
(g*g*g)(x)=6B_x+(3N-2)g(x).
\tag{4.11}
\]
Indeed, distinct triples give the six orderings of a unique affine block through `x`, while the union of the three degenerate patterns has exactly `3N-2` ordered triples, each of product `g(x)`.

Summing (4.11) over `x`,
\[
\sum_xB_x
=
\frac{N^3h^3-(3N-2)Nh}{6}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_-}\sum_{x\in P}g(x)
&=
\frac12\sum_P(1-f_P)\sum_{x\in P}g(x)\\
&=
\frac12\left[r_0Nh-\sum_xB_x\right]\\
&=
\boxed{\frac{N^3}{12}h(1-h^2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Combining (4.5), (4.10), and (4.12),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_k\mu_k
=:
\eta_f
=
2\sum_S p_S(1-p_S)\,\delta_{p_S}.
}
\tag{4.13}

Thus the **aggregate** selector measure is positive even though each gate measure `mu_k` can be signed.

Its moment identity is

\boxed{

\tag{4.14}

This is the exact signed telescoping that must be preserved. No gatewise absolute value was taken to obtain (4.13)–(4.14).

# 5. New theorem: `P03-HARMONIC-SELECTOR-BANK-44` — **PROVED**

The preceding `L^1` marked-bank argument pays coefficient sequences with summable absolute coefficients. There is also a strictly larger gate-independent signed class: harmonic `1/r` coefficients are solvent **after signed gate summation**.

Let
\[
c_r=C_{0,r}=1-\sum_Sp_S^r,
\qquad
d_r=c_{r+1}-c_r
=\sum_Sp_S^r(1-p_S)\ge0.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{d_r}{r}
=
\sum_S(1-p_S)\bigl[-\ln(1-p_S)\bigr].
\tag{5.2}
\]

After the minimizing-character gauge, multiply by a global sign so that
\[
\alpha=\Pr[F=-1]\le1/2.
\]
The constant spectral atom is
\[
p_\varnothing=(1-2\alpha)^2,
\qquad
\varepsilon:=1-p_\varnothing=4\alpha(1-\alpha).
\]
For nonconstant atoms,
\[
(1-p)(-\ln(1-p))\le p,
\]
so their total contribution to (5.2) is at most `epsilon`. Also Fourier Poincare gives
\[
\varepsilon=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}p_S\le I(F)=J(f).
\tag{5.3}
\]
For the constant atom,
\[
\varepsilon\ln(1/\varepsilon)
\le4\alpha\ln(1/\alpha).
\]
Cube edge isoperimetry in the present influence normalization gives
\[
J(f)=I(F)
\ge2\alpha\log_2(1/\alpha)
=\frac{2}{\ln2}\alpha\ln(1/\alpha).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{c_{r+1}-c_r}{r}
\le
(1+2\ln2)J(f).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

By (4.14), if a gate-independent real coefficient sequence satisfies
\[
|a_r|\le\frac{A}{r},
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\sum_k\sum_{r\ge1}a_rq_{k,r}\right|
&=
2\left|\sum_{r\ge1}a_r(c_{r+1}-c_r)\right|\\
&\le
\boxed{
2A(1+2\ln2)J(f).
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Important limitation: (5.6) is a **signed sum over gates**. It does not assert
\[
\sum_k\left|\sum_ra_rq_{k,r}\right|=O(J)
\]
for harmonic coefficients. That stronger statement is not proved and would discard the gate cancellation which makes (5.6) solvent.

This theorem goes beyond the earlier absolutely-summable marked bank, but it still does not reach the full Shannon singularity because the required selector weights are gate/scale dependent.
