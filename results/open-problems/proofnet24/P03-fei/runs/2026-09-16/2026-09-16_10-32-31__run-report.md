# 5. `P03-QUADRATIC-KERNEL-LEAKAGE-160` — PROVED

For a Boolean phase `F`, define its generalized translation disagreement

`I_v(F)=Pr_x[F(x) != F(x+v)]`,  `v in V`.

For fixed alternating `A`, let

`p_A(xi)=|widehat{F q_A}(xi)|^2`

and define the spectral leakage outside the comparator support flat

`epsilon_A(F)=sum_{xi notin L_A} p_A(xi)`.

Then

`boxed( epsilon_A(F) = 2 E_{v in K_A} I_v(F) )`.

Moreover, if `A` is uniform over alternating matrices and `m=E_x F(x)`, then

`boxed( E_A epsilon_A(F) = [N/(N-1)] [1-E_A 2^{-nu_A}] (1-m^2) )`,

and

`boxed( E_A[nu_A epsilon_A(F)] = [N/(N-1)] [E nu_A - E(nu_A 2^{-nu_A})] (1-m^2) )`.

These are exact identities.

## 5.1 Proof of the projection identity

If `v in K_A`, then `B_A(x,v)=0` for every `x`. Hence

`Q_A(x+v)=Q_A(x)+Q_A(v)`

and therefore

`q_A(x+v)=q_A(x) q_A(v)`.

The restriction of `q_A` to `K_A` is a character. Consequently the Fourier support of `q_A` is the affine isotypic flat

`L_A={xi : chi_xi(v)=q_A(v) for every v in K_A}`.

The orthogonal Fourier projection onto that isotypic flat is

`Pi_A h(x)=E_{v in K_A} q_A(v) h(x+v)`.

Apply this to `h=F q_A`. Using the kernel identity above,

`Pi_A(F q_A)(x)=q_A(x) E_{v in K_A} F(x+v)`.

By Parseval,

`1-epsilon_A = ||Pi_A(Fq_A)||_2^2`

`= E_x [ E_{v in K_A} F(x+v) ]^2`

`= E_{v,w in K_A} E_x F(x+v)F(x+w)`.

For uniform `v,w in K_A`, the difference `u=v+w` is uniform in `K_A`, so

`1-epsilon_A = E_{u in K_A} E_x F(x)F(x+u)`

`= E_{u in K_A} [1-2 I_u(F)]`.

Therefore

`epsilon_A=2 E_{u in K_A} I_u(F)`.

No independence of Fourier coordinates is used.

## 5.2 Averaging over alternating matrices

The uniform alternating-matrix law is invariant under `GL(n,2)` congruence. Hence for every fixed nonzero `v`,

`c_n := E_A[ 1_{v in K_A} / |K_A| ]`

is independent of `v`.

Summing this quantity over every `v` gives one. The `v=0` contribution is `E 2^{-nu_A}`. Hence

`c_n = [1-E 2^{-nu_A}]/(N-1)`.

Also

`sum_v I_v(F)=N(1-m^2)/2`.

Indeed

`sum_v E_x F(x)F(x+v)=N m^2`.

Substituting into the leakage identity gives

`E epsilon_A = 2 c_n sum_{v !=0} I_v`

`= [N/(N-1)] [1-E2^{-nu_A}](1-m^2)`.

For the nullity-weighted identity, invariance similarly gives for nonzero `v`

`d_n := E_A[ nu_A 1_{v in K_A}/|K_A| ]`

independent of `v`. Summing over all `v` gives `E nu_A`, while `v=0` contributes `E[nu_A 2^{-nu_A}]`. Hence

`d_n=[E nu_A-E(nu_A2^{-nu_A})]/(N-1)`.

Substitution gives the second displayed identity.

## 5.3 Uniform nullity moment

For completeness, the prior nullity calculation is short enough to rederive here. For uniform alternating `A`,

`E |ker A| = sum_v Pr[Av=0]`.

For `v=0` the probability is one. For each nonzero `v`, `Av` is uniform on the `(n-1)`-dimensional subspace `v^perp`, so

`Pr[Av=0]=2^{-(n-1)}`.

Therefore

`boxed( E 2^{nu_A} = 3-2/N )`.

Jensen yields

`E nu_A <= log_2(3-2/N) < log_2 3`.

This bound is dimension-free.
