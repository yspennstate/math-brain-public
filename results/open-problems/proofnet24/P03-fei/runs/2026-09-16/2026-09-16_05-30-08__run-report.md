# 5. `P03-HADAMARD-SCALAR-SANDWICH-129` — PROVED

Let

\[
h(x)=-x\ln x,\qquad h(0)=0,
\]

and define

\[
d(p,q):=e(p,q)-\frac12[h(p)+h(q)].
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then for all `p,q>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
(\ln2)(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2
\le d(p,q)
\le(\ln2)|p-q|.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If

\[
k(p,q):=(p+q)\ln2-d(p,q),
\tag{5.3}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{0\le k(p,q)\le2(\ln2)\sqrt{pq}.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This scalar lemma is the key new signed payment.

## 5.1 Upper bound in (5.2)

Both sides are homogeneous of degree one, so assume `p>=q`, `p>0`, and set

\[
t=\sqrt{q/p}\in[0,1].
\]

It suffices to prove

\[
d(1,t^2)\le(1-t^2)\ln2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

A direct expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(1,t^2)
&=(1+t^2)\ln2\\
&\quad-\frac12\left[(1+t)^2\ln(1+t)+(1-t)^2\ln(1-t)\right]
+t^2\ln t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus (5.5) is equivalent to `F(t)>=0`, where

\[
F(t)=
(1+t)^2\ln(1+t)+(1-t)^2\ln(1-t)
-2t^2\ln t-4t^2\ln2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

With the usual `0 ln0=0` endpoint convention,

\[
F(0)=F(1)=0.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Differentiating on `(0,1)` gives

\[
F''(t)=2\ln\frac{1-t^2}{16t^2},
\tag{5.9}
\]

\[
F'''(t)=\frac4{t^3-t}<0.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Hence `F''` is strictly decreasing from `+infinity` to `-infinity` and has exactly one zero. Also

\[
F'(0+)=0,
\qquad
F'(1-)=-4\ln2<0.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Therefore `F'` first increases, then strictly decreases, crossing zero exactly once after the unique zero of `F''`. Consequently `F` rises from zero and then falls to zero, so `F>=0` on `[0,1]`. This proves the upper bound in (5.2).

No convexity claim outside this exact interval is used.

## 5.2 Complement identity and the lower bound

Put

\[
P=\frac{(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2}{2},
\qquad
Q=\frac{(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2}{2}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is the squared-mass image of the normalized two-point Hadamard transform. A direct substitution shows

\[
\boxed{d(p,q)+d(P,Q)=(p+q)\ln2.}
\tag{5.13}
\]

Indeed a second normalized Hadamard transform returns the masses `p,q`, while the two entropy normalizations contribute exactly `(p+q)ln2`.

Moreover

\[
|P-Q|=2\sqrt{pq}.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Applying the already proved upper bound to `(P,Q)` gives

\[
k(p,q)=d(P,Q)\le2(\ln2)\sqrt{pq},
\]

which is (5.4). Subtracting from `(p+q)ln2` gives

\[
d(p,q)\ge(\ln2)(p+q-2\sqrt{pq})
=(\ln2)(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2,
\]

completing (5.2).

The proof retains the exact complementary signed term `k`; it does not replace it by a positive part before the identity (5.13) is used.
