## 5. Move #19 — make the defect recursion into a terminating classifier

The key new structural theorem is that every **connected** set in the cell has bounded active dimension.

Call a coordinate active if an internal edge of `M` uses that direction. For a connected set, the number of active coordinates equals the dimension of the smallest coordinate face containing `M`: if a coordinate varies on `M`, a path in the connected induced graph must flip it somewhere.

### `P03-C7-M13-B51-ACTIVE-DIMENSION-214 = PROVED`

**Theorem 5.1.** If `M subset Q_7` is connected, `|M|=13`, and `e(M)=20`, then `M` is contained in a 5-dimensional coordinate face.

#### Proof

Assume for contradiction that the active dimension is `r>=6`. Internal edges partition by coordinate direction, so a least-used active direction has
\[
1\le c\le \left\lfloor\frac{20}{r}\right\rfloor\le3
\tag{5.1}
\]
cross-edges. Split on that coordinate, exchange the two sections if necessary, and write their sizes as `13-b` and `b` with `1<=b<=6`.

Insert `D(M)=2`, `c<=3`, nonnegative section defects, and (3.5) into (3.4). The only possibilities are:

- `b=1,c=1`: the 12-section has defect one and the singleton has defect zero;
- `b=2,c=2`: both sections are extremal;
- `b=3,c=3`: both sections are extremal;
- `b=4,c=3`: both sections are extremal;
- `b=5,c=3`: both sections are extremal.

There is no `b=6` possibility. (The exact checker also records the formally symmetric defect notation before the size-one impossibility is removed.)

We first need one sublemma.

**Sublemma 5.2.** Every 12-vertex set of defect one (`e=19`) has active dimension at most four.

Suppose instead that such a 12-set `A` has active dimension at least five. A least-used active direction has `1<=c'<=floor(19/5)=3`. Split into sizes `12-b',b'`, `b'<=6`. The 12-set defect recursion with vector (3.6) and total defect one has exactly one possible budget:
\[
b'=4,\qquad c'=3,\qquad D(A_0)=D(A_1)=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus the two projected sections are an extremal 8-set and an extremal 4-set. The extremal 8-set is a coordinate 3-face and the extremal 4-set is a coordinate square. Three of the square's vertices lie in the 3-face because `c'=3`; but if three vertices of a coordinate square lie in a coordinate face, its fourth vertex lies there as well. Hence `c'=4`, contradiction. This proves the sublemma.

Return to the five main cases.

**Case `b=1`.** The 12-section has defect one, hence by Sublemma 5.2 uses at most four projected coordinate directions. Since `c=1`, the singleton's projected point belongs to that section. Thus the projected union uses at most four directions; restoring the split coordinate gives active dimension at most five.

**Case `b=2` or `b=3`.** The large sections have sizes 11 and 10 and are extremal; each lies in a coordinate 4-face. Because `c=b`, the entire smaller projected section is contained in the large one. The projected union therefore uses at most four directions, hence the full set at most five.

**Case `b=4`.** The size-9 extremal section lies in a coordinate 4-face. The size-4 extremal section is a coordinate square. Since `c=3`, three vertices of that square lie in the 4-face; the fourth must also lie in it. Again the projected union has dimension at most four.

**Case `b=5`.** The size-8 extremal section is a coordinate 3-face. The size-5 extremal section is contained in a coordinate 3-face (the canonical shape is a square plus one adjacent vertex). Since `c=3`, these two coordinate 3-faces intersect in at least three vertices. A nonempty intersection of coordinate faces is itself a coordinate face and has power-of-two cardinality, so its dimension is at least two. Hence the union of their direction sets has size at most `3+3-2=4`. Restoring the split coordinate again gives active dimension at most five.

Every possible least-used-direction split therefore contradicts `r>=6`. Thus `r<=5`. QED.

This theorem is the algorithmic payoff of the defect parameter: it converts a seven-variable cell into a complete finite core in dimensions four and five, while preserving actual cube realizability.
