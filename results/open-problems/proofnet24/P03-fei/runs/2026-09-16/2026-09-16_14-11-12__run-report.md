# 4. `P03-SYNDROME-ENTROPY-INFLUENCE-181` — PROVED

This is the main new physical-to-Fourier quotient lemma.

## 4.1 General translation influences are exact Fourier syndrome biases

Identify the cube with `F_2^n`. Let `X` be the spectral random variable

\[
\Pr(X=\xi)=\widehat f(\xi)^2.
\]

For any translation vector `v in F_2^n`, define

\[
I_v(f)=\Pr_x[f(x)\ne f(x+v)].
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\mathbb E_x f(x)f(x+v)
=
\sum_\xi \widehat f(\xi)^2(-1)^{v\cdot\xi}
=
1-2I_v(f).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(v\cdot X=1)=I_v(f).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a coordinate vector `e_i`, this is the usual identity `Pr(i in X)=I_i(f)`.

## 4.2 A uniform entropy bound for every translation parity

After a harmless global sign, write

\[
E=\{x:f(x)=-1\},
\qquad
\delta=|E|/2^n\le1/2.
\]

For every `v`,

\[
I_v(f)
=\frac{|E\triangle(E+v)|}{2^n}
\le2\delta.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The cube edge-isoperimetric inequality, in the present influence normalization, gives

\[
\boxed{
J(f)=I(f)\ge 2\delta\log_2(1/\delta).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `b(p)=-p ln p-(1-p)ln(1-p)` be binary entropy in nats. We claim

\[
\boxed{b(I_v(f))\le J(f)\quad\text{for every }v.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

If `delta<=1/4`, then `I_v<=2delta<=1/2`, so monotonicity of `b` on `[0,1/2]` and `-(1-x)ln(1-x)<=x` give

\[
\begin{aligned}
b(I_v)
&\le b(2\delta)\\
&\le2\delta\ln\frac1{2\delta}+2\delta\\
&=2\delta\ln\frac1\delta+2\delta(1-\ln2).
\end{aligned}
\]

From (4.3),

\[
2\delta\ln(1/\delta)\le(\ln2)J,
\]

and also `J>=2delta`, so the right side is at most `J`.

If `delta in [1/4,1/2]`, then (4.3) gives `J>=1`, while `b(I_v)<=ln2<1`, again proving (4.4).

No path-length comparison of `I_v` with coordinate influences was used. That would generally introduce an unacceptable multiplicity. The argument instead pays the entropy of the **one parity bit** directly from the original boundary.

## 4.3 Arbitrary Fourier syndrome

Let `K<=F_2^n` have dimension `nu` and choose any basis `v_1,...,v_nu`. The quotient random variable

\[
Z_K=X\pmod{K^\perp}
\]

is equivalently represented by the syndrome vector

\[
(v_1\cdot X,\ldots,v_\nu\cdot X).
\]

Shannon subadditivity, with no independence assumption, gives

\[
H_{\rm nat}(Z_K)
\le
\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}H(v_j\cdot X)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}b(I_{v_j}(f))
\le \nu J(f).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{H_{\rm nat}(Z_K)\le\nu J(f).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Finally, if we undo the minimizing-character gauge, the spectral random variable is translated by a deterministic vector. Quotient entropy is invariant under deterministic translation, while the gauged total influence is exactly the original `J`. Therefore (4.5) holds for the original `f` with `J(f)` on the right.

### Scope

The factor `nu` is real and cannot be paid afresh at every compiler state. The utility of (4.5) comes when the relevant nullity has a uniformly bounded **average** or when the `nu` factors themselves telescope with occupation/re-entry. Section 6 uses the first mechanism at the completed quadratic endpoint. A pathwise sum of `nu J` over all stars would be invalid.

# 5. `P03-QUADRATIC-COSET-TRANSPORT-182` — PROVED

Let

\[
q_A(x)=(-1)^{Q_A(x)}
\]

be a homogeneous quadratic phase on `F_2^n`, with alternating polar matrix `A`. Put

\[
K=\ker A,
\qquad
V=K^\perp=\operatorname{im}A,
\qquad
r=\operatorname{rank}A,
\qquad
\nu=n-r.
\]

## 5.1 Plateaued comparator spectrum from the radical

For `v in K`, the polar term vanishes, hence

\[
Q_A(x+v)=Q_A(x)+Q_A(v)
\]

and

\[
q_A(x+v)=q_A(x)q_A(v).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The restriction `q_A|_K` is a character, so there exists an affine offset `alpha` modulo `V` such that

\[
q_A(v)=(-1)^{\alpha\cdot v}
\qquad(v\in K).
\]

The Fourier autocorrelation identity gives

\[
\sum_\xi |\widehat q_A(\xi)|^2(-1)^{v\cdot\xi}
=
\mathbb E_x q_A(x)q_A(x+v).
\]

The right side equals `q_A(v)` for `v in K`, and vanishes for `v notin K` because then the linear polar phase `x -> x^TAv` is nontrivial. Fourier inversion therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat q_A(\xi)|^2
=
\begin{cases}
2^{-r},&\xi\in\alpha+V,\\
0,&\xi\notin\alpha+V.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular

\[
H_{\rm nat}(q_A)=r\ln2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

This rederives, from the radical character directly, the plateaued quadratic structure used in earlier rank-credit claims.

## 5.2 Exact coset-mass transport

Decompose an arbitrary Fourier coefficient vector `hat f` into the mutually orthogonal coset blocks of `V`. If one block is supported on `c+V`, then by Fourier convolution

\[
\widehat{f q_A}(\eta)
=
\sum_\xi \widehat f(\xi)\widehat q_A(\eta+\xi).
\]

Since `hat q_A` is supported on `alpha+V`, a source block `c+V` can contribute only to the target block

\[
c+\alpha+V.
\]

Multiplication by `q_A` is an `L^2` isometry. Distinct source cosets go to distinct target cosets. Hence the total squared Fourier mass of each block is preserved exactly, merely permuted by `alpha`.

Let

\[
Z_A=X\pmod V,
\qquad
m_z=\Pr(Z_A=z).
\]

Then the quotient mass law of `f q_A` is precisely `{m_z}` up to a deterministic permutation. This is stronger than the earlier leakage identity: it preserves the full quotient probability vector, not only its mass outside one comparator coset.

## 5.3 The block transform is flat orthogonal

Inside one nonzero source coset block, the convolution matrix has entries equal, up to signs, to the nonzero Fourier coefficients of `q_A`. By (5.2), every entry has magnitude

\[
2^{-r/2}.
\]

Because multiplication by `q_A` is an involutive isometry, the block map is a real orthogonal matrix of size

\[
S=2^r
\]

with every matrix entry of magnitude `S^{-1/2}`.

For any unit vector `u` and such a flat orthogonal matrix `U`, let `p_i=u_i^2`, `p'_j=(Uu)_j^2`. The Shannon uncertainty inequality gives

\[
\boxed{H(p)+H(p')\ge\ln S=r\ln2.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For completeness, (5.4) follows from the interpolation endpoints

\[
\|U\|_{1\to\infty}=S^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|U\|_{2\to2}=1.
\]

Riesz–Thorin yields the Hausdorff–Young family between them; differentiating the logarithm at the `2->2` endpoint gives (5.4). This is the same finite flat-transform uncertainty mechanism used earlier in `P03-QUADRATIC-UNCERTAINTY-DEFICIT-115`, now applied coset-by-coset.

## 5.4 Exact entropy sandwich

Condition on a quotient coset `z` with mass `m_z>0`. Let `H_z^{in}` and `H_z^{out}` be the normalized conditional Fourier entropies before and after multiplication by `q_A`. Then

\[
0\le H_z^{out}\le r\ln2
\]

and by (5.4)

\[
H_z^{out}\ge r\ln2-H_z^{in}.
\]

The chain rule gives

\[
H_{\rm nat}(f)
=H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)+\sum_zm_zH_z^{in},
\]

\[
H_{\rm nat}(f q_A)
=H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)+\sum_zm_zH_z^{out}.
\]

Using the upper conditional bound,

\[
H_{\rm nat}(f q_A)
\le H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)+r\ln2,
\]

while the uncertainty lower bound gives

\[
H_{\rm nat}(f q_A)
\ge H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)+r\ln2
-\sum_zm_zH_z^{in}
=
2H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)+r\ln2-H_{\rm nat}(f).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
2H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)-H_{\rm nat}(f)
\le
H_{\rm nat}(f q_A)-r\ln2
\le
H_{\rm nat}(Z_A).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

No absolute value has been introduced. The lower side explicitly allows a large **negative entropy credit**.

# 6. `P03-QUADRATIC-ENDPOINT-SYNDROME-183` — PROVED

For the completed homogeneous quadratic layer, `A` is uniform over all alternating `n x n` matrices over `F_2`, and

\[
R_2(f)
=
\mathbb E_A
\left[
H_{\rm nat}(f q_A)-\operatorname{rank}(A)\ln2
\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
\nu_A=\dim\ker A.
\]

By the upper half of (5.5) and the syndrome theorem (4.5),

\[
R_2(f)
\le
\mathbb E_A H_{\rm nat}(Z_A)
\le
\mathbb E_A\nu_A\,J(f).
\tag{6.2}
\]

It remains to bound the mean nullity dimension-freely.

For fixed nonzero `v`, the random vector `Av` is uniform on `v^perp`, which has `2^{n-1}` elements. Hence

\[
\Pr(Av=0)=2^{-(n-1)}.
\]

Therefore, with `N=2^n`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E 2^{\nu_A}
&=\mathbb E|\ker A|\\
&=1+\sum_{v\ne0}\Pr(Av=0)\\
&=1+(N-1)2^{-(n-1)}\\
&=3-\frac2N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Jensen gives

\[
2^{\mathbb E\nu_A}\le\mathbb E2^{\nu_A}=3-2/N,
\]

so

\[
\mathbb E\nu_A
\le
\log_2(3-2/N).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Combining (6.2)–(6.4),

\[
\boxed{
R_2(f)
\le
\log_2(3-2/N)J(f)
<\log_2(3)J(f).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The coefficient in (6.5) is a pure number multiplying `J`; the entropy on the left is in **nats**. Thus the corresponding bit-entropy coefficient for this endpoint alone is

\[
\frac{\log_2 3}{\ln2}\approx2.286.
\]
