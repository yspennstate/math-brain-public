## 4. Move #15 — break symmetry on purpose: split even/odd Fourier degrees

### Claim `P03-C7-ODD-INVERSION-MOD64-205 = PROVED`

For **odd** `m`, translate `M` by its XOR syndrome

`sigma(M)=xor_{x in M} x`.

Because `m` is odd,

`sigma(M+t)=sigma(M)+t`,

so choosing `t=sigma(M)` makes the translated set satisfy `sigma=0`. Cube translation preserves `B` and every Walsh magnitude, so this normalization loses nothing.

For the normalized set put

`N_S = #{x in M : S dot x = 1}`.

Since

`N_S mod 2 = S dot sigma(M)=0`,

`N_S` is even for every `S`, and therefore the **signed** Walsh coefficient obeys

`F_M(S)=m-2N_S == m (mod 4)`.

Now use unnormalized Walsh inversion twice. At `y=0`,

`sum_S F_M(S) = 128 1_M(0)`;

at `y=1^7`,

`sum_S (-1)^{|S|}F_M(S) = 128 1_M(1^7)`.

Let

`E=sum_{|S| even} F_M(S)`,  `O=sum_{|S| odd} F_M(S)`.

Both `E+O` and `E-O` are multiples of 128, hence

`E == O == 0 (mod 64)`.

Since `F_M(empty)=m`, this gives the two new exact signed side constraints

`sum_{S != empty, |S| even} F_M(S) == -m (mod 64)`,

`sum_{|S| odd} F_M(S) == 0 (mod 64)`.

These constraints are strictly stronger than the prior unsigned mod-4 magnitude condition. They preserve the sign information that disappears if one keeps only `a_S=|F_M(S)|`.

## 5. Move #18 — say it in another language: the odd mod-8 pattern is a quadratic form

The preceding parity invariant has more structure than a congruence. Continue in the odd-`m`, XOR-zero normalization and define

`q_M(S)=N_S/2 mod 2`.

Because `N_S` is even,

`N_S/2 == binom(N_S,2) (mod 2)`.

Hence

`q_M(S)=sum_{x<y in M}(S dot x)(S dot y)`

is a quadratic Boolean form in the Fourier label `S`. Its polar form is

`b(S,T)=q(S+T)+q(S)+q(T)`.

Expanding the pair sum gives

`b(S,T)= (sum_x S dot x)(sum_y T dot y) + sum_x (S dot x)(T dot x)`.

The first product vanishes in `F_2` because XOR zero makes each first moment even. Therefore

`b(S,T)=sum_{x in M}(S dot x)(T dot x) = S^T G_M T`,

where

`G_M=sum_{x in M} x x^T (mod 2)`.

Moreover `G_M` has zero diagonal because its `i`th diagonal entry is the `i`th coordinate of `sigma(M)=0`; hence `G_M` is alternating and has even rank `2s in {0,2,4,6}`.

Finally,

`F_M(S)=m-2N_S == m-4q_M(S) (mod 8)`.

So the **entire signed Walsh residue pattern modulo 8 is a quadratic form whose polar matrix is the second moment of the minority set**. This moves the residual finite problem from independent coefficient bins into Reed-Muller / quadratic-form geometry.

### Claim `P03-C7-QUADRATIC-MOD8-WEIGHTS-206 = PROVED`

## 8. Extremizers and source-matching class choice

The certified n<=6 extremizers are one-point flips and output complements. A one-point flip is, after input/output signs, an AND of all n literals. Therefore it is a constant-depth circuit (indeed a single conjunction gate, depending on circuit conventions) while it has full Fourier support and Fourier degree n.

This makes **constant-depth/DNF-style classes** the class attack most faithful to the observed finite extremizer. A bounded-Fourier-degree theorem or a sparse-spectrum theorem can still be useful, but it cannot literally contain the finite extremizer family as n grows. KKLMS is correspondingly relevant as a transfer mechanism for concentration and DNF/Mansour-type consequences, but its `O(I log I)` exponent is too lossy to identify the exact extremizer or sharp finite constant. I therefore do not repackage KKLMS as an exact C_n proof.

A promising class-level question for a future run is: can the one-point extremizer be shown maximal within width-n conjunctions / small DNF perturbations by a local Fourier-majorization argument that is stable under adding clauses? That would be genuinely source-matching progress, but no such theorem is claimed here.
