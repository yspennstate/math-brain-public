### 5.1 High-boundary Shannon/subadditivity route

The entropy bound `H_2(f)<=sum_i h_2(I_i)` plus concavity closes the high-influence / high-boundary range exactly enough to reduce the problem to finitely many low-boundary cells. ### 5.3 Edge-isoperimetric / Harper-Jovanovski structure

The exact minimum boundary and equality classification kill minimum-boundary cells and several near-extremal cases. What is missing is a source-usable classification of *all* sets in the small positive boundary-excess bands that remain, together with enough information on their complete Walsh magnitudes. F_M(S)\equiv m-2(S\cdot\sigma(M))\pmod4.

This eliminated 14 formal cells in the earlier run. It still leaves many cells because mod 4 does not distinguish enough magnitude arrangements within a degree layer.

This certified `(17,63)` and `(33,77)`, but the magnitude relaxation still loses the coherent sign assignment across all 127 nonzero characters.

### 5.6 Global quadratic mod-8 weight

and `q_M` is a quadratic form. The total number of `q=1` labels is highly restricted, but keeping only that scalar weight causes a large DP state space while discarding the degree profile that the boundary moment actually sees.

### 5.7 KKLMS Fourier concentration/min-entropy line

Kelman-Kindler-Lifshitz-Minzer-Safra prove a weaker FEI/concentration theorem giving concentration on `2^{O(I log I)}` characters for constant-variance Boolean functions and explicitly identify removal of the extra `log I` as the step that would essentially resolve FEI. In exploratory dual optimization, however, the fourth-moment multiplier is driven to zero or yields a weaker bound than the existing hard-cell fake spectra. ### 5.9 Direct truth-table enumeration

The full `n=7` space has `2^128` Boolean truth tables before symmetry. No complete symmetry quotient of manageable size has been proved. A sample, SAT fragment, random search, or restricted orbit traversal therefore cannot be called the exact optimum.

## 6. Section-B move #1 — change category: affine spectral quotient

The route explored was to stop viewing sparse Fourier support as merely a list of coefficients and instead view it as a quotient cube.

Suppose the Fourier support of a Boolean `f` lies in an affine subspace `A+V` of `F_2^n` of dimension `r`. Multiplying by the character `chi_A` translates the spectrum to `V`, preserves Fourier entropy, and leaves the folded influence `J=sum_i min(I_i,1-I_i)` invariant. A function with spectrum in `V` is constant on cosets of `V^perp`, hence factors through an `r`-dimensional quotient. Choosing `r` original coordinates whose quotient directions form a basis and then changing basis in `F_2^r` compares the quotient's ordinary coordinate folded influence to the original `J`.

This yields a clean candidate reduction of affine-spectral-dimension `r` functions to the exact `r`-variable constant. In particular, Fourier sparsity at most seven would reduce to `r<=6` because any seven points lie in an affine subspace of dimension at most six.

I checked the gauge, quotient-surjectivity, basis-column, and influence-direction steps. The route looks sound and source-matching, but **it is not promoted as a new theorem in this mandatory stuck run**. Novelty is also unverified; this quotient observation is close enough to standard Fourier folklore that a later claim must include a prior-art search rather than call it new merely because it is useful here.

Why it does not unblock `C_7`: the one-point extremizer itself has full Fourier support, so the exact seven-variable extremal problem is outside the sparse-support class.
