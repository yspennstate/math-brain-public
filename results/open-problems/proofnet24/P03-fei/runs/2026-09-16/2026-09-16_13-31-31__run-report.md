# 4. `P03-HADAMARD-MINLOG-175` — PROVED

The square-root envelope (3.2) is too coarse after comparator subtraction. The exact kernel has a stronger asymmetric logarithmic estimate.

## Theorem

For all `p,q>=0`, let

\[
a=\max(p,q),\qquad b=\min(p,q).
\]

With the right side interpreted as zero when `b=0`,

\[
\boxed{
K(p,q)\le b\ln\frac{4a}{b}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equality holds when `p=q` and in the limiting zero case.

## Proof

`K` is homogeneous of degree one, so assume `p=1`, `q=t=s^2` with `0<s<=1`. Let

\[
P=\frac{(1+s)^2}{4},\qquad
Q=\frac{(1-s)^2}{4}.
\]

Then

\[
e(1,s^2)=-P\ln P-Q\ln Q
\]

and

\[
K(1,s^2)=(1+s^2)\ln2-e(1,s^2)-s^2\ln s.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The desired inequality is

\[
K(1,s^2)\le s^2\ln\frac4{s^2}.
\]

Subtract the left side from the right and multiply by two. It is enough to prove `G(s)>=0` on `[0,1]`, where

\[
G(s)=
4s^2\ln2-2s^2\ln s
-(1+s)^2\ln(1+s)
-(1-s)^2\ln(1-s).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The endpoint limits are

\[
G(0)=G(1)=0,
\qquad
G'(0+)=0,
\qquad
G'(1-)=4\ln2-4<0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
G''(s)=
\ln\frac{256}{s^4(1-s^2)^2}-12
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
G'''(s)=\frac{4(1-2s^2)}{s(s^2-1)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus `G''` is strictly decreasing on `(0,1/sqrt(2))` and strictly increasing on `(1/sqrt(2),1)`. It tends to `+infinity` at both endpoints, while

\[
G''(1/\sqrt2)=12\ln2-12<0.
\]

Hence `G''` has exactly two zeros `alpha<1/sqrt(2)<beta`. Therefore `G'` first increases from zero, then decreases, then increases. Since its final limiting value is still negative, `G'` crosses zero exactly once, from positive to negative, in `(alpha,beta)`. Consequently `G` rises from zero to one maximum and returns to zero, proving `G>=0`.

This proves (4.1). `□`

## Why this matters

For highly unequal masses, (4.1) has the correct small-mass scale

\[
K(a,b)=O\!\left(b\,[1+\ln(a/b)]\right),
\qquad b\ll a,
\tag{4.7}
\]

whereas (3.2) only gives `O(sqrt(ab))`. This distinction is exactly the scale needed when a tiny amount of Fourier mass is born outside a comparator affine flat. Section 7 gives an explicit family where the square-root envelope produces a false non-summable obstruction while the min-log scale remains compatible with `O(J)`.

No positive part of the full compiler response has been taken in proving (4.1); it is a scalar pointwise majorant intended only for pieces that can subsequently be shown universally summable.

# 5. `P03-FOURIER-L1-INFLUENCE-176A` and first-star randomized payment — PROVED

## 5.1 Fourier `l1` lemma

For Boolean `g` on `r` bits, set

\[
L(g)=\sum_A|\widehat g(A)|.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{L(g)^2-1}{2^r}
\le
\frac{8}{r+1}I(g).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

Put `M=2^r`, `a=|hat g(empty)|`, and

\[
S=\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}|\widehat g(A)|.
\]

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and the Fourier influence identity give

\[
S^2
\le
\left(\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}|A|\widehat g(A)^2\right)
\left(\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}\frac1{|A|}\right)
=I(g)Q_r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `1/k <= 2/(k+1)`,

\[
Q_r
=\sum_{k=1}^r\binom rk\frac1k
\le2\sum_{k=1}^r\binom rk\frac1{k+1}
<\frac{4M}{r+1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If `g` is nonconstant, its `+/-` cut in the `r`-cube has at least `r` boundary edges. A self-contained induction proves this edge-connectivity statement: split into the two `(r-1)`-cubes; if both slices are nontrivial use the induction bound in both, if one slice is empty/full use at least one cross edge in addition to the nontrivial slice boundary, and if the two slices are respectively empty/full all `2^{r-1}>=r` cross edges leave the cut. Therefore

\[
I(g)\ge\frac{r}{2^{r-1}}=\frac{2r}{M},
\qquad
MI(g)\ge r+1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also

\[
L(g)^2-1
=(a+S)^2-1
\le2S+S^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equations (5.2)–(5.4) give

\[
\frac{2S}{M}\le\frac{4I(g)}{r+1},
\qquad
\frac{S^2}{M}\le\frac{4I(g)}{r+1},
\]

which proves (5.1). Constants are trivial. `□`

# 7. `P03-L1-COMPARATOR-EXCESS-NOGO-178` — DISPROVED AS A ROUTE

The Fourier-`l1` lemma (5.1) is legitimate for an unmasked Boolean source, but a second exact family shows that normalized `l1` excess over a quadratic comparator cannot be charged by the original source influence with a universal constant.

This also identifies exactly why the old square-root kernel bound (3.2) is unsafe after comparator subtraction.

## 7.1 Exact family

Take even `r>=4`, `M=2^r`. Let `q` be a homogeneous quadratic phase of alternating rank `r-2`, for example a direct sum of `(r-2)/2` disjoint hyperbolic pairs and two null coordinates. Its Fourier law is plateaued on an affine flat of size

\[
S=2^{r-2}=\frac M4,
\]

with coefficient magnitude

\[
\frac1{\sqrt S}=\frac2{\sqrt M}.
\]

Hence

\[
\|\widehat q\|_1=\frac{\sqrt M}{2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `f` be the one-point flip at the origin:

\[
f(0)=-1,
\qquad f(x)=1\ (x\ne0).
\]

Then

\[
I(f)=\frac{2r}{M}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Put `h=fq=q-2\,1_{\{0\}}`, taking `q(0)=1`.

Every Fourier coefficient is shifted by `-2/M`. On the `S=M/4` old support points the original coefficients are `+/-2/sqrt(M)`. Since Fourier inversion at zero gives

\[
1=q(0)=\sum_\xi\widehat q(\xi),
\]

the number of positive support coefficients minus the number of negative support coefficients is `sqrt(M)/2`. Therefore the total `l1` contribution on the old support is

\[
\frac{\sqrt M}{2}-\frac1{\sqrt M}.
\]

The `3M/4` newly born off-support coefficients each have magnitude `2/M`, contributing `3/2`. Thus exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat{fq}\|_1
=
\frac{\sqrt M}{2}+\frac32-\frac1{\sqrt M}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{\|\widehat{fq}\|_1^2-\|\widehat q\|_1^2}{M}
=
\frac{3}{2\sqrt M}
+\frac{5}{4M}
-\frac3{M^{3/2}}
+\frac1{M^2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Dividing by (7.2) gives

\[
\frac{
M^{-1}(\|\widehat{fq}\|_1^2-\|\widehat q\|_1^2)
}{I(f)}
\sim
\frac{3\sqrt M}{4r}
\to\infty.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore there is no universal constant `C` such that

\[
\boxed{
\left(
\frac{\|\widehat{fq}\|_1^2-\|\widehat q\|_1^2}{M}
\right)_+
\le C I(f)
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

for all Boolean `f` and quadratic comparators `q`.

The one-point source already has every coordinate influence `2/M<1/2`, so this is also a counterexample in the legitimately gauged regime.

## 7.2 Why `P03-HADAMARD-MINLOG-175` survives this counterexample

The `l1` excess in (7.4) is created by summing absolute amplitudes of `3M/4` newly born coefficients, each of size `2/M`; this produces a false `M^{-1/2}` scale.

The exact kernel sees their **squared masses**

\[
b=\frac4{M^2}.
\]

Every Fourier mass of `h` is at most

\[
a\le\left(\frac2{\sqrt M}+\frac2M\right)^2\le\frac9M
\qquad(M\ge4).
\]

Therefore the min-log bound (4.1) gives, for every pair in which at least one entry is a newly born off-flat mass,

\[
K(a,b)
\le
\frac4{M^2}\ln(9M).
\tag{7.7}
\]

There are at most `M^2` ordered pairs, and `K_rand` carries the factor `1/M`. Hence the entire contribution of all pairs involving at least one newly born coefficient is bounded by

\[
\boxed{
\frac{4\ln(9M)}{M}.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Since `I(f)=2r/M` and `ln M=r ln2`, (7.8) is `O(I(f))` uniformly in this family.

This is only a **restricted calibration**, not a proof of the complete comparator-centered star inequality: the signed change among pairs remaining on the old plateau support must still be handled relative to the comparator. But it proves the decisive scale distinction:

- the `sqrt(pq)` / Fourier-`l1` envelope creates an artificial non-summable `M^{-1/2}` cost;
- the exact `min × log contrast` kernel sees the correct `M^{-1} log M` scale for support birth.

Accordingly, any next proof that replaces the comparator-subtracted `K` kernel by `2 ln2 sqrt(pq)` before support/re-entry matching is now a **killed route**.

P14 claims that if `P` is any deterministic rank-`r` orthogonal projector and `R` is a Haar-uniform rank-`q` subprojection of `P`, then for the degree-`d` projection-DPP Pearson energy,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,\mathcal A_q^{(d)}(P,R)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}
\frac{(r-j)(q-j+2)}{(q-j)(r-j+2)}.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

I independently rederived the formula as follows.

For a coordinate `d`-set `S`, let

\[
v_S=P^{\wedge d}e_S\in\wedge^d\operatorname{ran}P.
\]

Then

\[
\|v_S\|^2=\det(P_S),
\]

while

\[
\det(R_S)=\|\operatorname{Proj}_{\wedge^d\operatorname{ran}R}v_S\|^2.
\]

By orthogonal invariance inside the `r`-dimensional parent range, for nonzero `v_S` the ratio

\[
X_S=\frac{\det(R_S)}{\det(P_S)}
\]

has the same law as the determinant of the `d x d` compression of a Haar rank-`q` projection in `R^r`. Its standard Jacobi/matrix-beta determinant moment gives

\[
\mathbb E X_S^2
=
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}
\frac{(q-j)(q-j+2)}{(r-j)(r-j+2)}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

This formula also covers the boundary cases by continuity and agrees trivially when `q=r`. If `det(P_S)=0`, then `v_S=0` and the corresponding parent DPP weight is zero, so no division-by-zero term contributes.

Now

\[
\pi_P^{(d)}(S)=\frac{\det(P_S)}{\binom rd},
\qquad
\pi_R^{(d)}(S)=\frac{\det(R_S)}{\binom qd}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal A_q^{(d)}(P,R)
=
\frac{\binom rd}{\binom qd^2}
\sum_{|S|=d}\frac{\det(R_S)^2}{\det(P_S)}.
\]

Taking expectation, using (11.2), and then Cauchy–Binet

\[
\sum_{|S|=d}\det(P_S)=\binom rd,
\]

gives

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal A_q^{(d)}
=
\frac{\binom rd^2}{\binom qd^2}
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}
\frac{(q-j)(q-j+2)}{(r-j)(r-j+2)},
\]

which simplifies exactly to (11.1).

No orientation of `P`, coordinate independence after projection, Gaussianity of a projected coordinate system, or hidden dimension factor is used in this calculation. The P14 claim passes this independent audit.
