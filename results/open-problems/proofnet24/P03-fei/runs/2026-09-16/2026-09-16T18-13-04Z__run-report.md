## 5. Move #12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED

The old relaxation forgot one simple additive invariant: the XOR sum of the minority set.

Define
\[
\sigma(M):=\bigoplus_{x\in M}x\in\mathbb F_2^7.
\tag{5.1}
\]
For each Fourier label `S`, set
\[
N_S:=|\{x\in M:S\cdot x=1\}|.
\]
Then
\[
F_M(S)=m-2N_S.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Modulo two,
\[
N_S\equiv\sum_{x\in M}S\cdot x
=S\cdot\left(\bigoplus_{x\in M}x\right)
=S\cdot\sigma(M)\pmod2.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{F_M(S)\equiv m-2(S\cdot\sigma(M))\pmod4.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

When `m` is even, the two possible residues are `0` and `2`, both invariant under sign. Hence the **magnitudes themselves** obey
\[
\boxed{a_S\equiv m-2(S\cdot\sigma(M))\pmod4.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
This is strictly stronger than the retained parity constraint (4.4).

Coordinate permutations preserve `m`, `B`, all Walsh magnitudes and `L_7`, so only
\[
w:=|\sigma(M)|\in\{0,1,\ldots,7\}
\]
matters. For degree `d`, the number of labels `S` with `S\cdot\sigma=e` is
\[
N_{d,0}(w)=\frac{\binom7d+K_d(w)}2,
\qquad
N_{d,1}(w)=\frac{\binom7d-K_d(w)}2,
\tag{5.6}
\]
where
\[
K_d(w)=\sum_j(-1)^j\binom wj\binom{7-w}{d-j}
\tag{5.7}
\]
is the binary Krawtchouk value. Equation (5.6) is simply the two counts `N_0+N_1=binom(7,d)` and `N_0-N_1=sum_{|S|=d}(-1)^{S\cdot\sigma}=K_d(w)`.

This gives an exact new relaxation: for each possible `w`, split every degree group into the two syndrome classes and permit only magnitudes

while retaining the exact two moments (4.2)–(4.3). If every `w` is impossible or gives `L_7>0`, no Boolean set in that `(m,B)` cell can beat the one-point candidate.

## 10. Why the extremizer points to a genuinely different class attack

The recovered extremizer is a one-point flip. As a Boolean circuit this is an AND of `n` literals (up to output sign), hence it already lies in an extremely simple constant-depth class; but spectrally it is the opposite of sparse: every Fourier coefficient is nonzero, and its Fourier degree is exactly `n`.

Therefore a bounded-Fourier-degree or sparse-spectrum theorem cannot by itself identify the finite extremizer through equality, although it may certify broad nonextremal classes. The natural source-matching class attack suggested by the extremizer is instead **conjunction/subcube indicators and their perturbations**. For an indicator of a codimension-`k` subcube, the nontrivial FEI ratio is exactly the `k`-variable one-point ratio because the unused coordinates contribute no Fourier entropy or influence. This explains why the finite sequence of one-point constants is the relevant extremal benchmark when moving from `n` to `n+1`.

No universal class theorem is claimed here; this is route selection, not a substitute for the 71-cell proof.

## 11. KKLMS transfer audit — hypotheses checked, no exact C7 black box

Kelman–Kindler–Lifshitz–Minzer–Safra, arXiv:1911.10579, use
\[
\widetilde I(f)=\frac{I(f)}{\operatorname{Var}(f)}.
\]
Their Theorem 1.1 gives the Fourier **min-entropy** estimate
\[
H_\infty(f)\le O(\widetilde I\log(1+\widetilde I)),
\]
and Theorem 1.2 gives corresponding Fourier concentration above a threshold of size `2^{-O(\widetilde I log(1+\widetilde I))}`. Their Theorem 1.3 gives a low-degree Shannon bound of the form
\[
H[\widehat{f^{\le D}}]
\le K\sum_{|S|\le D}|S|\log(|S|+1)\widehat f(S)^2+K I(f).
\]

These hypotheses apply to Boolean cube functions, so there is no domain mismatch. The obstruction is quantitative: the constants are not the exact seven-variable constants needed in (4.1), min-entropy is strictly weaker than Shannon entropy, and the low-degree estimate carries an extra logarithmic degree factor. The authors themselves frame removal of the extra logarithm as essentially the FEI barrier. Consequently no KKLMS theorem is imported as an exact `C_7` step or equality classification.

The transferable idea is structural rather than numerical: impose additional spectral concentration/syndrome constraints before entropy comparison. The XOR-mod-4 invariant above is an exact finite realization of that philosophy, but it is elementary and is not claimed as a theorem from KKLMS.
