# 9. The sole residual cell (m,B)=(8,26) is impossible

## `P03-C6-NO-8SET-11EDGES-197 = PROVED`

If `m=8` and `B=26`, the number `e(M)` of internal cube edges would satisfy

\[
26=6\cdot8-2e(M),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{e(M)=11.}
\tag{9.1}
\]

We prove that no 8-vertex subset of a hypercube has exactly 11 induced edges.

Choose an active coordinate and split `M` into the two corresponding `Q_5` layers. Let their sizes be `r` and `8-r`, with `1<=r<=4` after swapping layers. Let `e_0,e_1` be the induced-edge counts inside the layers and `c` the number of crossing matching edges. Then

\[
e(M)=e_0+e_1+c,
\qquad c\le r.
\tag{9.2}
\]

The exact edge-isoperimetric maxima in a cube for set sizes `1,...,7` are

\[
0,1,2,4,5,7,9.
\]

Therefore:

- if `r=1`, then `e(M)<=0+9+1=10`;
- if `r=2`, then `e(M)<=1+7+2=10`;
- if `r=3`, then `e(M)<=2+5+3=10`.

Hence every active coordinate would have to split `M` as `4+4`.

For such a split, `e_0,e_1<=4` and `c<=4`. If `c=4`, then under the natural identification of the two layers the two four-sets are identical, so `e_0=e_1` and `e(M)=2e_0+4` is even, contradicting `e(M)=11`. Hence `c<=3`. Equality `e(M)=11` would therefore force

\[
(e_0,e_1,c)=(4,4,3).
\tag{9.3}
\]

A four-vertex induced subgraph of a hypercube with four edges is a 4-cycle, hence an affine coordinate 2-subcube. Thus both layers are affine 2-subcubes of `Q_5`. The crossing count `c` is the size of their intersection after identifying the layers. The intersection of two affine coordinate subcubes is empty or another affine subcube, hence has cardinality

\[
0,1,2,\text{ or }4,
\]

never `3`. This contradicts (9.3).

Therefore `(m,B)=(8,26)` is impossible.
