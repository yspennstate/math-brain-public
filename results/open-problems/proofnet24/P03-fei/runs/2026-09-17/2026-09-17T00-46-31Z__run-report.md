# 4. Frozen successor metric

The create-only I0111 claim freezes the following structural quantity.

For a sequence of finite connected simple vertex-transitive graphs `G_n`, set

\[
N_n=|V(G_n)|,\qquad
\Delta_n=\deg(G_n),\qquad
D_n=\operatorname{diam}(G_n),\qquad
M_n=\frac{N_n}{\Delta_n+1}.

}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where the supremum ranges over all such cutoff sequences.

# 5. Move #18 — say it in another language: cutoff forces subpolynomial diameter

## Claim `P03-I0111-POLYNOMIAL-DIAMETER-REDUCTION-217 = KNOWN`

With the definitions in (4.1),

\[
\boxed{\rho_{\rm VT}=0.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, every source-eligible vertex-transitive cutoff sequence with `M_n -> infinity` satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D_n=M_n^{o(1)}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is **KNOWN-IMPLIED** by Tessera–Tointon plus the standard necessity of the product condition. I give the full implication here so the scope, normalization, and quantifiers are auditable; I do not claim originality.

### Proof

First recall the elementary cutoff necessity in the exact continuous-time normalization used here.

Let `G` be finite regular, let `P_t` be the rate-one continuous-time simple-random-walk semigroup, let `lambda` be its spectral gap, and let

\[
d(t)=\max_x\|P_t(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}.
\]

Choose a real spectral-gap eigenfunction `phi` with stationary mean zero and `||phi||_infinity=1`, and choose `x` with `|phi(x)|=1`. Then

\[
|\mathbb E_x\phi(X_t)-\pi(\phi)|=e^{-\lambda t}.
\]

By the dual bound for total variation,

\[
|\mu(\phi)-\nu(\phi)|\le2\|\phi\|_\infty\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{d(t)\ge\frac12e^{-\lambda t}.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If a sequence has cutoff and `t_n=t_mix^{(n)}(1/4)`, then for every fixed `a>1`,

\[
d_n(a t_n)\to0.
\]

If `lambda_n t_n` stayed bounded along a subsequence, (5.3) would give a positive lower bound on `d_n(a t_n)` there, a contradiction. Hence every cutoff sequence satisfies the necessary product condition

\[
\boxed{\lambda_n t_n\to\infty.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Now suppose a cutoff sequence entering (4.1) has

\[
\eta:=\limsup_n\frac{\log D_n}{\log M_n}>0.
\]

Fix `0<delta<eta`. Along an infinite subsequence,

\[
D_n\ge M_n^\delta
=\left(\frac{N_n}{\Delta_n+1}\right)^\delta.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For a simple `Delta_n`-regular graph, the one-ball size is

\[
\beta_{G_n}(1)=\Delta_n+1.
\]

Because `M_n -> infinity`, (5.5) also forces `D_n -> infinity`. Tessera–Tointon's finite vertex-transitive theorem therefore applies for this fixed `delta`: all sufficiently large graphs on the subsequence have moderate growth with constants depending only on `delta`.

The same paper records the standard consequence of moderate growth for lazy random walk:

\[
t_{\rm mix}\asymp_\delta D_n^2,
\qquad
t_{\rm rel}\asymp_\delta D_n^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Passing between lazy discrete time and the rate-one continuous-time simple walk multiplies both time scales by universal constants and does not alter their ratio. Consequently (5.6) yields

\[
\lambda_n t_{\rm mix}^{(n)}(1/4)
=
\frac{t_{\rm mix}^{(n)}(1/4)}{t_{\rm rel}^{(n)}}
=O_\delta(1),
\]

contradicting the necessary condition (5.4). Thus no positive `eta` is possible, so every sequence in (4.1) has limsup ratio zero.

It remains only to check that the class in the supremum is nonempty. Take the hypercubes `Q_d`. Then

\[
N_d=2^d,\qquad \Delta_d=d,\qquad D_d=d,
\]

so

\[
\frac{\log D_d}{\log(N_d/(d+1))}\to0.
\]

For completeness, the rate-one continuous-time walk on `Q_d` has cutoff at `d\log d/4`: the coordinates flip independently at rate `1/d`, and from a point start

\[
\chi^2(t)=\left(1+e^{-4t/d}\right)^d-1.
\]

At `t=(1+epsilon)d log d/4` this tends to zero. At `t=(1-epsilon)d log d/4`, the sum of coordinate signs has mean `d^{(1+epsilon)/2}` and variance at most `d`, whereas stationarity has mean zero and variance `d`; Chebyshev separation gives total variation tending to one. Hence the admissible class is nonempty and its supremum is exactly zero. QED.

### Interpretation

The finite-geometry part of Salez Problem 2.2 is therefore sharply compressed:

> **Any vertex-transitive cutoff sequence with `N/(Delta+1) -> infinity` must live in the subpolynomial-diameter regime `diam = (N/(Delta+1))^{o(1)}`.**

Polynomial-diameter families are not where an unresolved characterization can hide. This is a source-relevant reduction, but it is not new literature.

**Metric movement:** `rho_VT = UNMEASURED -> 0`.

\[
\pi(q)=\left(\frac{r+r^{-1}}2\right)^2,
\qquad
\pi(q)-1=\left(\frac{r-r^{-1}}2\right)^2,

\[
q^2+(2-4x)q+1=0,
\]

whose two roots are `q,q^{-1}` and are distinct away from `q=+/-1`. Thus every fibre is exactly `{q,q^{-1}}`.

Direct subtraction gives

\[
\pi(q)-\pi(r)
=
\frac{(q-r)(qr-1)}{4qr}.
\]

Because `4qr` is a nonzero square,

\[
\chi(\pi(q)-\pi(r))
=
\chi((q-r)(qr-1)).
\]

Within one fibre `qr=1`, so the two lifts are nonadjacent; between distinct fibres the adjacency is exactly that of the common base images. Hence the lifted graph is `G_{p,2}[\overline K_2]`.

\[
\vartheta(\overline G)
=
