# 6. Method #18 — say the periodic source subclass as a finite-state Markov-additive walk

Let horizontal line orientations be

\[
a_y\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad a_{y+q}=a_y,
\]

and vertical line orientations be

\[
b_x\in\{-1,+1\},\qquad b_{x+p}=b_x.
\]

From `(x,y)` the source walk moves with probability `1/2` to either

\[
(x+a_y,y)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(x,y+b_x).
\]

Define the period averages

\[
\bar a=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}a_j,
\qquad
\bar b=\frac1p\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}b_i.
\]

## Claim `P03-I0003-PERIODIC-CLASSIFICATION-230 = KNOWN-IMPLIED / VERIFIED TRANSFER`

For every deterministic doubly periodic line-orientation field as above,

\[
\boxed{
\text{the walk is recurrent}
\iff
\bar a=\bar b=0.
}
\]

If `(bar a,bar b) != (0,0)`, then in fact

\[
\frac{(X_n,Y_n)}n
\longrightarrow
\left(\frac{\bar a}{2},\frac{\bar b}{2}\right)
\quad\text{a.s.},
\]

so the walk is ballistic and transient.

The broad source Problem 3 remains PARTIAL.

## Proof

### 6.1 The finite torus/type chain is irreducible and uniform

Project the walk to

\[
\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z.
\]

Each torus state has two outgoing arrows, one horizontal and one vertical, each used with probability `1/2`. It also has exactly two incoming arrows: one from the predecessor on its horizontal line and one from the predecessor on its vertical line. Thus the torus transition matrix is doubly stochastic.

Its underlying undirected graph is the connected `p x q` torus. A finite directed graph with equal indegree and outdegree at every vertex and connected underlying undirected graph is strongly connected: a sink strongly connected component of the condensation cannot have incoming edges because total indegree equals total outdegree on that component. Therefore the torus/type chain is irreducible, and its stationary distribution is uniform.

### 6.2 Stationary drift

At torus state `(i,j)`, the conditional expected displacement is

\[
d(i,j)=\frac12(a_j,b_i).
\]

Averaging against the uniform stationary law gives

\[
\mu
=
\frac1{pq}\sum_{i,j}d(i,j)
=
\left(\frac{\bar a}{2},\frac{\bar b}{2}\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The finite-state Markov ergodic theorem applied to the type chain, together with the bounded martingale-difference part of the increments, gives

\[
\frac{(X_n,Y_n)}n\to\mu
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence if `mu != 0`, the norm grows linearly and the walk visits every fixed vertex only finitely many times. This proves transience in the nonzero-average case.

### 6.3 Balanced periodic fields are irreducible on the full lattice

Assume now `bar a=bar b=0`. Then each periodic sign sequence contains both `+1` and `-1`, with each sign recurring arbitrarily far in both directions.

Fix start `(x,y)` and target `(X,Y)`. Put `s=a_y` and `sigma=b_X`.

Choose an intermediate row `r` such that

- `a_r=-s`, and
- if `sigma=+1`, then `r<min{y,Y}`;
- if `sigma=-1`, then `r>max{y,Y}`.

Such an `r` exists by periodicity and the presence of both horizontal signs.

Next choose an intermediate column `c` such that

- `b_c=sign(r-y)`, and
- if `s=+1`, then `c>max{x,X}`;
- if `s=-1`, then `c<min{x,X}`.

Such a `c` exists by periodicity and the presence of both vertical signs.

Now concatenate four legal directed segments:

1. along row `y`, move from `x` to `c` in direction `s=a_y`;
2. along column `c`, move from `y` to `r` in direction `b_c=sign(r-y)`;
3. along row `r`, move from `c` to `X` in direction `a_r=-s`;
4. along column `X`, move from `r` to `Y` in direction `b_X=sigma`.

Thus every state reaches every other state: the full lattice walk is irreducible in the balanced periodic case.

### 6.4 Remove temporal period by lazification and verify Takenami's hypotheses

The source walk changes the parity of `x+y` at every step, so it is temporally period two. Define the lazy kernel

\[
\widetilde P=\frac12(I+P).
\]

Lazification does not change recurrence versus transience: deleting holding steps from the lazy chain gives exactly the original skeleton.

For the balanced periodic field, `\widetilde P` satisfies Takenami's Assumptions 1.1–1.4:

1. **spatial periodicity:** periods `(p,q)` are explicit;
2. **irreducible and aperiodic:** irreducibility is proved in §6.3 and the holding probability makes the chain aperiodic;
3. **zero stationary mean displacement:** the type stationary law is uniform and the lazy mean is `mu/2=0` by (6.1); first moments are finite;
4. **finite second moment:** all jumps have norm at most one.

Takenami's Theorem 1.1 therefore gives, in dimension two,

\[
\widetilde P^n(0,0)=\frac{c+o(1)}{n}
\]

for an explicit constant `c>0` determined by the positive-definite effective covariance and the stationary type mass.

Hence

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\widetilde P^n(0,0)=\infty,
\]

so the lazy chain is recurrent. Recurrence of the lazy chain is equivalent to recurrence of the original skeleton. Therefore every balanced doubly periodic Manhattan orientation field is recurrent.

Combining this with §6.2 proves the classification. QED.
