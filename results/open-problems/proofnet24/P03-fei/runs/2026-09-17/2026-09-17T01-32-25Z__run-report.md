# 4. Method #1 — change category: from a graph walk to a spectral Gibbs ensemble

Let `G` be a finite connected undirected vertex-transitive graph with `N` vertices. Let

\[
L=T-I
\]

be the generator of rate-one continuous-time simple random walk, and let

\[
0=\lambda_1<\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_N
\]

be the eigenvalues of `-L`. The invariant law is uniform. For a fixed starting vertex `x`, set

\[
Z(t):=\chi_x^2(t).
\]

Reversibility and the semigroup property give the standard identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(t)
&=N\sum_y\left(P_t(x,y)-\frac1N\right)^2\\
&=NP_{2t}(x,x)-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Vertex transitivity makes `P_{2t}(x,x)` independent of `x`, hence equal to the normalized heat trace. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Z(t)=\sum_{j=2}^N e^{-2\lambda_jt}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This exact identity is standard background, not a new claim.

Define the normalized nontrivial spectral Gibbs weights

\[
q_j(t):=\frac{e^{-2\lambda_jt}}{Z(t)},
\qquad 2\le j\le N.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Write, in natural logarithms,

\[
H_\infty(q_t):=-\log\max_j q_j(t),
\qquad
S(q_t):=-\sum_jq_j(t)\log q_j(t),
\]

and define the order-two effective rank / participation ratio

\[
R_2(t):=\frac1{\sum_jq_j(t)^2}
=\frac{Z(t)^2}{Z(2t)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

## Claim `P03-I0111-MIXING-SPECTRAL-EFFECTIVE-RANK-219 = PROVED`

Fix `0<\varepsilon<1/2`, and let `t_\varepsilon=t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)` for the vertex-transitive walk. Whenever `d_{\rm TV}(0)>\varepsilon`,

\[
\boxed{
H_\infty(q_{t_\varepsilon})
\ge
2\lambda_2t_\varepsilon+\log(4\varepsilon^2),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
S(q_{t_\varepsilon})
\ge
2\lambda_2t_\varepsilon+\log(4\varepsilon^2),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

while

\[
\boxed{
R_2(t_\varepsilon)
\ge
4\varepsilon^2e^{2\lambda_2t_\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

The total-variation distance is start-independent by vertex transitivity. Continuity of the finite continuous-time semigroup gives

\[
d_{\rm TV}(t_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives the standard comparison

\[
d_{\rm TV}(t)\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi_x^2(t)}=rac12\sqrt{Z(t)},
\]

so

\[
Z(t_\varepsilon)\ge4\varepsilon^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since the largest Gibbs weight occurs at the spectral gap,

\[
\max_jq_j(t_\varepsilon)
=\frac{e^{-2\lambda_2t_\varepsilon}}{Z(t_\varepsilon)}
\le\frac{e^{-2\lambda_2t_\varepsilon}}{4\varepsilon^2}.
\]

Taking minus logarithms proves (4.4). Shannon entropy dominates min-entropy, giving (4.5). Finally

\[
\sum_jq_j^2\le\max_jq_j,
\]

so inversion gives (4.6). QED.

### Consequence for cutoff, and why it does not solve the source problem

For finite reversible chains, cutoff implies the necessary product condition

\[
\lambda_2t_\varepsilon\to\infty.
\]

Therefore any vertex-transitive cutoff sequence has

\[
H_\infty(q_{t_\varepsilon}),\ S(q_{t_\varepsilon}),\ \log R_2(t_\varepsilon)\to\infty.
\]

In fact `R_2` grows at least exponentially in `lambda_2 t_mix`.

But this is **not cutoff-specific**. Any vertex-transitive sequence satisfying the ordinary product condition has the same conclusion, including the recovered Abelian-Cayley no-cutoff families from the retired Problem-2.4 line. Thus:

`P03-I0111-HEAT-TRACE-ONLY-ROUTE-220 = DISPROVED AS A CHARACTERIZATION ROUTE`.

This means only that eigenvalue-count/effective-rank divergence at the mixing time cannot by itself characterize cutoff. It is not a refutation of Salez Problem 2.2.

# 5. Method #5 — differentiate the spectral partition function

Set

\[
m(t):=\sum_jq_j(t)\lambda_j,
\qquad
v(t):=\operatorname{Var}_{q_t}(\lambda).

Elementary Gibbs differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{(\log Z)'=-2m(t),\qquad m'(t)=-2v(t),\qquad (\log Z)''=4v(t).}
\tag{5.1}

\[
S(q_t)=\log Z(t)+2tm(t),

# 6. Method #12 — find what is conserved: eigenspace traces under a point-start law

The spatial quantity suggested by Pedrotti--Salez is the local Poincaré constant of the evolving point-start law

\[
\mu_t=P_t(x,\cdot),
\qquad
\gamma(\mu_t)
=
\inf_{f\not\equiv\mathrm{const}}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f}{\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Vertex transitivity does give an exact conserved trace inside each eigenspace, but crucially it does **not** control the smallest local Rayleigh quotient.

Let `E_lambda` be an eigenspace of `-L` of positive eigenvalue `lambda` and dimension `m_lambda`. Choose an `L^2(pi)`-orthonormal real basis `phi_1,...,phi_m`. The spectral projector diagonal

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{m}\phi_a(y)^2
\]

is invariant under every graph automorphism; transitivity and its stationary average imply the pointwise identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{a=1}^{m}\phi_a(y)^2=m
\quad\text{for every }y.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Moreover

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\phi_a=e^{-\lambda t}\phi_a(x).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}(\phi_a)
=m(1-e^{-2\lambda t}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For the carré du champ,

\[
\Gamma\phi_a
=\frac12L(\phi_a^2)+\lambda\phi_a^2.
\]

Summing over the basis and using (6.2) gives the pointwise identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_a\Gamma\phi_a=\lambda m,
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

hence the same equality after integration against `mu_t`.

## Claim `P03-I0111-EIGENSPACE-LOCAL-POINCARE-221 = PROVED`

For every positive eigenvalue `lambda` and every `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma(\mu_t)
\le
\frac{\lambda}{1-e^{-2\lambda t}}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Proof

Apply the variational definition (6.1) to the basis functions. The minimum of their local Rayleigh quotients is at most the denominator-weighted average. By (6.3)--(6.4), that average is exactly

\[
\frac{\sum_a\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma\phi_a}
{\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}\phi_a}
=
\frac{\lambda m}{m(1-e^{-2\lambda t})}.
\]

This proves (6.5). QED.

Taking `lambda=lambda_2` shows that in the product-condition regime and at mixing scale,

\[
\gamma(\mu_{t_{mix}})\le(1+o(1))\lambda_2.
\]

This is an **upper** bound. It does not provide the lower bound on `gamma(mu_t)` needed to invoke Pedrotti--Salez. That directional mismatch is the main mathematical lesson of Method #12.

### What the conserved traces reveal

Inside an eigenspace, let `C_lambda(t)` be the covariance matrix of the basis under `mu_t`, and let `D_lambda(t)` be the corresponding local Dirichlet matrix. Equations (6.3)--(6.4) determine their traces exactly:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}C_\lambda(t)=m(1-e^{-2\lambda t}),
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}D_\lambda(t)=\lambda m.
\]

But `gamma(mu_t)` depends on the **smallest generalized eigenvalue**, not on these traces. Heat-trace thermodynamics sees eigenvalues and multiplicities; the missing variable is the anisotropy/eigenfunction geometry of `(D_lambda,C_lambda)` under the rooted law `mu_t`.

This explains why the category change did not close the problem: transitivity removes the diagonal projector weights after averaging, but the cutoff question lives in the directions that averaging erases.
