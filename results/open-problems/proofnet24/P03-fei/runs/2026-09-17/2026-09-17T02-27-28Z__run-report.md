# 4. Method #15 — break symmetry on purpose: freeze the rooted law into a reversible chain

Averaging over all vertices destroys exactly the information that failed in §3. Instead keep a root `x` and its time-`t` law.

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected `d`-regular undirected graph, let `P` be simple random walk, `L=P-I`, and

\[
\mu_t(y)=P_t(x,y).
\]

For `t>0`, irreducibility gives `mu_t(y)>0` for every `y`. The carré du champ is

\[
\Gamma f(y)=\frac12\sum_zP(y,z)(f(z)-f(y))^2.
\]

Define symmetric time-dependent conductances

\[
 c_t(y,z):=\frac{\mu_t(y)+\mu_t(z)}2P(y,z)
\tag{4.1}
\]

and a continuous-time generator `Q_t` by

\[
q_t(y,z)=\frac{c_t(y,z)}{\mu_t(y)}
=\frac12\left(1+\frac{\mu_t(z)}{\mu_t(y)}\right)P(y,z),
\quad y\ne z,
\tag{4.2}
\]

with diagonal chosen so rows sum to zero.

## Claim `P03-I0111-FROZEN-ROOTED-CHAIN-223 = PROVED`

For every `t>0`:

1. `Q_t` is an irreducible reversible Markov generator with stationary law `mu_t`;
2. its Dirichlet form is exactly the local Pedrotti–Salez form:
   \[
   -\langle f,Q_tf\rangle_{\mu_t}
   =\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f;
   \tag{4.3}
   \]
3. consequently
   \[
   \boxed{\gamma(\mu_t)=\operatorname{gap}(Q_t);}
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
4. its exit rate has the exact score-function form
   \[
   \boxed{
   r_t(y):=\sum_{z\ne y}q_t(y,z)
   =1+\frac12\partial_t\log\mu_t(y).
   }
   \tag{4.5}
   \]

### Proof

Detailed balance is immediate from (4.1):

\[
\mu_t(y)q_t(y,z)=c_t(y,z)=c_t(z,y)=\mu_t(z)q_t(z,y).
\]

Every original graph edge has positive conductance, so `Q_t` is irreducible.

For the Dirichlet form,

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\langle f,Q_tf\rangle_{\mu_t}
&=\frac12\sum_{y,z}\mu_t(y)q_t(y,z)(f(z)-f(y))^2\\
&=\frac14\sum_{y,z}(\mu_t(y)+\mu_t(z))P(y,z)(f(z)-f(y))^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Because `P` is symmetric, interchanging `y,z` in the second half shows that the last expression equals

\[
\frac12\sum_{y,z}\mu_t(y)P(y,z)(f(z)-f(y))^2
=\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f.
\]

The variational definition of spectral gap now gives (4.4).

Finally, summing (4.2) in `z` gives

\[
r_t(y)=\frac12\left(1+\frac{(P\mu_t)(y)}{\mu_t(y)}\right).
\]

Since `P` is symmetric and `partial_t mu_t=mu_t(P-I)`, viewed as a column this is `(P mu_t)(y)-mu_t(y)`, proving (4.5). QED.

### Immediate geometric consequence

For any set `A` with `0<mu_t(A)<1`, the indicator test gives

\[
\boxed{
\gamma(\mu_t)
\le
\frac{Q_{\mu_t}(A,A^c)+Q_{\mu_t}(A^c,A)}
{2\mu_t(A)(1-\mu_t(A))},
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where `Q_mu(A,B)=sum_{y in A,z in B}mu(y)P(y,z)`. Thus local-Poincaré collapse is literally a bottleneck in the frozen symmetrized edge flow. Equation (3.6) is an explicit instance.

The representation (4.4) is elementary; publication novelty is **UNVERIFIED**. Its role here is to change the Problem-2.2 obstruction from a moving non-equilibrium inequality into an ordinary spectral-gap problem on a time-indexed reversible weighted graph.

# 5. Method #15 continued — retain only the root stabilizer, not full transitivity

Now assume `G` is vertex transitive and fix the starting root `x`. Let

\[
K:=\operatorname{Aut}(G)_x
\]

be the root stabilizer. Since the semigroup is automorphism-equivariant and `K` fixes `x`, the law `mu_t` is `K`-invariant. Hence the frozen conductances (4.1) and the generator `Q_t` are also `K`-invariant.

Define the stabilizer-restricted local Poincaré constant

\[
\gamma_K(\mu_t)
:=
\inf_{\substack{f\in\operatorname{Fix}(K)\\\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f>0}}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f}{\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Clearly `gamma_K(mu_t)>=gamma(mu_t)`, because the infimum is over fewer functions.

Let `Omega=V/K` be the set of root-stabilizer orbits. For an orbit `A in Omega`, define

\[
\bar\mu_t(A)=\sum_{y\in A}\mu_t(y),
\]

and for distinct orbits

\[
\bar c_t(A,B)=\sum_{y\in A,z\in B}c_t(y,z),
\qquad
\bar q_t(A,B)=\frac{\bar c_t(A,B)}{\bar\mu_t(A)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

## Claim `P03-I0111-ROOT-ORBIT-QUOTIENT-224 = PROVED`

The matrix `bar Q_t` defined by (5.2), with diagonal row sums, is an irreducible reversible generator on `Omega` with stationary law `bar mu_t`, and

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_K(\mu_t)=\operatorname{gap}(\bar Q_t).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

The symmetry of `c_t` gives

\[
\bar\mu_t(A)\bar q_t(A,B)
=\bar c_t(A,B)=\bar c_t(B,A)
=\bar\mu_t(B)\bar q_t(B,A),
\]

so the quotient chain is reversible. Connectivity of the original graph implies connectivity of its orbit quotient.

Every `K`-invariant function `f` is uniquely `f(y)=g(A)` on the orbit `A` containing `y`. Under this identification,

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f
=\operatorname{Var}_{\bar\mu_t}g,
\]

and, using (4.3),

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f
=\frac12\sum_{A,B}\bar c_t(A,B)(g(B)-g(A))^2
=-\langle g,\bar Q_tg\rangle_{\bar\mu_t}.
\]

Taking the variational infimum proves (5.3). QED.

This is the exact computational object that full transitive averaging erased. Its dimension is the **rank of the rooted permutation action**, i.e. the number of `K`-orbits, not `|V|`. On distance-transitive graphs these are precisely the distance shells, so the quotient is the familiar one-dimensional distance process.

# 6. Method #27 — swap the quantifiers in Pedrotti–Salez

Pedrotti–Salez controls every function in order to control two particular evolving density ratios. For vertex-transitive point-start walks, those two ratios are automatically `K`-invariant. This allows a strictly weaker sufficient criterion.

First record the exact directional identity. For two initial laws `alpha,beta` on a finite regular graph, put

\[
F(t)=\chi^2(\alpha P_t\|\beta P_t),
\qquad
r_t=\frac{\alpha P_t}{\beta P_t}.
\]

Whenever `F(t)>0`, direct differentiation using symmetry of `L` gives

\[
\boxed{
F'(t)=-2\,\mathbb E_{\beta P_t}\Gamma r_t.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed, writing `alpha_t=beta_t r_t`,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\sum_y\frac{\alpha_t(y)^2}{\beta_t(y)}
=\sum_y\beta_t(y)\left(2r_tLr_t-Lr_t^2\right)(y)
=-2\mathbb E_{\beta_t}\Gamma r_t.
\]

Thus if

\[
\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
:=
\frac{\mathbb E_{\beta P_t}\Gamma r_t}
{\operatorname{Var}_{\beta P_t}r_t},
\tag{6.2}
\]

then

\[
F(T)=F(0)\exp\left(-2\int_0^T\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}(s)ds\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a standard finite reversible dissipation identity / exact version of the mechanism behind Pedrotti–Salez Lemma 1; I do not claim priority for it.

Now let `mu_t=P_t(x,.)`, fix `0<epsilon<1/2`, and write

\[
t_-:=t_{mix}(1-\varepsilon),
\qquad
t_+:=t_{mix}(\varepsilon).
\]

Define the **root-stabilizer restricted mixing-window constant**

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}
:=
\gamma_K(\pi)\wedge
\inf_{t\in[t_-,t_+]}\gamma_K(\mu_t).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

## Claim `P03-I0111-ROOT-STABILIZER-LOCAL-PRODUCT-225 = PROVED`

For every finite connected vertex-transitive graph and every `0<epsilon<1/2`, rate-one continuous-time simple random walk satisfies

\[
\boxed{
 w_{mix}(\varepsilon)
\le
\frac{3}{2\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}}
\log\frac1\varepsilon.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Consequently, for any vertex-transitive graph sequence, if for one fixed `epsilon in (0,1/2)`

\[
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}\,t_{mix}(\varepsilon)\to\infty,
\tag{6.6}
\]

then the sequence exhibits cutoff.

### Proof

Set

\[
T:=\frac{3}{2\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}}\log\frac1\varepsilon.
\]

If `T >= t_+-t_-`, (6.5) is already true. Assume for contradiction `T<t_+-t_-`. Choose `h>0` so small that

\[
t_0:=t_-+h,
\qquad t_0+T<t_+.
\]

Because `d_TV(mu_{t_0},pi)<1-epsilon`, Pedrotti–Salez's interpolation lemma gives a probability law `nu` with

\[
\left\|\frac{d\nu}{d\mu_{t_0}}\right\|_\infty
\vee
\left\|\frac{d\nu}{d\pi}\right\|_\infty
\le\frac1\varepsilon.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Moreover the canonical overlap construction is obtained by normalizing the pointwise minimum of the two densities. Since `mu_{t_0}` and `pi` are `K`-invariant, `nu` is `K`-invariant. The semigroup commutes with `K`, so for every `0<=s<=T`, all four laws

\[
\nu P_s,\quad \mu_{t_0}P_s=\mu_{t_0+s},\quad \pi P_s=\pi
\]

are `K`-invariant. Therefore the two density ratios

\[
\frac{d(\nu P_s)}{d\mu_{t_0+s}},
\qquad
\frac{d(\nu P_s)}{d\pi}
\]

belong to `Fix(K)`.

Their directional Rayleigh quotients in (6.2) are thus bounded below by the **restricted** constants, not merely the full ones:

\[
\kappa_{\nu,\mu_{t_0}}(s)
\ge\gamma_K(\mu_{t_0+s})
\ge\gamma_{K,\varepsilon},
\]

because `t_0+s in [t_-,t_+]`, and

\[
\kappa_{\nu,\pi}(s)
\ge\gamma_K(\pi)
\ge\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}.
\]

The density bound (6.7) implies both initial chi-square divergences are at most `1/epsilon`. Using (6.3),

\[
\chi^2(\nu P_T\|\mu_{t_0+T})
\vee
\chi^2(\nu P_T\|\pi)
\le
\frac{e^{-2\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}T}}\varepsilon
=\varepsilon^2.
\]

By `d_TV <= (1/2)sqrt(chi^2)` and the triangle inequality,

\[
d_{TV}(\mu_{t_0+T},\pi)
\le\frac12(\varepsilon+\varepsilon)=\varepsilon.
\]

This contradicts `t_0+T<t_+=t_mix(epsilon)`. Therefore `T>=t_+-t_-`, proving (6.5). Dividing by `t_mix(epsilon)` yields the cutoff consequence (6.6). QED.
