# 4. Method #3 — try to prove the stronger converse, and locate the quantifier obstruction

The previous report proved the sufficient criterion

\[
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}\,t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)\to\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{cutoff},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `K=Aut(G)_x`, and

\[
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}
=
\gamma_K(\pi)\wedge
\inf_{t\in[t_-,t_+]}
\gamma_K(\mu_t),
\qquad
\gamma_K(\mu_t)=\operatorname{gap}(\bar Q_t),
\tag{4.2}
\]

with `bar Q_t` the frozen reversible root-orbit quotient chain.

The stronger target was the converse:

\[
\text{cutoff}
\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)\to\infty.
\tag{4.3}
\]

I attacked (4.3) both by proof and by explicit positive families. It did not close.

## 4.1 Why the obvious converse proof fails

The quantity on the right of (4.3) is a **worst-direction** quantity:

\[
\gamma_K(\mu_t)
=
\inf_{\substack{f\in\operatorname{Fix}(K)\\\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f>0}}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f}
{\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Cutoff, by contrast, describes one specific trajectory `mu_t` and its distance to equilibrium. A fast drop of that trajectory does not visibly force every `K`-invariant test function to have a large Rayleigh quotient. A bad radial direction can in principle be almost orthogonal to the actual likelihood-ratio direction followed during the transition.

The preceding P03 work already encountered the same mismatch once in the larger all-function space: the full local gap can collapse in product-condition/no-cutoff families because of irrelevant test directions. Claim 225 fixed sufficiency by restricting from *all* functions to the root-stabilizer sector, but a converse would still need to control **all functions inside that sector**.

Thus a proof of (4.3) needs a genuinely new ingredient of one of two types:

- an orbit-geometric theorem saying every low-Rayleigh `K`-invariant function necessarily couples to the point-start mixing trajectory; or
- a replacement for `gamma_K` that retains only the actual directions through which total variation dissipates.

Neither follows from transitivity alone in the current argument.

## 4.2 Positive sanity test: the hypercube does not refute the converse

For the rate-one continuous-time walk on the hypercube `Q_d`,

\[
L=\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d(T_i-I),
\]

and from the all-zero root

\[
\mu_t=\operatorname{Ber}(p_t)^{\otimes d},
\qquad
p_t=\frac{1-e^{-2t/d}}2.
\]

For this product measure, Efron–Stein gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t}f
\le p_t(1-p_t)
\sum_{i=1}^d
\mathbb E[(f(X^{i,1})-f(X^{i,0}))^2],
\]

while the original-walk Dirichlet form is

\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_t}\Gamma f
=\frac1{2d}\sum_{i=1}^d
\mathbb E[(f(X^{i,1})-f(X^{i,0}))^2].
\]

A one-coordinate test attains equality, so the full moving local gap is exactly

\[
\gamma(\mu_t)
=\frac{1}{2d\,p_t(1-p_t)}
\ge\frac2d.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since restriction to `K=S_d`-invariant functions can only raise the infimum,

\[
\gamma_K(\mu_t)\ge 2/d.
\]

The standard rate-one hypercube cutoff location is

\[
t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)=\frac d4\log d+O_\varepsilon(d),
\]

so

\[
\gamma_{K,\varepsilon}t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)
\gtrsim \tfrac12\log d\to\infty.
\]

Thus the first explicit cutoff sanity family supports (4.3); it does not prove it.

No claim ID is assigned to this calculation. It is a route test / standard-model self-check.

# 5. Method #17 — look for the same numbers elsewhere: birth-and-death, distance-regular, Hamming

The key numerical pattern in Claim 225 is “transition window is controlled by an inverse gap.” The literature already contains an exact version of this philosophy in one dimension.

## 5.1 Distance-regular graphs collapse exactly to birth-and-death

For a distance-regular graph, fixing a root `x` and writing `D_t=d(x,X_t)`, the distance process is a birth-and-death chain on `{0,...,D}` with the usual intersection-number transition probabilities. Moreover, the heat kernel from `x` is constant on each distance sphere. Consequently the total-variation distance of the full walk is exactly the total-variation distance of the distance process from its stationary sphere-size distribution:

\[
\boxed{
\|P_t(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}
=
\|\mathcal L(D_t)-\rho\|_{\rm TV}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Ding–Lubetzky–Peres then give, for any sequence of continuous-time birth-and-death chains started at an endpoint,

\[
\text{cutoff}
\iff
\lambda_{\rm rad}\,t_{\rm mix}\to\infty,
\tag{5.2}
\]

with a window bounded by order

\[
\sqrt{t_{\rm rel}^{\rm rad}\,t_{\rm mix}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the vertex-transitive + distance-regular subclass already has a complete one-dimensional characterization through its radial chain. I record this as **KNOWN-IMPLIED / folklore corollary of published birth-and-death theory**, not as a P03 theorem. Belsley 1998 and Markowsky 2013 are direct distance-regular prior art and make a novelty claim especially inappropriate.

The important transferable feature is not the inequality (5.2) itself. It is the **median-hitting-time representation/concentration** available because the quotient has a total order and nearest-neighbor motion.

## 5.2 Hamming graphs were already the obvious laboratory — and the literature got there first

I also tested Hamming graphs `H(d,q)`, since they interpolate between the hypercube and high-alphabet fixed-dimension examples while remaining vertex-transitive and distance-regular.

The prospective route was to use the exact product-coordinate law to see whether the root-orbit local gap could develop a transient bottleneck in a cutoff regime. Before promoting that as new work, the literature search recovered Akihito Hora's 1997 paper **“The Cut-Off Phenomenon for Random Walks on Hamming Graphs with Variable Growth Conditions”**, which explicitly lets both parameters grow and identifies different Gaussian/Poisson transition profiles depending on their relative scale.

Therefore a general “characterize Hamming cutoff as `d,q` vary” project would largely repay known work. I stopped that route rather than manufacturing a duplicate theorem/checker.

This is exactly the purpose of method #17: the numbers and model already existed elsewhere, and that field's technique is hitting-time/profile analysis rather than a worst-direction local gap.

# 6. Method #30 — one direction at a time: the actual TV-dissipation direction

The failure of the stronger converse suggests keeping only the canonical direction that total variation actually sees.

Let the graph be regular, so `pi` is uniform, and define the density

\[
h_t=\frac{d\mu_t}{d\pi}.
\]

Let

\[
A_t=\{y:h_t(y)>1\}.
\]

Because `mu_t` is root-stabilizer invariant, `A_t` is a union of `K`-orbits. At every time at which no coordinate crosses the level `1` (and hence for almost every `t`), the maximizing TV set is locally fixed and

\[
\begin{aligned}
d(t)
&:=\|\mu_t-\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 =\langle h_t-1,1_{A_t}\rangle_\pi,\\
d'(t)
&=\langle Lh_t,1_{A_t}\rangle_\pi
=-\mathcal E_\pi(h_t,1_{A_t}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, by reversibility,

\[
-d'(t)
=
\sum_{y\in A_t,z\notin A_t}
\pi(y)P(y,z)\,[h_t(y)-h_t(z)].
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is standard semigroup algebra; **no novelty claim is made**.

What matters is the contrast with (4.4). The TV derivative probes a single canonical `K`-invariant cut `A_t`, weighted by the actual likelihood-ratio jump. It does not probe the worst `K`-invariant function.

Define, only as diagnostic notation,

\[
\eta(t):=\frac{\mathcal E_\pi(h_t,1_{A_t})}{d(t)}
=-\frac{d}{dt}\log d(t)
\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then for the fixed-`epsilon` TV window

\[
t_-=t_{\rm mix}(1-\varepsilon),
\qquad
t_+=t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon),
\]

continuity gives the tautological integrated identity

\[
\int_{t_-}^{t_+}\eta(s)\,ds
=\log\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Hence cutoff is equivalent to this fixed amount of log-TV dissipation occurring in a window negligible compared with `t_mix`. This by itself is not a structural characterization, but it says exactly what a successful orbit-geometric theorem must control: **the moving likelihood-ratio cut or an equivalent hitting-time observable**, rather than every radial test function.

This is the most useful outcome of the run. It points directly toward the birth-and-death median-hitting-time mechanism and away from another global Poincare inequality.

## Independent derivation

Let `p = 5 mod 24`, let

\[
V=\{x\in\mathbb F_p:\chi(x)=\chi(x-1)=1\},
\]

and let the anharmonic group `G ~= S_3` act through `x -> 1-x` and `x -> x^{-1}`. For these primes the action on `V` is free: transposition fixed points are excluded because `chi(2)=-1`, and 3-cycle fixed points would solve `x^2-x+1=0`, impossible because `chi(-3)=-1`. Thus `V` is a disjoint union of free six-point orbits.

Fix a nontrivial unitary irreducible representation `rho` of `S_3` and free-orbit representatives `x_1,...,x_q`. Since `rho` is nontrivial,

\[
\sum_{g\in G}\rho(g)=0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Define

\[
F_j(x)=\sum_{g\in G}\chi(x-gx_j)\rho(g).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Using the exact quadratic-character correlation

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(x-a)\chi(x-b)
=\begin{cases}p-1,&a=b,\\-1,&a\ne b,
\end{cases}
\tag{9.3}
\]

and the fact that distinct chosen orbits never meet, expansion gives

\[
\sum_x F_i(x)^*F_j(x)=6p\,\delta_{ij}I.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Indeed, for `i != j` every character correlation is `-1` and the result vanishes by (9.1); for `i=j`, the six diagonal pairs contribute `p-1` and the off-diagonal sum is exactly the negative of the diagonal group sum, yielding `6pI`.

The Paley character is invariant under simultaneous anharmonic action on `V`. For the generators,

\[
\chi((1-x)-(1-y))=\chi(x-y)
\]

because `chi(-1)=1`, and

\[
\chi(x^{-1}-y^{-1})
=\chi((y-x)/(xy))
=\chi(x-y)
\]

because `x,y` are nonzero squares and again `chi(-1)=1`. Hence

\[
F_j(hx)=\rho(h)F_j(x)
\quad(h\in G,\ x\in V).
\tag{9.5}
\]

Let the block matrix `R^rho` have entries

\[
R^\rho_{ij}=F_j(x_i).
\]

Equation (9.4) says the full-field transform has norm `sqrt(6p)`. Restricting rows to `V` cannot increase norm, while (9.5) shows each six-point source orbit contributes six equal squared norms. Therefore

\[
\|R^\rho\|_{op}\le\sqrt p.
\tag{9.6}
\]

For the invariant adjacency operator, the multiplicity/Fourier block is

\[
A^\rho_{ij}
=\sum_{g\in G}
\mathbf 1_{\{x_i\sim gx_j\}}\rho(g).
\]

For `i != j`, all points are distinct and the constant half of the Paley edge indicator vanishes by (9.1), giving `A^rho_{ij}=R^rho_{ij}/2`. On the diagonal, the identity group element is the forbidden self-loop; because

\[
\sum_{g\ne e}\rho(g)=-I,
\]

the exact correction is

\[
A^\rho_{ii}=\frac12R^\rho_{ii}-\frac12I.
\]

Thus

\[
A^\rho=\frac12(R^\rho-I).
\tag{9.7}
\]

The global adjacency operator is real symmetric, so the corresponding multiplicity block is Hermitian. Combining (9.6)–(9.7),

\[
\lambda_{max}(A^\rho)
\le\frac{\sqrt p-1}{2}.
\]

For the naive dual `B_0=I+A`,

\[
\lambda_{max}(B_0^\rho)
\le\frac{\sqrt p+1}{2}
<\frac23\sqrt p
\]

for every nonvacuous `p=5 mod 24` (in particular `p>=29`). This independently reproduces P04's claimed sign/standard-sector bound.
