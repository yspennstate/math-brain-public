# Preserved attempts and decisions

This record separates proved results, finite observations and unsuccessful closure routes. Full FEI remains unproved.

## Continuation of the arithmetic-remainder route

The previous spectral-lattice report isolated a nonnegative remainder D in H=2R+2D and bounded the divisibility contribution R by influence. The current continuation first investigated paying D without a fixed component grid.

The successful new universal reduction is quantitative rare Boolean spectral isolation: the old nonzero Fourier coefficients become singleton magnitude bands, almost all mass lies on those bands, R tends to zero, and H and I converge to their seed values. This proves equality C_FEI=2 sup D/I. It does not bound either supremum.

A direct local induction on D was then tested. The decisive counterexample is the transitive product of N-1 complements of k-bit zero tests, N=2^k. All its mask valuations vanish, but every one-child restriction creates a positive-mass even mask. Consequently every coordinate has an unbounded D decrement/influence ratio. The proof extends to every nontrivial adaptive tree of a fixed maximum depth, not merely to a fixed coordinate order.

A correction D+lambda R with fixed lambda<1 retains this obstruction. At lambda=1 it becomes exactly H/2. Thus this linear correction does not create a new independently payable potential.

## Spectrum class shortcuts

The assertion that the number of magnitude bands controls the component lattice denominator failed: the archived nine-variable cubic ANF has two magnitudes and quarter-grid components. It is an exact finite witness, not a proof of unbounded denominators for two bands.

The stronger hope of constant-four FEI for all power-of-two magnitudes also fails, independently of lattice questions. The explicit balanced four-input gate has H=3 and I=3/2. Its independent self-composition retains power-of-two amplitudes and gives ratios approaching six. This uses standard balanced composition and is a calibration, not a new general composition theorem or a best-known numerical FEI record.

The recursive signed-mask polynomials give actual physical-input certificates for a lower bound on D of the depth-five gate. Observed valuations bound a minimum from above. Unobserved bands use the dimension as a safe bound; they are never assigned an unjustified small valuation. The deterministic generator records the full input bit strings. The final checker reconstructs them, so the finite claim does not depend on trusting a search log.

Another possible resource, the entropy of the magnitude label, is insufficient alone: for 2^j-1 tensor copies of the earlier five-variable two-band example, D=2^j-1 but magnitude-label entropy is at most j. This does not obstruct paying D by the actual influence, which grows linearly on that family.

## Exploratory scope and reconstruction

The unfinished attempt used finite cubic-phase sampling to locate the nine-variable witness and a finite balanced four-variable gate scan to choose a useful composition example. Those observations motivated the explicit constructions but are not invoked as all-dimensional proofs. A separate NAND-remainder probe did not provide a useful general upper bound and is not a theorem.

Some temporary workspace files from the unfinished attempt were not available in the resumed runtime. This package reconstructs the substantive derivations, the explicit finite witnesses and an executable checker, rather than claiming byte-for-byte recovery of every temporary exploratory artifact. All code newly produced for this package is included. Earlier finalized packages are already in the repository or the conversation archives and are not silently replaced.

## Remaining obligation

A dimension-independent finite upper bound for D/I remains missing. The exact optimal-constant reduction, the local obstruction, and the finite lower-bound benchmarks do not settle that bound. Future work must retain the distinction between a global inequality and a recursively affordable local decrement, and between finite examples and uniform theorems.
