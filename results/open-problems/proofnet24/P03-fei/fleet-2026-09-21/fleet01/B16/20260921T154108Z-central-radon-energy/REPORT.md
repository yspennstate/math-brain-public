## 2. `B16-QFLIP-ENERGY-DISTANCE` — PROVED

For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\) on \(W_i\), define the q-flip energy distance
\[
\mathfrak D_i(\mu,\nu)
:=
2\,\mathbb E_{\substack{X\sim\mu\\Y\sim\nu}}p_i(X+Y)
-\mathbb E_{X,X'\sim\mu}p_i(X+X')
-\mathbb E_{Y,Y'\sim\nu}p_i(Y+Y').
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1

For every Boolean cut \(\eta_i\) and every probability measures \(\mu,\nu\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_i(\mu,\nu)
=
2\sum_{U\ne0}
a_i(U)\bigl(\widehat\mu(U)-\widehat\nu(U)\bigr)^2
\ge0.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Hence \(p_i\) is a conditionally negative-type translation-invariant metric kernel, and (2.2) is its exact Hilbertian energy distance.

### Proof

From (1.1),
\[
\mathbb E_{X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}p_i(X+Y)
=
2E_i-2\sum_{U\ne0}a_i(U)\widehat\mu(U)\widehat\nu(U).
\]
Insert this once for the cross term and once for each self term in (2.1). Constants cancel and the coefficient of \(a_i(U)\) becomes
\[
-4\widehat\mu(U)\widehat\nu(U)
+2\widehat\mu(U)^2+2\widehat\nu(U)^2
=
2(\widehat\mu(U)-\widehat\nu(U))^2.
\]
This proves (2.2). \(\square\)

This is stronger than merely saying that the nonconstant coefficients of \(p_i\) have one sign: it identifies the exact positive quadratic form generated by every signed prefix/suffix comparison.

## 3. Central Bernoulli prefixes select exactly odd Fourier layers

For \(0\le t\le1/2\), put
\[
\mu_t=\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{\otimes m},
\qquad
\nu_t=\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(\frac{1+t}{2}\right)^{\otimes m}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For a Walsh label \(U\),
\[
\widehat\mu_t(U)=t^{|U|},
\qquad
\widehat\nu_t(U)=(-t)^{|U|}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the two-sided source antisymmetry
\[
\delta_i(t):=\mathbb E_{\mu_t}p_i-\mathbb E_{\nu_t}p_i.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact odd-channel identities

For every \(t\in[0,1/2]\),
\[
\boxed{
\delta_i(t)
=
-4\sum_{\substack{U\ne0\\ |U|\ {\rm odd}}}
a_i(U)t^{|U|}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D_i(\mu_t,\nu_t)
=
8\sum_{\substack{U\ne0\\ |U|\ {\rm odd}}}
a_i(U)t^{2|U|}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\delta_i(t)^2
\le
2E_i\,\mathfrak D_i(\mu_t,\nu_t).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

Equations (3.4) and (3.5) follow immediately from (1.1), (2.2), and (3.2): even Walsh layers cancel and odd layers double.

For (3.6), weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\left(\sum_{|U|\ {\rm odd}}a_i(U)t^{|U|}\right)^2
\le
\left(\sum_{U\ne0}a_i(U)\right)
\left(\sum_{|U|\ {\rm odd}}a_i(U)t^{2|U|}\right).

Multiply by \(16\), then use (3.5). \(\square\)

The central Ward prefix/suffix symmetry therefore selects **exactly the odd physical-cut spectrum**. No triangle inequality across q-flip relations is needed.

## 4. `B16-CENTRAL-RADON-SQUARE-PAYMENT` — PROVED, SHARP

Define the source central-band square
\[
\mathcal S_i
:=
\int_0^{1/2}
t\left(\frac12-t\right)\delta_i(t)^2\,dt.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1

For every Boolean cut,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_i
&=

\sum_{\substack{U,V\ne0\\ |U|,|V|\ {\rm odd}}}
a_i(U)a_i(V)
\frac{2^{-(|U|+|V|+3)}}
{(|U|+|V|+2)(|U|+|V|+3)}
\\[1mm]
&\le
\frac1{40}
\left[
\sum_{\substack{U\ne0\\|U|\ {\rm odd}}}
2^{-(|U|-1)}a_i(U)
\right]^2
\\[1mm]
&\le
\frac{E_i^2}{40}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The final constant \(1/40\) is sharp for the class of Boolean cuts: equality holds for the one-bit affine cut
\[
\eta(x)=\frac{1-\chi_{\{1\}}(x)}2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Expand (3.4) and use, for \(r\ge2\),
\[
\int_0^{1/2}
t^{r+1}\left(\frac12-t\right)\,dt
=
\frac{2^{-(r+3)}}{(r+2)(r+3)}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
This gives the equality in (4.2).

Since \(|U|,|V|\ge1\),
\[
(|U|+|V|+2)(|U|+|V|+3)\ge20.
\]
Therefore the coefficient multiplying \(a_i(U)a_i(V)\) is at most
\[
\frac1{40}\,2^{-(|U|-1)}2^{-(|V|-1)},

which proves the first inequality. Dropping the damping factors proves the second.

For (4.3), the only nonconstant Fourier energy is
\(a(\{1\})=1/4=E\). Then
\(\delta(t)=-t\), so
\[

=
\int_0^{1/2}t^3\left(\frac12-t\right)dt

\frac{(1/4)^2}{40}.

Thus \(1/40\) cannot be improved for arbitrary Boolean cuts. \(\square\)

M t^{|U|}

(|U|\ {\rm odd},\ 0\le t\le1/2).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Define
\[
D_i^K(t):=\mathbb E_w K_t(w)p_i(w).
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Theorem 6.1

Under (6.1)--(6.2),
\[
\boxed{
\int_0^{1/2}
t\left(\frac12-t\right)
D_i^K(t)^2\,dt
\le
\frac{M^2}{160}E_i^2.

\tag{6.4}

\int_0^{1/2}
t\left(\frac12-t\right)
D_i^K(t)^2\,dt
\le
\frac{M^2}{1080}J(f).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Proof

By Parseval and (1.2),
\[
D_i^K(t)
=
-2\sum_{U\ne0}a_i(U)\widehat K_t(U).

Hence
\[

\le
4M^2E_i
\sum_{|U|\ {\rm odd}}a_i(U)t^{2|U|}

\frac{M^2}{2}E_i\mathfrak D_i(\mu_t,\nu_t).

\[

Equations (6.6)--(6.7) give (6.4), and (5.3) gives (6.5). \(\square\)
