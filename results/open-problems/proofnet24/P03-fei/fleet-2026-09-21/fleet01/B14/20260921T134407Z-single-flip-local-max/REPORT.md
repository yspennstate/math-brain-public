\[
\rho_0(f):=
\min_{i:\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)>0}

## 3. Stable exact Walsh evaluator

The computation avoids recomputing sixteen child Walsh transforms. If `W_S` is the unnormalised integer Walsh transform of a `2^n`-entry Boolean truth table and `T` does not contain coordinate `i`, put

\[
u=W_T,\qquad v=W_{T\cup\{i\}}.
\]

The restriction Fourier coefficients are exactly

\[
\widehat f_+(T)=\frac{u+v}{2^n},
\qquad
\widehat f_-(T)=\frac{u-v}{2^n}.
\]

After cancellation of the common entropy normalisation constants,

\[
2^{2n}\Delta_iH
=
\sum_T
\left[
\frac{(u+v)^2}{2}\log_2 (u+v)^2
+
\frac{(u-v)^2}{2}\log_2 (u-v)^2
-u^2\log_2u^2-v^2\log_2v^2
\right],
\]

where zero terms are omitted, while

\[
2^{2n}\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)=\sum_Tv^2.
\]

Thus the desired ratio is a linear combination of logarithms of positive integers divided by an exact positive integer.

Each `log_2 m` is enclosed without transcendental floating-point assumptions. Write

\[
m=2^e y,\qquad 1\le y<2,\qquad
z=\frac{y-1}{y+1}\in[0,1/3].
\]

Then

\[
\ln y
=
2\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+R_M,
\qquad
0\le R_M\le
\frac{2z^{2M+1}}{(2M+1)(1-z^2)}.
\]

The same positive series at `z=1/3` encloses `ln 2`. All endpoints are exact Python `Fraction`s. The script uses the sign of every linear coefficient when propagating lower and upper bounds and compares the resulting exact fractions before emitting decimal renderings. Float64 is used only to select a candidate coordinate; it does not certify any strict inequality.
