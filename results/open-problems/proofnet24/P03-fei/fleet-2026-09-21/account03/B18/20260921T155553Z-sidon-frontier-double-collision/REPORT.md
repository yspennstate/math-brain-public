## 2. PROVED: sharp Sidon complement threshold with the full stability reserve retained

### Theorem 2.1 — `B18-SIDON-FRONTIER-DICHOTOMY`

Assume the Fourier support of `g` projected onto `X` is Sidon. Then
\[
\boxed{

}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
{I_Y\over I}
\ge {b\over b+2}+{L_{X\mid Y}\over (b+2)I}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
In particular every Sidon projection satisfies
\[
\boxed{
{I_Y\over I}\ge \alpha(r):={b(r)\over b(r)+2}.

\tag{2.3}

#### Proof

The inherited Sidon address theorem gives `B_X<=2I_Y`. The inherited sharp block ledger gives `B_X=bI_X+L_{X|Y}`. Hence
\[
bI_X+L_{X\mid Y}\le2I_Y.

This strictly improves the preceding B18 bound `rI_X<=4I_Y`, which used only the coarser block floor `B_X>=(r/2)I_X`. The gain is structural rather than a new FEI constant.

## 4. PROVED: above an explicit total-influence threshold, two complementary projections must both be non-Sidon

Put

### Theorem 4.1 — `B18-DOUBLE-NONSIDON-BLOCK`

If
\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.1}
\]
then there is a partition `[n]=X\sqcup Y` such that
\[
\boxed{
\gamma I<I_X<\alpha I,
\qquad
\gamma I<I_Y<\alpha I,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
and **both** projected Fourier supports, onto `X` and onto `Y`, are non-Sidon.

#### Proof

By Lemma 3.1 every coordinate weight satisfies `p_i<=tau`. Order the relevant coordinates arbitrarily and add them to `X` until the running influence first becomes strictly larger than `gamma I`. Immediately before the last addition the sum is at most `gamma I`; therefore
\[
I_X\le\gamma I+\tau.
\]
Condition (4.1) is exactly
\[
\tau<(\alpha-\gamma)I,
\]
so
\[
\gamma I<I_X<\alpha I.
\]
Consequently `I_Y=I-I_X` also lies strictly between `gamma I` and `alpha I`.

If the support projected onto `X` were Sidon, Theorem 2.1 would require `I_Y>=alpha I`, contradiction. If the support projected onto `Y` were Sidon, it would require `I_X>=alpha I`, contradiction. Hence both projections are non-Sidon. QED.
