## 2. PROVED: exact factorial split into internal and macro pieces

For every realized terminal set `S`, the multinomial identity gives

\[
\log_2(K!)
=
\sum_{C\in\mathcal C}\log_2(K_C!)
+
\log_2\frac{K!}{\prod_C K_C!}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Taking expectation, define

\[
Q_{\rm int}:=\sum_C \mathbb E\log_2(K_C!),
\qquad
Q_{\rm mac}:=\mathbb E\log_2\frac{K!}{\prod_C K_C!}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{Q=Q_{\rm int}+Q_{\rm mac}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Both terms are nonnegative. Combinatorially, `Q_int` counts permutations within the component blocks and `Q_mac` counts interleavings of those component blocks. This distinction is exact, sample by sample, so it is suitable for nonreuse accounting.

Singleton blocks contribute zero to `Q_int`.

## 3. PROVED: the peer's robust-row payment localizes to `K_C!`

Fix one fine Palm-robust reciprocal component `C` of size `r>=2`. The peer trigger-locking hypothesis gives, for every `i in C`,

\[
F_i=C\setminus\{i\},\qquad m:=|F_i|=r-1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\operatorname{Law}(S\setminus\{i\}\mid i\in S),
U_{F_i}
\right)\le\epsilon_i,
\qquad
\epsilon_i\le\frac1{16m}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Instead of allocating the *global* factorial `log(K!)` to row `i`, allocate only the component factorial:

\[
Q_{C,i}:=
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{1_{\{i\in S\}}}{K_C}\log_2(K_C!)
\right],
\tag{3.3}
\]

with the integrand set to zero when `K_C=0`. For every realized `S`,

\[
\sum_{i\in C}\frac{1_{\{i\in S\}}}{K_C}=1
\quad\text{when }K_C>0,
\]

so exactly

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i\in C}Q_{C,i}=\mathbb E\log_2(K_C!).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Condition on `i in S` and put `B=S\setminus\{i\}`. Then

\[
K_C=1+|B\cap F_i|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Under the ideal law `U_{F_i}`, this is `1+X` with `X~Bin(m,1/2)`. Therefore the proof of the peer's Lemma 3.1 applies verbatim to the **local** count. For completeness, with

\[
g(k)=\frac{\log_2(k!)}k,
\]

the integral bound on `ln(k!)` yields

\[
g(k)\ge \log_2 k-\log_2 e.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Also

\[
\mathbb E\frac1{1+X}=\frac{2-2^{-m}}{m+1}
\tag{3.7}
\]

and `ln z <= z-1` imply

\[
\mathbb E\log_2(1+X)
\ge \log_2(m+1)-\log_2 e.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Hence under `U_{F_i}`,

\[
\mathbb E g(1+X)
\ge \log_2(m+1)-2\log_2 e.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For the TV transfer use the bounded truncation

\[
h(B)=\min\{g(1+|B\cap F_i|),\log_2(m+1)\}.
\]

It agrees with `g(K_C)` on the ideal support and lies in `[0,log_2(m+1)]` everywhere. Thus

\[
\frac{Q_{C,i}}{p_i}
\ge (1-\epsilon_i)\log_2(m+1)-2\log_2 e.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Since `m+1=r` and `epsilon_i log_2(m+1)<=1/16`, this gives

\[
\boxed{
p_i\log_2 r-Q_{C,i}\le c_0 p_i.}
\tag{3.11}
\]

This is the key new point: the robust row never needed the factorial permutations of labels in *other* components.

## 4. PROVED: internal component entropy consumes only `Q_int`

## 5. PROVED: exact ordered-flag decomposition at the macro interface

Let `M` denote the component containing the inserted coordinate `L`. Under the Palm edge law,

\[

\[
I_2(L;P)=I_2(M;P)+I_2(L;P\mid M).

Therefore

\[
I H(\rho)-D_{\rm seq}

The preceding B12 report used the Boolean selector-of-parities family

\[

\tag{6.1}
\]

\[
A,\quad B,\quad A\cup\{z\},\quad B\cup\{z\},
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now partition the coordinates into the three macro blocks

\[

\tag{6.3}
\]

This partition is used here only to stress-test the new **macro remainder**; the parity blocks are not being asserted to satisfy the peer's fine-uniform Palm hypothesis.

For the four terminal sets in (6.2),

\[
Q_{\rm mac}=\frac12\log_2(m+1).
\tag{6.4}
\]

The conditional entropy of the next macro label can also be computed exactly. At an empty prefix the macro probabilities are

\[
\left(
\frac{2m+1}{4(m+1)},
\frac{2m+1}{4(m+1)},
\frac1{2(m+1)}
\right).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The singleton prefix `{z}` contributes one bit with edge mass `1/[2I(m+1)]`. For a nonempty prefix of `t` labels inside one parity block, with `1<=t<=m-1` and with `z` unseen, the only macro uncertainty is whether the next label stays in the same block or is `z`; the conditional `z` probability is exactly

\[
\frac1{2m+2-t}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Summing all prefixes yields

\[
\begin{aligned}

={}&H_3\!\left(

\frac{2m+1}{4(m+1)},
\frac1{2(m+1)}

+\frac1{2(m+1)}\\

\frac{2m+2-t}{2(m+1)}
 h_2\!\left(\frac1{2m+2-t}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Put `r=2m+2-t`. Since

\[
r h_2(1/r)=\log_2 r+\log_2 e+O(1/r),
\tag{6.8}
\]

a Riemann sum (or Stirling on the sum of `log r`) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{2(m+1)}\sum_{r=m+3}^{2m+1}r h_2(1/r)
&=\frac12\log_2 m+1+o(1),\\
H_3(\text{the probabilities in (6.5)})&=1+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}

Indeed `int_1^2 log_2 x dx=2-log_2 e`, so the `log_2 e` term in (6.8) produces exactly the cancellation in (6.9). Consequently

\[
I H_2(M\mid P)=\frac12\log_2 m+2+o(1),

and with (6.4),

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm mac}=I H_2(M\mid P)-Q_{\rm mac}\longrightarrow 2\ \text{bits}.

\tag{6.10}

Thus the same Boolean family that made the previous stop-conditioned tensorization debt superlinear does **not** refute the localized macro code: its new macro debt is bounded and converges to two bits.
