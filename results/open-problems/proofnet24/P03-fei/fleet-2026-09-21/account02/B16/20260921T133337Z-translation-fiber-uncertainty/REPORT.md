## 3. Exact fiber Fourier channel

For fixed output label `s`, set
\[
\mathbf t=(t_2,\ldots,t_m)\in G^{m-1},
\]
\[
t_1=s\oplus t_2\oplus\cdots\oplus t_m,
\]
and
\[
c_s(\mathbf t)
=\alpha_1(t_1)\prod_{j=2}^m\alpha_j(t_j).
\]
Then
\[
q_s=\sum_{\mathbf t}c_s(\mathbf t)^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Translation multiplies the `j`th Fourier coefficient by the character `\chi_{t_j}(z_j)`, so
\[
\widehat h_Z(s)
=
\sum_{\mathbf t}
c_s(\mathbf t)
\prod_{j=2}^m\chi_{t_j}(z_j).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Hence
\[
p_Z(s)
=
\left|
\sum_{\mathbf t}
c_s(\mathbf t)
\chi_{\mathbf t}(Z)
\right|^2,
\tag{3.3}
\]
where `\chi_{\mathbf t}(Z)=\prod_{j=2}^m\chi_{t_j}(z_j)`.

Character orthogonality on `G^{m-1}` immediately yields the exact mixture identity
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_Z\,p_Z(s)=q_s.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now condition on an `s` with `q_s>0` and normalize
\[
\psi_s(\mathbf t)=\frac{c_s(\mathbf t)}{\sqrt{q_s}},
\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf t}|\psi_s(\mathbf t)|^2=1.
\]
The conditional incoherent multiplicity law is
\[
\boxed{
\Pr((T_2,\ldots,T_m)=\mathbf t\mid Q=s)
=
|\psi_s(\mathbf t)|^2.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Let `\mathcal F` be the **unitary** Walsh transform on `G^{m-1}`:
\[
(\mathcal F\psi_s)(Z)
=
N^{-(m-1)/2}
\sum_{\mathbf t}\psi_s(\mathbf t)\chi_{\mathbf t}(Z).
\]
Bayes' rule and (3.3) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(Z=z\mid S=s)
&=
\frac{N^{-(m-1)}p_z(s)}{q_s}\\
&=
|(\mathcal F\psi_s)(z)|^2,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.6}
\]
where the joint experiment is: first sample uniform `Z`, then sample spectral output `S` from the genuine law `p_Z`.

Thus on every exact XOR fiber:

* the classical multiplicity posterior is `|\psi_s|^2`;
* the translation posterior is `|\mathcal F\psi_s|^2`.

They are not analogous distributions; they are literally a Walsh-Fourier conjugate pair.

## 4. The uncertainty theorem: coherent information refund cannot exceed multiplicity

Define the translation information
\[
\mathcal J_m:=I(Z;S).
\tag{4.1}
\]
This is exactly the Jensen/KL refund of the translated coherent laws:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_m
=
H(q)-\mathbb E_Z H(p_Z)
=
\mathbb E_Z D_{\rm KL,2}(p_Z\|q).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
The final equality is the standard mixture identity because `q=\mathbb E_Zp_Z`.

### Theorem 4.1 — XOR-fiber uncertainty budget

For every finite collection of Boolean phases on the uniform cube,
\[
\boxed{0\le \mathcal J_m\le \mathcal M_m.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_ZH(h_Z)
=
\sum_{j=1}^m H(f_j)
-\mathcal M_m-\mathcal J_m,
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M_m
\le
\sum_jH(f_j)-\mathbb E_ZH(h_Z)
\le
2\mathcal M_m.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

For each `s` with `q_s>0`, equations (3.5)--(3.6) identify two probability laws arising from a unit vector and its unitary Walsh transform in dimension
\[
d=N^{m-1}=2^{(m-1)n}.
\]
Every Walsh matrix entry has modulus `d^{-1/2}`. The Maassen--Uffink entropic uncertainty relation therefore gives, in bits,
\[
H(|\psi_s|^2)
+
H(|\mathcal F\psi_s|^2)
\ge \log_2d=(m-1)n.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Average (4.6) with weights `q_s`. The first term becomes
\[
H(T_2,\ldots,T_m\mid Q)=\mathcal M_m.
\]
The second becomes `H(Z|S)`. Since `Z` is uniform on `G^{m-1}`,
\[
H(Z)=(m-1)n.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathcal M_m+H(Z|S)\ge H(Z),
\]
or
\[
I(Z;S)\le\mathcal M_m.
\]
Nonnegativity of mutual information gives the other side of (4.3).

Also
\[
H(q)
=
H(T_1,\ldots,T_m)-\mathcal M_m
=
\sum_jH(f_j)-\mathcal M_m
\]
because the independent spectral samples have additive entropy. Combine this with (4.2) to obtain (4.4); (4.5) follows from (4.3). ∎

### Why this is a signed-transport statement

The quantity `J_m` is generated by the actual signed Fourier amplitudes inside each fiber through (3.3). Replacing the signed coherent sum by absolute values changes the posterior `Z|S` and destroys (3.6). The theorem therefore does not license a positive coupling replacement; it quantifies the maximum refund available from the genuine signed interference.

## 5. Exact original-coordinate influence average

For physical coordinate `i`, put
\[
p_{j,i}=\operatorname{Inf}_i(f_j).
\]
The multiplicative derivative sign is
\[
D_{j,i}(x)=f_j(x)f_j(x\oplus e_i)\in\{\pm1\},
\qquad
\mathbb E_xD_{j,i}(x)=1-2p_{j,i}.
\]

The map
\[
(x,z_2,\ldots,z_m)
\longmapsto
(x,x+z_2,\ldots,x+z_m)
\]
is a bijection of `G^m`. Therefore under the joint uniform average the derivative signs of the translated factors are evaluated at independent uniform cube points. Since the product flips exactly when an odd number of factor derivatives equal `-1`,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Z\operatorname{Inf}_i(h_Z)
=
r_i
:=
\frac{1-\prod_{j=1}^m(1-2p_{j,i})}{2}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is also exactly
\[
\Pr(i\in Q),
\]
the `i`th marginal of the incoherent XOR spectral law.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Z I(h_Z)=I(q)=\sum_i r_i.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define the independent parity-overlap debit
\[
\Gamma_m
:=
\sum_i\left(\sum_j p_{j,i}-r_i\right)\ge0.
\tag{5.3}
\]
The inequality is simply `1_{\{\text{odd number of flips}\}}\le` number of flips, averaged. Crucially, the exact expansion retains its alternating signs:
\[
\boxed{

=

\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq[m]\\|A|\ge2}}
(-1)^{|A|}

\prod_{j\in A}p_{j,i}.

\tag{5.4}
\]
For `m=2`, `\Gamma_2=2\sum_i p_{1,i}p_{2,i}`.

## 7. Sharp genuine-Boolean obstruction: identical unsigned spectra, `J/M=1` versus `0`

The inequality `\mathcal J_m\le\mathcal M_m` is sharp, and more importantly there is **no positive phase-blind lower bound** on `\mathcal J_2` even when `\mathcal M_2` is maximal.

Consider `n=4`. For a quadratic form `q`, write
\[
f_q(x)=(-1)^{q(x)}.
\]
Take
\[
q_0=x_1x_2+x_3x_4,
\tag{7.1}
\]
\[
q_1=x_1x_2+x_1x_4+x_2x_3.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Their polar bilinear matrices, and that of `q_0+q_1`, all have rank four over `\mathbb F_2`. Explicitly the edge sets are

* `B_0`: `12,34`;
* `B_1`: `12,14,23`;
* `B_0+B_1`: `14,23,34`.

Hence `q_0`, `q_1`, and `q_0+q_1` are nondegenerate quadratics.

For completeness, if `q` has nondegenerate polar form `B_q`, then its Walsh sum
\[
W_q(s)=\sum_x(-1)^{q(x)+s\cdot x}
\]
satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_q(s)^2
&=
\sum_{x,h}
(-1)^{q(x)+q(x+h)+s\cdot h}\\
&=
\sum_h(-1)^{q(h)+s\cdot h}
\sum_x(-1)^{B_q(x,h)}\\
&=N.
\end{aligned}
\]
The inner sum vanishes for every nonzero `h` by nondegeneracy. Therefore every Fourier magnitude is `N^{-1/2}`: `f_q` is bent and its squared spectral law is uniform.

Thus `f_{q_0}` and `f_{q_1}` have exactly the same unsigned spectral law:
\[
P_0=P_1=\operatorname{Unif}(G).
\]
For either pair below the incoherent XOR law is also uniform, so
\[
H(P_0)=H(P_1)=n,\qquad
\mathcal M_2=n,
\qquad
\operatorname{Inf}_i=1/2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Endpoint A: same quadratic phase

Let `a=b=f_{q_0}`. For every translation `z`,
\[
q_0(x)+q_0(x+z)
\]
is affine in `x`, because the quadratic part cancels. Hence
\[
a(x)a(x+z)
\]
is a character up to sign and has spectral entropy zero. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_2=n=\mathcal M_2.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Endpoint B: nondegenerate quadratic difference

Let `a=f_{q_0}`, `b=f_{q_1}`. Translation of `q_1` changes only its affine part; every product
\[
a(x)b(x+z)
\]
has quadratic part `q_0+q_1`, which is nondegenerate. Therefore every translated product remains bent with uniform squared spectrum and entropy `n`. Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal J_2=0,\qquad \mathcal M_2=n.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

These pairs have the same:

* two unsigned factor spectral laws;
* XOR law `q`;
* multiplicity `\mathcal M_2=n`;
* all coordinate influence marginals `1/2`;
* overlap quantity `\Omega=n/4`.

Yet the genuine coherent translation refund ranges from its **maximum possible value** `n` to **zero**, solely because of the signed quadratic phase relation.

Direct sums of the displayed four-dimensional block give the same contrast in every dimension divisible by four.

### Corollary 7.1 — phase-blind lower payment is impossible

There is no universal strictly positive lower bound of the form
\[
\mathcal J_2
\ge
F(P_a,P_b)
\]
at the uniform bent spectral pair, nor any such bound based only on `q`, `\mathcal M_2`, influence marginals, or any other statistic determined by the unsigned factor spectral laws.

Any lower payment for the coherent KL refund must inspect signed phase data (equivalently, some plaquette/curl/frustration information). This is a genuine Boolean obstruction, not an abstract probability-law counterexample.
