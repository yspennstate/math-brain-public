## 2. An explicit weight-four basis of the whole relation code

Fix an anchor `a in V`. For every nonzero displacement `v` of Hamming weight at least two choose one pivot

\[
 i(v)\in\operatorname{supp}(v).
\]

Define the four-set

\[
 B_v(a)=
 \{a,\ a+e_{i(v)},\ a+v,\ a+v+e_{i(v)}\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

It is the union of two parallel physical `i(v)`-edges.

### Theorem 2.1 — `B16-QFLIP-BASIS`

For every anchor and every allowed pivot choice,

\[
 \mathcal B(a,i)=\{B_v(a): |v|\ge2\}
\]

is a basis of `C_0`. In particular every relation has a **unique** expansion

\[
 A=\bigtriangleup_{|v|\ge2} a_v(A)B_v(a),
 \qquad a_v(A)\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Each `B_v(a)` has cardinality four and xor zero, so it lies in `C_0`. There are

\[
 N-1-n=N-n-1=\dim C_0
\]

such sets, since `C_0` is the kernel of the `n+1` independent linear constraints “even cardinality” and “zero xor”.

It remains to prove independence. Translation is a coordinate permutation, so take `a=0`. Suppose a nonempty `F_2`-sum of the `B_v(0)` is zero and choose an index `v` with nonzero coefficient and maximal Hamming weight. The vertex `v` occurs in `B_v(0)`. If it occurs in another `B_u(0)`, then, because `|v|>=2`, it is neither `0` nor the singleton `e_{i(u)}`. Hence it must equal either `u` or `u+e_{i(u)}`. The first forces `u=v`; the second forces

\[
 |u|=|v|+1
\]

because `i(u)` belongs to the support of `u`. By maximality no such nonzero coefficient `u` exists. Thus the coordinate `v` occurs an odd number of times in the alleged zero sum, contradiction. Hence the family is independent, and its cardinality equals the dimension of `C_0`. `□`

This theorem is intentionally stronger than “`C_0` is generated by weight-four words”: it gives a concrete triangular basis for **every** anchor and every local pivot selection.

## 3. Relation syndrome and a local frustration witness

For a Boolean phase `f:V->{+1,-1}` write

\[
 f_A=\prod_{x\in A}f(x),\qquad
 z_v(f):=f_{B_v(a)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

By (2.2),

\[
 \boxed{f_A=\prod_{|v|\ge2} z_v(f)^{a_v(A)}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Therefore, if `f_A=-1`, the integer

\[
 q_A=q_A(f;\mathcal B)
 :=\#\{v:a_v(A)=1,\ z_v(f)=-1\}
\tag{3.3}
\]

is positive and odd.

Now `B_v(a)` consists of the two parallel edges

\[
 e_v^0=\{a,a+e_{i(v)}\},\qquad
 e_v^1=\{a+v,a+v+e_{i(v)}\}.
\]

If `tau_e=f(x)f(y)` for `e={x,y}`, then

\[
 z_v=\tau_{e_v^0}\tau_{e_v^1}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence `z_v=-1` iff **exactly one** of the two parallel edges is a physical disagreement edge. Denote that unique edge by

\[
 \epsilon_v(f)\in\mathcal D_f.
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Corollary 3.1 — frustrated high-weight relations have frustrated weight-four certificates

Every `A in C_0` with `f_A=-1` has an odd nonempty set of basis q-flips `B_v(a)` with `a_v(A)=1` and `f_{B_v(a)}=-1`. Each such q-flip canonically names one genuine disagreement edge `epsilon_v(f)`.

This does **not** assert that the q-flip is in the retained R32 relation set or outside `D_d`; it is a payment witness for the original relation `A`, whose exact coefficient and terminal status are unchanged.

## 4. Exact signed transport for arbitrary relation weights

The preceding corollary can be turned into an exact no-double-spending allocation.

Let `R subseteq C_0` be any finite relation family and let `c_A in R` be arbitrary real weights, with either sign. Put

\[
 S_c(f)=\sum_{\substack{A\in R\\f_A=-1}}c_A.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a basis generator define

\[
 L_v(f)
 =
 \mathbf 1_{\{z_v=-1\}}
 \sum_{\substack{A\in R\\f_A=-1\\a_v(A)=1}}
 \frac{c_A}{q_A}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then define the physical edge load

\[
 \Lambda_e(f)
 =
 \sum_{\substack{v:z_v=-1\\\epsilon_v(f)=e}}L_v(f),
 \qquad e\in\mathcal D_f.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — `B16-QFLIP-SIGNED-DISINTEGRATION`

For arbitrary signed `c_A`,

\[
 \boxed{
 S_c(f)=\sum_{v}L_v(f)=\sum_{e\in\mathcal D_f}\Lambda_e(f).
 }
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Fix a frustrated relation `A`. Its contribution to the middle sum is

\[
 c_A
 \sum_{\substack{v:a_v(A)=1\\z_v=-1}}\frac1{q_A}
 =c_A.
\]

No other relation is mixed into this identity. Summing over `A` proves the first equality. Every `v` with `z_v=-1` has exactly one edge `epsilon_v(f)`, so regrouping the generator loads by that edge proves the second. `□`

Crucially:

* a `2 mod 4` R32 coefficient stays negative;
* a relation of any weight keeps its exact coefficient and multiplicity;
* no absolute value is taken;
* each relation contributes net mass exactly one before multiplication by `c_A`;
* the same physical edge may receive several shares, but those shares sum to the original relation charge rather than duplicating it.

Applying Theorem 4.1 to

\[
 R=C_0\setminus D_d,\qquad
 c_A=(-1)^{|A|/2}I_{|A|,N}
\]

gives the exact alternative R32 edge disintegration

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{\rm nat}(f)-R_d(f)
 =\frac8N\sum_{e\in\mathcal D_f}\Lambda_e(f).
 }
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is complementary to the minimum-T-join disintegration: no A-join is chosen, so the long-T-join obstruction does not enter.

## 5. The transport is the Aumann–Shapley integral of the R32 polynomial

The `1/q_A` factor is not an ad hoc averaging constant.

Using the basis coordinates, define the multilinear signed relation polynomial

\[
 P_c(z)=
 \sum_{A\in R}
 c_A\frac{1-\prod_v z_v^{a_v(A)}}2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the actual syndrome `z(f)`, equation (3.2) gives

\[
 P_c(z(f))=S_c(f),
 \qquad P_c(\mathbf1)=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let `M={v:z_v(f)=-1}` and use the diagonal path from the affine syndrome to the actual syndrome,

\[
 z_v(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-2t,&v\in M,\\
 1,&v\notin M,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad 0\le t\le1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — `B16-AUMANN-SHAPLEY-R32`

For every generator,

\[
 \boxed{
 L_v(f)
 =
 -2\mathbf1_{\{v\in M\}}
 \int_0^1
 \partial_vP_c(z(t))\,dt.
 }
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently (4.4) is exactly the fundamental theorem of calculus

\[
 P_c(z(f))-P_c(\mathbf1)
 =
 \int_0^1\frac d{dt}P_c(z(t))\,dt.
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Consider one relation monomial and a negative active generator `v`. If that monomial has `q` negative active basis coordinates, its contribution to `-2 partial_v P_c(z(t))` is

\[
 c_A(1-2t)^{q-1}.
\]

The exact integral is

\[
 \int_0^1(1-2t)^{q-1}dt
 =
 \begin{cases}
 1/q,&q\ {\rm odd},\\
 0,&q\ {\rm even}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Odd `q` is exactly `f_A=-1`; even `q` is exactly `f_A=+1`. Thus the integral keeps precisely the terms and weights in (4.2). `□`

Equivalently, `L_v` is the Shapley value obtained by flipping the negative syndrome bits from `+1` to their final `-1` values in a uniformly random order: for a parity monomial with odd `q`, its alternating marginal contributions have expected value `1/q` at each participating negative bit.

This is the promised **integrated transport payment**. It stays signed throughout and moves the problem from arbitrary high-weight relations to derivatives along **weight-four syndrome coordinates**.

## 6. Random q-flip bases have an exact `4/n` physical channel sparsity

There are many bases of Theorem 2.1. This freedom gives a concrete geometric refund.

Choose the anchor `a` uniformly in `V`. Independently for every `v` with `|v|>=2`, choose `i(v)` uniformly from `supp(v)`.

For a fixed physical cube edge `e` of direction `i`, let `C_e` be the number of basis q-flips whose two-edge support contains `e`.

### Theorem 6.1 — `B16-RANDOM-BASIS-CHANNEL-SPARSITY`

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E C_e
 =
 \frac{4(N-n-1)}{nN}
 <\frac4n.
 }
\tag{6.1}
\]

If `e in D_f`, the number of basis q-flips that actually route through `e` is at most `C_e`, so the same expectation is an upper bound for the number of active q-flip channels reaching that disagreement edge.

#### Proof

A displacement `v` can use a direction-`i` edge only if `i in supp(v)`. Conditional on choosing pivot `i`, exactly four anchors make `e` one of the two parallel edges of `B_v(a)`: either endpoint of `e` can be the anchor of the first edge, and either endpoint translated by `v` can be the anchor of the second. Since `|v|>=2`, these four anchors are distinct. Thus the contribution of `v` to `E C_e` is

\[
 \frac4{N|v|}
\]

when `v_i=1`, and zero otherwise. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E C_e
 &=\frac4N
   \sum_{\substack{v_i=1\\|v|\ge2}}\frac1{|v|}\\
 &=\frac4N
   \sum_{k=2}^n\frac1k{n-1\choose k-1}\\
 &=\frac4{nN}
   \sum_{k=2}^n{n\choose k}\\
 &=\frac{4(N-n-1)}{nN}.
\end{aligned}
\]

`□`

The exact values begin

\[
 n=2:\frac12,\quad
 n=3:\frac23,\quad
 n=4:\frac{11}{16},\quad
 n=5:\frac{13}{20},
\]

and then decay asymptotically as `4/n`.

This is a genuine dimension refund: a randomly translated/pivoted relation basis uses any given original physical edge in only `O(1/n)` weight-four channels on average.

## 7. Convexly average the bases — no simultaneous-choice problem

The randomization in Section 6 need not be derandomized. For every sampled basis, Theorem 4.1 gives a valid relation-by-relation unit allocation to disagreement edges. Averaging these allocations over the basis distribution is still a valid fractional allocation and still preserves every signed `c_A`.

Hence define

\[
 \overline\Lambda_e
 =\mathbb E_{\mathcal B}\Lambda_e^{\mathcal B}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then exactly

\[
 \sum_e\overline\Lambda_e=S_c(f).
\tag{7.2}
\]

There is no union bound over physical edges and no need to find one basis that is simultaneously sparse everywhere.

This gives a concrete sufficient local derivative target. If one can prove, for the R32 polynomial at the paid prefix, the **one-q-flip signed derivative estimate**

\[
 \boxed{
 L_v^{\mathcal B}(f)\le K n
 \quad\text{whenever }z_v=-1,
 }
\tag{7.3}
\]

uniformly over anchors, pivots, and `f`, then (6.1) implies

\[
 \overline\Lambda_e
 \le Kn\,\mathbb E C_e
 <4K
\tag{7.4}
\]

for every physical disagreement edge. Therefore

\[
 H_{\rm nat}(f)-R_d(f)
 <\frac8N(4K)|\mathcal D_f|
 =16K\,J(f)
\tag{7.5}
\]

in the folded gauge. Adding the already separate terminal endpoint payment would close FEI with a dimension-independent constant.

**Status of (7.3): OPEN.** It is not asserted here. The gain is that the previously required `O(1)` edge congestion is reduced to an `O(n)` bound on an **integrated signed derivative in one minimal weight-four relation coordinate**, with the random-basis geometry supplying the compensating `1/n`.

The scale matters: a trivial bound by the full range of the R32 polynomial is far too large. The desired `O(n)` estimate must use the alternating `c_A`, exact dual-code multiplicity, R16 moment refunds / negative credits, or the account04 plaquette-curvature cancellation. Taking `|c_A|` destroys precisely the cancellation that could make (7.3) true.
