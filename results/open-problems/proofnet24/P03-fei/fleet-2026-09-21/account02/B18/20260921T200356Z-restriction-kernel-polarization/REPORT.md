### Theorem 3.1 — `P03-B18-FOUR-SINE-DIAGONAL-IS-RESTRICTION-KERNEL` — PROVED

#### Proof

# 4. PROVED — the Shannon and phase pieces are separately PSD

This matters because their compiler contributions can be separated **without taking termwise absolute values**.

### Theorem 4.1 — `P03-B18-SHANNON-PHASE-PSD-SPLIT`

The kernels `mathsf S`, `mathsf P`, and hence `mathsf G`, are positive semidefinite kernels on the real line.

#### Proof for the Shannon kernel

Write `X=x^2`, `Y=y^2`. In natural logarithms,
\[
(\ln2)\mathsf S(x,y)
=(X+Y)\ln(X+Y)-X\ln X-Y\ln Y.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The exact integral representation
\[
\boxed{
(\ln2)\mathsf S(x,y)
=\int_0^\infty
\frac{(1-e^{-tX})(1-e^{-tY})}{t^2}\,dt
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
follows by differentiating twice in `X,Y` and using the zero boundary values at `X=0` or `Y=0`. The right side is an integral of rank-one positive kernels, so `mathsf S` is PSD.

#### Proof for the phase kernel

Let
\[
D(s)=\frac12[(1+s)\ln(1+s)+(1-s)\ln(1-s)]
=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{s^{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Then
\[
(\ln2)\mathsf P(x,y)
=(X+Y)D\!\left(\frac{2\sqrt{XY}}{X+Y}\right)
\]
\[
=\sum_{k\ge1}
\frac{4^kX^kY^k}{2k(2k-1)(X+Y)^{2k-1}}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
For each `k`,
\[
\frac{X^kY^k}{(X+Y)^{2k-1}}
=\frac1{\Gamma(2k-1)}
\int_0^\infty t^{2k-2}
[X^ke^{-tX}][Y^ke^{-tY}]\,dt,
\tag{4.5}
\]
so every summand is PSD. The nonnegative series converges to (4.4), proving that `mathsf P` is PSD. □

The two ledgers are therefore genuinely separable at the quadratic-form level; the phase correction is not merely a pointwise remainder attached to the Shannon term.

# 5. PROVED — the whole phase kernel is dominated by a Brownian threshold kernel

Define
\[
\mathsf M(x,y)=\min\{x^2,y^2\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is PSD because
\[

{\bf1}_{u\le x^2}{\bf1}_{u\le y^2}\,du.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — `P03-B18-PHASE-TO-BROWNIAN-LOEWNER-CONTRACTION` — PROVED

In Loewner order of kernels,
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf P\preceq \frac{2}{\ln2}\,\mathsf M.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Equivalently, for every finite real coefficient vector `c_a` and real points `x_a`,
\[
\sum_{a,b}c_ac_b\mathsf P(x_a,x_b)
\le
\frac2{\ln2}
\sum_{a,b}c_ac_b\min(x_a^2,x_b^2).
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

It is enough to prove in natural logs that
\[
\mathsf P_{\rm nat}:=(\ln2)\mathsf P\preceq2\mathsf M.
\]
For `X,Y>0`, write
\[
z=\frac12\ln(X/Y).
\]
Homogeneity gives
\[
\mathsf P_{\rm nat}(x,y)=\sqrt{XY}\,\phi(z),
\qquad
2\mathsf M(x,y)=\sqrt{XY}\,2e^{-|z|},
\tag{5.5}
\]
where
\[
\phi(z)=2\cosh z\,D(\operatorname{sech}z).
\tag{5.6}
\]
For `z>0`, using
\[
D(s)=s\operatorname{artanh}s+\tfrac12\ln(1-s^2)
\]
and `artanh(sech z)=ln coth(z/2)`, one gets
\[
\phi(z)=2\ln\coth(z/2)+2\cosh z\ln\tanh z,
\]
\[
\phi'(z)=2\sinh z\ln\tanh z,
\]
and therefore
\[
\phi''(z)-\phi(z)
=-h(z),
\qquad
h(z)=2[\operatorname{artanh}(\operatorname{sech}z)-\operatorname{sech}z]\ge0.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Let
\[
\delta(z)=2e^{-|z|}-\phi(z).
\]
As an even tempered distribution,
\[
(\partial_z^2-1)\delta=h-4\delta_0.
\tag{5.8}
\]
With the Fourier convention `hat g(xi)=int e^{-i xi z}g(z) dz`,
\[
\widehat\delta(\xi)=\frac{4-\widehat h(\xi)}{1+\xi^2}.
\tag{5.9}
\]
Now
\[
\int_0^\infty\operatorname{artanh}(\operatorname{sech}z)\,dz
=\int_0^\infty[\ln(1+e^{-z})-\ln(1-e^{-z})]dz
=\frac{\pi^2}{4},
\]
while
\[
\int_0^\infty\operatorname{sech}z\,dz=\frac\pi2.
\]
Hence
\[
\|h\|_1=\pi^2-2\pi<4.
\tag{5.10}
\]
Because `h>=0`,
\[
\widehat h(\xi)\le|\widehat h(\xi)|\le\|h\|_1<4.
\]
Thus `hat delta(xi)>0` for every real `xi`. By Bochner's theorem `delta` is positive definite. Multiplication of a stationary Gram matrix by the diagonal factors `sqrt(X_a)sqrt(X_b)` preserves PSD, so (5.3) follows. The zero cases follow by continuity. □

This is stronger than the scalar estimate `P(x,y)<= (2/ln2)min(x^2,y^2)`: it is a **quadratic-form contraction**, so it may be inserted into the signed terminal tensor without losing cancellations.
