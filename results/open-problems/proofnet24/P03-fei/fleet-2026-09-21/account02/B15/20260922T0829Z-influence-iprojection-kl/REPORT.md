## 2. Maximum entropy on nonempty spectral subsets with prescribed influences

Let `P` be any probability law on

\[
\Omega_k=2^{[k]}\setminus\{\varnothing\}.
\]

Write its coordinate marginals

\[
r_i=P(i\in S),\qquad \mu=\sum_i r_i=\mathbb E_P|S|\ge1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — explicit nonempty-product I-projection

If `mu=1`, then `P` is supported on singletons and is already uniquely determined by `r`; put

\[
Q_r(\{i\})=r_i.
\tag{2.2}
\]

If `mu>1`, there is a unique positive `c in (0,1]` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
1-c=\prod_{i=1}^k(1-c r_i).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let independent Bernoulli variables `B_i` have probabilities

\[
t_i=c r_i,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and condition on `B\ne0`. The resulting law `Q_r` on `Omega_k` has the required marginals `r_i` and is the unique maximum-entropy law with those marginals. Its entropy is

\[
\boxed{
H_{\max}(r):=H(Q_r)
={\sum_i h_2(c r_i)-h_2(c)\over c}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

For every `P` with marginals `r`,

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm occ}(P):=D_{\rm KL,2}(P\|Q_r)
=H_{\max}(r)-H(P)\ge0.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### Proof

For `mu>1`, define

\[
F(c)=1-\prod_i(1-c r_i).
\]

On `[0,1]`, `F` is concave because

\[
F''(c)
=-\sum_{i\ne j}r_ir_j\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}(1-c r_\ell)\le0.
\]

Moreover `F(0)=0`, `F'(0)=mu>1`, and `F(1)<=1`. Hence `F(c)=c` has one positive solution (with the endpoint case `c=1` allowed). This proves (2.3).

For independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities `t_i=c r_i`, (2.3) says exactly

\[
\Pr[B\ne0]=c.
\]

Conditioning on `B\ne0` therefore gives

\[
Q_r(i\in S)={t_i\over c}=r_i.
\]

Now let `P` be any other law on nonempty sets with these marginals. Since `log Q_r(S)` is an affine function of the indicator vector `(1_{i in S})`, its expectation under `P` equals its expectation under `Q_r`. Thus

\[
D(P\|Q_r)=H(Q_r)-H(P),
\]

which proves maximum entropy and (2.6). Finally, if `Q^0` denotes the unconditioned independent Bernoulli law, then

\[
H(Q^0)=\sum_i h_2(c r_i)
=h_2(c)+c H(Q_r),
\]

because the empty branch has zero conditional entropy. Rearranging gives (2.5). `QED`

This is standard finite-dimensional maximum-entropy/I-projection machinery, applied here to the exact nonempty spectral law. No priority claim is made for the abstract maximum-entropy fact.

## 3. Exact Pythagorean split of every conditional-product Gibbs KL

For arbitrary positive odds `y_i`, let

\[
\nu_y^+(S)
={\prod_{i\in S}y_i
 \over
 \prod_i(1+y_i)-1},
\qquad S\ne\varnothing.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — influence payment plus irreducible dependence credit

For every `P` on nonempty subsets with coordinate marginals `r`,

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm KL,2}(P\|\nu_y^+)
=
D_{\rm KL,2}(P\|Q_r)
+
D_{\rm KL,2}(Q_r\|\nu_y^+).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The second term is determined entirely by the original-coordinate marginals `r`; the first term is independent of the chosen Gibbs prices.

### Proof

Expand

\[
D(P\|\nu_y^+)-D(P\|Q_r)
=\mathbb E_P\log_2{Q_r(S)\over\nu_y^+(S)}.
\]

Both `Q_r` and `nu_y^+` are conditional product laws, so the displayed logarithm is affine in the coordinate indicators. Since `P` and `Q_r` have the same coordinate marginals, the expectation may be replaced by `E_{Q_r}`, giving `D(Q_r||nu_y^+)`. `QED`

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm occ}(P)
=
\inf_{y_1,\ldots,y_k>0}
D_{\rm KL,2}(P\|\nu_y^+),
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

with the usual boundary interpretation when some marginals are `0` or `1`. Thus `K_occ` is the part of the negative relative-entropy credit which **cannot be paid by any choice of original-coordinate first moments**.

This is stronger than a one-parameter scalar-price tilt: the scalar family moves all odds along one prescribed direction, while (3.3) solves the full `k`-parameter product I-projection. It is an analytical decomposition only; it does not pretend that a symmetric Bellman repair may assign arbitrary potential values independently to repeated equal biases.

\boxed{
