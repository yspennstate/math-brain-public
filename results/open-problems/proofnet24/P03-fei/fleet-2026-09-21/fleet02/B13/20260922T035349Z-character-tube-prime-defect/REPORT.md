## 2. Exact signed character-tube identities

Work on `F_2^n`; write

\[
\chi_T(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in T}x_i}.
\]

Let `g:F_2^n->{-1,+1}` be Boolean, let nonempty `T subseteq [n]`, put `k=|T|`, and choose `sigma in {+1,-1}` minimizing

\[
\alpha
:=
\Pr[g(x)\ne \sigma\chi_T(x)]
\le \frac12.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Define the **actual Boolean correction**

\[
h(x):=\sigma\chi_T(x)g(x)\in\{-1,+1\},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and its defect set

\[
E=\{x:h(x)=-1\},\qquad \mu(E)=\alpha.
\]

This is a jointly realizable signed Boolean transformation, not a relaxation.

### Theorem 2.1 — exact gauge-cost identity and Boolean tube bounds

Let

\[
L=\log_2(1/\alpha)
\]

when `alpha>0`, and let

\[
m:=4\alpha(1-\alpha).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{H(g)=H(h),}
\tag{2.4}
\]

\[
\boxed{
I(g)-1
=I(h)+k-1-2\sum_{i\in T}\Inf_i(h),
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

\[
\boxed{
H(g)
\le h_2(m)+m\log_2(2^n-1),
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

and, for `0<alpha<=1/2`,

\[
\boxed{
I(g)-1
\ge
k-1+2\alpha\,[L-2k].
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

The lower bound is useful whenever its right side is positive. Equation (2.5), rather than an invariant-gauge claim, is the correct character-gauge bookkeeping.

### Proof

Multiplication by `chi_T` translates Fourier support:

\[
\widehat h(S)=\sigma\widehat g(S\triangle T).
\tag{2.8}
\]

Thus the squared Fourier probabilities are merely permuted, proving (2.4). If `U` is the Fourier spectral sample of `h`, then the spectral sample of `g` is `U triangle T`; hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(g)
&=\mathbb E|U\triangle T|\\
&=\mathbb E|U|+k-2\mathbb E|U\cap T|\\
&=I(h)+k-2\sum_{i\in T}\Pr(i\in U),
\end{aligned}
\]

and `Pr(i in U)=Inf_i(h)`, giving (2.5).

Because `Pr(h=-1)=alpha`,

\[
\widehat h(\varnothing)=1-2\alpha.
\]

Therefore the nonconstant Fourier mass is exactly

\[
1-(1-2\alpha)^2=4\alpha(1-\alpha)=m.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Among all probability vectors with one atom of mass `1-m` and remaining mass `m` on at most `2^n-1` atoms, entropy is maximized by spreading the tail uniformly. This gives (2.6).

For (2.7), use the sharp cube edge-isoperimetric inequality on the **actual defect set** `E`:

\[
I(h)\ge 2\alpha\log_2(1/\alpha)=2\alpha L.
\tag{2.10}
\]

For every coordinate,

\[
\Inf_i(h)
=\mu(E\triangle(E+e_i))
\le2\alpha.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Insert (2.10)--(2.11) into the exact identity (2.5):

\[
I(g)-1
\ge
2\alpha L+k-1-4k\alpha,
\]

which is (2.7). QED.

\[
\boxed{H(g)<6\,[I(g)-1].}
\tag{3.2}

If `alpha=0`, then `g` is a signed dictator and `H(g)=I(g)-1=0`.

#### Proof

Here `k=1`, so (2.7) gives

\[
I(g)-1\ge2\alpha(L-2).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Condition (3.1) gives `L>=n/2+3` and in particular `4alpha<1`. From (2.6), the elementary bound

\[
h_2(u)\le u\log_2(e/u),\qquad 0<u<1,

and `m<=4alpha`,

\[
H(g)
\le4\alpha\,[n+L-2+\log_2 e].
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{H(g)}{I(g)-1}
\le
2\frac{n+L-2+\log_2e}{L-2}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Since `log_2 e<2` and `L>=n/2+3`,

\[
n+\log_2e<2L-4,
\]

which makes the right side of (3.5) strictly smaller than `6`. QED.

## 4. Primitivity alone cannot imply a character gap

A tempting shortcut after the Campaign-A minimum-core reductions is:

> every balanced composition-prime/tensor-primitive Boolean function should stay a universal positive Hamming distance from all dictators/characters.

That statement is false for genuine Boolean functions.

### Theorem 4.1 — composition-prime antipodal two-point defects

For every `n>=3`, let `N=2^n` and define

\[
g_n(x)=\chi_1(x)
\]

except at the two antipodal points `0^n` and `1^n`, where the sign is flipped.

Then:

1. `g_n` is balanced and every coordinate is essential;
2. `g_n` is composition-prime for disjoint Boolean gate composition, hence in particular tensor-primitive;
3. its Hamming distance from the physical dictator `chi_1` is
   \[
   \Pr[g_n\ne\chi_1]=\frac2N=2^{1-n}\to0;
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
4. its Fourier coefficients are exactly
   \[
   \widehat g_n(\{1\})=1-\frac4N,
   \qquad
   \widehat g_n(S)=-\frac4N
   \quad\text{for odd }|S|,\ S\ne\{1\},
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
   with all even-cardinality coefficients zero;
5. consequently
   \[
   \boxed{I(g_n)-1=\frac{4(n-2)}N\to0,}
   \tag{4.3}
   \]
   yet
   \[
   \boxed{
   \frac{H(g_n)}{I(g_n)-1}<6
   \quad\text{for every }n\ge3,
   }
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
   and in fact the ratio tends to `4`.

Thus neither composition primitivity nor tensor primitivity supplies a fixed positive character-distance gap or a fixed positive excess-influence gap.

### Proof of balance and Fourier formula

At `0^n`, `chi_1=+1`; at `1^n`, `chi_1=-1`. Flipping both changes one `+1` and one `-1`, so balance is preserved.

Write `E={0^n,1^n}`. Then

\[
g_n=\chi_1-2\chi_1 1_E.
\]

For any `S`,

\[
\widehat{\chi_1 1_E}(S)
=\frac1N
\left[1+(-1)^{|S\triangle\{1\}|}\right].
\]

This equals `2/N` exactly when `|S|` is odd and is zero otherwise, proving (4.2).

The squared spectrum therefore has one atom

\[
q:=\left(1-\frac4N\right)^2
\tag{4.5}
\]

at `{1}` and equal mass

\[
r:=\frac{16}{N^2}
\tag{4.6}
\]

at each of the other `N/2-1` odd subsets. The sum of Hamming weights over all odd subsets is `nN/4`; hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(g_n)
&=q+r\left(\frac{nN}{4}-1\right)\\
&=1+\frac{4(n-2)}N,
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (4.3).

### Proof of the entropy bound

Let

\[
\tau=1-q=\frac8N-\frac{16}{N^2}.
\]

Because every tail atom has mass `r=16/N^2`,

\[
H(g_n)
=-q\log_2q+\tau(2n-4).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The tail contribution divided by (4.3) is exactly

\[
\frac{\tau(2n-4)}{4(n-2)/N}
=4\left(1-\frac2N\right).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Using

\[
-q\log_2q\le(1-q)\log_2e=\tau\log_2e,
\]

the head contribution divided by (4.3) is at most

\[
\frac{2(1-2/N)\log_2e}{n-2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{H(g_n)}{I(g_n)-1}
\le
\left(1-\frac2N\right)
\left(4+\frac{2\log_2e}{n-2}\right)<6
\tag{4.9}
\]

for every `n>=3` (use `log_2e<2`; for `n=3` the prefactor is `3/4`, and for `n>=4` the bracket itself is `<6`). Equation (4.8) tends to `4` and the head term tends to zero, proving the limit.

### Proof of composition primitivity

A nontrivial disjoint composition has some inner block `A` with `2<=|A|<n`. If a Boolean function factors through one Boolean child on `A`, then as `x_A` varies, the restricted functions on `B=A^c` can take **at most two distinct values**, because the dependence on `x_A` enters only through one child bit.

For `g_n`, every such `A` gives at least three distinct restrictions.

If `1 notin A`, use the three assignments `0_A`, `1_A`, and any mixed assignment. The corresponding functions on `B` are respectively: `chi_1` with only `0_B` flipped; `chi_1` with only `1_B` flipped; and exactly `chi_1`. They are distinct.

If `1 in A`, choose: `0_A`; a mixed assignment with `x_1=0`; and a mixed assignment with `x_1=1`. The corresponding restrictions are: constant `+1` with only `0_B` flipped; constant `+1`; and constant `-1`. They are distinct.

Thus no proper block of size at least two can be a child block of a nontrivial disjoint composition. Since all coordinates are essential, only the trivial singleton decomposition remains. Hence `g_n` is composition-prime. QED.
