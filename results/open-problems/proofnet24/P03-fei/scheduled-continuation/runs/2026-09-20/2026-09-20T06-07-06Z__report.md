# Approach 1 — B11: dualize the two-flat defect

The first task is the difficult stopping leaf: after the quadratic polar part vanishes, the remaining function is a character times `sigma_A sigma_B`. ## Lemma 1 — high-codimension two-flat entropy

Let `A,B` be distinct affine flats of codimensions `a,b>=4`. Then

\[
\boxed{H(\sigma_A\sigma_B)\le84S(A,B),}
\qquad
S(A,B)=2a2^{-a}+2b2^{-b}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
x=2^{-a},\qquad y=2^{-b},
\]

and assume without loss of generality `a<=b`, so `x>=y`.

Put `C=A cap B`. If the intersection is nonempty, write its codimension as `c` and density `z=2^{-c}`; otherwise put `z=0` and omit every term involving `C`.

Since

\[
\sigma_A\sigma_B
=(1-2\,1_A)(1-2\,1_B)
=1-2\,1_A-2\,1_B+4\,1_C,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and the Fourier transform of an affine-flat indicator has constant magnitude equal to its density on the annihilator of its direction and vanishes elsewhere, every nonzero Fourier coefficient belongs to one of four support regions.

Let `W_A,W_B` be the annihilators of the direction spaces of `A,B`; if `C` is nonempty let `W_C` be the annihilator of its direction. Partition the nonzero support into

\[
R_A=W_A\setminus W_B,\qquad
R_B=W_B\setminus W_A,\qquad
R_I=(W_A\cap W_B)\setminus\{0\},
\]

and, when `C` is nonempty,

\[
R_C=W_C\setminus(W_A\cup W_B).
\]

The affine phases can change signs but not magnitudes of the indicator coefficients, so the triangle inequality gives

\[
|\widehat h(\xi)|\le6x\quad(\xi\in R_A),
\]
\[
|\widehat h(\xi)|\le6y\quad(\xi\in R_B),
\]
\[
|\widehat h(\xi)|\le8x\quad(\xi\in R_I),
\]
\[
|\widehat h(\xi)|=4z\quad(\xi\in R_C),
\tag{1.3}
\]

where `h=sigma_A sigma_B`. Here `z<=y<=x`.

The region cardinalities satisfy

\[
|R_A|\le2^a,\qquad |R_B|\le2^b,\qquad
|R_I|\le2^a,\qquad |R_C|\le2^c.
\tag{1.4}
\]

If `m_A,m_B,m_I,m_C` denote the squared-Fourier masses in these regions, (1.3)--(1.4) imply

\[
m_A\le36x,\qquad
m_B\le36y,\qquad
m_I\le64x,\qquad
m_C\le16z.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Let

\[
D=A\triangle B,\qquad \delta=|D|/2^n.
\]

Because `h=-1` exactly on `D`,

\[
M:=\sum_{\xi\ne0}\widehat h(\xi)^2
=1-(\mathbb Eh)^2
=4\delta(1-\delta).
\tag{1.6}
\]

For two distinct flats and `x>=y`, one always has

\[
\frac x2\le\delta\le2x.
\tag{1.7}
\]

Indeed, if the flats are disjoint then `delta=x+y>=x`. If they meet but neither is contained in the other, `C` is a proper affine subflat of `B`, hence `z<=y/2` and
`delta=x+y-2z>=x`. If they are nested, distinctness forces `B subsetneq A`, hence `y<=x/2` and `delta=x-y>=x/2`.

Because `a>=4`, (1.7) gives `delta<=1/8`. Consequently

\[
x\le M\le8x.
\tag{1.8}
\]

The lower inequality uses `M>=2delta>=x`; the upper uses `M<=4delta<=8x`.

Now expose first whether the spectral label is zero, then which of the at most four nonzero regions contains it. Shannon's chain rule and the support-size bounds give

\[
H(h)
\le h_2(M)+2M
+m_Aa+m_Bb+m_Ia+m_Cc.
\tag{1.9}
\]

For the binary term,

\[
h_2(M)\le M\log_2\frac eM
\le8x(a+\log_2 e)
\le12ax
\le6S,
\tag{1.10}
\]

using `a>=4` and `S>=2ax`. Also

\[
2M\le16x\le2S.
\tag{1.11}
\]

The four region terms obey

\[
m_Aa\le36ax\le18S,
\qquad
m_Bb\le36by\le18S,
\]
\[
m_Ia\le64ax\le32S.
\tag{1.12}
\]

When `C` is nonempty, `c>=b>=4`, and `r2^{-r}` decreases for `r>=2`, so

\[
m_Cc\le16cz\le16by\le8S.
\tag{1.13}
\]

If `C` is empty this term is zero. Summing (1.10)--(1.13) yields

\[
H(h)\le(6+2+18+18+32+8)S=84S.
\]

QED.

The proof deliberately uses only support geometry and total region masses; it does not assume the two flat spectra are independent or that coefficients inside a region have a common affine sign.

## Lemma 2 — the structural proxy is paid by edge boundary

Under the same hypotheses,

\[
\boxed{S(A,B)\le3I(\sigma_A\sigma_B).}
\tag{1.14}
\]

### Proof

Use the standard cube edge-isoperimetric inequality, with the influence normalization used here:

\[
I(\sigma_D)\ge2\delta\log_2\frac1\delta
\qquad(0<\delta\le1/2).
\tag{1.15}
\]

Again assume `a<=b`, set `x=2^{-a}`, `y=2^{-b}`, and note that the function `r2^{-r}` decreases for `r>=2`, so

\[
by\le ax.
\tag{1.16}
\]

If the flats are disjoint or intersect non-nestedly, the proof of (1.7) gives

\[
x\le\delta\le2x.
\]

Therefore

\[
I(h)\ge2x(a-1),
\tag{1.17}
\]

while

\[
S=2ax+2by\le4ax.
\tag{1.18}
\]

Since `a>=4`,

\[
4ax\le6x(a-1)\le3I(h).
\]

It remains to consider the nested case. Then `B subsetneq A`; write `b=a+k`, `k>=1`. Thus

\[
y=2^{-k}x,\qquad
\delta=x(1-2^{-k}).
\tag{1.19}
\]

If `k=1`, then `delta=2^{-(a+1)}` and

\[
I(h)\ge x(a+1),
\qquad
S=x(3a+1)\le3x(a+1)\le3I(h).
\tag{1.20}
\]

If `k>=2`, then `log_2(1/delta)>=a`, so

\[
I(h)\ge2ax(1-2^{-k}).
\tag{1.21}
\]

Meanwhile

\[
S=2x\bigl[a+(a+k)2^{-k}\bigr].
\]

For `a>=4,k>=2`,

\[
a+(a+k)2^{-k}\le3a(1-2^{-k}),
\tag{1.22}
\]

equivalently `(4a+k)2^{-k}<=2a`; the latter is immediate at `k=2` and becomes weaker as `k` increases. Hence `S<=3I(h)`.

QED.

# Approach 2 — B30: codimension-by-codimension stopping

## Lemma 4 — one codimension-three flat plus one high-codimension flat costs 16

Let `A` have codimension exactly three and `B` codimension at least four. For arbitrary quadratic `q`,

\[
\boxed{
H((-1)^q\sigma_A\sigma_B)
\le16J((-1)^q\sigma_A\sigma_B)
\le16I((-1)^q\sigma_A\sigma_B).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

An affine codimension-three indicator is the product of three affine linear equations, hence its multilinear `F_2` degree is at most three. Put

\[
s=(-1)^{P},\qquad P=q+1_A,
\qquad h=\sigma_B.
\]

Then `deg P<=3`.

Let `E` be the set of physical coordinates appearing in at least one degree-at-least-two monomial of the ANF of `P`. Fixing all coordinates in `E` makes every residual restriction `P_z` affine.

For `i in E`, the derivative `D_iP` is nonconstant and has degree `r_i<=2`. The Reed--Muller derivative gap (R4) gives

\[
j_i(s)\ge2^{-r_i}\ge\frac14.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Since `B` has codimension at least four,

\[
j_i(h)\le\operatorname{Inf}_i(h)\le2^{1-\operatorname{codim}B}\le\frac18.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The folded triangle (R6) therefore yields

\[
j_i(sh)\ge j_i(s)-j_i(h)\ge\frac18=\frac2{16}
\qquad(i\in E).
\tag{2.4}
\]

After fixing `E`, `s_z` is a character times a sign, while `h_z` is either constant or the sign of an affine section of `B`. By (R3), including its codimension-zero/one character endpoints,

\[
H((sh)_z)=H(h_z)\le4J(h_z)=4J((sh)_z)\le16J((sh)_z).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Now apply physical deletion (R7):

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(sh)
&\le16\,\mathbb E_zJ((sh)_z)+2|E|\\
&\le16\left(J(sh)-\sum_{i\in E}j_i(sh)\right)+2|E|\\
&\le16J(sh).
\end{aligned}
\]

QED.

This is not a fresh-control argument and does not assume the two branches are independent.

## Lemma 5 — all stopping leaves have constant 252

Consider a restricted function of the form

\[
g=(-1)^{q'}\sigma_{A'}\sigma_{B'},
\tag{2.6}
\]

where an empty flat sign is `1`, a whole-cube flat sign is a global sign, and identical flat signs cancel. After these simplifications, every stopping state below has a direct estimate

\[
H(g)\le252I(g).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed:

1. **Zero or one surviving flat.** Apply the newest one-flat quadratic theorem; its direct constant is `5.145274443580206...` and its hereditary constant is `4`.
2. **One surviving flat has codimension at most two.** The indicator of that flat has algebraic degree at most two, so absorb its sign into `q'`; this reduces again to the one-flat theorem.
3. **Both flats have codimension three.** The whole exponent `q'+1_{A'}+1_{B'}` has degree at most three, so the Reed--Muller theorem gives `H<=kappa_3 I`, with `kappa_3<4`.
4. **One flat has codimension three and the other at least four.** Apply Lemma 4 with constant `16`.
5. **Both flats have codimension at least four and the polar part of `q'` is zero.** Then `(-1)^{q'}` is a character; apply Corollary 3 with constant `252`.

Every direct leaf estimate also supplies a hereditary completion with the same constant because `R_T<=H` on a full physical restriction tree.

\tag{3.2}
\]

A codimension-`r` affine-flat sign has directional influence either zero or `2^{1-r}`. Since both codimensions are at least four,

\[
\left|\operatorname{Inf}_i(g)-\frac12\right|\le\frac18+\frac18=\frac14.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{j_i(g)\ge\frac14.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since conditioning lowers entropy,

\[
B_i(g)\le H(Z_i)=h_2(j_i(g)).
\]

The ratio `h_2(t)/t` decreases on `(0,1/2]`, so from `j_i>=1/4`,

\[
\boxed{B_i(g)\le\kappa_3j_i(g),\qquad
\kappa_3=4h_2(1/4)=3.2451124978\ldots .}
\tag{3.4}
\]

QED.

Restrictions preserve the class: a quadratic remains quadratic, and a section of an affine flat is empty or an affine flat after deleting the queried coordinate. Codimension may change, which is exactly why the stopping cases were stratified.

## Theorem 7 — two arbitrary affine-flat defects under arbitrary quadratic coupling

For every dimension `n`, every affine flats `A,B subseteq F_2^n`, and every `deg q<=2`,

\[
f=(-1)^q\sigma_A\sigma_B
\]

admits a full physical restriction tree satisfying

\[
\boxed{R_T(f)\le252 I(f),}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{H(f)\le252 I(f).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

Run the stopping procedure above until every leaf is certified by Lemma 5. The tree terminates because every internal query removes one physical coordinate.

Let `T_0` be the stopping prefix. Summing Lemma 6 over its internal nodes gives

\[
R_{T_0}\le\kappa_3J_{T_0}\le\kappa_3Q_{T_0}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Every stopping leaf `ell` has

\[
H(f_\ell)\le252I(f_\ell)
\]

and hence can be completed by a full physical tree with allocation at most `252 I(f_ell)`. Splicing such completions below the prefix and using the exact influence telescope (R1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_T(f)
&\le\kappa_3Q_{T_0}+252I_L\\
&\le252(Q_{T_0}+I_L)\\
&=252I(f).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (3.5).

For direct entropy, use the exact partial-tree identity and the paid recombination (R1)--(R2):

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(f)
&=R_{T_0}+S_{T_0}+H_L\\
&\le\left(\kappa_3+\frac2{\ln2}\right)Q_{T_0}
+252I_L.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since

\[
\kappa_3+\frac2{\ln2}=6.1305025796\ldots<252,
\]

the exact influence telescope again gives

\[
H(f)\le252(Q_{T_0}+I_L)=252I(f).
\]

QED.

This theorem strictly answers the *two-flat recombination test* posed at the end of the newest P03 report. It does **not** imply a uniform constant for an unbounded number of affine flats; the constant `252` here is a one-time two-component arrangement certificate, not an induction invariant already proved stable under arbitrary further symmetric differences.

# B2 adversarial and archived-family stress

The critical claims were attacked before being promoted.

### Nested flats

The dangerous geometry for the proxy `S` is not transverse intersection but near-cancellation of nested flats. For codimensions `(a,a+1)`, the symmetric-difference density is `2^{-(a+1)}`, and the ratio of `S` to three times the edge-isoperimetric lower bound tends to one. Thus the proof explicitly keeps this case separate instead of assuming transverse geometry.

### One-point defects

A one-point defect is one affine flat of codimension `n`. The stopping algorithm simplifies it to the stronger one-flat theorem before the new `252` certificate is needed. This preserves the archived fact that rare single defects should not be repeatedly overcharged.

### Quadratic / bent-marker examples

Pure quadratic phases occur when the two flat signs cancel or disappear. They stop in the already-certified quadratic class. The new proof therefore does not reintroduce a raw square-curvature charge that would be sensitive to a quadratic gauge.

### Tensor products

A tensor product of two one-flat signs on disjoint variable blocks is exactly `sigma_A sigma_B` for two lifted affine cylinders. The new checker includes a `3+3` one-point tensor example; its observed `H/I` is `2.9558895822515994`. Cross-block noninteraction is retained, not replaced by independent samples from an averaged derivative.

### Addressing, coded, majority-substitution and generic fresh-control families

These are **not generally representable as a product of at most two affine-flat signs times a quadratic phase**. They are therefore outside Theorem 7. This is a scope failure, not a numerical success: the new class theorem does not answer the archived addressing/natural-tree obstruction, the majority drift obstruction, coded-budget obstructions, or the unknown-seed fresh-control issue. No claim in this report relies on those families satisfying the new hypotheses.

# remaining_full_scope_gap

The full conjecture still asks for one finite constant for **every** Boolean function in every dimension. Theorem 7 gives a new certified stopping class but does not produce a universal decomposition of an arbitrary Boolean function into two-flat leaves with bounded payment.

The next nonredundant mathematical question is now sharply identified:

> Can the two-flat Fourier-region argument and codimension stopping compiler be made **stable under adding a third affine-flat sign with a constant that does not grow with the number of flats**, or is there already a three-flat arrangement whose cancellation geometry forces a new resource?

A naive iteration of the present proof would multiply or accumulate constants and is therefore not a route to full FEI. The right next test is three flats with arbitrary intersections, searching first for a counterexample to a constant-preserving arrangement compiler. A separate universal route remains the hereditary weighted-erasure/allocation problem `sup_f R_*(f)/I(f)<infinity`. Nothing in this report proves that estimate, and nothing here reopens the retired scalar drift/resolvent/curvature shortcuts.
