## Theorem 4.1 — exact quadratic-normalized Wiener norm of a one-point defect

Let

\[
w_n(x)=1-2\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}(x),\qquad n\ge3.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\Lambda_2(w_n)=3-2^{2-n}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log_2\Lambda_2(w_n)}{J(w_n)}
=\frac{\log_2(3-2^{2-n})}{n2^{1-n}}\longrightarrow\infty .}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Hence no finite universal `K` can satisfy (4.1).

### Proof

Let `phi=(-1)^q` for an arbitrary quadratic `q`, and multiply by a global sign so that `phi(0)=1`. Let the alternating polar rank of `q` be the even integer `r`. The squared Fourier law of a quadratic phase is uniform on an affine support of size `2^r`; hence every nonzero Fourier coefficient of `phi` has modulus

\[
a=2^{-r/2}.
\]

Fourier inversion at zero gives

\[
\sum_s\widehat\phi(s)=\phi(0)=1.
\]

If `N_+` and `N_-` are the numbers of `+a` and `-a` coefficients on the support, then

\[
N_++N_-=2^r,\qquad N_+-N_-=2^{r/2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Put `b=2^{1-n}`. Since

\[
\phi w_n=\phi-2\mathbf1_{\{0\}},
\]

its Fourier coefficients are

\[
\widehat{\phi w_n}(s)=\widehat\phi(s)-b.
\]

For `n>=3`, every allowed polar rank satisfies `a>b`. Therefore the contribution from the quadratic Fourier support is

\[
N_+(a-b)+N_-(a+b)
=2^{r/2}(1-b).
\]

Outside that support, all coefficients equal `-b`, contributing

\[
(2^n-2^r)b=2-2^{r+1-n}.
\]

Writing `t=2^{r/2}` gives the exact formula

\[
\Lambda(\phi w_n)=2+t(1-b)-bt^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The right side is concave in `t`. The allowed values lie between `1` and `T=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}`, so the minimum is at an endpoint. At `t=1`,

\[
2+(1-b)-b=3-2b=3-2^{2-n}.
\]

If `n=2m+1`, the other endpoint equals

\[
1+2^m-2^{-m},
\]

which equals the first endpoint only for `n=3` and is larger thereafter. If `n=2m>=4`, the other endpoint equals

\[
2^m-2^{1-m}>3-2^{2-2m}.
\]

Thus the minimum is (4.2), attained already by any affine character (`r=0`).

Finally every coordinate of `w_n` has influence `2^{1-n}<1/2`, so

\[
J(w_n)=n2^{1-n}.
\]

This proves (4.3). QED.

What fails is the scalar attempt to pay the *root value* `log Lambda_2` directly by root folded influence. # 5. Approach B11 — change category: multiplication as an operator on Fourier coefficient space

## Theorem 5.1 — entropy perturbation by a bounded-Wiener Boolean factor

For arbitrary Boolean functions `f,g` on the same cube,

\[
\boxed{|H(fg)-H(f)|\le2\log_2\Lambda(g).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

There is no independence, sparsity, degree, symmetry, or fresh-variable assumption.

### Proof

Let `x=(fhat(S))_S` and `y=((fg)hat(S))_S`. Multiplication by `g` in physical space is an orthogonal operator `U_g` on Fourier coefficient space because `g^2=1`. Its matrix is Fourier convolution:

\[
(U_gx)_S=\sum_T\widehat g(S\triangle T)x_T.
\]

Every column has `l_1` norm `Lambda(g)`, while orthogonality gives `||U_g||_{2->2}=1`. By Riesz--Thorin interpolation, for `1<=p<=2`,

\[
\|U_gz\|_p\le\Lambda(g)^{2/p-1}\|z\|_p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `||x||_2=||y||_2=1`, define `F_z(p)=ln||z||_p`. For a unit `l_2` vector,

\[
F_z'(2)
=\frac14\sum_S|z_S|^2\ln|z_S|^2
=-\frac14H_{\rm nat}(|z|^2).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Taking logs in (5.2), subtracting `F_x(p)`, and differentiating from the left at `p=2` yields

\[
\frac{H_{\rm nat}(x^2)-H_{\rm nat}(y^2)}4
\ge-\frac12\ln\Lambda(g),
\]

or

\[
H(y^2)-H(x^2)\le2\log_2\Lambda(g).
\]

Because multiplication by `g` is its own inverse, apply the same inequality to `(fg,g)` to obtain the reverse difference. QED.

### Corollary 5.2 — a folded additive decomposition for the new `Lambda_2` parameter

The neighboring quadratic-normalized proof can be sharpened in a direction useful for stopping arguments:

\[
\boxed{H(f)\le10J(f)+6\log_2\Lambda_2(f).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Proof: multiply `f` by the character that folds every physical influence above `1/2`; this leaves `H` and `Lambda_2` invariant and makes ordinary influence equal `J(f)`. Let `q` minimize `Lambda_2`, put `u=(-1)^qf`, `L=Lambda(u)`, `ell=log_2L`, and let `r` be the polar rank of `q`. ## Lemma 6.1 — exact raw Wiener branch drop

For every Boolean `f` and every physical split coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Lambda(f^0)+\Lambda(f^1)}2
=\Lambda(f)-d_i^W(f),
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

with `d_i^W` from (3.1).

### Proof

For each residual Fourier label `S`, put

`a=fhat(S)`, `b=fhat(S union {i})`.

The two restriction coefficients are `a+b` and `a-b`. For real numbers,

\[
\frac{|a+b|+|a-b|}{2}
=\max\{|a|,|b|\}
=|a|+|b|-\min\{|a|,|b|\}.
\]

Sum over all pairs. QED.

The identity is cancellation-sensitive: exact coherent pairing, rather than a component count, determines the drop.

## Theorem 6.2 — quadratic-normalized branch-drop inequality

Let `q` be any minimizer for `Lambda_2(f)`, put

`u=(-1)^q f`, `L=Lambda(u)=Lambda_2(f)`.

Then for every physical coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Lambda_2(f^0)+\Lambda_2(f^1)}2
\le L-d_i^W(u).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\log_2L-rac{\log_2\Lambda_2(f^0)+\log_2\Lambda_2(f^1)}2
\ge
\log_2\frac{L}{L-d_i^W(u)}.

### Proof

## Corollary 6.3 — global accumulated normalized-Wiener overlap budget

Take **any** full adaptive physical restriction tree. At every internal node `v`, choose an actual `Lambda_2` minimizer `q_v`, write

`u_v=(-1)^{q_v} f_v`, `L_v=Lambda_2(f_v)`,

and let `i(v)` be the queried coordinate. Define

\[
c_v:=\log_2\frac{L_v}{L_v-d_{i(v)}^W(u_v)}.
\tag{6.4}

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{v\in T^\circ}w_v c_v\le\log_2\Lambda_2(f).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For a stopped tree the right side is replaced by

`log_2 Lambda_2(f)-sum_leaf w_leaf log_2 Lambda_2(f_leaf)`.

### Proof

Multiply (6.3) at each node by its cylinder weight and telescope the parent/child log potentials. On a full tree every leaf is constant and has `Lambda_2=1`. QED.

This is a genuine all-dimensional accumulated allocation-type inequality on fully dependent branches. No child is replaced by an independent sample and no averaged derivative spectrum appears.

# 7. Approach B30 — localize cubic dependence one derivative at a time

The current quadratic theorems exploit the fact that a quadratic multiplicative derivative is a character. For a cubic phase, the derivative is quadratic, not a character. The following anti-cancellation inequality uses the **flat Fourier spectrum of that quadratic derivative** and the actual Wiener norm of the residual derivative.

## Lemma 7.1 — high-link-rank cubic anti-cancellation

Let `P` be an `F_2` polynomial of degree at most three, let `u` be arbitrary Boolean, and put

\[
F=(-1)^P u.
\]

For coordinate `i`, let

\[
q_i=D_iP=P(x)+P(x+e_i)
\]

and let `r_i` be the polar rank of the quadratic part of `q_i`. Then

\[
\boxed{
 j_i(F)
 \ge\frac{1-\min\{1,\;2^{-r_i/2}\Lambda(D_i u)\}}2.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

In particular, since

`Lambda(D_i u)<=Lambda(u)^2`,

one also has

\[
 j_i(F)
 \ge\frac{1-\min\{1,\;2^{-r_i/2}\Lambda(u)^2\}}2.
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

The derivative factorizes exactly:

\[
D_iF=(-1)^{q_i}D_i u.
\tag{7.3}
\]

A quadratic phase of polar rank `r_i` has Fourier support of size `2^{r_i}` and every nonzero coefficient has magnitude `2^{-r_i/2}`. Therefore Fourier convolution at the zero frequency gives

\[
|E D_iF|
=|\widehat{(-1)^{q_i}D_i u}(0)|
\le2^{-r_i/2}\sum_s|\widehat{D_i u}(s)|
=2^{-r_i/2}\Lambda(D_i u).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Also `|E D_iF|<=1`. Since

`j_i(F)=(1-|E D_iF|)/2`,

(7.1) follows.

Finally `D_i u=u\,(u\circ\tau_i)`. Fourier `l_1` is submultiplicative under physical products and invariant under translation, so

`Lambda(D_i u)<=Lambda(u)^2`,

proving (7.2). QED.

This inequality automatically respects exact cancellation. If `D_i u` contains the inverse quadratic phase, its Wiener norm is large enough for the right side to lose the payment rather than falsely charging the coordinate.

## Lemma 7.2 — folded form of the quadratic-normalized Wiener theorem

\[

\tag{7.5}

`Lambda_2(g')=Lambda_2(g)`.

Apply the neighboring direct theorem to `g'`. QED.

This is an immediate corollary of the newly consumed neighboring theorem, not a claim of an independently discovered proof of that theorem.

\]

\[
E_zJ(F_z)\le J(F)-\sum_{i\in E}j_i(F).
\]

Together with (7.9),

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(F)
&\le C\left(J(F)-\sum_{i\in E}j_i(F)\right)+2|E|\\
&\le CJ(F)-2|E|+2|E|\\
&=CJ(F).
\end{aligned}
\]

Equation (7.8) follows from `Lambda(D_i u)<=L^2`. QED.

### Scope and mechanism gain

This theorem is genuinely beyond the character-valued derivative setting: the queried derivative phase may be any quadratic, of arbitrary rank, and the residual `u` may have arbitrary internal dependence and algebraic degree. It introduces no fresh controls and no symmetry/affine-flat factorization assumption. Its hypothesis is an explicit **anti-cancellation certificate** comparing quadratic derivative rank to the actual residual derivative Wiener norm.

It remains a restricted stopping class. Low-rank derivative phases against large-Wiener residual derivatives can fail (7.6), and no universal argument is supplied for choosing a cubic hitting set whose every coordinate passes the certificate.

For `u=1`, every active cubic derivative has nonzero quadratic part and hence rank at least two; with `C=15`, `2^{-r_i/2}<=1/2<11/15`. Thus Theorem 7.3 recovers a coarse constant-15 result for pure degree-three phases. The repository already has a much sharper Reed--Muller degree-three constant, so this special case is not counted as progress. The gain is the **arbitrary residual** allowed by (7.6).
