# Approach 1 — successful: transposition defect + hereditary allocation

Let `h` be `d`-part-symmetric with respect to parts `V_1,...,V_d`, let `P` be any multilinear polynomial over `F_2` of algebraic degree at most `r`, and put

\[
F(x)=(-1)^{P(x)}h(x).
\]

No independence, rarity, fresh-control, or quadratic-rank hypothesis is imposed on `P`; the core may have arbitrary algebraic degree.

## Lemma 1 — bad-coordinate payment beyond character-valued derivatives

At any physical restriction, call a free coordinate `i` **bad** if `D_iP` is not part-symmetric under permutations inside the residual parts (with `i` removed from its own part). If `i` is bad and the restricted phase has degree at most `r`, then

\[
\boxed{j_i(F)\ge2^{-r}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

More precisely, if `s=deg(D_iP)`, then `j_i(F)>=2^{-(s+1)}`.

**Proof.** The multiplicative derivative of the core,

\[
g_i(x)=h(x)h(x+e_i),
\]

is Boolean, independent of `x_i`, and invariant under every transposition inside each residual symmetry part. Moreover

\[
1-2\operatorname{Inf}_i(F)=\mathbb E\,g_i(x)(-1)^{D_iP(x)}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Because `i` is bad, some transposition `tau=(jk)` inside one residual part changes `D_iP`. Decomposing a multilinear polynomial with respect to `x_j,x_k` gives

\[
D_iP+D_iP\circ\tau=(x_j+x_k)R,
\tag{1.3}
\]

where `R` is nonzero, independent of `x_j,x_k`, and `deg R<=s-1`. Reed--Muller minimum distance gives `Pr(R=1)>=2^{-(s-1)}`. Since `x_j+x_k` is an independent fair bit relative to `R`,

\[
\delta:=\Pr[D_iP\ne D_iP\circ\tau]\ge2^{-s}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Let `psi=(-1)^{D_iP}`. Invariance of `g_i` and uniform measure under `tau` gives

\[
\left|\mathbb E g_i\psi\right|
=\left|\mathbb E g_i\frac{\psi+\psi\circ\tau}{2}\right|
\le\mathbb E\left|\frac{\psi+\psi\circ\tau}{2}\right|
=1-\delta.
\]

Thus, using `j_i=(1-|1-2Inf_i|)/2`,

\[
j_i(F)\ge\delta/2\ge2^{-(s+1)}\ge2^{-r},
\]

because `s<=r-1`. QED.

For `r=2`, this reproduces the quarter-influence scale of the quadratic theorem. For higher degree the payment remains nonzero but depends on derivative degree.

## Lemma 2 — no bad coordinates implies part symmetry modulo a character

Let `W` be the residual variables. Suppose every free derivative `D_iP`, `i in W`, is part-symmetric on `W\setminus\{i\}`. Then there is an affine linear polynomial `L` and a part-symmetric polynomial `Q` on the residual parts such that

\[
\boxed{P=L+Q.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

**Proof.** Write the ANF coefficient of monomial `A` as `c_A`. Fix `|A|>=2`. Any two subsets with the same vector of intersection sizes with the residual symmetry parts can be connected by single replacements `A -> A-j+k` with `j,k` in the same part. Consecutive sets share at least one coordinate `i`. In `D_iP`, their residual monomials have the same part-count vector and are related by a within-part permutation, so part symmetry of `D_iP` forces equality of their coefficients. Hence every nonlinear coefficient depends only on the part-count vector. The nonlinear portion of `P` is therefore part-symmetric. Degree-zero and degree-one terms form `L`. QED.

Consequently, after all bad coordinates have been restricted, the remaining function is a character times a part-symmetric Boolean function: the restricted core remains part-symmetric, the nonlinear phase `Q` is part-symmetric, and their product remains part-symmetric.

## Lemma 3 — local allocation on a bad coordinate

If `i` is bad, then

\[
\boxed{B_i(F)\le\phi(r)j_i(F).}
\tag{1.6}
\]

**Proof.** The spectral marginal identity gives `Pr(i in Z)=Inf_i(F)`. Conditioning reduces entropy, so

\[
B_i(F)\le H(Z_i)=h_2(Inf_i(F))=h_2(j_i(F)).
\]

By Lemma 1, `j_i>=2^{-r}`. Since `h_2(t)/t` decreases on `(0,1)`,

\[
h_2(j_i)\le\frac{h_2(2^{-r})}{2^{-r}}j_i=\phi(r)j_i.
\]
QED.

## Theorem 4 — hereditary allocation for arbitrary bounded-degree twists

For every degree-`<=r` phase `P` and every `d`-part-symmetric Boolean core `h`,

\[
\boxed{R_*((-1)^Ph)\le A_{d,r}I((-1)^Ph),}
\qquad
\boxed{A_{d,r}=\max\{12.04+\log_2d,\phi(r)\}.}
\tag{1.7}
\]

**Proof.** Build a partial adaptive restriction tree by repeatedly querying any bad coordinate and stop a branch when no bad coordinate remains. The process terminates because each query removes a physical variable. Lemma 3 gives

\[
R_T\le\phi(r)J_T\le\phi(r)Q_T.
\tag{1.8}
\]

At every stopping leaf, Lemma 2 makes the function a character times a part-symmetric Boolean function with at most `d` parts. The class is restriction-stable. From `sum_i B_i<=H<=C_d I`, some relevant coordinate has `B_i<=C_d Inf_i`; recurse on restrictions. Hence every stopping leaf has a continuation tree with allocation cost at most `C_d I(leaf)`. Graft those trees onto `T`. The exact influence telescope `Q_T+I_L=I(F)` yields

\[
R_*(F)\le\phi(r)Q_T+C_dI_L\le A_{d,r}I(F).
\]
QED.

This is genuinely hereditary: branch choices may differ after restrictions, and no fresh coordinates are appended.

## Theorem 5 — source-facing FEI bound

The same class satisfies

\[
\boxed{H((-1)^Ph)\le C_{d,r}J((-1)^Ph)\le C_{d,r}I((-1)^Ph),}
\tag{1.9}
\]
where

\[
\boxed{C_{d,r}=\max\left\{12.04+\log_2d,\ \phi(r)+\frac2{\ln2}\right\}.}
\tag{1.10}
\]

Moreover

\[
\phi(r)\le r+\frac1{\ln2},
\qquad
C_{d,r}\le\max\left\{12.04+\log_2d,\ r+\frac3{\ln2}\right\}.
\tag{1.11}
\]

**Proof.** Use the same partial tree. OWZ gives `H_L<=C_d I_L` at stopping leaves. On internal nodes Lemma 3 and P03's recombination theorem give

\[
R_T+S_T\le\left(\phi(r)+\frac2{\ln2}\right)J_T
\le\left(\phi(r)+\frac2{\ln2}\right)Q_T.
\]

Together with `H=H_L+R_T+S_T` and `Q_T+I_L=I(F)`, this proves the `I` bound. For the folded bound, multiply `F` by the character on precisely the physical coordinates whose influences exceed `1/2`. Entropy and `J` are unchanged, total influence becomes exactly `J(F)`, and the function stays in the same class because only a linear term is added to `P`. Apply the `I` bound to that character multiple. Finally `phi(r)<=r+1/ln2` follows from `-(1-q)ln(1-q)<=q` with `q=2^{-r}`. QED.

For `r=2`, `phi(2)+2/ln2=6.13050...`, so `C_d` dominates and this recovers the single-core quadratic theorem with the same published constant. For a fully symmetric core (`d=1`), the constant remains exactly `12.04` for arbitrary perturbation degree `r<=7`; at `r=8` the present bound becomes `12.325263...` and then grows linearly in `r`.

This does **not** cover arbitrary high-degree dependence with a dimension-free constant: `r` may grow with dimension. It removes the character-valued-derivative restriction only for uniformly bounded perturbation degree.

# Approach 2 — minor-child recursion: precise scaling obstruction

The newest P03 theorem gives every split

\[
B_i(f)\le C_\chi j_i(f)+\min\{I(f^+),I(f^-)\}.
\]

It is tempting to query a bad phase coordinate and recursively charge the smaller child. This does not exploit the symmetry core. Split the variables into parts `A,B`; let `h` be a high-influence symmetric Boolean function of `B` only (e.g. odd majority), and set

\[
P(x_A)=\prod_{i\in A}x_i,\qquad |A|=r.
\]

At every `i in A`,

\[
j_i((-1)^Ph)=2^{1-r},
\]

while both physical children retain every `B`-coordinate influence of `h`, hence have total influence at least `I(h)`. Enlarging the `B` block makes `I(h)` unbounded while the queried folded influence remains `2^{1-r}`. Therefore the minor-child term is not a local constant multiple of the bad-coordinate payment. A successful use of that theorem must **stop** on the certified symmetric core rather than repeatedly pay its child influence. The stopping algorithm in Theorem 4 does exactly this through `R_T` and the leaf certificate.

This is an obstruction to a naive combination of two valid theorems, not to the minor-child theorem or to FEI.

# Approach 3 — direct physical deletion: valid but exponentially lossy

One can delete every bad coordinate at once and use the quadratic-symmetry report's entropy-deletion lemma. Lemma 1 gives `j_i>=2^{-r}`, yielding the valid weaker bound

\[
H((-1)^Ph)\le\max\{C_d,2^{r+1}\}J((-1)^Ph).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The exponential factor cannot be removed from this two-bits-per-deleted-coordinate architecture. On `r+1` variables take one symmetry part, constant core `h=1`, and

\[
P=x_1x_2\cdots x_r.
\]

The bad coordinates are exactly `1,...,r`, and each has

\[
j_i=2^{1-r}.
\]

Deleting them costs `2r` entropy bits in the universal deletion bound, while their total folded payment is `r2^{1-r}`. Any argument that pays this deletion solely by `C sum_{i in E}j_i` therefore needs

\[
\boxed{C\ge2^r.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The family itself has bounded FEI ratio tending four; this diagnoses the proof architecture, not the conjecture. The hereditary allocation theorem replaces the exponential loss by `phi(r)=r+O(1)`.
