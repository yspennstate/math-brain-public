## exact reused hypotheses

Let `f:{-1,1}^n->{-1,1}` and let `Z` be its spectral subset with law `P(Z=S)=fhat(S)^2`. For a physical coordinate `i`, write

\[
B_i(f)=H(Z_i\mid Z_{-i}).
\]

For a finite adaptive physical restriction tree `T`, node `v` has weight `w_v=2^{-depth(v)}`, current restriction `f_v`, and queried physical coordinate `i(v)`. Reuse from the current P03 theorem:

\[
R_T(f)=\sum_{v\text{ internal}}w_vB_{i(v)}(f_v),
\qquad
Q_T(f)=\sum_{v\text{ internal}}w_v\operatorname{Inf}_{i(v)}(f_v),
\]

and for a full tree with constant leaves,

\[
H(f)=R_T(f)+S_T(f),
\qquad
0\le S_T(f)\le\frac2{\ln2}Q_T(f),
\qquad
Q_T(f)=I(f).
\tag{R1}
\]

Hence every full tree satisfies

\[
R_T(f)\le H(f)\le R_T(f)+\frac2{\ln2}I(f).
\tag{R2}
\]

Define

\[
R_*(f)=\min_{T\text{ full}}R_T(f),
\qquad
\mathcal A(f)=R_*(f)/I(f)
\tag{R3}
\]

for nonconstant `f`. The minimum exists because a full tree has depth at most `n` after eliminating irrelevant repeated queries and there are only finitely many such physical decision trees. Full FEI follows from a universal finite upper bound on `mathcal A`; conversely (R2) makes this allocation problem FEI-equivalent at the level of finiteness of universal constants.

# Approach 1 — B12/B25: Bellman equation and exact tensorization of the live hereditary allocation

The current P03 report leaves `R_*` as the precise full-scope obstruction. The first task is to understand which constructions can amplify it. Disjoint tensor products are the most important stress operation because `H` and `I` add exactly, and many previous false resources failed under tensor amplification.

## Theorem 1 (Bellman recursion)

For a nonconstant Boolean function,

\[
\boxed{
R_*(f)=\min_{i\text{ relevant}}
\left[B_i(f)+\frac{R_*(f_{i=+1})+R_*(f_{i=-1})}{2}\right],
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

with `R_*(constant)=0`.

### Proof

Every full physical restriction tree has some relevant root coordinate `i`. Its root contributes exactly `B_i(f)`. Conditional on the two physical signs, its two subtrees are full restriction trees for `f_{i=+1}` and `f_{i=-1}` and appear with weights one half. Therefore every tree with root `i` has cost at least the bracket in (1.1). Conversely, attach optimal child trees to root `i`; this realizes equality for that root. Minimize over the finitely many relevant root coordinates. Querying an irrelevant coordinate adds zero root allocation but merely inserts a redundant fair split before the same function, so it cannot beat (1.1). QED.

This turns the live obstruction into a finite Bellman functional while retaining all branch dependence.

## Theorem 2 (exact tensorization)

Let `f` and `g` be Boolean functions on disjoint physical coordinate blocks. Then

\[
\boxed{R_*(f\otimes g)=R_*(f)+R_*(g).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(f\otimes g)=
\frac{I(f)\mathcal A(f)+I(g)\mathcal A(g)}{I(f)+I(g)}
\le\max\{\mathcal A(f),\mathcal A(g)\}.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Here tensor product means pointwise multiplication on disjoint input blocks. Constants are interpreted in the evident way.

### Proof — upper bound

The squared Fourier law of `f tensor g` is the product of the two squared Fourier laws. Therefore for a physical coordinate `i` in the `f` block,

\[
B_i(f_a\otimes g_b)=B_i(f_a)
\tag{1.4}
\]

for every pair of physical restrictions `a,b`; conditional entropy of that spectral bit is unaffected by an independent spectral block.

Run an optimal full tree for `f`, and at every constant-`f` leaf run an optimal full tree for `g`. By (1.4), the first stage costs `R_*(f)`. The leaf weights sum to one, so the second stage costs `R_*(g)`. Hence

\[
R_*(f\otimes g)\le R_*(f)+R_*(g).
\tag{1.5}
\]

### Proof — lower bound by pruning and averaging

Take an arbitrary full restriction tree `T` for `f tensor g`. Split its internal nodes into queries of the `f` block and queries of the `g` block.

Fix a complete assignment `eta` of all physical `g` coordinates. Prune `T` by, whenever a `g` coordinate is queried, retaining only the branch matching `eta`; retain both branches at every `f` query. The resulting tree `T^eta_f` is a valid full physical restriction tree for `f`: if an original leaf makes `f_a tensor g_b` constant on its remaining disjoint variables, then `f_a` itself must be constant (otherwise fixing any value of the nonzero sign function `g_b` would leave variation in `f_a`).

Consider a particular `f`-query node `v` in the original tree. Suppose its root-to-node path has made `p` previous `f` queries and `q` previous `g` queries. In the original tree its weight is

\[
w_v=2^{-(p+q)}.
\]

It appears in the pruned tree for exactly a fraction `2^{-q}` of complete assignments `eta`, and when it appears its pruned-tree weight is `2^{-p}`. By (1.4), its allocation cost is the same `B_i(f_a)` in both descriptions. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E_\eta R_{T^\eta_f}(f)
=\sum_{v\text{ querying }f}w_vB_{i(v)}(f_v).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Every pruned tree is full, so its cost is at least `R_*(f)`. Thus the entire `f`-query part of the original cost is at least `R_*(f)`. Repeating the same argument after fixing a complete assignment of the `f` block shows that the `g`-query part is at least `R_*(g)`. Adding them gives

\[
R_T(f\otimes g)\ge R_*(f)+R_*(g).
\tag{1.7}
\]

Since `T` was arbitrary, (1.2) follows. Ordinary total influence tensorizes on disjoint products, so (1.3) follows. QED.

### Consequence for proof design

Tensor powers cannot make the live allocation ratio diverge:

\[
\mathcal A(f^{\otimes m})=\mathcal A(f).
\]

Thus, unlike the already-refuted bounded-mode drift potentials, a negative result for the hereditary allocation route requires a genuinely growing indecomposable family or factors whose own `mathcal A` already grows. Conversely, a class with bounded allocation ratio stays bounded under arbitrary disjoint products without multiplying its constant.

The theorem is about the current live `R_*` functional itself, not a new FEI subclass estimate. It therefore survives the most basic amplification test that eliminated several earlier resources.

# Approach 2 — B24/B16: exact one-point allocation and mechanism calibration

The one-point defect is a mandatory stress family because it defeats fixed linear-coordinate marginal entropy estimates and creates sharp variance accumulation. Here it is completely tractable for the live Bellman functional.

For `n>=1` set

\[
w_n(x)=1-2\mathbf 1_{\{x=0\}},\qquad N=2^n.
\]

Its squared Fourier law has mass

\[
A=\frac{(N-2)^2}{N^2}
\]

at spectral label zero and mass

\[
b=\frac4{N^2}
\]

at every nonzero label.

## Lemma 3 (exact root allocation)

For every physical coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{
B_i(w_n)
=(A+b)h_2\!\left(\frac b{A+b}\right)
+\frac{4(N-2)}{N^2}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Pair Fourier labels along coordinate `i`. The pair `{0,e_i}` has masses `(A,b)` and contributes `(A+b)h_2(b/(A+b))`. Every other pair has equal masses `(b,b)` and therefore contributes two bits of mass, namely `2b`. There are `N/2-1` such pairs. Their total is `(N-2)b=4(N-2)/N^2`. QED.

Every physical coordinate is symmetric. Restricting `x_i` to the exceptional sign gives `w_{n-1}`; the other restriction is constant. Hence the Bellman recursion is forced.

## Theorem 4 (exact recurrence and asymptotic allocation ratio)

Put `R_n=R_*(w_n)`. Then

\[
\boxed{R_1=0,\qquad R_n=B_i(w_n)+\tfrac12R_{n-1},}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and equivalently

\[
R_n=\sum_{m=2}^n2^{-(n-m)}B_i(w_m).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
\boxed{
R_*(w_n)=\frac{4(n-1)}{2^n}+O(2^{-n}),
\qquad
I(w_n)=\frac{2n}{2^n},
\qquad
\mathcal A(w_n)\longrightarrow2.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

A simple explicit bound valid for every `n>=2` is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A(w_n)\le2+\frac1{n\ln2}<3.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

### Proof

Equation (2.2) follows from Theorem 1 and the restriction description above; no coordinate choice can change it.

For `m>=2`, let `N_m=2^m`. The equal-mass term in (2.1) is

\[
4(N_m-2)/N_m^2=4/N_m-8/N_m^2.
\]

For the exceptional pair use

\[
h_2(t)\le t\log_2(1/t)+t/\ln2,
\qquad0\le t\le1/2.
\]

Since `(A+b)<=1` and `b=4/N_m^2`, the exceptional contribution is at most

\[
b\left[\log_2(1/b)+1/\ln2\right]
=\frac4{N_m^2}\left(2m-2+1/\ln2\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
B_i(w_m)\le\frac4{2^m}
+\frac{8m-16+4/\ln2}{4^m},
\tag{2.6}
\]

while directly from (2.1) its second term gives `B_i(w_m)=4/2^m+O(m/4^m)`. Substitute into (2.3):

\[
R_n=\frac{4(n-1)}{2^n}
+O\!\left(2^{-n}\sum_{m\ge2}\frac m{2^m}\right),
\]

proving the first asymptotic in (2.4). Each physical coordinate of `w_n` changes at exactly the two inputs `0` and `e_i`, so `I_i=2/2^n` and the influence formula follows.

For the explicit upper bound, summing the error in (2.6) gives

\[
\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{8m-16+4/\ln2}{2^m}
=4+\frac2{\ln2}.
\]

Hence

\[
R_n\le\frac{4(n-1)+4+2/\ln2}{2^n}
=\frac{4n+2/\ln2}{2^n}.
\]

Divide by `I=2n/2^n` to obtain (2.5). QED.

The same P03 paid-recombination identity that reduces FEI to `R_*` also prevents the live mechanism constant from being artificially small.

\[
R_T(f)=H(f)-S_T(f)

Taking the minimum over full trees yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A(f)\ge\frac{H(f)}{I(f)}-\frac2{\ln2}.

The current rare-control report records the published Hod lexicographic construction with

This is a mechanism-level lower bound, not a new FEI lower bound: the FEI ratio itself is the published input. # Approach 3 — B2/B20: direct logarithmic edge cost is not a restriction supermartingale

After tensorization removed a major amplification danger for `R_*`, I tested whether the alternative live weighted edge functional `K` could supply a simpler hereditary payment by decreasing under physical restriction coordinate-by-coordinate. The natural proposed lemma would be, for distinct physical coordinates `i,j`,

\[
\frac12\left[K_i(f_{j=+1})+K_i(f_{j=-1})\right]
\stackrel{?}{\le}K_i(f).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If true, repeated restriction would make the root `K` directly control descendant logarithmic edge costs. It is false even on the one-point family.

## Proposition 5 (exact restriction-growth obstruction for `K_i`)

For `w_n`, `n>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
K_i(w_n)=\frac8{4^n}\log_2(2^{n-1}-1).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For two distinct coordinates `i,j`, one restriction of `j` is constant and the other is `w_{n-1}`. Consequently, for every `n>=4`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac12K_i(w_{n-1})>K_i(w_n).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus (3.1) fails uniformly from dimension four onward.

### Proof

On spectral `i`-edges of `w_n`, every pair except `{0,e_i}` has equal masses `(b,b)` and contributes zero to `K_i`. The exceptional edge has masses

\[
A=(N-2)^2/N^2,\qquad b=4/N^2,
\]

so

\[
K_i=b\log_2(A/b)
=\frac8{N^2}\log_2\frac{N-2}{2}
=\frac8{4^n}\log_2(2^{n-1}-1),
\]

proving (3.2).

Now

\[
\frac12K_i(w_{n-1})>K_i(w_n)
\]

is equivalent to

\[
2\log_2(2^{n-2}-1)>\log_2(2^{n-1}-1),
\]

or

\[
(2^{n-2}-1)^2>2^{n-1}-1.
\]

With `a=2^{n-2}>=4`, the difference is `a^2-4a+2>0`. QED.

At `n=4`, the parent value is

\[
K_i(w_4)=\frac1{32}\log_2 7\approx0.08772984,
\]

whereas the average restricted value is

\[
\frac12K_i(w_3)=\frac1{16}\log_2 3\approx0.09906016.
\]

This obstruction does **not** refute the universal direct estimate `K(f)<=C I(f)`. It only shows that a proof cannot treat each coordinate's logarithmic edge cost as a naive restriction supermartingale. A successful hereditary `K` argument needs refunds, branch-dependent transport, or a nonlocal multiscale potential—consistent with the earlier signed-cancellation barriers.
