## 4. Approach A — the full endpoint-scale family

### Theorem 4.1: uniform entropy equivalence for every endpoint scale

For every Boolean `f` on `n>=2` variables and every `0<lambda<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\ln2}{2}H(f)\le\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)
\le c_{n,\lambda}H(f)<\frac12H(f),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
where
\[
c_{n,\lambda}=
\frac{2^{\lambda(1-1/n)}-1}{2\lambda(1-1/n)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For `n=1`, every Boolean spectrum is a point mass and both sides vanish. Hence, uniformly in `lambda`,
\[
2\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)\le H(f)\le\frac2{\ln2}\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f).
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

A nonzero Boolean Walsh coefficient is
\[
\widehat f(S)=2^{-n}\sum_x f(x)(-1)^{S\cdot x}.
\]
For `n>=1`, a nonzero numerator is an even integer, so
\[
|\widehat f(S)|\ge2^{1-n},\qquad p_S\ge2^{2-2n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Draw `S` from the squared Fourier law and set `X=-ln p_S`. Then
\[
0\le X\le2(n-1)\ln2,\qquad E X=(\ln2)H(f).
\]
Moreover
\[
M_{2-\lambda/n}(f)=\sum_Sp_S^{1-\lambda/(2n)}
=E\exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda X}{2n}\right).
\]
Let `r(y)=(e^y-1)/y`, `r(0)=1`. Then
\[
\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)
=\frac12 E\left[Xr\!\left(\frac{\lambda X}{2n}\right)\right].
\tag{4.5}
\]
The function `r` is increasing on `[0,infinity)`, while
\[
0\le\frac{\lambda X}{2n}\le a:=\lambda(1-1/n)\ln2.
\]
Thus
\[
\frac12EX\le\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)\le\frac12r(a)EX.
\]
Converting natural entropy to bits gives the first inequality and the coefficient (4.2). Put `u=lambda(1-1/n)`. For `n>=2`, `0<u<1`; convexity of `2^u` places its graph below the chord joining `(0,1)` and `(1,2)`, hence `2^u<1+u` and `c_{n,lambda}<1/2`. QED.

This is an entropy-equivalent reformulation, not an FEI bound.

### Theorem 4.2: exact physical restriction-tree telescope for every fixed lambda

For any full adaptive physical restriction tree `T`, with node weight `w_v`, remaining dimension `m(v)`, state `f_v`, and queried coordinate `i(v)`,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{v\in T^\circ}w_v A_{i(v),\lambda}^{(m(v))}(f_v)
=\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
No independence of sibling branches is used.

#### Proof

Multiply (3.2) by `w_v`. Each child has cylinder weight `w_v/2`; therefore every nonroot `Phi` term cancels exactly with the corresponding child term of its parent. Leaves have zero remaining dimension and `Phi_{0,lambda}=0`. Only the root survives. QED.

Consequently, for any **fixed** `lambda`, a universal selector
\[
\forall f\not\equiv\text{const}\quad\exists i:\quad
A_{i,\lambda}^{(n)}(f)\le K\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
\tag{4.7}
\]
would imply `Phi<=K I` by recursively choosing that coordinate, and then FEI with constant `2K/ln2`. Equation (4.7) is **not proved** here.

### Theorem 4.3: endpoint-scale tensor subadditivity

For Boolean functions on disjoint variable sets of sizes `m,k`, for every `0<lambda<=1`,
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{m+k,\lambda}(f\otimes g)
\le\Phi_{m,\lambda}(f)+\Phi_{k,\lambda}(g).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Therefore for any finite disjoint product of nonconstant factors,
\[
\frac{\Phi(\bigotimes_r f_r)}{I(\bigotimes_r f_r)}
\le\max_r\frac{\Phi(f_r)}{I(f_r)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
The dimension in each `Phi` is the physical dimension of the corresponding factor/product, and `lambda` is the same on both sides.

#### Proof

For a probability law `p`, put
\[
R_p(t)=\sum_xp_x^{1-t}=E_p e^{tX},\qquad F_p(t)=\ln R_p(t),
\quad X=-\ln p_x.
\]
`F_p` is a log moment-generating function, hence convex, and `F_p(0)=0`. Let
\[
t=\frac\lambda{2(m+k)},\quad t_m=\frac\lambda{2m},\quad t_k=\frac\lambda{2k},
\quad\theta=\frac m{m+k}.
\]
Since `t=theta t_m=(1-theta)t_k`, convexity from the origin gives
\[
R_f(t)\le R_f(t_m)^\theta,
\qquad
R_g(t)\le R_g(t_k)^{1-\theta}.
\]
The squared Fourier law of a disjoint tensor product is the product law, so its `R` is the product. Weighted AM--GM therefore yields
\[
R_{f\otimes g}(t)
\le R_f(t_m)^\theta R_g(t_k)^{1-\theta}
\le\theta R_f(t_m)+(1-\theta)R_g(t_k).
\]
Subtract one and multiply by `(m+k)/lambda`. This is exactly (4.8). Influence is additive on disjoint products, and (4.9) follows by iteration and the elementary weighted-average bound. QED.

This theorem is global. It does **not** say local adaptive charges are tensor-monotone; the concurrent checker already contains a `w_2` / `w_2 tensor w_2` numerical obstruction to that stronger statement, and the asymptotic theorem below explains the behavior.

## 5. Approach B — fully dependent Shannon branch recombination

The purpose of this route is to pay the actual two children of one physical restriction without any independent-child substitution.

Fix coordinate `i`. For each Fourier set `T` not containing `i`, write
\[
a_T=\widehat f(T),\qquad b_T=\widehat f(T\cup\{i\}),
\qquad q_T=a_T^2+b_T^2.
\]
The two child Fourier laws are
\[
P_+(T)=(a_T+b_T)^2,\qquad P_-(T)=(a_T-b_T)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Parseval for both Boolean restrictions gives
\[
\sum_Ta_Tb_T=0,
\tag{5.2}
\]
and `(P_++P_-)/2=q`.

### Theorem 5.1: exact branch-information identity and universal payment

Let
\[
D_i^H(f)=H(f)-\frac{H(f^0)+H(f^1)}2,
\qquad \alpha_i=\operatorname{Inf}_i(f).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
D_i^H(f)=H(B\mid T)+\operatorname{JS}(P_+,P_-),
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
where `(T,B)` is the parent squared-spectrum sample, `B=1` means `i` is present, and `JS` is base-two Jensen--Shannon divergence. Moreover
\[
\boxed{
0\le D_i^H(f)\le2h_2(\alpha_i)=2h_2(j_i(f)).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This is valid for every Boolean function and every physical coordinate, with no freshness, independence, symmetry, degree, density, or quadratic hypothesis. The constant two is sharp already in dimension two.

#### Proof

The parent law splits into the pair masses `a_T^2,b_T^2`. Therefore
\[
H(f)=H(q)+\sum_Tq_T h_2\!\left(\frac{b_T^2}{q_T}\right)
=H(q)+H(B\mid T).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Since `q=(P_++P_-)/2`,
\[
H(q)-\frac{H(P_+)+H(P_-)}2=\operatorname{JS}(P_+,P_-),
\]
which proves (5.3). In particular the drop is nonnegative.

The parent bit `B` has mean `Pr(B=1)=sum_T b_T^2=alpha_i`, so
\[
H(B\mid T)\le h_2(\alpha_i).
\tag{5.6}
\]
It remains to pay the Jensen--Shannon term. Under the equal mixture of `P_+,P_-`, condition on `T`. Put
\[
r_T=\frac{b_T^2}{q_T},\qquad
u_T=\frac{2a_Tb_T}{q_T}
\]
when `q_T>0`. The posterior probability of the `+` branch is `(1+nu_T)/2`. Hence
\[
\operatorname{JS}(P_+,P_-)
=\sum_Tq_T\left[1-h_2\!\left(\frac{1+\nu_T}{2}\right)\right].
\tag{5.7}
\]
For `|u|<=1`, elementary calculus gives
\[
1-h_2\!\left(\frac{1+u}{2}\right)\le u^2.
\tag{5.8}
\]
Indeed `g(u)=h_2((1+u)/2)-1+u^2` is even, vanishes at `0,1`, and
\[
g''(u)=2-\frac1{(\ln2)(1-u^2)}
\]
changes sign at most once on `(0,1)`; thus `g'` first increases from zero and then decreases to minus infinity, so `g` rises and then falls back to zero and is nonnegative.

Using (5.8),
\[
\operatorname{JS}(P_+,P_-)
\le4\sum_T\frac{a_T^2b_T^2}{q_T}
=4\sum_Tq_Tr_T(1-r_T).
\]
Since `sum q_T r_T=alpha_i`, Jensen gives `sum q_T r_T^2>=alpha_i^2`, hence
\[
\operatorname{JS}(P_+,P_-)
\le4\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i).
\tag{5.9}
\]
Putting `u=1-2alpha_i` in (5.8) gives the equivalent scalar bound
\[
4\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)\le h_2(\alpha_i).
\tag{5.10}
\]
Combining (5.6), (5.9), and (5.10) proves (5.4). For the two-variable one-point defect, `alpha_i=1/2`, the conditional-orientation term and the Jensen--Shannon term are both one bit, so equality `D_i^H=2h_2(alpha_i)=2` holds. QED.

### Why this does not prove FEI

For a rare influence `alpha`, `h_2(alpha)/alpha` diverges like `log(1/alpha)`. Thus (5.4) is a fully paid **dependent-branch recombination theorem**, but its scalar payment still has the familiar logarithmic rare-event loss. It must be combined with a negative/adaptive credit such as the endpoint parameter-change term; using (5.4) alone recursively would not yield a finite universal FEI constant.

## 6. Approach C — tensor powers and padding cannot by themselves create the selector blow-up

The previous finite checker showed that the local `lambda=1` charge can increase from `w_2` to `w_2 tensor w_2`. The right question is whether repeated tensorization can make that ratio diverge. It cannot for any fixed base.

### Theorem 6.1: exact tensor-power local adaptive asymptotic

Fix a Boolean function `f` on `m` variables and a coordinate `i` with `alpha_i=Inf_i(f)>0`. Let
\[
h=h(f)=(\ln2)H(f),\qquad
h_b=h(f^b),\qquad
D_i^{\rm nat}=h-\frac{h_0+h_1}{2}.
\]
For fixed `0<lambda<=1`, let `F_k=f^{tensor k}` on `km` variables and query coordinate `i` in the first factor. Set
\[
u=\frac{\lambda h}{2m}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
A_{i,\lambda}^{(km)}(F_k)
\longrightarrow
L_{i,\lambda}(f):=
\frac{e^u-1}{\lambda}
+e^u\left(\frac{D_i^{\rm nat}}2-\frac h{2m}\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
In particular the local charge/influence ratio tends the finite number `L_{i,lambda}(f)/alpha_i`. Also
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Phi_{km,\lambda}(f^{\otimes k})}{I(f^{\otimes k})}
\longrightarrow
\frac m{\lambda I(f)}\left(e^{\lambda h/(2m)}-1\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Let `p` be the squared Fourier law of `f`, let `X=-ln p`, and define
\[
R(t)=E e^{tX},\qquad K(t)=\ln R(t).
\]
Because the support is finite,
\[
K(t)=ht+\frac v2t^2+O(t^3)
\tag{6.3}
\]
for some finite varentropy `v`.

At the root, `t=lambda/(2km)` and the product spectrum gives
\[
R_{F_k}(t)=R(t)^k.
\]
Thus `kK(t)=u+O(1/k)`, which immediately gives (6.2) after dividing by `I(F_k)=kI(f)`.

For the local charge one more term is needed. Write
\[
c=\frac\lambda{2m},\qquad t=\frac c k.
\]
Then
\[
kK(t)=u+\frac{v c^2}{2k}+O(k^{-2}).
\tag{6.4}
\]
After restricting the chosen coordinate, child `b` is `f^b tensor f^{tensor(k-1)}` and has total dimension `km-1`. Its endpoint parameter is
\[
t'=\frac\lambda{2(km-1)}
=\frac c k+\frac c{m k^2}+O(k^{-3}).
\]
If `K_b(t)=h_b t+O(t^2)`, then
\[
(k-1)K(t')+K_b(t')
=u+\frac1k\left[c(h_b-h)+\frac{hc}{m}+\frac{vc^2}{2}\right]+O(k^{-2}).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Exponentiate (6.4)--(6.5), multiply by the prefactors `km/lambda` and `(km-1)/lambda`, and average the two children. The terms containing `v` cancel exactly. Since `(h_0+h_1)/2=h-D_i^{nat}`, the surviving constant term is precisely (6.1). QED.

This rules out a whole obstruction mechanism: repeated tensor powers of **one fixed finite base** cannot send every copied relevant coordinate's adaptive ratio to infinity. It does not control a sequence whose base itself changes with dimension.

### Corollary 6.2: dummy-variable padding has a different finite limit

Embed a fixed `m`-variable `f` into `n=m+d` variables by adding `d` irrelevant physical coordinates. For every fixed `lambda`, as `d->infinity`,
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)\to\frac{h(f)}2,
\qquad
A_{i,\lambda}^{(n)}(f)\to\frac{D_i^{\rm nat}(f)}2
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
for every active coordinate `i`.

#### Proof

The squared spectral law is unchanged by dummy padding. With `t=lambda/(2n)`,
\[
R(t)=1+h t+O(t^2),
\]
so `(n/lambda)(R(t)-1)->h/2`. Apply the same expansion to the two active-coordinate restrictions, whose spectral laws are likewise fixed while their ambient dimension tends to infinity, and subtract. QED.

Thus ambient dimension alone also cannot generate a selector divergence from a fixed base.

## 7. Fourth attempted closure: averaging the local endpoint charge

A stronger route would be a direct estimate such as
\[
\sum_i A_{i,\lambda}^{(n)}(f)\le K I(f),
\tag{7.1}
\]
which would imply an existence-coordinate selector immediately. I did not prove (7.1).

The useful local inequality obtained in the attempt is the exact convexity lower bound, for `1<r<2`,
\[
\frac{|a+b|^r+|a-b|^r}{2}-|a|^r
\ge\frac{r(r-1)}2\,b^2(|a|+|b|)^{r-2}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
It follows by integrating `d^2|x|^r/dx^2=r(r-1)|x|^{r-2}` against the triangular second-difference kernel; because `r-2<0`, `|a+t|<=|a|+|b|` gives the displayed lower bound.

The obstruction is precise: replacing the amplitude factor in (7.2) by a uniform dyadic worst-case bound loses a factor growing with dimension on the smallest nonzero Fourier coefficients. The local amplitude factor must remain coupled to the coordinate and spectrum. Therefore this run does not record another crude averaged selector conjecture as progress. The next analytical attack should combine (7.2), the branch-information payment (5.4), and the endpoint parameter-change rebate before averaging.
