## 3. Bidirectional cut and global dependency bank

Write `c_*=2/ln2`. Let `g` be Boolean on a coordinate block `U=A sqcup B`. Let `S` denote its Fourier random set under squared Fourier mass, and put

\[
\operatorname{MI}_g(A:B)=I(S_A;S_B)
=H(S_A)+H(S_B)-H(S_A,S_B).
\]

### Theorem 1 — bidirectional transverse cut

For every coordinate split `U=A sqcup B`,

\[
\boxed{
H(g)+\operatorname{MI}_g(A:B)
\le
\mathbb E_b H(g_b\text{ on }A)
+
\mathbb E_a H(g_a\text{ on }B)
+c_* I(g).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

There is no freshness, independence, symmetry, controller or degree hypothesis.

#### Proof

Apply the newest transverse section theorem to the retained spectral block `A` and physically reveal all of `B`:

\[
H(S_A)\le \mathbb E_bH(g_b)+c_* I_B(g).
\]

Apply the same theorem with the roles exchanged:

\[
H(S_B)\le \mathbb E_aH(g_a)+c_* I_A(g).
\]

Add and use

\[
H(g)=H(S_A,S_B)=H(S_A)+H(S_B)-\operatorname{MI}_g(A:B)
\]

and `I_A+I_B=I`. This proves (3.1). QED.

### Theorem 2 — accumulated dependency-bank partition bound

Let `T` be any full binary tree whose leaves are the active physical coordinates. Each internal node `U` is the disjoint union of its two children `U_0,U_1`. For an outside physical assignment `z` on `[n]\U`, let `f_z` be the resulting Boolean section on `U`. Define

\[
\mathcal M_T(f)
:=
\sum_{U\in\mathrm{Int}(T)}
\mathbb E_z\,
I_{\widehat{f_z}^{\,2}}(S_{U_0};S_{U_1}).
\tag{3.2}
\]

If `d_i` is the depth of coordinate `i`, then

\[
\boxed{
H(f)+\mathcal M_T(f)
\le c_*\sum_i d_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Apply Theorem 1 at the root cut. Apply it recursively to every physical section appearing on each child block, and average with the actual cylinder weights. Every internal section-entropy term cancels with the corresponding parent term. Singleton leaves have Fourier entropy zero, so only the root entropy and the mutual-information rebates remain on the left.

For the influence payment, fix a coordinate `i`. At every ancestor block `U` containing `i`, averaging `Inf_i(f_z)` over assignments outside `U` gives exactly `Inf_i(f)`, because the physical sections partition the original `i`-edges. Coordinate `i` has exactly `d_i` internal ancestors. Hence its total payment is `c_* d_i Inf_i(f)`. Summing over `i` proves (3.3). QED.

This is a genuinely global accumulated allocation/recombination statement: dependent branch information is paid once at the cut where it is created and kept as the nonnegative bank `M_T`.

## 4. Projected Tsallis Jensen data processing and adaptive endpoint payment

For `0<q<1` define bit-normalized Tsallis entropy

\[
T_q(P)=\frac{\sum_xp_x^q-1}{(1-q)\ln2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For distributions `P,Q` on the same finite alphabet, define their Tsallis Jensen gap

\[
J_q(P,Q)=T_q\!\left(\frac{P+Q}{2}\right)-\frac{T_q(P)+T_q(Q)}2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — Tsallis Jensen data processing for `0<q<1`

For every deterministic coarse graining `pi`,

\[
\boxed{J_q(P_\pi,Q_\pi)\le J_q(P,Q).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

It is enough to prove that merging any collection of atoms cannot increase the gap. For one atom pair write

\[
a=m(1+t),\qquad b=m(1-t),\qquad |t|\le1.
\]

The unnormalized Jensen gap is

\[
\phi_q(a,b)
=m^q k_q(t),
\]

where

\[
k_q(t)=1-\frac{(1+t)^q+(1-t)^q}{2}
=\sum_{r\ge1}c_r t^{2r},
\]

and `c_r=-binom(q,2r)>0` for `0<q<1`. Endpoint values follow by continuity.

Now merge pairs `(m_s,t_s)`. Put `m=sum_s m_s` and `t=sum_s(m_s/m)t_s`. For every `r>=1`, convexity of `|x|^{2r}` gives

\[
m^q|t|^{2r}
\le m^{q-1}\sum_sm_s|t_s|^{2r}.
\]

Since `q-1<0` and `m_s<=m`,

\[
m^{q-1}m_s\le m_s^q.
\]

Hence

\[
m^q|t|^{2r}\le\sum_sm_s^q|t_s|^{2r}.
\]

Multiply by `c_r>0`, sum over `r`, then divide by `(1-q)ln2`. Thus the scalar gap after merging is no larger than the sum of the pre-merge gaps. Repeated merging proves (4.3). QED.

### Lemma 4.2 — atom-floor comparison with Shannon Jensen--Shannon

Assume every nonzero atom of `P` and `Q` is at least `delta>0`. Put

\[
b_q=\frac{2^{1-q}-1}{(1-q)\ln2}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
J_q(P,Q)\le b_q\delta^{q-1}\operatorname{JS}(P,Q).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Write the separable entropy functions

\[
h_q(x)=\frac{x^q-x}{(1-q)\ln2},
\qquad
h_1(x)=-x\log_2x.
\]

If `a,b>=delta`, then

\[
\frac{-h_q''(x)}{-h_1''(x)}=q x^{q-1}\le q\delta^{q-1}.
\]

Also `b_q>=1>q`. Therefore `K h_1-h_q` is concave on `[delta,1]` for `K=b_q delta^{q-1}`, and its midpoint Jensen gap is nonnegative. Hence the scalar `h_q` gap is at most `K` times the Shannon gap.

If one endpoint is zero and the other is `a>=delta`, the two scalar midpoint gaps can be computed exactly. Their ratio is

\[
a^{q-1}b_q\le\delta^{q-1}b_q=K.
\]

If both are zero the gap is zero. Summing the coordinatewise inequalities proves (4.4). QED.

### Theorem 3 — adaptive projected endpoint transverse payment

Use the endpoint family already present in P03,

\[
\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)
=\frac n\lambda\left(\sum_S|\widehat f(S)|^{2-\lambda/n}-1\right),
\qquad0<\lambda\le1.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Put

\[
q_n=1-\frac\lambda{2n}.
\]

If `P_f` is the squared Fourier law, then exactly

\[
\Phi_{n,\lambda}(f)=\frac{\ln2}{2}T_{q_n}(P_f).
\tag{4.6}
\]

For a retained spectral coordinate block `A`, define the projected potential

\[
\Phi^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
:=\frac{\ln2}{2}T_{q_n}((P_f)_A)
\]

and the projected debiased potential

\[
G^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
:=\Phi^A_{n,\lambda}(f)-\frac{\ln2}{2}H((P_f)_A).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Let `i notin A`, and let `P,Q` be the full squared Fourier laws of the two physical `i`-children, which live on `n-1` variables. Then for

\[
K_{n,\lambda}
=2^{\lambda(n-1)/n}
\frac{2^{\lambda/(2n)}-1}{(\lambda/(2n))\ln2},
\tag{4.8}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
-\frac{\Phi^A_{n-1,\lambda}(f^+)+\Phi^A_{n-1,\lambda}(f^-)}2
\le K_{n,\lambda}j_i(f)
<2^\lambda j_i(f),
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
G^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
-\frac{G^A_{n-1,\lambda}(f^+)+G^A_{n-1,\lambda}(f^-)}2
\le (K_{n,\lambda}-1)j_i(f)
<(2^\lambda-1)j_i(f).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

#### Proof

A Boolean function on `d=n-1` variables has Fourier coefficients in `2^{-d} Z`. Hence every nonzero atom of each child squared spectrum is at least

\[
\delta=2^{-2d}=2^{-2(n-1)}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The same floor remains true after marginalization, because every nonzero marginal atom is a sum of full atoms.

Apply Lemma 4.2 at parent exponent `q_n`. Since `q_n-1=-lambda/(2n)`,

\[
\delta^{q_n-1}=2^{\lambda(n-1)/n},
\]

which gives exactly `K_{n,lambda}` in (4.8).

Next, `T_q(P)` is decreasing in `q`. Indeed, with `r=1-q` and `a=ln(1/p)>=0`, each summand can be written as

\[
p\,\frac{e^{ra}-1}{r\ln2},
\]

which increases with `r`. Since `q_{n-1}<q_n`, replacing the child entropy at exponent `q_n` by the actual adaptive child exponent `q_{n-1}` can only increase the child term, hence can only **decrease** the parent-minus-child charge. Therefore the adaptive projected `Phi` charge is bounded by the frozen-`q_n` Jensen gap.

By Lemma 4.1, that projected gap is no larger than the full child-law Tsallis Jensen gap. Lemma 4.2 then gives

\[
\Delta_i^A\Phi_{n,\lambda}
\le \frac{\ln2}{2}K_{n,\lambda}\operatorname{JS}(P,Q).
\]

The inherited sharp recombination theorem gives

\[
\operatorname{JS}(P,Q)\le\frac2{\ln2}j_i(f),
\]

so (4.9) follows.

For `G`, the projected Shannon parent-minus-child drop is exactly the projected Jensen--Shannon divergence. Thus

\[
\Delta_i^A G_{n,\lambda}
\le \frac{\ln2}{2}
\bigl(J_{q_n}(P_A,Q_A)-\operatorname{JS}(P_A,Q_A)\bigr)
\]

and Lemma 4.2 gives

\[
\Delta_i^A G_{n,\lambda}
\le\frac{\ln2}{2}(K_{n,\lambda}-1)\operatorname{JS}(P_A,Q_A).
\]

Shannon data processing and sharp recombination then imply (4.10).

Finally, set `r=lambda/(2n)>0`. Since `(e^x-1)/x<e^x` for `x>0`,

\[
\frac{2^r-1}{r\ln2}<2^r.
\]

Therefore

\[
K_{n,\lambda}
<2^{\lambda(n-1)/n+\lambda/(2n)}
=2^{\lambda(1-1/(2n))}
<2^\lambda.
\]

This completes the proof. QED.

### Corollary 3.1 — complete transverse block bank

Let `Y` be disjoint from retained block `A`, and reveal all coordinates of `Y` in any complete adaptive physical prefix. Summing (4.9)--(4.10) over the actual section tree and using `j_i<=Inf_i` plus exact edge partitioning gives

\[
\boxed{
\Phi^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
\le
\mathbb E_y\Phi_{|A|,\lambda}(f_y)
+2^\lambda I_Y(f),
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

\[
\boxed{
G^A_{n,\lambda}(f)
\le
\mathbb E_yG_{|A|,\lambda}(f_y)
+(2^\lambda-1)I_Y(f).
}
\tag{4.13}
\]

At `lambda=1`, the entire transverse **debiased** endpoint surcharge is paid with coefficient strictly below `1`. This is substantially smaller than paying Shannon recombination and endpoint charge independently.

### Why this still does not prove FEI

The theorem is transverse: `i notin A`. A complete FEI recursion must eventually query coordinates that still belong to the spectral object whose orientation/allocation entropy is being controlled. The known every-coordinate endpoint obstruction is therefore untouched. Equations (4.9)--(4.13) isolate the remaining problem rather than solve it.

The proof uses a new Tsallis Jensen data-processing lemma plus the dyadic Boolean Fourier atom floor. Separately, arbitrary repeated transverse cuts compile into a global mutual-information dependency bank with an exact weighted-depth influence payment.

These mechanisms remove fresh-control/dependency assumptions for the **transverse recombination part**. They do not remove the orientation/allocation obstruction inside the retained block.

## 7. Remaining full-scope gap and next legitimate target

Full FEI would follow from a genuinely hereditary dimension-free rule that, at every nonconstant section, chooses a proper block `A` so that the **inside-block orientation/allocation charge** plus the now-paid transverse recombination/debiased surcharge is `O(I_A)` and remains stable in descendants.

Equivalent productive next targets include:

1. prove a proper-block selector whose residual orientation charge is bounded by the dependency bank created by complementary cuts;
2. prove a global cancellation theorem coupling `M_T` to the accumulated endpoint orientation charge so that the Huffman/profile entropy term disappears;
3. combine the projected debiased theorem (4.13) with the current hybrid compiler to show that some proper block has bounded **inside** charge, not every coordinate;
4. find a new all-dimensional obstruction family on which every proper block simultaneously has large inside charge after the transverse term has been removed.

What does **not** count as closure: another fixed subclass constant, another fresh-control construction, a finite checker success, or a representation theorem that assumes the sought paid partition exists.

**Final scope: OPEN / PARTIAL.** The new theorems are all-dimensional and dependency-free in their stated transverse/partition scopes, but no universal FEI constant is proved.
