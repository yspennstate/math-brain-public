# Approach 1 — B18/B12: a tree-optimized logarithmic allocation equivalent to FEI

The root functional in the archived direct route is

\[
K(f)=\sum_i k_i(f),
\qquad
k_i(f)=\sum_T\min(a_T,b_T)\left|\log_2\frac{a_T}{b_T}\right|.
\]
A zero endpoint contributes zero. The failure of `k_i` to decrease under restriction does not preclude optimizing its **accumulated** cost over the restriction tree.

Define also the exact spectral-posterior Bayes error

\[
\eta_i(f)=\sum_T\min(a_T,b_T).
\tag{1.1}
\]
It is the minimum probability of guessing `Z_i` incorrectly from `Z_{-i}`.

## Lemma 1 (exact logarithmic decomposition with posterior remainder)

For every Boolean `f` and every physical coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{k_i(f)\le B_i(f)\le k_i(f)+\frac2{\ln2}\eta_i(f),}
\tag{1.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\eta_i(f)\le j_i(f):=\min\{\operatorname{Inf}_i(f),1-\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Proof

It is enough to work on one spectral fiber. Assume `a>=b>0`. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
e(a,b)
&=a\log_2(1+b/a)+b\log_2((a+b)/b)\\
&=b\log_2(a/b)+(a+b)\log_2(1+b/a).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first term is the fiber contribution to `k_i`; the second is nonnegative. Since `log_2(1+x)<=x/ln2` and `b<=a`,

\[
0\le e(a,b)-b\log_2(a/b)
\le\frac{a+b}{\ln2}\frac ba
\le\frac{2b}{\ln2}.
\]
The case `b>=a` is symmetric, and a zero endpoint follows by continuity. Summing proves (1.2).

Moreover

\[
\eta_i=\sum_T\min(a_T,b_T)
\le\min\left\{\sum_Ta_T,\sum_Tb_T\right\}
=\min\{1-\operatorname{Inf}_i,\operatorname{Inf}_i\}=j_i.
\]
QED.

The one-defect report shows exactly why keeping the fiber ratio matters: `h_2(j_i)` can be a factor of order dimension too large, while (1.2) retains the predictive information in `Z_{-i}`.

More explicitly, a universal `K_*<=C I` would give

### Proof

K_T\le R_T\le K_T+\frac2{\ln2}I(f).

H\le R_T+\frac2{\ln2}I
\le K_*+\frac4{\ln2}I,

proving (1.6). If FEI holds, `K_*<=H<=CI`; conversely (1.8) proves FEI. QED.

## Theorem 4 (Bellman recursion and exact tensorization)

For every nonconstant Boolean `f`,

\[
\boxed{K_*(f)=\min_{i\text{ relevant}}\left[k_i(f)+\frac12K_*(f_{i=+1})+\frac12K_*(f_{i=-1})\right],}
\tag{1.9}
\]
with `K_*(constant)=0`.

For Boolean `f,g` on disjoint coordinate blocks,

\[
\boxed{K_*(f\otimes g)=K_*(f)+K_*(g).}
\tag{1.10}
\]
Thus tensor powers cannot amplify `K_*/I`.

### Proof

The Bellman equation follows by splitting any full tree at its root and attaching optimal child trees, exactly as for `R_*`.

For tensorization, the squared Fourier law of `f_a\otimes g_b` is a product at every pair of physical restrictions. If `i` lies in the `f` block, each spectral `i`-fiber has masses `q_Ua_T,q_Ub_T`, where `q_U` is a mass from the `g` block. Since

\[
k(q a,q b)=q\,k(a,b),
\]
summing over `U` gives

\[
k_i(f_a\otimes g_b)=k_i(f_a).
\tag{1.11}
\]
Query an optimal `f` tree first and an optimal `g` tree at its leaves to obtain the upper bound in (1.10).

For the lower bound, take any full tree for `f\otimes g`. Fix a complete physical assignment `eta` of the `g` block and prune every `g` query to its `eta` branch while retaining both branches at each `f` query. Because the final product is constant and the fully assigned `g` factor is a sign, every resulting leaf has constant `f`; hence this is a full tree for `f`. Identity (1.11) shows that averaging its cost over uniform `eta` reproduces exactly the contribution of all `f`-block query nodes in the original tree: the probability that `eta` matches the queried `g` prefix supplies precisely the omitted cylinder-weight factor. Each pruned tree costs at least `K_*(f)`, so the original `f`-query contribution is at least `K_*(f)`. Reverse the two blocks for the `g` contribution. Their sum is the total tree cost, proving the lower bound. QED.

## Theorem 5 (exact posterior-error obstruction)

On `2m` variables let

\[
g_m(y)=\prod_{r=1}^m\frac{1+y_{2r-1}+y_{2r}-y_{2r-1}y_{2r}}2.
\]
This is Boolean and bent: every Fourier coefficient has magnitude `2^{-m}`. Let `d` be the indicator of the all-positive input, put `epsilon=2^{-2m}`, and let `h_0=g_m-2d`, which is Boolean. Add a selector sign `z` and set

\[
F_m(y,z)=g_m(y)-d(y)+z\,d(y).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Then for `m>=2`,

\[
\boxed{\eta_z(F_m)=\operatorname{Inf}_z(F_m)=\epsilon,}
\tag{2.2}
\]
while

\[
\boxed{\frac{k_z(F_m)}{\eta_z(F_m)}\ge2\log_2(2^m-1)\ge2m-2.}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Therefore no finite universal constant bounds `k_i/eta_i` or `B_i/eta_i` coordinatewise.

### Proof

The two selector branches are `g_m` and `h_0`; they differ only at the marked point, so `Inf_z=epsilon`. The Fourier coefficients in each selector pair are

\[
a_T=\widehat g_m(T)-\epsilon,\qquad b_T=\epsilon.
\tag{2.4}
\]
There are `2^{2m}=1/epsilon` labels `T`. Since `|ghat_m(T)|=2^{-m}` and `m>=2`,

\[
|a_T|\ge2^{-m}-\epsilon>\epsilon.
\]
Thus the smaller squared mass in every selector fiber is exactly `epsilon^2`, and

\[
\eta_z=(1/\epsilon)\epsilon^2=\epsilon.
\]
For every fiber,

\[
\frac{|a_T|}{\epsilon}\ge2^m-1.
\]
Hence its logarithmic contrast in squared masses is at least `2log_2(2^m-1)`. Summing the `1/epsilon` fibers, each with smaller mass `epsilon^2`, gives (2.3). Finally `B_z>=k_z` by Lemma 1. QED.

This reinterprets the archived butterfly mismatch as a posterior obstruction: even **optimal prediction error** of the queried spectral bit is too coarse. A successful universal payment must retain the distribution of likelihood ratios across the fibers, not merely the total error mass.

## Lemma 7 (exact logarithmic selector cost)

Let `g` be Boolean, `c in {-1,+1}`, add selector bit `z`, and define

\[
F(y,z)=\begin{cases}g(y),&z=+1,\\c,&z=-1.\end{cases}
\]
Put `delta=P[g!=c]` and `j=min(delta,1-delta)`. Then

\[
\boxed{k_z(F)=2j^2\log_2\frac{1-j}{j},}
\tag{3.3}
\]
with the endpoint values interpreted as zero, and

\[
\boxed{k_z(F)\le\frac{2}{e\ln2}\,j\le\frac{2}{e\ln2}\,\delta.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

For every nonempty old Fourier label `T`, the two `z`-pair coefficients are both `ghat(T)/2`, so their squared masses are equal and contribute zero logarithmic contrast. At the empty old label the coefficients are `(Eg+c)/2` and `(Eg-c)/2`. Since `Eg=c(1-2delta)`, their squared masses are `(1-delta)^2` and `delta^2`; the smaller is `j^2`, giving (3.3).

For `0<j<=1/2`,

\[
k_z=\frac{2j^2}{\ln2}\ln\frac{1-j}{j}
\le\frac{2j^2}{\ln2}\ln\frac1j.
\]
The elementary maximum `x ln(1/x)<=1/e` yields (3.4). QED.

For the selector above,

\[
\boxed{K_*(F)\le k_z(F)+\frac12K_*(g),\qquad
I(F)=\delta+\frac12I(g).}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Consequently, if

\[
K_*(g)\le C I(g),\qquad C\ge\frac{2}{e\ln2}=1.0614756908\ldots,

then

\boxed{K_*(F)\le C I(F).}
\tag{3.6}

### Proof

\[

QED.
