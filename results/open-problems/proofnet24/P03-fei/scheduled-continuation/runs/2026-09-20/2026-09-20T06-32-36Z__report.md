## new_mathematical_delta

### Approach B12 — a global logarithmic payment for the *entire* accumulated allocation

The point of this route is to pay \(\mathcal T\) directly by a root quantity, not by a first-step resolvent and not by an independence substitution.

For a physical coordinate \(i\), define
\[
D_i f(x)=f(x)f(x+e_i).
\]

#### Theorem 1 (universal accumulated-allocation log envelope)

For every Boolean function in every dimension,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T(f)
\le
2\sum_i j_i(f)\log_2\frac1{j_i(f)}
}
\tag{1}
\]
with the zero terms interpreted as zero.

This is a genuine all-dimensional bound on the same \(\mathcal T\) that is FEI-equivalent at the level of a dimension-free \(CJ\) estimate. The right side of (1) still has a logarithmic loss and therefore **does not prove FEI**.

#### Proof

Fix a Boolean \(g\) and draw independent spectral samples \(S,T\sim p_g\). Given \((U,W)=(S\triangle T,S\cap T)\), every coordinate with \(U_i=0\) has \(S_i=W_i\) and is determined. Only coordinates in \(U\) can carry an orientation bit. Hence the conditional support of \(S\) has size at most \(2^{|U|}\), giving
\[
\mathfrak A(g)=H(S\mid U,W)\le \mathbf E|U|.
\]
If \(\alpha_i=\Pr(i\in S)=\operatorname{Inf}_i(g)\), independence of the two *original* samples gives
\[
\Pr(U_i=1)=2\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i)=2j_i(g)(1-j_i(g)).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A(g)
\le2\sum_i j_i(g)(1-j_i(g))
=\frac12\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(D_i g).
}
\tag{2}
\]
The last identity uses
\[
\mathbf E D_i g=1-2\alpha_i,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(D_i g)=4\alpha_i(1-\alpha_i).
\]

Now use the exact physical commutation identity
\[
D_i(g(x)g(x+z))
=
D_i g(x)\,D_i g(x+z).
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, along the actual derivative chain, \(D_i f_k\) is the multiplicative-derivative chain of \(D_i f\), driven by the projected versions of the same uniform shifts. Since \(D_i f\) is invariant in coordinate \(i\), those projections are uniform and independent on the quotient. Thus summing (2) with the actual \(2^{-k}\) weights gives
\[
\mathcal T(f)
\le\frac12\sum_i \mathcal V(D_i f).
\tag{4}
\]

For \(g=D_i f\),
\[
\frac{1-|\mathbf E g|}{2}
=
\frac{1-|1-2\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)|}{2}
=j_i(f).
\]
Apply the scalar variance theorem to each \(D_i f\):
\[
\mathcal V(D_i f)\le4j_i(f)\log_2\frac1{j_i(f)}.
\]
Substitution into (4) proves (1). QED.

No shared-shift mistake occurs: the only independent pair is the defining pair \(S,T\) for the allocation of one fixed function \(g\). Descendant functions are generated by their genuine common physical shifts, and (3) is pointwise.

Combining (1) with the already proved upper comparison gives the explicit universal, but logarithmically non-FEI, consequence
\[
H(f)\le
2\sum_i j_i(f)\log_2\frac1{j_i(f)}
+\left(5+\frac5{2\ln2}\right)J(f).
\tag{5}
\]
On one-point defects and small majorities the checker confirms the expected log slack; on nondegenerate quadratics \(\mathcal T=n/2\) while the right side of (1) is \(n\). Thus (1) correctly survives the variance obstruction but does not remove the final logarithm.

### Approach B20 — why the naive three-flat density proxy fails exactly

For an affine flat \(A\), write
\[
\sigma_A=1-2\mathbf1_A.
\]
The previous two-flat theorem could use positive codimension-density proxies because two nonidentical affine flats do not admit a three-way parity cancellation. At three flats there is an exact new obstruction.

Let \(A\) be any affine flat and let \(B,C\) be its two complementary affine hyperplane sections, so
\[
A=B\sqcup C.
\]
Then over \(\mathbb F_2\),
\[
\mathbf1_A=\mathbf1_B+\mathbf1_C,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{\sigma_A\sigma_B\sigma_C\equiv1.}
\tag{6}
\]
Any raw proxy such as
\[
\sum_{R\in\{A,B,C\}}\operatorname{codim}(R)\,2^{-\operatorname{codim}(R)}
\]
is strictly positive, whereas the product has zero entropy and zero influence. Hence no universal positive lower payment from that unreduced raw proxy to \(I(\sigma_A\sigma_B\sigma_C)\) is possible.

This is **not** an FEI obstruction. It says that a three-flat compiler must first respect exact affine-flat parity relations rather than iterate the two-flat proxy blindly. The checker verifies (6) in a nested sequence through ambient dimension eight.

### Approach B25 — exact three-flat density dichotomy after parity reduction

The cancellation in (6) suggests studying the actual negative set
\[
D=A\triangle B\triangle C.
\]

#### Lemma 2 (three distinct affine flats cannot have an arbitrarily tiny nonzero XOR)

Let \(A,B,C\) be three pairwise distinct nonempty affine flats in \(\mathbb F_2^n\). Put
\[
x=\max\{\mu(A),\mu(B),\mu(C)\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
D=A\triangle B\triangle C=\varnothing
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mu(D)\ge x/4.
}
\tag{7}
\]
The factor \(1/4\) is attained in dimensions \(2,3,4\) by exact enumeration.

#### Proof

Choose \(A\) with \(\mu(A)=x\). Since \(B,C\) are distinct from \(A\) and no larger, \(P=A\cap B\) and \(R=A\cap C\) are either empty or proper affine subflats of \(A\), each of relative density at most \(1/2\).

Inside \(A\),
\[
D\cap A=A\setminus(P\triangle R).
\tag{8}
\]
Two proper affine subflats \(P,R\subset A\) satisfy the following elementary dichotomy:
\[
P\triangle R=A
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mu_A(A\setminus(P\triangle R))\ge1/4.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed, if either has relative density at most \(1/4\), their symmetric difference has relative density at most \(3/4\). Otherwise both must be affine hyperplanes of \(A\). Equal hyperplanes have symmetric difference zero; nonparallel hyperplanes intersect in relative density \(1/4\) and have symmetric difference \(1/2\); the only way to obtain all of \(A\) is that they are the two complementary parallel hyperplanes.

If the second alternative in (9) holds, (8) gives \(\mu(D)\ge x/4\).

It remains to consider the exceptional case in which \(P,R\) are complementary hyperplanes partitioning \(A\). If both \(B,C\subset A\), then \(B=P,C=R\) and \(D=\varnothing\). If exactly one extends outside \(A\), that flat has density \(x\) and contributes \(x/2\) outside \(A\), so \(\mu(D)\ge x/2\).

Finally suppose both extend outside \(A\). Since \(\mu(P)=\mu(R)=x/2\) and \(P,R\) are proper sections of \(B,C\), maximality of \(A\) forces
\[
\mu(B)=\mu(C)=x.
\]
For two distinct equal-size affine flats, \(B\cap C\) is empty or has density \(x/2,x/4,\ldots\). Hence either
\[
\mu(B\triangle C)=x
\]
(the \(x/2\)-intersection case), in which case the already-known inside contribution has size \(x\), so there is no outside contribution and \(B\triangle C=A\), yielding \(D=\varnothing\); or
\[
\mu(B\triangle C)\ge3x/2,
\]
so its outside-\(A\) contribution is at least \(x/2\), again giving \(\mu(D)\ge x/2\). This proves (7). QED.

The exact checker enumerates every affine flat and every unordered distinct triple through ambient dimension four: 4,775,385 triples in dimension four alone. The minimum nonzero ratio is exactly \(1/4\).

## A new dependent three-flat FEI class

The density lemma is enough to build an actual entropy estimate when all three surviving flats are sparse.

### Lemma 3 (character times three codimension-at-least-six flat signs)

Let \(A_1,A_2,A_3\) be pairwise distinct affine flats, each of codimension at least six, and
\[
h=\sigma_{A_1}\sigma_{A_2}\sigma_{A_3}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{H(h)\le4800\,J(h)=4800\,I(h).}
\tag{10}
\]
If exact cancellations reduce the product to at most two flat signs, the earlier one-/two-flat theorems apply instead.

#### Proof

Let
\[
a=\min_r\operatorname{codim}(A_r),\qquad x=2^{-a}.
\]
The negative set is \(D=A_1\triangle A_2\triangle A_3\), with density \(\delta\). If \(D=\varnothing\), the assertion is trivial. Otherwise Lemma 2 gives
\[
x/4\le\delta\le3x.
\tag{11}
\]

Expand
\[
h
=
1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}
(-2)^{|T|}\mathbf1_{A_T},
\qquad
A_T=\bigcap_{r\in T}A_r,
\tag{12}
\]
discarding empty intersections. If \(z_T=\mu(A_T)=2^{-r_T}\), then \(r_T\ge a\). For a nonzero Fourier label \(\xi\), each affine-flat indicator contributes either zero or an amplitude of magnitude \(z_T\). Therefore
\[
|\widehat h(\xi)|
\le
s_\xi:=
\sum_{T:\,\xi\in W_T}2^{|T|}z_T
\le
(3\cdot2+3\cdot4+8)x
=26x
\le13/32.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus both \(\widehat h(\xi)^2\) and \(s_\xi^2\) lie below \(1/e\); the function \(-t\log_2t\) is increasing there. If the at most seven nonzero summands in \(s_\xi\) are \(x_1,\ldots,x_R\), then
\[
s_\xi^2\le R\sum_r x_r^2,\qquad
\log_2(1/s_\xi^2)\le \log_2(1/x_r^2)
\]
for every nonzero \(x_r\). Hence
\[
-\widehat h(\xi)^2\log_2\widehat h(\xi)^2
\le
7\sum_r x_r^2\log_2(1/x_r^2).
\tag{14}
\]

For a term with \(|T|=t\), summing its contribution over the \(2^{r_T}=1/z_T\) frequencies in its annihilator gives
\[
4^t z_T(2r_T-2t)
\le2\cdot4^t r_Tz_T.
\tag{15}
\]
Because \(r2^{-r}\) decreases for \(r\ge2\),
\[
r_Tz_T\le ax.
\]
Summing (14)--(15) over the seven possible nonempty subsets and using
\[
3\cdot4+3\cdot16+64=124
\]
gives the nonzero-frequency entropy bound
\[
\sum_{\xi\ne0}
-\widehat h(\xi)^2\log_2\widehat h(\xi)^2
\le1736\,a x.
\tag{16}
\]

The zero-frequency mass is \(p_0=(1-2\delta)^2\). Since
\[
-p_0\log_2p_0\le\frac{1-p_0}{\ln2}
=\frac{4\delta(1-\delta)}{\ln2}
\le\frac{12x}{\ln2}
\le\frac{2}{\ln2}a x,
\tag{17}
\]
we obtain
\[
H(h)\le\left(1736+\frac2{\ln2}\right)a x.
\tag{18}
\]

Edge isoperimetry applied to the negative set and (11) gives
\[
I(h)\ge2\delta\log_2(1/\delta)
\ge\frac{x}{2}\bigl(a-\log_2 3\bigr).
\tag{19}
\]
Now \(\ln2>2/3\), and \(3^5<2^8\) gives \(\log_2 3<8/5\). For \(a\ge6\),
\[
\frac{H(h)}{I(h)}
<
1739\cdot \frac{2a}{a-8/5}
\le1739\cdot\frac{30}{11}
<4800.
\tag{20}
\]

Finally, a flat sign of codimension \(r\) has influence either zero or \(2^{1-r}\) in any physical coordinate. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Inf}_i(h)
\le3\cdot2^{-5}=3/32<1/2
\]
for every \(i\), so \(J(h)=I(h)\). This proves (10). QED.

The constant 4800 is intentionally crude; reducing it is not the research target.

### Theorem 4 (arbitrary quadratic coupling to three high-codimension dependent flats)

Let \(Q\) be an arbitrary quadratic polynomial on the same physical variables. Let \(A_1,A_2,A_3\) be arbitrary affine flats of codimension at least six; duplicate factors are cancelled first, so there are at most three surviving signs. Then
\[
\boxed{
H\!\left((-1)^Q\prod_{r=1}^3\sigma_{A_r}\right)
\le4803\,
J\!\left((-1)^Q\prod_{r=1}^3\sigma_{A_r}\right)
\le4803\,I\!\left((-1)^Q\prod_{r=1}^3\sigma_{A_r}\right).
}
\tag{21}
\]
No disjointness, nesting, fresh-variable, or joint-surjectivity assumption is made among the three flats. The codimension-at-least-six hypothesis is essential to this proof.

#### Proof

Cases with at most two surviving flat signs are covered by the preceding one-/two-flat theorems, so take three distinct signs and put \(h=\prod_r\sigma_{A_r}\). Its negative density obeys
\[
\delta\le3/64<1/8.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(\rho\) be the rank of the alternating polar matrix of \(Q\). The rare-base quadratic entropy theorem is therefore applicable and gives
\[
H((-1)^Qh)\le H(h)+\rho.
\tag{23}
\]

For each coordinate \(i\), \(j_i(h)=\operatorname{Inf}_i(h)\le3/32\). If the \(i\)-th polar row of \(Q\) vanishes, the quadratic derivative in direction \(e_i\) is constant, so multiplication by \((-1)^Q\) can only complement the directional influence and
\[
j_i((-1)^Qh)=j_i(h).
\tag{24}
\]
If that polar row is nonzero, the rare-base directional theorem gives
\[
j_i((-1)^Qh)\ge\frac12-2\delta\ge\frac12-\frac3{32}=\frac{13}{32}>j_i(h).
\tag{25}
\]
Hence
\[
J((-1)^Qh)\ge J(h).
\tag{26}
\]
The matrix rank \(\rho\) is at most the number of its nonzero physical rows, and each such row contributes at least \(13/32\) to the folded influence in (25). Therefore
\[
\rho\le\frac{32}{13}J((-1)^Qh).
\tag{27}
\]
Combining Lemma 3 and (23), (26), (27),
\[
H((-1)^Qh)
\le4800J(h)+\rho
\le\left(4800+\frac{32}{13}\right)J((-1)^Qh)
<4803J((-1)^Qh).
\]
This proves (21). QED.

This is a real enlargement of the preceding dependent-recombination class: the earlier theorem allowed two arbitrary affine-flat signs under arbitrary quadratic coupling with no codimension restriction; the present result handles a third fully dependent sign in the sparse regime. It still does not handle an arbitrary third low-codimension flat.

1. **Global allocation:** the entire actual derivative-chain allocation has the proved root logarithmic payment (1). This does not solve \(\mathcal T\le CJ\), but it converts the missing global quantity into a precise marginal-log loss without any false shared-shift independence.
2. **Obstruction:** a raw positive three-flat codimension-density proxy is decisively invalid because complementary sibling flats cancel the parent sign exactly.
3. **Dependent recombination:** after respecting parity cancellation, three distinct affine flats obey the sharp-order nonzero-density dichotomy (7), yielding the first proved arbitrary-quadratic theorem here for three fully dependent affine-flat signs in the codimension-at-least-six regime.

This is not a renamed FEI equivalence or a smaller auxiliary constant. The remaining difficulty is exactly the removal of the logarithm in (1) or the removal of the low-codimension/dependency gap in Theorem 4.

## remaining_full_scope_gap

**Status: OPEN / PARTIAL.**

Full FEI still requires one finite universal constant for **every** Boolean function in **every** dimension.

The two concrete remaining gaps exposed here are:

1. The allocation theorem (1) pays \(j_i\log(1/j_i)\), not \(O(j_i)\). One-point/bent-background phenomena show that a coordinatewise removal of this logarithm is impossible by naive local entropy continuity. A future proof has to pool this logarithmic debt globally/hereditarily or exploit signed Boolean constraints.
2. The three-flat theorem requires every surviving flat to have codimension at least six. Low-codimension affine indicators can interact coherently with the quadratic phase and with the other two flats. The previous two-flat theorem solves every two-factor leaf, but there is no proved compiler here reducing an arbitrary low-codimension third factor to those leaves at bounded payment.

A next useful run should therefore attack one of:
- a *pooled* version of (1) that replaces \(\sum j_i\log(1/j_i)\) by a paid global quantity already known to be \(O(J)\), without reviving the refuted \(Q_{\log}\)/resolvent pointwise targets; or
- the low-codimension third-flat case by exact parity reduction plus a bounded-deletion/stopping argument whose leaves are the existing two-flat theorem.

The source is not closed, and no independent validation has been recorded for the new theorems in this report.
