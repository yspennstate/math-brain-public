## 4. New theorem: arbitrary affine-block recombination

Work on `G=F_2^n`. Let

\[
\pi:G\to F_2^k
\]

be any surjective linear map, let `K=ker(pi)`, and let `K^perp` be its annihilator in the Fourier dual. For each `a in F_2^k`, write `f_a` for the restriction of a Boolean `f:G->{-1,1}` to the affine fibre `pi(x)=a`, regarded as a sign function on the affine copy of `K`. Fourier entropy on a fibre means entropy of the squared Fourier coefficients over the character group of `K`; it is independent of how those characters are labeled.

Let `S` have the parent squared Fourier law

\[
P(S=s)=\widehat f(s)^2.
\]

Define the block allocation

\[
\boxed{
B_\pi(f):=H(S\mid S+K^\perp).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the block entropy decrement

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_\pi H(f):=H(f)-2^{-k}\sum_{a\in F_2^k}H(f_a).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — affine-block sandwich

For every Boolean `f`, every dimension `n`, and every surjective `pi:G->F_2^k`,

\[
\boxed{
B_\pi(f)\le\Delta_\pi H(f)\le2B_\pi(f).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

No fresh variables, control structure, independence, density bound, quadratic phase, or derivative character condition is assumed.

For a physical coordinate block `A subseteq [n]`, take `pi(x)=x_A`. Then

\[
B_\pi(f)=H(S_A\mid S_{A^c}),
\]

so (4.3) is an ordinary fixed-cube physical-block theorem.

The upper factor two is sharp already for `k=1` by the controlled-character example `(1,1,1,-1)` from the newest P03 report. The lower factor one is also sharp, for example when the queried block is the whole cube: then the children are constants, `B_\pi=H(f)=Delta_\pi H`.

## 5. Full proof

### 5.1 Fourier coset decomposition

Choose one representative `rho` for each coset of `K^perp` in the dual group. Since `pi` is surjective, every element of `K^perp` is uniquely `pi^*b` with `b in F_2^k`. On a fixed coset `C=rho+K^perp`, set

\[
u_C(b)=\widehat f(\rho+\pi^*b),\qquad
w_C=\sum_bu_C(b)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

When `w_C>0`, define

\[
p_C(b)=\frac{u_C(b)^2}{w_C}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The coset masses sum to one. Therefore the parent entropy decomposes exactly as

\[
H(f)=H((w_C)_C)+\sum_Cw_CH(p_C).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The second term is precisely

\[
B_\pi(f)=\sum_Cw_CH(p_C).
\tag{5.4}
\]

### 5.2 Exact child transform

Choose `x_a` with `pi(x_a)=a`. For the character of `K` obtained by restricting `rho`, Fourier expansion and orthogonality on `K` give

\[
\widehat{f_a}(\rho|_K)
=(-1)^{\rho(x_a)}
\sum_{b\in F_2^k}u_C(b)(-1)^{a\cdot b}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The prefactor has modulus one and disappears after squaring. Thus, on every parent coset, the vector of the `2^k` child amplitudes is the unnormalized Walsh transform of `u_C`.

Put `d=2^k` and let `W_d` be the normalized Walsh matrix with entries `d^{-1/2}(-1)^{a.b}`. For `w_C>0`, set

\[
x_C=u_C/\sqrt{w_C},\qquad y_C=W_dx_C,
\]

and

\[
q_C(a)=y_C(a)^2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then `p_C=x_C^2` and `q_C=y_C^2` are probability distributions. If `c_{C,a}` is the child coefficient in (5.5), then

\[
c_{C,a}^2=d\,w_Cq_C(a).
\tag{5.7}
\]

### 5.3 Exact block entropy identity

Average the child entropies over all `a`. For one coset `C`, (5.7) gives

\[
\frac1d\sum_a -c_{C,a}^2\log_2 c_{C,a}^2
=w_C\bigl[-\log_2w_C-k+H(q_C)\bigr].
\]

Summing over cosets,

\[
2^{-k}\sum_aH(f_a)
=H((w_C)_C)-k+\sum_Cw_CH(q_C).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Subtracting (5.8) from (5.3),

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_\pi H(f)
=\sum_Cw_C\,[H(p_C)+k-H(q_C)].
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This is the exact dependent-block analogue of the one-coordinate `B+JS` identity. No two children from an averaged derivative law have been replaced by independent samples; the `2^k` child amplitudes in (5.5) come from the same parent Fourier fibre.

### 5.4 Walsh entropic uncertainty in every block dimension

For completeness, prove the needed uncertainty relation rather than import it. The normalized Walsh matrix satisfies

\[
\|W_d\|_{1\to\infty}=d^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|W_d\|_{2\to2}=1.
\]

For `0<=s<=1`, set

\[
p(s)=\frac2{1+s},\qquad q(s)=\frac2{1-s}.
\]

Riesz–Thorin gives

\[
\|W_dx\|_{q(s)}\le d^{-s/2}\|x\|_{p(s)}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

For a unit vector `z`,

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dr}\log\|z\|_r\right|_{r=2}
=\frac14\sum_jz_j^2\ln z_j^2
=-\frac14H_{\rm nat}(z^2).
\tag{5.11}
\]

Take logarithms in (5.10), move the right side to the left, and differentiate at `s=0+`, where equality holds. Since `p'(0)=-2` and `q'(0)=2`, the right derivative inequality is

\[
\frac{\ln d-H_{\rm nat}(x^2)-H_{\rm nat}((W_dx)^2)}2\le0.
\]

Hence, in bits,

\[
\boxed{H(p_C)+H(q_C)\ge\log_2d=k.}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is the arbitrary-`2^k` extension of the flat-transform uncertainty mechanism seen in the older four-point P03 report.

### 5.5 Finish the sandwich

Because `q_C` is supported on `d=2^k` points,

\[
H(q_C)\le k.
\]

Therefore every bracket in (5.9) obeys

\[
H(p_C)+k-H(q_C)\ge H(p_C).
\tag{5.13}
\]

By (5.12),

\[
k-H(q_C)\le H(p_C),
\]
so

\[
H(p_C)+k-H(q_C)\le2H(p_C).
\tag{5.14}
\]

Multiply (5.13)–(5.14) by `w_C>=0`, sum over `C`, and use (5.4). This proves (4.3). QED.

## 6. Block-tree compiler

The theorem composes without any dependency assumption. Consider an adaptive tree whose internal node `v` queries a nonempty block of independent affine bits on the current affine cube, producing all corresponding children. Let `w_v` be the cylinder probability of the node and let `B_v` be its block quantity (4.1). Define

\[
R_{\rm blk}(\mathcal T)=\sum_{v\text{ internal}}w_vB_v.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The entropy decrements telescope exactly:

\[
H(f)-H_L
=\sum_vw_v\Delta_vH,
\tag{6.2}
\]

where `H_L` is the weighted leaf entropy. Applying Theorem 4.1 nodewise gives

\[
\boxed{
R_{\rm blk}(\mathcal T)
\le H(f)-H_L
\le2R_{\rm blk}(\mathcal T).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a full block tree with constant leaves,

\[
\boxed{R_{\rm blk}(\mathcal T)\le H(f)\le2R_{\rm blk}(\mathcal T).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Physical coordinate trees are the special case of one-coordinate blocks, recovering the newest P03 theorem. The new statement allows fully dependent multi-coordinate or affine blocks and charges them directly to the parent block conditional spectral entropy, rather than summing independent-child surrogates.

For FEI itself one should still use physical block queries if the payment is expressed in the original physical influence metric. General affine queries may change the geometry of physical influence; no free affine-invariance of `I(f)` is asserted.
