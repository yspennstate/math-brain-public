# Approach 1 — B11/B12: derivative-spectral entropy and an exact restriction supermartingale

The square-curvature report pays the dangerous `j log(1/j)` behavior by the **boundary size** of the physical derivative. Here I keep the Fourier information of that derivative instead.

For a coordinate `i`, define the physical multiplicative derivative on the residual cube

\[
D_i f(x_{-i})=f(+1,x_{-i})f(-1,x_{-i}).
\]

Define the first derivative-spectral entropy

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_1(f):=\sum_i H(D_i f).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is not the entropy of an averaged derivative spectrum. Every `D_i f` is a fixed physical derivative, so there is no forbidden replacement of two same-shift samples by independent samples from an averaged law.

## Theorem 1 — local derivative-entropy payment

For every Boolean `f` and physical coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{
B_i(f)\le\frac14H(D_i f)+4j_i(f).
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Proof

Multiplying `D_i f` by `-1` changes no squared Fourier coefficient. Its minority density is therefore

\[
p=j_i(f)\in[0,1/2].
\]

The constant Fourier coefficient of a sign function with minority density `p` has squared mass

\[
a=(1-2p)^2,
\]

so the total nonconstant Fourier mass is

\[
r=1-a=4p(1-p).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Grouping the squared Fourier distribution into the constant atom and all nonconstant atoms gives, by the entropy chain rule,

\[
H(D_i f)\ge h_2(r).
\tag{1.4}
\]

On the other hand conditioning lowers entropy and the spectral marginal at `i` is `Inf_i(f)`, hence

\[
B_i(f)\le h_2(p).
\tag{1.5}
\]

It remains to prove the scalar inequality

\[
h_2(p)\le\frac14h_2(4p(1-p))+4p,
\qquad 0\le p\le\frac12.
\tag{1.6}
\]

If `p>=1/4`, then `h_2(p)<=1<=4p`. Suppose `p<=1/4`. Put `r=4p(1-p)`. We use

\[
h_2(p)\le p\log_2(1/p)+\frac p{\ln2},
\qquad
h_2(r)\ge r\log_2(1/r).
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
h_2(p)-\frac14h_2(r)
&\le p^2\log_2(1/p)
+p(1-p)\log_2(4(1-p))
+\frac p{\ln2}\\
&\le \frac p2+2p+\frac{3p}{2}=4p.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here `p log_2(1/p)<=1/2` on `[0,1/4]`, `log_2(4(1-p))<=2`, and `1/ln2<3/2`. This proves (1.6), hence (1.2). QED.

The constant four is deliberately coarse. This run does not spend effort optimizing it because a smaller auxiliary scalar constant does not address the full-scope gap.

## Theorem 2 — `E_1` is a physical-restriction supermartingale

For every split coordinate `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(f)
\ge
H(D_k f)+\frac{\mathcal E_1(f^+)+\mathcal E_1(f^-)}2.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

Consequently every adaptive physical restriction tree satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(f)
\ge
\mathcal E_T+\mathcal E_L,
}
\tag{1.8}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal E_T=\sum_{v\in T^\circ}w_v H(D_{i(v)}f_v),
\qquad
\mathcal E_L=\sum_{\ell\in L(T)}w_\ell\mathcal E_1(f_\ell).
\]

### Proof

Separate the queried derivative:

\[
\mathcal E_1(f)=H(D_k f)+\sum_{i\ne k}H(D_i f).
\]

Physical derivative and restriction commute for distinct coordinates:

\[
(D_i f)^+=D_i(f^+),\qquad (D_i f)^-=D_i(f^-).
\]

Apply the valid entropy monotonicity (E) to each Boolean function `D_i f`:

\[
H(D_i f)\ge\frac{H(D_i(f^+))+H(D_i(f^-))}{2}.
\]

Sum over `i!=k` to obtain (1.7). Multiply (1.7) by the node weights and telescope to obtain (1.8). QED.

## Corollary 3 — universal derivative-entropy bound and stopping theorem

For every Boolean `f`,

\[
\boxed{
H(f)\le\frac14\mathcal E_1(f)
+\left(4+\frac2{\ln2}\right)J(f).
}
\tag{1.9}
\]

More generally, if a stopping tree has

\[
\mathcal E_T\le KQ_T
\tag{1.10}

and every stopping leaf satisfies `H(f_l)<=C_0 I(f_l)`, then

\[
\boxed{
H(f)\le
\max\left\{C_0,\frac K4+4+\frac2{\ln2}\right\}I(f).
}
\tag{1.11}
\]

### Proof

Sum (1.2) over the tree and combine with the dependent-recombination budget (R):

\[
H(f)\le H_L+\frac14\mathcal E_T+\left(4+\frac2{\ln2}\right)J_T.
\tag{1.12}

For a full tree to constants, (1.8) gives `E_T<=E_1(f)` and `J_T<=J(f)`, proving (1.9). For a stopping tree, use (1.10), `J_T<=Q_T`, the leaf certificate, and `Q_T+I_L=I(f)` to obtain (1.11). QED.

### Two exact structural advantages

The potential `E_1` has two properties that the raw square-curvature budget does not.

**Quadratic-gauge invariance.** If `Q` is any polynomial of degree at most two, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1((-1)^Qf)=\mathcal E_1(f).
}
\tag{1.13}
\]

Indeed `D_i((-1)^Qf)` is `D_i f` multiplied by the character `(-1)^{D_iQ}`. Character multiplication merely translates the Fourier spectrum and preserves entropy. In particular every pure quadratic phase has `E_1=0`, including the dense complete quadratic phases for which root square curvature is quadratic in dimension.

**Tensor additivity.** If `f` and `g` use disjoint physical coordinates, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(fg)=\mathcal E_1(f)+\mathcal E_1(g).
}
\tag{1.14}
\]

For a derivative in the `f` block, the `g` factor squares to one, leaving `D_i f` embedded with dummy variables; likewise in the other block. Thus fixed-seed tensor amplification cannot manufacture a divergent `E_1/J` ratio from a bounded seed ratio.

These are mechanism advantages only. The next section shows that `E_1` itself is not globally `O(J)`.

## Obstruction A — one-point defects kill `E_1<=KJ`

Let

\[

\]

Every coordinate has

\[

\qquad

\]

and every physical derivative is again a one-point defect:

\[
D_iw_n=w_{n-1}.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{\mathcal E_1(w_n)}{J(w_n)}
=2^{n-1}H(w_{n-1}).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Writing `m=n-1`, the squared Fourier law of `w_m` has mass

\[
A=(1-2^{1-m})^2
\]

at zero and mass

\[
b=2^{2-2m}
\]

at each nonzero frequency. Therefore

\[
H(w_m)=-A\log_2A-(2^m-1)b\log_2b,
\]

and (2.1) grows as

\[
8(n-1)+O(1).
\]

Thus no finite universal `K` satisfies `E_1(f)<=KJ(f)`. This is not an FEI counterexample: one-point defects are a standard FEI-positive family, and the current certified symmetric classes already stop on them.

## Theorem 4 — certificate-aware hybrid local payment

The two reports can be spliced without double counting the same local idea. For any `C>=0`, define the derivative FEI excess

\[
\rho_C(u):=[H(u)-CI(u)]_+.
\]

Then every split obeys

\[
\boxed{
B_i(f)
\le
\frac14\rho_C(D_i f)
+\frac C4\,[I(f^+)+I(f^-)]
+4j_i(f).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

By definition,

\[
H(D_i f)\le\rho_C(D_i f)+C I(D_i f).
\]

Insert this into Theorem 1 and then use the neighboring exact derivative triangle (D). QED.

This is useful precisely where the raw derivative entropy overcharges a known leaf. # Approach 3 — B30: column-pattern robustification of the current quadratic-symmetry stopping class

The one-point obstruction to the static `E_1/J` ratio suggests a practical stopping rule: do not expand a function that differs from a certified leaf on only finitely many physical inputs. The current quadratic-symmetry theorem gives an exact way to compile such leaves.

## Theorem 5 — `k` arbitrary truth-table flips cost at most `k` in the quadratic-symmetry constant

Let `h` be any `d`-part-symmetric Boolean function, let `Q` be any polynomial of degree at most two on the same physical coordinates, and let `A` be any set of `k` physical inputs. Let `F` be obtained from `(-1)^Qh` by flipping its sign exactly on `A`. Then

\[
\boxed{
H(F)
\le
(12.04+\log_2d+k)J(F)
\le
(12.04+\log_2d+k)I(F).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

There is no dimension, density, rank, or geometric condition on the defect set `A`. The price depends on the **number** of flipped truth-table entries. It is therefore dimension-free for each fixed `k`, but it is not a universal FEI theorem when `k` grows.

### Proof

Write

\[
g_A(x)=1-2\mathbf1_A(x),
\qquad
F=(-1)^Qh g_A.
\]

Enumerate the defect points as `a^1,...,a^k`. For every physical coordinate `j`, form its defect column

\[
c(j)=(a^1_j,\ldots,a^k_j)\in\mathbb F_2^k.
\]

Refine each original symmetry part `V_r` of `h` according to this column. There are at most

\[
d\,2^k
\]

nonempty refined parts. A permutation within one refined part preserves `h` because it stays inside one original symmetry part. It also fixes each point `a^s` coordinate-by-coordinate because all coordinates in that refined part have the same defect column. Hence it preserves the set `A` and therefore preserves `g_A`.

Thus `h g_A` is `D`-part-symmetric for some

\[
D\le d2^k.
\]

Apply the current quadratic-symmetry theorem (Q) to the arbitrary quadratic twist `(-1)^Q` of this refined symmetric core:

\[
H(F)\le(12.04+\log_2D)J(F)
\le(12.04+\log_2d+k)J(F).
\]

QED.

This theorem enlarges the certified stopping class in a way tailored to the failed scalar potential. One-point defects (`k=1`) and fixed finite physical corruptions are stopped at the root rather than forced through a derivative-entropy tree. The current specialized one-point and affine-clause results retain much sharper constants where applicable; (3.1) is a robust compiler, not a constant improvement claim.
