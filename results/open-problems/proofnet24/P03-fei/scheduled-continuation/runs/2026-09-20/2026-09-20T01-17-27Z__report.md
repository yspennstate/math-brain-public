# Approach 1 — exact obstruction to the weighted derivative-degree tree charge

The newest P03 report proposes the sufficient full-scope target

\[
D_T^{\deg}:=\sum_{v\rm\ internal}w_v r_vj_{i(v)}(f_v)\le K I(f),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where `r_v` is the degree of the current nonconstant algebraic derivative.

## Theorem 1

Let

\[
w_n(x)=(-1)^{x_1x_2\cdots x_n},\qquad n\ge2.
\]

For **every** full physical restriction tree `T`,

\[
\boxed{D_T^{\deg}(w_n)=2^{1-n}\frac{n(n-1)}2,\qquad I(w_n)=n2^{1-n},}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{D_T^{\deg}(w_n)/I(w_n)=(n-1)/2\to\infty.}
\]

Hence no finite universal `K` satisfies (1.1), even after optimizing over all full physical restriction trees.

### Proof

At a node whose surviving nonconstant restriction is `w_m`, querying any free coordinate gives one constant child (bit fixed to zero) and one child exactly `w_{m-1}` (bit fixed to one). Thus every full tree has exactly one nonconstant branch at every stage, independent of its adaptive coordinate choices.

For `m>=2`, the queried derivative is the `(m-1)`-variable monomial, hence `r_v=m-1`. Its folded influence equals its ordinary influence, `j_v=2^{1-m}`. When `m` variables remain, the unique nonconstant node is at depth `n-m`, so its weight is `2^{-(n-m)}`. Consequently

\[
w_vj_v=2^{-(n-m)}2^{1-m}=2^{1-n}
\]

at every nonconstant stage. The final one-variable character has constant derivative and contributes zero. Therefore

\[
D_T^{\deg}(w_n)=2^{1-n}\sum_{m=2}^n(m-1)=2^{1-n}\frac{n(n-1)}2.
\]

Every coordinate of `w_n` changes on exactly two inputs, so `I(w_n)=n2^{1-n}`. QED.

This is a particularly sharp diagnostic because the preceding PROB10 report proved the **true** optimized hereditary allocation satisfies `R_*(w_n)/I(w_n)->2`. The derivative-degree surrogate overpays the same exponentially rare branch at every level. The source conjecture is unaffected.

# Approach 2 — folded influence is a coordinatewise metric under multiplication

## Lemma 2 (folded-influence triangle inequality)

For arbitrary Boolean `f,g` on the same cube and every physical coordinate `i`,

\[
\boxed{|j_i(f)-j_i(g)|\le j_i(fg)\le j_i(f)+j_i(g).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
U(x)=f(x)f(x+e_i),\qquad V(x)=g(x)g(x+e_i).
\]

The derivative of `fg` is `UV`. For a sign-valued random variable `X`, put

\[
d(X)=\min\{P(X=1),P(X=-1)\}=(1-|EX|)/2.
\]

Thus `j_i(f)=d(U)`, `j_i(g)=d(V)`, `j_i(fg)=d(UV)`. Choose signs `s,t` minimizing the exceptional masses, so `P(sU=-1)=d(U)` and `P(tV=-1)=d(V)`. If `stUV=-1`, at least one of `sU,tV` is `-1`; the union bound gives `d(UV)<=d(U)+d(V)`. Apply the same upper bound to `U=(UV)V` and symmetrically to obtain the two lower triangle inequalities. QED.

Summing gives `J(fg)<=J(f)+J(g)` and `J(fg)>=|J(f)-J(g)|`.

## Corollary 3 (Reed–Muller anti-cancellation)

Let `q=(-1)^P`. If `D_iP` is nonconstant of degree `r_i`, then for every Boolean `h`,

\[
\boxed{j_i(qh)\ge2^{-r_i}-j_i(h).}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Indeed, (R2) gives `j_i(q)>=2^{-r_i}` and Lemma 2 gives `j_i(qh)>=j_i(q)-j_i(h)`.

For `r_i=1`, this contains the quadratic pair-budget `j_i(h)+j_i(qh)>=1/2` from the newest quadratic-symmetry report.

## Theorem 4 (fixed hitting-set transfer for arbitrary low-degree phases)

Let `q=(-1)^P`, `F=qh`, and let `E` hit every monomial of `P` of degree at least two, so that after fixing `E` every remaining restriction of `P` is affine. Assume:

1. every restriction `h_z` fixing `E` satisfies `H(h_z)<=C J(h_z)` with the same `C`;
2. for every `i in E`, `j_i(q)-j_i(h)>=2/C`.

Then

\[
\boxed{H(F)\le C J(F).}
\]

### Proof

After fixing `E`, each `q_z` is a sign times a character. Character multiplication preserves Fourier entropy and folded influence, so the leaf certificate gives `H(F_z)<=C J(F_z)`. Lemma 2 and assumption 2 imply `j_i(F)>=2/C` for every deleted coordinate. Apply (R3):

\[
H(F)\le C\left(J(F)-\sum_{i\in E}j_i(F)\right)+2|E|\le CJ(F).
\]

QED.

A sufficient Reed–Muller condition is

\[
j_i(h)\le2^{-r_i}-2/C\qquad(i\in E).
\]

**Concrete cubic extension.** If `h` is fully symmetric, every physical restriction is symmetric and the retained leaf constant is `C=12.04`. If `P` has degree at most three and `E` hits its nonlinear monomials, every nonconstant derivative on `E` has degree at most two. Hence the same constant 12.04 survives provided

\[
j_i(h)\le\frac14-\frac2{12.04}=0.0838870431\ldots\qquad(i\in E).
\]

This is a rigorous extension beyond character-valued quadratic derivatives, with an explicit small-folded-influence hypothesis; it is not unrestricted FEI.

# Approach 3 — canalyzing selectors admit a constant hereditary compiler

Let `g` be Boolean, `c in {−1,+1}`, add selector bit `z`, and define

\[
F(y,z)=\begin{cases}g(y),&z=+1,\\ c,&z=-1.\end{cases}
\]

Put `delta=P[g!=c]` and `j=min(delta,1-delta)=j_z(F)`.

## Lemma 5 (exact selector allocation)

\[
\boxed{B_z(F)=\Psi(\delta)=m h_2\left(\frac{\delta^2}{m}\right)+2\delta(1-\delta),\quad m=(1-\delta)^2+\delta^2.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Moreover `Psi(delta)=Psi(1-delta)` and

\[
\boxed{B_z(F)<4j\quad(0<j\le1/2).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof of the formula

For a Fourier label `T` on old coordinates,

\[
\hat F(T)=\frac{\hat g(T)+c1_{T=\varnothing}}2,\qquad
\hat F(T\cup\{z\})=\frac{\hat g(T)-c1_{T=\varnothing}}2.
\]

For every nonempty `T`, the two squared masses in the `z` pair are equal. Their total mass is `1/2(1-(Eg)^2)=2delta(1-delta)`, so they contribute exactly this amount to `B_z`. At the empty label, since `Eg=c(1-2delta)`, the pair masses are `(1-delta)^2` and `delta^2`, yielding (3.1).

### Proof of the bound

By symmetry take `j=delta<=1/2`. Put `x=(1-j)^2`, `y=j^2`. The exceptional-pair entropy is

\[
E_0=x\log_2(1+y/x)+y\log_2(1+x/y).
\]

Using `ln(1+t)<=t`, `1/ln2<3/2`, and `j ln(1/j)<=1/e<3/8`,

\[
E_0\le\frac{j^2}{\ln2}+2j^2\log_2(1/j)<\frac34j+\frac98j<2j.
\]

The equal-mass term is `2j(1-j)<=2j`; hence `B_z<4j`. QED.

## Theorem 6 (canalyzing extension preserves every hereditary constant C>=4)

For the selector above,

\[
\boxed{R_*(F)\le B_z(F)+\frac12R_*(g),\qquad I(F)=\delta+\frac12I(g).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently, if `C>=4` and `R_*(g)<=C I(g)`, then

\[
\boxed{R_*(F)\le C I(F).}
\]

### Proof

Query `z` first. One child is constant; on the other attach an optimal tree for `g`, giving the first inequality. The ordinary influence telescope gives the second identity. Since `B_z<=4j<=4delta<=Cdelta`,

\[
R_*(F)\le C\delta+\frac C2I(g)=CI(F).
\]

QED.

Starting from a constant and repeatedly adding canalyzing coordinates yields

\[
\boxed{R_*(f)\le4I(f)}
\]

for nested canalyzing / decision-list Boolean functions. Ordinary finite lexicographic indicator functions admit this recursion. By (R1), this gives the direct class estimate

\[
H(f)\le\left(4+\frac2{\ln2}\right)I(f).
\]

FEI for read-once decision trees has antecedents; the new point here is explicit compatibility with the current hereditary-allocation mechanism, not a novelty claim for the class.
