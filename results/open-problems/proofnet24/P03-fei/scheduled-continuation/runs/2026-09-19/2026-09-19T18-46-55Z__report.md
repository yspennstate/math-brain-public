## new_mathematical_delta

### Theorem 1: a universal one-step bound by minimum affine influence

For every Boolean function,

\[
\boxed{D_\times(f)\le 6\,\mathfrak I(f).}
\tag{1}
\]

This is a genuine all-dimensional theorem, but by itself it does **not** prove FEI because the discounted sum of `Ifrak(f_k)` can be much larger than `Ifrak(f)`.

**Proof.** Choose an invertible linear input change and an output character attaining `Ifrak(f)`. Both Fourier entropy and `D_x` are invariant under these operations: the input change permutes Fourier characters and uniform shifts, while multiplying by a character changes every derivative only by a constant sign. In the resulting coordinates let the spectral marginal probabilities be

\[
q_i=\Pr(i\in S)\in[0,1/2],\qquad \sum_iq_i=\mathfrak I(f).
\]

For two independent spectral samples S,T and U=S xor T, conditional entropy is subadditive:

\[
H(S\mid U)\le\sum_i H(S_i\mid U_i).
\]

For one coordinate with `S_i,T_i iid Bernoulli(q)`,

\[
H(S_i\mid U_i)=\phi(q):=2h_2(q)-h_2(2q(1-q)).
\tag{2}
\]

The scalar inequality

\[
\boxed{\phi(q)\le3q\quad(0\le q\le1/2)}
\tag{3}
\]

holds. To verify it analytically, use natural logarithms, put `t=ln((1-q)/q)>=0`, and define

\[
F(q)=3q\ln2-\ln2\,\phi(q).
\]

Differentiation gives

\[
F'(q)=3\ln2-2t+2\tanh(t/2)\ln\cosh t.
\]

Since `ln cosh t >= t-ln2`,

\[
F'(q)\ge\frac{(e^t+5)\ln2-4t}{e^t+1}.
\]

The numerator is convex and has its unique minimum at `e^t=4/ln2`; the minimum equals

\[
4+5\ln2-4\ln(4/\ln2)>0.
\]

For a completely elementary certificate, `ln2>2/3`, hence `4/ln2<6`, while `ln6<9/5` because the Taylor polynomial of `e^{9/5}` through order six already exceeds six. Therefore the displayed minimum is larger than

\[
4+10/3-36/5=2/15.
\]

Thus `F` is increasing from `F(0)=0`, proving (3). The archived Walsh uncertainty bound now gives

\[
D_\times(f)\le2H(S\mid U)\le2\sum_i\phi(q_i)
\le6\sum_iq_i=6\mathfrak I(f).
\]

This proves (1). No independence among different spectral coordinates is used; only the two independent copies S,T are coordinatewise marginalized.

### Proposition 2: direct summation of Theorem 1 cannot close FEI

Let `w_n=1-2 1_{x=0}` be the one-point defect. Its initial affine minimum is

\[
\mathfrak I(w_n)=n2^{1-n}.
\]

After k independent derivative shifts, if the shifts are linearly dependent the derivative is constant. If they are independent, it is the sign of the k-dimensional shift span. For `0<=k<=n-2`, its affine minimum is

\[
(n-k)2^{k+1-n};
\]

at `k=n-1` it is a character and the affine minimum is zero. Writing

\[
P_{n,k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-2^{j-n}),
\]

we obtain the exact discounted sum

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}\mathbb E\mathfrak I((w_n)_k)
=2^{1-n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}P_{n,k}(n-k).
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently the ratio to the initial affine minimum is

\[
\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}P_{n,k}(n-k)\sim n/2,
\tag{5}
\]

so it is unbounded. Thus (1) cannot be inserted term-by-term into entropy telescoping and summed with a dimension-free loss.

### A stronger potential that survived finite attacks but remains UNPROVED

Define the character-invariant mode defect

\[
v(f)=1-\max_s\widehat f(s)^2,
\]

and the affine-minimum one-step dissipation

\[
\delta_{\mathfrak I}(f)=2\mathfrak I(f)-\mathbb E_z\mathfrak I(f_z).
\]

The already proved derivative comparison gives `delta_Ifrak>=0`. Two potentially decisive inequalities survived exhaustive exact testing through dimension three:

\[
D_\times(f)\stackrel{?}{\le}4[v(f)+\delta_{\mathfrak I}(f)],
\tag{6}
\]

and

\[
\mathfrak I(f)\stackrel{?}{\le}v(f)+\delta_{\mathfrak I}(f).
\tag{7}
\]

**Neither (6) nor (7) is proved.** They are recorded because either would materially advance the full problem. They must therefore be treated as high-risk targets and attacked adversarially rather than inferred from finite evidence.
