# Approach 1 — B2: majority refutes every finite affine-drift constant

The idea was to attack the surviving all-constant version of the drift route on an indecomposable family. Tensor powers cannot make `L/Delta_aff` diverge because both quantities are additive, so a genuine growing family is necessary. Odd majority is especially suitable because the archive already gives a logarithmically divergent lower bound for `L`, while its directional geometry has a two-dimensional period space after one derivative.

## Theorem 1

Let `f_n=Maj_n` for odd `n`, and set

\[
a_n=2^{-(n-1)}\binom{n-1}{(n-1)/2}.
\]

Then

\[
M(f_n)=(n-1)a_n,
\qquad
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_n)=O(1),
\tag{1}
\]

while by (R1)

\[
L(f_n)\ge \frac1{18}\sum_{a=1}^{(n-1)/2}\frac1a+\frac1{36}\to\infty.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{\sup_f\frac{L(f)}{\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)}=\infty.}
\tag{3}
\]

The convention is that a zero denominator with positive numerator gives infinity; majority itself has positive drift by the general lower bound `Delta_aff>=2 sum_i j_i^2`.

Thus no finite constant in `L<=C Delta_aff` can prove FEI. Since `D_cross>=L`, the same conclusion holds for `D_cross<=C Delta_aff`.

### Proof of the exact majority affine minimum

For a monotone Boolean `h`, if `i` is outside a direction `v`, write in sign coordinates

\[
h(x_i,y)=A(y)+x_iB(y),\qquad B(y)\ge0.
\]

The correlation in direction `v` contains `+E B(y)B(y+v)`, whereas the correlation in direction `v+e_i` contains the same term with a minus sign. Therefore

\[
\alpha_h(v+e_i)\ge\alpha_h(v).
\tag{4}
\]

For majority, every one-coordinate direction has influence `a_n`. Self-duality gives

\[
f_n(x+\mathbf1)=-f_n(x),
\qquad
\alpha_{f_n}(v+\mathbf1)=1-\alpha_{f_n}(v).
\tag{5}
\]

Combining (4)--(5), every direction outside `{0,mathbf1}` has folded cost at least `a_n`, while `j_f(mathbf1)=0`. Every basis has at least `n-1` vectors outside that two-point set. The basis consisting of `mathbf1` and any `n-1` coordinate vectors attains that lower bound. Hence

\[
M(f_n)=(n-1)a_n.
\tag{6}
\]

### One derivative and its period space

Fix a shift `z` of Hamming weight `k` with

\[
n/3\le k\le2n/3.
\tag{7}
\]

Put `g_z=f_n f_n^z`. It is invariant under both `z` and `mathbf1`, so

\[
W=\operatorname{span}\{z,\mathbf1\}
\tag{8}
\]

is a two-dimensional zero-cost period space (for nontrivial balanced `z`). Directional cost is constant on cosets of `W`.

Reduce a direction `v` modulo `W` so it flips `a<=k/2` coordinates in the `z` block and `b<=(n-k)/2` in its complement. Put `ell=a+b`. Split the input signs into independent sums `U,X,V,Y`, where `U,V` are the flipped coordinates and `X,Y` the unflipped coordinates in the two blocks. Then exactly

\[
g_z(x)=\operatorname{sgn}((Y+V)^2-(X+U)^2),
\]
\[
g_z(x+v)=\operatorname{sgn}((Y-V)^2-(X-U)^2).
\tag{9}
\]

No equality ambiguity occurs because `n` is odd.

We show that for some absolute `C` and all sufficiently large odd `n`, uniformly in balanced `z`,

\[
\boxed{j_{g_z}(v)\ge2a_n-C/n\quad\text{for every }v\notin W.}
\tag{10}
\]

That estimate is much stronger than necessary; any `2a_n-O(1/n)` lower bound suffices.

### Small reduced directions

Let `p=alpha_{f_n}(v)`. Let `E` be the event that `f_n` changes under `v`, and `E_z` the corresponding event for the translated majority factor. Then `P(E)=P(E_z)=p` and

\[
\alpha_{g_z}(v)=P(E\triangle E_z)=2p-2q,
\qquad q=P(E\cap E_z).
\tag{11}
\]

For `ell<=c n` with a sufficiently small fixed `c`, each of the outside block sums `X,Y` contains at least `n/4` fair signs. Conditional on `U,V`, the map `(X,Y)->(X+Y,X-Y)` is injective on the parity lattice. The two pivotal inequalities defining `E` and `E_z` restrict these two coordinates to slabs of widths `O(|U+V|)` and `O(|U-V|)`. The largest joint atom of `(X,Y)` is `O(1/n)`. Therefore, by Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
q\le C_1\ell/n.
\tag{12}
\]

On the other hand the standard central-binomial point-mass bounds give, uniformly for `1<=ell<=cn`,

\[
c_1\sqrt{\ell/n}\le p\le C_2\sqrt{\ell/n}.
\tag{13}
\]

For `ell` above a fixed absolute threshold, (13) already gives `p>=2a_n` and (12) is at most `p/2` after decreasing `c`. For bounded `ell`, monotonicity gives `p>=a_n`, while (12) is `O(1/n)`. Thus (11) yields

\[
j_{g_z}(v)=\alpha_{g_z}(v)\ge2a_n-C/n
\tag{14}
\]

throughout the small-direction regime. Folding is harmless here because the upper bound from (11),(13) is below `1/2` once `c` is fixed small enough.

For completeness, the point-mass estimates in (13) need no local limit theorem: the largest mass of a sum of `m` fair signs is comparable to `m^{-1/2}` by the central-binomial recurrence; in a window of `O(sqrt(ell))` lattice points with `ell<=cn`, the ratio to the mode stays bounded below by an absolute constant. Paley--Zygmund applied to the flipped sum supplies a constant probability of width `Theta(sqrt(ell))`.

### Macroscopic reduced directions

Now suppose `ell>=cn`. Normalize the four sums in (9) by `sqrt(n)`. Along any sequence of admissible block sizes, pass to a subsequence on which the four variance proportions converge. The possible limits form a compact set with

- nonnegative variances summing to one;
- the two original `z` blocks each having total variance in `[1/3,2/3]`;
- the unflipped portions `X,Y` each having variance at least `1/6`;
- the total flipped variance at least `c`.

The normalized sums converge jointly to independent centered Gaussians with those variances; zero limiting variance is allowed for `U` or `V` and is handled by Chebyshev.

Let `alpha(p)` be the disagreement probability of the two signs in (9) for the limiting Gaussian vector. Its boundary has probability zero because `X+/-U` and `Y+/-V` are nondegenerate Gaussian linear forms. The function `alpha(p)` is continuous in the variance parameters by coupling all four variables to fixed standard Gaussians.

Moreover

\[
0<\alpha(p)<1
\tag{15}
\]

at every point of the compact parameter set. If the flipped variance of `U` is positive, a small open neighborhood of `(U,X,Y,V)=(2,1,2,0)` gives opposite signs in (9); if the `U` variance is zero, the flipped variance of `V` is positive and the analogous neighborhood with `(V,Y,X,U)=(2,1,2,0)` does the same. An open neighborhood with `Y` large and all other live variables near zero gives agreement. Degenerate coordinates are simply fixed at zero.

Compactness and continuity therefore give an absolute `eta>0` with

\[
eta\le\alpha(p)\le1-eta.
\tag{16}
\]

The discrete probabilities converge uniformly over the admissible integer block tuples: otherwise a violating sequence has a convergent variance subsequence, contradicting the joint CLT and the null-boundary property. Hence, for all sufficiently large `n`, every macroscopic `v` outside `W` has

\[
j_{g_z}(v)\ge eta/2\ge2a_n.
\tag{17}
\]

Together (14) and (17) prove (10).

### Averaging the minimum basis cost

A basis contains at least `n-2` vectors outside the two-dimensional period space `W`. Hence on every balanced shift satisfying (7),

\[
M(g_z)\ge(n-2)(2a_n-C/n)
\ge2(n-2)a_n-C.
\tag{18}
\]

A uniform random shift has weight outside `[n/3,2n/3]` with probability exponentially small in `n` by a binomial Chernoff bound. Since `M>=0`, averaging (18) gives

\[
\mathbb E_zM(g_z)\ge2(n-2)a_n-C-o(1).
\tag{19}
\]

Using (6),

\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_n)
=2(n-1)a_n-\mathbb E_zM(g_z)
\le2a_n+C+o(1)=O(1).
\tag{20}
\]

The general fixed-basis identity from the affine-drift reports gives the matching nonnegativity and, in fact,

\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_n)\ge2(n-1)a_n^2>0.
\tag{21}
\]

Equations (R1), (20), and (21) prove Theorem 1.

### Scope and stress tests

This is precisely the kind of indecomposable obstruction that tensor tests cannot produce: `L` and `Delta_aff` are both tensor-additive, so a fixed bad seed keeps a fixed ratio. Majority instead has a ratio diverging with dimension. The result is compatible with the archived one-point and quadratic stress families, where the ratio stays finite, and with the fresh-control theorem because no fresh-control layer is present here. # Approach 2 — B5/B13: exact erasure-curvature flow

The second approach was to seek a genuinely hereditary form of the erasure quantity rather than a pointwise affine drift. The result below is an exact information identity for **every** binary-vector law, hence in particular for squared Boolean spectra. It does not by itself use the Boolean signed-convolution constraints and therefore does not close FEI.

Let `X=(X_1,...,X_n)` be a binary random vector. For `A subseteq[n]` put

\[
F(A)=H(X_A\mid X_{A^c})=H(X)-H(X_{A^c}).
\]

Let `R_t` include each coordinate independently with probability `t`, and define

\[
\mathcal E(t)=\mathbb E F(R_t),\qquad0\le t\le1.
\tag{22}
\]

## Theorem 2 (erasure curvature identity)

`mathcal E` is increasing and convex, with

\[
\mathcal E(0)=0,\qquad \mathcal E(1)=H(X),
\tag{23}
\]

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E'(t)=\sum_i\mathbb E_{R_t\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i\}}
 H\!\left(X_i\mid X_{[n]\setminus(R_t\cup\{i\})}\right),}
\tag{24}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E''(t)=
\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb E_{R_t\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i,j\}}
 I\!\left(X_i;X_j\mid
 X_{[n]\setminus(R_t\cup\{i,j\})}\right)\ge0.}
\tag{25}
\]

In particular, if

\[
B(X)=\sum_iH(X_i\mid X_{-i}),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{H(X)=B(X)+\int_0^1(1-t)\mathcal E''(t)\,dt.}
\tag{26}
\]

**Proof.** For any set function `G`, differentiation of the Bernoulli-subset expectation gives the finite Russo formula

\[
\frac d{dt}\mathbb E G(R_t)
=\sum_i\mathbb E[G(R_t\cup\{i\})-G(R_t)],
\]

where on the right `R_t` is sampled from coordinates other than `i`. Apply it to `F`. Since

\[
F(R\cup\{i\})-F(R)
=H(X_i\mid X_{[n]\setminus(R\cup\{i\})}),
\]

we obtain (24). Differentiate (24) once more. Adding `j` to the erased set removes `X_j` from the conditioning sigma-field, and the entropy increment is exactly

\[
H(X_i\mid Y)-H(X_i\mid Y,X_j)=I(X_i;X_j\mid Y),
\]

with `Y=X_{[n]\setminus(R\cup\{i,j\})}`. This proves (25). At `t=0`, (24) is `B(X)`. Integrating `mathcal E'(t)=B+int_0^t mathcal E''(s)ds` from zero to one gives (26). QED.

For a Boolean Fourier spectral law, Theorem 2 says that the exact FEI-equivalent erasure quantity `B` is the **initial slope** of a convex erasure flow, and all entropy not already visible in `B` is an integrated conditional pair-dependence curvature. The missing step is now explicit: one needs the Boolean identities `sum_S a_S a_{S xor U}=0` to pay this curvature by influence without discarding its cross-scale correlations. Arbitrary binary laws cannot satisfy such a bound, so (25) is an organizational identity rather than a universal estimate.

The new checker evaluates `mathcal E(t)` on several small squared Boolean spectra at five values of `t`, verifies the endpoint identities, and checks discrete convex slopes. It does not certify (24)--(26), whose proof is the finite differentiation above.

# Approach 3 — B7/B18: algebraic-degree descent through the actual derivative chain

The third approach asks how far the valid one-step bound `D_cross<=6M<=6J` can be pushed when multiplicative differentiation has a guaranteed structural stopping time. Write every Boolean function uniquely as

\[
f(x)=(-1)^{P(x)},
\]

where `P` is the multilinear polynomial over `F_2`; let `d=deg P` be its algebraic degree.

## Theorem 3 (degree-descent FEI bound)

Every Boolean phase of algebraic degree `d` satisfies

\[
\boxed{H(f)\le3d\,J(f)\le3d\,I(f).}
\tag{27}
\]

This is dimension-dependent through `d` and therefore is **not** full FEI. It is included because it extends the derivative-chain mechanism beyond character-valued quadratic derivatives and gives a linear-in-degree payment, improving the previously recorded exponential-in-degree fallback for sufficiently large fixed degree. No novelty claim is made.

**Proof.** A multiplicative derivative has phase

\[
f_z(x)=(-1)^{P(x)+P(x+z)},
\]

whose algebraic degree is at most `d-1`. Thus after `d` derivative steps the function is constant, so the exact entropy telescope terminates:

\[
H(f)=\frac12\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}2^{-k}\mathbb E D_\times(f_k).
\tag{28}
\]

For any Boolean `g` and physical coordinate `i`, let `q_i=I_i(g)` and `j_i=min(q_i,1-q_i)`. If `z` is uniform, then the two values of the pivotal sign `g(x)g(x+e_i)` at `x` and `x+z` are independent copies after averaging over `(x,z)`. Hence

\[
\mathbb E_z I_i(g_z)=2q_i(1-q_i)=2j_i(1-j_i)\le2j_i.
\tag{29}
\]

Since folded influence is no larger than ordinary influence,

\[
\mathbb E_z j_i(g_z)\le2j_i,
\qquad
\mathbb E_zJ(g_z)\le2J(g).
\tag{30}
\]

Inductively `E J(f_k)<=2^kJ(f)`. Using the previously proved universal `D_cross(g)<=6M(g)<=6J(g)` in (28),

\[
H(f)
\le3\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}2^{-k}\mathbb E J(f_k)
\le3dJ(f).
\]

This proves (27). QED.

The finite checker exhaustively verifies algebraic degree descent and (27) for every Boolean function through three variables. The theorem itself is analytical and does not depend on those finite checks.

It does show that the obstruction is not merely failure of quadratic/character derivatives: one can propagate the valid one-step inequality through arbitrary higher-degree derivatives at linear cost in the stopping depth.
