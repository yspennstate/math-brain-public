## new_mathematical_delta

### 1. Exact mixture representation of the controlled squared spectrum

Let `G=Gamma_c f`, `p=2^{-c}`, `d=1-Ef`, and suppose first `d>0`. Define a probability law `nu_f` on the old spectral group by

\[
\nu_f(0)=\frac d2,
\qquad
\nu_f(s)=\frac{p_f(s)}{2d}\quad(s\ne0).
\tag{1}
\]

This is normalized because

\[
d^2+\sum_{s\ne0}p_f(s)
=d^2+1-(1-d)^2=2d.
\]

Let `U_c` denote the uniform probability law on the c-dimensional control-frequency group and put

\[
Q=U_c\otimes\nu_f,
\qquad
\varepsilon=2pd.
\tag{2}
\]

From the exact Fourier coefficients of the rare-controls report,

\[
\widehat G(0,0)=1-pd,
\quad
\widehat G(a,0)=-pd\ (a\ne0),
\quad
\widehat G(a,s)=p\widehat f(s)\ (s\ne0).
\]

Squaring them gives the exact probability-mixture identity

\[
\boxed{p_G=(1-\varepsilon)\delta_0+\varepsilon Q.}
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed the uniform-mixture part contributes `p^2d^2` at every `(a,0)` and `p^2p_f(s)` at every `(a,s)` with `s!=0`, while the remaining atom at zero has mass `1-2pd`. No sign information is discarded after the exact Fourier expansion has already been squared.

The case `d=0` is constant and all quantities below vanish.

### 2. A general latent-mixture allocation lemma

Let `P=(1-epsilon)delta_0+epsilon Q` be any probability law on a finite binary abelian group, with `0<=epsilon<=1`. Let `X,Y` be iid `Q`, and let `B,C` be iid Bernoulli(epsilon), independent of `X,Y`. Generate

`S=0` if `B=0`, `S=X` if `B=1`, and similarly `T`; put `U=S xor T`.

Write `N=B+C`. Conditional on `(B,C)`, the entropy of `S` given `U` is zero unless `B=C=1`; in that case it is exactly `L(Q)`. Also, conditional on `N=1`, the orientation `(B,C)=(1,0)` or `(0,1)` remains a fair bit even after `U` is revealed. Therefore

\[
L(P)\le H(B,C\mid U)+\varepsilon^2L(Q)
=H(N\mid U)+2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^2L(Q).
\tag{4}
\]

Estimate `N` from `U` by guessing `N=0` when `U=0` and `N=1` otherwise. This estimator errs on every `N=2` event and, when `N=1`, exactly when the one `Q`-sample equals zero. Thus, if `q_0=Q(0)`, its exact error probability is

\[
e=\varepsilon^2+2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)q_0.
\tag{5}
\]

Fano's elementary three-symbol bound gives

\[
H(N\mid U)\le h_2(e)+e.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
L(P)\le2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)+h_2(e)+e+\varepsilon^2L(Q).}
\tag{6}
\]

This lemma is purely probabilistic and makes no independence substitution for two samples from the same random derivative; it concerns two iid samples from one fixed spectral law, exactly as in the definition of `L`.

### 3. Specialization to fresh controls

For (3),

\[
q_0=Q(0)=p\nu_f(0)=\frac{pd}{2}=\frac\varepsilon4,
\]

so (5) becomes

\[
\boxed{e=\frac{\varepsilon^2(3-\varepsilon)}2.}
\tag{7}
\]

Since `Q=U_c tensor nu_f` and allocation entropy tensorizes for product laws,

\[
L(Q)=c+L(\nu_f).
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to control the normalized indicator spectrum `nu_f`. Choose an `M_0(f)`-optimal basis with directional influences `a_i`. Since the negative set of `f` has density `d/2`, every directional influence satisfies `a_i<=d`. The i-th spectral marginal under `nu_f` equals

\[
q_i=\frac{a_i}{2d}\le\frac12.
\tag{9}
\]

Conditional subadditivity and the already proved scalar inequality `phi(q)<=3q` yield

\[
\boxed{
L(\nu_f)\le\sum_i\phi(q_i)\le3\sum_iq_i
=\frac{3M_0(f)}{2d}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Combining (6)--(10),

\[
\boxed{
L(\Gamma_c f)
\le2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)+h_2(e)+e
+4c p^2d^2+6p^2dM_0(f),}
\tag{11}
\]

where `epsilon=2pd` and `e=epsilon^2(3-epsilon)/2`. This is an all-dimensional analytical bound for every Boolean seed.

### 4. A constant-twelve affine-drift theorem for arbitrary seeds behind logarithmically many fresh controls

**Theorem.** Let `f` be any Boolean function, let `c>=3`, `p=2^{-c}`, and assume

\[
pM_0(f)\le1.
\tag{12}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{L(\Gamma_c f)\le12\,\Delta_{\rm aff}(\Gamma_c f).}
\tag{13}
\]

**Proof.** Since `c>=3` and `d<=2`, `epsilon=2pd<=1/2`. Put

\[
A=2p(1-p)d=\varepsilon(1-p),
\qquad
C_0=cp^2d^2.
\]

The exact drift identity is

\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(\Gamma_c f)=p^2\Delta_0(f)+A+C_0.
\tag{14}
\]

For (7), `epsilon^2<=e<=(3/2)epsilon^2<=(3/4)epsilon`. For `0<e<=1/2`,

\[
h_2(e)\le e\log_2(1/e)+e/\ln2.
\]

Because `e>=epsilon^2`, `log_2(1/e)<=2log_2(1/epsilon)`. The elementary maximum `x\ln(1/x)<=1/e_{\rm Euler}` on `(0,1]`, together with `e_{Euler}>2` and `ln2>1/2`, implies `epsilon log_2(1/epsilon)<1`. Also `1/ln2<3/2`. Hence

\[
h_2(e)+e
\le3\varepsilon+\frac98\varepsilon+\frac34\varepsilon
=\frac{39}{8}\varepsilon.
\tag{15}
\]

Therefore the first three terms in (11) are at most

\[
\frac{55}{8}\varepsilon.
\]

Since `p<=1/8`, `A>=7epsilon/8`, so this is at most `(55/7)A`. Under (12),

\[
6p^2dM_0(f)\le6pd=3\varepsilon\le\frac{24}{7}A.
\tag{16}
\]

The remaining control term in (11) is exactly `4C_0`. Thus

\[
L(\Gamma_c f)
\le\frac{79}{7}A+4C_0
<12(A+C_0)
\le12\Delta_{\rm aff}(\Gamma_c f),
\]

proving (13).

Since `M_0(f)<=n` for an n-variable function, condition (12) is automatic whenever

\[
\boxed{c\ge\max\{3,\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

Thus the surviving drift route is now proved, with a concrete universal constant, on **every arbitrary Boolean seed hidden behind logarithmically many fresh control bits**. The seed may have unrestricted algebraic degree and unrestricted internal dependencies.

### 5. Scope of the advance

This theorem does **not** prove FEI. `Gamma_c` appends fresh coordinates; it is not a transition of the fixed-domain derivative chain. Equation (13) is not asserted for every descendant of `Gamma_c f`, and the operation does not recover the original `f`. Therefore the exact drift telescope cannot be applied globally from (13) alone.

The theorem is nevertheless relevant to the remaining dependency problem: it shows that the allocation/drift obstruction is not arbitrary internal complexity once enough fresh control entropy is present. The unresolved difficulty is concentrated in functions without such a logarithmic fresh-control layer, or in proving a decomposition/recombination principle that pays for removing that layer.
