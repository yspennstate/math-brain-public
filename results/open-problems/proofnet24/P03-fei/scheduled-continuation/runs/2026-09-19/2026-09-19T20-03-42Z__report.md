## new_mathematical_delta

### 1. A sufficient affine-drift target that telescopes exactly

Define

\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)=2M(f)-\mathbb E_z M(f_z),
\qquad f_z(x)=f(x)f(x+z).
\tag{1}
\]

The new target is

\[
\boxed{L(f)\le C\,\Delta_{\rm aff}(f).}
\tag{2}
\]

This is a sufficient condition, not a proved equivalent reformulation of FEI. Any finite universal `C` in (2) would prove full FEI. Indeed, if

`a_k=E M(f_k)`,

then by conditioning on `f_k`,

\[
\mathbb E\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_k)=2a_k-a_{k+1}.
\]

Since the multiplicative-derivative chain terminates, exact summation gives

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}\mathbb E\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_k)=2M(f).}
\tag{3}
\]

Combining (2), `D_cross<=2L`, and entropy telescoping yields

\[
H(f)\le\sum_k2^{-k}\mathbb E L(f_k)
\le C\sum_k2^{-k}\mathbb E\Delta_{\rm aff}(f_k)
=2C M(f)\le2C I(f).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the particularly simple candidate

\[
\boxed{L(f)\stackrel{?}{\le}3\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)}
\tag{5}
\]

would give `H(f)<=6M(f)<=6I(f)` in all dimensions. Equation (5) is **UNPROVED universally**. The constant three was chosen only after attacking the ratio exactly on small functions and structured stress examples; finite survival is not evidence of a theorem.

This target survives the tensor obstruction that killed the bounded mode-defect correction: both sides of (2) are extensive under tensor products, as proved next.

### 2. Exact tensor and affine closure of the drift ratio

For Boolean functions on disjoint coordinates,

\[
\boxed{L(f\otimes g)=L(f)+L(g),\qquad
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f\otimes g)=\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)+\Delta_{\rm aff}(g).}
\tag{6}
\]

The first identity follows because the squared Fourier law is a product and `(S,U)` splits blockwise. For the second, use exact tensorization of `M` and

`(f\otimes g)_{(z,w)}=f_z\otimes g_w`.

Therefore any constant in (2) is preserved under arbitrary finite tensor products. Both quantities are also unchanged by characters and invertible affine input changes. Under a surjective affine lift, the Fourier law is injectively relabelled, while a uniform physical shift projects to a uniform shift in the target, so both `L` and `Delta_aff` are preserved.

This is why `w_3^{tensor 22}`, which refutes the old coefficient-four `D_cross<=4(v+delta)` inequality, is not a hidden obstruction to (5): its `L/Delta_aff` ratio is exactly the single-block ratio.

### 3. The candidate constant three is proved for all one-point defects

Let

\[
w_n(x)=1-2\mathbf1_{\{x=0\}},\qquad N=2^n.
\]

For `n=1` this is a character and both sides of (5) vanish. For `n>=2`, its squared Fourier law has

\[
A=\frac{(N-2)^2}{N^2}\quad\text{at }0,
\qquad b=\frac4{N^2}\quad\text{at each }s\ne0.
\tag{7}
\]

The affine minimum and its one-step drift are exact:

\[
M(w_n)=\frac{2n}{N},\qquad
\boxed{\Delta_{\rm aff}(w_n)=\frac{4(N+n-1)}{N^2}.}
\tag{8}
\]

For (8), a nonzero shift gives an affine lift of `w_{n-1}`, while the zero shift gives a constant.

For the XOR law `q=p*p`,

\[
q_0=A^2+(N-1)b^2,
\qquad
q_u=2Ab+(N-2)b^2=\frac{8(N-2)}{N^3}\quad(u\ne0).
\tag{9}
\]

Using `L=2H(p)-H(q)` in its conditional-entropy form gives the exact decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
L(w_n)=&\ A^2\log_2\frac{q_0}{A^2}
 +(N-1)b^2\log_2\frac{q_0}{b^2}\\
&+(N-1)\left[2Ab\log_2\frac{q_1}{Ab}
 +(N-2)b^2\log_2\frac{q_1}{b^2}\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]

For `n>=8`, use `ln 2>2/3` and `ln(1+x)<=x`. Since

\[
\frac{q_1}{Ab}=\frac{2N}{N-2},\qquad
\frac{q_1}{b^2}=\frac{N(N-2)}2,
\]

(10) yields

\[
L(w_n)\le \frac8N+\frac{32n+8}{N^2}+\frac{64n}{N^3}.
\tag{11}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
3\Delta_{\rm aff}(w_n)=\frac{12}{N}+\frac{12(n-1)}{N^2}.
\]

The difference between this last expression and the right side of (11) is

\[
\frac4N-\frac{20n+20}{N^2}-\frac{64n}{N^3}.
\]

It is positive for `n>=8`: after multiplication by `N`, the two subtracted terms are decreasing in `n`, and at `n=8` their sum is `180/256+512/65536<1<4`.

The exact checker certifies the finite core `2<=n<=7` with outward rational logarithm intervals. Therefore

\[
\boxed{L(w_n)\le3\Delta_{\rm aff}(w_n)\quad\text{for every }n.}
\tag{12}
\]

This is a stress-family theorem, not unrestricted FEI.

### 4. Quadratic phases satisfy the stronger identity `L=Delta_aff`

Let `q=(-1)^Q` have polar rank `rho`. Its squared Fourier law is uniform on an affine coset of a `rho`-dimensional subspace. Therefore `U=S xor T` is uniform on the corresponding linear subspace, and conditional on `U`, `S` is still uniform on the original affine coset. Hence

\[
L(q)=\rho.
\]

The affine-variance report proves `M(q)=rho/2`. Every multiplicative derivative of a quadratic phase is a character or a constant, so its affine minimum is zero. Thus

\[
\boxed{L(q)=\Delta_{\rm aff}(q)=\rho.}
\tag{13}
\]

Combining (6), (12), and (13) proves (5) for every finite tensor product of one-point defects and quadratic phases, and for every character multiple, invertible affine image, or surjective affine lift of such a product. This class includes arbitrary tensor amplification of the principal rare-defect and bent/quadratic stress families.

### 5. A universal basis-exchange lower bound for the affine drift

B11 gives a structural lower bound explaining what a proof of (5) must exploit.

Choose an affine representative and an optimal input basis `B=(v_1,...,v_n)` for which

\[
q_i=\alpha_f(v_i)=j_f(v_i)\le1/2,
\qquad M(f)=\sum_iq_i.
\]

For a shift `z`, write `z=sum_i xi_i(z)v_i` and put

\[
m_B(z)=\max_{i:\xi_i(z)=1}j_{f_z}(v_i),
\qquad m_B(0)=0.
\tag{14}
\]

Because `f_z` is invariant in direction `z`, its directional cost at `z` is zero. For every nonzero `z`, replacing any basis vector with `xi_i(z)=1` by `z` gives another basis. Choosing the most expensive removable vector gives

\[
M(f_z)\le\sum_i j_{f_z}(v_i)-m_B(z).
\tag{15}
\]

For each fixed `i`, with `u_i(x)=f(x)f(x+v_i)`,

\[
\mathbb E_z\alpha_{f_z}(v_i)
=\frac12\left[1-(\mathbb E u_i)^2\right]
=2q_i(1-q_i).
\]

Since `j<=alpha`, averaging (15) proves the universal estimate

\[
\boxed{\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)
\ge2\sum_iq_i^2+\mathbb E_z m_B(z).}
\tag{16}
\]

The first term is the fixed-basis dissipation; the second is the exact one-pivot saving forced by the new zero-cost derivative direction. This is strictly more informative than the coordinatewise comparison alone.

It is not yet enough to prove (5). The exact rank representation shows the missing mechanism: a derivative can create several new cheap directions at once, and an optimal descendant basis may exploit all of them, not only the single forced direction `z`. In rank form,

\[
\Delta_{\rm aff}(f)=\int_0^{1/2}
\left(2r_t(f)-\mathbb E_z r_t(f_z)\right)dt,
\qquad r_t(f)=n-\dim L_t(f).
\tag{17}
\]

A successful continuation must charge the conditional allocation uncertainty `L(f)` to this **full expected rank-filtration drop**, rather than to one local resolvent, one scalar marginal budget, or one basis pivot.

This is the present exact proof obligation.
