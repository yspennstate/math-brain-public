## 2. B14 + B29: quotient exact gradients first; holonomy becomes a character on the compact dual torus

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected graph with an orientation of its edges. Pin one vertex so that integer height gradients form a subgroup

\[
H:=\{dh:h\in\mathbb Z^{V},\ h(v_0)=0\}\subset\mathbb Z^E.
\]

Fix an integer background one-form `b in Z^E`. The affine winding sector is the coset

\[
b+H.
\]

For each edge let

\[
a_e(n):=e^{-V_e(n)},\qquad
w_e(\theta):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_e(n)e^{in\theta},
\]

and assume the summability needed for the Fourier manipulations below. Write

\[
H^\perp:=\{J\in\mathbb T^E:\langle x,J\rangle\in2\pi\mathbb Z\ \forall x\in H\}
\]

for the compact annihilator of the exact-gradient subgroup. This is the coclosed spin/dual group. The affine-sector Gibbs law is

\[
\nu_b(n)=Z_b^{-1}\prod_{e\in E}a_e(n_e),\qquad n\in b+H.
\]

### NEW-I0007-L5 — exact affine-coset Fourier–Pontryagin identity

For every real edge one-form `epsilon`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\nu_b}e^{i\langle n,\epsilon\rangle}
=
\frac{\displaystyle
\int_{H^\perp}e^{-i\langle b,J\rangle}
\prod_{e\in E}w_e(J_e+\epsilon_e)\,dJ}
{\displaystyle
\int_{H^\perp}e^{-i\langle b,J\rangle}
\prod_{e\in E}w_e(J_e)\,dJ}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Fourier inversion gives, edge by edge,

\[
a_e(n)e^{in\epsilon_e}
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb T}w_e(J_e+\epsilon_e)e^{-inJ_e}\,dJ_e.
\]

Write `n=b+x`, `x in H`, multiply over edges, and sum over `x in H`. The character sum over `H` is the Haar delta imposing `J in H^perp`; the fixed background contributes exactly

\[
e^{-i\langle b,J\rangle}.
\]

The same calculation at `epsilon=0` gives the denominator and hence (2.1). If `b` is changed by an exact element of `H`, its extra character is identically one on `H^perp`, so (2.1) depends only on the cohomology/holonomy class `[b] in Z^E/H`. If `[b]=0`, the character is trivial and the usual untwisted duality is recovered. QED.

This is the B14 group reduction: the correct invariant is the coset/cohomology class, not a particular affine representative. It is also B29 in the literal compactification sense: after Pontryagin duality, the winding lives as a character on the compact torus `H^perp`.

Assume now `w_e>0` so that `U_e=-log w_e` is real. Define the normalized twisted dual functional

\[
\langle F\rangle_b^\vee
:=
\frac{
\int_{H^\perp}e^{-i\langle b,J\rangle}e^{-\sum_eU_e(J_e)}F(J)\,dJ}
{
\int_{H^\perp}e^{-i\langle b,J\rangle}e^{-\sum_eU_e(J_e)}\,dJ}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The denominator is the original positive affine-sector partition function up to the harmless Fourier normalization, hence it is real and nonzero. But when `[b] != 0`, the numerator density in (2.2) is generally complex because of the character.

Differentiate the logarithm of (2.1) twice at zero. For real edge forms `epsilon,omega`, one obtains the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_b}(\langle n,\epsilon\rangle,\langle n,\omega\rangle)
+
\operatorname{Cov}_{b}^\vee(\langle U'(J),\epsilon\rangle,\langle U'(J),\omega\rangle)
=
\sum_{e\in E}\langle U_e''(J_e)\rangle_b^\vee\epsilon_e\omega_e.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For `[b]=0`, the dual functional is a probability measure and the second term on the left is nonnegative when `epsilon=omega`; this is the sign exploited by the van Engelenburg–Lis GFF comparison. For nontrivial winding, (2.2) is a character-twisted complex functional, so there is no such positivity to discard.

**This is the exact algebraic point where the literal untwisted GFF proof stops.** It is stronger and more precise than the previous report's statement that there is a vague “topological obstruction”: the winding does not destroy Fourier duality; it inserts a disorder character that destroys positivity.

## 3. B2: exact finite counterexample to the verbatim same-constant twisted GFF bound

The loss of positivity might in principle be only a proof defect. I therefore attacked the strongest naive transfer directly.

Take the oriented four-cycle `C4`. Let integer edge increments `n=(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)` satisfy the nontrivial winding constraint

\[
n_1+n_2+n_3+n_4=2.
\]

Use the discrete-Gaussian potential

\[
V(n)=\beta n^2,
\]

and set

\[
q=e^{-\beta}=\frac1{100}.
\]

Thus the sector weight is `q^(sum_i n_i^2)`. Pin `h_0=0` and look at the opposite height

\[
h_2=n_1+n_2.
\]

The involution

\[
n_i\mapsto1-n_i
\]

preserves the winding because `sum_i(1-n_i)=2`, and preserves the energy because

\[
\sum_i(1-n_i)^2=\sum_i n_i^2-2\sum_i n_i+4=\sum_i n_i^2.
\]

It sends `h_2` to `2-h_2`, so

\[
\mathbb E h_2=1.
\]

The ground states are exactly the six vectors with two `1`s and two `0`s, each of energy `2`. Exactly two of them have `h_2` equal to `0` or `2`, hence

\[
\sum_{\sum n_i=2} q^{\sum n_i^2}(h_2-1)^2\ge2q^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let

\[
F(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2}t^n.
\]

The partition function is

\[
Z_2=[t^2]F(t)^4.
\]

Since the coefficients are nonnegative, evaluation at `t=q^{-1}` gives

\[
Z_2\le q^2F(q^{-1})^4.
\]

Now

\[
F(q^{-1})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n(n-1)}
=2\sum_{k\ge0}q^{k(k+1)}
\le\frac{2}{1-q^2},
\]

because `k(k+1)>=2k` for `k>=0`. Hence

\[
Z_2\le\frac{16q^2}{(1-q^2)^4}.
\]

Combining with (3.1),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_b}(h_2)
\ge\frac{(1-q^2)^4}{8}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For the **untwisted** van Engelenburg–Lis comparison constant, write

\[
w(\theta)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}q^{n^2}e^{in\theta},\qquad U=-\log w,
\]

and

\[
C_\beta:=\sup_\theta|U''(\theta)|.
\]

Use `q^(n^2)<=q^n` for `n>=1` to obtain

\[
w(\theta)\ge1-2\sum_{n\ge1}q^{n^2}
\ge1-\frac{2q}{1-q}
=\frac{1-3q}{1-q}=:m>0,
\]

\[
|w'(\theta)|\le2\sum_{n\ge1}nq^{n^2}
\le\frac{2q}{(1-q)^2}=:2S_1,
\]

and

\[
|w''(\theta)|\le2\sum_{n\ge1}n^2q^{n^2}
\le\frac{2q(1+q)}{(1-q)^3}=:2S_2.
\]

Since

\[
U''=-\frac{w''}{w}+\left(\frac{w'}w\right)^2,
\]

we have the rigorous bound

\[
\boxed{
C_\beta\le\frac{2S_2}{m}+\frac{4S_1^2}{m^2}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

At `q=1/100`, the exact checker gives

\[

=\frac{9996000599960001}{80000000000000000},
\]

whereas the right side of (3.3) is

\[
\frac{1999400}{92217609}.
\]

The effective resistance between opposite vertices of the unit-conductance `C4` is exactly `1` (two length-two paths in parallel). The checker verifies strictly

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_{\text{winding }2}(h_2)
>
C_\beta R_{\rm eff}(0,2).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
