## 4. B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY: exact Hodge covariance identity for reversible jump dynamics

This subsection is a complete functional-analytic statement. It is a generic reversible-chain theorem and is **not claimed as novel**.

### 4.1 Setup

Let `(Omega,mu)` be a finite or countable state space with a reversible continuous-time jump generator

\[
(Lf)(\eta)=\sum_{\zeta}q(\eta,\zeta)[f(\zeta)-f(\eta)],
\]

where

\[
\mu(\eta)q(\eta,\zeta)
=\mu(\zeta)q(\zeta,\eta).
\]

Assume the associated Dirichlet form is closed. Choose one orientation of every unoriented transition edge and write `e^-`, `e^+` for its endpoints. Put

\[
c_e:=\mu(e^-)q(e^-,e^+)=\mu(e^+)q(e^+,e^-)>0.
\]

On `H_0=L^2(mu)` and the one-form space `H_1=ell^2(E)` define

\[
(df)(e)=\sqrt{c_e}\,[f(e^+)-f(e^-)].
\]

Then for form-domain functions `f,g`, reversibility gives

\[
\langle df,dg\rangle_{H_1}
=\frac12\sum_{\eta,\zeta}\mu(\eta)q(\eta,\zeta)
[f(\zeta)-f(\eta)][g(\zeta)-g(\eta)]
=\langle f,-Lg\rangle_{\mu}.
\]

Hence with

\[
A:=d^*d=-L,\qquad B:=dd^*,
\]

both `A` and `B` are nonnegative self-adjoint operators on their natural closed-form domains.

### 4.2 Exact resolvent intertwining

For every `epsilon>0`,

\[
\boxed{
 d(A+\epsilon)^{-1}=(B+\epsilon)^{-1}d.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, on a core,

\[
(B+\epsilon)d
=(dd^*+\epsilon)d
=d(d^*d+\epsilon)
=d(A+\epsilon),
\]

and multiplication by the bounded resolvents gives (4.1); closure extends the identity to the form domain.

Taking the adjoint on the left yields

\[
\boxed{
 d^*(B+\epsilon)^{-1}d
=A(A+\epsilon)^{-1}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore, for `F,G` in the form domain,

\[
\langle dF,(B+\epsilon)^{-1}dG\rangle_{H_1}
=
\langle F,A(A+\epsilon)^{-1}G\rangle_{\mu}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If the reversible chain is ergodic, `ker A` consists of constants. For centered `G`, the spectral theorem gives

\[
A(A+\epsilon)^{-1}G\longrightarrow G
\quad\text{in }L^2(\mu)
\qquad(\epsilon\downarrow0),
\]

because the multiplier `lambda/(lambda+epsilon)` converges boundedly to one off `lambda=0`, while `G` has no component in the zero eigenspace. Consequently, for centered `F,G`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu}(F,G)
=
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}
\langle dF,(B+\epsilon)^{-1}dG\rangle_{H_1}.
}
\tag{HODGE-COV}
\]

This is an exact discrete configuration-space covariance representation. It is the cleanest literal part of the Helffer--Sjöstrand port.

### 4.3 What HODGE-COV does and does not supply

For a linear height observable

\[
F(\phi)=\sum_x f_x\phi_x,
\]

and a single-site transition `phi -> phi +/- delta_x`, its configuration derivative is exactly `+/- f_x`, up to the conductance square root in `d`. Thus HODGE-COV remembers the physical site label of the jump.

However, Dario's method needs much more: the one-form resolvent must be interpretable through a positivity-preserving, spatially local random-conductance evolution. HODGE-COV alone does not imply such a structure. B19 tests that point exactly.

## 5. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM, THEN IDENTIFY THE EXACT FAILURE

### 5.1 The direct port algorithm

In the quadratic diagnostic model, take a finite pinned or twisted volume and Hamiltonian

\[
H(\phi)=\frac\beta2\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}
(\phi_y-\phi_x-\theta_{xy})^2,
\qquad \phi\in\mathbb Z^{\Lambda},
\]

where the fixed bond offsets `theta_xy` encode the prescribed winding/slope sector. Consider the symmetric exponential single-site jump rates

\[
q_x^+(\phi)
=
\exp\left\{-\frac12[H(\phi+\delta_x)-H(\phi)]\right\},
\]

\[
q_x^-(\phi)
=
\exp\left\{-\frac12[H(\phi-\delta_x)-H(\phi)]\right\}.
\]

They satisfy detailed balance exactly:

\[
e^{-H(\phi)}q_x^+(\phi)
=
e^{-\frac12(H(\phi)+H(\phi+\delta_x))}
=
e^{-H(\phi+\delta_x)}q_x^-(\phi+\delta_x).
\]

The tempting algorithm is:

1. use HODGE-COV to represent height covariance by `(B+epsilon)^{-1}`;
2. interpret `B=dd^*` as a Markov/random-conductance generator on transition one-forms;
3. apply a Dario/Mourrat--Otto type anchored Nash estimate to that process;
4. preserve the physical jump-site coordinate, Fourier-transform vertically, and extract a mass `c sin^2(pi m/L)` for each relative mode.

Step 2 already fails for the canonical local one-form basis.

### 5.2 Exact three-edge sign obstruction

Represent `d` in the orthonormal vertex basis of `L^2(mu)`. For an oriented transition edge `e` incident to a state `v`, let

\[
\sigma_e(v)=
\begin{cases}
+1,&v=e^+,\\
-1,&v=e^-.
\end{cases}
\]

The corresponding incidence entry is

\[
d_{e,v}=\sigma_e(v)\sqrt{\frac{c_e}{\mu(v)}}.
\]

Take three distinct transition edges `e_1,e_2,e_3` sharing a state `v` and otherwise having distinct endpoints. In the canonical edge basis, the off-diagonal entries of `B=dd^*` are

\[
B_{ij}
=
\sigma_{e_i}(v)\sigma_{e_j}(v)
\frac{\sqrt{c_{e_i}c_{e_j}}}{\mu(v)},
\qquad i\ne j.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore the sign product around this three-edge clique is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{sgn}(B_{12}B_{13}B_{23})
=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)(\sigma_1\sigma_3)(\sigma_2\sigma_3)
=+1.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Changing the orientation of any edge merely flips the sign of the corresponding row/column of `B`; equivalently it conjugates by a diagonal `+/-1` matrix. The triangle sign product (5.2) is invariant under every such gauge.

By contrast, if a symmetric positive operator is `-Q` for a continuous-time Markov generator in a local state basis, every nonzero off-diagonal entry is nonpositive. On a three-clique their product would therefore be negative. Since the entries in (5.1) are all nonzero, (5.2) rules this out.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The canonical Hodge one-form operator }dd^*
\text{ cannot be made into a Markov M-matrix by edge reorientation.}
}
\tag{NEW-I0007-L4}
\]

This is a local algebraic obstruction. It applies immediately to a single-site `+/-1` configuration graph, because every configuration has many distinct incident transitions.

The statement is deliberately narrow: it does **not** prove that no nonlocal transform, enlarged state space, monotone coupling, renormalized operator, or different discrete covariance representation can work. It proves that the most literal `d d^*` analogue of Dario's positive conductance walk is not itself such a walk in the canonical transition-edge representation.

## 6. B28 — REPLACE UNIFORM CONTROL BY TYPICAL CONTROL: inverse-rate moments in the quadratic diagnostic model

The sign obstruction says the canonical Hodge operator is not a Markov generator. Before abandoning the Dario analogy entirely, it is useful to ask whether a second possible obstruction — extremely small jump conductances — is already fatal in the clean quadratic case.

It is not.

### 6.1 Exact single-site conditional law

At a site `x` of degree `r=2d`, after conditioning on all other heights, the quadratic Hamiltonian as a function of `n=phi_x` has the form

\[
H_x(n)=a(n-t)^2+C,
\qquad
 a=\frac{\beta r}{2}=\beta d,
\tag{6.1}
\]

for some real center `t` determined by the neighboring heights and any twist offsets. Therefore

\[
\mathbb P_t(N=n)
=
\frac{e^{-a(n-t)^2}}{Z_a(t)},
\qquad
Z_a(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-a(n-t)^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For the `+1` rate,

\[
H_x(n+1)-H_x(n)=a[2(n-t)+1],
\]

so

\[
q^+(n)=e^{-a(n-t+1/2)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### 6.2 Uniform inverse moments of every order

For every `p>0`, completing the square gives exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_t[(q^+(N))^{-p}]
&=
\frac1{Z_a(t)}
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}
\exp\{-a(n-t)^2+ap(n-t)+ap/2\}\\
&=
\exp\left\{a\left(\frac{p^2}{4}+\frac p2\right)\right\}
\frac{Z_a(t+p/2)}{Z_a(t)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This ratio is uniform in the center `t`. Indeed, by taking the nearest integer to `t`,

\[
Z_a(t)\ge e^{-a/4}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For every shift, the standard sum-versus-integral bound for the unimodal Gaussian gives

\[
Z_a(t)\le 1+\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ax^2}\,dx
=1+\sqrt{\frac\pi a}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Combining (6.4)--(6.6),

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
\mathbb E_t[(q^+(N))^{-p}]
\le
\left(1+\sqrt{\frac\pi a}\right)
\exp\left\{\frac a4(p+1)^2\right\}
<\infty.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

The identical argument applies to `q^-`. Since the bound is conditional on an arbitrary environment, it also holds after averaging over the Gibbs measure. It is independent of volume and of the prescribed twist/slope.

Hence the quadratic diagnostic has no uniform ellipticity — rare spikes can make a rate arbitrarily small — but it has **all inverse moments of the natural reversible rates**, uniformly in volume and slope sector.

This is useful because Dario's degenerate real-valued argument also replaces pointwise ellipticity by stochastic control of a moderated conductance environment. In the present diagnostic case, the required kind of tail integrability is not obviously the bottleneck. The obstruction found in B19 occurs earlier: the exact one-form covariance operator is not a positivity-preserving local conductance generator.

Again, (6.7) is only a diagnostic theorem for the quadratic discrete interaction and does not move `k_loc`.

## 7. Counterexample tests and literature guardrails

### 7.1 Do not infer a continuous Brascamp--Lieb comparison

One possible shortcut would be to assert that because the quadratic energy is uniformly convex as a real function, the integer-valued Gibbs law satisfies the same covariance domination as its real-valued Gaussian counterpart. That inference is not justified. The integer-valued constraint destroys the literal continuous Hessian integration-by-parts mechanism; existing discrete-Gaussian work uses model-specific RG, Coulomb-gas, or Glauber-dynamics methods rather than a free continuous Brascamp--Lieb transfer.

Therefore this report does **not** claim BLOCK-I0007 from uniform convexity of the quadratic energy.

### 7.2 Do not overinterpret NEW-I0007-L4

The sign-frustration theorem refutes only the most direct algorithm: canonical configuration incidence `d`, canonical edge one-forms, and an interpretation of `dd^*` itself as `-Q` for a local random walk after orientation changes.

It does not refute:

- a heat-bath coupling representation on a different state space;
- a supersymmetric/discrete-gradient operator with an added local term;
- block-spin or hierarchical renormalization;
- a comparison inequality that bypasses a Markov representation entirely;
- a nonlocal similarity transform, provided one can still recover usable spatial heat-kernel information.

This distinction matters because discrete Gaussian Glauber models are known to admit multiscale functional-inequality control in hierarchical settings.
