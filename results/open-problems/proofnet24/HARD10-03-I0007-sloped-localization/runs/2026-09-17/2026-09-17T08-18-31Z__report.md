## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The preceding run found the exact zero-temperature block flux and its obstruction: after fixing a coordinate-fiber flux, the convex ground energy is piecewise affine across each integer-gradient cell, so deterministic energy creates no uniform coarse-flux curvature.

The natural next question is whether **finite-temperature entropy repairs exactly this defect**. I therefore dualized the constrained fiber partition function, solved the extremal two-level cell exactly, and counted the same constrained states both as gradients and as extra level crossings.

The answer is two-sided:

- entropy really does generate the missing `1/ell` curvature on a single fiber, with the weakest point exactly at half filling;
- but this cannot be promoted directly to I0007, because global curl compatibility couples fibers, and because strict slope free-energy convexity is already known in the general Sheffield theory without implying localization.

So this run identifies a real entropic stiffness but also disproves the stronger methodological premise that such stiffness alone is the missing theorem.

## 4. B11 — DUALIZE: exact canonical / grand-canonical identity

Fix a discrete convex/coercive interaction `V:Z -> R` and inverse-temperature parameter `beta>0`. For a fiber of length `ell`, define the finite-temperature constrained partition function

\[
Z_{\ell}(a)
:=
\sum_{\substack{z_1,\ldots,z_\ell\in\mathbb Z\\z_1+\cdots+z_\ell=a}}
\exp\!\left[-\beta\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}V(z_j)\right]
\tag{4.1}
\]

and constrained free energy

\[
A_{\ell}(a):=-\log Z_{\ell}(a).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Whenever the one-site moment generating sum is finite, set

\[
M(h):=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\beta V(z)+hz},
\qquad
\Psi_\ell(h):=\ell\log M(h).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The generating-function identity is exact:

\[
M(h)^\ell=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}Z_\ell(a)e^{ha}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define the tilted one-site law

\[
p_h(z)=\frac{e^{-\beta V(z)+hz}}{M(h)},
\]

and let `S_ell=X_1+...+X_ell` under the iid tilted product measure. Then

\[
\mathbb P_h(S_\ell=a)
=
Z_\ell(a)e^{ha-\Psi_\ell(h)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Rearranging gives the exact dual decomposition

\[
\boxed{
A_\ell(a)
=
ha-\Psi_\ell(h)-\log\mathbb P_h(S_\ell=a).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This identity is valid for every admissible `h`; it is not an asymptotic saddle-point formula.

If `h=h(a)` is chosen so that `E_h S_ell=a` (when such a choice exists), the first two terms are the Legendre part. On a continuous interpolation, its curvature is

\[
\frac{d h}{d a}
=
\frac{1}{\operatorname{Var}_h(S_\ell)}
=
\frac{1}{\ell\operatorname{Var}_h(X_1)}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus the finite-temperature flux stiffness is the inverse susceptibility **plus the exact lattice local-probability correction** `-log P_h(S_ell=a)`. This pinpoints what any multiscale use of B11 would have to control.

There is an additional classical fact, used only as orientation and not claimed as novel: because `e^{-beta V(z)}` is a log-concave sequence for discrete convex `V`, convolution preserves log-concavity, so `a -> Z_ell(a)` is log-concave and `A_ell(a)` is discretely convex. The issue is not the sign of the curvature; it is its quantitative strength after global curl compatibility is imposed.

**B11 outcome:** success as an exact dual reduction. It converts the missing entropy term into susceptibility plus a local central-probability term. It does **not** by itself move `k_loc`.

## 5. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: solve one affine cell exactly

The preceding run showed that the zero-temperature energy is affine whenever the mean flux remains between two adjacent integer gradients. That is therefore the hardest place for an energy-based proof. I solved exactly the finite-temperature entropy in this cell.

Fix consecutive integer gradients `q,q+1` and restrict the fiber increments to

\[
z_j\in\{q,q+1\}.
\]

Write the total flux as

\[
a=q\ell+r,\qquad r\in\{0,1,\ldots,\ell\}.
\]

Exactly `r` of the `ell` increments must equal `q+1`; all such configurations have the same energy. Hence

\[
\boxed{
Z^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(r)
=
{\ell\choose r}
\exp\{-\beta[(\ell-r)V(q)+rV(q+1)]\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
A^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(r):=-\log Z^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(r).
\]

For `1<=r<=ell-1`, the linear energy cancels from the discrete second difference, leaving

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^2 A^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(r)
&:=A^{(2)}(r+1)-2A^{(2)}(r)+A^{(2)}(r-1)\\
&=\log\frac{{\ell\choose r}^2}{{\ell\choose r-1}{\ell\choose r+1}}\\
&=\boxed{\log\frac{(r+1)(\ell-r+1)}{r(\ell-r)}}\\
&=\log\!\left(1+\frac1r\right)
 +\log\!\left(1+\frac1{\ell-r}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Since `log(1+x)>=x/(1+x)` for `x>=0`,

\[
\Delta^2 A^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(r)
\ge
\frac1{r+1}+\frac1{\ell-r+1}
\ge
\boxed{\frac4{\ell+2}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The curvature is symmetric under `r <-> ell-r`. Treating `r` continuously for the monotonicity check,

\[
\frac{d}{dr}
\left[
\log\!\left(1+\frac1r\right)
+
\log\!\left(1+\frac1{\ell-r}\right)
\right]
=
-\frac1{r(r+1)}
+
\frac1{(\ell-r)(\ell-r+1)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore it decreases to the midpoint and increases after the midpoint. The **weakest entropic stiffness is half filling**, `r≈ell/2`; for even `ell`,

\[
\Delta^2 A^{(2)}_{\ell,q}(\ell/2)
=2\log\left(1+\frac2\ell\right)
\sim\frac4\ell.
\tag{5.5}
\]

So finite-temperature combinatorial entropy repairs the zero-temperature affine cell at precisely the scale one would hope for from an extensive free energy: curvature `Theta(1/ell)` in total flux.

The same conclusion appears on the dual side. In the two-level model the grand-canonical occupation of `q+1` is Bernoulli with some parameter `p`, so

\[
\operatorname{Var}(S_\ell)=\ell p(1-p).
\]

Susceptibility is maximal at `p=1/2`, exactly the same point at which the canonical curvature is weakest.

**B24 outcome:** success. The extremal fraction is identified exactly, and entropy supplies a scale-compatible `1/ell` curvature inside the very cells where ground energy has zero curvature. This is a complete restricted fiber theorem, not a localization theorem.

## 6. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: gradients versus extra level crossings

Equation (5.1) has an exact double interpretation.

### Count 1: gradients

Choose which `r` of the `ell` edge increments take the upper value `q+1`. This gives `{ell choose r}` configurations.

### Count 2: extra wall crossings

Relative to the baseline of `q` units of signed crossing on each edge, an upper increment `q+1` contributes one additional signed level crossing. Choosing the locations of the `r` extra crossings along the fiber gives the same `{ell choose r}` count.

Thus the entropy term absent from the zero-temperature block energy is exactly

\[
-\log{\ell\choose r},
\tag{6.1}
\]

and its discrete curvature is exactly (5.2).

This is the clean local mechanism sought in the previous run. But it immediately exposes the gluing problem: **different fibers cannot be counted independently**, because a global height function is a curl-free gradient and its level crossings must assemble into nested surfaces. Multiplying the binomial counts over all fibers badly overcounts configurations that cannot coexist globally.

### A compatible exact global ground state

There is nevertheless an exact multi-slope construction that shows the local balanced constraints are mutually compatible at least at one configuration.

Consider an `L^3` twisted torus with integer windings `m_1,m_2,m_3`. Write

\[
m_i=q_iL+r_i,\qquad 0\le r_i<L.
\]

For any real phase `t`, define on the universal cover

\[
\boxed{
\phi_t(x)
=
\left\lfloor
 t+\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2+m_3x_3}{L}
\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\phi_t(x+Le_i)=\phi_t(x)+m_i,
\tag{6.3}
\]

so this has exactly the required windings. Along every coordinate-`i` fiber, each increment is either `q_i` or `q_i+1`; telescoping over one period shows that exactly `r_i` increments take the upper value. Consequently every coordinate fiber realizes the balanced minimizer from the previous run:

\[
\sum_{e\text{ on an }i\text{-fiber}}V_i(\nabla_i\phi_t(e))
=F_{V_i,L}(m_i).
\tag{6.4}
\]

For any configuration in the same winding sector, applying the one-fiber lower bound to all `L^2` coordinate-`i` fibers gives

\[
H(\phi)\ge
\sum_{i=1}^3L^2F_{V_i,L}(m_i).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The rounded plane (6.2) attains equality simultaneously in all three directions. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\phi_t\text{ is a global convex ground configuration for every winding vector }m.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The construction is elementary and may be folklore; **publication novelty is unverified**. Its role here is structural: positive multi-slope flux is compatible with simultaneous one-fiber balancing, so the missing theorem is not existence of a low-energy sloped state.

The preceding report already exhibited a much larger separable ground family for two-slope windings. Together, these facts show that the finite-temperature problem is an **order-by-disorder / compatibility problem on a large balanced ground manifold**, not a rare-defect perturbation of finitely many states.

**B25 outcome:** success locally and globally as a structural calculation, but failure as a direct factorization route. The one-fiber entropy is exact; lifting it requires controlling curl/nesting correlations across fibers.

## 7. Counterexample to the proposed transfer: free-energy curvature is not localization

The preceding three calculations might tempt one to declare the missing theorem to be uniform strict convexity of the block free energy in slope. That transfer is too strong, and existing primary literature already refutes the methodological implication.

Sheffield's *Random Surfaces* proves strict convexity of surface tension in the broad convex-gradient setting. Yet the 2026 Adhikari--Gheissari--Peled paper still states the all-slopes `d>=3` localization theorem as a conjecture and proves only the two-zero-coordinate regime. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{strict surface-tension convexity alone does not supply the missing localization argument.}}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The two-dimensional theory makes the separation even more concrete: Lammers proves logarithmically diverging pointwise variance in delocalized regimes of convex integer-valued height models. Thus a positive slope susceptibility / convex thermodynamic rate function can coexist with rough spatial height fluctuations in a neighboring dimension.

The actual missing input must control the **spatial infrared modes** of the curl-compatible field, not merely the scalar total-flux variable.

This is the main negative result of the run. The previous proposed milestone — prove strict convexity of the finite-temperature constrained flux free energy — is now known to be **insufficient on its own**, even though the one-fiber version is true and quantitatively sharp.

## 8. What a successful `k_loc=2` proof now appears to need

The source-known `k_loc=1` theorem exploits two zero-slope directions. For a two-nonzero-coordinate slope in `d=3`, the new calculations suggest the following mechanism:

1. the sloped ground manifold may be effectively two-dimensional and have many balanced states;
2. at any finite temperature, fluctuations in the third direction create a positive transverse coupling between these two-dimensional slices;
3. a genuinely three-dimensional massless field can have bounded point variance even though the decoupled two-dimensional limit is rough.

This points away from scalar slope curvature and toward an **anisotropic infrared / conductance estimate**. A sufficient benchmark would be a source-valid bound of the schematic form

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\Lambda,s}\big(\phi(0)-\text{affine}_s(0)\big)
\le
C
\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}
\frac{dk}
{\kappa_1\sin^2(k_1/2)+\kappa_2\sin^2(k_2/2)+\kappa_3\sin^2(k_3/2)},
\tag{8.1}
\]

with `kappa_i>0` depending on the frozen interactions/temperature but not on volume or the allowed two-coordinate slope. The integral is finite in three dimensions. Equation (8.1) is **not proved here**; it is a precise target explaining what kind of spatial stiffness would actually imply tightness.

The essential question is whether the low-temperature integer model admits any source-compatible route to such a positive transverse stiffness uniformly over slope, despite the large balanced ground-state manifold.
