## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The previous run established two facts that pull in opposite directions:

- on a single constrained fiber, finite-temperature entropy repairs the zero-temperature affine cell and creates curvature of order `1/ell` in the total flux;
- strict slope/free-energy curvature by itself is not localization, because it controls a spatially uniform response rather than every infrared mode.

The next question is therefore not whether the thermodynamic free energy is curved, but whether positive coupling in the *third spatial direction* converts the potentially rough two-dimensional layer modes into an integrable three-dimensional covariance kernel.

For a slope `(s_1,s_2,0)`, introduce a parameter `tau` multiplying the interaction on `e_3` edges. At `tau=0` one has a stack of uncoupled two-dimensional sloped layers; at every `tau>0` the geometry is genuinely three-dimensional. This is a singular interpolation, not a perturbative one.

The run has three outcomes:

1. **B5:** an exact derivative identity isolates the correlation inequality one would need to control the interpolation. It also shows why a naive first-order perturbation from `tau=0` is the wrong scale.
2. **B18:** a complete anisotropic Gaussian/Dirichlet-form theorem proves that *arbitrarily weak positive transverse stiffness is already enough for pointwise localization in three dimensions*, with only logarithmic blow-up as `tau downarrow 0`. It yields a precise conditional covariance-comparison criterion sufficient for `k_loc>=2`.
3. **B23:** an exact vertical-Fourier decomposition explains how an infinite stack can localize collectively even when every individual two-dimensional layer remains rough. The single rough common mode is diluted by `1/L`; the nonzero vertical modes acquire masses whose average is summable.

The new theorem is a neighboring-model theorem and a reduction, not a theorem for the integer-valued source model. Therefore the frozen metric does not move.

## 4. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: transverse coupling is singular

Fix a finite box with the required sloped boundary sector, and write

\[
H_\tau(\phi)=H_{12}(\phi)+\tau H_3(\phi),\qquad \tau>0,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `H_12` contains all `e_1,e_2` edge energies and `H_3` contains all `e_3` edge energies. Let `mu_tau` be the corresponding finite-volume Gibbs law. Coercivity makes every differentiation below legitimate in finite volume.

For every observable `F`, differentiating the normalized Gibbs expectation gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\tau}\,\mathbb E_\tau F
=-\operatorname{Cov}_\tau(F,H_3).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `X` be the height fluctuation at a fixed vertex after subtracting any deterministic affine reference. Since

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\tau X
=\mathbb E_\tau X^2-(\mathbb E_\tau X)^2,
\]

(4.2) yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{d\tau}\operatorname{Var}_\tau X
=-\operatorname{Cov}_\tau\!\left((X-\mathbb E_\tau X)^2,H_3\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is useful because it states the missing monotonicity exactly. A Rayleigh-style claim that increasing transverse coupling reduces point variance would follow from the nontrivial correlation inequality

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_\tau\!\left((X-\mathbb E_\tau X)^2,H_3\right)\ge0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

But neither factor in (4.4) is coordinatewise monotone in the height variables, so ordinary FKG does not supply this sign. I do **not** assume (4.4).

More importantly, the solvable harmonic model below shows that the interpolation is singular at `tau=0`: the variance changes from logarithmically divergent in system size at `tau=0` to uniformly finite for every fixed `tau>0`, while the infinite-volume variance itself diverges like `log(1/tau)` as `tau downarrow0`. Therefore a bounded Taylor expansion around the decoupled point cannot be the right proof mechanism.

**B5 outcome:** exact reduction, but no source-level monotonicity theorem. The correct objective is a nonperturbative positive-`tau` covariance comparison, not integration of (4.3) from zero.

## 5. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: anisotropic Dirichlet form and a complete dimension-lift theorem

Consider the harmonic surrogate on `Z^3` with stiffnesses `kappa_1,kappa_2,kappa_3>0` and transverse coupling `tau in (0,1]`:

\[
\mathcal H_\tau(u)
=\frac12\sum_x\left[
\kappa_1(\nabla_1u_x)^2+
\kappa_2(\nabla_2u_x)^2+
\tau\kappa_3(\nabla_3u_x)^2
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its Fourier symbol is

\[
D_\tau(k)=
4\kappa_1\sin^2(k_1/2)+
4\kappa_2\sin^2(k_2/2)+
4\tau\kappa_3\sin^2(k_3/2).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The infinite-volume point Green function is

\[
G_\tau(0)
=\frac1{(2\pi)^3}
\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}\frac{dk}{D_\tau(k)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### NEW-I0007-L3 — anisotropic Gaussian dimension lift

For fixed positive `kappa_1,kappa_2,kappa_3`, there are constants `0<c<C<infinity`, depending only on those stiffnesses, such that for all sufficiently small `tau>0`,

\[
\boxed{
 c\log(1/\tau)
\le G_\tau(0)
\le C\bigl(1+\log(1/\tau)\bigr).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

In particular `G_tau(0)<infinity` for every `tau>0`, while `G_0(0)=infinity`.

### Proof of the upper bound

For `|x|<=pi`,

\[
\sin(|x|/2)\ge |x|/\pi,
\]

so with positive constants `a,b` depending only on the `kappa_i`,

\[
D_\tau(k)
\ge a(k_1^2+k_2^2)+b\tau k_3^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For fixed `k_3`, polar comparison in the `(k_1,k_2)` plane gives

\[
\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^2}
\frac{dk_1dk_2}{a(k_1^2+k_2^2)+b\tau k_3^2}
\le C_1\left[
1+\log\!\left(1+\frac{C_2}{\tau k_3^2}\right)
\right].
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `0<tau<=1`,

\[
\log\!\left(1+\frac{C_2}{\tau k_3^2}\right)
\le C_3+\log(1/\tau)+2|\log|k_3||,
\tag{5.7}
\]

and

\[
\int_0^\pi |\log t|\,dt<\infty.
\]

Integrating (5.6) in `k_3` proves

\[
G_\tau(0)\le C(1+\log(1/\tau)).
\tag{5.8}
\]

### Proof of the lower bound

Restrict to a fixed slab, say `1<=|k_3|<=2`, and to `|k_1|,|k_2|<=delta` for a sufficiently small fixed `delta`. Using `sin(x/2)<=|x|/2`,

\[
D_\tau(k)
\le C_4(k_1^2+k_2^2+\tau)
\tag{5.9}
\]

throughout this slab. Hence

\[
G_\tau(0)
\ge c_1\int_{|q|\le\delta}\frac{dq}{|q|^2+\tau}
\ge c_2\log(1/\tau)-C_5,
\tag{5.10}
\]

which gives the lower half of (5.4) for sufficiently small `tau`.

At `tau=0`, the integrand is independent of `k_3` and the remaining two-dimensional integral has the infrared divergence

\[
\int_{|q|\le\delta}\frac{dq}{|q|^2}=\infty.
\]

This proves NEW-I0007-L3.

### A source-relevant conditional criterion

Let `L_kappa,tau` denote the anisotropic discrete Laplacian whose Dirichlet form is (5.1). Suppose one could prove, for the **actual integer-valued model** at every slope `(s_1,s_2,0)` in the frozen subclass and uniformly in finite volume / admissible boundary conditions, a covariance comparison of the form

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{\Lambda,s}}
\left(\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f_x\bigl(\phi_x-a_s(x)\bigr)\right)
\le
C_0\,\langle f,(-L_{\kappa,1}^{\Lambda})^{-1}f\rangle
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

with constants `C_0<infinity` and `kappa_i>0` depending only on the fixed interaction/temperature subclass, not on `Lambda` or the slope.

Taking `f=delta_0` and using (5.3) at `tau=1` would give

\[
\sup_{\Lambda,s}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{\Lambda,s}}
(\phi_0-a_s(0))<\infty.
\tag{5.12}
\]

Chebyshev then gives pointwise tightness. Applying the same estimate to finitely many sites gives finite-dimensional tightness; a diagonal subsequence supplies infinite-volume subsequential limits, and standard finite-range Gibbs stability gives DLR limits. Thus a source-valid version of (5.11), with the additional convergence/uniqueness input required by the source's exact finite-volume convention, is a direct route to `k_loc>=2`.

The crucial point is that (5.11) is **strictly stronger** than the one-fiber free-energy curvature proved last run. Surface-tension / flux curvature probes the spatially uniform response. Localization needs an inequality controlling the entire low-frequency Dirichlet form.

In continuous uniformly convex `nabla phi` models, Brascamp--Lieb / Helffer--Sjostrand machinery is designed precisely to produce estimates of the shape (5.11). In the present integer-valued low-temperature model, no such uniform comparison has been established here, and the merely convex discrete interactions need not have a positive pointwise second difference. Therefore NEW-I0007-L3 is not silently transferred to the source model.

**B18 outcome:** complete neighboring-model theorem plus a precise conditional bridge. It identifies the missing theorem as a spatial covariance domination, not another scalar free-energy estimate.

## 6. B23 — DO WHAT THE PROPOSER DID NOT EXPECT: let every layer stay rough

The usual instinct is to try to make each two-dimensional sloped layer rigid before coupling the layers. The harmonic calculation shows that this is unnecessary and likely the wrong target.

Take a periodic `L x L x L` harmonic box, remove the global constant mode, and write vertical Fourier frequencies

\[
k_3(m)=\frac{2\pi m}{L},\qquad m=0,\ldots,L-1.
\]

For each vertical mode define its two-dimensional mass

\[
a_m
=4\tau\kappa_3\sin^2(\pi m/L).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then the point variance decomposes exactly as

\[
G_{\tau,L}(0)
=\frac1L\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}g_L(a_m),
\tag{6.2}
\]

where

\[
g_L(a)
=\frac1{L^2}\sum_{(k_1,k_2)}
\frac1{
4\kappa_1\sin^2(k_1/2)+
4\kappa_2\sin^2(k_2/2)+a
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

with the total zero mode omitted when `a=0`.

The massless two-dimensional slice is rough:

\[
g_L(0)\le C\log L.
\tag{6.4}
\]

But in the three-dimensional point variance it appears with weight `1/L`, so

\[
\frac1L g_L(0)\le C\frac{\log L}{L}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `a>0`, standard dyadic annulus counting gives

\[
g_L(a)\le C\bigl(1+\log(1/a)\bigr).
\tag{6.6}
\]

If `q_m=min(m,L-m)`, then for `q_m<=L/2`,

\[
\sin(\pi q_m/L)\ge \frac{2q_m}{L},
\]

hence

\[
a_m\ge c\tau\frac{q_m^2}{L^2}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Combining (6.6)--(6.7),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1L\sum_{m=1}^{L-1}g_L(a_m)
&\le C\left[
1+\log(1/\tau)
+\frac1L\sum_{m=1}^{L-1}\log\frac{L^2}{q_m^2}
\right]\\
&\le C'\bigl(1+\log(1/\tau)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.8}
\]

because

\[
\frac1L\sum_{q=1}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\log(L/q)=O(1)
\]

by comparison with `int_0^{1/2} log(1/x) dx`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{L\ge2}G_{\tau,L}(0)
\le C\bigl(1+\log(1/\tau)\bigr)
\quad\text{for every fixed }\tau>0.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

This finite-volume proof contains the key mechanism in a particularly transparent way:

- each individual two-dimensional layer may have `log L` fluctuations;
- the exactly common vertical mode is only one mode among `L`, hence its roughness is diluted by `1/L`;
- every nonzero vertical mode acquires a mass;
- the average over those masses is finite.

So **collective three-dimensional localization does not require layerwise localization**.

For I0007 this changes the target. One should not try to prove that a two-slope horizontal layer is rigid on its own. Instead one should try to prove that, after decomposing the three-dimensional integer model into a collective vertical mode plus relative layer modes, the relative modes satisfy a positive-stiffness comparison while the collective mode has only a `1/L` share of the point fluctuation.

The previous run's exact one-fiber entropic curvature is compatible with this picture, but it does not yet control the full spatial spectrum. Curl/nesting compatibility is the remaining gap.

**B23 outcome:** success as an exact solvable-model refutation of the premise that constituent layers must localize. It points to a collective-mode/relative-mode proof architecture.

### 7.2 Surface-tension Hessian is not the covariance comparison

The previous run produced positive canonical flux curvature and Sheffield already proves strict surface-tension convexity in much greater generality. Yet the source localization conjecture remains open. Therefore any argument that replaces (5.11) merely by positivity of the Hessian of the scalar surface tension has discarded precisely the spatial information that remains unresolved.

### 7.3 Continuous Brascamp--Lieb cannot be cited verbatim

The continuous uniformly-convex theorem assumes a genuine positive Hessian of the interaction in real spin variables. The frozen source model is integer-valued and permits convex interactions whose discrete second difference can vanish over long cells. Low temperature amplifies energetic differences but does not create a pointwise Hessian where the discrete potential is affine. The last run showed that entropy repairs this only after a constrained sum; promoting that repair to a full matrix-valued spatial functional inequality is the unsolved step.
