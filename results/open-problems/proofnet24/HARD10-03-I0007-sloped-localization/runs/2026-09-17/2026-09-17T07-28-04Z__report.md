## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The R01 screen established that turning on a small slope is **not** a small-volume perturbation: a nonzero slope forces macroscopically many walls. I therefore changed the state variable. Rather than treating the walls themselves as rare defects, treat the **signed wall flux through a coordinate fiber** as the conserved quantity, integrate exactly over each one-dimensional fiber, and ask what survives block gluing.

This produces an exact deterministic renormalization identity. It also reveals a sharp obstruction: convex energy alone has no uniform coarse-flux stiffness because the integer constrained ground energy is piecewise affine. Any proof at positive wall density must therefore obtain its restoring force from entropy / compatibility / collective wall interaction, not from an ordinary rare-contour Peierls penalty.

I record this as a task-local structural lemma; publication novelty is unverified.

## 4. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: homological wall flux

Let `phi` be an integer-valued height function on a rectangular box, and fix a coordinate `i`. For a full coordinate-`i` fiber with transverse coordinate `y`, write its successive increments

\[
g_r=\phi(y+(r+1)e_i)-\phi(y+r e_i),\qquad r=0,\dots,L-1.
\]

Telescoping gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}g_r=m_i(y):=\phi(y+Le_i)-\phi(y).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the signed flux `m_i(y)` is fixed by the boundary/winding sector and is invariant under every local modification that preserves that sector. In particular,

\[
\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}|g_r|\ge |m_i(y)|.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For affine/sloped boundary approximants, `m_i(y)=s_i L+O(1)` uniformly on interior fibers. Summing (4.2) over order `L^2` parallel fibers shows that any fixed nonzero `s_i` forces order `L^3` total absolute coordinate gradient. So a positive slope has a **positive density of level crossings** in volume; the walls cannot be placed in a dilute Peierls gas whose total defect density vanishes with `L`.

There is an exact coarea interpretation. For `h in Z`, set

\[
\eta_h(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{\phi(x)\ge h\}}.
\]

For every oriented edge `(x,y)`,

\[
\phi(y)-\phi(x)
 =\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}(\eta_h(y)-\eta_h(x)),
\tag{4.3}
\]

and

\[
|\phi(y)-\phi(x)|
 =\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}|\eta_h(y)-\eta_h(x)|.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The sums are finite on each edge. Hence the slope/winding is literally the signed homological flux carried by the nested family of level-set boundaries. This is the conserved state variable I was looking for in B12.

**Outcome B12:** success as a structural reduction, but **no metric movement**. Conservation explains why positive wall density is unavoidable; it does not by itself control height fluctuations.

## 5. Exact convex fiber energy

The flux variable permits an exact one-dimensional minimization that will be the local block energy in B30.

Let `V:Z -> R` be convex in the discrete sense (successive differences are nondecreasing). For a fiber of `ell` edges and prescribed integer flux `a`, define

\[
F_{V,\ell}(a)
:=\min\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}V(z_j):
 z_j\in\mathbb Z,\ \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}z_j=a\right\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Write uniquely

\[
a=q\ell+r,\qquad 0\le r<\ell.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
F_{V,\ell}(a)
=(\ell-r)V(q)+rV(q+1).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

If two entries satisfy `u<=v-2`, discrete convexity gives

\[
V(u)+V(v)\ge V(u+1)+V(v-1).
\]

Thus repeatedly balancing the largest and smallest entries never raises energy and terminates exactly when all entries differ by at most one. Under the fixed sum `a=q ell+r`, every balanced vector has `ell-r` entries equal to `q` and `r` entries equal to `q+1`; this gives (5.2). Conversely such a vector is admissible, so the lower bound is attained. QED.

For even convex `V`, zero is a minimizer, but the formula itself does not require evenness or low temperature.

## 6. B30 — ONE SCALE / ONE DIRECTION AT A TIME: exact gluing identity

Split a coordinate fiber of total length `L=n ell` into `n` consecutive blocks of length `ell`. Let `a_j` be the integer flux of block `j`, with

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_j=m.
\]

For an actual increment configuration, let `E_j` be the energy in block `j`. Then there is the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^{n}E_j-F_{V,L}(m)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(E_j-F_{V,\ell}(a_j)\big)
+
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{V,\ell}(a_j)-F_{V,L}(m)\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Both terms on the right are nonnegative. The first is nonnegative by definition of each constrained block minimum. For the second, concatenate minimizing configurations for the `n` blocks; this is an admissible length-`L` configuration of total flux `m`, hence

\[
F_{V,L}(m)\le\sum_jF_{V,\ell}(a_j).
\]

So **integer block flux is an exact gluing state variable**. No information from neighboring blocks is needed to state the one-dimensional energy gluing inequality.

However, (5.2) exposes the exact obstruction to turning (6.1) into a conventional strongly-convex block RG. If

\[
\Delta V(q):=V(q+1)-V(q),
\]

then for every

\[
q\ell\le a<(q+1)\ell
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{F_{V,\ell}(a+1)-F_{V,\ell}(a)=\Delta V(q).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently the discrete second difference satisfies

\[
\Delta^2F_{V,\ell}(a)=0
\]

at every integer `a` that is not a multiple of `ell`; curvature appears only when the mean flux crosses an integer-gradient cell boundary.

Thus the zero-temperature constrained block energy is convex but has long affine stretches. There is **no uniform deterministic coarse-flux stiffness** to bootstrap across scales, even if the microscopic `V` is strictly convex as a real function. The integer balancing degeneracy is exactly what removes that stiffness.

**Outcome B30:** the exact gluing variable and exact failure are identified. To progress from `k_loc=1` to `k_loc=2`, a proof must generate scale-stable curvature from finite-temperature entropy and multi-fiber compatibility, not from (6.1) alone.

## 7. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: why ordinary Pirogov--Sinai is the wrong transfer

The conserved-flux picture suggests comparing with contour / Pirogov--Sinai / non-crossing-surface methods. Before transferring such a method, I searched for a counterexample to its basic finite-ground-state premise.

Consider an `L^3` twisted periodic box with integer windings

\[
0<m_1<L,\qquad 0<m_2<L,\qquad m_3=0,
\]

and even convex coordinate interactions. Choose any one-dimensional sequences `f,g` with

\[
f(x_1+L)-f(x_1)=m_1,
\qquad
g(x_2+L)-g(x_2)=m_2,
\]

whose increments are in `{0,1}`, with exactly `m_1` and `m_2` ones per period, respectively. Define

\[
\phi(x_1,x_2,x_3)=f(x_1)+g(x_2).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Every coordinate-1 fiber has the balanced minimizing multiset with `m_1` ones and `L-m_1` zeros; every coordinate-2 fiber has the analogous minimizing multiset; every coordinate-3 increment is zero. By (5.2), (7.1) therefore simultaneously attains the directional convex lower bound on **every** coordinate fiber and is a global ground configuration in that winding sector.

Already this separable family contains at least

\[
\boxed{\binom{L}{m_1}\binom{L}{m_2}}
\tag{7.2}
\]

distinct rooted gradient configurations. For slope fractions bounded away from `0` and `1`, this grows exponentially in `L`. I make no claim of volume-order entropy; the point is only that the number of ground states is unbounded and rapidly growing with system size.

Therefore a plain Pirogov--Sinai expansion around a **fixed finite list of isolated ground states** is structurally mismatched to the two-slope problem. This does not rule out contour methods with sliding phases, wall gases, or a collective state variable; it rules out the naive finite-phase transfer.

The closest source-compatible positive result is in the 2026 paper itself: it treats infinitely many **zero-slope** non-crossing surfaces with any average spacing and proves strong structure for that stack. This is much closer to the positive-density-wall geometry than a dilute contour gas. But for a target slope `(s_1,s_2,0)`, slicing the level-set family in one nonzero direction naturally produces constituent walls carrying the other nonzero tilt. The source theorem for a stack of zero-slope surfaces does not justify replacing these by rigid zero-slope walls.

The Laslier--Lubetzky tilted-SOS result gives an additional warning from a neighboring model: tilted two-dimensional surfaces can exhibit GFF-type roughness. It does not refute collective localization of the present three-dimensional height function, but it means that a successful transfer may have to prove **collective rigidity of a stack of individually tilted / possibly rough walls**, rather than rigidity of each wall separately.

**Outcome B21:** ordinary finite-phase Pirogov--Sinai fails a necessary structural test; the promising neighboring shape is the source's own positive-density non-crossing stack, but its zero-slope constituent hypothesis is exactly what is missing at `k_loc=2`.

## 8. What was actually learned

The three methods fit together into one sharper obstruction.

1. **Conserved quantity:** nonzero slope is an exact homological wall flux; positive wall density cannot be perturbatively removed.
2. **Correct local state variable:** block flux glues exactly under convex energy.
3. **Failure of energy-only RG:** the integer constrained ground energy is piecewise affine, so no scale-uniform flux curvature comes from deterministic energy.
4. **Failure of naive contour transfer:** even a two-slope convex ground sector has a rapidly growing family of balanced ground states, not finitely many isolated phases.
5. **What a proof must add:** an **entropic / compatibility-generated stiffness** for the effective finite-temperature block free energy of a positive-density non-crossing wall stack.

This is genuine structural progress but not localization. The metric remains `k_loc=1`.
