\[

\tag{6.2}

and the same diagonal asymptotic holds at `h_n`. Therefore

\[

=\left[\frac2\pi\left(2\beta-\frac{\beta^2}{\gamma}\right)+o(1)\right]\log n

\tag{6.3}

\[

\tag{6.4}

\[

\[

=n^{-c/\sigma_\gamma+o(1)}.
\tag{6.6}

\[

For `y in Y_R`, `G_R(0,y)` is bounded above and below by positive constants, `G_R(0,b_y)=G_R(0,y)+o(1)`, while

The pair upper bound (8.2), divided by (9.6) squared, gives relative shell exponent exactly `D_c^(gamma)(delta)`; the missing `q_n^{-2}` factor is only polylogarithmic. Proposition 8.1 supplies a uniform `-eta` power margin on the whole interval `0<=delta<=gamma`. There are only `O(log n)` dyadic shells. Hence

\[
\sum_{\substack{y\ne z\\|y-z|\le4r}}P(A_y\cap A_z)
=o((EZ_n)^2).
\tag{9.9}
\]

For far pairs, use Proposition 7.1. In a dyadic shell `d<=|y-z|<2d`, the number of ordered candidate pairs is at most `C R^2d^2`. Therefore its contribution, divided by (9.6) squared, is at most

\[
C\frac{R^2d^2\,q_nH_R(d)\nu_n^2}
{R^4q_n^2\nu_n^2}
\le
C\left(\frac dR\right)^2[1+\log(R/d)].
\tag{9.10}
\]

Now parameterize the far dyadic shells by `d asymp 2^{-j}R`. Then

\[
\sum_j
\left(\frac dR\right)^2[1+\log(R/d)]
\le C\sum_{j\ge0}4^{-j}(1+j)<\infty.
\tag{9.11}
\]

Thus the total far-pair contribution is at most `C(EZ_n)^2`. Combining this with (9.7) and (9.9) proves the first inequality in (9.1).

Paley–Zygmund now gives

\[
P(Z_n>0)
\ge\frac{(EZ_n)^2}{EZ_n^2}
\ge p_0>0,
\tag{9.12}
\]

uniformly for all sufficiently large `n`. Finally, (5.8) says every counted packet is a literal stopped-range translated-boundary witness, giving (9.2)–(9.3). `square`
