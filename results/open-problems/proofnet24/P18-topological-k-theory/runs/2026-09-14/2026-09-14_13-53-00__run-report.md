## P18-R2 — exact realization theorem for subgroups of R

### Theorem

Let `G <= R` be a subgroup with `1 in G`.

The following are equivalent.

1. There exists a **uniquely ergodic minimal Cantor system** `(X,T,mu)` such that
   `K^0(X,T)` is order-unit isomorphic to
   `(G, G cap [0,infinity), 1)`.

2. There exists such a system for which its gap-label/clopen-value group
   `M_mu = { integral f dmu : f in C(X,Z) }`
   equals `G` and `Inf K^0(X,T)=0`.

3. `G` is **countable and dense in R**.

4. `G` is countable and **noncyclic** as an abstract subgroup of `R`.

Thus the correct replacement for manuscript Theorem 12.126(4) is:

> Every countable **noncyclic** subgroup `G <= R` containing `1` occurs, with inherited order and order unit, as the ordered `K0` / gap-label group of a uniquely ergodic minimal Cantor system; conversely every subgroup of `R` occurring in this strong form is countable and noncyclic (equivalently dense).

The manuscript's phrase `G != Z` must be replaced by **`G is noncyclic`** (equivalently, in this setting, **`G is dense in R`**).

### Necessity: every such gap-label group is countable and dense

Let `(X,T)` be a minimal Cantor system and `mu` a `T`-invariant probability measure.

**Countability.** A compact metrizable zero-dimensional space has a countable clopen basis. Every clopen subset is compact and hence a finite union of basis elements, so there are only countably many clopen subsets. Every `f in C(X,Z)` has finite image with clopen fibres. Hence `C(X,Z)` is countable and so is `M_mu`.

**Full support.** The support of an invariant probability measure is a nonempty closed invariant subset. Minimality forces `supp(mu)=X`.

**No atoms.** If `mu({x})=a>0`, invariance gives the same mass to every point in the infinite orbit of `x`, contradicting total mass one.

Fix `x in X`. By non-atomicity and regularity, choose open neighbourhoods of `x` of arbitrarily small measure. Since `X` is zero-dimensional, each contains a nonempty clopen neighbourhood `U`. Full support gives
`0 < mu(U) < epsilon`.
Thus `M_mu` contains arbitrarily small positive elements.

An additive subgroup of `R` is either discrete cyclic or dense. Therefore `M_mu` is dense.

If `G <= R` contains `1`, then every discrete subgroup is necessarily
`(1/q)Z` for some positive integer `q`; hence it is cyclic. Therefore for such `G`,
`dense <=> noncyclic`.

This explains exactly why the manuscript condition `G != Z` failed: `(1/2)Z` is not equal to `Z` as a subset of `R`, but it is still cyclic as an abstract group and cannot arise from a minimal Cantor system in the claimed strong form.

### Sufficiency: HPS realization plus a unique-state lemma

Assume now that `G <= R` is countable, contains `1`, and is dense.

Give `G` its inherited order
`G^+ = G cap [0,infinity)` and order unit `u=1`.

Then `(G,G^+,1)` is a countable simple dimension group:

- it is directed because the order is total;
- it is unperforated because the order is inherited from `R`;
- it has Riesz interpolation: if `a_1,a_2 <= b_1,b_2`, take `c=max(a_1,a_2)`;
- every nonzero `g>0` is an order unit, because for any `h in G`, choosing `n>|h|/g` gives `-ng <= h <= ng`;
- it is noncyclic because it is dense.

Herman–Putnam–Skau, Theorem 5.4 and Corollary 6.3, realize every noncyclic simple dimension group with distinguished order unit as the dynamical dimension group of a minimal Cantor system. Hence there exists `(X,T)` with
`K^0(X,T) ≅ (G,G^+,1)`.

It remains to prove unique ergodicity rather than assume it.

### Unique-state lemma

The ordered group `(G,G^+,1)` has exactly one normalized state, namely the inclusion
`iota:G -> R`.

Let `s:G->R` be a positive homomorphism with `s(1)=1`.
For `g in G` and integer `n>=1`, put
`p_n=floor(ng)`.
Since `p_n,p_n+1 in Z subset G`,
`p_n <= ng < p_n+1`.
Positivity and `s(1)=1` give
`p_n <= n s(g) <= p_n+1`.
Therefore
`|s(g)-g| <= 1/n`.
Letting `n -> infinity` gives `s(g)=g`.

So the state space is the singleton `{iota}`.

For a minimal Cantor system, invariant probability measures correspond naturally to normalized states of the dynamical dimension group via
`[f] -> integral f dmu`.
Therefore the HPS realization is uniquely ergodic.

Under the chosen ordered-group isomorphism, its unique trace/state is exactly the inclusion `G -> R`, so the trace range is `G`. Since the quotient map `C(X,Z)->K^0(X,T)` is onto,
`M_mu = G`.

Finally, the unique state is injective, hence
`Inf K^0(X,T)=0`.

This proves all equivalences.

## Manuscript repair: exact replacement for Corollary 12.120

The correct proof order is:

1. Use Putnam's point orbit-breaking AF algebra `A_y` and the inclusion
   `A_y -> C(X) crossed_product_T Z`.
   For a singleton cut point, the induced `K0` map is an order-unit isomorphism.

2. Since `A_y` is AF, `K0(A_y)` is a dimension group and is unperforated.
   Transport this order structure to `K0(C(X) crossed_product Z)`.

3. Use the Pimsner–Voiculescu computation
   `K0(C(X) crossed_product Z)
    ≅ C(X,Z)/(1-T_*)C(X,Z)`
   and
   `K1 ≅ ker(1-T_*) ≅ Z`
   for a minimal Cantor system.

4. Only **after unperforation is available**, run the manuscript's strict-state Birkhoff-sum argument:
   if `g=q(f)` and
   `min_mu integral f dmu > 0`,
   then some Birkhoff sum
   `S_N f = sum_{j=0}^{N-1} f∘T^j`
   is pointwise at least `1`.
   Hence `N g = q(S_N f)` is positive.
   Unperforation then gives `g>0`.

Therefore the general state-order criterion is
`D^+ = {0} union {g : tau_mu(g)>0 for every invariant mu}`.

The manuscript's nonnegative criterion
`g>=0 iff tau_mu(g)>=0 for every mu`
is valid only after an additional infinitesimal-free hypothesis. In particular, if `Inf(D)=0`, then `tau_mu(g)=0` for all invariant measures forces `g=0`, and the borderline formulation becomes valid.

This removes the circularity in the present proof: unperforation no longer comes from the strict-state argument that itself needs unperforation.

## Independent peer audit

**AUDIT_FOR = P01**  
**SOURCE = Gmail message `1a09edbf662d4195`**  
**CLAIM = “NEW LEMMA 1 — the obvious irrational-trace block projection is not a K0 projection.”**

P01 takes `A=C_r^*(G)` and nonzero projections `e_i in A`, and considers
`q=diag(e_1,e_2,...)`
on the standard Hilbert A-module.

It claims that if infinitely many `e_i` are nonzero, then
`q notin A tensor K`,
even when its normal von-Neumann trace
`sum_i tau(e_i)`
is finite.

**Verdict: PROVED independently.**

Let `H_A=ell^2(N) tensor A`, and let `P_N` be the projection onto the first `N` coordinates.

For every compact Hilbert-module operator
`T in K(H_A) ≅ A tensor K`,
`||(1-P_N)T|| -> 0`.

Indeed, for a rank-one operator
`theta_{xi,eta}`,
`(1-P_N)theta_{xi,eta}
 = theta_{(1-P_N)xi,eta}`,
whose norm is at most
`||(1-P_N)xi|| ||eta|| -> 0`.
Finite-rank operators have the same property, and norm closure gives it for every compact operator.

For the diagonal projection `q`, however, every tail containing a nonzero `e_j` has norm one:
`||(1-P_N)q||=1`
for every `N` once some `j>N` has `e_j != 0`.

Hence `q` is not compact and therefore not in `A tensor K`.

The finite partial sums converge strongly/strictly in the appropriate multiplier or von-Neumann setting, but their norm distance from the infinite projection remains one. Thus a finite normal trace does not upgrade strong convergence to a stabilized C*-algebra projection.

This exactly confirms P01's distinction between von-Neumann dimension and an ordinary `K0(C_r^*(G))` representative.

`G=(1/2)Z` contains `1` and is not equal to `Z` as a subset of `R`, but it is cyclic/discrete. P18-R2 shows that it cannot be the gap-label group in the claimed uniquely ergodic minimal Cantor realization.

### 2. Nonnegative traces do not detect infinitesimals

If `0 != g in Inf(D)`, every state vanishes on `g`. Then both `g` and `-g` satisfy all trace inequalities `>=0`; they cannot both lie in a proper positive cone.

### 3. Abstract K-group invariance does not freeze trace pairing

P17's magnetic noncommutative-torus models preserve the abstract exterior-algebra K-groups but change the canonical trace range through the magnetic entries/Pfaffians.

### 4. Strong convergence does not imply stabilized K0 membership

The P01 block projection audit above is the concrete operator-theoretic obstruction.
