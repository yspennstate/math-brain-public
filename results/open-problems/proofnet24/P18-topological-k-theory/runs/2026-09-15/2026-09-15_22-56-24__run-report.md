# 2. Original-source / categorical assumptions checked

## 2.1 Equivariant K-homology Mayer–Vietoris

For an invariant cover of a proper `G`-CW complex by invariant subcomplexes, equivariant topological K-homology has the usual long exact Mayer–Vietoris sequence. We only use its boundary

\[
K_0^G(X_+\cup X_-)
\longrightarrow
K_1^G(X_+\cap X_-)
\]

and naturality under proper equivariant maps.

This is an ordinary generalized-homology axiom; no quantitative operator-K theorem is inserted at this source step.

## 2.2 Free normal subgroup quotient / Morita change of groups

The source split uses the standard fact that if a normal subgroup `N` acts freely and properly on a proper `H`-space `X`, then the transformation groupoids

\[
H\ltimes X
\quad\text{and}\quad
(H/N)\ltimes(N\backslash X)
\]

are equivalent, hence give the usual change-of-groups isomorphism in equivariant K-homology.

The foundational groupoid Morita source checked is:

P. S. Muhly, J. Renault, D. P. Williams, *Equivalence and isomorphism for groupoid C*-algebras*, J. Operator Theory 17 (1987), 3–22.

Current journal record:
`https://jot.theta.ro/jot/archive/1987-017-001/1987-017-001-001.html`.

The proof below applies this only after verifying that the normal kernel is torsion-free and therefore acts freely on the proper Rips complex.

## 2.3 Recent Rips-gluing source checked, but not overused

Kevin Li and Luis Jorge Sánchez Saldaña, *Contractible Rips complexes of groups via metric gluings*, arXiv:2608.24279v1 (2026), proves that Cayley graphs of free products are successive metric gluings of factor Cayley graphs and recalls a Rips homotopy-gluing theorem.

Current source:
`https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24279v1`.

In particular, their Theorem 1.3 / Corollary 3.1 treat free products and their Section 2 proves the Rips gluing homotopy equivalence for star-shaped gluing loci.

However, that published statement is an ordinary Rips homotopy result; by itself it does **not** state the finite-scale proper-equivariant K-homology splitting needed here. This run therefore does not promote an equivariant refinement that the paper does not state. The split is proved separately in Section 5 by the free-normal-subgroup quotient.

# 4. Height-band source Mayer–Vietoris boundary

Fix an integer `D>=2` and put

\[
X_D=P_D\Gamma.
\]

Restrict the action from `Gamma` to the base subgroup `H`.

Let

\[
\operatorname{ht}(ht^n)=n.
\]

This is 1-Lipschitz for the standard semidirect generating set. Put

\[
r_D=\lfloor D/2\rfloor.
\]

Define the full `H`-invariant subcomplexes

\[
X_D^+=X_D[\operatorname{ht}\ge-r_D],
\qquad
X_D^-=X_D[\operatorname{ht}\le r_D].
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.1 They cover every D-simplex

If a simplex were in neither subcomplex, it would contain vertices of heights

\[
n\le-r_D-1,
\qquad
m\ge r_D+1.
\]

Hence

\[
m-n\ge2r_D+2>D,
\]

contradicting the fact that height is 1-Lipschitz and the simplex has diameter at most `D`.

Therefore

\[
X_D=X_D^+\cup X_D^-.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Their intersection is the finite-height band

\[
X_D^0=X_D[|\operatorname{ht}|\le r_D].
\tag{4.3}
\]

The equivariant Mayer–Vietoris boundary is

\[
\partial_D:
K_0^H(X_D)
\longrightarrow
K_1^H(X_D^0).
\tag{4.4}
\]

# 5. Proper compression of the height band to the base

Every vertex of `X_D^0` has unique normal form

\[
g=ht^n,
\qquad h\in H,\quad |n|\le r_D.
\]

Define

\[
p_D(g)=h.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This map is `H`-equivariant. It is proper because a base vertex has at most

\[
2r_D+1
\]

preimages in the height band.

We now compute its Rips scale.

Let

\[
g_i=h_it^{n_i},
\qquad
g_j=h_jt^{n_j}
\]

belong to one `D`-simplex. Then

\[
d_\Gamma(h_i,h_j)
\le
|n_i|+d_\Gamma(g_i,g_j)+|n_j|
\le
D+2r_D
=:Q_D.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The inherited triangular normal-form theorem `P18-TRIANGULAR-GROWTH-32` says that an element of the base which has semidirect word length at most `Q` has base word length at most

\[
Q\binom{Q+m-1}{m-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus `(5.1)` extends to a proper `H`-equivariant simplicial map

\[
\boxed{
p_D:X_D^0\longrightarrow P_{F_m(D)}H,

\tag{5.4}

where

\[
F_m(D)
=Q_D\binom{Q_D+m-1}{m-1},
\qquad
Q_D=D+2\lfloor D/2\rfloor\le2D.
\tag{5.5}

This is the entire geometric control loss in the source boundary.

# 6. Identification of the source boundary

Let

\[
z_{m,L;D}=q_{2,D,*}(z_{m,L}).

We claim

\[
\boxed{
(p_D)_*\partial_D\operatorname{Res}_H^\Gamma(z_{m,L;D})
=
\pm y_{L,F_m(D)},

\tag{6.1}

where `y_{L,s}` is the structure image at scale `s` of the class obtained by inducing `x_{L,1}` from `C` to the free product `H`.

## Proof of `(6.1)`

The source class was built by induction from

\[
A=C\times\langle t\rangle.
\]

Because

\[
\Gamma=HA,
\qquad
H\cap A=C,
\]

the restriction/induction Mackey decomposition has exactly one double coset. Hence restricting the induced torus class from `Gamma` to `H` is the class induced from `C` obtained by restricting the torus product to `C`.

Now forget the `t`-translation symmetry and apply the height Mayer–Vietoris boundary. The scale-one line class `eta`, viewed in locally finite K-homology of the line, has Mayer–Vietoris boundary equal to the point generator. Therefore external-product naturality gives

\[
\partial(x_{L,1}\boxtimes\eta)=\pm x_{L,1}.
\tag{6.2}

Thickening the two rays to the overlap band `[-r_D,r_D]` does not alter this boundary class: the finite band deformation retracts to a point in the line direction, and Mayer–Vietoris is natural under the inclusions of such covers.

Induction from `C` to `H`, the ambient Rips maps, and the proper compression `p_D` all commute with this construction. On the central height slice the compression is exactly the identity on the base coordinate. This proves `(6.1)`.

Consequently, if

\boxed{y_{L,F_m(D)}=0.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This proves `P18-SOURCE-HEIGHT-MV-COMPRESSION-37`.

# 7. Free-factor finite-Rips K-homology split

This is the key new categorical lemma.

Let generally

\[
H=C_L*F
\]

with the standard union generating set, and let

\[
\pi:H\to C_L
\tag{7.1}
\]

be the group retraction which is the identity on `C_L` and kills `F`.

The map `pi` is 1-Lipschitz.

Put

\[
N=\ker\pi.
\]

## 7.1 `N` is torsion-free

A finite-order element in a free product is conjugate into one of the factors: a cyclically reduced word of syllable length at least two has powers of strictly increasing reduced length.

The free factor `F` is torsion-free. If

\[
n=hch^{-1}\in N,
\qquad c\in C_L,
\]

then

\[
e=\pi(n)=\pi(h)c\pi(h)^{-1},
\]

so `c=e`. Hence `n=e`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{N\text{ is torsion-free}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

## 7.2 `N` acts freely on every proper Rips complex

The left `H` action on the geometric Rips complex has finite point stabilizers: after barycentric subdivision, a stabilizer embeds into the permutation group of a finite simplex.

Since `N` is torsion-free, its intersection with every finite stabilizer is trivial. Therefore `N` acts freely on `P_sH` for every finite scale `s`.

Set

\[
\overline X_s=N\backslash P_sH.
\]

The residual group is

\[
H/N\cong C_L.
\]

By the standard free-normal-subgroup change of groups / transformation-groupoid Morita equivalence,

\[
\boxed{
K_*^H(P_sH)
\cong
K_*^{C_L}(\overline X_s).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

## 7.3 The quotient carries a proper map back to the cyclic Rips complex

Because `pi` is 1-Lipschitz, the vertex map

\[
Nh\longmapsto\pi(h)
\]

extends to a `C_L`-equivariant simplicial map

\[
\overline\pi_s:\overline X_s\to P_sC_L.
\tag{7.4}
\]

It is proper. Indeed `P_sH` is `H`-cocompact, so a compact set whose `H`-translates cover it descends to a compact subset of `overline X_s`; only the finitely many residual `C_L` translates are needed to cover the quotient.

Hence `(7.4)` induces a K-homology pushforward.

## 7.4 It splits the induced free-factor class

Let

\[
M_s:H\times_{C_L}P_sC_L\to P_sH,
\qquad
[h,x]\mapsto hx.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Quotienting `(7.5)` by `N` identifies

\[
N\backslash(H\times_{C_L}P_sC_L)
\cong
C_L\times_{C_L}P_sC_L
\cong
P_sC_L.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Under `(7.6)`, the composite of the quotient of `M_s` with `overline pi_s` is exactly the identity on `P_sC_L`.

Therefore the composite

\[
K_*^{C_L}(P_sC_L)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}}
K_*^H(H\times_{C_L}P_sC_L)
\xrightarrow{(M_s)_*}
K_*^H(P_sH)
\xrightarrow{(7.3)}
K_*^{C_L}(\overline X_s)
\xrightarrow{(\overline\pi_s)_*}
K_*^{C_L}(P_sC_L)
\]

is the identity.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_*^{C_L}(P_sC_L)
\longrightarrow
K_*^H(P_sH)
\text{ is split injective for every }s.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

In particular,

\[
y_{L,s}=0
\Longrightarrow
x_{L,s}=0.
\tag{7.8}
\]

This proves `P18-FREE-FACTOR-RIPS-K-SPLIT-37`.

### Important categorical warning

The bare retraction `pi:H->C_L` has infinite fibers and does **not** itself define the required proper K-homology pushforward. The valid split first quotients by its torsion-free kernel, where the kernel action is free, and only then uses the induced proper map of the quotient. This distinction is essential.

# 11. Failed routes, overstrong claims, and tests

## 11.1 Naive group retraction as a K-homology pushforward — **REJECTED**

Although

\[
C_L*F_{m-1}\to C_L
\]

is 1-Lipschitz, it is not proper. It cannot simply be used as a covariant map on locally finite equivariant K-homology. The valid proof must first divide by the torsion-free kernel and invoke the free-action change of groups.
