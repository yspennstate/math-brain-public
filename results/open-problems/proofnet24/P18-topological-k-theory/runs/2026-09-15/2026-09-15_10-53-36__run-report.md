# 3. Geometry of \(P_6(C_{15})\)

Write vertices as \(0,1,\ldots,14\) modulo \(15\). A finite set \(S\) is a Rips simplex iff every pair of vertices in \(S\) has cyclic word distance at most \(6\).

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}

\#\text{ simplices}&15&90&275&450&378&140&15.

So \(X\) has \(1363\) nonempty simplices and geometric dimension \(6\).

# 4. Barycentric subdivision and the proper \(G\)-CW structure

The original simplicial action has inversions: \(C_3\) cyclically permutes vertices of invariant triangles and \(C_5\) cyclically permutes vertices of invariant \(4\)-simplices.

Pass to \(\operatorname{sd}X\). A barycentric \(p\)-simplex is a strict chain

\[
S_0<S_1<\cdots<S_p
\]

of nonempty simplices of \(X\). Its stabilizer preserves every \(S_i\) setwise and therefore fixes the barycentric simplex pointwise. Hence \(\operatorname{sd}X\) is a proper \(G\)-CW complex.

For a cell \(\sigma\), write \(G_\sigma\) for its stabilizer. The representation-ring Bredon chain group is

\[
C_p^G(\operatorname{sd}X;R_\mathbb C)
=
\bigoplus_{[\sigma]\in G\backslash(\operatorname{sd}X)^{(p)}}R_\mathbb C(G_\sigma).
\]

For a boundary face whose stabilizer grows from \(H\) to \(K\), the coefficient map is \(\operatorname{Ind}_H^K\).

# 5. Equivariant AHSS input

For proper \(G\)-CW complexes, complex equivariant K-homology has the cellular Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

\[
E^2_{p,q}=H_p^{\mathrm{FIN}}(X;K_q^G(-))\Longrightarrow K_{p+q}^G(X).

\[

\[

and the remaining rows follow by Bott periodicity. Therefore the \(q=0\) cellular differential is exactly the representation-ring Bredon boundary.

A convenient explicit reference is Sam Hughes, *On the equivariant K- and KO-homology of some special linear groups*, Theorem 2.1, which states the same proper-equivariant AHSS and records these finite-stabilizer coefficient groups.
