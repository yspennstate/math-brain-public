# 4. Why barycentric subdivision is the correct proper \(G\)-CW model

The original simplicial action has inversions: for example, \(C_3\) cyclically permutes vertices of invariant triangles and \(C_5\) cyclically permutes vertices of invariant \(4\)-simplices.

Pass to the barycentric subdivision

\[
\operatorname{sd}X.
\]

A barycentric \(p\)-simplex is a strict chain

\[
S_0<S_1<\cdots<S_p
\]

of nonempty simplices of \(X\).

Its stabilizer is the subgroup preserving every \(S_i\) setwise.  Such a stabilizer fixes the barycentric simplex pointwise.  Hence \(\operatorname{sd}X\) is a genuine proper \(G\)-CW complex.

For a barycentric cell \(\sigma\) write \(G_\sigma\) for its stabilizer.

The complex representation-ring Bredon chain group is

\[
C_p^G(\operatorname{sd}X;R_\mathbb C)
=
\bigoplus_{[\sigma]\in G\backslash(\operatorname{sd}X)^{(p)}}
R_\mathbb C(G_\sigma).
\]

For a boundary face whose stabilizer grows from \(H\) to \(K\), the coefficient map is induction

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_H^K.
\]

# 5. Equivariant AHSS input

For proper \(G\)-CW complexes, complex equivariant K-homology has the cellular Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence

\[
E^2_{p,q}
=
H_p^{\mathrm{FIN}}
\left(
X;
K_q^G(-)
\right)
\Longrightarrow
K_{p+q}^G(X).
\]

For finite \(H\),

\[
K_0^G(G/H)
=
K_0(C_r^*H)
=
R_\mathbb C(H),
\]

\[
K_1^G(G/H)
=
K_1(C_r^*H)
=
0,
\]

and the remaining rows are obtained by Bott periodicity.

Therefore the \(q=0\) cellular differential is exactly the representation-ring Bredon boundary.

A convenient explicit reference for this form is Sam Hughes,
*On the equivariant K- and KO-homology of some special linear groups*,
Theorem 2.1, which states for a proper \(\Gamma\)-CW complex \(X\)

\[
E^2_{p,q}
=
H_p^{\mathrm{FIN}}(X;K_q^\Gamma(-))
\Rightarrow
K_{p+q}^\Gamma(X),
\]

and records that for finite cell stabilizer \(H\),

\[
K_0^\Gamma(\Gamma/H)=R_\mathbb C(H),
\qquad
K_1^\Gamma(\Gamma/H)=0.
\]

This is the exact spectral-sequence input used below.
