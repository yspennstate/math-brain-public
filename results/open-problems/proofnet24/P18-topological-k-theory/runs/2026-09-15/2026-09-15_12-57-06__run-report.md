# 3. `P18-SELFADJOINT-CYCLIC-NORMING-23`

Let

\[
F(\theta)=\sum_{k=-m}^{m}A_ke^{ik\theta}
\]

be self-adjoint for every `theta`, and put

\[
M=\sup_{\theta}\|F(\theta)\|.
\]

Compactness of the circle gives `theta_0` with

\[
\|F(\theta_0)\|=M.
\]

Because `F(theta_0)` is Hermitian, choose a unit vector `xi` and a real eigenvalue `lambda` such that

\[
F(\theta_0)\xi=\lambda\xi,
\qquad
|\lambda|=M.
\]

Define the scalar real trigonometric polynomial

\[
f(\theta)=\langle F(\theta)\xi,\xi\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\|f\|_\infty\le M,
\qquad
f(\theta_0)=\lambda,
\qquad
|\lambda|=M.
\]

Thus `theta_0` is a global maximum of `f` if `lambda=M`, and a global minimum if `lambda=-M`. In either case,

\[
\boxed{f'(\theta_0)=0.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

By the classical Bernstein inequality applied twice,

\[
\|f''\|_\infty
\le
m^2\|f\|_\infty
\le
m^2M.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose an `L`th-root sample angle `theta_j` with circular distance

\[
|\theta_j-\theta_0|
\le
\frac\pi L.
\]

Taylor's formula with integral remainder and `(3.1)` gives

\[
|f(\theta_j)-f(\theta_0)|
\le
\frac12\|f''\|_\infty
|\theta_j-\theta_0|^2
\le
\frac{m^2\pi^2}{2L^2}M.
\]

Therefore

\[
|f(\theta_j)|
\ge
M\left(
1-\frac{m^2\pi^2}{2L^2}
\right).
\]

Since

\[
|f(\theta_j)|
\le
\|F(\theta_j)\|
\le
S_L(F),
\]

we obtain `(E1)`:

\[
\boxed{
M
\le
\frac{S_L(F)}{1-m^2\pi^2/(2L^2)}.
}
\]

### Why self-adjointness matters

P25's generic matrix estimate uses only

\[
\|F'\|\le m\|F\|
\]

and therefore pays a first-order sampling loss `O(m/L)`.

For a self-adjoint matrix polynomial, one can scalarize at a norm-attaining eigenvector, and the scalar compression has zero first derivative at the extremum. The leading sampling loss is therefore quadratic:

\[
O(m^2/L^2).
\]

This is the entire source of the improved K-relation barrier.

# 4. Application to controlled unitary homotopies

Let

\[
U_t\in M_N(C_r^*C_L)
\]

be a path of `3epsilon`-unitaries of propagation at most `2r`, as supplied by the standard quantitative-`K_1` equality relation.

Assume for the moment that

\[

Write the coefficient lift as

\[
\widetilde U_t(z)
=
\sum_{|k|\le2r}A_k(t)z^k.

The two defects

\[
F_t(z)
=
\widetilde U_t(z)^*\widetilde U_t(z)-1,
\]

\[
G_t(z)
=
\widetilde U_t(z)\widetilde U_t(z)^*-1

\[
4r.

At every `L`th root,

\[
\|F_t\|,
\|G_t\|

Apply `(E1)` with `m=4r`. Provided

\[
8\pi^2r^2/L^2<1,

we get

\[
\boxed{
\|F_t\|_{C(\mathbb T)},
\|G_t\|_{C(\mathbb T)}
\le
\frac{3\varepsilon}
{1-8\pi^2r^2/L^2}.

\tag{4.1}

\boxed{

V_t

C(\mathbb T).

Condition `(4.2)` implies

\[

# 8. Counterexample: Nyquist uniqueness is not norm control

## `P18-SAMPLE-NYQUIST-NOT-NORM-CE-23` — PROVED

A tempting overstrong claim is:

> if a scalar/self-adjoint trigonometric polynomial has degree below the Nyquist threshold and is bounded by one at all `L`th roots, then it is bounded by one on the whole circle.

False.

Take

\[
L=4,
\qquad
m=1,
\qquad
f(\theta)
=
\frac65\cos(\theta-\pi/4).
\]

This is a real self-adjoint scalar trigonometric polynomial with

\[
m<L/2.
\]

At the four sample points

\[
0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2,
\]

one has

\[
|f|=\frac{3\sqrt2}{5}<1.
\]

But

\[
\|f\|_\infty=\frac65>1.
\]

Thus coefficient uniqueness below Nyquist does **not** imply a contractive sampling map. A quantitative norming inequality is genuinely necessary in the K-relation argument.

The new theorem `(E1)` gives, for this example,

\[
\|f\|_\infty
\le
\frac{3\sqrt2/5}{1-\pi^2/32},
\]

which is valid, while P25's first-order generic bound is much looser.

# 11. Stronger pointwise route — CONDITIONAL / not imported

A classical-looking stronger inequality for real trigonometric polynomials is often called a Bernstein–Szegő pointwise inequality and would imply a sharper grid norming constant of secant type.

The desired form would be:

\[
|f'(\theta)|
\le
m\sqrt{M^2-f(\theta)^2},
\qquad
M=\|f\|_\infty.
\tag{11.1}
\]

If `(11.1)` is available under exactly the needed hypotheses, then starting at a norm-attaining point and integrating the differential inequality would give

\[
|f(\theta_j)|
\ge
M\cos(m\pi/L),
\]

and hence, for self-adjoint matrix `F`,

\[
\|F\|_\infty
\le
\sec(m\pi/L)S_L(F)
\quad(m\pi/L<\pi/2).
\tag{11.2}
\]

For standard controlled K1 relations this would improve `(E4)` further to the conditional barrier

\[
3\varepsilon\sec(4\pi r/L)\ge1,
\]

i.e.

\[
\boxed{

\ge
\frac{L}{4\pi}\arccos(3\varepsilon).}
\tag{11.3}
\]

At `epsilon -> 0` the constant becomes `1/8`, larger than the proved `1/(2sqrt(2)pi)`.

A current source search found modern references discussing Bernstein–Szegő inequalities, but did not surface a directly inspectable primary statement of `(11.1)` with exactly the real-trigonometric hypotheses needed here. Therefore `(11.1)`–`(11.3)` are recorded only as a **CONDITIONAL next route**, not as part of the proved theorem.

This is preferable to silently importing an unverified sharp inequality.

# 12. Norm topology, stabilization, matrix size, and naturality audit

## Norm topology

Every step is in operator norm. The key threshold is exactly the condition that the lifted unitary defects have norm below one.

No strong-operator, trace-norm, or von-Neumann convergence is substituted.
