\[

- `phi_1` send `b -> ab` and fix `a,c`;

\[
\alpha=\phi_2\circ\phi_1.

with convention

\[
t h t^{-1}=\alpha(h).

All word radii on `F_3` use generators `a,b,c`; semidirect radii use `a,b,c,t`.

# 5. Exact quadratic growth of the triangular automorphism

For `n>=0`,

\[
\alpha^n(a)=a,
\qquad
\alpha^n(b)=a^n b.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since

\[
\alpha(c)=bc,
\]
we have the recursion

\[
\alpha^{n+1}(c)
=\alpha^n(b)\alpha^n(c)
=(a^n b)\alpha^n(c).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\alpha^n(c)
=(a^{n-1}b)(a^{n-2}b)\cdots(ab)b c.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

There is no free cancellation. Therefore

\[
|\alpha^n(c)|
=1+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(j+1)
=\frac{n(n+1)}2+1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For negative iterates, first compute

\[
\alpha^{-1}(a)=a,
\qquad
\alpha^{-1}(b)=a^{-1}b,
\qquad
\alpha^{-1}(c)=b^{-1}ac.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Put

\[
v_n=\alpha^{-n}(c).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_{n+1}
&=\alpha^{-n}(b^{-1}ac)\\
&=\alpha^{-n}(b)^{-1}a\,v_n\\
&=(a^{-n}b)^{-1}a\,v_n\\
&=b^{-1}a^{n+1}v_n.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\alpha^{-n}(c)
=(b^{-1}a^n)(b^{-1}a^{n-1})\cdots(b^{-1}a)c
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

with no cancellation, and

\[
|\alpha^{-n}(c)|
=1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(j+1)
=\frac{n(n+3)}2+1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Also

\[
|\alpha^{-n}(b)|=n+1.
\]

Inverses of the generators have the same image lengths. Hence for every integer `Q>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
A(Q)
:=
\max_{|n|\le Q}
\max_{s\in\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1},c^{\pm1}\}}
|\alpha^n(s)|
=
\frac{(Q+1)(Q+2)}2.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This proves `P18-QUADRATIC-NIELSEN-GROWTH-30`.

# 6. Semidirect normal-form radius

Let a group element `g in Gamma` have standard word length at most `Q` and write its unique normal form

\[
g=h t^k.
\]

Read a representative word from left to right while moving `t` letters to the right. At every point the current `t`-height `n` satisfies

\[
|n|\le Q.
\]

Every base generator encountered at height `n` contributes `alpha^n(s)`, whose free length is at most `A(Q)`. There are at most `Q` base letters. Thus, before free reduction,

\[
|h|_{F_3}
\le Q A(Q).
\]

Free reduction can only shorten the word, so

\[
\boxed{
|h|_{F_3}
\le
S_Q
:=
\frac{Q(Q+1)(Q+2)}2.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Also `|k|<=Q`.

The cubic base-radius loss is exactly the product

\[
\text{number of base letters}
\times
\text{quadratic monodromy image length}.
\]

where

\tag{10.1}
\]

At the same time,

\[
\alpha_L^L(a)=a,
\qquad
\alpha_L^L(b)=b.
\tag{10.2}

The word `W_L` is nontrivial. Indeed its free-product normal form contains alternating nontrivial `C_L` letters and nontrivial words in the free factor; after the final two `b` letters combine inside the free factor, a nontrivial reduced word remains.

Because `(10.2)` fixes every letter appearing in `W_L`,

\[

\]

Since `W_L^m` is never trivial,

(\alpha_L^L)^m\ne\mathrm{id}
\]

for every `m>=1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ord}(\alpha_L)=\infty.
}

This proves `P18-QUADRATIC-NONPERIODIC-STAGE-30`.
