Let

\[
F_2=\langle a,b\rangle

and

\[
G_L=C_L*\mathbb Z
=\langle a,b\mid a^L=1\rangle.

The canonical quotient is

\[
q_L:F_2\twoheadrightarrow G_L.

With canonical traces,

\[
C_r^*(F_2)
\cong
(C(\mathbb T),\tau)
*_{\rm red}
(C(\mathbb T),\tau),
\tag{2.1}

while

\[
C_r^*(G_L)
\cong
(C(\Omega_L),\tau_L)
*_{\rm red}

where

\Omega_L=\{\zeta:\zeta^L=1\}

g=a^{k_1}b^{\ell_1}a^{k_2}\cdots.

\ell_a(g)=\sum_i |k_i|.

# 4. Forward Fejér channel

Let

\[
K_L(\theta)
=
\frac1L\left|\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}e^{ij\theta}\right|^2
=
\sum_{|k|<L}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{L}\right)e^{ik\theta}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is a positive trigonometric polynomial of Haar integral one.

Let

\[
P_L:C(\mathbb T)\to C(\mathbb T),
\qquad
P_Lf=K_L*f,
\]

and let

\[
R_L:C(\mathbb T)\to C(\Omega_L)
\]

be restriction.

Then

\[
T_L:=R_LP_L
\tag{4.2}
\]

is unital completely positive.

Because `P_L f` has Fourier support strictly inside `(-L,L)`, averaging its root samples kills every nonzero frequency. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\tau_L\circ T_L=\tau.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `0<|k|<L`,

\[
\boxed{
T_L(z^k)
=
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{L}\right)u_L^k,
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `u_L` is the canonical generator of `C_r^*(C_L)`.

# 5. Reverse Fejér channel without aliasing

A full order-`L-1` cardinal Fejér interpolation has a harmless but inconvenient high-frequency alias. To obtain a clean low-mode formula, use a half-Nyquist kernel instead.

Set

\[
h_L=\left\lceil\frac L2\right\rceil,
\]

and define

\[
K_{h_L}(\theta)
=
\sum_{|k|<h_L}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{h_L}\right)e^{ik\theta}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `x=(x_0,...,x_{L-1}) in C(Omega_L)`, define

\[
S_Lx(e^{i\theta})
=
\frac1L
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
 x_j K_{h_L}(\theta-2\pi j/L).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The weights in (5.2) are nonnegative. Their sum is one because the Fourier support of `K_{h_L}` contains no nonzero multiple of `L`. Thus `S_L` is unital positive and hence completely positive.

Also

\[
\boxed{
\tau\circ S_L=\tau_L.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

If

\[
|k|<L/2,
\]

there is no frequency alias modulo `L` in the interval `|m|<h_L`, so discrete Fourier summation gives

\[
\boxed{
S_L(u_L^k)
=
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{h_L}\right)z^k.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the reverse low-frequency approximation needed below.

# 6. Reduced free products of the two channels

The primary source is:

Etienne Blanchard and Ken Dykema, **Embeddings of reduced free products of operator algebras**, Pacific J. Math. 199 (2001), 1–19; arXiv `math/9911012`.

Their Theorem 2.2 proves that state-preserving unital completely positive maps between C*-factors with faithful GNS states admit a reduced-free-product ucp map, and that on alternating centered words it acts letterwise:

\[
\Theta(a_1\cdots a_m)
=
\Theta_{i_1}(a_1)\cdots\Theta_{i_m}(a_m).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The source explicitly fixes the reduced-free-product issue which a purely universal Boca argument would not settle.

All states here are faithful. Therefore `(T_L,id)` gives a ucp contraction

\[
\mathcal T_L:
C_r^*(F_2)
\longrightarrow
C_r^*(G_L),
\tag{6.2}
\]

and `(S_L,id)` gives a ucp contraction

\[
\mathcal S_L:
C_r^*(G_L)
\longrightarrow
C_r^*(F_2).
\tag{6.3}
\]

These maps are automatically completely contractive at every matrix level.

For `g in B_R(F_2)` with free-product normal form as in Section 3, all nontrivial power syllables are centered for the canonical trace. Hence Theorem 2.2 and `(4.4)` give

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_L(\lambda_g)
=w_L^+(g)\lambda_{q_L(g)},
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

where

\[
w_L^+(g)
=
\prod_{a\text{-syllables }a^k}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{L}\right).
\]

Similarly, under `2R<L`, `(5.4)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal S_L(\lambda_{q_L(g)})
=w_L^-(g)\lambda_g,
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

where

\[
w_L^-(g)
=
\prod_{a\text{-syllables }a^k}
\left(1-\frac{|k|}{h_L}\right).
\]

Since for `0<=x_i<=1`,

\[
1-\prod_i(1-x_i)\le\sum_i x_i,
\]

we have

\[
0\le1-w_L^+(g)
\le
\frac{\ell_a(g)}L,
\tag{6.6}
\]

and

\[
0\le1-w_L^-(g)
\le
\frac{\ell_a(g)}{h_L}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

# 7. Proof of the main cb comparison

Let

\[
X=\sum_{g\in S}A_g\otimes\lambda_g
\in M_n(C_r^*(F_2)).
\]

For every coefficient,

\[
A_g
=
(\operatorname{id}_{M_n}\otimes\tau)
\bigl(X(1\otimes\lambda_g^*)\bigr).
\]

The matrix-valued canonical expectation is completely contractive, so

\[
\boxed{\|A_g\|\le\|X\|.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Put

\[
X_L=\sum_{g\in S}A_g\otimes\lambda_{q_L(g)}.
\]

Using `(6.4)`,

\[
X_L-(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathcal T_L)(X)
=
\sum_{g\in S}(1-w_L^+(g))A_g\otimes\lambda_{q_L(g)}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|X_L\|
&\le
\|(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathcal T_L)(X)\|
+
\sum_{g\in S}(1-w_L^+(g))\|A_g\|\\
&\le
\|X\|
+
\frac1L\sum_{g\in S}\ell_a(g)\|X\|.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is `(0.1)`.

For the reverse estimate, `2R<L` makes the elements `q_L(g)`, `g in S`, distinct. Thus the same coefficient extraction in `M_n(C_r^*(G_L))` gives

\[
\|A_g\|\le\|X_L\|.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Using `(6.5)`,

\[
X-(\operatorname{id}\otimes\mathcal S_L)(X_L)
=
\sum_{g\in S}(1-w_L^-(g))A_g\otimes\lambda_g.
\]

Hence

\[
\|X\|
\le
\left(1+\frac{\Sigma_a(S)}{h_L}\right)\|X_L\|,
\]

which is `(0.2)`.

The estimates are independent of `n`, so they are complete-norm estimates.

For the full radius-`R` ball,

\[
\Sigma_a(B_R)
\le
R|B_R|
=R(2\cdot3^R-1),
\]

which gives `(0.3)`–`(0.4)`.

This proves `P18-FEJER-FREE-PRODUCT-CB-GATE-24`.

# 11. Why this does not secretly use a global reduced quotient map

## `P18-GLOBAL-REDUCED-QUOTIENT-CE-24` — PROVED

The canonical group quotient

\[
F_2\twoheadrightarrow C_L*\mathbb Z
\]

does not extend by the same generator formula to a `*`-homomorphism

\[
C_r^*(F_2)\to C_r^*(C_L*\mathbb Z).
\]

Indeed its kernel is the normal closure of `a^L`. It contains the two noncommuting elements

\[
a^L,
\qquad
ba^Lb^{-1}.
\]

Every subgroup of a free group is free; these two noncommuting elements generate a free subgroup of rank two. Hence the kernel is nonamenable.

If the quotient regular representation were weakly contained in the regular representation of `F_2`, restriction to the kernel would force the trivial representation of this kernel to be weakly contained in its regular representation, hence the kernel would be amenable. Contradiction.

So the local maps `J_{L,R}` are genuinely local coefficient transfers. Their good cb norm comes from the two Fejér free-product channels, not from nonexistent reduced-C* functoriality.

This also explains why the estimate is allowed to deteriorate as `R` approaches relation depth.
