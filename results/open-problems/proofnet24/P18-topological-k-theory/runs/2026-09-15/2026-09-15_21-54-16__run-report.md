# 3. Spectral geometry of an arbitrary self-adjoint eta-idempotent

Let

\[
a=a^*,
\qquad
\|a^2-a\|\le\eta<1/4.
\]

For every spectral value `lambda`,

\[
|\lambda^2-\lambda|\le\eta.
\]

Solving the two quadratic inequalities gives

\[
\sigma(a)
\subset
[a_\eta,b_\eta]
\cup
[1-b_\eta,1-a_\eta],
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
a_\eta=\frac{1-\sqrt{1+4\eta}}2,
\qquad
b_\eta=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\eta}}2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Since `eta<1/4`,

\[
a_\eta>\frac{1-\sqrt2}{2}>-1,
\qquad
0<b_\eta<1/2.
\]

Therefore the spectral projection

\[
P=\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(a)
\tag{3.3}
\]

is defined and varies norm-continuously along every norm-continuous path with the same defect bound.

This spectral enclosure agrees with Remark 1.4 of Oyono-Oyono–Yu.

# 4. Iterated cubic rounding

Define

\[
\Phi(x)=3x^2-2x^3.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The key identities are

\[
\Phi(1-x)=1-\Phi(x),
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
\Phi'(x)=6x(1-x).
\tag{4.3}
\]

## 4.1 First iterate

On the lower spectral interval `[a_eta,b_eta]`:

- `Phi` decreases on `[a_eta,0]`;
- `Phi` increases on `[0,b_eta]`;
- `Phi(x)>=0` throughout that interval.

Hence

\[
\Phi([a_\eta,b_\eta])
\subset
[0,\delta_1(\eta)],
\]

where

\[
\delta_1(\eta)
=\max\{\Phi(a_\eta),\Phi(b_\eta)\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

We have `delta_1(eta)<1/2`. Indeed:

- because `0<b_eta<1/2`, `Phi(b_eta)<b_eta<1/2`;
- writing `q=-a_eta`, one has
  \[
  0<q<\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}
  \]
  and
  \[
  \Phi(-q)=3q^2+2q^3
  <0.147<1/2.
  \]

By the symmetry `(4.2)`, the upper spectral interval maps into

\[
[1-\delta_1(\eta),1].
\]

## 4.2 Iteration

For `0<x<1/2`,

\[
\Phi(x)-x
=-x(2x-1)(x-1)<0.
\]

Thus if

\[
\delta_{j+1}=\Phi(\delta_j),
\]

with `0<delta_1<1/2`, then

\[
\delta_j\downarrow0.
\]

Consequently the integer

\[
k_\eta
=\min\{k\ge1:\delta_k(\eta)\le10^{-4}\}
\tag{4.5}
\]

is finite for every fixed `eta<1/4`.

Put

\[
Q_\eta=\Phi^{\circ k_\eta}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Its degree is

\[
\deg Q_\eta=3^{k_\eta}=D_\eta.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Functional calculus and `(4.2)` give

\[
0\le Q_\eta(a)\le1
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_\eta(a)-P\|\le\delta_{k_\eta}\le10^{-4}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This proves the polynomial-rounding part of `P18-SMOOTHSTEP-PV-CUTOFF-36`.

### Why this is much better than the previous Bernstein cutoff

P18-P34 approximated a discontinuous-looking spectral cutoff by first choosing a global Lipschitz function and then an enormous Bernstein polynomial. But an `eta`-idempotent already has spectrum confined near `0` and `1`. The cubic `Phi` exploits exactly that two-cluster geometry and squares the spectral error near both fixed points.

No approximation theorem is needed.

# 5. Exact PV-ideal unitary formula at arbitrary tolerance

Let

\to\mathcal T_\alpha
\xrightarrow{\pi}

\tag{5.1}

Thus “directly lift the projection relation” is not an asymptotically sharp strategy for the Bott direction.

The stage Bott class is zero in ordinary `K_0`, but the new theorem forces every stable fixed-tolerance relation to the scale

No stable-rank or cancellation assumption can turn ordinary vanishing into a bounded-propagation relation here.
