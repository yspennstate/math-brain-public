# 3. Exact K-theoretic structure of the parent

This section isolates the obstruction class before any quantitative argument.

Let

\[
B=C_r^*(F_m).
\]

The standard free generators determine

\[
K_0(B)\cong\mathbb Z[1],
\qquad
K_1(B)\cong\mathbb Z^m,
\]

with basis

\[
e_j=[x_j].
\]

On `K_1(B)`, the triangular automorphism satisfies

\[
(\alpha_m)_*(e_1)=e_1,
\qquad
(\alpha_m)_*(e_j)=e_{j-1}+e_j
\quad(j\ge2).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
(1-\alpha_*)e_1=0,
\qquad
(1-\alpha_*)e_j=-e_{j-1}\quad(j\ge2).
\]

Hence

\[
\ker(1-\alpha_*:K_1(B)\to K_1(B))
=\mathbb Ze_1,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{im}(1-\alpha_*)
=\mathbb Ze_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb Ze_{m-1}.
\]

On `K_0(B)=Z`, `alpha_*` is the identity.

The PV six-term exact sequence for

\[
B\rtimes_{\alpha_m}\mathbb Z
=C_r^*(\Gamma_m)
\]

therefore contains the short exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[1]
\longrightarrow K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_m))
\xrightarrow{\partial_{\rm PV}}
\mathbb Ze_1
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Since the quotient is free abelian, `(3.3)` splits abstractly and

\[
K_0(C_r^*(\Gamma_m))\cong\mathbb Z^2.
\]

Now use the invariant subgroup

\[
\langle x_1\rangle\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

The automorphism is the identity there, so

\[
C_r^*(\langle x_1,t\rangle)
\cong C(\mathbb T^2).
\]

Let

\[
\beta=[P_B]-[e]
\]

be the reduced Bott class. Naturality of the PV sequence for the equivariant inclusion

\[
C^*(\mathbb Z)\hookrightarrow C_r^*(F_m)
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\partial_{\rm PV}(\beta)=\pm e_1.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus `beta` is primitive and, together with the unit, is a basis of the parent `K_0`.

This proves `P18-TRIANGULAR-PV-K0-BASIS-34`.

### Why this matters

The Bott obstruction is completely trace-zero. The canonical trace cannot detect it. Any attempt to lock it using only trace meshes is structurally incomplete. The natural detector is the PV boundary into the primitive free-group `K_1` class `[x_1]`.

# 5. A universal controlled PV boundary for approximate projection paths

We now prove the main technical lemma of this run.

## 5.1 Uniform spectral clusters for a `1/64` idempotent

Let

\[
a=a^*,
\qquad
\|a^2-a\|\le\varepsilon_0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_0=\frac1{64}.
\]

For every spectral value `lambda`,

\[
|\lambda^2-\lambda|\le\varepsilon_0.
\]

Thus the spectrum lies in two disjoint intervals around `0` and `1`. In particular,

\[
\sigma(a)\subset[-2,1/4]\cup[3/4,2].
\tag{5.1}
\]

The bounds `[-2,2]` and the gap `(1/4,3/4)` are deliberately very coarse but universal.

Choose the fixed continuous function

\[
f:[-2,2]\to[0,1]
\]

with

\[
f(x)=0\quad(x\le1/4),
\qquad
f(x)=1\quad(x\ge3/4),
\]

linear on `[1/4,3/4]`. Then

\[
P=f(a)=\mathbf1_{[1/2,\infty)}(a)
\]

is the rounded projection.

## 5.2 One explicit universal polynomial

Set

\[
F(t)=f(4t-2),
\qquad0\le t\le1.
\]

The Lipschitz constant of `F` is at most `8`. Let `B_dF` be the Bernstein polynomial of degree `d`. The elementary variance estimate gives

\[
\|B_dF-F\|_\infty
\le\frac4{\sqrt d}.
\]

Choose once and for all

\[
d_0=1,700,000,000.
\]

Define

\[
q(x)=B_{d_0}F\!\left(\frac{x+2}{4}\right).
\]

Then

\[
0\le q(x)\le1
\quad(-2\le x\le2),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\|q-f\|_\infty<10^{-4}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore for every `a` as above,

\[
h=q(a)
\]

is a positive contraction satisfying

\[
\|h-P\|<10^{-4}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The degree is intentionally enormous. Only universality, not numerical optimization, matters for the propagation exponent proved below.
