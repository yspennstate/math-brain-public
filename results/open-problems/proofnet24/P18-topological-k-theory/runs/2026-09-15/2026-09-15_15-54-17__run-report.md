# 4. Local matrix fibers and central phases

For a rational rotation factor `p/Q`, let `D,S in M_Q` be the standard clock/shift pair satisfying

\[
DS=e^{2\pi ip/Q}SD.
\]

A local representative of a central fiber with angular central phases `(alpha,beta)` may be chosen as

\[
U(\alpha)=e^{i\alpha/Q}D,
\qquad
V(\beta)=e^{i\beta/Q}S.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then

\[
U(\alpha)^Q=e^{i\alpha}I,
\qquad
V(\beta)^Q=e^{i\beta}I.
\]

Changing a chosen `Q`-th root changes the pair by a representation unitarily equivalent to the original one, by Lawton's irreducible-representation theorem. Therefore one may compare any two nearby central fibers by choosing roots continuously along the shortest phase arcs. The endpoint matrix may differ from a globally chosen representative by unitary conjugacy, which leaves its spectrum unchanged.

For the four-torus fiber set

\[
\phi=(\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2),
\]

use the tensor-product versions of (4.1).

The commuting hull generators `u_1,u_2` are diagonalizable simultaneously in this fiber. Hence each coefficient

\[
w_n\in C(\mathbb T^2)
\]

is evaluated **directly** by ordinary scalar functional calculus on a finite joint spectrum. No Fourier series for `w_n` is needed.

# 5. Sharper spatial decay

## `P18-SOURCE-43.3-EUCLIDEAN-HOPPING-TAIL-26` — **PROVED**

For `n=(n_1,n_2) != 0`, equation (4.15) gives

\[
p_{-n}(\omega)
=-n+d_n(\omega),
\]

where each coordinate of `d_n` has absolute value at most `2r`. Therefore

\[
\|d_n(\omega)\|_2\le2\sqrt2r.
\]

Since `p_0=0`,

\[
|p_0-p_{-n}|
=|p_{-n}|
\ge |n|_2-2\sqrt2r.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\|w_n\|_\infty
\le C_r e^{-|n|_2}
\le C_r e^{-\|n\|_\infty},
\qquad C_r=e^{2\sqrt2r}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The bound remains valid for the few short vectors for which the displayed lower bound is negative, because then the right side of (5.1) is greater than one while `0<w_n<=1`.

There are exactly `8M` lattice vectors with `||n||_infinity=M`. Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|h-h_R\|
&\le
\sum_{\|n\|_\infty>R}\|w_n\|_\infty\\
&\le
8C_r\sum_{M=R+1}^\infty M e^{-M}\\
&=
8C_re^{-(R+1)}
\frac{(R+1)-Re^{-1}}{(1-e^{-1})^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (0.5).

### Improvement over P18-25

The preceding P18 report used only the coordinate estimate

\[
|p_{-n}|\ge(1-2r)\|n\|_\infty,
\]

which at `r=0.4` decays as `e^{-0.2M}`. The Euclidean perturbation bound above keeps the true unit exponential rate `e^{-M}` and pays only the fixed prefactor `e^{2 sqrt(2) r}`. This reduces the required truncation radius from roughly `O(100)` to roughly `O(20)` for `10^-6` norm accuracy.

# 6. Phase derivative bounds

The coefficient `w_n=e^{-|p_{-n}|}` is smooth because `p_{-n}` cannot vanish for `n != 0`: a coordinate for which `|n_i|>=1` has magnitude at least `1-2r>0`.

Differentiating with respect to a hull angle `omega_i`,

\[
|\partial_{\omega_i}p_{-n}|
\le2r,
\]

and therefore

\[
|\partial_{\omega_i}w_n|
\le2r\,w_n
\le2rC_re^{-M},
\qquad M=\|n\|_\infty.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Summing over shells gives

\[
\sum_{n\ne0}
\|\partial_{\omega_i}w_n\|_\infty
\le
16rC_r\sum_{M\ge1}Me^{-M}
=L_\omega.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For the translation phase, changing `beta_1` by `Delta beta_1` multiplies the `n`-th translation monomial by

\[
e^{in_1\Delta\beta_1/Q_1}.
\]

Thus the derivative norm is bounded by

\[
\frac1{Q_1}
\sum_n |n_1|\|w_n\|_\infty.
\]

On the max-norm shell `M`, the exact combinatorial identity is

\[
\sum_{\max(|n_1|,|n_2|)=M}|n_1|=6M^2,
\tag{6.3}
\]

and similarly for `n_2`. Hence

\[
\sum_n|n_i|\|w_n\|_\infty
\le
6C_r\sum_{M\ge1}M^2e^{-M}
=L_{\rm tr}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Using

\[
\sum_{M\ge1}Mx^M=\frac{x}{(1-x)^2},
\qquad
\sum_{M\ge1}M^2x^M=\frac{x(1+x)}{(1-x)^3},
\]

with `x=e^-1` yields the constants in (0.1).

# 7. Why central-phase motion is divided by the denominators

Consider the first rational pair `(u_1,u_3)`.

Along a local central-phase lift,

\[
u_1(\alpha_1)=e^{i\alpha_1/Q_1}D_1.
\]

The joint eigenangles of the commuting hull generators therefore shift by exactly

\[
\Delta\alpha_1/Q_1.
\]

By (6.2), changing `alpha_1` contributes at most

\[
\frac{L_\omega}{Q_1}|\Delta\alpha_1|.
\]

Similarly `alpha_2` contributes `L_omega |Delta alpha_2|/Q_2`.

For the translations,

\[
u_3(\beta_1)^{n_1}
=e^{in_1\beta_1/Q_1}S_1^{n_1},
\]

so (6.4) gives the contribution

\[
\frac{L_{\rm tr}}{Q_1}|\Delta\beta_1|,
\]

and similarly for the second factor.

Changing all four phases successively and applying the triangle inequality proves the local fiber estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|H_R(\phi)-H_R(\phi')\|
\le
L_\omega\left(
\frac{d_{\mathbb T}(\alpha_1,\alpha'_1)}{Q_1}
+
\frac{d_{\mathbb T}(\alpha_2,\alpha'_2)}{Q_2}
\right)
+
L_{\rm tr}\left(
\frac{d_{\mathbb T}(\beta_1,\beta'_1)}{Q_1}
+
\frac{d_{\mathbb T}(\beta_2,\beta'_2)}{Q_2}
\right).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Strictly speaking the two displayed matrices may use different local root branches. Choosing the roots continuously along each shortest arc makes (7.1) valid; the final representative is unitarily equivalent to any globally chosen representative of that central fiber. Since spectra are invariant under unitary conjugacy, (7.1) is exactly what is needed for spectral certification.

# 8. Proof of the grid certificate

Let `phi in T^4`. Choose `g in G` with coordinate distances bounded by the four covering radii.

For self-adjoint matrices the Hausdorff spectral distance is bounded by the operator norm difference. Therefore (7.1) gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}\big(E,\operatorname{spec}H_R(\phi)\big)
\ge
\operatorname{dist}\big(E,\operatorname{spec}H_R(g)\big)
-\varepsilon_{\rm grid}.
\]

Taking the minimum over all `phi` and using the full-fiber union (3.2),

\[
\operatorname{dist}\big(E,\operatorname{spec}_{A_\Theta}(h_R)\big)
\ge
\delta_G-\varepsilon_{\rm grid}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Finally,

\[
\|h-h_R\|\le T_R
\]

and the same self-adjoint spectral perturbation estimate gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}\big(E,\operatorname{spec}_{A_\Theta}(h)\big)
\ge
\delta_G-\varepsilon_{\rm grid}-T_R.
\]

This is (0.3).

No compactness gap is hidden: compactness enters only through the compact base `T^4`, which ensures that the finite-dimensional matrix section attains its norm and that a finite phase cover exists.
