## 2.1 Ricard–Xu reduced free-product Khintchine theorem

Éric Ricard and Quanhua Xu, *Khintchine type inequalities for reduced free products and Applications*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 599 (2006), 27–59, arXiv `math/0505302`.

Primary record:

`https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505302`

The paper proves matrix-level fixed-word-length Khintchine inequalities for reduced free products and completely bounded natural projections onto homogeneous word-length spaces. The constants depend on free-product word length, not on the identity of an unchanged second free factor. Thus the P01/P18 argument replacing only the `C_r^*(Z)` factor by `C_r^*(C_L)` extends to

\[
C_r^*(\mathbb Z)*C_r^*(F_{m-1})
\longrightarrow
C_r^*(C_L)*C_r^*(F_{m-1})
\]

without a new dimension-dependent loss.

## 2.2 Pseudo-Sylvester stable rank

Primary record:

`https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08454`

The paper proves pseudo-Sylvester behavior for free-by-infinite-cyclic group rings without assuming full Sylvester cancellation. The inherited P25 theorem correctly uses **stable inner rank**, stabilizes until a finite factorization realizes that stable rank, and then pushes the factorization through the filling quotient. This yields the one-sided stage kernel-rank inequality without asserting an unavailable cancellation theorem.

# 3. Exact target and conventions

Fix `m>=2`. Let

\[
F_m=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\rangle
\]

with standard free length, and let `t` generate the semidirect `Z`, with convention

\[
t h t^{-1}=\alpha_m(h).
\]

The map in (0.1) is an automorphism. Define its inverse recursively by

\[
\beta_m(x_1)=x_1,
\qquad
\beta_m(x_j)=\beta_m(x_{j-1})^{-1}x_j.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\alpha_m(\beta_m(x_j))
=x_{j-1}^{-1}(x_{j-1}x_j)=x_j,
\]

and similarly `beta_m alpha_m=id` by triangular induction.

Because `alpha_m(x_1)=x_1`, the relation `x_1^L=1` is invariant and the quotient map

\[
q_L:\Gamma_m\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma_{m,L}
\tag{3.2}

is an actual group homomorphism. Importantly, its nonamenable kernel does **not** provide a global reduced-group-`C^*` homomorphism. Every norm statement below is local and finite-support.

# 4. Polynomial monodromy growth

For `n>=0`, let

\[
p_j(n)=|\alpha_m^n(x_j)|.
\]

Since

\[
\alpha_m^{n+1}(x_j)
=\alpha_m^n(x_{j-1})\alpha_m^n(x_j),
\]

one has

\[
p_j(n+1)\le p_{j-1}(n)+p_j(n),
\qquad p_1(n)=1,
\qquad p_j(0)=1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Pascal induction gives

\[
p_j(n)\le\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Negative iterates need a separate check. Put

\[
r_j(n)=|\beta_m^n(x_j)|.
\]

From (3.1),

\[
\beta_m^{n+1}(x_j)
=\beta_m^{n+1}(x_{j-1})^{-1}\beta_m^n(x_j),
\]

so

\[
r_j(n+1)\le r_{j-1}(n+1)+r_j(n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Using Pascal's identity in the shifted form

\[
\binom{n+j-1}{j-2}
+
\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}
=
\binom{n+j}{j-1},
\]

gives the same bound

\[
r_j(n)\le\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore for every `|n|<=Q`,

\[
\boxed{
|\alpha_m^n(x_j)|
\le
A_m(Q):=\binom{Q+m-1}{m-1}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Now take a semidirect word of standard length at most `Q`. Moving each base letter past all earlier `t` letters replaces it by `alpha_m^k(x_j^{\pm1})` for some `|k|<=Q`. There are at most `Q` base letters. Thus in normal form

\[
g=h t^k
\]

we have

\[
|k|\le Q,
\qquad
|h|\le Q A_m(Q)=S_{m,Q}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For `Q>=1`,

\[
\binom{Q+m-1}{m-1}
=
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(Q+i)}{(m-1)!}
\le
\frac{(mQ)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}
=B_mQ^{m-1},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{S_{m,Q}\le B_mQ^m.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This proves `P18-TRIANGULAR-GROWTH-32`.

### Sanity tests

Direct free-word iteration for `m=2,3,4,5` gives top-generator negative-iterate lengths

\[
\binom{Q+m-1}{m-1}
\]

for the first tested values of `Q`; this matches the recurrence exactly. The proof above does not depend on those computations.

# 5. Raw matrix-stable crossed-product comparison

Write a matrix-valued finite group-ring element supported in semidirect radius `Q` as

\[
X=\sum_{|k|\le Q}X_k t^k.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By Section 4 each `X_k` is supported in the radius-`S_{m,Q}` ball of the base free group. Fourier coefficient extraction in a reduced crossed product is completely contractive, so at every matrix level

\[
\|X_k\|\le\|X\|.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The base groups split as

\[
F_m\cong\mathbb Z*F_{m-1},
\qquad
H_{m,L}\cong C_L*F_{m-1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The Ricard–Xu/P01 local comparison acts only on the first free factor. The second factor is transported by the identity completely isometrically. Therefore, whenever

\[
L\ge8S_{m,Q}+1,
\]

we have

\[
\|(X_k)_L\|
\le
\Gamma_+(S_{m,Q},L)\|X_k\|.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Using only the triangle inequality after evaluation,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|X_L\|
&\le
\sum_{|k|\le Q}\|(X_k)_L\|\\
&\le
(2Q+1)\Gamma_+(S_{m,Q},L)\|X\|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Every step is matrix-level, so (5.5) is a cb estimate on the radius-`Q` operator space. This proves `P18-TRIANGULAR-RAW-CB-32`.

The factor `2Q+1` is deliberately crude. The power-amplification step removes it from the limiting distortion.

# 6. C*-power amplification and the `m`-th-root window

Let `X` have semidirect propagation at most `R`, and let `n>=1`. Algebraic evaluation through the group quotient is multiplicative, so

\[
((X^*X)^n)_L=(X_L^*X_L)^n.
\]

The element `(X^*X)^n` has propagation at most

\[
Q=2nR.
\]

Applying (5.5) gives

\[
\|X_L\|^{2n}
\le
(2Q+1)\Gamma_+(S_{m,Q},L)\|X\|^{2n}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Now put

\[
q=q_{m,L}=\frac{(L-1)^{1/m}}{\Lambda_m},
\qquad
\Lambda_m=(8B_m)^{1/m}.
\]

Assume `R<=q/8`. Choose

\[
n=\left\lfloor\frac{q}{4R}\right\rfloor.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Since `q/(4R)>=2`,

\[
n\ge\frac{q}{8R},
\qquad
Q=2nR\le q/2.
\tag{6.3}
\]

By (4.7),

\[
S_{m,Q}
\le B_m(q/2)^m
=\frac{L-1}{8\,2^m}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Hence the raw theorem's condition `L>=8S+1` holds automatically, and

\[
\frac{4S}{L-1}\le2^{-(m+1)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `L>=2`,

\[
\log13+3\log\frac{L-1}{2}
\le4\log L,
\]

so

\[
\log\Gamma_+(S,L)
\le2^{1-m}\log L
\le\frac12\log L.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Also `2Q+1<=q+1<=L` in this regime, so

\[
\log\big((2Q+1)\Gamma_+(S,L)\big)
\le\frac32\log L
\le2\log L.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Taking the `2n`-th root in (6.1),

\[
\log\frac{\|X_L\|}{\|X\|}
\le
\frac{\log L}{n}
\le
8\frac{R\log L}{q}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Substitution of `q` proves

\[
\boxed{
\|X_L\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
8\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)\|X\|.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Because the derivation is matrix-size independent, this is a cb estimate. This proves `P18-TRIANGULAR-MTH-ROOT-CB-GATE-32`.

# 7. Controlled K-theory consequences

Let `U_t` be a matrix-stabilized norm-continuous path with propagation at most `R` and

\[
\|U_t^*U_t-1\|,
\|U_tU_t^*-1\|\le\varepsilon.
\]

Each defect has propagation at most `2R`. Applying (6.9) to the defect gives, whenever `2R<=q/8`,

\[
\boxed{
\|U_{t,L}^*U_{t,L}-1\|,
\|U_{t,L}U_{t,L}^*-1\|
\le
\exp\!\left(
16\Lambda_m\frac{R\log L}{(L-1)^{1/m}}
\right)\varepsilon.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The bound is uniform in `t` because propagation and parent defect tolerance are uniform. The same argument applies to self-adjoint approximate projections.

This proves `P18-TRIANGULAR-CONTROLLED-K-TRANSFER-32`.

The theorem is one-sided: it transports parent approximate relations to the fillings. It does not claim a global reduced-`C^*` map, and it does not say a short representative has a short nullhomotopy.
