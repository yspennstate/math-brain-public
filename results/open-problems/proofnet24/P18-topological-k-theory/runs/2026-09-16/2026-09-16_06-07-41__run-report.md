# 3. Proof of `P18-C2-ROTATING-TETRAHEDRON-BOUNDARY-46`

## 3.1 Representation model

Let `C=C_2` act on four vertices as two transpositions. The real permutation representation is

\[
2\mathbb R[C].
\]

Removing the barycenter line gives

\[
V=2\mathbb R[C]-\mathbb R.
\]

Since

\[
\mathbb R[C]\cong\mathbb R\oplus\sigma,
\]

we obtain

\[
V\cong\mathbb R\oplus2\sigma.
\]

The pair of sign lines admits the standard complex structure on which `c` acts by multiplication by `-1`, so

\[
2\sigma\cong_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C_\varepsilon.
\]

Therefore

\[
V\cong\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb C_\varepsilon.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Centering the tetrahedron and radially rescaling its boundary gives the equivariant pair

\[
(\sigma,\partial\sigma)
\cong_C(D(V),S(V)).
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 3.2 Relative group and pair exact sequence

Complex Bott periodicity removes the complex line and the remaining trivial real line shifts degree by one. Thus

\[
K_1^C(D(V),S(V))\cong R(C),
\qquad
K_0^C(D(V),S(V))=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The disk `D(V)` is `C`-equivariantly contractible to its fixed center, so

\[
K_0^C(D(V))=R(C),
\qquad
K_1^C(D(V))=0.
\]

The pair exact sequence therefore contains

\[
0\longrightarrow R(C)
\xrightarrow{\partial_V}
K_0^C(S(V))
\xrightarrow{i_*}
R(C)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular

\[
\boxed{\partial_V\text{ is injective}.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

## 3.3 Hemisphere Mayer–Vietoris sequence

Write a point of `V` as `(x,z)\in\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb C_\varepsilon`. Let

\[
A_+=\{(x,z)\in S(V):x\ge0\},
\qquad
A_-=\{(x,z)\in S(V):x\le0\}.
\]

Each hemisphere is equivariantly contractible to a fixed pole `p_+` or `p_-`. Their intersection is

\[
E=A_+\cap A_-=S(\mathbb C_\varepsilon).
\]

The `C_2` action on `E\cong S^1` is free antipodal rotation, so

\[
K_0^C(E)\cong K_0(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_1^C(E)\cong K_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Also

\[
K_0^C(A_\pm)=R(C),
\qquad
K_1^C(A_\pm)=0.
\]

The map

\[
K_0^C(E)=\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
R(C)\oplus R(C)
\]

is

\[
n\longmapsto(n\rho,-n\rho),
\qquad
\rho=1+\varepsilon,
\tag{3.7}
\]

because a free point orbit pushed into either fixed hemisphere induces the trivial-subgroup representation, i.e. the regular representation `ρ`.

This map is injective, so Mayer–Vietoris gives

\[
K_1^C(S(V))=0
\]

and an exact segment

\[
\mathbb Z
\xrightarrow{(\rho,-\rho)}
R(C)\oplus R(C)
\xrightarrow{\beta}
K_0^C(S(V))
\xrightarrow{\gamma}
\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.8}
\]

## 3.4 The two-dimensional sign-disk boundary is dimension

Let

\[
W=\mathbb C_\varepsilon.
\]

For the pair `(D(W),S(W))`, Bott periodicity gives

\[
K_0^C(D(W),S(W))\cong R(C),
\qquad
K_1^C(D(W),S(W))=0.
\tag{3.9}
\]

The map

\[
K_0^C(S(W))\cong\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
K_0^C(D(W))\cong R(C)
\]

again sends `1` to `ρ`. Hence, in the pair exact sequence, the `R(C)`-linear map

\[
R(C)=K_0^C(D(W))
\longrightarrow
K_0^C(D(W),S(W))=R(C)
\]

has kernel `\mathbb Z\rho`.

Any `R(C)`-linear endomorphism of the free rank-one module `R(C)` is multiplication by an element of `R(C)`. Up to the unit ambiguity coming from the Bott orientation, the unique primitive element whose multiplication kernel is `\mathbb Z\rho` is `1-\varepsilon`. Thus this map is multiplication by

\[
\pm(1-\varepsilon).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Its image is `\mathbb Z(1-\varepsilon)`, so the next pair boundary

\[
\partial_W:R(C)\to K_1^C(S(W))\cong\mathbb Z
\]

is the primitive quotient by that image. The ordinary dimension homomorphism

\[
\dim(a+b\varepsilon)=a+b
\]

is primitive, surjective, and has exactly the same kernel. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\partial_W=\pm\dim.
}
\tag{3.11}
\]

## 3.5 Compatibility of pair boundary with the equator MV boundary

The decomposition

\[
V=\mathbb R\oplus W
\]

identifies `D(V)` as the suspension direction over `D(W)`. Equivalently, cutting the pair `(D(V),S(V))` by the two halfspaces `x\ge0` and `x\le0` gives a triad whose intersection pair is `(D(W),S(W))`.

Naturality of the long exact sequences for this triad gives the standard suspension/Mayer–Vietoris compatibility square. Up to the global choice of orientation sign,

\[
\gamma\circ\partial_V=\partial_W.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Combining `(3.11)` and `(3.12)` proves

\[
\boxed{
\gamma\partial_V(r)=\pm\dim(r).
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

This is `(0.1)`.

## 3.6 Exact augmentation-ideal boundary

Let

\[
I=\ker\dim=\mathbb Z(1-\varepsilon).
\]

For `r\in I`, `(3.13)` gives

\[
\gamma\partial_V(r)=0.
\]

By `(3.8)`, `\partial_V(r)` therefore lies in the hemisphere image `\operatorname{im}\beta`.

Because `\partial_V(r)` also lies in `\ker i_*` by `(3.4)`, it belongs to

\[
\ker i_*\cap\ker\gamma.
\]

For a hemisphere class `\beta(r_+,r_-)`, pushing into the contractible disk adds the two pole coefficients:

\[
i_*\beta(r_+,r_-)=r_++r_-.
\]

Hence

\[
\ker i_*\cap\ker\gamma
=
\{\beta(q,-q):q\in R(C)\}/\beta(\rho,-\rho)\mathbb Z
\cong R(C)/\mathbb Z\rho
\cong\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.14}
\]

The class `\beta(1,-1)` is a primitive generator of this group. Since `\partial_V` is injective and `I\cong\mathbb Z`, exactness shows that

\[
\partial_V|_I:I\xrightarrow{\cong}
\ker i_*\cap\ker\gamma.
\]

Therefore, up to the orientation sign,

\[
\partial_V(1-\varepsilon)
=\pm\beta(1,-1).
\tag{3.15}
\]

Under the hemisphere contractions, `\beta(1,-1)` is exactly the difference of the two fixed-pole point classes. This proves `(0.2)`.

# 4. Proof of `P18-POWER2-C2-ORBIT-AUGMENTATION-CANCELLATION-46`

\[

\tag{4.1}

The two `K`-fixed poles are

\[
p_0=\operatorname{bar}\{1,c\},
\qquad
p_1=\operatorname{bar}\{a,ca\}=a p_0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `Y` contain the entire boundary orbit and let

\[
B_\sigma:R(K)	o K_0^{\Gamma_L}(Y)
\]

be the induced attaching boundary.

By naturality of induction and `(3.15)`,

\[
B_\sigma(1-\varepsilon)
=\pm\bigl([p_1]-[p_0]\bigr)
\tag{4.3}
\]

where the notation means the corresponding induced orbit point classes in `Y`.

Now `a\in N_{\Gamma_L}(K)` and in fact `a` centralizes `K`. Right multiplication

\[
R_a:\Gamma_L/K\to\Gamma_L/K,
\qquad
gK\mapsto gaK,

is therefore a well-defined `\Gamma_L`-equivariant automorphism. Under the standard Shapiro identification

\[
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(\Gamma_L/K)\cong R(K),

its action is conjugation by `a` on `R(K)`, hence the identity.

The two orbit maps determined by `p_0` and `p_1=a p_0` differ exactly by `R_a`. Therefore they induce the same K-homology map, and `(4.3)` vanishes:

\[
\boxed{B_\sigma(1-\varepsilon)=0.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since

\[
R(K)/\mathbb Z(1-\varepsilon)\cong\mathbb Z
\]

via dimension, `(0.4)`–`(0.6)` follow.

This argument is ambient-group exact: it does not rely on passing to the cyclic subgroup or on a nonproper quotient map.

# 5. Fixed-axis necklace interpretation

For

\[
\sigma_j=a^j\sigma_0
\]

define

\[
p_j=\operatorname{bar}\{a^j,a^{j+d}\}.
\]

The fixed set of `K` in the three-ball `\sigma_j` is the line segment joining `p_j` to `p_{j+1}`. Its fixed set in the boundary is the two endpoints.

Transport an orientation of `\sigma_0` around the orbit by powers of `a`. Formula `(3.15)` then reads consistently as

\[
\partial_{\sigma_j}(1-\varepsilon)
=\pm([p_{j+1}]-[p_j]).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Summing over `j` gives

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}
([p_{j+1}]-[p_j])=0,
\qquad p_d=p_0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the singular character direction is literally a cellular boundary on the fixed-axis necklace. It cannot by itself produce the ambient source relation.

This is an important obstruction to an overstrong fixed-point route: **detecting one local tetrahedron at the nontrivial element of `C_2` does not decide the global orbit attachment**, because those local fixed-point boundaries cancel around the orbit.

### Independent derivation

Let `K=C_q` act freely on a vertex set consisting of `s` regular orbits. The centered real tangent representation is

\[
V_{q,s}=s\mathbb R[K]-\mathbb R.
\]

If `q` is odd, the nontrivial real irreducibles occur in conjugate character pairs and therefore form a complex representation. Thus

\[
V_{q,s}\cong (s-1)\mathbb R\oplus W_\mathbb C.
\]

Bott periodicity removes `W_\mathbb C`; the parity is that of `s-1`. Hence:

- `s` even gives relative `K_1\cong R(K)`, `K_0=0`;
- `s` odd gives relative `K_0\cong R(K)`, `K_1=0`.

If `q=2r` is even, the real regular representation contains in addition the real sign line `σ`. Thus

\[
V_{q,s}
\cong
(s-1)\mathbb R\oplus s\sigma\oplus W_\mathbb C.
\]

For `s` even, the sign lines pair into complex sign representations, leaving an odd number `s-1` of trivial real suspensions. Therefore

\[
K_1^K(D(V),S(V))\cong R(K),
\qquad K_0=0.
\]

For `s` odd, all but one sign line pair complexly and the even number of trivial lines contributes no parity shift. The problem reduces to the one-dimensional sign pair

\[
(D(\sigma),S(\sigma)).
\]

Here

\[
S(\sigma)\cong K/C_r.
\]

The pair exact sequence gives

\[
K_0^K(D(\sigma),S(\sigma))
\cong
R(K)/\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{K}R(C_r),
\]

and

\[
K_1^K(D(\sigma),S(\sigma))
=\ker\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{K}.
\]

For the standard characters `\eta_j` of `C_r` and `\chi` of `C_q`,

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{C_q}(\eta_j)

\]

These vectors have disjoint character-pair supports as `j` varies, so induction is injective. Therefore relative `K_1=0`, exactly as P25 states.
