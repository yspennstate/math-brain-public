# 4. Proof of `P18-LOW-SCALE-BASE-K0-DETECTOR-39`

Fix an integer

\[
1\le s<L/3.
\]

We use the standard generating set consisting of the cyclic generator `x_1` and free generators `x_2,...,x_m`.

## 4.1 The `H`-action on `P_sH` is free

Suppose a nontrivial point stabilizer exists. After barycentric subdivision, a nontrivial finite subgroup `K<H` preserves a finite Rips simplex setwise.

Every finite subgroup of a free product `C_L*F_{m-1}` is conjugate into `C_L`. Let `K` have order `q>1`; after conjugation it is the subgroup generated by

\[
x_1^{L/q}.
\]

Take a vertex `v` of the stabilized simplex. Its `K`-orbit lies in that simplex, hence has diameter at most `s`.

We now lower-bound that orbit diameter in the free-product word metric. If `w in H` and `c in C_L`, free-product normal-form reduction gives

\[
|w^{-1}cw|_H\ge |c|_{C_L}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, absorb any terminal cyclic syllable of `w` into `c`; if a noncyclic syllable remains, the conjugate has that word on both sides and cannot shorten the central cyclic word, while if none remains the cyclic length is unchanged.

Therefore the orbit diameter is at least the intrinsic diameter of the order-`q` subgroup in the cyclic factor:

\[
\rho_q
=
\left\lfloor\frac q2\right\rfloor\frac Lq.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For `q=2`, `rho_q=L/2`. For `q>=3`,

\[
\frac{\lfloor q/2\rfloor}{q}\ge\frac13.
\]

Thus every nontrivial finite subgroup orbit has diameter at least `L/3`, contradicting `s<L/3`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{H\curvearrowright P_sH\text{ is free}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

No factorization or divisor formula for `L` is used.

## 4.2 Homotopy type needed for the quotient

View the Cayley graph of

\[
H=C_L*\langle x_2\rangle*\cdots*\langle x_m\rangle
\]

as the Bass–Serre tree of factor Cayley graphs: cyclic `L`-gons and integer lines glued at cut vertices.

For a metric gluing at a point `p`, every Rips simplex meeting both sides can be enlarged by `p`: if `x` and `y` lie on opposite sides then

\[
d(x,y)=d(x,p)+d(p,y)\le s,
\]

so `d(x,p),d(y,p)<=s`; the same holds for all vertices of the simplex. Thus the point-gluing Rips theorem applies.

Any compact sphere or homotopy in `P_sH` meets only finitely many factor pieces. Repeated finite point-gluing therefore reduces its homotopy calculation to the factor Rips complexes.

For the factors:

- `P_s Z` is contractible;
- for `s<L/3`, the cyclic Rips theorem retained in P18 gives
  \[
  P_sC_L\simeq S^1,
  \]
  and the inclusion `P_1C_L -> P_sC_L` induces an isomorphism on `pi_1`.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\pi_n(P_sH)=0\quad(n\ge2),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and the structure inclusion

\[
P_1H\longrightarrow P_sH
\]

induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups.

## 4.3 The finite quotient is a rose up to homotopy

Because the action is free, put

\[
Q_s=H\backslash P_sH.
\]

This is a finite CW complex: after translating one vertex of a simplex to the identity, all other vertices lie in the finite radius-`s` ball.

At scale one,

\[
Q_1=H\backslash P_1H
\]

is the rose with `m` loops, one for each generator.

The regular covering sequences for `P_1H -> Q_1` and `P_sH -> Q_s` form a commutative diagram

\[
1\to\pi_1(P_1H)\to\pi_1(Q_1)\to H\to1
\]

\[
\phantom{1}\downarrow\cong\qquad\downarrow\qquad\Vert
\]

\[
1\to\pi_1(P_sH)\to\pi_1(Q_s)\to H\to1.
\]

The left arrow is an isomorphism by Section 4.2, so the middle arrow is an isomorphism. Also, for `n>=2`, covering-space invariance and `(4.4)` give

\[
\pi_n(Q_s)=0.
\]

Thus `Q_1 -> Q_s` is a weak equivalence between CW complexes, hence a homotopy equivalence:

\[
\boxed{Q_s\simeq\bigvee_{j=1}^m S^1.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

## 4.4 Finite-stage assembly is injective in degree zero

For a free proper action,

\[
K_0^H(P_sH)\cong K_0(Q_s).
\]

By `(4.5)`,

\[
K_0(Q_s)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

The vertex-orbit inclusion induces the generator because `K_0` of a connected finite graph is generated by the point class. Under finite-stage assembly that generator maps to the unit

\[
[1]\in K_0(C_r^*(H)).
\]

The canonical trace has value one on `[1]`, so this class is nonzero. A homomorphism from `Z` which sends the generator to a nonzero class is injective. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu_{H,s}:K_0^H(P_sH)\hookrightarrow K_0(C_r^*(H))
\quad(s<L/3).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This proves `P18-LOW-SCALE-BASE-K0-DETECTOR-39`.

# 5. Target base `K_0` and the triangular automorphism

We now compute only what the PV argument needs.

The group `H` is the fundamental group of a graph of finite groups with one vertex group `C_L` and `m-1` loop edges with trivial edge group. Its Bass–Serre tree is a model for `underline EH`.

The degree-zero Bredon cellular group at the vertex is

\[
R(C_L),
\]

while each loop edge contributes `R(1)=Z`. The two endpoint induction maps for a trivial loop-edge stabilizer agree, so their difference is zero. Hence

\[
K_0^H(\underline EH)\cong R(C_L).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The group is a-T-menable, so Higson–Kasparov assembly is an isomorphism for this family. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K_0(C_r^*(H))\cong R(C_L).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, inclusion of the finite factor generates all target `K_0`.

Since

\[
\alpha(x_1)=x_1,
\]

`alpha` is the identity on the subgroup `C_L`, and naturality of the finite-factor generators gives

\[
\boxed{\alpha_*=\mathrm{id}\text{ on }K_0(C_r^*(H)).}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No stable-rank or cancellation assertion is used.

# 7. Proof of `P18-HALFSPACE-ONE-STEP-PRISM-39`

Let

\[
X_D=P_D\Gamma,
\qquad
r_D=\lfloor D/2\rfloor,
\]

and use the inherited `H`-invariant halfspaces

\[
X_D^+=X_D[\operatorname{ht}\ge-r_D],
\qquad
X_D^-=X_D[\operatorname{ht}\le r_D].
\]

Since

\[
r_{D+2}=r_D+1,
\]

both the structure inclusion and right multiplication by `t` map `X_D^+` into `X_{D+2}^+`. Define

\[
R_+(g)=gt.
\]

Right multiplication commutes with the left `H`-action:

\[
R_+(hg)=hR_+(g),
\]

so the map is `H`-equivariant. It is proper because it is a bijection on vertices onto its image.

Take a `D`-simplex `sigma`. For vertices `g,h in sigma`,

\[
d(gt,ht)
\le d(gt,g)+d(g,h)+d(h,ht)
\le D+2,
\]

and

\[
d(g,ht)\le D+1.
\]

Thus

\[
\sigma\cup R_+(\sigma)
\]

has diameter at most `D+2`, hence is a simplex of `P_{D+2}\Gamma`. Therefore `R_+` and the structure inclusion are contiguous and induce the same map on equivariant K-homology.

The identical argument with

\[
R_-(g)=gt^{-1}
\]

works on `X_D^-`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
(i_{D,D+2}^\pm)_*=(R_\pm)_*.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Iterating yields an `N`-step prism into scale `D+2N`. The scale growth is linear in the number of height shifts; there is no bounded-scale infinite swindle hidden here.

This proves `P18-HALFSPACE-ONE-STEP-PRISM-39`.

# 9. Why boundary-zero alone cannot close the theorem

The classical PV sequence supplies an immediate counterexample to the overstrong inference.

Take

\[
A=\mathbb C,
\qquad
\alpha=\mathrm{id}.
\]

Then

\[
A\rtimes_\alpha\mathbb Z\cong C(\mathbb T).
\]

The PV boundary kills the image of

\[
j_*:K_0(\mathbb C)\to K_0(C(\mathbb T)),
\]

but

\[
j_*([1])=[1]\ne0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\partial(x)=0\not\Rightarrow x=0
}
\]

even in the untwisted mapping torus. Any finite-Rips proof that drops the halfspace/base term is invalid.

The new point is that, in the triangular family, once a controlled base preimage is actually produced, Sections 4–6 force that preimage to vanish at root scale.

The audited claim states that for every `s>=0` there is a height-zero word `W_{m,s} in H` with

\[
\pi(W_{m,s})=x_1^s
\]

and

\[
|W_{m,s}|_\Gamma
\le
(3m-2)\Bigl((m!s)^{1/m}+m-1\Bigr).
\tag{10.1}
\]

### 10.1 Triangular exponent recurrence

Put

\[
\pi(\alpha^r(x_j))=x_1^{E_j(r)}.
\]

Then `E_1(r)=1` and, for `j>=2`,

\[
E_j(r+1)=E_{j-1}(r)+E_j(r),
\qquad E_j(0)=0.
\]

Pascal's identity gives exactly

\[
E_j(r)=\binom r{j-1}.
\]

This part checks.

### 10.2 Orbit-block identity and length

With

\[
U_{j,q}=\prod_{r=0}^{q-1}\alpha^r(x_j),
\]

the semidirect convention `tht^{-1}=alpha(h)` gives

\[
U_{j,q}=(x_jt)^qt^{-q}.
\]

For `q>=1`, one final `t t^{-1}` cancellation yields

\[
|U_{j,q}|\le3q-2
\]

for `j>=2`; for `j=1`, one may use `x_1^q` directly with length `q`.

Applying `pi` and the hockey-stick identity gives

\[
\pi(U_{j,q})
=x_1^{\sum_{r=0}^{q-1}\binom r{j-1}}
=x_1^{\binom qj}.
\]

This part checks exactly.

### 10.3 Greedy combinadic bound

The greedy expansion

\[
s=\sum_{j=1}^m\binom{q_j}{j},
\qquad q_m>\cdots>q_1\ge0,
\]

is valid: maximality at each stage leaves a remainder strictly below the next Pascal difference and forces strict descent. If `Q=q_m`, then

\[
s\ge\binom Qm
\ge\frac{(Q-m+1)^m}{m!},
\]

so

\[
Q\le(m!s)^{1/m}+m-1.
\]

The product of orbit blocks therefore satisfies `(10.1)`.

A diagnostic check was also run for

\[
2\le m\le6,
\qquad
1\le s<5000,
\]

verifying exact reconstruction, strict index descent, and the bound on `Q`; no exception occurred. The theorem does not depend on this finite test.

### 10.4 Essential quotient warning

The proof establishes only

\[
\pi(W_{m,s})=x_1^s,
\]

not equality in `H`. P01 states this distinction explicitly, so there is no hidden collapse of the free normal kernel.
