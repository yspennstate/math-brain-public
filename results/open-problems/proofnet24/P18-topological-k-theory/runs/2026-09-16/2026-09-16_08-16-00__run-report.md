# 4. Maximality of the full cyclic critical simplex

We prove `P18-FULL-CYCLIC-RIPS-MAXIMALITY-48`.

## 4.1 Retained calibrated product map

P18-P47 uses the retained P54 calibration to a product
\[
S_L\times T\times\mathbb R
\]
with `\ell^1` metric, where:

- `S_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z`, with
  \[
  a:x\mapsto x+1,\qquad
  t:x\mapsto x+\tfrac12,\qquad
  b:x\mapsto -x;
  \]
- `T` is the Bass–Serre tree for the HNN vertex group
  \[
  B=\langle a,t\rangle\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z,
  \]
  and `B` fixes the base vertex `v_0`;
- the real coordinate is one half of the height homomorphism
  \[
  \nu(t)=1,\qquad \nu(a)=\nu(b)=0.
  \]

The orbit map is `1`-Lipschitz. Hence for every `g\in\Gamma_L`,
\[
|g|_{\Gamma_L}
\ge
 d_{S_L}(0,\rho(g)0)
 +d_T(v_0,gv_0)
 +\frac{|\nu(g)|}{2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 4.2 Distance from an arbitrary external vertex

Fix height `h` and
\[
\mathcal O_h=Ct^h.
\]
Let `y\in\Gamma_L`, and write
\[
\tau=d_T(v_0,yv_0),
\qquad n=\nu(y)-h.
\]
For
\[
x_j=a^jt^h\in\mathcal O_h,
\]
left multiplication by `x_j^{-1}\in B` does not change the tree displacement `\tau`, and
\[
\nu(x_j^{-1}y)=n.
\]

As `j` ranges modulo `L`, the circle coordinate of `x_j^{-1}y` is shifted through all integer translates. The fractional part is determined by `n/2`: reflection by `b` changes sign but not the class modulo `\mathbb Z` of an integer versus half-integer. Therefore
\[
\max_j d_{S_L}(0,\rho(x_j^{-1}y)0)
=
\begin{cases}
 d,& n\text{ even},\\[2mm]
 d-\tfrac12,& n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Combining `(4.1)` and `(4.2)`,
\[
\max_j d(x_j,y)
\ge
\begin{cases}
 d+\tau+|n|/2,& n\text{ even},\\[2mm]
 d-\tfrac12+\tau+|n|/2,& n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `\tau>0`, the right side is at least `d+1`. If `|n|\ge2`, it is again at least `d+1`.

The only borderline case is
\[
\tau=0,\qquad |n|=1.
\]
Then `y\in B` and has form
\[
y=a^rt^{h\pm1}.
\]
Choose `j=r-d`. Then
\[
x_j^{-1}y=a^dt^{\pm1},
\]
and P18-P47 (independently audited by P17) gives the exact length
\[
|a^dt^{\pm1}|=d+1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Finally, if `\tau=0` and `n=0`, then `y\in B` at height `h`, hence
\[
y=a^rt^h\in\mathcal O_h.
\]

Thus every vertex outside `\mathcal O_h` is at distance at least `d+1` from some vertex of `\mathcal O_h`.

Therefore no scale-`d` simplex can properly contain `\mathcal O_h`, proving maximality.

### Why this strengthening matters

P18-P47 excluded mixed **full-stabilizer** simplices. That alone did not exclude a larger **free** simplex containing a full cyclic face. The present maximality theorem excludes those free super-simplices as well. This is exactly what makes the barycentric proper-family decomposition in Section 7 a genuine subcomplex decomposition.

# 5. Proper-family cyclic detector

We prove the cyclic core of `P18-PROPER-FAMILY-WANG-DETECTOR-48`.

Let
\[

## 5.1 Explicit model for `E_{\mathcal P_C}C`

Let `EC_2` be a free contractible `C_2`-CW complex and inflate the action through
\[
C\twoheadrightarrow C/N\cong C_2.
\]
For every `K<C`, one has `K\le N`, hence
\[
(EC_2)^K=EC_2
\]
is contractible. For `C` itself,
\[
(EC_2)^C=(EC_2)^{C_2}=\varnothing.
\]
Thus inflated `EC_2` is a model for
\[
E_{\mathcal P_C}C.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## 5.2 First-filtration image of the primitive cyclic class

Use the equivariant K-homology AHSS for the model `(5.1)`. Every cell has isotropy `N`, and conjugation on `R(N)` is trivial because `C` is abelian. Therefore
\[
E^2_{p,q}
=
H_p(C_2;K_q^N(\mathrm{pt})).
\tag{5.2}
\]
For complex K-homology,
\[
K_q^N(\mathrm{pt})=
\begin{cases}
R(N),&q\text{ even},\\
0,&q\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Since `R(N)` is a free abelian group with trivial `C_2` action,
\[
H_1(C_2;R(N))=R(N)/2R(N),
\qquad
H_{2k}(C_2;R(N))=0\quad(k>0).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The canonical map from the free model `EC` to `E_{\mathcal P_C}C` sends a free orbit cell `C/1` to a `C/N`-cell. On K-homology coefficients the orbit map is induction
\[
R(1)=\mathbb Z
\longrightarrow
R(N),
\qquad
1\longmapsto
\rho_N:=\operatorname{Ind}_1^N(1),
\tag{5.4}
\]
the regular representation.

The retained cyclic class `\xi_L` has primitive first-filtration component
\[
1\in H_1(BC;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/L.
\]
By naturality of the cellular AHSS, its image in `(5.2)` has first-filtration component
\[
[\rho_N]\in R(N)/2R(N).
\tag{5.5}
\]
This is nonzero: in the irreducible-character basis of `R(N)`, the regular representation is the sum of all basis elements with coefficient `1`, hence is not divisible by `2`.

No differential can kill `(5.5)`. There is no outgoing differential from homological degree `1`. An incoming `d_r` has source bidegree
\[
(1+r,1-r).
\]
If `r` is even, the coefficient degree `1-r` is odd and the source vanishes. If `r` is odd, `1+r` is positive even, and `(5.3)` makes the source vanish. Thus `(5.5)` survives to `E^\infty`.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\xi_L\mapsto K_1^C(E_{\mathcal P_C}C)
\text{ is nonzero.}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is stronger than the previous “every direct proper stabilizer gives an even element” calculation: `(5.6)` survives **arbitrary nested proper-isotropy cellular incidence**.

## 7.2 Horizontal subcomplex and the cover

X_{\rm hor}=A\cup\Gamma_L\cdot P_dC

be P25's horizontal sector (subdivided). Define

Within a full cyclic simplex `\sigma`, deleting its barycenter leaves `\operatorname{sd}\partial\sigma`. Thus, by equivariant excision and induction,

K_*^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},J)
\cong

\tag{7.2}

## 7.3 Direct proof that the relative `K_1` in `(7.2)` vanishes

The vertex set of `\sigma` is one regular `C`-orbit. Its centered real permutation representation is
\[

For `L` even,
\[
V\cong \mathbb R_\varepsilon\oplus W_{\mathbb R},
\tag{7.3}

where `\varepsilon` is the real sign representation of the quotient `C\to C_2`, and `W` is a complex `C`-representation. Equivariant complex Bott periodicity removes `W_{\mathbb R}`, reducing the relative theory to the sign interval pair
\[
(D(\mathbb R_\varepsilon),S(\mathbb R_\varepsilon)).

The two boundary points form the orbit
\[
C/N,
\qquad N=\langle a^2\rangle.
\]
The long exact sequence contains
\[
K_1^C(D(\mathbb R_\varepsilon),S(\mathbb R_\varepsilon))
\longrightarrow
R(N)
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}_N^C}
R(C).
\tag{7.4}
\]
For the character bases, if `\eta_j` is a character of `N` and `\chi` generates the characters of `C`,
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_N^C(\eta_j)
=\chi^j+\chi^{j+L/2}.
\tag{7.5}

These images have disjoint character pairs, so induction is injective. Therefore `(7.4)` gives

\boxed{

\tag{7.6}

This independently recovers the special one-orbit case of P25's multiorbit simplex theorem.

By `(7.2)`,
\[
\boxed{

\tag{7.7}

## 7.4 Relative Mayer–Vietoris surjectivity

Use the triple
\[
A\subset J\subset X_{\rm hor}.
\]
Its exact sequence contains
\[
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},J)
\longrightarrow
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(J,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},A).
\]
By `(7.7)`,
\[
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(J,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},A)
\]
is injective.

Now apply relative Mayer–Vietoris to the cover `(7.1)`, relative to `A`. The relevant segment is
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(J,A)
&\to
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm prop},A)
\oplus
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},A)\\
&\to
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X,A)
\to
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(J,A)\\
&\to
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm prop},A)
\oplus
K_0^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm hor},A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.8}

The last arrow in `(7.8)` has an injective second component, so it is injective. Exactness therefore gives the crucial surjectivity

\boxed{
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X_{\rm prop},A)
\oplus

\twoheadrightarrow
K_1^{\Gamma_L}(X,A).

Equation `(7.9)` is the missing global-incidence theorem. It is stronger than saying that local equator channels generate first homology: it decomposes every **actual relative K-homology relation** before taking Wang or AHSS shadows.
