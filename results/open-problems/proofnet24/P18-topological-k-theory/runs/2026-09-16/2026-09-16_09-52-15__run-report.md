\boxed{|k|\ge3.}

\[

\[

\[

\qquad

\tag{0.6}

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_t:\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_D(C;G))
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{D+2}(C;G))

\tag{0.7}

No right-invariance of the word metric is assumed; the additive `2` is exactly the conjugation cost in `(0.6)`.

\nu_C(G):=\min\{D:\mathcal O_D(C;G)\text{ has a vertex}\},

# 4. Proof of `P18-TRIANGULAR-MAGNUS-HNN-TOWER-50`

Write the depth-`m` defining relation as

\[
tx_mt^{-1}=x_{m-1}x_m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Multiplying `(4.1)` on the left by `t^{-1}` and on the right by `x_m^{-1}` gives

\[
\boxed{x_mt^{-1}x_m^{-1}=t^{-1}x_{m-1}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No other defining relation contains `x_m`. Therefore the depth-`m` presentation is a Tietze rewrite of the HNN presentation with base group `\Gamma_{m-1,L}`, stable letter `x_m`, associated subgroup

\[
A_m=\langle t^{-1}\rangle,
\]

and target associated subgroup

\[
B_m=\langle t^{-1}x_{m-1}\rangle,
\]

with the isomorphism

\[
\phi_m((t^{-1})^k)=(t^{-1}x_{m-1})^k.
\]

The height homomorphism

\[
\operatorname{ht}:\Gamma_{m-1,L}\to\mathbb Z,
\qquad
\operatorname{ht}(t)=1,
\quad
\operatorname{ht}(x_j)=0,
\]

sends both `t^{-1}` and `t^{-1}x_{m-1}` to `-1`. Hence every nonzero power of either has nonzero height; both subgroups are infinite cyclic and `\phi_m` is an isomorphism.

By the HNN normal-form theorem/Britton lemma, the base group embeds in this presentation. Conversely `(4.2)` is algebraically equivalent to `(4.1)`, so no relation has been added or discarded. This proves `(0.1)`.

For `m=3`, set

u=t^{-1}x_2.

Then `(4.2)` becomes `(0.3)`.

# 5. Proof of `P18-M3-BRITTON-LONG-PINCH-GATE-50`

We use the symmetrized standard generating set

\[
S_2=\{x_1^{\pm1},x_2^{\pm1},t^{\pm1}\}
\]

for `\Gamma_{2,L}` and adjoin `x_3^{\pm1}` at depth three.

## 5.1 Exact lengths of the associated cyclic subgroups

First,

\[
|t^k|_{\Gamma_2}\ge|k|
\]

by height, while the literal word `t^k` has length `|k|`. Hence

\[
|t^k|_{\Gamma_2}=|k|.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Next abelianize `\Gamma_{2,L}`. The relation

\[
tx_2t^{-1}=x_1x_2
\]

forces `x_1=0` in the abelianization, so the map

\[
\psi:\Gamma_{2,L}\to\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
\psi(t)=(1,0),
\quad
\psi(x_2)=(0,1),
\quad
\psi(x_1)=0
\tag{5.2}
\]

is well defined. Each standard generator has `\ell^1` norm at most `1`, so

\[
|g|_{\Gamma_2}\ge\|\psi(g)\|_1.
\]

For `u=t^{-1}x_2`,

\[
\psi(u^k)=(-k,k),
\]

and therefore

\[
|u^k|_{\Gamma_2}\ge2|k|.
\]

The literal word `(t^{-1}x_2)^k` gives equality:

\[
\boxed{|u^k|_{\Gamma_2}=2|k|.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 5.2 Minimal stable-letter form

Fix `b\in\Gamma_{2,L}`. Among all `\Gamma_{3,L}` geodesic words for `b`, choose one `W` with the smallest number of letters `x_3^{\pm1}`. Rewrite `W` as alternating stable letters and base blocks, replacing each base block by a `\Gamma_2`-geodesic. This does not increase total length; if it created a shorter word or removed stable letters, it would contradict the choice of `W`.

If stable letters remain, an HNN-reduced word could not represent an element of the base group. Britton's lemma therefore supplies a pinch.

### Type A

A pinch

\[
x_3Vx_3^{-1}
\]

has `V\in A=\langle t^{-1}\rangle`, so `V` represents `t^{-k}` and by `(5.1)` has length `|k|`. The HNN relation replaces the whole pinch by `u^k`, for which `(5.3)` gives a base word of length `2|k|`.

Thus

\[
|x_3Vx_3^{-1}|_{\text{written}}=|k|+2,
\qquad
|u^k|_{\Gamma_2}=2|k|.
\]

- `k=0`: the stable letters cancel;
- `|k|=1`: replacement has length `2<3`;
- `|k|=2`: replacement has the same length `4` but strictly fewer `x_3` letters;
- only `|k|>=3` can evade both contradictions.

### Type B

A pinch

\[
x_3^{-1}Vx_3
\]

has `V\in B=\langle u\rangle`, so it represents `u^k` and has base-geodesic length `2|k|`. It may be replaced by `t^{-k}` of length `|k|`. For `k\ne0`,

\[
|k|<2|k|+2,
\]

contradicting geodesicity; `k=0` cancels.

Therefore every surviving stable-letter geodesic for a base element contains an A-type pinch with `|k|>=3`. This proves `(0.5)`.

### Scope warning

This theorem is deliberately about **base elements**. A general conjugator `g\in\Gamma_{3,L}` and the torsion element `g^{-1}x_1^sg` need not itself lie in the chosen `\Gamma_2` base without additional normal-form work. The theorem therefore does not prove

\[
|g^{-1}x_1^sg|\ge|s|_L
\]

and does not prove `\nu_{3,L}=L/2`. Those stronger statements remain open below.

# 6. Proof of the controlled right-shift prism

Let a clique of `\mathcal O_D(C;G)` be represented by orbit vertices

\[
Cg_0,\ldots,Cg_q.
\]

By the P49 clique model, the union

\[
S=\bigcup_i Cg_i
\]

is a `D`-Rips simplex. Right multiplication is automatically equivariant for the **left** `C` action:

\[
(ctext{\,acting on }g)t=c(gt).
\]

For any `x,y\in S`, left-invariance of the word metric gives

\[
d(xt,yt)
=|(xt)^{-1}(yt)|
=|t^{-1}x^{-1}yt|
\le |t^{-1}|+|x^{-1}y|+|t|
\le D+2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Similarly

\[
d(x,yt)
=|x^{-1}yt|
\le D+1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Distances inside `S` are already at most `D`. Therefore every pair of vertices in

\[
S\cup St
\]

has distance at most `D+2`. Since a Rips complex is flag at the metric level, `S\cup St` is a simplex of `P_{D+2}G`.

It follows simultaneously that:

1. the translated orbit vertices `Cg_it` span a clique at `D+2`;
2. the original and translated cliques together span a clique at `D+2`;
3. the scale inclusion and `R_t` are contiguous simplicial maps.

For a finitely generated group, every finite-radius ball is finite, so finite-scale Rips complexes are locally finite. The simplicial contiguity has uniformly bounded track and is proper. Hence the two maps are properly homotopic; because right multiplication commutes with the left `C` action, the homotopy is `C`-equivariant.

This proves `P18-FULL-CYCLIC-RIGHT-SHIFT-PRISM-50`.

# 7. Proof of the two-step edge gate

Suppose `Cg` is a vertex at the first full-orbit scale `D=\nu_C(G)`. By Section 6, `Cgt` is a vertex at `D+2` and

\[
Cg\cup Cgt
\]

is a `(D+2)`-Rips simplex.

If `Cg=Cgt`, then for some `c\in C`,

\[
gt=cg,
\]

hence

\[
gtg^{-1}=c.
\]

The left-hand side has the same order as `t`; the right-hand side belongs to finite `C`. Thus if `t` has infinite order the orbits are distinct. They form an edge of `\mathcal O_{D+2}`. Therefore

\[
\varepsilon_C(G)\le\nu_C(G)+2.
\]

The opposite inequality `\nu\le\varepsilon` is tautological. This proves `(0.8)`.

For the triangular group, height makes `t` infinite order. P25-P48 says no full-`C_L` orbit exists below `\rho^{\rm full}_{3,L}`, proving the left side of `(0.10)`.

For the explicit horizontal upper bound, the orbit `C` is a simplex at scale `L/2` because

\[
|x_1^j|\le |j|_L\le L/2.
\]

Also `t` centralizes `C`, so the orbit `Ct` has the same diameter. For cross terms,

\[
d(x_1^i,x_1^jt)
=|x_1^{j-i}t|
\le |j-i|_L+1
\le L/2+1.
\]

# 8. Why the controlled prism does not compute Wang monodromy

The P49 residual Weyl action comes from the **left** action of the normalizer

\[

\]

on the `C`-fixed stratum. It sends

\[
Cg\longmapsto Ctg.
\]

Section 6 instead used right multiplication

\[
Cg\longmapsto Cgt.
\]

These coincide on some horizontal orbits but not globally.

Take `g=x_3`. The triangular relation gives

\[
tx_3t^{-1}=x_2x_3,
\qquad

tx_3=x_2x_3t.
\]

Thus

\[

\qquad
(Cx_3)t=Cx_3t.

If these left cosets were equal, there would be `c\in C` with

Right cancellation gives `x_2=c`, impossible because `x_2` lies in the free factor of

\[

and is not in `C_L`.

Therefore the tempting route

\[
\text{right-shift prism}
\stackrel?=\text{Wang/normalizer monodromy}
\]

is **DISPROVED**. This failure is important: without it, one could incorrectly declare the first edge detector-null merely because the right shift is contiguous to inclusion after scale `+2`.
