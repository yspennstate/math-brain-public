# 5. Proof of `P18-HALFSTEP-GEODESIC-RIGIDITY-47`

We prove

\[
|a^dt|=d+1;
\]

the inverse-height case is identical.

The obvious word gives

\[
|a^dt|\le d+1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because `L=2d`, the circle displacement of `a^dt` is the half-integer antipodal displacement

\[
d_{S_L}(0,\rho(a^dt)0)
=d-\frac12.
\]

The element lies in `B`, so the Bass–Serre-tree displacement is zero, while its height contribution is `1/2`. Thus `(4.1)` gives only

\[
|a^dt|\ge d.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume for contradiction that equality holds. Choose a geodesic word

\[
a^dt=s_1\cdots s_d,
\qquad
s_i\in\{a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1},t^{\pm1}\}.
\]

Under `Phi`, every word edge has `ell^1` length exactly one, so the image path has total length exactly `d`. Its endpoint distance is also exactly

\[
\left(d-\frac12\right)+0+\frac12=d.
\]

Equality in the product triangle inequality forces equality in each coordinate variation. In particular, the total Bass–Serre-tree variation of the image path is zero. Hence every step has zero tree motion.

But a letter `b` or `b^{-1}` moves any current tree vertex by one edge, because

\[
d_T(gv_0,gb^{\pm1}v_0)=d_T(v_0,b^{\pm1}v_0)=1.
\]

Therefore the geodesic contains no `b^{\pm1}` letters. It is a word only in `a^{\pm1},t^{\pm1}`.

Inside

\[
B\cong C_L\times\mathbb Z,
\]

a word representing `a^dt` must have total `t`-exponent one, hence at least one `t^{\pm1}` letter, and total `a`-exponent congruent to `d mod 2d`, hence at least `d` letters `a^{\pm1}`. Thus its length is at least

\[
d+1,
\]

contradicting the assumed length `d`.

Together with `(5.1)`,

\[
\boxed{|a^dt|=d+1.}
\]

Replacing `t` by `t^{-1}` proves the second equality in `(0.1)`.

### Why this lemma matters

For odd cyclic order, the half-integer circle displacement already overshoots the critical radius in P54. For even `L`, the `n=+/-1` height layer saturates the coarse calibrated lower bound exactly at `d`; `(0.1)` closes precisely that exceptional equality case.

# 6. Proof of `P18-POWER2-FULL-CYCLIC-HORIZONTALITY-47`

Let `sigma` be a nonempty simplex of `P_d Gamma_L` stabilized setwise by a conjugate of the full cyclic subgroup `C`.

Translate first so that `C` itself stabilizes `sigma`.

Because `c=a^d` lies in `C`, every vertex `h in sigma` has `ch in sigma`; therefore

\[
|h^{-1}ch|\le d.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The P55C Bass–Serre argument applied to `(6.1)` forces every vertex into the HNN vertex group `B`. Thus

\[
\sigma\subset B.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Choose a vertex `h_0 in sigma` and left-translate by `h_0^{-1}`. Since `B=C_L\times Z` centralizes `C`, this translation keeps the setwise stabilizer `C`, and now

\[
1\in\sigma.
\]

Write an arbitrary vertex as

\[
v=a^qt^n.
\]

Since `C` stabilizes `sigma`, the entire orbit

\[
Cv=\{a^kt^n:k\in\mathbb Z/L\}
\]

lies in `sigma`. Every point of that orbit must be within distance `d` of `1`.

We prove `n=0`.

## 6.1 Nonzero even height

If `n` is nonzero even, `n/2` is integral. Choose `k` so that the circle coordinate

\[
k+n/2
\]

is the antipode `d mod L`. For that orbit point, `(4.1)` gives

\[
|a^kt^n|
\ge d+\frac{|n|}{2}
\ge d+1,

contradiction.

## 6.2 Odd height with `|n|>=3`

\[

Choose an orbit point realizing that maximum. If `|n|>=3`, `(4.1)` gives

\[
|a^kt^n|
\ge
d-\frac12+\frac{|n|}{2}
\ge d+1,

again a contradiction.

## 6.3 Exceptional height `n=+/-1`

By `P18-HALFSTEP-GEODESIC-RIGIDITY-47`, that element has word length exactly `d+1`, contradicting the scale-`d` simplex condition.

Hence every vertex has `n=0`, so

\[
\sigma\subset C.

A nonempty subset of the regular left `C`-set `C` which is invariant under all of `C` must be the whole set. Therefore

\[
\sigma=C

as a vertex set. Undoing the translations proves `(0.2)`.
