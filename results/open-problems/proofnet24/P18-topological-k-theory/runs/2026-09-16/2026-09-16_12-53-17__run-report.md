# 4. `P18-FREE-TO-SIGN-SOURCE-MAP-54` — proof

Let

\[
S_\chi=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\},
\qquad
c\cdot z=\chi(c)z,
\]

and

\[
S_\varepsilon=\{w\in\mathbb C:|w|=1\},
\qquad
c\cdot w=\varepsilon(c)w.
\]

The first action is free because `chi` is faithful. The second has constant stabilizer `N` and the residual group `C/N=C_2` acts freely by the antipodal half-turn.

Define

\[
f(z)=z^r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For the generator `c`,

\[
f(c\cdot z)
=(\chi(c)z)^r
=\chi(c)^rz^r
=\varepsilon(c)f(z),
\]

so `f` is `C`-equivariant.

## 4.1 Quotient degree

Use the quotient coordinates

\[
q_\chi(z)=z^{2r}
\]

for `S_chi/C`, and

\[
q_\varepsilon(w)=w^2
\]

for `S_epsilon/(C/N)`. Then

\[
q_\varepsilon(f(z))
=(z^r)^2
=z^{2r}
=q_\chi(z).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the induced map on quotient circles is literally the identity in these coordinates, hence has degree `+1`.

## 4.2 Isotropy coefficient

For the free source circle,

\[
K_1^C(S_\chi)\cong K_1(S_\chi/C)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Let `x_{L,1}` be the generator defined by the positive quotient-circle orientation.

For `S_epsilon`, the isotropy is constantly `N` and conjugation is trivial because `C` is abelian. Equivariant cellular K-homology therefore identifies

\[
K_1^C(S_\varepsilon)\cong R(N).
\]

Under a covariant map of orbit cells whose stabilizer increases from `1` to `N`, the coefficient map is induction

\[
R(1)\to R(N),
\qquad
1\mapsto\operatorname{Ind}_1^N(1)=\rho_N.
\]

Together with the quotient degree `+1` from `(4.2)`, this gives

\[
\boxed{f_*(x_{L,1})=\rho_N.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

If the global source orientation is reversed, both sides acquire the same harmless sign. This proves `P18-FREE-TO-SIGN-SOURCE-MAP-54`.

### Geometric interpretation

The scale-one cyclic Rips complex `P_1C` is the cyclic polygon and is `C`-equivariantly homeomorphic to `S_chi` after choosing the standard orientation. Thus `(4.3)` is an exact geometric model of the retained P18/P25 free cyclic source, not merely an AHSS coefficient computation.

# 7. `P18-TOP-EDGE-COEFFICIENT-AFFINE-IDEAL-54` — proof

The local equatorial coefficient problem can now be solved exactly.

For the complex representation

\[
E=W\oplus Z,
\]

its Euler class is

\[
\boxed{
e_E=\lambda_{-1}(E^*)
=(1-\varepsilon)e_Z.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Consider the pair exact sequence for `(D(E),S(E))`:

\[
K_0^C(D(E))
\longrightarrow
K_0^C(D(E),S(E))
\xrightarrow{\partial_E}
K_1^C(S(E)).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Both first groups identify with `R(C)`. Under the Thom identification, the first map is the zero-section map, i.e. multiplication by `e_E`. Exactness therefore gives

\[
\boxed{\ker\partial_E=e_ER(C).}
\tag{7.3}
\]

By `(5.8)`, `gamma partial_V` agrees with `partial_E` up to one fixed global orientation sign. Fix `s in {+1,-1}` so that

\[
\gamma\partial_V(sr_{\rm src})=(j\circ f)_*(x_{L,1}).
\]

Then for arbitrary `r in R(C)`,

\[
\gamma\partial_V(r)=(j\circ f)_*(x_{L,1})
\]

if and only if

\[
\partial_E(r-sr_{\rm src})=0.
\]

Using `(7.3)`, this is equivalent to

\[
r-sr_{\rm src}\in e_ER(C).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\{r:\gamma\partial_V(r)=(j\circ f)_*(x_{L,1})\}
=sr_{\rm src}+e_ER(C).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is the exact local answer to the coefficient problem.

## 7.1 Why this is the correct invariant

The cell itself carries the full module

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)\cong R(C).
\]

A global relative relation chooses a coefficient in that module. The edge geometry does not choose one. Equation `(7.4)` identifies precisely the coefficients capable of producing the retained source on the proper equator.

Therefore an “actual coefficient `a_sigma`” becomes meaningful only after specifying a particular global cellular/relative class and a representative. Without that extra datum the canonical object is the affine coset `(7.4)`.

This proves `P18-NO-DISTINGUISHED-EDGE-COEFFICIENT-54` as a correction to the overstrong wording of the P53 next target.

# 8. What remains global — constructive and obstruction routes

The exact local calculation still does **not** prove depth-three source death at the first full-cyclic edge.

There are three independent global obligations.

### 8.1 Fixed-pole / singular sector

The map `gamma` forgets information in the two hemisphere pieces. P52 identifies the complementary `tilde E_P` fixed-point-localized shadow and proves anti-diagonal cancellation for a centralizing normalizer loop, but it does not produce an actual same-scale proper-family finite-Rips relation.

Thus `(5.9)` plus P52 does not yet imply that the entire cell boundary equals the retained source in the ambient previous-scale K-homology.

### 8.2 Same-scale proper faces

The equator lies in the proper-isotropy sector of the boundary sphere, but a cellular approximation of it may use proper-isotropy faces that are themselves born at the **same Rips scale**, not in the previous subcomplex. This is exactly why P52's abstract family lift cannot be silently converted into a finite-Rips relation.
