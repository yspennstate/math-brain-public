# 4. Proof of `P18-FULL-CYCLIC-ORBIT-CLIQUE-MODEL-49`

Let `X=P_DG`. The left action of `C` on the vertex set `G` is free.

## 4.1 `C`-invariant simplices are unions of regular orbits

Let `\sigma` be a nonempty simplex of `X` stabilized setwise by `C`. For every vertex `g\in\sigma`, setwise invariance gives

\[
Cg\subseteq \sigma.
\]

Since left multiplication on `G` is free, every orbit `Cg` has cardinality `|C|`. Distinct orbits are disjoint. Thus

\[
V(\sigma)=Cg_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup Cg_s
\tag{4.1}
\]

for a unique finite collection of left `C`-orbits.

Conversely, any union of left `C`-orbits which is a Rips simplex is automatically `C`-invariant.

## 4.2 Exact metric criteria

For one orbit,

\[
d(c_1g,c_2g)
=
|g^{-1}c_1^{-1}c_2g|.
\]

As `c_1^{-1}c_2` runs over `C`, the orbit is a `D`-simplex exactly when

\[
\max_{c\in C}|g^{-1}cg|\le D.
\]

For two orbit vertices `Cg,Ch`, every cross distance has the form

\[
d(c_1g,c_2h)
=
|g^{-1}c_1^{-1}c_2h|,
\]

so, once each orbit is internally a simplex, their union is a simplex exactly when

\[
\max_{c\in C}|g^{-1}ch|\le D.
\]

This proves `(0.1)`–`(0.2)`.

## 4.3 Why the orbit object is a clique complex

Suppose `Cg_1,\ldots,Cg_s` are vertices of `\mathcal O_D`. If they form a graph clique, then for every pair `i,j` the union

\[
Cg_i\cup Cg_j
\]

has diameter at most `D`. Therefore every pair of ordinary group vertices in the total union

\[
\bigcup_i Cg_i
\]

is at distance at most `D`. Since a Rips complex is flag, the total union is a simplex.

The converse is immediate. Hence nonempty `C`-invariant simplices of `P_DG` are canonically the nonempty faces of

\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_D(C;G)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

## 4.4 Barycentric fixed set

Vertices of `sd X` are nonempty simplices of `X`. A barycentric vertex is `C`-fixed exactly when its underlying simplex is `C`-invariant.

A simplex of `sd X` is a strict chain

\[
\sigma_0\subsetneq\sigma_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\sigma_r.
\]

If `C` stabilizes this chain, it preserves cardinality and inclusion, so it fixes each `\sigma_j` setwise. Thus barycentric subdivision removes inversions: the simplicial `C`-fixed set is the order complex of the poset of nonempty `C`-invariant simplices.

Using `(4.2)`, that order complex is exactly

\[
sd\,\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_D(C;G)).
\]

This proves `(0.3)`.

### Why this matters

Earlier failed routes tried to infer quotient cells from quotient vertices. The theorem above does not do that. It records an entire stabilized simplex by the collection of full `C`-orbits it contains and preserves every inclusion relation through the face poset. It is therefore compatible with Bredon and equivariant cellular calculations.

# 5. Proof of `P18-TRIANGULAR-TOP-STRATUM-NORMALIZER-49`

Put

\[
H=C*F_{m-1}.
\]

A free factor is malnormal in a free product: if `h\notin C`, then a reduced-normal-form calculation gives

\[
hCh^{-1}\cap C=\{1\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `h` normalizes the nontrivial finite group `C`, then

\[
hCh^{-1}=C,
\]

so the intersection in `(5.1)` is all of `C`, a contradiction. Therefore

\[
N_H(C)=C.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Now every element of

\[
\Gamma=H\rtimes_\alpha\langle t\rangle
\]

has the form `ht^n`. Since `\alpha(x_1)=x_1`, the stable letter centralizes `C`:

\[
tct^{-1}=c\qquad(c\in C).
\]

Hence

\[
(ht^n)C(ht^n)^{-1}=hCh^{-1}.
\]

It equals `C` exactly when `h\in C`, by `(5.2)`. Thus

\[
N_\Gamma(C)=C\langle t\rangle=C\times\langle t\rangle.
\]

Since `C\cap\langle t\rangle=1`, quotienting gives

\[
W_\Gamma(C)=N_\Gamma(C)/C\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

The normalizer acts on `(sd P_D\Gamma)^C`; `C` acts trivially there, so the action factors through this residual `\mathbb Z`. The same statement for a conjugate `hCh^{-1}` follows by translation.

This proves `(0.4)` and identifies the exact residual monodromy that a higher-depth top-stratum Wang calculation must track.

# 6. Proof of `P18-TOP-STRATUM-ORBIT-PARITY-DICTIONARY-49`

Let `C=C_L` with `L` even. Suppose the vertex set of a `C`-invariant simplex is a disjoint union of `s` regular `C`-orbits. The real permutation representation on vertices is

\[
s\,\mathbb R[C].
\]

Centering at the barycenter removes one trivial line, so the affine tangent representation is

\[
V_s=s\,\mathbb R[C]-\mathbb R.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Radial rescaling gives a `C`-equivariant pair isomorphism

\[
(\sigma,\partial\sigma)
\cong_C
(D(V_s),S(V_s)).
\tag{6.2}
\]

For even `L`, the real regular representation decomposes as

\[
\mathbb R[C]
\cong
\mathbb R\oplus\varepsilon\oplus U_{\mathbb R},
\tag{6.3}
\]

where `\varepsilon` is the real sign representation with kernel `C_{L/2}` and `U` is a complex representation containing the remaining nonreal characters in conjugate pairs. Substituting `(6.3)` into `(6.1)` gives

\[
V_s
\cong
(s-1)\mathbb R
\oplus
s\varepsilon
\oplus
sU_{\mathbb R}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 6.1 `s` even

Then `s\varepsilon` is the underlying real representation of a complex representation, while `(s-1)\mathbb R` has one unpaired trivial real line. Hence

\[
V_s\cong\mathbb R\oplus W_{\mathbb R}
\]

for a complex `C`-representation `W`. Complex equivariant Bott periodicity followed by suspension by the real trivial line gives

\[
K_i^C(D(V_s),S(V_s))
\cong
K_{i-1}^C(pt).
\]

Therefore

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)\cong R(C),
\qquad
K_0^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)=0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

## 6.2 `s` odd

Now `(s-1)\mathbb R` is complexifiable in pairs, while `s\varepsilon` contains one unpaired sign line. Thus

\[
V_s\cong\varepsilon\oplus W_{\mathbb R}.
\]

Bott periodicity reduces to the sign-line pair. Its sphere is

\[
S(\varepsilon)=C/C_{L/2}.
\]

The pair exact sequence is

\[
0\to K_1^C(D\varepsilon,S\varepsilon)
\to R(C_{L/2})
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ind}}
R(C)
\to K_0^C(D\varepsilon,S\varepsilon)
\to0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

If `\eta_j` is a character of `C_{L/2}` and `\chi` is the standard character of `C_L`, then

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_{L/2}}^{C_L}(\eta_j)
=
\chi^j+\chi^{j+L/2}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

These pairs have disjoint support in the character basis of `R(C_L)`, so induction is injective. Hence

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)=0,
\]

and `(0.6)` follows.

Because `s` orbit vertices form an `(s-1)`-simplex of the orbit-clique complex, `(6.5)` says precisely:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local }K_0\text{-killing relative }K_1
\text{ occurs exactly on odd-dimensional orbit-clique simplices.}
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

This rederives the relevant P25 multiorbit calculation independently and translates it into the new fixed-stratum geometry.

# 7. Proof of `P18-TOP-STRATUM-MV-SURVIVAL-CRITERION-49`

Let

\[
X=X_{\rm prop}\cup X_{\rm top},
\qquad
J=X_{\rm prop}\cap X_{\rm top},
\qquad
A\subset J.
\]

The triple

\[
A\subset J\subset X_{\rm top}
\]

has exact segment

\[
K_1^G(X_{\rm top},J)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(J,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(X_{\rm top},A).
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `(0.7)` holds, the second arrow in `(7.1)` is injective.

Relative Mayer–Vietoris for the cover of the pair `(X,A)` contains

\[
K_1^G(X_{\rm prop},A)
\oplus
K_1^G(X_{\rm top},A)
\longrightarrow
K_1^G(X,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(J,A)
\longrightarrow
K_0^G(X_{\rm prop},A)
\oplus
K_0^G(X_{\rm top},A).
\tag{7.2}
\]

The last map in `(7.2)` is injective because its second component is injective. Hence the connecting arrow from `K_1^G(X,A)` is zero, and the first arrow is surjective. This proves `(0.8)`.

Now let

\[
\partial_X:K_1^G(X,A)\to K_0^G(A)
\]

be the pair boundary, and similarly `\partial_{\rm prop}`, `\partial_{\rm top}`. Naturality of the pair exact sequence gives

\[
\partial_X(u_{\rm prop}+u_{\rm top})
=
\partial_{\rm prop}(u_{\rm prop})
+
\partial_{\rm top}(u_{\rm top}).
\]

Therefore, if

\[
\Phi\partial_{\rm prop}=0,
\qquad
\Phi\partial_{\rm top}=0,
\tag{7.3}
\]

then every boundary in `\operatorname{im}\partial_X` lies in `\ker\Phi`. A class `z\in K_0^G(A)` with `\Phi(z)\ne0` cannot lie in that boundary image and therefore cannot die in `K_0^G(X)`.
