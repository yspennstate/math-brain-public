\[

\tag{5.1}

## 5.2 Quadratic lower bound

\[
\kappa_L\le L/2,

so

\[
L\ge2\kappa_L.

The lower full-source bound `(4.3)` then gives

\[
\Delta_L\ge L/3\ge\frac23\kappa_L.
\tag{5.2}

The upper boundary estimate in `(4.2)` gives

\[
\Theta_L
\le10\lceil\sqrt{\kappa_L}\rceil
\le10(\sqrt{\kappa_L}+1).

\[
\sqrt{\kappa_L}
\ge\max\left\{0,\frac{\Theta_L}{10}-1\right\}.

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_L
\ge
\frac23

\max\left\{0,\frac{\Theta_L}{10}-1\right\}

\tag{5.3}

Combining `(5.1)` and `(5.3)` proves `(0.1)`.

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_L}{\Theta_L^2}

# 7. `P18-M2-SCHREIER-SHEAR-MODEL-40` — PROVED

The preceding exponent theorem is numerical. We now identify an exact geometric source for square-root compression.

Let

\[
H=C_L*\langle b\rangle,
\qquad
\pi:H\to C_L,
\quad
\pi(a)=a,
\quad
\pi(b)=1,
\]

and

\[
N=\ker\pi.
\]

`N` is normal in `H`, though not normal in the full semidirect product `Gamma`.

Every element of `Gamma` has a unique normal form

\[
h t^n,
\qquad h\in H,\ n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Consider the set of left cosets `N\Gamma`.

## 7.1 Vertex coordinates

Define

\[
\Psi:N\backslash\Gamma\to C_L\times\mathbb Z
\]

by

\[
\Psi(Nht^n)=(\pi(h),n).
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is well defined. Indeed,

\[
Nh_1t^n=Nh_2t^n
\]

if and only if

\[
h_2h_1^{-1}\in N,
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
\pi(h_1)=\pi(h_2).
\]

Different heights cannot lie in one left `N`-coset because `N` lies in the height-zero subgroup `H`.

Thus `(7.1)` is a bijection. Write its vertices as

\[
(r,n)\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z.
\]

## 7.2 Generator edges

We use right multiplication by the standard generators to describe the Schreier graph.

Since `alpha(a)=a`,

\[
ht^n a=h\alpha^n(a)t^n=ha t^n,
\]

so

\[
a^{\pm1}:(r,n)\mapsto(r\pm1,n).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Obviously

\[
t^{\pm1}:(r,n)\mapsto(r,n\pm1).
\tag{7.3}
\]

For all integers `n`,

\[
\alpha^n(b)=a^n b.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For positive `n` this follows by induction from `alpha(b)=ab`; for negative `n`, `alpha^{-1}(b)=a^{-1}b` gives the same formula.

Therefore

\[
ht^n b=h\alpha^n(b)t^n=ha^n b t^n.
\]

Applying `pi`,

\[
\pi(ha^nb)=\pi(h)a^n.
\]

Hence

\[
b:(r,n)\mapsto(r+n,n).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Similarly

\[
b^{-1}:(r,n)\mapsto(r-n,n).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Equations `(7.2)`–`(7.6)` prove the exact shear model `(0.4)`.

The residual group `H/N\cong C_L` acts by

\[
a^j\cdot(r,n)=(r+j,n),
\]

so the model is `C_L`-equivariant.

# 8. Quadratic distortion in the Schreier quotient

Let `d_S` denote the graph metric in the Schreier graph of Section 7. Put

\[
|s|_L=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|s+kL|.
\]

We prove `(0.5)`.

## 8.1 Upper bound

Assume `1<=|s|_L<=L/2` and replace `s` by the signed representative with absolute value `|s|_L`. By symmetry it is enough to take `s>0`.

Choose

\[
q=\lceil\sqrt s\rceil,
\qquad
k=\lfloor s/q\rfloor,
\qquad
r=s-kq,
\]

so

\[
0\le r<q.
\]

In the shear graph, use the path

\[
(0,0)
\xrightarrow{t^q}
(0,q)
\xrightarrow{b^k}
(kq,q)
\xrightarrow{a^r}
(s,q)
\xrightarrow{t^{-q}}
(s,0).
\]

Its length is

\[
2q+k+r.
\]

Now

\[
q\le\sqrt s+1,
\qquad
k\le s/q\le\sqrt s,
\qquad
r<q\le\sqrt s+1.
\]

Therefore

\[
2q+k+r
<4\sqrt s+3.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
d_S((0,0),(s,0))\le4\sqrt{|s|_L}+3.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The path is explicit. No combinadic or divisor input is used.

## 8.2 Lower bound

Consider any Schreier path of length `ell` from `(0,0)` to `(s,0)`.

Lift the cyclic coordinate along the path from `Z/LZ` to an integer coordinate `R`, starting at zero and summing the signed coordinate increments of each generator step. At every intermediate point, the height satisfies

\[
|n|\le\ell,
\]

because changing height costs one `t`-edge.

An `a^{\pm1}` step changes `R` by at most `1`. A `b^{\pm1}` step at height `n` changes `R` by `\pm n`, whose absolute value is at most `ell`. A `t` step does not change `R`.

There are at most `ell` generator steps total. Thus a coarse but sufficient estimate is

\[
|R|\le\ell^2+\ell.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The endpoint condition says

\[
R\equiv s\pmod L,
\]

so

\[
|R|\ge|s|_L.
\]

Therefore

\[
|s|_L\le\ell^2+\ell.
\]

Solving the quadratic inequality gives

\[
\ell
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{1+4|s|_L}-1}{2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 d_S((0,0),(s,0))
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{1+4|s|_L}-1}{2}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Together `(8.1)` and `(8.3)` prove `(0.5)`.

### Interpretation

At height zero, the base quotient `N\H` is just the ordinary cyclic factor, where distance to `a^s` is linear in `|s|_L`. After the stable-letter direction is admitted, a single `b`-edge at height `n` moves the cyclic coordinate by `n`. Going to height `q`, taking about `s/q` shear steps, and returning gives the square-root optimization.

Thus the finite-height boundary construction sees a quotient geometry in which cyclic displacement is quadratically distorted. The full group, by P25-P39, retains the linear conjugate-length detector. The square-root-to-linear gap is therefore geometric, not an artifact of a loose asymptotic estimate.

# 9. `P18-GLOBAL-CYCLIC-RETRACTION-NOGO-40` — PROVED route obstruction

A tempting attempt to finish the restricted source problem would be to extend the P18 base free-factor split from `P_sH` to all of `P_D\Gamma` by quotienting by `N` and then projecting to the cyclic coordinate.

Section 7 shows exactly why that cannot have uniform finite control.

Define the obvious vertex projection

\[
\varphi:N\backslash\Gamma\to C_L,
\qquad
\varphi(r,n)=r.
\tag{9.1}
\]

At height `n`, a **single** `b`-edge joins

\[
(r,n)
\quad\text{to}\quad
(r+n,n).
\]

Its image under `varphi` has cyclic distance

\[
|n|_L.
\]

As `n` varies, this can be as large as `floor(L/2)`, even though the source edge has length one.

Therefore there is no constant `S`, independent of `L` and height, for which `(9.1)` extends to a simplicial map

\[
N\backslash P_1\Gamma\to P_SC_L.
\]

More generally, no Lipschitz bound independent of height exists for the naive cyclic-coordinate retraction.

This proves:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The finite-Rips free-factor split on the base cannot be extended to the whole restricted semidirect Rips space by the naive }N\text{-quotient cyclic projection.}
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This is precisely the sort of hidden control failure that the prior P18 conditional theorem left open.

It does **not** prove that the restricted class survives linearly; another, non-naive detector may exist. It does, however, eliminate the most direct attempt to use the P18 free-kernel quotient to obtain a root-scale restricted null relation.
