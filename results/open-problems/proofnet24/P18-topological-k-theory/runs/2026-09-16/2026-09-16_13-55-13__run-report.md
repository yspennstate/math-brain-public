\[

\[
d(u,v)=D.
\]

\[
\boxed{

=
\operatorname{Ind}(M_D(\sigma)),
}

where `Ind` denotes the independence complex. Thus all simultaneously born faces are encoded by one finite `C`-graph.

Writing

\[
K_D(\sigma):=\sigma\cap P_{D-1}G,

\[
K_D(\sigma)\subset B\subset\sigma

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,K_D)
\xrightarrow{q_D}
K_1^C(\sigma,B)
\xrightarrow{\omega_D}
K_0^C(B,K_D).

\[
\boxed{

\ker\omega_D.

# 4. Proof of `P18-RIPS-CRITICAL-GRAPH-LIFT-55`

Let `sigma` be a finite simplex of `P_D G` with diameter exactly `D`. Because the metric is integer-valued, every pair of vertices of `sigma` has distance at most `D`, and a face `F subset sigma` is a simplex of `P_{D-1}G` if and only if every pair in `F` has distance at most `D-1`.

Define `M_D(sigma)` on the same vertex set by

\[
\{u,v\}\in E(M_D(\sigma))
\iff d(u,v)=D.
\]

Then a subset `F` is a face of `sigma cap P_{D-1}G` exactly when it contains no edge of `M_D(sigma)`. That is precisely the definition of the independence complex:

\[
\boxed{
K_D(\sigma):=\sigma\cap P_{D-1}G
=
\operatorname{Ind}(M_D(\sigma)).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `sigma` is `C`-invariant, left invariance of the metric makes `M_D(sigma)` a `C`-graph, so `(4.1)` is `C`-equivariant.

Because `diam(sigma)=D`, the full simplex `sigma` is not in `P_{D-1}G`; hence

\[
K_D(\sigma)\subset\partial\sigma.
\]

Apply equivariant K-homology to the triple

\[
K_D(\sigma)\subset\partial\sigma\subset\sigma.
\]

The long exact sequence contains

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,K_D)
\xrightarrow{q_D}
K_1^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)
\xrightarrow{\omega_D}
K_0^C(\partial\sigma,K_D).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Exactness gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}q_D=\ker\omega_D.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This proves the lift-module statement.

For a two-regular-orbit full-`C` simplex and even `|C|`, P49 gives a Bott identification

\[
K_1^C(\sigma,\partial\sigma)\cong R(C).
\]

Under this identification define

\[
\boxed{L_D(\sigma):=\operatorname{im}q_D\subset R(C).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

P54 proves that the coefficients giving the oriented proper-equatorial source are exactly

\[
 s r_{\rm src}+e_E R(C).
\]

Therefore a local actual-Rips lift with the P54 source boundary exists if and only if

\[
\boxed{
L_D(\sigma)\cap
(s r_{\rm src}+e_E R(C))\ne\varnothing.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

No cellular approximation or filtration collapse is hidden in `(4.5)`; it is simply exactness of the genuine pair/triple at the Rips jump.

# 6. Singleton critical offset: geometry of the matching sphere

Assume now

\[
C=C_{2r},
\qquad
|S_D(g,h)|=1.
\]

Write the unique offset as `s_0`. Relabel the second block by

\[
b_c:=cs_0h,
\]

and the first by

\[
a_c:=cg.
\]

Then

\[
M_D(\sigma)
=
\bigsqcup_{c\in C}\{a_c,b_c\},
\]

a perfect matching.

A face belongs to the independence complex exactly when for each `c` it contains at most one of `a_c,b_c`. Therefore

\[
K_D(\sigma)
=
*_{c\in C}\{a_c,b_c\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

the join of `2`-point sets. This is the boundary of the `|C|`-dimensional cross-polytope.

The `C`-action shifts the matching index:

\[
k:a_c\mapsto a_{kc},
\qquad
k:b_c\mapsto b_{kc}.
\]

Identifying

\[
a_c\leftrightarrow +e_c,
\qquad
b_c\leftrightarrow -e_c
\]

in the real regular representation

\[
W:=\mathbb R[C],
\]
ngives a `C`-equivariant PL identification

\[
\boxed{K_D(\sigma)\cong_C S(W).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

There is also an explicit ambient-coordinate description. Write barycentric coordinates on `sigma` as

\[
(\alpha_c,\beta_c)_{c\in C},
\qquad
\alpha_c,\beta_c\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_c(\alpha_c+\beta_c)=1.
\]

Set

\[
d_c=\alpha_c-\beta_c,
\qquad
s_c=\alpha_c+\beta_c.
\]

The matching sphere condition is precisely

\[
\alpha_c\beta_c=0
\quad\forall c,
\]

or equivalently

\[
s_c=|d_c|
\quad\forall c.
\]

Hence `(6.2)` is concretely the `ell^1` unit sphere

\[
\sum_c|d_c|=1
\]

in `W`. The normal slack variables

\[
r_c=s_c-|d_c|\ge0
\]

have one linear sum relation; their normal link is the boundary of the simplex on `C`, carrying the centered regular representation

\[
W_0:=\mathbb R[C]-\mathbb R.
\]

Thus the stable normal `C`-representation of the matching sphere in the full boundary is exactly

\[
\boxed{W_0=\mathbb R[C]-\mathbb R.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The centered representation of the full two-orbit simplex is

\[
V
=2\mathbb R[C]-\mathbb R
\cong W\oplus W_0,
\tag{6.4}
\]

consistent with `(6.3)`.

The quotient map of pairs

\[
(\sigma,K_D)\longrightarrow(\sigma,\partial\sigma)
\]

is therefore the representation-sphere Euler/Thom map obtained by adding the normal representation `W_0`.
