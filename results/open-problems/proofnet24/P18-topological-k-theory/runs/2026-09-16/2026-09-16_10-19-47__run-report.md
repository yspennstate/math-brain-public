## `P18-TWO-ORBIT-TOP-FACE-ANTIDIAGONAL-51` — **PROVED**

# 4. Proof of `P18-EVEN-CYCLIC-BLOCK-SWAP-51`

Let `s` interchange the two regular vertex orbits.

## 4.1 Diagonal/anti-diagonal decomposition

Under the swap, the two copies of `R[C]` split into diagonal and anti-diagonal parts. After removing the global barycenter line,

\[
V_2
\cong
\widetilde{\mathbb R[C]}_+
\oplus
\mathbb R[C]_-.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The subscripts record the swap eigenvalue. Expanding the regular representation gives

\[
V_2
\cong
\mathbb R_-
\oplus
(\varepsilon_+\oplus\varepsilon_-)
\oplus
(U_+)_{\mathbb R}
\oplus
(U_-)_{\mathbb R}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Pair the two sign lines into the complex sign representation

\[
\varepsilon_+\oplus\varepsilon_-
\cong_{\mathbb R}
\mathbb C_\varepsilon.
\]

In these coordinates the block swap acts by:

- reflection `x -> -x` on the trivial real line `R_-`;
- complex conjugation on `C_epsilon`;
- identity on `U_+` and complex scalar `-1` on `U_-`.

The last two maps on `U_+ direct-sum U_-` are complex-linear and homotopic through `C`-equivariant complex-linear automorphisms to the identity, so they act trivially on the Bott factor.

## 4.2 Conjugation on the complex sign Bott class

The complex sign line factors through the quotient

\[
C\twoheadrightarrow C_2.
\]

For `C_2`, let `beta` be the equivariant Bott generator of

\[
K_0^{C_2}(D\mathbb C_\varepsilon,S\mathbb C_\varepsilon)
\cong R(C_2).
\]

Complex conjugation `kappa` is `C_2`-equivariant. On the zero section it is the identity, while after forgetting the group action it reverses the orientation of the two-real-dimensional disk. The equivariant Thom Euler class is `1-epsilon`. These two facts force

\[
\kappa_*(\beta)=-\varepsilon\beta.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed the multiplier must have ordinary dimension `-1`, and naturality with the zero-section Euler class excludes `-1`; the `C_2` representation ring leaves `-epsilon` as the compatible unit. Inflating along `C -> C_2` gives the same formula for `C_{2r}`.

## 4.3 Multiply the two orientation effects

Reflection of the remaining real suspension line acts by `-1` on the suspended K-class, while `(4.3)` contributes `-epsilon`. Therefore

\[
s_*=(-1)(-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
\]

on the free rank-one `R(C)` module `(3.3)`.

This proves `(0.1)`.

### Sanity check

The nonequivariant degree of the block swap is the sign of `2r` transpositions, hence `+1`. Since `dim(epsilon)=1`, `(0.1)` has the correct forgotten degree. This also shows why the tempting scalar `-1` answer is wrong.

# 5. Proper-face quotient and anti-diagonal boundary

Define `P subset partial sigma` to be the union of every proper face except the two full-orbit faces `tau_0,tau_1` themselves. It contains `partial tau_0` and `partial tau_1`.

A face of `sigma` is stabilized by all of `C` exactly when its vertex set is a union of complete regular `C`-orbits. Since there are only two orbit copies, the only nonempty proper full-`C` faces are `tau_0,tau_1`. Therefore every simplex of `P` has proper stabilizer.

Collapsing `P` gives

\[
K_0^C(\partial\sigma,P)
\cong
K_0^C(\tau_0,\partial\tau_0)
\oplus
K_0^C(\tau_1,\partial\tau_1).
\tag{5.1}

For one regular orbit, P18-P49 gives

\[
K_0^C(\tau_i,\partial\tau_i)
\cong
Q_C
:=R(C)/\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^{C}R(C_r).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `chi` is the standard character of `C`, then a character `eta_j` of `C_r` induces to

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^C(\eta_j)
=\chi^j+\chi^{j+r}
=\chi^j(1+\varepsilon).
\]

Hence

\[
\operatorname{Ind}_{C_r}^C R(C_r)
=(1+\varepsilon)R(C),
\tag{5.3}

and therefore

\[
\varepsilon=-1\quad\text{in }Q_C.
\tag{5.4}

Let

\[
\bar\partial:R(C)\to Q_C\oplus Q_C

be the pair boundary followed by `(5.1)`. The entire construction is `R(C)`-linear. Write

\[
\bar\partial(r)=(A(r),B(r)).

Under the label-preserving identifications of each orbit face with the standard regular `C`-simplex, the block swap merely exchanges the two `Q_C` summands. Naturality and `(0.1)` give

\[
\bar\partial(\varepsilon r)
=s_*\bar\partial(r)
=(B(r),A(r)).

By `R(C)`-linearity the left-hand side is

\[
(\varepsilon A(r),\varepsilon B(r)).

Hence

\[
B(r)=\varepsilon A(r).
\tag{5.5}

Combining `(5.4)` and `(5.5)` gives

\[
\boxed{
\bar\partial(r)=(A(r),-A(r)).

\tag{5.6}

This proves `P18-TWO-ORBIT-TOP-FACE-ANTIDIAGONAL-51`.

# 6. Proof of `P18-NORMALIZER-LOOP-EDGE-FAMILY-WANG-NOGO-51`

\[
\tau_1=n\tau_0

for some `n in N_G(C)` which centralizes `C`.

\boxed{
\text{the full-}C\text{ one-orbit-face shadow of the loop-edge boundary is zero.}

\tag{6.1}

The height of every vertex in `Cht^n` is `n`, so exactly one power of `t` moves the orbit to height zero: `k=-n`. Equation `(7.1)` then gives

\[

If it is a loop, the height difference forces the normalizer power identifying its two endpoints to be exactly one, so Section 6 applies.
