# 3. Proof of NEW-L109

## 3.1 B1: change category to a stopped/killed tagged walk

During the first physical period, before cyclic-time reuse occurs, one tagged interchange particle is exactly the continuous-time simple random walk on `Z^3` in which each of the six nearest-neighbor directions occurs at rate one. The three coordinate processes are independent one-dimensional walks with `+1` and `-1` jumps each at rate one.

We expose the tagged path algorithmically. At current physical time `s` and current vertex `v`, inspect only the incident-edge clock information needed to determine the first incident ring in `(s,beta]`; move to the corresponding neighbor and repeat. If the path exits `B_infty(x,R)`, stop and declare failure. No nonincident clocks are inspected. The search for the next ring is truncated at `beta`, so no clock after the physical slab is needed.

Thus, on the localization event, every queried edge has an endpoint in `B_infty(x,R)` and hence lies inside the deterministic spatial enlargement `B_infty(x,R+1)`. This is a stopped/killed-walk formulation of the desired local block step.

The finite adaptive no-peeking theorem already archived as `NEW-L28` applies to this exploration: conditional on a finite history generated by admissible adaptive queries, every unqueried portion of edge-time space still carries an independent rate-one Poisson process. It is essential that the random guard below is selected from the revealed history, not by looking at unseen marks.

## 3.2 One-dimensional heat-kernel bound

For one coordinate, Fourier inversion gives

\[
p_t(0,k)
=\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 e^{-2t(1-\cos\theta)}e^{-ik\theta}\,d\theta.
\]

For `|theta|<=pi`,

\[
1-\cos\theta\ge\frac{2\theta^2}{\pi^2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sup_k p_t(0,k)
\le
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
 e^{-4t\theta^2/\pi^2}\,d\theta
=
\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4\sqrt t}
<\frac4{9\sqrt t}.
\tag{L109.2}
\]

The final strict inequality follows from `pi<256/81`.

## 3.3 The oriented endpoint has probability at least 343/1024

By symmetry of the first coordinate,

\[
\mathbb P(X_\beta^{(1)}-x^{(1)}\ge0)\ge\frac12.
\]

Because `S=sqrt(beta)>=4096`,

\[
\frac dS
\le\frac18+\frac1S
\le\frac18+\frac1{4096}
<\frac9{64}.
\]

Using (L109.2),

\[
\mathbb P\left(0\le X_\beta^{(1)}-x^{(1)}<d\right)
\le d\frac4{9S}
\le\frac1{16}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(X_\beta^{(1)}-x^{(1)}\ge d)\ge\frac7{16}.
}
\tag{L109.3}
\]

Each transverse coordinate has variance `2 beta`. Chebyshev gives

\[
\mathbb P\left(
|X_\beta^{(i)}-x^{(i)}|>4S
\right)
\le\frac{2\beta}{16\beta}
=\frac18,
\qquad i=2,3.
\]

Hence each transverse condition has probability at least `7/8`. Since the three coordinate walks are independent,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_\beta)
\ge
\frac7{16}\left(\frac78\right)^2
=\frac{343}{1024},
}
\tag{L109.4}
\]

where `E_beta` denotes the three endpoint/port inequalities.

## 3.4 Explicit path-confinement bound

For a one-dimensional coordinate `Y_t`,

\[
M_t(\theta)
=\exp\left\{
\theta(Y_t-Y_0)-2t(\cosh\theta-1)
\right\}
\]

is a nonnegative martingale. If `0<a<=4T`, choose `theta=a/(4T)<=1`. Since `cosh theta-1<=theta^2` on `[0,1]`, Doob's inequality yields

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{s\le T}(Y_s-Y_0)\ge a\right)
\le e^{-a^2/(8T)}.
\]

If `a>4T`, choose `theta=1`; using `cosh1-1<1` and `2T<a/2`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{s\le T}(Y_s-Y_0)\ge a\right)
\le e^{-a/2}.
\]

Apply the same argument to `-Y` and union over the three coordinates. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\sup_{s\le T}\|X_s-X_0\|_\infty\ge a
\right)
\le
6\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{a^2}{8T},\frac a2\right\}\right].
}
\tag{L109.5}
\]

For `a=R`, `T=beta`, we have `R>=16sqrt(beta)`, so the exponent in (L109.5) is at least `32`.

For `a=r`, `T=G`, we have `r>=16sqrt(G)`. At `beta>=2^24`, `G>=64`, hence `r/2>=64`, while `r^2/(8G)>=32`; again the exponent is at least `32`. Therefore, writing `A_beta` for full-period confinement and `B_beta` for early confinement,

\[
\mathbb P(A_\beta^c)\le6e^{-32}<\frac6{2^{32}},
\qquad
\mathbb P(B_\beta^c)\le6e^{-32}<\frac6{2^{32}}.
\tag{L109.6}
\]

No independence between `E_beta`, `A_beta` and `B_beta` is needed.

## 3.5 B3: include the fresh successor port in the conclusion

It remains to prove that on `E_beta cap B_beta`, the early guard at the actual endpoint `a_1` has not been consumed by the first traversal.

Set

\[
u=\beta^{1/8}\ge8.
\]

Then

\[
d\ge\frac{\sqrt\beta}{8}=\frac{u^4}{8},
\qquad
r\le16\sqrt G+1\le16u+1.
\]

For `u>=8`,

\[
\frac{u^4}{8}>32u+4,
\]

because the left-minus-right side is positive at `u=8` and has positive derivative thereafter. Hence

\[
\boxed{d>2r+2.}
\tag{L109.7}
\]

On the oriented endpoint event, the centers `x` and `a_1` are separated in the first coordinate by at least `d`. Thus the expanded vertex sets

\[
B_\infty(x,r+1),
\qquad
B_\infty(a_1,r+1)
\]

are disjoint. Consequently no edge incident to `B_infty(x,r)` is incident to `B_infty(a_1,r)`.

During physical times `t<=G`, the early-confinement event forces the tagged particle to remain in `B_infty(x,r)`, so every edge whose clock can be queried there belongs to the first edge family, disjoint from the successor guard edge family. Once the exploration time exceeds `G`, the chronological no-peeking algorithm queries only physical clock times larger than `G`. It therefore cannot inspect the successor guard's time interval `[0,G]` later in the traversal.

At time `beta` the exact endpoint `a_1=pi_beta(x)` is known from the revealed tagged-path history. The random set `Gamma(a_1)` is then a measurable function of that history, but every one of its edge-time points is unqueried. By `NEW-L28`, conditional on the revealed first-traversal history, the clocks in `Gamma(a_1)` remain mutually independent rate-one Poisson processes with their original law.

This proves the fresh-successor-port clause.

## 3.6 Finish the probability bound

By the union bound and (L109.4)--(L109.6),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(C_\beta(x))
&\ge
\mathbb P(E_\beta)
-\mathbb P(A_\beta^c)
-\mathbb P(B_\beta^c)\\
&\ge
\frac{343}{1024}-\frac{12}{2^{32}}\\
&=
\frac{359661565}{1073741824}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally,

\[
\frac{359661565}{1073741824}-\frac13
=
\frac{5242871}{3221225472}>0.
\]

This proves (L109.1) and the theorem. `square`

# 5. B15 counterpressure: why one directed anchor cannot be the final block event

Breaking symmetry to one `+e_1` direction made the special case provable, but it also exposes a structural ceiling.

For every `d>=1`, symmetry of the tagged endpoint gives

\[
\mathbb P(X_\beta^{(1)}-x^{(1)}\ge d)
\le
\mathbb P(X_\beta^{(1)}-x^{(1)}>0)
<\frac12.
\]

Hence **any** one-anchor block event that requires a specified positive-direction displacement has success probability at most `1/2`, regardless of how large `beta` is. In particular, the present theorem cannot be upgraded merely by increasing `beta` into the very-high-probability good-block event required by the already archived crude 2-dependent Peierls gate.

This is not a counterexample to Tóth's conjecture. It is a counterexample to a tempting transfer step: “make a single previsible anchor cross one prescribed face with probability tending to one.” That strategy is impossible by reflection symmetry.

A source-level renormalization must instead exploit at least one genuinely new ingredient: multiple previsible candidate anchors, multiple acceptable exit directions/ports, branching, or a rigorously proved dependent-percolation comparison whose threshold is compatible with the actual local success probability. No such percolation conclusion is claimed here.
