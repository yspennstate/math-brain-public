## 3. B6 — quantitative failure of literal tree deformation

Hammond and Björnberg--Ueltschi succeed because the underlying graph is itself a tree: unique root-to-vertex paths and descendant-subtree factorization are built into the geometry. A tempting deformation on Z3 is to choose a nearest-neighbor spanning tree inside a block and suppress all non-tree clocks during a useful time window. The following elementary estimate shows that this literal transfer has exponentially bad probability in the number of block vertices.

### NEW-L104 — tree-isolation cost on Z3

Let `V` be a finite set of `n>=1` vertices in Z3 and let `T=(V,E_T)` be a nearest-neighbor spanning tree on `V`, so `|E_T|=n-1`. Let

\[
S_T:=\{e:\ e\text{ is a nearest-neighbor lattice edge incident to }V,\ e\notin E_T\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{|S_T|\ge 2n+1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently, for any physical-time interval of length `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P(\text{no clock in }S_T\text{ rings during the interval})
=e^{-t|S_T|}
\le e^{-t(2n+1)}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Every vertex of Z3 has six incident nearest-neighbor edges. Counting incidences from vertices in `V` therefore gives exactly `6n`. Each tree edge has both endpoints in `V`, so the `n-1` tree edges account for exactly `2(n-1)` of these incidences. Hence the incidences belonging to edges of `S_T` total

\[
6n-2(n-1)=4n+2.
\]

Each distinct edge of `S_T` can account for at most two such incidences, so

\[
2|S_T|\ge 4n+2,
\]

which is (3.1). The rate-one Poisson clocks on distinct lattice edges are independent. The probability that no clock among `|S_T|` specified edges rings in a length-`t` interval is exactly `exp(-t|S_T|)`, giving (3.2). `square`

### Consequence and scope

At every fixed positive `t`, literal isolation of an `n`-vertex tree inside Z3 has probability at most `e^{-2tn+O(1)}`. To keep this isolation probability bounded below as `n->infinity`, one would need `t=O(1/n)`, whereas the tree phase-transition theorems use a fixed nonzero time parameter on the tree. Thus the direct deformation “embed the tree theorem and silence every extra lattice edge” cannot be a high-probability good-block mechanism.

This does **not** prove that every sophisticated comparison to a tree must fail. It quantifies the failure of the most literal missing hypothesis: actual descendant-subtree independence cannot be manufactured on a large Z3 block by simply deleting the surplus Poisson clocks at acceptable probability cost.

## 4. B21 — a finite-budget multiscale decoupling theorem from C27's fresh-pair lemma

C27's `C27-FRESH-PAIR-01` supplies the correct Z3 replacement for one piece of tree independence: if two starts are selected nonanticipatively in a region whose clocks are still fresh during a physical-time interval, then their relative motion has the ordinary three-dimensional transience bound until they approach or leave the region.

The issue for a block argument is not one pair but the **number of pairs generated by an exploration**. The next theorem makes that budget explicit.

### NEW-L105 — finite-budget fresh-pair leakage

Fix a physical-time interval `I=[s,s+T]`, a region `U subset Z3`, and an admissible history `F` which has revealed no Poisson clock on any edge incident to `U` during `I`. Let

\[
x_1,\dots,x_M,\qquad y_1,\dots,y_N
\]

be `F`-measurable starting vertices in `U`. Assume

\[
|x_i-y_j|_2\ge D\ge2r\quad\text{for every }i,j,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and assume every start has `l_infinity` distance at least `b` from `U^c`.

Let `X_i(t)` and `Y_j(t)` be the corresponding interchange strands during `I`. Put

\[
\mathsf{Near}(r):=
\left\{\exists i,j,\ \exists 0\le t\le T:
|X_i(t)-Y_j(t)|_2\le r\right\}.
\]

There is an absolute constant `C` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P(\mathsf{Near}(r)\mid\mathscr F)
\le
C MN\frac rD
+(M+N)\psi(T,b),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where one may take

\[
\boxed{
\psi(T,b)=6\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{b^2}{8T},\frac b2\right\}\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Let `Exit` be the event that at least one of the `M+N` tagged strands leaves `U` by time `T`. For a single deterministic or `F`-measurable start at `l_infinity` buffer distance at least `b`, the untouched incident clocks make the tagged strand an ordinary rate-one-per-edge continuous-time simple random walk until exit. For each coordinate, the displacement is the difference of two independent rate-one Poisson processes. The standard exponential martingale and Doob inequality, with parameter `theta=min{b/(4T),1}`, give

\[
\mathbf P\left(\sup_{0\le t\le T}|X_t^{(a)}-X_0^{(a)}|\ge b\mid\mathscr F\right)
\le
2\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{b^2}{8T},\frac b2\right\}\right].
\]

Union over the three coordinates yields (4.3), and union over all `M+N` strands gives

\[
\mathbf P(\mathsf{Exit}\mid\mathscr F)
\le(M+N)\psi(T,b).
\tag{4.4}
\]

On `Exit^c`, if `Near(r)` occurs then at least one cross pair `(i,j)` approaches within `r` before either member exits `U`. For each fixed pair, all starting data are `F`-measurable and the clocks in the relevant region/time slab are still untouched. Thus `C27-FRESH-PAIR-01` applies to the approach-before-exit event and gives probability at most `C r/D`. Union over the `MN` cross pairs and add (4.4), proving (4.2). `square`

### Scale corollary

Fix `beta>0` and `c_0>0`. Suppose for a scale `L` that

\[
D\ge c_0L,
\qquad
r=L^\alpha,
\qquad
b\ge r,
\qquad
M,N\le L^\delta,
\qquad
T\le\beta,
\tag{4.5}
\]

with `0<alpha<1` and `delta>=0`. For all sufficiently large `L` such that `r>=4 beta`, (4.2)--(4.3) imply

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P(\mathsf{Near}(r)\mid\mathscr F)
\le
C_{c_0}L^{-(1-\alpha-2\delta)}
+12L^\delta e^{-L^\alpha/2}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Indeed, `MN r/D <= c_0^{-1}L^{2delta+alpha-1}`, while `r>=4 beta` gives

\[
\min\{r^2/(8T),r/2\}=r/2.
\]

Therefore any genuinely fresh block exploration whose cross-family budget obeys

\[
\boxed{\alpha+2\delta<1}
\tag{4.7}
\]

has a positive polynomial pair-leakage exponent

\[
\boxed{\gamma=1-\alpha-2\delta>0.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

For example, at the previously useful mesoscopic radius `r=L^{1/3}`, an exploration budget `M,N<=L^{1/8}` gives

\[
\gamma=1-\frac13-\frac14=\frac5{12}.
\]

This `5/12` is **not** a new value of the frozen metric (2.3); it is a conditional physical-decoupling exponent for a different auxiliary event under an explicit nonanticipative freshness and sparse-budget hypothesis.

### Why this is useful, and why it does not close the block construction

The theorem identifies a concrete resource that the tree proofs get for free and Z3 does not: the number of simultaneously relevant history pieces must remain sparse. If an attempted exploration exposes order `L^2` anchors at fixed beta, then `delta` is far too large and the pairwise union bound is useless. A viable Z3 renormalization must therefore compress the cyclic history to a sparse skeleton before asking for fresh-pair separation.

There is a second incompatibility with a naive neighboring-model renormalization. A single predetermined chronological tagged particle cannot normally cross a scale-`L` block in one fixed beta-period: the same martingale bound gives

\[
\mathbf P\left(\sup_{0\le t\le\beta}\|X_t-X_0\|_\infty\ge L/4\right)
\le
6\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{L^2}{128\beta},\frac L8\right\}\right].
\tag{4.9}
\]

For `L>=16 beta` this is at most `6e^{-L/8}`. Thus a standard oriented-percolation block event that asks **one fresh chronological strand** to cross a macroscopic block at fixed beta has probability tending to zero, not one. The block event must exploit the cyclic-time reuse/many-anchor structure while preserving enough nonanticipative freshness; treating the chronological path itself as the permutation cycle would be the wrong object.

## 5. B30 — the exact polynomial multiscale gluing step

The previous runs had an explicit low-density percolation gate but not a clean scale-to-scale theorem showing exactly what a physical block decomposition must provide. The next abstract lemma supplies that gluing step with constants.

### NEW-L106 — quadratic two-child gluing with polynomial leakage

Fix a scale factor `s>1` and scales

\[
L_j=s^jL_0.
\]

Let `B_j(z)` be bad events at scale `j` and write

\[
q_j:=\sup_z\mathbf P(B_j(z)).
\]

Assume that for every scale-`j+1` parent block there is a leakage event `E_j(z)` such that:

1. **Leakage:**
   \[
   \sup_z\mathbf P(E_j(z))\le aL_j^{-\epsilon}
   \tag{5.1}
   \]
   for fixed `a>0`, `epsilon>0`.
2. **Deterministic two-child decomposition:** outside `E_j(z)`, occurrence of the parent bad event implies occurrence of one of at most `N` prelisted pairs of scale-`j` child bad events.
3. **Pair independence:** the two child events in every listed pair depend on disjoint Poisson clock regions, hence are independent.

Then

\[
\boxed{q_{j+1}\le aL_j^{-\epsilon}+Nq_j^2.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define

\[
D:=2as^\epsilon.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If

\[
\boxed{
q_0\le DL_0^{-\epsilon},
\qquad
L_0^\epsilon\ge4Nas^{2\epsilon},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

then for every `j>=0`,

\[
\boxed{q_j\le DL_j^{-\epsilon}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

The union bound plus pair independence gives (5.2). Assume inductively that `q_j<=D L_j^{-epsilon}`. Then

\[
q_{j+1}
\le
aL_j^{-\epsilon}+ND^2L_j^{-2\epsilon}
=
L_j^{-\epsilon}\left[a+ND^2L_j^{-\epsilon}\right].
\]

Since `L_j>=L_0`, (5.4) and `D^2=4a^2s^{2epsilon}` imply

\[
ND^2L_j^{-\epsilon}
\le ND^2L_0^{-\epsilon}
\le a.
\]

Hence

\[
q_{j+1}
\le2aL_j^{-\epsilon}
=Ds^{-\epsilon}L_j^{-\epsilon}
=D(sL_j)^{-\epsilon}
=DL_{j+1}^{-\epsilon}.
\]

This closes the induction. `square`

### Explicit child-pair count

For a concrete `9`-adic cubic subdivision, a parent contains `9^3=729` scale-`j` children. Even if one pessimistically allows every unordered child pair as a candidate,

\[
N\le\binom{729}{2}=265356.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the base-scale size condition in (5.4) may be written explicitly as

\[
\boxed{
L_0^\epsilon\ge4(265356)a\,9^{2\epsilon}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The theorem itself is complete. What is **not** proved is hypothesis 2 for a source-matching local permutation-cycle block event. That deterministic implication is now the exact missing gluing statement: one must define a nonanticipative orbit-propagating good block for which a bad parent, except on a controlled leakage event, forces two spatially separated bad children whose clock supports are disjoint.

NEW-L105 shows that if the information connecting the two child families can be compressed to `L^delta` fresh starts with `alpha+2delta<1`, then the leakage term needed in (5.1) can indeed have a positive polynomial exponent. NEW-L106 then propagates that exponent across all scales. But the sparse-skeleton/deterministic-two-child decomposition has not yet been constructed, so no physical exponent and no source-level beta_0 are claimed.
