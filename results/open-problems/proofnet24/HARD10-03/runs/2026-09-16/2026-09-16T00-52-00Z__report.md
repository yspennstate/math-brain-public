# 3. Setup: the full same-cycle contact neighborhood of one chronological strand

Write

\[
P:=\pi_\beta,
\qquad Q_s:=\pi_s,
\qquad 0\le s\le\beta.
\]

Let `C_P(0)` be the (finite or infinite) permutation cycle of `0` under `P`.

For every vertex `y in C_P(0)`, the chronological strand of the label initially at `y` is

\[
s\longmapsto Q_s y.
\]

Define the **full-slab contact neighborhood** of the root strand by

\[
\mathcal N_\beta(0)
:=
\left\{
y\in C_P(0)\setminus\{0\}:
\exists s\in[0,\beta]
\text{ with }|Q_s y-Q_s0|_1=1
\right\},
\]

and its degree

\[
D_\beta(0):=|\mathcal N_\beta(0)|.
\]

This definition ranges over **all distinct labels in the root permutation cycle at once**. It is therefore not subject to deterioration of fixed-lag constants as the lag tends to infinity.

# 4. NEW-L41 — all-lag full-slab contact degree is O(beta)

## Theorem NEW-L41 — PROVED

For every dimension `d>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E D_\beta(0)
\le
2d+4d(2d-1)\beta.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In `d=3`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E D_\beta(0)
\le 6+60\beta.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

More strongly, if `theta>=0`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E e^{\theta D_\beta(0)}
\le
\exp\Bigl(
2d\theta+
\beta\Psi_d(\theta)
\Bigr),
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
\Psi_d(\theta)
=
2d\bigl(e^{(2d-1)\theta}-1\bigr)
+
2d(2d-1)\bigl(e^{\theta}-1\bigr).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus `D_beta(0)` has an exponential upper tail on its natural `O(beta)` scale, uniformly over the length of the permutation cycle.

### Proof

Set

\[
X_s:=Q_s0.
\]

At phase `s=0`, the vertices belonging to `C_P(0)\setminus{0}` are distinct, so at most the `2d` nearest neighbors of the origin can already be adjacent to the root strand. Hence at most `2d` members of `N_beta(0)` are present initially.

Every other first contact must be created at a Poisson ring. Consider a ring at time `s`, and use the left limit `x=X_{s-}`.

There are only two ways in which this ring can create a new adjacency between the root label and another label.

### Case A: the ringing edge is incident to the root

The root jumps from `x` to a neighbor `z`.

The label swapped with the root was already at `z`, hence was already adjacent to the root immediately before the ring; it does not create a new contact. The only possible newly adjacent labels are those occupying the other neighbors of `z`. There are at most

\[
2d-1
\]

such positions. Therefore one root-incident ring creates at most `2d-1` new members of `N_beta(0)`.

At every instant there are exactly `2d` root-incident edges.

### Case B: the ringing edge does not contain the root

For the adjacency status with the root to change, a tracked label must move across the ringing edge and land at a neighbor of `x`.

Because `Z^d` is triangle-free, an edge not incident to `x` has at most one endpoint in the neighbor shell `N(x)`. Hence this ring can create at most **one** new root contact.

The relevant edges are precisely the outward edges from the `2d` neighbors of `x`, excluding the edge back to `x`. Their number is

\[
2d(2d-1).
\]

### Predictable Poisson counting

Let `W_beta` be the weighted number of rings of the two types above, assigning weight `2d-1` to a root-incident ring and weight `1` to a neighbor-shell outward ring. The sets of relevant edges are predictable from the pre-ring root location. Therefore the standard exponential-compensator identity for independent rate-one Poisson clocks gives

\[
\mathbb E e^{\theta W_\beta}
\le
\exp\left\{
\beta\left[
2d(e^{(2d-1)\theta}-1)
+2d(2d-1)(e^\theta-1)
\right]
\right\}.
\]

Pathwise,

\[
D_\beta(0)\le 2d+W_\beta.
\]

This proves (4.3), and differentiating at `theta=0` gives

\[
\mathbb E D_\beta(0)
\le
2d+
\beta\left[2d(2d-1)+2d(2d-1)\right],
\]
which is (4.1).

### Important scope

NEW-L41 counts **distinct same-cycle strands** that ever become nearest-neighbor to the root strand. It does not say that a finite permutation cycle must be generated using contacts only among its own final-cycle labels. A counterexample to that tempting shortcut is given in Section 7 below.

# 5. Immediate uniform-in-lag consequences

Suppose first that the root cycle length is `L<infinity`. Then its distinct non-root orbit labels are

\[
P0,P^20,\ldots,P^{L-1}0.
\]

If the root cycle is infinite, use all distinct positive and negative orbit labels. NEW-L41 therefore gives an all-lag replacement for the fixed-lag theorem NEW-L40:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\#\left\{
1\le r<L:
\exists s\in[0,\beta],
|Q_s(P^r0)-Q_s0|_1=1
\right\}
\le
2d+4d(2d-1)\beta.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every deterministic `R>=1`, define

\[
A_R
=
\#\left\{
1\le r\le R:
L>R,
\exists s\in[0,\beta],
|Q_s(P^r0)-Q_s0|_1=1
\right\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E A_R
\le
2d+4d(2d-1)\beta.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `J` is independent and uniform on `{1,...,R}`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L>R,
\exists s\in[0,\beta]:
|Q_s(P^J0)-Q_s0|_1=1
\right)
\le
\frac{2d+4d(2d-1)\beta}{R}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In `d=3`, choosing `R=beta^{1+delta}` gives the genuinely uniform averaged-lag estimate

\[
\mathbb P(L>R,\ H_J)
\le
C\beta^{-\delta}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This does **not** prove a bound for each deterministic lag `r`; rather, it shows that the obstruction from the constants `C_r` in NEW-L40 disappears after averaging over a long orbit block.

# 6. NEW-L42 — sparse full-slab contact graph on long orbit blocks

On the event `L>R`, consider the first `R` orbit strands

\[
0,P0,\ldots,P^{R-1}0.
\]

Define a graph `G_R` on the index set `{0,...,R-1}` by placing an edge `{i,j}` if the two chronological strands ever become nearest-neighbors at the same physical phase:

\[
\exists s\in[0,\beta]:
|Q_s(P^i0)-Q_s(P^j0)|_1=1.
\]

The archived cyclic point-shift theorem says that re-rooting at `P^i0` preserves the law, and the event `L>R` is invariant under this cycle re-rooting. Therefore each vertex degree is bounded by a shifted copy of `D_beta(0)` in expectation.

Hence:

## Theorem NEW-L42 — PROVED

For every `R>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L>R\}}|E(G_R)|
\right]
\le
R\left[d+2d(2d-1)\beta\right].
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

In `d=3`,

\[
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L>R\}}|E(G_R)|]
\le
R(3+30\beta).
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

On `L>R`,

\[
2|E(G_R)|
=
\sum_{i=0}^{R-1}\deg_{G_R}(i)
\le
\sum_{i=0}^{R-1}D_\beta(P^i0).
\]

Cycle point-shift stationarity and invariance of `L>R` give

\[
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L>R\}}D_\beta(P^i0)]
=
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L>R\}}D_\beta(0)]
\le
\mathbb E D_\beta(0).
\]

Insert NEW-L41 and divide by two.

### Large pairwise-noncontacting subsets

By the elementary Turán/Caro--Wei bound,

\[
\alpha(G_R)
\ge
\frac{R^2}{R+2|E(G_R)|},
\tag{6.3}
\]

where `alpha` is the independence number.

Let

\[
c_d:=d+2d(2d-1).
\]

Since

\[
d+2d(2d-1)\beta
\le c_d(1+\beta),
\]
Markov's inequality implies that for every `A>1`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
L>R,
|E(G_R)|>A c_d R(1+\beta)
\right)
\le \frac1A.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L>R,
\alpha(G_R)
\ge
\frac{R}{1+2A c_d(1+\beta)}
\right)
\ge
\mathbb P(L>R)-\frac1A.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Thus whenever `R>>beta` and the event `L>R` itself has non-negligible probability, a long orbit block contains, with correspondingly non-negligible probability, on the order of `R/beta` chronological strands that are pairwise **never nearest-neighbor throughout the entire slab**.

This is a genuinely all-lag sparse-defect statement. The subset supplied by (6.5) is selected from the realized contact graph, so it is anticipative; it cannot yet be treated as an a priori independent family of simple random walks.

# 7. COUNTEREXAMPLE-L43 — a finite final cycle need not have any internal strand contact

A tempting route after NEW-L41 would be to claim that the chronological contact graph induced by the labels of every final permutation cycle must be connected. That statement is false, even on a bipartite nearest-neighbor graph that embeds in `Z^3`.

Consider the six-cycle `C6` with vertices `0,1,...,5`, and apply the following deterministic sequence of nearest-neighbor transpositions, in chronological order:

\[
(2,3),
(5,0),
(4,5),
(5,0),
(1,2),
(3,4),
(0,1),
(4,5).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Tracking labels from the identity, the final label-to-position permutation is

\[
\boxed{
P=(3,2,5,0,1,4),
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

so the final cycle decomposition contains

\[
(0\ 3)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(1\ 2\ 5\ 4).
\]

However, the labels `0` and `3` are **never nearest-neighbors at any time**, including the initial state and every interval between successive transpositions. Since positions are constant between rings, checking the states immediately after each transposition certifies this for the whole time interval.

Thus the final two-cycle `{0,3}` has **zero internal chronological contact edges**.

The graph `C6` embeds as the nearest-neighbor rectangle

\[
(0,0,0),(1,0,0),(2,0,0),(2,1,0),(1,1,0),(0,1,0)
\]

inside `Z^3`. By requiring the listed eight local rings in the given order and no other ring on edges incident to these six vertices during a small time window, the same mediator phenomenon has positive probability in the actual `Z^3` Poisson construction.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Sparse same-cycle strand contact alone cannot force an infinite final cycle.}
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Outside-cycle mediator strands are a real obstruction, not merely a proof artifact.

# 8. What NEW-L41--42 accomplish relative to NEW-L40

The previous checkpoint proved, for every fixed lag `r` in `d=3`,

\[
\mathbb P(H_{r,\beta})\le C_r\beta^{-1/2},
\]

but the constants were uncontrolled as `r->infinity`.

NEW-L41 gives a complementary statement with no lag constant at all:

* over **all distinct labels in the same permutation cycle**, only `O(beta)` distinct labels can ever contact the root strand on average during one entire slab;
* a uniformly chosen lag from a block of length `R` has contact probability `O(beta/R)` on `L>R`;
* an `R`-strand orbit block has only `O(R beta)` contact edges on average, and for `R>>beta` contains an `Omega(R/beta)` pairwise-noncontacting subset with high probability conditional on a non-negligible long-cycle event.

This is the first archived bound in this track that is simultaneously **full-slab and uniform over arbitrarily many orbit lags**.

It does not yet yield the desired summable fixed-lag estimate `C beta^{-1/2} r^{-3/2}`. Instead it replaces that pointwise target by a sparse-graph formulation.

# 9. New sharp obstruction and next attack

The C6 certificate shows exactly why an internal-contact sparse graph is not enough: final-cycle connectivity can be mediated entirely through labels belonging to other final cycles.

The next target is therefore a **mediator charging theorem** rather than another fixed-lag heat-kernel estimate.

A useful formulation is:

1. expose a long orbit block of the root cycle;
2. use NEW-L42 to extract many strands with no mutual same-phase contacts;
3. for every endpoint shift among those strands that is nevertheless realized in the final permutation, trace the first mediator strand outside the selected family that transports the permutation information;
4. prove that a single mediator cluster cannot service too many pairwise-noncontacting selected strands without paying either a repeated-return cost or a large local-ring budget;
5. iterate the charge through mediator depth.

The C6 example says that depth one is possible and must be allowed. What is needed is a quantitative statement that a large number of internally isolated strands forces a rapidly expanding mediator cluster. If that cluster expansion can be combined with transience/return bounds in `Z^3`, it would give a route around the lack of complete past-avoidance that restricts Elboim--Sly to `d>=5`.

A parallel analytic route is to choose a **nonanticipative random subset** of orbit indices before revealing the relevant fresh phase window, rather than the anticipative independent set from Turán's theorem. NEW-L42 says the expected number of contact conflicts in such a sparse sample should be small when the sampling density is `O(1/beta)`. The load-bearing next lemma would be to arrange the sampling at an exploration stopping time so that the chosen labels retain a usable independent-walk coupling until their first mutual contact.
