# 3. NEW-L49 — exact partition-lattice extraction of the one-cycle sector

Let `P` be any random permutation of a finite or countable set, and let `A` be a fixed finite subset. Write `Pi(A)` for the lattice of set partitions of `A`, ordered by refinement:

\[
\pi\le\sigma
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every block of }\pi\text{ is contained in a block of }\sigma.
\]

Let `hat0` be the discrete partition and `hat1={A}` the one-block partition. For `pi in Pi(A)` define

\[
E_\pi
:=
\{P(B)=B\text{ for every block }B\in\pi\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Whenever `A` is invariant under `P`, let `rho_A(P)` be the partition of `A` into cycles of the restricted permutation `P|_A`.

The Möbius function from a partition `sigma` to the top element is

\[
\mu(\sigma,\hat1)
=(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}(|\sigma|-1)!.
\tag{3.2}
\]

## Theorem NEW-L49 — PROVED

For every base partition `pi_0 in Pi(A)`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sigma\ge\pi_0}
\mu(\sigma,\hat1)\,
\mathbb P(E_\sigma)
=
\mathbb P\bigl(
P(A)=A,\ \pi_0\vee\rho_A(P)=\hat1
\bigr).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, taking `pi_0=hat0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(P|_A\text{ is one cycle})
=
\sum_{\sigma\in\Pi(A)}
(-1)^{|\sigma|-1}(|\sigma|-1)!\,
\mathbb P(E_\sigma).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If `0 in A`, the left-hand side is exactly

\[
\mathbb P(C_P(0)=A).
\]

### Proof

Fix a deterministic realization of `P`. If `E_sigma` occurs, then every block of `sigma` is invariant, hence their union `A` is invariant. On `{P(A)=A}`, the event `E_sigma` is equivalent to

\[
\rho_A(P)\le\sigma,
\]

because a block is invariant exactly when it is a union of cycles of `P|_A`.

Therefore, realization by realization,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\sigma\ge\pi_0}
\mu(\sigma,\hat1)\,\mathbf1_{E_\sigma}
&=
\mathbf1_{\{P(A)=A\}}
\sum_{\sigma\ge\pi_0\vee\rho_A(P)}
\mu(\sigma,\hat1).
\end{aligned}
\]

The defining Möbius identity on the partition lattice gives

\[
\sum_{\sigma:\,\tau\le\sigma\le\hat1}
\mu(\sigma,\hat1)
=\mathbf1_{\{\tau=\hat1\}}.
\]

Substitute `tau=pi_0 vee rho_A(P)` and take expectations. This proves (3.3). For `pi_0=hat0`, `hat0 vee rho=rho`, so the surviving event is precisely that `rho_A(P)=hat1`, i.e. `P|_A` is one cycle. QED.

### Checks against low orders

For `|A|=2`, (3.4) reads

\[
\mathbb P(A\text{ is a two-cycle})
=
\mathbb P(P(A)=A)
-
\mathbb P(P\text{ fixes both singletons}),
\]

which is exactly the unsigned version behind NEW-L48.

For `|A|=3`, writing `R(\pi)=P(E_\pi)`,

\[
\boxed{
P(A\text{ is a three-cycle})
=R(\hat1)
-\sum_{\substack{\pi:\,|\pi|=2}}R(\pi)
+2R(\hat0).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the desired connected sector is literally the top Möbius coefficient of the block-invariance probabilities. This makes the partition-lattice program proposed in the preceding checkpoint exact rather than heuristic.

# 4. NEW-L50 — a rigorous two-sided merge-defect bound

The next question is whether the differences between neighboring partition levels carry spatially summable crossing factors.

Suppose `pi` is obtained from `pi'` by splitting one block `U=B cup C` of `pi'` into two nonempty disjoint blocks `B,C`, leaving every other block unchanged. Then

\[
E_\pi\subseteq E_{\pi'}.
\]

On `E_{pi'}\setminus E_pi`, the union `U` is invariant but neither `B` nor `C` is invariant. Define

\[
N_{B\to C}
=\#\{b\in B:P(b)\in C\},
\qquad
N_{C\to B}
=\#\{c\in C:P(c)\in B\}.
\]

Because `P` is a bijection and `P(U)=U`,

\[
N_{B\to C}=N_{C\to B}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hence the merge defect forces a **two-sided crossing**:

\[
E_{\pi'}\setminus E_\pi
\subseteq
\{N_{B\to C}\ge1,\ N_{C\to B}\ge1\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `Q_beta^(2)((b,c),(y,z))` be the exact ordered two-label interchange transition probability

\[
\mathbb P(P(b)=y,P(c)=z).
\]

## Theorem NEW-L50 — PROVED

For every such adjacent pair of partitions,

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\mathbb P(E_{\pi'})-\mathbb P(E_\pi)
\le
\sum_{\substack{b\in B,\ c\in C\\y\in C,\ z\in B}}
Q_\beta^{(2)}((b,c),(y,z)).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

A weaker one-particle bound is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_{\pi'})-\mathbb P(E_\pi)
\le
\sum_{b\in B,\ y\in C}p_\beta(b,y),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and likewise with `B,C` reversed.

### Proof

The left inequality is the event inclusion `E_pi subset E_pi'`. By (4.2),

\[
\mathbf1_{E_{\pi'}\setminus E_\pi}
\le N_{B\to C}N_{C\to B}.
\]

Taking expectations and expanding the product gives exactly the right side of (4.3). Similarly,

\[
\mathbf1_{E_{\pi'}\setminus E_\pi}
\le N_{B\to C}
\]

gives (4.4) using the one-particle marginal identity

\[
\mathbb P(P(b)=y)=p_\beta(b,y).
\]

QED.

### Spatial consequence in `d=3`

Using the archived fixed-two-tag Gaussian bound, if

\[
\operatorname{dist}(B,C)\ge R,
\]

then for `beta>=1`

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_{\pi'})-\mathbb P(E_\pi)
\le
C |B|^2|C|^2\,\beta^{-3}
\exp(-cR^2/\beta),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

for absolute constants at tag number two. This is not claimed optimal for large blocks; the useful structural point is that invariance of the merged block forces two opposite tagged crossings, not merely one.

For singleton blocks `B={u}, C={v}` and `A={u,v}`, the two-tag event in (4.3) is exactly the two-cycle event. NEW-L48 remains sharper in that case because its determinant/negative-association cancellation produces `2 q_beta p_beta(u,v)`.

# 5. NEW-L51 — connected block sectors have a closed (unicyclic) crossing witness

NEW-L49 isolates the event

\[
\mathcal C_{\pi_0}
:=
\{P(A)=A,\ \pi_0\vee\rho_A(P)=\hat1\}
\tag{5.1}
\]

for an arbitrary base partition

\[
\pi_0=\{B_1,\dots,B_k\},\qquad k\ge2.
\]

On this event construct the directed **block-transition multigraph** `G` on vertices `1,...,k`: for every `x in B_i` with `P(x) in B_j`, `i!=j`, put a directed edge `i->j`, retaining the actual mapping `x->P(x)` as its label.

Two deterministic facts are load-bearing.

1. `G` is connected as an undirected graph exactly on the connected sector (5.1). If its block vertices split into two graph components, the union of blocks in either component is invariant and `pi_0 vee rho_A(P)` cannot be `hat1`; conversely every cross-block transition lies in a permutation cycle joining its endpoint blocks, so a connected `G` forces the join to be `hat1`.
2. `G` is balanced:
   \[
   \deg_G^+(i)=\deg_G^-(i)
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
   for every block. Indeed both numbers equal `|B_i|` minus the number of mappings from `B_i` back into `B_i`.

Because `G` is connected and balanced and has `k>=2` vertices, every vertex has positive outdegree. Therefore `G` contains at least `k` directed cross-block transitions. In particular one may choose `k-1` transitions whose underlying undirected edges contain a spanning tree and one further distinct cross transition. This is a connected **unicyclic-size witness**: `k` mapping constraints for `k` blocks, not merely the `k-1` constraints of a tree.

Let `W_k(pi_0)` denote the collection of ordered pairs

\[
(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
=((x_1,\dots,x_k),(y_1,\dots,y_k))
\]

such that

* all `x_i` are distinct and all `y_i` are distinct;
* each `x_i` and `y_i` lie in different blocks of `pi_0`;
* the undirected graph on block indices formed by the `k` block pairs containing `(x_i,y_i)` is connected.

## Theorem NEW-L51 — PROVED

For the interchange process,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal C_{\pi_0})
\le
\sum_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in W_k(\pi_0)}
Q_\beta^{(k)}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Every realization in `C_{pi_0}` has a connected balanced block-transition graph. Choose any spanning-tree set of `k-1` actual cross transitions and any further actual cross transition not among them; one exists because the total number of cross transitions is at least `k`. These are `k` distinct domain labels and, since `P` is a permutation, `k` distinct targets. Thus they produce a witness in `W_k(pi_0)`. The event that a fixed witness occurs is exactly the ordered `k`-tag transition event

\[
\{P(x_i)=y_i\text{ for }1\le i\le k\},
\]

of probability `Q_beta^(k)(x,y)`. Union bound over witnesses proves (5.3). QED.

### Gaussian fixed-`k` corollary

Using the archived fixed-`k` tagged heat kernel and rough-isometry comparison of configuration distance with product displacement,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal C_{\pi_0})
\le
C_k\beta^{-3k/2}
\sum_{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\in W_k(\pi_0)}
\exp\!\left[
-c_k\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k|x_i-y_i|^2}{\beta}
\right].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

No uniformity in `k` is claimed.

If the blocks are pairwise separated by at least `R`, then every witness displacement is at least `R`, so a crude finite-block bound is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal C_{\pi_0})
\le
C_k |A|^{2k}\beta^{-3k/2}
\exp(-c_k kR^2/\beta).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Why the extra crossing matters

A plain spanning-tree witness would have only `k-1` tagged transitions. Spatially summing `k-1` Gaussian factors over `k-1` free block locations loses the final `beta^{-3/2}` return factor needed by the Tóth target. The permutation-bijection balance (5.2) forces at least one additional crossing. Thus the correct connected diagram is naturally **unicyclic/Eulerian**, not merely a tree.

This matches the old twisted-trace sufficient condition: a closed `k`-step one-particle loop has total weight `p_{k beta}(0,0)`, while a tree has no residual return factor. NEW-L51 identifies, at the exact permutation level, why any successful connected cluster expansion should preserve a closed loop.

NEW-L50 proves that each single partition merge has a genuine two-sided crossing defect. NEW-L51 proves that a fully connected `k`-block sector must carry `k`, rather than `k-1`, distinct cross-block mappings.

This does **not** yet yield a uniform-in-cycle-length estimate. The remaining difficulty is quantitative: the current tagged heat-kernel constants `C_k,c_k` are only controlled for fixed `k`, and a naive union bound over all `k`-edge witnesses has rapidly growing combinatorics. No claim is made that (5.4) can be summed over arbitrary `k`.

# 7. A tempting shortcut that remains unavailable

One might hope to multiply the single-merge estimates (4.3) along a partition-lattice chain and obtain a product of crossing kernels. That is not justified: successive merge defects are strongly dependent, and Möbius coefficients alternate. NEW-L49 is exact, but it does not turn neighboring increments into independent factors.

Likewise, the already archived C4 counterexample shows that constant-one termwise comparison with independent-walker twisted traces is false. NEW-L51 should therefore be viewed as identifying the correct **closed witness geometry**, not as proving the missing uniform trace comparison.

# 9. Next attack

The next technical target is now more precise than “find a tree bound.” A tree is missing one return constraint. NEW-L51 shows that the permutation structure automatically supplies an extra edge and hence a closed/unicyclic witness.

The most useful next derivation is therefore a **uniform connected unicyclic estimate**. Two concrete versions are:

1. Prove, for the specific `k`-crossing witness sums arising from balanced block transitions, a comparison with independent walkers whose multiplicative loss is bounded independently of `k` (or at least summable after the Möbius coefficients are included). This is a narrower target than arbitrary `k`-particle heat-kernel domination.
2. Develop a Duhamel/defect expansion around the independent cyclic loop and show that adjacency defects renormalize the closed loop by a finite factor in `d=3`, using transience of relative motion. The old sufficient bound
   \[
   P(L_\beta(0)=\ell)\le A p_{\ell\beta}(0,0)
   \]
   with one finite `A` independent of `ell` would immediately solve the full conjecture.

At the partition level, a useful intermediate goal is a higher mixed-difference inequality for `R(pi)=P(E_pi)` whose Möbius transform is bounded directly by a sum of closed connected diagrams, rather than multiplying first-order merge bounds.
