# 3. NEW-L54 — exact finite-cycle arrow empirical identity

Let

\[
P=\pi_\beta,
\qquad
L=L_\beta(0),
\]

with `1/L=0` on `{L=infinity}`. Let `T` be the point-shift from archived `HARD10-03-L3`:

\[
T\omega=\theta_{P(0)}\omega.
\]

Then `T` preserves the interchange law, `T^j\omega=\theta_{P^j(0)}\omega`, and `{L=m}` is `T`-invariant for every finite `m`.

### Lemma 3.1 — cycle-average/Palm identity

For every nonnegative measurable `f`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac1L
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}f(T^j\omega)
\right]
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}f(\omega)
\right].
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Decompose by `L=m`. Since `{L=m}` is `T`-invariant and `T` preserves the law,

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L=m\}}\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}f\circ T^j
\right]
=
\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L=m\}}f]
=
\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L=m\}}f].
\]

Sum over `m`. QED.

Write the finite-cycle mapping increments

\[
\Delta_j=P^{j+1}(0)-P^j(0).
\]

Taking `f(omega)=phi(P(0))` in (3.1), for every nonnegative `phi:Z^3->R_+`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac1L
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\phi(\Delta_j)
\right]
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\phi(P(0))
\right]
\le
\mathbb E\phi(X_\beta),
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `X_beta` is the rate-one-per-edge simple random walk at time `beta`, because the one-particle marginal `P(0)` is exactly `X_beta`.

For example,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac1L
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}|\Delta_j|_2^2
\right]
\le 6\beta.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus a hypothetical all-finite phase cannot hide arbitrary one-step mapping geometry: the empirical arrow law along a size-biased finite cycle is, in this precise averaged sense, dominated by the ordinary one-particle displacement law.

# 4. NEW-L55 — coarse occupation and long-arrow sparsity of any surviving finite cycle

This section converts (3.1) into a geometric statement that is uniform in the unknown cycle length.

## 4.1 Randomly shifted diffusive grid

Fix an integer block side `ell>=1`. Let

\[
U\in\{0,1,\dots,\ell-1\}^3
\]

be uniform and independent of the Poisson environment. Partition `Z^3` into cubes

\[
Q_z^U
=U+\ell z+\{0,\dots,\ell-1\}^3,
\qquad z\in\mathbb Z^3.
\]

For a finite root cycle `C_0`, let

* `K_{ell,U}(C_0)` be the number of cubes met by its vertex set;
* `X_{ell,U}(C_0)` be the number of cycle mapping arrows whose endpoints lie in different cubes.

For a fixed finite cycle, collapse each cycle vertex to its cube. If at least two cubes are visited, the resulting directed multigraph is connected and balanced, so every visited cube has positive outdegree. Hence

\[
K_{\ell,U}(C_0)\le1+X_{\ell,U}(C_0)
\tag{4.1}
\]

pathwise (also true when only one cube is visited).

To average a **fixed absolute grid** correctly under point-shift, enlarge the state by `U` and use the skew point-shift

\[
\widetilde T(\omega,U)
=
\bigl(T\omega,\ U-P(0)\pmod\ell\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `U` is independent uniform modulo `ell`, `\widetilde T` preserves the joint law. Its `j`th iterate re-roots the same absolute grid at `P^j(0)`. Therefore (3.1) on the enlarged state gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\omega,U}\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{X_{\ell,U}(C_0)}L
\right]
=
\mathbb P_{\omega,U}\left(
L<\infty,\ 0\text{ and }P(0)\text{ lie in different }U\text{-cubes}
\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For deterministic `x,y`, a uniform grid shift separates them with probability at most

\[
\sum_{i=1}^3\min\left\{1,\frac{|x_i-y_i|}{\ell}\right\}
\le\frac{|x-y|_1}{\ell}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `P(0)` has the `X_beta` law and

\[
\mathbb E|X_\beta|_1
\le\sqrt{3\,\mathbb E|X_\beta|_2^2}
=\sqrt{18\beta},
\]

(4.1)--(4.4) imply:

### Theorem NEW-L55A — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega,U}\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{K_{\ell,U}(C_0)}L
\right]
\le
\mathbb E[1/L]
+
\frac{\sqrt{18\beta}}{\ell}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Using archived `HARD10-03-L4`, for `ell=A sqrt(beta)` (rounded to an integer),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega,U}\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{K_{\ell,U}(C_0)}L
\right]
\le
C\frac{1+\log\beta}{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{C}{A}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently, for every `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\omega,U}\left(
L<\infty,\ \frac{K_{\ell,U}(C_0)}L\ge\delta
\right)
\le
\frac1\delta\left[
C\frac{1+\log\beta}{\sqrt\beta}+\frac{C}{A}
\right].
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus if `A=A(beta)->infinity`, any finite root cycle outside an event of vanishing probability uses a sublinear number of `A sqrt(beta)`-blocks relative to its cycle length.

## 4.2 Long permutation arrows

For `R>0`, define

\[
J_R(C_0)
=
\#\left\{0\le j<L:
|\Delta_j|_2>R\sqrt\beta
\right\}.
\]

Equation (3.2) gives the exact upper bound

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{J_R(C_0)}L
\right]
\le
q_\beta(R)
:=
\mathbb P(|X_\beta|_2>R\sqrt\beta).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

A coordinate Chernoff bound from

\[
\mathbb E e^{\theta X_\beta^{(i)}}
=
\exp\{2\beta(\cosh\theta-1)\}
\]

gives absolute `c,C>0` with

\[
q_\beta(R)
\le
C\exp\left[-c\min\{R^2,R\sqrt\beta\}\right].
\tag{4.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L<\infty,\ \frac{J_R(C_0)}L\ge\eta
\right)
\le
\frac{q_\beta(R)}\eta.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

There is also a deterministic coarse-polymer consequence. If `ell>R sqrt(beta)`, delete the `J_R` long arrows from the cyclic word. Every remaining arrow changes each cube coordinate by at most one, so each remaining path segment visits a connected set of cubes in the nearest-neighbor `l_infinity` block graph. Hence the occupied block support can be covered by at most

\[
\boxed{
\max\{1,J_R(C_0)\}
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

connected block animals after removing the long-arrow defects.

Equations (4.7), (4.10), and (4.11) sharpen the geometric description of the unresolved regime: if a large-time finite root cycle survives, then after choosing a slowly growing `A`, it is a coarse polymer with few occupied super-diffusive blocks per orbit vertex and a vanishing fraction of arrows longer than a chosen multiple of `sqrt(beta)`. This does **not** imply the cycle is infinite; it identifies the geometry that any counterexample to the conjecture would need to sustain.

# 5. NEW-L56 — exact finite-torus cycle-count drift

Let `T_n^3=(Z/nZ)^3`, `n>=3`, and let `P_t^{(n)}` be interchange on the torus with rate one on each nearest-neighbor unoriented edge. Write

\[
C_n(t)=\#\{\text{cycles of }P_t^{(n)}\},
\qquad
\rho_n(t)=\frac1{n^3}\mathbb E C_n(t).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, by choosing a uniform vertex and then using transitivity,

\[
\rho_n(t)=\mathbb E\left[\frac1{L_{n,t}(0)}\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
a_n(t)
=
\mathbb P\bigl(0\text{ and }e_1\text{ belong to the same cycle of }P_t^{(n)}\bigr).
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem NEW-L56 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\rho_n'(t)=3\,[2a_n(t)-1].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence for every interval `[a,a+T]`, `T>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\frac1T\int_a^{a+T}a_n(t)\,dt-\frac12
\right|
\le\frac1{6T}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

**Proof.** A transposition across an edge whose endpoints lie in two different permutation cycles merges those cycles and changes `C_n` by `-1`. If the endpoints lie in the same cycle, the transposition splits that cycle into two and changes `C_n` by `+1`. Applying the continuous-time generator,

\[
\frac d{dt}\mathbb E C_n(t)
=
\sum_{e\in E(T_n^3)}
\bigl[2\mathbb P(e\text{ has same-cycle endpoints})-1\bigr].
\]

The torus is edge-transitive and has `3n^3` unoriented edges, proving (5.4). Integrating and using `0<=rho_n<=1` proves (5.5). QED.

This identity requires no monotonicity of `rho_n`; indeed the point is that the signed split-minus-merge drift integrates to a bounded quantity.

# 6. NEW-L57 — averaged diverging cycle scales on Z^3

The finite-torus drift can be combined with the archived fixed-orbit-lag heat-kernel theorem and then transferred back to `Z^3`. The result is substantially stronger than merely saying that each fixed short cycle has small probability.

Fix an integer `M>=2`. On the torus define

\[
b_{n,M}(t)
=
\mathbb P\bigl(
0,e_1\text{ lie in the same cycle and }L_{n,t}(0)\le M
\bigr).
\]

If this event occurs, there is some `1<=r<=M-1` for which

\[
(P_t^{(n)})^r(0)=e_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
b_{n,M}(t)
\le
\sum_{r=1}^{M-1}
\mathbb P\bigl((P_t^{(n)})^r(0)=e_1\bigr).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Also

\[
a_n(t)
\le
\mathbb P(L_{n,t}(0)>M)+b_{n,M}(t),
\]
so, using (5.5) on `[beta,2beta]`,

\[
\frac1\beta\int_\beta^{2\beta}
\mathbb P(L_{n,t}(0)>M)\,dt
\ge
\frac12-\frac1{6\beta}
-
\frac1\beta\int_\beta^{2\beta}b_{n,M}(t)\,dt.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For each fixed `M` and fixed `t`, the graphical coupling used in archived `HARD10-03-L5` gives, as `n->infinity`,

\[
\mathbb P(L_{n,t}(0)>M)
\to
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_t(0)>M),
\tag{6.3}
\]

and for every fixed `r`,

\[
\mathbb P((P_t^{(n)})^r(0)=e_1)
\to
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(P_t^r(0)=e_1).
\tag{6.4}
\]

All integrands are bounded by one, so dominated convergence applies on the compact time interval. The archived fixed-`r` endpoint theorem gives

\[
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(P_t^r(0)=e_1)
\le C_r t^{-3/2},
\qquad t\ge1.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Combining (6.1)--(6.5), with

\[
C_M:=C\sum_{r=1}^{M-1}C_r<\infty
\]

for each fixed `M`, yields:

### Theorem NEW-L57A — PROVED

For every fixed integer `M>=2` and all `beta>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1\beta\int_\beta^{2\beta}
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_t(0)>M)\,dt
\ge
\frac12-\frac1{6\beta}-C_M\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

No monotonicity in `t` is used.

### Diagonal consequence: a diverging scale

Although the constants `C_M` are not controlled uniformly in `M`, every `C_M` is finite. Choose an increasing sequence `beta_m` so large that for every `beta>=beta_m`,

\[
\frac1{6\beta}+C_m\beta^{-3/2}\le\frac1m.
\]

Define

\[
M(\beta)=\max\{m:\beta\ge\beta_m\}.
\]

Then `M(beta)->infinity` and (6.6) gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac1\beta\int_\beta^{2\beta}
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_t(0)>M(\beta))\,dt
\ge
\frac12-o(1).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

In particular, there exists `t_beta in [beta,2beta]` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}
\bigl(L_{t_\beta}(0)>M(\beta)\bigr)
\ge
\frac12-o(1).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

More quantitatively, if

\[
G_\beta
=
\left\{
t\in[\beta,2\beta]:
\mathbb P(L_t(0)>M(\beta))\ge\frac14
\right\},
\]

then from `0<=P<=1` and (6.7),

\[
\boxed{
|G_\beta|
\ge
\left(\frac13-o(1)\right)\beta.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus, on a positive proportion of times in each large dyadic-scale interval, the root cycle has probability bounded away from zero of exceeding a scale that itself diverges with `beta`.

### Why NEW-L57 does not solve the target

The order of limits is still insufficient. The threshold `M(beta)` is finite for each fixed `beta`, and (6.7) does not give a positive lower bound on

\[
\mathbb P(L_t(0)=\infty)
\]

at any prescribed time. Nor may (6.8) be propagated to later times by monotonicity, because monotonicity in the time parameter is unavailable. The theorem is therefore a genuine long-cycle result, not an infinite-cycle theorem.

Its significance is that it removes a different obstruction: **large-time cycle lengths cannot remain tight even in `d=3`, and this can be proved directly in infinite volume after a finite-torus drift calculation.** The unresolved step is to upgrade a slowly diverging cycle scale to infinity at each fixed sufficiently large time.

# 7. Relation to the 2026 drift criterion and the new geometric bottleneck

Klippel's 2026 criterion shows why the constant `1/2` in (5.5) is structurally important. In a sparse graph satisfying

\[
e_G(S)\lesssim |S|
\]

for every small loop support, a configuration consisting only of small loops cannot have enough same-loop edges to sustain the split rate required by the drift identity when the global edge density is sufficiently high. This forces macroscopic loops.

For cubic tori, however, a box `S` can have

\[
e_G(S)\approx3|S|,
\]

so the deterministic small-set sparsity step fails badly. NEW-L57 shows that the process nevertheless produces diverging cycle scales for many large times. The remaining `d=3` obstruction can therefore be reformulated sharply:

> control the probability that a very long but finite interchange cycle has a spatial support dense enough in nearest-neighbor lattice edges to supply roughly half of all edge insertions as same-cycle splits.

The recent strong-Rayleigh exact-set/heat-content estimates (`NEW-L44`--`NEW-L47`) and the random-cut connected-block estimates (`NEW-L52`--`NEW-L53A`) are natural tools for this stochastic replacement of deterministic small-set sparsity.

# 9. Next attack

The most promising next step is now a **stochastic small-set sparsity theorem for the actual root-cycle support**, rather than another fixed-lag estimate.

On a finite torus let

\[
I(C)=\#\{\{x,y\}\in E:\ x,y\in C\}
\]

be the number of underlying lattice edges induced by a permutation cycle support. The drift identity says that configurations with no infinite/macroscopic cycles can survive only if long finite cycles contribute enough induced lattice edges that the same-cycle edge probability is close to `1/2` on average.

A sufficient next theorem would be a uniform estimate of the form

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{I(C_0)}{|C_0|}\,;
M<|C_0|<\infty
\right]
\le\frac32-\delta+o_M(1)
\]

for some `delta>0` at sufficiently large `beta`, or an analogous high-probability statement. Such a bound would contradict the finite-torus drift requirement after short cycles are removed and would be capable of promoting NEW-L57 from diverging finite scales to genuine infinite cycles.

Two existing mechanisms should be combined toward this:

1. `NEW-L47` strongly penalizes any **prescribed** spatially heat-retentive cycle set;
2. `NEW-L53A` exponentially suppresses a cycle support that fragments into many blocks with low one-period heat communication.

The remaining family consists of long, strongly heat-connected coarse lattice animals. The next run should attack their entropy versus the heat-content penalty, while retaining the full cycle event and not replacing it by disconnected invariant-set returns.
