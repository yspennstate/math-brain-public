# 3. B30 SUCCESS: NEW-L100, replica-averaged binary-cut gluing

The previous theorem used one fair binary block coloring and paid for its random cut through a single exponential moment. The new idea is to use `m` independent fair binary cuts simultaneously and glue them **before** taking `m to infinity`. Hölder decouples the common interchange realization, while averaging many cuts drives the random cut heat to its exact mean `H/4`. This removes the previous worst-cut `Lambda` loss without changing the event.

## Theorem NEW-L100 — PROVED

For every deterministic block family above, every `v>=0`, and `n=k-1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P(\mathcal V(\mathcal B))
\le
\exp\left\{
-\frac{vn}{4}
+
\frac{e^v-1}{4}\,\mathcal H_\beta(\mathcal B)
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

No independence assumption between different particle trajectories is used.

### Proof

Fix an integer `m>=1`. Independently of the interchange realization choose `m` iid fair red/blue colorings

\[
\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m
\]

of the `k` blocks.

Fix a permutation realization in `V(B)`. Its root cycle visits every block, so its undirected block-transition graph is connected. Choose a spanning tree `T` by a deterministic tie-breaking rule depending only on the permutation realization.

For replica `r`, let `Z_r` be the number of bichromatic edges of `T`. Conditional on the permutation, `T` is fixed. On a tree, fixing the root color and all edge XORs parametrizes vertex colorings bijectively. Therefore the `n=k-1` edge XORs are iid fair bits and

\[
Z_r\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The `m` variables `Z_r` are iid conditional on the permutation because the colorings are iid.

For coloring `r`, let `R_r` be the union of red blocks and `D_r` the union of blue blocks, and define the **global** red-to-blue assignment count

\[
N_r
=
\#\{x\in R_r:\pi_\beta(x)\in D_r\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Every bichromatic edge of `T` is represented by at least one root-cycle permutation arrow whose endpoints lie in its two endpoint blocks. Hence the root cycle has at least `Z_r` color-changing arrows. On a directed cycle, red-to-blue and blue-to-red changes occur equally often, so the number of root-cycle red-to-blue arrows is at least `Z_r/2`. Since `N_r` counts all global red-to-blue arrows,

\[
N_r\ge Z_r/2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Fix `v>=0` and set

\[
a=v/m.
\]

Using (3.2)--(3.4), pathwise on `V(B)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf E_{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m}
\exp\left(a\sum_{r=1}^m N_r\right)
&\ge
\mathbf E
\exp\left(\frac a2\sum_{r=1}^m Z_r\right)\\
&=
\left[
\frac{1+e^{v/(2m)}}2
\right]^{mn}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Averaging over the interchange realization gives

\[
\mathbf P(\mathcal V)
\left[
\frac{1+e^{v/(2m)}}2
\right]^{mn}
\le
\mathbf E_{\pi,\xi}
\exp\left(a\sum_{r=1}^m N_r\right).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Now fix the entire coloring vector. Hölder with `m` equal exponents gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf E_\pi
\exp\left(a\sum_{r=1}^mN_r\right)
&=
\mathbf E_\pi
\prod_{r=1}^m e^{aN_r}\\
&\le
\prod_{r=1}^m
\left(
\mathbf E_\pi e^{amN_r}
\right)^{1/m}\\
&=
\prod_{r=1}^m
\left(
\mathbf E_\pi e^{vN_r}
\right)^{1/m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
\]

For each **fixed** binary cut `r`, color exactly the particles initially in `R_r` red. Their target-site occupation field at time `beta` is symmetric exclusion from a deterministic initial set. The hypothesis-checked strong-Rayleigh preservation / negative-association theorem used in `NEW-L98` therefore applies to the blue target sites. Thus

\[
\mathbf E_\pi e^{vN_r}
\le
\exp\{(e^v-1)\mu_r\},
\tag{3.8}
\]

where

\[
\mu_r
=
\sum_{i\ \mathrm{red},\,j\ \mathrm{blue}}h_{ij}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Substitution into (3.7) yields, for every fixed coloring vector,

\[
\mathbf E_\pi
\exp\left(a\sum_rN_r\right)
\le
\exp\left\{
\frac{e^v-1}{m}\sum_{r=1}^m\mu_r
\right\}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The `mu_r` are iid bounded random variables under the iid colorings. Moreover their mean is exact:

\[
\mathbf E_\xi\mu
=
\sum_{i\ne j}h_{ij}\,\mathbf P(i\text{ red},j\text{ blue})
=
\frac14\mathcal H_\beta(\mathcal B).
\tag{3.11}
\]

Averaging (3.10) over the colorings therefore gives

\[
\mathbf E_{\pi,\xi}
\exp\left(a\sum_rN_r\right)
\le
\left[
\mathbf E_\xi
\exp\left(
\frac{e^v-1}{m}\mu
\right)
\right]^m.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Combine (3.6) and (3.12). Since `mu` is bounded for the fixed finite block family,

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\left[
\mathbf E
\exp\left(
\frac{e^v-1}{m}\mu
\right)
\right]^m
=
\exp\left\{
(e^v-1)\mathbf E\mu
\right\}
=
\exp\left\{
\frac{e^v-1}{4}\mathcal H_\beta
\right\},
\tag{3.13}
\]

while

\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\left[
\frac{1+e^{v/(2m)}}2
\right]^{mn}
=
\exp(vn/4).
\tag{3.14}
\]

Taking `m to infinity` in (3.6) proves (3.1). `square`

## 5. B6 — deform from tree/loop solved regimes: exact failures, no imported theorem

Current primary sources checked:

- Hammond, `arXiv:1202.1322`, sharp phase transition on infinite **regular trees of high degree**;
- Hammond, `arXiv:1202.1319`, infinite cycles on rooted trees with at least two offspring at high parameter;
- Björnberg–Ueltschi, `arXiv:1608.08473`, random-loop/interchange critical parameter on **regular trees of large degree**.

Two load-bearing hypotheses fail on `Z^3`.

### 5.1 Branching geometry cannot be embedded at unit lattice scale

A rooted tree in which every vertex has at least two children has at least `2^R` vertices at generation `R`. A radius-`R` ball of `Z^3` has at most `(2R+1)^3` vertices. Hence no injective graph embedding preserving unit graph edges can realize such an infinite min-two-offspring tree inside `Z^3`.

Thus the descendant-subtree multiplication central to the tree proofs cannot simply be declared inside the cubic lattice.

### 5.2 Adding the missing lattice edges is not monotone for cycles

Even if one starts from a spanning tree, there is no allowed monotone comparison saying that adding transposition clocks can only help long cycles. A transposition whose endpoints lie in different permutation cycles merges them; a transposition whose endpoints lie in the same cycle splits that cycle. Thus the same operation can increase or decrease cycle lengths depending on the current permutation.

This is precisely why a tree theorem cannot be transferred by graph inclusion.

**B6 outcome:** direct deformation rejected. It identifies hypotheses that a valid port would have to replace: unique-path/descendant factorization and a monotone comparison. No source-level claim is imported.
