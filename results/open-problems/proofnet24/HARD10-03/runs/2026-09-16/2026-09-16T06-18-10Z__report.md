# 3. NEW-L61 — exact divisor/Möbius calculus for cyclic power returns

Let

\[
P=\pi_\beta,
\qquad
L=L_\beta(0)\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\},
\]

and define

\[
a_k(\beta):=\mathbb P(P^k0=0),
\qquad
p_\ell(\beta):=\mathbb P(L=\ell).
\]

For a deterministic permutation,

\[
P^k0=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L<\infty\text{ and }L\mid k.
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem NEW-L61 — PROVED

For every `k>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
a_k(\beta)=\sum_{\ell\mid k}p_\ell(\beta).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Arithmetic Möbius inversion gives, for every `ell>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
p_\ell(\beta)
=\sum_{d\mid\ell}\mu(\ell/d)\,a_d(\beta),}
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `mu` is the number-theoretic Möbius function.

For `|z|<1`, absolute convergence gives the Lambert-series identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\ge1}a_k(\beta)z^k
=
\sum_{\ell\ge1}p_\ell(\beta)
\frac{z^\ell}{1-z^\ell}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Finally,

\[
\frac1R\sum_{k=1}^R a_k(\beta)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac{\lfloor R/L\rfloor}{R}
\right]
\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}
\mathbb E[1/L].
\tag{3.4}
\]

The Cesàro limit in (3.4) is consistent with the spectral-atom interpretation archived in `HARD10-03-L4`; the genuinely new point here is the exact primitive-return Möbius inversion (3.2).

### Why this matters

The finite-cycle mass at length `ell` is a **primitive cyclic return**, not the raw return probability of `P^ell`. In particular, for a prime `q`,

\[
p_q=a_q-a_1.
\]

Thus any operator approach based on powers of the same slab must preserve divisor cancellations. Bounding each power-return probability separately loses precisely the cancellation that distinguishes a primitive `ell`-cycle from shorter cycles repeated many times. This is another exact reason that naive repeated-slab random-walk comparison is too coarse.

# 4. NEW-L62 — the annealed finite-cycle projection/autocorrelation kernel

Define the nonnegative kernel

\[
G_\beta(x)
:=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac{\mathbf1_{\{x\in C_0\}}}{L}
\right],
\qquad x\in\mathbb Z^3,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `C_0` is the vertex set of the permutation cycle through the origin.

Let

\[
q_\beta:=\mathbb P(L<\infty),
\qquad
c_\beta:=\mathbb E[1/L].
\]

Immediately,

\[
\sum_xG_\beta(x)=q_\beta,
\qquad
G_\beta(0)=c_\beta.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The archived point-shift theorem gives a much stronger structure.

## 4.1 Autocorrelation identity

On a finite root cycle, re-rooting successively at all cycle vertices preserves the law. Applying the archived cycle-Palm identity to the function

\[
\omega\mapsto \mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac{\mathbf1_{\{x\in C_0\}}}{L}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
G_\beta(x)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac1{L^2}
\#\{(u,v)\in C_0^2:v-u=x\}
\right].}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently, conditional on a finite cycle, choose two vertices independently and uniformly from that cycle; after averaging over the stationary root, `G_beta/q_beta` is their displacement law (when `q_beta>0`).

## 4.2 Fourier/form-factor representation

For `theta in [-pi,pi]^3`, put

\[
\widehat G_\beta(\theta)
:=
\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^3}G_\beta(x)e^{i\theta\cdot x}.
\]

By (4.3),

\[
\boxed{
\widehat G_\beta(\theta)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\left|
\frac1L\sum_{u\in C_0}e^{i\theta\cdot u}
\right|^2
\right]
\ge0.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence `G_beta` is symmetric and positive definite. Moreover,

\[
\widehat G_\beta(0)=q_\beta,
\qquad
(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}
\widehat G_\beta(\theta)\,d\theta
=c_\beta.
\tag{4.5}
\]

This identifies the old inverse-cycle-length quantity as the mean Fourier mass of a new positive form factor whose zero-frequency value is exactly the finite-cycle probability. The original conjecture at fixed `beta` is therefore equivalent to

\[
\widehat G_\beta(0)<1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The old bound `c_beta=O((log beta)/sqrt(beta))` controls the **average** of this form factor, but by itself does not control the spike at `theta=0`. The theorem below quantifies how narrow that spike must become if all cycles remain finite.

# 5. NEW-L63 — Fejér/van-der-Corput inequality for a finite permutation cycle

Fix a finite cycle

\[
x_j=P^j0,
\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z,
\]

and `theta`. Write

\[
z_j=e^{i\theta\cdot x_j},
\qquad
A_C(\theta)=\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}z_j,
\]

and for `r>=1`,

\[
B_{r,C}(\theta)
:=
\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
 z_{j+r}\overline{z_j}
=
\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
 e^{i\theta\cdot(x_{j+r}-x_j)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every integer `H>=1`, the global mean is also the mean of every cyclic `H`-block average:

\[
A_C
=
\frac1L\sum_j
\frac1H\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}z_{j+h}.
\]

Jensen and expansion of the square give the exact Fejér bound

\[
\boxed{
|A_C(\theta)|^2
\le
\frac1H
+
\frac{2}{H^2}
\sum_{r=1}^{H-1}(H-r)\Re B_{r,C}(\theta).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This remains valid when `H>L`; the indices are cyclic and repetitions are automatically handled.

By the point-shift/cycle-average identity,

\[
\mathbb E[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}B_{r,C}(\theta)]
=
\mathbb E[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
 e^{i\theta\cdot P^r0}].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let

\[
\Phi_r(\theta)
:=\mathbb E e^{i\theta\cdot P^r0}.
\]

The archived finite-window driver coupling `HARD10-03-L2` says in `d=3`

\[
d_{TV}(P^r0,X(r\beta))
\le C\frac r{\sqrt\beta},
\tag{5.4}
\]

where `X` is rate-one-to-each-neighbor continuous-time SRW. Hence, with

\[
\lambda(\theta)
=2\sum_{j=1}^3(1-\cos\theta_j),
\]

\[
\boxed{
|\Phi_r(\theta)-e^{-r\beta\lambda(\theta)}|
\le C\frac r{\sqrt\beta}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let

\[
p_\infty(\beta)=\mathbb P(L=\infty)=1-q_\beta.
\]

The infinite-cycle contribution to `Phi_r` has absolute value at most `p_infty`. Combining (5.2)--(5.5), using

\[
\sum_{r=1}^{H-1}r(H-r)=\frac{H(H^2-1)}6,
\]

gives the annealed bound

\[
\boxed{
\widehat G_\beta(\theta)
\le
p_\infty(\beta)
+
\frac1H
+C\frac H{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{2}{H^2}
\sum_{r=1}^{H-1}(H-r)e^{-r\beta\lambda(\theta)}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `theta !=0`, summing the geometric series yields

\[
\boxed{
\widehat G_\beta(\theta)
\le
p_\infty(\beta)
+
\frac1H
+C\frac H{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{2}{H\,[e^{\beta\lambda(\theta)}-1]}.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is a particle-number-free Fourier constraint. It uses only orbit windows of length `H` before the first synchronized defect and does not use any uncontrolled fixed-`r` tagged-particle constant.

# 6. NEW-L64 — superdiffusive pair spread forced in any all-finite large-time regime

The Fourier inequality can be converted back to a spatial statement without losing positivity.

For an integer `M>=1`, let

\[
B_\infty(M)=\{x\in\mathbb Z^3:|x|_\infty\le M\}
\]

and define

\[
S_\beta(M)
:=
\sum_{x\in B_\infty(M)}G_\beta(x)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac{|C_0\cap B_\infty(M)|}{L}
\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

By (4.3), the same quantity is the annealed probability mass that two independent uniform vertices of the finite root cycle differ by at most `M` in `l_infinity` distance.

## 6.1 Positive Fourier majorant

For an integer `N>=2`, define the one-dimensional tent

\[
w_N(m)=(1-|m|/N)_+,
\]

and in three dimensions

\[
W_N(x)=\prod_{j=1}^3w_N(x_j).
\]

Its Fourier transform is the product Fejér kernel

\[
K_N(\theta)
=
\prod_{j=1}^3
\frac1N
\left|
\sum_{h=0}^{N-1}e^{ih\theta_j}
\right|^2
\ge0,
\tag{6.2}
\]

with normalized integral one. If `N>=2M+2`, then

\[
W_N(x)\ge1/8
\qquad(x\in B_\infty(M)).
\]

Therefore

\[
S_\beta(M)
\le
8(2\pi)^{-3}
\int\widehat G_\beta(\theta)K_N(\theta)\,d\theta.
\tag{6.3}
\]

On `[-pi,pi]^3`,

\[
\lambda(\theta)\ge c|\theta|_2^2.
\]

Also `K_N<=N^3`. Splitting at `|theta|=1/N` gives the elementary estimate

\[
(2\pi)^{-3}
\int\frac{K_N(\theta)}{\lambda(\theta)}\,d\theta
\le CN^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Indeed, inside the radius-`1/N` ball the integral is at most
`CN^3 int_0^{1/N}dr = CN^2`; outside, `1/|theta|^2<=N^2` and the Fejér kernel has total mass one.

Use `(e^u-1)^{-1}<=u^{-1}` in (5.7), insert it into (6.3), and take `N=2M+2`. This gives:

### Theorem NEW-L64 — PROVED

There is an absolute `C<infinity` such that for every `beta>=1`, every integer `M>=1`, and every integer `H>=2`,

\[
\boxed{
S_\beta(M)
\le
8p_\infty(\beta)
+C\left[
\frac{1+M^2/\beta}{H}
+
\frac H{\sqrt\beta}
\right].}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Choosing an integer `H` nearest

\[
\beta^{1/4}\sqrt{1+M^2/\beta}
\]
(and enlarging `C` for the bounded small-parameter cases) yields

\[
\boxed{
S_\beta(M)
\le
8p_\infty(\beta)
+C\beta^{-1/4}
\sqrt{1+M^2/\beta}.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

## 6.2 Consequence for a hypothetical counterexample sequence

If along a sequence `beta_n->infinity` there are **no infinite cycles at the root**, so

\[
p_\infty(\beta_n)=0,
\]

then for every deterministic `M_n=o(beta_n^{3/4})`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{|C_0\cap B_\infty(M_n)|}{L}
\right]
\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

In particular, for every fixed `epsilon>0`, choosing

\[
M=\beta^{3/4-\epsilon}
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{|C_0\cap B_\infty(\beta^{3/4-\epsilon})|}{L}
\right]
\le C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon}
\quad\text{whenever }p_\infty(\beta)=0.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus an all-finite large-time phase cannot consist merely of cycles that are somewhat larger than the diffusive scale. A uniformly selected vertex of the finite root cycle must typically lie **beyond every `o(beta^{3/4})` spatial radius** from the root. Equivalently, two independent uniformly selected vertices of that cycle have superdiffusive separation in the strong averaged sense (6.7).

This is substantially stronger geometrically than the earlier deterministic-set confinement result, which showed only that the complete root cycle is unlikely to fit inside a fixed diffusive-volume block. Here, under the no-infinite-cycle hypothesis, the **fraction** of the cycle contained even in a superdiffusive `o(beta^{3/4})` neighborhood tends to zero.

The unconditional form (6.6) is also a useful sufficient-criterion template: any independent argument producing a nonvanishing lower bound on `S_beta(M)` at a scale `M=o(beta^{3/4})` would force `p_infty(beta)>0` quantitatively.

## 7. Checks, limitations, and failed shortcuts

### Checks actually performed

1. The divisor identity (3.1), Möbius inversion (3.2), autocorrelation identity (4.3), form-factor identity (4.4), and Fejér inequality (5.2) were independently checked by exact finite-state enumeration/matrix exponentiation on interchange on `C4`; an append-only checker is prepared after this mathematical report.
2. The only probabilistic approximation entering `NEW-L63/64` is archived `HARD10-03-L2`, whose error is `O(H/sqrt(beta))` uniformly for an `H`-period window. No fixed-`H` configuration-space constant is introduced.
3. The Fourier-to-space step uses a positive Fejér majorant, so there is no sign cancellation hidden in (6.3).
4. The singular factor `1/lambda(theta)` is integrable in dimension three; estimate (6.4) is exactly where `d=3` is used in this form.

### What is **not** proved

* `p_infty(beta)>0` is not established at any new finite `beta` in this run.
* `NEW-L64` is a necessary condition on any all-finite large-time regime, not a contradiction by itself. Very long finite cycles could in principle have most of their vertex mass at distances much larger than `beta^{3/4}`.
* No monotonicity in `beta` is used or inferred.
* Chronological SRW paths are never identified with permutation cycles.
* The withdrawn off-diagonal determinant/same-cycle interpretation remains withdrawn.

### Exact new bottleneck

The new sufficient criterion is now concrete. It would be enough to prove, for some fixed `epsilon>0` and all sufficiently large `beta`, a lower bound

\[
S_\beta(\beta^{3/4-\epsilon})
\ge c_0>0
\]

under the finite-cycle sector. `NEW-L64` would then force a positive infinite-cycle probability. The archived arrow-energy identity and random-cut block theorem are natural candidates for such a lower bound, but no valid derivation is presently available.

A second route is to bootstrap the `o(beta^{3/4})` escape: expose a first orbit window of length `H~beta^{1/2-epsilon}`, re-root at a typical remote cycle vertex, and seek a conditional restart estimate showing that repeated superdiffusive escapes cannot close into a finite cycle without paying a summable synchronized-contact cost. This is closer in spirit to the local-escape mechanism of Elboim–Sly, but the required post-defect conditional control remains open in `d=3`.
