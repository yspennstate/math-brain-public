# 3. NEW-L86 — mesoscopic orbit-packing frontier

The first-interaction theorem has so far mostly been used to deduce a cycle-length lower bound. There is substantially more geometric information in the same coupling: for almost `sqrt(beta)` periods, the permutation orbit is not merely nonclosed; a suitably sampled prefix is spatially self-avoiding at a polynomial mesoscopic scale.

Fix integers

\[
h\ge1,\qquad k\ge1,
\]

and a radius `R>=1`. Consider the sampled orbit points

\[
Y_j:=Z_{jh}=\pi_\beta^{jh}(0),\qquad 0\le j\le k.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal B_{h,k,R}
:=
\left\{
\exists\ 0\le i<j\le k:\ |Y_j-Y_i|_2\le 2R
\right\}.
\]

### Theorem NEW-L86 — PROVED

There is an absolute `C<infinity` such that, for `beta>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal B_{h,k,R})
\le
C\left(
\frac{kh}{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{kR^3}{(h\beta)^{3/2}}
\right),
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

whenever the displayed right side is interpreted trivially as at most one if it exceeds one.

### Proof

Apply the driver coupling (1.1) with total period horizon

\[
K=kh.
\]

Its failure probability is at most

\[
Ckh/\sqrt\beta.
\tag{3.2}
\]

On the coupling-success event,

\[
Y_j=X(jh\beta),\qquad 0\le j\le k.
\]

For a pair with lag `m=j-i>=1`, stationary independent increments of the driver give

\[
X(jh\beta)-X(ih\beta)
\stackrel d= X(mh\beta).
\]

Hence (2.2) gives

\[
\mathbb P\left(
|X(jh\beta)-X(ih\beta)|_2\le2R
\right)
\le
C R^3(mh\beta)^{-3/2}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

There are at most `k` pairs having any fixed lag `m`. A union bound, followed by summability of `sum_{m>=1}m^{-3/2}`, yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P_X(\mathcal B_{h,k,R})
&\le
C kR^3(h\beta)^{-3/2}
\sum_{m=1}^k m^{-3/2}\\
&\le
C\frac{kR^3}{(h\beta)^{3/2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Adding the coupling error (3.2) proves (3.1).

No independence of the permutation arrows is used. Independence enters only for increments of the auxiliary driver on the coupling-success event, precisely where the already proved cyclic-time construction permits it.

## 3.1 Exponent form

Let

\[
h=\lfloor\beta^a\rfloor\vee1,
\quad
k=\lfloor\beta^b\rfloor\vee1,
\quad
R=\beta^\rho,
\]

with fixed nonnegative exponents. Then (3.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal B_{h,k,R})
\le
C\left(
\beta^{a+b-1/2}
+
\beta^{b+3\rho-\frac32(a+1)}
\right).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus whenever

\[
 a+b<\frac12,
\qquad
 b+3\rho<\frac32(a+1),
\tag{3.6}
\]

the `k+1` sampled points are pairwise farther than `2 beta^rho` with probability tending to one.

This is a quantitative packing frontier for the actual permutation-cycle orbit, not for independent chronological particles.

---

## 3.2 Two useful slices of the frontier

### Consecutive-period coarse self-avoidance

Take `a=0`. For any fixed `delta>0`, choose

\[
 b=\frac12-\frac\delta2,
\qquad
 \rho=\frac13-\frac\delta2.
\]

Then

\[
 a+b-\frac12=-\frac\delta2,
\]

and

\[
 b+3\rho-\frac32=-2\delta.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
H_\beta=\left\lfloor\beta^{1/2-\delta/2}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
R_\beta=\beta^{1/3-\delta/2},
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\min_{0\le i<j\le H_\beta}
|\pi_\beta^j(0)-\pi_\beta^i(0)|_2
\le2R_\beta
\right)
\le C_\delta\beta^{-\delta/2}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus the first `beta^{1/2-o(1)}` period endpoints form, with high probability, a spatial packing at mutual scale `beta^{1/3-o(1)}`.

### Superdiffusive sparse packing

The same frontier also reaches much larger separation if the orbit is sampled more sparsely. For example, for small `epsilon>0`, take

\[
 b=\epsilon,
\qquad
 a=\frac12-\frac{7\epsilon}{6},
\qquad
 \rho=\frac34-\epsilon.
\]

Then the total horizon exponent is `a+b=1/2-epsilon/6`, and

\[
 b+3\rho-\frac32(a+1)=-\frac\epsilon4.
\]

Hence, for `epsilon<3/7`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Pack}_{\,c\beta^{3/4-\epsilon}}
(C_{\pi_\beta}(0))
\ge c\beta^\epsilon
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

with probability at least

\[
1-C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon/6}.
\]

Here `Pack_R(A)` denotes the maximum cardinality of a subset of `A` with pairwise distances greater than `R`.

This improves the earlier `NEW-L67` packing statement in a different direction: (3.8) is unconditional on `p_infinity(beta)=0`, is extracted from an explicit early orbit prefix, and gives `beta^epsilon` rather than `beta^{epsilon/2}` representatives at the same `beta^{3/4-epsilon}` separation scale (at the cost of a weaker failure exponent). It still does not by itself imply an infinite cycle at fixed `beta`.

# 4. NEW-L87 — every root cycle has a large nearest-neighbor Steiner hull with high probability

The consecutive-period packing has a deterministic geometric consequence that quantifies the size of any nearest-neighbor mediator skeleton joining the cycle support.

For a set `A subset Z^3`, define

\[
\mathfrak H(A)
:=
\inf\{|H|:\ A\subseteq H\subset\mathbb Z^3,
\ H\text{ is nearest-neighbor connected}\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

with `mathfrak H(A)=infinity` if `A` is infinite.

### Deterministic lemma

If `A` contains `q>=2` points whose mutual Euclidean distances exceed `2R`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak H(A)\ge c qR.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, balls of radius `R/3` around the `q` points are pairwise disjoint. Any nearest-neighbor connected set containing all the points has, from each marked point, a lattice path that exits its own `R/3` ball. The initial path segment contributes at least `cR` vertices inside that ball. These contributions are disjoint across the marked points, proving (4.2).

Apply this lemma on the event (3.7), with

\[
q=H_\beta+1,
\qquad
R=R_\beta.
\]

Then

\[
qR
\asymp
\beta^{1/2-\delta/2}
\beta^{1/3-\delta/2}
=
\beta^{5/6-\delta}.
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem NEW-L87 — PROVED

For every fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\mathfrak H(C_{\pi_\beta}(0))
< c_\delta\beta^{5/6-\delta}
\right)
\le
C_\delta\beta^{-\delta/2}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This theorem is unconditional: if the root cycle is infinite the hull size is infinite; if it is finite, then with high probability any nearest-neighbor connected lattice skeleton containing it must already contain at least `beta^{5/6-o(1)}` sites.

This does **not** say that the permutation-cycle support itself is nearest-neighbor connected. Earlier mediator counterexamples forbid that inference. It quantifies instead the minimal spatial mediator hull required to connect the support.

# 5. NEW-L88 — phase-wise braid separation for most of the Poisson slab

The endpoint packing has a continuous-phase analogue that is more directly aligned with the cyclic-time walk.

Write the cyclic-time walk as

\[
W(j\beta+s)=\pi_s\pi_\beta^j(0),
\qquad 0\le s<\beta.
\]

Fix the same

\[
H=\lfloor\beta^{1/2-\delta/2}\rfloor,
\qquad
R=\beta^{1/3-\delta/2}.
\]

For a configuration, define the bad-phase fraction

\[
F_{\beta,\delta}
:=
\frac1\beta
\operatorname{Leb}\left\{
0\le s<\beta:
\exists\ 0\le i<j\le H,
\ |W(j\beta+s)-W(i\beta+s)|_2\le2R
\right\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem NEW-L88 — PROVED

For every fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
F_{\beta,\delta}>\beta^{-\delta}
\right)
\le
C_\delta\beta^{-\delta/2}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Couple `W` to the driver through `(H+1)beta`. By the first-interaction estimate, coupling failure costs at most

\[
C(H+1)/\sqrt\beta
\le C\beta^{-\delta/2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

On coupling success,

\[
W(j\beta+s)=X(j\beta+s)
\]

for all relevant `j,s`. For a fixed phase `s` and pair lag `m=j-i`, the difference

\[
X(j\beta+s)-X(i\beta+s)
\]

has the law of `X(m beta)`. Hence by (2.2),

\[
\mathbb P_X\left(
|X(j\beta+s)-X(i\beta+s)|_2\le2R
\right)
\le C R^3(m\beta)^{-3/2},
\tag{5.4}
\]

uniformly in `s`. Summing over pair lags gives

\[
\sup_s
\mathbb P_X(\text{some close pair at phase }s)
\le
C H R^3\beta^{-3/2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

With the chosen exponents,

\[
H R^3\beta^{-3/2}
\asymp
\beta^{-2\delta}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Fubini therefore gives, under the driver,

\[
\mathbb E_X F_{\beta,\delta}
\le C\beta^{-2\delta}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Markov's inequality yields

\[
\mathbb P_X(F_{\beta,\delta}>\beta^{-\delta})
\le C\beta^{-\delta}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Adding (5.3) proves (5.2).

Thus, with probability tending to one, for all but a `beta^{-delta}` fraction of the cyclic phase coordinate, the first `beta^{1/2-delta/2}` strands are simultaneously separated from one another by at least `beta^{1/3-delta/2}`.

This is stronger than the old statement “there is no synchronized interaction before `H beta`”: it upgrades microscopic separation (`>1`) to a mesoscopic polynomial separation for almost every phase cross-section.

# 6. What these results buy, and a killed shortcut

The newest previous checkpoint isolated a remaining branch in which an exact finite support has a near-diffusive logarithmic spanning skeleton. NEW-L86--88 sharpen the geometry of what such a support must accommodate:

1. almost `sqrt(beta)` consecutive orbit vertices form a `beta^{1/3-o(1)}` packing;
2. sparse subsampling yields polynomially many representatives at the superdiffusive `beta^{3/4-o(1)}` scale;
3. any nearest-neighbor mediator hull of the support has size at least `beta^{5/6-o(1)}`;
4. the equal-phase braid of the first almost-`sqrt(beta)` periods is mesoscopically separated for almost every phase.

These are properties of the **actual cyclic orbit** obtained before the first same-phase defect; they do not identify the complete permutation cycle with a chronological SRW trajectory.

A tempting shortcut is to combine NEW-L87 directly with the `R_{beta,n}`-connectivity branch of NEW-L85 and infer a stronger cycle-length lower bound. This does not close anything. If a finite `n`-point support is connected by edges of length at most

\[
R_{\beta,n}\asymp\sqrt{\beta\log n}+\log n,
\]

then replacing a spanning-tree edge by a nearest-neighbor path only shows

\[
\mathfrak H(A)\le CnR_{\beta,n}.
\]

Together with NEW-L87 this gives, for polynomial `n`, only roughly

\[
n\gtrsim
\frac{\beta^{1/3-o(1)}}{\sqrt{\log\beta}},
\]

which is weaker than the already archived first-interaction length bound

\[
L_\beta(0)>\beta^{1/2-\varepsilon}
\]

with high probability. This route is therefore recorded as a failed strengthening, not presented as progress toward closure.

The useful new information is instead the **phase-wise separated braid** (NEW-L88). It offers a more concrete target for the missing local-escape/restart lemma: at most phases there are many simultaneously well-separated strand locations, hence many disjoint spatial neighborhoods. The outstanding issue is adaptivity/freshness: those neighborhoods are selected from the cyclic walk itself, so one cannot simply declare their surrounding clocks independent after conditioning.

# 9. Next attack

The highest-value next micro-target is now a **fresh-neighborhood extraction lemma from NEW-L88**.

For most phases `s`, there are `H=beta^{1/2-o(1)}` strand locations separated by `R=beta^{1/3-o(1)}`. One should expose only the clocks actually needed to determine the strand locations and then prove that a positive fraction of smaller balls, say radius `R^theta` for a fixed `theta in (0,1)`, retain enough unrevealed edge-time mass to be conditionally comparable to fresh exclusion/SRW evolution. If that can be done nonanticipatively, the archived conditional random-cut and heat-leakage theorems could charge a future finite closure across many simultaneously separated neighborhoods.

A second route is a multiscale phase decomposition: repeat NEW-L88 at a geometric sequence of spatial radii and show that any finite return must create a synchronized defect at one of those scales. The driver estimates are summable in the period lag (`m^{-3/2}`), so the missing point is not an unconditional pair count; it is preserving that summability after the first defect cluster.

No claim of a universal proof is made until that conditional restart step is closed.
