## 2. Source statement, Poisson convention, and fresh literature status

The original Tóth paper is bibliographically verified as B. Tóth, *Improved lower bound on the thermodynamic pressure of the spin 1/2 Heisenberg ferromagnet*, Lett. Math. Phys. 28 (1993), 75–84. The accessible original landing page does not expose an internal conjecture number/page, so that internal locator remains `SOURCE_LOCATOR_PARTIAL` rather than invented.

The exact later source formulation is Ioffe–Tóth, *Split-and-Merge in Stationary Random Stirring on Lattice Torus*, J. Stat. Phys. 180 (2020), §1.6, Conjecture 1(ii). Their process starts from the identity, on the original time scale with **unit stirring rate per unoriented nearest-neighbor edge**. For `d>=3` they conjecture a finite positive `beta_c(d)` such that for `t>beta_c`, every fixed ordered macroscopic cycle index has positive liminf probability to exceed a sufficiently small positive density. Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10955-020-02487-2 .

A fresh 16 September 2026 search found no `d=3` proof. Elboim–Sly, arXiv:2211.17023, prove the large-`beta` theorem for `d>=5`, not `d=3`: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17023 . El Alami–Pete–Timár, arXiv:2512.18435, prove indistinguishability statements for infinite interchange cycles conditional on their existence; this is not an existence theorem in `d=3`: https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18435 .

### Hammond gate

Hammond, arXiv:1202.1322, works on rooted regular trees of high offspring degree. His proof explicitly uses the unique root-to-vertex path, descendant subtrees, a unique bottleneck edge/bar, and untouched bars below that bottleneck. These are tree-topological hypotheses, not properties of `Z^3`. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.1322 . No monotonicity or bottleneck theorem is transferred here.

### Björnberg–Ueltschi gate

Björnberg–Ueltschi, arXiv:1608.08473 / Ann. Appl. Probab. 28 (2018), study regular trees of large degree; interchange is a special case, but the theorem is a tree theorem. Their method is a generation recursion obtained by conditioning on the local link configuration around the root and exploiting the decomposition into child subtrees. Their related open-access regular-tree paper makes the load-bearing step explicit: loop counts factor over child subtrees because the underlying graph is a tree, and the recursion is built from the root events `A_1,A_2`. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08473 and https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10955-018-2154-2 . There is no such child-subtree factorization on `Z^3`; none is assumed below.

# 3. NEW-L96 — large-deviation random cuts beat the constant-probability 1/4 ceiling

Put

\[
n=k-1.
\]

Conditional on a realization in `V(B)`, select any spanning tree of the root-cycle block support. Independently color each block red/blue fairly. Let `Z` be the number of bichromatic spanning-tree edges. Exactly as in archived `NEW-L53A`,

\[
Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2).
\]

Let `N_xi` be the number of red-to-blue permutation arrows for this cut. Balance of a permutation cycle gives equality of red-to-blue and blue-to-red arrows, hence

\[
N_\xi\ge Z/2.
\]

Fix any integer `m` with `ceil(n/2)<=m<=n`, and put

\[
s=\left\lceil\frac m2\right\rceil,
\qquad
p_{n,m}=\mathbb P(\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2)\ge m).
\]

On `V(B)`,

\[
\mathbb P_\xi(N_\xi\ge s\mid\pi_\beta)\ge p_{n,m}.
\]

Therefore, pathwise,

\[
\mathbf1_{\mathcal V(\mathcal B)}
\le
p_{n,m}^{-1}
\mathbb E_\xi\mathbf1_{\{N_\xi\ge s\}}.
\]

Take interchange expectation. For a fixed cut, the red-particle occupancy process is symmetric exclusion started from a deterministic set. The already hypothesis-checked strong-Rayleigh/negative-association Chernoff estimate used in `NEW-L53A` gives

\[
\mathbb P(N_\xi\ge s)
\le
\left(\frac{e\mu_\xi}{s}\right)^s,
\]

where `mu_xi<=Lambda_beta(B)`. Hence:

## Theorem NEW-L96 — PROVED

For every `n=k-1>=1` and every integer `m` with `ceil(n/2)<=m<=n`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathcal V(\mathcal B))
\le
p_{n,m}^{-1}
\left(\frac{e\Lambda_\beta(\mathcal B)}{\lceil m/2\rceil}\right)^{\lceil m/2\rceil}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the same one-color exclusion input as `NEW-L53A`, but it does **not** require the good-cut probability to be bounded below. The exponentially small cut probability is retained and paid.

### Why this does not contradict C27's directed-cycle counterexample

C27 proved that for a directed simple `k`-cycle the probability of getting more than `(1/4+delta)k` red-to-blue crossings tends to zero. NEW-L96 agrees: our chosen cut is exponentially rare. The improvement comes from the fact that, under sufficiently small cross heat, the strong-Rayleigh Chernoff cost of realizing that many crossings is even smaller. The net exponent improves after subtracting the cut large-deviation cost.

# 5. Percolation block attempt with explicit dependence and constants

## 5.1 A local chronological leakage event is 2-dependent and superpolynomially good

Fix a physical-time interval `J` of duration `S<=beta`. For an integer geometric scale `L>=4`, index coarse blocks by `z in Z^3` and set

\[
c_z=Lz,
\qquad
C_z=B_\infty(c_z,\lfloor L/4\rfloor),
\qquad
U_z=B_\infty(c_z,L).
\]

Let `Leak_z(J)` be the event that **for some** starting site `y in C_z`, the chronological tagged particle driven by clocks in `J` exits `U_z` before the end of `J`.

This event is measurable using only clocks in `J` on edges incident to `U_z`. If `||z-w||_infty>=3`, the corresponding incident-edge sets are disjoint (for `L>=4`), so the family is 2-dependent on the coarse lattice.

For one coordinate of the rate-one-per-direction tagged walk,

\[
X_t-X_0=N_+(t)-N_-(t),
\]

with independent rate-one Poisson processes. For `0<theta<=1`,

\[
\cosh\theta-1\le\theta^2.
\]

Doob's inequality for

\[
\exp\{\theta(X_t-X_0)-2t(\cosh\theta-1)\}
\]

gives, for `a>0`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{t\le S}|X_t-X_0|\ge a\right)
\le
2\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{a^2}{8S},\frac a2\right\}\right].
\]

Union over three coordinates gives factor six. The core-buffer distance is at least

\[
b_L=L-\lfloor L/4\rfloor-1\ge L/2.
\]

Also `|C_z|<=L^3`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\operatorname{Leak}_z(J))
\le
6L^3
\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{b_L^2}{8S},\frac{b_L}{2}\right\}\right].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `L>=8 beta+4`, then `b_L>4S` and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\operatorname{Leak}_z(J))
\le6L^3e^{-L/4}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed `A>0`, if also `L>=16(A+3)^2`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\operatorname{Leak}_z(J))\le6L^{-A}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This proves that **chronological fresh-tube leakage itself is not the polynomial bottleneck**. It can be made arbitrarily high-power at fixed `beta`. The missing event is cyclic-orbit propagation / renewable freshness. No chronological path is identified with a permutation cycle here.

## 5.2 Explicit 2-dependent Peierls gate

Here is a self-contained quantitative gate showing why any genuine positive bad-block exponent would be enough once the correct local event is found.

Let `B_z` be a 2-dependent family of bad-site events on the coarse `Z^3`, meaning collections indexed by sites at pairwise `l_infinity` distance at least three are independent, and suppose

\[
\sup_z\mathbb P(B_z)\le q.
\]

Call a site good when `B_z` fails. Use nearest-neighbor connectivity for good sites and star (`l_infinity`-nearest-neighbor) connectivity for boundaries. The star graph has degree

\[
\Delta_*=3^3-1=26.
\]

If the good cluster of the origin is finite while the origin is good, its external vertex boundary is a finite star-connected set of bad sites separating the origin from infinity. (The standard lattice-boundary lemma follows by taking the outer plaquette boundary of the union of unit cubes around the finite cluster; adjacent outer plaquettes map to star-neighboring exterior sites.)

For any fixed star-connected boundary set `S` of size `n`, color lattice sites by their three coordinate residues mod `3`. There are `27` color classes, so one class contains at least `n/27` sites, all pairwise at `l_infinity` distance at least three. Hence

\[
\mathbb P(S\text{ all bad})\le q^{n/27}.
\]

A star-connected `n`-set containing a prescribed root has at most `26^{2(n-1)}` possibilities: choose a canonical spanning tree and encode its depth-first traversal, a star-walk of length `2(n-1)`. A separating boundary intersects the positive coordinate ray at a site among its first `n` positions, because it also intersects the negative ray and is star-connected. Thus the number of candidate size-`n` boundaries is at most

\[
n\,26^{2n}.
\]

Put

\[
a=26^2q^{1/27}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb P(0\text{ good but in a finite good cluster})
\le
\sum_{n\ge1}na^n
=\frac{a}{(1-a)^2}.
\]

If

\[
\boxed{q\le q_0:=5408^{-27},}
\tag{5.4}
\]

then `a<=1/8` and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(0\text{ belongs to an infinite good cluster})
\ge1-q-\frac8{49}>0.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The exact checker verifies the constants `26^2=676`, `8*676=5408`, and positivity of the lower bound at `q=q_0`.

Therefore, if a **genuine orbit-propagating** 2-dependent bad-block event satisfies

\[
\mathbb P(B_z(L))\le C L^{-\epsilon}
\]

with any `epsilon>0`, then it crosses the explicit Peierls threshold once

\[
\boxed{
L\ge (C\,5408^{27})^{1/\epsilon}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

At that point the good blocks percolate with positive probability.

### Why this is still not the Tóth theorem

Neither `Bad_cut` from §4 nor the chronological `Leak_z` event from §5.1 has the missing **propagation semantics**:

- `Bad_cut` is a final-cycle containment/visitation event for a prescribed deterministic family. Its complement does not say that a cyclic exploration crosses a physical block in a reusable way.
- `Leak_z^c` says chronological tags stay in a fresh buffer. It does not say the cyclic-time orbit enters a chosen neighboring core, nor does it renew the complement-time clocks consumed in determining `pi_beta`.

Thus (5.5) is an explicit renormalization gate, not a hidden solution. The source-level missing lemma can now be stated precisely:

> Construct a local, orbit-propagating good-block event, measurable from a uniformly bounded coarse neighborhood of space-time blocks, whose failures have finite dependence and probability `O(L^{-epsilon})` for some `epsilon>0`, and prove that an infinite nearest-neighbor path of good blocks yields a non-closing cyclic-time orbit without reusing conditioned clocks.

The existing phase-window theorem `NEW-L93` supplies the local chronological leakage part; it does not yet supply this propagation/renewal part.
