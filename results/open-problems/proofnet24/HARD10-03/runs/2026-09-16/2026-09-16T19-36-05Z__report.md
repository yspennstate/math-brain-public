## 4. B18 — rewrite two-tag freshness as a relative-coordinate chain

The diagonal obstruction is naturally a two-particle question, not a fixed-center one. In a genuinely fresh interchange slab, take two distinct tagged labels and write their positions as `X_t,Y_t`. Put

\[
Z_t:=Y_t-X_t\in\mathcal R:=\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}.
\]

The process `Z` is a symmetric continuous-time Markov chain. Its generator can be read directly from the edge clocks:

- if `|z|_1>1`, then for every coordinate unit vector `e`, the transition `z -> z+e` has rate `2`; total jump rate is `12`;
- if `|z|_1=1`, the transition through `0` is absent, each of the five remaining nearest-neighbor relative moves has rate `2`, and the shared clock on the edge joining the two tags swaps their positions, producing `z -> -z` at rate `1`; total jump rate is `11`.

Thus `Z` is the reversible weighted walk on `Z^3\{0}` with conductance `2` on the retained nearest-neighbor edges and three extra conductance-`1` edges joining `e_i` to `-e_i`. It is a finite local modification of a uniformly elliptic Z^3 walk.

### Lemma 4.1 — relative Green bound

There is an absolute constant `C<infinity` such that the continuous-time Green kernel of `Z` obeys

\[
G_{\mathcal R}(z,w)
:=\int_0^\infty \mathbf P_z(Z_t=w)\,dt
\le \frac{C}{1+d_{\mathcal R}(z,w)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently, for

\[
A_r:=\{w\in\mathcal R:|w|_2\le r\},\qquad r\ge1,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{z\in\mathcal R}
\mathbf E_z\int_0^\infty 1_{\{Z_t\in A_r\}}dt
\le Cr^2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

The weighted relative graph has degrees/rates bounded above and below by absolute constants and differs from the ordinary cubic lattice only in a fixed neighborhood of the missing origin. Its graph distance and Euclidean distance are comparable. Volume doubling is therefore the usual cubic `R^3` estimate up to an absolute factor. A scale-invariant Poincaré inequality is also stable here: for balls outside the fixed modified neighborhood it is the standard lattice inequality; on large balls crossing that neighborhood, any lattice path which would use the removed origin can be rerouted through one of finitely many fixed detours of bounded length, changing the Dirichlet energy by only an absolute factor; the finitely many small-radius cases are absorbed into the constant.

Delmotte’s 1999 reversible-graph theorem then gives the Gaussian upper heat-kernel estimate for this uniformly elliptic bounded-degree graph. Uniformization (or adding a bounded holding probability) passes between its discrete-time and the present continuous-time version without changing the scale. Hence

\[
\mathbf P_z(Z_t=w)
\le C(t+1)^{-3/2}
\exp\left[-c\frac{d_{\mathcal R}(z,w)^2}{t+1}\right].
\]

Integrating in `t` gives (4.1). Reference: Thierry Delmotte, *Parabolic Harnack inequality and estimates of Markov chains on graphs*, Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 15 (1999), 181--232, DOI `10.4171/RMI/254`.

To obtain (4.2), sum (4.1) over `w in A_r`. If `z` lies within distance `O(r)` of `A_r`, shell counting gives

\[
\sum_{s\le Cr}\frac{Cs^2}{1+s}=O(r^2).
\]

If `z` is farther than a constant multiple of `r`, then every `w in A_r` is at distance comparable to `|z|`, so the sum is at most `Cr^3/|z|=O(r^2)`. This proves (4.2). `square`

### Lemma 4.2 — geometric tails for visits and occupation

Let `V_r` be the total number of embedded jump-chain visits of `Z` to `A_r`, and let

\[
O_r:=\int_0^\infty1_{\{Z_t\in A_r\}}dt.
\]

There is an absolute `C` such that for every integer `q>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_z\mathbf P_z(V_r>Cqr^2)
+\sup_z\mathbf P_z(O_r>Cqr^2)
\le C2^{-q}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

The total rate of `Z` is between `11` and `12`, so (4.2) implies `sup_z E_z V_r <= C_0r^2` as well as `sup_z E_z O_r<=C_1r^2`. Put `M=ceil(2C_0r^2)`. Markov gives `sup_z P_z(V_r>=M)<=1/2`. Apply the strong Markov property successively at the stopping times of the `M`-th, `2M`-th, ... visits to `A_r`; each additional block of `M` visits has conditional probability at most `1/2`. This gives the first tail.

For occupation, put `M'=2C_1r^2` and stop whenever accumulated occupation increases by another `M'`. At each such stopping time the future process starts from some state of `R`, and the uniform expected remaining occupation is at most `C_1r^2`; Markov again makes the probability of another `M'` units at most `1/2`. Iteration gives the second tail. `square`

## 5. NEW-L107 — previsible diagonal phase-sparsity theorem

Let `I=[s,s+S]` be a physical-time slab. Let `F` be a history such that all Poisson clocks used in `I` are still fresh conditionally on `F`, and let

\[
x_1,\ldots,x_Q
\]

be distinct `F`-measurable starting positions/labels chosen before the slab is exposed. Let

\[
I_1,\ldots,I_m\subset I
\]

be deterministic pairwise disjoint half-open intervals, each of length at least `1`. Write `X_i(t)` for the corresponding tagged interchange strands during the slab. Define

\[
J_{ij}(r)
:=\#\left\{\ell:\exists t\in I_\ell,\ |X_i(t)-X_j(t)|_2\le r\right\}
\]

and

\[
J_{\rm any}(r)
:=\#\left\{\ell:\exists i<j,\exists t\in I_\ell,\ |X_i(t)-X_j(t)|_2\le r\right\}.
\]

### Theorem `NEW-L107`

There is an absolute constant `C` such that for every `r>=1`, every integer `q>=1`, and every finite `Q`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P\left(
J_{\rm any}(r)>CQ^2 q r^2
\mid\mathscr F
\right)
\le CQ^2 2^{-q}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

No independence among the `Q` tagged strands is asserted or used.

There is also a local-fresh version. Suppose only the clocks on every edge incident to a region `U` are fresh during `I`, all starting points lie at `ell_infinity` distance at least `b` from `U^c`, and `S<=beta`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf P\left(
J_{\rm any}(r)>CQ^2 q r^2
\mid\mathscr F
\right)
\le CQ^2 2^{-q}
+6Q\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{b^2}{8S},\frac b2\right\}\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Fix a pair `(i,j)`. Conditional on `F`, before any exit from a fresh region their relative coordinate is exactly the chain `Z` of Section 4. Let `A_r={|z|_2<=r}`. For one sample path of `Z`, classify every interval `I_l` counted by `J_ij(r)`.

If `I_l` contains a jump whose pre-jump or post-jump relative state is in `A_r`, charge that interval to such a jump. Since the intervals are disjoint, and every embedded visit contributes to at most one entering and one leaving jump, the number of intervals of this type is at most `2V_r+1`.

Otherwise `I_l` intersects `A_r` but contains no jump entering or leaving `A_r`. Then the relative path must remain in `A_r` throughout `I_l`; because `|I_l|>=1`, it contributes at least one unit to `O_r`. Since the intervals are disjoint, the number of intervals of this second type is at most `O_r`.

Therefore, pathwise,

\[
J_{ij}(r)\le2V_r+O_r+1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Lemma 4.2 now gives

\[
\mathbf P(J_{ij}(r)>Cqr^2\mid\mathscr F)\le C2^{-q}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Union-bound (5.4) over the at most `Q^2/2` unordered pairs. On the event that all pair bounds hold,

\[
J_{\rm any}(r)
\le\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}(r)
\le CQ^2qr^2.
\]

This proves (5.1) without any pairwise-independence assumption.

For (5.2), stop at the first time any tagged strand exits `U`. Until that stopping time, the same relative-chain proof applies because every incident clock needed by a tagged strand is untouched. A single tagged strand is ordinary continuous-time SRW until exit. The coordinate exponential-martingale estimate already used in `NEW-L105` gives

\[
\mathbf P(\tau_{U^c}\le S\mid\mathscr F)
\le6\exp\left[-\min\left\{\frac{b^2}{8S},\frac b2\right\}\right].
\]

Union over `Q` labels and add this exit probability to (5.1). `square`

## 6. B28 — a typical-phase consequence with an explicit scale gap

The point of `NEW-L107` is that it controls the **moving pairwise center** directly through the relative coordinate. It is therefore not subject to C27’s deterministic counterexample in which a different fixed spatial center is contaminated in every phase.

Take a globally fresh slab of duration `beta`, partition it into intervals of length

\[
T=\beta^{3/16}
\]

(up to one leftover interval), and take

\[
Q\le\beta^{1/8},\qquad r=\beta^{1/8}.
\]

The number of candidate phase intervals is

\[
m\asymp\beta^{13/16}.
\]

Choose

\[
q=\left\lceil20\log_2(\beta+2)\right\rceil.
\]

Then `NEW-L107` gives, with failure probability at most

\[
C\beta^{1/4}(\beta+2)^{-20},
\]

that the number of intervals in which **any two** of the `Q` preselected fresh strands approach within `r` is at most

\[
C\beta^{1/2}\log(\beta+2).
\]

Thus the contaminated phase fraction obeys

\[
\boxed{
\frac{J_{\rm any}(\beta^{1/8})}{m}
\le C\beta^{-5/16}\log(\beta+2).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The exponent arithmetic is exact:

\[
2\cdot\frac18+2\cdot\frac18=\frac12,
\qquad
1-\frac{3}{16}=\frac{13}{16},
\qquad
\frac{13}{16}-\frac12=\frac5{16}.
\]

For a local-fresh region, if every selected start has buffer

\[
b\ge20\sqrt{\beta\log(\beta+2)}
\]

and `beta>=200`, then `beta>=25 log(beta+2)` and the exit term in (5.2) is at most

\[
6Q(\beta+2)^{-50}.
\]

Hence (6.1) survives with an additional negligible exit failure.

This is a **complete partial theorem with an explicit auxiliary beta regime (`beta>=200`)**, but it is not the requested source-level `beta_0`: it only says that polynomially many labels chosen before a fresh slab contaminate a vanishing fraction of deterministic phases. It does not yet prove macroscopic cycles.
