# 3. NEW-L58 — temporal regularity of every fixed short-cycle event

Fix an integer `M>=1`. For a permutation `sigma` of a finite cubic torus, write

\[
F_M(\sigma)=\mathbf1\{L_\sigma(0)\le M\}.
\]

For an unoriented lattice edge `e={u,v}`, let `tau_e` be its transposition and call `e` **M-pivotal** for `sigma` if

\[
F_M(\tau_e\sigma)\ne F_M(\sigma).
\]

Let `N_M(sigma)` be the number of `M`-pivotal edges.

## 3.1 Deterministic pivotal-edge classification

There are two cases.

### Case A: `L_sigma(0)<=M`

A transposition whose two endpoints both lie inside the root cycle merely splits that cycle into two cycles whose lengths are each at most the original length, so the root remains in a cycle of length at most `M`. A transposition disjoint from the root cycle also cannot change `F_M`.

Therefore an edge can make `F_M` change from `1` to `0` only if it crosses from the root cycle to another cycle. Since the cubic lattice has degree six,

\[
N_M(\sigma)\le6M
\qquad\text{on }\{L_\sigma(0)\le M\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Case B: `L_sigma(0)>M`

Merging the root cycle with any other cycle cannot create a short root cycle. Hence an `M`-pivotal transposition must have both endpoints on the root cycle and must split off a root-containing component of size at most `M`.

Put

\[
W_M(\sigma)
=\{\sigma^j(0):-M-1\le j\le M+1\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If multiplying by a transposition `tau_{u,v}` splits the root cycle and the new root component has size at most `M`, then the two cut locations `u,v` lie within at most `M+1` cyclic steps of the root on the original cycle. Thus `u,v in W_M(sigma)`. The harmless `+1` absorbs the left-versus-right multiplication convention at the cut endpoints.

Consequently,

\[
N_M(\sigma)
\le
\sum_{-M-1\le i<j\le M+1}
\mathbf1\bigl\{|\sigma^i0-\sigma^j0|_1=1\bigr\}
\qquad\text{on }\{L_\sigma(0)>M\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Combining the two cases,

\[
N_M(\sigma)
\le
6M\mathbf1_{\{L_\sigma(0)\le M\}}
+
\sum_{-M-1\le i<j\le M+1}
\mathbf1\bigl\{|\sigma^i0-\sigma^j0|_1=1\bigr\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

This estimate is deliberately wasteful but depends only on finitely many orbit iterates for fixed `M`.

## 3.2 Expected pivotal rate on `Z^3`

Point-shift stationarity along the permutation orbit, already archived for HARD10-03, gives for each fixed pair `i<j`

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(|\pi_t^i0-\pi_t^j0|_1=1\bigr)
=
\mathbb P\bigl(|\pi_t^{j-i}0|_1=1\bigr).
\tag{3.5}
\]

For negative lags the same bound follows from inversion/time-reversal symmetry of interchange. By (1.1), for every fixed `r`,

\[
\mathbb P(|\pi_t^r0|_1=1)
\le 6C_r t^{-3/2},
\qquad t\ge1.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Together with the fixed-short-cycle estimate (1.2), (3.4) implies that for each fixed `M` there is a finite constant `K_M` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E N_M(\pi_t)
\le K_M t^{-3/2},
\qquad t\ge1.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

No uniformity in `M` is claimed.

## 3.3 Integrated modulus of continuity

On a finite torus, the interchange generator gives

\[
\left|\frac d{dt}\mathbb E F_M(\pi_t^{(n)})\right|
\le
\mathbb E N_M(\pi_t^{(n)}).
\tag{3.8}
\]

For fixed `M` and fixed compact time interval, every term on the right side of (3.4) involves only finitely many orbit iterates. The archived finite-torus/infinite-volume graphical transfer therefore allows `n->infinity` in the integrated version of (3.8). Applying (3.7) after the limit yields:

### Theorem NEW-L58 — PROVED

Let

\[
s_M(t)=\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_t(0)\le M).
\]

For every fixed `M` there is `K_M<infinity` such that for all `1<=u<v`,

\[
\boxed{
|s_M(v)-s_M(u)|
\le
K_M\int_u^v r^{-3/2}\,dr
\le
K_M(v-u)u^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
f_M(t)=\mathbb P(L_t(0)>M)=1-s_M(t),
\]

\[
\boxed{
|f_M(v)-f_M(u)|
\le K_M(v-u)u^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

This is much stronger at large times than the trivial `O_M(v-u)` jump-rate bound: for every fixed cycle cutoff, the law becomes asymptotically flat on time windows of length `o(t^{3/2})`.

# 4. NEW-L59 — arbitrary-window averaged long-cycle lower bound

The proof of archived `NEW-L57A` did not require a dyadic interval. Repeating it on an arbitrary interval `[t,t+T]` gives a useful form for the pointwise upgrade.

On the finite torus, archived `NEW-L56` gives

\[
\frac1T\int_t^{t+T}a_n(r)\,dr
\ge
\frac12-\frac1{6T}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `0` and `e_1` are in the same permutation cycle of length at most `M`, then

\[
(\pi_r^{(n)})^j(0)=e_1
\]

for some `1<=j<=M-1`. Hence

\[
a_n(r)
\le
\mathbb P(L_{n,r}(0)>M)
+
\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}
\mathbb P((\pi_r^{(n)})^j0=e_1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For fixed `M,t,T`, let `n->infinity` using the archived finite-orbit graphical coupling. Then (1.1) bounds the finite-lag error uniformly for `r>=t>=1`.

### Theorem NEW-L59 — PROVED

For every fixed `M>=2`, there is `B_M<infinity` such that for every `t>=1` and every `T>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac1T\int_t^{t+T}
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_r(0)>M)\,dr
\ge
\frac12-\frac1{6T}-B_M t^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the arbitrary-window version of `NEW-L57A`.

# 5. NEW-L60 — pointwise diverging cycle scales at every sufficiently large time

Combine the average lower bound (4.3) with temporal flatness (3.10). For every `r in [t,t+T]`,

\[
f_M(r)
\le
f_M(t)+K_MTt^{-3/2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac1T\int_t^{t+T}f_M(r)\,dr
\le
f_M(t)+K_MTt^{-3/2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Substitute (4.3):

\[
f_M(t)
\ge
\frac12-rac1{6T}
-B_Mt^{-3/2}
-K_MTt^{-3/2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Choose

\[
T=t^{3/4}.
\]

Then for every fixed `M` there is `C_M<infinity` such that, for all `t>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(L_t(0)>M)
\ge
\frac12-C_M t^{-3/4}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\mathbb P(L_t(0)>M)
\ge\frac12
\qquad\text{for every fixed }M.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This removes the exceptional-time weakness of archived `NEW-L57B`: the lower bound is now pointwise in the time parameter for **every sufficiently large prescribed time**, while still making no monotonicity assumption.

## 5.1 Diagonal pointwise scale

Because the constants `C_M` need not be controlled uniformly in `M`, (5.3) still cannot send `M->infinity` at a fixed finite `t`. But it does yield a deterministic diverging scale valid at every large time.

Choose an increasing sequence `t_m` so large that

\[
C_m t^{-3/4}\le\frac1m
\qquad\text{for every }t\ge t_m.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Define

\[
M_*(t)=\max\{m:t\ge t_m\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Then `M_*(t)->infinity`, and for every sufficiently large `t`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(L_t(0)>M_*(t)\bigr)
\ge
\frac12-o(1).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

More concretely, if `M_*(t)=m`, then the right side is at least `1/2-1/m`.

Thus the root-cycle length distribution is pointwise non-tight along the **entire** large-time axis, not merely along selected times or a positive-density subset of times.

## 5.2 A weak-limit formulation

For any sequence `t_k->infinity`, every subsequential weak limit on the compactified state space `N union {infinity}` of the laws of `L_{t_k}(0)` assigns mass at least `1/2` to `infinity`. This is only a statement about limits of the cycle-length distributions as the time parameter tends to infinity; it does **not** say that `P(L_t(0)=infinity)>=1/2` at any finite `t`.

# 6. Why NEW-L60 still does not solve the conjecture

The target requires a positive infinite-cycle probability at each fixed sufficiently large time:

\[
\mathbb P(L_t(0)=\infty)>0.
\]

For each fixed finite `t`,

\[
\mathbb P(L_t(0)=\infty)
=
\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb P(L_t(0)>M).
\]

`NEW-L60` controls `t->infinity` for each fixed `M`, and then a very slowly growing diagonal `M_*(t)`. Without constants uniform in `M`, the two limits cannot be interchanged. Therefore (5.7) is a genuine pointwise long-cycle theorem, not an infinite-cycle theorem.

The improvement is nevertheless substantial: any hypothetical all-finite large-time phase must now carry, at every sufficiently large prescribed time, at least about one half of the root mass on finite cycles whose lengths diverge with time. The remaining obstruction is purely the escape of the cutoff `M`.

# 7. Relation to the stochastic-sparsity program

Archived `NEW-L56` rewrites the torus drift in geometric form. If `I(C)` is the number of nearest-neighbor lattice edges induced by a cycle support `C`, then by edge/root mass transport

\[
a_n(t)
=
\frac13\,
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{I(C_{n,t}(0))}{|C_{n,t}(0)|}
\right].
\tag{7.1}
\]

The time-average `a_n approximately 1/2` therefore demands induced-edge density about `3/2` in root-size-biased average. `NEW-L60` says short cycles contribute only `O_M(t^{-3/2})` pointwise. Hence the remaining mass supporting the drift must be carried by the long finite / macroscopic sector at every large time, not just at selected good times.

A still-sufficient next theorem is a **uniform-in-length stochastic small-set sparsity gap** for actual finite interchange cycles, for example a bound that prevents the long finite sector from sustaining

\[
\mathbb E\left[
\frac{I(C_0)}{|C_0|};\ L_t(0)<\infty
\right]
\ge\frac32-o(1)
\]

at all large times. The strong-Rayleigh exact-set heat-content estimates and the particle-number-uniform random-cut connected-block bound from earlier checkpoints remain the most promising inputs.

A second route is to make either (1.1) or (1.2) uniform enough in the orbit lag/cycle cutoff to let `M=M(t)` grow at a controlled rate. Any bound forcing `C_M=o(t^{3/4})` along an explicit diverging `M(t)` would make (5.3) quantitative; a summable-in-cycle-length estimate of the old target form

\[
\mathbb P(L_t(0)=\ell)
\le C(\ell t)^{-3/2}
\]

would still close the full conjecture immediately.
