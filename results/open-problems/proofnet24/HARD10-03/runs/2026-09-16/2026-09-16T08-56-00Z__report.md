# 3. NEW-L69 — exact cut-survival bound from heat leakage

Let `A subset Z^3` be deterministic, finite and nonempty, with

\[
n:=|A|.
\]

Color exactly the labels initially located in `A` red. At time `beta`, let

\[
\eta_\beta(y)\in\{0,1\}
\]

indicate whether site `y` is occupied by a red label. The red occupied set evolves as symmetric exclusion started from the deterministic configuration `1_A`. By Borcea--Brändén--Liggett, its law at each fixed time is strongly Rayleigh and therefore negatively associated.

Let

\[
K_\beta(x,y):=\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(x)=y)=p_\beta(x,y)
\]

be the one-particle heat kernel. Define the expected red mass retained in `A` by

\[
H_\beta(A)
:=\sum_{y\in A}\mathbb E\eta_\beta(y)
=\sum_{x,y\in A}p_\beta(x,y),
\tag{3.1}
\]

and define the one-period **heat leakage**

\[
\mu_\beta(A)
:=n-H_\beta(A)
=\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{y\notin A}p_\beta(x,y).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The event that the permutation preserves `A` setwise is exactly the event that every site of `A` is red at time `beta`:

\[
\{\pi_\beta(A)=A\}
=\bigcap_{y\in A}\{\eta_\beta(y)=1\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Negative association for the increasing single-site events gives

\[
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(A)=A)
\le
\prod_{y\in A}\mathbb E\eta_\beta(y).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Write

\[
h_A(y):=\mathbb E\eta_\beta(y)
=\sum_{x\in A}p_\beta(x,y).
\]

Then `0<=h_A(y)<=1` and

\[
\sum_{y\in A}h_A(y)=n-\mu_\beta(A).
\]

AM--GM therefore yields

\[
\prod_{y\in A}h_A(y)
\le
\left(1-\frac{\mu_\beta(A)}n\right)^n
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta(A)\}.
\]

Hence:

### Theorem NEW-L69 — PROVED

For every finite nonempty deterministic `A subset Z^3` and every `beta>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(\pi_\beta(A)=A\bigr)
\le
\left(1-\frac{\mu_\beta(A)}{|A|}\right)^{|A|}
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta(A)\}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

In particular, for any `u in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A\bigr)
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta(A)\},
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

because the event that the cycle through `u` has vertex set exactly `A` implies `pi_beta(A)=A`.

### What is new relative to the earlier exact-set theorem

The earlier strong-Rayleigh estimate

\[
\mathbb P(C_u=A)\le (|A|q_\beta)^{|A|},
\qquad q_\beta=p_\beta(0,0),
\]

is stronger for small sets with `|A|q_beta <<1`. NEW-L69 is not claimed to supersede it. Its useful feature is different: it expresses the cost of closure directly in terms of **actual heat leakage across the candidate cut**, and it remains meaningful for arbitrarily large sets whenever that leakage is large.

# 4. NEW-L70 — conditional stopping-set cut-survival theorem

The same proof is stable under the previously archived admissible no-peeking conditioning.

Let `F` be an admissible exploration history in the sense used in the earlier conditional determinant/strong-Rayleigh results. Let `A` be a finite nonempty `F`-measurable set that is fixed before the still-unrevealed pieces governing the remaining evolution are exposed. Let

\[
K_\beta^{\mathscr F}(x,y)
:=\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(x)=y\mid\mathscr F)
\]

be the conditional one-particle kernel, and define

\[
\mu_\beta^{\mathscr F}(A)
:=|A|-
\sum_{x,y\in A}K_\beta^{\mathscr F}(x,y).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The archived conditional strong-Rayleigh theorem says that, after conditioning on such an admissible history, the final red occupation law obtained from labels initially in `A` remains strongly Rayleigh. (The remaining dynamics is a composition of symmetric-exclusion pieces and deterministic transpositions; real stability is preserved.) Therefore the argument of Section 3 can be repeated conditionally, point for point.

### Theorem NEW-L70 — PROVED

Almost surely on every admissible history for which the above objects are defined,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(\pi_\beta(A)=A\mid\mathscr F\bigr)
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta^{\mathscr F}(A)\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence, for every `u in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A\mid\mathscr F\bigr)
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta^{\mathscr F}(A)\}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a genuine **stopping-set closure inequality**: once a candidate finite trap has been selected without peeking at the clocks that determine its remaining one-period communication, its closure probability is controlled by a single conditional one-particle leakage number. This directly addresses part of the anticipativity bottleneck left by NEW-L67/68, although it does not by itself construct the needed nonanticipative candidate sets.

# 5. NEW-L71 — two explicit lower bounds on leakage

## 5.1 Small-volume / heat-kernel bound

Translation invariance, symmetry and the semigroup/Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give

\[
p_\beta(x,y)\le p_\beta(0,0)=:q_\beta.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
H_\beta(A)
=\sum_{x,y\in A}p_\beta(x,y)
\le n^2q_\beta,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\beta(A)\ge n(1-nq_\beta).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular, if `n q_beta <= a<1`, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(A)=A)
\le e^{-(1-a)n}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Again, the older product bound may be numerically stronger in this regime. The point of (5.2) is that it is the first member of the leakage formulation and has an immediate conditional/fresh-buffer analogue below.

## 5.2 A beta-uniform surface-order bound for every finite set

Let `L` be the nonnegative graph Laplacian for the one-particle walk, so the heat semigroup is `P_t=e^{-tL}`. Then

\[
\mu_t(A)
=\langle 1_A,(I-P_t)1_A\rangle.
\tag{5.4}
\]

By the spectral theorem, for `t>=1`,

\[
1-e^{-t\lambda}\ge1-e^{-\lambda}
\quad(\lambda\ge0),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\mu_t(A)\ge\mu_1(A).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Now let `partial_E A` denote the set of unoriented nearest-neighbor edges with exactly one endpoint in `A`. If `{x,y}` is such an edge with `x in A`, then a single interchange label starting at `x` jumps at total rate six. The event that in time one it makes exactly one jump and that jump is along `{x,y}` has probability exactly

\[
e^{-6}.
\]

Therefore

\[
p_1(x,y)\ge e^{-6}.
\]

Summing over oriented exits corresponding to boundary edges gives

\[
\boxed{
\mu_1(A)
=\sum_{x\in A,y\notin A}p_1(x,y)
\ge e^{-6}|\partial_E A|.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

It remains to lower-bound the edge boundary without importing an isoperimetric black box. Let `Pi_i(A)` be the projection of `A` onto the coordinate plane perpendicular to `e_i`. Every nonempty fiber parallel to `e_i` contributes at least two boundary edges in direction `i`, hence

\[
|\partial_E A|
\ge2\sum_{i=1}^3|\Pi_i(A)|.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The discrete Loomis--Whitney inequality gives

\[
n^2\le\prod_{i=1}^3|\Pi_i(A)|.
\tag{5.8}
\]

AM--GM then gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^3|\Pi_i(A)|\ge3n^{2/3},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|\partial_E A|\ge6n^{2/3}.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Combining (5.5)--(5.9):

### Theorem NEW-L71 — PROVED

For every finite nonempty `A subset Z^3` and every `beta>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge6e^{-6}|A|^{2/3}.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(\pi_\beta(A)=A\bigr)
\le
\exp\{-6e^{-6}|A|^{2/3}\},
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

and, for every `u in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A\bigr)
\le
\exp\{-6e^{-6}|A|^{2/3}\}.
}
\tag{5.12}
\]

This is a surface-order Peierls-type bound for **every fixed candidate support**, uniform over all `beta>=1`. It requires no tagged-particle constants and no assumption that the candidate support is connected.

A useful combined deterministic-set bound is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_u=A)
\le
\exp\left\{-\max\left(
6e^{-6}|A|^{2/3},
|A|(1-|A|q_\beta)_+
\right)\right\},
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

with the earlier `(nq_beta)^n` estimate retained as an additional, often sharper, option when `nq_beta<1`.

# 6. NEW-L72 — fresh-buffer conditional exponential closure cost

Suppose an admissible exploration has selected an `F`-measurable finite set `A` of size `n` inside a still-unexposed buffer `U`, and

\[
b=\operatorname{dist}(A,U^c).
\]

The previously archived fresh-buffer estimate gives, uniformly over the admissible exterior history,

\[
K_\beta^{\mathscr F}(x,y)
\le p_\beta(x,y)+\varepsilon_{\beta,b},
\qquad x,y\in A,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where

\[
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
:=\sup_{x\in A}\mathbb P_x(\tau_{U^c}\le\beta).
\]

Put

\[
\alpha_{\beta,b}:=q_\beta+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_{x,y\in A}K_\beta^{\mathscr F}(x,y)
\le n^2\alpha_{\beta,b},
\]

and hence

\[
\mu_\beta^{\mathscr F}(A)
\ge n(1-n\alpha_{\beta,b}).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining with NEW-L70 yields:

### Corollary NEW-L72 — PROVED

If

\[
n\alpha_{\beta,b}\le a<1,
\]

then uniformly over admissible exterior histories,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(A)=A\mid\mathscr F)
\le e^{-(1-a)n},
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

and likewise

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A\mid\mathscr F)
\le e^{-(1-a)n}
\qquad(u\in A).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For a buffer thickness of order `sqrt(beta log beta)` chosen with a sufficiently large constant, the archived Gaussian exit estimate makes `epsilon_{beta,b}=O(beta^{-3/2})`; since `q_beta=Theta(beta^{-3/2})`, this gives an exponential closure cost for history-measurable candidate supports of size up to a sufficiently small fixed multiple of `beta^{3/2}`.

The earlier conditional root-cycle containment theorem can be stronger for some small-volume events. NEW-L72's advantage is that it is stated as a direct **closure cost for a stopping candidate**, in precisely the variable needed for a recursive exploration.

# 7. Finite-family consequence and the precise entropy obstruction

For any deterministic family `A_n` of `n`-point subsets containing a fixed root `u`, union bound and NEW-L71 give

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(C_u\in\mathcal A_n\bigr)
\le
|\mathcal A_n|\exp\{-6e^{-6}n^{2/3}\}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus any class of candidate supports whose logarithmic entropy is `o(n^(2/3))` is negligible at large `n`.

This does **not** solve the conjecture. The class of arbitrary finite subsets, or even ordinary connected lattice animals, has much larger entropy. More importantly, previous explicit mediator counterexamples show that a permutation cycle's vertex set need not be connected by direct chronological same-cycle contacts. One cannot simply restrict to a low-entropy family of connected supports.

The important gain is instead that any successful no-peeking exploration no longer needs a many-particle estimate to charge final closure: once it produces a candidate set with large conditional heat leakage, NEW-L70 supplies the exponential cost automatically.

# 8. Checks actually performed and failure analysis

Analytic checks completed in this run:

1. **Event equivalence:** `pi_beta(A)=A` iff all sites of `A` are occupied at time `beta` by labels initially in `A`; conservation of exactly `|A|` red labels makes the implication in both directions exact.
2. **Negative-association direction:** the events `{eta_beta(y)=1}` are increasing in disjoint coordinates, exactly the direction supplied by negative association. No unproved decreasing-event statement is used.
3. **Leakage identity:** `mu_beta(A)=|A|-sum_{x,y in A}p_beta(x,y)` equals the expected number of red particles outside `A` at time `beta`.
4. **Time monotonicity of leakage:** this is spectral monotonicity of the quadratic form `<1_A,(I-e^{-tL})1_A>`, not monotonicity of cycle events in `beta`.
5. **Boundary one-jump constant:** with six rate-one incident edges, total jump rate is six; exactly one jump in time one along a specified neighbor has probability `e^-6`.
6. **Boundary isoperimetry:** every coordinate fiber contributes two boundary edges, and Loomis--Whitney plus AM--GM gives `|partial_E A|>=6|A|^(2/3)`.
7. **Conditional scope:** NEW-L70/72 require the candidate `A` to be measurable with respect to an admissible no-peeking history before the relevant remaining clocks are exposed. They do not permit choosing `A` from the final cycle and then retroactively conditioning.

A finite-state numerical certificate is optional and is not used as a proof. The report is deliberately published before any such optional computation, as required.

No contradiction with the previous large-capacity / many-separated-representative results was found. Rather, NEW-L69--72 identify the quantity that a nonanticipative extraction lemma should target: conditional heat leakage.

# 9. What remains open and next attack

The full conjecture remains open because the strongest geometric information currently available (large capacity, many superdiffusively separated representatives, many effective diffusive boxes) is extracted from the **final** cycle and is therefore anticipative.

The new stopping-set theorem reduces the missing step to a cleaner problem:

> Construct, during an admissible no-peeking cyclic exploration, a history-measurable candidate finite trap `A` (or a controlled family of such candidates) such that every eventual finite closure of the root must pass through one candidate with either
> \[
> \mu_\beta^{\mathscr F}(A)\gg1
> \]
> or a fresh-buffer condition `|A|(q_beta+epsilon_{beta,b})<1-c`.

If the candidate family created by the exploration has entropy smaller than the accumulated leakage cost, NEW-L70 would close the union bound without any `k`-tag heat-kernel constant.

Two concrete next attacks are highest value:

1. **Capacity-to-leakage stopping rule.** Use the archived `beta^(1/3-o(1))` Green-capacity lower bound to stop an exploration when the revealed candidate first has capacity `R`; prove that in a fresh phase or buffer, such a set has conditional heat leakage at least `c R^gamma` for some `gamma>0` unless spatial progress occurs.
2. **Recursive block fragmentation.** Use the archived particle-number-uniform random-cut theorem when the candidate fragments into low-communication blocks; otherwise exploit a quantitatively large heat bridge to extend the exploration. NEW-L70 charges the final closure of each history-measurable candidate.

The surface bound NEW-L71 alone cannot beat the entropy of all possible large supports, so treating it as a standalone Peierls proof would be incorrect.
