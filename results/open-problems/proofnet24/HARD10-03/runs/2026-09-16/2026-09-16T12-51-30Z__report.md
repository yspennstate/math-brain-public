# 3. NEW-L90 — a quenched uniform local-mass theorem for every finite root cycle

The preceding checkpoint showed that a randomly rooted short cyclic window is mesoscopically self-avoiding with high probability. The point-shift identity allows this to be converted into a **uniform spatial density bound for the entire finite cycle**, simultaneously over every ball center.

Fix integers

\[
H\ge1,
\]

and a radius `R>=1`. Define the root-window bad event

\[
B_{H,R}(\omega)
:=
1\left\{
\exists\ 0\le u<v\le H:\
|Z_v-Z_u|_2\le2R
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

`NEW-L86` with stride `h=1` and sample count `k=H` gives

\[
\boxed{
q_\beta(H,R):=\mathbb E B_{H,R}
\le
C\left(
\frac{H}{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{H R^3}{\beta^{3/2}}
\right).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

On `{L<infinity}`, let

\[
\overline B_{H,R}
:=
\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} B_{H,R}(T^j\omega).
\tag{3.3}
\]

By (1.3),

\[
\mathbb E\left[1_{\{L<\infty\}}\overline B_{H,R}\right]
\le q_\beta(H,R).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence for every `eta in (0,1)`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
L<\infty,\ 
\overline B_{H,R}>\eta
\right)
\le
\frac{q_\beta(H,R)}{\eta}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The key new step is deterministic.

### Lemma 3.1 — uniform ball occupancy from good cyclic windows

Let

\[
C=\{x_0,\ldots,x_{L-1}\},
\qquad x_j=\pi_\beta^j(0),
\]

be a finite permutation cycle, with indices modulo `L`. Suppose at most `eta L` cyclic starting indices have an `H+1` window containing two points at mutual distance at most `2R`. Then, for every `z in Z^3`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|C\cap B_2(z,R)|}{L}
\le
\eta+\frac1{H+1}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof

Fix `z` and let

\[
S=\{j:x_j\in B_2(z,R)\}.
\]

Every pair of points indexed by `S` is at Euclidean distance at most `2R`.

Call `j in S` good if its cyclic window

\[
I_j=\{j,j+1,\ldots,j+H\}
\]

is not bad. There are at most `eta L` indices of `S` that are bad starts.

For distinct good `j,k in S`, the cyclic arcs `I_j` and `I_k` are disjoint. Indeed, if they overlapped, one of the two starting indices would lie within at most `H` forward steps of the other; the corresponding two cycle vertices both lie in `B_2(z,R)` and hence are within `2R`, contradicting goodness of the earlier start. If `L<=H`, every window contains a repeated cycle vertex and is bad, so under `eta<1` this case cannot occur.

Therefore the good arcs have total length at most `L`, and the number of good indices in `S` is at most `L/(H+1)`. Adding the at most `eta L` bad starts proves (3.6). `square`

Combining (3.5) and Lemma 3.1 gives the main theorem.

### Theorem NEW-L90A — PROVED

For every `H>=1`, `R>=1`, and `eta in (0,1)`,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\left(
L<\infty,\ 
\sup_{z\in\mathbb Z^3}
\frac{|C_0\cap B_2(z,R)|}{L}
>
\eta+\frac1{H+1}
\right)\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{C}{\eta}
\left(
\frac{H}{\sqrt\beta}
+
\frac{H R^3}{\beta^{3/2}}
\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is a **quenched cycle-wide** statement: outside the displayed exceptional event, the bound holds simultaneously for every spatial center `z`. It is not merely an annealed two-point estimate.

## 3.1 A scale frontier

Let

\[
R=\beta^\rho,
\qquad 0<\rho<1/2,
\]

and define

\[
\alpha(\rho)
:=
\begin{cases}
1/4,&0<\rho\le1/3,\\
3/4-3\rho/2,&1/3\le\rho<1/2.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Choose

\[
H=\lfloor\beta^{\alpha(\rho)}\rfloor\vee1.
\]

Then both terms in (3.2) are `O(beta^{-alpha(rho)})`. More explicitly:

* if `rho<=1/3`, `alpha=1/4`, the driver-coupling term is `beta^{-1/4}` and the collision term is at most `beta^{-1/4}`;
* if `rho>=1/3`, `alpha=3/4-3rho/2`, the collision term is exactly of order `beta^{-alpha}`, while the coupling term is no larger.

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, choose

\[
\eta=\beta^{-\alpha(\rho)+\epsilon}.
\]

Then (3.7) yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L<\infty,\ 
\sup_z\frac{|C_0\cap B_2(z,\beta^\rho)|}{L}
>C_{\rho,\epsilon}\beta^{-\alpha(\rho)+\epsilon}
\right)
\le
C_{\rho,\epsilon}\beta^{-\epsilon}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

At the especially useful scale `rho=1/3`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L<\infty,\ 
\sup_z\frac{|C_0\cap B_2(z,\beta^{1/3})|}{L}
>C_\epsilon\beta^{-1/4+\epsilon}
\right)
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus a hypothetical finite root cycle cannot put more than a `beta^{-1/4+o(1)}` fraction of its mass in **any** ball of radius `beta^{1/3}`.

A maximal `R`-separated subset has `R`-balls covering the cycle. Therefore (3.9) also gives the deterministic covering/packing corollary

\[
\operatorname{Pack}_{R}(C_0)
\ge c\beta^{\alpha(\rho)-\epsilon}
\]

on the same event. This corollary is weaker than some root-prefix packings of `NEW-L86`, but (3.9) is stronger in a different direction because it controls mass around **every** center of the complete finite cycle.

# 4. NEW-L91 — high-probability Green-energy/capacity bound in the finite-cycle sector

An earlier checkpoint, `2026-09-16T07-22-52Z__report.md` (blob `e4fc739060cb52979e6bab837f0065183d036c55`), proved under the contradiction hypothesis `P(L=infinity)=0` an annealed Green-energy estimate of order `beta^{-1/3+o(1)}`. `NEW-L90` removes that all-finite hypothesis and upgrades the conclusion to a high-probability statement **inside the finite-cycle sector**, even if infinite cycles already coexist.

Let

\[
g(x)=\int_0^\infty p_t(0,x)\,dt
\]

be the transient continuous-time SRW Green kernel on `Z^3`. The archived derivation gives

\[
\boxed{
g(x)\le \frac{C}{1+|x|_2}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a finite cycle `C`, define its uniform Green self-energy

\[
\mathcal E_g(C)
:=
\frac1{|C|^2}
\sum_{u,v\in C}g(v-u).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Fix `epsilon>0` sufficiently small. By (1.2), with

\[
K=\lfloor\beta^{1/2-\epsilon/3}\rfloor,
\]

\[
\mathbb P(L\le K)\le C\beta^{-\epsilon/3}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Apply (3.10) with `epsilon/3` in place of `epsilon`. Except on another event of probability `O(beta^{-epsilon/3})` inside `{L<infinity}`, every radius-`R` ball, with

\[
R=\beta^{1/3},
\]

contains at most the cycle-mass fraction

\[
m:=C_\epsilon\beta^{-1/4+\epsilon/3}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Work on the simultaneous good event `{K<L<infinity}` plus (4.4), and put

\[
r_0=\beta^{1/12}.
\]

Fix any `u in C`. Split the normalized Green sum around `u` into three ranges.

### Near field: `|v-u|<=r_0`

Without using any cycle geometry, the lattice ball contains at most all lattice sites in that ball. Hence, using (4.1),

\[
\frac1L\sum_{\substack{v\in C\\|v-u|\le r_0}}g(v-u)
\le
\frac{C}{L}
\sum_{|x|\le r_0}\frac1{1+|x|}
\le
C\frac{r_0^2}{L}
\le
C\beta^{-1/3+\epsilon/3}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Middle field: `r_0<|v-u|<=R`

All such vertices lie in the radius-`R` ball centered at `u`, whose cycle mass is at most `m`. On this range `g(v-u)<=C/r_0`, so

\[
\frac1L\sum_{r_0<|v-u|\le R}g(v-u)
\le
C\frac{m}{r_0}
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-1/3+\epsilon/3}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Far field: `|v-u|>R`

Here `g(v-u)<=C/R`, hence

\[
\frac1L\sum_{|v-u|>R}g(v-u)
\le
C/R
=C\beta^{-1/3}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The bounds are uniform in `u`. Averaging over `u in C` gives

\[
\mathcal E_g(C)
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-1/3+\epsilon/3}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Absorbing the fixed constant into `beta^{2epsilon/3}` for sufficiently large `beta` gives:

### Theorem NEW-L91A — PROVED

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, for all sufficiently large `beta`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L_\beta(0)<\infty,\ 
\mathcal E_g(C_0)>\beta^{-1/3+\epsilon}
\right)
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon/3}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

No assumption that all cycles are finite is made.

Define Green capacity by

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_g(A)
=
\left[
\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal P(A)}
\sum_{x,y\in A}\mu(x)g(y-x)\mu(y)
\right]^{-1}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

The uniform measure on `A` is an admissible competitor, so

\[
\frac1{\operatorname{Cap}_g(A)}
\le
\mathcal E_g(A).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore:

### Corollary NEW-L91B — PROVED

For every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
L_\beta(0)<\infty,\ 
\operatorname{Cap}_g(C_0)<\beta^{1/3-\epsilon}
\right)
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon/3}
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

for all sufficiently large `beta`.

This is a genuine strengthening of the earlier capacity statement: it is conditional only on the cycle actually being finite, not on the global hypothesis `p_infinity(beta)=0`, and it is high-probability rather than merely annealed.

# 5. What this changes in the contradiction picture

A hypothetical finite root cycle at large `beta` must now satisfy, with probability tending to one within the finite-cycle sector, all of the following simultaneously:

1. `L >= beta^{1/2-o(1)}` by the first-interaction theorem;
2. almost every short cyclic window is mesoscopically self-avoiding (`NEW-L89`);
3. **every** radius-`beta^{1/3}` ball contains at most a `beta^{-1/4+o(1)}` fraction of the full cycle (`NEW-L90`);
4. its uniform Newtonian/Green self-energy is at most `beta^{-1/3+o(1)}` and its Green capacity is at least `beta^{1/3-o(1)}` (`NEW-L91`).

This is stronger than an annealed pair-separation statement: `NEW-L90` rules out a hidden dense spatial core carrying a macroscopic fraction of an otherwise spread-out finite cycle.

The conclusion still does **not** prove an infinite cycle. Large finite sets can have small Green energy and large capacity, and the remaining closure event is adaptive: the finite cycle support is only known after the full Poisson slab has been exposed.

## 6. A killed shortcut: why capacity alone still does not close the proof

The archived strong-Rayleigh leakage theorem gives, for a deterministic finite candidate `A`,

\[
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A)
\le
\exp\{-\mu_\beta(A)\},
\]

with strong lower bounds on `mu_beta(A)`. `NEW-L91` says a realized finite cycle has large capacity. It is tempting to substitute that realized cycle back into a deterministic leakage inequality. That is invalid: `C_0` was selected using the same Poisson slab whose closure probability is being estimated. Doing so would reintroduce exactly the anticipativity error already identified in earlier checkpoints.

The capacity theorem becomes directly usable only against a **fresh** independent slab, or after a no-peeking exploration has selected a candidate set while leaving the relevant clock region unrevealed.

Thus this checkpoint strengthens the geometric resource available for a restart argument but does not silently assume freshness.

## 9. Next attack

The strongest next micro-target is now a **nonanticipative capacity-to-freshness lemma**.

`NEW-L91` says that a typical finite cycle carries capacity `beta^{1/3-o(1)}`; `NEW-L90` says that this capacity cannot be concentrated in one dense `beta^{1/3}` ball. A useful stopping construction would expose the cyclic walk only until it has certified a subset `A_*` with

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_g(A_*)\ge\beta^{1/3-o(1)}
\]

while revealing only thin tubes along the strands. One should then prove that a polynomial family of annuli around a capacity-separated subcollection remains Poisson-fresh. The existing conditional strong-Rayleigh and random-cut theorems could then charge finite closure using those unexposed annuli.

A second route is to combine the uniform local-mass frontier with a multiscale Frostman extraction: build a history-measurable measure supported on explored strand centers satisfying

\[
\mu(B(x,r))\lesssim (r/\sqrt\beta)^{s}
\]

for some `s>1`, and use this to force a quantitative fresh-slab escape probability. The current `alpha(rho)` frontier has effective coarse dimension `3/2` as `rho` approaches `1/2` from below, suggesting that `s>1` is not numerically implausible. The missing point remains admissible exploration/freshness, not unconditional geometry.
