## 3. Section-I(a): the exact statement that blocks

The multiscale gluing inequality itself is already available: if a physical parent bad event can be decomposed, outside a leakage event `E_j(z)`, into one of finitely many pairs of bad children supported on disjoint Poisson regions, then the recursion

\[
q_{j+1}\le aL_j^{-\eta}+Nq_j^2

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\mathbb P(E_j(z))\le C L_j^{-\eta}\quad\text{for some }\eta>0,\\
B_{j+1}(z)\setminus E_j(z)
\subseteq
\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{N}
\bigl(B_j(z_{\nu,1})\cap B_j(z_{\nu,2})\bigr),\\
\text{with the two child events in each pair measurable from disjoint Poisson clock regions.}

## 7. Section-I(e): nearest solved problem and what it has that `Z^3` lacks

The nearest solved problem is Elboim--Sly's interchange theorem on `Z^d`, `d>=5`, for sufficiently large `beta`.

Their proof has three ingredients that are directly relevant:

- cyclic-time walk rather than chronological-particle reasoning;
- a local escape algorithm for entanglement with history;
- multiscale concatenation of shorter nearly independent walks, ultimately yielding diffusive/Brownian behavior.

The dimension-specific ingredient is the availability of times after which a fresh random-walk piece avoids the old history with probability bounded away from zero. Their paper explicitly explains that this fails in lower dimensions: the avoidance probability decays polynomially in `d<=3` and only logarithmically in `d=4`.

The present `d=3` program must therefore replace **uniform history avoidance** by a different renewable resource. The most plausible recovered resource is **phase-space freshness with predictable sparse obstacles**: instead of asking a whole walk segment to avoid all history, ask for at least one phase among many in which the locally relevant revealed set is small before the guard is opened. C27's guarded theorem and HARD10-03's previsible finite-family theorem show this can work once predictability is granted. What remains unknown is whether the actual cyclic exploration can be organized to satisfy that predictability without revealing away the very randomness needed for renewal.

### B20 — find why the algorithm fails

The obvious algorithm is: traverse the cycle, choose the next spatially favorable phase, expose a fresh tube, and iterate. Its exact failure is causal. If the phase is selected using the final-cycle location, the selection already depends on the clocks one wants to call fresh. If instead the entire complementary period is conditioned on to make one phase fresh, future reserve phases are consumed. The missing algorithmic invariant is therefore **a filtration in which the next phase entry is known while a positive fraction of that phase's local edge-time boxes remain unexposed**.

No new bound is claimed; this is the recovered obstruction in operational form.

These are not unrelated technicalities. They all say that the proof needs a **renewable, causally selected separator** between the explored history and the next piece of cyclic motion.

### B27 — swap the quantifiers

The invalid form is effectively

`for the actual future cycle anchor x, there exists a phase that looks fresh around x`.

`there exists a phase selected from past information such that, for the subsequently generated entry x, the local obstacle is controlled`.

Swapping those quantifiers is the exact conceptual move required. The two-traversal special case in §6 is chosen because it is the first place where this order can potentially be enforced without conditioning on the final permutation.
