# 3. NEW-L74 — resolvent interpolation: Green energy controls leakage without a positive-part loss

The preceding NEW-L73 gives

\[
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge
|A|\left(1-\frac{|A|\mathcal E_g(A)}{\beta}\right),
\]

which is strong when `|A| E_g(A) << beta` but becomes vacuous once that ratio exceeds one. The following interpolation is strictly more robust and remains positive at every scale.

Let

\[
L f(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}(f(x)-f(y))
\]

be the nonnegative nearest-neighbor graph Laplacian, so `P_beta=e^{-beta L}`. For finite nonempty `A`, write

\[
f=1_A,\qquad n=|A|,
\]

and let `nu_f` be the spectral measure of `L` generated by `f`. Then

\[
\mu_\beta(A)
=\langle f,(I-e^{-\beta L})f\rangle
=\int_{(0,12]}(1-e^{-\beta\lambda})\,d\nu_f(\lambda).
\tag{3.1}
\]

There is no atom at zero in the `l^2(Z^3)` spectral measure of a finitely supported `f`; equivalently the Green quadratic form below is finite in `d=3`.

The elementary scalar inequality

\[
e^{-x}\le \frac1{1+x}\qquad(x\ge0)
\]

implies

\[
1-e^{-\beta\lambda}
\ge
\frac{\beta\lambda}{1+\beta\lambda}
=
\frac1{1+(\beta\lambda)^{-1}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Normalize `nu_f` by its total mass

\[
\nu_f((0,12])=\langle f,f\rangle=n.
\]

Because `y -> 1/(1+y)` is convex on `[0,infinity)`, Jensen gives

\[
\frac{\mu_\beta(A)}n
\ge
\frac1{1+\frac1{\beta n}\int \lambda^{-1}\,d\nu_f(\lambda)}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

In `d=3`, with

\[
g(z)=\int_0^\infty p_t(0,z)\,dt,
\qquad
\mathcal E_g(A)
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{x,y\in A}g(y-x),
\]

spectral calculus/Tonelli gives

\[
\int \lambda^{-1}\,d\nu_f(\lambda)
=\langle f,L^{-1}f\rangle
=\sum_{x,y\in A}g(y-x)
=n^2\mathcal E_g(A).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem NEW-L74 — PROVED

For every finite nonempty deterministic `A subset Z^3` and every `beta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge
\frac{|A|}{1+|A|\mathcal E_g(A)/\beta}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Combining with NEW-L69,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(A)=A)
\le
\exp\left\{-
\frac{|A|}{1+|A|\mathcal E_g(A)/\beta}
\right\},
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

and for every `u in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A)
\le
\exp\left\{-
\frac{|A|}{1+|A|\mathcal E_g(A)/\beta}
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

### Relation to NEW-L73

For `x=|A| E_g(A)/beta <1`,

\[
\frac1{1+x}\ge1-x,
\]

so NEW-L74 is at least as strong as the NEW-L73 exponent in its useful regime. Unlike NEW-L73 it does not collapse to zero when `x>=1`.

This theorem is unconditioned/self-adjoint. It is **not** automatically asserted for an arbitrary admissibly conditioned kernel, because after conditioning the one-particle kernel need not be self-adjoint and there is no automatic spectral Green representation. The conditional NEW-L70/72 route remains separate.

# 4. NEW-L75 — universal Green-energy bound for every finite lattice set

The Green kernel on `Z^3` satisfies the standard consequence of the one-particle Gaussian heat-kernel bound

\[
\boxed{g(z)\le \frac{C}{1+|z|_\infty}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For completeness, this follows by integrating

\[
p_t(0,z)\le Ct^{-3/2}e^{-c|z|^2/t}
\]

in `t`; the `z=0` value is finite by transience.

Fix `x in Z^3` and an arbitrary `n`-point set `A`. Let `R=max(1,ceil(c n^{1/3}))`, with `c` chosen so that a cube of radius `R` contains at least `n` lattice points up to an absolute constant. Split the row sum into near and far parts:

\[
\sum_{y\in A}g(y-x)
\le
C\sum_{|z|_\infty\le R}\frac1{1+|z|_\infty}
+
\frac{Cn}{R}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The first term is bounded by

\[
C\sum_{r=0}^R \frac{(r+1)^2}{r+1}
\le CR^2,
\]

while `n/R <= C n^{2/3}`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x\sum_{y\in A}g(y-x)
\le C n^{2/3}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Summing (4.3) over `x in A` gives

\[
\sum_{x,y\in A}g(y-x)
\le Cn^{5/3},
\]

hence:

### Lemma NEW-L75A — PROVED

For every finite nonempty `A subset Z^3`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_g(A)\le C|A|^{-1/3}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Substituting (4.4) into NEW-L74 yields

\[
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge
\frac{n}{1+C n^{2/3}/\beta}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently, for an absolute `c>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge
c\min\{n,\beta n^{1/3}\}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem NEW-L75 — PROVED

For every finite nonempty deterministic `A subset Z^3`, every `u in A`, and every `beta>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A)
\le
\exp\{-c\min(n,\beta n^{1/3})\},
\qquad n=|A|.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Combining with the already proved NEW-L71A surface bound gives the strictly stronger all-volume envelope

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A)
\le
\exp\left\{-c\max\left(
 n^{2/3},
 \min\{n,\beta n^{1/3}\}
\right)\right\}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Up to absolute multiplicative constants in the transition locations, the exponent has three regimes:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
 n\lesssim \beta^{3/2}: & \exp(-c n),\\[2mm]
 \beta^{3/2}\lesssim n\lesssim \beta^3: & \exp(-c\beta n^{1/3}),\\[2mm]
 n\gtrsim \beta^3: & \exp(-c n^{2/3}).
\end{array}
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The middle regime is new: after the small-volume `e^{-cn}` estimate has ceased to be uniform, exact finite-cycle supports still pay a stretched exponential `e^{-c beta n^{1/3}}` until the surface-order estimate takes over near `n~beta^3`.

### What this does and does not solve

This is a theorem for each **fixed deterministic candidate support**. It cannot simply be summed over all `n`-point subsets of `Z^3` containing the root: there are infinitely many such supports, and the invariant-set upper bound deliberately forgets the connected primitive-return information that makes far-away vertices costly. Thus (4.8) is not a proof of an exponential tail for `L_beta(0)` and is not a proof of the conjecture.

It does, however, significantly narrows the candidate-support problem. Any successful summation/cluster expansion now needs to recover only enough connected-return structure to control the entropy of supports against the piecewise exponent (4.9), rather than having only the surface exponent `n^{2/3}` available at all large volumes.

# 5. NEW-L76 — independent-slab instability of a finite cycle

NEW-L74 can be used without anticipativity if the candidate cycle is generated by one Poisson slab and tested for survival by an **independent** slab. This gives an exact semigroup-compatible statement connecting times `beta` and `2 beta` without any monotonicity claim.

Let `P` and `R` be independent time-`beta` interchange permutations on `Z^3`, and define

\[
Q=R\circ P.
\]

By independent increments of the Poisson clocks, `Q` has exactly the time-`2 beta` interchange law.

Let `C=C_P(0)` and `L=|C|`. On `{L<infinity}`, `P(C)=C`. Define the number of points of `C` sent outside `C` by the second slab,

\[
N_{\rm out}(R,C)
:=\#\{z\in C:R(z)\notin C\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `P` permutes `C`, the number of `Q`-arrows from `C` to `C^c` is exactly `N_out(R,C)`:

\[
\#\{x\in C:Q(x)\notin C\}
=
\#\{x\in C:R(Px)\notin C\}
=N_{\rm out}(R,C).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every `x in C` with `Q(x) notin C` belongs to a `Q`-cycle that is not contained in `C`. Hence, if

\[
E_C(Q)=\{x\in C:C_Q(x)\not\subseteq C\},
\]

then

\[
|E_C(Q)|\ge N_{\rm out}(R,C).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The archived point-shift stationarity of the finite `P`-cycle extends to the joint `(P,R)` law by translating the independent, translation-invariant second slab together with the re-rooting. Therefore root-averaging over the finite `P`-cycle gives

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(L<\infty,\ C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)\bigr)
=
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{|E_C(Q)|}{L}
\right].
\tag{5.4}
\]

Using (5.3), conditioning on the first slab, and using independence of `R`,

\[
\mathbb E[N_{\rm out}(R,C)\mid C]
=
\mu_\beta(C),
\tag{5.5}
\]

because `mu_beta(C)` is exactly the expected number of labels started in `C` whose second-slab endpoint lies outside `C`. Thus:

### Theorem NEW-L76 — PROVED

For two independent time-`beta` slabs `P,R` and `Q=R o P`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
L_P(0)<\infty,\
C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)
\bigr)
\ge
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L_P(0)<\infty\}}
\frac{\mu_\beta(C_P(0))}{L_P(0)}
\right].
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Applying NEW-L74 pointwise to the realized first-slab cycle (which is legitimate **after conditioning on the first slab**, because the leakage is evaluated for the independent second slab) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
L_P(0)<\infty,\
C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)
\bigr)
\ge
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\frac1{1+L\mathcal E_g(C)/\beta}
\right].
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Using only the universal energy estimate NEW-L75A,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
L_P(0)<\infty,\
C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)
\bigr)
\ge
c\,\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}
\min\{1,\beta L^{-2/3}\}
\right].
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

For `beta>=1`, the integrand satisfies

\[
\min\{1,\beta L^{-2/3}\}\ge L^{-1},
\]

so a weaker but simple corollary is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
L_P(0)<\infty,\
C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)
\bigr)
\ge c\,\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}/L].
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

### Important limitation

`C_Q(0) not subseteq C_P(0)` is **not** the same as `|C_Q(0)|>|C_P(0)|`: composition with the second slab may both recruit and discard vertices from the old cycle. Therefore NEW-L76 is an instability theorem, not a monotone growth theorem, and no monotonicity in `beta` is being smuggled in.

Still, it gives a genuine semigroup bridge: the same low-energy geometry that made a deterministic support hard to close also makes an independently resampled second time slab likely to destroy that support as an invariant trap. This is the first direct use of the new leakage technology across independent time slabs rather than only as a fixed-time exact-set bound.

# 6. Checks actually performed

The following points were checked explicitly in this run.

1. **Scalar resolvent inequality.** `e^{-x} <= 1/(1+x)` for `x>=0`, so (3.2) has the correct direction.
2. **Jensen direction.** `y -> 1/(1+y)` has second derivative `2/(1+y)^3>0`, so the normalized spectral average is bounded below by the function of the average, as used in (3.3).
3. **Green quadratic form.** In transient `d=3`, for finitely supported `f`, `<f,L^{-1}f>=integral_0^infinity <f,P_t f> dt`, which equals the double Green sum by Tonelli.
4. **Universal energy exponent.** `g(z)<=C/(1+|z|)` and cubic volume growth give row sum `O(n^{2/3})`, hence total Green sum `O(n^{5/3})` and normalized energy `O(n^{-1/3})`.
5. **Independent-slab composition.** With chronological convention `Q=R o P`, independent increments give the time-`2 beta` law. Because `P(C)=C`, the number of `Q` arrows leaving `C` equals the number of second-slab `R` arrows leaving `C`.
6. **No false growth claim.** The theorem records only failure of containment of the new root cycle in the old root cycle; it does not assert that cycle length is monotone or increases.
7. **No anticipative misuse.** NEW-L74 is applied to the random first-slab cycle only after conditioning on the entire first slab and then testing it with an independent second slab. It is not retroactively applied to the same slab that selected the cycle.

No finite-state computation is needed for these operator inequalities; no numerical experiment is promoted as proof.

# 7. Failure ledger and exact remaining obstruction

### Proved in this run

* NEW-L74: nonvacuous resolvent/Green-energy leakage bound
  `mu_beta(A) >= |A|/(1+|A| E_g(A)/beta)`.
* NEW-L75A: universal `Z^3` Green-energy bound `E_g(A)<=C|A|^{-1/3}`.
* NEW-L75: all-volume deterministic exact-cycle-support suppression
  `exp{-c max(n^{2/3}, min(n,beta n^{1/3}))}`.
* NEW-L76: an independent second `beta`-slab destabilizes a finite first-slab root-cycle support with probability at least the expected leakage fraction, with the Green-energy lower bounds (5.7)--(5.9).

### Still conditional/open

* There is still no summable bound over all possible finite supports `A` containing the root. Fixed-set invariant-set bounds lose the primitive connected-return structure and therefore cannot be union-bounded over arbitrary distant subsets.
* NEW-L76 does not imply monotone cycle growth under time doubling. A new cycle may leave the old support while also losing old vertices.
* The all-finite geometry from NEW-L65--68 is still anticipative for the **same** slab. The independent-slab theorem avoids that logically, but a mechanism is still needed to turn repeated support instability into positive probability of an actually infinite cycle at one prescribed time.

### New high-value attack suggested by this run

The middle-regime exponent

\[
\exp(-c\beta n^{1/3}),
\qquad
\beta^{3/2}\lesssim n\lesssim\beta^3,
\]

is much stronger than the previously available surface exponent. The next useful step is therefore a connected-return counting lemma whose entropy grows more slowly than `exp(C beta n^{1/3})` on that regime. A plausible way to obtain it is to encode a finite cycle by a sparse skeleton of `O(n^{1/3})` spatially separated representatives plus the minimum mediator data needed to connect successive skeleton points. The earlier fixed-set random-cut and contact-sparsity results are designed for precisely such a skeleton. If that entropy can be controlled, the new leakage exponent may be strong enough to sum the middle-volume sector.

A second route is to iterate NEW-L76 across independent slabs and find a Lyapunov quantity that decreases whenever the root cycle escapes its previous support, despite possible simultaneous vertex loss. A candidate is not raw cycle size but a potential-theoretic quantity such as Green capacity or low-frequency form factor. No such monotone/contractive Lyapunov theorem is proved here.
