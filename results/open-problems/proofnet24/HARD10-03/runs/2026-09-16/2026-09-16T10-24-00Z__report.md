# 3. NEW-L77 — optimal-scale nonlocal heat leakage

The previous resolvent bound has the correct diffusive-volume crossover but is not sharp for very large sets. For a macroscopic region of radius much larger than `sqrt(beta)`, one expects one-period leakage to be of order `sqrt(beta)` times surface area. This section proves exactly that lower-bound scaling for **every** finite lattice set.

Let

\[
n=|A|,
\qquad
\mu_\beta(A)=\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{y\notin A}p_\beta(x,y).
\tag{3.1}
\]

We assume `beta>=beta_1`, where `beta_1` is an absolute constant; bounded `beta` can be absorbed into constants and is irrelevant to the large-time target.

## 3.1 A uniform near-diagonal lower bound for the tagged-particle kernel

The continuous-time tagged particle is continuous-time simple random walk with generator

\[
\mathcal G f(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}[f(y)-f(x)].
\]

The two-sided Gaussian heat-kernel estimate on `Z^3` implies that there are absolute constants `a,c_0>0` such that for every `beta>=beta_1`,

\[
\boxed{
|z|_2\le 2a\sqrt\beta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p_\beta(0,z)\ge c_0\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Only this near-diagonal consequence is used below.

## 3.2 Embed the lattice set as unit cubes

For `x in Z^3`, let

\[
Q_x=x+[-1/2,1/2)^3,
\qquad
\Omega_A=\bigcup_{x\in A}Q_x\subset\mathbb R^3.
\]

Then `|Omega_A|=n` and, up to null boundaries,

\[
\Omega_A^c=\bigcup_{y\notin A}Q_y.
\]

Set

\[
r=a\sqrt\beta/2.
\]

Choose `beta_1` large enough that `r>=2sqrt(3)`. If `u in Q_x`, `v in Q_y`, and `|u-v|_2<=r`, then

\[
|x-y|_2\le |u-v|_2+\sqrt3\le 2r=a\sqrt\beta,
\]

so (3.2) applies. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_\beta(A)
&=\sum_{x\in A,y\notin A}p_\beta(x,y)\\
&\ge c_0\beta^{-3/2}
\sum_{x\in A,y\notin A}
\int_{Q_x}\int_{Q_y}
1_{\{|u-v|_2\le r\}}\,dv\,du\\
&=c_0\beta^{-3/2}
\int_{\Omega_A}\int_{\Omega_A^c}
1_{\{|u-v|_2\le r\}}\,dv\,du.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the lattice leakage dominates a continuum nonlocal perimeter with interaction radius `r asymp sqrt(beta)`.

## 3.3 Nonlocal isoperimetry

For a measurable finite-volume set `Omega subset R^3`, define

\[
I_r(\Omega)
=
\int_\Omega\int_{\Omega^c}1_{\{|u-v|_2\le r\}}\,dv\,du.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Because the kernel `1_{|z|<=r}` is nonnegative, radial and radially nonincreasing, the Riesz rearrangement inequality implies that among sets of fixed volume, `I_r` is minimized by a Euclidean ball. Indeed,

\[
I_r(\Omega)
=|\Omega||B_r|
-
\int_\Omega\int_\Omega1_{\{|u-v|\le r\}}\,dv\,du,
\]

and Riesz says the self-interaction term is maximized by the symmetric ball.

Let `B_R` have volume `n`, so `R asymp n^{1/3}`.

### Lemma 3.1 — ball nonlocal perimeter

There is an absolute `c>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
I_r(B_R)
\ge
c\min\{R^3r^3,R^2r^4\}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

**Proof.**

*If `R<=r`*: when `R<=r/4`, every `u in B_R` has at least a fixed fraction of the `r`-ball around it outside `B_R`, hence `I_r(B_R)>=cR^3r^3`. For `r/4<R<=r`, restrict `u` to an outer shell of thickness comparable to `R`; that shell has volume `cR^3`, and each such `u` has an exterior subset of `B_r(u)` of volume `cR^3`. Since `R` and `r` are comparable in this subcase, this again gives `cR^3r^3` up to an absolute constant.

*If `R>=r`*: restrict `u` to the shell
\[
R-r/4\le |u|\le R.
\]
Its volume is at least `cR^2r`. For each such `u`, choose a ball of radius `r/8` centered a distance `r/2` farther in the radial outward direction. It lies inside `B_r(u)` and outside `B_R`, so the exterior volume available from each `u` is at least `cr^3`. Hence
\[
I_r(B_R)\ge c(R^2r)(r^3)=cR^2r^4.
\]
This proves (3.5). `square`

Since `R^3 asymp n`, (3.5) is

\[
I_r(\Omega_A)
\ge I_r(B_R)
\ge
c\min\{nr^3,n^{2/3}r^4\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Insert `r asymp sqrt(beta)` into (3.3):

\[
\beta^{-3/2}nr^3\asymp n,
\qquad
\beta^{-3/2}n^{2/3}r^4\asymp \sqrt\beta\,n^{2/3}.
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem NEW-L77 — PROVED

There are absolute constants `c>0`, `beta_1<infinity` such that for every `beta>=beta_1` and every finite nonempty `A subset Z^3`,

\[
\boxed{
\mu_\beta(A)
\ge
c\min\{|A|,\sqrt\beta\,|A|^{2/3}\}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

The crossover is exactly the diffusive volume

\[
|A|\asymp\beta^{3/2}.
\]

For a cube of side `R>>sqrt(beta)`, the order `sqrt(beta)R^2` is the expected boundary-layer scale, so (3.7) has the correct large-set power law.

# 4. NEW-L78 — improved all-volume exact-support penalty

Combine NEW-L77 with NEW-L69:

### Theorem NEW-L78 — PROVED

For every `beta>=beta_1`, every finite nonempty deterministic `A subset Z^3`, `n=|A|`, and every `u in A`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(A)=A)
\le
\exp\{-c\min(n,\sqrt\beta\,n^{2/3})\},
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_{\pi_\beta}(u)=A)
\le
\exp\{-c\min(n,\sqrt\beta\,n^{2/3})\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This improves the previous NEW-L75 envelope for every sufficiently large set. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ll}
 n\lesssim\beta^{3/2}: & \mathbb P(C_u=A)\le e^{-cn},\\[1mm]
 n\gtrsim\beta^{3/2}: & \mathbb P(C_u=A)\le e^{-c\sqrt\beta\,n^{2/3}}.
\end{array}}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For `n>beta^{3/2}`, the old resolvent exponent was `beta n^{1/3}`; the new exponent is larger by the factor

\[
\frac{\sqrt\beta n^{2/3}}{\beta n^{1/3}}
=
\left(\frac{n}{\beta^{3/2}}\right)^{1/3}.
\]

Unlike the old surface-only estimate `e^{-cn^{2/3}}`, the new large-volume penalty retains the factor `sqrt(beta)` for arbitrarily large candidate supports.

### Important scope

(4.2) is still a bound for each fixed deterministic support. It cannot be summed naively over all finite subsets containing the root: there are infinitely many spatially arbitrary supports, and replacing the exact cycle event by invariant-set survival loses primitive connected-return information. NEW-L78 is therefore not by itself a proof of an infinite cycle.

# 5. NEW-L79 — stronger independent-slab instability

Reuse the exact NEW-L76 setup: `P,R` are independent time-`beta` interchange permutations, `Q=R o P`, `C=C_P(0)`, `L=|C|`. Conditional on the first slab, `C` is deterministic for the independent second slab, so NEW-L77 applies legitimately to the second-slab heat leakage of `C`.

NEW-L76 gives

\[
\mathbb P(L<\infty,\ C_Q(0)\not\subseteq C)
\ge
\mathbb E\left[1_{\{L<\infty\}}\frac{\mu_\beta(C)}L\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

By NEW-L77,

\[
\frac{\mu_\beta(C)}L
\ge
c\min\{1,\sqrt\beta\,L^{-1/3}\}.
\]

Hence:

### Corollary NEW-L79 — PROVED

For all sufficiently large `beta`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(L_P(0)<\infty,\ C_{R\circ P}(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)\bigr)
\ge
c\,\mathbb E\left[
1_{\{L_P(0)<\infty\}}
\min\{1,\sqrt\beta\,L_P(0)^{-1/3}\}
\right].
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(L_P<\infty,\ C_{R\circ P}(0)\not\subseteq C_P(0)\bigr)
\ge
c\,\mathbb P(L_P\le\beta^{3/2}).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is strictly stronger than the NEW-L76 universal resolvent corollary `min(1,beta L^{-2/3})` in the super-diffusive-volume regime `L>beta^{3/2}`.

Again, support noncontainment is **not** cycle-length monotonicity: the time-`2 beta` root cycle can gain vertices outside the first-slab cycle while losing other first-slab vertices. No monotonicity claim is made.

## 6. Checks actually performed and a rejected shortcut

1. **Hypothesis check for rearrangement.** The nonlocal kernel used in Section 3 is the Euclidean radial indicator `1_{|z|_2<=r}`, exactly within the classical Riesz/nonlocal-isoperimetric hypotheses. I deliberately did not treat the actual lattice heat kernel `p_beta(x,y)` as radial in graph distance; it is not.
2. **Heat-kernel hypothesis.** Only the standard near-diagonal lower bound `p_beta(0,z)>=c beta^{-3/2}` for `|z|<=a sqrt(beta)` is used. This is a direct consequence of the two-sided Gaussian estimates on `Z^3`; no off-diagonal lower estimate beyond diffusive distance is required.
3. **Cube embedding.** The identity between the sum of cube-pair integrals and the continuum cross interaction was checked with the half-open unit-cube partition; cube boundaries are null sets, so there is no missing contribution.
4. **Ball geometry.** The two regimes in Lemma 3.1 were checked separately. For `R>>r`, the boundary layer has volume `Theta(R^2 r)` and each layer point sees `Theta(r^3)` exterior volume within distance `r`, giving `Theta(R^2 r^4)`. For `R<=r`, the interaction is volume order `Theta(R^3r^3)`.
5. **Rejected shortcut.** The 2026 discrete Schwarz-rearrangement theorem is not applied directly to `p_beta`, because its displayed Riesz theorem assumes the interaction factor is a decreasing function of graph distance. Same-`l^1` lattice displacements need not have equal continuous-time heat-kernel probabilities. The continuum lower-kernel comparison avoids this mismatch.

No numerical calculation is needed for the analytic theorem. Optional finite-set numerics, if run later, are sanity checks only and do not upgrade verification status.

## 7. What this changes and exact remaining obstruction

The deterministic-support penalty now has the natural boundary-layer exponent

\[
\exp\{-c\sqrt\beta\,n^{2/3}\}
\qquad(n\gg\beta^{3/2}),
\]

instead of falling back to a `beta`-independent surface exponent. This is a meaningful gain because it remains stronger as the candidate support volume becomes arbitrarily large.

However, the full conjecture is still blocked by **support entropy / primitive connectivity**. A fixed-support survival estimate, no matter how strong in `n`, cannot be union-bounded over all spatially arbitrary finite subsets of `Z^3`. The final cycle support need not be nearest-neighbor connected, as the archived mediator counterexample already showed.

The most concrete next attack is now to recover just enough primitive-return geometry to make NEW-L78 summable. Two routes look highest-value:

* **deterministic shell skeleton:** decompose a finite cycle by deterministic concentric diffusive annuli and prove that either it makes many controlled cross-annulus transitions or it contains an atypically long mapping arrow; then combine the particle-number-uniform random-cut theorem with NEW-L78;
* **fresh-slab Lyapunov:** iterate NEW-L79 while tracking a quantity that cannot decrease too much when the new root cycle leaves the previous support, such as coarse capacity or a low-frequency form factor. Raw cycle length cannot be used because support reshuffling is nonmonotone.

A useful intermediate target is a theorem bounding the entropy of candidate mediator skeletons by `exp(o(sqrt(beta) n^{2/3}))` for large supports. Such a result would pair directly with (4.2).
