## 2. Infinite-volume construction is fixed before using cyclic time

Let `(omega_e)_{e in E(Z^3)}` be independent rate-one Poisson point processes on `[0,infinity)`. For a fixed finite beta and starting vertex `x`, follow the tagged particle chronologically through rings of edges incident to its current location. Its total jump rate is six, so it makes finitely many jumps on `[0,beta]` almost surely. Define `pi_beta(x)` as its location at time beta.

For every `x` this is well-defined almost surely. Reversing the chronological list of transpositions on `[0,beta]` gives the inverse map, so `pi_beta` is almost surely a permutation of `Z^3`.

The chronological tagged path over one period is an ordinary continuous-time simple random walk, but it is **not** the permutation cycle. The cyclic-time walk reuses the same period-beta bar configuration; at integer period times,

`Y(n beta) = pi_beta^n(x)`.

All renewal claims below are formulated for this reused environment, not for independent copies of the chronological walk.

## 4. New idea before code: use the seam between two cyclic periods

The failed one-window arguments try to find repeated freshness at arbitrary internal phases after the full complement map has already been inspected. Instead, use the **deterministic seam of the period**.

For one tagged traversal, reserve its *final* physical interval `[beta-G,beta]` before opening it. After this final interval produces the next permutation anchor `a_1=pi_beta(a_0)`, inspect the *initial* interval `[0,G]` around the new anchor during the next cyclic traversal.

These are two different time regions of the same original Poisson slab. The only way the first traversal can have contaminated the second guard is if its own early path `[0,G]` came spatially close to its eventual endpoint `a_1`. For a tagged particle that event is a late-return event of ordinary `Z^3` continuous-time simple random walk, and it has an explicit heat-kernel bound of order `G r^3 beta^{-3/2}`.

This avoids the diagonal endpoint-selection problem without pretending that a new independent period exists.

We take, throughout the theorem,

`x = beta^(1/4)`, `G=floor(x)`, `r=ceil(x)`.

The choice makes the guard radius much larger than the diffusive scale `sqrt(G)` while the early-endpoint contamination probability is `O(beta^(-1/2))`.

## 5. B19 — exact chronological no-peeking exploration

### Lemma 5.1 — local chronological exploration

Fix a tagged particle and a deterministic time interval. There is an exact exploration of its path which, at physical time `t`, queries only Poisson information on edges incident to the particle's current vertex at times not yet passed. It reveals the next incident ring and its edge, moves the tag, and repeats. No edge-time information outside the queried incident region is needed to determine the tagged path.

If the exploration is stopped and `Gamma` is a region of edge-time space chosen measurably from the revealed history but disjoint from everything queried so far, then conditional on that history the Poisson processes restricted to `Gamma` retain their original independent rate-one law.

### Justification

For finitely many deterministic edge-time cells this is induction from independence of disjoint Poisson increments. Refine the cells dyadically in time. The chronological algorithm is adapted to the increasing revealed sigma-fields, and on every finite refinement unqueried cells remain independent Poisson. Passing to the generated sigma-field gives the continuous-time statement. Equivalently, this is the standard strong-Markov/stopping-set property for a product Poisson environment.

The lemma is applied only to explicitly disjoint incident-edge guards below; no retrospective choice depending on an already-opened guard is permitted.

## 6. Heat-kernel input with explicit constants

Let `S_t` be continuous-time simple random walk on `Z^3` with rate one to each nearest neighbor. Its three coordinate processes are independent one-dimensional walks with generator

`f(n+1)+f(n-1)-2f(n)`.

For one coordinate,

`p_t^(1)(a,b) = (2 pi)^(-1) int_{-pi}^{pi} exp[-2t(1-cos theta)] exp[-i theta(b-a)] d theta`.

Using `1-cos theta >= 2 theta^2/pi^2` on `[-pi,pi]`,

`sup_{a,b} p_t^(1)(a,b) <= sqrt(pi)/(4 sqrt(t))`.

Therefore

`sup_{u,v} p_t(u,v) <= c_* t^(-3/2)`,  with  `c_* = pi^(3/2)/64`.

### Lemma 6.1 — late visit to a cube

Let `Q_R={z: ||z||_infty <= R}` and `0<t_0<t_1`. Then

`P(exists s in [t_0,t_1] : S_s in Q_R)`

is at most

`c_* t_0^(-3/2) [ (2R+1)^3 + 6(t_1-t_0)(2R+3)^3 ]`.

### Proof

If the walk is in `Q_R` at `t_0`, pay the first term. Otherwise its first entrance during `(t_0,t_1]` occurs at a jump landing in `Q_R`. The expected number of such landing jumps is, by the jump compensator,

`int_{t_0}^{t_1} sum_x p_s(0,x) q(x,Q_R) ds`,

where `q(x,Q_R)` is the total rate from `x` into `Q_R`. This vanishes unless `x in Q_{R+1}` and is at most six. The heat-kernel bound gives the second term. Markov's inequality for the integer number of landing jumps completes the proof.

## 7. B4/B19 success — NEW-L108: cross-period two-window guarded renewal

### Theorem NEW-L108

Consider the infinite-volume interchange process on `Z^3` at any `beta>=256`. Let `X_t` be the first-period chronological tagged path from `a_0`, and put

`a_1 = X_beta = pi_beta(a_0)`.

Set

`G=floor(beta^(1/4))`, `r=ceil(beta^(1/4))`.

There is an admissible exact exploration with the following event `W_2(beta)`:

1. before the final first-period window `I^-=[beta-G,beta]` is opened, the center `X_{beta-G}` and the buffer
   `U^- = B_infty(X_{beta-G},r)` are already measurable, while all clocks in `I^-` are still fresh;
2. the first tagged strand stays in `U^-` throughout `I^-`, so `a_1` is determined using only this fresh final guard;
3. after `a_1` is known, the second cyclic traversal starts from `a_1` at physical phase zero, and the entire early guard
   `Gamma^+ = {edges incident to B_infty(a_1,r)} x [0,G]`
   is still unexposed and hence conditionally an independent rate-one Poisson environment;
4. the second tagged strand stays inside `B_infty(a_1,r)` throughout `[0,G]`.

Moreover

`P(W_2(beta)) >= 1 - 151 beta^(-1/2) - 12 exp(-G/5)`.

In particular, for every

`beta >= 10^8`,

`P(W_2(beta)) > 1231/1250 = 0.9848`.

This bound is pointwise in beta and uses no monotonicity assumption.

### Proof

#### Step 1 — reserve the first final window

Explore `X` chronologically only up to time `beta-G`. By independent Poisson increments, the entire physical-time interval `I^-` is untouched. Choose the `F_{beta-G}`-measurable buffer `U^-` centered at `X_{beta-G}` and then open only clocks incident to this buffer in `I^-`.

Until exiting `U^-`, the tagged strand is ordinary continuous-time simple random walk started at the buffer center. For one coordinate and any `theta>0`,

`M_t(theta)=exp(theta S_t^(i)-2t(cosh theta-1))`

is a positive martingale. Take `theta=2/5`. Since `r>=G`, Doob's inequality and the same estimate for `-S_t^(i)` give

`P(sup_{t<=G} ||S_t||_infty >= r) <= 6 exp[-G c_0]`,

where

`c_0 = 2/5 - 2(cosh(2/5)-1) > 1/5`.

(The last inequality follows directly from the power series `cosh(2/5)<11/10`.) Hence the first final guard fails with probability at most

`6 exp(-G/5)`.

On the complementary event, `a_1` is known exactly and no clock outside the local final guard was inspected during `I^-`.

#### Step 2 — characterize contamination of the next early guard

During the original first period's early interval `[0,G]`, the chronological exploration queried only edges incident to `X_t`. Therefore the new early guard around `a_1` is completely untouched whenever

`min_{0<=t<=G} ||X_t-a_1||_infty >= r+2`.

Indeed, if the center distance is at least `r+2`, then the first path is graph-distance at least two from `B_infty(a_1,r)`, so an edge incident to the first path cannot also be incident to the guard ball.

All information revealed in the middle and final parts of the first period lies at different physical times and cannot contaminate `[0,G]`.

#### Step 3 — bound the early/end collision without anticipative conditioning

Marginally, `X` is ordinary continuous-time simple random walk. By stationary independent increments and time reversal,

`(X_beta - X_{beta-s})_{0<=s<=beta}`

has the same law as `(S_s)_{0<=s<=beta}`.

Thus

`{exists t in [0,G] : ||X_t-a_1||_infty <= r+1}`

has the same probability as

`{exists s in [beta-G,beta] : ||S_s||_infty <= r+1}`.

Apply Lemma 6.1 with `R=r+1`, `t_0=beta-G`, `t_1=beta`. For `beta>=256`, write `x=beta^(1/4)>=4`. Then

`r<=x+1`, `R<=x+2`, `G<=x`, `beta-G>=beta/2`,

and hence

`2R+1 <= (13/4)x`, `2R+3 <= (15/4)x`.

Also `c_* = pi^(3/2)/64 < pi^2/64 < 121/784` and `2^(3/2)<3`. Therefore the collision probability is at most

`(121/784)*3*[ (13/4)^3/4 + 6(15/4)^3 ] beta^(-1/2)`.

The exact rational coefficient equals

`30200511/200704 = 150.4728904... < 151`.

So

`P(first early exploration contaminates Gamma^+) <= 151 beta^(-1/2)`.

#### Step 4 — the second guard is genuinely fresh

On the non-contamination event, `Gamma^+` is a history-measurable subset of the unqueried complement of the first chronological exploration. Lemma 5.1 therefore says that, conditional on the completed first-period history, the clocks in `Gamma^+` are still independent rate-one Poisson clocks.

Now start the next cyclic traversal at `a_1` and physical phase zero. Until it exits `B_infty(a_1,r)`, it is ordinary continuous-time simple random walk driven by these fresh clocks. The same martingale estimate from Step 1 shows that its probability of leaving the guard before time `G` is at most

`6 exp(-G/5)`.

A union bound over the first final-window exit, early/end contamination, and second early-window exit proves

`P(W_2(beta)) >= 1 - 151 beta^(-1/2) - 12 exp(-G/5)`.

For `beta>=10^8`, `G>=100`, so `151 beta^(-1/2)<=151/10000`. Since `e>2`,

`12 exp(-G/5) <= 12 e^(-20) < 12/2^20`.

Exact arithmetic gives

`151/10000 + 12/2^20 < 19/1250`,

so the success probability is greater than `1231/1250=0.9848`. QED.

## 8. What NEW-L108 does and does not prove

This is the first completed theorem on this track that turns the period seam itself into a **renewable source of untouched space-time randomness for the actual cyclic-time walk**:

- the first fresh window is the final interval of one tagged traversal;
- it produces the actual next permutation anchor `a_1=pi_beta(a_0)`;
- the second fresh window is the initial interval of the next cyclic traversal, in the same original Poisson slab;
- the second guard is selected only after `a_1` is known, yet its clocks remain fresh because the first traversal's early segment is spatially separated from its endpoint with probability `1-O(beta^(-1/2))`.

The theorem therefore closes the **two-successive-fresh-window special case** selected by STUCK RECOVERY 1.

It does **not** yet give a renewable sequence of arbitrarily many windows. After the second early window is consumed, computing `a_2=pi_beta(a_1)` requires exploring the remainder of the second traversal, and the third-period initial guard must avoid the union of earlier revealments. Controlling that growing union without losing previsibility is the next obstruction.

It also does not imply that `a_1` or `a_2` lies in a prescribed neighboring spatial block. A fresh chronological subpath can move diffusively while the complementary part of the period can later bring the permutation anchor elsewhere. Thus NEW-L108 is a genuine cyclic-time renewal lemma, not yet an orbit-propagating percolation block.
