# 3. NEW-L65 — an annealed Newtonian-energy bound in any all-finite regime

Fix `beta>=1`. Let `p_t(x,y)` be the continuous-time simple-random-walk kernel on `Z^3` for the same convention as a single interchange label (rate one across each incident edge), and define its Green kernel

\[
g(x):=\int_0^\infty p_t(0,x)\,dt.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `d=3`, the walk is transient and `g(x)<infinity`. The standard Gaussian one-particle estimate implies

\[
\boxed{
g(x)\le \frac{C}{1+|x|_\infty}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

For completeness, when `r=|x|_infty>=1`, split the integral at `r^2` and use

\[
p_t(0,x)\le C t^{-3/2}\exp(-c r^2/t)
\]

on the long-time part (with the bounded-time part absorbed by the same estimate). The change of variables `u=r^2/t` gives an `O(r^{-1})` integral. At `x=0`, `g(0)<infinity` is absorbed into the constant.

For a finite cycle `C`, define the uniform Green self-energy

\[
\mathcal E_g(C)
:=\frac1{|C|^2}\sum_{u,v\in C}g(v-u).
\tag{3.3}
\]

By the exact autocorrelation identity (1.3),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L<\infty\}}\mathcal E_g(C_0)
\right]
=
\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^3}G_\beta(x)g(x).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The crucial extra input is that `G_beta` is positive definite and nonnegative. Its `2 x 2` principal minor at `0,x` therefore gives

\[
G_\beta(x)^2\le G_\beta(0)^2.
\]

Since `G_beta(x)>=0`,

\[
\boxed{0\le G_\beta(x)\le G_\beta(0)=c_\beta.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Now assume the contradiction regime at this particular time:

\[
p_\infty(\beta)=0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then `sum_x G_beta(x)=1`. Put

\[
L_\beta^{\log}:=1+\log\beta
\]

and choose, for all sufficiently large `beta`,

\[
R=\left\lfloor
\beta^{1/12}(L_\beta^{\log})^{-1/3}
\right\rfloor\vee1.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Split (3.4) into three regions.

### 3.1 Near field: `|x|_infty<=R`

By (3.5), (1.1), and (3.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|x|_\infty\le R}G_\beta(x)g(x)
&\le c_\beta
   \sum_{|x|_\infty\le R}\frac{C}{1+|x|_\infty}\\
&\le Cc_\beta R^2\\
&\le C\beta^{-1/3}(L_\beta^{\log})^{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

The shell count is `O(r^2)`, so the weighted sum is `O(R^2)`.

### 3.2 Middle field: `R<|x|_infty<=sqrt(beta)`

Here `g(x)<=C/R`. By (1.5) with `M=floor(sqrt(beta))` and (3.6),

\[
S_\beta(\lfloor\sqrt\beta\rfloor)
\le C\beta^{-1/4}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{R<|x|_\infty\le\sqrt\beta}G_\beta(x)g(x)
\le \frac C R S_\beta(\lfloor\sqrt\beta\rfloor)
\le C\beta^{-1/3}(L_\beta^{\log})^{1/3}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

### 3.3 Far field: `|x|_infty>sqrt(beta)`

Using total mass one and `g(x)<=C/sqrt(beta)`, 

\[
\sum_{|x|_\infty>\sqrt\beta}G_\beta(x)g(x)
\le C\beta^{-1/2}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Combining the three regions gives the main theorem.

### Theorem NEW-L65 — PROVED

There is an absolute finite constant `C` such that, whenever `p_infty(beta)=0` and `beta` is sufficiently large,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,\mathcal E_g(C_0)
\le
C\beta^{-1/3}(1+\log\beta)^{1/3}.
}
\tag{3.12}
\]

Because the hypothesis says the root cycle is finite almost surely, the indicator from (3.4) has disappeared in (3.12).

### Interpretation

`NEW-L64` forced two uniform cycle vertices to be typically superdiffusively separated. `NEW-L65` is stronger in a different direction: it says a hypothetical finite root cycle has vanishing **Newtonian self-interaction**, averaged over two uniform vertices, at a quantified rate. This rules out not only concentration in one ball but also many geometries with recurrently close pair structure across scales.

No chronological tagged-particle path is being called a permutation cycle here; the only random-walk object is the deterministic comparison kernel `g` applied to the exact permutation-cycle autocorrelation `G_beta`.

# 4. NEW-L66 — finite cycles must have large Green capacity

For a finite nonempty set `A subset Z^3`, define its Green capacity by the variational normalization

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_g(A)
:=
\left[
\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal P(A)}
\sum_{x,y\in A}\mu(x)g(y-x)\mu(y)
\right]^{-1},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `P(A)` denotes probability measures supported on `A`. This definition fixes the normalization, so no external capacity convention is needed.

For the uniform measure on `A`,

\[
\frac1{\operatorname{Cap}_g(A)}
\le
\frac1{|A|^2}\sum_{x,y\in A}g(y-x)
=\mathcal E_g(A).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Apply this with `A=C_0` and take expectations. Under (3.6), NEW-L65 gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[\frac1{\operatorname{Cap}_g(C_0)}\right]
\le
C\beta^{-1/3}(1+\log\beta)^{1/3}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore, for every fixed `epsilon>0`, Markov's inequality gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Cap}_g(C_0)
\le \beta^{1/3-\epsilon}
\right)
&\le
\beta^{1/3-\epsilon}
\mathbb E\left[\frac1{\operatorname{Cap}_g(C_0)}\right]\\
&\le
C\beta^{-\epsilon}(1+\log\beta)^{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Theorem NEW-L66 — PROVED

If `p_infty(beta)=0`, then for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Cap}_g(C_0)
>\beta^{1/3-\epsilon}
\right)
\ge
1-C\beta^{-\epsilon}(1+\log\beta)^{1/3}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus any hypothetical all-finite large-time regime must place the root, with probability tending to one, in finite cycles of diverging Newtonian capacity at least `beta^{1/3-o(1)}`.

This is a necessary condition, not a contradiction: very extended finite sets can have large capacity.

# 5. NEW-L67 — many mutually separated cycle representatives

The exact pair-autocorrelation interpretation also gives a direct packing theorem.

For a finite set `A`, let

\[
D_R(A)
:=
\frac1{|A|^2}
\#\{(u,v)\in A^2:|u-v|_\infty\le R\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

By (1.3), under `p_infty(beta)=0`,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E D_R(C_0)=S_\beta(R).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let `Pack_R(A)` denote the maximum cardinality of a subset of `A` whose distinct points have pairwise `l_infinity` distance strictly larger than `R`.

A deterministic metric lemma connects pair concentration to packing.

### Lemma 5.1 — PROVED

For every finite nonempty `A` and `R>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Pack}_R(A)
\ge \frac1{D_{2R}(A)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

Take a maximal `R`-separated family `z_1,...,z_m` in `A`. Maximality implies the `R`-balls around the `z_i` cover `A`. Assign every point of `A` to one covering center, producing cells `A_1,...,A_m`. Every two points in the same cell have distance at most `2R`, hence

\[
D_{2R}(A)
\ge
\sum_{i=1}^m\left(\frac{|A_i|}{|A|}\right)^2
\ge \frac1m
\]

by Cauchy--Schwarz. Since `m<=Pack_R(A)` (indeed this family itself is admissible), (5.3) follows. QED.

Now choose a fixed `epsilon in (0,1/4)` and

\[
R_\beta=\left\lfloor\frac14\beta^{3/4-\epsilon}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For large `beta`, `2R_beta<=beta^{3/4-epsilon}`. Under `p_infty(beta)=0`, NEW-L64 gives

\[
\mathbb E D_{2R_\beta}(C_0)
=S_\beta(2R_\beta)
\le C\beta^{-\epsilon}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

By Markov,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
D_{2R_\beta}(C_0)>C^{1/2}\beta^{-\epsilon/2}
\right)
\le C^{1/2}\beta^{-\epsilon/2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

On the complement, (5.3) gives a packing of at least `c beta^{epsilon/2}` cycle vertices at mutual distances greater than `R_beta`.

### Theorem NEW-L67 — PROVED

Assume `p_infty(beta)=0`. For every fixed `epsilon in (0,1/4)`, there are constants `c_epsilon,C_epsilon>0` such that for all sufficiently large `beta`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Pack}_{\frac14\beta^{3/4-\epsilon}}(C_0)
\ge c_\epsilon\beta^{\epsilon/2}
\right)
\ge
1-C_\epsilon\beta^{-\epsilon/2}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

A useful diffusive-scale specialization comes from (1.5) with `M=O(sqrt(beta))`, which gives `S_beta(M)=O(beta^{-1/4})` under the all-finite hypothesis. Therefore one may also state

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Pack}_{c\sqrt\beta}(C_0)
\ge c'\beta^{1/8}
\right)
\ge1-C\beta^{-1/8}
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

for suitable fixed positive constants `c,c'`.

### Why this is stronger than a diameter statement

A single remote vertex would make the diameter large. NEW-L67 instead says that, under the all-finite hypothesis, the root cycle typically contains a **polynomially growing number of mutually superdiffusively separated representatives**. This begins to resemble the many-block geometry needed by the particle-number-uniform random-cut theorem archived earlier.

# 6. NEW-L68 — random-grid inverse-participation bound

There is a complementary coarse-grained formulation that avoids selecting representatives.

Fix an integer cube side `r>=1`, and independently choose a uniform shift

\[
U\in\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}^3.
\]

Let `Q` range over the resulting shifted partition of `Z^3` into half-open `r`-cubes. For a finite cycle `C`, set

\[
w_Q(C)=\frac{|C\cap Q|}{|C|},
\qquad
\mathcal I_r(C,U)=\sum_Q w_Q(C)^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]

`I_r` is the collision probability that two independent uniform cycle vertices fall in the same coarse cube. If they fall in the same cube, their `l_infinity` distance is `<r`; hence pathwise

\[
\mathcal I_r(C,U)
\le
\frac1{|C|^2}
\#\{(u,v)\in C^2:|u-v|_\infty<r\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently, under `p_infty(beta)=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega,U}\mathcal I_r(C_0,U)
\le S_\beta(r).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

There are two useful upper bounds. First, NEW-L64 gives, for `r<=sqrt(beta)`,

\[
S_\beta(r)\le C\beta^{-1/4}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Second, (3.5) and the volume of an `l_infinity` ball give

\[
S_\beta(r)
\le C r^3 c_\beta
\le C(1+\log\beta)\frac{r^3}{\sqrt\beta}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\omega,U}\mathcal I_r(C_0,U)
\le
C\min\left\{
\beta^{-1/4},
(1+\log\beta)\frac{r^3}{\sqrt\beta}
\right\},
\qquad r\le\sqrt\beta.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

In particular, at diffusive block scale `r=floor(c sqrt(beta))` with fixed sufficiently small `c>0`, the first term applies. If

\[
N_{\rm eff}(C,U)=\frac1{\mathcal I_r(C,U)}
\tag{6.7}
\]

is the inverse-participation effective number of occupied boxes, Markov gives, for every fixed `delta>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\omega,U}\left(
N_{\rm eff}(C_0,U)<\beta^{1/4-\delta}
\right)
\le C\beta^{-\delta}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus a hypothetical all-finite phase typically spreads the cycle's mass over at least `beta^{1/4-o(1)}` diffusive boxes in the effective-number sense.

The random grid is intentional: a fixed grid breaks the spatial re-rooting symmetry behind (1.3). The inequality (6.2) itself is valid for every shift, and averaging over an independent shift avoids making any hidden stationarity claim about a fixed grid.

## 7. What these results do and do not achieve

### Proved this run

* `NEW-L65`: under `p_infty(beta)=0`, the uniform root-cycle Green self-energy is at most
  \[
  C\beta^{-1/3}(1+\log\beta)^{1/3}.
  \]
* `NEW-L66`: the finite root cycle then has Green capacity at least `beta^{1/3-o(1)}` with probability tending to one.
* `NEW-L67`: the cycle contains polynomially many mutually separated representatives; at scale `beta^{3/4-epsilon}` the count is at least `beta^{epsilon/2}` with high probability, and at diffusive scale it is at least `beta^{1/8}` with high probability.
* `NEW-L68`: the cycle's mass has inverse-participation effective support at least `beta^{1/4-o(1)}` across diffusive random-grid boxes.

These are exact consequences of the already proved finite-cycle autocorrelation/form-factor theorem plus the inverse-cycle-length estimate. No new numerical experiment is needed for their validity.

### Not proved

* No positive value of `p_infty(beta)` is established at a new finite `beta`.
* Large capacity, many separated representatives, and many occupied blocks are **necessary** conditions for a hypothetical all-finite phase, not contradictions by themselves.
* The archived random-cut connected-block theorem cannot yet simply be applied to the random occupied boxes from NEW-L67/68. Those boxes are selected anticipatively from the final cycle, and that theorem controls a cycle confined to a prescribed/history-measurable union of blocks. A union bound over all possible occupied-box sets would discard the heat-kernel geometry and is not justified as a closing argument.
* No monotonicity in `beta` is used or inferred.
* Chronological paths are never identified with permutation cycles.

### Failed shortcut explicitly recorded

It is tempting to combine “many separated boxes” with the particle-number-uniform random-cut theorem and declare an exponential contradiction. This is currently invalid because the final occupied-box family is itself a random function of the whole permutation slab, while the existing conditional random-cut theorem requires admissible no-peeking/history-measurable blocks. This anticipativity gap is the exact obstacle; the present report does not conceal it.

## 8. Sharpened next attack

The previous bottleneck after NEW-L64 was to obtain an independent lower bound on local cycle mass. NEW-L65--68 refine that into a more combinatorial target.

Under the contradiction hypothesis `p_infty(beta)=0`, a typical finite cycle simultaneously has:

1. very small Green self-energy;
2. capacity at least `beta^{1/3-o(1)}`;
3. polynomially many mutually separated representatives;
4. effective mass on at least `beta^{1/4-o(1)}` diffusive blocks.

The most useful next theorem would be a **nonanticipative extraction lemma**: during an admissible cyclic-time/no-peeking exploration, select `k=beta^c` still-unrevealed diffusive blocks which the eventual finite root cycle must visit, while retaining sufficiently small conditional inter-block heat. The archived conditional random-cut theorem could then charge an exponential cost in `k`.

A second route is a direct potential-theoretic cluster estimate: show that every finite permutation cycle with Green capacity `K` must pay at least `exp(-cK^a)` (or another summable cost) under the interchange law once `K` exceeds the scale forced by NEW-L66. The partition-lattice unicyclic witness theorem (`NEW-L49--51`) identifies the required closed crossing geometry, while NEW-L65 supplies the correct potential kernel against which to measure it.

A third route is to use capacity as a restart variable. Expose a first orbit window only until it creates a high-capacity set of candidate anchors; then seek a quenched escape estimate from that set in a fresh time phase. This may be closer to the local-escape architecture of Elboim--Sly while avoiding complete past-avoidance, which is too expensive in `d=3`.
