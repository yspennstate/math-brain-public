# 2. New representation-theoretic reduction of the twisted trace

This section is new mathematical work in the present run.

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite graph with \(|V|=N\), and let

\[
\Omega_\ell
=
\{(x_1,\ldots,x_\ell)\in V^\ell:
 x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal H_\ell=\ell^2(\Omega_\ell)
\]

and let \(K_t^{(\ell)}=e^{tL_\ell}\) be the ordered \(\ell\)-tag interchange semigroup.

The symmetric group \(S_\ell\) acts on the right by permuting the label coordinates. Write this action as \(U_\sigma\). Since physical edge transpositions act simultaneously on positions and do not depend on the names of the tagged labels,

\[
[L_\ell,U_\sigma]=0
\qquad(\sigma\in S_\ell).
\]

Therefore complete reducibility gives an orthogonal decomposition

\[
\mathcal H_\ell
\cong
\bigoplus_{\mu\vdash\ell}
S^\mu\otimes M_\mu,
\tag{2.1}
\]

where \(S^\mu\) is the irreducible \(S_\ell\)-module of shape \(\mu\), and \(M_\mu\) is its multiplicity space. By Schur's lemma,

\[
K_t^{(\ell)}
=
\bigoplus_{\mu\vdash\ell}
I_{S^\mu}\otimes A_{\mu,t}
\tag{2.2}
\]

for positive self-adjoint operators \(A_{\mu,t}\) on the multiplicity spaces.

Let \(c_\ell=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ \ell)\). The cyclic label shift in the twisted-trace formula is \(U_{c_\ell}\).

## NEW-L1 — exact hook-sector trace formula

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(K_t^{(\ell)}U_{c_\ell})
=
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
(-1)^j Z_{\ell,j}(t),
}
\tag{NEW-L1}
\]

where

\[
Z_{\ell,j}(t)
:=
\operatorname{Tr}A_{(\ell-j,1^j),t}
\ge0.
\]

Equivalently, using the already-backfilled cycle-count identity,

\[
\boxed{
\ell\,\mathbb E s_\ell(t)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^jZ_{\ell,j}(t).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Proof

From (2.1)–(2.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(K_t^{(\ell)}U_{c_\ell})
&=
\sum_{\mu\vdash\ell}
\operatorname{Tr}_{S^\mu}(\rho_\mu(c_\ell))
\operatorname{Tr}_{M_\mu}(A_{\mu,t})\\
&=
\sum_{\mu\vdash\ell}
\chi_\mu(c_\ell)\operatorname{Tr}A_{\mu,t}.
\end{aligned}
\]

By the Murnaghan–Nakayama rule, the character of an \(\ell\)-cycle is zero unless \(\mu\) is a hook

\[
\mu=(\ell-j,1^j),
\]

and in that case

\[
\chi_{(\ell-j,1^j)}(c_\ell)=(-1)^j.
\]

This proves NEW-L1. \(\square\)

### Why this is useful

The full \(\ell\)-tag state space contains many irreducible label-symmetry sectors, but the finite-cycle statistic sees **only hooks**. Thus a future uniform trace comparison does not require a uniform pointwise heat-kernel theorem for the entire labeled configuration process. It requires control of one alternating family of \(\ell\) hook-sector heat traces.

This is consistent with the hook structure appearing in Alon–Kozma's character analysis. It is not claimed as a new representation-theory theorem outside this project.

# 3. Pointwise character identity explaining the hook cancellation

The hook representation

\[
S^{(\ell-j,1^j)}
\]

is naturally isomorphic to

\[
\bigwedge^j \mathrm{Std}_\ell,
\]

where \(\mathrm{Std}_\ell\) is the \((\ell-1)\)-dimensional standard representation of \(S_\ell\). Therefore, for every \(h\in S_\ell\),

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
(-1)^j\chi_{(\ell-j,1^j)}(h)
=
\det(I-h|_{\mathrm{Std}_\ell}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

## NEW-L2 — exact cycle detector

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}
(-1)^j\chi_{(\ell-j,1^j)}(h)
=
\ell\,\mathbf1_{\{h\text{ is one }\ell\text{-cycle}\}}.
}
\tag{NEW-L2}
\]

### Proof

If \(h\) has \(c\ge2\) permutation cycles, the permutation representation has eigenvalue \(1\) with multiplicity \(c\). Removing the trivial one-dimensional subspace leaves eigenvalue \(1\) in the standard representation, so the determinant in (3.1) is zero.

If \(h\) is a single \(\ell\)-cycle, the standard-representation eigenvalues are

\[
\zeta,\zeta^2,\ldots,\zeta^{\ell-1},
\qquad
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/\ell}.
\]
Hence

\[
\det(I-h|_{\mathrm{Std}_\ell})
=
\prod_{k=1}^{\ell-1}(1-\zeta^k)
=\ell.
\]

This proves NEW-L2. \(\square\)

NEW-L2 gives a direct structural explanation for the alternating hook trace: it is the exterior-algebra detector for a single cycle on the tagged labels.

# 4. Exact fully antisymmetric sector = free fermions

Let \(L_1\) be the one-particle continuous-time random-walk generator on \(V\):

\[
L_1
=
\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\in E}(T_e-I),
\]

where \(T_e\) exchanges the basis vectors \(e_u,e_v\) and fixes all other vertex basis vectors.

The fully antisymmetric label sector of \(\mathcal H_\ell\), corresponding to the hook \((1^\ell)\), is naturally identified with

\[
\bigwedge^\ell \ell^2(V).
\]

## NEW-L3 — exact antisymmetric generator identity

On this sector,

\[
\boxed{
L_\ell^{\rm alt}
=d\Gamma(L_1),
}
\tag{NEW-L3}
\]

where \(d\Gamma(L_1)\) is the fermionic second quantization, i.e. the sum of \(L_1\) acting in each wedge coordinate.

### Edge-by-edge proof

Fix an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\) and a wedge basis vector representing an \(\ell\)-element occupied set.

* If neither \(u\) nor \(v\) is occupied, both the interchange edge operator and \(d\Gamma(T_e-I)\) contribute zero.
* If exactly one endpoint is occupied, the edge transposition moves that occupied basis vector to the other endpoint. The wedge edge contribution is exactly “moved wedge minus original wedge”, which is the same contribution from the one-particle generator in the occupied coordinate.
* If both \(u\) and \(v\) are occupied, the interchange edge transposition swaps the two occupied wedge vectors and therefore multiplies the wedge by \(-1\); its generator contribution is \(-2\) times the wedge. In \(d\Gamma(T_e-I)\), the \(u\)-coordinate and \(v\)-coordinate terms each contribute \(-1\) times the original wedge, again totaling \(-2\).

Summing over edges proves NEW-L3. \(\square\)

Let

\[
0=\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_{N-1}
\]

be the eigenvalues of \(-L_1\), and set

\[
z_i=e^{-t\lambda_i}.
\]

Then the antisymmetric-sector heat trace is exactly the elementary symmetric polynomial

\[
\boxed{
Z_{\ell,\ell-1}(t)
=
e_\ell(z_0,\ldots,z_{N-1}).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since an \(\ell\)-cycle has sign \((-1)^{\ell-1}\), its contribution to NEW-L1 is

\[
(-1)^{\ell-1}e_\ell(z).
\]

Thus one endpoint of the hook-sector alternating sum is completely explicit from the one-particle spectrum.

# 5. The trivial hook sector = simple exclusion

The other endpoint hook is \((\ell)\), the trivial representation of the label-permutation group. Its functions are symmetric in the \(\ell\) tagged coordinates and therefore identify exactly with functions on \(\ell\)-element subsets of \(V\). The induced process is the \(\ell\)-particle symmetric simple exclusion process.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
Z_{\ell,0}(t)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(e^{tL_{\mathrm{SEP},\ell}}\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The complete cycle statistic is therefore an alternating interpolation between the ordinary exclusion heat trace at one end and an explicit free-fermion heat trace at the other, with the intermediate hook sectors carrying the nontrivial label-symmetry corrections.

# 7. General hook-sector positivity inequalities

Since each \(A_{\mu,t}\) is positive self-adjoint,

\[
Z_{\ell,j}(t)\ge0.
\]

NEW-L1 and the fact that \(s_\ell(t)\ge0\) imply the exact alternating inequality

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j\text{ even}}Z_{\ell,j}(t)
\ge
\sum_{j\text{ odd}}Z_{\ell,j}(t).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(\ell=3\), for example,

\[
Z_{3,0}(t)+Z_{3,2}(t)
\ge Z_{3,1}(t).
\]

This by itself is not sharp enough for the Tóth target, but it shows that the required cancellations are constrained by positivity rather than being arbitrary character cancellation.

in the relevant thermodynamic normalization, with one \(A<\infty\) independent of \(\ell\).

The new load-bearing problem is to control the **intermediate hook sectors and their alternating cancellation** uniformly in \(\ell\).

# 9. Relation to Alon–Kozma representation formulas

Alon–Kozma, arXiv:1009.3723, decompose the class function counting \(k\)-cycles in \(S_N\) into irreducible \(S_N\)-characters. Their Theorem 3 gives coefficients in \(\{0,\pm1\}\); for the special case \(k=N\), only hook-shaped Young diagrams appear, and their Lemma 4 gives the familiar alternating hook expansion.

The present NEW-L1 is a label-side decomposition of the ordered \(\ell\)-tag semigroup. It is compatible with their vertex-side character picture but does not automatically turn their finite-graph spectral estimates into HOOK-A.

Their companion paper arXiv:1003.1710 studies the Aldous order on irreducible representations. Hook-shaped **vertex** representations have particularly explicit spectral structure, related to exterior powers of the standard representation. A promising next step is to write the label-hook multiplicity spaces \(M_{(\ell-j,1^j)}\) as sums of vertex irreducibles via the commuting \(S_N\)- and \(S_\ell\)-actions, and then test whether known Aldous-order comparisons control the alternating aggregate rather than each term separately.

No such uniform aggregate bound is proved here.

# 10. Why a naive termwise estimate is unlikely to suffice

The hook traces \(Z_{\ell,j}\) are individually nonnegative and their multiplicity dimensions can be large. Bounding

\[
\sum_jZ_{\ell,j}
\]

instead of the alternating sum would generally destroy the exact cancellations detected by NEW-L2. The \(C_4\) obstruction backfilled in BF4 already shows that even the simplest constant-one comparison with independent walkers fails.

Therefore the next approach should preserve the alternating hook structure throughout. A Duhamel/cluster expansion or representation-theoretic comparison that takes absolute values too early is likely to lose the needed \(\ell\)-uniformity.

# 11. A concrete next representation-theoretic attack

The ordered-injection space \(\Omega_\ell\) carries commuting actions of the vertex permutation group \(S_N\) and the label permutation group \(S_\ell\). The stabilizer of the canonical injection is the product of the unused-vertex permutations with a diagonally embedded label permutation group. Standard branching/Littlewood–Richardson theory should therefore decompose each label multiplicity space \(M_\mu\) into vertex irreducibles with coefficients governed by adding a horizontal strip of size \(N-\ell\).

The next run should make this bimodule decomposition completely explicit and verify the multiplicities. For each label hook

\[
\mu_j=(\ell-j,1^j),
\]
write

\[
Z_{\ell,j}(t)
=
\sum_{\lambda\vdash N}
 m_{\lambda,j}\,
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{tL_G^{\lambda}}),
\tag{11.1}

with exact nonnegative integers \(m_{\lambda,j}\). Then interchange the alternating sum:

\[
\ell\mathbb Es_\ell(t)
=
\sum_{\lambda\vdash N}

\operatorname{Tr}(e^{tL_G^\lambda}),

=
\sum_j(-1)^jm_{\lambda,j}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

The coefficients \(b_{\lambda,\ell}\) should reproduce the \(0,\pm1\) character coefficients of Alon–Kozma's cycle-count decomposition. Proving this directly would connect HOOK-A to their exact vertex-representation formula while retaining the cancellation transparently.

After that, the concrete analytic question is whether the aggregate

\[
\sum_\lambda b_{\lambda,\ell}
\operatorname{Tr}(e^{\beta L_G^\lambda})
\]

can be compared to the one-particle return trace with a constant independent of \(\ell\) in transient lattice geometry. Aldous-order information might then be applied only where the coefficient signs permit it.

# 12. Alternative next attack: connected defect expansion in hook-supertrace form

NEW-L2 suggests an exterior-algebra supertrace formulation. Instead of expanding the whole labeled heat kernel, write the cycle count as the supertrace over the standard-label exterior algebra. Then expand the interchange generator around an independent-walker or exclusion reference semigroup while keeping the supertrace intact.

Pair-adjacency interactions are local defects. In \(d=3\), the chronological relative two-particle Green mass is finite, as recorded and proved in the repository peer audit. The goal is to show that **connected** defect clusters renormalize the cycle supertrace by a finite factor, while disconnected insertions cancel in the exterior supertrace.

This is more precise than the previous generic cluster proposal: the hook supertrace gives an algebraic mechanism by which extensive-in-\(\ell\) disconnected contributions might cancel before any absolute-value estimate is taken.

This remains a program, not a proof.
