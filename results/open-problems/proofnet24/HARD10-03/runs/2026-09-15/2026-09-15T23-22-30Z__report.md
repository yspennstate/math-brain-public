# 3. Retained exact phase identity and fixed-lag endpoint bound

Write

\[
P:=\pi_\beta,
\qquad Q_s:=\pi_s,
\qquad 0\le s\le\beta.
\]

For integer `r>=1`, define the same-phase lag-`r` separation

\[
D_{r,s}:=Q_s(P^r0)-Q_s0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The archived cyclic phase-invariance theorem gives, for every fixed finite collection of lags and every deterministic phase `s`,

\[
(D_{r_1,s},\ldots,D_{r_m,s})
\stackrel d=
(P^{r_1}0,\ldots,P^{r_m}0).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The archived fixed-`r` tagged-kernel theorem gives, in every fixed `d>=2`,

\[
\mathbb P(P^r0=x)
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{-d/2}
\exp\left(-c_{r,d}|x|^2/\beta\right),
\qquad \beta\ge1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Only fixed `r` is asserted; the constants are not uniform in `r`.

For `r=1`, the earlier final-successor report proved the stronger exact entry-rate formula and in `d=3` the full-phase adjacency hitting estimate `O(beta^{-1/2})`. Before this run, the analogous **hitting** estimate for every fixed `r>1` was still open; only adjacency occupation was archived.

# 4. NEW-L38 — fixed deterministic transposition perturbations preserve fixed-lag anti-concentration

Let `tau_e` be the transposition of the endpoints of an arbitrary nearest-neighbor edge `e`. Let

\[
F=P\tau_e
\]

(the same statement holds for `tau_e P`).

## Theorem NEW-L38 — PROVED

For every fixed integer `r>=1` and fixed `d>=2`, there are constants `C_{r,d},c_{r,d}>0`, independent of the edge `e`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(F^r0=x)
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{-d/2}
\exp\left(-c_{r,d}|x|^2/\beta\right)
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

up to harmless adjustment of the Gaussian center by a bounded amount. In particular, for every fixed finite set `A`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_e\mathbb P((P\tau_e)^r0\in A)
\le C_{r,d,A}\beta^{-d/2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
x_0=0,\qquad x_j=F^j0.
\]

First consider the event that

\[
x_0,\ldots,x_r
\]

are pairwise distinct. Since

\[
F(x_j)=P(\tau_e x_j)=x_{j+1},
\]

the event for a fixed tuple is exactly the chronological `r`-tag interchange transition

\[
(\tau_e x_0,\ldots,\tau_e x_{r-1})
\longmapsto
(x_1,\ldots,x_r).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `tau_e` is a bijection, the starting labels are distinct. The archived fixed-`r` heat-kernel bound therefore gives

\[
Q_\beta^{(r)}
((\tau_e x_0,\ldots,\tau_e x_{r-1}),
 (x_1,\ldots,x_r))
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{-rd/2}
\exp\left[-c_{r,d}
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}|x_{j+1}-\tau_e x_j|^2}{\beta}
\right].
\tag{4.4}
\]

The transposition moves any vertex by at most one lattice step. Moreover, because the `x_j` are distinct, at most two of them can lie in the two-point support of `tau_e`. Hence the increment list in (4.4) differs from the ordinary polygon increment list

\[
x_{j+1}-x_j
\]

in only boundedly many entries, each by a bounded vector. A discrete Gaussian convolution over `x_1,...,x_{r-1}` therefore yields

\[
C_{r,d}\beta^{-d/2}
\exp(-c_{r,d}|x_r|^2/\beta).
\tag{4.5}
\]

It remains to handle an orbit repetition before time `r`. If the first repeat occurs at length `ell<=r`, then the root lies in an `ell`-cycle of `F`. For a fixed cycle tuple the constraints are

\[
P(\tau_e x_j)=x_{j+1},
\qquad j\pmod\ell.
\]

This is an exact `ell`-tag chronological transition. Applying the fixed-`ell` heat-kernel bound and summing the `ell-1` free cycle vertices leaves one residual `d`-dimensional Gaussian factor, hence total mass `O_{r,d}(beta^{-d/2})`. Summing the finitely many `ell<=r` proves (4.1)--(4.2).

The proof for `tau_e P` is identical after moving `tau_e^{-1}=tau_e` to the terminal coordinates.

### Scope

NEW-L38 is a **fixed-perturbation/fixed-lag** result. It does not give uniformity as the number of forced bars or the lag grows.

# 6. NEW-L39 — cyclic Palm-rotation lemma for lag-pair entry intensity

The deterministic phase rotation used in the archived BF-L6 proof extends to the Campbell/Palm measure of a distinguished Poisson bar.

More explicitly, remove one distinguished bar at physical phase `s`, rotate the remaining Poisson slab so that `s` becomes phase zero, and spatially re-root by the pre-bar position `Q_{s-}0`. The remaining clocks still have the original product-Poisson law; the distinguished bar becomes a phase-zero bar on the correspondingly translated edge. The same mass-transport argument as in BF-L6 handles the random spatial re-rooting.

The property

> “this distinguished bar changes the lag-`r` pair from nonadjacent to adjacent”

is covariant under this time rotation and spatial re-rooting. Consequently its Campbell intensity is independent of the physical phase `s`.

## Lemma NEW-L39 — PROVED

If `lambda_{r,beta}` is the expected entry-bar intensity per unit phase time, then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E N_{r,\beta}^{ent}
=
\beta\lambda_{r,\beta},
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

and `lambda_{r,beta}` can be evaluated at phase zero under a single forced Poisson bar.

### Phase-zero geometry

At phase zero the two tracked labels start at vertices

\[
0\quad\text{and}\quad b=F^r0,
\]

where, under a Palm insertion on edge `e`, the full one-period permutation has the form

\[
F=R\tau_e
\]

with `R` an ordinary duration-`beta` interchange permutation (the distinction between duration `beta` and deleting a single zero-time point is null).

For the phase-zero bar to turn a **nonadjacent** pair into an adjacent pair, the bar must touch exactly one of these two labels. Therefore:

1. `b` lies in a fixed finite lattice neighborhood of the origin (in fact L1 distance at most two suffices); and
2. the distinguished edge `e` belongs to a fixed finite set of edges inside that same neighborhood.

Thus, using NEW-L38,

\[
\lambda_{r,\beta}
\le
C_d\sup_{e}
\mathbb P((R\tau_e)^r0\in B_2)
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{-d/2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining (6.1) and (6.2),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E N_{r,\beta}^{ent}
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{1-d/2}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This argument uses the **Palm law of the distinguished bar**, not an unjustified predictable-intensity calculation for the anticipatively selected label `P^r0`.

# 7. NEW-L40 — full-phase fixed-lag contact probability

Combining (5.2), (5.3), and (6.3) gives the main theorem of this run.

## Theorem NEW-L40 — PROVED

For every fixed integer `r>=1` and fixed dimension `d>=3`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists s\in[0,\beta]:
|\pi_s(P^r0)-\pi_s0|_1=1
\right)
\le
C_{r,d}\beta^{1-d/2}
}
\qquad(\beta\ge1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

In dimension three,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(H_{r,\beta})
\le
C_r\beta^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This extends the earlier exact `r=1` final-successor hitting estimate to **every fixed orbit lag**, after arbitrary previous cyclic entanglement.

### Fixed finite collections of lags

For every fixed `R`, a union bound gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists 1\le r\le R,
\exists s\in[0,\beta]:
|\pi_s(P^r0)-\pi_s0|_1=1
\right)
\le
C_R\beta^{-1/2}
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

in `d=3`.

By the measure-preserving cyclic point shift from the archived baseline, the same estimate holds after re-rooting at any deterministic cyclic index `k`. Hence any **fixed finite** block of orbit strands has probability `1-O(beta^{-1/2})` of containing no same-phase adjacent pair throughout the whole slab.

No independence of those strands is asserted.

# 8. Why NEW-L40 is not already the full sparse-defect theorem

The earlier desired bound was substantially stronger:

\[
\mathbb P(H_{r,\beta})
\stackrel{?}{\lesssim}
\beta^{-1/2}r^{-3/2},
\tag{8.1}
\]

or another estimate summable in `r` with constants uniform over arbitrarily large lags.

NEW-L40 proves the correct `beta^{-1/2}` scale for each fixed lag but its constant `C_r` comes from fixed-`r` configuration-space heat-kernel estimates and may deteriorate rapidly with `r`. Therefore one cannot sum (7.2) over the infinitely many strands in a putative finite cycle.

This is the exact remaining quantitative obstruction on the contact side.

The distinction matters: the theorem is genuinely **post-entanglement** and full-slab, but only fixed-lag.

The previous checkpoint proved, for every fixed `r`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Cycle}_P(0)\subseteq S,
L_\beta(0)>2r
\right)

NEW-L40 gives a complementary dynamical statement: not only are finitely many cyclic orbit anchors spatially anti-concentrated, but for any fixed set of orbit lags their **entire synchronized chronological strands** almost surely avoid adjacency as `beta->infinity`.

Together, the retained picture is now:

* fixed finite orbit windows become spatially diffuse;
* fixed finite sets of same-phase strands have vanishing contact probability;
* a hypothetical all-finite regime at large fixed `beta` must therefore be sustained by genuinely large-lag, collective correlations.

That is precisely the regime where a summable-in-lag estimate such as (8.1) would be decisive.

# 10. A useful robustness corollary

The proof of NEW-L38 did not use any special location of the forced nearest-neighbor transposition. Therefore the fixed-lag endpoint anti-concentration is stable under one arbitrary deterministic forced bar:

\[
\sup_{e,x}
\mathbb P((P\tau_e)^r0=x)
\le C_{r,d}\beta^{-d/2}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

This is useful for future Palm/Mecke arguments: inserting one distinguished bar does not destroy the fixed-lag `beta^{-d/2}` scale. The same proof extends to any **fixed finite number** of deterministic bounded-range transpositions, with a constant depending on their number and on `r`; no growing-number uniformity is claimed here.

# 11. Failed shortcut explicitly avoided

It would be invalid to argue that, since each selected strand is marginally an SRW, entry bars occur at rate `4d` times adjacency occupation. The second label `P^r0` is selected using the entire Poisson slab and is anticipative. Ordinary predictable-intensity compensation cannot be applied without modification.

NEW-L39 avoids this error by working under the actual Campbell/Palm measure, rotating the distinguished bar to phase zero, and then using the fixed-transposition perturbation estimate NEW-L38.

1. use the pre-first-interaction driver calculation, which already carries the desired `r^{-3/2}` heat-kernel factor for a lag-`r` pair;
