# 2. Setup for the new spatial results

Fix \(\beta>0\) and a finite set \(S\subset\mathbb Z^3\). Let

\[
K_{\beta,S}:=p_\beta[S,S]
\]

be the principal matrix of the one-particle continuous-time simple-random-walk heat kernel. The walk jumps at rate one along each of its six neighboring edges.

Because \(p_\beta\) is positive and self-adjoint on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z^3)\), the finite matrix \(K_{\beta,S}\) is positive semidefinite. Since \(S\) is finite and the walk has positive probability to be outside \(S\) at time \(\beta\) from every starting point in \(S\), its spectral radius is strictly below one.

Write its eigenvalues as

\[
0\le \theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{|S|}<1.
\]

Translation invariance gives the exact trace

\[
\tau_{\beta,S}
:=
\operatorname{Tr}K_{\beta,S}
=
|S|p_\beta(0,0).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let

\[
E_{\beta,S}
:=
\{\exists\text{ a permutation cycle of }\pi_\beta
\text{ wholly contained in }S\}.
\]

By (1.1),

\[
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S})
=
1-\prod_i(1-\theta_i).
\tag{2.2}
\]

# 3. NEW-L8 — universal two-sided finite-set cycle-seed bound

## Theorem NEW-L8 — PROVED

For every finite \(S\subset\mathbb Z^3\) and every \(\beta>0\),

\[
\boxed{
1-e^{-\tau_{\beta,S}}
\le
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S})
\le
\min\{1,\tau_{\beta,S}\},
\qquad
\tau_{\beta,S}=|S|p_\beta(0,0).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

For the lower bound, use

\[
1-x\le e^{-x},\qquad x\in[0,1].
\]

Then

\[
\det(I-K_{\beta,S})
=
\prod_i(1-\theta_i)
\le
\exp\left(-\sum_i\theta_i\right)
=e^{-\tau_{\beta,S}}.
\]

Applying (2.2),

\[
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S})
\ge1-e^{-\tau_{\beta,S}}.
\]

For the upper bound, for numbers \(a_i\in[0,1]\),

\[
1-\prod_i(1-a_i)
\le
\sum_i a_i.
\]

Applying this to \(a_i=\theta_i\) gives

\[
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S})
\le
\sum_i\theta_i
=
\tau_{\beta,S}.
\]

A probability is at most one, proving (3.1). \(\square\)

## Corollary NEW-L8a — dilute-volume asymptotic

Suppose \(\beta\to\infty\) and \(S=S_\beta\) is any deterministic finite set satisfying

\[
|S_\beta|=o(\beta^{3/2}).
\]

The continuous-time local central limit theorem for the rate-one-per-edge walk gives

\[
p_\beta(0,0)
=
(4\pi\beta)^{-3/2}(1+o(1)).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus \(\tau_{\beta,S}\to0\). Since

\[
\tau-\frac{\tau^2}{2}
\le
1-e^{-\tau}
\le
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S})
\le
\tau,
\]

we get the shape-independent asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S_\beta})
=
\frac{|S_\beta|}{(4\pi\beta)^{3/2}}
\,(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus on every deterministic set whose volume is little-oh of the diffusive volume \(\beta^{3/2}\), the probability of containing an entire permutation cycle has an exact first-order asymptotic depending only on the number of vertices, not on the shape of the set.

## Corollary NEW-L8b — super-diffusive-volume existence

If

\[
|S_\beta|p_\beta(0,0)\longrightarrow\infty,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_{\beta,S_\beta})\longrightarrow1.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular, since \(p_\beta(0,0)\asymp\beta^{-3/2}\), any deterministic finite sets with

\[
|S_\beta|\gg\beta^{3/2}
\]
contain at least one whole finite permutation cycle with probability tending to one.

This statement is deliberately about **some** cycle in the set, not the root cycle. Fixed points are allowed and can contribute substantially.

## Corollary NEW-L8c — sharpened root-confinement asymptotic

If \(0\in S_\beta\) and \(|S_\beta|=o(\beta^{3/2})\), then the root-cycle event is a subset of \(E_{\beta,S_\beta}\), hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq S_\beta
\bigr)
\le
\frac{|S_\beta|}{(4\pi\beta)^{3/2}}
(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

For \(S_\beta=B_{R_\beta}\) with \(R_\beta=o(\sqrt\beta)\), this supplies the explicit leading constant behind the previous \(O(|S|\beta^{-3/2})\) confinement estimate.

# 4. Exact fugacity determinant for cycles wholly in a finite set

The multivariate determinant identity from the previous main report can be specialized more generally than at fugacity one.

For \(q\in[0,1]\), define

\[
Z_{\beta,S}(q)
:=
\mathbb E
\prod_{\substack{C\text{ cycle of }\pi_\beta\\C\subseteq S}}
\left(1-q^{|C|}\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

A cycle which is not wholly contained in \(S\) contributes factor one when the diagonal spatial fugacity is set to \(q\) on \(S\) and zero outside. Therefore the earlier master determinant identity gives:

## NEW-L9 — PROVED

For every finite \(S\subset\mathbb Z^3\), every \(\beta>0\), and every \(q\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
Z_{\beta,S}(q)
=
\det(I-qK_{\beta,S}).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
-\log Z_{\beta,S}(q)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^m}{m}
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,S}^m).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The series converges absolutely for \(q\le1\) because the spectral radius of \(K_{\beta,S}\) is strictly below one.

At \(q=1\), (4.2) reduces to the exact no-contained-cycle probability in (1.1).

# 5. NEW-L10 — thermodynamic cycle-pressure asymptotic in large boxes

Let

\[
B_R=[-R,R]^3\cap\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad
K_{\beta,R}=p_\beta[B_R,B_R].
\]

Consider a joint limit

\[
\beta\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{R}{\sqrt\beta}\to\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus the box is large compared with the one-period diffusive scale.

## Theorem NEW-L10 — PROVED

For every fixed \(q\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\beta^{3/2}}{|B_R|}
\left[-\log Z_{\beta,B_R}(q)\right]
\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
\operatorname{Li}_{5/2}(q),
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
\operatorname{Li}_{5/2}(q)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{q^m}{m^{5/2}}.
\]

In particular, at \(q=1\),

\[
\boxed{
-\log
\mathbb P\bigl(
\text{no }\pi_\beta\text{-cycle is wholly contained in }B_R
\bigr)
=
\left[
\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}+o(1)
\right]
|B_R|\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
\text{no whole cycle in }B_R
\bigr)
=
\exp\left\{
-\left[
\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}+o(1)
\right]
|B_R|\beta^{-3/2}
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Start from the exact trace expansion (4.3):

\[
-\log Z_{\beta,B_R}(q)
=
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{q^m}{m}
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,R}^m).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For every \(m\ge1\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,R}^m)
=
\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1}\in B_R}
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}
p_\beta(x_j,x_{j+1}),
\qquad x_m=x_0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the weight of \(m\)-step \(\beta\)-skeleton loops whose sampled positions remain in \(B_R\).

Dropping the constraint that the intermediate sampled positions stay in the box gives the uniform upper bound

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,R}^m)
\le
|B_R|p_{m\beta}(0,0).
\tag{5.7}
\]

For each fixed \(m\), the constraint is asymptotically negligible under (5.1). To see this, fix \(A>0\) and restrict starting points to the inner box whose distance to the boundary is at least

\[
A\sqrt{m\beta}.
\]

The fraction of excluded starting points is

\[
O_m\left(\frac{A\sqrt\beta}{R}\right)=o(1).
\]

For an inner starting point \(x\), the unconstrained loop weight is \(p_{m\beta}(0,0)\). The weight of loops for which at least one sampled intermediate position lies outside \(B_R\) is bounded, by a union bound over the finitely many intermediate times and the standard Gaussian heat-kernel estimate, by

\[
C_m e^{-c_mA^2}p_{m\beta}(0,0).
\]

Therefore, first taking the joint limit (5.1) and then \(A\to\infty\),

\[
\frac{
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,R}^m)
}{
|B_R|p_{m\beta}(0,0)
}
\longrightarrow1
\qquad(m\text{ fixed}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

The rate-one-per-neighbor continuous-time walk has Fourier exponent

\[
2\sum_{j=1}^3(1-\cos\theta_j),
\]
so the local central limit theorem gives

\[
\beta^{3/2}p_{m\beta}(0,0)
\longrightarrow
(4\pi m)^{-3/2}
\qquad(m\text{ fixed}).
\tag{5.9}
\]

For domination, the standard heat-kernel upper bound and (5.7) give

\[
0\le
\frac{\beta^{3/2}}{|B_R|}
\frac{q^m}{m}
\operatorname{Tr}(K_{\beta,R}^m)
\le
C\frac{q^m}{m^{5/2}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

The right side is summable uniformly for \(q\in[0,1]\). Dominated convergence in the series (5.5), using (5.8) and (5.9), yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim
\frac{\beta^{3/2}}{|B_R|}
[-\log Z_{\beta,B_R}(q)]
&=
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{q^m}{m}
(4\pi m)^{-3/2}\\
&=
(4\pi)^{-3/2}
\operatorname{Li}_{5/2}(q).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (5.2). Setting \(q=1\) gives (5.3)–(5.4). \(\square\)

# 6. Interpretation and relevance to the Tóth target

NEW-L8 and NEW-L10 identify the natural **spatial cycle-seed volume scale** as

\[
\beta^{3/2}.
\]

At volume \(o(\beta^{3/2})\), the probability that a deterministic set contains an entire finite cycle is asymptotic to

\[
|S|(4\pi\beta)^{-3/2}.
\]

At volumes much larger than \(\beta^{3/2}\), the probability of containing at least one whole finite cycle tends to one. For boxes much larger than the diffusive linear scale \(\sqrt\beta\), NEW-L10 gives the precise logarithmic rate and the polylogarithmic fugacity pressure.

This is useful structural information, but it is not itself evidence that the **root** is in an infinite cycle. In particular:

* the event in NEW-L8/NEW-L10 allows fixed points and other small finite cycles;
* finite cycles can coexist with infinite cycles;
* even if the root cycle is finite, it need not be contained in a box of radius comparable to \(\sqrt\beta\);
* for fixed \(\beta\), every finite root cycle is contained in some finite set, and the upper bounds become trivial as the set grows.

Thus these results do not close the conjecture.

They do sharpen the multiscale picture: the determinant identity supplies an exact solvable spatial observable at the same diffusive scale on which the unresolved cyclic walk must be controlled. Any proof of the target must accommodate a sparse but nonvanishing field of finite-cycle seeds rather than trying to prove global absence of finite cycles.
