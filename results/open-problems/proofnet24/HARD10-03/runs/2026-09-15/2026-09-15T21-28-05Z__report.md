# 3. CORRECTION C1 — the prior off-diagonal orbit-resolvent interpretation is false

The adaptive determinant identity itself is retained:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\det(I-A\Pi_t)\mid\mathscr F]
=
\det(I-AK_t^{\mathscr F})
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

for the admissible finite no-peeking adaptive exposures proved in the previous checkpoint. Here `Pi_t` is the final permutation matrix and

\[
K_t^{\mathscr F}=\mathbb E[\Pi_t\mid\mathscr F]
\]

is the conditional one-particle kernel.

However, a previous step differentiated (3.1) in an **off-diagonal** matrix direction and interpreted the derivative as a genuine permutation-orbit Green function. That interpretation is incorrect.

### Why it is incorrect

For diagonal `A=diag(a_x)`, one has the exact cycle factorization

\[
\det(I-A\Pi)
=
\prod_{C\text{ cycle of }\Pi}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the source of all valid cycle-event interpretations in the determinant method.

If one perturbs `A` by an off-diagonal matrix unit `E_{uv}`, (3.2) no longer applies: the perturbation inserts a non-permutation splice into the determinant expansion. Algebraically,

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{uv}}
\det(I-A\Pi)
=
-\det(I-A\Pi)
\bigl[\Pi(I-A\Pi)^{-1}\bigr]_{vu},
\tag{3.3}
\]

but the right-hand side is a cofactor/spliced-cycle observable, not the previously claimed indicator-weighted event that `v` lies on the actual permutation cycle through `u`.

This distinction disappears on the **diagonal** `u=v`, because diagonal differentiation is exactly differentiation of one vertex weight in (3.2). It does not disappear for `u ne v`.

# 4. Safe diagonal multivariate framework

The correction suggests staying entirely inside diagonal vertex weights, where the combinatorics is exact.

Fix a finite graph for the algebra, a finite vertex set `S`, and an admissible conditioning sigma-field `F` for which (3.1) holds. Put

\[
K=K_t^{\mathscr F}[S,S].
\]

For vertex weights `a=(a_x)_{x in S}`, let

\[
A(a)=\operatorname{diag}(a_x:x\in S)
\]

and define

\[
F_S(a)
:=
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{C\subseteq S}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right)
\middle|\mathscr F
\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Here the product runs over complete permutation cycles wholly contained in `S`; cycles leaving `S` contribute `1` because weights outside `S` are set to zero.

By the conditional determinant theorem,

\[
\boxed{
F_S(a)=\det(I-A(a)K).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

All new identities below come from mixed **diagonal** derivatives of (4.2).

Assume

\[
D_S:=\det(I-K)>0
\]

and put

\[
R:=K(I-K)^{-1}=(I-K)^{-1}-I.
\tag{4.3}
\]

# 5. NEW-L32 — exact anchored cycle-cluster identity

Let `A0={u_1,...,u_k} subset S` be `k` distinct anchor vertices. For `1<=m<=k`, let `E_{A0,m}` be the event that:

1. there are **exactly `m` complete permutation cycles wholly contained in `S`**;
2. every anchor in `A0` belongs to one of those `m` cycles;
3. every one of the `m` cycles contains at least one anchor from `A0`.

Thus the complete cycles in `S` induce a partition of the anchor set into exactly `m` nonempty groups, with non-anchor vertices allowed on the cycles.

## Theorem NEW-L32 — PROVED

Conditionally on every admissible exploration history for which `D_S>0`,

\[
\boxed{
D_S\det R[A_0,A_0]
=
\sum_{m=1}^{k}
(-1)^{k+m}
\mathbb P(E_{A_0,m}\mid\mathscr F).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof: permutation side

Differentiate (4.1) once with respect to each variable `a_u`, `u in A0`, and then set every `a_x=1`.

A complete cycle not hit by any anchor leaves an undifferentiated factor

\[
1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x
\]

which becomes zero at `a=1`. Hence a nonzero contribution requires every complete cycle in `S` to contain an anchor.

An anchor belonging to no complete cycle in `S` also gives zero. Hence a nonzero contribution requires every anchor to belong to a complete cycle.

If exactly `m` complete cycles survive these requirements, differentiating the factor of each such cycle one or more times gives exactly `-1` at `a=1`, because

\[
\partial_{a_{i_1}}\cdots\partial_{a_{i_r}}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right)
\Big|_{a=1}
=-1
\]

whenever all differentiated vertices lie in `C`. The total derivative therefore has sign `(-1)^m` on `E_{A0,m}`.

Multiplying the mixed derivative by `(-1)^k` gives the right-hand side of (5.1).

### Proof: matrix side

Write `B=I-K`. Perturbing the anchor diagonal weights by `h_u` gives

\[
I-AK
=
B-HK,
\]

where `H` is diagonal and supported on `A0`. Hence

\[
\det(B-HK)
=
D_S\det(I-HKB^{-1}).
\]

The coefficient of `prod_{u in A0} h_u` in the last determinant is

\[
(-1)^k\det R[A_0,A_0].
\]

Therefore

\[
(-1)^k
\partial_{A_0}F_S(1)
=
D_S\det R[A_0,A_0],
\]

which matches the permutation calculation. `square`

# 6. The two-anchor identity and an exact compensation law

For two distinct anchors `u,v`, define

* `E_1(u,v)`: exactly one complete cycle lies in `S`, and it contains both `u` and `v`;
* `E_2(u,v)`: exactly two complete cycles lie in `S`, one contains `u`, and the other contains `v`.

Then NEW-L32 gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_2(u,v)\mid\mathscr F)
-
\mathbb P(E_1(u,v)\mid\mathscr F)
=
D_S
\left(
R_{uu}R_{vv}-R_{uv}R_{vu}
\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is the correct safe replacement for the withdrawn off-diagonal orbit interpretation. It uses only mixed diagonal derivatives.

The formula says that the matrix two-point resolvent determinant is not itself a same-cycle probability. It is a **signed compensation between one-cycle and two-cycle anchored configurations**.

# 7. NEW-L33 — shadow determinantal process in the unconditioned reversible case

Now return to the ordinary unconditioned interchange process on a finite graph with symmetric one-particle heat kernel `K_t`, and restrict it to `S`.

Because

\[
K_t=e^{tL}
\]

is symmetric positive semidefinite with spectrum in `(0,1]`, the finite principal restriction `K=K_t[S,S]` is a positive semidefinite contraction. For a proper finite set in an irreducible graph, its spectral radius is strictly below one.

Hence

\[
R=K(I-K)^{-1}
\]

is positive semidefinite, and the numbers

\[
\boxed{
\nu_S(A_0)
:=
D_S\det R[A_0,A_0],
\qquad A_0\subseteq S,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

form the exact-set probabilities of the finite determinantal point process with `L`-ensemble kernel `R` (equivalently marginal kernel `K`). Indeed

\[
\sum_{A_0\subseteq S}
D_S\det R[A_0,A_0]
=
D_S\det(I+R)=1.
\]

Combining with NEW-L32 gives:

## Theorem NEW-L33 — PROVED

There exists a canonical **shadow DPP** `X_S` with marginal kernel `K_t[S,S]` such that, for every anchor set `A_0 subset S`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(X_S=A_0)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{|A_0|}
(-1)^{|A_0|+m}
\mathbb P(E_{A_0,m}).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is an equality of probabilities after averaging; no pathwise coupling between `X_S` and permutation cycles is claimed.

### Immediate one-anchor consequence

For `A_0={u}`,

\[
\mathbb P(X_S=\{u\})
=
\mathbb P(
C_u\subseteq S,\ N_S=1
).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since the event `X_S={u}` is contained in `u in X_S`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(C_u\subseteq S,\ N_S=1)
\le
K_t(u,u).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

On `Z^3`,

\[
K_\beta(u,u)=p_\beta(0,0)
\sim
(4\pi\beta)^{-3/2}.
\]

Thus the previously obtained `O(beta^{-3/2})` unique-anchor closure bound has, in the unconditioned reversible setting, the clean volume-independent upper bound `p_beta(0,0)`.

# 8. NEW-L34 — pair compensation is positive and second-order

For `u ne v`, positivity of the shadow DPP exact-set probability and the inclusion probability of the two-point set give

\[
0
\le
\mathbb P(E_2(u,v))-
\mathbb P(E_1(u,v))
\le
\mathbb P(u,v\in X_S).
\]

For a DPP with kernel `K`,

\[
\mathbb P(u,v\in X_S)
=
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
K_{uu}&K_{uv}\\
K_{vu}&K_{vv}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\mathbb P(E_2(u,v))-
\mathbb P(E_1(u,v))
\le
K_{uu}K_{vv}-K_{uv}^2.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

For the translation-invariant heat kernel on `Z^3`, `K_{uu}=K_{vv}=p_beta(0,0)`, hence each fixed-pair compensation error is `O(beta^{-3})`.

Summing over `v in S\setminus\{u\}` gives the sharper aggregate identity

\[
\sum_{v\ne u}
\bigl[
\mathbb P(E_2(u,v))-
\mathbb P(E_1(u,v))
\bigr]
\le
p_\beta(0,0)\operatorname{Tr}K
-(K^2)_{uu}
\le
|S|p_\beta(0,0)^2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

If

\[
|S|\le\eta\beta^{3/2},
\]

then in `d=3`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{v\ne u}
\bigl[
\mathbb P(E_2(u,v))-
\mathbb P(E_1(u,v))
\bigr]
\le
C\eta\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

There is an equivalent cycle-size form. On `E_1`, summing over `v ne u` counts `|C_u|-1`; on `E_2`, it counts the size of the *other* complete cycle. Thus

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{N_S=2,C_u\subseteq S\}}
|C_{\rm other}|
\right]
-
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{N_S=1,C_u\subseteq S\}}
(|C_u|-1)
\right]
\le
|S|p_\beta(0,0)^2.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is a new exact/quantitative **cycle-compensation law**: a large unique complete root cycle forces comparably large size-weighted mass in configurations with two complete cycles, up to a genuinely second-order heat-kernel error.

It does not yet bound the total probability that the root cycle closes inside `S`; rather, it reveals the compensating hierarchy that any such argument must control.

# 9. Higher-anchor hierarchy

NEW-L32 gives a full hierarchy. In the unconditioned reversible setting,

\[
\boxed{
0\le
\sum_{m=1}^{k}
(-1)^{k+m}
\mathbb P(E_{A_0,m})
=
\mathbb P(X_S=A_0)
\le
\det K[A_0,A_0].
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

For well-separated anchors at diffusive scale, the determinant on the right is typically of order `beta^{-3k/2}`. Thus the alternating `k`-cycle partition compensation is increasingly small.

The important warning is that (9.1) is an **alternating** statement. Taking absolute values sector-by-sector destroys the cancellation and does not give a bound on one particular `m`.

This hierarchy is a safer and more precise replacement for the incorrect off-diagonal spatial-profile route.

# 11. Failed route / exact lesson

The failed route was:

> differentiate the master determinant in an off-diagonal matrix direction, identify the cofactor resolvent with a real cyclic-orbit Green function, and then compare it to killed random walk.

The first algebraic equality is true; the middle combinatorial identification is false. The finite-state conditional kernel can be non-self-adjoint, making the flaw especially visible: an actual same-cycle membership event is symmetric in two anchors, whereas the off-diagonal resolvent entries need not be symmetric.

The correct replacement is mixed **diagonal** differentiation, which yields signed anchored cycle-cluster probabilities and, in the reversible case, the shadow-DPP hierarchy above.

# 13. Next attack

The shadow-DPP hierarchy suggests a more disciplined replacement for the withdrawn spatial-profile route.

1. **Exploit the compensation cascade.** Sum the `k`-anchor identities over anchor sets containing the root. The right side is controlled by DPP factorial moments, while the permutation side produces alternating size-biased moments of configurations with `1,2,...,k` complete cycles.
