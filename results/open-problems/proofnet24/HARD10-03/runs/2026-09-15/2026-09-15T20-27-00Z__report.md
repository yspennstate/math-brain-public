# 3. NEW-L28 — exact determinant identity under finite adaptive space-time exposure

The previous checkpoint froze a deterministic set of whole-edge Poisson processes. Actual cyclic-time exploration is adaptive and usually reveals only selected pieces of the space-time Poisson environment. The next theorem upgrades the algebra to this setting.

Work on a finite graph `G=(V,E)` for the proof. Each edge carries an independent rate-one Poisson point process on `[0,t]`.

## Definition: finite admissible adaptive exposure

Start with no revealed space-time region. At stage `j=1,...,J`, after observing the complete Poisson configuration on all previously revealed regions, choose a measurable region

\[
B_j\subset E\times[0,t]
\]

that is disjoint from all previously revealed regions and whose choice is measurable with respect to the already revealed data. Reveal the full Poisson configuration in `B_j`. Here `J<infty` is fixed or is an almost surely finite stopping index bounded by a deterministic finite constant.

Let

\[
\mathscr F_J
\]

be the final exploration sigma-field, and let `R_J` be the random revealed region. Conditional on `F_J`, the complement

\[
(E\times[0,t])\setminus R_J
\]

is a deterministic region and carries independent rate-one Poisson processes with the original intensity. This is just repeated independent-increments of Poisson processes; the measurability rule prevents looking into an unrevealed region before deciding to reveal it.

Let `Pi_t` be the final random permutation matrix and

\[
K_t^{\mathscr F}
:=
\mathbb E[\Pi_t\mid\mathscr F_J]
\]

its conditional one-particle kernel.

## Theorem NEW-L28 — PROVED

For every deterministic matrix `A`, almost surely in the adaptive exploration history,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\det(I-A\Pi_t)
\mid\mathscr F_J
\right]
=
\det\left(I-AK_t^{\mathscr F}\right).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Conditional on `F_J`, every revealed Poisson mark is deterministic, and every unrevealed portion of edge-time space still contains an independent Poisson process of intensity one. Thus, after conditioning, the remaining random interchange evolution is a time-inhomogeneous interchange process: at a physical time `s`, only those edges whose point `(e,s)` remains unrevealed carry random rate-one clocks, while already revealed marks are inserted as deterministic transpositions at their known times.

Fix an exterior degree `k`. For an edge transposition matrix `T_e`, on the alternating `k`-label sector one has the exact edgewise identity

\[
\wedge^k T_e-I
=
\mathrm d\Gamma_k(T_e-I).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This can be checked on a wedge basis. If neither endpoint is occupied, both sides vanish. If one endpoint is occupied, both sides replace that basis vector by the other endpoint and subtract the original wedge. If both endpoints are occupied, `wedge^k T_e` changes sign, so the left side is `-2`; on the right, each of the two occupied coordinates contributes `-1`.

Therefore the random unexposed interchange generator on the alternating sector is exactly the fermionic second quantization of the conditional one-particle generator, even when the allowed edge set varies with physical time. The deterministic revealed transpositions satisfy

\[
\wedge^k(T_{e_m}\cdots T_{e_1})
=
(\wedge^kT_{e_m})\cdots(\wedge^kT_{e_1}).
\]

Consequently the conditional alternating-sector propagator is exactly the exterior power of the conditional one-particle propagator:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\wedge^k\Pi_t\mid\mathscr F_J]
=
\wedge^k K_t^{\mathscr F}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Finally use

\[
\det(I-B)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{|V|}(-1)^k\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^kB)
\]

and `wedge^k(A Pi)=(wedge^kA)(wedge^kPi)` to obtain (3.1). `square`

### Scope

`NEW-L28` is proved for finite adaptive explorations obeying the no-peeking rule above. It is not claimed for an arbitrary random revealed set whose selection may depend on unseen Poisson points. The finite formulation is enough for any finite-stage rigorous exploration; limits require separate tightness/localization arguments.

# 4. NEW-L29 — adaptive localized orbit resolvent

Let `S subset V`, let `P_S` be projection onto `S`, and put

\[
K_S^{\mathscr F}=K_t^{\mathscr F}[S,S],
\qquad
D_{S,z}^{\mathscr F}=\det(I-zK_S^{\mathscr F}).
\]

For a permutation `pi`, define

\[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi)
=
\prod_{C\subseteq S}(1-z^{|C|})
\]

over complete permutation cycles wholly contained in `S`, and

\[
G_{S,z}^{\pi}(u,v)
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}
\mathbf 1\{\pi^m(u)=v,\ \pi^j(u)\in S\text{ for }1\le j<m\}.
\]

Differentiating NEW-L28 at `A=zP_S` gives exactly:

## Theorem NEW-L29 — PROVED

For `u,v in S` and `0<=z<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi_t)
G_{S,z}^{\pi_t}(u,v)
\mid\mathscr F_J
\right]
=
D_{S,z}^{\mathscr F}
\left[
K_S^{\mathscr F}(I-zK_S^{\mathscr F})^{-1}
\right]_{vu}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the adaptive counterpart of archived `NEW-L25`.

# 5. NEW-L30 — exact unique-anchor-cycle identity

The earlier `NEW-L27` bounds the probability that **some** unrelated complete cycle occurs inside a fresh block. For continuation of the distinguished orbit, the more relevant local failure is closure of the cycle through the current anchor `u`.

Assume from now on that `S` is such that

\[
q:=\sup_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}K_S^{\mathscr F}(x,y)<1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then the resolvent at `z=1` exists.

Let `C_u(pi)` be the permutation cycle through `u`, and let `N_S(pi)` be the number of complete permutation cycles wholly contained in `S`.

## Theorem NEW-L30 — PROVED

Almost surely in the adaptive exploration history,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
C_u(\pi_t)\subseteq S,\ N_S(\pi_t)=1
\mid\mathscr F_J
\right)
=
D_{S,1}^{\mathscr F}
\left[
K_S^{\mathscr F}(I-K_S^{\mathscr F})^{-1}
\right]_{uu}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Take `u=v` in (4.1) and let `z` increase to one. For a fixed permutation:

* if the root cycle exits `S`, the diagonal orbit kernel is zero;
* if the root cycle is wholly in `S` and has length `L`, then
  \[
  G_{S,z}^{\pi}(u,u)
  =\frac{z^{L-1}}{1-z^L};
  \]
* multiplying by `Delta_{S,z}` cancels the root-cycle factor `1-z^L`, leaving
  \[
  z^{L-1}
  \prod_{\substack{C\subseteq S\\C\ne C_u}}
  (1-z^{|C|}).
  \]

As `z upward 1`, this tends to one exactly when the root cycle is complete in `S` **and is the unique complete cycle in `S`**, and tends to zero otherwise. Since (5.1) makes the matrix resolvent continuous at one, bounded/dominated convergence on the permutation side proves (5.2). `square`

An equivalent determinant-minor identity is

\[
\mathbb P(C_u\subseteq S,\ N_S=1\mid\mathscr F_J)
=
\det(I-K_{S\setminus\{u\}}^{\mathscr F})
-
\det(I-K_S^{\mathscr F}),
\tag{5.3}
\]

which follows directly because “no complete cycle in `S\{u}` but at least one complete cycle in `S`” means precisely that the only complete cycle in `S` is the one through `u`.

# 6. NEW-L31 — quenched O(beta^{-3/2}) bound for the distinguished unique local closure

Now specialize to the fresh buffered geometry of archived `NEW-L26`.

Let

\[
S\subset U\subset\mathbb Z^3,
\qquad
b=\operatorname{dist}(S,U^c),
\]

and assume no clock incident to `U` has been exposed during `[0,beta]`; arbitrary clocks outside `U` may have been adaptively exposed. By NEW-L28 and the Poisson no-peeking property, the conditional law inside `U` is still fresh.

Write

\[
a:=C\beta^{-3/2}+\varepsilon_{\beta,b},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
=
\sup_{x\in S}\mathbb P_x(\tau_{U^c}\le\beta).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Archived `NEW-L26`, now valid conditionally after adaptive exterior exposure, gives

\[
\sup_{x,y\in S}K^{\mathscr F}(x,y)\le a.
\tag{6.2}
\]

In addition, for `x in S`, splitting according to exit from `U` gives the stronger row-sum estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
K^{\mathscr F}(x,S)
&\le
\mathbb P_x(X(\beta)\in S)
+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}\\
&\le
C|S|\beta^{-3/2}
+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Assume

\[
|S|\le\eta\beta^{3/2}
\tag{6.4}
\]

with `eta>0` chosen small enough, and choose a logarithmic diffusive buffer

\[
b=A\sqrt{\beta\log\beta}
\tag{6.5}
\]

with `A` large enough that

\[
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}\le C\beta^{-3/2}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Then for all sufficiently large `beta`,

\[
q:=\sup_{x\in S}K^{\mathscr F}(x,S)
\le C\eta+C\beta^{-3/2}
\le\frac12.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For every nonnegative substochastic matrix with row sums at most `q` and entries at most `a`,

\[
\max_{x,y}(K^m)(x,y)\le a q^{m-1}
\tag{6.8}
\]

by induction:

\[
(K^{m+1})(x,y)
=\sum_zK(x,z)K^m(z,y)
\le q\max_{z,y}K^m(z,y).
\]

Therefore

\[
\left[K_S^{\mathscr F}(I-K_S^{\mathscr F})^{-1}\right]_{uu}
=\sum_{m\ge1}(K_S^{\mathscr F})^m(u,u)
\le\frac{a}{1-q}
\le 2a.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since `D_{S,1}^F<=1`, NEW-L30 yields:

## Theorem NEW-L31 — PROVED

For fixed sufficiently small `eta`, sufficiently large `A`, and all sufficiently large `beta`, uniformly over every admissible adaptive exterior exposure that leaves the buffered region fresh,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
C_u(\pi_\beta)\subseteq S,\ N_S(\pi_\beta)=1
\mid\mathscr F
\right)
\le
C\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

The constant is independent of the block volume coefficient `eta` once `eta` is below the fixed row-sum threshold.

This is substantially sharper than the `O(eta)` bound of `NEW-L27` for existence of **any** unrelated complete cycle in the same block.

### What NEW-L31 does not say

It does **not** yet upper-bound

\[
\mathbb P(C_u\subseteq S\mid\mathscr F)
\]

without the `N_S=1` condition. The determinant cancellation at `z upward 1` suppresses configurations containing additional complete cycles in `S`. Removing this extra-cycle suppression without paying an extensive cycle-count cost remains a real gap, not a notational nuisance.

# 7. NEW-L32 — adaptive endpoint-recruitment bound

Under the same fresh-buffer hypotheses, let `A_old subset S` be any `F`-measurable set of already distinguished spatial target vertices. Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(u)\in A_{\rm old}\mid\mathscr F)
&=K^{\mathscr F}(u,A_{\rm old})\\
&\le
\mathbb P_u(X(\beta)\in A_{\rm old})
+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}\\
&\le
C|A_{\rm old}|\beta^{-3/2}
+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

With the logarithmic diffusive buffer (6.5),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(u)\in A_{\rm old}\mid\mathscr F)
\le
C(|A_{\rm old}|+1)\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This only controls **endpoint recruitment** into a listed target set; it does not control synchronized intersections of the whole chronological strand with old strands.

# 8. Sequential corollaries; no independence required

Suppose an exploration constructs stages `j=1,...,n`. At stage `j`, based only on previously revealed information, it selects an anchor `u_j` and a pair

\[
S_j\subset U_j
\]

such that the edge-time region incident to `U_j` for the relevant full period is still unrevealed, `|S_j|<=eta beta^(3/2)`, and the buffer satisfies (6.5).

Let `E_j` be the event that the anchor cycle closes wholly inside `S_j` and is the unique complete cycle in `S_j`.

Then NEW-L31 and iterated conditioning give

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^nE_j\right)
\le
Cn\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

No independence between stages is needed.

Thus, **conditional on the existence of genuinely fresh blocks**, unique local closure of the distinguished strand remains negligible for any

\[
n=o(\beta^{3/2}).
\]

Similarly, if at stage `j` the listed old endpoint set has size at most `j`, NEW-L32 yields the restricted endpoint-recruitment estimate

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\text{some endpoint hits a listed old anchor by stage }n)
\le
C\beta^{-3/2}\sum_{j\le n}(j+1)
\le
Cn^2\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Hence this particular failure mode is negligible for

\[
n=o(\beta^{3/4}).
\]

These are **restricted progression bounds**, not a proof that the actual cyclic-time exploration can supply such fresh blocks for that many stages.

# 9. Why the adaptive space-time theorem matters

A full-period spatially fresh neighborhood around every successive cyclic anchor is too strong: the new anchor is produced by the previous period and may lie on already partially inspected space-time columns. The usefulness of NEW-L28 is that the exact fermionic determinant algebra does not require whole-edge exposure. It survives finite adaptive exposure of arbitrary measurable edge-time regions.

This opens a more realistic next route: choose a **phase window** or space-time tube around a segment of the cyclic-time path that has not yet been inspected, rather than demanding an untouched spatial ball for the entire `[0,beta]` slab. Conditional on the exploration, that tube still carries independent Poisson clocks, and all determinant identities remain exact with the corresponding time-inhomogeneous one-particle propagator.

Quantitative local-escape estimates for such partially fresh tubes have not yet been proved here.

# 11. Exact remaining gap

The two load-bearing unresolved mechanisms are now separated more cleanly.

### Gap A — additional local cycles

NEW-L31 gives `O(beta^-3/2)` for closure of the distinguished anchor cycle when it is the unique complete cycle in the fresh core. It does not yet remove the possibility that the distinguished cycle closes while one or more unrelated complete cycles also lie in the core. `NEW-L27` only bounds the existence of any such extra cycle by `O(eta)`, which is too large to sum indefinitely over a fixed-size sequence of blocks.

A useful next algebraic target is a **root-cycle formula without the extra-cycle suppression factor**, or a proof that conditioning on the distinguished local closure cannot increase the chance of unrelated local cycles by more than a bounded factor.

### Gap B — construction of repeated partially fresh tubes

Even perfect local closure estimates are useless unless the actual cyclic-time exploration can repeatedly find uninspected space-time regions. Full spatial past avoidance is unavailable in `d=3`; this is exactly where the `d>=5` Elboim–Sly mechanism does not transfer.

NEW-L28 reduces the freshness requirement from whole edges/whole periods to finite adaptively selected edge-time regions. The next geometric problem is therefore to construct **partially fresh phase tubes** often enough, and to show that recruitment across their boundaries is a sparse perturbation rather than a recurrent trap.
