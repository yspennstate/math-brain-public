# 2. Finite-graph master identity used in this run

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected graph, \(|V|=N\), and let \(\Pi_t\) denote the random permutation matrix of the interchange process. Let \(p_t\) be the one-particle transition matrix at the same physical time.

The previously archived determinant theorem is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\det(I-A\Pi_t)=\det(I-Ap_t)
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

for every deterministic \(N\times N\) matrix \(A\).

Taking \(A=zI\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\det(I-z\Pi_t)=\det(I-zp_t).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

If the cycle lengths of a deterministic permutation \(\pi\) are \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_K\), then

\[
\det(I-z\pi)
=
\prod_{i=1}^K(1-z^{\ell_i}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Equation (2.2) is therefore an exact transform of the complete random cycle partition.

# 3. NEW-L11 — Bernoulli cycle-hitting determinant

Independently of the interchange process, mark every vertex of \(V\) with probability \(r\in[0,1]\), independently. Let \(R\subseteq V\) be the marked set. Say that `R hits every cycle` if every cycle of \(\pi_t\) contains at least one marked vertex.

Conditioned on a permutation with cycle lengths \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_K\), the mark sets on different cycles are independent, and

\[
\mathbb P_R(R\text{ hits every cycle}\mid\pi_t)
=
\prod_{i=1}^K\left[1-(1-r)^{\ell_i}\right].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Taking \(z=1-r\) in (2.2) gives the following exact positive-probability interpretation.

## Theorem NEW-L11 — PROVED

For every finite connected graph, every \(t\ge0\), and every \(r\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\pi,R}
(R\text{ intersects every cycle of }\pi_t)
=
\det\bigl(I-(1-r)p_t\bigr).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, with the one-particle Laplacian

\[
L_t:=I-p_t,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\pi,R}(R\text{ hits every cycle})
=
\det\bigl(rI+(1-r)L_t\bigr).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is an exact coupling-free identity. It converts a global random-permutation event into a one-particle determinant.

# 4. NEW-L12 — positive cycle-transversal / rooted-forest identity

Put

\[
z=\frac1{1+q},\qquad q\ge0.
\]

Multiplying (2.2) by \((1+q)^N\) and using (2.3),

\[
(1+q)^N
\prod_C\left(1-(1+q)^{-|C|}\right)
=
\prod_C\left((1+q)^{|C|}-1\right).
\]

On the one-particle side,

\[
(1+q)^N\det\left(I-\frac1{1+q}p_t\right)
=
\det(L_t+qI).
\]

Hence:

## Theorem NEW-L12 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\prod_{C\text{ cycle of }\pi_t}
\left((1+q)^{|C|}-1\right)
=
\det(L_t+qI).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a fixed permutation,

\[
\prod_C\left((1+q)^{|C|}-1\right)
=
\sum_{R\subseteq V:\ R\cap C\ne\varnothing\ \forall C}
q^{|R|}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, for each cycle one chooses a nonempty subset of its vertices, and the union of those choices is exactly a vertex set meeting every cycle.

Let

\[
H_k(\pi)
:=
\#\{R\subseteq V:|R|=k,\ R\text{ intersects every cycle of }\pi\}.
\]

Comparing coefficients of \(q^k\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E H_k(\pi_t)
=
[q^k]\det(L_t+qI).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Now apply the weighted matrix-forest theorem to \(L_t=I-p_t\). Since \(p_t\) is symmetric, \(L_t\) is the Laplacian of the complete weighted graph having off-diagonal conductances \(p_t(x,y)\). The coefficient of \(q^k\) in \(\det(L_t+qI)\) is the total weight of spanning rooted forests with exactly \(k\) components, one root chosen in each component.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E H_k(\pi_t)
=
\text{total }p_t\text{-weight of spanning rooted forests with }k\text{ rooted components}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a **positive** identity: unlike the alternating hook formulas, no cancellation remains after the change of variables.

### Spectral form

Let the eigenvalues of \(L_t\) be

\[
0=\lambda_1<\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_N.
\]

Then

\[
\det(L_t+qI)
=q\prod_{j=2}^N(q+\lambda_j),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E H_k(\pi_t)
=e_{N-k}(\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_N),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

with the standard convention for elementary symmetric functions.

# 5. NEW-L13 — exact one-cycle / spanning-tree formula

For \(k=1\), a one-vertex set hits every permutation cycle if and only if the permutation itself consists of one \(N\)-cycle. In that case all \(N\) choices of the marked vertex hit the unique cycle. Thus

\[
H_1(\pi)=N\,\mathbf1_{\{\pi\text{ is one }N\text{-cycle}\}}.
\]

The coefficient of \(q\) in \(\det(L_t+qI)\) is the total rooted spanning-tree weight, equal to \(N\) times any cofactor of \(L_t\).

## Theorem NEW-L13 — PROVED

For every finite connected graph,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_t\text{ consists of one }N\text{-cycle})
=
\det L_t^{(o)},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \(L_t^{(o)}\) is any principal cofactor obtained by deleting one row and column.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_t\text{ is one cycle})
=
\frac1N\prod_{j=2}^N\lambda_j.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the probability that the interchange permutation is one cycle is **exactly the weighted spanning-tree partition function** of the complete graph with conductances \(p_t(x,y)\).

### Independent derivative proof

Differentiating (2.2) at \(z=1\) provides a separate check. A permutation with at least two cycles contributes zero because its characteristic polynomial has a zero of order at least two at \(z=1\). A one-cycle permutation contributes derivative \(-N\). On the right, the stochastic matrix \(p_t\) has one eigenvalue one, hence

\[
\left.\frac d{dz}\det(I-zp_t)\right|_{z=1}
=-\prod_{j=2}^N\lambda_j.
\]

The resulting identity is (5.2).

# 6. NEW-L14 — exact one-cycle asymptotic on the three-dimensional torus

Let

\[
T_n^3=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^3,
\qquad N=n^3,
\]

and let \(p_{\beta,n}\) be the rate-one-per-neighbor continuous-time random-walk kernel. The nonnegative generator eigenvalues are

\[
\Lambda_n(k)
=
2\sum_{j=1}^3
\left(1-\cos\frac{2\pi k_j}{n}\right),
\qquad
k\in T_n^3,
\]

and the eigenvalues of \(L_\beta=I-p_{\beta,n}\) are

\[
1-e^{-\beta\Lambda_n(k)}.
\]

Therefore NEW-L13 gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{T_n^3}
(\pi_\beta\text{ is one }N\text{-cycle})
=
\frac1N
\prod_{k\ne0}
\left(1-e^{-\beta\Lambda_n(k)}\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define the infinite-volume cycle-pressure function

\[
\rho_3(\beta)
:=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{m}
 p_{m\beta}^{\mathbb Z^3}(0,0).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The series is finite because \(p_t(0,0)\le Ct^{-3/2}\) for \(t\ge1\).

## Theorem NEW-L14 — PROVED

For every fixed \(\beta>0\),

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
-\frac1{n^3}
\log\left[
 n^3\,
 \mathbb P_{T_n^3}(\pi_\beta\text{ is one cycle})
\right]
=
\rho_3(\beta).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Moreover, as \(\beta\to\infty\),

\[
\boxed{
\rho_3(\beta)
=
\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
\beta^{-3/2}
(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

Take logarithms in (6.1):

\[
-\frac1N\log[N\mathbb P(\text{one cycle})]
=
-\frac1N
\sum_{k\ne0}
\log(1-e^{-\beta\Lambda_n(k)}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The summand has an integrable logarithmic singularity at the zero mode in three dimensions. The Riemann sum therefore converges to

\[
-\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}
\log(1-e^{-\beta\Lambda(\theta)})
\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^3},
\]

where

\[
\Lambda(\theta)=2\sum_{j=1}^3(1-\cos\theta_j).
\]

Expand \(-\log(1-u)=\sum_{m\ge1}u^m/m\) and use monotone convergence:

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\int\log(1-e^{-\beta\Lambda(\theta)})\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^3}
&=
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac1m
\int e^{-m\beta\Lambda(\theta)}
\frac{d\theta}{(2\pi)^3}\\
&=
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac1m
p_{m\beta}^{\mathbb Z^3}(0,0)
=\rho_3(\beta).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (6.3). For (6.4), use

\[
p_{m\beta}(0,0)
=(4\pi m\beta)^{-3/2}(1+o(1))
\]

for each fixed \(m\), together with the uniform Gaussian bound

\[
p_{m\beta}(0,0)
\le C(m\beta)^{-3/2}.
\]

Dominated convergence over \(m\) yields

\[
\beta^{3/2}\rho_3(\beta)
\to
(4\pi)^{-3/2}
\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-5/2}
=
\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}.
\]
\(\square\)

# 7. NEW-L15 — a rigorous long-cycle lower bound from random cycle hitting

Let

\[
M(\pi):=\max_C |C|
\]

be the largest cycle length of a finite permutation. For Bernoulli marking density \(r\), put

\[
h_a(r):=1-(1-r)^a.
\]

If every cycle has length at most \(a\), then the number of cycles is at least \(N/a\), and every factor in (3.1) is at most \(h_a(r)\). Hence

\[
\mathbb P_R(R\text{ hits every cycle}\mid\pi)
\le
h_a(r)^{N/a}
\qquad\text{on }\{M\le a\}.
\]

On \(\{M>a\}\) the conditional probability is at most one. Averaging and using NEW-L11 gives:

## Theorem NEW-L15 — PROVED

For every finite graph, every \(a\ge1\), and every \(r\in[0,1]\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(M(\pi_t)>a)
\ge
\det(I-(1-r)p_t)
-
\left[1-(1-r)^a\right]^{N/a}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The right-hand side is to be replaced by zero if negative.

This is an exact one-particle-determinant lower bound for the existence of a long permutation cycle.

### Why NEW-L15 does not yet solve the target

At fixed \(\beta\) on growing three-dimensional tori, if \(a=a_N\to\infty\) and one chooses the natural marking scale \(r\asymp a_N^{-1}\), then

\[
[1-(1-r)^{a_N}]^{N/a_N}
=
\exp[-\Theta(N/a_N)],
\]

whereas the determinant in (7.1), for sparse enough marking, is governed by the fixed positive cycle pressure \(\rho_3(\beta)\) and is of order \(\exp[-\rho_3(\beta)N]\) up to subexponential factors. The second term is then much larger, making (7.1) vacuous. If \(r\) is made exponentially smaller in \(a_N\), the two exponential scales can be reversed, but the resulting lower bound remains exponentially small in \(N\), far below the positive vertex-mass statement needed by the torus-to-lattice transfer.

Thus the cycle-hitting transform is a new exact route and a useful diagnostic, but it does not by itself produce the positive diverging-cycle density required for the full theorem.

# 8. What the forest identity says about the finite-torus route

If \(R_k\) is a uniformly chosen \(k\)-subset of \(V\), independent of the interchange process, then

\[
\mathbb P(R_k\text{ hits every cycle})
=
\frac{\mathbb E H_k(\pi_t)}{\binom Nk}
=
\frac{[q^k]\det(L_t+qI)}{\binom Nk}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

The event that a \(k\)-set hits every cycle implies that the permutation has at most \(k\) cycles. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\#\text{cycles}(\pi_t)\le k)
\ge
\frac{[q^k]\det(L_t+qI)}{\binom Nk}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This is a rigorous lower bound on the probability of a small cycle count in terms of a rooted-forest coefficient.

However, a small number of cycles is much stronger than the target condition. Tóth's conjecture is compatible with an extensive density of finite cycles coexisting with positive mass in macroscopic/infinite cycles. Consequently, controlling the total cycle count alone cannot settle the problem.

# 10. Strongest current obstruction and next attack

The determinant/forest transform now makes one structural fact very clear. At fixed large \(\beta\), finite-cycle **count** statistics naturally live at density scale \(\beta^{-3/2}\), and the extreme few-cycle torus event has the same pressure constant as the local finite-cycle void probability. None of this rules out the all-finite scenario in which typical finite cycles have volume of order \(\beta^{3/2}\).

The missing theorem must therefore be size-biased: it must control the **vertex mass** in arbitrarily long finite cycles, not merely the number of finite cycles.

The next high-priority attack should return to the representation/twisted-trace route but use the new forest transform as a positivity benchmark:

1. derive the exact two-cycle and three-cycle derivatives of \(\det(I-zp_t)\) at \(z=1\), obtaining positive weighted statistics such as
   \[
   \mathbb E[\ell_1\ell_2;\ K=2]
   \]
   in closed spectral form;
2. identify these derivative weights with low-component rooted-forest coefficients;
3. determine whether a **size-biased** transform of one distinguished permutation cycle can be represented by a ratio or derivative of the determinant/forest partition function without the suppressing product over all other cycles;
4. if such a Palm/root transform exists, attempt to bound
   \[
   \sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)=\ell)
   \]
   by a summable one-particle loop series. This would attack the exact gap that cycle-count pressure cannot address;
5. in parallel, keep the finite-torus transfer A6: it remains enough to prove positive vertex mass in any cycle scale \(a_N\to\infty\) at one fixed sufficiently large \(\beta\).

A particularly concrete algebraic micro-target is to find a determinant identity for a **distinguished/rooted cycle Palm transform**. The ordinary determinant factorizes over every cycle simultaneously; differentiating in the root row produces the desired factor \(q^{L(0)-1}\) but also multiplies by suppression factors from all other cycles. Removing those factors without losing positivity is the current exact algebraic obstacle.
