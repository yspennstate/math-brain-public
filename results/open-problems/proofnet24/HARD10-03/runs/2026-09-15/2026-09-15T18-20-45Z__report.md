# HARD10-03 — Tóth infinite-cycle conjecture on Z^3

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL. The full d=3 conjecture is not proved or refuted here.**

## 0. Exact target and current literature boundary

On every unoriented nearest-neighbor edge of `Z^3`, put an independent rate-one Poisson transposition clock. Let `pi_beta` be the resulting random permutation at time `beta`. The full target is

\[
\exists\beta_0<\infty\quad\forall\beta>\beta_0:\qquad
\mathbb P\bigl(0\text{ belongs to an infinite cycle of }\pi_\beta\bigr)>0.
\]

The cycle orbit is

\[
0,\pi_\beta(0),\pi_\beta^2(0),\ldots,
\]

which reuses the same Poisson slab. It is not the chronological tagged-particle trajectory. No monotonicity in `beta` is assumed.

A fresh bounded public-source check on 15 September 2026 again found no theorem settling `d=3`. Dor Elboim and Allan Sly, *Infinite cycles in the interchange process in five dimensions*, arXiv:2211.17023v3, prove the large-`beta` theorem for `d>=5`, using the cyclic-time random walk, multiscale diffusion and local escape. This remains a boundary theorem rather than a solution of the assigned case.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17023

# 2. Archived dependency: the master determinant identity

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected graph, let `Pi_t` be the random permutation matrix of interchange at time `t`, and let `K_t` be the one-particle heat kernel. The previously archived theorem used here is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\det(I-A\Pi_t)=\det(I-AK_t)
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

for every deterministic matrix `A` of the correct size.

At the scalar point `A=zI`, `0<=z<1`, write

\[
D_t(z)=\det(I-zK_t),
\qquad
R_t(z)=K_t(I-zK_t)^{-1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The previous report used only diagonal perturbations. The main new step in this run is to differentiate (2.1) in an **off-diagonal matrix entry**. This exposes whether two specified vertices lie on the same permutation cycle and, if so, their oriented cycle distance.

# 3. NEW-L19 — exact two-vertex oriented cycle resolvent

Fix vertices `u,v in V`. For a deterministic permutation `pi`, if `v` lies in the cycle of `u`, let

\[
d_\pi(u,v)=\min\{m\ge1:\pi^m(u)=v\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus if `v=u`, then `d_pi(u,u)` is the positive cycle length `L_pi(u)`. If `v` is not in the cycle of `u`, leave `d_pi(u,v)` undefined.

Let `C_u` denote the permutation cycle containing `u`, and define

\[
W_z^{(u)}
:=
\prod_{C\neq C_u}(1-z^{|C|}).
\tag{3.2}
\]

## Theorem NEW-L19 — PROVED

For every finite graph, every `0<=z<1`, and every pair `u,v`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{v\in C_u\}}
 z^{d_\pi(u,v)-1}
 W_z^{(u)}
\right]
=
D_t(z)\,[R_t(z)]_{vu}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For `v=u`, this reduces exactly to the previously archived rooted identity.

### Proof

Differentiate the master identity (2.1) with respect to the matrix entry `A_{uv}` and then set `A=zI`.

For a fixed permutation matrix `Pi`, Jacobi's formula gives

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{uv}}
\det(I-A\Pi)
=
-\det(I-A\Pi)
[\Pi(I-A\Pi)^{-1}]_{vu}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

At `A=zI`,

\[
\Pi(I-z\Pi)^{-1}
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}\Pi^m.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence

\[
[\Pi(I-z\Pi)^{-1}]_{vu}
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}
\mathbf1_{\{\pi^m(u)=v\}}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `u,v` are on different cycles, this is zero. If they are on the same cycle of length `L` and oriented distance `d=d_pi(u,v)`, then the hitting times are `d,d+L,d+2L,...`, so

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}
\mathbf1_{\{\pi^m(u)=v\}}
=
\frac{z^{d-1}}{1-z^L}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Also

\[
\det(I-z\Pi)
=(1-z^L)W_z^{(u)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

The factor `1-z^L` cancels, so the negative of the derivative on the permutation side is precisely the random variable in (3.3).

On the heat-kernel side,

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{uv}}
\det(I-AK_t)
=
-\det(I-AK_t)
[K_t(I-AK_t)^{-1}]_{vu}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Set `A=zI` and equate derivatives. This proves (3.3). `square`

# 4. NEW-L20 — exact spatial profile under the other-cycles-hitting Palm tilt

The previous checkpoint defines

\[
\frac{d\mathbb Q_z^u}{d\mathbb P}(\pi)
=
\frac{W_z^{(u)}(\pi)}{\mathbb E W_z^{(u)}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The archived diagonal calculation gives

\[
\mathbb EW_z^{(u)}
=D_t(z)\bigl(1+zR_{uu}(z)\bigr).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Divide NEW-L19 by (4.2).

## Theorem NEW-L20 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_z^u}
\left[
\mathbf1_{\{v\in C_u\}}
 z^{d_\pi(u,v)-1}
\right]
=
\frac{R_{vu}(z)}{1+zR_{uu}(z)}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is an exact spatial/oriented-distance profile of the tilted root cycle in terms of a one-particle resolvent.

In particular, because the weight `z^{d-1}` is at most one,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb Q_z^u(v\in C_u)
\ge
\frac{R_{vu}(z)}{1+zR_{uu}(z)}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

On a transitive graph,

\[
R_{vu}(z)
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}p_{mt}(u,v).
\tag{4.5}
\]

So the tilted cycle has a rigorous spatial lower profile given by the geometrically stopped random-walk Green kernel.

### Exact cycle-length transform recovered by summing over `v`

For every deterministic cycle of length `L`,

\[
\sum_{v\in C_u}z^{d_\pi(u,v)-1}
=
1+z+\cdots+z^{L-1}
=
\frac{1-z^L}{1-z}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Since a stochastic heat kernel has row and column sums one,

\[
\sum_v R_{vu}(z)
=
\frac1{1-z}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Summing (4.3) over `v` gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_z^u}[1-z^L]
=
\frac1{1+zR_{uu}(z)}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_z^u}[z^L]
=
\frac{zR_{uu}(z)}{1+zR_{uu}(z)},
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

which agrees with the earlier one-root identity but is now obtained from the complete spatial profile.

Also, since `(1-z^L)/(1-z) <= L`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_z^u}L
\ge
\frac{1}{(1-z)(1+zR_{uu}(z))}.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

For `z=e^{-1/M}` on a three-dimensional torus with `M=o(V)` and large `beta`, the archived estimate `R_{uu}(z)=O(beta^{-3/2}+M/V)` makes the right side of (4.10) comparable to `M`. This gives an exact mean-size counterpart to the previous tilted long-cycle probability theorem.

# 6. NEW-L22 — localized off-diagonal determinant identity

The global tilt above still carries an exponentially small determinant normalization in large volume. To remove that global cost, localize the matrix variable to a finite spatial set.

Let `S subset V` be finite and let `P_S` be diagonal projection onto `S`. Define

\[
K_S:=K_t[S,S],
\qquad
D_S(z):=\det(I-zK_S).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For a deterministic permutation define

\[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi)
:=
\det(I-zP_S\Pi)
=
\prod_{C\subseteq S}(1-z^{|C|}),
\tag{6.2}
\]

where the product is only over permutation cycles wholly contained in `S`.

For `u,v in S`, define the discounted forward-orbit kernel before the first exit from `S` by

\[
G^{\pi}_{S,z}(u,v)
:=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}
\mathbf1\left\{
\pi^m(u)=v,
\ \pi^j(u)\in S\text{ for }1\le j<m
\right\}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

If the root cycle exits `S`, this sum stops after the first exit because every later term has a zero localization factor. If the root cycle is wholly contained in `S`, the geometric repetitions remain and are canceled by its factor in `Delta_{S,z}`.

## Theorem NEW-L22 — PROVED

For all `u,v in S` and `0<=z<1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi)
G^{\pi}_{S,z}(u,v)
\right]
=
D_S(z)
\left[K_S(I-zK_S)^{-1}\right]_{vu}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

Apply the master identity at

\[
A_0=zP_S
\]

and differentiate in the off-diagonal entry `A_{uv}`.

On the permutation side,

\[
\Pi(I-zP_S\Pi)^{-1}
=
\sum_{m\ge1}\Pi(P_S\Pi)^{m-1}z^{m-1}.
\]

Its `(v,u)` entry is exactly (6.3), because every intermediate factor `P_S` requires the corresponding forward orbit vertex to stay in `S`.

On the heat-kernel side the derivative is

\[
-D_S(z)
[K_t(I-zP_SK_t)^{-1}]_{vu}.
\]

For `u,v in S`, expanding the series shows

\[
[K_t(I-zP_SK_t)^{-1}]_{vu}
=
[K_S(I-zK_S)^{-1}]_{vu},
\]

because every intermediate index is forced into `S`. Equating derivatives proves (6.4). `square`

This theorem is under the **original interchange law**, not a globally tilted law. Its only weight is the local factor `Delta_{S,z}`, which depends exclusively on complete permutation cycles contained in `S`.

# 7. NEW-L23 — original-law lower bound on discounted orbit residence

Because every factor in (6.2) lies in `[0,1]`,

\[
0\le\Delta_{S,z}\le1.
\]

Hence NEW-L22 gives the unconditional inequality

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E G^{\pi}_{S,z}(u,v)
\ge
D_S(z)
[K_S(I-zK_S)^{-1}]_{vu}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Sum over `v in S`. Let

\[
N_S^\pi(u)
:=
\sup\{n\ge0:\pi(u),\ldots,\pi^n(u)\in S\},
\]

with `N_S^pi(u)=infinity` if the entire root cycle stays in `S`. Then

\[
\sum_{v\in S}G^\pi_{S,z}(u,v)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{N_S^\pi(u)}z^{m-1},
\tag{7.2}
\]

where the right side equals `1/(1-z)` when `N_S^pi(u)=infinity`.

For the discrete-time chain with one-step kernel `K_t`, killed on leaving `S`, let `N_S^K(u)` denote its number of consecutive in-`S` steps after time zero. Then

\[
\sum_{v\in S}
[K_S(I-zK_S)^{-1}]_{vu}
=
\mathbb E_u
\sum_{m=1}^{N_S^K(u)}z^{m-1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore:

## Theorem NEW-L23 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\sum_{m=1}^{N_S^\pi(u)}z^{m-1}
\ge
D_S(z)
\mathbb E_u
\sum_{m=1}^{N_S^K(u)}z^{m-1}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is a direct original-measure comparison between discounted residence of the **permutation orbit** and discounted residence of the `beta`-skeleton random walk, multiplied only by the local no-cycle determinant.

The determinant factor is explicitly controlled by the earlier finite-set checkpoint:

\[
1-D_S(1)
=
\mathbb P(\text{there is a complete permutation cycle inside }S)
\le
|S|p_t(o,o)
\tag{7.5}
\]

on transitive graphs. Thus whenever `|S|p_t(o,o)` is small, the local comparison factor is close to one.

# 8. What NEW-L23 gives, and the remaining volume/residence tradeoff

Equation (7.4) is stronger structurally than the earlier first-interaction coupling: it is valid after arbitrary cyclic entanglements and compares the actual permutation orbit with a killed one-particle skeleton through an exact determinant identity.

However it does not by itself prove infinite cycles. In `Z^3`, a `beta`-skeleton step has spatial scale `sqrt(beta)`. To keep such a walk inside a roughly isotropic set for `M` steps with high probability requires diameter of order `sqrt(M beta)` and hence volume of order

\[
(M\beta)^{3/2}.
\]

The local determinant cost from (7.5) is then naturally of order

\[
|S|p_\beta(0,0)
\asymp
M^{3/2},
\]

so the simple isotropic use of NEW-L23 loses control as `M` grows. This is the same three-dimensional critical tradeoff seen from a new, exact route.

A successful use of NEW-L23 would therefore need one of:

1. a geometry `S` with anomalously long `beta`-skeleton residence compared with its volume;
2. a sharper determinant lower bound exploiting geometry rather than only `Tr K_S`;
3. a multiscale chaining in which each local determinant cost is paid on essentially disjoint newly explored regions.

The third possibility is the closest determinant analogue of the Elboim–Sly local-escape strategy.

# 9. New exact sufficient multiscale direction

Suppose one can construct adaptive regions `S_1,S_2,...` around successive new orbit locations such that, conditionally on the exposed past,

* the killed `beta`-skeleton has probability at least `1-epsilon_j` to cross `S_j` into a fresh region before returning to the old core;
* the determinant penalty for complete cycles newly contained in `S_j` is at least `1-delta_j`;
* the unexplored Poisson information needed for the determinant comparison can be exposed without reusing a previous region;
* `sum_j(epsilon_j+delta_j)<infinity`.

Then a product/conditional version of NEW-L23 would give positive probability of indefinitely many fresh crossings, and hence an infinite permutation orbit.

This conditional route is **not proved**: the present determinant identity is annealed, and the key missing theorem is a conditional/local version stable under an adaptively exposed history. The statement is recorded because NEW-L22 identifies exactly what such a theorem would have to preserve.

# 10. Failed / killed routes retained

1. No monotonicity in `beta` may be assumed.
2. Chronological tagged-particle transience is not permutation-cycle transience.
3. A pure unconditioned fixed-`beta` Gaussian CLT for the raw cycle skeleton is impossible if finite cycles have positive mass; this was established in the repository adversarial audit.
4. Fixed-label chronological two-particle Green identities cannot simply be reused for cyclic slab reuse.
5. Bulk determinant pressure is cycle-count biased and cannot distinguish one giant cycle from diverging finite cycles at leading extensive order; this is proved in the 17:19 checkpoint.
6. The explicit all-cycle-hitting tilt forces long root cycles, but transferring it by the trivial Radon–Nikodym bound pays an exponentially small determinant normalization; this is proved in the 17:42 addendum.
7. NEW-L23 avoids the global determinant cost, but a naive single isotropic box faces the critical volume/residence tradeoff `|S| beta^{-3/2} ~ M^{3/2}`.

# 12. Next concrete attack

The highest-value next attack is to test whether NEW-L22 admits a **conditional spatial Markov form**.

1. Expose the Poisson bars only in a previously explored finite spacetime region and stop at the first entrance into a fresh spatial block.
2. Condition on the exposed sigma-field and identify the law of the still-unseen bars in the fresh block.
3. Determine whether the determinant differentiation proof can be repeated with a boundary condition induced by the exposed partial permutation, producing a conditional analogue of
   \[
   E[\Delta_{S,z}G_{S,z}\mid\mathcal F_{past}]
   \ge (1-\delta)\,G^{K,kill}_{S,z}.
   \]
4. If exact conditioning breaks the determinant identity, isolate the defect as a finite-rank boundary perturbation and bound it by a Schur complement/resolvent estimate.
5. Seek a sequence of fresh blocks whose failure probabilities are summable. This is the determinant-side route most directly analogous to Elboim–Sly local escape, but potentially uses only three-dimensional one-particle transience plus finite-rank corrections.

A secondary attack is to use NEW-L19 on large tori to derive exact two-point lower profiles for the tilted cycle and then search for a correlation/switching inequality comparing the other-cycles-hitting tilt with the original law at polynomial rather than exponential cost.
