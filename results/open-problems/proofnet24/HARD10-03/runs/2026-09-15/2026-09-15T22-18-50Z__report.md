# 3. Input: fixed-m tagged heat kernel

Let

\[
\mathcal C_m^{(3)}
=
\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in(\mathbb Z^3)^m:x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}
\]

and let

\[
Q_t^{(m)}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
\]

be the chronological ordered `m`-tag interchange heat kernel.

The archived fixed-tag result BF-HK-r states that, for every fixed `m`, there are constants `C_m,c_m>0` such that for `t>=1`,

\[
Q_t^{(m)}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
\le
C_m t^{-3m/2}
\exp\left[-c_m\frac{\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)^2}{t}\right].
\tag{3.1}
\]

Only fixed `m` is claimed; the constants may deteriorate badly with `m`.

The same archived report proves, for every fixed integer `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)\le K)
\le C_K\beta^{-3/2}
}
\qquad(\beta\ge1),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where `L_beta(0)` is the permutation-cycle length of the origin.

Neither (3.1) nor (3.2) was affected by the later off-diagonal determinant correction: they are direct tagged-kernel statements.

# 4. NEW-L35 — exact two-sided orbit-segment kernel representation

Fix an integer `r>=1` and write

\[
P:=\pi_\beta.
\]

On the event `L_beta(0)>2r`, define

\[
x_j=P^j0,
\qquad -r\le j\le r.
\]

Then the `2r+1` vertices

\[
x_{-r},\ldots,x_{-1},x_0=0,x_1,\ldots,x_r
\]

are pairwise distinct.

For any fixed pairwise-distinct such tuple, the event

\[
P(x_j)=x_{j+1},
\qquad -r\le j\le r-1,
\tag{4.1}
\]

is exactly the chronological `2r`-tag transition from

\[
\mathbf a
=(x_{-r},x_{-r+1},\ldots,x_{r-1})
\]

to

\[
\mathbf b
=(x_{-r+1},x_{-r+2},\ldots,x_r).
\]

Hence:

## Theorem NEW-L35 — PROVED

For every pairwise-distinct tuple as above,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
P^j0=x_j\text{ for all }-r\le j\le r
\right)
=
Q_\beta^{(2r)}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

The constraints (4.1) specify the images under the *single* permutation `P` of the `2r` distinct starting labels

\[
x_{-r},\ldots,x_{r-1}.
\]

By definition of the interchange process, the joint positions at physical time `beta` of those labels are exactly the ordered `2r`-tag chronological process. Their required terminal positions are

\[
x_{-r+1},\ldots,x_r.
\]

This proves (4.2). No independence between cyclic periods is assumed. `square`

# 5. NEW-L36 — arbitrary-order confinement suppression in deterministic sets

Let `S subset Z^3` be any finite deterministic set containing the origin. If the root cycle is wholly contained in `S` and has length greater than `2r`, then all `2r` nonzero two-sided orbit neighbors

\[
P^{-r}0,\ldots,P^{-1}0,P0,\ldots,P^r0
\]

lie in `S`, and they are distinct except for `P^00=0`.

Summing (4.2) over all admissible choices gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb P\bigl(
\operatorname{Cycle}_P(0)\subseteq S,
L_\beta(0)>2r
\bigr)\\
&\qquad\le
\sum_{(x_j)_{j\ne0}\in S^{2r}}
Q_\beta^{(2r)}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Dropping the distinctness restriction only enlarges the sum. The on-diagonal part of (3.1) gives

\[
Q_\beta^{(2r)}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)
\le
C_r\beta^{-3r}.
\]

There are at most `|S|^(2r)` choices of the nonzero orbit vertices. Therefore:

## Theorem NEW-L36 — PROVED

For every fixed integer `r>=1`, every finite deterministic `S subset Z^3` containing `0`, and every `beta>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq S,
L_\beta(0)>2r
\bigr)
\le
C_r
\left(
\frac{|S|}{\beta^{3/2}}
\right)^{2r}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining with the fixed-short-cycle estimate (3.2):

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\bigl(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq S
\bigr)
\le
C_r\beta^{-3/2}
+
C_r
\left(
\frac{|S|}{\beta^{3/2}}
\right)^{2r}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This estimate is **unconditional and does not suppress configurations containing unrelated complete cycles inside `S`**. That is the main advance over the unique-cycle determinant bounds from the previous checkpoints.

The constants are not claimed uniform in `r`.

# 6. Consequence: arbitrary fixed powers of the diffusive-volume fraction

Suppose

\[
|S|\le\eta\beta^{3/2}.
\]

Then (5.3) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq S)
\le
C_r\beta^{-3/2}+C_r\eta^{2r}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus for **every prescribed fixed power** `M`, choosing any fixed `r>=M/2` gives

\[
\mathbb P(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq S)
\le
C_M\beta^{-3/2}+C_M\eta^M.
\tag{6.2}
\]

This is much stronger than the previous first-moment finite-set bound

\[
O(|S|\beta^{-3/2})=O(\eta),
\]

when `eta` is small. It is also stronger than the determinant unique-cycle estimate in the sense that it controls the **actual distinguished root cycle**, regardless of how many other complete cycles lie in `S`.

The limitation is equally explicit: because `C_r` is uncontrolled as `r->infinity`, (6.2) is a family of fixed-order estimates, not a superpolynomial statement with one universal constant.

# 7. NEW-L37 — near-diffusive spatial spread of every non-negligible finite root cycle

Let

\[
B_R=\{x\in\mathbb Z^3:|x|\le R\}
\]

for any fixed lattice norm. Since `|B_R|<=C R^3`, (5.3) gives, for every fixed `r`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)\subseteq B_R)
\le
C_r\beta^{-3/2}
+
C_r\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt\beta}\right)^{6r}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Now fix any `epsilon>0` and take

\[
R_\beta=\beta^{1/2-\epsilon}.
\]

Then

\[
\left(\frac{R_\beta}{\sqrt\beta}\right)^{6r}
=\beta^{-6r\epsilon}.
\]

Choose once and for all

\[
r\ge \left\lceil\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\right\rceil.
\]

Then `6r epsilon>=3/2`, so:

## Theorem NEW-L37 — PROVED

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, there is `C_epsilon<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(0)
\subseteq
B_{\beta^{1/2-\epsilon}}
\right)
\le
C_\epsilon\beta^{-3/2}
}
\qquad(\beta\ge1).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Equivalently, a finite root cycle carrying non-negligible probability at large `beta` cannot be confined on any fixed subdiffusive spatial scale `beta^(1/2-epsilon)`.

This improves the earlier one-point finite-set estimate, which for the same ball only gave order

\[
|B_{\beta^{1/2-\epsilon}}|\beta^{-3/2}
\asymp
\beta^{-3\epsilon}.
\]

The improvement comes entirely from exposing a fixed two-sided orbit neighborhood and using a fixed-number tagged heat kernel; it does not require determinant positivity, off-diagonal determinant differentiation, or monotonicity in `beta`.

# 8. Why the theorem is correct despite reuse of the same Poisson slab

A possible pitfall is to read the `2r` orbit constraints as `2r` independent chronological walks. That is **not** what is used.

The equality (4.2) uses one chronological interchange process carrying `2r` tagged labels through the same physical interval `[0,beta]`. The labels interact through the ordinary stirring dynamics. The fixed-tag heat kernel (3.1) is precisely for that interacting labeled process. Thus repeated use of the frozen permutation slab is encoded exactly by the simultaneous mapping constraints.

The proof therefore avoids the chronological-particle/permutation-cycle confusion emphasized in the task statement.

# 10. A sharpened sufficient route suggested by NEW-L36

The estimate (5.3) shows that local confinement is no longer the main obstacle. If a finite root cycle survives with substantial probability at large `beta`, then, with overwhelming probability relative to every fixed subdiffusive scale, it must spread over diameter at least `beta^(1/2-o(1))`.

A full proof would follow from any theorem that forces a finite root cycle to admit, with probability bounded away from zero conditional on finiteness, a deterministic or exploration-measurable trap set `S` satisfying

\[
|S|=o(\beta^{3/2})
\]

and containing the entire cycle. NEW-L36 would then make that scenario negligible.

The actual unresolved geometry is therefore **diffusive-scale and super-diffusive finite cycles**, not short or tightly confined finite cycles.

# 11. New Green-domination target (CONJECTURAL, not used as a theorem)

The run also isolates a particularly strong inequality worth testing. For a finite proper set `S` and the one-particle heat-kernel restriction

\[
K_{\beta,S}=p_\beta[S,S],
\]

define

\[
R_{\beta,S}
=K_{\beta,S}(I-K_{\beta,S})^{-1}.
\]

The candidate inequality is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(
\operatorname{Cycle}_{\pi_\beta}(u)\subseteq S)
\stackrel{?}{\le}
[R_{\beta,S}]_{uu}.
}
\tag{GDC}
\]

This is **not proved** here. It survived the small-graph numerical checks performed during this run, but numerics are not evidence of universality.

Why GDC would be decisive: if it held uniformly for increasing finite boxes in `Z^3`, monotone convergence of the left side would give

\[
\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)<\infty)
\le
\sum_{m\ge1}p_{m\beta}(0,0).
\]

In `d=3`,

\[
\sum_{m\ge1}p_{m\beta}(0,0)
\le C\beta^{-3/2}\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-3/2}
=C\zeta(3/2)\beta^{-3/2},
\]

which is below one for all sufficiently large `beta` and would prove the full conjecture with no monotonicity assumption.

The old constant-one twisted-trace comparison being false does **not** refute GDC: that counterexample compares individual cycle-length/twisted-trace terms, whereas GDC allows compensation across all return lengths through the Green resolvent.

GDC is recorded only as a next target, not as a proved claim.
