# HARD10-03 — Tóth infinite-cycle conjecture on Z^3

**SOLUTION STATUS: PARTIAL. The full d=3 conjecture is not proved or refuted here.**

## 0. Exact target and literature boundary

On each unoriented nearest-neighbor edge of `Z^3`, put an independent rate-one Poisson transposition clock. Let `pi_beta` be the time-`beta` permutation. The target remains

\[
\exists\beta_0<\infty\quad\forall\beta>\beta_0:\qquad
\mathbb P\bigl(0\text{ lies in an infinite cycle of }\pi_\beta\bigr)>0.
\]

The relevant path is the cyclic-time orbit

\[
0,\pi_\beta(0),\pi_\beta^2(0),\ldots,
\]

which reuses the same Poisson slab. It is not the chronological tagged-particle walk. No monotonicity in `beta` is assumed.

A fresh bounded primary-source check on 15 September 2026 still leaves the rigorous large-time theorem at `d>=5`: Dor Elboim and Allan Sly, *Infinite cycles in the interchange process in five dimensions*, arXiv:2211.17023v3 (revised 2 Feb 2024), prove infinite cycles for every `d>=5` and sufficiently large `beta`. Their Section 1.2.2 explains that their method uses positive-probability avoidance of the walk's past, which is available in dimensions at least five and fails in lower dimension. They explicitly describe the remaining lower-dimensional difficulty as understanding self-intersections well enough to retain diffusive behavior.

# 2. NEW-L24 — conditional fermionic determinant identity after partial clock exposure

Work first on a finite graph `G=(V,E)`. Partition the edge set as

\[
E=E_{\rm free}\sqcup E_{\rm frozen}.
\]

Run the usual rate-one interchange process on every edge up to time `t`. Let

\[
\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}
\]

be the sigma-field generated by the complete Poisson clock configurations on the edges in `E_frozen` during `[0,t]`.

Conditional on `F_frozen`, the rings on frozen edges become a deterministic finite ordered list of transpositions, while the clocks on `E_free` remain independent rate-one Poisson processes.

Let `Pi_t` denote the random permutation matrix and define the conditional one-particle kernel

\[
K_t^{\mathcal F}
:=
\mathbb E[\Pi_t\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}].
\]

Equivalently, `K_t^F(x,y)` is the conditional probability that the label starting at `x` is at `y` at time `t`.

## Theorem NEW-L24 — PROVED

For every deterministic matrix `A`, almost surely in the frozen clock realization,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[\det(I-A\Pi_t)\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}\right]
=
\det\left(I-AK_t^{\mathcal F}\right).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus the archived master determinant identity survives **arbitrary complete exposure of a chosen subset of edge clocks**.

### Proof

Use the exterior-algebra identity

\[
\det(I-B)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{|V|}(-1)^k
\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^k B).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Conditional on `F_frozen`, list the frozen transposition times as

\[
0<s_1<\cdots<s_M<t.
\]

Between consecutive frozen times, only the free-edge clocks are random. Let `L_free` be the one-particle generator using the free edges only. On the `k`th exterior power, the interchange generator restricted to the alternating label sector is exactly the fermionic second quantization of `L_free`; therefore over an interval of length `a`,

\[
\mathbb E[\wedge^k\Pi_a^{\rm free}]
=
\wedge^k(e^{aL_{\rm free}}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

At a frozen ring on edge `e`, the permutation is multiplied by the deterministic transposition matrix `T_e`; on exterior power this is exactly `wedge^k T_e`.

The independent free-edge Poisson increments in the intervals `(0,s_1),(s_1,s_2),..., (s_M,t)` factor conditionally. Hence

\[
\mathbb E[\wedge^k\Pi_t\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}]
=
\wedge^k K_t^{\mathcal F},
\tag{2.4}
\]

because the conditional one-particle kernel is the same ordered product of the one-particle free semigroups and deterministic transposition matrices, and exterior powers preserve products.

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\det(I-A\Pi_t)\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}]
&=
\sum_k(-1)^k
\operatorname{Tr}
\left((\wedge^k A)
\mathbb E[\wedge^k\Pi_t\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}]
\right)\\
&=
\sum_k(-1)^k
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(\wedge^k(AK_t^{\mathcal F})\right)\\
&=
\det(I-AK_t^{\mathcal F}).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (2.1). `square`

### Scope

The theorem is exact for any finite graph, any deterministic edge partition, and any realization of the frozen clocks. It does **not** assume symmetry of the conditional kernel: after exterior clocks are frozen, `K_t^F` need not be self-adjoint.

By conditioning further, any estimate proved uniformly for complete exterior exposure also remains valid when only a smaller exterior sigma-field has actually been revealed by an exploration.

# 3. NEW-L25 — conditional localized determinant/orbit identity

Let `S subset V` and let `P_S` be diagonal projection onto `S`. Conditional on the frozen clocks, define

\[
K_S^{\mathcal F}
:=K_t^{\mathcal F}[S,S],
\qquad
D_{S,z}^{\mathcal F}
:=\det(I-zK_S^{\mathcal F}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a permutation `pi`, define as in the previous archived report

\[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi)
=
\prod_{C\subseteq S}(1-z^{|C|}),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
G^\pi_{S,z}(u,v)
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}
\mathbf1\{\pi^m(u)=v,\ \pi^j(u)\in S\text{ for }1\le j<m\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem NEW-L25 — PROVED

For every `u,v in S` and `0<=z<1`, almost surely in the frozen exterior environment,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\Delta_{S,z}(\pi_t)
G^{\pi_t}_{S,z}(u,v)
\mid\mathcal F_{\rm frozen}
\right]
=
D_{S,z}^{\mathcal F}
\left[
K_S^{\mathcal F}
(I-zK_S^{\mathcal F})^{-1}
\right]_{vu}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

Apply NEW-L24 at the matrix point

\[
A_0=zP_S
\]

and differentiate both sides in the entry `A_{uv}`. The same Jacobi/resolvent calculation as in the archived unconditional localized theorem identifies the permutation-side derivative with `Delta_{S,z} G^pi_{S,z}` and the heat-kernel-side derivative with the displayed conditional resolvent. `square`

This is the exact conditional analogue requested by the previous checkpoint's next-attack section. The remaining issue is quantitative control of `K_S^F` under the geometry of a fresh spatial block.

# 4. Exterior exposure around a fresh buffered region

Now specialize to a finite torus large enough that the sets below do not wrap; the bounds are uniform and hence pass to `Z^3` by local approximation.

Let

\[
S\subset U\subset\mathbb Z^3
\]

be finite. Freeze **all clocks on edges having both endpoints outside `U`**. Leave every clock on an edge incident to `U` free. Denote the frozen sigma-field by

\[
\mathcal F_{U^c}.
\]

This is deliberately stronger conditioning than a normal exploration needs. Uniform bounds under `F_{U^c}` therefore survive any coarser exposure by the tower property.

Let

\[
b:=\operatorname{dist}(S,U^c).
\]

For a tagged particle starting at `x in S`, until its first exit from `U` every incident edge clock it can see is still an ordinary independent rate-one Poisson clock. Consequently, before exit from `U`, its conditional trajectory is exactly the standard continuous-time simple random walk `X`.

Put

\[
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
:=
\sup_{x\in S}
\mathbb P_x(\tau_{U^c}\le\beta).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The usual continuous-time random-walk maximal deviation estimate gives constants `c,C` such that

\[
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
\le
C\exp(-c b^2/\beta)
\tag{4.2}
\]

when `b` is at least a constant multiple of `sqrt(beta)`.

# 5. NEW-L26 — quenched one-period anti-concentration in a fresh block

Let

\[
K^{\mathcal F}:=K_\beta^{\mathcal F_{U^c}}.
\]

## Theorem NEW-L26 — PROVED

For every exterior clock realization and every `x,y in S`,

\[
\boxed{
K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)
\le
p_\beta(x,y)+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
\le
C\beta^{-3/2}+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Moreover, if

\[
H_U(x,y)
:=
\mathbb P_x(X(\beta)=y,\ \tau_{U^c}>\beta),
\tag{5.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)\ge H_U(x,y).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Split the conditional tagged-particle event according to whether the path exits `U` before time `beta`.

On `{tau_{U^c}>beta}`, all clocks encountered by the particle are free clocks, so the conditional path law is exactly ordinary simple random walk. This gives the contribution `H_U(x,y)` and proves (5.3).

The contribution from paths that exit `U` is at most the probability of the exit event, regardless of the adversarially frozen exterior clock realization after the exit. Therefore

\[
K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)
\le
H_U(x,y)+\mathbb P_x(\tau_{U^c}\le\beta)
\le
p_\beta(x,y)+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}.
\]

The standard heat-kernel bound in dimension three gives `sup_{x,y}p_beta(x,y)<=C beta^{-3/2}`. `square`

The point of (5.1) is that **arbitrary exterior history cannot concentrate a fresh particle endpoint** unless the particle first traverses the buffer.

# 6. NEW-L27 — quenched fresh-block cycle-trap bound

The main difficulty after conditioning is that `K_S^F` is generally non-symmetric, so the earlier unconditional positive-semidefinite eigenvalue argument cannot be reused. A singular-value estimate repairs this.

Set

\[
a_{\beta,b}:=C\beta^{-3/2}+\varepsilon_{\beta,b},
\qquad
h^2:=|S|a_{\beta,b}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Assume

\[
h\le\frac12.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## Theorem NEW-L27 — PROVED

Uniformly over every realization of the clocks on edges wholly outside `U`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists\ \pi_\beta\text{-cycle wholly contained in }S
\ \middle|\ \mathcal F_{U^c}
\right)
\le
2h^2
\le
2|S|\left(C\beta^{-3/2}+\varepsilon_{\beta,b}\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\det(I-K_S^{\mathcal F})
\ge
\exp(-2h^2)
\ge
1-2h^2.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

At `z=1`, NEW-L24 with `A=P_S` gives

\[
D_S^{\mathcal F}:=
\det(I-K_S^{\mathcal F})
=
\mathbb P(
\text{no complete permutation cycle is wholly contained in }S
\mid\mathcal F_{U^c}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

Hence `D_S^F` is real and lies in `[0,1]` despite the possible non-normality of `K_S^F`.

By (5.1),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}K_S^{\mathcal F}
\le
|S|a_{\beta,b}=h^2.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Also, because `K^F` is stochastic and nonnegative,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|K_S^{\mathcal F}\|_{HS}^2
&=
\sum_{x,y\in S}K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)^2\\
&\le
\left(\sup_{x,y\in S}K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)\right)
\sum_{x\in S}\sum_{y\in S}K^{\mathcal F}(x,y)\\
&\le
a_{\beta,b}|S|
=h^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus every singular value of `K_S^F` is at most `h<=1/2`, so its spectral radius is less than one. Therefore

\[
-\log D_S^{\mathcal F}
=
\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}((K_S^{\mathcal F})^m)}{m}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The entries of `K_S^F` are nonnegative, so every trace in (6.8) is nonnegative. For `m>=2`, Schatten norm monotonicity gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}((K_S^{\mathcal F})^m)
\le
\left|\operatorname{Tr}((K_S^{\mathcal F})^m)\right|
\le
\|K_S^{\mathcal F}\|_{S_m}^m
\le
\|K_S^{\mathcal F}\|_{HS}^m
\le
h^m.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Together with (6.6),

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\log D_S^{\mathcal F}
&\le
h^2+
\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{h^m}{m}\\
&=
h^2-\log(1-h)-h.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.10}
\]

For `0<=h<=1/2`,

\[
-\log(1-h)-h
\le h^2,
\]

so

\[
-\log D_S^{\mathcal F}\le2h^2.
\]

This proves the first inequality in (6.4). Finally,

\[
1-D_S^{\mathcal F}
\le
1-e^{-2h^2}
\le2h^2,
\]

and by (6.5) the left side is precisely the conditional probability of at least one complete cycle wholly contained in `S`. `square`

# 7. Dimension-three diffusive fresh-block corollary

Choose a buffer width

\[
b=A\sqrt{\beta\log\beta}
\tag{7.1}
\]

with `A` large enough that

\[
\varepsilon_{\beta,b}
\le C\beta^{-3/2}
\tag{7.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `beta`. Then NEW-L27 gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
\exists\text{ complete }\pi_\beta\text{-cycle in }S
\mid\mathcal F_{U^c}
\right)
\le
C'|S|\beta^{-3/2},
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

uniformly over the entire exterior clock realization, provided the right side is sufficiently small.

In particular, for every sufficiently small fixed `eta>0`, if

\[
|S|\le\eta\beta^{3/2}
\tag{7.4}
\]

and `S` has the buffer (7.1), then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\text{all exterior clock realizations}}
\mathbb P(\exists\text{ complete cycle in }S\mid\mathcal F_{U^c})
\le C'\eta.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus a genuinely fresh spatial block of **diffusive volume order `beta^{3/2}`** has a uniformly small chance to contain a closed interchange cycle, even after arbitrary exposure of all clocks outside a logarithmically enlarged buffer.

This is qualitatively different from the earlier unconditional finite-set bound: (7.5) is a **quenched robustness statement against arbitrary exterior history**.

# 8. Coarser adaptive explorations are automatically covered

Suppose an actual cyclic-walk exploration has revealed some sigma-field `G` consisting entirely of information from edges wholly outside the buffer `U`, but has not exposed any clock on an edge incident to `U`. It need not reveal all exterior clocks.

Because (7.5) holds for every realization of the finer sigma-field `F_{U^c}`, the tower property gives

\[
\mathbb P(\exists\text{ complete cycle in }S\mid\mathcal G)
\le C'\eta.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Therefore the result is compatible with adaptive exploration: once a block and its buffer are genuinely untouched, arbitrary information accumulated elsewhere does not destroy the local cycle-trap estimate.

This is the exact sense in which the new lemma can feed a multiscale local-escape scheme.

# 9. Relation to Elboim–Sly's dimension barrier

Elboim–Sly explain that their `d>=5` proof relies on a fresh random walk having positive probability to completely avoid its past, while in lower dimensions past intersections become too frequent. Their escape algorithm nevertheless uses the principle that entering a small unexplored region gives a non-negligible chance to escape far before complicated history dominates.

NEW-L24–L27 isolate a different kind of freshness that does **not** require geometric avoidance of the entire past:

* arbitrary clocks outside a protected buffer may be exposed/adversarial;
* the exact determinant identity survives that conditioning;
* as long as the clocks incident to the buffer remain unexposed, the conditional one-particle kernel inside has ordinary heat-kernel anti-concentration up to the buffer-exit error;
* a block of volume `eta beta^{3/2}` then has only `O(eta)` conditional probability to contain a complete permutation cycle.

This does not yet prove local escape of the cyclic walk. It controls **local cycle trapping**, not all possible entanglements with earlier strands. But it removes one previously global obstruction in a way that is specific to the interchange's fermionic determinant structure and remains valid in dimension three.

# 10. A stronger conditional localized orbit comparison

NEW-L26 also gives an entrywise lower bound

\[
K_S^{\mathcal F}\ge H_U[S,S],
\tag{10.1}
\]

where `H_U` is the one-period simple-random-walk kernel killed on exiting `U` during the physical-time slab.

Since both matrices are nonnegative,

\[
K_S^{\mathcal F}(I-zK_S^{\mathcal F})^{-1}
\ge
H_U[S,S](I-zH_U[S,S])^{-1}
\tag{10.2}
\]

entrywise for `0<=z<1`.

Combining (10.2) with NEW-L25 gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\Delta_{S,z}G^\pi_{S,z}(u,v)
\mid\mathcal F_{U^c}
\right]
\ge
D_{S,z}^{\mathcal F}
\left[
H_U[S,S](I-zH_U[S,S])^{-1}
\right]_{vu}.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

When `S` satisfies the fresh-block hypotheses and `z` is bounded away from one, the determinant factor is bounded below uniformly. Hence the cyclic orbit has, in this weighted localized sense, at least a constant fraction of the killed random-walk Green profile **even after arbitrary exterior exposure**.

The caution is essential: (10.3) is an expectation with the local cycle-suppression factor `Delta_{S,z}`. It is not yet an unweighted pathwise coupling or a complete local-escape theorem.

# 11. Failed shortcut found in this run

A tempting argument is to try to reuse the old unconditional inequality

\[
1-\det(I-K_S)\le\operatorname{Tr}K_S
\]

after conditioning. This is invalid in general because the conditional kernel `K_S^F` is non-self-adjoint: frozen exterior transpositions can make it highly non-normal, and a nonnegative matrix may have `Tr K=0` while `Tr K^2>0` (the two-state swap matrix is the simplest example).

The singular-value/Hilbert–Schmidt argument in NEW-L27 is required. This is why the buffer anti-concentration estimate enters quadratically through

\[
\|K_S^F\|_{HS}^2\le |S|\sup_{x,y\in S}K^F(x,y).
\]

This route was checked explicitly to avoid importing a false PSD argument into the conditioned setting.

# 13. Exact remaining gap

The new result says that an untouched buffered block has low conditional probability of containing a **locally closed permutation cycle**, regardless of arbitrary information outside the buffer. It does not yet control all ways the current cyclic strand can interact with previously explored strands after leaving the block.

The missing theorem is a multiscale exploration statement of the form:

> with sufficiently large fixed `beta`, whenever the cyclic walk enters a fresh buffered block, the probability—conditional on the entire admissible past—that it acquires a useful spatial displacement before joining a large old entanglement cluster is bounded below strongly enough to keep the regenerated/concatenated walk diffusive in d=3.

NEW-L24 supplies the exact conditional algebra needed for the fresh block. What remains is the geometry of **recruitment from the old history across the buffer**.

1. Let `S` be a small diffusive block of volume `eta beta^(3/2)` and `U` its `A sqrt(beta log beta)` buffer.
