# 2. Exact bimodule decomposition of ordered tagged configurations

This section gives an exact representation-theoretic bridge between the tagged-particle twisted-trace formulation developed in the previous run and Alon–Kozma's irreducible character formula for cycle counts.

Let \(N=|V|\) on a finite graph and
\[
X_{N,k}=\operatorname{Inj}([k],[N])
\]
be the ordered injective \(k\)-tag configuration space. The group \(S_N\times S_k\) acts by
\[
(g,\sigma)\cdot f
=
g\circ f\circ\sigma^{-1}.
\]
For the standard injection \(\iota(i)=i\), its stabilizer is
\[
H
=
\{(\sigma\oplus\tau,\sigma):
\sigma\in S_k,\ \tau\in S_{N-k}\}
\cong S_k\times S_{N-k}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb C[X_{N,k}]
\cong
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{S_N\times S_k}{\bf1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let \(S_N^\lambda\) and \(S_k^\mu\) denote irreducible Specht modules. By Frobenius reciprocity, the multiplicity of
\[
S_N^\lambda\otimes S_k^\mu
\]
in (2.1) is
\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H}
\left(
{\bf1},
\operatorname{Res}_{H}(S_N^\lambda\otimes S_k^\mu)
\right).
\]
Restricting \(S_N^\lambda\) to \(S_k\times S_{N-k}\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Res}^{S_N}_{S_k\times S_{N-k}}S_N^\lambda
\cong
\bigoplus_{\alpha\vdash k,\nu\vdash N-k}
c_{\alpha,\nu}^{\lambda}
S_k^\alpha\otimes S_{N-k}^{\nu}.
\]
The diagonal \(S_k\) invariants force \(\alpha=\mu\), and the \(S_{N-k}\) invariants force \(\nu=(N-k)\). Therefore:

## NEW-L1 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb C[X_{N,k}]
\cong
\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash N,\mu\vdash k}
 c_{\mu,(N-k)}^{\lambda}
 \;S_N^\lambda\otimes S_k^\mu.
}
\tag{NEW-L1}
\]

By Pieri's rule,
\[
c_{\mu,(N-k)}^\lambda\in\{0,1\},
\]
and it is one exactly when \(\lambda/\mu\) is a horizontal strip of size \(N-k\).

The interchange generator belongs to the left \(S_N\) group algebra and therefore acts on the \(S_N^\lambda\) factor, while label permutations act on the \(S_k^\mu\) factor.

# 3. Exact bridge from tagged cyclic twist to Alon–Kozma coefficients

Let \(c_k=(1\,2\,\cdots\,k)\in S_k\). Let \(K_t^{(k)}\) be the ordered \(k\)-tag interchange semigroup. Taking the trace after the cyclic label twist and using NEW-L1 gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_t^{(k)}U_{c_k})
=
\sum_{\lambda\vdash N}
 b_{\lambda,k}
 \operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t\Delta_G^\lambda}),
\tag{3.1}
\]
where
\[
b_{\lambda,k}
=
\sum_{\mu\vdash k}
\chi_\mu(c_k)c_{\mu,(N-k)}^\lambda.
\]
Murnaghan–Nakayama says \(\chi_\mu(c_k)=0\) unless \(\mu\) is a hook \((k-j,1^j)\), in which case it is \((-1)^j\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
b_{\lambda,k}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
(-1)^j c_{(k-j,1^j),(N-k)}^\lambda.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Now use the symmetric-function identity
\[
p_k
=
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^j s_{(k-j,1^j)}
\]
and Pieri multiplication by \(h_{N-k}\). The coefficient of \(s_\lambda\) in \(p_kh_{N-k}\) is exactly (3.2).

Alon–Kozma Lemma 3 gives
\[
\operatorname{ch}(\alpha_k)
=
\frac1k p_k h_{N-k},
\]
where \(\alpha_k(g)\) is the number of \(k\)-cycles of \(g\). Therefore:

## NEW-L2 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
b_{\lambda,k}
=
k\langle\alpha_k,\chi_\lambda\rangle.}
\tag{NEW-L2}
\]

This is an exact, independent derivation of the bridge between the tagged cyclic twisted trace and the Alon–Kozma character decomposition. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(K_t^{(k)}U_{c_k})
=k\,\mathbb E s_k(t)
\]
recovers their spectral formula coefficient by coefficient.

For \(k\le N/2\), Theorem 3 of Alon–Kozma simplifies to
\[
\boxed{
 k\,\mathbb E s_k(t)
 =
 1+
 \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^j
 \operatorname{Tr}
 \left(
 e^{-t\Delta_G^{[N-k,k-j,1^j]}}
 \right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Only the trivial representation and the explicit chain of shapes
\[
[N-k,k],
[N-k,k-1,1],\ldots,
[N-k,1^k]
\]
appear.

On a vertex-transitive \(N\)-vertex graph,
\[
\mathbb P(L_t(o)=k)
=
\frac1N
\left[
1+
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^j
\operatorname{Tr}
(e^{-t\Delta_G^{[N-k,k-j,1^j]}})
\right].
\tag{3.4}
\]

This makes the required cancellation much more explicit than a general \(k\)-tag heat-kernel bound.

# 4. New exact character cutoff for the long-cycle vertex mass

Define the class function
\[
F_K(g)
:=
\sum_{r=K}^{N} r\,\alpha_r(g).
\]
It is exactly the number of vertices of \(g\) lying in cycles of length at least \(K\).

Alon–Kozma observe that every nontrivial shape
\[
\rho=[a,b,1^c],
\qquad a+b+c=N,
\]
appears in the cycle-count decomposition for exactly two cycle lengths, with opposite signs. Reading those lengths from their Theorem 3 gives
\[
r_-(\rho)=N-a=b+c,
\qquad
r_+(\rho)=N-b+1=a+c+1,
\]
with coefficients \((-1)^c\) at \(r_-\) and \((-1)^{c+1}\) at \(r_+\) in the scaled class function \(r\alpha_r\).

Therefore summing \(r\alpha_r\) over \(r\ge K\) cancels every representation for which both associated lengths lie on the same side of the cutoff.

## NEW-L3 — PROVED

For every \(1\le K\le N\),
\[
\boxed{
F_K
=
(N-K+1)\chi_{[N]}
+
\sum_{\substack{\rho=[a,b,1^c]\\
N-a<K\le N-b+1}}
(-1)^{c+1}\chi_\rho.
}
\tag{NEW-L3}
\]

If \(K\le N/2\), the second inequality is automatic once \(N-a<K\). Indeed \(a>N-K\) implies
\[
b\le N-a<K\le N/2,
\]
so \(N-b+1>K\). Hence:

## NEW-L3a — PROVED

For \(K\le N/2\),
\[
\boxed{
F_K
=
(N-K+1)\chi_{[N]}
+
\sum_{\substack{a+b+c=N\\a\ge b\ge1\\a>N-K}}
(-1)^{c+1}\chi_{[a,b,1^c]}.
}
\tag{NEW-L3a}
\]

Equivalently, writing \(m=N-a\),
\[
\boxed{
F_K
=
(N-K+1)\chi_{[N]}
+
\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}
\sum_{b=1}^{m}
(-1)^{m-b+1}
\chi_{[N-m,b,1^{m-b}]}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Taking interchange expectations gives the exact finite-graph spectral identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E F_K(\pi_t)
=
N-K+1
+
\sum_{m=1}^{K-1}
\sum_{b=1}^{m}
(-1)^{m-b+1}
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(e^{-t\Delta_G^{[N-m,b,1^{m-b}]}}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

On a vertex-transitive graph,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(L_t(o)\ge K)
=
\frac1N\mathbb E F_K(\pi_t).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Significance for the torus route

For a cutoff \(K\le N/2\), the long-cycle event depends spectrally only on irreducible representations with fewer than \(K\) boxes below the first row. Thus the fixed-torus route from the archived report can be sharpened:

> To prove a positive fraction of roots in cycles of a diverging scale \(K_N\), it is enough to control this explicit low-degree family of representations for \(m<K_N\), preserving the alternating cancellation in (4.2).

This is much narrower than controlling the full interchange spectrum or all \(k\)-tag kernels.

The boundary term \([N-m,1^m]\) is a full hook and is explicitly controlled by the one-particle spectrum: if \(0=\lambda_0\le\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_{N-1}\) are the one-particle Laplacian eigenvalues, then
\[
\operatorname{Tr}
(e^{-t\Delta_G^{[N-m,1^m]}})
=
 e_m(e^{-t\lambda_1},\ldots,e^{-t\lambda_{N-1}}).
\tag{4.4}
\]
The opposite two-row endpoint \([N-m,m]\) sits inside the ordinary \(m\)-particle exclusion representation. The unresolved terms are the intermediate shapes.

# 5. New master determinant identity for the interchange permutation

This is the strongest new exact identity in the present iteration.

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite weighted graph, \(|V|=N\). Let \(\Pi_t\) denote the random permutation matrix of the interchange process acting on
\[
H=\mathbb C^V.
\]
Let \(T_e\) be the one-particle transposition matrix for edge \(e\), and write the one-particle generator as
\[
L_1
=
\sum_{e}a_e(T_e-I),
\qquad
p_t=e^{tL_1}.
\]

For \(0\le j\le N\), let \(\bigwedge^jH\) be the \(j\)-th exterior power.

## Step 1: exterior expectation is exactly free

The interchange process in the exterior representation has generator
\[
\sum_e a_e(\bigwedge^j T_e-I).
\]
For one edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), put
\[
a_e^-=(e_u-e_v)/\sqrt2.
\]
Then
\[
T_e=I-2P_{a_e^-}.
\]
Because the antisymmetric mode \(a_e^-\) is one-dimensional, a fermionic wedge state can occupy it at most once. Hence on \(\bigwedge^jH\),
\[
\bigwedge^jT_e-I
=
d\Gamma_j(T_e-I),
\tag{5.1}
\]
where \(d\Gamma_j\) is fermionic second quantization. Summing edges,
\[
\sum_ea_e(\bigwedge^jT_e-I)
=
d\Gamma_j(L_1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\bigwedge^j\Pi_t]
=e^{t d\Gamma_j(L_1)}
=\bigwedge^j p_t.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This recovers, in a representation-independent way, the free-fermion endpoint found in the previous hook-sector report.

## Step 2: arbitrary deterministic matrix insertion

For any deterministic complex \(N\times N\) matrix \(A\), the exterior-power determinant identity gives
\[
\det(I-A\Pi_t)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{N}
(-1)^j
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(\bigwedge^j(A\Pi_t)\right).
\]
Since exterior powers are multiplicative,
\[
\bigwedge^j(A\Pi_t)
=(\bigwedge^jA)(\bigwedge^j\Pi_t).
\]
Taking expectations and using (5.2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\det(I-A\Pi_t)
&=
\sum_j(-1)^j
\operatorname{Tr}
\left((\bigwedge^jA)(\bigwedge^jp_t)\right)\\
&=
\sum_j(-1)^j
\operatorname{Tr}(\bigwedge^j(Ap_t))\\
&=
det(I-Ap_t).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus:

## NEW-L4 — PROVED: master determinant identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\det(I-A\Pi_t)
=
\det(I-Ap_t)
\qquad
\text{for every deterministic }A\in M_N(\mathbb C).
}
\tag{NEW-L4}
\]

No commutation assumption on \(A\) is required.

This identity controls every signed exterior minor of the random interchange permutation exactly by the one-particle heat kernel.

# 6. Exact cycle-generating consequences of NEW-L4

Let
\[
D_z=\operatorname{diag}(z_x:x\in V).
\]
For a deterministic permutation \(\pi\), its cycle decomposition gives
\[
\det(I-D_zP_\pi)
=
\prod_{C\in\mathcal C(\pi)}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}z_x\right).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Combining (6.1) with NEW-L4 yields:

## NEW-L5 — PROVED: multivariate cycle determinant

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\prod_{C\in\mathcal C(\pi_t)}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}z_x\right)
=
\det(I-D_zp_t).
}
\tag{NEW-L5}
\]

### Constant fugacity

Putting \(z_x=z\) for all vertices,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\prod_{C}(1-z^{|C|})
=
\det(I-zp_t)
=
\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}(1-ze^{-t\lambda_i}).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Because \(\lambda_0=0\), differentiating at \(z=1\) independently recovers the exact full-cycle formula. For a permutation with more than one cycle, the product on the left has a zero of order at least two at \(z=1\). For a single \(N\)-cycle its derivative at \(1\) equals \(-N\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\pi_t\text{ is one }N\text{-cycle})
=
\frac1N
\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1-e^{-t\lambda_i}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
This matches the known Alon–Kozma full-cycle formula and is a useful consistency check on NEW-L4.

### Spatial cycle avoidance

Let \(S\subseteq V\) and set
\[
z_x=\mathbf1_{\{x\in S\}}.
\]
For each cycle \(C\), its factor in (6.1) is zero exactly when \(C\subseteq S\); otherwise the factor is one. Hence the left side is the indicator that **no permutation cycle is entirely contained in \(S\)**. On the right, rows outside \(S\) are trivial, leaving the principal submatrix determinant. Therefore:

## NEW-L6 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\text{no cycle of }\pi_t\text{ is entirely contained in }S)
=
\det(I-p_t[S,S]).
}
\tag{NEW-L6}
\]

This is a positive-probability determinantal identity, not merely a signed character formula.

### Signed invariant-subset identity

Taking the coefficient of \(\prod_{x\in S}z_x\) in NEW-L5 gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\left[
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi_t|_S)
\mathbf1_{\{\pi_t(S)=S\}}
\right]
=
\det p_t[S,S].
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

For \(|S|=2\), this says that the probability of two separate fixed points minus the probability that the two vertices form a 2-cycle equals the corresponding \(2\times2\) heat-kernel determinant.

# 7. What NEW-L4 does and does not give

NEW-L4 is exact and substantially stronger than the scalar full-cycle formula, but it is still a **fermionic / signed** identity. The target probability of a finite root cycle is unsigned.

The key limitation is cancellation. For a fixed invariant set \(S\),
\[
\det p_t[S,S]
\]
is the difference between even and odd induced permutation probabilities, not their sum. Likewise (6.2) gives a product over cycles before expectation; taking a logarithm after expectation is not the same as the expected logarithm, so it does not directly recover \(\mathbb E s_k\).

Thus NEW-L4 does **not** by itself prove
\[
\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)<\infty)<1.
\]

Its main structural value is that the fully antisymmetric boundary of the Alon–Kozma chain is now completely explicit for arbitrary spatial insertions \(A\). The unresolved information in the long-cycle formula (4.2) lies in the non-fermionic intermediate representations.

Combine the archived torus-to-lattice transfer with NEW-L3a. Let \(T_n^3\) have \(N=n^3\) vertices, and choose a cutoff \(K_n\to\infty\) with \(K_n\le N/2\). Then exactly

# 9. New failure / caution identified this run

A tempting approach is to bound each trace in (3.3) or (8.1) separately and then take absolute values. That is structurally hostile to the problem: at \(t=0\) the nontrivial traces cancel the trivial term exactly, and the cycle-count class function itself is produced by alternating Pieri cancellations. Any estimate that loses those signs is likely exponentially too weak in \(k\) or \(m\).

Likewise, the exact master determinant controls only the exterior/fermionic information. It does not justify replacing the remaining intermediate representations by independent walkers or by absolute values.

The previous exact \(C_4\) obstruction also remains: constant-one domination of the whole cyclic twisted trace by the independent-walker comparator is false even for two labels.
