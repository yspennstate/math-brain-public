# 2. Master determinant identity

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected graph, \(|V|=N\). Let \(\Pi_t\) be the random permutation matrix of the interchange process at time \(t\), and let \(K_t\) be the one-particle heat kernel at the same time.

The previously archived identity is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\det(I-A\Pi_t)=\det(I-AK_t)
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

for every deterministic \(N\times N\) matrix \(A\).

For diagonal

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(a_x:x\in V),
\]

a deterministic permutation \(\pi\) with cycles \(C\) satisfies

\[
\det(I-A\pi)
=
\prod_{C}
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E
\prod_C
\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right)
=
\det(I-AK_t).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The previous run used the specialization \(a_x=z\) for every vertex. The present run singles out one root variable and obtains an exact Palm/resolvent identity.

# 3. NEW-L15 — exact rooted determinant / Palm identity

Fix a root \(o\in V\) and \(0\le z<1\). Let

\[
L=L_t(o)
\]

be the length of the permutation cycle containing \(o\), and let \(C_o\) denote that cycle. Define the suppression weight of all *other* cycles

\[
W_z
:=
\prod_{C\ne C_o}(1-z^{|C|}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define

\[
D_t(z):=\det(I-zK_t)
\tag{3.2}
\]

and the root resolvent quantity

\[
R_{t,o}(z)
:=
\bigl[K_t(I-zK_t)^{-1}\bigr]_{oo}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem NEW-L15 — PROVED

For every finite graph and every \(0\le z<1\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[z^{L-1}W_z\right]
=
D_t(z)R_{t,o}(z).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Also

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E W_z
=
D_t(z)\left(1+zR_{t,o}(z)\right).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof

Set all diagonal variables equal to \(z\), except at the root where \(a_o=u\). For a deterministic permutation,

\[
\prod_C\left(1-\prod_{x\in C}a_x\right)
=
(1-u z^{L-1})W_z.
\tag{3.6}
\]

On the matrix side let

\[
M=I-zK_t.
\]

Since

\[
\operatorname{diag}(a)
=zI+(u-z)e_oe_o^T,
\]

we have

\[
I-\operatorname{diag}(a)K_t
=M-(u-z)e_oe_o^TK_t.
\]

The matrix determinant lemma gives

\[
\det(I-\operatorname{diag}(a)K_t)
=D_t(z)
\left[1-(u-z)R_{t,o}(z)\right].
\tag{3.7}
\]

Differentiate in \(u\). From (3.6),

\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial u}
\mathbb E[(1-u z^{L-1})W_z]
=-\mathbb E[z^{L-1}W_z].
\]

From (3.7) the derivative is \(-D_t(z)R_{t,o}(z)\), proving (3.4).

Set \(u=0\). The root-cycle factor in (3.6) becomes one, so the left side is \(\mathbb EW_z\). Equation (3.7) becomes (3.5). \(\square\)

# 4. NEW-L16 — Bernoulli-mark Palm interpretation

Let each vertex be independently marked with probability

\[
r=1-z.
\]

Let \(H\) be the event that **every permutation cycle contains at least one marked vertex**. The previous checkpoint proves

\[
\mathbb P(H)=D_t(z).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let \(U_o\) be the event that \(H\) occurs and the root \(o\) is the **unique marked vertex on its own permutation cycle**.

Conditioned on the permutation, the probability of \(U_o\) is

\[
r z^{L-1}W_z.
\]

Therefore (3.4) gives:

## Theorem NEW-L16 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(U_o)
=
rD_t(z)R_{t,o}(z).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(U_o\mid H)
=
rR_{t,o}(z).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This ratio is notable because the exponentially small global determinant cancels completely.

There is a second useful version. Force the root \(o\) to be marked, while all other vertices are independently marked with probability \(r\). Let \(H_o\) be the event that every cycle is hit. Then conditioned on the permutation, the root cycle is automatically hit and only the other cycles contribute; hence

\[
\mathbb P(H_o)=\mathbb EW_z.
\]

By (3.5),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(H_o)
=D_t(z)(1+zR_{t,o}(z)).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Under this forced-root marking, the probability that the root remains the unique mark on its cycle and every other cycle is hit is exactly \(D_t(z)R_{t,o}(z)\). Thus

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(
 o\text{ is unique on its cycle}\mid H_o)
=
\frac{R_{t,o}(z)}{1+zR_{t,o}(z)}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equations (4.3) and (4.5) are exact rooted Palm formulas for the cycle-hitting ensemble.

# 5. NEW-L17 — explicit return-series formula on transitive graphs

Assume now that \(G\) is vertex-transitive. Since \(K_t\) commutes with \((I-zK_t)^{-1}\), the diagonal of the resolvent expression is constant. Expanding the geometric series,

\[
K_t(I-zK_t)^{-1}
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}K_t^m
=
\sum_{m\ge1}z^{m-1}K_{mt}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_t(z)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
 z^{m-1}p_{mt}(o,o).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

On three-dimensional tori, for each fixed \(0\le z<1\), the thermodynamic limit is

\[
\boxed{
R_{\beta,\infty}(z)
=
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
 z^{m-1}p_{m\beta}^{\mathbb Z^3}(0,0).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The convergence follows termwise from local convergence of torus heat kernels, with the geometric factor \(z^{m-1}\) providing immediate domination.

In infinite volume the right side also converges at \(z=1\), because in \(d=3\)

\[
p_t(0,0)\le Ct^{-3/2}.
\]

Using the local central limit theorem and dominated convergence, as \(\beta\to\infty\),

\[
\boxed{
R_{\beta,\infty}(z)
=
\frac{\beta^{-3/2}}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}
\left(
\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{3/2}(z)}{z}
+o(1)
\right)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

for fixed \(z\in(0,1]\). At \(z=0\), interpret the quotient by continuity, giving

\[
R_{\beta,\infty}(0)=p_\beta(0,0)
\sim(4\pi\beta)^{-3/2}.
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
R_{\beta,\infty}(1)
\sim
\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}}\beta^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

So the cycle-hitting Palm ensemble has a completely explicit root resolvent at the same \(\beta^{-3/2}\) scale that has repeatedly appeared in the determinant pressure calculations.

# 6. NEW-L18 — exact conditional density of singly-marked cycles

Under Bernoulli marking, let \(N_1\) be the number of permutation cycles that contain **exactly one** marked vertex. On the event \(H\), every such cycle has one distinguished marked vertex. Summing the indicator \(U_o\) over all roots counts every singly-marked cycle exactly once. Therefore on a transitive \(N\)-vertex graph,

\[
\mathbb E[N_1\mathbf1_H]
=N rD_t(z)R_t(z).
\]

Dividing by \(D_t(z)=\mathbb P(H)\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[N_1\mid H]
=N rR_t(z).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, the density of singly-marked cycles in the cycle-hitting tilted ensemble is exactly

\[
\boxed{
rR_t(z).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

This also follows by differentiating the determinant:

\[
-D_t'(z)
=D_t(z)\operatorname{Tr}
[K_t(I-zK_t)^{-1}]
=ND_t(z)R_t(z),
\]

while conditioned on the permutation

\[
-r\frac{d}{dz}
\prod_C(1-z^{|C|})
\]

is precisely the probability-weighted number of cycles containing exactly one mark.

# 7. NEW-NOGO1 — thermodynamic hitting pressure is blind to giant versus diverging finite cycles

The determinant and forest identities are exact, but the preceding runs encountered a persistent obstruction: they naturally control **cycle-count pressure**, whereas the conjecture asks for **size-biased root mass**. The following deterministic theorem makes that obstruction rigorous.

For a partition \(\mathcal P_N\) of \(N\) vertices into cycle blocks of sizes \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_k\), define its Bernoulli-hitting product at fugacity \(z\in[0,1)\) by

\[
\Phi_z(\mathcal P_N)
:=
\prod_{i=1}^k(1-z^{\ell_i}).
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is exactly the conditional probability that Bernoulli marks of density \(1-z\) hit every cycle.

Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1]\). Let \(L_N\to\infty\) with \(L_N=o(N)\). Consider two cycle partitions that are identical on \((1-\alpha)N+o(N)\) background vertices:

* **giant version** \(\mathcal P_N^{\rm giant}\): the remaining \(\alpha N+o(N)\) vertices form one cycle;
* **mesoscopic all-finite version** \(\mathcal P_N^{\rm meso}\): the remaining \(\alpha N+o(N)\) vertices are partitioned into cycles of length \(L_N\), up to at most one remainder block.

## Theorem NEW-NOGO1 — PROVED

For every fixed \(z\in[0,1)\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac1N
\left|
\log\Phi_z(\mathcal P_N^{\rm giant})
-
\log\Phi_z(\mathcal P_N^{\rm meso})
\right|
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

More generally, the same conclusion holds for a sequence \(z_N\uparrow1\) whenever

\[
\boxed{
\frac{-\log(1-z_N^{L_N})}{L_N}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

In particular, it holds in the natural resolution regime

\[
1-z_N\asymp L_N^{-1},
\]

because then \(z_N^{L_N}\to e^{-c}\in(0,1)\) along subsequences.

### Proof

The common background contributions cancel. The giant block contributes

\[
\frac1N\log(1-z_N^{\alpha N+o(N)}).
\]

Under (7.3), \(z_N^{L_N}\) is not so close to one that its logarithmic cost is exponential in \(L_N\). Since \(N/L_N\to\infty\), the giant exponent is at least of order \(N/L_N\) times the mesoscopic exponent scale, and the giant contribution divided by \(N\) vanishes. For fixed \(z<1\), this is immediate from exponential decay of \(z^{\alpha N}\).

The mesoscopic part contains \(\alpha N/L_N+o(N/L_N)\) blocks, so its normalized contribution is

\[
\frac{\alpha}{L_N}
\log(1-z_N^{L_N})+o(1),
\]

which tends to zero exactly by (7.3). The one remainder block contributes \(o(1)\) after normalization. \(\square\)

### Consequence

Any theorem based only on the thermodynamic limit

\[
-\frac1N\log\Phi_z
\]

at fixed fugacity, or even at the natural near-one scaling \(1-z\asymp1/L_N\), **cannot by itself distinguish** a positive macroscopic infinite/giant-cycle mass from the same mass broken into finite cycles whose lengths merely diverge.

To detect the difference through this observable one must tune \(z_N\) exponentially close to one on the candidate finite-cycle scale, so that

\[
-\log(1-z_N^{L_N})
\asymp L_N,
\]

i.e. roughly

\[
1-z_N\lesssim e^{-cL_N}/L_N.
\]

That regime probes subextensive finite-size corrections rather than the pressure controlled in the previous determinant checkpoints.

This is a rigorous no-go result for the **pressure-only** route, not for determinant methods altogether.

# 8. Why the rooted Palm identity is the right missing observable

The no-go theorem shows why the previous cycle-pressure asymptotics cannot settle the conjecture: normalized pressure charges cycles by their **number**, with each cycle of length \(L\) contributing only order \(1/L\) to the pressure when the total vertex mass is fixed. Replacing a giant cycle by diverging finite cycles therefore becomes invisible at leading extensive order.

The conjecture instead asks for the root-size-biased quantity

\[
\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)=\infty),
\]

or equivalently for control of

\[
\mathbb E[z^{L_\beta(0)};L_\beta(0)<\infty]
\]

at near-one fugacities resolving large cycle lengths.

NEW-L15 gives an exact rooted transform, but with the extra suppression factor

\[
W_z=
\prod_{C\ne C_o}(1-z^{|C|}).
\]

Since \(0\le W_z\le1\),

\[
\boxed{
D_t(z)R_{t,o}(z)
\le
\mathbb E[z^{L-1};L<\infty].
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus the determinant identity presently gives a lower bound on the ordinary root Laplace transform, whereas proving infinite cycles requires an effective **upper** control on its total finite-cycle mass as \(z\uparrow1\).

The exact unresolved determinant-side problem is now sharply isolated:

> Remove or control the other-cycle suppression factor \(W_z\) without losing positivity and without paying an extensive factor.

Equivalently, compare the ordinary root law to the cycle-hitting Palm law (4.5) with a bound uniform in the candidate cycle scale.

This is more precise than the previous statement that “cycle pressure is not size-biased.”

# 9. Relation to the finite-torus transfer

The archived transfer theorem says that, for fixed \(\beta\), if on \(T_N^3\) a positive fraction of roots lie in cycles whose lengths \(a_N\to\infty\), then the infinite lattice has a positive infinite-cycle probability.

NEW-NOGO1 explains why extensive determinant pressure does not force that hypothesis: an all-finite torus phase with a positive vertex fraction in cycles of length \(L_N\to\infty\) has the same leading hitting pressure as a phase with that fraction in giant cycles.

That all-finite scenario is precisely what the transfer theorem needs us to exclude. Therefore the next successful determinant argument must be **rooted or subextensive**, not solely a bulk pressure computation.
