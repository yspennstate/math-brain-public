## Exact cyclic phase invariance

Let \(P=\pi_\beta\). For \(0\le s\le\beta\), let \(Q_s\) be the permutation from marks in \([0,s]\), and \(R_s\) the independent future permutation from \((s,\beta]\), so \(P=R_sQ_s\).

For an integer \(r\ge1\), define
\[
D_{r,s}:=Q_s(P^r0)-Q_s(0).
\]
For any finite set \(r_1,\dots,r_m\) and bounded measurable \(F\),
\[
\mathbb E F(D_{r_1,s},\dots,D_{r_m,s})
=
\mathbb E F(P^{r_1}0,\dots,P^{r_m}0).
\tag{BF-L6}
\]

### Proof

Rotate the Poisson time circle by \(s\): first use the marks from \((s,\beta]\), then those from \([0,s)\). Denote the rotated environment by \(\rho_s\omega\). Its one-period permutation is
\[
P_s=Q_sR_s=Q_s P Q_s^{-1}.
\]
Let \(a=Q_s0\), and let \(\theta_a\) re-root space so that \(a\) becomes the origin. Define
\[
\Xi_s\omega:=\theta_a\rho_s\omega.
\]
To see that \(\Xi_s\) preserves the Poisson interchange law, use mass transport
\[
M(\omega,u,v)=\mathbf 1_{\{Q_su=v\}}f(\theta_v\rho_s\omega).
\]
Mass transport gives
\[
\mathbb E\sum_vM(\omega,0,v)=\mathbb E\sum_uM(\omega,u,0).
\]
The left side is \(\mathbb Ef(\Xi_s\omega)\). Since \(Q_s\) is a bijection, exactly one \(u\) maps to zero; the right side is \(\mathbb Ef(\rho_s\omega)=\mathbb Ef(\omega)\), because deterministic time rotation preserves the product Poisson law.

Let \(\widehat P\) be the period permutation in \(\Xi_s\omega\). Then
\[
\widehat P(x)=P_s(x+a)-a.
\]
Using \((Q_sR_s)^rQ_s=Q_s(R_sQ_s)^r=Q_sP^r\),
\[
\widehat P^r0=P_s^r(a)-a=Q_sP^r0-Q_s0=D_{r,s}.
\]
Since \(\Xi_s\omega\) has the original law, the vector of these powers has the same law as the original rooted orbit powers. This proves BF-L6.

Hence for every finite \(A\subset\mathbb Z^d\),
\[
\mathbb E\int_0^\beta\mathbf 1_{\{D_{r,s}\in A\}}\,ds
=
\beta\,\mathbb P(P^r0\in A).
\tag{BF-L6a}
\]

## Exact two-step orbit kernel

Let \(Q_t^{(2)}((a,b),(c,d))\) be the transition kernel of two distinct tagged chronological interchange labels.

For \(x\ne0\),
\[
\mathbb P(P^20=x)
=
\sum_{y\notin\{0,x\}}
Q_\beta^{(2)}((0,y),(y,x)).
\tag{BF-L7}
\]
For \(x=0\),
\[
\mathbb P(P^20=0)
=
p_\beta(0,0)
+
\sum_{y\ne0}Q_\beta^{(2)}((0,y),(y,0)).
\tag{BF-L7-0}
\]

### Proof

On \(P^20=x\), write \(y=P0\). If \(x\ne0\), bijectivity forces \(y\notin\{0,x\}\), and the event is exactly \(P(0)=y, P(y)=x\). For a fixed \(y\), this is the event that the two tagged labels initially at \((0,y)\) end at \((y,x)\). Summing over the unique intermediate \(y\) gives BF-L7. The \(x=0\) formula separates the fixed-point case \(y=0\).

## Fixed-r=2 diffusive pointwise bound

The two-tag process is a symmetric continuous-time random walk on
\[
\mathcal C_2=\{(x,y)\in(\mathbb Z^d)^2:x\ne y\},
\]
with bounded degrees. For fixed \(d\ge2\), \(\mathcal C_2\) is roughly isometric to \(\mathbb Z^{2d}\): one can route the two particles one at a time and detour around the other in dimension at least two. Delmotte's Gaussian heat-kernel theorem plus rough-isometry stability therefore yields
\[
Q_t^{(2)}(A,B)
\le Ct^{-d}\exp(-c\,d_{\mathcal C_2}(A,B)^2/t),
\qquad t\ge1.
\]
For the kernel in BF-L7, the product displacement is controlled by \(|y|^2+|x-y|^2\), so
\[
Q_\beta^{(2)}((0,y),(y,x))
\le C\beta^{-d}
\exp\left[-c\frac{|y|^2+|x-y|^2}{\beta}\right].
\]
Summing over \(y\) and using
\[
|y|^2+|x-y|^2=2|y-x/2|^2+|x|^2/2
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(P^20=x)
\le C_d\beta^{-d/2}e^{-c_d|x|^2/\beta}.
}
\tag{BF-L8}
\]
In \(d=3\),
\[
\sup_x\mathbb P(P^20=x)\le C\beta^{-3/2}.
\]
By BF-L6a, lag-two same-phase adjacency occupation obeys
\[
\mathbb E\int_0^\beta
\mathbf 1_{\{|Q_s(P^20)-Q_s0|_1=1\}}\,ds
\le C\beta^{-1/2}
\qquad(d=3).
\tag{BF-L8a}
\]
This is an occupation bound, not yet a hitting-probability bound.
