## 1. Infinite-volume and cyclic notation

The Harris construction makes \(\pi_\beta\) an almost-sure bijection because a tagged label has finite total jump rate on bounded physical time intervals and the graphical arrows can be followed backward.

For \(0\le s<\beta\),
\[
W(k\beta+s)=\pi_s\pi_\beta^k(0),
\qquad W(k\beta)=\pi_\beta^k(0).
\]
A finite cycle of length \(q\) is equivalent to \(W(q\beta)=0\).

Let \(p_t(x,y)\) be the one-particle continuous-time SRW heat kernel. Let \(Q_t^{(2)}((a,b),(x,z))\) be the ordered two-tag chronological interchange kernel. The two-tag generator is symmetric, so
\[
Q_t^{(2)}((a,b),(x,z))
=
Q_t^{(2)}((x,z),(a,b)),
\]
and both one- and two-tag kernels are translation invariant.

## 2. Final-successor pair and exact phase invariance

Fix \(0\le s\le\beta\). Let
\[
Y:=\pi_\beta(0),\qquad
A_s:=\pi_s(0),\qquad
B_s:=\pi_s(Y).
\]
Thus \((A_s,B_s)\) are the same-phase positions of label \(0\) and its **final permutation successor**.

### BF-S1 — PROVED

For every bounded \(\varphi:\mathbb Z^d\to\mathbb R\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\varphi(B_s-A_s)
=
\sum_{e\in\mathbb Z^d}
\varphi(e)p_\beta(0,e).
}
\tag{BF-S1}
\]
Hence
\[
B_s-A_s\stackrel d=X(\beta),
\]
where \(X\) is ordinary continuous-time SRW. This is a full cyclic-slab identity with no pre-first-interaction restriction.

### Proof

Write \(\pi_\beta=R\circ Q\), with \(Q=\pi_s\) and \(R\) the independent future interchange evolution of duration \(t=\beta-s\).

The contribution \(Y=0\) is
\[
\varphi(0)\mathbb P(\pi_\beta(0)=0)
=
\varphi(0)p_\beta(0,0).
\]
For \(Y=y\ne0\), condition on \(Q(0)=x\) and \(Q(y)=z\). Since \(Y=\pi_\beta(0)=R(x)\), independence of \(R\) gives
\[
\mathbb E[\varphi(B_s-A_s);Y\ne0]
=
\sum_{y\ne0}\sum_{x\ne z}
\varphi(z-x)
Q_s^{(2)}((0,y),(x,z))
p_t(x,y).
\]
Use two-tag reversibility, then set
\[
e=z-x,\qquad a=-x,\qquad b=y-x.
\]
Translation invariance gives
\[
Q_s^{(2)}((x,z),(0,y))
=
Q_s^{(2)}((0,e),(a,b)),
\quad p_t(x,y)=p_t(0,b).
\]
For fixed \(e,b\), summing over \(a\) leaves the second-tag marginal
\[
\sum_aQ_s^{(2)}((0,e),(a,b))=p_s(e,b).
\]
Therefore the coefficient of \(\varphi(e)\), for \(e\ne0\), is
\[
\sum_bp_s(e,b)p_t(0,b)
=
\sum_bp_s(e,b)p_t(b,0)
=
p_\beta(e,0)
=
p_\beta(0,e).
\]
Together with the \(e=0\) term this proves BF-S1.

### Corollary BF-S1a — exact adjacency occupation

Define
\[
\mathcal A_\beta
=
\int_0^\beta
\mathbf1_{\{|B_s-A_s|_1=1\}}ds.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\mathcal A_\beta
=2d\,\beta\,p_\beta(0,e_1).
}
\tag{BF-S1a}
\]
For fixed \(d>2\), local CLT gives \(O_d(\beta^{1-d/2})\); in \(d=3\),
\[
\mathbb E\mathcal A_\beta=O(\beta^{-1/2}).
\]

## 3. Direct swaps with the final successor

Let \(J_\beta\) count Poisson bars in \([0,\beta]\) that directly swap label \(0\) with its eventual final successor \(Y=\pi_\beta(0)\), with the count defined as zero if \(Y=0\).

### BF-S2 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EJ_\beta
=2d\,\beta\,p_\beta(0,0).
}
\tag{BF-S2}
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb P(J_\beta\ge1)
\le2d\beta p_\beta(0,0)
=O_d(\beta^{1-d/2}),
\]
and in \(d=3\) the probability is \(O(\beta^{-1/2})\).

### Proof

Apply Mecke/Campbell to insert one bar at phase \(s\) on an oriented nearest-neighbor edge \(u\to v=u+e\). Immediately before the inserted bar, suppose label \(0\) sits at \(u\) and its final successor label \(Y\) sits at \(v\). With \(Q=\pi_s\) and independent future \(R\) of duration \(t=\beta-s\), the inserted bar directly swaps \(0\) with \(Y\) exactly when
\[
Q(0)=u,\qquad Q(Y)=v,\qquad R(v)=Y.
\]
For fixed direction \(e\), the Palm intensity is
\[
\sum_u\sum_{y\ne0}
Q_s^{(2)}((0,y),(u,u+e))p_t(u+e,y).
\]
By two-tag reversibility and translation, with \(a=-u,b=y-u\), this becomes
\[
\sum_{a,b}Q_s^{(2)}((0,e),(a,b))p_t(e,b).
\]
Summing over \(a\) gives \(p_s(e,b)\), so the intensity is
\[
\sum_bp_s(e,b)p_t(e,b)=p_\beta(e,e)=p_\beta(0,0).
\]
There are \(2d\) oriented directions. Integrate over \(s\in[0,\beta]\).

## 4. Exact entry-rate formula for final-successor adjacency

Let \(H_\beta\) be the event that the two final-successor strands are adjacent at some phase:
\[
H_\beta=
\{\exists s\in[0,\beta]:|B_s-A_s|_1=1\}.
\]
Let \(N_\beta^{\rm ent}\) count bars at which the pair changes from nonadjacent immediately before to adjacent immediately after.

Write
\[
q_1=p_\beta(0,e_1),\quad
q_2=p_\beta(0,2e_1),\quad
q_{11}=p_\beta(0,e_1+e_2)
\]
for \(d\ge2\).

### BF-S3 — PROVED

For \(d\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EN_\beta^{\rm ent}
=
2d\beta[(2d-1)q_1+q_2+(2d-2)q_{11}].
}
\tag{BF-S3}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(H_\beta)
\le
2dq_1
+2d\beta[(2d-1)q_1+q_2+(2d-2)q_{11}].
}
\tag{BF-S3'}
\]
For fixed \(d>2\),
\[
\mathbb P(H_\beta)=O_d(\beta^{1-d/2}),
\]
and in dimension three
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(H_\beta)=O(\beta^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{BF-S3-3D}
\]

### Proof

The initial adjacency probability is \(2dq_1\). For entry bars, apply Mecke and split depending on which tracked label moves.

**Case I: the inserted bar moves label \(0\).** Suppose \(0\) moves from \(u\) to \(u+e\). To be adjacent after the bar, the successor label must be at \(u+e+f\), with \(|f|_1=1\) and \(f\ne-e\) so the two labels were distinct before the bar. The future must send the new position \(u+e\) to that successor label. As in BF-S2, reversibility and translation reduce the Palm intensity for fixed \((e,f)\) to
\[
p_\beta(e+f,e)=p_\beta(0,f)=q_1.
\]
There are \(2d(2d-1)\) ordered pairs \((e,f)\), so Case I contributes \(2d(2d-1)q_1\) per unit phase time.

**Case II: the inserted bar moves the successor label.** Label \(0\) stays fixed. Let the successor move in direction \(e\), ending at a neighbor of \(0\) with offset \(-f\), again with \(f\ne-e\). Reversibility and translation reduce the Palm intensity for fixed \((e,f)\) to
\[
p_\beta(0,e+f).
\]
If \(f=e\), there are \(2d\) choices and the displacement is \(2e\), contributing \(2dq_2\). If \(f\) is on a different coordinate direction, there are \(2d(2d-2)\) ordered choices and the displacement is of the \(e_1+e_2\) type, contributing \(2d(2d-2)q_{11}\).

Adding both cases and integrating over phase length \(\beta\) gives BF-S3. The union bound by initial adjacency plus entry count gives BF-S3'. Fixed-displacement heat-kernel bounds yield the asymptotic rate.

## 5. Orbit-wide consequence

Let \(x_k=P^k0\). By point-shift stationarity, the same final-successor-pair bound applies uniformly to every consecutive orbit pair \((x_k,x_{k+1})\). If \(H_{\beta,k}\) denotes the event that their phase-synchronized trajectories become adjacent during the slab, then in \(d=3\),
\[
\mathbb P(H_{\beta,k})\le C\beta^{-1/2}
\]
uniformly in \(k\).
