## Target

For the nearest-neighbor interchange process on \(\mathbb Z^3\), prove or refute existence of \(\beta_0<\infty\) such that for every \(\beta>\beta_0\), the origin belongs to an infinite cycle of \(\pi_\beta\) with positive probability. Chronological tagged paths are not permutation-cycle iterates, and no monotonicity in \(\beta\) is assumed.

## 1. Exact cycle counts as twisted heat traces

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected graph, \(|V|=N\), and run interchange for time \(t\). For \(\ell\ge1\), define the ordered distinct-label space
\[
\Omega_\ell
=
\{(x_0,\dots,x_{\ell-1})\in V^\ell:x_i\ne x_j\text{ for }i\ne j\}.
\]
Let \(P_t^{(\ell)}\) be the semigroup of the ordered \(\ell\)-tag interchange process on \(\Omega_\ell\). Let \(C_\ell\) be cyclic coordinate shift, oriented so that the twisted trace tests
\[
(x_0,x_1,\dots,x_{\ell-1})
\mapsto
(x_1,x_2,\dots,x_{\ell-1},x_0).
\]
Let \(s_\ell(\pi_t)\) be the number of \(\ell\)-cycles of the random permutation.

### BF-TT1 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(P_t^{(\ell)}C_\ell)
=
\ell\,\mathbb E s_\ell(\pi_t).
}
\tag{BF-TT1}
\]

### Proof

For a deterministic permutation \(g\), an ordered injective tuple \((x_0,\dots,x_{\ell-1})\) contributes to the cyclically twisted trace exactly when
\[
g(x_j)=x_{j+1}\quad(0\le j\le\ell-2),
\qquad g(x_{\ell-1})=x_0.
\]
These tuples are precisely rooted oriented listings of the \(\ell\)-cycles of \(g\), and every \(\ell\)-cycle contributes exactly \(\ell\) possible starting roots. Average over the interchange law.

If \(G\) is vertex transitive and \(L_t(v)\) is the cycle length of a fixed root,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(L_t(v)=\ell)
=
\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}(P_t^{(\ell)}C_\ell).
}
\tag{BF-TT1-root}
\]

## 2. Independent-walker comparator

Let \(p_t\) be the one-particle continuous-time SRW semigroup on \(G\). On the full product \(V^\ell\), let \(p_t^{\otimes\ell}\) be the semigroup of \(\ell\) independent walkers.

### BF-TT2 — PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}(p_t^{\otimes\ell}C_\ell)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(p_t^\ell)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(p_{\ell t}).
}
\tag{BF-TT2}
\]

### Proof

Expand the trace:
\[
\sum_{x_0,\dots,x_{\ell-1}}
 p_t(x_0,x_1)p_t(x_1,x_2)\cdots p_t(x_{\ell-1},x_0),
\]
which is \(\operatorname{Tr}(p_t^\ell)\). The semigroup property gives \(p_t^\ell=p_{\ell t}\).

On a vertex-transitive graph,
\[
N^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(p_{\ell t})=p_{\ell t}(o,o).
\]
Thus the independent comparator has exactly the desired return-time scale \(\ell t\).

## 3. Conditional uniform trace comparison that would solve d=3

The original report isolated the following sufficient infinite-volume inequality. Suppose there exists \(A<\infty\), independent of \(\ell\), such that for all sufficiently large \(\beta\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_{\mathbb Z^3}(L_\beta(0)=\ell)
\le A\,p_{\ell\beta}^{\mathbb Z^3}(0,0)
\quad\text{for all }\ell\ge1.
}
\tag{BF-UT}
\]
A sufficient finite-volume formulation is a per-vertex twisted-trace inequality on three-dimensional tori whose fixed-\(\ell\) local limit yields BF-UT, with the same \(A\).

### BF-TT3 — PROVED CONDITIONAL IMPLICATION

BF-UT implies the full large-\(\beta\) Tóth target and in fact
\[
\mathbb P(L_\beta(0)<\infty)=O(\beta^{-3/2}).
\]

### Proof

The SRW return kernel in dimension three obeys
\[
p_t(0,0)\le Ct^{-3/2},\qquad t\ge1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb P(L<\infty)
=
\sum_{\ell\ge1}\mathbb P(L=\ell)
\le
AC\beta^{-3/2}\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell^{-3/2}
=
AC\zeta(3/2)\beta^{-3/2}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(\beta\), this is strictly less than one, proving positive infinite-cycle probability. No monotonicity in \(\beta\) is required.

## 4. Exact obstruction to constant-one trace domination

A natural first guess would be
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_t^{(\ell)}C_\ell)
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(p_{\ell t})
\tag{BF-FALSE1}
\]
with constant one. The original report disproved this already for \(\ell=2\) on \(C_4\).

Let \(Q\) be the continuous-time generator of the ordered two-label interchange process on
\[
\Omega_2(C_4)=\{(x,y):x\ne y\},
\]
which has 12 states. Let \(S\) swap the two coordinates. Since \(Q\) commutes with \(S\), decompose into symmetric and antisymmetric sectors.

The exact sector eigenvalues are:

* \(S=+1\): \(0\) (mult. 1), \(-2\) (3), \(-4\) (1), \(-6\) (1);
* \(S=-1\): \(-2\) (2), \(-4\) (2), \(-6\) (2).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
A(t):=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{tQ}S)
=1+e^{-2t}-e^{-4t}-e^{-6t}.
}
\tag{BF-C4-ex}
\]
The one-particle rate-one-edge SRW generator on \(C_4\) has eigenvalues \(0,-2,-2,-4\), so
\[
\boxed{
B(t):=\operatorname{Tr}(p_{2t})
=1+2e^{-4t}+e^{-8t}.
}
\tag{BF-C4-ind}
\]
At \(t=1\),
\[
A(1)-B(1)
=e^{-2}-3e^{-4}-e^{-6}-e^{-8}.
\]
Let \(x=e^{-2}\). Since
\[
e^2>1+2+2^2/2!+2^3/3!+2^4/4!=7,
\]
we have \(x<1/7\). Hence
\[
1-3x-x^2-x^3
>
1-3/7-1/49-1/343
=188/343>0.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{A(1)>B(1),}
\]
so BF-FALSE1 is rigorously false.

This does not refute BF-UT: it only shows that any useful comparison must allow a multiplicative factor larger than one, exploit infinite-volume/transient geometry, or use the large-time regime.

## 5. Relation to Alon–Kozma

Alon–Kozma, *The probability of long cycles in interchange processes*, arXiv:1009.3723, express expected cycle counts through irreducible characters and give finite-graph convergence estimates. Their general error bound contains a factor of order \(3^N e^{-t\lambda_1}\), where \(N=|V|\) and \(\lambda_1\) is the one-particle spectral gap.

For a three-dimensional torus of side length \(n\), \(N=n^3\) and \(\lambda_1\asymp n^{-2}\). At fixed \(t=\beta\), the factor \(3^N e^{-\beta\lambda_1}\) explodes in the thermodynamic limit, so the published general mixing estimate does not imply BF-UT.

However, their character formulas and BF-TT1 are compatible descriptions of the same cycle statistic and could be useful for isolating which representations contribute to a uniform finite factor.

## 6. Concrete operator target

The original run proposed the finite-factor thermodynamic twisted-trace estimate
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{\ell,\beta}
:=
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\frac1{|T_N^3|}
\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\beta,N}^{(\ell)}C_\ell)
\le
A\,p_{\ell\beta}^{\mathbb Z^3}(0,0),
}
\tag{BF-TRACE-A}
\]
with one \(A<\infty\) valid for every \(\ell\) and all sufficiently large \(\beta\). BF-TRACE-A is exactly strong enough for BF-TT3 and strictly weaker than the disproved constant-one inequality.

Two attack routes were identified.

### Route A: Duhamel / defect cluster expansion

The labeled interchange process differs from independent labeled walkers at pair-adjacency configurations: occupied-edge clock rings swap two labels rather than allowing a collision. The terminal cyclic twist is one long cycle through all label coordinates. A Duhamel expansion around the independent-walker semigroup should organize corrections by adjacency-defect insertions. In \(d=3\), relative two-particle motion is transient, so defect kernels have finite Green mass. The essential quantitative challenge is to show that all connected defect diagrams renormalize the base twisted trace by a universal finite factor independent of \(\ell\), not by \(A^\ell\).

### Route B: representation/operator inequalities

Aldous spectral-gap/octopus inequalities compare interchange Dirichlet forms across particle numbers. The question is whether their structure can be combined with the specific cyclic coordinate permutation \(C_\ell\) to control the twisted trace. The \(C_4\) obstruction shows that a constant-one inequality is too strong, but not that a dimension-dependent finite factor is impossible in the large-time thermodynamic regime.

## 7. Finite-torus route remains complementary

The archived torus-to-lattice theorem remains valid. On a vertex-transitive torus,
\[
\mathbb P(L_{N,\beta}(0)\le K)
=
\frac1N\sum_{\ell=1}^K
\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\beta,N}^{(\ell)}C_\ell).
\]
If this stays below \(1-\delta\) for some \(K=K_N\to\infty\), the torus-to-lattice transfer yields positive infinite-cycle probability in \(\mathbb Z^3\).
