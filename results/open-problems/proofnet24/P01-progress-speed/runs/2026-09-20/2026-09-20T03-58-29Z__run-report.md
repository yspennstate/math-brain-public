## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the Galton--Watson offspring variable. The source problem assumes a supercritical tree without leaves:

`P(nu=0)=0`, `m=E nu>1`.

The parent-bias convention used in this report is

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,

`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

Thus increasing `lambda` pushes toward the root and the conjectural sign is `ell'_lambda<=0` in the transient region.

The Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.

If `v(beta)=ell_{1/beta}`, then wherever differentiable,

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2`.

The new restricted theorems impose bounded support

`d<=nu<=D` a.s., `2<=d<D<infinity`,

and work on `1<=lambda<=L<d`. In these rectangles `lambda<m` automatically because `m>=d`, so the walk is in the outward/transient side relevant to the source problem.

## 3. Exact derivative reduction inherited from P01

Fix `d<=nu<=D` and `1<=lambda<d`. Conditional on `nu=n`, let `B_0,...,B_n` be iid copies of the escape probability appearing in Aidekon's representation and put

`U_i=-B_i' >=0`,

`S=sum_{i=0}^n B_i`,

`U=sum_{i=0}^n U_i`,

`A=lambda-1+S`,

`w=S/((n+1)A)`.

With tilted law

`dmu = w dP / E[w]`

and

`r=E_mu nu`,

exchangeability in Aidekon's speed formula gives

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`.                              (3.1)

Direct differentiation gives

`w'/w=-q`,

where

`q=[S+(lambda-1)U]/(S A)`.                                     (3.2)

Therefore

`r'=-Cov_mu(nu,q)`,                                              (3.3)

and

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                      (3.4)

Let

`pi_n=mu(nu=n)`,

`m_n=E_mu[q | nu=n]`.

The covariance identity can be written exactly as

`r'=sum_{d<=n<m<=D} pi_n pi_m (m-n)(m_n-m_m)`.                  (3.5)

This pairwise form, rather than the separate Jensen/secant bounds on the two expectations in (3.3), is the entry point for the new proof.

## 4. Consumed escape cone and conditional q envelope

The consumed logarithmic escape cone and the regular-tree escape comparison imply, almost surely,

`1-lambda/d <= B_i <= 1-lambda/D`,                              (4.1)

`1/(D-lambda) <= U_i/B_i <= 1/(d-lambda)`.                     (4.2)

Exactly as in the preceding P01 report, conditioning on `nu=n` gives the pathwise envelope

`a_n(lambda)<=q<=b_n(lambda)`,                                  (4.3)

where

`a_n(lambda)`
` =(D-1)/[(D-lambda)(n+lambda(D-n-1)/D)]`,                      (4.4)

`b_n(lambda)`
` =(d-1)/[(d-lambda)(n+lambda(d-n-1)/d)]`.                      (4.5)

Hence the same inequalities hold for `m_n`.

For fixed `lambda`, both `a_n` and `b_n` decrease with `n`. At the extreme degrees define

`f_k(lambda)`
` := k(k-1)/[(k-lambda)(k^2-lambda)]`
`  = 1/(k-lambda)-1/(k^2-lambda)`.                              (4.6)

Then

`b_d(lambda)=f_d(lambda)`,

`a_D(lambda)=f_D(lambda)`.                                       (4.7)

Therefore, for every `d<=n<m<=D`,

`m_n-m_m <= b_n-a_m <= f_d-f_D=:Q_{d,D}(lambda)`.               (4.8)

Two monotonicities matter for uniformization.

First, `f_k` decreases with `k` for `k>=2`, `lambda<k`. Indeed

`partial_k f_k`
` = -1/(k-lambda)^2 + 2k/(k^2-lambda)^2 <0`,                    (4.9)

because

`(k^2-lambda)/(k-lambda)>=k`

and `2/k<=1` for `k>=2`, with strictness here.

Second,

`partial_lambda f_k`
` =1/(k-lambda)^2-1/(k^2-lambda)^2`.                             (4.10)

This derivative also decreases with `k`. Differentiating (4.10) in `k`,

`-2/(k-lambda)^3 + 4k/(k^2-lambda)^3 <0`,                       (4.11)

using `(k^2-lambda)^3>=k^3(k-lambda)^3`` and `4/k^2<=1<2` for `k>=2`.

Thus

`Q_{d,D}(lambda)>0`

and `Q_{d,D}` is increasing in `lambda`. Consequently

`lambda Q_{d,D}(lambda)` is increasing on `[1,L]`.              (4.12)

## 5. New lemma: sharp Gini control of the pairwise degree spread

Let `N,N'` be iid with law `(pi_n)` on `{d,...,D}` and mean `r`.

### Lemma 5.1

`(1/2) E|N-N'| <= (r-d)(D-r)/(D-d)`.                            (5.1)

#### Proof

For deterministic `x,y in [d,D]`, suppose `x>=y`. Then

`(x-d)(D-y)+(y-d)(D-x)-(D-d)(x-y)`
` =2(D-x)(y-d)>=0`.                                              (5.2)

The same inequality with `|x-y|` holds without ordering. Taking iid expectations gives

`(D-d)E|N-N'|`
` <=2 E[(N-d)(D-N')]`
` =2(r-d)(D-r)`.                                                 (5.3)

Divide by `2(D-d)`. QED.

This is sharp for a two-point distribution on the support endpoints, so there is no avoidable Popoviciu/Gruss constant in this step.

## 6. New pairwise-Gini derivative certificate

Combining (3.5), (4.8), and Lemma 5.1,

`r'`
` <=Q_{d,D}(lambda) sum_{n<m} pi_n pi_m (m-n)`
` = (Q_{d,D}(lambda)/2) E|N-N'|`
` <= Q_{d,D}(lambda) (r-d)(D-r)/(D-d)`.                         (6.1)

Fix `1<=lambda<=L<d` and define

`A_{d,D}(L):=L Q_{d,D}(L)`
` =L[f_d(L)-f_D(L)]`.                                            (6.2)

By (4.12),

`lambda Q_{d,D}(lambda)<=A_{d,D}(L)`.                            (6.3)

Write `H=D-d` and `x=r-d in [0,H]`. Then (6.1)--(6.3) give

`lambda r'-r`
` <= [A/H](r-d)(D-r)-r`
` = -(A/H)x^2+(A-1)x-d`.                                        (6.4)

This concave quadratic can be maximized exactly.

If `A<=1`, the maximum on `[0,H]` is `-d`.

If `A>1`, its vertex is

`x*=H(A-1)/(2A) in (0,H)`,                                      (6.5)

and the maximum is

`-delta_{d,D}(L)`, where

`delta_{d,D}(L)`
` := d - H(A-1)^2/(4A)`.                                        (6.6)

Therefore:

### Proposition 6.1 — pairwise-Gini certificate

Assume `d<=nu<=D` a.s., `2<=d<D`, and `1<=lambda<=L<d`. Define `A=A_{d,D}(L)` by (6.2), and

- if `A<=1`, set `delta=d`;
- if `A>1`, set `delta=d-(D-d)(A-1)^2/(4A)`.

If `delta>0`, then throughout `1<=lambda<=L`,

`lambda r'-r<=-delta`,                                           (6.7)

and hence

`BOX: ell'_lambda <= -2 delta/(D+L)^2 <0`.                       (6.8)

This is an analytic theorem for every probability distribution supported on the whole degree interval. No finite simulation is involved.

### Mechanism comparison with the preceding P01 proof

The previous conditional-secant method bounded the two terms in

`r'=r E_mu q-E_mu[nu q]`

separately, allowing low-degree and high-degree branches to saturate incompatible envelope extremes without paying for how much mass is actually separated in degree.

The new argument keeps the exact pairwise covariance first. The potentially bad envelope gap is then multiplied by the sharp mean absolute degree separation, and (5.1) forces that separation to vanish as the tilted mean approaches either support endpoint. This is the new cancellation.

## 10. Failed stronger extension and precise obstruction

I also tried to replace the global endpoint gap `Q_{d,D}` in (4.8) by the full pair-dependent kernel

`K_{n,m}(lambda)=(m-n)[b_n(lambda)-a_m(lambda)]`.                (10.1)

Numerically and algebraically this kernel has substantial extra room beyond the `D=45` boundary, because most degree pairs are much less adverse than `(d,D)`. However a source-valid uniform theorem requires two further facts that are not proved in this run:

1. a clean all-pair monotonicity/uniformization of `lambda K_{n,m}(lambda)` on `[1,L]` in the exact form needed for the covariance sum; and
2. a rigorous extremal theorem for the resulting quadratic form over every offspring law on `{d,...,D}` (the observed endpoint concentration is not enough without proof).

I therefore do **not** count the pair-kernel numerical room as progress. The proved theorem stops at the endpoint-gap/Gini certificate above.

The remaining conceptual debt is sharper than before: the escape envelopes still permit degree `d` and degree `D` conditional environments to saturate opposite regular-tree elasticities simultaneously even though they are generated by one common offspring law. A same-law branch-addition estimate on

`m_n-m_{n+1} = -d/dlambda log(c_n/c_{n+1})`

would attack exactly this incompatibility and is the natural route beyond the present method boundaries.
