## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

`P(nu=0)=0`, `E nu in (1,infinity)`.

This run treats the finite-mean two-point minimum-one family

`P(nu=1)=1-p`, `P(nu=D)=p`, `D>=2`, `0<p<1`.

Use **parent bias** `lambda`:

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,
`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

The older Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius outward child bias is reciprocal:

## 2. Consumed exact scalar reduction

Fix `0<=lambda<1`, put `c=1-lambda`, and let `B` be the quenched branch escape probability. Conditional on current offspring `k`, with iid copies `B_0,...,B_k`, set

`S_k=sum_{i=0}^k B_i`,
`w_k=S_k/((k+1)(S_k-c))`,
`c_k=E w_k`,
`a_k=-c'_k/c_k`.

Aidekon's symmetrized speed formula is

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`,
`r=E[nu c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.

For the two-point law define

`theta=p c_D/((1-p)c_1+p c_D)`.

Then exactly

`r=1+(D-1)theta`,
`r'=(D-1)theta(1-theta)(a_1-a_D)`,
`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.              (2.1)

The inherited minimum-one derivative estimate is

`0<=a_D`, `a_1<=1/(1-lambda)`.                            (2.2)

Two earlier P01 lemmas are the only other substantive dependencies used below.

### 2.1 Escape-mean quadratic

Let `mu=E B`. Concavity of `t/(lambda+t)` gives

`Q_{D,p,lambda}(mu)<=0`,                                  (2.3)

where

`Q_{D,p,lambda}(x)`
` =D x^2+[lambda(D+1)-D]x`
`  +lambda^2-lambda[1+p(D-1)]`.                          (2.4)

The constant term is negative on our range. Hence a positive `u` with `Q(u)>=0` lies to the right of the unique positive root and proves `mu<=u`. The earlier uniform-rectangle version says it suffices to check `Q` at the largest p and smallest lambda, plus positivity of `partial_lambda Q`.

### 2.2 Raw common-mean chord/Jensen ratio

For every `D>=2` and `0<=lambda<1`, the common-mean argument proved the *raw* inequality

`c_D/c_1`
` <= R_D(lambda,mu)`
` :=[(D+1-mu)(lambda+2mu-1)]/[D mu(D+lambda)].            (2.5)

Earlier runs then replaced the right side by `(1+lambda)/(D+lambda)` using only `mu<=1`. The new idea is to keep the much stronger same-law cap `mu<1` from (2.3)--(2.4).

For a uniform-in-D theorem, the asymptotic constraints of this scheme are exact. If `p` is fixed, the support-free split must obey

`2p lambda_0^2+lambda_0<=1`.                              (6.1)

For large D, the escape-mean quadratic requires

`u^2+(lambda_0-1)u-lambda_0 p>=0`.                        (6.2)

The asymptotic static numerator requires

`X(p,u):=[p/(1-p)](2-1/(3u))<1`.                          (6.3)

`du/dlambda_0=-(u-p)/(2u+lambda_0-1)<0`.                  (6.4)

`u=p/[3(3p-1)]`.                                          (6.5)

`P(p)=6561p^4-9686p^3+5262p^2-1245p+108`.                (6.6)
