## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the Galton--Watson offspring variable. The source problem assumes a supercritical tree without leaves:

`P(nu=0)=0`, `m=E nu>1`.

The parent-bias convention is

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,

`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

Increasing `lambda` biases toward the root, so the conjectural sign is `ell'_lambda<=0` in the transient regime.

The Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.

Thus, with `v(beta)=ell_{1/beta}`,

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2` wherever differentiable.

For the new theorems I impose the stronger bounded-support hypothesis

`d<=nu<=D` almost surely,

with `d>=3` and `1<=lambda<d`.

Bounded support gives the domination needed for the differentiated escape/speed formulas used below.

## 3. Exact derivative reduction inherited from P01

Fix `d<=nu<=D` and `1<=lambda<d`. Conditional on `nu=n`, let `B_0,...,B_n` be iid copies of the escape probability from a descendant vertex before hitting its parent. Put

`U_i=-B_i' >=0`,

`S=sum_{i=0}^n B_i`,

`U=sum_{i=0}^n U_i`,

`A=lambda-1+S`,

`w=S/((n+1)A)`.

By exchangeability in Aidekon's speed formula, if

`dmu = w dP / E[w]`

and

`r=E_mu nu=E[nu w]/E[w]`,

then

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`.                              (3.1)

Direct differentiation gives

`w'/w=-q`,

where

`q=[S+(lambda-1)U]/(S A)`.                                     (3.2)

Therefore

`r'=-Cov_mu(nu,q)=r E_mu q-E_mu[nu q]`,                         (3.3)

and

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                      (3.4)

Thus a uniform bound `lambda r'-r<=-delta<0` yields a quantitative derivative margin.

## 4. Partner input and general minimum-degree conditional q envelopes

The consumed R02 logarithmic escape cone says that for bounded support `d<=nu<=D`, `1<=lambda<d`, almost surely

`1/(D-lambda) <= U_i/B_i <= 1/(d-lambda)`.                       (4.1)

The standard regular-tree escape comparison gives

`1-lambda/d <= B_i <= 1-lambda/D`.                              (4.2)

Since `U/S` is a `B_i`-weighted average of `U_i/B_i`, (4.1) gives

`(D-1)/(D-lambda)`
` <= [S+(lambda-1)U]/S`
` <= (d-1)/(d-lambda)`.                                         (4.3)

Condition on `nu=n`. From (4.2),

`A>=A^-_n(lambda):=lambda-1+(n+1)(1-lambda/d)`
`                  =n+lambda(d-n-1)/d`,                         (4.4)

while

`A<=A^+_n(lambda):=lambda-1+(n+1)(1-lambda/D)`
`                  =n+lambda(D-n-1)/D`.                         (4.5)

Combining (3.2)--(4.5) gives the pathwise conditional interval

`a_n(lambda)<=q<=b_n(lambda)`,                                  (4.6)

where

`a_n(lambda)`
` =(D-1)/[(D-lambda)(n+lambda(D-n-1)/D)]`,                       (4.7)

`b_n(lambda)`
` =(d-1)/[(d-lambda)(n+lambda(d-n-1)/d)]`.                       (4.8)

This is the first substantive lift over the preceding P01 report: that report specialized the degree-conditioned secant argument to `d=2`; (4.7)--(4.8) preserve the exact minimum degree `d`.

Let

`m_n=E_mu[q | nu=n]`.

Then the same interval contains `m_n`.

## 5. New general minimum-degree secant certificate

Set

`g_n(lambda):=n a_n(lambda)`.

For fixed lambda extend `b_n` and `g_n` to real `x in [d,D]`. Write

`alpha=1-lambda/d>0`, `c=lambda(d-1)/d>0`.

Then

`b_lambda(x)` is a positive constant divided by `alpha x+c`, so

`b'_lambda(x)<0`, `b''_lambda(x)>0`: it is decreasing and convex. (5.1)

Similarly, with

`alpha_D=1-lambda/D>0`, `c_D=lambda(D-1)/D>0`,

`g_lambda(x)` is a positive constant times

`x/(alpha_D x+c_D)`,

so

`g'_lambda(x)>0`, `g''_lambda(x)<0`: it is increasing and concave. (5.2)

By (3.3) and (4.6),

`r'<=r E_mu[b_lambda(nu)]-E_mu[g_lambda(nu)]`.                  (5.3)

For a convex function the graph lies below its endpoint chord; for a concave function it lies above its endpoint chord. Thus the whole environment-change term can be bounded using the same tilted mean `r`.

### Uniformization on a rectangle `1<=lambda<=L<d`

For fixed `n>=d`, both positive factors in the denominator of `b_lambda(n)` decrease with lambda, hence `b_lambda(n)` increases with lambda.

At `n=D`,

`g_lambda(D)=D(D-1)/[(D-lambda)(D-lambda/D)]`

increases with lambda.

At `n=d`, the denominator of `g_lambda(d)` is

`f(lambda)=(D-lambda)(d+lambda(D-d-1)/D)`,

with derivative

`f'(lambda)=D-2d-1-2lambda(D-d-1)/D`.                           (5.4)

This derivative decreases in lambda. Therefore, if

`D-2d-1-2L(D-d-1)/D>0`,                                        (5.5)

then `g_lambda(d)` decreases throughout `[1,L]`.

Assume (5.5). Define `B*(r)` to be the affine chord through

`(d,b_L(d))`, `(D,b_L(D))`,

and `G*(r)` the affine chord through

`(d,g_L(d))`, `(D,g_1(D))`.

The monotonicities above and convexity/concavity imply, for every `1<=lambda<=L`,

`E_mu[b_lambda(nu)]<=B*(r)`,

`E_mu[g_lambda(nu)]>=G*(r)`.                                    (5.6)

Hence

`r'<=H(r):=r B*(r)-G*(r)`.                                     (5.7)

Define

`Psi_{d,D,L}(r):=L H(r)-r`.                                    (5.8)

Because `B*` and `G*` are affine and the slope of `B*` is negative, `Psi` is a concave quadratic.

Suppose

`max_{d<=r<=D} Psi_{d,D,L}(r)<=-delta<0`,                        (5.9)

with `delta<d`. If `H(r)>=0`, then using `lambda<=L`,

`lambda r'-r<=lambda H(r)-r<=L H(r)-r<=-delta`.                 (5.10)

If `H(r)<0`, then

`lambda r'-r<=lambda H(r)-r<-r<=-d<-delta`.                     (5.11)

Thus in every case

`lambda r'-r<=-delta`.

By (3.4) and `r+lambda<=D+L`,

`ell'_lambda<=-2delta/(D+L)^2`.                                 (5.12)

This proves:

### Proposition 5.1 — minimum-degree conditional-secant certificate

For `d<=nu<=D` a.s., `d>=2`, and `1<=lambda<=L<d`, assume (5.5) and (5.9). Then the speed derivative satisfies the uniform strict bound (5.12).

This is the mechanism-level theorem of the run. It is not a finite simulation: after the escape cone, the proof is an exact analytic reduction for every offspring distribution supported in the whole interval `{d,...,D}`.

## 9. Failed extension and the precise remaining obstruction

The immediate broad-support extension fails because the proof still decouples two endpoint behaviors of the **same** escape law. In (4.6), the upper conditional sensitivity at low current degree is allowed to saturate the minimum-degree regular-tree elasticity while the lower conditional sensitivity at high current degree is allowed to saturate the maximum-degree regular-tree elasticity. A single Galton--Watson law cannot generally realize both extremal descendant environments simultaneously unless it is degenerate.

The exact next-degree failures listed above quantify this incompatibility debt. For example, at `d=3,D=13,L=19/10`, the current quadratic maximum is already positive even though the conjectured speed sign may well remain negative.

A genuinely new next attack should therefore retain the common escape distribution across current degrees. Writing

`c_n(lambda)=E[(1/(n+1)) S_{n+1}/(lambda-1+S_{n+1})]`,

one has

`m_n=-d/dlambda log c_n`.

The next target is a same-law branch-addition inequality for

`m_n-m_{n+1}=-d/dlambda log(c_n/c_{n+1})`,

or an equivalent curvature bound on the sequence `log c_n`. Such an estimate would replace separate endpoint envelopes by adjacent-degree information and directly attack the method failures `D=13` at `19/10`, `D=62` at `9/5`, and the much wider boundaries above without duplicating NM13-05's rare-mixture analysis.
