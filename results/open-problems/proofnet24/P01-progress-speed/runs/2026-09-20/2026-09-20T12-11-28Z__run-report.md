## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the Galton--Watson offspring variable. The source assumptions are

`P(nu=0)=0`, `E nu in (1,infinity)`.

Thus the tree has no leaves and is supercritical. This report treats the two-point minimum-one family

`P(nu=1)=1-p`, `P(nu=D)=p`, `D>=2`, `0<p<1`.

Use **parent bias** `lambda`:

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,
`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

Larger `lambda` pushes toward the root. Write the speed as `ell_lambda`; the target sign is `ell'_lambda<0`.

The older Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.

Thus, wherever differentiable,

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2`.

All new regions lie in `0<=lambda<=2/3<1<m=1+p(D-1)`, so they are strictly inside the transient/ballistic mean boundary.

## 3. Consumed exact scalar reduction

Fix `0<=lambda<1` and put `c=1-lambda`.

Conditional on current offspring `k`, let `B_0,...,B_k` be iid branch escape probabilities and set

`S_k=sum_{i=0}^k B_i`,
`w_k=S_k/((k+1)(S_k-c))`,
`c_k=E w_k`.

Aidekon's symmetrized speed formula is

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`,

where

`r=E[nu c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.

Write

`a_k=-c'_k/c_k`.

The inherited minimum-one derivative estimate gives

`0<=a_k`,
`a_1<=1/(1-lambda)`.                                      (3.1)

For the two-point offspring law define the tilted high-degree mass

`theta=p c_D/((1-p)c_1+p c_D)`.                            (3.2)

Then exactly

`r=1+(D-1)theta`,                                         (3.3)

`r'=(D-1)theta(1-theta)(a_1-a_D)`.                       (3.4)

Differentiating the speed gives

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                (3.5)

The previous same-law static ratio lemma is

`c_D/c_1 <= (1+lambda)/(D+lambda)`                        (3.6)

whenever

`D(1-lambda)>=1+lambda`.                                  (3.7)

For `D>=5` and `lambda<=2/3`, (3.7) holds uniformly. This run shows that (3.1)--(3.7), without any positive lower bound on the high-degree score `a_D`, already contain an exact critical-mixture theorem that earlier runs had not extracted.

## 4. New theorem I: a static critical-mixture derivative certificate

Fix an integer `D>=5` and a mixture cap `0<p<=p0<1`. Define

`Theta=Theta(D,p0):=5p0/[3D+2-3(D-1)p0]`.                 (4.1)

Assume

`Theta<=1/4`                                               (4.2)

and

`delta:=1-(D-1)Theta+2(D-1)Theta^2>0`.                    (4.3)

Then for every two-point offspring law

`P(nu=1)=1-p`, `P(nu=D)=p`, `0<p<=p0`,                   (4.4)

the parent-bias speed is strictly decreasing on

`BOX: 0<=lambda<=2/3`,                                    (4.5)

with the uniform quantitative derivative margin

`BOX: -ell'_lambda >= M(D,p0)>0`,                         (4.6)

where

`M(D,p0):=2 delta/[5/3+(D-1)Theta]^2`.                    (4.7)

### Proof

On `lambda<=2/3`, the static ratio (3.6) is increasing in `lambda`, so

`c_D/c_1 <= (1+2/3)/(D+2/3)=5/(3D+2)`.                  (4.8)

The map from `(p,c_D/c_1)` to the tilted mass (3.2) is increasing in both variables. Therefore

`theta<=Theta`.                                            (4.9)

From (3.1) and `a_D>=0`,

`a_1-a_D<=1/(1-lambda)`.                                  (4.10)

Hence (3.4) yields

`lambda r' <= [lambda/(1-lambda)](D-1)theta(1-theta)`
`            <=2(D-1)theta(1-theta)`.                     (4.11)

Combining with (3.3),

`r-lambda r'`
` >=1+(D-1)theta-2(D-1)theta(1-theta)`
` =g_D(theta)`,                                            (4.12)

where

`g_D(x)=1-(D-1)x+2(D-1)x^2`.                              (4.13)

On `[0,1/4]`, `g_D'(x)=(D-1)(4x-1)<=0`. By (4.2) and (4.9),

`r-lambda r' >=g_D(Theta)=delta>0`.                       (4.14)

Also

`r+lambda<=1+(D-1)Theta+2/3`
`          =5/3+(D-1)Theta`.                              (4.15)

Substitute (4.14)--(4.15) into the negative of (3.5) to obtain (4.6)--(4.7). No simulation enters the argument. QED.

This certificate is the mechanism-level advance of the run: the static same-law suppression of high offspring is charged directly against the exact two-state covariance, instead of separately paying a worst derivative envelope for the high-degree branch.
