## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the Galton--Watson offspring variable. The source problem assumes a supercritical tree without leaves:

`P(nu=0)=0`, `m=E nu>1`.

This report treats the previously delicate minimum-one case under the exact assumptions

`P(nu=1)>0`, `P(nu>=2)>0`, `1<m=E nu<infinity`.                 (1.1)

No upper support bound is imposed.

Parent bias is `lambda`:

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,
`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.                      (1.2)

Larger `lambda` pushes toward the root, so the target sign is `ell'_lambda<0`.

The Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.                                                (1.3)

Thus, wherever differentiable,

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2`.                               (1.4)

All new intervals lie in `lambda<1<m`, hence on the transient side.

## 3. Exact Aidekon scalar reduction

Let `B=B(lambda)` be the quenched escape probability from a vertex before hitting its parent. Conditional on the current offspring count `nu=k`, let

`B_0,...,B_k`

be iid copies of `B`, set

`S_k=sum_{i=0}^k B_i`,
`A_k=lambda-1+S_k`,
`w_k=S_k/((k+1)A_k)`.                                          (3.1)

By exchangeability in Aidekon's speed formula, if

`c_k=E[w_k]`,
`C=sum_k p_k c_k`,
`r=(sum_k k p_k c_k)/C`,                                       (3.2)

where `p_k=P(nu=k)`, then exactly

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`.                             (3.3)

Thus

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                      (3.4)

The entire sign problem is reduced to controlling the derivative of the tilted offspring mean `r`.

## 4. New lemma: below lambda=1, the Aidekon degree tilt decreases with offspring count

Write

`c=1-lambda>0`.

A direct induction from the escape recursion

`B=T/(lambda+T)`

with at least one child gives the pathwise regular-line lower bound

`B>=1-lambda=c`.                                                (4.1)

Indeed the finite-depth approximants start at 1; if every child value is at least `c`, then `T>=c` and `T/(lambda+T)>=c`. Passing to the limit gives (4.1).

Now

`A_k=S_k-c`,
`w_k=(1/(k+1))*phi(S_k)`,
`phi(s)=s/(s-c)`.                                               (4.2)

For `s>c`,

`phi'(s)=-c/(s-c)^2<0`.                                        (4.3)

Couple `S_{k+1}=S_k+B_{k+1}` using one iid escape sequence. Since `phi` is decreasing,

`phi(S_{k+1})<=phi(S_k)` pointwise.                              (4.4)

Therefore

`c_{k+1}`
` = E[phi(S_{k+1})]/(k+2)`
` <= E[phi(S_k)]/(k+2)`
` <  E[phi(S_k)]/(k+1)=c_k`.                                   (4.5)

So the Aidekon degree weight is strictly decreasing in the current offspring count throughout `0<=lambda<1`.

### Consequence 1: the tilt lowers the offspring mean

Since `k` is increasing and `c_k` is decreasing,

`Cov_p(nu,c_nu)<=0`,                                            (4.6)

hence

`r=E[nu c_nu]/E[c_nu] <= E nu = m`.                            (4.7)

This is already qualitatively different from the previous bounded-support proof: no maximum offspring count is needed.

## 5. New static compression bounds for long offspring tails

The same representation gives useful degree-only bounds. Since `S_k>c`,

`phi(S_k)>1`,                                                   (5.1)

whereas (4.1) gives

`S_k>=(k+1)c`,
`S_k-c>=kc`,
`phi(S_k)<= (k+1)/k`.                                          (5.2)

Thus, pathwise and after expectation,

`BOX: 1/(k+1) <= c_k <= 1/k`.                                  (5.3)

This creates two further upper bounds on the tilted mean.

### Branching-probability compression

Let

`p=P(nu>=2)=1-P(nu=1) in (0,1)`.                               (5.4)

Then

`r-1=E[(nu-1)c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.                                   (5.5)

By (5.3), for `nu>=2`, `(nu-1)c_nu<1`, while `c_1>=1/2`. Hence

`r-1 <= p / ((1-p)/2)=2p/(1-p)`,                               (5.6)

so

`BOX: r <= R_p:=(1+p)/(1-p)`.                                  (5.7)

This bound is independent of how large the non-one offspring values are.

### Distribution-shape compression

Again from (5.3),

`E[(nu-1)c_nu] <= E[(nu-1)/nu]`,
`E[c_nu] >= E[1/(nu+1)]`.                                      (5.8)

Therefore

`BOX: r <= R_shape`
` := 1 + E[(nu-1)/nu]/E[1/(nu+1)]`.                            (5.9)

Define the source-statistic envelope

`BOX: R_*:=min{m,R_p,R_shape}`.                                 (5.10)

Then uniformly for every `0<=lambda<1`,

`1<r<=R_*`.                                                     (5.11)

The three terms in (5.10) capture different regimes: the mean bound is useful for genuinely low-mean laws, `R_p` handles rare but arbitrarily large branch events, and `R_shape` improves many concrete intermediate distributions.

## 6. New derivative estimate: the score singularity is paid by tilted excess above one

Put

`U_i=-B_i'>=0`, `U=sum_i U_i`.                                  (6.1)

Differentiating (3.1) gives

`w_k'/w_k=-q_k`,                                                (6.2)

where pathwise

`q_k=[S_k+(lambda-1)U]/(S_k A_k)`
`   =[1-(1-lambda)(U/S_k)]/A_k`.                               (6.3)

By the He--Wang--Xiang bound (2.2), `U/S_k` is a `B_i`-weighted average of numbers at most `1/(1-lambda)`. Therefore

`q_k>=0`.                                                       (6.4)

Also the numerator in (6.3) is at most 1, while (4.1) gives

`A_k=lambda-1+S_k >= k(1-lambda)`.                              (6.5)

Hence

`BOX: 0<=q_k<=1/[k(1-lambda)]`.                                (6.6)

The bounds (5.3) and (6.6) imply, on each compact `0<=lambda<=L<1`,

`|w_k'|<=1/[k^2(1-L)]`,
`k|w_k'|<=1/(1-L)`,                                             (6.7)

so the derivatives of `C` and `sum k p_k c_k` may be taken termwise even with unbounded support. This closes the main technical point introduced by removing the maximum-offspring hypothesis.

Let `pi_k=p_k c_k/C` be the Aidekon tilted offspring law and let

`m_k=E_tilt[q_k | nu=k]`.                                       (6.8)

Then

`0<=m_k<=1/[k(1-lambda)]`,                                      (6.9)

and differentiating `pi_k` gives the exact covariance identity

`r'=-Cov_pi(nu,m_nu)`.                                          (6.10)

Equivalently, for iid `N,N'` with law `pi`,

`r'=sum_{n<j} pi_n pi_j (j-n)(m_n-m_j)`.                        (6.11)

Using only (6.9),

`r'`
` <= (1/(1-lambda)) sum_{n<j} pi_n pi_j (j-n)`
` =  E|N-N'|/[2(1-lambda)]`.                                   (6.12)

Since `N,N'>=1`,

`|N-N'| <= (N-1)+(N'-1)`.                                      (6.13)

Therefore

`BOX: r' <= (r-1)/(1-lambda)`.                                 (6.14)

This is the mechanism-level advance of the run: **the singular score `1/(1-lambda)` is multiplied by the Aidekon-tilted excess offspring `r-1`, and Sections 4--5 show that this excess is much smaller than a raw support-width bound for low-mean or rare-branching laws.**

## 7. Main theorem

Combine (3.4), (5.11), and (6.14). For every `0<=lambda<1`,

`lambda r'-r`
` <= lambda(r-1)/(1-lambda)-r`
` = [(2lambda-1)r-lambda]/(1-lambda).                           (7.1)

For `lambda<=1/2`, the coefficient `2lambda-1<=0` and `r>=1`, so

`lambda r'-r<=-1`.                                              (7.2)

For `lambda>=1/2`, the right side of (7.1) is increasing in `r`; hence by `r<=R_*`,

`lambda r'-r`
` <= - h_{R_*}(lambda)`,                                       (7.3)

where

`h_R(lambda):=[R-(2R-1)lambda]/(1-lambda)`.                    (7.4)

For every fixed `R>1`, `h_R(1/2)=1` and

`h_R'(lambda)=(1-R)/(1-lambda)^2<0`.                            (7.5)

Thus if

`1/2 <= L < R_*/(2R_*-1)`,                                     (7.6)

then uniformly on `0<=lambda<=L`,

`lambda r'-r <= -h_{R_*}(L)<0`.                                (7.7)

Since `r+lambda<=R_*+L`, equation (3.4) gives the quantitative bound

`BOX: ell'_lambda`
` <= - Gamma(R_*,L)`,                                          (7.8)

with

`BOX: Gamma(R,L)`
` := 2 [R-(2R-1)L] / ((1-L)(R+L)^2) >0.                        (7.9)

### Theorem A — minimum-one no-maximum-support monotonicity wedge

Under (1.1), define `p`, `R_p`, `R_shape`, and `R_*` by (5.4), (5.7), (5.9), and (5.10). Then for every

`1/2<=L<R_*/(2R_*-1)`,                                         (7.10)

the speed of the parent-biased walk is strictly decreasing on

`0<=lambda<=L`,                                                 (7.11)

and satisfies the uniform derivative estimate (7.8)--(7.9).

In child-bias notation, the same theorem says `v'(beta)>0` for `beta in [1/L, infinity)` on the corresponding restricted offspring class.

No finite simulation is used.

## 9. Failed stronger attack and precise obstruction

I also tried to improve (6.14) directly by using the degree-dependent factor `1/k` in (6.9) inside the pairwise covariance:

`r' <= (1/(1-lambda)) sum_{n<j} pi_n pi_j (j-n)/n`.             (9.1)

For a fixed tilted mean, this is sometimes much smaller than the Gini bound. However, without an additional same-law constraint it is **not** uniformly better in the source class: two-point tilted laws on `{1,D}` with `D` large asymptotically saturate

`sum_{n<j} pi_n pi_j (j-n)/n <= r-1`.                           (9.2)

Thus the next improvement cannot come from a purely algebraic sharpening of the pairwise range inequality. It must exploit more of the common escape distribution, for example a positive lower bound on the high-degree conditional score `m_j` tied to the same offspring law that makes `m_1` large.

This is the precise remaining obstruction.
