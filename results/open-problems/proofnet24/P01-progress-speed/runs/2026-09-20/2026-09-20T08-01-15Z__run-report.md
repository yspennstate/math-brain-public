## 3. Consumed exact scalar reduction below lambda=1

Let `B=B(lambda)` be the quenched escape probability from a child before hitting its parent. In the no-leaf minimum-one regime and `0<=lambda<1`, the inherited recursion gives the pathwise bounds

`1-lambda <= B <=1`.                                             (3.1)

Put

`c=1-lambda>0`.                                                  (3.2)

Conditional on current offspring `nu=k`, take iid copies `B_0,...,B_k`, set

`S_k=sum_{i=0}^k B_i`,
`w_k=S_k/((k+1)(S_k-c))`,
`c_k=E[w_k]`.                                                    (3.3)

Aidekon's speed formula symmetrizes to

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`,                              (3.4)

where

`r=E[nu c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.                                         (3.5)

Hence

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                       (3.6)

The predecessor also established, with

`a_k=-c_k'/c_k`,                                                 (3.7)

that

`0<=a_k<=1/[k(1-lambda)]`.                                      (3.8)

For the two-point law (1.1), define the Aidekon-tilted mass at degree D by

`theta = p c_D / ((1-p)c_1+p c_D)`.                             (3.9)

Then

`r=1+(D-1)theta`,                                                (3.10)

and the exact two-state covariance identity is

`r'=(D-1)theta(1-theta)(a_1-a_D)`.                              (3.11)

The predecessor used only the separate pathwise bounds `c_D<=1/D` and `c_1>=1/(1+lambda)`, hence

`c_D/c_1 <= (1+lambda)/D`.                                      (3.12)

The new result below improves (3.12) by forcing both expectations to arise from the **same escape distribution**.

## 4. New lemma: common-mean chord/Jensen branch-ratio bound

### Lemma 4.1

Assume `0<=lambda<1`, `D>=2`, and

`D(1-lambda) >= 1+lambda`.                                      (4.1)

Then

`BOX: c_D/c_1 <= (1+lambda)/(D+lambda).`                        (4.2)

This is strictly stronger than (3.12) for every `lambda>0`.

### Proof

Let

`phi(s)=s/(s-c)`, `c=1-lambda`,                                 (4.3)

so `phi` is decreasing and convex on `(c,infinity)`. For iid copies `B_i` of `B`, put

`T_n=B_1+...+B_n`, `F_n=E phi(T_n)`.                             (4.4)

Then

`c_k=F_{k+1}/(k+1)`.                                            (4.5)

Write

`mu=E B in [c,1]`.                                               (4.6)

For `n=D+1`, we have

`T_n in [nc,n]`, `E T_n=n mu`.                                  (4.7)

Because a convex function lies below its endpoint chord on a bounded interval,

`F_n <= ((1-mu)/lambda) phi(nc)
       +((mu-c)/lambda) phi(n)`.                                (4.8)

Here

`phi(nc)=n/(n-1)=(D+1)/D`,
`phi(n)=n/(n-c)=(D+1)/(D+lambda)`.                              (4.9)

For `n=2`, Jensen gives

`F_2 >= phi(2mu)=2mu/(lambda+2mu-1)`.                           (4.10)

Combining (4.5), (4.8), and (4.10),

`c_D/c_1`
` <= [(D+1-mu)(lambda+2mu-1)]/[D mu (D+lambda)]`.               (4.11)

Compare this with the desired right side. Exact subtraction gives

`(1+lambda)/(D+lambda) - RHS(4.11)`
` = (mu-1)(D lambda-D+lambda+2mu-1)
    /[D mu(D+lambda)].                                          (4.12)

Now `mu-1<=0`, while by `mu<=1` and (4.1),

`D lambda-D+lambda+2mu-1`
` <= D lambda-D+lambda+1`
` = -[D(1-lambda)-(1+lambda)] <=0`.                             (4.13)

Thus the numerator in (4.12) is nonnegative and (4.2) follows. QED.

### Mechanism interpretation

The old box (3.12) could only be saturated by making the degree-D branch look like the smallest possible escape sum while making the degree-1 branch look like the largest possible escape sum. Lemma 4.1 prevents that incompatibility by introducing the common mean `mu=E B`: convex-chord control of the high-degree sum and Jensen control of the degree-1 sum must use the **same** `mu`.

The lemma is tight at the deterministic endpoint `B=1`, where

`c_k=1/(k+lambda)`                                               (4.14)

and equality holds in (4.2). Therefore further progress cannot come from improving (4.2) using only the support interval and the common mean; it must use non-degeneracy or joint derivative information.

## 8. Failed extension and mathematical obstruction

The first attempted extension was to push Theorem A farther in `lambda` without changing the derivative estimate `a_1-a_D<=1/(1-lambda)`. The exact cubic (6.10) shows this route fails immediately after `alpha_12`: already at `lambda=0.6685` the sufficient numerator has the wrong sign.

The second attempted extension was to increase D at fixed `(p,L)=(2/5,2/3)`. The exact certificate changes sign between `D=46` and `D=47`. Likewise, at `(p,L)=(19/50,2/3)` it changes sign between `D=221` and `D=222`.

These failures isolate the remaining incompatibility. Lemma 4.1 already uses the same escape mean and is tight at deterministic `B=1`; therefore a stronger **static** ratio estimate cannot hold using only `B in [1-lambda,1]` and `E B`. To move the boundary one must couple the static ratio with the derivative scores. Concretely, one needs a positive lower control on `a_D` when the ratio `c_D/c_1` is near its worst case, or an inequality of the form

`a_1-a_D <= [1-eta(c_D/c_1,lambda)]/(1-lambda)`                 (8.1)

with `eta>0` away from a true extremizer. This is a same-law **static/derivative compatibility** problem, not another endpoint arithmetic problem.

A natural next attack is to express both `c_k` and `-c_k'` as expectations of the same convex kernel in the branch sum `S_k`, and search for a joint secant inequality controlling

`d/dlambda log(c_1/c_D)=a_D-a_1`.                               (8.2)

That route is analytically complementary to NM13-05's branch-addition coupling at minimum degree three.
