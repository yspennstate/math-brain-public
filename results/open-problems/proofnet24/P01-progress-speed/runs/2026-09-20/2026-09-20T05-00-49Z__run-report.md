## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the Galton--Watson offspring variable. The source problem assumes a supercritical tree without leaves:

`P(nu=0)=0`, `m=E nu>1`.

This report uses parent bias `lambda`:

`P(parent | nu=k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,

`P(each fixed child | nu=k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

Larger `lambda` pushes toward the root, so the conjectural sign is `ell'_lambda<=0` throughout the transient interval `0<=lambda<m`.

The Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.

Thus, for `v(beta)=ell_{1/beta}` wherever differentiable,

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2`.

The new theorem assumes bounded support with **minimum one**:

`1<=nu<=D` a.s., `2<=D<infinity`, `P(nu>=2)>0`.

The last condition is exactly what makes the law supercritical in this no-leaf bounded-support setting. Since all new endpoints satisfy `lambda<1<m`, the walk is safely in the transient/ballistic regime.

## 3. Exact scalar derivative reduction

Fix `1<=nu<=D` and `0<=lambda<1`. Conditional on `nu=n`, let `B_0,...,B_n` be iid copies of Aidekon's escape probability and set

`U_i=-B_i' >=0`,

`S=sum_{i=0}^n B_i`,

`U=sum_{i=0}^n U_i`,

`A=lambda-1+S`,

`w=S/((n+1)A)`.

The regular-tree escape comparison in He--Wang--Xiang gives, when the minimum offspring is one,

`1-lambda <= B_i <= 1-lambda/D`.                                (3.1)

Therefore

`A >= lambda-1+(n+1)(1-lambda)=n(1-lambda)>0`.                  (3.2)

So Aidekon's formula is nonsingular throughout every compact subinterval of `[0,1)`.

With the tilted law

`dmu = w dP / E[w]`

and

`r=E_mu nu`,

exchangeability in Aidekon's numerator and denominator gives exactly

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`.                              (3.3)

Differentiating `w` gives

`w'/w=-q`,

where

`q=[S+(lambda-1)U]/(S A)`.                                     (3.4)

Hence

`r'=-Cov_mu(nu,q)`,                                              (3.5)

and

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                      (3.6)

All differentiations under expectation are dominated on `0<=lambda<=L<1`: (3.1), (3.2), the derivative bound from Lemma 2.4 and bounded `nu` give a deterministic integrable bound for `w`, `w'`, `nu w` and their derivatives.

Let

`pi_n=mu(nu=n)`,

`m_n=E_mu[q | nu=n]`.

Then the covariance can also be written exactly as

`r'=sum_{1<=n<m<=D} pi_n pi_m (m-n)(m_n-m_m)`.                  (3.7)

## 4. New lemma: extend the lower logarithmic escape cone into `m1=1`, `lambda<1`

The previous PROB10-06 elasticity-cone report proved a lower cone in its `d>=2`, `lambda>=1` application. Its recursive proof actually has a second useful regime. I spell it out because this extension is the first new mechanism in this run.

Let `B` be the escape probability at a generic vertex and put

`R=-B'/B`.

The exact escape recursion is

`B=T/(lambda+T)`,

where `T=sum_{j=1}^{nu} B_j`. Differentiating and writing `R_j=-B_j'/B_j` gives

`R=[1+lambda (sum_j B_j R_j)/T]/(lambda+T)`.                    (4.1)

By (3.1),

`lambda+T <= lambda+nu(1-lambda/D) <= D`.                       (4.2)

He--Wang--Xiang Lemma 2.4 gives `R_j>=0`. Thus (4.1)--(4.2) first give

`R>=1/D`.                                                        (4.3)

More generally, if the universal descendant bound is `R_j>=c`, then

`R >= (1+lambda c)/D`.                                          (4.4)

Starting from `c_0=0` and iterating

`c_{k+1}=(1+lambda c_k)/D`

gives an increasing deterministic sequence converging to

`1/(D-lambda)`.

Because every descendant has the same Galton--Watson escape-law distribution, (4.4) iterates almost surely at every depth. Letting `k` tend to infinity gives

`BOX: 1/(D-lambda) <= -B'/B`.                                   (4.5)

Combining this with He--Wang--Xiang Lemma 2.4 for `m1=1` gives the two-sided cone

`BOX: 1/(D-lambda) <= -B'/B <= 1/(1-lambda)`,                   (4.6)

valid for `0<=lambda<1`.

The two endpoints are sharp on the deterministic `D`-regular and one-child regular recursions, respectively; the latter deterministic degree-one tree is not supercritical, but it is the limiting extremal comparison that explains why no positive distribution-free gap can be extracted from the upper side alone.

## 5. The sign reversal at `lambda<1`: a new conditional score envelope

Equation (3.4) behaves differently below one. Write `c=1-lambda>0`. Then

`q=[1-c(U/S)]/A`.                                                (5.1)

Since `U/S` is the `B_i`-weighted average of the logarithmic elasticities `U_i/B_i`, (4.6) implies

`1/(D-lambda) <= U/S <= 1/(1-lambda)`.                          (5.2)

Therefore the numerator in (5.1) satisfies

`0 <= 1-c(U/S) <= 1-c/(D-lambda)=(D-1)/(D-lambda)`.             (5.3)

Together with `A>=n(1-lambda)` from (3.2), this yields the pathwise degree-dependent envelope

`BOX: 0 <= q <= (D-1)/[n(1-lambda)(D-lambda)]`.                 (5.4)

In particular, defining

`Q_D(lambda):=(D-1)/[(1-lambda)(D-lambda)]`,                    (5.5)

we have for every `1<=n<m<=D`

`m_n-m_m <= Q_D(lambda)`.                                        (5.6)

This is the key new sign estimate. Above one, the derivative numerator `S+(lambda-1)U` amplifies escape sensitivity and requires a lower bound on the denominator plus an upper elasticity budget. Below one, the coefficient changes sign; the new lower cone (4.5) **subtracts** a deterministic amount from the bad score.

## 6. Pairwise-Gini closure for minimum offspring one

Let `N,N'` be iid with the tilted degree law `(pi_n)` and mean `r`. The sharp range/mean inequality from the previous P01 report, specialized to `[1,D]`, is

`(1/2)E|N-N'| <= (r-1)(D-r)/(D-1)`.                             (6.1)

For completeness, for deterministic `x,y in [1,D]`,

`(D-1)|x-y| <= (x-1)(D-y)+(y-1)(D-x)`

after ordering `x,y`; taking iid expectations gives (6.1).

Using (3.7) and (5.6),

`r'`
` <= Q_D(lambda) sum_{n<m} pi_n pi_m(m-n)`
` = (Q_D(lambda)/2)E|N-N'|`
` <= Q_D(lambda)(r-1)(D-r)/(D-1)`.                              (6.2)

Fix a closed endpoint `0<L<1`. The function

`lambda Q_D(lambda)=lambda(D-1)/[(1-lambda)(D-lambda)]`

is strictly increasing on `(0,1)`. Define

`A_D(L):=L(D-1)/[(1-L)(D-L)]`.                                  (6.3)

Writing `H=D-1` and `x=r-1 in [0,H]`, equations (6.2)--(6.3) give uniformly for `0<=lambda<=L`

`lambda r'-r`
` <= [A_D(L)/H]x(H-x)-(1+x)`
` =-[A_D(L)/H]x^2+(A_D(L)-1)x-1`.                              (6.4)

If `A_D(L)<=1`, the right side is at most `-1`.

If `A_D(L)>1`, its maximum occurs at

`x*=H(A_D(L)-1)/(2A_D(L))`

and equals `-delta_D(L)`, where

`delta_D(L)`
` :=1-(D-1)(A_D(L)-1)^2/[4A_D(L)]`.                             (6.5)

Thus whenever `delta_D(L)>0`,

`lambda r'-r <= -delta_D(L)`                                    (6.6)

and, since `r+lambda<=D+L`,

`BOX: ell'_lambda <= -2 delta_D(L)/(D+L)^2 <0`                  (6.7)

throughout `0<=lambda<=L` (with the endpoint `lambda=0` understood by analyticity/continuity).

This is an analytic sign theorem, not a finite simulation.

## 7. Exact analytic frontier for every finite upper offspring bound

The condition (6.5) has a closed form. Put `A=A_D(L)` and `H=D-1`. For `A>1`,

`delta_D(L)>0`

is equivalent to

`H(A-1)^2<4A`.

The upper root of the corresponding quadratic in `A` is

`K_D=(sqrt(D)+1)/(sqrt(D)-1)`.                                  (7.1)

Since `A_D(L)` increases continuously from zero to infinity on `0<L<1`, there is a unique frontier `Lambda_D in (0,1)` satisfying

`A_D(Lambda_D)=K_D`.                                             (7.2)

Writing `t=sqrt(D)` and cancelling `t+1`, equality (7.2) is exactly

`Lambda_D^2-2(D-sqrt(D)+1)Lambda_D+D=0`.

Therefore the relevant smaller root is

`BOX: Lambda_D`
` =D-sqrt(D)+1-sqrt((D-sqrt(D)+1)^2-D)`.                        (7.3)

### Theorem 7.1 — bounded maximum offspring, minimum offspring one

Assume

`1<=nu<=D` a.s., `P(nu>=2)>0`, `2<=D<infinity`.

For every closed endpoint

`0<L<Lambda_D`,

the parent-bias speed is strictly decreasing on

`0<=lambda<=L`,

and satisfies the explicit derivative margin

`-ell'_lambda >= eta_{D,L}`

on that interval, where

`eta_{D,L}=2 delta_D(L)/(D+L)^2`                                (7.4)

and `delta_D(L)` is defined in (6.5), with `delta=1` if `A_D(L)<=1`.

The frontier tends to the old source-uniform `1/2` from above as `D` grows, while for each finite `D` it is strictly larger than `1/2`. Thus bounded maximum offspring buys a quantitative interval **precisely in the minimum-one regime omitted by the 2025 minimum-degree theorem**.
