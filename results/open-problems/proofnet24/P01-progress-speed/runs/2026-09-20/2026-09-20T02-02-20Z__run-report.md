## 1. Frozen source assumptions and bias convention

Let `nu` be the offspring variable. The source problem is the biased nearest-neighbor walk on a supercritical Galton--Watson tree without leaves:

`P(nu=0)=0`, `m=E nu>1`.

The parent-bias convention used here is the He--Wang--Xiang convention. At a non-root vertex with `k` children,

`P(parent | k)=lambda/(lambda+k)`,
`P(each fixed child | k)=1/(lambda+k)`.

Increasing `lambda` biases toward the root, so the conjectural sign is `ell'_lambda<=0`.

The Ben Arous--Fribergh--Sidoravicius child-bias convention is reciprocal:

`beta=1/lambda`.

If `v(beta)=ell_{1/beta}`, then where differentiable

`v'(beta)=-ell'_{1/beta}/beta^2`.

For the broad theorems below I impose the stronger bounded-support hypothesis

`2<=nu<=D` almost surely.

Bounded support supplies all moment/differentiation domination needed below.

## 3. Exact derivative identity inherited from P01

For `2<=nu<=D`, let `B_0,...,B_nu` be iid copies of the quenched escape probability from a child subtree before returning to its parent. Put

`U_i=-B_i' >=0`,
`S=sum_{i=0}^nu B_i`,
`U=sum_{i=0}^nu U_i`,
`A=lambda-1+S`,
`u=S/((nu+1)A)`.

By exchangeability in Aidekon's speed formula,

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`,                                  (3.1)

where

`r=E[nu u]/E[u]`.

Let `mu_lambda` be the `u`-tilted law:

`dmu=u dP/E[u]`.

Then `r=E_mu nu`. Direct differentiation gives

`u'/u=-q`,

where

`q=[S+(lambda-1)U]/(S A)`.                                         (3.2)

Therefore

`r'=-Cov_mu(nu,q)=r E_mu q-E_mu[nu q]`,                            (3.3)

and

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                          (3.4)

Thus it suffices to prove `lambda r'-r<0` with a quantitative gap.

## 4. Essential new partner input: two-sided logarithmic escape cone

The R02/PROB10-06 report cited in section 0 proves, for bounded support `d<=nu<=D` and `1<=lambda<d`,

`1/(D-lambda) <= U_i/B_i <= 1/(d-lambda)`.                          (4.1)

The right inequality is the He--Wang--Xiang derivative bound. The left inequality is the new R02 cone; it follows from the escape recursion

`B=S_child/(lambda+S_child)`

and the exact recursion for `U/B`, then iterating the lower affine map `m -> (1+lambda m)/D`. It is sharp on the deterministic `D`-regular tree. I consume (4.1) as a partner dependency and do not claim it as P01's result.

Specialize from now through section 8 to `d=2`. Since `U/S` is a `B_i`-weighted average of `U_i/B_i`, (4.1) gives

`(D-1)/(D-lambda)`
` <= [S+(lambda-1)U]/S`
` <= 1/(2-lambda)`.                                                (4.2)

The ordinary escape comparison gives

`1-lambda/2 <= B_i <= 1-lambda/D`.                                (4.3)

## 5. New mechanism lemma: conditional-degree q envelopes

Condition on `nu=n`, `2<=n<=D`. From (4.3),

`A^-_n(lambda):=lambda-1+(n+1)(1-lambda/2)`
`                = n-lambda(n-1)/2`

satisfies `A>=A^-_n`, while

`A^+_n(lambda):=lambda-1+(n+1)(1-lambda/D)`
`                = n+lambda(D-n-1)/D`

satisfies `A<=A^+_n`.

Combining with (4.2) gives the pathwise conditional bounds

`a_n(lambda) <= q <= b_n(lambda)`,                                  (5.1)

where

`a_n(lambda)`
` =(D-1)/[(D-lambda)(n+lambda(D-n-1)/D)]`,                          (5.2)

and

`b_n(lambda)`
` =1/[(2-lambda)(n-lambda(n-1)/2)]`.                               (5.3)

Let

`m_n=E_mu[q | nu=n]`.

Then the same interval contains `m_n`.

The key difference from the preceding P01 report is that (5.1) keeps the degree `n` instead of collapsing all degrees into one worst-case q range.

## 6. Secant reduction: exploit convexity and concavity before optimizing the tilted mean

Set `N=nu` under `mu_lambda` and `r=E_mu N`. Define

`g_n(lambda):=n a_n(lambda)`.

For fixed `lambda`, extend `b_n` and `g_n` to real `x in [2,D]`. Their forms are

`b_lambda(x)`
` =1/[(2-lambda)((1-lambda/2)x+lambda/2)]`,                         (6.1)

and

`g_lambda(x)`
` =(D-1)/(D-lambda)`
`   * x/[(1-lambda/D)x+lambda(D-1)/D]`.                            (6.2)

Hence:

- `b_lambda(x)` is decreasing and convex;
- `g_lambda(x)` is increasing and concave.

Using (3.3) and (5.1),

`r' <= r E_mu[b_lambda(N)]-E_mu[g_lambda(N)]`.                     (6.3)

For a convex function, the graph lies below the chord joining its endpoint values, while for a concave function it lies above that chord. Therefore, for fixed `lambda`, both expectations in (6.3) are controlled by endpoint secants at the same tilted mean `r`. This is exactly the information discarded by the previous range-product estimate.

### Uniformization over `1<=lambda<=L<2`

For every fixed `n`, `b_lambda(n)` increases with `lambda`, because both positive factors in its denominator decrease.

For the concrete rectangles used below,

`g_lambda(2)` decreases on `[1,L]`.

Indeed its denominator is

`h(lambda)=(D-lambda)(2+lambda(D-3)/D)`,

with

`h'(lambda)=D-5-2lambda(D-3)/D`,                                   (6.4)

which is positive throughout both rectangles below.

At the upper endpoint,

`g_lambda(D)=D(D-1)/[(D-lambda)(D-lambda/D)]`

increases with `lambda`, so its minimum is at `lambda=1`.

Define `B*(r)` as the affine chord through

`(2,b_L(2))`, `(D,b_L(D))`,

and `G*(r)` as the affine chord through

`(2,g_L(2))`, `(D,g_1(D))`.

For every `1<=lambda<=L`, concavity/convexity and the endpoint monotonicities imply

`E_mu[b_lambda(N)] <= B*(r)`,
`E_mu[g_lambda(N)] >= G*(r)`.                                     (6.5)

Thus

`r' <= H(r):=r B*(r)-G*(r)`.                                      (6.6)

If `H(r)<0`, then `lambda r'-r<0` immediately. If `H(r)>=0`, then `lambda<=L` implies

`lambda r'-r <= L H(r)-r =: Psi_{D,L}(r)`.                        (6.7)

Since `B*` is affine with negative slope and `G*` is affine, `Psi_{D,L}` is a concave quadratic. Therefore its exact maximum on `[2,D]` is attained at an endpoint or its explicit vertex.

### Proposition 6.1 — conditional-degree secant certificate

If

`max_{2<=r<=D} Psi_{D,L}(r) <= -delta <0`,                          (6.8)

then every offspring law supported in `{2,...,D}` satisfies

`ell'_lambda <= -2 delta/(D+L)^2`                                 (6.9)

for every `1<=lambda<=L`.

Proof: (6.7) gives the numerator gap whenever `H>=0`; when `H<0`, `lambda r'-r<=-r<=-2`, which is stronger for all concrete `delta<2` below. Equation (3.4) and `r+lambda<=D+L` then give (6.9). QED.

This proposition is P01's mechanism-level advance in this run.

## 10. Failed attacks and mathematical obstructions

### A. Pairwise degree kernel alone does not move the old breadth boundary

Before finding the secant reduction, I tried to use the exact pairwise covariance identity with conditional intervals and force every pair separately to satisfy a sign inequality. At `D=75`, `L=6/5`, the worst pair `(2,75)` already violates that sufficient condition: the exact ratio is

`1444305/1443442 >1`.

The obstruction is conceptual: pairwise domination discards the diagonal/self-mass contribution to `r` and therefore asks each cross pair to pay the whole local drift alone. The secant method fixes this by preserving the common tilted mean before optimizing.

### B. The new broad secant method hits `D=581` at `L=6/5`

Section 7 records the exact positive quadratic maximum. Pushing beyond 580 requires information linking the escape law to the degree distribution more tightly than the endpoint cones do. The present proof still allows the low-degree conditional branch to saturate the binary-tree upper elasticity and the high-degree branch to saturate the 581-regular lower elasticity simultaneously, even though both arise from one common Galton--Watson environment.

### C. The all-mixture nonadjacent two-point bound hits `{2,14}` at `L=3/2`

The exact one-variable certificate becomes positive. A further gain needs either a branch-addition comparison between `m_2` and `m_D` under the **same** escape distribution or a sharper relation between the tilted weight `theta` and the original mixture probability `p`; separate endpoint envelopes are no longer enough.

None of A--C is a counterexample to the Lyons--Pemantle--Peres conjecture.

## 13. Residual obstruction and next attack

The current broad-support bottleneck is no longer the global support-width covariance loss. It is the **incompatibility debt** between the conditional endpoint envelopes: the proof permits the degree-2 branch to behave as though all descendant subtrees were binary while simultaneously permitting the degree-D branch to behave as though all descendants were D-regular. A real Galton--Watson environment uses one common offspring law, so these two saturations cannot generally occur together unless the law is degenerate.

The next attack should quantify that incompatibility directly. A promising route is to couple `S_{n+1}` and `S_{n+2}` by adding one iid escape branch, then derive a bound on the logarithmic derivative ratio

`m_n-m_{n+1} = -d/dlambda log(c_n/c_{n+1})`

under the same escape distribution. Summing such adjacent degree comparisons could replace the separate `b_n-a_m` envelopes and may cross both current method boundaries (`D=581` at `6/5` and `{2,14}` at `3/2`) without entering NM13-05's rare-defect `{2,3}` scope.
