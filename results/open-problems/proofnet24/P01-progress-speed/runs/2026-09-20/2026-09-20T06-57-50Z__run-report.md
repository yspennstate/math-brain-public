## 3. Consumed exact scalar reduction

Let `B=B(lambda)` be the quenched escape probability from a vertex before hitting its parent. Conditional on current offspring `nu=k`, take iid copies

`B_0,...,B_k`,

put

`S_k=sum_{i=0}^k B_i`,
`A_k=lambda-1+S_k`,
`w_k=S_k/((k+1)A_k)`,
`c_k=E[w_k]`.                                                    (3.1)

Aidekon's speed formula symmetrizes to

`ell_lambda=(r-lambda)/(r+lambda)`,                              (3.2)

where

`r = E[nu c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.                                       (3.3)

Hence

`ell'_lambda=2(lambda r'-r)/(r+lambda)^2`.                       (3.4)

The task is to control the derivative of the Aidekon-tilted offspring mean `r`.

## 4. New lemma: the exact lambda-dependent static squeeze

Fix `0<=lambda<1` and write

`c=1-lambda>0`.                                                  (4.1)

The escape recursion and the no-leaf condition give the pathwise lower bound

`B>=1-lambda=c`,                                                 (4.2)

while trivially `B<=1`.

Therefore

`(k+1)c <= S_k <= k+1`.                                         (4.3)

Since

`w_k=(1/(k+1))*phi_c(S_k)`,
`phi_c(s)=s/(s-c)`,                                              (4.4)

and `phi_c` is strictly decreasing, (4.3) yields the **new two-sided pathwise bound**

`BOX: 1/(k+lambda) <= w_k <= 1/k`.                              (4.5)

Indeed the lower endpoint is obtained from `S_k<=k+1`:

`phi_c(S_k)>=phi_c(k+1)=(k+1)/(k+lambda)`,                       (4.6)

and the upper endpoint from `S_k>=(k+1)c`:

`phi_c(S_k)<=phi_c((k+1)c)=(k+1)/k`.                            (4.7)

Taking expectations,

`BOX: 1/(k+lambda) <= c_k <=1/k`.                               (4.8)

The previous P01 report used only the lambda-independent lower bound `c_k>=1/(k+1)`. Replacing it by `1/(k+lambda)` is the mechanism-level advance that drives the new support-free frontier.

For completeness, the same coupling `S_{k+1}=S_k+B_{k+1}` gives

`c_{k+1}<c_k`,                                                   (4.9)

so the tilt lowers the original offspring mean:

`r<=m`.                                                         (4.10)

## 5. Uniform lambda-dependent compression of the tilted offspring mean

Let

`p=P(nu>=2)=1-P(nu=1)`.                                         (5.1)

From (4.8),

`r-1 = E[(nu-1)c_nu]/E[c_nu]`.                                  (5.2)

### 5.1 Branching-probability compression

The numerator is at most p because `(k-1)c_k<=(k-1)/k<=1` for `k>=2`. On every interval `0<=lambda<=L<1`, the denominator has the degree-one contribution

`E[c_nu]>=P(nu=1)c_1 >= (1-p)/(1+L)`.                           (5.3)

Therefore uniformly on that interval,

`BOX: r <= R_p(L):=(1+pL)/(1-p)`.                               (5.4)

This strictly improves the predecessor's support-free bound `(1+p)/(1-p)` whenever `L<1`.

### 5.2 Distribution-shape compression

Set

`A:=E[(nu-1)/nu]`,
`B_L:=E[1/(nu+L)]`.                                              (5.5)

Again from (4.8), uniformly for `lambda<=L`,

`E[(nu-1)c_nu]<=A`,
`E[c_nu]>=B_L`,                                                  (5.6)

hence

`BOX: r <= R_shape(L):=1+A/B_L`.                                (5.7)

This strictly sharpens the predecessor's denominator `E[1/(nu+1)]`.

Define

`BOX: R_L:=min{m,R_p(L),R_shape(L)}`.                            (5.8)

Then

`1<r(lambda)<=R_L` for every `0<=lambda<=L<1`.                  (5.9)

## 6. Derivative estimate retained, now paired with the sharper static compression

Put

`U_i=-B_i'>=0`, `U=sum_i U_i`.                                  (6.1)

Differentiating (3.1),

`w_k'/w_k=-q_k`,                                                 (6.2)

where

`q_k=[S_k-(1-lambda)U]/[S_k(S_k-(1-lambda))]`.                  (6.3)

By (2.2), `U/S_k` is a `B_i`-weighted average of escape logarithmic derivatives bounded above by `1/(1-lambda)`. Thus

`q_k>=0`.                                                        (6.4)

The numerator in (6.3) is at most `S_k`, while (4.2) gives

`S_k-(1-lambda)>=k(1-lambda)`.                                  (6.5)

Therefore

`BOX: 0<=q_k<=1/[k(1-lambda)]`.                                 (6.6)

Let

`a_k:=E[w_k q_k]/E[w_k]`,                                       (6.7)

so that `c_k'=-c_k a_k`. Then

`0<=a_k<=1/[k(1-lambda)]`.                                      (6.8)

If `pi_k=p_kc_k/E[c_nu]` is the Aidekon-tilted degree law, differentiating (3.3) gives exactly

`r'=-Cov_pi(N,a_N)`                                              (6.9)

and hence

`r'=sum_{n<j} pi_n pi_j (j-n)(a_n-a_j)`.                        (6.10)

Using only (6.8),

`r' <= E|N-N'|/[2(1-lambda)]`                                   (6.11)

for iid `N,N'~pi`. Since `N,N'>=1`,

`|N-N'|<=(N-1)+(N'-1)`,                                         (6.12)

so

`BOX: r' <= (r-1)/(1-lambda)`.                                  (6.13)

The unbounded-support differentiation is legitimate on every compact `lambda<=L<1`: from `w_k<=1/k` and (6.6),

`|w_k'|<=1/[k^2(1-L)]`,
`k|w_k'|<=1/(1-L)`,                                              (6.14)

which supplies summable domination for the denominator and numerator derivatives in (3.3).

## 7. Main lambda-dependent compression theorem

Fix `L in [1/2,1)`. Combining (3.4) and (6.13),

`lambda r'-r`
` <= [(2lambda-1)r-lambda]/(1-lambda).                          (7.1)

For `lambda<=1/2`, the right side is at most `-1`. For `1/2<=lambda<=L`, use `r<=R_L` from (5.9):

`lambda r'-r`
` <= - h_{R_L}(lambda)`,                                        (7.2)

where

`h_R(lambda):=[R-(2R-1)lambda]/(1-lambda)`.                     (7.3)

For every `R>1`,

`h_R'(lambda)=(1-R)/(1-lambda)^2<0`.                            (7.4)

Therefore, if

`delta_L:=h_{R_L}(L)`
`=[L-(2L-1)R_L]/(1-L)>0`,                                       (7.5)

then throughout `0<=lambda<=L`,

`lambda r'-r <= -delta_L`,                                      (7.6)

and since `r+lambda<=R_L+L`,

`BOX: ell'_lambda <= -2 delta_L/(R_L+L)^2 <0`.                  (7.7)

This is the general improved theorem. It simultaneously sharpens the predecessor's p-envelope and distribution-shape envelope.

## 9. Extension: two-point tilt localization beats every mean-only compression

The support-free theorem deliberately forgets where the non-one mass lies. For an exact two-point law that loss can be repaired.

Assume

`P(nu=1)=1-p`,
`P(nu=D)=p`,                                                     (9.1)

with `D>=2` and `0<p<1`. Let `theta` be the Aidekon-tilted mass at D. Then

`r=1+(D-1)theta`.                                                (9.2)

Because (4.8) gives

`c_D<=1/D`, `c_1>=1/(1+lambda)`,                                (9.3)

we have

`c_D/c_1 <= (1+lambda)/D`.                                      (9.4)

Therefore, on `lambda<=L`,

`BOX: theta <= Theta:=p(1+L)/[D(1-p)+p(1+L)]`.                 (9.5)

The two-state version of (6.9) is exact:

`r'=(D-1)theta(1-theta)(a_1-a_D)`.                              (9.6)

Using `a_1<=1/(1-lambda)` and `a_D>=0`,

`r' <= (D-1)theta(1-theta)/(1-lambda)`.                         (9.7)

Thus

`G:=r-lambda r'`
` >= 1+(D-1)theta-[lambda/(1-lambda)](D-1)theta(1-theta).       (9.8)

For fixed `L>1/2`, replace `lambda/(1-lambda)` by its larger endpoint value `a=L/(1-L)`. The resulting quadratic in theta is convex, with vertex

`theta_0=(a-1)/(2a)=(2L-1)/(2L)`.                              (9.9)

If `Theta<=theta_0`, the quadratic decreases on `[0,Theta]`, so

`G >= G_L(Theta)`                                               (9.10)

with

`G_L(t)=1+(D-1)t-a(D-1)t(1-t)`.                                (9.11)

If `G_L(Theta)>0`, then

`BOX: ell'_lambda`
` <= -2 G_L(Theta)/[1+(D-1)Theta+L]^2 <0`.                     (9.12)

This is a second new theorem mechanism: it uses the **same lambda-dependent c_k squeeze twice**, first to localize the static tilted mixture and then to reduce the exact two-state derivative covariance.

## 10. Failed extension across lambda=1: exact obstruction

I also tried to push the minimum-one route through `lambda=1`, rather than only sharpening the interval below it.

The obstruction is structural. The He--Wang--Xiang escape derivative estimate (2.1) is available only for `lambda<m1`; here `m1=1`, so the usable pathwise score control ends at 1. Simultaneously, the lower escape bound `B>=1-lambda` becomes vacuous there.

Long degree-one pipes show why a pathwise substitute cannot simply exist. Writing `X=1/B`, at a one-child vertex the exact recursion is

`X = 1 + lambda X_child`.                                      (10.1)

Along a degree-one pipe of length n,

`X = 1+lambda+...+lambda^{n-1}+lambda^n X_branch`.              (10.2)

For `lambda>1`, arbitrarily long pipes therefore force arbitrarily large inverse escape and arbitrarily small B. Thus no uniform pathwise lower bound on B can continue the present proof across 1.

A successful cross-1 method must average the geometric pipe-length tail and control an inverse-escape moment or regeneration score rather than a pathwise escape cone. This is the precise residual obstruction and the next complementary route; it does not duplicate NM13-05's minimum-degree-at-least-three branch-addition program or PROB10-06's `{2,3}` polytope route.
