## 5. Finite-target Bellman setup

Let `A` be a finite or hyperplane-type target not containing a vertex `x`. Write

`U(v)=T(v,A)`.

Let `y_1,...,y_4` be the four neighbors of `x` and let

`E_i=tau_{x y_i}`.

Delete `x` and its four incident edges. In the deleted graph let

`a_i=T_{G\{x}}(y_i,A)`.

Conditional on every edge outside the star of `x`, the `a_i` are deterministic and `E_1,...,E_4` remain iid `Exp(1)`. Put

`X_i=a_i+E_i`, `M=min_i X_i`,

and let `I` be the unique minimizer. The Bellman equation is

`U(x)=M`.

For an oriented departure `x->y_i`, define its Bellman slack

`s_i=E_i+U(y_i)-U(x)>=0`.

The exact undirected return correction from the previous report is

- `s_I=0`;
- for every `i != I`,

`boxed{ s_i = min(X_i-M, 2 E_i). }`                              (5.1)

The `2E_i` term cannot be dropped: a path from `y_i` to the target may return through `x` using the same undirected edge and then leave along the winning channel.

Define the second Bellman slack / cheapest strictly nonoptimal departure

`S_x(A):=min_{i != I} s_i`.

The previous run bounded its lower tail by a union bound and obtained only `E S>=1/10`. The exact exponential structure permits a sharp result.

## 6. P20-FPP-SHARP-EXP-BELLMAN-GAP-04

**Claim: PROVED.** For every realization of the outside-star environment and every `u>=0`,

`boxed{ P(S_x(A)>u | outside star(x)) >= exp(-3u). }`             (6.1)

Consequently

`boxed{ E[S_x(A) | outside star(x)] >= 1/3. }`                    (6.2)

Both constants are sharp uniformly over the outside-star offsets: when `a_1=a_2=a_3=a_4`, equality holds in (6.1) for all `u` and in (6.2).

### Proof

Condition first on the outside-star offsets `(a_i)`. Condition further on the event that channel `i` wins and on its winning value `M=t`. Regular conditional distributions can be used; all subsequent statements hold for almost every such `t`.

For a fixed nonwinner `j != i`, the condition `X_j>t` is

`E_j > t-a_j`.

Write `d_j=t-a_j` and `L_j=max(0,d_j)`. By exponential memorylessness, conditional on `X_j>t`,

`E_j = L_j + Z_j`,

where `Z_j~Exp(1)`. The three variables `(Z_j)_{j!=i}` remain conditionally independent.

By (5.1), the event `s_j>u` is the conjunction

`E_j > d_j+u`                                                     (6.3)

and

`E_j > u/2`.                                                      (6.4)

There are two cases.

**Case 1: `d_j>=0`.** Then `L_j=d_j`; the first threshold is `d_j+u`, which dominates `u/2`. Thus `Z_j>u` implies, in fact is equivalent to, the two requirements. Hence

`P(s_j>u | I=i,M=t,outside) = e^{-u}`.                            (6.5)

**Case 2: `d_j<0`.** Then `L_j=0`. The required threshold is

`max(d_j+u,u/2) <= u`.

Therefore the event `Z_j=E_j>u` is sufficient for `s_j>u`, and

`P(s_j>u | I=i,M=t,outside) >= e^{-u}`.                           (6.6)

The three nonwinning channels are conditionally independent, so

`P(S_x(A)>u | I=i,M=t,outside)`
` = product_{j!=i} P(s_j>u | I=i,M=t,outside)`
` >= e^{-3u}`.                                                    (6.7)

Average over `(I,M)` to obtain (6.1). Integrating the survival function gives

`E[S_x(A)|outside] >= integral_0^infty e^{-3u} du = 1/3`,

which is (6.2).

For sharpness, if all `a_i` are equal, then for every nonwinner `j`, `X_j-M=E_j-E_I<E_j<2E_j`; hence `s_j=X_j-M`. Thus `S_x(A)=X_(2)-X_(1)`. The first spacing above the minimum of four iid rate-one exponentials is exactly `Exp(3)`, proving equality throughout. QED.

### General-degree form

The same proof on a degree-`q` undirected graph gives

`P(S>u|outside)>=e^{-(q-1)u}` and `E[S|outside]>=1/(q-1)`,

again sharp when all deleted-star offsets are equal. Only the `q=4` square-lattice case is used below.

## 7. P20-FPP-BUSEMANN-UNIQUE-SUCCESSOR-05

**Claim: PROVED for any ADH reconstructed Busemann weak limit obtained from these Exp(1) finite-target residual vectors.** At every fixed vertex `x`, the limiting residual vector

### Proof

For each finite hyperplane target in the ADH approximation, continuity of the passage-time law gives a unique first Bellman edge almost surely, hence exactly one zero residual. Its second residual is precisely the `S_x(A)` of Section 6 and satisfies the uniform tail (6.1).

Along the ADH subsequence defining the reconstructed Busemann measure, the four local residual coordinates converge in distribution (they are tight because `|B_alpha(x,y)|<=T(x,y)`). `P(S_x^B>=u) >= limsup P(S_x(A_alpha)>=u) >= e^{-3u}`.             (7.2)

Letting `u` decrease to zero shows `P(S_x^B>0)=1`. Since the smallest residual is zero by the Bellman/Busemann construction, there is exactly one zero coordinate. Integrating (7.2) yields `E S_x^B>=1/3`. QED.

### 8.1 Exponential controlled-error version

The full tail (6.1) gives more than an expectation. For every `lambda>0`, stochastic domination by `Exp(3)` implies

`E[e^{-lambda S_j} | past] <= 3/(3+lambda)`.                     (8.6)

The same induction remains valid for sequential predictable pivots. For any deterministic `k` and `0<a<1/3`,

`P(K_n>=k and sum_{j=1}^k Z_j <= a k)`

where

`I(q)=q-1-log q >0` for `0<q<1`.                                 (8.8)

## 10. New route considered: Jacquet pattern density plus reserved stars

Jacquet's pattern theorem is the most relevant newly consumed theorem for attacking the remaining alternative. In an unbounded-support law such as Exp(1), long geodesics cross translates of any admissible local pattern linearly often outside an exponentially small exceptional event.

This is promising because residual minimizers and ordinary FPP geodesics have the same path set: subtracting the endpoint Busemann term does not change which path minimizes.

However, **pattern density alone does not close the pivot hypothesis**. A local passage-time pattern may force an ordinary geodesic through a box, but whether a departure is calibrated by the diagonal Busemann cocycle depends on the outside target environment. If the pattern fully reveals the central star, it also destroys the fresh-star conditional law used in Section 6. If it leaves the star unrevealed, an unbounded Exp star can in general alter which local corridor the geodesic uses. Thus one still needs an annular/reserved-star construction or a bounded-conditioning version of the Bellman-gap lemma.

This is a real residual obstruction, not a reason to discard the pattern theorem. The next attack should try to build an admissible **annular pattern** that creates many candidate boxes while reserving at least one central star whose choice is fixed from annular/outside information before that star is exposed. Section 6 then supplies the exact toll automatically. If such a construction fails, the failure must identify why unbounded central-star weights cannot be decoupled from the crossing geometry.
