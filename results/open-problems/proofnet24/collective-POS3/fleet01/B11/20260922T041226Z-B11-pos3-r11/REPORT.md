## 3. Deep-fiber decay theorem

### Theorem 3.1

Let `P` be any finite poset. Let `a||b`, and suppose the only two relation-distinguishers of `a,b` are `d,u` with

`d<a`, `d||b`, `b<u`, `u||a`.

Assume additionally

`Pr(d<b)>2/3`, `Pr(a<u)>2/3`.

Let `A,C,X,Y,Z,W` be the six full-extension word counts above and `E=e(P)`. Then:

1. `A=C`, `X,Y<=A`, `Z<=min(X,Y)`, and either `W=0` (if `d<u`) or `W<=Z` (if `d||u`);
2. `A/E>2/9`;
3. `Z/E<1/6`;
4. if `d||u`, then `W/E<1/9`;
5. consequently `Z<3A/4`, and in the incomparable-outer case `W<A/2`.

If `d||u`, the last word `W=b u d a` is exactly the event `u<d`. Therefore

`Pr(d<u)>8/9`.                                                   (3.1)

So the incomparable outer pair is strictly more biased than the central pair, whose majority probability is `<7/9` in the inherited strict branch.

### Proof

The word injections in item 1 are the exact full-law exchange theorem of fleet02/B10; they are obtained skeleton-by-skeleton and survive summation.

Write normalized word masses

`alpha=A/E`, `xi=X/E`, `upsilon=Y/E`, `zeta=Z/E`, `omega=W/E`.

Then

`2 alpha + xi + upsilon + zeta + omega = 1`,                  (3.2)

and

`xi>=zeta>=omega`, `upsilon>=zeta>=omega`                     (3.3)

in the incomparable-outer case (with `omega=0` in the comparable-outer case).

The two neighboring probabilities are exactly

`Pr(d<b)=2 alpha+xi`,
`Pr(a<u)=2 alpha+upsilon`.                                     (3.4)

Hence

`xi+upsilon > 4/3 - 4 alpha`.                                  (3.5)

Also `Pr(d<b)<=3 alpha` because `xi<=alpha`, so `3 alpha>2/3`, proving

`alpha>2/9`.                                                     (3.6)

For the `Z` bound, suppose `zeta>=1/6`. By (3.3), `xi,upsilon>=zeta`, so (3.2) gives

`1 >= 2 alpha + 3 zeta >= 2 alpha + 1/2`,

hence `alpha<=1/4`. But (3.2) and (3.5) give

`1 > 2 alpha + (4/3-4 alpha) + zeta + omega
   = 4/3 - 2 alpha + zeta + omega
   >= 3/2 - 2 alpha`,

so `alpha>1/4`, a contradiction. Therefore

`zeta<1/6`.                                                      (3.7)

Now suppose `d||u` and `omega>=1/9`. From (3.3), each of `xi,upsilon,zeta` is at least `omega`; (3.2) then gives

`1 >= 2 alpha + 4 omega >= 2 alpha + 4/9`,

so `alpha<=5/18`. On the other hand, (3.2), (3.5), and `zeta+omega>=2/9` imply

`1 > 4/3 - 2 alpha + 2/9 = 14/9 - 2 alpha`,

so `alpha>5/18`, contradiction. Thus

`omega<1/9`.                                                     (3.8)

Combining (3.6) with (3.7)-(3.8) yields

`Z/A=(zeta/alpha)<(1/6)/(2/9)=3/4`,

and, when `d||u`,

`W/A=(omega/alpha)<(1/9)/(2/9)=1/2`.

Finally, when `d||u`, among the six admissible words only `W=b u d a` has `u<d`; hence `Pr(u<d)=omega<1/9`, proving (3.1). `□`

## 4. Width-three structural consequence: the only neutral third-chain set is a chain

### Lemma 4.1

For any incomparable pair `a,b` in a width-at-most-three poset, the set

`N(a,b)={v : v||a and v||b}`

is a chain.

### Proof

If `v,w in N(a,b)` were incomparable, then `{a,b,v,w}` would be a four-element antichain, contradicting width at most three. `□`

In the exact-two-distinguisher branch this matters because every vertex other than `d,u` that is noncomparable to one central endpoint must be neutral to both endpoints. Hence all genuinely third-chain vertices crossing the central pair lie on a **single chain**.
