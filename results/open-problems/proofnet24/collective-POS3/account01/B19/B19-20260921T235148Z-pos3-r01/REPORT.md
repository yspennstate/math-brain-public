# POS3 B19 — exact chain-insertion/deletion recurrence

**Outcome:** `ESSENTIAL_STEP_APPLIED`  
**Parent problem:** width-three `1/3–2/3` balanced-pair conjecture  
**Assigned subproblem:** prove a chain-insertion/deletion recurrence that can be used in a width-three induction without replacing the induced extension law by a uniform law.  
**Parent status after this report:** **OPEN**.

## 1. What is proved here

Let `P` be a finite poset, let

\[
C=\{c_1<\cdots<c_m\}
\]

be a chain of `P`, and let `Q=P\setminus C` be the induced subposet.  This report gives an exact fiber decomposition of `L(P)` over `L(Q)`.

For every `tau in L(Q)`, the number of full linear extensions of `P` restricting to `tau` is an explicitly computable integer `W_C(tau)`.  Hence the restriction to `Q` of a uniformly random linear extension of `P` has law

\[
\mu_P^Q(\tau)=\frac{W_C(\tau)}{\sum_{\sigma\in L(Q)}W_C(\sigma)},
\]

not the uniform law on `L(Q)` in general.

The same recurrence also counts, exactly, events involving a deleted-chain element `c_t` and a surviving element `q in Q`.  Thus the recurrence carries all pair-ordering numerators needed by a `1/3–2/3` induction, rather than only the total extension count.

The missing step is now sharply isolated: one needs a balance argument for the special weighted laws generated by these insertion multiplicities (or a different structural argument).  A simple bounded-density transfer from the uniform law cannot work: the multiplicity ratio can be arbitrarily large even when the ambient poset has width two.

## 2. Exact insertion coordinates

Fix `tau=(q_1,...,q_n) in L(Q)` and write

\[
\operatorname{pos}_\tau(q_i)=i.
\]

There are `n+1` gaps in `tau`, indexed by `r=0,...,n`; gap `r` lies after the first `r` elements of `tau`.

For `j=1,...,m`, define

\[
a_j(\tau)=\max\Bigl(\{\operatorname{pos}_\tau(q):q<_P c_j\}\cup\{0\}\Bigr),
\]

and

\[
b_j(\tau)=\min\Bigl(\{\operatorname{pos}_\tau(q)-1:c_j<_P q\}\cup\{n\}\Bigr).
\]

An insertion vector is an integer vector

\[
0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_m\le n,
\]

where `s_j` is the gap in which `c_j` is inserted.  Call it feasible when

\[
a_j(\tau)\le s_j\le b_j(\tau)\qquad(1\le j\le m).
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — monotone corridor

For every `tau in L(Q)`,

\[
a_1\le a_2\le\cdots\le a_m,
\qquad
b_1\le b_2\le\cdots\le b_m,
\qquad
a_j\le b_j.
\]

**Proof.** If `j<k`, then `c_j<c_k`.  Therefore every predecessor of `c_j` in `Q` is also a predecessor of `c_k`, so `a_j<=a_k`.  Likewise every successor of `c_k` in `Q` is also a successor of `c_j`; taking the minimum successor position gives `b_j<=b_k`.

For the last assertion, if `q<c_j<q'` with `q,q' in Q`, then `q<q'` in `Q`, so every linear extension `tau` places `q` strictly before `q'`.  Hence the largest predecessor position is at most one less than the smallest successor position.  The empty predecessor/successor conventions give the same conclusion at the boundaries.  Thus `a_j<=b_j`.  ∎

In particular, every `tau in L(Q)` has at least one lift: the choice `s_j=a_j` is feasible because the `a_j` are nondecreasing and `a_j<=b_j`.

## 3. Fiber-bijection theorem

### Theorem 3.1 — exact chain deletion/insertion formula

For fixed `tau in L(Q)`, restriction from `L(P)` to `Q` induces a bijection

\[
\{L\in L(P):L|_Q=\tau\}
\longleftrightarrow
\Bigl\{(s_1,...,s_m):
0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_m\le n,
\ a_j\le s_j\le b_j\Bigr\}.
\]

Consequently, defining

\[
W_C(\tau):=
\#\Bigl\{s_1\le\cdots\le s_m:
 a_j(\tau)\le s_j\le b_j(\tau)\Bigr\},
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{\quad e(P)=\sum_{\tau\in L(Q)}W_C(\tau).\quad}
\]

**Proof.** Given `L in L(P)` restricting to `tau`, let `s_j` be the number of `Q`-elements preceding `c_j` in `L`.  Because `c_1<...<c_m`, the sequence `s_1,...,s_m` is nondecreasing.  If `q<c_j`, then `q` precedes `c_j`, so `pos_tau(q)<=s_j`; hence `s_j>=a_j`.  If `c_j<q`, then `c_j` precedes `q`, so `s_j<pos_tau(q)`; hence `s_j<=b_j`.  Thus the vector is feasible.

Conversely, given a feasible vector, start from `tau`.  In each gap `r`, insert all `c_j` with `s_j=r`, in increasing `j` order.  Relations inside `Q` are respected because `tau` is a linear extension of `Q`; relations inside `C` are respected because the `s_j` are nondecreasing and ties are ordered by `j`.  If `q<c_j`, then `pos_tau(q)<=a_j<=s_j`, so `q` occurs before `c_j`.  If `c_j<q`, then `s_j<=b_j<pos_tau(q)`, so `c_j` occurs before `q`.  These exhaust all possible comparabilities involving `C`, so the resulting total order is in `L(P)`.  The two constructions are inverse.  Summing fiber cardinalities gives the displayed formula.  ∎

## 4. Dynamic recurrence for the exact multiplicity

Define the forward counts

\[
F_1(r)=\mathbf 1\{a_1\le r\le b_1\},
\]

and, for `j>=2`,

\[
\boxed{\quad
F_j(r)=\mathbf 1\{a_j\le r\le b_j\}
\sum_{u=0}^{r}F_{j-1}(u).
\quad}
\]

Then `F_j(r)` is exactly the number of feasible partial insertion vectors `(s_1,...,s_j)` with `s_j=r`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad W_C(\tau)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}F_m(r).\quad}
\]

Using prefix sums, all `F_j(r)` are computed in `O(mn)` arithmetic operations for a fixed `tau`.

For a single deleted element `C={c}`, this collapses to the familiar but crucially **weighted** recurrence

\[
W_{\{c\}}(\tau)=b(\tau)-a(\tau)+1,
\qquad
 e(P)=\sum_{\tau\in L(P-c)}\bigl(b(\tau)-a(\tau)+1\bigr).
\]

This is the correct one-element deletion rule: after deleting `c`, extensions of `P-c` with different numbers of admissible reinsertion slots are not equiprobable under restriction of the uniform law on `L(P)`.

## 5. Exact induced law and exact pair numerators

For every event `A` depending only on the relative order of elements of `Q`, Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
\boxed{\quad
\Pr_P(A)
=
\frac{\sum_{\tau\in L(Q)}W_C(\tau)\mathbf 1_A(\tau)}
{\sum_{\tau\in L(Q)}W_C(\tau)}.
\quad}
\]

Equivalently, relative to the uniform law `mu_Q` on `L(Q)`,

\[
\frac{d\mu_P^Q}{d\mu_Q}(\tau)
=
\frac{e(Q)W_C(\tau)}{e(P)}.
\]

So for incomparable `x,y in Q`, the exact balance numerator is

\[
N_P(x<y)
=
\sum_{\tau\in L(Q):\,x<_\tau y}W_C(\tau).
\]

### Pair events involving the deleted chain

The same corridor recurrence handles pairs `q,c_t` with `q in Q`.  Define backward counts

\[
G_m(r)=\mathbf 1\{a_m\le r\le b_m\},
\]

and for `j<m`,

\[
G_j(r)=\mathbf 1\{a_j\le r\le b_j\}
\sum_{u=r}^{n}G_{j+1}(u).
\]

For any fixed slice `t`,

\[
H_t(r):=F_t(r)G_t(r)
\]

is exactly the number of full feasible insertion vectors with `s_t=r`.  In particular,

\[
W_C(\tau)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}H_t(r)
\]

for every `t`.

If `k=pos_tau(q)`, then

\[
\boxed{\quad
N_\tau(q<c_t)=\sum_{r=k}^{n}H_t(r),
\qquad
N_\tau(c_t<q)=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}H_t(r).
\quad}
\]

Summing these quantities over `tau in L(Q)` gives the exact global pair counts in `L(P)`.  Thus the deletion state `(a_j,b_j,F_j,G_j)` is sufficient to recover every potentially relevant pair-order numerator, not merely `e(P)`.

This is the main reason the recurrence is usable in a width-three induction: the bias is retained as an exact integer multiplicity rather than normalized away.

## 6. Minimal counterexample to the uniform-law shortcut

Let `Q={x,y}` be an antichain, let `C={c}`, and impose only `x<c`.  Then `P` has three linear extensions:

`x y c`, `x c y`, `y x c`.

For `tau=xy`, there are two slots for `c`, so `W_C(xy)=2`.  For `tau=yx`, only the final slot is legal, so `W_C(yx)=1`.  Therefore

\[
\Pr_P(x<y)=\frac{2}{3},
\]

whereas the uniform law on `L(Q)` gives `1/2`.

Hence an induction step of the form “delete a chain, apply a theorem under the uniform law on the remainder, then lift the same probability back” is invalid unless one proves an additional weighted-transfer statement.

## 7. The bias can be arbitrarily large even at width two

The obstruction is not a small constant perturbation.

Let `Q` be the disjoint union of a one-element chain `A={a}` and a chain

\[
B=\{b_1<\cdots<b_s\}.
\]

Add one element `c` with `a<c` and with `c` incomparable to every `b_i`.  The resulting `P` has width two.

For an interleaving `tau in L(Q)`, the only predecessor of `c` is `a` and there is no successor of `c` in `Q`, so

\[
W_{\{c\}}(\tau)
=(s+1)-\operatorname{pos}_\tau(a)+1
=1+\#\{b_i:\operatorname{pos}_\tau(b_i)>\operatorname{pos}_\tau(a)\}.
\]

Thus

\[
1\le W_{\{c\}}(\tau)\le s+1,
\]

and both extremes occur.  The ratio `max W/min W=s+1` is unbounded.

**Consequence.** A future proof cannot obtain the width-three `1/3–2/3` conclusion merely from a universal constant bound comparing the induced deletion law to the uniform law.  The special structure of the insertion weights must be used.
