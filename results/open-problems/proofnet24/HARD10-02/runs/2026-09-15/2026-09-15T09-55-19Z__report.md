# 1. Exact local decomposition into a recurrent persistent walk plus trace-internal moves

At time `n>=1`, let `p=X_{n-1}`, `x=X_n`, and let `r` be the number of already traversed edges incident to `x`. The back edge `{p,x}` is old, so `1<=r<=4`. The ORRW denominator is

\[
D_r = cr+(4-r)=4+(c-1)r.
\]

Define a history-free persistent nearest-neighbor chain `B_c` on directed edges of `Z^2` by

- reverse the previous step with probability `c/(c+3)`;
- take each of the other three directions with probability `1/(c+3)`.

If `r>1`, let `U_n` be uniform on the `r-1` old neighbors of `x` other than `p`, and put

\[
\alpha_r=\frac{c+3}{D_r}.
\]

Then the ORRW kernel decomposes **exactly** as

\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm ORRW}(X_{n+1}\in\cdot\mid\mathcal F_n)
=
\alpha_r P_{B_c}(X_{n+1}\in\cdot\mid p,x)
+(1-\alpha_r)U_n.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

For `r=1`, `alpha_r=1` and the kernels coincide.

Indeed, a fresh edge receives probability `alpha_r/(c+3)=1/D_r`; the back edge receives `alpha_r c/(c+3)=c/D_r`; and an old non-back edge receives

\[
\frac1{D_r}+\frac{1-\alpha_r}{r-1}
=
\frac1{D_r}+\frac{c-1}{D_r}
=
\frac c{D_r}.
\]

Thus every deviation from `B_c` is an extra move along an already traversed, non-back edge. No correction move can discover a fresh edge.

## Proposition 1.2 — the baseline `B_c` is recurrent for every finite `c>0`

Let `J_n=X_n-X_{n-1}`. Then

\[
E[J_{n+1}\mid J_n]=\rho J_n,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{1-c}{c+3}.
\]

The direction chain is a finite irreducible chain with uniform stationary law. Under stationarity,

\[
E[J_0J_k^T]=\rho^k\frac{I_2}{2}.
\]

Hence the asymptotic covariance of the additive process is

\[
\Sigma
=
\frac{I_2}{2}
+2\sum_{k\ge1}\rho^k\frac{I_2}{2}
=
\frac1{c+1}I_2,
\]
which is nondegenerate. The standard two-dimensional Markov-additive Chung–Fuchs/Fourier criterion therefore gives recurrence. Equivalently, the Fourier transfer matrix has a simple eigenvalue

\[
\lambda(k)=1-\frac{|k|^2}{2(c+1)}+o(|k|^2)
\]
near zero, yielding parity-compatible return probabilities of order `1/n` and a divergent Green sum.

This does **not** prove ORRW recurrence: the adaptive `U_n` moves can relocate the walker within its old trace. Equation (1.1) isolates that as the exact obstruction.

# 2. General harmonic fresh-edge martingale

This is the strongest exact reduction in this run.

Let `f` be any function harmonic for ordinary nearest-neighbor simple random walk on a region, i.e.

\[
\sum_{y\sim x}(f(y)-f(x))=0
\]
at every interior point under consideration. Let

\[
I_k=1_{\{\{X_k,X_{k+1}\}\text{ is fresh just before time }k\}}.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
M_n^f
=
f(X_n)
+(c-1)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}I_k\big(f(X_{k+1})-f(X_k)\big).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

## Proposition 2.1 — PROVED

`M_n^f` is a martingale up to the first exit from the harmonicity region.

### Proof

At current vertex `x`, an old edge `x->y` has transition weight `c` and changes `M^f` by `f(y)-f(x)`. A fresh edge has transition weight `1` and changes `M^f` by

\[
(f(y)-f(x))+(c-1)(f(y)-f(x))
=c(f(y)-f(x)).
\]

Therefore, with denominator `D`,

\[
E[\Delta M_n^f\mid\mathcal F_n]
=
\frac cD
\sum_{y\sim x}(f(y)-f(x))
=0.
\]
QED.

For coordinate functions on `Z x Gamma`, this is exactly the structure of Kious–Schapira–Singh Lemma 4.1. The arbitrary-harmonic-function form above is a direct extension; novelty is not asserted.

# 3. Exact annular escape identity

Let `A,B` be disjoint absorbing boundary sets in a finite graph/domain, and let

\[
h(x)=P_x^{\rm SRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
\]
be the ordinary SRW equilibrium voltage, so `h=0` on `A`, `h=1` on `B`, and `h` is harmonic inside. Stop ORRW at

\[
\tau=\tau_A\wedge\tau_B.
\]

Applying Proposition 2.1 and bounded optional stopping gives

\[
h(x)
=
P_x^{\rm ORRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
+(c-1)E_x^{\rm ORRW} S_\tau(h),
\]
where

\[
S_\tau(h)
:=
\sum_{k<\tau}I_k\big(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)\big).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
P_x^{\rm ORRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
=
h(x)-(c-1)E_x^{\rm ORRW}S_\tau(h).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This turns the full excursion comparison into one signed quantity.

For a square annulus with `A={0}` and `B=partial B_R`, SRW gives `h(x)=Theta(1/log R)` for a neighbor `x` of the origin. Therefore planar recurrence would follow from either of the following sufficient statements:

\[
E^{\rm ORRW}S_\tau(h)\ge0
\quad\text{for every }R,
\tag{3.2}
\]

or even the weaker asymptotic lower bound

\[
E^{\rm ORRW}S_\tau(h)
\ge -o(1/\log R).
\tag{3.3}
\]

The sign in (3.2) is not proved.

Equation (3.1) also shows precisely why the familiar coordinate martingale is relevant: recurrence reduces to controlling the signed harmonic line integral over first traversals, not to controlling all old-edge occupation.

# 6. Exact obstruction: current equilibrium voltage is not a supermartingale

A tempting route is to let `h_S` be the equilibrium voltage in the **current** conductance environment and hope that `h_{S_n}(X_n)` is a supermartingale. This is false.

Take vertices `0,1,2,3,4`, root `0`, target `4`, reinforcement `c=2`, and edges

\[
(0,1),(1,2),(1,4),(1,3),(0,2),(2,3),(2,4).
\]

Let the already traversed set be

\[
S=\{(0,1),(1,2)\}
\]

and current vertex `2`. This state is reachable through `0->1->2` before hitting root or target. Exact rational computation gives

\[
h_S(2)=16/37,
\]

while the one-step expectation, using `h_S` after old steps and the updated equilibrium voltage `h_{S\cup\{e\}}` after a fresh step, is

\[
E[h_{S_{n+1}}(X_{n+1})\mid S_n=S,X_n=2]
=2329/5365.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E[\Delta h_{S_n}(X_n)\mid S,2]
=9/5365>0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

So a pointwise dynamic-voltage supermartingale cannot prove recurrence without an additional correction term or excursion-level averaging.

# 7. Continuous-time discovery-clock interpretation from current literature

Collevecchio–Tarres normalize traversed-edge jump rates to `1` and fresh-edge jump rates to `alpha`; here `alpha=1/c`. Their Proposition 1.4 gives the Radon–Nikodym factor relative to continuous-time SRW in terms of fresh-edge exposure times and the number of discovered edges. Their Proposition 2.3 shows that the increments of total fresh-boundary exposure between successive edge discoveries are iid `Exp(alpha)` under ORRW.

Equivalently, conditional on the present finite trace, the next discovery can be generated by running continuous-time SRW on the old trace and killing it when accumulated fresh-boundary local time exceeds an independent exponential threshold of mean `1/alpha=c`.

This exactly matches the elementary Feynman–Kac kernel

\[
P_x(\text{discover before hitting }0)
=
1-E_x\exp\{-A_{\tau_0}/c\},
\]

where `A_t` is accumulated number-of-fresh-half-edges exposure along the internal SRW. Stronger reinforcement therefore delays the next discovery for a **fixed** trace, but the location of that discovery changes with the threshold. This is why stagewise discovery-probability monotonicity alone does not imply (3.2).

# 8. Known control case: unique root-target path

If a finite graph has a unique simple path

\[
o=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_R=b
\]

and the process starts immediately after `o->v_1`, every off-path excursion returns to the same path vertex. When the discovered path reaches `v_k`, the `k` backward path edges have conductance `c` and the frontier edge has conductance `1`. Hence

\[
P_{v_k}(\tau_{v_{k+1}}<\tau_{v_0})
=
\frac{k}{k+c}
\]

and

\[
P_{v_1}^{\rm ORRW}(\tau_b<\tau_o)
=
\prod_{k=1}^{R-1}\frac{k}{k+c}
=
\frac{\Gamma(R)\Gamma(1+c)}{\Gamma(R+c)}
\le\frac1R.
\]

This is a control/special case; no novelty is claimed.
