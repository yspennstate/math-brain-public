# 2. Cumulative exact reduction retained from the durable/recovered checkpoints

Let `A,B` be disjoint absorbing boundary sets in a finite graph/domain and let

\[
h(x)=P_x^{\rm SRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
\]

be the unit-conductance SRW equilibrium voltage. If

\[
I_k=1_{\{\{X_k,X_{k+1}\}\text{ is fresh immediately before time }k\}},
\]

then

\[
M_n^h
=h(X_n)+(c-1)\sum_{k<n}I_k\bigl(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)\bigr)
\tag{2.1}
\]

is an ORRW martingale up to absorption. Consequently, with `tau=tau_A wedge tau_B`,

\[
\boxed{
P_x^{\rm ORRW(c)}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
=h(x)-(c-1)E_x^{\rm ORRW(c)}S_\tau(h),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

\[
S_\tau(h)=\sum_{k<\tau}I_k\bigl(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)\bigr).
\tag{2.3}
\]

At `c=1`, ORRW is SRW and differentiation of (2.2) gives

\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac{d}{dc}P_x^{\rm ORRW(c)}(\tau_B<\tau_A)\right|_{c=1}
=-D(G;A,B,x),
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

with

\[
D(G;A,B,x)
:=E_x^{\rm SRW}
\sum_{k<\tau}I_k\bigl(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)\bigr).
\tag{2.5}
\]

# 3. New exact edgewise first-crossing Green formula

This is the main new theorem of the run.

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite connected simple graph. Let `A,B` be absorbing boundary sets and let

\[
I=V\setminus(A\cup B)
\]

be the interior. Write `d(z)` for the full graph degree, including boundary neighbors. Let `x in I` be the post-first-step start. One distinguished edge `e_0` joining `x` to the root boundary is already traversed/reinforced and is therefore excluded from the fresh-edge sum.

For a fresh undirected edge `e={u,v}`, choose an arbitrary orientation `u->v`. Define the signed first-crossing probability

\[
q_e(x)
:=P_x(\text{first traversal of }e\text{ before }\tau\text{ is }u\to v)
-P_x(\text{first traversal is }v\to u).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If one orientation is impossible because an endpoint is absorbing, its probability is zero.

Define the matrix `A_e` on the interior vertices by

\[
(A_e)_{zz}=d(z),
\]

and, for distinct interior vertices `y,z`,

\[
(A_e)_{yz}=
\begin{cases}
-1,&\{y,z\}\in E,\ \{y,z\}\ne e,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus `A_e` is the killed-walk Dirichlet operator before the first crossing of `e`: the edge `e` is removed as a continuation edge, but an attempted crossing of it remains a unit-rate/one-step terminal event. Put

\[
b_e=1_{\{u\in I\}}\delta_u-1_{\{v\in I\}}\delta_v.
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — first-crossing resolvent formula (PROVED)

\[
\boxed{q_e(\cdot)=A_e^{-1}b_e.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
D(G;A,B,x)
=\sum_{e\ne e_0}
(h(v_e)-h(u_e))\,[A_e^{-1}b_e](x),
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

where each edge may be oriented arbitrarily; reversing an orientation changes both factors' signs and leaves the term invariant.

### Proof

Before the first traversal of `e` or absorption at `A union B`, define `q(z)` as the expected terminal sign `+1` if `e` is first crossed `u->v`, `-1` if first crossed `v->u`, and `0` if the walk is absorbed before crossing `e`.

For an interior vertex `z` not incident to `e`, first-step conditioning gives

\[
d(z)q(z)-\sum_{w\in I:\{z,w\}\in E}q(w)=0.
\]

At `u`, the attempted step across `e` contributes terminal payoff `+1` instead of continuing to `v`, so

\[
d(u)q(u)-\sum_{w\in I:\{u,w\}\in E,\ w\ne v}q(w)=1
\]

when `u` is interior. Similarly the right side is `-1` at an interior `v`. These equations are exactly `A_eq=b_e`. Since the killed system is transient on the finite domain, `A_e` is invertible, giving (3.4). Summing the harmonic increments of each edge at its first traversal gives (3.5). QED.

### Computational consequence

Equation (3.5) reduces computation of the first derivative at `c=1` from a dynamic program over up to `2^{|E|}` trace states to one ordinary linear solve per edge, i.e. polynomial work in the graph size.

# 4. Closed form using the ordinary killed Green kernel

Let `L` be the usual Dirichlet graph Laplacian on the interior and

\[
G=L^{-1}.
\]

Consider an interior edge `e={u,v}` oriented `u->v`. Write

\[
A=G(u,u),\quad B=G(v,v),\quad C=G(u,v),
\]

and

\[
X=G(x,u),\quad Y=G(x,v).
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — two-endpoint Green formula (PROVED)

\[
\boxed{
q_e(x)
=
\frac{(1+C+B)X-(1+C+A)Y}
{(1+C)^2-AB}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The denominator is positive because it is the determinant of the positive two-endpoint Schur complement associated with the killed first-crossing problem.

### Proof by future crossing counts

Let

\[
p_+=P_x(\text{first crossing is }u\to v),
\qquad
p_-=P_x(\text{first crossing is }v\to u).
\]

In continuous-time unit-conductance SRW, the expected number of directed crossings `u->v` before absorption is `G(x,u)=X`, and the expected number of directed crossings `v->u` is `G(x,v)=Y`.

After the first crossing `u->v`, one such crossing has already occurred and the future walk starts at `v`; after the first crossing `v->u`, the future walk starts at `u`. Hence

\[
X=(1+C)p_+ + A p_-,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
Y=B p_+ +(1+C)p_-.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Solving this `2x2` system and subtracting gives (4.1).

For a boundary edge `e=(u,b)` with `u` interior and `b` absorbing, oriented `u->b`, the reverse crossing is impossible and

\[
\boxed{q_e(x)=G(x,u).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equations (4.1) and (4.4) give a direct formula for the full first-order quantity (3.5) from the ordinary killed Green kernel alone.

### Alternative decomposition

Set

\[
\alpha=A+B+2C,
\quad
\beta=A-B,
\quad
\gamma=A+B-2C.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
q_e(x)=
\frac{2[(\alpha+2)(X-Y)-\beta(X+Y)]}
{(\alpha+2)(2-\gamma)+\beta^2}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Here `gamma` is the effective resistance between the adjacent endpoints in the killed network. Formula (4.5) separates the ordinary Green gradient `X-Y` from the diagonal-Green asymmetry `beta=A-B`. In nearly translation-invariant bulk regions, `beta` is small; this provides a concrete route for asymptotic annular analysis.

# 8. New pathwise parity identity for first crossings

There is a simple exact interpretation of why `q_e` differs from the ordinary expected net current.

Fix an unoriented edge `e` and orient it `u->v`. Let `N_e` be the total number of traversals of `e` before absorption, and let `F_e in {-1,0,1}` be the sign of its first traversal (`0` if never crossed). Traversals of a fixed edge alternate directions, so

\[
N_e^+-N_e^-=F_e\,1_{\{N_e\text{ is odd}\}}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Hence

\[
q_e(x)=E[F_e],
\]

whereas the ordinary expected net edge current is

\[
j_e(x)=E[F_e1_{\{N_e\text{ odd}\}}].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
q_e(x)-j_e(x)
=E[F_e1_{\{N_e>0\text{ is even}\}}].
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since the expected total harmonic increment of SRW is zero, the first-order correction can equivalently be viewed as a weighted contribution of **even-crossed edges**, together with the adjustment for the initially old root edge. This may be useful for a loop-erasure/cycle decomposition: the obstruction to a direct current identity is precisely the even-crossing/cycle sector of the path.

No sign theorem for this parity representation is proved here.
