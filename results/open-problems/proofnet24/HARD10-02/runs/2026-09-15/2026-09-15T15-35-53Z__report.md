# 3. Correction: the previous parity interpretation was false

The 14:35 report contained an auxiliary interpretation claiming that traversals of a fixed unoriented edge alternate directions and hence that the difference between first-crossing orientation and ordinary net current could be identified with an `even-crossing` sector.

That claim is false on graphs with cycles.

A deterministic counterexample is the triangle path

\[
u\to v\to w\to u\to v.
\]

The edge `{u,v}` is traversed twice in the **same** direction `u->v`, with no intervening traversal `v->u`. Therefore crossings of a fixed edge need not alternate directions.

This error affects only the final heuristic/parity interpretation of the 14:35 report. The first-crossing resolvent theorem, the Green formula, the exact finite `n<=6` certificate, and the square-annulus computations do **not** use the parity claim and remain intact.

The invalid parity statement is withdrawn.

# 4. Exact replacement: first-crossing orientation versus ordinary electrical current

Let `G=L^{-1}` be the ordinary Dirichlet Green kernel for SRW killed on `A union B`. For an oriented interior edge `e=(u,v)`, define

\[
A_e=G(u,u),\quad B_e=G(v,v),\quad C_e=G(u,v),
\]

and

\[
j_e(x)=G(x,u)-G(x,v).
\tag{4.1}
\]

`j_e(x)` is the expected total **net** number of crossings `u->v` minus `v->u` before absorption, equivalently the unit electrical current across `e` generated by a source at `x`.

Put

\[
a_e=A_e-C_e,\qquad b_e=B_e-C_e,
\]
\[
\gamma_e=a_e+b_e=A_e+B_e-2C_e,
\]
\[
\beta_e=a_e-b_e=A_e-B_e.
\]

By the maximum principle applied to the dipole Green potential,

\[
a_e\ge0,\qquad b_e\ge0.
\]

Moreover `gamma_e` is the effective resistance between adjacent `u,v` in the killed unit network, hence

\[
0\le\gamma_e\le1
\tag{4.2}
\]

because the direct unit-resistance edge itself is available.

## Theorem 4.1 — exact first-crossing/current identity (PROVED)

\[
\boxed{
 j_e(x)=\left(1-\frac{\gamma_e}{2}\right)q_e(x)
 +\frac{\beta_e}{2}r_e(x).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
 q_e-j_e
 =\frac{\gamma_e j_e-\beta_e r_e}{2-\gamma_e}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the first crossing of `e`.

If the first crossing is `u->v`, one unit of positive net current has already occurred and the expected future net current starting from `v` is

\[
G(v,u)-G(v,v)=C_e-B_e=-b_e.
\]

Thus the conditional expected total net crossing count is `1-b_e`.

If the first crossing is `v->u`, the initial contribution is `-1` and the expected future net current starting from `u` is

\[
G(u,u)-G(u,v)=a_e,
\]

so the conditional expected total is `-1+a_e`.

Therefore

\[
j_e=p_+(1-b_e)+p_-(-1+a_e)
=q_e-b_ep_++a_ep_-.
\]

Since

\[
b_e=(\gamma_e-\beta_e)/2,
\qquad
a_e=(\gamma_e+\beta_e)/2,
\]

this is exactly (4.3), hence (4.4). QED.

Two immediate rigorous bounds follow:

\[
|j_e|\le r_e,
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
|q_e-j_e|
=|b_ep_+-a_ep_-|
\le \gamma_e r_e
\le r_e.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the correct replacement for the withdrawn parity story.

# 5. New global decomposition of the first-order correction

Orient the initially old root edge as `e_0=(r,x)`, from the absorbing root to the start. Then

\[
g_{e_0}=h(x),
\]

and its ordinary electrical current is

\[
j_{e_0}=-G(x,x).
\]

For boundary edges, first-crossing current equals ordinary net current because crossing the boundary absorbs immediately. Also, Green's identity gives

\[
\sum_{e\in E} g_ej_e=0,
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `g_e=h(v)-h(u)` in the chosen orientation.

Since the actual first-order sum omits the already-old edge `e_0`, Theorem 4.1 gives the exact decomposition

\[
\boxed{
D
=h(x)G(x,x)
+
\sum_{e\text{ interior}}
 g_e\,
 \frac{\gamma_ej_e-\beta_er_e}{2-\gamma_e}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The first term is always positive and is exactly the benefit of having the root-start edge already reinforced. The second term is the genuine cyclic/interior correction.

Equation (5.2) is useful in two ways:

1. it explains why the special post-first-step initial state can behave differently from an artificial `all edges fresh` start;
2. it isolates the remaining first-order difficulty into ordinary Green quantities (`j`, `beta`, `gamma`) plus the edge-crossing probability `r`.

No sign is asserted for the interior correction.

# 6. New planar finite-graph counterexample to universal excursion domination

The 14:35 checkpoint treated the following statement as a high-leverage candidate:

\[
P_x^{ORRW(c)}(\tau_B<\tau_r)
\stackrel{?}{\le}
P_x^{SRW}(\tau_B<\tau_r)
\tag{C1}
\]

for every finite graph when the walk starts at `x` just after `r->x` and only `{r,x}` is old.

(C1) is false.

## Fan family `F_m`

Let the vertices be

\[
r,x,b,y_1,\ldots,y_m,
\]

with absorbing failure vertex `r`, absorbing success vertex `b`, and edges

\[
\{r,x\},\ \{x,b\},\ \{r,y_i\},\ \{x,y_i\},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Start at `x` immediately after `r->x`, so `{r,x}` is old.

This is a simple **planar** graph: it is a theta/book-type graph consisting of the direct `r-x` edge and `m` internally disjoint two-edge `r-y_i-x` paths, plus the leaf edge `x-b`. Its degrees grow with `m`, so it is not a square-lattice subgraph for large `m`.

### SRW voltage

By symmetry, if `h_y=h(y_i)`, then

\[
2h_y=h(x),
\]

while at `x`

\[
(m+2)h(x)=1+mh_y.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{h(x)=\frac{2}{m+4},\qquad h(y_i)=\frac1{m+4}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Exact first-crossing probabilities at `c=1`

For this family the killed Green kernel gives

\[
G(x,x)=\frac{2}{m+4},
\qquad
G(x,y_i)=\frac1{m+4}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore:

- on the target edge `x->b`,
  \[
  q_{xb}=\frac{2}{m+4};
  \]
- orienting a root spoke `r->y_i`,
  \[
  q_{ry_i}=-\frac1{m+4};
  \]
- orienting an interior spoke `x->y_i`, the two-endpoint Green formula gives
  \[
  q_{xy_i}=\frac{2}{m+5}.
  \]

Substituting the harmonic increments into (2.1) gives

\[
D_m
=
\frac{2(m+2)}{(m+4)^2}
-
\frac{m}{(m+4)^2}
-
\frac{2m}{(m+4)(m+5)}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
D_m=\frac{5-m}{(m+4)(m+5)}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\left.\frac{d}{dc}P_x^{ORRW(c)}(\tau_b<\tau_r)\right|_{c=1}
=
\frac{m-5}{(m+4)(m+5)}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For every `m>=6`, infinitesimal **positive reinforcement increases escape probability above its SRW value**. Hence universal first-order monotonicity is false, even on planar finite graphs.

For `m=6`,

\[
D_6=-\frac1{110},
\qquad
P'_1=\frac1{110}>0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

This does not refute recurrence on `Z^2`; the counterexample uses high-degree hubs and many triangles sharing the initial edge, structures absent from the square lattice.

# 7. Exact nonperturbative counterexample at a concrete reinforcement

The same family permits an exact full-`c` recursion, so no limiting/derivative argument is needed to refute (C1).

Let `P_k` be the ORRW success probability from `x` when exactly `k` of the `m` edges `{x,y_i}` are already old. At `x`, the transition denominator is

\[
Z_k=c(k+1)+(m-k)+1.
\]

If an old spoke is chosen, the walk reaches `y_i`; from there it returns to `x` over the old edge with probability `c/(c+1)` and hits the root over the fresh edge with probability `1/(c+1)`. The same statement holds after a new spoke is first crossed, except the state becomes `k+1` upon returning to `x`.

First-step conditioning therefore gives

\[
P_k
=
\frac1{Z_k}
+
\frac{kc}{Z_k}\frac{c}{c+1}P_k
+
\frac{m-k}{Z_k}\frac{c}{c+1}P_{k+1}.
\]

Solving,

\[
\boxed{
P_k
=
\frac{c+1+(m-k)cP_{k+1}}
{(c+1)(c+m+1)-k},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,m,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

with the `(m-k)` term absent at `k=m`.

This recursion is exact and uses only rational arithmetic for rational `c`.

Take

\[
m=6,\qquad c=\frac43
\]

which corresponds to the original reinforcement increment

\[
a=c-1=\frac13.
\]

The exact certificate gives

\[
P_0
=
\frac{528154735443}{2635499877725},
\]

whereas SRW gives

\[
h(x)=\frac15.
\]

Their difference is

\[
\boxed{
P_0-\frac15
=
\frac{1054759898}{2635499877725}
>0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore (C1) is rigorously false at a concrete positive reinforcement, not merely at derivative level.

# 8. Relevance to the square-lattice target

The counterexample changes the research strategy but does **not** answer the original `Z^2` question.

Important structural distinction:

- `F_m` obtains negative first-order `D_m` by putting many common neighbors between the root and the post-first-step vertex `x`, i.e. many triangles sharing the already-old edge `{r,x}`.
- In the nearest-neighbor square lattice, adjacent vertices have **no common neighbor** and the graph is bipartite of maximum degree four.

This suggests that the earlier positive square-box computations may reflect genuine lattice geometry rather than a universal finite-graph principle.

A bounded exploratory search in this run found no negative first-order `D` in random bipartite graphs tested up to moderate size, no negative example in random maximum-degree-four graphs tested up to moderate size, and no negative example among random connected subgraphs of small rectangular square grids. These are numerical observations only and are **not** promoted to the proved ledger.

A new plausible finite theorem to investigate is therefore:

> first-order escape monotonicity may hold on bipartite graphs, or on the narrower class of square-lattice domains, despite failing on general planar graphs.

This is now a better-matched subproblem than the refuted universal (C1).
