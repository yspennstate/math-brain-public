# 2. Cumulative exact first-order setup

Let `A,B` be two disjoint absorbing boundary sets in a finite unit-conductance graph. Contract each boundary set to a terminal when convenient. Let `x` be the post-first-step starting vertex and let the distinguished root edge

\[
e_0=\{r,x\},\qquad r\in A,
\]

already be old. Let

\[
h(z)=P_z^{SRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The durable checkpoints prove the fixed-harmonic fresh-edge martingale and therefore

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dc}P_x^{ORRW(c)}(\tau_B<\tau_A)\right|_{c=1}=-D,
\tag{2.2}
\]

where

\[
D=E_x^{SRW}\sum_{\substack{k<\tau:\\
\{X_k,X_{k+1}\}\text{ fresh}}}
\bigl(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)\bigr).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For an oriented interior edge `e=(u,v)`, write

\[
d_e=h(v)-h(u).
\]

Let `G` be the Dirichlet Green matrix for SRW killed on `A union B`, in the combinatorial-Laplacian normalization. Put

\[
A_e=G(u,u),\quad B_e=G(v,v),\quad C_e=G(u,v),
\]

\[
\gamma_e=A_e+B_e-2C_e,
\qquad
\beta_e=A_e-B_e,
\tag{2.4}
\]

and define the ordinary net current from the source `x`

\[
j_e=G(x,u)-G(x,v).
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let `p_+` and `p_-` be the probabilities that the first crossing of `e` occurs as `u->v` and `v->u`, respectively, and put

\[
q_e=p_+-p_-,\qquad r_e=p_++p_-.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The 15:35 checkpoint proved the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
q_e-j_e=\frac{\gamma_e j_e-\beta_e r_e}{2-\gamma_e}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

For a unit edge,

\[
0\le \gamma_e\le1.
\tag{2.8}
\]

# 3. Exact source-edge decomposition

This elementary identity is useful because boundary edges need no first-crossing correction.

Let

\[
g(z)=G(x,z),
\]

with `g=0` on both absorbing boundaries. For any oriented edge `e=(u,v)`, the ordinary expected net crossing count is

\[
j_e=g(u)-g(v).
\]

Because `h` is harmonic at every interior vertex and `g` vanishes on the boundary, discrete summation by parts gives

\[
\sum_{e\in E}d_ej_e=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Orient the already-old initial edge as `r->x`. Its contribution to (3.1) is

\[
(h(x)-h(r))(g(r)-g(x))
=-h(x)G(x,x).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For any fresh edge incident directly to an absorbing boundary, the first crossing is the absorbing crossing, so `q_e=j_e`. Hence only fresh **interior-interior** edges have a nontrivial `q-j` correction. Combining these facts with (2.3) gives

\[
\boxed{
D
=h(x)G(x,x)
+\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\subset I}d_e(q_e-j_e),
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where `I=V\setminus(A\cup B)` and all interior edges may be oriented arbitrarily.

Using (2.7),

\[
\boxed{
D
=h(x)G(x,x)
+\sum_{e\subset I}
\frac{\gamma_ed_ej_e-\beta_er_ed_e}{2-\gamma_e}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equation (3.4) is exact on every finite graph in the stated setup.

# 4. New theorem: three-terminal resistance identity

Let

\[
\mathcal R=R_{\rm eff}(A,B)
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the effective resistance between the two absorbing terminals in the original unit network. For an interior vertex `v`, let

\[
R_{vA}=R_{\rm eff}(v,A),
\qquad
R_{vB}=R_{\rm eff}(v,B),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where in each pairwise resistance the remaining vertices, including the other terminal, retain their ordinary network role. Define the **three-terminal excess**

\[
\boxed{
\xi(v)=\frac{R_{vA}+R_{vB}-\mathcal R}{2}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since effective resistance is a metric,

\[
\xi(v)\ge0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — exact diagonal-Green decomposition (PROVED)

For every interior vertex `v`,

\[
\boxed{
G(v,v)=\xi(v)+\mathcal R\,h(v)(1-h(v)).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
R_{vA}=G(v,v)+\mathcal R h(v)^2,
\tag{4.6}
\]

\[
R_{vB}=G(v,v)+\mathcal R(1-h(v))^2.
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Proof

Inject one unit of current at `v` and ground both terminals `A,B`. The resulting voltage is the Green potential `G(v,\cdot)`, and its energy is

\[
G(v,v).
\]

By reciprocity/Green's identity, the fraction of this unit current absorbed at `B` is exactly `h(v)`, while the fraction absorbed at `A` is `1-h(v)`.

Let `J` be the unit current flow from `B` to `A`; its energy is `mathcal R`. The Green flow from `v` to the grounded pair has equal potential `0` at both `A` and `B`. Therefore its energy inner product with `J` vanishes: by summation by parts the cross term is the Green boundary-potential difference between `B` and `A`, which is `0`.

To obtain a unit flow from `v` to `A` alone, add `h(v)J` to the grounded-pair flow. The `B` sink is cancelled and the `A` sink becomes one unit. Orthogonality gives energy

\[
G(v,v)+h(v)^2\mathcal R,
\]

which by Thomson's principle equals `R_{vA}`. This proves (4.6). Reversing the roles of `A,B` proves (4.7).

Subtracting (4.6) and (4.7) yields the standard voltage-resistance formula

\[
h(v)=\frac{\mathcal R+R_{vA}-R_{vB}}{2\mathcal R}.
\]

Adding (4.6) and (4.7), subtracting `mathcal R`, and dividing by two gives (4.5). QED.

### Interpretation

The three-terminal network obtained by retaining only `A,B,v` has the same three pairwise resistance distances as a `Y` network. In that `Y` representation, `xi(v)` is exactly the branch resistance from `v` to the three-terminal branch point. Formula (4.5) says that the killed diagonal Green resistance is the sum of this geometric excess and the elementary two-terminal term `mathcal R h(1-h)`.

# 5. New exact edge identity

For an oriented interior edge `e=(u,v)`, write

\[
\Delta\xi_e=\xi(v)-\xi(u),
\qquad d_e=h(v)-h(u).
\]

Theorem 4.1 gives

\[
G(v,v)-G(u,u)
=
\Delta\xi_e
+\mathcal R\{h(v)(1-h(v))-h(u)(1-h(u))\}.
\]

Since

\[
h(v)(1-h(v))-h(u)(1-h(u))
=d_e\,[1-h(u)-h(v)],
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
-\beta_ed_e
=
\Delta\xi_e\,d_e
+\mathcal R[1-h(u)-h(v)]d_e^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is exact and orientation invariant.

The final term has an immediate sign interpretation:

- if `h(u)+h(v)<1`, it is positive;
- if `h(u)+h(v)=1`, it vanishes;
- if `h(u)+h(v)>1`, it is negative.

Thus the large resistance-scale part of the diagonal-Green correction is automatically favorable on the **inner half-voltage region** and unfavorable only in the outer half-voltage region.

# 6. New exact three-terminal decomposition of the first-order ORRW correction

Substitute (5.1) into (3.4).

## Theorem 6.1 — exact decomposition (PROVED)

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D={}&h(x)G(x,x)\\
&+\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\subset I}
\frac{\gamma_e d_ej_e}{2-\gamma_e}\\
&+\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\subset I}
\frac{r_e\Delta\xi_e d_e}{2-\gamma_e}\\
&+\mathcal R\sum_{e=\{u,v\}\subset I}
\frac{r_e[1-h(u)-h(v)]d_e^2}{2-\gamma_e}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every quantity in (6.1) belongs to the **ordinary simple random walk / electrical network** in the fixed finite domain. No ORRW transition law remains.

This is the strongest static first-order reduction obtained in the HARD10-02 track so far.

### Structural consequences

1. `h(x)G(x,x)>0` is the positive contribution created by excluding the already-old initial root edge.

2. The last term is quadratic in the harmonic gradient and contains the large two-terminal resistance `mathcal R`. Its inner-half contribution is nonnegative without any further hypothesis.

3. The only sign-indefinite pieces are:
   - the source-current cross term `gamma*d*j`;
   - the three-terminal-excess gradient term `r*Delta xi*d`;
   - the portion of the quadratic term lying above the mid-voltage level.

This is substantially more localized than the raw first-crossing formula.

# 9. New asymptotic mechanism suggested by the potential kernel

This subsection is a **rigorous reduction plus a clearly marked unproved analytic step**, not a completed theorem.

For the infinite square lattice killed at the origin, let `a(z)` be the standard two-dimensional SRW potential kernel normalized by `a(e_1)=1`. The combinatorial killed Green kernel satisfies

\[
G_{\{0\}}(u,v)
=\frac14\{a(u)+a(v)-a(u-v)\}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Consequently, for every interior nearest-neighbor edge `e={u,v}` away from the origin,

\[
\gamma_e=\frac12,
\tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
\beta_e=\frac12\{a(u)-a(v)\}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus the diagonal-Green term is exactly aligned with the potential-kernel gradient in infinite volume. In particular, if `d_a=a(v)-a(u)`, then

\[
-\beta_e d_a=\frac12d_a^2\ge0.
\tag{9.4}
\]

This is the infinite-volume analogue of the favorable inner-half term in (5.1).

Standard potential-kernel asymptotics give, in a fixed cone and at radius `r`,

\[
|\nabla a|\asymp r^{-1}.
\]

Moreover, starting from `e_1` and killing at `0`, the probability of ever reaching a specified vertex `u` at radius `r` is of order `1/log r`: indeed

\[
P_{e_1}(\tau_u<\tau_0)
=\frac{G_{\{0\}}(e_1,u)}{G_{\{0\}}(u,u)},
\]

with numerator bounded away from zero in the far field and denominator of order `log r`. Therefore the probability `r_e` of ever crossing a specified nearby edge is also bounded below by a constant multiple of `1/log r` after choosing a fixed one-step crossing event.

On a shell of radius `r`, there are order `r` edges with `|d_a|asymp1/r`. Hence the positive squared-gradient/first-crossing mass has the formal shell scale

\[
r\cdot \frac1{r^2}\cdot\frac1{\log r}
\asymp\frac1{r\log r},
\]

whose sum diverges like

\[
\log\log R.
\tag{9.5}
\]

After restoring the finite-annulus normalization `h_R roughly a/log R`, this suggests a favorable first-order contribution of order

\[
\frac{\log\log R}{\log R}.
\tag{9.6}
\]

This mechanism is consistent with the observed nonuniformity of the derivative in `R` and explains why a naive fixed-radius Taylor expansion cannot be used uniformly in the annulus size.

### Exact missing analytic lemma

To turn (9.5)-(9.6) into a theorem one must prove, uniformly in a growing inner region of the square annulus, that:

1. the finite-domain diagonal-Green difference is approximated by the killed-origin potential-kernel difference with an error whose total contribution is `O(1/log R)`;
2. the weighted source-current term in (6.1) is `O(1/log R)` (or otherwise smaller than the positive `log log R/log R` term);
3. the `xi`-gradient error is likewise `O(1/log R)` after summation;
4. the outer-half negative quadratic contribution is only `O(1/log R)`.

The potential-kernel expansion and second-difference estimates make these statements plausible, but they are not proved here. They are now the precise analytic obligation for a first-order square-lattice theorem.
