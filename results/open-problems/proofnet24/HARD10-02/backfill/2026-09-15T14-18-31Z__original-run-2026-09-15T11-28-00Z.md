## 1. Fixed-harmonic fresh-edge martingale and annular reduction

For disjoint absorbing sets `A,B` and unit-conductance SRW equilibrium voltage

\[
h(x)=P_x^{SRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A),
\]

if `I_k` indicates that edge `{X_k,X_{k+1}}` is fresh immediately before time `k`, then

\[
M_n^h=h(X_n)+(c-1)\sum_{k<n}I_k(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k))
\]

is a martingale up to absorption. Therefore

\[
P_x^{ORRW}(\tau_B<\tau_A)
=h(x)-(c-1)E_x^{ORRW}S_\tau(h),
\]

where

\[
S_\tau(h)=\sum_{k<\tau}I_k(h(X_{k+1})-h(X_k)).
\]

For square annuli, recurrence follows from

\[
E S_\tau(h_R)\ge -o(1/\log R).
\]

## 2. Stage Green identity

Fix the current traversed trace `T` and current active vertex `x`. Until the next fresh edge is crossed, old edges have conductance `c`, each fresh edge has conductance `1` and terminates the stage, and hitting the root terminates the stage.

Build a stage network by replacing each directed fresh edge `e=(v,w)` with its own terminal edge of conductance `1`; old edges into the root are grounded terminal edges of conductance `c`. Let `V=V_{T,x}` be the unit-current voltage with one unit injected at `x` and all stage terminals grounded. Then for a fresh directed edge `e=(v,w)`,

\[
p_T(e|x)=V(v)
\]

is exactly the probability that `e` is the next fresh traversal before root absorption.

Define

\[
D_T(x;h)=\sum_{e=(v,w)\in\partial_fT}V(v)[h(w)-h(v)].
\]

If `h` is unit-conductance harmonic at each active trace vertex, then

\[
\boxed{
D_T(x;h)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}(V(u)-V(v))(h(u)-h(v)).
}
\]

Proof: at each active vertex `v`, harmonicity gives

\[
\sum_{(v,w)\in\partial_fT}(h(w)-h(v))
=-\sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in T}(h(u)-h(v)).
\]

Multiply by `V(v)`, sum over vertices, and perform discrete summation by parts over old edges.

## 3. One-stage capacity bound

Let `R_T(x)=V(x)` be effective resistance from `x` to the stage terminal set. Unit-current energy gives

\[
c\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}(V(u)-V(v))^2\le R_T(x).
\]

With

\[
\mathcal E_T(h)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}(h(u)-h(v))^2,
\]

Cauchy-Schwarz yields

\[
\boxed{|D_T(x;h)|\le\sqrt{\frac{R_T(x)}c\,\mathcal E_T(h)}.}
\]

This is exact but not summable over many stages without a telescope/capacity accounting mechanism.

## 4. Stage terminal drift identity

Let `F_T(x)` be the expected `h`-value at the end of the stage (root hit or first fresh crossing). Applying the fresh-edge martingale only until stage termination gives

\[
h(x)=F_T(x)+(c-1)D_T(x;h),
\]

so

\[
F_T(x)-h(x)=-(c-1)D_T(x;h).
\]

Thus a stagewise supermartingale theorem would be equivalent to `D_T>=0`. The original run then found an exact counterexample.

## 5. Exact reachable stage-start obstruction

Consider vertices `0,1,2,3,4,5`, root `0`, target `5`, and edges

\[
(0,2),(2,3),(3,4),(0,4),(0,1),(4,5),(2,4),(0,3).
\]

The SRW equilibrium voltage is

\[
h(0)=h(1)=0,\quad h(2)=h(3)=1/6,\quad h(4)=1/3,\quad h(5)=1.
\]

Take old trace

\[
T=\{(0,4),(3,4),(2,3)\}
\]

and current vertex `x=2`. This is a legitimate stage start, reachable through `0->4->3->2`. Symbolic calculation for arbitrary `c>1` gives

\[
D_T(2;h)=\frac{c(c-2)}{6(c^3+10c^2+14c+4)}.
\]

Therefore `D_T(2;h)<0` for `1<c<2`. Equivalently,

\[
F_T(2)-h(2)
=-\frac{c(c-2)(c-1)}{6(c^3+10c^2+14c+4)}>0
\]

for `1<c<2`. So even after averaging all old-edge movement until the next discovery, the fixed SRW harmonic voltage can drift outward.

This ruled out a universal whole-stage inward-drift theorem.

## 6. Cumulative stage identity

Let `sigma_j` be successive discovery-stage starts, `T_j` the old trace at stage `j`, and `V_j` the corresponding unit-current stage voltage. Then by the tower property,

\[
E S_\tau(h)
=E\sum_jD_{T_j}(X_{\sigma_j};h)
=E\sum_j\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T_j}(\nabla V_j)_{uv}(\nabla h)_{uv}.
\]

Thus planar recurrence by this route reduces to the annular cumulative estimate

\[
E\sum_j\langle\nabla V_j,\nabla h_R\rangle_{T_j}
\ge -o(1/\log R).
\]

The finite counterexample shows that the required mechanism is inter-stage cancellation/telescoping or specifically planar geometry, not pointwise sign.

## 7. Rank-one short-circuit barycenter invariant

For a finite Dirichlet network and a fresh interior edge `e={x,y}` whose conductance increases from `1` to `1+a`, let `h` be the old equilibrium voltage. Let `G=L^{-1}` be the Dirichlet Green operator and

\[
z=G(\delta_x-\delta_y),
\]

with

\[
\alpha_x=z(x),\qquad \alpha_y=-z(y),\qquad R=\alpha_x+\alpha_y.
\]

If `d=h(x)-h(y)`, the Sherman-Morrison update gives

\[
h'=h-\frac{a d}{1+aR}z,
\]

and the updated dipole is

\[
z'=\frac{z}{1+aR},\qquad R'=\frac{R}{1+aR}.
\]

Therefore the ratios

\[
\lambda_x=\alpha_x/R,\qquad\lambda_y=\alpha_y/R
\]

are invariant under reinforcing `e`. The weighted barycenter

\[
B_e=\lambda_yh(x)+\lambda_xh(y)
\]

satisfies

\[
\lambda_yh'(x)+\lambda_xh'(y)=B_e,
\]

and

\[
h'(x)-B_e=\frac{\lambda_xd}{1+aR},\qquad
h'(y)-B_e=-\frac{\lambda_yd}{1+aR}.
\]

Thus reinforcement contracts endpoint voltages toward the exact short-circuit barycenter by factor `1/(1+aR)`.
