## 1. Stage Green identity retained

For a current old trace `T` and stage start `x`, build the stage network with old edges conductance `c`, every fresh directed edge represented as a conductance-one terminal edge, and root absorption grounded. Let `V=V_{T,x}` be the unit-current stage voltage. For a fresh edge `f=(v,w)`, the probability it is the next discovery before root is `V(v)`.

For the fixed SRW equilibrium voltage `h`, define

\[
D_T(x;h)=\sum_{f=(v,w)\in\partial_fT}V(v)[h(w)-h(v)].
\]

Then harmonicity of `h` and summation by parts give

\[
\boxed{D_T(x;h)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}(V(u)-V(v))(h(u)-h(v)).}
\]

This is the expected harmonic correction contributed by the next discovery stage.

## 2. Boundary-flux transport theorem

For each active trace vertex define the signed fresh harmonic flux

\[
\mu_T(v)=\sum_{f=(v,w)\in\partial_fT}[h(w)-h(v)].
\]

By unit-conductance harmonicity,

\[
\mu_T(v)=\sum_{u:\{u,v\}\in T}[h(v)-h(u)].
\]

Thus `mu_T` is exactly the divergence of the old gradient flow of `h` restricted to the trace. Let

\[
\mu_T^+(v)=\max(\mu_T(v),0),\qquad
\mu_T^-(v)=\max(-\mu_T(v),0).
\]

Let `W_T(h)` be the minimum intrinsic graph-distance transport cost needed to route all negative mass `mu_T^-` to a submeasure of `mu_T^+`; surplus positive mass may flow to the root for free.

### Theorem — PROVED

\[
\boxed{D_T(x;h)\ge -\frac1cW_T(h).}
\]

Proof: the stage unit-current potential satisfies the intrinsic Lipschitz bound

\[
|V(y)-V(z)|\le d_T(y,z)/c,
\]

because each old edge has conductance `c` and the absolute current on any edge is at most one. Pair negative and positive divergence by an optimal transport plan; unmatched positive surplus contributes nonnegatively because `V>=0`. This gives the lower bound.

The old `h`-gradient flow itself provides a feasible transport from positive divergence to negative divergence/root, so

\[
W_T(h)
\le\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}|h(u)-h(v)|,
\]

and therefore

\[
D_T(x;h)
\ge-rac1c\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}|h(u)-h(v)|.
\]

This makes the finite-width cylinder mechanism geometric: negative stage drift requires transporting negative fresh harmonic flux across the visited trace. In finite width the transport distances can be controlled; in `Z^2` the missing theorem is a multiscale cumulative transport estimate.

## 3. Current-weighted and energy forms

Writing the stage unit current on an old edge as

\[
i_T(u,v)=c(V(u)-V(v)),
\]

one has

\[
D_T(x;h)=\frac1c\sum_{\{u,v\}\in T}i_T(u,v)[h(u)-h(v)].
\]

Hence

\[
D_T(x;h)
\ge-\frac1c\sum_{e\in T}|i_T(e)|\,|\nabla h(e)|.
\]

If `R_T(x)` is the stage effective resistance, then

\[
\sum_{e\in T}\frac{i_T(e)^2}{c}\le R_T(x),
\]

so

\[
|D_T(x;h)|
\le\sqrt{\frac{R_T(x)}c\sum_{e\in T}(\nabla h(e))^2}.
\]

Neither inequality is directly summable over all discovery stages without a telescope.

## 4. Whole-stage outward-drift counterexample retained

On vertices `0,1,2,3,4,5` with root `0`, target `5`, edges

\[
(0,2),(2,3),(3,4),(0,4),(0,1),(4,5),(2,4),(0,3),
\]

SRW voltage is `h(2)=h(3)=1/6`, `h(4)=1/3`, root-side zero, target one. With old trace

\[
T=\{(0,4),(3,4),(2,3)\}
\]

and stage start `x=2` (reachable via `0->4->3->2`), symbolic calculation gives

\[
D_T(2;h)=\frac{c(c-2)}{6(c^3+10c^2+14c+4)}.
\]

Thus for `1<c<2`, the whole next-discovery stage has negative `D_T`, and its expected terminal harmonic value exceeds its start value. This ruled out a universal per-stage sign theorem.

## 5. Coarea representation of the first-traversal correction

For `t in (0,1)`, define the harmonic sublevel/superlevel cut

\[
L_t=\{v:h(v)<t\},\qquad H_t=\{v:h(v)\ge t\}.
\]

For a sample path let `J_t` equal

- number of fresh first traversals crossing from `L_t` to `H_t`, minus
- number crossing from `H_t` to `L_t`,

before absorption. The initially reinforced root-neighbor edge is excluded from the fresh count.

For every directed fresh first traversal `u->v`,

\[
h(v)-h(u)
=\int_0^1[1_{\{h(u)<t\le h(v)\}}-1_{\{h(v)<t\le h(u)\}}]dt.
\]

Summing gives the exact sample-path coarea identity

\[
\boxed{S_\tau(h)=\int_0^1J_t\,dt.}
\]

Thus the correction is the integrated net first-crossing flux through harmonic equipotential cuts.

## 6. Cutwise positivity is false

Take the four-vertex graph with edges

\[
(0,1),(0,2),(1,2),(1,3),
\]

root `0`, target `3`, start at `1` immediately after the initial edge `(0,1)` is already old. For SRW,

\[
h(0)=0,\qquad h(2)=1/5,\qquad h(1)=2/5,\qquad h(3)=1.
\]

For any threshold `0<t<1/5`, the only still-fresh cut edge at the root side is `(0,2)`. A fresh `0->2` crossing cannot occur before root absorption, while the probability that the first crossing of `(0,2)` is `2->0` is exactly `1/5`: if `q_i` is the probability, starting from `i`, of absorption through `2->0`, then

\[
q_1=q_2/3,\qquad q_2=1/2+q_1/2,
\]

so `q_1=1/5`. Therefore

\[
E[J_t]=-1/5\qquad(0<t<1/5).
\]

Yet exact dynamic programming gives the integrated first-order correction

\[
E[S_\tau(h)]=2/15>0.
\]

So any coarea proof requires cancellation between harmonic levels; expected first-crossing flux is not nonnegative cut-by-cut.

## 7. Cumulative stage transport reduction

Let `sigma_j` be successive stage starts and `T_j` the old trace at stage `j`. By tower property,

\[
E S_\tau(h)=E\sum_jD_{T_j}(X_{\sigma_j};h).
\]

The transport theorem yields

\[
E S_\tau(h)
\ge-\frac1cE\sum_jW_{T_j}(h).
\]

This is not yet enough, but provides a concrete planar target: control the expected cumulative negative-flux transport cost over a whole excursion, ideally by a telescoping trace functional whose total scale is `o(1/log R)`.
