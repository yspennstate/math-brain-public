## 3. New theorem P13-MAIN-03 — bounded fourth moment with summable deviations

Let `xi_m` be a possibly varying family of real laws. Assume uniformly in `m`

\[
\mathbb E\xi_m=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E\xi_m^2=1,
\qquad
\|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,
\qquad
\mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B,
\tag{3.1}
\]

for fixed finite `K,B`. Let `A_m in R^{N_m x m}` have iid entries with law `xi_m`, assume

\[
\frac{N_m}{m}\to\gamma>1,
\qquad
k_m=r_m+1,
\qquad
k_m\log m=o(m).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\xrightarrow{P}-h(\gamma).
}
\tag{P13-MAIN-03}
\]

More quantitatively, for every fixed `epsilon>0` and every fixed `A>0`, there exist constants

\[
C=C(K,B,\gamma,\epsilon,A),
\qquad
c=c(K,B,\gamma,\epsilon,A)>0
\]

such that, for all sufficiently large `m`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left\{
\left|\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)+h(\gamma)\right|>\epsilon
\right\}
\le
C m^{-A}
+
C k_m e^{-c m/k_m}
+
C e^{-c m}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The constants are independent of `m`; no uniformity in `gamma` approaching one is claimed.

Since `k_m log m=o(m)`, the middle term in (3.3) is summable: `m/k_m >> log m`, so eventually `k_m exp(-c m/k_m) <= m^{-3}`. Choosing `A>2`, the whole right-hand side is summable. Therefore, on **any common probability space carrying matrices with the prescribed level-wise marginals**, not necessarily an independent or canonical coupling,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\longrightarrow-h(\gamma)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
}
\tag{P13-AS-02}
\]

This is stronger than the previous P13 fixed-law/Bai--Yin almost-sure statement. It comes from summable clean-row and staged-selection errors, so cross-level independence is unnecessary.

The conclusion does **not** include the public paper's exact `C exp(-c sqrt(m))` deviation rate. The polynomial term in (3.3) comes from the bounded-entry operator-norm theorem after cleaning.

## 4. Proof — whole-row cleaning avoids exponentially-small perturbation problems

### 4.1 Clean threshold and fourth-moment tail arithmetic

Fix a sufficiently small absolute `a>0` and set

\[
\tau_m=a\sqrt{\frac{m}{\log m}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write

\[
p_m=\Pr\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\},
\qquad
\eta_m=\mathbb E[\xi_m^2;|\xi_m|>\tau_m].
\]

The uniform fourth moment gives explicit bounds

\[
p_m\le \frac{B}{\tau_m^4}
=\frac{B\log^2m}{a^4m^2},
\tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
\eta_m\le \frac{B}{\tau_m^2}
=\frac{B\log m}{a^2m}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus

\[
mp_m=O\left(\frac{\log^2m}{m}\right)\to0,
\qquad
\eta_m=O\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right)\to0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Call a row clean if every coordinate has magnitude at most `tau_m`. Its dirty probability satisfies

\[
q_m=1-(1-p_m)^m\le mp_m
=O\left(\frac{\log^2m}{m}\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Among `N_m=Theta(m)` rows, the expected number of dirty rows is only `O(log^2 m)`.

Choose fixed `beta,delta>0` so that

\[
1<\beta<\beta+\delta<\gamma,
\qquad
h(\beta)>h(\gamma)-\epsilon.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The `beta m` clean rows will be the base block and the `delta m` additional clean rows will be reserved for the staged batches. Let `D_m` be the number of dirty rows. A binomial Chernoff bound gives

\[
\Pr\{N_m-D_m<(\beta+\delta)m\}
\le
\exp[-c m\log m]
\tag{4.7}
\]

for all large `m`. Thus with summably high probability there are enough clean rows for a base block and independent reserved batches.

Crucially, this is **not** an entrywise truncation plus Weyl comparison. The proof selects a submatrix made of rows on which the original matrix is unchanged. The final least singular value is always the least singular value of an actual row submatrix of `A_m`.

### 4.2 Rejection-sampling lemma

Let `R_1,R_2,...` be iid rows and let `C_i` be the event that row `i` is clean. Conditional on the clean/dirty indicator sequence, the values of the clean rows are independent with the conditional row law. Hence, on the event that there are at least `n_0` clean rows, the first `n_0` accepted rows are iid with coordinate law

\[
\zeta_m\stackrel d=\xi_m\mid\{|\xi_m|\le\tau_m\}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

This preserves exactly the row independence needed by the source staged proof, including independence between the base rows and reserved batches.

### 4.3 Truncation bias and variance

Put

\[
\mu_m=\mathbb E[\xi_m\mathbf 1_{|\xi_m|\le\tau_m}]
=-\mathbb E[\xi_m\mathbf 1_{|\xi_m|>\tau_m}].
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz and (4.2)--(4.3) give

\[
|\mu_m|^2\le p_m\eta_m,
\]

so

\[
\sqrt m|\mu_m|
\le
\frac{B}{a^3}\frac{(\log m)^{3/2}}{m}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The conditional mean

\[
a_m=\mathbb E\zeta_m=\frac{\mu_m}{1-p_m}
\]

therefore satisfies `sqrt(m)|a_m| ->0`. Its variance obeys

\[
\sigma_m^2
=\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_m)
=\frac{1-\eta_m}{1-p_m}-a_m^2
=1+O\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right).
\tag{4.10}
\]

The conditional density is bounded by `K/(1-p_m)<=2K`. After centering and normalizing by `sigma_m`, the density bound stays uniformly bounded.

This explicitly separates **truncation bias** from the final singular-value scale. We never perturb a least singular value by `||A-A_trunc||`; we only need the bias to be small enough that clean-row linear forms retain a density floor near zero.

### 4.4 Operator norm with summable failure probability

Center the clean coordinate law. Eventually its centered entries have magnitude at most `2 tau_m` and variance at most `2`. From the accepted clean-row sequence, designate the first `floor(beta m)` rows as the **base block** and keep the next `floor(delta m)` rows for the staged batches. These two blocks are independent under the clean conditional law. Pad the base block to a square matrix of size `n_* = Theta(m)` with independent entries of the same centered law. Choose `a` small enough that, after the harmless variance rescaling, Rebrova's Lemma 4.2 applies:

an iid mean-zero square matrix with variance at most one and entries bounded by a sufficiently small constant times `sqrt(n_*/log n_*)` has operator norm `O_A(sqrt(n_*))` with failure at most `n_*^{-A}`.

The nonzero clean mean contributes a rank-one matrix of norm

\[
|a_m|\sqrt{\lfloor\beta m\rfloor m}=o(\sqrt m)
\tag{4.11}
\]

by (4.9). Therefore, for every fixed `A`, there is a fixed `M_A` such that

\[
\Pr\{\|A_m^{base,clean}\|>M_A\sqrt m\}
\le C_A m^{-A}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

This is stronger for the present purpose than the previous Latała-plus-Markov argument, which gave only tightness. The norm event is **base-measurable only**; it does not involve the reserved batches, so after the base is fixed the staged rows remain fresh and independent. It is also why P13-AS-02 no longer needs Bai--Yin or a fixed entry law.

### 4.5 Incompressibility and the local density floor

On the base-block event (4.12), the standard bounded-density compressible-net proof gives fixed constants

\[
\delta=\delta(K,M_A)>0,
\quad
\rho=\rho(K,M_A)>0,
\quad
c=c(K,M_A)>0
\]

such that the normal to a fixed `(m-1) x m` clean-row matrix is compressible with probability at most `e^{-cm}` inside the bounded-norm event. Averaging over the exponentially many hyperplanes and applying Markov shows that a positive fraction of the candidate hyperplanes have `(delta,rho)`-incompressible normals, except with probability `e^{-cm}`.

For any such incompressible unit vector `u`, remove coordinates with

\[
|u_j|>1/\sqrt{\delta m}.
\]

The remainder has fixed `l2` mass at least `rho/2`; after normalization its coefficients are `O(m^{-1/2})`. For the centered, normalized clean coordinate law the third absolute moment can be as large as `C tau_m`, but Rosenthal on a fixed number of variance-balanced coefficient blocks gives block third moments bounded by

\[
C_R\left(1+C\frac{\tau_m}{\sqrt m}\right)
=
C_R(1+O((\log m)^{-1/2})).
\tag{4.13}
\]

The block densities are uniformly bounded by the product-measure projection-density theorem. Bobkov--Götze Theorem 1.1 therefore gives a density-level Gaussian approximation for the sum of the independent blocks. Choose the fixed number of blocks large enough that the error is below half the Gaussian density on a sufficiently large fixed interval. Convolving back the omitted coordinates and using Chebyshev yields, uniformly over all useful normals,

\[
\boxed{
ct\le
\Pr\{|\langle X^{clean},u\rangle|\le t\}
\le Ct,
\qquad 0<t\le1.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

The shift caused by the clean-row mean is bounded by

\[
|a_m\sum_j u_j|\le\sqrt m|a_m|=o(1),
\]

so it does not move the origin outside the fixed density-floor interval.

This is a **density** local CLT. A CDF Berry--Esseen bound would be useless at the exponentially small slab widths of the staged proof.

### 4.6 Nearly parallel pairs

Let two `(m-1)`-row sets differ in `q>=delta_0m` rows. Condition on the first set, so its normal `u` is fixed and the `q` fresh rows are independent. On the same **base-block** norm event, if the second normal is within projective distance `a_0` of `u`, then

\[
\|B_Ju\|_2\le M_Aa_0\sqrt m.
\]

Hence at least `q/2` fresh scalar projections lie in an interval of width `O(M_Aa_0)`. The bounded one-dimensional density makes this event at most

\[
2^q(CKM_Aa_0)^{q/2}.
\]

Choosing fixed `a_0` sufficiently small makes the bound `e^{-Cm}` with any prescribed fixed exponent. High-overlap pairs are handled by the manuscript's deterministic entropy count.

The fresh-row argument is valid because we do **not** condition on the global norm event before invoking independence. The norm event is used only as a deterministic implication inside the intersection being bounded.

### 4.7 Exact staged normalization and upper tail

Use exactly the manuscript normalization. Put

\[
\lambda=h(\gamma)-\epsilon,
\qquad
t=e^{-\lambda m/k},
\qquad
\boxed{s=t/\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Choose the clean-row aspect ratio and reserve fraction so the base aspect `beta>1` satisfies `h(beta)>lambda`. Split the reserved rows into `k` batches of size `b asymp m/k`. The one-stage second-moment lemma gives a fixed success probability `p>0` at every stage. The surviving-family and exceptional-edge recursions are

\[
M_j\ge\theta sM_{j-1},
\qquad
e_j\le As e_{j-1}.
\tag{4.16}
\]

The two entropy checks are

\[
\log(s^kL)
\ge
(h(\beta)-\lambda)m-\frac{k}{2}\log k-O(k)+o(m)
\to+\infty,
\tag{4.17}
\]

and

\[
\log\frac{e_j}{sM_j^2}
\le
-4\kappa m+\frac{k}{2}\log k+O(k)
\to-\infty.
\tag{4.18}
\]

Both use `k log m=o(m)`. Each stage fails with conditional probability at most `exp(-c m/k)`, so all stages succeed except with probability

\[
k e^{-c m/k}.
\tag{4.19}
\]

When they succeed, the selected `k` rows and one surviving base normal satisfy

\[
\sum_{j=1}^k|\langle X_j,u\rangle|^2
\le ks^2=t^2.
\]

Thus an actual `(m+k-1) x m` row submatrix `B` of the original matrix obeys

\[
\sigma_m(B)\le\|Bu\|\le t,
\]

which proves the required upper exponential bound.

### 4.8 Lower tail

The manuscript lower estimate uses no tail hypothesis. For one fixed `(m+k-1) x m` row submatrix, the negative-second-moment identity and the `k`-dimensional bounded-density projection theorem give

\[
\Pr\{\sigma_m(B)\le t\}
\le
\exp(O(k\log m))t^k.
\tag{4.20}
\]

There are

\[
\binom{N_m}{m+k-1}
=
\exp(h(\gamma)m+o(m))
\]

choices. At

\[
t_-=\exp(-(h(\gamma)+\epsilon)m/k)
\]

the union bound is `exp(-epsilon m+o(m))`, hence exponentially summable.

Combining Sections 4.1--4.8 gives (3.3), P13-MAIN-03, and P13-AS-02.

## 5. Stronger modular tail statement inherited from P12

The fourth moment is sufficient but not logically intrinsic. P12 identifies a whole-row cleaning package at the same threshold (4.1):

\[
m\Pr(|\xi_m|>\tau_m)\to0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\xi_m^2;|\xi_m|>\tau_m]\to0.
\tag{P12-T}
\]

Together with centered variance one and a uniform bounded density, these conditions are enough for the same convergence-in-probability proof. Every fixed law with a finite `(2+delta)` moment satisfies them, and the sufficient fixed-law condition

\[
t^2\log(e+t)\Pr(|\xi|>t)\to0
\]

is weaker than every fixed `(2+delta)` moment.

Thus **bounded fourth moment is not minimal**. It is, however, a clean uniform hypothesis under which the theorem now has the summable deviation strengthening (3.3).

I do not promote arbitrary finite variance to PROVED. P12 constructed a bounded-density finite-variance sparse-spike law for which this particular `sqrt(m/log m)` row-cleaning rule deletes a positive fraction of rows along a subsequence. That is a method obstruction, not a counterexample to the exponent itself.

## 6. Finite fourth moment versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

For one fixed law, `E|xi|^4<infinity` supplies one fixed constant `B`, so P13-MAIN-03 applies.

For a triangular array, the statement

\[
\mathbb E|\xi_m|^4<\infty
\quad\text{for every }m
\]

without a common bound is **not** enough to invoke this theorem. An explicit smooth bounded-density family shows why.

Take `p_m=c/m` and put total mass `p_m/2` in each of two tiny intervals centered at `+L_m` and `-L_m`, with

\[
L_m^2\asymp \alpha/p_m\asymp m
\]

for some fixed `alpha in (0,1)`. Put the remaining mass in a symmetric bounded-density compactly supported component whose variance is adjusted so the total variance is exactly one. Choose the spike interval widths proportional to `p_m`, so the overall density has one fixed finite bound. Then every fourth moment is finite but

\[
\mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\asymp p_mL_m^4\asymp m\to\infty.
\]

At the clean threshold `tau_m=a sqrt(m/log m)`, the spikes lie beyond the threshold and

\[
m\Pr(|\xi_m|>\tau_m)\gtrsim c,
\]

so a row is dirty with probability bounded away from zero. If `c` is large enough, the completely clean rows can even have asymptotic aspect ratio below one. Therefore **pointwise finite fourth moments do not imply the uniform whole-row cleaning hypotheses**.

This example does not prove that the extremal singular-value exponent itself fails; it disproves the overstrong inference “finite fourth moment at each dimension is enough for the uniform triangular-array proof.” The precise verified triangular-array statement uses a uniform fourth moment, or more generally explicit tail conditions such as P12-T.

## 7. Expectation, high probability, almost sure — separated

The theorem concerns an exponentially small minimum over row submatrices; it is not an expectation theorem.

- **Convergence in probability:** P13-MAIN-03 follows from (3.3).
- **High probability:** (3.3) is a quantitative summable bound, but it is not the public subgaussian `C exp(-c sqrt(m))` rate.
- **Almost sure:** P13-AS-02 follows from the first Borel--Cantelli lemma under any chosen coupling because the right side of (3.3) is summable.
- **Expectation of the logarithm:** not claimed. Convergence in probability/almost surely of the normalized log does not by itself provide uniform integrability of the negative tail.

This last distinction matters: exponentially rare exceptionally ill-conditioned submatrices can affect log-moments without changing the probability limit.

## 9. Primary-source verification

The public Yang--Wen--Shmalo preprint `arXiv:2608.07410` states the centered bounded-density subgaussian `r=0` theorem, the `C exp(-c sqrt(m))` deviation rate, and the real phase-retrieval base `1/4`.

Bobkov--Götze, *Berry--Esseen bounds in the local limit theorems*, Lithuanian Math. J. 65 (2025), Theorem 1.1, treats independent centered summands in density norm in terms of Lyapunov moments and density maxima; identical distribution is not required. This matches the variance-balanced block use above.

Rebrova, *Constructive regularization of the random matrix norm*, Lemma 4.2, explicitly assumes iid mean-zero entries, variance at most one, and bounded magnitude `c sqrt(n/log n)`, and gives a polynomially high-probability `O(sqrt n)` norm bound. Symmetry is used elsewhere in that paper's main regularization theorem, but not in Lemma 4.2.

Latała's 2005 expected-norm theorem remains a valid alternative under uniform fourth moment:

\[
\mathbb E\|A\|
\le C\left(
\max_i\sqrt{\sum_j\mathbb E A_{ij}^2}
+
\max_j\sqrt{\sum_i\mathbb E A_{ij}^2}
+
(\sum_{ij}\mathbb E A_{ij}^4)^{1/4}
\right),
\]

which is `O_B(sqrt m)` at fixed aspect ratio. The clean-row/Rebrova route is preferred here because it yields summable norm failures.

Bai--Yin is no longer needed for P13-AS-02, though it remains a valid fixed-law route to the untrimmed operator-norm edge under finite fourth moment.

## 10. Failed routes and sharp obstructions

**Naive entry truncation + Weyl:** rejected. Polynomial-scale operator perturbations are enormous compared with `exp(-Theta(m/k))`; the successful proof deletes whole bad rows only for the upper-bound construction and never perturbs the candidate submatrix.

**Reuse Nguyen--Vu under fourth moment:** rejected. The cited normal-vector theorem is genuinely subgaussian. Fixed-parameter incompressibility on a bounded-norm event is enough and is proved separately.

**Ordinary Berry--Esseen CDF control:** rejected. The staged slabs are exponentially narrow; a fixed CDF approximation error overwhelms them. A density local CLT is required.

**Global Lindeberg replacement:** rejected. The observable is a nonsmooth minimum over exponentially many dependent submatrices; derivative losses are uncontrolled at the target scale.

**MP/Bai--Yin bulk control alone:** insufficient. An operator-norm edge does not supply the local density floor or pair geometry. Conversely, the clean-row proof needs no full MP law.

**Pointwise finite fourth moment in a triangular array:** rejected as a uniform hypothesis. The smoothed-spike family in Section 6 has finite fourth moment at every `m` but violates the uniform tail package.

**Arbitrary finite variance:** OPEN. P12 shows the current threshold-cleaning route can delete a positive fraction of rows for some bounded-density finite-variance laws. A norm-free no-gaps/incompressibility theorem or a more adaptive regularization is needed.

## 12. Next attack

The highest-value next attack is no longer to re-prove the fourth-moment theorem. It is to test the **finite-variance frontier** with a norm-free normal-vector lemma:

> For an `(m-1) x m` iid matrix with centered variance-one bounded-density entries, prove that a fixed positive fraction of the exponentially many row-hyperplane normals have fixed `(delta,rho)` incompressibility, without conditioning on a global `O(sqrt m)` operator norm.

A successful theorem of this form would remove the only structural reason for the row-cleaning tail condition. Random rounding, regularized Hilbert--Schmidt methods, or a leave-one-row-out geometric argument are the most plausible routes. Any such argument must preserve whole rows; entrywise perturbation is unusable at the exponentially small final singular-value scale.

In parallel, the manuscript should be rewritten now with the bounded-fourth theorem stated cleanly, the stronger P12 tail package isolated as an optional extension, and no claim of the original subgaussian deviation rate.
