## Strongest result

I recovered the exact August 30 withdrawal request and the latest attached redundancy manuscript, then replaced the three genuinely subgaussian inputs in its proof by bounded-fourth-moment inputs.

**Candidate theorem.** Let \(\xi_m\) be centered real entry laws (a fixed law is allowed) satisfying uniformly
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=1,\qquad \|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,\qquad \mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B<\infty.
\]
Let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with law \(\xi_m\), \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\), and
\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=\min_{|T|=m+r_m}\sigma_m((A_m)_T),\qquad k_m=r_m+1.
\]
If \(k_m\log m=o(m)\), then
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma),
\qquad h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]
For fixed \(r\), this becomes \((1/m)\log M_{m,r}\to -h(\gamma)/(r+1)\) in probability. For one fixed bounded-density finite-fourth-moment law, the same proof gives an almost-sure statement along each fixed admissible dimension/surplus sequence under the canonical infinite iid-array coupling. What is **not** retained from the submitted subgaussian paper is its explicit \(Ce^{-c\sqrt m}\) deviation rate or its compact-aspect-ratio uniform almost-sure formulation.

This is a candidate complete proof and still requires two independent worker audits before closure.

## Where subgaussianity entered the draft

The lower estimate (negative second moment plus bounded-density projection and a union bound) does not require subgaussian tails. The upper proof uses subgaussianity in exactly three places:

1. the local-density lower bound, only to control third moments of block sums;
2. the Nguyen–Vu sup-norm delocalization theorem for hyperplane normals;
3. an exponential operator-norm tail used in the nearly-parallel-pair lemma.

The staged selection argument itself only needs a one-slab lower bound \(\asymp t\), a two-slab upper bound \(O(t^2)\) away from parallel normals, and an exponentially sparse exceptional-pair graph.

## Replacement I — incompressibility is enough for local density

Fix compressibility parameters \(\delta,\rho\in(0,1)\). Let \(u\in S^{m-1}\) be incompressible. Put
\[
J=\{j:|u_j|>1/\sqrt{\delta m}\},\qquad D=J^c,
\]
so \(|J|<\delta m\). If \(v=\|u_D\|_2<\rho/2\), then normalizing \(u_J\) gives a \(\delta m\)-sparse unit vector within distance less than \(\rho\) of \(u\), contradiction. Hence \(v\ge\rho/2\). For \(a_j=u_j/v\) on \(D\),
\[
\sum_Da_j^2=1,\qquad \|a\|_\infty\le \frac{2}{\rho\sqrt{\delta m}}.
\]

Let \(W=\sum_Du_j\xi_j=v\sum_Da_j\xi_j\). Apply the manuscript's greedy blocking construction to the weights \(a_j^2\), using a large fixed number of blocks. Projection-density bounds give a uniform density bound for each normalized block. Rosenthal at \(p=3\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\Big|\sum_{j\in B}a_j\xi_j\Big|^3
\le C\left[(\sum_Ba_j^2)^{3/2}+\mathbb E|\xi|^3\sum_B|a_j|^3\right]
\le C_B(\sum_Ba_j^2)^{3/2}.
\]
Uniform fourth moment implies a uniform third moment. The density Berry–Esseen theorem of Bobkov–Götze (Lithuanian Math. J. 65 (2025), DOI 10.1007/s10986-025-09659-1) therefore makes the density of the normalized diffuse sum uniformly close to the Gaussian density after choosing the fixed block count large enough. Consequently \(f_W(y)\ge c_*>0\) on a fixed interval such as \([-3,3]\), uniformly over incompressible \(u\) and large \(m\).

Now write \(L=\sum_{j\in J}u_j\xi_j\). The variables \(L,W\) are independent and \(\operatorname{Var}(L)\le1\), so \(\Pr(|L|\le2)\ge3/4\). For \(|x|\le1\),
\[
f_{\langle X,u\rangle}(x)=\mathbb E f_W(x-L)\ge \tfrac34 c_*.
\]
The bounded-density projection theorem gives the matching global upper density bound. Hence, uniformly over incompressible \(u\),
\[
ct\le\Pr\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t\}\le Ct,\qquad 0<t\le1.
\]
This is exactly the one-row input required by the staged proof. No subgaussian tail is used.

## Replacement II — many hyperplane normals are incompressible

For an \((m-1)\times m\) iid matrix \(B\), on the event \(\|B\|\le M\sqrt m\), a direct compressible-net argument gives
\[
\Pr\{\ker B\cap\mathrm{Comp}(\delta,\rho)\ne\varnothing,\ \|B\|\le M\sqrt m\}\le e^{-cm}.
\]
For a fixed unit vector \(z\), each coordinate of \(Bz\) is a bounded-density linear form, so a sufficiently small fixed interval has probability strictly below one. If \(\|Bz\|\le c_0\sqrt m\), a positive fraction of rows must fall in that interval; row independence makes this exponentially unlikely. A net of sparse vectors costs \(\exp(O(\delta m\log(1/(\delta\rho))))\); take \(\rho\asymp c_0/M\) and then \(\delta\) small.

This is also exactly supported by Rudelson–Vershynin, *No-gaps delocalization for general random matrices*, GAFA 26 (2016), Proposition 9.4: on a boundedness event, kernels contain only incompressible vectors with exponentially high accuracy under much milder assumptions than subgaussianity.

For the exponentially many \((m-1)\)-subsets \(S\) of the base rows, let \(u_S\) be the normal. If \(E_M\) denotes the single global event that the whole base matrix has norm at most \(M\sqrt m\), then
\[
\mathbb E[1_{E_M}N_{\rm bad}]\le L e^{-cm},
\]
where \(L\) is the number of hyperplanes and \(N_{\rm bad}\) counts compressible normals. Markov yields
\[
\Pr\{E_M,\ N_{\rm bad}>L/2\}\le2e^{-cm}.
\]
Thus at least half the normals are good, with exponentially high conditional accuracy.

## Replacement III — fourth moment supplies the global norm event

Latała's theorem (Proc. AMS 133 (2005), DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-04-07800-1) states
\[
\mathbb E\|A\|\le C\left(\max_i\sqrt{\sum_j\mathbb E A_{ij}^2}+\max_j\sqrt{\sum_i\mathbb E A_{ij}^2}+(\sum_{ij}\mathbb E A_{ij}^4)^{1/4}\right).
\]
At fixed rectangular aspect ratio and uniform fourth moment, this is \(O(\sqrt m)\). Therefore for every outer error tolerance \(\eta>0\), choose a fixed \(M=M(B,\gamma,\eta)\) so that \(\Pr(E_M^c)\le\eta\) uniformly by Markov. This is sufficient for convergence in probability: all other constants may depend on that fixed \(M\), and \(\eta\) is sent to zero at the end.

For a single fixed law, Bai–Yin (Ann. Probab. 21 (1993), DOI 10.1214/aop/1176989118) gives almost-sure convergence of the upper sample-covariance edge under finite fourth moment, hence one fixed \(M\) eventually controls the base matrix almost surely.

## Replacement IV — nearly parallel pairs without exponential norm tails

Fix row sets \(S,T\) with \(k=|T\setminus S|\ge\delta_0m\), condition on \(S\), and write \(J=T\setminus S\). If \(d_{\rm pr}(u_S,u_T)<a\) and the global base event \(E_M\) holds, choose the sign of \(u_T\) so \(\|u_S-u_T\|<a\). Since \(B_Ju_T=0\),
\[
\|B_Ju_S\|\le\|B_J\|a\le Ma\sqrt m.
\]
With \(\tau_a=Ma\sqrt{2/\delta_0}\), at least \(k/2\) fresh rows must satisfy \(|\langle R_i,u_S\rangle|\le\tau_a\). Each such event has probability at most \(CK\tau_a\), so by row independence
\[
\Pr\{d_{\rm pr}(u_S,u_T)<a,\ E_M\mid S\}
\le2^k(CK\tau_a)^{k/2}.
\]
Choose \(a\) small enough to make this \(e^{-Am}\) for any required fixed \(A\). The high-overlap pair count remains deterministically entropy-small. Therefore the exceptional-pair graph needed by the staged proof remains exponentially sparse. The original subgaussian exponential operator-norm tail is unnecessary; one global norm event suffices.

## Completion of the moderate-surplus theorem

Insert the four replacements above into the manuscript's Proposition 5.2. The stage recursion is unchanged. With
\[
t_m=\exp(-(h(\gamma)-\varepsilon)m/k),\qquad s=\sqrt{t_m/k},
\]
each batch has a fixed positive conditional success probability. If its size is \(b\asymp m/k\),
\[
\Pr\{\text{some batch fails}\}\le k e^{-c m/k}\to0
\]
because \(k\log m=o(m)\). On a fixed global norm event, compressible-normal and bad-pair failures are exponentially small. Hence
\[
\Pr\{M_{m,r_m}>t_m\}\le\Pr(E_M^c)+o_M(1).
\]
For any \(\eta>0\), Latała lets us choose \(M\) with limsup \(\Pr(E_M^c)\le\eta\); then limsup of the upper-tail failure is at most \(\eta\), and \(\eta\downarrow0\).

The manuscript lower estimate already gives the matching \(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon\) bound under bounded density. Thus
\[
(k_m/m)\log M_{m,r_m}\to_{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
\]

For one fixed law, Bai–Yin makes the norm event eventually almost sure. The remaining failures are summable (the stage failure is superpolynomially small under \(k\log m=o(m)\)), so Borel–Cantelli gives the fixed-sequence almost-sure law.

## Phase retrieval

At the critical real threshold \(N_m=2m-1\), bounded density gives full spark almost surely. The existing deterministic Balan–Wang reduction then transfers the \(\gamma=2,r=0\) exponent to
\[
(1/m)\log\omega(A_m)\to_{\mathbb P}-\log4.
\]
For a fixed fourth-moment law the same holds almost surely under canonical coupling. The August 24 draft also states a redundant phase-retrieval consequence for \(r_m>0\), but I have not re-audited every deterministic redundancy-transfer line this run, so that extension remains CONDITIONAL.

## What is not justified by fourth moment alone

The subgaussian paper's explicit \(Ce^{-c\sqrt m}\) deviation rate is not recovered. Fourth moment gives operator-norm tightness, not a subgaussian norm tail. The honest rewrite should therefore state the qualitative convergence-in-probability theorem, plus the fixed-law fixed-sequence almost-sure corollary, unless an additional tail hypothesis is imposed.

The proof actually isolates a more modular sufficient condition: bounded density, uniformly bounded third absolute moment, and \(\|A_m\|/\sqrt m=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\) for the relevant base matrices. Uniform fourth moment is a clean sufficient condition for the latter two properties.

## Failed / rejected routes

- Directly citing Nguyen–Vu after changing “subgaussian” to “finite fourth moment” is invalid; the cited theorem is subgaussian.
- Naive entrywise truncation followed by Weyl perturbation is badly scaled: a polynomial perturbation is huge compared with the target \(e^{-\Theta(m/k)}\) singular value.
- A whole-functional Lindeberg replacement is unattractive because the minimum over exponentially many submatrices is nonsmooth at the exponentially small scale.
- Bai–Yin alone is not the right triangular-array tool; Latała gives the uniform fourth-moment norm tightness needed for convergence in probability.
