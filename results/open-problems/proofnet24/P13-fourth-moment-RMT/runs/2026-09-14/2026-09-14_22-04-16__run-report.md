# 3. Why bounded critical intensity is not the true cleaning-cost threshold

The previous P13 truncation scale was

\[
A\sqrt{\frac m{\log m}}.
\]

At that scale, variance alone gives only

\[
mP\{|\xi_m|>\tau\}
\lesssim
\log m,
\]

so cleaning a linear-size spread subset may cost a negative power of \(m\).

A fixed positive cleaning probability was convenient because the inherited one-stage Paley–Zygmund lemma then had a fixed success probability.

But a fixed per-row success probability is stronger than necessary.

The source has a linear number of reserved fresh rows and needs only \(k_m\) successful marked rows, where

\[
k_m=o(m/\log m).
\]

A stage success probability which is a sufficiently small negative power of \(\log m\) still yields vastly more than \(k_m\) successes among \(\Theta(m)\) fresh rows.

This suggests truncating higher.

Fix

\[
L_m=\frac1{16}\log\log m,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\tau_m^\sharp
=
\sqrt{\frac m{L_m}}
=
4\sqrt{\frac m{\log\log m}}.
}
\]

Variance one alone gives, by Chebyshev,

\[
p_m
:=
P\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m^\sharp\}
\le
\frac{L_m}{m}
=
\frac{\log\log m}{16m}.
\]

If \(I\) is any spread subset with

\[
|I|=q\le m,
\]

then, for large \(m\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(E_I)
&=
(1-p_m)^q\\
&\ge
\exp(-2qp_m)\\
&\ge
\exp(-2L_m)\\
&=
\boxed{
(\log m)^{-1/8}.
}
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus variance alone makes linear-size spread cleaning cost only a fixed power of \(\log m\).

The quantity that is not controlled by variance alone is the conditional mean after cleaning.

That is why `(BT2)` appears.

# 6. Why fixed equal batches should be replaced by a sequential scan

The source and previous P12 proof split the reserved rows into \(k\) equal batches because the one-row stage success probability was fixed.

With the new logarithmic loss, a fixed batch of size \(m/k\) is not the cleanest architecture.

Instead retain one linear reservoir of fresh rows and scan them sequentially.

After a failed row, do not alter the current candidate family.

After a successful row, update the candidate family using (5.2)–(5.4).

Every unused row remains iid and independent of the current state.

Before the \(k\)-th success, the conditional probability that the next fresh row is a stage success is at least

\[
p_m
=
c\ell_m^2
=
c(\log m)^{-1/4}.
\]

Let the reserved reservoir contain

\[
R_m\ge c_Rm
\]

rows.

The adapted success indicators have conditional success probabilities at least \(p_m\).

A standard exponential-supermartingale Chernoff argument gives stochastic lower-tail control by a binomial with mean of order

\[

\asymp
\frac m{(\log m)^{1/4}}.

\[
k_m\log m=o(m),

\[
\frac{k_m}{R_mp_m}

\frac1{(\log m)^{3/4}}

\[
