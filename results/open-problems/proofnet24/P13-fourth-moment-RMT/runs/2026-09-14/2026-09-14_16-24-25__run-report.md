## 4. Why P13-MAIN-04 works without a fourth moment

The proof deliberately avoids perturbing an exponentially small singular value.

### 4.1 Whole-row rejection

Call a coordinate clean if
\[
|\xi_m|\le\tau_m,
\]
and a row clean if all \(m\) coordinates are clean.

If
\[
p_m=P\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\},
\]
then the row rejection probability is at most
\[
mp_m=u_m\to0.
\]

Therefore one can select a linear-size clean base block and linear-size clean reserved blocks from untouched original rows.

This is rejection sampling, not entrywise truncation plus Weyl.

### 4.2 Truncation bias

Let
\[
\zeta_m
=
\xi_m\mid\{|\xi_m|\le\tau_m\},
\qquad
a_m=E\zeta_m.
\]

The prior report proves
\[
\sqrt m\,|a_m|\to0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the mean-rank-one matrix added by conditioning has norm
\[
|a_m|\sqrt{\Theta(m)\,m}
=
o(\sqrt m).
\]

### 4.3 Clean variance

A bounded density prevents the conditional clean law from collapsing.

If a real variable has density at most \(L\), then
\[
\operatorname{Var}Y\ge\frac1{32L^2}.
\]

Eventually the clean conditional density is at most \(2K\), hence
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_m)
\ge
\frac1{128K^2}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

No tail-second-moment assumption is needed.

### 4.4 Operator norm

After centering and a harmless variance rescaling, the clean entries satisfy:

- mean zero;
- variance \(\le1\);
- deterministic bound
  \[
  O\left(\sqrt{\frac m{\log m}}\right).
  \]

Rebrova's bounded-entry norm lemma then gives, for every fixed \(A>0\),
\[
\boxed{
P\{\|A_{\rm base,clean}\|>M_A\sqrt m\}
\le
C_A m^{-A}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is enough for the clean compressible-net argument.

### 4.5 Local density floor

The clean coordinate third moment can grow like
\[
\sqrt{m/\log m},
\]
but an incompressible coefficient vector averages it.

After retaining a fixed positive amount of \(\ell_2\)-mass on coordinates
\[
|u_j|\lesssim m^{-1/2},
\]
split that mass into a fixed number of variance-balanced blocks.

Each normalized block has uniformly bounded third moment because
\[
E|\widehat Y_m|^3\,\max_j|c_j|
=
O\left(
\sqrt{\frac m{\log m}}\frac1{\sqrt m}
\right)
=
O((\log m)^{-1/2}).
\]

The density-level local CLT therefore yields
\[
\boxed{
ct
\le
P\{
|\langle X^{\rm clean},u\rangle|\le t
\}
\le
Ct,
\qquad
0<t\le1,
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
uniformly over useful incompressible normals.

### 4.6 Global geometry under \((R)\)

The clean \(O(\sqrt m)\) norm event supports the standard compressible-net argument:

- a fixed clean hyperplane normal is compressible with probability \(e^{-cm}\);
- averaging over the exponential hyperplane family gives a positive fraction of useful diffuse normals;
- low-overlap nearly parallel pairs are exponentially sparse;
- high-overlap pairs are controlled deterministically by entropy.

Then the source staged marked-row amplification closes the moderate-surplus theorem.

# 6. Finite fourth moment versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

These are different statements and must not be conflated.

### One fixed law

For one fixed law,
\[
E|\xi|^4<\infty
\]
means there is one finite number
\[
B=E|\xi|^4.
\]
Hence (5.1) holds trivially for the constant triangular array.

So **finite fourth moment is sufficient for one fixed law**.

### A triangular array

Merely assuming
\[
E|\xi_m|^4<\infty
\quad\text{for each }m
\]
with no common bound is not enough to imply the clean-row condition \((R)\).

A smooth bounded-density example can put probability
\[
p_m\asymp m^{-1}
\]
in symmetric smooth bumps near
\[
\pm c\sqrt m
\]
and adjust the central component to keep mean zero and variance one.

Then every fourth moment is finite, but
\[
E|\xi_m|^4\asymp m,
\]
and
\[
mP\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\}\asymp1.
\]

This is a counterexample to the **assumption implication / current cleaning route**. It is not presently a proof that the extremal singular-value exponent itself fails for that triangular array.

\[

\]

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{m\ge1}\sup_{t\ge1}

\le

\tag{7.1}

Then at
\[

\[
\begin{aligned}
mP\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\}
&\le
B_\delta m\tau_m^{-(2+\delta)}
\\
&=
B_\delta a^{-(2+\delta)}
m^{-\delta/2}
(\log m)^{1+\delta/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
mP\{|\xi_m|>\tau_m\}\to0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Theorem P13-WEAK-Lp-01 — PROVED

Uniform bounded density, centered variance one, and the uniform weak-\(L^{2+\delta}\) tail (7.1) imply the full P13-MAIN-04 theorem and its summable deviations.

In particular:

- a uniform weak fourth-moment bound suffices even if
  \[
  E|\xi_m|^4=\infty;

# 10. New two-normal \(k\)-row slab estimate at finite variance

Let \(u,v\) be the two random residual normals for a low-overlap pair, and condition first on them.

For one fresh product row \(X\), the joint density of
\[
(\langle X,u\rangle,\langle X,v\rangle)
\]
is bounded by
\[
\frac{C K^2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}},
\qquad
\rho=u\cdot v.
\tag{10.1}
\]

For \(k\) independent marked rows \(X_1,\dots,X_k\), define
\[
\mathcal E_{u,v,k}(t)
=
\left\{
\sum_{j=1}^k|\langle X_j,u\rangle|^2\le t^2,
\quad
\sum_{j=1}^k|\langle X_j,v\rangle|^2\le t^2
\right\}.
\]

The joint vector in
\[
\mathbb R^{2k}
\]
has density at most
\[
(CK^2)^k
(1-\rho^2)^{-k/2}.
\]
The product of the two \(k\)-balls has volume
\[
v_k^2t^{2k}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
P\{\mathcal E_{u,v,k}(t)\mid u,v\}
\le
(CK^2)^k
v_k^2t^{2k}
(1-\rho^2)^{-k/2}.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Now suppose the overlap stratum has
\[
D\ge\delta m
\]
for one fixed \(\delta>0\), and
\[
k\log m=o(m).
\]
By P13-PAIR-HIGHMOM-FV-01,
\[
E[
(1-\rho^2)^{-k/2}
\mid A
]
\le
2C_\xi^{k/2}.
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem P13-TWOSLAB-k-FV-01 — PROVED

For every fixed low-overlap fraction \(\delta>0\), under one fixed centered variance-one bounded-density finite-variance coordinate law, whenever
\[
D\ge\delta m,
\qquad

\]
\[
\boxed{
P\{
\mathcal E_{u,v,k}(t)
\mid A
\}
\le
2(C_{\xi,K})^k
v_k^2t^{2k},
\qquad
0<t\le1.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

No:

- \(2+\delta\) moment;
- fourth moment;
- subgaussian norm;
- global \(O(\sqrt m)\) operator-norm event

is used in this two-normal singular-pair estimate.

This directly replaces the old “fixed angle \(a>0\)” pair estimate with an **integrated finite-variance estimate** at the exact growing \(k/2\) singular power.

# 11. What P13-TWOSLAB-k-FV-01 does and does not solve

This is a major narrowing of the finite-variance frontier, but it does not by itself prove the full fixed-law finite-variance Conjecture 8.2.

### What it solves

The low-overlap pair second-moment singularity is no longer a fundamental obstruction.

One no longer needs to prove, pair by pair,
\[
d_{\rm pr}(u,v)\ge a
\]
outside an exponentially sparse exceptional graph merely to control the joint \(k\)-row slab probability.

The random singular factor can be averaged directly.

### What remains

The source staged proof was designed to avoid an
\[
e^{O(k)}
\]
loss accumulated over \(k\) stages.

The new integrated pair bound has a factor
\[
C^k.
\]

At the final \(k\)-row level this is harmless on the normalized exponent scale because
\[
k=o(m/\log m).
\]
But a naive stage-by-stage Paley–Zygmund implementation can reintroduce an unwanted constant loss at every stage.

More importantly, the proof still needs enough **first-moment mass**: a positive fraction of the exponentially many base hyperplanes must have normals for which the local density is uniformly positive near zero.

P12's finite-variance local density theorem handles a normal once it is diffuse.

What remains is the exponential-family geometric statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{positive fraction of base hyperplane normals are sufficiently diffuse}
}
\tag{11.1}
\]
under a fixed heavy-tailed finite-variance law, without a global clean \(O(\sqrt m)\) matrix-norm event.

That is now the leading obstacle.

# 12. Revised finite-variance frontier

The previous P13 report listed two major geometric requirements:

1. positive-fraction diffuse normals;
