## Executive result

This run recovered the exact August 30 withdrawal request and the last attached manuscript before that request, then replaced the three genuinely subgaussian inputs in the manuscript by bounded-fourth-moment inputs.

**Candidate theorem obtained.** Let \(\xi_m\) be a (possibly triangular-array) family of centered real laws with

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_m)=1,\qquad \|f_{\xi_m}\|_\infty\le K,\qquad \mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B
\]

for fixed finite \(K,B\), and let \(A_m\in\mathbb R^{N_m\times m}\) have iid entries with law \(\xi_m\). Put

\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=\min_{|T|=m+r_m}\sigma_m((A_m)_T),\qquad k_m=r_m+1,
\]

assume \(N_m/m\to\gamma>1\), \(0\le r_m\le N_m-m\), and \(k_m\log m=o(m)\). Then

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma),
\qquad
h(\gamma)=\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1).
\]

For fixed \(r\), this is

\[
\frac1m\log M_{m,r}(A_m)\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-\frac{h(\gamma)}{r+1}.
\]

For one fixed entry law \(\xi\) with bounded density and finite fourth moment, the same proof can be upgraded along any fixed deterministic dimension/surplus sequence to an almost-sure statement under the canonical infinite-array coupling, using Bai--Yin for the base-matrix norm and Borel--Cantelli for the remaining exponentially/superpolynomially small errors. What is **not** retained under fourth moment alone is the submitted paper's explicit \(C e^{-c\sqrt m}\) deviation rate or its uniform-in-\(\gamma\) almost-sure compact-range statement.

The proof is not merely truncation of the subgaussian proof. The key improvement is to stop requiring \(\ell_\infty\)-delocalization of every useful null normal. **Incompressibility is enough.** A bounded-density local-CLT argument on the diffuse coordinates of an incompressible vector supplies the needed uniform lower small-ball estimate. This lets the Nguyen--Vu subgaussian normal-vector theorem be replaced by a compressible-net argument on a bounded-operator-norm event. The latter event follows from a fourth-moment operator-norm bound (Latała), or more sharply from Bai--Yin for a fixed law.

This is a candidate complete proof, not portfolio closure: two independent workers still need to audit it.

## 2. Where subgaussianity actually enters the attached proof

The lower estimate does **not** need subgaussian tails. Proposition 3.3 conditions on all columns except one, projects onto a \(k_m\)-dimensional orthogonal complement, uses the bounded-density projection theorem, and union-bounds over row subsets. Its assumptions are absolute continuity/bounded density plus the combinatorial condition \(k_m\log m=o(m)\).

The upper estimate has three subgaussian dependencies:

1. **Lemma 2.4 local lower density.** The manuscript blocks a delocalized linear form and invokes a density-level Berry--Esseen theorem. Subgaussianity enters only when bounding the block third moments.
2. **Theorem 4.1 normal-vector delocalization.** It cites a Nguyen--Vu \(\ell_\infty\) bound for the normal to an \((m-1)\times m\) iid subgaussian matrix.
3. **Lemma 4.4 operator-norm tail.** It uses a subgaussian exponential tail \(\Pr\{\|B\|>R\sqrt m\}\le e^{-Am}\). Lemma 4.5 uses that event to turn two nearly parallel normals into linearly many fresh-row small-ball constraints.

The staged selection in Section 5 itself only needs: (i) a positive one-slab probability \(\asymp s\) for good normals, (ii) a two-slab upper bound \(O(s^2)\) away from nearly parallel pairs, and (iii) exponentially sparse exceptional pairs. These can all be recovered without subgaussian tails.

## 3. Replacement lemma I: incompressible directions have a uniform local density floor

This is the main new proof step.

Fix compressibility parameters \(\delta,\rho\in(0,1)\). A unit vector \(u\in S^{m-1}\) is incompressible if it is at Euclidean distance more than \(\rho\) from every vector supported on at most \(\delta m\) coordinates.

Assume throughout this section that \(\xi\) is centered, variance one, has density bounded by \(K\), and has \(\mathbb E|\xi|^3\le B_3\). Uniform fourth moment implies this by Hölder.

### Lemma 3.1 — diffuse mass of an incompressible vector

Let

\[
J=\{j: |u_j|>1/\sqrt{\delta m}\},\qquad D=J^c.
\]

Then \(|J|<\delta m\). Put \(v=\|u_D\|_2\). If \(v<\rho/2\), normalize \(u_J\) to a unit vector \(w\) supported on \(J\). Since

\[
\|u-w\|_2^2=v^2+(1-\sqrt{1-v^2})^2\le v^2+v^4<\rho^2,
\]

we contradict incompressibility. Hence

\[
v\ge \rho/2.
\]

With \(a_j=u_j/v\) for \(j\in D\),

\[
\sum_{j\in D}a_j^2=1,
\qquad
\|a\|_\infty\le \frac{2}{\rho\sqrt{\delta m}}.
\]

So the diffuse part contains a fixed amount of \(\ell_2\) mass and its normalized coefficients tend uniformly to zero.

### Lemma 3.2 — local CLT for the diffuse part under finite third moment

Let

\[
W=\sum_{j\in D}u_j\xi_j=v\sum_{j\in D}a_j\xi_j.
\]

Fix a large integer \(q\), depending only on \(K,B_3,\delta,\rho\). For sufficiently large \(m\), \(\max a_j^2\le1/(4q)\). Apply the greedy blocking construction already present in manuscript Lemma 2.4 to partition the nonzero weights \(a_j^2\) into \(r\asymp q\) blocks, each having weight \(\asymp1/q\).

For block \(B_\ell\), define

\[
Z_\ell=\sqrt r\sum_{j\in B_\ell}a_j\xi_j.
\]

Then the \(Z_\ell\) are independent, centered, their variances are bounded above and below by positive constants, and their densities are uniformly bounded. The density bound follows from the one-dimensional bounded-density projection theorem after normalizing the coefficients within each block.

For the third moment, Rosenthal at \(p=3\) gives

\[
\mathbb E\left|\sum_{j\in B_\ell}a_j\xi_j\right|^3
\le C\left[
\Big(\sum_{j\in B_\ell}a_j^2\Big)^{3/2}
+B_3\sum_{j\in B_\ell}|a_j|^3
\right].
\]

Since \(\max_{j\in B_\ell}|a_j|\le(\sum_{B_\ell}a_j^2)^{1/2}\), the second term is at most a constant multiple of the first. After multiplying by \(r^{3/2}\), \(\sup_\ell\mathbb E|Z_\ell|^3\) is bounded uniformly.

The Bobkov--Götze density Berry--Esseen estimate now gives

\[
\sup_x\left|f_{\sum a_j\xi_j}(x)-\phi(x)\right|\le C/\sqrt r.
\]

Choose \(q\) (and hence \(r\)) sufficiently large that this error is smaller than half the minimum Gaussian density on \([-R,R]\), where \(R\) is a fixed constant chosen below. We obtain a constant \(c_*>0\) such that

\[
f_W(y)\ge c_*
\]

for all \(|y|\le3\), after increasing \(R\) to absorb the fixed scaling \(v\ge\rho/2\). The constants depend only on \(K,B_3,\delta,\rho\), not on \(m\) or \(u\).

Reference: S. Bobkov and F. Götze, *Berry--Esseen bounds in the local limit theorems*, Lithuanian Math. J. 65 (2025), 50--66, DOI 10.1007/s10986-025-09659-1.

### Lemma 3.3 — restore the large coordinates by convolution

Write

\[
L=\sum_{j\in J}u_j\xi_j,
\qquad
\langle X,u\rangle=W+L.
\]

The variables \(W,L\) are independent and \(\operatorname{Var}(L)\le1\). Thus by Chebyshev,

\[
\Pr\{|L|\le2\}\ge3/4.
\]

For \(|x|\le1\), on \(|L|\le2\) we have \(|x-L|\le3\), and therefore

\[
f_{\langle X,u\rangle}(x)=\mathbb E f_W(x-L)
\ge \frac34 c_*.
\]

The projection-density theorem gives the matching global upper bound

\[
\|f_{\langle X,u\rangle}\|_\infty\le C K.
\]

Consequently, uniformly over all incompressible unit vectors \(u\),

\[
c t\le \Pr\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t\}\le C t,
\qquad 0<t\le1.
\]

This is exactly the one-row input required by Lemma 5.1. Importantly, **finite third moment + bounded density is enough once incompressibility is known.** Subgaussianity is not needed.

## 4. Replacement lemma II: many hyperplane normals are incompressible

The manuscript used a much stronger Nguyen--Vu \(\ell_\infty\) theorem. For Section 5, incompressibility suffices.

Let \(B\) be an \((m-1)\times m\) iid matrix and let

\[
\mathcal B_M=\{\|B\|\le M\sqrt m\}.
\]

A standard compressible-net argument gives constants \(\delta=\delta(K,M)>0\), \(\rho=\rho(K,M)>0\), and \(c=c(K,M)>0\) such that

\[
\Pr\{\ker B\cap\mathrm{Comp}(\delta,\rho)\ne\varnothing,
\ \mathcal B_M\}\le e^{-cm}.
\]

For completeness, this does not need a hidden subgaussian hypothesis. For any fixed unit vector \(z\), each coordinate of \(Bz\) is a bounded-density linear form, hence has small-ball probability \(O_K(t)\). If \(\|Bz\|\le c_0\sqrt m\), then at least a fixed positive fraction of its coordinates must lie in a sufficiently small interval. Independence across rows gives \(e^{-c m}\). A \(\rho\)-net of \(\delta m\)-sparse vectors has cardinality \(\exp(O(\delta m\log(1/(\delta\rho))))\). On \(\mathcal B_M\), the net approximation error is at most \(M\sqrt m\rho\); choose \(\rho\asymp c_0/M\) and then \(\delta\) small enough to absorb the entropy.

This is also a direct specialization of Rudelson--Vershynin, Proposition 9.4 in *No-gaps delocalization for general random matrices* (GAFA 26 (2016), 1716--1776): on a boundedness event, the kernel contains only incompressible vectors with exponentially high conditional accuracy under very mild distributional assumptions.

Now apply this to every \((m-1)\)-subset \(S\) of the base rows. Let \(u_S\) be its normal. Let \(E_M\) be the single event that the whole base matrix has operator norm at most \(M\sqrt m\). Then \(E_M\) implies the local boundedness event for every row submatrix. If \(L\) is the number of hyperplanes and \(N_{\rm bad}\) the number whose normal is compressible,

\[
\mathbb E[1_{E_M}N_{\rm bad}]
\le L e^{-cm}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Pr\{E_M,\ N_{\rm bad}>L/2\}\le2e^{-cm}.
\]

So on \(E_M\), with exponentially high probability, at least half the exponentially many normals are good for the new local-density lemma.

Reference: M. Rudelson and R. Vershynin, *No-gaps delocalization for general random matrices*, GAFA 26 (2016), 1716--1776, Proposition 9.4, DOI 10.1007/s00039-016-0389-0.

## 5. Replacement lemma III: fourth moment is enough for the global operator-norm event

For an \(n\times m\) iid centered matrix with variance one and fourth moment at most \(B\), Latała's theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E\|A\|
\le C\left(
\sqrt m+\sqrt n+(nmB)^{1/4}
\right).
\]

When \(n/m\) stays in a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), this is \(O_B(\sqrt m)\). Hence, for every \(\eta>0\), one may choose a fixed \(M=M(B,\gamma,\eta)\) so that

\[
\sup_m\Pr\{\|A\|>M\sqrt m\}\le\eta
\]

for all sufficiently relevant aspect ratios. This is enough for convergence in probability. The constants in Sections 3--4 may depend on \(M\); once \(M\) is fixed for a chosen \(\eta\), the staged proof still has fixed positive success constants, and then \(\eta\downarrow0\) at the end.

Reference: R. Latała, *Some estimates of norms of random matrices*, Proc. AMS 133 (2005), 1273--1282, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-04-07800-1.

For a **single fixed law** with finite fourth moment, Bai--Yin gives more: along rectangular aspect ratios, \(\|A_m\|/\sqrt m\) converges almost surely to the Marchenko--Pastur upper edge constant. Thus one may choose one fixed \(M\) for which the base boundedness event eventually holds almost surely.

Reference: Z. D. Bai and Y. Q. Yin, *Limit of the Smallest Eigenvalue of a Large Dimensional Sample Covariance Matrix*, Ann. Probab. 21 (1993), 1275--1294, DOI 10.1214/aop/1176989118 (the paper also records the finite-fourth-moment upper-edge limit).

## 6. Replacement lemma IV: nearly parallel normal pairs remain exponentially sparse

This is where the original proof appeared to need an exponentially small subgaussian operator-norm tail. It does not.

Fix two \((m-1)\)-row sets \(S,T\) with

\[
k=|T\setminus S|\ge\delta_0 m.
\]

Condition on the rows in \(S\), so \(u=u_S\) is fixed, while the \(k\) fresh rows in \(J=T\setminus S\) are independent of \(u\). Suppose the projective distance from \(u_S\) to \(u_T\) is below \(a\), and suppose the single global base event \(E_M\) holds. Choose the sign of \(u_T\) so that \(\|u-u_T\|<a\). Since \(B_Ju_T=0\),

\[
\|B_Ju\|_2
\le \|B_J\|\,\|u-u_T\|_2
\le M a\sqrt m.
\]

Set

\[
\tau_a=M a\sqrt{2/\delta_0}.
\]

If more than half of the \(k\) fresh rows satisfied \(|\langle R_i,u\rangle|>\tau_a\), the sum of their squared projections would exceed \(M^2a^2m\), contradiction. Hence at least \(k/2\) of the fresh rows obey

\[
|\langle R_i,u\rangle|\le\tau_a.
\]

For every deterministic unit \(u\), bounded-density projection gives

\[
\Pr\{|\langle R_i,u\rangle|\le\tau_a\}
\le C K\tau_a.
\]

Therefore, conditioned on \(S\),

\[
\Pr\{d_{\rm pr}(u_S,u_T)<a,\ E_M\}
\le 2^k(CK\tau_a)^{k/2}.
\]

Choose \(a>0\) small enough. Because \(k\ge\delta_0m\), the right side can be made \(e^{-A m}\) for any prescribed fixed \(A\). This reproduces the exponentially small pair probability used in manuscript Lemma 4.5 **without any tail estimate for \(\|B_J\|\)**: the operator norm is supplied deterministically on the one global event \(E_M\), whose probability is controlled separately.

The deterministic entropy estimate for high-overlap pairs is unchanged. Markov over the low-overlap pair count therefore gives, on \(E_M\), exactly the exponentially sparse exceptional-pair graph required by the staged argument.

## 7. Completion of the moderate-surplus theorem

Take the manuscript Section 5 proof verbatim after replacing:

- “delocalized” by “incompressible” in the good family;
- manuscript Lemma 2.4 by Section 3 above;
- Nguyen--Vu Theorem 4.1 / manuscript Lemma 4.2 by Section 4 above;
- manuscript Lemma 4.4 plus its use in Lemma 4.5 by Sections 5--6 above.

All other Section 5 ingredients are distribution-free once the one- and two-slab estimates and pair graph are available.

Reserve \(k=k_m\) batches, each of size \(b\asymp m/k\). For

\[
t_m=\exp\left(-(h(\gamma)-\varepsilon)\frac m k\right),
\qquad
s=\sqrt{t_m/k},
\]

the one-stage lemma retains a fixed success probability \(p=p(K,B,M,\gamma,\varepsilon)>0\). Hence

\[
\Pr\{\text{some staged batch fails}\}
\le k e^{-c m/k}\to0
\]

because \(k\log m=o(m)\) implies \(m/k\gg\log m\ge\log k\).

For any fixed \(M\), the compressible-normal and bad-pair failures are exponentially small. Thus, writing \(E_M\) for the base norm event,

\[
\Pr\{M_{m,r_m}>t_m\}
\le \Pr(E_M^c)+o_M(1).
\]

Under the uniform fourth-moment hypothesis, Latała and Markov imply: for every \(\eta>0\), choose \(M\) so \(\limsup_m\Pr(E_M^c)\le\eta\). Therefore

\[
\limsup_m\Pr\{M_{m,r_m}>t_m\}\le\eta.
\]

Since \(\eta\) is arbitrary, the upper estimate tends to zero.

The manuscript lower estimate already gives

\[
\Pr\left\{
M_{m,r_m}<\exp\left(-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)\frac m k\right)
\right\}\to0.
\]

Combining the two sides yields

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}\to_{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
\]

This proves the candidate theorem in the executive summary.

## 8. Fixed-law almost-sure strengthening

If the entry law \(\xi\) is fixed (not a triangular array), centered, variance one, bounded density, and \(\mathbb E|\xi|^4<\infty\), Bai--Yin allows one fixed \(M\) for which \(E_M\) holds eventually almost surely under a canonical infinite iid array.

For that fixed \(M\):

- compressible-normal failures are \(e^{-cm}\), summable;
- exceptional-pair failures are \(e^{-cm}\), summable;
- lower-tail failures are \(e^{-c_\varepsilon m+o(m)}\), summable;
- staged failures satisfy \(k_m e^{-c m/k_m}\), and \(k_m\log m=o(m)\) makes this eventually smaller than \(m^{-A}\) for every fixed \(A\), hence summable.

Borel--Cantelli therefore upgrades the fixed-sequence convergence to

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}\to -h(\gamma)
\quad\text{almost surely}
\]

along each fixed deterministic \((N_m,r_m)\) sequence obeying the hypotheses.

This is not the same as the public subgaussian paper's stronger **uniform over a compact range of aspect ratios** statement. That uniformity requires separate bookkeeping and is not claimed here.

## 10. What fourth moment does **not** automatically preserve

The submitted arXiv theorem has a quantitative deviation probability \(C e^{-c\sqrt m}\). The fourth-moment proof above does not produce that rate.

Under only \(\sup_m\mathbb E|\xi_m|^4\le B\), Latała controls the expected norm and gives tightness of \(\|A_m\|/\sqrt m\), but not a subgaussian exponential norm tail. Thus the honest fourth-moment rewrite should state qualitative convergence in probability (and, for a fixed law, the fixed-sequence almost-sure consequence via Bai--Yin), unless an additional tail assumption is imposed.

A clean sufficient abstract hypothesis for the same proof is actually weaker/more modular than “fourth moment”:

1. bounded density (uniform one- and two-dimensional projection anti-concentration);
2. a uniformly bounded third absolute moment (for the local density CLT on diffuse coordinates);
3. operator-norm tightness \(\|A_m\|/\sqrt m=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\) for the rectangular base matrices.

Uniform fourth moment implies (2) and (3), the latter by Latała. This identifies the exact role of the fourth moment.

Going below fourth moment may still be possible if one proves operator-norm tightness after a robust row-truncation/deletion scheme that does not change the extremal-submatrix exponent, but that is a separate theorem-sized problem.

## 12. Adversarial checks and failed routes

### 12.1 Directly retaining Nguyen--Vu under fourth moment — rejected

The normal-vector theorem cited in the manuscript is genuinely subgaussian. Replacing “subgaussian” by “finite fourth moment” in its statement without a source would be invalid. The proof above avoids it entirely.

### 12.2 Entrywise truncation first — unnecessary for the qualitative theorem

A natural plan was to truncate the entries at \(m^\alpha\), apply the subgaussian proof to the truncated matrix, and control the perturbation. This is awkward because the target singular values are \(e^{-\Theta(m/k)}\), so even a polynomially small operator perturbation is enormous relative to the signal. A naive Weyl perturbation argument cannot preserve the exponential scale. This route is therefore structurally inferior.

### 12.3 Lindeberg replacement of the entire extremal minimum — also unattractive

The map \(A\mapsto\min_T\sigma_{\min}(A_T)\) is nonsmooth and involves exponentially many highly dependent submatrices. Smoothing it enough for a fourth-moment replacement creates derivative losses at precisely the exponentially small scale of interest. The proof-by-structural inputs is much cleaner.

### 12.4 Bai--Yin alone — insufficient for a uniform triangular-array theorem

Bai--Yin is ideal for one fixed iid law and yields the almost-sure boundedness event. For a family of entry laws changing with \(m\), Latała's expectation bound is the appropriate robust input; the final convergence-in-probability proof uses an arbitrary outer error \(\eta\) and then sends \(\eta\to0\).
