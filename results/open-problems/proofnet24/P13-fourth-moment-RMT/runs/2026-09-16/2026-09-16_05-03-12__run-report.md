# 5. `P13-ROBUST-CLUSTER4-40` — PROVED

## 5.1 Statement

Let `X` be real with

\[
EX=0,
\qquad EX^2=1.
\]

Let

\[
p\in(1/2,1),
\qquad \rho\ge0,
\qquad p^2\rho^2<2p-1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If

\[
Q_X(\rho)>p,
\tag{5.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
EX^4\ge
1+\frac{\bigl((2p-1)-p^2\rho^2\bigr)^2}{p(1-p)}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

No density, tail, smoothness, or higher moment assumption is used.

## 5.2 Exact-probability thinning

Choose a center `z` such that

\[
P\{|X-z|\le\rho\}>p.
\]

If the event has probability larger than `p`, adjoin an independent uniform auxiliary variable and randomly thin it to an event `E` with exactly

\[
P(E)=p
\tag{5.4}
\]

while retaining

\[
X\in[z-\rho,z+\rho]
\quad\text{on }E.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This device removes any issue of atoms or nonattainment while leaving the law and moments of `X` unchanged.

Put

\[
\mu=E[X\mid E],
\qquad
s=E[X^2\mid E].
\tag{5.6}
\]

Since the conditional support on `E` lies in an interval of length `2rho`, Popoviciu's variance bound gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X\mid E)\le\rho^2,
\]

hence

\[
\mu^2\ge s-\rho^2.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Let

\[
\nu=E[X\mid E^c],
\qquad
t=E[X^2\mid E^c].
\]

Centering and unit variance give

\[
\nu=-\frac{p}{1-p}\mu,
\qquad
 t=\frac{1-ps}{1-p}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

By Jensen, `nu^2<=t`. Combining with (5.7),

\[
\frac{p^2}{(1-p)^2}(s-\rho^2)
\le
\frac{1-ps}{1-p}.
\]

After rearrangement,

\[
\boxed{
s\le U:=\frac{1-p}{p}+p\rho^2.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Condition (5.1) is exactly `U<1`.

## 5.3 Fourth-moment minimization

Jensen on each side of the partition gives

\[
EX^4
\ge p s^2+(1-p)t^2.
\]

Using (5.8),

\[
p s^2+(1-p)t^2
=1+\frac{p}{1-p}(s-1)^2.
\tag{5.10}
\]

The right-hand side decreases in `s` on `[0,1]`. Since (5.9) gives `s<=U<1`,

\[
EX^4
\ge
1+\frac{p}{1-p}(1-U)^2.
\]

Finally

\[
1-U
=\frac{(2p-1)-p^2\rho^2}{p},
\]

which is (5.3).

## 5.4 Endpoint check

At `rho=0`,

\[
1+\frac{(2p-1)^2}{p(1-p)}
=\frac1{p(1-p)}-3
=J(p).
\]

Thus the robust inequality exactly recovers the preceding sharp atomic theorem at zero radius.

# 6. `P13-EXPLICIT-PAIRPROFILE-40` — PROVED

Fix `1<gamma<2` and

\[
1\le B<B_{\rm pair}(\gamma)=J(\gamma^{-1/2}).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Because `J` is strictly increasing on `(1/2,1)`, the exact inverse of `J(p)=B` there is

\[
\boxed{
p_B=\frac12\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{B-1}{B+3}}\right).}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed `p_B(1-p_B)=1/(B+3)`. Since (6.1),

\[
p_B<p_\gamma:=\gamma^{-1/2}.
\]

Define `p_*`, `eta_*`, `a_*`, `c_*`, `rho_*` by (0.2)--(0.3). Because `p_*>p_B`,

\[
J(p_*)>B,
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
(2p_*-1)^2>(B-1)p_*(1-p_*).
\]

Therefore

\[
a_*>c_*\ge0,
\qquad \rho_*>0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that a centered variance-one `X` with `EX^4<=B` satisfies

\[
Q_X(\rho_*)>p_*.
\]

By construction,

\[
p_*^2\rho_*^2=\frac{a_*-c_*}{2}<a_*=2p_*-1,
\]

so Section 5 applies and gives

\[
EX^4
\ge
1+\frac{\left(a_*-p_*^2\rho_*^2\right)^2}
{p_*(1-p_*)}
=
1+\frac{(a_*+c_*)^2}{4p_*(1-p_*)}.
\]

Since `a_*>c_*`,

\[
\frac{(a_*+c_*)^2}{4}>c_*^2.
\]

Hence

\[
EX^4
>
1+\frac{c_*^2}{p_*(1-p_*)}
=B,
\]

contradiction. Thus

\[
\boxed{Q_X(\rho_*)\le p_*=p_\gamma-\eta_*.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

All constants are explicit functions of `(B,gamma)` and are uniform over the law.

### Why this matters

The preceding compactness proof established existence of some `rho,eta`; it did not expose the actual fourth-moment margin. Equation (6.4) is an exact certificate that can be inserted into later entropy and RLogLCD estimates without another compactness step.

# 8. `P13-FINITE4-NONUNIFORM-CE-40` — PROVED

This section separates **finite fourth moment** from **uniformly bounded fourth moment** exactly.

Let

\[
P\{L_m=0\}=1-\frac1m,
\qquad
P\{L_m=\sqrt m\}=P\{L_m=-\sqrt m\}=\frac1{2m}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then

\[
EL_m=0,
\qquad
EL_m^2=1,
\qquad
EL_m^4=m.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus each law has finite fourth moment, but the fourth moments are not uniformly bounded.

Fix one matrix column. The number `Z_m` of exact zeros in that column is

\[
Z_m\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,1-1/m).
\]

Since

\[
\frac{m+k_m-1}{N_m}\to\frac1\gamma<1
\]

while `1-1/m->1`, a standard Chernoff bound gives

\[
P\{Z_m\ge m+k_m-1\}\to1.
\tag{8.3}
\]

On this event choose `m+k_m-1` zero rows and test the corresponding submatrix on that coordinate vector. Hence

\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This already destroys (2.1) with high probability.

## Smooth heavy-tail variant

Let `Z` be a fixed symmetric compactly supported `C^infty` random variable with mean zero and variance `v>0`, independent of `L_m`, and put

\[
\delta_m=e^{-m^2},
\qquad
\xi_m=\frac{L_m+\delta_m Z}{\sqrt{1+v\delta_m^2}}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Then each `xi_m` has a `C^infty` density, mean zero and variance one. Its fourth moment is

\[
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{m+6v\delta_m^2+E Z^4\delta_m^4}
{(1+v\delta_m^2)^2}
<\infty
\]

for every `m`, but is asymptotic to `m` and therefore unbounded.

On every latent-zero row,

\[
|\xi_m|\le C\delta_m.
\]

By (8.3), with probability tending to one there are at least `n_m=m+k_m-1` such rows in the predetermined column. Selecting them yields

\[
\boxed{
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le C\sqrt{n_m}\,e^{-m^2}
\le C'\sqrt m\,e^{-m^2}
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

with probability tending to one.

Thus even smoothness plus **levelwise finite** fourth moments does not yield any triangular universality. The word “bounded” in a meaningful triangular fourth-moment theorem must mean a uniform numerical bound.

Here is an independent derivation.

For every unordered coordinate pair `e={i,j}`, let `E_e` denote the event that at least `n` rows place both coordinates in the fixed scalar ball. By symmetry

\[
P(E_e)=B.
\]

Let

\[
Z=\sum_{e\in\binom{[m]}2}1_{E_e},
\qquad
M=\binom m2.
\]

Then

\[
EZ=MB.
\tag{9.1}
\]

For two disjoint coordinate pairs the events depend on disjoint matrix columns and are independent, so their joint probability is `B^2`. For two distinct pairs sharing one coordinate, symmetry gives joint probability `J`. The number of unordered edge-pairs in `K_m` sharing a vertex is exactly

\[
3\binom m3.
\]

Therefore

\[
EZ^2
=EZ+2\sum_{e<f}P(E_e\cap E_f)
\le
MB+M^2B^2+6\binom m3J.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz / Paley--Zygmund at threshold zero gives

\[
P\{Z>0\}
\ge\frac{(EZ)^2}{EZ^2}
\ge
\left[
1+
\frac1{MB}
+
\frac{6\binom m3J}{M^2B^2}
\right]^{-1},
\tag{9.3}
\]

which is exactly the P12 formula.

On `E_{ij}`, select `n` successful rows and use

\[
x=\frac{e_i-e_j}{\sqrt2}.
\]

If both entries lie in one radius-`b` scalar ball, their difference has absolute value at most `2b`, hence each row inner product has magnitude at most `sqrt(2)b`. Thus

\[
\|A_Tx\|_2\le\sqrt{2n}\,b,
\]

and therefore

\[
M_{m,r}(A)\le\sqrt{2n}\,b.
\tag{9.4}
\]

No centering, variance, density, moment, tail, operator-norm, or delocalization hypothesis is hidden. The only independence invoked is original-column independence for disjoint coordinate pairs. The claim is correct as stated.

# 10. Primary-source checks and route separation

## 10.1 Latała / operator norm

Rafał Latała, *Some estimates of norms of random matrices*, Proc. AMS 133 (2005), proves for independent mean-zero entries the expected operator norm estimate controlled by maximal row/column second moments and the global fourth-moment term. Consequently a **uniform** fourth-moment bound gives coarse `O_P(sqrt m)` norm control in the present aspect-ratio regime.

This remains a coarse module only. The new cluster inequality in Sections 5--7 uses no operator norm at all, and prior smooth compact-support counterexamples already have excellent norm behavior while violating the microscopic theorem.

## 10.2 Bai--Yin type control

The classical Bai--Yin sample-covariance edge theorem is a fixed-law/global spectral statement under iid mean-zero variance-one entries with finite fourth moment. It is useful background for global norm/edge tightness but does not control exponentially small source-scale anti-concentration for an exponentially overlapping family of row submatrices in a triangular array.

## 10.3 Fernandez / robust small ball and leave-one-out

Manuel Fernandez's current distance theorem for inhomogeneous random rectangular matrices assumes independent uniformly anti-concentrated entries plus Hilbert--Schmidt second-moment control, permits codimension up to order `n/log n`, and obtains

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(X,H)\le t\sqrt d\}
\le (Ct)^d+e^{-cn}.
\]

Its hard incompressible structure is encoded by a **Randomized Logarithmic LCD**, which is law-adapted. This remains the relevant primary framework for the unresolved lower-side P13 problem after our global compressible and ordinary-FineLCD reductions.

## 10.4 Truncation / replacement

No truncation or matrix replacement is used in the new proof. The successful fixed finite-convolution proof uses conditioning/local smoothing rather than polynomial-scale matrix modification. Polynomial truncation plus Weyl remains rejected because a polynomial perturbation is enormous compared with the target singular value `e^{-Theta(m/k)}`.

## 10.5 Lindeberg

Generic entrywise matrix Lindeberg remains a nonprimary route. The retained Rademacher/Walsh and narrow-cluster examples show that low moments can agree while exponentially microscopic lattice/cluster resonance changes by orders of magnitude. A Lindeberg comparison that does not explicitly control this nonsmooth hard-edge statistic is not a proof.

## 10.6 Delocalization

Deterministic flatness/incompressibility is insufficient: the retained Walsh/Hadamard construction has perfectly flat leverage yet polynomial lattice mass at exponentially small radii. The unresolved question is randomness/arithmetic of the **actual frozen leave-one-out normals**, not merely their magnitudes.

## 10.7 High probability versus expectation

The retained `P13-B4-PROB-NOT-L1-36` rare-contamination construction shows that even smooth compact support, uniform subgaussian tails, uniformly bounded fourth moments, and the correct convergence-in-probability exponent do not force logarithmic expectation convergence. Deep-scale scalar power concentration or the stronger fixed finite-convolution mechanism supplies the needed uniform integrability; fourth moment alone does not.

## 10.8 Finite fourth versus uniformly bounded fourth

Section 8 gives the sharp conceptual split. “Finite fourth moment” separately for each triangular level supplies no uniform anti-concentration constant and allows a zero cluster of mass tending to one. Uniform `sup_m E|xi_m|^4<=B` is a genuinely stronger hypothesis and is exactly what is used in all positive fourth-moment constants.
