# 3. Proof of `P13-B4-FRESH-PROBE-ROWTRIM-49`

Assume `(0.4)` and fix `eta,Gamma_b,C,nu_m` as in `(0.5)`.

## 3.1 Deterministic base/probe split and enough clean base rows

For large `m`, `N_b=floor(Gamma_b m)<N_m`. The remaining probe pool has

\[
N_m-N_b
=\left(\gamma-1-\eta+o(1)\right)m
=\left(\frac{3(\gamma-1)}4+o(1)\right)m,
\]

so `(0.7)` follows eventually.

For one scalar entry, Markov gives

\[
P\{|\xi_m|>\nu_m\}
\le\frac{B}{C^4m}
=\frac{\eta}{8\Gamma_bm}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

A base row is bad if any of its `m` coordinates exceeds `nu_m`; hence, by a union bound,

\[
P\{\text{row bad}\}
\le\frac{\eta}{8\Gamma_b}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The base-row indicators are independent. Therefore the expected number of good base rows is at least

\[
N_b\left(1-\frac{\eta}{8\Gamma_b}\right)
=(1+7\eta/8+o(1))m.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because `k_m=o(m)`, for all large `m`,

\[
m+k_m-1\le(1+\eta/4)m.
\tag{3.4}
\]

A standard Chernoff lower-tail bound applied to the independent good-row indicators yields

\[
P\{|G_m|<m+k_m-1\}\le e^{-c_1m}
\tag{3.5}
\]

for `c_1=c_1(gamma)>0`.

## 3.2 Conditional good-row law

Let

\[
p_m=P\{|\xi_m|\le\nu_m\}
\ge1-\frac{\eta}{8\Gamma_bm}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Conditioning one row on being good factorizes across coordinates, so its coordinates remain iid with scalar law

\[
\widetilde\xi_m\sim\mathcal L(\xi_m\mid |\xi_m|\le\nu_m).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Let `mu_m=E tilde_xi_m`. Centering of the original law gives

\[
|\mu_m|
=\frac{|E[\xi_m1_{\{|\xi_m|>\nu_m\}}]|}{p_m}
\le\frac{B}{p_m\nu_m^3}
\le\frac{2B}{C^3}m^{-3/4}
\tag{3.8}
\]

for large `m`. Also

\[
E\widetilde\xi_m^2\le p_m^{-1}\le2,
\qquad
E|\widetilde\xi_m|^4\le Bp_m^{-1}\le2B.
\tag{3.9}
\]

For `zeta_m=tilde_xi_m-mu_m`,

\[
E\zeta_m=0,
\quad
E\zeta_m^2\le2,
\quad
|\zeta_m|\le2\nu_m
\tag{3.10}
\]

for large `m`.

Conditional on the exact good-row index set `I`, the entries in those rows are independent copies of `tilde_xi_m`; conditioning on the bad-row indicators outside `I` does not change this product law. Thus every probability bound below is uniform in `I` and can be integrated over the random set `G_m`.

## 3.3 Operator norm of all good base rows

Fix a good-row set `I`, `L=|I|<=N_b`, and decompose

\[
A_I=Z_I+\mu_m\mathbf1_L\mathbf1_m^T,
\tag{3.11}
\]

where `Z_I` has independent centered entries distributed as `zeta_m`.

Apply the rectangular matrix Bernstein inequality to the independent entry matrices `zeta_{ij}e_ie_j^T`. Their norms are at most

\[
R_m=2Cm^{1/4},
\]

and the variance parameter satisfies

\[
\nu^2\le2\max\{L,m\}\le2\Gamma_bm.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Take

\[
t=A\sqrt{m\log m},
\qquad
A=\sqrt{84\Gamma_b}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Since `R_mt=o(m)`, for large `m` the Bernstein denominator is at most `6Gamma_bm`, so

\[
P\{\|Z_I\|_{op}>A\sqrt{m\log m}\mid I\}
\le(\Gamma_b+1)m\,m^{-14}
=O(m^{-13}).
\tag{3.14}
\]

The conditional mean matrix satisfies

\[
\|\mu_m\mathbf1_L\mathbf1_m^T\|_{op}
=|\mu_m|\sqrt{Lm}
\le\frac{2B\sqrt{\Gamma_b}}{C^3}m^{1/4}
=o(\sqrt{m\log m}).
\tag{3.15}
\]

Hence for a fixed `K=K(B,gamma)`, uniformly over `I`,

\[
P\{\|A_I\|_{op}>K\sqrt{m\log m}\mid I\}=O(m^{-13}).
\tag{3.16}
\]

After averaging over `I`, the same holds for `A_{G_m}`. Deleting rows cannot increase operator norm, so every submatrix supported inside `G_m` obeys the same bound.

## 3.4 Uniform row norms in the good base pool

For one conditional good row,

\[
0\le\widetilde\xi_{mj}^2\le C^2\sqrt m,
\qquad
E\widetilde\xi_{mj}^2\le2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde\xi_{mj}^2)\le2B.
\]

Scalar Bernstein therefore gives

\[
P\left\{\sum_{j=1}^m\widetilde\xi_{mj}^2>4m\right\}
\le e^{-c_2\sqrt m}
\tag{3.17}
\]

with, for example, any sufficiently small

\[
c_2\le\frac4{4B+8C^2/3}.
\]

A union over at most `Gamma_bm` good base rows preserves an `e^{-c_3sqrt(m)}` bound. Thus

\[
\max_{i\in G_m}\|R_i\|_2\le2\sqrt m
\tag{3.18}
\]

outside an `e^{-c_3sqrt(m)}` event.

Combining `(3.5)`, `(3.16)`, and `(3.18)` proves `(0.6)`.

## 3.5 Freshness of the probe pool

The base/probe split is deterministic. Every event used above is measurable with respect to the base pool alone. Therefore all probe rows retain their original joint iid law and are independent of the entire trimmed-base sigma-field. This exact filtration property is the main reason for splitting before trimming rather than globally conditioning every row on a tail event.

## 3.6 Why this is not truncation-plus-Weyl

No entry is replaced. No matrix perturbation is formed, and no spectral error is compared by Weyl. The theorem merely selects rows from a deterministic base pool—an operation allowed by the definition of `M_{m,r}`—and leaves the future probe rows untouched. Thus there is no polynomial perturbation competing with the exponentially small source scale.

# 4. Proof of `P13-B4-L4-RELATION-LOCALIZATION-49`

Let `mu_4=EX^4<=B`, `||a||_2=1`, and `theta=sum a_j^4`.

Independence and centering give

\[
\begin{aligned}
ES^4
&=\mu_4\sum_ja_j^4
+6\sum_{i<j}a_i^2a_j^2\\
&=\mu_4\theta+3(1-\theta)\\
&=3+(\mu_4-3)\theta\\
&\le3+(B-3)\theta,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

which is `(0.10)`.

For `(0.11)`, let `E={|S|<=rho}` and `q=P(E)`. Since `ES^2=1`,

\[
E[S^2;E^c]\ge1-q\rho^2=1-qr.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on `E^c` gives

\[
(1-qr)^2
\le P(E^c)E[S^4;E^c]
\le(1-q)ES^4,
\]

proving `(0.11)`.

For fixed `r<=1/2`, define

\[
f_r(q)=\frac{(1-qr)^2}{1-q}.
\]

Then

\[
f_r'(q)
=\frac{(1-qr)(1-2r+qr)}{(1-q)^2}\ge0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore `q>=beta` forces `ES^4>=F_beta(r)`, proving `(0.13)`.

For the quantitative choices, note

\[
R_\gamma-F_\beta(r)
=\frac{2\beta r-\beta^2r^2}{1-\beta}
\le\frac{2\beta r}{1-\beta}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This yields `(0.14)` directly. Since `f_r` is continuous and increasing and `f_r(beta)>M_*`, the solution `q_*<beta` in `(0.16)` is unique. If desired, for `r>0` it is explicitly

\[
q_*
=\frac{2r-M_*+\sqrt{M_*(M_*-4r+4r^2)}}{2r^2},
\tag{4.4}
\]

while at `r=0`, `q_*=1-1/M_*`.

For `3/2<gamma<2`, setting `r=0` in `(0.13)` and using `B<3` gives

\[
3-(3-B)\theta\ge\frac\gamma{\gamma-1},
\]

which is exactly `(0.17)`. At the boundary `B=B_exact=(3-gamma)/(gamma-1)`, the right side gives `theta_c=1/2`; strict subcriticality makes it strictly smaller. This verifies `(0.18)` and identifies the two-coordinate relation as the endpoint effective-sparsity geometry rather than an accidental example.

Independence across rows and Markov yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
P\left\{\sum_{i=1}^NW_i^{(J)}>n\log\kappa\right\}
&\le\kappa^{-2n}(1+1/H)^N\\
&\le\exp[-2n\log\kappa+N/H]\\
&\le\kappa^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

On the complement, every row candidate `T`, `|T|=n`, obeys

\[
\prod_{i\in T}\alpha_i^{(J)}\ge\kappa^{-n},
\]

and `(6.1)` gives

\[
\mathcal B_\kappa((A_m)_{T,-J}^T)\le H(m-q)n.
\tag{6.4}
\]

For all `|J|<=q_m`, the number of held-out sets is at most

\[
(q_m+1)\exp\left[q_m\log\frac{em}{q_m}\right]
=e^{o(m)}
\]

when `q_m log(em/q_m)=o(m)`. Unioning `(6.3)` therefore gives total failure

\[
\exp[-n\log\kappa+o(m)].
\tag{6.5}
\]

For each `J`, the weights are functions only of `A_{-J}`; the whole block `A_J` remains jointly fresh. No centering, fourth moment, density, smoothness, norm tail, or number-theoretic input is hidden. The field-independent regularized-HS statement is valid over `R` or `C` after replacing squares by squared moduli. Verdict: **PASS**.

This strengthens P13's one-column fresh RegHS certificate and is potentially useful for a genuine two-fresh-column/projective reconstruction of the source upper stage.

# 8. Heavy-tail and microscopic counterexamples retained

The following previously validated negative results remain active.

1. **Levelwise finite fourth moment is not a uniform hypothesis.** The sparse law
   \[
   P\{L_m=0\}=1-1/m,

   has `EL_m^4=m<infinity` at each level but destroys the source exponent. Smooth microjitter preserves the obstruction while making each density `C^infinity`.

2. **Uniform B4 does not give a subgaussian full-matrix norm event.** Rare `Theta(sqrt(m))` spikes with probability `Theta(m^{-2})` obey a common fourth-moment ceiling but force `||A_m||>=Lsqrt(m)` with fixed positive probability. The new theorem explains why this does not obstruct an upper construction: spike rows in a predetermined base pool can be discarded while untouched probe rows remain fresh.

3. **Deterministic delocalization is not microscopic smoothing.** Smooth narrow perturbations of Rademacher laws admit perfectly flat deterministic one-/two-normal and Walsh rank-`k` resonances with polynomial or `exp[-o(m)]` microscopic mass. Thus B4 + flatness cannot yield the source `t^k` law.

4. **Moment-only triangular universality is false at the exact relation threshold.** For `1<gamma<2`, the retained sharp threshold is
   \[
   B_{exact}(\gamma)=
   \begin{cases}
   \gamma/(\gamma-1),&1<\gamma\le3/2,\\[4pt]
   (3-\gamma)/(\gamma-1),&3/2<\gamma<2,
   \end{cases}
   \]
   and for `gamma>=2`, balanced Rademacher already obstructs unrestricted moment-only universality at fourth moment one.

5. **Probability and logarithmic expectation are different claims.** The retained rare-contamination construction shows that correct convergence in probability can coexist with failure of normalized logarithmic expectation convergence even under smooth compact support, uniform subgaussianity, and uniform B4.
