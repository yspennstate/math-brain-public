# 5. Independent audit of P12 row-selection lemma

## `P13-AUDIT-P12-ROWSELECTION-38`

**Audited claim:** `P12-ROWSELECTION-BINOMIAL-39`.  
**Source path:** `proofnet24/P12-smooth-iid/runs/2026-09-16/2026-09-16_02-17-34__run-report.md`.  
**Introducing commit:** `06312c9543352bd35229f1d453ec892f984eb9c6`.  
**Blob:** `0174fff50d6d2db97add3b2654b79f3b8e510de7`.  
**Verdict:** **PASS, with a proof-wording correction**.

Let `A` be an iid-entry `N x m` matrix over the reals or complexes, let `n=m+r`, and let `R` be one original product row. Define

\[
\mathcal A_m(a)
=
\sup_{\|x\|_2=1}P\{|Rx|\le a\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The claimed inequality is

\[
\boxed{
P\{M_{m,r}(A)\le\sqrt n\,a\}
\ge
P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N,\mathcal A_m(a))\ge n\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Independent proof

Fix a unit vector `x` and put

\[
p_x=P\{|Rx|\le a\}.
\]

For the original independent rows `R_1,...,R_N`, the count

\[
S_x=\#\{i:|R_ix|\le a\}
\]

has law `Bin(N,p_x)`. On `{S_x>=n}`, choose any `n` successful rows as `T`. Then

\[
\|A_Tx\|_2^2
=\sum_{i\in T}|R_ix|^2
\le na^2,
\]

so

\[
\sigma_m(A_T)\le\|A_Tx\|_2\le\sqrt n\,a
\]

and hence `M_{m,r}(A)<=sqrt(n)a`. Therefore

\[
P\{M_{m,r}(A)\le\sqrt n\,a\}
\ge B_{N,n}(p_x),
\tag{5.3}
\]

where `B_{N,n}(q)=P{Bin(N,q)>=n}`.

Now let `eta>0`. By the definition of the supremum, there exists a unit vector `x_eta` with

\[
p_{x_\eta}\ge\mathcal A_m(a)-\eta.
\]

Since `B_{N,n}` is nondecreasing,

\[
P\{M_{m,r}(A)\le\sqrt n a\}
\ge
B_{N,n}(\mathcal A_m(a)-\eta).
\]

The function `B_{N,n}` is a finite polynomial in `q` and hence continuous. Sending `eta downarrow 0` gives `(5.2)`.

### Wording correction

The P12 report says that taking the supremum is justified by “continuity and monotonicity.” Monotonicity/continuity of the **binomial tail in its success parameter** is enough. Continuity of

\[
x\mapsto P\{|Rx|\le a\}
\]

need not hold for an arbitrary atomic row law, and the supremum need not be attained. The `eta`-maximizer argument above avoids both issues. The theorem itself, all its hypotheses, and its downstream use remain correct.

No centering, variance, density, tail, moment, operator norm, delocalization, or projected-coordinate independence is used.

# 6. New exact binomial critical-window theorem

## `P13-BINOMIAL-CRITICAL-WINDOW-38` — PROVED

Let

\[
N_m\to\infty,
\qquad
1\le n_m\le N_m,
\qquad
\beta_m=\frac{n_m}{N_m}\to\beta\in(0,1),
\]

and let `q_m in [0,1]`. Put

\[
B_m=P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,q_m)\ge n_m\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
B_m\to0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{N_m}(\beta_m-q_m)\to+\infty.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Furthermore, if

\[
\sqrt{N_m}(\beta_m-q_m)\to c\in\mathbb R,
\tag{6.2}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
B_m\to
1-\Phi\!\left(
\frac{c}{\sqrt{\beta(1-\beta)}}
\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If the left side of `(6.2)` tends to `-infinity`, then `B_m->1`.

## Proof

Write

\[
X_m\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,q_m),
\qquad
\mu_m=N_mq_m,
\qquad
v_m=N_mq_m(1-q_m).
\]

### Sufficiency in `(6.1)`

Assume

\[
g_m:=\sqrt{N_m}(\beta_m-q_m)\to+\infty.
\]

Then for large `m`, `n_m>mu_m`, and

\[
n_m-\mu_m=N_m(\beta_m-q_m)=\sqrt{N_m}g_m.
\]

Cantelli's inequality gives

\[
B_m
\le
\frac{v_m}{v_m+(n_m-\mu_m)^2}
\le
\frac{N_m/4}{N_mg_m^2}
=\frac1{4g_m^2}	o0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Necessity in `(6.1)`

Assume `g_m` does not tend to `+infinity`. Pass to a subsequence on which `g_m<=C`.

If along a further subsequence `g_m->-infinity`, then `mu_m-n_m=sqrt(N_m)(-g_m)`. By the same one-sided variance bound,

\[
P\{X_m<n_m\}
\le
\frac{N_m/4}{N_mg_m^2}	o0,
\]

so `B_m->1`, not zero.

Otherwise pass to a further subsequence on which `g_m` is bounded below as well. Then

\[
q_m=\beta_m-\frac{g_m}{\sqrt{N_m}}\to\beta,
\]

so `q_m` stays in a fixed compact subinterval of `(0,1)`. The Berry--Esseen theorem for Bernoulli sums is therefore uniform along this subsequence. Since

\[
B_m
=
1-P\{X_m\le n_m-1\},
\]

we have

\[
P\{X_m\le n_m-1\}
=
\Phi\!\left(
\frac{n_m-1-N_mq_m}{\sqrt{N_mq_m(1-q_m)}}
\right)
+O(N_m^{-1/2}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The standardized threshold in `(6.5)` is bounded above because `g_m<=C` and `q_m->beta`. Hence `B_m` is bounded below by a positive constant on the subsequence. This proves necessity.

### Critical limit `(6.3)`

Under `(6.2)`, `q_m->beta` and

\[
\frac{n_m-1-N_mq_m}{\sqrt{N_mq_m(1-q_m)}}
\to
\frac{c}{\sqrt{\beta(1-\beta)}}.
\]

Insert this in `(6.5)` to obtain `(6.3)`.

QED.

### Interpretation

The threshold `q=beta` is not merely a coarse phase boundary. The exact vanishing scale is

\[
\boxed{\beta_m-q_m\gg N_m^{-1/2}.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

A fixed gap gives exponentially small binomial tails. A `c/sqrt(N)` gap gives a nonzero Gaussian critical limit. A gap of the opposite sign larger than `N^{-1/2}` makes success overwhelmingly likely.

\dfrac{\gamma}{\gamma-1},&1<\gamma\le3/2,\\[3mm]
\dfrac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1},&3/2<\gamma<2.
\end{cases}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The retained theorem `P13-B4-ALLSUPPORT-COARSE-GAP-31` states that for every

\[
1\le B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)
\]

there exist fixed

\[
\rho=\rho(B,\gamma)>0,
\qquad
\delta=\delta(B,\gamma)>0
\]

such that for every centered variance-one law `X` with `EX^4<=B`, every finite unit coefficient vector `c`,

\[
P\left\{
\left|\sum_jc_jX_j\right|\le\rho
\right\}
\le\frac1\gamma-\delta.
\tag{9.2}
\]

These constants are uniform over the law, support size, and coefficient vector, but the compactness proof is existential rather than numerically explicit.

## `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-ROWPROFILE-CERT-38` — PROVED

Assume a triangular iid family satisfies uniformly

\[
E\xi_m=0,
\qquad
E\xi_m^2=1,
\qquad
E|\xi_m|^4\le B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then for every fixed `u>0`, eventually

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_m(a_m(u))
\le\frac1\gamma-\delta,
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{N_m}
\left[
\beta_m-\mathcal A_m(a_m(u))
\right]
\to+\infty.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Moreover there is `c=c(B,gamma)>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
B_{N_m,n_m}\!\left(\mathcal A_m(a_m(u))\right)
\le e^{-cm}
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

for all sufficiently large `m`.

### Proof

Because `k_m log m=o(m)`,

\[
\frac{m}{k_m}\to\infty,
\]

so `a_m(u)->0`. Hence `a_m(u)<=rho` eventually. For every unit `x in R^m`, `(9.2)` gives

\[
P\{|R_mx|\le a_m(u)\}
\le
P\{|R_mx|\le\rho\}
\le\frac1\gamma-\delta.
\]

Taking the supremum yields `(9.4)`.

Since `beta_m->1/gamma`, eventually

\[
\beta_m-\mathcal A_m(a_m(u))\ge\delta/2,
\]

which proves `(9.5)`. Hoeffding's binomial inequality then gives

\[
B_{N_m,n_m}(q_m)
\le
\exp\{-2N_m(\beta_m-q_m)^2\}
\le
\exp\{-N_m\delta^2/2\}
\le e^{-cm},
\]

proving `(9.6)`.

QED.

### What `(9.6)` means and what it does not mean

It means that **every fixed deterministic direction**, even after optimizing its law-level small-ball probability, is safely below the row-count threshold by a fixed margin. Therefore the direct row-selection obstruction cannot explain a failure below `B_exact`.

It does **not** union-bound over all data-dependent directions. The least singular vector or leave-one-out normal is chosen from the same matrix geometry. This is exactly why the remaining randomized-LCD problem is nontrivial.
