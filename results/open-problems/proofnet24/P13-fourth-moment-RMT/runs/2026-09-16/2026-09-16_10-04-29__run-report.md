# 4. Proof of `P13-B4-GLOBAL-LEFTCOMP-45`

This theorem is independent of `B_exact`.

Let `C_1,...,C_m in R^N` be the independent columns of `A`, and fix a unit vector `x in R^N`. Put

\[
S=\langle C_1,x\rangle.
\]

Since the entries are iid centered and variance one,

\[
ES^2=1.
\]

The exact fourth-moment expansion is

\[
ES^4
=
3+(E\xi^4-3)\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^4
\le B_*:=\max\{B,3\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Apply Paley--Zygmund to `S^2` at half its mean:

\[
P\{|S|\ge1/\sqrt2\}
\ge
\frac{(1-1/2)^2(ES^2)^2}{ES^4}
\ge
\frac1{4B_*}
=q_B.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the soft centered slab

\[
\phi(t)=
\begin{cases}
1,&|t|\le1/(2\sqrt2),\\
2-2\sqrt2|t|,&1/(2\sqrt2)<|t|<1/\sqrt2,\\
0,&|t|\ge1/\sqrt2.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus `0<=phi<=1`, `Lip(phi)=2 sqrt(2)`, and `(4.2)` gives, uniformly over all unit `x`,

\[
E\phi(\langle C_1,x\rangle)
\le1-q_B.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal C=\operatorname{Comp}_N(\alpha,\eta),
\qquad
F=
\sup_{x\in\mathcal C}
\left[
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\phi(\langle C_j,x\rangle)
-E\phi(\langle C_1,x\rangle)
\right].
\]

Introduce independent Rademacher signs and

\[
H=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_jC_j\in\mathbb R^N.
\]

The same safe symmetrization/contraction calculation used in P12 gives

\[
EF
\le
4\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)
E\sup_{x\in\mathcal C}|\langle H,x\rangle|
+\frac2{\sqrt m}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

If `s=floor(alpha N)`, then deterministically

\[
\sup_{x\in\mathcal C}|\langle H,x\rangle|
\le
\max_{|J|\le s}\|H_J\|_2+\eta\|H\|_2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For each coordinate `i`,

\[
EH_i^4
=
\frac{mE\xi^4+3m(m-1)}{m^4}
\le
\frac{B+3}{m^2}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
E\max_{|J|\le s}\|H_J\|_2
\le
(B+3)^{1/4}\alpha^{1/4}\sqrt{N/m},
\tag{4.8}
\]

while

\[
E\|H\|_2\le\sqrt{N/m}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Assume `N/m<=Gamma_0` eventually and put `R=sqrt(Gamma_0)`. With the explicit choices `(0.3)`, the first term in `(4.5)` is at most `q_B/8`; for all sufficiently large `m`, `2/sqrt(m)<=q_B/8`. Hence

\[
EF\le q_B/4.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Changing one column changes `F` by at most `1/m`. McDiarmid therefore gives

\[
P\{F-EF>q_B/4\}
\le
\exp[-q_B^2m/8].
\tag{4.11}
\]

On the complementary event, every `x in C` satisfies

\[
\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\phi(\langle C_j,x\rangle)
\le
1-q_B/2.
\tag{4.12}
\]

But if `A_{-j}^Tx=0`, then `m-1` of the `m` inner products vanish, and therefore the left side of `(4.12)` is at least

\[
\frac{m-1}{m}.
\]

For `m>2/q_B`, this is strictly larger than `1-q_B/2`, a contradiction. Thus no unit vector in any deleted-column global kernel belongs to `Comp_N(alpha_B,eta_B)`.

Finally, if `T` is any row candidate and `x in R^{|T|}` is a unit vector in the candidate deleted-column left kernel, zero-extend `x` to `R^N`. It lies in the corresponding global deleted-column kernel. Any `alpha_B|T|`-sparse approximant inside `T` is also `alpha_BN`-sparse after zero extension because `|T|<=N`. Therefore candidate incompressibility follows with the same radius.

This proves `P13-B4-GLOBAL-LEFTCOMP-45`.

\le

\le

# 8. From exponential RLogLCD to the source lower scale

Let `B_T=(A_m)_T` be an `n x m` candidate and write `C_j` for its columns. Let

\[
H_j=\operatorname{span}\{C_\ell:\ell\ne j\}.
\]

The negative second moment identity is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^m
\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)^{-2}
=
\sum_{i=1}^m\sigma_i(B_T)^{-2}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

If `sigma_m(B_T)<=tau`, then the right side is at least `tau^{-2}`, so some `j` obeys

\[
\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)
\le\sqrt m\,\tau.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For fixed `(T,j)`, `C_j` is independent of `C_{T,j}`. Its orthogonal complement

\[
E_j=H_j^\perp
\]

has dimension `k`.

Fernandez Corollary 4.3 states, if `D(E_j)=RLogLCD^X_{L,u}(E_j)` and `2L^2>=k+2`,

\[
\mathcal L(P_{E_j}X,t\sqrt k)
\le
\left(\frac{CL}{u}\right)^k
\left(
\frac{\max\{t,\sqrt k/D(E_j)\}}{\sqrt k}
\right)^k.
\tag{8.3}
\]

On the global event `(0.10)`, with `X=4C_j`,

\[
\sqrt k/D(E_j)
\le C e^{-c_Rm/k}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Take

\[
\tau=e^{-(h+\varepsilon)m/k}.
\]

The radius `(8.2)` for `4C_j` corresponds to

\[
t=4\sqrt{m/k}\,\tau.
\]

Because `k log m=o(m)`, all polynomial and constant prefactors raised to `k` are `e^{o(m)}`. If

\[
c_R>h+\varepsilon,
\]

then `(8.4)` is smaller than `t` and `(8.3)` becomes

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)\le\sqrt m\tau\}
\le
\exp[-(h+\varepsilon)m+o(m)].
\tag{8.5}
\]

Unioning `(8.5)` over all `m binom(N,n)` choices proves the source lower tail for that `epsilon`.

This establishes the certificate `(0.16)` and then the near-square theorem `(0.18)` as explained in the executive summary.

# 12. Separation of the requested proof mechanisms

## 12.1 Truncation / replacement

No truncation is used in any new theorem this run. Polynomial truncation followed by Weyl remains invalid at the source scale because a polynomial perturbation is enormous compared with

\[
e^{-\Theta(m/k)}.
\]

Artificial smoothing is safe only when used inside a frozen probability comparison with a proved transfer inequality; it is not a generic matrix replacement theorem.

## 12.2 Operator norm

Uniform B4 gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` norm tightness, but not an exponentially small full-matrix norm exception for triangular arrays. The new Section 4 compressibility theorem uses no norm event at all.

Fernandez's lattice-like net is particularly well matched to this regime: the primary source explicitly replaces operator-norm approximation by a **regularized Hilbert--Schmidt norm** because heavy-tailed operator norms can be prohibitively large.

## 12.3 Bai--Yin-type control

Bai--Yin is a macroscopic fixed-law spectral-edge theorem under finite fourth moment. It is not a triangular, exponentially microscopic anti-concentration theorem and it does not pay entropy from exponentially many overlapping row subsets. It is not used here.

## 12.4 Lindeberg replacement

A low-moment matrix Lindeberg scheme remains nonprimary. The retained Rademacher and narrow-cluster examples show that low moments can agree while source-scale lattice/cluster geometry differs radically. A successful replacement theorem would need to preserve the exact microscopic observable rather than merely moments or global spectra.

## 12.5 Robust small ball / leave-one-out

This remains the productive route. The new result shows that the full candidate family can be made simultaneously classically incompressible under B4 and simultaneously exponentially high in law-adapted RLogLCD at some positive height exponent. The remaining quantitative problem is `(9.1)`.

## 12.6 Delocalization

Classical incompressibility is now globally exponential under **any** uniform B4 in the bounded-aspect regime, by Section 4. But the retained balanced-Rademacher flat-vector calculation proves that no deterministic spread theorem can by itself imply exponential RLogLCD height.

## 12.7 High probability versus expectation

The new near-square theorem is only a high-probability lower-extreme theorem. It does not imply logarithmic expectation convergence. The retained rare-contamination construction prevents that inference even under substantially stronger tail/smoothness assumptions.

## 12.8 Finite versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

All new moment constants use one fixed numerical `B`. Merely having `E|xi_m|^4<infinity` separately at each level supplies no uniform `q_B`, no uniform anti-concentration constant, and no uniform structural constants; the retained sparse-heavy-tail example disproves such a theorem.
