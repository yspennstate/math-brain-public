Hence
\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M(A_m)
=
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M(A_m^{(X)})
-\frac{k_m}{m}\log\sigma_m.
\tag{8.14}
\]
Since `log sigma_m=O(p_m)` and `k_m=o(m)`, the final term is `o(1)`.  The fixed good law `X` obeys the source moderate-surplus theorem.  Together with `(8.12)`, this proves `(8.2)`.

For `r=0`, this probability statement already follows directly from the current public arXiv theorem.  For general moderate surplus it uses the source-certified private theorem / retained P13 theorem chain.

### 8.4 A vanishing event destroys expectation

Let `T_0` be the first `n_m` rows and consider the first column.  Define
\[
E_m=\{I_{i1}=1\text{ for all }i\in T_0\}.
\]
Then
\[
P(E_m)=p_m^{n_m}.
\tag{8.15}
\]
On `E_m`, since `|Z|<=1`, the first column of `(A_m)_{T_0}` has norm at most
\[
\frac{\sqrt{n_m}\,\delta_m}{\sigma_m}.
\]
Testing the least singular value on the first coordinate vector gives
\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le
\frac{\sqrt{n_m}\,\delta_m}{\sigma_m}.
\tag{8.16}
\]
Thus for all large `m`, on `E_m`,
\[
Z_m
\ge
\frac{k_m}{m}
\left(
L_m-\frac12\log n_m+\log\sigma_m
\right)
\ge
\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}.
\tag{8.17}
\]
Therefore
\[
EZ_m^+
\ge
p_m^{n_m}\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}.
\tag{8.18}
\]
Now `k_m log m=o(m)` implies `k_m=o(m)` and hence `n_m=m+o(m)`.  Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\left(
 p_m^{n_m}\frac{k_mL_m}{2m}
\right)
&=
-(m+C_0)n_m+3m^2+\log\frac{k_m}{2m}\\
&=
2m^2-o(m^2)\to+\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.19}
\]
Hence
\[
EZ_m^+\to\infty.
\tag{8.20}
\]
Because the entries are uniformly bounded, one fixed candidate has Frobenius norm at most `Cm`, so deterministically
\[
Z_m^-
\le
O\left(\frac{k_m\log m}{m}\right)
\to0.
\tag{8.21}
\]
Thus the extended expectation satisfies `(8.3)`; in fact the positive part diverges while the negative part vanishes.

This proves `P13-B4-PROB-NOT-L1-36`.

### 8.5 Meaning of the counterexample

The obstruction is **not** a heavy tail.  The entries are uniformly compactly supported and uniformly subgaussian.  It is **not** failure of convergence in probability; the probability theorem survives because the contaminating component is absent from the entire matrix with probability `1-o(1)`.  The obstruction is a microscopic rare-event tail of the logarithm that is invisible to probability convergence and to every fixed positive moment of the entries.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uniform bounded fourth moment + smooth compact support + probability convergence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{expectation convergence}.
}
\tag{8.22}
\]

The currently verified sufficient extra hypothesis for expectation is a **uniform deep-scale concentration profile**, for example `(5.1)`--`(5.2)`.  Fixed finite convolution supplies exactly such a profile.  This is a sufficient condition, not claimed to be mathematically necessary or optimal.

# 9. Finite fourth moment versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

The distinctions are now:

## 9.1 Fixed law

A fixed centered positive-variance law with the source microscopic smoothing can satisfy the theorem without a fourth moment.  Under fixed finite convolution, only the second moment is used by the assembled theorem.

## 9.2 Levelwise finite fourth moment in a triangular family

Merely having `E|xi_m|^4<infinity` separately at each level supplies no uniform constants.  It does not give a uniform operator-norm theorem, a uniform scalar UAC pair, or microscopic smoothing.  Earlier smooth-spike examples already kill any attempt to recover a uniform triangular proof from levelwise finiteness alone.

## 9.3 Uniformly bounded fourth moment

A common fourth-moment ceiling does give coarse uniform information:

- a fixed scalar anti-concentration gap via Paley--Zygmund;
- coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` operator-norm tightness via Latała's theorem;
- the fixed-candidate random-distance hypotheses after UAC is extracted.

It does **not** give source-scale linear concentration, and it does not give logarithmic uniform integrability.  The new theorem `(8.22)` shows that this failure persists under smooth densities, uniform compact support, and uniform subgaussianity.

## 9.4 Uniform fourth moment plus fixed microscopic smoothing

This is sufficient but nonminimal.  The repaired full theorem spends only the smoothing mechanism and second moments; the fourth moment can be deleted.

# 10. Route comparison

## 10.1 Truncation bias

In the successful fixed finite-convolution proof, the clean cutoff is conditioning/reveal, not matrix replacement.  The original scalar product is preserved, so truncation bias is exactly zero.

The new rare-bump counterexample is also not a truncation argument.  It is an exact mixture construction.

## 10.2 Operator norm and Bai--Yin

A uniform fourth moment gives coarse norm tightness by Latała, but global operator norm is not a dependency of the repaired fixed-FC theorem.  Bai--Yin is likewise a fixed-law global spectral-edge theorem, not a triangular microscopic anti-concentration theorem.

The current primary Bai--Yin statement assumes iid mean-zero variance-one entries with finite fourth moment and proves almost-sure sample-covariance extreme-edge limits.  Those hypotheses and conclusions are far coarser than the exponentially microscopic P13 statistic.

## 10.3 Latała

Latała's primary expected-norm theorem gives, for independent mean-zero entries,
\[
E\|A\|
\le C\left(
\max_i\sqrt{\sum_jEA_{ij}^2}
+
\max_j\sqrt{\sum_iEA_{ij}^2}
+
\left(\sum_{ij}E|A_{ij}|^4\right)^{1/4}
\right).
\tag{10.1}
\]
Under a uniform fourth-moment ceiling and `N=Theta(m)`, this is `O(sqrt(m))`.  It is useful for coarse geometry, not microscopic smoothing.

## 10.4 Lindeberg replacement

Generic matrix-level Lindeberg remains secondary.  The observable is nonsmooth at rank loss and minimizes over exponentially many overlapping row candidates.  More importantly, the narrow-lattice and rare-bump constructions show that microscopic resonances and deep logarithmic tails can change drastically while the first few moments remain essentially unchanged.

For expectation convergence under fixed finite convolution, Lindeberg is unnecessary: the scalar Levy tail plus tail integration is enough.

## 10.5 Robust small ball and leave-one-out

These remain primary.  The lower extreme freezes the adaptive span before applying probability to a genuinely fresh original column.  The constructive upper side likewise must retain genuinely fresh original rows; projected coordinates are never treated as iid.

## 10.6 Delocalization

Deterministic incompressibility, flatness, or low leverage is insufficient at source scale, as the retained Walsh/Rademacher examples show.  What remains important below the coarse fourth-moment threshold is the **random arithmetic geometry** of the actual leave-one-out normal spaces.

# 13. Adversarial checks for the new work

## 13.1 Candidate entropy in the UI proof

At `M=e^{-um/k}`, a power concentration exponent `alpha` contributes `e^{-alpha u m}` while candidate entropy is only `e^{h(gamma)m+o(m)}`.  Thus any fixed `alpha>0` wins for sufficiently large `u`.  The UI proof does not require the sharp source transition exponent.  PASS.

A merely finite but exponentially growing concentration constant is not silently accepted.  PASS.

\[

## 13.4 Rare-bump fourth moment

The normalized fourth moment is exactly `(8.10)`.  Since the base fourth moment is strictly below `B`, choosing one fixed `C_0` sufficiently large keeps the whole triangular family strictly below `B`.  No asymptotic-only moment statement is being substituted for a uniform one.  PASS.

## 13.5 Rare-bump probability coupling

The probability any of the `N_m m=O(m^2)` entries uses the rare component is at most `O(m^2e^{-m-C_0})`, which tends to zero.  Therefore convergence in probability is inherited from the fixed good base law.  PASS.

## 13.6 Rare event versus logarithmic magnitude

The damaging event uses only one predetermined candidate and one column; no candidate union or independence fiction is used.  Its probability is `p_m^{n_m}=exp[-m^2+o(m^2)]`, while the negative logarithm on it is at least `e^{3m^2}` up to polynomial factors.  Their product diverges like `exp[2m^2-o(m^2)]`.  PASS.

## 13.7 Smoothness and tails

Every level is a finite mixture of scaled `C_c^infty` densities.  The supports remain in one fixed compact interval after variance normalization.  Thus the family is uniformly subgaussian and has all positive moments uniformly bounded.  The counterexample is microscopic, not heavy-tail.  PASS.

## 13.8 No claim of necessity for the power profile

`P13-POWERCONC-LOG-UI-36` is a sufficient condition.  The report does not claim that a fixed power law is the exact weakest necessary UI hypothesis.  PASS.
