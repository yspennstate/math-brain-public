# 5. New one-column fourth-moment theorem

## `P13-ROBUST-ZERO4-41` — PROVED

Let `X` be any real random variable with

\[
EX^2=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Fix `rho>=0` and put

\[
q=P\{|X|\le\rho\}<1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
EX^4\ge\frac{(1-q\rho^2)^2}{1-q}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
E=\{|X|\le\rho\}.
\]

On `E`,

\[
E[X^2;E]\le q\rho^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Since `EX^2=1`,

\[
E[X^2;E^c]
=1-E[X^2;E]
\ge1-q\rho^2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on the complement gives

\[
\bigl(E[X^2;E^c]\bigr)^2
\le
P(E^c)E[X^4;E^c]
=(1-q)E[X^4;E^c].
\tag{5.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
EX^4
\ge E[X^4;E^c]
\ge
\frac{(1-q\rho^2)^2}{1-q},
\]

which is (5.3).

No centering, density, tail, smoothness, independence, or operator-norm hypothesis appears. At `rho=0`,

\[
EX^4\ge\frac1{1-q}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This endpoint is sharp: take mass `q` at zero and distribute the remaining mass symmetrically at `+/-1/sqrt(1-q)`.

namely

\[
p=\frac{1+\sqrt{2\beta-1}}2.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Let

\[
X=\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}
\quad\text{with probability }p,
\qquad
X=-\sqrt{\frac{p}{1-p}}
\quad\text{with probability }1-p.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Then `EX=0`, `EX^2=1`, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
EX^4
&=\frac{(1-p)^2}{p}+\frac{p^2}{1-p}\\
&=\frac1{p(1-p)}-3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.9}
\]

Since

\[
1-\beta=2p(1-p),
\]

we obtain

\[
EX^4
=\frac2{1-\beta}-3
=B_{\rm coll}(\gamma).
\tag{8.10}
\]

For an independent copy `X'`,

\[
P\{X=X'\}=p^2+(1-p)^2=\beta,
\tag{8.11}
\]

so for every `rho>0`,

\[
P\{|X-X'|\le\rho\}\ge\beta.
\tag{8.12}
\]

Again no uniform positive deficit in (0.2) exists.

At `gamma=3/2`, both branch thresholds equal `3`, and either construction witnesses failure at the boundary.

This completes the equivalence.

### 8.3 Interpretation

The result does **not** assert that these two scalar profiles characterize the full random-matrix theorem. It proves something narrower and exact: they characterize the entire fourth-moment threshold already known to govern the coarse/exact-relation obstruction. Therefore any counterexample strictly below `B_exact` must use structure beyond the two extremal scalar mechanisms that generate the threshold.

# 11. Primary-source hypothesis checks and route comparison

## 11.1 Public source theorem

Fresh check: Yang--Wen--Shmalo, arXiv:2608.07410 (7 August 2026), assumes a centered real subgaussian variable with positive variance and bounded Lebesgue density for the public `r=0` theorem. This matches the retained public-source description and does not certify the bounded-fourth rewrite.

## 11.2 Fernandez distance theorem / Randomized Logarithmic LCD

Fresh check: Manuel Fernandez V, *A distance theorem for inhomogenous random rectangular matrices*, arXiv:2408.06309. Its primary abstract assumes an `n x (n-d)` matrix with independent uniformly anti-concentrated entries and a Hilbert--Schmidt second-moment bound

\[
E|A|_{HS}^2\le K n(n-d),
\]

plus an independent vector with uniformly anti-concentrated entries. For

\[
1\le d\le\lambda n/\log n,
\]

it proves

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(X,H)\le t\sqrt d\}
\le(Ct)^d+e^{-cn}.
\tag{11.1}
\]

The work explicitly introduces **Randomized Logarithmic LCD**. This is the relevant primary-source structural tool for the unresolved P13 lower side. The current P13 debt is not to re-prove a fixed-candidate distance theorem but to reorganize its structural part **globally before choosing an exponentially numerous row candidate**.

## 11.3 Dabagia--Fernandez rectangular least singular value

Fresh check: Max Dabagia and Manuel Fernandez, *The smallest singular value of inhomogenous random rectangular matrices*, arXiv:2408.14389. The primary abstract assumes independent mean-zero entries, isotropic columns, and bounded `2+beta` moments and obtains the sharp codimension power plus an additive `e^{-cN}` term. Uniform fourth moment fits the `beta=2` hypothesis. This confirms that bounded fourth moment is already sufficient for strong **fixed-matrix/fixed-candidate** rectangular least-singular tails; the P13 difficulty is the extreme minimum over exponentially many overlapping row candidates and the structural exceptional event.

## 11.4 Bai--Yin-type control

Fresh check: Bai and Yin, *Limit of the Smallest Eigenvalue of a Large Dimensional Sample Covariance Matrix*, Annals of Probability 21(3), 1993, assumes iid mean-zero variance-one entries with finite fourth moment and proves almost-sure convergence of the global sample-covariance extreme eigenvalues to the Marchenko--Pastur edges. This is a global fixed-law spectral theorem. It does not imply source-scale anti-concentration at widths `exp[-Theta(m/k)]`, does not address arbitrary triangular microscopic resonances, and does not solve the exponentially overlapping candidate minimum.

## 11.5 Latała-type operator norm control

The retained primary result of Rafał Latała, *Some estimates of norms of random matrices*, Proc. AMS 133 (2005), gives for independent mean-zero entries an expected norm bound in terms of row/column second moments and the fourth-moment aggregate. For iid variance-one entries with a uniform fourth-moment ceiling and `N=Theta(m)`, this yields coarse `O(sqrt m)` operator-norm tightness. P13 uses such control only where a coarse norm event is genuinely needed (for example in a previous global compressibility/FineLCD conversion); it does not confuse it with microscopic small-ball control.

## 11.6 Route-by-route separation

### Truncation / replacement

No truncation or replacement is used in the new theorem. The retained negative conclusion remains: polynomial-scale entry truncation plus Weyl cannot prove the source exponent, because its deterministic perturbation scale is enormous compared with `exp[-Theta(m/k)]`.

### Operator norm

Uniform fourth moment can give coarse `O_P(sqrt m)` norm control, but both the earlier compact-support counterexamples and the present scalar threshold calculation show that the obstruction is anti-concentration / relation structure, not an oversized operator norm.

### Least singular value / small-ball

Fixed-candidate sharp powers are available from modern distance/least-singular theorems under anti-concentration or `2+beta` moments. The open difficulty is the structural additive error when one minimizes over `exp[h(gamma)m+o(m)]` heavily overlapping candidates. A candidate-by-candidate union bound is not justified unless the structural exponent beats `h(gamma)`, which current primary theorems do not assert uniformly.

### Delocalization

Deterministic flatness, low leverage, or incompressibility alone is not microscopic smoothing: the retained Walsh/Rademacher construction gives perfectly flat resonant subspaces. What can still help is randomness of the **actual source-generated normal geometry**.

### Lindeberg

A generic low-moment replacement argument is not a primary route. The retained narrow-lattice and rare-contamination examples show that two distributions with excellent low moments can differ radically at exponentially small scales. Any Lindeberg scheme would need a genuinely smoothed observable or a prior structural theorem that already controls the microscopic resonance.

### Robust small-ball + leave-one-out

This remains the viable positive architecture. The new theorem removes the two scalar extremal branches below `B_exact`; the next task is to exploit the fresh-column independence while controlling the law-adapted high-height normal structure globally.
