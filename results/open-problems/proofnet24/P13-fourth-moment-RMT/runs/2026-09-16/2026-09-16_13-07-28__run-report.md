## 2.2 Lower extreme in the source proof

For one candidate

\[
B\in\mathbb R^{(m+k-1)\times m}
\]

with columns `C_1,...,C_m`, let

\[
H_j=\operatorname{span}\{C_\ell:\ell\ne j\},
\qquad
d_j=\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j).
\]

The negative second-moment identity gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\sigma_i(B)^{-2}
=
\operatorname{tr}(B^TB)^{-1}
=
\sum_{j=1}^m d_j^{-2}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Hence `\sigma_m(B)\le t` forces `d_j\le\sqrt m\,t` for some `j`. After conditioning on the other columns, `H_j^\perp` has dimension `k` and the fresh column is independent. The source bounded-density projection theorem gives a fixed-candidate estimate of the form

\[
P\{d_j\le\sqrt m\,t\mid H_j\}
\le e^{O(k\log m)}t^k.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Meanwhile

\[
\log\binom{N_m}{m+k-1}=h(\gamma)m+o(m).
\tag{2.5}
\]

At `t=e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k}`, the microscopic factor in `(2.4)` beats the candidate entropy. The bounded-fourth program has to reproduce this microscopic `t^k` behavior without silently importing subgaussian normal geometry.

# 5. `P13-FRESH-GLOBAL-REGHS-48` — full proof

Fix `m>=2` and let

\[
n=n_m=m+k_m-1.
\]

For each held-out coordinate `j in [m]`, define

\[
Y_i^{(j)}
:=
\frac{\|R_{i,-j}\|_2^2}{m-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because the entries have second moment one,

\[
E Y_i^{(j)}=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For fixed `j`, the variables `Y_i^{(j)}` are independent over `i` and are measurable with respect to `A_{-j}`.

Take `H` as in `(0.4)` and put

\[
\alpha_i^{(j)}
:=
\min\left\{1,\sqrt{H/Y_i^{(j)}}\right\},
\qquad
W_i^{(j)}
:=
-\log\alpha_i^{(j)}
=
\frac12\log_+\frac{Y_i^{(j)}}H.
\tag{5.3}
\]

As before,

\[
E e^{2W_i^{(j)}}\le1+1/H.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
P\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}W_i^{(j)}>n\log\kappa\right\}
&\le
\exp[-2n\log\kappa]
(1+1/H)^{N_m}\\
&\le
\exp[-2n\log\kappa+N_m/H]\\
&\le
\exp[-n\log\kappa]
=\kappa^{-n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The last step uses `N_m/n<=Gamma_0` and `Gamma_0/H<=log kappa`.

Take a union bound over the `m` possible held-out columns. With probability at least `1-m kappa^{-n}`, every `j` satisfies

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}W_i^{(j)}\le n\log\kappa.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Now fix any candidate `T`, `|T|=n`. Because all weights are nonnegative,

\[
\sum_{i\in T}W_i^{(j)}
\le
\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}W_i^{(j)}
\le n\log\kappa.
\]

Therefore

\[
\prod_{i\in T}\alpha_i^{(j)}
\ge\kappa^{-n}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the restricted weight vector belongs to `Omega_kappa` for the transpose

\[
B_{T,j}:=(A_m)_{T,-j}^{T}\in\mathbb R^{(m-1)\times n}.
\]

Moreover each of its columns is `R_{i,-j}`, so

\[
(\alpha_i^{(j)})^2\|R_{i,-j}\|_2^2
\le H(m-1).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Summing over `i in T` gives

\[
\mathcal B_\kappa(B_{T,j})
\le
H(m-1)n.
\tag{5.9}
\]

If `Q B_{T,j}` is any coordinate-row restriction appearing later in the Fernandez recursion, then

\[
\|Q R_{i,-j}\|_2\le\|R_{i,-j}\|_2,
\]

so the same weights still prove `(5.9)`.

Finally, every `\alpha^{(j)}` is a function only of `A_{-j}`. Since P13 has iid entries, `A_j` is independent of `A_{-j}`. Hence the global regularized-HS event for held-out column `j` is leave-one-out measurable and does not contaminate the fresh-column small-ball step.

This proves `P13-FRESH-GLOBAL-REGHS-48`.

### 5.1 Why second moment is enough

A rare enormous row is not removed. Its weight is approximately inverse row norm, so its **logarithmic** cost is `O(log ||R||)`. The identity `(5.4)` converts only a second-moment assumption into an exponential tail for the sum of those logarithmic costs. This is fully compatible with the retained counterexample showing that uniform B4 cannot give an exponentially reliable full operator-norm bound.

# 9. Heavy-tail obstructions and minimum hypotheses preserved

No validated negative result is discarded.

## 9.1 Levelwise finite fourth moment is not enough

The retained sparse triangular family with mass `1-1/m` near zero and mass `1/(2m)` near each of `+-sqrt(m)` has

\[
E\xi_m^2\asymp1,
\qquad
E|\xi_m|^4\asymp m<\infty
\]

at each level but no uniform B4 ceiling. After exact normalization and arbitrarily narrow smooth compactly supported microjitter, it still produces enough almost-zero rows to make a candidate catastrophically small. Therefore “finite fourth moment” level by level is **DISPROVED** as a triangular-array replacement; “bounded fourth moment” must mean a uniform numerical ceiling.

## 9.2 Uniform B4 does not give a subgaussian norm event

The retained rare-spike construction puts spikes of size `Theta(sqrt(m))` at probability `Theta(m^{-2})`. It keeps a fixed fourth-moment ceiling but gives a positive limiting probability that the full-matrix operator norm exceeds any prescribed fixed multiple of `sqrt(m)`. Thus one cannot replace a subgaussian full-matrix norm tail by uniform B4. Those spike rows are nevertheless row-trimmable, so this is a proof-route obstruction, not by itself a counterexample to the P13 exponent.

## 9.3 Exact relation threshold

For `1<gamma<2`, pure moment-only triangular universality is already **DISPROVED** at and above the retained threshold

\[
B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\gamma}{\gamma-1},&1<\gamma\le3/2,\\[5pt]
\dfrac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1},&3/2<\gamma<2.
\end{cases}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Below this threshold the known one-column zero, two-copy collision, fixed scalar-cluster, compressible, and low-height lattice mechanisms are controlled. For `gamma>=2`, balanced Rademacher already supplies a fourth-moment-one obstruction to unrestricted moment-only universality.

These obstructions are not contradicted by the new structural RLogLCD theorem: RLogLCD reliability is one module in the lower-side proof, not a theorem that all B4 laws obey the source exponent.

## 9.4 Weakest currently complete sufficient hypothesis

The cleanest complete sufficient microscopic hypothesis remains one fixed bounded convolution power. It gives the full real source theorem under second moments only. Whether a weaker assumption than finite convolution smoothing, but stronger than a bare B4 ceiling, is the true minimal condition is still OPEN.

# 10. Route separation

## Truncation / replacement

Polynomial truncation plus Weyl perturbation remains invalid at the source scale. A polynomial perturbation norm is enormously larger than `exp[-Theta(m/k)]`. Truncation may be useful as an internal robustification device, but not as a direct spectral replacement argument.

## Operator norm and Bai--Yin

Uniform B4 gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` norm tightness (and fixed-law Bai--Yin gives macroscopic edge asymptotics), but neither gives the candidate-uniform microscopic anti-concentration required here. The new regularized-HS theorem is deliberately row-weighted and does not ask for a realized full operator-norm event.

## Least singular value / small ball

Fixed-candidate sharp rectangular least-singular tails under bounded `2+beta` moments are available in Dabagia--Fernandez. P13's problem is the exponentially many overlapping candidates and the source-scale arithmetic floor, not the fixed matrix theorem.

## Delocalization

Classical incompressibility is now globally available under B4. It is not enough by itself: the retained balanced-Rademacher flat-vector examples have strong coordinate spread yet low arithmetic LCD. The live structural module is law-adapted RLogLCD for actual random null normals.

## Lindeberg

A generic low-moment matrix Lindeberg replacement remains nonprimary. Matching low moments does not preserve narrow lattice or cluster resonances at exponentially small radii.

## Robust small ball and leave-one-out

This remains the viable lower-side architecture. The new theorem improves it by making the regularized-HS event explicitly measurable with respect to the deleted-column matrix, so the held-out column remains genuinely fresh.

## Expectation versus high probability

The retained rare-contamination construction still shows that correct convergence in probability need not imply convergence of normalized logarithmic expectations, even under smooth compact support, uniform subgaussianity, and uniform B4. No expectation theorem is inferred from the high-probability program.

# 12. Tests and falsification checks performed this run

1. **Candidate product direction.** Because `W_i>=0`, restricting from all `N` rows to a candidate `T` can only decrease `sum W_i`, hence can only increase the product of `alpha_i`. This is the correct direction for `Omega_kappa`.
2. **Energy scaling.** `Y_i^{(j)}` is normalized by `m-1`, so the weighted column energy is exactly at most `H(m-1)`; summing `n` selected rows gives `(0.6)`.
3. **Probability exponent.** With `H>=Gamma_0/log kappa`, `N/H<=n log kappa`, so the Chernoff exponent is at most `-n log kappa`; the extra union over `m` held-out columns is negligible.
4. **Freshness.** `alpha^{(j)}` depends only on entries outside column `j`; it is independent of the fresh column used in the distance small ball. This fixes the subtle dependence issue left implicit by full-row weights.
5. **Recursive restrictions.** Removing coordinate rows from `(A_{T,-j})^T` only reduces each weighted column norm, so the same weights control all such submatrices.
6. **No hidden fourth moment.** The proof of Section 5 uses only `E Y_i^{(j)}=1`; no variance of `Y`, no row fourth moment, and no operator norm are used.
7. **Lattice-spacing sanity check.** The new globalization holds at the fixed published `kappa=3`; no `kappa->infinity` step is made, so the previously audited `6^{-n}` lattice-spacing issue is not reintroduced.
8. **Entropy accounting.** Shared events (`G_j`, global incompressibility) are paid once and therefore need only have probability tending to one; candidate-specific low-RLogLCD level sets alone must beat `exp(h(gamma)m+o(m))`.
9. **No false closure from reliability.** The height floor `(8.6)` is retained explicitly, so arbitrary structural reliability is not mistaken for the source-scale lower theorem.
