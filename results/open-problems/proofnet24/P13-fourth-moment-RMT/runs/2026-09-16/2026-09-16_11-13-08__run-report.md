# 7. Uniform fourth moment supplies Fernandez UAC with an exact constant

Let `xi` be centered with

\[
E\xi^2=1,
\qquad
E\xi^4\le B.
\]

For an independent copy `xi'` and `Z=xi-xi'`,

\[
EZ^2=2,
\qquad
EZ^4=2E\xi^4+6(E\xi^2)^2\le2B+6.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Paley--Zygmund on `Z^2` at half its mean gives

\[
P\{|Z|\ge1\}
\ge
\frac{(1-1/2)^2(EZ^2)^2}{EZ^4}
\ge
\frac1{2B+6}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

If two independent copies lie in the same open radius-`1/2` interval, then `|Z|<1`. Therefore

\[
\left(\sup_zP\{|\xi-z|<1/2\}\right)^2
\le
1-\frac1{2B+6},
\]

which proves `(0.7)`.

For `Y=2xi`,

\[
\sup_zP\{|Y-z|<1\}\le b_B.
\]

Furthermore

\[
EY^2=4,
\]

so for an `n x (n-d)` iid `Y` matrix

\[
E\|A\|_{HS}^2=4n(n-d),
\]

and a fresh `n`-coordinate probe has expected squared norm `4n`. These meet Fernandez's expected-HS / second-moment hypotheses uniformly over the whole B4 class.

This step uses **uniform** B4. If `E|xi_m|^4` is merely finite level by level but tends to infinity, the constant `b_B<1` disappears, exactly matching the retained finite-versus-uniform counterexample.

Then `(A_m)_{T,-j}` is `n x (n-d)`. Scale its entries by two; its column span and left nullspace are unchanged. By Section 7 it satisfies Fernandez UAC / expected-HS assumptions with constants depending only on `B`.

By Section 8, on the already-global incompressibility event every unit vector of the candidate nullspace is in a fixed `Incomp(delta,rho)` class.

Choose

\[
a>h(\gamma)+2.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Apply `P13-FERNANDEZ-ARBITRARY-RELIABILITY-46` to this deterministic pair. Since

\[
k_m\log m=o(m),
\]

one has

\[
d=k_m\le\lambda n/\log n
\]

for all sufficiently large `m`, regardless of how small the fixed positive `lambda(a,B,gamma)` becomes.

Thus the probability that this pair has an incompressible null vector below the RLogLCD height in `(0.6)` is at most

\[
e^{-an}.

The number of pairs is

\[
m\binom{N_m}{n_m}.

Because `n_m/m->1` and `N_m/m->gamma`, uniform Stirling gives

\[
\log\binom{N_m}{n_m}

m\binom{N_m}{n_m}e^{-an_m}

which is exponentially small by `(9.1)`. Combining with `(8.1)` proves the theorem.

# 10. Proof of `P13-B4-RLOG-TRADEOFF-46`

Fix a candidate `T` and write

\[
B=(A_m)_T\in\mathbb R^{n_m\times m}.
\]

For each column `B_j`, let

\[
H_j=\operatorname{span}\{B_\ell:\ell\ne j\}.
\]

The negative-second-moment identity is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^m\operatorname{dist}(B_j,H_j)^{-2}
=
\sum_{i=1}^m s_i(B)^{-2}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

If `s_m(B)<=tau`, the right side is at least `tau^{-2}`. If all column distances exceeded `sqrt(m) tau`, the left side would be strictly smaller than `tau^{-2}`. Therefore `(0.10)` holds.

Now condition on `B_{-j}` only. The held-out column `B_j` is still a genuinely fresh iid product vector. On the global event `(0.6)`, its orthogonal residual subspace

\[
E_j=H_j^\perp
\]

has dimension at least `k_m` and every unit direction in the relevant `k_m`-dimensional residual space has RLogLCD at least

\[
D(E_j)\ge c_0\sqrt{k_m}\,e^{c_{\rm ht}m/k_m}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Fernandez Corollary 4.3 has the intrinsic normalized radius floor

\[
t_0=\frac{\sqrt{k_m}}{D(E_j)}
\le C e^{-c_{\rm ht}m/k_m}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

At physical distance `sqrt(m) tau`, the normalized radius is

\[
t=\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_m}}\,\tau.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Since `L_m/sqrt(k_m)=O(1)`, Corollary 4.3 yields

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(B_j,H_j)\le\sqrt m\tau\mid B_{-j}\}
\le
C^{k_m}\max\{t,t_0\}^{k_m}.
\]

Using `max(x,y)^k<=x^k+y^k` up to an absorbed factor gives `(0.11)`.

For `(0.9)`,

\[
\log\left[
C^{k_m}
\left(\sqrt{m/k_m}\,\tau_m(\varepsilon)\right)^{k_m}
\right]
=
-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m+o(m),
\]

because `k_m log m=o(m)`. The floor term has logarithm

\[
-c_{\rm ht}m+o(m).
\]

Unioning over columns and candidates adds exactly `h(gamma)m+o(m)`, producing `(0.12)` and the sufficient condition `(0.13)`.

\text{ without destroying the structural reliability }a>h(\gamma)?

\tag{10.5}

The public proof does not expose enough dependence of the Corollary 5.10 thinning parameter / final `vartheta` on `U,kappa` to answer `(10.5)` for all strict-subcritical `(B,gamma)`. This exact dependency is recorded as inaccessible rather than invented.

The current logically strongest statement is not a single blanket “bounded fourth moment theorem.” It is the following separation.

### Complete positive theorem with microscopic smoothing

Under centering, variance one, and one fixed bounded convolution power, the full source law `(0.1)` is mathematically proved with no moment above two.

### Pure moment-only triangular universality

- for `1<gamma<2`, counterexamples exist at and above `B_exact(gamma)`;

For `1<gamma<2` and

\[
B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),
\]

all currently identified coarse/low-complexity obstructions are excluded: deterministic row relations, scalar threshold extremizers, coordinate-pair amplification, left-kernel compressibility, right-vector compressibility, and low-height ordinary lattice relations. The remaining lower-side issue is the height coefficient `(10.5)`. The constructive upper extreme under pure B4 is separately OPEN.

### Minimum extra hypothesis currently proved sufficient

The cleanest complete extra microscopic condition remains:

> one fixed convolution depth whose density is uniformly bounded, plus centering and variance one.

This is sufficient with no fourth moment. Whether a substantially weaker law-adapted microscopic condition is both sufficient and close to necessary remains OPEN.

# 12. Route comparison and separation of proof roles

## 12.1 Truncation / replacement

Polynomial truncation followed by Weyl perturbation remains invalid at the source scale: a polynomial entrywise/matrix perturbation is vastly larger than `exp[-Theta(m/k)]`. The successful lower-side RLog route performs no such perturbation. Row-aware trimming may still be useful for the constructive upper extreme.

## 12.2 Operator norm

Uniform B4 gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` norm control, but `P13-B4-NORMTAIL-NOGO-43` shows it does not give the subgaussian exponential full-matrix norm tail uniformly over triangular laws. The new structural proof needs only expected Hilbert--Schmidt control and global incompressibility, not an exponentially good realized norm event.

## 12.3 Bai--Yin

Bai--Yin-type edge control is appropriate for one fixed iid law under finite fourth moment and macroscopic spectral questions. It does not provide the source-scale triangular anti-concentration or candidate-uniform arithmetic structure needed here. It remains a fixed-law coarse tool, not the microscopic mechanism.

## 12.4 Lindeberg

A generic low-moment matrix Lindeberg replacement remains nonprimary. The retained narrow-cluster, exact-relation, and Rademacher examples show that microscopic lattice/cluster geometry can change while low moments are preserved. Any replacement argument would need a source-scale smoothing hypothesis strong enough to control precisely the structure the moment-only program is testing.

## 12.5 Robust small ball / leave one out

This remains the viable moment-only lower route. Conditioning on `B_{-j}` preserves a genuinely fresh product column `B_j`. Fernandez RLogLCD converts the random-kernel arithmetic of `B_{-j}` into the correct `t^k` projection small-ball power plus an arithmetic floor. The present run shows the structural exception can be made reliable enough for all candidates; the height floor is the exact remaining quantitative issue.

## 12.6 Delocalization

Classical incompressibility is globally available under uniform B4. It is necessary for the arithmetic machinery but insufficient by itself: the flat Rademacher resonant-cap theorem gives maximally delocalized vectors with only polynomial RLogLCD. Random-kernel structure, not stronger deterministic spread, is the needed input.

## 12.7 Expectation versus high probability

The retained rare-contamination example shows correct probability convergence does not imply normalized logarithmic expectation convergence even with smooth compact support, uniform subgaussianity, and uniform B4. The expectation theorem requires a separate deep-scale scalar concentration / uniform-integrability condition.

## 12.8 Finite fourth moment versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

This distinction is exact and noncosmetic. Uniform B4 supplies the fixed anti-concentration constant `(0.7)`, coarse norm tightness, and uniform empirical incompressibility. A triangular family with `E|xi_m|^4<infinity` at every `m` but `sup_m E|xi_m|^4=infinity` can lose all of these uniform constants and can destroy the source law.

# 14. Sanity checks / tests

### 14.1 Entropy identity

For `n_m=m+o(m)` and `N_m/gamma m->1`,

\[
\frac1m\log\binom{N_m}{n_m}
\to
\gamma H(1/\gamma)
=
\gamma\log\gamma-(\gamma-1)\log(\gamma-1)
=h(\gamma).
\]

This is valid for every fixed `gamma>1`, including `gamma>=2`.

### 14.2 Codimension range

From `k_m log m=o(m)`, for every fixed `lambda>0`,

\[
\frac{k_m}{m/\log m}\to0,
\]

so the Fernandez condition `k_m<=lambda n_m/log n_m` eventually holds even if reliability tuning makes `lambda` extremely small.

### 14.3 Main small-ball exponent

At `tau=e^{-(h+epsilon)m/k}`,

\[
\left(C\sqrt{m/k}\,\tau\right)^k
=
\exp[-(h+\varepsilon)m+o(m)],
\]

since `k log m=o(m)`. Candidate entropy exactly cancels the `h m` portion and leaves `e^{-epsilon m+o(m)}`.

### 14.4 Arithmetic floor

If `D(E)>=c sqrt(k)e^{c_ht m/k}`, then

\[
t_0=\sqrt{k}/D(E)\le C e^{-c_{ht}m/k}
\]

and

\[
C^k t_0^k=e^{-c_{ht}m+o(m)}.
\]

Thus the comparison with candidate entropy is genuinely `c_ht` versus `h(gamma)`; no hidden factor `k` remains.

### 14.5 No contradiction with supercritical counterexamples

The new theorem controls **left residual arithmetic structure**. A repeated/near-repeated pair of columns is a right-vector relation and can make `s_m` small even if every left residual subspace has high RLogLCD. Therefore `(0.6)` is compatible with all retained B4 counterexamples.
