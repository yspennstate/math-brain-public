# 4. Why the newest P12 sparse theorem does not by itself finish P13

P12's fourth-moment height calculation is valuable but leaves two distinctions that matter here.

First, its support-dependent moment ceiling is explicit. For centered variance-one real `X`, `EX^4<=B`, and normalized `c`, let

\[
\kappa(c)=\sum_jc_j^4.
\]

Then

\[
E\left(\sum_jc_jX_j\right)^4
\le3+(B-3)\kappa(c).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For vectors supported on at most `s` coordinates, the worst ceiling is

\[
C_{B,s}
=
\max\left\{B,\ 3+\frac{B-3}{s}\right\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This gives an explicit zero-centered small-ball gap whenever

\[
C_{B,s}<B_0(\gamma),
\qquad
B_0(\gamma)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Second, conditioning on the rest of a linear form shifts the two-coordinate target. A zero-centered estimate for `aX+bX'` is **not** automatically an all-center estimate for `aX+bX'-z`. Section 8 gives a strict subcritical counterexample to that shortcut.

The retained P13 theorem (2.4) is stronger in a different direction: it is already uniform over **all support sizes**, but only for homogeneous zero-centered forms. The new work below exploits exactly the homogeneous structure of singular vectors and never inserts an unjustified shifted-center conditioning step.

# 6. New theorem A: global sparse primal exclusion from the all-support gap

The key point is to use rows as the empirical sample and choose the row candidate only **after** obtaining a uniform slab-count statement.

## `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-GLOBAL-PRIMAL-SPARSE-42` — NEW / PROVED

Assume

\[
1<\gamma<2,
\qquad
1\le B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),
\tag{6.1}
\]

and let `A_m in R^{N_m x m}` have iid centered variance-one entries with

\[
\sup_mE|\xi_m|^4\le B,
\qquad
N_m/m\to\gamma.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
n_m=m+k_m-1,
\qquad
k_m\log m=o(m),
\qquad
\beta_m=\frac{n_m}{N_m},
\qquad
\beta=\frac1\gamma.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then there exist fixed constants

\[
\alpha>0,
\qquad
a_0>0,
\qquad c>0,
\tag{6.4}
\]

depending only on `(B,gamma)` such that for all sufficiently large `m`, with probability at least `1-e^{-cm}`, simultaneously for every

\[
T\subset[N_m],\quad |T|=n_m,
\]

and every unit vector `x in R^m` with

\[
|\operatorname{supp}x|\le\alpha m,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\|(A_m)_T x\|_2\ge a_0\sqrt m.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Equivalently, for every nonempty `J subset[m]` with `|J|<=alpha m`,

\[
\boxed{
\min_{|T|=n_m}
\sigma_{|J|}((A_m)_{T,J})
\ge a_0\sqrt m.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

### Step 1. Population slab gap

Use the retained `P13-B4-ALLSUPPORT-COARSE-GAP-31`. There are fixed `rho_0,delta_0>0` such that for every support size `j`, every unit `x in R^j`, and every level `m`,

\[
P\{|\langle R^{(j)},x\rangle|\le\rho_0\}
\le
\beta-\delta_0,
\tag{6.7}
\]

where `R^{(j)}` is a row restricted to any `j` coordinates.

The uniformity in `j` is essential. No support-dependent fourth-moment margin is needed from this point onward.

### Step 2. Shatter coefficient of centered slabs

Fix a support `J` of size `j`. On `R^j` consider the centered slab class

\[
\mathcal S_J
=
\left\{
\{y:|\langle y,x\rangle|\le\rho_0\}:
 x\in S^{j-1}
\right\}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

A slab is the intersection of two affine halfspaces. The affine-halfspace class in `R^j` has VC dimension `j+1`, hence on any `N` points its number of traces is at most

\[
\sum_{\ell=0}^{j+1}\binom N\ell
\le
\left(\frac{eN}{j+1}\right)^{j+1}
\tag{6.9}
\]

when `j+1<=N`. Therefore

\[
\Pi_{\mathcal S_J}(N)
\le
\left(\frac{eN}{j+1}\right)^{2(j+1)}.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The standard VC deviation inequality gives, for every fixed `J`,

\[
P\left\{
\sup_{S\in\mathcal S_J}
|P_N(S)-P(S)|>\frac{\delta_0}{4}
\right\}
\le
8\Pi_{\mathcal S_J}(N_m)
\exp\left[-\frac{N_m\delta_0^2}{512}\right].
\tag{6.11}
\]

Here `P_N` is the empirical measure of the `N_m` iid restricted rows.

### Step 3. Union over a positive fraction of column supports

For large `m`, choose fixed constants

\[
0<\underline\gamma<\gamma<\Gamma<\infty
\]

with

\[
\underline\gamma m\le N_m\le\Gamma m.
\]

For `j<=alpha m`, union over the `binom(m,j)` supports. The logarithmic cost is bounded by

\[
\log\binom mj
+2(j+1)\log\left(\frac{eN_m}{j+1}\right).
\tag{6.12}
\]

After division by `m`, the supremum of this quantity over `1<=j<=alpha m` tends to zero as `alpha downarrow0`; more concretely it is bounded by a constant multiple of

\[
H(\alpha)+\alpha\log\frac{e\Gamma}{\alpha}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Choose `alpha=alpha(B,gamma)>0` so small that the support-plus-shatter entropy in (6.12) is less than half of

\[
\frac{\underline\gamma\delta_0^2}{512}m
\]

for all large `m`. Summing over `j<=alpha m` gives an event `G_m` satisfying

\[
P(G_m^c)\le e^{-cm}
\tag{6.14}
\]

on which, simultaneously for **every** unit vector supported on at most `alpha m` coordinates,

\[
\frac1{N_m}
\#\{i:|\langle R_i,x\rangle|\le\rho_0\}
\le
\beta-\frac{3\delta_0}{4}.
\tag{6.15}
\]

This is the global step. There is no row-candidate union bound.

### Step 4. Every candidate contains linearly many large rows

Since `k_m=o(m)` and `N_m/m->gamma`,

\[
\beta_m\to\beta.
\]

For all large `m`,

\[
\beta_m\ge\beta-\frac{\delta_0}{4}.
\tag{6.16}
\]

Take any candidate `T` of size `n_m=beta_m N_m` and any unit `alpha m`-sparse `x`. By (6.15), the total number of rows in the entire matrix satisfying the small-slab inequality is at most

\[
\left(\beta-\frac{3\delta_0}{4}\right)N_m.
\]

Therefore `T` contains at least

\[
\left[
\beta_m-\beta+\frac{3\delta_0}{4}
\right]N_m
\ge
\frac{\delta_0}{2}N_m
\tag{6.17}
\]

rows with

\[
|\langle R_i,x\rangle|>\rho_0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\|(A_m)_T x\|_2^2
\ge
\rho_0^2\frac{\delta_0}{2}N_m.

\[
\boxed{
a_0
=
\rho_0\sqrt{\frac{\delta_0\gamma}{4}}.

\tag{6.19}

This proves (6.5) and (6.6).

# 7. New theorem B: global primal compressibility exclusion

## `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-GLOBAL-PRIMAL-COMP-42` — NEW / PROVED

Under the hypotheses of Section 6, for every `zeta>0` there exist fixed

\[
r=r(B,\gamma,\zeta)>0,
\qquad
a_1=a_1(B,\gamma)>0
\tag{7.1}
\]

such that, for all large `m`, with probability at least

\[
1-\zeta-e^{-cm},
\tag{7.2}
\]

simultaneously for every candidate `T` and every

\[
x\in\operatorname{Comp}_m(\alpha,r),
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\|(A_m)_T x\|_2\ge a_1\sqrt m.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Proof

Latała's theorem for independent centered entries states

\[
E\|A_m\|
\le
C\left(
\max_i\sqrt{\sum_jEA_{ij}^2}
+
\max_j\sqrt{\sum_iEA_{ij}^2}
+
\Big(\sum_{ij}E|A_{ij}|^4\Big)^{1/4}
\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Under `N_m=Theta(m)` and `sup E|xi_m|^4<=B`, this gives

\[
E\|A_m\|\le C_{B,\gamma}\sqrt m.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Given `zeta>0`, choose `K=K(B,gamma,zeta)` so that by Markov

\[
P\{\|A_m\|>K\sqrt m\}\le\zeta.
\tag{7.6}

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(A_m)_T x\|_2
&\ge
\|(A_m)_Tz\|_2
-
\|(A_m)_T(x-z)\|_2\\
&\ge
 a_0\sqrt m-Kr\sqrt m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Choose

\[
r\le\frac{a_0}{4K}.
\tag{7.8}

Then one may take, for example,

\[
\boxed{a_1=3a_0/4.}
\tag{7.9}
\]

This proves the theorem.

# 8. New route-kill: all-center concentration is not controlled at the critical row fraction

The homogeneous qualifier in (2.4) cannot be dropped.

## `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-ALLCENTER-NOGO-42` — NEW / PROVED

For every

\[
1<\gamma<2,
\qquad
\beta=1/\gamma,
\]

there exists a centered variance-one real law `X` satisfying

\[
EX^4<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)
\tag{8.1}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\sup_zP\{X=z\}>\beta.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Consequently, no theorem of the form

\[
\sup_zP\{|X-z|\le\rho\}
\le\frac1\gamma-\delta
\tag{8.3}
\]

can follow from the strict subcritical fourth-moment condition alone.

### Proof

For `p in (1/2,1)`, let `X_p` be the standardized centered two-point law

\[
X_p=
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{\dfrac{1-p}{p}},&\text{with probability }p,\\[7pt]
-\sqrt{\dfrac{p}{1-p}},&\text{with probability }1-p.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Then

\[
EX_p=0,
\qquad
EX_p^2=1,
\]

and

\[
J(p):=EX_p^4
=
\frac{p^2}{1-p}+
\frac{(1-p)^2}{p}
=
\frac1{p(1-p)}-3.
\tag{8.5}
\]

At `p=beta`,

\[
J(\beta)
=
\frac{\gamma^2-3\gamma+3}{\gamma-1}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Compare this with both branches of `B_exact`.

If `1<gamma<=3/2`,

\[
\frac\gamma{\gamma-1}-J(\beta)
=3-\gamma>0.
\tag{8.7}
\]

If `3/2<=gamma<2`,

\[
\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1}-J(\beta)
=
\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma)}{\gamma-1}>0.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Hence `J(beta)<B_exact(gamma)` throughout `(1,2)`. By continuity choose

\[
p>\beta
\]

sufficiently close to `beta` that

\[
J(p)<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\]

But the first atom in (8.4) has mass `p>beta`, proving (8.2).

### Smooth triangular strengthening

Let `Z` be a fixed centered compactly supported `C^infty` variable and let `eta_m=e^{-m^2}`. Put

\[
\xi_m
=
\frac{X_p+\eta_mZ}
{\sqrt{1+\eta_m^2EZ^2}}.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Then every `xi_m` has a smooth compactly supported density, the family is uniformly subgaussian, and

\[
E\xi_m^4=J(p)+O(e^{-2m^2}).
\tag{8.10}
\]

After patching finitely many initial levels, one has a uniform fourth-moment budget strictly below `B_exact`. Nevertheless a ball of radius `O(eta_m)` around the first nonzero cluster contains probability asymptotic to `p>1/gamma`.

So even smooth triangular laws below the sharp homogeneous obstruction threshold can have an **all-center** microscopic concentration above the critical row fraction.

### Why this does not contradict Section 6

The singular relation is homogeneous. Testing one column against zero requires `A_{ij}` to be small around **zero**, not around an arbitrary nonzero atom. Likewise `aX+bX'` arising from a homogeneous two-column relation is evaluated at zero.

The counterexample kills only a proof shortcut: after conditioning on other coordinates, the remaining two-variable equation has a shifted target, and one cannot replace the required shifted estimate by P12's centered two-sparse theorem.

# 9. RLogLCD attack: exact progress and exact remaining obstruction

This section records the attempted continuation of `P13-B4-GLOBAL-RLCD-42` and why the new result is a reduction rather than a closure.

## 9.1 Current primary definition

Fernandez's current Randomized Logarithmic LCD for a deterministic vector `v in R^N` is

\[
\operatorname{RLogLCD}^{X}_{L,u}(v)
=
\inf\left\{
\theta>0:
E\operatorname{dist}^2(
\theta v\star\bar X,
\mathbb Z^N)
<
L^2\log_+\left(
\frac{u\|\theta v\|_2}{L}
\right)
\right\},
\tag{9.1}
\]

where `bar X` is the symmetrized source vector and the product is coordinatewise.

For a fixed `n x (n-d)` matrix with independent uniformly anti-concentrated entries, Hilbert--Schmidt second-moment control, and

\[
1\le d\le\lambda n/\log n,
\]

Fernandez proves that incompressible null normals have RLogLCD at least exponential height, with exceptional probability `e^{-eta n}`, and obtains

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(X,H)\le t\sqrt d\}
\le(Ct)^d+e^{-cn}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

In the P13 candidate problem, `d=k_m` and the theorem is perfectly aligned **for one fixed row candidate**. The difficulty is that the `e^{-cn}` structural error cannot simply be paid independently over `exp[h(gamma)m+o(m)]` overlapping candidates.

## 9.2 What is now globally excluded

Below `B_exact`, the cumulative P13 chain now excludes globally:

6. by Section 7, a fixed neighborhood of that entire sparse right-vector class.

Thus any remaining moment-only lower-edge obstruction is simultaneously:

- left-normal incompressible;
- right-vector incompressible;
- beyond the already-excluded low ordinary-LCD heights;

## 9.3 Failed shortcut: condition down to two variables and use a centered gap

A natural attempt was to use the P12 two-sparse theorem after conditioning all but two coordinates of a low-RLogLCD vector. This fails rigorously because conditioning changes the target center. Section 8 shows that a strict subcritical fourth moment does not control all-center concentration below `1/gamma`; indeed a nonzero atom can exceed that fraction while the fourth moment stays well below `B_exact`.

Therefore the remaining RLogLCD proof must preserve the **homogeneous/law-adapted** structure instead of replacing it by an arbitrary shifted scalar concentration estimate.

The only geometry-specific input needed here is the elementary halfspace shatter bound and the observation that a slab is an intersection of two halfspaces, giving (6.10). No distributional moment is hidden inside the VC step.
