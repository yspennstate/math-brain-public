# 4. New theorem: projective two-column slab obstruction

Let `xi_m'` be an independent copy of `xi_m`. For `rho>=0` define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Q_m(\rho)
=
\sup_{a^2+b^2=1}
P\{|a\xi_m+b\xi_m'|\le\rho\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the natural homogeneous two-coordinate anti-concentration quantity for singular vectors supported on two columns.

## `P13-TWOCOLUMN-PROJECTIVE-OBSTRUCTION-29` — PROVED

Assume

\[
N_m/m\to\gamma>1,
\qquad
k_m=r_m+1,
\qquad
k_m\log m=o(m).
\]

Suppose along a subsequence there are unit pairs `(a_m,b_m)`, radii `rho_m`, and one fixed

\[
p>1/\gamma
\]

such that

\[
P\{|a_m\xi_m+b_m\xi_m'|\le\rho_m\}\ge p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Then for some `c=c(gamma,p)>0`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le
\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\rho_m
\right\}
\ge1-e^{-cm}
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

for all large indices of that subsequence.

### Proof

Use only the first two columns. For row `i` define

\[
E_i
=
\{|a_mA_{i1}+b_mA_{i2}|\le\rho_m\}.
\]

The row events are iid and have probability at least `p`. Let

\[
C_m=\sum_{i=1}^{N_m}1_{E_i}.
\]

Because `k_m log m=o(m)` implies `k_m=o(m)`,

\[
\frac{m+r_m}{N_m}
=
\frac{m+k_m-1}{N_m}
\longrightarrow
\frac1\gamma<p.
\]

A binomial Chernoff bound therefore gives

\[
P\{C_m<m+r_m\}\le e^{-cm}.
\]

On `C_m>=m+r_m`, choose any `T` of exactly `m+r_m` successful rows and let

\[
x_m=(a_m,b_m,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb R^m.
\]

Then `||x_m||_2=1` and every selected row satisfies

\[
|(A_Tx_m)_i|\le\rho_m.
\]

Hence

\[
\|A_Tx_m\|_2
\le
\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\rho_m,
\]

and therefore

\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le
\sigma_m(A_T)
\le
\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\rho_m.
\]

No centering, moment, density, tail, delocalization or operator-norm hypothesis is used. QED.

\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}

Then for every `epsilon>0`, with

\[
\rho_m(\varepsilon)
=

one necessarily has

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\mathcal Q_m(\rho_m(\varepsilon))
\le

\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

If `(5.3)` failed, some subsequence and some fixed `p>1/gamma` would satisfy `(4.2)` at radius `(5.2)`. Then `(4.3)` would imply, with probability tending to one,

\[
M_{m,r_m}
\le
\exp[-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k_m],

or equivalently

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}
\le
-h(\gamma)-\varepsilon,

contradicting `(5.1)`. QED.

### Important special cases

1. **Difference concentration.** Taking `(a,b)=(1,-1)/sqrt(2)`,
   \[
   \mathcal Q_m(\rho)
   \ge
   P\{|\xi_m-\xi_m'|\le\sqrt2\rho\}.

   \[
   P\{\xi_m=\xi_m'\}>1/\gamma

3. **One scalar concentration ball.** If
   \[
   Q_{\xi_m}(a)=\sup_zP\{|\xi_m-z|\le a\}\ge q,

   \mathcal Q_m(\sqrt2 a)\ge q^2.
   \]
   Thus the P12 threshold `q>gamma^{-1/2}` is a corollary.

Condition `(5.3)` is **necessary, not claimed sufficient**. The strongest complete sufficient condition currently proved is fixed finite-convolution smoothing.

# 6. Fourth-moment extremality for the collision mechanism

The previous P13 run used an imbalanced two-point law to show that bounded fourth moment alone can fail. This run computes the exact moment price of the collision mechanism.

## 6.1 One heavy atom

Let `X` be centered, variance one, and suppose

\[
P\{X=a\}=p>1/2.
\]

Put `q=1-p` and `x=a^2`. Conditional on `X!=a`, let the second moment be `m_2`. Variance one gives

\[
m_2=\frac{1-px}{q}.
\]

The conditional mean is `-pa/q`. Hence Cauchy--Schwarz requires

\[
m_2\ge\frac{p^2x}{q^2},
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
0\le x\le q/p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By Jensen/Cauchy on the conditional law,

\[
E[X^4\mid X\ne a]\ge m_2^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
E X^4
\ge
p x^2+\frac{(1-px)^2}{q}
=:F_p(x).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Now

\[
F_p'(x)=\frac{2p}{q}(x-1).
\]

Because `p>1/2` implies `q/p<1`, `(6.1)` lies entirely to the left of one. Hence `F_p` is decreasing on the feasible interval and

\[
E X^4
\ge
F_p(q/p)
=
\frac{q^2}{p}+\frac{p^2}{q}
=
\frac1{pq}-3.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equality is attained exactly by the centered standardized two-point law

\[
X=
\begin{cases}
-\sqrt{q/p},&\text{probability }p,\\
+\sqrt{p/q},&\text{probability }q.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 6.2 Collision probability

For a discrete law with atom masses `(p_i)`,

\[
\kappa=P\{X'=X''\}=\sum_i p_i^2.
\]

If `kappa>1/2`, then the largest atom mass `p=max p_i` is `>1/2`. Moreover

\[
\kappa
\le
p^2+(1-p)^2
=
1-2p(1-p),
\tag{6.5}
\]

because the sum of squares of all remaining atom masses is at most the square of their total mass.

Thus

\[
p(1-p)\le\frac{1-\kappa}{2}.
\]

Combining with `(6.3)`,

\[
\boxed{
E X^4
\ge
\frac{2}{1-\kappa}-3.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The two-point law `(6.4)` has

\[
\kappa=p^2+q^2,
\]

so equality in `(6.6)` is attained for every `kappa in (1/2,1)`.

This proves `P13-COLLISION-FOURTH-EXTREMALITY-29`.

# 7. Sharp fourth-moment budget for the two-column collision route

The exact collision obstruction needs

\[
\kappa>1/\gamma.
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `1<gamma<2`, then `1/gamma>1/2`, so `(6.6)` implies that any exact-collision law satisfying `(7.1)` must have

\[
E X^4
>
\frac{2}{1-1/\gamma}-3
=
\boxed{\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Conversely, standardized two-point laws approach equality as `kappa downarrow1/gamma`.

If `gamma>=2`, the universal variance-one lower bound

\[
E X^4\ge(E X^2)^2=1
\]

is the only moment barrier. For `gamma>2`, balanced Rademacher has fourth moment exactly one and collision mass `1/2>1/gamma`. At `gamma=2`, arbitrarily slight imbalance gives collision mass `>1/2` and fourth moment arbitrarily close to one.

Thus the infimal fourth-moment budget for the exact two-column collision mechanism is precisely `(0.3)`:

\[
B_{\rm coll}(\gamma)
=
\begin{cases}
(3-\gamma)/(\gamma-1),&1<\gamma<2,\\
1,&\gamma\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

This threshold is **sharp for the collision / two-point mechanism**. It is not claimed to be a necessary threshold for every conceivable least-singular counterexample.

Examples:

\[
B_{\rm coll}(3/2)=3,
\qquad
B_{\rm coll}(2)=1.
\]

Hence at the phase-retrieval aspect ratio `gamma=2`, even a fourth-moment budget arbitrarily close to the Jensen minimum one cannot substitute for uniform microscopic smoothing in triangular families.

# 8. Smooth absolutely-continuous counterexample with prescribed fourth-moment budget

Fix `gamma>1` and

\[
B>B_{\rm coll}(\gamma).
\]

Choose `p in (0,1)` and `q=1-p` so that the standardized two-point variable `Y` in `(6.4)` satisfies simultaneously

\[
\kappa=p^2+q^2>1/\gamma
\tag{8.1}
\]

and

\[
E Y^4=\frac1{pq}-3<B.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Existence follows from the sharpness calculation in Section 7.

Let `Z` be independent of `Y`, with a symmetric `C_c^∞` probability density supported in `[-1,1]`. Write

\[
v_2=E Z^2>0,
\qquad
v_4=E Z^4<\infty.
\]

Choose a sufficiently small fixed `delta_0>0` and put

\[
\delta_m=\delta_0e^{-m^2},
\qquad
\boxed{
\xi_m
=
\frac{Y+\delta_m Z}
{\sqrt{1+v_2\delta_m^2}}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

## 8.1 Exact normalization

By independence and symmetry,

\[
E\xi_m=0,
\]

and

\[
E\xi_m^2
=
\frac{1+v_2\delta_m^2}
{1+v_2\delta_m^2}
=1.
\]

## 8.2 Smooth density and uniform tails

The density of `xi_m` is a mixture of two translates of a rescaled `C_c^∞` bump. Hence every level has a `C^∞` Lebesgue density.

Because `p,q` are fixed and `|Z|<=1`, the supports are contained in one fixed compact interval. Therefore the family has:

- one uniform subgaussian norm;
- uniformly bounded moments of every order;
- in particular no operator-norm/tail pathology.

The density ceiling, however, grows on the order of `delta_m^{-1}`. That is exactly the microscopic regularity being removed.

## 8.3 Uniform prescribed fourth moment

Symmetry of `Z` gives

\[
E(Y+\delta Z)^4
=
E Y^4+6v_2\delta^2+v_4\delta^4.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{
E Y^4+6v_2\delta_m^2+v_4\delta_m^4
}{(1+v_2\delta_m^2)^2}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

The right side is continuous at `delta=0` with value `EY^4<B`. Choose `delta_0` sufficiently small that

\[
\boxed{
\sup_mE\xi_m^4<B.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

## 8.4 Microscopic projective concentration

Take

\[
(a,b)=\frac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1).
\]

Whenever two independent copies choose the same atom of `Y`, which has probability

\[
\kappa=p^2+q^2>1/\gamma,
\]

their discrete parts cancel. Since both noises lie in `[-delta_m,delta_m]`,

\[
\left|
\frac{\xi_m-\xi_m'}{\sqrt2}
\right|
\le
\frac{\sqrt2\delta_m}
{\sqrt{1+v_2\delta_m^2}}.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal Q_m\!\left(
\frac{\sqrt2\delta_m}{\sqrt{1+v_2\delta_m^2}}
\right)
\ge\kappa>1/\gamma.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Apply `P13-TWOCOLUMN-PROJECTIVE-OBSTRUCTION-29`:

\[
P\left\{
M_{m,r_m}
\le
\sqrt{m+r_m}
\frac{\sqrt2\delta_m}{\sqrt{1+v_2\delta_m^2}}
\right\}
\to1.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Since `delta_m=delta_0e^{-m^2}`,

\[
\log M_{m,r_m}
\le
-m^2+O(\log m)
\]

with probability tending to one. Therefore

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}\log M_{m,r_m}
\le
-k_mm+o(1)
\to-\infty.
\]

This proves `P13-SMOOTH-BOUNDED4-NARROWSPIKE-CE-29`.

# 10. Primary-source verification and route comparison

## 10.1 Public source theorem

Current arXiv `2608.07410`, *Extreme least singular values of random row submatrices with bounded-density subgaussian entries* (Yang--Wen--Shmalo), states the public square theorem for one centered real subgaussian law with positive variance and bounded Lebesgue density and proves

## 10.2 Bai--Yin

Bai--Yin, *Annals of Probability* 21 (1993), 1275--1294, assumes iid entries with mean zero, variance one and finite fourth moment and proves almost-sure sample-covariance extreme-edge convergence at fixed aspect ratio.

This is a fixed-law global spectral-edge theorem. It does not imply the microscopic condition `(5.3)`, and it does not rescue the triangular narrow-spike family in Section 8. The latter family is already uniformly compactly supported.

## 10.3 Latała

Latała, *Proceedings of the AMS* 133 (2005), 1273--1282, proves for independent mean-zero entries

\[
E\|X\|
\le C\left[
\max_i\sqrt{\sum_jEX_{ij}^2}
+
\max_j\sqrt{\sum_iEX_{ij}^2}
+
\left(\sum_{ij}E|X_{ij}|^4\right)^{1/4}
\right].
\]

Thus a uniformly bounded fourth moment gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` operator-norm control at bounded aspect ratio. Again, Section 8 shows that this is orthogonal to the microscopic obstruction: the counterexample already has uniform bounded support and therefore stronger norm tails.

**Lindeberg replacement remains secondary.** P20's current hard-edge report provides an independent warning: even after capping a terminal inverse determinant, rowwise covariance matching does not control coupled determinant/cofactor sensitivity. In P13 the new narrow-spike counterexample is a true change in the statistic, not a defect of a proof technique, so no replacement argument can establish a false triangular theorem.
