## 4.1 Uniform scalar anti-concentration

Let `I=[z-r,z+r]`. If `xi_1,...,xi_q` are iid copies and every `xi_j` lies in `I`, then their sum lies in an interval of length `2qr`. Hence

\[
P\{\xi\in I\}^q
\le
P\{\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_q\in qI\}
\le
2qrM.
\]

Therefore:

## `P13-FINCONV-ANTICONC-27` — NEW / PROVED

\[
\boxed{
Q_\xi(r):=\sup_zP\{|\xi-z|\le r\}
\le
(2qMr)^{1/q}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, the scalar concentration can be made arbitrarily small by taking a fixed sufficiently small radius. This supplies the fixed anti-concentration gap required by the inherited heavy-tail random-rounding modules.

It also supplies the tunability needed for direct sparse-net arguments.

# 5. Fourier power directly from finite-convolution smoothing

The random sum

\[
S_q=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_q
\]

has density maximum at most `M` and characteristic function `phi^q`.

Bobkov--Götze Proposition 5.3 states in real dimension one that for any random variable with density maximum `M_0`, characteristic function `psi`, and integer `ell>=1`,

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int |\psi(t)|^{2\ell}dt
\le
\sqrt{\frac e{2\ell}}M_0.
\]

Applying this to `S_q` gives

\[
\int|\phi(t)|^{2q\ell}dt
\le
2\pi M\sqrt{\frac e{2\ell}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Exactly as in the preceding `L^p` proof, for every real exponent `r>=2q` choose `ell=1` for `r<4q` and `ell=floor(r/(2q))` otherwise.

## `P13-FINCONV-FOURIER-POWER-27` — NEW / PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R}|\phi(t)|^r dt
\le
\frac{2\pi\sqrt{2eq}\,M}{\sqrt r},
\qquad r\ge2q.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

No moment, centering, or variance assumption is used.

There is also a useful sharper identity at exponent `2q`. Since `f^{*q}` is a probability density with `L^infinity` norm at most `M`,

\[
\|f^{*q}\|_2^2\le M.
\]

Plancherel therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\int|\phi(t)|^{2q}dt
=2\pi\|f^{*q}\|_2^2
\le2\pi M.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
