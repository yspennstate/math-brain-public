# 4. New exact obstruction: atomic collisions force `M=0`

Let `xi',xi''` be independent copies of the entry law and define the collision mass

\[
\boxed{\kappa=P\{\xi'=\xi''\}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a purely discrete law with atom masses `p_a`,

\[
\kappa=\sum_a p_a^2.
\]

Consider just the first two columns of the full `N_m x m` iid matrix. Let

\[
C_m
=
\#\{1\le i\le N_m:A_{i1}=A_{i2}\}.
\]

The row events are iid, so

\[
C_m\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,\kappa).
\]

Because `k_m log m=o(m)`,

\[
k_m=o(m),
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{m+r_m}{N_m}
=
\frac{m+k_m-1}{N_m}
\longrightarrow
\frac1\gamma.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Assume

\[
\boxed{\kappa>1/\gamma.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Then `(4.2)` lies a fixed distance below the binomial mean fraction `kappa`. Chernoff's inequality gives

\[
P\{C_m<m+r_m\}
\le
\exp[-c(\gamma,\kappa)m]
\tag{4.4}
\]

for all sufficiently large `m`. More explicitly, any

\[
0<c<\gamma D(1/\gamma\,\|\,\kappa)
\]

works asymptotically, where `D(.||.)` is Bernoulli relative entropy.

On the event

\[
C_m\ge m+r_m,
\]

choose any row set `T` of size `m+r_m` among those equal-coordinate rows. Then columns 1 and 2 of `(A_m)_T` are identical. Hence

\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_m)_T\le m-1
\]

and

\[
\sigma_m((A_m)_T)=0.
\]

Therefore:

## `P13-ATOMIC-COLLISION-OBSTRUCTION-28` — NEW / PROVED

If `N_m/m->gamma>1`, `k_m log m=o(m)`, and `(4.3)` holds for a fixed scalar law, then

\[
\boxed{
P\{M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=0\}
\ge
1-e^{-c m}
\to1.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

No tail assumption is involved.

# 5. Bounded-fourth-moment-alone counterexample for every aspect ratio

Fix any

\[
\gamma>1.
\]

Let `B` be Bernoulli(`p`) and define the centered variance-one two-point variable

\[
\boxed{
\xi
=
\frac{B-p}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its two values are distinct, so its collision mass is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(p)
=p^2+(1-p)^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

As `p downarrow0`,

\[
\kappa(p)\to1.
\]

Since `1/gamma<1`, choose one fixed `p=p(gamma)>0` so small that

\[
\kappa(p)>1/\gamma.
\]

The fourth moment is the finite fixed constant

\[
\boxed{
E\xi^4
=
\frac{(1-p)^2}{p}
+
\frac{p^2}{1-p}
<\infty.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The law is bounded, hence it is in fact subgaussian and has moments of every order.

Apply `P13-ATOMIC-COLLISION-OBSTRUCTION-28`.

## `P13-BOUNDED4-ALONE-COUNTEREXAMPLE-28` — NEW / PROVED

For every `gamma>1` there is a fixed centered variance-one bounded random variable with a fixed finite fourth moment such that

\[
\boxed{
P\{M_{m,r_m}(A_m)=0\}\to1
}
\]

through every source surplus regime `k_m log m=o(m)`.

Thus the statement

> “bounded fourth moment alone replaces all of the source regularity”

is **DISPROVED**.

The correct distinction is:

1. **Replace only subgaussianity, retain the source bounded smooth density:** the theorem is true and fourth moment is unnecessary.
2. **Keep only bounded fourth moment and drop microscopic/non-atomic regularity:** false by `(5.1)`--`(5.3)`.

The collision theorem also gives a necessary obstruction threshold at fixed aspect ratio: any universal class allowing a law with

\[
P\{\xi'=\xi''\}>1/\gamma
\]

cannot satisfy a finite source exponent at that `gamma`. Non-atomicity removes this exact-zero obstruction, but is not known to be sufficient for the full exponent.

# 7. Variable-depth tunable sparse-kernel theorem

Let

\[
C_m\in\mathbb R^{N\times n}
\]

have iid variance-one entries satisfying `(6.1)`, with

\[
N\ge c_*n
\]

for one fixed `c_*>0`.

Let the target coordinate support budget be

\[
\boxed{
s_m=(2q_m-1)k_m.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_m
=
\log m+q_m+\log(e q_m\overline M_m).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Assume

\[
\boxed{q_mk_m\Lambda_m=o(m).}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since `q_m>=1`, `(7.3)` implies

\[
s_m=o(m)
\]

and in particular `s_m<n` for large `m`.

## Fixed sparse vector

For a unit vector `y` supported on at most `s_m` coordinates, choose a coordinate with

\[
|y_j|\ge n^{-1/2}.
\]

Conditioning on the other row coordinates, `(6.3)` gives

\[
\sup_z
P\{|\langle R,y\rangle-z|\le a_m/\sqrt n\}
\le\rho.
\tag{7.4}
\]

## Row-norm control

Since

\[
E\|R\|_2^2=n,
\]

choose a fixed `L=L(A_*,c_*)` so large that the iid indicator event

\[
\#\{i:\|R_i\|>L\sqrt n\}>\eta N
\]

has probability at most

\[
e^{-3A_*n}
\]

for one fixed small `eta>0`. This uses only Markov for one row followed by the elementary binomial Chernoff bound.

## Net

For each support of size at most `s_m`, take a Euclidean net with mesh

\[
\boxed{
\delta_m
=
\frac{a_m}{8Ln}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The logarithm of the union of all support nets is bounded by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\mathcal N_m
&\le
s_m\log\frac{en}{s_m}
+s_m\log\frac{C Ln}{a_m}\\
&\le
C_{A_*,c_*}
q_mk_m
\Big[
\log m+q_m+\log(e q_m\overline M_m)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.6}
\]

By `(7.3)`,

\[
\boxed{\log\mathcal N_m=o(m).}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Choose the fixed `rho` in `(6.2)` sufficiently small, depending on `A_*` and `c_*`, so that for each fixed net point the probability that at least `(1-eta)N` rows satisfy the small-slab event in `(7.4)` is at most `e^{-3A_*n}`. The binomial entropy is constant and is absorbed by the choice of `rho`.

Union over `(7.7)`.

## Transfer from a kernel vector to the sparse net

Suppose `x in ker C_m`, `||x||=1`, and for some coordinate subspace `R^S`, `|S|<=s_m`,

\[
\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb R^S)
\le
\frac{a_m}{32Ln}.
\]

Normalize the coordinate projection of `x` onto `S`, and approximate it by a net point `y`. For every good row `||R_i||<=L sqrt n`, the kernel identity and `(7.5)` force

\[
|\langle R_i,y\rangle|
\le
\frac{a_m}{\sqrt n}.
\]

Thus either more than `eta N` rows have large norm or one sparse net point has small inner product on at least `(1-eta)N` rows. The preceding bounds exclude both possibilities.

Hence:

## `P13-REAL-VARFC-SPARSEKERNEL-28` — NEW / PROVED

For every prescribed `A_*>0`, under `(7.3)`, for all sufficiently large `m`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}

\text{ for some }|S|\le(2q_m-1)k_m

where one may take

\boxed{

=

# 8. Extracting `2q_m` bases and the fresh projection density

Fix a candidate

\[
B\in\mathbb R^{(m+k-1)\times m}
\]

and freeze all but one column. Let

\[
E=H_j^\perp,
\qquad
\dim E=k,
\]

and let

\[
A\in\mathbb R^{k\times(m+k-1)},
\qquad
AA^\top=I_k,
\]

have rows spanning `E`.

On the good event `(7.8)`, deletion of every coordinate set `R` with

\[
|R|\le(2q_m-1)k
\]

leaves

\[
A_{R^c}A_{R^c}^\top
\succeq
\tau_m^2 I_k.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Cauchy--Binet recursively extracts `2q_m` pairwise-disjoint coordinate `k`-bases

\[
S_1,\ldots,S_{2q_m}.
\]

The first can be chosen with

\[
|\det A_{S_1}|
\ge
\binom{m+k-1}{k}^{-1/2}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Every subsequent basis can be chosen with

\[
|\det A_{S_a}|
\ge
\tau_m^k
\binom{m+k-1}{k}^{-1/2}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

The fixed-basis Fourier/Hölder proof does not require `q` to be constant. Plancherel gives

\[
\int|\phi_m(t)|^{2q_m}dt
=2\pi\|f_m^{*q_m}\|_2^2
\le2\pi M_m.
\]

Using the `2q_m` disjoint bases,

\[
\boxed{
\|f_{AX}\|_\infty
\le
M_m^k
\prod_{a=1}^{2q_m}
|\det A_{S_a}|^{-1/(2q_m)}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Insert `(8.2)`--`(8.3)`:

\[
\boxed{
\|f_{AX}\|_\infty
\le
M_m^k
\binom{m+k-1}{k}^{1/2}
\tau_m^{-(2q_m-1)k/(2q_m)}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

By `(7.9)`,

\[
\log\tau_m^{-1}
\le
C_{A_*,\gamma}
+
\log m
+q_m|\log\rho|
+
\log(e q_m\overline M_m).
\]

Therefore `(7.3)` implies

\[
\boxed{
\log\|f_{AX}\|_\infty=o(m).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

uniformly over candidate row sets and fresh-column indices, outside an `e^{-A_*m}` geometry failure.

# 9. Variable-depth lower extreme

The `k`-dimensional ball of radius `sqrt(m)t` has volume

\[
\le
\exp[O(k\log m)]t^k.
\]

Thus `(8.6)` gives

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)\le\sqrt m\,t\}
\le
\exp[o(m)]t^k+e^{-A_*m}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

The rectangular negative second-moment identity implies `sigma_m(B)<=t` forces `(9.1)` for at least one column. Union over the `m` columns costs only `e^{o(m)}`.

The number of candidate row sets is

\[
\binom{N_m}{m+k-1}
=
\exp[h(\gamma)m+o(m)].
\tag{9.2}
\]

Take

\[
\boxed{
t
=
\exp\left[-\frac{(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m}{k}\right].}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then

\[
\exp[h(\gamma)m+o(m)]
\exp[o(m)]t^k
=
\exp[-\varepsilon m+o(m)]
\to0.
\]

Choose

\[
A_*>h(\gamma)+2.
\]

The geometry failure term is then also killed after the candidate and column unions.

This proves:

## `P13-REAL-VARFC-LOWER-EXTREME-28` — NEW / PROVED

Under `(0.1)`, `N_m/m->gamma>1` and `E xi_m^2=1`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m}{k_m}\right]
\right\}
\to0.
}
\]

No centering, fourth moment, operator-norm event, Bai--Yin theorem, matrix truncation or Lindeberg replacement is used.

For fixed `q_m=q` and bounded `M_m`, `(0.1)` reduces to the source regime `k_m log m=o(m)`, recovering the inherited lower theorem.

## 11.1 Leverage-pruning depth grows with `q_m`

For large `q`,

The fixed-`q` adaptive pair module does not contain a uniform theorem for a growing number of coordinate deletions. It must be re-proved before a variable-depth pair estimate is used.

For fixed `q,M` this is a constant. For growing `q_m` it can vanish. The microscopic lower slab then needs a density-local-CLT estimate whose error is quantitatively smaller than a Gaussian density floor at the realized frozen shift. The fixed-`q` proof does not provide such a uniform statement.
