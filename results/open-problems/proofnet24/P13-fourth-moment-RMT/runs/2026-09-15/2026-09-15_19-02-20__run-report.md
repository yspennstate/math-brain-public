# 4. New theorem A: fourth moment versus a projective two-column slab

Let `X,X'` be iid real variables with

\[
EX=0,
\qquad EX^2=1,
\qquad EX^4=\mu_4<\infty.
\]

Take

\[
a^2+b^2=1,
\qquad
Y=aX+bX'.
\]

Then

\[
EY^2=1.
\]

Expanding the fourth moment and using centering gives

\[
\boxed{
EY^4
=(a^4+b^4)\mu_4+6a^2b^2
=\mu_4+2c(3-\mu_4),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
c=a^2b^2\in[0,1/4].
\]

Therefore, for fixed `mu_4`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{a^2+b^2=1}EY^4
=F(\mu_4)
:=
\begin{cases}
(\mu_4+3)/2,&1\le\mu_4\le3,\\
\mu_4,&\mu_4\ge3.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Now put

\[
p=P\{|Y|\le\rho\},
\qquad 0\le\rho<1.
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz on the complement,

\[
\begin{aligned}
1=EY^2
&\le
p\rho^2
+
\sqrt{(1-p)EY^4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
EY^4
\ge
T(p,\rho)
:=
\frac{(1-p\rho^2)^2}{1-p}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining `(4.2)` and `(4.3)` and inverting `F` gives `P13-PROJECTIVE-FOURTH-BARRIER-30`:

\[
\boxed{
\mu_4\ge\Psi(T(p,\rho)),
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

with

\[
\Psi(T)=
\begin{cases}
1,&T\le2,\\
2T-3,&2\le T\le3,\\
T,&T\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

No matrix theorem is used.

# 5. Sharp two-column fourth-moment obstruction budget

The preceding run proved that a two-column projective slab of fixed mass `p>1/gamma` at radius `rho_m` forces

\[
M_{m,r_m}\le\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\rho_m
\]

with exponentially high probability.

At zero radius, let `p downarrow 1/gamma`. Equation `(4.3)` gives

\[
T\downarrow\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}.
\]

Applying `(4.4)` yields

\[
\boxed{
B_{\rm proj}(\gamma)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\gamma}{\gamma-1},&1<\gamma\le3/2,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1},&3/2\le\gamma<2,\\[6pt]
1,&\gamma\ge2.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

as the infimal fourth-moment budget for the **entire two-column projective mechanism**.

## Sharpness, `1<gamma<3/2`

Take `p>1/gamma`, `q=1-p`, and

\[
Y=
\begin{cases}
0,&\text{probability }p,\\
+q^{-1/2},&\text{probability }q/2,\\
-q^{-1/2},&\text{probability }q/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
EY=0,
\qquad EY^2=1,
\qquad EY^4=1/q.
\]

The one-column direction `(a,b)=(1,0)` has zero-radius mass `p>1/gamma`. As `p downarrow1/gamma`,

\[
EY^4\downarrow\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}.
\]

## Sharpness, `3/2<=gamma<2`

The centered standardized two-point collision law from the preceding run has

\[
P\{Y'=Y''\}>1/\gamma
\]

and fourth moment approaching

\[
\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1}.
\]

## Sharpness, `gamma>=2`

For `gamma>2`, balanced Rademacher has fourth moment one and collision mass one half, exceeding `1/gamma`. At `gamma=2`, an arbitrarily small imbalance gives collision mass above one half and fourth moment arbitrarily close to one.

Numerical checks:

\[
B_{\rm proj}(6/5)=6
\quad\text{versus the older collision-only value }9,
\]

\[
B_{\rm proj}(3/2)=3,
\qquad
B_{\rm proj}(2)=1.
\]

This proves `P13-SHARP-TWOCOLUMN-BUDGET-30`.

The qualifier is essential: `(5.1)` is sharp for two-column projective obstructions. It is not claimed globally optimal among all possible many-column counterexamples.

# 6. Smooth bounded-fourth triangular counterexample at the improved budget

Fix `gamma>1` and

\[
B>B_{\rm proj}(\gamma).
\]

For `1<gamma<3/2`, choose the three-point variable in Section 5 with `p>1/gamma` and `EY^4<B`. For `gamma>=3/2`, use the preceding centered two-point collision variable with the same strict budget.

Let `Z` be an independent symmetric `C_c^∞` real variable supported in `[-1,1]`, with

\[
v_2=EZ^2>0,
\qquad v_4=EZ^4<\infty.
\]

Set

\[
\delta_m=\delta_0e^{-m^2},
\qquad
\boxed{
\xi_m=
\frac{Y+\delta_mZ}{\sqrt{1+v_2\delta_m^2}}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then exactly

\[
E\xi_m=0,
\qquad E\xi_m^2=1.
\]

Every law has a `C^∞` density, and all supports lie in one fixed compact interval. Hence the family is uniformly subgaussian and has uniformly bounded moments of every order.

Moreover

\[
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{EY^4+6v_2\delta_m^2+v_4\delta_m^4}
{(1+v_2\delta_m^2)^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

By choosing `delta_0` sufficiently small,

\[
\sup_mE\xi_m^4<B.
\]

### `1<gamma<3/2`

On the event that the discrete component equals zero, which has probability `p>1/gamma`,

\[
|\xi_m|\le C\delta_m.
\]

The number of such rows in the first column is binomial with mean fraction above `1/gamma`. Therefore, with probability `1-e^{-cm}`, there are at least `m+r_m` such rows. Choosing them and testing the unit vector `e_1` gives

\[
M_{m,r_m}\le C\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\delta_m.
\]

### `gamma>=3/2`

Use the same-discrete-atom event for two copies and the direction `(1,-1)/sqrt(2)` exactly as in the preceding collision counterexample.

Thus in all cases

\[
\boxed{
P\{M_{m,r_m}\le C\sqrt m\,e^{-m^2}\}\to1.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This proves `P13-SMOOTH-B4-PROJECTIVE-CE-30`.

The counterexample is not heavy-tailed. It has excellent tails. That is stronger: the obstruction is microscopic concentration, not norm control.

# 7. New theorem B: concentration-to-density by microscopic jitter

For an arbitrary scalar law define its Lévy concentration function

\[
Q_\xi(\delta)=\sup_zP\{|\xi-z|\le\delta\}.
\]

Let

\[
U_\delta\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-\delta,\delta]
\]

be independent of `xi`, and put

\[
\widetilde\xi=\xi+U_\delta.
\]

Then `widetilde xi` has density

\[
g(y)=\frac{P\{\xi\in[y-\delta,y+\delta]\}}{2\delta},
\]

so exactly

\[
\boxed{
\|g\|_\infty
\le
\frac{Q_\xi(\delta)}{2\delta}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This elementary identity is the bridge between a scale-local concentration assumption and the already-proved bounded-density theorem.

# 8. Full microscopic-jitter transfer theorem

Assume

\[
E\xi_m=0,
\qquad E\xi_m^2=1,
\qquad N_m/m\to\gamma>1,
\qquad k_m\log m=o(m).
\]

Assume `(0.1)`--`(0.2)` for every fixed `epsilon>0`.

Fix one `epsilon>0` and abbreviate

\[
\delta_m=\delta_{m,\varepsilon},
\qquad K=K_\varepsilon.
\]

Let `U_m` be an iid-entry matrix with entries uniform on `[-delta_m,delta_m]`, independent of `A_m`, and put

\[
\widetilde A_m=A_m+U_m.
\]

By `(7.1)`, every entry of `widetilde A_m` has density ceiling at most `K/2`. Its mean is zero and its variance is

\[
\sigma_m^2=1+\delta_m^2/3.
\]

Normalize by `sigma_m`. Since `delta_m->0`, the normalized density ceiling is eventually at most `K`, and

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}|\log\sigma_m|\to0.
\]

Thus the inherited `q=1` case of `P13-REAL-FINCONV-TRIANG-MAIN-27` gives

\[
\frac{k_m}{m}
\log M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)
\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}-h(\gamma).
\tag{8.1}
\]

For every row set `T` of size `m+r_m`, Weyl gives

\[
|\sigma_m((A_m)_T)-\sigma_m((\widetilde A_m)_T)|
\le\|(U_m)_T\|_{op}
\le\|(U_m)_T\|_F
\le\sqrt{m(m+r_m)}\,\delta_m.
\]

Taking minima over `T`,

\[
\boxed{
|M_{m,r_m}(A_m)-M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)|
\le D_m,
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

where

\[
D_m=\sqrt{m(m+r_m)}\,\delta_m.
\]

By `(0.2)`,

\[
D_m\le e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k_m}.
\]

On the event

\[
e^{-(h+\varepsilon/2)m/k_m}
\le
M(\widetilde A_m)
\le
e^{-(h-\varepsilon/2)m/k_m},
\]

whose probability tends to one by `(8.1)`, the ratio of `D_m` to the lower endpoint is at most

\[
e^{-\varepsilon m/(2k_m)}\to0
\]

because `k_m log m=o(m)` implies `m/k_m->∞`.

Therefore `(8.2)` yields the same normalized logarithmic limit for `A_m`. This proves `P13-MICROJITTER-TRANSFER-30`.

### Why this does not revive the rejected truncation+Weyl route

The rejected route perturbed entries at polynomial size and then tried to transport an exponentially small singular value. Here the perturbation is deliberately chosen **below the exponential target itself**. Weyl is therefore exact and harmless.

### Corollary for the August question

If one insists on assuming a uniform fourth moment `sup_m E|xi_m|^4<=B`, then `(0.1)`--`(0.2)` are sufficient extra microscopic hypotheses. But the fourth moment is not used in the proof: the extra regularity, not the moment, does the essential work.

# 9. New theorem C: direct scale-local rank-k projection small ball

The lower extreme can use a weaker radius than the full Weyl-transfer theorem.

Let `X=(xi_1,...,xi_n)` have iid coordinates with arbitrary common law. Let

\[
L\in\mathbb R^{k\times n},
\qquad LL^\top=I_k.
\]

Cauchy--Binet gives a `k`-set `J` with

\[
|\det L_J|
\ge\binom nk^{-1/2}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Add independent `Unif[-delta,delta]` noise only to the coordinates in `J`. By `(7.1)`, each smoothed selected coordinate has density at most

\[
Q_\xi(\delta)/(2\delta).
\]

If

\[
\|LX-z\|_2\le t\sqrt k,
\]

then for every realization of the selected jitter,

\[
\|L_J(X_J+U_J)+L_{J^c}X_{J^c}-z\|_2
\le(t+\delta)\sqrt k,
\]

because `||L_J||op<=1` and `||U_J||_2<=delta sqrt(k)`.

Condition on `X_{J^c}`. The transformed selected density is bounded by

\[
|\det L_J|^{-1}
\left(\frac{Q_\xi(\delta)}{2\delta}\right)^k.
\]

Since the Euclidean `k`-ball satisfies

\[
\operatorname{vol}(B_2^k((t+\delta)\sqrt k))
\le
(\sqrt{2\pi e}\,(t+\delta))^k,
\]

we obtain:

## `P13-LEVY-PROJ-SMALLBALL-30` — NEW / PROVED

\[
\boxed{
\sup_zP\{\|LX-z\|_2\le t\sqrt k\}
\le
\binom nk^{1/2}
\left[
\sqrt{2\pi e}\,
\frac{t+\delta}{2\delta}
Q_\xi(\delta)
\right]^k.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

In particular, if `delta<=t` and

\[
Q_\xi(\delta)\le K\delta,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\sup_zP\{\|LX-z\|_2\le t\sqrt k\}
\le
\binom nk^{1/2}
(\sqrt{2\pi e}\,K t)^k.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

No moment, centering, density, tail, delocalization, or operator-norm assumption is used.

## Lower-extreme consequence

Set

\[
\tau_m=e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k_m},
\qquad
t_m=\sqrt{m/k_m}\,\tau_m.
\]

If for this `epsilon` there are `delta_m<=t_m` and one fixed `K_epsilon` with

\[
Q_m(\delta_m)\le K_\varepsilon\delta_m,
\]

then conditioning on the frozen other columns and applying `(9.3)` to any `k_m`-dimensional subspace of their orthogonal complement gives

\[
P\{\operatorname{dist}(C_j,H_j)\le\sqrt m\,\tau_m\mid H_j\}
\le
\exp\{O(k_m\log m)\}\tau_m^{k_m}.
\]

Negative second moment and the candidate count

\[
\binom{N_m}{m+k_m-1}
=e^{h(\gamma)m+o(m)}
\]

then imply

\[
\boxed{
P\{M_{m,r_m}\le e^{-(h(\gamma)+\varepsilon)m/k_m}\}\to0.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

This direct lower-side theorem works at a substantially larger jitter radius than the full perturbative transfer in Section 8.

# 10. What uniform fourth moment does and does not buy

A uniform fourth moment gives a useful **coarse** anti-concentration gap, but not microscopic linear smoothing.

Assume

\[
EX=0,
\qquad EX^2=1,
\qquad EX^4\le B.
\]
