# 5. Primary-source checks relevant to this run

## 5.1 Livshyts–Paouris–Pivovarov: bounded coordinate density gives the exact real pair exponent

Primary source:

Galyna Livshyts, Grigoris Paouris, Peter Pivovarov, *On sharp bounds for marginal densities of product measures*, arXiv:1507.07949v2.

The primary source states that if independent real coordinate densities are bounded by one, every \(k\)-dimensional marginal density is bounded by \(2^{k/2}\). Scaling to a common coordinate ceiling \(K\), every rank-two orthogonal projection has density at most

\[
2K^2.
\tag{P1}
\]

For unit vectors \(u,v\), put \(\rho=\langle u,v\rangle\). The two-form map has Gram determinant

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{pmatrix}=1-\rho^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\|f_{(\langle X,u\rangle,\langle X,v\rangle)}\|_\infty\le\frac{2K^2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.}
\tag{P2}
\]

Hence the centered rectangle bound is

\[
\boxed{P\{|\langle X,u\rangle|\le t,\ |\langle X,v\rangle|\le t\}\le\frac{8K^2t^2}{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.}
\tag{P3}
\]

If \(1-\rho^2\ge1/4\), this is at most \(16K^2t^2\). This is the microscopic linear-smoothing behavior that the new counterexample violates.

## 5.2 Bobkov–Götze: density local CLT for the flat remainder

Primary source:

Sergey G. Bobkov and Friedrich Götze, *Berry–Esseen bounds in the local limit theorems*, arXiv:2407.20744 / Lithuanian Math. J. 65 (2025), 50–66.

The paper gives density Berry–Esseen bounds for sums of independent centered variables under bounded summand densities and Lyapunov control. In the application below, pairing two coordinates creates summands with:

- one common bounded density ceiling via \(L^2*L^2\to L^\infty\);
- variance one;
- uniformly bounded support, hence bounded third moment.

Therefore the normalized paired sum has a density uniformly close to the standard Gaussian and, for all sufficiently large \(m\), a uniform positive density floor on a fixed neighborhood of zero.

No unverified theorem stronger than this local-density conclusion is used.

\[

\qquad

\qquad

\tag{T0}
\]

Choose once and for all:

- an even \(C^\infty\) probability density \(h\), compactly supported, mean zero and variance one;
- an even \(C^\infty\) probability density \(g\), supported on \([-1,1]\), with variance \(v_g>0\) and \(g(0)>0\).

\[

\tag{T1}

\[
\boxed{f_m(x)=(1-p_m)a_m^{-1}h(x/a_m)+p_m\delta_m^{-1}g(x/\delta_m).}
\tag{T2}

Let \(\xi_m\sim f_m\).
