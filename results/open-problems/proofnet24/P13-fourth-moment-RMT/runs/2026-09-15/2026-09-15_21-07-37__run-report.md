\[
P\{\|U\|_{op}>C_\Gamma\sqrt m\,\delta\}
\le e^{-c_\Gamma m}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Independent derivation

For fixed unit vectors `x,y`,

\[
y^*Ux=\sum_{i,j}\overline y_i x_jU_{ij}.
\]

Every real projection is a sum of independent centered bounded variables. The square-sum of the deterministic coefficient bounds is

\[
\delta^2\sum_{i,j}|y_i|^2|x_j|^2=\delta^2.
\]

Hoeffding therefore gives, with a universal numerical constant `c>0`,

\[
P\{|y^*Ux|>t\delta\}\le4e^{-ct^2};
\tag{5.2}
\]

the factor four harmlessly covers the real and imaginary projections in the complex case.

Take `1/4`-nets of the underlying real unit spheres. Their cardinalities are at most

\[
9^{d_Fm},\qquad 9^{d_FN},

with `d_R=1`, `d_C=2`. The standard bilinear net inequality gives

\[
\|U\|_{op}
\le2\max_{x,y\text{ in the nets}}|y^*Ux|.

Since `N≤Γm`, choosing `t=C_Γ sqrt(m)` pays the entire net entropy and leaves `e^{-c_Γm}`. This proves `(5.1)`.

Finally, for every row set `T`,

\[
\|U_T\|_{op}\le\|U\|_{op},

so one global event controls all exponentially many candidate row submatrices. There is no hidden union over candidates.

# 6. New theorem: exact fourth-moment boundary failure with high probability

We now strengthen the previous boundary construction by exploiting many independent coordinate relations.

Put

\[
n_m=m+r_m.
\]

Since `k_m log m=o(m)`, we have `r_m=o(m)`. Choose the explicit row count

\[
\boxed{N_m=\lceil\gamma n_m\rceil.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then `N_m/m→γ`. Define

\[
\boxed{
\theta_m=\frac{n_m-1}{N_m}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus

\[
N_m\theta_m=n_m-1,
\qquad
\theta_m\to\frac1\gamma,
\qquad
\theta_m<\frac{n_m}{N_m}\le\frac1\gamma.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let

\[
p_m=P\{\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,\theta_m)\ge n_m\}.
\]

The threshold is exactly one above the mean. Since

\[
N_m\theta_m(1-\theta_m)=\Theta(m),
\]

the triangular Bernoulli CLT yields

\[
\boxed{p_m\to\frac12.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This one-line tuning is the amplification mechanism missing from the preceding boundary report.

## 6.1 Branch I: `1<γ≤3/2`

Let `Y_m` have the centered three-point law

\[
P\{Y_m=0\}=\theta_m,
\]

\[
P\left\{Y_m=\pm(1-\theta_m)^{-1/2}\right\}
=\frac{1-\theta_m}{2}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then

\[
EY_m=0,
\qquad
EY_m^2=1,
\qquad
EY_m^4=\frac1{1-\theta_m}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Because `θ_m<1/γ`,

\[
EY_m^4
<
\frac1{1-1/\gamma}
=
\frac\gamma{\gamma-1}
=B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Moreover the gap is `Theta(1/m)`: indeed

\[
\frac1\gamma-\theta_m
=\frac{N_m-\gamma(n_m-1)}{\gamma N_m}
\ge\frac1{N_m}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Choose one fixed symmetric `C_c^∞` variable `Z` supported in `[-1,1]`, with `EZ=0`, and set

\[
\varepsilon_m=e^{-m^2},
\qquad
\xi_m=
\frac{Y_m+\varepsilon_mZ}
{\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_m^2EZ^2}}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Each `ξ_m` has a `C^∞` density, is centered and variance one, and the family has uniformly bounded support, hence a uniform subgaussian norm and uniform moments of every order.

Writing `v_2=EZ^2`, `v_4=EZ^4`,

\[
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{EY_m^4+6v_2\varepsilon_m^2+v_4\varepsilon_m^4}
{(1+v_2\varepsilon_m^2)^2}
=EY_m^4+O(e^{-2m^2}).
\tag{6.10}
\]

The `Theta(1/m)` margin in `(6.7)` dominates `(6.10)`, so for all large `m`,

\[
E\xi_m^4<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\]

Patch finitely many initial levels by a fixed symmetric smooth two-bump law with fourth moment below `B_exact(γ)`. Therefore `(0.5)` holds globally.

For each column `j`, let `G_j` be the event that at least `n_m` latent variables among the `N_m` entries of that column equal zero. Then

\[
P(G_j)=p_m\to1/2.
\]

The events `G_1,...,G_m` are independent because they use disjoint iid columns. Therefore

\[
P\left\{\bigcup_{j=1}^mG_j\right\}
=1-(1-p_m)^m\to1.
\tag{6.11}
\]

On `G_j`, choose `n_m` successful rows `T`. In the selected submatrix the `j`th observed column consists only of the microscopic smoothing noise, so

\[
\|(A_m)_Te_j\|_2
\le C\sqrt{n_m}\,e^{-m^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le C_\gamma\sqrt m\,e^{-m^2}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Together with `(6.11)`, this proves `(0.6)` in Branch I.

## 6.2 Branch II: `3/2<γ<2`

Now `1/γ>1/2`, so `θ_m>1/2` eventually. Choose `p_m^* in (1/2,1)` satisfying

\[
(p_m^*)^2+(1-p_m^*)^2=\theta_m
\tag{6.13}
\]

and let `Y_m` be the centered variance-one standardization of a Bernoulli(`p_m^*`) variable. Its collision mass is exactly `θ_m`. The fourth moment is

\[
EY_m^4
=
\frac{2}{1-\theta_m}-3.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Since `θ_m<1/γ` and `x mapsto 2/(1-x)-3` is increasing,

\[
EY_m^4
<
\frac{2}{1-1/\gamma}-3
=
\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1}
=B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),
\tag{6.15}
\]

again with `Theta(1/m)` slack.

Smooth and variance-normalize exactly as in `(6.9)`. Equations `(6.10)` and `(6.15)` again give a `C^∞`, uniformly bounded/subgaussian triangular family satisfying `(0.5)`.

Use the disjoint column pairs

\[
(1,2),(3,4),\ldots.
\]

For each pair, let `G_j` be the event that its two **latent** columns agree on at least `n_m` rows. The rowwise equality probability is `θ_m`, hence

\[
P(G_j)=p_m\to1/2

by the same binomial calculation `(6.4)`. Events belonging to disjoint column pairs are independent. Therefore

\[
P\left\{\bigcup_{j\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor}G_j\right\}
=1-(1-p_m)^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\to1.
\tag{6.16}

On a successful pair choose `n_m` agreeing rows and test the unit vector

\[
\frac{e_{2j-1}-e_{2j}}{\sqrt2}.
\]

The latent pieces cancel exactly; only two bounded smoothing noises remain. Thus `(6.12)` holds, proving `(0.6)` in Branch II.

# 8. Operator-norm microscopic-jitter transfer

We now use the independently audited P12 lemma.

Let `U_m` have iid `Unif[-δ_m,δ_m]` entries, independent of `A_m`. Then

\[
\widetilde A_m=A_m+U_m
\]

has iid entries with density

\[
g_m(y)=
\frac{P\{\xi_m\in[y-\delta_m,y+\delta_m]\}}
{2\delta_m}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Under `(0.8)`,

\[
\|g_m\|_\infty\le K_\varepsilon/2.
\tag{8.2}
\]

The jitter is centered and has variance `δ_m^2/3`, so

\[
E\widetilde\xi_m=0,
\qquad
E\widetilde\xi_m^2=1+\delta_m^2/3.
\tag{8.3}
\]

After scalar variance normalization, the inherited common-bounded-density theorem applies. The normalization factor tends to one and is exponent-neutral by `(2.3)`.

Because `N_m/m→γ`, fix `Γ>γ` and apply `P12-MICROJITTER-OPNORM-33` for all large `m`:

\[
P\{\|U_m\|_{op}>C_\Gamma\sqrt m\,\delta_m\}
\le e^{-c_\Gamma m}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For every candidate row set `T`, Weyl gives

\[
|\sigma_m((A_m)_T)-\sigma_m((\widetilde A_m)_T)|
\le\|(U_m)_T\|_{op}
\le\|U_m\|_{op}.
\]

Taking minima over `T`,

\[
\boxed{
|M_{m,r_m}(A_m)-M_{m,r_m}(\widetilde A_m)|
\le\|U_m\|_{op}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Fix a target logarithmic tolerance `η>0` and invoke `(0.9)` with, say, `ε=3η`. Since `m/k_m→∞`, the fixed constant `C_Γ` is absorbed into `e^{η m/k_m}`. Thus on the high-probability event `(8.4)`,

\[
\|U_m\|_{op}
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{(h(\gamma)+2\eta)m}{k_m}\right]
\tag{8.6}
\]

for all large `m`.

The bounded-density theorem places the smoothed minimum in

\[
\exp\left[-\frac{(h(\gamma)\pm\eta)m}{k_m}\right]
\]

with probability tending to one. The perturbation `(8.6)` is exponentially smaller than the lower edge of that window. Equation `(8.5)` therefore preserves the normalized logarithm and proves `(0.1)`.

This proof uses a genuine additive perturbation, unlike the zero-bias conditioning cutoff in the fixed finite-convolution proof. It is safe only because its **operator norm itself lies below the target singular scale**.
