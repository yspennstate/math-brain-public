## `P13-AUDIT-P12-FINITE-ATOM-CAP-31`

**AUDIT_FOR:** P12  
**Source path:** `proofnet24/P12-smooth-iid/runs/2026-09-15/2026-09-15_19-19-26__run-report.md`

**Claim ID:** `P12-FINITE-LINEAR-ATOM-CAP-32`  
**Verdict:** **PROVED / PASS**

Let `X` be any real law. Write its atomic masses as `p_x=P{X=x}` and let

\[
p_0=P\{X=0\},
\qquad
\kappa=P\{X'=X''\}=\sum_xp_x^2.
\]

Take iid copies `X_1,…,X_s` and a nonzero coefficient vector `c`.

If exactly one coefficient is nonzero, then for the homogeneous zero event

\[
P\left\{\sum c_jX_j=0\right\}=p_0.
\]

If at least two coefficients, say `a,b`, are nonzero, condition on all other copies. It remains to bound, uniformly in the resulting target `t`,

\[
P\{aX+bY=t\}.
\]

The continuous parts contribute no mass to an exact affine graph. On the atomic part,

\[
P\{aX+bY=t\}
=
\sum_xp_xp_{(t-ax)/b}.
\]

The affine map `x↦(t-ax)/b` is injective. Hence Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\sum_xp_xp_{(t-ax)/b}
\le
\left(\sum_xp_x^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_xp_{(t-ax)/b}^2\right)^{1/2}
\le\kappa.
\]

Averaging over the conditioned variables preserves the bound. Therefore

\[
P\left\{\sum c_jX_j=0\right\}
\le\max\{p_0,\kappa\}.
\]

Sharpness is attained by one coordinate (`p_0`) and by the two-coordinate difference (`κ`). No density, moment, centering, tail, number theory, or projected-coordinate independence is hidden.

The immutable file was fetched at the introducing commit and returned the stated blob. **PASS.**

converges in `L^2`.

\[
\boxed{

# 7. Exact fourth-moment budget for all exact finite-column relations

For a centered variance-one law, a zero atom `p_0=p` obeys

\[
EX^4\ge\frac1{1-p}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Indeed, condition on `X≠0`, whose mass is `q=1-p`; the conditional second moment is `1/q`, so Jensen applied to `|X|^2` gives conditional fourth moment at least `1/q^2`.

For collision mass `κ>1/2`, apply the already proved P13 projective fourth barrier to

\[
Y=\frac{X-X'}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Since

\[
EY^4=\frac{EX^4+3}{2}
\]

and `P{Y=0}=κ`, Cauchy–Schwarz on the complement of `{Y=0}` gives

\[
EY^4\ge\frac1{1-\kappa}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
EX^4\ge\frac{2}{1-\kappa}-3.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

At a row-relation threshold `1/γ`, the zero-atom cost is

\[
B_0(\gamma)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1},
\]

while for `1<γ<2` the collision cost is

\[
B_{\rm coll}(\gamma)=\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma-1}.
\]

They cross exactly at `γ=3/2`.

Combining the audited P12 atom cap with `(7.1)`–`(7.2)` gives:

## `P13-ALLCOLUMN-EXACTREL-BUDGET-31` — NEW / PROVED

For `1<γ<2`,

\[
\boxed{
\inf\left\{
EX^4:
EX=0,
\ EX^2=1,
\ \exists s<\infty,\ \|c\|_2=1,
\ P\left\{\sum_{j=1}^sc_jX_j=0\right\}>1/\gamma
\right\}
=B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

For `γ≥2` the infimum is `1`.

Thus the preceding P13 quantity `B_proj(γ)` is not merely sharp for two-column projective relations: it is sharp for **all exact finite-column homogeneous relations**. Any remaining lower-budget obstruction must be genuinely approximate/microscopic or adaptive, not a hidden exact sparse identity.

# 8. New theorem: all-support coarse anti-concentration below the threshold

The newest P12 theorem gave a fixed-support-cap version. The support cap can be removed completely.

## `P13-B4-ALLSUPPORT-COARSE-GAP-31` — NEW / PROVED

Fix

\[
1<\gamma<2,
\qquad
1\le B<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\]

Then there exist

\[
\rho=\rho(B,\gamma)>0,
\qquad
\delta=\delta(B,\gamma)>0
\]

such that for every centered variance-one real random variable `X` satisfying

\[
EX^4\le B,
\]

every finite `s≥1`, and every `c∈R^s` with `||c||_2=1`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left\{
\left|\sum_{j=1}^sc_jX_j\right|\le\rho
\right\}
\le
\frac1\gamma-\delta.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The constants are uniform in the law, `s`, and the coefficient vector. The proof does not currently give a convenient explicit formula for them.

## Proof

Assume `(8.1)` is false. Then for each integer `n` there exist

- a centered variance-one law `X_n` with `EX_n^4≤B`;
- a finite unit coefficient vector `c^{(n)}`;

such that

\[
P\left\{
\left|L_n\right|\le\frac1n
\right\}
>
\frac1\gamma-\frac1n,
\qquad
L_n=\sum_jc_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Because the coordinates are iid, permute each coefficient vector so that

\[
|c_1^{(n)}|\ge|c_2^{(n)}|\ge\cdots,
\]

and extend it by zeros. Since the vector has norm one,

\[
|c_j^{(n)}|\le j^{-1/2}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Uniform fourth moments imply tightness of the laws. Pass to a subsequence such that

\[
X_n\Rightarrow X,
\qquad
c_j^{(n)}\to c_j
\quad\text{for every fixed }j.
\]

Uniform integrability from the fourth-moment bound gives

\[
EX=0,
\qquad
EX^2=1,
\]

and lower semicontinuity gives

\[
EX^4\le B.
\]

Put

\[
a^2=\sum_{j\ge1}c_j^2\le1.
\]

### Case 1: `a^2<1`

Choose a diagonal sequence `J_n→∞` slowly enough that

\[
\sum_{j\le J_n}(c_j^{(n)})^2
\to a^2.
\]

Let

\[
T_n=\sum_{j>J_n}c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}.
\]

Its variance tends to

\[
v=1-a^2>0.
\]

By `(8.3)`,

\[
\max_{j>J_n}|c_j^{(n)}|^2
\le\frac1{J_n+1}\to0.
\]

For every fixed `η>0`, the Lindeberg sum is bounded by

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{j>J_n}
E\left[
(c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j})^2
1_{\{|c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}|>\eta\}}
\right]\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{B}{\eta^2}
\sum_{j>J_n}|c_j^{(n)}|^4\\
&\qquad\le
\frac{B}{\eta^2}
\left(\max_{j>J_n}|c_j^{(n)}|^2\right)
\sum_{j>J_n}|c_j^{(n)}|^2
\to0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Therefore the scalar triangular-array Lindeberg theorem gives

\[
T_n\Rightarrow N(0,v).
\tag{8.5}
\]

The head and tail are independent. Hence

\[
P\{|L_n|\le1/n\}
\le
Q_{T_n}(1/n),
\tag{8.6}
\]

where `Q_Y(r)=sup_zP{|Y-z|≤r}`.

If a sequence converges weakly to a non-atomic law and `r_n↓0`, then its concentration functions satisfy `Q(r_n)→0`: otherwise choose almost-maximizing centers; tightness makes a subsequence of centers bounded, and the limit law would have an atom. Applying this to `(8.5)` makes the right side of `(8.6)` tend to zero, contradicting `(8.2)`.

Thus necessarily

\[
a^2=1.
\tag{8.7}
\]

### Case 2: `a^2=1`

For fixed `J`, the finite head

\[
H_{n,J}=\sum_{j\le J}c_j^{(n)}X_{n,j}
\]

converges in law to

\[
H_J=\sum_{j\le J}c_jX_j.
\]

The tail variance satisfies

\[
\limsup_n
E|L_n-H_{n,J}|^2
=
1-\sum_{j\le J}c_j^2
\to0
\qquad(J\to\infty).
\]

Hence

\[
L_n\Rightarrow
L:=\sum_{j\ge1}c_jX_j,
\tag{8.8}
\]

where the series converges in `L^2`.

Fix `η>0`. For all large `n`, `1/n<η`, so `(8.2)` gives

\[
P\{|L_n|\le\eta\}
\ge
\frac1\gamma-o(1).
\]

By the closed-set Portmanteau inequality and `(8.8)`,

\[
P\{|L|\le\eta\}
\ge
\frac1\gamma.
\]

Let `η↓0`:

\[
P\{L=0\}\ge\frac1\gamma.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Now apply `P13-INFINITE-LINEAR-ATOM-CAP-31`. Either

\[
p_0\ge1/\gamma
\]

or

\[
\kappa\ge1/\gamma.
\]

The first alternative and `(7.1)` imply

\[
EX^4\ge B_0(\gamma)\ge B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)>B.
\]

The second alternative and `(7.2)` imply

\[
EX^4\ge B_{\rm coll}(\gamma)\ge B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)>B.
\]

Both contradict `EX^4≤B`.

This proves `(8.1)`.

QED.

### What this theorem does not give

It is crucial not to overread `(8.1)`.

It gives a **fixed-radius probability gap below the row-selection threshold** for all homogeneous linear forms. It does not give

\[
P\{|L|\le t\}\lesssim t
\]

as `t→0`; it does not give the `t^{k_m}` rank-`k_m` projection power; and it does not give the one-normal microscopic lower slab required by the constructive upper extreme.

So a fourth-moment budget below `B_exact` eliminates the catastrophic sparse-relation mechanism but does not yet prove source universality.

and

\[
Y_m=\pm(1-p_m^*)^{-1/2}
\quad\text{with probabilities }(1-p_m^*)/2.
\]

Then

\[
EY_m=0,
\qquad
EY_m^2=1,
\qquad
EY_m^4=\frac1{1-p_m^*}
=B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)-\Theta(m^{-1/2}).
\tag{9.2}
\]

## Branch II: `3/2<γ<2`

Choose a centered standardized two-point law `Y_m` whose collision mass is

\[
P\{Y_m'=Y_m''\}=p_m^*.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Such a law exists because `p_m^*>1/2` for all large `m`. Its fourth moment is exactly

\[
EY_m^4
=
\frac{2}{1-p_m^*}-3
=B_{\rm exact}(\gamma)-\Theta(m^{-1/2}).
\tag{9.4}
\]

## Smooth the latent extremizers

Define

\[
\xi_m
=
\frac{Y_m+\delta_mZ}
{\sqrt{1+\delta_m^2EZ^2}}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Then every `ξ_m` is exactly centered and variance one and has a `C^∞` density. Because the latent laws stay uniformly bounded, the family has uniform compact support, uniform subgaussianity and all moments uniformly bounded.

Writing `v_2=EZ^2`, `v_4=EZ^4`,

\[
E\xi_m^4
=
\frac{EY_m^4+6v_2\delta_m^2+v_4\delta_m^4}
{(1+v_2\delta_m^2)^2}
=
EY_m^4+O(e^{-2m^2}).
\tag{9.6}
\]

The margin in `(9.2)` or `(9.4)` is `Θ(m^{-1/2})`, overwhelmingly larger than the smoothing error. Thus for all sufficiently large `m`,

\[
E\xi_m^4<B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
\]

Patch finitely many initial levels by any fixed centered variance-one smooth compactly supported law with fourth moment below `B_exact(γ)`; such laws exist because `B_exact(γ)>1` for `γ<2`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sup_mE\xi_m^4\le B_{\rm exact}(\gamma).
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

## Row count at the boundary

Let `C_m` count the relevant latent relation rows.

- In Branch I, `C_m` counts rows in which the first-column latent variable is zero; its success probability is `p_m^*`.
- In Branch II, `C_m` counts rows in which the first two latent labels agree; its success probability is again `p_m^*`.

Thus

\[
C_m\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N_m,p_m^*).
\]

The selection threshold is exactly

\[
m+r_m=N_m\alpha_m.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{(m+r_m)-N_mp_m^*}
{\sqrt{N_mp_m^*(1-p_m^*)}}
=
\frac{\beta N_m/\sqrt m}
{\sqrt{N_mp_m^*(1-p_m^*)}}
\to
z_{\gamma,\beta}
:=
\frac{\beta\gamma^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\gamma-1}}.
\tag{9.8}
\]

The ordinary Bernoulli CLT yields

\[
P\{C_m\ge m+r_m\}
\to
1-\Phi(z_{\gamma,\beta})
>0.
\tag{9.9}
\]

On this event choose any `m+r_m` successful rows.

In Branch I, testing `e_1` gives

\[
\|(A_m)_Te_1\|_2
\le C\sqrt{m+r_m}\,\delta_m.
\]

In Branch II, testing `(e_1-e_2)/\sqrt2` gives the same bound.

Hence:

## `P13-SMOOTH-B4-BOUNDARY-CE-31` — NEW / PROVED

For every `1<γ<2` there is a centered variance-one `C^∞` triangular family with uniform compact support, uniform subgaussianity, all moments uniformly bounded, and

\[
\sup_mE|\xi_m|^4\le B_{\rm exact}(\gamma),
\]

such that, for a row-count sequence with `N_m/m→γ`,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_m
P\left\{
M_{m,r_m}(A_m)
\le C\sqrt m\,e^{-m^2}
\right\}
\ge
1-\Phi(z_{\gamma,\beta})
>0.
}
\tag{9.10}
\]

Therefore the source normalized-log convergence fails in probability at the exact threshold budget.

This strengthens the preceding counterexample, which required `B>B_exact(γ)` in order to make the catastrophic event occur with probability tending to one.

# 10. Jensen endpoint `B=1` for `γ≥2`

Every variance-one law satisfies

\[
EX^4\ge1,
\]

with equality only when `|X|=1` almost surely. Thus an absolutely continuous law cannot attain the budget `B=1`.

But bounded-fourth **alone** already fails at this Jensen endpoint.

Take balanced Rademacher entries. Then

\[
EX=0,
\qquad
EX^2=1,
\qquad
EX^4=1,
\]

and the first two columns agree on each row with probability `1/2`.

If `γ>2`, then `1/2>1/γ`, so Chernoff gives at least `m+r_m` agreeing rows with probability tending to one; on those rows the two columns are identical, hence

\[
M_{m,r_m}=0.
\]

At `γ=2`, choose for example

\[
N_m=\left\lfloor2(m+r_m)\right\rceil.
\]

Then the threshold differs from the mean of `Bin(N_m,1/2)` by only `O(1)`, so the collision event has asymptotic probability `1/2`.

For the canonical real phase-retrieval row count

\[
N_m=2m-1+r_m,
\]

if

\[
r_m=o(\sqrt m),
\]

then

\[
\frac{(m+r_m)-N_m/2}{\sqrt{N_m}/2}
=
\frac{r_m+1}{\sqrt{N_m}}
\to0,
\]

and again

\[
P\{M_{m,r_m}=0\}\to\frac12.
\]

Record:

## `P13-RAD-ENDPOINT-B1-31` — NEW / PROVED

At `γ>2`, bounded fourth moment alone fails already at the minimal possible budget `B=1` with probability tending to one. At `γ=2`, it fails at `B=1` with nonvanishing probability on natural source/phase-retrieval row-count sequences, including fixed surplus.

# 12. Requested route comparison and bookkeeping

## Truncation bias

In the fixed finite-convolution theorem the constructive cutoff is only a conditioning reveal. No entry is modified, so truncation bias is exactly zero.

Polynomial matrix truncation followed by Weyl remains rejected because a polynomial perturbation is huge compared with the exponentially small target.

Microscopic additive jitter is different: its operator-norm perturbation is explicitly below the target scale, so Weyl is exact and safe.

## Operator norm / Latała / Bai–Yin

No global event `||A_m||=O(sqrt(m))` is a dependency of the strongest theorem.

Latała's primary 2005 theorem was rechecked: for independent mean-zero entries,

\[
E\|A\|
\le
C\left(
\max_i\sqrt{\sum_jEA_{ij}^2}
+
\max_j\sqrt{\sum_iEA_{ij}^2}
+
\left(\sum_{ij}E|A_{ij}|^4\right)^{1/4}
\right).
\]

Thus a uniform fourth moment gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` norm control at bounded aspect ratio.

Bai–Yin, *Annals of Probability* 21 (1993), 1275–1294, DOI `10.1214/aop/1176989118`, was rechecked in the primary source: iid mean-zero variance-one entries with finite fourth moment give the almost-sure sample-covariance extreme-edge limits `(1±sqrt(y))^2`.

Both are global spectral results. Neither yields source-scale microscopic anti-concentration. Sections 9–10 show the least-singular obstruction can survive under bounded support, so operator norm is not the missing hypothesis.

## Least singular value / small ball

The lower extreme needs an **upper rank-`k_m` projection small-ball** with full `t^{k_m}` power.

The new theorem `(8.1)` only gives a fixed probability gap for one scalar homogeneous form. It is useful geometry input, not an entropy-paying lower-extreme theorem.

## Delocalization

Uniform fourth moment gives coarse anti-concentration and therefore supports random-rounding/incompressibility arguments. This run strengthens that statement below `B_exact` to arbitrary finite homogeneous supports.

Delocalization still does not manufacture a microscopic density. The narrow-cluster counterexamples have excellent tails and nevertheless collapse the source scale.

## Lindeberg

Generic matrix Lindeberg replacement remains a secondary/rejected primary route because the statistic is a nonsmooth minimum over exponentially many overlapping submatrices and P14/P20 exhibit exact coupled cancellations missed by raw replacement sensitivities.

A **scalar** Lindeberg-Feller theorem is used safely in Section 8: after coefficients escape to infinitesimal scale, the object is a single linear form, its total variance stays positive, and the uniform fourth moment verifies the classical Lindeberg condition explicitly in `(8.4)`. No matrix observable is replaced there.

## Robust small ball and leave-one-out

These remain the successful structural tools. The lower extreme freezes the other columns and uses a genuinely fresh product column. The constructive upper proof freezes adaptive normal geometry before using fresh original rows.

## Expectation versus high probability

The source theorem and all positive repaired main theorems remain convergence in probability. No expectation convergence is inferred. Uniform integrability of the deep negative logarithmic tail remains a separate open problem.

The boundary counterexample `(9.10)` only needs a fixed positive failure probability to disprove convergence in probability.

## Finite versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

- levelwise finite fourth moment is insufficient even for uniform triangular `O(sqrt(m))` operator-norm control (inherited `m^{-2}`-spike / size-`m` counterexample);
- a **uniform** fourth-moment bound gives coarse norm control;
- a uniform fourth-moment bound still does **not** give triangular least-singular universality;
- below `B_exact(γ)` it does give the new all-support coarse homogeneous anti-concentration gap;
- under retained source smoothing, fourth moment is redundant.
