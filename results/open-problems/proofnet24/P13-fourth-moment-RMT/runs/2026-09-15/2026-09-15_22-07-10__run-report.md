# 4. New construction: smooth subcritical-fourth narrow-lattice laws

Fix

`1<gamma<2`, `1<B<B_exact(gamma)`.

Let `epsilon` be balanced Rademacher and let `Z` be an independent symmetric random variable with a `C_c^infinity` density supported in `[-1,1]`. Write

`v2=E Z^2`, `v4=E Z^4`.

Choose one small constant `delta_0>0` and set

`eta_m = delta_0 exp(-m^2)`,

`sigma_m^2 = 1 + v2 eta_m^2`,

`xi_m = (epsilon + eta_m Z)/sigma_m`.

Then exactly

`E xi_m=0`, `E xi_m^2=1`.

Every `xi_m` has a `C^infinity` density: it is a two-bump smooth mixture. Its support is uniformly bounded, so the triangular family has a uniform subgaussian norm and uniformly bounded moments of every order.

The fourth moment is

`E xi_m^4 = (1 + 6 v2 eta_m^2 + v4 eta_m^4)/(1+v2 eta_m^2)^2`.

As `eta_m -> 0`, this tends to `1`. The displayed expression is continuous at zero. Therefore choose `delta_0` sufficiently small that

`sup_m E xi_m^4 <= B`.

This proves the law-construction part of `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-FLATLATTICE-33`.

At the atomic endpoint `B=1`, simply take balanced Rademacher itself. Jensen shows that an absolutely continuous variance-one law cannot have fourth moment exactly one, so the smooth statement is correctly restricted to every `B>1`.

# 5. Rank-k flat Walsh projection: exact microscopic obstruction

Let `k=2^q` be a power of two and let `m=ks` with `s` even. Let `H_k` be the Sylvester `k x k` Hadamard matrix, so

`H_k H_k^T = k I_k`

and every entry is `+1` or `-1`.

Partition `[m]` into `k` groups `G_1,...,G_k`, each of cardinality `s`. Define `L in R^{k x m}` by

`L_{a j} = (H_k)_{a b}/sqrt(m)` when `j in G_b`.

## 5.1 Exact geometry

For rows `a,c`,

`(L L^T)_{ac} = (s/m) (H_k H_k^T)_{ac} = delta_{ac}`,

because `sk=m`. Hence

`L L^T=I_k`.

Every row is maximally flat:

`|L_{a j}|=m^{-1/2}` for every `a,j`.

Every coordinate has exactly uniform leverage:

`||L e_j||_2^2 = sum_a L_{a j}^2 = k/m`.

Thus there is no compressible/high-leverage defect to blame.

## 5.2 Lattice event

Write the latent Rademacher coordinates as `epsilon_1,...,epsilon_m`. For each group define

`S_b = sum_{j in G_b} epsilon_j`.

Since `s` is even,

`p_s := P{S_b=0} = 2^{-s} binom(s,s/2)`.

The central binomial coefficient is the largest coefficient in `(1+x)^s`, so

`p_s >= 1/(s+1)`

because the `s+1` binomial probabilities sum to one.

The group events `{S_b=0}` are independent. Therefore

`P{S_1=...=S_k=0} = p_s^k >= (s+1)^(-k)`.

On this event the latent Rademacher part of `L X` vanishes exactly because

`L epsilon = H_k (S_1,...,S_k)^T / sqrt(m) = 0`.

For the smooth perturbation, each coordinate of the noise contribution satisfies

`|(L (eta_m Z))_a| <= eta_m sqrt(m)`

because all coefficients have magnitude `1/sqrt(m)` and `|Z_j|<=1`. Thus

`||L X||_2 <= eta_m sqrt(m k)/sigma_m`.

Let

`t_m=exp(-lambda m/k)`

for any fixed `lambda>0`. Since `eta_m=delta_0 exp(-m^2)`, for all large `m`,

`eta_m sqrt(m) <= t_m`.

Hence the lattice event is contained in

`{ ||L X||_2 <= t_m sqrt(k) }`,

and therefore

`P{ ||L X||_2 <= t_m sqrt(k) } >= (s+1)^(-k)`.

If `k log m=o(m)`, then

`k log(s+1) <= k log(m+1)=o(m)`.

Thus

`P{ ||L X||_2 <= t_m sqrt(k) } >= exp(-o(m))`.

But

`t_m^k = exp(-lambda m)`.

Consequently an upper bound of the form

`exp(o(m)) t_m^k`

fails by the exponential factor `exp(lambda m-o(m))`.

This proves the rank-`k` part of `P13-SUBCRIT-B4-FLATLATTICE-33`.

## 5.3 Why this directly hits the lower-extreme proof route

The source lower extreme needs an entropy-paying projection estimate after the leave-one-out geometry is frozen. A deterministic theorem based only on

- bounded fourth moment;
- row/coordinate delocalization;
- low leverage;
- orthogonality;
- bounded support/subgaussian tails

cannot provide that estimate: the `L` above satisfies the best possible flatness geometry and still has only subexponential, not `exp(-Theta(m))`, source-scale small-ball probability.

This does **not** prove that the actual random leave-one-out projection is resonant. It proves that randomness/genericity of that projection must be used essentially if one wants a moment-only theorem.

# 6. One-normal and pair-stage microscopic failures

The same family gives exact route certificates for the constructive side.

## 6.1 Flat one-normal upper small-ball can be polynomial

Let `m` be even and

`u=m^{-1/2}(1,...,1)`.

If the latent Rademacher sum is zero, then

`|<X,u>| <= eta_m sqrt(m)`.

Therefore for every exponentially small `t_m` satisfying `eta_m sqrt(m)<=t_m`,

`P{|<X,u>|<=t_m} >= 2^{-m} binom(m,m/2) >= 1/(m+1)`.

So no deterministic bound

`P{|<X,u>-z|<=t} <= C t`

with entropy-neutral `C` can follow from subcritical bounded fourth moment and flatness alone.

At source radius `t=exp(-lambda m)`, any such constant would have to satisfy

`log C >= lambda m - O(log m)`.

## 6.2 Flat orthogonal pair can violate the `t^2` law

Assume `m` is divisible by four. Define

`u=m^{-1/2}(1,...,1)`

and

`v=m^{-1/2}(1,...,1,-1,...,-1)`,

with `m/2` plus signs and `m/2` minus signs. Then

`<u,v>=0`,

both vectors are maximally flat, and the pair leverage is exactly `2/m` at every coordinate.

Let `S_A,S_B` be the latent Rademacher sums over the two halves. If

`S_A=S_B=0`,

then both latent linear forms vanish. This event has probability

`p_{m/2}^2 >= (m/2+1)^(-2)`.

The smoothing contribution to each form is at most `eta_m sqrt(m)`. Hence for every source-scale exponential `t_m` with `eta_m sqrt(m)<=t_m`,

`P{|<X,u>|<=t_m, |<X,v>|<=t_m} >= (m/2+1)^(-2)`.

Since the projective angle determinant is perfect (`Delta=1`), this disproves any deterministic pair estimate `C t_m^2` based only on bounded fourth moment, delocalization and angle separation.

## 6.3 Flat one-normal centered lower slab can vanish

Now let `m` be odd and keep

`u=m^{-1/2}(1,...,1)`.

The latent Rademacher sum is an odd integer, so its absolute value is at least one. The smoothing term in the unnormalized sum has absolute value at most `eta_m m`. For all large `m`, `eta_m m<=1/4`; also `sigma_m<=2`. Therefore

`|<X,u>| >= 1/(4 sqrt(m))`

for every realization.

Hence whenever `t_m < 1/(4 sqrt(m))`, in particular at every exponential source scale,

`P{|<X,u>|<=t_m}=0`.

Thus a deterministic centered lower slab `P{|<X,u>|<=t} >= c t` is also impossible under bounded fourth moment plus delocalization alone.

## 6.4 Combined consequence

The obstruction is not “heavy tails.” The entry laws here are uniformly bounded and uniformly subgaussian. The obstruction is unresolved microscopic lattice structure hidden inside an arbitrarily narrow smooth mollification.

This separates three logically different inputs:

1. coarse delocalization / low leverage;
2. coarse anti-concentration below the row-selection threshold;
3. source-scale dimensional smoothing.

Only the third supplies `t`, `t^2`, and `t^k` powers.

# 9. Requested route separation

## 9.1 Truncation / replacement bias

In the fixed finite-convolution proof, the clean/tail cutoff is a conditioning reveal; no entry is replaced, so truncation bias is exactly zero.

Polynomial truncation plus Weyl remains invalid because a polynomial perturbation is enormous relative to the exponentially small target singular value.

Microscopic jitter is different: it deliberately perturbs entries but is valid only when the jitter operator norm itself is exponentially below the target.

The new narrow-lattice example shows why a microscopic perturbation hypothesis cannot be deduced from fourth moment alone: one may smooth Rademacher at width `exp(-m^2)` without materially changing any moment or tail statistic.

## 9.2 Operator norm

A global norm bound is not a dependency of the fixed finite-convolution theorem.

Uniform fourth moment gives coarse `O_P(sqrt(m))` norm control through Latała, but the new counterexample already has uniform compact support, so the global norm is completely benign while microscopic projection small-ball still fails.

Levelwise finite fourth moment without a uniform ceiling is much weaker. For example, the elementary triangular law

`X_m = +m` with probability `1/(2m^2)`, `-m` with probability `1/(2m^2)`, and `0` otherwise

has mean zero, variance one, and finite fourth moment `m^2` at every level. In a `Theta(m) x m` matrix there is a nonvanishing probability of an entry of magnitude `m`, so `||A_m||/sqrt(m)` is not tight. This retains the finite-vs-uniform fourth-moment distinction.

## 9.3 Bai–Yin

Bai–Yin controls global spectral edges for a fixed iid law under finite fourth moment. It does not imply uniform triangular source-scale concentration and does not repair the narrow-lattice obstruction.

## 9.4 Least singular / rank-k small-ball

The new Walsh certificate proves that even `LL^T=I`, completely flat rows, and exact uniform leverage `k/m` do not imply an entropy-paying `t^k` upper small-ball under subcritical bounded fourth moment.

Therefore a positive low-fourth proof must use more than incompressibility of the frozen leave-one-out plane. It must use stochastic anti-resonance of that plane relative to the entry lattice, or impose smoothing on the entry law.

## 9.5 Delocalization

This run gives a sharp negative answer to the proposed deterministic upgrade:

**delocalization does not imply microscopic smoothing.**

The vectors in Sections 5–6 are as flat as possible. Their failure is purely lattice/resonance structure.

## 9.6 Lindeberg

Generic matrix Lindeberg remains a nonprimary route. The observable minimizes over exponentially many overlapping candidates and is nonsmooth. Moreover, replacing the narrow-lattice law by a Gaussian at the entry level destroys exactly the microscopic resonances quantified above; controlling only low moments cannot justify that replacement at source scale.

The scalar Lindeberg-Feller step used in the prior all-support compactness theorem remains legitimate because it is invoked only for an infinitesimal coefficient tail and only to prove a fixed-radius coarse statement.

## 9.7 Robust small-ball and leave-one-out

These remain the structural route. The new obstruction does not attack leave-one-out itself. It shows that after freezing the leave-one-out geometry, a **uniform-over-all-delocalized-planes** small-ball theorem is impossible under fourth moment alone.

A positive proof must therefore average over, or conditionally analyze, the actual random frozen geometry rather than discard its coefficient randomness.

## 9.8 Expectation versus high probability

The fixed finite-convolution theorem and microjitter theorem are convergence-in-probability statements. Expectation convergence of the normalized logarithm remains a separate uniform-integrability problem.

The new deterministic projection obstruction is a small-ball route certificate, not a claim about the expectation of `log M`.

## 9.9 Finite versus uniformly bounded fourth moment

- levelwise finite fourth moment does not give uniform triangular coarse norm control;
- uniform fourth moment does give coarse norm control;
- uniform fourth moment alone is false as unrestricted triangular universality at/above `B_exact`;
- below `B_exact`, uniform fourth moment gives the all-support coarse gap but **still does not give deterministic microscopic smoothing even with perfect delocalization**;
- under fixed microscopic smoothing, fourth moment is unnecessary.
