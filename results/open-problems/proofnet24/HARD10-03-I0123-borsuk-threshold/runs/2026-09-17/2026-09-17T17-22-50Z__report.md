## 4. What a successful proof must contain that none of the routes contains

For a negative endpoint move (`U_B<=62`), a successful argument needs **both**:

1. an exact realization in dimension at most 62 whose diameter relation is certified; and
2. a genuine global obstruction to *every* 63-part smaller-diameter cover.

For a two-distance set this second obligation is exactly

\[
\theta(G)=\chi(\overline G)\ge64.
\]

The current work has repeatedly achieved the first item (rank/dimension 62) and repeatedly failed the second because an explicit cover of size at most 63 appears. Counting, eigenspace multiplicity, vertex count, and support minimization are therefore not enough. The missing ingredient is **cover hardness**.

For a positive endpoint move (`L_B>=4`), one would instead need a theorem for *all* bounded subsets of `R^4` giving five smaller-diameter parts. The current 2026 positive frontier `b(4)<=8` is still three parts away, and none of the I0123 graph/spectral arguments controls arbitrary `R^4` sets. Switching to that route now would therefore abandon the strongest finite leverage already exposed at dimension 62.
