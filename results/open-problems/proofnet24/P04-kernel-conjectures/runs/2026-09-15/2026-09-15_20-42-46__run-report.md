# 6. New theorem: exact exterior design of the volume-biased left nullspace

## `P04-T4-VOLUME-NULL-EXTERIOR-DESIGN-42` — **PROVED**

### 6.1 Statement

Let

\[
H\in\mathbb R^{n\times m},
\qquad
n=m+r,
\qquad
r\ge1,
\]

have iid centered rows. Assume full column rank almost surely and finite nonzero determinant expectation. Put

\[
G=H^TH,
\qquad
D=\det G,
\qquad
\frac{dQ}{dP}=\frac{D}{E_PD},
\]

and

\[
K=I_n-HG^{-1}H^T,
\]

the orthogonal projector onto `ker(H^T)`, of rank `r`.

Then for every `j=0,1,...,r`,

\[
\boxed{
E_Q[\wedge^jK]
=
\frac{\binom rj}{\binom nj}
I_{\wedge^j\mathbb R^n}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

At `j=1` this recovers P20's

\[
E_QK=\frac rnI_n.
\]

At `r=j=1` it recovers P04's exact volume-biased null-vector covariance.

### 6.2 Pluecker representation

Let the columns of `H` be `g_1,...,g_m in R^n`, and define their column wedge

\[
\eta=g_1\wedge\cdots\wedge g_m\in\wedge^m\mathbb R^n.
\]

Its squared norm is

\[
\|\eta\|^2=\det(H^TH)=D.
\]

Fix an orientation and define the unit `r`-vector

\[
\Omega=\frac{*\eta}{\sqrt D}.
\]

It spans `wedge^r ker(H^T)`. For every `r`-subset `R subset [n]`, its Pluecker coordinate is

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_R
=\varepsilon_R\frac{\det(H_{R^c})}{\sqrt D},
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

where `H_{R^c}` is the square `m x m` row submatrix and `epsilon_R` is the deterministic Hodge sign.

### 6.3 Contraction identity for `wedge^j K`

For `j`-subsets `I,J`, the matrix element of the exterior projector is the standard partial-contraction identity

\[
\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK)e_J\rangle
=
\sum_L
\epsilon(I,L)\epsilon(J,L)
\Omega_{I\cup L}\Omega_{J\cup L},
\tag{6.3}
\]

where the sum is over `(r-j)`-subsets `L` disjoint from both `I` and `J`; terms with repeated indices are zero.

Multiplying by `D` and using (6.2),

\[
D\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK)e_J\rangle
=
\sum_L \pm
\det(H_{(I\cup L)^c})
\det(H_{(J\cup L)^c}).
\tag{6.4}
\]

### 6.4 Off-diagonal cancellation by centering

Assume `I != J`. Choose an index

\[
a\in I\setminus J.
\]

For every nonzero term in (6.4), `a` is excluded from `(I union L)^c` but included in `(J union L)^c`, because `L` is disjoint from `I union J`.

Therefore the first determinant is independent of row `h_a`, whereas the second determinant is linear in row `h_a`. Conditioning on all rows except `h_a` and using

\[
Eh_a=0
\]

gives

\[
E_P\!\left[
\det(H_{(I\cup L)^c})
\det(H_{(J\cup L)^c})
\right]=0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Hence every off-diagonal exterior matrix element has zero `Q`-expectation.

### 6.5 Diagonal normalization

Now take `I=J`. Equation (6.4) becomes a sum of squares:

\[
D\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK)e_I\rangle
=
\sum_{L:|L|=r-j,\,L\cap I=\varnothing}
\det(H_{(I\cup L)^c})^2.
\tag{6.6}
\]

There are exactly

\[
\binom{n-j}{r-j}
\]

terms. By iid row exchangeability every square `m x m` minor has the same expectation; call it `delta_m`.

Cauchy-Binet gives

\[
D
=
\sum_{S\subset[n],\,|S|=m}
\det(H_S)^2,
\]

so

\[
E_PD
=
\binom nm\delta_m
=
\binom nr\delta_m.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
E_Q\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK)e_I\rangle
=
\frac{\binom{n-j}{r-j}}{\binom nr}
=
\frac{\binom rj}{\binom nj}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Together with the off-diagonal cancellation, this proves (6.1).

### 6.6 Consequences and route obstruction

1. **Uniform maximal null minors.** At `j=r`,
   \[
   E_Q|\Omega_R|^2=\binom nr^{-1}.
   \]
   Equivalently, the projection DPP generated by the left nullspace has an unconditional uniform `r`-subset under the volume law.

2. **All alternating quadratic diagnostics are already Haar.** For every deterministic linear map
   \[
   A:\wedge^j\mathbb R^n\to\mathcal K,
   \]
   one has
   \[
   E_Q\|A(\wedge^jK)\|_{HS}^2
   \]
   equal to the coefficient-Haar value after the obvious trace normalization. Thus exterior/Hodge quadratic information cannot distinguish the volume-biased nullspace from Haar at first exterior moment.

3. The theorem is **not** a full Haar law. Symmetric tensor moments, Jordan amplitudes, and inverse-volume ordinary-law effects can still differ. This is exactly the sector in which the live hard-edge obstruction already resides.

# 7. New theorem: growing-codimension radial-source scale under volume bias

## `P04-T4-VOLUME-GROWING-CODIM-SOURCE-SCALE-42` — **PROVED**

### 7.1 Setup

Use the actual radial source coordinate

\[
b_i
=\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{2}}\frac{S_i-d}{S_i},
\qquad
S_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\chi_d^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let

\[
\mu_d=Eb_i
=-\frac{\sqrt{2(d+2)}}{d-2},
\]

and

\[
\nu_d^2=\operatorname{Var}(b_i)
=\frac{d^2(d+2)}{(d-4)(d-2)^2}
=1+O(d^{-1}).
\tag{7.2}
\]

For sufficiently large `d`, the centered fourth moment

\[
M_{4,d}=E|b_i-\mu_d|^4
\]

is uniformly bounded. This follows directly from the inverse-chi-square formula

\[
E S^{-k}
=
\prod_{q=1}^k(d-2q)^{-1},
\qquad d>2k,
\]

applied to the rational polynomial in `S^{-1}` obtained from (7.1).

Let the angular block have `n=m+r` rows, with `r=r_d`, and tilt only by `D=det(H^TH)` as in Section 6. Since radial and angular variables are independent before tilting and the likelihood depends only on `H`, the source vector `b` remains independent of `K` under `Q`.

Put

\[
\xi=b-\mu_d\mathbf1.
\]

### 7.2 Centered quadratic contribution

Conditioned on `K`,

\[
E[\xi^TK\xi\mid K]=\nu_d^2\operatorname{tr}K=\nu_d^2r.
\]

For iid centered coordinates, the exact quadratic-form variance is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\xi^TK\xi\mid K)
=
2\nu_d^4\operatorname{tr}(K^2)
+
(M_{4,d}-3\nu_d^4)
\sum_iK_{ii}^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Because `K` is a rank-`r` orthogonal projector,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(K^2)=r,
\qquad
0\le K_{ii}\le1,
\qquad
\sum_iK_{ii}^2\le r.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
E_Q\left|
\frac{\xi^TK\xi}{r}-\nu_d^2
\right|^2
\le\frac Cr.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

with an absolute large-`d` constant `C`.

### 7.3 Source-mean cross term

Expand

\[
\|Kb\|^2=b^TKb
=
\xi^TK\xi
+2\mu_d\mathbf1^TK\xi
+\mu_d^2\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Conditionally on `K`, the middle term has variance

\[
4\mu_d^2\nu_d^2\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1.
\]

The `j=1` case of the new exterior theorem, equivalently P20's first-moment theorem, gives

\[
E_QK=\frac rnI_n,
\]

so

\[
E_Q\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1=r.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_Q\left|
\frac{2\mu_d\mathbf1^TK\xi}{r}
\right|^2
=O((rd)^{-1}).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

### 7.4 Mean-square shift

The last term in (7.5) is nonnegative. By (7.6),

\[
E_Q\left[
\frac{\mu_d^2\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1}{r}
\right]
=
\mu_d^2
=O(d^{-1}).
\tag{7.8}
\]

Thus it vanishes in `L^1(Q)` after normalization by `r`.

A coarse `L^2` bound is also available. Since a projection satisfies

\[
0\le\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1\le n,
\]

we have

\[
E_Q(\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1)^2
\le n\,E_Q\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1
=nr.
\]

Hence

\[
E_Q\left[
\frac{\mu_d^2\mathbf1^TK\mathbf1}{r}
\right]^2
\le
\mu_d^4\frac nr
=O\left(d^{-2}+r^{-1}\right),
\tag{7.9}
\]

because `n=m+r` and `m=Theta(d^2)`.

### 7.5 Conclusion

If

\[
r_d\to\infty,
\]

then (7.4), (7.7), (7.8), and `nu_d^2->1` imply

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\|Kb\|^2}{r_d}\to1
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

in `Q`-probability. The centered quadratic contribution even converges in `L^2` at rate `O(r^{-1/2})`, while the source-mean cross term is smaller, `O((rd)^-1/2)` after normalization.

The exact finite-`d` mean is inherited from P20:

\[
E_Q\|Kb\|^2
=r\,Eb_1^2
=
 r\frac{(d+2)(d+4)}{(d-2)(d-4)}.
\tag{7.11}
\]

For fixed `r`, P20 proves the sharper distributional statement

\[
\|Kb\|^2\Rightarrow\chi_r^2.
\]

Thus the volume-biased radial source now has a coherent two-regime description:

- fixed nullity: full chi-square law;
- growing nullity: correct chi-square scale `||Kb||^2/r ->1`.

This does not yet transfer to the ordinary iid angular law.

### 8.1 Hotelling identity

Now return to the original square hard-edge geometry, where `p=m+1` and `H` is `p x m`.

Define

\[
\bar h=\frac1pH^T\mathbf1,
\qquad
H_c=\left(I_p-\frac1p\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\right)H,
\qquad
C=H_c^TH_c.
\]

Let `v` be a unit vector spanning `ker(H^T)` and put

\[
s=\mathbf1^Tv.
\]

Decompose

\[
v=\frac sp\mathbf1+w,
\qquad
w\perp\mathbf1.
\]

Since `H_c` has `m=p-1` columns and, generically, column space `1^perp`, write uniquely

\[
w=H_ca.
\]

Then

\[
0=H^Tv
=H_c^Tw+s\bar h
=Ca+s\bar h,
\]

so

\[
a=-sC^{-1}\bar h
\]

and

\[
w=-sH_cC^{-1}\bar h.
\]

Orthogonality of the two components gives

\[
1=\|v\|^2
=
\frac{s^2}{p}
+s^2\bar h^TC^{-1}\bar h.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
q_d=\bar h^TC^{-1}\bar h,
\]

\[
\boxed{
(\mathbf1^Tv)^2
=\frac{p}{1+p q_d}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

This derivation is exact.

### 8.2 Rank-one-feature inverse vector

Let `F` be the `p x p` matrix whose `i`th row is the Frobenius-coordinate vector of

\[
u_i u_i^T\in\operatorname{Sym}_d
\]

in an orthonormal feature basis whose first vector is

\[
e_0=I/\sqrt d.
\]

The first column of `F` is exactly `1/sqrt(d)` times `1`. The remaining columns span the same traceless feature column space as `H`.

Set

\[
w_0=F^{-T}e_0.
\]

Then `F^Tw_0=e_0`. The traceless coordinates give

\[
H^Tw_0=0,
\]

so `w_0` is a scalar multiple of `v`. The trace coordinate gives

\[
\frac1{\sqrt d}\mathbf1^Tw_0=1,
\]

hence `1^T w_0=sqrt(d)`. Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|\mathbf1^Tv|}{\sqrt d}
=
\frac1{\|F^{-T}e_0\|}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Combining (8.1) and (8.2),

\[
\boxed{
\|F^{-T}e_0\|^2
=
\frac dp+dq_d.
}
\tag{8.3}

If `q=sqrt(d)F^{-T}e_0`, then `F^Tq=sqrt(d)e_0`, i.e.

\[
\sum_iq_i u_i u_i^T=I_d,
\qquad
\sum_iq_i=d,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
(\mathbf1^Tv)^2=\frac{d^2}{\|q\|^2}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the P20 claim, including its normalization, is correct.
