\[

\[

\tag{4.1}

\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.2}

# 6. New theorem: ordinary exterior design from row-sign symmetry

## `P04-T4-ORDINARY-SIGNSYMMETRIC-EXTERIOR-43` — **PROVED**

### 6.1 Statement

Let

\[
H\in\mathbb R^{n\times m},
\qquad n=m+r,
\]

have iid rows `h_1,...,h_n` such that

\[
h_i\stackrel d=-h_i.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Assume full column rank almost surely. Put

\[
K(H)=I-H(H^TH)^{-1}H^T.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then, for every `0<=j<=r`, under the ordinary product law,

\[
E[\wedge^jK]
=
\frac{\binom rj}{\binom nj}I.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### 6.2 Equivariance under independent row-sign flips

For any diagonal sign matrix

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_n),
\qquad \epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\},
\]

central symmetry and row independence imply

\[
SH\stackrel d=H.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The Gram matrix is unchanged:

\[
(SH)^T(SH)=H^TH.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{K(SH)=SK(H)S.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Passing to exterior powers,

\[
\wedge^jK(SH)
=(\wedge^jS)(\wedge^jK(H))(\wedge^jS).
\tag{6.6}
\]

### 6.3 Off-diagonal exterior coordinates vanish

Let `I,J` be distinct `j`-subsets of `[n]`. Choose

\[
a\in I\triangle J.
\]

Flip only row `a`. On the exterior basis vector `e_I`, `\wedge^jS` contributes the product of the signs indexed by `I`; on `e_J` it contributes the product indexed by `J`. Because `a` occurs in exactly one of `I,J`, the matrix element changes sign:

\[
\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK(SH))e_J\rangle
=-
\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK(H))e_J\rangle.
\]

The two random matrices have the same law, so

\[
E\langle e_I,(\wedge^jK)e_J\rangle=0,
\qquad I\ne J.
\tag{6.7}
\]

### 6.4 Diagonal normalization

Row exchangeability implies that every diagonal exterior coordinate has the same expectation. Since `K` is a rank-`r` orthogonal projection, its eigenvalues are `r` ones and `n-r` zeros. Therefore pointwise

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\wedge^jK)=\binom rj.
\tag{6.8}
\]

There are `binom(n,j)` diagonal exterior coordinates. Hence each has expectation

\[
\frac{\binom rj}{\binom nj}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Together with (6.7), this proves (6.3).

### 6.5 Significance and limitation

At `j=1`,

\[
E K=\frac rnI.
\tag{6.10}
\]

This is the exact ordinary-law first-moment identity that previously appeared naturally under determinant-volume bias for general centered rows. Here it holds without bias, but at the price of a much stronger symmetry assumption on the row law.

This theorem is **not** being applied to the actual harmonic orbit. Section 7 proves that the actual row law is strongly non-sign-symmetric even asymptotically.

The next source-side attack should exploit the new fact that inverse magnitude is no longer a mystery. Freeze `A`, diagonalize

\[

\[
\frac{
