# 6. New theorem: uniform radial-source CLT without null-vector delocalization

## `P04-T4-RADIAL-SOURCE-UNIFORM-CLT-41` — **PROVED**

Let `S_i iid chi_d^2`, and define `b_i` by (5.3). Put

\[
\mu_d=Eb_i
=-\frac{\sqrt{2(d+2)}}{d-2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For **every** dimension `n=n_d` and every deterministic unit vector

\[
v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n),
\qquad
\sum_i v_i^2=1,
\]

define

\[
X_d(v)=\sum_i v_i(b_i-\mu_d).
\]

Then, uniformly over `n` and `v`,

\[
\boxed{
\sup_x
\left|
P\{X_d(v)\le x\}-\Phi(x)
\right|
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

In fact the proof below gives a uniform `O(d^-1/3)` Kolmogorov bound.

Consequently, if `v=v_d` is any random unit vector independent of `b`, then conditional on `v`,

\[
X_d(v)\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\]
with an error bound uniform in the realized `v`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
v^Tb
=
X_d(v)+\mu_d\,\mathbf1^Tv.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If

\[
\frac{\mathbf1^Tv}{\sqrt d}\to\tau
\]

in probability, then

\[
\boxed{
v^Tb\Rightarrow N(-\sqrt2\,\tau,1).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf1^Tv=o_p(\sqrt d)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v^Tb\Rightarrow N(0,1).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

No assumption of the form `||v||_infinity ->0` is needed.

## 6.1 Exact one-coordinate moments

For `S~chi_d^2`,

\[
E S^{-1}=\frac1{d-2},
\qquad
E S^{-2}=\frac1{(d-2)(d-4)}.
\]

With `b` as in (5.3), direct algebra gives

\[
Eb=\mu_d
=-\frac{\sqrt{2(d+2)}}{d-2},
\tag{6.6}
\]

\[
E b^2
=
\frac{(d+2)(d+4)}{(d-2)(d-4)},
\tag{6.7}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(b)
=
\frac{d^2(d+2)}{(d-4)(d-2)^2}
=1+O(d^{-1}).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

## 6.2 Coupling to a normalized chi-square fluctuation

Write

\[
S=\sum_{a=1}^d g_a^2,
\qquad
T=\frac{S-d}{\sqrt{2d}}.
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(b,T)
=
\frac{\sqrt{d(d+2)}}{d-2}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since `ET=0` and `Var(T)=1`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[(b-\mu_d-T)^2]
&=
1+rac{d^2(d+2)}{(d-4)(d-2)^2}
-rac{2\sqrt{d(d+2)}}{d-2}\\
&=
\frac4d+O(d^{-2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Thus there is an absolute `C` such that for all sufficiently large `d`,

\[
E[(b-\mu_d-T)^2]\le\frac Cd.
\tag{6.11}
\]

For independent copies and any unit `v`, independence and centering imply

\[
E\left|
\sum_i v_i[(b_i-\mu_d)-T_i]
\right|^2
\le\frac Cd,
\tag{6.12}
\]

uniformly over the dimension and the vector. This is the key reason coordinate delocalization is unnecessary.

## 6.3 Uniform Berry-Esseen for the comparison sum

Set

\[
\xi_{ia}=\frac{g_{ia}^2-1}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Then

\[
T_i=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{a=1}^d\xi_{ia}.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_i v_iT_i
=
\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{a=1}^dY_a,
\qquad
Y_a=\sum_i v_i\xi_{ia}.
\]

The `Y_a` are iid over `a`, with

\[
EY_a=0,
\qquad
EY_a^2=1.
\]

Because `E xi^4=15`, the exact fourth-moment formula for a weighted sum of independent centered variance-one variables is

\[
EY_a^4
=
3+12\sum_i v_i^4
\le15.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Therefore

\[
E|Y_a|^3\le15^{3/4}
\]

uniformly in `v`, and classical Berry-Esseen yields

\[
\sup_x
\left|
P\left\{\sum_i v_iT_i\le x\right\}-\Phi(x)
\right|
\le\frac{C}{\sqrt d}.
\tag{6.14}
\]

Combining (6.12) with Chebyshev and a Gaussian smoothing interval of width `epsilon=d^-1/3` gives

\[
\sup_x
|P\{X_d(v)\le x\}-\Phi(x)|
\le C d^{-1/3},
\tag{6.15}
\]

uniformly in `n,v`. This proves (6.2).

Finally,

\[
\sqrt d\,\mu_d
=-\frac{\sqrt{2d(d+2)}}{d-2}
\longrightarrow-\sqrt2,

which proves (6.4)–(6.5).

# 7. New exact theorem: volume bias forces unit mean-square alignment

## `P04-T4-VOLUME-ALIGNMENT-41` — **PROVED**

Let `H` be an `(m+1) x m` matrix with iid centered rows `h_i in R^m`, with sufficient moments that the following determinants are integrable and with full column rank almost surely. Let `P` denote the ordinary product law. Define the Gram-volume-biased law

\[
\frac{dQ}{dP}(H)
=
\frac{\det(H^TH)}{E_P\det(H^TH)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `v` be a unit vector spanning `ker(H^T)`. Then

\[
\boxed{
E_Q(\mathbf1^Tv)^2=1.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This requires only centered iid rows; no Gaussianity, isotropy or rotational invariance is needed.

## 7.1 Cofactor proof

For each `i`, let

\[
c_i=(-1)^{i+1}\det(H_{-i}),
\]

where `H_{-i}` is the square `m x m` matrix obtained by deleting row `i`. The cofactor vector `c` satisfies

\[
H^Tc=0.
\]

Cauchy-Binet gives

\[
\boxed{
\|c\|^2
=
\sum_i c_i^2
=
\det(H^TH).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus we may take `v=c/||c||`, up to an irrelevant global sign. Then

\[
E_Q(\mathbf1^Tv)^2
=
\frac{E_P(\sum_i c_i)^2}{E_P\sum_i c_i^2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

For `i != j`, condition on all rows except row `i`. The cofactor `c_i` does not depend on row `i`, while `c_j` is linear in row `i`. Since `E h_i=0`,

\[
E[c_ic_j]=0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
E_P(\sum_i c_i)^2
=
\sum_iE_Pc_i^2
=
E_P\det(H^TH),
\]

which proves (7.2).

Equivalently, `sum_i c_i` is, up to a fixed sign convention, the determinant of the square augmented matrix `[H,1]`; equation (7.2) is an exact cofactor second-moment identity.

## 7.2 Actual harmonic consequence under volume bias

The pure harmonic row (5.2) is centered, and the volume tilt (7.1) depends only on `H`; therefore the independent source vector `b` remains independent of `H` under `Q`.

By (7.2),

\[
Q\left\{
\frac{|\mathbf1^Tv|}{\sqrt d}>\varepsilon
\right\}
\le
\frac1{\varepsilon^2d}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Hence under the actual harmonic Gram-volume law,

\[
\frac{\mathbf1^Tv}{\sqrt d}\to0
\]

in probability. Combining with `P04-T4-RADIAL-SOURCE-UNIFORM-CLT-41`,

\[
\boxed{
v^Tb\Rightarrow N(0,1)
\quad\text{under the volume-biased angular law}.}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus the radial source is already Gaussian at the scalar distance-to-span level after volume bias.

### Crucial limitation

The original hard-edge problem is under the **ordinary iid angular law**, not `Q`. It is invalid to transfer (7.7) back to the ordinary law without controlling the reciprocal volume likelihood. Indeed

\[
\frac{dP}{dQ}
\propto
