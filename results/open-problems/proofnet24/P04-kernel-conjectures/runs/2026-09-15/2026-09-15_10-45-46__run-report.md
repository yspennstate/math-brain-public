## 3.2 Exact Gaussian-kernel Mercer series

Write `gamma=h^{-2}`. On the unit sphere,
\[
K_h(x,y)=e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma x\cdot y}.
\]
The exact degree-`s` eigenvalue is
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s}
=
e^{-\gamma}
\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
\frac{\gamma^{s+2r}}
{2^r r!\,d(d+2)\cdots(d+2(s+r)-2)}.
}
\tag{Mercer}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\lambda_{d,s}
=
e^{-\gamma}\Gamma(d/2)(2/\gamma)^{d/2-1}
I_{s+d/2-1}(\gamma).
\]
Every term is positive, hence termwise
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{d,s+1}\le\frac{\gamma}{d+2s}\lambda_{d,s},
}
\tag{Mercer-ratio}
\]
uniformly in growing `s`. This is the factorial tail mechanism used below.

## 3.3 Growing-degree hypercontractivity / Rosenthal scale

At moment order `p=R`, the deliberately conservative accumulations remain

## 6.1 Pure-harmonic projection kernel

Let `E=Sym_d^0`, choose a Frobenius-orthonormal basis `(A_a)_{a=1}^m`, and set
\[
h_a(u)=\sqrt{\frac{d(d+2)}2}\,u^\top A_au,
\qquad u\sim\mu={\rm Unif}(S^{d-1}).
\]
Then `(h_a)` is orthonormal in `L^2(mu)`, and the rank-`m` projection kernel is
\[
K(u,v)=\sum_{a=1}^m h_a(u)h_a(v).
\]
The normalized kernel is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\rho(u,v):=\frac{K(u,v)}m
=
\frac{d(u^\top v)^2-1}{d-1}.
}
\tag{rho}
\]
Thus `rho(u,u)=1` and
\[
\int \rho(x,z)\rho(z,y)d\mu(z)=\frac1m\rho(x,y),
\qquad
\int \rho(x,z)^2d\mu(z)=\frac1m.
\tag{proj}
\]
For fixed `u`, if `T=(u^\top V)^2`, then
\[
T\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,(d-1)/2),
\qquad
E T^q=\frac{(1/2)_q}{(d/2)_q}.
\]
Hence, for every fixed integer `q`,
\[
\boxed{
E|\rho(u,V)|^{2q}\le C_qm^{-q}.
}
\tag{mom}
\]
Only `q=2,3` is used below.

## 6.2 Ordered DPP marginal density

For `r<=m`, let `P_m^{(r)}` be the ordered `r`-point marginal of the rank-`m` projection DPP. Relative to `mu^r`,
\[
f_r(u_1,\ldots,u_r)
=
\frac{\det[K(u_i,u_j)]}{(m)_r}
=
c_{m,r}\det(I+R),
\]
where
\[
c_{m,r}=\frac{m^r}{(m)_r},
\qquad
R_{ii}=0,
\qquad
R_{ij}=\rho(u_i,u_j).
\]
Of course
\[
f_r\ge0,
\qquad
E_{\mu^r}f_r=1.
\]

## 6.3 Quadratic trace

Put
\[
T_2=\operatorname{Tr}R^2
=2\sum_{i<j}\rho_{ij}^2.
\]
Then
\[
E T_2=\frac{(r)_2}{m}.
\]
Two distinct edges are independent if disjoint; if they share one vertex, their squared terms are still uncorrelated because `int rho(x,z)^2 dmu(z)=1/m` is constant. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_2)
\le 2(r)_2E\rho^4
\le C\frac{r^2}{m^2},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{E|T_2-ET_2|\le C r/m.}
\tag{T2fluc}
\]

Also, for `r<=m/2`,
\[
\log c_{m,r}
=
\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}-\log(1-j/m),
\]
and Taylor's inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
\left|\log c_{m,r}-\frac12ET_2\right|
\le C\frac{r^3}{m^2}.
}
\tag{norm-cancel}
\]

## 6.4 Cubic trace

Let
\[
T_3=\operatorname{Tr}R^3.
\]
For three distinct indices, the projection identity gives
\[
E[\rho_{12}\rho_{23}\rho_{31}]=\frac1{m^2},
\]
so
\[
ET_3=\frac{(r)_3}{m^2}.
\]
For an unordered triple `S={a,b,c}`, put
\[
\tau_S=\rho_{ab}\rho_{bc}\rho_{ca};
\qquad T_3=6\sum_{|S|=3}\tau_S.
\]
If two triples are disjoint, they are independent. If they share exactly one vertex, conditioning on that vertex gives the constant conditional mean `1/m^2`, so covariance is zero. If they share an edge,
\[
E[\tau_{abc}\tau_{abd}]
=
\frac{E\rho_{ab}^4}{m^2}
=O(m^{-4}).
\]
For one triple,
\[
E\tau_S^2
\le
(E|\rho|^6)^{1/3}(E|\rho|^6)^{1/3}(E|\rho|^6)^{1/3}
=O(m^{-3}).
\]
Counting overlap patterns yields
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(T_3)
\le
C\left(\frac{r^3}{m^3}+\frac{r^4}{m^4}\right).
}
\tag{T3var}
\]
Hence
\[
E|T_3|
\le
C\left(
\frac rm+\frac{r^3}{m^2}
\right)
\qquad (r\le m).
\tag{T3L1}
\]

## 6.5 Fourth trace and operator norm

For

\boxed{

\le

\tag{Gbad}

E[|\log f_r|\,1_{\mathcal G}]
\le
C\left(\frac rm+\frac{r^3}{m^2}\right).
}
\tag{logL1}
\]

Now use the density normalization rather than any exploding `L^infinity` bound. Because `Ef_r=1`,
\[
\|P_m^{(r)}-\mu^r\|_{TV}
=E(1-f_r)_+.
\]
On `G`, `(1-f_r)_+ <= |log f_r|`; on `G^c` it is at most one. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|P_m^{(r)}-\mu^r\|_{TV}
&\le
E[|\log f_r|1_{\mathcal G}]+P(\mathcal G^c)\\
&\le
C\left(\frac rm+\frac{r^3}{m^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves `P04-T4-SUPERLINEAR-DPP-TV-INVISIBILITY-31`.

In particular,
\[
r=o(m^{2/3})\implies TV\to0,
\]
i.e.
\[
\boxed{r=o(d^{4/3})\implies TV\to0.}
\]

## 6.7 Conditional local invisibility

Let `Y` be one labeled DPP point and `B_r` be `r` other labeled points. The one-point marginal is exactly `mu`. By triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\|\operatorname{Law}(Y,B_r)-\mu\otimes\operatorname{Law}(B_r)\|_{TV}\\
&\quad\le
\|P_m^{(r+1)}-\mu^{r+1}\|_{TV}
+\|P_m^{(r)}-\mu^r\|_{TV}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The left-hand side equals
\[
E_{B_r}\|\operatorname{Law}(Y\mid B_r)-\mu\|_{TV}.
\]
Hence this averaged conditional TV also vanishes for every `r=o(d^(4/3))`.
