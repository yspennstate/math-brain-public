`L_M(w) = (1/(2N)) sum_{i=1}^n sum_{j=1}^2 (t_i-(x_ij^M)^T w)^2 + (lambda/2)||w||^2.`

Both augmented rows associated with one original input are replaced **jointly as a single block**. The common latent variable creating correlation between the two views is preserved in the matched Gaussian block, and the teacher coordinate is included jointly in the comparison.

Delete both rows of original sample `i`. Let

`H_- = lambda I + (1/N) X_-^T X_-`, `B=H_-^{-1}`,

let `X_i` be the `2 x m` row block, `e_i=t_i 1_2-X_i w_-`, and

`Q_i=(1/N) X_i B X_i^T`.

`N_{d,k}=d^k/k! * exp(O(k^2/d))`.

# 6. New theorem: dimension-free negative moments for one harmonic quadratic

## `P04-T4-ALLQ-UNIFORM-NEGATIVE-MOMENT-47` — PROVED, scalar input

Let `A in Sym_d^0` with `||A||_F=1` and define

`f_A(U)=<A,h(U)>`.

For every fixed `0<r<1/2`, there is a finite constant `C_r`, depending only on `r`, such that

`sup_{d>=2} sup_A E |f_A(U)|^(-r) <= C_r`.      (6.1)

This independently recovers P26's scalar theorem; the point of rederiving it here is to use it as an all-rank fresh-row recursion.

### 6.1 Gaussian lift and exact moments

Let `G~N(0,I_d)` and

`Q_A(G)=G^T A G`.

Since `tr A=0` and `||A||_F=1`,

`E Q_A=0`,

`E Q_A^2=2`.

Writing the eigenvalues of `A` as `lambda_i`, with `sum lambda_i=0` and `sum lambda_i^2=1`, the fourth cumulant is `48 sum lambda_i^4`; hence

`E Q_A^4 = 12 + 48 sum_i lambda_i^4 <= 60`.      (6.2)

Therefore interpolation gives a universal positive lower bound on the `L1` norm:

`E|Q_A| >= ||Q_A||_2^3/||Q_A||_4^2 >= sqrt(2/15)`.      (6.3)

### 6.2 Carbery-Wright anti-concentration

The Carbery-Wright theorem for degree-`k` polynomials under a log-concave probability measure gives, in the standard degree-two form used here,

`P{|Q_A|<=t} <= C_CW t^(1/2)`      (6.4)

with an absolute constant, after using the universal normalization (6.3). Standard Gaussian measure is log-concave, and the polynomial degree is exactly two.

Primary-source note: the bibliographic record is Anthony Carbery and James Wright, *Distributional and L^q norm inequalities for polynomials over convex bodies in R^n*, Mathematical Research Letters 8(3), 233-248 (2001), Theorem 8. The publisher PDF endpoint returned a 403 on direct open in this runtime; the exact theorem statement/hypotheses were cross-checked against multiple published restatements. No stronger form is imported.

Layer-cake then gives, for `0<r<1/2`,

`E |Q_A|^(-r)`

` <= 1 + C_CW r int_0^1 t^(-r-1/2) dt`

` = 1 + C_CW r/(1/2-r)`.      (6.5)

Thus the Gaussian quadratic negative moment is dimension-free in this range.

### 6.3 Return to the sphere

Write `G=RU`, `R=||G||`, with `R` independent of `U`. Since

`Q_A=R^2 U^T A U`,

`f_A(U)=c_d U^T A U`,

we have

`E |f_A(U)|^(-r)`

` = c_d^(-r) E|Q_A|^(-r) / E R^(-2r)`.      (6.6)

Exactly,

`E R^(-2r)=2^(-r) Gamma(d/2-r)/Gamma(d/2)`.

Using the standard gamma-ratio bound `Gamma(x)/Gamma(x-r) <= x^r` for `x>=1`, `0<r<1`, and `c_d=sqrt(d(d+2)/2)`,

`c_d^(-r) / E R^(-2r) <= 2^(r/2)`.      (6.7)

Combining (6.5)-(6.7) proves (6.1), for example with

`C_r = 2^(r/2) [1+C_CW r/(1/2-r)]`.

### Scope

This dimension-free proof deliberately stops at `r<1/2`. P20's sharper plane-by-plane argument reaches every `r<1`, but with dimension-dependent constants. P04/P20's regular-zero analysis shows that the final determinant-selected critical exponent is `3/2`; the present theorem is a **uniformity** theorem, not a claim that `1/2` is the true scalar threshold.

### Proof by sequential fresh-row exposure

Expose the fresh rows `V_P(U_1),...,V_P(U_q)` one at a time. Let

`D_j = dist(V_P(U_j), span(V_P(U_1),...,V_P(U_{j-1})))`.

`D_j >= |c_j^T V_P(U_j)|`.      (7.2)

Define

`A(c_j)=sum_{a=1}^q (c_j)_a A_a`.

`A(c_j) in Sym_d^0`, `||A(c_j)||_F=1`,

and

`c_j^T V_P(U_j)=f_{A(c_j)}(U_j)`.      (7.3)

`E[D_j^(-r) | U_1,...,U_{j-1}] <= C_r`.      (7.4)

`|det Y|=prod_{j=1}^q D_j`.

Iterating (7.4) proves

This is the same fresh-row mechanism P20 uses for finite moments, but with the dimension-free Carbery-Wright scalar input. It extends P26's dimension-free rank-two determinant theorem to every residual rank.
