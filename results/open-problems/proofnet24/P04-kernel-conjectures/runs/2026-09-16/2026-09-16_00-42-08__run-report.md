# 6. New theorem — universal `3/2` divergence barrier for harmonic inverse-wedge radius

## `P04-T4-INVERSE-WEDGE-THREEHALF-DIVERGENCE-46` — PROVED

Let `P` be **any deterministic rank-`q` orthogonal projection** in

`E=Sym_d^0`, `1<=q<=m`,

and choose a Frobenius-orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_q` of `Ran(P)`.

For `u in S^{d-1}`, define the evaluation vector

`y_P(u)=(<A_1,h(u)>,...,<A_q,h(u)>) in R^q`.

Let `U_1,...,U_q` be iid uniform on the sphere and let `Y` be the `q x q` matrix whose `j`-th column is `y_P(U_j)`. Put

`W_P(U_1,...,U_q)=det Y`,

`K=Y^T Y`, so `det K=W_P^2`.

Because the projected row is isotropic in `R^q`,

`E det K=q!`.

Define the determinant-selected conditional law

`dQ_P = (det K/q!) dmu^q`,

and define the inverse-wedge squared radius

`R_P = 1/det K`.

This is exactly the final-`q` conditional law/radius arising in the full square determinant-tilted inverse-column representation.

Then for every `alpha>=3/2`,

`E_{Q_P} R_P^alpha = infinity`.      (6.1)

More quantitatively, for every deterministic `P` there are constants `c(P)>0`, `L_0(P)<infinity` such that

`Q_P{R_P>L} >= c(P) L^(-3/2)` for `L>=L_0(P)`.      (6.2)

No uniform lower-tail constant over all planes is claimed.

### 6.1 Isotropy normalizer

The functions

`f_a(u)=<A_a,h(u)>`

satisfy

`E f_a(U)f_b(U)=delta_ab`.

Thus the columns of `Y` are iid centered isotropic vectors in `R^q`. Expanding the determinant and using independence gives

`E(det Y)^2=q!`,

which is the claimed selected-law normalizer.

### 6.2 Existence of a regular determinant zero for every plane

First note that the coherent orbit spans all of `E`: if `A in E` satisfies

`<A,h(u)>=0` for every `u`,

then `u^T A u=0` for every unit `u`, hence `A=0`.

Therefore the `q` functions `f_1,...,f_q` are linearly independent, and their evaluation vectors `y_P(u)` span `R^q`. We may choose points

`u_1^0,...,u_{q-1}^0`

such that

`y_P(u_1^0),...,y_P(u_{q-1}^0)`

are linearly independent. For `q=1`, this list is empty.

Let `c in R^q` be a nonzero vector orthogonal to their span. The determinant with the first `q-1` columns frozen is a nonzero constant multiple of

`g(u)=c.y_P(u)=<A_c,h(u)>`,

where

`A_c=sum_a c_a A_a`.

The matrix `A_c` is nonzero, symmetric, and traceless. Hence it has at least one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Choose unit eigenvectors `e_+`, `e_-` with eigenvalues `lambda_+>0>lambda_-`, and put

`u_0 = sqrt((-lambda_-)/(lambda_+-lambda_-)) e_+`

`      + sqrt(lambda_+/(lambda_+-lambda_-)) e_-`.

Then

`u_0^T A_c u_0=0`,

while `A_c u_0 !=0`.

Since

`g(u)=sqrt(d(d+2)/2) u^T A_c u`,

the spherical tangent gradient at `u_0` is

`grad_S g(u_0)=2 sqrt(d(d+2)/2) A_c u_0 !=0`,

because the radial correction vanishes when `u_0^T A_c u_0=0`.

Therefore

`x_0=(u_1^0,...,u_{q-1}^0,u_0)`

is a **regular zero** of the determinant function `W_P` on `(S^{d-1})^q`.

### 6.3 Local coarea / implicit-coordinate divergence

By the implicit function theorem, there is a local coordinate chart `(t,z)` around `x_0` in which

`t=W_P`

and the product spherical measure has a smooth density bounded above and below by positive constants.

Hence for every `r>=1`,

`int_near_x0 |W_P|^{-r} dmu^q = infinity`,

because the one-dimensional factor contains

`int_{-epsilon}^{epsilon} |t|^{-r} dt`.

Under `Q_P`,

`E R_P^alpha`

` = (1/q!) int (det K)^(1-alpha) dmu^q`

` = (1/q!) int |W_P|^{2(1-alpha)} dmu^q`.

For `alpha>=3/2`,

`r=2(alpha-1)>=1`,

so (6.1) follows.

For the tail, restrict to the same chart. There,

`Q_P{R_P>L}`

` = (1/q!) int_{|W_P|<L^-1/2} W_P^2 dmu^q`

is bounded below by a positive constant times

`int_{|t|<L^-1/2} t^2 dt`,

which equals a positive constant times `L^-3/2`. This proves (6.2).

### 6.4 Scope

The theorem proves divergence for `alpha>=3/2`. It does **not** prove finiteness for every `1<alpha<3/2`; singular determinant zeros could in principle create additional integrability restrictions. P20 already gives finite conditional moments for powers `0<=alpha<=1` by concavity. The interval `(1,3/2)` remains a legitimate target for sharper harmonic anti-concentration.

`chi^2(Qtilde || Q | P)`

` = E_Q[(q!R_P-1)^2 | P]`.

By the theorem above, `E_Q R_P^2=infinity` for every deterministic harmonic residual plane. Therefore

`chi^2(Qtilde || Q | P)=infinity`      (7.1)

for every `P`, and hence the unconditional chi-square divergence is also infinite.

More generally,

`E_Q[(dQtilde/dQ)^alpha | P]=infinity`

for every `alpha>=3/2`. Thus the exact de-selection density is not conditionally in `L^(3/2)`, and in particular is not in `L^2`.

### Consequence

Any route that tries to transfer a residual-plane estimate from determinant-selected final rows to fully fresh final rows by a Cauchy-Schwarz / chi-square / Renyi-2 change of measure is impossible even before addressing the Jordan observable. This is a genuine integrability obstruction, not a missing constant.

P23's first-moment de-selection identity remains completely valid. The obstruction begins only when one asks for sufficiently high powers of the density.
