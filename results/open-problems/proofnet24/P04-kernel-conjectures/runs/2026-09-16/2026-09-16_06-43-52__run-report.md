\[

\operatorname{tr}(q^2q_0)=\kappa_dt,

\operatorname{tr}(qq_0^2)=\kappa_dt.
\]

Expand the cube:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(y^3)
&=\operatorname{tr}(q^3)
-3x\operatorname{tr}(q^2q_0)
+3x^2\operatorname{tr}(qq_0^2)
-x^3\operatorname{tr}(q_0^3)\\
&=\kappa_dn_d(1-3x^2+2x^3)\\
&=\boxed{\kappa_dn_d(1-x)^2(1+2x)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Substitution into the exact mark formula (5.3) gives

\[
M_{m-1}
=\frac{c_d^2\kappa_d}{m-1}
E\left[
\frac{(1-x)^2(1+2x)}{1+x}
\right].
\tag{7.9}
\]

By rotational invariance, if

\[
S=(u_0^Tu)^2,
\]

then

\[
S\sim\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\frac12,\frac{d-1}{2}\right),
\qquad
x=\frac{dS-1}{d-1}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus `Ex=0`. Also

\[
E S^2=\frac{3}{d(d+2)},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{E x^2=\frac{2}{(d-1)(d+2)}.}
\tag{7.11}
\]

Algebraically,

\[
\frac{(1-x)^2(1+2x)}{1+x}
=2x^2-5x+5-\frac4{1+x}.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Since

\[
\frac1{1+x}=1-x+\frac{x^2}{1+x},
\]

and `Ex=0`, (7.12) yields the exact expectation identity

\[
E\left[
\frac{(1-x)^2(1+2x)}{1+x}
\right]
=\boxed{
1-2E\frac{x^2(1-x)}{1+x}
}.
\tag{7.13}
\]

Finally,

\[
\frac{c_d^2\kappa_d}{m-1}
=\boxed{\frac{d^2-4}{d^2+d-4}}.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Equations (7.9)--(7.14) prove (0.1).

For the explicit bounds, note

\[
-\frac1{d-1}\le x\le1,
\]

hence for `d>2`,

\[
0\le\frac{1-x}{1+x}\le\frac d{d-2}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Combining (7.11), (7.13), and (7.15),

\[
1-\frac{4d}{(d-2)(d-1)(d+2)}
\le
E\left[
\frac{(1-x)^2(1+2x)}{1+x}
\right]
\le1.
\tag{7.16}
\]

This proves (0.3), and since the correction in brackets is `1+O(d^-2)` while

\[
\frac{d^2-4}{d^2+d-4}=1-\frac1d+O(d^{-2}),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{M_{m-1}=1-\frac1d+O(d^{-2}).}
\tag{7.17}
\]

No inverse-moment estimate, local CLT, or asymptotic replacement is used.
