# 6. New theorem: universal determinant-volume partition function

## `P04-T4-VOLUME-PARTITION-HESSIAN-48` — PROVED

Let `y_1,...,y_k in R^m` be iid centered isotropic rows,

\[
E[y_i y_i^T]=I_m,
\]

and let `Y` be the `k x m` matrix with these rows. For any positive-definite `m x m` matrix `M`, define

\[
Z_k(M)=E\det(YMY^T).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{Z_k(M)=k!\,e_k(M),}
\tag{6.1}
\]

where `e_k(M)` is the `k`th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues of `M`.

### Proof

Expand the determinant by permutations:

\[
\det(YMY^T)
=\sum_{\pi\in S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)
\prod_{i=1}^k y_i^TMy_{\pi(i)}.
\]

For a fixed permutation, each row `y_i` appears exactly twice in the product: once as a left vector and once as a right vector. Since the rows are independent, integrating one row at a time uses only

\[
E[y_i y_i^T]=I_m.
\]

Equivalently, the same computation is the squared exterior-volume identity for `y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge y_k` after applying `M^{1/2}`. The expectation is precisely the trace of `\wedge^k M` times `k!`, namely `k!e_k(M)`. No fourth or higher row moment enters. QED.

At `M=I`,

\[
Z_k(I)=k!\binom mk.
\]

Let `Q` denote the orthogonal projector onto the row span of `Y`, on the determinant-selected law with density proportional to `det(YY^T)`. Differentiating the fixed-matrix determinant gives

\[
D\det(YMY^T)|_{I}[H]
=\det(YY^T)\,\operatorname{tr}(QH),
\]

so (6.1) recovers the exact first design

\[
\boxed{E_Q Q=\frac{k}{m}I.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The second derivative is

\[
\frac{D^2\det(YMY^T)|_I[H,K]}{\det(YY^T)}
=
\operatorname{tr}(QH)\operatorname{tr}(QK)
-
\operatorname{tr}(QHQK).
\tag{6.3}
\]

On the elementary-symmetric side,

\[
\frac{D^2e_k(I)[H,K]}{e_k(I)}
=
\frac{k(k-1)}{m(m-1)}
\left[
\operatorname{tr}H\operatorname{tr}K-\operatorname{tr}(HK)
\right].
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E_Q\!\left[
\operatorname{tr}(QH)\operatorname{tr}(QK)
-\operatorname{tr}(QHQK)
\right]
=
\frac{k(k-1)}{m(m-1)}
\left[\operatorname{tr}H\operatorname{tr}K-\operatorname{tr}(HK)\right].
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Now put `P=I-Q`, of rank `s=m-k`. Using (6.2) to expand the cross terms in (6.4), one obtains the dual residual identity

\[
\boxed{
E_Q\!\left[
\operatorname{tr}(PH)\operatorname{tr}(PK)
-\operatorname{tr}(PHPK)
\right]
=
\frac{s(s-1)}{m(m-1)}
\left[\operatorname{tr}H\operatorname{tr}K-\operatorname{tr}(HK)\right].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This holds for every iid isotropic row law for which the determinant selection is defined. It is not special to Gaussian or to the harmonic orbit.

\[
\boxed{

\tag{7.1}
\]

### 7.2 The second contraction is the positive Jordan-product energy

Similarly,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\gamma=1}^m
\operatorname{tr}(P L_{T_\gamma}P L_{T_\gamma})
=J(P),
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

where

\[
J(P)=
\sum_{a,b=1}^s
\left\|
\Pi_E\frac{A_aA_b+A_bA_a}{2}
\right\|_F^2.
\]

Put

\[
\delta(P)=\frac12\sum_{a,b}\|[A_a,A_b]\|_F^2.
\]

A direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
J(P)=e(P)+\frac{s(s-1)}d-\frac12\delta(P).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Indeed, if

\[
\mathcal A=\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(A_a^2A_b^2)
=\operatorname{tr}C(P)^2,
\]

and

\[
R=\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(A_aA_bA_aA_b),
\]

then `delta=mathcal A-R`, while the squared symmetric-product energy before removing traces is `(mathcal A+R)/2`.

### 7.3 Exact Hilbert-Schmidt norm of `L_C`

For traceless Frobenius-unit `C`, diagonalize `C`. On the full symmetric matrix space, the anticommutator map `B -> (CB+BC)/2` has squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm

\[
\frac{d+2}{4}.
\]

Relative to `Sym_d = span(I/sqrt d) \oplus E`, the scalar-to-traceless and traceless-to-scalar blocks each have squared norm `1/d`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}_E(L_C^2)
=\frac{(d-2)(d+4)}{4d}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Also `tr_E L_C=0` for traceless `C`.

### 7.4 Hessian contraction and cancellation

Insert `H=K=L_{T_\gamma}` into (6.5) and sum over `gamma`. By (7.1)--(7.4),

\[
E[e(P)-J(P)]
=
-\frac{s(s-1)}{m(m-1)}
\sum_{\gamma=1}^m\operatorname{tr}(L_{T_\gamma}^2)
\]

\[
=
-\frac{s(s-1)(d-2)(d+4)}{4d(m-1)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

But (7.3) says pointwise

\[
e(P)-J(P)
=-\frac{s(s-1)}d+\frac12\delta(P).
\]

Solving gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
E\delta(P_s)
=
\frac{d\,s(s-1)}{2(m-1)}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This reproduces the earlier exact commutator mean and nothing more: **the unknown `e(P)` cancels identically**. Thus the covariance-volume Hessian controls the antisymmetric/commutator sector but is exactly blind to the symmetric Jordan defect that remains open.

This is a rigorous failed-route certificate, not an assertion that all possible partition-function ideas fail. It says specifically that the natural coefficient covariance deformation `M -> E det(YMY^T)` and all information obtained from differentiating it cannot by itself determine `e_2`.

# 8. Strong no-go: identical full partition function, different Jordan defect

\[

\frac{\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k},-k,0,\ldots,0)}{\sqrt{k(k+1)}},
\qquad k=1,2,3.

Each off-diagonal basis element has fourth trace `1/2`, contributing `3` in total. For the diagonal directions,

\[

Now instead take iid standard Gaussian coefficient rows in `R^9`. This law is also centered and isotropic, so by (6.1) it has the **same partition function**

for every positive-definite `M`.

\[
E[\operatorname{tr}(PH)^2]
=
\frac{2s(m-s)}{m(m-1)(m+2)}\operatorname{tr}(H^2).

Applying this to the Jordan operators `L_{T_\gamma}` and using (7.4) yields

\[
e_s^H
=\frac{s(m-s)}{m-1}\,c_H,
\qquad

At `d=4,m=9,s=2`,

\[
c_H=\frac2{11},
\qquad
\boxed{e_2^H=\frac7{22}.}
\tag{8.3}

Finally,

\[

Thus two centered isotropic iid row laws have exactly the same `Z_7(M)` for **every** coefficient covariance deformation `M`, hence the same derivatives of every order, while their determinant-selected rank-two Jordan defects differ.
