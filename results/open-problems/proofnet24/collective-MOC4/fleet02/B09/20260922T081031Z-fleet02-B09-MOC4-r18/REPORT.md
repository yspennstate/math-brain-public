The bound (16) is sharp at the level of genuine Spin(6) block rows for every probability vector `(R_1,R_2,R_3)`.

### Proof of the universal cap

Identify the two-dimensional real space of traceless symmetric `2 x 2` matrices with `R^2` using the `rho` inner product. Let `v_j` represent `V_j`, so `|v_j|=t_j`. Equation (12) gives

\[

\]

Let `T=t_1+t_2+t_3` and `m=max_j t_j`. The closed-triangle condition gives

\[

\qquad\text{hence}\qquad m\le T/2. \tag{17}
\]

By (13) and `sum R_j=1`, `T<=1`. Therefore

\[
E=\sum_jt_j^2\le m\sum_jt_j=mT\le\frac{T^2}{2}\le\frac12,
\]

proving (15).

### Proof of the mass-sensitive cap

If `r<=1/2`, then simply `t_j<=R_j` gives

\[
E\le\sum_jR_j^2=F.
\]

If `r>1/2`, let `k` be the unique largest-mass index. From `sum v_j=0`,

\[
t_k\le\sum_{j\ne k}t_j\le\sum_{j\ne k}R_j=1-r.
\]

Thus

\[
E\le(1-r)^2+\sum_{j\ne k}R_j^2
=\sum_jR_j^2-(r^2-(1-r)^2)
=\sum_jR_j^2-(2r-1),
\]

which is (16).

### Sharpness

If `r<=1/2`, choose three planar traceless vectors `v_j` of lengths `R_j` forming a closed triangle. Then `M_j=R_jI+V_j` are rank-one positive semidefinite matrices summing to `I_2`, and equality holds in (16).

Factor each `M_j=B_jB_j^T`. Then `[B_1\ B_2\ B_3]` is a real orthonormal two-frame and extends to an element of `SO(6)`. By the standard `Spin(6)=SU(4)` double cover, that `SO(6)` element is the exterior-square image of a genuine unitary after the same complementary-pair basis identification. Hence the block-row bound is not merely sharp for arbitrary bistochastic data; it is sharp on the true common-unitary exterior geometry. `QED`
