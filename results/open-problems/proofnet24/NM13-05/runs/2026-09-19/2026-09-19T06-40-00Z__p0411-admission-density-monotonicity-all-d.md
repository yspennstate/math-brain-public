## 7. Theorem: the boundary-distance density is strictly decreasing for every d >= 2

### 7.1 Source identity

Put `n=d-1` and `J_d=1/H_d`. The source identity, also used by the archived P0411 packet, is

\[
J_d(u)=\frac1{c_d}\int_{\mathbb R^n}
\left(\sqrt{\cosh^2u+|x|^2}-\sinh u\right)^{-2n}\,dx,
\]

where `c_d>0` is the source normalization.

We prove the density claim separately for `n>=2` and `n=1`.

### 7.2 Dimensions d >= 3: radial dualization

Assume `n>=2`, set

\[
a=e^{-u},\qquad q=e^{2u}=a^{-2},\qquad \alpha=\frac{n-2}{2}\ge0.
\]

In the radial integral use

\[
y=\sqrt{\cosh^2u+r^2}-\sinh u,
\]

for which

\[
r^2=(y-a)(y+a^{-1}),\qquad r\,dr=(y+\sinh u)\,dy.
\]

Then put `y=az`. Up to a positive constant `K_n` independent of `u`, direct substitution gives

\[
J_d(u)=K_n\int_1^\infty (z-1)^\alpha z^{-2n}
q^{n/2}(q+z)^\alpha(q+2z-1)\,dz. \tag{1}
\]

For fixed `u`, normalize the positive integrand in (1) to a probability measure `\mu_u` on `[1,\infty)`. Define its log-score

\[
A(q,z):=\partial_u\log\bigl(q^{n/2}(q+z)^\alpha(q+2z-1)\bigr).
\]

Since `dq/du=2q`, this is

\[
A(q,z)=n+\frac{2\alpha q}{q+z}+\frac{2q}{q+2z-1}. \tag{2}
\]

A second `u` derivative gives

\[
\partial_u A(q,z)
=\frac{4\alpha qz}{(q+z)^2}
 +\frac{4q(2z-1)}{(q+2z-1)^2}. \tag{3}
\]

Because `q>=1`, `z>=1` and `\alpha>=0`, equations (2)–(3) imply the pointwise bounds

\[
n\le A(q,z)\le 2n, \tag{4}
\]

and, using `4ab/(a+b)^2<=1`,

\[
0\le \partial_u A(q,z)\le \alpha+1=\frac n2. \tag{5}
\]

The base integrand in (1) behaves like `z^{-n-1}` as `z->infinity`; near `z=1` it is bounded by a constant times `(z-1)^\alpha`. Together with the uniform bounds (4)–(5), this gives an integrable dominator for the first two `u` derivatives on every compact `u` interval. Hence differentiation under the integral is justified.

Let

\[
L_d(u)=\log J_d(u).
\]

The usual log-normalizer identities, here derived directly by differentiating the normalized positive integral, are

\[
L_d'(u)=\mathbb E_{\mu_u}[A], \tag{6}
\]

and

\[
L_d''(u)=\mathbb E_{\mu_u}[\partial_u A]
            +\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_u}(A). \tag{7}
\]

From (4), `L_d' >= n`. Also, because `A` lies in an interval of length `n`, Popoviciu's variance inequality gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_u}(A)\le \frac{n^2}{4}.
\]

Combining with (5) and (7),

\[
L_d''(u)\le \frac n2+\frac{n^2}{4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
(L_d')^2-L_d''
\ge n^2-\left(\frac{n^2}{4}+\frac n2\right)
=\frac{n(3n-2)}4>0. \tag{8}
\]

Since `H_d=e^{-L_d}` and

\[
g_d=-H_d'=H_dL_d',
\]

we have

\[
g_d'=H_d\bigl(L_d''-(L_d')^2\bigr)<0
\]

by (8). Thus the density is strictly decreasing for every `d>=3`.

### 7.3 Dimension d = 2: exact closed-form endpoint

Now `n=1`, where the previous `\alpha>=0` score bound does not apply. Use the source's dimension-two formula

\[
H_2(u)=\frac{\pi}{D(u)},\qquad
D(u)=4A(u)\cosh^2u+2\sinh u,
\]

with

\[
A(u)=\arctan(e^u).
\]

Because `A'(u)=1/(2\cosh u)`, writing `s=\sinh u`, `c=\cosh u` gives the exact identities

\[
D'(u)=4c(2As+1),
\]

\[
D''(u)=8\bigl(A(2s^2+1)+s\bigr).
\]

The density is `g_2=\pi D'/D^2`. Hence `g_2'<0` is equivalent to

\[
2(D')^2-DD''>0.
\]

Exact simplification yields

\[
\frac{2(D')^2-DD''}{16}
=4A^2s^4+2A^2s^2-2A^2+4As^3+5As+s^2+2. \tag{9}
\]

Set `x=s>=0`. Since

\[
\frac{dA}{dx}=\frac1{2(1+x^2)},\qquad
A(x)=\frac\pi4+\frac12\arctan x,
\]

all terms in (9) except `-2A^2` are nonnegative, so the right side is bounded below by

\[
F(x)=2-2A(x)^2+5A(x)x.
\]

Differentiate:

\[
F'(x)=\frac{A(x)(6+10x^2)+5x}{2(1+x^2)}>0. \tag{10}
\]

At the endpoint,

\[
F(0)=2-\frac{\pi^2}{8}>0
\]

(for example `\pi<4` suffices). Therefore `F(x)>0` for every `x>=0`, so (9) is strictly positive and `g_2'(u)<0` for every `u>=0`.

Together with the `d>=3` argument, this proves:

**Theorem.** For every hyperbolic dimension `d>=2`, the source boundary-distance density `g_d` is strictly decreasing on `[0,infinity)`.

Equivalently, the frozen obstruction threshold is exactly

\[
\boxed{d_{\rm bad}=+\infty}.
\]

### 7.4 Median consequence

The source's suggested median conclusion now follows in every dimension, not only the odd-dimensional range covered by the archived packet. A strictly decreasing density makes the survival function `H_d` strictly convex. Let `M` be a median and put `v=g_d(M)>0`. Convexity gives the supporting-line lower bound

\[
H_d(x)\ge \max\{1/2-v(x-M),0\},\qquad x\ge0.
\]

Integrating and using the source exponential tail to ensure finiteness,

\[
\mathbb E R_d
>\frac{(1/2+vM)^2}{2v}
\ge M,
\]

where strictness comes from strict convexity on a positive-length interval and the second inequality is the square identity

\[
\frac{(1/2+vM)^2}{2v}-M=\frac{(vM-1/2)^2}{2v}\ge0.
\]

Thus **median(`R_d`) < mean(`R_d`) for every `d>=2`**.
