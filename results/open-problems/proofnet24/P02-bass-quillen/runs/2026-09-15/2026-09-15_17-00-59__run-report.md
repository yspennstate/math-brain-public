# 3. Required elementary audits

## 3.1 Rank-one stable-to-actual freeness

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a rank-one projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then top exterior powers give

\[
P\cong\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

This proves only the displayed rank-one cancellation implication. It does not prove higher-rank cancellation or Bass–Quillen.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and substituting `y=x` gives

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct because equality would force their difference `x` into the common ideal, impossible since `x` is a unit in `A`.

Hence projectives over `A` cannot be declared free by applying the local-ring theorem unless locality is separately proved.

## 3.3 Higher rank is genuinely unstable

The inherited identities `K_0(A)=Z` and stable `SK_1(A)=0` do not by themselves imply rank-two or rank-three cancellation. The rank-three problem is an actual `Um_4(A)` completion/orbit problem; rank two remains a distinct `Um_3`, Euler/Mennicke and Hermitian problem.

# 5. `P02-C88-TWO-TAIL-UNIT-CLOSURE` — PROVED

Let `A` be any commutative ring and

\[
v=(f,ub,uc,ud)\in Um_4(A).
\]

Assume that the image of `f` is a unit in

\[
A/(c,d)A.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\ker(v)\cong A^3.}
\tag{C88}
\]

No Noetherian, dimension, regularity, characteristic, locality or primality assumption is required.

### Proof

By the unit hypothesis there exist `h,p,q in A` such that

\[
1-hf=pc+qd.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Apply the elementary operation which adds `-hub` times the first coordinate to the second coordinate. The second coordinate becomes

\[
ub-hubf
=ub(1-hf)
=u(bp)c+u(bq)d.
\]

Since the third and fourth coordinates are `uc` and `ud`, two further elementary operations kill this new second coordinate. Thus `v` is elementary-equivalent to

\[
(f,0,uc,ud).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Put

\[
w=(f,uc,ud)\in Um_3(A),
\qquad
P=\ker(w:A^3\to A).
\]

Because `w` is unimodular, the exact sequence

\[
0\to P\to A^3\xrightarrow{w}A\to0
\]

splits. Hence

\[
P\oplus A\cong A^3.
\tag{5.3}
\]

On the other hand,

\[
\ker(f,0,uc,ud)
\cong A\oplus P.
\]

Combining with (5.3) gives

\[
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
\]

QED.

### Consequences

1. `P02-C84-TWO-TAIL-PRIME-SLICE` remains valid but is now a special case of C88 whenever its source-unit condition holds.
2. The earlier proof's principalization of the two-tail ideal was unnecessary for **rank-three freeness**. Principalization remains useful only if one wants to reduce all the way to an explicit unimodular pair.
3. This does **not** prove that the residual rank-two module `P` in (5.3) is free. It uses exactly one further free summand—the zero coordinate—to obtain rank-three freeness. This distinction is essential.

# 6. `P02-C89-POWER-OF-r-SLICE-CERTIFICATE` — PROVED

Now assume

\[
A=R[1/r],
\qquad
f=F/r^N,
\qquad
c,d,F\in R.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\bar f\in\bigl(A/(c,d)A\bigr)^\times
\iff
\exists M\ge0:\ r^M\in(F,c,d)R.
}
\tag{C89.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\exists M:\ r^M\in(F,c,d)
\Longrightarrow
\ker(f,ub,uc,ud)\cong A^3.
}
\tag{C89.2}
\]

### Proof

Set

\[
C=R/(c,d).
\]

Then

\[
A/(c,d)A\cong C[1/r].
\]

Since `r^N` is a unit after localization, `f=F/r^N` is a unit iff the image of `F` is a unit in `C[1/r]`.

If `r^M=FG` in `C`, then `G/r^M` is an inverse for `F` in `C[1/r]`.

Conversely, suppose `F` is a unit in `C[1/r]`. Write an inverse as `G/r^n`. Equality

\[
F(G/r^n)=1
\]

in the localization means that for some `m>=0`,

\[
r^m(FG-r^n)=0
\]

in `C`. Hence

\[
F(r^mG)=r^{m+n},
\]

so a power of `r` lies in `(F)C`, equivalently

\[
r^{m+n}\in(F,c,d)R.
\]

This proof allows zero divisors in `C`; no domain assumption is hidden.

### Geometric form

C89 says that the two-tail slice closes whenever

\[
V(F,c,d)\cap D(r)=\varnothing.
\]

The old C85 codimension-one divisor criterion was a useful domain test for the same phenomenon, but C89 is the exact ideal-theoretic criterion with no normality or reducedness hypothesis.

# 7. `P02-C90-BASS-REDUCIBILITY-CLOSURE` — PROVED

Return to the abstract relative row

\[
v=(f,ub,uc,ud),
\qquad
f=1+ua.
\]

Put

\[
B=A/fA.
\]

In `B`,

\[
ua=-1,
\]

so the image of `u` is a unit, with inverse `-a`. Reducing the unimodular row modulo `f` therefore gives

\[
(\bar b,\bar c,\bar d)\in Um_3(B).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Assume this normalized tail row is **Bass-reducible**: there exist `x,y in B` such that

\[
(\bar c+x\bar b,\ \bar d+y\bar b)\in Um_2(B).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\ker(v)\cong A^3.}
\tag{C90}
\]

### Proof

Lift `x,y` to elements of `A` and perform the corresponding tail elementary operations:

\[
c'=c+xb,
\qquad
d'=d+yb.
\]

Equation (7.2) says that there exist `p,q in A` whose images satisfy

\[
\bar p\,\bar c'+\bar q\,\bar d'=1
\]

in `B`. Therefore for some `h in A`,

\[
1-pc'-qd'=fh.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus `f` is a unit modulo `(c',d')A`, with inverse represented by `h`. Apply C88 to the tail-transformed row.

QED.

### Why this is sharper than the previous C55 interface

C55 closed the row when the residual `Um_3(B)` tail could be driven all the way to `(1,0,0)` by `E_3(B)`. C90 needs only one explicit stable-range reduction step: after modifying the last two coordinates by multiples of the first tail coordinate, those last two need merely generate the unit ideal. No claim is made that this is equivalent to `E_3`-triviality.

# 8. `P02-C91-DIM2-HYPERSURFACE-HARD-CORE` — PROVED

Assume the declared H4 laboratory:

\[
(R,m)\text{ regular local},\qquad \dim R=4,
\qquad r\in m\setminus m^2,
\qquad A=R[1/r],
\]

and a nontrivial C50 row with

\[
f=\frac{F}{r^N}=1+\frac{u\alpha}{r^N}.
\]

If `f` is a unit, the row is already elementary-trivial. Assume `f` is a nonunit.

Then

\[
B=A/fA\cong(R/(F))[1/r]
\tag{8.1}
\]

is Noetherian of dimension at most two.

Indeed `A` is a three-dimensional domain in H4, `f` is nonzero, and every minimal prime over the nonzero principal ideal `(f)` has height at least one. Hence

\[
\dim B\le2.
\]

Moreover, on `B`,

\[
ua=-1,
\]

so `u` is a unit, and reducing the original row shows

\[
\bar g=(\bar b,\bar c,\bar d)\in Um_3(B).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Combining with C90 gives the exact current closure theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\bar g\text{ Bass-reducible in }B
\Longrightarrow
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
}
\tag{C91.1}
\]

In the integral denominator presentation, a chosen reduced pair is equivalent by C89 to a certificate

\[
r^M\in(F,c',d')R
\]

for some `M` after tail elementary movement.

### Special geometry of `B`

Inside `S=R/(F)`, the relation

\[
r^N=-u\alpha
\]

shows that on `D(r)` both `u` and `alpha` are units. Thus

\[
\operatorname{Spec}B
=V(F)\cap D(r)
=V(F)\cap D(u)\cap D(\alpha).
\tag{8.3}
\]

So the remaining problem is not arbitrary dimension-two stable range. It is the reducibility of a special unimodular tail row on a principal open of a three-dimensional local hypersurface.

### Stable-range check

The ordinary Noetherian dimension bound reaches the next stable length but does **not** automatically reduce every `Um_3` row to `Um_2` in dimension two. D29 below shows that no such universal strengthening is possible even for smooth regular affine surfaces.

# 9. `P02-D29-DIM2-UM3-REDUCTION-FAILS` — rigorous method counterexample

A tempting strengthening of C91 would be:

> every unimodular row of length three over every regular ring of dimension two is Bass-reducible.

This is false.

Let

\[
S=\mathbb R[x,y,z]/(x^2+y^2+z^2-1).
\]

This is a smooth regular affine ring of dimension two. The row

\[
q=(x,y,z)\in Um_3(S)
\]

is unimodular because

\[
x^2+y^2+z^2=1.
\]

Richard Swan's classical Theorem 3 in *Vector Bundles and Projective Modules* identifies

\[
P=\ker(q:S^3\to S)
\]

with the algebraic tangent module of the real two-sphere and proves

\[
P\oplus S\cong S^3,
\qquad
P\not\cong S^2.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Equivalently, Swan states that `(x,y,z)` cannot be the first row of an invertible `3 x 3` matrix over `S`.

Primary source checked this run:

`https://wiki.math.ntnu.no/_media/ma8107/2022h/swan_-_vector_bundles_and_projective_modules.pdf`

(the scanned 1962 paper; Theorem 3 is on page 270 of the journal pagination).

Now suppose `q` were Bass-reducible. Then for some `a,b in S`,

\[
(y+ax,\ z+bx)\in Um_2(S).
\]

After the corresponding elementary operations, write the row as

\[
(x,y',z')
\]

with `(y',z')` unimodular. Choose `p,q in S` with

\[
py'+qz'=1.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
 y'&z'\\
 -q&p
\end{pmatrix}
\in SL_2(S).
\]

A change of the last two coordinates therefore sends `(x,y',z')` to `(x,1,0)`, which is immediately completable to a basis of `S^3`. This contradicts Swan's theorem.

Hence

\[
\boxed{(x,y,z)\text{ is not Bass-reducible in }Um_3(S).}
\tag{D29}
\]

### Correct interpretation

D29 is **not** a counterexample to Quillen's localization problem. The sphere ring is not asserted to arise from the H4 residual hypersurface construction. It proves only that regularity plus dimension two cannot by itself settle C91. A new ingredient must use the special principal-open local-hypersurface geometry, the special origin of the tail row, or a stronger Mennicke/Euler/Hermitian input.

# 10. Comparison of closure strategies after C88–C91

## 10.1 Elementary transitivity / stable range

C90 isolates the exact elementary target: reduce the special residual row

\[
\bar g\in Um_3(B)
\]

to a unimodular pair. Dimension `<=2` alone is insufficient by D29. Thus a generic statement “stable range applies” is invalid at the actual rank.

## 10.2 Vaserstein / Mennicke

The residual ring `B` is now the natural home for a Mennicke or Vaserstein obstruction. Full `E_3(B)` triviality remains a sufficient condition by C55, but C90 shows it is stronger than what this particular rank-three closure mechanism needs: a single Bass reduction suffices.

A Vaserstein-symbol vanishing theorem may therefore still close a branch when its exact hypotheses hold, but a nonzero `Um_3/E_3` class is not automatically a nonfreeness certificate for the original rank-three kernel. The extra stabilization in C88 is crucial.

## 10.3 Euler-class / rank-two core

Once a zero coordinate is created, the remaining three-entry kernel `P` is rank two and stably free, but the original rank-three kernel is

\[
A\oplus P\cong A^3.
\]

Thus C88 does **not** solve the independent rank-two Quillen problem. It only shows that the rank-three row closes after one normalized two-tail source-unit certificate.

## 10.4 Hermitian K-theory

The inherited classical elementary-Witt/Vaserstein comparison remains certified only under the exact checked `2 in A^x` hypotheses. The earlier all-characteristic `C35R` remains conditional/open. No characteristic-two conclusion is imported into C90.

## 10.5 Localization / patching / descent

C89 converts the source-unit problem into finite ideal membership

\[
r^M\in(F,c',d').
\]

This is substantially more concrete for approximation than a statement about an inverse in a localization. However, finite-type descent must still preserve the chosen elementary tail transformation, dimension, regularity, and the actual membership certificate. No such general ramified mixed-characteristic descent theorem is claimed here.

## 10.6 Low-rank reduction

C91 is now the sharp rank-three low-rank interface:

\[
\boxed{
\text{special }Um_3\big((R/(F))[1/r]\big)\text{ reducibility}
\Longrightarrow
\text{rank-three freeness over }A.
}
\]

D29 proves that the adjective **special** cannot be dropped.

**Verdict:** **PASS** for the exact sector-repulsion identities. This audit does not address inverse-Gram control or close P04 Target 4.

Let

\[
F_P(u)=\sum_{a=1}^s f_a(u)^2,
\qquad
K_P(u,v)=\sum_{a=1}^s f_a(u)f_a(v),
\qquad
q_P=F_P/s.
\]

For conditionally independent `U,U'` from `q_P`, the reproducing-kernel identity for the orthogonal projector `Pi_l` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
E[K_l(U,U')\mid P]
&=\frac1{s^2}\iint K_l(u,v)F_P(u)F_P(v)\,d\mu(u)d\mu(v)\\
&=\frac1{s^2}\langle F_P,\Pi_lF_P\rangle\\
&=\frac{\|\Pi_lF_P\|_2^2}{s^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is exactly (0.2).

For two distinct labeled DPP points, the pair density is

\[
\frac{F_P(u)F_P(v)-K_P(u,v)^2}{s(s-1)}.
\]

The first term contributes

\[
\frac{\|\Pi_lF_P\|_2^2}{s(s-1)}.
\]

For the second term,

\[
K_P(u,v)^2
=\sum_{a,b}f_a(u)f_b(u)f_a(v)f_b(v).
\]

Therefore

\[
\iint K_l(u,v)K_P(u,v)^2\,d\mu(u)d\mu(v)
=\sum_{a,b}\|\Pi_l(f_af_b)\|_2^2
=S_l(P).
\]

Hence

\[
D_l(P)
=\frac{\|\Pi_lF_P\|_2^2-S_l(P)}{s(s-1)},
\]

and subtracting gives
