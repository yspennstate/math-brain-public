# 3. Required elementary audits and stable/unstable distinction

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a rank-one finite projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

taking top exterior powers gives

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong P,
\qquad
\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

Thus `P congruent A`. This proves only the displayed rank-one stable-to-actual cancellation.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and similarly

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

So `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct because equality would put their difference `x` in the common ideal, while `x` is a unit of `A`.

Thus localization at a parameter need not preserve locality.

## 3.3 Stable H4 information is not cancellation

The inherited localization-sequence calculation gives

\[
K_0(A)\cong\mathbb Z
\]

and hence stable freeness of finite projectives. Likewise

\[
K_1(A)\cong A^\times,\qquad SK_1(A)=0.
\]

These are stable statements. They do not prove elementary transitivity on `Um_4(A)` or the rank-two `Um_3`/Pfaffian orbit problem. The current rank-three obligation is genuinely unstable.

### `P02-C60` — parabolic denominator index

For the localized tail ideal `I=KA` and contraction `J=I cap R=K:r^infty`,

\[
d_I(\alpha/r^N)\le M
\iff
\alpha\in J+r^{N-M}R.

# 5. New theorem `P02-C71-HIGHER-BOCKSTEIN-LADDER` — PROVED

Let `R` be the regular local domain in H4, let `r` be its regular parameter, and let

\[
K=(g_1,g_2,g_3)\subset R.
\]

For `q>=1`, set

\[
S_q=R/r^qR,
\qquad
Z_q=\operatorname{Syz}_{S_q}(\bar g_1,\bar g_2,\bar g_3),
\]

and put

\[
Q=R/(K+rR).
\]

Define

\[
\beta_{K,q}:Z_q\longrightarrow Q
\]

as follows. For a syzygy `bar c=(bar c_1,bar c_2,bar c_3)` modulo `r^q`, choose lifts `c_i in R`. Then

\[
\sum_i c_i g_i=r^q t
\]

for a unique `t in R` because `R` is a domain and `r` is a nonzerodivisor. Set

\[
\boxed{\beta_{K,q}(\bar c)=t\bmod(K+rR).}
\tag{C71.1}
\]

## 5.1 Well-definedness

If the lifts are changed to

\[
c_i'=c_i+r^q d_i,
\]

then

\[
\sum_i c_i'g_i
=r^q\left(t+\sum_i d_i g_i\right).
\]

The new quotient differs from `t` by an element of `K`, hence defines the same class in `Q`.

Thus `beta_{K,q}` is well defined and additive.

## 5.2 Exact image = the `q`th colon layer

Claim:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,q}
=
\frac{(K:r^q)+rR}{K+rR}
\subset Q.
}
\tag{C71.2}
\]

If a Bockstein value is represented by `t`, then

\[
r^q t=\sum_i c_i g_i\in K,
\]

so `t in K:r^q`.

Conversely, if `t in K:r^q`, write

\[
r^q t=\sum_i c_i g_i.
\]

Reducing the coefficient vector modulo `r^q` gives a syzygy in `Z_q`, and its Bockstein value is the class of `t`.

Therefore for every `alpha in R`,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha\in(K:r^q)+rR
\iff
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,q}
\iff
r^q\alpha\in K+r^{q+1}R.
}
\tag{C71.3}
\]

For `q=1`, this is exactly the C65 first-colon Bockstein.

## 5.3 Exact lifting obstruction between adjacent stages

Let

\[
\rho_q:Z_{q+1}\longrightarrow Z_q
\]

be reduction modulo `r^q`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{im}\rho_q=\ker\beta_{K,q}.
}
\tag{C71.4}
\]

### Proof: `im rho_q subset ker beta_q`

If a syzygy modulo `r^q` comes from a syzygy modulo `r^{q+1}`, choose a common lift `c`. Then

\[
\sum_i c_i g_i=r^{q+1}s.
\]

Viewed at level `q`, its quotient is `rs`, whose class vanishes in `Q=R/(K+rR)`.

### Proof: `ker beta_q subset im rho_q`

Let `bar c in Z_q` and write

\[
\sum_i c_i g_i=r^q t.
\]

If `beta_{K,q}(bar c)=0`, then

\[
t=\sum_i d_i g_i+rs
\]

for some `d_i,s in R`. Put

\[
c_i'=c_i-r^q d_i.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i c_i'g_i
=r^q(t-\sum_i d_i g_i)
=r^{q+1}s.
\]

Hence `c' mod r^{q+1}` is a syzygy at level `q+1` reducing to `bar c`.

This proves (C71.4).

## 5.4 Consequences

Define

\[
B_q=\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,q}
=\frac{(K:r^q)+rR}{K+rR}.
\]

Since

\[
K:r^q\subseteq K:r^{q+1},
\]

the `B_q` form an ascending filtration of the special-fiber quotient `Q`. The saturation criterion becomes

\[
\boxed{
\alpha\in K:r^\infty+rR
\iff
[\alpha]\in\bigcup_{q\ge1}B_q.
}
\tag{C71.5}
\]

Equation (C71.4) explains the higher layers: a level-`q` syzygy survives to the next level exactly when its `q`th Bockstein vanishes; a nonzero `(q+1)`st Bockstein is therefore a secondary defect attached to a lift through all previous stages.

This is the precise higher-colon version of the earlier informal “Bockstein staircase.”

No characteristic, completeness, finite-type, normality, or `2^{-1}` hypothesis is used.

# 6. New theorem `P02-C72-UNBOUNDED-COLON-DEPTH` — PROVED

The first-stage Hilbert–Burch problem is important, but it cannot be universal. For every prescribed integer

\[
m\ge1,
\]

there is a completely explicit H4 row for which a source-changing elementary move is captured **first at colon depth exactly `m`**, while the row is nevertheless elementary-trivial.

## 6.1 Construction

Let `k` be any field and

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],
\qquad
A=R[1/r].
\]

Take denominator exponent

\[
N=1,
\qquad
\alpha=x,
\qquad
F=r+ux,
\qquad
f=F/r=1+ux/r.
\]

Define

\[
k_1=r^m x-F=r^m x-r-ux,
\qquad
k_2=y,
\qquad
k_3=0,
\]

and the row

\[
v_m=(f,uk_1,uy,0).
\tag{C72.1}
\]

## 6.2 The initial tail is `r`-saturated

The linear term of `k_1` is `-r`. Hence

\[
k_1,u,x,y
\]

is a regular system of parameters of `R`. Therefore

\[
R/(k_1,y)
\]

is a two-dimensional regular local domain.

The image of `r` in this quotient is nonzero. Indeed, if it were zero, the defining equation

\[
r^m x-r-ux=0
\]

would give `ux=0`; but the images of `u,x` are nonzero parameters in the domain.

Thus `r` is a nonzerodivisor on `R/(k_1,y)`, and

\[
K_m:=(k_1,y)
\]

is `r`-saturated:

\[
K_m:r^\infty=K_m.
\tag{C72.2}
\]

## 6.3 The raw denominator obstruction is nonzero

Modulo `(r,y)`,

\[
k_1\equiv-ux.
\]

Hence

\[
R/(K_m+rR)\cong k[[u,x]]/(ux).
\]

The class of `x` is nonzero in this quotient, so

\[
x\notin K_m+rR.
\tag{C72.3}
\]

Therefore the tail-preserving C60 denominator index does not drop initially.

## 6.4 One allowed source-changing twist creates a depth-`m` torsion class

Apply the elementary first-to-second operation with coefficient `ur`:

\[
E_{1,2}(ur).
\]

Since the first row coordinate is `f=F/r`, the normalized integral second generator changes by

\[
k_1\longmapsto k_1+F=r^m x.
\]

Thus the new integral tail is

\[
K_m'=(r^m x,y).
\tag{C72.4}
\]

For every `0<=q<=m`,

\[
\boxed{
K_m':r^q=(r^{m-q}x,y).
}
\tag{C72.5}
\]

To verify this, reduce modulo `y`. The ring `R/(y)` is a regular local UFD, `r` is prime, and `r` is not associated to `x`. Therefore

\[
(r^m x):r^q=(r^{m-q}x)
\]

for `q<=m`, and pulling back gives (C72.5).

For `q<m`, adding `rR` kills the generator `r^{m-q}x`; modulo `(r,y)` the class of `x` survives. Therefore

\[
x\notin(K_m':r^q)+rR
\qquad(q<m).
\tag{C72.6}
\]

At the exact stage `q=m`,

\[
K_m':r^m=(x,y),
\]

so

\[
x\in(K_m':r^m)+rR.
\tag{C72.7}
\]

By C71, the class `[x]` lies in `im beta_{K_m',m}` and in no earlier `im beta_{K_m',q}`.

Thus the **minimal successful colon depth is exactly `m`**.

## 6.5 The row is nevertheless elementary-trivial

After the source twist the row is

\[
v_m'=(1+ux/r,\;ur^m x,\;uy,\;0).
\]

Now apply

\[
E_{2,1}(-r^{-(m+1)}).
\]

It changes the first coordinate by

\[
-r^{-(m+1)}(ur^m x)=-ux/r,
\]

so the first coordinate becomes exactly `1`. The remaining coordinates can then be killed by elementary operations.

Hence

\[
\boxed{v_m' E_4(A)=e_1,}
\]

and since `v_m` differs from `v_m'` by one elementary source twist,

\[
\boxed{v_m E_4(A)=e_1.}
\tag{C72.8}
\]

The associated rank-three projective kernel is free.

## 6.6 What this proves and what it does not

Because `m` is arbitrary while

- `dim R=4` is fixed;
- the denominator exponent is fixed at `N=1`;
- the row is actually elementary-trivial;

there is no universal fixed colon cutoff `q_0`, no dimension-only bound, and no bound `q<=N` that can make the saturation route complete.

This establishes the method falsification

\[
\boxed{\texttt{P02-D24-FIXED-COLON-CUTOFF}.}
\]

It does **not** disprove Quillen. It shows instead that arbitrarily deep vertical torsion can occur inside an elementary orbit.

It also changes the interpretation of C68: the stage-`q` problem is finite over `R/r^q`, but the required `q` is not uniformly bounded across rows.

## 7.1 Tail-preserving elementary transitivity

C60 completely describes denominator movement while the localized tail ideal `I` is fixed. The obstruction is membership of the numerator in the saturated contraction plus a power of `r`.

## 7.2 Source-changing movement

## 7.3 Normal/transverse quotient closure remains valuable

C67 remains a qualitatively different success criterion: if source movement reaches a tail contraction whose quotient is normal, the deleted parameter remains prime, and the transverse parameter remains regular modulo it, the denominator obstruction vanishes without walking the entire colon tower.

Thus the leading rank-three alternatives are now:

1. **geometric route:** move the tail to a normal/transverse or regular-curve quotient and close directly;
2. **obstruction route:** understand the entire higher-Bockstein filtration and prove the numerator eventually enters it;
3. **global orbit route:** prove `Um_4(A)/E_4(A)` transitivity by a Mennicke/Euler-class calculation that packages the tower at once;
4. **descent route:** descend the finite data while preserving dimension, the relative ideal and the row, then invoke a verified relative-completion theorem under its exact hypotheses.

A fixed low-order saturation calculation is no longer a credible universal closure strategy without an additional geometric ingredient bounding the torsion exponent for a specially chosen tail.

## 7.4 Rank two remains separate

Nothing in C71–C72 closes rank two. The retained interfaces are:

\sum_{y\in P}q_{ij}(y)

# 11. Adversarial checks and failed routes

## 11.1 Could C72 violate C68?

No. C68 says a **fixed stage `q`** depends only on the source twist modulo `r^q`. C72 changes the algebraic colon depth itself and exhibits rows whose first nonzero stage is arbitrarily large. The statements are complementary.

## 11.2 Could C72 be a Quillen counterexample?

No. The row is explicitly elementary-trivial by (C72.8). It is a counterexample only to a proposed proof strategy that truncates the colon tower.

## 11.3 Could the depth be bounded by the denominator exponent `N`?

No. C72 fixes `N=1` while the minimal colon depth equals arbitrary `m`.

## 11.4 Could the depth be bounded by dimension?

No. C72 keeps `dim R=4` fixed while `m` is arbitrary.

## 11.5 Could q=1 Hilbert–Burch analysis still be universal after a different source choice?

Possibly, but only with an additional theorem proving that one can first move every row to a tail whose successful capture occurs at `q=1`. C72 shows this is not automatic for a given successful elementary source move. Such a “torsion-exponent reduction” theorem is now an explicit missing ingredient, not a harmless normalization.

## 11.6 Stable K-theory shortcut

Still invalid. The explicit C72 rows are elementary, but the proof is a fixed-rank elementary calculation; nothing in `K_0` or `SK_1` alone supplies this calculation for arbitrary rows.
