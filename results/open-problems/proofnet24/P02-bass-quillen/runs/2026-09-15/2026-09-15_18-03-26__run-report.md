# 3. Required elementary audits

## 3.1 Rank-one stable-to-actual freeness

Let `A` be commutative and `P` rank-one projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then taking top exterior powers gives

\[
P\cong\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

This proves only the displayed rank-one implication. It proves neither higher-rank cancellation nor Bass–Quillen.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and substituting `y=x` gives

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are both maximal. They are distinct because equality would force their difference `x` into the common ideal, impossible since `x` is a unit in `A`.

Hence one cannot apply “projectives over a local ring are free” to `R[1/r]` without separately proving locality.

## 3.3 Higher rank remains unstable

For the rank-three row `v in Um_4(A)`, the actual obligation is an unstable completion/cancellation problem. Stable `K_0(A)=Z`, stable `SK_1(A)=0`, or a stable Hermitian invariant cannot replace a proof at the actual rank.

# 5. `P02-C92-UNIT-NUMERATOR-REGULAR-RESIDUAL` — PROVED

Assume explicitly:

- `(R,m)` is a regular local ring of dimension four;
- `r,u in m \ m^2` have linearly independent images in `m/m^2`;
- `alpha in R^x`;
- `N>=1`;
- \(F=r^N+u\alpha\).

Then:

\[
\boxed{F\in m\setminus m^2,}
\tag{C92.1}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{S:=R/(F)\text{ is a three-dimensional regular local ring}.}
\tag{C92.2}
\]

Moreover, the image of `r` in `S` lies outside the square of the maximal ideal of `S`:

\[
\boxed{\bar r\in m_S\setminus m_S^2.}
\tag{C92.3}
\]

Thus `r` remains a regular parameter on the residual threefold.

### Proof

Write `k=R/m`, and let `[r],[u]` denote the tangent classes in `m/m^2`.

If `N>=2`, then `r^N in m^2`, so

\[
[F]=\overline\alpha\,[u]\ne0
\]

because `alpha` is a unit and `[u]` is nonzero.

If `N=1`, then

\[
[F]=[r]+\overline\alpha\,[u],
\]

which is nonzero because `[r]` and `[u]` are linearly independent.

Hence `F in m\m^2`. In a regular local ring, any element of `m\m^2` extends to a regular system of parameters. Therefore `S=R/(F)` is regular local of dimension three.

Now

\[
m_S/m_S^2\cong m/(m^2+(F)).
\]

For `N>=2`, the one-dimensional tangent relation generated by `[F]` is the line `k[u]`, so `[r]` survives because `[r],[u]` are independent.

For `N=1`, suppose `[r]` vanished in the quotient. Then `[r]` would be a scalar multiple of

\[
[r]+\overline\alpha[u].
\]

By independence of `[r],[u]`, this would force simultaneously that scalar to be `1` and `\overline\alpha=0`, contradicting `alpha in R^x`.

Thus the image of `r` is nonzero in `m_S/m_S^2`, proving (C92.3).

QED.

### Consequence

The residual ring

\[
B=S[1/r]
\tag{5.1}
\]

is now **exactly** in the hypothesis of Gabber's dimension-three localization theorem. No coefficient field, characteristic-zero, or unramified hypothesis has been introduced.

# 6. `P02-C93-UNIT-NUMERATOR-RESIDUAL-K1` — PROVED

Continue under C92 and put

\[
B=S[1/r].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\text{every finitely generated projective }B\text{-module is free}.}
\tag{C93.1}
\]

This is Gabber's theorem in the form proved by Swan: `S` is regular local of dimension three and `r notin m_S^2`.

There is also a stable `K_1` collapse:

\[
\boxed{K_1(B)\cong B^\times,\qquad SK_1(B)=0.}
\tag{C93.2}
\]

### Proof of the `K_1` statement

Because `S` is local,

\[
K_1(S)=S^\times.
\]

Because `r` is a regular parameter, `S/(r)` is a two-dimensional regular local ring, so

\[
K_0(S/(r))\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_0(S)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Quillen localization and devissage give the exact segment

\[
K_1(S)\longrightarrow K_1(B)
\xrightarrow{\partial}
K_0(S/(r))
\longrightarrow K_0(S).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The final map sends the generator `[S/(r)]` to zero because

\[
0\to S\xrightarrow{r}S\to S/(r)\to0
\]

shows `[S/(r)]=[S]-[S]=0` in `K_0(S)`. Hence `partial` is surjective.

The localization boundary of the unit `r in B^x` is, up to the conventional sign, the generator of `K_0(S/(r))`. Therefore every class `xi in K_1(B)` can be multiplied by a suitable power of `[r]` so that it lies in the image of `K_1(S)=S^x`. Consequently every class of `K_1(B)` is represented by a unit of the form

\[
\varepsilon r^n,
\qquad \varepsilon\in S^\times,
\quad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Conversely, since `S` is regular local it is a UFD and the parameter `r` is prime, so

\[
B^\times=S^\times r^{\mathbb Z}.
\]

The determinant map

\[
\det:K_1(B)\to B^\times
\]

therefore identifies the two groups and has zero kernel.

QED.

### Residual consequence

Let

\[
Q=\ker(g:B^3\to B)
\]

for the residual row (4.3). By Gabber, `Q` is free of rank two. Therefore `g` is completable: there exists

\[
M\in GL_3(B),
\qquad gM=e_1.
\]

Since columns two and three map to zero under `g`, rescaling one of those columns by the inverse determinant changes neither `gM=e_1` nor the first column. Thus we may choose

\[
M\in SL_3(B).
\tag{6.2}
\]

By (C93.2), this matrix is stably elementary. What is **not** proved is

\[
M\in E_3(B).
\]

That is precisely the pre-stabilization gap at the actual rank.

# 7. `P02-C94-LIFTABLE-RESIDUAL-COMPLETION` — PROVED

The next lemma is purely algebraic and does not require H4.

Let `A` be a commutative ring, let

\[
v=(f,ub,uc,ud)\in Um_4(A),
\]

put

\[
B=A/fA,
\]

and assume the image of `u` is a unit in `B`. Let

\[
g=(\bar b,\bar c,\bar d)\in Um_3(B).
\]

Assume there is a residual completion

\[
M\in GL_3(B),\qquad gM=e_1,
\tag{7.1}
\]

which lifts to

\[
\widetilde M\in GL_3(A).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\ker(v)\cong A^3.}
\tag{C94}
\]

### Proof

Apply the block diagonal automorphism

\[
1\oplus\widetilde M
\]

to the last three coordinates of the row. The transformed tail is some

\[
(h_1,h_2,h_3),
\]

whose reduction modulo `f` is

\[
(\bar u,0,0).
\]

Hence

\[
h_2=fq_2,
\qquad
h_3=fq_3
\]

for some `q_2,q_3 in A`, and `h_1 mod f` is a unit.

Elementary operations using the first coordinate kill `h_2,h_3`, leaving

\[
(f,h_1,0,0).
\]

Since `h_1 mod f` is a unit, `(f,h_1)` is a unimodular pair. The kernel of a unimodular pair is free rank one: if

\[
pf+qh_1=1,
\]

then every solution of `fx+h_1y=0` is

\[
(x,y)=t(-h_1,f).
\]

The two zero coordinates contribute two further free summands. Hence the original rank-three kernel is free.

QED.

### Strategic meaning

C94 is genuinely different from C90:

- C90 asks for Bass reduction of the residual row itself;
- C94 asks for one residual **completion matrix to lift** through `A -> B`.

A residual row may be completable without being Bass-reducible; D30 below gives an explicit example. Thus the two routes must not be conflated.

# 8. `P02-C95-FINITE-LIFT-CERTIFICATE` — PROVED

Return to the H4 localization form and assume an **integral tail presentation**

\[
g=(b,c,d)\in R^3,
\qquad
A=R[1/r],
\qquad
B=(R/(F))[1/r].
\]

Because `R` is regular local, it is a UFD, and the regular parameter `r` is prime.

The following are equivalent.

### (A) Liftable residual completion

There exists

\[
\widetilde M\in GL_3(A)
\]

such that

\[
\bar g\,\overline{\widetilde M}=e_1
\quad\text{in }B^3.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### (B) Finite integral certificate

There exist

\[
M_0\in M_3(R),
\qquad t,L,s\ge0,
\qquad \varepsilon\in R^\times
\]

such that

\[
\det M_0=\varepsilon r^s
\tag{8.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
r^L\bigl(gM_0-r^t e_1\bigr)\in F R^3.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Hence either condition implies rank-three freeness by C94.

### Proof `(A) => (B)`

Choose `t` sufficiently large that

\[
M_0:=r^t\widetilde M
\]

has all entries in `R`.

Since `M_0` is invertible over `A`, its determinant is an element of `R` which becomes a unit after inverting the prime `r`. Unique factorization therefore gives

\[
\det M_0=\varepsilon r^s
\]

for some unit `epsilon` and `s>=0`.

Equation (8.1) says

\[
g\widetilde M-e_1\in F A^3.
\]

Multiplying by `r^t` gives

\[
gM_0-r^t e_1\in F A^3.
\]

Clear the finitely many `r`-denominators with one power `r^L` to obtain (8.3).

### Proof `(B) => (A)`

Equation (8.2) makes `M_0` invertible over `A`. In `B`, both `r` and `r^L` are units, so (8.3) gives

\[
\bar g\,\bar M_0=r^t e_1.
\]

Set

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(r^{-t},1,1)\in GL_3(A),
\qquad
\widetilde M=M_0D.
\]

Then

\[
\bar g\,\overline{\widetilde M}=e_1.
\]

QED.

### Why C95 matters for approximation

The lift problem is no longer an abstract assertion about an inverse matrix over a localization. A successful lift has a certificate involving only:

- one finite `3 x 3` integral matrix;
- one determinant identity;
- one finite power of `r`;
- one finite ideal-membership relation modulo `F`.

This is the kind of data that can plausibly survive Popescu-style finite-type approximation or explicit patching. What is **not** proved is that an arbitrary residual completion admits such a certificate.

# 9. `P02-C96-ORIENTED-TWO-OPEN-CLUTCHING` — PROVED

This is the strongest structural reduction of the run.

Let `A` be Noetherian and let `P` be the rank-three projective kernel of a unimodular row

\[
v=(f,ub,uc,ud).
\]

Assume:

1. `P_f` is free;
2. `P/fP` is free of rank three.

Then there exists

\[
s\in A,
\qquad s\equiv1\pmod f,
\tag{9.1}
\]

such that `P_s` is free. Thus

\[
D(f)\cup D(s)=\operatorname{Spec}A.
\]

Choose free bases on the two opens. Their transition matrix is

\[
\Gamma\in GL_3(A_{fs}).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
P\cong A^3
\iff
\Gamma=G_fG_s^{-1}
\text{ for some }
G_f\in GL_3(A_f),\ G_s\in GL_3(A_s).
}
\tag{C96.1}
\]

Moreover, because `P` is the kernel of a unimodular row of length four,

\[
\det P\cong A.
\]

Using this global orientation, the two local bases can be chosen oriented, so

\[
\Gamma\in SL_3(A_{fs}),
\tag{9.3}
\]

and freeness is equivalent to the analogous factorization through `SL_3(A_f)` and `SL_3(A_s)`.

Finally,

\[
P\oplus A\cong A^4
\]

because the defining row is split surjective. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma\oplus1
\text{ always factors at rank four through }
SL_4(A_f)\text{ and }SL_4(A_s).
}
\tag{C96.2}
\]

The remaining obstruction is exactly one-step unstable cancellation of a stably trivial clutching class.

### Proof of the second open

Choose a basis of `P/fP` and lift its three vectors to elements of `P`. They define a map

\[
\phi:A^3\to P
\]

which is an isomorphism modulo `f`.

The isomorphism locus of `phi` is open and contains `V(f)`. Since `A` is Noetherian, its complement is a closed set `Z=V(I)` disjoint from `V(f)`. Thus

\[
I+(f)=A.
\]

Choose `s in I` with

\[
s\equiv1\pmod f.
\]

Every prime in `Z` contains `s`, so `D(s)` is contained in the isomorphism locus. Hence `P_s` is free. Since `(f,s)=A`, the two principal opens cover `Spec A`.

### Proof of the factorization criterion

The transition matrix `Gamma` records the change from the chosen `D(s)` basis to the chosen `D(f)` basis on the overlap. If

\[
\Gamma=G_fG_s^{-1},
\]

changing the two local bases by `G_f^{-1}` and `G_s^{-1}` makes them agree on the overlap, so they glue to a global basis of `P`.

Conversely, any global basis restricts to bases on the two opens; comparing those restrictions with the chosen local bases gives such a factorization.

### Orientation

The split exact sequence

\[
0\to P\to A^4\to A\to0
\]

gives a canonical trivialization of `det P` once an orientation of `A^4` and `A` is fixed. Rescale one vector in each local basis so that its determinant agrees with this global orientation. The transition then has determinant one. The same argument applies to a global basis if `P` is free.

### One stabilization already vanishes

A global basis of `P\oplus A\cong A^4` restricts to the two opens. Comparing it with the stabilized local bases gives an explicit factorization of

\[

# 11. `P02-D30-COMPLETABLE-NOT-BASS-REDUCIBLE` — rigorous method counterexample

A natural but false strengthening of C90 would be:

> If the rank-two kernel of a residual `Um_3` row is free, then the row is Bass-reducible.

This is false even over a very classical polynomial ring.

Let

\[
T=\mathbb Z[x]
\]

and consider Guyot's explicit row

\[
q=(3,x+1,x^2+16)\in Um_3(T).
\tag{11.1}
\]

Its unimodularity can be checked directly:

\[
1
=6\cdot3+(x-1)(x+1)-(x^2+16).
\tag{11.2}
\]

Luc Guyot proves that this row is **not stable**; equivalently, it cannot be shortened by the Bass stable-row operation at length three.

Primary source:

L. Guyot, *The stable rank of Z[x] is 3*, arXiv:2111.02965; published in Expositiones Mathematicae 43 (2025), 125659.

On the other hand, Seshadri's theorem says that for a commutative principal ideal domain `Lambda`, every finitely generated projective `Lambda[t]`-module is free. Taking `Lambda=Z` shows every finitely generated projective `T`-module is free.

A source explicitly recording this exact PID formulation is Bass, *Algebraic K-Theory*, §6 (“Seshadri's theorem”); the original reference is C. S. Seshadri, *Triviality of vector bundles over the affine space K^2*, PNAS 44 (1958), 456–458.

The kernel

\[
Q=\ker(q:T^3\to T)
\]

is projective of rank two because the row is unimodular, hence it is free. Therefore `q` is completable to an invertible `3 x 3` matrix, yet it is not Bass-reducible.

After permuting coordinates to

\[
(x^2+16,3,x+1),
\]

this is exactly the orientation relevant to C90: the first tail coordinate cannot in general be used to make the last two a unimodular pair, despite the residual kernel being free.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{free residual kernel / completability}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{C90 Bass reducibility}.
}
\tag{D30}
\]

### Correct interpretation

D30 is a method counterexample, not a counterexample to Quillen. It shows why C92 + Gabber cannot be followed by the sentence “therefore C90 applies.” A new unstable ingredient is mandatory: lift the completion (C94/C95), factor the clutching matrix (C96/C97), or compute a Mennicke/Vaserstein obstruction.
