The strongest new results are:

1. **`P02-C84-TWO-TAIL-PRIME-SLICE` — PROVED.** If two normalized tail generators cut out a height-one prime slice and the source becomes a unit on that slice after inverting `r`, then the whole rank-three kernel is free. This is an actual unstable closure, not a stable `K_0` statement.

2. **`P02-C85-HORIZONTAL-DIVISOR-CRITERION` — PROVED.** On a domain slice, the source is a unit after inverting `r` exactly when every codimension-one divisor of the source is vertical over `r`.

3. **`P02-C86-SMOOTH-BRANCH-UNLOCK` — PROVED locally.** In the intermediate primary regime `F in P \ Q`, if the two-tail slice is smooth at the vertical height-two component, then the isolated local tail can always be dropped from height two to height one by a local coefficient. The obstruction is therefore not local DVR algebra but effectiveness/patching of that coefficient into an allowed global move.

4. **`P02-C87-HEIGHT1-NO-EMBEDDED-PRINCIPAL` — PROVED.** In a regular local ring, every nonzero height-one ideal with no embedded associated primes is principal. Thus, after a global height drop, embedded components are the only remaining algebraic obstruction to the principal-tail closure C83.

5. **`P02-D28-LOCAL-UNLOCK-NOT-GLOBAL` — DISPROVED as a method strengthening.** An explicit H4 row has a smooth `F in P \ Q` local branch whose required height-drop coefficient has an unavoidable horizontal pole, so the local coefficient does not patch through the chosen integral slice. Unit twists do not even strictly lower its `r`-thickness. Nevertheless the row is elementary-trivial by a different tail combination. Hence local unlockability is not a global completion theorem, and patching failure is not a nonfreeness invariant.

The conceptual sharpening is:

> On a height-one two-tail slice, there are now two exact exits. If the source has no horizontal codimension-one divisor, C84 closes the row. If a vertical height-two primary component lies in the intermediate regime `F in P \ Q`, C86 kills it locally on a smooth branch, but the local coefficient may have horizontal poles and fail to globalize. The remaining problem is an effective patching/Mennicke problem on a ring of dimension at most two, not a stable `K`-theory problem.

# 3. Required elementary audits

## 3.1 Rank one

Let `A` be commutative and `P` rank-one projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then taking top exterior powers gives

\[
P\cong\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

This proves only rank-one cancellation under the displayed stable-freeness premise.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and substituting `y=x` gives

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Hence `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct because equality would put their difference `x` in the common ideal, impossible since `x` is a unit of `A`.

Thus local-ring freeness cannot be applied to `A` merely because `R` was local.

## 3.3 Stable information is not unstable cancellation

The inherited stable identities `K_0(A)=Z` and `SK_1(A)=0` do not by themselves imply rank-two or rank-three cancellation. The rank-three problem is an actual `Um_4(A)/E_4(A)` or completion problem; rank two remains a distinct `Um_3` / Euler / Mennicke / Hermitian problem.

# 5. `P02-C84-TWO-TAIL-PRIME-SLICE` — PROVED

Let `R` be a regular local ring, `A=R[1/r]`, and let

\[
v=(f,ug_1,ug_2,ug_3)\in Um_4(A),
\qquad g_i\in R.
\]

Put

\[
J=(g_2,g_3)\subset R.
\]

Assume `J` is a height-one prime.

Then either `r in J`, in which case `ker(v)` is free, or `r notin J` and the following implication holds:

\[
\boxed{
\bar f\in (R/J)[1/r]^\times
\Longrightarrow
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
}
\tag{C84.1}
\]

Here `bar f` is the image of `f` on the two-tail slice.

### Proof: the case `r in J`

A regular local ring is a UFD. Since `J` is height one and contains the prime parameter `r`,

\[
J=(r).
\]

Because `g_2,g_3` generate `J`, after an invertible change among those two tail coordinates they become `(r,0)`. In `A`, `r` is a unit, so the row is equivalent to

\[
(f,u,0,0).
\]

The pair `(f,u)` is unimodular because the original four-entry row is. The kernel of a unimodular pair is free rank one, hence the full kernel is `A^3`.

### Proof: the case `r notin J`

Set

\[
B=A/JA\cong (R/J)[1/r].
\]

Assume the image `bar f` is a unit in `B`. Choose `c in A` lifting its inverse. Then

\[
1-cf\in JA.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Apply the elementary source-to-first-tail operation

\[
ug_1\longmapsto ug_1-ugc f
\]

with coefficient `-u g_1 c`. The new first tail coordinate is

\[
ug_1(1-cf)\in uJA.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The other two tail coordinates already lie in `uJ`.

Again, regular local `R` is a UFD, so the height-one prime is principal:

\[
J=\pi R.
\]

Because `g_2,g_3` generate `pi R`, their coefficient pair is unimodular after factoring `pi`; an invertible `2 x 2` tail change sends them to `(pi,0)`. Equation (5.2) is also a multiple of `u pi`, so another tail operation kills it. The row is therefore equivalent to

\[
(f,u\pi,0,0).
\]

Since the row is unimodular, `(f,u pi)` is a unimodular pair. If `pf+q(u pi)=1`, the matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
 f&u\pi\\
 -q&p
\end{pmatrix}
\]

has determinant one. Hence the kernel of `(f,u pi):A^2 to A` is free rank one, and the two zero coordinates give two additional free summands.

Thus

\[
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
\]

### Importance

C84 uses an allowed coefficient in the localized ring `A`, not merely an integral fixed-`F` twist. It is therefore a genuinely new ingredient beyond the C79–C82 primary-lock calculus.

# 6. `P02-C85-HORIZONTAL-DIVISOR-CRITERION` — PROVED

Let `C` be a Noetherian domain and let nonzero `r,F in C`. Then

\[
\boxed{
F\in C[1/r]^\times
\iff
\text{every height-one prime }\mathfrak q\supset(F)\text{ contains }r.
}
\tag{C85.1}
\]

### Proof

If `F` is a unit after inverting `r`, no proper prime of `C[1/r]` contains it. Therefore no prime of `C` disjoint from the powers of `r` can contain `F`; in particular every height-one prime containing `F` contains `r`.

Conversely, suppose `F` is not a unit in `C[1/r]`. Choose a prime minimal over the principal ideal `(F)` in `C[1/r]`. By the principal ideal theorem it has height at most one. Since `C[1/r]` is a domain and `F` is nonzero, the zero prime does not contain `F`; hence this minimal prime has height exactly one. Contracting it to `C` gives a height-one prime containing `F` but disjoint from `r`, contradicting the stated condition.

Thus C84 has the geometric form:

> **If a height-one prime two-tail slice has no horizontal codimension-one component of `F=0` after quotienting by the slice, the rank-three row closes.**

No normality hypothesis is needed for C85.

# 7. `P02-C86-SMOOTH-BRANCH-UNLOCK` — PROVED locally

This resolves the local algebra of the intermediate C82 case `F in P \ Q` under a precise smoothness hypothesis.

Let

\[
K=(g_1,g_2,g_3)\subset R
\]

and let `P` be a height-two minimal prime of `K`. Put

\[
B=R_P,
\qquad
J=(g_2,g_3)B.
\]

Assume:

1. `J` is a height-one prime of the two-dimensional regular local ring `B`;
2. `D=B/J` is a DVR (equivalently, this height-one branch is smooth at `P`);
3. `I=KB=J+(g_1)` is `PB`-primary;
4. `r in PB` but `r notin J`;
5. `F in PB \ I`.

Let `nu` be the normalized valuation of the DVR `D`. Write bars for images in `D`.

Since

\[
I/J=(\bar g_1),
\]

and `F notin I`, one has

\[
\nu(\bar F)<\nu(\bar g_1).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The original local vertical thickness is exactly

\[
e(I)=\min\{e:r^e\in I\}
=\left\lceil\frac{\nu(\bar g_1)}{\nu(\bar r)}\right\rceil.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For every unit `h in B^times`, unequal valuation in (7.1) prevents cancellation, so

\[
\nu(\bar g_1+\bar h\bar F)=\nu(\bar F).
\]

Hence the new local thickness is

\[
\boxed{
 e_h=
 \left\lceil\frac{\nu(\bar F)}{\nu(\bar r)}\right\rceil
 \le e(I).
}
\tag{C86.1}
\]

It need not be strictly smaller because of the ceiling.

More strongly, since `nu(bar g_1)>nu(bar F)`, the quotient

\[
-\bar g_1/\bar F
\]

belongs to the DVR `D`. Choose a lift `h_0 in B`. Then

\[
g_1+h_0F\in J,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
(g_1+h_0F,g_2,g_3)B=J.
}
\tag{C86.2}
\]

The local tail drops from height two to height one.

### Exact limitation

The coefficient `h_0` lies in the local ring `R_P`. It need not lie in `R`, and even an equality in the fraction field need not be representable by a coefficient whose only allowed denominators are powers of `r`. Thus C86 is a **local unlock theorem**, not a global elementary-completion theorem.

This identifies a precise patching problem rather than a vague obstruction.

# 8. `P02-C87-HEIGHT1-NO-EMBEDDED-PRINCIPAL` — PROVED

Let `R` be a regular local ring and let `0 != K subset R` be an ideal of height one. Assume `R/K` has no embedded associated primes; equivalently, an irredundant primary decomposition of `K` has only height-one minimal radicals.

Then

\[
\boxed{K\text{ is principal}.}
\tag{C87.1}
\]

### Proof

A regular local ring is a UFD. Write an irredundant primary decomposition

\[
K=\bigcap_{i=1}^t Q_i,
\qquad
\sqrt{Q_i}=P_i,
\]

where every `P_i` is a height-one minimal prime. Thus

\[
P_i=(\pi_i)
\]

for pairwise nonassociate prime elements `pi_i`.

Localizing at `P_i`, the DVR `R_{P_i}` has

\[
Q_iR_{P_i}=(\pi_i^{e_i})
\]

for some `e_i>=1`. Because `Q_i` is `P_i`-primary,

\[
Q_i=Q_iR_{P_i}\cap R.
\]

The contraction is exactly `(pi_i^{e_i})`: if `s x` is divisible by `pi_i^{e_i}` with `s notin P_i`, then the `pi_i`-valuation of `s` is zero, so the `pi_i`-valuation of `x` is at least `e_i`.

Therefore

\[
K=\bigcap_i(\pi_i^{e_i}).
\]

In a UFD, an element lies in every `(pi_i^{e_i})` exactly when it is divisible by their product. Hence

\[
\boxed{
K=\left(\prod_i\pi_i^{e_i}\right).
}
\]

### Rank-three corollary

If a global source move drops the integral tail to height one and the new height-one quotient has no embedded associated primes, C87 makes the tail principal, and C83 gives

\[
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
\]

Thus the non-Cohen–Macaulay height-one case is now sharply reduced to **embedded components**; Cohen–Macaulayness was a sufficient but not necessary hypothesis in C83.

# 9. `P02-D28-LOCAL-UNLOCK-NOT-GLOBAL` — rigorous method counterexample

The local C86 coefficient need not patch globally, and unit source twists need not strictly reduce thickness.

Let

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],\qquad A=R[1/r],
\]

and set

\[
J=(r-x^2),
\qquad
g_1=x^4,
\qquad
g_2=r-x^2,
\qquad g_3=0,
\]

\[
N=2,
\qquad
\alpha=x^3,
\qquad
F=r^2+ux^3,
\qquad
f=F/r^2.
\]

Let

\[
P=(r,x).
\]

Then

\[
K=(x^4,r-x^2)
\]

is `P`-primary: modulo `J`, the ring is `k[[u,x,y]]` and the tail becomes `(x^4)`.

Localize at `P`. Since `u` and `y` become units and `r=x^2` modulo `J`,

\[
D=R_P/JR_P
\]

is a DVR with uniformizer `x`. Its valuation satisfies

\[
\nu(r)=2,
\qquad
\nu(g_1)=4,
\]

while

\[
\bar F=x^4+ux^3=x^3(u+x)
\]

has

\[
\nu(F)=3
\]

because `u+x` is a unit in `D`. Therefore

\[
F\in P\setminus KR_P.
\]

The original local `r`-thickness is

\[
\left\lceil 4/2\right\rceil=2.
\]

For every unit local twist `h`, C86 gives the new thickness

\[
\left\lceil 3/2\right\rceil=2.
\]

So support-unlocking does **not** imply strict thickness drop.

The exact local height-drop coefficient is

\[
h_0=-\frac{x}{u+x},
\]

since

\[
-x^4/F=-x/(u+x)
\]

in the DVR quotient. But this coefficient has a horizontal pole along `(u+x)` in

\[
C=R/J\cong k[[u,x,y]].
\]

It is not in `C[1/x]` (equivalently `C[1/r]`), because the height-one prime `(u+x)` does not contain `x` and the numerator `x` is not divisible by `u+x`. Multiplying by any power of `r=x^2` does not remove that pole.

Thus the exact local ratio is not the image of a global coefficient with only `r`-power denominators. C86 does not globalize on this slice.

Nevertheless the original row is completely elementary:

\[
v=\left(\frac{F}{r^2},ux^4,u(r-x^2),0\right).
\]

Use

\[
x^4+(r+x^2)(r-x^2)=r^2.
\]

A tail elementary operation turns the second tail coordinate into `u r^2`. Then the source operation with coefficient

\[
-\frac{x^3}{r^4}
\]

changes

\[
1+\frac{ux^3}{r^2}
\quad\text{to}\quad
1+\frac{ux^3}{r^2}
-
\frac{x^3}{r^4}(ur^2)
=1.
\]

Hence the row is elementary-trivial over `A`.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local smooth-branch unlockability need not patch on the chosen slice,}
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\text{failure of that patching is not a nonfreeness invariant.}
}
\tag{D28}
\]

This is a falsification of an overstrong method, not of Quillen localization.

# 10. Exact low-rank interface after C84–C87

Suppose the two-tail ideal

\[
J=(g_2,g_3)
\]

is a height-one prime not containing `r`. Put

\[
C=R/J.
\]

Since `R` is four-dimensional local, `C` is a three-dimensional local domain. Every prime of `C` surviving in `C[1/r]` avoids the maximal ideal of `C`, because the maximal ideal contains the nonunit `r`; therefore

\[
\dim C[1/r]\le2.
\]

Modulo `JA`, the original row becomes the genuine unimodular pair

\[
(\bar f,u\bar g_1)\in Um_2(C[1/r]).
\tag{10.1}
\]

C84 closes immediately if `bar f` is a unit. C85 says failure means that `bar F` has a horizontal height-one divisor in `C`. On every such horizontal divisor, unimodularity of (10.1) forces `u bar g_1` to be a unit locally.

Thus the remaining slice problem is an honest **length-two unimodular-pair / Mennicke patching problem on a ring of dimension at most two**. Stable `K_1` or `K_0` cannot substitute for this unstable pair problem.

This is the cleanest current bridge from the primary/saturation calculations back to Mennicke-symbol and low-rank methods.

# 11. Comparison of genuinely distinct closure strategies

## 11.1 Elementary transitivity / unimodular rows

The rank-three target remains completion of a length-four row. C84 gives a new explicit closure when a two-tail prime slice sees a source unit. C86 identifies a local height-drop move in the intermediate primary case, but D28 shows that its coefficient can fail to globalize.

## 11.2 Mennicke / Vaserstein / Euler-class route

After quotienting by a height-one two-tail prime, the residual object is the pair (10.1) over a ring of dimension at most two. When the source is not a unit, horizontal codimension-one divisors remain and are exactly where the second coordinate is locally invertible. The natural next invariant is therefore a patching/Mennicke class for this pair, not a stable determinant.

The older Vaserstein package in rank two remains separately constrained by its exact `2 in A^times` hypotheses where used; the all-characteristic bridge remains conditional.

## 11.3 Hermitian K-theory

Nothing in C84–C87 promotes stable symplectic or Grothendieck–Witt information to fixed-rank cancellation. Rank two still requires the explicit C32–C33 Pfaffian class / C23 special-fiber comparison, with characteristic two treated separately until an exact bridge is proved.

## 11.4 Localization and patching

C85 is now an exact codimension-one localization criterion. C86 shows that the intermediate primary obstruction vanishes in the local DVR, and D28 shows precisely why patching may fail: the required local quotient can have a horizontal pole invisible to the vertical local branch.

This gives a concrete effectivity question for approximation/descent rather than a generic appeal to patching.

## 11.5 Descent / approximation

Popescu-style approximation and finite-type relative completion remain conditional routes. They must preserve the actual row, its dimension, the principal relative ideal, the horizontal divisor data in C85, and the unstable pair in (10.1). Merely approximating the regular local ring or proving stable homotopy invariance is not enough.
