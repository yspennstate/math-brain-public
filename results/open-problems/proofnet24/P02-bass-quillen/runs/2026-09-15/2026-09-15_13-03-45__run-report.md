# 3. Required elementary audits and stable/unstable distinction

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a finite projective of constant rank one. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

top exterior powers give

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong P,
\qquad
\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

Therefore `P congruent A`. This proves only the displayed rank-one stable-to-actual cancellation.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct, since equality would put their difference `x` in the common ideal even though `x` is a unit of `A`.

Hence localization at a parameter need not preserve locality.

## 3.3 Stable H4 information is not cancellation

The inherited localization calculations give stable facts

\[
K_0(A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_1(A)\cong A^\times,
\qquad
SK_1(A)=0.
\]

These do not prove transitivity on `Um_4(A)` or the rank-two `Um_3` / Pfaffian orbit problem. The rank-three obligation remains genuinely unstable.

# 5. `P02-C73-HIGHER-BOCKSTEIN-JET-LAW` — PROVED

Fix `q>=1`. Let

\[
h'=h+r^qz,
\qquad z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in R^3.
\]

Then

\[
g_i'=g_i+r^qz_iF.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because `q>=1`,

\[
K_{h'}+rR=K_h+rR,
\]

so the target quotient is the same ring

\[
Q=R/(K_h+rR).
\]

Also `g_i'=g_i mod r^q`, hence

\[
Z_q(h')=Z_q(h)
\]

canonically.

Take `bar c in Z_q(h)=Z_q(h')` and choose lifts `c_i in R` with

\[
\sum_i c_i g_i=r^qt.
\]

Then by (5.1),

\[
\sum_i c_i g_i'
=
 r^q\left(t+F\sum_i c_i z_i\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{h',q}(\bar c)
=
\beta_{h,q}(\bar c)
+[F]\sum_i[c_i][z_i]
\quad\text{in }Q.
}
\tag{C73.1}
\]

Since `r=0` in `Q` and

\[
F=r^N+u\alpha,
\]

we have

\[
[F]=u[\alpha].
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{h+r^qz,q}(\bar c)
=
\beta_{h,q}(\bar c)
+u[\alpha]\sum_i[c_i][z_i].
}
\tag{C73.2}
\]

The expression only uses the classes of `c_i,z_i` modulo `r` after passage to `Q`; changing chosen lifts does not affect the formula.

## 5.1 Capture invariance recovered at map level

Suppose

\[
[\alpha]=\beta_{h+r^qz,q}(\bar c).
\]

Then

\[
\left(1-u\sum_i[c_i][z_i]\right)[\alpha]
=
\beta_{h,q}(\bar c).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The parenthesized element is a unit because it is `1` modulo the maximal-ideal element `u`. The image of `beta_{h,q}` is an `R`-submodule of `Q`, so multiplication by the inverse unit preserves it. Therefore

\[
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{h,q}.
\]

Interchanging `h` and `h+r^qz` proves the converse. Hence

\[
\boxed{
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{h+r^qz,q}
\iff
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{im}\beta_{h,q}.
}
\tag{C73.3}
\]

This is the exact map-level refinement of C68. It shows what the deeper `q`-jet change actually does: it shears the Bockstein by a rank-one term proportional to the very class `[alpha]` one is trying to capture. Such a shear cannot change whether `[alpha]` belongs to the image.

## 5.2 Limitation

C73 does **not** compare different `q` or source moves that alter lower jets. C72 proves that changing the colon stage itself can be essential. The theorem eliminates only same-stage deeper tuning.

# 6. `P02-C74-NILRADICAL-LOCALIZATION` — PROVED

Let

\[
M=R/K,
\qquad
\mathcal N=\sqrt K/K\subset M
\]

be the nilradical of the tail quotient, and put

\[
T=H^0_{(r)}(M)
=
\{t\in M:\exists q,\ r^qt=0\}.
\]

Then multiplication by `r` gives a canonical map

\[
r:T\longrightarrow H^0_{(r)}(\mathcal N).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof that the image lies in the nilradical

Take `t in T`, so `r^q t=0` for some `q>=1`. Let `bar t` denote its image in the reduced quotient

\[
M_{\rm red}=M/\mathcal N=R/\sqrt K.
\]

Then

\[
r^q\bar t=0.
\]

Since `M_red` is a reduced ring,

\[
(r\bar t)^q
=
 r^q\bar t^q
=
\bar t^{q-1}(r^q\bar t)
=0
\]

implies

\[
r\bar t=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
rt\in\mathcal N.
\]

Also `r^{q-1}(rt)=0`, so in fact

\[
rt\in H^0_{(r)}(\mathcal N).
\]

The kernel of (6.1) is exactly

\[
0:_M r.
\]

Hence there is an injection

\[
\boxed{
T/(0:_M r)
\hookrightarrow
H^0_{(r)}(\mathcal N).
}
\tag{C74.1}
\]

## 6.1 First-colon consequence

If

\[
H^0_{(r)}(\mathcal N)=0,
\tag{6.2}
\]

then (C74.1) gives

\[
T=0:_M r.
\]

Translating back to ideals,

\[
T=(K:r^\infty)/K,
\qquad
0:_M r=(K:r)/K.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
H^0_{(r)}(\sqrt K/K)=0
\Longrightarrow
K:r^\infty=K:r.
}
\tag{C74.2}
\]

Consequently every higher Bockstein image equals the first-colon image:

\[
\boxed{
\bigcup_{q\ge1}\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,q}
=
\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,1}.
}
\tag{C74.3}
\]

Thus any colon depth beyond one is necessarily supported inside the **nilpotent vertical structure** of `R/K`. Higher Bocksteins are not needed on the locus where the nilradical has no `r`-primary torsion.

# 7. `P02-C75-RADICAL-TAIL-COLLAPSE` — PROVED

A particularly important special case of C74 is when `K` itself is radical.

Then

\[
\mathcal N=0,
\]

so C74 gives

\[
\boxed{
K:r^\infty=K:r.
}
\tag{C75.1}
\]

There is also a direct one-line proof. If

\[
x\in K:r^q,
\]

then

\[
r^qx\in K.
\]

Hence

\[
(rx)^q=r^qx^q=x^{q-1}(r^qx)\in K.
\]

Since `K` is radical,

\[
rx\in K,
\]

so

\[
x\in K:r.
\]

This proves `(K:r^q) subset (K:r)` for every `q`; the reverse inclusion is automatic.

## 7.1 Geometric primary-decomposition form

Because `R` is Noetherian, write the radical ideal as its irredundant minimal-prime decomposition

\[
K=\bigcap_{i=1}^sP_i.
\]

Colon distributes over finite intersections, and for a prime `P_i`,

\[
P_i:r=
\begin{cases}
P_i,&r\notin P_i,\\
R,&r\in P_i.
\end{cases}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K:r^\infty
=K:r
=\bigcap_{r\notin P_i}P_i.
}
\tag{C75.2}
\]

So on a reduced integral tail scheme, saturation simply deletes the irreducible components contained in the deleted divisor `V(r)`; it introduces no higher nilpotent thickness.

## 7.2 Consequence for the P02 search

If a source-changing move produces a radical tail ideal, then the full C71 ladder collapses to the first Bockstein C65. All higher `q` are redundant for that move.

Accordingly the genuinely higher-stage route is now confined to **nonreduced integral tails with `r`-primary nilpotent structure**.

This is a strong reduction because it gives a concrete new movement target:

> seek source changes that make the integral tail quotient reduced, or more generally make `H^0_(r)(sqrt(K)/K)=0`; on that locus only the already-explicit first Hilbert–Burch/Bockstein defect remains.

No Bertini/descent theorem establishing such a move in arbitrary ramified mixed characteristic is currently imported.

# 8. `P02-D25-REDUCED-SPECIAL-FIBER-NOT-ENOUGH` — DISPROVED as a method claim

It is tempting to weaken C75 to “the special-fiber ideal `bar K` is radical.” C72 gives a direct counterexample.

For any prescribed `m>=2`, let

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],
\qquad
K_m'=(r^m x,y).
\]

Then in

\[
S=R/rR=k[[u,x,y]],
\]

the special-fiber ideal is

\[
\overline{K_m'}=(y),
\]

which is prime and therefore radical.

Nevertheless C72 proves

\[
K_m':r^q=(r^{m-q}x,y)
\qquad(0\le q\le m),
\]

so for `alpha=x`,

\[
x\notin(K_m':r^q)+rR
\quad(q<m),
\]

while

\[
x\in(K_m':r^m)+rR.
\]

Thus the first successful colon stage is exactly `m`, arbitrarily large.

The integral ideal `K_m'` is nonradical for `m>1`; the vertical component has nilpotent thickness `m`. This is precisely the phenomenon isolated by C74.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\overline K\text{ radical in }R/rR
\not\Rightarrow
K:r^\infty=K:r.
}
\tag{D25}
\]

Reduced special fiber does not control the higher Bockstein tower. One must control the integral tail scheme, or at least the `r`-primary torsion of its nilradical.

This is a failure of an overstrong method claim, not a counterexample to Quillen localization.

**Verdict:** **PASS / independently rederived.**

Take the row law on `R^m`

\[
x=\sqrt m\,\varepsilon e_J,
\]

where `J` is uniform on `{1,...,m}` and `epsilon` is an independent sign. Then

\[
E[xx^T]
=
 m\cdot\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m e_je_j^T
=I_m,
\]

so the law is isotropic.

Under row-volume sampling with `r=m-s` rows, the Gram determinant is zero exactly when a coordinate index repeats. When all `r` indices are distinct, the rows are orthogonal with squared norm `m`, so

\[
\det(XX^T)=m^r,
\]

a constant. Therefore volume sampling is uniform over ordered `r`-tuples of distinct coordinate axes. The residual rank-`s` projection `P_s` is exactly coordinate projection onto a uniformly random `s`-subset of omitted axes.

On the standard basis vector

\[
e_I=e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_j}
\]

of `wedge^j R^m`, the operator `wedge^j P_s` has eigenvalue one iff all `j` coordinates of `I` are in the omitted subset. The probability is

\[
\frac{\binom{m-j}{s-j}}{\binom ms}
=
\frac{(s)_j}{(m)_j}.
\]

Off-diagonal matrix elements vanish by coordinate diagonality. Hence

\[
E[\wedge^jP_s]
=
\frac{(s)_j}{(m)_j}I
\]

for every `j<=s`: the complete exterior-design hierarchy is exact.

At `s=1`, fix `a=e_1`. Then the residual line is a uniformly random coordinate axis and

\[
p_1=a^TP_1a
=1_{\{\text{missing axis}=1\}}.
\]

The normalized likelihood is

\[
L_1=mp_1.
\]

Therefore

\[
E L_1=1,
\qquad
E L_1^2=m,
\]

and

\[
\chi^2=E(L_1-1)^2=m-1.
\]

So exact isotropy plus exact exterior moments at every order does not imply an `O(1)` endpoint second likelihood moment. The P04 route falsification is correct.

This audit does **not** prove P04 Target 4. It also does not infer inverse-Gram control from isotropy; the entire point of the counterexample is that isotropy and exterior/Hodge information are insufficient.
