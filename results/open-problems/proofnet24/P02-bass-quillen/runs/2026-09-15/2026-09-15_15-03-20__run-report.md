   If an integral rank-three relative tail becomes principal, the projective kernel is free. In particular, if the tail has height one and its quotient is Cohen–Macaulay, regular-local Auslander–Buchsbaum makes the tail ideal principal and closes the rank-three kernel.

5. **`P02-D27-LOCKED-THICKNESS-NOT-NONFREE` — DISPROVED as a method claim.**

The central sharpening is therefore:

> **Fixed-`F` source movement has two qualitatively different escape mechanisms: remove an `F`-unlocked component, or leave the height-two primary regime. An `F`-primary-locked component cannot have its thickness reduced while the height is preserved.**

This is a method-level structural theorem, not a nonfreeness invariant and not a complete solution.

# 3. Required elementary audits and stable/unstable distinction

## 3.1 Rank one

Let `A` be commutative and `P` rank-one projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

taking top exterior powers gives

\[
P\cong\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

This proves only the displayed rank-one cancellation under the stable-freeness premise.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and the substitution `y=x` likewise gives

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

So `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct: equality would put their difference `x` in the common ideal, impossible because `x` is a unit of `A`.

Thus projective-over-local-ring freeness cannot be applied to `A` merely because `R` was local.

## 3.3 Higher rank is genuinely unstable

Inherited stable information such as

\[
K_0(A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad SK_1(A)=0
\]

is not fixed-rank cancellation. Rank three still requires the actual length-four unimodular-row/completion problem; rank two remains a separate `Um_3` / Pfaffian / Hermitian problem. The present run proves actual freeness only in explicit low-height cases and does not silently replace it by stable `K`-theory.

# 5. `P02-C79-F-SECTION-INVARIANCE` — PROVED

Let `R` be any commutative ring, let

\[
K=(g_1,g_2,g_3),
\qquad
K_h=(g_1+h_1F,g_2+h_2F,g_3+h_3F).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{K_h+(F)=K+(F).}
\tag{C79.1}
\]

### Proof

Modulo `(F)`, each new generator has the same class as the old generator:

\[
g_i+h_iF\equiv g_i\pmod F.
\]

Therefore both sums with `(F)` are generated by the same four elements `g_1,g_2,g_3,F`.

Equivalently, the closed subscheme cut out by the tail on the hypersurface `F=0` is invariant under every fixed-`F` source twist:

\[
\boxed{
V(K_h)\cap V(F)=V(K)\cap V(F)
}
\tag{C79.2}
\]

scheme-theoretically.

### Unit one-coordinate twist

Put

\[
K^+=(g_1+F,g_2,g_3).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{K+K^+=K+(F).}
\tag{C79.3}
\]

Indeed `F=(g_1+F)-g_1` lies in the left side, and the reverse inclusion is obvious.

Hence

\[
\boxed{V(K)\cap V(K^+)=V(K+(F)).}
\tag{C79.4}
\]

This is the first exact support-level source-movement law in the current P02 route.

# 6. `P02-C80-LOCKED-MINIMAL-SUPPORT` — PROVED

Let `R` be Noetherian. Let `P` be a minimal prime of `K` and set

\[
c=\operatorname{ht}P=\operatorname{ht}K.
\]

Assume a fixed-`F` twist `K_h` still has height `c`.

## 6.1 Locked support

If

\[
F\in P,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{P\text{ is a minimal prime of }K_h.}
\tag{C80.1}
\]

### Proof

Because `K subset P` and `F in P`, every generator

\[
g_i+h_iF
\]

lies in `P`; hence `K_h subset P`.

Choose a prime `P_h` minimal over `K_h` with `P_h subseteq P`. By the definition of height of an ideal,

\[
\operatorname{ht}P_h\ge\operatorname{ht}K_h=c,
\]

while `P_h subseteq P` gives

\[
\operatorname{ht}P_h\le\operatorname{ht}P=c.
\]

Thus the heights are equal. A strict inclusion `P_h subsetneq P` would extend a prime chain by at least one step and force strictly larger height for `P`; hence `P_h=P`.

So a minimal component lying on the invariant section `F=0` cannot be removed by a fixed-`F` source twist without leaving the height-`c` regime.

## 6.2 Unlocked old components are removed by one unit twist

Let

\[
K^+=(g_1+F,g_2,g_3).
\]

For every prime `P` containing `K`,

\[
\boxed{
K^+\subseteq P
\iff
F\in P.
}
\tag{C80.2}
\]

Indeed `g_2,g_3 in P`, and

\[
g_1+F\in P
\iff F\in P
\]

because `g_1 in P`.

Thus the same unit twist simultaneously removes **every old component with `F notin P`**. It may create new components; C80 makes no claim that the new minimal-prime set is a subset of the old one.

## 6.3 Exact vertical locking criterion in the C50 geometry

For a vertical prime `P`, meaning

\[
r\in P,
\]

we have

\[
F=r^N+u\alpha\in P
\iff
u\alpha\in P.
\]

Since `P` is prime,

\[
\boxed{
F\in P
\iff
u\in P\ \text{or}\ \alpha\in P.
}
\tag{C80.3}
\]

Therefore:

- every vertical component lying in `V(u)` is automatically support-locked;
- away from `V(u)`, a vertical component is support-locked exactly where the numerator `alpha` vanishes.

This is a geometric sharpening of C62–C78: the numerator determines which old vertical supports a fixed relative presentation can even hope to remove.

# 7. `P02-C81-PRIMARY-LOCK-THICKNESS` — PROVED

The support lock `F in P` does not by itself control primary thickness. There is, however, a stronger exact condition that does.

Let `P` be a minimal prime of `K`. Define its canonical isolated primary component

\[
Q:=KR_P\cap R.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Because `P` is minimal over `K`, `KR_P` has radical `PR_P`; in the local ring `R_P` it is `PR_P`-primary, and its contraction `Q` is `P`-primary.

Assume

\[
F\in Q.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let `K_h` be any fixed-`F` source twist with

\[
\operatorname{ht}K_h=\operatorname{ht}P.
\]

Then C80 makes `P` a minimal prime of `K_h`. Let

\[
Q_h:=K_hR_P\cap R
\]

be its isolated `P`-primary component.

Then

\[
\boxed{Q_h\subseteq Q.}
\tag{C81.1}
\]

### Proof

Every old generator lies in `K subset Q`, and every correction `h_iF` lies in `Q` by (7.2). Hence

\[
K_h\subseteq Q.
\]

Localizing at `P`,

\[
K_hR_P\subseteq QR_P=KR_P.
\]

Contracting back to `R` gives

\[
Q_h=K_hR_P\cap R
\subseteq
KR_P\cap R=Q.
\]

## 7.1 Vertical thickness cannot decrease

Suppose now `r in P`, and define

\[
e(Q)=\min\{e\ge1:r^e\in Q\},
\qquad
e(Q_h)=\min\{e\ge1:r^e\in Q_h\}.
\]

Since `Q_h subseteq Q`, membership in `Q_h` is harder. Therefore

\[
\boxed{e(Q_h)\ge e(Q).}
\tag{C81.2}
\]

Thus a vertical primary component containing the entire source hypersurface equation `F` is **primary-locked**: while the tail height is preserved, no fixed-`F` source twist can reduce its `r`-thickness.

This is stronger than C80. There are three possible positions for a vertical minimal component:

1. `F notin P`: unlocked support; the unit twist removes the old component;
2. `F in P\setminus Q`: support is locked but the primary thickness may still move;
3. `F in Q`: support is locked and the thickness cannot decrease while height is preserved.

New components can still appear, so this is a transformation law, not a complete primary-decomposition formula.

## 7.2 Numerator criterion for primary locking

Continue in the C50 geometry and assume

\[
r\in P,\qquad u\notin P,
\]

and let

\[
e=e(Q)\le N.
\]

Then `r^N in Q`. Hence

\[
F=r^N+u\alpha\in Q
\iff
u\alpha\in Q.
\]

Because `Q` is `P`-primary and `u notin P=sqrt Q`, multiplication by `u` is cancellable modulo `Q`:

\[
u\alpha\in Q\iff\alpha\in Q.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F\in Q\iff\alpha\in Q
\qquad(r\in P,\ u\notin P,\ e(Q)\le N).
}
\tag{C81.3}
\]

So, in this common transverse case, the numerator itself detects whether a vertical primary component is thickness-locked for the fixed relative presentation.

# 8. `P02-C83-PRINCIPAL-TAIL-CLOSURE` — PROVED

C81 exposes an important escape not visible in the old `vertical thickness` language: a source twist may **drop the tail height**. When that produces a principal tail, the rank-three kernel closes immediately.

Let

\[
v=(f,ug_1,ug_2,ug_3)\in Um_4(A)
\]

with all `g_i in R`. Suppose the integral tail ideal

\[
K=(g_1,g_2,g_3)
\]

is principal:

\[
K=dR.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\ker(v)\cong A^3.}
\tag{C83.1}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
g_i=da_i.
\]

Since the `g_i` generate `dR`, there exist `lambda_i in R` with

\[
d=\sum_i\lambda_i g_i=d\sum_i\lambda_i a_i.
\]

If `d=0`, all tail coordinates vanish and unimodularity forces `f` to be a unit, so the result is immediate. Assume `d nonzero`. The regular local ring `R` is a domain, so canceling `d` gives

\[
1=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i.
\]

Because `R` is local, at least one `a_i` is a unit. Relabel so `a_1 in R^times`. Then `g_1` itself generates `dR`, and for `j=2,3` there are `c_j in R` with

\[
g_j=c_jg_1.
\]

Elementary tail column operations kill the last two tail entries, reducing the row to

\[
(f,ug_1,0,0).
\]

Since the original row was unimodular, the pair `(f,ug_1)` is unimodular. Choose `p,q in A` with

\[
pf+q(ug_1)=1.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
f&ug_1\\
-q&p
\end{pmatrix}
\in SL_2(A),
\]

so the kernel of the two-coordinate map is free of rank one, generated for example by `(-ug_1,f)`. The two zero tail coordinates contribute two additional free summands. Hence the full kernel is `A^3`.

This proves **actual freeness**, not an `E_2`-transitivity statement.

## 8.1 Height-one Cohen–Macaulay corollary

Assume now

\[
\operatorname{ht}K=1,
\qquad
R/K\text{ is Cohen--Macaulay}.
\]

Since `R` is regular local of dimension four,

\[
\operatorname{depth}(R/K)=3.
\]

Auslander–Buchsbaum gives

\[
\operatorname{pd}_R(R/K)=4-3=1.
\]

From

\[
0\to K\to R\to R/K\to0,
\]

`K` is a projective `R`-module. It has rank one as a nonzero ideal in the domain `R`, and every finitely generated projective over local `R` is free. Thus `K` is principal, and C83.1 applies:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ht}K=1,\ R/K\text{ CM}
\Longrightarrow
\ker(v)\cong A^3.
}
\tag{C83.2}
\]

This is a new low-height reduction that is logically distinct from the vertical-primary saturation route.

# 9. `P02-D27-LOCKED-THICKNESS-NOT-NONFREE` — rigorous adversarial family

C81 could be overread as suggesting that a primary-locked thick component is a candidate nonfreeness invariant. It is not.

Fix any integer

\[
m\ge1
\]

and any field `k`. Let

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],
\qquad A=R[1/r],
\]

and put

\[
F=r+ux,
\qquad
f=F/r=1+\frac{ux}{r}.
\]

Define the integral tail ideal

\[
K_m=(F,x^m).
\tag{9.1}
\]

## 9.1 Exact primary geometry

Because `F` has coefficient one in `r`,

\[
R/(F)\cong k[[u,x,y]],
\qquad r\mapsto -ux.
\]

Under this isomorphism, `K_m/(F)` is `(x^m)`, which is `(x)`-primary. Hence

\[
\boxed{
K_m\text{ is }P\text{-primary for }P=(r,x).
}
\tag{D27.1}
\]

The vertical `r`-thickness is exactly `m`. In `R/K_m`,

\[
r=-ux,
\]

so

\[
r^m=(-u)^m x^m=0.
\]

For `q<m`, the monomial `u^q x^q` is nonzero in

\[
k[[u,x,y]]/(x^m),
\]

hence `r^q nonzero`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{e(K_m)=m.}
\tag{D27.2}
\]

And, crucially,

\[
F\in K_m.
\]

Thus C81 applies: **every fixed-`F` source twist that preserves height two has isolated `P`-primary thickness at least `m`.** The primary thickness can be made arbitrarily large and is locked inside that height-two regime.

## 9.2 Yet the rank-three kernel is free

Consider the relative row

\[
v_m=(f,uF,ux^m,0).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Set

\[
t=\frac{ux}{r}.
\]

Then `f=1+t`. Let

\[
S_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-t)^j.
\]

The finite geometric identity gives

\[
(1+t)S_m=1+(-1)^{m-1}t^m.
\]

Since

\[
t^m=\frac{u^mx^m}{r^m}
=\frac{u^{m-1}}{r^m}(ux^m),
\]

we obtain the explicit Bezout identity

\[
\boxed{
1
=fS_m
+(-1)^m\frac{u^{m-1}}{r^m}(ux^m).
}
\tag{D27.3}
\]

Therefore the pair `(f,ux^m)` is unimodular in `A`.

Also

\[
uF=ur f,
\]

so one elementary source-to-tail operation kills the second coordinate:

\[
(f,uF,ux^m,0)
\longmapsto
(f,0,ux^m,0).
\]

The remaining two-coordinate unimodular pair has a determinant-one completion, and the kernel is free of rank three.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\ker(v_m)\cong A^3
\quad\text{for every }m.
}
\tag{D27.4}
\]

Popescu approximation remains potentially useful, but C72 and C79–C81 identify data that any effective descent must preserve:

A filtered-colimit theorem without control of these data is not yet an unstable cancellation theorem.
