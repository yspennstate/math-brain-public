# 3. Required elementary audits and stable/unstable distinction

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a rank-one projective. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then taking top exterior powers gives

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)\cong P,
\qquad
\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

Hence `P congruent A`. This proves only the stated rank-one cancellation under the displayed stable premise.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\cong k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\cong k(x),
\]

and similarly

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct because equality would force their difference `x` into the common ideal, but `x` is a unit of `A`.

Therefore projective-over-local-ring freeness cannot be applied to `A` merely because `R` was local.

## 3.3 Higher rank remains unstable

The inherited stable calculations give

\[
K_0(A)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad
K_1(A)\cong A^\times,
\qquad
SK_1(A)=0.
\]

These are stable statements. Rank three still requires the actual `Um_4(A)/E_4(A)` orbit/cancellation problem, and rank two remains a separate `Um_3` / Pfaffian / Hermitian problem.

\operatorname{im}\beta_{K,q}

# 5. `P02-C76-PRIMARY-THICKNESS-LAW` — PROVED

Let `R` be Noetherian, `K subset R` an ideal, and `r in R`. Fix an irredundant primary decomposition

\[
K=\bigcap_{i=1}^s Q_i,
\qquad
P_i=\sqrt{Q_i}.
\]

Partition the indices as

\[
H=\{i:r\notin P_i\},
\qquad
V=\{i:r\in P_i\}.
\]

For every `i in V`, define the finite vertical thickness

\[
e_i=\min\{e\ge1:r^e\in Q_i\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then for every `q>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
K:r^q
=
\left(\bigcap_{i\in H}Q_i\right)
\cap
\left(\bigcap_{i\in V}(Q_i:r^q)\right).
}
\tag{C76.1}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
K:r^\infty
=
\bigcap_{i\in H}Q_i.
}
\tag{C76.2}
\]

If

\[
E=\max_{i\in V}e_i
\]

(with `E=0` if `V` is empty), then

\[
\boxed{K:r^E=K:r^\infty.}
\tag{C76.3}
\]

### Proof

Colon by a principal power distributes over finite intersections, giving (C76.1).

If `i in H`, then `r notin sqrt(Q_i)`. Since `Q_i` is primary, from

\[
r^q x\in Q_i
\]

and `r^q notin sqrt(Q_i)` one gets `x in Q_i`. Thus

\[
Q_i:r^q=Q_i
\qquad(i\in H,\ q\ge0).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `i in V`, then `r in sqrt(Q_i)`, so some positive power of `r` lies in `Q_i`, and `e_i` is finite. For `q>=e_i`,

\[
r^q\in Q_i,
\]

hence

\[
Q_i:r^q=R.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Taking `q>=E` in (C76.1) deletes every vertical primary component and leaves exactly the horizontal intersection, proving (C76.2)–(C76.3).

### First-colon criterion

A useful immediate corollary is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{if every vertical primary component contains }r,
\text{ then }K:r^\infty=K:r.
}
\tag{C76.4}
\]

This strictly extends C75. Radicality is sufficient because then the primary components are prime, so every vertical component automatically contains `r`; but radicality is not necessary.

# 6. `P02-C77-UNMIXED-EXACT-COLON-DEPTH` — PROVED

Assume in addition that `R/K` is **unmixed**, equivalently in the chosen minimal primary decomposition every `P_i` is a minimal prime of `K`.

Let

\[
E=\max_{i\in V}e_i.
\]

Then `E` is not merely an upper bound: it is the exact first stabilization stage.

\[
\boxed{
\min\{q\ge0:K:r^q=K:r^\infty\}=E.
}
\tag{C77.1}
\]

### Proof

C76 gives stabilization by stage `E`, so only the lower bound remains.

Choose `i in V` with `e_i=E`, and let `q<E`. By minimality of `e_i`,

\[
r^q\notin Q_i,
\]

so `Q_i:r^q` is proper. It remains proper after localization at `P_i`: if

\[
(Q_i:r^q)_{P_i}=R_{P_i},
\]

there would exist `s notin P_i` with

\[
sr^q\in Q_i.
\]

Since `Q_i` is `P_i`-primary and `s notin sqrt(Q_i)`, this would force `r^q in Q_i`, contradiction.

Because the `P_j` are distinct minimal primes, they are pairwise incomparable. For every `j ne i` choose

\[
s_j\in P_j\setminus P_i.
\]

Some power of `s_j` lies in `Q_j`, so `Q_jR_{P_i}=R_{P_i}`. Therefore

\[
(K:r^q)_{P_i}=(Q_i:r^q)_{P_i}
e R_{P_i}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

But the saturation deletes the vertical component `Q_i`; all surviving horizontal components also localize to the unit ideal at `P_i`. Hence

\[
(K:r^\infty)_{P_i}=R_{P_i}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus

\[
K:r^q\ne K:r^\infty
\qquad(q<E).
\]

Combining with C76 proves (C77.1).

### Interpretation

For an unmixed tail scheme, the higher Bockstein ladder is exactly measuring the largest scheme-theoretic `r`-thickness of a vertical irreducible component.

There is no additional hidden colon depth beyond that primary multiplicity.

# 7. `P02-C78-PERFECT-HEIGHT2-THICKNESS` — PROVED

Return to the H4 setting with regular local `R`. Suppose the integral tail ideal `K` has height two and

\[
R/K
\]

is Cohen–Macaulay; in particular this holds for the perfect three-generated Hilbert–Burch case used in C66.

Then `R/K` is unmixed. Indeed, if `P in Ass(R/K)`, then localization gives depth zero at `P`; Cohen–Macaulayness localizes, so

\[
\dim (R/K)_P=0,
\]

which means `P` is minimal in the support. Thus there are no embedded associated primes.

Therefore C77 applies:

\[
\boxed{
\text{colon depth of }K
=
\max_{P_i\supset(r)}
\min\{e\ge1:r^e\in Q_i\}.
}
\tag{C78.1}
\]

This is the precise higher-stage extension of the first Hilbert–Burch/Bockstein criterion C66.

## 7.1 Exact recovery of the C72 family

For any `m>=1`, in

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],
\]

take

\[
K_m=(r^m x,y).
\]

The sequence `y, r^m x` is regular, so `K_m` is a height-two complete intersection and `R/K_m` is Cohen–Macaulay/unmixed.

A primary decomposition is

\[
K_m=(x,y)\cap(r^m,y).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The first component is horizontal (`r notin (x,y)`); the second is `(r,y)`-primary with

\[
\min\{e:r^e\in(r^m,y)\}=m.
\]

Therefore C77 gives

\[
\boxed{
\min\{q:K_m:r^q=K_m:r^\infty\}=m,
}
\]

exactly recovering the C72 unbounded-depth computation.

Thus C72 is not an accidental coordinate trick: it is the universal primary-thickness mechanism of C77 realized by a complete intersection.

# 8. `P02-D26-NILPOTENCE-INDEX-NOT-COLON-DEPTH` — DISPROVED as a method claim

C74 says that higher colon depth lives in nilpotent vertical structure. A tempting strengthening is:

> the nilpotence index of the nilradical should determine, or at least force comparably large, colon depth.

False.

Fix any `m>=2` and let

\[
R=k[[r,u,x,y]],
\qquad
K=(x^m,rx,y).
\]

Then

\[
\sqrt K=(x,y).
\]

In `R/K`, the class of `x` is nilpotent of exact index `m`: `x^m=0`, while `x^{m-1}` is nonzero. Thus the nilradical can have arbitrarily large nilpotence index.

However

\[
x\in K:r
\]

because `rx in K`. Hence

\[
K:r\supseteq(x,y).
\]

Conversely modulo `(x,y)` the element `r` is a nonzerodivisor, so no element outside `(x,y)` can enter the saturation. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
K:r=K:r^\infty=(x,y).
}
\tag{D26.1}
\]

The entire saturation occurs at the first colon despite arbitrarily high nilpotence.

One can see the mechanism directly from

\[
(x^m,rx,y)
=(x,y)\cap(r,x^m,y).
\tag{8.1}
\]

The second component is vertical but already contains `r`, so its vertical thickness is exactly one. The large nilpotence index comes from the `x`-direction and is irrelevant to the `r`-colon ladder.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{nilpotence index alone is not the higher-Bockstein depth.}
}
\]

The correct invariant in the unmixed case is C77's vertical primary `r`-thickness.

This is a method falsification, not a counterexample to Quillen localization.

# 9. Consequences for the rank-three attack

## 9.1 Elementary transitivity / `Um_4/E_4`

The global target remains the actual unstable orbit problem. C76–C78 do not prove that every row can be moved into a favorable tail geometry.

They do identify what a source-changing move must accomplish: it must alter the **vertical primary decomposition** of the integral tail, not merely tune higher jets inside one fixed geometry.

## 9.2 Bockstein / Hilbert–Burch route

C71 gave an infinite-looking ladder. C77 converts it, for unmixed tails, into finite geometric data:

\[
\text{higher Bockstein depth}
=
\max \text{ vertical }r\text{-primary thickness}.
\]

In the perfect height-two case the first Hilbert–Burch defect C66 detects the thickness-one layer, while deeper Bocksteins detect successive powers inside the same vertical primary component.

The next problem is therefore not to search arbitrary `q` blindly, but to control the primary exponents under allowed source-changing elementary moves.

## 9.3 Mennicke / Euler / symbol route

If a vertical primary component survives every admissible source change with a fixed nontrivial thickness and keeps `[alpha]` outside the corresponding captured layer, that persistence becomes a concrete candidate unstable symbol/orbit obstruction. At present no such invariant has been proved.

## 9.4 Localization / patching

C76 gives an exact patching picture. Localization at `r` deletes every component whose radical contains `r`; the colon exponent records how much scheme-theoretic thickness must be removed before that deletion is already visible integrally.

On radical tails, or more generally when every vertical primary component contains `r`, patching is one-step. Deep patching comes only from powers `r^e` with `e>1` inside vertical primary components.

## 9.5 Descent / approximation

C68 says a fixed stage depends on a finite jet, while C72 says no universal stage exists. C77 now refines the obstruction: a descent theorem useful for P02 should preserve or simplify **vertical primary thickness**, not merely reduced special fiber or total nilpotence index.

No current finite-type descent theorem is imported as providing this for arbitrary ramified H4.

## 9.6 Hermitian K-theory / rank two

Nothing in C76–C78 closes rank two. The separate C23/C25/C32–C35 interfaces remain live. The all-characteristic `C35R` remains downgraded; classical elementary-Witt identifications are used only under their actually checked hypotheses, particularly `2 in A^times` where required.

# 12. Adversarial checks of the new P02 arguments

1. **C76 decomposition dependence:** the upper exponent `E` depends on a chosen primary decomposition when embedded components are present. The saturation formula itself is invariant; C77's exact depth is stated only in the unmixed case where the minimal components give the intrinsic geometric thickness.
2. **C77 localization:** properness of `(Q_i:r^q)_{P_i}` uses primaryness explicitly; without it a denominator outside `P_i` could kill the component.
3. **C77 incomparability:** the proof uses minimal primes, not arbitrary associated primes. This is why the exact statement is restricted to unmixed tails.
4. **C78 Cohen–Macaulay use:** the only role of CM is to exclude embedded associated primes. No freeness or regularity of `R/K` is inferred from CM alone.
5. **D26:** the example deliberately has an embedded vertical component. This is why large nilpotence index and colon depth separate; it does not contradict C77.
6. **No cancellation overclaim:** even perfect height-two primary thickness is only a description of the saturation mechanism. It does not prove that a source-changing `E_4(A)` move can reduce that thickness or capture `alpha`.
