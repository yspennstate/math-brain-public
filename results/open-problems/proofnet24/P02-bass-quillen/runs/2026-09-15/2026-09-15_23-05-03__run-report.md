# 3. Mandatory elementary audits

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation — exactly rank one

Let `A` be commutative and let `P` be a finitely generated projective module of constant rank one. If

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`,

take the top exterior power. The determinant of the left side is

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P tensor det(A^m) ~= P`,

while the determinant of the right side is `A`. Therefore

`P ~= A`.

This proves only the stated rank-one cancellation. It gives no rank-two or rank-three cancellation theorem.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`, `A=R[1/x]`.

Then

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`. They are distinct, since equality would put

`y-(y-x)=x`

in a proper ideal although `x` is a unit in `A`.

Hence localization at a parameter need not be local. Local-ring freeness may be used only after an actual localization at a maximal ideal or another proof of locality.

# 6. `P02-C116-UNIT-QUOTIENT-SURJECTIVITY` — PROVED

## Statement

Assume the C92 branch. Thus

`R` is regular local of dimension four,

`S=R/(F)` is regular local of dimension three,

and the image of the parameter `r` remains a parameter of `S`. Put

`A=R[1/r]`, `B=S[1/r]=A/(F)`.

Then:

1. the quotient map on units
   
   `A^x -> B^x`
   
   is surjective;
2. determinant identifies stable `K_1(A)` with `A^x` and stable `K_1(B)` with `B^x`;
3. consequently the stable map
   
   `K_1(A) -> K_1(B)`
   
   is surjective.

In particular, if `deltabar in B^x`, then there is an **actual unit** `epsilon in A^x` lifting `deltabar`.

## Proof: units

A regular local ring is a UFD. Since `r` is a parameter, it is prime in both `R` and `S`.

Let `a/r^n` be a unit of `A`. Then there exist `b in R` and `m>=0` with

`(a/r^n)(b/r^m)=1`,

so

`ab=r^(n+m)`.

Unique factorization forces

`a=epsilon r^k`

for a unit `epsilon in R^x` and some `k>=0`. Therefore

`A^x=R^x r^Z`.  (C116.1)

The identical argument in `S` gives

`B^x=S^x r^Z`.  (C116.2)

The local quotient map

`R^x -> S^x`

is surjective. Indeed, a unit of `S=R/(F)` has a lift outside the maximal ideal of `R`, hence a unit of `R`. Equivalently, because `F in m`, any lift of a residue-class unit is a unit.

Combining with (C116.1)–(C116.2) proves

`boxed{A^x -> B^x is surjective}.`  (C116.3)

## Proof: stable `K_1`

For completeness, the same exact localization calculation used earlier for C93 also applies to `A`.

Because `R` and `R/(r)` are regular local,

`K_0(R)=Z=K_0(R/(r))`,

and `K_1(R)=R^x`. Quillen localization contains

`K_1(R) -> K_1(A) -> K_0(R/(r)) -> K_0(R)`.

The final map is zero because

`0 -> R --r--> R -> R/(r) -> 0`

makes `[R/(r)]=0` in `K_0(R)`. The localization boundary of the unit `r` generates `K_0(R/(r))` up to sign. Hence every stable `K_1(A)` class is represented by

`epsilon r^n`, `epsilon in R^x`.

Its determinant is exactly that unit. Thus

`K_1(A) ~= A^x`.  (C116.4)

C93 gives

`K_1(B) ~= B^x`.  (C116.5)

The determinant diagrams commute, and (C116.3) proves the stable map is surjective.

QED.

## Interpretation

This eliminates one possible red herring. In the C92 branch there is no scalar obstruction to lifting a residual determinant: every residual unit already has an actual unit lift in `A`.

The failure of a chosen C113 determinant certificate is therefore about how that determinant sits inside a **specific finite-size matrix presentation**, not about the unit group or stable `K_1`.

# 7. `P02-C117-WHOLE-COMPLETION-TWO-OPEN-LIFT` — PROVED

This is the main structural result of the run.

## Statement

Let `A` be any commutative ring, `F in A`, and

`B=A/(F)`.

Let

`Mbar in SL_n(B)`

for any `n>=2`. Choose an arbitrary entrywise lift

`M0 in M_n(A)`.

Since `det(Mbar)=1`, there is `h in A` such that

`delta:=det(M0)=1+Fh`.  (C117.1)

Then:

1. `(F,delta)=A`, hence
   
   `Spec A = D(F) union D(delta)`;  (C117.2)
2. over `A_delta`,
   
   `G_delta := diag(delta^(-1),1,...,1) M0`
   
   lies in `SL_n(A_delta)` and reduces to `Mbar`;  (C117.3)
3. a global lift
   
   `G in SL_n(A)`, `G mod F = Mbar`,
   
   exists **if and only if**, inside `SL_n(A_{F delta})`, one has a factorization
   
   `boxed{G_delta = Q_delta^(-1) H_F}`  (C117.4)
   
   with
   
   `Q_delta in SL_n(A_delta,F):=ker(SL_n(A_delta)->SL_n(B))`
   
   and
   
   `H_F in SL_n(A_F)`.

Because `delta mod F=1`, localizing `B` at the image of `delta` changes nothing, so the relative group in (C117.4) is unambiguous.

## Proof of the cover and local lift

From (C117.1),

`1=delta-Fh`,

so `(F,delta)=A`. This proves the two-open cover.

On `D(delta)`, `M0` is invertible. Left multiplication by

`diag(delta^(-1),1,...,1)`

corrects its determinant to one. Modulo `F`, `delta` becomes `1`, so this diagonal correction reduces to the identity. Thus `G_delta` reduces exactly to `Mbar`.

This is a genuine use of locality only on the open where the determinant is known invertible; no global claim that `A` is local appears.

## Necessity of the relative double-coset factorization

Suppose a global lift

`G in SL_n(A)`

exists.

Over `D(delta)`, define

`Q_delta := G G_delta^(-1)`.

Both `G` and `G_delta` reduce to `Mbar`, hence

`Q_delta mod F = I_n`.

Therefore

`Q_delta in SL_n(A_delta,F)`.

Over `D(F)`, put

`H_F:=G`.

On the overlap,

`Q_delta G_delta=H_F`,

which is exactly (C117.4).

## Sufficiency

Conversely, assume (C117.4). On `D(F)` use the matrix `H_F`. On `D(delta)` use

`Q_delta G_delta`.

By (C117.4), these matrices agree on the overlap `D(Fdelta)`. Since matrices form a Zariski sheaf entrywise on the principal-open cover, they glue to one

`G in M_n(A)`.

Both local determinants equal one, so

`G in SL_n(A)`.

Modulo `F`, only the `D(delta)` chart meets `V(F)`, and there

`Q_delta mod F=I`, `G_delta mod F=Mbar`.

Hence

`G mod F=Mbar`.

This proves the equivalence.

QED.

## Statement

Let

`Vbar=(I+Dbar Ybar)(I+Ybar Dbar)^(-1) in SL_3(B)`

be a C112 Vaserstein generator, and choose lifts

`D=diag(lambda,1,1)`, `Y in M_3(A)`.

Put

`L=I+DY`, `delta=det L`.

For `i=2` or `3`, let

`J_i=(C_{i1},C_{i2},C_{i3})A`

be the ideal generated by the corresponding cofactor row of `L`.

If

`J_i=A`  (C118.1)

for at least one `i in {2,3}`, then `Vbar` admits an `SL_3(A)` lift.

## Proof

Modulo `F`, the matrix `Lbar=I+Dbar Ybar` is invertible, so

`deltabar=det Lbar in B^x`.

By C116 choose an actual unit

`epsilon in A^x`

lifting `deltabar`. Since `epsilon` and `delta` have the same image modulo `F`, write

`epsilon-delta=Fh`  (C118.2)

for some `h in A`.

Assume `J_i=A`. Then choose `z_1,z_2,z_3 in A` with

`h=sum_j z_j C_{ij}`.  (C118.3)

For `i=2` or `3`, the diagonal coefficient `d_i` in C115 equals one. Modify row `i` of `Y` by

`F(z_1,z_2,z_3)`.

C115 gives the exact new determinant

`delta' = delta + F sum_j z_j C_{ij} = epsilon`.

Thus `delta'` is a unit. Sylvester's determinant identity gives the same unit determinant for `I+Y'D`, so both matrices are invertible and C113 produces an `SL_3(A)` lift of `Vbar`.

QED.

## Scope

`J_i+(F)=A`.

Because `A` is nonlocal, that is strictly weaker than `J_i=A`. D36 shows the gap is real even for a residual identity factor.

# 9. `P02-D36-FIXED-PRESENTATION-COFACTOR-CORRECTION-FAILS` — method claim DISPROVED

D35 showed that one chosen determinant certificate can fail although the residual Vaserstein element is the identity. The same example gives a stronger no-go statement.

## Example

Let

`R0=k[x,y]_(x,y)`,

`A0=R0[1/x]`,

`F=y`,

`B0=A0/(y) ~= k(x)`.

Take

`D=I_3`,

`Y=diag(y-x-1,0,0)`.

Then

`L=I+DY=diag(y-x,1,1)`,

and the companion matrix `I+YD` is the same. Therefore the residual Vaserstein element is

`Vbar=I_3`.

So the group element itself has the trivial lift `I_3`.

The chosen determinant is

`delta=y-x`,

a nonunit because `(y-x)A0` is a proper maximal ideal.

## Cofactor rows

For

`L=diag(y-x,1,1)`,

the unrestricted cofactor-row ideals are

`J_2=(y-x)A0`,

`J_3=(y-x)A0`.  (D36.1)

They satisfy

`J_i+(F)=A0`

because `(y,y-x)` contains the unit `x` after inverting `x`, exactly as residual invertibility predicts. But neither `J_i` is the unit ideal.

The one-row C115 affine correction sets are

`delta + y J_i
 = (y-x)(1+yA0)`.  (D36.2)

Every element of (D36.2) is divisible by the nonunit `y-x`, so none is a unit.

Thus C118 cannot be weakened from `J_i=A` to merely `J_i+(F)=A`.

## Even simultaneous lower-row corrections fail with the first row frozen

Now allow **arbitrary simultaneous** changes of rows 2 and 3 of `Y` by multiples of `F=y`, while keeping the first row/presentation fixed.

The first row of the resulting `L` remains

`(y-x,0,0)`.

Therefore every resulting determinant is divisible by `y-x`: expansion along the first row gives

`det L'=(y-x) * D'`

for some `D' in A0`.

A product can be a unit only if each factor is a unit. Since `y-x` is not a unit, **no such lower-row correction can ever satisfy C113**.

Nevertheless the residual Vaserstein element is `I_3` and has the trivial presentation/lift obtained by taking `Y=0`.

Hence the overstrong method claim

> fixed-presentation row/cofactor correction must eventually produce a unit determinant whenever the residual Vaserstein element lifts

is false.

Record:

`P02-D36-FIXED-PRESENTATION-COFACTOR-CORRECTION-FAILS` — **DISPROVED AS A METHOD CLAIM**.

This is not a counterexample to Quillen, C112, C113, C115, C117, or C118. It proves that presentation/factorization freedom is an essential part of the route.

## Audited statement

Let `A` be an `m x m` random matrix with iid centered isotropic rows in `R^m`, nonsingular almost surely. Define determinant-square tilt by

For deterministic `c in R^m`, put

The peer claims

`E_Q x=0`,

and

`E_Q[xx^T]=(||c||^2/m) I_m`.

In particular, for `c=1_m`,

`E_Q ||A^(-1)1_m||^2=m`.

## Audit step 1 — determinant square

Expand

`det A = sum_{sigma in S_m} sgn(sigma) product_i A_{i,sigma(i)}`.

For two permutations `sigma,tau`, row independence gives

`E product_i A_{i,sigma(i)}A_{i,tau(i)}`

as the product of row covariances. Isotropy gives

`E A_{ij}A_{ik}=delta_{jk}`.

Hence the term vanishes unless `sigma=tau`, and every surviving term equals one. There are `m!` permutations. Therefore

`E det(A)^2=m!`.

PASS.

## Audit step 2 — Cramer numerators

Let `A^(j)(c)` be `A` with column `j` replaced by `c`. Cramer's rule gives

`det(A) x_j = det A^(j)(c)`.

Expand along the deterministic column:

`det A^(j)(c)=sum_i (-1)^(i+j)c_i det A_{-i,-j}`.

Thus

`E_Q[x_jx_k]`

is `1/m!` times the expectation of the product of two such replaced-column determinants.

If the deleted rows differ, say `i != ell`, condition on all rows except row `i`. The minor deleting row `i` is independent of row `i`, while the other minor is linear in row `i`; centeredness kills the term.

If the deleted rows agree, the remaining `(m-1)` rows are again independent centered isotropic rows. The determinant-covariance expansion gives

`E[det A_{-i,-j} det A_{-i,-k}] = (m-1)! delta_{jk}`.

Therefore

`E[det A^(j)(c) det A^(k)(c)] = ||c||^2 (m-1)! delta_{jk}`,

and division by `m!` gives

`E_Q[x_jx_k]=(||c||^2/m)delta_{jk}`.

PASS.

## Audit step 3 — first moment

For

`E[det A det A^(j)(c)]`,

expand the second determinant by deleted row `i`. Conditional on all rows except row `i`, its minor is fixed while `det A` is linear in the centered row `i`. Every term vanishes. Hence

`E_Q x=0`.

PASS.
