# 3. Mandatory elementary audits and stable/unstable distinction

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation — rederived

Let `A` be commutative and let `P` be a rank-one finitely generated projective module. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then taking top exterior powers gives

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)
\cong
P\otimes\bigwedge^m A^m
\cong P,
\]

while

\[
\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{P\cong A.}
\]

This proves only the displayed rank-one implication. It does **not** prove rank-two or rank-three cancellation and does not prove that an arbitrary P02 projective is stably free.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local — rederived

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},
\qquad
A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)
\cong
k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]
\cong k(x).
\]

Substituting `y=x` gives likewise

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)A` and `(y-x)A` are maximal. They are distinct: if they were equal, their common ideal would contain

\[
y-(y-x)=x,
\]

but `x` is a unit in `A`.

Therefore localization at a parameter need not preserve locality. No argument below applies “projective over local implies free” to `R[1/r]`.

## 3.3 The new theorem is genuinely unstable

The inherited stable facts

\[

SK_1(B)=0
\]

are not by themselves a proof that a length-three unimodular row is elementary. The new bridge is the **elementary symplectic Witt group** and the dimension-two Vaserstein bijection. This distinction is essential and is maintained in Claims C142–C144.

# 5. `P02-C142-PROJECTIVEFREE-SK1-WE-VANISHING` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `B` be a commutative ring satisfying:

1. every finitely generated projective `B`-module is free;
2. `SK_1(B)=0`.

Then

\[
\boxed{W_E(B)=0.}
\tag{C142.1}
\]

This statement is characteristic-free.

## Proof

Take an arbitrary class in `W_E(B)` represented by an invertible alternating matrix

\[
M\in M_{2n}(B),
\qquad
\operatorname{Pf}(M)=1.
\]

Let `F=B^{2n}` and let

\[
\omega(x,y)=x^tMy
\]

be the associated nondegenerate alternating form.

### Step 1: split one hyperbolic plane

Choose a free basis vector `e in F`. Since `M` is invertible, the row functional

\[
\omega(e,-):F\to B
\]

is unimodular. Therefore there exists `f in F` with

\[
\omega(e,f)=1.
\]

Put

\[
H=Be\oplus Bf.
\]

Because `omega` is alternating,

\[
\omega(e,e)=\omega(f,f)=0,
\]

and the restriction to `H` is the standard hyperbolic/symplectic plane.

For any `x in F`, define

\[
\pi_H(x)
=
-\omega(f,x)e+\omega(e,x)f.
\tag{C142.2}
\]

A direct check gives

\[
\pi_H(e)=e,
\qquad
\pi_H(f)=f,
\]

and

\[
\ker\pi_H=H^\perp.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{F=H\oplus H^\perp.}
\tag{C142.3}
\]

The complement `H^perp` is finitely generated projective, therefore free by hypothesis (1). The restriction of `omega` to `H^perp` is again nondegenerate alternating.

### Step 2: induct to a symplectic basis

Repeat the argument on `H^perp`. Induction produces a basis in which `omega` is the standard symplectic form

\[
\psi_{2n}=\psi_2\perp\cdots\perp\psi_2.
\]

Thus there is some

\[
G\in GL_{2n}(B)
\]

such that

\[
G^tMG=\psi_{2n}.
\tag{C142.4}
\]

### Step 3: the change of basis has determinant one

Apply the Pfaffian identity (4.4):

\[
1
=
\operatorname{Pf}(\psi_{2n})
=
\operatorname{Pf}(G^tMG)
=
\det(G)\operatorname{Pf}(M)
=
\det(G).
\]

Therefore

\[
G\in SL_{2n}(B).
\tag{C142.5}
\]

### Step 4: use `SK_1(B)=0` only after the determinant is fixed

Since the stable class of `G` lies in `SK_1(B)=0`, there exists an integer `q>=0` such that

\[
G\oplus I_q\in E_{2n+q}(B).
\]

If necessary stabilize once more, so that `q=2s` is even. Then

\[
G\oplus I_{2s}\in E_{2n+2s}(B).
\]

Stabilizing (C142.4) by `psi_{2s}` gives

\[
(G\oplus I_{2s})^t
(M\perp\psi_{2s})
(G\oplus I_{2s})
=
\psi_{2n+2s}.
\]

This is exactly elementary Witt equivalence of `M` with the neutral hyperbolic form. Hence `[M]=0` in `W_E(B)`.

Since `M` was arbitrary,

\[
W_E(B)=0.
\]

QED.

## Characteristic-two audit

No step divides by `2`.

- Alternating means `omega(x,x)=0`; the hyperbolic splitting remains valid in characteristic two.
- Formula (C142.2) uses only addition, multiplication, and the alternating pairing.
- The Pfaffian congruence identity is polynomial over `Z` and hence survives every characteristic.
- No identification with a higher Grothendieck–Witt group is used.

Thus C142 is genuinely all-characteristic.

## Why C142 is not the forbidden stable/unstable shortcut

`SK_1(B)=0` is used only after an arbitrary Pfaffian-one alternating form has first been reduced to the standard form by **projective-freeness**. It does not assert `SL_3(B)=E_3(B)`. The actual size-three conclusion comes later from Vaserstein's dimension-two theorem.

## Proof

\[
\text{every finitely generated projective }B\text{-module is free}.
\tag{C143.3}

\[

### Step 3: the elementary symplectic Witt group vanishes

\[
W_E(B)=0.

### Step 4: `dim B=2`

Extend `r` to a regular system of parameters

\[
r,x,y
\]

for `S`.

The ideal

\[
(x,y)
\]

is prime of height two because

\[
S/(x,y)

\[

The right side is zero by Step 3. Hence the orbit set is a singleton:

\[
Um_3(B)=e_1E_3(B).

QED.

Then

\[

\]

Because `alpha in m`,

\[
u\alpha\in\mathfrak m^2.
\]

Therefore in the tangent space

\[
[F]=[r]\ne0.
\]

\[
\boxed{F\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2,}
\]

so

\[

\tag{C145.1}

\[

\[
\boxed{\bar r\in\mathfrak m_S^2.}
\tag{C145.2}

\[
\mathfrak m_S/\mathfrak m_S^2.

\[
\boxed{\bar u\in\mathfrak m_S\setminus\mathfrak m_S^2.}
\tag{C145.3}

\[
\bar r=-\bar u\bar\alpha

and localization gives

\[
\boxed{
S[1/r]
\cong
S[1/(u\alpha)]
\cong
S[1/u,1/\alpha].
}
\tag{C145.4}
\]

If the image of `alpha` vanishes in `S`, the original C50 first coordinate is already a unit after the corresponding simplification, so that degenerate subcase is not a resistant branch.

## Case B: `N>=2`

Now

\[
r^N\in\mathfrak m^2
\]

and, because `alpha in m`,

\[
u\alpha\in\mathfrak m^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{F\in\mathfrak m^2.}
\tag{C145.5}
\]

The independent parameters `r,u` are nonassociate primes in the regular local UFD `R`, so `F` is nonzero; since `R` is a domain, it is a nonzerodivisor. Therefore

\[
\dim R/(F)=3.

But because `F in m^2`,

\[
\mathfrak m_S/\mathfrak m_S^2
\cong
\mathfrak m/(\mathfrak m^2+(F))
\cong
\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2
\]

has dimension four over the residue field. Thus the embedding dimension is four while the Krull dimension is three:

\[
\boxed{S=R/(F)\text{ is singular}.}
\tag{C145.6}
\]

Gabber/Swan is therefore unavailable for a second, more basic reason.

Combining the two cases:

\[
\boxed{

\begin{cases}
N=1:& S\text{ regular dim }3,\ r\in\mathfrak m_S^2,\ u\text{ a parameter};\\

\end{cases}}
\tag{C145.7}
\]

This is the precise geometry of the branch not closed by C144.
