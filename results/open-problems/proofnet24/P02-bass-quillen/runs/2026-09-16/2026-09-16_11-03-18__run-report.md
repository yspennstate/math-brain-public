# 3. Mandatory elementary audits rederived

## 3.1 Rank one only

Let `P` be rank one and suppose

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`.

Taking top exterior powers gives

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P`

and

`det(A^(m+1)) ~= A`.

Hence `P ~= A`.

This proves **only** rank-one cancellation under the displayed stable-freeness premise. It does not cancel rank two or rank three and does not prove stable freeness of arbitrary projectives.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`, `A=R[1/x]`.

Then

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus both `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal. They are distinct, because equality would imply that their difference `x` lies in the common ideal, impossible since `x` is a unit in `A`.

So `R[1/r]` cannot be treated as local merely because `R` is local.

# 5. `P02-C161-N1-SINGULAR-DIVISOR-TRANSPOSE-SIGN` — PROVED

## 5.1 Statement

Retain the full inherited `N=1` setup above. No regularity assumption is made on

`X=Spec(C/(q))`.

Let

`tau:SK_1(B)->SK_1(B)`

be induced by matrix transpose,

`tau([M])=[M^T]`.

Then under the inherited isomorphism

`beta:SK_1(B)->CH_0(X)`

one has

`boxed: beta(tau x) = - beta(x)`

for every `x in SK_1(B)`.

## 5.2 Localization boundary in support `K_0`

Let

`delta:K_1(B)->K_0^{V(q)}(C)`

be the localization boundary. We use the explicit boundary convention already fixed in inherited C154.

Take `M in SL_n(B)` representing `x`. Choose `N` so that

`L=q^N M in M_n(C)`.

Since

`det L=q^(Nn)`

and `C` is a domain, `L:C^n->C^n` is injective. Put

`T=coker L`.

Then the boundary is

`delta([M])=[T]-Nn[C/q]`.                                      (5.1)

This is the standard localization formula: the scalar denominator `q^N I_n` contributes `Nn[C/q]`.

The current Weibel–Yao localization source explicitly describes `partial:K_1(S^-1A)->K_0 Perf(A,S)` by the two-term complex `0->A --s-> A->0` for a denominator `s`; Weibel's *K-book* supplies the ordinary commutative localization/dévissage framework.

## 5.3 Ambient derived duality

Let

`D_C(-)=RHom_C(-,C)`

on perfect complexes supported on `V(q)`.

Dualizing

`0 -> C^n --L-> C^n -> T -> 0`

gives

`0 -> C^n --L^T-> C^n -> Ext_C^1(T,C) -> 0`,

because `Hom_C(T,C)=0`: `T` is torsion and `C` is a domain. Therefore

`Ext_C^1(T,C) ~= coker(L^T)`.

In support `K_0`, the shift by one gives

`D_C([T]) = -[coker(L^T)]`.                                   (5.2)

Likewise

`D_C([C/q])=-[C/q]`,

because `q` is a nonzerodivisor and `C/q` has the length-one resolution

`0 -> C --q-> C -> C/q ->0`.

Applying `D_C` to (5.1) yields

`D_C delta([M])`

`= -[coker(L^T)] + Nn[C/q]`

`= - delta([M^T])`.

Hence

`boxed: delta(tau x) = - D_C(delta x).`                        (5.3)

This identity is exact before any passage to the reduced divisor.

## 5.4 Effect of ambient duality on the C151 zero-cycle quotient

Inherited C151 identifies the quotient of the support group by generic component classes with `CH_0(X)`. It remains to compute the action induced by `D_C` on that quotient.

For a closed point `z in X`, the local ring `C_z` is regular local of dimension two. A regular local ring is Gorenstein, and for its residue field `k(z)` one has

`Ext^i_{C_z}(k(z),C_z)=0` for `i != 2`,

`Ext^2_{C_z}(k(z),C_z) ~= k(z)`.

The shift is even. Therefore in support `K_0`,

`D_C([k(z)])=[k(z)]`.                                         (5.4)

By contrast, for a generic component `C/p_i`, codimension is one and duality changes its sign. Thus the subgroup generated by the generic component classes is preserved, while every closed-point generator of the C151 `CH_0` term is fixed.

Consequently the map induced by `D_C` on

`CH_0(X)`

is the identity, even when `X` is singular or reducible.

Combining this with (5.3) proves

`beta(tau x)=-beta(x)`.

## `P02-C164-NONSPLIT-DOUBLEPOINT-SYMPLECTIC-SATURATION` — PROVED

Let

`C_0 = R[x,y]_(x,y)`

with `R` the real numbers, and put

`q=x^2+y^2`,

`A_0=C_0/(q)`,

`X_0=Spec(A_0)`,

`B_0=C_0[1/q]`.

Then:

1. `X_0` is an integral singular local curve;
2. its normalization is

   `tilde A_0 = C[t]_(t)`

   `x |-> t`, `y |-> i t`;
3. `CH_0(X_0) ~= Z/2`;
4. the generator of `SK_1(B_0) ~= CH_0(X_0)` is represented by an explicit `SL_2(B_0)=Sp_2(B_0)` matrix;
5. consequently

   `Sigma_q = CH_0(X_0)`

   and

   `W_E(B_0)=0`.

### 8.1 Computing the singular `CH_0`

Because `A_0` is local one-dimensional and integral, its zero-cycle group is generated by the unique closed point `m=(x,y)`.

The normalization has one closed point over `m`, with residue extension

`C/R`

of degree two. For a rational function `h`, pushforward of the normalization divisor gives coefficient

`2 v_t(h)`

at `m`. Hence every principal zero-cycle has even coefficient.

Conversely `t=x` has valuation one, and directly

`length_{A_0}(A_0/(x))=length_R(R[y]_(y)/(y^2))=2`.

Thus `2[m]` is principal and no odd multiple is principal. Therefore

`CH_0(X_0)=Z[m]/2Z[m] ~= Z/2`.

The normalization itself is a DVR, hence has zero `CH_0`; the entire `Z/2` is a singular gluing/residue-degree correction.

### 8.2 An explicit symplectic generator

Set

`P=[[x,-y],[y,x]]`,

so `det P=q`.

Over `B_0`, define

`M=P diag(q^-1,1)`.

Then

`det M=1`,

so

`M in SL_2(B_0)=Sp_2(B_0)`.

Clear one denominator:

`L=qM=P diag(1,q)`.

Since `P` is injective and `diag(1,q)C_0^2` has quotient `C_0/q`, there is an exact sequence

`0 -> C_0/q -> coker L -> coker P -> 0`.                 (8.1)

Now `coker P` is the normalization module `tilde A_0`. Indeed the map

`C_0^2 -> tilde A_0`,

`(a,b) |-> a+i b`

kills the two columns `(x,y)` and `(-y,x)` of `P`; it is surjective. Both sides are rank-one maximal Cohen–Macaulay `A_0`-modules. The generic map is an isomorphism, so the kernel after passing through `coker P` is finite length; a depth-one MCM module has no nonzero finite-length submodule, hence the kernel vanishes.

The normalization quotient is explicit:

`0 -> A_0 -> tilde A_0 -> R -> 0`,

where the quotient is generated by the class of `i`, annihilated by `(x,y)`.

Using the inherited C154 localization convention,

`delta([M])=[coker L]-2[C_0/q]`.

By (8.1),

`delta([M])=[tilde A_0]-[A_0]=[R]`.

Therefore

`beta([M])=[m]`,

the nonzero generator of `CH_0(X_0)=Z/2`.

But `M` is already symplectic. Hence the entire `Z/2` lies in `Sigma_q`, proving

`Sigma_q=CH_0(X_0)`

and therefore

`W_E(B_0)=0`.

For completeness, `B_0` is a one-dimensional localization of the regular local UFD `C_0`, hence a one-dimensional regular UFD; its finite projectives are free, so the inherited stable symplectic exact sequence applies.

`Pi_A h(x)=E_{v in K_A} q_A(v) h(x+v)`.

For `h=Fq_A`,

`Pi_A(Fq_A)(x)=q_A(x) E_{v in K_A}F(x+v)`.

By Parseval,

`1-epsilon_A`

`=E_x (E_{v in K_A}F(x+v))^2`

`=E_{v,w in K_A} E_x F(x+v)F(x+w)`.

Since `v+w` is uniform in `K_A`, this equals

`E_{u in K_A} E_x F(x)F(x+u)`

`=E_{u in K_A}(1-2I_u(F))`.

This proves (12.1) exactly.
