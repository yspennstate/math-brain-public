# 3. Mandatory elementary audits

## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation — exactly the stated rank

Let `A` be commutative and let `P` be a constant-rank-one projective. If

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`,

then taking top exterior powers gives

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P tensor det(A^m) ~= P`,

while

`det(A^(m+1)) ~= A`.

Hence

`P ~= A`.

This proves only the displayed rank-one implication under its stable-freeness premise. It proves neither rank-two nor rank-three cancellation and does not prove that an arbitrary relevant projective is stably free.

## 3.2 `R[1/r]` need not be local

Let

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`, `A=R[1/x]`.

Then

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and, after substituting `y=x`,

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`. They are distinct: if equal, their common ideal would contain

`y-(y-x)=x`,

but `x` is a unit in `A`.

Therefore localization at a regular parameter need not be local. No local-ring projective-freeness theorem is applied to `R[1/r]` merely because `R` is local.

No characteristic assumption is required.

## 5.2 Transpose acts by inversion on the reduced divisor class

Let

`x=[M] in SK_1(B)`, `M in SL_n(B)`.

Choose `N` so that

`L=q^N M in M_n(C)`.

Because `det M=1`,

`det L=q^(Nn)`.

The ordinary localization boundary is represented by the perfect torsion presentation

`P_L = [ C^n --L--> C^n ]`,

with the scalar `q^N I_n` contribution removed. This is exactly the finite-cokernel mechanism already used in inherited C154/C151.

Let `tau` denote transpose on stable `K_1`:

`tau([M])=[M^T]`.

Dualizing the two-term presentation gives the transpose presentation. For the principal regular immersion

`i:D -> Spec C`,

codimension-one devissage identifies the shifted `C`-dual of a perfect complex supported on `D` with ordinary vector-bundle duality on `D`; the normal-line twist is trivial because `D=(q)` is principal. Thus, after passing to the reduced rank-zero part of `K_0(D)`, transpose sends the determinant line to its dual.

Concretely, under

`K_0(D) -> rank on components + Pic(D)`, 

vector-bundle duality sends

`(r,L) -> (r,L^{-1})`.

The generic component-rank terms and the scalar class `[C/q]` have trivial reduced Picard part, so after the C151 quotient one gets

`boxed: beta(tau x) = - beta(x).`                                      (5.1)

This is the only duality statement needed. It is ordinary `K/G`-theory devissage for a principal regular immersion, not a comparison between classical `W_E` and a higher Grothendieck–Witt group.

## 5.3 Hyperbolic matrices produce doubles

For `M in SL_n(B)` define

`H(M)=diag(M,M^{-T})`.

With the standard symplectic matrix

`J=[[0,I],[-I,0]]`,

a direct multiplication gives

`H(M)^T J H(M)=J`.

Hence

`H(M) in Sp_{2n}(B)`.

Its underlying stable `K_1` class is

`[H(M)] = [M] + [M^{-T}]`

`          = x - tau(x)`.

Applying (5.1),

`beta([H(M)]) = beta(x)-beta(tau x)`

`             = beta(x)-(-beta(x))`

`             = 2 beta(x)`.

Since every element of `CH_0(D)` is `beta(x)` for some `x` by C151,

`boxed: 2 CH_0(D) subset Sigma_alpha.`                               (5.2)

That proves C158.

# 7. `P02-C160-N1-ALGEBRAICALLY-CLOSED-SMOOTH-DIVISOR-CLOSURE` — PROVED under explicit extra hypotheses

## 7.1 Statement

Continue under C159 and assume in addition that every connected component of `D` is a regular affine curve of finite type over an algebraically closed field `k`.

Then

`CH_0(D)=Pic(D)`

is divisible. In particular it is `2`-divisible, so C159 gives

`boxed: Um_3(B)=e_1 E_3(B)`

and the inherited original rank-three kernel is free.

## 7.2 Divisibility of the Picard group of a smooth affine curve

It suffices to treat one connected component `U`.

Let

`j:U -> \bar U`

be its smooth projective completion. Since `U` is affine and one-dimensional, the boundary

`Z=\bar U\U`

is finite and nonempty.

There is the standard exact divisor/Picard sequence

`Div_Z(\bar U) -> Pic(\bar U) -> Pic(U) -> 0.`                         (7.1)

Over an algebraically closed field every closed point has degree one. The degree map gives

`0 -> Pic^0(\bar U) -> Pic(\bar U) -> Z -> 0.`                        (7.2)

Because `Z` is nonempty, the subgroup generated by the boundary points maps surjectively to the degree `Z` in (7.2). Hence `Pic(U)` is a quotient of

`Pic^0(\bar U)(k)`.

The group `Pic^0(\bar U)(k)` is the group of `k`-points of the Jacobian abelian variety. For every integer `n>=1`, multiplication by `n` on an abelian variety is a finite surjective isogeny; over algebraically closed `k` it is therefore surjective on `k`-points. Thus

`Pic^0(\bar U)(k)`

is divisible, including for `n` divisible by `char(k)`.

A quotient of a divisible abelian group is divisible. Therefore `Pic(U)` is divisible. A finite direct product over the connected components of `D` is again divisible.

So

`2 CH_0(D)=CH_0(D)`.

C159 closes all residual rows and inherited C55 closes the original rank-three module.

# 8. `P02-D47-SYMPLECTIC-IMAGE-EQUALS-DOUBLES-NOGO` — DISPROVED method claim

C158 proves only

`2 SK_1 subset image(K_1Sp->SK_1)`

after transport to the regular-divisor zero-cycle model. A tempting next shortcut would be to assert equality in general. That equality is false.

Let

`R0 = R[x,y]/(x^2+y^2-1)`

with real coefficients, and define

`A(x,y)=[[x,y],[-y,x]]`.

Then

`det A=x^2+y^2=1`,

so

`A in SL_2(R0)`.

For `2x2` matrices,

`Sp_2=SL_2`,

hence the stable class `[A]` lies in

`image(K_1Sp(R0)->SK_1(R0))`.

Now evaluate polynomial matrices on the real circle

`S^1={(x,y) in R^2:x^2+y^2=1}`

and stabilize to `SL_n(R)`, `n>=3`. Polar decomposition deformation-retracts `SL_n(R)` onto `SO(n)`. The loop `A` is the standard full rotation in a fixed two-plane, so after stabilization it represents the nonzero class

`1 in pi_1(SO(n)) ~= Z/2`.

Every elementary matrix loop is null-homotopic as a free loop: for

`e_ij(h(x,y))`

scale the off-diagonal function continuously by `t in [0,1]`. Products of elementary matrices are therefore null in the stable `Z/2` loop invariant. Consequently `[A]` is nonzero in stable `SK_1(R0)`.

The evaluation/homotopy invariant is a group homomorphism

`nu: SK_1(R0) -> Z/2`.

For every `z in SK_1(R0)`,

`nu(2z)=0`,

whereas

`nu([A])=1`.

Thus `[A]` is **not** a double in `SK_1(R0)`, even though it lies in the symplectic image.

Therefore

`boxed: image(K_1Sp->SK_1) can be strictly larger than 2 SK_1.`

This kills the proposed equality method. It does not contradict C158 and is not a counterexample to Quillen localization.

## 10.2 Picard sign check

On a regular curve the rank/determinant description sends a vector bundle `E` to

Dualization sends

## 10.3 Why the principal-divisor hypothesis matters

For `D=(q)` the normal-line twist in codimension-one duality is globally trivial. On a nonprincipal Cartier divisor there is an additional normal-line twist. In the present ring-theoretic localization `D` is principal by construction, so no hidden twist is discarded.

## 10.4 Why regularity is not silently extended to singular `X`

If `X` is singular, `G_0(X)` is not controlled by rank plus determinant alone; conductor/singularity classes can survive. Therefore C158 is **not** asserted for arbitrary singular reduced divisors. The next attack explicitly isolates that missing singular correction instead of pretending the regular proof survives unchanged.
