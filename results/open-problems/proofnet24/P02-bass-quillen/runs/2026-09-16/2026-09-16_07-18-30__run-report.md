## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation — only rank one

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a constant-rank-one projective. If

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`,

then

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P tensor det(A^m) ~= P`,

while

`det(A^(m+1)) ~= A`.

Hence `P ~= A`.

This proves exactly the stated rank-one cancellation under the stable-freeness premise. It does not imply rank-two or rank-three cancellation, and it does not show that an arbitrary projective is stably free.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`,  `A=R[1/x]`.

Then

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`. They are distinct: equality would put

`y-(y-x)=x`

in the common ideal, but `x` is a unit in `A`.

Therefore no local-ring projective-freeness theorem is applied to `R[1/r]` merely because `R` is local.

# 5. `P02-C147-N1-REFLEXIVE-EXTENSION-PROJECTIVEFREE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `C` be a noetherian regular domain of Krull dimension at most two such that every finitely generated projective `C`-module is free. Let `0 != a in C`. Then every finitely generated projective module over

`B=C[1/a]`

is free.

In particular this applies to the C146 ring `C=S[1/u]` and `a=alpha`.

## Proof

Let `P` be a finite projective `B`-module of rank `n`. Choose a split embedding

`P -> B^N`.

Choose finitely many `B`-generators `p_1,...,p_s` of `P`. Multiplying all of them by one sufficiently large common power `a^L`, arrange that

`m_j=a^L p_j in C^N`.

Let

`M = C m_1 + ... + C m_s subset C^N`.

Then `M` is finite and torsion-free, and localization gives

`M_a=P`

inside `B^N` because multiplication by `a^L` is invertible over `B`.

Form the reflexive hull

`N=M**=Hom_C(Hom_C(M,C),C)`.

Because `C` is noetherian, `M` is finitely presented, so localization commutes with the relevant Homs. Hence

`N_a ~= (M_a)** ~= P** ~= P`,

since a finite projective module is reflexive.

Now `N` is a finite reflexive `C`-module. `Spec C` is regular of dimension at most two. Stacks Project, Lemma 31.13.15 (Tag `0B3N`), states precisely that on a regular scheme of dimension at most two, a coherent reflexive module is finite locally free. Therefore `N` is a finite projective `C`-module.

By the hypothesis that every finite projective over `C` is free,

`N ~= C^n`.

Localizing at `a` gives

`P ~= N_a ~= B^n`.

So every finite projective `B`-module is free. QED.

## Why this is useful but not rank-three closure by itself

For a residual row in `Um_3(B)`, projective-freeness says its rank-two kernel is free and therefore the row is completable by some invertible matrix. It does **not** say that the row lies in the elementary orbit of `e_1`. The difference is exactly unstable/stable special-linear information; a general completion need not lift through `A -> B`, whereas elementary matrices do lift coefficientwise.

Thus C147 removes projective-module obstruction but leaves the correct `SL/E` / Mennicke / Vaserstein obligation.

# 6. `P02-C148-N1-DIVISOR-G0-SK1-EXACT-SEQUENCE` — PROVED

## Statement

Continue the C146 setting. Since `C` is a localization of the regular local UFD `S`, it is a UFD. Write the nonzero nonunit

`alpha = epsilon p_1^{e_1} ... p_t^{e_t}`

with `epsilon in C^x`, pairwise nonassociate primes `p_i`, and `e_i>=1`. Put

`D=C/(alpha)`,  `B=C[1/alpha]`.

Then there is a natural short exact sequence

`0 -> Z^t -> G_0(D) -> SK_1(B) -> 0`,

where the `i`th standard basis vector maps to the class `[C/p_i]` under the devissage identification of `alpha`-torsion modules with `G_0(D)`.

Equivalently,

`SK_1(B) ~= G_0(C/(alpha)) / <[C/p_1],...,[C/p_t]>`.

Call the quotient

`Q_alpha := G_0(C/(alpha)) / <[C/p_i]>`.

## Proof

### Step 1: localization exact sequence

For a noetherian ring `C` and the multiplicative system generated by `alpha`, Quillen localization and devissage give

`K_1(C) -> K_1(B) --partial--> G_0(D) -> G_0(C) -> G_0(B) -> 0`.

This is the `n=0` part of Weibel, *The K-book*, V.6.1.1. Example V.6.1.2 fixes the sign convention: for a domain and a nonzero `s`,

`partial(s)=[C/sC]`.

Because `C` and `B` are regular, `K_0=G_0` for them.

### Step 2: the two K0 groups are both Z

Every finite projective over `C` is free by inherited C143, so

`K_0(C)=Z`

via rank.

Every finite projective over `B` is free by C147, so

`K_0(B)=Z`.

Localization preserves rank and sends `[C]` to `[B]`, hence

`K_0(C) -> K_0(B)`

is the identity on `Z`. Therefore its kernel is zero, so the transfer

`G_0(D) -> K_0(C)`

is zero and `partial:K_1(B)->G_0(D)` is surjective.

### Step 3: the left term is exactly C^x

Inherited C143 gives `SK_1(C)=0`. For a commutative ring, determinant splits

`K_1(C)=C^x direct_sum SK_1(C)`,

so here

`K_1(C)=C^x`.

The map `C^x -> K_1(B)` is injective: composing it with determinant gives the ordinary injective inclusion of units `C^x -> B^x`.

Therefore localization sharpens to

`0 -> C^x -> K_1(B) --partial--> G_0(D) -> 0`.   (6.1)

### Step 4: split determinant on K1(B)

Again because `B` is commutative,

`K_1(B)=B^x direct_sum SK_1(B)`.

Since `C` is a UFD and `B` is obtained by inverting `alpha`, every unit of `B` has a unique form

`c p_1^{n_1}...p_t^{n_t}`

with `c in C^x` and `n_i in Z`. Thus

`B^x/C^x ~= Z^t`.

Quotienting (6.1) by `C^x` gives an isomorphism

`(B^x/C^x) direct_sum SK_1(B) ~= G_0(D)`.   (6.2)

### Step 5: identify the divisor-unit summand

For each `p_i`, naturality of localization through

`C -> C[1/p_i] -> B`

and Weibel's boundary formula give

`partial(p_i)=[C/p_i] in G_0(D)`.

Equivalently, this follows by regarding `C/p_i` as an `alpha`-power-torsion module and using devissage. Hence the `Z^t=B^x/C^x` summand in (6.2) is exactly the subgroup generated by the prime-divisor classes `[C/p_i]`.

Taking the quotient leaves

`SK_1(B) ~= G_0(D)/<[C/p_i]> = Q_alpha`.

QED.

## Exact interpretation

The second localization does not hide its stable obstruction in an abstract localization sequence. It is exactly the part of the one-dimensional divisor `G_0(C/(alpha))` not already accounted for by inverting the irreducible components of the divisor.

Nilpotent thickness, singularities, component gluing, and Picard/class-group phenomena are allowed to contribute to `Q_alpha`. The formula does not assume `alpha` reduced or irreducible.

# 7. `P02-C149-N1-SUPPORT-G0-HERMITIAN-QUOTIENT` — PROVED

## Statement

Continue the C146 setting and define `Q_alpha` as in C148. There is a natural surjection of pointed sets

`Q_alpha ~= SK_1(B) ->> W_E(B)`.

If `dim B=2`, Vaserstein's theorem gives a bijection

`Um_3(B)/E_3(B) ~= W_E(B)`,

and hence a surjection

`Q_alpha ->> Um_3(B)/E_3(B)`.

If `dim B<=1`, ordinary stable-range transitivity already makes `Um_3(B)/E_3(B)` trivial.

Thus `Q_alpha=0` is a sufficient exact certificate for residual `E_3`-transitivity and therefore, by inherited C55, for actual rank-three freeness in the C50 row.

## Proof

Let a class of `W_E(B)` be represented by a nondegenerate alternating matrix `M` of size `2n` and Pfaffian one.

By C147 every finite projective `B`-module is free. The same symplectic Gram–Schmidt argument used in inherited C142 splits a hyperbolic plane, then repeats on the free orthogonal complement. Therefore there exists

`H in GL_{2n}(B)`

such that

`H^t M H = psi_n`,

where `psi_n` is the standard hyperbolic symplectic form.

Pfaffians satisfy

`pf(H^t M H)=det(H) pf(M)`.

Both Pfaffians are one, so `det(H)=1`. Thus `G=H^{-1}` lies in `SL_{2n}(B)` and

`M=G^t psi_n G`.

Therefore every `W_E(B)` class is obtained from a stable special-linear class.

If the stable class of `G` is zero in `SK_1(B)`, then after adding identity blocks it becomes elementary. Elementary congruence is exactly part of the defining equivalence in `W_E`, so the resulting alternating form represents the neutral class. More generally, replacing `G` by a stably elementary-equivalent representative does not change the `W_E` class. Hence the construction factors through a surjective pointed-set map

`SK_1(B) ->> W_E(B)`.

C148 identifies the source with `Q_alpha`.

For `dim B=2`, the dimension-two Vaserstein theorem identifies

`Um_3(B)/E_3(B) -> W_E(B)`

bijectively. The theorem is stated for a commutative ring of Krull dimension two without a displayed characteristic or `2`-invertibility hypothesis in the checked source (R. A. Rao, *The Elementary Symplectic Witt Groups*, theorem on the Vaserstein symbol, page 4 of the PDF). Thus the claimed surjection follows.

For `dim B<=1`, the ordinary Bass stable-range bound gives elementary transitivity on length-three unimodular rows, so no Vaserstein input is required.

QED.

## Why this is sharper than “use Hermitian K-theory”

The surviving rank-three class cannot come from an arbitrary higher Grothendieck–Witt group. In the N=1 branch it is fed by the concrete quotient `Q_alpha`, and only its image in the finite-rank elementary symplectic Witt group can obstruct the residual `E_3` orbit.

This is also characteristic-safe at the level actually used: no identification with a higher Grothendieck–Witt group in characteristic two is asserted.

# 8. `P02-C150-N1-TRANSVERSE-PARAMETER-RANK3-CLOSURE` — PROVED

## Statement

Assume the inherited H4/C50 N=1 branch:

- `R` regular local, `dim R=4`;
- `r,u in m\m^2` with independent tangent classes;

- `f=1+u alpha/r=(r+u alpha)/r`;
- `alpha in m` is nonunit;
- `S=R/(r+u alpha)`.

Inherited C145 says `S` is regular local of dimension three and the image of `u` is a regular parameter.

Add the explicit hypothesis:

- the image `bar alpha` lies in `m_S\m_S^2`;
- `[bar u]` and `[bar alpha]` are linearly independent in `m_S/m_S^2`.

Then

`ker(v) ~= A^3`.

## Proof

Put

`C=S[1/bar u]`,  `B=C[1/bar alpha]=S[1/r]`.

By inherited C143, every projective over `C` is free and `SK_1(C)=0`.

Since `bar alpha` is a regular parameter independent of `bar u`,

`T=S/(bar alpha)`

is a regular local ring of dimension two and the image of `bar u` remains a regular parameter. Therefore

`D=C/(bar alpha) ~= T[1/bar u]`.

The regular local ring `T` is a UFD. Its localization `D` is again a UFD. Moreover `D` has dimension one: no height-two prime of `T` avoids the parameter `bar u`, while a height-one prime generated by the other parameter does avoid it. A one-dimensional noetherian UFD is a PID. Consequently every finite projective `D`-module is free and, since `D` is regular,

`G_0(D)=K_0(D)=Z[D]`.

Because `bar alpha` is prime in the UFD `C`, C148 has `t=1` and says

`SK_1(B) ~= G_0(D)/<[D]> = 0`.

C147 says every finite projective `B`-module is free. Applying inherited C142 gives

`W_E(B)=0`.

If `dim B=2`, Vaserstein gives

`Um_3(B)/E_3(B)=0`.

If `dim B<=1`, the same conclusion follows from stable range.

Now reduce the C50 row modulo `f`. Since `r=-u alpha` and `r` is inverted in `B`, both `u` and `alpha` are units in `B`. The normalized residual tail `(bar b,bar c,bar d)` is a unimodular length-three row. The preceding paragraph makes it elementary-equivalent to `e_1`.

Inherited C55 lifts that finite elementary sequence coefficientwise from `B=A/fA` to `A` and performs the final source operations on `v`. It follows that

`ker(v) ~= A^3`.

QED.

# 9. `P02-D44-SECOND-LOCALIZATION-SK1-PERSISTENCE-NOGO` — DISPROVED as a method claim

## Overstrong claim being killed

> If a regular two-dimensional ring `C` is projective-free and has `SK_1(C)=0`, then every second principal localization `C[1/a]` also has `SK_1=0`.

This is false.

## Counterexample

Let

`C_ex = Complex[x,y]`,

and

`a = y^2 - x^3 + x`.

Quillen–Suslin makes every finite projective `C_ex`-module free. Homotopy invariance of `K_1` over the field gives

`K_1(C_ex)=Complex^x`,

so `SK_1(C_ex)=0`.

The affine plane curve

`U=Spec C_ex/(a)`

is smooth: the partial derivatives are

`a_x=-3x^2+1`,  `a_y=2y`.

A common zero of the derivatives has `y=0`, `x^2=1/3`, but then

`a=x(1-x^2)=2x/3 != 0`.

The projective closure

`E: Y^2 Z = X^3 - X Z^2`

is also smooth; at the unique point at infinity `[0:1:0]`, the `Z`-partial derivative is nonzero. A smooth plane cubic has genus one, so `E` is an elliptic curve and `U=E\{O}` for the point at infinity `O`.

Stacks Project, Section 53.17, gives for a smooth projective genus-one curve over `Complex`

`Pic(E)[2] ~= (Z/2)^2`.

Choose a nontrivial two-torsion line bundle `L` on `E`. Its degree is zero. If `L|_U` were trivial, a rational trivializing section would have divisor supported only at `O`. The divisor of a rational section of a degree-zero line bundle has degree zero, so the coefficient at the sole support point `O` would have to be zero. The section would then trivialize `L` globally, contradiction. Hence

`Pic(U) != 0`.

Now apply the C148 localization calculation to `C_ex` and the irreducible prime `a`. The divisor ring `C_ex/(a)` is regular of dimension one, hence a Dedekind domain, and the standard rank/determinant classification gives

`G_0(U) ~= Z direct_sum Pic(U)`.

The boundary class `[C_ex/(a)]` is the `Z` summand. Therefore

`SK_1(C_ex[1/a]) ~= G_0(U)/Z[U] ~= Pic(U) != 0`.

So a second principal localization can create stable special-linear obstruction even when the source surface is regular, projective-free, and has `SK_1=0`.

## Interpretation

This does **not** disprove Quillen's localization question. The example is not the special `C=S[1/u]` punctured-local geometry of C146, and C147 still says the target localization is projective-free.

What D44 kills is the shortcut

`SK_1(C)=0 => SK_1(C[1/alpha])=0`

without computing the divisor localization term. C148 is therefore necessary, not ornamental.
