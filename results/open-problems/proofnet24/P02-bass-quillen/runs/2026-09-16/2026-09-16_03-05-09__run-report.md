## 3.1 Rank one means rank one

Let `P` be a constant-rank-one projective over a commutative ring `A` and suppose

\[
P\oplus A^m\simeq A^{m+1}.

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)\simeq P,
\qquad
\det(A^{m+1})\simeq A,

# 4. `P02-C132-HB-DETERMINANT-POLE-CLASSIFICATION` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `R` be a commutative ring, let `F,r in R`, and let

\[
K=(\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3),\qquad Q=R/K.
\]

Assume multiplication by `F` is injective on `Q`.

Suppose for some `m>=0`

\[
r^m\in K+FR.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then there is a **unique** class

\[
\eta_m\in Q
\]

such that

\[
F\eta_m=r^m\quad\text{in }Q.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every relation

\[
r^m=\Delta_1c_1+\Delta_2c_2+\Delta_3c_3+Fy
\tag{4.3}
\]

has `y mod K = eta_m`, and conversely every representative of `eta_m` occurs in such a relation after changing the `c_i`.

Assume now that the `Delta_i` are the signed `2 x 2` minors of a `3 x 2` matrix with columns `a,b`, so

\[
\det[a\ b\ c]=\Delta\cdot c.
\]

Over `A=R[1/r]`, relation (4.3) gives a residual determinant-one completion

\[
M_m=[a\ b\ c/r^m],
\qquad
\det M_m=1-\frac{Fy}{r^m}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Fix `0<=k<=m`. The following are equivalent:

1. by changing the relation (4.3), the determinant error in (4.4) can be represented with `r`-pole at most `k`;
2. `eta_m in r^{m-k}Q`;
3.

\[
\boxed{
r^k\in FQ+(0:_Q r^{m-k}).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

If `r` is also a nonzerodivisor on `Q`, these conditions reduce to

\[
\boxed{r^k\in FQ.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
\nu=\min\{j\ge0:r^j\in FQ\}<\infty,
\tag{4.7}
\]

then for every sufficiently large common denominator exponent `m`, the minimum determinant-error pole attainable while the Hilbert–Burch frame `(a,b)` is frozen is **exactly `nu`**. Equivalently, `nu` is the nilpotence index of the class of `r` in `Q/FQ`.

## Proof

Existence of `eta_m` follows from (4.1). If `Fy=Fy'` in `Q`, then

\[
F(y-y')=0
\]

in `Q`; `F`-regularity gives `y=y'` in `Q`. Hence `eta_m` is unique.

If two relations (4.3) have coefficients `y,y'`, subtraction gives

\[
F(y-y')\in K,
\]

so again `y-y' in K`. Conversely, if `y'=y+k` with `k in K`, write `k=Delta dot w`; replacing `c` by `c-Fw` changes (4.3) to a relation with coefficient `y'`. Thus the possible `y` are exactly the representatives of `eta_m`.

The determinant formula (4.4) is immediate:

\[
\det[a\ b\ c/r^m]
=
\frac{\Delta\cdot c}{r^m}
=
1-\frac{Fy}{r^m}.
\]

The error has pole at most `k` exactly when a representative `y` may be chosen in `r^{m-k}R`. By the preceding paragraph this is equivalent to

\[
\eta_m\in r^{m-k}Q.
\]

If `eta_m=r^{m-k}z`, then (4.2) gives

\[
r^{m-k}(Fz-r^k)=0,
\]

so

\[
r^k\in FQ+(0:_Q r^{m-k}).
\]

Conversely, if

\[
r^k=Fz+a,\qquad a\in(0:_Q r^{m-k}),
\]

then

\[
r^m=F r^{m-k}z.
\]

By uniqueness in (4.2),

\[
\eta_m=r^{m-k}z.
\]

This proves (4.5). If `r` is `Q`-regular, the annihilator is zero, proving (4.6).

Finally, if `r` is `Q`-regular and `nu` is as in (4.7), then `r^m in FQ` for every `m>=nu`, and (4.6) says pole at most `k` is possible exactly for `k>=nu`. Hence the minimum pole is `nu`. Modulo `F`, the same `nu` is by definition the least exponent killing the class of `r`. QED.

## Interpretation

This theorem identifies the exact content of the determinant-pole strategy **before any change of Hilbert–Burch frame or source row**. It is not a Quillen obstruction invariant. It is a rigid invariant of the frozen tail quotient `Q=R/K` plus the chosen regular element `F`.

It also explains the relation with the earlier saturation/Bockstein work: the annihilator term

\[
(0:_Q r^{m-k})
\]

is precisely where vertical `r`-torsion can lower the apparent determinant pole. When `r` is regular on `Q`, that escape disappears completely.

# 5. `P02-C133-UNIMODULAR-COMPLETION-VECTOR-CLOSURE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `A` be any commutative ring and let

\[
q=(-F,\Delta_1,\Delta_2,\Delta_3)\in Um_4(A).
\]

Put

\[
B=A/FA,\qquad \bar\Delta=(\bar\Delta_1,\bar\Delta_2,\bar\Delta_3).
\]

Suppose there is a residual right inverse

\[
\bar z\in B^3,
\qquad
\bar\Delta\cdot\bar z=1,
\tag{5.1}
\]

that lifts to a **unimodular vector**

\[
z\in Um_3(A).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\ker(q)\simeq A^3.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

A full `GL_3(A)` or `SL_3(A)` lift of a residual completion matrix is not required.

## Proof

Choose any lift `z` satisfying (5.2). From (5.1),

\[
\Delta\cdot z=1+Fh
\]

for some `h in A`.

Because `z` is unimodular, choose `t=(t_1,t_2,t_3)` with

\[
t\cdot z=-h.
\]

Set

\[
\Delta' = \Delta+Ft.
\]

Then

\[
\Delta'\cdot z=1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The row

\[
q'=(-F,\Delta'_1,\Delta'_2,\Delta'_3)
\]

is obtained from `q` by elementary source operations: adding `-t_i` times the first coordinate `-F` to the corresponding tail coordinate replaces `Delta_i` by `Delta_i+Ft_i`. Hence `ker(q') ~= ker(q)`.

By (5.4), the tail row `Delta'` is unimodular. Let

\[
K_{\Delta'}=\ker(\Delta':A^3\to A).
\]

The split exact sequence

\[
0\to K_{\Delta'}\to A^3\xrightarrow{\Delta'}A\to0
\]

gives

\[
K_{\Delta'}\oplus A\simeq A^3.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Using the right inverse `z`, define

\[
A\oplus K_{\Delta'}\longrightarrow\ker(q'),
\qquad
(a,w)\longmapsto(a,Fa z+w).
\]

Because `Delta' dot z=1`, this map lands in the kernel, and its inverse is

\[
(a,v)\longmapsto(a,v-Faz).
\]

Therefore

\[
\ker(q')\simeq A\oplus K_{\Delta'}\simeq A^3
\]

by (5.5). QED.

## Why this is stronger than inherited C94

C94 closed the row when a whole residual `3 x 3` completion matrix lifted invertibly to `A`. C133 needs only one **completion vector** to lift unimodularly. This is a strictly weaker lifting obligation and moves the live problem directly into unimodular-row geometry.

No `K_0` cancellation or local-ring freeness is used.

# 7. `P02-D40-MIXED-CHAR-TWISTED-CUBIC-POLE-NOGO` — DISPROVED AS METHOD CLAIM

The next example lies inside the declared four-dimensional mixed-characteristic C92 geometry and shows that the fixed-frame pole-lowering program can fail **at every common denominator** even when the original rank-three row is elementary-trivial.

## Construction

Fix a prime `p` and `N>=2`. Let

\[
R=\mathbf Z_{(p)}[u,x,z]_{(p,u,x,z)},
\qquad
r=p,
\qquad
F=p^N+u.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then `R` is a four-dimensional unramified regular local ring of mixed characteristic `(0,p)`, `[p]` and `[u]` are linearly independent in `m/m^2`, and `F` is a regular parameter. Replacing the coordinate `u` by `F-p^N` identifies

\[
R\simeq\mathbf Z_{(p)}[F,x,z]_{(p,F,x,z)}.
\]

Take

\[
\Phi=
\begin{pmatrix}
p&F\\
x&p\\
z&x
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Its signed minors generate

\[
K=(x^2-pz,\ px-Fz,\ p^2-Fx).
\tag{7.3}
\]

A direct symbolic determinant check gives exactly these three minors.

## `K` is a height-two perfect prime

The ideal is the standard twisted-cubic determinantal ideal in the coordinates `(F,p,x,z)`, with `p` now the mixed-characteristic parameter.

For completeness, the needed facts can be checked internally. Localizing at `F`, the last two relations solve

\[
x=p^2/F,
\qquad
z=p^3/F^2,
\]

and the first relation follows. Hence `(R/K)_F` is a domain and `K_F` has height two. The `2 x 2` minors of a `3 x 2` matrix have height at most two, so `ht K=2`; Hilbert–Burch gives that `R/K` is Cohen–Macaulay and unmixed.

If a height-two minimal prime over `K` contained `F`, then `p^2-Fx` would force `p` into that prime, and `x^2-pz` would then force `x` into it. It would contain `(F,p,x)`, a height-three ideal, contradiction. Thus every minimal prime avoids `F`; after localizing at `F` there is only the one prime `K_F`, so `K` has a unique minimal prime. The quotient is generically reduced because its `F`-localization is a domain. An unmixed Cohen–Macaulay ring with a unique generically reduced minimal prime has zero nilradical. Hence `K` is prime.

Therefore

\[
Q=R/K
\]

is a domain, and both `F` and `p` are nonzerodivisors on `Q`.

## Fixed-frame minimum pole is exactly two

Modulo `F`, relation `p^2-Fx` gives

\[
p^2=0\quad\text{in }Q/FQ.
\]

But `p` is not zero in `Q/FQ`: after setting `F=0`, the remaining defining relations are quadratic in `(p,x,z)`, so the linear class of `p` survives. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\min\{j:p^j\in FQ\}=2.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

C132 now implies that, while the Hilbert–Burch frame (7.2) is frozen, **no choice of common denominator and completion relation can reduce the determinant-error pole below two**.

Indeed the visible relation is

\[
p^2=(p^2-Fx)+Fx,
\]

which produces the residual completion column

\[
z=(0,0,p^{-2})
\]

and determinant

\[
1-\frac{Fx}{p^2}.
\]

Multiplying the relation by higher powers of `p` does not improve the reduced pole; C132 proves that no alternative relation does either.

## But the original row is free immediately

Consider

\[
q=(-F,\ x^2-pz,\ px-Fz,\ p^2-Fx)
\in Um_4(A),
\qquad A=R[1/p].
\]

Perform the allowed elementary source operation on the fourth coordinate:

\[
(p^2-Fx)-x(-F)=p^2.
\]

Since `p^2` is a unit in `A`, the transformed row has a unit coordinate and its rank-three kernel is free.

Equivalently, the residual completion vector `(0,0,p^{-2})` is itself unimodular over `A`, so C133 closes the row directly.

Therefore the method claim

> “varying the common-denominator completion inside a fixed Hilbert–Burch frame must eventually lower the determinant pole to one or zero”

is false, even in a four-dimensional mixed-characteristic C92 example whose original rank-three projective is free.

This is **not** a counterexample to Quillen. It kills only the frozen-frame pole-lowering strategy.
