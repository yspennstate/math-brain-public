# 4. Mandatory imported-claim rechecks

## 4.1 Rank one means rank one

Let `P` be rank one and suppose

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`.

Taking top exterior powers gives

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P`,

while `det(A^(m+1)) ~= A`. Hence `P ~= A`.

This proves only the displayed rank-one cancellation. It does not cancel a free summand from rank 2 or rank 3, does not establish elementary transitivity, and does not turn `K_0` information into fixed-rank freeness.

## 4.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

For

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`, `A=R[1/x]`,

we have

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus both ideals are maximal. They are distinct because equality would put

`x = y-(y-x)`

in a proper ideal although `x` is a unit of `A`.

No local-ring freeness theorem is applied to `R[1/r]` without a separate locality proof.

# 5. `P02-C129-REGULAR-DETERMINANT-DIVISOR-G0-CLOSURE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `(R,m)` be a regular local domain, let `r in R` be nonzero, and put

`A=R[1/r]`.

Let

`M_0 in M_n(A)`, `D=det(M_0) != 0`, `T=coker(M_0)`.

Assume that for some `epsilon in A^x` and some regular parameter

`d in m\m^2`

we have

`D = epsilon d` in `A`.

Put

`H=A/(D) ~= A/(d)`.

Then either `d` becomes a unit in `A`, in which case `M_0` is already invertible, or else

`boxed(T ~= H)`

as `H`-modules. In particular

`boxed([T]=[H] in G_0(H))`.

Therefore every C127 fixed-lift obstruction vanishes whenever its determinant divisor is, up to an `A`-unit, the localization of a regular parameter of `R`.

## Proof

Because `R` is regular local, it is a domain. Hence `A` is a domain. Since `D!=0`, the adjugate identity

`adj(M_0) M_0 = D I_n`

shows that `M_0:A^n -> A^n` is injective. Thus

`0 -> A^n -> A^n -> T -> 0`                                           (5.1)

is exact, and `D T=0`.

If `d` becomes a unit after inverting `r`, then `D` is a unit and `T=0`; this is the direct-lift case. Assume henceforth that `d` is not inverted.

Since `d in m\m^2`, the standard regular-local parameter theorem gives

`R/(d)` regular local of dimension `dim R-1`.

Therefore

`H ~= (R/(d))[1/r]`

is a regular Noetherian ring.

Fix `q in Spec(H)` and let `p` be its inverse image in `Spec(A)`. Localizing (5.1) gives

`0 -> A_p^n -> A_p^n -> T_q -> 0`.                                    (5.2)

The local ring `A_p` is regular. Since `T_q` is a nonzero torsion module killed by the nonzerodivisor `D`, it cannot be free over the domain `A_p`; hence its projective dimension over `A_p` is exactly one. Auslander–Buchsbaum gives

`depth_{A_p}(T_q) = dim(A_p)-1 = dim(H_q)`.

Because `D` annihilates `T_q`, depth computed over `A_p` is the same as depth over `H_q=A_p/(D)`. Thus `T_q` is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over the regular local ring `H_q`. Every maximal Cohen–Macaulay module over a regular local ring is free, so `T` is finite locally free over `H`.

It remains to determine its rank. Since `d` is a prime element of the regular local UFD `R`, and we are in the non-inverted case, `H` is a domain. At its generic point, the corresponding localization of `A` is the DVR `A_(d)`. The determinant `D=epsilon d` has valuation exactly one. For a square matrix over a DVR, the length of its torsion cokernel equals the valuation of its determinant, so

`length_{A_(d)} T_(d) = 1`.

Because `T` is already locally free over `H`, this says its generic `H`-rank is one. Hence `T` is an invertible `H`-module.

Finally, Stacks Project Tag `0AFZ` states that for any regular local ring `S` and any `f in S`,

`Pic(S[1/f])=0`.

Apply this to `S=R/(d)` and `f=r`. Thus `Pic(H)=0`, so the invertible module `T` is trivial:

`T ~= H`.

This proves the claim. ∎

## Source check

The standard commutative-algebra inputs were rechecked against the current Stacks Project:

- Tag `00NQ`: quotient of a regular local ring by a member of a regular system of parameters is regular local;
- Tag `00NT`: maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over regular local rings are free;
- Tag `0AFZ`: `Pic(S_f)=0` for regular local `S`.

No locality of `A` or `H` is assumed; the proof localizes at primes when it uses local regularity.

# 6. `P02-C130-C92-R-POLE-PARAMETER-TEST` — PROVED

## Statement

Work in the C92 branch

`F=r^N+u alpha`,

with `[r],[u]` linearly independent in `m/m^2` and `alpha in R^x`.

For a fixed C117 lift `M_0`, write

`det(M_0)=1+Fh`.

Write `h` in **r-reduced form**

`h=a/r^k`, `a in R`, `k>=0`,

where `k` is minimal (equivalently, if `k>0` then `a notin rR`). Put

`d_k = r^k + F a`,

so

`det(M_0)=d_k/r^k`.

Then:

1. If `k=0`, `d_0=1+Fa` is a unit of `R`; hence `det(M_0)` is a unit of `A` and the residual completion lifts directly to `SL_3(A)` after determinant normalization.

2. If `k=1`, then

   `boxed(d_1 in m\m^2)`

   for **every** `a in R`.

3. If `k>=2`, then

   `boxed(d_k in m\m^2  <=>  a in R^x)`.

Consequently C129 kills the C127 `G_0` gate whenever `k=1`, and also for every deeper pole whose reduced numerator is a unit.

## Proof

Inherited C92 already proves

`F in m\m^2`.

More explicitly, in the cotangent space `m/m^2`:

- if `N>=2`, `[F]=bar(alpha)[u]`;
- if `N=1`, `[F]=[r]+bar(alpha)[u]`.

### Case `k=0`

Here

`d_0=1+Fa`.

Since `F in m`, we have `Fa in m`, hence `d_0 in 1+m subset R^x`.

### Case `k=1`

Here

`d_1=r+Fa`.

If `a in m`, then `Fa in m^2`, so

`[d_1]=[r] != 0`.

If `a` is a unit, write `bar a !=0` in the residue field. For `N>=2`,

`[d_1]=[r]+bar a bar(alpha)[u]`,

which is nonzero because `[r],[u]` are independent. For `N=1`,

`[d_1]=(1+bar a)[r]+bar a bar(alpha)[u]`,

and the coefficient of `[u]` is nonzero. Hence again `[d_1]!=0`.

Thus `d_1 in m\m^2` for every `a`.

### Case `k>=2`

Now `r^k in m^2`.

If `a in m`, then `Fa in m^2`, so `d_k in m^2`.

If `a` is a unit, then modulo `m^2`

`[d_k]=bar a [F] != 0`,

because C92 gives `[F]!=0`. Hence `d_k in m\m^2`.

This proves all three assertions. ∎

## Interpretation

The first genuinely difficult fixed-lift determinant errors are therefore not arbitrary. In r-reduced form they begin at

`k>=2` with `a in m\rR`.

This is a sharper target than merely asking whether `det(M_0)` is a unit.

# 8. `P02-D39-R-PRIMITIVE-NOT-PARAMETER-NOT-OBSTRUCTION` — method claim disproved

A natural overstatement after C130 would be:

> if the reduced numerator is merely not divisible by `r`, then the determinant numerator should be a parameter; and if it is not a parameter, the `G_0` gate should be nonzero.

Both implications fail.

Take the complete mixed-characteristic four-dimensional regular local ring

`R = Z_p[[u,x,y]]`, `r=p`,

and choose `N>=2`, `alpha=1`,

`F=p^N+u`.

This is inside the C92 tangent geometry: `[p]` and `[u]` are independent.

Let

`h=x/p^2`,

so the reduced numerator `x` is not divisible by `p` but is not a unit. Then

`delta = 1+Fh = (p^2+Fx)/p^2`.

Put

`d=p^2+Fx = p^2+p^N x+ux`.

Every term lies in `m^2`, so

`d in m^2`.

Thus mere `p`-primitivity of the numerator does **not** make the determinant numerator a regular parameter.

Now take the explicit lift

`M_0 = diag(delta,1,1)`.

Modulo `F`, `delta` becomes `1`, so the residual matrix is the identity. Moreover

`T=coker(M_0) ~= A/(delta)=H`.

Hence

`[T]-[H]=0 in G_0(H)`

exactly, despite the failure of the regular-parameter certificate.

Therefore:

- C129/C130 are sufficient closure certificates, not necessary conditions;
- a failed parameter-numerator test is not a Quillen obstruction;
- deep pole order plus nonunit leading numerator is a **selection problem**, not a disproof.

This is a method falsification only.

# 10. Route comparison after C129–C131

## Elementary / prestabilization

This is still the leading route. C124 gives one stabilization; C131 gives stable-relative vanishing for pole order one; the missing theorem is size-three destabilization, not another stable computation.

## Mennicke / Vaserstein symbols

If `sr(FC)<=3` cannot be proved, the next legitimate move is to extract the surviving finite-size class modulo changes of lift and local trivialization. D35, D36, D38, and D39 prohibit treating one failed presentation as an invariant symbol.

## Euler classes

The new C129 proof shows that on a regular determinant divisor the fixed-lift cokernel is only a line bundle, and the line-bundle class vanishes. Thus any Euler-class obstruction worth pursuing must come from the genuinely singular/deeper-pole divisor branch or from rank two; there is no Euler content left in the regular-divisor fixed-lift case.

## Hermitian K-theory

The characteristic-two bridge remains conditional. No odd-characteristic Pfaffian/Vaserstein theorem is imported into arbitrary mixed characteristic. The new result does not use `2 in A^x` and is therefore useful before any Hermitian split by residue characteristic.

## Exact localization / descent

C129 is compatible with exact localization: its certificate is the finite equation

`det(M_0) = epsilon d`, `d in m\m^2`.

C130 makes this still more explicit in the C92 branch by reducing it to the pole order and leading numerator of `h`. This is a finite condition that can in principle be tracked through approximation/descent. No claim is made that arbitrary Popescu descent preserves the required unstable factorization.

## Low-rank reduction

The fixed-lift defect on a regular determinant divisor is rank one and hence disappears by Picard vanishing. The problem then returns exactly to the rank-three finite-size factorization gate. Rank two remains a separate harder cancellation obligation and is not inferred from any of the rank-three statements.
