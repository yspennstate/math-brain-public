# 3. Required elementary audits, rederived

## 3.1 Rank-one stable cancellation proves only rank one

Let `A` be commutative and `P` finite projective of constant rank one. If

\[
P\oplus A^m\cong A^{m+1},
\]

then

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)
\cong
P\otimes\det(A^m)
\cong P
\]

while

\[
\det(A^{m+1})\cong A.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{P\cong A.}
\]

This proves exactly the rank-one cancellation statement. It does not imply cancellation of stably free modules of ranks two or three.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not remain local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},
\qquad
A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)
\cong
k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]
\cong k(x),
\]

and substituting `y=x` gives

\[
A/(y-x)\cong k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`. They are distinct, because if they were equal their difference `x` would lie in a proper ideal, while `x` is a unit in `A`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{A\text{ need not be local}.}
\]

No local-ring freeness theorem is applied to `R[1/r]` merely because `R` was local.

# 6. `P02-C177-ALLN-C107-DESATURATION` — PROVED

## Statement

Assume the inherited C50 normal form

\[
v=(f,ub_1,ub_2,ub_3)\in Um_4(A),
\qquad
f=1+\frac{u\alpha}{r^N},
\]

with `R` a regular local domain and the inherited tangent hypotheses on `r,u`.

If `alpha=0`, then `f=1` and the kernel is free.

If `alpha\neq0`, then

\[
\boxed{
\ker_A(f,ub_1,ub_2,ub_3)
\cong
\ker_A(f,b_1,b_2,b_3).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This holds for every `N>=1`; no unit assumption on `alpha` is needed.

## Proof

First, `F=r^N+u alpha` is nonzero. If `F=0`, reduce modulo `(u)`. Since `u` is part of a regular system of parameters in the inherited H4 setup, `R/(u)` is a domain and the image of `r` is nonzero. We would obtain

\[
\bar r^N=0
\]

in the domain `R/(u)`, a contradiction.

Thus `f=F/r^N` is a nonzero element of the domain `A`, hence a nonzerodivisor.

Now pass to

\[
B=A/(f).
\]

The relation `f=0` gives

\[
1+\frac{u\alpha}{r^N}=0,
\]

so in `B`

\[
u\left(-\frac{\alpha}{r^N}\right)=1.
\]

Therefore the image of `u` in `B` is a **unit**, and in particular a nonzerodivisor.

Apply inherited C107 over the ring `A`, with source element `F_0=f`, common factor `a=u`, and `g_i=-b_i`. It gives

\[
\ker_A(f,ub_1,ub_2,ub_3)
\cong
\ker_A(-f,-b_1,-b_2,-b_3).
\]

Multiplying the target row by `-1` does not change its kernel, so the right side is

\[
\ker_A(f,b_1,b_2,b_3).
\]

This proves (6.1). `□`

## Consequence

The common tail factor `u` is **not** an invariant of the actual kernel module in the inherited normal form, even in the `N>=2` singular residual branch.

This does not say that the bare tail `(b_1,b_2,b_3)` is a unimodular row over `A`; it is only unimodular modulo `(f)`. Section 11 gives a counterexample to that overstrong inference.

# 7. `P02-C178-ALLN-INTEGRAL-CORE` — PROVED

## Statement

Under the hypotheses of C177, every rank-three kernel in the inherited normal form is isomorphic to the localization of the kernel of an `r`-primitive integral row

\[
q=(q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3)\in R^4
\]

such that, for

\[
J=(q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3)\subset R,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
JA=A,\qquad
r^L\in J\text{ for some }L,\qquad
J\not\subset(r).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

If `J` is proper, then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}J\ge2.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

## Proof

By C177, it suffices to study

\[
v'=(f,b_1,b_2,b_3).
\]

Multiply the first coordinate by the unit `r^N` in `A`:

\[
(f,b_1,b_2,b_3)
\sim
(F,b_1,b_2,b_3)
\]

at the level of kernels.

Choose integers `m_i>=0` such that

\[
c_i=r^{m_i}b_i\in R.
\]

Right multiplication of the free source `A^4` by the diagonal automorphism

\[
\operatorname{diag}(1,r^{m_1},r^{m_2},r^{m_3})
\]

changes the row to

\[
q^{(0)}=(F,c_1,c_2,c_3)\in R^4
\]

and gives an isomorphic kernel over `A`.

Because `(r)` is a prime principal ideal in the regular local domain `R`, there is a largest integer `k>=0` such that every coordinate of `q^{(0)}` lies in `r^kR`. Put

\[
q=r^{-k}q^{(0)}\in R^4.
\]

The scalar `r^k` is a unit in `A`, so `q_A` has the same kernel and is still unimodular. By maximality of `k`,

\[
J=(q)\not\subset(r).
\]

Since `JA=A`, write in `A`

\[
1=\sum_i q_i a_i
\]

with each `a_i` having a finite power of `r` in its denominator. Clearing a common denominator gives

\[
r^L\in J
\]

for some `L`.

Suppose `J` is proper and `ht J=1`. Let `p` be a height-one prime minimal over `J`. Since `r^L in J`, one has `r in p`. But `(r)` itself is a height-one prime, because `r` is a regular parameter. Hence

\[
p=(r).
\]

Then `J subset (r)`, contradicting `r`-primitivity. Therefore `ht J>=2`. `□`

## Interpretation

The difficult `N>=2` branch admits a global integral representative over the original regular local ring `R`, without passing first to the affine modification `T_N`.

The price is not singularity of a residual hypersurface; it is a **boundary extension problem** for the syzygy module of the integral row.

# 8. `P02-C179-ALLN-C109-DEPTH-GATE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `q` and `J` be the integral core from C178. If

\[
\operatorname{depth}_R(R/J)\ge2,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\ker_A(v)\cong A^3.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

For a resistant proper `J`, one must therefore have either

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ht}J\ge3}
\tag{8.2}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ht}J=2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{depth}(R/J)\le1.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

## Proof

Inherited C109 applies to any integral row `q in R^4` whose localization `q_A` is unimodular.

It proves that if

\[
\operatorname{depth}(R/J)\ge2,
\]

then the kernel

\[
K=\ker(q:R^4\to J)
\]

has depth four. Since `R` is regular local of dimension four, Auslander–Buchsbaum gives `pd_R K=0`. Hence `K` is free of rank three and

\[
K[1/r]\cong A^3.
\]

By C178, `K[1/r]` is the original kernel.

C178 already gives `ht J>=2` for a proper resistant core. In dimension four, if `ht J=2` and `R/J` is Cohen–Macaulay, then `depth(R/J)=2` and the branch closes. Therefore a proper resistant height-two ideal must have a genuine depth defect. `□`

## Why this is useful

C179 removes every all-`N` rank-three case whose integral core has an ordinary Cohen–Macaulay height-two quotient. No Vaserstein, Hermitian, or stable-range theorem is needed there.

The difficult branch is now algebraically narrower:

- codimension at least three; or
- codimension two with embedded/depth-defect geometry.

# 9. `P02-C180-ALLN-ORIENTED-REFLEXIVE-LATTICE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let

\[
K=\ker(q:R^4\to J)
\]

for the `r`-primitive integral core of C178.

Then:

1. `K` is finite, torsion free, rank three;
2. `K` satisfies Serre `(S_2)` and hence is reflexive;
3. `K_p` is free for every prime `p` with `ht p<=2`;
4. the nonfree locus satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{
   \operatorname{NF}(K)\subseteq V(J)\subseteq V(r);
   }
   \tag{9.1}
   \]
5. consequently
   \[
   \boxed{\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{NF}(K)\ge3;}
   \tag{9.2}
   \]
6. `det K` is trivial, so `K` is oriented;
7. localizing gives
   \[
   \boxed{
   K[1/r]\cong\ker_A(v).
   }
   \tag{9.3}
   \]

## Proof

The exact sequence is

\[
0\to K\to R^4\to J\to0.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Since `R` is a domain and `K` is a submodule of a free module, `K` is torsion free. Its generic rank is `4-1=3`.

Let `p` be a prime.

If `p` does not contain `J`, then

\[
J_p=R_p,
\]

so (9.4) splits and

\[
K_p\cong R_p^3.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Now suppose `p` contains `J`. By C178, `ht J>=2`, so `ht p>=2`. The localized ideal `J_p` is a nonzero ideal of the regular local domain `R_p`. Hence `J_p` is torsion free and has depth at least one.

Applying the depth lemma to

\[
0\to K_p\to R_p^4\to J_p\to0
\]

gives

\[
\operatorname{depth}K_p
\ge
\min\{\operatorname{depth}R_p,\operatorname{depth}J_p+1\}
\ge
\min\{\operatorname{ht}p,2\}.
\tag{9.6}
\]

Thus `K` satisfies `(S_2)`.

The ring `R` is regular, hence a Noetherian normal domain. By Stacks Lemma 15.24.18 (`0AVB`), finite torsion-free plus `(S_2)` is equivalent to reflexivity. Therefore `K` is reflexive.

If `ht p<=2`, then either `p` does not contain `J`, and (9.5) applies, or `ht p=2` and `p` contains `J`. In the latter case (9.6) gives

\[
\operatorname{depth}K_p=2=\dim R_p.
\]

So `K_p` is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over the regular local ring `R_p`, hence free by Stacks Lemma 10.106.6. This proves local freeness in codimension two.

Outside `V(J)`, (9.5) already makes `K` free. Thus

\[
\operatorname{NF}(K)\subseteq V(J).
\]

Since `r^L in J`, every prime containing `J` contains `r`, so

\[
V(J)\subseteq V(r).
\]
