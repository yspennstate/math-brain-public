# 3. Mandatory elementary audits rederived

## 3.1 Rank-one stable cancellation

Let `P` be a constant rank-one projective over a commutative ring `A` and suppose

\[
P\oplus A^m\simeq A^{m+1}.
\]

Taking top exterior powers gives

\[
\det(P\oplus A^m)\simeq P,
\]

while

\[
\det(A^{m+1})\simeq A.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{P\simeq A}.
\]

This proves only the stated rank-one cancellation under the displayed stable-freeness hypothesis. It does not imply rank-two or rank-three cancellation.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

\[
R=k[x,y]_{(x,y)},\qquad A=R[1/x].
\]

Then

\[
A/(y)\simeq k[x]_{(x)}[1/x]\simeq k(x),
\]

and, after substituting `y=x`,

\[
A/(y-x)\simeq k(x).
\]

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`.

They are distinct: if they were equal, their difference `x` would lie in that proper ideal, but `x` is a unit in `A`.

Therefore `R[1/r]` need not be local even when `R` is local and `r` is a parameter. No local-ring freeness theorem is applied to `A` without a separate locality proof.

# 8. `P02-C126-C92-K2-QUOTIENT-SURJECTIVITY` — PROVED

The preceding report left two logically separate hypotheses in the relative prestabilization gate C122:

1. the comparison class must already be stably relative elementary;
2. the relative ideal must satisfy the sharper stable-range bound needed at size three.

This section makes a substantial advance on the first item.

## 8.1 Local relative `K_1` lemma

Let `(L,n)` be a commutative local ring and let `J subset n` be an ideal. Then determinant induces an isomorphism

\[
\boxed{
K_1(L,J)\simeq 1+J.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proof

Take

\[
g\in GL_m(L,J),
\qquad g\equiv I_m\pmod J.
\]

The first diagonal entry is congruent to `1 mod J`, hence is a unit. Relative elementary row and column operations, whose coefficients lie in `J`, clear the first row and column. Inductively, `g` is relative-elementary equivalent to a diagonal matrix

\[
\operatorname{diag}(u_1,\ldots,u_m),
\qquad u_i\in1+J.
\]

Weibel's Relative Whitehead Lemma gives, for `u in 1+J`,

\[
\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})\in E(L,J)
\]

after stable embedding. Therefore the diagonal class equals the class of

\[
\operatorname{diag}(u_1\cdots u_m,1,\ldots,1).
\]

Its determinant is `u_1...u_m`, so determinant is both surjective onto `1+J` and injective.

This proves (8.1).

Primary reference for the relative Whitehead identity and definition of `K_1(L,J)`: Charles Weibel, *The K-book*, Chapter III, §2, Relative Whitehead Lemma 2.1.

## 8.2 Quotient of a local ring by `F`

Apply the relative exact sequence to

\[
R\longrightarrow S=R/(F).
\]

It contains

\[
K_2(R)\longrightarrow K_2(S)
\longrightarrow K_1(R,(F))
\longrightarrow K_1(R).
\tag{8.2}
\]

By (8.1),

\[
K_1(R,(F))\simeq1+FR,
\]

and its map to

\[
K_1(R)=R^\times
\]

is the literal inclusion of `1+FR` into the unit group. It is injective.

Therefore the connecting map in (8.2) is zero, and

\[
\boxed{
K_2(R)\twoheadrightarrow K_2(S).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

No residue-field-size or characteristic hypothesis is used.

## 8.3 Localization at `r`

Recall

\[
A=R[1/r],
\qquad
B=S[1/r].
\]

Since `r` is a regular parameter in both regular local rings `R` and `S`, Quillen localization gives exact rows

\[
K_2(R)\to K_2(A)\xrightarrow{\partial_R}K_1(R/(r))\to K_1(R),
\tag{8.4}
\]

\[
K_2(S)\to K_2(B)\xrightarrow{\partial_S}K_1(S/(r))\to K_1(S).
\tag{8.5}
\]

The last maps in (8.4) and (8.5) are zero.

Indeed, for a unit `ubar` of `R/(r)`, lift it to a unit `u in R`. The transfer to `K_1(R)` is computed from the length-one resolution

\[
0\to R\xrightarrow r R\to R/(r)\to0.
\]

Multiplication by `ubar` lifts to multiplication by `u` on both copies of `R`, so its alternating `K_1` class is

\[
[u]-[u]=0.
\]

The same argument applies to `S`.

Hence both boundary maps

\[
\partial_R,
\qquad
\partial_S
\]

are surjective.

## 14.1 Setup

Let `P` be a deterministic rank-`q` orthogonal projection in the traceless-symmetric harmonic coefficient space `E=Sym_d^0`. Choose a Frobenius-orthonormal basis `A_1,...,A_q` of its range and define

\[
y_P(u)=

\]

For iid sphere points `U_1,...,U_q`, let `Y` have these evaluation vectors as columns, put

\[
W=\det Y,
\qquad
K=Y^TY,
\qquad
\det K=W^2.
\]

The determinant-selected law is

\[
dQ=\frac{W^2}{q!}\,d\mu^q,
\]

and the inverse-wedge squared radius is

\[
R=\frac1{W^2}.
\]

## 14.2 A regular determinant zero exists for every deterministic plane

The coherent orbit spans `E`: if a traceless symmetric matrix `A` satisfies

\[
u^TAu=0
\]

for every unit `u`, then `A=0`.

Hence the `q` coordinate functions of `y_P` are linearly independent. Choose `q-1` sphere points so their evaluation vectors are linearly independent. Let `c` be a nonzero vector orthogonal to their span. With

\[
A_c=\sum_a c_aA_a,
\]

the determinant as a function of the last point is a nonzero scalar multiple of

\[
g(u)=u^TA_cu.
\]

The matrix `A_c` is nonzero and traceless, so it has a positive and a negative eigenvalue. If `e_+,e_-` are corresponding unit eigenvectors with eigenvalues `lambda_+>0>lambda_-`, choose the normalized combination

\[
u_0=
\sqrt{\frac{-\lambda_-}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}}e_+
+
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_+-\lambda_-}}e_-.
\]

Then

\[
u_0^TA_cu_0=0,
\]

while

\[
A_cu_0\ne0.
\]

At a zero of the quadratic form the radial correction to the spherical gradient vanishes, so

\[
\nabla_{S^{d-1}}g(u_0)=2A_cu_0\ne0
\]

up to the harmless harmonic normalization constant.

Thus the product determinant `W` has a regular zero.

## 14.3 Coarea/implicit-coordinate exponent

Near that regular zero, the implicit function theorem supplies coordinates `(t,z)` with

\[
t=W
\]

and product spherical measure comparable to ordinary Lebesgue measure.

Therefore

\[
E_Q R^\alpha
=
\frac1{q!}
\int |W|^{2(1-\alpha)}d\mu^q.
\]

Locally the one-dimensional singular factor is

\[
\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}|t|^{2(1-\alpha)}dt.
\]

This diverges exactly when

\[
2(1-\alpha)\le-1,
\]

i.e.

\[
\alpha\ge\frac32.
\]

Likewise

\[
Q\{R>L\}
\]

contains a chart contribution proportional to

\[
\int_{|t|<L^{-1/2}}t^2dt
\asymp L^{-3/2}.
\]

This verifies both the moment-divergence threshold and the asserted lower-tail exponent.

**Audit verdict: PASS.** The argument is local finite-dimensional differential geometry. It does not infer inverse-Gram control from isotropy, does not transfer determinant-selected estimates to the ordinary law by an illicit `L^2` bound, and does not close P04 Target 4.
