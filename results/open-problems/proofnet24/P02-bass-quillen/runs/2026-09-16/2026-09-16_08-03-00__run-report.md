## 3.1 Rank-one cancellation — exactly rank one

Let `A` be commutative and `P` a constant-rank-one projective. If

`P direct_sum A^m ~= A^(m+1)`,

then

`det(P direct_sum A^m) ~= P tensor det(A^m) ~= P`,

while

`det(A^(m+1)) ~= A`.

Hence `P ~= A`.

This proves only the stated rank-one cancellation under the stable-freeness premise. It proves neither rank-two nor rank-three cancellation, and does not establish stable freeness of an arbitrary relevant projective.

## 3.2 Localization at a parameter need not be local

Let

`R=k[x,y]_(x,y)`,  `A=R[1/x]`.

Then

`A/(y) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`

and

`A/(y-x) ~= k[x]_(x)[1/x] ~= k(x)`.

Thus `(y)` and `(y-x)` are maximal ideals of `A`. They are distinct: equality would force their difference `x` into the common ideal, but `x` is a unit in `A`.

No local-ring projective-freeness theorem is applied to `R[1/r]` merely because `R` is local.

# 5. `P02-C151-N1-REDUCED-DIVISOR-CH0-IDENTIFICATION` — PROVED

## Statement

Write the UFD factorization

`alpha = epsilon p_1^{e_1} ... p_t^{e_t}`

with distinct prime elements `p_i`, and put

`q=p_1...p_t`,

`D=C/(alpha)`,

`X=Spec(C/(q))`.

Then `X=D_red` is a reduced pure one-dimensional noetherian scheme and

`boxed( SK_1(B) ~= CH_0(X) )`.

Under this identification, the exponents `e_i` and all nilpotent thickness in `D` disappear. Equivalently,

`Q_alpha ~= Q_q ~= CH_0(X)`.

Here `CH_0(X)` means the group of closed-point cycles modulo principal divisors on the irreducible one-dimensional components, with the usual length boundary at singular closed points.

## Proof

### Step 1: reduce the nonreduced divisor by Quillen devissage

The ideal

`J=(q)/(alpha) subset D`

is nilpotent. Indeed, if `E=max_i e_i`, then `q^E` is divisible by `alpha`, so `J^E=0`. Moreover

`D/J ~= C/(q)`.

Quillen's devissage theorem applies to the exact subcategory of finite `D`-modules killed by `J`. Equivalently, Quillen's scheme-theoretic Proposition 3.1 in *Higher Algebraic K-theory I* says that a nilpotent closed thickening induces an isomorphism on coherent `K'`-theory. Hence

`G_0(C/(q)) ->~ G_0(C/(alpha)).`   (5.1)

Each module `C/p_i` is already killed by `q`, so under (5.1) its class is the same component class on both sides.

This already proves that the C148 quotient is independent of all multiplicities `e_i`.

### Step 2: the reduced divisor is a pure curve

Each `p_i` is a nonzero prime element in the two-dimensional regular domain `C`, hence `(p_i)` has height one. The quotient by the nonzerodivisor `q` is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension one, and squarefreeness plus primality of the `p_i` makes `(q)` radical. Thus `X` is reduced and pure one-dimensional, with generic points

`eta_1,...,eta_t`.

### Step 3: Quillen's codimension spectral sequence in dimension one

Quillen, *Higher Algebraic K-theory I*, Theorem 5.4, gives for any finite-dimensional noetherian scheme the support spectral sequence

`E_1^{p,q} = direct_sum_{x in X^(p)} K_{-p-q}(k(x))  =>  G_{-p-q}(X).`   (5.2)

Because `dim X=1`, the total-degree-zero part has only two columns.

At the generic points,

`E_1^{0,0}=direct_sum_i K_0(k(eta_i)) = Z^t`.

At the closed points,

`E_1^{1,-1}=direct_sum_{x in X^(1)} K_0(k(x))`

is the free abelian group of zero-cycles. Its incoming differential is

`d_1: direct_sum_i K_1(k(eta_i)) -> direct_sum_{x in X^(1)} Z[x]`,

which is exactly the principal-divisor boundary. Therefore

`E_2^{1,-1}=CH_0(X).`

There are no further possible differentials in dimension one. The generic term `E_1^{0,0}=Z^t` also survives because there is no incoming column and the target of its degree-one differential involves negative K-groups of fields, which are zero in Quillen's connective theory.

Thus the coniveau filtration on `G_0(X)` gives a short exact sequence

`0 -> CH_0(X) -> G_0(X) --rho--> Z^t -> 0`,   (5.3)

where `rho` is the vector of generic ranks on the irreducible components.

### Step 4: the C148 component classes split generic rank

For each `i`, the coherent sheaf `C/p_i` has generic rank one on component `i` and generic rank zero on every other component. Hence

`rho([C/p_i])=e_i^(std)`

(the `i`th standard basis vector of `Z^t`). Therefore the subgroup generated by the component classes maps isomorphically onto `Z^t` and splits (5.3).

Consequently

`G_0(X)/<[C/p_1],...,[C/p_t]> ~= CH_0(X).`   (5.4)

Combining (5.1), (5.4), and inherited C148 gives

`SK_1(B) ~= Q_alpha ~= CH_0(X)`.

QED.

## Consequence

The second localization obstruction is **insensitive to nilpotent multiplicity**. What survives is entirely a reduced-curve zero-cycle phenomenon: class-group/Picard effects on regular components and singular/component-gluing effects encoded by rational equivalence on the reduced divisor.

and hence every C50 rank-three row in this `N=1` branch has free kernel.

`(S/(pi_i))[1/u]`

`CH_0(X_i)=Pic(X_i)=0`.   (7.1)

`direct_sum_i CH_0(X_i) -> CH_0(X)`,

where `X=union_i X_i`: every closed point lies on at least one component, and every component principal divisor remains a valid rational equivalence after pushforward to `X`.

# 8. `P02-C154-N1-CLEARED-DENOMINATOR-ZEROCYCLE-CERTIFICATE` — PROVED

## Statement

Let `G in SL_n(B)` with `B=C[1/alpha]`. Choose `N>=0` so that

`M=alpha^N G in M_n(C)`.

Then:

1. `M:C^n -> C^n` is injective;
2. `T=coker(M)` is a finite `alpha`-power-torsion module;
3. under the C151 isomorphism
   
   `SK_1(B) ~= CH_0(X)`,  `X=(Spec C/(alpha))_red`,
   
   the stable class `[G]` maps exactly to the zero-cycle class obtained from `[T]` after nilpotent devissage and quotienting generic component ranks.

Equivalently, if `zeta(T)` denotes the image of `[T]` in `CH_0(X)`, then

`boxed( [G] = 0 in SK_1(B)  iff  zeta(T)=0 in CH_0(X). )`

The class is independent of the chosen clearing exponent `N`.

## Proof

Because `det G=1`,

`det M = alpha^(Nn)`,

which is nonzero in the domain `C`. Hence `M` is injective: if `Mx=0`, multiplying by the adjugate gives `alpha^(Nn)x=0`, and `C^n` is torsion-free.

The adjugate identity also shows that `alpha^(Nn)` annihilates `T`, so `T` is supported on `V(alpha)`.

Quillen localization gives the boundary

`partial:K_1(B) -> G_0(C/(alpha)).`

For an injective square matrix over `C` which becomes invertible over `B`, the boundary is its cokernel class. Therefore

`partial([M_B])=[T].`   (8.1)

In `K_1(B)`,

`[M_B]=[alpha^N I_n]+[G]`.

Using the boundary convention `partial(alpha)=[C/alpha]`,

`partial([G])=[T]-Nn[C/alpha].`   (8.2)

But in `G_0(C/(alpha))`, additivity of the boundary for

`alpha=epsilon product p_i^{e_i}`

gives

`[C/alpha]=sum_i e_i [C/p_i].`   (8.3)

The scalar term in (8.2) therefore vanishes in the C148 quotient `Q_alpha`. By C151 that quotient is exactly `CH_0(X)`. Hence `[G]` corresponds to the class of `[T]` in `CH_0(X)`.

Because the left side is independent of `N`, so is this zero-cycle class; changing `N` only adds a multiple of the component combination (8.3).

QED.

## Finite-rank use and limitation

Inherited C149 supplies a surjection

`SK_1(B) ->> W_E(B)`.

Thus, if one identifies a stable preimage `G` of the **actual** residual Vaserstein class and C154 gives `zeta(coker(alpha^N G))=0`, then the Vaserstein class is zero and C55 closes rank three.

The converse is deliberately **not** asserted: a nonzero zero-cycle can lie in the kernel of `SK_1(B)->W_E(B)`. Such a class is a stable certificate, not automatically a row obstruction.
