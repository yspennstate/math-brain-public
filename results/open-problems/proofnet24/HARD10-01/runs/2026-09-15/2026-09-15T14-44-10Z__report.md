## 1. Exact full target

Let `G=(V,E)` be any finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, isolated vertices allowed. For every two distinct edges `e,f`, prove or refute

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every finite simple graph and every edge pair. The stronger I-Rayleigh statement asks for nonnegativity at every positive edge-weight evaluation.

Equivalently, every pair with exactly one inclusion-minimal `R` witness is settled. This is a universal structural theorem, not a finite computation.

## 4. Reused five-class language

Fix distinct `e,f` and put `H=G\setminus\{e,f\}`. Every forest `A` of `H` lies in exactly one of the five classes `N0,Ne,Nf,B,R`, where `R` consists of forests for which each of `e,f` is separately addable but the pair is not jointly addable.

The earlier exact polynomial identity is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=N_eN_f+R(N_e+N_f+B+R)-BN_0.
}\tag{4.1}
\]

At unit weights, if `T` is the number of `H` forests and `U,V` indicate that the endpoints of `e,f` respectively are already connected, then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=TR-T^2\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V).
}\tag{4.2}
\]

## 5. NEW LEMMA L20 — minimal `R` witnesses are exactly common cycles with `e,f` removed

### Statement

There is a bijection

\[
\{\text{simple cycles }C\subseteq G\text{ containing both }e,f\}
\longleftrightarrow
\{\text{inclusion-minimal forests }A\in R\}
\]

given by

\[
C\mapsto C\setminus\{e,f\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence `e,f` lie on exactly one common simple cycle if and only if the `R` family has exactly one inclusion-minimal member.

### Proof

Let `C` be a simple cycle containing `e,f` and put `A=C\setminus\{e,f\}`. Removing either distinguished edge from `C` breaks the cycle, so `A+e` and `A+f` are forests, while `A+e+f=C` is cyclic. Thus `A\in R`. Every edge of `A` lies on the unique two path pieces of `C\setminus\{e,f\}` needed for the terminal connections; deleting any such edge destroys the required `R` pairing, so `A` is inclusion-minimal.

Conversely let `A` be inclusion-minimal in `R`. Since `A` is a forest and `A+e` is a forest, adding `f` to `A+e` creates a **unique** cycle `C`; because `A+f` is also a forest, this cycle contains both `e` and `f`. If some edge `g\in A` were outside `C`, then `A-g` would still have `e,f` separately addable and `C` would still appear after adding both, so `A-g\in R`, contradicting minimality. Hence `A=C\setminus\{e,f\}`. QED.

## 6. NEW THEOREM L21 — unique common cycle implies full weighted pair-Rayleigh

### Statement

Let `G` be any finite graph with positive edge weights. Fix distinct edges `e,f`. Assume there is **exactly one** simple cycle `C` containing both `e` and `f`.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}\ge0
\quad\text{for every positive evaluation of all edge weights.}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the forest measure is pairwise negatively correlated for `e,f` at unit weights. No hypothesis is imposed on the internal structure of graph pieces attached to `C`; they may themselves be non-series-parallel.

### Proof

We use the standard `C`-bridge decomposition, but every step needed for the sign is spelled out.

#### Step 1. Split the reference cycle into two path sides

Delete the two distinguished edges from `C`. The remaining cycle edges form two path components, denoted `X` and `Y`. If `e,f` are adjacent, one of these components may be a single isolated cycle vertex; that causes no change below.

Orient `X` and `Y` consistently so that `e` joins their initial endpoints and `f` joins their terminal endpoints.

#### Step 2. Every `C`-bridge has at most one attachment on each side

A `C`-bridge is either a chord of `C` or a connected component of `G-V(C)` together with all of its incident edges to `C` and its attachment vertices.

Suppose a `C`-bridge `B` had two distinct attachments `x,y` on the same side, say `X`. Since `B` is connected between its attachments, choose a simple `x-y` path `Q` through `B` whose internal vertices are off `C` (for a chord, `Q` is the chord itself).

The complementary `x-y` arc of `C`—the one not contained in the `X` subpath between `x,y`—contains **both** `e` and `f`. Therefore `Q` plus that complementary arc is a second simple cycle containing `e,f`, contradicting uniqueness of `C`.

Hence each bridge has at most one attachment on `X` and at most one on `Y`; in particular it has at most two attachments total. Any two-attachment bridge has one attachment on each side.

#### Step 3. Two-attachment bridges are order-preserving

Take two distinct two-attachment bridges `B_1,B_2` with attachment pairs

\[
(x_1,y_1),\qquad (x_2,y_2),
\]

where `x_i\in X`, `y_i\in Y`. Suppose `x_1` precedes `x_2` along the chosen orientation of `X` but `y_2` precedes `y_1` along `Y`.

Choose simple internal bridge paths `Q_i` from `x_i` to `y_i`. Different `C`-bridges have disjoint interiors. Then the closed walk

* `e`,
* the initial segment of `Y` to `y_2`,
* `Q_2`,
* the terminal segment of `X` from `x_2` to `f`,
* `f`,
* the terminal segment of `Y` backwards to `y_1`,
* `Q_1`,
* the initial segment of `X` backwards to the start of `e`

is a simple cycle containing both `e,f`, distinct from `C`. This again contradicts uniqueness.

Therefore all two-attachment pairs are order-preserving.

#### Step 4. Factor one-attachment pieces and replace two-attachment pieces by virtual edges

A bridge with zero or one attachment to `C` meets the rest of the graph in at most one vertex. Forests therefore factor across it: if `J` is such a piece, its forest polynomial contributes a direct multiplicative factor `I_J`. Consequently its contribution to the `e,f` Rayleigh difference is the positive square factor `I_J^2`.

For each attachment pair `(x,y)` occurring among two-attachment bridges, combine **all** bridges with that same pair into one two-terminal network `K_{xy}` with terminals `x,y`. Let

\[
D_{xy}=\sum_{A\text{ forest in }K_{xy}:x\not\leftrightarrow y}\mathbf y^A,
\qquad
C_{xy}=\sum_{A\text{ forest in }K_{xy}:x\leftrightarrow y}\mathbf y^A.
\tag{6.2}
\]

At positive weights, `D_{xy}>0` because the empty forest is counted, and `C_{xy}\ge0`.

Create a skeleton `S` consisting of the cycle `C` plus one virtual edge `g_{xy}=xy` for each distinct two-attachment pair.

The exact two-terminal replacement identity proved in the prior backfill says that replacing a virtual edge `g=xy` by `K_{xy}` multiplies the Rayleigh difference for the untouched pair `e,f` by `D_{xy}^2` and substitutes

\[
y_g=\frac{C_{xy}}{D_{xy}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Iterating over the bridge networks and multiplying the one-attachment direct-sum factors gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
=
\left(\prod_{J\in\mathcal B_1} I_J^2\right)
\left(\prod_{K_{xy}\in\mathcal B_2} D_{xy}^2\right)
\Delta_S\{e,f\}
\bigg|_{y_{g_{xy}}=C_{xy}/D_{xy}}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Here `\mathcal B_1` denotes the zero/one-attachment direct-sum pieces and `\mathcal B_2` the combined two-terminal networks.

#### Step 5. The skeleton is series-parallel

Draw `X` and `Y` as the two long boundary sides of a rectangle, with `e,f` as the two end edges. By Step 3, the virtual attachment pairs are order-preserving, so their virtual edges can be drawn as noncrossing rungs. Parallel virtual edges with the same attachment pair were already combined into one network; if a virtual edge is parallel to `e` or `f`, that is merely a standard parallel extension.

Thus the skeleton is outerplanar (allowing harmless parallel extensions), hence series-parallel in the graphic-matroid sense used by Erickson. Erickson's Theorem 1.2 therefore gives

\[
\Delta_S\{e,f\}\ge0
\]

for every positive evaluation of its edge variables. By continuity the same holds when one of the effective weights `C_{xy}/D_{xy}` is zero.

Every prefactor in (6.4) is nonnegative, so (6.1) follows. QED.

### Important scope point

The inserted two-terminal bridge networks `K_{xy}` need **not** be series-parallel and need not be known Rayleigh. Their entire effect on the distinguished pair is compressed to the positive factor `D_{xy}^2` and the nonnegative effective weight `C_{xy}/D_{xy}`. Only the small ordered-rung skeleton must be series-parallel.
