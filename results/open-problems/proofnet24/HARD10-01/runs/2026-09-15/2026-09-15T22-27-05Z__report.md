## 1. Exact target and new result

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices.  For every distinct pair `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every finite simple graph and every distinct edge pair.

## 4. Reused `R`-root language

Fix `e,f` and put `H=G\setminus\{e,f\}`.  The class `R` consists of H-forests `A` such that `A+e` and `A+f` are forests but `A+e+f` is cyclic.

If `P` is an inclusion-minimal member, then

\[
C_P=P+e+f
\]

is a simple cycle containing both distinguished edges.  Its fiber is

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{A\in R:P\subseteq A\}.
\]

The one-edge criterion gives

\[
\boxed{
P+h\in R
\iff
h\text{ has an endpoint outside }V(C_P).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the one-edge extensions of a root are exactly the graph edges meeting the outside of its common cycle.

## 5. NEW LEMMA L64 — an odd-cardinality size-four fiber is a three-spoke star

### Statement

Assume the total `R`-cardinality is odd.  If one root fiber has cardinality four, then its abstract down-set cannot be a Boolean square.  Hence it has the form

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+h_1,P+h_2,P+h_3\},
\tag{5.1}
\]

and there is one vertex `w\notin V(C_P)` and three distinct vertices `u_1,u_2,u_3\in V(C_P)` such that

\[
\boxed{h_i=wu_i\quad(i=1,2,3).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

A four-element downward-closed fiber has only two shapes:

* the three-single-extension star (5.1); or
* the Boolean square
  \[
  \{P,P+h,P+k,P+h+k\}.
  \tag{5.3}
  \]

Assume (5.3).  By (4.1), `h,k` are the only H-edges having an endpoint outside `V(C_P)`.

We claim both are bridges of `G`.  If, say, `h` belonged to a graph cycle, that cycle must leave `V(C_P)` through `h` and return through another edge meeting the outside.  The only possibility is `k`.  With only these two outside edges, the outside excursion is a two-edge path

\[
u-w-v
\]

with `u,v\in V(C_P)`.  But then `P+h+k` cannot lie in `R`: if `u,v` lie in the same component of `P`, the ear creates a cycle; if they lie in different components, the ear merges the two terminal components and destroys separate addability of `e,f`.  This contradicts (5.3).  Hence `h` is on no graph cycle, and similarly `k` is a bridge.

The archived bridge-toggle lemma says a bridge pairs the entire `R` family by inclusion/exclusion.  Therefore `|R|` would be even, contrary to the present odd-cardinality assumption.  So (5.3) is impossible.

We now have the star down-set (5.1).  No pair `P+h_i+h_j` lies in `R`.  For two individually valid outside edges, the double extension can fail only when the two edges form a two-edge ear: if they are disjoint tree attachments, or form a dangling path attached to the old cycle at only one vertex, their union remains a forest and leaves the two terminal components unchanged.  Thus every pair `h_i,h_j` shares an outside endpoint and has its other two endpoints on `C_P`.

Fix `h_1,h_2`; let their common outside endpoint be `w`.  For `h_3` to form an ear with `h_1`, it must share the **outside** endpoint of `h_1`, not its cycle endpoint.  Therefore `h_3` also meets `w`.  This proves (5.2). QED.

This lemma applies in particular to every size-four fiber when `|R|=7`.

## 6. NEW THEOREM L65 — the `4+3` stratum reduces to the solved weighted `4+1` stratum

### Statement

No edge-minimal counterexample can have `|R|=7` with root-fiber partition

\[
4+3.
\]

In fact every such graph at the unit point reduces by a length-two two-terminal substitution to a graph in the already solved weighted `|R|=5`, partition `4+1` class.

### Proof

Let `P` be the size-three root and `Q` the size-four root.

By the archived size-three theorem,

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+h,P+k\},
\qquad
h=uw,\quad k=wv,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where `w\notin V(C_P)`, `u,v\in V(C_P)`, and `h,k` are the only H-edges meeting the outside of `C_P`.

By L64, the size-four fiber is a three-spoke star

\[
\mathcal F(Q)=\{Q,Q+a,Q+b,Q+c\},
\tag{6.2}
\]

where `a,b,c` share one outside endpoint relative to `C_Q`.

The root `Q` either uses both edges `h,k` or neither.  It cannot use exactly one, because `w` has graph degree two: `h,k` are the only graph edges incident to the outside of `C_P`, and neither distinguished edge is incident to `w`.

Suppose `Q` used neither.  Then `w\notin V(C_Q)`, so (4.1) gives

\[
Q+h,Q+k\in R.
\]

They must be two of the three one-edge extensions in (6.2).  Since every pair of the three star extensions shares the same outside endpoint, the common outside endpoint of the `Q`-star is `w`.  Hence the third extension edge, say `c`, is also incident to `w`.  But `w\notin V(C_P)`, so `c` is a third H-edge meeting the outside of `C_P`, contradicting (6.1).  Therefore

\[
\boxed{h,k\subseteq Q.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Now consider the three star edges `a,b,c` of `Q`.  The root cycle `C_P` either avoids their outside vertex or passes through it.  If it avoided it, all three would be one-edge extensions of `P`, contradicting the fact that `P` has only the two extensions `h,k`.  A simple cycle cannot use exactly one or all three edges incident to that outside vertex.  Therefore `P` uses exactly **two** of the three `Q`-star spokes.

Let

\[
g=uv.
\]

We next show that `g` is absent from `G`.  If `g` existed, suppress the ear `u-w-v` in the common cycle of `Q`, replacing `h,k` by `g`.  This produces an `R(G)` forest avoiding both `h,k`.  Among the seven members of the two fibers, the only forest avoiding both ear edges is `P`; hence the suppression must equal `P`.

Thus `P` and `Q` would differ only by the replacement

\[
g\longleftrightarrow\{h,k\}.
\]

But the three star-extension edges `a,b,c` are absent from `Q`, and this replacement does not introduce any of them, so `P` would contain none of `a,b,c`.  This contradicts the preceding conclusion that `P` uses exactly two.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{g=uv\notin E(G).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Delete `w,h,k` and add the new edge `g=uv`, obtaining a simple graph `B`.

We can classify `R(B)` exactly.

* If `A\in R(B)` and `g\notin A`, then `A` is an `R(G)` forest avoiding both `h,k`, hence `A=P`.
* If `g\in A`, subdivide `g` to `u-w-v`.  Subdivision preserves acyclicity and the three tests involving `e,f`.  The result is an `R(G)` forest containing **both** `h,k`, hence it is one of
  \[
  Q,\quad Q+a,\quad Q+b,\quad Q+c.
  \]
* Conversely suppressing the ear in these four forests gives four distinct members of `R(B)` containing `g`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{|R(B)|=5,\qquad\text{fiber partition }4+1.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The corrected 20:45 checkpoint proves that every exact `4+1` geometry is weighted pair-Rayleigh for all positive weights; its proof uses the repaired suppression/exact-two route and does not use the retracted L45.

Finally `G` is obtained from `B` by replacing the nondistinguished edge `g` by a two-edge path with unit edge weights.  For that path

\[
D_K=3,\qquad C_K=1.
\]

The exact two-terminal replacement identity (3.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=9\,\Delta_B\bigl(y_g=1/3,\ y_{r\ne g}=1\bigr)\ge0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus the `4+3` stratum cannot contain a counterexample. QED.

## 7. NEW THEOREM L66 — the `3+3+1` stratum is excluded

### Statement

No edge-minimal counterexample can have `|R|=7` with root-fiber partition

\[
3+3+1.
\]

### Proof

Let the size-three roots be `P,Q`, with ears `E_P,E_Q`, and let `S` be the singleton root.  By the size-three-ear theorem, each ear consists of exactly two edges and its internal outside vertex has graph degree two.

Relative to the root `P`, the simple root cycle `C_Q` can use either both edges of `E_P` or neither, never exactly one.  This gives the same common-ear/cross-ear dichotomy as in the corrected `3+3` proof.

### Case I: common ear

Suppose `Q` uses neither edge of `E_P`.  Then both ear edges have an endpoint outside `C_Q`, so they are exactly the two one-edge extensions of `Q`.  Hence

\[
E_Q=E_P=:\{uw,wv\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The singleton root `S` must use both ear edges.  If it avoided them, the degree-two vertex `w` would lie outside `C_S` and either ear edge would extend `S`; a simple cycle cannot use exactly one of the two degree-two incident edges.

Delete the common ear.  The remaining graph `B_0` has exactly two `R` forests, namely the roots `P,Q`.

Let `g=uv`.  If `g` were absent, add it to `B_0`, obtaining `B`.  Any `R(B)` forest avoiding `g` is one of `P,Q`.  Any `R(B)` forest containing `g`, after subdivision, becomes an `R(G)` forest containing both ear edges, hence must be the singleton root `S`.  Conversely suppressing `S` gives such a third root.  Therefore `R(B)` consists of exactly three distinct minimal roots, contradicting the universal `|R|=3` theorem.

Hence `g` already exists.  Suppressing the ear in `S` gives an `R(B_0)` forest and therefore equals either `P` or `Q`.  Consequently `G` is obtained from the exact-two graph `B_0` by inserting the length-two ear in parallel with an existing edge `g` of one of the exact-two pieces.

The corrected common-ear argument from L56 of the 20:45 checkpoint now applies verbatim: the exact-two decorated-`K_4` factorization handles a nondistinguished exchange edge by the effective-weight theorem, while if `g` lies in one distinguished two-terminal piece, the enlarged terminal response is itself the Rayleigh difference of a strictly smaller simple graph and is nonnegative by edge minimality.  Thus `\Delta_G\ge0`, contradiction.

### Case II: cross ears

Suppose `Q` uses both edges of `E_P`.  Symmetrically `P` uses both edges of `E_Q`.

The singleton root `S` must use **both ears in full**.  Indeed, applied to `P`, the degree-two internal vertex of `E_P` forces a singleton root to use both edges rather than zero or one; applying the same argument to `Q` forces both edges of `E_Q`.

Let `g` be the direct edge joining the endpoints of `E_P`.  We claim `g` is absent.  If it existed, suppress `E_P` inside `Q`.  The resulting `R(G)` forest avoids `E_P`.  The only member of the seven-element family avoiding `E_P` is the root `P`: its two nonminimal extensions use one ear half, while `Q`, its extensions, and `S` use the full ear.  But the suppressed forest cannot equal `P`, because `P` contains both edges of `E_Q` while `Q` contains neither and the suppression changes only `E_P`.  Contradiction.  Hence `g` is absent.  The same argument shows that the direct edge `g'` for `E_Q` is absent.

Suppress both ears simultaneously, adding the two new direct edges `g,g'`, and call the resulting simple graph `B`.

Subdivision gives a bijection between `R(B)` and those `R(G)` members whose use of each ear is either zero or two edges.  Exactly three members have this `(0/2,0/2)` parity pattern:

* `P`, which uses `E_Q` but not `E_P`;
* `Q`, which uses `E_P` but not `E_Q`;
* `S`, which uses both ears.

The four nonminimal fiber members use exactly one half of one ear.  Hence

\[
\boxed{|R(B)|=3.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Suppressing the three root cycles gives three distinct minimal roots in `B` (their `(g,g')` states are respectively `(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)`).  This contradicts the universal theorem that a three-element `R` family has a unique minimal root.

Thus the cross-ear case is impossible, and the common-ear case is pair-Rayleigh.  The entire `3+3+1` stratum is excluded. QED.

## 8. NEW THEOREM L67 — the `3+1+1+1+1` stratum is excluded

### Statement

No edge-minimal counterexample can have `|R|=7` with root-fiber partition

\[
3+1+1+1+1.
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+uw,P+wv\}
\]

be the size-three fiber, with the degree-two ear `u-w-v`, and let `Q_1,\ldots,Q_4` be the singleton roots.

Every singleton root must use both ear edges.  If it used neither, the degree-two vertex `w` would be outside its common cycle and an ear edge would be a forbidden one-edge extension; a simple cycle cannot use only one edge incident to `w`.

If the direct edge `uv` existed, suppressing the same ear in every `Q_i` would give an `R(G)` forest avoiding the ear.  The only such member is `P`, so all four singleton roots would be identical.  Therefore `uv` is absent.

Delete `w,uw,wv` and add a new edge

\[
g=uv,
\]

obtaining a smaller simple graph `B`.

Any `R(B)` forest avoiding `g` is an `R(G)` forest avoiding the ear and hence is `P`.  Any `R(B)` forest containing `g` subdivides to an `R(G)` forest containing the full ear and hence is one of the four singleton roots.  Conversely suppressing those roots gives four distinct minimal roots containing `g`.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{|R(B)|=5,\quad\text{and all five members are minimal roots}.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

The structural part of the archived all-minimal theorem gives a proper two-vertex separator `{s,t}` in `B` separating `e` from `f`.  The edge `g` lies on one separator side.  Replacing it by the path `u-w-v` keeps the replacement entirely on that same side, so `{s,t}` is also a proper separator in `G`.

The exact cross-2-separation identity gives

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
=\alpha_K(e;s,t)\alpha_L(f;s,t).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Each response factor is a Rayleigh difference of a strictly smaller simple graph after adjoining a virtual terminal edge if necessary.  Edge minimality of `G` therefore makes both factors nonnegative, contradicting `\Delta_G<0`.

Thus the partition `3+1+1+1+1` is excluded. QED.
