## 1. Exact target and result of this iteration

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices.  For every two distinct edges `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every finite simple graph and every distinct edge pair.

**New universal progress in this run:** the final edge-minimal `|R|=5` obstruction from the previous checkpoint is eliminated.  Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{every edge-minimal counterexample must satisfy }|R|\ge 6.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The proof is stronger on the surviving `4+1` geometry: once its exact structure is imposed, the distinguished pair is **weighted Rayleigh for every positive edge weighting**, by an exact decorated-adjacent-`K_4` factorization.

## 4. Reused structure of the only surviving edge-minimal `|R|=5` case

Fix `e,f` and put `H=G\setminus\{e,f\}`.  Recall that `R` is the class of H-forests `A` such that `A+e` and `A+f` are forests but `A+e+f` is cyclic.  Inclusion-minimal R-forests are in bijection with simple cycles containing both `e,f`.

The 18:31 checkpoint proves that if an edge-minimal counterexample has `|R|=5`, then the root-fiber partition is exactly `4+1`.  There are exactly two common `e,f` cycles `C_P,C_Q`.  For the size-four root `P`, its three one-edge extensions are three spokes meeting at one outside vertex `w`:

\[
wu,\quad wv,\quad wz,
\]

where `u,v,z` lie on `C_P`, `w` has degree exactly three, and all other vertices outside `V(C_P)` are isolated.

There are two `u-v` arcs on `C_P`.  Write them as `A` and `R`, where

\[
C_P=A\cup R,
\qquad
C_Q=(u-w-v)\cup R.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The arc `A` contains neither distinguished edge.  The retained arc `R` contains both, and `z\in R` separates them: one of `e,f` lies on the `u-z` subpath and the other on the `z-v` subpath.  Consequently the displayed cycles plus the third spoke `wz` form a topological `K_4` with branch vertices `u,v,z,w`.

The previous report stopped at a three-terminal transfer identity.  The key new observation is that the singleton fiber of `Q` forces the omitted `u-v` arc `A` to be a **single edge**, and then the absence of a third common `e,f` cycle forces every remaining chord to stay inside one of the two distinguished rim branches.  This converts the whole graph into an exact decorated adjacent-`K_4` instance.

## 6. NEW LEMMA L48 — every remaining edge is confined to one distinguished rim branch

Let

\[
U=C_P[u,z],\qquad V=C_P[z,v]
\]

be the two retained rim paths, chosen so that `e\in E(U)` and `f\in E(V)` after possibly swapping the labels `e,f`.  By L47,

\[
C_P=U\cup V\cup\{uv\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Statement

Every edge of `G` other than the three spokes and the cycle edge `uv` either lies entirely in the vertex set of `U` or entirely in the vertex set of `V`.  Thus there are no extra edges between the interiors of the two rim branches.

More precisely, if `g` is a non-cycle edge not incident with `w`, then

\[
\boxed{
V(g)\subseteq V(U)
\quad\text{or}\quad
V(g)\subseteq V(V).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

By the previous structural theorem, `w` is the only non-isolated vertex outside `V(C_P)` and has exactly the three spoke edges.  Hence a non-spoke edge `g` has both endpoints on `C_P`.

Delete `e,f` from `C_P`.  The result has exactly two path components:

* the **middle** component `M`, running from the far endpoint of `e` through `z` to the near endpoint of `f`;
* the **outer** component `O`, running from the other endpoint of `e` through `u`, the edge `uv`, and `v` to the other endpoint of `f`.

A non-cycle edge `g` cannot have both endpoints in the same component of `C_P\setminus\{e,f\}`.  Indeed, if both endpoints lie on one path component, replace the corresponding subpath of `C_P` by `g`.  The replaced subpath contains neither `e` nor `f`, so the resulting simple cycle is distinct from `C_P` and still contains both distinguished edges.  It is also distinct from `C_Q` because it does not use `w`.  This would give a third R-root, contradiction.

Therefore every non-cycle `g` joins `M` to `O`.

There are four possible placements.  Two stay within a single rim branch:

* the `U`-middle side joined to the `U`-outer side;
* the `V`-middle side joined to the `V`-outer side.

The two cross-branch placements create a third common cycle immediately.

Suppose first that

\[
x\in U\cap M,
\qquad
y\in V\cap O,
\qquad g=xy.
\]

Then the closed walk

\[
u\xrightarrow{U}x\xrightarrow{g}y\xrightarrow{V}z\to w\to u
\]

is a simple cycle.  The `u-x` portion of `U` contains `e`, and the `y-z` portion of `V` contains `f`.  Thus this is a third simple cycle containing both `e,f`, contradiction.

For the opposite cross placement

\[
x\in V\cap M,
\qquad y\in U\cap O,
\]

the cycle

\[
v\xrightarrow{V}x\xrightarrow{g}y\xrightarrow{U}z\to w\to v
\]

contains both `f` and `e`, again a contradiction.

Hence only the two branch-local placements are possible, proving (6.2). QED.

## 7. NEW LEMMA L49 — each distinguished branch is a two-terminal series-parallel network

Let `K_U` be the graph on `V(U)` consisting of the path edges of `U` and all additional graph edges with both endpoints in `V(U)`.  Define `K_V` analogously.

### Statement

With terminals `(u,z)` and `(z,v)` respectively,

\[
\boxed{K_U\text{ and }K_V\text{ are two-terminal series-parallel networks}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Consequently their terminal response factors satisfy, at every nonnegative edge-weight evaluation,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{K_U}(e;u,z)\ge0,
\qquad
\alpha_{K_V}(f;z,v)\ge0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Here

\[
\alpha_K(g;s,t)=D_K(C_K)_g-C_K(D_K)_g
\]

is the terminal response polynomial from the archived two-terminal calculus.

### Proof

It is enough to treat `K_U`.  Write the underlying path in order as

\[
u=x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_r, x_{r+1},\ldots,x_m=z,
\]

where `e=x_rx_{r+1}`.

By the first part of the proof of L48, every non-path edge of `K_U` has one endpoint on the left side

\[
\{x_0,\ldots,x_r\}
\]

and the other on the right side

\[
\{x_{r+1},\ldots,x_m\}.
\]

Consider two such chords

\[
g_1=x_i x_k,
\qquad
g_2=x_j x_\ell,
\]

with

\[
i<j\le r<k<\ell.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then there is a second simple `u-z` path containing `e`:

\[
u\to x_i\xrightarrow{g_1}x_k
\to x_{r+1}\xrightarrow{e}x_r
\to x_j\xrightarrow{g_2}x_\ell\to z,
\tag{7.4}
\]

where the unlabeled portions follow the original path, in the indicated forward/backward direction.  The inequalities in (7.3) ensure that the pieces in (7.4) are internally disjoint, so this is a simple path.

Joining (7.4) to the complementary `z-u` arc `V\cup\{uv\}` of `C_P` produces a third simple cycle containing both `e,f`, contradiction.

Therefore chords cannot have the ordering (7.3).  If their left endpoints are ordered increasingly, their right endpoints must be ordered decreasingly.  Hence all chords are nested.

A path with a nested family of chords is two-terminal series-parallel.  One direct induction is as follows.  If there is no chord, the network is a series composition of its path edges.  Otherwise choose an outermost chord `x_i x_k`; every other chord lies inside the path interval `x_i,\ldots,x_k`.  By induction the interval network is two-terminal series-parallel.  Put the outermost chord in parallel with that interval network, then compose in series with the path prefix from `u` to `x_i` and the path suffix from `x_k` to `z`.  Shared chord endpoints cause only degenerate prefix/suffix cases and do not affect the construction.

Thus `K_U` is two-terminal series-parallel, and similarly `K_V`.

The archived response recurrences give, for a parallel composition with the distinguished edge in the first factor,

\[
\alpha_{K_1\parallel K_2}=D_2^2\alpha_{K_1},
\]

and for a series composition,

\[
\alpha_{K_1\circ K_2}=C_2 I_2\alpha_{K_1},
\]

with the symmetric versions when the distinguished edge is in the second factor.  Starting from a single edge, whose response is `1`, induction gives (7.2). QED.

## 8. NEW THEOREM L50 — exact decorated adjacent-`K_4` factorization

The preceding lemmas show that the entire non-isolated graph is exactly a `K_4` with its two adjacent core edges `uz,zv` replaced by `K_U,K_V`.  The other four core edges

\[
uv,\quad uw,\quad vw,\quad zw
\]

remain literal edges.

Let their weights be

\[
b=y_{uv},\qquad c=y_{uw},\qquad d=y_{vw},\qquad a=y_{zw},
\]

and put

\[
S=1+a+c+d.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Statement

For arbitrary positive weights on **all** edges of the graph,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
={}&\alpha_{K_U}(e;u,z)\,\alpha_{K_V}(f;z,v)\\
&\times\Big[
 b^2S^2+bS(S+2cd)+cd(1+c)(1+d)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\Delta_G\{e,f\}\ge0}
\tag{8.3}
\]

for every positive edge weighting.

### Proof

Map the `K_4` vertices as

\[
1=z,\quad 2=u,\quad 3=v,\quad 4=w.
\]

The core distinguished edges are therefore the adjacent pair

\[
12=zu,\qquad 13=zv.
\]

The archived exact adjacent-`K_4` calculation is

\[
\Delta_{K_4}\{12,13\}
=b^2S^2+bS(S+2cd)+cd(1+c)(1+d),
\tag{8.4}
\]

where

\[
a=y_{14}=y_{zw},\quad
b=y_{23}=y_{uv},\quad
c=y_{24}=y_{uw},\quad
d=y_{34}=y_{vw}.
\]

Now use the archived distinguished-edge substitution identity.  Replacing a core edge `g=st` by a two-terminal network `K` containing the actual distinguished edge `q` gives exactly

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{new}}\{q,r\}
=\alpha_K(q;s,t)\,\Delta_{\mathrm{old}}\{g,r\}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Apply (8.5) first to replace `zu` by `K_U` and then to replace `zv` by `K_V`.  The two substitutions multiply the core Rayleigh difference by the two response factors and leave the four other core weights unchanged.  This gives (8.2).

By L49 both response factors are nonnegative, while every term in the bracket in (8.2) is nonnegative for nonnegative core weights and is strictly positive for positive core weights.  Hence (8.3). QED.

so the core factor is

\[
16+24+4=44.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)=44\,\alpha_{K_U}(e;u,z;\mathbf1)\,\alpha_{K_V}(f;z,v;\mathbf1).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]
