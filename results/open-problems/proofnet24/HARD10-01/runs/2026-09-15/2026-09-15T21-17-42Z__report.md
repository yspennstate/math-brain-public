## 5. NEW LEMMA L59 — exactly one of `uv` and `uz` exists

### Statement

In the `4+1+1` configuration (4.1)–(4.2), exactly one of the two direct edges

\[

\qquad

### Preliminary observation

If a singleton common cycle uses the two-edge ear `u-w-v`, then a direct edge `uv`, if present, cannot be one of the distinguished edges `e,f`.  Otherwise that common cycle would contain both the direct edge `uv` and the alternate path `u-w-v`, which already form a proper cycle and leave no room in the same simple cycle for the other distinguished edge.  Thus any present direct ear-endpoint edge lies in `H` and may be used in an `R` forest.

### Proof: the two direct edges cannot both be absent

Assume first

\[
uv,uz\notin E(G).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Delete the outside vertex `w` and its three spokes, and add the two new simple edges `uv,uz`.  Call the resulting graph `B`.

Suppressing the ear in the singleton cycle for `Q_1` gives a minimal `R(B)` root

\[
Q_1'=(Q_1-\{uw,wv\})\cup\{uv\},
\]

and suppressing the ear in `Q_2` gives

\[
Q_2'=(Q_2-\{uw,wz\})\cup\{uz\}.
\]

The principal root `P` is also a minimal `R(B)` root.  These three roots are distinct: `Q_1'` contains the newly added `uv` but not `uz`, `Q_2'` contains `uz` but not `uv`, and `P` contains neither.

We now classify any `A\in R(B)` according to its use of the two new edges.

* If `A` contains neither, then it is an `R(G)` forest avoiding all three spokes, hence `A=P`.
* If `A` contains `uv` but not `uz`, subdivide `uv` into the path `u-w-v`.  Subdivision preserves acyclicity and all three tests “add `e`”, “add `f`”, and “add both”, so the expanded forest lies in `R(G)` and contains exactly the spoke pair `{uw,wv}`.  The only such `R(G)` member is the singleton root `Q_1`.  Therefore `A=Q_1'`.
* Similarly, if `A` contains `uz` but not `uv`, then `A=Q_2'`.

If `R(B)` consisted only of `P,Q_1',Q_2'`, it would have cardinality three but **three distinct minimal roots**, contradicting the archived universal `|R|=3` theorem.  Hence there is another `A\in R(B)`, and by the classification above it must contain **both** `uv` and `uz`.

Write

\[
F=A\setminus\{uv,uz\}.
\]

The two edges `{uv,uz}` form a tree connecting the three terminals `u,v,z`.  Replace that tree by the three-spoke tree

\[
\{uw,wv,wz\}.
\]

These two tree gadgets induce the same connectivity partition on the old terminals: all three are connected.  Since `A` is a forest, `F` cannot already connect any two of `u,v,z`; therefore the replacement is again a forest.  More generally, on all old vertices the two unions have exactly the same connectivity relation.  Consequently adding `e`, adding `f`, or adding both has exactly the same cycle/no-cycle outcome before and after the replacement.

Thus

\[
A' =F\cup\{uw,wv,wz\}\in R(G).
\]

But no member of the six-element family (4.1)–(4.2) contains all three spokes.  This is a contradiction.  Therefore at least one of `uv,uz` exists.
