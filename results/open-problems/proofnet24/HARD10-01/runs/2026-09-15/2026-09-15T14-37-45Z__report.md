## 1. Exact target

Let `G=(V,E)` be any finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, with isolated vertices allowed. For every distinct pair `e,f in E`, prove or refute

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\ A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every finite simple graph and every edge pair.  The stronger I-Rayleigh statement asks for nonnegativity at every positive edge-weight evaluation.

## 4. Reused exact five-class reduction

Fix `e,f` and let `H=G\setminus\{e,f\}`.  Every forest `A` of `H` lies in exactly one class:

* `N0`: neither `e` nor `f` is addable;
* `Ne`: `e` is addable, `f` is not;
* `Nf`: `f` is addable, `e` is not;
* `B`: both are jointly addable;
* `R`: each is separately addable but adding both creates a cycle.

Using the same letters for the corresponding generating polynomials,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=N_eN_f+R(N_e+N_f+B+R)-BN_0.
}\tag{4.1}
\]

At unit weights, with `T=N0+Ne+Nf+B+R`, and with `U,V` indicating that the endpoints of `e,f` respectively are already connected in the `H` forest,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=TR-T^2\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V).
}\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the exact pair target is

\[
\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V)\le \Pr_H(R).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Earlier checkpoints already settle `R=0`, `R=1`, and the comparable `|R|=2` case.  The only first unresolved stratum was therefore exactly two incomparable `R` forests.

## 5. Reused terminal-pair description and two-terminal calculus

For disjoint distinguished edges

\[
e=ab,\qquad f=cd,
\]

an `H`-forest lies in `R` iff the four terminals occupy exactly two components and the two distinguished edges join the same two components.  Thus an `R` forest realizes exactly one of the two cross pairings

\[
ac\mid bd
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
ad\mid bc.
\tag{5.1}
\]

An inclusion-minimal `R` forest is precisely the disjoint union of the two simple paths realizing its pairing.

For a two-terminal graph `K` with terminals `s,t`, let `D_K` and `C_K` denote respectively the terminal-disconnected and terminal-connected forest polynomials.  For `e in E(K)` define

\[
\alpha_K(e;s,t)=D_K(C_K)_e-C_K(D_K)_e.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `h` is a virtual terminal edge, then

\[
\alpha_K(e;s,t)=\Delta_{K+h}\{e,h\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The backfilled exact substitution identities are:

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=\alpha_K(e;s,t)\Delta_H\{g,f\}

\tag{5.4}

when an outside edge `g=st` is replaced by a two-terminal network `K` containing `e`, and

\boxed{

## 6. NEW LEMMA L14 — minimality, common support, and cross-edge necessity

Assume the entire `R` class consists of exactly two incomparable forests

\[
R=\{P,Q\}.
\]

Then:

1. `P,Q` are inclusion-minimal `R` forests;
2. `P,Q` have exactly the same edge-support vertex set;
3. every edge of `Q\setminus P` joins the two connected components of `P`;
4. symmetrically, every edge of `P\setminus Q` joins the two connected components of `Q`.

### Proof

If `P` were not inclusion-minimal, deleting an edge outside its two terminal paths would yield a strictly smaller `R` forest.  Because `Q` is incomparable with `P`, that smaller forest is neither `P` nor `Q`, contradicting `|R|=2`.  Thus both witnesses are minimal.

Suppose a vertex `v` is incident to an edge of `P` but to no edge of `Q`.  Then `v` is isolated in `Q`.  Choose any `P` edge `h` incident to `v`.  The forest `Q+h` is still acyclic and merely attaches an isolated vertex (or two isolated vertices) to one existing component; it does not merge the two terminal components.  Therefore `Q+h` remains in `R`.  Since `Q+h` strictly contains `Q` and cannot equal incomparable `P`, this is a third `R` forest.  Hence the supports coincide.

Now let `h in Q\setminus P`.  If both endpoints of `h` lie in the same component of the forest `P`, then `P+h` has a unique simple cycle `C_h`.  Because the graph is simple and `h notin P`, this cycle contains at least two `P` edges.  For every `g in C_h\setminus\{h\}`, the set

\[
P+h-g
\]

is again a forest with the same two terminal-connected components as `P`, hence lies in `R`.  Distinct choices of `g` give distinct `R` forests containing `h`; at most one can be `Q`, and none is `P`.  This contradicts `|R|=2`.  Therefore every `Q\setminus P` edge crosses the component cut of `P`.  Interchanging `P,Q` proves the symmetric statement.  QED.

## 7. NEW LEMMA L15 — inversion switching

Orient the two path components of the minimal witness `P` as

\[
X:a\to c,
\qquad
Y:b\to d,
\]

so `P` realizes pairing `ac|bd`.

Every edge of `Q\setminus P` crosses between `X` and `Y` by L14.  Write two such edges as

\[
h_1=x_1y_1,
\qquad
h_2=x_2y_2,
\]

with `x_i in X`, `y_i in Y`.  If

\[
x_1\text{ precedes }x_2\text{ on }X,
\qquad
y_2\text{ precedes }y_1\text{ on }Y,
\tag{7.1}
\]

then the union of the two paths

\[
X[a,x_1]+h_1+Y[y_1,d]
\]

and

\[
Y[b,y_2]+h_2+X[x_2,c]
\]

is an `R` forest realizing the **opposite** pairing `ad|bc`.

### Proof

The two displayed paths are simple.  Their `X` portions are disjoint because `x_1` precedes `x_2`; their `Y` portions are disjoint because `y_2` precedes `y_1`.  The cross edges have distinct endpoints under the strict inequalities.  Hence the two paths are vertex-disjoint.  They connect `a` to `d` and `b` to `c`, so by the terminal-pair criterion their union is an `R` forest.  QED.

Call such a pair of cross edges an **inversion pair**.

## 8. NEW THEOREM L16 — two incomparable `R` witnesses must have opposite pairings

Under the assumptions `R={P,Q}` and `P,Q` incomparable, the two witnesses cannot realize the same terminal pairing.

### Proof

Assume for contradiction that both realize `ac|bd`.  By L15, there can be no inversion pair among the cross edges `Q\setminus P`, because an inversion would create a third `R` forest of the opposite pairing.

Since `Q ne P`, choose an edge `h=xy in Q\setminus P` whose endpoint `x` is earliest along `X` from `a`; among all cross edges incident to this earliest `x`, choose `y` earliest along `Y` from `b`.

There is no cross edge with a `Y` endpoint earlier than `y`: such an edge cannot have an earlier `X` endpoint by minimality of `x`, and if it has a later `X` endpoint it forms an inversion with `h`, forbidden above.  Thus `h` is simultaneously earliest on both oriented paths.

All `P` edges on the prefix from `a` to `x` must belong to `Q`.  Otherwise the `Q` component containing `a` would be blocked before reaching `x`, because L14 says every non-`P` edge of `Q` is a cross edge and there is no earlier cross edge through which the path can leave the `X` prefix.  Similarly every `P` edge on the prefix from `b` to `y` belongs to `Q`.

Hence `a` is connected to `x` in `Q`, and `b` is connected to `y` in `Q`.  Since `h=xy` belongs to `Q`, the vertices `a,b` lie in the same component of `Q`.  But `e=ab` is separately addable in every `R` forest, so its endpoints must lie in different components.  Contradiction.

Therefore `P,Q` realize opposite pairings.  QED.

This completes the same-pairing exclusion that remained open in the unsaved 12:16 draft.

## 9. NEW THEOREM L17 — exact four-edge exchange classification

Then there exist vertices `x_1,x_2` on `X` and `y_2,y_1` on `Y`, in the orders

a\cdots x_1-x_2\cdots c,

b\cdots y_2-y_1\cdots d,
\tag{9.1}

such that

\[
\boxed{
P\setminus Q=\{x_1x_2,\ y_2y_1\},
\qquad
Q\setminus P=\{x_1y_1,\ x_2y_2\}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

In particular `P triangle Q` is exactly a 4-cycle.

### Proof

If the cross edges `Q\setminus P` contained no inversion pair, the earliest-edge argument from L16 would again force `a,b` into the same component of `Q`, impossible because every `R` forest separates the endpoints of `e`.  Thus an inversion pair exists.

Choose an inversion pair

\[
h_1=x_1y_1,
\qquad
h_2=x_2y_2
\]

with the order (7.1).  By L15, the forest

\[
F=X[a,x_1]\cup\{h_1\}\cup Y[y_1,d]
\cup Y[b,y_2]\cup\{h_2\}\cup X[x_2,c]
\tag{9.3}
\]

lies in `R` and has the opposite pairing.  It contains cross edges, so `F ne P`; because the entire `R` class is `{P,Q}`, necessarily

\[
F=Q.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Consequently `Q` has no non-`P` edges other than `h_1,h_2`, and it contains exactly the four displayed `P` tails.

By L14 the supports of `P` and `Q` coincide.  If the `P` subpath strictly between `x_1` and `x_2` had an internal vertex, that vertex would be incident to no edge of the forest `F=Q`, contradicting common support.  Hence `x_1,x_2` are adjacent on `P`.  The same argument shows that `y_2,y_1` are adjacent.  Thus the only `P` edges omitted by `Q` are exactly `x_1x_2` and `y_2y_1`, proving (9.2).  QED.

## 10. NEW THEOREM L18 — complete decorated-`K_4` decomposition when `|R|=2` incomparable

Let

\[
A=X\cap U,\quad B=Y\cap V,\quad C=X\cap V,\quad D=Y\cap U,
\tag{10.1}
\]

where `X,Y` are the two components of `P`, and `U,V` are the two components of `Q`, labeled so that

\[
a\in A,\quad b\in B,\quad c\in C,\quad d\in D.
\]

Then, after deleting isolated vertices, the **entire graph `G`** is obtained from `K_4` by replacing two opposite core edges by two arbitrary two-terminal networks `K_e` and `K_f`, containing `e` and `f` respectively; the other four core edges remain single edges.

More precisely, the four branch vertices are

\[
x_1\in A,\quad y_2\in B,\quad x_2\in C,\quad y_1\in D.
\]

The four single core edges are

\[
x_1x_2,\quad y_2y_1,\quad x_1y_1,\quad x_2y_2.
\tag{10.2}
\]

All remaining non-isolated edges lie either in the two-terminal subgraph on `A\cup B` with terminals `x_1,y_2` and distinguished edge `e=ab`, or in the two-terminal subgraph on `C\cup D` with terminals `x_2,y_1` and distinguished edge `f=cd`.

### Proof

The common forest `P\cap Q` has exactly four path components `A,B,C,D`: L17 says the only `P` edges removed in `Q` are `x_1x_2` and `y_2y_1`, so those cuts split the two `P` paths into the four displayed pieces.  Likewise the two `Q\setminus P` edges connect `A` to `D` and `C` to `B`.

First there can be no non-isolated vertex outside the common support of `P,Q`.  If an edge `h` had an endpoint outside that support, then `P+h` would remain acyclic and would merely attach an isolated outside vertex without merging the two terminal components.  Hence `P+h` would be a third `R` forest, contradiction.

Now take any edge `h` of `G` outside `P\cup Q\cup\{e,f\}`.  If both endpoints lie in the same component `X` or the same component `Y` of `P`, then the cycle-exchange argument from L14 creates at least two new `R` forests containing `h`, contradiction.  Therefore `h` crosses the `P` partition `X|Y`.  By the symmetric argument applied to `Q`, `h` also crosses the `Q` partition `U|V`.

An edge crossing **both** bipartitions can only join cells whose two binary labels both differ.  With the cell labels in (10.1), the only possibilities are

\[
A\leftrightarrow B
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
C\leftrightarrow D.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Thus every extra edge is confined to one of these two diagonal regions.  Edges inside a single cell, between `A-C` or `B-D`, or between `A-D` or `B-C`, are ruled out by the same-component cycle-exchange argument for `P` or `Q` unless they are already one of the four witness-core edges in (10.2).

Hence the whole non-isolated graph decomposes into:

* a two-terminal network `K_e` on `A\cup B`, with terminals `x_1,y_2`, containing the distinguished edge `e=ab`;
* a two-terminal network `K_f` on `C\cup D`, with terminals `x_2,y_1`, containing `f=cd`;
* the four single edges in (10.2).

Those six pieces are exactly a `K_4` with two opposite edges replaced by `K_e,K_f`.  QED.

This proves the structural conjecture that had only been computationally suggested in the backfilled drafts.

## 12. NEW THEOREM L19 — every edge-minimal counterexample has `|R|>=3`

Assume, for contradiction, that the full Grimmett conjecture is false.  Choose a counterexample pair `(G,e,f)` with the minimum possible number of edges.  Then its `R` class has at least three members.

### Proof

The cases `R=0`, `R=1`, and comparable `|R|=2` were already proved nonnegative in earlier checkpoints.  It remains to exclude exactly two incomparable `R` forests.

By L18, `G` is a decorated opposite-edge `K_4` with two distinguished two-terminal networks `K_e,K_f` and four single core edges.  Formula (11.2) gives

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=6\alpha_e\alpha_f.
\]

We show each terminal response is nonnegative by edge-minimality.

Consider `K_e` with terminals `s,t`.

* If the distinguished edge `e` itself is the direct terminal edge `st`, then `D_e=0` and `C_e=D`, so
  \[
  \alpha_e=D^2>0.
  \]
* Otherwise, if `K_e` contains a direct terminal edge `r=st`, then
  \[
  \alpha_e=\Delta_{K_e}\{e,r\}.
  \]
  The graph `K_e` has strictly fewer edges than `G`, so minimality of `G` gives `\alpha_e\ge0` at unit weights.
* If `K_e` has no direct terminal edge, adjoin one new simple edge `r=st`.  Then by (5.3)
  \[
  \alpha_e=\Delta_{K_e+r}\{e,r\}.
  \]
  The augmented graph has `|E(K_e)|+1` edges, still strictly fewer than `G` because `G` also contains the entire nonempty `K_f` piece and the four single core edges.  Minimality again gives `\alpha_e\ge0`.

The same argument gives `\alpha_f\ge0`.  Hence (11.2) implies `\Delta_G\{e,f\}\ge0`, contradicting that `G` is a counterexample.

Therefore an edge-minimal counterexample cannot have `|R|<=2`, and necessarily

\[
\boxed{|R|\ge3.}
\tag{12.1}
\]

QED.

This is a genuine reduction of the full conjecture: any future counterexample search or proof by minimal obstruction may discard all configurations with zero, one, or two `R` witnesses.
