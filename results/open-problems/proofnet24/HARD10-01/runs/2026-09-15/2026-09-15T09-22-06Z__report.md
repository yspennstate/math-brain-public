## 1. Exact target and present status

For every finite simple graph G=(V,E) and every pair of distinct edges e,f, let F be uniform over all acyclic edge subsets of E.  The target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the independent-set/forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every G,e,f.  The stronger I-Rayleigh conjecture asks for nonnegativity at every positive evaluation of all edge variables.

The full arbitrary-graph target remains OPEN in this checkpoint.  A fresh bounded literature check on 2026-09-15 found no full resolution. ## 3. Cumulative exact five-class reduction

Fix e,f and write H=G\setminus\{e,f\}.  Every forest A of H belongs to exactly one of five classes:

* N0: neither e nor f can be added while retaining a forest;
* Ne: e can be added and f cannot;
* Nf: f can be added and e cannot;
* B: both can be added jointly;
* R: e and f can each be added separately, but A+e+f is cyclic.

Use the same letters for the corresponding generating polynomials in the H-edge variables.  Direct extension counting gives the exact Rayleigh identity

\[
\boxed{\Delta_G\{e,f\}=N_eN_f+R(N_e+N_f+B+R)-BN_0.}\tag{3.1}
\]

At unit weights, with

\[
T=N_0+N_e+N_f+B+R
\]

and under the uniform forest measure on H,

\[
U=1\{\text{endpoints of e are connected}\},\qquad
V=1\{\text{endpoints of f are connected}\},
\]

one has

\[
T^2\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V)=N_0(B+R)-N_eN_f,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\Delta_G\{e,f\}=TR-T^2\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V).}\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the exact unit-weight target for this pair is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}_H(U,V)\le \Pr_H(R).}\tag{3.3}
\]

The existing 10-vertex certificate nearly saturates coefficient 1 in (3.3), so a uniform strengthening with a fixed c<1 is not a viable general route.

## 4. Re-derived theorem: singleton R has an exact weighted factorization

### Theorem L4 — PROVED

Assume the R-class consists of a single forest P.  Then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=\mathbf y^P\prod_{g\in P}(1+y_g).
}\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular, at unit weights,

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=2^{|P|}>0.
\]

### Proof

Since P is a forest, P+e and P+f are forests, and P+e+f is dependent.  Hence P+e+f has exactly one cycle C, and C contains both e and f.  If g\in P\setminus C, then P-g still belongs to R, contradicting singleton R.  Thus

\[
P=C\setminus\{e,f\}.\tag{4.2}
\]

If C' were a second simple cycle containing both e,f, then C'\setminus\{e,f\} would be a second R-forest.  Hence C is the unique e,f-cycle.

Delete e,f from C.  The remainder P consists of two path components Q1,Q2 (allowing one component to be a singleton vertex when e,f are adjacent).  Any edge h outside C must join Q1 to Q2.  Indeed:

* if h has an endpoint outside V(C), then P+h is a second R-forest;
* if both endpoints of h lie on the same Qi, then h together with the Qi subpath between its endpoints yields a second e,f-cycle;
* h cannot be a loop because G is simple.

Orient Q1,Q2 consistently from the e-side to the f-side.  Two outside cross edges cannot have inverted endpoint order: if h=a_i b_j and h'=a_k b_l with i<k but j>l, the two cross edges together with disjoint initial/final path segments produce another simple cycle through e,f.  Therefore all cross edges are noncrossing in the rectangle drawing with Q1,Q2 as opposite boundary paths.  The non-isolated graph is outerplanar, hence series-parallel.

Erickson's Theorem 1.2 therefore supplies Wagner's proved SOS identity for this graph:

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=
\sum_{S\in\mathscr S}\mathbf y^S
\left(
\sum_{A\in\mathscr A(S)}c(S,C_A)\mathbf y^{A-S}
\right)^2.
\]

Because C is the unique e,f-cycle,

\[
\mathscr S=\{S:S\subseteq P\}.
\]

Because R has only P, every inner A-set is P itself.  Thus each inner sum contains one monomial, the sign disappears on squaring, and

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
&=\sum_{S\subseteq P}\mathbf y^S(\mathbf y^{P-S})^2\\
&=\mathbf y^P\sum_{T\subseteq P}\mathbf y^T\\
&=\mathbf y^P\prod_{g\in P}(1+y_g).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (4.1).  No open SOS conjecture is used; Erickson's theorem is applied only after the graph is proved series-parallel.  QED.

## 5. NEW THEOREM: nested two-R case reduces exactly to singleton R

### Theorem L5 — PROVED

Suppose the R-class contains exactly two forests and they are nested:

\[
R=\{P,Q\},\qquad P\subsetneq Q.
\]

Then necessarily

\[
Q=P\cup\{h\}
\]

for one edge h, and h is a bridge of G.  Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
=(1+y_h)^2\,\mathbf y^P\prod_{g\in P}(1+y_g).
}\tag{5.1}
\]

At unit weights,

\[
\boxed{\Delta_G\{e,f\}=4\,2^{|P|}>0.}\tag{5.2}
\]

Thus the full weighted pairwise Rayleigh inequality holds whenever |R|=2 and the two R-forests are comparable.

### Proof

Because P is the smaller R-forest and there are only two R-forests, P is inclusion-minimal.  As in the first paragraph of the proof of L4, the unique cycle in P+e+f is C and minimality forces

\[
P=C\setminus\{e,f\}.
\]

If |Q\setminus P|\ge2, choose h\in Q\setminus P.  Since Q is a forest, deleting all other edges of Q\setminus(P\cup\{h\}) leaves the forest P+h.  The original cycle C remains present in (P+h)+e+f, while e and f remain separately addable because they were separately addable to Q and deletion cannot create a cycle.  Hence P+h belongs to R.  It is distinct from P and Q, contradiction.  Therefore

\[
Q=P+h
\]

for one edge h.

The forest P=C\setminus\{e,f\} has two connected components on the cycle vertices.  Since P+h is a forest, h cannot have both endpoints in the same component of P.  Since P+h is itself in R, h also cannot join the two components: doing so would make the endpoints of each of e and f already connected and would make at least one of e,f non-addable.  Hence h has an endpoint x outside V(C).

If h were not a bridge of G, there would be an edge g\ne h incident to x on an alternate path leaving x after deleting h.  But x is isolated in P, so P+g is a forest.  Moreover g is a pendant/disjoint attachment to the cycle forest P and cannot destroy separate addability of e or f; adding both still contains the original cycle C.  Thus P+g is a third R-forest, contradiction.  Hence h is a bridge.

A bridge belongs to no cycle, so its inclusion is an independent binary choice in every forest:

\[
I_G=(1+y_h)I_{G-h}.
\]

Since e,f\ne h,

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(1+y_h)^2\Delta_{G-h}\{e,f\}.
\]

Deleting h removes Q and leaves singleton R={P}.  Apply L4 to G-h to obtain (5.1).  QED.

## 6. NEW STRUCTURAL LEMMA: two incomparable R-forests force disjoint distinguished edges

### Lemma L6 — PROVED

Suppose the R-class contains exactly two forests P,Q and they are incomparable under inclusion.  Then e and f are vertex-disjoint.

### Proof

Assume instead that e and f share a vertex x, and let a,b be their other endpoints.  Because P,Q are the only R-forests and are incomparable, neither can have an off-cycle edge: deleting such an edge would produce a third R-forest.  Therefore P and Q are precisely two distinct simple a-b paths avoiding x; adjoining e,f closes the corresponding cycles through x.

Consider the standard decomposition of the union of two simple a-b paths into common subpaths separated by maximal divergence–reconvergence bubbles.  In each bubble the P-segment and Q-segment are internally vertex-disjoint paths with the same endpoints.

If there are at least two bubbles, choose the P-branch in one bubble and the Q-branch in another, keeping the common portions elsewhere.  This produces a third simple a-b path, hence a third R-forest, contradiction.

So there is exactly one bubble.  The two paths differ only by two internally vertex-disjoint u-v branches A and B.  Because G is simple, A and B cannot both consist of the same single u-v edge.  Hence one branch, say A, has at least two edges and contains an internal vertex not lying on Q.  Choose an edge h of A incident to such an internal vertex.  Then Q+h is still a forest: h has an endpoint outside the vertex set of Q's bubble branch and therefore cannot close a cycle in Q.  The forest Q+h still contains the complete a-b path Q, so e and f are individually addable while adding both closes the Q-cycle.  Thus Q+h is an R-forest distinct from P and Q, contradiction.

Therefore e,f cannot share a vertex.  QED.

This identifies the first unresolved |R|=2 configuration much more sharply: it consists of two incomparable R-forests and the distinguished edges are opposite/disjoint.

## 7. NEW GENERAL LEMMA: arbitrary two-terminal edge replacement acts by a positive effective weight

This lemma is independent of the cardinality of R and is useful for reducing complicated pair-local graphs to a small core.

### Lemma L7 — PROVED

Let H be a graph with a distinguished edge g=st, and let K be any finite graph sharing with H only the two terminals s,t and having otherwise disjoint vertices and edges.  Form G by deleting g from H and inserting K.

Let

\[
D_K=\sum_{B\subseteq E(K):\,B\text{ forest},\ s\not\leftrightarrow t}\mathbf y^B
\]

and

\[
C_K=\sum_{B\subseteq E(K):\,B\text{ forest},\ s\leftrightarrow t}\mathbf y^B.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
I_G=D_K\, I_H\big|_{y_g=C_K/D_K}.
}\tag{7.1}
\]

For any e,f\in E(H)\setminus\{g\},

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=D_K^2\,
\Delta_H\{e,f\}\big|_{y_g=C_K/D_K}.
}\tag{7.2}
\]

At every positive edge-weight evaluation, D_K>0 and C_K/D_K\ge0.  In particular, if the H-pair Rayleigh difference is nonnegative for all nonnegative y_g, then replacing g by an arbitrary two-terminal network preserves the inequality for e,f, with no Rayleigh assumption on the internal network K.

### Proof

A forest A of H-g has the terminals s,t either disconnected or connected.  The derivative (I_H)_g is exactly the generating polynomial of those A for which s,t are disconnected, because A+g is a forest precisely in that case.  Hence

\[
I_H^{g}-(I_H)_g
\]

is the polynomial of H-g forests with s,t connected.

If A disconnects s,t, it can be combined with any K-forest; if A connects s,t, it can only be combined with a K-forest that disconnects s,t, otherwise the two terminal connections create a cycle.  Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_G
&=(I_H)_g(D_K+C_K)+\big(I_H^{g}-(I_H)_g\big)D_K\\
&=D_K I_H^{g}+C_K(I_H)_g\\
&=D_K\left(I_H^{g}+\frac{C_K}{D_K}(I_H)_g\right),
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (7.1), since I_H=I_H^g+y_g(I_H)_g.

The factor D_K is independent of e,f.  Differentiate (7.1) with respect to y_e,y_f; every term in the Rayleigh difference acquires D_K^2 and the same substitution y_g=C_K/D_K.  This proves (7.2).  QED.
