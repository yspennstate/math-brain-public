## 5. NEW LEMMA L52 — third-spoke lemma for an exact-two decorated `K_4`

\[

### Proof

Use the four-cell decomposition from the exact-two theorem. Relabel the four literal exchange edges so that the two roots differ by

\[
P\setminus Q=\{up,qv\},
\qquad
Q\setminus P=\{uv,pq\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The common forest contains two disjoint simple paths:

* `E`, from `u` to `q`, containing the distinguished edge `e`;
* `F`, from `p` to `v`, containing the distinguished edge `f`.

Every support vertex lies on `E` or `F`.

Suppose first that `z\in V(E)`. Split `E` at `z`. Exactly one of the two subpaths `E[z,u]` and `E[z,q]` contains `e`. Consider the two simple cycles

\[
(wz)\cup E[z,u]\cup(up)\cup F[p,v]\cup(vw),
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
(wz)\cup E[z,q]\cup(qp)\cup F[p,v]\cup(vw).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Both contain `f`, and exactly one contains `e`. Hence one is a new common `e,f` cycle.

If `z\in V(F)`, use instead

\[
(wz)\cup F[z,p]\cup(pu)\cup E[u,q]\cup(qv)\cup(vw),
\tag{5.4}

and

\[
(wz)\cup F[z,v]\cup(vq)\cup E[q,u]\cup(uw).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Both contain `e`, and exactly one contains `f`. Again a third common cycle exists. QED.

## 7. NEW LEMMA L53 — every size-three root fiber is a two-edge ear

### Statement

If a root `P=C_P\setminus\{e,f\}` has fiber of cardinality exactly three, then

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+h,P+k\}
\]

and the two extension edges have the form

\[
\boxed{h=uw,\qquad k=wv}
\tag{7.1}
\]

for one vertex `w\notin V(C_P)` and distinct `u,v\in V(C_P)`. Moreover these are the only `H`-edges meeting vertices outside `V(C_P)`.

### Proof

Downward closure rules out a two-edge extension in a three-element fiber: if `P+h+k` belonged to the fiber, then `P,P+h,P+k,P+h+k` would give at least four members. Hence the two strict members are one-edge extensions.

By the one-edge extension criterion, `h,k` are exactly the two `H`-edges with an endpoint outside `V(C_P)`.

Since `P+h+k\notin R`, the two edges cannot be two independent tree attachments (or a longer dangling path attached at only one cycle vertex), because then their union with `P` would still be a forest and would leave the two terminal components unchanged. The only way the double extension can fail is that the two edges share an outside endpoint `w` and return to the cycle at two distinct vertices `u,v`. If `u,v` lie in the same component of `P`, the ear plus the `P` path creates a cycle; if they lie in different components, the ear merges the two terminal components and destroys separate addability. Either way the double extension is excluded, exactly as required. QED.

This gives a degree-two outside ear for every size-three fiber.
