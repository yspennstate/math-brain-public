## 1. Exact target and new result

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices.  For distinct edges `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}:=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative for every finite simple graph and distinct `e,f`.

The full arbitrary-graph conjecture remains OPEN.

## 4. Root-fiber setup

Put

\[
H=G\setminus\{e,f\}.
\]

The class `R` consists of those `H`-forests `A` for which `A+e` and `A+f` are forests but `A+e+f` is cyclic.  Every `A\in R` contains a unique minimal root `P`; the fiber of `P` is

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{A\in R:P\subseteq A\}.
\]

It is a finite down-set above `P`.

Assume throughout Sections 5–9 that the fiber partition is exactly

\[
7+1.
\]

Let `P` be the size-seven root and `Q` the singleton root.

## 5. NEW LEMMA L73 — classification of a size-seven fiber

### Statement

A size-seven root fiber has only two possible forms:

**Type A: six-spoke star**

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+h_1,\ldots,P+h_6\},
\]

and all six `h_i` share one outside endpoint `w\notin V(C_P)`.

**Type D: three-atom boundary**

\[
\mathcal F(P)
=
\{P,
P+h_1,P+h_2,P+h_3,
P+h_1+h_2,P+h_1+h_3,P+h_2+h_3\},
\]

while

\[
P+h_1+h_2+h_3\notin R.
\]

No other seven-element down-set can occur as a root fiber.

### Proof

Let `k` be the number of one-edge extensions (atoms) of the fiber.

A down-set with `k<=2` has at most four members, so `k>=3`.

* If `k=3`, the root plus the three atoms contributes four members.  To reach seven, all three doubles must be present and the triple absent.  This is Type D.
* If `k=4`, the root plus four atoms contributes five members.  A triple would force all three of its doubles by downward closure and would already push the size beyond seven.  Hence exactly two doubles are present.
* If `k=5`, exactly one double is present.
* If `k=6`, no larger extension is present.  Every atom pair is bad.  By L68 every pair is a two-edge ear.  Fix `h_1,h_2`; they share one outside endpoint `w`.  Any other `h_i` forms a bad pair with `h_1`, and L68 forces it to share the unique outside endpoint of `h_1`, namely the same `w`.  Thus this is Type A.

It remains to rule out the `k=4` and `k=5` abstract shapes.

For `k=5`, let `a,b` be the unique allowed double.  Choose a third atom `c`.  Both `a+c` and `b+c` are absent.  L68 applied to `(a,c)` and `(b,c)` says `c` has one cycle endpoint and one outside endpoint `w`, and both `a,b` share that same `w`.  Their cycle endpoints are distinct because the graph is simple.  Hence `a,b` themselves form a two-edge ear, so `P+a+b\notin R`, contradiction.

For `k=4`, consider the graph on the four atoms whose edges are the two allowed doubles.  Whether those two allowed pairs are adjacent or disjoint, one of them, say `a,b`, has a third atom `c` that is nonadjacent to both `a` and `b` in this allowed-pair graph.  Thus `a+c` and `b+c` are absent.  The same L68 argument forces `a,b` to form an ear, contradicting that `a+b` is allowed.

Therefore only Types A and D remain. QED.

## 6. NEW LEMMA L74 — Type A is impossible in a `7+1` family

### Statement

Under the exact partition `7+1`, the size-seven fiber cannot be Type A.

### Proof

Suppose

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+wu_1,\ldots,P+wu_6\},
\]

with six distinct attachment vertices `u_i\in V(C_P)` and one outside center `w`.

The singleton common cycle `C_Q` cannot avoid `w`: if it did, every spoke `wu_i` would have an endpoint outside `V(C_Q)` and hence would be a one-edge `R`-extension of `Q`, contradicting singleton status.

A simple cycle through `w` uses exactly two incident spokes.  Therefore `C_Q` uses exactly two of the six spokes, leaving four unused spokes `wx_1,\ldots,wx_4`.

Because `Q` is singleton, an unused spoke `wx_i` cannot have its attachment vertex outside `V(C_Q)`; otherwise the one-edge criterion applied to `Q` would give `Q+wx_i\in R`.  Hence each `x_i` lies on `C_Q`.  In particular all four `x_i` are vertices of `C_P`.

Deleting `e,f` from `C_P` gives two path components.  Among four distinct vertices `x_1,\ldots,x_4`, two, say `x,y`, lie in the same component.  The `x-y` subpath in that component avoids both distinguished edges, so the complementary `x-y` arc of `C_P` contains both `e,f`.  Adding the two spokes `xw,wy` to that complementary arc yields a simple cycle containing both `e,f`.

This common cycle is distinct from `C_Q` because it uses two spokes that `C_Q` does not use.  Hence it gives another minimal root besides `P,Q`, contradicting the `7+1` partition. QED.

Thus the size-seven fiber is necessarily Type D.

## 7. NEW LEMMA L75 — the Type-D triple is a length-three ear and the singleton uses all of it

Write the three atoms as

\[
T=\{h_1,h_2,h_3\}.
\]

Every proper subset of `T` extends `P` inside `R`, while the full triple does not.

### Statement

Under the `7+1` partition, the three atom edges form a simple path

\[
\boxed{u-x-y-v}
\]

of length three, where

\[
u,v\in V(C_P),\qquad x,y\notin V(C_P).
\]

The singleton root `Q` uses all three edges of this path.

Moreover, the only graph edges having an endpoint outside `V(C_P)` are the three edges of `T`; hence `x,y` have degree two in the non-isolated part of the graph.

### Proof

By the one-edge criterion, each `h_i` has an endpoint outside `V(C_P)`.  Because every one- and two-edge subset of `T` gives an `R`-forest but the full triple does not, the first obstruction occurs only when all three edges are present.

There are two ways for `P+T` to fail the `R` conditions:

1. it becomes cyclic; or
2. it stays a forest but connects the two path components of `P`, so `e` and `f` are no longer separately addable.

Minimality with respect to deletion of any one of the three edges forces all three edges to lie in the unique witness for that failure.  With three edges, the witness can only be:

* a three-edge path whose two endpoints lie on `C_P`; or
* a three-cycle made entirely of the new edges.

A branching three-edge tree cannot be minimally responsible: if two of its leaves attach to the old forest, the two-edge path between those leaves is already a bad pair, while with fewer than two old attachments the triple cannot create a cycle or join the two old components.

In the path case, the two internal vertices are outside `V(C_P)`: otherwise an end edge of the path would have both endpoints on `C_P`, contrary to the one-edge extension criterion.  Thus the path is `u-x-y-v` as claimed.

In the three-cycle case, at most one triangle vertex lies on `C_P`, because every triangle edge must have an endpoint outside `V(C_P)`.  Also, there is no other graph edge incident to any vertex outside `C_P`: any such edge `g` would itself satisfy `P+g\in R` and would be a fourth atom, impossible.

Therefore a common `e,f` cycle distinct from `C_P` cannot traverse the three-cycle obstruction: it is either detached from `C_P` or attached at only one old vertex.  Hence `C_Q` would avoid all of `T`.  But then a triangle edge has an endpoint outside `V(C_Q)`, so it extends `Q`, contradicting singleton status.  The triangle case is impossible.

We are left with the path `u-x-y-v`.  Since `x,y` are outside `C_P`, the preceding atom count again implies that their only incident graph edges are the three path edges.

The singleton cycle `C_Q` cannot avoid `T`, since then an edge incident to `x` would extend `Q`.  Once a simple cycle uses one path edge, degree two at `x,y` forces it to traverse the entire path.  Thus `Q` contains all three edges of `T`. QED.

## 8. NEW THEOREM L76 — every `7+1` graph is weighted pair-Rayleigh

Let the length-three path from L75 be

\[
T:u-x-y-v.
\]

There are two cases depending on whether the direct endpoint edge

\[
g=uv
\]

is already in `G`.

### Case I: `g` is present

Delete the three path edges `T` and call the resulting graph `G_0`.

An `R(G_0)`-forest is exactly an `R(G)`-forest using no edge of `T`.  In the size-seven fiber, only `P` avoids all three atoms, while the singleton `Q` uses all three by L75.  Therefore

\[
R(G_0)=\{P\}.
\]

The archived unique-root theorem says `G_0` is weighted pair-Rayleigh for `e,f` at every positive weighting.

Now regard `G` as obtained from `G_0` by deleting the edge `g` and replacing it by the two-terminal network `K` consisting of the direct edge `g` in parallel with the length-three path `T`.  The general two-terminal replacement identity gives

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}
=D_K^2\,
\Delta_{G_0}\{e,f\}\big|_{y_g=C_K/D_K}\ge0.
\]

At unit weights this parallel network is a four-cycle, so

\[
D_K=7,\qquad C_K=8,
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=49\,
\Delta_{G_0}\bigl(y_g=8/7,\ y_{r\ne g}=1\bigr)\ge0.
}
\]

### Case II: `g` is absent

Delete the path `T`, add the new edge `g=uv`, and call the resulting simple graph `B`.

We claim that `R(B)` has exactly two members.

If `A\in R(B)` avoids `g`, then `A` is also an `R(G)`-forest avoiding all three edges of `T`.  Hence `A=P`.

If `A\in R(B)` contains `g`, replace `g` by the path `u-x-y-v`.  Subdividing a forest edge by a path through new degree-two vertices preserves acyclicity and all connectivity relations among the old vertices.  Therefore the expanded forest `A^\uparrow` belongs to `R(G)` and contains all three edges of `T`.  The Type-D fiber contains no triple extension, and by L75 the only `R(G)` member using all of `T` is the singleton root `Q`.  Thus `A^\uparrow=Q`, so `A` is uniquely determined.

Conversely, suppressing the path in `Q` gives that second member of `R(B)`.  Hence

\[
|R(B)|=2.
\]

The archived complete two-element theorem gives weighted pair-Rayleigh for `B` (nested pairs reduce by a bridge and incomparable pairs have the proved decorated opposite-edge `K_4` form).

Finally, `G` is obtained from `B` by replacing `g` by the length-three path `T`.  For a unit-weight three-edge path,

\[
D_T=7,\qquad C_T=1.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=49\,
\Delta_B\bigl(y_g=1/7,\ y_{r\ne g}=1\bigr)\ge0.
}
\]

The same argument works for arbitrary positive internal path weights, so the conclusion is weighted, not merely unit-weight.

This proves L76. QED.
