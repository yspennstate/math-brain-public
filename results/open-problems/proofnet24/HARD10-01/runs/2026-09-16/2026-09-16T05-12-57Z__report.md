## 1. Exact target and result of this run

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all**
acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices. For
distinct edges `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the forest independence polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\qquad
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef},
\]

one wants `Delta_G{e,f}(1)>=0` for every finite simple graph and every
distinct pair.

## 4. Start with a `5+3+1` family

Assume the exact root-fiber partition is

\[
5+3+1.
\]

Let `Q` be the size-three root. By the size-three theorem, after relabelling,

\[
\mathcal F(Q)=\{Q,Q+ux,Q+xv\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
x\notin V(C_Q),\qquad u,v\in V(C_Q).
\]

Call

\[
E_x=\{ux,xv\}
\]

the ear.

Let `P` be the size-five root and `S` the singleton root.

### 4.1 The ear center has graph degree two

If a graph edge `xy` other than `xu,xv` were incident with `x`, then because
`x` is outside `C_Q`, the one-edge extension criterion would make `Q+xy` a
third one-edge extension of `Q`, contradicting (4.1). Hence

\[
\boxed{\deg_G(x)=2.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### 4.2 The singleton root uses the whole ear

If `C_S` avoided `x`, either ear edge would have an endpoint outside `C_S` and
would extend the singleton root, impossible. Therefore `C_S` contains `x`.
By (4.2), every simple cycle through `x` must use both incident edges, so

\[
\boxed{ux,xv\in S.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.3 The size-five root also uses the whole ear

Suppose `C_P` avoided `x`. Then `ux` and `xv` would both be one-edge
extensions of `P`.

The archived size-five classification says the four one-edge extensions of
`P` form a four-spoke star with a common outside center. Since two of those
spokes, `ux` and `xv`, already share the outside endpoint `x`, the common star
center would have to be `x`. The other two star atoms would then also be graph
edges incident with `x` and, because `x` is outside `C_Q`, would be additional
one-edge extensions of `Q`. This contradicts (4.1).

Hence `C_P` contains `x`; by (4.2), it traverses the full ear:

\[
\boxed{ux,xv\in P.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

All five members of the size-five fiber contain the full ear.

## 5. The direct endpoint edge is absent

Let

\[
g=uv.
\]

We claim `g` is not already a graph edge.

Suppose `g in E(G)`. For each of the five members `A` of the size-five fiber,
replace the path `u-x-v` by the edge `g`. Because `x` has degree two, this
suppression preserves all connectivity relations among the old vertices. It
therefore preserves acyclicity and the three addability tests defining `R`.
Thus each suppressed set is again an `R(G)`-forest.

The five suppressed forests are distinct because the members of the size-five
fiber differ outside the ear. They all avoid both `ux` and `xv`.

But among the original nine `R`-members the only forest avoiding both ear
edges is the root `Q` itself:

* `Q+ux` and `Q+xv` use one ear edge;
* all five members of the `P` fiber use both by (4.4);
* `S` uses both by (4.3).

Five distinct `R`-forests cannot all equal `Q`. Contradiction. Therefore

\[
\boxed{uv\notin E(G).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

## 6. Compress the ear: exact partition `5+1+1`

Delete `ux,xv` and insert the new simple edge

\[
g=uv,
\]

producing a graph `B`.

There is an exact bijection between `R(B)` and those `R(G)` states whose ear
parity is `0` or `2`:

* if an `R(B)`-forest avoids `g`, it is unchanged in `G` and must be an
  `R(G)`-forest avoiding both ear edges, hence it is exactly `Q`;
* if an `R(B)`-forest contains `g`, subdividing `g` into `u-x-v` preserves
  acyclicity and all old-vertex connectivity, hence produces an `R(G)`-forest
  containing both ear edges;
* conversely every `R(G)`-forest containing both ear edges suppresses to an
  `R(B)`-forest containing `g`.

The `R(G)` members containing both ear edges are exactly the five members of
the `P` fiber and the singleton `S`. Hence

\[
|R(B)|=7.
\]

Suppression preserves minimality of the three relevant roots. The five
suppressed `P`-fiber members remain one root fiber, while `Q` and the
suppressed singleton remain singleton roots. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{the exact root-fiber partition of }B\text{ is }5+1+1.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

The marked edge `g` is present in all five members of the large fiber and in
the suppressed singleton `S'`, while it is absent from `Q`. Thus

\[
\boxed{g\text{ has }R(B)\text{-incidence }6.}
\tag{6.2}
\]
