## 4. Same-center `4+4+1+1`: reduction to exact-two

Assume an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample has partition

\[
4+4+1+1
\]

and that the two size-four star fibers have the **same** outside center `w`.

Because a star fiber has exactly three one-edge extensions and every graph edge incident with `w` has an endpoint outside the principal root cycle, `w` has degree exactly three. Let its three neighbors be

\[
A=\{a,b,c\}.
\]

Both size-four roots `P,Q` avoid `w`; each singleton root contains `w` and uses exactly two of the three spokes.

Delete `w` and its three spokes. Any `R` forest of the resulting graph is an `R(G)` forest avoiding all three spokes. In the original `4+4+1+1` family the only such members are the roots `P,Q`. Therefore the deletion graph `B` has exactly two incomparable `R` roots:

\[
R(B)=\{P,Q\}.
\]

By the exact-two theorem, the full nonisolated part of `B` is a decorated opposite-edge `K_4`. Write the two common root-support paths as

\[
E:u\leadsto q\quad(e\in E),
\qquad
F:p\leadsto v\quad(f\in F),
\]

and the four literal exchange edges so that

\[
P\setminus Q=\{up,qv\},
\qquad
Q\setminus P=\{uv,pq\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### 4.1 All three star attachments lie on the common support

Take two spokes `wa,wb`. Their double extension is forbidden in the size-four `P`-fiber, so the bad-pair/two-edge-ear lemma places `a,b` on `C_P`. Doing this for all pairs gives

\[
A\subseteq V(C_P).
\]

The same three spokes are the atoms of the `Q`-fiber, so equally

\[
A\subseteq V(C_Q).
\]

Hence

\[
A\subseteq V(C_P)\cap V(C_Q)=V(E)\cup V(F).
\tag{4.2}
\]

### 4.2 Component-pair square

Remove `e,f` from each root cycle. Each root becomes a two-component forest. A support vertex therefore has a pair of component labels, one for `P` and one for `Q`.

The four possible label pairs are exactly the four sides of the two common paths around the distinguished edges: the `u`-side of `E`, the `q`-side of `E`, the `p`-side of `F`, and the `v`-side of `F`. These form a `2x2` component-pair square. Opposite corners lie on the same common path (`E` or `F`).

If two attachments lie in the same component of the root forest `P`, their two spokes together with the complementary arc of `C_P` make a new simple cycle containing both `e,f`. Distinct spoke pairs produce distinct roots. Since only two roots besides `P,Q` exist, the three attachments cannot all lie in one component of `P`; they split `2+1`, and there is a unique same-`P`-component pair. The same is true for `Q`.

Call these unique pairs `J_P,J_Q`. Their associated cycles are precisely the two singleton roots, possibly with `J_P=J_Q`.
