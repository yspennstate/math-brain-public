## 4. New theorem: `5+1+1+1+1` is structurally impossible

Assume an exact root-fiber partition

\[
5+1+1+1+1.
\]

Let the size-five root be `P`.  The archived size-five classification gives

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+wa,P+wb,P+wc,P+wd\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

with one outside center `w` and four distinct attachment vertices `a,b,c,d` on `C_P`.

Every singleton common cycle contains `w`, otherwise all four spokes would be one-edge extensions.  A simple cycle through `w` uses exactly two incident spokes.  Associate to every singleton root its two-element spoke pair, viewed as an edge in a graph `Gamma` on `{a,b,c,d}`.

### 4.1 The four spoke pairs are distinct

Suppose `m>=2` singleton roots use the same pair `{x,y}`.

If direct edge `xy` exists, suppress the ear `x-w-y` in each such singleton.  The suppressed forest lies in `R(G)` and avoids all four spokes.  The only member of the displayed family and the singleton fibers that avoids every spoke is `P`; hence every singleton using `{x,y}` would equal `P-xy+{wx,wy}`, contradicting distinctness.

If `xy` is absent, delete all four spokes and add a fresh edge `g=xy`.  Every `R`-forest avoiding `g` is `P`; every `R`-forest containing `g` expands to one of the `m` singleton roots using `{x,y}`.  Thus the compressed graph has `m+1` minimal roots and `g` has root incidence `m`.

For `m=2`, three all-minimal roots contradict the separator/product theorem.  For `m=3`, four roots factor `2 x 2`, so every edge has even incidence, contradicting incidence three of `g`.  For `m=4`, five all-minimal roots cannot factor as `rs` with `r,s>=2`.  Hence all four spoke pairs are distinct.

### 4.2 The pair graph is not a paw

A simple four-edge graph on four vertices is, up to isomorphism, either a `4`-cycle or a paw (triangle with one pendant edge).

Suppose `Gamma` is a paw, with triangle `ab,ac,bc` and pendant edge `ad`.  Delete the pendant spoke `wd`.  The singleton `Q_ad` disappears, the large fiber becomes the three-spoke star

\[
\{P,P+wa,P+wb,P+wc\},
\]

and the three remaining singleton pairs are `ab,ac,bc`.  This is exact partition `4+1+1+1`, ruled out universally by archived L73.  Therefore `Gamma` is a `4`-cycle.

Relabel cyclically so

\[
\Gamma=\{ab,bc,cd,da\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### 4.3 The direct-pair pattern alternates

Delete `wd`.  The surviving exact `4+1+1` configuration has singleton pairs `ab,bc`.  Archived L59 says exactly one of the direct edges `ab,bc` exists.  Deleting `wa`, `wb`, `wc` in turn gives

\[
[ab]\oplus[bc]=1,\quad
[bc]\oplus[cd]=1,\quad
[cd]\oplus[da]=1,\quad
[da]\oplus[ab]=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus direct-pair status alternates around the `4`-cycle.  After relabeling,

\[
\boxed{ab,cd\in E(G),\qquad bc,da\notin E(G).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In the `4+1+1` graph obtained by deleting `wd`, the `ab` pair is direct and `bc` is absent.  The archived absent-pair singleton lemma says

\[
ab\notin Q_{bc}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Deleting `wa` instead gives the same conclusion for `cd`:

\[
cd\notin Q_{bc}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Hence

\[
\{ab,cd\}\subseteq P\setminus Q_{bc}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

### 4.4 Compress the absent pair and obtain the contradiction

Delete all four spokes and add a fresh edge

\[
g=bc.
\]

Call the resulting graph `B`.

Any `R(B)` forest avoiding `g` is an `R(G)` forest avoiding every spoke, hence is `P`.  Any `R(B)` forest containing `g` expands `g` to `b-w-c` and becomes an `R(G)` forest containing exactly the pair `wb,wc`; the only such member is `Q_{bc}`.  Conversely `Q_{bc}` suppresses to such a root.  Therefore `B` has exactly two incomparable minimal roots, `P` and `Q'_{bc}`.

By (4.7), the exact-two theorem forces

\[
P\setminus Q'_{bc}=\{ab,cd\}.
\]

The four literal exchange edges of the exact-two decorated `K_4` form an alternating `4`-cycle on the endpoints `a,b,c,d`.  Since `g=bc` belongs to `Q'_{bc}\setminus P`, the other exchange edge in that matching must be

\[
ad.
\]

Thus `ad` is a literal graph edge, contradicting (4.4), which says `da` is absent.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{There is no graph/pair with partition }5+1+1+1+1.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

## 7. New theorem: `8+1` is structurally impossible

Let `P` be the size-eight root and `Q` the singleton root.  Let `k` be the number of one-edge extensions (atoms) in the fiber.  Downward closure gives exactly five possible size-eight shapes:

* `k=7`: root plus seven atoms, no doubles;
* `k=6`: root plus six atoms and one double;
* `k=5`: root plus five atoms and two doubles;
* `k=4`: root plus four atoms and three doubles, no triples;
* `k=3`: the full Boolean cube on three atoms (three doubles and the triple).

No other `k` is possible.

### 7.1 `k=7`: seven-spoke star

Every atom pair is bad, so L68 forces all seven atoms to share one outside center `w`.  The singleton uses exactly two spokes and leaves five unused.  Among those five attachment vertices, two lie in the same component of `C_P-\{e,f\}`.  Their complementary root-cycle arc contains `e,f`, and together with their two spokes gives another common `e,f` cycle distinct from `Q`.  Contradiction.

### 7.2 `k=6`: one allowed double

Let `a+b` be the unique allowed double and choose any third atom `c`.  Both `a+c` and `b+c` are absent.  L68 forces `a,c` and `b,c` to be ears.  The atom `c` has a unique outside endpoint, so all three atoms share that endpoint; hence `a,b` also form an ear.  This contradicts that `a+b` is allowed.

### 7.3 `k=5`: two allowed doubles

Choose one allowed pair `a+b`.  There are three further atoms and only one other allowed double, so at least one atom `c` is nonadjacent to both `a,b` in the allowed-double graph.  Thus `a+c` and `b+c` are absent.  The same ear argument forces `a+b` to be absent, contradiction.

### 7.4 `k=4`: three allowed doubles

The allowed-double graph has three edges on four atoms.  If it is a `P_4` or a triangle plus an isolated vertex, it contains an allowed edge `a+b` and a third atom `c` nonadjacent to both endpoints.  Again L68 forces `a+b` to be an ear, contradiction.

The only surviving allowed-double graph is `K_{1,3}`.  Let its center atom be `a`, and let the leaves be `b,c,d`.  All leaf pairs are bad, so `b,c,d` are three spokes with one common outside center `w`.  The center atom `a` forms no ear with any leaf.  Since every atom has an endpoint outside `C_P` and these four atoms are all graph edges incident to outside vertices, `a` has a degree-one outside endpoint unless it is incident with `w`; if it is incident with `w`, its other endpoint must be outside `C_P` (otherwise it would form an ear with each leaf), and that other endpoint has degree one.  Thus `a` is a graph bridge.

But a nondistinguished bridge toggles freely in every `R`-forest, so `|R|` would be even.  Here `|R|=9`.  Contradiction.

### 7.5 `k=3`: full Boolean cube

Let the three atoms be `a,b,c`, and let `T` be their union.  Because the triple extension belongs to `R`, `P+T` is a forest.  Hence the atom subgraph is acyclic.

A connected component of the atom subgraph cannot meet `C_P` in two distinct vertices.  If the two attachment vertices lie in the same component of `P`, the unique atom path between them plus the `P` path creates a cycle.  If they lie in the two different components of `P`, the atom path joins those components, so one of `e,f` is no longer separately addable, contradicting `P+T\in R`.

Thus every atom-tree component meets `C_P` in at most one vertex.  There are no other graph edges incident to outside vertices, because any such edge would be a fourth atom.  Therefore every atom edge is a bridge of the full graph.  Again bridge toggling would make `|R|` even, contradiction.

All five size-eight shapes are impossible under `8+1`.  Hence

\[
\boxed{\text{There is no graph/pair with partition }8+1.}
\tag{7.1}
\]
