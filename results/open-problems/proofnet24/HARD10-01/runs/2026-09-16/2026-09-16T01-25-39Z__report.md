## 4. Correction to archived L56 (`3+3` cross-ear case)

### 4.1 The gap

The 2026-09-15T20-45-10Z checkpoint treated the `3+3` cross-ear case by suppressing the two degree-two ears to direct edges \(g,g'\), then asserting that the compressed graph is simple and has exactly two incomparable roots.

That assertion silently fails when the two ears have the **same endpoint pair**.

This is not a hypothetical pathology. The exact graph

\[
e=01,\qquad f=23,
\]

with edge set

\[
\{01,23,03,14,24,15,25\}
\]

has root-fiber partition

\[
3+3.
\]

The two fibers are generated by the two ears

\[
1-4-2,\qquad 1-5-2,
\]

which have the same endpoints \(\{1,2\}\). Suppressing both ears in a simple graph produces only the single edge \(12\), and the compressed graph has a **unique** \(R\)-root, not two incomparable roots.

Exact forest counts for this witness are

\[
(Z,Z_e,Z_f,Z_{ef})=(114,54,54,24),
\]

so

\[
\Delta=54^2-114\cdot24=180>0.
\]

Thus the gap is in the auxiliary proof, not in Grimmett's conjecture.

### 4.2 Repair of the same-endpoint subcase

Let a `3+3` cross-ear graph have roots \(P,Q\). Suppose the two ears have the same endpoints \(u,v\):

\[
E_P=\{uw,wv\},
\qquad
E_Q=\{ux,xv\}.
\]

The direct edge \(uv\) is absent by the original cross-ear argument: if it existed, suppressing one ear in the opposite root would create an additional \(R\)-forest avoiding that ear.

Delete both ears and insert the single direct edge

\[
g=uv,
\]

forming a simple graph \(B\).

Every \(R(B)\)-forest must contain \(g\). Indeed, expanding \(g\) to either ear gives an \(R(G)\)-forest using that ear in parity two; among the six members of the two size-three fibers, the only such parity-zero/two members are the roots. Conversely both roots suppress to the same forest because the ears have the same endpoints and the two roots differ only in which ear realizes the \(u-v\) connection. Therefore

\[
\boxed{|R(B)|=1.}
\]

So \(B\) is weighted pair-Rayleigh by the archived unique-root theorem.

The original graph \(G\) is obtained from \(B\) by replacing the nondistinguished edge \(g\) by the two-terminal network \(K\) consisting of two parallel length-two paths

\[
u-w-v,\qquad u-x-v.
\]

At unit edge weights an exact terminal enumeration gives

\[
D_K=9,\qquad C_K=6.
\]

Hence

\[
\Delta_G(\mathbf 1)
=
81\,\Delta_B\left(y_g=\frac23,\ y_{r\ne g}=1\right)\ge0.
\]

This repairs the missing same-endpoint subcase of L56.

## 5. The `4+4` stratum: preliminary dichotomy

Assume for contradiction that \(G,e,f\) is an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample and that the root-fiber partition is

\[
4+4.
\]

A four-element down-set is either:

* a Boolean square
  \[
  \{P,P+h,P+k,P+h+k\},
  \]
  or
* a three-spoke star
  \[
  \{P,P+h_1,P+h_2,P+h_3\}.
  \]

### Lemma 5.1 — a Boolean-square fiber gives bridges

Suppose

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+h,P+k,P+h+k\}.
\]

The edges \(h,k\) are exactly the edges of \(G\) having an endpoint outside \(V(C_P)\).

If \(h\) were contained in a graph cycle, that cycle would have to leave and return to \(C_P\). Since the only edges incident with vertices outside \(C_P\) are \(h,k\), the return edge must be \(k\). Thus \(h,k\) form a two-edge ear between two vertices of \(C_P\). By the bad-pair ear lemma this would imply

\[
P+h+k\notin R,
\]

contrary to the Boolean square.

Therefore \(h\) is a graph bridge, and the same argument applies to \(k\).

For any nondistinguished bridge \(b\),

\[
I_G=(1+y_b)I_{G-b}
\]

and hence

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(1+y_b)^2\Delta_{G-b}\{e,f\}.
\]

At unit weights, deleting \(h,k\) strictly reduces the edge set. Edge minimality therefore gives a nonnegative smaller-graph Rayleigh difference, contradicting \(\Delta_G<0\).

Hence an edge-minimal `4+4` counterexample can have **no Boolean-square fiber**.

Both fibers must therefore be three-spoke stars.

## 6. Star-star `4+4`: geometry

Write the first fiber as

\[
\mathcal F(P)
=
\{P,P+wu,P+wv,P+wz\},
\]

where \(w\notin V(C_P)\) and \(u,v,z\in V(C_P)\).

Let \(Q\) be the other root.

### Lemma 6.1 — the opposite root uses exactly two spokes

A simple cycle can use the star center \(w\) with either zero or exactly two incident spokes.

It cannot use zero. If \(C_Q\) avoided \(w\), choose two of \(u,v,z\) lying in the same path component of

\[
C_P\setminus\{e,f\}.
\]

Such a pair exists by pigeonhole. The two corresponding spokes through \(w\), together with the complementary arc of \(C_P\), form a new simple cycle containing both \(e,f\). It is distinct from both \(C_P\) and \(C_Q\), contradicting that there are only two roots.

Thus \(C_Q\) uses exactly two spokes. Relabel so they are

\[
wu,\qquad wv,
\]

and \(wz\) is unused by \(Q\).

By symmetry, the root \(P\) uses exactly two spokes from the star fiber of \(Q\).

### Lemma 6.2 — an unused spoke cannot also be an atom of the other fiber

Suppose \(z\notin V(C_Q)\). Then the unused edge \(wz\) has an endpoint outside \(C_Q\), so it is a one-edge extension atom of \(Q\).

Because \(Q\)'s fiber is also a three-spoke star, its other two atoms have the form

\[
za,\qquad zb,
\]

with outside center \(z\). Symmetry and the fact that \(P\) does not contain its own atom \(wz\) force \(P\) to use both \(za,zb\).

Delete the shared unused edge \(wz\). The remaining graph has partition `3+3`, with cross ears

\[
u-w-v,\qquad a-z-b.
\]

There are two possibilities.

#### Same endpoint pair

If

\[
\{a,b\}=\{u,v\},
\]

then the common complementary \(u-v\) path contains both \(e,f\). Reinsert \(wz\). The two mixed \(u-v\) paths

\[
u-w-z-v,
\qquad
u-z-w-v
\]

are distinct from the original ears, and each joined to the complementary \(u-v\) path gives another simple common \(e,f\)-cycle. This contradicts the `4+4` root count.

#### Distinct endpoint pairs

If the endpoint pairs differ, use the corrected distinct-endpoint cross-ear compression. Suppressing the two ears gives the exact-two decorated opposite-edge \(K_4\). The two direct edges obtained from the ears belong to opposite root differences.

In the four-cell notation

\[
P'\setminus Q'=\{up,qv\},
\qquad
Q'\setminus P'=\{uv,pq\},
\]

an edge from one root difference and an edge from the other always share one branch vertex. By relabeling, suppose they are \(up\) and \(uv\). Subdivide them as

\[
u-z-p,\qquad u-w-v
\]

and add \(zw\). Then

\[
z-w-v
\cup F[v,p]
\cup pq
\cup E[q,u]
\cup uz
\]

is a simple cycle containing both distinguished edges, where \(E\) is the common \(u-q\) distinguished path and \(F\) the common \(p-v\) distinguished path. A second mixed cycle is obtained from

\[
z-w-u
\cup E[u,q]
\cup qv
\cup F[v,p]
\cup pz.
\]

Thus adding the midpoint edge again creates additional common roots.

So both subcases contradict `4+4`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{z\in V(C_Q).}
\]

By symmetry, the unused spoke of \(Q\)'s star has its attachment vertex on \(C_P\).

### Lemma 6.3 — deleting the two unused spokes gives a cross-ear `3+3`

Let the second star center be \(x\), and let its two spokes used by \(P\) have endpoints \(a,b\); write \(xc\) for its unused spoke.

By Lemma 6.2,

\[
z\in V(C_P)\cap V(C_Q),
\qquad
c\in V(C_P)\cap V(C_Q).
\]

Delete \(wz\) and \(xc\). Neither deleted edge occurs in the opposite root or in any member of the opposite star fiber. Thus the two four-element fibers drop to two three-element fibers:

\[
\{P,P+wu,P+wv\},
\qquad
\{Q,Q+xa,Q+xb\}.
\]

This is a cross-ear `3+3` graph: \(Q\) uses the whole ear \(u-w-v\), and \(P\) uses the whole ear \(a-x-b\).

If the endpoint pairs were distinct, the corrected distinct-endpoint compression would give an exact-two decorated \(K_4\). Reinsert the spoke \(wz\). Since \(z\) lies on the common support, the archived third-spoke construction applies; subdividing the other exchange edge does not destroy the constructed cycle. A third common \(e,f\)-cycle results, contradiction.

Hence the endpoint pairs agree:

\[
\boxed{\{a,b\}=\{u,v\}.}
\]

After relabeling,

\[
C_P=R\cup(u-x-v),
\qquad
C_Q=R\cup(u-w-v),
\]

where \(R\) is a common simple \(u-v\) path containing both \(e,f\).

The two unused spokes are

\[
wz,\qquad xc,
\]

with \(z,c\in V(R)\).

### Lemma 6.4 — the two unused attachment vertices coincide

First, \(z\) separates \(e\) from \(f\) along \(R\). Otherwise one of the two subpaths \(R[u,z]\), \(R[z,v]\) contains both distinguished edges. Joining that subpath to respectively \(u-w-z\) or \(z-w-v\) gives a third common cycle.

The same argument shows that \(c\) separates \(e\) from \(f\) along \(R\).

Suppose \(z\ne c\). Orient \(R\) from \(u\) to \(v\). After swapping \(e,f\) and/or \(z,c\) if necessary, their order is

\[
u\;\cdots\;e\;\cdots\;z\;\cdots\;c\;\cdots\;f\;\cdots\;v.
\]

Then

\[
R[u,z]
\cup z-w-v
\cup R[v,c]
\cup c-x-u
\]

is a simple cycle containing both \(e,f\), distinct from \(C_P,C_Q\). Contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{z=c.}
\]

Thus both star centers attach to the same three old vertices:

\[
\{u,v,z\}.
\]

## 7. Branch confinement and the decorated wheel

Put

\[
U=R[u,z],
\qquad
V=R[z,v].
\]

The edge \(e\) lies on one of \(U,V\) and \(f\) on the other because \(z\) separates them.

The only graph edges incident with \(w\) are

\[
wu,wv,wz,
\]

since these are exactly the one-edge extensions of \(P\). Similarly the only edges incident with \(x\) are

\[
xu,xv,xz.
\]

Any other non-isolated graph edge therefore has both endpoints on \(R\).

### Lemma 7.1 — no edge crosses from \(U\) to \(V\)

Suppose \(g=ab\) has

\[
a\in V(U)\setminus\{z\},
\qquad
b\in V(V)\setminus\{z\}.
\]

There are four natural \(a-b\) routes in the subgraph \(R\cup\{wu,wv,wz\}\):

\[
R[a,z]\cup z-w-v\cup R[v,b],
\]

\[
R[a,u]\cup u-w-z\cup R[z,b],
\]

\[
R[a,b],
\]

and

\[
R[a,u]\cup u-w-v\cup R[v,b].
\]

According to whether \(a\) lies on the \(u\)-side or \(z\)-side of \(e\), and whether \(b\) lies on the \(z\)-side or \(v\)-side of \(f\), exactly one of these routes contains both distinguished edges. Together with the chord \(ab\), it gives a third common \(e,f\)-cycle.

Hence no such crossing edge exists.

Therefore all noncore edges lie entirely inside \(U\) or entirely inside \(V\). Let \(K_U\) and \(K_V\) denote the resulting two-terminal networks, with terminals \(u,z\) and \(z,v\), containing \(e\) and \(f\) respectively after swapping names if needed.

After contracting \(K_U,K_V\) to abstract edges \(uz,zv\), the five branch vertices

\[
u,v,z,w,x
\]

have exactly the six other literal edges

\[
wu,wv,wz,\qquad xu,xv,xz.
\]

The missing edges are

\[
uv,\qquad wx.
\]

Thus the core is

\[
\boxed{K_5\setminus\{uv,wx\}=W_4,}
\]

with the two abstract distinguished edges \(uz,zv\) adjacent at \(z\).

## 8. Exact wheel-core factorization

For the bare core \(W=K_5-\{uv,wx\}\), distinguish the adjacent rim edges

\[
E=uz,\qquad F=zv.
\]

Exact enumeration over the remaining six core edges gives full forest counts

\[
(Z,Z_E,Z_F,Z_{EF})=(134,48,48,16).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_W\{E,F\}
=
48^2-134\cdot16
=
160.
}
\]

Now restore the two arbitrary distinguished networks \(K_U,K_V\). Let

\[
\alpha_U=\alpha_{K_U}(e;u,z),
\qquad
\alpha_V=\alpha_{K_V}(f;z,v)
\]

at unit edge weights.

Applying the exact distinguished-edge substitution identity twice gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G\{e,f\}(\mathbf1)
=
160\,\alpha_U\alpha_V.
}
\]

Because \(G\) is assumed edge-minimal, both response factors are nonnegative. If a branch already contains the direct terminal edge, the response is the Rayleigh difference for that smaller branch graph; if not, adjoin a fresh terminal edge \(r\), in which case

\[
\alpha_U=\Delta_{K_U+r}\{e,r\},
\qquad
\alpha_V=\Delta_{K_V+r}\{f,r\}.
\]

Each comparison graph has strictly fewer edges than \(G\), so edge minimality gives

\[
\alpha_U,\alpha_V\ge0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}(\mathbf1)\ge0,
\]

contradiction.

We have proved:

\[
\boxed{
\text{No edge-minimal counterexample has root-fiber partition }4+4.
}
\]
