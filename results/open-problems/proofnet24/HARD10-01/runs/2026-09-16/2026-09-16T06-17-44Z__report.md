Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph, and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices. For distinct edges `e,f`, write

\[

\qquad
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}.

\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).

## 4. `4+4+1`: both four-fibers are stars

Assume for contradiction that `G` is an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample with partition `4+4+1`. Let `P,Q` be the size-four roots and `S` the singleton root.

A Boolean-square four-fiber has bridge atom edges, so bridge factorization excludes it in an edge-minimal counterexample. Thus both four-fibers are three-spoke stars.

Write

\[
\mathcal F(P)=\{P,P+wa_1,P+wa_2,P+wa_3\},
\]

with center `w` outside `C_P`, and similarly write the `Q`-star with center `x`. By the one-edge extension criterion the displayed spokes are all graph edges meeting vertices outside the relevant root cycle. Hence `w` is the only non-isolated vertex outside `C_P` and `deg(w)=3`; similarly for `x` and `C_Q`.

A singleton root has no one-edge extensions, so every non-isolated graph vertex lies on `C_S`. Hence `w,x` lie on `C_S`, and the singleton cycle uses exactly two spokes at each center.

## 5. Star-center dichotomy and exclusion of the same-center case

Relative to `w`, either `C_Q` avoids `w`, in which case all three `P`-spokes are atoms of the `Q`-fiber and the two stars have the same center and same three spokes; or `C_Q` contains `w`, in which case it traverses exactly two `P`-spokes. Symmetrically, in the latter case `C_P` traverses exactly two `Q`-spokes. Thus there are only two cases: same-center identical stars, or distinct centers with mutual traversal.

The same-center case is impossible. Let the common spokes be `wa,wb,wc`, and let the singleton use `wa,wb` but not `wc`. Deleting `wc` gives exact partition `3+3+1` with common ear `a-w-b`. The archived common-ear theorem says the direct edge `ab` is the corresponding exchange edge, deleting the ear leaves an exact-two decorated `K4`, and the singleton suppresses to one exact-two root. Since the singleton omits `wc` yet is singleton, `c` lies on its cycle and therefore on the compressed root support. Restoring `wc` invokes the archived third-spoke construction and creates another common `e,f` cycle using `wc`, distinct from all three existing roots. Contradiction.

Therefore the two star centers are distinct.
