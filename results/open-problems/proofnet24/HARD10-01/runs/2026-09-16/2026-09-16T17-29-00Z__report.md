## 2. Exact original source locator and current-source checks

The campaign alias `Grimmett Conjecture 4.2` is a later restatement. The original numbered locator recovered from Grimmett--Winkler is:

**Conjecture 1.1 (uniform-forest half).** For a finite graph, choose uniformly from all acyclic edge subsets (isolated vertices remain in the spanning vertex set). Distinct edge indicators are conjectured to be negatively correlated.

Equivalently, for distinct edges `e,f`,

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

The present target is this **unit/unweighted pairwise** statement. It is not the uniform-spanning-tree theorem, not a fixed-component forest ensemble, not full negative association of arbitrary events, and not the stronger all-positive-weight `I`-Rayleigh property.

Fresh status checks dated **2026-09-16**:

1. Grimmett, arXiv:2205.07318, Section 4.2, restates the uniform-all-forests pairwise question as Conjecture 4.2.
2. Tang--Zhang, arXiv:2603.10738 (11 March 2026), proves pairwise negative correlation for several uniform spanning-subgraph families of `K_n` for sufficiently large `n`, including forests with a **fixed number of components**. It does not settle arbitrary graphs under the uniform measure over all forests.
3. Tang--Zhang, arXiv:2609.11578 (10 September 2026), still states that for random-cluster parameter below one, even pairwise edge negative correlation is open on general finite graphs. This is consistent with the present OPEN label.

## 3. Method #5 — add one edge field and differentiate

Fix a graph (or matroid independence system) and three distinct edges `g,e,f`. Give `g` weight `t>0` and every other edge weight one. Split the forest counts by whether `g` is absent or present:

\[
Z(t)=A+tB,
\]
\[
Z_e(t)=A_e+tB_e,\qquad
Z_f(t)=A_f+tB_f,\qquad
Z_{ef}(t)=A_{ef}+tB_{ef}.
\]

Then the Rayleigh numerator for the old pair is the exact quadratic

\[
q(t)=\Delta_{ef}(t)=q_0+q_1t+q_2t^2,
\]

with

\[
q_0=A_eA_f-AA_{ef},
\]
\[
q_1=A_eB_f+B_eA_f-A B_{ef}-B A_{ef},
\]
\[
q_2=B_eB_f-BB_{ef}.
\]

This is algebra, not a new conjecture. The useful point is what the **linear coefficient means** probabilistically, derived next.

The previous B1 exact example has

\[
q(t)=88-8t+4t^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
q'(0)=-8<0.
\]

So a proposed proof by monotonically increasing a virtual-edge field from deletion (`t=0`) toward the unit point is false even on a five-vertex graph. The same example remains safe for every positive field: its minimum occurs at `t=1` and equals `84`. Thus the failure is specifically **monotonicity**, not Rayleigh positivity.

**Outcome of method #5:** the deformation variable is useful, but its derivative does not have a fixed sign. Any interpolation proof must control the quadratic minimum, not just its endpoints or derivative direction.

## 4. Method #18 — another language: the exact two-slice covariance formula

This is the main new conceptual reduction of the run.

Let `mu_0` be the uniform forest law conditional on `g` absent and `mu_1` the corresponding law conditional on `g` present (with `g` then removed from the indicator list). Define

\[
p_e^0=\frac{A_e}{A},\quad p_f^0=\frac{A_f}{A},\quad p_{ef}^0=\frac{A_{ef}}A,
\]

and

\[
p_e^1=\frac{B_e}{B},\quad p_f^1=\frac{B_f}{B},\quad p_{ef}^1=\frac{B_{ef}}B.
\]

Write the two within-slice negative-covariance margins as

\[
a=p_e^0p_f^0-p_{ef}^0=-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_0}(1_e,1_f),
\]

\[
b=p_e^1p_f^1-p_{ef}^1=-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_1}(1_e,1_f),
\]

and the two conditional mean shifts as

\[
u=p_e^0-p_e^1,\qquad v=p_f^0-p_f^1.
\]

At field `t`, the probability that the sample lies in the `g`-present slice is

\[
\theta(t)=\frac{tB}{A+tB}.
\]

### Proposition: exact slice-mixture identity

For every `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cov}_t(1_e,1_f)
=-(1-\theta)a-\theta b+\theta(1-\theta)uv.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
q(t)=-(A+tB)^2\operatorname{Cov}_t(1_e,1_f).
}
\]

The quadratic coefficients satisfy

\[
\boxed{q_0=A^2a,\qquad q_1=AB(a+b-uv),\qquad q_2=B^2b.}
\]

### Proof

Condition on the binary variable `1_g`. The ordinary law of total covariance gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(1_e,1_f)
=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(1_e,1_f\mid 1_g)]
+\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[1_e\mid1_g],\mathbb E[1_f\mid1_g]).
\]

The first term is

\[
-(1-\theta)a-\theta b.
\]

A two-point random variable taking values `p_e^0,p_e^1` has covariance with the analogous `f` variable equal to

\[
\theta(1-\theta)(p_e^0-p_e^1)(p_f^0-p_f^1)
=\theta(1-\theta)uv.
\]

This proves the covariance identity. Since `Z_e/Z`, `Z_f/Z`, and `Z_ef/Z` are the weighted inclusion probabilities,

\[
q(t)=Z_eZ_f-ZZ_{ef}=-(A+tB)^2\operatorname{Cov}_t(1_e,1_f).
\]

Substituting the definitions of `a,b,u,v` gives the displayed three coefficient identities. QED.

### Sharp all-field criterion when the two slices are themselves nonpositively correlated

Assume `a,b>=0`. If `uv<=0`, the mixture term is harmless and every field is safe. If `uv>0`, Rayleigh for **every** `t>0` is equivalent to

\[
\theta(1-\theta)uv\le(1-\theta)a+\theta b
\quad\hbox{for every }0<\theta<1.
\]

Dividing by `theta(1-theta)` gives

\[
uv\le \frac a\theta+\frac b{1-\theta}.
\]

The right side has minimum

\[
(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{all one-edge fields are safe}
\iff
uv\le(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2
}
\]

under the stated `a,b>=0` assumption. Squaring the excess form is exactly the integer discriminant condition `q_1^2<=4q_0q_2` when `q_1<0`.

At the **unit source point** `t=1`, with

\[
\theta_0=\frac B{A+B},
\]

the exact requirement is only

\[
\boxed{
uv\le \frac a{\theta_0}+\frac b{1-\theta_0}.
}
\]

Thus the all-positive-field Rayleigh assertion is quantitatively stronger than the source's unit statement, not merely terminologically stronger.

No novelty claim is made for the law-of-total-covariance identity itself. Its value here is that it exposes the precise missing quantity in the current induction attempts.

## 5. Method #20 — find why the induction/transport algorithm fails

The slice formula identifies the obstruction exactly.

Suppose both conditional slices are already negatively correlated (`a,b>=0`). A deletion/contraction-style induction controls only the first two terms

\[
-(1-\theta)a-\theta b.
\]

If both `e` and `f` become less likely when `g` is forced present, then `u,v>=0`, and mixing the two slices contributes the **positive** term

\[
\theta(1-\theta)uv.
\]

So even two individually good slices do not glue automatically. The missing proof ingredient is a quantitative transport bound on the product of the two conditional mean shifts.

For the all-field strengthening, the exact target is

\[
\boxed{
(p_e^0-p_e^1)(p_f^0-p_f^1)
\le
\left(
\sqrt{-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_0}(e,f)}
+
\sqrt{-\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_1}(e,f)}
\right)^2.
}
\]

For the source unit point, the weaker `theta_0`-weighted version above suffices.

This also gives a cleaner interpretation of P14's 2-cut theorem. Marginalizing the opposite side of a 2-separation creates exactly one such external field. The obstacle to turning the complete `n<=9` unit certificate into a ten-vertex 2-cut theorem is not a mysterious weighted issue: it is the positive between-slice covariance term in the displayed identity.

### Consequence for counterexample search

The previous B15 search optimized a small **unit normalized gap**. That is not the right score for killing the one-edge-field route. A more targeted refutation score is:

- first require `q_1<0`, equivalently `uv>a+b`;
- then maximize the positive-axis discriminant defect

\[
q_1^2-4q_0q_2,
\]

or, in probability language,

\[
uv-(\sqrt a+\sqrt b)^2.
\]

A positive defect with `q_0,q_2>=0` would give an exact one-edge-field counterexample and kill the proposed stronger induction invariant. It would **not** by itself refute Grimmett--Winkler's unit conjecture unless the negative field were `t=1`.

This is the next counterexample-first search objective for HARD10. P14 already owns the exhaustive OEFR route, so HARD10 should preferentially use this score on structurally hard / 3-connected ten-vertex candidates rather than re-run P14's all-graph enumeration.

## 7. What this says about Lorentzian / log-concave approaches

The new covariance formula makes the limitation precise.

For a positive multiaffine polynomial, log concavity controls the Hessian as a matrix, but the source requires a sign on each individual off-diagonal covariance. Conditioning on one edge and mixing back introduces the explicit positive term `theta(1-theta)uv`. A general Hessian concavity statement does not by itself bound this term by the two within-slice negative covariances.

Likewise, Fang--Ma's theorem would settle a polynomial once the **Gårding** hypothesis is proved, but Lorentzianity by itself does not manufacture the slice-shift inequality above. This run therefore found no valid shortcut from modern log-concavity machinery to Conjecture 1.1.
