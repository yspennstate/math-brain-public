## 2. Exact original source locator recovered again

Primary source checked on 2026-09-16:

- G. R. Grimmett and S. N. Winkler, *Negative association in uniform forests and connected graphs*, arXiv:`math/0302185`, submitted 17 February 2003; *Random Structures & Algorithms* 24 (2004), 444--460; DOI `10.1002/rsa.20012`.
- Open author PDF: `https://www.statslab.cam.ac.uk/~grg/papers/negass.pdf`.

The campaign alias “Grimmett Conjecture 4.2” is the later 2022/2023 restatement. The original numbered locator is **Conjecture 1.1**, whose forest half says that for every finite graph `G=(V,E)`, the uniform forest is edge-negatively-associated. In the paper's standing scope the graph has no loops or multiple edges. Equivalently, if `F` is chosen uniformly from all acyclic edge subsets and `e != f`,

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Theorem 1.5 of the same paper verifies the forest statement by direct computation for all graphs on at most eight vertices and all nine-vertex graphs with at most eighteen edges.

The source itself also formulates stronger positive-weight versions in equations (1.6)--(1.7). Those are not silently substituted for the unit-weight frozen target.

## 3. Method #22 — read the failure that P14 rediscovered

The relevant historical failure is unusually exact.

Charles Semple and Dominic Welsh, *Negative Correlation in Graphs and Matroids*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 17 (2008), 423--435, define a matroid `M` to be **independence correlated** when for every pair `e != f` and every positive real weighting `y` of the ground set,

\[
\Delta_I(M;y)
= I_e(M;y)I_f(M;y)-I_{ef}(M;y)I(M;y)\ge0,
\tag{3.1}
\]

where the `I`-polynomials sum weights over independent sets.

For a graphic matroid, independent sets are precisely forests, so (3.1) is the all-positive-edge-weight forest Rayleigh inequality. It is stronger by definition than testing only the all-ones point.

Semple--Welsh Proposition 3.7 proves series/parallel extensions preserve independence correlation, but immediately before its proof they explicitly say they were **unable** to show that merely knowing a graph satisfies unit Conjecture 1.1 implies that its series/parallel extensions do. That is exactly the obstruction P14 met in 2026: a two-separator turns the other side into a non-unit virtual edge field.

The crucial final-published note at the end of Semple--Welsh then records the missing theorem:

> Cocks showed that if Conjecture 1.1 holds for a graph `G`, then `M(G)` is independence correlated.

This settles their Open Question (3), which had asked whether a graph could satisfy Conjecture 1.1 while its graphic matroid failed independence correlation.

Cocks' later paper is Clifford C. Cocks, *Correlated Matroids*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 17 (2008), 511--518, DOI `10.1017/S0963548308009152`. Its accessible Cambridge abstract states that the Grimmett--Winkler and Pemantle conjectures are equivalent to statements about correlated graphic matroids and that correlated matroids are closed under 2-sum.

**Access gap:** the body of the Cocks article was not openly accessible in this run. The individual implication above is nevertheless stated explicitly in the final published Semple--Welsh paper, while the Cocks abstract independently confirms the global equivalence theme. I do not invent a theorem number from the inaccessible body.

This matters because the implication is **not automatic from definitions**. The previous HARD10 report correctly warned against silently equating the unit source statement with all-positive-weight Rayleigh, but it missed this specific published theorem. That omission is corrected here: **for each graphic matroid, Cocks supplies the nontrivial bridge from the unit Conjecture 1.1 inequality to all-positive-weight independence correlation.**

## 4. Method #27 — swap the quantifiers: finite unit certificates upgrade graph-by-graph

The immediate consequence of Cocks' theorem is stronger than the previous campaign ledger had recognized.

### Proposition HARD10-COCKS-FINITE-UPGRADE-61

Let `G` be a finite graph. If `G` satisfies the uniform-forest pairwise inequality of Grimmett--Winkler Conjecture 1.1 at unit weights for every pair of distinct edges, then for **every positive real edge-weight vector** `y` and every `e != f`,

\[
\Delta_I(M(G);y)\ge0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

This is exactly the individual graph implication attributed to Cocks in the final note of Semple--Welsh: unit Conjecture 1.1 for `G` implies `M(G)` is independence correlated. By Semple--Welsh's definition, independence correlated means (4.1) for all positive real `y`. QED.

HARD10 has a complete exact unit certificate for every simple graph with at most nine vertices. Applying the proposition graph-by-graph yields:

\[
\boxed{\text{Every simple graph on at most 9 vertices is independence correlated.}}
\tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, P14's `OEFR(9)` condition (all old edges unit, one distinguished edge given an arbitrary positive field) is automatically true. No separate `OEFR(9)` exhaustive computation is necessary.

## 5. Method #11 — matroid-weight dual language closes all graph 2-cuts

P14's two-separator theorem supplies the exact analytic interface.

Let a graph `G` have a nontrivial two-vertex separation with terminals `s,t` and edge-disjoint sides `A,B`, meeting only at `{s,t}`. Let `D_B` be the number of forests of `B` in which `s,t` are disconnected and `C_B` the number in which they are connected.

For a pair `e,f` lying on the same side `A`, P14 proves that after replacing the opposite side by a virtual edge `g=st`,

\[
\Delta_G(e,f)
=D_B^2\,
\Delta_{A+g}^{(y_g=\lambda)}(e,f),
\tag{5.1}
\]

with

\[
\lambda=\frac{C_B}{D_B}>0
\tag{5.2}
\]

when there is no actual separator edge, and

\[
\lambda=1+\frac{C_B}{D_B}>0
\tag{5.3}
\]

in the formulation where an actual separator edge is removed and accounted for separately.

For a cross-side pair `e in A`, `f in B`, P14 proves the unit factorization

\[
\Delta_G(e,f)
=(D_A C_{A,e}-C_A D_{A,e})
 (D_B C_{B,f}-C_B D_{B,f}),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and identifies each factor as a unit Rayleigh numerator in the corresponding side with one virtual terminal edge. P14 also gives the direct nonnegative formula for a pair involving an actual separator edge.

These identities were already proved and self-checked in the P14 archive. The new point is that Cocks now supplies exactly the missing sign in (5.1) at arbitrary `lambda>0`.

## 6. New structural theorem — a vertex-minimal counterexample is 3-connected

### Theorem HARD10-MINIMAL-3CONNECTED-61

If Grimmett--Winkler Conjecture 1.1 (forest half) has a finite simple counterexample, then a counterexample with the minimum possible number of vertices is 3-connected.

### Proof

Let `G` be a vertex-minimal simple counterexample.

First, disconnected components and cut-vertex blocks factor at the level of the forest independence polynomial; equivalently, the probability law is a product across graph blocks for edge events on different blocks. The archived P14 block-factorization proof therefore reduces any violating pair to a strictly smaller block. Vertex minimality excludes this, so `G` is 2-connected.

Suppose `G` has a 2-vertex cut `{s,t}`. Because `G` is 2-connected, `G-{s,t}` has at least two nonempty components. Group these into two nonempty sides `A,B`, each containing `s,t`. Thus each side, after adding at most the virtual terminal edge `g=st`, has strictly fewer vertices than `G`.

By vertex minimality, each simple augmented side satisfies unit Conjecture 1.1. By Proposition HARD10-COCKS-FINITE-UPGRADE-61, each augmented side's graphic matroid is independence correlated, so its forest Rayleigh difference is nonnegative under **every positive real edge weighting**.

Now inspect a candidate violating pair.

1. If both edges lie in `A`, use P14 identity (5.1). The field `lambda` in (5.2) or (5.3) is positive, so independence correlation of `A+g` gives

\[
\Delta_{A+g}^{(y_g=\lambda)}(e,f)\ge0,
\]

and hence `Delta_G(e,f)>=0`.

2. If the pair crosses the separator, P14's factorization (5.4) writes `Delta_G` as a product of two unit Rayleigh factors on the smaller augmented sides. Both are nonnegative by vertex minimality (or by the stronger independence-correlation upgrade).

3. If one edge is the actual separator edge `st`, P14's direct formula expresses the Rayleigh numerator as a positive square/count factor times the corresponding smaller-side unit Rayleigh factor, again nonnegative.

Every possible pair is therefore nonnegative, contradicting that `G` is a counterexample. Hence `G` has no 2-vertex cut. Since it is already 2-connected and is nontrivial, it is 3-connected. QED.

### Novelty label

This theorem is **new to the current repository proof chain**, not claimed as new to the literature. It is a direct synthesis of Cocks' published graph-to-independence-correlation theorem with the P14 exact two-terminal identities. Cocks' own 2-sum results suggest that closely related connectivity reductions may already be implicit in the literature.

## 8. Why the same trick does not immediately promote 3-connected to 4-connected

The next natural attack is a 3-vertex separator. Here an opposite side has five terminal-connectivity states rather than two.

Let the terminals be `x,y,z`. For a three-terminal network `B`, define total forest weights/counts:

- `d`: terminal partition `x|y|z`;
- `a`: `xy|z`;
- `b`: `xz|y`;
- `c`: `yz|x`;
- `t`: `xyz` (all three connected).

Ask whether this response can always be replaced, up to one positive scale factor, by a positive weighted triangle on `x,y,z` with edge weights `p,q,r`.

A triangle forest has terminal-state weights

\[
1,\quad p,\quad q,\quad r,\quad pq+pr+qr.
\]

Matching the first four states forces

\[
p=\frac ad,\qquad q=\frac bd,\qquad r=\frac cd,
\]

and the fifth state then matches if and only if

\[
\boxed{dt=ab+ac+bc.}
\tag{8.1}
\]

This identity does not hold for arbitrary three-terminal networks. The unit three-spoke star with one central vertex has

\[
(d,a,b,c,t)=(4,1,1,1,1),
\]

because the empty set and the three one-spoke forests all leave the three terminals mutually disconnected; each two-spoke state connects exactly one terminal pair; and the three-spoke state connects all three. Thus

\[
dt=4\ne3=ab+ac+bc.
\]

So a general 3-cut side cannot be compressed to an ordinary positive weighted triangle. The Cocks weighted-edge theorem naturally closes 2-cuts because a two-terminal response has a single scalar odds parameter; it does **not** by itself close 3-cuts.

This is the precise next obstruction rather than a vague failure of induction.

A potentially useful next deformation is to augment the triangle response with one genuine three-body/Steiner parameter measuring

\[
\kappa:=dt-(ab+ac+bc),
\]

and derive the Rayleigh numerator on the other side as an affine or quadratic function of `kappa`. The star witness has `kappa=1`. If graphic three-terminal networks impose a sign or interval constraint on `kappa`, that may be the correct 3-sum analogue of terminal odds.
