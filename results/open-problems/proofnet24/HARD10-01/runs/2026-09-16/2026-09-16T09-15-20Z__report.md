## 1. Exact correction

The 09:15:19 report said, in effect, that if the size-seven Type-D root cycle `C_P` avoided the center `x` of the size-three ear `u-x-v`, then `ux,xv` would be atoms of the Type-D fiber and their double would contradict the bad-pair ear lemma.

That is **not a valid argument**: `ux,xv` form an ear relative to the size-three root `Q`, but they need not be a bad pair relative to the different root `P`.  In a Type-D size-seven fiber every atom pair is allowed, so this subcase requires a separate argument.

## 2. Corrected lemma: the size-seven root still traverses the size-three ear

Assume exact partition `7+3`.  Let

\[
\mathcal F(Q)=\{Q,Q+ux,Q+xv\}
\]

be the size-three fiber.  Its center `x` has graph degree exactly two.

Let `P` be the size-seven root.

### 2.1 Six-spoke Type A

If the size-seven fiber is the six-spoke Type A fiber and `C_P` avoids `x`, both `ux,xv` are atoms of `P`.  A Type-A fiber has all atoms incident with one outside star center.  Since `ux,xv` already share `x`, that center is `x`, forcing four further graph edges incident with `x`.  This contradicts `deg_G(x)=2`.

Hence Type A forces `C_P` to contain `x`, and a simple cycle through the degree-two vertex `x` uses both `ux,xv`.

### 2.2 Three-atom Type D: obstruction shape

Now suppose the size-seven fiber is Type D.  Write its three atoms as `h_1,h_2,h_3`; every proper subset extends `P` in `R`, while the full triple does not.

The obstruction-shape part of archived L75 does **not** use singleton status: a minimal three-edge obstruction of this kind is either

1. a simple length-three path whose two endpoints lie on `C_P` and whose two internal vertices lie outside `V(C_P)`; or
2. a three-cycle made from the three atom edges, meeting `C_P` in at most one vertex.

The singleton hypothesis in L75 was used only later to rule out the triangle in the `7+1` setting.

Assume for contradiction that `C_P` avoids `x`.  Then the two size-three-ear edges

\[
a=ux,\qquad b=xv
\]

are two of the three Type-D atoms.

Because `a,b` share the degree-two vertex `x`, they are adjacent edges in the three-edge obstruction.

#### Triangle obstruction is impossible

If the Type-D atoms form the triangle `u-x-v-u`, then at most one of its three vertices lies on `C_P`.  Since these three edges are **all** atoms of `P`, there is no further graph edge incident with any triangle vertex outside `V(C_P)`; any such edge would be a fourth one-edge extension of `P`.

The size-three root cycle `C_Q` must contain both ear endpoints `u,v` but contains neither ear edge `ux,xv` (those are the two one-edge extensions of `Q`).  At least one of `u,v` is outside `V(C_P)`.  Such an outside endpoint has only its two triangle edges available, one of which is an excluded ear edge.  It therefore has degree at most one inside `C_Q`, impossible for a vertex on a simple cycle.

Thus the triangle obstruction cannot coexist with the size-three root `Q`.

#### Length-three path obstruction is impossible

If the Type-D atoms form a path `p_0-p_1-p_2-p_3`, its endpoints `p_0,p_3` lie on `C_P` and its internal vertices `p_1,p_2` lie outside `V(C_P)`.

The two edges `a=ux,b=xv` share `x`, so within this three-edge path they must be either the first two edges or the last two edges.  Consequently one of the **ear endpoints** `u,v` is the other internal path vertex and therefore lies outside `V(C_P)`.

Call that endpoint `z`.  Since the three path edges are all atoms of `P`, there is no additional graph edge incident with the outside internal vertex `z`; otherwise that edge would be a fourth atom.  Hence `z` has graph degree two, with one incident edge equal to one of the size-three ear edges `a` or `b`.

But `C_Q` must contain the ear endpoint `z` while omitting both ear edges `a,b`, because `Q` is the root of the size-three fiber.  Then at most one graph edge incident with `z` remains available to `C_Q`, impossible for a simple cycle.

This contradiction eliminates the length-three-path obstruction as well.

Therefore the assumption `x\notin V(C_P)` is impossible in Type D.

### 2.3 Conclusion

In both Type A and Type D,

\[
x\in V(C_P).
\]

Because `deg_G(x)=2`, the simple cycle `C_P` traverses the whole ear:

\[
ux,xv\in P.
\]

Thus every member of the size-seven fiber contains the full size-three ear, exactly as required for the remaining compression argument in the 09:15:19 report.

## 3. The rest of the `7+3` reduction is valid

Let `g=uv`.

If `g` were already present, suppressing the full ear in the seven members of the size-seven fiber would give seven distinct `R(G)` forests avoiding both ear halves.  In the exact `7+3` family only the root `Q` avoids both halves; its two other fiber members contain one half each.  This is impossible, so `g` is absent.

Delete `ux,xv` and insert the fresh edge `g=uv`.  The seven large-fiber members suppress bijectively to the size-seven fiber containing `g`, while `Q` is the unique `R`-forest avoiding `g`.  The compressed graph therefore has exact partition

\[
7+1.
\]

The archived L76 theorem proves every `7+1` graph weighted pair-Rayleigh.  Exact two-terminal replacement for the unit length-two path gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)=9\,\Delta_B(y_g=1/3,\ y_h=1\text{ otherwise})\ge0.
}
\]

Hence the corrected conclusion is unchanged:

\[
\boxed{\text{every }7+3\text{ graph is pair-Rayleigh at unit weights}.}
\]
