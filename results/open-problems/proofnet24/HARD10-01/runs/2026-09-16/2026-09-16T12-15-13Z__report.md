## 1. Exact target and result of this iteration

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, isolated vertices allowed.  For distinct edges `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F),
\]

or equivalently, for the multivariate forest polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\]

the unit-weight Rayleigh difference

\[
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}
\]

should be nonnegative.

The full conjecture remains **OPEN**.

The preceding checkpoint proved that an edge-minimal counterexample with `|R|=10`, if one exists, must have the unique remaining root-fiber partition

\[
4+4+1+1.
\]

### New theorem

Assume an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample has partition `4+4+1+1`, both size-four fibers are the required three-spoke stars, and their centers are distinct.  If the two star centers are joined by an edge, then this configuration cannot be an edge-minimal counterexample.

More precisely, after deleting the center connector, the two size-four fibers become a corrected cross-ear `3+3` configuration.  There are only two possible endpoint geometries:

1. the two ears have the same endpoint pair; this case is in fact pair-Rayleigh by an exact two-terminal replacement with terminal counts `(D,C)=(14,10)`;
2. the endpoint pairs are distinct; the corrected cross-ear theorem forces them to share one endpoint, and the full graph is a decorated six-vertex core with exact factor
   \[
   \boxed{\Delta_G(\mathbf1)=288\,\alpha_U(e)\alpha_V(f)}.
   \]
   Edge minimality makes both terminal responses nonnegative.

Thus **all distinct-center / connector-present `4+4+1+1` cases are closed**.

The cumulative global obstruction bound remains

\[
\boxed{|R|\ge 10},
\]

not `|R|>=11`, because the same-center regime and the distinct-center/no-connector regime are not yet universally closed.

## 4. Distinct centers with a center connector

Assume the `4+4+1+1` partition and let the two size-four roots be `P,Q`.

Write the `P`-fiber as the three-spoke star centered at `w`, and the `Q`-fiber as the three-spoke star centered at `x`, with

\[
w\ne x.
\]

The principal cycle `C_Q` must pass through `w`.  Otherwise all three `w`-spokes have an endpoint outside `C_Q`, hence all three are atoms of the `Q`-fiber; since those atoms must share center `x`, simplicity forces `x=w`, contradiction.  Thus `C_Q` uses exactly two `w`-spokes.  Symmetrically `C_P` uses exactly two `x`-spokes.

Call the traversed endpoint pairs

\[
A=\{u,v\}\quad\text{for the }w\text{-ear},
\qquad
B=\{r,s\}\quad\text{for the }x\text{-ear}.
\]

Now suppose the center connector

\[
wx\in E(G).
\]

Because `x\notin C_Q`, the edge `wx` cannot be one of the two `w`-spokes traversed by `C_Q`; it is the third atom of the `P`-star.  Likewise it is the third atom of the `Q`-star.

### 4.1 Both singleton cycles use the connector whenever the ear pairs intersect

Let `S` be either singleton root.  Singleton status forces `C_S` to contain every nonisolated vertex, so it contains `w,x`.  At each degree-three center it uses exactly two incident spokes.

Suppose `C_S` omitted `wx`.  Then it would have to use both edges of both ears.

* If `A=B`, those four ear edges already form the closed cycle `u-w-v-x-u`, leaving no room for the distinguished edges `e,f` on the same simple cycle.
* If `A` and `B` are distinct but share one endpoint, their four ear edges give degree four at the shared endpoint, impossible in a simple cycle.

Hence, whenever `A\cap B\ne\varnothing`, both singleton cycles use `wx`.

Delete `wx`.  The two singleton roots disappear and each four-fiber loses exactly its connector atom.  Therefore the resulting graph has exact partition

\[
\boxed{3+3},
\]

with the two size-three fibers precisely the ears `u-w-v` and `r-x-s`.

The corrected `3+3` cross-ear theorem now completes the endpoint classification.  If the endpoint pairs differ, suppressing the ears gives the exact-two decorated `K_4`, and the two inserted direct edges lie in opposite root differences.  In the four-edge exchange cycle, any edge from one root difference and any edge from the other share exactly one branch vertex.  Therefore the distinct endpoint pairs necessarily share one endpoint.  In particular, a hypothetical connector case with disjoint endpoint pairs cannot occur.

Thus the connector-present distinct-center regime has only the following two subcases.

## 5. Connector case I: equal ear endpoint pair

Assume

\[
A=B=\{u,v\}.
\]

After deleting `wx`, the corrected same-endpoint `3+3` geometry consists of two ears

\[
u-w-v,
\qquad
u-x-v
\]

and a common complementary `u-v` route carrying both distinguished edges.

The direct edge `uv` is absent: if it were present, the direct edge together with the common complementary route would be a third minimal common `e,f` cycle.

Let `N` be the five-edge two-terminal network with terminals `u,v` and internal vertices `w,x`:

\[
E(N)=\{uw,wv,ux,xv,wx\}.
\]

Equivalently `N=K_4-uv`.

Replace all of `N` by a fresh terminal edge `g=uv`, obtaining a graph `H`.

### 5.1 `H` has a unique minimal `R` root

A minimal common `e,f` cycle of `H` avoiding `g` would remain a minimal common cycle after expanding back to the connector-deleted graph, giving a root other than the two `3+3` roots.  Impossible.

A minimal common cycle using `g` can be expanded through either of the two ears.  It therefore lifts to one of the two `3+3` roots.  Conversely the two known roots both suppress to the same root of `H`.

Thus `H` has a unique minimal root.  By the archived unique-root theorem, `H` is weighted pair-Rayleigh.

### 5.2 Exact terminal response of the connector gadget

Direct enumeration of forests of `N` gives

\[
D_N=14,
\qquad
C_N=10,
\qquad
\frac{C_N}{D_N}=\frac57.
\]

The exact nondistinguished replacement identity therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=14^2\,
\Delta_H\!\left(y_g=\frac57\right)
=196\,
\Delta_H\!\left(y_g=\frac57\right)
\ge0.

\]

So the equal-endpoint connector case is pair-Rayleigh; no edge-minimality sign argument is required after the star structure has been established.
