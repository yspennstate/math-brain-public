## 1. Target and new result

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices.  For distinct edges `e,f`, write

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\qquad
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef}.
\]

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).

## 4. A local allowed-pair obstruction

The following elementary consequence of the bad-pair ear lemma is used repeatedly.

Let a root fiber have atoms `a,b,c`.  Suppose the double `a+b` is present but both doubles `a+c` and `b+c` are absent.  The two absent pairs are bad pairs, hence each pair is a two-edge ear.  Thus `a,c` share the same outside ear center and `b,c` share that same center.  It follows that `a,b` also form a two-edge ear, so `a+b` must be absent, contradiction.

Equivalently, in the graph `H` whose vertices are atoms and whose edges are the **allowed doubles**, every allowed edge `ab` must dominate all other atom vertices: for every third atom `c`, at least one of `ac,bc` must also be allowed.

This is the key combinatorial filter in the `9+1` classification below.

## 5. New theorem: `9+1` is pair-Rayleigh

### 5.1 Complete abstract size-nine down-set classification

Because the fiber has size nine, direct downward-closure counting gives only the following signatures:

* `k=8`: no doubles;
* `k=7`: one double;
* `k=6`: two doubles;
* `k=5`: three doubles;
* `k=4`: either four doubles and no triple, or three doubles together with the unique triple supported by those three doubles.

The exact checker independently enumerates all such labelled down-sets and obtains

```

k=7:  21 families, 1 double,  0 triples
k=6: 105 families, 2 doubles, 0 triples
k=5: 120 families, 3 doubles, 0 triples
k=4:  15 families, 4 doubles, 0 triples
      +4 families, 3 doubles, 1 triple
```

For `k=7,6,5`, the number `m` of allowed doubles satisfies `m<k-1`.  Take any allowed edge `ab`; to satisfy the local obstruction of Section 4, each of the other `k-2` vertices needs a distinct allowed edge to `a` or `b`, requiring at least `1+(k-2)=k-1` allowed doubles.  Contradiction.

For `k=4` with a triple, the allowed-double graph is a triangle plus an isolated fourth atom.  Any triangle edge has that isolated atom nonadjacent to both endpoints, contradicting Section 4.

For `k=4` with four doubles, the allowed-double graph is either a paw or a `4`-cycle.  In a paw, the edge opposite the pendant attachment has the pendant vertex nonadjacent to both endpoints, again contradicting Section 4.  Hence the only surviving four-atom form is a `4`-cycle.

Finally, for `k=8` every atom pair is bad.  The same bad-pair argument used in the archived star classifications forces all eight atoms to be spokes from one outside center.  The singleton cycle uses exactly two of those spokes and leaves six unused attachments on the two components of `C_P-{e,f}`.  Two unused attachments lie in the same component; their two spokes plus the complementary root-cycle arc form another common `e,f` cycle.  This contradicts the existence of only one singleton root.

Therefore every actual `9+1` graph is in the unique remaining shape: four atoms whose allowed-double graph is a `4`-cycle.

### 5.2 The `C_4` atom shape consists of two disjoint bad ears

Relabel the four atoms `a,b,c,d` so that the allowed doubles are

\[
ab,\ bc,\ cd,\ da
\]

and the two absent doubles are the diagonals `ac` and `bd`.

By the bad-pair lemma, `a,c` form a two-edge ear `E_1=u-w-v`, and `b,d` form a two-edge ear `E_2=r-x-s`.  Their centers are distinct: if the centers coincided, every cross pair would also be an ear and no `C_4` allowed-double pattern would remain.

Because `a,b,c,d` are all atoms, there is no further graph edge incident with either outside center; in particular both centers have degree two in the non-isolated graph.  The singleton common cycle must contain both centers (otherwise an atom would extend it), so it traverses **both ears in full**.

Let `g_1=uv` and `g_2=rs` be the direct terminal edges of the two ears.  Neither direct edge exists in `G`.  If, say, `g_1` existed, suppress `E_1` inside the singleton root.  Degree-two suppression preserves the old-vertex connectivity partition and hence preserves all three `R` tests.  The resulting `R(G)` forest contains `E_2` in full but not `E_1` in full.  No member of the nine-element `P`-fiber contains `E_2` in full because `bd` is a bad pair, and the only singleton root is the original one, which contains both ears.  Contradiction.  Thus `g_1,g_2` are both absent.

## 6. New theorem: `7+3` is pair-Rayleigh

Assume exact partition

\[
7+3.
\]

Let the size-three root be `Q`.  Its fiber has the standard ear form

\[
\mathcal F(Q)=\{Q,Q+ux,Q+xv\},
\]

where the outside center `x` has graph degree exactly two.

Let `P` be the size-seven root.  The archived seven-element classification gives two possible fiber shapes.

### 6.1 The large root must traverse the entire ear

If the size-seven fiber is a pure six-spoke star and `C_P` avoided `x`, both `ux,xv` would be atoms of that star.  Since they share outside center `x`, the star center would be `x`, forcing four additional graph edges incident with `x`.  This contradicts `deg(x)=2`.

If the size-seven fiber is the three-atom boundary (all proper subsets of three atoms), and `C_P` avoided `x`, then `ux,xv` would again be atoms.  Their double is present in the three-atom boundary, but the bad-pair ear lemma says the double of the two halves of an ear is absent.  Contradiction.

Therefore `C_P` contains `x`; degree two forces it to traverse both edges `ux,xv`.  Every member of the `P`-fiber contains that full ear.

### 6.2 The direct endpoint edge is absent

Let `g=uv`.  If `g` were already a graph edge, suppress the full ear in each of the seven members of the `P`-fiber.  Suppression is injective on these seven forests and preserves the `R` property.  Every image avoids both ear halves.  But in the original `7+3` family the only member avoiding both ear halves is the root `Q`; its two other fiber members contain one half each, while all seven members of the other fiber contain the full ear.  Seven distinct images cannot all equal `Q`.  Thus

\[
uv\notin E(G).
\]

Delete the ear and insert the fresh edge `g=uv`.  Exactly the seven members of the `P`-fiber suppress to `R`-forests containing `g`; the root `Q` is the unique `R`-forest avoiding `g`.  Hence the compressed graph `B` has exact partition

\[
\boxed{7+1}.
\]

The archived L76 theorem states that every `7+1` graph is **weighted** pair-Rayleigh.  Replacing `g` by the unit two-edge path gives

\[
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=9\,\Delta_B(y_g=1/3,\ y_h=1\text{ otherwise})\ge0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{every }7+3\text{ graph is pair-Rayleigh at unit weights}.}
\]

This conclusion is universal; it does not use edge minimality.

## 7. New theorem: no edge-minimal counterexample has `4+3+3`

Assume for contradiction that an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample has exact partition

\[
4+3+3.
\]

Let `P` be the size-four root and `Q,R` the two size-three roots, with ears `E_Q,E_R`.

A Boolean-square size-four fiber has bridge atoms and is excluded from an edge-minimal counterexample by the bridge factorization.  Thus the `P`-fiber is a three-spoke star.

### 7.1 The large root traverses both size-three ears

Consider `E_Q=u-x-v`.  If `C_P` avoided its center `x`, both ear edges would be atoms of the three-spoke `P`-fiber.  Since those two atoms share center `x`, the star center would be `x`, forcing the third atom to be another graph edge incident with `x`, contradicting the size-three fact `deg(x)=2`.  Therefore `P` traverses `E_Q` in full.  The same argument applies to `E_R`.

The standard relation between two size-three ears now leaves two cases: the ears are identical, or they are distinct and each size-three root traverses the other ear in full.

### 7.2 Cross-ear case is structurally impossible

Assume the ears are distinct.  Let their terminal pairs be represented by direct edges `g_Q,g_R`.

Neither direct edge exists.  For example, if `g_Q` existed, the four members of the `P`-fiber and all three members of the `R`-fiber contain `E_Q` in full.  Suppressing `E_Q` would give seven distinct `R(G)` forests avoiding both halves of `E_Q`, while among the original ten `R`-members only the root `Q` avoids both halves.  Contradiction.  Similarly `g_R` is absent.

Compress both ears.  The resulting graph `B` has exact partition

\[
4+1+1.
\]

All four members of the large fiber contain both compressed edges.  The singleton coming from `Q` contains `g_R` but not `g_Q`, while the singleton coming from `R` contains `g_Q` but not `g_R`.  Consequently **both** `g_Q` and `g_R` lie in all four large-fiber members and exactly one singleton: both have total `R(B)` incidence five.

But the archived corrected `4+1+1` wheel normal form, specifically Lemma 6.4 of the 02:26 checkpoint, has a **unique** incidence-five edge of this kind: the literal wheel exchange edge.  Two distinct compressed edges cannot both satisfy that characterization.  Contradiction.

Thus the cross-ear case cannot occur.

### 7.3 Common-ear case compresses to a marked `4+1+1` wheel

The two size-three fibers therefore share one ear

\[
E=u-x-v.
\]

The direct edge `g=uv` is absent.  If it existed, suppressing the full ear in the four members of the `P`-fiber would produce four distinct `R(G)` forests avoiding both ear halves, whereas only the two roots `Q,R` avoid both halves.  Contradiction.

Compress the common ear to a fresh edge `g`.  The four members of the `P`-fiber become the size-four fiber of a compressed graph `B`; the two size-three roots become the two singleton roots.  Hence `B` has exact partition

\[
4+1+1.
\]

The marked edge `g` is in all four members of the large fiber and in neither singleton, so it has total `R(B)` incidence four.  In the archived `4+1+1` normal form, this is the literal **present-direct** pair edge of the decorated wheel (the incidence-five literal exchange edge is the other marked orbit).

Use the canonical bare wheel with distinguished abstract edges `E=01`, `F=23` and the incidence-four present-direct edge `g=02` assigned weight `s`.  Exact forest enumeration gives

\[
A(s)=30+22s,
\]
\[
B(s)=18+12s,
\qquad
C(s)=24+10s,
\]
\[
D(s)=14+4s.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Delta_{W_4}(s)
=(18+12s)(24+10s)-(30+22s)(14+4s)
=12+40s+32s^2.
\]

At the effective path weight `s=1/3`,

\[
\Delta_{W_4}(1/3)=260/9.
\]

Restoring the two distinguished terminal networks in the wheel normal form gives

\[
\Delta_B(s)
=(12+40s+32s^2)\,\alpha_U(e)\alpha_V(f).
\]

Finally replace `g` by the original unit two-edge ear.  The replacement multiplier is `9`, so

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_G(\mathbf1)
=9\cdot\frac{260}{9}\,\alpha_U(e)\alpha_V(f)
=260\,\alpha_U(e)\alpha_V(f).
}
\]

In an edge-minimal counterexample the two terminal responses are nonnegative by the archived smaller-simple-graph realization.  Hence `Delta_G>=0`, contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{no edge-minimal counterexample has partition }4+3+3.}
\]
