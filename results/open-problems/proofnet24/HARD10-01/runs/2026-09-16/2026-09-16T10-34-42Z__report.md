## 4. Two reusable counting lemmas

### Lemma 4.1 — star pair-cycle lower bound

Let a minimal root `P` have a pure `d`-spoke star fiber

\[
\{P,P+wu_1,\ldots,P+wu_d\}.
\]

Delete the distinguished edges `e,f` from the principal cycle `C_P`. The remaining cycle edges form two path components. If two attachment vertices `u_i,u_j` lie in the same component, then

* the two spokes `wu_i,wu_j`, together with
* the complementary `u_i-u_j` arc of `C_P`

form a simple cycle containing both `e,f`. Hence they give a minimal `R` root distinct from `P` and from the one-spoke members of the `P`-fiber.

If the two path components contain `a` and `d-a` attachments, the number of these canonical extra roots is therefore at least

\[
\binom a2+\binom{d-a}{2}.
\]

In particular the minimum is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
d&\text{minimum}\cr\hline
4&2\\
5&4\\
6&6\\
7&9.
\end{array}
\]

Different attachment pairs give different roots because the two-spoke sets are different.

### Lemma 4.2 — repeated singleton spoke pairs

Suppose a star fiber has center `w`, and `m>=2` distinct singleton roots use the same pair of spokes `wx,wy`.

If the direct edge `xy` exists, replace `x-w-y` by `xy` in each singleton. Connectivity among the old vertices, acyclicity, and the three `R` tests are unchanged. Every image avoids all star spokes, and the only `R` member with that property is the principal root `P`. Distinct singleton roots would therefore collapse to the same root, impossible.

If `xy` is absent, delete the star spokes and insert a fresh edge `g=xy`. The compressed graph has exactly `m+1` minimal roots: `P` with `g` absent, and the `m` suppressed singleton roots with `g` present. Thus `g` has incidence `m` in an all-minimal family of size `m+1`.

For the values needed in this run, `m=2,...,7`, the all-minimal product theorem rules this out:

* `m+1=3,5,7` cannot factor as `rs` with `r,s>=2`;
* `m+1=4=2x2`, but incidence `3` is not even;
* `m+1=6=2x3`, but incidence `5` is divisible by neither `2` nor `3`;
* `m+1=8=2x4`, but incidence `7` is divisible by neither `2` nor `4`.

Hence no two singleton roots can use the same star-spoke pair in any of the cases below.

For `4+4+1+1`, the six-vertex exact data show same-center and distinct-center regimes, with the distinct-center cases separated by the presence of the center connector and by whether the two traversed ear pairs coincide or share one endpoint. The correct next deliverable is a universal structural classification into these regimes followed by symbolic core Rayleigh polynomials, not a larger blind enumeration.

A separate conceptual route worth keeping active is the matroid independence-polynomial formulation: the graph forest polynomial is the independence polynomial of the graphic matroid. The current regular-matroid `I`-Rayleigh conjecture is a natural stronger umbrella, and any decomposition theorem compatible with regular-matroid sums could potentially replace the current multiplicity-by-multiplicity analysis.
