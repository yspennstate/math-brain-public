## 1. Exact target and new result

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple graph and let `F` be uniform over **all** acyclic edge subsets of `E`, including forests with isolated vertices. For distinct edges `e,f`, the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\text{ acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\qquad
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef},
\]

one wants `Delta_G{e,f}(1)>=0` for every finite simple graph and every distinct pair.

## 4. Set up a hypothetical `5+4+1` edge-minimal counterexample

Assume for contradiction that `G` is an edge-minimal unit-weight counterexample whose root-fiber partition is

\[
5+4+1.
\]

Let `P` be the size-five root. By the universal size-five classification,

\[
\mathcal F(P)
=
\{P,P+wa,P+wb,P+wc,P+wd\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `w` is the outside center and `a,b,c,d` are attachment vertices on the principal cycle `C_P`.

Let `Q` be the size-four root and `S` the singleton root.

### 4.1 The four `P` attachments split `2+2`

Delete `e,f` from the simple cycle `C_P`. The remaining cycle edges form two path components. If two attachment vertices lie in the same component, the two corresponding `w`-spokes together with the complementary arc of `C_P` form a new simple cycle containing both `e,f`, hence a minimal root outside the `P` fiber.

For four attachments the minimum number of such roots is two, attained only by a `2+2` split between the two components.

There are exactly two roots outside the size-five fiber, namely `Q,S`. Therefore equality is forced. After relabelling,

* `a,b` lie in one component of `C_P-{e,f}`;
* `c,d` lie in the other;
* `Q` is the canonical two-spoke root using `wa,wb`;
* `S` is the canonical two-spoke root using `wc,wd`.

Thus the two outside spoke pairs are disjoint.

In particular

\[
C_Q
=
wa\cup wb\cup
\bigl(\text{the complementary }a\text{-}b\text{ arc of }C_P\bigr),
\tag{4.2}
\]

and that complementary arc contains both distinguished edges and the entire opposite `c,d` component.

A useful consequence of (4.2) is:

\[
\boxed{a,b,c,d\in V(C_Q).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The pair endpoints `a,b` lie on `C_Q` by definition, while `c,d` lie on the complementary arc.

## 5. The size-four fiber is a star with a distinct center

A Boolean-square size-four fiber has bridge atoms. Bridge factorization would reduce a negative Rayleigh difference to a strictly smaller graph, contradicting edge minimality. Therefore `Q` has a three-spoke star fiber:

\[
\mathcal F(Q)
=
\{Q,Q+xr,Q+xs,Q+xt\},
\tag{5.1}
\]

with star center

\[
x\notin V(C_Q).
\]

Since `Q` contains the center `w` of the `P` star, we have

\[
x\ne w.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The one-edge extension criterion applied to `Q` shows that the only graph edges incident with `x` are the three spokes in (5.1). Thus

\[
\deg_G(x)=3.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.1 Both `P` and `S` pass through `x`

Suppose first that `x\notin V(C_P)`. Then all three `x`-spokes have an endpoint outside `C_P`, so all three are atoms of the size-five fiber of `P`. But every atom of the `P` fiber is a `w`-spoke. Since `x\ne w`, at most the connector `xw` can be an edge belonging to both star edge sets; three distinct `x`-spokes cannot all be `w`-spokes. Contradiction.

Hence `x\in V(C_P)`. By (5.3), the simple cycle `C_P` uses exactly two of `xr,xs,xt`.

Because `S` is a singleton root, every nonisolated graph vertex lies on `C_S`: otherwise an edge incident with a vertex outside `C_S` would be a one-edge extension. In particular `x\in V(C_S)`, and again by (5.3), `C_S` uses exactly two `x`-spokes.

Therefore the two spoke pairs selected by `P` and `S` from the three-element set `{xr,xs,xt}` are either identical or overlap in exactly one spoke.

### 5.2 There is no center connector `xw`

In fact the two star edge sets are disjoint.

If `xw` were an atom of the `P` star, then `x` would be one of the four `P` attachment vertices `a,b,c,d`. But (4.3) says every one of those attachment vertices lies on `C_Q`, whereas the star center `x` is outside `C_Q`. Contradiction.

Thus

\[
\boxed{xw\notin E(G)}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and no `Q`-star spoke is a `P`-star atom.

This point removes the only possible ambiguity in the deletion arguments below.

## 6. Overlapping `x`-spoke pairs are impossible

Assume the pairs selected by `P` and `S` are distinct. Relabel the three `Q` spokes so that

\[
C_P\text{ uses }xr,xs,
\qquad
C_S\text{ uses }xs,xt.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus `xt` is used by `S` but not by `P`.

Delete the graph edge `xt`.

Because of (5.4), `xt` is not an atom of the `P` fiber, so all five members of `\mathcal F(P)` survive unchanged. In the `Q` fiber, exactly the member `Q+xt` disappears, leaving

\[
\{Q,Q+xr,Q+xs\},
\]

a size-three fiber. The singleton `S` disappears because its cycle uses `xt`.

Deleting a nondistinguished graph edge does not change the `R` status of any subset that does not use that edge. Therefore the resulting graph has exact root-fiber partition

\[
\boxed{5+3}.
\tag{6.2}

But the archived Theorem 5.1 of `2026-09-16T02-26-12Z__report.md` proves universally that no graph has root-fiber partition `5+3`.

Contradiction.

Hence the overlapping-pair case is impossible.

## 7. Equal `x`-spoke pairs are also impossible

We are left with the case

\[
C_P\text{ and }C_S\text{ both use }xr,xs,
\qquad
xt\text{ is used by neither.}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Delete `xt`. Again (5.4) guarantees that the entire size-five `P` fiber remains unchanged. The `Q` fiber drops from size four to size three, while the singleton `S` survives. Thus the deleted graph `H=G-xt` has exact partition

\[
\boxed{5+3+1}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The archived complete `5+3+1` theorem applies.

### 7.1 Location of the third attachment `t`

The edge `xt` is omitted by `P`. Since `x\in V(C_P)` and `xt` is not a `P` atom by (5.4), the one-edge extension criterion forces

\[
t\in V(C_P).
\tag{7.3}
\]

The singleton `S` also omits `xt`, and `x\in V(C_S)`. Singleton status therefore forces

\[
t\in V(C_S).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Hence

\[
t\in V(C_P)\cap V(C_S).
\tag{7.5}
\]

### 7.2 Use the complete `5+3+1` wheel geometry

In `H`, the size-three ear is

\[
r-x-s.

Compress it to the direct edge `rs`. The archived `5+3+1` theorem says the resulting graph is the complete decorated `5+1+1` four-wheel:

* the other direct-pair rim edge `cd` is literal;
* the two opposite distinguished branches are paths/networks joining `r` to `c` and `s` to `d` and contain `e` and `f` separately;
* the singleton descended from `Q` uses the spoke pair `{wr,ws}`;
* the singleton `S` uses the disjoint pair `{wc,wd}`.

Choose the root-cycle paths and call them

\[
X:c\leadsto r,
\qquad
Y:d\leadsto s,
\]

with, after interchanging the distinguished labels if necessary,

\[
e\in E(X),
\qquad
f\in E(Y).
\tag{7.6}
\]

The two relevant root cycles in the uncompressed `H` are

\[
C_P=(r-x-s)\cup Y\cup(cd)\cup X,
\tag{7.7}
\]

and

\[
C_S=(r-x-s)\cup Y\cup(dw)\cup(wc)\cup X.
\tag{7.8}
\]

By (7.5), and because `xt` is distinct from the two ear edges, `t` lies on `X` or `Y` (including the endpoints `c,d`).

### 7.3 A forced additional common cycle

Suppose first that `t\in V(X)`. Consider

\[
D_1
=
xt\cup X[t,r]\cup rw\cup wd\cup Y[d,s]\cup sx,
\tag{7.9}
\]

and

\[
D_2
=
xt\cup X[t,c]\cup cw\cup wd\cup Y[d,s]\cup sx.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Both are simple cycles. Both contain the distinguished edge `f`, because both contain all of the root path `Y[d,s]`. The two subpaths `X[t,r]` and `X[t,c]` partition the path `X` at `t`, so exactly one of `D_1,D_2` contains the distinguished edge `e`.

Therefore one of them is a new simple cycle containing **both** `e,f`.

If instead `t\in V(Y)`, use

\[
D_3
=
xt\cup Y[t,s]\cup sw\cup wc\cup X[c,r]\cup rx,
\tag{7.11}

and

\[
D_4
=
xt\cup Y[t,d]\cup dw\cup wc\cup X[c,r]\cup rx.
\tag{7.12}

Both contain `e`, and exactly one contains `f`.

In either case there is a simple common `e,f` cycle using the edge `xt`. Its cycle-minus-`{e,f}` root is distinct from `P,Q,S`, so the full graph has an additional minimal root. That contradicts the assumed root-fiber partition `5+4+1`.

Thus the equal-pair case is impossible as well.
