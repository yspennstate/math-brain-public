## 1. Exact target and result of this run

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph and let \(F\) be uniform over **all**
acyclic edge subsets of \(E\), including forests with isolated vertices.  For
distinct edges \(e,f\), the target is

\[
\Pr(e,f\in F)\le \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F).
\]

Equivalently, for the forest independence polynomial

\[
I_G(\mathbf y)=\sum_{A\subseteq E:\,A\ {\rm acyclic}}\mathbf y^A,
\qquad
\Delta_G\{e,f\}=(I_G)_e(I_G)_f-I_G(I_G)_{ef},
\]

one wants \(\Delta_G\{e,f\}(\mathbf 1)\ge0\) for every finite simple graph and
every distinct pair \(e,f\).

The full arbitrary-graph conjecture remains **OPEN**.

The new theorem in this run closes the last unresolved root-fiber partition at
\(|R|=8\):

\[
\boxed{\text{No edge-minimal counterexample has partition }5+1+1+1.}
\]

eight-member layer to exactly that partition, gives the new cumulative bound

\[
\boxed{\text{every edge-minimal counterexample, if one exists, has }|R|\ge9.}
\]

The proof is structural.  Exact finite enumeration is used only as an

  arXiv:2205.07318, Section 4.2, is the primary source for the uniform-forest
  conjecture.  The measure is uniform on **all forests** of a finite simple
  graph, not uniform spanning trees and not a fixed-component ensemble.
  Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07318 .
* Pengfei Tang and Zibo Zhang, *Pairwise Negative Correlation for Uniform
  Spanning Subgraphs of the Complete Graph*, arXiv:2603.10738, submitted
  11 March 2026, treats specified spanning-subgraph families of \(K_n\),
  including forests with exactly \(k\) components for sufficiently large
  \(n\).  This is not the arbitrary-graph/all-forest target.
  Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10738 .

  high-degree regular graphs*, arXiv:2609.11578, submitted 10 September 2026,
  explicitly says that even pairwise negative correlation for \(q<1\) remains
  open on general finite graphs.
  Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11578 .

## 4. The last \(|R|=8\) configuration

Assume for contradiction that \(G\) is an edge-minimal unit-weight
counterexample with root-fiber partition

\[
5+1+1+1.
\]

Let \(P\) be the size-five root.  By the archived size-five classification,

\[
\mathcal F(P)
=
\{P,\ P+wa,\ P+wb,\ P+wc,\ P+wd\},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where \(w\notin V(C_P)\), the attachment vertices \(a,b,c,d\) are distinct
vertices of \(C_P\), and the four displayed spokes are all the one-edge
extensions of \(P\).

Every singleton root must pass through \(w\).  Otherwise every spoke has the
outside endpoint \(w\) outside its common cycle and would extend that root,
contrary to singleton status.  A simple cycle through \(w\) uses exactly two
incident spokes.  Hence each singleton is labelled by a two-element subset of

\[
\{a,b,c,d\}.
\]

Call these three spoke pairs the **pair graph** \(\Gamma\) on
\(\{a,b,c,d\}\).

## 5. The three singleton spoke pairs are distinct

Suppose \(m\ge2\) singleton roots use the same spoke pair \(\{x,y\}\).

If the direct edge \(xy\) already exists, suppress the ear \(x-w-y\) in each
such singleton root.  The suppressed forest is in \(R(G)\) and avoids all four
spokes.  In (4.1) and the three singleton fibers, the only \(R(G)\)-member
avoiding every spoke is \(P\).  Therefore every singleton using \(\{x,y\}\)
would equal

\[
P-xy+\{wx,wy\},
\]

so they would not be distinct.

Now suppose \(xy\notin E(G)\).  Delete all four spokes and add the new simple
edge \(g=xy\), obtaining \(B\).  An \(R(B)\)-forest avoiding \(g\) is exactly
\(P\).  An \(R(B)\)-forest containing \(g\) expands \(g\) to \(x-w-y\) and is
exactly one of the \(m\) singleton roots using that pair.  Thus \(B\) has
exactly \(m+1\) minimal roots, and \(g\) is present in exactly \(m\) of them.

There are only two possibilities:

* \(m=2\): \(B\) has three all-minimal roots, impossible by the all-minimal
  Cartesian-product theorem;
* \(m=3\): \(B\) has four all-minimal roots, hence a \(2\times2\) product, so
  every edge has even root incidence; but \(g\) has incidence three.

Therefore the three singleton spoke pairs are distinct.

## 6. The pair graph is a path \(P_4\)

\(ab,ad\) exists, while deleting \(wb\) says exactly one of \(ac,ad\) exists.
Three Boolean variables cannot be pairwise unequal.  Contradiction.

\[
\boxed{

ab,\quad bc,\quad cd.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let the corresponding singleton roots be \(Q_{ab},Q_{bc},Q_{cd}\).
