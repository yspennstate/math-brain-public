## 3. B1 — change category: exact Hellinger transfer cocycle

The scalar sequence `A_n` has a more useful exact one-step decomposition.

For a level-`n` dyadic cell `v` with positive mass under both measures, let

`p_v := mu(v0)/mu(v)`,

`q_v := nu(v0)/nu(v)`,

where `v0,v1` are the two dyadic children (zero-mass children are interpreted in the evident limiting way). Define the normalized Hellinger weight on level `n`

`w_n(v) := sqrt(mu(v)nu(v))/A_n`.

Then `sum_v w_n(v)=1`, and direct expansion gives the exact cocycle identity

`A_{n+1}/A_n = sum_v w_n(v) h(p_v,q_v)`.

Equivalently, with

`L_n := sum_v w_n(v) ell(p_v,q_v)`,

we have

`A_{n+1} = A_n (1-L_n)`.

Thus the source question is a deterministic Feynman--Kac / transfer-operator problem: the row weight from parent `v` to child `u` is

`sqrt( mu(u)/mu(v) * nu(u)/nu(v) )`,

whose row sum is the local Hellinger factor `h(p_v,q_v)<=1`. The total surviving mass after `n` steps is exactly `A_n`.

### Quantitative discrepancy lower bound

For every `p,q in [0,1]`,

`ell(p,q) >= (p-q)^2/2`.

Proof: set

`a=(sqrt(p),sqrt(1-p))`, `b=(sqrt(q),sqrt(1-q))`.

Both are unit vectors, and

`ell(p,q)=1-a.b = (1/2)||a-b||_2^2`.

Also

`|p-q|`

`= (1/2)( |p-q| + |(1-p)-(1-q)| )`

`= (1/2) sum_i |a_i^2-b_i^2|`

`<= (1/2)||a-b||_2 ||a+b||_2`

`<= ||a-b||_2`.

Squaring gives the claim. The constant `1/2` is sharp to second order near `p=q=1/2`.

Consequently

`-log A_N = sum_{n=0}^{N-1} -log(1-L_n)`

`>= sum_{n=0}^{N-1} L_n`

`>= (1/2) sum_{n=0}^{N-1} sum_{v in D_n} w_n(v)(p_v-q_v)^2.`

Therefore the following is a sufficient source-matching singularity criterion:

`sum_n E_{w_n}[(p_v-q_v)^2] = infinity  =>  nu_2 \perp nu_3.`

Conversely, if `A_infinity>0`, then `sum_n -log(1-L_n)<infinity`, hence `sum_n L_n<infinity`, and in particular

`sum_n E_{w_n}[(p_v-q_v)^2] < infinity.`

So a counterexample to singularity would have to force the harmonic-vs-Cantor conditional split discrepancy to become square-summable under the *Hellinger-tilted* level weights. This is much sharper than the historical iid-digit analogy and identifies the exact extremal behavior a non-singular pair would need.

### Block version

The same identity iterates. For a level-`n` cell `v`, let `H_m(v)` be the Hellinger affinity between the conditional `m`-step descendant distributions of `mu` and `nu` inside `v`. Then

`A_{n+m}/A_n = sum_{v in D_n} w_n(v) H_m(v)`.

Hence any recurrent block class carrying uniformly positive Hellinger weight and satisfying `H_m(v)<=1-delta` yields a multiplicative contraction. The unresolved issue is now to prove recurrence of such a class for the actual conductance scenery, not merely to find one asymmetric cell.

## 4. B7 — take the limit: finite wired conductances really converge to the infinite harmonic split

Let `T_v` be any descendant coding subtree rooted at a dyadic vertex `v`. Let `C_r(v)` be its effective conductance when all descendants at graph distance `r` from `v` are wired to a sink, with unit edge conductances.

### Lemma 4.1 — wired exhaustion

`C_r(v)` is nonincreasing in `r` and converges to the effective conductance `C_infinity(v)` from `v` to infinity.

Proof. Shorting the level-`r` vertices in the depth-`r+1` network to the sink can only increase conductance, and produces the depth-`r` wired network. Hence `C_{r+1}(v)<=C_r(v)`. Equivalently the finite effective resistances `R_r=1/C_r` are nondecreasing.

Let `R_infinity` be the infimum energy of unit flows from `v` to infinity. Restricting any infinite unit flow to a depth-`r` cutset gives a unit flow to that wired cutset, so `R_infinity>=R_r` for all `r`. Conversely, if `sup_r R_r<infinity`, take the finite energy-minimizing unit flows and use diagonal compactness on the countable edge set; a subsequence converges edgewise to a unit flow to infinity, and lower semicontinuity of the sum of squared edge currents gives energy at most `sup_r R_r`. Thus `R_infinity<=sup_r R_r`. If `sup_r R_r=infinity`, both sides are infinite. Therefore `R_r ↑ R_infinity` and `C_r ↓ C_infinity`. QED.

For a child `u` of `v`, the branch conductance through the parent edge is

`G(u)=C_infinity(u)/(1+C_infinity(u))`.

The finite wired branch weights converge monotonically to these values. At a transient branching vertex, the infinite harmonic-current split is therefore

`p_v = G(u_0)/(G(u_0)+G(u_1))`.

This justifies taking finite wired data seriously, provided one has a strict lower/upper comparison that survives the limit.

### Theorem 5.1 — an actual infinite coding-tree split differs from Cantor--Lebesgue

`>= 1022889314685026304 / 2016575497240830415`

`= 0.5072407733...`,

`29203132129222193 / 4033150994481660830`

`= 0.0072407733...`.

#### Proof, step 1: Cantor masses

The exact Cantor masses are

`nu(I_4^7)=1/32`,

`nu(I_8^8)=nu(I_9^8)=1/64`.

Thus `q=1/2`.

#### Proof, step 2: a uniform Frostman estimate for Cantor measure

Let `d=log_3 2`. For every real interval `J`,

`nu(J) <= 2 |J|^d`.

To see this, choose `m` so that `a:=3^{-(m+1)} <= |J| < 3a`. The level-`m+1` Cantor basic intervals have length `a`, mass `a^d=2^{-(m+1)}`, and gaps between consecutive basic intervals are at least `a`. An interval of length `<3a` can meet positive Cantor measure in at most two of these basic intervals (three would require traversing a gap, a full intervening basic interval, and another gap, total at least `3a`; endpoint equalities carry zero mass). Hence

`nu(J) <= 2 a^d <= 2 |J|^d`.

#### Proof, step 3: explicit finite-energy unit flow on the left subtree

Condition `nu` on `A:=I_8^8`, whose mass is `1/64`, and use this conditional boundary measure as a unit flow down the coding subtree rooted at `A`. At descendant depth `r`, the energy contribution is

`S_r := sum_{J subset A, J in D_{8+r}} (nu(J)/nu(A))^2`.

The exact first eight levels, recomputed by the checker, satisfy

`sum_{r=1}^8 S_r = 955961/524288`.

For `r>=9`, since the conditional masses sum to one,

`S_r <= max_J nu(J)/nu(A)`.

Every such `J` has length `2^{-(8+r)}`, so the Frostman bound gives

`S_r <= 128 q0^{8+r}`,

where `q0:=2^{-d}`. The elementary integer inequalities

`3^3<2^5` and `3^5<2^8`

imply `d>3/5` and then `q0<2^{-3/5}<2/3`. Therefore

`sum_{r>=9} S_r`

`< 128 sum_{r>=9} (2/3)^{8+r}`

`= 384(2/3)^17`

`= 16777216/43046721`.

Thus the total energy of this explicit unit flow is strictly less than

`E_A := 955961/524288 + 16777216/43046721`

`= 49947079476089/22568879259648`.

By Thomson's principle the effective resistance below `A` is at most `E_A`. Including the single parent-to-`A` edge, the true branch conductance therefore satisfies

`G_A >= 1/(1+E_A)`

`= 22568879259648/72515958735737`

`= 0.3112263790...`.

#### Proof, step 4: finite wired upper bound on the right subtree

For `B:=I_9^8`, the exact radius-6 wired effective conductance below `B` is

By Lemma 4.1, `C_infinity(B)<=C_6(B)`. Hence its true branch conductance from the parent is at most

`= 13703/45323`

The exact difference between the lower bound for `G_A` and the upper bound for `G_B` is positive:

`22568879259648/72515958735737 - 13703/45323`

`= 29203132129222193/3286640797779808051 > 0`.

Therefore `G_A>G_B`, so the true infinite harmonic current at the parent is left-biased. Substituting the lower/upper branch bounds gives the displayed quantitative split lower bound. QED.
