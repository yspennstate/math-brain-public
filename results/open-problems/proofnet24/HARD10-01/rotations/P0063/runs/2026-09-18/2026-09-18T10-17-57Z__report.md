## 2. B18 — exact reformulation by dyadic Hellinger affinity

This is the strongest mathematical outcome of the run. It is a general measure-theoretic reduction, not claimed as novel.

Let

`mu := nu_2`, `nu := nu_3`.

For each `n`, let `D_n` denote the usual half-open dyadic partition of `[0,1]` into `2^n` cells, with the final cell closed at `1`, and restrict those cells to `E`. Define the finite-partition Hellinger affinity

`A_n := sum_{I in D_n} sqrt(mu(I) nu(I)).`

### Theorem 2.1 — exact source-matching singularity criterion

For these two probability measures on `E`,

`nu_2 \perp nu_3  <=>  A_n -> 0.`

This statement is exact: it does not assume a product structure, stationarity, an iid digit process, or an asymptotic dimension formula.

### Proof

Put

`rho := (mu + nu)/2`,

and let

`f := dmu/drho`, `g := dnu/drho`.

Since `mu <= 2 rho` and `nu <= 2 rho`, we have `0 <= f,g <= 2` `rho`-a.e. Let `F_n` be the sigma-field generated by the dyadic partition `D_n`, and define

`f_n := E_rho[f | F_n]`, `g_n := E_rho[g | F_n]`.

On a dyadic cell `I` with `rho(I)>0`, these conditional expectations are constant and satisfy

`f_n|_I = mu(I)/rho(I)`, `g_n|_I = nu(I)/rho(I)`.

Therefore

`integral sqrt(f_n g_n) d rho`

`= sum_{I in D_n} rho(I) sqrt( mu(I)/rho(I) * nu(I)/rho(I) )`

`= sum_{I in D_n} sqrt(mu(I) nu(I))`

`= A_n.`

The dyadic sigma-fields increase to the Borel sigma-field on `[0,1]` (hence on `E`). By the martingale convergence theorem,

`f_n -> f`, `g_n -> g`

`rho`-a.s. and in `L^1(rho)`. Moreover `0 <= f_n,g_n <= 2`, so

`0 <= sqrt(f_n g_n) <= 2`.

Dominated convergence gives

`A_n -> integral sqrt(f g) d rho =: A_infty.`

Finally,

`mu \perp nu`

is equivalent to the existence of disjoint measurable supports modulo null sets, which is equivalent to `f g = 0` `rho`-a.e. Since `sqrt(fg)>=0`, that is equivalent to

`integral sqrt(fg) d rho = 0.`

Hence `mu \perp nu` iff `A_infty=0`, equivalently iff `A_n -> 0`. QED.

### Monotonicity

The sequence `A_n` is nonincreasing. Indeed, if a parent dyadic cell `I` is split into children `I_0,I_1`, then Cauchy--Schwarz gives

`sqrt(mu(I_0)nu(I_0)) + sqrt(mu(I_1)nu(I_1))`

`<= sqrt((mu(I_0)+mu(I_1))(nu(I_0)+nu(I_1)))`

`= sqrt(mu(I)nu(I)).`

Summing over parents gives `A_{n+1} <= A_n`.

This converts the source question into a scalar asymptotic problem: prove that the exact finite-partition affinities go to zero, or refute singularity by proving their limit is positive.

### Local binary loss identity

Whenever a parent cell `I` has positive mass under both measures, write the two conditional left-child probabilities as

`p_I := mu(I_0)/mu(I)`, `q_I := nu(I_0)/nu(I)`.

Then the contribution of its children relative to the parent contribution is

`h(p_I,q_I) := sqrt(p_I q_I) + sqrt((1-p_I)(1-q_I)) <= 1`,

with equality iff `p_I=q_I`. Moreover the loss has the exact identity

`1-h(p,q)`

`= 1/2 [ (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2 + (sqrt(1-p)-sqrt(1-q))^2 ].`

Thus the source problem can be attacked by proving that local conditional mismatches accumulate under the Hellinger-weighted mass rather than by pretending that the dyadic digits are iid Bernoulli. This is the main conceptual replacement for the historical failed Bernoulli analogy.

At a branching vertex `v` with children `u_0,u_1`, if `C(u)` is the effective conductance from `u` to infinity inside the descendant subtree, the branch conductance through edge `(v,u)` is

`G(u) = C(u)/(1+C(u))`,

and the harmonic-current split is proportional to `G(u)`. This is a global recursive quantity, not a fixed digit probability.

The exact finite test in Section 4 found a concrete dyadic parent where `nu_3` splits exactly `1/2 : 1/2` while every checked finite wired approximation to the harmonic current splits strictly away from one half in the same direction. ## 5. What the run learned

The old analogy “different Bernoulli digit biases imply singularity” was too crude. The exact source problem is better viewed as a comparison between two measures on the **same Euclidean Cantor set through dyadic refining partitions**:

- `nu_3` has exactly computable dyadic cylinder masses from the Cantor function;
- `nu_2` is induced by effective-conductance current splits on the deterministic dyadic coding tree;
- mutual singularity is **exactly** equivalent to decay of the dyadic Hellinger affinity `A_n` to zero.

Thus the live problem is to show that conductance-driven local mismatch recurs strongly enough along increasingly fine dyadic scenery to force cumulative Hellinger loss. One local mismatch is not enough. What is needed is either:

1. a block Hellinger contraction that occurs with a uniform positive frequency/mass; or
2. a dimension/entropy theorem showing that the harmonic measure on `T_[2](E)` has Euclidean dimension strictly below `log 2/log 3`; or
3. on the contrary, a mechanism keeping `A_n` bounded away from zero, which would refute the source assertion.

The incommensurability of scales `2` and `3` is now visible as the central difficulty: simple subtree inclusion fails, and the deterministic coding environment does not immediately fall under the stationary Galton--Watson dimension-drop theorem.
