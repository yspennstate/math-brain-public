## 2. B22 — read the proof as a hostile referee

I targeted the first lines most likely to fail under expert review rather than producing another ordinary lemma.

### 2.1 Source coding-tree / endpoint convention

The source coding tree uses closed b-adic coding intervals, whereas the Hellinger calculation is cleanest with a half-open dyadic partition. This does not change either pushforward measure. Cantor--Lebesgue measure is atomless. Harmonic measure on a transient unit-conductance tree has no atom at a single end: an atom of mass `a>0` would force current at least `a` along every edge of that ray, giving infinite energy, contrary to finiteness of the unit current. The countable dual-coding endpoint set is therefore null for both measures. This exact issue had already been recorded in the preceding P0063 report and survives reinspection.

### 2.2 Electrical-current interpretation

At a branching vertex, conditional harmonic measure among descendant branches is the normalized electrical current split. If a child subtree has descendant effective conductance `C`, then after the unit parent-child edge its branch conductance is `G=C/(1+C)`. Repeated random-walk excursions that return to the branching vertex cancel when conditioning on the successful escaping branch, leaving probabilities proportional to these `G` values. Hence the theorem's use of

`p = G_L/(G_L+G_R)`

is source-valid for simple random walk on the actual coding tree.

The open-neighborhood argument also survives. For the left trial flow, finite-depth energies are continuous in `(u,r)` and the Frostman estimate supplies a geometric tail bound uniform when `r` stays in a compact interval away from zero. Thus the complete trial-flow energy varies continuously. ### 2.4 Hellinger overlap law

Under hypothetical non-singularity, the theorem defines `rho=(mu+nu)/2`, dyadic martingale densities `f_n,g_n`, and

`d lambda_n/d rho = sqrt(f_n g_n)/A_n`.

Because `0<=f_n,g_n<=2`, dominated martingale convergence gives `sqrt(f_n g_n)->sqrt(fg)` in `L^1(rho)`. Since `A_n->A_infinity>0`, this is total-variation convergence `lambda_n->lambda`, not merely weak convergence. On every level-n dyadic cell `v`, exact integration gives `lambda_n(v)=sqrt(mu(v)nu(v))/A_n=w_n(v)`. Therefore the later transfer of the **varying** good-cell events is justified by

`|lambda_n(G_n)-lambda(G_n)| <= ||lambda_n-lambda||_TV -> 0`.

This bridge survives hardening.

## 3. B23 — try to falsify the proof

I attacked four concrete failure modes.

### 3.1 Could overlap mass hide on balanced sceneries?

Not if `A_infinity>0`. The overlap law `lambda` is fixed, satisfies `lambda<<nu_3`, and `lambda_n->lambda` in total variation. Lemma 5.1 applies to every law absolutely continuous to `nu_3`, so the Hellinger weights cannot be postulated to live on an exceptional nonmixing scenery law without contradicting the proved TV limit.

### 3.2 Could tiny geometric perturbations destroy the harmonic gap?

Globally, yes: a scaled Cantor set can cross a dyadic boundary and alter the finite coding topology. The proof does **not** assume global conductance continuity. It works inside a no-tangency neighborhood certified at all finite boundaries relevant to the radius-6 comparison, while controlling the infinite left side by a uniform variational tail. Thus the natural counterexample mechanism is explicitly excluded only where needed.

### 3.3 Could Allen--Chow--Yu's digit positions be reversed relative to the proof?

The published Lemma 2.6 explicitly counts positions from the left and its proof identifies the `(j-1)`st and `j`th ternary digits of `2^{n+1}m`, exactly matching the product index `j`. This agrees with the theorem report's use of the middle digit interval `K+1,...,m_n-L`.

### 3.4 Could a bad scenery subsequence evade irrational recurrence?

The proof does not assume unique scenery ergodicity. The scale variable is deterministic:

`r_n = 3^({alpha n}-1)`, `alpha=log_3 2`.

Irrational rotation alone gives positive density of visits to the fixed open scale interval `R`. On those times, Lemma 5.1 supplies the position/suffix distribution for the fixed overlap law. Multiple other subsequential scenery laws are irrelevant to this particular recurrent open event.

No source-level counterexample or genuine proof gap was found.

## 4. B27 — quantifier and limit-order audit

The quantifiers were checked explicitly.

### 4.1 Robust gap

The order is: first choose the strict base electrical margin; then shrink a **single fixed** open product neighborhood `U x R`; then choose a **single fixed** `eta>0` valid for every `(u,r)` in that neighborhood. Later recurrence uses these fixed objects. There is no `eta_n` tending to zero.

### 4.2 Arithmetic mixing

For Lemma 5.1, the valid limit order is:

1. fix suffix length `L`, suffix word `s`, Fourier frequency `k`, and cylinder approximation depth `K`;
2. let `n->infinity`;
3. only afterwards let `K->infinity` in the `L^1(nu_3)` approximation of `d lambda/d nu_3`;
4. pass from trigonometric polynomials to continuous functions, and then to interval indicators whose endpoints are Lebesgue-null.

For fixed nonzero `k`, with `y_n=2^{n+1}|k|`, multiplication by `2^{n+1}` appends binary zeroes, so `D_2(y_n)` is bounded. The imported digit-change theorem forces total `D_3(y_n)->infinity`. Meanwhile the ternary digit length satisfies

`Delta_3(y_n)=floor((n+1)log_3 2 + log_3|k|)+1 = m_n + O_k(1)`.

Removing the first `K` positions, the final `L` positions, and the bounded `O_k(1)` digit-length discrepancy deletes only `O_{K,L,k}(1)` changes. Hence the truncated middle count required by Allen--Chow--Yu Lemma 2.6 still tends to infinity. No interchange of `k` with `n` is used.

### 4.3 Recurrence subsequence

Lemma 5.1 converges along the full sequence `n`, so it remains valid on the deterministic positive-density subsequence `N_R={n:r_n in R}`. Total-variation convergence also holds along every subsequence. Thus, along `N_R`, the fixed positive limit `|U|/4` transfers from `lambda` to the varying level measures `lambda_n=w_n`. This is enough to produce a fixed positive Hellinger-loss contribution infinitely often; no summation-density estimate stronger than this is needed.

### 4.4 Suffix geometry

For a level-m Cantor cylinder of length `ell` ending in ternary suffix `22`, the previous cylinder (if present) has right endpoint exactly `ell` to the left. If the terminal run of `2`s has length `r>=2`, the next cylinder begins after a gap `3^r ell>=9ell`. The fixed neighborhood inequalities `u/r<1` and `(1-u-r)/r<9` therefore isolate exactly that cylinder inside the ambient dyadic cell, while `(1/2-u)/r in (1/3,2/3)` puts the midpoint in its central third gap and forces Cantor split `q=1/2`. The direction of all four inequalities was rechecked and is correct.
