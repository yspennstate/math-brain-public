## 4. A necessary overlap-measure lemma under the negation

The main counterexample-first concern was that recurrence under `nu_3` might be irrelevant because the Hellinger weights `w_n` could concentrate on exceptional balanced sceneries. The following exact lemma removes that problem.

Assume for contradiction that `mu` and `nu` are **not** mutually singular. By Section 2, `A_n -> A_infinity>0`.

Set

`rho := (mu+nu)/2`,

`f := dmu/drho`, `g := dnu/drho`,

and let `F_n` be the dyadic sigma-field. Put

`f_n := E_rho[f | F_n]`, `g_n := E_rho[g | F_n]`.

Define a probability measure `lambda_n` by

`dlambda_n/drho := sqrt(f_n g_n)/A_n`.      (4.1)

On every level-`n` dyadic cell `v`,

`lambda_n(v) = sqrt(mu(v)nu(v))/A_n = w_n(v)`.      (4.2)

By martingale convergence, `f_n -> f` and `g_n -> g` almost surely. Also `0<=f_n,g_n<=2`. Dominated convergence therefore gives

`sqrt(f_n g_n) -> sqrt(fg)` in `L^1(rho)`.

Since `A_n -> A_infinity>0`, (4.1) implies convergence in total variation

`lambda_n -> lambda`,      (4.3)

where

`dlambda/drho = sqrt(fg)/A_infinity`.      (4.4)

Because the density in (4.4) vanishes wherever `g=0`,

`lambda << nu`; similarly `lambda << mu`.      (4.5)

Crucially, total-variation convergence is uniform over **varying events**:

`|lambda_n(B_n)-lambda(B_n)| <= ||lambda_n-lambda||_TV -> 0`      (4.6)

for every sequence of measurable sets `B_n`.

Thus, under the negation, Hellinger weights cannot permanently hide from an event that has positive probability under the fixed overlap law `lambda`.

## 5. B14 — exact dyadic/ternary scenery action and a mixing lemma

Let

`alpha := log_3 2`.

This number is irrational: a rational identity `alpha=p/q` would imply `2^q=3^p`, impossible by unique factorization.

For each `n>=1`, put

`m_n := ceil(alpha n)`,

`r_n := 2^n/3^{m_n}`.      (5.1)

Since `alpha n` is never integral,

`r_n = 3^{ {alpha n}-1 } in (1/3,1)`.      (5.2)

Let `x in E` have its non-endpoint ternary expansion

`x = sum_{j>=1} xi_j 3^{-j}`,  `xi_j in {0,2}`,

and let

`a_m(x) := sum_{j=1}^m xi_j 3^{-j}`

be the left endpoint of its level-`m` Cantor cylinder. Define the normalized dyadic position

`u_n(x) := { 2^n a_{m_n}(x) } in [0,1)`.      (5.3)

Endpoint ambiguities are null under both source measures, as already established in the preceding report.

The key arithmetic statement is stronger than ordinary `nu`-typical recurrence: it holds for **every** law absolutely continuous with respect to Cantor measure.

### Lemma 5.1 — prefix-position / suffix mixing under every `lambda << nu`

Let `lambda << nu`. Fix `L>=1` and a word `s in {0,2}^L`. Then for every continuous `phi` on the circle,

`int 1_{(xi_{m_n-L+1},...,xi_{m_n})=s} phi(u_n(x)) dlambda(x)`

`   -> 2^{-L} int_0^1 phi(t) dt`.      (5.4)

Consequently, for every interval `U` whose endpoints are Lebesgue-null,

`lambda( suffix_L(m_n)=s, u_n in U ) -> 2^{-L}|U|`.      (5.5)

**Proof.**

Write `h=dlambda/dnu`. Approximate `h` in `L^1(nu)` by a cylinder function `h_K` depending only on the first `K` ternary digits. It therefore suffices to prove (5.4) with `h_K dnu` in place of `dlambda`, uniformly enough to pass through this `L^1` approximation.

By trigonometric approximation, it suffices to take `phi(t)=e(k t)` for fixed integer `k`.

For `k=0`, when `m_n>K+L`, the last `L` digits are independent of the first `K` digits under the product Cantor law `nu`, hence the left side is exactly `2^{-L} int h_K dnu`.

Now take `k != 0`. Condition on the first `K` digits and on the prescribed final word `s`. The middle digits `xi_{K+1},...,xi_{m_n-L}` remain independent and equiprobable in `{0,2}`. The modulus of the conditional Fourier coefficient is therefore

`prod_{j=K+1}^{m_n-L} | (1 + e(2^{n+1} k/3^j))/2 |`.      (5.6)

Allen--Chow--Yu, Lemma 2.6, bounds (5.6) by

`rho0^{ D_3(2^{n+1}|k|; K, m_n-L) }`      (5.7)

for a fixed `rho0<1`, where the exponent counts ternary digit changes in the indicated digit range.

It remains to prove that the exponent in (5.7) tends to infinity. Set

`y_n := 2^{n+1}|k|`.

Multiplication by `2^{n+1}` only shifts the binary expansion of `|k|`, so `D_2(y_n)` is bounded by a constant depending on `k`. Allen--Chow--Yu Theorem 1.13 (Stewart; Bugeaud--Cipu--Mignotte) gives

`D_2(y_n)+D_3(y_n) -> infinity`.

Therefore `D_3(y_n)->infinity`. The number `Delta_3(y_n)` of ternary digits satisfies

`Delta_3(y_n) = alpha n + O_k(1) = m_n + O_k(1)`.

Discarding the first `K` digit positions, the final `L` positions, and the `O_k(1)` possible positions caused by the bounded discrepancy between `Delta_3(y_n)` and `m_n` removes only a bounded number of digit changes. Thus

`D_3(2^{n+1}|k|; K,m_n-L) -> infinity`.      (5.8)

Equations (5.7)--(5.8) make every nonzero Fourier coefficient tend to zero. This proves (5.4) for cylinder densities `h_K`. The `L^1(nu)` approximation of `h` then proves it for arbitrary `lambda<<nu`, and approximation of interval indicators by continuous functions proves (5.5). QED.

This lemma is where the published `times 2 / times 3` digit-change theorem enters. No iid surrogate for harmonic measure is used.

### Lemma 6.1 — the suffix `22` isolates the cylinder

Let `C` be a level-`m` Cantor basic cylinder of length `ell=3^{-m}` whose last two ternary digits are `22`.

If a previous level-`m` cylinder exists, its right endpoint is exactly `ell` to the left of `C`'s left endpoint. Indeed, decrementing the corresponding binary address ending in `11` changes the suffix `11` to `10`, so the left endpoints differ by `2 ell`.

If a next level-`m` cylinder exists and the terminal run of `2` digits has length `r>=2`, its left endpoint is `(3^r+1)ell` to the right of the left endpoint of `C`. Hence the gap from the right endpoint of `C` to the next cylinder is `3^r ell >= 9 ell`.

Therefore any ambient interval extending less than one cylinder length to the left of `C` and less than nine cylinder lengths to its right intersects `E` **only in `C`**. QED.

## 7. Source theorem

a contradiction. Therefore `A_infinity=0`. By the exact Hellinger criterion from the first P0063 run, this is equivalent to `nu_2 \perp nu_3`. QED.
