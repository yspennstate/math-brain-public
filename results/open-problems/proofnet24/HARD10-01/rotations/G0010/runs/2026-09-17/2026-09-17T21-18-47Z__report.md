## 4. Complete theorem for arbitrary biregular hosts

### Theorem 4.1 (uniform biregular subcritical upper tail)

Let `G=(L union R,E)` be a finite connected `(D,d)`-biregular bipartite graph, meaning every vertex of `L` has degree `D` and every vertex of `R` has degree `d`. Assume `D>=d>=1`. Write

\[
n=|V(G)|,\qquad \lambda=\lambda(G)=\sqrt{Dd},\qquad
w:=\sqrt{D/d}=\frac{D}{\lambda}\ge1.
\]

Fix `0<epsilon<=1`, put `c=1-epsilon`, and let

\[
p\le \frac{c}{\lambda}.
\]

Then for every `delta in (0,1)`, Bernoulli bond percolation on `G` satisfies

\[
\Pr\!\left(
|C_{\max}(G_p)|\ge
\frac{8w}{\varepsilon^2}
\left[
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\log\frac{|L|}{\delta}
\right]
\right)
\le \delta. \tag{4.1}
\]

Since

\[
|L|=\frac{nd}{D+d}=\frac{n}{1+w^2}
=\frac{n\lambda^2}{D^2+\lambda^2},
\]
this is equivalently

\[
\Pr\!\left(
|C_{\max}(G_p)|\ge
\frac{8\Delta}{\varepsilon^2\lambda}
\left[
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
+
\log\frac{n\lambda^2}{(\Delta^2+\lambda^2)\delta}
\right]
\right)
\le\delta, \tag{4.2}
\]

where here `Delta=D`.

In particular, for any graph sequence in this class and any chosen `delta_n -> 0`, (4.2) is an a.a.s. bound with `delta=delta_n`.

### Proof

#### Step 1: spectral radius and side multiplicity

Because `G` is connected and biregular, the vector constant `sqrt(D)` on `L` and constant `sqrt(d)` on `R` is a positive Perron eigenvector of the adjacency matrix with eigenvalue `sqrt(Dd)`. By Perron-Frobenius,

\[
\lambda(G)=\sqrt{Dd}.
\]

Counting edges from both sides gives

\[
D|L|=d|R|.
\]

Since `n=|L|+|R|`,

\[
|L|=\frac{nd}{D+d}.
\]

With `w^2=D/d`, this becomes `|L|=n/(1+w^2)`. Also `w=D/lambda`, hence the final source-parameter expression above.

#### Step 2: collision-blind two-type domination

Fix `x in L`. Explore the open cluster `C(x)` breadth-first. Each time an `L` vertex is processed, there are at most `D` incident edges that could reveal previously unseen `R` vertices. Each is open with probability `p`. Each time an `R` vertex is processed, there are at most `d` incident edges that could reveal previously unseen `L` vertices.

Couple this exploration to a two-type Galton-Watson process in which **all** `D` or `d` Bernoulli trials are retained independently, even when the corresponding real edge points to a parent or an already discovered vertex. Therefore the real cluster is stochastically dominated in total type counts by a branching process with offspring laws

\[
L\to \operatorname{Bin}(D,p)\text{ children of type }R,
\qquad
R\to \operatorname{Bin}(d,p)\text{ children of type }L.
\]

Let `Z_L,Z_R` be the total progeny of the dominating process started from one `L` particle. Define

\[
S:=Z_L+\frac{Z_R}{w}.
\]

Since `w>=1`,

\[
|C(x)|\le Z_L+Z_R\le wZ_L+Z_R=wS. \tag{4.3}
\]

The two-type mean matrix is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&Dp\\dp&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose spectral radius is `p sqrt(Dd) <= c<1`; therefore the branching process dies out almost surely.

#### Step 3: the weighted Perron contraction

The vector `(w,1)` is superharmonic for the expected child weight. Indeed

\[
Dp\le \frac{cD}{\sqrt{Dd}}=cw,
\]

while

\[
dp\,w\le \frac{cd}{\sqrt{Dd}}\sqrt{\frac Dd}=c.
\]

Thus one generation contracts expected weighted frontier mass by the factor at most `c=1-epsilon`. This is the exact B12 invariant.

#### Step 4: uniform exponential moment

Set

\[
a:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad
\theta:=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{8}.
\]

For `0<=a<=1/2`,

\[
e^a-1\le a+a^2. \tag{4.4}
\]

For completeness, let `g(a)=1+a+a^2-e^a`. Then `g(0)=g'(0)=0` and `g''(a)=2-e^a>0` on `[0,1/2]`, so `g(a)>=0` there.

Using (4.4),

\[
a-c(e^a-1)
\ge a-c(a+a^2)
=\varepsilon a-(1-\varepsilon)a^2
\ge \frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\theta+c(e^a-1)\le a. \tag{4.5}
\]

Let `F_L^{(m)}` and `F_R^{(m)}` be the mgfs of the weighted total progeny truncated after `m` generations, started respectively from one `L` or one `R` particle. Their recursions are

\[
F_L^{(m+1)}
=e^\theta\bigl(1-p+pF_R^{(m)}\bigr)^D,
\]

\[
F_R^{(m+1)}
=e^{\theta/w}\bigl(1-p+pF_L^{(m)}\bigr)^d.
\]

We prove inductively

\[
F_L^{(m)}\le e^a,
\qquad
F_R^{(m)}\le e^{a/w}. \tag{4.6}
\]

The depth-zero statement follows from `theta<=a`. Suppose (4.6) holds at depth `m`. Using `log(1+u)<=u`,

\[
\log F_R^{(m+1)}
\le \frac\theta w+dp(e^a-1).
\]

Because `dp<=c/w`,

\[
\log F_R^{(m+1)}
\le \frac{\theta+c(e^a-1)}w
\le \frac aw
\]

by (4.5).

Similarly,

\[
\log F_L^{(m+1)}
\le \theta+Dp(e^{a/w}-1)
\le \theta+cw(e^{a/w}-1).
\]

Convexity of the exponential gives, for `w>=1`,

\[
e^{a/w}\le 1+\frac{e^a-1}{w},
\]

hence

\[
w(e^{a/w}-1)\le e^a-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\log F_L^{(m+1)}
\le \theta+c(e^a-1)
\le a.
\]

The induction is complete. By monotone convergence as the truncation depth tends to infinity,

\[
\mathbb E_L e^{\theta S}\le e^a. \tag{4.7}
\]

Markov's inequality now yields, for every `s>=0`,

\[
\Pr_L(S\ge s)\le e^{a-\theta s}. \tag{4.8}
\]

Combining (4.3) and (4.8),

\[
\Pr(|C(x)|\ge ws)\le e^{a-\theta s}. \tag{4.9}
\]

#### Step 5: exploit the rare high-degree side

Every component of size at least two meets `L`. Therefore for the thresholds used below,

\[
\Pr(|C_{\max}|\ge ws)
\le |L|e^{a-\theta s}. \tag{4.10}
\]

Choose

\[
s=\frac{a+\log(|L|/\delta)}\theta.
\]

Then the right-hand side of (4.10) is exactly `delta`. Substituting `a=epsilon/2` and `theta=epsilon^2/8` proves (4.1), and Step 1 gives (4.2). QED.

## 5. What the theorem explains

The theorem resolves the overlap problem **inside the selected biregular class** because the upper envelope is insensitive to collisions: collisions can only delete children. The multiplicity term is not guessed; it is exactly the population of the high-degree side,

\[
|L|=\frac{n}{1+(\Delta/\lambda)^2}.
\]

The burst size is the Perron degree ratio

\[
w=\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}.
\]

Thus the natural biregular scale emerging from the proof is

\[
\frac{\Delta}{\lambda}
\log\!\left(
\frac{n\lambda^2}{\Delta^2+\lambda^2}
\right)
\]

up to `epsilon`-dependent constants and the confidence term. This is **not promoted as the universal all-graph answer**.

### Benchmark 1: regular bipartite hosts

If `D=d`, then `w=1` and `|L|=n/2`. The theorem gives

\[
|C_{\max}|=O_\varepsilon(\log n)
\]

with the usual high-probability choice of `delta_n`. This matches the standard subcritical regular-graph scale up to constants.

### Benchmark 2: a star

For `K_{1,Delta}`, take `D=Delta,d=1`. Then `lambda=sqrt(Delta)`, `w=sqrt(Delta)`, and `|L|=1`. At fixed confidence the theorem gives

\[
|C_{\max}|=O_\varepsilon(\sqrt\Delta),
\]

the correct burst scale of the percolated center.

### Benchmark 3: the source's complete-bipartite pathology

For `K_{s,s^3}`, take `D=s^3,d=s`. Then

\[
\lambda=s^2,\qquad w=s,\qquad |L|=s.
\]

Thus (4.1) gives

\[
|C_{\max}|=O_\varepsilon(s\log s)
\]

at polynomially high confidence (for example `delta=s^{-2}`). This is the complete-bipartite logarithmic mechanism that the earlier rootwise analysis could not recover: `s` is the burst size and the `log s` comes from the number of high-degree roots.

## 6. Failed / rejected routes in this run

1. **Scalar max-degree Galton-Watson domination.** Using offspring `Bin(Delta,p)` gives mean `p Delta = c Delta/lambda`, which can be much larger than one. It destroys the alternating contraction and is useless in the unbalanced regime.

2. **Only looking at the two-generation mean.** The two-step product is at most `c^2`, but an individual high-degree generation can expand by `Delta/lambda`. Controlling only pairs of generations misses the actual vertex count unless the Perron weights are retained.

3. **Claiming full biregular tightness from the upper theorem.** The bound is order-sharp on the regular, star and complete-bipartite benchmark mechanisms, but this run does not prove a matching lower family for every feasible `(n,D,d)`. Therefore the result is an upper theorem for the selected class, not a complete classification of that class and certainly not source closure.

4. **Promoting `w log |L|` as the arbitrary-graph formula.** The earlier finite-tree counterexample already shows that static degree/spectral summaries can hide transient depth. General graphs need a mechanism that simultaneously accounts for weighted contraction and the number of vertices carrying a given amplification profile.
