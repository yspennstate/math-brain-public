## 4. B27 — swap the quantifiers: from one worst root to the number of exceptional roots

### 4.1 Idea before proof

The previous report solved the pointwise extremal problem. That is too pessimistic for largest-component analysis: an endpoint-amplified root can be very large while only a few roots have that profile. Swap

`for every root v, how large can N_k(v) be?`

for

`for a threshold t, how many roots can have N_k(v)>=t?`

Here and below

\[
N_k(v):=N_k^{\rm NB}(v)
\]

denotes the number of length-`k` nonbacktracking walks from `v`.

### Theorem 4.1 — spectral branching-budget inequalities

Let `G` be a finite simple graph on `n` vertices, with adjacency matrix `A` and spectral radius `lambda`. For every integer `k>=0`,

\[
\sum_{v}N_k(v)\le n\lambda^k,                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\sum_v N_k(v)^2\le n\lambda^{2k}.                                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, for every `t>0`,

\[
|R_k(t)|
\le n\min\left\{1,{\lambda^k\over t},{\lambda^{2k}\over t^2}\right\}. \tag{4.3}
\]

Together with the preceding run's pointwise theorem,

\[
R_k(t)=\varnothing\quad\text{if}\quad
 t>U_k:=\min\left\{\Delta(\Delta-1)^{k-1},
 {\lambda^{2k}\over4^{k-1}}\right\}                               \tag{4.4}
\]

for `k>=1`.

#### Proof

Let

\[
W_k(v):=(A^k\mathbf 1)_v
\]

be the number of all length-`k` walks from `v`. Every nonbacktracking walk is a walk, hence pointwise

\[
0\le N_k(v)\le W_k(v).                                             \tag{4.5}
\]

Since `A` is symmetric and `||A||_2=lambda`,

\[
\sum_v W_k(v)=\mathbf1^T A^k\mathbf1
\le \|\mathbf1\|_2\,\|A^k\mathbf1\|_2
\le n\lambda^k.                                                     \tag{4.6}
\]

This proves (4.1). Similarly,

\[
\sum_v W_k(v)^2
=\|A^k\mathbf1\|_2^2
\le \lambda^{2k}\|\mathbf1\|_2^2
=n\lambda^{2k},                                                      \tag{4.7}
\]

which proves (4.2). Markov's counting inequality applied once to `N_k` and once to `N_k^2` gives (4.3). Equation (4.4) is the sharp pointwise bound already proved in the preceding immutable report. `□`

### 4.2 Percolation-normalized form

Put

\[
c:=1-\varepsilon,
\qquad p\le {c\over\lambda},
\qquad B_k(v):=p^kN_k(v).                                           \tag{4.8}
\]

Then Theorem 4.1 immediately gives

\[
\sum_v B_k(v)\le nc^k,
\qquad
\sum_v B_k(v)^2\le nc^{2k}.                                        \tag{4.9}
\]

Hence

\[
\left|\{v:B_k(v)\ge x\}\right|
\le n\min\left\{{c^k\over x},{c^{2k}\over x^2}\right\}            \tag{4.10}
\]

for every `x>0`. In particular,

\[
|\{v:B_k(v)\ge1\}|\le nc^{2k}.                                     \tag{4.11}
\]

Taking a union over horizons gives the deterministic exceptional-root budget

\[
\left|\left\{v:\exists k\ge K,\ B_k(v)\ge1\right\}\right|
\le {nc^{2K}\over1-c^2}.                                            \tag{4.12}
\]

Thus roots whose expected number of open nonbacktracking paths fails to contract at a long horizon occupy an exponentially small fraction of the host vertices.

### 4.3 Exact stress tests: the tail budget is already sharp in opposite geometries

This marginal estimate cannot simply be improved by another universal power of `t`.

**Regular bulk.** In a `d`-regular graph, `lambda=d` and

\[
N_1(v)=d=\lambda
\]

for every one of the `n` roots. Thus (4.3) is exact at `k=1,t=lambda`.

**Single-hub star.** In `K_{1,D}`, `n=D+1`, `lambda^2=D`. At `k=1`, the center has `N_1=D=lambda^2`. The second-moment bound gives

\[
{n\lambda^2\over D^2}={D+1\over D}\to1,
\]

matching the one exceptional center. At `k=2`, each of the `D` leaves has `N_2=D-1`, and the same second-moment tail is asymptotically of order `D`.

**Complete bipartite hub cloud.** Let `G=K_{a,b}`, `a<=b`. Then

\[
\lambda^2=ab,\qquad \Delta=b.
\]

The `a` vertices on the small side all have degree `b`, so at `k=1,t=b`,

\[
{n\lambda^2\over b^2}
={(a+b)ab\over b^2}
=a\left(1+{a\over b}\right),                                       \tag{4.13}
\]

which is asymptotically exactly the number `a` of high-degree hubs when `b/a -> infinity`.

Therefore the `L^2` exceptional-root count is not merely a loose proof artifact: stars and highly unbalanced complete bipartite graphs asymptotically saturate its rare-root side, while regular graphs saturate its bulk side.

**B27 outcome:** a complete all-graph theorem for the marginal tail `R_k(t)`, sharp on natural extremizers. It does **not** close the source because sharp marginal cardinalities still omit how exceptional roots overlap and connect to one another.

## 5. B25 — count paths twice: a spectral hub budget and a collision/endpoint statistic

### 5.1 Degree-level hub budget

Taking `k=1` in (4.2) gives the exact source-parameter inequality

\[
\sum_v d(v)^2\le n\lambda^2.                                       \tag{5.1}
\]

Therefore for every `0<eta<=1`,

\[
\left|\{v:d(v)\ge \eta\Delta\}\right|
\le {n\lambda^2\over \eta^2\Delta^2}.                              \tag{5.2}
\]

Writing

\[
w:={\Delta\over\lambda},                                          \tag{5.3}
\]

this says that the number of near-maximum-degree hubs is at most `n/(eta^2 w^2)`. In `K_{a,b}` with `b>>a`, `w=sqrt(b/a)` and the `eta=1` right side is asymptotic to `a`, again exactly the small-side hub count.

This identifies a source-parameter quantity that was invisible to the pointwise theorem: the spectral radius budgets not only the growth per root but also **how many roots can carry degree `Delta`**.

### 5.2 The missing information is collision, not merely path count

Let

\[
M_k(u,v):=\#\{\text{length-}k\text{ nonbacktracking walks from }u\text{ to }v\}.
\]

Define the endpoint-collision energy

\[
Q_k(u):=\sum_v M_k(u,v)^2,                                         \tag{5.4}
\]

and, when `N_k(u)>0`, the participation/effective-endpoint number

\[
E_k(u):={N_k(u)^2\over Q_k(u)}.                                    \tag{5.5}
\]

Set `E_k(u)=0` when `N_k(u)=0`.

### Proposition 5.1 — collision-energy spectral bound

For every `u,k`,

\[
Q_k(u)\le (A^{2k})_{uu}\le\lambda^{2k}.                            \tag{5.6}
\]

Consequently

\[
E_k(u)\ge {N_k(u)^2\over\lambda^{2k}}.                             \tag{5.7}
\]

Also

\[
E_k(u)\le |\{v:M_k(u,v)>0\}|,                                     \tag{5.8}
\]

so `E_k` is a genuine lower bound on the effective number of distinct endpoints reached by the path mass.

#### Proof

Entrywise,

\[
0\le M_k(u,v)\le (A^k)_{uv}.
\]

Therefore

\[
Q_k(u)
\le\sum_v(A^k_{uv})^2
=(A^{2k})_{uu}
\le\|A^{2k}\|_2
=\lambda^{2k},
\]

which proves (5.6) and then (5.7). Finally, Cauchy--Schwarz on the support of `M_k(u,.)` gives

\[
N_k(u)^2
=\left(\sum_vM_k(u,v)\right)^2
\le |\operatorname{supp}M_k(u,\cdot)|\,Q_k(u),
\]

which is (5.8). `□`

Summing (5.6) over roots also gives

\[
\sum_uQ_k(u)\le\operatorname{tr}(A^{2k})\le n\lambda^{2k}.          \tag{5.9}
\]

### 5.3 Tree versus complete-bipartite: the collision statistic separates the two source mechanisms exactly

**Tree.** On a tree, a nonbacktracking walk from a fixed root is a simple path, so each reachable endpoint has exactly one such path. Hence

\[
Q_k(u)=N_k(u),\qquad E_k(u)=N_k(u).                                 \tag{5.10}
\]

The transient branching mass spreads to maximally many distinct vertices.

**Complete bipartite graph.** Take `K_{a,b}` and a root `u` on the side of size `a`. At horizon two,

\[
N_2(u)=b(a-1).                                                      \tag{5.11}
\]

There are exactly `a-1` possible endpoints, and each receives exactly `b` two-step nonbacktracking paths. Thus

\[
Q_2(u)=(a-1)b^2,
\qquad
E_2(u)=a-1.                                                         \tag{5.12}
\]

So a path count of order `lambda^2=ab` collapses onto only `a-1` effective endpoints. This is the opposite geometry from the tree and is precisely the overlap information missing from `R_k(t)`.

The same source parameters can therefore support two qualitatively different mechanisms:

- **tree-like:** large transient `N_k` with almost no collisions, producing polynomial-depth exploration;
- **hub-cloud/bipartite:** large one-step amplification followed by heavy collisions onto a much smaller exceptional-root set, producing a coarse subcritical chain with a logarithmic maximum over many hubs.

**B25 outcome:** the marginal tail profile is not the final variable. A viable all-regime theorem needs a joint growth/overlap or coarse-transition profile.

## 6. B9 — randomize the root, then stress-test the size-bias failure

### 6.1 Uniform-root contraction

Let `V` be a uniformly random host vertex, independent of percolation. From (4.9),

\[
\mathbb E_V B_k(V)^2\le c^{2k}.                                   \tag{6.1}
\]

Hence for every `K`,

\[
\Pr_V\left(\exists k\ge K:\ B_k(V)\ge1\right)
\le {c^{2K}\over1-c^2}.                                            \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus a typical **host vertex** is strongly subcritical at every sufficiently long horizon, with an exponential `L^2` margin.

If `X_k(v)` denotes the random number of open length-`k` nonbacktracking paths from `v`, then

\[
\mathbb E_{V,\mathrm{perc}}X_k(V)
={1\over n}\sum_v p^kN_k(v)
\le c^k,                                                           \tag{6.3}
\]

so

\[
\Pr_{V,\mathrm{perc}}(X_k(V)>0)\le c^k.                            \tag{6.4}
\]

These are genuine all-graph source-regime contraction statements.

### 6.2 Why uniform-root randomization still does not control the largest component

Largest components size-bias the root toward precisely the exceptional vertices excluded by (6.2). The complete-bipartite benchmark makes the failure explicit.

For `K_{a,b}`, `a<=b`, put

\[
p={c\over\sqrt{ab}},\qquad w=\sqrt{b/a}={\Delta\over\lambda}.       \tag{6.5}
\]

Starting from the side of size `a`, the exact nonbacktracking counts are, for `r>=1`,

\[
N_{2r}=b(a-1)^r(b-1)^{r-1},                                       \tag{6.6}
\]

and for `r>=0`,

\[
N_{2r+1}=b(a-1)^r(b-1)^r.                                         \tag{6.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
B_{2r}
=c^{2r}(1-1/a)^r(1-1/b)^{r-1},                                   \tag{6.8}
\]

while

\[
B_{2r+1}
=c^{2r+1}w\,[(1-1/a)(1-1/b)]^r.                                  \tag{6.9}
\]

So the two-step hub-to-hub reproduction is uniformly subcritical, asymptotically `c^2`, while each visit to the small side can carry a one-step amplification of order `w=Delta/lambda`. This is the source's logarithmic complete-bipartite mechanism in deterministic path-count form: a subcritical chain can visit `O(log a)` exceptional hubs in its largest realization, with each hub carrying an `O(w)` ordinary-vertex cloud.

The spectral hub budget (5.2) predicts the correct effective hub count:

\[
{n\lambda^2\over\Delta^2}
=(a+b){ab\over b^2}
=a(1+a/b)\asymp a.                                                 \tag{6.10}
\]

However, neither (4.10) nor (5.2) alone controls the **transition geometry among those hubs**. Conversely, a tree has essentially no collisions and defeats any formula built only from the hub count `n(lambda/Delta)^2` and the one-step amplification `Delta/lambda`.

**B9 outcome:** uniform-root randomization gives a strong exponentially decaying average profile, but the source problem is governed by the size-biased geometry of the exceptional set. The missing theorem must control how exceptional roots connect across scales.
