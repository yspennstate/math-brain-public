## 3. Preliminary lemma — finite SFE forces finite local potential energy in the nearest-neighbor SAP core

This subsection is deliberately scoped. It treats the standard integer-valued nearest-neighbor convex SAP core in which the local one-edge Gibbs partition sums are finite. A fully general perturbed-SAP extension would require additional finite-range bookkeeping and is not claimed here.

Let `eta_e` denote the integer gradient across an oriented nearest-neighbor edge `e`, and let `V_e: Z -> R union {+infinity}` be the corresponding convex one-edge potential. Subtract an irrelevant constant so that `V_e>=0` on its effective domain. Assume for some, hence every, `alpha in (0,1)` needed below,

\[
Z_{e,\alpha}:=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\alpha V_e(m)}<\infty.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is the usual one-edge normalizability/coercivity condition in the present SAP core; hard constraints simply remove forbidden `m` from the sum.

Consider a finite connected box with one height pinned to remove the additive constant. The collection of all oriented internal edge gradients determines the pinned height configuration injectively. Hence, for any finite-volume law `mu_Lambda`, Shannon entropy satisfies

\[
H(\mu_\Lambda)
\le H((\eta_e)_{e\in E_\Lambda})
\le \sum_{e\in E_\Lambda} H(\eta_e).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For one edge and any probability law `p` on `Z`, nonnegativity of relative entropy with respect to

\[
\pi_{e,\alpha}(m)=Z_{e,\alpha}^{-1}e^{-\alpha V_e(m)}
\]

gives the exact entropy-energy inequality

\[
H(p)\le \alpha\,\mathbb E_p V_e + \log Z_{e,\alpha}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Applying (3.3) edgewise in (3.2),

\[
H(\mu_\Lambda)
\le
\alpha\sum_{e\in E_\Lambda}\mathbb E_{\mu_\Lambda}V_e(\eta_e)
+
\sum_{e\in E_\Lambda}\log Z_{e,\alpha}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

With the standard finite-volume free-energy convention `FE = energy - entropy` (up to harmless reference/additive constants),

\[
FE_\Lambda(\mu)
\ge
(1-\alpha)
\sum_{e\in E_\Lambda}\mathbb E V_e(\eta_e)
-
C_\alpha |E_\Lambda|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For a translation-invariant state, dividing by `|Lambda|` and sending `Lambda` along a Følner sequence yields:

> **Local-energy consequence.** In this nearest-neighbor SAP core, finite specific free energy forces
> \[
> \boxed{\mathbb E_\mu V_i(\eta_i)<\infty\qquad\text{for every coordinate direction }i.}
> \tag{3.6}
> \]

In particular, convex coercivity implies the first moment `E|eta_i|<infinity`, so the slope coordinates are honest integrable expectations.

This is a standard entropy-coercivity mechanism, not claimed as novel. Its purpose here is to justify the integrability needed for the typical-boundary argument without silently adding an `L^p` hypothesis beyond finite SFE.

## 4. B28 — REPLACE “FOR ALL” WITH “FOR TYPICAL”: directional ergodicity is forced by curl-free structure

A subtle obstruction must be removed first. Full `Z^d` ergodicity does **not** in general imply ergodicity of a single coordinate shift `T_i`. Thus ordinary one-dimensional Birkhoff along a coordinate line would a priori converge to a random conditional mean rather than to the deterministic slope.

For a curl-free gradient cocycle, however, the conditional mean is forced to be deterministic.

### 4.1 Setup

Let `(Omega,mu,(T_x)_{x in Z^d})` be a stationary ergodic `Z^d` system. Let

\[
\eta_i(\omega),\qquad i=1,\dots,d,
\]

be integrable coordinate increments satisfying the cocycle/curl-free relation

\[
\eta_i\circ T_j-\eta_i
=
\eta_j\circ T_i-\eta_j
\qquad\text{a.s. for all }i,j.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write the slope

\[
u_i:=\mathbb E\eta_i.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Let `I_i` be the invariant sigma-field of the single shift `T_i`, and define

\[
m_i:=\mathbb E[\eta_i\mid I_i].
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.2 Exact conditional-mean rigidity

Because all lattice shifts commute, `I_i` is invariant under every `T_j`. Taking conditional expectation of (4.1) with respect to `I_i`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
m_i\circ T_j-m_i
&=
\mathbb E[\eta_i\circ T_j-\eta_i\mid I_i]\\
&=
\mathbb E[\eta_j\circ T_i-\eta_j\mid I_i].
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

For any integrable `f`, conditional expectation onto the `T_i`-invariant sigma-field annihilates the coboundary `f\circ T_i-f`. Hence the right side of (4.4) vanishes. Therefore

\[
m_i\circ T_j=m_i
\qquad\text{for all }j.
\tag{4.5}
\]

By definition `m_i\circ T_i=m_i` as well, so `m_i` is invariant under the full `Z^d` action. Full ergodicity now gives that `m_i` is almost surely constant, and taking expectations identifies the constant:

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[\eta_i\mid I_i]=u_i\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the key point: curl-freeness upgrades full lattice ergodicity into exactly the directional mean statement needed below, without assuming the coordinate shift itself is ergodic.

### 4.3 Long directional chords converge to the slope

Define the length-`L` directional average

\[
A_{i,L}(\omega)
:=
\frac1L\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\eta_i(T_{r e_i}\omega).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The one-dimensional Birkhoff theorem and (4.6) give

\[
\boxed{A_{i,L}\longrightarrow u_i\quad\text{a.s. and in }L^1.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus the endpoint displacement of a long coordinate column is asymptotically the deterministic affine displacement `Lu_i`, even though a single coordinate shift need not itself be ergodic.

### 4.4 Energy convergence for the same directional potential

Let `\overline V_i` be the convex piecewise-linear extension of the discrete convex potential `V_i` from `Z` to `R`. Jensen gives

\[
\overline V_i(A_{i,L})
\le
\frac1L\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}V_i(\eta_i\circ T_{r e_i}).
\tag{4.9}
\]

The right side is not automatically uniformly integrable merely because its expectation is bounded. Here stationarity and finite local potential energy let us prove uniform integrability cleanly. Put

\[
X:=V_i(\eta_i)\in L^1.
\]

By the de la Vallée-Poussin criterion there exists an increasing convex superlinear function `Psi` with

\[
\mathbb E\Psi(X)<\infty.
\tag{4.10}
\]

For

\[
Y_L:=\frac1L\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}X\circ T_{r e_i},
\]

convexity of `Psi` and stationarity imply

\[
\mathbb E\Psi(Y_L)
\le
\frac1L\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\mathbb E\Psi(X\circ T_{r e_i})
=
\mathbb E\Psi(X).
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore `{Y_L}` is uniformly integrable. After shifting `V_i` by its finite infimum if needed, (4.9) makes `{\overline V_i(A_{i,L})}` uniformly integrable as well. Combining (4.8), continuity of the convex finite-valued extension at the interior slope and Vitali convergence yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,\overline V_i(A_{i,L})
\longrightarrow
\overline V_i(u_i).
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

Choose any subgradient `lambda_i in partial \overline V_i(u_i)` and define the Bregman excess

\[
D_{i,\lambda_i}(a,u_i)
:=
\overline V_i(a)-\overline V_i(u_i)-\lambda_i(a-u_i)\ge0.
\tag{4.13}
\]

Because `E A_{i,L}=u_i`, (4.12) is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E D_{i,\lambda_i}(A_{i,L},u_i)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\mathbb P\big(D_{i,\lambda_i}(A_{i,L},u_i)>\epsilon\big)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.15}
\]

By stationarity, if a macroscopic face contains `N^{d-1}` parallel columns of length `L`, the expected fraction of columns violating the `epsilon` bound equals the one-column probability in (4.15). Markov's inequality therefore shows that the **fraction of bad columns tends to zero in probability** as `L -> infinity`.

**B28 outcome:** the adversarial spike from the previous report is not representative of long directional chords of a finite-SFE ergodic gradient Gibbs state in the nearest-neighbor SAP core. The normal displacement pays asymptotically affine energy under its **own normal potential**, no matter that this potential may grow faster than the tangential Sobolev target. ## 5. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: exact one-dimensional bridge capacity and convex duality

The previous subsection concerns the endpoint displacement of a sampled column. We now compute the least possible energy needed to connect those endpoints using only edges in the same coordinate direction.

Fix a discrete convex potential `V:Z->R union {+infinity}` and an integer displacement `D` across `L` nearest-neighbor edges. Consider

\[
\mathcal C_L(D)
:=
\min\left\{
\sum_{r=1}^L V(g_r):
 g_r\in\mathbb Z,
 \ \sum_{r=1}^L g_r=D
\right\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
q=\left\lfloor\frac DL\right\rfloor,
\qquad
r=D-Lq\in\{0,\dots,L-1\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Discrete convexity implies the elementary exchange inequality

\[
a\le b-2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V(a)+V(b)\ge V(a+1)+V(b-1).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Repeatedly balancing any pair whose values differ by at least two proves that every minimizer has `L-r` increments equal to `q` and `r` increments equal to `q+1`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_L(D)
=(L-r)V(q)+rV(q+1)
=L\,\overline V(D/L).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the long-column bridge problem is exactly a one-dimensional convex capacity with no hidden constant.

Let `\overline V^*` be the convex conjugate of the piecewise-linear extension. Fenchel duality gives the equally exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_L(D)
=
\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\{\lambda D-L\overline V^*(\lambda)\}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For the Gibbs column displacement

\[
D_{i,L}=\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\eta_i\circ T_{r e_i}=L A_{i,L},
\]

(5.4) and (4.12) imply

\[
\frac1L\mathbb E\mathcal C_L(D_{i,L})
=
\mathbb E\overline V_i(A_{i,L})
\longrightarrow
\overline V_i(u_i).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equivalently, the expected excess bridge action satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathcal C_L(D_{i,L})-L\overline V_i(u_i)
-\lambda_i(D_{i,L}-Lu_i)
\right]
=o(L).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The linear term has zero expectation, so the expected bridge energy itself differs from the affine reference by `o(L)` in Bregman excess.

If a slab has transverse area of order `N^{d-1}` and all its columns have length `L`, summing expectations gives

\[
\mathbb E[\text{total one-direction bridge excess}]
=o(LN^{d-1}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

When `L` is a fixed positive fraction of the box scale `N`, this is `o(N^d)`, exactly the scale needed by a variational argument.

This result is stronger than the previous deterministic Sobolev estimate in one important sense: it is insensitive to cross-direction growth mismatches because the `i`th potential controls an average of **its own `i`th increments**.

**B18 outcome:** one-directional typical-boundary gluing has an exact convex-capacity formula and subvolume expected excess. The hard part has moved to fitting different columns together.

## 6. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: microscopic energy versus endpoint bridge action

The same endpoint displacement can be counted in two exact ways.

For one column with microscopic increments `g_1,...,g_L`, define `A=L^{-1}sum g_r`. Convexity gives

\[
L\overline V(A)
\le
\sum_{r=1}^L V(g_r).
\tag{6.1}
\]

After subtracting an affine support at the slope `u`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{
L D_\lambda(A,u)
\le
\sum_{r=1}^L D_\lambda(g_r,u),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

where

\[
D_\lambda(a,u)=\overline V(a)-\overline V(u)-\lambda(a-u),
\qquad\lambda\in\partial\overline V(u).
\]

The left side is exactly the **minimum bridge excess determined only by the two endpoints**; the right side is the microscopic directional excess already present in the sampled configuration.

This double count reveals the coarse-graining mechanism:

- microscopic energy can fluctuate at order `L` along a typical column;
- endpoint averaging kills that fluctuation, because the chord slope converges to `u`;
- the least energy needed to reconnect the same endpoints approaches the affine cost even when no deterministic Sobolev estimate controls an arbitrary boundary spike.

Combined with (4.14),

\[
\mathbb E\,[L D_\lambda(A_{i,L},u_i)]=o(L).
\tag{6.3}
\]

So the energetic price of replacing a sampled directional column by its convexly optimal bridge is asymptotically negligible relative to volume.

### 6.1 The exact point where independent column minimization fails

Suppose, however, that we minimize each normal column independently in a shell. Let two neighboring columns have endpoint displacements `D_x` and `D_y`. Their balanced minimizers need not choose the locations of the `q+1` increments coherently. Even if both columns individually have optimal normal action, their reconstructed heights at intermediate layers can differ by a staircase of size comparable with the discrepancy between the two balancing patterns. That discrepancy appears on **tangential edges** of the shell and is paid by tangential potentials.

Thus

\[
\sum_{\text{columns}}\min(\text{normal energy})
\]

is not the minimum of the full multidimensional shell energy. Independently optimal columns need not form a curl-free gradient field compatible with a single integer-valued height function having controlled tangential increments.

This is the new obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{one-dimensional chord optimality does not automatically glue into a multidimensional curl-compatible shell.}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

The previous report's cross-Sobolev obstruction identified a failure of a **particular worst-case face interpolation**. The present report shows that finite SFE plus ergodic cocycle structure bypasses that obstruction at the one-dimensional energetic level. What remains is a geometrically different problem: couple the balanced bridge increments across neighboring columns so that their tangential discrepancy also costs `o(volume)`.

### 6.2 Why this is genuinely the next blocker for the variational clause

Recall the earlier exact DLR relative-entropy reduction: for two states with the same specification, the bulk free-energy difference is carried by a boundary screening shell. To prove Conjecture 10.3.1 by gluing, one wants a random shell map that takes a typical inner sample of slope `u` to an affine outer boundary of slope `u` while paying only `o(N^d)` total free-energy overhead.

Equations (4.14) and (5.8) now show that **normal endpoint energy is not the obstacle for typical finite-SFE samples**. A source-level proof would therefore follow if one could establish a simultaneous shell-extension theorem of the following form.

> **Curl-compatible typical-shell target.** For a finite-SFE ergodic gradient Gibbs state of interior slope `u`, and a box of scale `N`, construct (with probability tending to one, or in expected free-energy cost) an integer-valued shell extension matching the sampled inner boundary to an affine outer boundary of slope `u` such that the total multidirectional energy excess plus the logarithm of the extension multiplicity is `o(N^d)`.

This is substantially narrower and more structural than the previous generic statement “prove an anisotropic Orlicz–Sobolev gluing lemma.”

**B25 outcome:** endpoint displacement has a favorable exact double count, but the independent bridge construction fails at the curl/tangential compatibility step. No metric movement.
