## 2. Mathematical idea before bookkeeping

The previous run identified a precise obstruction: independently optimized columns are energetically cheap but need not be the gradient of one scalar height. It proposed the scalar interpolation

\[
w_k(x)=h(x)+\left\lfloor \frac{k}{L}(g(x)-h(x))\right\rfloor.
\]

The new idea is to add **one common phase** `theta` to every site and every layer:

\[
\boxed{
 w_\theta(k,x)
 :=\left\lfloor
 (1-k/L)h(x)+(k/L)g(x)+\theta
 \right\rfloor,
 \qquad 0\le k\le L,
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

with `theta in [0,1)`. Because this is a scalar height, curl is identically zero. The common phase is not cosmetic: it gives an exact expectation identity for every tangential absolute-value edge, allowing simultaneous derandomization without paying the previous `+1` rounding loss edge-by-edge. The quadratic direction is controlled separately by the elementary fact that common-shift rounding changes a real difference by less than one.

This turns the simplest anisotropic prism problem into an explicit, auditable deterministic existence theorem.

## 3. B10 DERANDOMIZE — exact common-shift identities

We first isolate the arithmetic mechanism.

### Lemma 3.1 — unbiased common-shift floor

For every real `a`, if `Theta` is uniform on `[0,1)`,

\[
\mathbb E_\Theta\lfloor a+\Theta\rfloor=a.
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

Write `a=n+s`, where `n in Z` and `s in [0,1)`. Then

\[
\lfloor a+\Theta\rfloor=n+\mathbf 1_{\{\Theta\ge 1-s\}},
\]

whose expectation is `n+s=a`. QED.

### Lemma 3.2 — exact `L^1` distance under one common shift

For all real `a,b`,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1
\left|\lfloor a+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor b+\theta\rfloor\right|\,d\theta
=|a-b|.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Assume `a>=b`. Monotonicity of the floor function gives

\[
\lfloor a+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor b+\theta\rfloor\ge0
\]

for every `theta`. Therefore the absolute value may be removed. Integrating and applying Lemma 3.1 to each term gives `a-b`. The case `a<b` follows by symmetry. QED.

This exact identity is a one-dimensional common-shift randomized-rounding fact. The general randomized-rounding/probabilistic-method mechanism is classical; no novelty claim is made for Lemmas 3.1-3.2.

## 4. Complete deterministic scalar-prism interpolation theorem for the diagnostic potentials

Let `F` be any finite induced nearest-neighbor subset of `Z^2`, with coordinates called directions 2 and 3. Let `h,g:F->Z` be arbitrary integer face heights. For an integer slab thickness `L>=1`, define `w_theta` on `{0,...,L} x F` by (2.1).

Write `E_2(F)` and `E_3(F)` for the face edges in directions 2 and 3, respectively. For a face height `f`, write

\[
TV_2(f):=\sum_{e\in E_2(F)}|\nabla_2 f(e)|,
\qquad
Q_3(f):=\sum_{e\in E_3(F)}|\nabla_3 f(e)|^2.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — shared-phase scalar slab bound

There exists `theta_* in [0,1)` such that `w=w_{theta_*}` satisfies all of the following.

**(i) Exact endpoint and curl compatibility.**

\[
w(0,x)=h(x),\qquad w(L,x)=g(x)
\]

for all `x`, and `nabla w` has zero plaquette circulation identically because `w` is one scalar height.

**(ii) Exact normal `V_1=|.|` cost.**

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in F}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}
|w(k+1,x)-w(k,x)|
=
\sum_{x\in F}|g(x)-h(x)|.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

**(iii) Tangential `V_2=|.|` cost.**

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{L}\sum_{e\in E_2(F)}
|\nabla_2 w(k,e)|
\le
\frac{L+1}{2}\,[TV_2(h)+TV_2(g)].
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

**(iv) Tangential quadratic `V_3(t)=t^2` cost.**

For every `theta`, hence in particular for `theta_*`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{L}\sum_{e\in E_3(F)}
|\nabla_3 w(k,e)|^2
\le
(L+1)[Q_3(h)+Q_3(g)+2|E_3(F)|].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Endpoint exactness follows because `h(x),g(x)` are integers and `0<=theta<1`:

\[
\lfloor h(x)+\theta\rfloor=h(x),\qquad
\lfloor g(x)+\theta\rfloor=g(x).
\]

Curl-freeness is automatic for a scalar height.

For (ii), fix `x` and set `D=g(x)-h(x)`. The real affine path

\[
a_k=h(x)+(k/L)D
\]

is monotone in `k`; applying the monotone floor map preserves that monotonicity. Therefore its total variation telescopes:

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}
|\lfloor a_{k+1}+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor a_k+\theta\rfloor|
=|\lfloor g(x)+\theta\rfloor-\lfloor h(x)+\theta\rfloor|
=|D|.
\]

Summing over `x` proves (4.1), for every `theta`.

For (iii), fix a direction-2 edge `e=(x,y)` and layer `k`, and put `t=k/L`. The two underlying unrounded heights are

\[
a=(1-t)h(y)+tg(y),\qquad b=(1-t)h(x)+tg(x).
\]

By Lemma 3.2,

\[
\int_0^1|\nabla_2 w_\theta(k,e)|\,d\theta
=|(1-t)\nabla_2h(e)+t\nabla_2g(e)|
\]

and convexity of absolute value gives

\[
\le (1-t)|\nabla_2h(e)|+t|\nabla_2g(e)|.
\]

Sum first over `k=0,...,L`. Since

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{L}(1-k/L)=\sum_{k=0}^{L}k/L=(L+1)/2,
\]

then sum over all direction-2 edges. The average over `theta` of the complete direction-2 cost is at most the right side of (4.2), so at least one common phase `theta_*` attains that bound. This is a simultaneous derandomization; no sitewise or edgewise phases are used.

For (iv), again fix a direction-3 edge and layer, and set

\[
z=(1-t)\nabla_3h(e)+t\nabla_3g(e).
\]

For two real numbers rounded with one common phase, their integer difference lies within one of the real difference:

\[
|\nabla_3w_\theta(k,e)-z|<1.
\]

Hence

\[
|\nabla_3w_\theta(k,e)|^2
\le (|z|+1)^2
\le 2z^2+2.
\]

Since square is convex,

\[
z^2\le (1-t)|\nabla_3h(e)|^2+t|\nabla_3g(e)|^2.
\]

Summing in `k` gives

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{L}|\nabla_3w_\theta(k,e)|^2
\le
(L+1)(|\nabla_3h(e)|^2+|\nabla_3g(e)|^2)+2(L+1).
\]

Summing over direction-3 edges proves (4.3). QED.

### Why this is genuinely different from the failed independent-column construction

Theorem 4.1 never chooses column jump locations independently. One scalar interpolation (2.1) is used for the whole slab, so every plaquette has exactly zero curl. The common phase is global, and the existence argument chooses a single `theta_*` controlling all direction-2 edges at once. Thus the previous staircase/curl objection is removed in this diagnostic geometry.

## 5. B27 TYPICAL INSTEAD OF ALL — same-slope face mismatch is subvolume in `L^1`

and analogously `H^nu`, with each representative anchored separately. Assume the direction-2 and direction-3 increments are integrable. The preceding I0008 directional-cocycle argument proved that full `Z^3` ergodicity plus curl-freeness forces the conditional mean under each coordinate shift to equal the deterministic slope. Therefore, for `i=2,3`,

\[
A_{i,n}:=\frac1n\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\eta_i\circ T_{re_i}
\to u_i
\quad\text{in }L^1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Set

\[
a_i^\mu(n):=\mathbb E_\mu|A_{i,n}-u_i|\to0,
\qquad
a_i^\nu(n):=\mathbb E_\nu|A_{i,n}-u_i|\to0.
\]

For `y_2,y_3>=0`, use the coordinate path first in direction 2 and then direction 3. Stationarity of the second segment gives

\[
\mathbb E_\mu|H^\mu(y)-u_2y_2-u_3y_3|
\le y_2a_2^\mu(y_2)+y_3a_3^\mu(y_3).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The same holds for `nu`. Therefore

\[
\mathbb E|H^\mu(y)-H^\nu(y)|
\le
 y_2[a_2^\mu(y_2)+a_2^\nu(y_2)]
+y_3[a_3^\mu(y_3)+a_3^\nu(y_3)].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Summing over the face `F_N={0,...,N-1}^2`,

\[
\sum_{y\in F_N}\mathbb E|H^\mu(y)-H^\nu(y)|
\le
N\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}m[a_2^\mu(m)+a_2^\nu(m)]
+
N\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}m[a_3^\mu(m)+a_3^\nu(m)].
\tag{5.4}
\]

If `a(m)->0`, then `sum_{m<N}m a(m)=o(N^2)` by a standard epsilon split. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{y\in F_N}\mathbb E|H^\mu(y)-H^\nu(y)|=o(N^3).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the exact probabilistic fact that worst-case spikes miss: two **typical same-slope** faces may have large pointwise height differences, but their total `L^1` mismatch is subvolume after anchoring.

## 8. Failed/generalization routes exposed by this run

The shared-phase lemma also shows exactly where the diagnostic success stops.

### 8.1 If the slab-normal potential is superlinear

For a normal potential comparable to `|m|^p`, `p>1`, monotone interpolation costs roughly

\[
L\,\overline V((g-h)/L),
\]

which requires more than an `L^1` bound on the cross-state height mismatch. Same slope plus finite first directional energy does not provide that stronger height moment. Thus the present route does **not** solve the fully anisotropic class by simply rotating the slab.

### 8.2 Common-shift exactness is special to the absolute-value direction

Equation (3.2) is exact because the cost is `|integer difference|`. For a general convex tangential potential, common-shift rounding yields a nearby integer difference but does not preserve the convex energy exactly. One needs either source-valid Orlicz control or a different coupling/transport inequality.

### 8.3 One slab is not six faces

A cube cannot be completed by six independent slab interpolations without checking their intersections. The scalar construction removes local curl inside a slab, but different slab prescriptions can disagree on edges and corners. The next source-relevant geometric problem is to order or nest the slab deformations so that only lower-dimensional seams remain, and then prove their cost is subvolume.
