## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The two conjectures look at first like separate questions: one about a Gibbs measure being variationally minimal, the other about the SFE even being finite. I tried to force both through a common obstruction.

The useful observation is that **both problems can be moved to the boundary**.

- On the counterexample side, the most obvious Gaussian construction is to add a stationary random harmonic function to a Gaussian free field. I show below that every such harmonic cocycle with square-integrable increments is actually affine. Thus any genuinely new Gaussian infinite-SFE counterexample must lie beyond `L^2`; the heavy-tail/infinite-energy character is structural, not an accident of a bad example.
- On the variational side, the DLR/Markov property and the chain rule for relative entropy imply an exact finite-volume identity: when an infinite-volume Gibbs state and a finite-volume Gibbs reference have the same conditional kernel in an interior region, their entire finite-volume relative entropy is carried by the boundary shell. Therefore the missing variational theorem is not a bulk DLR identity. It is a **same-slope boundary cancellation theorem** strong enough to show that the leading volume-order boundary free-energy cost is independent of the ergodic Gibbs state.

This explains why the known isotropic/Lipschitz proof needed boundary analytic control and why a naive entropy-density argument does not immediately settle the anisotropic case.

No code is needed for either result; both are analytic identities/proofs.

## 4. B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST

### 4.1 Elementary counterexample attempts fail at the hypotheses

There are two immediate ways one might try to violate the conjectures.

**Random slope mixture.** Start with a Gaussian/minimal gradient Gibbs state and add an affine harmonic function `x -> A·x` with random vector `A`. The resulting gradient law is a mixture of slope-shifted Gibbs measures. But `A` is translation invariant, hence a non-degenerate law of `A` produces a non-ergodic mixture. Each ergodic component fixes `A` deterministically and returns to an ordinary affine-slope state. Therefore this does not attack either source conjecture.

**Mixture of distinct same-slope phases.** If two extremal same-slope Gibbs states exist, their nontrivial convex mixture is again Gibbs and translation invariant, but it is not ergodic. Thus the easiest way to make a finite-SFE nonminimal-looking mixture is also excluded before minimality is tested.

The source's Gaussian harmonic construction is much more serious because it can retain ergodicity. That route therefore deserves the first exact calculation.

### 4.2 An exact `L^2` rigidity lemma for stationary harmonic cocycles

I use the standard stationary-cocycle formulation of the harmonic route. This is a **restricted structural lemma**, not a claim that it settles the source's open harmonic problem.

Let `(Omega,P,(T_x)_{x in Z^d})` be an ergodic probability-preserving `Z^d` action. A real degree-one cocycle is a map

`H : Omega x Z^d -> R`

with `H(omega,0)=0` and

\[
H(\omega,x+y)=H(\omega,x)+H(T_x\omega,y).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write

\[
\eta_i(\omega)=H(\omega,e_i),\qquad U_i f=f\circ T_{e_i}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Assume the spatial function `x -> H(omega,x)` is discrete harmonic almost surely and each `eta_i in L^2(P)`.

**Lemma 4.1 (square-integrable stationary harmonic cocycles are affine).** Under these assumptions there is a deterministic vector `s in R^d` such that

\[
H(\omega,x)=s\cdot x
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb Z^d\text{ a.s.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Set `s_i=E eta_i` and `xi_i=eta_i-s_i`. The cocycle identity around an elementary square gives the curl equation

\[
(U_j-I)\xi_i=(U_i-I)\xi_j
\qquad(1\le i,j\le d).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Harmonicity at the origin gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^d\bigl(H(e_i)+H(-e_i)\bigr)=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The cocycle relation says `H(-e_i)=-U_i^{-1} eta_i`, so after subtracting the deterministic means,

\[
\sum_{i=1}^d (I-U_i^{-1})\xi_i=0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The commuting unitary operators `U_1,...,U_d` have a joint spectral representation over the torus `T^d`. At a spectral point `z=(z_1,...,z_d)`, write

\[
a_i(z)=z_i-1.
\]

Equation (4.4) becomes

\[
a_j\widehat\xi_i=a_i\widehat\xi_j.
\tag{4.7}
\]

For `z != (1,...,1)`, choose `k` with `a_k != 0`. Then (4.7) implies

\[
\widehat\xi_i=c(z)a_i(z)
\quad\text{for all }i
\tag{4.8}
\]

for a scalar spectral coefficient `c(z)`. Equation (4.6) becomes

\[
0=\sum_i(1-\overline z_i)\widehat\xi_i
  =c(z)\sum_i(1-\overline z_i)(z_i-1)
  =-c(z)\sum_i|z_i-1|^2.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Hence `c(z)=0` off the common fixed point `z=(1,...,1)`. The spectral mass at that common fixed point is exactly the subspace invariant under every lattice shift. Ergodicity makes that subspace the constants, and `E xi_i=0` removes the constant component. Thus `xi_i=0` in `L^2` for every `i`, i.e.

\[
\eta_i=s_i\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{4.10}
\]

The cocycle identity then yields `H(omega,x)=s·x` on all lattice points. QED.

### 4.3 Consequence for the Gaussian counterexample route

The source links a Gaussian infinite-SFE Gibbs construction to a nontrivial ergodic harmonic object in dimensions `d>=3`. Lemma 4.1 shows that, in the stationary degree-one cocycle realization, **a nontrivial example cannot have square-integrable increments**. Any successful Gaussian counterexample must therefore be genuinely heavy tailed / outside the natural quadratic-energy class.

This is consistent with, but stronger in direction than, a generic appeal to finite Dirichlet energy. It also explains why simply searching among Gaussian harmonic correctors from uniformly elliptic random-conductance theory is unlikely to produce the desired counterexample: the usual corrector theory is deliberately built in `L^2` or nearby integrability regimes.

A literature check located Jérôme Depauw's 2013 harmonic-cocycle ergodic theorem, which emphasizes that harmonicity in elliptic random environments allows `L^2` integrability where general cocycles need stronger Lorentz integrability. I do **not** claim Lemma 4.1 as novel; on deterministic `Z^d` it is a short joint-spectral argument and should be regarded as likely folklore unless a novelty search establishes otherwise.

## 6. B11 — DUALIZE: exact finite-volume free energy lives on a boundary shell

This method produced the strongest structural reduction of the run.

### 6.1 Abstract Markov-relative-entropy lemma

Let a finite region `Delta` be decomposed as

\[
\Delta=I\sqcup B,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where `B` is a boundary shell thick enough that all interactions from the interior `I` to the complement of `Delta` are screened by `B` (one lattice layer for a nearest-neighbor specification, modulo the source's gradient/gauge bookkeeping).

Let `mu_Delta` and `rho_Delta` be probability measures on the finite-volume configuration space. Suppose they have **the same conditional kernel on `I` given `B`**:

\[
\mu(d\omega_I\mid\omega_B)
=ho(d\omega_I\mid\omega_B)
=\gamma_I(d\omega_I\mid\omega_B)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then the chain rule for relative entropy gives, in the extended-real sense,

\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_\Delta\|\rho_\Delta)
=D(\mu_B\|\rho_B).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed,

\[
D(\mu_\Delta\|\rho_\Delta)
=D(\mu_B\|\rho_B)
+\int D\!\left(\mu(\cdot\mid\omega_B)\|\rho(\cdot\mid\omega_B)\right)\mu_B(d\omega_B),
\tag{6.4}
\]

and the second term is identically zero by (6.2).

For a finite-range Gibbs specification, an infinite-volume DLR state restricted to `Delta` and the normalized finite-volume free-boundary Gibbs reference have the same interior conditional law once `B` contains the interaction boundary. Therefore (6.3) is the exact finite-volume information-theoretic skeleton behind the variational problem.

### 6.2 Free-energy form

Let the unnormalized finite-volume Gibbs reference be

\[
q_\Delta(d\omega)=e^{-H_\Delta(\omega)}\lambda_\Delta(d\omega),
\qquad
Z_\Delta=q_\Delta(1),
\tag{6.5}
\]

and let

\[
\rho_\Delta=Z_\Delta^{-1}q_\Delta.
\tag{6.6}
\]

With the finite-dimensional convention used by Sheffield,

\[
FE_\Delta(\mu)=D(\mu_\Delta\|q_\Delta)
=D(\mu_\Delta\|\rho_\Delta)-\log Z_\Delta.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Combining (6.3) and (6.7) yields the boundary identity

\[
\boxed{
FE_\Delta(\mu)+\log Z_\Delta
=D(\mu_B\|\rho_{\Delta,B}).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

The only source-specific work needed to instantiate this identity is the usual gradient quotient/gauge choice and the exact local Hamiltonian convention; those do not change the chain-rule mechanism.

For two DLR states `mu,nu` for the same specification,

\[
\boxed{
FE_\Delta(\mu)-FE_\Delta(\nu)
=
D(\mu_B\|\rho_{\Delta,B})
-
D(\nu_B\|\rho_{\Delta,B}).
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus the finite-volume **difference** in free energy between two Gibbs states is exactly a difference of boundary-shell relative entropies.

### 6.3 Why this does not trivially prove Conjecture 10.3.1

Let `mu` be the finite-SFE ergodic Gibbs state of interior slope `u`, and let `nu` be a minimal same-slope state whose existence is available in Sheffield's general framework. If one could prove

\[
\frac{1}{|\Delta_n|}
\left[
D(\mu_{B_n}\|\rho_{\Delta_n,B_n})
-
D(\nu_{B_n}\|\rho_{\Delta_n,B_n})
\right]
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{6.10}
\]

then (6.9), divided by volume and passed to the SFE limit, would give

\[
SFE(\mu)=SFE(\nu)=\sigma(u),
\tag{6.11}
\]

which is exactly the minimality clause.

But the obvious cardinality argument is false. Even though `|B_n|=O(n^{d-1})`, the boundary values required to enforce slope `u` vary on scale `n`, so a boundary-shell relative entropy can carry a **volume-order** cost. This is precisely how the surface tension can be encoded by a codimension-one boundary.

Finite SFE only says that this boundary information cost is at most volume order. It does **not** say that two same-slope Gibbs states have the same leading boundary cost. The missing theorem is the cancellation (6.10).

This matches Sheffield's own diagnosis: the proved perturbed-isotropic variational principle uses substantial analytic boundary control, and Chapter 10 points toward anisotropic Orlicz-Sobolev analogues for the general case.

So B11 converts the broad conjecture into a concrete boundary theorem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Same-slope finite-SFE DLR states must have identical leading boundary information cost.}
}
\tag{6.12}
\]

This is not merely the statement that two Gibbs phases have zero specific relative entropy with respect to each other. Distinct equilibrium phases can have zero relative-entropy density, and such a statement alone does not identify their SFE against the source's free-boundary reference. Equation (6.9) shows exactly which common-reference boundary term has to cancel.

**B11 outcome:** exact finite-volume reduction obtained; source-level cancellation (6.10) remains unproved. No metric movement.
