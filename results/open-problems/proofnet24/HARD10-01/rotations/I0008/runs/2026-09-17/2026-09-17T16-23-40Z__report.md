## 4. B7 — continuum limit: an exact anisotropic thin-shell obstruction

Work in dimension three. Take the anisotropic convex energy

\[
\mathcal H(f)=\int \bigl(|\partial_1 f|+|\partial_2 f|+|\partial_3 f|^p\bigr)\,dx,
\qquad p>2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the continuum analogue of a legitimate anisotropic SAP with cheap linear tangential directions and a superquadratic normal direction.

On the face `G subset R^2`, centered at the origin, define the square pyramid

\[
h_M(x_1,x_2)=M\bigl(1-M\|x\|_\infty\bigr)_+.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its support is the square `[-1/M,1/M]^2`.

### 4.1 Tangential energy stays bounded

Partition the support into the eight triangles on which one signed coordinate realizes `||x||_infty`. On each such triangle exactly one weak derivative has magnitude `M^2`, and each triangle has area `1/(2M^2)`. Therefore

\[
\int_{\mathbb R^2} \bigl(|\partial_1h_M|+|\partial_2h_M|\bigr)dx
=8\cdot M^2\cdot \frac1{2M^2}=4.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the tangential anisotropic energy is uniformly bounded.

### 4.2 The L1 mismatch tends to zero

Using level sets,

\[
|\{h_M>t\}|=\frac4{M^2}\left(1-\frac tM\right)^2,
\qquad 0<t<M.
\]

Hence

\[
\int h_M dx
=\int_0^M |\{h_M>t\}|dt
=\frac{4}{3M}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

So this is exactly the type of trace that becomes arbitrarily close to the affine zero trace in `L^1` while retaining bounded tangential energy.

### 4.3 Every thickness-epsilon interpolation has exploding normal p-energy

Let `g(x',s)` be **any** Sobolev interpolation on `G x [0,epsilon]` satisfying

\[
g(x',0)=h_M(x'),\qquad g(x',\varepsilon)=0.
\]

For almost every `x'`, the fundamental theorem of calculus and Holder/Jensen give

\[
\int_0^\varepsilon |\partial_3g(x',s)|^pds
\ge \varepsilon^{1-p}|h_M(x')|^p.
\tag{4.5}
\]

A second exact level-set calculation gives

\[
\int h_M^p dx
=p\int_0^M t^{p-1}|\{h_M>t\}|dt
=\frac{8}{(p+1)(p+2)}M^{p-2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Combining (4.5) and (4.6),

\[
\boxed{
\int_{G\times[0,\varepsilon]}|\partial_3g|^p
\ge
\frac{8}{(p+1)(p+2)}\,\varepsilon^{1-p}M^{p-2}
\xrightarrow[M\to\infty]{}\infty.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Therefore there is **no** deterministic anisotropic analogue of Sheffield's face interpolation lemma whose hypotheses are merely:

1. bounded tangential anisotropic energy;
2. arbitrarily small `L^1` distance from the affine trace;
3. arbitrary SAP growth in the normal direction;

and whose conclusion uniformly bounds shell energy.

### 4.4 Why Cianchi does not repair (4.7)

Cianchi controls a function from the integral of a convex function of its **already existing full gradient**. The shell problem is different: from a `(d-1)`-dimensional trace one must *create* a normal derivative of size roughly `h/epsilon`. In the example above the trace information supplied by the cheap tangential directions controls `h_M` only weakly enough to allow (4.2), while the newly created normal derivative is charged by `|.|^p`. A full-gradient embedding cannot manufacture the missing cross-direction trace integrability.

Thus the continuum part of the selected port fails at the exact point where Sheffield's isotropic proof uses the same `A` in tangential and normal directions.

## 5. B8 — exact discrete single-column obstruction to a literal anisotropic Lemma 5.3.5

The continuum spike is not a scaling artefact. There is a simpler exact lattice obstruction.

Let

\[
Q_n=\{0,1,\dots,n\}^3,
\]

choose an interior horizontal site `c=(floor(n/2),floor(n/2))`, and use directional nearest-neighbor potentials

\[
V_1(t)=V_2(t)=|t|,\qquad V_3(t)=|t|^p,\qquad p>2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

These are convex, coercive directional SAP potentials. Set slope `u=0`. Given a small `delta>0`, put

\[
H_n=\left\lfloor \frac{\delta n^2}{4}\right\rfloor
\tag{5.2}
\]

and define the one-column spike

\[
\phi_n(x_1,x_2,x_3)=
\begin{cases}
H_n,&(x_1,x_2)=c,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.1 The input energy is only volume order

All vertical increments vanish. At each height there are at most four tangential edges crossing from the spike column to a zero column. Therefore

\[
H^o_{Q_n}(\phi_n)\le4(n+1)H_n
\le \delta(n+1)n^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For any fixed energy allowance `C|Q_n|`, choose `delta` below a fixed fraction of `C`; then (5.4) satisfies the analogue of Sheffield's energy hypothesis for all large `n`.

### 5.2 The global L1 mismatch can be arbitrarily small

Only `n+1` vertices are nonzero, so

\[
\sum_{x\in Q_n}|\phi_n(x)|=(n+1)H_n
\le \frac{\delta}{4}n^2(n+1)
\le \delta |Q_n|
\tag{5.5}
\]

for all large `n`. Hence the analogue of Sheffield's global `L^1` closeness assumption also holds.

### 5.3 Any outside shell interpolation has supervolume normal energy

Now take any candidate outside interpolation `psi_n` which agrees with `phi_n` on the centered inner cube and with the affine zero function on the outer boundary, with shell width at most

\[
L_n\le \varepsilon n+2.
\]

Along the normal lattice segment from the spike column's inner endpoint to the corresponding outer face, `psi_n` must drop by exactly `H_n`. If the successive vertical increments are `d_1,...,d_{L_n}`, then

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}d_j=-H_n.
\]

Convexity/Jensen gives the exact lower bound

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}|d_j|^p
\ge L_n\left(\frac{H_n}{L_n}\right)^p
=\frac{H_n^p}{L_n^{p-1}}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

For large `n`, `H_n >= delta n^2/8`, so

\[
H^{\rm shell}(\psi_n)
\ge
\frac{(\delta n^2/8)^p}{(\varepsilon n+2)^{p-1}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac{H^{\rm shell}(\psi_n)}{n^3}
\ge c_p\,\delta^p\,\varepsilon^{1-p}\,n^{p-2}
\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(p>2).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

No constant `C_0` can make this shell energy `<= C_0 C epsilon n^3` for all large `n`.
