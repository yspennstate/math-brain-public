## 3. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: identify the sharp closest critical linear target

### 3.1 Exact closest-point lemma

Consider all source-admissible parameters in the first branch of Theorem 8.26 in dimension three, and compare `(p,q)` with the variational point `(2,2)` using the sup-distance

`D(p,q)=max(p-2,q-2)`

for `p,q>=2`. Then

**Lemma.** The minimum possible `D(p,q)` is exactly `1/2`, attained at

`delta=0`, `p=q=5/2`, `kappa=0`.

**Proof.** Since `delta<=0`, every admissible point satisfies

`2/p + 3/q <= 2+delta <= 2`.

If `D(p,q)<=t`, then `p<=2+t` and `q<=2+t`. Because `x -> 1/x` decreases,

`2/p + 3/q >= 5/(2+t)`.

Admissibility therefore forces

`5/(2+t) <= 2`,

hence `t>=1/2`. At `t=1/2`, choosing `p=q=5/2` gives

`2/p+3/q = (2+3)/(5/2)=2`.

The source range is `3/2<5/2<3`, so `delta=0` is admissible, and

`kappa = -1 + (5/4)(2-6/5) = -1 + (5/4)(4/5)=0`.

Thus the lower bound is attained. Moreover, equality in the estimate `2/p+3/q >= 5/(2+t)` at `t=1/2` forces both `p` and `q` to equal `5/2`, and equality in `2+delta<=2` forces `delta=0`. QED.

The corresponding critical trace smoothness is

`3/q - 1 = 3/(5/2)-1 = 1/5`,

so the critical initial-data scale is the no-slip analogue of `B^{1/5}_{5/2,5/2}`.

### 3.2 Consequence for the variational-extrapolation route

Agresti–Veraar Problem 3 records that for non-small `B`, the known variational extrapolation result reaches only `r in [2,2+epsilon)` for a small `epsilon>0` in the self-adjoint variational setting, with a separate small improvement in spatial integrability. The closest-point lemma shows that a rectangular extrapolation neighborhood around `(2,2)` must reach at least `epsilon=1/2` in both time and space exponents before it can intersect the critical Theorem-8.26 region.

So “extrapolate a little from `(2,2)`” is not merely incomplete in a qualitative sense: the exact critical geometry demands a **half-unit jump** in the worst exponent. This is a useful target specification for the linear problem.

### 3.4 A failed stronger route: higher-moment energy does not produce SMR

At the Hilbert variational scale let `H=L^2_sigma(O)`, `V=H^1_{0,sigma}(O)`, and for a smooth zero-trace solenoidal field `u` let

`B_n u = P[(b_n dot grad)u]`.

Because `P` is orthogonal on the solenoidal `L^2` space,

`<u,B_nu> = <u,(b_n dot grad)u>`.

Integration by parts gives

`<u,(b_n dot grad)u> = -(1/2) int_O (div b_n)|u|^2 dx`,

because the boundary term contains `|u|^2` and vanishes under no-slip. Thus the radial quadratic-variation term in Ito's formula for `||u||_H^r` is lower order when `(div b_n)_n` is bounded in `l^2`, while the principal noise term is controlled by the same natural parabolicity bound as at `r=2`:

`sum_n ||B_nu||_2^2 <= 2 nu_0 ||grad u||_2^2`.

Therefore there is no immediate `r`-dependent *principal* energy obstruction to taking higher moments. But the resulting estimate controls weighted energy quantities such as

`int ||u||_H^{r-2} ||u||_V^2 dt`

and moments of `int ||u||_V^2 dt`; it does **not** give the source SMR norm

`E int ||u||_V^r dt`,

nor does it improve the spatial integrability from `q=2` to `q=5/2`. Hence ordinary Ito-energy iteration does not bridge the exact half-unit critical gap. This route is recorded as failed, not silently recycled.

## 4. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: current primary literature changes the nearest technique

### 4.1 Portal–Veraar reduction explains exactly why the rigid case worked

Portal and Veraar, *Stochastic maximal regularity for rough time-dependent problems* (arXiv:1810.01183; published 2019), Section 3.5, give a reduction of non-small first-order linear noise to a deterministic/random operator problem when each noise generator `B_j` generates bounded groups, the groups commute with one another, and crucially the groups commute with `A`. Their Assumption 3.17 and Theorem 3.18 then replace `(A,B)` by an operator containing the Ito correction `A + (1/2)[B,B]`.

For the present no-slip transport problem, the predecessor report's principal commutator

`[Delta,b dot grad]_principal = 2 sym(grad b):D^2`

shows that generic strain is exactly what destroys this commutation. Thus the previous rotational/Killing construction is not an isolated trick: it lies precisely in the structural class where the strongest existing non-small-noise reduction applies.

This also prevents a false move: Portal–Veraar cannot simply be invoked for generic no-slip transport coefficients, because its commutation/group hypotheses are part of the theorem, not cosmetic assumptions.

## 5. B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS: separate the fixed-domain and moving-boundary difficulties

The full source coefficient class permits transport fields that need not be tangent to the boundary. Trying to solve all such fields at once mixes two different hard mechanisms:

1. **tangent fields:** a geometric stochastic flow can preserve the physical domain, so the hard issue is a random/nonautonomous Stokes operator on a fixed domain;
2. **normal fields:** the stochastic flow itself moves the boundary, so an additional Piola/ALE moving-domain layer is unavoidable.

As a research decomposition, not a source closure, it is therefore sensible to attack “all sufficiently smooth tangent fields” first.

### 5.1 Exact conditional geometric reduction for the Lie-transport subclass

There is one source-admissible subclass where this split can be written exactly. Suppose a finite family `b_n` is smooth, divergence free and tangent to the boundary, and choose the source diffusion nonlinearity so that

`g_n(x,u)=-(u dot grad)b_n(x)`.

Then the total linear stochastic coefficient is the Lie derivative

`K_n u = (b_n dot grad)u - (u dot grad)b_n = [b_n,u]`.

For smooth divergence-free no-slip `u`, tangency of `b_n` and `u|boundary=0` imply that `K_nu` is again divergence free and has zero boundary trace. The Stratonovich stochastic flow generated by the `b_n` preserves the domain, and the vector-field pushforward/pullback is generated by these Lie brackets.

Writing the Ito equation schematically as

`du + A u dt = sum_n K_n u dW_n`,

its Stratonovich form is

`du + (A + (1/2)sum_n K_n^2)u dt = sum_n K_nu circle dW_n`.

At the smooth formal level, pullback by the stochastic flow removes the stochastic first-order term and gives a random nonautonomous deterministic equation

`partial_t v + A_t v = 0`,

with

`A_t = S_t^{-1}(A + (1/2)sum_n K_n^2)S_t`.

No claim is made here that the source's general projected transport operator `P(b_n dot grad)` is a Lie generator; the matched `g_n` and tangent/divergence-free assumptions are essential to this exact geometric subclass.

The principal symbol before pullback is

`Q(x,xi)=nu|xi|^2 - (1/2)sum_n (b_n(x) dot xi)^2`.

The source parabolicity condition gives

`Q(x,xi) >= (nu-nu_0)|xi|^2`.

If `Phi_t` is the stochastic diffeomorphism with Jacobian `J_t`, the pulled-back principal form is evaluated at `J_t^{-T}xi`. Hence, up to the standard Piola/vector transformation, on the stopping event `||J_t||<=R` its ellipticity satisfies

`Q_t(y,xi) >= (nu-nu_0) R^{-2}|xi|^2`.

Thus natural parabolicity is not destroyed by the flow; it becomes a pathwise ellipticity bound whose constant depends on the Jacobian distortion. This points to stopping-time local maximal regularity rather than global-in-omega uniform coefficients.

What is missing is not ellipticity. It is the correct **nonautonomous no-slip Stokes maximal-regularity theorem with the transformed pressure/divergence constraint**, at the source-critical `p=q=5/2` scale, for Brownian-time random coefficients.
