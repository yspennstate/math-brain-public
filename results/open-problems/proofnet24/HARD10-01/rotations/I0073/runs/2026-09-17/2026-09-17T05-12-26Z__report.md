## 3. Mathematical idea before code

The previous report solved a highly symmetric point: a rotational Killing field on a ball. The natural next question is not “can the same group trick be repeated?” but:

> **What is the first term that appears when the transport field is deformed away from a Killing field?**

The answer can be computed exactly. If `D_b = b dot grad` denotes the first-order transport operator, then the principal part of the commutator `[Delta,D_b]` is controlled by

`E(b) := sym grad b = (grad b + (grad b)^T)/2`.

This is a decisive distinction. The source parabolicity condition controls the **amplitude** of the vector fields through quadratic forms `b_n tensor b_n`; the deformation of the Laplacian/Stokes principal part is controlled by the **strain** `E(b_n)`. A vector field may be large while having zero strain (a rigid rotation), exactly the situation of the previous restricted theorem.

Thus the right deformation parameter around the solved symmetry point is not `||b||` but `||sym grad b||`.

No finite checker is relevant to this claim; the derivation below is an exact differential-operator calculation.

## 4. B6 — deform the rotational model: exact commutator and Killing rigidity

### 4.1 Pure transport

Let `b` be `C^3` on an open subset of `R^3`, and define componentwise

`D_b u = (b dot grad)u`.

For a smooth vector field `u`, direct differentiation gives, for each component `i`,

`Delta(D_b u_i)`

`= (Delta b_j) partial_j u_i`

`  + 2 (partial_k b_j) partial_{kj} u_i`

`  + b_j partial_j Delta u_i`.

Consequently

`[Delta,D_b]u_i`

`:= Delta(D_b u_i) - D_b(Delta u_i)`

`= (Delta b_j) partial_j u_i`

`  + 2 (partial_k b_j) partial_{kj}u_i`.

Since the Hessian `partial_{kj}u_i` is symmetric in `(k,j)`, the antisymmetric part of `grad b` disappears from the second-order term. Hence

`2 (partial_k b_j) partial_{kj}u_i`

`= 2 E(b)_{kj} partial_{kj}u_i`.

Therefore the complete second-order part of the commutator is

`2 E(b) : Hess(u_i)`.

In particular, the transport field commutes with the Euclidean Laplacian **at principal order** if and only if

`E(b)=0`.

Adding an arbitrary matrix-valued zeroth-order term `M(x)u` to the transport generator changes the commutator only at first and zeroth order. Thus the principal statement remains true for operators of the form

`K u = (b dot grad)u + M(x)u`.

This includes the Lie-transport/stretched generator used in the previous rotational model.

### 4.2 Exact formula for the Lie generator

For the geometric Lie bracket

`K_b u = (b dot grad)u - (grad b)u`,

the full componentwise commutator is

`[Delta,K_b]u_i`

`= (Delta b_j) partial_j u_i`

`  + 2(partial_k b_j) partial_{kj}u_i`

`  - (partial_j Delta b_i)u_j`

`  - 2(partial_k partial_j b_i)partial_k u_j`.

Again its second-order part is exactly

`2 E(b)_{kj} partial_{kj}u_i`.

Thus the stretching correction can repair divergence geometry, but it cannot remove the generic second-order deformation created by a non-Killing flow.

### 4.3 Rigidity lemma

**Lemma.** Let `U subset R^3` be connected and let `b in C^2(U;R^3)`. If `E(b)=0` in `U`, then

`b(x)=Sx+c`

for a constant skew-symmetric matrix `S` and a constant vector `c`.

**Proof.** The Killing equation is

`partial_j b_k + partial_k b_j = 0`.

Set

`T_{ijk}=partial_i partial_j b_k`.

Mixed derivatives give `T_{ijk}=T_{jik}`, while differentiating the Killing equation gives

`T_{ijk}+T_{ikj}=0`.

Using these two symmetries cyclically,

`T_{ijk}`

`= -T_{ikj}`

`= -T_{kij}`

`=  T_{kji}`

`=  T_{jki}`

`= -T_{jik}`

`= -T_{ijk}`.

Hence `T_{ijk}=0` for all indices. Therefore `grad b` is constant. The original Killing equation says this constant matrix is skew-symmetric, so `b(x)=Sx+c`. QED.

### 4.4 Consequence for the previous exact conjugation

The ball/rotation theorem from the preceding report used precisely a field `b(x)=Sx` with `S^T=-S`. The lemma shows that, locally in Euclidean space, this is not merely a convenient example: **rigid motions are the only smooth fields for which the transport deformation produces no second-order commutator with the Euclidean Laplacian.**

For a bounded domain, a rigid field must additionally generate a flow preserving the domain if one wants a fixed-domain exact conjugation. On a ball centered at the origin, rotations do so. On a generic bounded domain, the available continuous isometry group may be trivial.

This does not prove an impossibility theorem for Problem 9. It proves only that the specific “conjugate the noise away while keeping the same constant-principal-part Stokes operator” mechanism is rigid.

### 4.5 The useful deformation parameter

Write

`b = b_0 + epsilon c`,

where `b_0` is Killing. Then

`E(b)=epsilon E(c)`.

Thus one can have **non-small transport amplitude** because `b_0` is large, while the top-order deformation from the exactly solvable point is small because `epsilon E(c)` is small.

This suggests a genuinely different restricted route to Problem 9:

- treat the large Killing part nonperturbatively by stochastic conjugation;
- treat only the **strain defect** as a perturbation of the resulting random/nonautonomous Stokes operator.

The important point is that “small strain around a large rigid motion” is not the same as “small transport noise”. It therefore respects the conceptual distinction the source makes between non-small noise and perturbatively small total noise, although it still imposes an extra structural hypothesis and would remain PARTIAL unless that hypothesis is removed.

I do **not** claim the resulting stochastic maximal-regularity theorem here; the next sections identify exactly what it would require.

## 5. B18 — say it in another language: the commutator is the first variation of the metric

The same calculation becomes more transparent geometrically.

Let `Phi_s` be the deterministic flow of a smooth vector field `b`,

`d/ds Phi_s(x) = b(Phi_s(x)),   Phi_0(x)=x`,

and write

`J_s(x)=D Phi_s(x)`.

In coordinates pulled back by `Phi_s`, the Euclidean Laplacian acquires a variable principal coefficient. Up to the standard Jacobian/divergence factors, the contravariant principal matrix is

`a_s = J_s^{-1} J_s^{-T}`.

At `s=0`, `J_0=I` and

`(d/ds)J_s|_{s=0}=grad b`.

Therefore

`(d/ds)a_s|_{s=0}`

`= -(grad b) - (grad b)^T`

`= -2 E(b)`.

So the B6 commutator calculation and the flow-coordinate calculation are exactly the same obstruction in two languages:

- **operator language:** `[Delta,b dot grad]` acquires the second-order term `2 E(b):D^2`;
- **geometric language:** the stochastic/deterministic flow changes the inverse metric at first order by `-2E(b)`.

A Killing field is precisely the case in which the flow preserves the Euclidean metric, so the principal operator remains unchanged.

### 5.1 Fixed boundary versus moving boundary

This translation also separates two issues which are often conflated.

**Case A: `b dot n = 0` on `partial O`.** For a sufficiently smooth field, the deterministic flow preserves `partial O` and hence preserves `O`. The no-slip condition remains a zero trace on a fixed domain after pullback. The price is a random/time-dependent **principal coefficient** whenever `E(b) != 0`.

**Case B: `b dot n != 0` somewhere on `partial O`.** The ambient flow does not preserve the fixed domain. Pulling the transport out of the equation naturally produces a **moving-domain** problem. No-slip is still geometrically meaningful — zero velocity pulls back to zero on the transported boundary — but one must now handle the Stokes operator, divergence constraint and pressure on a random moving domain (or equivalently on the reference domain with Piola-transformed random coefficients).

This is a more precise diagnosis than the original heuristic “normal transport violates the trace”. At the weak level that heuristic was false; in flow coordinates the normal component changes the geometry rather than immediately destroying the boundary condition.

### 5.2 Multiple stochastic transport fields

For a Stratonovich stochastic flow generated by smooth fields `b_n`, the same geometry appears pathwise. The random Jacobian produces a random metric, and the Itô correction contributes second-order terms built from the same transport fields. The source equation is more general than a pure geometric Lie-transport system because it also allows lower-order `g` terms. Therefore a stochastic-flow reduction of the **leading transport part** is a route, not yet a source theorem.

The key structural message survives: the generic non-small problem is naturally transformed into a random-coefficient/nonautonomous Stokes problem, not into the constant Stokes operator treated by the exact rotational argument.

## 6. B22 — read the failures: what the nearest solved problems have that Problem 9 lacks

The literature checked above lines up unusually well with the B18 reformulation.

### 6.1 Goodair: boundary cancellations are geometry-specific

Goodair's high-order bounded-domain work obtains delicate cancellations under the Leray projection in **2D** with a specific **Navier** boundary condition. This shows that high-order projected transport estimates can exist, but it also demonstrates why boundary geometry matters: the estimates are built around powers of the corresponding Stokes operator and that boundary realization. It does not supply the no-slip 3D critical `L^p(L^q)` pair required here.

The 2026 anisotropic no-slip paper proves a different type of result — convergence of weak martingale solutions in an inviscid limit between plates — and therefore does not provide the high-`p` local theory required by Problem 9.

### 6.2 Agresti–Sauerbrey–Veraar: stochastic characteristics can remove transport

The 2025 stochastic-flow paper confirms that smooth transport noise can be removed by a Kunita flow and replaced by a random PDE. That is strong evidence that B18 is the right category change. However, their target is a parabolic regularity problem, not the divergence-free no-slip Stokes system with pressure and critical Navier–Stokes nonlinearity.

### 6.3 Saal: moving boundaries are not automatically fatal

Saal's deterministic work proves `L^p-L^q` maximal regularity for Stokes systems on noncylindrical space-time domains by pulling the moving-domain problem back to a nonautonomous problem on a fixed cylinder. This is directly relevant to the `b dot n != 0` branch above: the moving boundary itself is not an automatic dead end.

But the source problem needs a random family generated by stochastic characteristics, with constants and function spaces compatible with the source's critical stochastic framework. That bridge is not contained in the deterministic moving-domain theorem.

### 6.4 Gallarati–Veraar: rough time dependence can be compatible with maximal regularity

Gallarati–Veraar establish deterministic maximal regularity for broad elliptic operators whose leading coefficients are merely measurable in time and continuous in space. This is important because Brownian-flow-generated coefficients are rough in time; temporal roughness alone need not be fatal.

Again the missing system is more specific: incompressibility, pressure, the no-slip Stokes realization, Piola transforms, and source-critical weak spaces must all survive simultaneously.

The source parabolicity condition controls

`sum_n (b_n dot xi)^2`,

not

`sum_n |sym grad b_n|^2`.
