## 4. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY: an exact parity/reflection obstruction

A very tempting half-space strategy is to extend a no-slip solenoidal field to the whole space by simple parity, apply a whole-space stochastic maximal-regularity theorem, and restrict back. For scalar Dirichlet problems the odd extension often works. For incompressible velocity, however, divergence and full no-slip parity conflict.

Let

\[
\mathbb H^3=\{(x',x_3):x_3>0\},\qquad x'=(x_1,x_2),
\]

and let `u` be a smooth divergence-free field in `H^3`. Consider the most general **constant-sign componentwise reflection** across `x_3=0` with one sign for tangential components and one for the normal component:

for `y>0`,

\[
\widetilde u_a(x',-y)=\sigma_t u_a(x',y),\quad a=1,2,
\]
\[
\widetilde u_3(x',-y)=\sigma_n u_3(x',y),
\qquad \sigma_t,\sigma_n\in\{\pm1\}.
\]

Because `div u=0` above the boundary,

\[
\partial_1u_1+\partial_2u_2=-\partial_3u_3.
\]

Below the boundary, remembering that differentiating through the reflected normal coordinate contributes a sign,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{div}\widetilde u(x',-y)
&=\sigma_t(\partial_1u_1+\partial_2u_2)-\sigma_n\partial_3u_3\\
&=-(\sigma_t+\sigma_n)\partial_3u_3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore this entire two-sign reflection family preserves arbitrary solenoidal fields **if and only if**

\[
\boxed{\sigma_n=-\sigma_t.}
\]

There are then only two possibilities.

### Case 1: tangential even, normal odd

`(sigma_t,sigma_n)=(+1,-1)`.

Continuity across the plane forces `u_3|_{x_3=0}=0`, but places no parity-forced zero condition on `u_1,u_2`.

### Case 2: tangential odd, normal even

`(sigma_t,sigma_n)=(-1,+1)`.

Continuity forces the tangential trace `u_1=u_2=0`, but places no parity-forced zero condition on `u_3`.

Thus no constant-sign local reflection simultaneously

1. preserves every divergence-free field, and
2. encodes the **full no-slip trace** `u|_{x_3=0}=0` by parity.

In particular, making **all three components odd** enforces full zero trace but has `sigma_t=sigma_n=-1`, so for a generic solenoidal field

\[
\operatorname{div}\widetilde u(x',-y)=2\partial_3u_3(x',y),
\]

which does not vanish.

### What this proves, and what it does not

This is a complete elementary no-go lemma for **simple constant-sign local parity extensions**. It does **not** rule out a nonlocal solenoidal extension operator, a Bogovskii correction, a Hodge extension, or a pressure-corrected whole-space reduction. It nevertheless kills the most obvious scalar-Dirichlet shortcut and explains why the half-space incompressible problem cannot be reduced to the whole-space transport theorem by naive odd reflection.

This result is new only to this track; I make no literature-novelty claim for the reflection observation.

## 5. B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: exact constant-tangential reduction to a deterministic anisotropic Stokes pencil

The previous recovery asked for a case simpler than general tangent variable coefficients. The cleanest one is a finite family of **constant tangent transport vectors**.

Let

\[
\beta_j\in\mathbb R^2\times\{0\},\qquad j=1,\dots,m,
\]

and define on smooth no-slip solenoidal fields

\[
B_j u=\beta_j\cdot\nabla_{x'}u.
\]

Let `A` denote the standard no-slip Stokes operator with viscosity `nu`, interpreted on the negative-positive extrapolation pair appropriate to `q=5/2`.

### 5.1 Tangential translations form the exact symmetry group

Define

\[
(S_j(s)u)(x',x_3)=u(x'+s\beta_j,x_3).
\]

Because the translation is parallel to the boundary:

- it preserves the half-space;
- it preserves the zero boundary trace;
- it preserves divergence;
- it is an isometry on the natural `L^q` and Sobolev/extrapolation scales;
- different `S_j` commute;
- it commutes with constant-coefficient derivatives and, hence, with the standard flat-boundary Stokes realization.

Thus this is exactly the structural setting for the known Portal–Veraar commuting-group transform, subject to the standard generator/domain identifications on the chosen no-slip extrapolation scale. Those identifications are compatible with modern half-space Stokes fractional-power theory, but I am not claiming a new proof of every Portal–Veraar domain hypothesis here; the identities below are exact on the smooth solenoidal core and extend once those standard identifications are invoked.

### 5.2 Corrected deterministic operator

Portal–Veraar's transform replaces the multiplicative transport noise by the corrected deterministic operator

\[
\widetilde A=A+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^m B_j^2.
\]

Set the tangential covariance matrix

\[
C:=\sum_{j=1}^m \beta_j\beta_j^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}.
\]

Since

\[
\sum_j B_j^2u
=\sum_{a,b=1}^2 C_{ab}\,\partial_{ab}u,
\]

we obtain on the smooth core

\[
\boxed{
\widetilde A u
=P\left[-\nu\,\partial_3^2u-\sum_{a,b=1}^2D_{ab}\partial_{ab}u\right],
\qquad
D:=\nu I_2-\frac12C.
}
\]

Here `P` is the half-space Helmholtz projection in the chosen realization.

### 5.3 Natural stochastic parabolicity becomes ordinary deterministic ellipticity

For constant vectors, the source natural parabolicity condition says

\[
\sum_j(\beta_j\cdot\xi)^2\le 2\nu_0|\xi|^2
\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb R^3.
\]

Because the `beta_j` are tangential, this is exactly

\[
C\le 2\nu_0I_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D=\nu I_2-\frac12C\ge (\nu-\nu_0)I_2>0.
}
\]

So in this special case the entire **non-small** stochastic transport problem is transformed, without assuming small `||beta||`, into a deterministic uniformly elliptic anisotropic Stokes problem. The only small parameter is the forbidden degeneracy of the natural parabolicity gap `nu-nu_0`; the total transport amplitude may be a fixed order-one fraction of the parabolicity threshold.

### 5.4 What remains

Portal–Veraar's theorem says, roughly, that stochastic maximal regularity for `(A,B)` is equivalent to zero-multiplicative-noise maximal regularity for `widetilde A` under its group assumptions. Thus this restricted half-space problem is reduced to the following deterministic statement:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uniform maximal }L^{5/2}\text{-regularity / }H^\infty\text{-calculus for }
\mathcal A_D:=P[-\nu\partial_3^2-\nabla'\!\cdot D\nabla']
}
\]

on the no-slip `H^{-1,5/2}_\sigma -> H^{1,5/2}_{0,\sigma}` scale, uniformly for positive `2x2` matrices satisfying

\[
(\nu-\nu_0)I_2\le D\le \nu I_2.
\]

The checked Breit–Gaudin theorem gives the standard Stokes–Dirichlet operator very strong maximal-regularity and functional-calculus control. The checked anisotropic half-space literature gives useful explicit dynamics. I did **not** recover a theorem whose statement directly matches this projected anisotropic `A_D` family on precisely the source extrapolation scale with the uniformity needed here. Therefore I stop at the reduction and do not claim the constant-tangent stochastic theorem as fully closed.

Even if that deterministic family is settled, it would remain a **restricted half-space / constant-tangent result**, hence PARTIAL for Problem 9 rather than movement of `C_ns`.

## 6. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: amplitude continuation becomes circular

The previous recovery proposed an amplitude parameter. In the constant-tangent case the transformed formulation makes the continuation obstruction completely explicit.

Introduce `lambda in [0,1]` and replace each noise operator by

\[
B_j^{(\lambda)}=\lambda B_j.
\]

The corrected operator is

\[
\widetilde A_\lambda
=A+\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\sum_jB_j^2,
\]

with tangential diffusion matrix

\[
D_\lambda
=\nu I_2-\frac{\lambda^2}{2}C.
\]

Natural parabolicity gives the **uniform ellipticity bound**

\[
\boxed{
D_\lambda\ge (\nu-\nu_0)I_2
\quad\text{for every }\lambda\in[0,1].
}
\]

At `lambda=0` one has the classical no-slip Stokes operator. Standard perturbation gives local openness of the set of `lambda` for which maximal regularity holds. But an open/closed continuation to `lambda=1` requires a uniform a priori maximal-regularity / resolvent `R`-bound for the deterministic family `A_{D_lambda}` depending only on the ellipticity ratio and the fixed `q=5/2` geometry.

Without such a uniform estimate, the continuation step size can collapse before `lambda=1`. Therefore the naive continuation argument is **circular**: its missing global a priori bound is precisely the deterministic anisotropic maximal-regularity theorem isolated in Section 5.

For general variable tangent fields `b_j(x)`, the situation is harder. The preceding I0073 report proved the principal commutator identity

\[
[\Delta,b\cdot\nabla]_{\rm principal}
=2\,\operatorname{sym}(\nabla b):D^2,
\]

so the group commutation used above fails whenever the strain is nonzero. Stochastic-flow conjugation then creates a random, time-dependent principal metric. The amplitude-continuation strategy would consequently need uniform `R`-bounds for a random/nonautonomous no-slip Stokes family, not merely the constant anisotropic pencil.

This identifies the exact gluing obstruction rather than producing another small-noise perturbation.
