## 4. Every route tried so far, and the precise reason each did not close the blocker

### Route A — naive boundary-trace obstruction (B2 / B11)

Initial idea: if `u=0` on the boundary and `b dot n != 0`, then `(b dot grad)u` sees `(b dot n) partial_n u`; perhaps the noise operator fails the no-slip domain.

Why it did not close: at the natural weak variational scale the no-slip condition kills the boundary term in the energy pairing. The first report showed that natural parabolicity controls the leading gradient-noise quadratic form without requiring `b dot n=0`. Thus the naive trace heuristic is not a source-valid impossibility argument.

What survived: the difficulty is not elementary preservation of pointwise zero trace; it is the compatibility of the projected first-order noise with the **higher-`q` extrapolated Stokes scale and stochastic maximal regularity**.

### Route B — exact symmetry / stochastic-flow conjugation (B30, then B6/B18)

Initial success: on a ball, rotational/Killing transport with matched stretching can be removed by an exact stochastic symmetry, yielding a random/deterministic transformed Stokes problem and a genuine restricted non-small-noise local theorem.

Why it did not close: the second report computed

`[Delta,b dot grad]_principal = 2 sym(grad b):D^2`.

Exact retention of the same Euclidean principal operator is therefore rigid: vanishing strain forces a Euclidean Killing field. For generic source coefficients, stochastic-flow conjugation produces a random variable-coefficient principal metric rather than the original Stokes operator.

What survived: the flow route does not fail; it changes the problem into **random/nonautonomous Stokes maximal regularity on a transformed domain/metric**. That is a different theorem, not an automatic reduction to the classical Stokes calculus.

### Route C — ordinary higher-moment energy estimates (B3)

Initial idea: perhaps one can extrapolate the `p=2` variational estimate directly by applying Itô's formula to `||u||_2^r`.

Why it did not close: no-slip again controls the leading boundary contribution and natural parabolicity controls the gradient-noise term, but the estimate produced is of weighted-energy form such as

`int ||u||_2^{r-2} ||grad u||_2^2 dt`,

not the stochastic maximal-regularity norm `L^p_t H^{1,q}_x`, and it does not create the needed spatial-integrability jump from `q=2` to `q=5/2`.

What survived: there is no obvious principal **energy** obstruction at `r>2`; the missing step is genuinely vector-valued `L^p(L^q)` stochastic regularity, not merely moments of the Hilbert energy.

### Route D — small-neighborhood extrapolation from the variational point (B3)

Initial idea: use known non-small variational SMR around `p=q=2` and extrapolate a little.

Why it did not close: the exact admissibility geometry gives a hard quantitative target. ### Route E — Portal–Veraar non-small-noise group reduction (B21)

Initial idea: use Doss–Sussmann / bounded-group transference to remove first-order noise nonperturbatively.

Why it did not close: the strongest available theorem requires structural group/commutation hypotheses. 6. **Nonlinear handoff.** Once the linear pair is in the required SMR class, verify the source nonlinear mapping assumptions for the no-slip extrapolated Besov trace space at the exact critical point.
