The source asks for local well-posedness of the three-dimensional incompressible stochastic Navier–Stokes equations on a bounded sufficiently regular domain with **no-slip boundary conditions** and **non-small transport noise**, equivalently an extension of the source's Theorem 8.26 to this boundary setting for at least one admissible `(p,kappa,delta,q)` regime. `sum_n (b_n(x)·xi)^2 <= 2 nu_0 |xi|^2`, with `nu_0<nu`,

## 3. Mathematical idea before any code

The first prompt heuristic was that a transport field with nonzero normal component should destroy the no-slip trace because, for smooth `u|_{partial O}=0`,

`(b·grad)u|_{partial O} = (b·n) partial_n u`

up to tangential terms. That heuristic turns out to be **too crude**. The right question is scale-dependent:

- at the weak variational scale `H^1_{0,sigma} -> H^{-1}_sigma`, the projected transport operator is perfectly meaningful and natural parabolicity gives coercivity without any tangency hypothesis;
- at stronger velocity Sobolev scales, the Leray projection need not preserve zero trace, and the boundary obstruction becomes real;
- in the first source-admissible 3D weak critical regime, the unresolved linear step is therefore not `p=2` coercivity but **high-`p` stochastic maximal regularity**, with the first clean target at `p=4,q=2,delta=0,kappa=0`.

That separation suggested a second idea: instead of forcing a generic high-`p` theorem immediately, look for a non-small coefficient family in which the stochastic transport can be conjugated away exactly while preserving the no-slip geometry. Rotational Killing fields on a ball provide such a test family. This yields a complete restricted theorem and, more importantly, a concrete model in which the natural parabolicity threshold is exactly the deterministic ellipticity threshold after conjugation.

## 4. B2 — attack the negation: the naive boundary-trace obstruction is false at the weak scale

Let `O` be a bounded `C^2` domain in `R^3` and set

`H = L^2_sigma(O)`,

`V = H^1_0(O;R^3) cap L^2_sigma(O)`,

`V* = H^{-1}_sigma(O)`.

Let `P` denote the Helmholtz projection where it is used on `L^2`, and consider the weak no-slip Stokes/transport pair

`A u = -nu P Delta u`,

`(B u)e_n = P[(b_n·grad)u]`.

For `u in V`, the projection is an `L^2` contraction. Hence the source parabolicity assumption gives

`||Bu||_{L^2(O;l^2)}^2`

`<= integral_O sum_n |(b_n·grad)u|^2 dx`

`<= 2 nu_0 ||grad u||_2^2`.

At the same time the weak Stokes form is

`<Au,u>_{V*,V} = nu ||grad u||_2^2`.

Therefore

`<Au,u> - (1/2)||Bu||^2 >= (nu-nu_0)||grad u||_2^2`.

Since `nu_0<nu`, this is the standard variational coercivity inequality. The stochastic maximal-regularity result for coercive Gelfand triples (the source's variational theorem) gives the `p=2` stochastic estimate for `(A,B)`.

**Conclusion B2.** Non-tangential transport is **not**, by itself, a weak-scale no-slip obstruction. One does not need `(b_n·grad)u` to have zero trace in order to interpret it as an `H`-valued stochastic coefficient followed by the weak Helmholtz map. The pressure/projection formulation bypasses the naive pointwise trace argument at `V -> V*` level.

This does not solve Problem 9. It only kills an incorrect obstruction route.

## 5. B11 — dualize: the actual boundary obstruction is a change-of-scale obstruction

The source's 2D no-slip treatment already uses the weak Gelfand triple and projected transport operator. In that formulation, the projection acts into the divergence-free dual space; there is no demand that `P[(b·grad)u]` itself lie in a strong zero-trace Sobolev domain.

The situation changes when one bootstraps to higher spatial regularity in velocity variables. On a bounded domain, `P` is nonlocal and in general does **not** preserve zero trace. This is exactly the difficulty emphasized in bounded-domain stochastic Lie-transport work: a velocity-form calculation that tries to remain in high-order no-slip Sobolev domains develops a projection/boundary mismatch, which is why vorticity or Navier/Lions boundary formulations are often used instead.

Thus the dual/weak formulation identifies the real obstruction more precisely:

`weak scale:   V --B--> H(l^2) --> V*   works under natural parabolicity;`

`strong scale: D(A^theta) --P(b·grad)--> ?   boundary compatibility becomes nontrivial.`

This explains why proving `p=2` coercivity is easy while obtaining the high-`p` critical theorem needed for 3D remains genuinely difficult.

## 7. A complete restricted theorem: rotational Lie-compensated non-small noise on a ball

The next result is a **restricted partial theorem**, not source closure.

### Theorem (restricted rotational no-slip family)

Let `O=B_R(0) subset R^3`. Let `S` be a fixed nonzero real `3x3` skew-symmetric matrix, and let `nu>0`. Put

`M = sup_{x in O}|Sx|`.

Choose `sigma != 0` such that

`sigma^2 M^2 < 2 nu`.

Let one Brownian motion `W` drive the source-form noise coefficients

`b(x)=sigma Sx`,

`g(x,y)=-sigma S y`,

and set all other noise coordinates to zero. Consider the Itô incompressible Navier–Stokes equation on `O` with no-slip boundary condition

`du + A u dt = F(u)dt + P[(b·grad)u + g(u)] dW`,

where `A=-nu P Delta` is the weak Dirichlet Stokes operator and `F(u)=-P[(u·grad)u]` in weak form.

Then for the explicit source-admissible tuple

`(p,kappa,delta,q)=(4,0,0,2)`

and each initial datum in the weak critical trace space `B^{1/2}_{2,4,sigma}(O)` (with the standard solenoidal/no-slip realization), there is a unique local pathwise solution in the corresponding weak critical class. The transport amplitude is controlled only by the natural strict parabolicity inequality above; no independent perturbative condition `||b||_infinity <= epsilon` is imposed.

In particular one may choose, for example,

`sigma^2 M^2 = nu`,

which lies a fixed fraction of the natural parabolicity threshold and is not a “take the noise norm to zero” argument.

### Proof

#### Step 1. The noise is exactly a rotation generator

Define

`K u = (Sx·grad)u - S u`.

Because `S` is skew-symmetric, the vector field `Sx` is tangent to every sphere centered at the origin. If `u` is smooth, divergence-free and has zero boundary trace, then `Ku` is also divergence-free and has zero boundary trace: the tangential derivative of the zero boundary trace vanishes, and the `-Su` term vanishes there. The divergence cancellation is exact:

`div((Sx·grad)u) - div(Su)=0`

when `div u=0`.

Consequently `P Ku=Ku`, and

`P[(b·grad)u+g(u)] = sigma K u`.

At the weak level `K:V->H` is bounded and, distributionally, `K:H->V*` is bounded. Hence `K^2:V->V*` is well-defined and bounded.

#### Step 2. Rotation group and equivariance

Let `R_s=e^{sS}` and define on vector fields

`(T_s u)(x)=R_{-s}u(R_s x)`.

Because rotations preserve the ball, Lebesgue measure, the no-slip condition and divergence, `(T_s)_{s in R}` is a strongly continuous unitary group on `H` and an isometric group on the corresponding weak Sobolev scale. Its generator is `K`:

`d/ds|_{s=0} T_s u = (Sx·grad)u-Su = Ku`.

Rotational invariance of the ball and Euclidean Laplacian gives

`T_s A = A T_s`,

and the Navier–Stokes bilinear term is equivariant:

`F(T_su)=T_sF(u)`.

#### Step 3. Exact stochastic conjugation

Write the projected SPDE as

`du = [-Au+F(u)]dt + sigma K u dW`.

Set

`u(t)=T_{sigma W_t} v(t)`.

For smooth data, Itô's formula for the group action gives

`du = T_{sigma W_t} dv + sigma K u dW + (sigma^2/2)K^2u dt`.

Using the equivariance from Step 2, the stochastic equation is therefore equivalent to the deterministic random-initial-data equation

`v' + A_eff v = F(v)`,

where

`A_eff = A + (sigma^2/2)K^2`.

The formula extends from smooth Galerkin data to the weak critical solution by density because `K:V->H`, `K:H->V*`, and `T_s` is uniformly bounded on all spaces involved.

#### Step 4. Natural parabolicity is exactly what keeps A_eff parabolic

Since `K` is skew-adjoint on `H`, the weak form of `A_eff` is

`a_eff(u,v)=nu(grad u,grad v) - (sigma^2/2)(Ku,Kv)`.

For every `eta>0`, boundedness of `Sx` and the zero-order term `Su` give

`||Ku||_2^2 <= (1+eta)M^2||grad u||_2^2 + C_eta ||u||_2^2`.

Because `sigma^2M^2<2nu`, choose `eta>0` so that

`c_0 := nu-(sigma^2/2)(1+eta)M^2 >0`.

Then

`a_eff(u,u) >= c_0||grad u||_2^2 - C||u||_2^2`.

After adding a harmless scalar shift `omega`, the form is coercive and closed on `V`. It therefore defines a sectorial weak operator on the Hilbert space `X_0=V*` with domain `X_1=V`, and `-(omega+A_eff)` generates an analytic semigroup. Scalar shifts do not affect local well-posedness.

The key point is that the principal loss is precisely `(sigma^2/2)M^2`, so the allowed amplitude is the natural parabolicity scale, not an unrelated tiny perturbation constant.

#### Step 5. Deterministic maximal regularity at p=4

`X_0=V*` is Hilbert. Analytic semigroup generators on Hilbert spaces have deterministic maximal `L^4`-regularity. Thus, for the shifted `A_eff`,

`w -> w' + A_eff w`

is an isomorphism from the zero-trace space

`_0E_T = _0W^{1,4}(0,T;X_0) cap L^4(0,T;X_1)`

onto `L^4(0,T;X_0)`.

#### Step 6. Critical bilinear estimate

Let

`beta=7/8`.

Complex interpolation gives an embedding

`X_beta=[X_0,X_1]_{7/8} -> H^{3/4}(O) -> L^4(O)`.

For the weak convection bilinear form `N(u,v)=P div(u tensor v)`, testing against `phi in V` yields

`|<N(u,v),phi>| <= ||u||_4 ||v||_4 ||grad phi||_2`.

Hence

`||N(u,v)||_{X_0} <= C ||u||_{X_beta}||v||_{X_beta}`.

The critical index identity is

`2(1-beta)=1/4=(1+kappa)/p`.

The mixed-derivative/Sobolev embedding for maximal-regularity functions gives

`E_T -> L^8(0,T;X_beta)`.

Therefore

`||F(u)-F(v)||_{L^4 X_0}`

`<= C (||u||_{L^8X_beta}+||v||_{L^8X_beta}) ||u-v||_{L^8X_beta}`.

#### Step 7. Critical contraction

Let `v_0 in (X_0,X_1)_{3/4,4}` and let

`z(t)=e^{-tA_eff}v_0`.

The trace characterization of maximal regularity gives

`z in E_T` and `z in L^8(0,T;X_beta)`.

Moreover

`||z||_{L^8(0,T;X_beta)} -> 0`

as `T downarrow 0` by absolute continuity of the integral.

Write `v=z+w`, with `w(0)=0`. Applying the inverse maximal-regularity operator to `F(z+w)` defines a map on `_0E_T`. The estimate in Step 6 and the uniform zero-trace embedding `_0E_T -> L^8X_beta` show that, after first choosing a small ball radius and then `T` so that the displayed norm of `z` is sufficiently small, this map sends the ball into itself and is a strict contraction. Banach's theorem gives a unique local `v in E_T`, with continuous trace in `(X_0,X_1)_{3/4,4}`.

For an `F_0`-measurable random initial datum, the deterministic solution map is applied pathwise and localized by the initial norm; measurability follows from the locally Lipschitz solution map. This yields the usual positive stopping time.

#### Step 8. Transform back

Set

`u(t)=T_{sigma W_t}v(t)`.

The rotation group preserves `V`, `V*`, the critical trace space, divergence-free fields and the zero boundary trace. The Itô calculation in Step 3 therefore reconstructs a local pathwise solution of the original stochastic equation, and uniqueness transfers in both directions through the invertible map `T_{sigma W_t}`.

This completes the restricted theorem. `square`

### Why this theorem is only PARTIAL

Problem 9 asks for the no-slip analogue under the source coefficient class, not merely a specially conjugable coefficient. The theorem above assumes all of the following extra structure:

- the domain is a ball;
- only one Brownian direction is used;
- the transport field is the rotational Killing field `b(x)=sigma Sx`;
- the lower-order noise is tuned as `g(x,y)=-sigma Sy`, so that the total noise is exactly the infinitesimal action of a symmetry of the deterministic Navier–Stokes equation.

These assumptions are not consequences of the source hypotheses. Therefore this theorem is a **source-valid restricted family**, not an extension of Theorem 8.26 for the full no-slip coefficient class. The frozen metric remains `C_ns=0`.

I also make no novelty claim for the conjugation mechanism. Stochastic-flow/Lie-transport changes of variables are standard machinery; the useful point here is the exact compatibility with the weak critical `p=4,q=2` no-slip scale and the natural parabolicity threshold.

## 8. What this run actually teaches about the full problem

Three conclusions survive beyond the restricted theorem.

1. **Do not impose tangency as a fake necessary condition at the weak scale.** Natural parabolicity already yields the `p=2` variational estimate with general bounded `b`; the naive zero-trace calculation attacks the wrong codomain.

2. **The first generic missing theorem is high-p, not energy-level.** For the weak 3D critical regime the exact first target is `SMR^bullet_{4,0}` on `H^{-1}_sigma -> H^1_{0,sigma}`.

3. **Boundary trouble is encoded in commutators/projection at stronger scales.** The rotational family succeeds because its noise generator is a true symmetry, so all troublesome commutators vanish. A plausible generic route is therefore to quantify the defect from this symmetry rather than treat `P(b·grad)` as a black box.

The next obstruction to study can be written schematically as the failure of

`[A^{1/2}, P(b·grad)]`

or its weak-scale analogue to obey the square-function/R-bounded estimates needed for `p=4` stochastic maximal regularity, with boundary terms made explicit.
