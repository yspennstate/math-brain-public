# 3. B1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: exact edge-variable conjugacy

The first useful change of category is to remove the equilibrium interaction completely and put all difficulty into the dynamics.

## Proposition 3.1 — tree Ising equilibrium is an exact product measure in root/edge coordinates

Fix a root `o`. Define

\[
R:=\sigma_o,
\qquad
\eta_e:=\sigma_u\sigma_v\in\{-1,+1\}
\quad(e=uv\in E(T)).
\]

Then

\[
\Phi:\sigma\mapsto (R,(\eta_e)_{e\in E})
\]

is a bijection, and under the zero-field ferromagnetic Gibbs law

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_\#\pi_\beta
=
\operatorname{Unif}\{-1,+1\}
\otimes
\bigotimes_{e\in E(T)}\nu_\theta,
\qquad
\nu_\theta(\eta_e=s)=\frac{1+s\theta}{2}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Because `T` is a tree, for every vertex `v`

\[
\sigma_v
=R\prod_{e\in[o,v]}\eta_e,
\]

so `Phi` is bijective. Moreover

\[
\sum_{uv\in E(T)}\sigma_u\sigma_v
=
\sum_{e\in E(T)}\eta_e.
\]

Hence

\[
\pi_\beta(R,\eta)
\propto
\prod_{e\in E(T)}e^{\beta\eta_e},
\]

which contains no `R` term and factorizes edge by edge. Finally

\[
\frac{e^{\beta s}}{e^\beta+e^{-\beta}}
=\frac{1+s\tanh\beta}{2}.
\]

This proves (3.1). `QED`

## Proposition 3.2 — exact dynamic conjugacy is a state-dependent star-flip chain

Under `Phi`, flipping the spin at a non-root vertex `v` toggles every edge variable incident to `v`; flipping the root toggles every incident edge variable and also toggles `R`. The flip rate is

\[
\boxed{
c_v(\eta)
=\frac{1}{1+\exp(2\beta\sum_{e\ni v}\eta_e)}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Every incident product `sigma_v sigma_u` changes sign when `sigma_v` changes sign, while all nonincident edge products remain fixed. At the root, `R=sigma_o` also changes sign. The energy difference of a spin flip is exactly `-2 beta sum_{e\ni v} eta_e`, and inserting this Gibbs ratio into the heat-bath flip probability gives (3.2). `QED`

### B1 outcome

The tree's equilibrium dependence is completely removable: in `(R,eta)` coordinates the target law is a product law for **every** `beta`. Therefore the tree cutoff difficulty is not an equilibrium factorization problem. It is a problem about a local, overlapping, state-dependent **star-update dynamics with product invariant measure**.

This is useful because it tells us exactly what an attempted tree proof must control. It also exposes a tempting but false shortcut: product equilibrium does **not** mean the non-equilibrium time marginals remain product.

## Exact finite counterexample to dynamic product preservation

Counterexample-first was applied before using (3.1) as a dynamic ansatz. Take `T_{2,1}` (a root with two leaves), start all spins `+`, and set `theta=tanh(beta)=1/4`, so `2 theta=1/2<1` is strictly inside tree uniqueness. Let the two edge variables be `eta_1,eta_2`.

At time zero,

\[
c_{\rm leaf}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}=\frac38,
\qquad
c_{\rm root}=\frac{1-\tanh(2\beta)}2=\frac9{34},
\]

because `tanh(2 beta)=2 theta/(1+theta^2)=8/17`. A root flip toggles both edge variables and hence leaves their product unchanged; a leaf flip toggles exactly one. Therefore

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_1(t),\eta_2(t))\right|_{t=0}
=
4c_{\rm root}
=
\boxed{\frac{18}{17}}>0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the initially deterministic edge law immediately develops positive edge-edge dependence. The exact rational checker and output named in the header certify (3.3); under a common source time normalization all rates, and hence the derivative, are simply multiplied by that common positive factor.

**Conclusion:** the product-coordinate transform is a structural simplification, not a solution. Any proof that treats the evolving edge law as a product law is invalid even on the smallest branching tree in strict uniqueness.

# 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVABLE POINT: where the high-temperature argument actually breaks

At `beta=0`, `theta=0`, all heat-bath choices ignore neighbors, the original spins evolve independently, and the model is exactly the hypercube/product benchmark exhibiting cutoff. Proposition 3.1 shows something stronger than expected: the **stationary** product representation survives for every `beta`. The first thing that breaks as `beta` increases is therefore entirely dynamical: the star rates in (3.2) become state dependent and neighboring stars overlap.

To locate the quantitative boundary of the naive route, define the local heat-bath conditional mean for a degree-`d` site by

\[
F_d(z_1,\ldots,z_d)
:=
\tanh\!\left(\beta\sum_{j=1}^d z_j\right).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For coordinate `i`, define the discrete derivative

\[
D_iF_d(z_{-i})
:=\frac12\left[
F_d(z_i=+1,z_{-i})-F_d(z_i=-1,z_{-i})
\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

Section 5 below proves the exact bound `0<=D_iF_d<=theta`. Consequently, any absolute-value/Dobrushin estimate gives only

\[
\sum_{i=1}^d |D_iF_d|\le d\theta.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For an internal vertex of `T_{b,h}`, `d=b+1`. The target uniqueness assumption only gives

\[
(b+1)\theta
\le
\frac{b+1}{b}(1-\delta),
\tag{4.4}
\]

which can exceed one. For example, with `b=2` and `theta` close to `1/2`, the right side is close to `3/2`. This pinpoints the first failure of the naive deformation from `beta=0`: **absolute one-step dependency mass becomes supercritical before tree uniqueness ends.**

On the other hand, a directed cavity step that excludes the parent has only `b` forward children and satisfies the static directional bound

\[
b\theta\le1-\delta.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The obstacle is that a genuine backwards dynamical history can revisit the edge it came from at an earlier time. One cannot simply decree histories to be nonbacktracking. Any successful deformation argument must either resum those temporal backtracks or use a norm in which they do not destroy contraction.

### B6 literature reconciliation

This diagnosis matches the existing literature more closely than the earlier generic high-temperature comparison. Martinelli--Sinclair--Weitz already obtain uniform equilibrium spectral-gap/log-Sobolev control on the complete tree through (and beyond) uniqueness by variance/entropy mixing methods related to tree non-reconstruction. Lubetzky--Sly's 2014 general cutoff criterion then fails specifically because a tree has exponential ball growth: their parameter `rho` is polynomial in `n` at radius order `log n`, producing an `O(log n)` window bound rather than `o(log n)`. The missing improvement must exploit **weighted/directional dependency**, not merely a stronger stationary log-Sobolev bound.

# 5. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: a strict local L2 dependency budget throughout uniqueness

The strongest mathematical output of this run is an exact local squared-influence inequality that survives throughout the entire tree-uniqueness interval, including parameter values where the L1/Dobrushin budget (4.3) fails.

## Proposition 5.1 — exact single-neighbor derivative formula

Let `theta=tanh(beta)` and, in (4.2), put

\[
s:=\sum_{j\ne i}z_j,
\qquad
t:=\tanh(\beta s).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
D_iF_d(z_{-i})
=\theta\,\frac{1-t^2}{1-\theta^2t^2},
\qquad
0\le D_iF_d(z_{-i})\le\theta.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

The tanh addition law gives

\[
\tanh(\beta(s+1))=\frac{t+\theta}{1+t\theta},
\qquad
\tanh(\beta(s-1))=\frac{t-\theta}{1-t\theta}.
\]

Subtracting and dividing by two yields

\[
D_iF_d
=\theta\frac{1-t^2}{1-\theta^2t^2}.
\]

For `beta>=0`, `theta in [0,1)` and `|t|<1`, so the expression is nonnegative. Since `theta^2<=1`,

\[
1-\theta^2t^2\ge1-t^2,
\]

hence the fraction is at most one. `QED`

## Proposition 5.2 — local Walsh/L2 influence is strictly subcritical in tree uniqueness

Expand `F_d` in the Walsh basis under the uniform product measure on `{+/-1}^d`:

\[
F_d(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[d]}\widehat F_d(S)\chi_S(z),
\qquad
\chi_S(z)=\prod_{i\in S}z_i.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{S\subseteq[d]} |S|\,\widehat F_d(S)^2
=
\sum_{i=1}^d \|D_iF_d\|_2^2
\le d\theta^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For every internal vertex of `T_{b,h}`, where `d=b+1`, the target assumption `b theta <=1-delta` gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_S |S|\widehat F_{b+1}(S)^2
\le
(b+1)\theta^2
\le
\frac{b+1}{b^2}(1-\delta)^2
\le
\frac34(1-\delta)^2
<1.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For a directed cavity update with the parent spin held fixed and only the `b` children varying, the sharper uniform bound is

\[
\boxed{
q_{\rm cav}
\le b\theta^2
\le\frac{(1-\delta)^2}{b}
\le\frac12(1-\delta)^2<1.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

For Walsh characters,

\[
D_i\chi_S
=
\begin{cases}
\chi_{S\setminus\{i\}}, & i\in S,\\
0,&i\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus Parseval gives

\[
\|D_iF_d\|_2^2
=
\sum_{S\ni i}\widehat F_d(S)^2.
\]

Summing over `i` proves the identity in (5.2), and Proposition 5.1 gives the upper bound `d theta^2`. Substituting `theta <=(1-delta)/b` proves (5.3). For a directed cavity update, fixing the parent leaves exactly `b` variable child inputs; Proposition 5.1 is uniform in the fixed background field, so the same argument gives (5.4). `QED`

## Interpretation

This is the clean separation the selected tree action was looking for:

- the L1 budget can fail: `(b+1)theta>1` is possible inside uniqueness;
- the full local L2 budget remains bounded by at most `3/4(1-delta)^2`;
- the directed cavity L2 budget is even smaller, at most `(1-delta)^2/b`.

The quantity in (5.2) is a one-update **squared dependency energy**. It suggests replacing unweighted Information-Percolation ancestry by an L2/square-function history whose branches carry message derivatives rather than unit mass.

This mechanism is deliberately **not claimed as novel**. It is closely aligned with the classical variance-mixing/non-reconstruction technology on trees; in particular the well-known reconstruction threshold is governed by a squared-correlation parameter of Kesten--Stigum type. The new value for this track is diagnostic: it identifies an exact local contraction that remains strongly subcritical on the full selected uniqueness strip and therefore tells us what a dynamic cutoff proof would have to preserve.

# 6. Counterexample-first stress tests of the B12 quantity

### Alternating levels

If spins alternate sign by depth, every local background field `s` in Proposition 5.1 changes, but the bound `D_iF_d<=theta` is uniform in `s`. Therefore alternating levels do **not** break the one-update L2 budget.

### Mixed subtrees

The root has only `b` neighbors and leaves only one, so their local squared budgets are smaller than the internal `(b+1)theta^2` budget. Hence the local estimate does not fail at the geometric boundary.

### What does fail

The finite `T_{2,1}` certificate in Section 3 proves that local contraction cannot simply be tensorized by pretending the evolving neighbors remain independent. Even though equilibrium edge variables are independent, a single root update couples them instantly. More generally:

1. the Walsh orthogonality in (5.2) is exact under a product reference, but the actual non-equilibrium posterior inputs to later updates are correlated;
2. backwards branches can revisit the same spatial edge at earlier times and can merge, so distinct formal monomials need not remain orthogonal after conditioning;
3. the previously derived pinned-sector equation injects mass from neighboring pinnings, so one cannot apply the same independent-child argument separately in every pinning sector;
4. a global near-kernel for the auxiliary sampler could in principle be invisible to all one-update local estimates unless a square-function/martingale decomposition prevents it.

Therefore I do **not** promote

\[

from Proposition 5.2. That implication is precisely the missing theorem.
