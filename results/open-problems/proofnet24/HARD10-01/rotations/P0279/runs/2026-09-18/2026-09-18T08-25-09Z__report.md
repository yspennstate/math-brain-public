## 3. B13 — a genuine monovariant exists, but in the wrong norm

### 3.1 The obvious block-correlation defect cannot be monotone

A first natural attempt is to monitor inter-subtree covariance or conditional mutual information and hope it decreases after time zero. The already certified `T_{2,1}` calculation rules out such a raw monovariant.

For the all-plus start at `theta=1/4`, in the root/edge variables `eta_1,eta_2`,

\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_1(t),\eta_2(t))\right|_{t=0}
=\frac{18}{17}>0,
\]

while the equilibrium root/edge law is product and hence has covariance zero. Therefore the covariance defect starts at zero, becomes positive, and must later return to zero. Neither covariance, absolute covariance, nor its square can be a globally decreasing burn-in quantity. This kills the most literal B13 idea without imposing an illegitimate plus/minus extremality assumption.

### 3.2 Relative entropy gives an exact decreasing upper envelope for factorization defect

There is nevertheless a correct monovariant at a coarser information-theoretic level. Let a tree separator `S` split the remaining vertices into descendant components `C_1,...,C_m`. The equilibrium Gibbs measure has the exact Markov factorization

\[
\pi(dx_S,dx_{C_1},\ldots,dx_{C_m})
=\pi_S(dx_S)\prod_{j=1}^m\pi_j(dx_{C_j}\mid x_S).
\]

For any law `mu << pi`, the KL chain rule gives the exact decomposition

\[
D(\mu\|\pi)
= D(\mu_S\|\pi_S)
+ \mathbb E_{\mu_S}\!\left[
D\!\left(\mu_{C_1,\ldots,C_m\mid S}
\middle\|
\prod_j\pi_j(\cdot\mid S)
\right)
\right].
\]

Inside the conditional divergence, insert the product of the **actual** conditional marginals. A second KL chain rule gives

\[
D\!\left(\mu_{C_1,\ldots,C_m\mid S}
\middle\|
\prod_j\pi_j(\cdot\mid S)
\right)
=
I_\mu(C_1;\ldots;C_m\mid S)
+
\sum_j
D\!\left(\mu_{C_j\mid S}\|\pi_j(\cdot\mid S)\right),
\]

where `I_mu(C_1;...;C_m | S)` is conditional total correlation (multi-information). Hence

\[
\boxed{
I_\mu(C_1;\ldots;C_m\mid S)
\le D(\mu\|\pi).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

For the reversible Glauber semigroup, `D(mu_t^x || pi)` is nonincreasing in `t`. Thus relative entropy is a true burn-in monovariant that upper-bounds **average** failure of subtree factorization.

### 3.3 Why (3.1) does not close the spectral/variance problem

The VM/Pedrotti target is an `L^2` operator statement. Small mutual information does not control maximal correlation or a Poincare constant uniformly. The elementary rare-event example is decisive: let `A=B` be a Bernoulli(`p`) bit. Then

\[
I(A;B)=H(p)\longrightarrow0\quad(p\downarrow0),
\]

but the maximal correlation between `A` and `B` is exactly one because `A=B` deterministically. Thus an arbitrarily small average information defect can hide a perfect `L^2` mode on a rare event.

This is precisely the kind of mode that can destroy `gamma(mu_t)` because that constant takes an infimum over **all** test functions. Therefore entropy decay supplies a useful monotone envelope but not the norm needed to port VM. This is a concrete failure reason, not merely a missing estimate.

**B13 outcome:** PARTIAL NEGATIVE/POSITIVE. Raw covariance-type block defects are nonmonotone; relative entropy is a valid decreasing factorization envelope, but it is too weak to control the `L^2` operator norm required for local Poincare.

## 4. B25 — exact robust form of the MSW product step

The productive part of the run was to rewrite the static VM->PVM argument so that the missing non-equilibrium term appears explicitly.

### 4.1 Conditional-expectation projections and the static convexity step

Fix an arbitrary probability law `mu` on a finite spin space and subsets `A,B`. Let

\[
P_A f:=\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid\sigma_{V\setminus A}],
\qquad
P_B f:=\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid\sigma_{V\setminus B}].
\]

These are orthogonal projections on `L^2(mu)`. Integrated conditional variance is

\[
\mathbb E_\mu[\operatorname{Var}_A(f)]
=\|(I-P_A)f\|_{2,\mu}^2.
\]

The exact algebraic identity

\[
\boxed{
(I-P_A)P_B
=P_B(I-P_A)+[P_B,P_A]
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

immediately yields

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{Var}_A(P_Bf)}
\le
\sqrt{\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{Var}_A(f)}
+
\|[P_B,P_A]f\|_{2,\mu}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** Take `L^2(mu)` norms in (4.1), use the triangle inequality, and use that every orthogonal projection is a contraction:

\[
\|(I-P_A)P_Bf\|_2
\le \|P_B(I-P_A)f\|_2+\|[P_B,P_A]f\|_2
\le \|(I-P_A)f\|_2+\|[P_B,P_A]f\|_2.
\]

Squaring is unnecessary; (4.2) is already the useful form. QED.

For a tree Gibbs law and separated subtrees after conditioning on the separating spins, `P_A` and `P_B` commute. The commutator vanishes and (4.2) becomes exactly the static “convexity of variance” step used in the VM/block recursion. For the dynamic law `mu_t^x`, current-time conditioning does not screen off common past randomness, so the commutator is generally nonzero.

This gives a precise porting target:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\in\text{mixing window}}
\|[P_A^{(t)},P_B^{(t)}]\|_{2\to2}
\quad\text{must be controlled for separated tree blocks.}
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

A bounded/summable version of (4.3), together with the already available local squared influence contraction, would allow the MSW block recursion to tolerate non-equilibrium dependence. No previous P0279 route had isolated the failure in this operator form.

### 4.2 Exact latent-boundary-history mixture lemma

There is a second, more probabilistic formulation of the same defect.

Let `H` denote the complete spin trajectories on a separator during `[0,t]`. With a deterministic initial configuration, once `H` is fixed, the Poisson clocks/update randomness in distinct descendant components are independent. Hence terminal descendant components are **conditionally product given `H`**.

Abstract the situation as follows. Conditional on a latent variable `H=h`, let

\[
(X_1,Y_1),\ldots,(X_m,Y_m)
\]

be independent pairs. Think of `X_j` as the far terminal configuration in descendant subtree `j` and `Y_j` as the near/root terminal data used in a VM projection. Suppose every conditional pair channel has GVM coefficient at most `epsilon`, uniformly in `j,h`; equivalently, on mean-zero functions the local conditional-expectation operator has norm at most `sqrt(epsilon)`.

Let `X=(X_1,...,X_m)` and `Y=(Y_1,...,Y_m)`. Then for every square-integrable `f=f(X)`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y])
\le
\varepsilon\,\mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(f\mid H)]
+\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid H]).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

**Proof.** Since `sigma(Y) subset sigma(Y,H)`, conditional expectation is an orthogonal projection and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y])
\le
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y,H]).
\]

Apply total variance with respect to `H`:

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y,H])
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y,H]\mid H)
\right]
+
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid H]).
\]

For fixed `H=h`, the channel from `X` to `Y` is a tensor product of the component channels. Its nonconstant singular value is at most the largest component nonconstant singular value, so the product GVM coefficient is at most `epsilon`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y,H=h]\mid H=h)
\le \varepsilon\operatorname{Var}(f\mid H=h).
\]

Average over `h` to obtain (4.4). QED.

Using total variance for `f` itself, define the latent-history predictability ratio on a class `F` of far observables,

\[
r_{\mathcal F}(t)
:=
\sup_{f\in\mathcal F:\,\operatorname{Var}(f)>0}
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid H])}
{\operatorname{Var}(f)}.
\]

Then (4.4) implies

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[f\mid Y])
\le
\bigl[\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)r_{\mathcal F}(t)\bigr]
\operatorname{Var}(f).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equation (4.5) is the exact non-equilibrium analogue of the product step: static Gibbs factorization corresponds to having no hidden separator history after the boundary is fixed; dynamically, the only extra term is how well far terminal observables can reconstruct that hidden separator history.

This is not yet a tree cutoff theorem. It exposes two obligations:

1. a local GVM/VM estimate conditional on the realized separator trajectory, and
2. a mixing-window bound on the latent-history predictability `r_F(t)` (equivalently, a commutator/overlap bound).

The second term is exactly where Harris-history reconvergence enters. The first can itself be iterated down the tree, but only if the second remains controlled at each scale.

### 4.3 Relation to the prior squared budget

The previous run gives a pointwise local square-function budget

\]

For internal binary-tree vertices, `d=3`, so `3 theta^2 < 3/4`. **B25 outcome:** MATERIAL PARTIAL PROGRESS. The exact static product-factorization step has been replaced by two rigorous non-equilibrium formulas, (4.2) and (4.5). They isolate the missing quantity as an `L^2` commutator / latent-history predictability coefficient.

## 5. B28 — ordinary “typical low-overlap histories” is the wrong statement

The requested B28 move was to replace a pathwise all-history statement with a typical-history statement in the actual mixing window. The first thing to check is whether raw Harris histories are typically small or low-overlap in the selected non-Dobrushin strip.

### 5.1 The standard unweighted backward exploration is supercritical here

For an internal binary-tree vertex the degree is three. The non-oblivious probability is therefore

\[
q=\tanh(3\beta)
=\frac{3\theta+\theta^3}{1+3\theta^2}.

A crude backwards Information-Percolation exploration branches to three neighbors on a non-oblivious update, with mean offspring `3q`. Across the selected strip,

\[

\qquad
q(1/2)=\frac{13}{14},
\]

so

\[
3q\in\left(\frac73,\frac{39}{14}\right),
\]

far above one. Thus the ordinary unweighted history is not in a subcritical branching regime anywhere in this test strip. Asking that most raw histories be small/low-overlap is therefore not a plausible port of Information Percolation; it simply recreates the high-temperature obstruction already identified by Salez.

### 5.2 The correct “typical” object must be weighted by squared influence

The same strip has

\[
3\theta^2<\frac34<1.
\]

Hence raw path count can proliferate while **squared influence mass** contracts. This suggests replacing history size by an overlap-weighted second moment.

If a base update coin `a` can influence the target through causal paths `p`, assign each path a multiplicative influence weight `w_p` (bounded locally by products of one-edge factors at most `theta`). The diagonal square-function mass is

\[
D:=\sum_a\sum_{p\ni a}w_p^2.
\]

The reconvergence obstruction is the off-diagonal collision mass

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{Coll}:=
\sum_a
\left[
\left(\sum_{p\ni a}w_p\right)^2
-
\sum_{p\ni a}w_p^2
\right]
=
2\sum_a\sum_{p<q:\,a\in p\cap q}w_pw_q.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The local `3 theta^2<1` calculation controls the diagonal term generated by one layer. It does **not** control (5.1). Equation (5.1) is precisely the cross-term seen abstractly in the commutator/latent-history formulations above.

A successful B28 theorem would therefore not say “with high probability the Harris cluster is small.” It would say that, in the Pedrotti--Salez mixing window, the weighted collision amplification

\[
\frac{D+\mathrm{Coll}}{D}
\]

is uniformly bounded (or grows slowly enough that a sufficiently deep spatial block, whose static factor is roughly `(2 theta^2)^ell`, still contracts). A constant overlap amplification would already be enough in principle because `ell` can be chosen so that the static/local factor is arbitrarily small.

I do **not** have a proof of such a weighted-overlap estimate. Temporal backtracking can generate many causal paths sharing the same old coin, and no current P0279 argument bounds the pair sum in (5.1). This is now the exact obstruction for the selected port.

**B28 outcome:** FAILED in its naive unweighted form, but it identifies the correct weighted target. Typical raw low-overlap is incompatible with the supercritical unweighted exploration in this regime; a weighted second-moment collision theorem is required instead.
