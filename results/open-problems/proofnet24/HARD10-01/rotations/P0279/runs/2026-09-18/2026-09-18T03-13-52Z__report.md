# 3. B11 — DUALIZE: dynamic approximate tensorization is exactly an auxiliary spectral-gap problem

This is the cleanest exact structural outcome of the run.

Let `mu` be any strictly positive law on `{−1,+1}^V`. Define the auxiliary single-site heat-bath generator `H_mu` whose stationary law is `mu` by

\[
(H_\mu f)(\sigma)
=
\sum_{i\in V}
q_i^\mu(\sigma)
\bigl(f(\sigma^i)-f(\sigma)\bigr),
\qquad
q_i^\mu(\sigma)
=
\frac{\mu(\sigma^i)}{\mu(\sigma)+\mu(\sigma^i)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Here `sigma^i` flips spin `i`. This is exactly the Gibbs sampler for the artificial Gibbs law `mu`, whether or not `mu` itself has pairwise Ising form.

## 3.1 Exact Dirichlet-form identity

**Lemma 3.1.** For every `f`,

\[
\boxed{
-\langle f,H_\mu f\rangle_\mu
=
\sum_{i\in V}
\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{Var}_\mu
(f\mid X_{V\setminus\{i\}}).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.** Fix coordinate `i` and a boundary configuration `eta` of all other coordinates. Write

\[
a=\mu(\eta,+),\qquad b=\mu(\eta,-),
\qquad f_+=f(\eta,+),\quad f_-=f(\eta,-).
\]

The unordered flip edge `{(eta,+),(eta,-)}` has conductance

\[
a\frac{b}{a+b}
=b\frac{a}{a+b}
=\frac{ab}{a+b}.
\]

Consequently its contribution to the reversible Dirichlet form is

\[
\frac{ab}{a+b}(f_+-f_-)^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

But the same boundary fiber contributes to the conditional-variance expression

\[
(a+b)
\left(\frac{a}{a+b}\right)
\left(\frac{b}{a+b}\right)
(f_+-f_-)^2
=
\frac{ab}{a+b}(f_+-f_-)^2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Sum `(3.3)=(3.4)` over boundary fibers and coordinates. `square`

It follows immediately that

\[
\boxed{
C_{\rm AT}(\mu)
=
\frac{1}{\operatorname{gap}(H_\mu)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the non-equilibrium bridge can be restated without approximate-tensorization terminology:

> **Dynamic auxiliary-gap problem.** Given `mu_t^x = delta_x P_t` for the original Ising Gibbs sampler, lower-bound the spectral gap of the *auxiliary heat-bath chain whose invariant law is the current time marginal* `mu_t^x`.

At equilibrium, `H_pi` is exactly the original Gibbs sampler. Hence the strongest natural comparison is

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^x})
\stackrel{?}{\ge}
\operatorname{gap}(H_\pi)
\qquad(t>0).
}
\tag{DG}
\]

If `(DG)` held in the source Ising class, then every equilibrium `O(1)` relaxation-gap estimate in the rapid-mixing regime would transfer automatically to every non-equilibrium marginal, and `(2.1)` plus Pedrotti-Salez would produce a uniformly narrow mixing window. This is a **candidate route**, not a theorem.

## 3.2 The local-score deformation

Write

\[
\mu_t(\sigma)=\pi(\sigma)h_t(\sigma).
\]

Reversibility of the original generator gives

\[
\partial_t h_t=Lh_t.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Define the local log-density score

\[
g_i(t,\sigma)
=
\log\frac{h_t(\sigma^i)}{h_t(\sigma)}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Then the auxiliary heat-bath rate is the exact local deformation

\[
q_i^{\mu_t}(\sigma)
=
\frac{\pi(\sigma^i)h_t(\sigma^i)}
{\pi(\sigma)h_t(\sigma)+\pi(\sigma^i)h_t(\sigma^i)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Moreover

\[
\partial_t\log h_t(\sigma)
=
\frac{Lh_t(\sigma)}{h_t(\sigma)}
=
\sum_j c_j(\sigma)
\bigl(e^{g_j(t,\sigma)}-1\bigr),
\tag{3.9}
\]

so subtracting `(3.9)` at `sigma` and `sigma^i` yields the exact score evolution

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t g_i(t,\sigma)
=
\sum_j
\left[
 c_j(\sigma^i)
 \bigl(e^{g_j(t,\sigma^i)}-1\bigr)
-
 c_j(\sigma)
 \bigl(e^{g_j(t,\sigma)}-1\bigr)
\right].
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

Equation `(3.10)` exposes the main difficulty of a naive gradient argument. Even when `j` is neither `i` nor adjacent to `i`, so that the original Ising rate satisfies `c_j(sigma^i)=c_j(sigma)`, the difference still contains

\[
e^{g_j(t,\sigma^i)}-e^{g_j(t,\sigma)},
\]

a **second discrete derivative of `log h_t`**. First-order local scores do not close. Positive-curvature methods control precisely such gradient commutators by absolute contraction; outside that regime, a tree/cavity/nonbacktracking norm must retain cancellation instead of replacing `(3.10)` by a Dobrushin row-sum estimate.

This gives a precise version of the non-equilibrium obstruction rather than the vague statement “spectral independence is only equilibrium.”

# 4. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: test `(DG)` at the smallest exact source system

Before attempting `(DG)` generally, this run attacked it at the smallest nontrivial zero-field ferromagnetic Ising system. No exact counterexample exists at two spins; in fact the stronger comparison is true there for every inverse temperature and every deterministic start.

## 4.1 Two-spin exact theorem

Let `G` be one edge with spins `sigma_1,sigma_2 in {−1,+1}` and

\[
\pi(\sigma)
\propto
\exp\{\beta\sigma_1\sigma_2\},
\qquad \beta\ge0.
\]

Set

\[
\theta=\tanh\beta\in[0,1).
\]

Let `mu_t^x` be the law of the continuous-time heat-bath dynamics from any deterministic configuration `x`.

**Theorem 4.1.** For every `t>0`,

\[
\boxed{
C_{\rm AT}(\mu_t^x)
\le C_{\rm AT}(\pi)
=
\frac1{1-\theta},
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^x})
\ge
\operatorname{gap}(H_\pi)
=1-\theta.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is a parameter-uniform symbolic theorem, not a finite enumeration; the proof below is exact and no numerical checker is used.

### Step 1: the two-variable AT constant

For any strictly positive binary pair `(X_1,X_2)`, let `rho` be its Pearson correlation coefficient. The centered subspaces of functions of `X_1` and of `X_2` are each one-dimensional. The two conditional-expectation projections therefore meet at angle with cosine `|rho|`. Since

\[
-H_\mu=(I-P_1)+(I-P_2),
\]

where `P_1 f=E[f|X_2]` and `P_2 f=E[f|X_1]`, the nonzero eigenvalues on the span of those two centered one-coordinate functions are `1-|rho|` and `1+|rho|`; the remaining interaction direction has eigenvalue `2`. Hence

\[
\operatorname{gap}(H_\mu)=1-|\rho|,
\qquad
C_{\rm AT}(\mu)=\frac1{1-|\rho|}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

At equilibrium `rho_pi=theta`, proving the last equality in `(4.1)`.

### Step 2: aligned deterministic start

By global spin-flip symmetry, it suffices to start from `++`. Put

\[
m(t)=\mathbb E[\sigma_1]=\mathbb E[\sigma_2],
\qquad
q(t)=\mathbb E[\sigma_1\sigma_2].
\]

The heat-bath generator gives

\[
\dot m=-(1-\theta)m,
\qquad
\dot q=-2q+2\theta,
\]

with `m(0)=q(0)=1`. Therefore

\[
m(t)=e^{-(1-\theta)t},
\qquad
q(t)=\theta+(1-\theta)e^{-2t}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The correlation coefficient is

\[
\rho_t
=
\frac{q(t)-m(t)^2}{1-m(t)^2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

First, `rho_t<=theta` is equivalent to

\[
(1-\theta)e^{-2t}
\le
(1-\theta)e^{-2(1-\theta)t},
\]
which is true because `1-theta<=1`.

Second, `rho_t>=0` is equivalent, after setting `s=e^{-2t} in (0,1)`, to

\[
\theta+(1-\theta)s
\ge s^{1-\theta},
\tag{4.6}
\]
which is the weighted AM-GM inequality applied to `1` and `s` with weights `theta` and `1-theta`.

Thus

\[
0\le\rho_t\le\theta.
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Step 3: anti-aligned deterministic start

Now start from `+-`; the other anti-aligned state follows by symmetry. Write

\[
\mathbb E\sigma_1=-\mathbb E\sigma_2=m(t).
\]

Then

\[
m(t)=e^{-(1+\theta)t},
\qquad
q(t)=\theta-(1+\theta)e^{-2t},
\tag{4.8}
\]

and

\[
\rho_t
=
\frac{q(t)+m(t)^2}{1-m(t)^2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

The upper bound `rho_t<=theta` reduces to

\[
e^{-2(1+\theta)t}\le e^{-2t},
\]
which is immediate. For the lower bound set again `s=e^{-2t}`. Then `rho_t>=0` is equivalent to

\[
F(s):=\theta-(1+\theta)s+s^{1+\theta}\ge0.
\tag{4.10}
\]

But `F(1)=0` and

\[
F'(s)=(1+\theta)(s^\theta-1)\le0
\qquad(0<s\le1),
\]
so `F(s)>=F(1)=0`. Hence `(4.7)` again holds.

Combining `(4.3)` and `(4.7)` proves `(4.1)-(4.2)`. `square`

# 5. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: pinned time marginals have an exact boundary-injection term

Equilibrium spectral independence is defined robustly under pinnings. A natural algorithmic plan is therefore:

1. evolve `mu_t`;
2. pin arbitrary coordinates;
3. propagate an influence bound for every pinned law;
4. infer spectral independence / approximate tensorization for `mu_t`.

The precise failure is that **conditioning does not commute with the Glauber evolution**. The conditioned time marginal is not a closed Glauber chain on the unpinned coordinates. The missing terms can be written exactly.

Let

\[
A=A_{S,\tau}
=\{\sigma:\sigma_S=\tau\}
\]

be a pinning event and write

\[
\nu_t^A(g)
=
\mathbb E_{\mu_t}[g\mid A].
\]

Assume `g` depends only on the unpinned coordinates. For `i in S`, let `A^i` be the neighboring pinning sector obtained by flipping the prescribed spin at `i`, and put

\[
r_i(t)=\frac{\mu_t(A^i)}{\mu_t(A)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Using the forward equation and differentiating a quotient gives

\[
\frac d{dt}\nu_t^A(g)
=
\frac{\mu_t(L(g1_A))}{\mu_t(A)}
-
\nu_t^A(g)
\frac{\mu_t(L1_A)}{\mu_t(A)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Flips outside `S` preserve `A`; flips at a pinned coordinate transfer mass between `A` and `A^i`. A direct split yields

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\nu_t^A(g)
={}&\nu_t^A(L_{S^c}g)\\
&+\sum_{i\in S}
\Bigl[
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_t^A}(c_i,g)
+r_i(t)\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_t^{A^i}}(c_i,g)\\
&\hspace{30mm}
+r_i(t)\nu_t^{A^i}(c_i)
\bigl(\nu_t^{A^i}(g)-\nu_t^A(g)\bigr)
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Derivation of the pinned-coordinate term

For `sigma in A`, the `i`-flip exits `A`, so

\[
L_i(g1_A)(\sigma)=-c_i(\sigma)g(\sigma),
\qquad
L_i1_A(\sigma)=-c_i(\sigma).
\]

For `sigma in A^i`, the flip enters `A`, and because `g` ignores the pinned coordinate,

\[
L_i(g1_A)(\sigma)=c_i(\sigma)g(\sigma),
\qquad
L_i1_A(\sigma)=c_i(\sigma).
\]

All other pinning sectors contribute zero to coordinate `i`. Substitution into `(5.2)` gives first

\[
-\nu_A(c_i g)
+r_i\nu_{A^i}(c_i g)
-\nu_A(g)
\bigl[-\nu_A(c_i)+r_i\nu_{A^i}(c_i)\bigr],
\]

which rearranges to the summand in `(5.3)`.

Equation `(5.3)` isolates the exact non-equilibrium term absent from equilibrium SI arguments:

\[
\boxed{
 r_i(t)\nu_t^{A^i}(c_i)
 \bigl(\nu_t^{A^i}(g)-\nu_t^A(g)\bigr).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

It is a **boundary injection between neighboring pinning sectors**. Even if `L_{S^c}` contracts influence inside each pinned sector, `(5.4)` continually imports discrepancies from adjacent sectors. A proof of full dynamic spectral independence therefore needs a norm or Lyapunov function controlling the entire network of pinned sectors simultaneously, not a separate equilibrium estimate on each pinning.

## 5.1 A tempting positivity shortcut is unavailable

For ferromagnetic Ising one might hope to declare every `mu_t` log-supermodular/MTP2 and use positivity to control `(5.4)`. The literature check above rules out this shortcut in that generality. Harris's theorem gives preservation of **association** under attractive spin systems, but Liggett's 2005 analysis shows that preservation of the stronger FKG lattice condition is essentially special to independent flips. Thus the dynamic time marginal has useful monotonicity, but not automatically the full conditional lattice structure used by equilibrium ferromagnetic Ising arguments.

This distinction is important: association controls signs of unconditioned increasing observables, while Problem 2.8 needs a uniform mechanism robust under the pinnings that enter spectral independence.
