# 3. B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: exact symmetry exists, but all-plus extremality is false

## 3.1 What global spin flip really gives

Let `J sigma = -sigma`. In the zero-field ferromagnetic Ising model,

\[
\pi(J\sigma)=\pi(\sigma)
\]

and the heat-bath flip rates satisfy

\[
c_i(J\sigma)=c_i(\sigma).
\]

Hence the original Glauber semigroup commutes with `J`. For deterministic initial state `x`,

\[
\mu_t^{-x}=J_*\mu_t^x.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The auxiliary heat-bath chains are conjugate:

\[
H_{J_*\mu}=U_J H_\mu U_J^{-1},
\qquad
(U_J f)(\sigma)=f(J\sigma),
\tag{3.2}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^{-x}})
=
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^x}).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Graph automorphisms give the same conjugacy. Thus starts can be quotiented by `Aut(G) x {global spin flip}`.

What does **not** follow is that the worst start is all-plus/all-minus. The gap functional is not monotone under stochastic domination. The next two sections produce an exact short-time perturbation formula and use it to refute that stronger reduction already on `K_4` strictly inside both tree uniqueness and the Dobrushin high-temperature region.

# 4. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE: exact short-time auxiliary-gap perturbation

This section differentiates the dynamic auxiliary gap at deterministic initialization. It is the main structural result of the run.

Let the original continuous-time single-site heat-bath generator on `{−1,+1}^V` be

\[
(Lf)(\sigma)=\sum_i c_i(\sigma)
\bigl(f(\sigma^i)-f(\sigma)\bigr),
\tag{4.1}
\]

with all rates positive. Fix a deterministic start `x`, put

\[
\mu_t=\delta_x e^{tL},
\]

and let `H_t:=H_{\mu_t}` be the auxiliary heat-bath generator of the current law.

Write

\[
c_i:=c_i(x),
\qquad
c_j^i:=c_j(x^i).
\]

For `i != j`, define

\[
\boxed{
M_{ij}
=
\frac12\left(
 c_j^i+c_j\frac{c_i^j}{c_i}
\right)-c_j,
\qquad
M_{ii}=0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — first derivative of the dynamic auxiliary gap

As `t downarrow 0`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t})
=
1-t\,\lambda_{\max}(M_x)+O(t^2).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Moreover `M_x` is diagonally similar to a real symmetric matrix:

\[
\boxed{
c_i M_{ij}=c_j M_{ji}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently all eigenvalues of `M_x` are real. Since `tr(M_x)=0`,

\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_x)\ge0,
\tag{4.5}
\]

with strict inequality whenever `M_x != 0`. Thus the auxiliary gap cannot initially increase away from its deterministic-start limiting value `1`; the first-order deterioration is the static spectral quantity `lambda_max(M_x)`.

### Proof

Let `d(y,x)` be Hamming distance. The semigroup expansion gives

\[
\mu_t(x)=1+O(t),
\tag{4.6}
\]

\[
\mu_t(x^i)=c_i t+O(t^2),
\tag{4.7}
\]

and for distinct `i,j`,

\[
\mu_t(x^{ij})
=
\frac{t^2}{2}
\left[c_i c_j^i+c_j c_i^j\right]
+O(t^3).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Indeed the only length-two paths from `x` to `x^{ij}` flip `i` then `j`, or `j` then `i`.

The auxiliary heat-bath rate is

\[
q_k^t(\sigma)
=
\frac{\mu_t(\sigma^k)}
{\mu_t(\sigma)+\mu_t(\sigma^k)}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

At `x`, `(4.7)` yields

\[
q_j^t(x)=c_j t+O(t^2).
\tag{4.10}
\]

At `x^i`, for `j != i`, combining `(4.7)-(4.8)` gives

\[
q_j^t(x^i)
=
\frac t2
\left[
 c_j^i+c_j\frac{c_i^j}{c_i}
\right]
+O(t^2).
\tag{4.11}
\]

For the inward flip,

\[
q_i^t(x^i)=1-c_i t+O(t^2).
\tag{4.12}
\]

As `t downarrow0`, `H_t` therefore converges to the pure-death-to-`x` generator

\[
(H_0f)(\sigma)
=
\sum_{i:\sigma_i\ne x_i}
\bigl(f(\sigma^i)-f(\sigma)\bigr).
\tag{4.13}
\]

Its eigenvalues are `0,-1,...,-|V|`. The eigenvalue `-1` is semisimple with right eigenbasis

\[
f_j(\sigma)=1_{\{\sigma_j\ne x_j\}},
\tag{4.14}
\]

and dual left eigenfunctionals

\[
\ell_i(g)=g(x^i)-g(x),
\qquad
\ell_i(f_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Every auxiliary rate along a hypercube edge has an analytic extension to `t=0`: the two endpoints have Hamming distances differing by one, so the common leading power of `t` factors out of `(4.9)`. Hence standard finite-dimensional semisimple perturbation applies to the `-1` cluster.

The first-order splitting matrix is

\[
\ell_i(H_1 f_j).
\]

For `i=j`, `(4.10)` and `(4.12)` give the same first-order coefficient `c_i` at `x` and `x^i`, hence `M_{ii}=0`. For `i != j`, `(4.10)-(4.11)` give exactly `(4.2)`. Therefore the eigenvalues near `-1` are

\[
-1+t\lambda_k(M_x)+O(t^2).
\tag{4.16}
\]

The spectral-gap eigenvalue is the member of this cluster closest to zero, giving `(4.3)`.

Finally multiply `(4.2)` by `c_i`:

\[
2c_iM_{ij}
=
 c_i c_j^i+c_j c_i^j-2c_ic_j,
\tag{4.17}
\]

whose right side is symmetric in `i,j`. Thus `(4.4)` holds. If `D=diag(sqrt(c_i))`, then `D M D^{-1}` is symmetric. This proves real spectrum, and `(4.5)` follows from zero trace. `square`

## 4.2 Ising locality and the signed spectral obstruction

For Ising heat-bath rates, if `i` and `j` are non-neighbors then flipping `i` does not change `c_j` and flipping `j` does not change `c_i`; hence

\[
M_{ij}=0
\qquad\text{when }ij\notin E(G).
\tag{4.18}
\]

Thus the infinitesimal deterioration of the dynamic auxiliary gap is governed by a **signed weighted adjacency operator on the original graph**. The signs depend on the start `x`.

At very high temperature one has, for edges,

\[
M_{ij}=\beta x_i x_j+O(\beta^2),
\tag{4.19}
\]

so to first order in `beta`, `M_x` is gauge-conjugate to `beta A_G` and its spectrum is start-independent. Start dependence is therefore a genuinely nonlinear interaction effect, not visible in the linearized high-temperature influence matrix.

# 5. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: the all-plus worst-start principle fails exactly on K4

For a fixed finite graph, Theorem 4.1 turns the short-time worst-initialization problem into

\[
\boxed{
\max_{x\in\{\pm1\}^V}\lambda_{\max}(M_x).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a static finite signed-spectral extremal problem. It immediately gives a counterexample to the tempting B14 claim that attractiveness reduces the minimum dynamic auxiliary gap to all-plus/all-minus starts.

Take `G=K_4`, and write

\[
\theta=\tanh\beta\in(0,1),
\qquad
T=\tanh(3\beta)
=
\frac{3\theta+\theta^3}{1+3\theta^2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

## 5.1 All-plus start

At `x=++++`, every site has local alignment `3`, so

\[
c_i=\frac{1-T}{2}.
\]

After flipping one neighboring spin, the local alignment at another site becomes `1`, so its rate is `(1-\theta)/2`. Therefore every off-diagonal entry of `M_+` is

\[
a:=\frac{T-\theta}{2}>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_+)=3a.
\tag{5.4}
\]

## 5.2 Balanced `++--` start

At a balanced start, each spin has one same-sign and two opposite-sign neighbors, so every initial flip rate is

\[
\frac{1+\theta}{2}.
\]

For a same-sign pair, flipping one endpoint changes the other's local alignment from `-1` to `-3`, giving

\[
M_{ij}=a.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For an opposite-sign pair, the local alignment changes from `-1` to `+1`, giving

\[
M_{ij}=-\theta.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus, in the ordering `++--`, the vector `(1,1,-1,-1)` is an eigenvector with eigenvalue

\[
\lambda_{\rm bal}=a+2\theta.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The other eigenvalues are `a-2\theta,-a,-a`, so `(5.7)` is the maximum.

Compare `(5.4)` and `(5.7)`:

\[
\lambda_{\rm bal}-\lambda_{+}
=2\theta-2a
=3\theta-T
=
\boxed{
\frac{8\theta^3}{1+3\theta^2}>0.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Therefore Theorem 4.1 yields

\[
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^{++--}})
<
\operatorname{gap}(H_{\mu_t^{++++}})
\tag{5.9}
\]

for all sufficiently small `t>0`, at **every** `beta>0`.

So global spin-flip symmetry is valid, but the stronger all-plus/all-minus worst-start reduction is false.

This failure occurs even in a very safe source subregime. Choose exactly

\[
\theta=\frac14.
\]

Then `Delta(K_4)=3`,

\[
(\Delta-1)\theta=\frac12<1
\]

(strict tree uniqueness) and even

\[
\Delta\theta=\frac34<1
\]

(strict Dobrushin row-sum regime). Here

\[
T=\frac{49}{76},
\qquad
a=\frac{15}{76},
\]

and exactly

\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_+)=\frac{45}{76},
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(M_{++--})=\frac{53}{76}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

The short-time deterioration differs by

\[
\frac{53-45}{76}=\frac{2}{19}.
\tag{5.11}
\]
