\inf_h\ \inf_{x\in\{\pm1\}^{V(T_{b,h})}}
\ \inf_{t\in[t_{\rm mix}^x(1-\varepsilon),\,t_{\rm mix}^x(\varepsilon)]}
\gamma(\mu_{h,t}^x)
\;>\;0.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

\[

would suffice. Since the selected strict-uniqueness tree regime has logarithmic mixing scale in the rate-one-per-site normalization, even a slowly vanishing window-local bound can be enough.

# 4. B17 — the number `b theta^2` is not accidental

The local P0279 square budget

\[
b\theta^2
\]

is precisely the squared-correlation scale that appears in tree information flow. For a binary symmetric broadcast edge, the nontrivial channel eigenvalue is `theta`; on an independent `b`-ary broadcast tree, Kesten--Stigum/census information is governed by `b theta^2`.

In the present fixed-slack uniqueness regime,

\[
b\theta\le 1-\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
b\theta^2\le \frac{(1-\delta)^2}{b}<1,
\]

so the local L2 budget is not merely subcritical but has substantial slack. This strongly suggests that the remaining obstruction is **not** local amplification.

The nearest rigorous static analogue is Martinelli--Sinclair--Weitz **Variance Mixing**: point-to-set information from a distant boundary to the root contracts in an L2/variance sense, and block recursion converts this static spatial contraction into equilibrium spectral-gap/log-Sobolev bounds. Blanca--Chen--Stefankovic--Vigoda later give a spectral view of the same Variance Mixing mechanism for tree dynamics.

What fails to match P0279 is exact and important:

- Kesten--Stigum/SDPI broadcast arguments use independent edge channels on a static tree;
- Variance Mixing uses the spatial Gibbs Markov property, so conditioned descendant subtrees factorize in exactly the way required by the block recursion;
- the actual `mu_t^x` is neither a static Gibbs law nor an independent broadcast law;
- conditioning/pinning the time marginal introduces boundary-injection between sectors;
- the Harris dependency object is a space-time DAG in which branches can revisit the same spatial vertex and reconverge onto shared past randomness.

Thus the local `b theta^2` calculation matches a known successful **static** mechanism, but the missing theorem is a dynamic transfer principle, not another numerical threshold.

**B17 outcome:** positive diagnostic progress, no theorem. The right nearest technique is Variance Mixing / block recursion, but its subtree-factorization hypothesis must be replaced rather than silently assumed.

# 5. B10 — derandomizing onto a fixed Harris realization exposes the exact cross-term obstruction

Condition on the graphical update times/locations and expose only the independent heat-bath update coins in chronological order. Any terminal observable `f(X_t)` then has a Doob martingale with orthogonal increments at the level of the independent base coins. This is the most plausible way to exploit the already-found square budget without pretending the evolving spin law is product.

The desired pathwise proof would assign an L2 influence/energy to every base coin and show that when influences pass through one heat-bath update, their predictable quadratic variation contracts by a factor controlled by the local `d theta^2` or directed `b theta^2` budget.

The attempt fails at **reconvergence**. Two neighbor messages entering a later update can depend on the same earlier coin because their Harris ancestries have merged or revisited a vertex. The static Walsh inequality controls a function of independent coordinate inputs; it does not by itself control the cross term created when several inputs are correlated functions of the same base coin. Orthogonality of the original Doob increments does not automatically survive after those increments are duplicated into several branches and subsequently recombined by a nonlinear heat-bath map.

Equivalently, one-site means are not a closed state descriptor for the dynamic posterior: one must carry enough covariance/higher-order information to control shared ancestors. This is the exact point where a naive product-of-Jacobians argument ceases to be valid.

A successful derandomized proof would therefore need a new lemma of the following **shape** (not claimed here): a coalescing-DAG Efron--Stein/SDPI inequality saying that predictable quadratic variation cannot increase through a heat-bath node by more than a strict factor even when its incoming messages share past base randomness, with a defect term that remains summable under repeated reconvergence.

**B10 outcome:** the fixed-Harris martingale language is promising, but the unsupplied ingredient is an overlap-stable square-function inequality at reconvergence. No existing route in this track contains it.

# 6. B27 — swap the quantifiers: the all-time dynamic-gap target was unnecessarily strong

Pedrotti--Salez Remark 2 changes the final route materially. Their proof does **not** require

\[
\inf_{t>0}\gamma(\mu_t)>0.
\]

For a fixed initial law/start and fixed `epsilon`, it is enough to control `gamma(mu_t)` on the interval during which total variation falls from `1-epsilon` to `epsilon`:

\[
\gamma_\varepsilon
=\gamma\wedge
\inf_{t\in[t_{\rm mix}(1-\varepsilon),\,t_{\rm mix}(\varepsilon)]}
\gamma(\mu_t).
\]

This has two consequences for P0279.

1. The short-time `K_4` deterioration and other early-time non-product pathologies may be irrelevant if sufficient burn-in occurs before the mixing window.
