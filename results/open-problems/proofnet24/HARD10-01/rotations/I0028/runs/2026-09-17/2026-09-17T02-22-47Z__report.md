## 3. Definitions: positive two-terminal excursions and coverage tables

Let `H=(V,E;s,t)` be a finite connected simple graph with ordered terminals `s,t`.

A **positive terminal excursion** starting at terminal `x in {s,t}` is the random walk segment obtained by taking at least one step and stopping at the first subsequent hit of `{s,t}`. Its type is `xy` if it starts at `x` and ends at `y`. Write

\[
p_H^{xy}=\Pr_x(X_{\tau^+_{\{s,t\}}}=y)
\]

for the type probability and `nu_H^{xy}` for the conditional path law given type `xy`.

For a nonnegative integer count vector

\[
\mathbf n=(n_{ss},n_{st},n_{ts},n_{tt}),
\]

define

\[
\mathcal C_H(\mathbf n)
\]

to be the probability that a collection of independent conditional excursions, with exactly `n_xy` excursions sampled from `nu_H^{xy}` for each ordered type `xy`, collectively visits every interior vertex `V\setminus\{s,t\}`.

This is a local coverage table. It does **not** assert that arbitrary count vectors can occur in one physical global walk; feasibility imposes the usual boundary-flow constraints. The point is that once a feasible outer skeleton specifies the type multiset, the strong Markov property makes these conditional excursion paths independent.

## 4. B4 — exact series-composition theorem

Let

- `H_1` have terminals `(L,M)`,
- `H_2` have terminals `(M,R)`,

with disjoint interiors and common vertex only `M`. Let

\[
H=H_1\odot H_2.
\]

Write

\[
d_1=\deg_{H_1}(M),\qquad
d_2=\deg_{H_2}(M),\qquad
\alpha=\frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2},\qquad
\beta=\frac{d_2}{d_1+d_2}.
\]

Introduce formal markers `z_{1,xy}` and `z_{2,xy}` for the local excursion types in the two children and abbreviate

\[
A=p_1^{LL}z_{1,LL},\qquad
B=p_1^{LM}z_{1,LM},
\]

\[
C=\alpha p_1^{ML}z_{1,ML},\qquad
U=\alpha p_1^{MM}z_{1,MM},
\]

\[
D=p_2^{RR}z_{2,RR},\qquad
E=p_2^{RM}z_{2,RM},
\]

\[
F=\beta p_2^{MR}z_{2,MR},\qquad
V=\beta p_2^{MM}z_{2,MM},
\]

and

\[
W=U+V.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact outer-excursion generating functions

The joint probability generating functions of local excursion-type counts inside one positive outer excursion of `H`, with outer terminals `(L,R)`, are

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{LL}=A+B(1-W)^{-1}C,
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{LR}=B(1-W)^{-1}F,
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{RR}=D+E(1-W)^{-1}F,
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{RL}=E(1-W)^{-1}C.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Here `(1-W)^{-1}` is interpreted as the formal power series

\[
1+W+W^2+\cdots.
\]

#### Proof

Consider an outer excursion starting at `L`.

If it returns to `L` without ever reaching `M`, it consists of one `H_1` excursion of type `LL`, giving the direct term `A`.

Otherwise its first local excursion is an `H_1` excursion `L->M`, giving `B`. Once at `M`, every next local positive excursion is determined by the first edge from `M`:

- with probability `alpha` it enters `H_1`; conditional on doing so, the child excursion is either `M->M` or `M->L`;
- with probability `beta` it enters `H_2`; conditional on doing so, the child excursion is either `M->M` or `M->R`.

Therefore an `M->M` loop has marked total weight

\[
W=\alpha p_1^{MM}z_{1,MM}+\beta p_2^{MM}z_{2,MM},
\]

while escape to `L` has marked weight `C` and escape to `R` has marked weight `F`.

An arbitrary number `j>=0` of independent `M->M` loops can occur before escape. Summing

\[
B W^j C
\]

over `j` gives (4.1), while summing

\[
B W^j F
\]

gives (4.2). The `R`-started formulas are identical with left and right exchanged, yielding (4.3) and (4.4). This exhausts all possible outer positive excursions. ∎

Setting every marker equal to 1 gives the ordinary outer type probabilities

\[
p_H^{xy}=\Phi_{xy}(\mathbf 1).
\]

### Corollary 4.2 — exact local-count law for many prescribed outer excursions

Let the feasible outer type-count vector be

\[
\mathbf n=(n_{LL},n_{LR},n_{RL},n_{RR}).
\]

Conditional on these outer types, the probability generating function of the aggregate local child-type count vectors is

\[
\boxed{
G_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)
=
\prod_{xy}
\left(
\frac{\Phi_{xy}(\mathbf z_1,\mathbf z_2)}
     {p_H^{xy}}
\right)^{n_{xy}},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where factors with `p_H^{xy}=0` are omitted and the prescribed count must then be zero.

Let

\[
g_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)
=
[\mathbf z_1^{\mathbf a}\mathbf z_2^{\mathbf b}]
G_{\mathbf n}.
\]

Then the exact coverage recurrence is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_H(\mathbf n)
=
\sum_{\mathbf a,\mathbf b}
g_{\mathbf n}(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\,
\mathcal C_{H_1}(\mathbf a)\,
\mathcal C_{H_2}(\mathbf b)\,
\mathbf 1_{\{M\ {\rm visited}\}}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The shared terminal `M` is visited exactly when at least one local excursion touches it; in count notation this is

\[
a_{LM}+a_{ML}+a_{MM}
+b_{RM}+b_{MR}+b_{MM}>0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

Successive positive outer excursions are independent conditional on their ordered outer types by the strong Markov property at the outer boundary. Hence the aggregate local-count PGF is the product (4.5).

Now condition further on the realized local type counts `(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)`. All local child excursions are independent conditional on their types. The event that every strict interior vertex of `H_1` is covered depends only on the `H_1` excursion collection and therefore has probability `C_{H_1}(\mathbf a)`. The analogous event for `H_2` has probability `C_{H_2}(\mathbf b)`. These two events are independent because their random paths live in edge-disjoint child interiors and the conditional child excursion collections are independent. The only interior vertex of `H` omitted from both child interiors is `M`, so we multiply by the indicator that `M` was visited. Averaging over the coefficient law `g_{\mathbf n}` proves (4.6). ∎

This proves the **exact finite-interface series recurrence** selected as the simplest special-case obligation by STUCK RECOVERY 1.

## 5. B14 — the group acting and two exact flow constraints

The preceding recurrence still appears to depend on complete episode histories. The relevant symmetry removes that order information.

For each ordered local type `xy`, the symmetric group

\[
S_{n_{xy}}
\]

acts by permuting the excursions of that type. Conditional excursion paths of the same type are i.i.d., and the event “their union covers the child interior” is invariant under this permutation. Therefore the product group

\[
\prod_{xy} S_{n_{xy}}
\]

acts measure-preservingly, and the orbit statistic needed by the local coverage event is precisely the type-count vector.

There are also exact conservation laws. For any physically feasible aggregate local counts,

\[
\boxed{
a_{LM}-a_{ML}=n_{LR}-n_{RL},
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
b_{MR}-b_{RM}=n_{LR}-n_{RL}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

To prove (5.1), count net crossings of the cut separating `L` from the shared terminal `M` in the compressed child skeleton. Every outer `LR` excursion contributes one more `L->M` than `M->L`, every outer `RL` contributes one fewer, and `LL`/`RR` outer excursions have zero net flux. Equation (5.2) is the same argument on the right child.

Thus the eight raw child-type count coordinates satisfy at least two exact linear constraints. For fixed caps, naive coefficient enumeration can be organized in at most six independent count dimensions. This is a complexity reduction for the recurrence, not a source-level metric movement.

## 6. B18 — transfer matrix / stochastic-complement language

The same algebra becomes cleaner as a finite-state transfer kernel. Define the marked kernel on boundary states `(L,M,R)` by

\[
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
A & B & 0\\
C & W & F\\
0 & E & D
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Partition outer states `O={L,R}` and inner state `I={M}`. Eliminating `M` gives the stochastic-complement / Schur-complement expression

\[
\boxed{
\Phi
=
K_{OO}
+
K_{OI}(I-K_{II})^{-1}K_{IO},
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

and its four entries are exactly (4.1)–(4.4).

This language matters because state elimination is associative: for a path of several two-terminal gadgets, eliminating internal boundary vertices in different parenthesizations yields the same formal transfer kernel. ## 7. Polynomial one-composition loop truncation

The formal series in `(1-W)^{-1}` is infinite. For simple unweighted graphs, however, its tail has an explicit polynomial scale.

Let

\[
r=\alpha p_1^{MM}+\beta p_2^{MM}
\tag{7.1}
\]

be the unmarked probability that a positive episode starting at `M` returns to `M` rather than escaping to `L` or `R`.

Let

\[
R_1=R_{\rm eff}^{H_1}(M,L),\qquad
R_2=R_{\rm eff}^{H_2}(M,R).
\]

For a connected unit-conductance graph, the standard escape-probability/effective-resistance identity is

\[
p_1^{ML}=\Pr_M^{H_1}(T_L<T_M^+)=\frac{1}{d_1R_1},
\tag{7.2}
\]

\[
p_2^{MR}=\Pr_M^{H_2}(T_R<T_M^+)=\frac{1}{d_2R_2}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since from `M` a child is chosen with probabilities `alpha=d_1/d` and `beta=d_2/d`, where `d=d_1+d_2`,

\[
1-r
=
\alpha p_1^{ML}+\beta p_2^{MR}
=
\frac1d\left(\frac1{R_1}+\frac1{R_2}\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

If the entire composed simple graph has `n` vertices, then

\[
d\le n-1,\qquad
R_1\le n-1,\qquad
R_2\le n-1,
\]

because effective resistance is at most graph distance. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
1-r\ge \frac{2}{(n-1)^2}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — geometric loop tail

Conditioned on a feasible outer type, let `J` be the number of consecutive `M->M` local loops in that outer excursion before its final escape, taking `J=0` on a direct `LL` or `RR` branch. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(J\ge k\mid \text{outer type})\le r^k
\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{2k}{(n-1)^2}\right).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

#### Proof

For outer type `LR` or `RL`, the expansion `B W^j F` (or its mirror) shows exactly

\[
\Pr(J=j\mid LR)=(1-r)r^j.
\]

Hence the tail is `r^k`. For `LL` or `RR`, the conditional law is a mixture of a direct branch with `J=0` and an `M`-mediated geometric branch; its tail is therefore at most `r^k`. Finally `r\le 1-2/(n-1)^2` by (7.5), and `1-x\le e^{-x}` gives the exponential bound. ∎

If a feasible outer collection contains

\[
M_{\rm out}=|\mathbf n|_1
\]

outer excursions, the union bound gives

\[
\Pr(\exists\text{ outer excursion with }J>K)
\le
M_{\rm out}\exp\!\left(-\frac{2K}{(n-1)^2}\right).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Thus for `M_out>=1`, taking

\[
\boxed{
K=
\left\lceil
\frac{(n-1)^2}{2}
\log\frac{M_{\rm out}}{\delta}
\right\rceil
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

makes the overflow probability at most `delta`.

On the complement of the overflow event, each outer excursion generates at most `K+2` local child excursions, so the total local count mass is at most

\[
M_{\rm out}(K+2).
\tag{7.9}
\]

Because the number of local type coordinates is fixed (eight raw, at most six after the two flow constraints), coefficient extraction and summation over all bounded count vectors are polynomial in the cap for this **single composition step**.

This is the first explicit polynomial truncation for the proposed two-terminal interface. It is not yet a global PTAS theorem.
