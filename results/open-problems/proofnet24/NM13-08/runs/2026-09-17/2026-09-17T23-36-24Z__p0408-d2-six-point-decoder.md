## 4. New idea before proof: dualize a local 3-star, then glue two stars by one scalar identity

The finite obstruction from the first P0408 attack says that a vertex location by itself does not identify its three ideal nuclei. In `d=2`, however, if the **three incident geodesic edge lines** at a Voronoi vertex were known, Möbius normalization at that vertex makes the three participating radial marks equal. The local star then determines the dual ideal triangle up to exactly one binary ambiguity: the hyperbolic half-turn about the vertex.

For one proposed edge `vw`, that ambiguity has an especially simple dual description. Each local 3-star predicts two possible ideal Delaunay edges across the line `vw`; both are perpendicular to `vw`, and their feet are the two points at a computable distance `rho` on opposite sides of the base vertex. Two proposed stars, one at `v` and one at `w`, are therefore compatible exactly when two two-point subsets of the geodesic line `vw` intersect. This collapses the gluing condition to one exact scalar equation involving **six vertices only**.

## 5. Local star inversion in H^2

### Lemma `NM13-08-P0408-D2-STAR-INVERSION-02A`

Let `v in H^2` and let `L0,L1,L2` be three distinct geodesic lines through `v`. Choose any orientation-preserving isometry `phi_v` from `H^2` to the Poincare disk with `phi_v(v)=0`. Then `phi_v(Lj)` is a Euclidean diameter. Write its unoriented angle as `beta_j in R/pi Z` and put

`q_j := exp(2 i beta_j)`.

There are exactly two unordered triples of distinct ideal points

`{zeta_0,zeta_1,zeta_2} subset S^1`

such that, for each permutation `{i,j,k}={0,1,2}`, the diameter `phi_v(L_i)` is the equal-mark ideal-Voronoi bisector of `zeta_j` and `zeta_k`. They are related by the half-turn `zeta -> -zeta`, and they satisfy

`zeta_0^2 = q_1 q_2 / q_0`,

`zeta_1^2 = q_0 q_2 / q_1`,

`zeta_2^2 = q_0 q_1 / q_2`.

After applying `phi_v^{-1}`, this two-element set of candidate ideal triangles is intrinsic: it does not depend on the chosen normalized chart.

If `L0,L1,L2` are the actual three IPVT edge lines through a Voronoi vertex `v`, then the actual ideal Delaunay triangle dual to `v` is one of these two candidates.

### Proof

For equal marks at the origin, Lemma 3.3 says that the bisector of ideal points `zeta_j=e^{i alpha_j}` and `zeta_k=e^{i alpha_k}` is

`|z-zeta_j|^2 = |z-zeta_k|^2`.

This is the diameter through the origin of angle

`beta_i = (alpha_j+alpha_k)/2 mod pi`.

Equivalently,

`q_i = exp(2 i beta_i) = zeta_j zeta_k`.

Thus any solution obeys

`q_0=zeta_1 zeta_2`, `q_1=zeta_0 zeta_2`, `q_2=zeta_0 zeta_1`.

Multiplying and dividing gives the three displayed square identities. Choosing one square root fixes all three `zeta_i`; changing that root changes all three signs simultaneously. Hence there are exactly two solutions, exchanged by `z -> -z`.

For an actual Voronoi vertex, apply the separation-preserving corona action of Lemma 3.10 while sending `v` to the origin. The three nuclei defining `v` have equal separation there. Since `K(0,zeta)=1`, their transformed radial marks are therefore equal. Their three true bisectors are exactly the three diameters above, so their ideal directions form one of the two candidate triples.

Any second normalized chart differs by a rotation about the origin; the formula rotates both candidates equivariantly. Pulling back makes the two-candidate set intrinsic. `QED`

### Geometric meaning of the ambiguity

The two candidates are not an arbitrary algebraic doubling. In the original hyperbolic plane they are related by the unique half-turn `h_v` about `v`. Thus a local geometric 3-star knows its dual ideal triangle only up to `T` versus `h_v(T)`.

## 6. Dual-foot formula

Fix one line, say `L0`, and regard it as the proposed Voronoi edge separating the two candidate nuclei opposite `zeta_0`. Let

`varphi_v in (0,pi/2]`

be the smaller angle between the **other two** unoriented geodesic lines `L1` and `L2` at `v`.

### Lemma `NM13-08-P0408-D2-DUAL-FOOT-02B`

For either candidate ideal triangle from Lemma 02A, the ideal Delaunay geodesic `G0` joining the two candidate nuclei whose Voronoi bisector is `L0` is perpendicular to `L0`. Its perpendicular foot on `L0` is one of exactly two points, exchanged by the half-turn about `v`, and its distance from `v` is

`rho_v(L0;L1,L2) = log cot(varphi_v/2)`.                         (6.1)

### Proof

Normalize again so that `v=0` and rotate so that `L0` is the real diameter. Reflection in `L0` swaps the two ideal endpoints of the corresponding dual Delaunay edge, hence their geodesic `G0` is perpendicular to `L0`.

The angle between `L1` and `L2` is half the smaller circular angular separation of those two ideal endpoints. Therefore, after choosing the candidate whose perpendicular foot lies on the positive real ray, the endpoints can be written

`exp(i varphi_v)` and `exp(-i varphi_v)`.

For `0<varphi_v<pi/2`, their hyperbolic geodesic is the Euclidean circle orthogonal to the unit circle with center `sec(varphi_v)` and radius `tan(varphi_v)`. Its intersection with the real diameter inside the disk is

`x = sec(varphi_v)-tan(varphi_v) = tan(pi/4-varphi_v/2)`.

In the Poincare disk, the distance from `0` to `x` is

`2 artanh(x) = log((1+x)/(1-x)) = log cot(varphi_v/2)`.

At `varphi_v=pi/2`, the dual geodesic itself is a diameter through the origin and both sides equal `0`. The antipodal candidate sends the foot to `-x`, preserving the distance. Pulling back proves (6.1). `QED`

This formula is the useful dualization: a proposed local 3-star turns into a **two-point set of possible feet on each proposed edge line**.

## 7. The exact six-vertex compatibility identity

Take six distinct points

`v,w,a,b,c,d in H^2`

such that the three lines `vw,va,vb` are distinct at `v` and `wv,wc,wd` are distinct at `w`. Define

`phi_v := smaller angle between geodesics va and vb`,

`phi_w := smaller angle between geodesics wc and wd`,

`rho_v := log cot(phi_v/2)`,

`rho_w := log cot(phi_w/2)`,

`D := d_H(v,w)`.

The points `a,b` are being proposed as the other two neighbors of `v`, and `c,d` as the other two neighbors of `w`.

### Proposition `NM13-08-P0408-D2-SIXPOINT-COMPAT-02C`

The two candidate local stars

`(vw,va,vb)` at `v` and `(wv,wc,wd)` at `w`

have a **common candidate dual ideal geodesic across `vw`** if and only if

`D in { rho_v + rho_w, |rho_v-rho_w| }`.                       (7.1)

In particular, if `vw` is a true IPVT edge, `a,b` are the other two true neighbors of `v`, and `c,d` are the other two true neighbors of `w`, then (7.1) holds exactly.

### Proof

Identify the complete geodesic `L` through `v,w` isometrically with the real line, taking coordinate `v=0` and `w=D`.

By Lemma 02B, the local star at `v` gives exactly two candidate Delaunay geodesics across `L`; their perpendicular feet on `L` have coordinates

`{+rho_v,-rho_v}`.

The local star at `w` gives exactly two candidate Delaunay geodesics across the same `L`, whose feet have coordinates

`{D+rho_w,D-rho_w}`.

A hyperbolic geodesic perpendicular to `L` is uniquely determined by its foot on `L`. Therefore the two stars have a common candidate dual edge iff these two two-point sets intersect. Such an intersection is exactly an equation

`epsilon rho_v = D + eta rho_w`, with `epsilon,eta in {+1,-1}`,

which, since `D>0` and the `rho` are nonnegative, is equivalent to

`D=rho_v+rho_w` or `D=|rho_v-rho_w|`.

For a true IPVT edge, Proposition 3.13 says the two dual Delaunay triangles at its endpoints share the same ideal Delaunay edge. That actual geodesic is one candidate on both sides, so their feet coincide and (7.1) follows. `QED`

## 9. Why the obvious genericity proof failed — and what a proof must now contain

I tried to finish `SP6` by saying that (7.1) is one real-analytic scalar equation in six vertex locations and therefore should have probability zero for a false sextuple. That argument is **not valid as stated**.

The reason is important: the sixth factorial moment measure of the vertex process cannot simply be assumed absolutely continuous on all of `(H^2)^6`. Every genuine adjacent two-star produces a sextuple satisfying (7.1) identically, so the six-point law necessarily has incidence-induced singular strata supported on exactly this compatibility hypersurface. A blanket `six-point density => zero` argument would erase the true edges together with the false ones.

What is needed is a **stratified Mecke/transversality statement**:

> decompose a selected sextuple of Voronoi vertices by the overlap pattern of the six underlying ideal Delaunay triangles; the genuine adjacent-two-star overlap type is allowed to live on (7.1), while every other overlap type must be shown transverse to (7.1), hence to contribute zero mass there.

The source's normality proof already uses the same broad style—label finitely many nuclei and rule out nongeneric equal-separation coincidences using the continuous Poisson marks—but the present six-triangle overlap classification is not written in the paper and was not completed in this run.

The classical Heinrich--Muche second-order node-process formulas show that factorial-moment methods are natural for Voronoi vertices, but they stop far short of this source-specific sixth-order incidence decomposition. Thus they are a useful neighboring technique, not a missing citation that closes `SP6`.

This failure is productive: the remaining obligation is no longer “recover an infinite graph somehow.” It is a finite list of Delaunay-triangle overlap types plus one explicit analytic transversality check for each non-two-star type.
