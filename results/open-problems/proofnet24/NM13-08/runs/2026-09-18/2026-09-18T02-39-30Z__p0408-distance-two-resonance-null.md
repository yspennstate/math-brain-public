## 7. Fixed-line geometry

Let

`L_AB := {z : sep(z,A)=sep(z,B)}`.

This is the complete geodesic containing the true Voronoi edge `vu`. Let `G_AB` be the hyperbolic geodesic joining the ideal directions of `A` and `B`, and let

`p_AB := L_AB ∩ G_AB`.

The prior dual-foot lemma `NM13-08-P0408-D2-DUAL-FOOT-02B`, applied to the **true** edge `vu`, gives

\[
\boxed{\rho_v=d_{\mathbb H}(v,p_{AB}).}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus if `gamma:R->L_AB` is signed arclength with `gamma(0)=p_AB`, and `v=gamma(t)`, then

\[
\rho_v=|t|.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The point `w` does not lie on `L_AB`. Indeed `w` lies on the distinct true edge line `L_BC`, and `L_AB` and `L_BC` meet at `u`; two distinct hyperbolic geodesics through `u` cannot meet again at `w`.

Let `q` be the signed coordinate on `L_AB` of the perpendicular projection of `w`, and let

`h=d_{\mathbb H}(w,L_AB)>0`.

Put

`D(t):=d_{\mathbb H}(gamma(t),w)`.

The hyperbolic right-triangle formula gives

\[
\cosh D(t)=\cosh h\,\cosh(t-q).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Differentiating,

\[
D'(t)=\frac{\cosh h\,\sinh(t-q)}{\sinh D(t)}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Moreover

\[
\sinh^2 D(t)-\cosh^2h\,\sinh^2(t-q)=\sinh^2 h>0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|D'(t)|<1\quad\text{for every }t.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This strict slope bound is the monovariant needed by method #13.

## 8. Finite-root lemma for the resonance equation

Fix `c:=rho_w>=0`. By (7.2), the distance-two resonance is

\[
D(t)\in\{|t|+c,\ ||t|-c|\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Lemma `NM13-08-P0408-D2-FINITE-ROOT-05A`

For fixed `w notin L_AB` and fixed `c>=0`, the set of real `t` satisfying (8.1) is finite.

### Proof

For the branch `D(t)=|t|+c`:

- on `t>0`, `D(t)-t-c` has derivative `D'(t)-1<0`;
- on `t<0`, `D(t)+t-c` has derivative `D'(t)+1>0`.

So there is at most one root on each open half-line, with `t=0` handled separately.

For the branch `D(t)=||t|-c|`, split at `t=-c,0,c`.

- on `t>=c`, `D(t)-t+c` has derivative `D'(t)-1<0`;
- on `0<=t<=c`, `D(t)+t-c` has derivative `D'(t)+1>0`;
- on `-c<=t<=0`, `D(t)-t-c` has derivative `D'(t)-1<0`;
- on `t<=-c`, `D(t)+t+c` has derivative `D'(t)+1>0`.

Hence each interval contributes at most one root; endpoints add only finitely many possibilities. `QED`
