## 4. Counterexample-first continuation: can eighteenth-root Butson bases even form one MUB pair?

The global negative-search target remains seven exact mutually unbiased bases. The previous run excluded fixed-gauge pairings in `BH(6,3)`, `BH(6,4)`, `BH(6,6)` and the mixed `BH(6,3)`/`BH(6,6)` case, and explicitly found a twelfth-root scalar sum of norm squared six, showing that naive root-order extension is false.

This run tests the next genuinely larger exact cyclotomic stratum: eighteenth roots.

Let `BH(6,18)` denote an order-six complex Hadamard matrix all of whose entries are eighteenth roots of unity. In the fixed identity gauge, if

\[
U=H/\sqrt6,\qquad V=K/\sqrt6,
\]

with `H,K in BH(6,18)`, then every cross entry has the form

\[
(U^*V)_{ij}=\frac16 S,
\qquad
S=\sum_{r=1}^{6}\zeta_{18}^{a_r}.
\]

Mutual unbiasedness would require

\[
\left|\frac S6\right|^2=\frac16,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\boxed{S\overline S=6.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The following theorem rules this out without classifying the Hadamard matrices themselves.

## 5. Theorem `C29-CYCLOTOMIC-18-03`

**Theorem.** In one fixed identity coordinate gauge, no two `BH(6,18)` bases are mutually unbiased. More generally, no pair of bases whose coordinatewise cross-ratios all lie among eighteenth roots of unity can be mutually unbiased. Consequently all pairings among fixed-gauge Butson bases with root orders dividing `18` — in particular all mixed pairings drawn from `BH(6,3)`, `BH(6,6)`, `BH(6,9)` and `BH(6,18)` — are excluded.

### Proof

Let

\[
\zeta=\zeta_{18}=e^{\pi i/9},
\qquad
\xi=\zeta_9=e^{2\pi i/9}.
\]

Since

\[
\zeta=-\xi^5,
\]

we have the same cyclotomic field

\[
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{18})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_9),
\qquad [K:\mathbb Q]=6,
\]

with ring of integers `O_K=Z[xi]`. Let

\[
K^+=\mathbb Q(\xi+\xi^{-1})
\]

be its maximal real subfield, of degree three. Complex conjugation is the nontrivial element of the quadratic CM extension `K/K^+`.

Suppose, for contradiction, that an algebraic integer `S in O_K` satisfies

\[
S\overline S=6.
\tag{5.1}
\]

We look only at the rational prime `2`.

Because `2` does not divide the conductor `9`, it is unramified in `K`. Its residue degree in the ninth cyclotomic field is the multiplicative order of `2 mod 9`. Directly,

\[
2,\ 2^2=4,\ 2^3=8\equiv-1\pmod9,
\qquad 2^6\equiv1\pmod9,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{ord}_9(2)=6.
\]

Thus `(2)` is inert in the degree-six extension `K/Q`: there is one prime `Q` above `2`, with residue degree six.

In the real subfield the Galois group is

\[
(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\},
\]

of order three. Since `2^3 congruent -1 mod 9`, the image of `2` has order three in this quotient. Hence `(2)` is also inert in `K^+/Q`, with a unique prime `P` above `2` and residue degree three.

Therefore the unique prime `Q` above `P` in the quadratic extension `K/K^+` has relative residue degree

\[
f(Q/P)=2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For any ideal `I` of `O_K`, the exponent of `P` in its relative ideal norm is therefore even:

\[
v_P\!\left(N_{K/K^+}(I)\right)=2v_Q(I).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Apply this to the principal ideal `(S)`. Equation (5.1) gives

\[
N_{K/K^+}((S))=(S\overline S)=(6)
\]

as ideals of `O_{K^+}`. But `P` occurs to exponent exactly one in `(6)`: it occurs once in `(2)`, and the prime `3` contributes no factor above `2`. This contradicts the even valuation forced by (5.3).

Hence no algebraic integer of `K` satisfies `S\overline S=6`. In particular no six-term sum of eighteenth roots can satisfy (4.1). Therefore no fixed-gauge `BH(6,18)` pair can be mutually unbiased. The same argument applies whenever all cross-ratios are eighteenth roots. QED.

### Scope note

This theorem is gauge-sensitive in exactly the same way as the preceding Butson lemma. Common monomial operations preserving the identity basis as a set are legitimate. Independently rephasing the rows of different nonidentity bases is **not** a common unitary gauge operation and can change their relative overlaps. The theorem is not a statement about arbitrary Hadamards after independently chosen equivalence representatives.

## 6. Quantitative restricted-domain residual certificate

The ideal argument also yields a positive explicit residual gap on this stratum.

For any six-term eighteenth-root sum `S`, define

\[
\alpha=S\overline S-6\in\mathcal O_{K^+}.
\]

The theorem gives `alpha != 0`. Therefore its degree-three field norm is a nonzero integer:

\[
|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)|\ge1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Under either of the two other real embeddings of `K^+`, choose an extension to `K`. It sends eighteenth roots to eighteenth roots, so the conjugate `S'` is still a sum of six unit complex numbers. Hence

\[
0\le |S'|^2\le36,
\qquad
\bigl||S'|^2-6\bigr|\le30.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Writing the three real conjugates of `alpha` as `alpha,alpha_2,alpha_3`, equations (6.1)–(6.2) give

\[
1\le |\alpha\alpha_2\alpha_3|
\le 900|\alpha|,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|S\overline S-6|\ge\frac1{900}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

For one vector pair from two normalized Butson bases,

\[
\left(|\langle u,v\rangle|^2-\frac16\right)^2
=\frac{(S\overline S-6)^2}{36^2}
\ge\frac1{1,049,760,000}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

A cross-basis block has 36 entries, hence contributes at least

\[
\boxed{\frac1{29,160,000}}
\tag{6.5}
\]

to the unnormalized residual sum.

If all six nonidentity bases in `(I,U_1,...,U_6)` lie in `BH(6,18)` in the same fixed coordinate gauge, the six identity-vs-`U_k` blocks vanish exactly because each Butson Hadamard is flat, while all 15 nonidentity pair blocks obey (6.5). Thus

\[
\boxed{
\Phi\ge \frac1{1,469,664,000}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

The same bound holds for any fixed-gauge six-basis collection whose pairwise coordinate ratios are all eighteenth roots, including mixtures of root orders dividing 18.

This is a **restricted-domain** lower bound. It does not move the track metric because the global domain is all of `U(6)^6`.
