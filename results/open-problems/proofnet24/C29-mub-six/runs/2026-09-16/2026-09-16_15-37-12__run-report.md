## 4. Full compact gauge domain and exact equivalence

The fixed global domain for C29 is now frozen.

Represent an ordered orthonormal basis by a unitary matrix whose columns are its basis vectors. Let a candidate ordered seven-tuple be

\[
V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_6\in U(6).
\]

Pairwise overlaps are unchanged if all bases are left-multiplied by the same unitary. Taking the common unitary `V_0^*` sends the first basis to the identity. Thus every seven-basis candidate is gauge-equivalent to exactly the form

\[
I_6,U_1,\ldots,U_6,
\qquad (U_1,\ldots,U_6)\in\mathcal D:=U(6)^6.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Conversely every point of `D` gives seven orthonormal bases by adjoining `I_6`. This is a complete global-unitary gauge slice; it does not discard entangled, non-Butson, non-Hadamard-family, or otherwise unrestricted candidates.

`U(6)` is compact, hence

\[
\mathcal D=U(6)^6
\]

is compact. As a real algebraic set one may write `U_k=A_k+iB_k`, with

\[
A_k^T A_k+B_k^T B_k=I,
\qquad
A_k^T B_k-B_k^T A_k=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

No quotient singularities or incomplete phase boxes are being hidden in the domain.

## 5. Frozen normalized residual

For `U=(U_1,...,U_6) in D`, define

\[
\Phi(U)=\frac1{756}\left[
\sum_{k=1}^6\sum_{a,j=1}^6
\left(|(U_k)_{aj}|^2-\frac16\right)^2
+
\sum_{1\le k<\ell\le6}\sum_{i,j=1}^6
\left(|(U_k^*U_\ell)_{ij}|^2-\frac16\right)^2
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are `6*36=216` residuals between the fixed identity basis and the six remaining bases, plus `C(6,2)*36=540` residuals among those six bases, for a total of

\[
216+540=756=\binom72\,6^2.
\]

The denominator therefore gives the average squared squared-overlap residual over all 21 unordered basis pairs and all 36 cross-vector pairs.

### Proposition C29-GAUGE-01

\[
\mathrm{MUB}(6)\ge7
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\min_{U\in\mathcal D}\Phi(U)=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathrm{MUB}(6)<7
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\min_{U\in\mathcal D}\Phi(U)>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

`Phi` is a sum of squares, hence `Phi>=0`. It is zero exactly when every pair consisting of the identity and `U_k`, and every pair `U_k,U_l`, has every squared overlap equal to `1/6`. This is precisely the definition of seven pairwise MUBs in the gauge `(4.1)`, proving `(5.2)`. Since `D` is compact and `Phi` is continuous, if the zero set is empty then `Phi` attains a strictly positive minimum; conversely a positive minimum excludes a zero. This proves `(5.3)`. QED.

This proves that the fleet's tracked quantity is not an arbitrary numerical objective: a strictly positive certified lower bound on the **entire** frozen domain settles Conjecture 22 exactly.

The first globally certified lower bound is the trivial but exact one

\[
\boxed{\Phi(U)\ge0\quad\text{for every }U\in\mathcal D.}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus the metric moves from `UNMEASURED` to `0`. I do not relabel zero as progress toward nonexistence beyond freezing and certifying the correct objective/domain.

### 6.1 Exact MUB graph in the product family

For product bases,

\[
|\langle q_i\otimes r_j,q'_{i'}\otimes r'_{j'}\rangle|^2
=
|\langle q_i,q'_{i'}\rangle|^2
|\langle r_j,r'_{j'}\rangle|^2.
\]

Hence two bases `B_ab` and `B_a'b'` are mutually unbiased exactly when

\[
a\ne a'\quad\text{and}\quad b\ne b'.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The checker independently verifies the complete local MUBs in dimensions 2 and 3, then enumerates the resulting exact MUB graph on all 12 product bases. It finds 36 MUB edges and exact clique number

\[
\boxed{\omega=3},
\]

with 24 maximum cliques. Therefore this complete 12-basis product construction contains no seven-MUB counterexample.

This restricted negative result is consistent with and much weaker than the known McNulty–Weigert 2012 theorem that any hypothetical complete seven-MUB set in dimension six can contain at most one product basis. I therefore mark the product route as **KNOWN/DEAD for a complete counterexample**, not as a novel obstruction to Conjecture 22.

### 6.2 Exact best seven-tuple residual inside the 12 product bases

The same finite search also evaluates `(5.1)` on every seven-element subset of the 12 product bases.

For distinct product bases, the unnormalized 36-entry pair block has exactly three possible residuals:

- if both local labels differ: block residual `0`;
- if the qubit label agrees and the qutrit label differs: 18 squared overlaps equal `1/3`, 18 equal `0`, so the block residual is
  \[
  18(1/3-1/6)^2+18(0-1/6)^2=1;
  \]
- if the qutrit label agrees and the qubit label differs: 12 squared overlaps equal `1/2`, 24 equal `0`, so the block residual is
  \[
  12(1/2-1/6)^2+24(0-1/6)^2=2.
  \]

For a seven-set `S` in the `3 x 4` label grid, let row counts be `r_1,r_2,r_3` and column counts be `c_1,...,c_4`. Its unnormalized pair residual is exactly

\[
P(S)=\sum_{a=1}^3\binom{r_a}{2}
+2\sum_{b=1}^4\binom{c_b}{2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because `sum r_a=sum c_b=7`, convexity of `binom{x}{2}` gives

\[
\sum_a\binom{r_a}{2}\ge
\binom32+\binom22+\binom22=5,
\]

and

\[
\sum_b\binom{c_b}{2}\ge
\binom22+\binom22+\binom22+\binom12=3.
\]

Therefore

\[
P(S)\ge5+2\cdot3=11.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Margins `(3,2,2)` and `(2,2,2,1)` are jointly realizable by a `0/1` `3 x 4` table, so equality occurs. Thus the best normalized residual among all seven-tuples from this complete product family is exactly

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{\rm product,min}=\frac{11}{21\cdot36}=\frac{11}{756}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

The checker exhausts all `C(12,7)=792` seven-subsets and independently confirms `(6.5)`, finding 144 minimizers.

Since these are admissible seven-tuples of orthonormal bases, `(6.5)` supplies the useful global **upper** benchmark

\[
0\le\min_{\mathcal D}\Phi\le\frac{11}{756}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

A future positive global lower certificate must therefore lie in `(0,11/756]`.
