## 4. Counterexample-first branch: exact cyclotomic/Butson obstruction

The global counterexample target is still seven exact MUBs. Rather than treating numerical near-solutions as candidates, this run asks whether exact cyclotomic complex-Hadamard branches can even contain **two** mutually unbiased bases in the fixed identity gauge.

Let `BH(6,q)` mean a 6x6 complex Hadamard matrix whose entries are q-th roots of unity. If `H,K in BH(6,q)`, then `U=H/sqrt(6)` and `V=K/sqrt(6)` are orthonormal bases unbiased to the identity. A cross inner product is

\[
(U^*V)_{ij}=\frac16 S,
\qquad
S=\sum_{r=1}^6 \zeta_q^{a_r},
\]

because each coordinatewise ratio is a q-th root. Mutual unbiasedness of this pair requires

\[
\left|\frac{S}{6}\right|^2=\frac16,
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\boxed{|S|^2=6.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus an arithmetic obstruction to a six-term root-of-unity sum of norm 6 excludes an entire fixed-gauge Butson pair.

### Theorem `C29-CYCLOTOMIC-BUTSON-02`

In the fixed identity gauge:

1. no two `BH(6,3)` bases are mutually unbiased;
2. no two `BH(6,4)` bases are mutually unbiased;
3. no two `BH(6,6)` bases are mutually unbiased;
4. no `BH(6,3)` basis is mutually unbiased with a `BH(6,6)` basis.

The assertion concerns bases that are Butson in the **same fixed coordinate gauge**. It does not permit independently rephasing rows of different bases, because independent row rephasings are not a common unitary and can change their mutual overlaps.

### Proof for q=3

Let `omega=e^{2 pi i/3}`. Any sum of six cube roots can be written

\[
S=A+B\omega,
\]

with integers `A,B`. Its squared modulus is the Eisenstein norm

\[
|S|^2=A^2-AB+B^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Modulo 2, the quadratic form in (4.2) vanishes only when `A` and `B` are both even. Hence if the norm is even then `A=2a`, `B=2b` and the norm is divisible by 4. It therefore cannot equal 6. By (4.1), no two `BH(6,3)` bases are mutually unbiased.

### Proof for q=4

Any sum of six fourth roots has the form

\[
S=A+iB,
\qquad |S|^2=A^2+B^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If `A^2+B^2` is divisible by 3, the fact that the only quadratic residues modulo 3 are 0 and 1 forces `A` and `B` both to be divisible by 3. Thus a norm divisible by 3 is actually divisible by 9. It cannot equal 6. This excludes a mutually unbiased `BH(6,4)` pair.

### Proof for q=6

Let `zeta=e^{pi i/3}`. Since `zeta^2-zeta+1=0`, every six-root sum has

\[
S=A+B\zeta,
\qquad
|S|^2=A^2+AB+B^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Again, modulo 2 the form in (4.4) vanishes only when both coefficients are even; an even norm is therefore divisible by 4 and cannot be 6. This excludes a mutually unbiased `BH(6,6)` pair.

Finally, if one basis is `BH(6,3)` and the other is `BH(6,6)`, every coordinatewise ratio is a sixth root. The q=6 argument applies verbatim. QED.

This is a branch-pruning theorem, not a resolution of Conjecture 22.

## 6. Sharp limitation: q=12 arithmetic does not exclude unbiasedness

The simple root-of-unity norm obstruction cannot be promoted blindly to mixed fourth/sixth-root branches. Let `zeta=e^{pi i/6}`. The six-term sum

\[
S=2\zeta^6+\zeta^7+\zeta^{10}+2\zeta^{11}
\tag{6.1}
\]

has

\[
\Re S=\frac{\sqrt3-3}{2},
\qquad
\Im S=-\frac{3+\sqrt3}{2},
\]

and therefore

\[
|S|^2
=\frac{(\sqrt3-3)^2+(3+\sqrt3)^2}{4}
=6.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus six twelfth roots **can** meet the scalar MUB magnitude exactly. This does not construct a pair of order-six complex Hadamards, but it proves that the q=3/4/6 norm argument alone cannot exclude a mixed `BH(6,4)`/`BH(6,6)` pair. I record this killed overgeneralization explicitly rather than silently extending the lemma.

## 8. Relation to the August 2026 complete Hadamard classification

The Cárdenes Wuttig–Tindall classification gives a serious unrestricted algebraic route, but it must be imported carefully. In the identity gauge, every additional MUB is a normalized order-six complex Hadamard matrix. One may use a common monomial unitary preserving the identity basis as a set, plus column phases/permutations of one chosen second basis, to put **one** Hadamard into a classified representative. One may not independently apply arbitrary row phases to every other basis, because that changes their relative MUB constraints.

Therefore a correct use of the classification is:

1. branch-completely fix one second basis `U_1` up to the allowed common gauge;
2. retain all relative phase data for `U_2,...,U_6`;
3. impose the 15 remaining pairwise Hadamard/MUB compatibility systems exactly;
4. certify each classification branch by algebraic elimination or a complete interval cover.

The cyclotomic result above removes some fixed-gauge Butson subbranches exactly, but it is nowhere near a complete cover of the classification atlas. In particular, it supplies no positive global lower bound on all of `U(6)^6`.
