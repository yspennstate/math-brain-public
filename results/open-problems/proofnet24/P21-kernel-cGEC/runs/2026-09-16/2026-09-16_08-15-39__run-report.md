# 6. `P21-FENCHEL-BLOCK-44` — exact two-dimensional convex-dual block influence

This section supplies a new independent derivation of the P04 block formula and exposes the exact nonlinear generalization.

Let `F_-:R^m -> R union {+infty}` be proper, closed and strongly convex, with unique minimizer `w_-`. The current two-view contribution is

\[
\frac1N\phi(t-Xw),
\qquad
\phi(r)=\ell(r_1)+\ell(r_2).
\tag{6.1}
\]

By Fenchel duality,

\[
\phi(t-Xw)
=
\sup_{s\in\mathbb R^2}
\left\{
 s^T(t-Xw)-\phi^*(s)
\right\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Strong convexity gives the standard Fenchel-Rockafellar interchange, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_i^\star
&=
\sup_s
\left[
\frac1N(s^Tt-\phi^*(s))
+
\min_w
\left\{
F_-(w)-w^T\frac{X^Ts}{N}
\right\}
\right]\\
&=
\sup_s
\left[
\frac1N(s^Tt-\phi^*(s))
-
F_-^*\!\left(\frac{X^Ts}{N}\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Since

\[
L_-^\star=-F_-^*(0),
\qquad
\nabla F_-^*(0)=w_-,
\tag{6.4}
\]

and `e=t-Xw_-`, subtracting `L_-^star` yields

\[
L_i^\star-L_-^\star
=
\frac1N
\sup_s
\left\{
 s^Te-\phi^*(s)-K_N(s)
\right\},
\tag{6.5}
\]

with `K_N` defined in (0.1). This proves (0.2).

Convexity of `F_-^*` and the tangent inequality at zero imply

\[
K_N(s)\ge0,
\qquad
K_N(0)=0,
\qquad
\nabla K_N(0)=0.
\tag{6.6}
\]

If `F_-` is smooth Legendre, differentiate (0.1). With

\[
q_s=\frac{X^Ts}{N},
\qquad
w_s=\nabla F_-^*(q_s),
\]

one gets

\[
\nabla K_N(s)=X(w_s-w_-),
\tag{6.7}
\]

and hence

\[
\nabla^2K_N(s)
=
\frac1N X\nabla^2F_-^*(q_s)X^T
=
\frac1N X[\nabla^2F_-(w_s)]^{-1}X^T.
\tag{6.8}
\]

If `0<mu<=ell''<=L`, then the scalar conjugate satisfies

\[
\frac1L\le (\ell^*)''\le\frac1\mu.
\]

Thus `phi*+K_N` is `1/L`-strongly convex, and conjugacy gives the exact dimension-free Hessian bound (0.4).

This theorem is a deterministic local expansion under explicit regularity; it is not claimed as a completed random-feature nonquadratic universality theorem.

Assume `F_-^*` is `C^4` in a neighborhood of zero, let

\[
B=\nabla^2F_-^*(0)=H_-^{-1},
\]

and suppose

\[
\|X\|_{op}\le C\sqrt N
\]

and the fourth derivative of `F_-^*` is uniformly bounded on the relevant `q_s` ball. For bounded `s`, Taylor expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_N(s)
&=
\frac12s^TQs
+
\frac N6
D^3F_-^*(0)[q_s,q_s,q_s]
+O(N\|q_s\|^4)\\
&=
\frac12s^TQs
+
\frac1{6\sqrt N}\Gamma_N(s)
+O(N^{-1}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
Q=\frac1N XBX^T,
\qquad
\Gamma_N(s)
=N^{3/2}D^3F_-^*(0)[q_s,q_s,q_s].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define the frozen-quadratic dual value

\[
\Psi_Q(e)
=
\sup_s
\left\{
 s^Te-\phi^*(s)-\frac12s^TQs
\right\}
\tag{7.3}
\]

and let `s_0(e)` denote its unique maximizer. Strong convexity of `phi*` keeps the maximizer in a fixed ball for bounded `e`. Standard strong-concavity perturbation/envelope expansion applied to (7.1) yields

\[
\boxed{
L_i^\star-L_-^\star
=
\frac1N\Psi_Q(e)
-
\frac1{6N^{3/2}}\Gamma_N(s_0(e))
+O(N^{-2}).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The sign is negative because `K_N` is subtracted in the dual objective.

The key normalization conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{generic cubic optimizer response}
=
O(N^{-3/2})\ \text{per original block}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

After `n=N/2` replacements, this is potentially `O(N^-1/2)`, exactly the cGEC scale. It cannot be swept into the ordinary `O(N^-2)` bounded-`C^2` Taylor debt.

# 9. `P21-CROSSHAT-L4-44` — basepoint projected influence delocalization

The failed `O(N^-2)` route does not mean a nonquadratic extension is impossible. It means the cubic response must itself be compared at the `N^-1/2` total scale.

Assume now the empirical leave objective (0.7) with

\[
0<\mu\le\ell''\le L,
\qquad
|\ell'''|\le M_3.
\tag{9.1}

At `w_-`, let

\[
H

\lambda I
+

\qquad

\qquad
B=H^{-1}.
\tag{9.2}

\[
q_s=\frac{X_i^Ts}{N},
\qquad
h_s=Bq_s,

\[

=
c_a^T\sigma(F^T(a_i+\Delta z_{ij}))

is centered because `sigma` is odd and the fresh Gaussian latent is symmetric. Its gradient with respect to that current Gaussian latent has norm at most

C_\Delta\|F\|_{op}\|\sigma'\|_\infty\|c_a\|.

\le
C(1+\|F\|_{op})^4\|c_a\|^4.

For the matched Gaussian current block the same inequality follows directly from its conditional Gaussian covariance, preserving the shared `a_i,b_i` structure.

\[
\frac\mu N x_ax_a^T\preceq H,

\tag{9.6}

=x_a^TB^2x_a

\frac1\lambda x_a^TBx_a

\frac N{\mu\lambda}.
\tag{9.7}

\[
\sum_{a\notin i}\|Bx_a\|^4
\le

# 10. `P21-PROJECTED-CUBIC-BASE-44` — the cubic coefficient has the correct scale

Differentiate the empirical leave objective three times. At `w_-`,

\[
D^3F_-(w_-)[h,h,h]
=
-\frac1N
\sum_{a\notin i}
\ell'''(r_a)(x_a^Th)^3.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Set `h=h_s`. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\[
\sum_a|v_a|^3
\le
\left(\sum_a v_a^2\right)^{1/2}
\left(\sum_a v_a^4\right)^{1/2}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Using (9.9)-(9.10),

\[
E\sup_{\|s\|\le S}
\left|
D^3F_-(w_-)[h_s,h_s,h_s]
\right|
\le
\frac{C_S}{N^{3/2}}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

The third-derivative identity for a Legendre conjugate is

\[
D^3F_-^*(0)[q,q,q]
=
-
D^3F_-(w_-)[Bq,Bq,Bq].
\tag{10.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
E\sup_{\|s\|\le S}
\left|
N^{3/2}D^3F_-^*(0)[q_s,q_s,q_s]
\right|
\le C_S.
}
\tag{10.5}
\]

This proves `P21-PROJECTED-CUBIC-BASE-44`.

The important dimension audit is explicit. A naive bound on a third derivative in ambient `m` dimensions could pay a large feature factor. Here the current two-row block is conditioned on **last**, (9.4) gives the fresh projection moment before the training-row sum, (9.6) supplies the exact ridge/curvature leverage bound, and the final result has the correct `N^-3/2` projected scale.

## 10.1 Why the pathwise extension is still open

Taylor's theorem for `K_N` samples the dual response not just at zero but along

\[
q=\tau X_i^Ts/N,
\qquad 0\le\tau\le1.
\]

Equivalently, the relevant inverse Hessian is

\[
H(w_{\tau,s})^{-1},
\qquad
w_{\tau,s}=\nabla F_-^*(\tau q_s).
\]

Although `F_-` contains only leave rows, `w_{tau,s}` depends on the current block through `q_s`. Thus the vector

\[
H(w_{\tau,s})^{-1}x_a
\]

is no longer fixed when conditioning on the fresh current block. The argument of Section 9 cannot simply be repeated. Bounding `||H(w_tau)^-1-H(w_-)^-1||` by an ambient operator norm is too crude and can reintroduce the exact dimension factors the block proof was designed to avoid.

The remaining lemma is therefore (0.10), or an equivalent averaged resolvent/Stein contraction that preserves the fresh-current gain despite this adaptive path dependence.

# 11. Method comparison and dimension/dependence audit

## Whole-original-sample block Lindeberg

This remains the **sharp method for the requested squared-ridge theorem**. It preserves the shared `a_i`, teacher `t_i`, and source-Gaussian shared `b_i`. The exact `1/N` influence from the two-row block is obtained before summation, giving total `O(d^-1/2)` rather than the false `O(sqrt d)` accumulation.

## Correlated Stein / odd-Hermite comparison

This is target-aligned for squared ridge. Once the rank-two leverage is frozen and view parity is used, only three same-parity second moments survive. A strong vector CLT would be unnecessary even if it were available.

## Contracted second-order Poincare / interpolation

The retained `P21-BLOCK-C2-LOGFREE-43` gives a stronger fixed-dimensional smooth block comparison and is useful for extensions. It does not imply coordinatewise feature Gaussianity. This run shows that, for nonquadratic loss, the missing object is not the local block CLT but the adaptive optimizer-response path `K_N`.

## Fenchel duality versus local resolvent/Woodbury

Equation (0.2) is the nonlinear convex analogue of the rank-two resolvent formula. For squared ridge, `F_-^*` is quadratic and the entire response reduces to

\[
K_N(s)=\frac12s^TQ_is,
\]

so the local resolvent `(I_2+Q_i)^-1` is exact. For a nonquadratic loss the two-dimensional dual remains exact, but the local Hessian changes along the dual path. This is why the quadratic problem closes with a frozen rank-two resolvent while the general case has a cubic path-response term.

## Global resolvents / nonlinear Gram universality

Still unnecessary for Target 1. Attempting to prove a full global nonlinear-Gram universality theorem first is strictly stronger than the target and is liable to introduce spurious `m`, `m^2`, or `dm` multiplicities.

## Naive CLTs

Rejected for three independent reasons retained from earlier P21 work:

1. the two views share `a_i` and the teacher scalar;
2. the actual plus/minus channels can be dependent despite zero mixed covariance;
3. a bounded `tanh` feature coordinate stays a positive total-variation distance from its Gaussian surrogate coordinate.

The correct theorem is a target-functional whole-block comparison.

## New nonquadratic dimension warning

The generic cubic response in (7.5) is `N^-3/2` per block. Since there are `Theta(N)` blocks, it is **not** a harmless local third-order remainder: it accumulates to `N^-1/2`. A proof that calls it `O(N^-2)` without using model-specific cancellation or comparing it explicitly loses the exact target scale.
