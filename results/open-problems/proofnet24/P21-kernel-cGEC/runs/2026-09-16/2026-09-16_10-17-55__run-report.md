# 4. New deterministic theorem `P21-MOMENT-SPACE-INTERP-46`

## 4.1 Why interpolate moments instead of source coordinates

After leave-block conditioning and leverage freezing, the squared-ridge block statistic is quadratic in the current two residual coordinates. Consequently its expectation depends only on:

- the conditional residual covariance `C`;
- the conditional mean two-by-two leverage `Qbar`.

The previous Hermite-radial theorem already proves that these endpoint moment objects match to `O(d^(-1/2))`. The present theorem shows that this is enough through an **exact finite-dimensional interpolation**, with no source-coordinate interpolation and no strong CLT.

## 4.2 Deterministic statement

Let `Q_0,Q_1` and `C_0,C_1` be symmetric positive semidefinite `2 x 2` matrices. Define

`DeltaQ=Q_1-Q_0`, `DeltaC=C_1-C_0`,

`Q_s=Q_0+s DeltaQ`, `C_s=C_0+s DeltaC`, `0<=s<=1`,

and

`A_s=(I_2+Q_s)^(-1)`.

Because the PSD cone is convex, `Q_s>=0`; hence

`||A_s||op<=1`.

Define

`Phi(s)=Tr(A_s C_s)`.

Then

`A_s'=-A_s DeltaQ A_s`,

so exactly

`Phi'(s)=Tr(A_s DeltaC)-Tr(A_s DeltaQ A_s C_s)`.

Therefore

`|Phi'(s)| <= ||DeltaC||_* + ||DeltaQ||op Tr(C_s)`.

Integrating from `0` to `1` gives the exact endpoint inequality

`|Tr((I+Q_1)^(-1)C_1)-Tr((I+Q_0)^(-1)C_0)|`

`<= ||DeltaC||_* + K_C ||DeltaQ||op`,

where

`K_C=max_{0<=s<=1} Tr(C_s)=max(Tr C_0,Tr C_1)`.

In block dimension two,

`||DeltaC||_* <= sqrt(2)||DeltaC||F`.

This is a deterministic theorem. It has no hidden ambient dimension and no probabilistic approximation inside the interpolation.

## 4.3 Application to the actual and Gaussian current blocks

Condition on the leave-original-sample environment `E_-`, including `F`, `w_-`, and `B`. For `M=A,G`, define

`C_M=E[e_M e_M^T | E_-]`,

`Qbar_M=E[Q_M | E_-]`.

The inherited P21 Hermite-radial and leverage-mean theorems give

`E||C_A-C_G||F = O(d^(-1/2))`,

`E||Qbar_A-Qbar_G||op = O(d^(-1/2))`,

and

`sup_M E Tr(C_M)^2 < infinity`.

Hence the exact moment interpolation yields

`E | Tr((I+Qbar_A)^(-1)C_A)-Tr((I+Qbar_G)^(-1)C_G) | = O(d^(-1/2))`.

This is precisely the frozen unscaled training-block comparison.

## 4.4 Restoring the random leverage

For each model,

`A(Q)=(I_2+Q)^(-1)`

satisfies the resolvent identity

`||A(Q)-A(Qbar)||op <= ||Q-Qbar||op`.

The joint current-block Gaussian-Poincare calculation already gives

`E||Q-Qbar||F^2=O(d^(-1))`.

Since `E||e||^4=O(1)`, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

`E | e^T[A(Q)-A(Qbar)]e | = O(d^(-1/2))`.

Therefore

`E | e_A^T(I+Q_A)^(-1)e_A - e_G^T(I+Q_G)^(-1)e_G | = O(d^(-1/2))`.

This proves `P21-MOMENT-SPACE-INTERP-46`.

### Interpretation

The interpolation is not between high-dimensional feature vectors. It is between the **two-by-two endpoint moment objects already selected by the rank-two resolvent**. That is why it does not pay `m`, `m^2`, or `dm` multiplicities and why it cannot accidentally destroy the shared latent.

`Cclean=B S_0 B`.

For the current training block define

`R=(1/N) X Cclean X^T`,

`A=(I_2+Q)^(-1)`.

The exact rank-two optimizer update gives

`Gamma_i=(1/N)[2 e^T A h + e^T A R A e]`.

This identity is separate from the optimized training value.

## 5.2 Frozen clean functional

For a model `M=A,G`, define conditional moment objects

`C_M=E[e_M e_M^T |E_-]`,

`M_M=E[h_M e_M^T |E_-]`,

`Qbar_M=E[Q_M|E_-]`,

`Rbar_M=E[R_M|E_-]`.

With

`Abar_M=(I_2+Qbar_M)^(-1)`,

`Psi_M = 2 Tr(Abar_M M_M) + Tr(Abar_M Rbar_M Abar_M C_M)`.

The first identity follows from

`e^T A h = Tr(A h e^T)`.

## 5.3 Exact moment-space interpolation derivative

Interpolate linearly:

`Q_s=Q_0+s DeltaQ`,

`R_s=R_0+s DeltaR`,

`C_s=C_0+s DeltaC`,

`M_s=M_0+s DeltaM`,

and `A_s=(I+Q_s)^(-1)`.

Define

`Psi(s)=2Tr(A_s M_s)+Tr(A_s R_s A_s C_s)`.

Differentiation gives exactly

`Psi'(s)`

`= 2Tr(A_s DeltaM) - 2Tr(A_s DeltaQ A_s M_s)`

`  + Tr(A_s DeltaR A_s C_s) + Tr(A_s R_s A_s DeltaC)`

`  - Tr(A_s DeltaQ A_s R_s A_s C_s)`

`  - Tr(A_s R_s A_s DeltaQ A_s C_s)`.

Assume along the interpolation

`Tr(C_s)<=K_C`, `||R_s||op<=K_R`, `||M_s||F<=K_M`.

Because `||A_s||op<=1` and the block dimension is two,

`|Psi'(s)|`

`<= 2sqrt(2)||DeltaM||F`

`   + 2sqrt(2)(K_M+K_R K_C)||DeltaQ||op`

`   + K_C||DeltaR||op`

`   + sqrt(2)K_R||DeltaC||F`.

Thus the frozen clean comparison is dimension-free once the four endpoint moment mismatches are controlled.

Uniform fixed-ridge moment bounds give finite `K_C,K_R,K_M` in `L^q` for fixed `q`.

`E|Psi_A-Psi_G|=O(d^(-1/2))`.

The first clean term is frozen using

`E|e^T(A-Abar)h|`

`<= (E||e||^2||h||^2)^(1/2)(E||A-Abar||op^2)^(1/2)`

`=O(d^(-1/2))`.

For the quadratic term use the deterministic stability estimate

`||ARA-Abar Rbar Abar||`

`<= ||R-Rbar|| + (||R||+||Rbar||)||A-Abar||`.

`E||A-Abar||F^2=O(d^(-1))`,

# 6. New no-go theorem `P21-VIEWWISE-LEVERAGE-NOGO-46`

This section gives an exact leading-order certificate showing why the two augmentations must remain one dependence block.

## 6.1 One `tanh` feature coordinate

Fix a feature column `f` with radius

`R=||f||`.

Put

`tau=sqrt(1+Delta^2)`.

For one original sample define

`G_1=f^T(a+Delta z_1)`,

`G_2=f^T(a+Delta z_2)`.

Conditional on `f`, `(G_1,G_2)` is centered bivariate Gaussian with

`Var(G_j)=tau^2 R^2`,

and standardized correlation

`rho=1/tau^2>0`.

Let

`U_j=tanh(G_j)`.

Define

`kappa(r)=E sech^2(tau r Z)`, `Z~N(0,1)`.

For the orthonormal Hermite coefficient

`c_1(r)=E[Z tanh(tau r Z)] = tau r kappa(r)`

by Gaussian integration by parts.

Mehler's formula gives

`Cov(U_1,U_2|f)=sum_{k odd>=1} c_k(R)^2 rho^k`.

Every term is nonnegative because `rho>0`, so

`Cov(U_1,U_2|f) >= c_1(R)^2 rho = R^2 kappa(R)^2`.

On the compact radius interval `R in [1/2,3/2]`, continuity and positivity of `kappa` imply

`c_*(Delta):=min_{r in [1/2,3/2]} r^2 kappa(r)^2 > 0`.

Therefore

`Cov(U_1,U_2|f) >= c_*(Delta)`

on that event.

## 6.2 Independent marginal view replacement destroys an order-one covariance

Now construct the naive viewwise surrogate that preserves the marginal law of each `U_j` but makes the two views independent conditional on `f`. Because `tanh` is odd and the marginal preactivation is centered,

`E U_j=0`.

Hence the independent-view cross covariance is exactly zero.

The actual-versus-viewwise cross-covariance discrepancy is therefore at least `c_*(Delta)` whenever `R in [1/2,3/2]`.

This is not a small `d^(-1/2)` defect for one coordinate; it is order one.

## 6.3 Order-one mismatch in the two-by-two leverage mean

The failure remains visible after forming the rank-two object used by Woodbury.

Take the bounded PSD probe

`B=I_m`.

Then

`Q_12=(1/N)x_1^T x_2`.

Conditional on `F`, the actual mean is

`Qbar_12^A=(1/N) sum_{r=1}^m Cov(U_{1r},U_{2r}|f_r)`.

Under the independent marginal view replacement,

`Qbar_12^V=0`.

Since the Gaussian feature-column radii are iid and concentrate at one, the fraction of columns with

`R_r in [1/2,3/2]`

tends to one in probability. Therefore

`Qbar_12^A-Qbar_12^V`

`>= (m/N)(1-o_P(1)) c_*(Delta)`

and, because

`m/N -> beta/(2 alpha)`,

we obtain

`liminf_P (Qbar_12^A-Qbar_12^V) >= [beta/(2 alpha)] c_*(Delta) > 0`.

Thus a separate-view marginal replacement is not even asymptotically matched at the off-diagonal leverage-mean level for this bounded PSD probe.

This is the exact dependence certificate requested by the target:

- the common original latent creates an order-one two-view covariance;
- separate-view replacement removes it;
- the mismatch survives the `m/N=Theta(1)` leverage normalization;
- only a **whole-original-sample correlated replacement** can be correct.

The theorem does not assert that the actual leave Hessian equals `I`; it proves something stronger about the proposed route: any generic marginal-only/viewwise comparison theorem that is supposed to control bounded PSD probes is false already for `B=I`.

## 6.4 Numerical smoke test only

This proves `P21-VIEWWISE-LEVERAGE-NOGO-46`.

# 8. Block Lindeberg versus interpolation versus resolvents

The three methods play different roles and are compatible once the dependence block is chosen correctly.

## 8.1 Whole-block Lindeberg is the outer proof skeleton

The hybrid sequence replaces one entire original-sample block at a time. This is the only replacement scale that keeps

`(t_i,x_i1,x_i2)`

and their common latent together.

The exact `1/(2N)` Woodbury increment is available at every hybrid step, so total-error bookkeeping is transparent.

## 8.2 Moment-space interpolation is the correct inner interpolation

A source-latent interpolation would differentiate nonlinear features, the rank-two leverage, and potentially optimizer response terms. A feature-coordinate interpolation risks paying ambient multiplicities before Hermite cancellation becomes visible.

For the squared-ridge target, none of that is necessary. After fixed-rank freezing, the statistic depends only on a finite list of conditional moments. Sections 4 and 5 interpolate **those moment objects directly**.

This is exact, finite-dimensional, and preserves the endpoint shared-latent covariance information.

## 8.3 Resolvents are local, not global

Resolvents are indispensable locally:

- `B=H_-^(-1)` gives fixed-ridge coercivity `||B||<=lambda^(-1)`;
- rank-two Woodbury gives the exact current-block optimized-value increment;
- `A(Q)=(I_2+Q)^(-1)` gives the exact derivative `A'=-A DeltaQ A` and one-Lipschitz endpoint stability on the PSD cone.

A global nonlinear Gram resolvent universality theorem is unnecessary. The random-radius covariance perturbation can be order one in full Frobenius norm while every normalized/probed scalar entering the objective and clean risk is only `O(d^(-1/2))`.

Let `B,B'` be iid Bernoulli(`p`). Let

`P(B)=u_B e_1^*`

### Independent derivation

If `B=B'`, then `P(B)-P(B')=0`.

If `B!=B'`, then

`P(B)-P(B')=(u_B-u_B')e_1^*`.

Because `e_1,e_2` are orthonormal,

`||u_1-u_0||=sqrt(2)`,

The mismatch probability is

`P(B!=B')=2p(1-p)`.

Therefore

`P{||P-P_0||op>=1}=2p(1-p) ~ 2p`,

while

`E||P-P_0||F^2 = 2 * 2p(1-p) = 4p(1-p) -> 0`.
