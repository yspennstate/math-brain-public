## 2. Exact model and normalization

Let
\[
N=2n.
\]
For every original sample \(i\), let
\[
a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,I_d),
\qquad
 t_i=\xi^\top a_i,
\qquad
\|\xi\|=1.
\]
The two nonlinear augmented feature rows are
\[
x_{ij}=\sigma\!\left(F^\top(a_i+\Delta z_{ij})\right)\in\mathbb R^m,
\qquad j=1,2.
\]
The squared-ridge objective is exactly
\[
\boxed{
L_A(w)=\frac1{4n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^2
(t_i-x_{ij}^\top w)^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2
=\frac1{2N}\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
If older P04 notation instead writes
\[
J(w)=\frac1N\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac{\lambda_{04}}2\|w\|^2,
\]
then the same minimizer corresponds to
\[
\boxed{\lambda_{04}=2\lambda},
\qquad
\boxed{\Gamma=N\lambda}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For an odd activation, \(\mu_0=0\). Let \(z,v,v_1,v_2\) be iid standard Gaussians and
\[
U=z+\Delta v,
\qquad
U_j=z+\Delta v_j.
\]
Define
\[
\mu_e=\mathbb E[z\sigma(U)]=\mathbb E\sigma'(U),
\qquad
\mu_b=\mathbb E[v\sigma(U)]=\Delta\,\mathbb E\sigma'(U),
\tag{2.3}
\]
\[
\mu_2^2=\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_e^2,
\tag{2.4}
\]
\[
\mu_3^2=\mathbb E\sigma(U)^2-
\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_b^2.
\tag{2.5}
\]
The matched correlated Gaussian pair is
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde x_{ij}
=\mu_eF^\top a_i
+\mu_bF^\top z_{ij}
+\mu_2 b_i
+\mu_3 p_{ij},
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
where one \(b_i\sim N(0,I_m)\) is **shared by the two augmentations** and \(p_{i1},p_{i2}\) are private iid \(N(0,I_m)\). The two augmentations must always be replaced as one block; replacing them separately would destroy the common \(a_i\), common teacher scalar \(t_i\), and common surrogate residual \(b_i\).

## 3. Exact two-row leave-one-original-sample formula

Remove the complete two-row block \(i\). Write \(X_i\in\mathbb R^{2\times m}\) for its feature rows and \(y_i=t_i\mathbf1_2\). Let
\[
H_-=
\lambda I+
\frac1N\sum_{r\ne i}X_r^\top X_r,
\qquad
w_-=\arg\min L_{-i}(w),
\tag{3.1}
\]
\[
e=y_i-X_iw_-,
\qquad
Q=\frac1N X_iH_-^{-1}X_i^\top.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
L_i^*=L_-^*+
\frac1{2N}
 e^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}e.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
This is exact. To verify it, write \(w=w_-+\delta\). The increment is
\[
\frac12\delta^\top H_-\delta
+\frac1{2N}\|e-X_i\delta\|^2.
\]
The minimizer obeys
\[
\left(H_-+\frac1N X_i^\top X_i\right)\delta
=\frac1N X_i^\top e,
\]
and Woodbury gives (3.3).

The current block enters through six scalars:
\[
(t,u_1,u_2,q_{11},q_{12},q_{22}),
\qquad
u_j=x_{ij}^\top w_-.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Hence a CLT for only \((t,u_1,u_2)\) is not sufficient unless the leverage matrix \(Q\) is separately controlled.

## 4. Conditional covariance matching, with dependence preserved

Condition on \(F\) and on the usual high-probability event
\[
\|F\|_{\rm op}\le C,
\qquad
\max_r\big|\|f_r\|-1\big|
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}},
\qquad
\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
For view indices \(a,b\in\{1,2\}\), let
\[
C_{ab}^A=\mathbb E[x_{ia}x_{ib}^\top\mid F],
\qquad
C_{ab}^G=\mathbb E[\widetilde x_{ia}\widetilde x_{ib}^\top\mid F].
\]
For two feature coordinates \(r\ne s\), the preactivation covariance is
\[
c_{ab}f_r^\top f_s,
\qquad
c_{ab}=\begin{cases}
1+\Delta^2,&a=b,\\
1,&a\ne b.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Define
\[
a_r=\mathbb E\sigma'\!\left(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}\,\|f_r\|Z\right).
\]
Price differentiation in the covariance parameter gives
\[
\mathbb E[\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)]
=(c_{ab}f_r^\top f_s)a_ra_s
+O(|f_r^\top f_s|^3).
\tag{4.3}
\]
The quadratic term vanishes: for odd \(\sigma\), \(\sigma''\) is odd, so its centered Gaussian expectation is zero. Bounded \(\sigma'''\) makes the cubic remainder uniform.

Let \(A=\operatorname{diag}(a_r)\). Since
\[
\max_r|a_r-\mu_e|
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right),
\]
\[
\|AF^\top FA-\mu_e^2F^\top F\|_{\rm op}
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right).
\tag{4.4}
\]
The cubic off-diagonal remainder satisfies the deterministic row-sum estimate
\[
\|R\|_{\rm op}
\le
C m\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|^3
=O\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]
The diagonal terms differ from their unit-column surrogate values only by the smooth norm error from (4.1). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\max_{a,b\le2}
\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\rm op}
\le\varepsilon_d,
\qquad
\varepsilon_d
=O\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For the teacher cross moment, Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
\mathbb E[t_i x_{ij,r}\mid F]
=(\xi^\top f_r)a_r,
\]
whereas the surrogate gives \(\mu_e\xi^\top f_r\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\|c_j^A-c_j^G\|_2
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
The common \(a_i\) is never conditioned away inside the pair: it remains the shared source of the two augmented rows and the teacher scalar.

## 5. Leverage freezing under the hybrid base Hessian

In each hybrid swap, remove the entire current original-sample block first. Conditional on \(F\) and all other blocks, \(H_-\) and \(w_-\) are fixed and independent of the current \((a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2})\), with
\[
H_-\succeq\lambda I.
\]
For
\[
q_{ab}=\frac1N x_{ia}^\top H_-^{-1}x_{ib},
\]
the gradient in the current Gaussian latent variables obeys
\[
\|\nabla q_{ab}\|
\le C\frac{\sqrt m}{N\lambda}
=O(d^{-1/2}).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Gaussian Poincare therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(q_{ab}\mid F,H_-)=O(d^{-1})
\tag{5.2}
\]
for the nonlinear block; the Gaussian surrogate has the same scale from the exact quadratic/bilinear variance formula.

The conditional means satisfy
\[
\bar q_{ab}^M
=\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}(H_-^{-1}C_{ab}^M),
\]
so
\[
|\bar q_{ab}^A-\bar q_{ab}^G|
\le
\frac{m}{N\lambda}
\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\rm op}
=O(\varepsilon_d).
\tag{5.3}
\]
Ridge coercivity gives
\[
\sup_d\mathbb E\|w_-\|^4<\infty.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Conditional on \(F,w_-\), the logit \(x_{ij}^\top w_-\) is a centered Lipschitz Gaussian functional with fourth moment controlled by \(1+\|w_-\|^4\). Thus multiplying the \(O(d^{-1/2})\) leverage fluctuation by the residual square is uniformly integrable.

## 6. The restricted squared-ridge simplification

Define
\[
\Psi(t,u,Q)
=(t\mathbf1_2-u)^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}(t\mathbf1_2-u).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Since \(Q\succeq0\),
\[
\|(I_2+Q)^{-1}\|\le1,
\]
and
\[
|\Psi(e,Q)-\Psi(e,\bar Q)|
\le \|e\|^2\|Q-\bar Q\|.
\tag{6.2}
\]
After \(Q\) is frozen, \(\Psi\) is exactly a quadratic polynomial in
\[
(t,u_1,u_2).
\]
Its conditional expectation is therefore determined entirely by first and second moments. Equations (4.6), (4.7), (5.2), and (5.3) imply
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E_i\Psi_A-
\mathbb E_i\Psi_G
\right|
\le
C(1+\|w_-\|^2)(\varepsilon_d+d^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
After averaging over the leave-block sigma-field, this is \(O(\varepsilon_d)\).

This is why a genuine 3-dimensional correlated Stein theorem is not needed in the present restricted problem. Hu–Lu's more general loss produces a nonlinear leave-one-out envelope and really does require a CLT/Stein step. Here, the exact quadratic objective collapses the local comparison to second moments once the leverage matrix has concentrated.

## 7. Total training replacement error

Let \(H_k\) be the hybrid problem in which the first \(k\) original-sample blocks are Gaussian-surrogate blocks and the remaining \(n-k\) are nonlinear. For \(\varphi\in C_b^2(\mathbb R)\), condition on the leave-block sigma-field for the \(k\)-th swap. From (3.3),
\[
L_k^*=C+\frac1{2N}\Psi_k.
\]
A second-order Taylor expansion of \(\varphi\) gives
\[
\left|
\mathbb E\varphi(L_k^*)-
\mathbb E\varphi(L_{k-1}^*)
\right|
\le
\frac{C\|\varphi'\|_\infty}{N}\varepsilon_d
+
\frac{C\|\varphi''\|_\infty}{N^2}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Since \(n=N/2\), summing all original-sample swaps yields
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E\varphi(L_A^*)-
\mathbb E\varphi(L_G^*)
\right|
\le
C_\varphi\left(\varepsilon_d+\frac1N\right)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
The raw conditional block discrepancy is indeed \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), but it enters the optimized value multiplied by \(1/(2N)\). The frequently feared accumulation \(n\varepsilon_d\) is therefore a normalization error, not a real obstruction.

## 8. Clean-test risk: corrected proportional ghost-block proof

### 8.1 Why a single test sample is not the right perturbation

For one clean test pair \((t_0,x_0)\), a perturbation
\[
\frac\tau2(t_0-x_0^\top w)^2
\]
adds Hessian \(\tau x_0x_0^\top\). Since \(\|x_0\|^2=\Theta(m)=\Theta(d)\), a fixed negative \(\tau\) need not preserve dimension-free strong convexity. A literal one-sample two-sided perturbation is therefore the wrong scale.

### 8.2 Independent proportional clean block

Take an independent clean test block of size
\[
M=\lfloor\kappa d\rfloor,
\qquad \kappa>0.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Conditional on the shared feature matrix \(F\), it is independent of the training sample.

Actual clean row:
\[
x_r^{A,0}=\sigma(F^\top a_r^0),
\qquad
t_r^0=\xi^\top a_r^0.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Matched clean Gaussian row:
\[
x_r^{G,0}
=\mu_{1s}F^\top a_r^0+\mu_{2s}p_r^0,
\tag{8.3}
\]
where
\[
\mu_{1s}=\mathbb E[Z\sigma(Z)],
\qquad
\mu_{2s}^2=\mathbb E\sigma(Z)^2-\mu_{1s}^2.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Define
\[
\widehat R_M^M(w)
=\frac1M\sum_{r=1}^M
(t_r^0-(x_r^{M,0})^\top w)^2,
\qquad M\in\{A,G\},
\tag{8.5}
\]
and the perturbed optimized value
\[
\Phi_M(\tau)
=\min_w\left\{
L_M(w)+\frac\tau2\widehat R_M^M(w)
\right\}.
\tag{8.6}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), the optimizer depends only on the training data and \(F\), hence is independent of the ghost block.

### 8.3 Uniform stability, including negative perturbations

Conditional on the good \(F\)-event, the actual and Gaussian clean rows have covariance operators bounded by a constant. Standard subgaussian sample-covariance concentration gives, with probability \(1-e^{-cd}\),
\[
\left\|\frac1M X_T^\top X_T\right\|_{\rm op}\le C_T,
\qquad
\left\|\frac1M X_T^\top t_T\right\|\le C_T
\tag{8.7}
\]
for either endpoint model and for every row-by-row hybrid after coupling the two endpoint test blocks.

There is also a uniform row-level bound needed below. For the actual rows,
\[
\|x_r^{A,0}\|^2\le m\|\sigma\|_\infty^2
\]
deterministically. For the Gaussian rows, a union bound over \(M\asymp d\) Gaussian/subgaussian norm tails gives
\[
\max_{r\le M}\frac{\|x_r^{G,0}\|^2}{M}\le C_x
\tag{8.8}
\]
with probability \(1-e^{-cd}\). The same bound controls every test hybrid.

Choose
\[
0<h_0<\min\left\{\frac{\lambda}{4C_T},\frac{\lambda}{4C_x}\right\}.
\tag{8.9}
\]
For every \(|\tau|\le h_0\), every hybrid Hessian is at least \(\lambda I/2\). If one test row is removed and
\[
Q_r=\frac1M x_r^\top H_-^{-1}x_r,
\]
then on the same event
\[
0\le Q_r\le \frac{2C_x}{\lambda}.
\tag{8.10}
\]
Consequently, for \(|\tau|\le h_0\),
\[
\boxed{1+\tau Q_r\ge\frac12.}
\tag{8.11}
\]
This explicitly closes the negative-\(\tau\) denominator issue.

### 8.4 Training swaps with the test perturbation present

For fixed \(|\tau|\le h_0\), the normalized test quadratic is part of the common leave-training-block base objective. Its Hessian remains at least \(\lambda I/2\), so Sections 3–7 repeat with constants depending on \(\lambda\) and \(h_0\). Therefore all \(n\) training-block swaps still cost
\[
O(\varepsilon_d+N^{-1})
\tag{8.12}
\]
for a fixed smooth test of \(\Phi(\tau)\), uniformly over \(|\tau|\le h_0\).

### 8.5 Exact test-row update

Now hold the training surrogate fixed and replace the test rows one at a time. Remove row \(r\), let \(w_-\) be the leave-test-row optimizer, and define
\[
e=t_r^0-x_r^\top w_-,
\qquad
Q_r=\frac1M x_r^\top H_-^{-1}x_r.
\]
The exact weighted rank-one formula is
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r(\tau)-\Phi_{-r}(\tau)
=
\frac{\tau}{2M}\frac{e^2}{1+\tau Q_r}.
}
\tag{8.13}
\]

The clean \(\Delta=0\) covariance comparison gives
\[
\|C_0^A-C_0^G\|_{\rm op}
+\|c_0^A-c_0^G\|_2
\le C\varepsilon_d.
\tag{8.14}
\]
Conditional on the leave-row base,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Q_r^M)=O(d^{-1}),
\qquad
|\bar Q_r^A-\bar Q_r^G|=O(\varepsilon_d).
\tag{8.15}
\]
The first estimate follows exactly as in the training block: for the nonlinear row,
\[
\|\nabla Q_r\|
\le
C\frac{\sqrt m}{M}=O(d^{-1/2}),
\]
while the Gaussian surrogate follows from its quadratic-form variance formula.

Ridge coercivity under \(|\tau|\le h_0\) gives
\[
\sup_d\mathbb E\|w_-\|^4<\infty,
\qquad
\mathbb E[e^4\mid\mathcal F_-]
\le C(1+\|w_-\|^4).
\tag{8.16}
\]
By (8.11), for \(q,q'\in[0,2C_x/\lambda]\),
\[
\left|
\frac{e^2}{1+\tau q}-
\frac{e^2}{1+\tau q'}
\right|
\le C|\tau|e^2|q-q'|.
\tag{8.17}
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz with (8.15)–(8.16) therefore gives the conservative leverage-freezing error
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\left|
\frac{e^2}{1+\tau Q_r}-
\frac{e^2}{1+\tau\bar Q_r}
\right|
\middle|\mathcal F_-
\right]
\le
C|\tau|(1+\|w_-\|^2)d^{-1/2}.
\tag{8.18}
\]
After freezing \(Q_r\), the numerator \(e^2\) is quadratic in \((t_r^0,x_r^\top w_-)\), so (8.14) and the mean comparison in (8.15) imply
\[
\left|
\mathbb E_A\frac{e^2}{1+\tau\bar Q_r^A}
-
\mathbb E_G\frac{e^2}{1+\tau\bar Q_r^G}
\right|
\le C(1+\|w_-\|^2)\varepsilon_d.
\tag{8.19}
\]
Combining (8.13), (8.18), and (8.19), then averaging over the leave-row sigma-field, yields the per-row first-order discrepancy
\[
O\!\left(
\frac{|\tau|\varepsilon_d}{M}
+
\frac{\tau^2}{M\sqrt d}
\right).
\tag{8.20}
\]
Summing all \(M\) test rows gives
\[
\boxed{
O\!\left(
|\tau|\varepsilon_d+
\frac{\tau^2}{\sqrt d}
\right).
}
\tag{8.21}
\]

**Quantitative repair.** The previous P21 report displayed \(O(|\tau|\varepsilon_d+\tau^2/M)\). That sharper second term does not follow from the stated \(O(d^{-1/2})\) leverage concentration alone. It may be recoverable from a more delicate covariance calculation, but it is unnecessary. Equation (8.21) is directly justified and still vanishes uniformly for fixed \(\tau\).

For a bounded smooth test of the **two-dimensional vector** \((\Phi(h),\Phi(-h))\), the second-order Taylor remainder of each test-row replacement is \(O(M^{-2})\) after the \(h^{-2}\) derivative scaling is canceled by the \(O(h^2/M^2)\) squared increment. Summing the \(M\) rows adds
\[
O(M^{-1}).
\tag{8.22}
\]

### 8.6 Envelope derivatives

Let
\[
S_T=\frac1M X_T^\top X_T,
\qquad
r_T=\frac1M X_T^\top t_T.
\]
Then
\[
\nabla\widehat R_M(w)=2(S_Tw-r_T),
\qquad
\nabla^2\widehat R_M=2S_T.
\tag{8.23}
\]
On the stability event, \(\|S_T\|+\|r_T\|\le C\), and strong convexity plus ridge coercivity give
\[
\sup_{|\tau|\le h_0}\|w_\tau\|=O_{L^4}(1).
\tag{8.24}
\]
The envelope theorem yields
\[
\Phi_M'(\tau)=\frac12\widehat R_M(w_\tau),
\tag{8.25}
\]
and differentiating the first-order condition gives
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_M''(\tau)
=-\frac14
\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau)^\top
H_\tau^{-1}
\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau).
}
\tag{8.26}
\]
Hence
\[
\sup_{|\tau|\le h_0}|\Phi_M''(\tau)|=O_{L^2}(1).
\tag{8.27}
\]
For the centered finite difference
\[
D_h\Phi_M
=\frac{\Phi_M(h)-\Phi_M(-h)}{2h},
\]
we therefore have
\[
2D_h\Phi_M-
\widehat R_M(w_M^*)=O_{L^1}(h).
\tag{8.28}
\]

### 8.7 Ghost empirical risk to clean population risk

At \(\tau=0\), the training optimizer \(w_M^*\) is independent of the ghost block conditional on \((F,\text{training})\). The clean squared losses are iid with conditional second moments bounded by \(C(1+\|w_M^*\|^4)\). Therefore
\[
\widehat R_M(w_M^*)-
R_M(w_M^*\mid F)
=O_{L^1}(M^{-1/2}),
\tag{8.29}
\]
where \(R_A\) uses a fresh nonlinear clean row and \(R_G\) uses a fresh matched clean Gaussian row.

### 8.8 Explicit bounded-smooth clean-risk comparison

Let \(g\in C_b^2(\mathbb R)\). Apply the hybrid comparison to the smooth function of the pair
\[
G_h(x_+,x_-)=g\!\left(\frac{x_+-x_-}{h}\right).
\]
Its first derivatives are \(O(h^{-1})\) and second derivatives are \(O(h^{-2})\). Combining training swaps, test-row swaps, the finite-difference bias, and ghost-block concentration gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\mathbb E g(R_A(w_A^*\mid F))-
\mathbb E g(R_G(w_G^*\mid F))
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
C_g\left[
\frac{\varepsilon_d}{h}
+
\frac1{Nh^2}
+
\varepsilon_d
+
\frac{h}{\sqrt d}
+
\frac1M
+

## 10. Route comparison

### Block Lindeberg / leave-one-original-sample-block-out — succeeds

This is the preferred proof. It keeps the shared latent structure intact, exposes the \(2\times2\) leverage matrix, and makes the crucial \(1/N\) normalization explicit.

### Correlated Stein in \((t,u_1,u_2)\) — valid but stronger than needed here

For a general smooth convex loss, a nonlinear leave-one-out envelope would require a quantitative joint Gaussian comparison, and Hu–Lu's Stein/Lindeberg machinery is the right precedent. In squared ridge, after leverage freezing, the current block is quadratic; exact second-moment matching is shorter and yields a stronger error accounting.

### Continuous interpolation — no advantage

Interpolating one block continuously and differentiating the optimized value recreates third-derivative/Stein terms unless one uses the same quadratic simplification. It adds bookkeeping without improving the rate.

### Global resolvent comparison — unnecessarily strong

The nonlinear and Gaussian-surrogate full Gram matrices need not be close in operator norm under any natural coupling. The Woodbury block formula is the local resolvent identity at exactly the scale needed.

### One fixed-weight clean test sample is not a stable two-sided perturbation

### The previous \(O(\tau^2/M)\) ghost-row remainder was over-claimed from the available estimate

The stated variance \(\operatorname{Var}(Q_r)=O(d^{-1})\) directly yields the conservative total \(O(\tau^2/\sqrt d)\), not \(O(\tau^2/M)\), by Cauchy–Schwarz. A sharper \(1/M\) term may hold after additional covariance analysis, but it is unnecessary and is not used in the repaired theorem.
