## 1. Exact objective normalization

The Dhifallah–Lu noisy-data objective with two augmentations is

\[
L(w)=\frac1{4n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^2(y_i-x_{ij}^Tw)^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
\]

With \(N=2n\),

\[
L(w)=\frac1{2N}\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
\]

The older P04 report used

\[
J(w)=N^{-1}\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac{\lambda_{P04}}2\|w\|^2.
\]

These have the same optimizer when \(\lambda_{P04}=2\lambda\). Thus the older P04 dual shift \(\Gamma=N\lambda_{P04}/2\) is exactly \(N\lambda\) in the paper normalization. This factor-of-two mapping must be retained in all later resolvent calculations.

## 2. Exact leave-one-original-sample block formula

Let block \(i\) contain the two feature rows \(x_1,x_2\), collected in \(X_i\in\mathbb R^{2\times m}\), and duplicated label \(b=t\mathbf1_2\). Remove the whole block and define

\[
L_{-i}(w)=\frac1{2N}\sum_{r\ne i}\|b_r-X_rw\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
\]

Let \(w_-\) minimize \(L_{-i}\) and let

\[
H_-=\lambda I+\frac1N\sum_{r\ne i}X_r^TX_r,\qquad e=b-X_iw_-,
\]

\[
Q=\frac1N X_iH_-^{-1}X_i^T.
\]

Then exactly

\[
L_i^*=L_{-i}^*+\frac1{2N}e^T(I_2+Q)^{-1}e. \tag{B1}
\]

Proof: write \(w=w_-+\delta\). The increment is

\[
\frac12\delta^TH_-\delta+\frac1{2N}\|e-X_i\delta\|^2.
\]

Minimizing this quadratic and applying Woodbury gives (B1). No approximation is used.

This formula reveals an important issue with the P04 reduction. The exact current-block functional depends on six scalar quantities

\[
(t,u_1,u_2,q_{11},q_{12},q_{22}),
\]

where \(u_j=x_j^Tw_-\) and \(q_{ab}=N^{-1}x_a^TH_-^{-1}x_b\). A CLT for only \((t,u_1,u_2)\) is therefore not sufficient unless \(Q\) is also controlled.

## 3. Matched cGEC covariance lemma

Condition on the Gaussian feature matrix \(F\). For a fresh original sample \(g\) and independent perturbations \(z_1,z_2\),

\[
x_j=\sigma(F^T(g+\Delta z_j)),\qquad j=1,2.
\]

Assume \(\sigma\) is bounded, odd, \(C^3\), with bounded first three derivatives (tanh satisfies this). Write \(f_r\) for the columns of \(F\). On the standard high-probability Gaussian-\(F\) event,

\[
\|F\|_{op}=O(1),\quad \max_r|\|f_r\|-1|=O(\sqrt{\log d/d}),
\]

\[
\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^Tf_s|=O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
\]

Let

\[
a_0=\mathbb E\sigma'(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}\,Z).
\]

The matched cGEC surrogate has the usual shared linear term \(a_0F^Tg\), augmentation-specific linear terms \(a_0\Delta F^Tz_j\), and shared/private independent Gaussian residuals chosen to match same-coordinate variance and same-coordinate cross-view covariance.

For \(r\ne s\), the pair of preactivations is bivariate Gaussian. Price’s theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E[\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)]=c\,\mathbb E\sigma'(X)\mathbb E\sigma'(Y)+O(c^3),
\]

where \(c=\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\). The quadratic term vanishes because \(\sigma\) is odd, hence \(\sigma''\) is odd and has zero Gaussian mean. For the same view \(c=(1+\Delta^2)f_r^Tf_s\); across the two views \(c=f_r^Tf_s\).

Let \(a_r=\mathbb E\sigma'(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}\|f_r\|Z)\). Smoothness gives

\[
\max_r|a_r-a_0|=O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
\]

The leading off-diagonal covariance discrepancy has matrix form controlled by

\[
AF^TFA-a_0^2F^TF,\qquad A=\operatorname{diag}(a_r),
\]

whose operator norm is \(O(\sqrt{\log d/d})\). The cubic remainder has maximum row sum

\[
O\!\left(m\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^Tf_s|^3\right)
=O((\log d)^{3/2}/\sqrt d).
\]

Diagonal same-view and same-coordinate cross-view terms vary smoothly with \(\|f_r\|\) and therefore contribute only \(O(\sqrt{\log d/d})\). Consequently, for \(a,b\in\{1,2\}\),

\[
\|C_{ab}^{act}-C_{ab}^{sur}\|_{op}
=O_p((\log d)^{3/2}/\sqrt d). \tag{B2}
\]

For the unit-norm linear teacher \(t=\xi^Tg\), Gaussian integration by parts gives

\[
\mathbb E[t x_{j,r}\mid F]
=(\xi^Tf_r)\mathbb E\sigma'(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}\|f_r\|Z).
\]

Thus the teacher-feature cross vector differs from the matched surrogate by

\[
\|c_j^{act}-c_j^{sur}\|_2=O_p(\sqrt{\log d/d}). \tag{B3}
\]

So all first/second moments of \((t,w^Tx_1,w^Tx_2)\) match uniformly for any leave-out \(w\) with bounded norm, at error

\[
\varepsilon_d=O_p((\log d)^{3/2}/\sqrt d).
\]

## 4. Leverage matrix Q concentrates

Condition on \(F\) and all other hybrid blocks. Then \(H_-\) and \(w_-\) are independent of the current \((g,z_1,z_2)\), and \(H_-\succeq\lambda I\).

For

\[
q_{ab}=\frac1N x_a^TH_-^{-1}x_b,
\]

view \(q_{ab}\) as a function of the Gaussian current-block variables. Because \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma'\) are bounded and \(\|F\|_{op}=O(1)\),

\[
\|\nabla q_{ab}\|\le C\frac{\sqrt m}{N\lambda}=O(d^{-1/2}).
\]

Gaussian Poincare/concentration therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E[(q_{ab}-\mathbb E q_{ab})^2\mid F,H_-]\le C/d. \tag{B4}
\]

The matched Gaussian surrogate has the same \(O(1/d)\) quadratic-form variance. Its conditional mean is

\[
\bar q_{ab}=\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}(H_-^{-1}C_{ab}).
\]

Using (B2),

\[
|\bar q_{ab}^{act}-\bar q_{ab}^{sur}|
\le \frac{m}{N\lambda}\|C_{ab}^{act}-C_{ab}^{sur}\|_{op}
=O_p(\varepsilon_d). \tag{B5}
\]

Thus the full 2x2 \(Q\) can be frozen to matched conditional means at \(O(d^{-1/2}+\varepsilon_d)\) cost.

## 5. Why full correlated Stein is not needed for squared ridge

Define

\[
\Psi(t,u,Q)=(t\mathbf1_2-u)^T(I_2+Q)^{-1}(t\mathbf1_2-u).
\]

Since \(Q\succeq0\), \(\|(I+Q)^{-1}\|\le1\). The resolvent identity gives

\[
|\Psi(e,Q)-\Psi(e,\bar Q)|\le \|e\|^2\|Q-\bar Q\|.
\]

Ridge coercivity gives \(\|w_-\|=O(1)\). Conditional on \(F\), \(w_-^Tx_j\) is a Lipschitz Gaussian functional with \(O(1)\) Lipschitz constant, so its fourth moments are uniformly bounded. Hence (B4)-(B5) justify freezing \(Q\) at total conditional expectation cost \(O(\varepsilon_d+d^{-1/2})\).

Once \(Q\) is frozen, \(\Psi\) is exactly a quadratic form in the 3-vector \((t,u_1,u_2)\). Therefore its conditional expectation depends only on the first two moments of that vector. Equations (B2)-(B3) imply

\[
|\mathbb E\Psi_{act}-\mathbb E\Psi_{sur}|\le C\varepsilon_d. \tag{B6}
\]

This is the key simplification: the P04 3-dimensional Stein/CLT lemma is not required for the optimized squared-ridge value. Second-moment matching suffices after the leverage matrix is separately concentrated.

## 6. Total hybrid error over n original-sample blocks

The exact block increment (B1) contains the crucial prefactor \(1/(2N)=1/(4n)\). Thus a per-block \(O(\varepsilon_d)\) error in \(\mathbb E\Psi\) becomes \(O(\varepsilon_d/N)\) in the optimized objective. Summing \(n=N/2\) hybrid swaps gives only \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), not \(O(n\varepsilon_d)\).

More precisely, for any bounded \(C^2\) scalar test \(h\), condition on the leave-block sigma-field and Taylor-expand

\[
h(C+\Psi/(2N)).
\]

The first-order comparison costs \(C\|h'\|\varepsilon_d/N\) by (B6); the second-order Taylor remainder is \(O(\|h''\|/N^2)\) because \(\Psi\) has bounded second moment. Summing \(N/2\) blocks yields

\[
|\mathbb Eh(L_{act}^*)-\mathbb Eh(L_{sur}^*)|
\le C_h(\varepsilon_d+N^{-1})\to0. \tag{B7}
\]

This proves bounded-smooth equivalence of the optimized ridge objective in the stated restricted regime, conditional only on the standard high-probability Gaussian-\(F\) event already used above. Since that event has probability tending to one, the unconditional statement follows.

I regard (B7) as a **CANDIDATE COMPLETE PROOF** of the restricted optimized-objective cGEC statement, pending independent audits of the exact residual-coefficient identification in the cGEC surrogate and every conditioning step.

## 7. Clean test prediction risk — separate argument

Objective equivalence alone does not imply test-risk equivalence. Let \(x_0=\sigma(F^Tg_0)\) be a fresh clean feature vector and \(t_0=\xi^Tg_0\). Let the matched clean surrogate use its own unaugmented Gaussian-equivalence coefficients. Define conditional risks

\[
R_A(w\mid F)=\mathbb E[(t_0-x_0^Tw)^2\mid F],
\]

\[
R_G(w\mid F)=\mathbb E[(t_0-\tilde x_0^Tw)^2\mid F].
\]

The same Price/Stein covariance calculation with \(\Delta=0\) gives, uniformly for \(\|w\|\le C\),

\[
|R_A(w\mid F)-R_G(w\mid F)|\le C\varepsilon_d. \tag{B8}
\]

To compare \(R_G\) evaluated at the two learned optimizers without illegitimately differentiating a weak-convergence statement, introduce the quadratic perturbation

\[
\Phi_M(\tau)=\min_w\{L_M(w)+\tau R_G(w\mid F)\},\qquad M\in\{A,G\}.
\]

For \(|\tau|\le\tau_0\) small enough, \(\lambda\)-strong convexity survives uniformly because the clean covariance operator is \(O(1)\). The entire block proof above remains valid uniformly at each fixed \(\tau\), with the leave-block Hessian inverse bounded by a constant depending only on \(\lambda,\tau_0\). The same hybrid argument also works jointly for a finite tuple of \(\tau\) values.

The envelope theorem gives

\[
\Phi_M'(0)=R_G(w_M^*\mid F).
\]

Moreover

\[
\Phi_M''(\tau)
=-\nabla R_G(w_\tau)^TH_\tau^{-1}\nabla R_G(w_\tau),
\]

so \(|\Phi_M''(\tau)|\le C\) uniformly on the ridge-bounded event. Hence for fixed \(h>0\),

\[
D_h\Phi_M=\frac{\Phi_M(h)-\Phi_M(-h)}{2h}
\]

satisfies

\[
|D_h\Phi_M-R_G(w_M^*)|\le Ch.
\]

Joint objective universality at \(\pm h\) transfers the law of \(D_h\Phi\). First send \(d\to\infty\) at fixed \(h\), then \(h\to0\) (or choose \(h\) slowly with \(\varepsilon_d/h\to0\)). Together with (B8), this yields bounded-Lipschitz equivalence of the actual clean squared prediction risks.

Thus the clean-test statement has a candidate complete route too; it is genuinely separate from the training-objective proof and preserves the matched train/test dependence correctly.

## 8. Audit of P04 Target-1 claim

Source: P04 Gmail message `1a09ee796dd857e4`, with normalization cross-check from `1a09e74e5cbc5e70`.

P04 claim: the main missing result is a 2-dimensional correlated Stein comparison for \((\langle\beta,v_{i1}\rangle,\langle\beta,v_{i2}\rangle)\) jointly with the teacher scalar, after leave-one-original-sample-block-out.

**Verdict: REFINED / PARTLY DISPROVED AS A MINIMAL OBLIGATION.**

The block replacement viewpoint is correct and superior to whole-column Lindeberg. However:

1. the exact block objective also depends on \(Q=N^{-1}X_iH_-^{-1}X_i^T\), so a CLT for only the three logits is not sufficient by itself;
2. \(Q\) concentrates at \(d^{-1/2}\) scale;
3. after \(Q\) is frozen, the squared-ridge block functional is quadratic in the three logits, so a full Stein CLT is unnecessary — covariance and teacher cross-moment matching suffice;
4. the \(1/(4n)\) block normalization means an \(O(\varepsilon_d)\) raw moment-comparison error per block sums to \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), not \(O(n\varepsilon_d)\).
