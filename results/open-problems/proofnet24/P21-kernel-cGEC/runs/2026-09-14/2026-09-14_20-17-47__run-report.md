# 2. Hermite replacement for the Price expansion

Put
\[
q_0=1+\Delta^2.
\]
For \(q>0\), define
\[
a(q)=q^{-1/2}E[G\sigma(\sqrt q\,G)].
\]

Let \(X,Y\) be centered jointly Gaussian with
\[
\operatorname{Var}X=q,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}Y=q',\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=c,
\]
where \(q,q'\) stay in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\).

Write
\[
\rho=\frac{c}{\sqrt{qq'}}.
\]
Expand
\[
\sigma(\sqrt q\,x)=\sum_{k\ge0}\widehat\sigma_k(q)h_k(x)
\]
in the orthonormal Hermite basis. Since \(\sigma\) is odd,
\[
\widehat\sigma_k(q)=0
\quad\text{for every even }k.
\]
Mehler's identity gives
\[
E[\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)]
=
\sum_{k\ge1,\ k\ {\rm odd}}
\widehat\sigma_k(q)\widehat\sigma_k(q')\rho^k.
\]
The first coefficient is
\[
\widehat\sigma_1(q)=E[G\sigma(\sqrt q\,G)]=\sqrt q\,a(q),
\]
so the degree-one contribution is exactly
\[
a(q)a(q')c.
\]
For the remainder,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\sum_{k\ge3,\ k\ {\rm odd}}
\widehat\sigma_k(q)\widehat\sigma_k(q')\rho^k
\right|
&\le
|\rho|^3
\sum_{k\ge3}
|\widehat\sigma_k(q)\widehat\sigma_k(q')|\\
&\le
|\rho|^3
\|\sigma(\sqrt q\,G)\|_2
\|\sigma(\sqrt{q'}\,G)\|_2\\
&\le
C_\sigma |c|^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
E[\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)]
=
a(q)a(q')c+O_\sigma(|c|^3).
}
\tag{H}
\]

This is the same cubic cancellation formerly obtained from three Price derivatives, but it uses only oddness and \(L^2\)-boundedness.

# 3. Variance and teacher coefficients without differentiability

If \(q,q'\) lie in a fixed positive compact interval and \(\sigma\) is globally Lipschitz,
\[
|a(q)-a(q')|\le C_\sigma|q-q'|.
\]
Indeed
\[
|E[G\sigma(\sqrt q\,G)]-E[G\sigma(\sqrt{q'}\,G)]|
\le
L_\sigma|\sqrt q-\sqrt{q'}|EG^2.
\]

Similarly,
\[
v(q)=E\sigma(\sqrt q\,G)^2
\]
is Lipschitz in \(q\).

For
\[
U=Z+\Delta V,
\]
Gaussian regression gives
\[
\mu_e=E[Z\sigma(U)]=a(q_0),
\qquad
\mu_b=E[V\sigma(U)]=\Delta a(q_0).
\]

For the teacher scalar \(t=\xi^\top a\) and coordinate preactivation
\[
X_r=f_r^\top(a+\Delta z),
\qquad
q_r=q_0\|f_r\|^2,
\]
Gaussian regression gives exactly
\[
E[t\sigma(X_r)]
=
(\xi^\top f_r)a(q_r).
\]
Thus the teacher cross-moment discrepancy is
\[
(D_a-\mu_eI)F^\top\xi,
\qquad
D_a=\operatorname{diag}(a(q_1),\ldots,a(q_m)),
\]
and on the standard good-\(F\) event it has norm
\[
O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
\]

The residual variances \(\mu_2^2,\mu_3^2\) remain nonnegative by Hilbert-space projection/Cauchy–Schwarz; no derivative identity is needed.

# 4. Covariance operator bound and dimension count

On the standard Gaussian-\(F\) event,
\[
G=F^\top F,\qquad
\|G\|_{\rm op}=O(1),
\]
\[
\delta_F=\max_r|\|f_r\|^2-1|
=
O(\sqrt{\log d/d}),
\]
and
\[
\eta_F=\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|
=
O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
\]

For same-view covariance the degree-one actual/surrogate mismatch is
\[
q_0(D_aGD_a-\mu_e^2G).
\]
It must be bounded structurally:
\[
D_aGD_a-\mu_e^2G
=
(D_a-\mu_eI)GD_a+\mu_eG(D_a-\mu_eI),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
\|D_aGD_a-\mu_e^2G\|_{\rm op}
=
O(\delta_F).
}
\]
There is no extra \(m\)-factor here.

By (H), the off-diagonal Hermite remainder has entries
\[
O(|g_{rs}|^3).
\]
Hence
\[
\|R\|_{\rm op}
\le
Cm\eta_F^3
=
O((\log d)^{3/2}/\sqrt d).
\]
The cross-view calculation is identical except that the shared preactivation covariance is \(g_{rs}\) rather than \(q_0g_{rs}\).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\max_{a,b\in\{1,2\}}
\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\rm op}
\le
\varepsilon_d,
\qquad
\varepsilon_d=
C_\sigma\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}.
}
\]

Exactly one \(m\)-factor appears: in the cubic remainder row sum.

# 5. Leave-one-original-sample block and leverage

\tag{W}

The two current rows are correlated through \(a_i\). They are **not** separated.

\[
q_{ab}=\frac1N x_{ia}^\top H_-^{-1}x_{ib},

\[
(a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2}).

O_\sigma(\|F\|_{\rm op}).

# 6. Why no full correlated-logit CLT is needed

After freezing the \(2\times2\) leverage matrix, the local statistic is
\[
(t\mathbf1_2-u)^\top A(t\mathbf1_2-u),
\]
where
\[
u=(x_{i1}^\top w_-,x_{i2}^\top w_-)^\top.
\]
Its expectation depends only on
\[
Et^2,\qquad E[tu_j],\qquad E[u_ju_k].
\]
Those are controlled by the covariance and teacher calculations above. Hence the unscaled local discrepancy is
\[
O(\varepsilon_d(1+\|w_-\|^2)).

A uniform Gaussian approximation of
\[

\]
over arbitrary bounded optimizer directions is unnecessary and false: for example, with \(\sigma=\tanh\) and a coordinate vector \(w\), the actual logit is bounded while the Gaussian surrogate logit is not.
