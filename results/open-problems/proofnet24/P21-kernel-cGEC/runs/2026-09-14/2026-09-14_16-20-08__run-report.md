## 1. Exact normalization and training model

Let \(N=2n\). For original sample \(i\),
\[
a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2}\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,I_d),\qquad
t_i=\xi^\top a_i,\qquad \|\xi\|=1,
\]
and
\[
x_{ij}=\sigma(F^\top(a_i+\Delta z_{ij})),\qquad j=1,2.
\]

The exact squared-ridge objective is
\[
\boxed{
L_A(w)=
\frac1{4n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^2(t_i-x_{ij}^\top w)^2
+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2
=
\frac1{2N}\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
}
\]
If older P04 notation uses \(N^{-1}\|y-Xw\|^2+\lambda_{04}\|w\|^2/2\), then the same minimizer has
\[
\lambda_{04}=2\lambda,\qquad \Gamma=N\lambda.
\]

The factor \(1/(2N)\) is the decisive factor in the accumulated Lindeberg error.

## 2. Matched two-view correlated Gaussian surrogate

Let \(U=Z+\Delta V\) and \(U_j=Z+\Delta V_j\), with iid standard normals. Define
\[
\mu_e=\mathbb E[Z\sigma(U)]=\mathbb E\sigma'(U),
\]
\[
\mu_b=\mathbb E[V\sigma(U)]=\Delta\mathbb E\sigma'(U),
\]
\[
\mu_2^2=\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_e^2,
\]
\[
\mu_3^2=
\mathbb E\sigma(U)^2-\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_b^2.
\]

The matched pair is
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde x_{ij}
=
\mu_eF^\top a_i+\mu_bF^\top z_{ij}
+\mu_2b_i+\mu_3p_{ij},
}
\]
with \(b_i\sim N(0,I_m)\) **shared by the two augmentations** and private iid \(p_{ij}\).

Hermite expansion makes the nonnegativity explicit. Put \(v=1+\Delta^2\), \(\rho=1/v\), and
\[
\sigma(\sqrt v\,Y)=\sum_{k\ {\rm odd}}a_kh_k(Y).
\]
Then \(a_1=\sqrt v\,\mu_e\), so
\[
\mu_2^2=\sum_{k\ge3,\ k\ {\rm odd}}a_k^2\rho^k\ge0,
\]
and, since \(\mu_b=\Delta\mu_e\),
\[
\mu_3^2=\sum_{k\ge3,\ k\ {\rm odd}}a_k^2(1-\rho^k)\ge0.
\]

Replacing the two augmentations separately is invalid: it destroys the shared \(a_i\), repeated teacher \(t_i\), and shared residual \(b_i\).

## 3. Exact leave-one-original-sample block identity

Remove both rows of original sample \(i\). Let
\[
H_-=\lambda I+\frac1N\sum_{r\ne i}X_r^\top X_r,
\]
let \(w_-\) be the leave-block optimizer, and define
\[
e=t_i\mathbf1_2-X_iw_-,
\qquad
Q=\frac1N X_iH_-^{-1}X_i^\top.
\]
Then exactly
\[
\boxed{
L_i^*=L_-^*+\frac1{2N}e^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}e.
}
\]
Thus the current block depends on
\[
(t,u_1,u_2,q_{11},q_{12},q_{22}),
\qquad u_j=x_{ij}^\top w_-.
\]
A logit-only theorem is insufficient before the \(2\times2\) leverage matrix \(Q\) is controlled.

## 4. Conditional covariance and teacher matching

On the standard good Gaussian-\(F\) event,
\[
\|F\|_{\rm op}=O(1),
\]
\[
\max_r|\|f_r\|-1|
=O(\sqrt{\log d/d}),
\]
\[
\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|
=O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
\]

For the two training views, Price expansion gives, for \(r\ne s\),
\[
\mathbb E[\sigma(X_r)\sigma(X_s)]
=
(c_{ab}f_r^\top f_s)a_ra_s
+O(|f_r^\top f_s|^3),
\]
with \(c_{aa}=1+\Delta^2\) and \(c_{12}=1\). Oddness of \(\sigma\) implies \(\sigma''\) is odd, so the quadratic Price coefficient vanishes. Bounded \(\sigma'''\) controls the cubic remainder.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\max_{a,b\le2}
\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\rm op}
\le
\varepsilon_d,
\qquad
\varepsilon_d
=
O((\log d)^{3/2}/\sqrt d).
}
\]
The teacher-feature cross vector satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\|c_a^A-c_a^G\|_2
=
O(\sqrt{\log d/d}).
}
\]

## 5. The literal uniform correlated-Stein/logit CLT is false

For \(\sigma=\tanh\) and deterministic \(w=e_1\),
\[
u_1^A=\tanh(f_1^\top(a+\Delta z_1))
\]
is supported on \([-1,1]\), while the matched surrogate logit is a nondegenerate Gaussian. A smooth bounded test supported outside \([-1,1]\) separates the laws by a fixed amount.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\text{A uniform full joint Gaussian limit for }(t,u_1,u_2)\text{ over arbitrary bounded }w\text{ is false.}}
\]

This does not contradict Hu–Lu's structured weak-correlation Stein argument.

For squared ridge, a full CLT is unnecessary. Conditional on the leave-block base,
\[
|\mathbb E_A[t u_a]-\mathbb E_G[t u_a]|
\le C\varepsilon_d\|w_-\|,
\]
\[
|\mathbb E_A[u_au_b]-\mathbb E_G[u_au_b]|
\le \varepsilon_d\|w_-\|^2.
\]
Thus every fixed quadratic polynomial \(Q_2(t,u_1,u_2)\) obeys
\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb E_AQ_2-\mathbb E_GQ_2|
\le C_{Q_2}\varepsilon_d(1+\|w_-\|^2).
}
\]
This degree-two moment comparison is the valid restricted substitute for a correlated Stein CLT.

## 6. Leverage freezing and total training replacement error

For
\[
q_{ab}=\frac1N x_{ia}^\top H_-^{-1}x_{ib},
\]
conditional Gaussian differentiation gives
\[
\|\nabla q_{ab}\|
\le C\frac{\sqrt m}{N\lambda}=O(d^{-1/2}),
\]
so Gaussian Poincaré yields
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(q_{ab}\mid\mathcal F_-)=O(d^{-1}).
}
\]
The actual/surrogate conditional means satisfy
\[
|\bar q_{ab}^A-\bar q_{ab}^G|
\le
\frac{m}{N\lambda}\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\rm op}
=
O(\varepsilon_d).
\]

Ridge coercivity gives
\[
\sup_d\mathbb E\|w_-\|^4<\infty.
\]

Define
\[
\Psi(t,u,Q)
=
(t\mathbf1_2-u)^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}(t\mathbf1_2-u).
\]
Since \(Q\succeq0\), freezing \(Q\) costs
\[
O((1+\|w_-\|^2)d^{-1/2})
\]
in conditional expectation. Once \(Q\) is frozen, \(\Psi\) is exactly quadratic in \((t,u_1,u_2)\), so the degree-two comparison applies:
\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb E_i\Psi_A-\mathbb E_i\Psi_G|
=
O((1+\|w_-\|^2)\varepsilon_d).
}
\]

For a bounded \(C^2\) test \(\varphi\), one original-sample block therefore contributes
\[
O(\varepsilon_d/N+N^{-2}).
\]
There are \(n=N/2\) such blocks. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb E\varphi(L_A^*)-\mathbb E\varphi(L_G^*)|
\le
C_\varphi(\varepsilon_d+N^{-1})
\to0.
}
\]
The naive \(O(n\varepsilon_d)\) accumulation is a normalization error.

## 9. Proportional independent ghost block and two-sided stability

Take
\[
M=\lfloor\kappa d\rfloor
\]
iid clean test examples independent of training. Use nonlinear rows \(x_r^{0,A}\) and matched clean Gaussian rows \(x_r^{0,G}\) as above.

Define
\[
\widehat R_M(w)
=
\frac1M\sum_{r=1}^M(t_r^0-(x_r^0)^\top w)^2,
\]
\[
\Phi_M(\tau)
=
\min_w\left\{
L_{\rm train}(w)+\frac\tau2\widehat R_M(w)
\right\}.
\]

For every deterministic \(w\), \(w^\top x^{0,A}\) is a centered Lipschitz function of \(a^0\), with Lipschitz constant at most
\[
\|F\|_{\rm op}\|\sigma'\|_\infty\|w\|.
\]
Thus clean nonlinear rows are uniformly subgaussian in every direction. The clean surrogate rows are Gaussian with covariance operator \(O(1)\). A sphere-net argument therefore gives, uniformly over rowwise actual/Gaussian test hybrids,
\[
\left\|
\frac1M X_0^\top X_0
\right\|_{\rm op}
\le C_0
\]
with probability \(1-e^{-cd}\).

Also
\[
\max_{r\le M}\frac{\|x_r^0\|^2}{M}\le C_1
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\); for actual rows this follows directly from bounded \(\sigma\), and for surrogate rows from Gaussian norm tails.

The perturbed Hessian is
\[
H_\tau
=
\lambda I+\frac1N X_{\rm tr}^\top X_{\rm tr}
+\tau\frac1M X_0^\top X_0.
\]
Choose
\[
h_0
\le
\min\left\{
\frac{\lambda}{2C_0},
\frac{\lambda}{4C_1}
\right\}.
\]
Then for \(|\tau|\le h_0\),
\[
\boxed{H_\tau\succeq\lambda I/2.}
\]

After removing one test row, define
\[
Q_r
=
\frac1M(x_r^0)^\top H_{-r,\tau}^{-1}x_r^0.
\]
For negative \(\tau\), removing the negative rank-one term only increases the Hessian, so \(H_{-r,\tau}\succeq\lambda I/2\). Hence
\[
Q_r\le2C_1/\lambda
\]
and
\[
\boxed{1+\tau Q_r\ge1/2.}
\]

The exact row update is
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r(\tau)-\Phi_{-r}(\tau)
=
\frac{\tau}{2M}
\frac{e_r^2}{1+\tau Q_r}.
}
\]

Conditional on the leave-row base,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Q_r)=O(d^{-1}),
\]
and the actual/clean-Gaussian mean mismatch is \(O(\varepsilon_d^{(0)})\).

Freezing \(Q_r\) costs, inside the bracket,
\[
O(|\tau|(1+\|w_{-r,\tau}\|^2)d^{-1/2}).
\]
Multiplying by \(|\tau|/(2M)\) and summing \(M\asymp d\) rows yields
\[
O(\tau^2/\sqrt d).
\]
The frozen quadratic moment mismatch yields
\[
O(|\tau|\varepsilon_d^{(0)}).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{summed clean ghost-row discrepancy}
=
O\!\left(
|\tau|\varepsilon_d^{(0)}
+\frac{\tau^2}{\sqrt d}
\right).
}
\]

## 10. Envelope derivatives and clean-risk extraction

The envelope identities are
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_M'(\tau)=\frac12\widehat R_M(w_\tau),
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_M''(\tau)
=
-\frac14
\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau)^\top
H_\tau^{-1}
\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau).
}
\]

Moreover
\[
\nabla\widehat R_M(w)
=
-\frac2M X_0^\top(t^0-X_0w),
\]
so
\[
\|\nabla\widehat R_M(w)\|^2
\le
4\left\|\frac1M X_0^\top X_0\right\|_{\rm op}
\widehat R_M(w).
\]
Uniform strong convexity and the normalized cross-term bounds give
\[
\sup_{|\tau|\le h_0}\mathbb E\|w_\tau\|^4<\infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|\tau|\le h_0}
|\Phi_M''(\tau)|
=
O_{L^2}(1).
}
\]

At \(\tau=0\), the training optimizer is independent of the ghost block. A centered finite difference at \(\pm h\), training-block replacement, clean-test-row replacement, and empirical-test-to-population concentration give, for \(g\in C_b^2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&
|\mathbb E g(R_A(w_A^*\mid F))
-
\mathbb E g(R_G(w_G^*\mid F))|\\
&\quad\le
C_g\left[
\frac{\varepsilon_d}{h}
+\frac1{Nh^2}
+\varepsilon_d^{(0)}
+\frac h{\sqrt d}
+\frac1M
+h
+\frac1{\sqrt M}
\right]
+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\]
With
\[
h_d=\sqrt{\varepsilon_d},
\]
all terms vanish; the slow displayed term is
\[
O((\log d)^{3/4}d^{-1/4}).
\]

Thus the nonlinear and matched-correlated-Gaussian models have asymptotically equivalent **clean** population prediction risk in bounded-smooth distribution. If the Gaussian-side risk converges to a deterministic scalar, the nonlinear model converges to the same scalar.

## 11. Route comparison and dimension traps

**Block Lindeberg** is the preferred route because it preserves the shared latent structure and keeps the \(1/N\) normalization visible.

**Uniform correlated Stein on the whole logit triple** is false for arbitrary bounded \(w\); only degree-two matching is needed here after leverage freezing.

**Continuous interpolation** adds derivative bookkeeping without improving the quadratic argument.

**Global resolvent comparison** is stronger than necessary; only the leave-block resolvent enters.

The main invalid shortcuts are:

- replace the two augmentations separately;
- sum local \(O(\varepsilon_d)\) errors without the objective factor;
- use one unnormalized test sample with fixed two-sided \(\tau\);
- use noisy-training cGEC coefficients for the clean test law;
- compare logits but ignore the leverage matrix;
- demand global Gram operator-norm closeness.
