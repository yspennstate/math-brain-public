## Executive status

The optimized-training-objective proof from the previous P21 run survives a second independent line-by-line audit. I make its conditioning and accumulated error explicit below.

The main new result is a **direct ghost-test-block proof of clean-test prediction-risk equivalence**. It avoids a subtle dimension trap in the most literal “single test sample perturbation” approach: a single test square loss has Hessian \(x_0x_0^\top\) of operator norm \(\Theta(d)\), so a fixed perturbation parameter does not have dimension-free derivative stability. The repair is to introduce an independent proportional-size clean test block, normalized by its block size. Its empirical covariance has \(O(1)\) operator norm, all \(\pm h\) perturbed objectives remain uniformly strongly convex, and the same leave-block-out comparison can be run over both training blocks and test rows. Since the training optimizer at \(h=0\) is independent of the test block, the empirical test risk concentrates around the clean population risk. This gives a clean, separate proof of prediction-risk equivalence that literally includes the matched independent test data.

No new obstruction was found for the restricted squared-ridge/linear-teacher theorem. The remaining ProofNet issue is certification by a second outside worker, not an identified mathematical gap.

## 1. Exact model and normalization

Let \(N=2n\). For original sample \(i\), let
\[
a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}\sim N(0,I_d),\qquad t_i=\xi^\top a_i,\qquad \|\xi\|=1,
\]
and define the two nonlinear feature rows
\[
x_{ij}=\sigma(F^\top(a_i+\Delta z_{ij}))\in\mathbb R^m,\qquad j=1,2.
\]
The paper objective at \(\ell=2\) is exactly
\[
L_A(w)=\frac1{4n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^2(t_i-x_{ij}^\top w)^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2
      =\frac1{2N}\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac\lambda2\|w\|^2.
\]
Thus if older P04 notation is
\[
J(w)=\frac1N\|y-Xw\|^2+\frac{\lambda_{04}}2\|w\|^2,
\]
then the same minimizer is obtained with
\[
\boxed{\lambda_{04}=2\lambda},\qquad \boxed{\Gamma=N\lambda}.
\]
This matches Dhifallah–Lu equation (3).

Because \(\sigma\) is odd, \(\mu_0=0\). Put \(U=z+\Delta v\) with independent standard Gaussians. The matched cGEC coefficients are
\[
\mu_e=\mathbb E[z\sigma(U)]=\mathbb E\sigma'(U),\qquad
\mu_b=\mathbb E[v\sigma(U)]=\Delta\,\mathbb E\sigma'(U),
\]
\[
\mu_2^2=\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_e^2,
\]
\[
\mu_3^2=\mathbb E\sigma(U)^2-\mathbb E[\sigma(U_1)\sigma(U_2)]-\mu_b^2,
\]
where \(U_j=z+\Delta v_j\). The surrogate row pair is
\[
\tilde x_{ij}=\mu_eF^\top a_i+\mu_bF^\top z_{ij}+\mu_2b_i+\mu_3p_{ij},
\]
with one **shared** \(b_i\sim N(0,I_m)\) across the two augmentations and private \(p_{ij}\sim N(0,I_m)\). This is exactly the paper's correlated Gaussian surrogate, not merely an arbitrary covariance-matched Gaussian pair.

## 2. Exact leave-one-original-sample-block formula

Remove the entire two-row block \(i\). Let \(X_i\in\mathbb R^{2\times m}\) contain its rows and \(b_i=t_i\mathbf1_2\). Write
\[
H_-:=\lambda I+\frac1N\sum_{r\ne i}X_r^\top X_r,
\qquad w_-:=\arg\min L_{-i}(w),
\]
\[
e:=b_i-X_iw_-,\qquad Q:=\frac1N X_iH_-^{-1}X_i^\top.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
L_i^*=L_{-i}^*+\frac1{2N}e^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}e.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
This is exact. It follows by writing \(w=w_-+\delta\), minimizing the resulting quadratic, and applying Woodbury.

The current block therefore enters through six scalars
\[
(t,u_1,u_2,q_{11},q_{12},q_{22}),\qquad u_j=x_{ij}^\top w_-.
\]
Hence a CLT for only \((t,u_1,u_2)\) is not sufficient without control of the leverage matrix \(Q\).

## 3. Conditional covariance matching with the shared augmentation latent preserved

Condition on \(F\). On the usual Gaussian-feature event
\[
\|F\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C,
\quad
\max_r|\|f_r\|-1|\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}},
\quad
\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}},
\tag{3.1}
\]
which has probability tending to one.

Let \(C_{ab}^A=\mathbb E[x_{ia}x_{ib}^\top\mid F]\), and let \(C_{ab}^G\) be the corresponding surrogate covariance. For off-diagonal feature coordinates \(r\ne s\), the two preactivations are bivariate Gaussian with covariance
\[
c_{ab} f_r^\top f_s,
\qquad
c_{ab}=\begin{cases}1+\Delta^2,&a=b,\\1,&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]
Their variances are \((1+\Delta^2)\|f_r\|^2\) and \((1+\Delta^2)\|f_s\|^2\).

Price differentiation in the covariance parameter gives, with
\[
a_r:=\mathbb E\sigma'\!\left(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}\,\|f_r\|Z\right),
\]
\[
\mathbb E\sigma(X)\sigma(Y)
=(c_{ab}f_r^\top f_s)a_ra_s+O(|f_r^\top f_s|^3).
\tag{3.2}
\]
There is no quadratic term because \(\sigma\) odd implies \(\sigma''\) odd, hence its centered Gaussian expectation is zero. Since \(\sigma'''\) is bounded, the cubic remainder is uniform.

With \(A=\operatorname{diag}(a_r)\) and \(\mu_e=\mathbb E\sigma'(\sqrt{1+\Delta^2}Z)\),
\[
\|AF^\top FA-\mu_e^2F^\top F\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le C\max_r|a_r-\mu_e|\,\|F\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\right).
\]
The cubic off-diagonal remainder obeys the deterministic row-sum bound
\[
\|R\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le \max_r\sum_{s\ne r}|R_{rs}|
\le C m\max_{r\ne s}|f_r^\top f_s|^3
=O\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\right).
\]
The diagonal same-view and cross-view entries differ from their unit-column cGEC values only by the smooth column-norm error in (3.1). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\max_{a,b\le2}\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le \varepsilon_d,
\qquad
\varepsilon_d=O\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{3/2}}{\sqrt d}\right).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For the teacher cross moment, Gaussian integration by parts yields
\[
\mathbb E[t_i x_{ij,r}\mid F]
=(\xi^\top f_r)a_r.
\]
The surrogate gives \(\mu_e\xi^\top f_r\), hence
\[
\boxed{
\|c_j^A-c_j^G\|_2
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
The shared latent \(a_i\) has not been broken: it is the common source of both augmented rows and of the teacher scalar throughout this comparison.

## 4. Leverage-matrix concentration

In a hybrid replacement, remove the current original-sample block first. Conditional on \(F\) and all other blocks, \(H_-\) and \(w_-\) are fixed and independent of the current \((a_i,z_{i1},z_{i2})\). Also \(H_-\succeq\lambda I\).

For
\[
q_{ab}=\frac1N x_{ia}^\top H_-^{-1}x_{ib},
\]
the gradient with respect to the current Gaussian latent variables has norm
\[
\|\nabla q_{ab}\|
\le C\frac{\sqrt m}{N\lambda}
=O(d^{-1/2}).
\]
Indeed the Jacobian of \(x_{ij}\) has operator norm at most \(\|F\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\sigma'\|_\infty\), while \(\|x_{ij}\|\le\|\sigma\|_\infty\sqrt m\). Gaussian Poincare therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(q_{ab}\mid F,H_-)=O(d^{-1}).
\tag{4.1}
\]
For the Gaussian surrogate, the same conclusion follows directly from the variance formula for Gaussian quadratic/bilinear forms.

Moreover
\[
\bar q_{ab}^M:=\mathbb E[q_{ab}^M\mid F,H_-]
=\frac1N\operatorname{Tr}(H_-^{-1}C_{ab}^M),
\]
so (3.3) implies
\[
|\bar q_{ab}^A-\bar q_{ab}^G|
\le \frac{m}{N\lambda}\|C_{ab}^A-C_{ab}^G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O(\varepsilon_d).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Thus the full \(2\times2\) leverage matrix can be frozen to matching conditional means at cost \(O(d^{-1/2}+\varepsilon_d)\).

Ridge coercivity supplies the moment bound needed to multiply this by the residual square. In the leave-block problem,
\[
\frac\lambda2\|w_-\|^2\le L_{-i}(0)=\frac1N\sum_{r\ne i}t_r^2,
\]
so \(\sup_d\mathbb E\|w_-\|^4<\infty\). Conditional on \(F,w_-\), each \(x_{ij}^\top w_-\) is a centered Lipschitz Gaussian functional with Lipschitz constant \(O(\|w_-\|)\), so its fourth moment is uniformly integrable. Hence freezing \(Q\) inside the block statistic is legitimate in expectation.

## 5. Why squared ridge needs only second moments after leverage freezing

Define
\[
\Psi(t,u,Q):=(t\mathbf1_2-u)^\top(I_2+Q)^{-1}(t\mathbf1_2-u).
\]
Because \(Q\succeq0\), \(\|(I+Q)^{-1}\|\le1\), and the resolvent identity gives
\[
|\Psi(e,Q)-\Psi(e,\bar Q)|
\le \|e\|^2\|Q-\bar Q\|.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Once \(Q\) is frozen, \(\Psi\) is exactly a quadratic polynomial in
\[
(t,u_1,u_2).
\]
Therefore its conditional expectation is determined by the first two moments of that 3-vector. Equations (3.3)–(3.4) and (4.1)–(4.2) imply
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E_i\Psi_A-
\mathbb E_i\Psi_G
\right|
\le C(1+\|w_-\|^2)\big(\varepsilon_d+d^{-1/2}\big).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
After averaging over the leave-block sigma-field, the right side is \(O(\varepsilon_d)\).

This is the key restricted-regime simplification: a full 3-dimensional Stein theorem is neither sufficient by itself (because it omits \(Q\)) nor necessary (because after \(Q\) concentration the exact squared-ridge block functional is quadratic).

## 6. Total training replacement error after all n original-sample blocks

Let \(H_k\) be the hybrid problem with the first \(k\) original-sample blocks Gaussian-surrogate and the rest nonlinear, so \(H_0=A\), \(H_n=G\). For \(\varphi\in C_b^2(\mathbb R)\), condition on the leave-block sigma-field for swap \(k\). By (2.1),
\[
L_k^*=C+\frac1{2N}\Psi_k.
\]
Taylor expansion around the common leave-block optimum gives
\[
\left|
\mathbb E\varphi(L_k^*)-
\mathbb E\varphi(L_{k-1}^*)
\right|
\le
\frac{C\|\varphi'\|_\infty}{N}\varepsilon_d
+
\frac{C\|\varphi''\|_\infty}{N^2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
There are \(n=N/2\) swaps. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E\varphi(L_A^*)-
\mathbb E\varphi(L_G^*)
\right|
\le
C_\varphi\left(\varepsilon_d+\frac1N\right)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
This is the exact accumulated error. The raw per-block comparison is only \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), but the objective contribution carries the factor \(1/(2N)=1/(4n)\). Forgetting that factor creates a spurious \(n\varepsilon_d\) obstruction.

Since the Gaussian formulation has a deterministic asymptotic optimized cost under the Dhifallah–Lu assumptions, (6.2) implies the nonlinear optimized objective converges to the same limit in this restricted regime.

## 7. NEW: direct clean-test equivalence with an independent matched ghost test block

The previous P21 report used the conditional population surrogate risk \(R_G(w\mid F)\) as a quadratic perturbation. That route is sound. This run supplies a more literal independent-test-block proof and exposes why a single test example is not a dimension-free perturbation.

### 7.1 Why one test sample is the wrong perturbation scale

For one clean test sample \((t_0,x_0)\), adding
\[
\frac\tau2(t_0-x_0^\top w)^2
\]
changes the Hessian by \(\tau x_0x_0^\top\). Since \(\|x_0\|^2=\Theta(m)=\Theta(d)\), its operator norm is \(\Theta(|\tau|d)\). Therefore a fixed negative \(\tau\) can destroy strong convexity, and the second derivative of the value function can carry an unwanted factor \(d\). Any argument that treats one unnormalized test sample as a dimension-free smooth perturbation is invalid.

### 7.2 Proportional ghost block

Take an independent clean test block of size
\[
M=\lfloor\kappa d\rfloor,
\qquad \kappa>0\ \text{fixed},
\]
independent of the training sample conditional on the common feature matrix \(F\).

Actual clean rows:
\[
x_r^{A,0}=\sigma(F^\top a_r^0),\qquad t_r^0=\xi^\top a_r^0.
\]
Matched clean Gaussian rows:
\[
x_r^{G,0}=\mu_{1s}F^\top a_r^0+\mu_{2s}p_r^0,
\]
where
\[
\mu_{1s}=\mathbb E[Z\sigma(Z)],\qquad
\mu_{2s}^2=\mathbb E\sigma(Z)^2-\mu_{1s}^2.
\]
Define the empirical clean risk
\[
\widehat R_M^M(w)=\frac1M\sum_{r=1}^M(t_r^0-(x_r^{M,0})^\top w)^2,
\qquad M\in\{A,G\}.
\]
For each model define the perturbed optimized value
\[
\Phi_M(\tau)=\min_w\left\{L_M(w)+\frac\tau2\widehat R_M^M(w)\right\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), the optimizer is the training optimizer and is independent of the ghost test block.

### 7.3 Uniform stability event for the ghost block

Conditional on the good \(F\)-event, for every deterministic \(v\in S^{m-1}\),
\[
v^\top x_r^{A,0}
\]
is a centered Lipschitz function of \(a_r^0\) with Lipschitz constant at most \(C\|F\|_{\mathrm{op}}\); hence the clean actual row is a uniformly subgaussian random vector with covariance operator \(O(1)\). The surrogate row is Gaussian with covariance operator \(O(1)\). The augmented vector \((x_r^{M,0},t_r^0)\) is likewise uniformly subgaussian.

Standard sample-covariance concentration therefore yields, with probability \(1-e^{-c d}\),
\[
\left\|\frac1M(X_T^M)^\top X_T^M\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C,
\qquad
\left\|\frac1M(X_T^M)^\top t_T\right\|\le C
\tag{7.2}
\]
for both models. One may couple both candidate rows for every test index; any hybrid covariance is dominated by the sum of the two full PSD covariance sums, so the same event controls every row-by-row test hybrid.

Choose
\[
0<h_0<\frac{\lambda}{4C}.
\]
Then for every \(|\tau|\le h_0\), every training/test hybrid objective in (7.1) has Hessian at least \(\lambda I/2\). Thus all leave-one-block inverses are uniformly bounded by \(2/\lambda\).

### 7.4 Training-block swaps with the test perturbation present

Fix \(|\tau|\le h_0\). The independent test quadratic is simply part of the common leave-training-block base objective. The proof of Sections 2–6 is unchanged, with \(H_-^{-1}\) bounded by \(2/\lambda\) instead of \(1/\lambda\). Therefore the sum over the \(n\) training-block swaps contributes
\[
O(\varepsilon_d+N^{-1})
\tag{7.3}
\]
to any fixed smooth test of \(\Phi(\tau)\), uniformly for \(|\tau|\le h_0\).

### 7.5 Test-row swaps and their total error

Now hold the training surrogate fixed and replace the \(M\) clean test rows one at a time. Remove test row \(r\), let \(w_-\) and \(H_-\succeq\lambda I/2\) denote the leave-test-row optimizer and Hessian, and put
\[
e=t_r^0-x_r^\top w_-,\qquad
Q_r=\frac1M x_r^\top H_-^{-1}x_r.
\]
The exact weighted rank-one value update is
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r(\tau)-\Phi_{-r}(\tau)
=
\frac{\tau}{2M}\frac{e^2}{1+\tau Q_r}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
For negative \(\tau\), the denominator stays uniformly positive on the stability event by the choice of \(h_0\).

The clean \(\Delta=0\) version of Section 3 gives
\[
\|C_0^A-C_0^G\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|c_0^A-c_0^G\|_2\le C\varepsilon_d.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Also \(Q_r\) concentrates at \(O(d^{-1/2})\) scale and its actual/surrogate conditional means differ by \(O(\varepsilon_d)\). After freezing \(Q_r\), (7.4) is quadratic in the pair \((t_r^0,x_r^\top w_-)\), so only first and second moments are needed. Hence the raw conditional bracket in (7.4) differs by \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), and the objective difference per test-row swap is
\[
O\!\left(\frac{|\tau|\varepsilon_d}{M}+\frac{\tau^2}{M^2}\right).
\]
Summing all \(M\) test rows gives
\[
\boxed{
O\!\left(|\tau|\varepsilon_d+\frac{\tau^2}{M}\right).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
Thus for any fixed finite tuple of perturbation values \(\tau_1,\dots,\tau_k\in[-h_0,h_0]\), the joint optimized-value vector for the nonlinear training+test model and the matched Gaussian training+test model has vanishing smooth-test discrepancy.

### 7.6 Envelope derivative and uniform second derivative

Let
\[
S_T=\frac1M X_T^\top X_T,\qquad r_T=\frac1M X_T^\top t_T.
\]
The gradient and Hessian of \(\widehat R_M\) are
\[
\nabla \widehat R_M(w)=2(S_Tw-r_T),\qquad
\nabla^2\widehat R_M=2S_T.
\]
On (7.2), \(\|S_T\|+\|r_T\|\le C\). At \(\tau=0\), ridge coercivity gives \(\|w_0\|=O_{L^4}(1)\). Strong convexity and the bounded test gradient then imply uniformly for \(|\tau|\le h_0\),
\[
\|w_\tau\|=O_{L^4}(1).
\tag{7.7}
\]
The envelope theorem gives
\[
\Phi_M'(\tau)=\frac12\widehat R_M(w_\tau).
\tag{7.8}
\]
Differentiating the first-order condition yields
\[
\boxed{
\Phi_M''(\tau)
=-\frac14\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau)^\top
H_\tau^{-1}
\nabla\widehat R_M(w_\tau),
}
\tag{7.9}
\]
so
\[
\sup_{|\tau|\le h_0}|\Phi_M''(\tau)|=O_{L^2}(1).
\tag{7.10}
\]
Therefore the centered finite difference
\[
D_h\Phi_M:=\frac{\Phi_M(h)-\Phi_M(-h)}{2h}
\]
satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\left|2D_h\Phi_M-\widehat R_M(w_M^*)\right|\le Ch
}
\tag{7.11}
\]
in probability, uniformly for both actual and Gaussian models.

### 7.7 Empirical ghost risk converges to clean population risk

At \(\tau=0\), \(w_M^*\) is a function only of the training data and \(F\), hence independent of the ghost test block. Conditional on \((F,\text{training})\), the variables
\[
(t_r^0-(x_r^{M,0})^\top w_M^*)^2
\]
are iid with uniformly bounded second moment because \(\|w_M^*\|=O_{L^4}(1)\). Therefore
\[
\widehat R_M(w_M^*)-R_M(w_M^*\mid F)\xrightarrow{P}0,
\tag{7.12}
\]
where \(R_A\) uses a fresh actual clean feature and \(R_G\) a fresh matched clean Gaussian feature.

Combining (7.3), (7.6), (7.11) and (7.12) gives clean-test risk equivalence.

For a direct vanishing-parameter quantitative choice, let
\[
h_d=\sqrt{\varepsilon_d}.
\]
When a smooth test of the finite difference is composed with \((\Phi(h_d),\Phi(-h_d))\), the training-swap first-order error is \(O(\varepsilon_d/h_d)\), while the summed second-order Taylor remainder is \(O((Nh_d^2)^{-1})\); the test-row contribution is \(O(\varepsilon_d)\), and the finite-difference bias is \(O(h_d)\). Since \(N\asymp d\) and \(\varepsilon_d\asymp (\log d)^{3/2}d^{-1/2}\), all terms vanish. Thus for every bounded smooth scalar test \(g\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E g(R_A(w_A^*\mid F))-\mathbb E g(R_G(w_G^*\mid F))\to0.
}
\tag{7.13}
\]
Since the Gaussian surrogate risk converges to the deterministic Dhifallah–Lu limit in the restricted model, the nonlinear clean prediction risk converges to the same limit.

This is a genuinely separate proof from the optimized training-objective argument and explicitly includes an independent matched clean test block.

## 9. Route comparison and dimension-factor audit

### Block Lindeberg / leave-one-original-sample-block-out — preferred

This is the cleanest route. The two augmentations must be swapped together so that the shared latent \(a_i\), duplicated teacher label \(t_i\), and shared surrogate residual \(b_i\) remain intact. The exact rank-two update exposes the leverage matrix explicitly and the objective normalization automatically supplies the crucial \(1/N\) factor.

### Full Stein comparison of \((t,u_1,u_2)\)

Hu–Lu's unaugmented universality theorem genuinely needs a scalar CLT/Stein step because its general loss produces a nonlinear Moreau-envelope function of the leave-one-out logit. In the present squared-ridge special case, after leverage freezing, the current block is a quadratic function of the three logits. A full CLT is therefore strictly stronger than needed. It would also be insufficient if one forgot the leverage matrix.

### Continuous interpolation

One can interpolate the current block from nonlinear to Gaussian and differentiate the expected optimized value. But unless one exploits the same quadratic simplification, the derivative creates third-derivative/Stein terms and additional bookkeeping. Endpoint second-moment comparison is shorter and sharper here.

### Direct resolvent comparison

The ridge optimizer is explicit in terms of \((X^\top X/N+\lambda I)^{-1}\), so a resolvent method is possible. However there is no reason for the full nonlinear and surrogate Gram matrices to be close in operator norm under a natural coupling. A global resolvent comparison therefore risks demanding an unnecessarily strong matrix approximation. The block Woodbury method is effectively the local resolvent method at the exact scale needed.

### Naive per-block CLT error

A raw per-block error of order \(d^{-1/2}\) would indeed be fatal if it entered an unnormalized sum: \(n d^{-1/2}=\Theta(\sqrt d)\). Here it enters the optimized value multiplied by \(1/N\), so the total is only \(O(d^{-1/2})\). This normalization must never be dropped.

### Single-test perturbation

A single clean feature row has norm \(\Theta(\sqrt d)\), hence rank-one Hessian norm \(\Theta(d)\). Fixed-\(\tau\) two-sided finite differences around a single sample are not uniformly stable. Population-risk perturbation or a normalized \(M\asymp d\) ghost block repairs this exactly.
