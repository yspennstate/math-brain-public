## 2. Exact model convention and radius conventions

Bosch–Winter define incremental aggregation by adding one site from the outer vertex boundary
\[
\partial A=\{y\notin A:\exists x\in A,\ |x-y|=1\}.
\]
For a finite set \(B\), harmonic measure \(h_B\) is the limit, as the simple-random-walk starting point tends to infinity, of the conditional first-hit distribution on \(B\). DLA chooses the next site from \(\partial A_n\) according to \(h_{\partial A_n}\).

Equivalently, with
\[
\overline A:=A\cup\partial A,
\]
a walk arriving from infinity first hits \(\overline A\) on \(\partial A\), so
\[
h_{\partial A}=h_{\overline A}.
\]
This is the convention used by Losev–Smirnov: the attachment probability of \(x\in\partial A\) is
\[
p_A(x):=\omega(x,\overline A).
\]
Thus the two primary formulations agree exactly.

The Euclidean radius is
\[
R(A)=\max_{x\in A}|x|.
\]
The diameter satisfies
\[
R(A)\le \operatorname{diam}(A)\le 2R(A),
\]
so radius and diameter have the same logarithmic growth exponent. Also
\[
R(A)\le R(\overline A)\le R(A)+1,
\]
so closing by the outer boundary does not change the exponent. Any fixed \(\ell^p\)-norm radius is constant-factor equivalent in \(\mathbb R^2\) and therefore has the same exponent. By contrast, radius of gyration is not deterministically constant-factor equivalent to the maximal radius for arbitrary dendritic sets and is not silently substituted here.

If
\[
T(r):=\min\{n:R_n\ge r\},
\]
then monotonic generalized-inverse inequalities show, for any \(\beta>0\),
\[
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}\to\beta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{\log T(r)}{\log r}\to\frac1\beta.
\]
It is enough in the right-hand limit to check dyadic radii \(r=2^j\). This gives an equivalent annular-passage formulation of the target.

## 3. Known rigorous exponent window

For any connected \(n\)-site subset of \(\mathbb Z^2\) containing the origin,
\[
R_n\ge \frac12(\sqrt n-1).
\]
Kesten's planar DLA theorem gives, almost surely for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
R_n\le Cn^{2/3}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac12\le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le \frac23
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\]
The 2024 Bosch–Winter source mentions the older conjectural value \(\delta_f=5/3\), corresponding to \(\beta=3/5\), whereas recent off-lattice numerical work quoted by Halsey is closer to \(D\approx1.71\), i.e. \(\beta\approx0.585\). Neither is a theorem for the lattice process, and the disagreement is one reason not to hard-code a candidate value into a rigorous proof attempt.

# 4. New result of this run: a rigorous capacity-drift martingale reduction

The useful new observation is that the random attachment choice can be separated from the unresolved morphology on the exact \(\log n\) scale relevant for the exponent.

Let \(\mathcal F_n\) be the DLA filtration and put
\[
C_n:=\operatorname{Cap}(\overline A_n),
\]
where \(\operatorname{Cap}\) is the planar lattice logarithmic capacity in the normalization of Losev–Smirnov/Lawler.

For \(x\in\partial A_n\), define the deterministic one-step capacity gain from attaching \(x\):
\[
d_n(x):=
\operatorname{Cap}(\overline{A_n\cup\{x\}})-
\operatorname{Cap}(\overline A_n).
\]
Let
\[
a_n:=\mathbb E[C_{n+1}-C_n\mid\mathcal F_n]
=\sum_{x\in\partial A_n}p_n(x)d_n(x),
\qquad p_n(x):=\omega(x,\overline A_n).
\]

### Proposition 4.1 — capacity drift tracks the logarithmic radius pathwise

There is an almost-sure estimate
\[
\boxed{
\log R_n
=\frac\pi2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1} a_k
+O\!\left(\sqrt{\log n\,\log\log n}\right)
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
-
\frac{\pi}{2\log n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_k
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the full existence/determinism question is exactly reduced to a logarithmic Cesàro law for the predictable capacity drift \(a_n\).

### Proof

Losev–Smirnov Lemma 5 gives universal constants \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\) such that, for every finite connected \(A\subset\mathbb Z^2\) and every \(x\in\partial A\),
\[
c_0\,\omega(x,\overline A)^2
\le
\operatorname{Cap}(\overline{A\cup\{x\}})-\operatorname{Cap}(\overline A)
\le
C_0\,\omega(x,\overline A)^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]
In particular \(0\le d_n(x)\le C_0\).

Define martingale differences
\[
\xi_{n+1}:=(C_{n+1}-C_n)-a_n,
\qquad
M_n:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\xi_{k+1}.
\]
Then
\[
C_n=C_1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_k+M_n.
\tag{4.4}
\]
The increments satisfy \(|\xi_{n+1}|\le C_0\).

For the conditional quadratic variation, (4.3) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\xi_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]
&\le \mathbb E[(C_{n+1}-C_n)^2\mid\mathcal F_n]\\
&\le C_0^2\sum_{x\in\partial A_n}p_n(x)^5\\
&\le C_0^2\,p_n^*{}^4,
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]
where \(p_n^*:=\max_xp_n(x)\).

The planar discrete Beurling estimate gives
\[
p_n^*\le C R(\overline A_n)^{-1/2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]
The deterministic volume-radius bound gives
\[
R(\overline A_n)\ge R(A_n)\ge \tfrac12(\sqrt n-1),
\]
so for all large \(n\),
\[
\mathbb E[\xi_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]\le \frac{C}{n}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Hence the predictable quadratic variation obeys
\[
\langle M\rangle_n\le C\log n.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Apply the maximal Freedman inequality on dyadic horizons \(N_j=2^j\). With
\[
t_j=K\sqrt{j\log j},
\]
(4.8) and the bounded martingale increments yield
\[
\mathbb P\left(\max_{m\le2^j}|M_m|>t_j\right)
\le 2\exp\{-cK^2\log j\}
\]
for all large \(j\). Choosing \(K\) large makes the right-hand side summable. Borel–Cantelli and interpolation between dyadic horizons give
\[
M_n=O\!\left(\sqrt{\log n\,\log\log n}\right)
\qquad\text{a.s.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

Losev–Smirnov equation (16) states, uniformly for finite connected planar lattice sets,
\[
\left|\operatorname{Cap}(B)-\frac2\pi\log R(B)\right|\le C.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Apply (4.10) to \(B=\overline A_n\). Since \(R(A_n)\le R(\overline A_n)\le R(A_n)+1\),
\[
C_n=\frac2\pi\log R_n+O(1).
\tag{4.11}
\]
Combining (4.4), (4.9), and (4.11) proves (4.1) and (4.2). QED.

## 5. Rigorous third-harmonic-moment consequence

Define the cubic harmonic-energy statistic
\[
S_3(A_n):=\sum_{x\in\partial A_n}p_n(x)^3.
\]
By multiplying (4.3) by the DLA selection probability \(p_n(x)\) and summing,
\[
\boxed{c_0S_3(A_n)\le a_n\le C_0S_3(A_n).}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Combining (4.1), the trivial lower radius bound, and Kesten's upper radius bound gives the new pathwise cumulative statement
\[
\boxed{
0<c\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1{\log n}\sum_{k<n}S_3(A_k)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1{\log n}\sum_{k<n}S_3(A_k)
\le C<\infty
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
almost surely, for deterministic universal constants \(c,C\).

This is a rigorous lattice counterpart of the long-standing physics relation between the third moment of harmonic measure and DLA growth. Halsey's July 2026 paper discusses an amplitude relation of this type for off-lattice Hastings–Levitov DLA under scaling assumptions; equations (4.1)–(5.2) require no assumed fractal exponent and hold pathwise for the standard lattice process.

The exact one-step multiplier in (4.3) is geometry dependent, so (5.1) alone does **not** identify \(\beta\). It does, however, show that the unresolved information is contained in the predictable geometry, not in the coin-flip noise of choosing the next attachment site.

## 6. Exact reformulation of the exponent problem

Define
\[
H_n:=n a_n.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{k<n}a_k=\sum_{k<n}\frac{H_k}{k}.
\]
Therefore (4.2) gives
\[
\boxed{
\beta\text{ exists a.s.}
\iff
\frac1{\log n}\sum_{k<n}\frac{H_k}{k}
\text{ has an a.s. limit},
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
and if the latter limit is \(L\), then
\[
\beta=\frac\pi2 L.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Determinism of \(\beta\) is equivalent to determinism of this logarithmic Cesàro limit.

From the known radius window,
\[
\boxed{
\frac1\pi
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac1{\log n}\sum_{k<n}a_k
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1{\log n}\sum_{k<n}a_k
\le
\frac4{3\pi}
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
almost surely.

This is a useful separation of tasks:
1. stochastic attachment noise has been shown negligible on the \(\log n\) scale;
2. the remaining target is a self-averaging theorem for the predictable capacity drift along the evolving harmonic geometry.

## 9. Next concrete attack

The next target should be a **logarithmic-block stabilization theorem for the predictable capacity drift**. Define particle-number dyadic block drifts
\[
B_j:=\sum_{k=2^j}^{2^{j+1}-1}a_k.
\]
Along \(n=2^m\), (4.1) says
\[
\frac{\log R_{2^m}}{m\log2}
-
\frac\pi{2m\log2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}B_j\to0.
\]
By monotonicity of \(R_n\), convergence along dyadic particle counts is enough for convergence along the full sequence. Thus it is enough to prove an a.s. deterministic Cesàro limit for \(B_j\).

A promising route is to condition on the cluster at the start of a logarithmic block and seek a contraction/self-averaging estimate for \(B_j\) that is insensitive to microscopic fjord geometry. The capacity increment identity suggests working with the cubic harmonic-energy measure rather than the maximal harmonic measure. One precise milestone is a bound of the form
\[
\operatorname{Var}(B_j\mid\mathcal F_{2^j})\le C
\]
plus a quantitative comparison of \(\mathbb E[B_j\mid\mathcal F_{2^j}]\) across admissible clusters with comparable capacity/radius. The first part may be approachable with the martingale structure above; the second is the genuine screening/renormalization obstacle.

A second attack is annular: for \(\tau_j=T(2^j)\), estimate the conditional law of
\[
\log(\tau_{j+1}/\tau_j)
\]
in terms of annular capacity growth, then seek an ergodic or contraction principle for the cluster viewed from its active harmonic-measure tips. This would attack existence directly rather than only bounds.
