# 3. Exact likelihood structure under core erasure

Let a finite obstacle be split as
\[
K=I\sqcup O,
\qquad O\ne\varnothing.
\]
Let
\[
p(x):=\omega_K(x),
\qquad
q(x):=\omega_O(x),
\]
with \(q\) extended by zero to \(I\). Put
\[
\varepsilon:=p(I).
\]

The preceding checkpoint's exact balayage theorem gives, for \(x\in O\),
\[
q(x)=p(x)+r(x),
\qquad r(x)\ge0,
\tag{3.1}
\]
where
\[
\sum_{x\in O}r(x)=\varepsilon.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Also
\[
\|p-q\|_{\rm TV}=\varepsilon.
\]

For \(x\in O\) with \(p(x)>0\), define the likelihood inflation
\[
\Lambda(x):=\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}\ge1,
\qquad
L(x):=\log\Lambda(x)\ge0.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Set \(L(x)=0\) on \(I\); sites with \(p(x)=0\) are irrelevant under \(X\sim p\).

### Proposition 3.1 — PROVED

If \(X\sim p\), then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p[L(X)]\le\varepsilon.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
More generally, for every \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p e^{\theta L(X)}
\le
\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)^{1-\theta}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
If \(\varepsilon\le1/2\), then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_p e^{\theta L(X)}
\le e^{2\theta\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

Using \(\log(1+u)\le u\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_pL(X)
&=\sum_{x\in O}p(x)\log\left(1+\frac{r(x)}{p(x)}\right)\\
&\le\sum_{x\in O}r(x)=\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (3.4).

For \(0\le\theta\le1\), Hölder gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_pe^{\theta L(X)}
&=p(I)+\sum_{x\in O}p(x)^{1-\theta}q(x)^\theta\\
&\le\varepsilon+
\left(\sum_{x\in O}p(x)\right)^{1-\theta}
\left(\sum_{x\in O}q(x)\right)^\theta\\
&=\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)^{1-\theta}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(\varepsilon\le1/2\),
\[
(1-\varepsilon)^{1-\theta}
=(1-\varepsilon)e^{-\theta\log(1-\varepsilon)}
\le(1-\varepsilon)e^{2\theta\varepsilon},
\]
so
\[
\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)^{1-\theta}
\le e^{2\theta\varepsilon}.
\]
QED.

### Consequences

For every \(u>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_p\{X\in I\text{ or }\Lambda(X)>1+u\}
\le
\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon}{u}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Indeed on \(O\),
\[
\mathbb E_p[(\Lambda-1)\mathbf1_O]
=\sum_Or=\varepsilon.
\]
Likewise, for every \(t>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_p\{L(X)>t\}
\le\frac{\varepsilon}{t}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus total variation locality has a stronger multiplicative form from the viewpoint of a typical real DLA particle: large multiplicative distortion can occur only on a correspondingly small amount of ordinary harmonic measure.

# 4. Almost-sure empirical log-likelihood locality in a radial epoch

Fix a dyadic epoch
\[
[\tau_R,\tau_{2R}),
\qquad
M_R:=\tau_{2R}-\tau_R,
\]
and a deterministic cutoff \(2\le s\le R/4\).

At time \(n\) in the epoch let
\[
p_n:=\omega(\cdot,K_n),
\qquad
q_n:=\omega(\cdot,K_n\setminus B_s),
\]
and let \(X_{n+1}\sim p_n\) be the actual DLA attachment. Put
\[
L_{n+1}:=
\begin{cases}
\log(q_n(X_{n+1})/p_n(X_{n+1})),&X_{n+1}\notin B_s,\\
0,&X_{n+1}\in B_s.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The previous checkpoint's Beurling penetration theorem gives, uniformly through the epoch,
\[
\varepsilon_n:=p_n(B_s)
\le
C\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{1/2}
=:\varepsilon_R.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For large \(R\), \(\varepsilon_R\le1/2\).

By Proposition 3.1, conditionally on \(\mathcal F_n\), for every \(0\le\theta\le1\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^{\theta L_{n+1}}\mid\mathcal F_n]
\le
\exp(2\theta\varepsilon_R).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Stop the increments after \(\tau_{2R}\), and extend them by zero up to the deterministic epoch horizon \(H_R=CR^2\) from the archived area bound. Then
\[
\exp\left(
\theta\sum_{i=1}^{m}L_{\tau_R+i}
-2\theta\varepsilon_Rm
\right),
\qquad 0\le m\le H_R,
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale.

Ville's maximal inequality therefore gives
\[
\mathbb P\left\{
\exists m\le H_R:
\sum_{i=1}^{m}L_{\tau_R+i}
>2\varepsilon_Rm+3\log R
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right\}
\le R^{-3}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
The dyadic sum of \(R^{-3}\) is finite. The archived local Kesten theorem gives
\[
M_R\ge cR^{3/2}
\tag{4.5}
\]
almost surely for all sufficiently large dyadic \(R\). Hence Borel–Cantelli proves:

### Theorem 4.1 — PROVED

Almost surely, for all sufficiently large dyadic \(R\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{M_R}
\sum_{n=\tau_R}^{\tau_{2R}-1}L_{n+1}
\le
C\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{1/2}
+
C\frac{\log R}{R^{3/2}}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For the slowly diverging-memory choice
\[
s_R=Re^{-L_R},
\qquad L_R\to\infty,
\]
this becomes
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{M_R}\sum L_{n+1}
\le
Ce^{-L_R/2}+o(1).
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is stronger than sparse-edit density. On the real attachments that remain outside the erased core, the log harmonic measure itself changes by vanishing average amount under annular truncation.

# 6. The third-moment core error has a sharp \(3/2\) barrier

The exponent target is governed by
\[
S_3(n):=\sum_xp_n(x)^3.
\]
The capacity increment estimate gives
\[
a_n\asymp S_3(n),
\]
and the archived reduction shows that stabilization of
\[
-\frac{\log S_3(n)}{\log R_n}
\]
would imply the radial exponent.

Let
\[
I_s:=K_n\cap B_s.
\]
The discrete Beurling estimate gives
\[
p_n^*:=\max_xp_n(x)\le CR^{-1/2}
\tag{6.1}
\]
while the archived penetration estimate gives
\[
p_n(I_s)\le C(s/R)^{1/2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x\in I_s}p_n(x)^3
&\le (p_n^*)^2p_n(I_s)\\
&\le C R^{-1}\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus:

### Theorem 6.1 — PROVED

For every connected planar DLA cluster of radius at least \(R\) and every \(2\le s\le R/4\),
\[
\boxed{
S_3^{\rm core}(n;s)
:=\sum_{x\in K_n\cap B_s}p_n(x)^3
\le
C s^{1/2}R^{-3/2}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

If \(s=R^\gamma\), \(0\le\gamma<1\), the bound is
\[
S_3^{\rm core}
\le
C R^{-(3-\gamma)/2}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
The best power obtainable from a fixed or bounded core is therefore \(R^{-3/2}\).

### Scalar sharpness of this barrier

This \(3/2\) power cannot be improved using only the two scalar facts (6.1)–(6.2). Abstractly, put one inner atom of mass
\[
R^{-1/2}
\]
and distribute the remaining mass over \(R\) outer atoms of order \(R^{-1}\). Then
\[
p^*\asymp R^{-1/2},
\qquad
p(I)\asymp R^{-1/2},
\]
and the inner atom alone contributes
\[
R^{-3/2}
\]
to \(S_3\). The Shannon entropy is still
\[
\log R+o(\log R).
\]

This is not asserted to be a realizable DLA cluster. It is a sharpness certificate for the currently used scalar inequalities.

### Consequence

The combination
\[
\text{core erasure} + \text{Beurling penetration} + \text{Beurling }p^*
\]
can at best control \(S_3\) to additive order \(R^{-3/2}\), exactly the threshold corresponding through the archived reciprocal relation to Kesten's \(\beta\le2/3\).

Therefore a proof of an actual third-moment exponent strictly larger than \(3/2\), let alone its exact value, requires a genuinely new estimate coupling **depth** and **local harmonic-measure size**. A separate bound on total deep mass and a separate bound on the largest atom cannot suffice.

# 7. Particle-Palm large-deviation formulation of the third moment

At a fixed large-radius configuration, sample
\[
X\sim p
\]
from ordinary harmonic measure and define the local exponent
\[
\alpha_R(X):=-\frac{\log p(X)}{\log R}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Then exactly
\[
\boxed{
S_3
=\sum_xp(x)^3
=\mathbb E_p[p(X)^2]
=\mathbb E_p\left[e^{-2(\log R)\alpha_R}\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The Beurling estimate implies asymptotically
\[
\alpha_R\ge\frac12-o(1)
\]
on the support of \(p\), while discrete Makarov controls only the first moment:
\[
\mathbb E_p\alpha_R=1+o(1).
\]

The third moment is instead a **lower-tail large-deviation functional** of \(\alpha_R\).

### Conditional Theorem 7.1 — PROVED AS A REDUCTION

Suppose that along the DLA trajectory, or along capacity blocks in a form sufficient for the archived comparison theorem, the laws of \(\alpha_R\) under particle-Palm sampling satisfy a large-deviation principle at speed \(\log R\) with a deterministic good rate function \(J\) on \([1/2,\infty)\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\tau_3
:=
-\lim\frac{\log S_3}{\log R}
=
\inf_{a\ge1/2}\{2a+J(a)\}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
If the same deterministic rate function governs all sufficiently large capacity scales, the archived third-moment theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\beta
=
\frac1{\inf_{a\ge1/2}(2a+J(a))}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Justification

Equation (7.2) is a Laplace transform at speed \(\log R\). The standard Laplace principle for a good LDP gives
\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\frac1{\log R}
\log\mathbb E e^{-2(\log R)\alpha_R}
= -\inf_a(2a+J(a)).
\]
The integrand is bounded by one and decreases for large \(a\), so no positive exponential tail issue occurs at \(a\to\infty\). Equation (7.3) follows.

This reduction is not a claim that such an LDP is known. It identifies exactly the rare-event object that must self-average.

# 9. A sharper next target: deep-tip anti-correlation

The previous run suggested proving contraction of annular morphology. This run shows that the contraction must be **tip-sensitive**.

A useful new deterministic/probabilistic target is any estimate improving (6.3), for example
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\in K_n\cap B_s}p_n(x)^3
\le
R^{-3/2-\delta}\,\Phi(s/R)
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
for some universal \(\delta>0\) and controlled \(\Phi\), at least for fixed or slowly growing \(s\). Even a small \(\delta>0\) would break the exact Kesten barrier in the third-moment channel and would be genuinely new.

The pointwise form that would imply such an estimate must couple geometric depth with harmonic-measure size, not treat them separately. Schematically one wants a two-arm/screening inequality of the form
\[
p_n(x)
\le
CR^{-1/2}\,\Psi\left(\frac{|x|}{R},\text{local arm geometry at }x\right)
\tag{9.2}
\]
with a nontrivial gain for deeply screened sites after averaging over the inner harmonic measure.

A second, more global route is to prove an LDP or stretched-LDP contraction for the particle-Palm exponent
\[
\alpha=-\log p/\log R
\]
under the annular renormalization. The positive likelihood theorem of Sections 3–5 shows that typical exponents are already stable under core erasure. What is missing is control of the **lower tail** \(\{\alpha\le a\}\) at probabilities \(R^{-c}\), with \(c\in[1/2,2/3]\).

Thus the next concrete attack should focus on one of:

1. a two-sided Beurling / multi-arm bound for deep accessible sites that improves the cubic deep-mass estimate;
2. a polynomial-scale coupling of lower-tail events for \(-\log p/\log R\) under annular truncation;
3. an annular renormalization theorem directly for the particle-Palm rate function \(J\), rather than for bounded observables.

Any of these would address the exact rare-event obstruction identified here.

# 10. Checks and failed routes

### Checks performed

1. **Balayage orientation.** On retained sites \(q=p+r\) with \(r\ge0\); deleting the core redistributes its mass outward.
2. **MGF direction.** Hölder applies with exponents \(1/(1-\theta)\) and \(1/\theta\), giving \(\sum p^{1-\theta}q^\theta\le(\sum p)^{1-\theta}(\sum q)^\theta\).
3. **Stopping argument.** The epoch is extended by zero increments after \(\tau_{2R}\), so the exponential supermartingale is valid up to the deterministic \(O(R^2)\) horizon.
4. **Almost-sure summability.** The failure probability \(R^{-3}\) is summable along dyadic \(R\).
5. **Normalization at random epoch length.** The archived local Kesten theorem supplies \(M_R\ge cR^{3/2}\) eventually a.s., making the additive \(3\log R\) term negligible.
6. **Third-moment depth bound.** \(\sum_Ip^3\le p_*^2p(I)\), not \(p_*p(I)^2\); the displayed \(R^{-3/2}\) power is the correct consequence of the available bounds.
7. **Large-deviation sign.** Since \(S_3=E[e^{-2(\log R)\alpha}]\), the exponent is \(\inf(2a+J(a))\), not a supremum.
8. **Scope.** The LDP theorem is explicitly conditional. No LDP, exact \(\tau_3\), or exact \(\beta\) is claimed.

### Routes shown insufficient

- **Ordinary total-variation locality:** controls typical particles but not polynomially rare capacity-dominating tips.
- **Slowly diverging capacity memory:** gives edit probability \(R^{-o(1)}\), which has zero rate at speed \(\log R\).
- **Fixed-core Beurling truncation:** gives only an \(R^{-1/2}\) particle-error scale and an \(R^{-3/2}\) cubic-moment error floor.
- **Shannon/Makarov plus typical-exponent locality:** fixes the center of the particle-Palm exponent distribution but not the lower large-deviation tail that determines \(S_3\).

These are self-checks and strategy certificates, not independent/formal verification.
