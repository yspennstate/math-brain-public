# 4. New pathwise balayage theorem for successive harmonic measures

Let \(\mu_K\) and \(\mu_L\) denote harmonic measure from infinity on the nested finite sets \(K\subset L\). Extend \(\mu_K\) to \(L\) by assigning zero mass to \(S=L\setminus K\).

### Theorem 4.1 — PROVED

For every \(z\in K\),
\[
\boxed{
\mu_K(z)
=
\mu_L(z)
+
\sum_{y\in S}\mu_L(y)\,\mathbb P^y\{S_{T_K}=z\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mu_K(z)\ge\mu_L(z)
\qquad(z\in K).
\tag{4.2}
\]
If
\[
W:=\mu_L(S)=\sum_{i=1}^kw_i,
\tag{4.3}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\|\mu_L-\mu_K\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=W.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

Start a simple random walk at a point \(z_0\) tending to infinity. Because two-dimensional simple random walk is recurrent, it hits every nonempty finite set almost surely. Since \(K\subset L\), first stop the walk at \(T_L\). If the first point hit lies in \(K\), the eventual first hit of \(K\) is already determined. If it lies in \(S=L\setminus K\), restart by the strong Markov property and run until the walk hits \(K\). Thus, for finite \(z_0\), the hitting distribution of \(K\) decomposes through the first hit of \(L\). Letting \(|z_0|\to\infty\) gives (4.1).

The second term in (4.1) is nonnegative, proving (4.2). Summing the difference \(\mu_K-\mu_L\) over \(K\) gives exactly \(W\), because both measures have total mass one and \(\mu_L(S)=W\). On \(S\), the extended old measure is zero and the new measure has total mass \(W\). Hence the \(\ell^1\) distance is \(2W\), so total variation distance is \(W\). QED.

### Projective consequence

Iterating (4.1), for every \(m>n\), the harmonic measure on \(\overline A_n\) is the balayage of harmonic measure on \(\overline A_m\) back onto \(\overline A_n\) by simple-random-walk first hitting. Thus the successive DLA harmonic measures form a **pathwise projective family under balayage**. This is not a statement of independence or stationarity; it is an exact nesting identity.

# 7. Predictable quadratic-variation clock and the origin of the cubic tilt

Condition on \(\mathcal F_n\). For each possible attachment \(x\), write
\[
\delta_n(x):=
\|\omega(\cdot,\overline{A_n\cup\{x\}})-
\omega(\cdot,\overline A_n)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Define the predictable squared-TV motion
\[
v_n:=
\mathbb E[\delta_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]
=
\sum_xp_n(x)\delta_n(x)^2.
\tag{7.1}
\]
By (5.7),
\[
\boxed{
\frac13v_n\le a_n\le4v_n.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus the predictable capacity clock is universally equivalent to the predictable quadratic-variation clock of harmonic measure in total variation.

Now use (5.9):
\[
p_n(x)^2\le\delta_n(x)^2\le16p_n(x)^2.
\]
Multiplying by the attachment probability \(p_n(x)\) and summing,
\[
\boxed{
S_3(n)
\le v_n\le16S_3(n).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Combining with (7.2) recovers
\[
a_n\asymp S_3(n)
\]
from an intrinsic metric statement.

### Exact capacity-Palm form

The one-time capacity-biased boundary law is
\[
r_n(x)
=
\frac{p_n(x)d_n(x)}{a_n}.
\]
By (5.4),
\[
\boxed{
r_n(x)
=
\frac{p_n(x)\rho_n(x)\delta_n(x)^2}
{\sum_y p_n(y)\rho_n(y)\delta_n(y)^2},
\qquad
\frac13\le\rho_n(x)\le4.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Therefore the capacity-Palm law is exactly the ordinary attachment law size-biased by the **squared displacement of the harmonic-measure state**, with only a bounded local response coefficient.

Since \(\delta_n(x)\asymp p_n(x)\),
\[
r_n(x)\asymp\frac{p_n(x)^3}{S_3(n)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]
This gives a geometric explanation for the special role of \(q=3\):

- one power of \(p\) is the probability that the DLA particle chooses the site;
- two further powers arise because logarithmic-capacity growth is quadratic variation of harmonic measure and the TV step is of order \(p\).

The cubic harmonic-measure tilt is therefore not merely an algebraic artifact of Lemma 5; it is the Palm law of the quadratic variation of the evolving harmonic measure.

## 10. Failed / insufficient routes and new obstruction map

### Static Makarov alone remains insufficient

Discrete Makarov fixes the Shannon entropy of harmonic measure to \(\log R+O(\log\log R)\) but does not determine how the squared-TV quadratic variation is distributed among boundary sites. The new theorem explains why its missing input is cubic/Rényi-3 information.

### Beurling alone controls only the largest metric step

Beurling gives
\[
p^*\lesssim R^{-1/2},
\]
and hence by (5.9)
\[
\delta^*\lesssim R^{-1/2}.
\]
This prevents a single attachment from carrying too much quadratic variation, yielding Kesten-scale anti-concentration, but it does not prevent quadratic variation from being spread across nearly \(R^2\) particles. This is the same obstruction as the unresolved lower-growth problem.

### Minimal harmonic measure does not give the missing lower-tail control

Recent sharp results on minimal positive harmonic measure show that individual accessible sites can have exponentially tiny harmonic measure in the size of a finite set. Therefore a route based on a uniform lower bound for all \(\delta_A(x)\) or all \(p_A(x)\) cannot work.

### Direct martingale noise remains closed

The recovered capacity martingale has finite total quadratic variation and converges. The new metric theorem shows that the *state path* itself nevertheless has infinite squared-TV variation: the infinite quadratic variation is predictable morphology evolution, not the centered one-step martingale error in capacity.

### No full exponent from quadratic variation alone

A generic path may accumulate unit quadratic variation using many tiny steps or fewer larger steps. Exactly the same freedom appears here: \(N_j\) can in principle range between Kesten-scale \(R^{3/2}\) and compact-scale \(R^2\). The missing theorem must control the distribution of TV step sizes dynamically, not merely their squared sum.

## 11. Adversarial checking pass

1. **Nested sets.** After attaching \(x\), \(\overline A\subset\overline{A\cup\{x\}}\); only newly exposed outer-neighbour sites are added to the closure. The attached site \(x\) was already in \(\overline A\).
2. **Balayage direction.** The old harmonic measure is obtained by first hitting the larger new closure and then, only if a new site was hit, continuing until the old closure is hit. This gives \(\mu_K\) as the balayage of \(\mu_L\), not the reverse.
3. **TV normalization.** The loss of mass on old sites totals \(W\), and the mass added on new sites totals \(W\); hence \(\ell^1\) distance is \(2W\) and total variation distance is \(W\).
4. **Capacity quadratic form.** Losev–Smirnov's first-entry identity gives \(g=Hw\), while their exact capacity increment formula gives \(d=w^Tg=w^THw\).
5. **Matrix bounds.** The recovered local-response proof gives \(H=(I-Q)^{-1}\) with \(0\preceq Q\preceq(3/4)I\); therefore \(I\preceq H\preceq4I\). No stronger unstated spectral bound is used.

7. **Comparison \(p\le W\le4p\).** From \(w=(I-Q)g\), symmetry/nonnegativity and \(Q\mathbf1\le(3/4)\mathbf1\) give \(W\in[(1/4)\mathbf1^Tg,\mathbf1^Tg]\); the discrete normal-derivative identity gives \(\mathbf1^Tg=4p\).

9. **Cubic exponent.** Predictable squared TV motion is \(\sum p(x)\delta(x)^2\); because \(\delta(x)\asymp p(x)\), it is comparable to \(\sum p(x)^3\), with no assumed multifractal scaling.
