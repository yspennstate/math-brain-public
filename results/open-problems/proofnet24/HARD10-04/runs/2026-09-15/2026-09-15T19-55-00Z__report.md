# 3. Exact semigroup property of nested first-hit kernels

For nested DLA closures \(K_a\subset K_b\), define the direct first-hit kernel from \(K_b\) to \(K_a\) by
\[
\Pi_{a:b}^{\rm dir}(y,z)
:=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf1_{\{y=z\}},&y\in K_a,\\
\mathbb P^y\{S_{T_{K_a}}=z\},&y\in K_b\setminus K_a.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.1}
\]
The recovered genealogy uses the one-level kernels \(\Pi_n:K_{n+1}\to K_n\) and satisfies \(\mu_n=\mu_{n+1}\Pi_n\).

### Proposition 3.1 — PROVED

For every \(a<b\), the composition of all intermediate one-level kernels equals the direct first-hit kernel:
\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{b-1}\Pi_{b-2}\cdots\Pi_a
=
\Pi_{a:b}^{\rm dir}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
(Here row measures act on kernels from left to right; equivalently, starting from a point of \(K_b\), apply \(\Pi_{b-1}\), then \(\Pi_{b-2}\), and so on down to \(K_a\).)

### Proof

It is enough to prove the two-set composition law for \(K_a\subset K_c\subset K_b\). Start a simple random walk at \(y\in K_b\). If \(y\in K_a\), both sides are the point mass at \(y\). Otherwise stop first at \(K_c\), and from that hitting point continue until first hitting \(K_a\). By the strong Markov property, the resulting distribution on \(K_a\) is exactly the direct first-hit distribution from \(y\) to \(K_a\). Induction over the nested chain proves (3.2). QED.

This removes all intermediate cluster states from multi-time lineage calculations: only ordinary hitting probabilities of an earlier closure remain.

# 4. Exact two-time renewal correlation kernel

Let
\[
S_k:=K_{k+1}\setminus K_k
\tag{4.1}
\]
be the newly exposed shell at level \(k+1\). In the recovered harmonic-measure lineage, define
\[
J_{k+1}:=\mathbf1\{Y_{k+1}\in S_k\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Then \(\mathbb P(J_{k+1}=1\mid\text{DLA trajectory})=\delta_{k+1}\).

For \(k<\ell\) and \(y\in S_\ell\), define the old-shell entrance probability
\[
h_k(y)
:=
\mathbb P^y\{S_{T_{K_{k+1}}}\in S_k\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — PROVED

Conditional on the fixed DLA trajectory,
\[
\boxed{
 c_{k\ell}
:=
\mathbb P(J_{k+1}=1,J_{\ell+1}=1)
=
\sum_{y\in S_\ell}\mu_{\ell+1}(y)h_k(y).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Equivalently, whenever \(\delta_{\ell+1}>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(J_{k+1}=1\mid J_{\ell+1}=1)
=
\sum_{y\in S_\ell}
\frac{\mu_{\ell+1}(y)}{\delta_{\ell+1}}
\,h_k(y).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Thus the conditional probability of an old renewal, given a later renewal, is precisely the old-shell hitting probability averaged under the **normalized harmonic measure carried by the later newly exposed shell**.

### Proof

Condition on \(Y_{\ell+1}=y\in S_\ell\), which is exactly the event of renewal at level \(\ell+1\). By Proposition 3.1, the ancestor \(Y_{k+1}\) is distributed as the direct first hit of \(K_{k+1}\) by a walk started at \(y\). Renewal at level \(k+1\) occurs exactly when this hit lies in \(S_k\), with probability \(h_k(y)\). Averaging over \(y\in S_\ell\) with mass \(\mu_{\ell+1}(y)\) gives (4.4). Divide by \(\mu_{\ell+1}(S_\ell)=\delta_{\ell+1}\) for (4.5). QED.

### Far-field interpretation

For fixed earlier closure \(K_{k+1}\) and fixed shell \(S_k\), the definition of harmonic measure from infinity gives
\[
 h_k(y)\longrightarrow
\mu_{k+1}(S_k)=\delta_{k+1}
\qquad\text{as }|y|\to\infty.
\tag{4.6}
\]
Hence exact factorization
\[
 c_{k\ell}\approx\delta_{k+1}\delta_{\ell+1}
\]
would follow if the later-renewal law in (4.5) saw the old shell essentially from infinity. The genuine obstruction is therefore later new-shell mass that remains geometrically close to, or strongly screened by, the old cluster.

A uniform pointwise bound in \(y\) is not expected: a later exposed site can lie near an old fjord. The correct target is the **weighted average (4.5)**.

# 5. Exact second moment formula for the pair-overlap clock

Take two conditionally independent lineages, and set
\[
I_{n+1}:=J_{n+1}^{(1)}J_{n+1}^{(2)}.
\]
For a finite index set \(B\), define
\[
L_2(B):=\sum_{n\in B}I_{n+1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
The recovered report gives \(\mathbb E I_{n+1}=\delta_{n+1}^2\).

### Theorem 5.1 — PROVED

Conditional on the DLA trajectory,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[L_2(B)^2]
=
\sum_{n\in B}\delta_{n+1}^2
+
2\sum_{\substack{k<\ell\\k,\ell\in B}}c_{k\ell}^2,
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
where \(c_{k\ell}\) is the one-lineage correlation kernel in (4.4).

### Proof

Expand the square. Since \(I_{n+1}\) is an indicator, its diagonal expectation is \(\delta_{n+1}^2\). For \(k<\ell\), the two lineages are conditionally independent copies, so
\[
\mathbb E[I_{k+1}I_{\ell+1}]
=
\mathbb P(J_{k+1}=1,J_{\ell+1}=1)^2
=c_{k\ell}^2.
\]
Summing proves (5.2). QED.

This converts the previously stated second-moment goal into a single explicit potential-theoretic sum.

### Corollary 5.2 — precise sufficient mixing estimate

Suppose on every unit-capacity block \(B_j\) one can prove
\[
 c_{k\ell}
\le
C\delta_{k+1}\delta_{\ell+1}+e_{k\ell},
\qquad k<\ell\in B_j,
\tag{5.3}
\]
with
\[
\sum_{k<\ell\in B_j}e_{k\ell}^2\le C.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[L_2(B_j)^2\mid\text{trajectory}]\le C'.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Indeed, the recovered capacity/TV theorem gives \(\sum_{B_j}\delta^2=O(1)\), so (5.2), (5.3), (5.4), and \((a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2\) yield a universal bound.

Since \(\mathbb E L_2(B_j)\asymp1\), Paley–Zygmund would then give a uniformly positive probability of at least one true pair common renewal in every capacity block.

The most concrete new analytic target is therefore the weighted screening estimate
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{y\in S_\ell}
\frac{\mu_{\ell+1}(y)}{\delta_{\ell+1}}
\,h_k(y)
\lesssim
\delta_{k+1}+\text{square-summable error}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
This is a much more precise obligation than generic “annular mixing.”

# 6. Sharpness certificate: scalar harmonic-measure constraints allow every candidate dimension

The current rigorous scalar toolkit includes, at the power scale relevant here:

1. discrete Makarov: Shannon entropy \(H(p)=\log R+o(\log R)\);
2. Beurling: \(p_*\lesssim R^{-1/2}\);
3. capacity response: \(d(x)\asymp p(x)^2\);
4. predictable drift: \(a\asymp\sum p^3\);
5. higher conditional moments: \(m_q:=\sum p(x)d(x)^q\lesssim p_*^{2q-2}a\);
6. Kesten: a unit-radius/capacity-scale block cannot have substantially fewer than \(R^{3/2}\) particles.

The following explicit construction shows these statements cannot, by themselves, select an exponent.

### Theorem 6.1 — PROVED abstract sharpness certificate

Fix any \(D\in[3/2,2]\). For every large integer \(R\), define a probability vector with one heavy atom
\[
h_R:=R^{-D/3}
\tag{6.1}
\]
and \(R\) light atoms, each of mass
\[
\ell_R:=\frac{1-h_R}{R}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then:

#### (i) Beurling-scale maximum
\[
\boxed{p_* = h_R\le R^{-1/2}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### (ii) Makarov-scale Shannon entropy
\[
\boxed{H(p)=\log R+o(\log R).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### (iii) Arbitrary third-moment exponent in the full Kesten-compatible interval
\[
\boxed{
S_3:=\sum p^3
=R^{-D}+O(R^{-2}),
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
so
\[
-\frac{\log S_3}{\log R}\to D.
\tag{6.6}
\]

#### (iv) Unit-capacity synthetic block with \(N\asymp R^D\)
Choose any fixed \(\kappa\in[4/3,16]\) and set abstract one-site capacity increments
\[
d(x)=\kappa p(x)^2.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Then
\[
a=\sum p(x)d(x)=\kappa S_3\asymp R^{-D}.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Taking
\[
N_R:=\left\lfloor a^{-1}\right\rfloor
\tag{6.9}
\]
identical abstract steps gives
\[
N_R\asymp R^D,
\qquad
N_Ra=1+o(1).
\tag{6.10}
\]
Because \(D\ge3/2\), the Kesten lower particle-count scale \(N_R\gtrsim R^{3/2}\) is satisfied.

#### (v) Previously proved higher-moment hierarchy is also satisfied
For fixed \(q>1\),
\[
m_q:=\sum_xp(x)d(x)^q
=\kappa^q\sum_xp(x)^{2q+1}.
\tag{6.11}
\]
The heavy contribution is \(R^{-D(2q+1)/3}\), while the light contribution is \(O(R^{-2q})\). Therefore
\[
N_Rm_q
\lesssim
R^{-\frac{2D}{3}(q-1)}+R^{D-2q}.
\tag{6.12}
\]
Since \(D\ge3/2\) and \(D\le2\), both exponents are at most \(-(q-1)\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
N_Rm_q\lesssim R^{-(q-1)},
}
\tag{6.13}
\]
which matches or improves the higher conditional-moment block bound proved in the archived DLA reports.

### Proof details

Equation (6.3) follows because \(D/3\ge1/2\). For entropy,
\[
H(p)
=-h_R\log h_R-R\ell_R\log\ell_R
=(1-h_R)\log R+O(h_R\log R)+O(1)
=\log R+o(\log R).
\]
For the third moment,
\[
S_3=h_R^3+R\ell_R^3
=R^{-D}+\frac{(1-h_R)^3}{R^2}.
\]
The remaining claims follow from the displayed computations. QED.

### What this theorem does and does not say

It does **not** construct a DLA cluster, and it does **not** refute the expected existence of a DLA exponent. It is a logical sharpness certificate for proof strategies: the presently archived scalar inequalities are compatible with every \(D\in[3/2,2]\), i.e. every radial exponent \(\beta=1/D\in[1/2,2/3]\). Therefore no manipulation of only those scalar bounds can determine the exponent.

Any successful proof must introduce an additional structural input that this synthetic family deliberately omits: spatial screening geometry, correlations between successive harmonic measures, locality of the response state, the projective genealogy, or a true renormalization/ergodic principle.

## 7. A second sharpness check from variation alone

The recovered variation theorem gives, in a capacity-scale annulus of radius \(R\), order-one quadratic variation, maximum TV step \(O(R^{-1/2})\), and for every \(q>2\) a \(q\)-variation upper bound of order \(R^{-(q-2)/2}\). The local Kesten result supplies a particle lower bound of order \(R^{3/2}\) over a bounded capacity window.

For any \(D\in[3/2,2]\), the synthetic deterministic block
\[
N=\lfloor R^D\rfloor,
\qquad
\delta_i=N^{-1/2}
\tag{7.1}
\]
satisfies
\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\delta_i^2=1,
\qquad
\max_i\delta_i=R^{-D/2+o(1)}\le R^{-1/2},
\tag{7.2}
\]
and for \(q>2\),
\[
\sum_i\delta_i^q
=N^{1-q/2}
=R^{-\frac D2(q-2)+o(1)}
\le R^{-\frac34(q-2)+o(1)},
\tag{7.3}
\]
which is stronger than the general archived upper bound \(O(R^{-(q-2)/2})\). It also has \(N\ge R^{3/2+o(1)}\).

Thus the pathwise scalar variation hierarchy by itself likewise permits the full interval \(D\in[3/2,2]\). The unresolved information is in the **dependence and geometry of the steps**, not their scalar variation spectrum alone.

\bar h_{k\ell}

\[
c_{k\ell}=\delta_{\ell+1}\bar h_{k\ell}.

\bar h_{k\ell}\lesssim\delta_{k+1}+\varepsilon_{k\ell},
