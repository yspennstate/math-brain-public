## 0. Exact target and result of this iteration

For standard external site diffusion-limited aggregation on \(\mathbb Z^2\), start from \(A_1=\{0\}\). Given a finite connected cluster \(A_n\), let \(\partial A_n\) be its outer vertex boundary and \(\overline A_n=A_n\cup\partial A_n\). The next site \(X_{n+1}\in\partial A_n\) is sampled by simple-random-walk harmonic measure from infinity,
\[
p_n(x)=\omega(x,\overline A_n),
\]
and attached. Let
\[
R_n:=\max_{x\in A_n}|x|_2.
\]
The full target is to prove
\[
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}\to\beta
\qquad\text{almost surely}
\]
for a deterministic \(\beta\), and identify \(\beta\), or rigorously refute existence/determinism for this exact lattice model.

The full target remains **OPEN**. No exact exponent is claimed.

This run adds a new exact information-theoretic reduction. For an arbitrary finite interval of particle times, define two joint laws on `(time, boundary site)`:

- the **particle Palm law**: choose a time uniformly and then choose the DLA attachment site by harmonic measure;
- the **capacity Palm law**: choose a `(time, site)` in proportion to its contribution to expected capacity growth.

The Kullback–Leibler divergence between these two laws gives an exact entropy budget for the number of particle times in the interval. On a unit-capacity block this becomes
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\log N_j}{\log R_j}
=
2-
\frac{D(\mathsf P_j\|\mathsf Q_j)}{\log R_j}
+o(1),
}
\]
where \(N_j\) is the particle count in the block and \(R_j\asymp e^{\pi j/2}\).

Moreover the KL divergence splits **exactly** into two nonnegative mechanisms:
\[
\boxed{
D(\mathsf P_j\|\mathsf Q_j)
=
D(U_j\|w_j)
+
\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k.
}
\]
Here \(D(U_j\|w_j)\) measures **temporal intermittency** of predictable capacity growth within the block, while
\[
I_k=D(p_k\|r_k)
\]
is the previously identified **spatial harmonic-measure intermittency** at time \(k\).

Therefore the local particle dimension satisfies
\[
\boxed{
D_j^{\rm block}
=
2-
\frac{D(U_j\|w_j)}{\log R_j}
-
\frac{1}{N_j\log R_j}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k
+o(1).
}
\]
Using the recovered Shannon–Rényi identity, the second defect is twice the average Shannon/Rényi-3 entropy gap, up to \(o(1)\) after normalization.

This is a stricter decomposition than the previous block free-energy formula: it identifies exactly why a block can have particle dimension below the compact value \(2\). The deficit is the sum of (i) heterogeneity across particle times and (ii) spatial screening/intermittency within the harmonic measure.

A second new theorem treats the reverse direction. For every fixed \(q>1\), the Rényi divergence from the capacity Palm law back to the particle Palm law satisfies
\[
\boxed{
D_q(\mathsf Q_j\|\mathsf P_j)
\le
\log N_j-\log R_j+O_q(\log\log R_j).
}
\]
Combining both directions gives the information-diameter inequality
\[
\boxed{
D(\mathsf P_j\|\mathsf Q_j)
+
D_q(\mathsf Q_j\|\mathsf P_j)
\le
\log R_j+O_q(\log\log R_j).
}
\]
In particular the Jeffreys divergence obeys the same upper bound.

The full exponent problem is thus reduced to stabilization of this **particle-versus-capacity Palm information defect** across large intrinsic-capacity scales. The formulas also make clear why existing static potential-theory estimates stall at \(\beta=1/2\): they do not force either nonnegative defect term to remain a positive fraction of \(\log R\).

# 4. New general Palm entropy-budget identity

The key observation of this run works for **any finite interval of particle times**, not only capacity blocks.

Let \(J\) be a nonempty finite set of consecutive particle times. Write
\[
N:=|J|,
\qquad
A_J:=\sum_{k\in J}a_k.
\]
Assume \(A_J>0\), which is automatic for the DLA intervals considered here.

Define two probability laws on pairs \((k,x)\), where \(k\in J\) and \(x\in\partial A_k\) is accessible.

### Particle Palm law

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf P_J(k,x):=\frac1N p_k(x).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Thus one first chooses a particle time uniformly from \(J\), then chooses the site using the ordinary DLA attachment law.

### Capacity Palm law

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_J(k,x):=
\frac{p_k(x)d_k(x)}{A_J}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Thus a pair is chosen in proportion to its contribution to predictable capacity growth.

The time marginal of \(\mathsf P_J\) is uniform,
\[
U_J(k)=\frac1N,
\tag{4.3}
\]
while the time marginal of \(\mathsf Q_J\) is
\[
w_J(k)=\frac{a_k}{A_J}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Conditioned on time \(k\), the spatial laws are respectively \(p_k\) and \(r_k\).

### Theorem 4.1 — exact entropy budget — PROVED

Let
\[
\overline H_J:=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}H_k,
\qquad
\overline\mu_J:=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}\mu_k,
\qquad
\overline I_J:=\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}I_k.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_J\|\mathsf Q_J)
=
\log A_J-\log N-\overline\mu_J+2\overline H_J.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\log N
=
2\overline H_J-\overline\mu_J+\log A_J
-
D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_J\|\mathsf Q_J).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The same divergence has the exact chain-rule decomposition
\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm KL}(\mathsf P_J\|\mathsf Q_J)
=
D_{\rm KL}(U_J\|w_J)
+
\overline I_J.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\log N
=
2\overline H_J-\overline\mu_J+\log A_J
-
D_{\rm KL}(U_J\|w_J)
-
\overline I_J.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof

For an accessible pair \((k,x)\),
\[
\frac{\mathsf P_J(k,x)}{\mathsf Q_J(k,x)}
=
\frac{A_J}{N\,d_k(x)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\mathsf P_J\|\mathsf Q_J)
&=
\sum_{k\in J}\sum_x\frac{p_k(x)}N
\log\frac{A_J}{N\,d_k(x)}\\
&=
\log A_J-\log N
-
\frac1N\sum_{k\in J}\sum_xp_k(x)\log d_k(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.9}
\]
Since
\[
d_k(x)=\kappa_k(x)p_k(x)^2,
\qquad
\kappa_k(x):=d_k(x)/p_k(x)^2,
\]
we have
\[
\sum_xp_k(x)\log d_k(x)
=
\mu_k+2\sum_xp_k(x)\log p_k(x)
=
\mu_k-2H_k.
\]
Substitution proves (4.5)–(4.6).

For (4.7), apply the ordinary KL chain rule to the time coordinate. The time marginals are \(U_J\) and \(w_J\); conditioned on time \(k\), the two spatial laws are \(p_k\) and \(r_k\). Therefore
\[
D(\mathsf P_J\|\mathsf Q_J)
=
D(U_J\|w_J)
+
\sum_{k\in J}U_J(k)D(p_k\|r_k),
\]
which is exactly (4.7). QED.

### Interpretation

Equation (4.8) is an exact accounting identity. The leading compact two-dimensional count \(2\overline H_J\) is reduced by two distinct nonnegative information costs:

1. **temporal intermittency**
   \[
   D(U_J\|w_J),
   \]
   measuring how nonuniformly predictable capacity is produced across particle times;
2. **spatial intermittency**
   \[
   \overline I_J,
   \]
   measuring how different a typical harmonic-measure attachment is from a capacity-effective attachment at the same time.

The bounded amplitude term \(\overline\mu_J\) cannot affect a power-law exponent.

# 10. Exact remaining gap and a sharper route to \(\beta>1/2\)

Formula (5.4) gives an exact sufficient condition for a strict improvement over the trivial lower growth exponent.

If there exists a deterministic \(\delta>0\) such that almost surely, for all sufficiently large capacity blocks,
\[
\boxed{

+
\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k
\ge
\delta\log\mathcal R_j,
}
\tag{10.1}
\]
then
\[
D_j^{\rm block}\le2-\delta+o(1).
\]
Consequently the number of particles needed per unit capacity is at most \(\mathcal R_j^{2-\delta+o(1)}\), and the radial growth exponent obeys
\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\ge
\frac1{2-\delta}
>
\frac12.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

This would already be a major rigorous advance for planar DLA. Importantly, (10.1) offers **two** possible sources for the needed positive defect:

1. persistent temporal heterogeneity in the capacity drift \(a_k\), or
2. persistent spatial screening measured by the average KL divergence \(I_k\).

A proof does not need to establish a pointwise multifractal exponent if it can show that these two mechanisms cannot both be sublinear throughout a large capacity block.

For full exponent existence, it would suffice to prove that the normalized combined information defect
\[
\boxed{
\delta_j
:=
\frac{
D(U_j\|w_j)+N_j^{-1}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k

\tag{10.3}

has a deterministic limiting running-envelope in the sense already developed for the block dimension. If \(\delta_j\to\delta\), then
\[
D_j^{\rm block}\to2-\delta

and
\[
\boxed{\beta=\frac1{2-\delta}.}
\tag{10.4}
\]

The relation with the earlier notation is
\[
\delta_j=\Theta_j+o(1).
\]
The new contribution is the exact decomposition of \(\Theta_j\) into temporal and spatial information production.

## 11. Why the present static bounds still do not close the problem

The identity also clarifies the limitation of all source estimates currently available.

### Discrete Makarov

It controls
\[
H_k=\log R_k+O(\log\log R_k),
\]
which supplies the leading compact term \(2\log R\), but does not force a positive power-scale lower bound on either divergence in (10.1).

### Beurling

It controls the hottest site and therefore high conditional moments of capacity growth. This yields the anti-condensation hierarchy and reverse-divergence bound, but again does not force forward KL away from zero.

### Kesten / Losev–Smirnov branch estimate

It prevents capacity/radius from growing too efficiently and yields recurrent \(D\ge3/2\) blocks. It does not rule out alternating or sparse near-compact blocks with \(D\approx2\).

### Local one-site response

The recovered finite-dimensional formula shows that the factor multiplying \(p^2\) is bounded and largely local. Hence amplitude fluctuations cannot change the power exponent; the obstruction really is the distribution of harmonic measure and its temporal organization.

Thus the missing theorem is necessarily dynamic: one must prove that DLA cannot repeatedly organize itself into capacity blocks in which both temporal and spatial intermittency are only subpower.

## 12. Adversarial checking pass

The following points were separately checked in this run.

1. **Both joint laws normalize.**  \(\sum_{k,x}\mathsf P_J(k,x)=1\) because each \(p_k\) is a probability law and time is uniform. Also
   \[
   \sum_{k,x}\mathsf Q_J(k,x)
   =A_J^{-1}\sum_ka_k=1.
   \]

2. **Likelihood ratio cancellation.**  In \(\mathsf P/\mathsf Q\), the factor \(p_k(x)\) cancels, leaving exactly \(A_J/(Nd_k(x))\). No extra harmonic-measure term is omitted.

3. **KL chain-rule orientation.**  For \(D(\mathsf P\|\mathsf Q)\), the time term is \(D(U\|w)\) and the conditional spatial term is the **uniform-time** average \(N^{-1}\sum D(p_k\|r_k)\). It is not a capacity-weighted average.

4. **Entropy sign.**  Since \(\log d=\log\kappa+2\log p\),
   \[
   \mathbb E_p\log d=\mu-2H,
   \]
   leading to the positive \(2H\) term in the particle-count budget.

5. **Capacity-block scale.**  Throughout \(B_j\), \(\log R=\pi j/2+O(1)\), while Makarov contributes only \(O(\log j)\), so after division by \(\log R\asymp j\) all displayed error terms vanish.

6. **Reverse Rényi algebra.**  In \(\mathsf Q^q\mathsf P^{1-q}\), the powers of \(p\) reduce to a single factor \(p\), giving exactly \(m_{q,k}=\sum p d^q\).

7. **Rényi monotonicity.**  For \(q>1\), \(D(\mathsf Q\|\mathsf P)\le D_q(\mathsf Q\|\mathsf P)\), so the Jeffreys bound follows from the proved reverse Rényi bound.

8. **No circular exponent assumption.**  The Palm identities are finite-block algebraic identities plus primary-source potential theory. They do not assume existence of \(\beta\), a scaling limit, or a multifractal spectrum.

9. **Model scope.**  Every proved statement is for standard external site DLA on \(\mathbb Z^2\). Off-lattice/Hastings–Levitov and numerical dimension estimates are status/context only.

This is adversarial self-review, not independent verification.

## 13. Failed / insufficient routes retained

- **Static Makarov entropy alone:** fixes the compact leading entropy but allows both information-defect terms to vanish at power scale.
- **Beurling / high moments:** controls concentration and fast growth, but not excessively diffuse slow capacity blocks.
- **Local Kesten recurrence:** guarantees frequent blocks of dimension at least \(3/2\), but does not exclude sparse dimension-\(2\) blocks that could destroy convergence or force liminf exponent \(1/2\).
- **Pointwise third-moment convergence:** sufficient by earlier work, but it is stronger than necessary and remains unproved.
- **Amplitude/local-response analysis alone:** the response coefficient is bounded, so it cannot create the missing power law.
- **Numerical/off-lattice evidence:** not an exact theorem for the standard lattice process.

## 14. Next concrete attack

The Palm decomposition suggests a sharper two-pronged attack than the previous single-observable formulation.

### Attack A: temporal-information lower bound

The time weights are
\[
w_j(k)=\frac{a_k}{A_j}.
\]
Try to prove that in a large capacity block the predictable capacity increments cannot remain too close to the uniform value \(1/N_j\). A power-scale lower bound
\[
D(U_j\|w_j)\ge\delta_t\log R_j
\]
would directly reduce the particle dimension.

Potential mechanisms:

- quantify how an attachment changes the hot-tip set and therefore forces drift oscillation;
- use the recovered local response/locality formula to show that a capacity-producing tip cannot sustain nearly constant drift for \(R^{2-o(1)}\) consecutive attachments;
- couple equal-capacity blocks and exploit square-summable direct martingale noise.

### Attack B: spatial-information lower bound

Try to prove
\[
\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k
\ge\delta_s\log R_j.
\]
By the Shannon–Rényi identity this is equivalent, up to lower-order terms, to an averaged positive gap between Shannon and order-3 Rényi entropy.

Potential mechanisms:

- study the cubic harmonic-measure tilt \(p^3/S_3\), which is uniformly comparable to capacity bias;
- prove that the environment seen from this cubic tilt cannot be asymptotically uniform on an \(R^{1+o(1)}\)-sized boundary set;
- exploit the local Kesten branch theorem to force recurring screened/hot-tip configurations.

### Combined target

The weakest genuinely new power-law milestone is not separate control of A or B. It is enough to prove
\[
\boxed{
D(U_j\|w_j)+\frac1{N_j}\sum_{k\in B_j}I_k
\ge\delta\log R_j
}
\]
for some universal \(\delta>0\) and all sufficiently large blocks. This would give the first rigorous strict improvement \(\beta>1/2\).

For full exponent existence, the target becomes stabilization of this combined defect in capacity time.
