## 0. Exact target and result of this iteration

For standard external site diffusion-limited aggregation on \(\mathbb Z^2\), start from \(A_1=\{0\}\). Given a finite connected cluster \(A_n\), let \(\partial A_n\) be its outer vertex boundary and \(\overline A_n=A_n\cup\partial A_n\). The next site \(X_{n+1}\in\partial A_n\) is sampled from simple-random-walk harmonic measure from infinity,
\[
p_n(x)=\omega(x,\overline A_n),
\]
and attached. Let
\[
R_n:=\max_{x\in A_n}|x|_2.
\]
The full target is to prove
\[
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}\to\beta
\qquad\text{almost surely}
\]
for a deterministic \(\beta\), and identify \(\beta\), or rigorously refute existence/determinism for this exact lattice model.

The full target remains **OPEN**. No exact exponent is claimed.

This iteration adds two substantive structural results to the verified capacity-block framework.

**(A) Local Kesten annular barrier.**  Specializing the Losev–Smirnov branch-counting proof to DLA and using it conditionally from an arbitrary current connected cluster gives a uniform fast-doubling estimate: there are universal \(c,R_0>0\) such that, from any finite connected cluster of radius \(R\ge R_0\),
\[
\mathbb P\{\text{radius reaches }2R\text{ within }cR^{3/2}\text{ new particles}\}\le 2^{-R}.
\]
Consequently, almost surely every sufficiently large radial doubling requires at least \(cR^{3/2}\) particles. Translated into the verified capacity blocks, there is a fixed integer \(L_0\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{j+L_0}-\sigma_j\ge \exp\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}j-O(1)\right)
}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\), almost surely. Therefore in **every fixed-length capacity window** \([j,j+L_0)\), some block satisfies
\[
\boxed{\Theta_\ell\le\frac12+O\!\left(\frac{\log j}{j}\right).}
\]
This upgrades the previous report's much weaker statement that a low-intermittency block occurs somewhere in a macroscopic recent fraction of the history: low-intermittency/Kesten-scale blocks recur with bounded gaps in capacity time.

**(B) Higher-moment anti-condensation.**  Let \(r_n(x)=p_n(x)d_n(x)/a_n\) be the capacity-biased boundary law, \(I_n=D_{\rm KL}(p_n\|r_n)\), and for \(q>1\) let \(D_q(r_n\|p_n)\) be Rényi divergence. For each unit-capacity block \(B_j\), the verified first partition function obeys
\[
\log\sum_{k\in B_j}e^{I_k}=\pi j+O(\log j).
\]
This run proves, for every fixed \(q>1\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in B_j}
\exp\!\bigl(qI_k+(q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)\bigr)
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{\pi(q+1)}2j+O_q(\log j)\right).
}
\]
If
\[
\nu_j(k):=\frac{e^{I_k}}{\sum_{m\in B_j}e^{I_m}},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in B_j}\nu_j(k)^q
\exp\!\bigl((q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)\bigr)
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\pi(q-1)}2j+O_q(\log j)\right).
}
\]
In particular, dropping the nonnegative divergence term,
\[
\boxed{
H_q(\nu_j)\ge\frac\pi2j-O_q(\log j),
}
\]
so the intermittency/capacity partition weight cannot condense onto fewer than \(\exp((\pi/2)j-o(j))\asymp R\) particle times in any fixed Rényi order. For \(q=2\), the penalty is exactly
\[
D_2(r_n\|p_n)=\log\frac{\mathbb E[\Delta_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]}{a_n^2},
\]
the logarithm of one plus the conditional squared coefficient of variation of the capacity increment.

These results still do not control the **upper** particle dimension of a capacity block, which is precisely what would be needed to prove \(\beta>1/2\) or full convergence. They do, however, sharply isolate the remaining obstruction: persistent oscillation toward unusually diffuse, near-two-dimensional capacity blocks.

# 4. Uniform fast-doubling lemma extracted from the Kesten/Losev–Smirnov proof

### Lemma 4.1 — PROVED

There are universal constants \(c>0\) and \(R_0<\infty\) with the following property. Let \(A\subset\mathbb Z^2\) be any finite connected cluster with radius \(R\ge R_0\), and continue **standard external DLA** from \(A\). Let \(T_{2R}\) denote the number of additional particles needed before the evolving cluster first attains radius at least \(2R\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P_A\{T_{2R}\le cR^{3/2}\}\le2^{-R}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
The bound is uniform over the shape of \(A\).

### Proof

This is the DLA \((\eta=1)\) specialization of the branch count in the proof of Losev–Smirnov Theorem 3, written conditionally from an arbitrary initial connected cluster.

Assume the radius reaches \(2R\) within
\[
m:=\lfloor cR^{3/2}\rfloor
\]
new attachments. Before the first such crossing there exists an outward nearest-neighbour branch of newly attached sites crossing the annulus from radius \(R\) to radius \(2R\). Let its length be \(L\). Up to harmless lattice constants, \(L\ge R\). Choose the \(L\) attachment times and the lattice path.

While this first crossing branch is being formed, all relevant cluster radii lie between \(R\) and \(2R<100R\). Losev–Smirnov Corollary 6 therefore gives, for the harmonic measures \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_L\) of the branch sites at their attachment times,
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{L}\omega_i
\le
(C_1L^{-1/2})^L.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For DLA, attachment probability equals harmonic measure, with no DBM normalization factor. The number of choices of attachment times is \(\binom mL\). The number of possible outward nearest-neighbour branch paths is bounded by \(CR\,3^L\): there are \(O(R)\) possible first crossings of the radius-\(R\) shell and at most three nonbacktracking choices per subsequent branch step. Thus
\[
\mathbb P_A\{T_{2R}\le m\}
\le
CR\sum_{L=R}^{m}
\binom mL
(3C_1L^{-1/2})^L.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Use
\[
\binom mL\le\left(\frac{em}{L}\right)^L.
\]
The summand is at most
\[
CR\left(3eC_1mL^{-3/2}\right)^L.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Since \(L\ge R\) and \(m\le cR^{3/2}\),
\[
3eC_1mL^{-3/2}\le3eC_1c.
\]
Choose \(c\) so small that \(3eC_1c<1/8\). Then
\[
\mathbb P_A\{T_{2R}\le m\}
\le
CRm\sum_{L\ge R}8^{-L}
\le2^{-R}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(R\), after enlarging \(R_0\). No property of the initial shape except finite connectedness and radius \(R\) entered the estimate. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — local annular Kesten bound

Let
\[
\rho_m:=\inf\{n:R_n\ge2^m\}.
\]
Then almost surely, for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\boxed{
\rho_{m+1}-\rho_m
\ge c\,2^{3m/2}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

At the stopping time \(\rho_m\), apply Lemma 4.1 conditionally to the current connected cluster. The conditional probability of violating (4.5) is at most \(2^{-2^m}\), and
\[
\sum_m2^{-2^m}<\infty.
\]
Taking expectations and applying Borel–Cantelli proves the claim. QED.

This is stronger than merely knowing the global asymptotic inequality \(R_n\lesssim n^{2/3}\): it gives the \(3/2\)-particle lower exponent separately for every sufficiently large dyadic annulus.

# 5. Fixed-capacity-window consequence

Let \(K_0\) be a deterministic constant in the capacity-radius estimate
\[
\left|C_n-\frac2\pi\log R_n\right|\le K_0.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Choose once and for all an integer \(L_0\) satisfying
\[
\frac\pi2\bigl(L_0-2K_0-1\bigr)>\log2.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — PROVED

Almost surely, for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{j+L_0}-\sigma_j
\ge
c_1\exp\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}j\right)
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
for a deterministic \(c_1>0\).

### Proof

By the vanishing capacity overshoot,
\[
C_{\sigma_j}=j+o(1),
\qquad
C_{\sigma_{j+L_0}}=j+L_0+o(1).
\]
Using (5.1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{R_{\sigma_{j+L_0}}}{R_{\sigma_j}}
&\ge
\frac\pi2\left[
(C_{\sigma_{j+L_0}}-C_{\sigma_j})-2K_0
\right]\\
&=
\frac\pi2(L_0-2K_0-o(1))
>\log2
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]
for all large \(j\). Hence the radius must at least double during the interval \([\sigma_j,\sigma_{j+L_0}]\).

At time \(\sigma_j\), (5.1) gives
\[
R_{\sigma_j}
=\exp\!\left(\frac\pi2j+O(1)\right).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}\), Lemma 4.1 says that the probability this doubling occurs in fewer than
\[
cR_{\sigma_j}^{3/2}
=
\exp\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}j+O(1)\right)
\]
particles is at most
\[
2^{-R_{\sigma_j}}
\le
\exp\{-c_2e^{\pi j/2}\}.
\]
These probabilities are summable in \(j\). Borel–Cantelli yields (5.3). QED.

# 6. Higher conditional moments and Rényi divergence of the capacity tilt

For fixed \(q>1\), define the conditional \(q\)-th moment of one-step capacity growth
\[
m_{q,n}:=
\mathbb E[\Delta_{n+1}^q\mid\mathcal F_n]
=
\sum_xp_n(x)d_n(x)^q.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — PROVED

For every fixed \(q>1\),
\[
\boxed{
m_{q,n}\le C_q(p_n^*)^{2q-2}a_n.}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Consequently, on a capacity block \(B_j\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}m_{q,n}
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac\pi2(q-1)j+O_q(1)\right).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof

From (3.2),
\[
m_{q,n}
\le
C_0^q\sum_xp_n(x)^{2q+1}.
\]
Since
\[
p_n(x)^{2q+1}
=p_n(x)^{2q-2}p_n(x)^3
\le
(p_n^*)^{2q-2}p_n(x)^3,
\]
we get
\[
m_{q,n}
\le
C_q(p_n^*)^{2q-2}S_3(n).
\]
The lower side of (3.2), after multiplying by \(p_n(x)\) and summing, gives \(a_n\ge cS_3(n)\), proving (6.2).

On \(B_j\), Beurling plus (3.8) gives
\[
p_n^*\le C R_n^{-1/2}
=\exp\!\left(-\frac\pi4j+O(1)\right),
\]
while \(\sum_{B_j}a_n=1+o(1)\). Summing (6.2) proves (6.3). QED.

Now recall the capacity-biased boundary law
\[
r_n(x)=\frac{p_n(x)d_n(x)}{a_n}.
\]
For \(q>1\), its order-\(q\) Rényi divergence from the DLA attachment law is
\[
D_q(r_n\|p_n)
:=
\frac1{q-1}
\log\sum_x r_n(x)^q p_n(x)^{1-q}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
A direct substitution yields the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
(q-1)D_q(r_n\|p_n)
=
\log\frac{m_{q,n}}{a_n^q}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Indeed
\[
\sum_xr_n(x)^qp_n(x)^{1-q}
=
\frac{\sum_xp_n(x)d_n(x)^q}{a_n^q}
=
\frac{m_{q,n}}{a_n^q}.
\]

For \(q=2\),
\[
\boxed{
D_2(r_n\|p_n)
=
\log\frac{\mathbb E[\Delta_{n+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_n]}{a_n^2}
=
\log\left(1+rac{\operatorname{Var}(\Delta_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)}{a_n^2}\right).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Thus the reverse Rényi divergence of the capacity-biased law measures the conditional multiplicative noise of capacity growth.

# 7. Higher-moment anti-condensation theorem inside a capacity block

Define the verified intermittency partition function
\[
Z_j:=\sum_{k\in B_j}e^{I_k},
\qquad
\log Z_j=\pi j+O(\log j),
\tag{7.1}
\]
and the corresponding Gibbs law on particle times
\[
\nu_j(k):=\frac{e^{I_k}}{Z_j},
\qquad k\in B_j.
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — PROVED

For every fixed \(q>1\), almost surely for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in B_j}
\exp\!\left(
qI_k+(q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)
\right)
\le
\exp\!\left(
\frac{\pi(q+1)}2j+O_q(\log j)
\right).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in B_j}
\nu_j(k)^q
\exp\!\left((q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)\right)
\le
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{\pi(q-1)}2j+O_q(\log j)
\right).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
In particular, since Rényi divergence is nonnegative,
\[
\boxed{
H_q(\nu_j)
:=\frac1{1-q}\log\sum_{k\in B_j}\nu_j(k)^q
\ge
\frac\pi2j-O_q(\log j).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

### Proof

On \(B_j\), the verified identity (3.11) gives
\[
a_k^q
=
\exp\!\left(-q\pi j+qI_k+O_q(\log j)\right).
\tag{7.6}
\]
By the exact Rényi identity (6.5),
\[
m_{q,k}
=a_k^q
\exp\!\left((q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{k\in B_j}m_{q,k}
=
e^{-q\pi j+O_q(\log j)}
\sum_{k\in B_j}
\exp\!\left(qI_k+(q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)\right).
\tag{7.7}
\]
Lemma 6.1 bounds the left side by
\[
\exp\!\left(-\frac\pi2(q-1)j+O_q(1)\right).
\]
Multiplying by \(e^{q\pi j}\) yields (7.3). Divide by
\[
Z_j^q
=
\exp(q\pi j+O_q(\log j))
\]
to obtain (7.4). Finally drop the factor
\[
\exp((q-1)D_q)\ge1
\]
and rearrange to get (7.5). QED.

### Interpretation

At capacity level \(j\), radius is
\[
R\asymp e^{\pi j/2}.
\]
Equation (7.5) says that in every fixed Rényi order, the block intermittency weight \(e^{I_k}\) has effective support at least
\[
\exp\!\left(\frac\pi2j-O(\log j)\right)
=R\,j^{-O(1)}.
\]
Thus expected capacity growth cannot be carried by a sublinear-in-radius number of particle times. This is an **anti-condensation theorem** in particle time.

For \(q=2\), (7.4) is particularly transparent:
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in B_j}\nu_j(k)^2
\frac{\mathbb E[\Delta_{k+1}^2\mid\mathcal F_k]}{a_k^2}
\le
\exp\!\left(-\frac\pi2j+O(\log j)\right).
}
\tag{7.8}
\]
So concentration of the capacity/intermittency Gibbs weight and large conditional coefficient of variation cannot occur simultaneously on a significant fraction of a capacity block.

## 8. What these results do and do not solve

The previous verified report defines the block particle exponent through
\[
\frac{\log N_j}{\log R_{\sigma_j}}
=
2-\Theta_j+o(1).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Theorem 5.1 implies that every bounded capacity-time window contains a block with
\[
2-\Theta_j\ge\frac32-o(1).
\]
Theorem 7.1 implies, independently, that capacity/intermittency weight within any single block is spread across at least \(R^{1-o(1)}\) particle times.

Neither statement prevents occasional blocks with
\[
2-\Theta_j
\]
close to \(2\). Such blocks correspond to anomalously **slow** radial growth, not fast growth. Ruling out or self-averaging these diffuse blocks is exactly what is needed to improve the trivial lower exponent \(\beta\ge1/2\) and, ultimately, prove convergence.

This clarifies why sharpening Beurling/Kesten alone is unlikely to finish the problem: those arguments forbid capacity growth from concentrating too aggressively on a tiny set of tips, while existence of \(\beta\) also requires controlling the opposite phenomenon—long capacity intervals in which harmonic measure is unusually diffuse and particle count per capacity increment is near the compact \(R^2\) scale.

## 9. Failed or insufficient routes retained

- **Global Kesten alone:** gives \(\beta\le2/3\), but before this run only forced a good block somewhere in a large recent capacity fraction. The conditional branch proof upgrades this to bounded-gap good blocks, but still gives no upper control on block particle dimension.
- **Beurling alone:** forces at least order \(R\) particles per unit capacity and yields anti-condensation, but this corresponds only to the weak \(\beta\le1\) scale.
- **Discrete Makarov alone:** controls Shannon entropy but permits large Shannon–Rényi gaps; it does not rule out near-two-dimensional slow blocks.
- **Third-moment scaling ansatz:** if \(S_3=R^{-\tau_3+o(1)}\) with a limit, the backfilled theorem gives \(\beta=1/\tau_3\), but proving that limit is the target, not an available input.
- **Off-lattice/Hastings–Levitov relations:** remain heuristic/contextual for this lattice target.
- **Numerical dimension estimates:** do not prove almost-sure exponent existence or an exact value.
- **Direct capacity martingale noise:** already eliminated as a power-law obstruction by its convergence.
- **Higher conditional moments alone:** Theorem 7.1 prevents capacity condensation but does not prevent arbitrarily diffuse blocks; therefore it cannot by itself prove \(\beta>1/2\).

## 10. Adversarial checking pass

1. **Model scope.** Lemma 4.1 is specialized to DLA \(\eta=1\), where attachment probability is exactly harmonic measure. No DBM normalization remains.
2. **Uniformity in initial shape.** The branch count uses only connectedness, current radius, path counting, and Corollary 6. It does not require the cluster to have started from \(\{0\}\) at time zero, so the strong Markov restart at stopping times is legitimate.
3. **First crossing branch.** Before first radius \(2R\), all branch attachment times occur while radius lies in \([R,2R]\subset[R,100R]\), exactly the hypothesis needed for Losev–Smirnov Corollary 6.
4. **Branch length.** Crossing an annulus of width \(R\) requires \(L\ge cR\) nearest-neighbour steps. Replacing \(R\) by \(cR\) only changes constants; the proof used \(L\ge R\) after absorbing lattice constants into the scale definition.
5. **Combinatorial factor.** \(\binom mL\le(em/L)^L\), and with \(m=cR^{3/2}\), \(L\ge R\), the base is uniformly \(O(c)\). Choosing \(c\) small gives exponential decay in \(R\).
6. **Capacity-window radius growth.** The fixed integer \(L_0\) is chosen only after fixing the deterministic constant in the uniform capacity-radius comparison, so a capacity increase of \(L_0\) forces a radial factor above two despite the bounded comparison error.
7. **Borel–Cantelli.** Conditional fast-doubling probabilities are bounded by \(2^{-R_{\sigma_j}}\), and capacity-radius comparison gives \(R_{\sigma_j}\ge e^{cj}\), making the unconditional probability series summable.
8. **Higher moment power.** Because the selected site has probability \(p\) and \(d=O(p^2)\), the \(q\)-th conditional moment is \(O(\sum p^{2q+1})\), not \(O(\sum p^{2q})\).
9. **Rényi identity orientation.** The divergence in (6.5) is \(D_q(r\|p)\), not \(D_q(p\|r)\). Substituting \(r=pd/a\) gives exactly \(m_q/a^q\).
10. **Nonnegativity.** \(D_q(r\|p)\ge0\), so dropping its exponential factor in (7.4) gives a valid upper bound on \(\sum\nu^q\).
11. **No circular exponent assumption.** None of the new results assumes existence of \(\beta\), a fractal dimension, or convergence of a multifractal spectrum.

## 11. Next concrete attack

The bounded-gap Kesten barrier shows that the fast-growth side is locally controlled in intrinsic capacity time. The unresolved side is **diffuse slow blocks** with particle dimension close to two. The next attack should target those directly.

A concrete route is to study the capacity-block particle law
\[
w_{j,k}:=\frac{a_k}{\sum_{m\in B_j}a_m}
\]
and its relation to the uniform law on \(B_j\). Define the block entropy deficit
\[
\mathcal D_j:=D_{\rm KL}(w_j\|U_j)
=\log N_j-H(w_j).
\]
Beurling gives \(\max w_{j,k}\lesssim R^{-1}\), hence \(H(w_j)\gtrsim\log R\). A block with particle dimension near two has \(\log N_j\approx2\log R\), so such a block requires an entropy deficit of order at most \(\log R\) while spreading unit capacity across nearly \(R^2\) particles. The needed theorem is a **positive lower bound on capacity concentration**: prove that, under the cubic/capacity tilt, a fixed fraction of unit capacity is typically carried by only \(R^{2-\delta}\) particles for some deterministic \(\delta>0\). Any such statement would give \(\beta\ge1/(2-\delta)>1/2\), the first strict improvement over the trivial lower exponent.

A more quantitative next lemma is to combine the new hierarchy
\[
\sum_{B_j}\nu_j(k)^q e^{(q-1)D_q(r_k\|p_k)}
\]
with discrete Makarov to seek a **reverse anti-diffusion estimate** at some \(q<1\) or through truncated harmonic measure. The current \(q>1\) bounds rule out excessive concentration; a complementary lower-tail estimate for the harmonic measure would rule out excessive diffusion. This is exactly the missing direction not supplied by Beurling or Kesten.
