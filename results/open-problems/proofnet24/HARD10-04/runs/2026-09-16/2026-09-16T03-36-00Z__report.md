# 7. A one-step anti-persistence theorem

Since \(E_n^{(3)}\subset E_n^{(2)}\), the previsible conditional probability that a successful direct parent-child continuation persists for one additional generation is
\[
\mathbb P(E_n^{(3)}\mid E_n^{(2)},\mathcal F_n)
=
\frac{q_3(n)}{q_2(n)}
\]
whenever \(q_2(n)>0\).

From (4.4) and (5.7),
\[
\frac{q_3(n)}{q_2(n)}
\le
64\frac{S_3(n)}{S_2(n)}.
\]
But
\[
S_3(n)
=\sum_xp_n(x)^3
\le
p_n^*\sum_xp_n(x)^2
=p_n^*S_2(n).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(E_n^{(3)}\mid E_n^{(2)},\mathcal F_n)
\le64p_n^*
\le CR_n^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

This is a rigorous direct-tip anti-persistence statement.

It is important to state its scope accurately. Equation (7.1) is conditional on the **pre-burst cluster** \(\mathcal F_n\) and on the event that the next two particles form some direct parent-child pair. It averages over which parent and child realize that event. It does not claim that, after revealing a particular unusually exposed child, that child's next-step continuation probability is always \(O(R^{-1/2})\) relative to its own conditional information; such a pointwise statement is false at this level of information and is not used.

Equation (7.1) nevertheless says that direct genealogical growth is strongly anti-persistent in the attachment-law average: one direct continuation is common on the relevant block scale, while two consecutive direct continuations are down by an additional factor of order at least \(R^{-1/2}\).

# 9. Actual abundance via stopped Bernoulli martingales

\[

\[

For a Bernoulli variable \(I\) of conditional mean \(q\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-I}\mid\mathcal G]
=1-q(1-e^{-1})
\le
\exp[-c_*q],
\qquad c_*:=1-e^{-1}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Hence for each residue class the stopped process
\[
\exp\left(
 c_*\sum q_2(n)-\sum I_n^{(2)}
\right)

is a nonnegative supermartingale.

\[
H_{2,j}
:=
\sum_{n=\sigma_j}^{\sigma_{j+1}-2}I_n^{(2)}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
If the total predictable mass satisfies \(Q_{2,j}\ge A\), at least one parity class has predictable mass at least \(A/2\). If simultaneously
\[
H_{2,j}\le\frac{c_*}{4}A,
\]
then that parity class has realized count at most half of the supermartingale scale corresponding to its predictable mass. Ville's inequality and a union over the two parities give
\[
\mathbb P\left\{
H_{2,j}\le c_0A,
\ Q_{2,j}\ge A
\right\}
\le
2e^{-c_1A}
\tag{9.3}
\]
for universal \(c_0,c_1>0\).

H_{2,j}\le c'R_j^{1/2}

\le
2e^{-c''R_j^{1/2}}

# 13. Why this does not yet determine the radial exponent

The new results strongly constrain **temporal persistence of a single direct tip**, but they still do not determine how many total particles are needed for one unit of capacity.

The reason is visible in the separation
\[
H_{2,j}\gtrsim R_j^{1/2},
\qquad
H_{3,j}=O(\log\log R_j),
\]
while the total block particle count is only known rigorously to lie between the Kesten scale and the compact scale,
\[
R_j^{3/2-o(1)}\lesssim N_j\lesssim R_j^2.
\]
The direct one-edge micro-bursts form only a small subset of all particles even at the lower Kesten scale.

The theorem therefore rules out a particularly simple persistence mechanism for a nontrivial exponent: capacity cannot be generated by long runs in which the active tip is repeatedly selected in immediately successive generations. Such direct persistence is suppressed at the next generation by the Beurling factor \(R^{-1/2}\), and three-particle direct bursts occur only on the capacity/log-radius scale.

But the process can still preserve morphology by **switching among different tips or nearby branches**. After an isolated direct pair \(x\to y\), the next attachment is not a newborn child of \(y\), but it may occur on an old neighboring arm whose harmonic field is strongly correlated with the same large-scale fjord geometry.

Thus the remaining obstruction has sharpened from “tip renewal” to **spatial relocation after a failed direct continuation**.

D_n:=|Z-Y|
\]
and, more invariantly, the annular/capacity separation between the boundary component carrying \(Z\) and the short arm \(X_{n+1}\to Y\).

The new theorem guarantees \(\Omega(\sqrt R)\) such isolated micro-bursts per unit capacity. Therefore even a weak nontrivial statement about the switch kernel could accumulate to a strong block-level geometric constraint.

Two complementary sufficient directions are now concrete:

1. **Far-switch theorem.** Prove that a fixed positive fraction of isolated micro-bursts switch by a mesoscopic Euclidean scale \(R^\theta\), uniformly for some \(\theta>0\). Repeated \(\Omega(\sqrt R)\) far switches would force substantial spatial diversification of active harmonic measure.
2. **Near-switch multi-arm theorem.** If most switches remain within distance \(R^\theta\), prove that maintaining many such locally competing active tips creates a multi-arm/shielding penalty that improves the present cubic-moment bound.

Either branch would finally couple the exact local capacity/burst clock to spatial arm geometry. The present report shows why this is the right next scale: direct same-lineage persistence itself has already been reduced to only order-one predictable events per capacity unit.
