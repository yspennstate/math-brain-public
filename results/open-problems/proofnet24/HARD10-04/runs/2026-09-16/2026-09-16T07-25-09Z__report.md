# 4. Uniform spacetime local-time theorem

The previous checkpoint controlled quick returns from a typical starting particle and the occupancy of a box over the whole block. The present theorem is stronger because it is simultaneous over **every spatial ball and every temporal subinterval**.

### Theorem 4.1 — uniform sliding-window local time

There is a universal \(C<\infty\) such that, almost surely for all sufficiently large capacity blocks, the following holds simultaneously.

For every integer radius \(1\le L\le cR_j\), every lattice center \(y\) whose \(L\)-ball meets the deterministic \(O(R_j)\) enclosure of the block, and every particle-time subinterval
\[
I=[u,v)\subseteq B_j,
\qquad H:=v-u,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
N_I(y,L)
:=
\sum_{n=u}^{v-1}
\mathbf 1_{\{|X_{n+1}-y|\le L\}}
\le
C\left[
H\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R_j}}+\log R_j
\right].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

It is enough to state the theorem for dyadic \(L\); arbitrary \(L\) changes only the universal constant by enlargement to the next dyadic scale.

### Proof

Fix a block start \(\sigma_j\), a deterministic lattice center \(y\), a dyadic \(L\le cR_j\), and deterministic integer offsets
\[
0\le a<b\le C_1R_j^2,
\qquad H=b-a.
\]
Gate all indicators after the random block endpoint to zero and set, for \(k=a,\ldots,b-1\),
\[
I_k
=
\mathbf 1_{\{\sigma_j+k<\sigma_{j+1}\}}
\mathbf 1_{\{|X_{\sigma_j+k+1}-y|\le L\}}.
\]
The gate is predictable at the corresponding time. By (3.5),
\[
\mathbb E[I_k\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_j+k}]
\le
\varepsilon_j(L)
:=C_2\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R_j}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
For \(\lambda=1\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^{I_k}\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_j+k}]
=
1+(e-1)\mathbb E[I_k\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_j+k}]
\le
\exp((e-1)\varepsilon_j(L)).
\]
Iterating the conditional expectation therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\exp\left(
\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}I_k
-(e-1)\varepsilon_j(L)H
\right)
\middle|\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right]
\le1.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Consequently, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\mathbb P\left\{
\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}I_k
>(e-1)\varepsilon_j(L)H+u
\middle|\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right\}
\le e^{-u}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now count the tuples over which uniformity is required.

* By (3.2), only \(O(R_j^2)\) integer centers can have an \(L\)-ball meeting the \(O(R_j)\) disk containing the block.
* By (3.3), there are at most \(O(R_j^4)\) offset pairs \((a,b)\).
* There are \(O(\log R_j)\) dyadic radii.

Thus the total number of tuples is at most \(C R_j^6\log R_j\). Choose
\[
u=12\log R_j.
\]
The conditional union bound in (4.4) is then at most
\[
C R_j^6\log R_j\,R_j^{-12}
\le
C R_j^{-5}
\]
for large \(R_j\). Because the capacity/radius comparison implies exponential growth of \(R_j\) in the block index \(j\),
\[
\sum_jR_j^{-5}<\infty.
\]
Conditional Borel-Cantelli (or ordinary Borel-Cantelli after taking expectations) gives that only finitely many blocks violate the simultaneous estimate. Absorbing \(e-1\), the factor from replacing arbitrary \(L\) by the next dyadic value, and the constant \(12\) proves (4.1). \(\square\)

### Comment on random intervals

Once (4.1) has been proved simultaneously for all deterministic offset pairs inside a block, it automatically applies to intervals chosen from the realized trajectory after the fact. No optional-selection or independence claim is needed.

# 5. Uniform spatial confinement ceiling

Theorem 4.1 rules out long episodes during which the process remains inside one submacroscopic region.

### Corollary 5.1 — no long confinement in one \(o(R)\)-ball

Let \(L_j=o(R_j)\). Then almost surely eventually, no particle-time interval \(I\subseteq B_j\) whose attachment locations are all contained in a single radius-\(L_j\) ball can have length larger than \(C\log R_j\).

### Proof

If all \(H\) attachments in \(I\) lie in one radius-\(L_j\) ball, Theorem 4.1 gives
\[
H
\le
C\left[
H\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}+\log R_j
\right].
\]
Since \(L_j/R_j\to0\), the coefficient of \(H\) on the right is eventually less than \(1/2\) after increasing the harmless universal threshold. Absorb that term to the left to obtain
\[
H\le C'\log R_j.
\]
\(\square\)

Thus the prior statement that *typical consecutive particles are far apart* strengthens to a worst-window statement: a submacroscopic ball cannot trap a consecutive episode of the DLA dynamics for more than logarithmically many particles.

# 6. Uniform sliding-window spatial support

Fix a square grid with box side length \(L\). Every box is contained in a Euclidean ball of radius \(C(L+1)\), so Theorem 4.1 gives the same local-time bound for a box.

For an interval \(I\subseteq B_j\) of length \(H\), let \(M_I(L)\) denote the number of grid boxes visited by its attachment locations. The \(H\) particles are distributed among those boxes, while each box receives at most
\[
C\left[
H\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R_j}}+\log R_j
\right]
\]
particles. Therefore
\[
M_I(L)
\ge
\frac{H}{C\left[H\sqrt{(L+1)/R_j}+\log R_j\right]}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Using \(1/(a+b)\ge \tfrac12\min\{1/a,1/b\}\) for positive \(a,b\),
\[
\boxed{
M_I(L)
\ge
c\min\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L+1}},
\frac{H}{\log R_j}
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
This holds simultaneously for every interval in every sufficiently large block.

Two regimes are worth recording.

1. If
   \[
   H\gg \sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L+1}}\,\log R_j,
   \]
   then every such window visits at least
   \[
   c\sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L+1}}
   \]
   spatial boxes.

2. For shorter windows the theorem still forces at least
   \[
   cH/\log R_j
   \]
   boxes, ruling out repeated logarithmically long local cycling.

For polynomial scales \(L=R_j^\alpha\), \(0\le\alpha<1\), every window of length
\[
H\gg R_j^{(1-\alpha)/2}\log R_j
\]
therefore visits at least
\[
R_j^{(1-\alpha)/2+o(1)}
\]
boxes.

This is stronger than the preceding whole-block empirical support theorem because it is uniform over sliding temporal windows.

# 7. New local capacity-deposition speed limit

The previous sections concern particle counts. The pointwise capacity response (3.7) converts them into actual realized capacity bounds.

For an interval \(I=[u,v)\subseteq B_j\) and a ball \(B(y,L)\), define the actual local capacity deposited there by
\[
\Gamma_I(y,L)
:=
\sum_{n=u}^{v-1}
\Delta_{n+1}
\mathbf1_{\{|X_{n+1}-y|\le L\}}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
By (3.7),
\[
\Gamma_I(y,L)
\le
\frac{C_3}{R_j}N_I(y,L).
\]
Theorem 4.1 therefore gives the pathwise eventual estimate
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_I(y,L)
\le
C\left[
\frac{H\sqrt{L+1}}{R_j^{3/2}}
+
\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}
\right].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
This is simultaneous over all balls and all temporal subintervals in a sufficiently large block.

### Corollary 7.1 — time required for a local capacity burst

Suppose an interval \(I\subseteq B_j\) deposits capacity at least \(\gamma_j\) inside one radius-\(L_j\) ball and
\[
\gamma_j\ge 2C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j},
\]
where \(C\) dominates the second term in (7.2). Then
\[
\boxed{
H
\ge
c\,\gamma_j\,
\frac{R_j^{3/2}}{\sqrt{L_j+1}}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

For a fixed positive capacity fraction \(\gamma\) and polynomial radius \(L=R^\alpha\), this reads
\[
\boxed{
H\ge c_\gamma R^{3/2-\alpha/2}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

At microscopic scale \(L=O(1)\), any one local region needs order \(R^{3/2}\) particles to generate order-one logarithmic capacity. At scale \(L=R^{1/3}\), it still needs order \(R^{4/3}\) particles. At scale \(L=R^{1-\varepsilon}\), it needs order \(R^{1+\varepsilon/2}\) particles.

The statement is not a new radial exponent: capacity can be produced simultaneously in many distant regions. Its value is that it quantitatively excludes **fast local recycling** as the mechanism for capacity growth.

# 8. Space-time capacity support tradeoff

Let \(I\subseteq B_j\) have length \(H\) and total actual capacity increment
\[
A_I:=\sum_{n\in I}\Delta_{n+1}.
\]
Suppose the capacity-producing attachment locations of \(I\) are covered by \(M\) radius-\(L\) balls. Summing (7.2) over the cover gives
\[
A_I
\le
CM\left[
\frac{H\sqrt{L+1}}{R_j^{3/2}}
+
\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}
\right].
\tag{8.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
M
\ge
c\,
\frac{A_I}
{H\sqrt{L+1}/R_j^{3/2}+\log R_j/R_j}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

In the regime where
\[
H\sqrt{L+1}\gg R_j^{1/2}\log R_j,
\]
the logarithmic correction is lower order and (8.2) becomes the clean uncertainty-type relation
\[
\boxed{
M H
\gtrsim
A_I\frac{R_j^{3/2}}{\sqrt{L+1}}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
A capacity-bearing episode must therefore pay either in temporal duration or in the number of spatial regions used.

There is a complementary pathwise cardinality bound from the preceding checkpoint: a single radius-\(L\) ball contains only \(O(L^2)\) lattice sites and hence can generate at most \(CL^2/R_j\) capacity in the whole block. Combining the two gives
\[
\Gamma_I(y,L)
\le
C\min\left\{
\frac{L^2+1}{R_j},
\frac{H\sqrt{L+1}}{R_j^{3/2}}+
\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}
\right\}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
The first term is a lifetime/site-budget constraint; the second is a dynamic harmonic-measure constraint.

# 9. Attempted capacity-time return contraction and the precise failure

The natural next hope was to turn (0.1) directly into a contraction for the **fraction of instantaneous cubic capacity drift** carried by a ball. That step does not follow from the present inputs.

For a spatial set \(D\), the predictable local capacity drift is
\[
a_n(D)
\asymp
\sum_{x\in D}p_n(x)^3.
\]
The local Beurling theorem and pointwise Beurling bound give
\[
\sum_{x\in B(y,L)}p_n(x)^3
\le
(p_n^*)^2\mu_n(B(y,L))
\le
C\frac{\sqrt{L+1}}{R_n^{3/2}}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
However, the global denominator
\[
S_3(n)=\sum_xp_n(x)^3
\]
can in principle be of the same order as the contribution of a single rare high-harmonic-measure tip. There is no proved shape-uniform lower bound on \(S_3(n)\) strong enough to make
\[
\frac{a_n(B(y,L))}{a_n}
\]
small.

This is exactly the rare-tip obstruction isolated in the earlier Palm/large-deviation and dynamic-sharpness checkpoints. The new spacetime theorem controls actual local recycling **after averaging over particle time**, but it does not by itself control the instantaneous capacity-Palm law.

Thus the correct next target remains a planar estimate that couples high harmonic measure to geometry more strongly than ordinary Beurling—e.g. a multi-arm/shielding theorem, or a capacity-time return theorem specifically for cubic-moment-bearing tips.

# 10. Relation to Kesten and what would constitute a strict advance

At \(L=O(1)\), (7.3) says that order-one capacity produced in one microscopic region costs order \(R^{3/2}\) particles. This recovers the same dimensional scale that appears in Kesten's bound, but it is a **local-capacity speed statement**, not a proof of a new radial estimate.

The reason no exponent improvement follows is now explicit: DLA can distribute the unit capacity across many spatially separated regions, and the present theorem permits that possibility. A strict improvement beyond Kesten would follow from either of the following types of new input:

1. **capacity-support restriction:** prove that, during a radial doubling, a positive fraction of the capacity must be produced by only \(R^{o(1)}\) or otherwise insufficiently many mesoscopic regions; then (8.3) would force more than \(R^{3/2}\) particles;
2. **rare-tip return penalty:** prove that boxes carrying the cubic-moment-dominant tips cannot be revisited or regenerated at the generic local-Beurling rate;
3. **capacity-time box mixing:** prove a contraction/covariance bound for the capacity shares of separated boxes over positive capacity separation, and combine it with the archived Rényi/Palm closure reduction.

The present theorem supplies the particle-time spacetime side needed for such arguments but not the missing cubic-harmonic-measure geometry.

\]
for mesoscopic \(Q\), or the capacity-time return/covariance of \(\rho_n(Q)\) after the process leaves \(Q\).

Ordinary Beurling gives a numerator bound but not a denominator bound. Therefore the promising new ingredient must be geometrically selective: multi-arm separation, shielding of a regenerated tip, or a large-deviation estimate for the particle-Palm lower tail strong enough to control cubic mass.

If such a theorem yields a deterministic mixing law for the cubic field over unit-capacity blocks, the previously archived capacity/Rényi reduction would convert it directly into existence and determinism of \(\beta\).
