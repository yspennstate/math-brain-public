## 4. Selection-energy bound

Define
\[
\boxed{
F(P):=\sum_{i=1}^m s_i^2.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

At the DLA step that selects \(v_i\), the archived one-particle capacity response theorem gives
\[
\Delta C_{t_i+1}
\ge
\frac43 s_i^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]
The branch vertices are selected at distinct DLA times. Summing (4.2) over the branch therefore gives
\[
F(P)
\le
\frac34
\sum_{i=1}^m\Delta C_{t_i+1}
\le
\frac34
\left(C_{\tau_{2R}}-C_{\tau_R}\right)
+O(R^{-1}).
\tag{4.3}
\]
The harmless endpoint term accounts for the lattice-scale ambiguity in whether the root/terminal convention includes one endpoint increment; it can be absorbed into a universal constant.

By (3.1), radius doubling costs bounded logarithmic capacity:
\[
C_{\tau_{2R}}-C_{\tau_R}=O(1).
\tag{4.4}
\]
Hence there exists a deterministic universal \(Q_1<\infty\) such that
\[
\boxed{F(P)\le Q_1}
\tag{4.5}
\]
for every realized annulus-crossing genealogy and all large \(R\).

This is the selection-time counterpart of the predecessor birth-energy estimate \(E(P)=\sum b_i^2\le Q_0\). Since \(s_i\le b_i\), one also has \(F(P)\le E(P)\).

## 7. Predictable product lemma with actual selection hazards

The preceding supplement proved a general predictable likelihood-ratio lemma. Here it becomes especially natural.

Fix at time \(\tau_R\):

- a root;
- a nearest-neighbour spatial path \(v_0,\ldots,v_m\);
- prescribed increasing DLA times at which \(v_1,\ldots,v_m\) are required to be selected.

On the event that the first \(i-1\) requirements are met, immediately before the \(i\)-th prescribed selection time the value
\[
s_i=p_{t_i}(v_i)
\]
is measurable, and the conditional probability that the next DLA attachment is exactly \(v_i\) is **equal** to \(s_i\).

Let \(A_i\) denote that required selection event. Then the likelihood-ratio process
\[
M_k
=
\prod_{i=1}^k\frac{\mathbf1_{A_i}}{s_i}
\tag{7.1}
\]
has conditional expectation exactly one until failure (and at most one under the zero-probability convention). Hence
\[
\mathbb E[M_k\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}]\le1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
For every deterministic \(K>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
A_1\cap\cdots\cap A_m,
\prod_{i=1}^m s_i\le K
\middle|\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right)
\le K.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
This handles the full random-history dependence of the selection hazards.

For a realized branch,
\[
\prod_{i=1}^m s_i
=
\left(\frac{\Psi(P)}m\right)^{m/2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
If \(\mathfrak A(P)\ge B\), then
\[
\Psi(P)\le\frac{m^3}{B^2},
\tag{7.5}
\]
and therefore
\[
\prod_i s_i
\le
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
\tag{7.6}
\]
Applying (7.3) with \(K=(m/B)^m\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{specified genealogy/time tuple is realized},\\
\mathfrak A(P)\ge B
\end{array}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right\}
\le
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

## 8. Selection-action annular counting theorem

Define
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A_R^*
:=
\min\{\mathfrak A(P):P\text{ is a realized crossing genealogy from }R\text{ to }2R\}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Theorem 8.1 — PROVED

For every \(T\ge R-C_0\) and \(B>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{T_R\le T,\ \mathfrak A_R^*\ge B\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\}
\le
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
\left(\frac{4eT}{B}\right)^m.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

### Proof

On \(T_R\le T\), the first particle to reach radius \(2R\) has a crossing ancestry of some length
\[
R-C_0\le m\le T.
\]
Overcount possibilities as follows:

- at most \(CR^2\) possible roots, since the cluster at radius \(R\) lies in an \(O(R)\)-disk;
- at most \(4^m\) nearest-neighbour spatial paths of length \(m\);
- at most \(T\binom Tm\) choices of root-time/descendant-selection-time data.

For each fixed genealogy/time tuple, (7.7) gives probability at most \((m/B)^m\) on the event that its realized selection action is at least \(B\). Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb P\{T_R\le T,\mathfrak A_R^*\ge B\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\}
&\le
CR^2T
\sum_m
4^m\binom Tm
\left(\frac mB\right)^m\\
&\le
CR^2T
\sum_m
\left(\frac{4eT}{B}\right)^m,
\end{aligned}
\]
using \(\binom Tm\le(eT/m)^m\). QED.

This is formally parallel to the predecessor birth-action count, but it retains all screening between birth and eventual selection.

## 9. Selection-action exponent and radial reduction

Define
\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_{\rm sel}
:=
\liminf_{R\to\infty}
\frac{\log(\mathfrak A_R^*\vee1)}{\log R}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

From \(m\ge R-C_0\) and \(\Psi(P)\le Q_1\),
\[
\mathfrak A_R^*\ge cR^{3/2}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\underline\chi_{\rm sel}\ge\frac32.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Also \(\mathfrak A(P)\ge\mathfrak B(P)\), so
\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_{\rm sel}
\ge
\underline\chi_{\rm br}.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Fix \(\chi<\underline\chi_{\rm sel}\) and \(\eta>0\). Eventually
\[
\mathfrak A_R^*\ge R^\chi.
\]
Put
\[
T=R^{\chi-\eta},
\qquad
B=R^\chi.
\]
Then the ratio in (8.2) is
\[
\frac{4eT}{B}=4eR^{-\eta},
\]
so the probability that \(T_R\le R^{\chi-\eta}\) is superpolynomially small, indeed of order \(\exp[-cR\log R]\) after the first term of the sum. These probabilities are summable. Borel–Cantelli on dyadic annuli gives
\[
T_R\ge R^{\chi-\eta}
\]
eventually almost surely. Standard inversion of radius hitting times yields
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le
\frac1{\underline\chi_{\rm sel}}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

### Upper ceiling \(\underline\chi_{\rm sel}\le2\)

Before radius \(2R\) is first reached, the cluster lies in an \(O(R)\)-disk and has distinct occupied lattice sites, so deterministically
\[
T_R\le C_A R^2.
\tag{9.6}

Apply (8.2) with
\[
T=C_A R^2,
\qquad
B=R^{2+\delta}.
\]
The exponential ratio is \(O(R^{-\delta})\), giving a summable bound for the event \(\mathfrak A_R^*\ge R^{2+\delta}\). Therefore, for every \(\delta>0\), almost surely eventually
\[
\mathfrak A_R^*<R^{2+\delta}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\frac32\le\underline\chi_{\rm sel}\le2.
}
\tag{9.7}
\]
At the endpoint \(\underline\chi_{\rm sel}=2\), (9.5) gives radial limsup at most \(1/2\), while the deterministic area bound gives radial liminf at least \(1/2\); therefore the full exponent would exist and equal \(1/2\).

## 10. Four-penalty radial criterion

The exact birth-plus-screening representation (6.11) gives the following reusable conditional theorem.

Suppose there are exponents
\[
\delta,\gamma_E,\gamma_D,\gamma_S\ge0
\]
such that every sufficiently large \(R\to2R\) crossing genealogy satisfies, up to \(o(\log R)\) errors,
\[
m(P)\ge R^{1+\delta},
\tag{10.1}
\]
\[
E(P)\le R^{-\gamma_E},
\tag{10.2}
\]
\[
D(\nu_m\|q^{\rm birth})\ge\gamma_D\log R,
\tag{10.3}
\]
and
\[
\Sigma(P)\ge\gamma_S\log R.
\tag{10.4}
\]
Then (6.11) yields
\[
\mathfrak A(P)
\ge
R^{
\frac12(3+3\delta+\gamma_E+\gamma_D+2\gamma_S)-o(1)
}.
\tag{10.5}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le
\frac{2}{3+3\delta+\gamma_E+\gamma_D+2\gamma_S}.
}
\tag{10.6}
\]
The coefficient \(2\) in front of \(\gamma_S\) reflects that screening multiplies each probability before the square/geometric-mean normalization used in the action.

on every crossing branch eventually.

## 11. Critical-generation density criterion

This is the most concrete new consequence.

Fix \(\kappa>0\). For a crossing branch \(P\), define the set of **\(\kappa\)-screened selection generations**
\[
J_\kappa(P)
:=
\left\{
1\le i\le m:
\ s_i\le C_B R^{-1/2-\kappa}
\right\},
\tag{11.1}
\]
where \(C_B\) is the same universal constant as in the pointwise Beurling bound (3.6). Put
\[
\theta_\kappa(P):=\frac{|J_\kappa(P)|}{m}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

For every generation, \(s_i\le C_B R^{-1/2}\); for every \(i\in J_\kappa(P)\), there is the additional factor \(R^{-\kappa}\). Hence
\[
\prod_{i=1}^m s_i
\le
C_B^m
R^{-m/2}
R^{-\kappa|J_\kappa(P)|}.
\tag{11.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\Psi(P)
=
m\left(\prod_i s_i\right)^{2/m}
\le
C_B^2
mR^{-1}
R^{-2\kappa\theta_\kappa(P)}.
\tag{11.4}
\]
Using \(m\ge cR\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak A(P)
&=
\frac{m^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\Psi(P)}}\\
&\ge
c\,mR^{1/2}
R^{\kappa\theta_\kappa(P)}\\
&\ge
cR^{3/2+\kappa\theta_\kappa(P)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.5}
\]
Thus:

### Theorem 11.1 — PROVED

If there exist fixed \(\kappa,\theta>0\) such that every sufficiently large annulus-crossing genealogy has
\[
\theta_\kappa(P)\ge\theta,
\tag{11.6}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\underline\chi_{\rm sel}
\ge
\frac32+\kappa\theta
}
\tag{11.7}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le
\frac1{3/2+\kappa\theta}
=
\frac{2}{3+2\kappa\theta}
<\frac23.
}
\tag{11.8}
\]

This criterion asks only for a positive density of generations whose **selection-time** harmonic measure pays any fixed extra polynomial factor beyond the one-arm Beurling scale.

### Converse rigidity

If a crossing branch satisfies
\[
\mathfrak A(P)\le R^{3/2+\delta},
\tag{11.9}
\]
then (11.5) gives
\[
\kappa\theta_\kappa(P)
\le
\delta+O(1/\log R).
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\theta_\kappa(P)
\le
\frac\delta\kappa+o(1).
}
\tag{11.10}
\]
In particular, a sequence of branches with
\[
\mathfrak A(P_R)=R^{3/2+o(1)}
\]
must satisfy, for every fixed \(\kappa>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{m(P_R)}
\#\left\{
i:
 s_i\le C_BR^{-1/2-\kappa}
\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{11.11}
\]
Thus Kesten saturation requires density-one repetition of selection-time harmonic measure at the critical polynomial scale \(R^{-1/2-o(1)}\).

This is stronger than a statement about birth masses: a site that is born with near-critical mass but becomes screened before eventual use counts as a genuinely subcritical selection generation here.

## 12. Fast-crossing rigidity

The selection-action count converts the preceding branch statement into a theorem about any annulus that happens to cross unusually fast.

Fix \(\delta\ge0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). Set
\[
T=R^{3/2+\delta},
\qquad
B=R^{3/2+\delta+\varepsilon}.
\tag{12.1}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{4eT}{B}=4eR^{-\varepsilon}.
\tag{12.2}
\]
The right side of (8.2) is bounded by
\[
CR^2T\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}(4eR^{-\varepsilon})^m,
\tag{12.3}
\]
which is superpolynomially small and summable over integer \(R\).

By Borel–Cantelli:

### Theorem 12.1 — PROVED

For every fixed \(\delta\ge0\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), almost surely for all sufficiently large \(R\),
\[
\boxed{
T_R\le R^{3/2+\delta}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak A_R^*<R^{3/2+\delta+\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{12.4}
\]

Combining with (11.10), on such a fast-crossing annulus there exists a realized crossing genealogy \(P\) for which, for every fixed \(\kappa>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\theta_\kappa(P)
\le
\frac{\delta+\varepsilon}{\kappa}+o(1).
}
\tag{12.5}
\]

A useful limiting form is the following. Suppose along a sequence \(R_j\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{\log T_{R_j}}{\log R_j}\to\frac32.
\tag{12.6}
\]
Then, after choosing \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) along a countable diagonal sequence, there exist realized crossing genealogies \(P_j\) such that for every fixed rational \(\kappa>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\theta_\kappa(P_j)\to0.
}
\tag{12.7}
\]
Thus any true Kesten-scale crossing subsequence must exhibit a genealogy with density-one near-critical **actual selection hazards**.

This is a precise target for a future planar shielding theorem.

## 13. Screening-ratio density corollary

The exact screening factor \(\Sigma(P)\) itself also has a direct density meaning.

For \(\kappa>0\), define
\[
H_\kappa(P)
:=
\left\{
i:
\frac{s_i}{b_i}\le R^{-\kappa}
\right\}.
\tag{13.1}
\]
Every \(i\in H_\kappa(P)\) contributes at least \(\kappa\log R\) to \(\log(b_i/s_i)\). Since all summands are nonnegative,
\[
\Sigma(P)
\ge
\kappa\log R\,
\frac{|H_\kappa(P)|}{m}.
\tag{13.2}
\]
Hence if a positive fraction \(\vartheta\) of generations lose a factor \(R^\kappa\) between birth and selection, then
\[
\Sigma(P)\ge\kappa\vartheta\log R
\tag{13.3}
\]
and
\[
\mathfrak A(P)
\ge
\mathfrak B(P)R^{\kappa\vartheta}
\ge
cR^{3/2+\kappa\vartheta}.
\tag{13.4}
\]
Thus the radial conclusion (11.8) also follows from a positive density of polynomial **birth-to-selection screening losses**, even without separately proving that the birth masses themselves are below critical.

Conversely, if
\[
\mathfrak A(P)\le R^{3/2+\delta}
\]
then, because \(\mathfrak B(P)\ge cR^{3/2}\),
\[
\Sigma(P)\le\delta\log R+O(1),
\tag{13.5}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac{|H_\kappa(P)|}{m}
\le
\frac\delta\kappa+o(1).
}
\tag{13.6}
\]
Near-Kesten efficiency therefore requires not just high birth masses but also near-preservation of those masses up to selection on density one of the genealogy.

## 14. Why existing archived ingredients still stop at the Kesten barrier

The new criterion is strong enough to expose exactly what is missing, but the present archive does not yet prove (11.6).

The earlier results imply:

- direct same-lineage chains of four particles occur only finitely often;
- ordinary successive attachments typically relocate by almost macroscopic distance;
- operational capacity-Palm burst markers have local hazard \(O(R^{-3/2})\) and undergo almost-macroscopic post-burst relocation;
- old boundary influence and shallow genealogy carry vanishing future capacity;
- hot-site births satisfy a quadratic \(u^{-2}\) budget;
- actual capacity is spread over many mesoscopic boxes.

None of these presently prevents a single future annulus-crossing genealogy from behaving as follows: a child is born with mass \(\asymp R^{-1/2}\), the process grows elsewhere for about \(R^{1/2}\) particles, yet the child's mass loses only a subpolynomial factor, then that child is selected and exposes another descendant with mass again \(R^{-1/2-o(1)}\). Repeating this through \(R^{1+o(1)}\) generations costs exactly the Kesten \(R^{3/2+o(1)}\) particle scale.

The new selection action shows that **any polynomial decay during the waiting periods, on a positive density of generations, is enough to improve the exponent**. Therefore the next theorem should directly compare harmonic measure of a marked descendant at birth and at its eventual selection time after substantial intervening growth.

## 15. Strongest next attack: repeated selection screening

A concrete target is the following.

### Target A — positive-density screening theorem

Prove that there exist universal \(\kappa,\theta>0\) such that, almost surely for every sufficiently large annulus, every genealogy crossing \(R\to2R\) contains at least \(\theta m\) generations with
\[
p_{\rm selection}(v_i)
\le
C R^{-1/2-\kappa}.
\tag{15.1}
\]
By Theorem 11.1 this would immediately give
\[
\overline\beta
\le
\frac{2}{3+2\kappa\theta}
<\frac23.
\]

### Target B — birth-to-selection loss theorem

It is enough to prove that for some \(\kappa,\theta>0\), every crossing branch contains at least \(\theta m\) generations with
\[
\frac{p_{\rm selection}(v_i)}{p_{\rm birth}(v_i)}
\le
R^{-\kappa}.
\tag{15.2}
\]
Then \(\Sigma(P)\ge\kappa\theta\log R\) and the same strict exponent improvement follows.

This version may be better suited to a planar screening proof because it compares the same marked site before and after intervening cluster growth. The earlier exact balayage theorem guarantees monotonicity but gives no quantitative loss. One now needs a two-sided Beurling/multi-arm mechanism showing that a marked high-harmonic site cannot repeatedly survive substantial external growth without a polynomial loss in access from infinity.

### Possible route

For a marked descendant \(v\), expose the growth occurring between its birth and selection. If that intervening growth creates a connected crosscut separating a positive angular/prime-end portion of infinity from \(v\), a second Beurling-type crossing cost should multiply the original one-arm factor. The central difficulty is to make such a crosscut unavoidable on a positive density of ancestry generations without assuming a morphology theorem. The operational Palm-burst and far-switch results may be used to produce candidate intervening crosscuts, but a rigorous implication from spatial relocation to shielding is still missing.

## 17. Checks actually performed

This iteration explicitly checked:

1. **Selection-time measurability.** \(s_i\) is measurable immediately before the prescribed selection event and equals its one-step conditional probability.
2. **Energy direction.** From \(\Delta C\ge(4/3)p^2\), one gets \(s_i^2\le(3/4)\Delta C\), so summing selected branch sites gives an upper bound on \(F(P)\).
3. **Distinct increments.** Different genealogy vertices are selected at different DLA steps, so their capacity costs do not double-count a single increment.
4. **Endpoint convention.** Any one-step ambiguity at \(\tau_R\) or \(\tau_{2R}\) is uniformly bounded and does not affect polynomial exponents.
5. **Selection entropy identity.** The direction/sign in \(\Psi=F e^{-D(u\|q^{\rm sel})}\) was recomputed from logarithms.
6. **Screening sign.** Since \(s_i\le b_i\), \(\Sigma\ge0\) and \(\mathfrak A=\mathfrak B e^{\Sigma}\ge\mathfrak B\).
7. **Likelihood-ratio conditioning.** The selection hazards are random/history-dependent, so the proof uses the predictable product lemma rather than putting a random product outside a probability.
8. **Counting cancellation.** \(4^m\binom Tm(m/B)^m\le(4eT/B)^m\).
9. **Critical-density algebra.** A fraction \(\theta\) of extra \(R^{-\kappa}\) losses multiplies the geometric mean by \(R^{-\kappa\theta}\) and the action by \(R^{\kappa\theta}\).
10. **Fast-crossing summability.** Choosing \(B/T=R^\varepsilon\) makes the branch-counting tail \(\exp[-\Theta(R\log R)]\), summable even over all integer radii.
11. **Literature overlap.** Losev–Smirnov already use selected harmonic measures in their Kesten-scale argument; this report does not claim that general technique as new.

These are self-checks only, not an independent referee audit or formal verification.

## 19. Next attack

The highest-value next attack is to prove a **marked-descendant quantitative screening lemma**.

A useful local form would be: there exist \(\kappa,c>0\) such that when a boundary site \(v\) is born with harmonic mass at least \(R^{-1/2-o(1)}\), then after a specified amount of intervening capacity or after an appropriate geometrically separating growth event, either \(v\) is selected quickly or its harmonic measure falls by at least a factor \(R^{-\kappa}\), with conditional probability bounded away from zero uniformly over the old cluster.

One then needs a renewal/martingale argument showing that such opportunities occur on a positive fraction of generations of every annulus-crossing ancestry. By Section 13, positive-density polynomial losses immediately produce a selection-action excess and cross the Kesten barrier.

The most promising geometric mechanism is a two-sided Beurling or multi-arm crosscut: intervening growth that places a connected obstacle on both sides of the prime-end access channel to \(v\) should impose a second crossing penalty. The difficulty is not the one-step estimate; it is proving that enough such separating events are unavoidable along every efficient crossing genealogy.
