and add an exterior-boundary site according to simple-random-walk harmonic measure from infinity, with the same first-hit convention pinned in the preceding HARD10-04 checkpoints.

The full target remains **OPEN**. This run does not prove existence, determinism, or the value of
\[
\beta=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_n}{\log n},
\]
and does not refute existence/determinism.

The preceding run identified a serious obstruction to the annular-locality route: the cubic harmonic-measure moment
\[
S_3(n):=\sum_x p_n(x)^3,
\qquad p_n(x):=\omega(x,K_n),
\]
can in principle be controlled by polynomially rare high-harmonic-measure sites, whereas ordinary core-erasure locality only controls typical particles. Its deterministic per-time deep-core estimate stopped at the Kesten power \(R^{-3/2}\).

This iteration proves a genuinely dynamical replacement. The key observation is that a site can be attached only once. Combined with the Beurling pointwise estimate, this converts the attachment hazard itself into a self-normalized occupation bound.

The main theorem is:

> **Dynamic occupation theorem.** Let \(B_j\) be a unit-capacity block and \(R_j\) its starting radius. For any predictable family of candidate sets \(D_n\subseteq\partial A_n\), let
> \[
> T_j(D):=\left|\bigcup_{n\in B_j}D_n\right|
> \]
> be the number of distinct lattice sites that ever enter the family during the block. Then for every fixed \(q>0\), almost surely for all sufficiently large \(j\),
> \[
> \boxed{
> \sum_{n\in B_j}\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^{q+1}
> \le
> C_q R_j^{-q/2}\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
> }
> \]

For \(q=2\), this controls precisely the cubic moment that drives capacity:
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^3
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\]

Taking \(D_n=B(0,s)\cap\partial A_n\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}\sum_{|x|\le s}p_n(x)^3
\le
C\frac{s^2+\log R_j}{R_j}.
}
\]
Since the total cubic mass over a unit-capacity block is bounded above and below by positive constants, the capacity-Palm fraction produced inside a sub-square-root core \(s=o(R_j^{1/2})\) tends to zero. In particular, the previous static \(R^{-3/2}\) barrier is broken **after time integration**: deep high-probability sites cannot keep contributing cubic mass forever, because their harmonic measure is also their attachment hazard and each site disappears after it is selected.

A second consequence is a rigorous turnover requirement: any predictable class of sites carrying a fixed positive fraction of capacity production during one unit of capacity time must pass through \(\Omega(R_j)\) distinct lattice sites. Thus a putative rare-tip mechanism determining a non-Kesten exponent cannot be supported by a fixed or sublinear-in-\(R\) collection of tips; it must continually create new high-impact sites at a linear-in-radius rate.

This is partial progress. It does not determine the rate of that tip turnover, and therefore does not close the radial exponent.

# 4. Dynamic occupation theorem for harmonic-measure moments

Fix \(q>0\). For each \(n\in B_j\), let
\[
D_n\subseteq\partial A_n
\]
be any \(\mathcal F_n\)-measurable set. It may change adaptively with the cluster. Define the block turnover
\[
T_j(D):=
\left|\bigcup_{n\in B_j}D_n\right|.
\tag{4.1}
\]
No deterministic upper bound on \(T_j(D)\) is assumed.

Let \(X_{n+1}\) be the actual DLA attachment at time \(n+1\). Define
\[
Y_{n+1}^{(q,D)}
:=
 p_n(X_{n+1})^q
 \mathbf1_{\{X_{n+1}\in D_n\}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[Y_{n+1}^{(q,D)}\mid\mathcal F_n]
&=
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)\,p_n(x)^q\\
&=
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^{q+1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Set
\[
h_j:=K^qR_j^{-q/2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
By (3.5),
\[
0\le W_{n+1}:=Y_{n+1}^{(q,D)}/h_j\le1.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Write
\[
\mu_{n+1}:=\mathbb E[W_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n].
\tag{4.6}
\]

The crucial pathwise observation is that every lattice site is attached at most once. Therefore
\[
\sum_{n\in B_j}W_{n+1}
\le
\#\{n\in B_j:X_{n+1}\in D_n\}
\le
T_j(D).
\tag{4.7}
\]

The rest of the proof converts the realized occupation bound (4.7) into a predictable one.

## Lemma 4.1 — self-normalized Bernoulli-hazard inequality

Let \((W_m)\) be adapted random variables with \(0\le W_m\le1\), and put \(\mu_m=\mathbb E[W_m\mid\mathcal G_{m-1}]\). Then with
\[
c_*:=1-e^{-1},
\]
the process
\[
Z_M=
\exp\left(
 c_*\sum_{m\le M}\mu_m
 -\sum_{m\le M}W_m
\right)
\tag{4.8}
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale.

### Proof

For \(0\le w\le1\), convexity of \(e^{-w}\) gives the chord bound
\[
e^{-w}\le1-(1-e^{-1})w=1-c_*w.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-W_m}\mid\mathcal G_{m-1}]
\le
1-c_*\mu_m
\le
e^{-c_*\mu_m}.
\]
Multiplying by \(e^{c_*\mu_m}\) proves the supermartingale property. \(\square\)

Ville's inequality therefore yields, conditionally on the start of the block,
\[
\mathbb P\left\{
\sup_M
\left(
 c_*\sum_{m\le M}\mu_m
 -\sum_{m\le M}W_m
\right)\ge u
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right\}
\le e^{-u}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
We stop the increments after \(\sigma_{j+1}\), so (4.9) applies directly to the whole random-length block.

Choose
\[
u_j:=4\log R_j.
\]
Then the conditional failure probability is at most \(R_j^{-4}\). By (3.7),
\[
\sum_jR_j^{-4}<\infty.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Thus conditional Borel–Cantelli followed by ordinary Borel–Cantelli gives, almost surely for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
c_*\sum_{n\in B_j}\mu_{n+1}
\le
\sum_{n\in B_j}W_{n+1}+4\log R_j
\le
T_j(D)+4\log R_j.
\tag{4.11}
\]
Multiplying by \(h_j\) and using (4.3) proves:

## Theorem 4.2 — dynamic moment occupation theorem — PROVED

For every fixed \(q>0\), there is a deterministic \(C_q<\infty\) such that, almost surely, for all sufficiently large unit-capacity blocks \(B_j\), simultaneously for the chosen predictable family \((D_n)_{n\in B_j}\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^{q+1}
\le
C_qR_j^{-q/2}
\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\tag{4.12}
\]

The theorem is dynamic in an essential way. A site with large \(p_n(x)\) contributes strongly to the moment, but the same \(p_n(x)\) is also the hazard that the site is selected and permanently removed from the exterior boundary. Formula (4.12) quantifies this self-depletion.

# 5. Cubic occupation and capacity-Palm turnover

Set \(q=2\). Theorem 4.2 gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^3
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is exactly the moment relevant to capacity growth.

Define the predictable capacity mass of the selected family by
\[
\Gamma_j(D)
:=
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)d_n(x).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Using \(d_n(x)\le C_0p_n(x)^2\),
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j(D)
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Let
\[
A_j:=\sum_{n\in B_j}a_n=1+o(1)
\]
and define the block capacity-Palm law on time/site pairs by
\[
\mathsf Q_j(n,x)
:=
\frac{p_n(x)d_n(x)}{A_j}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Then (5.3) yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_j\{x\in D_n\}
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Corollary 5.1 — linear turnover is necessary for positive capacity share

Suppose along a subsequence of blocks a predictable family carries at least \(\eta>0\) of capacity-Palm mass:
\[
\mathsf Q_j\{x\in D_n\}\ge\eta.
\]
Then necessarily
\[
\boxed{
T_j(D)
\ge
c\eta R_j-C\log R_j.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus any mechanism carrying a fixed fraction of capacity production for one intrinsic capacity unit must cycle through linearly many distinct lattice sites.

This is a new dynamical obstruction to a static-tip explanation of the DLA exponent.

# 6. Sub-square-root cores carry asymptotically zero capacity

Take the fixed spatial core
\[
D_n=B(0,s_j)\cap\partial A_n
\]
throughout block \(B_j\), where \(s_j\) is allowed to depend on \(R_j\) and is \(\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}\)-measurable. Since all candidate sites lie in the lattice ball,
\[
T_j(D)
\le
|B(0,s_j)\cap\mathbb Z^2|
\le
Cs_j^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The cubic occupation theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{|x|\le s_j}p_n(x)^3
\le
C\frac{s_j^2+\log R_j}{R_j}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Likewise the capacity-Palm law satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_j\{|x|\le s_j\}
\le
C\frac{s_j^2+\log R_j}{R_j}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently, if
\[
s_j=o(R_j^{1/2}),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_j\{|x|\le s_j\}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is stronger in the relevant dynamical sense than the preceding run's static per-time estimate. Even if a deep site temporarily has nearly maximal Beurling harmonic measure, it cannot make an order-one contribution to capacity throughout a unit-capacity block unless a linear number of distinct deep sites are successively exposed. A sub-square-root Euclidean core contains too few sites for that.

### Polynomial formulation

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing
\[
s_j=R_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_j\{|x|\le R_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}\}
\le
CR_j^{-2\varepsilon}
+C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Critical logarithmic formulation

For any \(a>0\), choosing
\[
s_j=
\frac{R_j^{1/2}}{(\log R_j)^a}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf Q_j\left\{
|x|\le
\frac{R_j^{1/2}}{(\log R_j)^a}
\right\}
\le
\frac{C}{(\log R_j)^{2a}}
+C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

If \(a>1/2\), then because \(\log R_j\gtrsim j\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_j
\mathsf Q_j\left\{
|x|\le
\frac{R_j^{1/2}}{(\log R_j)^a}
\right\}
<\infty
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Thus the capacity-Palm mass assigned to sufficiently subcritical square-root cores is not merely vanishing; it is globally summable across intrinsic capacity blocks.

### A median-scale consequence

From (6.3), choose a universal \(c_*>0\) small enough that \(Cc_*^2<1/3\). Then for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\mathsf Q_j\{|x|\le c_*R_j^{1/2}\}<1/2.
\]
Hence a median of the Euclidean radius under the block capacity-Palm law is at least
\[
\boxed{
c_*R_j^{1/2}.}
\tag{6.8}
\]
This is a statement about where a typical **unit of predictable capacity production** occurs, not about a uniformly sampled DLA particle.

\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}

# 8. Reformulation in terms of high-tip turnover

The new theorem suggests a sharper rare-event target than the previous generic request for a multifractal large-deviation principle.

Fix a threshold \(u_j\) and define the predictable hot set
\[
H_n(u_j):=
\{x\in\partial A_n:p_n(x)\ge u_j\},
\qquad n\in B_j.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal T_j(u_j)
:=
\left|
\bigcup_{n\in B_j}H_n(u_j)
\right|
\tag{8.2}
\]
be the number of distinct sites that ever become hot during the capacity block.

Then (5.1) gives the exact rigorous reduction
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{x:p_n(x)\ge u_j}p_n(x)^3
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\bigl(\mathcal T_j(u_j)+\log R_j\bigr).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Therefore:

> **Any hot-tip class with sublinear turnover \(\mathcal T_j(u_j)=o(R_j)\) contributes \(o(1)\) of the integrated cubic moment and hence \(o(1)\) of capacity production.**

This shifts the missing theorem from a one-time statement to a dynamical one. A rare high-harmonic-measure tip cannot dominate just by being large. It must be replaced by new high-harmonic-measure sites at least linearly often in radius during each unit of capacity time.

The previous scalar sharpness family is fully consistent with this: its heavy atom may select a candidate third-moment exponent, but to represent a genuine long DLA block it would have to **rotate** through \(\Omega(R)\) fresh spatial sites. Static scalar constraints do not encode that turnover.

This identifies a concrete next route: prove an upper bound on \(\mathcal T_j(u)\) for thresholds in the multifractal range
\[
u=R_j^{-a},
\qquad
\frac12\le a\lesssim\frac23,
\]
using planar screening / multi-arm geometry. Any nontrivial sublinear turnover bound for the portion of the hot set carrying positive cubic mass would immediately improve the capacity-side picture.

# 9. Why this does not yet prove a better radial exponent

The theorem removes one major loophole from the preceding run — persistent deep hot spots — but it does not control the outer and mesoscopic annuli, which contain vastly more than \(R\) sites.

A unit-capacity block can still, within current rigorous knowledge, generate its order-one cubic mass from a sequence of \(\Theta(R)\) or more distinct hot sites located outside the sub-square-root core. Equation (5.6) says such turnover is **necessary**, not impossible.

Likewise, the theorem does not imply convergence of
\[
-\frac{\log S_3(n)}{\log R_n}
\]
or of the block free energy archived earlier. It only shows that any failure of self-averaging must be sustained by a continually renewing spatial population of tips rather than by a fixed small exceptional set.

Accordingly, no strict lower radial exponent above \(1/2\) is claimed in this run.

# 10. Next attack

The most concrete next target is a **hot-tip turnover theorem**.

A useful form would be: for some threshold exponent \(a>1/2\) and some \(\delta>0\), the subset of sites that ever satisfy
\[
p_n(x)\ge R_j^{-a}
\]
during a unit-capacity block and that collectively carry a fixed fraction of the cubic mass has cardinality at most
\[
R_j^{1-\delta}.
\]

Combined with (8.3), this would be impossible for large \(j\), so it would force the cubic mass into a different harmonic-measure scale. Iterating such exclusions over thresholds could begin to pin down the particle-Palm lower-tail rate function discussed in the preceding checkpoint.

The planar mechanism one would need is not ordinary one-arm Beurling. It must constrain the **creation and turnover** of high-harmonic-measure sites, probably through a multi-arm/screening statement: making a genuinely new hot site should require destruction or shielding of previously hot geometry, and the total number of such reorganizations per unit capacity may be limited by the same quadratic-variation / capacity budget proved in earlier reports.

A second route is to combine (8.3) with the archived local-response theorem. Since a hot attachment changes harmonic measure by total variation comparable to its own \(p\), a sequence of \(\Omega(R)\) distinct hot sites forces a definite amount of harmonic-measure path variation. The existing \(\ell^q\) variation hierarchy controls powers \(q>2\), but not yet strongly enough at the critical square scale. A thresholded variation-counting argument is the next calculation to attempt.
