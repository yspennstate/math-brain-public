## 1. Coordinatewise monotonicity of harmonic measure

Let
\[
K_n=\overline A_n,
\qquad
\mu_n=\omega(\cdot,K_n).
\]
The archived exact balayage identity for the transition \(K_n\subset K_{n+1}\) is
\[
\mu_n(z)
=
\mu_{n+1}(z)
+
\sum_{y\in K_{n+1}\setminus K_n}
\mu_{n+1}(y)
\mathbb P^y\{S_{T_{K_n}}=z\},
\qquad z\in K_n.
\]
Every correction term is nonnegative. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mu_{n+1}(z)\le\mu_n(z)
\quad\text{for every }z\in K_n.
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus, once a lattice site first enters the closure \(K_n\), its harmonic-measure coordinate can never increase at any later time. A site can become newly high-harmonic-measure only at the instant it first enters the closure as a newly exposed boundary site.

This is stronger than merely saying old total mass decreases: it is coordinatewise monotonicity.

## 2. Hot sites can only be born hot

Fix a unit-capacity block
\[
B_j=\{\sigma_j,\ldots,\sigma_{j+1}-1\}
\]
and a threshold \(u\in(0,1]\). Define
\[
H_n(u):=\{x\in K_n:\mu_n(x)\ge u\}.
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal H_j(u):=
\bigcup_{n\in B_j}H_n(u)
\]
be the set of all sites that are ever hot during the block.

At the start of the block,
\[
|H_{\sigma_j}(u)|\le u^{-1}
\tag{2.1}
\]
because \(\mu_{\sigma_j}\) is a probability measure.

By (1.1), a site not hot when it first appears can never become hot later. Therefore every site in
\[
\mathcal H_j(u)\setminus H_{\sigma_j}(u)
\]
must belong to a newly exposed shell
\[
S_n:=K_{n+1}\setminus K_n
\]
with initial mass
\[
\mu_{n+1}(y)\ge u.
\]

In external site DLA on \(\mathbb Z^2\), attaching one boundary site exposes at most three new closure sites, so each step can create at most three newly hot sites.

## 3. Capacity budget for hot births

Let
\[
\delta_{n+1}:=
\mu_{n+1}(S_n)
=
\|\mu_{n+1}-\mu_n\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
The archived quadratic-variation theorem gives the pathwise one-step comparison
\[
\frac13\delta_{n+1}^2
\le
C_{n+1}-C_n
\le
4\delta_{n+1}^2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If step \(n+1\) creates a newly hot site, then for some \(y\in S_n\),
\[
\mu_{n+1}(y)\ge u,
\]
so
\[
\delta_{n+1}\ge u.
\]
Consequently that step consumes at least
\[
\frac13u^2
\]
of actual capacity increment.

Let \(N_j^{\rm birth}(u)\) be the number of steps in \(B_j\) that create at least one newly hot site. Since the total actual capacity increment across a unit-capacity block is
\[
C_{\sigma_{j+1}}-C_{\sigma_j}=1+o(1)
\]
(the archived overshoot theorem), we obtain
\[
\boxed{
N_j^{\rm birth}(u)
\le
C u^{-2}
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\).

Since each such step creates at most three newly exposed sites,
\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal H_j(u)|
\le
u^{-1}+C u^{-2}
\le
C u^{-2}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is a deterministic/pathwise turnover upper bound once the DLA trajectory is fixed. No martingale concentration is required.

## 4. Combined with the dynamic cubic-occupation theorem

The primary same-run report proves that for any predictable family \(D_n\), if
\[
T_j(D)=\left|\bigcup_{n\in B_j}D_n\right|,
\]
then almost surely eventually
\[
\sum_{n\in B_j}\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^3
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\bigl(T_j(D)+\log R_j\bigr).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Take
\[
D_n=H_n(u)\cap\partial A_n.
\]
Then (3.3) and (4.1) give
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{x:p_n(x)\ge u}
p_n(x)^3
\le
\frac{C}{R_j}
\left(u^{-2}+\log R_j\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

The corresponding capacity-Palm mass obeys the same bound up to a universal multiplicative constant.

Thus the full time-integrated contribution of all sites that ever exceed threshold \(u\) is controlled by the cost of creating those sites.

## 5. The Beurling scale is exactly critical for this mechanism

Write
\[
u=R_j^{-a}.
\]
Then (4.2) becomes
\[
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{p_n(x)\ge R_j^{-a}}p_n(x)^3
\le
C R_j^{2a-1}
+C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(a<1/2\), the right side vanishes. But planar Beurling already says
\[
p_n(x)\lesssim R_j^{-1/2},
\]
so thresholds with \(a<1/2\) are eventually above the possible harmonic-measure maximum and are not the multifractal regime of interest.

At the critical Beurling scale
\[
u\asymp R_j^{-1/2},
\]
(4.2) gives only an order-one bound. For smaller thresholds \(u=R^{-a}\), \(a>1/2\), the bound is no longer decaying.

Therefore the new monotonicity + capacity-budget mechanism is **exactly critical at exponent \(1/2\)**. It rigorously explains why the existing quadratic-variation budget by itself cannot beat Kesten's barrier.

## 6. Higher variation does not improve this threshold

The archived variation theorem gives for every fixed \(r>2\), on a unit-capacity block at radius scale \(R_j\),
\[
\sum_{n\in B_j}\delta_{n+1}^r
\le
C_rR_j^{-(r-2)/2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

A newly hot birth at threshold \(u\) has \(\delta_{n+1}\ge u\), so
\[
N_j^{\rm birth}(u)
\le
C_rR_j^{-(r-2)/2}u^{-r}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
For \(u=R_j^{-a}\), the exponent of \(R_j\) on the right is
\[
1+r\left(a-\frac12\right).
\tag{6.3}
\]

When \(a\ge1/2\), this is minimized by taking \(r\downarrow2\), which recovers the quadratic estimate \(R_j^{2a}\). Thus none of the already archived \(r>2\) variation bounds improves hot-tip turnover in the physically relevant regime \(a\ge1/2\).

This is a useful no-go statement: **the existing variation hierarchy cannot by algebra alone move the hot-tip threshold past the Beurling exponent.** A new planar geometric input is required.

## 7. What the genuinely missing theorem must control

The two same-run theorems now give both a lower and upper turnover logic.

1. If a predictable class carries a fixed positive fraction of capacity production, the primary report proves it must pass through
   \[
   \Omega(R_j)
   \]
   distinct sites in one unit-capacity block.
2. The present supplement proves that sites at threshold \(u\) can be born at most
   \[
   O(u^{-2})
   \]
   times, and that this upper bound is exactly of order \(R_j\) at \(u\asymp R_j^{-1/2}\).

So the near-Beurling tips sit at a genuinely critical turnover balance:
\[
\text{required turnover }\asymp R
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{available quadratic-variation budget }\asymp R.
\]

Any strict improvement must show that actual planar DLA cannot saturate this balance for a positive fraction of capacity time. That is now a precise geometric target.

A suitable theorem would say, for example, that the number of newly exposed sites with harmonic measure \(\gtrsim R^{-1/2}\) in one unit-capacity block is
\[
o(R)
\]
or, more realistically, that the subset of those sites which carries a positive fraction of cubic mass is \(o(R)\). Either conclusion would contradict the linear-turnover necessity from the primary report and force capacity production to a lower-probability scale.

Such a statement cannot follow from one-arm Beurling or the existing \(\ell^r\) variation hierarchy. It needs a genuinely two-dimensional multi-arm/screening argument controlling how often a fresh near-maximal harmonic-measure tip can be created.
