### Main new conclusions

1. For every fixed \(m\ge2\), if \(q_m(n)\) is the conditional probability that the next \(m\) particles form one direct parent-child-... chain in the forward attachment genealogy, then
   \[
   c_m S_m(n)\le q_m(n)\le C_m S_m(n),
   \qquad

   \]

   \[
   \frac1{27}S_4(n)\le q_4(n)\le4096S_4(n).
   \]
   Since \(S_4\le p_n^*S_3\), \(p_n^*\lesssim R_n^{-1/2}\), and the capacity clock is comparable to \(S_3\), one gets
   \[
   \sum_n q_4(n)<\infty\qquad\text{a.s.}
   \]
   Hence, by conditional Borel-Cantelli after a residue-class decomposition,
   \[
   \boxed{\text{only finitely many four-particle direct genealogical chains occur a.s.}}
   \]
   Equivalently, after a finite random time, every direct genealogical run contains at most **three particles / two direct edges**.

3. Let \(I_n^{(2)}\) denote the event that particles \(n+1,n+2\) form a direct parent-child pair. If, after such a pair, the next attachment is required to lie within Euclidean distance \(L\) of the child, then its conditional probability is at most
   \[
   C(L+1)^2R_n^{-1/2}\,q_2(n).
   \]
   This is a purely planar consequence of the pointwise Beurling bound and the fact that a radius-\(L\) lattice ball contains \(O(L^2)\) possible sites.

4. On a unit-capacity block beginning at radius \(R_j\), write \(H_{2,j}\) for the actual number of direct parent-child starts and \(H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)\) for those for which the immediately following particle lies within distance \(L_j\) of the child. Then almost surely eventually
   \[
   \frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}
   \le
   C(L_j+1)^2R_j^{-1/2}
   +C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j^{1/2}}.
   \]
   Therefore, for every deterministic \(L_j=o(R_j^{1/4})\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   \frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}\longrightarrow0
   \quad\text{a.s.}
   }
   \]
   Since the preceding run proved \(H_{2,j}=\Omega(R_j^{1/2})\), this yields
   \[
   \boxed{
   \Omega(R_j^{1/2})\text{ direct one-edge bursts per capacity unit are followed by a switch of distance }>L_j.
   }
   \]

In particular, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), taking
\[
L_j=R_j^{1/4-\varepsilon}

shows that asymptotically almost every direct parent-child burst is followed by a **mesoscopic switch of Euclidean size at least \(R_j^{1/4-\varepsilon}\)**.

# 4. Full fixed-length direct-chain hierarchy

For \(m\ge2\), define the event \(E_n^{(m)}\) that particles
\[
X_{n+1},X_{n+2},\ldots,X_{n+m}
\]
form one direct line in the forward attachment genealogy: for every \(r=1,\ldots,m-1\), the particle \(X_{n+r+1}\) belongs to the newly exposed shell created by \(X_{n+r}\).

Set
\[
q_m(n):=\mathbb P(E_n^{(m)}\mid\mathcal F_n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

We prove that every fixed direct-chain hazard is equivalent to the corresponding harmonic moment.

## 4.1 Continuation probability from a specified first site

For a state \(A\) and a specified possible attachment \(x\) of harmonic mass \(p\), let \(F_r(A,x)\) denote the probability that, after attaching \(x\), the next \(r\) particles successively attach to the newborn shell of their immediate predecessor.

Thus \(F_1(A,x)=\delta(x)\), and (3.1) gives
\[
p\le F_1(A,x)\le4p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Claim: for every integer \(r\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
3^{-r(r-1)/2}p^r
\le
F_r(A,x)
\le
4^{r(r+1)/2}p^r.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Proceed by induction on \(r\). The case \(r=1\) is (4.2).

Suppose the result holds for \(r-1\). After attaching \(x\), let its newborn sites have harmonic masses \(w_1,\ldots,w_k\), \(1\le k\le3\). Conditioning on which newborn child is selected next,
\[
F_r(A,x)
=
\sum_{i=1}^kw_iF_{r-1}(A\cup\{x\},y_i).
\tag{4.4}
\]
The induction hypothesis gives
\[
3^{-(r-1)(r-2)/2}\sum_iw_i^r
\le
F_r(A,x)
\le
4^{(r-1)r/2}\sum_iw_i^r.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Since \(k\le3\),
\[
\sum_iw_i^r
\ge
k^{1-r}\left(\sum_iw_i\right)^r
\ge
3^{1-r}\delta(x)^r
\ge
3^{1-r}p^r,
\tag{4.6}
\]
while
\[
\sum_iw_i^r
\le
\left(\sum_iw_i\right)^r
=\delta(x)^r
\le4^rp^r.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Combining exponents,
\[
3^{-(r-1)(r-2)/2}3^{1-r}
=3^{-r(r-1)/2},
\]
and
\[
4^{(r-1)r/2}4^r
=4^{r(r+1)/2}.
\]
This proves (4.3). \(\square\)

# 6. A local-mass bound around a revealed attachment

The next goal is to quantify where the process goes after a direct parent-child burst.

Consider any DLA state of radius at least \(R\), and fix any lattice point \(y\). For \(L\ge1\), the number of lattice sites in
\[
B(y,L)=\{z:|z-y|_2\le L\}
\]
is at most \(C_0(L+1)^2\). Every accessible boundary site in the current state has harmonic mass at most \(CR^{-1/2}\) by the planar pointwise Beurling bound. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\omega\bigl(\partial A\cap B(y,L),\overline A\bigr)
\le
C(L+1)^2R^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

This bound uses no independence and no assumption that \(y\) is on the boundary. It is deliberately crude but completely shape-uniform.

Notice that a same-lineage direct continuation is included in \(N_n(L)\) whenever \(L\ge1\). Thus the complement of \(N_n(L)\) among direct parent-child events is automatically a genuine switch away from the child's local neighborhood.

# 9. Multiplicative concentration for actual direct edges

Let
\[
I_n^{(2)}=\mathbf1_{E_n^{(2)}}.
\]
Because an event \(E_n^{(2)}\) uses the two future particles \(n+1,n+2\), split starting indices into their two residue classes modulo \(2\). Within one residue class, each event is fully revealed before the next start in that class.

For a conditional Bernoulli variable \(I\) of mean \(q\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-I}\mid\mathcal G]
=1-(1-e^{-1})q
\le
\exp[-(1-e^{-1})q].
\tag{9.1}
\]
Hence, on each residue class, with
\[
c_-:=1-e^{-1},
\]
\[
\exp\left(-\sum I+c_-\sum q\right)
\tag{9.2}
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale.

Gate the increments predictably to starts in \(B_j\). Applying the maximal/terminal exponential bound with threshold \(4\log R_j\), then summing over the two residue classes, gives conditional failure probability at most \(2R_j^{-4}\) for
\[
H_{2,j}
<
c_-Q_{2,j}-8\log R_j,
\tag{9.3}
\]
where
\[
H_{2,j}:=\sum_{n\in B_j}I_n^{(2)}.
\]
Because \(\sum_jR_j^{-4}<\infty\), Borel-Cantelli yields
\[
\boxed{
H_{2,j}
\ge
c_-Q_{2,j}-8\log R_j
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
almost surely eventually.

Together with (8.5), this reproves and slightly sharpens the preceding lower bound \(H_{2,j}=\Omega(R_j^{1/2})\): the realized direct-edge count tracks its full predictable mass from below, up to a logarithmic error.

# 10. Concentration for near switches

Let
\[
J_n(L_j)=\mathbf1_{N_n(L_j)}.
\]
These events use three future particles, so split starts into the three residue classes modulo \(3\).

For a conditional Bernoulli variable \(J\) of mean \(r\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^J\mid\mathcal G]
=1+(e-1)r
\le e^{(e-1)r}.
\tag{10.1}
\]
Thus
\[
\exp\left(\sum J-(e-1)\sum r\right)
\tag{10.2}
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale on each residue class.

Again gating to starts in \(B_j\), taking threshold \(4\log R_j\), and summing over three residue classes gives conditional failure probability at most \(3R_j^{-4}\) for
\[
H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)
>
(e-1)R^{\rm near}_{2,j}+12\log R_j,
\tag{10.3}
\]
where
\[
H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)
:=
\sum_{n\in B_j}J_n(L_j).
\]
Borel-Cantelli and (8.7) give
\[
\boxed{
H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)
\le
(e-1)\varepsilon_jQ_{2,j}+12\log R_j
}
\tag{10.4}
\]
almost surely eventually.

# 11. Mesoscopic far-switch theorem

Combine (9.4) and (10.4). Since \(Q_{2,j}\ge cR_j^{1/2}\), the logarithmic terms are negligible relative to \(Q_{2,j}\). Therefore
\[
\frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}
\le
C\varepsilon_j
+C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j^{1/2}}
\tag{11.1}
\]
almost surely eventually, i.e.
\[
\boxed{
\frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}
\le
C(L_j+1)^2R_j^{-1/2}
+C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j^{1/2}}.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

Hence, whenever
\[
L_j=o(R_j^{1/4}),
\tag{11.3}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}\to0
\qquad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{11.4}
\]

Let
\[
H^{\rm far}_{2,j}(L_j)
:=H_{2,j}-H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
H^{\rm far}_{2,j}(L_j)
=(1-o(1))H_{2,j}
=\Omega(R_j^{1/2})
\qquad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{11.5}
\]

In words: among the order-\(\sqrt R\) direct parent-child bursts occurring during one unit of logarithmic capacity, asymptotically almost all are followed by an attachment farther than \(L=o(R^{1/4})\) from the child.

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing
\[
L_j=R_j^{1/4-\varepsilon}
\]
gives the explicit fraction bound
\[
\boxed{
\frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(R_j^{1/4-\varepsilon})}{H_{2,j}}
\le
CR_j^{-2\varepsilon}
+o(1).
}
\tag{11.6}
\]

A logarithmically near-critical choice also works. If
\[
L_j=\frac{R_j^{1/4}}{(\log R_j)^A},
\qquad A>0,
\]
then
\[
\frac{H^{\rm near}_{2,j}(L_j)}{H_{2,j}}
\le
C(\log R_j)^{-2A}+o(1).
\tag{11.7}
\]

This proves a genuine spatial-switching statement for the actual DLA particle sequence, rather than merely an auxiliary genealogy or harmonic-measure law.

# 12. Combined structural picture

The preceding checkpoint proved:

- at least \(\Omega(R^{1/2})\) direct parent-child continuations per capacity unit;
- only \(O(\log\log R)\) three-particle direct-chain starts per capacity unit;
- most direct edges are isolated one-edge bursts.

This run strengthens that picture in two distinct ways.

First,
\[
\boxed{\text{four-particle direct chains occur only finitely many times in the entire process.}}
\]
Thus eventual same-lineage immediate persistence has a hard pathwise ceiling of two direct edges.

Second,
\[
\boxed{\text{almost every direct edge is followed by a spatial jump of size }R^{1/4-o(1)}.}
\]
This is stronger than merely saying the next particle is not a direct child of the child: it leaves every \(o(R^{1/4})\) Euclidean neighborhood of that child on an asymptotic density-one subset of the direct bursts.

So the remaining obstruction to exponent self-averaging is no longer persistent local-tip continuation. DLA is forced to perform frequent mesoscopic relocations of the attachment location.

However, these relocations need not be independent or morphologically fresh. The process could alternate between a small number of old arms, or jump between separated sites whose harmonic measures remain coupled by the same old fjord/arm geometry. Therefore (11.5) does not by itself yield a stationary annular law or a deterministic particle-per-capacity ratio.

# 13. Why this still does not determine the growth exponent

The radial exponent is controlled by the number of physical particles required to accumulate one unit of logarithmic capacity. The new theorem says that within such a capacity unit there are many mesoscopic switches, but it does not control how many **distinct macroscopic sectors or arms** those switches explore.

A two-arm alternation can satisfy large switch distances repeatedly without producing morphological mixing. Likewise, a deep fjord can contain spatially separated exposed tips whose harmonic fields are highly correlated.

Thus the next missing geometric input is a **sector-diversification / repeated-return penalty**.

A concrete target suggested by the new theorem is the following.

Partition the annulus at radius \(R\) into angular/spatial boxes of diameter \(R^{1/4-\varepsilon}\) (or a slightly larger mesoscopic scale). Among the \(\Omega(R^{1/2})\) far switches in a unit-capacity block, prove either:

1. a positive power of \(R\) distinct boxes are visited; or
2. if the process repeatedly returns to only \(O(1)\) or \(R^{o(1)}\) boxes, a multi-arm/shielding estimate imposes an additional harmonic-measure cost beyond the ordinary \(R^{-1/2}\) Beurling factor.

Either statement would begin coupling the frequent switching clock proved here to the long-arm geometry behind Kesten's \(3/2\) threshold.

Another equivalent target is a return-kernel estimate: after a far switch from a direct child \(y\) to \(z\), bound the chance that one of the next \(k\) capacity-relevant attachments returns to an \(R^{1/4-\varepsilon}\)-neighborhood of \(y\), uniformly in the old cluster morphology. A summable or contracting bound would create a genuine spatial regeneration mechanism.
