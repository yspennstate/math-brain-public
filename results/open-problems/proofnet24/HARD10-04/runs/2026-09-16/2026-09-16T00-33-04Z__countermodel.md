# HARD10-04 — dynamic turnover sharpness countermodel

UTC checkpoint: 2026-09-16T00:33:04Z

This append-only same-run supplement tests the strength of the newly proved dynamic occupation and hot-birth turnover bounds. It is **not a DLA construction**. Its purpose is to determine whether the presently archived algebraic, martingale, balayage-monotonicity, Beurling-scale, capacity-response, and variation constraints are already sufficient to select a unique radial exponent.

They are not.

For every candidate block dimension
\[
D\in[3/2,2],
\]
I construct an abstract nested-measure growth process with
\[
N_R\asymp R^D
\]
steps per unit expected capacity, Shannon entropy \(\log R+o(\log R)\), maximum atom \(\le R^{-1/2}\), one-site response \(\Delta C=p^2\), exact projective balayage, coordinatewise decrease of old masses, exact TV step \(\delta=p\) for the selected atom, the full archived \(q>2\) variation scale, and hot-tip turnover saturating the new \(O(u^{-2})\) upper bound.

Thus even the new **dynamic** inequalities remain compatible with every \(D\in[3/2,2]\). A genuinely planar geometric input is still indispensable.

## 1. Parameters

Fix
\[
D\in[3/2,2],
\qquad
a:=D/3\in[1/2,2/3].
\]
Let \(R\to\infty\), and define the hot mass
\[
h:=R^{-a}.
\]
Take
\[
M:=\lfloor R\rfloor
\]
light atoms of equal mass
\[
\ell:=\frac{1-h}{M}.
\]
The active probability vector therefore consists of one hot atom of mass \(h\) and \(M\) light atoms of mass \(\ell\).

At every discrete step, sample one active atom according to its current mass. Remove that active label and create one new label carrying **exactly the same mass**. All other active masses remain unchanged.

Thus the multiset of active masses is constant in time, while labels are continually renewed.

---

## 2. Nested supports and exact projective balayage

Let \(K_n\) be the set of every label that has appeared by time \(n\); hence
\[
K_n\subset K_{n+1}.
\]
Define \(\mu_n\) on \(K_n\) by assigning the active labels their masses and every previously removed label mass zero.

Suppose label \(x\) of mass \(m\in\{h,\ell\}\) is selected at step \(n+1\), and new label \(y\) is created with mass \(m\). Then
\[
\mu_{n+1}(x)=0,
\qquad
\mu_{n+1}(y)=m,
\]
and all other coordinates are unchanged.

Define a reverse kernel \(\Pi_n:K_{n+1}\to K_n\) by
\[
\Pi_n(y,x)=1
\]
for the newly created label, and let \(\Pi_n(z,z)=1\) on every old label \(z\ne x\). Then
\[
\boxed{
\mu_n=\mu_{n+1}\Pi_n.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
So the process has exact projective balayage in the same algebraic sense as the archived DLA genealogy.

Moreover every old coordinate is nonincreasing:
\[
\boxed{
\mu_{n+1}(z)\le\mu_n(z),
\qquad z\in K_n.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Only a newly born site can carry fresh positive mass.

The total-variation distance of the step is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\delta_{n+1}:=\|\mu_{n+1}-\mu_n\|_{\rm TV}=m,
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
where \(m\) is the mass of the selected atom.

## 3. Capacity response and predictable drift

Define the step capacity increment abstractly by
\[
\boxed{
\Delta C_{n+1}:=\delta_{n+1}^2=m^2.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Thus the one-site response is exactly
\[
\Delta C=p^2
\]
with coefficient one, and capacity equals the accumulated squared-TV motion exactly.

The predictable capacity drift is
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_R
&:=\mathbb E[\Delta C_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n]\\
&=h\,h^2+M\ell\,\ell^2\\
&=h^3+M\ell^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Since
\[
h^3=R^{-3a}=R^{-D},
\qquad
M\ell^3=R^{-2+o(1)},
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
a_R=R^{-D+o(1)}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]
For \(D<2\), the hot atom dominates; at \(D=2\), hot and light terms are of the same power.

Take the deterministic block length
\[
N_R:=\lfloor a_R^{-1}\rfloor.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
N_R=R^{D+o(1)},
\qquad
N_Ra_R=1+o(1).
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Thus every candidate \(D\in[3/2,2]\) appears as a unit-expected-capacity particle dimension.

---

## 4. Actual capacity concentrates around one

Because the active mass multiset is restored after every step, the category/mass selected at successive steps may be generated i.i.d. from the fixed distribution
\[
\mathbb P\{m=h\}=h,
\qquad
\mathbb P\{m=\ell\}=1-h.
\]
Hence the increments \(\Delta C=m^2\) are i.i.d. within this abstract block.

Their second moment is
\[
\mathbb E[(\Delta C)^2]
=h^5+M\ell^5.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_R\mathbb E[(\Delta C)^2]
&\le
R^{3a+o(1)}
\left(R^{-5a}+R^{-4+o(1)}\right)\\
&=
R^{-2a+o(1)}+R^{3a-4+o(1)}\\
&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n=1}^{N_R}\Delta C_n
\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in }L^2\text{ and probability}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
So this is not merely an expected-capacity normalization: unit capacity is sharply concentrated.

## 5. Beurling-scale maximum and Makarov-scale entropy

The maximum active mass is
\[
\boxed{
p_*=h=R^{-a}\le R^{-1/2},}
\tag{5.1}
\]
matching the planar Beurling power restriction.

The Shannon entropy is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_R
&=-h\log h-M\ell\log\ell\\
&=(1-h)\log M+O(h\log R)+O(1)\\
&=\boxed{\log R+o(\log R).}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus the leading discrete-Makarov entropy law is also satisfied.

The cubic moment is
\[
S_3=h^3+M\ell^3=R^{-D+o(1)},
\tag{5.3}
\]
so the abstract third-moment exponent is exactly \(D\).

## 6. Full higher-variation scale

For every fixed \(q>2\),
\[
\mathbb E[\delta^q]
=h^{q+1}+M\ell^{q+1}.
\]
Over \(N_R\) steps,
\[
N_R\mathbb E[\delta^q]
\le
R^{3a-(q+1)a+o(1)}
+
R^{3a-q+o(1)}.
\]
The first exponent is
\[
-a(q-2)\le-\frac{q-2}{2},
\]
and, since \(a\le2/3\),
\[
3a-q\le2-q\le-\frac{q-2}{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
N_R\mathbb E[\delta^q]
\le
R^{-(q-2)/2+o(1)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
This matches or improves the archived DLA \(q>2\) variation bound.

At the critical exponent \(q=2\),
\[
N_R\mathbb E[\delta^2]=1+o(1),
\]
so the abstract process also reproduces unit quadratic variation per unit capacity.

## 7. Hot-tip turnover saturates the new theorem

Use threshold
\[
u=h=R^{-a}.
\]
There is always exactly one active hot atom of mass \(h\).

A hot site is replaced precisely when the hot atom is selected. Over \(N_R\) steps, the number \(B_R\) of hot selections is binomial with mean
\[
\mathbb E B_R=N_Rh
=R^{3a-a+o(1)}
=\boxed{R^{2a+o(1)}=u^{-2+o(1)}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Its variance is of the same order as its mean, so
\[
\boxed{
B_R=R^{2a+o_{\mathbb P}(1)}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus the hot-site turnover saturates the supplement's rigorous upper bound
\[
O(u^{-2}).
\]

Each hot selection contributes actual capacity
\[
h^2=R^{-2a}.
\]
Therefore the total hot capacity over the block is
\[
B_Rh^2=1+o_{\mathbb P}(1)
\tag{7.3}
\]
for \(D<2\), where the light contribution is lower order. In other words, a continually renewing rare hot tip can carry essentially the entire capacity budget while respecting all currently archived scalar and dynamic inequalities.

The fraction of particle times at which the hot atom is selected is
\[
\frac{B_R}{N_R}=h+o_{\mathbb P}(h)=R^{-a+o_{\mathbb P}(1)},
\tag{7.4}
\]
exactly the particle-Palm rare-event scale from the preceding HARD10-04 report.

## 8. Dynamic occupation theorem is also saturated

Take \(D_n\) to be the currently active hot atom. The union of hot labels across the block has cardinality
\[
T_R=B_R+1=R^{2a+o_{\mathbb P}(1)}.
\]
At every time,
\[
\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^3=h^3=R^{-3a}=R^{-D}.
\]
Thus
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{N_R}\sum_{x\in D_n}p_n(x)^3
=N_Rh^3=1+o(1).
\tag{8.1}
\]

The new rigorous DLA occupation theorem has right side
\[
\frac{C}{R}T_R
=CR^{2a-1+o_{\mathbb P}(1)}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
At the critical Beurling scale \(a=1/2\) this is order one and is saturated. For \(a>1/2\) it is weaker than the true value, exactly as identified in the same-run supplement.

So even adding turnover to the scalar sharpness family does not select \(D\).

## 9. Exact lesson of the countermodel

The abstract process satisfies, at the correct power scales:

- nested supports;
- exact projective reverse kernels / balayage algebra;
- coordinatewise nonincrease of every old harmonic-measure coordinate;
- at most one new active label per step (hence no violation of the DLA at-most-three-new-sites combinatorics);
- TV step equal to the selected attachment probability;
- capacity increment exactly equal to squared TV step / selected-probability squared;
- unit concentrated capacity per block;
- \(p_{\max}\le R^{-1/2}\);
- Shannon entropy \(\log R+o(\log R)\);
- cubic moment exponent \(D\);
- the full fixed-\(q>2\) variation hierarchy;
- hot-birth turnover \(u^{-2}\);
- particle-Palm rare-event scale and dynamic hot-tip renewal.

Yet \(D\) is arbitrary in \([3/2,2]\).

Therefore the following statement is rigorous at the level of proof strategy:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The currently archived scalar, martingale, balayage, monotonicity,}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\text{quadratic-variation and turnover inequalities do not determine the DLA exponent.}
}
\]

What this model deliberately lacks is **two-dimensional simple-random-walk geometry**. In real DLA, moving an \(R^{-a}\)-mass hot tip to a fresh spatial site is not free; it must be created by a concrete change in a planar cluster and its harmonic field. The missing theorem must quantify the geometric cost of repeatedly renewing such tips.

This makes the next target sharper than before: prove that the abstract renewal mechanism above cannot be embedded, even approximately, into planar DLA at a sustained rate. A multi-arm / annular-separation / shielding estimate for the birth of fresh high-harmonic-measure sites is the natural missing input.
