The main new input is a shape-uniform **local Beurling mass bound**: for every finite connected planar obstacle of radius \(R\), the harmonic measure from infinity of its intersection with *any* Euclidean ball of radius \(L\ll R\) is at most
\[
C\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R}}.
\]
Unlike the previous checkpoint's crude counting bound \(CL^2R^{-1/2}\), this uses connectedness at all scales and is sharp at the Beurling exponent.

Applying this to the actual DLA trajectory yields two new consequences.

1. **Strong mesoscopic refractory theorem.** If \(H_j\) and \(L_j\) are known at the start of a unit-capacity block and
   \[
   H_j\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}\to0,
   \]
   then asymptotically almost every particle attached in that block has its radius-\(L_j\) neighborhood avoided by the next \(H_j\) particles. For polynomial scales
   \[
   H_j=R_j^\eta,\qquad L_j=R_j^\alpha,
   \]
   the non-return region is
   \[
   \boxed{2\eta+\alpha<1.}
   \]
   In particular, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), almost every pair of **consecutive** attachments is separated by more than \(R_j^{1-\varepsilon}\), and a balanced choice \(H_j=L_j=R_j^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) is also admissible.

2. **Empirical particle-box anti-concentration.** If the unit-capacity block is partitioned into boxes of side \(L_j=o(R_j)\), then the empirical distribution of the actual particle attachment locations assigns at most
   \[
   C\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}+o(1)
   \]
   mass to any one box, almost surely eventually. Hence the particle trace visits at least
   \[
   c\sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L_j+1}}
   \]
   boxes, its box Shannon/min/Renyi entropies have the corresponding lower bound, and two independent empirical particle samples from the block lie within distance \(L_j\) with probability at most \(C\sqrt{L_j/R_j}+o(1)\).

Combining this particle-box theorem with the preceding checkpoint's actual-capacity box theorem gives a two-measure spatial support spectrum: at scale \(L=R^\alpha\), \(0\le\alpha<1\), the set of boxes visited in a unit-capacity block has cardinality at least
\[
R^{\psi(\alpha)+o(1)},
\qquad
\psi(\alpha)=\max\left\{1-2\alpha,\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\right\},
\]
where the first term comes from actual capacity and the second from actual particle occupancy (with the first term relevant only while positive). The crossover is \(\alpha=1/3\).

These are genuine standard-lattice DLA statements. They do **not** yet prove a growth exponent: macroscopic switching and multiscale spatial support do not imply that the harmonic field forgets old arm/fjord geometry.

# 4. Standard annular discrete Beurling form

I isolate the exact external lemma used below.

### Lemma 4.1 — annular Beurling crossing estimate

There is a universal constant \(C\) such that the following holds for nearest-neighbor simple random walk in \(\mathbb Z^2\). Let \(1\le r\le R/16\), let \(y\in\mathbb Z^2\), and let \(\Gamma\) contain a nearest-neighbor connected crossing of the annulus centered at \(y\), from radius at most \(2(r+1)\) to radius at least \(R/4\). A walk entering from outside radius \(R/2\) has probability at most
\[
\boxed{
C\sqrt{\frac{r+1}{R}}
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
to reach \(B(y,r)\) before hitting \(\Gamma\).

This is the translated/rescaled annular form of the discrete Beurling estimate (Lawler-Limic, Theorem 6.8.1). Only nearest-neighbor connectivity of the crossing and simple random walk are used. Lattice \(+1\) corrections are absorbed into the constants.

No DLA property is used in Lemma 4.1.

# 5. New local harmonic-measure theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — shape-uniform local Beurling mass

Let \(K\subset\mathbb Z^2\) be finite and nearest-neighbor connected, contain the origin, and contain a point \(z_R\) with \(|z_R|\ge R\). Let \(\omega_K\) be harmonic measure from infinity on \(K\). Then for every \(y\in\mathbb Z^2\) and every \(1\le L\le R/16\),
\[
\boxed{
\omega_K\bigl(K\cap B(y,L)\bigr)
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R}}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

If \(K\cap B(y,L)=\varnothing\), there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix
\[
x\in K\cap B(y,L).
\]
Because
\[
|z_R|
\le
|y|+|z_R-y|,
\]
at least one of the two points \(0,z_R\in K\) has distance at least \(R/2\) from \(y\). Call such a point \(z_*\).

Since \(K\) is connected, choose a nearest-neighbor path \(\gamma\subset K\) from \(x\) to \(z_*\). Starting from \(x\), follow \(\gamma\) and retain the terminal portion after its **last** visit to \(B(y,L)\). This terminal portion, call it \(\Gamma\), is connected, is contained in \(K\setminus B(y,L)\) except possibly for its initial endpoint, begins at distance at most \(L+1\) from the ball, and reaches distance at least \(R/2\) from \(y\). Therefore it contains a connected annular crossing from scale \(O(L+1)\) to scale \(R/4\).

Now start simple random walk at a point \(w\) tending to infinity. On the event
\[
S_{T_K}\in K\cap B(y,L),
\]
the walk must reach \(B(y,L)\) before hitting \(K\setminus B(y,L)\), hence in particular before hitting \(\Gamma\). Applying Lemma 4.1 gives, uniformly for remote \(w\),
\[
\mathbb P^w\{S_{T_K}\in K\cap B(y,L)\}
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R}}.
\]
Taking the defining limit from infinity proves (5.1). \(\square\)

### Scope check

The proof needs only:

- finite connectedness of \(K\);
- two points of \(K\) at mutual scale \(R\) (here supplied by \(0,z_R\));
- the standard annular Beurling estimate.

It does **not** use the DLA growth exponent, Makarov, capacity response, a shape theorem, stationarity, or any assumed fractal dimension.

For DLA, \(K_n=\overline A_n\) is connected and contains the seed and a point at distance \(R_n+O(1)\), so uniformly in the realized cluster,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{y\in\mathbb Z^2}
\mu_n\bigl(B(y,L)\bigr)
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{R_n}}
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
whenever \(L\le cR_n\).

This is the key improvement over the preceding counting estimate
\[
\mu_n(B(y,L))\le C(L+1)^2R_n^{-1/2}.
\]
At polynomial scale \(L=R^\alpha\), the old exponent was \(2\alpha-1/2\), while (5.2) gives \((\alpha-1)/2\).

G_n(H_j,L_j)

# 7. Almost-sure strong refractory theorem

Let
\[
V_j(H_j,L_j)
=
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathbf1_{G_n(H_j,L_j)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The windows overlap, so the indicators are not an ordinary martingale-difference sequence. Partition their start indices according to the residue of \(n-\sigma_j\) modulo
\[
M_j:=H_j+1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Within a fixed residue class, one complete quick-return window has been revealed by the time the next start is conditioned upon.

For a conditional Bernoulli variable \(J\) with conditional mean \(g\),
\[
\mathbb E[e^J\mid\mathcal G]
=1+(e-1)g
\le e^{(e-1)g}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Thus, on each residue class,
\[
\exp\left(
\sum J-(e-1)\sum g
\right)
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale. Use a barrier \(8\log R_j\) on each class. In every regime in which (6.3) can tend to zero one automatically has \(H_j\le R_j^{1/2+o(1)}\), so unioning over \(H_j+1\) classes leaves a summable error along capacity blocks because \(R_j\) grows exponentially in \(j\) by (3.2).

Borel-Cantelli gives almost surely eventually
\[
V_j(H_j,L_j)
\le
C\sum_{n\in B_j}g_n
+
C(H_j+1)\log R_j.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Using (6.3) and \(N_j\ge cR_j^{3/2}\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{V_j(H_j,L_j)}{N_j}
\le
C H_j\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}
+
C\frac{(H_j+1)\log R_j}{R_j^{3/2}}.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

### Corollary 7.1 — sharp polynomial non-return region from Beurling

If
\[
H_j=R_j^\eta,
\qquad
L_j=R_j^\alpha,
\qquad
\eta\ge0,\quad0\le\alpha<1,
\]
then the first term in (7.5) is
\[
R_j^{\eta+(\alpha-1)/2+o(1)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
2\eta+\alpha<1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{V_j(R_j^\eta,R_j^\alpha)}{N_j}\to0
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This strictly contains the preceding checkpoint's region \(\eta+2\alpha<1/2\).

### Corollary 7.2 — consecutive attachments are almost macroscopically separated

Take \(H_j=1\) and any fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), with
\[
L_j=R_j^{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{N_j}
\#\left\{
n\in B_j:
|X_{n+2}-X_{n+1}|\le R_j^{1-\varepsilon}
\right\}
\le
C R_j^{-\varepsilon/2}+o(1)
\to0.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

So, in exponent scale, almost every consecutive attachment jump is macroscopic. This does not mean the particles themselves move ballistically; each new particle still arrives from infinity by random walk and attaches to the cluster. The statement concerns the **locations of two successive attachments**.

### Corollary 7.3 — balanced mesoscopic refractory scale

For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), take
\[
H_j=R_j^{1/3-\varepsilon},
\qquad
L_j=R_j^{1/3-\varepsilon}.
\]
Then
\[
2\eta+\alpha=1-3\varepsilon<1,
\]
so for \(1-o(1)\) of block particles, the radius-\(R_j^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) neighborhood of the attachment is avoided by the next \(R_j^{1/3-\varepsilon}\) particles.

### Corollary 7.4 — microscopic refractory time remains near \(\sqrt R\)

For fixed \(L\), any
\[
H_j=R_j^{1/2-\varepsilon}
\]
still gives vanishing quick-return density, now as the endpoint \(\alpha=0\) of the same tradeoff.

# 8. Empirical particle-box anti-concentration

The local Beurling mass theorem also gives a spatial statement with no short-time window.

Fix an axis-aligned square grid of side \(L_j\), where \(1\le L_j\le cR_j\), chosen at block start. Let
\[
N_{j,Q}
:=
\#\{n\in B_j:X_{n+1}\in Q\}
\tag{8.1}
\]
for a grid square \(Q\).

By the capacity-radius comparison (3.2), during one unit-capacity block all attachment locations stay within a deterministic constant multiple of \(R_j\). Hence only \(O(R_j^2)\) grid boxes can be visited (this crude count is enough for a union bound).

A square of side \(L_j\) is contained in a ball of radius \(C(L_j+1)\). Therefore Theorem 5.1 gives, throughout the block,
\[
\mathbb P(X_{n+1}\in Q\mid\mathcal F_n)
\le
q_j,
\qquad
q_j:=C\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For a fixed box, if \(I_n^Q=\mathbf1_{\{X_{n+1}\in Q\}}\), then
\[
\exp\left(
\sum I_n^Q-(e-1)q_j\,m
\right)
\]
is a nonnegative supermartingale through the first \(m\) starts of the block. Ville's inequality therefore gives, simultaneously at the random block end,
\[
N_{j,Q}
\le
(e-1)q_jN_j+6\log R_j
\tag{8.3}
\]
except with conditional probability at most \(R_j^{-6}\) for that box. Unioning over \(O(R_j^2)\) possible boxes and then applying Borel-Cantelli over \(j\) gives almost surely eventually
\[
\boxed{
\max_Q\frac{N_{j,Q}}{N_j}
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}
+
C\frac{\log R_j}{R_j^{3/2}}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Define the empirical particle-location measure
\[
\nu_j
:=
\frac1{N_j}
\sum_{n\in B_j}\delta_{X_{n+1}}.
\tag{8.5}
\]
Then (8.4) says
\[
\boxed{
\|\nu_j\|_{\ell^\infty(\text{boxes})}
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}+o(1).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

### Consequence 8.1 — particle-box support and entropy

The number \(M_j^{\rm part}(L_j)\) of grid boxes actually visited during the block obeys
\[
\boxed{
M_j^{\rm part}(L_j)
\ge
c\sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L_j+1}}
}
\tag{8.7}
\]
almost surely eventually (up to harmless constants when the right-hand side is large).

For the box probabilities \(\nu_{j,Q}\),
\[
H_\infty(\nu_j)
\ge
\frac12\log\frac{R_j}{L_j+1}-O(1),
\tag{8.8}
\]
and hence the Shannon entropy satisfies the same lower bound. For every fixed Renyi order \(s>1\),
\[
\sum_Q\nu_{j,Q}^s
\le
\|\nu_j\|_\infty^{s-1},
\]
so the Renyi effective number of particle boxes is also at least \(c\sqrt{R_j/(L_j+1)}\).

### Consequence 8.2 — empirical pair anti-clustering

Let \(U_j,V_j\) be independent samples from the empirical measure \(\nu_j\), conditional on the realized block. If \(|U_j-V_j|\le L_j\), the two points' grid boxes are equal or belong to one of a uniformly bounded number of neighboring boxes. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(\nu_j\otimes\nu_j)
\{|x-y|\le L_j\}
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{L_j+1}{R_j}}+o(1).
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

For every \(\alpha<1\), choosing \(L_j=R_j^\alpha\) makes the right-hand side \(R_j^{-(1-\alpha)/2+o(1)}\). Thus two independent empirical particle locations from one capacity block are separated by more than \(R_j^\alpha\) with probability tending to one for every fixed \(\alpha<1\).

The preceding checkpoint controls the **actual-capacity** distribution among \(L\)-boxes:
\[
M_j^{\rm cap}(L)
\ge
c\frac{R_j}{(L+1)^2}
\tag{9.1}

whenever the right-hand side is nontrivial.

\[
M_j^{\rm part}(L)
\ge
c\sqrt{\frac{R_j}{L+1}}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Every box carrying capacity is necessarily visited by a particle, so the total number of visited boxes satisfies both lower bounds.

At scale
\[
L=R_j^\alpha,
\qquad0\le\alpha<1,
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
M_j^{\rm visit}(R_j^\alpha)
\ge
R_j^{\psi(\alpha)+o(1)},
\qquad
\psi(\alpha)

\max\left\{
1-2\alpha,

The capacity lower bound dominates for \(\alpha<1/3\); the particle-occupancy lower bound dominates for \(\alpha>1/3\); and the two meet at

\psi(1/3)=1/3.

# 10. Why the new results still do not close \(\beta\)

The new Beurling-ball theorem is much stronger than the preceding site-counting local estimate. In particular, it forces almost every consecutive attachment to relocate on essentially macroscopic exponent scale, and it forces the empirical attachment cloud to remain anti-concentrated even in boxes of side \(R^{1-\varepsilon}\).

However, none of this proves that the **harmonic field** regenerates.

A connected old arm can extend through many macroscopically separated boxes. Consecutive attachments may alternate among distant tips whose probabilities remain coupled through the same old shielding/fjord geometry. Likewise, a large empirical support does not imply stationarity, independence, or contraction of the normalized annular morphology.

The cubic moment
\[
S_3(n)=\sum_xp_n(x)^3
\]
can still be controlled by polynomially rare high-harmonic-measure tips. The present local ball bound controls where harmonic mass may concentrate spatially, but it does not yet give the large-deviation law of those rare tip weights under repeated morphological renewal.

Therefore no improved radial exponent is claimed here.

# 11. Strongest next attack

The most concrete next target is now a **macroscopic return kernel in capacity time**, rather than particle time.

This report proves that at particle time scale the process almost never returns quickly to a ball once its radius/time pair obeys \(2\eta+\alpha<1\). The earlier archived backwards-genealogy theorem proves exponential loss of old ancestry information in logarithmic-capacity time. The missing bridge is to show that a spatial region which has just carried a non-negligible amount of *capacity* cannot regain another non-negligible capacity share after a bounded capacity separation, except with a quantitatively small probability.

A useful target statement is: for boxes \(Q\) of side \(R^\alpha\), define their actual capacity shares \(\pi_{j,Q}\). Prove for some \(\alpha>0\), fixed \(t>0\), and \(\rho<1\) that uniformly over realized old morphology,
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\sum_Q \pi_{j,Q}\pi_{j+t,Q}^{\rm enlarged}
\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right]
\le \rho+o(1),
\]
or preferably a decaying bound in \(t\). Here `enlarged` allows a controlled neighborhood of \(Q\) to avoid grid artifacts.

A planar multi-arm/shielding estimate giving such contraction would combine:

- this run's macroscopic spatial relocation and empirical anti-concentration;
- the preceding actual-capacity box diversification;
- the archived exponential genealogy screening in capacity time;
- and the exact capacity/Renyi-3 reduction.

That is currently the clearest route toward a genuine annular renormalization theorem.
