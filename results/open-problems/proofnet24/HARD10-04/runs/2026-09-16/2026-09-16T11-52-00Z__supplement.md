## 1. Predictable rare-event product lemma

Let
\[
\mathcal G_0\subseteq\mathcal H_1\subseteq\mathcal G_1
\subseteq\mathcal H_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal H_m\subseteq\mathcal G_m
\]
be filtrations/checkpoint sigma-fields. For \(i=1,\ldots,m\), let

- \(A_i\in\mathcal G_i\) be an event whose occurrence is decided at checkpoint \(i\);
- \(b_i\) be a nonnegative \(\mathcal H_i\)-measurable random variable;
- and assume
  \[
  \mathbb P(A_i\mid\mathcal H_i)\le b_i
  \tag{1.1}
  \]
  on the event \(A_1\cap\cdots\cap A_{i-1}\).

Define, with the convention that a zero denominator forces the product to be zero unless the corresponding event has zero probability,
\[
M_k
:=
\prod_{i=1}^k
\frac{\mathbf1_{A_i}}{b_i}.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[M_k]\le1}
\tag{1.3}
\]
for every \(k\).

### Proof

Condition at the checkpoint immediately before \(A_k\) is decided. On the event that all earlier \(A_i\) occurred, \(M_{k-1}\) and \(b_k\) are already measurable. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[M_k\mid\mathcal H_k]
&=
M_{k-1}
\frac{\mathbb P(A_k\mid\mathcal H_k)}{b_k}\\
&\le
M_{k-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Outside the preceding-event set both sides vanish. Iterating gives (1.3). QED.

### Threshold corollary

For every deterministic \(K>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left(
A_1\cap\cdots\cap A_m,
\ \prod_{i=1}^m b_i\le K
\right)
\le K.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]
Indeed, on the displayed event,
\[
M_m
=
\frac1{\prod_i b_i}
\ge\frac1K.
\]
Markov's inequality and (1.3) give (1.4).

This lemma is the precise device needed whenever the random birth-mass product is itself part of the event being union-bounded.

## 2. Application to a specified DLA genealogy/time tuple

Fix the DLA cluster at \(\tau_R\). Fix:

- a root \(v_0\);
- a nearest-neighbour spatial path \(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_m\);
- increasing particle times at which the required parent/child selections are to occur.

For \(i\ge1\), on the event that generations \(0,\ldots,i-1\) have been realized, the child \(v_i\) has already been born. Let
\[
b_i
\]
be its harmonic measure at birth. This is measurable before the prescribed future selection of \(v_i\).

The archived coordinatewise balayage monotonicity gives
\[
p_{\rm prescribed\ selection\ time}(v_i)
\le b_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Therefore the hypotheses of the predictable product lemma hold with \(A_i\) equal to the event that \(v_i\) is selected at its prescribed time.

Consequently, for every deterministic \(K>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{specified genealogy/time tuple is realized},\\
\prod_{i=1}^m b_i\le K
\end{array}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right\}
\le K.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
This statement is stronger and more precise than writing an upper bound by a random product on the right-hand side.

## 3. Rigorous recovery of the free-energy branch bound

For a realized branch,
\[
\prod_{i=1}^m b_i
=
\left(\frac{\Theta(P)}m\right)^{m/2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Hence on the event
\[
\Theta(P)\le\varepsilon,
\]
we have
\[
\prod_i b_i
\le
\left(\frac\varepsilon m\right)^{m/2}.
\]
Applying (2.2) with
\[
K=\left(\frac\varepsilon m\right)^{m/2}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{specified genealogy/time tuple is realized},\\
\Theta(P)\le\varepsilon
\end{array}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right\}
\le
\left(\frac\varepsilon m\right)^{m/2}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
This is the fully rigorous form used by the predecessor branch-free-energy counting argument.

## 4. Rigorous recovery of the new branch-action count

For the action
\[
\mathfrak B(P)=\frac{m^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\Theta(P)}},
\]
the event
\[
\mathfrak B(P)\ge B
\]
implies
\[
\Theta(P)\le\frac{m^3}{B^2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\prod_i b_i
=
\left(\frac{\Theta(P)}m\right)^{m/2}
\le
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Applying (2.2) with
\[
K=(m/B)^m
\]
gives the deterministic threshold bound
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{specified genealogy/time tuple is realized},\\
\mathfrak B(P)\ge B
\end{array}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\tau_R}
\right\}
\le
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Now union over at most \(CR^2\) roots, at most \(4^m\) nearest-neighbour spatial paths, and at most
\[
T\binom Tm
\]
time tuples. Since
\[
\binom Tm\le(eT/m)^m,
\]
we recover exactly
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{T_R\le T,\ \mathfrak B_R^*\ge B\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\}
\le
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
\left(\frac{4eT}{B}\right)^m.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus Theorem 6.1 of the main report is valid with the random-history dependence of the birth masses handled explicitly.
