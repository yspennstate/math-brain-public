# 5. Pathwise annular birth-energy bound

Define the branch birth energy
\[
E(P):=\sum_{i=1}^m b_i^2.
\tag{5.1}

### Theorem 5.1 — PROVED

There is a deterministic constant \(Q_0<\infty\) such that for every sufficiently large \(R\), every realized annulus-crossing forward genealogical branch satisfies

\boxed{E(P)\le Q_0.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

For every edge \(v_{i-1}\to v_i\), the child \(v_i\) is created in the newborn shell of the step at which its parent \(v_{i-1}\) is selected. By (3.4),
\[

\le

\le
\Delta C_{t_i+1},
\tag{5.3}

where \(t_i+1\) is the parent-selection step creating \(v_i\).

E(P)

C_{\tau_{2R}}-C_{\tau_R}.

By the capacity-radius comparison (3.1), the right-hand side is

\frac2\pi\log2+O(1),

Thus \(\Theta\) is a dimensionless refinement of the crude \(O(1)\) energy bound.

When \(E(P)>0\), define

q_i:=\frac{b_i^2}{E(P)},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m,
\tag{6.3}

and let \(u_m\) be the uniform law on \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\): \(u_m(i)=1/m\).

\[
\boxed{
\Theta(P)
=
E(P)\exp\{-D(u_m\|q)\}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\prod_{i=1}^m b_i
=
\left(\frac{E(P)}m\right)^{m/2}
\exp\left\{-\frac m2D(u_m\|q)\right\}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

### Proof

By definition,
\[
D(u_m\|q)
=
\frac1m\sum_{i=1}^m
\log\frac{1/m}{q_i}
=
-\log m-rac1m\sum_{i=1}^m\log q_i.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Since \(b_i^2=E(P)q_i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^m\log b_i
&=
\frac m2\log E(P)
+
\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m\log q_i\\
&=
\frac m2\log\frac{E(P)}m
-
\frac m2D(u_m\|q).
\end{aligned}

Exponentiating gives (6.5), and raising the geometric mean to the square and multiplying by \(m\) gives (6.4). QED.

D(u_m\|q)=o(\log R).

This is the first precise rigidity statement in the project for a Kesten-saturating genealogical branch.

# 7. Probability of a fixed genealogical branch

The next lemma converts \(\Theta(P)\) into an actual path probability.

Fix the cluster at time \(\tau_R\). Consider:

- a specified root \(v_0\);
- a specified nearest-neighbour spatial path \(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_m\);
- specified increasing particle times at which the genealogy is to be realized.

### Lemma 7.1 — PROVED

Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\), the probability that the specified branch is realized at the specified times is at most
\[
\boxed{
\prod_{i=1}^m b_i
=
\left(\frac{\Theta(P)}m\right)^{m/2}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Here the root-selection factor is simply bounded by one.

### Proof

After a child \(v_i\) is born, its future harmonic-measure coordinate can only decrease by the archived balayage monotonicity. Therefore, conditional on all preceding required genealogy events, the probability of selecting \(v_i\) at its prescribed future time is at most its birth mass \(b_i\).

Sequential conditioning and multiplication give at most \(\prod_i b_i\). The second equality is exactly the definition (6.1). QED.

### Important scope

This is an **upper bound** for realizing a fixed forward genealogy. We do not assume independence of selections, nor do we freeze the harmonic field. The monotonicity \(p_t(v_i)\le b_i\) is what makes the sequential product valid.

# 8. Annular branch-counting inequality

Define the annular crossing time
\[
T_R:=\tau_{2R}-\tau_R.
\tag{8.1}
\]
For the realized forward genealogy during this epoch, define
\[
\Theta_R^*
:=
\max\{\Theta(P):P\text{ is an annulus-crossing branch}\}.
\tag{8.2}
\]
There is at least one such branch when \(\tau_{2R}<\infty\), which holds almost surely.

### Theorem 8.1 — PROVED exact counting bound

There is a universal \(C<\infty\) such that, conditional on \(\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\), for every \(T\ge R\) and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{T_R\le T,\ \Theta_R^*\le\varepsilon\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\}
\le
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
\left[
\frac{4eT\sqrt\varepsilon}{m^{3/2}}
\right]^m.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
The harmless constant \(C_0\) accounts for the root lying on the exterior boundary rather than exactly on the Euclidean circle of radius \(R\).

### Proof

If \(T_R\le T\), the first particle reaching radius \(2R\) has a unique ancestral chain back to a root active at \(\tau_R\). By (4.3), its number \(m\) of child edges is at least \(R-C_0\), and clearly \(m\le T\).

The number of possible roots is at most the number of lattice sites in an \(O(R)\)-disk, hence at most \(CR^2\).

For a fixed root and branch length \(m\), there are at most \(4^m\) nearest-neighbour spatial paths.

The root selection time and the \(m\) ordered descendant-selection times can be chosen in at most
\[
T\binom{T}{m}
\tag{8.4}
\]
ways; this deliberately overcounts and is sufficient.

On the event \(\Theta_R^*\le\varepsilon\), every realized crossing branch satisfies \(\Theta(P)\le\varepsilon\). By Lemma 7.1, each fixed path/time specification has probability at most
\[
\left(\frac\varepsilon m\right)^{m/2}.
\tag{8.5}
\]
Unioning over roots, paths, times, and lengths gives
\[
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
4^m\binom{T}{m}
\left(\frac\varepsilon m\right)^{m/2}.
\]
Using
\[
\binom{T}{m}\le\left(\frac{eT}{m}\right)^m
\]
produces (8.3). QED.

# 11. The branch exponent cannot exceed one: deterministic area sharpness

Before radius \(2R\) is reached, all occupied sites lie in a deterministic \(O(R)\)-disk. Since each lattice site is attached at most once,
\[
\boxed{T_R\le C_{\rm area}R^2}
\tag{11.1}
\]
pathwise.

This elementary upper bound forces a lower bound on the maximum crossing-branch free energy.

### Theorem 11.1 — PROVED

For every fixed \(\delta>0\), almost surely eventually
\[
\boxed{
\Theta_R^*>R^{-1-\delta}.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{R\to\infty}
\frac{-\log(\Theta_R^*\wedge1)}{\log R}
\le1.
}
\tag{11.3}
\]
In particular \(0\le\underline\gamma_{\rm br}\le1\).

### Proof

Apply (8.3) with
\[
T=C_{\rm area}R^2,
\qquad
\varepsilon=R^{-1-\delta}.
\]
For \(m\ge R-C_0\), the bracket is at most
\[
C\frac{R^2R^{-(1+\delta)/2}}{R^{3/2}}
=
CR^{-\delta/2}.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb P\{T_R\le C_{\rm area}R^2,\ \Theta_R^*\le R^{-1-\delta}\}

is bounded by a polynomial times \((CR^{-\delta/2})^{R-O(1)}\), hence is summable.

The exact allowed branch-free-energy exponent range is therefore the same interval naturally suggested by the rigorous particle/radius window:

0\le\gamma_{\rm br}\le1.

The two endpoints correspond to:

This does not prove that \(\gamma_{\rm br}\) converges or is deterministic.

# 12. A precise rigidity dichotomy for beating Kesten

Combine Theorem 6.1 with Theorem 10.1. For every crossing branch,
\[
\Theta(P)
=
E(P)e^{-D(u_m\|q)}.
\tag{12.1}
\]
Therefore a sufficient route to \(\underline\gamma_{\rm br}\ge\gamma>0\) is the following branchwise dichotomy:

> For every sufficiently large annulus and every crossing genealogy \(P\), either
> \[
> E(P)\le R^{-\gamma+o(1)},
> \]
> or
> \[
> D(u_m\|q)\ge\gamma\log R-o(\log R).
> \]

More generally, if one can prove exponents \(\gamma_E,\gamma_D\ge0\) with
\[
E(P)\le R^{-\gamma_E+o(1)},
\qquad
D(u_m\|q)\ge\gamma_D\log R-o(\log R)
\]
uniformly for crossing branches, then
\[
\Theta_R^*\le R^{-(\gamma_E+\gamma_D)+o(1)}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\overline\beta
\le
\frac{2}{3+\gamma_E+\gamma_D}.
}
\tag{12.2}
\]

This separates two mechanisms that previous scalar Kesten inequalities conflate.

### Why this is sharper than asking only for smaller \(p_{\max}\)

A branch can obey the universal pointwise Beurling bound
\[
b_i\lesssim R^{-1/2}
\]
while still having \(\Theta(P)=O(1)\), if order \(R\) successive generations all have birth mass of order \(R^{-1/2}\). Thus improving only a typical harmonic measure is insufficient.

Conversely, even if \(E(P)=O(1)\), a sufficiently uneven distribution of that energy along generations produces a logarithmic KL penalty and therefore a polynomially smaller branch realization probability.

So the exact obstruction to beating Kesten is now a very rigid object: an annulus-crossing lineage with **order-one total birth energy distributed nearly uniformly over order-\(R\) genealogical generations**.

1. **No use of the unknown exponent.** Capacity-radius comparison is deterministic up to universal additive constants; no assumed particle/radius power law enters.
2. **Branch length.** The lower bound \(m\ge R-O(1)\) uses only nearest-neighbour genealogy and Euclidean displacement from a root near radius \(R\) to a particle at radius \(2R\).
3. **Birth versus selection mass.** The probability of selecting a child later is bounded by its birth mass because old harmonic-measure coordinates are monotone nonincreasing under the archived balayage identity.
4. **Energy bookkeeping.** A branch uses distinct parent-selection steps, so summing child \(b_i^2\) is bounded by the total newborn squared energy, hence by total capacity in the annulus.
5. **No independence assumption.** Lemma 7.1 uses sequential conditional probabilities only.
6. **Combinatorial overcounting is safe.** Root count \(CR^2\), path count \(4^m\), and time count \(T\binom{T}{m}\) are upper bounds; overcounting can only weaken the result.
7. **Exponent algebra.** In (8.3), with \(T=R^{(3+\gamma)/2-\eta}\) and \(\varepsilon=R^{-\gamma}\), the bracket is \(O(R^{-\eta})\) at \(m\asymp R\).
8. **Area converse.** Before reaching \(2R\), at most \(O(R^2)\) distinct lattice sites can be attached, so \(T_R\le CR^2\). Inserting \(\varepsilon=R^{-1-\delta}\) makes the branch-counting bracket \(O(R^{-\delta/2})\).

10. **Literature overlap.** The basic geometric-mean/capacity mechanism is explicitly acknowledged as closely related to Kesten/Losev–Smirnov. Novelty of the free-energy exponent packaging is not claimed without a dedicated literature audit.
11. **Not a full exponent theorem.** The result gives an upper bound on \(\overline\beta\) in terms of a still-uncontrolled branch exponent. It does not prove convergence or determinism.
