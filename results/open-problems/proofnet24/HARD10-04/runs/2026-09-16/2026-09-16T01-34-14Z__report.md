For standard external site diffusion-limited aggregation on \(\mathbb Z^2\), start from \(A_1=\{0\}\). Let
\[
K_n:=\overline A_n=A_n\cup\partial A_n,
\qquad
p_n(x):=\omega(x,K_n),
\qquad
R_n:=\max_{x\in A_n}|x|_2,
\]
and attach \(X_{n+1}\in\partial A_n\) with conditional law \(p_n\), using the first-hit convention fixed in the preceding HARD10-04 reports.

The full target remains **OPEN**. This iteration does not prove that
\[
\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\]
converges almost surely, does not determine a deterministic \(\beta\), and does not refute existence/determinism.

The new progress is a rigorous **capacity-renewal theorem for the moving boundary**.

1. If one freezes the entire boundary present at a large stopping time \(T\), then the total amount of capacity that those inherited boundary sites can ever generate in the future has conditional expectation only
   \[
   O(R_T^{-1/2}).
   \]
   At the intrinsic unit-capacity stopping times \(\sigma_j\), their future capacity contribution is therefore almost surely polynomially negligible.

2. There is a canonical attachment-genealogy on boundary sites: the current boundary is generation zero; when a generation-\(r\) site is selected, every newly exposed boundary site is generation \(r+1\). The squared harmonic-measure mass created in the children is bounded by the actual capacity increment of the parent selection. Combining this with the inherited-boundary theorem yields
   \[
   \mathbb E[D_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
   \le C16^r R_T^{-1/2},
   \]
   where \(D_r(T)\) is all future capacity generated by selections of generation-\(r\) sites.

3. Consequently, for every
   \[
   \kappa<\frac{1}{2\log16},
   \]
   almost surely, for every sufficiently large unit-capacity block starting at radius \(R_j\), the first \(\kappa\log R_j\) generations together generate only \(o(1)\) capacity. Thus asymptotically almost all capacity in the block is produced by boundary sites lying **more than logarithmically deep in attachment genealogy** from the boundary that existed when the block began.

4. As a complementary particle-time statement, high-harmonic-measure sites have short lifetime. Uniformly over a radial epoch \(R\to2R\), a boundary site of particle-age \(a\) satisfies eventually
   \[
   p\lesssim \min\left\{R^{-1/2},\frac{\log R}{1+a}\right\}.
   \]
   Hence capacity production from sites older than \(R(\log R)^{1+\varepsilon}\) particle steps is negligible in a unit-capacity block.

These results strengthen the preceding hot-tip turnover theorem: not only must capacity-producing tips turn over, but the inherited front itself rapidly becomes irrelevant for future capacity and capacity production is pushed to successively newborn generations. They still do not determine the exponent, because old **sites** can disappear while old **geometry and screening** continue to influence the harmonic measure of newly born sites.

# 4. Site-lifetime capacity lemma

The central new elementary observation is that the capacity a boundary site can ever produce is controlled by its harmonic measure **at birth/current observation time**.

Let \(T\) be a finite stopping time and let \(y\in\partial A_T\). Define its future selection time
\[
\tau_y:=\inf\{n\ge T:X_{n+1}=y\},
\]
with \(\tau_y=\infty\) if it is never selected. Before selection, \(y\) remains an old closure site, so (3.4) gives
\[
p_n(y)\le p_T(y)
\qquad(T\le n\le\tau_y).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the eventual capacity produced by \(y\)
\[
G_T(y):=
\begin{cases}
 d_{\tau_y}(y),&\tau_y<\infty,\\
 0,&\tau_y=\infty.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — PROVED

For every finite stopping time \(T\) and every \(y\in\partial A_T\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[G_T(y)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le16p_T(y)^2.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Using (3.3), (4.1), and the fact that a lattice site is selected at most once,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[G_T(y)\mid\mathcal F_T]
&=
\mathbb E\left[
\sum_{n\ge T}
\mathbf1_{\{X_{n+1}=y\}}d_n(y)
\middle|\mathcal F_T
\right]\\
&\le
16p_T(y)^2
\mathbb E\left[
\sum_{n\ge T}
\mathbf1_{\{X_{n+1}=y\}}
\middle|\mathcal F_T
\right]\\
&\le16p_T(y)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last random sum is at most one pathwise. \(\square\)

There is an equivalent hazard formulation. While \(y\) is active,
\[
\mathbb P(X_{n+1}=y\mid\mathcal F_n)=p_n(y),
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\sum_{n\ge T}
\mathbf1_{\{y\text{ active at }n\}}p_n(y)
\middle|\mathcal F_T
\right]
=
\mathbb P(\tau_y<\infty\mid\mathcal F_T)
\le1.
\tag{4.4}
\]
This identity is useful below and makes clear that no independence assumption is being made.

# 5. Inherited-boundary capacity renewal

Let
\[
\partial_T:=\partial A_T
\]
be the entire boundary present at stopping time \(T\). Define the **total future actual capacity generated by inherited boundary sites**
\[
G_T^{\rm old}
:=
\sum_{n\ge T}
\Delta_{n+1}
\mathbf1_{\{X_{n+1}\in\partial_T\}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Each site in \(\partial_T\) can appear in the sum at most once.

### Theorem 5.1 — PROVED

For every finite stopping time \(T\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[G_T^{\rm old}\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le16\sum_{y\in\partial_T}p_T(y)^2
\le16p_T^*
\le C R_T^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof

Sum Lemma 4.1 over \(y\in\partial_T\). Since \(\sum_y p_T(y)=1\),
\[
\sum_y p_T(y)^2\le p_T^*.
\]
Apply Beurling (3.5). \(\square\)

Thus the currently visible boundary has vanishing future capacity value as the cluster radius grows.

There is also a predictable version. Define
\[
A_T^{\rm old}
:=
\sum_{n\ge T}
\sum_{y\in\partial_T}
\mathbf1_{\{y\in\partial A_n\}}
 p_n(y)d_n(y).
\tag{5.3}
\]
By Tonelli and the tower property,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[A_T^{\rm old}\mid\mathcal F_T]
=
\mathbb E[G_T^{\rm old}\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le C R_T^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
So the theorem is not an artifact of realized selections: the predictable capacity drift assigned to inherited sites is equally negligible.

## Corollary 5.2 — almost-sure renewal at the capacity clock

Take \(T=\sigma_j\). For every fixed \(\gamma<1/2\), Markov's inequality and (5.2) give
\[
\mathbb P\left(
G_{\sigma_j}^{\rm old}>R_j^{-\gamma}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right)
\le
C R_j^{-(1/2-\gamma)}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
The right side is summable by (3.8). The same bound holds for \(A_{\sigma_j}^{\rm old}\). Hence Borel–Cantelli yields
\[
\boxed{
G_{\sigma_j}^{\rm old}
+A_{\sigma_j}^{\rm old}
\le
R_j^{-\gamma}
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
almost surely for all sufficiently large \(j\), after harmless adjustment of constants/exponent.

Because (5.6) controls **all future time**, it in particular controls the current unit-capacity block. Combining with (3.7):

\[
\boxed{
\text{asymptotically all capacity in a unit-capacity block is generated by}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\text{sites that were not on the boundary when the block began.}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is a stronger renewal statement than the preceding report's turnover lower bound: the entire inherited boundary, not merely a fixed small set of tips, becomes capacity-negligible.

S_n:=K_{n+1}\setminus K_n=\{y_1,\ldots,y_k\},

be the newly exposed sites, and put

w_i:=\mu_{n+1}(y_i).

\tag{6.1}

Therefore the newly created squared harmonic-measure energy
\[
E_{n+1}^{\rm birth}:=\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2=\|w\|_2^2

# 7. Attachment-generation forest

Fix a finite stopping time \(T\). Define generations of boundary sites recursively.

- Every site of \(\partial A_T\) has generation \(0\).
- If a generation-\(r\) boundary site \(x\) is selected at some future step and creates newly exposed sites \(S_n\), every site of \(S_n\) is assigned generation \(r+1\).

Each newly exposed lattice site has a unique birth time; assigning the selected site as its parent gives a well-defined forest rooted at the boundary present at \(T\).

For a site \(y\) born after \(T\), write
\[
b(y):=\text{its harmonic measure at its birth time}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
For generation \(r\ge1\), define its total squared birth energy
\[
B_r(T):=\sum_{y:\,\mathrm{gen}(y)=r}b(y)^2,
\tag{7.2}
\]
and set
\[
B_0(T):=\sum_{y\in\partial A_T}p_T(y)^2.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Define
\[
D_r(T):=
\sum_{n\ge T}
\Delta_{n+1}
\mathbf1_{\{\mathrm{gen}(X_{n+1})=r\}},
\tag{7.4}
\]
the total actual future capacity generated by selecting generation-\(r\) sites.

### Lemma 7.1 — pathwise offspring-energy inequality

For every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
B_{r+1}(T)\le D_r(T).
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

### Proof

For each selected generation-\(r\) parent, (6.3) says that the sum of squared birth masses of all children exposed by that selection is at most that parent's actual capacity increment. Summing over all selected generation-\(r\) parents proves (7.5). \(\square\)

### Lemma 7.2 — lifetime bound for a newborn generation

For every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le16\,\mathbb E[B_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T].
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

For \(r=0\), this is exactly Theorem 5.1 before applying Beurling. For \(r\ge1\), condition at each random birth time of every generation-\(r\) site and apply Lemma 4.1 with \(p_T(y)\) replaced by its birth mass \(b(y)\). Tonelli's theorem allows summation over the random set of births. \(\square\)

Combining (7.5) and (7.6) gives the recursion
\[
\mathbb E[D_{r+1}(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le16\mathbb E[D_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T].
\tag{7.7}
\]
The base estimate is
\[
\mathbb E[D_0(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le16B_0(T)
\le16p_T^*
\le C R_T^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.8}
\]
Therefore:

## Theorem 7.3 — generation-capacity bound — PROVED

For every integer \(r\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le C16^r R_T^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]
More generally, for
\[
D_{\le L}(T):=\sum_{r=0}^{L}D_r(T),
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_{\le L}(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]
\le C16^L R_T^{-1/2}.
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

The constants are deliberately not optimized. The important point is that every additional genealogical generation costs only a fixed factor while the initial inherited-boundary energy starts at the Beurling-small scale \(R_T^{-1/2}\).

# 8. Logarithmic genealogical depth of capacity production

Apply Theorem 7.3 at \(T=\sigma_j\). Fix
\[
0<\kappa<\frac{1}{2\log16}
\tag{8.1}
\]
and define
\[
L_j:=\lfloor\kappa\log R_j\rfloor.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Then
\[
16^{L_j}
\le
R_j^{\kappa\log16},
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E[D_{\le L_j}(\sigma_j)\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}]
\le
C R_j^{-\theta},
\qquad
\theta:=\frac12-\kappa\log16>0.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Choose any
\[
0<\eta<\theta.
\]
Markov gives
\[
\mathbb P\left(
D_{\le L_j}(\sigma_j)>R_j^{-(\theta-\eta)}
\middle|\mathcal F_{\sigma_j}
\right)
\le C R_j^{-\eta}.
\tag{8.4}
\]
The right side is summable by (3.8). Therefore, almost surely eventually,
\[
\boxed{
D_{\le L_j}(\sigma_j)
\le
R_j^{-(\theta-\eta)}.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Since this controls the first \(L_j\) generations over **all future time**, it certainly controls their contribution inside the current unit-capacity block. By (3.7), that block has actual capacity increment \(1+o(1)\). Hence:

## Corollary 8.1 — logarithmic genealogical renewal — PROVED

For every
\[
\kappa<\frac{1}{2\log16},
\]
almost surely for all sufficiently large \(j\),
\[
\boxed{
\text{capacity generated inside }B_j\text{ by generations }
0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\kappa\log R_j\rfloor
=o(1).
}
\tag{8.6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
1-o(1)\text{ of the block's capacity is generated by sites of attachment-genealogical depth}
>\kappa\log R_j
}
\tag{8.7}
\]
relative to the boundary existing at the block start.

This is a genuine many-generation renewal theorem. It is stronger than merely saying the original boundary disappears from relevance: a logarithmically thick attachment genealogy is also negligible for the capacity budget.

Geometrically, every parent-child edge joins nearest-neighbour lattice sites, so a genealogy is an actual nearest-neighbour chain in the growth history. The conclusion is about genealogical depth, not Euclidean distance: a deep chain may fold or remain in a narrow spatial region.

# 9. Complementary lifetime bound in particle time

The same monotonicity also gives a direct age-versus-harmonic-measure estimate.

For a boundary site \(y\), let \(b(y)\) be its birth time and define its particle-age at time \(n\) by
\[
\mathrm{age}_n(y):=n-b(y),
\]
while it remains unselected.

Fix a radial epoch
\[
\tau_R:=\inf\{n:R_n\ge R\},
\qquad
\tau_{2R}:=\inf\{n:R_n\ge2R\}.
\]
Before \(\tau_{2R}\), all attached particles lie in a deterministic ball of radius \(2R+O(1)\), so there are at most \(CR^2\) attachment times and at most \(CR^2\) boundary-site births (up to another universal constant from the at-most-three newly exposed sites per attachment).

Fix a threshold \(u\in(0,1)\). Conditional on the birth history of a site, if it survives \(L\) subsequent particle steps while still satisfying \(p\ge u\), then at every one of those steps its conditional non-selection probability is at most \(1-u\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{\text{a given born site survives }L\text{ steps with }p\ge u\}
\le(1-u)^L\le e^{-uL}.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Use dyadic thresholds down to order \((\log R)/R^2\). There are only \(O(\log R)\) relevant thresholds. Taking
\[
L(u)=A u^{-1}\log R
\tag{9.2}
\]
with a sufficiently large universal \(A\), a union bound over all \(O(R^2)\) births and all thresholds gives a summable failure probability along dyadic \(R\). Borel–Cantelli therefore yields:

## Theorem 9.1 — harmonic-measure lifetime envelope — PROVED

Almost surely, for all sufficiently large dyadic \(R\), uniformly for all times
\[
n\in[\tau_R,\tau_{2R})
\]
and all active boundary sites \(y\),
\[
\boxed{
 p_n(y)
\le
C\min\left\{
R^{-1/2},
\frac{\log R}{1+\mathrm{age}_n(y)}
\right\}.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]
The first term is Beurling; the second is the new survival-hazard bound.

For any \(L\ge1\), at a time in this epoch,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y:\,\mathrm{age}_n(y)\ge L}p_n(y)^3
&\le
\left(\max_{\mathrm{age}\ge L}p_n(y)\right)^2
\sum_y p_n(y)\\
&\le
\boxed{C\frac{(\log R)^2}{L^2}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Inside a unit-capacity block at radius scale \(R_j\), capacity-radius comparison keeps the radius within a fixed multiplicative factor, and the block contains at most \(CR_j^2\) particle additions deterministically. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}
\sum_{y:\,\mathrm{age}_n(y)\ge L}p_n(y)^3
\le
C\frac{R_j^2(\log R_j)^2}{L^2}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]
Since total cubic mass in a unit-capacity block is \(\asymp1\), taking
\[
L=R_j(\log R_j)^{1+\varepsilon}
\]
gives
\[
\boxed{
\text{capacity-Palm mass produced by sites older than }
R_j(\log R_j)^{1+\varepsilon}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Thus capacity-producing boundary sites are also young in particle time, at least on the \(R\,\mathrm{polylog}(R)\) scale.

# 10. Why the exponent is still not closed

The preceding dynamic-turnover countermodel already warned that repeated renewal of labels can coexist with every candidate block dimension \(D\in[3/2,2]\). The new theorems go further for real DLA, but they still do not eliminate the essential obstruction.

The key distinction is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{old boundary sites become irrelevant, but old cluster geometry need not.}
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

A newly exposed site may be very young and very deep in attachment genealogy while its harmonic measure is still determined by screening from a large, old, global cluster. The inherited geometry can therefore transmit memory without transmitting inherited boundary labels.

Likewise, the generation recursion contains the factor \(16\), inherited from the sharp uniform one-site response upper bound. It proves logarithmic depth, but not a contraction of the distribution of birth masses across generations.

The next genuinely useful theorem should control the **offspring harmonic-measure law**, not merely the lifespan of parent labels. Natural targets are:

1. a planar contraction theorem for the normalized child-mass vector
   \[
   \frac{(\mu_{n+1}(y))_{y\in S_n}}{\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)}
   \]
   after many attachment generations;
2. a multi-arm / shielding estimate showing that a lineage cannot repeatedly regenerate near-critical harmonic measure without paying a strictly super-quadratic geometric cost;
3. an annular renormalization theorem proving that the distribution of the cubic drift \(S_3(n)\) becomes asymptotically independent of geometry more than \(O(1)\) capacity units behind the active genealogical front.

Any one of these, if strong enough to force a deterministic limiting third-moment exponent, would plug directly into the archived Rényi-3/capacity-clock reduction and yield the radial exponent.

A potentially useful synthesis is now visible: the earlier genealogy theorem proves exponential forgetting in **capacity time** for the auxiliary harmonic-measure lineage, while the present theorem proves repeated renewal in the **actual attachment genealogy**. The missing bridge is to show that the distribution of newborn harmonic mass itself contracts under this repeated planar renewal.

  \[
  \mathbb E[G_T^{\rm old}\mid\mathcal F_T]\le CR_T^{-1/2};
  \]
- the same \(R_T^{-1/2}\) bound for inherited predictable capacity mass;
- almost-sure polynomial negligibility of the block-start boundary at intrinsic capacity times;
- newborn squared-energy comparison
  \[
  \sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2\le\Delta_{n+1}\le4\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2;
  \]
- attachment-generation forest and generation recursion
  \[
  \mathbb E[D_r(T)\mid\mathcal F_T]\le C16^rR_T^{-1/2};
  \]
- logarithmic genealogical renewal: the first \(\kappa\log R\) generations produce \(o(1)\) of a unit-capacity block for every \(\kappa<1/(2\log16)\);
- uniform harmonic-measure lifetime envelope
  \[
  p\lesssim\min\{R^{-1/2},(\log R)/(1+\mathrm{age})\};
  \]
- negligible capacity-Palm production from sites older than \(R(\log R)^{1+\varepsilon}\) particle steps.

### Conditional

- contraction/self-averaging of the newborn harmonic-measure law would combine with the archived third-moment/Rényi reductions to establish a deterministic growth exponent;
- a multi-arm estimate making repeated high-mass offspring generation strictly more expensive than the current quadratic budget would break the abstract renewal countermodel.

### Still open

- existence, determinism and exact value of \(\beta\);
- any strict rigorous radial improvement beyond the Kesten window by the present route;
- contraction of offspring harmonic-measure distributions across attachment generations;
- a planar multi-arm/shielding estimate preventing persistent regeneration of high-impact tips;
- self-averaging of particle count per unit capacity.

## 12. Checks and limitations

The new arguments were explicitly checked for:

- use of coordinatewise monotonicity only while an inherited site remains unselected;
- the identity between attachment hazard and conditional harmonic measure;
- the fact that each lattice site can be attached at most once;
- Tonelli/tower interchange over random birth sets;
- the direction of the Green-matrix inequality \(I\preceq H\preceq4I\), giving newborn squared energy \(\le\) parent capacity;
- the generation indexing and unique birth/parent assignment;
- the summability of Markov failures at capacity times via \(R_j\ge ce^{\pi j/2}\);
- the deterministic \(O(R^2)\) number of attachments/births before the first radius-\(2R\) crossing used in the lifetime union bound;
- the distinction between genealogical depth and Euclidean distance.

These are self-checks, not independent referee review or formal verification. Novelty is unassessed. No numerical experiment is represented as a proof.
