# 6. Action-conditioned annular branch counting

The action makes the combinatorics especially clean.

### Theorem 6.1 — PROVED exact action counting inequality

There is a universal \(C<\infty\) such that, conditional on \(\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\), for every \(T\ge R\) and \(B>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P\{T_R\le T,\ \mathfrak B_R^*\ge B\mid\mathcal F_{\tau_R}\}
\le
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
\left(\frac{4eT}{B}\right)^m.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

Suppose \(T_R\le T\). The first particle reaching radius \(2R\) has a crossing genealogy of some length
\[
R-C_0\le m\le T.
\]
The number of possible roots is at most \(CR^2\). For a fixed root and length \(m\), there are at most \(4^m\) nearest-neighbour spatial paths. The root time and \(m\) descendant-selection times can be overcounted by
\[
T\binom{T}{m}.
\]

On the event \(\mathfrak B_R^*\ge B\), every realized crossing branch satisfies
\[
\frac{m^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\Theta(P)}}\ge B,
\]
so
\[
\Theta(P)
\le
\frac{m^3}{B^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
For a fixed path/time tuple, (3.11) therefore gives
\[
\left(\frac{\Theta(P)}m\right)^{m/2}
\le
\left(\frac{m^2}{B^2}\right)^{m/2}
=
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Unioning over roots, paths, time choices, and lengths yields
\[
CR^2T
\sum_{m\ge R-C_0}^{T}
4^m\binom{T}{m}
\left(\frac mB\right)^m.
\]
Using
\[
\binom{T}{m}
\le
\left(\frac{eT}{m}\right)^m
\]
cancels the factor \(m^m\) exactly and gives (6.1). QED.

### Why this formulation is useful

The predecessor counting inequality had the factor
\[
\frac{T\sqrt\Theta}{m^{3/2}}.
\]
The action \(\mathfrak B=m^{3/2}/\sqrt\Theta\) is precisely its reciprocal obstruction. No separate choice of a free-energy threshold and a minimum branch length is needed.

3\log\frac{m(P_R)}R
-
\log E(P_R)
+
D(\nu_m\|q)
=o(\log R).
\tag{11.1}
\]
Every term is asymptotically nonnegative up to \(O(1)\) constants because \(m\ge cR\), \(E\le Q_0\), and \(D\ge0\). Thus a truly Kesten-saturating ancestry must simultaneously have
\[
m(P_R)=R^{1+o(1)},
\tag{11.2}
\]
\[
E(P_R)=R^{-o(1)},
\tag{11.3}
\]
and
\[
D(\nu_m\|q)=o(\log R).
\tag{11.4}
\]

This is a stronger rigidity statement than in the predecessor: the bad branch must be not only energy-rich and generation-homogeneous, but also have only subpolynomial genealogical detour beyond the Euclidean annulus width.

The operational capacity-Palm burst checkpoint (`2026-09-16T09-26-06Z__report.md`, creation commit `5bffaf4118943d13ac810522ee5782ec22e79729`) is relevant because high-birth-energy generations are precisely those preferentially visible under cubic/capacity bias. A plausible route to a positive action excess is now:

1. show that near-critical Palm-visible generations cannot regenerate at positive density along an almost-geodesic forward ancestry without creating additional planar shielding;
2. convert the shielding either into reduced total branch energy \(E(P)\), increased KL heterogeneity \(D(\nu_m\|q)\), or forced genealogical detours \(m/R\);
3. feed any resulting polynomial contribution directly into Theorem 10.1.

This formulation is robust to where the gain appears. A multi-arm theorem need not be pre-classified as an energy theorem, an entropy theorem, or a tortuosity theorem: any of the three increases the same branch action.

# 12. Attempted stronger step and current obstruction

A natural next attempt is to prove directly that repeated generations with birth mass near the Beurling ceiling \(R^{-1/2}\) force a positive action excess.

The present archive already implies:

- every branch has total squared birth energy \(O(1)\);
- almost all inherited boundary capacity disappears;
- direct same-lineage runs longer than two edges eventually disappear;
- ordinary attachment locations exhibit strong spatial refractory behavior;
- operational capacity-Palm burst markers exhibit an \(R^{-3/2}\) local hazard and almost-macroscopic post-burst relocation.

None of these alone gives a lower bound on the action of **every** crossing branch. A branch generation can be born with harmonic mass near \(R^{-1/2}\), survive while growth occurs far away, and later be selected after the global geometry has changed. The current monotonicity theorem only gives the upper relation
\[
p_{\rm selection}(v)\le b(v),
\]
not a quantitative lower loss from birth to selection.

Thus the missing estimate is now a genuinely history-sensitive one. A useful target is a repeated-regeneration inequality of schematic form
\[
\mathbb P\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{one forward ancestry crosses the annulus,}\
\text{has }m\le R^{1+\epsilon},\text{ and maintains}\
E(P)\ge R^{-\epsilon},\ D(\nu_m\|q)\le\epsilon\log R
\end{array}
\right\}
\le
\exp(-R^c)
\tag{12.1}
\]
for some fixed \(c>0\) and sufficiently small \(\epsilon\).

Any theorem of this kind would imply a positive lower excess for \(\mathfrak B_R^*\) and therefore improve the radial exponent immediately.

The geometric mechanism most plausibly capable of producing (12.1) is repeated two-sided Beurling / multi-arm shielding along the same ancestry. The birth-energy/KL/action identities show exactly how any such penalty would be consumed by the exponent calculation.

No such estimate is proved in this run, and claiming an improved radial exponent would therefore be incorrect.

# 14. Checks actually performed

The following points were explicitly rechecked in this run:

1. **Action algebra.** From \(\Theta=Ee^{-D}\),
   \[
   \mathfrak B=m^{3/2}E^{-1/2}e^{D/2}.
   \]
2. **Counting cancellation.** On \(\mathfrak B\ge B\),
   \[
   \Theta\le m^3/B^2,
   \]
   so the fixed-branch probability is at most \((m/B)^m\); multiplying by \(\binom Tm\le(eT/m)^m\) cancels \(m^m\) exactly.
3. **No independence assumption.** The fixed-branch bound is inherited from coordinatewise harmonic-measure monotonicity and sequential conditioning.
4. **Lower action baseline.** \(m\ge R-C_0\) and \(\Theta\le Q_0\) give \(\mathfrak B\gtrsim R^{3/2}\).
5. **Area converse.** Taking \(T=CR^2\), \(B=R^{2+\delta}\) makes the counting ratio \(O(R^{-\delta})\), summable after the exponential-in-\(R\) branch-length factor.
6. **Efficient-branch exponent.** \(\mathfrak B\le R^{2+\delta}\) implies \(m^3/\Theta\le R^{4+2\delta}\); using \(\Theta\le Q_0\) gives \(m\le R^{4/3+2\delta/3+o(1)}\).
7. **Penalty coefficients.** If \(m=R^{1+\delta+o(1)}\), \(E\le R^{-\gamma_E+o(1)}\), and \(D\ge(\gamma_D-o(1))\log R\), then
   \[
   \mathfrak B\ge R^{(3+3\delta+\gamma_E+\gamma_D)/2-o(1)}.
   \]
8. **Endpoint direction.** The area counting inequality gives \(R_n\gtrsim n^{1/2}\), so an action upper radial exponent \(\le1/2\) really closes the limit at \(1/2\).
9. **Model scope.** No internal DLA, Eden, off-lattice Hastings–Levitov process, numerical fractal dimension, or Hausdorff dimension of the countable aggregate is substituted for the requested exponent.

These are self-checks only; no independent referee, formal proof assistant, or external certification was used.

# 15. Next attack

The highest-value next theorem is now sharply formulated.

Prove that there exists \(\delta_0>0\) such that every sufficiently large annulus-crossing actual-DLA genealogy satisfies
\[
\boxed{
3\log\frac{m(P)}R
-
\log E(P)
+
D(\nu_m\|q)
\ge
2\delta_0\log R-o(\log R).
}
\tag{15.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathfrak B_R^*
\ge
R^{3/2+\delta_0-o(1)}.
\]
Theorem 7.1 would then give immediately
\[
\limsup_n\frac{\log R_n}{\log n}
\le
\frac1{3/2+\delta_0}<\frac23.
\]

The most plausible route is to focus on branches for which the first two penalties are small, i.e. almost-geodesic, energy-rich branches, and prove that repeated near-critical birth masses necessarily create logarithmic KL heterogeneity through planar shielding. The operational capacity-Palm burst skeleton provides an observable way to sample the high-cubic generations that should drive this screening mechanism.

If no positive uniform action excess can be established, the next fallback is to construct a true **harmonic-measure-realizable** counterexample geometry saturating \(\mathfrak B\asymp R^{3/2}\); such a construction would rigorously show that even the new combined branch mechanism cannot improve Kesten without an additional dynamical input.
