# 4. Exact squared-energy balance

Define the instantaneous squared harmonic-measure energy
\[
\mathcal E_n:=\sum_{z\in K_n}\mu_n(z)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Because harmonic measure is supported on the accessible exterior boundary, this is just the \(\ell^2\)-energy of the current growth measure.

For the step \(n\to n+1\), define the newborn energy
\[
\mathcal B_n:=\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2=\|w\|_2^2,
\tag{4.2}
\]
and the old-site energy loss
\[
\mathcal L_n
:=
\sum_{z\in K_n}
\bigl(\mu_n(z)^2-\mu_{n+1}(z)^2\bigr).
\tag{4.3}
\]
By (3.3), every term in \(\mathcal L_n\) is nonnegative.

### Lemma 4.1 — the attached site has zero new harmonic measure

For \(x=X_{n+1}\),
\[
\mu_{n+1}(x)=0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof

After attachment, \(x\in A_{n+1}\). Every nearest neighbor of \(x\) lies in \(K_{n+1}\): it is either occupied, or it is unoccupied and adjacent to the occupied site \(x\), hence belongs to \(\partial A_{n+1}\). A nearest-neighbor path arriving from outside \(K_{n+1}\) must therefore hit one of these neighbors before it can hit \(x\). Thus \(x\) cannot be the first hit of \(K_{n+1}\) from infinity. \(\square\)

Consequently the parent-removal term is contained in the old-site loss:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_n\ge p_n(X_{n+1})^2.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.2 — exact energy balance

For every DLA step,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{n+1}-\mathcal E_n
=
\mathcal B_n-\mathcal L_n.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Proof

Split \(K_{n+1}=K_n\sqcup S_n\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_{n+1}
&=
\sum_{z\in K_n}\mu_{n+1}(z)^2
+
\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2\\
&=
\sum_{z\in K_n}\mu_n(z)^2
-
\mathcal L_n
+
\mathcal B_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is (4.6). \(\square\)

# 5. Capacity is the newborn \(\ell^2\)-energy clock

Equation (3.1) and the spectral bounds on \(H_n\) immediately imply
\[
\|w\|_2^2
\le
w^TH_nw
\le
4\|w\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac14\Delta_{n+1}
\le
\mathcal B_n
\le
\Delta_{n+1}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is stronger than a statement merely of comparable expectations: it is a **pathwise, one-step clock equivalence**.

Summing over an arbitrary interval \([m,N)\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac14(C_N-C_m)
\le
\sum_{n=m}^{N-1}\mathcal B_n
\le
C_N-C_m.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus logarithmic capacity is, up to universal factors, the accumulated squared harmonic measure injected into newly exposed boundary sites.

There is a parallel parent clock. From (3.2),
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{16}\Delta_{n+1}
\le
p_n(X_{n+1})^2
\le
\frac34\Delta_{n+1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
So the three quantities
\[
\Delta_{n+1},
\qquad
p_n(X_{n+1})^2,
\qquad
\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^2
\]
are mutually comparable at every actual attachment.

This is a useful local reformulation of capacity production: a particle spends order \(p^2\) capacity, removes order \(p^2\) parent \(\ell^2\)-energy, and injects order \(p^2\) newborn \(\ell^2\)-energy.

# 6. Unit-capacity blocks have order-one birth and dissipation flux

Inside \(B_j\), (3.6) and (5.1) give
\[
\boxed{
\frac14+o(1)
\le
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal B_n
\le
1+o(1).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

On the other hand,
\[
\mathcal E_n
=
\sum_z\mu_n(z)^2
\le
\left(\max_z\mu_n(z)\right)\sum_z\mu_n(z)
\le
CR_j^{-1/2}
\tag{6.2}
\]
throughout the block, because radius is nondecreasing and at least \(R_j\).

Telescoping (4.6),
\[
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal L_n
=
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal B_n
+
\mathcal E_{\sigma_j}
-
\mathcal E_{\sigma_{j+1}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal L_n
=
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal B_n
+O(R_j^{-1/2}).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\frac14-o(1)
\le
\sum_{n\in B_j}\mathcal L_n
\le
1+o(1).
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

At the same time, the direct parent-removal component satisfies by (5.3)
\[
\boxed{
\frac1{16}+o(1)
\le
\sum_{n\in B_j}p_n(X_{n+1})^2
\le
\frac34+o(1).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

### Interpretation

A unit-capacity block carries order-one squared-energy throughput even though its instantaneous squared-energy inventory is only \(O(R_j^{-1/2})\). Therefore the growth front is not merely replacing labels: in the \(\ell^2\) metric relevant to high-harmonic-measure sites, boundary energy is continuously created and destroyed on a scale much larger than the snapshot inventory.

This complements the previous checkpoint's attachment-generation renewal. The earlier theorem said inherited sites and finitely/logarithmically many attachment generations become capacity-negligible. The present theorem says that the **harmonic-measure energy itself** has order-one birth/dissipation flux per capacity unit.

# 7. Newborn Rényi-energy hierarchy

For \(q\ge2\), define the newborn \(q\)-energy of step \(n\):
\[
\mathcal B_n^{(q)}
:=
\sum_{y\in S_n}\mu_{n+1}(y)^q

\|w\|_q^q.
\tag{7.1}

Because \(|S_n|=k\le3\), norm comparison gives
\[
\|w\|_q^q
\ge
k^{1-q/2}\|w\|_2^q
\ge
3^{1-q/2}\|w\|_2^q,
\tag{7.2}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(1-q/2\le0\) and \(k\le3\). Also
\[
\|w\|_q^q\le\|w\|_2^q.
\tag{7.3}
\]
By (5.1),
\[
\frac14\Delta_{n+1}\le\|w\|_2^2\le\Delta_{n+1}.
\]
Therefore:

### Theorem 7.1 — local Rényi clock equivalence

For every \(q\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
3^{1-q/2}4^{-q/2}(\Delta_{n+1})^{q/2}
\le
\mathcal B_n^{(q)}
\le
(\Delta_{n+1})^{q/2}.

\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus every temporal power moment of actual capacity increments is represented, up to constants depending only on \(q\), by a strictly local observable on the at-most-three newborn boundary sites.

\mathcal J_{q,j}

Then

\mathcal J_{q,j}\asymp_q

\tag{7.6}

This gives a new local observable for temporal intermittency of capacity production.

# 8. Deterministic particle-count lower bound from newborn flux

# 9. Effective number of capacity-producing particles

Normalize the capacity increments in a unit block:
\[
v_{n,j}
:=
\frac{\Delta_{n+1}}
{C_{\sigma_{j+1}}-C_{\sigma_j}},
\qquad n\in B_j.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Then \(\sum v_{n,j}=1\). For \(s>1\), define the temporal Rényi effective count
\[
N_{\mathrm{eff},s}(j)
:=
\left(
\sum_{n\in B_j}v_{n,j}^s
\right)^{-1/(s-1)}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Always
\[
1\le N_{\mathrm{eff},s}(j)\le N_j.
\]
Taking \(q=2s\) in (7.6),
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{eff},s}(j)
\asymp_{s}
\mathcal J_{2s,j}^{-1/(s-1)},
}
\tag{9.3}
\]
up to the harmless unit-block normalization \(1+o(1)\).

Thus the effective number of capacity-producing particles is measurable, to universal multiplicative accuracy, from the newborn harmonic-measure field alone.

\[
N_{\mathrm{eff},2}(j)
=
\left(\sum v_{n,j}^2\right)^{-1}
\asymp
\mathcal J_{4,j}^{-1}.

The unresolved gap between \(N_j\) and \(N_{\mathrm{eff},s}(j)\) is exactly a **temporal intermittency gap**: a block may contain many particles that contribute negligible capacity while a much smaller subset carries most of the capacity clock.

# 10. Why this does not determine \(\beta\)

The new identities are strong local structure, but they do not close the exponent.

The standard Kesten obstruction remains visible. If \(N_j\asymp R_j^D\), \(D\in[3/2,2]\), and capacity increments were approximately regular, then
\[
\Delta\sim N_j^{-1}\sim R_j^{-D}
\]
and (7.4) would predict
\[
\mathcal J_{q,j}
\sim
N_j^{1-q/2}
=
R_j^{-D(q-2)/2}.
\tag{10.1}
\]
However, the rigorous upper bound (8.3) only forces exponent \((q-2)/2\), corresponding to \(D\ge1\). It does not even recover Kesten's \(D\ge3/2\), because Kesten's extra information is spatial: reaching a new radius requires an arm of length order \(R\).

The previously archived sharpness constructions also allow strong temporal intermittency. Therefore a proof based only on the one-step response, Beurling maximum, and the newborn energy clocks cannot select \(D\).

The genuinely new value of (7.4) is that it identifies a local renormalization observable whose scaling would close a large part of the problem:

> **Newborn-flux target.** Prove that for some \(q>2\), the logarithmic exponent
> \[
> \zeta_q
> :=
> -\lim_{j\to\infty}
> \frac{\log\mathcal J_{q,j}}{\log R_j}
> \]
> exists almost surely and is deterministic, together with a quantitative anti-intermittency theorem forcing
> \[
> \log N_j
> =
> \frac{2\zeta_q}{q-2}\log R_j+o(\log R_j).
> \]
> Then the block particle dimension would be deterministic and the archived capacity-block reduction would give
> \[
> \beta=
> \frac{q-2}{2\zeta_q}.
> \]

The second clause is essential: existence of the Rényi flux exponent alone only controls the effective capacity-support size, not all physical particles.
