## 4. P02-C001 — dependence-preserving weighted-cover transfer

This proposition is rederived here because it is the exact finite-to-infinite bridge P02 needs. It is known in substance from the R17 C027 lineage and the Dančík--Paterson--Lueker covering method; the value here is to isolate the assumptions and make explicit why no independent-match approximation enters.

### Proposition P02-LCS-C001

Let \(\Sigma\) have size \(k\), and let \(\mathcal M\) be a covering machine for **actual ordered string pairs** in the following sense. An accepted list of \(m\) factors, followed by a null pair, generates every pair \((u,v)\) with \(\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)=m\), possibly with overcounting. Give an actual generated pair the bivariate weight
\[
x^{|u|}y^{|v|},\qquad 0<x,y<1/k.
\]
Let \(f_m(x,y)\) be the total weight of accepted lists of exactly \(m\) factors. Assume that for some \(K<\infty\) and \(0<\lambda<1\),
\[
f_m(x,y)\le K\lambda^m
\tag{4.1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\). Then for independent iid uniform \(\Sigma\)-valued strings,
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_k\le \frac{\log(k^2xy)}{\log\lambda}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

If the accepted-list dynamics is finite-state with nonnegative weighted transition operator \(A_{x,y}\), then (4.1) follows from any positive cone witness \(V>0\) satisfying
\[
A_{x,y}V\le\lambda V
\tag{4.3}
\]
componentwise.

### Proof

Let
\[
g_m(x,y)=
\sum_{(u,v):\operatorname{LCS}(u,v)\ge m}
 x^{|u|}y^{|v|}.
\]
The null-pair generating function is bounded by the generating function of **all** ordered pairs,
\[
N(x,y)\le \sum_{a,b\ge0}k^{a+b}x^ay^b
=\frac1{(1-kx)(1-ky)}<\infty.
\tag{4.4}
\]
By the covering property, every pair with LCS at least \(m\) is generated by some accepted list of length \(r\ge m\), followed by a null pair. Bidegrees add under concatenation. Since all coefficients are nonnegative, any multiple representations can only enlarge the right side, hence
\[
g_m(x,y)
\le N(x,y)\sum_{r\ge m}f_r(x,y)
\le C\lambda^m
\tag{4.5}
\]
for a constant \(C\) independent of \(m\).

Let \(D(n,m)\) be the number of **actual** ordered pairs \((u,v)\in\Sigma^n\times\Sigma^n\) with LCS at least \(m\). Positivity of coefficients gives
\[
D(n,m)x^ny^n\le g_m(x,y).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Because the two input strings are independent and uniform, every ordered pair has probability \(k^{-2n}\), so
\[
\Pr\{L_n\ge m\}
=\frac{D(n,m)}{k^{2n}}
\le C\,(k^2xy)^{-n}\lambda^m.
\tag{4.7}
\]
This identity is where the genuine dependence is preserved: we count underlying string pairs and only at the final step divide by their exact product-law probability. We never assign independent probabilities to the \(n^2\) match indicators.

Put
\[
r=\frac{\log(k^2xy)}{\log\lambda}.
\]
For any \(t>r\), since \(\log\lambda<0\),
\[
\lambda^t<k^2xy.
\]
Taking \(m=\lceil tn\rceil\) in (4.7) gives exponentially decaying upper tail. Since \(0\le L_n/n\le1\),
\[
\frac{\mathbf EL_n}{n}
\le t+\Pr\{L_n\ge tn\}.
\]
Letting \(n\to\infty\) and then \(t\downarrow r\) proves (4.2).

For the finite-state corollary, scale the initial positive vector below a multiple of \(V\). Positivity and (4.3) give by induction
\[
A_{x,y}^m V\le\lambda^mV,
\]
so the total accepted-list weight is at most a constant times \(\lambda^m\). This yields (4.1). QED.

### Consequence for the inherited C045 certificate

The C045 machine generates actual binary-string pairs. Its weights retain the common raw-letter constraints through the canonical covering construction; it is not the independent Bernoulli matching model. Therefore (4.2) applies directly to (3.1), yielding (3.2).

## 5. P02-C002 — a geometric interpolation theorem for exact cone witnesses

The next question was whether two exact cone certificates can be interpolated to create a strictly better intermediate fugacity without building a new huge witness. This is a natural stationary/variational attack because it would convert old exact certificates into a continuum of new ones essentially for free.

### Proposition P02-LCS-C002 (log-convex cone interpolation)

Let \(A_{x,y}\) be a finite nonnegative transition operator
\[
(A_{x,y}V)(s)=\sum_{e:s\to s'}w_e(x,y)V(s'),
\]
where each transition weight is log-convex in the logarithmic coordinates: for
\[
x_\theta=x_0^{1-\theta}x_1^\theta,
\qquad
y_\theta=y_0^{1-\theta}y_1^\theta,
\qquad 0\le\theta\le1,
\]
we have
\[
w_e(x_\theta,y_\theta)
\le w_e(x_0,y_0)^{1-\theta}w_e(x_1,y_1)^\theta.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Suppose positive vectors \(V_0,V_1\) satisfy
\[
A_{x_i,y_i}V_i\le\lambda_iV_i,
\qquad i=0,1.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Define componentwise
\[
V_\theta=V_0^{1-\theta}V_1^\theta,
\qquad
\lambda_\theta=\lambda_0^{1-\theta}\lambda_1^\theta.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
A_{x_\theta,y_\theta}V_\theta
\le\lambda_\theta V_\theta.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

For a fixed state \(s\), write each outgoing transition term at the two endpoints as
\[
a_e=w_e(x_0,y_0)V_0(s'),
\qquad
b_e=w_e(x_1,y_1)V_1(s').
\]
By (5.1), each interpolated term is at most \(a_e^{1-\theta}b_e^\theta\). Hölder's inequality therefore gives
\[
\sum_e a_e^{1-\theta}b_e^\theta
\le
\left(\sum_ea_e\right)^{1-\theta}
\left(\sum_eb_e\right)^\theta.
\]
Using the endpoint cone inequalities (5.2), this is at most
\[
(\lambda_0V_0(s))^{1-\theta}
(\lambda_1V_1(s))^\theta
=\lambda_\theta V_\theta(s).
\]
This is (5.3). QED.

### Applicability to the LCS fugacity weights

The finite-history covering weights are monomials
\[
x^{a+1}y,
\qquad xy^{a+1},
\]
and geometric-tail weights
\[
\frac{x^8y}{1-x},
\qquad
\frac{xy^8}{1-y}.
\]
Monomials have affine logarithm in \((\log x,\log y)\). For the tail,
\[
\log\frac{x^8y}{1-x}=8u+v-\log(1-e^u),
\qquad u=\log x,\ v=\log y,
\]
and
\[
\frac{d^2}{du^2}\{-\log(1-e^u)\}
=\frac{e^u}{(1-e^u)^2}>0
\]
for \(u<0\). Thus the tail weights are also log-convex, and C002 applies to these operators.

The theorem is a finite-dimensional Collatz--Wielandt/Kingman-type fact and is very likely folklore in positive-operator theory; no publication novelty is claimed.

## 6. P02-D001 — why geometric interpolation cannot itself beat both endpoint LCS bounds

C002 supplies infinitely many exact cone certificates from two endpoint certificates, but does it improve the transferred LCS bound? The answer is no, for a simple structural reason.

At the interpolated parameters,
\[
\log(4x_\theta y_\theta)
=(1-\theta)\log(4x_0y_0)
+\theta\log(4x_1y_1),
\]
and
\[
\log\lambda_\theta
=(1-\theta)\log\lambda_0+	heta\log\lambda_1.
\]
Hence the transferred endpoint is
\[
B(\theta)
=
\frac{(1-\theta)a_0+\theta a_1}
{(1-\theta)b_0+\theta b_1},
\qquad
a_i,b_i<0.
\tag{6.1}
\]
This is a fractional-linear function. Its derivative has constant sign wherever the denominator is nonzero:
\[
B'(\theta)=
\frac{a_1b_0-a_0b_1}
{((1-\theta)b_0+\theta b_1)^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
B(\theta)\ge\min\{B(0),B(1)\}
\quad(0\le\theta\le1).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
So geometric interpolation of already-certified cones is useful for continuity and for transporting witnesses, but it can never create an interior bound stronger than the better endpoint. This kills the cheapest possible stationary/variational reuse of C027 and C045. A genuine improvement needs either a new witness at a new parameter, a nonlinear comparison sharper than Hölder, or a different operator/cover.

This is a mechanism-level advance/no-go, not a source-level deterioration. It prevents future P02 runs from spending time on a continuum interpolation that cannot improve the fixed metric.
