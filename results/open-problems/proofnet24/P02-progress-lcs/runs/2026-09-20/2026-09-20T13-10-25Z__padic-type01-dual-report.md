## 8. Dependence-preserving finite-to-infinite transfer

This step concerns the original two-source-string LCS problem, not the independent Bernoulli matching model.

The C045 covering machine enumerates accepted collations of actual ordered source pairs `(X,Y)`. At fugacities `(x,y)`, if the finite covering operator `A(x,y)` has spectral radius below `z<1`, finite dimensionality implies its accepted weight at collation length `m` is at most `K_z z^m` for some finite `K_z`. Coefficient positivity and the covering property imply, for actual iid uniform binary strings of common length `n`,
\[
\Pr\{L_n\ge m\}
\le K_z(4xy)^{-n}z^m.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The factor `4^{-n}` is the probability of each actual ordered length-`n` binary source pair. No independent probabilities are assigned to the `n^2` match indicators.

For any
\[
c>\frac{\log(4xy)}{\log z},
\]
the right side of (8.1) at `m=floor(cn)` decays exponentially. Since `0<=L_n<=n`, either summing the upper tail or splitting the expectation at `cn` gives
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf E L_n}{n}\le c.
\]
Letting `c` decrease to the threshold yields
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_2\le\frac{\log(4xy)}{\log z}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
This is a dependence-preserving finite-to-infinite transfer.

Take `x=y=t=256717/800000` and `z=lambda_01`. By (7.4),

\boxed{

Because `0<4t^2<1` and `0<lambda_01<lambda_prev<1`, the map `z -> log(4t^2)/log z` is strictly increasing, hence
