## 2. Exact source statement and quantifiers recovered

The original paper defines a forward Brownian motion (FBM) by the existence of witness times tending to `-infinity` from which the forward increments are standard Brownian motion. Problem 7.8 is exactly the one-line question:

> Can one generalize Theorem 4.3 from parabolas to other space-time shapes?

The next sentence in the source is Problem 7.9 on forward Levy processes and is not part of 7.8.

Theorem 4.3 concerns the parabolic space-time region bounded by `c_1 sqrt(|t|)` and `c_2 sqrt(|t|)`, with the principal killed-Ornstein–Uhlenbeck exponent `lambda_0(c_1,c_2)`. It has three logically different clauses:

1. **Universal obstruction:** if `lambda_0(c_1,c_2) >= 1` and `X` is any FBM, then almost surely at least one backward asymptotic boundary is crossed: `limsup_{t->-infinity} X_t/sqrt(|t|) >= c_2` or `liminf <= c_1`.
2. **Decomposable existence:** if `lambda_0(c_1,c_2) <= 1`, there exists a decomposable FBM whose backward limsup is at most `c_2` and liminf at least `c_1`, almost surely.
3. **Pointwise containment:** if `c_1 <= 0 <= c_2` and `lambda_0(c_1,c_2) < 1`, there exists a decomposable FBM satisfying `c_1 sqrt(|t|) <= X_t <= c_2 sqrt(|t|)` for every `t <= 0`, almost surely.

Thus any future C29 admission using the owner's proposed “simultaneously realized conditions” metric would have to freeze exactly which of these three theorem clauses are being generalized to a single new shape family before counting anything.
