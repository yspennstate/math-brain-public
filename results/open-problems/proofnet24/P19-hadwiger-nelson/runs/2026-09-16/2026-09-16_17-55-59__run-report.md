## 4. (a) Exact statement that blocks

The source-level target is the existence of a finite exact unit-distance obstruction to five colours:

\[
\boxed{
\exists\,P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_N\}\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}^{\,2}
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\operatorname{CNF}_5(G_1(P))\ \text{is UNSAT},
\qquad
G_1(P)=\bigl(V,\{\{i,j\}:\|p_i-p_j\|=1\}\bigr).
}
\]

Here “planar unit-distance graph” means a graph realized by points in the Euclidean plane with the relevant edges at exact distance one; it does **not** mean graph-planar in the graph-theoretic sense.

To count as a P19 success, the obstruction must have exact algebraic coordinates, an exact unit-distance reconstruction/checker, and an independently checkable 5-colour UNSAT certificate. A timeout, heuristic lower bound, floating embedding, extra forbidden distance, or non-planar Euclidean realization is not a certificate.

## 5. (b) Routes tried and the precise reason each failed

### Route 1 — replay the 5-chromatic record

The Parts 509 graph is an exact high-quality incumbent for the known lower bound, but it is 5-chromatic, hence it has proper 5-colourings. It cannot itself witness `chi(R^2)>=6`.

### Route 2 — simply rerun the de Grey/Heule/Parts minimization paradigm

That machinery certifies **4-colour UNSAT** for 5-chromatic graphs and then minimizes the witness. The present blocker is **5-colour UNSAT**. No exact planar unit-distance seed with that property is known, so minimization has no target object to shrink.

### Route 3 — force-same relations inside the Parts 509 geometry

The previous finite attacks tested whether selected non-unit terminal pairs were equal in every 5-colouring. The reduced-graph 18-pair search and the original-graph exhaustive squared-distance-3/4 search both failed: for every tested pair an explicit valid 5-colouring exists with the terminals different. Therefore those terminal classes do not provide a forced-same gadget.

This failure does **not** imply that those pairs are not forced-different. That is a different SAT question and has not yet been run exhaustively.

### Route 4 — import a small relation gadget with an extra edge length

Previously inspected small forced-relation constructions such as the `G16` style relation use two edge lengths. They are therefore not themselves admissible Hadwiger-Nelson witnesses. An extra-distance relation may only be used as an intermediate scaffold if every non-unit constraint is replaced by a pure unit-distance gadget with exact geometry and a proved terminal relation.

### Route 5 — use a published two-distance six-chromatic scaffold as if it solved the target

De Neve et al. give an exact 33-vertex 6-chromatic graph whose edges have lengths `1` and `sqrt(3)`. The preceding HARD8-08 exact checkpoint reconstructed it as 81 unit edges plus 32 `sqrt(3)` edges, 113 total. This is not a unit-distance-only graph, so it does not move the Hadwiger-Nelson lower bound by itself.

What it *does* provide is a clean conditional reduction: a pure unit-distance gadget whose terminals are exactly `sqrt(3)` apart and are forced **different** in every 5-colouring could replace each of the 32 `sqrt(3)` constraints. No such gadget has yet been certified.

### Route 6 — Haugland’s 2026 Moser-spindle-free construction

The 2131-vertex graph is still 5-chromatic. It is useful structural evidence but does not supply 5-colour UNSAT.

### Route 7 — fractional chromatic number, abstract criticality, or graph-only obstructions

These can rule out colourings in weaker/different invariants or produce abstract six-chromatic graphs, but without an exact Euclidean unit-distance realization they do not meet the source target.

### Route 8 — floating-point embeddings, three-dimensional unit-distance graphs, or extra-distance variants

These are explicitly outside P19 scope and cannot be promoted to source progress.

## 6. (c) What a proof would have to contain that no route so far contains

A successful direct route must simultaneously provide:

1. a finite set of **exact algebraic coordinates** in `R^2`;
2. an exact arithmetic procedure reconstructing every unit-distance edge used by the certificate;
3. a standard 5-colouring CNF (or transparently equivalent encoding) for that exact graph;
4. a machine-checkable UNSAT certificate such as DRAT/LRAT, independently replayed;
5. a written argument connecting the finite UNSAT result to `chi(R^2)>=6`.

A successful gadget-substitution route needs, in addition:

1. a pure unit-distance gadget with terminals at an exact prescribed non-unit separation `d`;
2. a proof/certificate that in **every** proper 5-colouring of the gadget the terminal relation required by the scaffold holds;
3. an exact finite scaffold that is not 5-colourable when both its unit edges and its `d`-constraints are enforced;
4. exact rigid placements of all gadget copies on the scaffold terminals, followed by a fresh exact reconstruction of the union graph and a final 5-colour UNSAT certificate.

The published `{1,sqrt(3)}` 33-vertex scaffold supplies item 3 for `d=sqrt(3)`. The missing ingredient is item 1+2: a pure unit-distance `sqrt(3)` terminal gadget enforcing inequality in five colours.
