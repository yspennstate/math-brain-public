## 2. Original source and fresh literature/status check

The source target is the ordinary unit-distance graph of the Euclidean plane: determine the minimum number of colours needed so that every pair of points exactly distance one apart receives different colours. A finite pure unit-distance graph with chromatic number at least six would improve the currently certified lower bound from five to six; it would **not** by itself establish the exact plane chromatic number because the classical upper bound remains seven.

Primary/current sources checked on 16 September 2026:

1. Aubrey de Grey, arXiv:1804.02385. The abstract gives finite planar unit-distance graphs not 4-colourable, proving `chi(R^2)>=5`.
2. Marijn Heule, arXiv:1805.12181. Clausal proof minimization yields mechanically checkable 553-vertex 5-chromatic unit-distance graphs.
3. Jaan Parts, arXiv:2010.12665. The published minimization record used here is a 509-vertex, 2442-edge 5-chromatic planar unit-distance graph.
4. Jan Kristian Haugland, arXiv:2608.04542, submitted 5 August 2026. It gives a 2131-vertex **Moser-spindle-free** 5-chromatic unit-distance graph; this is a structural result, not an unrestricted vertex-count improvement and not a 6-chromatic graph.
5. Akos Ducz and Daniel Varga, arXiv:2606.28157, concerns a fractional/independence-ratio strengthening. It is not an ordinary 6-chromatic finite unit-distance witness.

Fresh bounded searches for 2026 Hadwiger-Nelson / unit-distance / six-chromatic developments surfaced the Haugland result and variants but no primary source claiming a pure planar unit-distance graph of chromatic number at least six.
