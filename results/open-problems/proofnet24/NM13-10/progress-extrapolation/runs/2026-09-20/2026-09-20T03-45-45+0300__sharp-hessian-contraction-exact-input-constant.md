## 4. New sharp Hessian contraction

### Lemma 4.1

For every `p>=2`, positive self-adjoint `A`, and vectors `z,y`,

`D^2 Psi_p(z)[A^(1/2)y,A^(1/2)y]`

`<= (p-1)||z||^(p-2)||y||^2`.                          (4.1)

The constant `p-1` is sharp, already on a single eigenspace.

### Proof

Write

`v(s)=e^{-sA}z`, `w(s)=e^{-sA}y`, `r(s)=||v(s)||^2`,

`E(s)=||A^(1/2)w(s)||^2`,

and, when `r(s)>0`,

`m(s)=<v(s),Av(s)>/r(s)`.

By (3.2),

`(1/p) D^2 Psi_p(z)[A^(1/2)y,A^(1/2)y]`

`= integral_0^infinity { r^(c/2) E`

`  + c r^(c/2-1) <v,A^(1/2)w>^2 } ds`.                (4.2)

The mixed scalar in the second term has two simultaneous Cauchy bounds:

`<v,A^(1/2)w>^2 <= r E`,                               (4.3)

and, since `<v,A^(1/2)w>=<A^(1/2)v,w>`,

`<v,A^(1/2)w>^2 <= <v,Av> ||w||^2 = r m ||w||^2`.     (4.4)

For any `theta in [0,1]`, the minimum of the two right-hand sides is no larger than their convex combination. Choose the specific weight

`theta=1/p`.                                            (4.5)

Then

`<v,A^(1/2)w>^2`

`<= r[(1/p)E + (1-1/p)m||w||^2]`.                      (4.6)

Substitution into (4.2) gives the pointwise integrand bound

`r^(c/2)[(1+c/p)E + c(1-1/p)m||w||^2]`.

Since `c=p-2`,

`1+c/p=2(p-1)/p`,

`c(1-1/p)=c(p-1)/p`.                                   (4.7)

Now define

`F(s)=r(s)^(c/2)||w(s)||^2`.                            (4.8)

Using

`r'(s)=-2m(s)r(s)`, `d/ds ||w(s)||^2=-2E(s)`,

we obtain the exact derivative identity

`-F'(s)=r(s)^(c/2)[2E(s)+c m(s)||w(s)||^2]`.            (4.9)

Combining (4.7) and (4.9), the integrand in (4.2) is bounded by

`-(p-1)/p F'(s)`.                                       (4.10)

Because `A>0`, `F(s)->0` as `s->infinity`, while

`F(0)=||z||^c ||y||^2`.

Therefore

`D^2 Psi_p(z)[A^(1/2)y,A^(1/2)y]`

`<= p * (p-1)/p * [F(0)-F(infinity)]`

`= (p-1)||z||^(p-2)||y||^2`,

which proves (4.1).

If `z` and `y` lie in one common eigenspace and are parallel, both Cauchy bounds coincide and all inequalities above are equalities; hence `p-1` is sharp. QED.

### Corollary 4.2 — sharp mixed Hessian coefficient

The Hessian is positive semidefinite, so Cauchy–Schwarz in the Hessian metric and Lemma 4.1 give

`|D^2 Psi_p(z)[A^(1/2)z,A^(1/2)y]|`

`<= (p-1)||z||^(p-1)||y||`.                             (4.11)

This sharpens the previous run's `kappa_p` coefficient, and Lemma 4.1 itself sharpens P24's pure-input coefficient from `p(p-1)/2` to `p-1`.

where

`delta_(p,beta)=1-beta^2(p-1)/2`.                       (5.2)

For `delta>0` and `I,J>0`, set

`R=(I/J)^(1/p)`.

`delta R^2 <= |beta|(p-1)R + (p-1)/2`.                 (5.3)

Write `s=p-1`. The positive root is

`R <= [|beta|s + sqrt(beta^2 s^2 + 2 delta s)]/(2 delta)`.

`beta^2 s^2 + 2 delta s = 2s`.                          (5.4)
