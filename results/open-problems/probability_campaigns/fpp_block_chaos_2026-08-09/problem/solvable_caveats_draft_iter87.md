(C-a) MEAN VERSUS LAW. What the half-space literature proves is distributional: in the subcritical
boundary phase, (H(N,N) - 4N)/N^{1/3} converges to a boundary-dependent Tracy-Widom-type law (GSE
at generic subcritical parameters, a GOE-squared-type law at the critical point). A limit law at
scale N^{1/3} bounds the MEAN deficit at that scale only together with uniform integrability. The
solvable literature does contain the tail estimates that give moment convergence, so the
conclusion stands - but the honest citation is to moment convergence, not to the limit law alone,
and the phrase "could not be missed by the exact formulas" should be read with that qualifier.

(C-b) PHASE IDENTIFICATION. FPP's hard wall carries no boundary parameter, so identifying it with
the SUBCRITICAL phase of the solvable models is an argument, not a computation. The argument: the
wall only REMOVES paths; nothing in the FPP geometry rewards hugging it, so there is no analogue
of the supercritical reinforcement that pins the LPP polymer to the diagonal, changes the LLN
constant, and produces N^{1/2} Gaussian fluctuations. That is a good argument and we believe it,
entire force rests on excluding pinning by analogy. Said plainly: if FPP's wall somehow acted as
a pinning boundary, the mean deficit would be at wandering scale and the corner route's condition
would fail; nothing in the solvable world rules that out for FPP - only the removal-not-reward
structure of the wall does.

(C-c) SIGN. In LPP the wall DECREASES the maximum (the deficit is negative); in FPP it INCREASES
"the boundary's mean effect sits at fluctuation scale in the exactly solvable cousin" would need
to confront the sign flip head-on.
