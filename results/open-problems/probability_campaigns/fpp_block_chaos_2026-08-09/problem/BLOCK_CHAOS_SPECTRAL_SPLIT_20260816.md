## Exact identification

Let `I` be the finite set of blocks meeting the deterministic localization
box in MG2B. Write `w=(w_i)_(i in I)` for the independent block variables,
and for a block label `L` put

`f_L(w)=1{the fixed canonical geodesic uses L},  p_L=E f_L.`

The block laws may differ and may have atoms. Let

`f_L=sum_(S subseteq I) f_(L,S)`

be the Hoeffding--ANOVA decomposition on the product space: the components are
orthogonal, `f_(L,empty)=p_L`, and each nonempty component has conditional mean
zero in every coordinate that it uses.

Set `t=1-kappa`. In MG2B, the random set

`R_t={i: tau_i<=t}`

has independent Bernoulli-`t` membership. The sigma-field `F_t` reveals the
original and reservoir weights on `R_t` and reveals no weight on its
complement. Since `f_L` depends only on the original weights and the reservoirs
are independent,

`E[f_L|F_t]=E[f_L|w_(R_t)]=sum_(S subseteq R_t) f_(L,S).`       (1)

Orthogonality and `P(S subseteq R_t)=t^|S|` therefore give the exact identity

`E[(E[f_L|F_t])^2]
 =p_L^2+sum_(empty != S subseteq I) t^|S| ||f_(L,S)||_2^2.`     (2)

Summing over labels,

`Pbar(kappa)
 =sum_L p_L^2
  +sum_L sum_(S != empty) (1-kappa)^|S| ||f_(L,S)||_2^2.`       (3)

This is not an analogy with noise sensitivity. It is the exact resampling-noise
operator generated by the filtration used in the variance engine.

There is an equivalent coupling proof. Conditional on `F_t`, the original
field and `omega_kappa` share the weights in `R_t` and use independent original
and reservoir weights outside `R_t`; they are conditionally iid. Thus the left
side of (2) is `E[f_L(omega)f_L(omega_kappa)]`. A component supported on `S`
survives in both fields exactly when all coordinates of `S` are shared, which
has probability `t^|S|`.

The endpoints check the normalization:

`Pbar(1)=sum_L p_L^2` at full resampling, and
`Pbar(0)=sum_L p_L` with no resampling.                         (4)

Here and below the endpoint notation means the evident limit if the engine is
stated only for `kappa in (0,1)`. Equation (3) also shows that `Pbar` is
increasing in the retention parameter `t`; all of its power-series
coefficients are nonnegative.

## A low-degree/high-degree split

For an integer `r>=1`, define

`B_<r=sum_L sum_(1<=|S|<r) ||f_(L,S)||_2^2,
 C_0=sum_L p_L^2.`                                            (5)

Since `f_L` is an indicator,

`sum_(S != empty)||f_(L,S)||_2^2=Var(f_L)=p_L(1-p_L)<=p_L.`    (6)

If the occupation input `(L3)` gives `sum_L p_L<=C_occ M`, then
(3), (5), and (6) yield

`Pbar(kappa)<=C_0+B_<r+(1-kappa)^r C_occ M.`                   (7)

This separates three genuinely different tasks:

1. `C_0` is the static collision floor already present under fully independent
   resampling.
2. `B_<r` is the low-order dependence of route membership on the environment.
3. Degrees at least `r` are damped by `(1-kappa)^r`; their total mass needs no
   further geometric estimate beyond `(L3)`.

The straight-path control from the preceding note has only a degree-zero term,
so (7) cannot mistakenly certify chaos there. A two-route diamond has
`C_0=1/2` across its two route labels even at full resampling. These recover the
two earlier obstructions from the endpoints of the same identity.

## A decision-algorithm route to the low levels

For each `L`, suppose a randomized decision algorithm `A_L` determines `f_L`
by adaptively querying whole block variables. Its private randomness is
independent of the weights, and its next query may depend only on its private
randomness and the values already queried. Define its revealment

`delta_L=max_(i in I) P(A_L queries i).`

The product-space form of the Schramm--Steif level estimate is

`sum_(|S|=k)||f_(L,S)||_2^2 <= k delta_L Var(f_L),  k>=1.`      (8)

For completeness, (8) follows by the same argument as the Boolean-cube
theorem. Apply the algorithm to the centered function and let `g` be its
level-`k` ANOVA projection. The algorithm transcript makes the centered
function measurable. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz reduces the problem to the
squared constant component of `g` after the queried coordinates are fixed.
The exact level-`k` terms left after fixing the query set are precisely those
whose supports avoid that set. Orthogonality, followed by the union bound
`P(S meets the query set)<=sum_(i in S)P(i is queried)<=k delta_L`, gives (8).
This proof uses only product independence and ANOVA orthogonality, so querying
an arbitrary block-valued coordinate does not require discretizing its law.

The original Boolean-cube statement is Theorem 1.8 of Schramm and Steif,
*Quantitative noise sensitivity and exceptional times for percolation*,
arXiv:math/0504586.

Summing (8) over `1<=k<r` gives

`B_<r <= r(r-1)/2 sum_L delta_L Var(f_L).`                     (9)

Put

`Delta_alg=(1/M) sum_L delta_L Var(f_L).`

Then (7) becomes the explicit sufficient bound

`Pbar(kappa)/M
 <= C_0/M + r(r-1) Delta_alg/2 + (1-kappa)^r C_occ.`           (10)

In particular, if every `delta_L<=delta`, then

`Pbar(kappa)/M
 <= C_0/M + delta r(r-1) C_occ/2 + (1-kappa)^r C_occ.`         (11)

This is a one-environment exploration target, not a reformulation of the
two-environment overlap event. The missing construction is an algorithm that
decides membership of the canonical geodesic in `L` while querying any fixed
block with sufficiently small probability.

## Constants needed by the variance engine

The corrected CRUX 9.5 quarter-budget condition is

`C_occ Pbar(kappa)/M <= 1/(16 kappa_4 beta^2).`                 (12)

Equivalently set

`eta_*=1/(16 kappa_4 beta^2 C_occ)`

and require `Pbar(kappa)/M<=eta_*`. One transparent, nonoptimal allocation of
this budget is

`C_0/M <= eta_*/3,`

`(1-kappa)^r C_occ <= eta_*/3,`

`r(r-1) Delta_alg/2 <= eta_*/3.`                              (13)

For a uniform revealment bound and `r>=2`, the last condition follows from

`delta <= 1/(24 kappa_4 beta^2 C_occ^2 r(r-1)).`               (14)

The high-degree condition in (13) follows, for example, when

`r >= log(48 kappa_4 beta^2 C_occ^2)/[-log(1-kappa)]`,         (15)

with the right side replaced by zero if its numerator is negative. The full
halving step still separately needs `(L1')`--`(L3)` and the first-moment budget
`kappa<=1/(4 beta C_occ)`.

Equations (13)--(15) expose the remaining tradeoff. The engine wants `kappa`
small, so the spectral cutoff may have to be moderately large; the resulting
`r^2` cost forces correspondingly low revealment. At the same time `C_0` must
already be small, which requires marginal route spread before any shared-noise
analysis begins.

## Concrete next lemma

It is enough to prove, on the relevant block family and uniformly along the
bootstrap path, both:

1. a marginal collision estimate at the first line of (13); and
2. randomized algorithms for `U_L` whose weighted aggregate revealment
   `Delta_alg` meets the third line of (13).

The algorithm may explore passage-time comparisons, dual barriers, or a
randomized transverse seed, but it must determine the event for the already
fixed canonical selector. A localization cover by itself does not provide this:
it limits where the path can be, whereas revealment limits how much of the
environment must be inspected to decide one label. This spectral split gives a
precise way for those two kinds of information to cooperate without treating
the chaos hypothesis as an input to its own proof.
