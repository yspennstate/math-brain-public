# Referee report on I0061

## Does it address the problem?

No. The record reads "lim n 1 nd log pu n(ϵ) = Iu(ϵ)". This is a garbled extraction of the survey (Auffinger–Damron–Hanson, *50 Years of First-Passage Percolation*, Question 18). In the intended statement the rate is almost certainly I_u(ε) = −lim n^{-d} log p_n^u(ε). Rate functions are nonnegative by convention, and the minus sign is the token most likely to be lost in PDF extraction.

The solver refutes the version with a plus sign. That version fails for an elementary structural reason:
- L = lim n^{-d} log p is ≤ 0.
- L(0+) = 0 with L'(0+) = 0, by a quadratic lower bound.
- A convex function lies above its tangent at 0, so L ≥ 0, and therefore L ≡ 0.
- This contradicts the volume-order decay L < 0, which is Kesten's theorem itself.

Disproving the sign-flipped statement amounts to observing that a concave I_u cannot vanish to second order at 0 and still be positive. It says nothing about the actual question: whether the limit exists and whether −lim is convex. The solver says this openly ("not a certified refutation of the survey's intended question"; "general existence and convexity are not settled"). It is still not an answer.

## Line-by-line check of the mathematics

The mathematics is correct as far as it goes.

**L1 (quadratic lower bound)**
- The box truncation is valid.
- The variance bound is valid. It is Efron–Stein with a harmless lost factor of 2.
- Subadditivity holds, and the bound μ ≤ 3/2 holds.
- The Beta(3,3) moments are correct: E(1/Z) = B(2,3)/B(3,3) = 5/2 and E(1/Z²) = B(1,3)/B(3,3) = 10.
- The entropy formula H_s = −5log(1−s) − 2E log(1+h/Z) is right, and so is the bound H_s ≤ 10h² ≤ 40s².
- With s = 4ε, the coupling margin μ + s(2−μ) − (1−s)ε ≥ μ + ε + 4ε² holds because 2 − μ ≥ 1/2.
- The change-of-measure inequality is standard.

**L2 (upper bound)**
- The transverse cost is ≤ 4(d−1)R_n ≤ n/8, so H_y ≥ 13n/8.
- The rows are disjoint, hence independent.
- Hoeffding with θ = 1/2 gives exp(−n/32) per row.
- The exponent 1/[32(16(d−1))^{d−1}] is correct.

**L3 (two-ball forcing)**
- The forcing argument is sound.
- The edge count M_d(r) ~ 2^d r^d/(d−1)! is right.
- The minimization over unequal radii is only within the stated family, as the solver says.

**Small cases**
- I checked n = 3 by hand: the value 961/8192 is right.
- These cases are irrelevant to the claim.

So T1 is a valid theorem about the plus-sign statement. However, that statement is a transcription artifact.

## Literature

- Cranston–Gauthier–Mountford (2009) extended Kesten's Theorem 3.38. The source itself mentions this extension.
- Basu–Ganguly–Sly, "Upper tail large deviations in first passage percolation" (arXiv 1712.01255; Comm. Pure Appl. Math., 2021), proved that the upper-tail rate function lim −n^{-2} log P(T_n ≥ n(μ+ε)) exists on Z² for bounded weights under a mild regularity assumption.
- As far as I recall, they do not establish convexity, and they do not treat general d. The intended question therefore appears only partially resolved.
