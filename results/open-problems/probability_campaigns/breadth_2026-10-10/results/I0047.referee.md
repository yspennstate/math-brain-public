# Referee report: I0047, first-passage percolation on the affine preferential attachment tree

## Scope

The source problem concerns the general preferential attachment model. In van der Hofstad's notes this is primarily the m ≥ 2 case, where first-passage percolation means optimising over competing paths. The answer instead proves the one-edge (tree) case, with a slightly non-standard start (an initial edge {1,2} and no self-loops). It labels this case accurately and calls the result "partial", so it does not overclaim. It does not settle the open problem. In the tree case the weighted distance is just a sum of weights along the unique path, so the result is close to routine given known depth and typical-distance results for PA trees.

## Literature

- **Tree depths and typical distances** in linear PA trees are classical (e.g. Pittel 1994 on heights; Bhamidi 2007 preprint on PA trees; Dommers–van der Hofstad–Hooghiemstra 2010 on diameters). The m = 1 first-passage percolation law here follows readily from them.
- **The genuinely open m ≥ 2 case** is partially settled by Jorritsma & Komjáthy, *Weighted distances in scale-free preferential attachment models*. My recollection is arXiv around 2018, published in Random Structures & Algorithms around 2020, but I have not verified the dates. It covers the 2 < τ < 3 regime with general weights and the explosive/conservative dichotomy.
- To my knowledge, the τ > 3 regime with m ≥ 2 is not fully resolved.
