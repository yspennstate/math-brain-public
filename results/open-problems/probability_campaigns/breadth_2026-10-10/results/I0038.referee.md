# Referee report on I0038 (Schramm 2006, Problem 3.2)

## Does it answer the problem?

No. Problem 3.2 asks how fast the *discrete lattice processes* known to converge to SLE actually converge. Those are models such as LERW, percolation exploration, UST Peano curves, and Ising/FK interfaces, measured against SLE in a lattice mesh. The submission instead studies a numerical scheme: chordal Loewner evolution with a Brownian driver, discretized in capacity time and run as slit maps. It compares this scheme with true SLE only at interior points z with Im z > 2√T. Every SLE hull up to time T lies in {Im ≤ 2√T}, so these points never come near the curve.

No lattice model appears, and none of the steps transfers to one. Calling this a "proved special case" of 3.2 is a mislabel; it is a different and much easier problem. Problem 3.3, the shortest-crossing exponent, is correctly declared unaddressed.

## Line-by-line check of the mathematics

The mathematics is correct.

**Interior bound**
- (y²)' = −4y²/|Z|² ≥ −4 holds, so y ≥ η.
- The Itô computation is right: A = 2(κ−2)/w³ and |H| = 2√κ/w².
- The kernel representation of R_n with weight w_h is right, with ∫w_h = Th/2 and ∫w_h² = Th²/3.
- Complex Doob gives ‖max|M|‖₂ ≤ 2‖M_T‖₂, as claimed.
- The bound |S_n| ≤ 2Th/η³ holds.
- The Lipschitz constant L = 2/η² and the discrete Grönwall step are right.
- The assembled constant (|κ−2|+2)T/η³ + 4√(κT/3)/η², times e^{2T/η²}, matches.

**Second coefficient**
- a₂ = 2∫U follows from the expansion of 2/(z+q).
- Var J_j = h³/3 − h³/4 = h³/12.
- The decomposition a₂ = Q_* + V is right, and V is independent of the grid σ-field. So Q_* is the conditional expectation and the lower bound κTh²/3 is exact.
- Q_* − a₂^h = √κ·h·B_T is independent of V, giving total variance 4κTh²/3.

**Table and example**
- With T=1, κ=2, the table entries 8/(3N²) and 2/(3N²) check.
- The one-step example checks: E g₁(3i) = 7i/3 and the slit map gives i√5.

**Novelty**
Time-discretization error for Loewner/SLE is already studied, for example by Kennedy (2007–09) and by H. Tran (2015, Ann. Acad. Sci. Fenn.), who gives curve-level rates. These interior-point estimates are elementary by comparison.

## Literature

Problem 3.2 is partially addressed, model by model:
- Beneš, Johansson Viklund and Kozdron (CMP 2013), LERW → SLE₂.
- Johansson Viklund (Ann. Probab. 2015), rates for LERW and other Loewner curves.
- Mendelson, Nachmias and Watson (2014), and Binder, Chayes and Lei (2015), polynomial rates for percolation crossing probabilities (Cardy).

As far as I know, no paper settles the question in general. Problem 3.3 remains open; only Aizenman–Burchard-type bounds and numerical estimates (α ≈ 1.13) exist.
