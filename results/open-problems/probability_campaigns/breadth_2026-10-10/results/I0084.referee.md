## Referee report: I0084 (Hasebe–Ueda Conjecture 5.5)

**Scope.** The conjecture says "classical or free α-stable process" with no symmetry assumption. The solver reports that the published version (ALEA 15, 2018, §5.2) explicitly treats the positive 1/2-stable process. So a one-sided strictly 1/2-stable kernel falls under the literal statement. One reading risk remains: if the authors privately meant symmetric kernels, this refutes the literal statement but not the intended one. I could not check §5.2 myself, so this rests on the solver's report.

**Step-by-step check.**

1. **Kernel.** g_t(u) = t(2√π)⁻¹u^{-3/2}e^{-t²/(4u)} is the Lévy density with c = t²/2. Its Laplace transform is exp(-√(2cλ)) = exp(-t√λ), which matches. The J'(a) = -2J(a) derivation is the standard one and is correct. The scaling g_t(u) = t⁻²g₁(u/t²) checks out (t⁻²·t³ = t), so the semigroup is strictly 1/2-stable.
2. **Moments.** Z ∈ [1/2, 1]. For n ≥ 2r, -n² + rn ≤ -n²/2, so all moments are finite, including the (2+α) = 5/2 moment.
3. **Derivative.** The factor e^{-a/u}u^{-7/2}(a - 3u/2) with a = t²/4 gives strict decrease for u > t²/6. Correct. g_t is bounded and continuous on ℝ, since it vanishes to all orders at 0⁺.
4. **Density of the convolution.** Uniform convergence gives a continuous density, and Tonelli gives total mass 1. Correct.
5. **Decreasing intervals.** Take x ∈ (-2ⁿ + b_t, -2^{n-1}).
   - For j < n, x + 2^j < 0, so the term is 0.
   - For j ≥ n, x + 2^j > b_t, so the term is strictly decreasing.
   - The interval is nonempty when 2^{n-1} > b_t.
   
   The intervals exist for every n large enough and drift to -∞. Correct.
6. **No mode.** Any finite mode m forces q_t to be nondecreasing on (-∞, m). This holds after transferring monotonicity from an a.e.-equal density to the continuous q_t, and the solver does that transfer correctly. That contradicts step 5. The argument works for every t > 0, which is stronger than needed.

**Structure.** The mechanism is a one-sided kernel combined with initial mass escaping in the opposite direction through ever-widening gaps. It is elementary and airtight. The same argument works for any positive strictly α-stable law with α < 1, though the solver does not claim this. It fails for α ≥ 1, where totally skewed stable laws have full support.

**Not settled by this answer:**
- the symmetric (or two-sided-support) classical case;
- the free branch;
- α ∈ [1, 2).

The solver states these limits correctly. The numerical section is just a supplement and plays no part in the proof.

**Literature.** I know of no paper resolving Conjecture 5.5 or giving this counterexample.

VERDICT: KEEP
