# B11 — dualize: normalize the source solution before gauging

The useful move this run is to normalize by the **unknown gap probability itself** before asking for a Lax interpretation.

Let

\[
q(\tau)=\partial_\tau\log G(e^\tau)
=\frac{sG'(s)}{G(s)}.
\]

Set

\[
\widehat R(\tau,y)=\frac{R(\tau,y)}{G(e^\tau)}.
\]

Then

\[
\widehat R_\tau
=\widehat R_{yy}+b\widehat R_y-q(\tau)\widehat R.
\]

Thus the logarithmic derivative of the **gap itself** appears as a scalar term in the same source-side generator equation, before any Fredholm determinant or integrable-kernel input.

After the same gauge and analytic rotation, with

\[
\widehat\Phi_0(t,z)=\frac{\Phi_0(t,z)}{G(s(t))},
\qquad s(t)=i e^{2t}
\]

(on the analytically rotated contour, wherever the continuation is valid), one gets

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\widehat\Phi_0
=\bigl(H_0-Q(t)\bigr)\widehat\Phi_0,
\qquad
Q(t)=\partial_t\log G(s(t))=2q(\tau),
}
\]

where

\[
H_0=\frac12\partial_z^2
-\frac{1}{8\cosh^2z}
+\frac{1}{8\sinh^2z}
-\frac{e^{4t}}{16}\cosh4z
+\frac{e^{2t}}2\cosh2z.
\]

This is stronger than the preceding run's gauge-fixed observation. Zabrodin-Zotov's full PV time-Lax equation has exactly the form

\[
\partial_t\psi=(H_{\rm quantum}-\widetilde H_{\rm classical})\psi,
\]

so **if** the carousel-normalized solution can be shown to lie in the corresponding full PV Lax system, then the scalar identification is forced:

\[
\boxed{Q(t)=\widetilde H_{\rm V}(t)}.
\]

Since `Q = d_t log G`, this would identify `G` with the PV tau function up to the multiplicative constant fixed by `G(0)=1`. This is now the exact missing bridge; it is no longer merely “find some logarithmic derivative that closes.”

The formal adjoint formulation says the same thing from the other side. If `rho` is the normalized source flux below, its mass-one condition is an adjoint pairing constraint. The scalar `q` is the Lagrange multiplier needed to keep that pairing fixed. This is a source-side dual meaning for the prospective Hamiltonian/tau derivative.

# B12 — conserved structure: an exact Darboux partner from the carousel flux

Differentiate the **source** equation rather than postulating a second Lax component. Put

\[
m(\tau,y)=R_y(\tau,y),\qquad
\rho(\tau,y)=\frac{m(\tau,y)}{G(e^\tau)}.
\]

Then

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho(\tau,y)\,dy=1
\]

and direct differentiation of the carousel PDE gives

\[
\rho_\tau
=\rho_{yy}+b\rho_y+(b_y-q)\rho.
\]

Using **the same gauge** `rho=e^phi Xi` gives

\[
\Xi_\tau
=\Xi_{yy}
+\left[
\frac{3}{4\cosh^2y}
-\frac{e^\tau}{4}\sinh y
-\frac{e^{2\tau}}{32}\cosh2y
-q(\tau)
\right]\Xi.
\]

After `y=2z-i pi/2`, `tau=2t+i pi/2`, this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\Phi_1=(H_1-Q(t))\Phi_1,
}
\]

with

\[
H_1=\frac12\partial_z^2
+\frac{3}{8\cosh^2z}
-\frac{3}{8\sinh^2z}
-\frac{e^{4t}}{16}\cosh4z
-\frac{e^{2t}}2\cosh2z.
\]

Matching coefficient-by-coefficient to Zabrodin-Zotov (their hyperbolic PV potential (7.25)) gives

\[
(\zeta,\xi+\sigma,\sigma)=\left(\frac12,\frac12,0\right)
\]

(up to signs of the first two monodromy exponents), hence the classical partner parameters

\[
\boxed{
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)
=\left(\frac12,-\frac12,-1,-\frac12\right).
}
\]

More importantly, this second quantum-PV equation is not an imported integrable object. It is produced by differentiating the carousel solution itself.

The two source-side wavefunctions are linked by the exact first-order operator

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_1=\frac12 A\widehat\Phi_0,
\qquad
A=\partial_z-\coth(2z)+\frac{e^{2t}}2\sinh(2z).
}
\]

Writing `H_j = (1/2) d_z^2 + V_j`, direct algebra gives

\[
V_1=V_0-\partial_z a,
\qquad
\partial_ta+\partial_zV_0-\frac12\partial_z^2a+a\partial_za=0,
\]

where

\[
a=-\coth(2z)+\frac{e^{2t}}2\sinh(2z).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
(\partial_t-H_1)A=A(\partial_t-H_0).
}
\]

This is an exact **time-dependent Darboux intertwiner** between two quantum-PV Hamiltonians obtained directly from the Brownian-carousel generator and its flux. The common scalar `-Q(t)` commutes with the intertwiner, so the normalized pair satisfies the same relation.

This is the strongest new structural fact of the run.

## Exact obstruction exposed by the same calculation

The same calculation proves a no-go for a purely local closure. For any smooth scalar `h(t)`, the simultaneous gauge change

\[
(\widehat\Phi_0,\Phi_1)
\mapsto e^{h(t)}(\widehat\Phi_0,\Phi_1)
\]

preserves the Darboux relation and changes

\[
Q(t)\mapsto Q(t)-h'(t).
\]

Therefore the two local time-PDEs plus their exact Darboux intertwiner **cannot by themselves determine** `Q=d_t log G`. The missing information is genuinely global: the Brownian-carousel connection/boundary normalization must be converted into the missing spatial/isomonodromic constraint (or an equivalent closed Darboux chain). This is a sharper obstruction than the historical statement “the logarithmic derivative does not close.”

Hence the normalized flux density is

\[
\boxed{
\rho_0(y)=\frac{1}{\pi\cosh y},

Also `G(0)=1`, hence `q(\tau)->0` as `s=e^tau->0` provided the source gap function has its usual differentiable small-gap expansion. These are explicit source-side branch conditions that any claimed PV-Lax embedding must match; they cannot be replaced by a Fredholm-determinant normalization.

# B21 — who else has this shape?

Three nearby mechanisms were checked.

1. **Rumanov, hard edge (arXiv:1212.5333).** Rumanov starts from a diffusion/Fokker-Planck PDE, introduces a 2x2 spatial/time Lax ansatz, and obtains Painleve III from compatibility for beta=2 (and beta=4 cases). The decisive ingredient is not recognition of the time Hamiltonian alone; it is a compatible spatial Lax equation with algebraic constraints. This is the nearest solved source-side analogue.

2. **Zabrodin-Zotov (arXiv:1107.5672 and arXiv:1212.5813).** Their quantum Painleve-Calogero correspondence gives the exact PV time Hamiltonian above, while the inverse/functional-relations paper emphasizes that the nonlinear Painleve motion comes from compatibility with the companion scalar/matrix problem. Their formula (7.25) contains an x-independent `-H_tilde`; the present source normalization has produced exactly such a scalar term `-Q=d_t log G` without importing a determinant.

3. **Willox-Hietarinta, “Painleve equations from Darboux chains: I. PIII-PV” (J. Phys. A 36 (2003), arXiv:nlin/0302012).** Periodically closing Darboux chains of Schrodinger problems yields PV and its tau/bilinear forms. The exact carousel-derived intertwiner above is therefore not just an incidental supersymmetric factorization: it is precisely the kind of object from which a PV closure can arise. What is still missing is the rest of a source-side closed chain (or an equivalent spatial Lax equation), not another recognition of the same scalar potential.

This suggests a more concrete continuation than the previous generic “find monodromy” instruction: recover the other Darboux/Baecklund links from Brownian-carousel symmetries and/or winding-threshold generating PDEs, and test whether they close after the PV period.
