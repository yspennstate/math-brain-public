## 3. Brownian-carousel generator PDE with source-side boundary data

The Brownian-carousel phase SDE in Virág’s normalization is

\[
 d\alpha_t^\lambda
 =
 \lambda\frac{\beta}{4}e^{-\beta t/4}\,dt
 +\operatorname{Re}\!\left((e^{-i\alpha_t^\lambda}-1)dZ_t\right),
 \qquad \alpha_0^\lambda=0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The real martingale term has quadratic variation

\[
 |e^{-i\alpha}-1|^2dt
 =4\sin^2(\alpha/2)\,dt,
\]

so up to an adapted one-dimensional Brownian motion the scalar phase has diffusion coefficient
`2 sin(alpha/2)`.

Introduce the remaining drift scale

\[
 s=\lambda e^{-\beta t/4},
 \qquad \dot s=-\frac{\beta}{4}s,
\]

and let `U_beta(s,alpha)` be the probability of avoiding the next `2 pi` winding threshold, starting at phase `alpha in (0,2 pi)` with remaining scale `s`. The backward generator gives

\[
 -\frac{\beta}{4}s\,U_s
 +\frac{\beta}{4}s\,U_\alpha
 +2\sin^2(\alpha/2)U_{\alpha\alpha}=0.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \boxed{
 sU_s
 =
 sU_\alpha
 +\frac4\beta(1-\cos\alpha)U_{\alpha\alpha}.
 }
\tag{3.2}
\]

For beta=2,

\[
 \boxed{
 sU_s
 =
 sU_\alpha+2(1-\cos\alpha)U_{\alpha\alpha}.
 }
\tag{3.3}
\]

The source-side boundary data are not optional. At the failed winding boundary,

\[
 U(s,2\pi-)=0,
\tag{3.4}
\]

while the gap probability in the carousel spectral normalization is

\[
 G(s)=U(s,0+).
\tag{3.5}
\]

At `s=0` the drift vanishes and the phase diffusion is a bounded martingale on `[0,2 pi]`. Its limiting endpoint probabilities are fixed by the starting mean, giving

\[
 \boxed{
 U(0,\alpha)=1-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}.
 }
\tag{3.6}
\]

This is the actual boundary-value problem whose connection coefficient must become the bulk gap probability.

## 4. B20 — the exact non-closure is global connection data, not merely “linear versus nonlinear”

The old D0047-A001 already noted the linear-PDE / nonlinear-ODE mismatch. The sharper obstruction found here is that **local jets of the generator PDE remain linear and cannot themselves manufacture the scalar nonlinear closure**.

Set

\[
 z=e^{i\alpha}.
\]

Then (3.3) becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 sU_s
 =
 iszU_z+(z-1)^2(zU_{zz}+U_z).
 }
\tag{4.1}
\]

If locally

\[
 U(s,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(s)(z-1)^n,
\]

then coefficient comparison gives a linear nearest-neighbor jet hierarchy,

\[
 s a_n'
 =
 is\bigl((n+1)a_{n+1}+n a_n\bigr)
 +n(n-1)a_n
 +(n-1)^2a_{n-1},
\tag{4.2}
\]

with the obvious edge modifications for `n=0,1`.

The key geometry is that `z=1` represents **both sides of the cut**:

\[
 \alpha=0+
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \alpha=2\pi-,
\]

but the two boundary values are different:

\[
 U(s,0+)=G(s),
 \qquad
 U(s,2\pi-)=0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore the unknown `G(s)` is naturally a **global connection / monodromy coefficient for a punctured-circle boundary-value problem**, not a quantity obtainable from one local Taylor jet at `z=1`. This is the precise source-side reason a naive logarithmic derivative such as `s d_s log U(s,0)` does not close from the backward PDE alone.

**B20 outcome:** the missing nonlinearity has been localized to global connection data. This does not solve the source question, but it rules out spending further runs on purely local differentiation of (3.3).

## 5. B18 — exact translation of the beta=2 generator PDE into the quantum Painlevé-V time equation

This is the main new mathematical advance of the run.

### 5.1 Cotangent coordinate

Set

\[
 x=\cot(\alpha/2).
\]

Then

\[
 \frac{dx}{d\alpha}=-\frac{1+x^2}{2},
 \qquad
 2(1-\cos\alpha)=\frac4{1+x^2}.
\]

Substituting into (3.3) gives the exact real-line PDE

\[
 \boxed{
 sU_s
 =
 (1+x^2)U_{xx}
 +
 \left(
 2x-\frac{s}{2}(1+x^2)
 \right)U_x.
 }
\tag{5.1}
\]

The two sides of the winding cut become

\[
 x=+\infty\quad(\alpha=0+),
 \qquad
 x=-\infty\quad(\alpha=2\pi-),
\]

so

\[
 U(s,-\infty)=0,\qquad U(s,+\infty)=G(s),
\tag{5.2}
\]

and (3.6) becomes

\[
 U(0,x)=\frac12+\frac1\pi\arctan x.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### 5.2 Hyperbolic coordinate and gauge

Now put

\[
 x=\sinh y,\qquad \tau=\log s,
 \qquad R(\tau,y)=U(e^\tau,\sinh y).
\]

A direct calculation turns (5.1) into

\[
 R_\tau
 =
 R_{yy}
 +
 \left(
 \tanh y-\frac{e^\tau}{2}\cosh y
 \right)R_y.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Gauge away the first derivative by

\[
 R=e^\phi\Psi,
\]

with

\[
 \boxed{
 \phi(\tau,y)
 =
 -\frac12\log\cosh y
 +\frac{e^\tau}{4}\sinh y
 -\frac{\tau}{4}
 -\frac{e^{2\tau}}{64}.
 }
\tag{5.5}
\]

Indeed `2 phi_y + tanh(y) - e^tau cosh(y)/2 = 0`. The scalar time terms in (5.5) are chosen only to remove a coordinate-independent potential. One obtains

\[
 \boxed{
 \Psi_\tau
 =
 \Psi_{yy}
 +
 \left[
 -\frac{1}{4\cosh^2y}
 +\frac{e^\tau}{4}\sinh y
 -\frac{e^{2\tau}}{32}\cosh2y
 \right]\Psi.
 }
\tag{5.6}
\]

### 5.3 Analytic rotation to the hyperbolic quantum-PV Hamiltonian

Make the explicit analytic change

\[
 y=2z-\frac{i\pi}{2},
 \qquad
 \tau=2t+\frac{i\pi}{2},
 \qquad
 \Phi(t,z)=\Psi(\tau,y).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Because

\[
 \Phi_t=2\Psi_\tau,\qquad
 \Phi_{zz}=4\Psi_{yy},
\]

and

\[
 \cosh(2z-i\pi/2)=-i\sinh2z,\quad
 \sinh(2z-i\pi/2)=-i\cosh2z,
\]

equation (5.6) becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_t
 =
 \frac12\Phi_{zz}
 +
 \left[
 -\frac1{8\cosh^2z}
 +\frac1{8\sinh^2z}
 -\frac{e^{4t}}{16}\cosh4z
 +\frac{e^{2t}}2\cosh2z
 \right]\Phi.
 }
\tag{5.8}
\]

The reciprocal term uses

\[
 \frac1{\sinh^2z}-\frac1{\cosh^2z}
 =\frac4{\sinh^22z}.
\]

Zabrodin–Zotov’s quantum Painlevé-V time equation has potential

\[
 V_{\rm PV}(z,t)
 =
 \frac{4\zeta^2-\frac14}{2\cosh^2z}
 -

\tag{5.9}
\]

Equation (5.8) agrees **term by term** with (5.9) at

\[

\tag{5.10}

Under their classical parameter map this is
