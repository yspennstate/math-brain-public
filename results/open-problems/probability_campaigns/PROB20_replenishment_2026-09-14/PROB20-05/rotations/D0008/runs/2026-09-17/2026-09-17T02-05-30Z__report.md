## 3. Why the selected checkpoint is source-valid

For `(r,s)=(-1/2,2)`, the previously derived two-step family

\[

becomes exactly (1.1), because

\[
\frac{s-1}{s-r}=\frac25,

Its density is

p_*(x)=

## 4. B14 — exploit permutation symmetry first

The identical-marginal constraint is easiest if each zero-sum geometric component is symmetrized over the coordinate permutation group `S_3`.

Let `T~Unif[0,1]`. For three branch labels define

\[
Z^{(0)}(T)=
\left(-1+\frac T2,-1+\frac T2,2-T\right),
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
Z^{(1)}(T)=
\left(-1+\frac{3T}{2},-T,1-\frac T2\right),
\tag{4.2}
\]

\[
Z^{(2)}(T)=
\left(-1+T,\frac{1-T}{2},\frac{1-T}{2}\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Each branch is pointwise zero-sum:

\[
Z^{(j)}_1(T)+Z^{(j)}_2(T)+Z^{(j)}_3(T)=0
\quad\text{for every }T\in[0,1].
\tag{4.4}
\]

Now choose an independent branch variable `J` with

\[
\mathbb P(J=0)=\frac35,
\qquad
\mathbb P(J=1)=\frac3{10},
\qquad
\mathbb P(J=2)=\frac1{10},
\tag{4.5}
\]

and an independent uniformly random permutation `Pi in S_3`. Define

\[
(X_1,X_2,X_3)=\Pi Z^{(J)}(T).
\tag{4.6}
\]

Equation (4.4) immediately gives

\[
X_1+X_2+X_3=0\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Uniform permutation makes all three coordinate marginals identical. It remains only to identify that common marginal.

## 5. B16 — exact small geometric search, then discard the decimal locator

The useful finite ansatz was: line segments between rational zero-sum endpoint triples on the half-grid

\[
-1,-\frac12,0,\frac12,1,\frac32,2,
\]

followed by coordinate symmetrization. Each segment contributes an exactly computable average of three uniform interval laws.

A numerical feasibility locator was used only to identify a small support pattern. **No solver decimal is retained as evidence.** Once the three branches (4.1)–(4.3) were identified, the weights in (4.5) were solved and checked exactly over `Q`; the proof below is entirely rational and independent of the locator.

For one randomly permuted coordinate, the three branch densities on the six half-unit cells are:

| cell | `[-1,-1/2]` | `[-1/2,0]` | `[0,1/2]` | `[1/2,1]` | `[1,3/2]` | `[3/2,2]` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| branch 0 | `4/3` | `0` | `0` | `0` | `1/3` | `1/3` |
| branch 1 | `5/9` | `5/9` | `2/9` | `2/3` | `0` | `0` |
| branch 2 | `1/3` | `1/3` | `4/3` | `0` | `0` | `0` |

These entries follow directly from the coordinate interval lengths:

- branch 0 has two coordinates uniform on `[-1,-1/2]` and one uniform on `[1,2]`;
- branch 1 has coordinate intervals `[-1,1/2]`, `[-1,0]`, `[1/2,1]`;
- branch 2 has one coordinate interval `[-1,0]` and two on `[0,1/2]`.

The weights are forced transparently by the tail cells. On `[1,3/2]` and `[3/2,2]`, only branch 0 contributes, so

\[
\frac{w_0}{3}=\frac15
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w_0=\frac35.
\]

On `[1/2,1]`, only branch 1 contributes, so

\[
\frac{2w_1}{3}=\frac15
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w_1=\frac3{10}.
\]

Then `w_2=1-w_0-w_1=1/10`. Substituting these exact weights gives

\[
(1,\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15,\tfrac15),
\]

which is exactly the density (3.1) cell by cell. Therefore every `X_i` in (4.6) has law `P_*`.

This proves the restricted theorem:

> **Exact P-star complete mix.** The source-valid decreasing law `P_*` in (1.1) is 3-completely mixable via the explicit sampler (4.1)–(4.6), and the sum is identically zero.

## 6. Explicit copula form

The CDF of `P_*` is

\[
F_*(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x<-1,\\
x+1,&-1\le x\le-\tfrac12,\\
\dfrac{x+3}{5},&-\tfrac12\le x\le2,\\
1,&x>2.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.1}
\]

It is continuous and strictly increasing on its support. Hence

\[
(U_1,U_2,U_3)=\bigl(F_*(X_1),F_*(X_2),F_*(X_3)\bigr)
\tag{6.2}
\]

has uniform `[0,1]` marginals. The law of (6.2), with `(X_1,X_2,X_3)` generated by the finite branch/permutation recipe above, is therefore an **explicit copula** of a complete mix with margin `P_*`. Equivalently, (4.6) itself is an explicit joint complete mix and exact sampler, which is already one of the two forms requested by Wang's Open Problem 8.

This copula is a finite mixture of permuted one-dimensional curve measures; no numerical inversion, weak limit, or implicit existence selection is involved.
