## 2. Exact source scope and dated literature check

Wang's §2.2 condition (b) is:

> if `F` has a monotone density on essential support `[a,b]`, with mean `mu`, then `F` is `n`-CM iff
> `a + (b-a)/n <= mu <= b - (b-a)/n`.

In §2.7, Wang states that the major sufficient-condition proofs are combinatorial/discretization arguments whose dependence structures are unclear, and Open Problem 8 asks for a possible copula of an `n`-complete mix when `F` satisfies such a sufficient condition.

Fresh bounded check on **2026-09-17 (Asia/Jerusalem; 2026-09-16 UTC)**:

- Wang's current homepage (`https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~wang/`) was updated 14 September 2026 and still lists the 2015 survey and the 2011 monotone-density paper; no source-wide explicit Open-Problem-8 resolution was identified there.
- Wang–Wang (2011), *The complete mixability and convex minimization problems with monotone marginal densities*, proves existence for condition (b), but the source survey itself emphasizes that the proof proceeds through discretization/decomposition and does not expose the desired dependence structure.
- Puccetti–Rueschendorf (2013), *Sharp bounds for sums of dependent risks*, derives optimal couplings in a related tail-bound problem, but its construction explicitly invokes complete mixability of conditional pieces; it does not supply a source-general explicit copula for condition (b).
- A 2017 paper by Qing Hu and Yuxu Feng, *Complete Mixability and Minimum Value-at-Risk of Sum of Dependent Variable* (DOI `10.12677/sa.2017.64055`), advertises a copula/dependence structure for the monotone-density VaR problem. Inspection of its pp. 497–498 shows that the key middle-block variables are asserted to exist by invoking the earlier monotone-density complete-mixability corollary; that step is not an explicit source-wide construction and therefore is not treated here as a resolution of Wang's Open Problem 8.

## 3. New idea before computation: leave graph copulas and put the mix directly on a simplex (B24)

The earlier D0008 attempts concentrated heavily on one-dimensional orbit/shuffle representations. That is unnecessarily restrictive. A complete mix with center `mu` naturally lives on the affine hyperplane

`x_1 + ... + x_n = n mu`.

For the lower boundary of Wang's decreasing-density condition after affine normalization to `[0,1]`, the center is `mu=1/n`; the hyperplane becomes the standard simplex

`Delta_{n-1} = {v_i >= 0 : sum_i v_i = 1}`.

This suggests using **full-dimensional distributions on the simplex**, not a one-dimensional graph. Symmetric Dirichlet laws immediately give an explicit sampler and beta marginals. The key question is whether those beta marginals lie inside Wang's monotone-density class. They do for a sharp interval of Dirichlet parameters.

## 4. Theorem A — an explicit source-valid complete-mix family for every dimension

### Theorem A

Let `n >= 3`, let `a < b`, and let

`1/(n-1) <= alpha <= 1`.

Let `G_1,...,G_n` be independent Gamma(`alpha`, rate `1`) variables, set

`S = G_1 + ... + G_n`,

`V_i = G_i/S`,

and define

`X_i = a + (b-a)V_i`.

Then:

1. `X_1,...,X_n` have the same continuous distribution `F_{n,alpha}^{a,b}` on `[a,b]`;
2. its density is

   `f_{n,alpha}^{a,b}(x)`
   `= [Gamma(n alpha)/(Gamma(alpha) Gamma((n-1)alpha) (b-a))]`
   `  ((x-a)/(b-a))^(alpha-1)`
   `  ((b-x)/(b-a))^((n-1)alpha-1)`;

3. this density is nonincreasing on `(a,b)`;
4. its mean is exactly

   `mu = a + (b-a)/n`,

   i.e. equality in the lower Wang condition-(b) bound;
5. almost surely,

   `X_1 + ... + X_n = n a + (b-a) = n mu`.

Consequently this is an explicit `n`-complete mix in the exact source sufficient-condition class. If `B_alpha` denotes the CDF of `Beta(alpha,(n-1)alpha)`, then

`U_i = B_alpha(V_i)`

has uniform margins, so the law of `(U_1,...,U_n)` is an explicit copula for this complete mix. Replacing `X_i` by

`X_i^+ = b - (b-a)V_i`

gives the reflected increasing-density source-valid family with mean `b-(b-a)/n` and the same explicit constant-sum property.

### Proof

The gamma density is

`g_alpha(t)=t^(alpha-1)e^{-t}/Gamma(alpha)`, `t>0`.

Make the change of variables

`g_i = s v_i`, `i=1,...,n`,

with `v_i>0`, `sum_i v_i=1`, and `s>0`. Using `v_n=1-sum_{i<n}v_i`, the Jacobian is `s^(n-1)`. The joint density in `(s,v_1,...,v_{n-1})` is therefore

`[Gamma(alpha)^(-n)] exp(-s) s^(n alpha-1) prod_i v_i^(alpha-1)`.

Integrating out `s` gives

`Gamma(n alpha)/Gamma(alpha)^n * prod_i v_i^(alpha-1)`

on the simplex, which is the symmetric Dirichlet(`alpha,...,alpha`) density. In particular, integrating out `v_2,...,v_n` (equivalently using the beta integral on the remaining simplex) gives

`V_i ~ Beta(alpha,(n-1)alpha)`

with density

`q_alpha(v)=Gamma(n alpha)/(Gamma(alpha)Gamma((n-1)alpha))`
`             v^(alpha-1)(1-v)^((n-1)alpha-1)`, `0<v<1`.

The affine transformation `x=a+(b-a)v` gives the displayed density for `X_i`.

To check monotonicity, differentiate the logarithm on `(0,1)`:

`d/dv log q_alpha(v)`
`= (alpha-1)/v - ((n-1)alpha-1)/(1-v)`.

Under

`alpha <= 1`,

and

`(n-1)alpha >= 1`,

both terms are nonpositive. Hence `q_alpha`, and therefore `f_{n,alpha}^{a,b}`, is nonincreasing.

By symmetry of the Dirichlet vector and the deterministic identity `sum_i V_i=1`,

`E[V_i]=1/n`.

Thus

`E[X_i]=a+(b-a)/n`.

This is precisely the lower boundary of Wang's mean condition for decreasing densities. Finally,

`sum_i X_i = sum_i [a+(b-a)V_i]`
`            = n a + (b-a) sum_i V_i`
`            = n a + (b-a)`

almost surely, and this equals `n E[X_i]`. Hence `(X_1,...,X_n)` is an explicit complete mix.

Because the beta CDF `B_alpha` is continuous and strictly increasing on `(0,1)`, the probability-integral transform gives each `U_i=B_alpha(V_i)` a uniform `[0,1]` marginal. Their joint law is therefore a copula, and the inverse-beta marginal map recovers the complete mix. Reflection proves the increasing-density statement. `QED`.

### Concrete members

- `alpha=1`: `V` is uniform (Lebesgue) on the simplex and

  `f(x)=(n-1)(b-x)^(n-2)/(b-a)^(n-1)`.

- `n=3`, `alpha=1`: after normalization to `[0,1]`, the marginal density is `2(1-x)`, so the familiar triangular boundary law has the explicit complete mix given by a uniform point on the triangle `x+y+z=1`.

- `alpha=1/(n-1)`: the second beta exponent is zero; this is the other endpoint of the monotonicity range and gives another explicit, generally unbounded-at-`a` decreasing density.

Thus B5 deforms continuously from one explicit boundary law to another while staying inside the exact source condition-(b) class.

## 5. Why the obvious deformation to an arbitrary target `F` fails (B5): a rigidity theorem

A tempting route would be: sample a full-dimensional constant-sum simplex vector `V`, then apply the **same** scalar monotone map `g` to every coordinate so that `g(V_i)` has an arbitrary desired source marginal `F`. The next theorem shows this cannot preserve constant sum unless `g` is affine.

### Lemma (measurable Pexider rigidity on a triangle)

Let `L>0`. Suppose measurable real-valued functions `g:(0,L)->R` and `H:(0,L)->R` satisfy

`g(x)+g(y)=H(x+y)`

for Lebesgue-a.e. `(x,y)` with `x>0`, `y>0`, `x+y<L`. Then `g(x)=c x+d` for a.e. `x in (0,L)`.

#### Proof

For almost every `t in (0,L)`, translating the a.e. identity by `t` is legitimate on its overlap. For such `t`, whenever `x,y>0` and `x+y+t<L`, comparison of

`g(x+t)+g(y)=H(x+y+t)`

with

`g(x)+g(y+t)=H(x+y+t)`

gives

`g(x+t)-g(x)=g(y+t)-g(y)`

for a.e. admissible `(x,y)`. Hence, for almost every fixed `t`, the difference

`Delta_t g(x)=g(x+t)-g(x)`

is a.e. independent of `x` on `(0,L-t)`; call the common value `A(t)`. (To see constancy, first restrict `x,y` to a small interval whose pairwise sums are `<L-t`, and then compare any other admissible `x` with a sufficiently small `y`.)

For almost every pair `s,t>0` with `s+t<L`, choose `x` where all relevant equalities hold. Then

`A(s+t)`
`=g(x+s+t)-g(x)`
`=[g(x+s+t)-g(x+s)]+[g(x+s)-g(x)]`
`=A(t)+A(s)`.

Thus the measurable function `A` satisfies the Cauchy equation almost everywhere on the local additive semigroup. The standard measurable-Cauchy argument (modify on a null set and extend locally by addition) gives

`A(t)=c t`

for a.e. `t`. Therefore, with `q(x)=g(x)-cx`,

`q(x+t)=q(x)`

for a.e. admissible `(x,t)`. Equivalently `q(y)=q(x)` for a.e. pair `0<x<y<L`, so `q` is a.e. constant. Hence `g(x)=cx+d` a.e. `QED`.

### Theorem B (simplex constant-sum rigidity)

Let `n>=3` and let `g:(0,1)->R` be measurable. Suppose

`g(v_1)+...+g(v_n)=C`

for `(n-1)`-dimensional Lebesgue-a.e. point in the open simplex

`Delta_{n-1}^o={v_i>0: sum_i v_i=1}`.

Then `g` is affine a.e. on `(0,1)`.

#### Proof for `n=3`

Parameterize the simplex by `x=v_1`, `y=v_2`, `v_3=1-x-y`. The hypothesis is

`g(x)+g(y)+g(1-x-y)=C`

for a.e. `(x,y)` in the open triangle. Put

`H(s)=C-g(1-s)`.

Then

`g(x)+g(y)=H(x+y)`

for a.e. `x,y>0`, `x+y<1`. The lemma with `L=1` makes `g` affine a.e.

#### Proof for `n>3`

Parameterize by `v_1,v_2` together with `w=(v_4,...,v_n)`, and set

`r=sum_{i=4}^n v_i`,

`v_3=1-r-v_1-v_2`.

Fubini's theorem says that for a.e. admissible `w`, the relation

`g(v_1)+g(v_2)+g(1-r-v_1-v_2)=C_w`

holds for a.e. `(v_1,v_2)` in the triangle `v_1,v_2>0`, `v_1+v_2<1-r`, where `C_w=C-sum_{i=4}^n g(v_i)`. Applying the `n=3` case on that smaller simplex shows that `g` is affine a.e. on `(0,1-r)`.

For every `epsilon>0`, the set of positive `w` with `r<epsilon` has positive measure, so we may choose good `w` with `r` arbitrarily close to zero. The affine representatives on the resulting intervals agree on overlaps (two affine functions agreeing on a positive-measure set have identical coefficients). Letting `r downarrow 0` gives one affine representative on all of `(0,1)`. `QED`.

### Consequence for D0008

The symmetric Dirichlet density is strictly positive a.e. on the open simplex for every `alpha>0`. Therefore, if one applies the same measurable scalar function `g` to all Dirichlet coordinates and demands that the transformed vector still have deterministic component sum, Theorem B forces `g` to be affine a.e. So the natural plan

`Dirichlet complete mix -> coordinatewise target quantile map -> arbitrary monotone F`

cannot solve Wang's condition-(b) class except for affine images of the original beta marginal.

This is a structural obstruction, not a counterexample to complete mixability.

## 6. B10 — explicit algorithm succeeds for the restricted family, but the general peeling limit is still the wall

For Theorem A the algorithm is genuinely explicit:

1. sample independent `G_i ~ Gamma(alpha,1)`;
2. normalize `V_i=G_i/sum_j G_j`;
3. output `X_i=a+(b-a)V_i` (or the reflected version).

Every output already has exact marginal law and exact deterministic sum; no discretization or limiting choice is involved.

By contrast, the Wang–Wang monotone-density existence proof and the previous D0008 exact-marginal epsilon construction use finite peelings at resolution `N` and then compactness / weak convergence. A direct attempt to turn those into one exact sampler by taking `N->infinity` still lacks **coherent refinement**: the greedy residual decomposition at scale `2N` is not canonically a refinement of the decomposition at scale `N`. Consequently one does not obtain a pathwise Cauchy sequence of complete-mix triples from the existing proof. Choosing a convergent subsequence of laws is precisely the implicit compactness step Open Problem 8 is asking us to replace.

So B10 produced a complete explicit sampler for the Dirichlet-beta subfamily but did not yet derandomize the general monotone-density theorem.
