## 3. Recovered `n=3` reduction

The strongest recovered reduction for the decreasing `n=3` class is the two-step family

\[
P_{r,s}
=\frac{s-1}{s-r}\,\operatorname{Unif}[-1,r]
+\frac{1-r}{s-r}\,\operatorname{Unif}[-1,s],
\qquad -1<r<1<s\le2.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Its density is

\[
p_{r,s}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{s+r}{(r+1)(s+1)},&-1\le x\le r,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{1-r}{(s-r)(s+1)},&r<x\le s,\\[2mm]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Every law in (3.1) has mean zero. The preceding D0008 work explains how solving this family explicitly would lift through the fixed-mean layer decomposition to the full decreasing source class at `n=3`, with reflection handling the increasing class. ## 4. B5 — deform in `(r,s)`: the unsplit two-uniform algorithm has an exact integrality obstruction

A first natural algorithm is to keep the decomposition (3.1) literal: label each coordinate either

\[
S\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,r]
\quad\text{or}\quad
L\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,s],
\]

then, conditional on a label pattern, couple the chosen whole uniforms to have zero sum.

Write

\[
\beta=\frac{1-r}{s-r}
\tag{4.1}
\]

for the long-component weight. Suppose one zero-sum branch has exactly `k` long coordinates and `3-k` short coordinates and **retains those whole component laws**. Since the branch sum is zero almost surely, its expectation is zero. But

\[
\mathbb E S=\frac{r-1}{2},
\qquad
\mathbb E L=\frac{s-1}{2},
\]

so necessarily

\[
0=(3-k)\frac{r-1}{2}+k\frac{s-1}{2}.
\]

Solving gives

\[
\boxed{
 k=\frac{3(1-r)}{s-r}=3\beta.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `k` is an integer in `{0,1,2,3}`, the unsplit-label architecture can only work on the special strata

\[
\beta\in\left\{0,\frac13,\frac23,1\right\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Randomizing among several such zero-sum branches does not remove the obstruction: every used branch that preserves the whole short/long component laws must separately satisfy (4.2). Thus a generic `(r,s)` cannot be solved by first choosing whole-component labels and then joint-mixing those unchanged uniforms.

This does **not** obstruct complete mixability. It says exactly what an explicit general construction must add: it must split at least one of the two uniform components into moving subintervals, or use a continuum mixing law whose conditional pieces are not the original two uniforms.

This is the useful B5 deformation diagnosis. It explains why the isolated and curve solutions found earlier do not analytically continue by merely varying their branch probabilities.

## 5. B19 — make the source existence proof an algorithm: the missing state is projective compatibility

\[
(-N,\;N-M+i,\;M-i),
\qquad i=0,1,\ldots,2M-N.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every block has sum zero. Averaging uniformly over `i` turns (5.1), after coordinate symmetrization, into the discrete fan kernel

\[
\frac13\delta_{-N}
+\frac23\operatorname{Unif}_{\{N-M,\ldots,M\}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The finite recursion is therefore genuinely constructive at each fixed lattice scale.

The source proof then forms decreasing mean-corrected discrete laws on finer grids and uses weak convergence plus closure of the completely-mixable class. A useful warning follows from (5.2): naively freezing one continuum analogue of the Case-2 fan leaves a `1/3` anchor atom and cannot equal a generic absolutely continuous target. ## 6. B30 — one scale at a time: a fresh handoff route via ternary rather than dyadic refinement

A previous D0008 diagnostic showed why the most local dyadic refinement fails for three coordinates: if three equal-width zero-sum parent cells are each split into two equal children, the child-center shifts are `+/- h/4`; an odd number of such signs cannot sum to zero.

The obstruction is specific to dyadic refinement. Under a **ternary** split, the three child-center shifts are

\[
-\frac h3,\quad 0,\quad +\frac h3,
\tag{6.1}
\]

and one can assign the three different shifts to the three coordinates so that their center shifts sum to zero. A uniformly randomized permutation of the three labels gives each coordinate each child label with probability `1/3`.

Thus ternary refinement removes the parity obstruction at the level of cell centers. It does **not** yet solve the source problem: for a nonuniform monotone density, the three child masses inside a parent cell are generally unequal, and the refinement must solve an exact label-coupling/mass-balance problem while retaining zero sum. The missing object is therefore finite-dimensional and local at each scale: a nonnegative coupling of child labels compatible with the three marginal child-mass vectors and the allowed zero-sum label triples.

This route is materially different from the exhausted fixed fan, fixed incidence, cyclic shift, and dyadic routes. A future reassignment should start here rather than recreating the old grids: formulate the ternary child-label feasibility polytope symbolically, determine whether the monotonicity/mean constraints imply feasibility, and if so iterate the kernels to obtain an exact infinite-digit sampler. If the local polytope can be made projectively consistent, it directly addresses the B19 gap.

No theorem that this program always works is claimed here.
