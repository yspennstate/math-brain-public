## 2. Recalled block coordinates

Fix global resolution `n`, mesh `h=1/n`, and a consecutive block of `m` bins with target-rank interval

`B=[a,a+mh]`.

Use rotated coordinates

`U=X+Y-1`, `V=X-Y`.

Original Euclidean squared cost is one half of squared Euclidean cost in `(U,V)`.

Let `p=F_U(U)` for the triangular `U` marginal and write

`t=(p-a)/(mh) in [0,1]`.

Put

`Q(t)=F_U^{-1}(a+mh t)`

and

`A(t)=1-|Q(t)|`.

Conditioned on `p in B`, the normalized fiber coordinate

`z=V/A(t)`

is independent of `t` and uniform on `[-1,1]`.

Let

`C=2a+mh-1`

be the center of the block in the `U` coordinate and set

`w=2t-1`.

The exact diagonal-optimal target on this block is

`U_0=C+mh w`, `V_0=0`.

Define the two block functionals

`G_B=E|Q(T)-Q(T')|`

for iid uniform `T,T'`, and

`H_B=E[1-|Q(T)|]=E A(T)`.

The quantile-Gini identity gives

`E[(Q(T)-C)w]=G_B/2`.                                      (2.1)

The previous checkpoint proves:

* every one-sided rank interval satisfies `G_B/H_B<=2/5`;
* if the rank interval length is at most `1/3`, then `G_B/H_B<=2/5`;
* every rank interval satisfies `G_B/H_B<=7/10`.

## 3. New maximal-run aggregation lemma

Let `sigma in S_m` be a local positive shuffle permutation.

A **run** is a maximal consecutive set of domain bins on which the displacement

`d=j-sigma(j)`

is constant. Equivalently, inside a run `sigma(j+1)=sigma(j)+1`, so the graph branches join into one horizontal segment in `(U,V)`.

Index the runs by `g`. Let

* `ell_g` be the run length;
* `d_g` its displacement;
* `j_g` its starting bin.

The joined target segment lies at

`V=d_g h`

and has `U`-half-length `ell_g h`. Its center is

`C+c_g h`,

where

`c_g=2j_g+ell_g-d_g-m`.                                  (3.1)

Order the runs by increasing `d_g`. Partition the source `z`-interval `[-1,1]` into consecutive intervals with probabilities

`alpha_g=ell_g/m`

in that same order, and let `mu_g` be the mean of the `g`-th `z` interval.

Because `t` and `z` are independent, `t` remains uniform inside every group. Map group `g` by

`U'_g=C+c_g h+ell_g h w`,
`V'_g=d_g h`.                                             (3.2)

This is exactly uniform on the joined target segment, with the correct mass `ell_g h`, hence the construction is an admissible transport onto the local shuffle.

Define

`A_run(sigma)=sum_g ell_g d_g mu_g`,                      (3.3)

and

`C_run(sigma)=(m^2-sum_g ell_g^2)/2`.                    (3.4)

### Theorem 3.1 — exact run-aggregation cost

The global change in squared transport cost relative to the diagonal-optimal transport is

`Delta = h^2 [ C_run(sigma) G_B - A_run(sigma) H_B ]`.    (3.5)

### Proof

For one run, compare its target `(U'_g,V'_g)` with the diagonal target `(U_0,0)`.

The horizontal conditional cost difference is

`-2 c_g h E(Q-C)
 -2(ell_g-m)h E[(Q-C)w]
 +h^2[c_g^2+(ell_g^2-m^2)/3]`.

The vertical conditional difference is

`h^2 d_g^2 - 2h H_B d_g mu_g`.

Weight by `alpha_g`.

Two identities remove all terms except the Gini and vertical-correlation terms.

First, the target block has mean `U=C`, so

`sum_g alpha_g c_g=0`.                                    (3.6)

Second, both target coordinate marginals are uniform on the same block. Therefore its centered rotated second moment equals the diagonal block's:

`sum_g alpha_g [c_g^2+ell_g^2/3+d_g^2]=m^2/3`.           (3.7)

Using (2.1), the conditional rotated-coordinate cost difference becomes

`(2h/m)[ C_run G_B - A_run H_B ]`.

Original Euclidean cost is half the rotated cost, and the source block has mass `mh`. Multiplying gives exactly (3.5). QED.

This strictly improves the earlier branch-by-branch equal-slab formula whenever a permutation has a run of length greater than one.

## 4. Corollary: every localized cyclic rotation is solved for every block length

Fix `1<=r<=m-1` and let

`sigma_r(j)=j+r mod m`.

There are exactly two runs:

* length `m-r`, displacement `-r`;
* length `r`, displacement `m-r`.

The corresponding `z`-group means are

`-r/m` and `(m-r)/m`.

Hence

`A_run=r(m-r)`,                                           (4.1)

while

`C_run=[m^2-(m-r)^2-r^2]/2=r(m-r)`.                      (4.2)

Therefore

`Delta=r(m-r) h^2 (G_B-H_B)`.                            (4.3)

The archived universal block inequality gives

`G_B/H_B<=7/10`.

Thus for every nontrivial cyclic rotation,

`Delta <= -(3/10) r(m-r) h^2 H_B <0`.                    (4.4)

So every localized cyclic rotation of an arbitrary consecutive block is strictly below the diagonal value `1/10`.

This theorem subsumes the cyclic five-bin exceptional family from the previous checkpoint and removes the first six-bin obstruction identified there.

## 6. A second aggregation for the exceptional six-bin pattern

It suffices to treat

`hardA=(1,3,0,5,2,4)`,

because swapping the two square coordinates sends a graph permutation to its inverse and preserves the product source.

For `hardA`, the six branches have the following displacement groups:

* `d=-2`: two branches;
* `d=-1`: one branch;
* `d=+1`: one branch;
* `d=+2`: two branches.

Their target `U` intervals, measured relative to block center `C` in units of `h`, are

* `d=-2`: `[-2,0]` and `[2,4]`;
* `d=-1`: `[-5,-3]`;
* `d=+1`: `[3,5]`;
* `d=+2`: `[-4,-2]` and `[0,2]`.

Partition `z` into four intervals, ordered by increasing displacement, with probabilities

`1/3, 1/6, 1/6, 1/3`.

Their means are

`-2/3, -1/6, 1/6, 2/3`.                                  (6.1)

Inside each displacement group, map the uniform horizontal rank `t` monotonically to the union of that group's target `U` intervals.

The target quantile functions, in units of `h` relative to `C`, are:

For `0<=t<=1/2`,

`R_-2=-2+4t`,
`R_-1=-5+2t`,
`R_+1=3+2t`,
`R_+2=-4+4t`.

For `1/2<=t<=1`,

`R_-2=4t`,
`R_-1=-5+2t`,
`R_+1=3+2t`,
`R_+2=-2+4t`.

Weighting by the group probabilities gives

`Rbar-R0 = 11/3-(26/3)t` for `t<=1/2`,                   (6.2)

`Rbar-R0 = 5-(26/3)t` for `t>=1/2`,                      (6.3)

where the diagonal target is

`R0=12t-6`.

Equivalently,

`Rbar-R0 = -(26/3)(t-1/2)+(2/3)sgn(t-1/2)`.              (6.4)

The vertical correlation coefficient is exactly

`B = sum alpha_g d_g mu_g = 17/18`.                       (6.5)

Define

`S_B = int_{1/2}^1 Q(t)dt - int_0^{1/2}Q(t)dt`.          (6.6)

Since `Q` is increasing,

`S_B>=0`.                                                 (6.7)

The target block has the same total second moment as the diagonal block, so the cost difference is entirely a source-target cross-moment difference. Using

`G_B=4 int_0^1 (t-1/2)Q(t)dt`,

(6.4), and (6.5), direct expansion gives the global cost difference

`Delta_hard = -(h^2/3)[17H_B+12S_B-39G_B]`.              (6.8)

If `G_B/H_B<=2/5`, then by `S_B>=0`,

`17H_B+12S_B-39G_B`
`>= 17H_B-39(2/5)H_B`
`= (7/5)H_B >0`.                                         (6.9)

Hence

`Delta_hard<0`.                                           (6.10)

The inverse `hardB` is solved by coordinate swapping.

Thus for every `n>=18`, **all** indecomposable six-bin permutations are solved: (3.5) handles all but `hardA,hardB`, and (6.8)-(6.10) handle those two.
