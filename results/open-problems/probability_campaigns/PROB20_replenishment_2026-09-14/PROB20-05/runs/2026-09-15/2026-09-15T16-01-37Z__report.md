## 1. Exact target and current literature status

Let `lambda` be uniform probability on `[0,1]`, let `mu=lambda⊗lambda`, and let `pi` range over all couplings with both marginals `lambda`. The target is

`W_2^2(mu,pi) <= 1/10`.

The diagonal `(id,id)_#lambda` and antidiagonal `(id,1-id)_#lambda` attain `1/10`; no uniqueness assertion is required.

## 2. Positive shuffle setup and source product coordinates

Fix a global resolution `n` and put `h=1/n`. Consider a consecutive block of `m` bins beginning at bin `k`; write

`a=kh`, `b=a+mh`.

Let `sigma` be a permutation of `{0,...,m-1}`, applied only inside this block, while the global shuffle is the identity outside the block. Thus on local branch `j`,

`x=(k+j+s)h`,
`y=(k+sigma(j)+s)h`,
`0<=s<1`.

Use normalized rotated coordinates

`U=X+Y-1`, `V=X-Y`.

The independent source `mu` has constant density `1/2` on the diamond

`D={|u|+|v|<=1}`,

and original Euclidean squared cost is

`[(Delta U)^2+(Delta V)^2]/2`.

The `U` marginal has triangular density

`rho(u)=1-|u|`, `-1<=u<=1`.

Let

`p=F_U(U)`, `q=F_U^{-1}`,
`s(u)=1-|u|`,
`z=V/s(U)`.

The change of variables is especially useful: since `dp=s(U)dU` and `dV=s(U)dz`,

`dmu = (1/2) dU dV = (1/2) dp dz`.

Hence

`p ~ Unif[0,1]`, `z ~ Unif[-1,1]`, and `p,z` are independent.          (2.1)

The exact optimal transport from `mu` to the diagonal sends rank `p` to

`(U_d',V_d')=(2p-1,0)`

and has total cost `1/10`.

## 3. Equal-slab transport for an arbitrary m-bin block

Restrict attention to the source rank band

`p in [a,b]`, `b-a=mh`.

Write

`t=(p-a)/(mh) in [0,1]`.

Partition the normalized vertical variable `z` into `m` equal intervals

`J_l=[-1+2l/m,-1+2(l+1)/m]`, `l=0,...,m-1`.

Each slab has conditional probability `1/m` for every `p`, and its mean is

`mu_l=(2l+1-m)/m`.                                                     (3.1)

For local target branch `j`, define

`s_j=j+sigma(j)`,
`d_j=j-sigma(j)`.                                                       (3.2)

The target branch in `(U,V)` coordinates is

`U_j'(t)=2a-1+h s_j+2h t`,
`V_j'=h d_j`.                                                          (3.3)

Choose a bijection beta between target branches and vertical slabs. If `z` lies in the slab assigned to branch `j`, send it to branch `j` and use the same `t` as the target branch parameter.

### Target marginal check

By (2.1), for every slab, `t` is uniform on `[0,1]` and independent of slab membership. Each slab has total source mass

`mh * (1/m) = h`,

exactly the mass of one target branch. Equation (3.3) then makes the target point uniform along that branch. Therefore the construction is an admissible transport to the required block shuffle. Outside the block rank band, keep the exact diagonal transport.

## 4. Exact block cost identity

Let

`Q(t)=q(a+mh t)`.

Define two scalar statistics of the source `U` law conditioned on the block rank interval:

`G_B = E|Q(T)-Q(T')|`,                                                  (4.1)

where `T,T'` are iid uniform `[0,1]`, and

`H_B = E[1-|Q(T)|]`.                                                    (4.2)

Thus `G_B` is the conditional Gini mean difference and `H_B` is the conditional average vertical half-width of the source diamond.

For a slab assignment beta, set

`D_beta(sigma)=sum_j d_j mu_{beta(j)}`.                                 (4.3)

A direct expansion of the new block cost minus the exact diagonal block cost gives

`Delta_beta`
`= h^2 [ m(m-1) G_B/2 - D_beta(sigma) H_B ]`.                          (4.4)

Here is the algebra. For branch `j`, compare (3.3) with the diagonal target `U_d'=2p-1`. Averaging over its slab produces

`(1/2m) int_a^b {`
`[(q-U_j')^2-(q-U_d')^2]`
`+ d_j^2 h^2 - 2 d_j h mu_{beta(j)}(1-|q|)`
`} dp`.

Use

`sum_j s_j = m(m-1)`,                                                  (4.5)

and

`sum_j (s_j^2+d_j^2)`
`=2 sum_j [j^2+sigma(j)^2]`
`=4 sum_{j=0}^{m-1}j^2`
`=2m(m-1)(2m-1)/3`.                                                    (4.6)

After writing `p=a+mh t`, all pure polynomial `h^3` terms cancel because of (4.6). The remaining horizontal term contains

`K_B=int_0^1 (1-2t)Q(t)dt`.

For an increasing quantile,

`G_B=4 int_0^1 (t-1/2)Q(t)dt = -2K_B`.                                (4.7)

The vertical correlation term is exactly `-h^2 D_beta H_B`. Combining these identities gives (4.4).

By the rearrangement inequality, the best slab assignment pairs the sorted displacement list

`{d_j=j-sigma(j)}`

with the increasing list `{mu_l}`. Define

`D(sigma)=max_beta D_beta(sigma)`.                                     (4.8)

Therefore the explicit transport proves the block shuffle whenever

`D(sigma) H_B >= m(m-1)G_B/2`.                                        (4.9)

This is an exact analytic sufficient condition valid for every block position and every global resolution.

## 5. Sharp-enough universal bounds for G_B/H_B

The conditional `U` law is the triangular density `1-|u|`, restricted to an interval of `u` values.

### 5.1 Intervals wholly on one side of zero

After reflection if necessary, write `w=1-|u|`. On a one-sided interval, after scaling its outer endpoint, the conditional density is

`f_r(x)=2x/(1-r^2)`, `r<=x<=1`,

for some `0<=r<1`.

Exact integration gives

`G_B/H_B`
`= 2(1-r)(r^2+3r+1) / [5(r+1)(r^2+r+1)]`.                             (5.1)

Moreover

`2/5 - G_B/H_B`
`=4r^2(r+2)/[5(r+1)(r^2+r+1)] >=0`.                                   (5.2)

Hence every one-sided block satisfies

`G_B/H_B <= 2/5`.                                                      (5.3)

### 5.2 Intervals crossing zero: a universal 7/10 bound

Write the support as `[-A,B]`, `0<=A,B<=1`. Its rank mass is

`L=A-A^2/2+B-B^2/2`.                                                   (5.4)

Exact integration gives

`H_B = [A-A^2+A^3/3+B-B^2+B^3/3]/L`.                                 (5.5)

The corresponding `G_B` is obtained by integrating the negative-negative, positive-positive, and cross-sign pairs under density proportional to `1-|u|`.

To certify the inequality

`G_B/H_B <= 7/10`,                                                     (5.6)

write

`7H_B-10G_B = 2 Q_7(A,B) / [3(A^2-2A+B^2-2B)^2]`.                     (5.7)

In the tensor Bernstein basis of bidegree `(5,5)` on `[0,1]^2`, the exact coefficient matrix of `Q_7` is

```
[0, 0, 21/5, 43/10, 3, 3]
[0, 84/25, 423/50, 202/25, 6, 6]
[21/5, 423/50, 651/50, 1147/100, 42/5, 42/5]
[43/10, 202/25, 1147/100, 43/5, 23/5, 23/5]
[3, 6, 42/5, 23/5, 0, 0]
[3, 6, 42/5, 23/5, 0, 0]
```

Every coefficient is nonnegative, so (5.6) holds throughout the square. Equality includes the full triangular law `A=B=1`, where `G_B/H_B=(7/15)/(2/3)=7/10`.

Together with the one-sided estimate, (5.6) is valid for **every** rank interval.

### 5.3 Stronger 2/3 bound for block rank mass at most 3/4

Suppose a crossing interval has rank mass `L<=3/4`. If both `A>1/2` and `B>1/2`, then from (5.4)

`L>2(1/2-1/8)=3/4`,

a contradiction. Thus, by symmetry, assume `A<=1/2`; write `A=x/2`, `B=y`, with `x,y in [0,1]`.

For the desired inequality

`G_B/H_B <= 2/3`,                                                     (5.8)

write

`2H_B-3G_B = 4 Q_{2/3}(x,y) / [15((x/2)^2-x+y^2-2y)^2]`.              (5.9)

The exact tensor Bernstein coefficient matrix of `Q_{2/3}` is

```
[0, 0, 3, 3, 2, 2]
[0, 6/5, 9/2, 43/10, 3, 3]
[3/4, 27/10, 483/80, 441/80, 31/8, 31/8]
[3/2, 77/20, 563/80, 489/80, 33/8, 33/8]
[2, 9/2, 297/40, 243/40, 15/4, 15/4]
[9/4, 19/4, 59/8, 45/8, 3, 3]
```

Again every coefficient is nonnegative. Therefore every block whose rank mass is at most `3/4` satisfies (5.8).

## 6. A general block-permutation criterion

Combining (4.4) with Section 5 gives immediately:

### Theorem 6.1 — block displacement criterion

For an `m`-bin consecutive block permutation `sigma`, the explicit equal-slab transport proves `W_2^2<=1/10` whenever

`D(sigma) >= [m(m-1)/2] * (G_B/H_B)`.                                 (6.1)

In particular:

1. for an arbitrary block position,

   `D(sigma) >= 7m(m-1)/20`                                            (6.2)

   is sufficient;

2. if the block rank mass `mh<=3/4`,

   `D(sigma) >= m(m-1)/3`                                              (6.3)

   is sufficient;

3. if the block lies wholly on one side of the median rank,

   `D(sigma) >= m(m-1)/5`                                              (6.4)

   is sufficient.

These are all-resolution analytic criteria; no finite permutation enumeration is used.

## 7. Complete theorem for every permutation of three consecutive bins

For `m=3`, the displacement statistic (4.8) takes the exact values

```
sigma                 D(sigma)
(0,1,2)                0
(0,2,1), (1,0,2)       4/3
(1,2,0), (2,0,1)       2
(2,1,0)                 8/3
```

We now cover every case.

### 7.1 Adjacent transpositions

The two permutations with `D=4/3` are single adjacent transpositions. The prior archived checkpoint at commit

`4405249247bbca48e9bac56dd4ae2c046f376e1b`

proved by a sharper local surgery that **every** adjacent transposition in every resolution has strict cost below `1/10`, including bands crossing the median. No new assumption is needed here.

### 7.2 Endpoint transposition / local reversal

For `sigma=(2,1,0)`,

`D=8/3`.

Using the universal ratio (5.6),

`Delta/h^2`
`<= H_B [3*(7/10)-8/3]`
`= -(17/30)H_B <0`.                                                    (7.1)

Thus every reversal of any three consecutive bins is strictly below `1/10`.

### 7.3 The two three-cycles for n>=4

For either 3-cycle,

`D=2`.

If the global resolution is `n>=4`, the block rank mass is

`3/n<=3/4`.

By (5.8),

`Delta/h^2 <= H_B[3*(2/3)-2]=0`.                                      (7.2)

Therefore both 3-cycles satisfy the conjectured bound in every resolution `n>=4`.

### 7.4 The remaining n=3 three-cycles

When `n=3`, the block is the whole interval and the ratio bound `2/3` is not available. Use instead the universal two-sample endpoint plan.

For iid `P=(A,B),Q=(A',B')~pi`, the crossed point `(A,B')` has law `mu`. Choosing whichever of `P,Q` is closer to the crossed point gives

`W_2^2(mu,pi) <= 1/6 - J(pi)/2`,                                      (7.3)

where

`J(pi)=E |(A-A')^2-(B-B')^2|`.

For either `n=3` three-cycle, an exact rational calculation gives

`J=13/81 > 2/15`.

Hence

`W_2^2 <= 1/6-13/162 = 7/81 < 1/10`.                                  (7.4)

Combining all cases proves:

### Theorem 7.1 — three-bin block theorem

**Every positive equal-bin shuffle that is the identity outside three consecutive bins and is arbitrary on those three bins satisfies**

`W_2^2(lambda⊗lambda,gamma_sigma) <= 1/10`.                            (7.5)

Every nontrivial endpoint swap is strict; adjacent swaps are strict by the prior checkpoint. No uniqueness statement is needed or claimed for the remaining cases.

## 8. Disjoint three-bin block corollary

If a shuffle differs from the identity on a collection of pairwise disjoint consecutive blocks of size at most three, apply the corresponding local transports on the disjoint source rank bands and retain the exact diagonal transport elsewhere.

The source bands are disjoint, the target mass constraints are disjoint, and the total cost change is the sum of the block changes. Therefore:

### Corollary 8.1

Every positive equal-bin shuffle whose nontrivial action is a disjoint union of permutations supported on consecutive blocks of length at most three satisfies

`W_2^2(lambda⊗lambda,gamma_sigma) <= 1/10`.                            (8.1)

This is a new infinite all-resolution family, strictly larger than the disjoint-adjacent-swap family from the previous checkpoint.

## 9. Relation to the full Catalano–Lavenant problem

The standard equal-grid/Birkhoff approximation remains the bridge from finite positive shuffles to arbitrary copulas: if every positive equal-bin permutation shuffle in every resolution obeys `1/10`, then every copula does by cellwise approximation and convexity of `W_2^2(mu,.)` in the target.

The new identity (4.4) is a candidate structural tool for that finite problem. It reduces a local block permutation to two quantities:

- a one-dimensional geometric ratio `G_B/H_B` determined only by where the block sits in rank space;
- a discrete displacement score `D(sigma)` determined only by the block permutation.

This separation is useful because the geometric side now has exact universal bounds `7/10`, `2/3`, and `2/5` in increasingly favorable regimes.

The next major obligation is to understand low-`D` permutations of larger blocks. For `m=4`, the new criterion already settles all sufficiently displaced permutations; the remaining low-displacement types are exactly the place where the previous adjacent/three-bin local surgeries should interact with a four-bin construction.
