## 1. Exact target and inherited reductions

Let `lambda` be uniform on `[0,1]`, `mu=lambda⊗lambda`, and `pi` range over copulas. The target is

`W_2^2(mu,pi) <= 1/10`.

The diagonal and antidiagonal attain `1/10`; no uniqueness claim is required.

Earlier archived checkpoints prove the quantitative continuum reduction to positive equal-bin graph shuffles. Therefore an all-resolution theorem for every finite shuffle would settle the continuum conjecture. The present checkpoint addresses only the remaining nine-bin local finite bridge.

For a local nine-bin permutation `sigma in S_9` embedded in a global `n`-bin shuffle, the archived displacement-group construction gives

`Delta = -9 h^2 [ H_B B_sigma + int_0^1 Q(t) F_sigma(t) dt ]`,

and the archived Stieltjes argument yields the sufficient condition

`B_sigma/C_sigma >= G_B/H_B`.                                      (1.1)

Here `B` is the corresponding diagonal-rank interval of length `9/n`, `H_B` and `G_B` are its exact triangular-quantile moments, and `B_sigma/C_sigma` is checked by finitely many rational quadratic inequalities.

For a target not certified by (1.1), the archived cellwise diagonal-residual construction supplies another exact sufficient condition. Let

`a_i=2i+1-n`.

A balanced cell assignment `L(r,s)` gives integer score

`W(L,sigma)=sum_{r,s}[a_r a_{L(r,s)}+a_s a_{sigma(L(r,s))}]`.

The explicit transport cost is

`C = 1/3 - W/(2n^4) - 7/(30n^2)`,

so the exact certificate is

`15 W >= 7 n^2(n^2-1)`.                                             (1.2)

The Hungarian algorithm is used only to FIND an assignment. The returned branch capacities and integer `W` are recomputed exactly. Thus a successful certificate does not depend on a floating-point optimality assertion.

Both (1.1) and (1.2) are constructive upper bounds on `W_2^2`; neither relies on classifying continuum extreme copulas by finite Birkhoff vertices.
