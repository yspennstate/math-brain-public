## 2. Positive equal-bin shuffles and rotated coordinates

Fix `n>=2` and put `h=1/n`. For a permutation `sigma` of `{0,...,n-1}`, define the positive equal-bin shuffle

`f_sigma((i+s)/n)=(sigma(i)+s)/n`, `0<=s<1`,

and graph copula

`gamma_sigma=(id,f_sigma)_#lambda`.

Use normalized rotated coordinates

`U=X+Y-1`, `V=X-Y`.

Then the product source `mu=lambda⊗lambda` becomes constant density `1/2` on the diamond

`D={|u|+|v|<=1}`,

and original squared Euclidean cost is

`[(Delta U)^2+(Delta V)^2]/2`.

The marginal law of `U` is triangular with density

`rho(u)=1-|u|`, `-1<=u<=1`.

Its quantile is

`q(p)=-1+sqrt(2p)` for `0<=p<=1/2`,

`q(p)=1-sqrt(2(1-p))` for `1/2<=p<=1`.

Let `p=F_U(U)`. Then `p` is uniform on `[0,1]`.

The exact optimal source-to-diagonal transport sends the source point with rank `p` to the diagonal point

`(U_d',V_d')=(2p-1,0)`.

Its total squared Euclidean cost is `1/10`.

## 3. One adjacent transposition: an explicit local surgery

Fix `k in {0,...,n-2}` and let `tau_k` exchange `k` and `k+1`, fixing every other index.

Set

`alpha=kh`,

`a=alpha`,

`b=alpha+2h`,

`m=alpha+h=(a+b)/2`.

The shuffle differs from the diagonal only on the target diagonal-parameter interval `[a,b]`.

In `(U,V)` coordinates, the two affected target branches are horizontal segments:

- one at `V'=-h`;
- one at `V'=+h`;

and both have the same `U'` interval

`[2alpha+h-1, 2alpha+3h-1]`.

Consider the source band

`A={p=F_U(U) in [a,b]}`.

It has source mass `2h`.

Define the transport on this band by

- if `V<0`, send to the lower branch `V'=-h`;
- if `V>0`, send to the upper branch `V'=+h`;
- in either case set

`U'=p+m-1`.

Outside `A`, keep the exact optimal diagonal transport.

### Target marginal check

Conditional on `U`, the source diamond has

`V|U=u ~ Unif[-(1-|u|),1-|u|]`.

Hence `P(V>0|U)=P(V<0|U)=1/2`. Since `p` is uniform on `[a,b]` within the band and the sign of `V` is balanced independently of the rank density, each affected target branch receives mass `h`.

Moreover `U'=p+m-1` is uniform on an interval of length `2h`; restricting to either sign leaves `p` uniform on `[a,b]`, so after the two-to-one sign split each branch obtains exactly its required uniform one-dimensional graph measure. Therefore this is a valid transport from `mu` to `gamma_{tau_k}`.

## 4. Exact cost difference from the diagonal

Let `U=q(p)`. Conditional on `U`,

`E(|V| | U)= (1-|U|)/2`.

A direct expansion of the new band cost minus the old diagonal band cost gives the **unconditional** total-cost change

`Delta(a,b)`

`= int_a^b (p-m) q(p) dp - h^2 + (h/2) int_a^b |q(p)| dp`,            (4.1)

where `h=(b-a)/2` and `m=(a+b)/2`.

Thus proving `Delta(a,b)<0` for every nondegenerate adjacent-bin interval `[a,b]` proves strict improvement over the diagonal benchmark `1/10`.

There are three geometric cases.

---

## 5. Case I: interval entirely to the left of 1/2

Assume

`0<=a<b<=1/2`.

Write

`r=sqrt(a/b)`.

Using

`q(p)=-1+sqrt(2p)`

and evaluating the elementary integrals in (4.1), exact simplification gives

`Delta(a,b)`

`= -(sqrt(2)/30) b^(5/2) (1-r)^2 (7r^3+14r^2+6r+3)`.                (5.1)

Every factor except the leading minus sign is nonnegative, and because `a<b`, `(1-r)^2>0`. Hence

`Delta(a,b)<0`.

The corresponding right-half case `1/2<=a<b<=1` follows from the symmetry

`q(1-p)=-q(p)`.

---

## 6. Case II: interval crosses 1/2

Assume

`a<1/2<b`.

Set

`r=sqrt(2a)`,

`s=sqrt(2(1-b))`.

Then `0<=r,s<=1`. Exact integration and simplification gives

`Delta(a,b)=-P(r,s)/120`,                                               (6.1)

where

`P(r,s)=`

`7r^5 + 15r^3 s^2 -30r^3 + 15r^2 s^3 -30r^2 s^2 +20r^2`

`+7s^5 -30s^3 +20s^2 +6`.                                             (6.2)

To certify `P>=0` on the whole square `[0,1]^2`, write it in the tensor Bernstein basis of bidegree `(5,5)`:

`P(r,s)=sum_{i=0}^5 sum_{j=0}^5 beta_ij B_i^5(r) B_j^5(s)`,

where

`B_i^5(x)=binom(5,i)x^i(1-x)^(5-i)`.

The exact Bernstein coefficient matrix is

```
[6, 6, 8, 9, 6, 3]
[6, 6, 8, 9, 6, 3]
[8, 8, 97/10, 41/4, 34/5, 7/2]
[9, 9, 41/4, 51/5, 63/10, 3]
[6, 6, 34/5, 63/10, 12/5, 0]
[3, 3, 7/2, 3, 0, 0]
```

Every coefficient is nonnegative. Since the Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one,

`P(r,s)>=0` on `[0,1]^2`.

For a nondegenerate crossing interval, one cannot have simultaneously `r=s=1`, and the displayed representation is strictly positive in the interior relevant to `a<b`; hence

`Delta(a,b)<0`.

## 8. Corollary: arbitrary disjoint adjacent transpositions

Suppose `sigma` is a product of pairwise disjoint adjacent transpositions. Equivalently, the affected target parameter intervals

`[k_j/n,(k_j+2)/n]`

are pairwise disjoint up to endpoints.

Apply the local transport surgery from Sections 3–6 independently on each affected source rank band, and retain the exact diagonal optimal transport on the complement.

Because the bands are disjoint, target mass constraints do not interact, and total cost change is the sum of the individual changes:

`W_2^2(mu,gamma_sigma)`

`<= 1/10 + sum_j Delta_j`.

Every nonempty swap has `Delta_j<0`. Therefore:

### Corollary 8.1

Every positive equal-bin shuffle obtained from the identity by a nonempty set of pairwise disjoint adjacent transpositions satisfies

`W_2^2(mu,gamma_sigma) < 1/10`.                                        (8.1)

This is an infinite all-resolution family, not a finite enumeration statement.

## 9. Relation to the full problem

A standard grid approximation plus Birkhoff decomposition shows that the full continuum conjecture follows if the `1/10` bound is proved for every positive equal-bin permutation shuffle in every resolution. The present theorem proves a structured but nontrivial all-n subclass of those shuffles.

The local-surgery calculation suggests a more general program: compare a finite block permutation directly against the diagonal optimal map on the corresponding rank interval. For a block of `m` consecutive target bins, the source preimage under the diagonal Brenier map is still explicit in triangular/diamond coordinates, so one can seek a finite-dimensional optimal block transport and a negative cost deficit for every nonidentity block permutation.

The immediate next target is the `m=3` consecutive-block case. If every nontrivial 3-bin permutation admits a negative local deficit uniformly over block position, then arbitrary permutations decomposable into disjoint small local blocks become analytically controlled. A stronger block lemma with constants depending only on inversion structure could form the missing bridge to all finite shuffles.

## 10. Failed / insufficient routes in this iteration

   `tau(x)=x(1-x)/2`.

   This points to a weighted anisotropic coarea inequality on the square, but the needed sharp isoperimetric statement with the correct `1/10` constant has not been established here.

3. **Finite matching evidence.** Previous computational matching patterns are suggestive but cannot replace an all-n proof. The new local theorem is analytic and independent of those enumerations.
