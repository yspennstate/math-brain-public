`W_2^2(mu,pi) <= 1/10`.

## 2. Recalled exact consecutive-block transport identity

Fix global resolution `n`, `h=1/n`, and a block of `m` consecutive bins with rank interval

`B=[a,a+mh]`.

Use rotated coordinates

`U=X+Y-1`, `V=X-Y`.

The independent source is uniform with density `1/2` on the diamond `|U|+|V|<=1`, and original squared Euclidean cost is half the squared `(U,V)` cost.

Let `p=F_U(U)` and `q=F_U^{-1}` for the triangular `U` marginal. Put

`z=V/(1-|U|)`.

Then `p` and `z` are independent, with

`p~Unif[0,1]`, `z~Unif[-1,1]`.

For a local permutation `sigma in S_m`, define displacements

`d_j=j-sigma(j)`.

Partition `z` into `m` equal slabs with means

`mu_l=(2l+1-m)/m`.

The archived block construction assigns slabs bijectively to branches and preserves the within-block horizontal rank. If

`G_B=E|Q(T)-Q(T')|`, `Q(t)=q(a+mh t)`,

and

`H_B=E[1-|Q(T)|]`,

then the exact cost change relative to the diagonal-optimal transport is

`Delta/h^2 = [m(m-1)/2] G_B - D(sigma) H_B`,                 (2.1)

where

`D(sigma)=max_beta sum_j d_j mu_{beta(j)}`.                  (2.2)

By rearrangement, `D(sigma)` is obtained by pairing the sorted displacement list with the increasing slab means.

Thus the block is certified whenever

`D(sigma) >= [m(m-1)/2] (G_B/H_B)`.                        (2.3)

The archived report also proves:

* every one-sided rank interval satisfies `G_B/H_B<=2/5`;
* every rank interval satisfies `G_B/H_B<=7/10`;
* blocks of rank mass at most `3/4` satisfy `G_B/H_B<=2/3`.

## 3. New short-rank-interval lemma

### Lemma 3.1

If a rank interval has length `L<=1/3`, then

`G_B/H_B <= 2/5`.                                         (3.1)

### Proof

If the interval lies wholly on one side of median rank `1/2`, this is the archived exact `2/5` inequality.

Suppose instead that the interval crosses `1/2`. Let `P,P'` be iid uniform on the rank interval. Because the interval has length `L` and crosses `1/2`,

`P in [1/2-L, 1/2+L]`.

The triangular quantile is

`q(p)=-1+sqrt(2p)` for `p<=1/2`,

`q(p)=1-sqrt(2(1-p))` for `p>=1/2`.

Hence on the interval,

`|q'(p)| <= 1/sqrt(1-2L)`.                                (3.2)

Also

`1-|q(p)| >= sqrt(1-2L)`.                                 (3.3)

Therefore

`G_B=E|q(P)-q(P')|`
`<= [1/sqrt(1-2L)] E|P-P'|`
`= L/[3 sqrt(1-2L)]`,                                     (3.4)

because the mean absolute difference of two iid uniforms on an interval of length `L` is `L/3`.

Meanwhile

`H_B=E[1-|q(P)|] >= sqrt(1-2L)`.                          (3.5)

Thus

`G_B/H_B <= L/[3(1-2L)] <= 1/3 < 2/5`                    (3.6)

for `L<=1/3`. QED.

This simple lemma is useful because it turns the local block problem at sufficiently fine global resolution into a purely finite displacement inequality.

## 5. A self-contained cellwise diagonal-residual certificate

The five-bin proof below needs one finite fallback construction. Because an earlier report containing this idea was not successfully archived, the full derivation is recorded here rather than assumed as history.

Partition source and target into `n` equal bins and define centered integer coordinates

`a_i=2i+1-n`.

Take a balanced assignment `L(r,s)` of the `n^2` source cells `(r,s)` to target branch labels `i`, with each branch label receiving exactly `n` cells.

Inside source cell `(r,s)`, write

`X=(r+R)/n`, `Y=(s+S)/n`

with iid `R,S~Unif[0,1]`.

For a cell assigned to branch `i`, the target point is

`X'=(i+U)/n`, `Y'=(sigma(i)+U)/n`.

Choose `U` by the exact optimal diagonal coupling of the residual square: let

`M=(R+S)/2`

and let `U=F_M(M)`. Then `U` is uniform. Moreover

`E[(R-U)^2+(S-U)^2]=1/10`.                             (5.1)

Indeed,

`(R-U)^2+(S-U)^2=(R-S)^2/2+2(M-U)^2`.

The first expectation is `1/12`; direct one-dimensional quantile integration for the midpoint law gives

`W_2^2(Law(M),Unif[0,1])=1/120`,

so (5.1) is `1/12+2/120=1/10`.

Also

`E(R-U)=E(S-U)=0`,

so cell-center/residual cross terms vanish.

Define the integer weight

`W(L,sigma)=sum_{r,s} [a_r a_{L(r,s)} + a_s a_{sigma(L(r,s))}]`.    (5.2)

A direct expansion gives the total transport cost

`C = 1/3 - W(L,sigma)/(2n^4) - 7/(30n^2)`.             (5.3)

Therefore

`C<=1/10`

whenever

`15 W(L,sigma) >= 7 n^2(n^2-1)`.                       (5.4)

This is an exact integer certificate.

The checker uses a Hungarian maximum-weight matching only to *find* a balanced assignment. It then independently recomputes branch capacities and `W` using exact integers. Hence successful certificates do not rely on floating-point optimality of the matching algorithm.

## 7. Disjoint block corollary

All local constructions above modify the exact diagonal transport only on the source rank interval corresponding to the block and send that interval into the matching target block.

Therefore constructions on disjoint consecutive rank blocks can be combined without interference, and their cost changes add.

### Corollary 7.1

Every positive equal-bin shuffle whose nontrivial action is a disjoint union of consecutive permutation blocks of size at most five satisfies

`W_2^2(mu,gamma)<=1/10`.

This strictly extends the previously archived disjoint adjacent- and three-bin results.

## 10. Failed / insufficient routes this run

A direct attempt to prove the stronger statement

`G_B/H_B<=2/5`

for every rank interval of mass at most `1/2` led to a nontrivial constrained polynomial positivity problem. Numerical evidence supports it, but no clean global proof was completed here.

That stronger statement turned out not to be necessary. The simpler Lipschitz argument for intervals of mass at most `1/3`, combined with exact finite bridge checks, is sufficient for the four- and five-bin theorems.

The equal-slab construction also reveals its first structural barrier at six bins: the minimum `D` among genuinely indecomposable `S_6` permutations is `5`, attained by the two cyclic six-shifts, while the short-interval `2/5` threshold for `m=6` is

`[6*5/2]*(2/5)=6`.

So the present equal-slab criterion alone cannot close six bins. A new transport idea is genuinely required for those minimally displaced six-cycles.

## 11. Next attack

The immediate target is the six-consecutive-bin theorem.

Exact finite enumeration shows that the smallest displacement among genuinely indecomposable six-bin permutations is

`D=5`,

attained only by the two cyclic shifts

`(1,2,3,4,5,0)` and `(5,0,1,2,3,4)`.

Most indecomposable six-bin permutations have larger `D` and should be handled by the existing block identity once the block rank mass is small enough.

The next promising route is therefore highly localized:

1. classify the low-`D` indecomposable `S_6` types exactly;
2. isolate the two cyclic six-shifts as the principal obstruction;
3. construct a specialized cyclic-block transport, analogous to the previously successful circle-translation and adjacent-tail surgeries, that correlates branch choice with both horizontal rank and normalized vertical coordinate rather than using equal vertical slabs;
4. use the block identity for the remaining high-`D` six-bin classes.

If the six-bin cyclic shift admits a uniform negative local deficit, the same architecture is likely to continue inductively: low-displacement indecomposable `m`-blocks appear to be the two cyclic shifts, while high-displacement blocks are accessible to the generic slab criterion.
