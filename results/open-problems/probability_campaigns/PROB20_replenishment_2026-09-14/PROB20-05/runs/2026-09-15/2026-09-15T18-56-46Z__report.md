## 1. Block coordinates

Fix global resolution `n`, mesh `h=1/n`, and a local block of `m` consecutive bins. Let its diagonal target-rank interval be

`B=[a,a+mh]`.

Use rotated coordinates

`U=X+Y-1`, `V=X-Y`.

Original Euclidean squared cost is one half of squared Euclidean cost in `(U,V)`.

Let `p=F_U(U)`, where the `U` marginal under the independent source has triangular density `1-|u|` on `[-1,1]`. Conditional on `p in B`, write

`t=(p-a)/(mh) in [0,1]`,
`Q(t)=F_U^{-1}(a+mh t)`,
`A(t)=1-|Q(t)|`,
`z=V/A(t)`.

Then, conditional on the source block,

`t ~ Unif[0,1]`, `z ~ Unif[-1,1]`,

and `t,z` are independent.

Write

`C=2a+mh-1`, `w=2t-1`.

The diagonal-optimal target on the block is

`(U_0,V_0)=(C+mh w,0)`.

Define

`H_B = int_0^1 A(t) dt`,

and the Gini mean difference

`G_B = E|Q(T)-Q(T')| = 4 int_0^1 (t-1/2)Q(t)dt`,

for iid uniform `T,T'`.

We will also use the median-split moment

`S_B = int_{1/2}^1 Q(t)dt - int_0^{1/2} Q(t)dt`.

## 2. New displacement-group aggregation transport

Let `sigma in S_m` be the local shuffle permutation. For branch `j` define

`d_j=j-sigma(j)`,
`c_j=j+sigma(j)+1-m`.

In block-centered rotated coordinates, branch `j` is the horizontal segment

`V=d_j h`,
`U in C+h[c_j-1,c_j+1]`.

Group branches by their common displacement. For each distinct displacement `d`, let

`J_d={j:d_j=d}`, `k_d=|J_d|`, `alpha_d=k_d/m`.

Order the displacement groups increasingly in `d`. Partition the source `z` interval into consecutive intervals of probabilities `alpha_d` in this same order. Let `mu_d` be the mean of the source `z` interval for group `d`.

Within a fixed displacement group, the target horizontal law is uniform on the union

`union_{j in J_d} [c_j-1,c_j+1]`.

Because equal-displacement branches have disjoint or touching intervals of equal length, define `R_d(t)` to be the increasing quantile of this union law, with `t` uniform on `[0,1]`.

Map the source group to

`U'=C+h R_d(t)`,
`V'=h d`.

This is an admissible transport: the `z` group has exactly the target mass `alpha_d`; `t` remains uniform because it is independent of `z`; and the monotone map `R_d` fills every branch in the group uniformly.

Define the averaged horizontal quantile error

`F_sigma(t)=sum_d alpha_d R_d(t)-m(2t-1)`,                 (2.1)

and vertical score

`B_sigma=sum_d alpha_d d mu_d`.                          (2.2)

The overall shuffle block and the diagonal block have the same two coordinate marginals, hence the same target second moment. Expanding the source-target cross moment therefore gives the exact global cost change

`Delta = -m h^2 [ H_B B_sigma + int_0^1 Q(t)F_sigma(t)dt ]`.     (2.3)

Also

`int_0^1 F_sigma(t)dt=0`.                                (2.4)

### 2.1 A one-dimensional convex-order bound

Let

`A_sigma(s)=int_0^s F_sigma(t)dt`.

Suppose `c>=0` satisfies

`A_sigma(s) <= 2c s(1-s)` for every `0<=s<=1`.           (2.5)

Since `w(t)=2t-1`, the cumulative integral of

`F_sigma+2c w`

is

`A_sigma(s)-2c s(1-s)<=0`,

and its total integral is zero. Because `Q` is nondecreasing, Stieltjes integration by parts gives

`int_0^1 Q(t)[F_sigma(t)+2c w(t)]dt >=0`.

Using

`G_B=2 int_0^1 Q(t)w(t)dt`,

we obtain

`int Q F_sigma >= -c G_B`.                              (2.6)

Therefore the displacement-group plan proves the conjectured bound whenever

`B_sigma H_B >= c G_B`.                                 (2.7)

Define the sharp constant for this plan

`C_sigma = sup_{0<s<1} A_sigma(s)/(2s(1-s))`.           (2.8)

Then a sufficient condition is

`B_sigma/C_sigma >= G_B/H_B`.                           (2.9)

This is the central new reduction of this checkpoint.

### 2.2 Exact finite certification of `C_sigma`

For a finite shuffle, every `R_d` is piecewise affine with rational coefficients and rational breakpoints, hence so is `F_sigma`. On one affine piece,

`A_sigma(s)=u s^2+v s+w`

with rational coefficients. To certify a rational threshold `rho` in

`B_sigma/C_sigma >= rho`,

it is enough to verify

`A_sigma(s) <= 2(B_sigma/rho)s(1-s)`                    (2.10)

piecewise. The difference is a rational quadratic, so its exact maximum is checked at the two endpoints and, if concave, its rational vertex. No floating-point extremization is used.

## 4. Two triangular-quantile inequalities

These estimates close the asymptotic/small-rank regime analytically.

### 4.1 `G_B/H_B <= 2/5` for rank intervals of length at most `1/3`

If the rank interval is one-sided, say `0<=a<b<=1/2`, put

`r=sqrt(a/b)`.

Direct integration gives

`G_B/H_B = 2(1-r)(r^2+3r+1) / [5(1+r)(r^2+r+1)]`.       (4.1)

Its derivative is

`-4r(4r^2+7r+4) / [5(1+r)^2(r^2+r+1)^2] <=0`.          (4.2)

Thus its maximum is the endpoint value `2/5` at `r=0`. The right-sided case follows by reflection.

If the interval crosses rank `1/2` and has length `L<=1/3`, then every rank point lies in `[1/6,5/6]`. Hence

`A(t)=1-|Q(t)| >= 1/sqrt(3)`,

and

`Q'(t)=L/A(t) <= sqrt(3)L`.

Therefore

`G_B <= Lip(Q) E|T-T'| <= sqrt(3)L/3`,

while

`H_B>=1/sqrt(3)`.

Consequently

`G_B/H_B <= L <=1/3 <2/5`.                              (4.3)

### 4.2 New median-split inequality `S_B >= (2/3)G_B`

This extra moment is needed for Type A.

For an increasing quantile `Q`, Stieltjes integration gives

`S_B=int_0^1 min(t,1-t)dQ(t)`,

`G_B=2int_0^1 t(1-t)dQ(t)`.                             (4.4)

Hence

`3S_B-2G_B = int_0^1 K(t)dQ(t)`,                         (4.5)

where

`K(t)=3min(t,1-t)-4t(1-t)`.

For a one-sided triangular-quantile interval, after removing an irrelevant positive scale and writing

`Q(t)=sqrt(r^2+(1-r^2)t)`, `0<=r<1`,

direct integration reduces `3S_B-2G_B>=0` to

`P(r) >= 15sqrt(2)(1+r)(1+r^2)^(3/2)`,                  (4.6)

where

`P(r)=22r^4+22r^3+32r^2+22r+22`.

Both sides are nonnegative, and the exact square difference is

`P(r)^2 - 450(1+r)^2(1+r^2)^3`

`=2(1-r)^2[17r^6+68r^5+165r^4+100r^3+165r^2+68r+17] >=0`.   (4.7)

Thus the one-sided inequality holds for every length.

For a crossing interval of length `L<=1/3`, as above

`L <= Q'(t) <= sqrt(3)L`.

The kernel `K` is negative only on `(0,1/4)` and `(3/4,1)`. Exact elementary integration gives

`int_{K<0} |K(t)|dt = 1/48`,

`int_{K>0} K(t)dt = 5/48`.

Therefore

`3S_B-2G_B`

`>= L(5/48)-sqrt(3)L(1/48)`

`= L(5-sqrt(3))/48 >0`.                                 (4.8)

Hence every rank interval of length at most `1/3` satisfies

`S_B >= (2/3)G_B`.                                      (4.9)

## 10. Relation to the continuum conjecture

The already established grid/Birkhoff approximation says that the full continuum conjecture follows if the `1/10` inequality is proved for every positive finite equal-bin shuffle at every resolution. This does not classify continuum extreme copulas by finite Birkhoff vertices; it is an approximation plus convexity argument.

The present theorem advances the exact local-block frontier from six to seven bins. It is still insufficient for arbitrary finite permutations, so the full Catalano–Lavenant conjecture remains open.

A blanket claim for arbitrary unions of seven-bin blocks is deliberately not made here: the `n=7,...,13` bridge sometimes uses a global cell certificate rather than a source-rank-local surgery. For `n>=14`, the seven-bin proof is local and can be spliced with other disjoint local rank-block constructions, but that limited corollary is not needed for the main claim.

This is not repaired by sharper bookkeeping; it requires a different coupling. The explicit two-horizontal-band/seven-vertical-slab endpoint construction supplies a large strict cross-moment margin.

5. **Finite computation is kept subordinate to exact certificates.** It is used only for the bounded bridge `n=7,...,13` and exact symbolic checks `n=14,...,20`; the entire `n>=21` regime is handled analytically after a finite exact `S_7` structural enumeration.
