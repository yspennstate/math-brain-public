## 4. New theorem: one reflected branch is strictly subextremal

> **Theorem 4.1 (one-flip exclusion away from an asymmetric center crossing).**
> Let `0<=a<b<=1` and `I=[a,b]`.
>
> 1. If `I subset [0,1/2]`, put
>    \[
>    u=\sqrt{a/2},\qquad v=\sqrt{b/2}.
>    \]
>    Then
>    \[
>    W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_I)
>    \le \frac1{10}
>    -\Gamma_-(a,b),
>    \]
>    where
>    \[
>    \boxed{
>    \Gamma_-(a,b)=\frac4{15}(v-u)^2
>    \left(12u^3+19u^2v+6uv^2+3v^3\right)>0.
>    }
>    \]
> 2. The same statement holds for `I subset [1/2,1]` by the square isometry `(x,y)->(1-x,1-y)`.
> 3. If `I=[a,1-a]` is symmetric about `1/2`, with `0<a<1/2` and
>    \[
>    r=\sqrt{a/2},
>    \]
>    then
>    \[
>    W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_I)
>    \le \frac1{10}-\Gamma_0(a),
>    \]
>    where
>    \[
>    \boxed{
>    \Gamma_0(a)=\frac83 r^2(1-2r)^2(1+2r)>0.
>    }
>    \]
>
> Thus every proper interval covered by these cases is strictly below the conjectured maximum.

The theorem deliberately does not claim the remaining asymmetric intervals crossing `1/2`; they are not needed for the equal-bin one-flip corollary below.

### Corollary 4.2 (complete equal-bin one-flip family)

Fix `m>=2` and `j in {0,...,m-1}`. Define

\[
T_{m,j}(x)=
\begin{cases}
(2j+1)/m-x,&x\in[j/m,(j+1)/m],\\
x,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2\bigl(\lambda^{\otimes2},(\mathrm{id},T_{m,j})_\#\lambda\bigr)<\frac1{10}
}
\]

for every `m>=2` and every branch `j`.

Indeed, every equal bin lies entirely in one half of `[0,1]`, except when `m` is odd and `j=(m-1)/2`; in that unique crossing case the bin is symmetric about `1/2`, so Theorem 4.1(3) applies.

For the endpoint reversal `j=0`, the bound simplifies to

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2\bigl(\lambda^{\otimes2},(\mathrm{id},T_{m,0})_\#\lambda\bigr)
\le \frac1{10}-\frac{\sqrt2}{10m^{5/2}}.
}
\]

For the central branch when `m` is odd, write

\[
\sigma_m=\sqrt{1-1/m}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2\le\frac1{10}
-\frac{2}{3m}\left(1-\frac1m\right)(1-\sigma_m).
}
\]

These are explicit continuum transport certificates, not discretization error bounds.

## 5. Proof of Theorem 4.1

### 5.1 Recovered diagonal transport used as the baseline

Use the rotated coordinates

\[
s=\frac{x+y}{2},\qquad d=\frac{x-y}{2}.
\]

Then `dx dy = 2 ds dd`, and the unit square becomes

\[
\Omega=\{(s,d):0\le s\le1,\ |d|\le \min(s,1-s)\}.
\]

The preceding report already established the exact diagonal benchmark and the optimal diagonal plan. We reuse it rather than re-deriving it. Its parameter is

\[
z=F_S(s)=
\begin{cases}
2s^2,&0\le s\le1/2,\\
1-2(1-s)^2,&1/2\le s\le1,
\end{cases}
\]

which is uniform on `[0,1]`. It sends `(s,d)` to the diagonal target point `(z,0)` in rotated coordinates and has total cost exactly `1/10`.

The Euclidean squared cost in rotated coordinates is

\[
|(x,y)-(x',y')|^2=2\bigl((s-s')^2+(d-d')^2\bigr).
\]

We modify this known diagonal plan only on the source set whose diagonal target parameter `z` lies in `I`.

### 5.2 Lower-half interval `I=[a,b] subset [0,1/2]`

Put

\[
u=\sqrt{a/2},\qquad v=\sqrt{b/2},\qquad h=b-a=2(v^2-u^2),\qquad c=\frac{a+b}{2}=u^2+v^2.
\]

Because `F_S(s)=2s^2` on the lower half, the source mass previously sent to diagonal parameters in `I` is exactly

\[
A=\{(s,d):u\le s\le v,\ |d|\le s\}.
\]

Its mass is

\[
2\int_u^v\int_{-s}^s dd\,ds=2(v^2-u^2)=h.
\]

The unnormalized marginal density of `d` on `A` is even and equals

\[
f_A(d)=
\begin{cases}
2(v-u),&|d|\le u,\\
2(v-|d|),&u\le|d|\le v,\\
0,&|d|>v.
\end{cases}
\]

Define the odd map `G` by, for `d>=0`,

\[
G(d)=
\begin{cases}
2(v-u)d,&0\le d\le u,\\
2vd-d^2-u^2,&u\le d\le v,
\end{cases}
\qquad G(-d)=-G(d).
\]

Then

\[
G'(d)=f_A(d)\quad(d>0),
\]

and

\[
G(v)=v^2-u^2=\frac h2.
\]

Therefore `G` pushes the restricted source measure on `A` to Lebesgue measure of total mass `h` on `[-h/2,h/2]`: equivalently, its conditional output is uniform on that interval.

Now define a transport to `pi_I` as follows.

- Outside `A`, keep the recovered diagonal transport `(s,d)->(F_S(s),0)`. Since `F_S(s) notin I`, this lands on the unchanged diagonal part of the graph of `T_I`.
- On `A`, send
  \[
  (s,d)\longmapsto(c,G(d)).
  \]

In original coordinates this target point is

\[
(c+G(d),c-G(d)).
\]

Because `c+G(d)` is uniform on `[a,b]` and

\[
c-G(d)=a+b-(c+G(d))=T_I(c+G(d)),
\]

the image of `A` is exactly the reflected graph segment with its required Lebesgue parameter mass. Hence the full target law is exactly `pi_I`.

It remains to compute the cost. For a positive `d`, define

\[
D(s,d;g)=2\Bigl[(s-c)^2+(d-g)^2-(s-2s^2)^2-d^2\Bigr].
\]

This is the pointwise Euclidean squared-cost change relative to the recovered diagonal plan. Using `dxdy=2 dsdd` and the symmetry `d<->-d`, the total cost change is

\[
\Delta_-(u,v)=4\left[
\int_0^u\int_u^v D\bigl(s,d,2(v-u)d\bigr)\,ds\,dd
+
\int_u^v\int_d^v D\bigl(s,d,2vd-d^2-u^2\bigr)\,ds\,dd
\right].
\]

Both integrands are polynomials in `s,d,u,v`, so these are elementary exact integrals. Expanding and integrating gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_-(u,v)
=-\frac4{15}(v-u)^2
\left(12u^3+19u^2v+6uv^2+3v^3\right).
}
\]

Since `0<=u<v`, every factor in the parenthesis is nonnegative and the last term is strictly positive. Thus `Delta_-<0`, proving part 1. Part 2 follows because the simultaneous reflection `(x,y)->(1-x,1-y)` preserves `lambda^2`, Euclidean cost, and the one-flip graph family while carrying an upper-half interval to a lower-half interval.

For `a=0`, `u=0` and `v=sqrt(h/2)`, so

\[
\Delta_-=-\frac{\sqrt2}{10}h^{5/2}.
\]

This gives the endpoint formula in Corollary 4.2 after `h=1/m`.

### 5.3 Symmetric interval `I=[a,1-a]`

Let

\[
r=\sqrt{a/2},\qquad h=1-2a=1-4r^2,
\]

so the reflected graph segment is vertical at `c=1/2` in `(s,d)` coordinates. The source mass whose recovered diagonal parameter lies in `I` is

\[
A_0=\{(s,d):r\le s\le1-r,\ |d|\le\min(s,1-s)\}.
\]

For `d>=0`, its unnormalized `d`-marginal density is

\[
f_0(d)=
\begin{cases}
2(1-2r),&0\le d\le r,\\
2(1-2d),&r\le d\le1/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Define the odd map `G_0` by

\[
G_0(d)=
\begin{cases}
2(1-2r)d,&0\le d\le r,\\
2d-2d^2-2r^2,&r\le d\le1/2,
\end{cases}
\qquad G_0(-d)=-G_0(d).
\]

Then `G_0'=f_0` for positive `d` away from the join and

\[
G_0(1/2)=\frac12-2r^2=\frac h2.
\]

Hence `G_0` pushes the restricted source measure to the required uniform vertical segment `[-h/2,h/2]`. Map `A_0` to `(1/2,G_0(d))` and keep the recovered diagonal transport outside `A_0`. This again produces exactly `pi_I`.

By the simultaneous symmetries `d<->-d` and `s<->1-s`, it is enough to integrate over `d>=0` and `r<=s<=1/2`. On that lower half the recovered diagonal parameter is still `z=2s^2`. Put

\[
D_0(s,d;g)=2\Bigl[(s-1/2)^2+(d-g)^2-(s-2s^2)^2-d^2\Bigr].
\]

Including the source Jacobian and both symmetries, the total cost change is

\[
\Delta_0(r)=8\left[
\int_0^r\int_r^{1/2}D_0\bigl(s,d,2(1-2r)d\bigr)\,ds\,dd
+
\int_r^{1/2}\int_d^{1/2}D_0\bigl(s,d,2d-2d^2-2r^2\bigr)\,ds\,dd
\right].
\]

Exact polynomial integration gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_0(r)=-\frac83r^2(1-2r)^2(1+2r).
}
\]

For a proper symmetric interval `0<a<1/2`, we have `0<r<1/2`, so this is strictly negative. This proves part 3.

For the central bin of an odd equal partition,

\[
a=\frac{m-1}{2m},\qquad 2r=\sqrt{1-1/m},
\]

and the factor simplifies exactly to

\[
-\frac{2}{3m}\left(1-\frac1m\right)\left(1-\sqrt{1-1/m}\right),
\]

proving the central formula in Corollary 4.2.
