## 4. Exact statement that currently blocks the source conjecture

The useful obstruction is easiest to state after rewriting the quadratic transport objective as a maximal-correlation problem. This is an elementary reformulation of the existing target, not a claimed new theorem.

Let

\[
\mu=\lambda^{\otimes2},
\]

and let `pi` be any copula on `[0,1]^2`. Since both `mu` and `pi` have uniform one-dimensional marginals,

\[
\int |x|^2\,d\mu(x)=\int |y|^2\,d\pi(y)=\frac23.
\]

Thus the quadratic transport identity gives

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi)
=
\frac43
-2\sup_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\pi)}
\int x\cdot y\,d\gamma(x,y).
\]

Therefore the Catalano-Lavenant target is equivalent to the universal lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\gamma\in\Pi(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi)}
\int x\cdot y\,d\gamma(x,y)
\ge \frac{37}{60}
\qquad\text{for every bivariate uniform copula }\pi.
}
\tag{BLOCK}
\]

Because the source measure is absolutely continuous, the quadratic optimal coupling may be represented by a Brenier map `T=grad phi` to `pi`. In that language the missing estimate is

\[
\int_{[0,1]^2} x\cdot T(x)\,dx\ge\frac{37}{60},
\qquad
T_\#\lambda^{\otimes2}=\pi,
\]

under the additional information that each coordinate of `T(X)` is uniform. Centering shows what quantitative strength is missing:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^2 \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,T_i(X))\ge\frac7{60}.
\tag{QMON}
\]

Cyclic monotonicity alone gives only a nonnegative total covariance: applying monotonicity of `T` to two independent source points and averaging yields a bound at the level `>=0`, not the required `7/60`. In other words, the source conjecture demands a **quantitative** monotonicity gain forced jointly by (i) cyclic monotonicity and (ii) the two uniform target-coordinate marginals. None of the recovered routes contains such a quantitative principle.

That is the blocking statement for this track.

## 5. B11 — DUALIZE: what it exposes and why it does not close

### Idea written before computation

For fixed `pi`, Kantorovich duality writes

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi)
=
\sup_{f(x)+g(y)\le |x-y|^2}
\left(\int f\,d\mu+\int g\,d\pi\right).
\]

If one now takes the supremum over all copulas `pi`, the inner extremization over `pi` of `int g dpi` is itself an optimal-transport/assignment problem with prescribed uniform marginals. This is exactly the kind of second dualization one would like: replace the unknown copula by one-dimensional potentials `a(y_1)+b(y_2)` majorizing `g(y_1,y_2)`.

### Precise failure

The resulting object still asks for a **uniform bound over every admissible quadratic Kantorovich potential `g`**. The first dual is a supremum, and maximizing a convex functional over copulas does not create the concave-convex saddle structure needed to reverse the problematic optimization order into one explicit universal certificate. Put differently, this dualization repackages `(BLOCK)` but does not supply the missing quantitative estimate `(QMON)`.

The useful information gained is structural: a successful dual proof must produce a reusable domination principle that converts a two-variable quadratic transport potential into one-dimensional marginal potentials with total slack exactly at the `1/10` threshold. Merely solving a finite LP or writing the standard dual does not do this.

**Outcome B11: no metric movement; no theorem claim.** The route isolates the missing universal dual domination inequality.

## 6. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT: why graph copulas are insufficient

### Idea written before computation

The set of bivariate copulas is convex and weakly compact, while `pi -> W_2^2(mu,pi)` is continuous on the compact square and convex in its second argument. Thus an extremal search naturally points toward the extreme points of the copula set.

### What the literature says and why it blocks the naive reduction

Finite Birkhoff-von Neumann intuition is not enough. The continuum extreme-copula literature is substantially richer. Ghosh-Bhandari (arXiv:1709.02472) states that an extreme copula must be singular and gives sufficient constructions; survey notes citing Losert record that continuum extreme copulas can even have full support. Older Douglas/Lindenstrauss characterizations are functional-analytic rather than a classification by permutation graphs.

Therefore the finite slogan `extreme = permutation` does **not** justify restricting the present maximization to measure-preserving graph maps, much less to finite shuffles. The long graph-copula program recovered in this track is valuable evidence and yields many exact partials, but it does not identify the full extremal class required by the source statement.

A successful B24 route would need either:

1. a new structural theorem showing that **for this particular quadratic-distance functional**, some maximizing extreme copula may be chosen from a tractable graph-like subclass; or
2. a direct argument on arbitrary singular extreme copulas using their support/conditional structure, without classifying them all.

Neither statement is present in recovered history or in the inspected literature.

**Outcome B24: no metric movement; no theorem claim.** It rules out a logically invalid shortcut and sharpens the scope of any future extremal reduction.

## 7. B15 — BREAK DIAGONAL SYMMETRY: why first variation is singular/circular

### Idea written before computation

The obvious counterexample attack is to leave the diagonal in a direction that preserves both marginals exactly: move a small amount of diagonal mass through a balanced local four-cycle, or equivalently perturb by a signed measure whose horizontal and vertical marginals vanish.

### Precise failure of the simplest perturbation

If the perturbation is merely a convex mixture

\[
\pi_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\pi_{\rm diag}+\varepsilon\nu,

with another copula `nu`, convexity of `W_2^2(mu,.)` gives

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi_\varepsilon)
\le
(1-\varepsilon)\frac1{10}

Such a perturbation cannot reveal an excess unless `nu` is already a counterexample, so this direction is circular.

The non-mixture directions are subtler because the diagonal baseline is singular: the negative part of a marginal-preserving signed perturbation has to be removed from a one-dimensional support while its positive part may live off that support. ## 9. What a full proof would need that no recovered route contains

A proof by the current optimal-transport language would need at least one of the following genuinely new ingredients.

**Quantitative cyclic monotonicity.** Prove that a cyclically monotone map from the independent square whose two output coordinates are exactly uniform has

\[
\sum_i \operatorname{Cov}(X_i,T_i(X))\ge 7/60.
\]

Ordinary monotonicity supplies only `>=0`, leaving the whole quantitative gap.

**Universal dual domination.** Show that every quadratic Kantorovich target potential relevant to a copula can be majorized, after the second marginal dualization, by one-dimensional functions with total dual value at most `1/10`.

**Functional-specific extremal reduction.** Prove that although arbitrary extreme copulas are complicated, at least one maximizer of this specific Wasserstein functional must have a special support (for example graph-like, finitely cyclic, or otherwise transport-compatible) on which an exact rearrangement argument works.

A counterexample, conversely, must be a genuine continuum copula with exact uniform marginals plus a rigorous **lower bound** on its `W_2^2` value exceeding `1/10`; a grid optimum without continuum extension is insufficient.
