## 4. Idea before computation

The previous stuck report identified the simplest unresolved genuinely two-dimensional family as the `2 x 2` checkerboards

\[
f_\theta(x,y)=1+\theta s(x)s(y),\qquad -1\le\theta\le1,

where `s=+1` on the left half of `[0,1]` and `s=-1` on the right half.

The new idea is to avoid a two-dimensional LP entirely. Instead, preserve `y` exactly and transport only the `x` coordinate **conditional on `y`**. For a two-column copula, each conditional `x`-law is just a binary redistribution of uniform mass between two intervals. Its monotone one-dimensional quantile map can be written exactly. ## 5. New theorem — all two-slab conditional-density copulas

### Theorem 5.1

Fix `0<p<1`. Let

\[
\delta:[0,1]\to[-p,1-p]
\]

be measurable and satisfy

\[
\int_0^1\delta(y)\,dy=0.
\]

Define a density on `[0,1]^2` by

\[
f_\delta(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
1+\dfrac{\delta(y)}p,&0\le x\le p,\\[1ex]
1-\dfrac{\delta(y)}{1-p},&p<x\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `pi_delta` be the corresponding probability measure. Then `pi_delta` has both marginals equal to `lambda`, and

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_\delta)
\le \frac13\int_0^1\delta(y)^2\,dy
\le \frac{p(1-p)}3
\le \frac1{12}<\frac1{10}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently every nontrivial or trivial member of this entire continuum family is rigorously excluded as a counterexample to the Catalano–Lavenant conjecture. In fact the displayed transport certificate places the whole family at least `1/60` below the diagonal benchmark.

### Proof

The range condition on `delta` gives nonnegativity in both lines of (5.1). For every fixed `y`,

\[
\int_0^1 f_\delta(x,y)\,dx
=p+\delta(y)+(1-p)-\delta(y)=1,
\]

so the `y` marginal is uniform. For `x\le p`,

\[
\int_0^1f_\delta(x,y)\,dy
=1+\frac1p\int_0^1\delta(y)\,dy=1,
\]

and similarly the integral equals `1` for `x>p`, so the `x` marginal is uniform.

Now fix `y` and abbreviate `d=delta(y)` and `c=p+d`. The conditional target `x`-law has total mass `c` on `[0,p]` and total mass `1-c` on `[p,1]`, with constant density on each interval. Its quantile map from `U~Unif[0,1]` is

\[
Q_d(u)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{pu}{p+d},&0\le u\le p+d,\\[1.2ex]
p+\dfrac{(1-p)(u-p-d)}{1-p-d},&p+d\le u\le1.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

At the endpoint cases `d=-p` or `d=1-p`, the empty branch is omitted and (5.3) is understood by its direct limit. Therefore

\[
T(u,y)=(Q_{\delta(y)}(u),y)
\]

pushes `lambda tensor lambda` to `pi_delta`. It is an admissible continuum transport; no grid or limiting argument is being used.

On the first branch,

\[
u-Q_d(u)
=u\left(1-\frac p{p+d}\right)
=\frac{du}{p+d},
\]

hence

\[
\int_0^{p+d}|u-Q_d(u)|^2du
=\frac{d^2}{(p+d)^2}\frac{(p+d)^3}{3}
=\frac{d^2(p+d)}3.
\tag{5.4}
\]

On the second branch a direct simplification gives

\[
u-Q_d(u)=\frac{d(1-u)}{1-p-d},
\]

so

\[
\int_{p+d}^{1}|u-Q_d(u)|^2du
=\frac{d^2(1-p-d)}3.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Adding (5.4) and (5.5), the exact conditional cost is

\[
\boxed{\frac{d^2}{3}.}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Integrating over `y` proves the first inequality in (5.2).

It remains to use only the admissible range and the zero-mean condition. Since

\[
(\delta+p)(1-p-\delta)\ge0,
\]

expansion yields the pointwise inequality

\[
\delta^2\le p(1-p)+(1-2p)\delta.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Integrating (5.7) and using `int delta=0` gives

\[
\int_0^1\delta(y)^2dy\le p(1-p).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Finally `p(1-p)<=1/4`, proving

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi_\delta)\le\frac1{12}.
\]

Since

\[
\frac1{10}-\frac1{12}=\frac1{60}>0,
\]

the family is uniformly below the diagonal benchmark. QED.

## 6. Corollaries

### Corollary 6.1 — full `2 x 2` checkerboard family

Any `2 x 2` checkerboard density with uniform marginals has cell densities

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
a&2-a\\
2-a&a
\end{pmatrix}
\]

with `0<=a<=2`; equivalently `a=1+theta`, `|theta|<=1`, and the density is

\[
f_\theta(x,y)=1+\theta s(x)s(y).
\]

Taking `p=1/2` and

\[
\delta(y)=\begin{cases}
\theta/2,&0\le y\le1/2,\\
-\theta/2,&1/2<y\le1,
\end{cases}
\]

in Theorem 5.1 gives the explicit continuum certificate

\[
\boxed{W_2^2(\mu,\pi_\theta)\le\frac{\theta^2}{12}\le\frac1{12}<\frac1{10}.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the complete one-parameter family selected by the preceding stuck report is closed.

### Corollary 6.2 — every two-column or two-row rational checkerboard

A finite checkerboard copula with exactly two `x`-columns, of widths `p` and `1-p`, has a step-function `delta(y)` and is exactly a special case of Theorem 5.1. Therefore every such `2 x n` checkerboard, with arbitrary rational or real row cuts and any admissible cell masses, satisfies `W_2^2<=1/12`. By swapping coordinates, the same holds for every `n x 2` checkerboard.

This is materially broader than the originally selected `2 x 2` family.

## 7. Why this does not solve the full conjecture

The proof exploits a binary mass-shift variable `delta(y)`. The key global estimate (5.8) is exactly the variance ceiling for a zero-mean variable constrained to the interval `[-p,1-p]`. For three or more `x`-slabs, a conditional copula has multiple independent mass-transfer coordinates; the same one-dimensional scalar ceiling does not directly survive, and transporting `x` while freezing `y` can be substantially nonoptimal.

Therefore this result is a **restricted-family theorem only**. It does not characterize continuum extreme copulas, does not imply finite Birkhoff vertices classify continuum extremes, and does not prove the Catalano–Lavenant conjecture.
