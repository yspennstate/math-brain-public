## 3. Exact diagonal normalization: why the benchmark is `1/10`

Let \((X,Y)\sim\lambda^{\otimes2}\), and let the target diagonal point be \((Z,Z)\) with \(Z\sim\lambda\). Put \(S=(X+Y)/2\). Then identically

\[
(X-Z)^2+(Y-Z)^2=\frac{(X-Y)^2}{2}+2(S-Z)^2.
\]

The first expectation is \(1/12\). The law of \(S\) has distribution function

\[
F_S(s)=2s^2\quad(0\le s\le1/2),\qquad
F_S(s)=1-2(1-s)^2\quad(1/2\le s\le1).
\]

Hence its quantile is

\[
F_S^{-1}(u)=\sqrt{u/2}\quad(u\le1/2),\qquad
F_S^{-1}(u)=1-\sqrt{(1-u)/2}\quad(u\ge1/2).
\]

The one-dimensional monotone coupling gives

\[
W_2^2(\mathcal L(S),\lambda)
=2\int_0^{1/2}(\sqrt{u/2}-u)^2\,du
=\frac1{120}.
\]

Therefore

\[
W_2^2((\mathrm{id},\mathrm{id})_\#\lambda,\lambda^{\otimes2})
=\frac1{12}+2\cdot\frac1{120}=\boxed{\frac1{10}}.
\]

The antidiagonal has the same value by the square isometry \((x,y)\mapsto(x,1-y)\).

## 4. Proof of the full-branch piecewise-linear exclusion theorem

Fix the partition and orientations from the theorem. On a branch \(I_k\), write

\[
X=a_k+\ell_k U,
\]

where conditional on \(X\in I_k\), \(U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\). Let \(Y\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\) independently. Since the branch \(T|_{I_k}\) maps normalized Lebesgue measure on \(I_k\) to \(\lambda\), and branch \(k\) has mass \(\ell_k\),

\[
T_\#\lambda=\sum_k\ell_k\lambda=\lambda.
\]

Now construct an explicit transport from the product law of \((X,Y)\) to the graph law. Let \(K\) be the branch containing \(X\), and define

\[
Z=\begin{cases}
a_K+\ell_KY,&\varepsilon_K=+1,\\
a_K+\ell_K(1-Y),&\varepsilon_K=-1.
\end{cases}
\]

Conditional on \(K=k\), \(Z\) is uniform on \(I_k\). Since \(\mathbb P(K=k)=\ell_k\), the unconditional density of \(Z\) is one on every branch, so \(Z\sim\lambda\). By the definition of the orientation,

\[
T(Z)=Y
\]

in either case. Therefore the target \((Z,Y)=(Z,T(Z))\) has exactly the graph law \(\pi_T\).

The second-coordinate transport cost is zero. Conditional on \(K=k\), the first-coordinate cost is

\[
|X-Z|^2=\ell_k^2|U-Y|^2
\]

in the positive branch and \(\ell_k^2|U-(1-Y)|^2\) in the negative branch. In both cases the two variables inside the difference are independent uniform random variables, so

\[
\mathbb E|U-Y|^2=\mathbb E|U-(1-Y)|^2=\frac16.
\]

Averaging over the branch probability \(\ell_k\) yields the explicit transport cost

\[
\sum_k \ell_k\frac{\ell_k^2}{6}=\frac16\sum_k\ell_k^3.
\]

Since \(W_2^2\) is the infimum over all transports, this proves

\[
W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_T)\le\frac16\sum_k\ell_k^3.
\]

For equal branches, \(\sum_k\ell_k^3=m(1/m)^3=1/m^2\), proving the stated strict bound for every \(m\ge2\). More generally, \(\sum_k\ell_k^3\le3/5\) is sufficient for the conjectured benchmark.

This proof is continuum-exact and does not pass through a discretized Birkhoff problem.

## 5. Restricted rational-mixture corollary

For fixed reference \(\nu=\lambda^{\otimes2}\), the map \(\mu\mapsto W_2^2(\mu,\nu)\) is convex: if \(\Gamma_r\) is any transport from \(\mu_r\) to \(\nu\), then \(\sum_r q_r\Gamma_r\) transports \(\sum_rq_r\mu_r\) to \(\nu\) with the corresponding averaged cost. Thus

\[
W_2^2\!\left(\sum_rq_r\pi_{T_r},\lambda^{\otimes2}\right)
\le\sum_rq_rW_2^2(\pi_{T_r},\lambda^{\otimes2}).
\]

Consequently any finite mixture, in particular any mixture with rational weights and rational branchpoints, of certified full-branch graph copulas remains at or below \(1/10\). This is a genuine continuum rational family but does **not** cover arbitrary rational checkerboard copulas.
