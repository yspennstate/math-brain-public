## 5. New theorem — all equal `3 x 3` checkerboard copulas

### Theorem 5.1

Let

\[
I_i=[i/3,(i+1)/3],\qquad i=0,1,2.
\]

Let `P=(P_ij)` be any `3 x 3` doubly stochastic matrix. Define the checkerboard copula `pi_P` to have constant density

\[
3P_{ij}
\]

on `I_i x I_j`. Then both marginals of `pi_P` are uniform and

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_P)\le\frac8{81}<\frac1{10}.
}
\]

In particular the complete equal `3 x 3` checkerboard family is rigorously excluded as a counterexample, with certified gap

\[
\frac1{10}-\frac8{81}=\frac1{810}.
\]

### Proof

Because `P` is a finite `3 x 3` doubly stochastic matrix, the finite Birkhoff theorem gives

\[
P=\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\theta_\sigma P_\sigma,
\qquad
\theta_\sigma\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma=1,
\]

where `P_sigma` is the permutation matrix of `sigma`. Consequently

\[
\pi_P=\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma\pi_\sigma,
\]

where `pi_sigma` has density `3` on the three cells

\[
I_j\times I_{\sigma(j)},\qquad j=0,1,2,
\]

and density `0` elsewhere.

It therefore suffices to give, for every permutation `sigma`, a transport from `mu=lambda tensor lambda` to `pi_sigma` of cost at most `8/81`. This is a use of finite Birkhoff convexity **within the fixed checkerboard family only**; no statement about continuum extreme copulas is being made.

Partition the source square into

\[
S_{ab}=I_a\times I_b,\qquad a,b\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Each source cell has mass `1/9`. For each permutation `sigma`, choose an assignment `J_sigma(a,b) in {0,1,2}` using the following tables, where rows are `a`, columns are `b`, and the displayed entry is `j=J_sigma(a,b)`:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\sigma & J_\sigma & \text{coarse integer cost}\\ \hline
(0,1,2) & \begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&2\\1&2&2\end{pmatrix} & 8\\[2mm]
(0,2,1) & \begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&2&1\\2&2&1\end{pmatrix} & 6\\[2mm]
(1,0,2) & \begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1&1&2\\1&2&2\end{pmatrix} & 6\\[2mm]
(1,2,0) & \begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\2&1&1\\2&2&1\end{pmatrix} & 6\\[2mm]
(2,0,1) & \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&1&0\\2&2&2\end{pmatrix} & 6\\[2mm]
(2,1,0) & \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\2&1&0\\2&2&1\end{pmatrix} & 8.
\end{array}
\]

In every table each target index `j` appears exactly three times. Now take a source point in `S_ab` and write it uniquely up to null boundaries as

\[
(x,y)=\left(\frac{a+u}{3},\frac{b+v}{3}\right),
\qquad 0\le u,v<1.
\]

Set `j=J_sigma(a,b)` and transport it to

\[
\Phi_\sigma(x,y)
=
\left(\frac{j+u}{3},\frac{\sigma(j)+v}{3}\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

On each source cell this is a translation. It maps `S_ab` bijectively onto the occupied target cell `I_j x I_{sigma(j)}`. Since exactly three source cells are assigned to each `j`, their three unit source densities superpose to density `3` on that target cell. Hence

\[
(\Phi_\sigma)_\#\mu=\pi_\sigma.
\]

The squared displacement is constant on each source cell:

\[
|\Phi_\sigma(x,y)-(x,y)|^2
=
\frac{(a-j)^2+(b-\sigma(j))^2}{9}.
\]

After multiplying by source-cell mass `1/9` and summing over all nine cells, the total transport cost is

\[
\frac1{81}
\sum_{a,b=0}^2
\left[(a-J_\sigma(a,b))^2
+(b-\sigma(J_\sigma(a,b)))^2\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

The six displayed tables give integer sums

\[
8,6,6,6,6,8,
\]

respectively. Therefore every permutation checkerboard satisfies

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi_\sigma)\le\frac8{81}.
\]

Finally, if `gamma_sigma` denotes the corresponding feasible transport plan, then

\[
\gamma:=\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma\gamma_\sigma
\]

has first marginal `mu`, second marginal `pi_P`, and cost equal to the same convex combination of the six costs. Thus

\[
W_2^2(\mu,\pi_P)
\le\sum_\sigma\theta_\sigma W_2^2(\mu,\pi_\sigma)
\le\frac8{81}
<\frac1{10}.
\]

This proves the theorem. QED.
