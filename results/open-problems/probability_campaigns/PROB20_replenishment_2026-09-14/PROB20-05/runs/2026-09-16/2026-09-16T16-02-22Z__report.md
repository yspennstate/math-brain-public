## 3. Reused exact lemma from the previous run

Replacing the diagonal target segment over `I` by the antidiagonal segment in `I\times I` can be done by an explicit continuum transport supported on the source band `F_S(s) in I`, with exact cost improvement

\Gamma_-(a,b)=\frac4{15}(v-u)^2
\left(12u^3+19u^2v+6uv^2+3v^3\right)>0.

## 4. New theorem: local replacements add exactly on disjoint diagonal-quantile bands

Fix `m>=2` and equal bins

\[
I_j=[j/m,(j+1)/m],\qquad j=0,\dots,m-1.
\]

For a subset `F` of bin indices define the measure-preserving piecewise-affine map

\[
T_F(x)=\begin{cases}
(2j+1)/m-x,&x\in I_j,\ j\in F,\\
x,&x\in I_j,\ j\notin F.
\end{cases}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and let `pi_F=(id,T_F)_# lambda`.

> **Theorem 4.1 (all signed identity-bin shuffles).** There are explicit numbers `Gamma_{m,j}>0` such that
> \[
> \boxed{
> W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_F)
> \le \frac1{10}-\sum_{j\in F}\Gamma_{m,j}.
> }
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> For every bin,
> \[
> \boxed{
> \Gamma_{m,j}\ge \frac{\sqrt2}{10m^{5/2}}.
> }
> \tag{4.3}
> \]
> Consequently
> \[
> \boxed{
> W_2^2(\lambda^{\otimes2},\pi_F)
> \le \frac1{10}-\frac{|F|\sqrt2}{10m^{5/2}}.
> }
> \tag{4.4}
> \]
> Equality with the diagonal benchmark is therefore possible in this family only for `F=empty`.

### Proof of additivity

For each bin let

\[
A_j:=\{(s,d):F_S(s)\in I_j\}.
\]

Because the bins `I_j` are disjoint up to endpoints and `F_S(s)` is the diagonal target parameter of the recovered optimal diagonal plan, the source bands `A_j` are pairwise disjoint up to null sets. The diagonal plan sends exactly mass `1/m` from `A_j` to the diagonal graph segment parametrized by `I_j`.

For each `j in F`, apply the explicit one-flip transport from the previous lemma **only on `A_j`**. It sends the restricted source mass exactly to the reflected graph segment

\[
\{(x,(2j+1)/m-x):x\in I_j\}
\]

with Lebesgue parameter on `I_j`, and changes the total cost by exactly `-Gamma_{m,j}`. On every `A_j` with `j notin F`, retain the diagonal transport.

Since the `A_j` are disjoint, these definitions assemble into a single measurable transport from the product measure to `pi_F`. There are no cross-band compatibility conditions: each target graph segment receives exactly its required mass and parameter law. The total cost is the baseline `1/10` plus the sum of the local cost changes, proving (4.2).

It remains only to prove the uniform lower bound (4.3).

## 5. New exact monotonicity: among equal lower-half bins, the endpoint flip has the smallest certified gap

Take an equal bin contained in `[0,1/2]`. In (3.1), its width is

\[
h=b-a=1/m.
\]

Set

\[
\delta=v-u,\qquad s_0=u+v,\qquad x=s_0/\delta\ge1.
\]

Because `v^2-u^2=h/2`,

\[
\delta s_0=h/2,
\qquad
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{h}{2x}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The cubic in (3.1) has the exact expansion

\[
12u^3+19u^2v+6uv^2+3v^3
=\frac{\delta^3}{2}\left(1+5x-10x^2+10x^3\right).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Define

\[
Q(x)=\frac{1+5x-10x^2+10x^3}{2},
\qquad q(x)=\frac{Q(x)}{x^{5/2}}.
\]

Then

\[
\Gamma_{m,j}=\frac4{15}\left(\frac h2\right)^{5/2}q(x).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Direct differentiation gives the exact factorization

\[
\boxed{
q'(x)=\frac{5(x-1)(2x^2+4x+1)}{4x^{7/2}}\ge0
\quad(x\ge1).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Also `Q(1)=3`. Hence `q(x)>=q(1)=3`, and therefore

\[
\Gamma_{m,j}
\ge \frac4{15}\left(\frac h2\right)^{5/2}3
=\frac{\sqrt2}{10}h^{5/2}
=\frac{\sqrt2}{10m^{5/2}}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is attained by the endpoint lower-half bin. Upper-half bins have the same bound by the square reflection symmetry.

For odd `m`, the only equal bin crossing `1/2` is the symmetric central bin. With `h=1/m<=1/3`, (3.3) and

\[
1-\sqrt{1-h}=\frac{h}{1+\sqrt{1-h}}\ge h/2
\]

give

\[
\Gamma_c(m)\ge\frac{h^2(1-h)}3\ge\frac{2h^2}{9}.
\]

Meanwhile

\[
\frac{\sqrt2}{10}h^{5/2}
\le \frac{\sqrt{2/3}}{10}h^2,
\]

and

\[
\frac29>\frac{\sqrt{2/3}}{10}
\]

(the squares are `4/81 > 1/150`). Thus the central bin also satisfies (4.3). Theorem 4.1 is proved.

## 7. Reflection extends the theorem to all signed reversal-bin shuffles

Consider now a signed equal-bin shuffle whose underlying bin permutation is the reversal `j -> m-1-j`. On bin `I_j` there are exactly two affine measure-preserving branches onto `I_{m-1-j}`:

- the decreasing branch `y=1-x`, which is the pure antidiagonal branch;
- the increasing branch
  \[
  y=x+\frac{m-1-2j}{m}.
  \]

Apply the square isometry

\[
R_y(x,y)=(x,1-y).
\]

It preserves `lambda tensor lambda` and Euclidean squared cost. The decreasing branch becomes `1-y=x`, while the increasing branch becomes

\[
1-y=\frac{2j+1}{m}-x,
\]

which is exactly the local reflected branch in (4.1). Therefore every signed reversal-bin graph is isometric to a signed identity-bin graph. If `r` branches differ from the pure antidiagonal orientation, then

\[
\boxed{
W_2^2\le\frac1{10}-\frac{r\sqrt2}{10m^{5/2}}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus among all signed shuffles with underlying permutation equal to identity or reversal, the only maps not certified strictly below the benchmark are the actual diagonal and actual antidiagonal.

At fixed `m>=2` these two families contain `2^(m+1)` distinct maps and therefore

\[
2^{m+1}-2
\]

strictly subextremal maps plus the two benchmark extremizers.
