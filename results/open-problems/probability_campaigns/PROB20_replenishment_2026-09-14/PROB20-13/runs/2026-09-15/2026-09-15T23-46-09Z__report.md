## 2. Exact LP dual retained from earlier checkpoints

Let \(G\) be any finite connected simple unweighted graph with \(m=|E|\). Since
\[
\pi_xP_{xy}=\frac1{2m}
\]
on every oriented edge, the source-flow problem with
\[
\operatorname{div}f^a=\delta_a-\pi
\]
is exactly equivalent to the undirected stationary product-demand instance with unordered commodity demand
\[
h_{uv}=2\pi_u\pi_v
\]
and common physical-edge capacity
\[
c_e=\frac1{2m}.
\]

The finite multicommodity-flow LP dual is
\[
\boxed{
F(G)=
\max_{\ell_e\ge0,\ \sum_ec_e\ell_e\le1}
\sum_{u,v}\pi_u\pi_vd_\ell(u,v),
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(d_\ell\) is the shortest-path metric generated by the nonnegative edge lengths \(\ell_e\).

Therefore **any** feasible length assignment with objective exceeding \(2\tau_{\rm rel}D\) refutes the owner target. We only need a dual lower bound; no primal routing is required.

# 3. The base graph: the incidence graph of \(\mathrm{PG}(2,49)\)

Let
\[
s=49=7^2.
\]
The Desarguesian projective plane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,49)\) exists because \(49\) is a prime power. Concretely, points are one-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_{49}^3\), lines are two-dimensional subspaces, and incidence is containment (equivalently the standard homogeneous dot-product description).

The number of points, and also the number of lines, is
\[
N=s^2+s+1=49^2+49+1=2451.
\]
Let \(H\) be the bipartite point-line incidence graph. Thus
\[
|V(H)|=2N=4902.
\]
Every point lies on \(s+1=50\) lines and every line contains \(50\) points, so \(H\) is 50-regular and
\[
|E(H)|=N(s+1)=2451\cdot50=122550.
\]

Any two distinct points determine exactly one common line, and dually any two distinct lines meet in exactly one point. Hence, from any fixed point (and symmetrically from any fixed line), the distance layers in \(H\) have sizes
\[
1,\quad 50,\quad 49\cdot50=2450,\quad 49^2=2401.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Thus \(H\) has diameter three.

The mean distance between two independent uniform vertices of \(H\) is therefore
\[
\bar d_H
=
\frac{50+2\cdot2450+3\cdot2401}{4902}
=
\frac{12153}{4902}
=
\boxed{\frac{4051}{1634}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

# 5. Exact relaxation time

Let \(B\) be the \(2451\times2451\) point-line incidence matrix of \(\mathrm{PG}(2,49)\). Every point lies on 50 lines, and any two distinct points lie on exactly one common line. Therefore
\[
BB^T=49I+J.
\tag{5.1}
\]

On the all-ones vector, the eigenvalue of \(BB^T\) is
\[
49+2451=2500=50^2.
\]
On the orthogonal complement of the all-ones vector, the eigenvalue is 49. Hence the singular values of \(B\) are
\[
50\quad\text{and}\quad 7.
\]
The adjacency matrix of the bipartite incidence graph is
\[
A_H=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&B\\
B^T&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
so its eigenvalues are
\[
\boxed{50,\ -50,\ 7,\ -7}
\]
with the appropriate multiplicities.

Now pass to the cone \(G\). Every base vertex has degree 51. If \(z\) is an eigenvector of \(A_H\) orthogonal to the base all-ones vector and has adjacency eigenvalue \(\theta\), then extending it by zero at the hub gives a random-walk eigenvector of \(G\) with eigenvalue
\[
\frac{\theta}{51}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus we obtain eigenvalues
\[
\pm\frac7{51},\qquad -\frac{50}{51}.
\]

The remaining two-dimensional subspace, constant on the base together with the hub coordinate, has transition matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\[1mm]
1/51&50/51
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose eigenvalues are
\[
1,\qquad -\frac1{51}.
\]
Consequently the second-largest random-walk eigenvalue is exactly
\[
\boxed{\lambda_2(P_G)=\frac7{51}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm rel}(G)
=\frac1{1-7/51}
=\frac{51}{44}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
With \(D=2\), the proposed right-hand side is
\[
\boxed{
2\tau_{\rm rel}D
=4\cdot\frac{51}{44}
=\frac{51}{11}.
}
\tag{5.5}

\[
4902\frac{156}{53}+122550\frac{104}{53}.
\]
Since
\[
122550=25\cdot4902
\]
and
\[
156+25\cdot104=2756=52\cdot53,
\]
the total length is exactly
\[
4902\cdot52=254904=2m.

Using \(c_e=1/(2m)\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_ec_e\ell_e=1.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
So this is a feasible dual solution.

## 6.2 The induced shortest-path metric

The direct hub-to-base path has cost \(x\), and every alternative first leaves the hub by a spoke and adds nonnegative cost, so
\[
d_\ell(h,v)=x
\qquad(v\in V(H)).
\tag{6.4}
\]

For two base vertices at incidence-graph distance \(t\in\{1,2,3\}\), an all-base geodesic has cost \(ty\), while the hub route costs \(2x=3y\). Hence
\[
d_\ell(u,v)=\min\{ty,2x\}=ty
\qquad(t=1,2,3).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus on the base the weighted metric is exactly \(y\) times the ordinary incidence-graph metric.

## 6.3 Exact objective

The ordered hub/base contribution is
\[
2\pi_h\pi(V(H))x
=
2\cdot\frac1{52}\cdot\frac{51}{52}\cdot\frac{156}{53}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Conditional on both vertices lying in the base, they are independent uniform vertices of \(H\). Therefore the base/base contribution is
\[
\left(\frac{51}{52}\right)^2
\frac{104}{53}
\frac{4051}{1634}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Adding (6.6) and (6.7) and reducing gives

# 8. Infinite rational counterexample family

The construction is not isolated. Let \(t\ge7\) be any prime power and set
\[
s=t^2.
\]
Then the Desarguesian plane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,s)\) exists. Let
\[
G_t=K_1\vee\operatorname{Inc}(\mathrm{PG}(2,t^2)).
\]
The base degree is \(s+1\), the cone-base degree is \(s+2\), and the positive nontrivial incidence adjacency eigenvalue is \(\sqrt s=t\). Hence
\[
\lambda_2(P_{G_t})=\frac{t}{t^2+2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm rel}(G_t)=\frac{t^2+2}{t^2+2-t}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Use the same breakpoint-three metric:
\[
x=\frac{3(s+3)}{s+4},
\qquad
y=\frac{2(s+3)}{s+4},
\qquad2x=3y.
\tag{8.2}
\]
The distance layers in the incidence graph are
\[
1,\ s+1,\ s(s+1),\ s^2,
\]
so its mean distance is
\[
\bar d_H=\frac{5s^2+3s+1}{2(s^2+s+1)}.
\tag{8.3}
\]
The resulting exact dual objective simplifies to
\[
\boxed{
L(s)=
\frac{(s+2)(5s^3+19s^2+13s+8)}
{(s+3)(s+4)(s^2+s+1)}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

For \(s=t^2\), comparison with
\[
2\tau_{\rm rel}D
=4\frac{t^2+2}{t^2+2-t}
\]
reduces, after cancelling positive factors and cross-multiplying positive denominators, to
\[
P(t)>0,
\]
where
\[
\boxed{
P(t)=
 t^8-5t^7-3t^6-19t^5-29t^4-13t^3-42t^2-8t-32.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

For \(t\ge7\),
\[
t^8-5t^7=t^6t(t-5)\ge14t^6,
\]
while
\[
3t^6+19t^5+29t^4+13t^3+42t^2+8t+32<11t^6
\]
(the crude bounds \(19/t<3\), \(29/t^2<1\), \(13/t^3<1\), \(42/t^4<1\), \(8/t^5<1\), \(32/t^6<1\) suffice at \(t\ge7\)). Thus
\[
P(t)>3t^6>0.
\]
Therefore every prime power \(t\ge7\) yields a finite exact rational counterexample.

The first member of this square-order subfamily is \(t=7\), i.e. the explicit \(\mathrm{PG}(2,49)\) graph above.

This does **not** claim that 4903 is the smallest counterexample. For example, the same projective-plane certificate already becomes numerically supercritical at some nonsquare orders, but then the relaxation time involves \(\sqrt s\); finding the smallest graph with a fully explicit exact certificate is a separate optimization problem.
