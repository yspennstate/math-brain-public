## 2. Source normalization and exact LP formulation

Aldous–Fill Lemma 4.21 uses a family of unit flows from \(a\) to \(\pi\), with exactly
\[
\psi(\mathbf f)=
\max_{(i,j)}\sum_a\pi_a\frac{|f^{(a)}_{ij}|}{\pi_iP_{ij}}.
\]
Open Problem 4.49(ii) asks whether graph diameter can replace the logarithmic factor in the multicommodity-flow estimate. Primary source:

https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.pdf

For a finite simple unweighted graph with \(m=|E|\),
\[
\pi_xP_{xy}=\frac1{2m}
\tag{2.1}
\]
on every oriented edge.

Set
\[
g^a:=\pi_af^a.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{div}g^a=\pi_a\delta_a-\pi_a\pi.
\]
After cancelling directed cycles and decomposing into paths, this is exactly the undirected multicommodity instance with unordered pair demand
\[
h_{ab}=2\pi_a\pi_b\quad(a<b)
\tag{2.2}
\]
and uniform edge capacity
\[
c_e=\frac1{2m}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Its minimum congestion is exactly \(F(G)\).

The finite path-LP dual is
\[
F(G)=
\max_{\ell_e\ge0,\ \sum_ec_e\ell_e\le1}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_\ell(x,y),
\tag{2.4}
\]
where \(d_\ell\) is shortest-path pseudometric for edge lengths \(\ell\). Equivalently,
\[
F(G)=2\sup_{d\in\mathsf{Met}(V)}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d(X,Y)}
{\mathbb E_{\pi P}d(X_0,X_1)}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For every cut \(S\), scalar spectral Poincaré applied to \(1_S\) gives
\[
\pi(S)\pi(S^c)
\le
\tau_{\rm rel}\frac{|\delta S|}{2m},
\tag{2.6}
\]
so the product-demand congestion of every cut is at most \(2\tau_{\rm rel}\). The universal difficulty is therefore the non-cut portion of the metric cone/product-demand flow-cut gap.

# 3. New exact theorem: every distance-regular graph

Let \(G\) be a connected distance-regular graph of diameter \(D\), order \(n\), and valency \(k\). Fix a base vertex \(x\). Write
\[
\Gamma_r(x)=\{y:d_G(x,y)=r\},\qquad
k_r:=|\Gamma_r(x)|.
\]
Distance-regularity means that for \(y\in\Gamma_r(x)\), the numbers of neighbors of \(y\) in \(\Gamma_{r-1}(x),\Gamma_r(x),\Gamma_{r+1}(x)\) are constants
\[
c_r,\ a_r,\ b_r
\]
depending only on \(r\), not on \(x,y\). In particular,
\[
k_rb_r=k_{r+1}c_{r+1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The graph is regular, hence
\[
\pi\equiv\frac1n,
\qquad
m=\frac{nk}{2},
\qquad
\pi_uP_{uv}=\frac1{nk}=\frac1{2m}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the stationary mean graph distance
\[
\bar d:=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{x,y}d_G(x,y)
=\frac1n\sum_{r=0}^D r k_r.
\tag{3.3}
\]
(The second equality is independent of the chosen base vertex by distance-regularity.)

## Theorem 3.1
For every finite connected distance-regular graph,
\[
\boxed{F(G)=2\bar d.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Consequently,
\[
oxed{F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Equality in (3.5) occurs for complete graphs; every noncomplete distance-regular graph satisfies the strict inequality
\[
F(G)<2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\]

The proof below is self-contained apart from the standard distance-regular fact that the intersection numbers \(p^h_{ij}\) depend only on \(i,j,h\).

## 4. Explicit radial source flows

For \(0\le r\le D-1\), define
\[
M_r:=\sum_{t=r+1}^D k_t,
\tag{4.1}
\]
the number of vertices farther than radius \(r\) from a base point. Define
\[
q_r:=\frac{M_r}{n k_r b_r}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every source \(x\), orient each edge joining \(\Gamma_r(x)\) to \(\Gamma_{r+1}(x)\) outward from \(x\), and put flow amount \(q_r\) on that oriented edge. Put zero flow on edges joining vertices in the same distance layer. Call the resulting antisymmetric flow \(f^x\).

### Lemma 4.1
\[
\operatorname{div}f^x=\delta_x-\frac1n\mathbf 1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
At the source \(x\), there are \(k=b_0\) outgoing edges and
\[
q_0=\frac{n-1}{nk},
\]
so the outward divergence is
\[
kq_0=1-\frac1n.
\]

Now take \(y\in\Gamma_r(x)\), \(1\le r\le D-1\). It has \(c_r\) incoming edges from layer \(r-1\) and \(b_r\) outgoing edges to layer \(r+1\). By (3.1),
\[
c_rq_{r-1}
=
\frac{c_rM_{r-1}}{n k_{r-1}b_{r-1}}
=
\frac{M_{r-1}}{n k_r},
\]
while
\[
b_rq_r=\frac{M_r}{n k_r}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\text{incoming}-\text{outgoing}
=
\frac{M_{r-1}-M_r}{n k_r}
=
\frac{k_r}{n k_r}
=
\frac1n.
\]
Thus the outward divergence is \(-1/n\).

For \(y\in\Gamma_D(x)\), there is no outward flow and
\[
c_Dq_{D-1}
=
\frac{M_{D-1}}{nk_D}
=
\frac{k_D}{nk_D}
=
\frac1n,
\]
so again the outward divergence is \(-1/n\). This proves (4.3). \(\square\)

Hence the family \((f^x)_{x\in V}\) is admissible for the exact Aldous–Fill problem.

# 5. The weighted absolute edge load is constant

For a fixed undirected edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), define
\[
L_e:=\frac1n\sum_{x\in V}|f^x_e|.
\tag{5.1}
\]
We claim that \(L_e\) is independent of \(e\).

For \(0\le r\le D-1\), a source \(x\) contributes \(q_r\) to \(|f_e^x|\) precisely when
\[
(d(x,u),d(x,v))=(r,r+1)
\]
or
\[
(d(x,u),d(x,v))=(r+1,r).
\]
Because \(d(u,v)=1\), the numbers
\[
|\{x:d(x,u)=r,\ d(x,v)=r+1\}|
\]
and
\[
|\{x:d(x,u)=r+1,\ d(x,v)=r\}|
\]
are the standard intersection numbers \(p^1_{r,r+1}\) and \(p^1_{r+1,r}\). In a distance-regular graph these depend only on the distance indices, not on the particular adjacent pair \(u,v\). Therefore
\[
L_e=
\frac1n\sum_{r=0}^{D-1}
q_r\bigl(p^1_{r,r+1}+p^1_{r+1,r}\bigr)
\tag{5.2}
\]
is the same for every edge.

### Dependency check for the intersection-number fact
The defining three-term distance-matrix recurrence of a distance-regular graph is
\[
A A_r=b_{r-1}A_{r-1}+a_rA_r+c_{r+1}A_{r+1}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Starting from \(A_0=I\), \(A_1=A\), this shows inductively that every distance matrix \(A_r\) is a polynomial in \(A\). Hence products \(A_iA_j\) lie in the span of the distance matrices \(A_h\), which means the entries of \(A_iA_j\)—the counts of vertices at prescribed distances \(i,j\) from an ordered pair—depend only on the pair distance \(h\). This is exactly the required existence of \(p^h_{ij}\). No graph automorphism/edge-transitivity assumption is being used.

# 6. Compute the common edge load by total variation

For a fixed source \(x\), every nonzero flow edge points monotonically away from \(x\), so there is no absolute-value cancellation. The total absolute amount crossing the boundary between layers \(r\) and \(r+1\) is
\[
(k_rb_r)q_r
=
\frac{M_r}{n}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{e\in E}|f^x_e|
=
\frac1n\sum_{r=0}^{D-1}M_r.
\tag{6.2}
\]
By the layer-cake identity,
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{D-1}M_r
=
\sum_{t=1}^D t k_t.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Combining (3.3), (6.2), and (6.3),
\[
\sum_e|f^x_e|=\bar d.
\tag{6.4}
\]
This value is independent of \(x\).

Average over sources with \(\pi_x=1/n\):
\[
\sum_eL_e
=
\frac1n\sum_x\sum_e|f^x_e|
=
\bar d.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Since \(L_e\) is constant over all \(m\) edges,
\[
L_e=\frac{\bar d}{m}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Using physical edge capacity \(c_e=1/(2m)\), the congestion of this explicit family is
\[
\psi(\mathbf f)
=
\frac{\bar d/m}{1/(2m)}
=2\bar d.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Thus
\[
F(G)\le2\bar d.
\tag{6.8}
\]

# 7. Matching dual certificate: exact optimality

Take the dual edge length
\[
\ell_e=2\qquad(e\in E).
\]
Because \(c_e=1/(2m)\),
\[
\sum_ec_e\ell_e
=m\cdot\frac1{2m}\cdot2
=1.
\]
The induced shortest-path metric is
\[
d_\ell(x,y)=2d_G(x,y).
\]
Its dual objective is
\[
\sum_{x,y}\frac1{n^2}\,2d_G(x,y)
=2\bar d.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Therefore LP duality gives
\[
F(G)\ge2\bar d.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Together with (6.8),
\[
\boxed{F(G)=2\bar d.}
\]
This completes the exact theorem. \(\square\)

# 8. Deduce the sharp diameter inequality

If \(G\) is complete, then \(D=1\), \(\bar d=(n-1)/n\), and
\[
F(G)=\frac{2(n-1)}n
=2\tau_{\rm rel}D,
\]
since \(\tau_{\rm rel}=(n-1)/n\).

If \(G\) is noncomplete, then there exist nonadjacent vertices. The standard two-coordinate Rayleigh-quotient argument gives
\[
\lambda_2(P)\ge0,
\qquad
\tau_{\rm rel}\ge1.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Also \(\bar d<D\): graph distances are at most \(D\), while diagonal pairs have distance zero with positive stationary weight. Hence
\[
F(G)=2\bar d
<2D
\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Thus every distance-regular graph satisfies the full requested inequality, strictly unless complete.

# 9. Strongly regular corollary and consistency checks

A connected noncomplete strongly regular graph with parameters \((v,k,\lambda,\mu)\) is distance-regular of diameter two. Its distance distribution from every vertex is
\[
k_0=1,\qquad k_1=k,\qquad k_2=v-k-1.
\]
Hence
\[
\bar d
=\frac{k+2(v-k-1)}v
=2-\frac{k+2}{v}.
\]
The theorem gives the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
F(G)=4-\frac{2(k+2)}v.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

This reproduces independent exact values already present in the repository:

- Petersen \((v,k)=(10,3)\): \(F=3\).
- Paley 17 \((17,8)\): \(F=48/17\approx2.823529\).
- Balanced complete bipartite \(K_{m,m}\): \(F=3-2/m\), agreeing with peer report `agents/055.md`.
- Cocktail-party graphs similarly match the memo's exact values.

These are algebraic consistency checks, not independent review of the general proof.

# 10. New failed route documented this run: naive regular diameter-two routing

The strongly-regular proof suggests routing every nonedge uniformly through its common neighbors. For a general regular diameter-two graph this natural scheme can exceed congestion \(4\); finite random regular examples were found with this particular routing above \(5\). Thus the strongly-regular theorem genuinely uses the constant intersection numbers; it does not silently extend to all regular diameter-two graphs.

This failure is useful because it blocks an attractive but false one-line generalization.
