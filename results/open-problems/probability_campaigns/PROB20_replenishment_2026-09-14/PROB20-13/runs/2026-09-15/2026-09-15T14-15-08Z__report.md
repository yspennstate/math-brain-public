\[
\operatorname{div}g^a=\pi_a\delta_a-\pi_a\pi.

# 3. New exact theorem: distance-biregular graphs

Let \(G=(X_0\sqcup X_1,E)\) be a finite connected **distance-biregular** graph. Thus it is bipartite and every vertex in a fixed color class has the same distance intersection array; equivalently, it is distance-semiregular on both color classes.

Write
\[
n_\epsilon:=|X_\epsilon|,
\qquad
d_\epsilon:=\deg(x)\quad(x\in X_\epsilon),
\qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Because the graph is biregular,
\[
n_0d_0=n_1d_1=m.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Hence every vertex in color class \(X_\epsilon\) has stationary mass
\[
p_\epsilon:=\frac{d_\epsilon}{2m}=\frac1{2n_\epsilon}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
In particular each color class has total stationary mass \(1/2\).

Define the stationary mean graph distance
\[
\bar d_\pi:=\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_G(x,y)
=\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G(X,Y).
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem 3.1
For every finite connected distance-biregular graph,
\[
\boxed{F(G)=2\bar d_\pi.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Consequently, every such graph satisfies the Aldous–Fill sharp diameter target
\[
\boxed{F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.}
\tag{3.5}
\]
For every distance-biregular graph other than \(K_2\), one even has the stronger elementary bound
\[
F(G)=2\bar d_\pi\le2D\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The proof has four parts: stationary radial flows, exact divergence, edge-load homogeneity from the distance-biregular coherent configuration, and a matching metric dual certificate.

# 4. Radial source flows with stationary shell masses

Fix a source \(x\in X_\epsilon\), \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Let its eccentricity be \(D_\epsilon\), and write
\[
\Gamma_r(x)=\{y:d_G(x,y)=r\},
\qquad
k_{\epsilon,r}:=|\Gamma_r(x)|.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Distance-biregularity makes \(k_{\epsilon,r}\) depend only on the source color \(\epsilon\) and radius \(r\), not on the chosen source.

For a vertex in layer \(r\), the color is \(\epsilon+r\pmod2\), hence its stationary mass is
\[
p_{\epsilon+r}:=p_{(\epsilon+r)\bmod2}.
\]
The stationary mass of the whole radius-\(r\) layer is therefore
\[
\sigma_{\epsilon,r}:=k_{\epsilon,r}p_{\epsilon+r}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Define the stationary mass strictly outside radius \(r\):
\[
M_{\epsilon,r}
:=\sum_{t=r+1}^{D_\epsilon}\sigma_{\epsilon,t}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let \(b_{\epsilon,r}\) be the number of neighbors in layer \(r+1\) of a vertex in layer \(r\), and \(c_{\epsilon,r+1}\) the number of neighbors in layer \(r\) of a vertex in layer \(r+1\). Standard layer edge-counting gives
\[
k_{\epsilon,r}b_{\epsilon,r}
=k_{\epsilon,r+1}c_{\epsilon,r+1}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Write
\[
E_{\epsilon,r}:=k_{\epsilon,r}b_{\epsilon,r}
\tag{4.5}
\]
for the number of edges joining layers \(r\) and \(r+1\).

Define
\[
q_{\epsilon,r}:=\frac{M_{\epsilon,r}}{E_{\epsilon,r}}
\qquad (0\le r<D_\epsilon).
\tag{4.6}
\]
For source \(x\), orient every edge joining layers \(r\) and \(r+1\) outward from \(x\), put flow amount \(q_{\epsilon,r}\) on it, and put zero on any same-layer edge (there are none because the graph is bipartite). Denote this antisymmetric flow by \(f^x\).

## Lemma 4.1 — exact source-to-stationary divergence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{div}f^x=\delta_x-\pi.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

### Proof
At the source, \(k_{\epsilon,0}=1\), \(E_{\epsilon,0}=d_\epsilon\), and
\[
M_{\epsilon,0}=1-\pi_x=1-p_\epsilon.
\]
Thus its total outward flow is
\[
d_\epsilon q_{\epsilon,0}=1-p_\epsilon,
\]
which is the required source divergence.

Now take \(y\in\Gamma_r(x)\), \(1\le r<D_\epsilon\). Its incoming flow is
\[
c_{\epsilon,r}q_{\epsilon,r-1}
=
\frac{c_{\epsilon,r}M_{\epsilon,r-1}}
{k_{\epsilon,r-1}b_{\epsilon,r-1}}
=
\frac{M_{\epsilon,r-1}}{k_{\epsilon,r}},
\tag{4.8}
\]
using (4.4). Its outgoing flow is
\[
b_{\epsilon,r}q_{\epsilon,r}
=
\frac{M_{\epsilon,r}}{k_{\epsilon,r}}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Therefore
\[
\text{incoming}-\text{outgoing}
=
\frac{M_{\epsilon,r-1}-M_{\epsilon,r}}{k_{\epsilon,r}}
=
\frac{\sigma_{\epsilon,r}}{k_{\epsilon,r}}
=p_{\epsilon+r}
=\pi_y.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Thus the outward divergence is \(-\pi_y\).

At the final layer \(r=D_\epsilon\), the same calculation has no outgoing term and uses
\[
M_{\epsilon,D_\epsilon-1}
=\sigma_{\epsilon,D_\epsilon},
\]
again giving incoming flow \(\pi_y\). Hence every nonsource vertex has outward divergence \(-\pi_y\). This proves (4.7). \(\square\)

So \((f^x)_{x\in V}\) is an admissible family for the exact original problem, with no altered demands or weighted-chain substitution.

# 5. Total absolute length of one source flow

For fixed source \(x\in X_\epsilon\), every nonzero edge flow is monotone away from \(x\), so no cancellation occurs in the absolute edge sum. Across the layer boundary \(r\leftrightarrow r+1\), the total absolute flow is
\[
E_{\epsilon,r}q_{\epsilon,r}=M_{\epsilon,r}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{e\in E}|f^x_e|
=
\sum_{r=0}^{D_\epsilon-1}M_{\epsilon,r}.
\tag{5.2}
\]
By the layer-cake identity,
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{D_\epsilon-1}M_{\epsilon,r}
=
\sum_{t=1}^{D_\epsilon}t\,\sigma_{\epsilon,t}
=
\sum_y\pi_y d_G(x,y).
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sum_e|f^x_e|
=\mathbb E_{Y\sim\pi}d_G(x,Y).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This quantity depends only on the source color class, since the distance-layer data do.

Averaging over sources with weight \(\pi_x\) gives
\[
\sum_e\sum_x\pi_x|f^x_e|
=
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_G(x,y)
=\bar d_\pi.
\tag{5.5}
\]

# 6. Why every physical edge gets the same weighted load

For an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), define
\[
L_e:=\sum_x\pi_x|f^x_e|.
\tag{6.1}
\]
We need to show \(L_e\) does not depend on the chosen edge.

This is the one place where full distance-biregularity, not mere biregularity, is used.

Fix source color \(\epsilon\). Because the graph is bipartite, for a source \(x\in X_\epsilon\) and adjacent vertices \(u,v\), the distances \(d(x,u),d(x,v)\) differ by exactly one. The contribution of source \(x\) to edge \(e\) is then the corresponding radial coefficient \(q_{\epsilon,r}\), where \(r=\min\{d(x,u),d(x,v)\}\).

Thus the contribution from color class \(X_\epsilon\) is determined by the counts
\[
N^{\epsilon,+}_r(u,v)
:=|\{x\in X_\epsilon:
 d(x,u)=r,\ d(x,v)=r+1\}|,
\tag{6.2}
\]
and
\[
N^{\epsilon,-}_r(u,v)
:=|\{x\in X_\epsilon:
 d(x,u)=r+1,\ d(x,v)=r\}|.
\tag{6.3}
\]

A standard structural consequence of distance-biregularity is that the distance relations on the two color classes form a two-fiber coherent configuration (equivalently, a \(P\)-polynomial coherent configuration in the modern characterization). Therefore the intersection numbers of these relations depend only on the relation class of the ordered pair \((u,v)\). Since every oriented physical edge from \(X_0\) to \(X_1\) lies in the same distance-one cross relation, the numbers (6.2)–(6.3) depend only on \(\epsilon,r\), not on the particular edge.

Concretely, if \(R_r^{\alpha\beta}\) denotes the 0–1 matrix of the distance-\(r\) relation from color \(X_\alpha\) to color \(X_\beta\), then (6.2) is an entry on the adjacency relation of a product of two such relation matrices. Coherent-configuration closure says that this product is a linear combination of relation matrices, so its entry is constant on the adjacency relation. The same holds for (6.3).

Hence
\[
L_e
=
\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}}
 p_\epsilon
 \sum_r q_{\epsilon,r}
 \bigl(N^{\epsilon,+}_r+N^{\epsilon,-}_r\bigr)
\tag{6.4}
\]
is independent of \(e\).

### External dependency checked

The standard definition used here is supported by Mohar–Shawe-Taylor (1985): distance-biregular means bipartite with vertices in each color class sharing an intersection array. Modern polynomial/coherent-configuration characterizations are given by Sabrina Lato, *Polynomial Characterizations of Distance-Biregular Graphs*, Journal of Graph Theory 109 (2025), 282–293, DOI `10.1002/jgt.23227`. The 2026 paper Bannai–Suda–Zhu, *P-polynomial coherent configurations*, arXiv:2608.03834, explicitly describes Lato's \(P\)-polynomial coherent-configuration notion as characterizing distance-biregular graphs. No flow result is imported from these references; only the standard intersection-number closure is used.

Now (5.5) and edge-load constancy imply, with \(m=|E|\),
\[
mL_e=\bar d_\pi,
\qquad
L_e=\frac{\bar d_\pi}{m}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Physical edge capacity is \(1/(2m)\), so the congestion of this explicit admissible family is
\[
\psi(\mathbf f)
=
\frac{\bar d_\pi/m}{1/(2m)}
=2\bar d_\pi.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Therefore
\[
F(G)\le2\bar d_\pi.
\tag{6.7}
\]

# 7. Matching dual certificate and exact optimality

Take uniform dual edge length
\[
\ell_e=2\qquad(e\in E).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Because every physical edge has capacity \(c_e=1/(2m)\),
\[
\sum_ec_e\ell_e
=m\cdot\frac1{2m}\cdot2=1,
\tag{7.2}
\]
so this is dual-feasible with exact normalization. Its shortest-path metric is
\[
d_\ell(x,y)=2d_G(x,y).
\]
Hence the dual objective equals
\[
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_\ell(x,y)
=2\bar d_\pi.
\tag{7.3}
\]
By weak/strong LP duality,
\[
F(G)\ge2\bar d_\pi.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Together with (6.7),
\[
\boxed{F(G)=2\bar d_\pi.}
\]
This proves Theorem 3.1's exact formula.

# 8. Deduce the sharp diameter inequality

Trivially
\[
\bar d_\pi\le D,
\tag{8.1}
\]
so
\[
F(G)=2\bar d_\pi\le2D.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For every connected bipartite graph with at least three vertices, the normalized adjacency spectrum is symmetric about zero and contains the Perron eigenvalue \(1\). Therefore the second-largest eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\lambda_2(P)\ge0,
\]
so
\[
\tau_{\rm rel}=\frac1{1-\lambda_2(P)}\ge1.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
F(G)\le2D\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The exceptional two-vertex bipartite graph is \(K_2\), which is also complete. There
\[
\lambda_2=-1,
\qquad
\tau_{\rm rel}=\frac12,
\qquad
D=1,
\qquad
F(K_2)=1=2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\]
Thus the target holds for every finite connected distance-biregular graph.

# 9. New unified corollary: every distance-regularized graph

A connected graph is called **distance-regularized** if every vertex admits an intersection array. A standard theorem, recorded for example in Mohar–Shawe-Taylor (1985), says that every connected distance-regularized graph is either:

1. distance-regular; or
2. distance-biregular.

The previous durable checkpoint proved (1.1) for every distance-regular graph. The new Theorem 3.1 proves the corresponding stationary version for every distance-biregular graph. Therefore:

## Corollary 9.1
For every finite connected distance-regularized graph,
\[
\boxed{
F(G)=2\,\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G(X,Y).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
In particular every distance-regularized graph satisfies
\[
\boxed{F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

This extends the exact distance-regular theorem to nonregular bipartite examples such as subdivision graphs of suitable cages and other genuinely distance-biregular graphs.

# 14. Next concrete attacks

### Attack A — broaden the exact `F=2 mean distance` mechanism
Characterize graphs for which there exists an admissible family \(f^x\) satisfying:

1. \(\sum_e|f^x_e|=\sum_y\pi_y d_G(x,y)\) for each source;
2. weighted edge load \(\sum_x\pi_x|f^x_e|\) is independent of \(e\).

Any such graph has exact optimum \(F=2\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G\) by the uniform metric dual. Distance-regular and distance-biregular graphs are now two instances. Natural next candidates are distance-semiregular graphs on only one side, weakly distance-regular digraph shadows, and suitable multi-fiber coherent configurations.

### Attack B — exact low-symmetry diameter-two LP search
Exclude now-settled complete multipartite, distance-regular, and distance-biregular examples. Search diameter-two graphs with small \(\tau_{\rm rel}\), nonuniform degree and trivial automorphism group. Any numerical violation must be upgraded to exact rational primal/dual certificates before it counts as a refutation.

### Attack C — dual inequality through coherent decomposition
The exact dual target is
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d
\le
\tau_{\rm rel}D\,\mathbb E_{\pi P}d
\quad\text{for every semimetric }d.
\]
Try decomposing an arbitrary dual metric into its projection onto the graph-distance/coherent algebra plus a residual. The new exact theorems show that the graph-distance component itself is completely understood; a universal proof would follow from a sharp residual estimate controlled by the diameter.
