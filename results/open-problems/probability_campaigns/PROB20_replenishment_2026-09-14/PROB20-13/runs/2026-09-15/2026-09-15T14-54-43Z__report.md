## 2. Exact normalization and LP retained

For a simple unweighted graph with \(m=|E|\),
\[
\pi_xP_{xy}=\frac1{2m}
\]
on every oriented edge.

With \(g^a=\pi_af^a\),
\[
\operatorname{div}g^a=\pi_a\delta_a-\pi_a\pi.
\]
After directed-cycle cancellation and path decomposition, \(F(G)\) is exactly the minimum congestion for unordered pair demands
\[
h_{ab}=2\pi_a\pi_b,\qquad a<b,
\]
with edge capacity \(1/(2m)\).

The finite dual is
\[
F(G)=
\max_{\ell_e\ge0,\ \sum_e c_e\ell_e\le1}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_\ell(x,y),
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(c_e=1/(2m)\) and \(d_\ell\) is the shortest-path pseudometric. Zero edge lengths are allowed.

Equivalently,
\[
F(G)=2\sup_{d\in\mathsf{Met}(V)}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d(X,Y)}
{\mathbb E_{\pi P}d(X_0,X_1)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The primary normalization remains Aldous--Fill, Lemma 4.21 and Open Problem 4.49(ii):
https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.pdf

# 3. New exact theorem: regular Cartesian products

Let \(G\) be a finite connected \(r\)-regular simple graph on \(n\) vertices, and \(H\) a finite connected \(s\)-regular simple graph on \(q\) vertices, with \(r,s>0\).

Their Cartesian product
\[
K:=G\square H
\]
has vertex set \(V(G)\times V(H)\), with
\[
(x,y)\sim(x',y')
\]
iff either \(y=y'\) and \(x\sim_Gx'\), or \(x=x'\) and \(y\sim_Hy'\).

Then \(K\) is \((r+s)\)-regular and its stationary distribution is uniform.

## Theorem 3.1 — exact tensorization
\[
\boxed{
F(G\square H)=
\max\left\{
\frac{r+s}{r}F(G),
\frac{r+s}{s}F(H)
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is an exact identity, not merely an upper bound.

The proof is given separately in primal and dual form.

# 4. Primal upper bound

Because the graphs are regular,
\[
\pi_G(x)=\frac1n,\qquad
\pi_H(y)=\frac1q,\qquad
\pi_K(x,y)=\frac1{nq}.
\]

Let \((f_G^a)_{a\in V(G)}\) be an optimal Aldous--Fill source-flow family on \(G\), and \((f_H^b)_{b\in V(H)}\) an optimal family on \(H\).

For source \((a,b)\in V(K)\), decompose the required divergence as
\[
\delta_a\delta_b-\pi_G\pi_H
=
(\delta_a-\pi_G)\delta_b
+
\pi_G(\delta_b-\pi_H).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define \(F^{(a,b)}\) on \(K\) as follows.

### G-direction edges
On a \(G\)-edge
\[
((x,y),(x',y))
\]
put
\[
F^{(a,b)}_{(x,y),(x',y)}
=
\mathbf 1_{\{y=b\}}\,
f_G^a(x,x').
\tag{4.2}
\]

### H-direction edges
On an \(H\)-edge
\[
((x,y),(x,y'))
\]
put
\[
F^{(a,b)}_{(x,y),(x,y')}
=
\frac1n\,f_H^b(y,y').
\tag{4.3}
\]

The first term in (4.1) is exactly the divergence of (4.2), and the second is exactly the divergence of (4.3). Hence
\[
\operatorname{div}F^{(a,b)}
=
\delta_{(a,b)}-\pi_K.
\tag{4.4}
\]

So this is an admissible source-flow family for the original problem on \(K\).

## 4.1 Congestion on a G-edge

Fix a physical \(G\)-edge copy
\[
e=((x,y_0),(x',y_0)).
\]
Only sources with \(b=y_0\) contribute. Thus its weighted absolute load is
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_K(e)
&=
\sum_{a,b}\frac1{nq}
\left|
\mathbf1_{\{b=y_0\}}f_G^a(x,x')
\right|\\
&=
\frac1q
\sum_a\frac1n|f_G^a(x,x')|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

A \(G\)-edge has capacity
\[
c_G=\frac1{nr},
\]
whereas a \(K\)-edge has capacity
\[
c_K=\frac1{nq(r+s)}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Since the factor family has congestion \(F(G)\),
\[
\sum_a\frac1n|f_G^a(x,x')|
\le
\frac{F(G)}{nr}.
\]
Therefore
\[
L_K(e)
\le
\frac{F(G)}{nqr},
\]
and dividing by \(c_K\) gives
\[
\frac{L_K(e)}{c_K}
\le
\frac{r+s}{r}F(G).
\tag{4.7}
\]

## 4.2 Congestion on an H-edge

Fix a physical \(H\)-edge copy
\[
e=((x_0,y),(x_0,y')).
\]
Using (4.3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_K(e)
&=
\sum_{a,b}\frac1{nq}
\frac1n|f_H^b(y,y')|\\
&=
\frac1n
\sum_b\frac1q|f_H^b(y,y')|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]
Since \(H\)-edge capacity in \(H\) is \(1/(qs)\),
\[
L_K(e)
\le
\frac{F(H)}{nqs}.
\]
Dividing by \(c_K\) gives
\[
\frac{L_K(e)}{c_K}
\le
\frac{r+s}{s}F(H).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Thus
\[
F(K)
\le
\max\left\{
\frac{r+s}{r}F(G),
\frac{r+s}{s}F(H)
\right\}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

# 5. Dual lower bound

Let \(\ell^G\) be an optimal dual edge-length assignment for \(G\), normalized by
\[
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac1{nr}\ell^G_e=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\ell^G_e=nr.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Lift it to \(K=G\square H\) by assigning

- every copy of a \(G\)-edge \(e\) length
\[
\widetilde\ell_e
=
\frac{r+s}{r}\ell^G_e,
\tag{5.3}
\]
- every \(H\)-direction edge length \(0\).

There are \(q\) copies of each \(G\)-edge. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{e\in E(K)}c_K\widetilde\ell_e
&=
q\cdot
\frac1{nq(r+s)}
\cdot
\frac{r+s}{r}
\sum_{e\in E(G)}\ell^G_e\\
&=
1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]
So the lifted length vector is dual-feasible.

Because all \(H\)-direction edges have zero length, the \(H\)-coordinate may be changed for free. Any path changing the \(G\)-coordinate from \(x\) to \(x'\) still has to pay at least the shortest \(G\)-length between \(x,x'\). Hence
\[
d_{\widetilde\ell}\big((x,y),(x',y')\big)
=
\frac{r+s}{r}\,
d_{\ell^G}(x,x').
\tag{5.5}
\]

Averaging over the uniform product stationary measure,
\[
\sum_{(x,y),(x',y')}
\frac1{nq}\frac1{nq}
d_{\widetilde\ell}
=
\frac{r+s}{r}F(G).
\tag{5.6}
\]
By duality,
\[
F(K)\ge\frac{r+s}{r}F(G).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Repeating with an optimal dual on \(H\) gives
\[
F(K)\ge\frac{r+s}{s}F(H).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Combining (4.10), (5.7), and (5.8) proves (3.1).

# 6. Exact relaxation time and diameter tensorization

The transition matrix of \(K=G\square H\) is
\[
P_K
=
\frac r{r+s}P_G\otimes I
+
\frac s{r+s}I\otimes P_H.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Therefore its eigenvalues are
\[
\frac r{r+s}\lambda_i(G)
+
\frac s{r+s}\lambda_j(H).
\tag{6.2}
\]
The second-largest eigenvalue is obtained by exciting exactly one factor, so
\[
1-\lambda_2(K)
=
\min\left\{
\frac r{r+s}(1-\lambda_2(G)),
\frac s{r+s}(1-\lambda_2(H))
\right\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm rel}(G\square H)
=
\max\left\{
\frac{r+s}{r}\tau_{\rm rel}(G),
\frac{r+s}{s}\tau_{\rm rel}(H)
\right\}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The graph distance is additive:
\[
d_{G\square H}((x,y),(x',y'))
=
d_G(x,x')+d_H(y,y'),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
D(G\square H)=D(G)+D(H).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

# 7. New closure theorem for the sharp Aldous--Fill inequality

## Corollary 7.1
If both regular graphs \(G\) and \(H\) satisfy
\[
F(G)\le2\tau_GD_G,
\qquad
F(H)\le2\tau_HD_H,
\]
then their Cartesian product satisfies
\[
\boxed{
F(G\square H)
\le
2\tau_{G\square H}D_{G\square H}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof
From (3.1),
\[
F(G\square H)
\le
2\max\left\{
\frac{r+s}{r}\tau_GD_G,
\frac{r+s}{s}\tau_HD_H
\right\}.
\]
Using (6.3),
\[
F(G\square H)
\le
2\tau_{G\square H}\max\{D_G,D_H\}
\le
2\tau_{G\square H}(D_G+D_H).
\]
Then use (6.4). \(\square\)

Thus any regular counterexample cannot be created as a Cartesian product of regular factor graphs that already satisfy the conjecture.

# 8. Multi-factor form and Cartesian powers

Let \(G_i\) be finite connected \(r_i\)-regular graphs, \(i=1,\dots,k\), and put
\[
R:=\sum_{i=1}^k r_i.
\]
By induction on Theorem 3.1,
\[
\boxed{
F\left(\square_{i=1}^kG_i\right)
=
R\max_i\frac{F(G_i)}{r_i}.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Likewise
\[
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm rel}\left(\square_iG_i\right)
=
R\max_i\frac{\tau_{\rm rel}(G_i)}{r_i},
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
D\left(\square_iG_i\right)
=
\sum_iD(G_i).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Hence the sharp target is closed under finite Cartesian products of regular graphs.

For a single \(r\)-regular graph \(G\),
\[
\boxed{
F(G^{\square k})=kF(G),\qquad
\tau_{\rm rel}(G^{\square k})=k\tau_{\rm rel}(G),\qquad
D(G^{\square k})=kD(G).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
Therefore its normalized target ratio improves by a factor \(k\):
\[
\frac{F(G^{\square k})}
{2\tau_{\rm rel}(G^{\square k})D(G^{\square k})}
=
\frac1k
\frac{F(G)}{2\tau_{\rm rel}(G)D(G)}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

# 9. Exact examples beyond distance-regular graphs

The previous durable checkpoints prove the exact formula for distance-regular and distance-biregular graphs. Cartesian products now produce many exact examples outside those classes.

For cycles,
\[
F(C_n)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac n2,& n\text{ even},\\[1ex]
\dfrac{n^2-1}{2n},& n\text{ odd},
\end{cases}
\tag{9.1}
\]
because cycles are distance-regular and \(F=2\) times their mean distance.

Since every cycle is 2-regular,
\[
\boxed{
F(C_m\square C_n)
=
2\max\{F(C_m),F(C_n)\}.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
When \(m\ne n\), these toroidal graphs are generally not distance-regular, so (9.2) is a genuinely new exact family relative to the immediately preceding radial theorem.

Examples:
\[
F(C_3\square C_4)=4,
\qquad
F(C_3\square C_5)=\frac{24}{5},
\]
matching direct finite metric-LP computations in this run.

Another checked example is
\[
F(K_3\square\mathrm{Petersen})=5,
\]
again matching the exact tensor formula.

# 11. Literature/status check

A fresh bounded search through 2026-09-15 found no explicit resolution of Aldous--Fill Open Problem 4.49(ii).

There is important prior art specifically on Cartesian-product concurrent flows:

Paul S. Bonsma, *Sparsest cuts and concurrent flows in product graphs*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 136 (2004), 173--182, DOI 10.1016/S0166-218X(03)00439-6.

Its abstract states that an optimal uniform concurrent flow in a Cartesian product is constructed from optimal flows in the factors and proved optimal. Since regular graphs have uniform stationary weights, Theorem 3.1 is very likely an Aldous--Fill-normalized rederivation/specialization of that product-flow phenomenon, not a novelty claim. The useful new point for this project is the exact normalization, the source-flow proof, the matching dual scaling, and the direct closure calculation with \(\tau_{\rm rel}\) and graph diameter.
