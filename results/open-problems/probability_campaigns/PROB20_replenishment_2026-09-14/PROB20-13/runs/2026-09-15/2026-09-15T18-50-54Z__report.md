# 2. A diameter-two family

For an integer \(a\ge1\), define
\[
G_a:=K_1\vee\bigl(K_{1,a}\sqcup K_{2a}\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Use the following names.

- \(h\): the universal vertex from the outer \(K_1\);
- \(x\): the center of the star \(K_{1,a}\);
- \(L=\{\ell_1,\dots,\ell_a\}\): the star leaves;
- \(C=\{c_1,\dots,c_{2a}\}\): the \(K_{2a}\) clique.

Thus \(h\) is adjacent to every other vertex; \(x\) is adjacent to every leaf; \(C\) is a clique; there are no other edges. The graph is simple, connected, noncomplete, and has diameter
\[
D(G_a)=2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The degrees are
\[
d_h=3a+1,\qquad
d_x=a+1,\qquad
d_{\ell_i}=2,\qquad
d_{c_j}=2a.
\tag{2.3}
\]
The number of edges is
\[
\begin{aligned}
m
&=(3a+1)+a+\binom{2a}{2}\\
&=2a^2+3a+1\\
&=(a+1)(2a+1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

# 3. Exact optimum on the family

## Theorem 3.1
For every integer \(a\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
F(G_a)=\frac{4a(3a+1)}{(a+1)(2a+1)}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Moreover this optimum is exactly a cut lower bound, hence
\[
F(G_a)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}(G_a),
\tag{3.2}
\]
which is stronger than the requested
\(F(G_a)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}D\) because \(D=2\).

### 3.1 Exact cut lower bound
Take the cut
\[
C\mid (V\setminus C).
\]
There are exactly \(2a\) cut edges, namely \(hc_j\), \(1\le j\le2a\).

The degree sum on the clique side is
\[
\sum_{c\in C}d_c=(2a)(2a)=4a^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Since \(2m=4a^2+6a+2\), the degree sum on the complementary side is
\[
2m-4a^2=6a+2=2(3a+1).
\tag{3.4}
\]
In the scaled product-demand normalization, the total demand crossing the cut is therefore
\[
(4a^2)\,2(3a+1)=8a^2(3a+1).
\tag{3.5}
\]
Any routing must send this through the \(2a\) cut edges. Hence some cut edge has scaled load at least
\[
R:=\frac{8a^2(3a+1)}{2a}=4a(3a+1).
\tag{3.6}
\]
By (1.1),
\[
F(G_a)\ge\frac{R}{m}
=\frac{4a(3a+1)}{(a+1)(2a+1)}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

### 3.2 Explicit matching routing
We now route every product commodity with maximum scaled edge load exactly \(R\).

First route every pair internal to the clique \(C\) directly on its clique edge. For a fixed clique vertex \(c_j\), route every pair from \(c_j\) to \(V\setminus C\) first over \(c_jh\), and then from \(h\) to the final target according to the routing below. Thus the scaled load on \(c_jh\) from all cross-cut commodities is exactly
\[
d_{c_j}\sum_{v\notin C}d_v
=(2a)\,2(3a+1)
=R.
\tag{3.8}
\]

After the first hop from clique vertices to \(h\), all remaining routing takes place in the `book' subgraph on \(\{h,x\}\cup L\). It is convenient to aggregate commodities that now have the same endpoints in that book; the routing is then applied proportionally to the original constituent commodities, so no demand is changed.

Define
\[
T:=4a^2+3a+1.
\tag{3.9}
\]
The effective scaled demand between \(h\) and \(x\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=d_hd_x+\sum_{c\in C}d_cd_x\\
&=(3a+1)(a+1)+(2a)(2a)(a+1)\\
&=(a+1)T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.10}
\]
For each leaf \(\ell_i\), the effective scaled demand between \(h\) and \(\ell_i\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
U
&=d_hd_{\ell_i}+\sum_{c\in C}d_cd_{\ell_i}\\
&=2(3a+1)+(2a)(2a)(2)\\
&=2T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.11}
\]
The own demand between \(x\) and each \(\ell_i\) is
\[
V=2(a+1),
\tag{3.12}
\]
and each leaf pair \(\ell_i,\ell_j\) has demand \(4\).

Route the effective \(h\)-to-\(x\) demand as follows. Send
\[
p:=(3a+1)^2
\tag{3.13}
\]
directly over \(hx\). Send, for each \(i\), the amount
\[
D_0:=2(a-1)(2a+1)
\tag{3.14}
\]
over the two-edge path
\[
h-\ell_i-x.
\]
This is exact because
\[
p+aD_0=W.
\tag{3.15}
\]
Route every effective \(h\)-to-\(\ell_i\) demand \(U\) directly on \(h\ell_i\). Route every \(x\)-to-\(\ell_i\) demand \(V\) directly on \(x\ell_i\). Finally, route every leaf-pair demand \(4\) along
\[
\ell_i-x-\ell_j.
\]

To make the aggregation completely explicit: the commodities contributing to \(W\) are \(\{h,x\}\) and \(\{c_j,x\}\). Split each of those constituent demands in the same proportions \(p/W,D_0/W,\dots,D_0/W\). A \(c_j,x\) path first uses \(c_jh\), then takes the selected book path. Likewise the commodities contributing to each \(U\) are \(\{h,\ell_i\}\) and \(\{c_j,\ell_i\}\); each is routed through \(h\ell_i\) after the optional first edge \(c_jh\). Thus this is a literal fractional routing of the original stationary product demands, not a modified demand problem.

### 3.3 Exact edge loads
The clique cut edges already have load \(R\) by (3.8).

A clique-internal edge \(c_jc_k\) carries only its direct commodity, hence load
\[
(2a)^2=4a^2<R.
\tag{3.16}
\]

The edge \(hx\) has scaled load
\[
L_{hx}=p=(3a+1)^2.
\tag{3.17}
\]
The difference to \(R\) factors as
\[
R-L_{hx}
=(a-1)(3a+1)\ge0.
\tag{3.18}
\]

For each leaf,
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{h\ell_i}
&=D_0+U\\
&=2(a-1)(2a+1)+2(4a^2+3a+1)\\
&=4a(3a+1)\\
&=R.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.19}
\]

For each edge \(x\ell_i\), the load consists of its \(D_0\) share of the detoured \(h\)-to-\(x\) demand, the direct demand \(V\), and the \(a-1\) leaf-pair commodities of size \(4\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_{x\ell_i}
&=D_0+V+4(a-1)\\
&=4a^2+4a-4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.20}
\]
Here
\[
R-L_{x\ell_i}=4(2a^2+1)>0.
\tag{3.21}
\]
Thus every physical edge has scaled load at most \(R\), and some edges attain \(R\). By (1.1), this routing has congestion \(R/m\). Combined with (3.7), this proves (3.1). \(\square\)

### 3.4 Consequence for the target
The value in (3.1) is exactly the cut-congestion ratio of \(C\mid C^c\). The scalar Poincaré inequality applied to the cut indicator gives every cut ratio at most \(2\tau_{\rm rel}\). Hence
\[
F(G_a)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}(G_a)
<2\tau_{\rm rel}(G_a)D(G_a)
\]
for this noncomplete diameter-two family.

# 4. Shortest-path routing can be arbitrarily worse

The preceding exact family supplies a strong failed-route theorem.

## Theorem 4.1
Let \(F_{\rm geo}(G_a)\) denote the minimum congestion when every commodity is required to use only unweighted graph-geodesic paths. Then
\[
\boxed{
F_{\rm geo}(G_a)
\ge
\frac{4a^2+3a+1}{2a+1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{F_{\rm geo}(G_a)}{F(G_a)}
\ge
\frac{(a+1)(4a^2+3a+1)}{4a(3a+1)}
\sim\frac a3.
\tag{4.2}
\]
In particular the ratio between best geodesic routing and unrestricted optimal routing is unbounded, even among diameter-two simple unweighted graphs with the exact stationary product demands of Aldous–Fill.

### Proof
Consider the physical edge \(hx\).

The adjacent commodity \(\{h,x\}\) has scaled demand
\[
d_hd_x=(3a+1)(a+1)
\]
and every geodesic for this adjacent pair is the one-edge path \(hx\).

For every clique vertex \(c_j\), the vertices \(c_j\) and \(x\) are nonadjacent. Their only common neighbor is \(h\): the other neighbors of \(c_j\) lie in the clique, while the other neighbors of \(x\) are leaves. Therefore the unique length-two geodesic from \(c_j\) to \(x\) is
\[
c_j-h-x.
\]
The total scaled demand of these \(2a\) commodities is
\[
(2a)(2a)(a+1)=4a^2(a+1).
\]
Hence every geodesic routing has scaled load at least
\[
\begin{aligned}
W
&=(3a+1)(a+1)+4a^2(a+1)\\
&=(a+1)(4a^2+3a+1)
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]
on the single edge \(hx\). Dividing by \(m=(a+1)(2a+1)\) gives (4.1). Dividing (4.1) by the exact value (3.1) gives (4.2). \(\square\)

This kills a major candidate approach from the previous run: in a diameter-two graph, simply assigning every nonedge commodity among its common-neighbor two-hop paths (while adjacent commodities remain on their one-edge geodesics) cannot prove the universal theorem. Longer-than-geodesic paths are sometimes not just helpful but asymptotically essential for low congestion.

### Small explicit threshold failure
Already at \(a=2\),
\[
m=15,
\qquad
F(G_2)=\frac{56}{15}<4,
\tag{4.4}
\]
whereas every geodesic-only routing has
\[
F_{\rm geo}(G_2)\ge\frac{23}{5}=4.6.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Thus even the strengthened diameter-two target \(F\le4\) cannot be established by a universal shortest-path-only routing theorem.

# 5. What the family says about the remaining proof strategy

The obstruction is stronger than the previously archived canonical-electrical counterexample in a different direction.

- The electrical obstruction showed that one canonical non-geodesic flow family can exceed the diameter-two threshold even when the optimum is below it.
- The present family shows that **every** graph-geodesic routing can be arbitrarily suboptimal relative to the unrestricted fractional optimum.

Therefore a universal proof must genuinely exploit nonlocal path redistribution, cycles/circulations, an unrestricted metric-dual argument, or another mechanism that can deliberately route some adjacent or distance-two demand along longer paths to relieve local bottlenecks.

The example also clarifies why the metric LP dual is the natural object: an optimal dual may place length on a local bottleneck edge, while the primal escapes that bottleneck by using combinatorially longer but dual-cheaper routes.

# 8. Next attack

The most useful next step is to derive a diameter-two LP that allows controlled non-geodesic cycle detours rather than freezing adjacent commodities on their own edges.

One concrete formulation is to take the unrestricted path LP and eliminate long paths by cycle augmentation variables relative to an initial two-hop routing. The family \(G_a\) identifies the exact operation that must be representable: demand nominally using the bottleneck \(hx\) is pushed around triangles \(h-\ell_i-x-h\). A general theorem would need to show that diameter-two graphs contain enough such cycle space, in an aggregate weighted sense, to drive every edge below scaled load \(4m\).

A dual version may be cleaner. To prove \(F\le4\), it is enough to show for every nonnegative edge-length assignment \(\ell\),
\[
\sum_{u<v}d_ud_v\,d_\ell(u,v)
\le
4m\sum_{e\in E}\ell_e.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The present family shows why replacing \(d_\ell(u,v)\) by the cheapest **unweighted-geodesic** path cost is too crude: the true \(\ell\)-shortest path can and sometimes must use more graph edges to avoid an expensive bottleneck. The next attack should therefore work directly with weighted shortest paths in (8.1), perhaps by charging each pair to a low-length walk of bounded *weighted* cost rather than bounded combinatorial length.

Parallel exact computation should search diameter-two graphs for dual optimizers whose support forces genuinely long weighted-shortest paths, and attempt to infer a local cycle-charging inequality from those certificates.
