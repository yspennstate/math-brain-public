## 2. Exact stationary product-demand formulation retained

For a finite simple unweighted graph with \(m=|E|\), every oriented physical edge satisfies
\[
\pi_xP_{xy}=\frac1{2m}.
\]
With \(g^a=\pi_af^a\), cycle cancellation and finite path decomposition give the exact undirected product-demand problem with unordered demands
\[
h_{uv}=2\pi_u\pi_v\qquad(u<v)
\]
and edge capacity
\[
c_e=\frac1{2m}.
\]
Its minimum congestion is exactly \(F(G)\).

The metric LP dual is
\[
F(G)=\max_{d\in\mathsf{Met}(V)}
\left\{
\sum_{u,v}\pi_u\pi_v d(u,v):
\frac1{2m}\sum_{e\in E}d(e)\le1
\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
F(G)=2\sup_d
\frac{\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d(X,Y)}
{\mathbb E_{\pi P}d(X_0,X_1)}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
No weighted-chain or altered-demand problem is substituted.

Primary source for the normalization and the diameter question: Aldous–Fill, *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on Graphs*, Lemma 4.21 and Open Problem 4.49(ii):

https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/book.pdf

# 3. New general lemma: noncomplete simple graphs have \(\tau_{\rm rel}\ge1\)

## Lemma 3.1
Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph. If \(G\) is not complete, then
\[
\boxed{\lambda_2(P)\ge0,\qquad \tau_{\rm rel}\ge1.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof
Let
\[
S=D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}
\]
be the normalized adjacency matrix. It is real symmetric and similar to \(P=D^{-1}A\), so it has the same eigenvalues.

Because \(G\) is connected and noncomplete, choose two nonadjacent vertices and a shortest path between them. The first three vertices of that shortest path, call them \(x_0,x_1,x_2\), induce a copy of \(P_3\): \(x_0x_1\) and \(x_1x_2\) are edges while \(x_0x_2\) is not, otherwise the chosen path would not be shortest.

The principal \(3\times3\) submatrix of \(S\) on these vertices is
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a&0\\
a&0&b\\
0&b&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a=\frac1{\sqrt{d_0d_1}}>0,
\quad
b=\frac1{\sqrt{d_1d_2}}>0.
\]
Its eigenvalues are
\[
\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\quad0,\quad-\sqrt{a^2+b^2}.
\]
By Cauchy interlacing for principal submatrices,
\[
\lambda_2(S)\ge\lambda_2(M)=0.
\]
Hence \(\lambda_2(P)=\lambda_2(S)\ge0\), proving (3.1). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2: the diameter-two target becomes \(F\le4\)
Every connected noncomplete diameter-two graph satisfies \(\tau_{\rm rel}\ge1\), hence
\[
2\tau_{\rm rel}D\ge4.
\]
Therefore the stronger statement
\[
\boxed{F(G)\le4\quad\text{for every connected diameter-two simple graph}}
\tag{3.2}
\]
would settle the entire noncomplete diameter-two case of Aldous–Fill 4.49(ii). Complete graphs are already exact equality cases with \(D=1\) and \(\tau_{\rm rel}<1\).

This removes \(\tau_{\rm rel}\) from the first unresolved diameter regime and sharpens the next target substantially.

# 5. Exact optimum \(F(H)=15/4\)

## 5.1 Dual/cut lower bound
From (4.3),
\[
\pi(S)=\frac38,
\qquad
\pi(T)=\frac58.
\]
The total stationary product demand crossing the cut is
\[
2\pi(S)\pi(T)
=2\cdot\frac38\cdot\frac58
=\frac{15}{32}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Each physical edge has capacity \(1/(2m)=1/24\), and the cut has three edges, hence total cut capacity \(1/8\). Therefore every routing has congestion at least
\[
\frac{15/32}{1/8}=\frac{15}{4}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equivalently, the metric dual certificate is the cut pseudometric
\[
d(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
0,&u,v\text{ on the same side of }S\mid T,\\

\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Only the three cut edges have dual length 8, so
\[
\frac1{24}(8+8+8)=1,

and its objective is exactly \(15/4\).

# 6. New obstruction: canonical electrical routing exceeds the diameter-two threshold

For each source \(a\), define the canonical electrical potential by
\[
L v^{(a)}=\delta_a-\pi,
\tag{6.1}
\]
where \(L\) is the combinatorial Laplacian, and set
\[
f^{a}_{xy}=v^{(a)}_x-v^{(a)}_y.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Because every physical conductance \(\pi_xP_{xy}=1/(24)\) is equal, this is precisely the standard unit-conductance electrical source-to-stationary flow, up to a common conductance scaling that cancels in the divergence normalization.

Solving (6.1) exactly over \(\mathbb Q\), with gauge \(v^{(a)}_6=0\), gives on the edge \(35\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
a&v^{(a)}_3-v^{(a)}_5\\\hline
0&-17/120\\
1&19/120\\
2&19/120\\
3&43/120\\
4&-17/120\\
5&-29/120\\
6&-1/24
\end{array}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Using the stationary weights (4.3), the weighted absolute load is
\[
\sum_a\pi_a|f^a_{35}|
=\frac{27}{160}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Dividing by the physical edge capacity \(1/24\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\psi_{35}^{\rm elec}
=24\cdot\frac{27}{160}
=\frac{81}{20}
=4.05.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
The exact checker verifies that this is the maximum electrical edge congestion on \(H\).

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\psi(\mathbf f^{\rm elec})=\frac{81}{20}>4>rac{15}{4}=F(H).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is an important failed-route certificate. Even in diameter two, and even on a graph whose *optimal* stationary product flow is a cut-tight \(15/4\), the most canonical electrical source-to-stationary routing can cross the clean universal threshold \(4\). Consequently a proof of (3.2) cannot simply assert that the raw canonical electrical family always has congestion at most 4. Any electrical proof must modify, randomize, truncate, or otherwise exploit the electrical potentials beyond using them directly as the final routing.

# 10. Next concrete attacks

Introduce routing variables \(q_{uv}(z)\) over common neighbors and seek a direct proof that stationary product demands can be assigned so that every edge load is at most \(2/m\), equivalent to congestion 4. ## B. LP-dual attack on diameter two
The target \(F\le4\) is equivalent to
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d(X,Y)
\le
2\,\mathbb E_{\pi P}d(X_0,X_1)
\tag{10.1}

for every metric \(d\) on the vertex set. Seek a decomposition of every finite metric into inequalities generated by length-two graph paths and cut inequalities. The graph \(H\) shows a cut metric can still be extremal even when the electrical primal is not.

## C. Modified electrical routing
Use electrical potentials only to choose or bias two-hop paths, then rebalance residual edge overload by cycle circulations. Equation (6.6) identifies a concrete local overload to repair, while the exact optimum (5.4) supplies a target balanced circulation.

## D. Certified counterexample search

A numerical violation of \(F\le4\) must be converted into exact rational primal/dual evidence before it counts as a refutation.
