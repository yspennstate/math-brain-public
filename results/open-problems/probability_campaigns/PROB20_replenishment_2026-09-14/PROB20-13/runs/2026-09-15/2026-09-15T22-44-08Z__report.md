# 2. Setup: a cone over a strongly regular graph

Let `H` be a connected noncomplete strongly regular graph with parameters
\[
(q,r,\lambda,\mu).
\]
Thus `H` has `q` vertices, is `r`-regular, every adjacent pair has exactly `lambda` common neighbors, and every nonadjacent pair has exactly `mu` common neighbors. Connected noncomplete strong regularity implies `mu>0` and diameter two.

Let
\[
G=K_1\vee H
\]
be the cone over `H`, with hub `h` adjacent to every base vertex.

The degrees in `G` are
\[
d_h=q,\qquad d_v=r+1\quad(v\in V(H)).
\tag{2.1}
\]
The edge count is
\[
m=q+\frac{qr}{2}=\frac{q(r+2)}2.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\pi_h=\frac1{r+2},
\qquad
\pi_v=\frac{r+1}{q(r+2)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
Every physical edge, spoke or base edge, has the same Aldous–Fill capacity
\[
c_e=\frac1{2m}=\frac1{q(r+2)}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Also
\[
D(G)=2.
\tag{2.5}
\]

We will prove the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
F(G)=\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q(r+2)}.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]

# 3. Strongly regular counting identity

We use only the standard parameter identity
\[
\boxed{
(q-r-1)\mu=r(r-\lambda-1).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
For completeness, fix a base vertex `x` and count ordered incidences `(y,z)` where `y` is adjacent to `x`, while `z` is adjacent to `y` but not to `x` and `z!=x`.

For each of the `r` choices of `y`, exactly `r-1-lambda` neighbors of `y` lie outside `N[x]`, giving
\[
r(r-\lambda-1).
\]
For each of the `q-r-1` nonneighbors `z` of `x`, exactly `mu` common neighbors of `x,z` may serve as `y`, giving
\[
(q-r-1)\mu.
\]
Equating the counts proves (3.1).

# 4. Explicit product-demand routing

Write
\[
a:=\frac{2(r+1)}{q(r+2)^2}
\tag{4.1}
\]
for the unordered stationary product demand between the hub and any fixed base vertex, and
\[
b:=\frac{2(r+1)^2}{q^2(r+2)^2}
\tag{4.2}
\]
for the unordered stationary product demand between any two distinct base vertices.

Route as follows.

### 4.1 Hub/base pairs
For every base vertex `u`, route the full demand `a` directly on the spoke `hu`.

### 4.2 Adjacent base pairs
For every edge `uv` of `H`, route the full demand `b` directly on the base edge `uv`.

### 4.3 Nonadjacent base pairs
For every nonedge `{u,v}` of `H`:

- route the fraction
  \[
  \theta:=\frac1{r+1}
  \tag{4.3}
  \]
  of its demand along `u-h-v`;
- route the remaining fraction
  \[
  1-\theta=\frac r{r+1}
  \tag{4.4}
  \]
  equally among the `mu` common neighbors `w` of `u,v`, along the two-edge paths `u-w-v`.

This is a valid fractional routing of exactly the original stationary product demands. Every path used has graph length at most two.

# 5. Exact spoke load

Fix a spoke `hu`. It receives the direct hub/base demand `a`.

The base vertex `u` has exactly
\[
q-r-1
\]
nonneighbors in `H`. Each corresponding nonedge commodity sends the fraction `theta=1/(r+1)` through the hub, and therefore across `hu`.

Thus
\[
L_{\rm spoke}
=a+(q-r-1)\frac{b}{r+1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Substituting (4.1)–(4.2),
\[
L_{\rm spoke}
=\frac{2(r+1)}{q(r+2)^2}
+\frac{2(r+1)(q-r-1)}{q^2(r+2)^2}
\]
\[
=\boxed{
\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q^2(r+2)^2}
}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

# 6. Exact base-edge load

Fix a base edge `xy` of `H`. It first receives its direct adjacent-pair demand `b`.

We count nonadjacent base commodities whose common-neighbor routing can use `xy`.

For a commodity `{x,z}`, the edge `xy` is used through the common neighbor `y` exactly when `z` is adjacent to `y` but not adjacent to `x`. Since `x,y` are adjacent and have exactly `lambda` common neighbors, there are
\[
r-1-\lambda
\]
such vertices `z`.

The same count occurs with `x,y` interchanged. Therefore there are exactly
\[
2(r-1-\lambda)
\tag{6.1}
\]
nonedge commodities for which `xy` is one of the `mu` possible common-neighbor two-hop routes.

Each contributes
\[
\frac r{r+1}\frac b\mu
\]
to `xy`. Hence
\[
L_{\rm base}
=b+2(r-1-\lambda)\frac r{r+1}\frac b\mu.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Using (3.1),
\[
\frac{r(r-1-\lambda)}\mu=q-r-1,
\]
so
\[
L_{\rm base}
=b\left(1+\frac{2(q-r-1)}{r+1}\right)
\]
\[
=b\frac{2q-r-1}{r+1}
\]
\[
=\boxed{
\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q^2(r+2)^2}
}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Comparing (5.2) and (6.3), **every physical edge of the cone carries exactly the same stationary product load**.

Dividing by the common capacity (2.4), the routing congestion is
\[
\boxed{
\Psi
=\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q(r+2)}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Therefore
\[
F(G)\le\Psi.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The choice `theta=1/(r+1)` is not arbitrary: it is exactly the value that equalizes spoke and base-edge loads.

# 7. Matching metric-dual certificate

Assign the constant dual length
\[
\ell_e=2
\qquad(e\in E(G)).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Since there are `m` physical edges and each has capacity `1/(2m)`,
\[
\sum_ec_e\ell_e
=m\frac1{2m}\,2=1,
\]
so this is dual-feasible.

Its shortest-path metric is exactly twice ordinary graph distance:
\[
d_\ell(u,v)=2d_G(u,v).
\tag{7.2}
\]
Thus the dual objective is
\[
2\,\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G(X,Y).
\tag{7.3}
\]

We compute the mean graph distance. The ordered hub/base contribution is
\[
2\pi_h\pi(V(H))
=\frac{2(r+1)}{(r+2)^2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
For base/base ordered pairs, there are `qr` adjacent ordered pairs at distance one and `q(q-r-1)` nonadjacent ordered pairs at distance two. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E d_G(X,Y)
=\frac{2(r+1)}{(r+2)^2}
+\frac{(r+1)^2}{q^2(r+2)^2}
\left(qr+2q(q-r-1)\right)
\]
\[
=\boxed{
\frac{(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q(r+2)}
}.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Hence the dual value is
\[
\boxed{
2\mathbb E d_G(X,Y)
=\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q(r+2)}
=\Psi.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

The explicit primal routing and constant-metric dual certificate match. Therefore:

## Theorem 7.1 — exact cone formula
For every connected noncomplete strongly regular graph `H=srg(q,r,lambda,mu)`,
\[
\boxed{
F(K_1\vee H)
=\frac{2(r+1)(2q-r-1)}{q(r+2)}
=2\,\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G(X,Y).
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

No automorphism transitivity is used. Strong regularity enters only through the exact common-neighbor counts and identity (3.1).

# 8. The Aldous–Fill target for this entire family

From (7.7),
\[
F(G)
<\frac{2(r+1)\,2q}{q(r+2)}
=\frac{4(r+1)}{r+2}
<4.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The cone is connected and noncomplete, so the previously archived interlacing lemma gives
\[
\tau_{\rm rel}\ge1.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Since `D=2`,
\[
F(G)<4\le4\tau_{\rm rel}=2\tau_{\rm rel}D.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Thus every cone over a connected noncomplete strongly regular graph satisfies the requested sharp diameter inequality.

If one also allows the degenerate complete base `H=K_q`, then the cone is `K_{q+1}`, already covered by the complete-graph equality case.

# 10. Why this is a useful extension of the existing campaign

The previous wheel theorem handled cones over cycles by a two-orbit metric optimization and showed that `F` can exceed `4` and approach `6` when the base cycle grows.

The present theorem shows a contrasting regime: when the base is strongly regular, hence has diameter two and exact common-neighbor regularity, a single mixture
\[
\frac1{r+1}\times\text{hub route}
+\frac r{r+1}\times\text{common-neighbor route}
\]
perfectly balances every edge. The uniform dual metric `ell=2` is then exactly optimal and `F<4`.

This family is not obtained merely from graph automorphism transitivity: the proof works for every strongly regular graph with the given parameters, including ones without enough automorphisms to reduce the full LP by orbit averaging.

The result suggests a broader possible principle for cones over **coherent diameter-two bases**: if nonedge demand can be split through common neighbors so that the induced base-edge load is sufficiently uniform, then a small hub fraction can equalize spoke and base loads. Strong regularity makes the balancing exact.

# 13. Next attack

The cone calculation suggests two concrete directions.

### A. Cones over association schemes / distance-regular bases of higher diameter
Let `H` have finitely many distance relations with exact intersection numbers. Introduce one routing fraction per relation: some mass through the hub, the rest through canonical base geodesics. Solve the finite load-balancing LP symbolically and compare its dual to the cone relaxation time. Wheels (cycle base) and strongly regular bases are now two endpoints of this program.

### B. Search by the actual sharp ratio
Wheel graphs show that large `F` alone is misleading. Future certified searches should rank candidates by
\[
\rho(G):=\frac{F(G)}{2\tau_{\rm rel}D}
\]
and rationalize both the metric-LP optimum and the spectral factor. Priority should go to low-symmetry diameter-two or three graphs with moderate `tau_rel`, not merely large raw congestion.

### C. Perturb strong regularity
Start from an SRG cone, delete or switch a small number of base edges, and compute the first-order imbalance of the explicit equal-load routing. This may reveal either a robust inequality neighborhood or a way to drive the sharp ratio upward while keeping `tau_rel` controlled.
