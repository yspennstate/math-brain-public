## 6. B2 — ATTACK THE NEGATION FIRST: no Cayley / regular-group construction

The first construction-side attack was to ask whether the unresolved graph could be a Cayley graph or, equivalently here, admit a regular automorphism subgroup.

### Proposition 6.1 — exact Cayley exclusion

There is **no** Cayley strongly regular graph with parameters `(69,20,7,5)`. Equivalently, a hypothetical `srg(69,20,7,5)` has no regular automorphism subgroup of order `69`.

### Proof

Every group of order `69=3\cdot23` is cyclic. Indeed, the number of Sylow-23 subgroups divides `3` and is `1 mod 23`, hence equals `1`; the number of Sylow-3 subgroups divides `23` and is `1 mod 3`, and since `23` is `2 mod 3`, it also equals `1`. The two normal Sylow subgroups give

\[
H\cong C_3\times C_{23}\cong C_{69}.
\]

Suppose a graph `G` with the target parameters were Cayley on `C_69`. Write `G=Cay(C_69,D)` with `|D|=20` and `D=-D`. If `\zeta` is a primitive 69th root of unity, the nonprincipal adjacency eigenvalues are

\[
\lambda_j=\sum_{d\in D}\zeta^{jd},\qquad j=1,\dots,68.
\]

By (5.2), each `\lambda_j` is one of the rational integers `5` or `-3`, with multiplicities `23` and `45` respectively.

The Galois group `Gal(Q(\zeta)/Q)=(Z/69Z)^\times` sends `\lambda_j` to `\lambda_{uj}`. Because `\lambda_j` is rational, it is fixed by every Galois automorphism. Hence each Galois orbit of nonzero characters must be monochromatic: all its eigenvalues are the same one of `5,-3`.

The nonzero characters split into exactly three unit-group orbits, according to character order:

- order `69`: size `\varphi(69)=44`;
- order `23`: size `\varphi(23)=22`;
- order `3`: size `\varphi(3)=2`.

Therefore the multiplicity of eigenvalue `5` would have to be a subset-sum of `{44,22,2}`. The possible subset-sums are

\[
0,2,22,24,44,46,66,68,
\]

none of which is `23`. This contradicts (5.2). QED.

### Outcome

This is an exact infinite-class exclusion, but it is only **PARTIAL**: the source asks about all graphs, not Cayley graphs. It does, however, remove the most obvious algebraic construction family and shows that any positive construction must have less regular symmetry.

No checker was created for this proof because its finite arithmetic and Galois-orbit argument are completely written on the page.

## 7. B8 — DISCRETIZE: an exact rooted block-equation equivalence

Fix a vertex `x`. Let

- `N=N(x)`, so `|N|=20`;
- `M=V(G)\setminus(\{x\}\cup N)`, so `|M|=48`;
- `L` be the `20 x 20` adjacency matrix of the induced local graph `G[N]`;
- `C` be the `48 x 48` adjacency matrix of `G[M]`;
- `B` be the `20 x 48` incidence matrix of edges from `N` to `M`.

Then, after ordering vertices as `x,N,M`,

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
0 & \mathbf1^T & 0\\
\mathbf1 & L & B\\
0 & B^T & C
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7.1}
\]

The degrees are forced exactly:

\[
L\mathbf1=7\mathbf1,\qquad
B\mathbf1=12\mathbf1,\qquad
B^T\mathbf1=5\mathbf1,\qquad
C\mathbf1=15\mathbf1. \tag{7.2}
\]

Explanation: a neighbour of `x` has exactly `\lambda=7` neighbours within `N`, hence `12` neighbours in `M`; a non-neighbour of `x` has exactly `\mu=5` neighbours in `N`, hence `15` in `M`.

Taking the three nontrivial blocks of (5.1) gives the exact identities

\[
BB^T=15I_{20}+4J_{20}+2L-L^2, \tag{7.3}
\]

\[
B^TB=15I_{48}+5J_{48}+2C-C^2, \tag{7.4}
\]

and

\[
LB+BC=2B+5J_{20\times48}. \tag{7.5}
\]

Conversely, if zero-diagonal symmetric `0/1` matrices `L,C` and a `0/1` matrix `B` satisfy (7.2)--(7.5), then the block matrix (7.1) is a `0/1` symmetric zero-diagonal adjacency matrix satisfying (5.1) and degree 20. Thus it is exactly an `srg(69,20,7,5)`.

So the source problem is **equivalent** to the finite rooted feasibility system (7.2)--(7.5). This is not a relaxation.

### Design interpretation

\[

for distinct local vertices `u,v`. Their block co-occurrence multiplicity is

\[
m_{uv}=(BB^T)_{uv}
=\begin{cases}
6-c_{uv},&u\sim_L v,\\

## 8. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: spectral / Gram pruning

Define

\[

Likewise, for every nonprincipal eigenvalue `\eta` of the 15-regular graph `C`, equation (7.4) gives

\[

There is more: `rank(B)\le20`, so `B^TB` has nullity at least `28`. Its principal eigenvector has eigenvalue `60`, not zero. Since (7.4) is a polynomial in `C` plus `5J`, `B^TB` commutes with `C`; on `\mathbf1^\perp` its zero eigenvalues occur exactly when `q(\eta)=0`. Hence:

> **At least 28 of the 47 nonprincipal eigenvalues of `C` are exactly `5` or `-3`.**

Ordinary principal-submatrix interlacing with the global spectrum (5.2) sharpens the bookkeeping: if

\[
15=\eta_1\ge\eta_2\ge\cdots\ge\eta_{48}

\eta_{25}=\cdots=\eta_{48}=-3. \tag{8.4}
