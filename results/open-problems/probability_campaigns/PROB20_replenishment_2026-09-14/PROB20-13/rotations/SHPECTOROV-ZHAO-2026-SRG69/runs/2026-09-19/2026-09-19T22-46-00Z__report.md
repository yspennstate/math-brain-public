## 9. Section-I(e): nearest solved problem and the missing ingredient here

Nearest solved analogue selected for recovery:

**Jernej Azarija and Tilen Marc, `srg(75,32,10,16)` nonexistence, arXiv:1509.05933 / Linear Algebra Appl. 557 (2018).**

Their decisive mechanism was not merely “use star complements.” They first forced a sufficiently large induced structure, extended only the remaining few vertices needed to a star complement, canonically reduced the resulting family, built comparability graphs, and then used exact clique-number elimination. Their paper reports that the approach works because the star complement is small (19 vertices for their high-multiplicity eigenvalue), many generated cases are isomorphic, and the comparability graphs often have useful automorphisms. Their final remarks explicitly list `(69,20,7,5)` as a plausible future target for the same architecture.

What that solved problem has that the present work still lacks:

- a **forced induced seed family** already small enough to enumerate completely;
- a certified extension pipeline from each seed to all star complements;
- comparability graphs whose required clique obstruction is computationally tractable after isomorphism/symmetry reduction;
- an end-to-end completeness proof tying the generated list to every hypothetical source graph.

For SRG69, the `b=0` root-star gives 21 of the required 24 star-complement vertices, which is promising, but the three-vertex extension is still parameterized by an unclassified 7-regular local graph and its 5-subset incidence data. That is the exact gap to port, not another generic star-complement definition.

The newer Shpectorov-Zhao `(85,14,3,2)` proof supplies a second comparison: it gets a finite local family because the local graph is cubic of small order, then applies combinatorial and Euclidean elimination. SRG69 lacks that cheap local classification.
