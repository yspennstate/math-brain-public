## 3. B29 — add the point at infinity: a new exact `V_4` symmetry after the third anchor

Let `chi` denote the quadratic character of `F_p`, with `p = 1 (mod 4)`, so `chi(-1)=1`. As in the preceding checkpoint, normalize a clique edge to `{0,1}` and define

`H_p = {x in F_p : chi(x)=chi(x-1)=1}`,

with two vertices adjacent when their difference is a quadratic residue.

Fix `u in H_p`. The next localization is

`K_u = H_p[N_{H_p}(u)]`

so that

`K_u = {x : chi(x)=chi(x-1)=chi(x-u)=1}`.

The useful way to see its symmetry is projectively: regard the four marked points as

`{0,1,u,infinity}`.

The three double transpositions of this four-point set give the Möbius maps

`a_u(x) = u/x`,

`b_u(x) = (x-u)/(x-1)`,

`c_u(x) = u(x-1)/(x-u)`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact Klein-four automorphisms of `K_u`

For every prime `p = 1 (mod 4)` and every `u in H_p`, the identity together with `a_u,b_u,c_u` acts on `K_u` as a Klein four group `V_4`, and every element is a graph automorphism of the induced Paley graph on `K_u`.

### Proof

Since `u`, `u-1`, and `-1` are quadratic residues, so are `1-u` and all products/quotients below whose factors are displayed as residues.

For `a=a_u`, if `x in K_u`, then

`a(x)=u/x`,

`a(x)-1=(u-x)/x=-(x-u)/x`,

`a(x)-u=u(1-x)/x=-u(x-1)/x`.

Each has quadratic character `1`, hence `a(x) in K_u`. Also

`a(x)-a(y) = -u(x-y)/(xy)`.

The multiplier `-u/(xy)` is a square for `x,y in K_u`, so adjacency is preserved.

For `b=b_u`,

`b(x)=(x-u)/(x-1)`,

`b(x)-1=(1-u)/(x-1)`,

`b(x)-u=(1-u)x/(x-1)`.

Again all three are residues. Moreover, as a Möbius transformation `b` has determinant `u-1`; therefore

`b(x)-b(y) = (u-1)(x-y)/((x-1)(y-1))`,

and the multiplier is a square on `K_u`. Thus `b` preserves adjacency.

For `c=c_u`,

`c(x)=u(x-1)/(x-u)`,

`c(x)-1=(u-1)x/(x-u)`,

`c(x)-u=u(u-1)/(x-u)`,

so `c(x) in K_u`. Its Möbius determinant is `u(1-u)`, again a residue, while the denominators `(x-u)(y-u)` are residues. Hence `c` also preserves adjacency.

Direct composition gives

`a^2=b^2=c^2=id`,

`ab=ba=c`.

Thus `{id,a,b,c}` is a `V_4` action by graph automorphisms. QED.

This is more than a code trick: it identifies the next exact projective stabilizer after the `S_3` symmetry of the two-point localization. It also reveals a limitation. Möbius transformations are determined by three marked points; after a generic four-point configuration is fixed, only a constant-size set stabilizer remains, and after additional labelled anchors there is generically no nontrivial projective stabilizer. Therefore symmetry breaking can buy large finite-search constant factors but cannot, by itself, yield the source-level polylogarithmic upper bound.

## 4. B19 — make the two-level stabilizer chain into a complete exact algorithm

The preceding report proved that the maps `x -> 1-x` and `x -> 1/x` generate an `S_3` automorphism action on `H_p`. Combining that action with Lemma 3.1 gives a two-level exact exclusion scheme.

Suppose a proposed exact value is `omega(G_p)=w`. To exclude a `(w+1)`-clique:

1. Paley affine edge-transitivity normalizes an edge of a hypothetical `(w+1)`-clique to `{0,1}`. The remaining vertices form a `(w-1)`-clique in `H_p`.
2. Choose one of those vertices. The `S_3` action maps it to the canonical representative `u` of its `S_3` orbit. The other `w-2` vertices form a `(w-2)`-clique in `K_u`.
3. Choose one of those vertices. Lemma 3.1 maps it to the canonical representative `v` of its `V_4` orbit inside `K_u`. The remaining vertices form a `(w-3)`-clique in `K_u[N(v)]`.

Therefore it suffices, for **every** `S_3` orbit representative `u` and **every** `V_4` orbit representative `v` in `K_u`, to prove that `K_u[N(v)]` has no clique of size `w-3`.

### Lemma 4.1 — completeness of the double-orbit certificate

If every one of those doubly anchored decision problems is negative, then `G_p` has no clique of size `w+1`.

### Proof

If a `(w+1)`-clique existed, step 1 would produce a `(w-1)`-clique `C` in `H_p`. Select any `u_0 in C`; an `S_3` automorphism maps `u_0` to the canonical representative `u` of its orbit and maps the rest of `C` to a `(w-2)`-clique in `K_u`. Select any vertex `v_0` of that clique; a `V_4` automorphism of `K_u` maps `v_0` to the canonical representative `v` of its orbit. The other `w-3` vertices then lie in `K_u[N(v)]` and form a `(w-3)`-clique, contradicting the corresponding negative decision certificate. QED.

Each final decision problem is solved by deterministic integer/bitset branch-and-bound. At every recursive node the program greedily partitions the current candidate set into independent color classes. If fewer than `r` classes are required, no `r`-clique can exist in that candidate graph, giving a rigorous upper-bound prune. Otherwise the search branches over candidate vertices and removes exactly the explored branch before continuing. A completed negative return therefore exhausts the finite search tree. No floating-point SDP, random sample, timeout, or incomplete branch is accepted as an upper certificate.

The new program also checks the `V_4` maps and adjacency preservation before accepting each `u` anchor. Witness finding is separate: a deterministic degree-first DFS finds a lower-bound clique and the witness is then checked directly by quadratic-residue arithmetic. The lower-bound finder need not be exhaustive.

## 5. B25 — count the same forbidden clique through canonical anchor incidences

The double-orbit proof can be read as a counting/completeness statement rather than only an algorithmic one. Let `C` be the family of forbidden `(w-1)`-cliques in `H_p`, and consider incidences `(C,u_0,v_0)` with distinct `u_0,v_0 in C`. Mapping `u_0` to its `S_3` representative and then `v_0` to its `V_4` representative sends every such incidence into one of the finitely many archived doubly anchored obligations. Conversely every search obligation is exactly a candidate location for such a canonical incidence.

Thus a hypothetical forbidden clique cannot hide between orbit ranges: it contributes at least one canonical incidence to the exhaustive obligation list. The structural audit independently reconstructs all `S_3` and `V_4` orbit counts and verifies that the archived search ranges partition the entire canonical incidence list.

This same count exposes the asymptotic limitation: the quotient gains only constant orbit factors, roughly `6` and then `4`. There is no growing projective symmetry factor left to exploit. A uniform source-level proof must add a genuinely analytic/additive ingredient controlling the **simultaneous** quadratic-character constraints after several anchors, rather than iterating finite group quotients alone.
