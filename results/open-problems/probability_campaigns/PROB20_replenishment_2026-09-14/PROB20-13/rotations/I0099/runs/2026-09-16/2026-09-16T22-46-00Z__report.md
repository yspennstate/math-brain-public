## 3. Setup: the sign-pattern distribution seen from a clique

Let `C={c_1,...,c_k}` be any Paley clique and let `chi` be the quadratic character on `F_p`, extended by `chi(0)=0`. For `x notin C`, define the sign pattern

\[
\sigma_C(x)=(\chi(x-c_1),\ldots,\chi(x-c_k))\in\{-1,+1\}^k.
\]

For `epsilon in {-1,+1}^k`, write

\[
n_\epsilon:=|\{x\in F_p\setminus C:\sigma_C(x)=\epsilon\}|,
\qquad N:=p-k.
\]

For each subset `T subseteq C`, define the full and punctured character sums

\[
A_T:=\sum_{x\in F_p}\chi\!\left(\prod_{c\in T}(x-c)\right),
\]

\[
B_T:=\sum_{x\in F_p\setminus C}\prod_{c\in T}\chi(x-c),
\]

with the empty product equal to one. The vector `(B_T)` is exactly the Boolean Fourier transform of the external sign-pattern histogram `(n_epsilon)`.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact puncturing correction

For every `T subseteq C`,

\[
\boxed{B_T=A_T-(k-|T|).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

For `x=c_j in C`, the summand in `A_T` is zero if `c_j in T`. If `c_j notin T`, every factor `chi(c_j-c)` with `c in T` equals `+1`, because all nonzero differences of clique vertices are quadratic residues and `p=1 mod 4` makes the sign orientation irrelevant. Hence each clique point outside `T` contributes exactly one to `A_T`, while the points of `T` contribute zero. The total contribution removed when passing from `A_T` to `B_T` is therefore exactly `k-|T|`. QED.

### Lemma 3.2 — exact Boolean Fourier inversion

For every `epsilon in {-1,+1}^k`,

\[
\boxed{
 n_\epsilon
 =2^{-k}\sum_{T\subseteq C}
 \left(\prod_{c_i\in T}\epsilon_i\right)B_T.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

For `x notin C`, the indicator that `sigma_C(x)=epsilon` is

\[
2^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^k(1+\epsilon_i\chi(x-c_i)).
\]

Expand the product and sum over `x notin C`. The coefficient indexed by `T` is exactly `B_T`. QED.

The all-plus cell `n_+` is the number of vertices outside `C` adjacent to every point of `C`. Hence if `C` is a **maximal** clique (in particular, if it is a maximum clique),

\[
\boxed{n_+=0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This converts maximality into the vanishing of one atom of a highly structured sign-pattern distribution.

## 4. B9 — RANDOMIZE: identify the correct null model and what Paley already matches exactly

A random `G(p,1/2)` heuristic treats the signs `chi(x-c_i)` as independent fair signs. Under that null model, the all-plus external cell has expected size approximately

\[
\frac{p-k}{2^k},
\]

so the natural extension threshold is logarithmic in `p`. The question is what deterministic Paley information can rigorously force a comparable atom estimate.

The first two extension-degree moments can in fact be computed exactly for **every** Paley clique. For `x notin C`, let

\[
t_C(x):=|N(x)\cap C|.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact first two factorial moments of clique extension degree

For every Paley clique `C` of size `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\notin C} t_C(x)
=
\frac{k(p-2k+1)}2,
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{x\notin C}(t_C(x))_2
=
\frac{k(k-1)(p-4k+3)}4.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

The Paley graph is regular of degree `(p-1)/2`. Every vertex of `C` has exactly `k-1` neighbors inside `C`, so counting incidences `(c,x)` with `c in C`, `x notin C`, `c~x` gives (4.1).

For (4.2), count ordered triples `(c,d,x)` with distinct ordered `c,d in C`, `x notin C`, and `x` adjacent to both. Since `c,d` are adjacent, strong regularity gives exactly `(p-5)/4` common neighbors. Of these, the other `k-2` clique vertices lie in `C`, so the number outside `C` is

\[
\frac{p-5}{4}-(k-2)=\frac{p-4k+3}{4}.
\]

There are `k(k-1)` ordered pairs `(c,d)`. QED.

**Outcome B9.** Paley cliques already match the random-graph extension picture at the level of the first two factorial moments, up to the exact finite corrections forced by deleting the clique itself. But first and second moments do not control the extreme atom `t_C(x)=k`. The source problem is therefore not a missing variance calculation; it is a high-dimensional atom/anticoncentration problem.

## 5. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: exact empty-cell Fourier-energy theorem

Let `q=2^k`. Boolean Parseval applied to (3.2) gives

\[
\sum_{\epsilon}n_\epsilon^2
=
\frac1q\sum_{T\subseteq C}B_T^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Since `B_empty=N`, subtracting the uniform component gives

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\epsilon}\left(n_\epsilon-\frac Nq\right)^2
=
\frac1q\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq C}B_T^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `C` is maximal, the all-plus bin is zero. Among all nonnegative vectors with total mass `N` and one prescribed zero coordinate, the squared Euclidean distance from the uniform vector is minimized by distributing all mass uniformly over the remaining `q-1` coordinates. The minimum is

\[
\frac{N^2}{q(q-1)}.
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem 5.1 — maximal-clique deficit energy

For every maximal Paley clique `C` of size `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\varnothing\ne T\subseteq C}B_T^2
\ge
\frac{(p-k)^2}{2^k-1}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is an exact deterministic consequence of maximality, with no probabilistic approximation.

The first two Fourier levels are also exact. For `|T|=1`, `A_T=0`; for `|T|=2`, the standard quadratic-character correlation gives `A_T=-1`. Using (3.1), in both cases

\[
\boxed{B_T=-(k-1).}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{1\le|T|\le2}B_T^2
=
\left(k+\binom{k}{2}\right)(k-1)^2
=
\frac{k(k+1)(k-1)^2}{2}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Subtracting these forced low levels from (5.3) gives the fully explicit high-order requirement

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|T|\ge3}B_T^2
\ge
\left[
\frac{(p-k)^2}{2^k-1}
-
\frac{k(k+1)(k-1)^2}{2}
\right]_+.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus whenever the right side is positive, maximality cannot be carried only by the degree-one and degree-two Paley pseudorandomness: some genuinely higher-order character correlation is forced.

For `r=|T|>=3`, the squarefree polynomial in `A_T` is not a square, so the usual Weil bound gives

\[
|A_T|\le(r-1)\sqrt p,
\]

while (3.1) gives

\[
|B_T|\le(r-1)\sqrt p+k-r.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Combining (5.6) with these termwise estimates does **not** point in the desired source direction: it mainly says that a maximal clique cannot be too small unless sufficiently high-order correlations appear. This explains precisely why the naive plan "use Boolean Parseval plus individual Weil bounds" does not prove a polylogarithmic **upper** bound for `omega(G_p)`.

**Outcome B10.** The random heuristic can be derandomized into an exact Fourier-energy identity, but the resulting inequality exposes a direction-of-inequality barrier. A source-level proof must control the *shape* or collective cancellation of the high-order Fourier spectrum, not just its total energy and individual Weil envelopes.

## 6. B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS: a rigorous no-go for universal low-degree sign tests

A tempting strategy is to hope that the absence of an all-plus external vertex for a maximal clique must already be witnessed by one character sum `B_T` of bounded or otherwise low degree. Abstract Boolean Fourier analysis shows that this implication is false without using additional finite-field structure.

### Proposition 6.1 — parity obstruction

For every `k>=2`, let `mu_odd` be the uniform probability measure on

\[
\{\epsilon\in\{-1,+1\}^k:\prod_{i=1}^k\epsilon_i=-1\}.
\]

Then:

1. the all-plus vector has zero mass;
2. every nonempty Fourier coefficient of degree `<k` is exactly zero;
3. the only nonconstant Fourier coefficient is the full parity character, whose expectation is `-1`.

#### Proof

The support is a coset of the codimension-one subgroup `prod_i epsilon_i=+1`. Characters of the Boolean group average to zero over a subgroup coset unless they lie in its annihilator. The annihilator contains only the trivial character and the full parity character. The all-plus vector has parity `+1`, so it is outside the support. QED.

This gives a clean methodological barrier:

> **No abstract argument that only knows low-degree sign moments up to any degree `<k` can, by itself, force the all-plus atom to be nonzero.**

Equivalently, even exact `(k-1)`-wise independence is compatible with complete absence of the common-neighbor sign pattern.

The Paley sign-pattern distribution is of course not an arbitrary Boolean distribution; it comes from the algebraic family

\[
x\mapsto(\chi(x-c_1),\ldots,\chi(x-c_k)).
\]

Therefore the parity example is **not** a counterexample to the Paley conjecture. It is a rigorous route-kill for a proof architecture that treats the `2^k` sign cells as an abstract small-bias distribution and uses only proper-subset Fourier coefficients. Any successful argument along this language must exploit algebraic realizability of the sign patterns, collective relations among many `A_T`, or a genuinely full-degree input.

**Outcome B27.** Swapping the quantifiers identifies the missing theorem sharply. The desired statement is not "every low-degree character sum is small"; that is compatible with an empty target atom. One needs a theorem of the form: **the particular quadratic-character sign distribution generated by a clique cannot imitate the parity obstruction at all relevant degrees.** This is a concrete higher-order algebraic pseudorandomness target.

## 7. Synthesis: what a source-level proof must add

The three routes combine into a more precise picture:

1. **Random null (B9):** a logarithmic clique threshold comes from atom size roughly `p/2^k`.
2. **Exact derandomization (B10):** maximality forces a quantified Boolean-Fourier deficit energy, with levels one and two completely explicit.
3. **Quantifier swap (B27):** low-degree moment control alone can never certify the target atom; a parity-type obstruction survives all proper low-degree tests.

Thus a plausible source-level route must prove a specifically **algebraic high-order anticoncentration theorem** for

\[
\{(\chi(x-c))_{c\in C}:x\in F_p\setminus C\}
\]

when `C-C` consists only of squares. The previous projective/cross-ratio work and the present Boolean-Fourier formulation now meet at the same missing object: collective high-order correlation, rather than another single Weil estimate.

One concrete next target is to classify or rule out parity-like high-order spectra using the clique constraint. For example, if most proper-subset `B_T` were tiny while the all-plus cell vanished, Proposition 6.1 suggests that the spectrum would have to concentrate near the full-set character. The finite-field realization may make such concentration impossible. Proving any quantitative version of that statement would be qualitatively different from the square-root-scale arguments already exhausted.
