## 3. Recovered quartic identities from the previous report

Let `chi` be the quadratic character of `F_p`, extended by `chi(0)=0`, and let `C` be a Paley clique of size `k`. Put

\[
S_C(x)=\sum_{a\in C}\chi(x-a).
\]

For an ordered four-tuple of distinct clique vertices define

\[
\lambda(a,b,c,d)
=\frac{(b-d)(c-a)}{(b-a)(c-d)}.
\]

Since every nonzero clique difference is a square and `chi(-1)=1`, every such cross-ratio belongs to

\[
\mathcal H_p
=\{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(\lambda)=\chi(1-\lambda)=1\},
\qquad
h:=|\mathcal H_p|=\frac{p-5}{4}.
\]

For the Legendre curve

\[
E_\lambda:y^2=t(t-1)(t-\lambda)
\]

write

\[
a_p(\lambda)=p+1-\#E_\lambda(\mathbf F_p)
=-\sum_t\chi(t(t-1)(t-\lambda)).
\]

The preceding report proved, by an explicit Möbius change of variables,

\[
\sum_x\chi((x-a)(x-b)(x-c)(x-d))
=-1-a_p(\lambda(a,b,c,d)).
\tag{3.1}
\]

If `m_C(lambda)` is the number of ordered distinct clique quadruples of cross-ratio `lambda`, then

\[
M_4(C):=\sum_x S_C(x)^4
=P_0(k,p)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where

\[
P_0(k,p)=-k^4-6k^3+(3p+15)k^2-(2p+9)k.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Also

\[
\sum_{\lambda}m_C(\lambda)=(k)_4=:M
\tag{3.4}
\]

and the exact cross-ratio/projective-energy identity is

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm cr}(C)
:=\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)^2
=\sum_{g\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)}(I_C(g))_4,
\tag{3.5}
\]

where `I_C(g)=|C cap g^{-1}C|` (poles excluded). The same prior report proved that if `I_C(g)>=3`, then `g` has square determinant class and its denominator quadratic character is constant on the overlap.

Finally, with

\[
J(\lambda):=\sum_x\chi(x(x-1)(x-\lambda))=-a_p(\lambda)
\]

and

\[
\Sigma_{12}(p)
:=\sum_{x,\lambda}
\chi\!\left(x(x-1)\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda-x)\right),
\tag{3.6}
\]

the preceding report proved directly from the exact indicator of `H_p` that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda)

\tag{3.7}
\]

## 4. B18 — say it in another language: the two-variable sum is a finite-field Selberg sum

Let `rho` be a quartic multiplicative character on `F_p`, so

\[
\rho^2=\chi,
\]

and extend all multiplicative characters by zero at zero.

For a multiplicative character `Psi`, define the direct discriminant sum

\[
\mathcal L_2(A,B;\Psi)
:=\sum_{\substack{F\text{ monic}\\\deg F=2}}
A(F(0))B(F(1))\Psi(D_F),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `D_F` is the discriminant. This is simply Evans's `L_2(A,B,C phi)` with `Psi=C phi`.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact Selberg reduction

For every prime `p=1 mod 4`,

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma_{12}(p)
=
\mathcal L_2(\chi,\chi;\rho)
+
\mathcal L_2(\chi,\chi;\bar\rho).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

A monic split quadratic with distinct roots `x,y` is

\[
F(T)=(T-x)(T-y).
\]

Then

\[
F(0)=xy,
\qquad
F(1)=(1-x)(1-y),
\qquad
D_F=(x-y)^2.
\]

If `D_F` is a nonzero square, then

\[
\rho(D_F)=\rho((x-y)^2)=\chi(x-y)
\]

and likewise `\bar rho(D_F)=chi(x-y)`. Therefore the sum of the two discriminant characters is `2 chi(x-y)`.

If `D_F` is a nonsquare, `rho(D_F)` and `bar rho(D_F)` are opposite fourth roots of unity, so their sum is zero. If `D_F=0`, both extended characters vanish.

Thus the right side of (4.2) receives contributions only from distinct split quadratics. Each unordered pair `{x,y}` determines one such monic quadratic and contributes

\[
2\chi(xy)\chi((1-x)(1-y))\chi(x-y).
\]

Because `chi(-1)=1`, the two ordered pairs `(x,y)` and `(y,x)` contribute equally to `Sigma_12`; their total is exactly the displayed quantity. Terms with `x=y`, or with a root at `0` or `1`, vanish on both sides. Summing proves (4.2). QED.

### Proposition 4.2 — square-root cancellation after averaging over the Paley cross-ratio locus

For every prime `p=1 mod 4`, `p>=13`,

\[
\boxed{|\Sigma_{12}(p)|\le 2p.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda)\right|
\le \frac{p+3}{2}
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and, writing

\[
\bar a_p
:=\frac1h\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda),
\]

we obtain the uniform average bound

\[
\boxed{
|\bar a_p|
\le b_p:=\frac{2(p+3)}{p-5}
=2+\frac{16}{p-5}
\le4.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Apply Evans's Lemma 3.5 to each of the two sums in Proposition 4.1.

For the first, in Evans's notation take

\[
A=B=\phi=\chi,
\qquad
C=\bar\rho,
\]

so that `C phi = rho`. The lemma's hypothesis is

\[
\{A,B,ABC\}\not\subseteq\{1,\bar C\}.
\]

Here the left set contains `phi`, while the right set is `{1,rho}`; since `phi`, `rho`, and `1` are distinct for a genuine quartic character, the hypothesis holds. The second sum is the conjugate parameter choice `C=rho` and satisfies the same hypothesis.

Evans's displayed evaluation is a quotient of Gauss sums. Under these substitutions every Gauss sum occurring in the numerator and denominator is attached to a nontrivial character. Using the standard exact modulus

\[
|G(\eta)|=\sqrt p
\]

for nontrivial multiplicative characters `eta`, the product in Lemma 3.5 has absolute value exactly `p`. Therefore

\[
|\mathcal L_2(\chi,\chi;\rho)|=p,
\qquad
|\mathcal L_2(\chi,\chi;\bar\rho)|=p.
\]

Equation (4.2) and the triangle inequality prove (4.3). Equation (3.7) gives

\[
\left|\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda)\right|
\le\frac{2p+6}{4}=\frac{p+3}{2},
\]

which is (4.4). Since `h=(p-5)/4`, division by `h` gives (4.5); for `p>=13`, `2+16/(p-5)<=4`. QED.

### What this actually adds

Individual Hasse bounds are

\[
|a_p(\lambda)|\le2\sqrt p.
\]

Proposition 4.2 shows that **the mean trace over the Paley cross-ratio locus is bounded by an absolute constant**. Thus the raw average of the Legendre traces is not the obstruction to a polylogarithmic clique bound. Any quartic-moment obstruction must lie in the correlation between the clique's highly nonuniform cross-ratio multiplicity and the centered Legendre trace.

This is exactly the kind of cancellation that the previous run's termwise-Hasse argument could not see. The mechanism is classical finite-field Selberg theory; no novelty claim is made for the Selberg evaluation itself.

A stronger phase identity observed in the previous finite checks,

\[
\Sigma_{12}(p)=2\operatorname{Re}J(\rho,\rho)^2,
\]

is very likely a specialization of the same classical Selberg formula (and agrees with the standard CM description of `y^2=x^3-x`), but I **still do not use or promote that phase identity here** because this run did not complete a convention-safe source derivation of its exact phase. The modulus theorem above is enough for the new rigorous consequence.

## 5. B24 — find the extremal object: center the trace and isolate excess projective energy

The Selberg estimate allows us to remove the uniform component of `m_C` exactly.

Set

\[
\mu:=\frac{M}{h}=\frac{(k)_4}{h},
\qquad
\bar a_p:=\frac1h\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda).
\]

Define the **excess cross-ratio energy**

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)
:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}(m_C(\lambda)-\mu)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Because `sum m_C=M`, direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)
=\mathcal E_{\rm cr}(C)-\frac{M^2}{h}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Using (3.5), this is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)
=
\sum_{g\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)}(I_C(g))_4
-
\frac{(k)_4^2}{h}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus the new quantity is exactly the amount by which projective self-overlap exceeds the uniform cross-ratio baseline.

Define also the centered Legendre-trace variance

\[
V_p
:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}
(a_p(\lambda)-\bar a_p)^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — exact centered fourth-moment decomposition

For every Paley clique `C`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\lambda}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)
=M\bar a_p
+
\sum_{\lambda}(m_C(\lambda)-\mu)
(a_p(\lambda)-\bar a_p).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\sum_{\lambda}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)-M\bar a_p
\right|
\le
\sqrt{\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)V_p}.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

For `p>=13`, Hasse plus Proposition 4.2 yields the explicit crude variance bound

\[
\boxed{
V_p\le h(2\sqrt p+b_p)^2,
\qquad
b_p=\frac{2(p+3)}{p-5}\le4.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

#### Proof

Expand

\[
m_C(\lambda)=\mu+(m_C(\lambda)-\mu),
\quad
a_p(\lambda)=\bar a_p+(a_p(\lambda)-\bar a_p).
\]

The two centered families each have sum zero, so the mixed expansion collapses to (5.5). Cauchy--Schwarz gives (5.6). Finally

\[
|a_p(\lambda)-\bar a_p|
\le 2\sqrt p+b_p
\]

for each `lambda` by Hasse and (4.5), and summing squares over `h` parameters gives (5.7). QED.

### Extremal interpretation

Suppose one forgets all arithmetic feasibility of `m_C` and remembers only its total mass `M` and excess `L^2` energy. Then (5.6) is **best possible as a Hilbert-space inequality**: the extremal centered weight is aligned with the centered trace vector. Therefore no improvement can come from another manipulation using only

1. `sum m_C=M`, and
2. `sum(m_C-mu)^2=E_exc`.

A source-level improvement has to use additional feasibility of actual clique cross-ratio measures: integrality, the `S_3` anharmonic orbit constraints, or, most structurally, the `PGL_2/PSL_2` overlap identity and determinant/denominator-sign restrictions proved in the preceding run.

This is a useful negative result: after centering, the remaining obstacle is no longer a generic moment inequality. It is specifically an **arithmetic projective-overlap discrepancy problem**.

## 6. B3 — prove something stronger: a refined excess-energy inverse theorem

The clique itself creates a deterministic fourth-moment spike:

\[
M_4(C)\ge k(k-1)^4,
\tag{6.1}
\]

because `S_C(x)=k-1` for every `x in C`.

Define as before

\[
\Delta_4(k,p):=k(k-1)^4-P_0(k,p).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Combining (3.2), Theorem 5.1, Proposition 4.2 and (5.7) gives a refinement of the previous run's raw-energy inverse theorem.

### Theorem 6.1 — excess projective energy forced by a large clique

For every prime `p=1 mod 4`, `p>=13`, and every Paley clique `C` of size `k`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)
\ge
\frac{
\bigl(\Delta_4(k,p)-M b_p\bigr)_+^2
}{
 h(2\sqrt p+b_p)^2
},
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

where

\[
M=(k)_4,
\quad h=\frac{p-5}{4},
\quad b_p=\frac{2(p+3)}{p-5},
\quad (u)_+=\max(u,0).
\]

Equivalently, using projective overlaps,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{g\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)}(I_C(g))_4
-
\frac{(k)_4^2}{h}
\ge
\frac{
\bigl(\Delta_4(k,p)-(k)_4 b_p\bigr)_+^2
}{
 h(2\sqrt p+b_p)^2
}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

From (3.2) and (6.1),

\[
\Delta_4(k,p)
\le
-\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda).
\]

In particular,

\[
\Delta_4(k,p)
\le
\left|\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)\right|.
\]

By (5.5), (5.6), and `|bar a_p|<=b_p`,

\[
\left|\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)\right|
\le
M b_p+\sqrt{\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)V_p}.
\]

Hence

\[
(\Delta_4-Mb_p)_+
\le
\sqrt{\mathcal E_{\rm exc}(C)V_p}.
\]

Use (5.7) and square to obtain (6.3). Substitute (5.3) to obtain (6.4). QED.

### Interpretation and limitation

The preceding run forced large **raw** projective energy. The new theorem forces large **excess over the uniform cross-ratio baseline**, after proving that the uniform trace component is only `O(1)` per ordered quadruple.

This is strictly better aligned with the source problem: a pseudorandom clique should have nearly uniform cross-ratio distribution, so the relevant obstruction is excess projective self-correlation rather than total energy.

However, the quartic spike still becomes coercive only around the cube-root regime. A hypothetical source counterexample with

\[
k=p^{o(1)}
\]

and `k` larger than every fixed power of `log p` can evade this fourth-order inequality. Therefore Theorem 6.1 remains **PARTIAL**. It identifies what a higher-order route must control but does not prove Conjecture 25.

The strongest next analytic target is now precise: obtain a clique-specific upper bound on the centered correlation

\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}
(m_C(\lambda)-\mu)
(a_p(\lambda)-\bar a_p)
\tag{6.5}
\]

that beats generic Cauchy--Schwarz by exploiting the fact that `m_C` comes from determinant-square projective overlaps with coherent denominator signs. A generic trace-function large sieve is insufficient unless it sees those adversarial feasibility constraints.
