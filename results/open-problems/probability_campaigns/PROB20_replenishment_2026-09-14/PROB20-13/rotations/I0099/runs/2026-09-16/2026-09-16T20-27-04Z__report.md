## 2. Mathematical idea before code

The preceding run isolated a concrete fourth-moment target but stopped at the statement

\[
M_4(C):=\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\left|\sum_{a\in C}\chi(x-a)\right|^4.
\]

The new idea is to **not** bound the quartic character sums individually. Instead:

1. identify every four-distinct shift sum exactly with the Frobenius trace of a Legendre elliptic curve indexed by the cross-ratio of the four clique points;
2. group the entire fourth moment by cross-ratio;
3. count equal cross-ratios a second way through `PGL_2(F_p)` overlaps;
4. use the clique condition to force all transformations contributing large overlap into `PSL_2(F_p)` together with a denominator-sign rigidity.

This converts the analytic cancellation problem into an exact projective-energy/inverse-theorem problem.

No literature novelty is claimed for the classical quartic-to-elliptic transformation or for standard cross-ratio facts. The point of this run is the exact synthesis in the present Paley-clique attack and the identification of the next missing estimate.

## 3. B25 — exact fourth moment as a trace-weighted cross-ratio statistic

Let `C` be a Paley clique of size `k`, and write `chi` for the quadratic character of `F_p`, with `chi(0)=0`. Set

\[
S_C(x)=\sum_{a\in C}\chi(x-a).
\]

For four distinct `a,b,c,d in C`, define

\[
\lambda(a,b,c,d)
 =\frac{(b-d)(c-a)}{(b-a)(c-d)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because every nonzero difference of two clique vertices is a square and `chi(-1)=1`, both

\[
\chi(\lambda)=1,\qquad \chi(1-\lambda)=1.
\]

Thus every such cross-ratio lies in

\[
\mathcal H_p=\{\lambda\in\mathbf F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(\lambda)=\chi(1-\lambda)=1\},
\qquad |\mathcal H_p|=\frac{p-5}{4}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

For `lambda != 0,1`, let

\[
E_\lambda:y^2=t(t-1)(t-\lambda),
\]

and denote its Frobenius trace by

\[
a_p(\lambda)=p+1-\#E_\lambda(\mathbf F_p)
=-\sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p}\chi(t(t-1)(t-\lambda)).
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proposition 3.1 — exact quartic sum

For four distinct clique vertices,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_x\chi((x-a)(x-b)(x-c)(x-d))=-1-a_p(\lambda(a,b,c,d)).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
\alpha=\frac{b-d}{b-a},
\qquad
t=\alpha\frac{x-a}{x-d}.
\]

This Möbius transformation sends `a,b,d` to `0,1,infinity` and sends `c` to the cross-ratio (3.1). Solving for the four linear factors gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
x-a&=\frac{t(d-a)}{t-\alpha},\\
x-b&=\frac{(d-b)(t-1)}{t-\alpha},\\
x-c&=\frac{(d-c)(t-\lambda)}{t-\alpha},\\
x-d&=\frac{\alpha(d-a)}{t-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence, away from `x=d`, the quartic character equals

\[
\chi(K)\chi(t(t-1)(t-\lambda)),
\quad
K=\alpha(d-a)^2(d-b)(d-c).
\]

Every factor contributing to `chi(K)` is a quotient/product of clique differences, so `chi(K)=1`. The map `x != d` is a bijection onto `t != alpha`. Moreover `alpha`, `alpha-1`, and `alpha-lambda` are all quadratic residues (again by the clique-difference condition and `chi(-1)=1`), so

\[
\chi(\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-\lambda))=1.
\]

The omitted `x=d` term is zero, while the omitted `t=alpha` term in the complete cubic sum is `1`. Thus the quartic sum is the complete cubic character sum minus `1`, namely `-a_p(lambda)-1`. QED.

Now define the ordered cross-ratio multiplicity

\[
m_C(\lambda)=\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in C^4:\ a,b,c,d\text{ distinct and }\lambda(a,b,c,d)=\lambda\}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem 3.2 — exact fourth-moment identity

\[
\boxed{
M_4(C)
=P_0(k,p)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda),
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
P_0(k,p)=-k^4-6k^3+(3p+15)k^2-(2p+9)k.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

### Proof

Expand `S_C(x)^4` and classify ordered quadruples of shifts by multiplicity pattern.

- Pattern `4`: `k` ordered tuples, each contributes `p-1`.
- Pattern `3+1`: `4k(k-1)` tuples, and after removing a square factor the standard nondegenerate quadratic sum is `-1`.
- Pattern `2+2`: `6 binom(k,2)=3k(k-1)` tuples, each contributes `p-2`.
- Pattern `2+1+1`: `12 k binom(k-1,2)` tuples. Removing the repeated square factor gives a nondegenerate quadratic sum `-1`, but the repeated root was counted as `+1` in that reduced quadratic sum whereas the original term there is zero. Hence each contributes `-2`.
- Pattern `1+1+1+1`: Proposition 3.1 gives `-1-a_p(lambda)` for every ordered distinct quadruple.

The number of ordered all-distinct quadruples is `(k)_4`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_4(C)
={}&k(p-1)-4k(k-1)+3k(k-1)(p-2)\\
&-12k(k-1)(k-2)-(k)_4
-\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda).
\end{aligned}
\]

Expanding the polynomial part gives exactly (3.7). QED.

The exact self-check independently evaluates both sides for maximum Paley cliques at `p=29,37,41` and verifies Proposition 3.1 for every ordered distinct quadruple in those examples.

## 4. B25 continued — cross-ratio energy equals projective self-overlap energy

Define the cross-ratio energy

\[
\mathcal E_{\rm cr}(C)=\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For `g in PGL_2(F_p)`, define

\[
I_C(g)=|\{x\in C:g(x)\in C\}|,
\]

where a pole maps to infinity and is not counted in the affine set `C`.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact projective-energy identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm cr}(C)
=\sum_{g\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_p)}(I_C(g))_4.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

The left side counts ordered pairs of ordered distinct quadruples `(q,q')` from `C` having the same cross-ratio. `PGL_2` acts sharply 3-transitively on the projective line: for the first three coordinates of `q,q'` there is a unique projective transformation `g`; equality of their cross-ratios is exactly the condition that the same `g` sends the fourth coordinate of `q` to the fourth coordinate of `q'`. Conversely, choosing `g` and an ordered four-tuple of distinct points from `C cap g^{-1}C` produces exactly one such pair. There are `(I_C(g))_4` such ordered four-tuples. Summing over `g` gives (4.2). QED.

The small-prime exact checker enumerates all `|PGL_2(F_29)|=24360` transformations for a 4-clique at `p=29` and verifies both sides equal `96`.

### Proposition 4.2 — determinant-square and denominator-sign rigidity

Write

\[
g(x)=\frac{Ax+B}{Cx+D},\qquad \Delta=AD-BC.
\]

If `I_C(g)>=3`, then

\[
\boxed{\chi(\Delta)=1.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Furthermore, when `C != 0` in the matrix coefficient (to avoid collision with the clique notation, call this coefficient `gamma` below), the values

\[
\chi(\gamma x+D)
\]

are constant on the overlap `C cap g^{-1}C`.

### Proof

For two distinct overlap points `x,y`, both `x-y` and `g(x)-g(y)` are clique differences and hence squares. The exact identity

\[
g(x)-g(y)=\frac{\Delta(x-y)}{(\gamma x+D)(\gamma y+D)}
\]

therefore gives

\[
\chi(\Delta)\,\chi(\gamma x+D)\,\chi(\gamma y+D)=1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Apply (4.4) to three distinct overlap points `x,y,z`. Dividing the `(x,y)` and `(x,z)` equations shows the denominator signs at `y,z` agree; cyclically all three agree. Then the `(y,z)` equation forces `chi(Delta)=1`. Repeating with each additional overlap point shows the denominator sign is constant on the whole overlap. QED.

Thus every projective transformation contributing nontrivially to the fourth-order energy lies in `PSL_2(F_p)` and has an additional sign coherence on its clique overlap. This is a much smaller and more structured inverse problem than arbitrary `PGL_2` energy.

## 5. B2 — what a polynomially large counterexample would force

The clique itself gives a fourth-moment spike:

\[
M_4(C)\ge k(k-1)^4,
\tag{5.1}
\]

because `S_C(x)=k-1` for every `x in C`.

Define

\[
\Delta_4(k,p)=k(k-1)^4-P_0(k,p).
\tag{5.2}
\]

By (3.6), if `Delta_4>0` then

\[
\left|\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)\right|\ge\Delta_4(k,p).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hasse's bound gives `|a_p(lambda)|<=2 sqrt(p)`. Since `|H_p|=(p-5)/4`,

\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda)^2\le p(p-5).
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz and (4.1) therefore prove the exact inverse consequence

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{\rm cr}(C)
\ge \frac{\Delta_4(k,p)^2}{p(p-5)}
\qquad(\Delta_4(k,p)>0).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Here

\[
\Delta_4(k,p)
=k\left(k^4-3k^3+12k^2-3kp-19k+2p+10\right).
\tag{5.5}
\]

So once `k^3` is a sufficiently large multiple of `p`, a clique must have abnormally large projective self-overlap energy, and Proposition 4.2 says that this energy is supported on determinant-square transformations with coherent denominator signs.

This is a genuine inverse theorem **only in the polynomially-large regime**. It does not settle the source negation. A refutation of the polylogarithmic conjecture could have `k=p^{o(1)}` while still exceeding every fixed power of `log p`; then `Delta_4` need not be positive. The quartic route therefore identifies a first exponent-breaking milestone, not the final source scale. To reach polylogarithmic scale one needs the higher-moment analogue of the previous run's restricted hypercontractive condition, or a high-order projective-energy hierarchy.

**Outcome B2:** the negation-first route now has a precise structural consequence for any polynomially large clique, but it exposes why fourth order alone cannot confront a `p^{o(1)}` counterexample.

## 6. B21 — trace-function / Jacobi-sum literature port and its exact mismatch

The quartic sum in Proposition 3.1 is not an arbitrary Weil sum: it is the Frobenius trace of the Legendre family. This matches two bodies of machinery located in the literature check:

1. Nica (Finite Fields Appl. 110, 2026; arXiv:2507.09991) develops transformation formulae for quadratic character sums with quartic/cubic polynomial arguments and explicitly relates them to elliptic curves.
2. Katz/Perret-Gentil style trace-function and large-sieve methods control parameter families when the relevant monodromy is known; Perret-Gentil's IMRN paper gives large-sieve/zero-density estimates for coherent trace-function families, and his short-sum work proves Gaussian behavior under suitable averaging.

The obstacle is now exact: our weight is not uniform in `lambda`. It is the adversarial, clique-dependent multiplicity `m_C(lambda)`. A theorem about the unweighted distribution of `a_p(lambda)` does not directly bound

\[
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda).
\tag{6.1}
\]

What would transfer is a **weighted discrepancy / large-sieve estimate** that exploits a norm or structural constraint on `m_C`; Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 give exactly such structural constraints in group-action language.

There is also a sharper centering lead. Let

\[
J(\lambda)=\sum_x\chi(x(x-1)(x-\lambda))=-a_p(\lambda)
\]

and

\[
\Sigma_{12}(p)=\sum_{x,\lambda}
\chi\bigl(x(x-1)\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda-x)\bigr).
\tag{6.2}
\]

An elementary indicator expansion gives the **proved identity**

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal H_p}a_p(\lambda)
=-\frac{\Sigma_{12}(p)+6}{4}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed,

\[
1_{\mathcal H_p}(\lambda)
=\frac{(1+\chi\lambda)(1+\chi(1-\lambda))}{4}
-\frac12 1_{\lambda=0}-\frac12 1_{\lambda=1},
\]

and after summing against `J(lambda)` the four terms are respectively `0,1,1,Sigma_12`, while `J(0)=J(1)=-1`.

The exact checker found a striking **finite lead** for every tested prime `p=1 mod 4` up to `97`: if `rho` is a quartic character and `J(rho,rho)` its quartic Jacobi sum, then

\[
\Sigma_{12}(p)=2\operatorname{Re}J(\rho,\rho)^2.
\tag{6.4?}
\]

This was checked with exact Gaussian-integer arithmetic for `p=5,13,17,29,37,41,53,61,73,89,97`. **I do not promote (6.4?) to a theorem in this report**: I did not recover or write a complete source-grade derivation of that exact identity. It is recorded as a concrete Jacobsthal/Jacobi-sum lead for the next run. If (6.4?) is established, the standard `|J(rho,rho)|=sqrt(p)` would give an `O(p)` bound for the unweighted trace sum on `H_p`, allowing (6.1) to be centered around the uniform cross-ratio distribution. The remaining hard object would then be the *excess* projective energy, rather than raw energy.

**Outcome B21:** the literature port is promising but not plug-and-play. The exact missing theorem is a clique-weighted trace discrepancy estimate, and the new projective-energy identity supplies a candidate norm/structure for such an estimate.
