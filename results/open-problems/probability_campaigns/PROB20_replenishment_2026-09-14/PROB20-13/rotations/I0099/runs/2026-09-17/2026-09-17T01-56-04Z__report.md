## 3. B1 -- change category: the binary nonneighbor matroid

Let p be a prime with p congruent to 1 modulo 4. Let N be the p by p matrix over F_2 defined by

\[
N_{a,x}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x-a\text{ is a nonzero quadratic nonresidue modulo }p.
\]

Thus N is the adjacency matrix of the complement Paley graph, regarded over F_2. Because p congruent to 1 modulo 4, the matrix is symmetric.

Put

\[
q=\frac{p-1}{4}.
\]

The complement Paley graph has the same strongly regular parameters

\[
\left(p,\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p-5}{4},\frac{p-1}{4}\right)
=(p,2q,q-1,q).
\]

### Theorem 3.1: exact quadratic relation over F_2

Over F_2,

\[
\boxed{N^2=N+(q\bmod2)(I+J)},
\qquad
\boxed{N\mathbf 1=0}.
\]

Here J is the all-ones matrix.

#### Proof

For a strongly regular adjacency matrix with parameters (v,k,lambda,mu),

\[
N^2=kI+\lambda N+\mu(J-I-N).
\]

Substituting k=2q, lambda=q-1, mu=q gives over the integers

\[
N^2=qI-N+qJ.
\]

Reducing modulo 2 changes `-N` to `N`, proving the first identity. Every row has weight 2q, which is even, proving `N 1=0`. QED.

Let

\[
W=\left\{v\in\mathbf F_2^p:\sum_i v_i=0\right\}.
\]

Since p is odd, `1` is not in W and F_2^p=W direct-sum <1>. Because N is symmetric and has even row sums, its image lies in W and N kills 1.

### Theorem 3.2: exact binary rank and the p congruent to 5 modulo 8 no-go

If p congruent to 5 modulo 8, then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf F_2}N=p-1},
\qquad
\boxed{\ker N=\langle\mathbf 1\rangle},
\]

and the binary row span of N is exactly the even-weight hyperplane W.

If p congruent to 1 modulo 8, then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf F_2}N=\frac{p-1}{2}}.
\]

#### Proof: p congruent to 5 modulo 8

Then q is odd. On W we have Jv=0, so Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
N^2+N=I\quad\text{on }W.
\]

Hence N is invertible on W: if Nv=0 with v in W, then v=(N^2+N)v=0. Therefore the restriction has rank p-1. Since N kills 1 and its image is contained in W, the full matrix has rank p-1 and one-dimensional kernel generated by 1. Its row span is contained in W because every row has even weight, and both spaces have dimension p-1, so the row span equals W. QED.

#### Proof: p congruent to 1 modulo 8

Then q is even and Theorem 3.1 reduces to

\[
N^2=N.
\]

Let A be the Paley adjacency matrix. Over F_2,

\[
A+N=J+I.
\]

On W this becomes `A=I+N`. Thus N and A are complementary idempotent projections on W, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}(N|_W)+\operatorname{rank}(A|_W)=\dim W=p-1.
\]

Multiplication of vertices by a fixed quadratic nonresidue is a permutation taking the Paley graph to its complement. Consequently A and N are permutation-similar and have equal binary rank. Both kill 1, so because F_2^p=W direct-sum <1>, their full ranks equal the ranks of their restrictions to W. The two equal ranks therefore sum to p-1, and each is (p-1)/2. QED.

### Corollary 3.3: uniform binary row matroid for p congruent to 5 modulo 8

When p congruent to 5 modulo 8, the p rows of N represent the uniform binary matroid `U_{p-1,p}`: the only nonzero row dependence is the sum of all p rows.

Indeed a row-dependence vector lies in `ker N^T=ker N=<1>`, so its support is either empty or all p vertices.

Now let C be any proper Paley clique. For c in C, let nu_c be the nonneighbor-incidence row. On all coordinates x in C, nu_c(x)=0: the diagonal is zero and every difference of two distinct clique vertices is a quadratic residue. Therefore puncturing the rows from F_p to F_p\C loses no information about linear combinations of rows indexed by C. Hence for p congruent to 5 modulo 8,

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf F_2}\langle\nu_c|_{\mathbf F_p\setminus C}:c\in C\rangle=|C|.}
\]

This is a useful negative result for the source problem. In the entire congruence class p congruent to 5 modulo 8, the ambient binary span is simply the full even-weight code; as an abstract code/matroid it has forgotten essentially all of the Paley arithmetic. Therefore an attempted coding-theory proof that retains only ambient span, dimension, or ordinary binary matroid structure cannot distinguish large Paley cliques from generic row subsets. A successful coding route must retain the distinguished quadratic-character generators and higher intersection data.

This is a route diagnosis, not a bound on omega(G_p).

## 5. B29 -- add the points at infinity: Kummer interpretation and a level-4 gluing failure

Fix a Paley triangle T={a,b,c}. Consider

\[
E_T:\quad y^2=(x-a)(x-b)(x-c).
\]

The previous report proved that E_T has full rational 4-torsion, hence in particular E_T[2] is contained in 2E_T(F_p).

For a point P=(x,y) not in E_T[2], the standard full-2-torsion Kummer criterion says

\[
P\in2E_T(F_p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x-a,\ x-b,\ x-c
\text{ are all squares in }F_p^*.
\]

Thus x is a common Paley neighbor of a,b,c exactly when the two points +/-P above x lie in 2E_T(F_p) away from E_T[2]. Since the doubling map has kernel E_T[2] of size four,

\[
|2E_T(F_p)|=\frac{|E_T(F_p)|}{4}.
\]

And because the full rational 4-torsion gives E_T[2] subset 2E_T(F_p), the four 2-torsion points account for the non-common-neighbor exceptional points in 2E. Therefore, writing t(T) for the triple common-neighbor count,

\[
2t(T)+4=|2E_T(F_p)|=\frac{|E_T(F_p)|}{4},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{t(T)=\frac{|E_T(F_p)|-16}{8}.}
\]

This recovers the previous parity theorem in a cleaner group-theoretic form.

### Attempted finite-state gluing

Because E_T[4] is rational, the quotient

\[
2E_T(F_p)/4E_T(F_p)
\]

has at most four classes. For a common neighbor x choose either point P=(x,+/-y) above it. The class of P modulo 4E is independent of the sign because `-P` has the same class in this exponent-two quotient.

A tempting route is then:

> encode each common neighbor by its level-4 quotient class and hope that adjacency of two common neighbors is determined by their two classes.

If true, larger-clique compatibility after anchoring a triangle would reduce to a fixed finite-state graph, potentially giving a uniform obstruction.

### Proposition 5.1: level-4 quotient classes do not determine Paley adjacency

The proposed gluing statement is false already for p=53.

Take T=(0,1,7) in G_53 and

\[
E:y^2=x(x-1)(x-7).
\]

The executed exact checker finds

\[
|E(F_{53})|=64,\qquad |2E|=16,\qquad |4E|=4,
\]

and therefore four quotient classes in 2E/4E. The six common neighbors of the triangle have the following `x : quotient-class` labels under the checker's canonical enumeration:

\[
11:1,\quad16:2,\quad17:3,\quad43:2,\quad44:0,\quad47:3.
\]

Now compare the pairs (16,17) and (16,47). Both have the same unordered quotient-class pair (2,3). But

\[
16-17\equiv52\pmod{53}
\]

is a quadratic residue, so 16 and 17 are adjacent, whereas

\[
16-47\equiv22\pmod{53}
\]

is a quadratic nonresidue, so 16 and 47 are not adjacent.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{adjacency is not a function of the pair of classes in }2E/4E.}
\]

So the naive level-4 projectivized state space is too coarse to glue triangle-local information into a large-clique obstruction. Any continuation of this route must retain finer data -- for example level-8/Kummer coordinates, cross-ratio/trace information, or another invariant that sees the difference lost modulo 4E.

This is a genuine route elimination, not a source refutation.

## 7. What this changes about the source attack

The three moves fit together as a negative map of two tempting proof architectures.

First, B1 shows that on the infinite prime congruence class p congruent to 5 modulo 8, the ambient binary incidence code is maximally generic: its row span is simply the full even-weight hyperplane and its row matroid is uniform U_{p-1,p}. Thus abstract linear-code structure cannot by itself encode the Paley clique obstruction.

Second, B19 shows that the currently available 2-adic triangle identities are properties of every partial clique, so they do not automatically become exact-search pruning rules. They need a constraint involving the *remaining candidate set* or target clique size.

Third, B29 shows that even after keeping the full rational level-4 elliptic quotient attached to a triangle, pairwise adjacency among triangle common neighbors is not determined by quotient class. Thus the first natural finite-state elliptic gluing model loses essential information.

A source-level proof still needs a genuinely higher-order mechanism coupling the quadratic-character translates across many anchors. The most concrete open subproblem left by this run is:

> Find the weakest refinement of the triangle state (level-8 Kummer data, cross-ratio trace data, or another exact invariant) for which future adjacency/extension information is constrained nontrivially, and prove that the resulting state complexity cannot support a super-polylogarithmic clique.
