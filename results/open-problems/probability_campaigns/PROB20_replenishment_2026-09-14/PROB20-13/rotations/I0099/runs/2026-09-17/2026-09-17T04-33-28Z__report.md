## 4. B3 — the four-clique parity theorem does not lift to mod 4 from level-4 edge data

The preceding report proved that for every Paley four-clique C,

\[
t_4(C):=\#\bigcap_{a\in C}N(a)
\equiv
\begin{cases}
1\pmod2,&p\equiv1\pmod8,\\
0\pmod2,&p\equiv5\pmod8.
\end{cases}
\]

A natural next guess is that `t_4(C) mod 4` might be determined by p modulo 16 together with the quartic-residue classes of the normalized edge differences. This run refutes that route exactly.

Normalize a four-clique as `C={0,1,c,d}`. For a nonzero square `a`, define

\[
\kappa(a):=a^{(p-1)/4}\in\{+1,-1\},
\]

which records whether the square is itself a fourth power. Consider the five normalized square differences

\[
(c,\ 1-c,\ d,\ 1-d,\ d-c).
\]

At the **same prime** p=89, the two Paley four-cliques

\[
C_1=\{0,1,5,21\},\qquad
C_2=\{0,1,9,10\}
\]

have the identical quartic sign vector

\[
(\kappa(c),\kappa(1-c),\kappa(d),\kappa(1-d),\kappa(d-c))
=(-1,+1,-1,-1,+1),
\]

but exact enumeration gives

\[
\boxed{t_4(C_1)=3,\qquad t_4(C_2)=5.}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{t_4(C)\bmod4\text{ is not determined by }p\bmod16
\text{ and these five quartic edge classes}.}
\]

This is a useful negative result: the proved mod-2 law is genuinely sharper than any naive lift using only the level-4 residue data already present in a normalized four-clique. A mod-4 refinement must retain octic/level-8 or more global coupled information.

## 5. B12 — exact five-clique parity hierarchy and a one-bit no-go theorem

Let

\[
C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_5\}
\]

be a Paley five-clique and let

\[
X=\mathbf F_p\setminus C.
\]

For each i define the external nonneighbor set

\[
A_i=\{x\in X:\chi(x-c_i)=-1\}.
\]

Put

\[
\epsilon:=\frac{p-1}{4}\pmod2,
\]

so `epsilon=0` for p=1 mod8 and `epsilon=1` for p=5 mod8.

### Proposition 5.1 — all proper intersection parities through level four

For every nonempty `J subset {1,...,5}` with `|J|<=4`,

\[
\boxed{
\left|\bigcap_{j\in J}A_j\right|\equiv
\begin{cases}
0\pmod2,&|J|=1,\\
\epsilon\pmod2,&2\le |J|\le4.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

For `|J|=1`, each clique vertex has exactly `(p-1)/2`, an even number, of nonneighbors, and none of the other clique vertices is among them.

For `|J|=2`, two adjacent Paley vertices have exactly `(p-1)/4` common nonneighbors by the strongly-regular parameters, giving parity `epsilon`.

For a clique triangle T, write `I_3(T)` for its common external nonneighbors and `t_3(T)` for its common neighbors. Inclusion-exclusion in `F_p\setminus T` gives

\[
t_3(T)\equiv \epsilon+I_3(T)\pmod2.
\]

The preceding I0099 triangle theorem gives `t_3(T)` even, hence `I_3(T)=epsilon mod2`.

For a four-clique D, inclusion-exclusion in `F_p\setminus D` gives

\[
t_4(D)\equiv 1+I_4(D)\pmod2,
\]

because the four singleton terms are even and the six pair plus four triple terms contribute zero modulo two. The proved four-clique theorem says `t_4(D)=1+epsilon mod2`, so `I_4(D)=epsilon mod2`. QED.

### Proposition 5.2 — the proper parity hierarchy leaves exactly one free bit

Let

\[
z:=|A_1\cap\cdots\cap A_5|\pmod2
\]

and let `t_5(C)` be the common-neighbor count of all five clique vertices. Inclusion-exclusion immediately gives

\[
\boxed{t_5(C)\equiv\epsilon+z\pmod2.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

More importantly, the parity data (5.1) do **not** determine z. Over `F_2`, Möbius inversion on the Boolean lattice shows that for each fixed `epsilon`, both assignments `z=0` and `z=1` extend consistently to parities of all 32 exact Venn atoms. Explicitly:

- if `epsilon=0,z=0`, all exact atoms are even;
- if `epsilon=0,z=1`, all exact atoms are odd;
- if `epsilon=1,z=0`, exact atoms of sizes 2,3,4 and the empty atom are odd, while sizes 1 and 5 are even;
- if `epsilon=1,z=1`, exact atoms of sizes 1 and 5 are odd, while sizes 0,2,3,4 are even.

Thus there is a literal one-dimensional parity obstruction left after **all** one-, two-, three-, and four-fold nonneighbor intersection congruences are imposed.

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\text{no proof using only the established proper-intersection parity hierarchy can determine }t_5(C)\bmod2.}
\]

A successful extension must supply a genuinely level-five arithmetic invariant, not another recombination of the already proved lower-level parity statements.

## 6. B29 — the five-clique multiquadratic cover and an exact genus-17 identity

The one-bit obstruction above has a natural geometric home.

For a Paley five-clique `C={c_1,...,c_5}`, let `X_C` be the smooth projective normalization of

\[
y_i^2=x-c_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,5.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This is a `(Z/2Z)^5` cover of the x-line, branched at the five finite clique points and at infinity. Riemann--Hurwitz gives

\[
2g(X_C)-2=32(-2)+6\cdot16=32,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{g(X_C)=17.}
\]

Every common neighbor x of the five anchors has 32 rational lifts, one for each choice of the five square roots. At a branch value x=c_i there are 16 rational lifts because all four other clique differences are squares, contributing `5*16=80`; the fiber at infinity has 16 rational points. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\#X_C(\mathbf F_p)=32t_5(C)+96.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The nontrivial characters of the deck group give quotient curves indexed by nonempty subsets `S subset C`. Subsets of size one or two have genus zero. The ten three-subsets and five four-subsets give fifteen genus-one quotients, while the full five-subset gives the genus-two hyperelliptic quotient

\[
Y_C:\quad y^2=\prod_{c\in C}(x-c).
\]

Writing `N_S` for the smooth projective point count of the genus-one quotient attached to S and `N_C^(2)=#Y_C(F_p)`, the character decomposition of `H^1` (equivalently, expansion of the quadratic-character sums) gives

\[
\#X_C(\mathbf F_p)
=
\sum_{|S|=3}N_S+
\sum_{|S|=4}N_S+
N_C^{(2)}-15(p+1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining (6.2) and (6.3) yields the exact level-five identity

\[
\boxed{
32t_5(C)=
\sum_{|S|=3}N_S+
\sum_{|S|=4}N_S+
N_C^{(2)}-15p-111.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This precisely localizes what a level-five parity theorem would need: to prove `t_5(C)` even, one needs the right-hand side of (6.4) divisible by 64. Full rational 4-torsion on the fifteen genus-one factors only yields their individual divisibility by 16, so it does not by itself supply the missing factor of two. The genus-two factor and the coupling among all sixteen positive-genus character quotients are essential.

There is a further exact low-resolution congruence. The XOR of the five nonneighbor columns is the set of external x for which the quintic character product is `-1`; combining this with the already proved mod-8 code-weight law gives

\[
\boxed{N_C^{(2)}\equiv15-p\pmod{16}.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Again, mod 16 is one 2-adic level short of the mod-64 total congruence needed in (6.4).
