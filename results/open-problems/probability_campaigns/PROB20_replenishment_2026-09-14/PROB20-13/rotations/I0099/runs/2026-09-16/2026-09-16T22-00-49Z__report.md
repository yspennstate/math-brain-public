## 2. B28 — typical projective maps have tiny clique overlap

Let `C` be a Paley clique of size `k`, viewed inside `P^1(F_p)`, and for `g in PGL_2(F_p)` define

\[
I_C(g)=|\{x\in C:g(x)\in C\}|.
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — exact third factorial moment

For every such clique,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{g\in PGL_2(F_p)}(I_C(g))_3=(k)_3^2.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Moreover every `g` with `I_C(g)>=3` has square determinant class, hence lies in the image of `PSL_2(F_p)`. Thus

\[
\boxed{\sum_{g\in PSL_2(F_p)}(I_C(g))_3=(k)_3^2,\qquad
\sum_{g\notin PSL_2(F_p)}(I_C(g))_3=0.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently, for every `t>=3`,

\[
\boxed{|\{g:I_C(g)\ge t\}|\le\frac{(k)_3^2}{(t)_3}.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since `|PSL_2(F_p)|=p(p^2-1)/2`, the fraction of determinant-square projective maps having overlap at least three is at most

\[
\boxed{\frac{(k)_3^2}{3p(p^2-1)},}
\tag{2.4}
\]

which tends to zero whenever `k=o(sqrt p)`.

#### Proof

`PGL_2(F_p)` acts sharply 3-transitively on `P^1(F_p)`. Expanding `(I_C(g))_3` counts ordered triples of distinct source points in `C` whose three images lie in `C`. For each ordered source triple and ordered target triple there is exactly one projective map. There are `(k)_3^2` such pairs, proving (2.1).

For the determinant assertion write

\[
g(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d},\qquad \Delta=ad-bc.
\]

If `x,y,z` are three distinct overlap points, then for any two

\[
g(x)-g(y)=\frac{\Delta(x-y)}{(cx+d)(cy+d)}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

All nonzero differences among points of `C`, and among their images in `C`, are quadratic residues; `-1` is also a square because `p=1 mod 4`. Taking quadratic characters in (2.5) for `(x,y)`, `(x,z)`, `(y,z)` shows the three denominator characters are equal, and substitution gives `chi(Delta)=1`. Determinant square class is well-defined in `PGL_2` because scalar rescaling changes `Delta` by a square. For odd `p`, the square-determinant subgroup is the image of `PSL_2`, proving (2.2). Markov's inequality on the nonnegative factorial moment gives (2.3), and division by the group size gives (2.4). QED.

The previously proved identity

\[
\mathcal E_{cr}(C)=\sum_g(I_C(g))_4
\]

therefore also gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_{cr}(C)\le(k-3)(k)_3^2,}
\tag{2.6}
\]

because `(r)_4=(r)_3(r-3)` and `I_C(g)<=k`.

**Outcome B28.** Fourth-order projective energy is necessarily supported on a sparse exceptional family of determinant-square transformations in the source-relevant regime `k << sqrt p`. This is information that generic Cauchy–Schwarz on the cross-ratio vector does not see.

## 3. B26 — the old excess-energy statistic contains a forced collision floor

Use the preceding reports' cross-ratio convention

\[
\lambda(a,b,c,d)=\frac{(b-d)(c-a)}{(b-a)(c-d)}
\]

for distinct ordered clique quadruples, and let `m_C(lambda)` be its multiplicity. Then `sum_lambda m_C(lambda)=(k)_4` and `E_cr=sum m_C(lambda)^2`.

### Theorem 3.1 — stabilizer divisibility and genuine collisions

Let `s(lambda)` be the stabilizer size of a cross-ratio value under positional permutations in `S_4`. In characteristic different from 2 and 3,

\[
s(\lambda)=\begin{cases}
12,&\lambda^2-\lambda+1=0,\\
8,&\lambda\in\{-1,2,1/2\},\\
4,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{s(\lambda)\mid m_C(\lambda).}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_{cr}(C)=\sum_\lambda s(\lambda)m_C(\lambda)+\mathcal E_{genuine}(C),}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_{genuine}(C)=\sum_\lambda m_C(\lambda)(m_C(\lambda)-s(\lambda))\ge0.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_{cr}(C)\ge4(k)_4.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

The `S_4` action on ordered quadruples induces the classical six anharmonic transforms of the cross-ratio. Its generic kernel is the Klein four group, giving stabilizer size four. Harmonic values `{-1,2,1/2}` have an orbit of size three and stabilizer eight; equianharmonic roots of `lambda^2-lambda+1` have an orbit of size two and stabilizer twelve. For a fixed distinct ordered quadruple, its cross-ratio stabilizer acts freely by positional permutations because all four entries are distinct. Thus the quadruples counted by `m_C(lambda)` break into free orbits of size `s(lambda)`, proving (3.2). Since `m` is a nonnegative multiple of `s`, `m(m-s)>=0`; summing `m^2=sm+m(m-s)` gives (3.3)–(3.4), and `s>=4` plus `sum m=(k)_4` gives (3.5). QED.

This corrects the *interpretation*, not the algebra, of the preceding centered quantity

\[
\mathcal E_{exc}=\sum_\lambda\left(m_C(\lambda)-\frac{(k)_4}{h}\right)^2,\qquad h=(p-5)/4.
\]

Indeed, with `M=(k)_4`,

\[
\mathcal E_{exc}=\mathcal E_{genuine}+\sum_\lambda s(\lambda)m_C(\lambda)-M^2/h.
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `k=polylog p`, then `M^2/h=o(M)` whereas the stabilizer term is at least `4M`. Thus even with **no genuine cross-ratio collisions beyond forced positional reorderings**, `E_exc >= (4-o(1))M`. The source-scale statistic that matters is therefore `E_genuine`, not simply distance from a continuous uniform cross-ratio vector.

### Sporadic values are completely classifiable

Let

\[
H_p=\{\lambda\ne0,1:\chi(\lambda)=\chi(1-\lambda)=1\}.
\]

The harmonic values lie in `H_p` iff `chi(2)=1`, which for `p=1 mod 4` is exactly `p=1 mod 8`. The equianharmonic roots exist and lie in `H_p` iff `p=1 mod 12`: the equation gives `lambda(1-lambda)=1` and `lambda^3=-1`, and when `12|(p-1)` the order-six roots are squares.

For any fixed `lambda`, an ordered triple `(a,b,c)` determines at most one fourth point `d` solving the cross-ratio equation, so

\[
\boxed{m_C(\lambda)\le(k)_3.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

There are at most five special anharmonic values. Therefore Hasse's `|a_p(lambda)|<=2sqrt p` yields

\[
\boxed{\left|\sum_{\lambda\ special}m_C(\lambda)a_p(\lambda)\right|\le10(k)_3\sqrt p.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

**Outcome B26.** Harmonic/equianharmonic parameters are too sparse to explain a logarithmic saving, and the raw `E_exc` statistic is contaminated by deterministic positional self-collisions at the polylogarithmic scale. A projective proof must control **generic nontrivial collisions** and their centered trace correlation.
