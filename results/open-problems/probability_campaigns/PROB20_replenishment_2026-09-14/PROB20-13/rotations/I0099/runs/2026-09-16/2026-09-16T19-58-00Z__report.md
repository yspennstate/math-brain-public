## 2. B7 — exact row-code limit picture and the second-moment barrier

This method asks what the Paley constraints look like before taking a pseudorandom limit.

Let `C` be a Paley clique of size `k`, and let `chi` be the quadratic character of `F_p`, extended by `chi(0)=0`. For each outside vertex `x in F_p \ C` and clique vertex `a in C`, form

\[
R_{x,a}:=\chi(x-a)\in\{\pm1\}.
\]

Because every nonzero difference of two clique vertices is a square and `p=1 mod 4`, the standard two-shift character identity gives an **exact** Gram matrix.

### Proposition 2.1

\[
\boxed{R^{\mathsf T}R=(p-1)I_k+(1-k)J_k.}
\]

### Proof

For one column `a`, summing `chi(x-a)^2` over all `x` gives `p-1`. Removing the `k` clique rows removes `k-1` ones and one zero, so the outside diagonal entry is `p-k`.

For distinct `a,b`,

\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_p}\chi((x-a)(x-b))=-1.
\]

Among `x in C`, the terms at `a,b` vanish and the other `k-2` terms equal `1`. Thus the outside-column inner product is `-1-(k-2)=1-k`. This is exactly the displayed matrix. `square`

Let

\[
S_C(x)=\sum_{a\in C}\chi(x-a).
\]

On `C`, `S_C(x)=k-1`, while over the outside rows `S_C=R 1`. The exact Gram formula gives

\[
\sum_{x\notin C}S_C(x)^2
 = k\bigl(p-1+k-k^2\bigr),
\]

and `sum_x S_C(x)=0` gives

\[
\sum_{x\notin C}S_C(x)=-k(k-1).
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz on the `p-k` outside rows therefore gives

\[
k^2(k-1)^2\le (p-k)k(p-1+k-k^2).
\]

The right side minus the left side factors exactly as

\[
\boxed{k(p-1)(p-k^2)}.
\]

Hence this exact second-order route recovers `k <= sqrt(p)` and nothing below the square-root exponent. The value of the reformulation is diagnostic: the outside rows are a highly constrained almost-orthogonal binary code, and a source-level improvement must use information beyond their pair correlations.

**Outcome B7:** useful structural reduction and an exact explanation of the second-moment barrier; no source theorem yet.

## 3. B11 — Fourier dualization and a concrete hypercontractive bridge to Conjecture 25

This is the strongest new route from this run.

Use normalized `L^q` norms

\[
\|g\|_q=\left(\frac1p\sum_x|g(x)|^q\right)^{1/q}
\]

and unitary Fourier transform

\[
\widehat g(\xi)=p^{-1/2}\sum_x g(x)e_p(-\xi x).
\]

Let

\[
f_C=1_C-\frac{k}{p}.
\]

Define the mean-zero Paley multiplier `T` by

\[
\widehat{Tg}(0)=0,\qquad
\widehat{Tg}(\xi)=\chi(\xi)\widehat g(\xi)\quad(\xi\ne0).
\]

For `p=1 mod 4`, the quadratic Gauss-sum evaluation gives

\[
\boxed{S_C=\sqrt p\,T f_C.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Also

\[
\|f_C\|_2^2=\frac{k(p-k)}{p^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The clique property appears as a spike condition:

\[
S_C(x)=k-1\qquad(x\in C).
\tag{3.3}
\]

This yields a direct sufficient analytic statement for the full source conjecture.

### Proposition 3.1 — restricted hypercontractivity would prove the source conjecture

Assume there is an absolute `B >= 1` such that, for every sufficiently large admissible prime and every Paley clique `C`, if

\[
m=\left\lfloor\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}\right\rfloor,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\|T f_C\|_{2m}\le B\sqrt m\,\|f_C\|_2.
}
\tag{HC}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\omega(G_p)\le 4B^2\frac{(\log p)^2}{\log\log p}
}
\]

for all sufficiently large admissible primes. In particular Conjecture 25 follows, with a fixed logarithmic power.

### Proof

By (3.1)--(3.3), the `k` clique points alone give

\[
\|T f_C\|_{2m}^{2m}
=\frac{1}{p^{m+1}}\sum_x |S_C(x)|^{2m}
\ge \frac{k(k-1)^{2m}}{p^{m+1}}.
\]

On the other hand (HC) and (3.2) give

\[
\|T f_C\|_{2m}^{2m}
\le (B^2m)^m\left(\frac{k(p-k)}{p^2}\right)^m.
\]

Combining and using `(p-k)/p <= 1`,

\[
k(k-1)^{2m}\le p(B^2mk)^m.
\]

For `k>=2`, `k-1>=k/2`, so

\[
\boxed{k^{m+1}\le p(4B^2m)^m.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Put `L=log p`. Since `m=floor(L/log L)`, one has `m+1>L/log L`, hence

\[
p^{1/(m+1)}<L.
\]

Also `m<=L/log L`. Taking the `(m+1)`st root in (3.4), and using `m/(m+1)<1`, gives

\[
k
\le p^{1/(m+1)}(4B^2m)^{m/(m+1)}
< L\,(4B^2m)
\le 4B^2\frac{L^2}{\log L}.
\]

The finitely many smaller primes can be absorbed into the constant. `square`

This bridge is deliberately **not** Conjecture 26/27 theta localization, degree-four SOS, or the ETF problem. It is a direct high-moment inequality for the quadratic-character Fourier involution, restricted to centered indicators of actual cliques.

### Why ordinary Weil term-by-term estimates do not prove (HC)

Expanding the `2m`-th moment produces sums indexed by `C^{2m}` of

\[
\sum_x\chi\!\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2m}(x-a_j)\right).
\]

Tuples in which every multiplicity is even give the expected Gaussian-scale main contribution. For a nonsquare product, a pointwise Weil estimate is only `O(m sqrt(p))`. Summing absolute values over all nonsquare tuples creates an error of order `m sqrt(p) k^{2m}` before normalization, which returns the same square-root barrier. Thus (HC) requires **aggregate cancellation across the correlated family of character sums**, not a stronger use of the same pointwise inequality.

The exact finite witnesses produced later in this report provide a small-scale diagnostic. Their exact values of

\[
\frac{\sum_xS_C(x)^4}{p k^2}
\]

range from about `3.13` to `4.41`; the audit artifact records the exact rational values. This is compatible with a Gaussian-scale fourth moment in these examples, but it is only finite evidence and is not a universal estimate.

**Outcome B11:** a complete conditional reduction of the full polylogarithmic conjecture to one explicit clique-restricted high-moment multiplier estimate, together with a precise explanation of why termwise Weil bounds fail.

## 4. B23 — assume a huge clique and extract projective rigidity

Let `a,b,c,d` be four distinct clique vertices and define the cross-ratio

\[
\lambda=\frac{(c-a)(d-b)}{(c-b)(d-a)}.
\]

Every factor is a nonzero quadratic residue up to orientation, and `chi(-1)=1`. Therefore

\[
\chi(\lambda)=1.
\]

Moreover

\[
1-\lambda
=\frac{(b-a)(c-d)}{(c-b)(d-a)},
\]

so also

\[
\chi(1-\lambda)=1.
\]

Thus every cross-ratio of four distinct vertices of a Paley clique lies in the fixed quarter-density set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_p=
\{\lambda\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(\lambda)=\chi(1-\lambda)=1\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The standard quadratic-character sum gives

\[
|\mathcal H_p|=\frac{p-5}{4}.
\]

Indeed, expand `(1+chi(lambda))(1+chi(1-lambda))/4` away from `0,1`; the two linear-character sums contribute `-1` each and `sum chi(lambda(1-lambda))=-1` for `p=1 mod 4`.

This is the projective version of the finite `S_3`/cross-ratio symmetries already exploited computationally, but it suggests a different asymptotic target: a hypothetical super-polylogarithmic clique would have an enormous four-point configuration whose entire cross-ratio set lies inside `H_p`.

The known cross-ratio growth theorem of Murphy--Petridis--Roche-Newton--Rudnev--Shkredov gives, in its applicable range, a cardinality lower bound of shape

\[
|R[A]|\gtrsim |A|^{8/5}.
\]

Combining only such cardinality growth with a constant-density target such as the quadratic residues or `H_p` yields at best a polynomial bound (for example `|A| \lesssim p^{5/8}` from the displayed exponent), which is weaker than the existing square-root clique bound. So **plain cross-ratio expansion is not enough**.

The missing strengthening is character-sensitive: one needs cancellation showing that the cross-ratios of a large set cannot all have the simultaneous signs in (4.1), or an inverse theorem saying that such a severe character bias forces a structure incompatible with pairwise quadratic-residue differences.

There is a useful synthesis with B11. At the fourth moment, the genuinely four-distinct terms are quartic character sums whose isomorphism class is governed by a cross-ratio. Hence an averaged trace estimate over the clique's restricted cross-ratio multiset is exactly the kind of cancellation that could improve the fourth moment. A uniform estimate

\[
\sum_x S_C(x)^4\ll p k^2
\]

would already imply `k=O(p^{1/3})` from the clique spike, a genuine improvement over the square-root exponent. It still would not prove the polylogarithmic source statement, but it is a concrete first analytic milestone on the B11/B23 route.

**Outcome B23:** exact projective rigidity plus a precise failure of cardinality-only cross-ratio growth; next useful theorem must control character bias / averaged quartic traces, not merely the number of cross-ratios.

3. **Cardinality-only cross-ratio growth:** the best directly located growth statement is too weak even to beat the spectral square-root exponent after comparison with a positive-density target set.
4. **Existing localized-theta / SOS / ETF programs:** neighboring tasks own different source conjectures. Their outputs cannot be substituted for Conjecture 25.

A source-level proof now needs something not present in those routes: **uniform high-order cancellation tied to the fact that the shift set itself is a Paley clique**.

with absolute `K`. It would already imply `|C|=O(p^{1/3})`. The full source conjecture would follow from the growing-moment version (HC) at `m ~ log p / log log p`.
