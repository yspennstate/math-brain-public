## 2. The pinned level-five geometry

Let

\[
C=\{c_1,\dots,c_5\}\subset\mathbf F_p
\]

be a Paley five-clique. The previous report proved for the smooth projective normalization

\[
X_C:\qquad y_i^2=x-c_i,\quad i=1,\dots,5,
\]

that `g(X_C)=17` and

\[
\boxed{\#X_C(\mathbf F_p)=32t_5(C)+96.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Its character decomposition has fifteen genus-one factors, indexed by the ten 3-subsets and five 4-subsets of C, and one genus-two factor

\[
Y_C:\quad y^2=\prod_{c\in C}(x-c).
\]

If `A_C` denotes the product of those fifteen elliptic curves with `J(Y_C)`, then `dim A_C=17` and the Frobenius trace on `T_2(A_C)` is exactly the Frobenius trace on `H^1(X_C)`. Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi_C)=p+1-\#X_C(\mathbf F_p)
=p-95-32t_5(C).
\tag{2.2}
\]

This trace equality is the representation-theoretic form of the exact point-count identity proved last run.

## 3. B11 DUALIZE — the free parity bit is exactly a mod-4 Frobenius coefficient

The new useful object is not another point count. It is the second elementary symmetric coefficient of the *divided Frobenius increment*.

### Lemma 3.1 — all factors have rational 4-torsion

The fifteen elliptic quotient factors have full rational 4-torsion by the already proved triangle/four-clique square-difference argument, which is reused as a pinned checkpoint.

The genus-two factor has full rational 4-torsion as well.

**Proof.** The five finite Weierstrass points of `Y_C`, together with infinity, are rational. Put

\[
D_i=[(c_i,0)-\infty]\in J(Y_C)[2](\mathbf F_p).
\]

Four independent `D_i` generate the full rank-four group `J(Y_C)[2]`. For an odd-degree hyperelliptic curve with rational infinity, the standard `x-T` descent map has kernel `2J(\mathbf F_p)`. Applied to `D_i`, its nontrivial coordinates are represented by the branch differences `c_i-c_j` (and their product in the distinguished coordinate). Because C is a Paley clique and `p\equiv1 mod 4`, every signed branch difference is a square in `\mathbf F_p`. Hence the descent class of every `D_i` is trivial, so every `D_i` lies in `2J(Y_C)(\mathbf F_p)`.

Choose rational `Q_i` with `2Q_i=D_i` for four independent `D_i`. If a relation `\sum n_iQ_i=0` holds modulo 4, doubling first forces every `n_i` even by independence of the `D_i`; dividing the coefficients by two and doubling again forces each coefficient divisible by four. Thus the four `Q_i` generate `4^4=256` rational points, which is all of `J(Y_C)[4]`. QED.

Therefore

\[
A_C[4]\subset A_C(\mathbf F_p).
\]

On the integral 2-adic Tate module we may write Frobenius uniquely as

\[
\boxed{\Phi_C=I+4M_C}
\tag{3.1}
\]

for an integral endomorphism `M_C` of rank 34.

Let `e_j(M_C)` be the elementary symmetric coefficients defined by

\[
\det(I+uM_C)=\sum_{j=0}^{34}e_j(M_C)u^j.
\]

### Theorem 3.2 — exact Tate-module form of the level-five bit

For every prime-field Paley five-clique,

\[
\boxed{e_2(M_C)\equiv 2t_5(C)\pmod4.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{t_5(C)\text{ is even}\iff e_2(M_C)\equiv0\pmod4.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

**Proof.** Since `dim A_C=17`,

\[
\det(\Phi_C)=p^{17}.
\]

Expanding (3.1) modulo 64 gives

\[
p^{17}\equiv1+4e_1(M_C)+16e_2(M_C)\pmod{64}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The trace identity (2.2) gives

\[
34+4e_1(M_C)=p-95-32t_5(C),
\]

hence

\[
4e_1(M_C)=p-129-32t_5(C).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Substituting (3.5) into (3.4),

\[
p^{17}\equiv p-128-32t_5(C)+16e_2(M_C)\pmod{64}.
\]

For every odd p, `p^{16}\equiv1 mod 64`; therefore `p^{17}\equiv p mod 64`. Cancelling p and reducing gives

\[
32t_5(C)\equiv16e_2(M_C)\pmod{64},
\]

which is equivalent to (3.2). QED.

This is the cleanest localization of the one free bit found so far. The previous genus-17 identity said that one extra factor of two was missing from a coupled point-count congruence. The present theorem says exactly where that bit lives: in

\[
\frac{e_2((\Phi_C-I)/4)}{2}\pmod2.
\]

Thus the empirical parity law is a genuinely *second-order 2-adic Frobenius constraint*.

## 4. B7 TAKE THE LIMIT — generic level-4 symplectic information is provably one bit too weak

Because `A_C[4]` is rational, the Weil pairing makes Frobenius a symplectic similitude satisfying

\[
\Phi_C^T J\Phi_C=pJ.
\]

Writing `\Phi_C=I+4M_C`, dividing by four and reducing modulo two gives

\[
\boxed{M_C^TJ+JM_C\equiv qJ\pmod2},
\qquad
q=\frac{p-1}{4}\pmod2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

So the first 2-adic layer puts `M_C mod 2` in the appropriate conformal symplectic Lie algebra. It is tempting to hope this forces (3.3). It does not.

### Proposition 4.1 — explicit mod-64 no-go models

The generic congruence conditions `\Phi\equiv I mod4` and `\Phi^TJ\Phi\equiv pJ mod64` admit both values

\[
e_2((\Phi-I)/4)\equiv0\pmod4
\quad\text{and}\quad
2\pmod4.
\]

This already occurs in diagonal symplectic congruence models in rank 34.

For `p\equiv5 mod64`:

- seventeen blocks `diag(1,5)` give divided increments `diag(0,1)` and total `e_2\equiv0 mod4`;
- seventeen blocks `diag(9,29)` give divided increments `diag(2,7)`, with `9\cdot29\equiv5 mod64`, and total `e_2\equiv2 mod4`.

For `p\equiv1 mod64`:

- seventeen identity blocks give `e_2\equiv0 mod4`;
- three identity blocks together with fourteen `diag(5,13)` blocks, where `5\cdot13\equiv1 mod64`, give `e_2\equiv2 mod4`.

These are deliberately only congruence models, not claimed to be Frobenius matrices of actual abelian varieties. Their role is exact and limited: they prove that **generic rational-4-torsion plus symplectic-similitude congruence data do not select the missing half-bit**. Any proof of (1.1) through this route must use the special coupling among the sixteen quotient factors coming from the *same five branch points*, or must move to finer level-8/level-16 Kummer data.

The executed checker verifies all four models exactly modulo 64.

## 5. B27 SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS — any fixed-order parity hierarchy is source-scale insufficient

Suppose a hypothetical k-clique is encoded by exact Boolean sign-pattern counts. For an atom `A subset [k]`, let

\[
n_A\in\mathbf F_2
\]

be the parity of the number of outside vertices with exactly that adjacency pattern. Its intersection moments are

\[
m_S=\sum_{A\supseteq S}n_A\in\mathbf F_2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The previous run showed explicitly at k=5 that moments through order four leave one bit free. The general obstruction is stronger.

### Theorem 5.1 — fixed-level parity no-go

For every `k>r`, knowing **all** moment parities `m_S` with `|S|<=r` does not determine the parity of the all-plus/top atom `n_[k]`.

**Proof.** Toggle every atom parity:

\[
n_A' = n_A+1\qquad\text{for all }A\subseteq[k].
\]

For fixed `S` with `|S|<k`, the change in (5.1) is the number of supersets of S,

\[
2^{k-|S|}\equiv0\pmod2.
\]

Thus every moment of order at most r is unchanged. But the top atom has only one superset, itself, so `n_[k]` flips. QED.

Consequently, even a proof of the empirical five-clique parity law (1.1), applied to every five-subset of a large clique, cannot by itself produce a polylogarithmic bound through parity bookkeeping alone. Any source-level argument based on this hierarchy must either:

1. let the controlled order grow with k; or
2. use quantitative integer counts rather than only their parities; or
3. impose algebraic compatibility constraints on the sign patterns that are not expressible as a bounded collection of low-order parity moments.

This is a proof-class no-go result, not a counterexample to the Paley conjecture.
