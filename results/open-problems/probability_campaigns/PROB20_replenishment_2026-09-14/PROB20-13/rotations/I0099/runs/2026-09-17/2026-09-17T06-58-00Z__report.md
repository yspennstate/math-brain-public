# 5. B22 — read the failure: the published decomposition computes a kernel order, not the level-8 Galois module we need

The preceding B21 run identified the relevant prior art:

Robert Auffarth, Giancarlo Lucchini Arteche, Anita M. Rojas, *A decomposition of the Jacobian of a Humbert-Edge curve*, Contemp. Math. 766 (2021), arXiv:1905.12690.

For a Humbert-Edge curve of type `n`, their Theorem 4 gives the Prym-Tyurin decomposition and exponent `2^(n-3)`. For `n=5`, every factor has exponent `4`, and the decomposition-isogeny kernel has order

\[
4^{17}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The important point this run is **where their proof stops**. Their Lemma 12 is a general polarization-degree lemma. If

\[
\varphi:\bigoplus_j A_j\to A
\]

is the sum isogeny and the induced polarization on each factor is `d` times a principal polarization, they compute

\[
|\ker\varphi|=d^{\dim A}
\tag{5.2}
\]

by comparing the top self-intersection of `\varphi^*\Theta` with `\deg\varphi\,\Theta^{\dim A}` and invoking Riemann--Roch. In other words, the argument determines the **degree/cardinality** of the isogeny kernel. It does not, in that lemma, identify the kernel as a subgroup scheme with an explicit basis, a Galois module, or a Frobenius action modulo `8`.

That distinction is exactly the current gap. The previous I0099 run already showed that rational 4-torsion on the factors and generic symplectic-similitude constraints permit both values of the missing second-order Frobenius bit. Knowing only (5.1) does not restore the information lost when the factors are glued.

Two additional literature checks sharpen the boundary:

1. Abel Castorena and Juan Bosco Frías-Medina, *Geometric aspects on Humbert-Edge curves of type 5, Kummer surfaces and hyperelliptic curves of genus 2*, Glasgow Math. J. 65 (2023), 612--624, characterizes type-5 Humbert-Edge curves through Kummer surfaces and identifies their moduli with genus-two hyperelliptic moduli. This strongly confirms that the genus-two/Kummer object is the right geometric place to look, but that paper works over the complex numbers and does not supply the finite-field level-8 Frobenius module required by (4.1).
2. Miret--Pujolàs--Rio, *Explicit 2-power torsion of genus 2 curves over finite fields* (2010), gives a finite-field algorithm for the maximal 2-subgroup of a genus-two Jacobian by successive halving of 2-power torsion; the halving step requires solving explicit degree-16 equations. This is the nearest directly transferable technical mechanism recovered in the bounded search.

**B22 outcome:** the relevant published Humbert-Edge proof does not fail by an inequality that can simply be sharpened. It forgets the exact object we need: the 2-primary gluing kernel as a Frobenius/Galois module. A serious continuation must either compute that module one level deeper or bypass the decomposition entirely.

This is a limitation-of-route statement, not a claim that no other literature contains the missing module.

# 6. B20 — find why the obvious algorithm fails: the natural Klein-four action dies when the fourth and fifth clique points are imposed

The coefficient count (1.1) practically begs for an orbit proof of divisibility by four. There is indeed a canonical Klein-four symmetry at the **three-point** level.

Normalize a Paley triangle to

\[
T=\{0,1,c\},
\qquad
\chi(c)=\chi(c-1)=1.
\]

For a point `x` adjacent to all three vertices, define

\[
\tau_0(x)=\frac c x,
\qquad
\tau_1(x)=\frac{x-c}{x-1},
\qquad
\tau_c(x)=\frac{c(x-1)}{x-c}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

These are the three nontrivial x-coordinate maps induced by translation by the rational 2-torsion points on

\[
E:y^2=x(x-1)(x-c).
\]

They can also be checked without elliptic curves. Because `p congruent 1 mod 4`, `-1` is a square. If `x`, `x-1`, and `x-c` are all squares, then for example

\[
\tau_0(x)=c/x,
\quad
\tau_0(x)-1=-(x-c)/x,
\quad
\tau_0(x)-c=-c(x-1)/x
\]

are all squares. The analogous identities for `\tau_1` and `\tau_c` prove preservation of the three-point condition. The three involutions together with the identity form a Klein four group.

Now let

\[
f(T)=T^2-sT+n
\]

be the monic irreducible polynomial of a conjugate pair `x,x^p` in `F_{p^2}`. The map `\tau_0:x\mapsto c/x` acts explicitly on coefficients by

\[
\boxed{
(s,n)\longmapsto
\left(\frac{cs}{n},\frac{c^2}{n}\right).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the triple-level coefficient set genuinely carries the natural V4 action one would first try to use for a mod-4 orbit count.

# 7. B28 — replace “for every configuration” with a controlled weakening and identify exactly what the clique hypothesis buys

The previous reports specialized immediately to a Paley five-clique. Here it is useful to keep an arbitrary set of five distinct points

\[
C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_5\}\subset\mathbf F_p
\]

and ask what changes as the ten pairwise Paley edges are removed.

Let

\[
t(C)=\#\{x\notin C:\chi(x-c)=1\ \forall c\in C\},
\tag{7.1}
\]

let `b(C)` be the number of vertices of `C` adjacent to **all four** other vertices of `C`, and let `R(C)` be the irreducible-quadratic count (1.1), now defined for this arbitrary five-set.

Let `X_C` be the smooth projective normalization of

\[
y_i^2=x-c_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,5.
\tag{7.2}
\]

## Theorem 7.1 — exact two-level point counts for an arbitrary five-set

For every prime `p congruent 1 mod 4` and every five distinct points `C` in `F_p`,

\[
\boxed{
\#X_C(\mathbf F_p)=32t(C)+16b(C)+16,
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\#X_C(\mathbf F_{p^2})=32p-64+64R(C).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof of (7.3)

For an unbranched base point `x in F_p \ C`, the fibre has 32 rational points exactly when all five `x-c_i` are nonzero squares, and otherwise has no `F_p`-points. This contributes `32t(C)`.

Above a finite branch point `x=c_i`, one square root is zero. The remaining four equations have rational square roots exactly when every difference `c_i-c_j`, `j!=i`, is a square. In that case the fibre has `2^4=16` rational points; otherwise it has none. Summing over branch points gives `16b(C)`.

At infinity all five local radicands `x-c_i` have the same local square class: `(x-c_i)/x=1-c_i/x` is a square in the completed local field. Hence the local compositum has inertia degree two and the degree-32 cover has 16 rational points above infinity. This gives the final `+16` and proves (7.3).

### Proof of (7.4)

Every nonzero element of `F_p` is a square in `F_{p^2}`. Therefore every one of the `p-5` unbranched base points in `F_p\C` has a 32-point fibre over `F_{p^2}`, every finite branch point contributes 16, and infinity contributes 16. The total contribution from base points in `P^1(F_p)` is

\[
32(p-5)+5\cdot16+16=32p-64.
\]

Every point of `F_{p^2}\setminus F_p` has a Frobenius orbit of size two. For such an `x`, the fibre has 32 rational points exactly when all five `x-c_i` are squares. If

\[
f(T)=(T-x)(T-x^p)=T^2-sT+n,
\]

then `x-c_i` is a square in `F_{p^2}` exactly when its norm `f(c_i)` is a square in `F_p`; this is the elementary cyclic-group norm criterion for quadratic extensions. Thus the good Frobenius orbits are in bijection with the coefficient pairs counted by `R(C)`, and each good orbit contributes `2*32=64` points. This proves (7.4). QED.

For a genuine Paley five-clique, `b(C)=5`, so (7.3) recovers the pinned formula

\[
\#X_C(F_p)=32t_5(C)+96.
\]

If exactly one of the ten Paley edges is deleted, then its two endpoints cease to be universal while the other three vertices remain universal, so `b(C)=3`. The `F_p` point count changes by 32 immediately; the `F_{p^2}` formula still packages the much subtler coefficient count `R(C)`.

## 7.2 Exact p=97 near-clique census

To see whether the parity/divisibility is robust, normalize the always-present edge to `{0,1}` and restrict the other three points to the common neighborhood

At `p=97`, `|H|=23`. Thus every tested five-set has the seven edges joining `0,1` to each other and to the three additional points; only the three edges among the additional points vary.

The checker exhausts all
