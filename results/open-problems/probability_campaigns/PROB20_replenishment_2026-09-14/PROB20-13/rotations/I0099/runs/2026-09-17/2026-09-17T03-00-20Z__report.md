## 4. B13 — search for a future-extension invariant: an exact five-curve identity and a sharp 2-adic blocker

The previous run showed that a parity identity which is true for every partial clique has no pruning power unless it constrains the *future candidate set*. I therefore looked one level deeper: after a four-clique, what arithmetic is forced on the number of vertices that extend all four anchors?

Let

\[
C=\{a,b,c,d\}\subset\mathbf F_p
\]

be a Paley 4-clique, and let

\[
t_4(C)=\#\{x\in\mathbf F_p\setminus C:\chi(x-a)=\chi(x-b)=\chi(x-c)=\chi(x-d)=1\}.
\]

For each triple `T` contained in `C`, define the smooth projective elliptic curve

\[
E_T:\quad y^2=\prod_{u\in T}(x-u),
\]

and let `N_T=#E_T(F_p)`. Also let `E_C` denote the smooth projective genus-one curve

\[
E_C:\quad y^2=(x-a)(x-b)(x-c)(x-d),
\]

with `N_C=#E_C(F_p)`; since the leading coefficient is a square, its quartic model has two rational points at infinity.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact four-clique / five-curve identity

For every prime p congruent to 1 modulo 4 and every Paley 4-clique C,

\[
\boxed{
16t_4(C)=\sum_{T\in{C\choose3}}N_T+N_C-4p-44.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

For x not in C, the indicator that x is adjacent to all four members of C is

\[
2^{-4}\prod_{u\in C}(1+\chi(x-u)).
\]

If x equals one of the four clique vertices, exactly one character factor is zero and the other three are +1, so the same expression contributes `1/2`. Therefore

\[
16(t_4(C)+2)
=
\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\prod_{u\in C}(1+\chi(x-u)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Expand the product. Each one-factor character sum is zero. For every pair u!=v,

\[
\sum_x\chi((x-u)(x-v))=-1,
\]

and there are six pairs, contributing `-6`. For a triple T,

\[
\sum_x\chi\!\left(\prod_{u\in T}(x-u)\right)=N_T-p-1,
\]

because the cubic model has one point at infinity. For the quartic,

\[
\sum_x\chi\!\left(\prod_{u\in C}(x-u)\right)=N_C-p-2.
\]

Thus

\[
16(t_4+2)
=p-6+\sum_T(N_T-p-1)+(N_C-p-2)
=\sum_TN_T+N_C-4p-12,
\]

which is equivalent to (4.1). QED.

### 4.2 Full rational 4-torsion and the exact missing congruence

For each cubic E_T, all three root differences are quadratic residues because T is a Paley triangle. The explicit halving argument proved in the preceding I0099 run therefore gives full rational E_T[4], hence

\[
16\mid N_T.
\]

The quartic E_C is birational, after choosing a rational point at infinity, to a Legendre model whose parameter is a cross-ratio of the four branch points. Both `lambda` and `1-lambda` are products/quotients of pairwise clique differences. They are therefore squares; since p=1 mod4, the sign changes are squares as well. The same explicit halving criterion on the Legendre model gives full rational 4-torsion and hence

\[
16\mid N_C.
\]

Consequently (4.1) may be written

\[
\boxed{
 t_4(C)=
 \sum_{T\in{C\choose3}}\frac{N_T}{16}
 +\frac{N_C}{16}
 -\frac{p+11}{4}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This exposes a very concrete 2-adic route. Exact enumeration in the new self-check covered **102,356 four-cliques** over every admissible prime up to 97 that actually contains a 4-clique (p=29,37,41,53,61,73,89,97). It found no exception to

\[
 t_4(C)\equiv
 \begin{cases}
 1\pmod2,&p\equiv1\pmod8,\\
 0\pmod2,&p\equiv5\pmod8.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.4, observed only}
\]

For 64 sampled four-cliques the checker also recomputed all five point counts and (4.1) directly. In every such sample,

\[
\sum_T\frac{N_T}{16}+\frac{N_C}{16}\equiv0\pmod2.
\tag{4.5, observed only}
\]

Equation (4.5) would imply (4.4) immediately from (4.3), because `(p+11)/4` is odd exactly when p=1 mod8.

**I do not claim (4.4) or (4.5) as theorems.** The exact new blocker is now much sharper than “find another parity lemma”:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{T\in{C\choose3}}\frac{\#E_T(\mathbf F_p)}{16}
+
\frac{\#E_C(\mathbf F_p)}{16}
\stackrel{?}{\equiv}0\pmod2
\quad\text{for every Paley 4-clique }C.
}
\tag{B13-BLOCK}
\]

This is a compatibility statement about the level-8 / 2-adic Frobenius data of **five related genus-one curves** arising from one four-point configuration. It is qualitatively different from separately applying Hasse or Weil. A proof would have to couple their 8-division data; termwise point-count bounds cannot see this congruence.

So B13 produced one proved exact identity, one strong exact experimental law, and a precise new obstruction, but not yet a monotone source-level invariant.

## 5. B8 — discretize more finely: level-8 elliptic quotient states still do not determine adjacency

The previous run disproved the naive level-4 state model `2E/4E`: two common neighbors can carry the same pair of level-4 classes yet have different Paley adjacency. The natural next attempt is to retain level-8 information.

Take the exact previous example

\[
p=53,\qquad T=\{0,1,7\},\qquad E:y^2=x(x-1)(x-7).
\]

The new exact checker enumerates the whole group and obtains

\[
|E(\mathbf F_{53})|=64,\qquad |2E|=16,\qquad |4E|=4,\qquad |8E|=2.
\]

Thus `2E/8E` has eight cosets. A common neighbor x corresponds to the two points `P` and `-P` above x, so to remove the arbitrary sign choice I label x by the unordered pair of cosets

\[
L_8(x)=\{[P]_{8E},[-P]_{8E}\}.
\]

Under the checker's deterministic coset numbering, the six common neighbors have labels

\[
11:(2,2),\quad
16:(3,4),\quad
17:(5,6),\quad
43:(3,4),\quad
44:(7,7),\quad
47:(5,6).
\]

Now the two unordered label pairs for `(16,17)` and `(16,47)` are identical:

\[
\{(3,4),(5,6)\}.
\]

But

\[
16-17\equiv52\pmod{53}
\]

is a quadratic residue, while

\[
16-47\equiv22\pmod{53}
\]

is a quadratic nonresidue. Hence `(16,17)` is an edge and `(16,47)` is not.

Therefore the refined finite-state proposal is false:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Paley adjacency between triangle-common-neighbors is not determined by their sign-invariant classes in }2E/8E.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is a complete finite counterexample to the *method*, not to Conjecture 25. It says that simply climbing from level 4 to level 8 in the triangle elliptic quotient still throws away arithmetic needed to glue local states into a clique obstruction. Any continuation needs richer data than the isolated quotient class—e.g. explicit Kummer coordinates, coupled cross-ratios, or compatibility among several anchored elliptic curves.

### 6.2 Complete symmetry-reduced exclusion of an 18-clique

\[
x\mapsto1-x,\qquad x\mapsto x^{-1}

For p=3617 they partition the 903 third-anchor choices into exactly **151 S_3 orbits**.

Fix an S_3 representative `u`. Inside its common-neighbor graph K, the two maps

\[
A_u(x)=\frac{u}{x},
\qquad
B_u(x)=\frac{x-u}{x-1}
\]

generate the Klein-four symmetry used for the fourth anchor. They preserve K. They also preserve adjacency, because for x,y in K

\[
\frac{A_u(x)-A_u(y)}{x-y}=-\frac{u}{xy}
\]

and

\[
\frac{B_u(x)-B_u(y)}{x-y}=\frac{u-1}{(x-1)(y-1)},

and every factor on the right is a quadratic residue. Thus it is enough to test one fourth anchor from every V_4 orbit.
