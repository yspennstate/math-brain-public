## 5. B3 — PROVE SOMETHING STRONGER: finite-portal closure theorem and exact induction failure

The strongest useful theorem found in this final run is conditional but global. It says exactly what kind of strengthening of the anchor observation would finish a positive proof.

### Theorem (finite anchor–portal closure implies ultimate periodicity)

Let `S` be any increasing greedy strict 3-sumfree sequence with characteristic function `chi`. Suppose there are finite sets `A,B subset S` and an integer `N` such that for every `n>N`,

`n notin S` if and only if there exist pairwise-distinct `a in A`, `b in B`, `s in S` with `n=a+b+s`.

Then `S` is ultimately periodic.

### Proof

Let

`C = {a+b : a in A, b in B, a != b}`

and let `M=max C`. Increase `N` if necessary so that whenever `n>N` and `c=a+b in C`, the possible third summand `n-c` is larger than every element of `A union B`. Then distinctness from `a,b` is automatic.

For every such `n`, the assumed equivalence becomes

`chi(n)=0  <=>  exists c in C with chi(n-c)=1`.

Equivalently,

`chi(n)=1  <=>  chi(n-c)=0 for every c in C`.

Thus `chi(n)` is a deterministic Boolean function of the preceding `M` characteristic bits. Consider the length-`M` state

`X_n=(chi(n-M+1),...,chi(n))`.

There are only `2^M` possible states, and the recurrence makes `X_{n+1}` a deterministic function of `X_n`. Hence two states must eventually coincide; from the first repetition onward determinism forces the entire future state sequence, and therefore `chi`, to repeat periodically. So `S` is ultimately periodic. QED.

This theorem is stronger than merely finding a Lemma-6 block: a finite global anchor–portal collapse itself forces ultimate periodicity.

### Why the observed anchor phenomenon does not induct

The finite `A0` observation does **not** supply the theorem's hypothesis. Suppose a new accepted late value `y` enters. New strict triples have the form `y+x+z` with later accepted `x,z`. To preserve a fixed anchor reduction one would have to replace every relevant later-later pair sum `x+z` by a pair sum involving a member of a fixed portal set, uniformly for all future `y`. The statement “every excluded integer seen so far has one anchored representation” contains no such closure assertion about the support of later-later pair sums. This is the precise induction failure: the missing invariant lives one order lower, in a pair-sum support that is still unbounded.
