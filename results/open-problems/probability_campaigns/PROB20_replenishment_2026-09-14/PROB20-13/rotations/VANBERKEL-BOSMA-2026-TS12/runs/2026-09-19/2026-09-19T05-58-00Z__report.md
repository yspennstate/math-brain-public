## 4. B19 — exact event-driven block lemma

The useful new mechanism is not another long prefix computation. It is a block-jump rule.

Let `P` be the already certified sequence prefix through its current last accepted value `L`, and let

`F(P) := {a+b+c : a,b,c in P are pairwise distinct}`.

Because `f<g<h` and all later values exceed `h`, the two smallest sequence values are always `f,g`; hence the smallest possible sum of two distinct sequence values is

`q=f+g`.

### Lemma (q-short complement components are accepted wholesale)

Suppose `[u,v]` is the first connected component of integers greater than `L` which is disjoint from `F(P)`, and every integer in `[L+1,u-1]` belongs to `F(P)`. If

`v-u+1 <= q`,

then the greedy sequence accepts **every** integer of `[u,v]`.

### Proof

The integers `L+1,...,u-1` are forbidden by triples from the old prefix, so no new sequence element appears before `u`.

Proceed inductively through `x in [u,v]`. By definition of the complement component, `x` has no representation by three distinct elements entirely from the old prefix `P`. Any new representation created after `u` was accepted must use at least one newly accepted value `y>=u`. The other two distinct positive sequence values have sum at least `f+g=q`. Therefore every such new triple has value at least

`u+q`.

But `x <= v <= u+q-1`. Hence no representation involving a newly inserted block element can reach `x`. Thus `x` remains admissible and is greedily accepted. Induction gives the whole component. ∎

This converts exact evolution from integer-by-integer scanning to an **event-driven interval algorithm**: from a certified finite prefix, compute the union of old distinct triple sums, jump across a forbidden component, and accept any next complement component of width at most `q` in one theorem step. The state is still not proved finite, so this is not yet a periodicity theorem.
