## (b) Every materially distinct route tried, and why it stopped

### 1. Recursive-line and signed searches — useful lower constructions, not `z_2` upper proofs

**Logical status: insufficiency of method, not a failed theorem.**

The source has `z_2 >= z_SL >= z_RL`. Recursive-line or signed admissibility proves irreducibility when it succeeds, but failure of those sufficient criteria does not prove reducibility. Therefore the source's finite `z_RL` exclusions cannot simply be imported as `z_2` upper bounds. This was the main soundness correction at admission.

### 2. Counterexample-first search on the exceedance layers

**Logical status: finite searches and candidate eliminations; no exhaustive `z_2` counterexample exclusion.**

The cell count reduces the only source-relevant exceedance layers to

- `6 x 6`: `N=25` with `(E2,H)=(9,2)` and `N=26` with `(10,0)`;
- `7 x 6`: `N=29` with `(11,2)` and `N=30` with `(12,0)`.

No exact irreducible exceedance candidate has been found. An early obsolete checker was explicitly invalidated and is not evidence. The strongest independent `6 x 6`, `N=25` near miss from the valid signed search was later proved reducible by a positive displayed-range Gram kernel. The `7 x 6` half has not yet received a comparably deep source-valid classification, so search failure there says essentially nothing.

### 3. Displayed-range Gram-kernel criterion

**Logical status: proved one-way theorem plus exact finite certificates.**

For a displayed decomposition `F=(f_1,...,f_N)`, any nonzero symmetric zero-diagonal coefficient-preserving perturbation `H` gives a trace-zero nonzero symmetric matrix. It is indefinite, so at a boundary parameter `t_*` the Gram matrix `I+t_*H` is PSD singular while representing the same polynomial. Thus a positive displayed-range kernel is a sound reducibility certificate.

This proved three of the six direct `N=25` extensions of the source `N=24` witness reducible and proved the independent near miss reducible.

**Why it stops:** zero displayed-range kernel is only a screen. It does not prove irreducibility and misses Gram freedom outside the original displayed span.

### 4. Full Gram fibre / rectangle transfers

**Logical status: proved mechanism and exact reductions for the three zero-screen extensions.**

In the cell-monomial basis, PSD forces hole rows/columns to vanish; same-row/column cross entries are fixed; fully occupied `2 x 2` rectangles generate opposite-diagonal transfer directions. The three direct source-witness extensions that had zero displayed-range kernel each had 176 such full-fibre rectangle-transfer dimensions. Exact identities gave explicit `25 -> 23` SOS compressions.

**Why it stops:** the full Gram fibre is large. The existence of many local transfer directions explains false negatives of the displayed-span test, but it does not by itself classify arbitrary eight-pair `N=24` forms or produce a canonical low-rank boundary point for every candidate.

### 5. Complete elimination of the six direct `N=25` extensions of the source witness

**Logical status: theorem for one `N=24` orbit's direct extensions.**

All six direct one-edge extensions of the known source `N=24` witness are reducible: three by positive displayed-range kernel, three by exact rectangle identities.

**Why it stops:** this closes `N=25` only if every irreducible `N=24` deletion orbit that an `N=25` candidate could have is the source witness orbit. That uniqueness/classification is not proved.

### 6. Hereditary irreducibility

**Logical status: proved structural reduction; known technique in neighbouring literature.**

If an `N`-square displayed form has SOS rank `N`, deleting a complete displayed square leaves an `(N-1)`-square form of rank `N-1`. Therefore an irreducible `N=25` candidate forces each appropriate `N=24` deletion to be irreducible; ruling out `N=25` then automatically rules out `N=26` by another deletion.

**Why it stops:** heredity moves the burden to classifying irreducible `N=24` configurations. It does not classify them.

### 7. Row/column stabilizer and raw orbit reduction

**Logical status: exact finite count, strategically negative.**

The stabilizer of the canonical ordinary skeleton has order 8. There are 31 orbits of unordered free-cell pairs. But raw `N=25` augmentation enumeration still leaves `818,459,851` orbits under that stabilizer.

**Why it stops:** symmetry is too weak before algebraic/hereditary pruning. Orbit reduction must come after stronger local nonface generation, not before it.

### 8. Attempted `N=24` uniqueness via a hinge obstruction

**Logical status: proved local reducibility theorem plus exact finite classification of single candidates.**

For a one-edge hinge center `h`, if both halves of a selected two-edge lie in its complement-arm set `A_h`, Segre rectangle identities give a nonzero displayed product dependence and hence reducibility. Exactly 56 of the 190 possible individual two-edges are hinge-forbidden, leaving 134 hinge-free; the source witness's eight pairs all survive.

**Why it stops:** 134 single candidates are still far too many, and the hinge condition is first-order only.

### 9. Necessary pairwise compatibility graph

**Logical status: source-valid necessary graph, not an irreducibility graph.**

On the 134 hinge-free vertices, among 7247 support-disjoint pairs, exact displayed-range rank proves 122 pairs reducible and leaves 7125 pairwise compatible. The source witness is an eight-vertex matching clique.

**Why it stops:** the graph is extremely dense. Pairwise compatibility is only necessary. Higher-order product relations, strip overloads, full-fibre Gram reductions, or global shorter SOS can destroy an eight-set whose every pair survives. Xu-Yan's `5 x 5` exceptions demonstrate exactly this phenomenon.

**Why it stops:** the missing object is not more raw enumeration; it is a stronger hereditary family of exact reducibility obstructions that collapses the target-clique set before orbit classification.
