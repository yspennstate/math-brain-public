# B11 — DUALIZE: describe the full Gram fibre, not only the displayed-square span

The preceding report left three direct `N=25` extensions of the source `N=24` witness with displayed-range Gram-kernel dimension zero. That zero did not prove irreducibility. This run identifies exactly what the displayed-range test was missing.

Write the cell monomials as

\[
z_{ij}=x_i y_j,
\]

and write any SOS Gram representation of the same biquadratic form as

\[
P(z)=z^TQz,\qquad Q\succeq0,
\]

in the full 36-dimensional cell-monomial basis.

Let `O` be the occupied cells of a simple displayed configuration and `H` its holes.

### Full-fibre lemma

**Lemma.** After the diagonal coefficients are fixed, the affine freedom of a PSD Gram representation is exactly one scalar rectangle-transfer direction for every `2 x 2` rectangle whose four cells are all occupied. In particular, if the occupied set has `R(O)` fully occupied rectangles, the coefficient-preserving affine Gram fibre has dimension `R(O)`.

**Proof.**

For a hole cell `h`, the coefficient of `z_h^2` in `P` is zero, so `Q_hh=0`. Since `Q` is PSD, every `2 x 2` principal minor on `{h,a}` has

\[
0\le Q_{hh}Q_{aa}-Q_{ha}^2=-Q_{ha}^2,
\]

hence `Q_ha=0` for every `a`. Thus all hole rows and columns vanish, and only occupied cells matter.

Now take two occupied cells `a=(i,j)` and `b=(k,l)`.

- If `i=k` or `j=l`, the quartic monomial produced by `z_a z_b` has a repeated `x`-index or a repeated `y`-index. No other unordered pair of cell monomials produces the same quartic monomial. Hence the corresponding off-diagonal Gram entry is fixed individually by the polynomial coefficient.
- If `i!=k` and `j!=l`, the monomial

\[
x_i x_k y_j y_l
\]

has exactly two opposite-diagonal factorizations in the cell basis:

\[
z_{ij}z_{kl}=z_{il}z_{kj}.
\]

Therefore the polynomial coefficient fixes only the sum of the two opposite-diagonal Gram entries. If one complementary corner is a hole, its Gram row is zero and the surviving entry is again fixed. A free scalar remains if and only if all four corners are occupied.

Different unordered row pairs and column pairs give different quartic monomials, so these rectangle constraints are independent. Hence there is exactly one affine transfer parameter per fully occupied rectangle. QED.

This is the exact off-span obstruction to the first report's displayed-range kernel test. The displayed-range calculation only perturbs correlations among the 25 displayed squares. The full Gram fibre can instead rotate mass through cell-level opposite-diagonal identities that are not linear combinations inside that displayed coordinate range.

For each of the three formerly unresolved zero-displayed-kernel direct extensions of the source witness, there are 34 occupied cells and exactly

\[
\boxed{176}
\]

fully occupied rectangles. The full affine Gram fibre is therefore 176-dimensional before PSD facial reduction. This is why `kernel_dim=0` in the old screen was far from a converse irreducibility theorem.

## Exact rectangle identities

For opposite corners

\[
a=z_{ij},\quad b=z_{kl},\quad c=z_{il},\quad d=z_{kj},
\]

one has `ab=cd`. Two useful exact consequences are

\[
(a+b)^2+c^2+d^2=a^2+b^2+(c+d)^2, \tag{1}
\]

and

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2=(a+b)^2+(c-d)^2. \tag{2}
\]

Identity (1) is a square-preserving rectangle flip; identity (2) collapses four one-cell squares to two bilinear squares.

The key discovery is that all three formerly zero-kernel direct extensions admit exact flips followed by (2), so they are not irreducible after all.

# B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: hereditary irreducibility isolates the missing classification

The local theorem above becomes useful through a general hereditary fact.

**Hereditary irreducibility lemma.** Suppose

\[
P=f_1^2+\cdots+f_N^2
\]

has SOS rank exactly `N`. Then, for every displayed square `f_k^2`,

\[
P-f_k^2
\]

has SOS rank exactly `N-1`.

**Proof.** The remaining displayed squares give rank at most `N-1`. If `P-f_k^2` had an SOS using at most `N-2` squares, adding `f_k^2` would give an SOS of `P` using at most `N-1` squares, contradicting rank `N`. QED.

This lemma is standard in spirit and is explicitly consonant with the hereditary method in the current Xu-Yan preprint; no novelty claim is made for the general principle.

Now specialize to `6 x 6`. Any irreducible `N=25` candidate has `|E1|=16`, `|E2|=9`, `H=2`. Deleting any of its nine two-edge squares produces an irreducible `N=24` configuration with `|E1|=16`, `|E2|=8`, `H=4` on the same ordinary extremal skeleton.

The source proves that the ordinary 16-edge `C4`-free skeleton is unique up to row/column relabelling. The present run proves that **if** such an `N=24` deletion is in the source witness pairing orbit, re-adding the ninth two-edge lands in one of the six direct extensions and is reducible. Therefore an irreducible `N=25` counterexample, if one exists, must have all of its two-edge deletions in some other irreducible `N=24` pairing orbit.

This gives the clean remaining `6 x 6` obstruction:

\[
\boxed{\text{Classify irreducible }N=24\text{ pairings on the unique 16-edge skeleton; are there any non-source orbits?}} \tag{9}
\]

If the answer to (9) is no, then no irreducible `N=25` form exists. The hereditary lemma then also excludes `N=26`, because deleting any displayed square from an irreducible `N=26` form would produce an irreducible `N=25` form. Consequently `z_2(6,6)=24`; the source hierarchy would immediately force

\[
z_2(6,6)=z_{SL}(6,6)=z_{RL}(6,6)=24.
\]

So the current route has reduced the entire `6 x 6` source equality to a concrete `N=24` irreducible-pairing classification, rather than separate `N=25` and `N=26` searches.

This reduction is substantial but does **not** move `R_SMALL6`, because (9) remains unproved.

# B14 — FIND THE GROUP ACTING: exact orbit arithmetic, and why symmetry alone is not enough

\[

\]

\[

\]

so this row-column stabilizer is the dihedral group of order eight.

On the 20 complementary cells, the cycle types are:

- identity: `1^20`;
- one involution: `1^4 2^8`;
- four involutions: `1^2 2^9`;
- two order-four elements: `2^2 4^4`.

There are only

\[
\boxed{31}
\]

orbits of unordered pairs of complementary cells, with orbit-size distribution `5` orbits of size 2, `7` of size 4, and `19` of size 8.

That small local number initially looks promising. But the complete `N=25` augmentation layer is much larger. Since all 20 complement cells are either two holes or halves of nine two-edges, an augmentation is exactly an involution of the 20 complement cells with exactly two fixed points. Burnside counting under the eight-element stabilizer gives fixed-augmentation counts

\[
6547290750,\ 66894,\ 80218,\ 80218,\ 80218,\ 80218,\ 146,\ 146.
\]

Their sum is

\[
6547678808,
\]

so the number of row-column-stabilizer orbits is

\[
\boxed{818459851}. \tag{10}
\]

For completeness, the fixed-point formula used in the checker is exact. If an automorphism has `c_l` cycles of length `l`, a commuting involution either pairs two cycles of equal length, with `l` possible phase shifts, or acts within one cycle. On an odd self-cycle only the identity is possible; on an even self-cycle the identity or half-turn is possible. Summing these choices subject to exactly two fixed points gives the fixed counts above.

Equation (10) is a useful negative result: **global skeleton symmetry by itself does not make exhaustive `N=25` orbit enumeration remotely small enough**. The right order of operations is the one emphasized by Xu-Yan: hereditary/local reducibility first, compatibility graph second, clique/orbit enumeration only after severe pruning.
