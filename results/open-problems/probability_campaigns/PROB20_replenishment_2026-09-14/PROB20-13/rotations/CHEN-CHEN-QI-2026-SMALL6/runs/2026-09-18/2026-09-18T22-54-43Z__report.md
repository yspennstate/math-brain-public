## Hinge reducibility lemma

**Lemma.** If a selected two-edge has halves `a,b in A_h` for some `h in S`, then every displayed decomposition containing the full one-edge skeleton `S` and that two-edge is reducible. Consequently no irreducible limited form on this skeleton may use such a pair.

**Proof.** Write

\[
h=(r_0,c_0),\quad a=(r_1,c_1),\quad b=(r_2,c_2).
\]

Because `a,b in A_h`, the four cross cells

\[
(r_1,c_0),\ (r_0,c_1),\ (r_2,c_0),\ (r_0,c_2)
\]

all belong to `S`, hence their monomials occur as displayed one-edge forms. The Segre rectangle identities give

\[
z_{r_0c_0}z_{r_1c_1}=z_{r_1c_0}z_{r_0c_1},
\qquad
z_{r_0c_0}z_{r_2c_2}=z_{r_2c_0}z_{r_0c_2}.
\]

If the selected two-edge form is

\[
f=z_{r_1c_1}+z_{r_2c_2},
\]

then summing the two identities gives the displayed-product dependence

\[
\boxed{
 z_{r_0c_0} f
 =z_{r_1c_0}z_{r_0c_1}
 +z_{r_2c_0}z_{r_0c_2}.}
\tag{H}
\]

This is a nonzero linear relation among products of **distinct displayed forms**. Encode its coefficients as symmetric off-diagonal Gram entries (halving coefficients in the usual `F^T H F` convention). The resulting symmetric matrix `H` is nonzero, has zero diagonal, and preserves every polynomial coefficient. By the displayed-range Gram-perturbation lemma proved in the first immutable report, a nonzero zero-diagonal coefficient-preserving perturbation makes the displayed SOS reducible: `H` is trace-zero and nonzero, hence indefinite, and a boundary value of `I+tH` is PSD singular while representing the same polynomial. Therefore the sub-decomposition consisting of the 16 one-edge squares and this two-edge square already has rank below 17. Adding any other displayed squares cannot restore irreducibility of the full displayed decomposition. QED.

This is a source-valid `z_2` obstruction: it proves an actual SOS rank drop and does not appeal to failure of `RW3+`, signed admissibility, or another merely sufficient criterion.
