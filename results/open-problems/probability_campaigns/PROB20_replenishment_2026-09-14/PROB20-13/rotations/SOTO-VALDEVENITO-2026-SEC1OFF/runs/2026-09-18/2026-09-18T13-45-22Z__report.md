## 6. What a full proof must contain that no existing route contains

A closure proof on the upper-bound side needs one genuinely uniform mechanism. The finite-prefix route is currently the most concrete. It would be enough to provide an explicit family `mu_m` and prove all of the following on the page:

1. **Source-validity uniformly over sample size.** `mu_m` is chosen before the experiment and does not depend on the realized sample or sample size. The hard-law statement must survive arbitrary randomized exact sample-size choices.
2. **A base no-query bound.** Before using the oracle, every stopping policy has success at most `1/e+o(1)` under `mu_m`, uniformly over sampling density.
3. **A filtration-level query bound.** At every reachable history at which the query remains unused, the extra success obtainable from the reliable count response is `o(1)`, uniformly over all histories that matter to the value. Average mutual information alone is not enough unless exceptional histories are also controlled.
4. **Delayed timing.** The bound must survive the algorithm's choice of query time, not only a predetermined or immediate query.
5. **Uniform continuum control.** The argument must cover every sampling fraction `p`, or work directly in the exact hypergeometric model, without a growing numerical grid.
6. **Asymptotics.** The resulting error must tend to zero with `m` so that `B_m -> 1/e`.
7. **Source lift.** The already-understood finite-prefix iid-to-hypergeometric approximation must be incorporated with an error that vanishes as `n->infinity` for each chosen `m`.

No current route supplies items 2–6 simultaneously. The projective nested-end program supplies symmetry but leaks. The finite common-tail program suppresses leakage but currently lacks an analytic asymptotic construction. The information-theoretic program describes what must be small but does not construct a law making it small at every reachable belief.

On the lower-bound/refutation side, a full proof would instead require one explicit ordinal one-query policy whose success exceeds `1/e` against **every** source-valid offline preliminary order. Nothing in the current best-response calculations provides that minimax robustness.

The technique to port is therefore **conditional hard symmetry at the level of the observable filtration**, not the Pólya recursion itself. The source's immediate-query upper-bound argument is the second-nearest analogue: it already works with unrestricted arrivals but removes adaptive query timing. The one-rejection-delay special case above is the minimal bridge between that result and the full problem.
