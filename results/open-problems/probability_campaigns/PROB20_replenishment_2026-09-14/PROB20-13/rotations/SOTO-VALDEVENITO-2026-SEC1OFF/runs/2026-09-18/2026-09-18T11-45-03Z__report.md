This run asks the smallest and then the general version of exactly that question.

Use the same reindexing as the previous report. A nested-end order is encoded by bits `X_1,X_2,... in {0,1}`, and

`S_n := X_1+...+X_n`.

The position of the best rank among the first `n+1` distinguished ranks is `S_n+1`. An **exact-uniform projective nested-end law** means

`S_m ~ Unif{0,1,...,m}`

for every relevant scale `m`.

## 5. B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY: smallest non-Markov routing cell

The first genuine routing freedom occurs when two previous bits have been generated and the third bit is chosen. Exact uniformity at scales 1 and 2 already fixes the length-two history masses.

From `S_1 ~ Unif{0,1}`,

`P(X_1=0)=P(X_1=1)=1/2`.

The forced aggregate Pólya flow from scale 1 to scale 2 gives

`P(X_2=1 | X_1=0)=1/3`,

`P(X_2=1 | X_1=1)=2/3`.

Hence

`P(00)=1/3`,

`P(01)=1/6`,

`P(10)=1/6`,

`P(11)=1/3`.

At the next extension, exact uniformity of `S_3` forces

`P(X_3=1 | 00)=1/4`,

`P(X_3=1 | 11)=3/4`.

The middle Pascal vertex `S_2=1` contains two distinct histories, so here non-Markov routing is possible. Put

`a := P(X_3=1 | 01)`.

The total upward edge flow out of `S_2=1` must equal `1/6`; because `P(01)=P(10)=1/6`, this forces

`P(X_3=1 | 10)=1-a`,

with `0<=a<=1`.

Thus this one real parameter is the **complete** smallest non-Markov exact-uniform routing family.

Define the signed predictive contrasts

`d_1 := P(X_3=1 | X_1=1)-P(X_3=1 | X_1=0)`,

`d_2 := P(X_3=1 | X_2=1)-P(X_3=1 | X_2=0)`.

Direct substitution gives

`P(X_3=1 | X_1=0)=1/6+a/3`,

`P(X_3=1 | X_1=1)=5/6-a/3`,

hence

`d_1 = 2(1-a)/3`.

Similarly,

`P(X_3=1 | X_2=0)=1/2-a/3`,

`P(X_3=1 | X_2=1)=1/2+a/3`,

so

`d_2 = 2a/3`.

Therefore

`d_1+d_2 = 2/3`

for every admissible non-Markov routing parameter `a`.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact minimax routing at the first non-Markov cell

For the entire one-parameter family above,

`max(d_1,d_2) >= 1/3`,

and equality holds exactly at `a=1/2`.

### Proof

Because `d_1+d_2=2/3`, their maximum is at least their average `1/3`. Equality requires `d_1=d_2`, i.e.

`2(1-a)/3 = 2a/3`,

so `a=1/2`. Conversely `a=1/2` gives `d_1=d_2=1/3`. QED.

The point `a=1/2` is exactly the Markov/Pólya routing at the middle vertex. Extreme non-Markov rerouting can hide one pairwise dependence completely, but only by making the other much larger. For example `a=1` gives

`d_1=0`, `d_2=2/3`.

This is the smallest complete demonstration that “route within the Pascal layer to hide the leak” merely redistributes pairwise predictive information; it does not remove it.

Outcome B4: **complete restricted theorem, no source-metric movement**.

## 6. B13 — FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES: exact pairwise-leakage conservation

The B4 identity is not a three-bit accident. There is an exact all-scale law.

For `1<=i<=n`, define

`d_i^(n) := P(X_{n+1}=1 | X_i=1)-P(X_{n+1}=1 | X_i=0)`.

### Theorem 6.1 — pairwise predictive contrasts sum to `n/3`

For every exact-uniform projective nested-end law and every `n>=1`,

`sum_{i=1}^n d_i^(n) = n/3`.

Equivalently, the average signed predictive contrast of the next side bit with the past bits is exactly `1/3` at every scale.

### Proof

Exact uniformity of `S_j` gives

`E[S_j]=j/2`

and

`E[S_j^2]=j(2j+1)/6`.

Since `X_j=S_j-S_{j-1}`, exact uniformity at adjacent scales implies

`E[X_j]=1/2`

for every `j`. Thus every individual side bit is fair.

Now use

`S_{n+1}=S_n+X_{n+1}`.

Squaring and taking expectations,

`E[S_{n+1}^2] = E[S_n^2] + 2E[S_n X_{n+1}] + E[X_{n+1}]`.

Therefore

`E[S_n X_{n+1}]`

`= (1/2) * ( E[S_{n+1}^2]-E[S_n^2]-E[X_{n+1}] )`

`= (1/2) * ( ((n+1)(2n+3)-n(2n+1))/6 - 1/2 )`

`= (1/2) * ( (4n+3)/6 - 3/6 )`

`= n/3`.

Since `E[S_n]=n/2` and `E[X_{n+1}]=1/2`,

`Cov(S_n,X_{n+1}) = n/3 - n/4 = n/12`.

But `S_n=sum_{i=1}^n X_i`, so

`sum_{i=1}^n Cov(X_i,X_{n+1}) = n/12`.

For two fair Bernoulli variables `A,B`, one has the exact identity

`P(B=1|A=1)-P(B=1|A=0)=4 Cov(A,B)`.

Applying it to every pair `(X_i,X_{n+1})` yields

`sum_i d_i^(n) = 4 * n/12 = n/3`.

QED.

### Monovariant interpretation

Define the total signed predictive-memory score

`M_n := sum_{i=1}^n d_i^(n)`.

Then every exact-uniform projective nested-end law satisfies

`M_n=n/3`,

so its forced lower envelope grows linearly and

`M_{n+1}-M_n=1/3`.

This is not a statement that the same pairwise correlations increase one by one; rather, it is an exact scale-by-scale conservation/growth identity. Non-Markov routing can redistribute `M_n` among past coordinates, but cannot reduce the total.

### Corollary 6.2 — at least one old bit has Pólya-sized correlation

For every `n`,

`max_i d_i^(n) >= 1/3`.

Thus some past side bit predicts the next side direction with signed contrast at least the exchangeable Pólya value.

### Corollary 6.3 — Pólya minimizes average pairwise mutual information

Because both variables in each pair are fair, a pair with signed contrast `d in [-1,1]` has mutual information in nats

`phi(d) = ((1+d)/2) log(1+d) + ((1-d)/2) log(1-d)`.

A direct derivative gives

`phi''(d)=1/(1-d^2)>0`

for `|d|<1`, so `phi` is convex. Jensen and Theorem 6.1 imply

`(1/n) sum_{i=1}^n I(X_i;X_{n+1})`

`= (1/n) sum_i phi(d_i^(n))`

`>= phi( (1/n)sum_i d_i^(n) )`

`= phi(1/3)`

`= (5/3)log 2 - log 3`

`≈ 0.05663301226513249` nats.

The exchangeable Beta(1,1)-Pólya law has `d_i^(n)=1/3` for every `i`, and therefore attains equality in this pairwise-information lower bound. The theorem does **not** claim that Pólya is the unique full process attaining equality; higher-order nonexchangeable freedom may remain even when all pairwise contrasts equal `1/3`.

This is stronger than the previous aggregate-flow obstruction. An exact-uniform non-Markov routing cannot lower the average pairwise predictive information below the Pólya process. If it suppresses some pairwise correlations, convexity forces compensating concentration elsewhere.

Outcome B13: **new exact structural barrier within the nested-end category, no source-metric movement**.

## 7. B28 — REPLACE “FOR ALL” WITH “FOR TYPICAL”: a positive fraction of past bits must leak

The conservation identity also gives a quantitative typicality statement.

Fix a threshold `tau<1/3` and let

`A_tau := { i in {1,...,n} : d_i^(n) >= tau }`.

Since every signed contrast is at most `1`, if `r=|A_tau|/n`, then

`1/3 = (1/n)sum_i d_i^(n)`

`<= r*1 + (1-r)*tau`.

Therefore

`r >= (1/3 - tau)/(1-tau)`.

### Theorem 7.1 — positive-density pairwise leakage

For every exact-uniform projective nested-end law and every `tau<1/3`,

`|A_tau|/n >= (1/3-tau)/(1-tau)`.

Concrete consequences are:

- at least `n/3` past bits have nonnegative signed correlation with `X_{n+1}`;
- at least `n/5` past bits satisfy `d_i^(n)>=1/6`;
- at least `n/9` past bits satisfy `d_i^(n)>=1/4`.

Thus the forced memory cannot be concentrated into only one sporadic coordinate.

There is an equally useful randomized form. Let `I` be uniform on `{1,...,n}`, independent of the process, and suppose a predictor is told `(I,X_I)` and must predict `X_{n+1}`. For a fixed fair pair with signed contrast `d`, Bayes success is

`(1+|d|)/2`.

Therefore

`P(Bayes predicts X_{n+1} correctly from (I,X_I))`

`= 1/2 + (1/(2n)) sum_i |d_i^(n)|`

`>= 1/2 + (1/(2n)) |sum_i d_i^(n)|`

`= 2/3`.

So **a uniformly random past side bit is, on average, a 2/3-success predictor of the next side bit**, regardless of how non-Markov routing is arranged, as long as all peak layers remain exactly uniform.

This does not yet imply a source-valid secretary strategy: the algorithm does not receive arbitrary side bits for free. Random sample deletion and ordinal arrival history determine which side information is actually observable, and a hard law may try to place its strongest correlations on histories that are rarely reconstructible. That observability bridge is now the precise missing theorem.

Outcome B28: **typical leakage proved; source metric unchanged**.
