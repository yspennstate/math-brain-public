## 3. B2 — attack the conjecture's negation first: quantify the leakage exactly

This does **not** refute the source conjecture. It proves instead that the natural Theorem 2.6 Yao distribution is far from a `1/e` hard distribution once sub-threshold arrivals are visible.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact Pólya/Beta representation

For any finite side string `(X_2,...,X_n)` with `ell` left choices and `r=n-1-ell` right choices, the source insertion process assigns probability

`ell! r! / n!`.

**Proof.** Along a particular string, the successive left-choice numerators are exactly `1,2,...,ell`, and the successive right-choice numerators are exactly `1,2,...,r`; the denominators are `2,3,...,n`. Therefore the product is `ell! r!/n!`.

On the other hand,

`int_0^1 theta^ell (1-theta)^r dtheta = B(ell+1,r+1) = ell! r!/(ell+r+1)! = ell!r!/n!`.

Thus the law agrees on every cylinder event with first sampling `Theta~Unif[0,1]` and then taking the side bits conditionally iid Bernoulli(`Theta`). QED.

This also gives the source's uniform location statement immediately: among the top `k` elements,

`J-1 | Theta ~ Bin(k-1,Theta)`,

and beta-binomial mixing with `Theta~Unif[0,1]` gives `P[J=j]=1/k` for `j=1,...,k`.

### The unrestricted information in the event `r=k`

Fix an exact sample size `s`, query immediately after the sample, and condition on response `r=k`. Then `x^{k+1}` is sampled and `x^1,...,x^k` are unsampled. There are exactly

`N = n-s-k`

unsampled elements below the sample maximum, namely unsampled ranks among `x^{k+2},...,x^n`.

Under the representation above and conditional on `Theta=theta`, each of those `N` lower unsampled elements independently lies on the left with probability `theta`. Every lower-left element arrives **before** the top-`k` block, and every lower-right element arrives **after** it. Therefore, if

`M = number of sub-threshold arrivals observed before the first above-threshold arrival`,

then

`M | Theta=theta, r=k ~ Bin(N,theta)`.

The algorithm knows `N` from `(n,s,k)` and directly observes `M`. With the uniform prior,

`Theta | M=m, r=k ~ Beta(m+1,N-m+1)`.

In particular the lower arrivals are not a vague leakage channel: they are an exact Bayesian sample from the latent variable controlling the top-block position.

### Exact finite posterior for the peak position

For `j=1,...,k`, integrating the top-block binomial law against that posterior gives

`P[J=j | M=m,r=k]`

`= C(k-1,j-1) * (m+1)_(j-1) * (N-m+1)_(k-j) / (N+2)_(k-1)`,

where `(a)_q` is the rising factorial.

Moreover `M` itself is uniform on `{0,...,N}` after mixing over the uniform `Theta`, because the beta-binomial law with parameters `(N,1,1)` is uniform.

Hence an explicit immediate-query best response to this particular Yao distribution is:

1. query immediately and obtain `r=k`;
2. ignore sub-threshold candidates as selections, but count them until the first above-threshold arrival, obtaining `M`;
3. choose a position `j` maximizing the displayed posterior;
4. select the `j`-th above-threshold arrival.

Before `x^1`, all above-threshold arrivals in this mountain order are increasing records; after `x^1`, success is already impossible. Conditional on the pre-block information there is therefore no additional success-relevant signal before the peak beyond the candidate index. This justifies the posterior-mode rule for this distribution.

### A fully explicit `r=2` theorem

For `k=2`, the posterior rule simplifies completely. The order of the two top candidates is `(x^2,x^1)` with posterior probability `(m+1)/(N+2)` and `(x^1,x^2)` with posterior probability `(N-m+1)/(N+2)`. Thus the algorithm takes the second high arrival iff `m>N/2` (ties arbitrary).

Its exact conditional success is

`q_N = 1/(N+1) * sum_{m=0}^N max(m+1,N-m+1)/(N+2)`.

Writing `N=2h` or `N=2h+1` gives

`q_{2h} = (3h+1)/(2(2h+1))`,

`q_{2h+1} = (3h+4)/(2(2h+3))`,

so

`q_N -> 3/4`.

The exact checker verifies the sum and closed forms for every `0<=N<=50` and verifies the underlying Pólya/Beta cylinder probabilities for every side string through `n=10`.

### Quantitative failure of the Theorem 2.6 hard distribution in the unrestricted model

Take a deterministic sample sequence `s_n/n -> p in (0,1)`. Use Algorithm 2's uniform choice among the `r` above-threshold arrivals for every `r != 2`, but use the posterior rule above when `r=2`.

The default uniform rule has conditional success exactly `1/r` irrespective of the preliminary order. Under fixed-size sampling,

`P[r=2] = C(n-3,s_n-1)/C(n,s_n)`

`= s_n (n-s_n)(n-s_n-1) / (n(n-1)(n-2)) -> p(1-p)^2`.

The source calculation for Algorithm 2 tends to `-p ln p`. Since the `r=2` conditional success improves from `1/2` to `3/4`, this hybrid has success against the Theorem 2.6 order distribution tending to at least

`-p ln p + (1/4) p(1-p)^2`.

At `p=1/e` this is the exact expression

`1/e + (1/(4e))(1-1/e)^2 ≈ 0.40462842694 > 1/e`.

So the source's restricted hard distribution cannot yield the conjectured unrestricted upper bound, even if one retains the source's immediate query time. This is a quantitative sharpening of the qualitative leakage remark after Theorem 2.8, not a source-level counterexample.

For general fixed `k`, as `N->infinity`, `M/N->Theta`, and the posterior-mode success tends

`a_k = int_0^1 max_{0<=j<=k-1} C(k-1,j) theta^j (1-theta)^(k-1-j) dtheta`.

The maximizing `j` is constant on `[j/k,(j+1)/k]`, so each `a_k` is rational. The exact artifact records, for example,

`a_2=3/4`, `a_3=17/27`, `a_4=71/128`, `a_5=1569/3125`.

For every `k>=2`, `a_k>1/k` because the maximizing binomial atom changes with `theta` on sets of positive measure, whereas each fixed atom integrates to `1/k`. Thus the leakage is systematic, not a peculiarity of `r=2`.

## 4. B11 — dualize: a no-go theorem for the natural nested Markov repair

The preceding leakage suggests trying to alter the left/right insertion probabilities while preserving a uniform peak location. In the most natural Markov version of that idea, there is no freedom.

### Proposition 4.1 — uniqueness inside the position-Markov nested-block class

Suppose a family of preliminary-order distributions is generated as follows.

- `B_1=(x^1)`.
- At step `t`, `x^t` is inserted at the left or right end of `B_{t-1}`.
- Let `K_t` be the position of `x^1` in `B_t`.
- The probability of inserting left at time `t` depends on the past only through `K_{t-1}`; write it `q_t(i)` when `K_{t-1}=i`.
- For every `t`, `K_t` is exactly uniform on `{1,...,t}`.

Then necessarily

`q_t(i)=i/t` for every `i=1,...,t-1`,

so this process is exactly the Pólya/source construction of Theorem 2.6.

**Proof.** Since `K_{t-1}` is uniform, the boundary state `K_t=1` can arise only from `K_{t-1}=1` followed by a right insertion. Thus

`(1/(t-1))(1-q_t(1)) = 1/t`,

hence `q_t(1)=1/t`.

For an interior `i in {2,...,t-1}`, `K_t=i` arises either from `K_{t-1}=i` followed by right, or from `K_{t-1}=i-1` followed by left. Uniformity therefore gives

`(1/(t-1))[(1-q_t(i))+q_t(i-1)] = 1/t`,

or

`q_t(i)-q_t(i-1)=1/t`.

Starting from `q_t(1)=1/t` yields `q_t(i)=i/t` inductively. The `K_t=t` boundary is then automatically satisfied. QED.

This is a genuine route obstruction: **within nested mountain orders whose update is Markov in the current peak position, exact conditional hiding of the peak forces the same Pólya law whose lower tail reveals the latent bias.** A successful `1/e` Yao proof must therefore leave at least one of these features: exact per-`k` uniformity, nested two-ended mountain structure, or position-Markov updating.

### Exchangeable corollary

There is an analogous uniqueness statement for consistent exchangeable side assignments. By de Finetti, write the side bits conditionally iid Bernoulli(`Theta`) for a mixing law `nu` on `[0,1]`. If the peak position among the top `k` elements is uniform for every `k`, then

`E[Theta^m] = P[X_2=...=X_{m+1}=1] = 1/(m+1)`

for every `m>=0`. These are exactly the moments of the uniform law on `[0,1]`; the Hausdorff moment problem on a compact interval is determinate, so `nu` must be uniform. Hence the source Pólya/Beta mixture is also the **unique consistent exchangeable** nested-side law with exact uniform peak positions.

Again this is a route filter, not a novelty claim.

**B11 outcome:** merely changing the local insertion probabilities while keeping the same nested position-Markov architecture cannot remove the leakage. The next dual distribution needs a different architecture, or it must accept nonuniform conditional peak positions and win only after averaging over `r`.

## 5. B19 — make it an algorithm: what finite state is justified, and what is not

For the source distribution of Theorem 2.6, the calculation above proves a real sufficient statistic after an immediate query:

`(r=k, N=n-s-k, M=number of lower arrivals before the first above-threshold arrival)`.

Given this state, the posterior of the latent bias is exactly `Beta(M+1,N-M+1)`, and the top-peak position has the displayed beta-binomial law. Thus the posterior-mode policy is an exact finite Bellman decision for this **specific** order distribution.

This simultaneously shows why the tempting smaller state `(r, number of above-threshold records, query-used flag)` is not sufficient in the unrestricted source problem: two histories with the same `r=2` and the same record count but different `M` have different posterior-optimal decisions.

For arbitrary source-valid offline order distributions, no constant-dimensional sufficient statistic has been proved. What *is* rigorous for each fixed finite `n` is the following finite extensive-form reduction:

1. by ordinal invariance, canonicalize adversarial weights to ranks `1,...,n`;
2. a pure adversarial input is a preliminary rank permutation;
3. a deterministic algorithm's information set is its sample size plus the marked ordinal history of sampled/arriving ranks, query-used status, and (if queried) the count response;
4. there are finitely many such histories;
5. with perfect recall, behavioral randomization and mixed normal-form randomization are equivalent, so the fixed-`n` minimax value can be written as a finite sequence-form linear program.

This does **not** by itself give an asymptotic source theorem: the number of information states grows rapidly with `n`, and no finite-state stabilization has been established. It does, however, give an exact computational route for searching small hard order supports without silently assuming the missing sufficient statistic.

A particularly promising family is the source's Theorem 2.8 style: choose a distribution over finitely many permutations of the top `m` ranks and append a common decreasing tail. For fixed `m`, arbitrary one-query behavior before the global maximum reduces to a finite Bayesian game once the top-rank sampling pattern is included. The research target is then to find a **single** order distribution whose best-response value is below the current unrestricted endpoint uniformly over deterministic sample sizes; one universal Yao distribution then handles randomized sample-size algorithms by linearity. No such certificate is claimed in this run.

**B19 outcome:** an exact posterior/Bellman state was proved for the Pólya hard distribution, and a source-valid finite sequence-form search architecture was isolated. The hoped-for constant-size sufficient statistic for the full SBOffAO1 model remains unproved.
