## 5. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE: Blackwell/value-of-information formulation

The finite hidden-state game has a clean Bayesian experiment formulation that does not depend on secretary-specific syntax.

Fix a reachable history and suppose the single query has not yet been used. Let `H` be the finite hidden state (preliminary order, distinguished sample mask, and whatever latent components remain after conditioning on the ordinary history). Let `b` be its posterior distribution. If the query is never used again, every deterministic continuation policy `pi` has a success vector

`g_pi : H -> [0,1]`.

The optimal no-query continuation value is

`F(b) := max_pi sum_h b(h) g_pi(h)`.

Therefore `F` is the maximum of linear functionals of the belief.

### Lemma 5.1 — exact one-query value-of-information bound

Let a reliable ordinal query at this history produce response `Q`, with posterior `b_Q`. Then

`0 <= E[F(b_Q)] - F(b) <= E[ TV(b_Q,b) ]`.

Equivalently,

`E[ TV(b_Q,b) ] = TV(P_{H,Q}, P_H tensor P_Q)`.

By Pinsker,

`E[F(b_Q)] - F(b) <= sqrt(I(H;Q)/2)`

when natural logarithms are used.

### Proof

Because `E[b_Q]=b` and `F` is convex as a maximum of linear maps, Jensen gives

`E[F(b_Q)] >= F(E[b_Q])=F(b)`.

For the upper bound, any policy payoff lies in `[0,1]`. Hence for any two beliefs `b,b'`,

`|sum_h (b(h)-b'(h))g_pi(h)| <= TV(b,b')`.

Taking maxima over `pi` on both sides yields the 1-Lipschitz property

`|F(b)-F(b')| <= TV(b,b')`.

Apply this with `b'=b_Q` and average over `Q`:

`E[F(b_Q)]-F(b) <= E|F(b_Q)-F(b)| <= E TV(b_Q,b)`.

Finally,

`sum_q P(q) TV(P_{H|Q=q},P_H)`

is exactly the total variation distance between the joint law `P_{H,Q}` and the product `P_H tensor P_Q`; Pinsker gives the mutual-information corollary. QED.

### Why this matters here

Because there is only one query, every query decision in the Bellman tree can be interpreted as purchasing one Blackwell experiment. A future analytic hard law can therefore be designed to make the **incremental** query channel nearly uninformative at every reachable belief, rather than trying to flatten the complete Bellman envelope directly.

This does not yet prove `1/e`: for the present finite-prefix laws the query response can reveal substantial information about the distinguished sample mask, so the right-hand side need not be small. The point is that it isolates a source-matching sufficient route:

- keep the no-query success at `1/e+o(1)`;
- prove every reachable one-query experiment has conditional information gain `o(1)` in the above TV sense.

Then the unrestricted one-query value is also `1/e+o(1)`.

Outcome B18: **new analytic reduction, no metric movement by itself**.

## 6. B25 — COUNT THE SAME THING TWICE: Bernstein success polynomials

For a fixed hard law with denominator `D`, a fixed deterministic one-query policy `pi`, and `m` iid distinguished sample bits, define `c_k(pi)` as follows:

`c_k(pi) = sum_o hard_numerator(o) * #{k-element masks M on which pi wins under order o}`.

Then the exact policy success function is

`S_pi(p) = (1/D) sum_{k=0}^m c_k(pi) p^k (1-p)^(m-k)`.

This is simply a degree-`m` Bernstein-form polynomial. The complete Bellman value is the upper envelope

`V(p)=max_pi S_pi(p)`.

### Proposition 6.1 — uniform-density / uniform-size double count

For every deterministic policy,

`int_0^1 S_pi(p) dp`

`= 1/[D(m+1)] * sum_{k=0}^m c_k(pi)/binom(m,k)`.

### Proof

For each `k`,

`int_0^1 p^k(1-p)^(m-k) dp`

`= k!(m-k)!/(m+1)!`

`= 1/[(m+1) binom(m,k)]`.

Substitution gives the formula. Equivalently, sample `p` uniformly from `[0,1]` and then sample the `m` mask bits iid Bernoulli(`p`). The marginal law first chooses the mask size `K` uniformly from `{0,...,m}` and then a uniform `K`-subset. Thus the same success mass has been counted once by integrating over densities and once by averaging uniformly over sample-mask cardinalities. QED.

This representation is the finite object that a human-sized sequence-form dual should constrain. Instead of treating 28,000 continuum sample points as unrelated, one can seek inequalities on the integer coefficient vectors `c(pi)` that dominate **all** policies simultaneously.
