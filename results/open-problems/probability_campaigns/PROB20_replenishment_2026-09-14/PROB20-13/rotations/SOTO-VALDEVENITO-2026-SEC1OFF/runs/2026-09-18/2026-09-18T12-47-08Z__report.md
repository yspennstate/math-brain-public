# 5. B21 — ASK WHO ELSE HAS THIS SHAPE: hypergeometric thinning as the exact observability channel

## 5.1 General fixed-size deletion setup

Let `M` binary side indicators

`L_1,...,L_M in {0,1}`

encode whether a chosen collection of lower-ranked candidates lies on the left side of a better block in a nested-end preliminary order. Write

`T := L_1+...+L_M`.

Let `H` be any hidden variable determined before sampling: for example, a top-block peak position, a top-prefix side history, or any statistic of the hidden preliminary order.

Now condition on the source's uniform sample in such a way that **exactly `R` of these `M` candidates remain unsampled**, with the retained `R`-subset uniform among the `M` candidates and independent of their hidden left/right labels conditional on the preliminary order. This is exactly the deletion mechanism induced by a uniform fixed-size sample once the number retained in this collection is fixed.

Let

`Z := number of retained candidates among these M whose L-value is 1`.

Conditional on `T=t`,

`Z | T=t ~ Hypergeometric(population M, successes t, draws R)`.

Equivalently,

`P(Z=z | T=t) = C(t,z) C(M-t,R-z) / C(M,R)`.

This is the exact fixed-size analogue of binomial thinning.

## 5.2 The factorial-moment transfer theorem

For an integer `r>=0`, write the falling factorial

`(x)_r := x(x-1)...(x-r+1)`, with `(x)_0=1`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact fixed-size observability spectrum

For every `0<=r<=R`,

`E[(Z)_r | T] = ((R)_r/(M)_r) (T)_r`.

Consequently, for every hidden variable `H`,

`E[(Z)_r | H] = ((R)_r/(M)_r) E[(T)_r | H]`, `0<=r<=R`.

Thus the distribution of the observable count `Z` is independent of `H` **if and only if** the first `R` conditional factorial moments

`E[(T)_r | H]`, `r=1,...,R`,

are all independent of `H`.

When `R=M`, the deletion channel is injective and this becomes

`H independent of Z  <=>  H independent of T`.

When `R<M`, the channel has a genuine information kernel: dependence of `T` on `H` can be hidden only in factorial degrees strictly larger than `R`.

### Proof

Condition on `T=t`. Among the `t` left-labelled candidates, `Z` counts how many are contained in a uniform `R`-subset of the `M` candidates.

The falling factorial `(Z)_r` counts ordered `r`-tuples of distinct retained left-labelled candidates. There are `(t)_r` ordered `r`-tuples of distinct left-labelled candidates in total. Any fixed ordered `r`-tuple is retained with probability

`(R)_r/(M)_r`,

because a uniform `R`-subset contains a prescribed ordered set of `r` distinct candidates with exactly this probability. Summing the indicators of all `(t)_r` ordered tuples gives

`E[(Z)_r | T=t] = (t)_r (R)_r/(M)_r`.

Taking conditional expectation given `H` proves the second formula.

A probability distribution on `{0,...,R}` is uniquely determined by its factorial moments of orders `0,...,R`, because the functions

`1,(z)_1,...,(z)_R`

form a basis of all real functions on that `(R+1)`-point set. Therefore `Law(Z|H=h)` is constant in `h` iff all its factorial moments are constant in `h`, and by the nonzero scale factors above this is equivalent to constancy of `E[(T)_r|H]` for `r<=R`.

If `R=M`, factorial moments through order `M` determine the law of `T` on `{0,...,M}`, so the channel is injective. If `R<M`, only `R+1` output probabilities are available for an `(M+1)`-dimensional input law; the displayed moment representation shows exactly that the degrees `0,...,R` survive and higher-degree directions may lie in the kernel. QED.

## 5.3 Iid deletion is qualitatively different

If each candidate is independently retained with probability `q>0`, then

`Z | T=t ~ Binomial(t,q)`

and

`E[(Z)_r|T] = q^r (T)_r`, `0<=r<=M`.

All degrees survive, so the iid thinning map is injective on distributions of `T`. Equivalently its probability-generating function is

`E[z^Z|H] = E[(1-q+qz)^T|H]`.

Since the affine substitution `z -> 1-q+qz` is invertible for `q>0`, iid random deletion cannot make a genuinely `H`-dependent `T` independent of `H`.

This distinction matters: replacing the source's exact-size sample by iid sampling is safe for asymptotic approximation of a *fixed finite prefix*, but it can be qualitatively too informative when one studies an entire tail whose dimension grows with `n`.

## 5.4 Specialization to the source Theorem-2.6 geometry

Use the source's notation from the first SEC1OFF report. Condition on an immediate query response `r=k`: the top `k` candidates are unsampled and rank `k+1` is sampled. Among the `M=n-k-1` lower ranks below that threshold, exactly

`R = n-s-k`

remain unsampled.

For a nested-end order, let `T` be the total number of those `M` lower ranks inserted to the **left** of the top block. Let `Z` be the number of those left-side lower ranks that remain unsampled. Every such retained left-side item arrives before the top block, while retained right-side lower ranks arrive after it. Therefore `Z` is exactly the number of sub-threshold arrivals seen before the first above-threshold arrival.

Theorem 5.1 says that fixed-size source deletion transmits precisely the first `R=n-s-k` factorial moments of the hidden left-tail count `T`.

At a fixed sample fraction `p=s/n` and fixed `k`, `R` is of order `(1-p)n`; hence the deletion channel still exposes a **linear number of moment degrees**. # 7. B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE: exact projection of the non-Markov routing polytope

The previous report showed a global conservation law but left open how violently a non-Markov routing can move the leak from one local pair to another. Here that local freedom can be solved exactly at every scale.

Let `X_1,...,X_n in {0,1}` be any finite side-bit law such that every prefix count

`S_t := X_1+...+X_t`

is exactly uniform on `{0,...,t}` for `t=1,...,n`.

Define the last adjacent predictive contrast

`D_n := P(X_n=1|X_{n-1}=1)-P(X_n=1|X_{n-1}=0)`.

Every `X_j` is fair under the prefix-uniform conditions, so

`D_n = 4 P(X_{n-1}=1,X_n=1)-1`.

We determine the exact feasible interval of `D_n`.

## 7.1 Forced two-step aggregate flows

Fix an intermediate count `s in {1,...,n-1}` at time `n-1`.

There are two ways to enter `S_{n-1}=s` from time `n-2`:

- an upward edge from `S_{n-2}=s-1`, meaning `X_{n-1}=1`;
- a horizontal/right edge from `S_{n-2}=s`, meaning `X_{n-1}=0`.

Exact uniformity forces their aggregate masses to be

`A_s := P(S_{n-2}=s-1, X_{n-1}=1) = s/[n(n-1)]`,

`C_s := P(S_{n-2}=s, X_{n-1}=0) = (n-s-1)/[n(n-1)]`.

At the next step, exact uniformity forces the total mass leaving state `s` with `X_n=1` to be

`B_s := P(S_{n-1}=s, X_n=1) = (s+1)/[n(n+1)]`.

Let

`z_s := P(S_{n-2}=s-1, X_{n-1}=1, X_n=1)`.

The remaining `X_n=1` mass from state `s` is `B_s-z_s` and must come from the `X_{n-1}=0` incoming class. Therefore the exact feasibility interval is

`max(0,B_s-C_s) <= z_s <= min(A_s,B_s)`.

Substituting the displayed flows gives

`max(0,(2s+1-n)/(n^2-1)) <= z_s`

and

`z_s <= min( s/[n(n-1)], (s+1)/[n(n+1)] )`.

Moreover

`P(X_{n-1}=1,X_n=1) = sum_{s=1}^{n-1} z_s`.

## 7.2 Achievability is genuine, not merely a relaxation

The intervals above can be chosen independently across `s`.

To see this, start with any exact-uniform law through time `n-2` (the Pólya law is one example). Extend to time `n-1` using the forced aggregate edge masses. At state `s`, the two incoming edge classes have exactly masses `A_s` and `C_s`. Choose any admissible `z_s` in the interval above. Send a fraction `z_s/A_s` of the first class upward at time `n` and a fraction `(B_s-z_s)/C_s` of the second class upward, interpreting zero-mass boundary classes in the obvious way. Both fractions lie in `[0,1]` precisely because of the interval constraints.

These choices can be implemented by history-dependent continuation probabilities inside each incoming class. They preserve every earlier prefix distribution and give the required total upward flow `B_s`, hence exact uniformity at time `n`. Different `s` cells are disjoint, so their choices do not interfere.

Thus the following bounds are **exact feasible bounds**, not LP relaxations.

## 7.3 Exact adjacent-contrast interval

### Theorem 7.1

For every `n>=2`, the exact feasible interval of `D_n` under prefix-count uniformity is:

- if `n` is odd,

  `0 <= D_n <= (n-1)/n`;

- if `n` is even,

  `1/(n^2-1) <= D_n <= 1 - n/(n^2-1)`.

Every value in the corresponding interval is attainable by a history-dependent exact-uniform routing.

### Proof

Because the `z_s` cells are independent, the minimum and maximum of their sum are the sums of their individual minima and maxima.

For the lower endpoint,

`B_s-C_s = (2s+1-n)/(n^2-1)`.

If `n=2h+1` is odd, the positive terms occur for `s=h+1,...,2h` and sum to

`[2(1+...+h)] / [(2h+1)^2-1] = h(h+1)/[4h(h+1)] = 1/4`.

Hence the minimum of `D_n=4 sum z_s-1` is zero.

If `n=2h` is even, the positive terms occur for `s=h,...,2h-1`; their numerators are `1,3,...,2h-1`, summing to `h^2`. Thus

`min sum z_s = h^2/(4h^2-1)`

and

`min D_n = 4h^2/(4h^2-1)-1 = 1/(4h^2-1)=1/(n^2-1)`.

For the upper endpoint, compare

`A_s = s/[n(n-1)]`

with

`B_s=(s+1)/[n(n+1)]`.

One has `A_s<=B_s` exactly when `2s<=n-1`. Summing `A_s` below that switch and `B_s` above it gives, after elementary arithmetic,

`max D_n=(n-1)/n` for odd `n`,

and

`max D_n=1-n/(n^2-1)` for even `n`.

The achievability construction above realizes every independent choice of `z_s`; convex interpolation inside one or more cells realizes every intermediate value. QED.

## 7.4 Sporadic consequences

The first few exact intervals are

`n=2:  D_2 = 1/3`,

`n=3:  0 <= D_3 <= 2/3`,

`n=4:  1/15 <= D_4 <= 11/15`,

`n=5:  0 <= D_5 <= 4/5`,

`n=6:  1/35 <= D_6 <= 29/35`,

`n=7:  0 <= D_7 <= 6/7`.

So a striking parity phenomenon appears.

- At every **odd** terminal scale `n>=3`, one may route so that the final adjacent bits are exactly independent (`D_n=0`).
- At every **even** scale, exact uniformity forces a positive adjacent leak, but its sharp minimum is only `1/(n^2-1)`, which tends to zero.

At the same time, the maximum feasible adjacent contrast tends to one.

This is precisely the sort of sporadic routing freedom that the global conservation identity allows: total signed predictive memory remains fixed, but any chosen local pair can carry almost none or almost all of it as the scale grows.

It therefore blocks a tempting shortcut. One cannot obtain the source observability bridge by proving a scale-uniform positive lower bound on the **last adjacent** side-bit correlation. The required source statistic must aggregate information across many coordinates or across the deletion channel's surviving factorial moments.

**B26 outcome:** exact all-scale projection of the non-Markov routing polytope and a parity obstruction to local-leak arguments; no frozen-metric movement.
