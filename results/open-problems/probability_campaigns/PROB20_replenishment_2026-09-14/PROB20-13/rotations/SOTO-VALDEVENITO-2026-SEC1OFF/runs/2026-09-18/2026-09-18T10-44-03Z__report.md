## 4. B6 — DEFORM TO A SOLVABLE POINT: an erasure-query homotopy

Fix any finite hard law `mu` and any fixed iid distinguished-rank sampling density `p`. Replace the reliable one-query oracle by a `lambda`-erasure oracle: when the algorithm spends its one query, independently with probability `lambda` it receives the true ordinal count response, and with probability `1-lambda` it receives a special erasure symbol `⊥`; either outcome consumes the query. Let

`V_{mu,p}(lambda)`

be the optimal success probability in this modified finite game. This is only a research deformation; `lambda<1` is not the source model and cannot move the frozen metric.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact shape of the one-query deformation

For every finite hard law and fixed `p`, `V_{mu,p}(lambda)` on `[0,1]` is nondecreasing, convex, piecewise affine, and `1`-Lipschitz. Moreover

`V_{mu,p}(0) = V^{no-query}_{mu,p}`

and

`V_{mu,p}(1) = V^{one-query}_{mu,p}`.

The right derivative at zero exists and is the largest slope among deterministic contingent plans that are optimal at `lambda=0`.

### Proof

The finite information tree has finitely many deterministic contingent plans. Fix one such plan `pi`. Because there is at most one query, the only `lambda`-dependent random event is whether that single query, if used, is informative or erased. Conditioning on all ordinary history and on the plan's query time therefore gives

`Succ_pi(lambda) = a_pi + lambda b_pi`

for constants `a_pi,b_pi`. Since success indicators lie in `[0,1]`, `|b_pi|<=1`. Hence

`V_{mu,p}(lambda) = max_pi (a_pi + lambda b_pi)`

is a finite maximum of affine functions, so it is convex, piecewise affine and `1`-Lipschitz.

If `0<=lambda_1<=lambda_2`, the `lambda_2` experiment Blackwell-dominates the `lambda_1` experiment: whenever `lambda_2` returns a real response, independently erase it with probability `1-lambda_1/lambda_2` (and keep an existing erasure erased). This simulates exactly the `lambda_1` oracle. Thus the optimal value is nondecreasing.

At `lambda=0` the query always erases and supplies no information, so an optimal policy can simply never spend it; this is exactly the no-query game. At `lambda=1` one recovers the source-valid reliable one-query finite game. Finally, for a finite maximum of affine functions, the one-sided derivative at `0` is the maximum slope among affine pieces active at `0`. QED.

### Consequence

This produces a concrete analytic diagnostic, the **query susceptibility**

`chi(mu,p) := V'_{mu,p}(0+)`.

A finite-prefix family can approach `1/e` only if it simultaneously controls its no-query envelope and prevents the active policy slopes from becoming large as information is turned on. Combined with the previous B18 value-of-information lemma, this says that the key asymptotic object is not merely the endpoint `V(1)`, but the whole convex information-response curve `lambda -> V(lambda)`.

Outcome B6: **useful exact deformation, no source-metric movement**. It identifies a quantity that can be attacked symbolically at `lambda=0` before returning to the fully reliable oracle.

## 5. B9 — RANDOMIZE: independent refresh cannot preserve uniform peak positions

The most obvious way to destroy the Pólya leakage is to refresh the side decisions: take the insertion bits `X_2,...,X_m` independent, perhaps with rank-dependent biases `q_2,...,q_m`. Then observations about one scale do not create the same de Finetti-style latent parameter shared by all scales.

Unfortunately this destroys the exact anti-stopping symmetry in the strongest possible algebraic sense.

### Proposition 5.1 — no independent side-bit law has an exactly uniform peak for `m>=3`

Let `X_2,...,X_m` be independent Bernoulli variables with arbitrary parameters `q_j in [0,1]`, and let

`K_m = 1 + sum_{j=2}^m X_j`.

For every `m>=3`, `K_m` cannot be uniform on `{1,...,m}`.

### Proof

Put `S=sum_{j=2}^m X_j`. Independence gives the probability generating polynomial

`E[z^S] = product_{j=2}^m ((1-q_j)+q_j z)`.

If `S` were uniform on `{0,...,m-1}`, then

`E[z^S] = (1+z+...+z^(m-1))/m`.

The right-hand polynomial has as roots all nontrivial `m`-th roots of unity, hence for `m>=3` it has nonreal roots. Every nonconstant factor on the left has its root at `-(1-q_j)/q_j`, a nonpositive real number. Parameters `q_j=0` reduce the degree and parameters `q_j=1` force a zero root while the uniform polynomial has positive constant term. Therefore the two polynomials cannot agree. QED.

This is stronger than the earlier observation that the exchangeable exact-uniform construction is uniquely Pólya: **even fully inhomogeneous independent refresh is impossible** if exact uniform peak position is retained.

### Failure mode of block refresh

A block-refresh process, in which distant rank blocks use independent latent parameters, does remove long-range dependence between blocks. But within each finite block the same conflict remains, and if one refreshes all the way to independent bits then the peak position becomes a Poisson-binomial law rather than uniform. Thus the simple B9 idea cannot simultaneously achieve both goals:

1. erase long-range query leakage by independent refresh;
2. keep the exact uniform peak-position symmetry used by the source hard argument.

Outcome B9: **negative but decisive**. The next hard-law family must use dependence; independence is not merely numerically suboptimal but algebraically incompatible with exact uniform peak locations.

## 6. B29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: Cantor-space boundary and a forced Pólya flow

The nested-end family has a natural compact boundary. Reindex the side bits as `X_1,X_2,...`, where after `n` insertions

`S_n = X_1+...+X_n`

and the rank-1 position is `S_n+1` among the `n+1` distinguished ranks.

A law on length-`n` nested-end orders is exactly a law on `{0,1}^n`. A projectively consistent family of such laws is therefore exactly the family of finite-dimensional marginals of a Borel probability measure on Cantor space `{0,1}^N` by the Kolmogorov extension theorem.

The anti-stopping condition “the maximum has a uniform position at every scale” becomes

`P(S_n=k)=1/(n+1),  k=0,...,n`.

This condition has an exact conservation law that does **not** assume exchangeability or Markovianity.

Define

`a_{n,k} := P(S_n=k)`

and the upward edge flow

`e_{n,k} := P(S_n=k, X_{n+1}=1)`.

Mass conservation on the Pascal graph gives

`a_{n+1,k} = a_{n,k} - e_{n,k} + e_{n,k-1}`

with `e_{n,-1}=0`. Telescoping yields the exact identity

`e_{n,k} = sum_{j=0}^k (a_{n,j}-a_{n+1,j}).`

### Proposition 6.1 — exact uniform peaks force the Pólya one-step flow

If `S_n` is uniform on `{0,...,n}` and `S_{n+1}` is uniform on `{0,...,n+1}`, then for every `k=0,...,n`,

`e_{n,k} = (k+1)/((n+1)(n+2))`

and therefore

`P(X_{n+1}=1 | S_n=k) = (k+1)/(n+2)`.

### Proof

Substitute `a_{n,j}=1/(n+1)` and `a_{n+1,j}=1/(n+2)` into the telescoping identity:

`e_{n,k} = (k+1)(1/(n+1)-1/(n+2))`

`          = (k+1)/((n+1)(n+2)).`

Divide by `a_{n,k}=1/(n+1)`. QED.

Thus the Pólya reinforcement rule is not merely one convenient exchangeable construction. It is the **forced one-step Markov projection of every projective nested-end law having exact uniform peak positions at consecutive scales**. Non-Markov hard laws still have freedom inside the collection of histories reaching the same Pascal vertex, but they cannot alter the aggregate flow through that vertex.

### Proposition 6.2 — stability under approximately uniform peak positions

Let `u_n` be the uniform law on `{0,...,n}` and set

`delta_n := TV(Law(S_n),u_n)`.

Let

`e^*_{n,k}:=(k+1)/((n+1)(n+2))`

be the Pólya edge flow. Then for every `k`,

`|e_{n,k}-e^*_{n,k}| <= delta_n + delta_{n+1}`.

### Proof

The telescoping identity can be written as a difference of cumulative distribution functions:

`e_{n,k}=P(S_n<=k)-P(S_{n+1}<=k)`.

The same identity for the two uniform laws gives `e^*_{n,k}`. Total variation bounds the probability error of every event, in particular every prefix event `{0,...,k}`. Therefore

`|e_{n,k}-e^*_{n,k}|`

`<= |P(S_n<=k)-u_n({0,...,k})|`

`   + |P(S_{n+1}<=k)-u_{n+1}({0,...,k})|`

`<= delta_n+delta_{n+1}`. QED.

This is the central structural result of the run. It quantifies the tradeoff that the finite certificates have been discovering numerically: **if a hard law keeps peak positions close to uniform at two adjacent scales, then its aggregate side-flow is automatically close to the leaking Pólya flow.** A successful asymptotic construction must therefore exploit history-dependent routing *within* each Pascal layer, or deliberately accept enough nonuniformity to break this flow.

### Proposition 6.3 — the Pólya law is the maximum-entropy exact-uniform law at each finite scale

Fix `n` and consider all laws on `{0,1}^n` satisfying `S_n ~ Unif{0,...,n}`. The unique entropy maximizer is the finite Pólya law

`P(X_1=x_1,...,X_n=x_n) = k!(n-k)!/(n+1)!`

for every string with `k` ones.

### Proof

For any such law,

`H(X_1,...,X_n)=H(S_n)+H(X_1,...,X_n | S_n)`.

The first term is fixed at `log(n+1)`. Conditional on `S_n=k`, the path lies in a set of size `binom(n,k)`, so conditional entropy is at most `log binom(n,k)`, with equality iff the conditional law is uniform on that layer. Averaging over the uniform `S_n` shows that the unique maximizer is uniform inside every layer. Each string with `k` ones therefore has probability

`1/((n+1) binom(n,k)) = k!(n-k)!/(n+1)!`,

which is exactly the finite Pólya law. QED.

This does not prove that Pólya minimizes query leakage for the source observation channel; entropy and Blackwell informativeness are different orderings. It does show that the source hard law already occupies the least-structured point under the natural entropy criterion, while still leaking enough to fail in the unrestricted model.
