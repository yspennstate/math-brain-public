## 3. New mathematical idea before code: an exact weighted tail-neighbor inequality

The previous recovery proposed porting Musin's modified-Delsarte architecture. Such a proof permits an auxiliary positive-definite kernel to be positive on a controlled interval near inner product `-1`, then bounds the total positive contribution around each row geometrically.

The first question is therefore: **what exact geometry is forced on points lying deep in the antipodal cap of one code point?**

Fix a kissing code `C subset S^4`, a point `x in C`, and distinct points `y_1,...,y_m in C\{x}` satisfying

\[
\langle x,y_i\rangle=-s_i.
\]

For an antipodal tail one has `s_i>0`. Define the tangential projections

\[
p_i:=y_i+s_i x\in x^\perp.
\]

Then

\[
\|p_i\|^2=1-s_i^2
\]

and, because every pair of code points has inner product at most `1/2`,

\[
p_i\cdot p_j
= y_i\cdot y_j-s_i s_j
\le \frac12-s_i s_j.
\]

This yields the following exact inequality.

### Theorem 3.1 (weighted antipodal-tail inequality)

For every choice of nonnegative real weights `w_1,...,w_m`,

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i s_i\right)^2
\le
\frac12\left[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^m w_i\right)^2
+\sum_{i=1}^m w_i^2
\right].
}
\]

#### Proof

Since `p_i in x^perp`, positivity of squared norm and the pairwise bound above give

\[
0\le \left\|\sum_i w_i p_i\right\|^2
\le
\sum_i w_i^2(1-s_i^2)
+2\sum_{i<j}w_iw_j\left(\frac12-s_i s_j\right).
\]

The right side simplifies exactly to

\[
\frac12\left[
\left(\sum_iw_i\right)^2+\sum_iw_i^2
\right]
-\left(\sum_iw_i s_i\right)^2.
\]

Rearranging proves the claim. No numerical input is used. `square`

### Corollary 3.2 (all subset/prefix inequalities)

Taking `w_i` to be indicators of a subset `I` gives

\[
\boxed{
\left(\sum_{i\in I}s_i\right)^2
\le \frac{|I|(|I|+1)}2.
}
\]

After sorting `s_1>=s_2>=...>=s_m`, it is enough to retain the prefix constraints

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^k s_i\le\sqrt{\frac{k(k+1)}2},
\qquad 1\le k\le m.
}
\]

These are exact necessary conditions on the radial data of every antipodal tail around every point of every five-dimensional kissing code.

### Corollary 3.3 (exact tail multiplicity cutoff)

If every tail point satisfies `s_i>=a` with `a>1/sqrt(2)`, then

\[
ma\le \sqrt{\frac{m(m+1)}2}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
 m(2a^2-1)\le1,
\qquad
m\le\left\lfloor\frac1{2a^2-1}\right\rfloor.
}
\]

In particular,

\[
a>\sqrt{\frac23}\implies m\le2,
\qquad
a>\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\implies m\le1.
\]

Thus a sufficiently narrow positive tail in a Musin-style kernel reduces the local row problem to at most two tail points, with exact scalar constraints.

I do **not** claim novelty for this cap-geometry inequality. It is a direct Gram-positivity consequence and sits close to existing spherical-cap methods. Its value here is that it turns the proposed dimension-five local-correction search into an explicit finite scalar optimization rather than an unspecified geometric subproblem.

## 4. B5 — ADD A PARAMETER: exact finite row-reduction for a Musin-style kernel

Let

\[
f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^d f_kG_k^{(5)}(t),
\qquad f_0>0,\quad f_k\ge0,
\]

where `G_k^(5)(1)=1`. Suppose for some `a>1/sqrt(2)` that

\[
f(t)\le0\qquad(-a\le t\le1/2).
\]

Then the only positive off-diagonal row terms can occur for `t=-s` with `s>=a`. The exact tail theorem above bounds their multiplicity by

\[
M(a)=\left\lfloor\frac1{2a^2-1}\right\rfloor
\]

and bounds every sorted radial vector by

\[
a\le s_m\le\cdots\le s_1\le1,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k s_i\le\sqrt{\frac{k(k+1)}2}.
\]

Define the scalar relaxation

\[
B_m(f,a):=
\max\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^m \max(f(-s_i),0):
\begin{array}{l}
1\ge s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_m\ge a,\\
\sum_{i=1}^k s_i\le\sqrt{k(k+1)/2}\quad(1\le k\le m)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

Every genuine row is feasible for this scalar relaxation. Therefore positive definiteness gives the rigorous implication

\[
\boxed{
|C|\le
\frac{f(1)+\max_{0\le m\le M(a)}B_m(f,a)}{f_0},
}
\]

where `B_0=0`.

This is the concrete B5 reduction generated by the recovery idea. It is weaker than exact cap feasibility because it forgets angular compatibility beyond the weighted Gram inequalities, but it is source-global: it applies around every point of every kissing code, not merely around a known 40-point configuration.

For `a>sqrt(2/3)` the exact tail theorem leaves only one- and two-point tail rows, so those row constraints can be sampled directly. A crucial correction is to include the empty-tail row `H>=f(1)`; omitting it creates a false apparent improvement.

## 5. B21 — adjacent literature: this failure mode is already structurally visible

The nearest relevant literature substantially sharpens what should and should not be attempted next.

### Pfender

Florian Pfender, *Improved Delsarte bounds for spherical codes in small dimensions* (arXiv:math/0501493; JCTA 2007), explicitly enlarges the classical Delsarte function space and explains that Musin's three- and four-dimensional arguments fit the same general framework. His angle-specialized functions produce kissing-number improvements in dimensions 10, 16, 17, 25 and 26, and spherical-code improvements in dimensions 3, 4 and 5, but he states that the other then-known kissing-number bounds up to dimension 30 are not improved by this construction. In particular, dimension 5 is not among the kissing-number improvements.

This is important negative prior art: simply replacing the classical Gegenbauer cone by the most obvious diagonal-dominance/local-correction extension is already known not to solve the five-dimensional endpoint.

### Barg--Musin spherical caps

Alexander Barg and Oleg Musin, *Codes in spherical caps* (Advances in Mathematics of Communications 1 (2007), 131--149), develop cap-code bounds and an extended Delsarte method that relates spherical-code bounds to codes in caps, with applications to kissing and one-sided kissing numbers.

That is precisely the literature family containing the local geometry in Theorem 3.1. Consequently the weighted-tail inequality above must be treated as route infrastructure unless a careful prior-art comparison establishes a genuinely new strengthening.

### Musin's solved four-dimensional analogue

Musin's proof `tau_4=24` succeeds because the modified Delsarte function is paired with sufficiently strong **dimension-specific local geometry** to control the positive rows. The present dimension-five scalar tail reduction is too weak numerically: it does not reproduce the decisive correction.

**B21 outcome:** the literature says the next useful route should not be another generic two-point/cap function. It should inject information that is genuinely stronger than the classical/Pfender local-correction cone, most naturally three-point information or rank-five compatibility.
