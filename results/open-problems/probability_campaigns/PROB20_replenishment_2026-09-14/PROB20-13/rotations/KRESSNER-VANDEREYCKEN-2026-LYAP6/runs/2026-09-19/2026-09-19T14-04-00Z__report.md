# 3. B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE

The previous run proved the entire locus commuting with an orthogonal complex structure to be safe. The natural question is whether a small transverse perturbation immediately creates a positive skew-minus-symmetric gap. There is an exact parity obstruction to such a first-order instability.

## Proposition 1 — transverse first variation vanishes on simple parity branches

Fix an orthogonal complex structure `J` on `R^6`, and define

\[
\Gamma(X)=JXJ^T.
\]

Let `A_0` commute with `J`, and let `E` anticommute with `J`:

\[
A_0J=JA_0,
\qquad
EJ=-JE.
\]

Set `A(t)=A_0+tE` and

\[
H(t)=\mathcal L_{A(t)}^*\mathcal L_{A(t)}.
\]

Then

\[
\dot H(0)=\mathcal L_{A_0}^*\mathcal L_E+
\mathcal L_E^*\mathcal L_{A_0}
\]

**anticommutes** with `Gamma`.

Consequently, if `X` is any unit eigenvector of `H(0)` lying in a definite `Gamma` parity sector, `Gamma X=epsilon X`, then

\[
\boxed{\langle X,\dot H(0)X\rangle_F=0.}
\]

In particular, every simple eigenvalue branch of either restricted operator `H(t)|S_6` or `H(t)|K_6` has zero first derivative at `t=0` in a `J`-breaking direction. If the active top symmetric and skew branches are both simple, the gap itself satisfies

\[
\boxed{\gamma'(0)=0.}
\]

### Proof

Since `A_0` and `A_0^T` commute with `J`, direct substitution gives

\[
\Gamma\mathcal L_{A_0}=\mathcal L_{A_0}\Gamma.
\]

Since `E` and `E^T` anticommute with `J`, the same calculation gives

\[
\Gamma\mathcal L_E=-\mathcal L_E\Gamma.
\]

The adjoints have the same respective parity. Therefore each summand in `dot H(0)` is the product of one commuting and one anticommuting operator, so

\[
\Gamma\dot H(0)=-\dot H(0)\Gamma.
\]

If `Gamma X=epsilon X`, then `dot H(0)X` has parity `-epsilon`. The two parity spaces are orthogonal because `Gamma` is an orthogonal self-adjoint involution. Hence

\[
\langle X,\dot H(0)X\rangle_F=0.
\]

For a simple eigenvalue, the standard symmetric-eigenvalue derivative is exactly this Rayleigh derivative. QED.

### What B15 did and did not accomplish

This explains why the already-safe complex-linear locus can be a sticky numerical ridge: a simple active branch cannot become skew-dominant to first order under a purely `J`-breaking perturbation. The first potentially decisive term is second order.

The proposition does **not** settle perturbations through a degenerate top eigenspace. In that case `dot H(0)` has zero diagonal on each parity sector but can have nonzero off-diagonal compression between opposite parities, so a linear eigenvalue splitting is still possible. That is the exact B15 obstruction rather than a reason to run more random restarts.

# 5. B27 — SWAP THE QUANTIFIERS

This method gives the strongest new theorem in the run. Instead of maximizing first, fix a skew input and construct a symmetric competitor.

## Theorem 3 — pointwise symmetric competitor for every skew input of rank at most four

Let `A in R^{n x n}` be arbitrary, and let `U` be a real skew-symmetric matrix with

\[
\operatorname{rank}U\le4.
\]

Then there exists a real symmetric matrix `V` such that

\[
\boxed{\|V\|_F=\|U\|_F}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal L_A(V)\|_F\ge\|\mathcal L_A(U)\|_F.
}
\]

This statement is pointwise in both `A` and `U`; no normality, stability, reducibility, or complex-structure assumption on `A` is used.

### Step 1 — energy identities

For symmetric `V`, direct expansion gives

\[
\|\mathcal L_A(V)\|_F^2
=2\operatorname{tr}(A^TA V^2)+2\operatorname{tr}(AVAV).
\]

For skew `U`, because `U^T=-U`,

\[
\|\mathcal L_A(U)\|_F^2
=2\operatorname{tr}(A^TA(-U^2))-2\operatorname{tr}(AUAU).
\]

Therefore, whenever a symmetric `V` satisfies

\[
V^2=-U^2,
\]

we have the exact cancellation

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal L_A(V)\|_F^2-\|\mathcal L_A(U)\|_F^2
=2\{\operatorname{tr}(AVAV)+\operatorname{tr}(AUAU)\}.
}
\]

The same square identity also implies `||V||_F=||U||_F`.

### Step 2 — canonicalize the rank-four skew input

By orthogonal similarity, a skew matrix of rank at most four can be written

\[
U=\operatorname{diag}(aJ,bJ,0),
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

with `a,b>=0`; rank two is the case `b=0`.

Let `P,Q` be symmetric orthogonal `2 x 2` reflections and set

\[
V=\operatorname{diag}(aP,bQ,0).
\]

Then `V^2=-U^2` automatically. It remains to choose `P,Q` so that the remaining trace bracket is nonnegative.

Only the two active `2 x 2` coordinate blocks of `A` contribute. Write them as `A_11,A_12,A_21,A_22`. Expanding the trace bracket gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{tr}(AVAV)+\operatorname{tr}(AUAU)\\
={}&a^2\{\operatorname{tr}(A_{11}PA_{11}P)+\operatorname{tr}(A_{11}JA_{11}J)\}\\
&+b^2\{\operatorname{tr}(A_{22}QA_{22}Q)+\operatorname{tr}(A_{22}JA_{22}J)\}\\
&+2ab\{\operatorname{tr}(A_{12}QA_{21}P)+\operatorname{tr}(A_{12}JA_{21}J)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

We now control the diagonal and cross brackets separately.

### Step 3 — diagonal `2 x 2` lemma

For every real `2 x 2` matrix `B` and every reflection `P`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(BPBP)+\operatorname{tr}(BJBJ)\ge0.
}
\]

Indeed every reflection is `P=RKR^T` for a planar rotation `R`, where

\[
K=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),
\qquad R^TJR=J.
\]

Replacing `B` by `R^TBR`, it is enough to take `P=K`. If

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}b_{11}&b_{12}\\b_{21}&b_{22}\end{pmatrix},
\]

then direct multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(BKBK)+\operatorname{tr}(BJBJ)
=(b_{11}-b_{22})^2+(b_{12}-b_{21})^2\ge0.
}
\]

Thus both diagonal terms above are nonnegative for every choice of the two reflections.

### Step 4 — cross `2 x 2` lemma

For any real `2 x 2` matrices `X,Y`, there exist reflections `P,Q` such that

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(XQYP)\ge
\left|\operatorname{tr}(XJYJ)\right|.
}
\]

To prove this, identify `R^2` with `C`. Every real-linear `2 x 2` map has a unique form

\[
X:z\mapsto \alpha z+\beta\bar z,
\qquad
Y:z\mapsto \gamma z+\delta\bar z.
\]

Every reflection has the form

\[
P_p:z\mapsto p\bar z,
\qquad |p|=1,
\]

and similarly `Q_q`, `|q|=1`. A direct composition gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb R}(XQ_qYP_p)
=2\Re\!\left[
\bar p\left(\alpha\bar\gamma q+\beta\delta\bar q\right)
\right].
\]

Choose `q` so that the two complex numbers inside the parentheses have the same phase, and then choose `p` to make their sum real and nonnegative. Therefore

\[
\max_{P,Q}\operatorname{tr}(XQYP)
=2\bigl(|\alpha||\gamma|+|\beta||\delta|\bigr).
\]

On the other hand, with our orientation `J` is multiplication by `-i`, and

\[
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb R}(XJYJ)
=2\Re(-\alpha\gamma+\beta\bar\delta).
\]

Hence by the triangle inequality

\[
|\operatorname{tr}(XJYJ)|
\le2\bigl(|\alpha||\gamma|+|\beta||\delta|\bigr),
\]

which proves the cross lemma.

### Step 5 — conclude the pointwise theorem

Apply the cross lemma to `X=A_12` and `Y=A_21`, choosing `P,Q` so that

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A_{12}QA_{21}P)+
\operatorname{tr}(A_{12}JA_{21}J)\ge0.
\]

The two diagonal brackets are already nonnegative. Since `a,b>=0`, the entire trace bracket is nonnegative. The energy identity from Step 1 now gives

\[
\|\mathcal L_A(V)\|_F^2\ge\|\mathcal L_A(U)\|_F^2.
\]

Together with `||V||_F=||U||_F`, this proves Theorem 3. QED.

## Corollary 4 — a hypothetical order-six counterexample has a unique full-rank skew maximizer

Suppose, contrary to the desired source inequality, that for some real `6 x 6` matrix `A`,

\[
\|\mathcal L_A|K_6\|>\|\mathcal L_A|S_6\|.
\]

Then **every** unit top skew eigenmatrix `U` of `H_A|K_6` must have rank six. Otherwise Theorem 3 supplies a unit symmetric competitor with output norm at least the skew optimum, a contradiction.

There is a further consequence. Let `E_max` be the top eigenspace of `H_A|K_6`. If `dim E_max>=2`, consider the Pfaffian on `6 x 6` skew matrices. It is a homogeneous cubic polynomial, so on the unit sphere of `E_max` it is continuous and odd:

\[
\operatorname{pf}(-U)=-\operatorname{pf}(U).
\]

The unit sphere of a real vector space of dimension at least two is connected. Hence either the Pfaffian is already zero somewhere or, along a path from `U` to `-U`, it must cross zero. Thus `E_max` contains a nonzero singular skew matrix, necessarily of rank at most four. That contradicts the preceding paragraph.

Therefore any genuine order-six counterexample must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}U=6
\quad\text{and}\quad
\dim E_{\max}(H_A|K_6)=1.
}
\]

So the **skew** top eigenvalue is automatically simple at a counterexample. This removes one of the degeneracies left open by the B24 stationarity analysis. The symmetric top eigenvalue may still be multiple.

## Why B27 is not closure

The only unresolved skew inputs in dimension six are now full-rank skew matrices, whose canonical form contains three nonzero `2 x 2` skew blocks. The rank-four proof succeeds because there is only one cross interaction between two active blocks, and two reflection phases can always make that single cross term favorable. With three active blocks there are three coupled cross interactions but only three reflection phases modulo an overall redundancy; compatibility of the three pairwise phase choices becomes the new exact obstruction. The present report does not prove that all three can be satisfied simultaneously and does not construct a counterexample where they cannot.

This is therefore substantial structural progress but **not** movement of the frozen source metric.
