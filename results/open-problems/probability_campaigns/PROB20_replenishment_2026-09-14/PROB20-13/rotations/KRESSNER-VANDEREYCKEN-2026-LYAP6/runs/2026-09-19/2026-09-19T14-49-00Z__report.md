## 4. What a complete proof must contain that no current route contains

A positive proof must resolve the simultaneous three-block interaction, not merely each pair separately. In the reflection-competitor language, one obtains three phase/reflection variables and three pairwise cross contributions. For two active blocks, one relative phase can be chosen optimally. With three active blocks, the pairwise phase differences are constrained around a cycle; pairwise optima cannot simply be selected independently.

Therefore a successful positive route needs at least one of the following, proved globally rather than numerically:

1. a **global compatibility theorem** showing that the three pairwise cross terms coming from one real matrix `A` satisfy extra algebraic constraints that force a simultaneous nonnegative choice;
2. a **larger symmetric competitor family** that escapes any phase frustration of block-diagonal reflections while retaining the exact norm/energy comparison; or
3. a representation/quotient argument that turns the full-rank case into a finite family of canonical inequalities with a complete proof on every stratum.

Conversely, a negative route must do much more than display phase frustration for the restricted competitor family. It must produce an exact matrix `A` for which the offending full-rank `U` is actually top on `K_6`, and it must certify that every symmetric input lies strictly below it. The published order-seven rational-separator/positive-definiteness strategy gives the correct certification standard once such an `A` is found.

The current work contains neither the required global compatibility theorem nor such an exact counterexample.
