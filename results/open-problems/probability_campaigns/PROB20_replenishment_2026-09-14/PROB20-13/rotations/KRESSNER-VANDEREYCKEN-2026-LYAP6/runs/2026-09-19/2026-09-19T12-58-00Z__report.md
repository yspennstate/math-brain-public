# 3. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

The tensor language produces one useful failure diagnosis and one genuinely nontrivial positive stratum theorem.

## 3.1 First tensor moment: an exact identity that is too weak

Identify matrix space with `V tensor V`, `V=R^n`, so

\[
\mathcal L_A=A\otimes I+I\otimes A.
\]

Hence

\[
H_A=\mathcal L_A^*\mathcal L_A
=A^TA\otimes I+A^T\otimes A+A\otimes A^T+I\otimes A^TA.
\]

Let `F(x tensor y)=y tensor x` be the flip. Since `H_A` commutes with `F`, the restrictions to `Sym^2(V)` and `Lambda^2(V)` are obtained by `P_+=(I+F)/2` and `P_-=(I-F)/2`. Using

\[
\operatorname{tr}(F(B\otimes C))=\operatorname{tr}(BC),
\]

one gets

\[
\operatorname{tr}H_A=2n\|A\|_F^2+2(\operatorname{tr}A)^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(F H_A)=4\|A\|_F^2.
\]

Therefore, in every dimension,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(H_A|_{S_n})=(n+2)\|A\|_F^2+(\operatorname{tr}A)^2,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(H_A|_{K_n})=(n-2)\|A\|_F^2+(\operatorname{tr}A)^2.
}
\]

In particular the symmetric restriction always has exactly `4||A||_F^2` more total squared singular-value mass. This prospective invariant **does not** imply the desired top-eigenvalue comparison: it also holds for the published order-seven counterexample, where the skew top eigenvalue is nevertheless larger. So trace domination is a theorem but a failed closure route. Higher moments, not the first moment, would have to carry any such argument.

## 3.2 A dimension-six complex-structure theorem

The useful representation-theoretic phenomenon is specific to real dimension six because an orthogonal complex structure turns the problem into complex dimension three.

### Theorem 1 — orthogonal-complex-structure stratum

Let `A in R^{6 x 6}`. Suppose there exists an orthogonal complex structure `J`, meaning

\[
J^T=-J,\qquad J^2=-I,
\]

such that

\[
AJ=JA.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal L_A|_{K_6}\|\le \|\mathcal L_A|_{S_6}\|.
}
\]

Thus no order-six counterexample can commute with an orthogonal complex structure.

### Proof

Define the orthogonal involution

\[
\Gamma_J(X)=JXJ^T.
\]

Because `A` and `A^T` commute with `J`, `Gamma_J` commutes with `L_A`; it also commutes with transpose. Hence both `S_6` and `K_6` split orthogonally into the `+1` and `-1` eigenspaces of `Gamma_J`:

\[
S_6=S_+\oplus S_-,\qquad K_6=K_+\oplus K_-.
\]

The `+` spaces consist of matrices commuting with `J`; the `-` spaces consist of matrices anticommuting with `J`.

Choose an orthonormal basis identifying `(R^6,J)` with `C^3`. Since `AJ=JA`, `A` is the realification of a complex `3 x 3` matrix `B`.

### Commuting sector

A real matrix commuting with `J` is the realification of a complex-linear matrix `Z`. Real transpose corresponds to conjugate transpose, so

- `S_+` corresponds to complex Hermitian `Z=Z^*`;
- `K_+` corresponds to complex skew-Hermitian `Z=-Z^*`.

On this sector,

\[
\mathcal L_A \longleftrightarrow T_B(Z):=BZ+ZB^*.
\]

Multiplication by `i` is an isometry from Hermitian to skew-Hermitian matrices and intertwines `T_B`:

\[
T_B(iZ)=iT_B(Z).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal L_A|_{S_+}\|=\|\mathcal L_A|_{K_+}\|.
}
\]

### Anticommuting sector

A real matrix anticommuting with `J` is a complex-antilinear map

\[
z\mapsto C\bar z,
\qquad C\in\mathbb C^{3\times3}.
\]

Its real transpose corresponds to `C^T`. Consequently

- `S_-` corresponds to complex symmetric matrices `C^T=C`;
- `K_-` corresponds to complex skew-symmetric matrices `C^T=-C`.

On this sector,

\[
\mathcal L_A\longleftrightarrow M_B(C):=BC+CB^T.
\]

It remains to prove

\[
\|M_B|_{\mathrm{Skew}_3(\mathbb C)}\|
\le
\|M_B|_{\mathrm{Sym}_3(\mathbb C)}\|.
\]

Take an arbitrary nonzero complex skew-symmetric `C`. By the unitary congruence canonical form for complex skew-symmetric matrices, because the size is three there is a unitary `U` and `sigma>0` such that, after replacing `B` by `U^*BU` and `C` by `U^*C\bar U`, one has

\[
C=\sigma K_0,
\qquad
K_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
-1&0&0\\
0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Unitary congruence preserves Frobenius norms and the symmetric/skew conditions, so this loses nothing. Write

\[
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
a&b&p\\c&d&q\\r&s&t\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Choose the symmetric competitor

\[
D_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0).
\]

The two inputs have the same Frobenius norm, `||K_0||_F=||D_0||_F=sqrt(2)`. Direct multiplication gives

\[
M_B(K_0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&a+d&s\\
-(a+d)&0&-r\\
-s&r&0
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
M_B(D_0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2a&b+c&r\\
b+c&2d&s\\
r&s&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Therefore the output-energy difference is the exact sum of squares

\[
\boxed{
\|M_B(D_0)\|_F^2-\|M_B(K_0)\|_F^2
=2|a-d|^2+2|b+c|^2\ge0.
}
\]

Scaling by `sigma` proves that every skew input in the anticommuting sector has a symmetric input of the same norm whose output norm is at least as large. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\|\mathcal L_A|_{K_-}\|\le\|\mathcal L_A|_{S_-}\|.
}
\]

Combining the two orthogonal invariant sectors,

\[
\|\mathcal L_A|_{K_6}\|
=\max\{\|L_A|_{K_+}\|,\|L_A|_{K_-}\|\}
\le
\max\{\|L_A|_{S_+}\|,\|L_A|_{S_-}\|\}
=\|\mathcal L_A|_{S_6}\|.
\]

This proves the theorem. QED.

### Interpretation

This is not merely the normal-matrix case. `B` above is an arbitrary complex `3 x 3` matrix, hence may be highly nonnormal. The resolved real stratum consists of every `6 x 6` real matrix that is complex-linear for some orthogonal complex structure. In a compatible real basis it is the realification of an arbitrary complex `3 x 3` matrix.

For real-Schur forms with three complex `2 x 2` modes, this settles the full **complex-linear** sub-stratum, i.e. the forms whose `2 x 2` blocks and inter-block couplings respect one common orthogonal complex structure. It does **not** settle the whole three-`2 x 2`-block Schur stratum, because a general real Schur coupling need not commute with such a `J`.

The dimension-six mechanism is transparent: the difficult anticommuting sector becomes a complex `3 x 3` symmetric-versus-skew problem, and a complex `3 x 3` skew matrix has only one `2 x 2` canonical skew block. This exact reduction is unavailable in the same form for the odd-dimensional order-seven witness.

# 4. B24 — FIND THE EXTREMAL OBJECT

Normalize `||A||_F=1` and write

\[
\alpha(A)=\|L_A|_{K_6}\|^2,
\qquad
\beta(A)=\|L_A|_{S_6}\|^2,
\qquad
\gamma(A)=\alpha(A)-\beta(A).
\]

The Frobenius unit sphere is compact and `gamma` is continuous, so a global maximizer exists. If the source inequality is false, some global maximizer has `gamma>0`.

The useful output of B24 is an exact first-order equation for every **simple-spectrum** positive stationary point.

### Proposition 2 — stationarity equation at a nondegenerate positive maximizer

Assume `A` is Frobenius-normalized, the top eigenvalue of `H_A|K_6` is simple with unit eigenmatrix `U in K_6`, and the top eigenvalue of `H_A|S_6` is simple with unit eigenmatrix `V in S_6`. Put

\[
Y=L_A(U),\qquad Z=L_A(V).
\]

If `A` is stationary for `gamma` on the Frobenius sphere, then

\[
\boxed{
Y U+Z V=-\frac{\gamma(A)}2 A.
}
\]

### Proof

For fixed unit `X`, with `W=L_A(X)`,

\[
d\,\|L_A(X)\|_F^2[E]
=2\langle W,EX+XE^T\rangle.
\]

The Euclidean gradient with respect to `A` is

\[
2(WX^T+W^TX).
\]

For skew `U`, both `U` and `Y` are skew, so

\[
\nabla\alpha(A)=-4YU.
\]

For symmetric `V`, both `V` and `Z` are symmetric, so

\[
\nabla\beta(A)=4ZV.
\]

Simplicity of the two top eigenvalues justifies the usual envelope derivative. Hence

\[
\nabla\gamma(A)=-4(YU+ZV).
\]

At a constrained stationary point, `nabla gamma=lambda A`. Since `gamma` is homogeneous of degree two,

\[
\langle\nabla\gamma(A),A\rangle=2\gamma(A),
\]

and `||A||_F=1`, so `lambda=2gamma(A)`. Substitution gives the displayed equation. QED.

The symmetric and skew parts can equivalently be written

\[
\operatorname{sym}A
=-\frac1{\gamma}
\bigl(YU+UY+ZV+VZ\bigr),
\]

\[
\operatorname{skew}A
=-\frac1{\gamma}
\bigl(YU-UY+ZV-VZ\bigr).
\]

This is a genuine necessary condition, not yet a contradiction. The unresolved B24 obstruction is the multiple-top-eigenvalue stratum: there the gap is nonsmooth and one needs a correct subdifferential/stratified version rather than pretending the simple formula still applies.

Theorem 1 already removes an entire stationary family from consideration: a positive maximizer cannot commute with any orthogonal complex structure.

# 5. B30 — ONE SCHUR TYPE / REDUCING STRUCTURE AT A TIME

The cleanest complete dimension-six stratum is not a fixed number of Schur `2 x 2` blocks; it is the entire orthogonally **reducible** locus. This has an exact direct-sum formula.

### Theorem 3 — every orthogonally reducible order-six matrix satisfies the conjecture

Suppose `A in R^{6 x 6}` has a nontrivial reducing subspace, equivalently there is an orthogonal `Q` such that

\[
Q^TAQ=B\oplus C
\]

with `1<=dim B,dim C<=5`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\|L_A|_{K_6}\|\le\|L_A|_{S_6}\|.
}
\]

### Proof

By orthogonal-similarity invariance it is enough to treat `A=B\oplus C`. Besides the block-diagonal symmetric/skew sectors, both `S` and `K` have a cross-block sector parametrized by a rectangular matrix `X`:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\ X^T&0\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\ -X^T&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

On either cross sector, `L_A` is exactly the same rectangular Sylvester operator

\[
T_{B,C}(X)=BX+XC^T,
\]

and the structured input/output Frobenius norms are `sqrt(2)` times the rectangular Frobenius norms. Hence the cross-sector operator norm is identical in `S` and `K`; call it `t(B,C)`.

Writing

\[
s(B)=\|L_B|_{S}\|,\qquad k(B)=\|L_B|_{K}\|,
\]

and similarly for `C`, the three invariant orthogonal sectors give the exact formulas

\[
\boxed{
s(B\oplus C)=\max\{s(B),s(C),t(B,C)\},
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
k(B\oplus C)=\max\{k(B),k(C),t(B,C)\}.
}
\]

Each diagonal block has order at most five, so the Chen--Tian theorem gives `k(B)<=s(B)` and `k(C)<=s(C)`. The two max formulas then imply `k(A)<=s(A)`. QED.

### Consequence

Any order-six counterexample must be **orthogonally irreducible**: it can have no proper subspace invariant under both `A` and `A^T`.

Combined with Theorem 1, any order-six counterexample must satisfy both structural exclusions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{orthogonally irreducible}
\quad\text{and}\quad
[A,J]\ne0\ \text{for every orthogonal complex structure }J.
}
\]

This is materially stronger route information than a random-search miss. It eliminates all proper orthogonal direct sums and, independently, all complex-linear order-six matrices, including many nonnormal irreducible matrices.

It still does not settle a full generic real-Schur block-count stratum. In particular, a quasi-upper-triangular order-six matrix with nonzero couplings between real Schur blocks can be orthogonally irreducible, and a three-`2 x 2`-block Schur form can fail to respect any common orthogonal complex structure. Those are now the correct B30 residuals rather than the already-resolved reducible/complex-linear loci.
