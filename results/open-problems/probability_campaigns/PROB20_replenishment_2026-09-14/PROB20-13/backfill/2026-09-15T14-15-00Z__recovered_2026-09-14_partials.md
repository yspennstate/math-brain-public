### A. Exact stationary product-demand / metric dual reduction

For a connected simple unweighted graph with `m=|E|`, simple random walk has

\[
\pi_xP_{xy}=\frac1{2m}
\]

on every oriented edge. Setting `g^a=pi_a f^a` converts the source-to-stationary flow constraints into the exact ordered product-demand problem. Equivalently, the undirected pair demand is

\[
h_{ab}=2\pi_a\pi_b,
\]

with edge capacity `1/(2m)`. The optimum is exactly

\[
F(G):=\inf_{\mathbf f}\psi(\mathbf f).
\]

Finite LP duality gives

\[
F(G)=
\max_{\ell_e\ge0,\ \sum_e\ell_e/(2m)\le1}
\sum_{x,y}\pi_x\pi_y d_\ell(x,y),
\]

or

\[
F(G)=2\sup_{d\in\mathsf{Met}(V)}
\frac{\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d(X,Y)}
{\mathbb E_{\pi P}d(X_0,X_1)}.
\]

For every cut metric, scalar spectral Poincaré yields cut congestion at most `2 tau_rel`.

### B. Tree theorem

Original self-contained proof:

Route every pair demand along the unique tree path. If deleting edge `e` gives cut `S|S^c`, then its load is `2 pi(S)pi(S^c)`, hence congestion

\[
4m\pi(S)\pi(S^c).
\]

Since that tree cut has exactly one crossing graph edge, scalar Poincaré gives

\[
\pi(S)\pi(S^c)\le\frac{\tau_{\rm rel}}{2m},
\]

so every tree edge has congestion at most `2 tau_rel`. Therefore every tree satisfies the stronger bound

\[
F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}.
\]

### C. Edge-transitive theorem

Route each ordered product demand uniformly over unweighted shortest paths. Edge-transitivity makes all edge loads equal, while total routed length is `E_{pi x pi} dist(X,Y)`. Therefore

\[
F(G)\le2\,\mathbb E_{\pi\otimes\pi}d_G(X,Y)\le2D.
\]

For a connected noncomplete edge-transitive graph, the original argument used a nonadjacent two-vertex principal submatrix plus eigenvalue interlacing to obtain `lambda_2(P)>=0`, hence `tau_rel>=1`; complete graphs were checked separately with equality. Thus the target holds on this class.

### D. Spanning-tree congestion lemma

For a spanning tree `T` and tree edge `e`, let `S_e|S_e^c` be its fundamental cut in `T`, and define

\[
c_T(e)=|\delta_G(S_e)|,
\qquad
\operatorname{STC}(G)=\min_T\max_{e\in T}c_T(e).
\]

Routing product demands along `T` and applying spectral Poincaré to each fundamental cut gives

\[
F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}\operatorname{STC}(G).
\]

Consequences recorded in the original run:

- every cactus graph has a spanning tree with fundamental-cut size at most `2`, hence satisfies the target;
- with cyclomatic number `beta=m-|V|+1`, every spanning-tree fundamental cut contains its tree edge plus at most all `beta` non-tree edges, giving
  \[
  F(G)\le2\tau_{\rm rel}(\beta+1),
  \]
  so the target follows whenever `beta+1<=D`.

### F. Block decomposition and clique-block routing

The earlier task response also derived a block-cut-tree decomposition. For a block `B`, let `C_v^B` be the branch attached to `B` through port `v`, and set

\[
\mu_v^B=\pi(C_v^B).
\]

The aggregate product demand traversing block `B` between ports `u,v` is exactly

\[
h^B_{uv}=2\mu_u^B\mu_v^B.
\]

For `A subset V(B)`, the union of branches `C_A^B` has graph boundary equal to the block boundary `delta_B(A)`, so global Poincaré implies that the local block instance also has cut congestion at most `2 tau_rel`. Local block multiflows concatenate along the block-cut tree, and different blocks have disjoint edge sets.

For a clique block `K_k`, the earlier response proposed the explicit routing: for demand `2 mu_i mu_j`, choose an intermediate vertex `z` uniformly in the clique and route `i-z-j`, suppressing zero-length legs. For edge `{r,s}` the exact local load is

\[
L_{rs}=\frac2k\Bigl[\mu_r(1-\mu_r)+\mu_s(1-\mu_s)\Bigr].
\]

The branch cut at one clique vertex has exactly `k-1` crossing edges, so Poincaré gives

\[
\mu_r(1-\mu_r)\le\tau_{\rm rel}\frac{k-1}{2m}.
\]

Hence clique-block edge congestion is at most

\[
4\tau_{\rm rel}\frac{k-1}{k}<4\tau_{\rm rel}.
\]

The original task response concluded the target for block graphs when `D>=2`, with complete graphs treated separately at `D=1`. This argument is self-contained modulo the block concatenation bookkeeping and was not independently/formally reviewed.
