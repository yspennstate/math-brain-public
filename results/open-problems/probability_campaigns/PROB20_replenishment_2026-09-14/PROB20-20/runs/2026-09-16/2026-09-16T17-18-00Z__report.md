## 4. Method B7 — compare subsequential scaling limits before trying to transfer them

### 4.1 Source counterexample to a naive subsequential-transfer argument

Example 18.36 is already a fixed-law warning. For one recursively constructed fixed support \(\Sigma=\{k_j\}\) and fixed weights, Janson gets

- along \(n=k_j\), \(Y_{(1)}/n\to0\);
- along \(n=3k_j\), two boxes of size \(n/3\) occur with high probability.

Thus the same law admits a dust-like subsequential ranked-mass limit and an oligopoly subsequential limit. Consequently, a proof based only on compactness of ranked mass partitions, or only on the existence of a dust subsequential limit, cannot decide the full-sequence question. Some quantitative observable must connect adjacent or intervening scales.

This kills the naive B7 transfer before attempting a theorem. The useful part of B7 is instead to identify what a scaling-limit proof would need: a quantity that cannot reset when the admissible index crosses from a gap between support records to a newly accessible record scale.

### 4.2 Boundary scaling test suggested by this run

The fixed law in Section 5 has boundary probabilities of order
\[
\pi_k\asymp \frac{1}{k^2(\log k)^2},
\qquad
\Pr(X>x)\asymp \frac{1}{x(\log x)^2}.
\]
Its natural heavy-tail fluctuation scale is far below \(n\), while the conditioning displacement is \((\lambda-\nu)n\). This is exactly where one expects a one-big-jump mechanism, but it lies on the regular-variation boundary that the most immediate Doney theorem does not cover. In Denisov–Dieker–Shneer, the automatic centering criterion in Section 9 also does not simply cover the index \(-1\) boundary. Indeed, at the standard scale \(a_n\) satisfying \(n\Pr(X>a_n)\asymp1\), one has
\[
a_n\asymp \frac{n}{(\log n)^2},
\qquad
E[(X-\nu);X>a_n]\asymp \frac1{\log n},
\]
so the naive centered truncated-mean ratio is of order \(\log n\), not bounded. I therefore record this as a literature-transfer obstruction, not as a theorem application.

## 5. Method B23 — build one fixed opposite-behavior candidate first

The first B23 objective was not to prove Janson's conjecture. It was to try to build one fixed admissible law for which the current proof mechanism is maximally weak and then ask whether that law can plausibly support full dust.

Define one fixed weight law by
\[
w_0=2,\qquad w_1=1,
\]
and, for \(k\ge2\),
\[
 j(k):=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil,
 \qquad
 w_k:=\frac{1}{k^2j(k)^2}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is a single law; it does not vary with \(n\).

### Theorem 5.1 — the existing amplified tail-entropy bridge can vanish identically at the law level

For the fixed law (5.1):

1. the radius of convergence is \(\rho=1\);
2. its boundary mean satisfies \(0<\nu<2/3\), so e.g. \(\lambda=1\) is supercritical;
3. for every admissible \(m_n/n\to\lambda>\nu\) and every amplified cutoff sequence from the previously certified construction satisfying
   \[
   L_n\to\infty,\qquad L_n=o(n),
   \]
   if
   \[
   B_n:=-\log\Pr_\pi(S_n=m_n),
   \]
   and
   \[
   H_n(L):=-\log\!\left(\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}\right),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}\longrightarrow0.
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
Consequently the law-level quantity \(\Theta_{\rm amp}(w,\lambda)\) used in the recovered 15:40 report equals zero for this fixed law, and therefore the current Palm metric gives only
\[
b_{\rm int}(w,\lambda)=0
\]
on this example.

#### Proof

**Radius and finite mean.** Since \(j(k)=O(\log k)\),
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}w_k^{1/k}=1,
\]
so \(\rho=1\). Also \(\Phi(1)<\infty\) because \(w_k\le k^{-2}\) for \(k\ge2\).

On the dyadic block
\[
2^{j-1}\le k\le2^j-1,
\]
we have \(j(k)=j\). Hence
\[
\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^j-1}kw_k
=
\frac1{j^2}\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^j-1}\frac1k
\le \frac1{j^2}.
\]
Using \(\sum_{j\ge2}j^{-2}<1\),
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}kw_k<2.
\]
But \(\Phi(1)\ge w_0+w_1=3\), so
\[
0<\nu=\frac{\sum kw_k}{\Phi(1)}<\frac23.
\]

**Uniform anti-concentration.** Normalize \(\pi_k=w_k/\Phi(1)\). Since \(\pi_0,\pi_1>0\), let
\[
\alpha=2\min(\pi_0,\pi_1)>0.
\]
Then the law of \(X\sim\pi\) can be written as a mixture
\[
X=\begin{cases}
B,&\text{with probability }\alpha,\\
R,&\text{with probability }1-\alpha,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(B\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and \(R\) is a fixed residual law. For \(n\) independent copies let \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,\alpha)\) be the number of Bernoulli-component coordinates. Conditional on all mixture indicators and all residual values, the remaining randomness contains \(\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2)\). Therefore
\[
\sup_m\Pr(S_n=m\mid N,\text{residual data})\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{N+1}}.
\]
Splitting according to \(N\ge \alpha n/2\) and its exponentially small complement gives
\[
\sup_m\Pr(S_n=m)\le \frac{C'}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Thus for every admissible \(m_n\),
\[
B_n\ge\frac12\log n-O(1).
\tag{5.3}
\]

**The size-biased band is never exponentially thin on the \(B_n\) scale.** Up to the fixed normalization constant,
\[
k\pi_k\asymp \frac{1}{k j(k)^2}.
\]
Since \(L_n=o(m_n)\), eventually \(m_n>4L_n\). Let \(2^{j-1}\) be the smallest dyadic power strictly larger than \(L_n\). Then
\[
L_n<2^{j-1}<2L_n,
\qquad
2^j<4L_n<m_n,
\]
so the whole block \([2^{j-1},2^j-1]\) lies inside \((L_n,m_n]\). On that block,
\[
\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^j-1}\frac1{k j^2}
\ge
\frac{2^{j-1}}{2^j j^2}
=
\frac1{2j^2}.
\]
Because \(j\le 1+\log_2 m_n=O(\log n)\), there is a fixed \(c>0\) such that
\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}
\ge \frac{c}{(1+\log n)^2}.
\]
Hence
\[
H_n(L)\le 2\log\log n+O(1).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Combining (5.3) and (5.4) proves (5.2). ∎

## 6. Method B30 — one scale at a time and the exact gluing obstruction

The preceding example says that raw tail mass is too coarse. The next question is exactly what stops a sparse-scale argument from gluing.

Retain the previously used size-biased weights
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,
\]
fix \(c_0>0\) with \(c_0q_k<1\) on the positive support, and define
\[
a_k:=-\log(c_0q_k).
\]
If \(K\) is a strict record of the lower rate envelope, put
\[
h_K:=\frac{a_K}{K}.
\]
For any finite set of smaller fragment sizes \(A\subset\{k<K:q_k>0\}\), define the record excess
\[
g_K(k):=a_k-h_Kk>0
\]
and the record-Gibbs partition function
\[
Z_K(A):=\sum_{k\in A}e^{-g_K(k)}
=c_0\sum_{k\in A}q_ke^{h_Kk}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — exact entropy/record variational identity

For every probability vector \(\mu=(\mu_k)_{k\in A}\),
\[
\sum_{k\in A}\mu_k g_K(k)-H(\mu)
=
D(\mu\Vert\gamma_K)-\log Z_K(A),
\tag{6.2}
\]
where
\[
\gamma_K(k)=\frac{e^{-g_K(k)}}{Z_K(A)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal P(A)}
\left\{
\sum_k\mu_kg_K(k)-H(\mu)
\right\}
=-\log Z_K(A).
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

By definition,
\[
\log\frac{\mu_k}{\gamma_K(k)}
=\log\mu_k+g_K(k)+\log Z_K(A).
\]
Average under \(\mu\):
\[
D(\mu\Vert\gamma_K)
=\sum_k\mu_k\log\mu_k+\sum_k\mu_kg_K(k)+\log Z_K(A)
=-H(\mu)+E_\mu g_K+\log Z_K(A).
\]
Rearranging gives (6.2). Since relative entropy is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at \(\mu=\gamma_K\), (6.3) follows. ∎

### Why this is the gluing obstruction

A pointwise strict-record theorem only says \(g_K(k)>0\), or on a chosen band perhaps \(g_K(k)\ge\Gamma_K\). That is not enough. If there are \(N\) near-envelope sizes each with \(g_K(k)\approx\log N\), then every single fragment pays a diverging-looking pointwise cost while
\[
Z_K(A)\approx N e^{-\log N}=1,
\]
so entropy cancels the entire record gap. This is exactly the mechanism that a one-scale proof misses.

The crude support-count inequality
\[
Z_K(A)\le |A|e^{-\min_A g_K}
\]
is only a corollary. Equation (6.3) identifies the correct object: a proof that glues scales must control the *weighted* record partition function, not merely the smallest pointwise rate gap and not merely the number of support points.

This dovetails with the previously archived support-sensitive/critical-annulus work but is now stated as the exact variational obstruction. It also explains why the dyadic law in Section 5 defeats the un-tilted tail-entropy route: a slowly decaying amount of mass can be distributed across many scales while the record tilt changes the relevant free energy.

### What was tried and rejected under B30

1. **Single sparse atom per scale.** Rejected as a candidate for full-sequence dust. When a new record atom first becomes accessible, it is macroscopic at an index of the same order as its size; Example 18.36 demonstrates this re-condensation phenomenon explicitly.
2. **Pointwise record gap alone.** Rejected by Lemma 6.1: entropy of many near-envelope sizes can cancel it.
3. **Raw support cardinality.** Too crude. The correct gluing quantity is \(Z_K(A)\), which automatically discounts badly weighted support points.

**B30 outcome:** no metric movement, but the next universal route is narrowed to a tilted/record free-energy comparison. The old un-tilted \(H_n/B_n\) bridge is now known to be insufficient.
