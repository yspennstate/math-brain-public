## 1. Normalized boundary setup and retained amplified cloud

In the finite-mean boundary branch normalize

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu.
\]

After dividing by the lattice span,

\[
(Y_1,\dots,Y_n)\overset d=(X_1,\dots,X_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]

where the `X_i` are iid with law `pi` and `S_n=sum_i X_i`.

Write

\[
p_n=\Pr(S_n=m_n),\qquad b_n=-\log p_n,
\qquad \delta=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]

Retained checked facts include

\[
b_n\to\infty,\qquad b_n=o(n).
\]

Put

\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,\qquad \bar q_k:=\frac{q_k}{\nu}=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}.
\]

The retained amplified-cloud theorem supplies deterministic thresholds

\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),\qquad
R_n\to\infty,\qquad R_n=o(b_n),
\tag{1.1}
\]

such that, under the microcanonical law,

\[
T_n^{>L}:=\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The present run assumes full no-condensation only for contradiction analysis:

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Then choose any deterministic high-probability cap

\[
U_n\to\infty,\qquad U_n=o(n),\qquad U_n\ge L_n,
\qquad
\Pr(Y_{(1)}>U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to0.
\tag{1.4}
\]

For the actual amplified band define

\[
B_n=(L_n,U_n]\cap\mathbb Z,
\qquad
Q_n^{\rm band}:=\sum_{k\in B_n}\bar q_k.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Because (1.2) holds and the mass above `U_n` vanishes on the cap event, for every fixed `c` with

\[
0<c<\delta
\tag{1.6}
\]

we have

\[
\Pr\left(\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\in B_n\}}\ge cn\mid S_n=m_n\right)\to1.
\tag{1.7}
\]

This simple observation lets us avoid the repeated cloud-Palm change of measure entirely for the first new theorem.

# 2. New theorem: direct capped-band Chernoff bound

Let

\[
Z=X\mathbf1_{\{L_n<X\le U_n\}}.
\]

Then `0<=Z<=U_n` and

\[
E_\pi Z
=\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}k\pi_k
=\nu Q_n^{\rm band}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `s>=0`, convexity of `z -> exp(sz/U_n)` on `[0,U_n]` gives

\[
e^{sz/U_n}
\le
1+\frac z{U_n}(e^s-1).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Hence

\[
E e^{sZ/U_n}
\le
1+p_n^{B}(e^s-1),
\qquad
p_n^{B}:=\frac{\nu Q_n^{\rm band}}{U_n}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let

\[
a_n:=\frac c{U_n}.
\]

If `p_n^B>=a_n`, then `Q_n^band>=c/nu` and the conclusion below is trivial. Otherwise standard Chernoff optimization for a Bernoulli mgf yields

\[
\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i\ge cn\right)
\le
\exp\{-nD(a_n\|p_n^B)\},
\tag{2.4}
\]

where

\[
D(a\|p)=a\log\frac ap+(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-p}.
\]

Using

\[
(1-a)\log\frac{1-a}{1-p}\ge p-a\ge-a,
\]

we obtain

\[
D(a_n\|p_n^B)
\ge
\frac c{U_n}
\left[
\log\frac{c}{\nu Q_n^{\rm band}}-1
\right].
\tag{2.5}
\]

On the other hand, by (1.7),

\[
\Pr\left(\sum_i Z_i\ge cn\right)
\ge
p_n(1-o(1)).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Combining (2.4)--(2.6) gives the new quantitative necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{Q_n^{\rm band}}
\le
\frac{U_n}{cn}\,b_n
+1-\log\frac c\nu
+o(1).
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus, for every fixed `0<c<delta`,

\[
\boxed{
Q(L_n,U_n)
\ge
C_{c,\nu}
\exp\left\{-\frac{U_n}{cn}b_n+o(1)\right\}.
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

### Why this is stronger than the preceding band theorem

The `05-32-13Z` report proved only the scale-free statement

\[
-\log Q(K_n,U_n)=o(b_n)
\]

for an iterable lower cutoff `K_n`, using a growing-horizon cloud-Palm transfer.

Equation (2.7) instead:

1. uses the actual amplified-cloud cutoff `L_n` carrying essentially the full excess;
2. applies directly under the original iid law, so no cloud-path likelihood-ratio factor is paid;
3. quantifies the exponent by the actual cap fraction `U_n/n`:

\[
-\log Q(L_n,U_n)
\le
O\left(\frac{U_n}{n}b_n\right)+O(1).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Since `U_n/n->0`, (2.9) implies the earlier `o(b_n)` conclusion but is strictly more informative.

This also shows that any counterexample must place a very large amount of the fixed size-biased tail inside the very same sublinear interval that carries its conditioned amplified cloud.

# 3. New exact marginal entropy route to the record envelope

The previous attacks compared record points with pathwise Palm or truncated-Cramer budgets. There is a simpler exact information inequality at the one-coordinate marginal.

Let `Q_n^mic` be the microcanonical law and let

\[
\mu_n(k):=Q_n^{\rm mic}(Y_1=k)
\tag{3.1}
\]

be its common one-coordinate marginal.

Because

\[
Q_n^{\rm mic}
=\pi^{\otimes n}(\,\cdot\mid S_n=m_n),
\]

we have the exact relative entropy

\[
D(Q_n^{\rm mic}\|\pi^{\otimes n})=b_n.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The KL decomposition against the product of the marginals gives

\[
D(Q_n^{\rm mic}\|\pi^{\otimes n})
=
D(Q_n^{\rm mic}\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})
+nD(\mu_n\|\pi),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_n\|\pi)\le\frac{b_n}{n}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is exact and uses no local limit theorem.

# 4. Record envelope adapted to the size-biased weights

Because

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}q_k=\nu,
\]

we have `q_k<=nu` for every `k`. Fix once and for all

\[
c_0:=\frac1{2(1+\nu)},
\]

so that `0<c_0q_k<1` whenever `q_k>0`.

Define

\[
h_k:=-\log(c_0q_k),
\qquad
r(U):=
\min_{\substack{1\le k\le U\\q_k>0}}
\frac{h_k}{k}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the same type of lower-rate envelope used by the retained strict-record theorem, up to an irrelevant fixed normalization constant.

For every `k<=U` with `pi_k>0`,

\[
h_k\ge k r(U).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since `q_k=k pi_k`,

\[
-\log\pi_k
=h_k+\log c_0+\log k.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For the amplified band `B_n=(L_n,U_n]`, define

\[
c_{n,k}:=k\mu_n(k),
\tag{4.4}
\]

its marginal mass profile,

\[
C_n:=\sum_{k\in B_n}c_{n,k}
=E_{Q_n^{\rm mic}}\left[\frac1n\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\in B_n\}}\right],
\tag{4.5}
\]

and its box-frequency mass

\[
A_n:=\sum_{k\in B_n}\mu_n(k)
=\sum_{k\in B_n}\frac{c_{n,k}}k.
\tag{4.6}
\]

By (1.2), the cap event, and boundedness by `m_n/n`,

\[
\boxed{C_n\to\delta.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Also

\[
A_n\le\frac{C_n}{L_n}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

# 5. Exact record-rate lower bound for the marginal KL

Split the KL sum into the band and its complement. By the log-sum inequality,

\[
\sum_{k\notin B_n}\mu_n(k)
\log\frac{\mu_n(k)}{\pi_k}
\ge
(1-A_n)
\log\frac{1-A_n}{1-\pi(B_n)}.
\]

Using `x log(x/y)>=x-y`, with `x=1-A_n` and `y=1-pi(B_n)`,

\[
(1-A_n)
\log\frac{1-A_n}{1-\pi(B_n)}
\ge
\pi(B_n)-A_n
\ge-A_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

On the band, (4.2)--(4.4) give

\[
\log\frac{\mu_n(k)}{\pi_k}
=
h_k+\log(k\mu_n(k))+\log c_0
\ge
k r(U_n)+\log c_{n,k}+\log c_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sum_{k\in B_n}\mu_n(k)
\log\frac{\mu_n(k)}{\pi_k}
\ge
r(U_n)C_n
+
\sum_{k\in B_n}\frac{c_{n,k}}k\log c_{n,k}
+
A_n\log c_0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (5.1) and (5.2),

\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_n\|\pi)
\ge
r(U_n)C_n
-
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}
-
(1+|\log c_0|)A_n,
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

where the exact cloud size-label entropy cost is

\[
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}
:=-\sum_{k\in B_n}
\frac{c_{n,k}}k\log c_{n,k}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

If some `c_{n,k}>1`, its contribution to (5.4) is negative, which only helps (5.3); no positivity assumption on (5.4) is needed.

Combining with (3.3),

\[
\boxed{
C_n r(U_n)
\le
\frac{b_n}{n}
+
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}
+
(1+|\log c_0|)A_n.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the cleanest record-envelope inequality obtained in this campaign so far: the only loss beyond the exact conditioning cost is the entropy of how the conditioned cloud spreads its mass among occupancy labels, plus its vanishing box-frequency mass.

# 6. Universal bound on the cloud size-label entropy

The entropy term in (5.4) can be bounded without counting the number of possible labels up to `U_n`.

For every `x>=0` and every real `a`, the convex conjugate inequality for `x log x` gives

\[
x\log x\ge ax-e^{a-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Take

\[
a=-\theta k,
\qquad
\theta:=\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{c_{n,k}}k\log c_{n,k}
\ge
-\theta c_{n,k}
-
\frac{e^{-1-\theta k}}k.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Summing over `k>L_n`, using `C_n<=m_n/n=O(1)`, and the geometric bound

\[
\sum_{k>L_n}\frac{e^{-\theta k}}k
\le
\frac{2}{L_n\log L_n}
\tag{6.4}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`, gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}
\le
C_1\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

for a constant `C_1` depending only on an eventual upper bound for `m_n/n`.

Together with (4.8) and (5.5),

\[
\boxed{
C_n r(U_n)
\le
\frac{b_n}{n}
+
C_2\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Since `C_n->delta`,

\[
\boxed{
(\delta-o(1))\,n r(U_n)
\le
b_n
+
C_2\frac{n\log L_n}{L_n}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Using the retained amplified scale (1.1),

\[
\frac{n\log L_n}{L_n}
=
(1+o(1))\frac{b_n\log L_n}{R_n},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
(\delta-o(1))\,n r(U_n)
\le
b_n
+
O\left(\frac{b_n\log L_n}{R_n}\right).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

No local limit theorem, one-big-jump theorem, regular variation, or tail monotonicity enters (6.7)--(6.8).

# 7. Relation to the strict-record subsequence

Let `M_j` be a synchronized strict macroscopic record from the retained record-crossing theorem, so

\[
M_j\sim\delta n_j,
\qquad
h_{M_j}=M_j r(M_j),
\]

and define the record-over-cap advantage

\[
G_j:=M_j\bigl(r(U_{n_j})-r(M_j)\bigr).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The retained deterministic theorem gives an unbounded `G_j` along a subsequence for every diverging sublinear cap.

Equation (5.5) gives the sharper structural comparison

\[
M_jr(U_{n_j})
\le
\frac{M_j}{n_jC_{n_j}}b_{n_j}
+
\frac{M_j}{C_{n_j}}
\left[
\mathcal H_{n_j}^{\rm cloud}
+(1+|\log c_0|)A_{n_j}
\right].
\tag{7.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 b_{n_j}-h_{M_j}
\ge
G_j
-
\left|\frac{M_j}{n_jC_{n_j}}-1\right|b_{n_j}
-
O\left(
 n_j\mathcal H_{n_j}^{\rm cloud}+n_jA_{n_j}
\right).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The universal entropy estimate gives

\[
n_j\mathcal H_{n_j}^{\rm cloud}+n_jA_{n_j}
=
O\left(\frac{n_j\log L_{n_j}}{L_{n_j}}\right)
=
O\left(\frac{b_{n_j}\log L_{n_j}}{R_{n_j}}\right).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus the leading exact-conditioning cost again cancels against the macroscopic record cost. The surviving obstruction is now identified as a genuine **cloud size-label entropy term**, rather than a path-counting artifact.

To turn (7.3) into the desired contradiction one still has to pay the retained residual exact-local/Harnack cost for

\[
p_{n_j-1}(m_{n_j}-M_j).
\]

A sufficient closure condition is therefore that on some synchronized record subsequence

\[
G_j
\gg
\left|\frac{M_j}{n_jC_{n_j}}-1\right|b_{n_j}
+
\frac{b_{n_j}\log L_{n_j}}{R_{n_j}}
+
\text{residual-Harnack-cost}_j.
\tag{7.5}
\]

The retained record theorem proves only `G_j->infinity` along a subsequence; it does not currently prove domination of the explicit terms in (7.5). Therefore (7.5) is a conditional closure criterion, not a completed proof of Problem 18.39.

# 8. Why the new entropy inequality is useful

Earlier record comparisons had correction terms coming from truncated convolution composition counts, sequential Palm horizons, or Renyi support complexity. Equation (5.5) shows that these can be compressed into one intrinsic object:

\[
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}
=-\sum_{k\in(L_n,U_n]}
\mu_n(k)\log\bigl(k\mu_n(k)\bigr).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This quantity measures how much label entropy is gained by spreading a fixed linear amount of excess mass across many distinct sublinear occupancy values.

The universal worst-case estimate is

\[
\mathcal H_n^{\rm cloud}=O(\log L_n/L_n),
\]

but a substantially smaller actual entropy immediately strengthens the record comparison. In particular, if along a record-crossing subsequence

\[
n_j\mathcal H_{n_j}^{\rm cloud}
+n_jA_{n_j}
+\left|\frac{M_j}{n_jC_{n_j}}-1\right|b_{n_j}
+
\text{residual-Harnack-cost}_j
=o(G_j),
\]

then macroscopic condensation follows on that subsequence.

This identifies a concrete next target: prove that a fixed finite-mean boundary law cannot simultaneously have

1. the actual-band lower mass (2.8),
2. the record-envelope inequality (5.5),
3. cloud label entropy as large as the worst-case `log L/L` scale at every synchronized strict record,
4. and residual Harnack penalties large enough to erase every unbounded record advantage.

A useful next attack is to classify near-extremizers of (6.1)--(6.5). The Fenchel calculation shows that worst-case cloud label entropy is approximately achieved by a geometric profile in the occupancy label `k`, with decay parameter of order `log L/L`. If one can show such a profile is incompatible with the fixed-law record envelope or with the direct fixed-band mass bound (2.8) at infinitely many strict records, the remaining entropy loss can be reduced below the record advantage.
