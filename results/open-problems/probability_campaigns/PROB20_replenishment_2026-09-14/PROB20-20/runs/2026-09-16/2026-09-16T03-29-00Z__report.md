## 1. Normalization and notation

Work with the normalized boundary probability law

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu,
\]

in the finite-mean boundary regime. Let

\[
p_N(M)=\Pr(S_N=M),
\qquad
b(N,M)=-\log p_N(M).
\]

For the target sequence write

\[
p_n=p_n(m_n),
\qquad
b_n=b(n,m_n),
\qquad
\delta=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]

The normalized size-biased boundary law is

\[
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},\qquad k\ge1.
\]

As retained from the checked earlier campaign, `b_n -> infinity` and `b_n=o(n)`.

Fix constants

\[
0<c<\delta,
\qquad
\eta:=\frac{\delta-c}{3}>0,
\qquad
\Lambda>\lambda+1.
\]

For compact supercritical residual states define the uniform exact-sum rate

\[
\epsilon_n
:=
\sup\left\{

\frac n2\le N\le n,

p_N(M)>0

\tag{1.1}

The compactness upgrade of Janson's Theorem 17.1 proved in the preceding checkpoint gives

## 2. New universal quantitative lower bound for the truncated Cramér rate

For an integer cutoff `K`, put

\[
Z^{(K)}:=X\mathbf1_{\{X\le K\}}
\]

and, at the fixed target

\[
a:=\nu+\eta/2>\nu,
\]

define

\[
J_K
=
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left\{
ta-
\log E e^{tZ^{(K)}}
\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — universal `1/K` rate

There is a constant

\[
c_*=c_*(\nu,\eta)>0
\]

such that for every sufficiently large `K`,

\[
\boxed{J_K\ge c_*/K.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof
For `s>0` and `0<=z<=K`, convexity of the exponential on `[0,1]` gives

\[
e^{sz/K}
\le
1+\frac zK(e^s-1).
\]

Since `E Z^(K) <= nu`,

\[
E e^{(s/K)Z^{(K)}}
\le
1+\frac\nu K(e^s-1)
\le
\exp\left\{\frac\nu K(e^s-1)\right\}.
\]

Hence, testing the variational formula at `t=s/K`,

\[
J_K
\ge
\frac1K\left[
sa-\nu(e^s-1)
\right].
\]

The bracket has derivative `a-nu=eta/2>0` at `s=0`, so choose one fixed sufficiently small `s_0>0` for which

\[
c_*:=s_0a-\nu(e^{s_0}-1)>0.
\]

This proves (2.2). `square`

### Consequence
The slow diagonal in the preceding checkpoint can be replaced by a broad quantitative admissible class. Any deterministic cutoff `K_n` satisfying

\[
K_n\to\infty,
\qquad
K_n=o(n),
\qquad
K_n\epsilon_n\to0
\tag{2.3}
\]

automatically satisfies

\[
\epsilon_n/J_{K_n}\to0,
\qquad
nJ_{K_n}\to\infty.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus the append-only correction `03-08-00Z` requiring `nJ_{K_n}->infinity` is automatically met for every cutoff in (2.3).

One explicit choice is

\[
K_n=
\left\lfloor
\min\{\epsilon_n^{-1/2},\sqrt n\}
\right\rfloor,
\tag{2.5}
\]

after harmlessly forcing monotone divergence if desired.

## 3. Uniform residual tail mass for every cutoff satisfying (2.3)

Let `K_n` satisfy (2.3). For any exact-sum state in the compact interval, define

\[
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}
=
\sum_{i=1}^N
Y_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}.
\]

If

\[
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}<\frac\eta4N,
\]

then the truncated contribution exceeds `(nu+eta/2)N`. The unconditioned Chernoff bound and (1.1) therefore give

\[
\Pr\left(
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}<\frac\eta4N
\mid S_N=M
\right)
\le
\exp\{-N(J_{K_n}-\epsilon_n)\}.
\]

By (2.4), this tends to zero uniformly over all admissible residual states. Consequently the cloud-ball Palm probability

\[
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}
:=
E\left[
\frac{T_{N,M}^{>K_n}}M
\middle|S_N=M
\right]
\]

obeys, uniformly,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\ge a_0+o(1),
\qquad
a_0:=\frac\eta{4\Lambda}>0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

After reducing `a_0` by a factor two, assume below that

\[
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\ge a_0
\tag{3.2}
\]

at every pre-stop state for all large `n`.

This strengthens the preceding slow-cutoff statement: the residual theorem is valid uniformly for **every** cutoff satisfying (2.3).

## 4. Retained exact cloud-Palm telescope

At state `(N,M)`, the ordinary size-biased Palm kernel is

\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{Nk\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{Mp_N(M)}.
\]

Condition it on `k>K_n`:

\[
K_{N,M}^{(K_n)}(k)
=
\frac{K_{N,M}(k)}{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}}
\mathbf1_{\{k>K_n\}}.
\]

Let

\[
Q_n:=\sum_{k>K_n}\bar q_k.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact one-step identity from the preceding checkpoint is

\[
 b(N,M)-E b(N-1,M-B)
=
D(K_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\Vert\bar q^{(K_n)})
+
\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}
+
\log\frac{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}}{Q_n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Run this **auxiliary cloud-conditioned Palm chain** until the cumulative selected mass first reaches `cn`; call the stopping time `tau`. Every selected box is larger than `K_n`, hence

\[
\tau\le cn/K_n+1=o(n).
\]

Every pre-stop residual density stays at least `nu+eta` for all large `n`. Therefore (3.2) and the exact KL chain rule give

\[
\boxed{
E_{\rm cl}\tau\,H_n\le b_n,
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

where

\[
H_n
:=
\log\left(1+\frac\eta\nu\right)
+
\log\frac{a_0}{Q_n}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Since `K_n->infinity` and `bar q` is a fixed probability law,

\[
Q_n\to0,
\qquad
H_n\to\infty.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equation (4.3) is retained as correct.

## 5. Important correction: the cloud chain is a tilted path measure

The auxiliary cloud-conditioned chain in Section 4 is **not** the same law as first sampling the original conditioned allocation and then repeatedly choosing a cloud box from it.

At one state, let `mathcal Q_{N,M}` denote the original exact-sum allocation law. If one first samples an allocation `x` and then one uniformly chosen ball, the joint marked-Palm law is

\[
\mathcal Q_{N,M}(x)\frac{x_i}{M}.
\]

Conditioning the marked box to have size `>K_n` gives joint density

\[
\frac1{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}}
\mathcal Q_{N,M}(x)
\frac{x_i}{M}
\mathbf1_{\{x_i>K_n\}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Its marginal on the allocation itself is therefore biased by

\[
\frac{T_{>K_n}(x)/M}{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}},
\]

not left unchanged. Iterating this conditioning repeatedly compounds the bias.

Therefore the statements in Section 5 of `2026-09-16T02-55-00Z__report.md` that directly converted a short cloud-chain stopping time into **w.h.p. statements under the original allocation law** were missing a change-of-measure factor. In particular:

- equation (5.4) there, claiming a w.h.p. unconditional lower bound for `Y_(1)`, is **retracted**;
- equation (5.5) there, claiming a deterministic high-probability-cap lower bound, is **retracted**.

The exact cloud-Palm identity, the uniform residual theorem, and the entropy budget (4.3) do not depend on that transfer and remain valid.

The next section supplies the correct transfer inequality.

## 6. New exact path-to-original transfer bound

Let `P_cl` denote the auxiliary cloud-conditioned path law of Section 4, including the reconstructed initial allocation and the successive marked box choices. Let `Q_n^mic` denote the original microcanonical allocation law at `(n,m_n)`.

### Lemma 6.1 — bounded-length likelihood-ratio transfer

For every event `E` depending only on the initial allocation and every integer `r>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm cl}(E,\tau\le r)
\le
a_0^{-r}\,Q_n^{\rm mic}(E).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
Q_n^{\rm mic}(E)
\ge
a_0^r P_{\rm cl}(E,\tau\le r).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof
Compare the cloud-conditioned path to the ordinary sequential marked-Palm experiment that starts from `Q_n^mic` and, at each residual state, chooses one box by a uniformly sampled ball without conditioning on its size.

At cloud step `j`, conditioning the chosen box on `B_j>K_n` multiplies the joint density by exactly

\[
\frac{\mathbf1_{\{B_j>K_n\}}}{\alpha_j},
\qquad
\alpha_j=\alpha_{N_j,M_j}^{(K_n)}\ge a_0.
\]

For a path of length `s<=r`, the total likelihood ratio relative to the ordinary marked-Palm experiment is therefore at most

\[
a_0^{-s}\le a_0^{-r}.
\]

After integrating out all ordinary marked-box choices, the initial allocation marginal of the ordinary experiment is exactly `Q_n^mic`. Summing over all stopped paths with `s<=r` gives (6.1). `square`

This is the change-of-measure factor missing from the superseded Section 5.

## 7. New rigorous lower bound for a large maximum under the original law

From (4.3), set

\[
r_n
:=
\max\left\{1,
\left\lceil\frac{2b_n}{H_n}\right\rceil
\right\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Markov's inequality under the cloud chain gives

\[
P_{\rm cl}(\tau\le r_n)\ge\frac12.
\tag{7.2}
\]

On the event `tau<=r_n`, the selected cloud boxes have total mass at least `cn`, so at least one selected box has size at least

\[
\frac{cn}{r_n}.
\]

Every selected box is a box of the reconstructed initial allocation. Therefore, for

\[
E_n
:=
\left\{
Y_{(1)}\ge\frac{cn}{r_n}
\right\},
\]

we have

\[
P_{\rm cl}(E_n,\tau\le r_n)
=P_{\rm cl}(\tau\le r_n)
\ge\frac12.
\]

Applying Lemma 6.1 yields the new valid original-law estimate

\[
\boxed{
Q_n^{\rm mic}\left(
Y_{(1)}\ge\frac{cn}{r_n}
\right)
\ge
\frac12 a_0^{r_n}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

When `b_n/H_n -> infinity`, this can be written more transparently as

\[
\boxed{
Q_n^{\rm mic}\left(
Y_{(1)}
\ge
\left(\frac c2+o(1)\right)
\frac{nH_n}{b_n}
\right)
\ge
\exp\left\{-O\left(\frac{b_n}{H_n}\right)\right\}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is weaker than the retracted w.h.p. assertion, but it is rigorous and quantitatively records the exact price of repeatedly conditioning the Palm path to remain in the cloud.

## 8. New closure criterion and a necessary condition for any full counterexample

### Corollary 8.1 — fixed-tail entropy comparable to the conditioning cost forces macroscopic mass

If along some subsequence there is a constant `theta>0` such that

\[
H_n\ge\theta b_n,
\tag{8.1}
\]

then `r_n` is bounded by a constant depending only on `theta`, and (7.3) yields constants

\[
\varepsilon>0,
\qquad
p_*>0
\]

such that along that subsequence

\[
\boxed{
Q_n^{\rm mic}(Y_{(1)}\ge\varepsilon n)
\ge p_*.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus `Y_(1)/n ->_p 0` is impossible.

This gives failure of convergence in probability; it does **not** by itself upgrade the subsequence probability to `1-o(1)`, so it is not the stronger w.h.p. form of Janson's conjecture.

### Corollary 8.2 — necessary fixed-tail restriction under full no-condensation

If, hypothetically,

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\tag{8.3}
\]

along the full admissible sequence, then for **every** quantitative residual cutoff `K_n` satisfying (2.3),

\[
\boxed{
H_n=o(b_n).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{Q(K_n)}=o(b_n),
\qquad
Q(K):=\sum_{k>K}\bar q_k.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

So the fixed size-biased boundary tail must remain exponentially thick on the `b_n` scale at every cutoff that is simultaneously sublinear and below the inverse uniform exact-sum rate.

This conclusion survives the change-of-measure audit and replaces the invalid high-probability-cap inequality from the previous report.

## 9. New fixed-tail quantile obstruction

For `theta>0`, define the `b_n`-scale tail quantile

\[
K_n^{(\theta)}
:=
\min\left\{
K:\ Q(K)\le e^{-\theta b_n}
\right\}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

(For the infinite-support boundary laws relevant here, this is finite and diverges as `b_n->infinity`.)

### Theorem 9.1 — quantile closure criterion

Suppose that for some fixed `theta>0` and some subsequence,

\[
K_n^{(\theta)}=o(n)
\tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
K_n^{(\theta)}\epsilon_n\to0.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then along that subsequence

\[
H_n
\ge
\theta b_n+O(1),
\]

so Corollary 8.1 applies. Hence there are constants `epsilon,p_*>0` such that

\[
Q_n^{\rm mic}(Y_{(1)}\ge\epsilon n)\ge p_*
\]

along a further subsequence.

Therefore any hypothetical full-sequence counterexample must satisfy, for every fixed `theta>0`, the following obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the }e^{-\theta b_n}\text{ tail quantile cannot simultaneously be sublinear and obey }
K_n^{(\theta)}\epsilon_n\to0.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

In other words, a counterexample would need a very specific synchronization between two irregularities of the **same fixed law**:

1. the size-biased boundary tail must stay thick until a scale at least comparable to the inverse uniform supercritical exact-sum rate; or
2. the relevant tail quantile itself must already be macroscopic.

This is a sharper, fully checked alternative to the previous unsupported cap transfer.

## 10. Explicit admissible scale from the universal rate bound

Taking the concrete cutoff

\[
K_n^*=
\left\lfloor
\min\{\epsilon_n^{-1/2},\sqrt n\}
\right\rfloor
\tag{10.1}
\]

in Corollary 8.2 gives the unconditional necessary condition under full no-condensation

\[
\boxed{
-\log Q(K_n^*)=o(b_n).
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

More generally, because Lemma 2.1 only requires `K_n epsilon_n ->0`, one may take `K_n` almost as large as `1/epsilon_n` (subject also to `K_n=o(n)`), losing only an arbitrary diverging factor.

It is useful to define the uniformity inflation

\[
\Gamma_n:=\frac{n\epsilon_n}{b_n}\ge1
\tag{10.3}
\]

whenever the target pair `(n,m_n)` lies in the compact interval. Then an admissible cutoff can be chosen near

\[
K_n
\asymp
\frac{n}{b_n\Gamma_n\ell_n},
\qquad
\ell_n\to\infty
\tag{10.4}
\]

when this diverges. Thus a putative counterexample has a new dichotomy:

- either `Gamma_n` is large, meaning some nearby compact-supercritical exact sums are much more expensive per site than the target conditioning;
- or the fixed size-biased tail must satisfy `-log Q(K_n)=o(b_n)` already near the basic `n/b_n` mesoscopic scale (up to `Gamma_n` and a slowly diverging factor).

No claim is made that this dichotomy alone closes the problem.
