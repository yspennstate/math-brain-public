## 1. Exact support-sensitive entropy correction

Keep the notation of the main report. At a synchronized strict record `R_j`, write

\[
h_j=r(R_j),
\]

and on the actual cloud band `[L_j,U_j]` define

\[
\Gamma_j
=
\inf_{L_j\le x\le U_j}
 x(r(x)-h_j).
\]

For every positive support point `k in (L_j,U_j]`, define its nonnegative excess above the uniform record barrier by

\[
d_j(k)
:=
-\log(c_0q_k)-k h_j-\Gamma_j
\ge0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Then

\[
\bar q_k
=\frac1{c_0\nu}
 e^{-\Gamma_j-h_jL_j}
 e^{-d_j(k)}e^{-h_j(k-L_j)}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Hence the entire actual band mass has the **exact** representation

\[
\boxed{
Q(L_j,U_j)
=
\frac1{c_0\nu}
 e^{-\Gamma_j-h_jL_j}
 Z_j,
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

where

\[
Z_j
:=
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
 e^{-d_j(k)}e^{-h_j(k-L_j)}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Thus, writing

\[
\beta_j=-\log Q(L_j,U_j),
\]

we have the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j+h_jL_j
=
\beta_j+\log Z_j-\log(c_0\nu).
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The geometric bound in the main report was simply

\[
Z_j\le\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-h_jm}
=\frac1{e^{h_j}-1}
\asymp h_j^{-1}.
\]

Equation (1.5) shows that the true remaining entropy is not intrinsically `log(1/h_j)`. It is the much sharper fixed-law quantity

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_j:=\log^+ Z_j.}
\tag{1.6}
\]

For a hypothetical full counterexample, the previous actual-band theorem gives `beta_j=o(b_{n_j})`, so

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j+h_jL_j
\le
 o(b_{n_j})+\mathcal E_j+O(1).
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

This replaces the worst-case geometric entropy `log(1/h_j)` by an exact support-sensitive truncated partition term.

## 2. Near-envelope support-count dichotomy

For any deterministic threshold `theta_j>=0`, define the near-envelope support count

\[
N_j(\theta_j)
:=
\#\left\{
 k\in(L_j,U_j]\cap\operatorname{supp}(q):
 d_j(k)\le\theta_j
\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Split the sum in (1.4) according to whether `d_j(k)<=theta_j`. The near part contributes at most `N_j(theta_j)`. The far part satisfies

\[
\sum_{d_j(k)>\theta_j}
 e^{-d_j(k)}e^{-h_j(k-L_j)}
\le
\frac{e^{-\theta_j}}{e^{h_j}-1}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Z_j
\le
N_j(\theta_j)
+
\frac{e^{-\theta_j}}{e^{h_j}-1}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_j
\le
\log^+\left(
N_j(\theta_j)
+
\frac{e^{-\theta_j}}{e^{h_j}-1}
\right).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This gives a clean dichotomy for the only entropy mechanism left by the uniform record theorem:

1. either the cloud band contains **many** support points lying within `theta_j` of the uniform record barrier;
2. or the entropy correction is reduced by choosing `theta_j` large enough that the geometric remainder is small.

Thus a surviving full counterexample is forced toward a genuinely **near-envelope-rich** sublinear support band. Merely having many support points with much worse weights does not help.

This is the natural regime for the next multi-atom forced-sum/smoothing attack.

## 3. Adversarial check: `Gamma_j >> log(1/h_j)` is not a purely record-theoretic theorem

The main report identified

\[
\Gamma_j-\log(1/h_j)\to+\infty
\]

as one possible sufficient strengthening. It is important not to assume that such a statement follows from summability plus radius one alone. It does not.

Here is an explicit valid finite-mean radius-one boundary law showing the failure.

Let

\[
R_j=2^{2^j},\qquad j\ge1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Fix small constants, for example

\[
a=\frac14,\qquad c=\frac1{10},
\]

and define a probability law `pi` by

\[
\pi_{R_j}=\frac{c e^{-j}}{R_j},
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\pi_1=a,
\]

and put all remaining probability at zero:

\[
\pi_0
=1-a-\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{c e^{-j}}{R_j}>0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Then the mean is finite and positive:

\[
\nu
=a+\sum_{j\ge1}ce^{-j}
<\infty.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The support contains `0` and `1`, so the lattice span is one. Moreover

\[
\pi_{R_j}^{1/R_j}
=\exp\left[
-\frac{j+\log R_j-\log c}{R_j}
\right]
\to1,
\]

so the generating radius is exactly one.

For the size-weighted masses

\[
q_{R_j}=R_j\pi_{R_j}=ce^{-j}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Take `c_0=1` (all these `q_k` are below one). For all sufficiently large `j`, the strict record rate at `R_j` is

\[
h_j
=\frac{-\log q_{R_j}}{R_j}
=\frac{j-\log c}{R_j}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The values `R_j` are strict record points because their denominators grow doubly exponentially while the numerators grow only linearly.

Choose the sublinear cap

\[
U_j=R_{j-1}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Since

\[
R_j=R_{j-1}^2,
\]

we have `U_j/R_j->0`. The cap-scaled record gap is

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_j(h_{j-1}-h_j)
&=(j-1-\log c)
-
\frac{j-\log c}{R_{j-1}}\\
&=j+O(1)\to\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]

So Theorem 2.1 from the main report holds strongly.

However,

\[
\log\frac1{h_j}
=
\log R_j-\log(j-\log c)
=2^j\log2-O(\log j),
\tag{3.9}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_j(h_{j-1}-h_j)}{\log(1/h_j)}\to0.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

In particular,

\[
U_j(h_{j-1}-h_j)-\log(1/h_j)\to-\infty.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus the proposed entropy-dominating strengthening is **false as a theorem of record geometry alone**.

This law is **not** claimed to be a counterexample to Janson Problem 18.39. In fact its macroscopic record costs

\[
A_j=R_jh_j=j-\log c
\]

are extremely cheap, so one expects strong condensation pressure at those record scales. The construction is only a certified obstruction to an overly ambitious deterministic next lemma.

## 4. Why the exact entropy term is the correct next target

The example above also shows why replacing the exact support by the geometric majorant can lose almost everything.

Between `R_{j-1}` and `R_j` the constructed law has essentially no large support points. The geometric factor

\[
(1-e^{-h_j})^{-1}\asymp h_j^{-1}
\]

pretends that support is available at every integer, producing entropy of order `log(1/h_j)~2^j`, while the actual fixed-law band may contain only one relevant support point.

For such sparse laws, the exact quantity `Z_j` in (1.4) is tiny and the geometric entropy is fictitious. Conversely, if `Z_j` really is large, (2.2) shows that the law must contain many support points close to the record barrier.

Therefore the next attack should split into two genuinely different regimes:

* **Sparse/energetically separated band:** use (1.5) directly; the exact entropy correction is small, and the uniform record advantage should be combined with the one-giant residual estimate.
* **Near-envelope-rich band:** exploit the abundance of cheap support values to build multi-atom exact-sum corrections with substantially better local alignment than the generic Gaussian Harnack penalty.

This is a sharper and noncircular version of the next-step plan in the main report.
