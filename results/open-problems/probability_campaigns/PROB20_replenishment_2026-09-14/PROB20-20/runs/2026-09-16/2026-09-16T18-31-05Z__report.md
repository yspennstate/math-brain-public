\[
0<\nu<\Lambda,\qquad m_n/n\to\Lambda,
\]

is

\[

\[
\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k t^k,

\tag{2.1}

## 3. Method B26 — hunt the non-regular heavy-tail cases that could defeat the preceding theorem

The preceding run covers local regular variation. A natural counterexample attempt is therefore to move to smooth but strongly non-regular tails where the ratio `pi_(k+1)/pi_k` changes with scale.

Two fixed-law families are especially hostile to a direct regular-variation transfer:

1. logarithmic-hazard / discrete lognormal-type laws
   \[
   \pi_k\asymp p(k)\exp\{-c(\log k)^\beta\},\qquad \beta>1,
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
   with a slowly varying or intermediate-regularly-varying prefactor;
2. stretched exponentials
   \[
   \pi_k\asymp A\exp\{-c k^\alpha\},\qquad 0<\alpha<1.
   \tag{3.2}
   \]

Both have finite moments of every order, are subexponential, and lie outside the stable regular-variation class of the previous report. If full-sequence dust were going to arise merely from leaving regular variation, these are among the first fixed laws to test.

The attempt to use either family as a counterexample fails rigorously: (3.1) lies in a local one-big-jump domain at every linear supercritical displacement, while (3.2) is covered by zero-range condensation results which force the maximum to absorb asymptotically all excess mass.

This gives genuine metric movement because, for example, the fixed law `w_k=exp(-k^(3/4))` was not in the preceding BBY local-stable class but is now certified to have the exact metric `Lambda-nu`.

## 4. Method B11 — dualize the allocation into an exact local large-deviation problem

The useful dual language is the centered random walk. Let

\[
\xi_i=X_i-\nu,
\qquad T_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i=S_n-n\nu,
\qquad \delta=\Lambda-\nu>0.
\]

Conditioning on `S_n=m_n` is conditioning on the exact local event

\[
T_n=m_n-n\nu=\delta n+o(n).
\]

The question becomes: at that exact local large deviation, is the event asymptotically carried by one coordinate of size `delta n+o(n)`?

Denisov, Dieker and Shneer, *Large deviations for random walks under subexponentiality: the big-jump domain*, Ann. Probab. 36 (2008), arXiv:math/0703265v3, give a local theorem precisely of this form. Their Corollary 2.1 assumes, for some `1<kappa<=2`,

- `E xi=0` and `E|xi|^kappa<infinity`;
- the local tail `x -> F(x+Delta)` is almost decreasing;
- `x^kappa F(x+Delta)` is either in the subexponential-density class `S_d` or is O-regularly varying;
- the local tail is insensitive to shifts up to `x^(1/kappa)`.

Then for every fixed `a>0`, uniformly over linear displacements `x>=a`,

\[
P\{T_n\in nx+\Delta\}
\sim n P\{\xi_1\in nx+\Delta\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Their Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 2.5 prove (4.1) by decomposing the event into no-large-jump, exactly-one-large-jump, and multiple-large-jump pieces; the first and third are negligible, and on the unique-large-jump term the remaining sum stays on a natural scale `b_n=o(n)`.

### Theorem 4.1 — full-sequence Janson condensation under the DDS local hypotheses

Assume the finite-mean boundary normalization (2.1), span one, and suppose the centered fixed law `xi=X-nu` satisfies the hypotheses of Denisov–Dieker–Shneer Corollary 2.1 for the unit local interval `Delta=(0,1]`. Let `m_n/n->Lambda>nu` along any full admissible sequence. Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow{p}\Lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\Lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Set

\[
z_n=m_n-n\nu-1.
\]

Because `S_n` is integer valued,

\[
\{S_n=m_n\}=\{T_n\in z_n+(0,1]\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Moreover `z_n/n->delta>0`. Therefore (4.1) applies at the exact conditioning event.

In the DDS proof, take their natural-scale sequence `b_n`. Under the moment hypothesis in Corollary 2.1 it may be chosen of order `n^(1/kappa)` up to a constant factor, hence

\[
b_n=o(n).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Let `h_n=O(b_n)` be their truncation level. Lemma 2.5 decomposes the exact local probability into the no-jump term, the exactly-one-coordinate-above-`h_n` term, and a negligible remainder with at least two coordinates above `h_n`. On the linear domain `z_n~delta n`, the no-jump term is also negligible compared with the denominator in (4.1). Consequently, conditional on (4.3), with probability tending to one there is exactly one coordinate above `h_n` and it carries the local deviation.

It remains to locate that coordinate macroscopically. Fix `K>0`. The natural-scale property used in the DDS proof gives

\[
P\left(\left|\sum_{i=2}^n\xi_i\right|\le Kb_n\right)\to 1
\]

as first `n->infinity` and then `K->infinity`. Their local insensitivity condition is precisely what makes the one-large-coordinate local mass stable under all shifts of this residual sum in the natural-scale window. Hence, after normalizing by the full local probability, the part of the unique-large-jump term for which the residual is outside `[-Kb_n,Kb_n]` vanishes as `K->infinity`. Thus under the exact conditional law,

\[
\sum_{i\ne i_*}(X_i-\nu)=O_p(b_n)=o_p(n),
\tag{4.5}
\]

where `i_*` is the unique large coordinate on the asymptotically full event.

Using the identity `S_n=m_n`,

\[
X_{i_*}
=m_n-(n-1)\nu-\sum_{i\ne i_*}(X_i-\nu)
=(\Lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
\]

Since every other coordinate is at most `h_n=o(n)` on the same asymptotically full one-jump event, `X_{i_*}` is the maximum. Therefore

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p\Lambda-\nu.
\]

Finally `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n`, and the right side is eventually bounded, so convergence in probability implies convergence of expectations. This proves (4.2). ∎

### Explicit new non-regular family from DDS

DDS Section 8.3 treats local masses of the form

\[
F(x+\Delta)=p(x)e^{-c\log^\beta x},
\qquad \beta>1,
\tag{4.6}
\]

with `p` O-regularly varying and, for their stated local theorem, intermediate regularly varying. They note that lognormal and Benktander-I tails fit this framework. Their Theorem 8.2 gives the local one-big-jump asymptotic above thresholds

\[
O\!\left(\sqrt{n\log^\beta n}\right),\quad 1<\beta<2,
\]

or

\[
\gg \sqrt{n\log^{2\beta-2} n},\quad \beta\ge2.
\]

Both thresholds are `o(n)`, so every fixed positive linear excess lies in the local big-jump domain. Therefore any span-one fixed boundary law whose centered unit-window tail has (4.6) with the stated DDS prefactor hypotheses satisfies (4.2). This is not a regularly varying family and is genuine new scope relative to the preceding BBY transfer.

## 5. A second new class: stretched exponentials condense even beyond the simplest DDS ratio criterion

The more revealing case is a stretched exponential. Armendáriz, Grosskinsky and Loulakis, *Zero Range Condensation at Criticality*, Stochastic Processes and their Applications 123 (2013), arXiv:0912.1793v2, analyze fixed stationary weights

\[
w(k)\sim A_\theta
\exp\left\{-\frac{b}{1-\theta}k^{1-\theta}\right\},
\qquad 0<\theta<1,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with the subleading restrictions specified in their setup when needed. These are exactly weighted-allocation product weights after conditioning on the total: their conditional measure `mu_(L,N)` is the iid boundary product law conditioned on `S_L=N`, hence is the same mathematical object as Janson's allocation law.

Write

\[
\alpha=1-\theta\in(0,1),
\]

so (5.1) is `exp(-c k^alpha)` up to the stated asymptotic constant. The boundary law has finite mean and variance. Let `rho_c=nu` in their notation and let `N/L->Lambda>nu`. Their Theorem 2.3 parameterizes the excess by

\[
N-\nu L=t_L(\sigma^2L)^{1/(1+\theta)}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For a fixed supercritical density,

\[
t_L
=\frac{(\Lambda-\nu)L+o(L)}{(\sigma^2L)^{1/(1+\theta)}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Theorem 2.3(b) permits `t=+infinity` and gives

\[
\frac{M_L}{N-\nu L}\xrightarrow{p}a(+\infty)=1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus we get the following source-valid transfer.

### Theorem 5.1 — stretched-exponential full-sequence condensation

Assume the fixed Janson boundary weights satisfy the Armendáriz–Grosskinsky–Loulakis stretched-exponential hypothesis (5.1), equivalently tail exponent `alpha=1-theta in (0,1)`, including their stated subleading assumptions. Then for every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->Lambda>nu`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow{p}\Lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\Lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Take `L=n`, `N=m_n`, and identify their boundary product law with (2.1). The conditional measure is identical to Janson's weighted allocation by the cancellation of the fixed fugacity factor. Equation (5.3) places every linear supercritical sequence in Theorem 2.3(b) with `t_L->+infinity`; (5.4) then yields

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}{m_n-\nu n}\to_p1.
\]

Since `(m_n-nu n)/n->Lambda-nu`, the first statement in (5.5) follows. The deterministic bound `Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)` yields uniform integrability and hence the expectation limit. ∎

This supplies a particularly clean concrete metric improvement: for the fixed law

\[
w_k=e^{-k^{3/4}}
\]

(up to an irrelevant positive constant/equivalent exponential tilt), the frozen metric is now exactly

\[
b(w,\Lambda)=\Lambda-\nu.
\]

This law was outside the previous local stable regular-variation theorem.

## 6. Method B20 — why the obvious exact-atom algorithm is too strong

The comparison between DDS and the stretched-exponential theorem isolates a useful obstruction.

For a Weibull/local stretched-exponential tail with

\[
F(x+\Delta)\asymp e^{-c x^\alpha},\qquad 0<\alpha<1,
\]

DDS Theorem 8.3 gives the simple local asymptotic

\[
P\{T_n\in x+\Delta\}\sim nF(x+\Delta)
\]

provided

\[
\frac{x}{R(x)}\gg\sqrt n,
\qquad R(x)\asymp x^\alpha.
\tag{6.1}
\]

At a linear displacement `x~delta n`, condition (6.1) becomes

\[
n^{1-\alpha}\gg n^{1/2},
\]

which holds automatically only when

\[
\alpha<\frac12.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the simplest exact-atom one-big-jump criterion ceases to apply at the midpoint `alpha=1/2`. But Theorem 5.1 above proves macroscopic one-site condensation for **every** `0<alpha<1`.

This is not a contradiction: for lighter stretched exponentials the dominant giant need not sit at the nominal excess to microscopic accuracy. The zero-range analysis allows a mesoscopic amount of excess to remain in the bulk. Its discussion gives, in the supercritical regime, a bulk excess of order

\[
(1-a_L)(N-\nu L)
\asymp L^{\alpha}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for the corresponding tail exponent `alpha=1-theta`. Since `alpha<1`, this is still `o(L)`. Consequently

\[
M_L=(N-\nu L)-O(L^\alpha)+\text{smaller fluctuations},
\]

so the normalized maximum still converges to the full macroscopic excess even though a proof demanding a giant at one exact atom can fail to apply.

**B20 outcome:** the universal Problem 18.39 strategy should not try to prove

\[
P(S_n=m_n)\sim n\pi_{\lfloor(\Lambda-\nu)n\rfloor}
\]

for every fixed law. That target is unnecessarily strong. What is enough for the frozen metric is a **mesoscopically shifted one-big-jump statement**: there should exist a window `r_n=o(n)` such that the conditional mass is forced to configurations containing a coordinate in

\[
[(\Lambda-\nu)n-r_n,\ (\Lambda-\nu)n+r_n].
\tag{6.4}
\]

The stretched-exponential family proves that this distinction is not cosmetic. It supplies a model where the macroscopic conclusion is robust while microscopic tail insensitivity is the wrong proof objective.

This is the main route correction of the run and is directly relevant to the arbitrary irregular-law case: record-scale arguments should compare a macroscopic **window of near-record sizes**, not insist on one exact record atom.

## 9. What failed, what changed, and the next attack

The negation-first stress test did not find a counterexample. Instead, two important non-regular families that might have been plausible counterexample territory are now ruled out completely: their maximum captures the full linear excess along the whole admissible sequence.

The new obstruction is also clearer. The unresolved laws must be more irregular than smooth log-hazard and stretched-exponential tails; moreover, demanding an exact local ratio at the nominal excess is too strong. The next arbitrary-law attack should therefore modify the existing record-Gibbs / nested-cap machinery as follows:

1. replace a single record size `R_j` by an `o(n_j)` **record window** around the macroscopic excess;
2. sum the fixed-law weight over that whole window, allowing the mesoscopic shift seen in stretched-exponential condensation;
3. compare this windowed one-giant mass with the exact capped-canonical fragmentation cost already archived;
4. try to prove that if every such macroscopic window is too light, then the support must sustain enough near-envelope entropy over all intervening scales to violate the nested-cap KL budget.

That target is strictly weaker than a universal exact-atom one-big-jump theorem and is now justified by a concrete solved family rather than by heuristic preference.
