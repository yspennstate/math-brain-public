# 1. The moving amplified tilt itself is enough

For a cutoff `L`, define

\[
Z_L:=X\mathbf 1_{\{X\le L\}},
\qquad
M_L(t):=Ee^{tZ_L},
\qquad
\Lambda_L(t):=\log M_L(t).
\]

Let

\[
\mu_n\downarrow\nu
\]

be the moving target from the retained amplified diagonal, and let

\[
\theta_n>0
\]

be the unique optimizer satisfying

\[
\Lambda_{L_n}'(\theta_n)=\mu_n.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
J_n:=J_{L_n}(\mu_n)
=\mu_n\theta_n-\Lambda_{L_n}(\theta_n).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The retained diagonal gives

\[
\frac{nJ_n}{b_n}\to\infty.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Lemma 1.1

\[
\boxed{
\theta_n\to0,\qquad
\frac{n\theta_n}{b_n}\to\infty,\qquad
L_n\theta_n\to\infty.
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Proof
For any fixed `epsilon>0`, the exponentially tilted mean of the truncated law at `epsilon`, namely `Lambda_L'(epsilon)`, tends to infinity as `L->infinity`: radius one implies `sum pi_k exp(epsilon k)=infinity`; as larger support points enter, the weighted average of `k` under these partial exponential sums eventually exceeds every fixed constant. Since `mu_n->nu<infinity`, (1.1) therefore forces `theta_n<epsilon` eventually. Hence `theta_n->0`.

Also `M_L(theta)>=1`, so `Lambda_L(theta)>=0`; therefore

\[
J_n\le \mu_n\theta_n.
\]

Since `mu_n` stays bounded away from infinity, (1.3) implies

\[
\frac{n\theta_n}{b_n}\to\infty.
\]

Finally

\[
L_n\theta_n
=\mathcal R_n\frac{n\theta_n}{b_n}(1+o(1))\to\infty.
\]
\(\square\)

This is useful because it avoids the fixed auxiliary level `mu in (nu,lambda)` used in the preceding checkpoint. The moving amplified tilt already has all the required scale separation, while its low-part target converges all the way down to the boundary mean `nu`.

# 2. Moving-tilt saturation of the actual no-condensation band

Assume for contradiction analysis

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Choose a deterministic cap

\[
U_n=o(n),\qquad U_n\ge2L_n,
\tag{2.2}
\]

such that

\[
\eta_n
=P(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define the defective capped generating function

\[
F_n(t):=\sum_{0\le k\le U_n}\pi_ke^{tk}
\tag{2.4}
\]

and the amplified-band part

\[
B_n(t):=\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\pi_ke^{tk}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The capped exact-sum probability is

\[
P(S_n=m_n,\max_iX_i\le U_n)=\eta_ne^{-b_n}.
\]

Coefficient positivity gives, for every `t>=0`,

\[
\eta_ne^{-b_n}
\le e^{-tm_n}F_n(t)^n.
\tag{2.6}
\]

At `t=theta_n`, Lemma 1.1 yields `b_n/n=o(theta_n)`, while `log eta_n/n=o(theta_n)` because `n theta_n->infinity`. Therefore

\[
\log F_n(\theta_n)
\ge \lambda_n\theta_n-o(\theta_n)
=\lambda\theta_n+o(\theta_n),
\tag{2.7}
\]

so

\[
F_n(\theta_n)
\ge1+\lambda\theta_n+o(\theta_n).
\tag{2.8}
\]

The low contribution is bounded by the truncated mgf:

\[
\sum_{k\le L_n}\pi_ke^{\theta_nk}
\le M_{L_n}(\theta_n)
=\exp\{\mu_n\theta_n-J_n\}
\le e^{\mu_n\theta_n}
=1+\nu\theta_n+o(\theta_n).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Subtracting gives the moving-level strengthening of the preceding tilted-band theorem:

## Theorem 2.1 — moving amplified-tilt saturation

Under hypothetical full no-condensation,

\[
\boxed{
B_n(\theta_n)
=\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\pi_ke^{\theta_nk}
\ge(\lambda-\nu+o(1))\theta_n.
}
\tag{2.10}
\]

Thus the analytically distinguished tilt used in the **original amplified-cloud construction itself** already forces first-order mass into the actual purported sublinear condensate band.

# 3. The capped canonical saddle lies strictly below the amplified tilt

Let

\[
\varphi_n(t):=\frac{F_n'(t)}{F_n(t)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At `t=0`, because `U_n->infinity` and `E X=nu`,

\[
\varphi_n(0)\to\nu<\lambda.
\tag{3.2}
\]

At `t=theta_n`, the band derivative satisfies

\[
F_n'(\theta_n)
\ge L_nB_n(\theta_n).
\]

The low part of `F_n(theta_n)` is at most `M_{L_n}(theta_n)<=1+o(1)`. Hence, using (2.10),

\[
\varphi_n(\theta_n)
\ge
\frac{L_nB_n(\theta_n)}{M_{L_n}(\theta_n)+B_n(\theta_n)}
\ge c\frac{L_n\theta_n}{1+\theta_n}
\to\infty.
\tag{3.3}
\]

(The displayed lower bound remains valid if `B_n(theta_n)` is larger: `x/(M+x)` is increasing in `x`.)

Therefore there is a unique

\[
s_n\in(0,\theta_n)
\tag{3.4}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\varphi_n(s_n)=\lambda_n:=m_n/n.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is the canonical saddle of the *actual capped law*.

Define the capped canonical distribution

\[
P_n^{\rm can}(X=k)
=\frac{\pi_ke^{s_nk}}{F_n(s_n)},
\qquad 0\le k\le U_n.
\tag{3.6}
\]

It has exact mean

\[
E_n^{\rm can}X=\lambda_n.
\tag{3.7}
\]

# 4. Exact capped rate budget

Let

\[
I_n:=\lambda_ns_n-\log F_n(s_n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

The exact exponential-change-of-measure identity is

\[
\eta_ne^{-b_n}
=e^{-s_nm_n}F_n(s_n)^n
P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence

\[
nI_n
=b_n-\log\eta_n
+\log P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n)
\le b_n-\log\eta_n.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since `s_n` maximizes `lambda_n t-log F_n(t)`,

\[
I_n\ge -\log F_n(0)\ge0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0\le I_n\le b_n/n+o(1/n),
\qquad I_n\to0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because `s_n<theta_n->0`,

\[
\log F_n(s_n)=\lambda_ns_n-I_n\to0,
\qquad
\boxed{F_n(s_n)\to1.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Equation (4.5) is also the exact KL identity

\[
\boxed{
D(P_n^{\rm can}\Vert\pi)=I_n,
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

where `P_n^{can}` is extended by zero outside `[0,U_n]`.

Thus any full no-condensation counterexample would produce a sequence of capped probability laws whose mean is shifted from `nu` to `lambda` while their per-box relative entropy from the one fixed boundary law is at most `b_n/n=o(1)`.

# 5. New theorem: almost the entire excess is carried by the amplified band at the canonical saddle

Write

\[
A_n(t):=\sum_{k\le L_n}\pi_ke^{tk}.
\]

For the truncated mgf,

\[
M_{L_n}(t)=P(X>L_n)+A_n(t),
\qquad
M_{L_n}'(t)=A_n'(t).
\]

Its logarithmic derivative is increasing. Since

\[
0<s_n<\theta_n
\]

and

\[
\Lambda_{L_n}'(\theta_n)=\mu_n,
\]

we have

\[
\Lambda_{L_n}'(0)
\le\Lambda_{L_n}'(s_n)
\le\mu_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

But

\[
\Lambda_{L_n}'(0)=E[X;X\le L_n]\to\nu,
\qquad
\mu_n\to\nu.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_{L_n}'(s_n)\to\nu.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Also

\[
1\le M_{L_n}(s_n)
\le M_{L_n}(\theta_n)
\le e^{\mu_n\theta_n}
\to1.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
A_n'(s_n)
=M_{L_n}'(s_n)
=M_{L_n}(s_n)\Lambda_{L_n}'(s_n)
\to\nu.
\tag{5.4}
\]

At the capped saddle,

\[
F_n'(s_n)=\lambda_nF_n(s_n)\to\lambda.
\]

Subtracting (5.4) gives:

## Theorem 5.1 — capped canonical amplified-band first-moment saturation

\[
\boxed{
B_n'(s_n)
=\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}k\pi_ke^{s_nk}
\longrightarrow\lambda-\nu=:\delta>0.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}q_ke^{s_nk}
=\delta+o(1).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is stronger than the preceding moving-tilt mass statement in one respect: it identifies a fixed, non-vanishing **first moment** of the actual amplified band under the true capped canonical saddle, while the low part contributes asymptotically exactly `nu`.

No local limit theorem is used.

# 6. Fragment multiplicity and canonical variance

Let

\[
p_n^{\rm band}
:=P_n^{\rm can}(L_n<X\le U_n)
=\frac{B_n(s_n)}{F_n(s_n)}.
\]

Since every band value lies between `L_n` and `U_n`, (5.5) and (4.6) imply

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta+o(1)}{U_n}
\le
p_n^{\rm band}
\le
\frac{\delta+o(1)}{L_n}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
np_n^{\rm band}\ge(\delta+o(1))\frac{n}{U_n}\to\infty
\tag{6.2}
\]

because `U_n=o(n)`. Thus the capped canonical ensemble is necessarily in a many-fragment regime: the expected number of amplified-band boxes diverges.

The second moment satisfies

\[
E_n^{\rm can}[X^2;L_n<X\le U_n]
\ge
L_nE_n^{\rm can}[X;L_n<X\le U_n]
=(\delta+o(1))L_n.
\]

Since the canonical mean is `lambda_n=O(1)`, there is a fixed `c>0` such that

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_n^{\rm can}(X)\ge cL_n
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

for all large `n`.

Conversely, because the capped law is supported on `[0,U_n]`,

\[
E_n^{\rm can}X^2\le U_nE_n^{\rm can}X=\lambda_nU_n,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
cL_n\le\operatorname{Var}_n^{\rm can}(X)\le C U_n.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This identifies a new canonical signature forced by any counterexample: the I-projection has vanishing KL cost per box, fixed supercritical mean, and diverging variance at least on the amplified scale.

# 7. Quantitative separation of the two saddles

Put

\[
d_n:=\theta_n-s_n>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For `L_n<k\le U_n`,

\[
e^{s_nk}=e^{\theta_nk}e^{-d_nk}
\ge e^{-d_nU_n}e^{\theta_nk}.
\]

Hence

\[
B_n(s_n)
\ge e^{-d_nU_n}B_n(\theta_n).
\tag{7.2}
\]

Using `B_n'(s_n)>=L_nB_n(s_n)`, Theorem 2.1, and Theorem 5.1,

\[
\delta+o(1)
=B_n'(s_n)
\ge
cL_n\theta_ne^{-d_nU_n}.
\]

Therefore:

## Theorem 7.1 — amplified-to-canonical saddle separation

\[
\boxed{
(\theta_n-s_n)U_n
\ge
\log(L_n\theta_n)-O(1)
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus the actual capped canonical tilt is not merely smaller than the amplified truncated tilt: their separation is large on the scale `1/U_n`.

This is an exact quantitative version of the heuristic left at the end of the `09-15-00Z` checkpoint.

# 8. New support-sensitive record constraint at the canonical saddle

Retain the strict lower-rate records of the fixed size-weighted boundary law. Choose fixed `c_0>0` with `c_0q_k<1` on positive support and define

\[
r(x)
=\min_{1\le k\le x,q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(c_0q_k)}k.
\]

Let `R_j` be strict record locations, `h_j=r(R_j)`, and synchronize them with admissible `n_j` by the checked Gaussian-Harnack first-crossing construction, so

\[
R_j/n_j\to\delta.
\]

Under the hypothetical counterexample, set

\[
L_j=L_{n_j},\qquad U_j=U_{n_j},\qquad s_j=s_{n_j}.
\]

The retained uniform record-band theorem supplies, along a subsequence,

\[
\Gamma_j
:=
\inf_{L_j\le x\le U_j}x(r(x)-h_j)
\to\infty.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Thus every supported `k in (L_j,U_j]` satisfies

\[
q_k\le c_0^{-1}e^{-h_jk-\Gamma_j}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Insert (8.2) into the new canonical saturation (5.6):

\[
\delta+o(1)
\le
c_0^{-1}e^{-\Gamma_j}
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
e^{(s_j-h_j)k}.
\]

Therefore:

## Theorem 8.1 — canonical record-band partition obstruction

Along the synchronized record subsequence,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
e^{(s_j-h_j)k}
\ge c\,e^{\Gamma_j}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j
\le
\log\left(
C
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
e^{(s_j-h_j)k}
\right).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is the canonical counterpart of the preceding report's `theta`-tilted partition obstruction. It is sharper in structure: there is no `1/k` weight and no vanishing prefactor `theta_j`; the price is that the tilt is the smaller, endogenous capped saddle `s_j`.

Let

\[
N_j^{\rm supp}
:=\#\{k\in(L_j,U_j]\cap\operatorname{supp}(q)\}.
\]

A support-free corollary is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j
\le
(s_j-h_j)_+U_j
+\log N_j^{\rm supp}
+O(1).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus every hypothetical counterexample must, on the record subsequence, fall into at least one of two regimes:

1. **canonical-over-record tilt:** `(s_j-h_j)_+ U_j` carries essentially the record barrier; or
2. **near-envelope support richness:** the actual amplified band contains exponentially many (on the `Gamma_j` scale) supported sizes.

Because `N_j^{supp}<=U_j`, a coarse form is

\[
\Gamma_j\le(s_j-h_j)_+U_j+\log U_j+O(1).
\tag{8.6}
\]

This does not yet contradict `Gamma_j->infinity`, because `log U_j` can diverge arbitrarily fast relative to the record advantage.

# 9. How this sharpens the preceding record–tilt dichotomy

The prior report used a fixed auxiliary target `mu in (nu,lambda)` and a tilt `t_n`; this report replaces it by the actual moving amplified tilt `theta_n` and introduces the endogenous capped saddle `s_n`.

The three scales now satisfy

\[
0<s_n<\theta_n\to0,
\tag{9.1}
\]

\[
(\theta_n-s_n)U_n\ge\log(L_n\theta_n)-O(1)\to\infty,
\tag{9.2}
\]

and

\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}q_ke^{s_nk}=\delta+o(1).
\tag{9.3}
\]

At synchronized strict records, the entire actual cloud band must additionally satisfy (8.3).

Hence the formerly undifferentiated branch `U_j(theta_j-h_j)` splits into two mathematically distinct pieces:

\[
U_j(\theta_j-h_j)
=U_j(\theta_j-s_j)+U_j(s_j-h_j).
\tag{9.4}
\]

The first term is now known to diverge at least like `log(L_j theta_j)`, purely because the capped canonical mean must come back down from the exploding mean at the amplified tilt. The second term is the genuine record-vs-canonical obstruction, and (8.5) shows exactly how much of it can be replaced by support entropy.

This does **not** yet solve Problem 18.39: the mandatory separation in the first term is compatible with the fixed law, while the second term can still be paid by a very rich near-envelope support set.

# 10. Failed shortcut and why it fails

A tempting conclusion from (6.3) is to invoke a triangular-array local CLT for the capped canonical sum, since its variance diverges at least like `L_n`. That is not justified universally.

The cap only satisfies `U_n=o(n)`. The Lindeberg ratio

\[
\frac{U_n}{\sqrt{n\operatorname{Var}(X)}}
\]

need not tend to zero from the present estimates; using only (6.4), it can be as large as `U_n/sqrt(nL_n)`, which is not controlled. Sparse support can also make the effective lattice structure at mesoscopic scales highly irregular even though the original fixed law has span one. Therefore no local CLT is claimed here.

Likewise, a crude support-count bound in (8.6) is not enough: prior explicit extremal constructions show that `log U_j` can dwarf an arbitrarily slowly growing record advantage.
