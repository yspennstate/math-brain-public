# 4. New deterministic lemma: exponential survival bound for a weighted size-biased permutation

Consider a deterministic vector of nonnegative box masses

\[
y=(y_1,\ldots,y_N),
\qquad
M:=\sum_i y_i>0.
\]

Generate its size-biased permutation by repeatedly choosing a remaining box with probability proportional to its mass and removing the whole box. Let `C` be any subset of boxes such that

\[
y_i\ge L>0
\qquad(i\in C),
\]

and let

\[
T_C:=\sum_{i\in C}y_i.
\]

Let `T_C^{(r)}` denote the mass of boxes in `C` that survive the first `r` size-biased removals. If the process exhausts all positive mass before `r` removals, append dummy zero removals.

## Lemma 4.1 — exponential cloud survival

For every integer `r>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
E\bigl[T_C^{(r)}\mid y\bigr]
\le
T_C\left(1-\frac{L}{M}\right)^r
\le
T_C\exp\left(-\frac{rL}{M}\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof
Fix `i in C`. Conditional on box `i` still being present after `s` removals, the remaining total mass `M_s` satisfies `M_s<=M`. Hence the conditional probability of avoiding box `i` at the next removal is

\[
1-\frac{y_i}{M_s}
\le
1-\frac{y_i}{M}
\le
1-\frac{L}{M}.
\]

Iterating,

\[
\Pr(i\text{ survives the first }r\text{ removals}\mid y)
\le
\left(1-\frac{L}{M}\right)^r.
\]

Multiply by `y_i`, sum over `i in C`, and use `1-u<=e^{-u}`. \(\square\)

This estimate is stronger than the Bernoulli-hit argument when one wants to remove **almost all** cloud mass: the cost is logarithmic in the desired residual fraction rather than reciprocal in that fraction.

# 5. New theorem: near-full excess capture with logarithmic overhead

\[

\tag{5.1}
\]

Define

\[
\varepsilon_n:=e^{-s_n}	o0
\tag{5.2}
\]

and

\[
r_n:=
\left\lceil
\frac{3m_n}{L_n}s_n
\right\rceil.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Using `m_n/n->lambda` and (2.3),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{r_n}{b_n}
=O\left(\frac{s_n}{R_n}\right)
\to0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

On the high-probability event in (3.6), initially

\[
T_n^{>L}\ge(\delta-4e^{-8R_n})n.
\]

Condition on the realized allocation and apply Lemma 4.1 to the amplified cloud. Since the total initial mass is `m_n`,

\[
E\left[T_{n,\mathrm{rem}}^{>L}(r_n)\mid Y\right]
\le
m_n\exp\left(-\frac{r_nL_n}{m_n}\right)
\le
m_ne^{-3s_n}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

By Markov's inequality,

\[
\Pr\left(
T_{n,\mathrm{rem}}^{>L}(r_n)>e^{-s_n}n
\middle|Y
\right)
\le
\frac{m_n}{n}e^{-2s_n}
=O(e^{-2s_n}).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Because `s_n=o(R_n)`,

\[
e^{-8R_n}=o(e^{-s_n}).
\]

Therefore the total cloud mass removed by the first `r_n` size-biased peels is at least

\[
(\delta-2e^{-s_n})n
\]

with probability tending to one. Since cloud mass is part of total removed mass,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
\sum_{j=1}^{r_n}B_j
\ge
x_n
\right)\to1,
\qquad
x_n:=(\delta-2e^{-s_n})n,
\qquad
r_n=o(b_n).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

### Comparison with the preceding checkpoint

The preceding near-full theorem used a hit-probability lower bound and required roughly

\[
r_n=O\left(\frac{b_n}{\epsilon_nR_n}\right)
\]

peels to leave an `epsilon_n n` residual cloud mass. The new estimate needs only

\[
\boxed{
r_n=O\left(\frac{b_n}{R_n}\log\frac1{\epsilon_n}\right).}
\tag{5.8}

For example, taking `s_n=sqrt(R_n)` gives a residual cloud fraction `e^{-sqrt(R_n)}` after only

\[
r_n=O(b_n/\sqrt{R_n})=o(b_n)
\]

peels. The older reciprocal estimate at the same peel length only gave a residual fraction of order `R_n^{-1/2}`.

# 6. Retained exact stopped Palm KL budget at the stronger target

The exact sequential Palm identity from the authoritative `23-44-00Z` checkpoint is retained. If the process is stopped before cumulative removed mass reaches `x_n`, then every pre-stop residual state has `N_j=n-j` boxes and total mass

\[
M_j>m_n-x_n.

By (3.6), (5.2), and `m_n/n->lambda`, for all large `n`,

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}
\ge
\frac{m_n-x_n}{n}
\ge
\nu+e^{-s_n}>\nu.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus every density-premium term in the exact Palm telescope is nonnegative. Stopping either when the cumulative removed mass first reaches `x_n` or after `r_n` removals therefore gives

\[
\boxed{

\tag{6.2}

Here the iid sum is larger than the stopped hit event because increments are nonnegative.

# 8. New maximal Chernoff lemma by a stopped defective exponential martingale

Let `q` be any probability law on the positive integers. For `U>=1` and `t>=0`, define the defective truncated mgf

\[
M_U(t):=\sum_{1\le k\le U}q_ke^{tk}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For iid `X_i~q`, let `S_s=X_1+\cdots+X_s`, and define

\[
E(x,r,U)
:=
\left\{
\exists s\le r:\ S_s\ge x,\ \max_{i\le s}X_i\le U
\right\}.
\]

## Lemma 8.1 — maximal defective-mgf bound

For every `x>0`, integer `r>=1`, `U>=1`, and `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(E(x,r,U))
\le
\exp\left\{
-tx+r[\log M_U(t)]_+
\right\}.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

### Proof
Assume first `M_U(t)>0` and define

\[
Z_s
:=
\frac{
 e^{tS_s}\mathbf1_{\{\max_{i\le s}X_i\le U\}}
}{M_U(t)^s},
\qquad Z_0=1.
\]

Because

\[
E[e^{tX_1}\mathbf1_{\{X_1\le U\}}]=M_U(t),
\]

`(Z_s)` is a nonnegative martingale.

Stop at the first time `tau<=r` at which either `S_tau>=x` or `X_tau>U`, and use `tau=r` if neither occurs earlier. On `E(x,r,U)` we hit `S_tau>=x` before violating the cap.

If `M_U(t)>=1`, then `tau<=r` implies

\[
Z_\tau\ge e^{tx}M_U(t)^{-r}.
\]

If `M_U(t)<=1`, then `M_U(t)^{-\tau}>=1`, so

\[
Z_\tau\ge e^{tx}.
\]

Since `tau` is bounded, optional stopping gives `E Z_tau=1`. Markov's inequality on `Z_tau` yields (8.2). The case `M_U(t)=0` is trivial. \(\square\)

### Immediate improvement over the previous checkpoint

There is **no union factor `r`** and hence no additive `log r` term. The positive part is still essential: when the defective mgf is below one, stopping earlier only strengthens the exponential martingale.

Combining (7.5) and (8.2) gives the new necessary condition for every hypothetical full counterexample:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left\{
 x_nt-r_n[\log M_{U_n}(t)]_+
\right\}
\le
(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This is the corrected truncated-reference obstruction at the stronger near-full scale, now without the previous union loss.

# 9. New zero-entropy corollary: the truncated martingale root

Whenever `bar q((U,\infty))>0`, we have `M_U(0)<1`, while `M_U(t)->infinity` as `t->infinity` provided the truncated support is nonempty. Hence there is a unique

\[
\kappa(U)>0
\]

such that

\[
M_U(\kappa(U))=1.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Taking `t=kappa(U_n)` in (8.3), the entropy/composition term vanishes exactly:

\[
\boxed{
 x_n\kappa(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus any full no-condensation counterexample must force the positive adjustment coefficient of the **defective truncated size-biased law** down to the exact conditioning scale `b_n/n`.

This is weaker than the rate-envelope bound in some regular examples, because aggregate mass below the cap can make `kappa(U)` much smaller than the best pointwise coding rate. It is nevertheless a genuinely support-free necessary condition with no composition entropy at all.

# 10. Sharpened support-free rate-envelope bound: exact binary-entropy correction

Define the best per-unit code rate below the cap

\[
a(U)
:=
\min_{\substack{1\le k\le U\\ \bar q_k>0}}
\frac{-\log\bar q_k}{k}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Then for every supported `k<=U`,

\[
\bar q_k\le e^{-a(U)k}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Put

\[
p:=\frac{r}{x},
\qquad 0<p<1/2,
\qquad
d:=-\log(1-p).
\tag{10.3}
\]

If `a(U)<=d`, then

\[
xa(U)\le xd\le xH(p),
\]

where

\[
H(p):=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
\]

is binary entropy.

If `a(U)>d`, take `t=a(U)-d`. By (10.2),

\[
M_U(t)
\le
\sum_{k\ge1}e^{-dk}
=
\frac1{e^d-1}
=
\frac{1-p}{p}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Since `p<1/2`, the right side is at least one. Lemma 8.1 yields

\[
-\log\Pr(E(x,r,U))
\ge
x(a(U)-d)
-r\log\frac{1-p}{p}.
\]

The correction simplifies **exactly**:

\[
xd+r\log\frac{1-p}{p}
=xH(p).
\tag{10.5}
\]

Therefore, in both branches,

\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr(E(x,r,U))
\ge
xa(U)-xH(r/x).
}
\tag{10.6}
\]

Applying this to the iid reference event in Section 7 and using (7.5),

\[
\boxed{
 x_na(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+
x_nH(r_n/x_n).
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

This improves the previous checkpoint in two ways simultaneously:

1. the near-full target now requires only logarithmic, rather than reciprocal, overhead in the residual cloud fraction;
2. the `+log r_n` union penalty disappears and the remaining composition loss is the exact binary-entropy term `x_n H(r_n/x_n)`.

Since `r_n/x_n -> 0`,

\[
H(u)\le u\log\frac eu,
\qquad 0<u\le1/2,
\]

and therefore, from (5.4),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta n\,a(U_n)}{b_n}
\le
1
+O(e^{-s_n})
+O\left(
\frac{s_n}{R_n}
\log\frac{e nR_n}{b_ns_n}
\right)
+o(1).
}
\tag{10.8}
\]

The sequence `s_n` is a free deterministic tradeoff parameter subject only to `1<<s_n<<R_n`.

# 12. What the new tradeoff buys

The survival theorem permits extremely small residual cloud mass while keeping `r_n=o(b_n)`.

For example, taking

\[
s_n=\sqrt{R_n}
\]

gives

\[
\varepsilon_n=e^{-\sqrt{R_n}},
\qquad
r_n=O(b_n/\sqrt{R_n}),
\]

and

\[
\mathcal E_n
\le
C\frac{b_n}{\sqrt{R_n}}
\log\frac{e n\sqrt{R_n}}{b_n}.
\tag{12.1}
\]

More generally, writing `s_n=R_n/ell_n` with

\[
\ell_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\ell_n=o(R_n),
\]

gives

\[
\varepsilon_n=e^{-R_n/\ell_n},
\qquad
r_n=O(b_n/\ell_n),
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_n
\le
C\frac{b_n}{\ell_n}
\log\frac{e n\ell_n}{b_n}.
}
\tag{12.2}
\]

Thus the residual-mass error can be made exponentially small in `R_n/ell_n` while the number of reference draws is only a `1/ell_n` fraction of `b_n`.

This does not by itself imply that (11.7) holds, because `b_n`, the admissible centering error, and the record advantage may have arbitrarily irregular relative rates. It does, however, eliminate the previous `1/epsilon_n` loss as a genuine obstruction.
