## 1. Normalized boundary law and retained scale facts

When the finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu.
\]

After dividing by the lattice span, assume span one. Then

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]

where `xi_i` are iid with law `pi` and `S_n=sum_i xi_i`.

Write

\[
p_N(M)=\Pr(S_N=M),\qquad
b(N,M)=-\log p_N(M),
\]

and for the target sequence

\[
p_n=p_n(m_n),\qquad b_n=-\log p_n.
\]

Retained checked facts give

\[
\boxed{b_n\to\infty,\qquad b_n=o(n).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

The fixed Bernoulli smoothing theorem also gives

\[
p_n\le Cn^{-1/2},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{b_n\ge \tfrac12\log n-O(1).}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Normalize the fixed size-biased boundary law by

\[
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{1.3}
\]

For `A<B` put

\[
Q(A,B)=\sum_{A<k\le B}\bar q_k,
\qquad
Q(A)=\sum_{k>A}\bar q_k.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The amplified-cloud checkpoint constructs deterministic `R_n` and `L_n` with

\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
R_n\to\infty,
\qquad
R_n=o(b_n),
\tag{1.5}
\]

such that, with `delta=lambda-nu`,

\[
T_n^{>L}:=
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{1.6}
\]

We need slightly more than the bare `o_p(n)` statement on the **lower** side. The proof in the amplified-cloud checkpoint gives, for its diagonal construction,

\[
\Pr\left(
T_n^{>L}< (\delta-2\varepsilon_n)n
\mid S_n=m_n
\right)
\le
\exp\{-nJ_{L_n}(\nu+\varepsilon_n)+b_n\},
\tag{1.7}
\]

where `epsilon_n -> 0` blockwise and, writing

\[
g_n=L_nJ_{L_n}(\nu+\varepsilon_n),
\]

one has

\[
\frac{g_n}{R_n}\to\infty.
\tag{1.8}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{nJ_{L_n}}{b_n}
=\frac{g_n}{R_n}(1+o(1))\to\infty,
\]

(1.2) implies the useful strengthened failure estimate

\[
\boxed{
n\,\Pr(G_n^c)\to0,
\qquad
G_n:=\left\{T_n^{>L}\ge(\delta-2\varepsilon_n)n\right\}.
}
\tag{1.9}
\]

Indeed, eventually the exponent in (1.7) is at most `-4b_n`, and `e^{-4b_n}=O(n^{-2})` by (1.2).

This quantitative observation is new to the present run and is what permits an ordinary-Palm stopping argument without a repeated cloud-conditioning likelihood ratio.

# 2. Ordinary sequential Palm peeling

At a residual exact-sum state `(N,M)`, select one ball uniformly from the `M` balls and remove its entire box. If that box has size `k`, the exact transition kernel is

\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{Nk\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{Mp_N(M)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the ordinary sequential size-biased permutation of boxes under the original conditioned allocation.

The retained exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
 b(N,M)-E[b(N-1,M-B)]
=
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
+
\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Unlike the auxiliary cloud-conditioned chain used in earlier runs, this process requires **no** change-of-measure correction: if the initial allocation is sampled from the microcanonical law and then boxes are removed size-biased, its initial-allocation marginal remains exactly the microcanonical law.

Fix throughout this report a small constant

\[
0<c<\min\{\delta/4,1/4\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let

\[
\tau_c
=
\inf\left\{r:\sum_{j=1}^r B_j\ge cn\right\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Before `tau_c`, the cumulative removed mass is `<cn`. Because every size-biased selected box has at least one ball,

\[
j<\tau_c\implies j<cn,
\]

and therefore

\[
N_j\ge(1-c)n.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Moreover

\[
M_j\ge m_n-cn,
\]

so, eventually,

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}\ge\lambda-c-o(1)>\nu,
\tag{2.6}
\]

while

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}\le\frac{m_n}{(1-c)n}\le\Lambda
\tag{2.7}
\]

for a fixed finite `Lambda`. Thus the density premium in (2.2) is nonnegative, indeed uniformly positive, at every pre-stop state.

# 3. Pathwise amplified-cloud hit probability

The key advantage of the ordinary size-biased permutation is that the amplified cloud can be tracked directly on the realized initial allocation.

On `G_n`, the initial mass in boxes larger than `L_n` is at least

\[
(\delta-2\varepsilon_n)n.
\]

Before `tau_c`, at most `cn` total mass has been removed, so the mass still residing in boxes larger than `L_n` is at least

\[
(\delta-2\varepsilon_n-c)n.
\]

For all large `n`, by (2.3), this is at least `delta n/2`. The residual total mass is at most `m_n\le(\lambda+1)n`. Hence, conditional on the realized allocation in `G_n` and on the entire ordinary peeling history up to a pre-stop time,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(B_{j+1}>L_n\mid Y,\mathcal F_j)
\ge p_0,
\qquad
p_0:=\frac{\delta}{2(\lambda+1)}>0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The precise constant is irrelevant; any fixed smaller positive constant works.

This lower bound is **pathwise** on `G_n`. It requires no uniform exact-sum local limit estimate at the residual state and no conditioning of the Palm selection on being in the cloud.

Let

\[
H_{\tau}:=\sum_{j<\tau_c}\mathbf1_{\{B_{j+1}>L_n\}}.
\]

Taking conditional expectations in (3.1) and then averaging over initial allocations gives

\[
E H_{\tau}
\ge
p_0 E[\tau_c\mathbf1_{G_n}]
\ge
p_0\left(E\tau_c-(cn+1)\Pr(G_n^c)\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]

By (1.9),

\[
(cn+1)\Pr(G_n^c)=o(1).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Because `tau_c>=1`, (3.2) therefore implies eventually

\[
\boxed{
E H_{\tau}\ge \frac{p_0}{2}E\tau_c.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is the bridge that was unavailable in the superseded cloud-conditioned-Palm argument: it controls a long cloud path while staying under the original microcanonical allocation marginal.

# 4. New theorem: ordinary-Palm entropy budget at the amplified scale

Define the feasible amplified fixed-law tail

\[
q_n^{\rm amp}
:=Q(L_n,m_n)
=
\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\bar q_k,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}:=\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm amp}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `q_n^{amp}<=Q(L_n)` and `L_n->infinity`, finite mean gives

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}\to\infty.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For a pre-stop state `(N_j,M_j)`, put

\[
\alpha_j:=K_{N_j,M_j}(B>L_n).
\]

Under the reference law `qbar`, the event

\[
E_j=\{L_n<B\le M_j\}
\]

has probability

\[
Q(L_n,M_j)\le Q(L_n,m_n)=q_n^{\rm amp},
\]

whereas under the Palm kernel its probability is exactly `alpha_j`, since a box cannot exceed the residual total. Binary data processing gives

\[
D(K_{N_j,M_j}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\operatorname{kl}(\alpha_j\Vert Q(L_n,M_j)).
\]

Using

\[
\operatorname{kl}(a\Vert q)
\ge
 a\log\frac1q-\log2,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
D(K_{N_j,M_j}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\alpha_jH_n^{\rm amp}-\log2.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Telescope the exact identity (2.2) to the bounded stopping time `tau_c`. The terminal exact-sum cost is nonnegative and the density premium is nonnegative. Therefore

\[
b_n
\ge
H_n^{\rm amp}E H_{\tau}
-(\log2)E\tau_c.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Combine with (3.4). Since `H_n^{amp}->infinity`, eventually

\[
\boxed{
 b_n
\ge
\frac{p_0}{4}H_n^{\rm amp}E\tau_c.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
E\tau_c
\le
\frac{4}{p_0}\frac{b_n}{H_n^{\rm amp}}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `tau_c>=1`, (4.6) also forces the automatic consistency bound

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}=O(b_n).
\tag{4.8}
\]

This theorem is unconditional: no assumption `Y_(1)/n ->p 0` has been used.

# 5. New unconditional w.h.p. lower bound for the maximum

Whenever `tau_c<=t`, the `tau_c` selected boxes carry at least `cn` mass. Hence at least one selected box has size at least

\[
\frac{cn}{t}.
\]

Every selected box belongs to the initial allocation. Therefore, from (4.7), for every deterministic sequence `A_n>=1`, Markov's inequality gives

\[
\Pr\left(
Y_{(1)}
\ge
\frac{c p_0}{4A_n}
\frac{nH_n^{\rm amp}}{b_n}
\right)
\ge
1-\frac1{A_n}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, taking

\[
A_n=\sqrt{H_n^{\rm amp}},
\]

we obtain the unconditional amplified maximum theorem

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
Y_{(1)}
\ge
c_1\frac{n}{b_n}\sqrt{H_n^{\rm amp}}
\right)
\to1,
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

for a fixed `c_1>0`.

More generally, one may take any `A_n->infinity` with `A_n=o(H_n^{amp})`, yielding a w.h.p. lower bound within an arbitrarily slowly diverging factor of

\[
\frac{nH_n^{\rm amp}}{b_n}.
\]

### Why this does not repeat the old error

An earlier superseded checkpoint obtained a superficially similar w.h.p. lower bound from the **cloud-conditioned** Palm chain, but that chain tilts the initial allocation law at every step. That claim was correctly retracted in `03-29-00Z`.

Equation (5.2) is different: it is proved entirely with the **ordinary** size-biased permutation. The initial-allocation marginal is exactly the original microcanonical law, and the amplified-cloud input enters only through the pathwise hit estimate (3.1). No repeated cloud-conditioning likelihood ratio is omitted.

# 6. Hypothetical no-condensation: quantitative mass inside the actual amplified sublinear band

Now assume only for contradiction analysis that

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Choose a deterministic high-probability cap

\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\qquad
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Enlarge it if necessary so that `U_n>=2L_n`; this preserves `U_n=o(n)` and the high-probability cap property.

Define the fixed size-biased mass in the **actual amplified band**

\[
q_n^{\rm band}
:=Q(L_n,U_n)
=
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\bar q_k.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let

\[
r_n=\left\lfloor\frac{cn}{2U_n}\right\rfloor.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Because `U_n=o(n)`,

\[
r_n\to\infty.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Stop the ordinary Palm path at

\[
\sigma_n=r_n\wedge\tau_c.
\]

On the joint high-probability initial-allocation event

\[
G_n\cap\{Y_{(1)}\le U_n\},
\]

the first `r_n` selected boxes remove at most

\[
r_nU_n\le cn/2,
\]

so `sigma_n=r_n`. Moreover every selected box is at most `U_n`, while the pathwise probability of selecting a box above `L_n` remains at least `p_0` by (3.1). Hence, if

\[
H_n^{\rm band}
:=
\sum_{j<\sigma_n}
\mathbf1_{\{L_n<B_{j+1}\le U_n\}},
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
E H_n^{\rm band}
\ge
p_0r_n(1-o(1)).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

At each pre-stop state let

\[
\beta_j=K_{N_j,M_j}(L_n<B\le U_n).
\]

Under the fixed reference law `qbar`, the same event has probability exactly `q_n^{band}`. Binary data processing yields

\[
D(K_{N_j,M_j}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\beta_j\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm band}}-\log2.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Telescope (2.2) to `sigma_n`. Using the tower identity

\[
E\sum_{j<\sigma_n}\beta_j
=E H_n^{\rm band},
\]

we obtain

\[
b_n
\ge
E H_n^{\rm band}\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm band}}
-(\log2)E\sigma_n.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Since `E sigma_n<=r_n`, (6.6) gives the quantitative theorem

\[
\boxed{
-\log Q(L_n,U_n)
\le
C\left(1+\frac{b_nU_n}{n}\right)
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

for a fixed constant `C=C(lambda,nu,c)` and all sufficiently large `n`.

Because `U_n/n->0`, this implies the sharper asymptotic localization

\[
\boxed{
-\log Q(L_n,U_n)=o(b_n).
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}
=
\exp\{-o(b_n)\}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]

This is strictly stronger than the preceding `05-32-13Z` actual-band theorem: its lower endpoint was an iterable cutoff `K_n` constrained by a uniform residual exact-sum rate. The present lower endpoint is the **amplified cloud scale**

\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n},\qquad R_n\to\infty.
\]

No uniform residual local theorem at the scale `L_n` is required.

# 7. New cap lower bound and amplified-tail closure criterion

Because

\[
Q(L_n,U_n)\le Q(L_n,m_n)=q_n^{\rm amp},
\]

we have

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}
\le
-\log Q(L_n,U_n).
\]

Combining with (6.9), any hypothetical no-condensation cap must satisfy

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}
\le
C\left(1+\frac{b_nU_n}{n}\right).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since `H_n^{amp}->infinity`, this rearranges to

\[
\boxed{
U_n
\ge
c_2\frac{n}{b_n}H_n^{\rm amp}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

for a fixed `c_2>0` eventually.

Thus a full counterexample cannot merely have its maximum cap above the basic self-consistent scale `n/b_n`; it must lie above that scale by the diverging fixed-law factor

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}
=
-\log\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\bar q_k.
\]

In particular, full no-condensation forces

\[
\boxed{
H_n^{\rm amp}=o(b_n).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Otherwise, if for some fixed `theta>0`

\[
H_n^{\rm amp}\ge\theta b_n
\]

along a subsequence, (7.2) forces every high-probability cap to be at least a fixed positive fraction of `n`, contradicting `Y_(1)/n->p0`.

Hence we obtain the new conditional closure criterion:

> **Amplified-tail criterion.** If along some subsequence
> \[
> -\log Q(L_n,m_n)\ge\theta b_n
> \]
> for a fixed `theta>0`, then full-sequence no-condensation is impossible.

The key improvement over the earlier corrected cloud-Palm criterion is that the cutoff here is the amplified scale `L_n >> n/b_n`, not a slowly iterable scale constrained by `K_n epsilon_n -> 0`.

# 8. Interaction with the record route

The retained strict-record theorem applies against every diverging sublinear cap. Therefore, under hypothetical full no-condensation and at the record-crossing subsequence, the actual cap `U_n=o(n)` still has an unbounded macroscopic record-over-cap advantage.

The new information is that the fixed size-biased law must simultaneously carry

\[
\exp\{-o(b_n)\}
\]

mass **above** the amplified threshold

\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n},\qquad R_n\to\infty,
\]

but **below** the same actual sublinear cap `U_n` used in the record comparison.

This removes a substantial part of the earlier escape space. A proposed counterexample can no longer place all of the `e^{-o(b_n)}` reference mass near microscopic/merely iterable scales while using a separate very sparse cloud at the amplified scale. The actual amplified cloud band itself must be exponentially thick on the conditioning-cost scale.

A simple corollary of (6.9) is that some support point `k_n in (L_n,U_n]` obeys

\[
-\log\bar q_{k_n}
\le
C\left(1+\frac{b_nU_n}{n}\right)+\log(U_n+1).
\tag{8.1}
\]

This follows from

\[
\max_{L_n<k\le U_n}\bar q_k
\ge
\frac{Q(L_n,U_n)}{U_n+1}.
\]

Thus every counterexample must contain genuinely cheap support atoms **inside the amplified cloud range itself**. The remaining difficulty is quantitative: the cost in (8.1) can still be much larger than `b_nL_n/n=R_n`, and the deterministic record advantage is known only to diverge, possibly arbitrarily slowly.

The next record attack should therefore use the **integrated band mass** in (6.9), rather than extracting only one atom as in (8.1). One promising deterministic target is a record-slice inequality of the following form. Partition `(L_n,U_n]` by the rate

\[
a_k=-\frac1k\log\bar q_k.
\]

Because the total band mass is at least

\[
\exp\left\{-C\left(1+b_nU_n/n\right)\right\},
\]

either:

1. a positive fraction of that mass lies in a rate slice whose per-unit cost is close to the current lower envelope, giving many cheap mesoscopic compositions; or
2. the mass is spread through many successively improving rate slices, in which case the cumulative strict-record drops across the band should be much larger than the currently used single-record advantage.

A rigorous version of this dichotomy is not yet proved here.
