## 2. Exact source target and fixed-law assumptions

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), arXiv:1112.0510, Problem 18.39.

The source target is: for one **fixed** weight sequence, in the supercritical regime
\[
0<\nu<\lambda,\qquad m_n/n\to\lambda,
\]
can
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to0\quad\text{in probability}
\]
hold along the **full admissible sequence**? Janson notes that Example 18.36 gives such behavior on a selected subsequence, conjectures that it cannot hold on the full sequence, and conjectures more strongly that some fixed positive macroscopic fraction occurs with high probability along a subsequence. Problem 18.40 begins separately at `nu=0` and is not substituted here.

For a fixed weight sequence,
\[
\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k,
\qquad
\nu=\lim_{t\uparrow\rho}\frac{t\Phi'(t)}{\Phi(t)}.
\]
Only when the finite-mean boundary normalization is valid do I write
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad E_\pi X=\nu.
\]
For fixed `n,m`, conditioning i.i.d. `X_i~pi` on `S_n=m` gives exactly the weighted-allocation law because the factor `rho^m/Phi(rho)^n` cancels on the constraint hyperplane.

## 3. Counterexample checks before transfer

Before using a literature theorem, I checked three obvious invalid transfers.

### 3.1 Integral regular variation alone is not enough for the local conditioned event

The exact event is lattice-local, `S_n=m_n`. A theorem stated only for integrated tails or nonlocal large deviations is insufficient without a local mass hypothesis. This matters because Janson's Example 18.36 shows that a fixed law with extremely irregular local support can alternate between dust-like and macroscopic behavior on different subsequences.

Accordingly, the theorem below retains the **local** mass asymptotic required by the source theorem that is actually used.

### 3.2 The Cauchy power `k^{-2}` itself is outside the finite-mean target

At exponent two with no slowly varying correction, the boundary mean diverges. Thus pure `pi_k~c k^{-2}` does not lie in the source regime `0<nu<infinity`. For the endpoint `alpha=1` theorem below, finite mean is retained as an explicit additional hypothesis; a logarithmic correction such as `(log k)^{-2}` is enough.

### 3.3 Generic subexponential conditioning does not automatically reach a linear displacement

Armendáriz–Loulakis prove a strong conditional one-big-jump statement for subexponential laws above a sequence-dependent threshold. That result is important shape information, but by itself it does not give the sublinear threshold needed to assert that every centered displacement `(lambda-nu)n` is already in the one-big-jump domain for an arbitrary irregular fixed law. Thus it does not solve Problem 18.39 universally.

These checks are the reason the theorem below is stated with the precise stable/local-regular-variation assumptions under which the linear displacement is rigorously beyond the fluctuation scale.

## 4. Method B21 — a source-shaped theorem in the conditioned-sum / zero-range literature

A fresh literature search located a theorem with exactly the missing local structure:

Quentin Berger, Matthias Birkner, Linglong Yuan, *Collective vs. individual behaviour for sums of i.i.d. random variables: appearance of the one-big-jump phenomenon*, arXiv:2303.12505v2 (10 Oct 2023), Annales de la Faculté des sciences de Toulouse, Series 6, 33(5) (2024), 1413–1485, DOI 10.5802/afst.1802.

Their Section 3.2 treats integer-valued variables in the domain of attraction of an `alpha`-stable law. Under the local regular-variation condition
\[
P(X=k)\sim p\alpha L(k)k^{-(1+\alpha)},
\tag{4.1}
\]
with `alpha in (0,2)` and `p>0`, Corollary 3.5 states that if the centered excess `x_n` satisfies `x_n/a_n -> infinity`, then conditioned on the corresponding exact lattice sum, removing the maximum leaves a vector whose law converges in total variation to the unconditioned product law on the remaining `n-1` coordinates. Their paper explicitly places this in the one-big-jump/condensation context and cites Berger's 2019 Cauchy-domain work for the endpoint mechanisms.

This is stronger and better matched to Janson 18.39 than the generic integrated-tail heuristics previously considered in this track.

## 5. New complete restricted theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — full-sequence condensation under local stable regular variation

Assume the finite-mean boundary normalization is valid and the fixed boundary law satisfies, for some
\[
\alpha\in[1,2)
\]
and some positive slowly varying function `L`,
\[
\boxed{
\pi_k\sim \alpha L(k)k^{-(1+\alpha)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Assume `E_pi X=nu<infinity`; for `alpha=1` this finite-mean condition is essential and is not automatic from (5.1).

Let `m_n` be any full admissible sequence such that
\[
\frac{m_n}{n}\to\lambda>\nu.
\]
Then under the weighted-allocation law conditioned on total `m_n`,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ \xrightarrow{p}\ \lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ \longrightarrow\ \lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus on this fixed-law subclass the frozen lower-bound metric is exactly
\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\]

### Proof

Let `X_1,X_2,...` be i.i.d. with law `pi`, and write
\[
S_n=X_1+\cdots+X_n.
\]
Because
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\]
for every configuration `y_1+...+y_n=m_n` we have
\[
\prod_{i=1}^n\pi_{y_i}
=\frac{\rho^{m_n}}{\Phi(\rho)^n}\prod_{i=1}^n w_{y_i}.
\]
The prefactor depends only on `n,m_n`. Consequently
\[
\mathcal L((Y_1,\ldots,Y_n))
=
\mathcal L((X_1,\ldots,X_n)\mid S_n=m_n).
\tag{5.4}
\]
This is the exact fixed-law conditioning identity.

By (5.1) and the discrete Karamata theorem,
\[
P(X>x)\sim L(x)x^{-\alpha}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
The left tail is absent because `X>=0`, so the stable-domain balance parameter is `p=1`. Let `a_n` be the stable normalizing sequence used in Berger–Birkner–Yuan, characterized up to asymptotic equivalence by
\[
L(a_n)a_n^{-\alpha}\asymp n^{-1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Let `b_n^{\rm st}` denote their centering. For `alpha>1`, finite mean gives
\[
b_n^{\rm st}=n\nu.
\tag{5.7}
\]
For `alpha=1`, their canonical centering is
\[
b_n^{\rm st}=nE[X\mathbf 1_{\{X\le a_n\}}].
\tag{5.8}
\]
Since `E X=nu<infinity`, monotone convergence yields
\[
\frac{b_n^{\rm st}}n\to\nu.
\tag{5.9}
\]

We next verify that the conditioning displacement is far beyond the stable scale.

If `alpha>1`, regular variation of `a_n` gives
\[
a_n=n^{1/\alpha}\ell(n)
\]
for a slowly varying `ell`, hence
\[
a_n=o(n).
\tag{5.10}
\]

If `alpha=1`, (5.5) and finite mean imply
\[
\int^\infty \frac{L(x)}x\,dx<\infty
\]
in the standard regular-variation sense, in particular `L(x)->0`. Equation (5.6) then gives
\[
\frac{a_n}{n}\asymp L(a_n)\to0,
\tag{5.11}
\]
so again `a_n=o(n)`.

Define the integer centered excess
\[
x_n:=m_n-\lfloor b_n^{\rm st}\rfloor.
\]
By `m_n/n -> lambda` and (5.7) or (5.9),
\[
\frac{x_n}{n}\to\lambda-\nu=:\delta>0.
\tag{5.12}
\]
Combining (5.10)–(5.12),
\[
\frac{x_n}{a_n}\to\infty.
\tag{5.13}
\]

All hypotheses of Berger–Birkner–Yuan Corollary 3.5 are therefore met: the stable-domain tail condition, the local mass asymptotic (5.1), and the large-deviation scale (5.13). Let `R` be their operation that removes the largest coordinate. Their conclusion gives
\[
\left\|
\mathcal L\bigl(R(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\mid S_n=m_n\bigr)
-
\pi^{\otimes(n-1)}
\right\|_{\rm TV}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.14}
\]

Under the product law on the right, the weak law of large numbers gives
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_i\xrightarrow{p}\nu.
\tag{5.15}
\]
Total-variation convergence transfers every bounded-probability event, so (5.14)–(5.15) imply that under the exact conditioning `S_n=m_n`,
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\ne i_*}X_i\xrightarrow{p}\nu,
\tag{5.16}
\]
where `i_*` is an index of a largest coordinate.

But on the conditioning event,
\[
\frac{X_{i_*}}n
=
\frac{m_n}{n}
-
\frac1n\sum_{i\ne i_*}X_i.
\]
Using (5.16) and `m_n/n -> lambda` yields
\[
\frac{X_{i_*}}n\xrightarrow{p}\lambda-\nu.
\]
By (5.4), this is exactly (5.2) for `Y_(1)`.

Finally, deterministically
\[
0\le \frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\frac{m_n}{n},
\]
and the right side is eventually bounded, say by `lambda+1`. Thus the family `Y_(1)/n` is uniformly integrable. Convergence in probability in (5.2) therefore implies convergence of expectations, proving (5.3). ∎

### Scope check

This theorem is **not** asserted for arbitrary fixed weights. The local asymptotic (5.1), stable-domain regular variation, and finite boundary mean are retained hypotheses. It is a complete PARTIAL result relative to Janson 18.39.

## 6. Method B6 — deform Janson's power law to the finite-mean Cauchy boundary

Janson's Theorem 18.33 already proves complete one-giant condensation for pure power weights `w_k~c k^{-beta}`, `beta>2`, which corresponds after boundary normalization to local regular variation with
\[
\alpha=\beta-1>1.
\]
Theorem 5.1 extends the same mechanism through slowly varying factors and, crucially, down to the finite-mean `alpha=1` boundary whenever the slowly varying factor is integrable enough to keep `nu<infinity`.

That deformation reaches the stress law from the previous run. Indeed,
\[
\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil\sim\frac{\log k}{\log2},
\]
so
\[
w_k
\sim
\frac{(\log2)^2}{k^2(\log k)^2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Since its radius is one,
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k}{\Phi(1)}
\sim
\frac{C}{k^2(\log k)^2}
\tag{6.2}
\]
for a constant `C>0`. This is exactly (5.1) with
\[
\alpha=1,
\qquad
L(k)=\frac{C}{(\log k)^2}.
\]
The boundary mean is finite because
\[
\sum_{k\ge2}k\pi_k
\asymp
\sum_{k\ge2}\frac1{k(\log k)^2}<\infty.
\]
Therefore Theorem 5.1 proves for this **single fixed stress law**, for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible `m_n/n->lambda`,
\[
\boxed{
Y_{(1)}/n\xrightarrow{p}\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E[Y_{(1)}/n]\to\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

This is a useful structural result: the previous report proved that the un-tilted Palm entropy functional can vanish on this law, while the present theorem proves that full-sequence macroscopic condensation nevertheless holds maximally. Hence any universal proof of Janson 18.39 must see a tilted/local one-big-jump feature that the raw tail-entropy ratio misses.

## 10. Remaining universal obstruction

The arbitrary fixed-law problem is now cleaner. The stress law showed that un-tilted tail entropy can be too weak; this run shows that once the local law is regular enough for stable-domain local equivalence of ensembles, full-sequence condensation follows immediately.

Thus a genuine counterexample, if one exists, has to exploit **local irregularity across scales**, not merely a slowly varying heavy tail. It must evade every linear-scale local one-big-jump theorem while maintaining finite positive boundary mean and keeping the largest conditioned box sublinear for every sufficiently large admissible `n`.

The next universal attack should therefore focus on one of two statements:

1. prove that every fixed finite-mean boundary law has infinitely many linear displacements at which a local one-big-jump lower bound is strong enough to force a macroscopic box; or
2. construct a fixed irregular law whose local convolution probabilities are genuinely collective at **every** linear supercritical displacement, not merely on selected sparse subsequences.

The previously archived nested-cap KL / critical-annulus machinery remains relevant precisely to the second possibility: it measures the entropy price of keeping many fragment sizes simultaneously competitive when no regular-variation theorem is available.
