## 4. B24 first: attack the opposite answer and identify the extremal obstruction

Before extending the proof, I tried the most obvious way to build a fixed full-sequence dust counterexample: make the support so sparse that no box of order `n` is available.

That mechanism cannot by itself work on the full sequence. Let `S=supp(w)` be any unbounded fixed support and fix `delta>0`. Choose any `a_j in S`, `a_j->infinity`, and put

\[
n_j:=\left\lceil \frac{a_j}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{a_j}{n_j}\to\delta.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus every unbounded fixed support automatically contains macroscopic supported occupancies along a subsequence tuned to its support records. Geometric holes alone can create dust on selected subsequences—as in Janson's examples—but cannot remove *all* macroscopic candidates from the full sequence.

This does **not** prove condensation, because a supported macroscopic atom can still be energetically negligible. The neighboring 18.41 cliff construction is the exact warning: carefully chosen cliffs can make one macroscopic box lose to two macroscopic boxes. But that construction still has macroscopic boxes; it refutes the stronger one-giant claim, not Problem 18.39.

So the extremal object for a possible 18.39 counterexample cannot be merely a sparse support. It must suppress every macroscopic candidate **energetically**, across all intervening scales, while preserving enough collective weight for a fragmented cap. This is the precise point where the invalid F0059 pointwise record comparison had overreached.

B24 therefore did not produce a counterexample. It narrows the shape a genuine counterexample would need: persistent energetic cliffs/plateaus, not support gaps alone.

## 5. B1: change category — from tail estimates to secant–tangent geometry

The preceding run's exact Bregman identity shows that the smooth-tail problem is better viewed as a geometric comparison between a **secant slope** and a **tangent slope**.

For an eventual hazard `h`, define

\[
r(x):=\frac{h(x)}x.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a cap at `K=tn` and a denominator giant near `k=delta n`, the relevant two slopes are

\[
s_{\rm cap}\approx r(K),
\qquad
s_{\rm tan}\approx h'(k).
\tag{5.2}
\]

The macroscopic energy gain is

\[
k[r(K)-r(k)],
\tag{5.3}
\]

while, after optimally tilting the background, the convex bulk penalty is not `n s_cap^2`, but the Bregman term

\[
(n-1)D_\psi(s_{\rm cap},s_{\rm tan})
=O\bigl(n[s_{\rm cap}-s_{\rm tan}]^2\bigr)
\tag{5.4}
\]

when the truncated background variance stays bounded.

This is the category change used below: prove that the secant advantage is first order in a small slope scale `a_n`, whereas the compensated background cost is second order, `a_n^2`.

## 6. New theorem: finite iterated-log near-exponential hazards

### Theorem 6.1

Fix an integer `q>=1`, `c>0`, and real numbers

\[
\gamma_1>0,\qquad \gamma_2,\dots,\gamma_q\in\mathbb R.
\]

For sufficiently large `x`, write

\[
\ell_1(x)=\log x,
\qquad
\ell_{j+1}(x)=\log \ell_j(x),
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
D(x):=\prod_{j=1}^q \ell_j(x)^{\gamma_j}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let a fixed full-support weight law satisfy, for every sufficiently large integer `k`,

\[
w_k=\exp\{-h(k)\},
\qquad
h(x)=\frac{c x}{D(x)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Then `rho=1`, the boundary law

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k}{\Phi(1)}
\tag{6.4}
\]

has finite moments of every order, positive finite mean `nu`, positive finite variance, and span one.

For every `lambda>nu` and every admissible integer sequence `m_n` with

\[
\frac{m_n}{n}\to\lambda,
\tag{6.5}
\]

the corresponding fixed-law random allocation satisfies

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\tag{6.6}
\]

and

\[
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\to\lambda-\nu.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu}
\tag{6.8}
\]

on the entire class (6.3).

### Proof

The proof uses the already archived tangent-compensated denominator/local-CLT machinery in Sections 7–13 of

`runs/2026-09-16/2026-09-16T20-29-08Z__report.md`

at creation commit

That archived proof contains the coefficient-positive cap inequality, the triangular-array lattice local CLT with all hypotheses checked, the exact Bregman identity, and the matching upper-tail argument. I do not re-prove those unchanged components. I verify below, from scratch, every asymptotic hypothesis that changes when (3.2) is replaced by (6.3).

#### 6.1 Boundary normalization and moments

Because every finite product of iterated logarithms is `x^{o(1)}`,

\[
\frac{h(x)}x=\frac{c}{D(x)}\to0.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus `w_k^{1/k}->1` and `rho=1`. Since `gamma_1>0`,

\[
\frac{h(x)}{\log x}
=
\frac{c x}{(\log x)D(x)}\to\infty.
\tag{6.10}
\]

Hence for every fixed `p`, eventually `h(k)>(p+3)log k`, so

\[
\sum_k k^p e^{-h(k)}<\infty.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Therefore `Phi(1)<infinity` and all moments of `pi` are finite. Full support gives span one and positive variance; the fixed positive initial weights give `nu>0`.

#### 6.2 Derivative geometry

Set

\[
r(x):=\frac{h(x)}x=\frac{c}{D(x)}
\tag{6.12}
\]

and define the logarithmic derivative

\[
\eta(x):=\frac{xD'(x)}{D(x)}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Differentiating the finite iterated-log product gives

\[
\eta(x)
=
\sum_{j=1}^q
\frac{\gamma_j}{\ell_1(x)\ell_2(x)\cdots\ell_j(x)}
=
\frac{\gamma_1}{\log x}(1+o(1)).
\tag{6.14}
\]

Thus `eta(x)>0` eventually and `eta(x)->0`. Since

\[
\frac{x r'(x)}{r(x)}=-\eta(x),
\tag{6.15}
\]

we obtain

\[
h'(x)=r(x)+x r'(x)=r(x)(1-\eta(x)).
\tag{6.16}
\]

Also `eta'(x)=-gamma_1/[x(log x)^2](1+o(1))`, and differentiating (6.16) yields

\[
h''(x)
=-\frac{r(x)\gamma_1}{x\log x}(1+o(1))<0.
\tag{6.17}
\]

So `h` is eventually increasing and concave, exactly the geometric hypothesis used by the cap-secant bound in the preceding proof.

#### 6.3 Fixed-scale secant asymptotic

Fix `a in (0,1)`. From (6.15),

\[
\log\frac{r(ax)}{r(x)}
=
\int_{ax}^{x}\eta(u)\frac{du}{u}.
\tag{6.18}
\]

Uniformly for `u in [ax,x]`, equation (6.14) gives `eta(u)=gamma_1/log x(1+o(1))`. Hence

\[
\log\frac{r(ax)}{r(x)}
=
\frac{\gamma_1\log(1/a)}{\log x}(1+o(1)).
\tag{6.19}
\]

The right-hand side tends to zero, so exponentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
r(ax)-r(x)
=
r(x)\frac{\gamma_1\log(1/a)}{\log x}(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{6.20}
\]

Combining (6.16) and (6.20),

\[
\boxed{
r(ax)-h'(x)
=
r(x)\frac{\gamma_1[1+\log(1/a)]}{\log x}(1+o(1)).
}
\tag{6.21}
\]

These are the first-order secant and secant–tangent scales.

#### 6.4 A truncation on which the background tilt is genuinely microscopic

Let

\[
R_n:=\left\lceil (\log n)^{\gamma_1/2}\right\rceil.
\tag{6.22}
\]

For every fixed real choice of the later exponents `gamma_2,...,gamma_q`,

\[
R_n r(n)
=
(\log n)^{-\gamma_1/2}
\prod_{j=2}^q\ell_j(n)^{-\gamma_j}\,c
\to0,
\tag{6.23}
\]

because a positive power of `log n` dominates every finite product of later iterated logarithms. Also `R_n=o(sqrt n)`.

At this truncation scale,

\[
h(R_n)
=
\frac{c(\log n)^{\gamma_1/2}}
     {[(\gamma_1/2)\log\log n]^{\gamma_1}
      \prod_{j=2}^q \ell_j(R_n)^{\gamma_j}}
\tag{6.24}
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{h(R_n)}{\log\log n}\to\infty.
\tag{6.25}
\]

Moreover `h(2x)/h(x)->2`. Hence eventually `h(2x)>3h(x)/2`; a dyadic decomposition yields, for every fixed `p`,

\[
\sum_{k>R_n} k^p e^{-h(k)}
\le C_p R_n^{p+1}e^{-h(R_n)}.
\tag{6.26}
\]

By (6.25), the right-hand side is smaller than every fixed negative power of `log n`.

Consequently, if

\[
\psi_n(u):=\log\sum_{k\le R_n}\pi_ke^{uk},
\tag{6.27}
\]

then uniformly for `0<=u<=C r(n)`,

\[
\psi_n'(u)=\nu+O(u)+o((\log n)^{-M}),
\tag{6.28}
\]

for every fixed `M`, and

\[
0<c_0\le\psi_n''(u)\le C_0<\infty
\tag{6.29}
\]

for all large `n`. These are exactly the truncated-background hypotheses needed by the archived tangent/local-CLT argument.

#### 6.5 Cap transform

Fix `t in (0,delta)` and put

\[
K_n=\lfloor tn\rfloor.
\tag{6.30}
\]

Define

\[
a_n:=r(n)\eta(n)
\sim
\frac{c\gamma_1}{D(n)\log n}
\tag{6.31}
\]

and choose

\[
\varepsilon_n:=\frac{a_n}{\log n},
\qquad
s_n:=r(K_n)-\varepsilon_n.
\tag{6.32}
\]

Then `epsilon_n=o(a_n)`, while

\[
n\varepsilon_n
\asymp
\frac{n}{D(n)(\log n)^2}\to\infty.
\tag{6.33}
\]

Because `h` is concave, `-h(x)+s_nx` is convex. Its right endpoint at `K_n` equals `-epsilon_n K_n`, while at `R_n` it is `-h(R_n)+o(1)`. Equations (6.24)–(6.25) and (6.33) imply `epsilon_n K_n >> h(R_n)` and

\[
\frac{e^{-h(R_n)+s_nR_n}}{\varepsilon_n}=o(a_n).
\tag{6.34}
\]

The same secant-sum estimate used in Section 9 of the preceding report therefore gives

\[
\log F_{K_n}(s_n)=\psi_n(s_n)+o(a_n),
\tag{6.35}
\]

where `F_K(s)=sum_{k<=K}pi_k e^{sk}`.

#### 6.6 Tangent-compensated denominator

Let

\[
x_n=(\lambda_n-\nu)n=\delta n+o(n),
\qquad \lambda_n=m_n/n.
\tag{6.36}
\]

The archived denominator construction solves the truncated background mean equation with a tilt `widehat theta_n` whose giant remainder `k_n` obeys

\[
\frac{k_n}{n}\to\delta
\tag{6.37}
\]

and

\[
\widehat\theta_n=h'(k_n)+o(a_n).
\tag{6.38}
\]

Here is the only changed hypothesis check. Under a tilt of order `r(n)`, (6.28) changes the background mean by `O(r(n))`, so the candidate giant differs from `x_n` by `O(nr(n))=o(n)`. On such a relative `1+o(1)` window, the explicit iterated-log formulas imply

\[
h'(x_n+O(nr(n)))=h'(x_n)+o(a_n).
\tag{6.39}
\]

Indeed (6.17) gives a derivative of order `a_n/n`, so multiplying by `O(nr(n))` gives `O(a_nr(n))=o(a_n)`. The integer rounding error in the mean equation changes the tilt by `O(1/n)=o(a_n)` because

\[
n a_n\asymp\frac{n}{D(n)\log n}\to\infty.
\tag{6.40}
\]

Thus (6.38) follows with the same intermediate-value and local-CLT argument already proved in the preceding report.

The exact denominator identity from that report is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&m_ns_n-n\psi_n(s_n)

\Bigl[h(k_n)+(n-1)(\widehat\theta_n\widehat\mu_n-\psi_n(\widehat\theta_n))\Bigr]

\end{aligned}
\tag{6.41}
\]

where

\[
D_{\psi_n}(s,\theta)
=\psi_n(s)-\psi_n(\theta)-\psi_n'(\theta)(s-\theta)\ge0.
\]

By (6.29),

\[
D_{\psi_n}(s,\theta)\le C(s-\theta)^2.
\tag{6.42}
\]

#### 6.7 First-order chord gain versus second-order background penalty

Put `a=t/delta in (0,1)`. Since `K_n/k_n->a`, (6.20) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
k_n[r(K_n)-r(k_n)]
&=
\left[c\delta\gamma_1\log(\delta/t)+o(1)\right]
\frac{n}{D(n)\log n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.43}
\]

This tends to `+infinity`. The epsilon subtraction costs only

\[
k_n\varepsilon_n
=O\left(\frac{n}{D(n)(\log n)^2}\right)
=o\left(\frac{n}{D(n)\log n}\right).
\tag{6.44}
\]

By (6.21), (6.32), and (6.38),

\[
s_n-\widehat\theta_n
=
O\left(\frac{1}{D(n)\log n}ight).
\tag{6.45}
\]

Therefore

\[
(n-1)D_{\psi_n}(s_n,\widehat\theta_n)
=O\left(\frac{n}{D(n)^2(\log n)^2}\right)
=o\left(\frac{n}{D(n)\log n}\right).
\tag{6.46}
\]

The cap-transform replacement error from (6.35), multiplied by `n`, is also `o(n/[D(n)log n])`, and the `O(log n)` local-CLT prefactor is negligible on this scale. Combining the cap inequality, the denominator lower bound, and (6.41)–(6.46) yields

\[
P(Y_{(1)}\le tn)\to0
\qquad\text{for every fixed }t<\delta.
\tag{6.47}
\]

All probabilities here and below are under the weighted-allocation law, equivalently the iid law conditioned on `S_n=m_n`.

#### 6.8 Matching upper tail

For fixed `epsilon>0`, the event

\[
Y_{(1)}>(\delta+\epsilon)n
\]

forces the other `n-1` coordinates to have average below `nu-epsilon/2` for all large `n`. The denominator has probability at least `e^{-o(n)}` because `h(delta n)=o(n)` and an ordinary lattice local-CLT background plus one macroscopic coordinate supplies an explicit configuration. Hence

\[
P(Y_{(1)}>(\delta+\epsilon)n)\to0.
\tag{6.48}

Together, (6.47) and (6.48) prove (6.6). Since

\[
0\le Y_{(1)}/n\le m_n/n=O(1),
\]

convergence in probability implies convergence of expectations, proving (6.7)–(6.8). `square`

## 7. B29: add the point at infinity — the compactified slope profile

The preceding calculation has a clean asymptotic quotient that survives after all lower-order iterated logarithms are removed.

Define

\[
A(x):=r(x)\eta(x).
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then for every fixed `u in (0,1)`, equations (6.14) and (6.20) give

\[
\boxed{
\frac{r(ux)-r(x)}{A(x)}\to\log(1/u).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Moreover (6.16) gives the exact identity

\[
\frac{r(x)-h'(x)}{A(x)}=1.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{r(ux)-h'(x)}{A(x)}\to1+\log(1/u).
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This is the compactified secant–tangent profile of the entire iterated-log class. The macroscopic chord gain is of order

\[
xA(x)\log(1/u),
\tag{7.5}
\]

whereas the optimally compensated background cost is only

\[
xA(x)^2[1+\log(1/u)]^2.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Because `A(x)->0` while `xA(x)->infinity`, the latter is negligible relative to the former.

This suggests a broader next route for irregular weights: compactify the rate envelope around macroscopic record scales and ask whether any subsequential profile has a non-flat secant–subgradient limit. If it does, the same first-order-versus-second-order mechanism should create a macroscopic subsequence. A putative full-dust counterexample would therefore have to evade not just a pointwise record gap but every useful compactified **window/subgradient** profile. This is a research direction, not claimed here as a universal theorem.
