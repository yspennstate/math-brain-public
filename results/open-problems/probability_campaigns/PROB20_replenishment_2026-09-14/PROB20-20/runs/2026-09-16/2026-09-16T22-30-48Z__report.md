## 4. B10: construct the hostile fixed law explicitly before proving anything

The previous smooth-tail work suggests that a true counterexample, if one exists, must use energetic cliffs across scales. Instead of hiding those cliffs inside an abstract envelope, B10 constructs a fixed law out of widely separated macroscopic windows.

Fix constants

\[
\eta>0,
\qquad
\kappa\in(0,1).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
1<K_1<K_2<\cdots,
\qquad
h_1>h_2>\cdots>0
\tag{4.2}
\]

and define integer windows

\[
I_j:=\{K_j,K_j+1,\dots,\lfloor(1+\eta)K_j\rfloor\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Assume

\[
\frac{K_{j+1}}{K_j}\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{4.4}
\]

and a geometric record-rate drop

\[
h_j\le(1-\kappa)h_{j-1}
\qquad(j\ge2).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Define one fixed weight sequence by

\[
w_0=w_1=1,
\qquad
w_k=e^{-h_jk}\quad(k\in I_j),
\qquad
w_k=0\quad\text{otherwise for }k\ge2.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is a single law fixed once and for all. It does **not** vary with `n`.

For the proof we impose three explicit summability/separation conditions:

\[
\sum_{j\ge1}K_j^3e^{-(\kappa/2)h_jK_j}<\infty,
\tag{4.7}
\]

\[
T_j:=\sum_{i\ge j}K_i e^{-h_iK_i}=o(h_{j-1}),
\tag{4.8}
\]

and

\[
\frac{K_jh_{j-1}}{\log K_j}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{4.9}
\]

These hypotheses are deliberately stronger than necessary; they keep every local-limit and truncation step explicit and noncircular.

A concrete example is

\[
\eta=\frac12,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac12,
\qquad
h_j=2^{-j},
\qquad
K_j=\left\lceil e^{j^3}\right\rceil.
\tag{4.10}
\]

For this example (4.4), (4.7), (4.8), and (4.9) hold immediately from the superexponential growth of `K_j` and the fact that `2^{-j}e^{j^3}` dominates every polynomial/exponential function of `j`.

### Counterexample-first outcome

The construction is intentionally hostile to one-big-jump regularity: there are huge support gaps, abrupt rate drops, and no local regular variation across windows. The first attempt was to use these same windows to force full-sequence dust. That attempt fails for a structural reason proved below: every new cheaper window creates an interior scale at which one macroscopic coordinate can be paid at the new rate while every fragmented competitor confined to older windows pays the previous, strictly larger rate.

So B10 does not yield a counterexample. It yields a new positive theorem for precisely the kind of irregular law that the previous smooth methods did not cover.

## 5. Basic properties of the sparse-window law

Let

\[
\Phi(1)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k.
\]

By (4.7),

\[
\sum_{k\in I_j}w_k
\le C K_j e^{-h_jK_j},
\]

and the series over `j` converges. Thus

\[
0<\Phi(1)<\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Moreover, along `k=K_j`,

\[
w_{K_j}^{1/K_j}=e^{-h_j}\to1,
\]

while `w_k<=1`; hence the radius of convergence is

\[
\rho=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Normalize

\[
\pi_k:=\frac{w_k}{\Phi(1)}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Condition (4.7) is stronger than

\[
\sum_jK_j^3e^{-h_jK_j}<\infty,
\]

so `pi` has finite second moment. Since `w_1>0`, its mean

\[
0<\nu:=E_\pi X<\infty
\tag{5.4}
\]

and its variance satisfies

\[
0<\sigma^2:=\operatorname{Var}_\pi X<\infty.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because both 0 and 1 have positive mass, the lattice span is one. Therefore the ordinary span-one finite-variance local central limit theorem applies to sums of iid `pi` variables.

The support is unbounded, so Janson's `omega=infinity` hypothesis for Lemma 18.31 holds.

## 6. B13: the decreasing record rate is the monovariant

The scale parameter

\[
h_1>h_2>\cdots
\]

is the monovariant. A new window is exponentially cheaper per particle than every older window by a fixed fractional gap.

For `j>=2` define the old-window test tilt

\[
s_j:=\left(1-\frac\kappa2\right)h_{j-1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

By (4.5),

\[
s_j-h_j
\ge
\frac\kappa2h_{j-1}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus the new window has a strictly lower per-particle rate than the strongest tilt that still leaves every older window exponentially summable.

Define the truncated moment generating function

\[
F_j(s):=E_\pi[e^{sX};X<K_j].
\tag{6.3}
\]

The key technical lemma is the exact first-order expansion

\[
\boxed{
\log F_j(s_j)=\nu s_j+o(s_j).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof of (6.4)

Write

\[
F_j(s_j)-1
=
\sum_{k<K_j}\pi_k(e^{s_jk}-1)-P(X\ge K_j).
\tag{6.5}
\]

For a point `k` in an older window `I_i`, `i<j`, monotonicity of the `h_i` and (6.1) give

\[
s_j\le\left(1-\frac\kappa2\right)h_i.
\]

Hence

\[
0\le
\pi_k\frac{e^{s_jk}-1}{s_j}
\le
C k e^{-(\kappa/2)h_i k}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Summing (6.6) over `I_i` yields a bound

\[
C K_i^2 e^{-(\kappa/2)h_iK_i},
\]

which is summable by (4.7). For each fixed supported `k`, eventually `k<K_j` and

\[
\frac{e^{s_jk}-1}{s_j}\to k.
\]

Dominated convergence therefore gives

\[
\frac1{s_j}
\sum_{k<K_j}\pi_k(e^{s_jk}-1)
\longrightarrow
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For the missing tail, the window definition gives

\[
P(X\ge K_j)
\le
C\sum_{i\ge j}K_i e^{-h_iK_i}
=C T_j=o(h_{j-1})=o(s_j)
\tag{6.8}
\]

by (4.8). Combining (6.5)–(6.8),

\[
F_j(s_j)=1+\nu s_j+o(s_j).
\]

Since `s_j->0`, taking a logarithm proves (6.4). `square`

### Why weaker monotonicity is not enough

This step was deliberately counterexample-tested conceptually. Merely asking `h_j` to decrease does not suffice: if the rate gap `s_j-h_j` shrinks on the same scale as the polynomial placement entropy, the comparison below can be inconclusive. Conditions (4.5) and (4.9) are therefore retained explicitly; they are not silently discarded after the proof.

## 7. Main theorem: persistent one-giant subsequences for sparse record windows

### Theorem 7.1 — sparse record-window persistence

Let `w` be the one fixed weight law (4.6) satisfying (4.4)–(4.9), and let `nu` be its finite positive boundary mean. Fix any

\[
\lambda>\nu
\]

and any full admissible integer sequence `m_n` with

\[
\frac{m_n}{n}\to\lambda.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Then there exists a deterministic subsequence `n_j->infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\xrightarrow{p}\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n=\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

In particular, Janson's stronger persistent-condensation alternative holds for this class: for every `epsilon<lambda-nu`, along the same subsequence

\[
P(Y_{(1)}\ge\epsilon n_j)\to1.
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof

Fix once and for all

\[
0<c<\min\{\eta,1\}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Put

\[
n_j:=\left\lfloor\frac{(1+c)K_j}{\delta}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\delta=\lambda-\nu.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Because `m_n/n->lambda`, this is a deterministic subsequence of the given full sequence.

#### 7.1 A supported interior denominator giant

Choose an integer `r_j` nearest to

\[
\nu(n_j-1).
\tag{7.7}
\]

The span-one finite-variance local CLT gives

\[
P(S_{n_j-1}=r_j)
=\frac{1+o(1)}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi n_j}}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

Define

\[
k_j:=m_{n_j}-r_j.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{k_j}{n_j}\to\lambda-\nu=\delta
\]

and, by (7.6),

\[
\frac{k_j}{K_j}\to1+c.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Since `0<c<eta`, (7.10) implies that `k_j` lies strictly inside `I_j` for all sufficiently large `j`. In particular,

\[
\pi_{k_j}=\frac{e^{-h_jk_j}}{\Phi(1)}>0.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Using the configuration in which coordinate 1 has size `k_j` and the remaining coordinates sum to `r_j`,

\[
P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})
\ge
\pi_{k_j}P(S_{n_j-1}=r_j)
\ge
c_0 n_j^{-1/2}e^{-h_jk_j}
\tag{7.12}
\]

for a constant `c_0>0` and all large `j`.

Because `h_j->0` and `k_j=Theta(n_j)`, the denominator in (7.12) is `e^{-o(n_j)}`.

Condition (4.4) also implies

\[
K_{j+1}>m_{n_j}
\tag{7.13}
\]

for all large `j`. Hence no future window is even arithmetically available at total mass `m_{n_j}`. Every supported coordinate at least `K_j` must lie in the current window `I_j`.

#### 7.2 Zero current-window coordinates are negligible

Let

\[
R_j:=|\{i:X_i\ge K_j\}|.
\tag{7.14}
\]

On `R_j=0`, all coordinates are below `K_j`. Chernoff's inequality at the old-window tilt `s_j` gives

\[
P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j},R_j=0)
\le
 e^{-s_jm_{n_j}}F_j(s_j)^{n_j}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Divide by the denominator lower bound (7.12). Since

\[
m_{n_j}=k_j+r_j,
\qquad
r_j=\nu(n_j-1)+O(1),
\]

and (6.4) holds,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j},R_j=0)}{P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})}
&\le
-(s_j-h_j)k_j
+o(n_js_j)
+O(\log n_j).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.16}
\]

By (6.2), `s_j-h_j` is a fixed positive fraction of `h_{j-1}`, while `n_j=Theta(K_j)`. Condition (4.9) therefore implies that the right-hand side of (7.16) tends to `-infinity`. Thus

\[
P(R_j=0\mid S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})\to0.
\tag{7.17}
\]

#### 7.3 Two or more current-window coordinates are negligible

If `R_j>=2`, two coordinates contribute at least `2K_j`. From (7.6),

\[
\frac{2K_j}{n_j}\to\frac{2\delta}{1+c}.
\]

Since `c<1`,

\[
\lambda-\frac{2\delta}{1+c}
=
\nu-\delta\frac{1-c}{1+c}
<\nu.
\tag{7.18}
\]

Hence there is a fixed `a>0` such that, for all large `j`, after any two coordinates of size at least `K_j` are removed, the remaining `n_j-2` variables must sum to at most `(nu-a)(n_j-2)`.

For nonnegative iid variables of mean `nu`, a negative exponential Chernoff bound gives

\[
P(S_{n_j-2}\le(\nu-a)(n_j-2))\le e^{-c_a n_j}
\tag{7.19}
\]

for some `c_a>0`. Taking a union bound over the possible pair of exceptional coordinates,

\[
P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j},R_j\ge2)
\le n_j^2e^{-c_a n_j}.
\tag{7.20}
\]

The denominator (7.12) is only `e^{-o(n_j)}`. Therefore

\[
P(R_j\ge2\mid S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})\to0.
\tag{7.21}
\]

Thus the conditioning asymptotically forces **exactly one** coordinate into the new cheap window.

#### 7.4 That unique current-window coordinate cannot be macroscopically too small

Fix any

\[
0<t<\delta.
\tag{7.22}
\]

Consider the event

\[
A_j(t):=\{S_{n_j}=m_{n_j},\ R_j=1,\ Y_{(1)}\le t n_j\}.
\]

If the unique current-window coordinate equals `q`, then `q<=tn_j` on this event and the other coordinates are below `K_j`. Hence

\[
P(A_j(t))
\le
n_j\sum_{\substack{q\in I_j\\q\le t n_j}}
\pi_q e^{-s_j(m_{n_j}-q)}F_j(s_j)^{n_j-1}.
\tag{7.23}
\]

On the current window,

\[
\frac{\pi_q}{\pi_{k_j}}
=e^{-h_j(q-k_j)}.
\tag{7.24}
\]

Using (7.12), (6.4), and `r_j=nu(n_j-1)+O(1)`, each summand in (7.23), after division by the denominator, has logarithm at most

\[
-(s_j-h_j)(k_j-q)
+o(n_js_j)
+O(\log n_j).
\tag{7.25}
\]

Uniformly over `q<=tn_j`,

\[
k_j-q\ge(\delta-t+o(1))n_j.
\tag{7.26}
\]

There are only `O(K_j)` possible values of `q`, producing another `O(log K_j)` entropy term. By (6.2), (4.9), and (7.26), the negative first term in (7.25) dominates both the `o(n_js_j)` error and all logarithmic placement/support entropy. Therefore

\[
P(A_j(t)\mid S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})\to0.
\tag{7.27}
\]

Combining (7.17), (7.21), and (7.27),

\[
P(Y_{(1)}\le t n_j\mid S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})\to0
\qquad(0<t<\delta).
\tag{7.28}
\]

#### 7.5 Matching upper tail

Fix `epsilon>0`. If

\[
Y_{(1)}>(\delta+\epsilon)n_j,
\]

then, for large `j`, after removing that coordinate the remaining `n_j-1` variables must have average at most `nu-epsilon/2`. A negative exponential Chernoff bound and a union bound over the exceptional coordinate give

\[
P(S_{n_j}=m_{n_j},Y_{(1)}>(\delta+\epsilon)n_j)
\le
n_je^{-c_\epsilon n_j}.
\tag{7.29}
\]

Again divide by (7.12), which is `e^{-o(n_j)}`. Hence

\[
P(Y_{(1)}>(\delta+\epsilon)n_j\mid S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})\to0.
\tag{7.30}
\]

Equations (7.28) and (7.30) prove

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\xrightarrow p\delta.
\]

This proves (7.2).

Because `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`, convergence in probability along the subsequence implies

\[
E[Y_{(1)}/n_j]\to\delta.
\tag{7.31}
\]

Finally, Janson's Lemma 18.31 gives the universal upper bound (2.5), and boundedness upgrades it to

\[
\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]\le\delta.
\tag{7.32}
\]

Together with (7.31),

\[
\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]=\delta.
\]

This proves (7.3) and completes the theorem. `square`

## 8. B25: count the same exact-sum event twice

The proof's core is an honest two-count comparison of the **same** event `{S_n=m}`.

The lower count is one current-window giant plus an ordinary local-CLT background:

\[
P(S_n=m)
\ge
\pi_{k_j}P(S_{n-1}=r_j).
\tag{8.1}
\]

The competing capped count is the exact positive exponential transform

\[
P(S_n=m,\max X_i<K_j)
\le
 e^{-s_jm}F_j(s_j)^n.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Because the new window drops the rate by a fixed fraction while `F_j` still has only the old mean `nu` at first order, the log-ratio between (8.2) and (8.1) contains

\[
-(s_j-h_j)\delta n_j
\]

to first order. This diverges negatively by (4.9).

This is deliberately different from the invalid F0059 route. No pointwise record-rate condition is asserted to be necessary for every law, and no comparison is imported from Problem 18.40. The theorem uses a **sufficient** energetic gap in a specific fixed-law class and checks exactly where polynomial placement entropy enters.

### Counterexample checks against B25

Three possible failures were tested before promoting the argument:

1. **Window boundary problem.** If the denominator giant landed at a boundary, small changes in `m_n` could put it outside the support. This is why (7.6) uses a fixed interior factor `1+c`, with `0<c<eta`.
2. **Future-window leakage.** If the next cheaper window were already arithmetically accessible at total mass `m_n`, it could invalidate the capped transform. This is why (4.4) is retained and used explicitly in (7.13).
3. **Two cheap giants.** A second current-window coordinate might beat the one-giant reference by entropy. Choosing `c<1` makes two coordinates of size at least `K_j` overshoot the supercritical excess and forces an exponentially unlikely lower deviation in the background, yielding (7.20).

These are theorem hypotheses/choices, not post-hoc omissions.

## 9. What this says about sparse-scale gluing

The earlier B30 record-Gibbs identity showed that a pointwise record gap can be cancelled by entropy across many near-envelope fragment sizes. The present theorem identifies a complementary regime where that cancellation cannot persist.

A new window with a **fixed fractional decrease in per-particle rate** creates a scale at which:

- zero new-window fragments lose by the Chernoff/free-energy gap;
- two or more new-window fragments force a linear lower deviation in the background;
- exactly one new-window fragment, if substantially smaller than the missing mass, loses by the same rate gap times the unfilled macroscopic distance.

Thus sparse windows do not glue into full-sequence dust when each new record scale has a genuine geometric rate drop and enough separation. A putative counterexample to Janson 18.39 must evade this mechanism too. In particular, if it is built from sparse scales, its record-rate improvements must become asymptotically **flat** relative to all accessible macroscopic windows, or the transition windows must overlap strongly enough that the previous-window tilt cannot be isolated.

This is a sharper obstruction than “support gaps are insufficient.” It says exactly which energetic sparse-window construction still forces the source's persistent macroscopic subsequence.
