## 4. Retained setup

Assume the finite-mean boundary normalization is valid:

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad E_\pi X=\nu,
\qquad 0<\nu<\lambda,
\]

and let

\[
S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i,\qquad
B_n=-\log\Pr(S_n=m_n),\qquad
\delta=\lambda-\nu.
\]

Write the size-biased boundary law

\[
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}.
\]

For every full admissible sequence `m_n/n -> lambda`, the checked amplified-cloud construction supplies deterministic cutoffs `L_n -> infinity` (indeed one may take the previously constructed `L_n=R_n n/B_n(1+o(1))`) and `epsilon_n -> 0` such that

\[
G_n:=\left\{
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
\ge(\delta-2\epsilon_n)n
\right\}
\tag{4.1}
\]

satisfies

\[
n\Pr(G_n^c\mid S_n=m_n)\to0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For such a cutoff define

\[
Q_n(L)=\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\bar q_k,
\qquad
H_n(L)=-\log Q_n(L).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Because `bar q` is a probability law and `L_n -> infinity`, `H_n(L)->infinity`.

The retained sequence-specific and law-level tail-entropy functionals are

## 5. New deterministic path lemma: integrate the residual cloud fraction

Run the **ordinary** sequential size-biased peeling process from the conditioned allocation: at each step select one residual ball uniformly and remove its entire box. Let `Z_{j+1}` be the size of the next selected box and

\[
x_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^jZ_i
\]

be the removed mass fraction before step `j+1`. Fix

\[
0<c<\delta,
\qquad
\tau_c=\inf\{j:x_j\ge c\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
y_n=Y_{(1)}/n.
\]

On `G_n`, before `tau_c`, even if every already removed ball came from an `L_n`-large box, the residual amplified-cloud mass is at least

\[
(\delta-2\epsilon_n-x_j)n.
\]

The residual total mass is

\[
(\lambda_n-x_j)n,
\qquad \lambda_n=m_n/n.
\]

Hence, conditional on the **full initial allocation and the peeling history**, the next box is larger than `L_n` with probability at least

\[
g_n(x_j)
:=
\frac{\delta-2\epsilon_n-x_j}{\lambda_n-x_j}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, `g_n` is positive and decreasing on `[0,c]`, and uniformly there

\[
g_n(x)\longrightarrow
 g(x):=\frac{\delta-x}{\lambda-x}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Each path increment satisfies

\[
x_{j+1}-x_j=Z_{j+1}/n\le y_n.
\]

Since `g_n` is decreasing,

\[
\int_{x_j}^{\min\{x_{j+1},c\}}g_n(u)\,du
\le
 g_n(x_j)(x_{j+1}-x_j)
\le
 y_n g_n(x_j).
\tag{5.4}
\]

The intervals in (5.4), for `j<tau_c`, tile `[0,c]`. Therefore, **pathwise on `G_n`**,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j<\tau_c}g_n(x_j)
\ge
\frac{F_n(c)}{y_n},
\qquad
F_n(c):=\int_0^c g_n(u)\,du.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Uniform convergence gives

\[
F_n(c)\to F(c):=\int_0^c\frac{\delta-x}{\lambda-x}\,dx.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is the new ingredient. The preceding report replaced the whole path by the worst endpoint value `c g(c)`; (5.5) preserves the full decreasing cloud profile.

## 6. New theorem: integrated ordinary-Palm expectation bound

### Theorem 6.1

For every fixed `0<c<delta` and every certified amplified cutoff sequence,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
F(c)\limsup_n\frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\bigl[\lambda-\nu-\nu\log(\lambda/\nu)\bigr]
\limsup_n\frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Let `N_c` be the number of selected boxes exceeding `L_n` before `tau_c`. The full-allocation filtration from Section 5 gives, on `G_n`,

\[
E[N_c\mid Y]
=
E\left[\sum_{j<\tau_c}
\Pr(Z_{j+1}>L_n\mid Y,\mathcal F_j)\,\middle|\,Y\right]
\ge
E\left[\sum_{j<\tau_c}g_n(x_j)\,\middle|\,Y\right].
\]

By the pathwise inequality (5.5),

\[
\boxed{
E N_c\ge F_n(c)E\left[\frac{\mathbf1_{G_n}}{y_n}\right].
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Now pass to the coarser exact-sum state filtration. At a residual state `(N,M)`, the ordinary Palm kernel is

\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{Nk\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{Mp_N(M)},
\tag{6.4}
\]

and the already checked exact identity is

\[
b(N,M)-E[b(N-1,M-Z)]
=
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
+
\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Let

\[
\alpha_j=K_{N_j,M_j}(Z>L_n).
\]

The `bar q`-probability of the feasible event `L_n<Z<=M_j` is at most `Q_n(L)=e^{-H_n}`. Binary data processing therefore gives

\[
D(K_{N_j,M_j}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\alpha_jH_n-\log2.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Before `tau_c`,

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}
\ge
\frac{m_n-cn}{n}
=
\lambda-c+o(1)>\nu,
\]

so the density premium in (6.5) is nonnegative. Telescoping to the bounded stopping time `tau_c`, dropping the nonnegative terminal exact-sum cost, and using the tower identity

\[
E\sum_{j<\tau_c}\alpha_j=EN_c,
\]

gives

\[
B_n\ge H_n EN_c-(\log2)E\tau_c.
\tag{6.7}
\]

We now absorb the binary-entropy loss without discarding the path profile. On `G_n`, for every pre-stop state,

\[
\Pr(Z_{j+1}>L_n\mid Y,\mathcal F_j)
\ge
p_{c,n},
\qquad
p_{c,n}:=
\frac{\delta-2\epsilon_n-c}{\lambda_n-c}
\longrightarrow
p_c:=\frac{\delta-c}{\lambda-c}>0.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
EN_c\ge p_{c,n}E[\tau_c\mathbf1_{G_n}],
\]

while `tau_c<=n`. Hence

\[
E\tau_c
\le
\frac{EN_c}{p_{c,n}}+n\Pr(G_n^c).
\tag{6.9}
\]

Insert (6.9) into (6.7):

\[
B_n
\ge
\left(H_n-\frac{\log2}{p_{c,n}}\right)EN_c
-(\log2)n\Pr(G_n^c).
\tag{6.10}
\]

By (4.2), the last term is `o(1)`. Since `H_n->infinity`, combining (6.10) with (6.3) yields

\[
B_n+o(1)
\ge
\bigl(H_n-O_c(1)\bigr)F_n(c)
E\left[\frac{\mathbf1_{G_n}}{y_n}\right].
\tag{6.11}
\]

Finally, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\Pr(G_n)^2
=
\left(E\left[\sqrt{y_n}\,
\frac{\mathbf1_{G_n}}{\sqrt{y_n}}\right]\right)^2
\le
E[y_n]\,
E\left[\frac{\mathbf1_{G_n}}{y_n}\right].
\tag{6.12}
\]

Here `y_n>0` on every admissible supercritical allocation. Since `Pr(G_n)->1`, (6.11)–(6.12), `F_n(c)->F(c)`, and `H_n->infinity` imply

\[
E[y_n]
\ge
(1-o(1))F(c)\frac{H_n}{B_n}.
\]

Taking limsups proves (6.1).

It remains to evaluate and optimize the integral. Since

\[
\frac{\delta-x}{\lambda-x}
=1-\frac{\nu}{\lambda-x},
\]

\[
F(c)
=c-\nu\log\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-c}.
\tag{6.13}
\]

Its derivative is

\[
F'(c)=\frac{\delta-c}{\lambda-c}>0
\qquad(0<c<\delta).
\]

Thus, taking fixed `c` arbitrarily close to `delta` after the `n->infinity` limit,

\[
\sup_{0<c<\delta}F(c)
=
F(\delta)
=
\boxed{\lambda-\nu-\nu\log(\lambda/\nu)}.
\tag{6.14}
\]

This proves (6.2). `square`
