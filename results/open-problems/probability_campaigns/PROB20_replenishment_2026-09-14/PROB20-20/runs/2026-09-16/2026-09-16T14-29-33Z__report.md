## 4. Fixed metric and notation

Assume the finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, as in the checked parent reports:

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu,
\qquad
0<\nu<\lambda.
\]

Let

\[
S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i,
\qquad
p_n=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad
B_n=-\log p_n,
\]

where `m_n/n -> lambda` through admissible values. Put

\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0,
\qquad
\bar q_k:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},\quad k\ge1.
\]

The checked amplified-cloud theorem provides at least one deterministic cutoff sequence `L_n -> infinity` such that, for some `epsilon_n -> 0`, with

\[
G_n:=\left\{
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
\ge(\delta-2\epsilon_n)n
\right\},
\]

one has

\[
n\Pr(G_n^c\mid S_n=m_n)\to0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

(The parent construction in fact has `L_n=R_n n/B_n(1+o(1))` with `R_n -> infinity` and `R_n=o(B_n)`.)

For such an amplified cutoff define its feasible fixed size-biased tail and its entropy cost by

\[
Q_n(L):=\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}\bar q_k,
\qquad
H_n(L):=-\log Q_n(L).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Finite mean and `L_n -> infinity` give `H_n(L) -> infinity`.

The revised contract asks for the strongest proved lower-bound function for

\[
\limsup_n E[Y_{(1)}/n].
\]

The next theorem converts `(4.2)` into exactly such a lower bound.

## 5. New theorem: sharp stopping/Jensen conversion of amplified tail entropy into the expectation metric

### Theorem 5.1

For every fixed stopping-mass fraction

\[
0<c<\delta,
\]

and every amplified cutoff sequence satisfying (4.1),

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{c(\delta-c)}{\lambda-c}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Optimizing over `c` gives the explicit coefficient

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0<c<\delta}\frac{c(\delta-c)}{\lambda-c}
=(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt\nu)^2.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt\nu)^2
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This is unconditional within the checked amplified-cloud scope: no assumption `Y_(1)/n ->p 0` is used.

### Proof

Run the **ordinary** sequential size-biased peeling process from the conditioned allocation. At a residual exact-sum state `(N,M)`, one ball is selected uniformly among the `M` residual balls and its entire box is removed. If the selected box has size `k`, the exact Palm kernel is

\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{Nk\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{Mp_N(M)}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The checked exact one-step identity is

\[
 b(N,M)-E[b(N-1,M-B)]
=
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
+
\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let `C_j` be the cumulative mass removed after `j` box removals and stop at

\[
\tau_c:=\inf\{j:C_j\ge cn\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Because `c<delta<lambda`, the stop occurs before all mass is removed. Also `tau_c<=n` deterministically.

On `G_n`, before the stop, if `C_j=xn<cn`, then the mass still contained in boxes larger than `L_n` is at least

\[
(\delta-2\epsilon_n-x)n.
\]

The residual total mass is exactly

\[
M_j=m_n-xn=(\lambda-x+o(1))n.
\]

Therefore the conditional probability that the next ordinary Palm-selected box lies above `L_n` is bounded **pathwise** by

\[
\alpha_j
\ge
\frac{\delta-2\epsilon_n-x}{\lambda_n-x},
\qquad
\lambda_n:=m_n/n.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The function

\[
x\mapsto\frac{\delta-x}{\lambda-x}
\]

is decreasing because its derivative equals `-nu/(lambda-x)^2`. Hence uniformly at every pre-stop time on `G_n`,

\[
\alpha_j\ge p_c-o(1),
\qquad
p_c:=\frac{\delta-c}{\lambda-c}>0.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Let

\[
N_c:=\sum_{j<\tau_c}\mathbf1_{\{B_{j+1}>L_n\}}
\]

be the number of amplified-cloud boxes hit before stopping. Since `tau_c<=n`, (4.1) and (5.8) imply

\[
E N_c
\ge
(p_c-o(1))E\tau_c-O(n\Pr(G_n^c)).
\tag{5.9}
\]

We next control the factor `H_n(L)` multiplying the small error. At the initial state, let `alpha_0` be the Palm probability of selecting a box above `L_n`. Since a uniform ball lands in such a box with probability equal to the fraction of total mass in the cloud, (4.1) gives

\[
\alpha_0\ge\frac{\delta-o(1)}\lambda.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Binary data processing applied to (5.5) at the initial state gives

\[
B_n\ge D(K_{n,m_n}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\alpha_0H_n(L)-\log2.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Thus

\[
H_n(L)=O(B_n).
\tag{5.12}
\]

At any pre-stop state, the reference probability under `bar q` of the feasible event `L_n<B<=M_j` is at most `Q_n(L)`. Hence the same binary-KL inequality gives

\[
D(K_{N_j,M_j}\Vert\bar q)
\ge
\alpha_j H_n(L)-\log2.
\tag{5.13}
\]

Moreover the residual density stays supercritical: before stopping,

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}
\ge
\frac{m_n-cn}{n}
=
\lambda-c+o(1)>\nu,
\tag{5.14}
\]

so the density-premium term in (5.5) is nonnegative. Telescoping (5.5) to `tau_c`, dropping the nonnegative terminal exact-sum cost and density premiums, yields

\[
B_n
\ge
H_n(L)E N_c-(\log2)E\tau_c.
\tag{5.15}
\]

Insert (5.9). By (4.1) and (5.12),

\[
H_n(L)\,n\Pr(G_n^c)=o(B_n).
\]

Because `H_n(L)->infinity`, equations (5.9)–(5.15) give

\[
E\tau_c
\le
(1+o(1))\frac{B_n}{p_cH_n(L)}.
\tag{5.16}
\]

Now use a deterministic fact that was not exploited sharply in the earlier checkpoint. At the stopping time, the selected boxes together contain at least `cn` balls, so one of those `tau_c` boxes contains at least `cn/tau_c` balls. Every selected box belongs to the original allocation. Therefore pathwise

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ge\frac c{\tau_c}.
\tag{5.17}
\]

Since `x -> 1/x` is convex on `(0,infinity)`, Jensen gives

\[
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
cE\frac1{\tau_c}
\ge
\frac c{E\tau_c}.
\tag{5.18}
\]

Combining (5.16) and (5.18),

\[
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
(1-o(1))c p_c\frac{H_n(L)}{B_n}.
\tag{5.19}
\]

Taking limsups proves (5.1).

It remains to optimize. Define

\[
f(c)=\frac{c(\delta-c)}{\lambda-c}.
\]

Its derivative has numerator

\[
c^2-2\lambda c+\delta\lambda.
\]

The unique critical point in `(0,delta)` is

\[
c_*=\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda\nu},
\]

and substituting `delta=lambda-nu` gives

\[
f(c_*)=(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt\nu)^2.
\]

The endpoint values are zero, so this is the maximum. This proves (5.2)–(5.3). `square`

## 6. The revised tracked lower-bound function

For a **fixed admissible target sequence** `m_n`, let `A(m)` denote the nonempty class of amplified cutoffs for which the checked amplified-cloud theorem gives (4.1), and define

\[
\Theta_{\rm amp}(\pi,\lambda;m_\bullet)
:=
\sup_{L_\bullet\in A(m)}
\limsup_n
\frac{-\log\sum_{L_n<k\le m_n}k\pi_k/\nu}
{-\log\Pr(S_n=m_n)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then Theorem 5.1 gives the sequence-specific proved metric lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]
\ge
(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt\nu)^2
\Theta_{\rm amp}(\pi,\lambda;m_\bullet).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

To obtain a function of only the fixed law and `lambda`, as requested by the revised metric, let `M_lambda` be the set of all full admissible sequences with `m_n/n -> lambda` and define

\[
\Theta_{\rm amp}(w,\lambda)
:=
\inf_{m_\bullet\in M_\lambda}
\Theta_{\rm amp}(\pi,\lambda;m_\bullet),
\tag{6.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
b_{\rm Palm}(w,\lambda)
:=(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt\nu)^2\Theta_{\rm amp}(w,\lambda).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus, within the boundary-normalizable finite-mean scope,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{m_\bullet\in M_\lambda}
\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]
\ge b_{\rm Palm}(w,\lambda).}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is the **after** value of the tracked metric for the unrestricted class in this run. It is a genuine law/`lambda` lower-bound functional, but it may still equal zero: the remaining universal obligation is to prove

\[
\Theta_{\rm amp}(w,\lambda)>0
\tag{6.6}
\]

for every fixed law, or construct a fixed law with `Theta_amp=0` and then determine whether it is an actual full-sequence counterexample.

If `(6.6)` holds, the source's weak question is settled for that law immediately. Indeed `Y_(1)/n` is uniformly bounded by `m_n/n`, so convergence to zero in probability would force its expectation to zero; any positive right side of (6.2) forbids that.

### Stronger known value under extra hypotheses

Under Janson's power-law hypothesis `w_k ~ c k^{-beta}`, `beta>2`, Theorem 18.33 gives the much stronger exact value

\[
\lim_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]=\lambda-\nu.
\]

Therefore the strongest proved metric in that restricted class remains `lambda-nu`; `b_Palm` is intended for the fully irregular fixed-law regime and is not claimed to improve the power-law theorem.
