## 2. Retained normalized model and amplified cloud

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put

\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu>0.
\]

Then

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]

where the `xi_i` are iid with law `pi` and `S_n=sum_i xi_i`.

Write

\[
p_n=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad b_n=-\log p_n,
\qquad \delta=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]

The weak law gives `b_n -> infinity`; Janson's Theorem 17.1 at the normalized boundary gives

\[
\boxed{b_n=o(n).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Normalize the fixed size-biased boundary law

\[
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Retain the verified amplified-cloud threshold

\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),
\qquad R_n\to\infty,
\qquad R_n=o(b_n),
\tag{2.3}
\]

with

\[
T_n^{>L}:=\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{2.4}
\]

In particular

\[
\frac{n}{L_n}=\frac{b_n}{R_n}(1+o(1))\to\infty.
\tag{2.5}
\]

This run does **not** try to improve the near-full total-mass peeling theorem from the preceding checkpoint. Instead it asks a different question: before a fixed amount of **total** mass has been removed, how much of that removed mass must already come from the amplified cloud? # 3. New theorem: a fixed-total size-biased prefix must already contain linear amplified-cloud mass

Fix a constant

\[
0<c<\delta.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For definiteness one may take `c=delta/4`.

Generate the size-biased permutation of the boxes: at each step choose one remaining box with probability proportional to its occupancy and remove the entire box. Write `B_j` for the size of the `j`-th removed box and

\[
S_r^{\rm rem}:=\sum_{j=1}^rB_j.
\]

Let

\[
\tau_c:=\inf\{r:S_r^{\rm rem}\ge cn\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define the amplified-cloud mass removed by this time by

\[
C_{\tau_c}
:=
\sum_{j=1}^{\tau_c}B_j\mathbf1_{\{B_j>L_n\}}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — marked mass before a fixed total-removal threshold

There is a constant

\[
\gamma=\gamma(\lambda,\nu,c)>0
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\bigl(C_{\tau_c}\ge\gamma n\mid S_n=m_n\bigr)\to1.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof
On the high-probability cloud event from (2.4),

\[
T_n^{>L}\ge(\delta-o(1))n.
\]

Before the stopping time `tau_c`, less than `cn` total mass has been removed. Hence, immediately before every pre-stop selection, the remaining amplified-cloud mass is at least

\[
(\delta-c-o(1))n.
\]

The remaining total mass is at most `m_n=(lambda+o(1))n`. Consequently, for all sufficiently large `n`, the conditional probability that the next size-biased removal is an amplified-cloud box is bounded below by a fixed constant

\[
p_0>0,
\qquad
p_0<\frac{\delta-c}{\lambda}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For example, after increasing `n`, one may take

\[
p_0=\frac{\delta-c}{2\lambda}.
\]

Choose `gamma>0` so small that

\[
q_0:=\frac{\gamma}{c}<\frac{p_0}{2}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Suppose that at the stopping time

\[
C_{\tau_c}<\gamma n.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The non-cloud mass removed is then at least

\[
(c-\gamma)n.
\]

Every non-cloud box has occupancy at most `L_n`, so the number `N_s` of non-cloud selections by `tau_c` obeys

\[
N_s\ge\frac{(c-\gamma)n}{L_n}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Every cloud box has occupancy strictly larger than `L_n`; if the total selected cloud mass is below `gamma n`, the number `N_c` of cloud selections obeys

\[
N_c<\frac{\gamma n}{L_n}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus the total number of selections is at least

\[
t_n:=\frac{(c-\gamma)n}{L_n}\to\infty
\tag{3.10}
\]

and the cloud-hit fraction at the stopped time is at most

\[
\frac{N_c}{N_c+N_s}\le\frac{\gamma}{c}=q_0<p_0.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Up to `tau_c`, the cloud-hit indicators are Bernoulli variables with predictable conditional success probabilities at least `p_0`. The standard exponential supermartingale for lower deviations therefore gives constants `A,kappa>0`, depending only on `p_0,q_0`, such that

\[
\Pr\left(
\text{for some stopped time }t\ge t_n,
\ \#\{\text{cloud hits up to }t\}\le q_0t
\right)
\le Ae^{-\kappa t_n}.
\tag{3.12}
\]

For completeness: for `theta>0`, before stopping,

\[
E[e^{-\theta I_j}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
\le 1-p_0+p_0e^{-\theta};
\]

choosing `theta` so that the resulting Chernoff exponent at `q_0` is positive and applying Ville/optional stopping yields (3.12). Since `t_n -> infinity` by (2.5), the right side tends to zero. Together with the high-probability cloud event, this proves (3.4). \(\square\)

### Why this is different from the previous near-full peeling result

The preceding checkpoint proved that after `o(b_n)` **steps**, almost all cloud mass has been removed. Theorem 3.1 instead stops after only a fixed amount `cn` of **total removed mass**, with `c<delta`. Therefore every pre-stop residual density remains strictly above `nu`; this makes the exact Palm entropy budget available without the moving-density correction that would arise after crossing the critical residual density.

# 4. Transfer under a hypothetical full no-condensation cap

Assume now, only for contradiction analysis, that

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose a deterministic cap

\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad U_n=o(n),
\qquad U_n\ge L_n,
\tag{4.2}
\]

such that

\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `E_n` be the event for the size-biased peeling path that

1. total removed mass reaches `cn`;
2. by that stopping time at least `gamma n` of the removed mass came from boxes larger than `L_n`;
3. no selected box exceeds `U_n`.

By Theorem 3.1 and (4.3),

\[
P_n^{\rm stop}(E_n)\to1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Use the exact sequential Palm path identity retained from the `23-44-00Z` checkpoint. Before `tau_c`,

\[
M_j>m_n-cn,
\qquad
N_j=n-j\le n,
\]

so

\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}
\ge
\frac{m_n-cn}{n}
\to\lambda-c>\nu.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus every density-premium term in the Palm entropy telescope is eventually nonnegative. The terminal exact conditioning cost is nonnegative as well. Hence the stopped path law satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D(P_n^{\rm stop}\Vert Q_n^{*,\rm stop})\le b_n,
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where the reference process uses iid increments with the fixed law `bar q` and the same total-mass stopping rule.

Binary data processing through `E_n`, together with (4.4), gives

\[
\boxed{
-\log Q_n^{*,\rm stop}(E_n)
\le(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is the information-theoretic input for the next new bound.

# 5. New bivariate defective exponential martingale

Let `q` be a probability law on the positive integers and let `X_1,X_2,...` be iid with law `q`. Fix `L<U`, and define

\[
S_s=\sum_{i=1}^sX_i,
\qquad
C_s=\sum_{i=1}^sX_i\mathbf1_{\{X_i>L\}}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `t,u>=0`, define the capped bivariate transform

\[
M_{L,U}(t,u)
:=
\sum_{1\le k\le L}q_ke^{-uk}
+
\sum_{L<k\le U}q_ke^{(t-u)k}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
\tau_c=\inf\{s:S_s\ge cn\}
\]

with a deterministic terminal truncation at `n` steps if necessary.

## Lemma 5.1 — bivariate stopped-martingale bound

If

\[
M_{L,U}(t,u)\le1,
\tag{5.3}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
C_{\tau_c}\ge\gamma n,
\ \max_{i\le\tau_c}X_i\le U,
\ \tau_c\le n
\right)
\le
\exp\{-t\gamma n+u(cn+U)\}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof
Set

\[
Z_s
=
\frac{
\exp(tC_s-uS_s)
\mathbf1_{\{\max_{i\le s}X_i\le U\}}
}{M_{L,U}(t,u)^s},
\qquad Z_0=1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For positive `M_{L,U}(t,u)`, this is a nonnegative martingale; if the transform is zero the claim is trivial. Stop it at the bounded stopping time given by first passage of total mass `cn`, first violation of the cap, or `n` steps.

On the event in (5.4), the previous total before the final selected increment is below `cn`, while the final increment is at most `U`; hence

\[
S_{\tau_c}\le cn+U.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Moreover `C_{tau_c}>=gamma n`, and because `M_{L,U}(t,u)<=1`, the denominator in (5.5) can only increase `Z_{tau_c}`. Therefore

\[
Z_{\tau_c}
\ge
\exp\{t\gamma n-u(cn+U)\}.
\]

Bounded optional stopping gives `E Z_tau=1`, and Markov's inequality proves (5.4). \(\square\)

Applying the lemma with `q=bar q`, `L=L_n`, `U=U_n` to the reference event in (4.7) yields the new necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{t,u\ge0:\\M_{L_n,U_n}(t,u)\le1}}
\left\{
 t\gamma n-u(cn+U_n)
\right\}
\le(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is a genuinely two-dimensional obstruction: it simultaneously prices the amount of amplified-cloud mass and the total mass budget. It is not a local limit statement.

# 6. New support-free amplified-tail rate bound

Define the best fixed-law code rate **inside the amplified band**

\[
a(L,U)
:=
\min_{\substack{L<k\le U\\\bar q_k>0}}
\frac{-\log\bar q_k}{k}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Under the hypothetical counterexample and event (4.4), this interval is nonempty for all large `n`.

For every supported `L<k<=U`,

\[
\bar q_k\le e^{-a(L,U)k}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Let

\[
\phi(u)=E_{\bar q}e^{-uX},
\qquad
\psi(u)=1-\phi(u).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Because `bar q` is a fixed probability law on positive integers, choose one fixed supported `k_0` with mass `bar q_{k_0}>0`. Then for `0<u<=1`,

\[
\psi(u)
\ge
\bar q_{k_0}(1-e^{-uk_0})
\ge c_{\bar q}u
\tag{6.4}
\]

for a fixed constant `c_barq>0`.

Take

\[
u_n=\frac1{L_n},
\qquad
d_n=\frac{3\log L_n}{L_n}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

If `a(L_n,U_n)<=d_n`, then directly

\[
\gamma n a(L_n,U_n)
\le 3\gamma\frac{n\log L_n}{L_n}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Otherwise set

\[
t_n=u_n+a(L_n,U_n)-d_n>0.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For the small part,

\[
\sum_{k\le L_n}\bar q_ke^{-u_nk}
\le\phi(u_n)=1-\psi(u_n).
\tag{6.8}
\]

For the amplified part, by (6.2),

\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}
\bar q_ke^{(t_n-u_n)k}
\le
\sum_{k>L_n}e^{-d_nk}
=
\frac{e^{-d_n(L_n+1)}}{1-e^{-d_n}}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since `d_n L_n=3 log L_n`, the right side is

\[
O\left(\frac1{L_n^2\log L_n}\right).
\tag{6.10}
\]

By (6.4),

\[
\psi(u_n)\ge\frac{c_{\bar q}}{L_n},
\]

so (6.10) is eventually smaller than `psi(u_n)`. Therefore

\[
M_{L_n,U_n}(t_n,u_n)\le1.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Insert this pair into (5.7):

\[
\gamma n[a(L_n,U_n)-d_n+u_n]
-u_n(cn+U_n)
\le(1+o(1))b_n.
\]

Hence

\[
\gamma n a(L_n,U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+\gamma nd_n
+(c-\gamma)nu_n
+u_nU_n.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Because `U_n=o(n)`,

\[
\frac{U_n}{L_n}=o\left(\frac{n}{L_n}\right).
\]

Combining this with (6.5) yields the support-free amplified-tail rate constraint

\[
\boxed{
\gamma n a(L_n,U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+O\left(\frac{n\log L_n}{L_n}\right).
}
\tag{6.13}
\]

Using `n/L_n=(b_n/R_n)(1+o(1))`,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma n a(L_n,U_n)
\le
b_n
+O\left(\frac{b_n\log L_n}{R_n}\right)
+o\left(b_n+\frac{b_n\log L_n}{R_n}\right).
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
a(L_n,U_n)
\le
\frac{b_n}{\gamma n}
\left[
1+O\left(\frac{\log L_n}{R_n}\right)
\right]
+o\left(\frac{b_n}{n}ight)
}
\tag{6.15}
\]

whenever the displayed `O` term is bounded; (6.14) is the safe general form.

### Relation to the preceding all-support rate-envelope theorem

The previous checkpoint controlled

\[
a(U)=\min_{1\le k\le U}\frac{-\log\bar q_k}{k}
\]

with a binary-entropy correction tied to a near-full total-mass path. The new quantity `a(L,U)` ignores all cheap microscopic and moderately sized points and controls only the **actual amplified-cloud band** `L<k<=U`. Since

\[
a(L,U)\ge a(U),
\]

(6.13) is a stronger localization statement, but its leading coefficient is `gamma<delta`, so it does not by itself improve the earlier leading-order cancellation in the one-giant record comparison. The gain is different: the entropy/composition loss is now only

\[
O((n/L_n)\log L_n)=O((b_n/R_n)\log L_n),
\]

with no auxiliary near-full parameter `s_n` and no time-union loss.

# 7. New exact cloud-conditioned Palm kernel

The marked argument above uses the ordinary sequential Palm process. There is another exact object that may be more directly adapted to the remaining gap.

For a generic exact-sum state `(N,M)`, write

\[
p_{N}(M)=\Pr(S_N=M)
\]

and let the ordinary size-biased one-box Palm kernel be

\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{Nk\pi_k p_{N-1}(M-k)}{M p_N(M)}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For a cutoff `L`, define

\[
\alpha_{N,M}^{(L)}
:=
\sum_{k>L}K_{N,M}(k),
\qquad
Q_L:=\sum_{k>L}\bar q_k.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Condition the selected box to be in the cloud:

\[
K_{N,M}^{(L)}(k)
:=
\frac{K_{N,M}(k)}{\alpha_{N,M}^{(L)}}
\mathbf1_{\{k>L\}},
\tag{7.3}
\]

and normalize the fixed boundary tail

\[
\bar q^{(L)}_k
:=
\frac{\bar q_k}{Q_L}
\mathbf1_{\{k>L\}}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

A direct cancellation gives, for every supported `k>L`,

\[
\frac{K_{N,M}^{(L)}(k)}{\bar q_k^{(L)}}
=
\frac{N\nu Q_L}{M\alpha_{N,M}^{(L)}}
\frac{p_{N-1}(M-k)}{p_N(M)}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
D(K_{N,M}^{(L)}\Vert\bar q^{(L)})
=
 b(N,M)
-E\,b(N-1,M-B)
-\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}
+\log\frac{Q_L}{\alpha_{N,M}^{(L)}}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
 b(N,M)-E\,b(N-1,M-B)
=
D(K_{N,M}^{(L)}\Vert\bar q^{(L)})
+\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}
+\log\frac{\alpha_{N,M}^{(L)}}{Q_L}.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

This is an exact **cloud-only Palm decrement identity**. It shows that every cloud-selected box pays not only its relative-entropy cost inside the normalized tail law, but also the logarithmic amplification factor `log(alpha/Q_L)` comparing the conditioned cloud-ball probability with the fixed boundary size-biased tail.

At the original state, the retained amplified-cloud theorem gives

\[
\alpha_{n,m_n}^{(L_n)}
=E\left[\frac{T_n^{>L}}{m_n}\middle|S_n=m_n\right]
\to\frac{\delta}{\lambda}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

The missing step for a long iteration of (7.7) is **uniform control of** `alpha_{N,M}^{(L_n)}` along all residual exact-sum states generated by repeated cloud-only removals. Janson's Lemma 18.31(i) does not give that lower bound at an arbitrary moving amplified cutoff, and Lemma 18.31(ii) cannot be invoked because `L_n` is not known to lie below its existential `Omega_n`. I therefore do **not** iterate (7.7) as though such a uniform bound had been proved.

This isolates a precise new route: prove a compact-density, residual-state version of the truncated-overload lower bound at a deliberately slower cloud cutoff, then telescope (7.7). If successful, the fixed tail factor `Q_L` would enter once per cloud box without any composition count.

# 9. Failure checks and nonclaims

### 9.1 No misuse of Lemma 18.31(ii)
The lower bound in Theorem 3.1 uses the already verified amplified-cloud theorem at the original state. The new cloud-conditioned Palm identity is **not** iterated using an unproved residual-state version of that theorem.

### 9.2 No unrestricted local CLT
No local central limit theorem is used anywhere in Sections 3--7.

### 9.3 No claim that the new `gamma` equals the full excess `delta`
The fixed-total stopping argument yields some fixed `gamma>0`, generally much smaller than `delta`. Consequently (6.13) is not substituted into the previous one-giant comparison as if its leading coefficient were `delta`. Doing so would create a false cancellation.

### 9.4 The two-dimensional martingale requires the cap only in the contradiction branch
The marked-cloud theorem itself is unconditional. The cap `U_n=o(n)` enters only after assuming full no-condensation, where it is legitimate to choose a deterministic high-probability cap.

### 9.5 Generic one-big-jump theorems remain insufficient
No subexponential, regular variation, monotone-tail, or local-subexponential hypothesis is assumed. Classical big-jump results requiring such hypotheses therefore remain supporting intuition, not universal dependencies.
