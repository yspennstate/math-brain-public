\[

## 3. New mesoscopic cheap-atom envelope

Choose once and for all an integer `k_0` so large that

\[
k_0>\nu+1,
\qquad
\frac{\Lambda-\nu}{k_0-\nu}<\frac14.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `x>=k_0`, define

\[
\mathfrak r(x)
:=
\min_{\substack{k_0\le k\le x\\ \pi_k>0}}
\frac{-\log\pi_k}{k-\nu}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The normalized generating radius is one, so

\[
\liminf_{k\to\infty,\ \pi_k>0}
\frac{-\log\pi_k}{k}=0.
\]

Since `(k-nu)/k -> 1` along large support points,

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak r(x)\downarrow0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let `k(x)` be any support point attaining the minimum in (3.2). Necessarily

\[
k(x)\to\infty
\tag{3.4}
\]

as `x->infinity`; otherwise the minimum would eventually be achieved in a fixed finite set and could not tend to zero.

This envelope is the exact per-unit cost relevant to forcing many copies of one cheap mesoscopic atom while leaving the remainder at its ordinary mean.

It is asymptotically equivalent, on large record points, to the size-biased rate envelope used in the earlier record-crossing argument, because

\[
-\log\pi_k
=
-\log\bar q_k+\log(k/\nu).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the difference between the two per-unit costs is only `O(log k/k)` on large support points.

## 4. New theorem: mesoscopic cheap atoms bound every compact-supercritical exact-sum cost

### Theorem 4.1 — forced-atom/Harnack uniform rate bound

Let `x_n` be any deterministic sequence satisfying

\[
x_n\to\infty,
\qquad
x_n=o(n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then there is a constant `C_0<infinity`, depending only on the fixed law and the compact density interval, such that

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_n
\le
(\Lambda-\nu+1)\,\mathfrak r(x_n)
+
C_0\left(E_n+\frac{x_n}{n}\right)^2
+
C_0\frac{\log n}{n}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

In particular the right side tends to zero for every such `x_n`.

### Proof
Fix a pair `(N,M)` entering the supremum (1.1), so

\[
\frac n2\le N\le n,
\qquad
\nu+\eta\le\frac MN\le\Lambda,
\qquad
p_N(M)>0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Write

\[
k_n:=k(x_n),
\qquad
r_n^*:=
\operatorname{nint}
\left(
\frac{M-\nu N}{k_n-\nu}
\right),
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `nint` denotes a nearest integer. Then

\[
\left|
M-\nu N-r_n^*(k_n-\nu)
\right|
\le\frac{k_n-\nu}{2}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because `M/N<=Lambda`, (3.1), `k_n->infinity`, and `N>=n/2`, for large `n`,

\[
0\le r_n^*\le \frac N3,
\tag{4.6}
\]

say. Put

\[
R:=N-r_n^*,
\qquad
T:=M-r_n^*k_n.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then `R` lies in `[n/3,n]`. Moreover

\[
T-\nu R
=
M-\nu N-r_n^*(k_n-\nu),
\]

so by (4.5),

\[
|T-\nu R|\le k_n/2.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Compare `T` with the Harnack center `s_R^dagger`. By (2.2),

\[
|T-s_R^\dagger|
\le
\frac{k_n}{2}+E_nR
\le
\frac{x_n}{2}+E_nn.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Since `x_n=o(n)` and `E_n->0`, the right side is `o(n)`. Thus for all large `n` it is at most `gamma R`, and the Harnack bound (2.1) applies at `T`:

\[
-\log p_R(T)
\le
\log(W_R/c)
+
C\frac{(T-s_R^\dagger)^2+|T-s_R^\dagger|+1}{R}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Because `R>=n/3`, `W_R=o(R)` and hence `W_R<=n` eventually, division by `N` gives

\[
\frac{-\log p_R(T)}N
\le
C_1\left(E_n+\frac{x_n}{n}\right)^2
+C_1\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Now force the first `r_n^*` iid variables to equal `k_n`. Independence gives the exact lower bound

\[
p_N(M)
\ge
\pi_{k_n}^{r_n^*}\,p_R(T).
\tag{4.12}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{b(N,M)}N
\le
\frac{r_n^*}{N}(-\log\pi_{k_n})
+
\frac{-\log p_R(T)}N.
\tag{4.13}
\]

From the nearest-integer choice,

\[
\frac{r_n^*}{N}
\le
\frac{M/N-\nu}{k_n-\nu}+rac1{2N}
\le
\frac{\Lambda-\nu}{k_n-\nu}+rac1{2N}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

By the definition of `mathfrak r`,

\[
-\log\pi_{k_n}
=(k_n-\nu)\mathfrak r(x_n).
\tag{4.15}
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{r_n^*}{N}(-\log\pi_{k_n})
\le
(\Lambda-\nu)\mathfrak r(x_n)
+
\frac{x_n}{n}\mathfrak r(x_n)
\le
(\Lambda-\nu+1)\mathfrak r(x_n)
\tag{4.16}
\]

for large `n`. Combining (4.11), (4.13), and (4.16), then taking the supremum over `(N,M)`, proves (4.2). `square`

### Arithmetic note
The span-one normalization is used here only so that (2.1) applies at every integer in its window. For a general lattice span `d`, divide by the span first. Since the original exact-sum pair `(N,M)` is admissible and the forced atom lies in the support lattice, the residual target remains in the admissible lattice class.

## 5. Why Theorem 4.1 is useful

The preceding checkpoint obtained `epsilon_n->0` by compactness from Theorem 17.1. Theorem 4.1 identifies a concrete mechanism and a concrete upper envelope.

The conditioning cost of **every** compact-supercritical exact sum is no worse, to first exponential order, than:

1. forcing `Theta(N/k_n)` copies of one mesoscopic support atom `k_n` whose per-unit fixed-law coding cost is nearly `mathfrak r(x_n)`; and
2. asking the remaining `N-o(N)` variables to land only `o(N)` away from their ordinary mean, paid for by the universal Gaussian Harnack bound.

No subexponential-tail theorem, moving local CLT, or regular variation is used.

The theorem also applies to the target pair itself. Thus, for every `x_n` satisfying (4.1),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_n}{n}
\le
(\Lambda-\nu+1)\mathfrak r(x_n)
+C_0\left(E_n+\frac{x_n}{n}\right)^2
+C_0\frac{\log n}{n}
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

for all large `n` after fixing a compact interval containing `lambda`.

This is a quantitative fixed-law refinement of the qualitative `b_n=o(n)` conclusion.

## 6. A completely explicit fixed-law residual-rate envelope

Take

\[
x_n=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define

\[
\Psi_n
:=
(\Lambda-\nu+1)\mathfrak r(\sqrt n)
+C_0\left(E_n+n^{-1/2}\right)^2
+C_0\frac{\log n}{n}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_n\le\Psi_n,
\qquad
\Psi_n\to0.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Every quantity in `Psi_n` is determined by the one fixed boundary law and the fixed compact density interval; the target sequence enters only later through `b_n`.

The choice `sqrt(n)` is not essential. More generally, (4.2) allows optimization over any deterministic `x_n->infinity`, `x_n=o(n)`:

\[
\Psi_n(x_n)
:=
(\Lambda-\nu+1)\mathfrak r(x_n)
+C_0(E_n+x_n/n)^2
+C_0\log n/n.
\tag{6.4}
\]

One may choose a slower or faster mesoscopic record scale to trade cheap-atom rate against Harnack displacement.

## 7. New near-inverse uniformly iterable cutoff

The preceding checkpoint used a conservative example cutoff of order `epsilon_n^{-1/2}`. Condition (1.2) actually permits an almost inverse-rate cutoff.

Let

\[
A_n:=\min\{\Psi_n^{-1},n\}
\tag{7.1}
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
\widehat K_n
:=
\left\lfloor
\frac{A_n}{\log(e+A_n)}
\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since `Psi_n->0`,

\[
A_n\to\infty,
\qquad
\widehat K_n\to\infty.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Also

\[
\widehat K_n=o(n).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Finally,

\[
\widehat K_n\epsilon_n
\le
\widehat K_n\Psi_n\to0.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Indeed:

- if `A_n=1/Psi_n<=n`, then `Khat_n Psi_n <= 1/log(e+A_n)`;
- if `A_n=n<1/Psi_n`, then `Psi_n<1/n` and again `Khat_n Psi_n <= 1/log(e+n)`.

Thus `Khat_n` is a valid uniformly iterable cutoff in the corrected cloud-Palm theorem, and it is within only a logarithmic factor of

\[
\min\{\Psi_n^{-1},n\}.
\]

## 8. New explicit integrated-tail obstruction under full no-condensation

Assume only for contradiction analysis that

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\tag{8.1}
\]

along the full admissible sequence.

Apply the corrected preceding checkpoint's necessary tail condition (1.3) at the new cutoff (7.2). Because (7.3)–(7.5) verify all of its hypotheses,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{Q(\widehat K_n)}=o(b_n).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This removes the abstract `epsilon_n` from the tail obstruction. A putative full counterexample must keep the fixed size-biased boundary tail exponentially thick, on the exact-conditioning `b_n` scale, all the way to a cutoff that is nearly the reciprocal of the explicit mesoscopic-record/Harnack rate `Psi_n`.

Equivalently, for every fixed `theta>0`, define the `b_n`-scale tail quantile

\[
K_n^{(\theta)}
:=
\min\{K:Q(K)\le e^{-\theta b_n}\}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Then (8.2) implies the pointwise eventual lower bound

\[
\boxed{
K_n^{(\theta)}>\widehat K_n
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

Indeed, if `K_n^(theta)<=Khat_n`, monotonicity of `Q` would give

\[
Q(\widehat K_n)\le e^{-\theta b_n},
\]

contradicting (8.2).

This is stronger and more concrete than merely saying that the quantile cannot simultaneously be sublinear and satisfy `K_n^(theta) epsilon_n -> 0`.

## 9. Relation to the record-crossing route

The earlier record theorem uses a size-biased lower-rate envelope

\[
r(x)=
\min_{1\le k\le x,\ q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(c_0q_k)}{k},
\qquad q_k=k\pi_k,
\]

and proves that against every diverging sublinear cap `U_j=o(R_j)`, strict record points `R_j` have unbounded total rate advantage

\[
R_j(r(U_j)-r(R_j))
\]

along a subsequence.

The new `mathfrak r` is not a competing object; it is the same fixed-law cheap-atom geometry with the exact normalization needed to create supercritical sums by repeated mesoscopic atoms. By (3.5), the two per-unit costs agree asymptotically up to `O(log k/k)` on large support points.

The combined necessary picture for a hypothetical counterexample is now:

1. **mesoscopic construction:** every nearby supercritical exact sum has per-site cost at most `Psi_n`;
2. **uniform residual iteration:** cutoffs almost as large as `1/Psi_n` are legitimate, up to a logarithm;
3. **integrated fixed-tail thickness:** `Q(Khat_n)=exp(-o(b_n))`;
4. **macroscopic record crossing:** despite that thick integrated tail below the macroscopic scale, some macroscopic records are cheaper per unit than every sublinear cap by an unbounded total amount;
5. **one-giant Harnack test:** those records must nevertheless lose to the exact conditioning coefficient after paying only the explicit crossing/Harnack remainder.

The remaining missing theorem is deterministic/probabilistic synchronization between items 3 and 4. The present report does not prove that the thick integrated tail at `Khat_n` forces a record advantage large enough to dominate the crossing/Harnack error.

## 10. Why this does not yet solve Problem 18.39

The new rate bound is uniform and quantitative, but `Psi_n` may tend to zero arbitrarily slowly. Likewise, the fixed size-biased tail `Q(K)` may decay arbitrarily slowly, and the strict record scales can be arbitrarily sparse.

Thus (8.2) is a genuine restriction but is not by itself contradictory. A fixed law could, in principle, try to satisfy all three irregular requirements simultaneously:

- very cheap mesoscopic atoms making `Psi_n` small;
- a thick integrated tail out to almost `1/Psi_n`;
- sparse macroscopic record points whose one-giant gain is still swallowed by the admissible-sequence crossing/Harnack remainder.

No construction satisfying all accumulated constraints is known here, but no universal exclusion is proved in this run.

Therefore the full Problem 18.39 remains open.
