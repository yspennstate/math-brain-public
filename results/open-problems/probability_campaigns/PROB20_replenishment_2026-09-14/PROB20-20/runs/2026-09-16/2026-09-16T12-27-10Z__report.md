# 2. Capped I-projections and an exact Pythagorean identity

For an integer cap `K` with `L_n<=K<=U_n`, define

\[
F_K(t)=\sum_{k\le K}\pi_ke^{tk}.
\]

For large `n`, the mean `lambda_n` lies in the interior of the convex hull of the support below `K`; let `s_K` be the unique saddle satisfying

\[
\frac{F_K'(s_K)}{F_K(s_K)}=\lambda_n,
\]

and define the capped I-projection

\[
P_K(k)=\frac{\pi_ke^{s_Kk}}{F_K(s_K)},\qquad k\le K,
\]

with rate

\[
I_K=\lambda_ns_K-\log F_K(s_K)=D(P_K\Vert\pi).
\]

Because both `P_K` and the original `P_{U_n}` have the same mean `lambda_n`, there is an exact nested-projection identity:

## Lemma 2.1 — exact cap Pythagorean identity

\[
\boxed{
I_K-I_{U_n}=D(P_K\Vert P_{U_n})\ge0.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof
On the support of `P_K`,

\[
\log\frac{P_{U_n}(k)}{\pi_k}
=s_nk-\log F_{U_n}(s_n).
\]

Its expectation under either `P_K` or `P_{U_n}` equals

\[
s_n\lambda_n-\log F_{U_n}(s_n)=I_{U_n}.
\]

Thus

\[
D(P_K\Vert\pi)
=D(P_K\Vert P_{U_n})+I_{U_n}.
\]

This identity is exact and will be useful for interpreting the compression argument below.

# 3. New cap-compression lemma

The key point is that a fixed amount of **canonical excess first moment** below a cap `K` lets us remove everything above `K` and restore mean `lambda_n` using only an `O(1/L_n)` extra tilt.

For `L_n<=K<=U_n`, put

\[
D_n(L_n,K)
:=\sum_{L_n<k\le K}q_ke^{s_nk}.
\]

## Lemma 3.1 — fixed retained excess implies only `O(1/L_n)` extra rate

Fix `c>0`. Along any subsequence on which

\[
D_n(L_n,K_n)\ge c,
\tag{3.1}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
0\le s_{K_n}-s_n\le \frac{a_c}{L_n},
\qquad
a_c:=\log\frac{8\lambda}{c}+O(1),
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
0\le I_{K_n}-I_{U_n}\le\frac{C_c}{L_n}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The constants depend only on the fixed retained amount `c`, `lambda`, and the boundary law.

### Proof
Set

\[
t_n=s_n+a/L_n
\]

with a fixed `a>0` chosen below.

For `k<=L_n`, convexity of `x -> e^{ax/L_n}` on `[0,L_n]` gives

\[
e^{ak/L_n}
\le1+\frac{k}{L_n}(e^a-1).
\]

Hence, using (1.5),

\[
\sum_{k\le L_n}\pi_ke^{t_nk}
\le
A_n(s_n)
+\frac{e^a-1}{L_n}A_n'(s_n)
=1+o(1)+O_a(1/L_n).
\tag{3.4}
\]

The retained-band first moment at the new tilt satisfies

\[
D_n(L_n,K_n;t_n)
:=\sum_{L_n<k\le K_n}q_ke^{t_nk}
\ge e^aD_n(L_n,K_n;s_n)
\ge e^ac.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Its partition-mass contribution is at most

\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le K_n}\pi_ke^{t_nk}
\le\frac{D_n(L_n,K_n;t_n)}{L_n}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore the capped mean at `t_n` obeys

\[
\frac{F_{K_n}'(t_n)}{F_{K_n}(t_n)}
\ge
\frac{D_n(L_n,K_n;t_n)}{1+o(1)+O_a(1/L_n)+D_n(L_n,K_n;t_n)/L_n}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The right side is increasing in the displayed band first moment. Choose `a` so `e^ac>=8lambda`. Then (3.7) is strictly larger than `lambda_n` for all sufficiently large `n`.

At the old saddle `s_n`, deleting values `>K_n` cannot increase the normalized mean because every deleted value is eventually larger than `lambda_n`. Thus the new mean-`lambda_n` saddle satisfies

\[
s_n\le s_{K_n}\le t_n=s_n+a/L_n,
\]

proving (3.2).

For the rates, monotonicity of `F_{K_n}` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{K_n}-I_{U_n}
&=\lambda_n(s_{K_n}-s_n)
+\log F_{U_n}(s_n)-\log F_{K_n}(s_{K_n})\\
&\le\lambda_n a/L_n
+\log\frac{F_{U_n}(s_n)}{F_{K_n}(s_n)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The deleted partition mass is bounded using the already checked total band first moment:

\[
F_{U_n}(s_n)-F_{K_n}(s_n)
\le
\frac1{K_n}
\sum_{K_n<k\le U_n}q_ke^{s_nk}
\le\frac{C}{K_n}
\le\frac{C}{L_n}.
\]

Since `F_{K_n}(s_n)>=A_n(s_n)=1+o(1)`, the logarithmic ratio is `O(1/L_n)`. This proves (3.3). `square`

### Interpretation
A large upper support may look essential, but if a fixed positive part of the canonical excess is already available below `K_n`, then deleting all larger values changes the optimal exponential cost by only

\[
O(n/L_n)=O(b_n/\mathcal R_n)=o(b_n).
\]

Thus a cap whose truncated overload cost is much larger than `b_n/n` cannot simultaneously contain a fixed amount of the actual canonical excess.

# 4. New Cramér-forbidden-zone theorem

Let `K_n` be any deterministic or canonical sequence satisfying

\[
L_n\le K_n\le U_n
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nJ_{K_n}(\mu_n)}{b_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — high truncated-Cramér cost forces canonical excess to vanish below the cap

Under hypothetical full no-condensation,

\[
\boxed{
D_n(L_n,K_n)
=\sum_{L_n<k\le K_n}q_ke^{s_nk}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof
Suppose not. Then along a subsequence `D_n(L_n,K_n)>=c>0`. Lemma 3.1 gives

\[
I_{K_n}\le I_{U_n}+C_c/L_n.
\]

Using (1.2) and `n/L_n=(1+o(1))b_n/\mathcal R_n`, we get

\[
 nI_{K_n}\le b_n+o(b_n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

On the other hand, for every `t>=0`,

\[
F_{K_n}(t)\le M_{K_n}(t),
\]

and eventually `lambda_n>mu_n`. If `t_n^*` is the optimizer for `J_{K_n}(mu_n)`, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{K_n}
&=\sup_t\{\lambda_nt-\log F_{K_n}(t)\}\\
&\ge \lambda_nt_n^*-\log F_{K_n}(t_n^*)\\
&\ge \mu_nt_n^*-\log M_{K_n}(t_n^*)\\
&=J_{K_n}(\mu_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Equations (4.1), (4.3), and (4.4) contradict each other. `square`

This theorem is more flexible than the earlier statement at the single amplified cutoff `L_n`: **every** cutoff whose truncated overload rate still overwhelms the exact conditioning cost is asymptotically empty of canonical excess first moment.

# 5. A last high-Cramér cutoff and the critical transition annulus

The preceding theorem suggests defining the lower edge of the actual fragmentation mechanism endogenously rather than using `L_n` itself.

Recall

\[
H_n=\frac{nJ_{L_n}(\mu_n)}{b_n}\to\infty
\]

and set

\[
a_n:=\sqrt{H_n}\to\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]

We first show that the variance-scale cap `V_n` is already far outside this high-rate regime.

At `t=s_n`, the truncated variable with cutoff `V_n` has logarithmic derivative

\[
\frac{M_{V_n}'(s_n)}{M_{V_n}(s_n)}.
\]

By (1.5), (1.6), `V_n->infinity`, and the bound

\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le V_n}\pi_ke^{s_nk}
\le D_n(L_n,V_n)/L_n=O(1/L_n),
\]

we have

\[
M_{V_n}(s_n)=1+o(1)
\]

and

\[
M_{V_n}'(s_n)
\ge\nu+\delta/3+o(1).
\]

Hence the optimizer for the moving target `mu_n=nu+epsilon_n` lies below `s_n` for all large `n`. Since

\[
M_{V_n}'(0)=E[X;X\le V_n]	o\nu,
\]

convex duality gives

\[
J_{V_n}(\mu_n)
\le
\bigl(\varepsilon_n+E[X;X>V_n]\bigr)s_n.
\]

The parenthesis tends to zero, while `ns_n=O(b_n)`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nJ_{V_n}(\mu_n)}{b_n}\to0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because `K -> J_K(mu_n)` is nonincreasing, we may define the **last high-Cramér cutoff**

\[
\boxed{
K_n^-:=
\max\left\{
K\in[L_n,V_n]\cap\mathbb Z:
\frac{nJ_K(\mu_n)}{b_n}\ge a_n
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

It is well defined for large `n`, satisfies

\[
L_n\le K_n^-<V_n=o(n),
\tag{5.4}
\]

and, by Theorem 4.1,

\[
\boxed{
D_n(L_n,K_n^-)=o(1).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Subtracting (5.5) from the retained half-excess bound (1.6) gives the main localization theorem of the run:

## Theorem 5.1 — a fixed amount of canonical excess lives in the Cramér transition annulus

\[
\boxed{
D_n(K_n^-,V_n)
:=\sum_{K_n^-<k\le V_n}q_ke^{s_nk}
\ge\delta/3-o(1).
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus every hypothetical full counterexample is forced into a sharply characterized annulus:

- below `K_n^-`, producing even a vanishing supercritical target still costs `a_n b_n` with `a_n->infinity`, so canonical excess there vanishes;
- by `V_n=O(sigma_n^2)`, a fixed part of the excess has already appeared;
- the annulus `(K_n^-,V_n]` therefore carries a fixed amount of the actual fragmented condensate.

Its canonical box probability is at least

\[
\Pr_n^{\rm can}(K_n^-<X\le V_n)
\ge\frac{c}{V_n},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
 n\Pr_n^{\rm can}(K_n^-<X\le V_n)
\ge c\frac n{V_n}\to\infty.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Hence the Cramér transition annulus contains a diverging expected number of canonical fragments.

It also yields a sharper variance lower bound:

\[
E_n^{\rm can}X^2
\ge cK_n^-+O(1),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sigma_n^2\ge cK_n^-.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

# 6. A strict fixed-target Cramér rate cliff

The moving target above is useful for defining the forbidden zone. A fixed target shows that the rate has genuinely collapsed by the variance scale.

Set

\[
\mu_*:=\nu+\delta/5.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The same derivative estimate at `V_n` gives

\[
\frac{M_{V_n}'(s_n)}{M_{V_n}(s_n)}
\ge\nu+\delta/3+o(1)>\mu_*
\]

for large `n`, so the optimizer for `J_{V_n}(mu_*)` lies in `[0,s_n]`. Since `M_{V_n}(0)=1` and

\[
M_{V_n}'(0)=\nu-E[X;X>V_n],
\]

we obtain

\[
J_{V_n}(\mu_*)
\le
\left(\frac\delta5+E[X;X>V_n]\right)s_n.
\]

Using the retained decomposition `delta n s_n<=b_n+o(b_n)`,

\[
\boxed{
 nJ_{V_n}(\mu_*)
\le
\left(\frac15+o(1)\right)b_n.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

On the other hand, `mu_*>mu_n` eventually, so

\[
J_{K_n^-}(\mu_*)
\ge J_{K_n^-}(\mu_n),
\]

and by (5.3)

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nJ_{K_n^-}(\mu_*)}{b_n}\ge a_n\to\infty.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore the same fixed law undergoes a genuine truncated-Cramér rate collapse **inside the actual canonical fragmentation annulus**:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nJ_{K_n^-}(\mu_*)}{b_n}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{nJ_{V_n}(\mu_*)}{b_n}\le\frac15+o(1).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

This is a new quantitative description of the only surviving counterexample mechanism. The fragmented condensate must live exactly where increasing the fixed-law cutoff changes a fixed supercritical overload from vastly more expensive than the conditioning event to strictly cheaper than the conditioning event.

# 7. Record synchronization on the Cramér transition annulus

Retain the strict record envelope

\[
r(x)
:=\min_{1\le k\le x,\ q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(c_0q_k)}k,
\]

strict record locations `R_j`, and `h_j=r(R_j)`. Use the already checked Gaussian-Harnack synchronization to choose admissible indices `n_j` with

\[
R_j/n_j\to\delta.
\]

Write

\[
K_j^-:=K_{n_j}^-,
\qquad
V_j:=V_{n_j},
\qquad
s_j:=s_{n_j}.
\]

Since

\[
K_j^-\to\infty,
\qquad
K_j^-<V_j=o(R_j),
\]

the checked uniform cap-scaled record theorem applies to this **critical** interval. Along a strict-record subsequence,

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j^{\rm crit}
:=\inf_{K_j^-\le x\le V_j}
 x\bigl(r(x)-h_j\bigr)
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Normalize the actual transition-annulus excess:

\[
D_j^{\rm crit}
:=\sum_{K_j^-<k\le V_j}q_ke^{s_jk}
\ge\delta/3-o(1),
\]

\[
\mu_j^{\rm crit}(k)
:=\frac{q_ke^{s_jk}}{D_j^{\rm crit}}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

For supported `k` in the annulus, the record barrier gives

\[
q_k\le c_0^{-1}e^{-h_jk-\Gamma_j^{\rm crit}}.
\]

Hence

\[
-\log\mu_j^{\rm crit}(k)
\ge
\Gamma_j^{\rm crit}
+(h_j-s_j)k
+O(1).
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let

\[
W_j:=E_{\mu_j^{\rm crit}}(K-K_j^-)
\le V_j-K_j^-.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Because shifting an integer random variable does not change Shannon entropy, and the geometric law maximizes entropy at fixed nonnegative mean,

\[
H(\mu_j^{\rm crit})
\le\log(e(1+W_j)).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Averaging (7.3) and writing

\[
a_j:=(s_j-h_j)_+
\]

gives the new centered transition-width obstruction:

## Theorem 7.1 — Cramér-transition record/entropy inequality

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j^{\rm crit}-a_jK_j^-
\le
\log(e(1+W_j))
+a_jW_j
+C.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This sharpens the previous variance-scale inequality in two ways:

1. the lower endpoint is no longer the arbitrary amplified cutoff `L_j`, but the **last cutoff at which the fixed-law truncated overload still costs an unbounded multiple of the true conditioning exponent**;
2. after subtracting the unavoidable tilt payment `a_jK_j^-`, the entropy term depends only on the **width** `W_j` of the Cramér transition annulus, not on the full variance scale.

A hypothetical counterexample therefore requires a transition annulus which is simultaneously:

- far above every high-Cramér-cost scale;
- sublinear and populated by `n/V_j -> infinity` fragments;
- rich enough in near-record support to pay the diverging `Gamma_j^crit` barrier;
- wide enough, or tilted enough, to satisfy (7.6).
