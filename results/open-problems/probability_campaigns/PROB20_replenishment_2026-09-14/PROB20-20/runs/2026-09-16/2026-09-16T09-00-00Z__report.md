## 1. Truncated Cramér tilt at the amplified cutoff

Fix once and for all a number

\[
\mu\in(\nu,\lambda),
\]

for example `mu=(nu+lambda)/2`.

For an integer cutoff `L`, define the bounded truncated variable

\[
Z_L:=X\mathbf 1_{\{X\le L\}},
\]

its logarithmic mgf

\[
\Lambda_L(t):=\log E e^{tZ_L},
\]

and the Cramér rate

\[
J_L(\mu):=\sup_{t\ge0}\{\mu t-\Lambda_L(t)\}.
\]

Let `t_L(mu)>0` be the unique optimizer for large `L`, equivalently

\[
\Lambda_L'(t_L(\mu))=\mu.
\]

The checked amplified-cloud checkpoint proves, for every fixed `mu>nu`,

\[
t_L(\mu)\to0,
\qquad
L t_L(\mu)\to\infty,
\qquad
J_L(\mu)\sim(\mu-\nu)t_L(\mu).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Put

\[
t_n:=t_{L_n}(\mu),
\qquad
J_n:=J_{L_n}(\mu).
\]

The original amplified diagonal was chosen using a slowly moving level `mu_n downarrow nu` and satisfies

\[
\frac{nJ_{L_n}(\mu_n)}{b_n}\to\infty.
\]

For all large `n`, `mu_n<mu`; since the Cramér rate is nondecreasing to the right of the truncated mean,

\[
J_n\ge J_{L_n}(\mu_n).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nJ_n}{b_n}\to\infty.
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Combining (1.1) and (1.2),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{nt_n}{b_n}\to\infty,
\qquad
L_nt_n\to\infty.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The first relation is the key scale separation in this report: the conditioning cost per box, `b_n/n`, is negligible compared with the fixed-level truncated saddlepoint `t_n`.

## 2. Hypothetical full no-condensation and a capped exact-sum coefficient

For the remainder of Sections 2–7, assume for contradiction analysis that

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Choose a deterministic high-probability cap

\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\qquad
U_n\ge2L_n,
\tag{2.2}
\]

such that

\[
\eta_n
:=
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)
\to1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Such a cap exists by a standard diagonal choice from (2.1), and enlarging it to dominate `2L_n` preserves `o(n)` because `L_n=o(n)`.

Define the defective capped exponential generating function

\[
F_{U_n}(t)
:=
\sum_{0\le k\le U_n}\pi_k e^{tk}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The unconditioned probability of the exact-sum event together with the cap is exactly

\[
A_n
:=
\Pr(S_n=m_n,\max_iX_i\le U_n)
=
\eta_n e^{-b_n}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For every `t>=0`, positivity of coefficients gives

\[
A_n
\le
 e^{-tm_n}F_{U_n}(t)^n.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Using `t=t_n`, taking logarithms, and writing `lambda_n=m_n/n`,

\[
\boxed{
\log F_{U_n}(t_n)
\ge
\lambda_n t_n
-rac{b_n}{n}
+rac{\log\eta_n}{n}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

By (1.3), `b_n/n=o(t_n)`. Also `eta_n->1`, so `log eta_n=o(1)`; because `nt_n->infinity`,

\[
\frac{\log\eta_n}{n}=o(t_n).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\log F_{U_n}(t_n)
\ge
\lambda t_n+o(t_n).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
F_{U_n}(t_n)
\ge
1+\lambda t_n+o(t_n).
\tag{2.9}
\]

This lower bound uses only exact coefficient positivity and the fact that the capped event contains asymptotically all of the microcanonical probability under the hypothetical counterexample.

# 3. New theorem: the actual amplified band must saturate the truncated saddlepoint

Separate the low and amplified-band contributions:

\[
\widetilde F_{L_n}(t)
:=
\sum_{0\le k\le L_n}\pi_k e^{tk},
\]

\[
B_n(t)
:=
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\pi_k e^{tk}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus

\[
F_{U_n}(t)=\widetilde F_{L_n}(t)+B_n(t).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The mgf of `Z_{L_n}=X 1_{X<=L_n}` is

\[
M_{L_n}(t)
:=E e^{tZ_{L_n}}
=
\Pr(X>L_n)+\widetilde F_{L_n}(t).
\tag{3.3}
\]

At the Cramér optimizer `t_n`,

\[
\log M_{L_n}(t_n)
=
\mu t_n-J_n.
\tag{3.4}
\]

By (1.1),

\[
J_n=(\mu-\nu)t_n+o(t_n),
\]

so

\[
M_{L_n}(t_n)
=
\exp\{\nu t_n+o(t_n)\}
=
1+\nu t_n+o(t_n).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Because `widetilde F <= M`, (2.9) and (3.5) imply

\[
B_n(t_n)
\ge
(\lambda-\nu)t_n+o(t_n).
\]

Therefore:

## Theorem 3.1 — tilted amplified-band saturation

Under hypothetical full no-condensation, for every deterministic high-probability cap `U_n=o(n)` enlarged past `2L_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}
\pi_k e^{t_nk}
\ge
(\lambda-\nu+o(1))t_n.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

In particular, for some fixed `c_*>0`,

\[
\boxed{
B_n(t_n)\ge c_*t_n
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

This is a new necessary condition of a different type from the retained un-tilted actual-band theorem

\[
Q(L_n,U_n)=e^{-o(b_n)}.
\]

It says that the *same actual sublinear cloud band* must carry a fixed first-order share of the exponential generating mass at the analytically distinguished truncated-Cramér saddlepoint.

No local limit theorem is used in Theorem 3.1.

## 4. Size-biased consequences

Recall

\[
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}.
\]

For `L_n<k<=U_n`,

\[
\pi_k
=
\frac{\nu}{k}\bar q_k
\le
\frac{\nu}{L_n}\bar q_k,
\]

and

\[
e^{t_nk}\le e^{t_nU_n}.
\]

Consequently

\[
B_n(t_n)
\le
\frac{\nu}{L_n}
 e^{t_nU_n}
 Q(L_n,U_n).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Combining with (3.7),

\[
\boxed{
Q(L_n,U_n)
\ge
c_1L_nt_n e^{-t_nU_n}
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for a fixed `c_1>0`.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
t_nU_n
\ge
-\log Q(L_n,U_n)
+
\log(L_nt_n)
-O(1).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equation (4.3) by itself does not close the conjecture: both terms on its right may vary arbitrarily slowly relative to the scales already known. Its value is that it ties the previously obtained un-tilted band mass to the same `t_n` that controls the truncated overload cost.

# 5. New witness-atom theorem inside the actual cloud band

The sum in (3.7) has at most `U_n+1` integer terms. Hence there is a supported

\[
k_n^*\in(L_n,U_n]
\]

such that

\[
\pi_{k_n^*}e^{t_nk_n^*}
\ge
\frac{c_*t_n}{U_n+1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Put again

\[
q_k:=k\pi_k.
\]

Since `k_n^*>L_n`, (5.1) implies

\[
q_{k_n^*}
\ge
c_2\frac{L_nt_n}{U_n+1}
e^{-t_nk_n^*}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Choose the same fixed `c_0>0` as in the record reports and define

\[
r(x)
=
\min_{1\le k\le x,q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(c_0q_k)}k.
\]

From (5.2), after increasing a fixed constant `C`,

\[
-\log(c_0q_{k_n^*})
\le
 t_nk_n^*
+
\log^+\frac{CU_n}{L_nt_n}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
r(U_n)
\le
 t_n
+
\frac1{L_n}
\log^+\frac{CU_n}{L_nt_n}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This converts full no-condensation into an explicit upper bound on the fixed-law lower rate envelope at its *actual* maximum cap.

The correction in (5.4) is support-count free; it comes only from the number of possible integer sizes in the actual cloud band.

# 6. Synchronization with macroscopic strict records: a record–tilt dichotomy

Retain the strict record locations

\[
R_1<R_2<\cdots,
\qquad
h_j=r(R_j).
\]

Use the checked Gaussian-Harnack first-crossing construction to synchronize each macroscopic strict record with an admissible index `n_j` so that

\[
\frac{R_j}{n_j}\to\delta:=\lambda-\nu.
\]

Under hypothetical full no-condensation, write

\[
L_j:=L_{n_j},
\qquad
U_j:=U_{n_j},
\qquad
t_j:=t_{n_j}.
\]

Since `U_j=o(n_j)` and `R_j~delta n_j`,

\[
U_j/R_j\to0.
\]

The cap-scaled record theorem from `07-41-00Z` says

\[
\limsup_j
G_j^{\rm cap}=+\infty,
\qquad
G_j^{\rm cap}:=
U_j(r(U_j)-h_j).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equation (5.4) yields

\[
G_j^{\rm cap}
\le
U_j(t_j-h_j)
+
\frac{U_j}{L_j}
\log^+\frac{CU_j}{L_jt_j}.
\]

Since the left side is nonnegative, we may weaken the first term to its positive part:

\[
\boxed{
G_j^{\rm cap}
\le
U_j(t_j-h_j)_+
+
\frac{U_j}{L_j}
\log^+\frac{CU_j}{L_jt_j}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Hence, on some strict-record subsequence, at least one of the following must diverge:

\[
\boxed{
U_j(t_j-h_j)_+\to\infty
}
\tag{6.3a}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\frac{U_j}{L_j}
\log^+\frac{CU_j}{L_jt_j}\to\infty.
}
\tag{6.3b}
\]

This is the **record–tilt dichotomy**.

It narrows the previous support-entropy obstruction. A surviving full counterexample cannot merely have an unbounded strict-record advantage over its sublinear cap. That advantage must be paid either by a genuine mismatch between the truncated-Cramér tilt `t_j` and the macroscopic record rate `h_j`, or by a quantitatively large width/entropy factor across the actual amplified band.

# 7. Stronger support-sensitive form using the uniform record gap

The `07-41-00Z` report defines, on a strict-record subsequence,

\[
\Gamma_j
:=
\inf_{L_j\le x\le U_j}
 x(r(x)-h_j),
\qquad
\Gamma_j\to\infty.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For every supported `k in (L_j,U_j]`, it proves

\[
-\log(c_0q_k)
\ge
kh_j+\Gamma_j,
\]

or equivalently

\[
q_k
\le
c_0^{-1}e^{-kh_j-\Gamma_j}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since `pi_k=q_k/k`, Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
c_*t_j
\le
\sum_{L_j<k\le U_j}\pi_ke^{t_jk}
\le
c_0^{-1}e^{-\Gamma_j}
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
\frac{e^{(t_j-h_j)k}}k.
\]

Thus we obtain the exact support-sensitive necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j
\le
\log\left[
\frac{C}{t_j}
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
\frac{e^{(t_j-h_j)k}}k
\right].
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This is the main interface between the new truncated-saddlepoint theorem and the previous support-sensitive record program.

A crude support-free corollary follows from

\[
\sum_{L<k\le U}\frac{e^{ak}}k
\le
 e^{a_+U}
\left(1+\log\frac UL\right),
\qquad a_+=\max(a,0),
\]

namely

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_j
\le
(t_j-h_j)_+U_j
+
\log\frac{C\bigl(1+\log(U_j/L_j)\bigr)}{t_j}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

The point of (7.3), however, is precisely not to replace the actual support by every integer unless necessary. In the sparse regime the support-sensitive sum can be dramatically smaller than the geometric majorant.

Define the new **tilted record-band partition term**

\[
\mathcal Z_j^{\rm tilt}
:=
\sum_{\substack{L_j<k\le U_j\\q_k>0}}
\frac{e^{(t_j-h_j)k}}k.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Then any full counterexample must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z_j^{\rm tilt}
\ge
c\,t_j e^{\Gamma_j}
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

on the uniform-gap subsequence.

This is stronger in direction than the prior un-tilted near-envelope count alone: the support-richness required to defeat the strict record is now tested at the *specific Cramér tilt selected by the amplified overload problem*.

## 8. Self-review and what this does not prove

The proof above was checked specifically for the following possible hidden assumptions.

### 8.1 No local-limit input

The coefficient inequality (2.6) is one-sided positivity and does not require a local CLT. The only earlier local statement retained for later record synchronization is the already proved Bernoulli-smoothing Gaussian-Harnack lower bound.

### 8.2 Why `b_n/(nt_n)->0` is legitimate

The amplified diagonal was constructed at moving levels `mu_n downarrow nu`. For fixed `mu>nu`, convexity of the truncated Cramér rate gives `J_L(mu)>=J_L(mu_n)` eventually, and the archived diagonal has `nJ_L(mu_n)/b_n->infinity`. Together with `J_L(mu)~(mu-nu)t_L(mu)` this proves (1.3). No unstated rate for `mathcal R_n` is required.

### 8.3 Defective versus ordinary mgfs

`F_U(t)=sum_{k<=U} pi_k e^{tk}` is defective because the cap discards the tail. This is intentional. By contrast the mgf of `Z_L=X 1_{X<=L}` is

\[
M_L(t)=P(X>L)+\sum_{k<=L}\pi_ke^{tk},
\]

because values above `L` are mapped to zero. The proof only uses the inequality `widetilde F_L(t)<=M_L(t)`, so no tail term is accidentally omitted.

### 8.4 The high-probability cap

Full no-condensation implies existence of a deterministic `U_n=o(n)` with conditional cap probability tending to one. Enlarging it to `max(U_n,2L_n)` remains sublinear. No rate of convergence of that cap probability is assumed.

### 8.5 No solution claim

Equations (6.2), (7.3), and (7.6) are necessary conditions, not contradictions. The quantities `t_j-h_j`, `U_j/L_j`, and the actual support-sensitive tilted partition term can all in principle vary irregularly. A universal theorem ruling out simultaneous satisfaction has not yet been proved.

## 10. Next attack

The most promising next step is to combine the saddlepoint *derivative* identity

\[
\Lambda'_{L_n}(t_n)=\mu
\]

with (3.6), rather than using only the saddlepoint value. The band contribution to the derivative at `t_n` is at least

\[
L_nB_n(t_n)
\gtrsim
L_nt_n\to\infty,
\]

so the capped mgf has derivative far above the target density `lambda` at `t_n`. Therefore its exact capped canonical saddlepoint for mean `lambda_n` lies strictly below `t_n`. The next goal is to quantify this separation in terms of `Gamma_j` and the support-sensitive tilted partition, and then compare the resulting capped canonical KL cost with `b_n/n`. A sufficiently strong separation estimate would potentially eliminate one branch of the record–tilt dichotomy without any local CLT.

A second, complementary route is to split the support in `(L_j,U_j]` according to the tilted excess

\[
-\log(c_0q_k)-h_jk-t_j(k-L_j),
\]

and prove that if the tilted partition in (7.6) is large, then many near-envelope atoms necessarily occur in a window narrow enough to force a substantially cheaper exact residual correction than the generic Gaussian-Harnack penalty.
