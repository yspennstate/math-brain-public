## 1. Uniform subexponential exact-sum cost on compact supercritical density intervals

Work with the normalized boundary probability law `pi`, with finite mean `nu`, and write

\[
p_N(M)=\Pr(S_N=M),
\qquad
b(N,M)=-\log p_N(M)
\]

for admissible exact sums.

Fix constants

\[
\eta>0,
\qquad
\Lambda>\nu+\eta.
\]

For `n>=1`, define

\[
\epsilon_n
:=
\sup\left\{
\frac{b(N,M)}{N}:
\frac n2\le N\le n,
\ \nu+\eta\le M/N\le\Lambda,
\ p_N(M)>0
\right\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

## Lemma 1.1

\[
\boxed{\epsilon_n\to0.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Proof
If not, there are `epsilon>0` and admissible pairs `(N_j,M_j)` with `N_j->infinity`, densities in `[nu+eta,Lambda]`, and

\[
b(N_j,M_j)\ge\epsilon N_j.
\]

By compactness, pass to a subsequence with

\[
M_j/N_j\to\mu\in[\nu+\eta,\Lambda].
\]

After boundary normalization, Janson's Theorem 17.1 has saddle `tau=1` for every `mu>nu`, and therefore

\[
\frac1{N_j}\log p_{N_j}(M_j)\to0,
\]

a contradiction. \(\square\)

This is only a compactness upgrade of Theorem 17.1; no local limit theorem is used.

## 2. Slow cutoff chosen against the uniform exact-sum cost

For an integer cutoff `K`, put

\[
Z^{(K)}:=\xi\mathbf1_{\{\xi\le K\}}
\]

and define the bounded-variable upper-tail rate at the fixed target `nu+eta/2` by

\[
J_K
:=
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left\{
 t(\nu+\eta/2)
-
\log E e^{tZ^{(K)}}
\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Because

\[
E Z^{(K)}\uparrow\nu<\nu+\eta/2,
\]

we have

\[
J_K>0
\]

for every sufficiently large fixed `K`.

Since `epsilon_n->0`, a diagonal choice gives a deterministic cutoff

\[
K_n\to\infty,
\qquad K_n=o(n),
\tag{2.2}
\]

so slowly that

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_n/J_{K_n}\to0.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For example, choose `K_n=j` on a sufficiently late block on which `epsilon_n<=J_j/j` and `n>=j^2`. No monotonicity of `J_K` is required.

## 3. New uniform residual-state overload theorem

For an exact-sum state `(N,M)` in the compact density interval, let

\[
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}
:=
\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
\]

under the conditioned allocation

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N)\mid\{S_N=M\}.
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — compact-density uniform tail-mass lower bound

Uniformly over all admissible states satisfying

\[
n/2\le N\le n,
\qquad
\nu+\eta\le M/N\le\Lambda,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}<\frac\eta4 N
\middle|S_N=M
\right)
\to0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}
:=
E\left[
\frac{T_{N,M}^{>K_n}}M
\middle|S_N=M
\right],
\]

then uniformly over the same states,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\ge a_0+o(1),
\qquad
a_0:=\frac{\eta}{4\Lambda}>0.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
If

\[
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}<\frac\eta4N,
\]

then the contribution from boxes at most `K_n` satisfies

\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
>
M-\frac\eta4N
\ge
(\nu+3\eta/4)N
>
(\nu+\eta/2)N.
\]

Under the unconditioned iid law, Chernoff's bound gives

\[
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^N Z_i^{(K_n)}
\ge(\nu+\eta/2)N
\right)
\le e^{-NJ_{K_n}}.
\]

Divide by the exact conditioning probability `p_N(M)`. By (1.1),

\[
p_N(M)\ge e^{-N\epsilon_n}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Pr\left(
T_{N,M}^{>K_n}<\frac\eta4N
\middle|S_N=M
\right)
\le
\exp\{-N(J_{K_n}-\epsilon_n)\},
\]

which tends to zero uniformly by (2.3). Since `M<=Lambda N`, (3.2) follows. \(\square\)

### Scope check

This theorem is **not** an invocation of Lemma 18.31(ii). The moving cutoff is constructed directly from a uniform exact-sum cost and a bounded truncated Chernoff rate. It is deliberately slow; no claim is made that it can be replaced by the amplified cutoff `L_n` from the previous report.

## 4. Legitimate long iteration of the cloud-conditioned Palm identity

Fix now a constant

\[
0<c<\delta=\lambda-\nu.
\]

Choose

\[
\eta:=\frac{\delta-c}{3}>0
\]

and take `Lambda>lambda+1`. Let `K_n` be the slow cutoff supplied by Theorem 3.1 for these constants.

At an exact-sum state `(N,M)`, select a box by the size-biased Palm kernel conditioned on occupancy exceeding `K_n`; this is the exact cloud-only kernel

\[
K_{N,M}^{(K_n)}(k)
=
\frac{K_{N,M}(k)}{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}}
\mathbf1_{\{k>K_n\}}.
\]

After selecting occupancy `k`, remove that entire box and continue from `(N-1,M-k)`. Stop at the first time `tau` that cumulative removed cloud mass reaches `cn`.

Before stopping, removed mass is below `cn`. Every selected box exceeds `K_n`, so the number already removed is at most `cn/K_n=o(n)`. Hence along every pre-stop state,

\[
N=n-o(n),
\]

and

\[
\frac MN
\ge
\frac{m_n-cn}{n}
\to\lambda-c
=
u+3\eta.
\]

Also `M/N` stays below a fixed `Lambda` for large `n`. Therefore Theorem 3.1 applies **uniformly at every pre-stop state**, and after slightly reducing the constant we may assume

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\ge a_0>0
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

throughout the stopped path.

Let

\[
Q_n:=\sum_{k>K_n}\bar q_k,
\qquad
\bar q_k=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Because `K_n->infinity` and `bar q` is a fixed probability law,

\[
Q_n\to0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The exact one-step identity from the preceding report is

\[
 b(N,M)-E\,b(N-1,M-B)
=
D(K_{N,M}^{(K_n)}\Vert\bar q^{(K_n)})
+\log\frac{M/N}{\nu}
+\log\frac{\alpha_{N,M}^{(K_n)}}{Q_n},
\tag{4.4}
\]

where `bar q^(K_n)` is `bar q` conditioned on `k>K_n`.

Iterate (4.4) to the bounded stopping time `tau`. The KL chain rule gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(P_n^{\rm cloud}\Vert Q_n^{\rm tail})
=
&\ b_n-E\,b(N_\tau,M_\tau)\\
&-E\sum_{j<\tau}\log\frac{M_j/N_j}{\nu}\\
&-E\sum_{j<\tau}\log\frac{\alpha_{N_j,M_j}^{(K_n)}}{Q_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Every term on the left is nonnegative. The terminal exact conditioning cost is nonnegative. The residual density is uniformly at least `nu+eta`, so

\[
\log\frac{M_j/N_j}{\nu}
\ge
\log\left(1+\frac\eta\nu\right).
\]

Together with (4.1), this yields the new exact cloud-count budget

\[
\boxed{
E\tau\,
\left[
\log\left(1+\frac\eta\nu\right)
+
\log\frac{a_0}{Q_n}
\right]
\le b_n.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

For all large `n`, `Q_n<a_0`, so the bracket is positive. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
E\tau
\le
\frac{b_n}{
\log((1+\eta/\nu)a_0/Q_n)
}.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Since `Q_n->0`, the denominator diverges and therefore

\[
\boxed{E\tau=o(b_n).}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This is the first long cloud-only Palm telescope in the campaign whose residual-state tail probability is proved uniformly rather than assumed.

## 5. Consequences for the maximum and for any hypothetical no-condensation cap

The cloud-only process removes at least `cn` mass by time `tau`. Deterministically,

\[
\max_{1\le j\le\tau}B_j\ge\frac{cn}{\tau}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Write

\[
H_n:=
\log\left((1+\eta/\nu)a_0/Q_n\right)
\to\infty.
\tag{5.2}
\]

From (4.7), for any `A_n->infinity`, Markov's inequality gives

\[
\Pr\left(\tau>A_n\frac{b_n}{H_n}\right)
\le\frac1{A_n}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Choosing, for example,

\[
A_n=\sqrt{H_n},
\]

yields the unconditional mesoscopic lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
Y_{(1)}
\ge
c\frac{n}{b_n}\sqrt{H_n}
\right)
\to1.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a fixed-tail version of the previously obtained amplified lower scale: the factor `sqrt(H_n)` is explicitly determined by the tail of the single fixed size-biased boundary law at the rigorously chosen slow cutoff.

Now assume hypothetically

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\]

and choose any deterministic high-probability cap `U_n=o(n)`. On the cap event, every cloud-only selected box is at most `U_n`, so to remove `cn` mass requires

\[
\tau\ge\frac{cn}{U_n}.
\]

Hence

\[
E\tau\ge(1-o(1))\frac{cn}{U_n}.
\]

Combine with (4.7):

\[
\boxed{
U_n
\ge
(1-o(1))
\frac{cn}{b_n}
H_n.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus every full-sequence no-condensation counterexample must admit only caps that are at least a **diverging fixed-tail multiple** of the basic `n/b_n` scale.

Equation (5.5) is not by itself a contradiction: `H_n` may grow arbitrarily slowly, and `U_n=o(n)` is compatible with a diverging multiple of `n/b_n` because `b_n->infinity`. Its value is that the multiplier is now tied to the actual fixed law through `Q_n`, not only to an existential diagonal amplification index.

## 6. Interaction with the amplified cutoff and remaining gap

The slow cutoff `K_n` and amplified cutoff `L_n` play different roles:

- `K_n` is chosen slowly enough that uniform truncated overload survives along **all residual exact-sum states** in a compact supercritical density interval. This enables the rigorous cloud-only Palm telescope.
- `L_n=R_n n/b_n` is much larger and captures essentially the complete missing mass in the original conditioned state. Uniform residual-state lower bounds at this larger cutoff remain unproved.

A natural next target is to bridge the two scales. If one could choose `K_n` while also forcing either

\[
H_n\gg R_n
\]

or a comparable record-rate advantage between `K_n` and the macroscopic record scale, then (5.5) would collide with the previously accumulated amplified-cloud/record constraints. No such universal comparison is proved here.
