# 1. The missing exact-sum lemma can be proved without a local CLT

The preceding checkpoint identified a multi-atom exact-sum lower bound for the capped canonical family as the next missing bridge. The first result of this run supplies such a bound with a variance-dependent loss.

As in the checked Gaussian-Harnack report, divide the fixed lattice by its span first. I state the span-one version; the general span-`d` version is the same argument on the admissible rescaled lattice.

Because the fixed boundary law `pi` has span one, there exist a fixed integer `ell>=1` and an integer `a` such that

\[
\pi^{*\ell}(a)>0,\qquad \pi^{*\ell}(a+1)>0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For each `n`, let `p_n` denote the one-variable capped canonical law. Since `s_n->0`, `F_n(s_n)->1`, and `U_n->infinity`,

\[
p_n(k)\to\pi_k
\]

for every fixed `k`. Hence

\[
p_n^{*\ell}(a)\to\pi^{*\ell}(a)>0,
\qquad
p_n^{*\ell}(a+1)\to\pi^{*\ell}(a+1)>0.
\]

Therefore there is one fixed `epsilon>0` such that, for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
\boxed{
 p_n^{*\ell}=\varepsilon\rho+(1-\varepsilon)\zeta_n,
}
\tag{1.2}
\]

where `rho` is the uniform law on `{a,a+1}` and `zeta_n` is a probability law.

Partition the first `ell floor(n/ell)` canonical variables into blocks of length `ell`. Realize every block through (1.2), with independent Bernoulli(`epsilon`) type indicators. Let `K` be the number of `rho` blocks and choose

\[
N=\left\lfloor\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left\lfloor\frac n\ell\right\rfloor\right\rfloor.
\]

Chernoff gives

\[
\Pr(K<N)\le e^{-c n}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

On `G={K>=N}`, select the first `N` successful `rho` blocks. Their increments above `a` are iid Bernoulli(`1/2`) and, because the selection depends only on the type indicators, are independent of the unselected remainder. Thus under the conditional law given `G`,

\[
S_n=aN+B_N+R_n,
\qquad
B_N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2),
\tag{1.4}
\]

with `B_N` independent of `R_n`.

The event `G^c` is exponentially rare, while `0<=S_n<=nU_n`. Since the unconditional canonical mean is exactly `m_n`,

\[
|E(S_n\mid G)-m_n|
\le CnU_ne^{-cn}=o(1).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Likewise

\[
\operatorname{Var}(S_n\mid G)
\le \frac{E[(S_n-m_n)^2]}{\Pr(G)}
\le 2n\sigma_n^2
\tag{1.6}
\]

for large `n`, and independence in (1.4) gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(R_n\mid G)\le2n\sigma_n^2.
\tag{1.7}
\]

By Chebyshev,

\[
\Pr\left(
 |R_n-E(R_n\mid G)|\le 2\sqrt{2n(1+\sigma_n^2)}
 \mid G
\right)\ge\frac34.
\tag{1.8}
\]

For every residual value in this event, the required binomial displacement from `N/2` is at most

\[
C\sqrt{n(1+\sigma_n^2)}+o(1).
\tag{1.9}
\]

Because the canonical support is contained in `[0,U_n]` and its mean stays bounded,

\[
\sigma_n^2\le E X^2\le U_nEX\le C U_n=o(n).
\tag{1.10}
\]

Hence the displacement in (1.9) is `o(n)` and is eventually inside the central half of the binomial support. Stirling's formula gives fixed constants `c,C>0` such that, uniformly over all such displacements,

\[
\Pr(B_N=x)
\ge
\frac{c}{\sqrt n}
\exp\left\{-C\frac{(x-N/2)^2}{n}\right\}
\ge
\frac{c}{\sqrt n}e^{-C(1+\sigma_n^2)}.
\tag{1.11}
\]

Averaging (1.11) over the residual event (1.8), rather than pigeonholing one residual value, loses no additional polynomial factor. Multiplying by `Pr(G)=1-o(1)` proves:

## Theorem 1.1 — canonical exact-mean smoothing lower bound

Under hypothetical full no-condensation,

\[
\boxed{
P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n)
\ge
\frac{c}{\sqrt n}\exp\{-C(1+\sigma_n^2)\}.
}
\tag{1.12}
\]

This is precisely the kind of exact-sum lower bound sought in the previous checkpoint. It uses the fixed Bernoulli smoothing component and only a second-moment estimate; it does **not** assume a triangular-array local CLT or a Lindeberg condition.

# 2. Matching universal anti-concentration for the same triangular family

The same block decomposition also gives a uniform upper bound.

Condition on all block-type indicators. On `G={K>=N}`, the sum contains an independent convolution factor `Bin(N,1/2)`, so

\[
\sup_m\Pr(S_n=m\mid\text{type indicators})
\le
\sup_x\Pr(B_N=x)
\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.
\]

The complement of `G` has exponentially small probability. Therefore

## Theorem 2.1 — canonical anti-concentration

\[
\boxed{
\sup_m P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m)\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular, with

\[
\ell_n:=-\log P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n),
\tag{2.2}
\]

Theorems 1.1 and 2.1 give the two-sided exact-mean window

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\log n-O(1)
\le
\ell_n
\le
\frac12\log n+C\sigma_n^2+O(1).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The previous run stopped because a local CLT was unjustified. Equation (2.3) replaces that missing CLT by a robust deterministic-smoothing statement. The remaining price is the variance term `C sigma_n^2`, which can be as large as order `U_n` under the present information.

# 3. New asymptotic identity for the capped canonical rate

The next result sharpens the previous crude `I_n<=b_n/n` bound.

Write

\[
A_n(t)=\sum_{k\le L_n}\pi_ke^{tk},
\qquad
B_n(t)=\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}\pi_ke^{tk},
\qquad
F_n=A_n+B_n.
\]

Also put

\[
T_n=\Pr_\pi(X>L_n),
\qquad
M_n(t)=T_n+A_n(t)=Ee^{tX\mathbf1_{\{X\le L_n\}}}.
\]

For every `0<=t<=s_n<theta_n`, the truncated logarithmic derivative is trapped between its value at zero and its value at `theta_n`:

\[
E[X;X\le L_n]
\le
\frac{M_n'(t)}{M_n(t)}
\le
\mu_n.
\]

Both endpoints tend to `nu`, and `1<=M_n(t)<=M_n(theta_n)->1`. Hence, uniformly on `[0,s_n]`,

\[
A_n'(t)=M_n'(t)=\nu+o(1).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Also `F_n(0)->1` and `F_n(s_n)->1`, so uniformly on the same interval

\[
F_n(t)=1+o(1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, for the canonical mean function

\[
\varphi_n(t)=F_n'(t)/F_n(t),
\]

we have uniformly

\[
\varphi_n(t)
\ge\nu-o(1),
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
\varphi_n(t)
\le\nu+o(1)+(1+o(1))B_n'(t).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Now

\[
I_n-I_n(0)
=
\int_0^{s_n}(\lambda_n-\varphi_n(t))dt,
\tag{3.5}
\]

where

\[
I_n(0)=-\log F_n(0)=-\log\Pr_\pi(X\le U_n).
\]

Finite mean gives

\[
I_n(0)=o(1/U_n).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Since `U_n>=2L_n` and `n/L_n=(1+o(1))b_n/\mathcal R_n`,

\[
nI_n(0)=o(n/U_n)=o(b_n).
\tag{3.7}
\]

For the upper bound in (3.5), (3.3) gives

\[
I_n\le I_n(0)+(\delta+o(1))s_n.
\tag{3.8}
\]

For the lower bound, integrate (3.4). The band derivative is increasing and the already proved canonical first-moment saturation gives

\[
B_n'(s_n)=\delta+o(1).
\]

Since every band point exceeds `L_n`,

\[
B_n(s_n)\le \frac{B_n'(s_n)}{L_n}=O(1/L_n),
\]

and hence

\[
\int_0^{s_n}B_n'(t)dt
=B_n(s_n)-B_n(0)
\le O(1/L_n).
\tag{3.9}
\]

Thus

\[
I_n
\ge
(\delta-o(1))s_n-O(1/L_n).
\tag{3.10}
\]

The exact cap identity gives

\[
nI_n\le b_n-\log\eta_n=b_n+o(1).
\tag{3.11}
\]

Combining (3.10), (3.11), and `n/L_n=(1+o(1))b_n/\mathcal R_n=o(b_n)` first yields

\[
\boxed{ns_n=O(b_n).}
\tag{3.12}
\]

Now multiply (3.8) and (3.10) by `n`. Equations (3.7), (3.9), (3.12), and `\mathcal R_n->infinity` make every error `o(b_n)`. Therefore:

## Theorem 3.1 — canonical saddle energy asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
 nI_n=\delta n s_n+o(b_n).
}
\tag{3.13}
\]

This is stronger than the previous rate budget: the exponential cost of the capped I-projection is asymptotically its saddle energy `delta n s_n`, up to an error negligible on the actual conditioning scale `b_n`.

# 4. Three-term decomposition of the full conditioning cost

The exact change-of-measure identity is

\[
nI_n=b_n-\log\eta_n-\ell_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
b_n=nI_n+\ell_n+\log\eta_n.
\]

Since `eta_n->1`, Theorem 3.1 gives the new decomposition

## Theorem 4.1 — conditioning cost decomposition

\[
\boxed{
 b_n=\delta n s_n+\ell_n+o(b_n),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

where the exact lattice/local term satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\log n-O(1)
\le
\ell_n
\le
\frac12\log n+C\sigma_n^2+O(1).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus every hypothetical full counterexample must pay its exact conditioning cost in three conceptually distinct pieces:

1. **canonical tilt energy:** `delta n s_n`;
2. **unavoidable lattice smoothing cost:** at least `(1/2) log n-O(1)`;
3. **non-Gaussian / high-variance local defect:** at most `C sigma_n^2+O(1)` beyond the universal half-logarithmic term.

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\delta n s_n
\le
b_n-\frac12\log n+o(b_n)+O(1).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
 b_n-\delta n s_n
\le
\frac12\log n+C\sigma_n^2+o(b_n)+O(1).
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

A useful conditional corollary is that whenever

\[
\sigma_n^2=o(b_n),
\qquad
\log n=o(b_n),
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\delta n s_n\sim b_n.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The universal problem remains harder because neither condition in this corollary is presently known: the retained variance window is only

\[
cL_n\le\sigma_n^2\le CU_n.
\]

# 5. New synchronization with the one-giant record comparison

This decomposition also sharpens the previously archived record-vs-fragmented comparison.

Let `R_j` be synchronized strict record sizes and `n_j` their Gaussian-Harnack first-crossing indices, so

\[
R_j/n_j\to\delta.
\]

Write

\[
h_j=r(R_j),
\qquad
A_j=R_jh_j=-\log(c_0q_{R_j}),
\qquad q_k=k\pi_k.
\]

Let `D_j=o(n_j)` denote the residual displacement in the checked Harnack theorem and abbreviate its residual local-loss bound by

\[
\mathcal H_j
=
\log W_{n_j-1}
+C\frac{D_j^2+D_j+1}{n_j}+O(1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The one-coordinate giant contribution obeys

\[
\Pr(X_1=R_j,S_{n_j}=m_{n_j})
\ge
\exp\{-A_j-\log R_j-\mathcal H_j+O(1)\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Under full no-condensation, `R_j/n_j->delta>0` forces the corresponding conditioned probability to tend to zero. Since the total exact-sum probability is `e^{-b_{n_j}}`, (5.2) therefore implies the necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
 b_{n_j}-A_j-\log R_j-\mathcal H_j\to-\infty.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Insert (4.2) and the lower bound `ell_j>= (1/2)log n_j-O(1)`. Since `log R_j=log n_j+O(1)`, every hypothetical counterexample must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\delta n_js_{n_j}-R_jh_j
\le
\mathcal H_j+rac12\log n_j+o(b_{n_j})-\omega(1).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is a more transparent record-saddle synchronization constraint than the earlier comparison written directly in terms of `b_n`: the unavoidable half-logarithmic exact-lattice cost has been separated explicitly.

Combine this with the previously proved uniform record-band support obstruction

\[
\Gamma_j
\le
(s_{n_j}-h_j)_+U_{n_j}
+\log N_j^{\rm supp}+O(1).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equation (5.4) shows that the `s_j>h_j` branch can survive only if the Harnack alignment loss is large enough after multiplication by the small ratio `U_j/n_j`; while (5.5) shows that otherwise the actual amplified band must be support-rich on the `Gamma_j` scale. This sharpens, but does not eliminate, the near-envelope-rich regime identified in the previous record reports.

A sufficient contradiction criterion now reads: along any synchronized strict-record subsequence on which

\[
\frac{n_j}{U_j}
\bigl(\Gamma_j-\log N_j^{\rm supp}-O(1)\bigr)_+
\gg
\mathcal H_j+\log n_j+|R_j-\delta n_j|h_j+o(b_{n_j}),
\tag{5.6}
\]

full no-condensation is impossible. The remaining universal issue is proving that some subsequence must satisfy a criterion of this kind, or handling the complementary support-rich branch by a stronger multi-atom comparison.

# 6. What this run closes, and what it does not

### Newly proved this run

1. The capped canonical triangular family inherits a **uniform fixed Bernoulli smoothing block** from the one fixed boundary law.
2. Exact-mean lower bound, without a local CLT:
   \[
   P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}e^{-C(1+\sigma_n^2)}.
   \]
3. Matching anti-concentration:
   \[
   \sup_mP_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m)\le Cn^{-1/2}.
   \]
4. The exact local penalty is trapped by
   \[
   \frac12\log n-O(1)\le\ell_n\le\frac12\log n+C\sigma_n^2+O(1).
   \]
5. The capped canonical rate satisfies
   \[
   nI_n=\delta ns_n+o(b_n),\qquad ns_n=O(b_n).
   \]
6. The full conditioning cost decomposes as
   \[
   b_n=\delta ns_n+\ell_n+o(b_n).
   \]
7. The record-vs-giant comparison therefore has the sharper necessary form (5.4), with the universal half-logarithmic exact-lattice cost explicit.

### Still open

The variance defect can be large:

\[
\sigma_n^2\le CU_n,
\]

and `U_n=o(n)` alone does not make `sigma_n^2` small relative to `b_n` or to the synchronized record advantage. Thus Theorem 1.1 solves the **exact-sum lower-bound lemma** identified in the previous checkpoint, but its variance-dependent loss is not yet universally small enough to close Problem 18.39.

The most useful next attack is now narrower: exploit the canonical band identity

\[
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n}k\pi_ke^{s_nk}=\delta+o(1)
\]

to control `sigma_n^2` or to turn a large `sigma_n^2` into a stronger record-band advantage. A natural dichotomy is:

- **moderate canonical variance:** the new exact-sum theorem makes the local penalty small enough for the one-giant record comparison;
- **large canonical variance:** a non-negligible fraction of the canonical excess first moment must lie at larger band scales, which should strengthen the cap-scaled record penalty or permit a second-level truncation at an endogenous variance quantile.

That variance/record dichotomy is the next concrete target. No universal contradiction is claimed here.
