\tag{2.1}

## 4. Mathematical idea before proof

### B7 — compare the correct subsequential scaling object

The previous route compared the source to a target with essentially the same macroscopic mass in one favorable coordinate. That is impossible when favorable sites have geometric gaps.

The right scaled object is not a landing distance but a **normalized target energy**. For `0<p<1`, measure the cost of a macroscopic decomposition `x_1+...+x_r=1` by

\[
\mathcal E_p(x_1,\ldots,x_r)=\sum_r x_r^p.
\tag{4.1}
\]

A sparse law may have no favorable site near a given total and still admit a finite decomposition whose normalized energy is bounded uniformly over all scales. Compare that target energy to the minimum energy of every configuration whose largest component is at most a fixed fraction `alpha` of the missing mass.

This comparison survives scale changes and is exactly what the partition-function ratio sees.

### B23 — construct one fixed law with the opposite-looking geometry

Take favorable sites only at powers of two. The gap to the preceding favorable site is a positive fraction of scale, exactly the regime the previous theorem left uncovered. Make every non-power-of-two weight catastrophically smaller, of order `exp(-k^2)`.

At first sight this is a strong candidate for sparse-scale dust because neither the target total nor its first residual need be favorable. But the binary expansion of an integer glues the geometric scales with only logarithmically many favorable pieces. The question becomes whether that finite binary energy is cheaper than forcing all macroscopic boxes below a cap.

### B30 — solve each sparse scale, then identify the gluing obstruction

For powers of two the gluing obstruction is explicit. Binary expansion gives a worst-case normalized target energy

\[
C_p:=\frac{1}{1-2^{-p}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a cap fraction `alpha`, the cheapest way to split unit mass into pieces at most `alpha` has energy

\[
H_p(\alpha)
:=q\alpha^p+(1-q\alpha)^p,
\qquad q=\lfloor 1/\alpha\rfloor.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If

\[
H_p(\alpha)>C_p,
\tag{4.4}
\]

then capped macroscopic fragmentation is exponentially more expensive than the canonical sparse-scale target, by order `n^p`, while the exact combinatorial multiplicity is only `exp(o(n^p))`. This is the new persistence mechanism.

## 5. A finite-energy decomposition criterion

### Definition 5.1 — finite-energy target class

Fix `0<p<1` and constants `a>0`, `C_+>0`, `B>1`, `C_*>0`, `L_0>0`.

Let one fixed full-support radius-one weight law satisfy

\[
\Phi(1)<\infty,
\qquad
0<\nu<\infty,
\tag{5.1}
\]

and the global upper envelope

\[
\boxed{w_k\le C_+e^{-a k^p}\qquad(k\ge0).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume that every sufficiently large integer `c` has a deterministic canonical target tuple

\[
\mathbf z(c)=(z_1(c),\ldots,z_{\ell(c)}(c)),
\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^{\ell(c)}z_r(c)=c,
\tag{5.3}
\]

with

\[
\ell(c)\le L_0\log(c+2)
\tag{5.4}
\]

and product-weight lower bound

\[
\boxed{
\prod_{r=1}^{\ell(c)} w_{z_r(c)}
\ge
B^{-\ell(c)}e^{-a C_* c^p}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Padding the target with zeros changes only the factor `B^{-s}` after enlarging `B` to absorb `w_0`.

This criterion is deliberately representation-based. It does not require one favorable site near `c`, monotonicity, regular variation, or a direct lower bound on any residual integer.

## 6. Exact cap-energy lemma

Fix `d>0` and `0<t<d`. Define

\[
q=\lfloor d/t\rfloor,
\qquad
r=d-qt\in[0,t).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — minimum concave energy under a cap

Among all finite families `x_i>=0` satisfying

\[
\sum_i x_i=d,
\qquad
x_i\le t,
\tag{6.2}
\]

the minimum of `sum_i x_i^p` is

\[
\boxed{
E_p(d,t)=q t^p+r^p.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
E_p(d,t)=d^p H_p(t/d),
\tag{6.4}
\]

with `H_p` from (4.3).

### Proof

The function `x^p` is strictly concave on `(0,infinity)` and vanishes at zero. If two coordinates `x,y` both lie strictly between zero and `t`, replace them by

\[
(x',y')=(\min\{t,x+y\},\max\{0,x+y-t\}).
\tag{6.5}
\]

This preserves their sum and makes the pair more unequal. Concavity therefore gives

\[
(x')^p+(y')^p\le x^p+y^p.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Iterating leaves at most one coordinate strictly between `0` and `t`: exactly `q` coordinates equal `t`, one coordinate equals `r` if `r>0`, and all others are zero. This proves (6.3). `square`

The same statement for integer occupancies after scaling by `n` has an `o(n^p)` rounding error, uniformly when `d` and `t` stay fixed positive constants.

## 7. Conditioning denominator and the missing mass above a power cutoff

Choose once and for all

\[
1-p<\gamma<1,
\qquad
K_n=\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `X,X_1,...` be iid with law `pi_k=w_k/Phi(1)`.

### Lemma 7.1 — exact-conditioning denominator

There is `C<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge n^{-1/2}e^{-C n^p}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

### Proof

Let

\[
L_n=\lceil L_1\log(n+2)\rceil
\tag{7.3}
\]

with `L_1` large enough for every canonical target up to `m_n=O(n)` to fit in `L_n` coordinates. Put `N=n-L_n` and choose an integer `r_n` nearest `nu N`. Full support gives span one; the stretched envelope gives finite positive variance. The lattice local central limit theorem yields

\[
P(S_N=r_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Set

\[
c_n=m_n-r_n=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Force the remaining `L_n` coordinates to equal the canonical target `z(c_n)`, padded with zeros. By (5.5), after normalization by `Phi(1)`, this target probability is at least

\[
\exp\{-aC_*c_n^p-O(L_n)\}
\ge e^{-Cn^p}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Independence gives (7.2). `square`

### Lemma 7.2 — tail-adapted upper deviation below `K`

Let

\[
Z_K=X\mathbf1_{\{X\le K\}},
\qquad
t_K=(a/4)K^{p-1}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Then

\[
\log Ee^{t_KZ_K}=\nu t_K+o(t_K)
\tag{7.8}
\]

as `K->infinity`. Consequently, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left(\sum_{i=1}^n Z_{i,K}\ge(\nu+\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K^{1-p}}\right\}.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

### Proof

For `1<=x<=K`, because `p-1<0`,

\[
t_Kx=(a/4)K^{p-1}x\le(a/4)x^p.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{e^{t_Kx}-1}{t_K}
\le xe^{(a/4)x^p},
\tag{7.11}
\]

and after multiplication by `pi_x<=Ce^{-a x^p}` the right side is dominated by the summable sequence `Cx e^{-(3a/4)x^p}`. Dominated convergence gives

\[
\frac{Ee^{t_KZ_K}-1}{t_K}\to EX=\nu,
\tag{7.12}
\]

which implies (7.8). Chernoff at `t_K` gives (7.9). `square`

A fixed negative tilt similarly gives, for every `epsilon>0` and all large `K`,

\[
P\left(\sum_iZ_{i,K}\le(\nu-\epsilon)n\right)\le e^{-c_\epsilon n}.
\tag{7.13}
\]

### Corollary 7.3 — conditioned missing mass at the chosen cutoff

Under the exact balls-in-boxes conditioning,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{7.14}
\]

### Proof

Divide (7.9) at `K=K_n` by (7.2). Its negative exponent has scale

\[
\frac{n}{K_n^{1-p}}
=n^{1-\gamma(1-p)+o(1)}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Because `gamma<1`,

\[
1-\gamma(1-p)>p.
\tag{7.16}
\]

Hence (7.9) beats the `e^{-O(n^p)}` denominator. Equation (7.13) beats it because `p<1`. Therefore the conditioned mass at or below `K_n` is `nu n+o_p(n)`. Subtract from `m_n=lambda n+o(n)`. `square`

This is the only place an iid deviation estimate is divided by the conditioning event; the comparison below is performed directly in the conditioned partition function.

## 8. Exact coalescence of all macroscopic fragments

Fix `0<t<delta`. Let

\[
\mathcal A_n(t)=\{Y_{(1)}\le tn\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Let `G_n` be the event that the mass above `K_n` lies in `[delta n-o(n),delta n+o(n)]`; by Corollary 7.3, `P(G_n)->1`.

For a source allocation `y` in `A_n(t) cap G_n`, let

\[
B(y)=\{i:y_i>K_n\},
\qquad r(y)=|B(y)|.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since total mass is `O(n)`,

\[
r(y)\le \frac{m_n}{K_n}=O(n^{1-\gamma}).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Choose a deterministic selected set `I(y)` as follows:

- if `r(y)>=L_n`, let `I(y)=B(y)`;
- if `r(y)<L_n`, take all of `B(y)` and add the smallest unused labels until `|I(y)|=L_n`.

Write

\[
s(y)=|I(y)|,
\qquad C(y)=\sum_{i\in I(y)}y_i.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Added filler coordinates are at most `K_n`, so

\[
L_nK_n=o(n).
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus uniformly on `A_n(t) cap G_n`,

\[
C(y)=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{8.6}
\]

The canonical target for `C(y)` fits inside `I(y)`: if `r>=L_n` then `s=r>=L_n`; if `r<L_n` then `s=L_n`. Replace all occupancies on `I(y)` by `z(C(y))` and pad with zeros; leave every other box unchanged. Call the target `T(y)`.

This is an exact finite map preserving the number of boxes, total mass, and fixed weight law.

### Lemma 8.1 — source energy under the cap

Uniformly on `A_n(t) cap G_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i\in B(y)} y_i^p
\ge
n^p E_p(\delta,t)-o(n^p).
}
\tag{8.7}
\]

### Proof

The coordinates in `B(y)` have total `delta n+o(n)` by `G_n`, and every coordinate is at most `tn` by `A_n(t)`. Scale by `n`, apply Lemma 6.1, and absorb the `o(n)` total-mass and integer-rounding errors by continuity of the finite-dimensional minimum. `square`

### Lemma 8.2 — source/target weight ratio

If

\[
\boxed{E_p(\delta,t)>C_*\delta^p,}
\tag{8.8}
\]

then there is `eta>0` such that uniformly on `A_n(t) cap G_n`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\prod_iw_{y_i}}{\prod_iw_{T(y)_i}}
\le
\exp\{-\eta n^p+o(n^p)+O(s(y))\}.
}
\tag{8.9}
\]

### Proof

Outside `I(y)` the weights cancel. On `I(y)`, the global envelope gives

\[
\prod_{i\in I(y)}w_{y_i}
\le
C_+^{s(y)}
\exp\left\{-a\sum_{i\in I(y)}y_i^p\right\}
\le
C_+^{s(y)}
\exp\{-aE_p(\delta,t)n^p+o(n^p)\},
\tag{8.10}
\]

using (8.7). The target lower bound (5.5), padded to `s(y)` entries, is

\[
\prod_{i\in I(y)}w_{T(y)_i}
\ge
B^{-s(y)}e^{-aC_*C(y)^p}
=
B^{-s(y)}e^{-aC_*\delta^pn^p+o(n^p)}.
\tag{8.11}
\]

The strict gap (8.8) gives a fixed positive coefficient in front of `n^p`. `square`

### Lemma 8.3 — preimage entropy is subleading

For a fixed target allocation `x`, the number of source allocations in `A_n(t) cap G_n` mapping to `x` is at most

\[
\exp\{O(n^{1-\gamma}\log n)\}.
\tag{8.12}
\]

In particular, because `gamma>1-p`,

\[
\boxed{n^{1-\gamma}\log n=o(n^p).}
\tag{8.13}
\]

### Proof

Every source selected set has size at most

\[
R_n:=O(n^{1-\gamma})+L_n.
\tag{8.14}
\]

For a crude exact upper bound, choose the selected set and then choose each source occupancy in `[0,m_n]`. Hence the number of source records is at most

\[
\sum_{s\le R_n}\binom ns(m_n+1)^s
\le
(R_n+1)\,[n(m_n+1)]^{R_n},
\tag{8.15}
\]

whose logarithm is `O(R_n log n)`. Since `1-gamma<p`, (8.13) follows. `square`

### Proposition 8.4 — finite-energy persistence criterion

Under Definition 5.1, if `0<t<delta` satisfies (8.8), then

\[
\boxed{P(Y_{(1)}>tn)\longrightarrow1.}
\tag{8.16}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ge t.
}
\tag{8.17}
\]

### Proof

Expand the unnormalized partition-function weight of `A_n(t) cap G_n`. Map every source allocation to `T(y)`. Lemma 8.2 gives an `e^{-eta n^p+o(n^p)}` weight loss. Lemma 8.3 bounds the total preimage entropy by `e^{o(n^p)}`. Summing target weights gives at most the full partition function `Z(m_n,n)`. Therefore

\[
P(A_n(t)\cap G_n)\le e^{-\eta n^p/2}
\tag{8.18}
\]

for all large `n`. Since `P(G_n)->1`, (8.16) follows. Uniform boundedness `Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)` then gives (8.17). `square`

This proposition is unconditional and full-sequence. It does not infer an unconditional frozen metric from a conditional-on-a-subsequence estimate.

## 9. Dyadic sparse favorable scales

Fix `0<p<1` and define favorable sites

\[
G=\{2^j:j\ge0\}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Consider a law whose favorable-site weights are

\[
w_{2^j}=e^{-a(2^j)^p}
\tag{9.2}
\]

and whose other positive weights obey the same global upper envelope but may be arbitrarily smaller.

For an integer `c`, write its binary expansion

\[
c=\sum_{j\in J(c)}2^j.
\tag{9.3}
\]

This uses at most `1+log_2 c` favorable coordinates. If `2^J<=c<2^(J+1)`, then

\[
\sum_{j\in J(c)}(2^j)^p
\le
\sum_{j=0}^J2^{jp}
<
\frac{2^{p(J+1)}}{2^p-1}
\le
\frac{2^p}{2^p-1}c^p.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Thus the target-energy constant is

\[
\boxed{
C_p=\frac{2^p}{2^p-1}=\frac{1}{1-2^{-p}}.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

The positive-fraction gaps are real: for `c=2^(J+1)-1`, the largest favorable site below `c` is `2^J`, and

\[
\frac{c-2^J}{c}\to\frac12.
\tag{9.6}
\]

So the previous sublinear-gap theorem cannot be transferred to this class.

### Corollary 9.1

Let `alpha_p` be the threshold

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_p
:=
\sup\{\alpha\in(0,1):H_p(\alpha)>C_p\}.
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

Then `alpha_p>0`, and for every `alpha<alpha_p`, every full admissible `m_n/n->lambda>nu` satisfies

\[
P\left(\frac{Y_{(1)}}n>\alpha(\lambda-\nu)\right)\to1.
\tag{9.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\alpha_p(\lambda-\nu).
}
\tag{9.9}
\]

### Proof

For `t=alpha delta`, Lemma 6.1 gives

\[
E_p(\delta,t)=\delta^pH_p(\alpha).
\tag{9.10}
\]

Binary expansion gives `C_*=C_p`. Proposition 8.4 applies whenever `H_p(alpha)>C_p`. Let `alpha` increase to `alpha_p` in (8.17). `square`

The positivity of `alpha_p` follows because as `alpha downarrow0`, the minimum number of capped pieces grows like `1/alpha` and

\[
H_p(\alpha)\asymp\alpha^{p-1}\to\infty.
\tag{9.11}
\]

This theorem does not claim one-giant condensation; it certifies the source-relevant persistent macroscopic alternative despite fixed-fraction sparse-scale gaps.
