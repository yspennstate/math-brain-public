## 2. Exact source target and the source cutoff that is actually available

Primary source rechecked 2026-09-17:

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton--Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), 103--252, arXiv:1112.0510v1, Section 18.6, printed page 122, Problem 18.39.

The currently exposed arXiv record is still v1, dated 2 December 2011.

The exact source asks whether

\[
 Y_{(1)}/n\xrightarrow p0
\]

can occur for a **single fixed weight sequence** when

\[
 0<\nu<\lambda,\qquad m_n/n\to\lambda.
\]

Example 18.36 shows that such vanishing can occur on a subsequence. Janson conjectures that it cannot occur on the full sequence and states the slightly stronger expected alternative that some fixed `epsilon>0` and some subsequence satisfy `Y_(1)>epsilon n` with high probability. Problem 18.40 is separately the `nu=0` question.

The exact source lemma used below is Janson Lemma 18.31. If `w_0>0`, the support is unbounded, `m_n/n->lambda`, and `nu<lambda<infinity`, then:

1. for every deterministic cutoff `K_n->infinity`, the mass above the cutoff is at most

\[
 (\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n);
\tag{2.1}
\]

2. there exists a deterministic source-produced `Omega_n->infinity` such that for every `K_n->infinity` with `K_n<=Omega_n`, the mass above the cutoff is exactly

\[
 (\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
\tag{2.2}
\]

The distinction is essential: equality is not claimed for an arbitrary fast cutoff.

Put

\[
 \delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For later use, (2.1) with any `K_n->infinity`, `K_n=o(n)`, gives

\[
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta+o_p(1).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Since `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`,

\[
 \limsup_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus `delta` is the ceiling for the frozen metric.

## 3. B1 — change category: from binary scales to a bounded mixed-radix numeration system

Fix once and for all an integer sequence

\[
 2\le c_r\le Q<\infty,\qquad r\ge1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `Q` is a fixed finite integer. Define supported scales

\[
 s_0:=1,\qquad s_r:=c_rs_{r-1}\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus

\[
 2^r\le s_r\le Q^r.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The dyadic law in the previous report is the special case `c_r=2`. A concrete genuinely new example is

\[
 c_r=\begin{cases}2,&r\text{ odd},\\3,&r\text{ even},\end{cases}
\tag{3.4}
\]

so successive favorable scales alternate by factors two and three.

Fix `eta>0` and define

\[
 u_r:=\frac1{(r+2)(\log(r+2))^{1+\eta}}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Define one fixed weight law by

\[
 w_0=1,\qquad w_{s_r}:=\frac{u_r}{s_r}\quad(r\ge0),
\tag{3.6}
\]

and

\[
 w_k=0\qquad(k\ge1,\ k\notin\{s_0,s_1,\ldots\}).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Everything below uses this one fixed law. The radix sequence, `eta`, and hence every weight are independent of `n`, `m_n`, and any subsequence later selected.

### Lemma 3.1 — source admissibility

The law (3.6)--(3.7) has

\[
 \rho=1,\qquad \omega=\infty,\qquad \operatorname{span}(w)=1,
 \qquad 0<\nu<\infty.
\tag{3.8}
\]

#### Proof

Along the support,

\[
 w_{s_r}^{1/s_r}
 =\exp\left\{\frac{-\log s_r+\log u_r}{s_r}\right\}\longrightarrow1,
\]

because `s_r>=2^r`; hence the radius is one. The support is unbounded. Since `s_0=1` and `w_1=u_0>0`, the span is one.

Moreover,

\[
 \Phi(1)=1+\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{u_r}{s_r}<\infty
\tag{3.9}
\]

and

\[
 \Phi'(1)=\sum_{r\ge0}s_rw_{s_r}=\sum_{r\ge0}u_r<\infty,
\tag{3.10}
\]

with a positive numerator. Thus

\[
 \nu=\frac{\Phi'(1)}{\Phi(1)}\in(0,\infty).
\]

`square`

Every sufficiently large integer total is admissible with `n` boxes: the mixed-radix expansion uses digits below the fixed radices `c_r<=Q`, hence only `O(log m)` supported occupancies, while `n` grows linearly.

## 4. Main theorem

### Theorem 4.1 — bounded mixed-radix critical-log persistence

For the single fixed law (3.6)--(3.7), let `m_n/n->lambda>nu` along any full admissible sequence and write `delta=lambda-nu`. Then for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
 \boxed{
 P\!\left(
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ge\frac\delta Q-\varepsilon
 \right)\longrightarrow1.
 }
\tag{4.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ge\frac{\lambda-\nu}{Q}.
 }
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover, for every already-fixed admissible sequence `m_n`, there is a deterministic subsequence `n_j` such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\xrightarrow p\delta,
 \qquad
 E\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\longrightarrow\delta.
 }
\tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (4.3) with the source upper bound (2.5),

\[
 \boxed{
 b(w,\lambda):=\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]
 =\lambda-\nu.

\tag{4.4}
\]

For the explicit alternating `2,3,2,3,...` law (3.4), the full-sequence floor in (4.2) is `(lambda-nu)/3`, while the limsup metric still reaches the exact ceiling `lambda-nu`.

 \Lambda_{n,r}:=nw_{s_r}=\frac{n}{s_r}u_r.

 b_{n,r}:=\lfloor A_r\Lambda_{n,r}\rfloor,

 B_n:=\sum_{r\ge r_n}s_rb_{n,r},

 B_n=o(n).

## 7. Mixed-radix carrying supplies a macroscopic target scale

For a positive integer `E`, define its top supported scale

\[
 P(E):=s_j\quad\text{where }s_j\le E<s_{j+1}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Because `s_(j+1)=c_(j+1)s_j` and `c_(j+1)<=Q`,

\[
 P(E)\le E<QP(E),
\qquad
 P(E)>\frac EQ.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By (6.7), the event

\[
 \mathcal G_n:=\left\{\frac\delta2n\le E_n\le\frac{3\delta}{2}n\right\}
\tag{7.3}
\]

has probability tending to one. On `G_n`, put

\[
 P=P(E_n)=s_j.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Then `P=Theta(n)`. From (3.3),

\[
 j=\Theta(\log n)
\tag{7.5}
\]

uniformly on `G_n`.

### Lemma 7.1 — the top scale has zero baseline

Uniformly on `G_n`, for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
 b_{n,j}=0.
\tag{7.6}
\]

#### Proof

By (7.2)--(7.3), `n/s_j=O(1)`. Hence

\[
 A_j\Lambda_{n,j}
 =\frac n{s_j}A_ju_j
 =O\left(
 \frac1{(j+2)(\log(j+2))^{1+\eta/2}}
 \right)\to0.
\]

Its floor is zero. `square`

Now consider a configuration in `G_n` with no actual box of size at least `P`. Its excess multiset is made only of supported sizes below `P`, but its total excess mass `E_n` has top mixed-radix scale `P`.

### Lemma 7.2 — one ancestry submultiset sums exactly to `P`

There exist integers `d_r`, `r_n<=r<j`, such that

\[
 0\le d_r\le e_{n,r},
 \qquad
 \sum_{r<j}d_rs_r=s_j=P.
\tag{7.7}
\]

#### Proof

Repeatedly perform the mixed-radix carry

\[
 c_{r+1}\text{ copies of }s_r
 \longrightarrow
 1\text{ copy of }s_{r+1}.
\tag{7.8}
\]

This terminates in the canonical mixed-radix expansion of `E_n`. Since `s_j` is its top supported scale, at least one terminal `s_j` is present. Trace the ancestry of one such terminal copy backward through the carries. Its original excess boxes form a submultiset of total mass exactly `s_j`. `square`

Fix a deterministic carrying and tie-breaking rule. Let

\[
 D:=\sum_{r<j}d_r.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Map the source histogram to a target histogram by replacing these `D` selected boxes by

- one box of size `P=s_j`, and
- `D-1` zero boxes.

The total number of boxes and total mass are preserved.

The excess mass is also preserved. At every selected lower scale, `d_r<=e_(n,r)` implies that after removing `d_r` boxes the count remains at least `b_(n,r)`, so the excess drops by exactly `d_r`. At scale `j`, the baseline is zero by Lemma 7.1 and the new box contributes exactly `s_j`. Thus

\[
 E_n(\text{target})=E_n(\text{source}),
\tag{7.10}
\]

so the random top scale `P(E_n)` is fiberwise preserved.

## 8. B25 — count the same configuration fiber two ways

For a supported occupancy histogram `N=(N_k)`, its aggregate contribution to the conditioned partition function is

\[
 W(N):=\frac{n!}{\prod_kN_k!}\prod_kw_k^{N_k}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Fix a bad source histogram `N` and its target `T` from Section 7. Since the source has no `P` box and the target has exactly one newly created `P` box,

\[
 \frac{W(N)}{W(T)}
 =\frac{(N_0+D-1)!}{N_0!}
 \prod_{r<j}\frac{(N_{s_r}-d_r)!}{N_{s_r}!}
 \frac{\prod_{r<j}w_{s_r}^{d_r}}{w_{s_j}w_0^{D-1}}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Because `w_0=1`, `N_0+D-1<=n`, and `N_(s_r)-d_r>=b_(n,r)`,

\[
 \frac{W(N)}{W(T)}
 \le
 \frac1{\Lambda_{n,j}}
 \prod_{r<j}
 \left(
 \frac{\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}
 \right)^{d_r}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Define post-baseline activities

\[
 q_{n,r}:=\frac{\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Since `b_(n,r)=floor(A_r Lambda_(n,r))`,

\[
 \boxed{
 q_{n,r}\le\min\{\Lambda_{n,r},A_r^{-1}\}.
 }
\tag{8.5}
\]

For a fixed target histogram, summing over **all** possible lower-scale decompositions of `s_j` only enlarges the true inverse fiber. Define

\[
 F_{n,j}:=
 \sum_{\substack{d_r\ge0,\ r_n\le r<j\\
                   \sum_rd_rs_r=s_j}}
 \prod_{r<j}q_{n,r}^{d_r}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Then the entire bad-source weight mapping to one target is at most

\[
 \frac{F_{n,j}}{\Lambda_{n,j}}W(T).
\tag{8.7}
\]

The remaining problem is purely a mixed-radix fragmentation estimate.

## 9. Mixed-radix tree domination

Define recursively

\[
 T_{n,r_n}:=q_{n,r_n},
\tag{9.1}
\]

and, for `r>r_n`,

\[
 T_{n,r}:=q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^{c_r}.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Interpret `T_(n,r)` as the total weight of all ordered mixed-radix decomposition trees of mass `s_r`: a node may stop as one leaf of mass `s_r`, contributing `q_(n,r)`, or split into `c_r` ordered children of mass `s_(r-1)`, contributing `T_(n,r-1)^(c_r)`.

### Lemma 9.1 — every supported partition of `s_j` has a mixed-radix tree representation

If nonnegative integers `(d_r)_(r_n<=r<=j)` satisfy

\[
 \sum_rd_rs_r=s_j,
\]

then there is a rooted mixed-radix decomposition tree with root `s_j` whose leaf histogram is `(d_r)`.

#### Proof

Apply the carrying operation (7.8) repeatedly to the multiset of leaves. Since the total is exactly `s_j`, carrying terminates at one root `s_j`. Reverse the carries. A reversed carry at level `r` splits one `s_r` node into exactly `c_r` children of size `s_(r-1)`. `square`

A histogram can have more than one such tree; this only helps an upper bound. Because (8.6) forbids a leaf at the root scale `j`, the root must split. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 F_{n,j}\le T_{n,j-1}^{c_j}.
 }
\tag{9.3}
\]

## 10. Uniform collapse of the mixed-radix fragmentation tree

From (8.5) and monotonicity of `A_r`, set

\[
 \varepsilon_n:=A_{r_n}^{-1}\to0.
\tag{10.1}

Then

\[
 q_{n,r}\le\varepsilon_n\qquad(r\ge r_n).
\tag{10.2}

For all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
 T_{n,r}\le2\varepsilon_n\qquad(r\ge r_n).
\tag{10.3}

#### Proof

For large `n`, `epsilon_n<=1/4`. The base case is immediate. If `T_(n,r-1)<=2epsilon_n<=1`, then `c_r>=2` and

\[
 T_{n,r}
 \le\varepsilon_n+(2\varepsilon_n)^{c_r}
 \le\varepsilon_n+4\varepsilon_n^2
 \le2\varepsilon_n.
\]

Induction proves (10.3). `square`

The crude estimate tends to zero but does not yet compare with the tiny top activity `u_j`. We use only the final `O(log j)` scales, where the profile varies slowly.

Let

\[
 \kappa:=\frac1{4\log_2Q},
 \qquad
 L_j:=\lfloor\kappa\log_2j\rfloor.
\tag{10.4}

Because `s_j=Theta(n)` on `G_n` while `s_(r_n)<=sqrt n`,

\[
 \frac{s_j}{s_{r_n}}\ge c\sqrt n

for a fixed positive `c`, and since every radix is at most `Q`,

\[
 j-r_n\ge\frac{\log(c\sqrt n)}{\log Q}.
\tag{10.5}

As `L_j=O(log log n)`, the index

\[
 r_*:=j-L_j-1
\tag{10.6}

satisfies `r_*>=r_n` for all sufficiently large `n` on `G_n`.

Since (10.3) gives `T<=1`, the variable-arity recursion is bounded by the binary recursion

\[
 T_{n,r}\le q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^2.
\tag{10.7}

For any nonnegative recursion `x_t<=a_t+x_(t-1)^2`, repeated use of `a+b<=2max(a,b)` yields

\[
 x_t\le
 \max\left\{
 \frac12(4a_{t-h})^{2^h}:0\le h\le t-1;
 \ \frac12(2x_0)^{2^t}
 \right\}.
\tag{10.8}

Apply this from `r_*` to `j-1`.

For `0<=h<=L_j-1`, the relevant scale is `r=j-1-h`. Since

 \frac{s_j}{s_{j-1-h}}\le Q^{h+1}
\tag{10.9}

 \Lambda_{n,j-1-h}

\tag{10.10}

Uniformly for `h<=L_j=O(log j)=o(j)`,

\[
 u_{j-1-h}\asymp u_j.
\tag{10.11}

Hence

\[
 q_{n,j-1-h}\le C Q^{h+1}u_j.
\tag{10.12}

By the choice of `kappa`,

 Q^h\le Q^{L_j}\le j^{1/4}.
\tag{10.13}

The `h=0` term in (10.8) is therefore `O(u_j)`. For every `h>=1`, the base in (10.8) is at most

 Cj^{1/4}u_j
 =O\left(
 \frac1{j^{3/4}(\log j)^{1+\eta}}
 \right),
\tag{10.14}

and it is raised to a power at least two. Thus those terms are `o(u_j)` uniformly in `1<=h<L_j`.

Finally, by (10.3), `x_0<=2epsilon_n`. Eventually `2x_0<=1/2`, and

 2^{L_j}=j^{\kappa+o(1)}.

\[
 \boxed{T_{n,j-1}=O(u_j)}
\tag{10.16}

uniformly for every possible top index on `G_n`.

 F_{n,j}\le T_{n,j-1}^{c_j}=O(u_j^2).
\tag{10.17}

\[
 \Lambda_{n,j}=\frac n{s_j}u_j\asymp u_j.
\tag{10.18}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{F_{n,j}}{\Lambda_{n,j}}=O(u_j)\longrightarrow0
 }
\tag{10.19}

uniformly over the random top phase.

## 12. B27 — phase selection reaches the frozen metric ceiling for every admissible sequence

The previous section is uniform in the mixed-radix phase but loses a factor `Q`. The frozen metric is a limsup, so the quantifiers allow a stronger deterministic phase selection **inside each already-fixed admissible sequence**.

\[
 n_l:=\left\lceil\frac{s_{j_l}}{\delta-\varepsilon_l}\right\rceil,
\tag{12.1}

we have

\[
 P\!\left(
 \left|\frac{E_{n_l}}{n_l}-\delta\right|>\frac{\varepsilon_l}{4}

\tag{12.2}
\]

For all large `l`, on the event in (12.2)'s complement,

\[
 s_{j_l}<E_{n_l}<2s_{j_l}\le s_{j_l+1}.
\tag{12.3}
\]

The lower inequality follows because the deterministic gap between `delta n_l` and `s_(j_l)` is asymptotically `epsilon_l n_l`, while the allowed error is only `epsilon_l n_l/4`; the upper inequality holds because `epsilon_l/delta->0` and every next radix is at least two.

\[

\tag{12.4}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \frac{s_{j_l}}{n_l}\to\delta.
\tag{12.5}
\]

Together with the source upper bound (2.4),

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\xrightarrow p\delta.
 }
\tag{12.6}
\]

Uniform boundedness gives

\[
 \boxed{
 E\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\to\delta.

The universal ceiling (2.5) and the subsequence (12.7) prove

This is a limsup statement. It is **not** full-sequence convergence of `Y_(1)/n` to `delta`.
