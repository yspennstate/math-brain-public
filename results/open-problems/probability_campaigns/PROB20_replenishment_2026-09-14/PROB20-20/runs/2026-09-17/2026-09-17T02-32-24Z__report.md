## 2. Exact source statement and fixed-law model

Primary source checked again on 2026-09-17:

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton-Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), arXiv:1112.0510, Section 18.6, Problem 18.39.

The source asks whether

`Y_(1)/n ->_p 0`

is possible when

`0 < nu < lambda`.

Janson states that Example 18.36 shows this can happen on a subsequence, but conjectures that it cannot happen for the full sequence and, a little more strongly, that there is always some fixed `epsilon>0` and some subsequence along which

`Y_(1) >= epsilon n`

with high probability. Problem 18.40 immediately following is the separate `nu=0` question.

The quantifiers that matter here are therefore:

- **one fixed** weight sequence `w=(w_k)`;
- an admissible integer sequence `m=m(n)` with `m/n -> lambda`;
- `0<nu<lambda`;
- full-sequence vanishing is different from dust on a chosen subsequence.

As usual, after the legitimate boundary normalization set

\[
\Phi(1)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k,\qquad
\pi_k=\frac{w_k}{\Phi(1)},\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu,
\]

and let `X_1,X_2,...` be iid with law `pi`. Then the weighted allocation satisfies exactly

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\qquad S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Write

\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Janson's Lemma 18.31 remains an important source-side constraint. For any deterministic `K_n -> infinity`, the conditioned mass in boxes at most `K_n` is at least `nu n+o_p(n)`, and the mass in boxes above `K_n` is at most `delta n+o_p(n)`. In particular,

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta+o_p(1),
\tag{2.3}
\]

so because `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`,

\[
\limsup_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Theorem 18.33 in the source proves the one-giant conclusion under the asymptotic hypothesis `w_k ~ c k^{-beta}`, `beta>2`. The present theorem keeps a weaker, deliberately oscillatory-compatible two-sided envelope and obtains the same one-giant conclusion by a different elementary coalescence argument.

## 4. Mathematical idea before code

The previous q-box argument proved

\[
E(R_{n,K})_q\le(n)_qQ_{q,K}(m),
\tag{4.1}
\]

where `R_{n,K}` is the number of boxes above `K` and `Q_{q,K}` is the exact q-fold interior-convolution/coalescence defect.

For a power envelope and `q=2`, (4.1) costs roughly

\[
n^2K^{1-\beta},
\]

which can be made small with a sublinear cutoff only when `beta>3`. That is precisely the old artificial barrier.

### B20 — find why the algorithm fails

The failure is not in the convolution estimate. It is in a **wasted counting factor** in (4.1).

After q large boxes are coalesced, their sum is at least `q(K+1)`. A target allocation of total mass `m` can therefore contain at most

\[
\frac{m}{q(K+1)}
\]

possible coordinates that could be the coalesced target. The crude `(n)_qQ` estimate threw this structure away and charged essentially `n` choices for the target coordinate.

Recovering the deterministic `1/K` factor is enough to shift the q=2 threshold from `beta>3` all the way to the natural finite-mean threshold `beta>2`.

### B25 — count the same thing twice

The right way to recover the missing factor is an exact fiber count: count ordered q-tuples of large source coordinates directly, then count the same weighted objects after coalescing them into one coordinate and setting the other q-1 source positions to zero.

### B27 — swap the quantifiers

The preceding theorem said: for each `beta>2`, choose a sufficiently large q depending on beta. The refined count proves the stronger statement:

\[
\boxed{\text{one fixed choice }q=2\text{ works for every }\beta>2.}
\]

This is the useful quantifier swap in this run.

## 5. Exact refined q-box coalescence identity

Fix `n,m,K` and an integer `q>=2`. Assume `w_0>0` and that `w_c>0` for the totals appearing below. Define

\[
A_{q,K}(c)
:=
\sum_{\substack{a_1+\cdots+a_q=c\\a_i>K}}
\prod_{i=1}^q w_{a_i}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
Q_{q,K}(m)
:=
\sup_{q(K+1)\le c\le m}
\frac{A_{q,K}(c)}{w_cw_0^{q-1}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

For an allocation `y=(y_1,...,y_n)` define

\[
R_K(y):=\#\{i:y_i>K\},
\qquad
N_0(y):=\#\{i:y_i=0\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Also write `(r)_s=r(r-1)\cdots(r-s+1)`.

### Theorem 5.1 — refined exact coalescence identity

For the weighted allocation conditioned on total `m`,

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_q
=
E\left[
(N_0)_{q-1}
\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\ge q(K+1)\}}
\frac{A_{q,K}(Y_i)}{w_{Y_i}w_0^{q-1}}
\right].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_q
\le
Q_{q,K}(m)
E\left[(N_0)_{q-1}R_{\ge q(K+1)}\right],
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

where

\[
R_{\ge L}:=\#\{i:Y_i\ge L\}.
\]

Since deterministically

\[
R_{\ge q(K+1)}\le\frac{m}{q(K+1)}
\tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
(N_0)_{q-1}\le n^{q-1},
\tag{5.7}
\]

we obtain the refined bound

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_q
\le
\frac{m\,n^{q-1}}{q(K+1)}
Q_{q,K}(m).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

#### Proof

Let

\[
Z_{n,m}=\sum_{y_1+\cdots+y_n=m}\prod_{j=1}^nw_{y_j}
\]

be the partition function.

The numerator of `E(R_K)_q` is

\[
\sum_{y_1+\cdots+y_n=m}
\left(\prod_{j=1}^nw_{y_j}\right)
(R_K(y))_q.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Expanding the falling factorial means choosing an **ordered** q-tuple of distinct indices

\[
(i_1,\ldots,i_q)
\]

with

\[
y_{i_1},\ldots,y_{i_q}>K.
\]

For such a marked source configuration, put

\[
c=y_{i_1}+\cdots+y_{i_q}.
\]

Map it to a target configuration `z` by setting

\[
z_{i_1}=c,
\qquad
z_{i_2}=\cdots=z_{i_q}=0,
\]

and leaving every other coordinate unchanged.

The source and target have the same total mass `m`. Their weights satisfy exactly

\[
\prod_jw_{y_j}
=
\left(\prod_jw_{z_j}\right)
\frac{\prod_{r=1}^qw_{y_{i_r}}}
{w_cw_0^{q-1}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Now fix a target configuration `z` and reconstruct all marked sources mapping to it.

First choose the distinguished target coordinate `i_1`. It must satisfy

\[
z_{i_1}=c\ge q(K+1).
\]

Then choose an ordered `(q-1)`-tuple of distinct zero coordinates for `i_2,...,i_q`. There are exactly

\[
(N_0(z))_{q-1}
\]

such choices.

Finally choose the ordered positive split

\[
a_1+\cdots+a_q=c,
\qquad a_r>K.
\]

Summing the weight ratios in (5.10) over all such splits gives exactly

\[
\frac{A_{q,K}(c)}{w_cw_0^{q-1}}.
\]

Therefore the source-side sum (5.9), after grouping by target fibers, is exactly

\[
\sum_{z_1+\cdots+z_n=m}
\left(\prod_jw_{z_j}\right)
(N_0(z))_{q-1}
\sum_{i=1}^n
\mathbf1_{\{z_i\ge q(K+1)\}}
\frac{A_{q,K}(z_i)}{w_{z_i}w_0^{q-1}}.
\tag{5.11}
\]

Divide by `Z_{n,m}` to obtain (5.4).

Bounding the ratio by `Q_{q,K}(m)` gives (5.5). Equations (5.6) and (5.7) then give (5.8). `square`

### Why this is stronger than the preceding identity

The old bound was of order

\[
n^qQ_{q,K}.
\]

The refined bound is, because `m=O(n)`, of order

\[
\frac{n^q}{K}Q_{q,K}.
\]

The extra factor `1/K` is not cosmetic. It is the entire reason the q=2 power-envelope argument now reaches every `beta>2`.

## 6. Refined finite-q persistence criterion

### Theorem 6.1

Suppose `K_n -> infinity`, `m_n/n -> lambda`, and under the conditioned allocation

\[
T_n
:=
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Fix `q>=2`. If

\[
\frac{n^q}{K_n}Q_{q,K_n}(m_n)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{6.2}
\]

then

\[
P(R_{n,K_n}\ge q)\to0.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently, for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
P\left(
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{q-1}
\right)\to1,
\tag{6.4}
\]

and

\[
\liminf_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{\delta}{q-1}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For `q=2`, (6.2) and (6.1) imply the stronger one-giant conclusion

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\delta,
\qquad
\frac{Y_{(2)}}n\xrightarrow p0,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\to\delta.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

#### Proof

By (5.8) and `m_n=O(n)`, condition (6.2) implies

\[
E(R_{n,K_n})_q\to0.
\]

On the event `R_{n,K_n}>=q`, the falling factorial is at least `q!`, so Markov gives (6.3).

By (6.1), with probability tending to one,

\[
T_n\ge(\delta-\epsilon)n.
\]

On the intersection of this event with `R_{n,K_n}<=q-1`, at most `q-1` boxes carry `T_n`, hence one carries at least

\[
\frac{(\delta-\epsilon)n}{q-1}.
\]

This proves (6.4), and boundedness by `m_n/n=O(1)` gives (6.5).

If `q=2`, then (6.3) says `R_{n,K_n}<=1` w.h.p. Equation (6.1), with `delta>0`, simultaneously rules out `R=0` w.h.p. Hence exactly one box exceeds `K_n` w.h.p., and that unique box has size

\[
T_n=\delta n+o_p(n).
\]

All other boxes are at most `K_n`. Whenever `K_n=o(n)`, this gives both claims in probability in (6.6). The expectation convergence follows from deterministic boundedness. `square`

## 7. Main theorem: every bounded power envelope with beta>2 has one giant

### Theorem 7.1 — full-sequence one-giant condensation under a two-sided power envelope

Assume one fixed full-support weight sequence has `w_0>0` and, for some constants

\[
\beta>2,
\qquad
0<c_-\le c_+<\infty,
\]

satisfies for all sufficiently large integers `k`

\[
\boxed{
c_-k^{-\beta}\le w_k\le c_+k^{-\beta}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `m_n/n -> lambda>nu`, where `nu` is the boundary mean of this same fixed law. Then along the **full admissible sequence**,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(2)}}n\xrightarrow p0,
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore on this class the frozen metric reaches the source-side upper ceiling:

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

#### Proof, Step 1: boundary facts

The envelope (7.1) has radius one. It gives

\[
\sum_kw_k<\infty
\]

and, because `beta>2`,

\[
\sum_kkw_k<\infty.
\]

Thus after boundary normalization `pi_k=w_k/Phi(1)` has finite positive mean `nu` and the same two-sided power envelope up to changed constants. Full support gives span one and, crucially for the denominator lower bound below, positive weight at every sufficiently large reference giant.

Write

\[
\delta=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]

Choose any exponent

\[
\boxed{
\frac{2}{\beta}<\gamma<1.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

This is possible exactly because `beta>2`. Set

\[
K_n=\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Then `K_n->infinity` and `K_n=o(n)`.

#### Step 2: polynomial lower bound for the exact conditioning event

By the ordinary law of large numbers there is a deterministic sequence `eta_n downarrow0` such that

\[
P\left(
|S_{n-1}-(n-1)\nu|\le\eta_nn
\right)\to1.
\tag{7.8}
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, this probability is at least `1/2`. The interval in (7.8) contains at most `2n+3` integers once `eta_n<=1`, so by pigeonhole there is an integer `r_n` in that interval with

\[
P(S_{n-1}=r_n)\ge\frac1{2(2n+3)}.
\tag{7.9}
\]

Put

\[
k_n=m_n-r_n.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Then

\[
k_n=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{7.11}
\]

The lower half of the power envelope therefore gives

\[
\pi_{k_n}\ge Cn^{-\beta}
\tag{7.12}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`. Independence yields the single explicit route

\[
\{X_1=k_n,\ S_{n-1}=r_n\}
\subseteq
\{S_n=m_n\},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge Cn^{-(\beta+1)}.
}
\tag{7.13}
\]

No local one-big-jump asymptotic is used here.

#### Step 3: the chosen sublinear cutoff captures all missing mass

Define

\[
Z_i=X_i\mathbf1_{\{X_i\le K_n\}}.
\]

Then

\[
0\le Z_i\le K_n,
\qquad EZ_i\le\nu,
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)
\le EZ_i^2
\le K_nEZ_i
\le\nu K_n.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Fix `epsilon>0`. Bernstein's inequality gives a constant `c_epsilon>0` such that

\[
P\left(
\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i>(\nu+\epsilon)n
\right)
\le
\exp\{-c_\epsilon n/K_n\}.
\tag{7.15}
\]

Conditioning and using (7.13),

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P\left(
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}>(\nu+\epsilon)n
\right)\\
&\qquad\le
C^{-1}n^{\beta+1}
\exp\{-c_\epsilon n/K_n\}
\longrightarrow0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.16}
\]

because

\[
\frac{n}{K_n}=n^{1-\gamma}(1+o(1))\to\infty
\]

faster than every logarithm.

Janson's Lemma 18.31(i), applied to this same deterministic `K_n->infinity`, gives the opposite conditioned estimate

\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
\ge\nu n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.17}
\]

Combining (7.16) and (7.17),

\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
=\nu n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.18}
\]

Since the total is `m_n=lambda n+o(n)`, the high-box mass is

\[
\boxed{
T_n
:=
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{7.19}
\]

Thus hypothesis (6.1) is verified for the explicit cutoff (7.7).

#### Step 4: q=2 convolution defect under the envelope

For `c>=2(K+1)`,

\[
A_{2,K}(c)
=
\sum_{a=K+1}^{c-K-1}w_aw_{c-a}.
\tag{7.20}
\]

In every split `a+(c-a)=c`, at least one summand is at least `c/2`. By symmetry and the upper envelope,

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{2,K}(c)
&\le
2c_+^2(c/2)^{-\beta}
\sum_{a>K}a^{-\beta}\\
&\le
C c^{-\beta}K^{1-\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.21}
\]

The lower envelope gives

\[
w_c\ge c_-c^{-\beta}.
\]

Therefore, uniformly for `2(K+1)<=c<=m_n`,

\[
\boxed{
Q_{2,K}(m_n)
\le
C K^{1-\beta}.
}
\tag{7.22}
\]

#### Step 5: the refined target-count factor removes the beta=3 barrier

Apply (5.8) with `q=2`:

\[
E(R_{n,K_n})_2
\le
\frac{m_n n}{2(K_n+1)}Q_{2,K_n}(m_n).
\tag{7.23}
\]

Using `m_n=O(n)` and (7.22),

\[
E(R_{n,K_n})_2
\le
C n^2K_n^{-\beta}.
\tag{7.24}
\]

With `K_n=n^\gamma(1+o(1))`,

\[
E(R_{n,K_n})_2
\le
C n^{2-\gamma\beta+o(1)}.
\tag{7.25}
\]

The choice `gamma>2/beta` makes the exponent negative, hence

\[
\boxed{
E(R_{n,K_n})_2\to0.
}
\tag{7.26}
\]

Thus

\[
P(R_{n,K_n}\ge2)\to0.
\tag{7.27}
\]

Equation (7.19) and `delta>0` imply at the same time

\[
P(R_{n,K_n}=0)\to0.
\]

Hence with probability tending to one there is **exactly one** box above `K_n`, and it carries all of `T_n`. Therefore

\[
Y_{(1)}
=T_n
=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.28}
\]

Every other box is at most `K_n=o(n)`, so

\[
Y_{(2)}=o_p(n).
\tag{7.29}
\]

Finally, deterministic boundedness `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)` turns convergence in probability into convergence of expectations. This proves (7.2)--(7.5). `square`

## 9. Concrete fixed laws and actual frozen-metric movement

Take the single fixed, full-support parity-modulated law

\[
w_0=1,
\qquad
w_k=c_kk^{-\beta},
\qquad
c_k=
\begin{cases}
2,&k\text{ odd},\\
1,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{9.1}
\]

The multiplier `c_k` never converges. In particular this law does **not** satisfy `w_k~c k^{-beta}` for any single constant `c`, so Theorem 18.33 does not apply literally through its stated asymptotic-equivalence hypothesis.

It does satisfy the two-sided envelope (7.1).

The preceding report certified only

- at `beta=5/2`: `b(w,lambda)>=delta/3`;
- at `beta=3`: `b(w,lambda)>=delta/2`;
- at `beta>3`: exact `delta`.

The new theorem upgrades both formerly lossy cases to

\[
\boxed{
b(w,\lambda)=\delta=\lambda-\nu
\quad\text{for every }\beta>2.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus, for example, the `beta=5/2` parity law moves from the proved lower bound

\[
\frac{\lambda-\nu}{3}
\]

to the optimal exact value

\[
\lambda-\nu.
\]

This is genuine frozen-metric movement under unchanged hypotheses for that fixed law class.

## 10. Counterexample checks before promoting the theorem

The following failure modes were checked explicitly.

### 10.1 The beta=2 boundary

The proof needs a cutoff exponent satisfying

\[
\frac2\beta<\gamma<1.
\]

At `beta=2` this is impossible. Moreover a two-sided `k^{-2}` envelope has divergent first moment, so it lies outside the source regime with finite positive `nu` used here. The threshold `beta>2` is therefore consistent with the actual fixed-law assumptions, not an unexplained proof artifact.

### 10.2 An upper envelope alone is not enough for this proof

The denominator lower bound (7.13) uses the weight of an exact supported reference giant

\[
k_n=\delta n+o(n).
\]

If large individual sites can be arbitrarily suppressed, an upper envelope alone gives no such lower bound. Therefore the theorem retains the two-sided lower envelope; it is not silently generalized to one-sided O-regularity.

### 10.3 Support holes matter

The coalescence ratio contains `w_c` in the denominator. If the support has holes, a coalesced target can have zero weight even when all fragments are supported. The full-support assumption is therefore retained in this theorem. Sparse-support laws remain a separate branch and are not claimed covered.

### 10.4 No selected-subsequence transfer

Every estimate above is proved for the given full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`. The result is not a statement about a specially selected favorable subsequence.

### 10.5 No varying law

The parity-modulated example and the theorem both fix `w` once and for all. No parameter in the weight law changes with `n`.

### 10.6 No conditional-to-unconditional metric mistake

The final statement is directly about the conditioned allocation and gives full-sequence convergence in probability plus expectation convergence. Thus it genuinely moves the frozen unconditional metric `limsup E[Y_(1)/n]`.
