## 4. Exact conditioning denominator: a binary representation costs only `exp(-O(log^2 n))`

This section proves the conditioning lower bound needed later; it does not invoke Lemma 18.31(ii).

Let

\[
r_n:=\lceil\log_2(m_n+1)\rceil+2,
\qquad
N_n:=n-r_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then `r_n=O(log n)` and `N_n/n->1`.

By the weak law for the fixed integrable law `pi`, there exists a deterministic sequence `eta_n downarrow0` such that

\[
P(|S_{N_n}-\nu N_n|\le \eta_nN_n)\to1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The interval in (4.2) contains at most `2n+1` integers. Hence, for all sufficiently large `n`, some integer `s_n` in that interval satisfies

\[
P(S_{N_n}=s_n)\ge\frac1{4n}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Set

\[
c_n:=m_n-s_n.
\tag{4.4}
\]

From `m_n/n->lambda`, (4.2), and `r_n=o(n)`,

\[
c_n=\delta n+o(n)>0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Write `c_n` in binary. Its nonzero binary digits use at most `r_n` reserved coordinates. Put those powers of two into the final `r_n` coordinates and fill unused reserved coordinates by zeros. This gives an exact supported configuration of the reserved block summing to `c_n`.

If `J_n=O(log n)` is the top binary index, then

\[
-\log\pi_{2^j}
=\log\Phi(1)+j\log2+a\log(j+1).
\]

Even in the worst case where every digit through `J_n` is present,

\[
\sum_{j\le J_n}-\log\pi_{2^j}
=O(J_n^2)=O((\log n)^2).
\tag{4.6}
\]

The zero fillers add only `O(log n)` more to the negative log-probability. Multiplying the background event (4.3) and the exact reserved configuration therefore yields

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge\exp\{-C(\log n)^2\}
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

for a finite law-dependent constant `C` and all sufficiently large `n`.

This denominator is deliberately crude but source-valid and uniform along the full admissible sequence.

## 5. Direct missing-mass theorem at a useful cutoff

\[
Z_n:=X\mathbf1_{\{X\le L_n\}}.

\[
EZ_n\to\nu.
\tag{5.3}

Moreover

\[
E Z_n^2\le L_nE Z_n\le \nu L_n+o(L_n).
\tag{5.4}

For iid copies `Z_{n,i}`, Bernstein's inequality therefore gives, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
P\left(
\left|\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{n,i}-nEZ_n\right|>\epsilon n

\le

\tag{5.5}

Because the total is `m_n=lambda n+o(n)`, the mass in boxes above `L_n` satisfies

## 6. B25: exact double count for the top dyadic bit

Fix a deterministic allocation histogram

\[
N=(N_k)_{k\ge0},
\qquad
\sum_kN_k=n,
\qquad
\sum_kkN_k=m_n,
\]

with positive source weight. Its aggregate contribution to the partition function is

\[

\frac{n!}{\prod_kN_k!}
\prod_kw_k^{N_k}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Fix the cutoff `L=L_n` and let the tail mass be

\[
C=\sum_{k>L}kN_k.
\]

Suppose

\[

\tag{6.2}
\]

for fixed positive constants `c_-<c_+`; (5.7) will provide such a window with probability tending to one. Let

\[
P=P(C):=2^{\lfloor\log_2C\rfloor}=2^j,
\tag{6.3}
\]

so

\[
P\le C<2P
\quad\text{and}\quad
P=\Theta(n).
\tag{6.4}
\]

Assume the source histogram has no box of size `P`, i.e. `N_P=0`.

Every positive tail occupancy is a power of two because of (0.1). Since `C<2P`, no tail occupancy can exceed `P`. Under `N_P=0`, every tail occupancy is therefore strictly smaller than `P`.

### Lemma 6.1 — subset coalescence to the top bit

Among the tail boxes there is a submultiset whose occupancies sum exactly to `P`.

#### Proof

Repeatedly pair equal powers of two and carry each pair to the next power. The final carried multiset is the binary expansion of the integer `C`. Since `P` is the top binary bit of `C`, the terminal carried multiset contains one `P`. Trace the ancestry of that terminal `P` backward through the pairings. Its ancestors form a submultiset of the original tail boxes and have total mass exactly `P`. `square`

Let `d_r` be the number of selected boxes of size `2^r`, so

\[
L<2^r<P,
\qquad
\sum_rd_r2^r=P,
\qquad
D:=\sum_rd_r.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Replace those `D` selected boxes by one box of size `P` and `D-1` zero boxes. This preserves both total mass and the number of coordinates.

Call the resulting target histogram `T`. For a fixed target `T` and any possible inverse decomposition `d=(d_r)`, the exact factorial ratio from (6.1) satisfies

\[
\frac{W(N)}{W(T)}
\le
\frac{n^{D-1}}{\prod_rd_r!}
\frac{\prod_rw_{2^r}^{d_r}}{w_Pw_0^{D-1}}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Indeed, the target has `D-1` additional zeros, giving a factorial ratio at most `n^{D-1}`, while

\[
\frac{T_{2^r}!}{(T_{2^r}+d_r)!}\le\frac1{d_r!}.
\]

Define activities

\[
\lambda_r:=\frac{nw_{2^r}}{w_0}
=\frac{n}{2^r(r+1)^a},
\qquad
\lambda_P:=\frac{nw_P}{w_0}
=\frac{n}{P(j+1)^a}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Then (6.6) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\frac{W(N)}{W(T)}
\le
\frac1{\lambda_P}
\prod_r\frac{\lambda_r^{d_r}}{d_r!}.
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Summing over **all** possible inverse decompositions of `P` only enlarges the fiber. Thus the total source/target ratio for one fiber is at most

\[
R_{n,P}
:=
\frac1{\lambda_P}
[z^P]
\exp\left\{
\sum_{L<2^r<P}\lambda_rz^{2^r}
\right\}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

This is the exact B25 count. It accounts simultaneously for placement entropy, repeated fragment sizes, and zero fillers; no informal `n^D` count remains outside (6.9).
