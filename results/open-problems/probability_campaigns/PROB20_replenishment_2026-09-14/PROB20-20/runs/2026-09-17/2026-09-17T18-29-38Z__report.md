## 2. Exact source statement and source-valid cutoff

Primary source rechecked on 2026-09-17:

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton--Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), 103--252, arXiv:1112.0510v1, Section 18.6, Problem 18.39.

The current arXiv record still exposes v1, submitted 2 December 2011.

Problem 18.39 asks whether

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\xrightarrow p0
\tag{2.1}
\]

can occur for a **single fixed weight sequence** when

\[
0<\nu<\lambda,
\qquad
m_n/n\to\lambda.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The source says that Example 18.36 can produce (2.1) on a subsequence, conjectures that it cannot occur on the full sequence, and conjectures a little more strongly that there are some fixed `epsilon>0` and some subsequence along which

\[
Y_{(1)}\ge\epsilon n
\]

with high probability.

The source cutoff theorem used here is Lemma 18.31. For a fixed nonnegative weight sequence with `w_0>0`, unbounded support (`omega=infinity` in Janson's notation), and `m_n/n->lambda` with `nu<lambda<infinity`:

1. for every deterministic `K_n->infinity`,
   \[
   \sum_{k>K_n}kN_k\le(\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n);
   \tag{2.3}
   \]
2. there exists a deterministic source-produced `Omega_n->infinity` such that, whenever `K_n->infinity` and `K_n<=Omega_n`,
   \[
   \sum_{k>K_n}kN_k=(\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
   \tag{2.4}
   \]

Write

\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

As in the preceding reports, (2.3) with any `K_n=o(n)` gives the universal ceiling

\[
\boxed{\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]\le\delta.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed `Y_(1)/n` is at most `K_n/n` plus the normalized mass above `K_n`, and it is deterministically bounded by `m_n/n=O(1)`.

Thus `delta` is the maximum possible value of the frozen limsup metric.

## 3. B23 — attack the remaining counterexample architecture directly

The preceding regular-majorant theorem leaves open a natural escape route: make the activity profile consist of very sparse record spikes separated by gaps so long that any nonincreasing majorant must remain elevated over enormous intervals and therefore fails summability.

The explicit fixed law used below is on dyadic support. Let

\[
j_k:=4^k\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define positive activities for every `r>=0` by

\[
\boxed{
u_r:=
\begin{cases}
2^{-k},&r=j_k\text{ for some }k\ge1,\\[1mm]
\exp(-2^{r/4}),&r\notin\{j_k:k\ge1\}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{3.2}
\]

(The symbol in (3.2) is the actual activity `u_r`; the displayed leading `u` is intended, not Janson's mean `nu`.)

Set

\[
\boxed{
w_0=1,\qquad
w_{2^r}=\frac{u_r}{2^r}\ (r\ge0),\qquad
w_m=0\text{ for positive non-powers of two}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is one law fixed once and for all.

### Lemma 3.1 — source admissibility

The law (3.3) has radius `rho=1`, span one, unbounded support, and

\[
0<\nu
=
\frac{\sum_{r\ge0}u_r}
     {1+\sum_{r\ge0}u_r/2^r}
<\infty.
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

The spike contribution `sum_k 2^{-k}` is finite, and the trough contribution

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}e^{-2^{r/4}}
\]

is finite, so `sum_r u_r<infinity`. Hence both `Phi(1)` and `Phi'(1)` are finite, and positivity is clear.

Since `u_0>0`, we have `w_1>0`, hence span one. Every dyadic scale has positive weight, so the support is unbounded.

The sequence `u_r` is bounded. Therefore

\[
\limsup_r(w_{2^r})^{1/2^r}\le1.
\]

At the spike indices,

\[
\frac{-\log w_{2^{j_k}}}{2^{j_k}}
=
\frac{(j_k+k)\log2}{2^{j_k}}
\longrightarrow0,
\]

so the root-test limsup is exactly one and `rho=1`. Equation (3.4) follows from `nu=Phi'(1)/Phi(1)`. `square`

### Lemma 3.2 — no summable nonincreasing majorant exists

If a sequence `v_r` is nonincreasing and satisfies `v_r>=u_r` for every sufficiently large `r`, then

\[
\sum_rv_r=\infty.
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

For every sufficiently large `k` and every integer

\[
j_{k-1}<r\le j_k,
\]

monotonicity gives

\[
v_r\ge v_{j_k}\ge u_{j_k}=2^{-k}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_{r=j_{k-1}+1}^{j_k}v_r
\ge
(j_k-j_{k-1})2^{-k}
=
(4^k-4^{k-1})2^{-k}
=
\frac34\,2^k.
\]

These block lower bounds diverge. `square`

Thus the law is not covered by the preceding regular-majorant contact theorem for **any** bounded-ratio parameter `R`: that theorem requires a summable majorant with `1<=v_(r-1)/v_r<=R`, hence in particular a summable nonincreasing majorant, and Lemma 3.2 rules out even the latter.

This is the intended B23 stress test. The rest of the report shows that it nevertheless has maximal limsup condensation.

## 4. B30 — isolate a deep low-activity barrier below a root

The regular-majorant proof controlled every level. Here that is impossible. The replacement is local.

Consider a general dyadic activity law

\[
w_0=1,\qquad
w_{2^r}=u_r/2^r,
\qquad
u<\infty,
\tag{4.1}
\]

with every `u_r>0` and `sum u_r<infinity`.

Let `j_l->infinity` be a sequence of proposed macroscopic root indices and let integers `h_l` satisfy

\[
h_l\to\infty,
\qquad
1\le h_l\le j_l/3.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Define the backward barrier activity

\[
\boxed{
\zeta_l:=
\max_{1\le d\le h_l}2^d u_{j_l-d}.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The factor `2^d` is not cosmetic: on a phase with `n` of order `2^{j_l}`, the Poisson-scale activity of a box at level `j_l-d` is proportional to `2^d u_(j_l-d)`.

The two barrier hypotheses are

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\zeta_l^2}{u_{j_l}}\longrightarrow0,}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{u_{j_l}}=o(2^{h_l}).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Condition (4.4) says that every fresh fragment activity in the final `h_l` generations is small enough that even a two-child root split loses to the root. Condition (4.5) says the barrier is long enough that repeated squaring can erase an arbitrarily slowly vanishing inherited error from lower levels.

## 5. Main barrier theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — deep-trough barriers force a maximal limsup condensate

Let `u_r>0` satisfy `sum_r u_r<infinity`, and define the fixed dyadic law by (4.1). Suppose there are `j_l,h_l` satisfying (4.2)--(4.5).

Then the law has radius one and `0<nu<infinity`. For every `lambda>nu` and every already-fixed admissible sequence

\[
m_n/n\to\lambda,
\tag{5.1}
\]

there exists a deterministic subsequence `n_l` such that

\[
\boxed{
Y_{(1)}/n_l\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E[Y_{(1)}/n_l]\to\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The theorem is a subsequence/limsup result. It does not assert full-sequence one-box convergence.

#### Radius check

Summability makes `(u_r)` bounded, so the root-test limsup for `w_(2^r)` is at most one. Along `j_l`, condition (4.5) and `h_l<=j_l/3` imply

\[
-\log u_{j_l}=o(2^{h_l})=o(2^{j_l}),
\]

and also `j_l=o(2^{j_l})`. Therefore

\[
\frac{-\log w_{2^{j_l}}}{2^{j_l}}
=
\frac{-\log u_{j_l}+j_l\log2}{2^{j_l}}
\to0.
\]

Hence the radius is exactly one. The mean formula is finite and positive because `sum u_r<infinity` and `u_0>0`.

The probabilistic proof occupies Sections 6--10.

## 7. Exact inverse-fragmentation fiber

Put

\[
q_{n,r}:=
\frac{\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Since `A_r` is nondecreasing,

\[
q_{n,r}
\le
\min\{\Lambda_{n,r},A_r^{-1}\}
\le
\min\{\Lambda_{n,r},\varepsilon_n\},
\qquad
\varepsilon_n:=A_{r_n}^{-1}\to0,
\tag{7.2}
\]

for every `r>=r_n`.

Fix a root `j>r_n` whose baseline is zero. Suppose a source histogram has excess phase

\[
2^j<E_n<2^{j+1}
\tag{7.3}
\]

and has no box of size at least `2^j`.

Binary carrying of the excess multiset identifies integers `d_r`, `r_n<=r<j`, such that

\[
0\le d_r\le e_{n,r},
\qquad
\sum_{r<j}d_r2^r=2^j.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Replace these lower boxes by one `2^j` box and `D-1` zero boxes, where `D=sum d_r`. Total mass and total box count are unchanged. Since the removed boxes were excess and `b_(n,j)=0`, the excess mass is unchanged as well.

For a histogram `N`, let

\[
W(N):=\frac{n!}{\prod_mN_m!}\prod_mw_m^{N_m}
\tag{7.5}
\]

be its aggregate partition-function weight. Exact factorial cancellation gives

\[
\frac{W(\text{source})}{W(\text{target})}
\le
\frac1{\Lambda_{n,j}}
\prod_{r<j}q_{n,r}^{d_r}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Indeed the added zeros cost at most `n^(D-1)`, while removing `d_r` excess boxes produces a falling factorial at least `(b_(n,r)+1)^{d_r}`. The identity

\[
n^{D-1}\frac{\prod_rw_{2^r}^{d_r}}{w_{2^j}}
=
\frac1{nw_{2^j}}\prod_r(nw_{2^r})^{d_r}
\]

then yields (7.6).

Define

\[
F_{n,j}:=
\sum_{\substack{d_r\ge0,\ r_n\le r<j\\
                 \sum_rd_r2^r=2^j}}
\prod_{r<j}q_{n,r}^{d_r}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Every inverse source histogram of a fixed target corresponds to one such integer vector, so summing (7.6) over the entire inverse fiber gives the exact bad/target bound

\[
\boxed{\text{inverse-fiber ratio}\le F_{n,j}/\Lambda_{n,j}.}
\tag{7.8}
\]

Introduce the scalar tree recursion

\[
T_{n,r_n}:=q_{n,r_n},
\qquad
T_{n,r}:=q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^2\quad(r>r_n).
\tag{7.9}
\]

Every dyadic partition of `2^j` into smaller supported powers can be represented by a binary fragmentation tree, so

\[
\boxed{F_{n,j}\le T_{n,j-1}^2.}
\tag{7.10}
\]

The recursion may overcount; overcounting is harmless because only an upper bound is needed.

Finally, from (7.2), whenever `epsilon_n<=1/4`, induction gives

\[
\boxed{T_{n,r}\le2\varepsilon_n\qquad(r\ge r_n).}
\tag{7.11}
\]

This global estimate alone can be useless when the root activity is much smaller than `epsilon_n^2`. The barrier is what removes that dependence.

## 8. B7 — the barrier scaling limit and washout lemma

### Lemma 8.1 — repeated squaring washes out an arbitrary slow entry error

Let `0<=eta<=1/8`, `0<=x_0<=1/4`, and suppose

\[
x_{m+1}\le\eta+x_m^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then for every `m>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
x_m\le2\eta+\frac12(2x_0)^{2^m}.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

#### Proof

Put

\[
a_m:=\frac12(2x_0)^{2^m}.
\]

The claim is immediate at `m=0`. If `x_m<=2eta+a_m`, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{m+1}
&\le\eta+(2\eta+a_m)^2\\
&\le\eta+8\eta^2+2a_m^2\\
&\le2\eta+2a_m^2
=2\eta+a_{m+1},
\end{aligned}
\]

where `eta<=1/8` was used. `square`

### Applying the lemma at a proposed root

Fix one of the barrier roots `j=j_l`, put `h=h_l`, and set

\[
t:=j-h.
\tag{8.3}
\]

We will later choose `n` with `n/2^j->1/delta`. Since `h<=j/3`,

\[
t\ge2j/3.
\tag{8.4}
\]

On the other hand the cutoff construction (6.1) gives

\[
r_n\le\tfrac12\log_2 n+O(1)
=\tfrac12j+O(1)
\tag{8.5}
\]

on such a phase. Thus eventually

\[
r_n<t.
\tag{8.6}
\]

At the entrance to the barrier, (7.11) gives

\[
T_{n,t-1}\le2\varepsilon_n.
\tag{8.7}
\]

For `r=j-d` with `1<=d<=h`, if `n/2^j` is bounded above by `2/delta`, then

\[
q_{n,r}\le\Lambda_{n,r}
=\frac n{2^j}2^du_{j-d}
\le\frac2\delta\zeta_l.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Set

\[
\eta_l:=\frac2\delta\zeta_l.
\tag{8.9}
\]

By (4.4), `eta_l->0` and

\[
\eta_l^2/u_{j_l}\to0.
\tag{8.10}
\]

Apply Lemma 8.1 through the `h` barrier levels with `x_0=T_(n,t-1)`. Since `epsilon_n->0`, eventually `x_0<=1/4`, and

\[
T_{n,j-1}
\le
2\eta_l+rac12(4\varepsilon_n)^{2^{h_l}}.
\tag{8.11}
\]

Eventually `4epsilon_n<=1/2`, hence

\[
\frac12(4\varepsilon_n)^{2^{h_l}}
\le
2^{-2^{h_l}-1}.
\tag{8.12}
\]

Condition (4.5) implies

\[
\frac{2^{-2^{h_l+1}}}{u_{j_l}}\to0.
\tag{8.13}
\]

Combining (8.10)--(8.13),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{T_{n,j_l-1}^2}{u_{j_l}}\longrightarrow0}
\tag{8.14}
\]

uniformly along phase choices with `n/2^(j_l)->1/delta`.

This is the new gluing mechanism. The error entering the barrier may decay arbitrarily slowly; the length `h_l` raises it to a doubly-exponential power. Fresh fragmentation activities do not accumulate because each barrier level is uniformly bounded by `eta_l`.
