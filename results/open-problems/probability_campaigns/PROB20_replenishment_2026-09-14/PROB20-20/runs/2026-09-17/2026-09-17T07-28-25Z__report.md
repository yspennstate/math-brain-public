\sum_i y_i=m.
\tag{2.1}

## 4. Mathematical idea before proof

### B5 — add a parameter and tune it

The old background estimate used a generic bounded-variable Bernstein inequality and paid the scale `n/K`. That ignores the stretched-exponential envelope. Introduce a cutoff-dependent Chernoff parameter

\[
t_K=bK^{p-1},\qquad 0<b<a.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Since `p-1<0`, every `1<=x<=K` satisfies

\[
t_Kx\le b x^p.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the positive tilt never cancels the original tail energy `a x^p`. The logarithmic moment-generating function has first derivative asymptotically `nu`, yielding an upper-deviation exponent

\[
nt_K\asymp \frac{n}{K^{1-p}},
\tag{4.3}

strictly stronger than `n/K` when `0<p<1`.

## 5. New fixed-law class

### Definition 5.1 — matched stretched envelope with subleading residual energy

Fix constants

\[
0<p<1,
\qquad
a>0,
\qquad\alpha>0,
\qquad C_+,C_->0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `w=(w_k)_{k>=0}` be one fixed full-support sequence with `w_0>0`, satisfying eventually

**Global upper envelope**

\[
w_k\le C_+e^{-a k^p}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

**Macroscopic matched landing**

for every sufficiently large integer `c`, there is an integer `g(c)` with

\[
0\le g(c)\le c,
\qquad
g(c)\ge\alpha c,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
w_{g(c)}\ge C_-e^{-a g(c)^p}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Subleading residual energy**

with

\[
d(c):=c-g(c),
\tag{5.5}
\]

there is a deterministic nonnegative function `h(c)` such that

\[
w_{d(c)}\ge C_-e^{-h(c)}
\tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{h(c)=o(c^p).}
\tag{5.7}
\]

The matching leading coefficient `a` in (5.2) and (5.4) remains essential. The theorem below removes the old power restriction on the residual but does **not** remove a positive order-`c^p` landing-coefficient mismatch.

Because (5.3)-(5.4) provide an unbounded subsequence with kth roots tending to one and (5.2) gives the matching upper root bound, the radius of convergence is one. The stretched upper envelope gives moments of every order. Full support gives span one, positive mean, and positive finite variance after normalization.

## 6. Exact two-coordinate coalescence lemma retained

For an integer cutoff `K`, let

\[
R_K:=\#\{i:Y_i>K\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For `c>=2(K+1)`, define

\[
A_K(c)
:=
\sum_{\substack{u+v=c\\u>K,\ v>K}}w_uw_v
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
Q_K(m)
:=
\sup_{2(K+1)\le c\le m}
\frac{A_K(c)}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Lemma 6.1

For all sufficiently large `K`,

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_2
\le
\frac{mn}{2\alpha(K+1)}Q_K(m).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Expectation is under the exact conditioned allocation.

### Proof

Expand `(R_K)_2` over ordered pairs of distinct coordinates. For one ordered pair with occupancies `u,v>K`, put `c=u+v` and replace that pair by

\[
(g(c),d(c)).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The total mass is unchanged. For fixed `c`, summing the exact unnormalized weight ratios over all ordered splits `u+v=c` gives precisely

\[
\frac{A_K(c)}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Every target image has its first selected coordinate at least

\[
g(c)\ge\alpha c\ge2\alpha(K+1).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Since the total target mass is `m`, there are at most

\[
\frac{m}{2\alpha(K+1)}
\tag{6.8}
\]

possible indices for this first target coordinate and at most `n` choices for the second selected index. Once those two positions and their target occupancies are fixed, their sum recovers `c`. Therefore every target configuration has at most `mn/[2alpha(K+1)]` preimage index choices. Multiplying the exact split-weight ratio by this deterministic fiber bound proves (6.4). `square`

This is a finite exact comparison. No rate transfer from Problem 18.40 is present.

## 7. Fragmentation energy with a general residual penalty

### Lemma 7.1

Under Definition 5.1, for all sufficiently large `K` and all

\[
2(K+1)\le c\le m,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\frac{A_K(c)}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}
\le
C(c+1)
\exp\{-a(1-p)(K+1)^p+h(c)\}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

For `u,v>K`, `u+v=c`, concavity of `x^p` gives

\[
u^p+v^p
\ge
(K+1)^p+(c-K-1)^p.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By the mean-value theorem and `p-1<0`,

\[
c^p-(c-K-1)^p
\le
p(K+1)(c-K-1)^{p-1}
\le
p(K+1)^p.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{u^p+v^p\ge c^p+(1-p)(K+1)^p.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Using (5.2) and at most `c+1` splits,

\[
A_K(c)
\le
C(c+1)e^{-a c^p-a(1-p)(K+1)^p}.
\tag{7.5}
\]

On the other hand, by (5.4)-(5.6) and `g(c)<=c`,

\[
w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}
\ge
C e^{-a g(c)^p-h(c)}
\ge
C e^{-a c^p-h(c)}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Dividing proves (7.1). `square`

The only new unresolved term relative to the preceding report is now the exact residual energy `h(c)`.

## 8. New tail-adapted moderate deviation bound

This is the step that removes the old `gamma<1-p` power-cutoff obstruction.

Let `X` have the normalized boundary law `pi_k=w_k/Phi(1)`, and define

\[
Z_K:=X\mathbf 1_{\{X\le K\}}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Fix

\[
b:=a/4,
\qquad
t_K:=bK^{p-1}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then `t_K downarrow 0` as `K -> infinity`.

### Lemma 8.1 — tilted derivative at the boundary mean

\[
\boxed{
\log E e^{t_K Z_K}=\nu t_K+o(t_K).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

### Proof

For every `1<=x<=K`, since `p-1<0`,

\[
K^{p-1}\le x^{p-1},
\]

and therefore

\[
t_Kx\le b x^p.
\tag{8.4}
\]

The normalized law inherits

\[
\pi_x\le C e^{-a x^p}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

For `x<=K`,

\[
0\le
\frac{e^{t_Kx}-1}{t_K}
\le
x e^{t_Kx}
\le
x e^{b x^p}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Multiplying by (8.5) gives the summable majorant

\[
C x e^{-(a-b)x^p}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

For every fixed `x`, eventually `x<=K` and

\[
\frac{e^{t_Kx}-1}{t_K}\to x.
\tag{8.8}
\]

Dominated convergence therefore yields

\[
\frac{Ee^{t_KZ_K}-1}{t_K}
=
\sum_{x\le K}\pi_x\frac{e^{t_Kx}-1}{t_K}
\longrightarrow
EX=\nu.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Thus

\[
Ee^{t_KZ_K}=1+\nu t_K+o(t_K),
\]

and taking the logarithm gives (8.3). `square`

### Lemma 8.2 — upper and lower linear deviations

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, there are positive constants `c_epsilon` and `K_epsilon` such that, for every `K>=K_epsilon` and iid copies `Z_{i,K}`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left(\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{i,K}\ge(\nu+\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K^{1-p}}\right\}
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

along any sequence `K->infinity`, and

\[
\boxed{
P\left(\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{i,K}\le(\nu-\epsilon)n\right)
\le e^{-c_\epsilon n}
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

for all sufficiently large `K`.

### Proof

For the upper tail, Chernoff and Lemma 8.1 give

\[
P\left(\sum_iZ_{i,K}\ge(\nu+\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-t_K(\nu+\epsilon)n+n\log Ee^{t_KZ_K}\right\}
\]

\[
=
\exp\{-\epsilon nt_K+o(nt_K)\}.
\tag{8.12}
\]

Since `t_K=b/K^{1-p}`, the exponent is at most `-c_epsilon n/K^{1-p}` for all large `K`, proving (8.10).

For the lower tail, choose a fixed sufficiently small `s>0`. Finite variance gives the standard expansion

\[
\log Ee^{-sX}=-\nu s+O(s^2).
\tag{8.13}
\]

Choose `s` so small that

\[
\log Ee^{-sX}\le-(\nu-\epsilon/2)s.
\tag{8.14}
\]

Since `Z_K -> X` almost surely and `0<=e^{-sZ_K}<=1`, dominated convergence gives

\[
Ee^{-sZ_K}\to Ee^{-sX}.
\tag{8.15}
\]

For large `K`, Chernoff applied to `e^{-s\sum Z}` then yields

\[
P\left(\sum_iZ_{i,K}\le(\nu-\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\{s(\nu-\epsilon)n\}(Ee^{-sZ_K})^n
\le e^{-c_\epsilon n}.
\tag{8.16}
\]

This proves (8.11). `square`

The key gain over generic Bernstein is

\[
\boxed{\frac{n}{K^{1-p}}}
\tag{8.17}
\]

rather than `n/K`.

## 9. Adaptive cutoff and main theorem

Fix a constant `M>lambda+1`; then `m_n<=Mn` for all large `n`.

For large `n`, define the worst residual energy up to the entire relevant macroscopic range by

\[
H_n:=\max_{c_0\le c\le\lfloor Mn\rfloor}h(c),
\tag{9.1}
\]

where `c_0` is large enough that Definition 5.1 applies.

### Lemma 9.1

\[
\boxed{H_n=o(n^p).}
\tag{9.2}
\]

### Proof

Given `eta>0`, choose `C_eta` such that `h(c)<=eta c^p` for every `c>=C_eta`. Then for `C_eta<=c<=Mn`,

\[
h(c)\le eta M^p n^p.
\]

The finitely many values below `C_eta` contribute a fixed maximum, which is `o(n^p)`. Since `eta` is arbitrary, (9.2) follows. `square`

Set

\[
e_n:=\frac{H_n}{n^p},
\qquad
r_n:=\max\{e_n^{1/(2p)},n^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
K_n:=\lfloor nr_n\rfloor.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then

\[
r_n\to0,
\qquad
K_n\to\infty,
\qquad
K_n=o(n).
\tag{9.4}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{\frac{H_n}{K_n^p}\to0.}
\tag{9.5}
\]

Indeed, if `e_n^{1/(2p)}` dominates, the ratio is asymptotic to `e_n^{1/2}`; if `n^{-1/2}` dominates, then `e_n<=n^{-p}`, so `H_n=O(1)` while `K_n^p` grows like `n^{p/2}`.

Also

\[
\frac{n}{K_n^{1-p}}
=
 n^p r_n^{-(1-p)}(1+o(1)),
\tag{9.6}
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\frac{n/K_n^{1-p}}{n^p}\to\infty.
}
\tag{9.7}
\]

This is precisely what was unavailable under the old Bernstein exponent.

### Lemma 9.2 — conditioning denominator

There is `C<infinity` such that, for all large `n`,

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge n^{-1/2}e^{-Cn^p}.
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

### Proof

By the lattice local central limit theorem, choose integers `r_n=nu(n-2)+O(1)` with

\[
P(S_{n-2}=r_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{9.9}
\]

Put

\[
c_n:=m_n-r_n=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{9.10}
\]

Force

\[
X_1=g(c_n),
\qquad
X_2=d(c_n),
\tag{9.11}
\]

and let the remaining `n-2` coordinates sum to `r_n`. This is an exact subset of `{S_n=m_n}`. Hence

\[
P(S_n=m_n)
\ge
\pi_{g(c_n)}\pi_{d(c_n)}P(S_{n-2}=r_n).
\tag{9.12}
\]

By Definition 5.1,

\[
\pi_{g(c_n)}\pi_{d(c_n)}
\ge
C\exp\{-a g(c_n)^p-h(c_n)\}
\ge
C\exp\{-a c_n^p-h(c_n)\}.
\tag{9.13}
\]

Since `c_n=O(n)` and `h(c_n)=o(n^p)`, (9.8) follows. `square`

### Lemma 9.3 — the small boxes carry ordinary mass

Under the conditioned allocation,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
=\nu n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{9.14}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{9.15}
\]

### Proof

Apply Lemma 8.2 to the iid unconditioned variables `Z_{i,K_n}`. For any fixed `epsilon>0`, the upper-deviation probability is at most

\[
\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K_n^{1-p}}\right\}.
\tag{9.16}
\]

Divide by the exact conditioning probability (9.8). By (9.7),

\[
n^{1/2}\exp\left\{Cn^p-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K_n^{1-p}}\right\}\to0.
\tag{9.17}
\]

The lower-deviation probability is at most `e^{-c_epsilon n}`, and after division by (9.8) it also tends to zero because `p<1`. This proves (9.14). Subtracting from the deterministic total `m_n=lambda n+o(n)` yields (9.15). `square`

### Theorem 9.4 — subleading-residual one-giant theorem

Let one fixed full-support weight law satisfy Definition 5.1 for some `0<p<1`. Then for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
\frac{Y_{(2)}}n\xrightarrow p0.
}
\tag{9.18}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{9.19}
\]

Thus on this class the frozen metric reaches the source-side ceiling:

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{9.20}
\]

### Proof

Use the adaptive cutoff `K_n` above. Since `m_n<=Mn`, Lemma 7.1 and the definition of `H_n` give

\[
Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
Cn\exp\{-a(1-p)(K_n+1)^p+H_n\}.
\tag{9.21}
\]

By (9.5), for some `c>0` and all large `n`,

\[
Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
Cn e^{-cK_n^p}.
\tag{9.22}
\]

Lemma 6.1 therefore gives

\[
E(R_{K_n})_2
\le
\frac{m_nn}{2\alpha(K_n+1)}Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
C\frac{n^3}{K_n}e^{-cK_n^p}.
\tag{9.23}
\]

Because `K_n>=cn^{1/2}` eventually, `K_n^p>=c n^{p/2}`, and the right side tends to zero. Thus

\[
P(R_{K_n}\ge2)\le\frac12E(R_{K_n})_2\to0.
\tag{9.24}
\]

Lemma 9.3 says that the total mass in boxes above `K_n` is

\[
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{9.25}
\]

Since `delta>0`, at least one such box exists with high probability. Combining with (9.24), exactly one exists with high probability, and it carries all the mass in (9.25). Therefore

\[
Y_{(1)}=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{9.26}
\]

Every other box is at most `K_n=o(n)`, proving `Y_(2)/n->_p0`. Finally `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`, so boundedness plus convergence in probability yields (9.19). `square`

The theorem is full-sequence. No chosen subsequence appears in the proof.

\frac{a_j^{2/3}}{(\log a_j)^2}
=o(a_j).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Define one fixed full-support law, with harmless positive `w_0,w_1`, and for all large `k`,

\[
\boxed{
w_k=
\begin{cases}
\exp(-k^{1/3}),&k\in G,\\
\exp(-k^{1/2}),&k\notin G.
\end{cases}}
\tag{10.4}
\]

This has global upper envelope

\[
w_k\le e^{-k^{1/3}}.
\tag{10.5}
\]

For a large target `c`, let `g(c)` be the largest preferred site at most `c`. By (10.3),

\[
d(c):=c-g(c)
=O\left(\frac{c^{2/3}}{(\log c)^2}\right),
\tag{10.6}
\]

and `g(c)=c-o(c)`, hence eventually `g(c)>=c/2`. Moreover

\[
w_{g(c)}=e^{-g(c)^{1/3}}.
\tag{10.7}
\]

For every integer `d>=1`, regardless of whether `d` belongs to `G`,

\[
w_d\ge e^{-d^{1/2}}.
\tag{10.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
-\log w_{d(c)}
\le
O(d(c)^{1/2})
=
O\left(\frac{c^{1/3}}{\log c}\right)
=o(c^{1/3}).
\tag{10.9}
\]

The finitely many cases with very small residual are absorbed into the constant in Definition 5.1. Thus Theorem 9.4 applies and gives, for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible `m_n/n->lambda`,

\[
\boxed{
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
Y_{(2)}/n\to_p0,

E[Y_{(1)}/n]\to\lambda-\nu.

\tag{10.10}
\]

Hence for this one fixed law,

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{10.11}
\]

This is genuinely outside the preceding report's theorem. Its residual energy has order `c^(1/3)/log c`, and for every fixed `theta<1/3`,

\[
\frac{c^{1/3}/\log c}{c^\theta}
=
\frac{c^{1/3-\theta}}{\log c}
\to\infty.
\tag{10.12}
\]

Thus it is not `O(c^theta)` for **any** `theta<p`, in particular not for any `theta<p(1-p)=2/9`.
