## 4. Counterexample-first stress test

Before proving a theorem, I tried to make the previous persistence mechanism fail at its worst dyadic phase.

The most hostile totals for binary representation are

\[
 c_J=2^{J+1}-1,
\tag{4.1}
\]

whose binary expansion uses every power `1,2,...,2^J`. At those totals the largest favorable atom is only about one half of the total. Thus this phase is a genuine counterexample to any naive transfer of the previous argument to **full-sequence one-giant condensation**.

This suggests separating two claims:

- a **uniform full-sequence** persistence floor coming from the fact that the top dyadic bit is always more than half the total;

## 5. B24: exact extremal dyadic energy

For an integer `c>=0`, define its binary `p`-energy

\[
 E_p(c):=\sum_{j:\,\operatorname{bit}_j(c)=1} 2^{jp}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is exactly the negative log-weight of the canonical binary target in the law (3.2).

### Lemma 5.1 — binary expansion minimizes energy among dyadic decompositions

Let a finite multiset of powers of two have sum `c`. Then its `p`-energy is at least `E_p(c)`.

#### Proof

Whenever two copies of `2^j` occur, replace them by one copy of `2^{j+1}`. The total mass is unchanged, while the energy changes from

\[
 2\,2^{jp}
\]

to

\[
 2^{(j+1)p}=2^p2^{jp}.
\]

Since `0<p<1`, `2^p<2`; every such coalescence strictly decreases the energy. Repeating until no duplicate power remains yields the unique binary expansion of `c`. Hence every original dyadic decomposition has energy at least `E_p(c)`. `square`

### Lemma 5.2 — subadditivity

For all nonnegative integers `a,b`,

\[
 \boxed{E_p(a+b)\le E_p(a)+E_p(b).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Take the union of the binary expansions of `a` and `b`. It is a dyadic decomposition of `a+b` with energy `E_p(a)+E_p(b)`. Lemma 5.1 says the binary expansion of `a+b` has no larger energy. `square`

The significance is that the canonical target energy is not merely bounded; it is the exact extremal energy in the discrete category relevant to this law.

## 6. B5: the sharp uniform binary target constant

The preceding report used the convenient but non-sharp estimate

\[
 E_p(c)\le \frac{2^p}{2^p-1}c^p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

The exact best constant is smaller by one unit.

### Lemma 6.1 — sharp binary energy envelope

For every integer `c>=1`,

\[
 \boxed{
 E_p(c)\le C_p^\sharp c^p,
 \qquad
 C_p^\sharp:=\frac{1}{2^p-1}.
 }
\tag{6.2}
\]

Moreover the constant is optimal.

#### Proof

Proceed by induction on the binary scale. Write

\[
 c=2^J+r,
 \qquad 0\le r<2^J.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Assume the claim below scale `2^J`. Put `x=r/2^J in [0,1)`. Then

\[
 E_p(c)=2^{Jp}+E_p(r)
 \le 2^{Jp}\bigl(1+C_p^\sharp x^p\bigr).
\tag{6.4}
\]

It remains to prove

\[
 1+C_p^\sharp x^p
 \le C_p^\sharp(1+x)^p,
\tag{6.5}
\]

or equivalently

\[
 C_p^\sharp\bigl((1+x)^p-x^p\bigr)\ge1.
\tag{6.6}
\]

This is where the added scale parameter pays off. Differentiate

\[
 D_p(x):=(1+x)^p-x^p.
\tag{6.7}
\]

For `x>0`,

\[
 D_p'(x)=p\bigl((1+x)^{p-1}-x^{p-1}\bigr)<0
\tag{6.8}
\]

because `p-1<0`. Hence `D_p` decreases on `[0,1]`, and

\[
 D_p(x)\ge D_p(1)=2^p-1.
\tag{6.9}
\]

With `C_p^sharp=1/(2^p-1)`, (6.6) follows.

For optimality, take `c_J=2^{J+1}-1`. Then

\[
 E_p(c_J)=\sum_{j=0}^J2^{jp}
 =\frac{2^{p(J+1)}-1}{2^p-1},
\tag{6.10}
\]

while `c_J^p=(2^{J+1}-1)^p`. Therefore

\[
 \frac{E_p(c_J)}{c_J^p}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2^p-1}=C_p^\sharp.
\tag{6.11}
\]

So no smaller uniform constant can hold. `square`

This improvement alone would strengthen the preceding continuous cap-energy threshold, but the next lemma extracts substantially more information from the discrete top bit.

## 7. Forced top-bit splitting

For `c>=1`, let

\[
 P(c):=2^{\lfloor\log_2 c\rfloor}
\tag{7.1}
\]

be its largest dyadic bit. Thus

\[
 \frac c2<P(c)\le c.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Define

\[
 \kappa_p:=2^{1-p}-1>0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 — cap below the top bit costs a fixed `p`-energy gap

Let a dyadic multiset have total mass `c` and suppose every part is at most `H<P(c)`. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{energy}\ge E_p(c)+\kappa_p P(c)^p.
 }
\tag{7.4}
\]

#### Proof

Coalesce equal powers exactly as in Lemma 5.1 until the binary expansion of `c` is reached. The final binary expansion contains one copy of `P(c)`. The initial multiset contains no `P(c)` because every part is `<P(c)`. Therefore at some stage the coalescence history must create `P(c)` by the merge

\[
 P(c)/2+P(c)/2\longrightarrow P(c).
\tag{7.5}
\]

That single merge decreases the energy by exactly

\[
 2(P(c)/2)^p-P(c)^p
 =\bigl(2^{1-p}-1\bigr)P(c)^p
 =\kappa_pP(c)^p.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Every other coalescence also decreases energy. Hence the initial energy exceeds the terminal binary energy by at least (7.6). `square`

This is the central new observation. The previous report compared a continuous cap minimum against a uniform target-energy constant; here the exact dyadic arithmetic forces an `Omega(n^p)` penalty as soon as the cap lies below the highest bit of the actual missing mass.

## 8. The fixed dyadic law dominates its own binary refinement

Write the log-cost of the fixed law as

\[
 \varphi(k):=-\log w_k
 =
 \begin{cases}
 k^p,&k\text{ is a power of two},\\
 k^2,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Lemma 8.1

There exists `K_0` such that for every integer `k>=K_0`,

\[
 \boxed{\varphi(k)\ge E_p(k).}
\tag{8.2}
\]

#### Proof

If `k` is dyadic, equality holds. Otherwise Lemma 6.1 gives

\[
 E_p(k)\le C_p^\sharp k^p.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Since `2-p>0`, eventually

\[
 k^2\ge C_p^\sharp k^p.
\tag{8.4}
\]

Thus `varphi(k)=k^2>=E_p(k)` for every sufficiently large non-dyadic `k`. `square`

Consequently, splitting a large source occupancy into its binary bits never increases its energy in the comparison relevant below.

## 10. Exact coalescence map with a dyadic energy gap

Let

\[
 B(y):=\{i:y_i>K_n\},
 \qquad
 C_B(y):=\sum_{i\in B(y)}y_i.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Choose

\[
 L_n:=\lceil\log_2(m_n+1)\rceil+2.
\tag{10.2}
\]

For a source allocation `y`, select all coordinates in `B(y)`. If there are fewer than `L_n` of them, add arbitrary unused coordinates until exactly `L_n` are selected. Call the selected set `I(y)`, and let

\[
 D(y):=\sum_{i\in I(y)\setminus B(y)}y_i,
 \qquad
 C_I(y):=C_B(y)+D(y).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Every filler coordinate is at most `K_n`, so

\[
 0\le D(y)\le L_nK_n=o(n).
\tag{10.4}
\]

Replace all selected occupancies by the binary expansion of `C_I(y)`, padded with zeros. Leave all unselected occupancies unchanged. Denote the resulting allocation by `T(y)`. This is an exact finite mass-preserving map within the same fixed law.

The number of binary target coordinates is at most `L_n`, so the target always fits inside `I(y)`.

### Lemma 10.1 — exact source/target gap below the top bit

Suppose every large source box satisfies

\[
 y_i\le H<P(C_B(y)).
\tag{10.5}
\]

Then uniformly for all sufficiently large `n`,

\[
 \boxed{
 -\log\frac{\prod_iw_{y_i}}{\prod_iw_{T(y)_i}}
 \ge
 \kappa_pP(C_B(y))^p-E_p(D(y)).
 }
\tag{10.6}
\]

#### Proof

For every `i in B(y)`, Lemma 8.1 gives

\[
 \varphi(y_i)\ge E_p(y_i).
\tag{10.7}
\]

The union of the binary expansions of the `y_i`, `i in B(y)`, is a dyadic decomposition of `C_B(y)`. Every dyadic part is at most `y_i<=H<P(C_B)`. Lemma 7.1 therefore gives

\[
 \sum_{i\in B(y)}\varphi(y_i)
 \ge E_p(C_B(y))+\kappa_pP(C_B(y))^p.
\tag{10.8}
\]

The filler source costs are nonnegative, so they may be discarded from the lower bound. The target consists of dyadic bits and zeros, hence its selected log-cost is exactly `E_p(C_I)`. By Lemma 5.2,

\[
 E_p(C_I)=E_p(C_B+D)
 \le E_p(C_B)+E_p(D).
\tag{10.9}
\]

Subtracting proves (10.6). `square`

By Lemma 6.1 and (10.4),

\[
 E_p(D(y))
 \le C_p^\sharp(L_nK_n)^p
 =o(n^p).
\tag{10.10}
\]

### Lemma 10.2 — preimage entropy is subleading

For a fixed target allocation, the number of source allocations mapping to it is at most

\[
 \exp\{O(n^{1-\gamma}\log n)\}
 =\exp\{o(n^p)\}.
\tag{10.11}
\]

#### Proof

There are at most `m_n/K_n=O(n^{1-gamma})` large source coordinates, plus `L_n=O(log n)` fillers. A crude exact encoding of a preimage chooses this selected set and each of its occupancies in `[0,m_n]`, giving logarithmic multiplicity

\[
 O((n^{1-\gamma}+\log n)\log n).
\tag{10.12}
\]

Since `gamma>1-p`, this is `o(n^p)`. `square`

Combining Lemmas 10.1--10.2, if `P(C_B)>=cn` and the cap lies below `P(C_B)`, then the total conditioned weight of all such capped source configurations is at most

\[
 \exp\{-c'n^p+o(n^p)\}
\tag{10.13}
\]

times the full partition function.

## 11. B3 stronger theorem, part I: full-sequence half-condensation floor

### Theorem 11.1

For the single fixed law (3.2), every `lambda>nu`, every admissible full sequence `m_n/n->lambda`, and every fixed

\[
 0<\alpha<\frac12
\tag{11.1}
\]

satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 P\left(Y_{(1)}>\alpha(\lambda-\nu)n\right)\to1.
 }
\tag{11.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
 \ge \frac{\lambda-\nu}{2}.
 }
\tag{11.3}
\]

#### Proof

Fix `alpha<1/2`. Choose `epsilon>0` so small that

\[
 \alpha\delta<\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}.
\tag{11.4}
\]

By (9.8), the event

\[
 G_{n,\epsilon}:=\{|C_n/n-\delta|\le\epsilon\}
\tag{11.5}
\]

has probability tending to one.

On `G_{n,epsilon}`,

\[
 C_n\ge(\delta-\epsilon)n.
\tag{11.6}
\]

For every positive integer `c`, its largest dyadic bit satisfies `P(c)>c/2`. Hence

\[
 P(C_n)>\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}n
 >\alpha\delta n.
\tag{11.7}
\]

Now restrict to the bad event `Y_(1)<=alpha delta n`. Every source large box is then below `P(C_n)`. Lemma 10.1 gives an energy gap at least

\[
 \kappa_p\left(\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{2}n\right)^p-o(n^p),
\tag{11.8}
\]

while Lemma 10.2 contributes only `o(n^p)` preimage entropy. Summing over targets inside the conditioned partition function yields

\[
 P\left(Y_{(1)}\le\alpha\delta n,\ G_{n,\epsilon}\right)
 \le e^{-c_{\alpha,\epsilon}n^p}
\tag{11.9}
\]

for all large `n`. Since `P(G_{n,epsilon})->1`, (11.2) follows.

Finally `Y_(1)/n` is uniformly bounded by `m_n/n=O(1)`. Therefore (11.2) gives

\[
 \liminf_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]\ge\alpha\delta
\tag{11.10}
\]

for every `alpha<1/2`. Let `alpha` increase to `1/2`. `square`

The one-half constant is not asserted to be the true full-sequence limit for this law. It is the uniform phase-free value delivered by the fact that the largest dyadic bit always exceeds half the total.

## 12. B3 stronger theorem, part II: a deterministic subsequence has one giant carrying all missing mass

The frozen metric is a limsup. The source itself explicitly distinguishes this persistent-subsequence question from full-sequence convergence. The dyadic phase can be selected deterministically without changing the law.

### Theorem 12.1 — phase-selected one-giant subsequence

For the same fixed law (3.2) and every full admissible `m_n/n->lambda>nu`, there exists a deterministic subsequence `n_j->infinity` such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\xrightarrow{p}\delta=\lambda-\nu
 }
\tag{12.1}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 E\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\longrightarrow\delta.
 }
\tag{12.2}
\]

#### Proof

From (9.8), `C_n/n ->_p delta`. Choose any deterministic sequence `epsilon_j downarrow0` with `epsilon_j<delta/4`. By convergence in probability there exists an increasing deterministic sequence `N_j` such that for every `n>=N_j`,

\[
 P\left(\left|\frac{C_n}{n}-\delta\right|>\epsilon_j\right)<\epsilon_j.
\tag{12.3}
\]

Choose a power of two `P_j=2^{J_j}` so large that

\[
 n_j:=\left\lceil\frac{P_j}{\delta-\epsilon_j}\right\rceil\ge N_j
\tag{12.4}
\]

and `n_j` is strictly increasing. Let

\[
 G_j:=\left\{\left|\frac{C_{n_j}}{n_j}-\delta\right|\le\epsilon_j\right\}.
\tag{12.5}
\]

Then `P(G_j)->1`. On `G_j`, by (12.4),

\[
 C_{n_j}\ge(\delta-\epsilon_j)n_j\ge P_j.
\tag{12.6}
\]

Also

\[
 \frac{(\delta+\epsilon_j)n_j}{P_j}
 \longrightarrow1,
\tag{12.7}
\]

so for all large `j`,

\[
 C_{n_j}<2P_j.
\tag{12.8}
\]

Thus on `G_j` the largest dyadic bit of the actual large-box mass is exactly

\[
 \boxed{P(C_{n_j})=P_j.}
\tag{12.9}
\]

Moreover (12.4) gives

\[
 \frac{P_j}{\delta n_j}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{12.10}
\]

Fix any `alpha<1`. By (12.10), for all large `j`,

\[
 \alpha\delta n_j<P_j=P(C_{n_j})
\tag{12.11}
\]

on `G_j`. Apply the exact coalescence comparison of Section 10 to the bad event

\[
 Y_{(1)}\le\alpha\delta n_j.
\tag{12.12}
\]

The source/target energy gap is

\[
 \kappa_pP_j^p-o(n_j^p)=\Omega(n_j^p),
\tag{12.13}
\]

while preimage entropy is `o(n_j^p)`. Therefore

\[
 P\left(Y_{(1)}\le\alpha\delta n_j,\ G_j\right)\to0.
\tag{12.14}
\]

Because `P(G_j)->1`,

\[
 P\left(\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}\le\alpha\delta\right)\to0
\tag{12.15}
\]

for every fixed `alpha<1`.

For the upper bound, deterministically

\[
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_j}
 \le \frac{K_{n_j}}{n_j}+\frac{C_{n_j}}{n_j}.
\tag{12.16}
