## 4. Mathematical idea before proof

### B19 — make the coalescence target an algorithm

The preceding proof mapped two large fragments of total mass `c` to two target coordinates `(g(c),d(c))`. That route fails if `w_{d(c)}` is extremely small.

Instead, recursively apply the same good-landing map to the residual:

\[
x_0=c,
\qquad
z_r=g(x_r),
\qquad
x_{r+1}=x_r-z_r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `x-g(x)=o(x)`, then eventually `x_{r+1}<=x_r/2`. The recursion terminates after `O(log c)` steps at a bounded residual. The total target energy is

\[
\sum_r z_r^p
\le c^p + C\,[c-g(c)]^p+O(1),
\tag{4.2}
\]

so the catastrophic weight of the *first residual site itself* is never paid.

### B20 — identify exactly why the old algorithm failed

The old two-coordinate algorithm failed because it demanded one lower bound on `w_{d(c)}`. That was an artefact of the target representation, not necessarily an obstruction in the law. Recursive landing shows that **arbitrary direct suppression of the residual is harmless whenever the nearest matched landing has sublinear backward gap**.

The obstruction left by this route is stronger and geometrically clearer: a genuine difficult law must have matched favorable landing sites separated by a positive fraction of the current scale (or suffer a leading landing-energy mismatch), not merely assign tiny weight to the first leftover integer.

### B25 — count source and target configurations exactly

The recursive target uses `L_n=O(log n)` coordinates rather than two. That loses the earlier simple two-coordinate fiber count, but it is still exact: for each ordered large pair choose a deterministic set of `L_n` coordinates, replace their total mass by the canonical recursive decomposition, and pad with zeros. A target configuration has at most

\[
n^2(m_n+1)^{L_n}
=\exp\{O((\log n)^2)\}
\tag{4.3}
\]

preimage records. The fragmentation-energy gain is `exp(-c K_n^p)` with `K_n>=n^(1/2)`, so it overwhelms this subexponential multiplicity.

## 5. New fixed-law class: sublinearly dense matched landings

### Definition 5.1

Fix

\[
0<p<1,
\qquad
a>0,
\qquad C_+,C_->0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `w=(w_k)_{k>=0}` be one fixed **full-support** sequence (`w_k>0` for every `k`) such that, after enlarging constants to cover finitely many small indices,

\[
\boxed{w_k\le C_+e^{-a k^p}\qquad(k\ge0).}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Assume there is an integer-valued landing map `g(c)` for all sufficiently large integers `c`, with

\[
0\le g(c)\le c,
\qquad
d(c):=c-g(c),
\tag{5.3}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{\frac{d(c)}c\longrightarrow0,}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{w_{g(c)}\ge C_-e^{-a g(c)^p}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

There is **no lower bound whatsoever on `w_{d(c)}`**.

Because (5.4) implies `g(c)/c->1`, the sequence of good landing sites is unbounded and (5.5) gives `w_{g(c)}^(1/g(c))->1`. Together with (5.2), the radius of convergence is exactly one. The stretched upper envelope gives `Phi(1)<infinity` and moments of every order. Full support gives span one, `nu>0`, and positive finite variance.

Choose a fixed threshold `c_*` so large that for every `c>=c_*`,

\[
d(c)\le c/2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

## 6. Canonical recursive reservoir decomposition

For every integer `c>=c_*`, define recursively

\[
x_0=c,
\qquad
z_r=g(x_r),
\qquad
x_{r+1}=d(x_r)
\tag{6.1}
\]

for as long as `x_r>=c_*`. Let `R=R(c)` be the first index with `x_R<c_*`. Then

\[
\boxed{c=z_0+z_1+\cdots+z_{R-1}+x_R.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

By (5.6),

\[
x_r\le2^{-(r-1)}d(c),\qquad r\ge1,
\tag{6.3}
\]

and

\[
R(c)\le C_0+C_1\log(c+2).
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 — recursive target energy

There is a constant `A<infinity`, depending only on the fixed law and `p,a`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{R(c)-1} z_r^p
\le
c^p+A d(c)^p+A.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Moreover, if this decomposition is placed in any `L>=R(c)+1` coordinates and padded with zeros, its unnormalized product weight is bounded below by

\[
\boxed{
W_{\rm tar}(c,L)
\ge
B^{-L}\exp\{-a c^p-A d(c)^p\}
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

for a fixed constant `B>1`.

### Proof

Since `z_0<=c`, `z_0^p<=c^p`. For `r>=1`, `z_r<=x_r`, so by (6.3),

\[
\sum_{r\ge1}z_r^p
\le
\sum_{r\ge1}x_r^p
\le
d(c)^p\sum_{s\ge0}2^{-sp}
=
\frac{d(c)^p}{1-2^{-p}}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

This proves (6.5) after enlarging `A` for the finitely many terminal cases.

For each nonterminal good piece, (5.5) gives

\[
w_{z_r}\ge C_-e^{-a z_r^p}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

The terminal residual `x_R` belongs to the finite set `{0,1,...,c_*-1}` and has positive weight by full support. Padding uses `w_0>0`. Hence all fixed multiplicative constants can be bounded below by `B^{-L}`. Combining with (6.5) proves (6.6). `square`

The important point is that no term `-log w_{d(c)}` occurs in (6.6).

## 7. Fragmentation energy for any selected block containing two large boxes

Let `0<p<1`. Suppose nonnegative integers `y_1,...,y_L` have total

\[
C=\sum_{r=1}^L y_r
\tag{7.1}
\]

and at least two coordinates exceed an integer cutoff `K`.

### Lemma 7.1

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=1}^L y_r^p
\ge
C^p+(1-p)(K+1)^p.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

Choose one large coordinate `u>K`. The sum of all remaining coordinates is `C-u>K` because another selected coordinate is also larger than `K`. Since `x^p` is subadditive for `0<p<1`,

\[
\sum_r y_r^p
\ge
u^p+(C-u)^p.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The concave function `s^p+(C-s)^p` takes its minimum on `[K+1,C-K-1]` at an endpoint, so

\[
u^p+(C-u)^p
\ge
(K+1)^p+(C-K-1)^p.
\tag{7.4}
\]

By the mean-value theorem and `p-1<0`,

\[
C^p-(C-K-1)^p
\le
p(K+1)(C-K-1)^{p-1}
\le
p(K+1)^p.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Subtracting proves (7.2). `square`

(The symbol `u` in (7.3) denotes the chosen large coordinate; no source parameter `nu` is involved in this lemma.)

## 8. Exact `L`-coordinate coalescence map

Fix `n`, total mass `m=m_n`, an integer cutoff `K`, and an integer `L<=n`. Let

\[
R_K:=\#\{i:Y_i>K\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For each ordered pair `(i,j)` with `i!=j`, deterministically choose an ordered set

\[
I_{ij}=(i,j,i_3,\ldots,i_L)
\tag{8.2}
\]

of `L` distinct box indices; for example take `i,j` followed by the smallest unused labels.

For a source allocation `y` with `y_i,y_j>K`, let

\[
C=\sum_{r\in I_{ij}}y_r.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Replace the occupancies on `I_{ij}` by the canonical recursive decomposition of `C` from Section 6, padded with zeros to exactly `L` entries, in a fixed deterministic order. Leave every other box unchanged. Call the resulting allocation `T(y,i,j)`.

This map preserves exactly:

- the number `n` of boxes;
- the total mass `m`;
- the fixed weight law.

### Lemma 8.1 — uniform source/target weight ratio

Suppose `C>=2(K+1)` and the recursive decomposition of every `C<=m` uses at most `L` coordinates. Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\prod_r w_{y_r}}{\prod_r w_{T(y,i,j)_r}}
\le
B_1^{L}
\exp\{-a(1-p)(K+1)^p+A d(C)^p\}
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

for fixed constants `A,B_1` independent of `n,m,K,C`.

### Proof

The factors outside `I_{ij}` cancel. After enlarging `C_+` in (5.2) to all indices,

\[
\prod_{r\in I_{ij}}w_{y_r}
\le
C_+^L\exp\left\{-a\sum_{r\in I_{ij}}y_r^p\right\}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Lemma 7.1 gives

\[
\sum_{r\in I_{ij}}y_r^p
\ge
C^p+(1-p)(K+1)^p.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Lemma 6.1 gives the lower bound

\[
\prod_{r\in I_{ij}}w_{T(y,i,j)_r}
\ge
B^{-L}e^{-aC^p-Ad(C)^p}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

Divide (8.5) by (8.7) and absorb constants into `B_1`. `square`

### Lemma 8.2 — exact preimage multiplicity

For a fixed target allocation `x`, the number of source records `(y,i,j)` mapping to `x` is at most

\[
\boxed{n^2(m+1)^L.}
\tag{8.8}
\]

### Proof

There are fewer than `n^2` ordered pairs `(i,j)`. Once `(i,j)` is fixed, the selected set `I_{ij}` is known. Since the map preserves the total selected mass, `C` is recovered from the target as

\[
C=\sum_{r\in I_{ij}}x_r.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Outside `I_{ij}`, the source is identical to the target. The source values on `I_{ij}` are a nonnegative `L`-tuple summing to `C`; ignoring the additional restrictions `y_i,y_j>K` only enlarges the count. The number of such tuples is at most `(C+1)^L<=(m+1)^L`. Multiply by the ordered-pair count. `square`

### Corollary 8.3 — exact factorial-moment bound

Let

\[
D(m):=\max_{c_*\le c\le m}d(c).
\tag{8.10}
\]

Whenever `L` is large enough for all recursive targets up to `m`,

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_2
\le
n^2(m+1)^L B_1^L
\exp\{-a(1-p)(K+1)^p+A D(m)^p\}.
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

### Proof

Expand the unnormalized numerator of `E(R_K)_2` over all records `(y,i,j)` with `y_i,y_j>K`. Map every record to its target allocation. Lemma 8.1 bounds each source weight by the displayed uniform ratio times its target weight, and Lemma 8.2 bounds the number of records landing on each target. Summing target weights gives at most the full partition function `Z(m,n)`. Divide by `Z(m,n)`. `square`

This is an exact finite double count. It does not use a local-limit theorem or any probability transfer between different source problems.

## 9. Adaptive cutoff from the geometric landing gap

Fix `M>lambda+1`. For all sufficiently large `n`, `m_n<=Mn`. Define

\[
D_n:=\max_{c_*\le c\le\lfloor Mn\rfloor}d(c).
\tag{9.1}
\]

Because `d(c)/c->0`,

\[
\boxed{D_n=o(n).}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Indeed, for every `epsilon>0`, all sufficiently large `c` satisfy `d(c)<=epsilon c<=epsilon Mn`, while the finitely many smaller values contribute only `O(1)` to `D_n`.

Choose

\[
\boxed{
K_n=\left\lfloor
\max\left\{n^{1/2},\sqrt{n\max(D_n,1)}\right\}
\right\rfloor .
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Then

\[
K_n\to\infty,
\qquad
K_n=o(n),
\qquad
\frac{D_n}{K_n}\to0,
\tag{9.4}
\]

and hence

\[
D_n^p=o(K_n^p).
\tag{9.5}
\]

Choose

\[
L_n=\left\lceil C_2\log(n+2)\right\rceil
\tag{9.6}
\]

with `C_2` large enough that (6.4) guarantees every recursive decomposition of every `c<=Mn` fits inside `L_n` coordinates.

Corollary 8.3 now gives

\[
E(R_{K_n})_2
\le
\exp\Big\{
O((\log n)^2)
-a(1-p)K_n^p
+A D_n^p
\Big\}.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Since `K_n>=n^(1/2)`,

\[
K_n^p\ge n^{p/2}\gg(\log n)^2,
\tag{9.8}
\]

and by (9.5) the positive `D_n^p` term is negligible. Therefore

\[
\boxed{E(R_{K_n})_2\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{9.9}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{P(R_{K_n}\ge2)\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{9.10}
\]

The rest of the proof must still show, unconditionally under the exact conditioning, that a positive linear amount of mass lies above this particular adaptive cutoff. That is the next section; it is not inferred merely from a conditional estimate.

## 10. Exact-conditioning denominator and mass above the adaptive cutoff

Normalize to `pi_k=w_k/Phi(1)` and let `X,X_1,...` be iid with law `pi`. The global stretched envelope and full support give finite positive variance and lattice span one.

### Lemma 10.1 — polynomial-times-stretched conditioning lower bound

There is a constant `C<infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge n^{-1/2}e^{-C n^p}
}
\tag{10.1}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`.

### Proof

Set `L=L_n` and `N=n-L`. Choose an integer `r_n` nearest `nu N`. The lattice local central limit theorem gives

\[
P(S_N=r_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}
\tag{10.2}
\]

for large `n`. Put

\[
c_n=m_n-r_n=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{10.3}
\]

Use the canonical recursive decomposition of `c_n` in the remaining `L` designated coordinates and pad with zeros. Lemma 6.1, with the harmless normalization factor `Phi(1)^{-L}` absorbed into `B^L`, gives

\[
P(\hbox{these }L\hbox{ coordinates equal the target})
\ge
\exp\{-a c_n^p-A d(c_n)^p-O(L)\}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

Since `d(c_n)=o(c_n)=o(n)` and `L=O(log n)`, the exponent is `-O(n^p)`. Independence between the `N` background coordinates and the `L` target coordinates, together with (10.2), gives (10.1). `square`

### Lemma 10.2 — tail-adapted truncated upper deviation

Let

\[
Z_K:=X\mathbf1_{\{X\le K\}},
\qquad
t_K=(a/4)K^{p-1}.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Then

\[
\log Ee^{t_KZ_K}=\nu t_K+o(t_K)
\tag{10.6}
\]

as `K->infinity`. Consequently, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left(\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{i,K}\ge(\nu+\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K^{1-p}}\right\}
}
\tag{10.7}
\]

for all large `K,n`.

### Proof

For `1<=x<=K`, because `p-1<0`,

\[
t_Kx\le(a/4)x^p.
\tag{10.8}
\]

The normalized law has `pi_x<=Ce^{-a x^p}`. Hence

\[
0\le\frac{e^{t_Kx}-1}{t_K}
\le xe^{(a/4)x^p},
\tag{10.9}
\]

and after multiplying by `pi_x` the right side is dominated by the summable sequence `Cx e^{-(3a/4)x^p}`. Dominated convergence gives

\[
\frac{Ee^{t_KZ_K}-1}{t_K}\to EX=\nu,
\tag{10.10}
\]

which implies (10.6). Chernoff's inequality at `t_K` then gives

\[
P\left(\sum Z_{i,K}\ge(\nu+\epsilon)n\right)
\le
\exp\{-n t_K(\epsilon+o(1))\},
\tag{10.11}
\]

and `t_K=(a/4)/K^(1-p)`, proving (10.7). `square`

### Lemma 10.3 — truncated lower deviation

For every fixed `epsilon>0`, for all sufficiently large `K`,

\[
\boxed{
P\left(\sum_{i=1}^nZ_{i,K}\le(\nu-\epsilon)n\right)
\le e^{-c_\epsilon n}.
}
\tag{10.12}
\]

### Proof

Choose a small fixed `s>0`. Since `EX=nu`,

\[
\log Ee^{-sX}=-\nu s+o(s)
\tag{10.13}
\]

as `s downarrow0`. Choose `s` so small that the right side is less than `-(nu-epsilon/2)s`. Since `e^{-sZ_K}->e^{-sX}` and is bounded by one, dominated convergence yields the same strict inequality with `Z_K` in place of `X` for all sufficiently large `K`. Apply Chernoff's inequality to `-Z_K`. `square`

### Lemma 10.4 — conditioned small-box mass

For the adaptive cutoff `K_n` from (9.3), under the conditioned allocation,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
=\nu n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{10.14}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{10.15}
\]

### Proof

For the upper deviation, divide (10.7) by the conditioning probability lower bound (10.1). Since `K_n=o(n)`,

\[
\frac{n/K_n^{1-p}}{n^p}
=
\left(\frac{n}{K_n}\right)^{1-p}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{10.16}
\]

Thus

\[
n^{1/2}\exp\left\{Cn^p-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K_n^{1-p}}\right\}\to0.
\tag{10.17}
\]

For the lower deviation, divide (10.12) by (10.1); since `p<1`, `e^{-c n+Cn^p}n^{1/2}->0`. This proves (10.14). Subtracting from the deterministic total `m_n=lambda n+o(n)` gives (10.15). `square`

## 11. Main theorem and an explicit law beyond the previous class

### Theorem 11.1 — sublinear-gap recursive-reservoir condensation

Let one fixed full-support weight law satisfy Definition 5.1 for some `0<p<1`. Then for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
\frac{Y_{(2)}}n\xrightarrow p0.
}
\tag{11.1}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

Thus on this class the frozen metric attains the source-side ceiling:

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{11.3}
\]

### Proof

Lemma 10.4 says that boxes above `K_n` collectively carry `delta n+o_p(n)` balls. Because `delta>0`, at least one such box exists with probability tending to one. Equation (9.10) says that two such boxes exist with probability tending to zero. Hence with high probability there is exactly one box above `K_n`, and its occupancy is

\[
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{11.4}
\]

Every other box is at most `K_n=o(n)`. This proves (11.1). Finally,

\[
0\le Y_{(1)}/n\le m_n/n=O(1),
\tag{11.5}
\]

so convergence in probability plus uniform boundedness gives (11.2). Janson's general upper ceiling (2.5) shows that (11.3) is exact. `square`

The conclusion is a **full-sequence unconditional** result. No selected subsequence is used.

### Explicit stress law

Take

\[
p=1/3.
\tag{11.6}
\]

Choose a sufficiently large even integer `a_1` and recursively define the even gap

\[
\Delta_j
=
2\left\lceil\frac{a_j}{2\log a_j}\right\rceil,
\qquad
a_{j+1}=a_j+\Delta_j.
\tag{11.7}
\]

Every `a_j` is even. Put

\[
G:=\{a_j:j\ge1\}.
\tag{11.8}
\]

Define one fixed full-support law by `w_0=1` and, for `k>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
w_k=
\begin{cases}
\exp(-k^{1/3}),&k\in G,\\
\exp(-k^2),&k\notin G.
\end{cases}}
\tag{11.9}
\]

This has the global upper envelope

\[
w_k\le e^{-k^{1/3}}.
\tag{11.10}
\]

For a large `c`, let `g(c)` be the largest `a_j<=c`. Then

\[
w_{g(c)}=e^{-g(c)^{1/3}},
\tag{11.11}
\]

and

\[
0\le d(c)=c-g(c)<\Delta_j
=O(c/\log c)=o(c).
\tag{11.12}
\]

Therefore Theorem 11.1 applies and proves, for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible `m_n/n->lambda`,

\[
\boxed{
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
Y_{(2)}/n\to_p0,
\qquad
E[Y_{(1)}/n]\to\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{11.13}
\]

Hence for this one fixed law,

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{11.14}
\]

### Why the immediately preceding theorem does not apply

Take

\[
c_j=a_{j+1}-1.
\tag{11.15}
\]

Then the preceding good landing is `g(c_j)=a_j` and

\[
d(c_j)=\Delta_j-1.
\tag{11.16}
\]

Because `a_j` and `Delta_j` are even, `d(c_j)` is odd, whereas every member of `G` is even. Thus `d(c_j)\notin G`, and

\[
-\log w_{d(c_j)}=d(c_j)^2
\asymp\frac{c_j^2}{(\log c_j)^2}.
\tag{11.17}
\]

In particular,

\[
\frac{-\log w_{d(c_j)}}{c_j^{1/3}}\to\infty.
\tag{11.18}
\]

So there is no direct residual lower bound of the preceding form `w_{d(c)}>=C e^{-h(c)}` with `h(c)=o(c^(1/3))`. The new recursive-reservoir theorem is a genuine extension.

## 12. Counterexample checks and exact limits of transfer

### 12.1 Catastrophic direct residual suppression is not enough

The explicit law (11.9) suppresses the first residual site by an energy of order `c^2/log^2 c` along (11.15), far larger than the matched `c^(1/3)` landing scale. Nevertheless the recursive target never pays that site. This refutes the *methodological guess* that a super-leading first-residual penalty by itself is a promising route to full dust. It does **not** refute any statement of Janson.

### 12.2 Positive fractional gaps remain uncovered

The sublinear gap hypothesis is essential to this proof. If favorable landing sites are geometric, for example near powers of two, then there are totals with

\[
d(c)\asymp c.
\tag{12.1}
\]

The recursive target can then pay an additional order-`c^p` energy already at its second piece. A sublinear cutoff gain `K_n^p=o(n^p)` does not uniformly defeat that term. The theorem makes no claim in this regime.

### 12.3 Leading landing-coefficient mismatch remains uncovered

If good sites satisfy only

\[
w_{g(c)}\gtrsim e^{-a_+g(c)^p},
\qquad a_+>a,
\tag{12.2}
\]

against a global upper envelope `e^{-a k^p}`, the target incurs a positive order-`c^p` mismatch. The present proof does not absorb it.

### 12.4 Neighboring Problem 18.41 is not contradicted

The repository's fixed monotone-cliff law for Problem 18.41 has polynomial-scale weights and produces two macroscopic boxes along cliff subsequences. It is outside Definition 5.1. That result correctly warns that **unique one-giant condensation is not universal**. The present theorem concerns a restricted stretched-exponential class and is compatible with that counterexample.

### 12.5 One fixed law, arbitrary full sequence

The set `G` and all weights in (11.9) are fixed once and for all. The landing map merely selects already-existing good sites. The proof works for every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`, not for a law or subsequence chosen separately at each `n`.

### 12.6 No conditional metric promotion

The pair-scarcity estimate (9.9) is already under the exact conditioned allocation, because it is derived by an exact partition-function double count. The mass estimate (10.15) is promoted from iid probabilities only after division by the explicit unconditional lower bound (10.1) for the conditioning event. Thus the frozen expectation metric moves through an unconditional full-sequence conclusion.

## 16. Remaining universal obligation and next route

The recursive argument eliminates all direct-residual suppression when matched stretched-exponential landing sites are sublinearly dense. The simplest surviving obstruction in this family is now

\[
\boxed{\limsup_{c\to\infty}\frac{c-g(c)}c>0,}
\tag{16.1}

\[

and compare the best finite-part target energy with the two-fragment source energy. A universal positive variational gap would extend persistence; a single fixed law for which every finite target partition has zero/nonpositive gap on all large scales would be a credible counterexample architecture. That is a genuinely new route rather than another residual lemma.
