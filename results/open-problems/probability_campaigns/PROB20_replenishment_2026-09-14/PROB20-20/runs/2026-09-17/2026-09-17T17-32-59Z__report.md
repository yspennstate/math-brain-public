## 2. Exact source statement and fixed-law hypotheses

Primary source rechecked on 2026-09-17:

Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton--Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), 103--252, arXiv:1112.0510v1, Section 18.6, Problem 18.39, printed page 122.

The current arXiv record still exposes only v1, submitted 2 December 2011.

Problem 18.39 asks whether

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\xrightarrow p0
\tag{2.1}
\]

can occur for a **single fixed weight sequence** in the regime

\[
0<\nu<\lambda,
\qquad
m_n/n\to\lambda.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The source immediately distinguishes Example 18.36, where vanishing can occur on a subsequence, from the full-sequence question. Janson conjectures that full-sequence vanishing is impossible and states the stronger expected alternative that there should be a fixed positive macroscopic fraction and a subsequence on which the largest box exceeds that fraction with high probability. Problem 18.40 is a separate `nu=0` question.

The exact source cutoff theorem used here is Lemma 18.31. For a fixed nonnegative weight sequence `w=(w_k)`, with `w_0>0`, unbounded support, `m_n/n->lambda`, and `nu<lambda<infinity`:

1. for every deterministic `K_n->infinity`, the mass above `K_n` is at most
   \[
   (\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n);
   \tag{2.3}
   \]
2. there exists a deterministic source-produced `Omega_n->infinity` such that for every `K_n->infinity` with `K_n<=Omega_n`, the mass above `K_n` is exactly
   \[
   (\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
   \tag{2.4}
   \]

The arbitrary-cutoff versus source-slow-cutoff distinction is preserved exactly.

Write

\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Applying (2.3) with any deterministic `K_n->infinity`, `K_n=o(n)`, yields

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta+o_p(1).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, either `Y_(1)<=K_n=o(n)` or it belongs to the mass above `K_n`. Since `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`,

\[
\limsup_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus `delta` is the ceiling of the frozen limsup metric for every fixed law in the source regime.

## 3. B2 — attack the negation first: alternate enormous cliffs forever

The preceding theorem might suggest that full dust could be engineered simply by violating its bounded adjacent-ratio hypothesis at every other scale.

Take binary support

\[
s_r=2^r,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and a smooth summable reference activity

\[
v_r=\frac1{(r+2)^2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define one fixed actual activity profile by

\[
\boxed{
u_r=
\begin{cases}
v_r,&r\text{ even},\\[1mm]
v_r\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{2^r}{\sqrt{r+2}}\right),&r\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\tag{3.3}
\]

and actual weights

\[
w_0=1,
\qquad
w_{2^r}=\frac{u_r}{2^r},
\qquad
w_k=0\text{ off the powers of two}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is deliberately hostile to the preceding proof. For odd `r`,

\[
\frac{u_{r-1}}{u_r}
=
\frac{v_{r-1}}{v_r}
\exp\!\left(\frac{2^r}{\sqrt{r+2}}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{3.5}
\]

whereas for even `r`,

\[
\frac{u_{r-1}}{u_r}
=
\frac{v_{r-1}}{v_r}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{2^{r-1}}{\sqrt{r+1}}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

So neither eventual monotonicity nor a bounded adjacent activity ratio survives. The obstruction repeats at every parity change, not merely at sparse exceptional scales.

The suppression is also genuinely enormous on the bad scales. Nevertheless the root-scale cost remains compatible with radius one because

\[
\frac1{2^r}\log\left(\exp\!\left[-\frac{2^r}{\sqrt{r+2}}\right]\right)
=-\frac1{\sqrt{r+2}}\to0.
\tag{3.7}

This looks like a plausible cliff-based route toward diffuse condensation.

It fails. The even scales still touch the regular summable profile `v` exactly, and the inverse-fragmentation tree can be dominated by the regular profile. The rest of this report proves a general theorem explaining that failure.

This is the B2 outcome: **the direct alternating-cliff counterexample attempt fails, and the reason is a new comparison principle rather than a local repair of the old ratio proof.**

## 4. B18 — say the exact fragmentation argument in comparison language

The preceding proof used actual activities at every level. The useful reformulation is monotone:

- decreasing a lower-level source activity can only decrease the total inverse-fragmentation fiber;
- decreasing the target root activity is dangerous because it shrinks the denominator;
- therefore an arbitrary cliffy law is safe whenever it lies below a regular summable majorant everywhere and returns within a fixed factor of that majorant at infinitely many possible root scales.

This turns the exact recursion into a domination theorem.

### Definition 4.1 — regular-majorant contact pair

Let

\[
s_0=1,
\qquad
s_r=c_rs_{r-1},
\qquad
c_r\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
\tag{4.1}
\]

be an arbitrary divisibility-chain support. No upper bound or growth hypothesis is placed on the radices `c_r`.

Let `(v_r)` be a positive reference activity profile satisfying

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}v_r<\infty
\tag{4.2}
\]

and, for some fixed finite `R>=1` and all sufficiently large `r`,

\[
\boxed{
1\le\frac{v_{r-1}}{v_r}\le R.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Let `(u_r)` be an arbitrary positive actual activity profile satisfying

\[
0<u_r\le v_r
\qquad(r\ge0).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Assume there are a constant `kappa>0` and an infinite set of contact indices `J` such that

\[
\boxed{
u_j\ge\kappa v_j
\qquad(j\in J).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

No adjacent-ratio regularity is assumed for `u` itself.

Define the one fixed actual weight sequence

\[
\boxed{
w_0=1,
\qquad
w_{s_r}=\frac{u_r}{s_r},
\qquad
w_k=0\quad(k\ge1,\ k\notin\{s_r:r\ge0\}).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The profile `v` is only a proof comparison object. The random allocation uses `w`, hence `u`, at every `n`; there is no changing law.

## 5. Source admissibility of the actual cliffy law

### Lemma 5.1 — radius one and finite positive boundary mean

Under (4.1)--(4.5), the actual weight sequence (4.6) has radius of convergence `rho=1`, span one, unbounded support, and

\[
0<\nu
=
\frac{\sum_{r\ge0}u_r}
     {1+\sum_{r\ge0}u_r/s_r}
<\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

Condition (4.3) implies for all sufficiently large `r`

\[
v_r\ge C R^{-r}
\tag{5.2}
\]

for some constant `C>0`. Since `s_r>=2^r`,

\[
\frac{|\log v_r|+\log s_r}{s_r}\to0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus

\[
\left(\frac{v_r}{s_r}\right)^{1/s_r}\to1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `u_r<=v_r`,

\[
\limsup_r\left(\frac{u_r}{s_r}\right)^{1/s_r}\le1.
\tag{5.5}
\]

On the infinite contact set `J`, (4.5) gives

\[
\left(\frac{u_j}{s_j}\right)^{1/s_j}
\ge
\kappa^{1/s_j}
\left(\frac{v_j}{s_j}\right)^{1/s_j}
\to1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence the root-test limsup is exactly one and `rho=1`.

Also

\[
\Phi(1)=1+\sum_r\frac{u_r}{s_r}<\infty,
\qquad
\Phi'(1)=\sum_ru_r<\infty
\tag{5.7}
\]

because `u_r<=v_r` and `sum v_r<infinity`. Positivity follows from `u_r>0`. Since `s_0=1`, `w_1=u_0>0`, so the span is one. All `u_r` are positive and `s_r->infinity`, so the support is unbounded. Equation (5.1) follows from `nu=Phi'(1)/Phi(1)`. `square`

### Lemma 5.2 — a summable slowly growing majorant baseline

There is a deterministic nondecreasing sequence `A_r->infinity` such that

\[
\boxed{
\sum_rA_rv_r<\infty.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
A_rv_r\to0.
\tag{5.9}
\]

#### Proof

Choose increasing indices `R_l` with

\[
\sum_{r\ge R_l}v_r\le2^{-2l}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Set `A_r=2^l` on `R_l<=r<R_(l+1)` and define `A_r` positively and nondecreasingly on the finite initial segment. Then

\[
\sum_rA_rv_r
\le C+\sum_{l\ge1}2^l\sum_{r\ge R_l}v_r
\le C+\sum_{l\ge1}2^{-l}<\infty.
\tag{5.11}
\]

The terms of a convergent positive series tend to zero, proving (5.9). `square`

## 6. Main theorem

### Theorem 6.1 — regular-majorant contacts force a maximal limsup condensate

Let the one fixed actual law (4.6) satisfy (4.1)--(4.5). Let `lambda>nu`, and let **any already-fixed admissible sequence** satisfy

\[
\frac{m_n}{n}\to\lambda.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then there exists a deterministic subsequence `n_l` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\to\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
b(w,\lambda):=\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

No regularity is assumed on the actual adjacent ratios `u_(r-1)/u_r`; they may oscillate between zero and infinity along subsequences. The result is a deterministic-subsequence/limsup theorem and does **not** assert full-sequence one-box condensation.

The proof occupies Sections 7--11.

## 8. Exact actual-law source-to-target comparison

Fix `j>r_n`. Suppose an actual source histogram lies on a phase where

\[
s_j<E_n<2s_j\le s_{j+1}
\tag{8.1}
\]

and has no actual box of size at least `s_j`.

Repeated mixed-radix carrying

\[
c_{r+1}\text{ copies of }s_r
\longrightarrow
1\text{ copy of }s_{r+1}
\tag{8.2}
\]

converts the excess multiset to canonical mixed-radix form. Because of (8.1), its top canonical digit is exactly one copy of `s_j`. Trace the ancestry of that digit backward. This selects integers `d_r`, `r_n<=r<j`, such that

\[
0\le d_r\le e_{n,r},
\qquad
\sum_{r<j}d_rs_r=s_j.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Let `D=sum d_r`. Replace those `D` lower boxes by one `s_j` box and `D-1` zero boxes. The total number of boxes and total mass are unchanged. Since the removed boxes were excess above their majorant baselines and the root baseline will be zero on the selected phase subsequence, this map also preserves the excess mass `E_n`.

For an occupancy histogram `N`, let

\[
W_u(N):=\frac{n!}{\prod_kN_k!}\prod_kw_k^{N_k}
\tag{8.4}
\]

be its aggregate actual conditioned partition-function weight.

Define the actual Poisson-scale activities

\[
\Lambda^u_{n,r}:=n w_{s_r}=\frac n{s_r}u_r.
\tag{8.5}
\]

If the root baseline `b_(n,j)=0`, the exact factorial ratio gives

\[
\frac{W_u(\mathrm{source})}{W_u(\mathrm{target})}
\le
\frac1{\Lambda^u_{n,j}}
\prod_{r<j}
\left(
\frac{\Lambda^u_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}
\right)^{d_r}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The proof is the same exact histogram calculation as the corrected preceding report: the new zeros contribute at most `n^(D-1)`, each removed excess box gives a factorial denominator at least `b_(n,r)+1`, and the normalization identity

\[
n^{D-1}\frac{\prod_rw_{s_r}^{d_r}}{w_{s_j}}
=
\frac1{n w_{s_j}}\prod_r(n w_{s_r})^{d_r}
\tag{8.7}
\]

produces (8.6).

Put

\[
q^u_{n,r}:=
\frac{\Lambda^u_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}.
\tag{8.8}
\]

For the majorant define

\[
q^v_{n,r}:=
\frac{\overline\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}.
\tag{8.9}
\]

Since `u_r<=v_r`,

\[
\boxed{
q^u_{n,r}\le q^v_{n,r}.
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

Moreover, by the definition of `b_(n,r)`,

\[
\boxed{
q^v_{n,r}
\le
\min\{\overline\Lambda_{n,r},A_r^{-1}\}.
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

This is the key monotonicity: all source-fragment activities of the actual law are dominated by the regular comparison profile.

## 9. Majorant fragmentation tree and two-scale invariant

For the actual law, define the total lower-scale inverse fiber

\[
F^u_{n,j}:=
\sum_{\substack{d_r\ge0,\ r_n\le r<j\\
                 \sum_rd_rs_r=s_j}}
\prod_{r<j}(q^u_{n,r})^{d_r}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Define `F^v_(n,j)` identically with `q^v` in place of `q^u`. By (8.10), term by term,

\[
\boxed{F^u_{n,j}\le F^v_{n,j}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Now define the majorant tree recursion

\[
T^v_{n,r_n}:=q^v_{n,r_n},
\tag{9.3}
\]

and, for `r>r_n`,

\[
T^v_{n,r}:=q^v_{n,r}+(T^v_{n,r-1})^{c_r}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Every lower-scale partition of `s_j` is represented by reversing mixed-radix carries. A partition in `F^v_(n,j)` has no root leaf, so the root splits into `c_j` children. Hence

\[
F^v_{n,j}\le(T^v_{n,j-1})^{c_j}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Set

\[
\varepsilon_n:=A_{r_n}^{-1}\to0
\tag{9.6}

and

\[
M^v_{n,r}:=
\min\{\overline\Lambda_{n,r},\varepsilon_n\}.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Since `A_r` is nondecreasing, (8.11) gives

\[
q^v_{n,r}\le M^v_{n,r}
\qquad(r\ge r_n).
\tag{9.8}
\]

For all sufficiently large `n`, every `r>=r_n` is in the local-regularity range of `v`. Therefore

\[
\frac{\overline\Lambda_{n,r-1}}
     {\overline\Lambda_{n,r}}
=
c_r\frac{v_{r-1}}{v_r},
\tag{9.9}

and thus

\[
\boxed{
c_r
\le
\frac{\overline\Lambda_{n,r-1}}
     {\overline\Lambda_{n,r}}
\le Rc_r.}
\tag{9.10}
\]

### Lemma 9.1 — majorant invariant

For all sufficiently large `n`, uniformly for every `r>=r_n`,

\[
\boxed{T^v_{n,r}\le2M^v_{n,r}.}
\tag{9.11}
\]

#### Proof

The base case is immediate from (9.8). Suppose

\[
T^v_{n,r-1}\le2M^v_{n,r-1}
\tag{9.12}
\]

and write `c=c_r`. Exactly as in the corrected preceding theorem, the three possibilities according to whether `overline Lambda_(n,r-1)` and `overline Lambda_(n,r)` lie above or below `epsilon_n` give

\[
\frac{(2M^v_{n,r-1})^c}{M^v_{n,r}}
\le
Rc\,2^c\varepsilon_n^{c-1}.
\tag{9.13}
\]

For `epsilon<=1/6`,

\[
\frac{(c+1)2^{c+1}\varepsilon^c}
     {c2^c\varepsilon^{c-1}}
=
2\varepsilon\frac{c+1}{c}
\le3\varepsilon\le\frac12,
\tag{9.14}
\]

so the sequence `c 2^c epsilon^(c-1)` decreases for `c>=2`. Hence

\[
\sup_{c\ge2}Rc2^c\varepsilon_n^{c-1}
=8R\varepsilon_n\to0.
\tag{9.15}
\]

Eventually the right side of (9.13) is at most one. Thus

\[
(T^v_{n,r-1})^c
\le
M^v_{n,r},
\]

and

\[
T^v_{n,r}
=q^v_{n,r}+(T^v_{n,r-1})^c
\le2M^v_{n,r}.
\]

This closes the induction. `square`

## 13. Intrinsic cliff-budget criterion via the least regular envelope

The comparison theorem can be converted into a criterion involving only the actual activity profile `u`.

Fix `R>1`. Define the tail supremum

\[
a_r:=\sup_{k\ge r}u_k.
\tag{13.1}
\]

Define recursively

\[
\boxed{
v^{(R)}_0:=a_0,
\qquad
v^{(R)}_r:=\max\left\{a_r,\frac{v^{(R)}_{r-1}}R\right\}.
}
\tag{13.2}
\]

### Lemma 13.1 — least `R`-regular decreasing majorant

The sequence `v^(R)` satisfies

\[
u_r\le v^{(R)}_r,
\tag{13.3}
\]

\[
1\le\frac{v^{(R)}_{r-1}}{v^{(R)}_r}\le R,
\tag{13.4}
\]

and it is pointwise minimal among all nonincreasing sequences `z_r` such that

\[
z_r\ge u_r,
\qquad
z_{r-1}/z_r\le R.
\tag{13.5}
\]

#### Proof

Since `a_r` is nonincreasing and `a_r<=a_(r-1)<=v^(R)_(r-1)`, both entries in the maximum (13.2) are at most `v^(R)_(r-1)`. Hence `v^(R)` is nonincreasing. The second entry gives `v^(R)_r>=v^(R)_(r-1)/R`, proving (13.4). Also `v^(R)_r>=a_r>=u_r`, proving (13.3).

If `z` satisfies (13.5), monotonicity and domination of `u` imply

\[
z_r\ge\sup_{k\ge r}u_k=a_r.
\]

The ratio condition implies `z_r>=z_(r-1)/R`. Starting from `z_0>=a_0=v^(R)_0` and inducting in (13.2) gives `z_r>=v^(R)_r` for every `r`. `square`

### Corollary 13.2 — an intrinsic necessary obstruction for dust in the chain class

Suppose for some `R>1`

\[
\sum_rv^{(R)}_r<\infty
\tag{13.6}
\]

and

\[
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{u_r}{v^{(R)}_r}>0.
\tag{13.7}
\]

Then for the corresponding fixed divisibility-chain law and every `lambda>nu`,

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{13.8}
\]

#### Proof

Choose `kappa>0` below the limsup in (13.7). Infinitely many indices satisfy

\[
u_r\ge\kappa v^{(R)}_r.
\]

Lemmas 13.1 and (13.6) give all hypotheses of Theorem 6.1 with comparison profile `v^(R)`. `square`

This yields a concrete **cliff budget**. Within the sparse divisibility-chain architecture, a genuine full-dust candidate must evade Corollary 13.2 for every `R>1`. Equivalently, for every `R` it must force at least one of the following:

1. the least `R`-regular decreasing majorant `v^(R)` is not summable; or
2. even if `v^(R)` is summable, the actual law loses all positive-fraction contact:
   \[
   u_r/v^{(R)}_r\to0
   \]
   along every infinite favorable record family.

This is substantially sharper than the preceding statement that `u_(r-1)/u_r` merely has to have large spikes.

### Why this obstruction is nonvacuous

A summable sequence `u` need not have a summable monotone or bounded-ratio majorant. For example, if very sparse indices `n_j` carry spikes of height about `j^{-2}` while the gaps `n_(j+1)-n_j` grow exponentially, then the tail-supremum `a_r` stays near `j^{-2}` across an exponentially long interval. The sum of `a_r`, and hence of every nonincreasing majorant, can diverge even though `sum u_r<infinity`.

So Corollary 13.2 does not secretly settle all summable activity profiles. It identifies precisely the sparse-cliff geometry that still escapes the comparison theorem.
