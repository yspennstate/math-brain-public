## 3. Exact blocker, in one displayed formula

The preceding adaptive-baseline theorem would force a one-box subsequence if one can produce deterministic source-compatible phases `(n_l,j_l,R_l,a_l)` satisfying

## 4. A useful reformulation of the blocker, not a new theorem

Put

\[
a_r:=-\log u_r,
\qquad
q_r:=\frac{a_r}{2^r}.
\]

Then every summand in `H` is exactly

\[
\frac{u_r}{u_j^{1/2^{j-r}}}
=
\exp\!\left[-2^r(q_r-q_j)\right].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Hence

\[
H_{R,j}
=
\sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
\exp\!\left[-2^r(q_r-q_j)\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

This identity is the cleanest way I found to state what the proof still lacks. Radius one supplies record values of `q_j` tending to zero, and finite mean supplies `u_r -> 0` with a summable tail. But `H -> 0` asks for something stronger: at a usable root `j`, the **whole source-visible backward profile** must separate from the root at the exponentially weighted scale `2^r(q_r-q_j)`.

A record root is attractive because `q_r-q_j>=0` for every earlier `r`, but a sequence of almost-flat record plateaux can keep some terms in (4.2) competitive. Conversely, an abrupt rate drop creates a favorable root but can be useless if the only source-valid missing-mass cutoff at that phase lies too far down the hierarchy to make the total `H` small with the required margin.

The issue is therefore not merely "find a record" and not merely "make the cutoff diverge". It is a **three-clock synchronization problem**:

- the record/root clock in `j`;
- the source-valid conditioned cutoff clock `Omega_{n(j)}` at `n(j)~2^j/delta`;
- the hierarchical fragmentation clock encoded by all weighted gaps in (4.2).

## 5. B12 — find what is conserved: record-drop budget

### Attempt

I tried to use the normalized exponential rate `q_r` as the conserved budget. On running record minima, let

\[
\Delta_j:=\min_{r<j}q_r-q_j>0.
\]

At a new record one automatically has

\[
q_r-q_j\ge\Delta_j\qquad(r<j),
\]

so (4.2) gives the formal upper envelope

\[
H_{R,j}
\le
\sum_{r=R}^{j-1} e^{-2^r\Delta_j}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus a sufficient record-phase condition would be roughly

\[
2^R\Delta_j\to\infty.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is exactly the sort of invariant one wants: all backward scales are paid by a **single record drop**.

### Why it fails to close the source problem

The total downward record budget is finite: record minima decrease to zero, so the sum of their drops is bounded by the initial rate. Nothing forces a record drop `Delta_j` to dominate the inverse source-valid cutoff at its tied phase `n~2^j/delta`.

Finite mean does rule out the most naive permanently flat defenses. For example, if every late record drop were only of order `2^{-j}` and the remaining record value were comparable to the sum of such tails, then `a_j=2^j q_j` would fail to diverge and `u_j` would fail to tend to zero. But this observation is not enough: a law can distribute its rate drops irregularly and can alternate long near-flat stretches with sharp transitions. What is needed is not merely an infinite sequence with `2^j Delta_j -> infinity`; it is a sequence satisfying the **source-clock version** (5.2) with `R` constrained by the actual conditioned cutoff at `n(j)`.

**Outcome: no theorem, no metric movement.** The conserved-budget idea identifies a promising scalar obstruction but does not synchronize it with the source cutoff.

## 6. B27 — swap the quantifiers: why the obvious diagonalization is invalid

### Attempt

The tempting argument is:

1. fix a desired lower-scale cutoff `K`;
2. use Lemma 18.31 to go far enough in `n` that missing mass above `K` is close to `delta n`;
3. after that, choose a favorable record root `j` and set `n~2^j/delta`.

Or reverse the order:

1. choose a favorable record root `j`;
2. choose a cutoff `K(j)` large enough that (5.1) is tiny;
3. invoke Lemma 18.31 at the corresponding phase.

### Exact failure

Neither swap is justified.

For a macroscopic root `2^j`, the sample size is pinned up to relative `o(1)` by

\[
n\sim 2^j/\delta.
\]

So after choosing `j`, one cannot move `n` arbitrarily far out while preserving that same macroscopic phase. Conversely, after fixing `K` and waiting until source convergence is valid, the record drops available at later roots may all be much smaller than `1/K`.

The existence of some `Omega_n -> infinity` is a diagonal conclusion of the source theorem; it supplies no law-independent rate. Therefore

\[
\text{"for every fixed cutoff, eventually"}

cannot be exchanged for

\[
\text{"at infinitely many root-tied phases, the cutoff is large enough for that root"}

without a new argument.

This is the same reason a conditional phase estimate cannot be promoted to the unconditional frozen metric.

**Outcome: the quantifier swap is invalid; no metric movement.**
