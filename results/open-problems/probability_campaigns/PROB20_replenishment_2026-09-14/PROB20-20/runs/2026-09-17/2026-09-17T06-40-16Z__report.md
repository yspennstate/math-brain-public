## 4. Mathematical idea before proof

### B2 — attack the negation first

The previous report says that a plausible escape from finite-q coalescence is a fixed law whose favorable sites are separated enough that the exact residual box costs super-polynomially in the macroscopic scale. So try exactly that.

Put favorable weights near

\[
\exp(-k^p),\qquad 0<p<1,
\]

on a sparse backbone, make the gaps grow like a positive power of k, and make all other sites much smaller. Then a target c generally cannot be replaced by one favorable coordinate plus a polynomial-cost residue. At first sight this is a plausible fixed-law full-dust mechanism.

The counterexample attempt fails in a nontrivial range. The reason is that stretched-exponential concavity creates a new energy gap: splitting a macroscopic target into two parts that are both larger than a cutoff K costs an extra amount of order \(K^p\), while the exact residual coordinate can cost as much as \(\exp(-C n^\theta)\). If K is chosen as \(n^\gamma\), the two demands are

\[
\gamma p>\theta
\tag{4.1}
\]

for coalescence to beat the reservoir, and

\[
1-\gamma>p
\tag{4.2}
\]

for the truncated-background concentration to beat the exact conditioning cost. These are simultaneously feasible precisely when

\[
\boxed{\theta<p(1-p).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### B18 — say the proof in energy language

There are three asymptotic energies, not one:

1. **conditioning energy:** a supported macroscopic landing has cost \(O(n^p)\);
2. **background-concentration energy:** excluding an O(n) error from boxes below K costs \(\exp[-\Theta(n/K)]\);
3. **fragmentation energy:** forcing two boxes above K instead of a coalesced landing costs \(\exp[-\Theta(K^p)]\), up to the reservoir price \(\exp[O(n^\theta)]\).

The cutoff must lie in the nonempty exponent window

\[
\boxed{\theta/p<\gamma<1-p.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the organizing mechanism of the theorem. It is different from the preceding polynomial exponent count and from the earlier smooth tangent/Bregman proof.

### B25 — count the two-fragment fiber exactly

Once the energy window is identified, the exact combinatorial step is a two-to-two coalescence count. Two large coordinates of total c are compared with the exact target pair

\[
(g(c),d(c)),
\qquad d(c)=c-g(c).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The map preserves total mass exactly. Counting preimages gives an unconditional factorial-moment bound under the conditioned allocation. No conditional estimate is presented as movement by itself.

## 5. New definition: matched stretched-exponential landing scheme

Fix parameters

\[
0<p<1,
\qquad
a>0,
\qquad
0\le\theta<p(1-p),
\qquad
B<\infty,
\qquad
\alpha>0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

A fixed full-support weight sequence \(w\), with \(w_0>0\), is said here to have a **matched stretched-exponential landing scheme** with parameters \((p,a,\theta,B,\alpha)\) if, for all sufficiently large integers k and c:

**Global upper envelope**

\[
w_k\le C_+e^{-a k^p}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

**Macroscopic preferred landing**

there is an integer \(g(c)\in[0,c]\) such that

\[
g(c)\ge\alpha c
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
w_{g(c)}\ge C_-e^{-a g(c)^p}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Residual reservoir**

with

\[
d(c):=c-g(c),
\tag{5.5}
\]

we have

\[
w_{d(c)}\ge C_-e^{-B c^\theta}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The same leading coefficient a in (5.2) and (5.4) is important: this theorem is about a sparse set of sites that attain the stretched-exponential envelope to leading order. Arbitrarily increasing the landing coefficient would insert an O(c^p) penalty and destroy the comparison.

Because (5.3)-(5.4) provide an unbounded subsequence whose kth roots tend to one, while (5.2) gives the matching upper root bound, the radius of convergence is one. Moreover (5.2) gives finite moments of every order. Full support implies span one, positive mean and positive finite variance after normalization.

## 6. Exact two-coordinate coalescence lemma

Let K be an integer and define

\[
R_K:=\#\{i:Y_i>K\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For c >= 2(K+1), define the two-fragment convolution

\[
A_K(c)
:=
\sum_{\substack{u+v=c\\u>K,\ v>K}}w_uw_v,
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
Q_K(m)
:=
\sup_{2(K+1)\le c\le m}
\frac{A_K(c)}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Lemma 6.1

For all sufficiently large K,

\[
\boxed{
E(R_K)_2
\le
\frac{m n}{2\alpha(K+1)}Q_K(m).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Here expectation is under the exact conditioned allocation \(B_{m,n}\).

### Proof

Expand \((R_K)_2\) over ordered pairs of distinct coordinates. Fix one such ordered pair \((i,j)\), and suppose its occupancies are u,v>K with total

\[
c=u+v.
\]

Keeping every other coordinate unchanged, replace this pair by

\[
(g(c),d(c)).
\tag{6.5}
\]

The total mass is unchanged because \(g(c)+d(c)=c\). The ratio of unnormalized configuration weights is exactly

\[
\frac{w_uw_v}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Summing (6.6) over every ordered split u+v=c with u,v>K gives the ratio in (6.3).

It remains only to bound the multiplicity of this many-to-one map. In every target image the first selected coordinate carries

\[
g(c)\ge\alpha c\ge 2\alpha(K+1).
\tag{6.7}
\]

Since the target allocation has total mass m, it has at most

\[
\frac{m}{2\alpha(K+1)}
\tag{6.8}
\]

possible indices that could serve as this first target coordinate. Once that index is chosen, there are at most n possibilities for the second ordered selected index. The target pair itself determines c from its sum. Thus every target configuration has at most

\[
\frac{mn}{2\alpha(K+1)}
\]

preimage index choices. Multiplying this deterministic fiber bound by the worst exact weight ratio proves (6.4). \(\square\)

This is a finite exact identity/inequality on the page; it does not use a local one-big-jump theorem or a pointwise record-rate heuristic.

## 7. Stretched-exponential fragmentation gap

The crucial new estimate is uniform in every two-fragment total c.

### Lemma 7.1

Under Definition 5, for all sufficiently large K and all

\[
2(K+1)\le c\le m,
\]

\[
\frac{A_K(c)}{w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}}
\le
C(c+1)
\exp\left\{-a(1-p)(K+1)^p+B c^\theta\right\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof

For every u,v>K with u+v=c, concavity of \(x\mapsto x^p\) says the minimum of \(u^p+v^p\) under the lower bounds u,v>=K+1 occurs at an endpoint. Therefore

\[
u^p+v^p
\ge
(K+1)^p+(c-K-1)^p.
\tag{7.2}
\]

By the mean-value theorem,

\[
c^p-(c-K-1)^p
\le
p(K+1)(c-K-1)^{p-1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Since \(c-K-1\ge K+1\) and p-1<0,

\[
(c-K-1)^{p-1}\le(K+1)^{p-1},
\]

so

\[
c^p-(c-K-1)^p\le p(K+1)^p.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Combining (7.2)-(7.4),

\[
\boxed{
u^p+v^p\ge c^p+(1-p)(K+1)^p.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Using the global upper envelope and summing over at most c+1 splits,

\[
A_K(c)
\le
C(c+1)
\exp\{-a c^p-a(1-p)(K+1)^p\}.
\tag{7.6}
\]

On the other hand, since \(g(c)\le c\),

\[
w_{g(c)}w_{d(c)}
\ge
C
\exp\{-a g(c)^p-Bc^\theta\}
\ge
C
\exp\{-a c^p-Bc^\theta\}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Divide (7.6) by (7.7) to obtain (7.1). \(\square\)

The gain is the **cutoff-scale energy** \((1-p)K^p\), not a polynomial tail exponent.

## 8. Conditioning denominator and recovery of the missing mass

We now show that the cutoff can be chosen so that the boxes above it carry all missing mass on the full sequence.

Choose once and for all

\[
\boxed{\frac{\theta}{p}<\gamma<1-p,}
\tag{8.1}
\]

which is possible precisely because \(\theta<p(1-p)\). Put

\[
K_n:=\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Let \(\pi_k=w_k/\Phi(1)\), let \(X_i\) be iid with law pi, and write \(S_n=\sum_iX_i\).

### Lemma 8.1 — polynomial-prefactor stretched denominator

There is C<infinity such that, for all large n,

\[
\boxed{
P(S_n=m_n)\ge n^{-1/2}e^{-C n^p}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

### Proof

Because pi has span one and finite positive variance, the lattice local central limit theorem gives integers \(r_n\) with

\[
r_n=\nu(n-2)+O(1)
\tag{8.4}
\]

and

\[
P(S_{n-2}=r_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Set

\[
c_n:=m_n-r_n.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Then

\[
c_n=\delta n+o(n),
\tag{8.7}
\]

so c_n is positive and large. Force the first two coordinates to be exactly

\[
X_1=g(c_n),
\qquad
X_2=d(c_n),
\tag{8.8}
\]

and the remaining coordinates to sum to r_n. This is an exact subset of the event \(S_n=m_n\). Hence

\[
P(S_n=m_n)
\ge
\pi_{g(c_n)}\pi_{d(c_n)}P(S_{n-2}=r_n).
\tag{8.9}
\]

Definition 5 gives

\[
\pi_{g(c_n)}\pi_{d(c_n)}
\ge
C\exp\{-a g(c_n)^p-Bc_n^\theta\}
\ge
C\exp\{-a c_n^p-Bc_n^\theta\}.
\tag{8.10}
\]

Since theta<p and c_n=O(n), (8.3) follows from (8.5), after changing C. \(\square\)

### Lemma 8.2 — the cutoff mass is asymptotically ordinary

Under the conditioned allocation,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
=\nu n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{8.11}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{8.12}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
Z_{i,n}:=X_i\mathbf 1_{\{X_i\le K_n\}}.
\tag{8.13}
\]

Then \(0\le Z_{i,n}\le K_n\),

\[
E Z_{i,n}\to\nu,
\tag{8.14}
\]

and, because pi has finite second moment,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{i,n})\le E X^2<\infty
\tag{8.15}
\]

uniformly in n. Fix epsilon>0. For large n, \(|EZ_{i,n}-\nu|<\epsilon/4\). Bernstein's inequality gives constants c_epsilon,C_epsilon>0 such that

\[
P\left(\left|\sum_iZ_{i,n}-\nu n\right|>\epsilon n\right)
\le
C_\epsilon\exp\left\{-c_\epsilon\frac{n}{K_n}\right\}.
\tag{8.16}
\]

Divide by the exact conditioning probability (8.3). Under \(S_n=m_n\), the probability of the same deviation is at most

\[
C_\epsilon n^{1/2}
\exp\left\{C n^p-c_\epsilon n^{1-\gamma}\right\}.
\tag{8.17}
\]

The choice \(\gamma<1-p\) gives

\[
1-\gamma>p,
\]

so (8.17) tends to zero. This proves (8.11). Since the total conditioned mass is \(m_n=\lambda n+o(n)\), subtracting (8.11) yields (8.12). \(\square\)

This step is unconditional in the frozen metric sense: it controls the actual conditioned allocation on the full admissible sequence.

## 9. Main theorem: super-polynomial residual costs can still force one giant

### Theorem 9.1 — stretched-reservoir one-giant theorem

Let one fixed full-support weight law satisfy Definition 5 for some

\[
0<p<1,
\qquad
0\le\theta<p(1-p).
\]

For every \(\lambda>\nu\) and every full admissible sequence \(m_n/n\to\lambda\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
\frac{Y_{(2)}}n\xrightarrow p0.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\longrightarrow\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Thus on this class the frozen metric reaches its source-side ceiling:

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

### Proof

Use K_n from (8.2). Since \(m_n=O(n)\), Lemma 7.1 gives

\[
Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
C n
\exp\{-a(1-p)K_n^p+B' n^\theta\}.
\tag{9.4}
\]

The other half of the cutoff choice, \(\gamma p>\theta\), implies

\[
\frac{n^\theta}{K_n^p}\to0.
\]

Hence there exists c>0 such that

\[
Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
C n e^{-cK_n^p}
\tag{9.5}
\]

for all large n. Apply the exact Lemma 6.1:

\[
E(R_{K_n})_2
\le
\frac{m_n n}{2\alpha(K_n+1)}Q_{K_n}(m_n)
\le
C\frac{n^3}{K_n}e^{-cK_n^p}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
P(R_{K_n}\ge2)
\le
\frac12E(R_{K_n})_2
\to0.
\tag{9.7}
\]

On the other hand, Lemma 8.2 says the total mass in boxes above K_n is

\[
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{9.8}
\]

Since delta>0, (9.8) implies \(R_{K_n}\ge1\) w.h.p. Combining with (9.7),

\[
P(R_{K_n}=1)\to1.
\tag{9.9}
\]

On this event the unique box above K_n carries all the mass in (9.8), so

\[
Y_{(1)}=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{9.10}
\]

Every other box is at most K_n=o(n), proving \(Y_{(2)}/n\to_p0\). Finally \(0\le Y_{(1)}/n\le m_n/n=O(1)\), so boundedness plus convergence in probability yields (9.2). \(\square\)

The proof is full-sequence. A chosen subsequence is not used anywhere in Theorem 9.1.

## 10. Explicit new fixed-law stress test with power-sized gaps

Choose

\[
p=\frac13.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Define an increasing sequence of preferred sites by taking a_1 sufficiently large and setting

\[
a_{j+1}
=
a_j+\left\lceil a_j^{1/6}\right\rceil,
\qquad
G:=\{a_j:j\ge1\}.
\tag{10.2}
\]

Thus the gaps tend to infinity at power scale

\[
a_{j+1}-a_j\asymp a_j^{1/6}.
\tag{10.3}
\]

Define one fixed full-support weight sequence by, say, \(w_0=w_1=1\), and for k>=2,

\[
\boxed{
w_k=
\begin{cases}
\exp(-k^{1/3}),&k\in G,\\
\exp(-k^{1/2}),&k\notin G.
\end{cases}}
\tag{10.4}
\]

This law is deliberately rough. At preferred sites it lies on the \(e^{-k^{1/3}}\) envelope; between them it is suppressed to \(e^{-k^{1/2}}\). It is not eventually represented by one smooth concave hazard and it has no polynomial lower envelope.

For all large k,

\[
w_k\le e^{-k^{1/3}},
\tag{10.5}
\]

so Definition 5 has a=1 and p=1/3. For every large c let g(c) be the largest element of G not exceeding c. Then

\[
d(c):=c-g(c)
\le Cc^{1/6},
\tag{10.6}
\]

and

\[
\frac{g(c)}c\to1.
\tag{10.7}
\]

At the landing site,

\[
w_{g(c)}=e^{-g(c)^{1/3}}.
\tag{10.8}
\]

For the residual coordinate, regardless of whether d(c) itself happens to belong to G,

\[
w_{d(c)}
\ge
\exp\{-d(c)^{1/2}\}
\ge
\exp\{-C c^{1/12}\}.
\tag{10.9}
\]

Thus Definition 5 holds with

\[
\theta=\frac1{12}.
\tag{10.10}
\]

The threshold is comfortably satisfied:

\[
\frac1{12}
<
\frac13\left(1-\frac13\right)
=
\frac29.
\tag{10.11}
\]

For example, the proof may take

\[
\gamma=\frac12,
\tag{10.12}
\]

because

\[
\gamma p=\frac16>\frac1{12},
\qquad
1-\gamma=\frac12>\frac13=p.
\tag{10.13}
\]

Theorem 9.1 therefore gives, for **every** \(\lambda>\nu\) and every full admissible \(m_n/n\to\lambda\),

\[
\boxed{
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
Y_{(2)}/n\to_p0,
\qquad
E[Y_{(1)}/n]\to\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{10.14}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
\tag{10.15}
\]

### Why this is genuinely beyond the immediately preceding theorem

The preferred-site weights themselves are stretched exponential:

\[
e^{-k^{1/3}}k^M\to0
\]

for every fixed M, so no finite polynomial landing exponent beta_+ exists. Moreover, at targets whose residual is a fixed fraction of a backbone gap, d(c) is of order \(c^{1/6}\), and even the more favorable possible residual weight is at most stretched exponential in a positive power of c. Thus the finite polynomial reservoir exponent kappa from the preceding report is unavailable.

### Why this is not the earlier smooth-hazard case

The effective hazard in (10.4) repeatedly jumps between \(k^{1/3}\) and \(k^{1/2}\) on gaps tending to infinity. It is not eventually one smooth concave function, and local ratios near transitions are highly nonuniform. The proof above needs only a matched sparse envelope plus the residual-cost exponent.

This fixed law is therefore a useful stress test against the idea that power-sized holes and super-polynomial suppression alone can produce full dust.
