## 3. B23 first: try to break persistence with rapidly growing gaps

The preceding theorem needed a bounded support ratio to get a full-sequence deterministic floor. The most direct opposite-behavior attempt is therefore to make the successive ratios diverge.

Let

\[
s_0:=1,\qquad s_r:=c_rs_{r-1},\qquad c_r\in\{2,3,\ldots\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Fix `eta>0` and

\[
u_r:=\frac1{(r+2)(\log(r+2))^{1+\eta}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Define one fixed weight sequence

\[
w_0:=1,\qquad
w_{s_r}:=\frac{u_r}{s_r}\quad(r\ge0),\qquad
w_k:=0\quad(k\ge1,\ k\notin\{s_r:r\ge0\}).
\tag{3.3}

The counterexample-first stress law is the factorial-radix choice

\[
\boxed{c_r=r+2},
\tag{3.4}

for which

\[
s_r=\frac{(r+2)!}{2}.
\tag{3.5}

\frac{s_r}{s_{r+1}}=\frac1{r+3}\to0.
\tag{3.6}

So the previous bounded-radix phase-independent floor disappears completely: between consecutive favorable scales the largest supported scale below a target may be an arbitrarily small fraction of that target.

This is exactly the geometry one would try if attempting to make the largest box vanish on badly placed phases.

## 4. Admissibility of the fixed sparse law

Let

\[
\Phi(t):=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k.
\]

Because `sum_r u_r<infinity` and `s_r>=2^r`,

\[
\Phi(1)=1+\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{u_r}{s_r}<\infty.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Also

\[
\Phi'(1)=\sum_{r\ge0}s_rw_{s_r}=\sum_{r\ge0}u_r<\infty.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\nu=\frac{\Phi'(1)}{\Phi(1)}
=\frac{\sum_r u_r}{1+\sum_r u_r/s_r}
\in(0,\infty).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since

\[
\frac1{s_r}\log w_{s_r}
=\frac{\log u_r-\log s_r}{s_r}\to0,
\]

the radius of convergence is `rho=1`. Moreover `s_0=1` is supported, so the support has span one. Thus (3.3) is a valid single fixed Janson weight law in the regime relevant to Problem 18.39 for every `lambda>nu`.

## 5. The rapid-radix phase theorem

### Theorem 5.1

Consider the fixed law (3.3). Suppose there is an infinite index set `J` such that, along `j in J`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{2^{-c_j}}{u_j}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Then for every `lambda>nu` and every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`, there exists a deterministic subsequence `n_l` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\to\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
b(w,\lambda)
:=\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]
=\lambda-\nu.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular the factorial-radix law `c_j=j+2` satisfies (5.1), because

\[
\frac{2^{-(j+2)}}{u_j}
=O\!\left(
\frac{j(\log j)^{1+\eta}}{2^j}
\right)\to0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This theorem is a **limsup/subsequence** theorem. No positive full-sequence lower floor is asserted when the radices are unbounded.

## 7. Exact post-baseline source/target ratio

Suppose for the moment that `s_j` is the top supported scale of `E_n` and that the source configuration has no actual box of size `s_j` or larger.

Repeated carrying

\[
c_{r+1}\text{ copies of }s_r
\longrightarrow
1\text{ copy of }s_{r+1}
\tag{7.1}
\]

converts the excess multiset into its mixed-radix canonical form. Since `s_j` is the top scale of `E_n`, at least one terminal `s_j` occurs. Trace the ancestry of one such terminal copy backward. This selects integers `d_r`, `r_n<=r<j`, with

\[
0\le d_r\le e_{n,r},
\qquad
\sum_{r<j}d_rs_r=s_j.
\tag{7.2}

Let

\[
D:=\sum_{r<j}d_r.
\]

Replace those `D` selected boxes by one `s_j`-box and `D-1` zero boxes. The total mass and total number of boxes are unchanged. Because each selected lower-scale count lies inside the excess above baseline, and because the top baseline will be zero on the phase used later, the excess mass `E_n` is also unchanged.

For a histogram `N`, its aggregate conditioned partition-function weight is

\[
W(N):=\frac{n!}{\prod_kN_k!}\prod_kw_k^{N_k}.
\tag{7.3}

For one source/target pair,

\[
\frac{W(\text{source})}{W(\text{target})}
\le
\frac1{\Lambda_{n,j}}
\prod_{r<j}
\left(
\frac{\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}

q_{n,r}:=
\frac{\Lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}.
\tag{7.5}

## 8. Fragmentation-tree domination without bounded radices

Define recursively

\[
T_{n,r_n}:=q_{n,r_n},
\tag{8.1}
\]

and, for `r>r_n`,

\[
T_{n,r}:=q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^{c_r}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Every supported partition of `s_j` can be represented by a mixed-radix fragmentation tree obtained by reversing carries. Since a source counted by `F_(n,j)` has no root-scale leaf, its root must split into exactly `c_j` children. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
F_{n,j}\le T_{n,j-1}^{c_j}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

The crucial point is that no upper bound on `c_r` is needed for the crude collapse.

Set

\[
\varepsilon_n:=A_{r_n}^{-1}\to0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

By (7.6), `q_(n,r)<=epsilon_n` for every `r>=r_n`. Eventually `epsilon_n<=1/4`. If `T_(n,r-1)<=2epsilon_n`, then using only `c_r>=2`,

\[
T_{n,r}
\le \varepsilon_n+(2\varepsilon_n)^{c_r}
\le \varepsilon_n+4\varepsilon_n^2
\le2\varepsilon_n.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Induction gives

\[
\boxed{
T_{n,r}\le2\varepsilon_n
\quad\text{for all }r\ge r_n.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]

The bounded-radix proof had to sharpen this crude estimate down to the tiny top activity `u_j`; that sharpening used local control of support ratios. Here we instead let the **root arity itself** be large enough to amplify (8.6).

## 9. B7 — choose a supported phase and compare the subsequential scaling limits

Let `J` be the infinite index set in Theorem 5.1. Choose numbers `theta_l downarrow0`, for instance `theta_l=1/l`.

\tag{9.1}

Choose recursively `j_l in J` so large that `j_l>j_(l-1)` and, with

n_l:=
\left\lceil
\frac{s_{j_l}}{\delta-\theta_l}

\tag{9.2}

we have `n_l>=N_l`.

On the complement of the event in (9.1),

\[
(\delta-\theta_l/4)n_l
\le E_{n_l}\le
(\delta+\theta_l/4)n_l.
\tag{9.3}

## 10. The root-arity gain beats the entire inverse fragmentation fiber

On the phase in Section 9, the top baseline is zero. Indeed,

\[
A_j\Lambda_{n_l,j}
=\frac{n_l}{s_j}A_ju_j
\sim
\frac1\delta
\frac1{(j+2)(\log(j+2))^{1+\eta/2}}
\to0.
\tag{10.1}

Thus `b_(n_l,j_l)=0` for all large `l`.

On the good phase event, if there is no actual `s_(j_l)` box, Sections 7--8 map the source histogram to a target with one such box. The aggregate inverse-fiber ratio is bounded by

\[
\frac{F_{n_l,j_l}}{\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}}
\le
\frac{T_{n_l,j_l-1}^{c_{j_l}}}{\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}}.
\tag{10.2}

By (8.6), `2epsilon_(n_l)->0`, so eventually

\[
2\varepsilon_{n_l}\le\frac12.
\tag{10.3}

Therefore

\[
\frac{F_{n_l,j_l}}{\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}}
\le
\frac{2^{-c_{j_l}}}{\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}}.
\tag{10.4}

\[
\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}
=\frac{n_l}{s_{j_l}}u_{j_l}
\sim\frac{u_{j_l}}\delta.
\tag{10.5}

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{n_l,j_l}}{\Lambda_{n_l,j_l}}\to0.

\tag{10.6}

\boxed{
P(Y_{(1)}\ge s_{j_l})\to1.

\tag{10.8}

\[
\liminf_l\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\ge\delta
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{10.9}

\[
\boxed{

\tag{10.10}

\boxed{

\tag{10.11}

## 12. B30 — isolate the exact gluing obstruction

The proof identifies a clean competition between two quantities at a favorable scale `s_j`.

After baseline renormalization, every lower-scale decomposition tree has total activity at most roughly

\[
(2\varepsilon_n)^{c_j},
\tag{12.1}
\]

while the target one-box activity is

\[
\Lambda_{n,j}\asymp u_j
\tag{12.2}
\]

on the phase `n\asymp s_j/delta`.

Thus the gluing criterion is

\[
\boxed{
(2\varepsilon_n)^{c_j}\ll u_j.
}
\tag{12.3}
\]

Because the source cutoff may grow arbitrarily slowly, this run deliberately avoids relying on any quantitative rate for `epsilon_n`. The robust sufficient condition

\[
2^{-c_j}=o(u_j)
\tag{12.4}
\]

makes (12.3) automatic once `epsilon_n<1/4`.

For the critical-log profile,

\[
\log_2\frac1{u_j}
=\log_2j+(1+\eta)\log_2\log j+O(1).
\tag{12.5}
\]

Hence a convenient sufficient condition is

\[
c_j-\log_2j-(1+\eta)\log_2\log j\to+\infty
\tag{12.6}
\]

along an infinite set of indices.

The factorial-radix law has `c_j~j`, far inside this region.

This narrows the live counterexample architecture. **Unbounded support gaps alone are not enough.** A plausible full-dust law in this sparse-chain class must keep recurring large gaps while also preventing every phase from satisfying the energy/entropy root-collapse inequality. Two genuinely live possibilities remain:

1. radices that grow only on the borderline logarithmic scale, so `2^{-c_j}` remains comparable with `u_j`;
2. violent activity cliffs with `u_j` much smaller than every lower-scale fragmentation tree, even when `c_j` is large.

Those are sharper obstructions than the preceding report's generic phrase “unbounded radix”.

### B7 — TAKE THE LIMIT / compare subsequential scaling limits: success

The huge-gap law has two sharply different geometric phases. Near the bottom of a gap, one supported scale can carry asymptotically all missing mass; near the top of the same gap, the largest lower supported scale may be a vanishing fraction. The proof rigorously exploits only the first phase and keeps the second unresolved. ### B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME / identify failure to glue: success

\]

A full-dust construction must keep this competitive on every potentially favorable macroscopic phase, while preserving finite mean. That is the next target.

## 15. Relation to neighboring condensation work
