## 4. B23 — assume the proposer is wrong: the strongest defensive-cascade architecture

Put

\[
a_r:=-\log u_r,
\qquad
q_r:=\frac{a_r}{2^r}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Then (3.2) has the exact form

\[
H_{R,j}
=
\sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
\exp\{-2^r(q_r-q_j)\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

A negative construction wants every plausible root `j` to have a lower-scale defender `r<j` with

\[
2^r(q_r-q_j)=O(1),
\tag{4.3}
\]

or, equivalently,

\[
u_r^{\,2^{j-r}}\asymp u_j.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The strongest natural version is hierarchical: when the most recent lower scale ceases to defend, jump to an older scale, and recursively arrange enough old defenders that at least one remains competitive at every record root.

The first new observation is that the **last record block cannot carry such a defense forever**.

### Proposition 4.1 — unavoidable record-block innovation

Let

\[
j_0<j_1<j_2<\cdots
\]

be the strict record-minimum indices of `q_r`, and write

\[
s_k:=q_{j_k},
\qquad
A_k:=2^{j_k}s_k=a_{j_k}=-\log u_{j_k}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Under (3.1),

\[
s_k\downarrow0,
\qquad
A_k\to\infty.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Define the weighted record drop

\[
B_{k+1}
:=2^{j_k}(s_k-s_{k+1})>0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\sup_{t>k}B_t\ge A_k/2,}
\tag{4.8}
\]

and consequently the sequence of weighted record drops is unbounded. Moreover, along any subsequence `k_l` with `B_{k_l}->infinity`, the entire immediately preceding record block contributes vanishingly to the fragmentation quantity at the new root:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=j_{k_l-1}}^{j_{k_l}-1}
\exp\{-2^r(q_r-s_{k_l})\}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

#### Proof

Because `s_k -> 0`, telescoping gives

\[
s_k
=
\sum_{t=k+1}^{\infty}(s_{t-1}-s_t).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Using (4.7),

\[
A_k
=2^{j_k}s_k
=
\sum_{t=k+1}^{\infty}
2^{j_k-j_{t-1}}B_t.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Since the record indices are strictly increasing integers,

\[
j_{t-1}\ge j_k+(t-k-1).
\tag{4.12}
\]

Hence

\[
A_k
\le
\left(\sup_{t>k}B_t\right)
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m}
=
2\sup_{t>k}B_t,
\tag{4.13}
\]

which proves (4.8). Since `A_k -> infinity`, weighted record drops are unbounded.

Now fix a record transition `k-1 -> k`. There is no new record before `j_k`, so for every

\[
j_{k-1}\le r<j_k
\]

we have

\[
q_r\ge s_{k-1}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Therefore

\[
2^r(q_r-s_k)
\ge
2^r(s_{k-1}-s_k)
=
2^{r-j_{k-1}}B_k.
\tag{4.15}
\]

Summing over the block,

\[
\sum_{r=j_{k-1}}^{j_k-1}
 e^{-2^r(q_r-s_k)}
\le
\sum_{d\ge0}e^{-2^dB_k}.
\tag{4.16}
\]

Since `2^d >= d+1`,

\[
\sum_{d\ge0}e^{-2^dB_k}
\le
\frac{e^{-B_k}}{1-e^{-B_k}},
\tag{4.17}
\]

which tends to zero whenever `B_k -> infinity`. This proves (4.9). `square`

### Interpretation

This does **not** solve Problem 18.39 or even the dyadic special case. It does, however, kill the simplest form of the selected negative architecture. A fixed source-admissible law cannot arrange for the immediately preceding record block to defend every later record root with uniformly bounded exponent. Infinitely often there is a record transition at which the whole last block becomes asymptotically irrelevant in the scale-free fragmentation sum.

Thus a genuine dust counterexample, if one exists, needs defense that jumps **strictly deeper into the past** at these innovation roots.

**B23 outcome:** the counterexample attempt is not obtained; the immediate-record defense is proved impossible infinitely often. Restricted structural progress only; frozen metric unchanged.

## 5. B29 — compactify the defensive cascade by record blocks

The previous proposition suggests quotienting the entire lower-scale profile by record blocks rather than individual scales.

For an older record block `t` and a later record root `k>t`, define the cumulative discounted drop

\[
C_{t,k}
:=
2^{j_{t-1}}(s_{t-1}-s_k).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Using the weighted drops,

\[
C_{t,k}
=
\sum_{r=t}^{k}
2^{j_{t-1}-j_{r-1}}B_r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every scale `r'` inside the record block

\[
j_{t-1}\le r'<j_t
\]

satisfies `q_{r'}>=s_{t-1}`, hence its exponent against the root `j_k` is at least

\[
2^{r'}(q_{r'}-s_k)
\ge
2^{r'-j_{t-1}}C_{t,k}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the entire block contribution to `H` is bounded by

\[
\sum_{r'=j_{t-1}}^{j_t-1}
 e^{-2^{r'}(q_{r'}-s_k)}
\le
\sum_{d\ge0}e^{-2^dC_{t,k}}
\le
\frac{e^{-C_{t,k}}}{1-e^{-C_{t,k}}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The crucial compactification identity is obtained by taking the root to infinity. Since `s_k -> 0`,

\[
\boxed{
C_{t,\infty}
:=\lim_{k\to\infty}C_{t,k}
=2^{j_{t-1}}s_{t-1}
=A_{t-1}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Hence every **fixed** defender block ultimately dies. For large `t`, its eventual exponent is huge because `A_{t-1}->infinity`.

Even more informative is the exact remaining-reserve identity

\[
\boxed{
A_{t-1}-C_{t,k}
=2^{j_{t-1}}s_k
=2^{j_{t-1}-j_k}A_k.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This says precisely what a hierarchical counterexample must do. If block `t` still defends root `k` in the sense that `C_{t,k}` is small while `A_{t-1}` is already large, then almost all of the block's total normalized drop budget must remain in the **future** beyond root `k`. Equivalently,

\[
2^{j_{t-1}-j_k}A_k
\approx A_{t-1}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Thus the current root must have an enormous `A_k`, almost at the maximal geometric amplification allowed by the gap `j_k-j_{t-1}`, while the normalized record rate `s` has barely fallen since `t-1`.

This gives a compact description of the surviving negative architecture:

1. defender blocks must move to infinity; no fixed block works forever;
2. at the unbounded innovation transitions from Proposition 4.1, the immediately previous block fails;
3. any older block that still defends must carry a large **future reserve** as in (5.6);
4. eventually the law must spend that reserve to force `s_k -> 0`, at which point that defender dies and the defense must jump deeper again.

The construction therefore needs a recursively nested sequence of plateaux and resets. A simple sparse-spike law, a single quiet barrier, or a one-generation defender is insufficient.

**B29 outcome:** no compact limiting all-defended profile was constructed. The exact state variable `(A_{t-1},C_{t,k})` reveals a future-reserve constraint that every candidate must satisfy. No unrestricted metric movement.

## 6. B19 — turn the counterexample into a recursive algorithm and locate its failure mode

The record data admit an exact recursive generation rule. Given a current state `(j_k,s_k,A_k)` with

\[
A_k=2^{j_k}s_k,
\]

choose a gap

\[
d_{k+1}:=j_{k+1}-j_k\ge1
\]

and a weighted drop

\[
0<B_{k+1}<A_k.
\]

Then

\[
s_{k+1}=s_k-B_{k+1}2^{-j_k},
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
A_{k+1}
=2^{d_{k+1}}(A_k-B_{k+1}).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

A source-admissible dyadic boundary cascade must make

\[
s_k\to0,
\qquad
A_k\to\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]

A counterexample algorithm would additionally need to assign, at every potentially useful root `k`, an older defender block `t(k)<k` for which the exact cumulative exponent `C_{t(k),k}` stays small enough that the aggregate conditioned fragmentation weight never becomes negligible.

This exposes a sharp tension:

- If the algorithm keeps all future `B` small, (4.13) forces `A_k` to stay bounded, contradicting `A_k->infinity`.
- Therefore arbitrarily large reset drops `B_k` are mandatory.
- At such a reset, Proposition 4.1 kills the whole immediately preceding record block.
- To survive, the algorithm must jump to an older defender `t`, but (5.6) then says that almost all of `A_{t-1}` must still be stored as future reserve.
- Spending enough reserve to make `s_k->0` eventually kills that older defender too.

So the surviving algorithm is not a simple recursive cascade. It needs **nested reserve transfer**: before one defender is killed, another much older block must already be positioned to take over, while all of this remains compatible with summability of `u_r`, radius one, and the exact conditioned partition function rather than only the scale-free quantity `H`.

I tried the two obvious schedules:

### Schedule A: many small drops, rare near-total resets

Long stretches with `B_k=O(1)` preserve a recent defender, while a rare `B_k` comparable with `A_{k-1}` pushes `s` downward. The reset itself has exponent of order `B_k` against the preceding block and therefore destroys that defense. Making the preceding record gap enormous does not repair this at the immediately preceding block because `B_k` is already normalized by `2^{j_{k-1}}`.

### Schedule B: use an older block across the reset

Take a block `t<k-1` so the large reset `B_k` is discounted by `2^{j_{t-1}-j_{k-1}}` in (5.2). This can indeed keep the **single reset term** small if the intervening gap is huge. But the full cumulative quantity is constrained by (5.6): doing this repeatedly while `s_k` actually tends to zero consumes the old block's entire budget `A_{t-1}`. The same old defender cannot be recycled indefinitely.

I did not find a schedule that recursively hands off defenders forever while also proving full-sequence dust in the conditioned model. Conversely, the reserve identities above are not yet a contradiction: an infinitely nested construction with defender indices tending to infinity remains logically possible.

**B19 outcome:** the algorithmic counterexample search fails at the nested-reserve handoff. The exact failure is now explicit rather than qualitative. No fixed-law counterexample is claimed.
