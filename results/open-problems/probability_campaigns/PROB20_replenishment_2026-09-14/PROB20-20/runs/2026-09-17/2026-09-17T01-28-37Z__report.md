## 2. Exact source statement and fixed-law model

Primary source checked again on 2026-09-17: Svante Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton-Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, Probability Surveys 9 (2012), arXiv:1112.0510, Section 18.6, Problem 18.39.

The source asks:

> Is `Y_(1)/n ->_p 0` possible when `0 < nu < lambda`? Example 18.36 shows this is possible for a subsequence, but Janson conjectures that it is impossible for the full sequence and, a little more strongly, that some fixed `epsilon>0` and some subsequence satisfy `Y_(1)>epsilon n` w.h.p.

The quantifiers important here are:

- one fixed weight sequence `w=(w_k)`;
- `m=m(n)` with `m/n -> lambda`;
- `0<nu<lambda`;
- the full admissible sequence is distinguished from a selected subsequence;
- Problem 18.40 immediately following is the separate `nu=0` question.

For the present theorem, rescale the fixed law to boundary radius one. Write

\[
\Phi(1)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_k,\qquad
\pi_k=\frac{w_k}{\Phi(1)},\qquad
E_\pi X=\nu,
\]

and let `X_1,X_2,...` be iid with law `pi`. Then exactly

\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\overset d=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\qquad S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Write

\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Janson's Lemma 18.31 is retained exactly: for any deterministic `K_n -> infinity`, under the conditioned allocation the mass in boxes `<=K_n` is at least `nu n+o_p(n)`, while the mass above `K_n` is at most `delta n+o_p(n)`; moreover for at least some sufficiently slowly growing cutoffs the two inequalities are asymptotic equalities. In particular

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le \delta+o_p(1),
\tag{2.3}
\]

and because `0<=Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`,

\[
\limsup_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\le\delta.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The arXiv record checked on 2026-09-17 still exposes the original v1 submission of 2011-12-02. A later-author check surfaced Janson's 2021 survey/work on tree limits, but the surfaced scope did not state a resolution of Problem 18.39. A citing-paper check surfaced Stufler's 2024 *Gibbs partitions: A comprehensive phase diagram*, which cites Janson and proves structured Gibbs-partition phase diagrams; the bounded search did not surface an arbitrary-fixed-weight theorem deciding 18.39. These are dated bounded checks, not a novelty certificate or an exhaustive proof of current open status.

## 4. B14 — find the group acting: exact q-box coalescence

The permutation symmetry of the boxes gives more than exchangeability. It lets us compare an ordered `q`-tuple of large boxes with the orbit obtained by **coalescing their mass into one coordinate and setting the other `q-1` coordinates to zero**.

Fix integers `q>=2`, `K>=0`, total `m`, and assume `w_0>0` and `w_c>0` for the totals used below. Define the interior `q`-fold convolution

\[
A_{q,K}(c)
:=
\sum_{\substack{a_1+\cdots+a_q=c\\a_i>K}}
\prod_{i=1}^q w_{a_i}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the exact coalescence defect

\[
Q_{q,K}(m)
:=
\sup_{q(K+1)\le c\le m}
\frac{A_{q,K}(c)}{w_cw_0^{q-1}}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The crucial correction relative to a naive pairwise comparison is that **the whole convolution `A_{q,K}(c)` is required**. A pointwise supremum of `w_{a_1}\cdots w_{a_q}/(w_cw_0^{q-1})` misses the multiplicity of all compositions of `c` and is therefore not a valid replacement.

### Lemma 4.1 — exact orbit identity

For `a_i>K` and `c=a_1+...+a_q`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P(Y_1=a_1,\ldots,Y_q=a_q)\\
&\quad=
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^q w_{a_i}}{w_cw_0^{q-1}}
P(Y_1=c,Y_2=\cdots=Y_q=0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Here and below probabilities are under `B_{m,n}`.

**Proof.** After the first `q` coordinates are fixed, both events leave exactly `m-c` balls for coordinates `q+1,...,n`. Summing the common remaining factor over those coordinates and dividing by the same partition function `Z(m,n)` leaves only the displayed ratio of the first `q` weight factors. No asymptotics are used. `square`

Summing (4.3) first over all compositions of `c` and then over `c` gives

\[
P(Y_1>K,\ldots,Y_q>K)
\le Q_{q,K}(m).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed the coalesced events indexed by `c` are disjoint and their total probability is at most one.

If

\[
R_{n,K}:=\#\{i:Y_i>K\},
\tag{4.5}
\]

then exchangeability gives the exact factorial-moment identity

\[
E(R_{n,K})_q
=(n)_qP(Y_1>K,\ldots,Y_q>K)
\le(n)_qQ_{q,K}(m).
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is the new deterministic/probabilistic bridge. It never compares Problem 18.39 to Problem 18.40 and never changes the weight law with `n`.

## 5. General q-coalescence criterion

### Theorem 5.1 — finite-number-of-condensates criterion

Fix a single law and an admissible sequence `m_n/n -> lambda>nu`. Suppose there are an integer `q>=2` and deterministic cutoffs `K_n->infinity` such that

\[
T_n:=\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf 1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n),
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
(n)_qQ_{q,K_n}(m_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Then

\[
P(R_{n,K_n}\ge q)\to0,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
P\!\left(
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{\delta-\epsilon}{q-1}
\right)\to1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\liminf_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac\delta{q-1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

**Proof.** Equation (4.6) and (5.2) imply `E(R_{n,K_n})_q ->0`. Since `(r)_q>=q!` whenever `r>=q`, Markov's inequality gives (5.3).

By (5.1), with probability tending to one, `T_n>=(delta-epsilon)n`. On the intersection with `R_{n,K_n}<=q-1`, the `R_{n,K_n}` boxes above the cutoff carry total mass at least `(delta-epsilon)n`; hence one of them carries at least `(delta-epsilon)n/(q-1)`. The event `R=0` is impossible on `T_n>0`. This proves (5.4). Taking expectations in (5.4) and then letting `epsilon downarrow0` yields (5.5). `square`

This criterion isolates the exact gluing obstruction: full dust requires either that every useful cutoff fails to capture the full missing mass or that the interior convolution defect remains large enough to support arbitrarily many large coordinates. No pointwise record-rate comparison is asserted.

## 7. New theorem: bounded power envelopes force full-sequence macroscopic condensation

The main gain is that Theorem 5.1 can be verified under only a two-sided polynomial envelope, without requiring an asymptotic multiplier limit.

### Theorem 7.1 — bounded power-envelope theorem

Assume one fixed full-support weight law satisfies, for some `beta>2`, constants `0<c_-<=c_+<infinity`, and all sufficiently large `k`,

\[
c_-k^{-\beta}\le w_k\le c_+k^{-\beta}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Let `w_0>0`, and let `m_n/n -> lambda>nu`, where `nu` is the boundary mean. Define

\[
q_\beta
:=
\left\lfloor\frac{\beta-1}{\beta-2}\right\rfloor+1,
\tag{7.2}
\]

which is the smallest integer strictly larger than `(beta-1)/(beta-2)`. Then for every `epsilon>0`,

\[
P\!\left(
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{\lambda-\nu-\epsilon}{q_\beta-1}
\right)\to1,
\tag{7.3}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_nE\frac{Y_{(1)}}n
\ge
\frac{\lambda-\nu}{q_\beta-1}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus the source's persistent-condensation conclusion holds here along the **full sequence**, not merely a subsequence.

If `beta>3`, then `q_beta=2`; combining (7.3) with Janson's universal upper bound gives the stronger conclusion

\[
\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\xrightarrow p\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
E\frac{Y_{(1)}}n\to\lambda-\nu.
\tag{7.5}
\]

#### Proof, Step 1: basic boundary facts

The envelope (7.1) gives radius `rho=1`, `Phi(1)<infinity`, and finite mean because `beta>2`. Full support gives span one and ensures every linear reference giant used below has positive weight. After normalization, `pi_k=w_k/Phi(1)` has the same two-sided envelope with changed constants.

Fix an integer `q>(beta-1)/(beta-2)`. Then

\[
\frac{q}{(q-1)(\beta-1)}<1.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Choose once and for all

\[
\gamma\in\left(
\frac{q}{(q-1)(\beta-1)},1
\right)
\tag{7.7}
\]

and set

\[
K_n=\lfloor n^\gamma\rfloor.
\tag{7.8}
\]

We verify both hypotheses of Theorem 5.1.

#### Step 2: a polynomial lower bound on the conditioning event

By the ordinary law of large numbers, there exists a deterministic `eta_n downarrow0` such that

\[
P\left(
|S_{n-1}-(n-1)\nu|\le\eta_n n
\right)\to1.
\tag{7.9}
\]

For all large `n`, the probability in (7.9) is at least `1/2`. The interval contains at most `2n+3` integers (we may assume `eta_n<=1`), so for some integer `r_n` in the interval,

\[
P(S_{n-1}=r_n)\ge\frac1{2(2n+3)}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Put

\[
k_n=m_n-r_n.
\tag{7.11}
\]

Since `m_n/n->lambda` and `r_n/n->nu`,

\[
k_n=\delta n+o(n).
\tag{7.12}
\]

Thus the lower half of (7.1) gives `pi_{k_n}>=C n^{-beta}` for all large `n`. Independence and the single decomposition `X_1=k_n`, `S_{n-1}=r_n` yield

\[
P(S_n=m_n)
\ge
\pi_{k_n}P(S_{n-1}=r_n)
\ge
C n^{-(\beta+1)}.
\tag{7.13}
\]

This denominator bound is only polynomial; no local one-big-jump theorem is imported.

#### Step 3: the chosen cutoff captures all missing mass

Let

\[
Z_i=X_i\mathbf1_{\{X_i\le K_n\}}.
\tag{7.14}
\]

Then `0<=Z_i<=K_n`, `E Z_i<=nu`, and

\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_i)
\le E Z_i^2
\le K_nE Z_i
\le\nu K_n.
\tag{7.15}
\]

For fixed `epsilon>0`, Bernstein's inequality gives a constant `c_epsilon>0` such that

\[
P\left(
\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i>(\nu+\epsilon)n
\right)
\le
\exp\{-c_\epsilon n/K_n\}
=
\exp\{-c_\epsilon n^{1-\gamma}(1+o(1))\}.
\tag{7.16}
\]

Conditioning and (7.13) therefore give

\[
\begin{aligned}
&P\left(
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}>(\nu+\epsilon)n
\right)\\
&\qquad\le
C^{-1}n^{\beta+1}
\exp\{-c_\epsilon n^{1-\gamma}(1+o(1))\}
\to0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.17}
\]

Janson's Lemma 18.31(i), applied to this same deterministic `K_n->infinity`, supplies the opposite inequality

\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
\ge\nu n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.18}
\]

Combining (7.17) and (7.18),

\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le K_n\}}
=\nu n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.19}
\]

Since `m_n=lambda n+o(n)`, the high-box mass is

\[
T_n
:=
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}
=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.20}
\]

This proves hypothesis (5.1) for the explicit polynomial cutoff (7.8), rather than merely invoking the existential cutoff in Lemma 18.31(ii).

#### Step 4: the q-fold convolution defect decays

For `c>=q(K+1)`, every ordered composition `a_1+...+a_q=c` has a largest coordinate at least `c/q`. Assign one of the `q` indices as a largest coordinate. By (7.1),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{A_{q,K}(c)}{w_cw_0^{q-1}}
&\le
C_q c^\beta
\sum_{\substack{a_1+\cdots+a_q=c\\a_i>K}}
\prod_{i=1}^q a_i^{-\beta}\\
&\le
C'_q
\left(\sum_{a>K}a^{-\beta}\right)^{q-1}\\
&\le
C''_q K^{-(q-1)(\beta-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.21}
\]

The second line follows because, once an index carrying a maximum is chosen, `c^beta a_j^{-beta}<=q^beta`; dropping the remaining sum constraint only enlarges the sum. The last line is the elementary tail estimate for `beta>1`.

Hence uniformly for `c<=m_n`,

\[
Q_{q,K_n}(m_n)
\le
C K_n^{-(q-1)(\beta-1)}.
\tag{7.22}
\]

Therefore

\[
(n)_qQ_{q,K_n}(m_n)
\le
C n^{q-\gamma(q-1)(\beta-1)}
\longrightarrow0
\tag{7.23}
\]

by the choice (7.7) of `gamma`. This is hypothesis (5.2).

#### Step 5: conclude

Theorem 5.1 now yields

\[
P\left(
Y_{(1)}/n\ge(\delta-\epsilon)/(q-1)
\right)\to1
\tag{7.24}
\]

for every integer `q>(beta-1)/(beta-2)`. Choosing the minimal such integer `q=q_beta` gives (7.3)-(7.4).

When `beta>3`, `(beta-1)/(beta-2)<2`, so `q_beta=2`; (7.24) says `Y_(1)/n>=delta-epsilon` w.h.p., while (2.3) gives the matching upper bound. This proves (7.5). `square`

## 8. Concrete fixed laws and metric movement

The theorem applies to fixed laws that do **not** satisfy an asymptotic equivalence `w_k~c k^{-beta}`. A simple family is

\[
w_0=1,
\qquad
w_k=c_k k^{-\beta},
\qquad
c_k=\begin{cases}
2,&k\text{ odd},\\
1,&k\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\tag{8.1}
\]

This is one fixed full-support law. The multiplier never converges, and adjacent local ratios inherit a persistent parity oscillation. Thus it is outside the prior track's retained asymptotic-equivalence and local-smooth hypotheses, even though it has the same coarse power envelope.

For this fixed law:

- if `beta=5/2`, then `(beta-1)/(beta-2)=3`, so `q_beta=4` and
  \[
  \boxed{
  \liminf_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]\ge(\lambda-\nu)/3;
  }
  \tag{8.2}
  \]
  indeed `P(Y_(1)/n >= (delta-epsilon)/3)->1` along the full sequence;
- if `beta=3`, then `q_beta=3` and the certified lower bound is `delta/2`;
- if `beta>3`, then `q_beta=2` and the optimal value is
  \[
  \boxed{b(w,\lambda)=\lambda-\nu.}
  \tag{8.3}
  \]

Thus the frozen metric genuinely moves on a fixed law class not covered by the previous report's sparse-window model or the track's earlier `w_k~c k^{-beta}` branch.

For the bounded-envelope class define

\[
\boxed{
 b_{\rm env}(w,\lambda)
 :=
 \frac{\lambda-\nu}{q_\beta-1},
 \qquad
 q_\beta=
 \left\lfloor\frac{\beta-1}{\beta-2}\right\rfloor+1,
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

with hypothesis (7.1) retained. Equation (7.4) proves this lower-bound branch. It is not asserted outside that class.

## 10. Counterexample checks against the new route

Three failure modes were tested before promoting the theorem.

1. **Pointwise pair comparison is insufficient.** A small ratio `w_aw_b/(w_{a+b}w_0)` for every split does not by itself control the probability of two large boxes, because there may be many splits. The exact convolution `A_{q,K}` is required. This is why (4.1)-(4.2), rather than a pointwise record gap, is the theorem's input.
2. **`q=2` is not always enough.** Under a `k^{-beta}` envelope, the exponent from (7.23) can be negative with `q=2` only when `beta>3`. For `2<beta<=3`, the theorem must allow more than two possible condensates; the optimal finite `q_beta` is exactly what recovers a positive macroscopic lower bound.
3. **No varying law or selected-dust subsequence is used.** The parity law (8.1) is fixed once and for all, while the conclusion (7.3) holds for every full admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`.

The exact rational B16 checker separately confirmed the finite coalescence identity on irregular weights. It is labelled a self-check and does not replace the written proof.

## 12. What this says about a genuine counterexample

A fixed-law full-dust counterexample must now defeat a more intrinsic mechanism than the earlier record-rate comparisons. It must prevent the missing mass from being captured by any cutoff at which a finite-order coalescence defect satisfies

\[
(n)_qQ_{q,K_n}(m_n)\to0
\tag{12.1}
\]

for some fixed `q`.

Equivalently, at every useful scale it must retain enough **interior convolution mass** for arbitrarily many macroscopic fragments to compete with their one-box coalescence. This is a sharper obstruction than saying that pointwise weights have small record gaps. It also explains why Janson's Example 18.36 can exhibit dust on selected scales while leaving macroscopic scales elsewhere: the relevant object is the whole convolution of supported fragment sizes, not one atom.

The natural next mathematical target is to combine (12.1) with the archived nested-cap KL / record-Gibbs bookkeeping: prove that if `Q_{q,K}` stays large for every fixed `q` across all useful scales, then the cumulative convolution entropy itself consumes more than the available conditioning budget. That implication is not proved here.
