## 3. Pinned exact conditioned-fiber checkpoint from earlier proved reports

The following facts were already proved exactly in the preceding dyadic reports and are reused unchanged.

Choose a source-valid supported cutoff by

\[
 M_n:=\min\{\Omega_n,\sqrt n\},
 \qquad
 K_n:=2^{\lfloor\log_2M_n\rfloor},
 \qquad
 R_n:=1+\lfloor\log_2M_n\rfloor.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
 R_n\to\infty,
 \qquad
 R_n\le \tfrac12\log_2n+1,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and the source lemma gives under the actual conditioned allocation

\[
 C_n:=\sum_{r\ge R_n}2^rN_{2^r}
 =\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{3.3}
\]

For any deterministic baseline counts `b_(n,r)` on a set of lower tail scales, define the corresponding excess counts

\[
 e_{n,r}:=(N_{2^r}-b_{n,r})_+.
\]

If the deterministic baseline mass

\[
 B_n:=\sum_r2^rb_{n,r}=o(n),
\tag{3.4}
\]

and scales not assigned a baseline are treated with baseline zero, then the total excess mass `E_n` satisfies

\[
 0\le C_n-E_n\le B_n,
\]

hence

\[
 E_n=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{3.5}
\]

If `P=2^j` is the top binary bit of `E_n` and a source histogram has no `P` box, exact dyadic carrying selects a submultiset of lower excess boxes summing exactly to `P`. Coalescing that submultiset to one `P` box and zeros preserves total mass, number of boxes, and `E_n` itself.

Writing

\[
 \lambda_{n,r}:=n2^{-r}u_r,
\tag{3.6}
\]

and

\[
 q_{n,r}:=\frac{\lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1},
\tag{3.7}
\]

the exact histogram factorial ratio shows that the total inverse fiber over one target is bounded by

\[
 \frac{F_{n,j}}{\lambda_{n,j}},
\tag{3.8}
\]

where

\[
 F_{n,j}
 :=\sum_{\substack{d_r\ge0,\ R_n\le r<j\\
                    \sum_rd_r2^r=2^j}}
       \prod_{r<j}q_{n,r}^{d_r}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Finally, the exact binary-tree domination gives

\[
 F_{n,j}\le T_{n,j-1}^2,
\tag{3.10}
\]

with

\[
 T_{n,R_n}:=q_{n,R_n},
 \qquad
 T_{n,r}:=q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^2.
\tag{3.11}
\]

Thus the only mathematical task left by the previous run was to force

\[
 \frac{T_{n,j-1}^2}{\lambda_{n,j}}\to0
\tag{3.12}
\]

on a legitimate macroscopic phase, without assuming a quantitative rate for `Omega_n` and without replacing the actual `lambda_(n,r)` by the scale-free `u_r`.

## 4. B11 — dualize first: useful failure and the right quantity it exposes

Before constructing a baseline, write the inverse fiber (3.9) as the coefficient of a geometric-product generating function:

\[
 F_{n,j}
 =[z^{2^j}]
 \prod_{r=R_n}^{j-1}
 \frac{1}{1-q_{n,r}z^{2^r}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

This identity is exact: expanding the `r`-th geometric factor chooses `d_r` leaves and contributes `q_(n,r)^(d_r) z^(d_r2^r)`.

At a candidate root `j`, the natural dual point is

\[
 z_j:=u_j^{-1/2^j},
\tag{4.2}
\]

because

\[
 z_j^{-2^j}=u_j.
\]

Put

\[
 s_{n,r}:=q_{n,r}u_j^{-1/2^{j-r}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If every `s_(n,r)<=1/2`, positivity of coefficients yields

\[
 \frac{F_{n,j}}{u_j}
 \le
 \prod_r(1-s_{n,r})^{-1}-1
 \le
 \exp\!\left(2\sum_rs_{n,r}\right)-1.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a clean exact dual bound, but optimizing it directly against baseline mass is too expensive. If one informally treats `q_r` as a free positive control, the two costs are

\[
 \mathcal B:=\sum_r\frac{u_r}{q_r}
\]

for protected baseline mass and

\[
 \mathcal D:=\sum_rq_r u_j^{-1/2^{j-r}}
\]

for the dual coefficient bound. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \mathcal B\mathcal D
 \ge
 \left(
  \sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
  \sqrt{\frac{u_r}{u_j^{1/2^{j-r}}}}
 \right)^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore the one-shot dual route would require the stronger square-root condition

\[
 G_{R,j}:=
 \sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
 \sqrt{\frac{u_r}{u_j^{1/2^{j-r}}}}
 \to0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

That is unnecessarily strong. Algebraically, `H:=sum h_r->0` does not imply `sum sqrt(h_r)->0`: with `L` entries each equal to `L^{-2}`, the first sum is `L^{-1}` while the second equals one. So a proof based only on the single dual evaluation (4.4) throws away the repeated-squaring structure that made the previous dyadic arguments powerful.

**B11 outcome:** useful failure. The dual calculation isolates the phase-normalized leaf quantity exactly, but a one-shot coefficient estimate pays a square-root loss. The next step must retain recursive squaring.

## 6. B30 — the adaptive root functional

For a lower tail start `R<j`, define

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{R,j}
 :=
 \sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
 \frac{u_r}{u_j^{1/2^{j-r}}}.
 }
\tag{6.1}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
 c_j:=\frac{-\log u_j}{2^j},
\]

we have

\[
 H_{R,j}
 =\sum_{r=R}^{j-1}u_r e^{2^rc_j}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus `H_(R,j)` is a truncated tail mass evaluated at the exact root-normalizing exponential tilt. It is intrinsic to the fixed law and the candidate root.

The new theorem says that `H->0` is enough to absorb the exact phase multiplier without any rate assumption on Janson's `Omega_n`.

## 7. Main theorem — adaptive-baseline conditioned transfer

### Theorem 7.1

Fix a law (2.1)--(2.2), `lambda>nu`, and an already-fixed admissible sequence `m_n/n->lambda`. Let `R_n` be the source-valid dyadic tail index (3.1).

Suppose there is a deterministic sequence of phase pairs `(n_l,j_l)` with

\[
 n_l\to\infty,
 \qquad
 P_l:=2^{j_l}=\Theta(n_l),
\tag{7.1}
\]

such that

\[
 \frac{P_l}{n_l}\to\delta,
\tag{7.2}
\]

and, after harmless thinning of the phase pairs if necessary,

\[
 \mathbb P\big(P_l\le C_{n_l}<2P_l\big)\to1,
\tag{7.3}
\]

where `C_n` is the source-valid missing mass (3.3). Assume moreover

\[
 \boxed{
 H_l:=H_{R_{n_l},j_l}\to0.
 }
\tag{7.4}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\xrightarrow p\delta
 }
\tag{7.5}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 E\!\left[\frac{Y_{(1)}}{n_l}\right]\to\delta.
 }
\tag{7.6}
\]

Consequently this fixed law has

\[
 b(w,\lambda)
 :=\limsup_nE[Y_{(1)}/n]
 =\delta=\lambda-\nu.
\tag{7.7}
\]

The conclusion is only subsequential; it does **not** assert full-sequence one-box convergence.

### Proof

Write `n=n_l`, `j=j_l`, `R=R_n`, `H=H_l`, and `P=2^j`. We may assume `H<1` and put

\[
 \varepsilon:=\sqrt H.
\tag{7.8}
\]

#### Step 1: construct recursive target thresholds backwards from the root

Set

\[
 t_{j-1}:=\varepsilon\sqrt{u_j}
\tag{7.9}
\]

and recursively for `R<=r<j-1`,

\[
 t_r:=\sqrt{t_{r+1}/2}.
\tag{7.10}
\]

Thus

\[
 t_{r-1}^2=t_r/2.
\tag{7.11}
\]

For depth `d=j-r>=1`, direct iteration gives

\[
 t_{j-d}
 =2^{-1+2^{1-d}}
  (\varepsilon\sqrt{u_j})^{2^{1-d}}.
\tag{7.12}
\]

Hence

\[
 \frac1{t_{j-d}}
 \le
 2\varepsilon^{-1}u_j^{-1/2^d},
\tag{7.13}
\]

because `0<epsilon<1` and `2^(1-d)<=1`.

#### Step 2: choose a root-tuned deterministic baseline

For `R<=r<j`, define

\[
 \boxed{
 b_{n,r}:=
 \left\lfloor\frac{2\lambda_{n,r}}{t_r}\right\rfloor.
 }
\tag{7.14}
\]

Use baseline zero at scales `r>=j`. Then

\[
 b_{n,r}+1>\frac{2\lambda_{n,r}}{t_r},
\]

so the exact post-baseline activities obey

\[
 \boxed{
 q_{n,r}
 =\frac{\lambda_{n,r}}{b_{n,r}+1}
 <\frac{t_r}{2}.
 }
\tag{7.15}
\]

This is the point at which the dangerous factor `n/2^r` is absorbed rather than ignored.

#### Step 3: the baseline costs only `o(n)` balls

By (7.14), (3.6), and (7.13),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{B_n}{n}
 &\le
 \sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
 \frac{2^r}{n}\frac{2\lambda_{n,r}}{t_r}\\
 &=2\sum_{r=R}^{j-1}\frac{u_r}{t_r}\\
 &\le
 4\varepsilon^{-1}
 \sum_{r=R}^{j-1}
 \frac{u_r}{u_j^{1/2^{j-r}}}\\
 &=4\frac{H}{\varepsilon}
 =4\sqrt H.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7.16}
\]

Thus

\[
 B_n=o(n).
\tag{7.17}
\]

By the pinned source-valid missing-mass checkpoint,

\[
 E_n=\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.18}
\]

Moreover, because the phase pairs may be thinned and `H_l->0`, condition (7.3) can be taken with a deterministic margin larger than `B_n/n`; hence

\[
 \mathbb P(P\le E_n<2P)\to1.
\tag{7.19}
\]

A short phase-selection lemma establishing this thinning from any infinite prescribed root sequence appears in Section 8.

#### Step 4: the entire exact lower-scale inverse fiber collapses

Use the exact recursion (3.11). At the bottom scale,

\[
 T_{n,R}=q_{n,R}<t_R/2<t_R.
\]

Inductively, if `T_(n,r-1)<=t_(r-1)`, then by (7.15) and (7.11),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_{n,r}
 &=q_{n,r}+T_{n,r-1}^2\\
 &\le t_r/2+t_{r-1}^2\\
 &=t_r.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7.20}
\]

Therefore

\[
 T_{n,j-1}\le t_{j-1}
 =\varepsilon\sqrt{u_j}.
\tag{7.21}
\]

The exact tree domination (3.10) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 F_{n,j}
 \le T_{n,j-1}^2
 \le\varepsilon^2u_j
 =Hu_j.
 }
\tag{7.22}
\]

At the macroscopic phase,

\[
 \lambda_{n,j}
 =\frac nP u_j,
\tag{7.23}
\]

and (7.2) gives `n/P->1/delta`. Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{F_{n,j}}{\lambda_{n,j}}
 \le
 \frac{P}{n}H
 = (\delta+o(1))H
 \to0.
 }
\tag{7.24}
\]

This is exactly the previously missing conditioned estimate (3.12).

#### Step 5: exact coalescence forces the root box

On the high-probability phase event `P<=E_n<2P`, suppose there is no box of size `P`. Because baseline is zero at and above `P`, no box larger than `P` can occur: a single such box would already contribute at least `2P>E_n`. Dyadic carrying therefore selects a submultiset of lower excess boxes with total mass exactly `P`.

Apply the pinned exact coalescence map. It creates one `P` box, preserves `E_n`, and maps all bad histograms into targets whose aggregate inverse-fiber/source weight is bounded by (7.24). Summing over targets gives

\[
 \mathbb P(Y_{(1)}<P,\ P\le E_n<2P)\to0.
\tag{7.25}
\]

Combining with (7.19),

\[
 \mathbb P(Y_{(1)}\ge P)\to1.
\tag{7.26}
\]

Since `P/n->delta`, this gives the lower bound

\[
 \liminf \frac{Y_{(1)}}n\ge\delta
\]

in probability along the phase subsequence. Janson Lemma 18.31(i), applied to any diverging cutoff, gives the matching one-sided upper bound

\[
 Y_{(1)}\le\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{7.27}
\]

Thus (7.5) follows.

Finally `Y_(1)/n<=m_n/n=O(1)`, so the sequence is uniformly bounded and convergence in probability implies convergence of expectations, proving (7.6). The universal upper bound on the limsup is `delta`; (7.6) attains it and proves (7.7). `square`

## 8. Phase selection is legitimate and does not assume a convergence rate

The theorem is deliberately phrased in terms of phase pairs. Here is the standard diagonal selection that shows no rate is hidden in (7.3).

### Lemma 8.1

Let `C_n/n->p delta>0`, and let `P_k->infinity` be any infinite deterministic sequence of candidate root masses. Then one may thin `(P_k)` and choose integers `n_l` such that

\[
 \frac{P_{k_l}}{n_l}\to\delta
\]

from below and

\[
 \mathbb P(P_{k_l}\le C_{n_l}<2P_{k_l})\to1.
\]

#### Proof

For each integer `l>=3`, convergence in probability gives an `N_l` such that for every `n>=N_l`,

\[
 \mathbb P\left(
 \left|C_n/n-\delta\right|>\frac{\delta}{4l}
 \right)<\frac1l.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Choose a still-unused root `P=P_(k_l)` so large that

\[
 n_l:=
 \left\lceil
 \frac{P}{\delta(1-1/(2l))}
 \right\rceil
 \ge N_l.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \frac P{n_l}
 =\delta(1-1/(2l))+o(1/l).
\tag{8.3}
\]

On the event complementary to (8.1), for all large `l`,

\[
 C_{n_l}/n_l
 >\delta(1-1/(4l))
 >P/n_l,
\]

so `C_(n_l)>=P`; and

\[
 C_{n_l}/n_l
 <\delta(1+1/(4l))
 <2P/n_l,
\]

so `C_(n_l)<2P`. The exceptional probability is below `1/l`. `square`

In Theorem 7.1, baseline removal changes `C_n` by at most `B_n`. Because `B_n/n<=4sqrt(H_l)->0`, thin once more so that this deterministic loss is smaller than half the lower phase margin in (8.1). Then (7.19) follows. No rate for `Omega_n` or for the probabilistic convergence in Lemma 18.31 is assumed.

## 9. A concrete fixed law where the new phase criterion works but two previous root mechanisms fail

The theorem is not merely a reformulation of the preceding quiet-barrier or regular-majorant criteria at the selected roots.

Let

\[
 j_k:=\lceil e^{k^4}\rceil,
 \qquad k\ge3,
\tag{9.1}
\]

and define one fixed positive activity sequence by

\[
 u_{j_k}:=e^{-k^2},
 \qquad
 u_{j_k-2}:=e^{-k^2/3},
\tag{9.2}
\]

while at every other index put

\[
 u_r:=e^{-r}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

The gaps are so large that the special indices are disjoint for large `k`; finitely many initial values may be fixed arbitrarily positive.

### 9.1 Source admissibility

The series `sum u_r` converges: the background is geometric, and both special series `sum exp(-k^2)` and `sum exp(-k^2/3)` converge. Hence `0<nu<infinity`.

At the roots,

\[
 \frac{-\log u_{j_k}}{2^{j_k}}
 =\frac{k^2}{2^{j_k}}\to0,
\]

so the weight radius is exactly one. The law has span one because `w_1>0` and has unbounded support.

### 9.2 No summable nonincreasing regular majorant exists

Let `v_r>=u_r` be any nonincreasing majorant. On the long interval before the defender `j_k-2`, monotonicity forces

\[
 v_r\ge v_{j_k-2}\ge e^{-k^2/3}
\]

for every `r` from just after the previous special block up to `j_k-2`. The interval has length asymptotic to `e^{k^4}`, so its contribution to `sum v_r` is at least of order

\[
 e^{k^4-k^2/3}\to\infty.
\]

Thus no summable nonincreasing majorant exists, and in particular the earlier summable bounded-ratio-majorant/contact theorem cannot be applied to this law.

### 9.3 The preceding deep-trough barrier estimate fails at the selected roots

At root `j=j_k`, the preceding barrier functional included

\[
 \zeta_k
 :=\max_{1\le d\le h}2^du_{j-d}
\]

for a growing depth `h`. Every `h>=2` sees the defender at depth two, so

\[
 \zeta_k\ge4e^{-k^2/3}.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \frac{\zeta_k^2}{u_{j_k}}
 \ge16e^{k^2/3}\to\infty.
\tag{9.4}
\]

Hence the previous quiet-barrier hypothesis fails violently at these root phases.

### 9.4 The new `H` criterion succeeds at the same roots

For a root `j=j_k`, `u_j=e^{-k^2}` and

\[
 H_{R,j}
 =\sum_{r=R}^{j-1}u_r e^{k^2/2^{j-r}}.
\tag{9.5}
\]

The source-valid index (3.2) satisfies `R_n->infinity` and, on a phase `n=Theta(2^j)`,

\[
 R_n\le j/2+O(1).
\tag{9.6}
\]

Split the sum into three pieces.

**The defender.** At `r=j-2`,

\[
 u_{j-2}e^{k^2/4}
 =e^{-k^2/3+k^2/4}
 =e^{-k^2/12}\to0.
\tag{9.7}
\]

**The near half `j/2<r<j`, excluding the defender.** For large `k`, no earlier special index lies in this range because `j_(k-1)=o(j_k)`. Thus these are background terms `u_r=e^{-r}`. Since the largest tilt factor is `e^(k^2/2)`,

\[
 \sum_{j/2<r<j,\ r\ne j-2}
 u_r e^{k^2/2^{j-r}}
 \le
 Ce^{k^2/2}e^{-j/2}
 \to0.
\tag{9.8}
\]

**The far half `R<=r<=j/2`.** Here

\[
 e^{k^2/2^{j-r}}
 \le e^{k^2/2^{j/2}}
 =1+o(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
 \sum_{R\le r\le j/2}
 u_re^{k^2/2^{j-r}}
