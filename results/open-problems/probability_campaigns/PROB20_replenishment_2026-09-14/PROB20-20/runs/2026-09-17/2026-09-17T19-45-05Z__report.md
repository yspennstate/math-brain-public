## 3. The dyadic boundary parametrization

The new deterministic theorem concerns the broad fixed-law subfamily

\[
\operatorname{supp}(w)=\{0\}\cup\{2^r:r\ge0\},
\qquad
w_0=1,
\qquad
w_{2^r}=\frac{u_r}{2^r},
\tag{3.1}
\]

with every `u_r>0`. Since `2^0=1`, the span is one and all sufficiently large total masses are admissible.

Assume

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}u_r<\infty
\tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
\liminf_{r\to\infty}
\frac{-\log u_r}{2^r}=0.
\tag{3.3}
\]

These are precisely the relevant boundary conditions after the harmless radius-one normalization.

Indeed,

\[
\Phi(1)=1+\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{u_r}{2^r}<\infty,
\qquad
\Phi'(1)=\sum_{r\ge0}u_r<\infty,
\tag{3.4}
\]

so

\[
0<\nu=\frac{\Phi'(1)}{\Phi(1)}<\infty.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Moreover

\[
\left(w_{2^r}\right)^{1/2^r}
=\exp\left(
\frac{\log u_r-r\log2}{2^r}
\right),
\tag{3.6}
\]

and the negligible term `r log 2 / 2^r` shows that (3.3) is equivalent to radius `rho=1` on this support.

Because of (3.2),

\[
u_r\to0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

No regular variation, adjacent-ratio bound, quiet barrier, comparison envelope, or prescribed rate of decay is assumed.

## 4. B2 — attack the negation first

The surviving counterexample architecture from the previous run is a **hierarchical defensive cascade**: at every favorable macroscopic root, some collection of lower scales should remain competitive enough that the exact inverse fragmentation fiber can overwhelm the root.

Before carrying the full conditioned-allocation entropy, strip the problem to the most favorable possible scale-free version of that defense.

Fix a tail starting index `R`. Define recursively

\[
T_{R,R}:=u_R,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and for `r>R`,

\[
\boxed{
T_{R,r}:=u_r+T_{R,r-1}^2.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Expanding (4.2) gives the partition function of ordered full binary decomposition trees rooted at mass `2^r` whose leaves occur only at supported scales `2^s`, `R<=s<=r`, and whose leaf at scale `s` carries activity `u_s`.

The first term `u_r` means “stop at the root.” The second term means “split into two equal children and continue independently.” Thus, if the root is forbidden, the total scale-free hierarchical fragmentation weight is at most

\[
T_{R,r-1}^2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The strongest possible defensive-cascade negation would say that for some fixed constant `M`, every sufficiently far root satisfies

\[
u_r\le M T_{R,r-1}^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The next theorem shows that (4.4) is impossible in **every sufficiently far tail** of every source-admissible dyadic boundary law.

## 5. B13/B4 — universal tail-innovation theorem

### Theorem 5.1 — no bounded defensive cascade in a dyadic boundary tail

Assume (3.2)--(3.3). Fix any constant `M>0`. Then for every sufficiently large tail start `R`, there exists an index `j>R` such that

\[
\boxed{
 u_j>M\,T_{R,j-1}^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, no matter how far out in the fixed law one starts, the scale-free hierarchical fragmentation recursion eventually encounters a root whose direct activity beats **the entire lower-scale binary fragmentation tree partition** by an arbitrarily prescribed factor.

#### Proof

Fix `M>0`. Since `u_R->0`, choose `R` so large that

\[
q:=(M+1)u_R<1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that no innovation (5.1) occurs after this tail start. Then for every `r>R`,

\[
 u_r\le M T_{R,r-1}^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Substituting into the exact recursion (4.2) gives

\[
T_{R,r}
=u_r+T_{R,r-1}^2
\le(M+1)T_{R,r-1}^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

I claim that for every integer `t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
T_{R,R+t}
\le
\frac{1}{M+1}\,q^{2^t}.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

For `t=0`, the right side is

\[
\frac{q}{M+1}=u_R=T_{R,R}.
\]

If (5.5) holds at `t`, then (5.4) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{R,R+t+1}
&\le(M+1)
\left[
\frac{1}{M+1}q^{2^t}
\right]^2\\
&=
\frac{1}{M+1}q^{2^{t+1}},
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves the induction.

Since the direct root term is one of the nonnegative terms in (4.2),

\[
 u_{R+t}\le T_{R,R+t}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.5)--(5.6),

\[
 -\log u_{R+t}
\ge
2^t\log\frac1q+\log(M+1).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Divide by `2^(R+t)`:

\[
\frac{-\log u_{R+t}}{2^{R+t}}
\ge
\frac{1}{2^R}\log\frac1q
+
\frac{\log(M+1)}{2^{R+t}}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

The first term on the right is a **strictly positive constant** depending on the fixed tail start `R`. Hence

\[
\liminf_{r\to\infty}
\frac{-\log u_r}{2^r}
\ge
\frac{1}{2^R}\log\frac1q
>0,
\tag{5.9}
\]

contradicting the radius-one boundary condition (3.3).

Therefore (5.3) cannot hold for all `r>R`, proving (5.1). `square`

### Corollary 5.2 — arbitrarily large innovation factors occur in arbitrarily far tails

There exist increasing tail starts `R_l->infinity` and roots `j_l>R_l` such that

\[
\boxed{
\frac{u_{j_l}}{T_{R_l,j_l-1}^2}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

#### Proof

Apply Theorem 5.1 with `M=l`, choose `R_l` larger than `j_(l-1)`, and select a corresponding root `j_l`. `square`

This is the main new result of the run.

It is stronger than merely finding a root that beats two equal halves: `T_(R,j-1)^2` already sums every ordered hierarchical binary decomposition using every supported scale from `R` through `j-1`.

## 6. A simpler local corollary: two-half innovation is unbounded

Put

\[
a_r:=-\log u_r.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define the immediate split gain

\[
g_r:=2a_{r-1}-a_r
=\log\frac{u_r}{u_{r-1}^2}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Corollary 6.1

Under (3.2)--(3.3),

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{r\to\infty}g_r=+\infty.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{r\to\infty}
\frac{u_r}{u_{r-1}^2}=+\infty.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Direct proof

Suppose instead that `g_r<=C` eventually. Then

\[
a_r\ge2a_{r-1}-C.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Since `u_r->0`, we have `a_r->infinity`; choose `R` with `a_R>C`. Iterating (6.5),

\[
a_{R+t}
\ge
C+2^t(a_R-C).
\tag{6.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{a_{R+t}}{2^{R+t}}
\ge
\frac{a_R-C}{2^R}+o(1)>0,
\tag{6.7}
\]

again contradicting (3.3). `square`

Corollary 6.1 is the one-generation shadow of Theorem 5.1. The theorem is more useful because it rules out a defense built from an arbitrary hierarchy of binary lower-scale splits, not only the first split.

## 7. What the theorem says about the attempted opposite behavior

The counterexample-first conclusion is now exact at the **scale-free energy** level.

A fixed dyadic source-admissible law cannot maintain the following strategy in every far tail:

> “Whenever a direct macroscopic root is favorable, compensate by arranging enough lower-scale activity that the total hierarchical fragmentation tree has comparable weight.”

For every prescribed comparison factor `M`, every sufficiently far tail eventually contains a root where that entire strategy loses by more than `M`.

This is independent of:

- regular variation;
- bounded adjacent activity ratios;
- a summable regular majorant;
- a quiet interval of any prescribed length;
- a quantitative decay rate for `u_r`;
- a fixed choice of a special explicit law.

So the surviving “hierarchical defensive spike cascade” must rely on something **outside** the scale-free energy recursion.

The only such ingredient still present in the exact conditioned allocation is the phase-dependent placement/activity factor described next.

## 8. Counterexample check before any transfer: why this does NOT yet move `b(w,lambda)`

It would be tempting, but incorrect, to promote Theorem 5.1 directly into persistent condensation. The earlier exact histogram calculations show precisely why that transfer is not automatic.

At a phase with a candidate macroscopic root `P=2^j=Theta(n)`, the un-baselined placement activity at a lower dyadic scale is

\[
\lambda_{n,r}
=rac{n w_{2^r}}{w_0}
=n2^{-r}u_r.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Since `n/2^j=Theta(1)`,

\[
\lambda_{n,r}
=\Theta(2^{j-r}u_r).
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the exact inverse fiber does **not** use the scale-free activity `u_r`. It receives an extra factor of order `2^(j-r)` for each selected lower-scale box.

The baseline machinery from the earlier proved reports replaces (8.1) by

\[
 q_{n,r}
=\frac{\lambda_{n,r}}
        {\lfloor A_r\lambda_{n,r}\rfloor+1}
\le
\min\{\lambda_{n,r},A_r^{-1}\},
\tag{8.3}
\]

where one may choose `A_r->infinity` with

\[
\sum_rA_ru_r<\infty.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For the actual conditioned inverse fiber, the exact binary-tree upper recursion is therefore

\[
T^{\mathrm{cond}}_{n,r}
=q_{n,r}+
\left(T^{\mathrm{cond}}_{n,r-1}\right)^2,
\tag{8.5}
\]

not the scale-free recursion (4.2).

The two recursions can differ drastically because

\[
\frac{\lambda_{n,r}}{u_r}
=\frac{n}{2^r}
=\Theta(2^{j-r}).
\tag{8.6}
\]

This factor is harmless at any fixed depth below the root but is unbounded when the relevant defensive fragmentation depth grows with `j`.

Therefore a proof that silently substitutes `u_r` for `q_(n,r)` would repeat exactly the kind of conditional/unconditional or rate-transfer error this track is instructed to avoid.

### 8.1 The second clock: the source-valid cutoff

There is another synchronization issue. Janson supplies a cutoff `Omega_n->infinity`, but no rate. If `R_n` is the dyadic support index corresponding to a source-valid cutoff, then

\[
R_n\to\infty
\]

may happen arbitrarily slowly compared with a phase root `j~log_2 n`.

Theorem 5.1 says that **for every fixed far tail start `R`** a scale-free innovation root eventually appears. To turn that into a conditioned theorem one has to align three objects simultaneously:

1. a phase root `j` with `2^j=Theta(n)`;
2. a source-valid or independently justified lower cutoff `R_n`;
3. an innovation root strong enough for the phase-dependent recursion (8.5), not merely (4.2).

That three-way synchronization is not proved here.

Hence the unconditional metric does not move.

## 9. B13 interpretation: a normalized exponent monovariant

The proof of Theorem 5.1 can be read as a monovariant statement.

Under the hypothetical bounded-defense condition

\[
u_r\le M T_{R,r-1}^2,
\]

set

\[
Q_r:=(M+1)T_{R,r}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Then

\[
Q_r\le Q_{r-1}^2.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Once `Q_R<1`, the quantity

\[
-2^{-r}\log Q_r
\tag{9.3}
\]

is bounded below by a fixed positive constant. In other words, a permanently successful defensive hierarchy forces a positive normalized exponential decay rate.

But radius one requires normalized rate zero along a subsequence. Therefore the defense must break somewhere.

This is the cleanest conceptual form of the new obstruction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{radius-one recurrence}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{unbounded tail innovation.}
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
