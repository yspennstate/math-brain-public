# 3. Truncated saddle notation

Let \(U\) be a positive integer and define the truncated exponential sum
\[
\Phi_U(t):=\sum_{k\le U}\pi_ke^{tk}.
\]
For a target mean \(\mu\) in the interior of the convex hull of the support below \(U\), let \(t=t(U,\mu)\) solve
\[
\frac{\sum_{k\le U}k\pi_ke^{tk}}{\Phi_U(t)}=\mu.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Define the tilted truncated law
\[
p_{U,t}(k):=rac{\pi_ke^{tk}}{\Phi_U(t)}\mathbf1_{\{k\le U\}}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Let
\[
I_U(\mu):=\mu t-\log\Phi_U(t).
\tag{3.3}
\]
If \(X_{U,t}\sim p_{U,t}\), write
\[
\sigma_{U,t}^2:=\operatorname{Var}(X_{U,t}).
\]

Whenever \(m=n\mu\) is an admissible lattice point, exact exponential tilting gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_n=m,\ \max_i\xi_i\le U)
=
\exp[-nI_U(\mu)]\,
\Pr_{U,t}(X_1+\cdots+X_n=m).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
No approximation appears in (3.4).

# 4. Fixed finite smoothing template

Let \(d\) be the lattice span of \(\pi\). Choose once and for all a finite set
\[
F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_s\}\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi)
\]
whose differences generate \(d\), with at least two points. Choose a probability vector \(\rho\) on \(F\) with every coordinate strictly positive.

Because all saddle parameters used below tend to zero and \(\Phi_U(t)\to1\), there is a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(U\),
\[
p_{U,t}(f)\ge \varepsilon\rho(f)
\qquad(f\in F).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Thus every moving tilted law admits the mixture decomposition
\[
X_{U,t}\overset d=
\begin{cases}
Z,&I=1,\\
W,&I=0,
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]
where

- \(I\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)\);
- \(Z\sim\rho\), fixed once and for all;
- \(W\) is a residual law depending on \(U,t\);
- \(I,Z,W\) are coupled in the standard mixture way.

The mean of \(W\) stays uniformly bounded whenever the target mean \(\mu\) stays in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\). Moreover
\[
\operatorname{Var}(W)\le C_F(1+\sigma_{U,t}^2).
\tag{4.3}
\]
Indeed the second moment of \(W\) is bounded by the second moment of \(X_{U,t}\) divided by \(1-\varepsilon\), while all fixed-template moments are constants.

# 5. Finite-template local moderate-deviation lemma

## Lemma 5.1

Let \(Z_1,Z_2,\ldots\) be iid with the fixed finite-support law \(\rho\), mean \(\mu_F\), and lattice span \(d\). There exist constants \(c,C,\eta>0\), depending only on \(\rho\), such that for every sufficiently large \(N\) and every attainable lattice point \(z\) satisfying
\[
|z-N\mu_F|\le\eta N,
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(Z_1+\cdots+Z_N=z)
\ge
cN^{-C}
\exp\left[-C\frac{(z-N\mu_F)^2}{N}\right].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Proof

This can be proved directly by a finite-dimensional method of types, so no moving local limit theorem is invoked.

For a target mean \(z/N\) within \(\eta\) of \(\mu_F\), perturb the probability vector \(\rho\) inside the finite simplex to a vector \(r_z\) satisfying
\[
\sum_f r_z(f)=1,
\qquad
\sum_f f\,r_z(f)=z/N,
\]
and
\[
\|r_z-\rho\|=O(|z/N-\mu_F|).
\]
For \(\eta\) small all coordinates remain bounded below. Since the integer difference lattice generated by \(F\) is \(d\mathbb Z\), standard bounded lattice rounding gives integer counts \(N_f\) with
\[
\sum_fN_f=N,
\qquad
\sum_ffN_f=z,
\qquad
N_f=Nr_z(f)+O_F(1).
\]
The probability of this single multinomial type is
\[
\frac{N!}{\prod_fN_f!}\prod_f\rho(f)^{N_f}.
\]
Stirling's formula gives
\[
\Pr(\text{that type})
\ge
cN^{-C}\exp[-N D(\widehat r_z\Vert\rho)].
\]
Since relative entropy is quadratically bounded in a compact interior neighborhood of \(\rho\),
\[
D(\widehat r_z\Vert\rho)
\le
C|z/N-\mu_F|^2+O(N^{-2}),
\]
which yields (5.1). \(\square\)

# 6. Main new theorem: variance-controlled central local lower bound

## Theorem 6.1

Let \(U_n\to\infty\) with \(U_n=o(n)\). Let \(\mu_n\) remain in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\), and let \(t_n=t(U_n,\mu_n)\) solve the truncated saddle equation (3.1). Assume
\[
t_n\to0
\]
and that \(m_n=n\mu_n\) is an admissible lattice point. Put
\[
\sigma_n^2:=\operatorname{Var}_{U_n,t_n}(X).
\]
Then there are constants \(c,C>0\), depending only on the original law through the fixed smoothing template and on the compact mean range, such that
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{U_n,t_n}(X_1+\cdots+X_n=m_n)
\ge
c n^{-C}\exp[-C(1+\sigma_n^2)].
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr_{U_n,t_n}(S_n=m_n)
\le
C\sigma_n^2+C\log n+C.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Use the mixture representation (4.2) independently in each coordinate. Let
\[
N:=\#\{i:I_i=1\}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\varepsilon).
\]
Choose
\[
N_0=\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor
\]
with the correct parity/residue adjustment if necessary; Stirling gives
\[
\Pr(N=N_0)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Conditional on \(N=N_0\), write
\[
A:=Z_1+\cdots+Z_{N_0},
\qquad
B:=W_1+\cdots+W_{n-N_0}.
\]
The target mean identity for the mixture implies
\[
m_n-\mathbb E B-N_0\mu_F=O(1).
\tag{6.4}
\]
By (4.3),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(B)\le Cn(1+\sigma_n^2).
\]
Hence, for a sufficiently large fixed constant \(K\), Chebyshev gives an event \(G_n\) with conditional probability at least \(3/4\) on which
\[
|B-\mathbb EB|
\le
K\sqrt{n(1+\sigma_n^2)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Because \(X\in[0,U_n]\) and \(\mathbb EX=\mu_n=O(1)\),
\[
\sigma_n^2\le \mathbb EX^2\le U_n\mathbb EX=O(U_n)=o(n).
\tag{6.6}
\]
Therefore the right side of (6.5) is \(o(n)\). For every value of \(B\) on \(G_n\), the required template sum
\[
z:=m_n-B
\]
satisfies
\[
|z-N_0\mu_F|
\le
C\sqrt{n(1+\sigma_n^2)}
=o(n).
\]
The global lattice-span condition guarantees that this target has the correct residue class; for large \(n\) it lies in the interior range of the \(N_0\)-fold template sum.

Lemma 5.1 therefore gives, uniformly for all such \(B\),
\[
\Pr(A=m_n-B\mid B,N=N_0)
\ge
c n^{-C}
\exp[-C(1+\sigma_n^2)].
\]
Average over \(B\in G_n\), multiply by (6.3), and absorb the additional \(n^{-1/2}\) into the polynomial factor. This proves (6.1). \(\square\)

### Why this matters

The exact local coefficient at the moving saddle is no longer an unrestricted \(e^{-o(n)}\) black box. Its entire possible logarithmic loss is controlled by
\[
O(\sigma_n^2+\log n).
\]
This is proved without a local CLT for the triangular array.

# 7. Application to a hypothetical full-sequence counterexample

Assume for contradiction analysis that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Then by a standard diagonal quantile choice there is a deterministic sequence
\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\]
such that
\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let \(\lambda_n=m_n/n\), and let \(t_n>0\) solve
\[
\frac{\sum_{k\le U_n}k\pi_ke^{t_nk}}{\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)}=\lambda_n.
\tag{7.3}
\]
For all large \(n\) this solution exists because the truncated support extends above \(\lambda_n\).

The universal boundary-layer argument gives
\[
\boxed{
t_n\to0,\qquad U_nt_n\to\infty,\qquad
I_{U_n}(\lambda_n)\sim(\lambda-\nu)t_n.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
For completeness:

- fixed positive \(t\) makes the truncated tilted mean diverge as \(U\to\infty\), so \(t_n\to0\);
- if \(U_nt_n\) stayed bounded, dominated convergence would force the tilted mean back to \(\nu\), contradicting \(\lambda>\nu\);
- the saddle identity and a fixed-level split give \(\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)\to1\);
- every fixed fractional tilt returns to mean \(\nu\), and integrating \(\lambda_n-\Psi_{U_n}(s)\) over \([0,t_n]\) yields the rate asymptotic.

Define the central tilted atom
\[
a_n:=\Pr_{U_n,t_n}(S_n=m_n).
\]
By exact tilting,
\[
\Pr(S_n=m_n,\max_i\xi_i\le U_n)
=e^{-nI_{U_n}(\lambda_n)}a_n.
\tag{7.5}
\]
The left side equals
\[
p_n(1-o(1))
\]
by (7.2). Hence
\[
\boxed{
b_n
=
nI_{U_n}(\lambda_n)-\log a_n+o(1).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
Since \(a_n\le1\),
\[
\boxed{
nI_{U_n}(\lambda_n)\le b_n+o(1).}
\tag{7.7}
\]
Theorem 6.1 gives the complementary upper control
\[
\boxed{
0\le
b_n-nI_{U_n}(\lambda_n)
\le
C\sigma_n^2+C\log n+C+o(1),
}
\tag{7.8}
\]
where
\[
\sigma_n^2=\operatorname{Var}_{U_n,t_n}(X).
\]
Thus any full counterexample necessarily admits a sublinear truncation for which its exact conditioning cost decomposes as
\[
\boxed{
\text{conditioning cost}
=
\text{canonical truncated Cramer cost}
+
\text{central local cost},
}
\]
with the central local cost bounded by \(O(\sigma_n^2+\log n)\).

This is a significantly sharper replacement for the previous generic statement that the central factor is only \(e^{-o(n)}\).

# 8. Canonical mesocondensation and variance scale

The same saddle yields another useful universal fact. For every fixed \(A>0\), dominated convergence on the range \(k\le A/t_n\) gives
\[
\sum_{k\le A/t_n}k\,p_{U_n,t_n}(k)\to\nu.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Since the total mean is \(\lambda_n\to\lambda\), the tilted first moment above \(A/t_n\) tends to \(\lambda-\nu\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_{U_n,t_n}[X^2]
\ge
\frac{A}{t_n}
\mathbb E_{U_n,t_n}[X;X>A/t_n].
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(A\),
\[
\liminf t_n\sigma_n^2\ge A(\lambda-\nu),
\]
and since \(A\) is arbitrary,
\[
\boxed{t_n\sigma_n^2\to\infty.}
\tag{8.2}
\]
Combining (7.4), (7.7), and (8.2) gives
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_n^2\gg \frac{n}{b_n}.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
Thus the moving canonical law required by any no-condensation counterexample itself has a diverging mesoscopic variance scale strictly above \(n/b_n\).

The local-factor theorem and (8.3) isolate the remaining obstruction very sharply: the exact arithmetic loss is not arbitrary, but it may still be as large as a constant multiple of a canonical variance that is itself forced to diverge faster than \(n/b_n\).

# 9. A useful dichotomy

From (7.6)–(7.8), every hypothetical full counterexample falls into at least one of the following two asymptotic mechanisms along every subsequence:

1. **Canonical-cost regime.** If
   \[
   \sigma_n^2+\log n=o(b_n),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \boxed{
   b_n\sim nI_{U_n}(\lambda_n)
   \sim(\lambda-\nu)nt_n.
   }
   \tag{9.1}
   \]
   The conditioning cost is asymptotically explained by the moving truncated canonical saddle.

2. **Variance/local regime.** Otherwise
   \[
   \boxed{
   b_n=O(\sigma_n^2+\log n)
   }
   \tag{9.2}
   \]
   along a further subsequence. Then the canonical variance itself is at least of the conditioning-cost scale (up to the logarithmic alternative).

This dichotomy is not yet a contradiction, but it eliminates an unstructured third possibility in which the central lattice coefficient is super-exponentially bad relative to every natural canonical scale.
