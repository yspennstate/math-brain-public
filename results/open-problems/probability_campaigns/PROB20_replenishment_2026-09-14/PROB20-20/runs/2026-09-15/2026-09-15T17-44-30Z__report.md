## 1. Exact target and source quantifiers

For one fixed weight sequence \(w=(w_k)\), with
\[
\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k,
\qquad
\nu=\lim_{t\uparrow\rho}\frac{t\Phi'(t)}{\Phi(t)},
\]
Janson's Problem 18.39 asks whether, when
\[
0<\nu<\lambda,
\qquad
m_n/n\to\lambda,
\]
one can have
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\]
along the **full admissible sequence**. Example 18.36 gives this only on a subsequence. Janson conjectures that it is impossible for the full sequence and, more strongly, that some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and some subsequence satisfy
\[
Y_{(1)}\ge\varepsilon n
\quad\text{w.h.p.}
\]
Primary source: S. Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, arXiv:1112.0510, Problem 18.39, PDF p. 122.

The source was re-read directly this run, including a page-image check of p. 122. A fresh bounded web search found no exact later published resolution of Problem 18.39. Standard big-jump literature found in the search assumes subexponential/local-subexponential or related regularity and therefore does not cover the arbitrary irregular radius-one law allowed here.

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid,
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu,
\]
and the allocation is
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\), \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\), and the normalized generating radius is one.

Let
\[
p_n:=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=-\log p_n.
\]
The weak law plus Janson's Theorem 17.1 give
\[
\boxed{b_n\to\infty,\qquad b_n=o(n).}
\tag{1.1}
\]

# 4. New theorem: one-ball Palm change of measure has an exact KL cost

Define the product boundary measure
\[
P_n(x):=\prod_{i=1}^n\pi_{x_i}
\]
on \(x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n\), and the conditioned law
\[
Q_n(x):=P_n(x\mid S_n=m_n).
\]

Augment each measure by a distinguished box index selected by a uniformly chosen ball.

Under the unconditioned product measure define
\[
\widetilde P_n(x,i)
:=
P_n(x)\frac{x_i}{n\nu}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
This is a probability measure because \(E_{P_n}S_n=n\nu\).

Under the conditioned measure define
\[
\widetilde Q_n(x,i)
:=
Q_n(x)\frac{x_i}{m_n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is a probability measure because \(S_n=m_n\) under \(Q_n\).

## Theorem 4.1 — exact one-ball Palm KL identity

\[
\boxed{
D(\widetilde Q_n\Vert\widetilde P_n)
=
b_n+\log\frac{n\nu}{m_n}
=
b_n-\log\frac{m_n/n}{\nu}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
On the support of \(\widetilde Q_n\), namely \(S_n=m_n\),
\[
\frac{d\widetilde Q_n}{d\widetilde P_n}(x,i)
=
\frac{P_n(x)p_n^{-1}\,x_i/m_n}{P_n(x)x_i/(n\nu)}
=
\frac{n\nu}{m_np_n},
\]
which is constant. Taking its logarithm proves (4.3). \(\square\)

Let \(B_n\) be the occupancy of the box containing a uniformly sampled ball under \(Q_n\). Under \(\widetilde P_n\), the corresponding occupancy has the ordinary size-biased boundary law
\[
\pi_k^*:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},\qquad k\ge1.
\]
By data processing,
\[
\boxed{
D(\mathcal L(B_n)\Vert\pi^*)
\le
b_n+\log\frac{n\nu}{m_n}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is an exact information budget for the distribution seen by a uniformly sampled ball. It requires no local limit theorem and no tail regularity.

# 5. Universal upper bound on conditioned tail mass from the fixed boundary tail

Write the size-biased boundary tail
\[
q(L):=\pi^*((L,\infty))
=
\frac1\nu\sum_{k>L}k\pi_k.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
p_n(L):=\Pr(B_n>L)
=
\frac1{m_n}\,E_{Q_n}\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L\}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Applying data processing in (4.4) to the binary event \(\{B_n>L\}\) gives
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p_n(L)\Vert q(L))
\le
b_n+\log\frac{n\nu}{m_n}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
For \(0<q<1\),
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p\Vert q)
\ge
p\log\frac1q-\log2,
\tag{5.4}
\]
because the binary entropy is at most \(\log2\) and the \((1-p)\log(1/(1-q))\) term is nonnegative.

Therefore:

## Corollary 5.1 — Palm tail-mass bound

For every deterministic cutoff \(L\),
\[
\boxed{
p_n(L)
\le
\frac{b_n+\log(n\nu/m_n)+\log2}
{\log(1/q(L))}
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
whenever \(0<q(L)<1\). If \(q(L)=0\), then necessarily \(p_n(L)=0\).

Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_n}\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L\}}
\le
m_n\,
\frac{b_n+O(1)}{\log(\nu/Q(L))},
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
where
\[
Q(L):=\sum_{k>L}k\pi_k.
\]

At the amplified cutoff \(L_n\), (3.2) implies
\[
p_n(L_n)\to\frac\delta\lambda.
\]
Hence (5.3) gives the necessary fixed-law tail constraint
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\nu}{Q(L_n)}
\le
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\delta}+o(1)\right)b_n.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
This holds unconditionally for the amplified scale constructed in the previous checkpoint.

# 6. New theorem: exact r-box Palm KL identity

Fix an integer \(r\le n\). Under the product law define the ordered-distinct-box Palm measure
\[
\widetilde P_{n,r}(x;i_1,\ldots,i_r)
:=
P_n(x)
\frac{\prod_{j=1}^r x_{i_j}}{(n)_r\nu^r},
\tag{6.1}
\]
where \(i_1,\ldots,i_r\) are distinct. This is normalized because under the product law
\[
E\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r\ \rm distinct}
\prod_jX_{i_j}
=(n)_r\nu^r.
\]
Under \(\widetilde P_{n,r}\), the selected occupancies are iid with law \(\pi^*\).

Let \(D_{n,r}\) denote, as in the previous checkpoint, the probability that \(r\) uniformly sampled labeled balls from the conditioned allocation lie in \(r\) distinct boxes. Define the conditioned ordered-distinct-box Palm measure
\[
\widetilde Q_{n,r}(x;i_1,\ldots,i_r)
:=
Q_n(x)
\frac{\prod_{j=1}^r x_{i_j}}{(m_n)_rD_{n,r}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — exact r-Palm KL formula

\[
\boxed{
D(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\Vert\widetilde P_{n,r})
=
b_n+\log\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_r}-\log D_{n,r}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof
Again the density ratio is constant on \(S_n=m_n\):
\[
\frac{d\widetilde Q_{n,r}}{d\widetilde P_{n,r}}
=
\frac1{p_n}
\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_rD_{n,r}}.
\]
Taking logs proves (6.3). \(\square\)

Let
\[
A_{n,r}:=
\Pr\left(
S_{n-r}+\xi_1^*+\cdots+\xi_r^*=m_n
\right),
\tag{6.4}
\]
where the \(\xi_j^*\) are iid \(\pi^*\) and independent of \(S_{n-r}\). The preceding checkpoint proved the exact identity
\[
D_{n,r}
=
\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_r}
\frac{A_{n,r}}{p_n}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Substituting (6.5) into (6.3) gives the particularly clean new interpretation
\[
\boxed{
D(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\Vert\widetilde P_{n,r})
=-\log A_{n,r}.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Thus the exact growing-order size-biased convolution coefficient is itself the exponential of a Palm relative entropy.

### Independent universal distinctness bound

Since relative entropy is nonnegative, (6.3) also gives
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,r}
\le
\exp(b_n)\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_r}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
If \(r_n=c b_n\) with fixed
\[
c>1/\log(\lambda/\nu),
\]
then \(r_n=o(n)\) and
\[
\log\frac{(n)_{r_n}\nu^{r_n}}{(m_n)_{r_n}}
=
-r_n\log(\lambda/\nu)+o(b_n),
\]
so (6.7) gives \(D_{n,r_n}\to0\). This is weaker than the amplified-cloud birthday bound from the previous checkpoint, but it is an independent exact consequence of the Palm change of measure.

# 7. New theorem: under full no-condensation the size-biased tail must be subexponential on the b-scale even at an amplified cutoff

Assume now, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\tag{7.1}
\]
along the full admissible sequence.

For every fixed \(r\), pair-union bounds imply
\[
D_{n,r}\to1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Indeed the collision probability of a fixed pair is at most a constant times \(E(Y_{(1)}/n)\to0\).

Let
\[
q_n:=q(L_n)=\frac{Q(L_n)}{\nu}.
\]
Because \(L_n\to\infty\), \(q_n\to0\). By (3.2), a uniformly sampled ball lies in a box above \(L_n\) with asymptotic probability
\[
\frac\delta\lambda.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Conditioning on the event that a fixed number \(r\) of sampled balls occupy distinct boxes changes any one selected-ball marginal by \(o(1)\), because the conditioning event has probability \(D_{n,r}\to1\). Thus under \(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\), every selected occupancy has
\[
\Pr(K_j>L_n)
=
\frac\delta\lambda+o(1).
\tag{7.4}
\]

Under \(\widetilde P_{n,r}\), the selected occupancies are iid \(\pi^*\), and hence each corresponding tail probability is \(q_n\). Data process (6.6) to the selected occupancy vector. Relative to the product reference \((\pi^*)^{\otimes r}\), the KL chain rule gives
\[
D(\mathcal L_{\widetilde Q}(K_1,\ldots,K_r)
\Vert(\pi^*)^{\otimes r})
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^r
D(\mathcal L_{\widetilde Q}(K_j)\Vert\pi^*),
\tag{7.5}
\]
because the difference is the nonnegative mutual information of the selected occupancies. Applying binary data processing to each coordinate and (7.4),
\[
-\log A_{n,r}
\ge
r\left(\frac\delta\lambda+o(1)\right)
\log\frac1{q_n}-O(r).
\tag{7.6}
\]

On the other hand, for fixed \(r\), (6.5), (7.2), and \(m_n/n\to\lambda\) give
\[
A_{n,r}
=
\left(\frac\lambda\nu\right)^r
p_n(1+o(1)),
\]
so
\[
-\log A_{n,r}
=
b_n-r\log(\lambda/\nu)+o(1).
\tag{7.7}
\]
Combining (7.6) and (7.7) yields, for every fixed integer \(r\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\frac{b_n}{\log(1/q_n)}
\ge
r\frac\delta\lambda.
\]
Since \(r\) is arbitrary,
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\nu}{Q(L_n)}=o(b_n)
\quad\text{under any full-sequence no-condensation counterexample.}
}
\tag{7.8}
\]

This strengthens the unconditional \(O(b_n)\) Palm constraint (5.7) to a strict \(o(b_n)\) requirement under the hypothesized counterexample, and it holds at the **amplified** scale
\[
L_n=R_n n/b_n,
\qquad R_n\to\infty.
\]

# 8. Quantitative version using the pair-collision probability

Let
\[
C_n:=1-D_{n,2}
\tag{8.1}
\]
be the probability that two uniformly sampled balls lie in the same box. Under the no-condensation hypothesis, \(C_n\to0\).

For every \(r\), a union bound over sampled-ball pairs gives
\[
1-D_{n,r}\le\binom r2 C_n.
\tag{8.2}
\]
Fix a sufficiently small constant \(a>0\), depending only on \(\delta/\lambda\), and put
\[
r_n:=\left\lfloor\frac{a}{\sqrt{C_n}}\right\rfloor.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Then \(r_n\to\infty\), while (8.2) gives a fixed lower bound
\[
D_{n,r_n}\ge d_0>0.
\tag{8.4}
\]
The amplified-cloud theorem below also implies \(b_nC_n\to\infty\), so \(r_n=o(\sqrt{b_n})=o(\sqrt n)\); in particular the factorial prefactor in (6.3) is harmless.

Before conditioning on distinctness, a sampled ball belongs to the amplified cloud with probability \(\delta/\lambda+o(1)\). By choosing \(a\) small enough, (8.2) makes the total probability of a collision less than, say, one quarter of this cloud probability. Hence under the distinct-box Palm measure \(\widetilde Q_{n,r_n}\), every selected occupancy still satisfies
\[
\Pr(K_j>L_n)\ge c_0>0
\tag{8.5}
\]
with \(c_0\) depending only on \(\lambda,\nu\).

Using the same KL tensorization as in (7.5),
\[
-\log A_{n,r_n}
\ge
c_0r_n\log\frac1{q_n}-O(r_n).
\tag{8.6}
\]
From (6.3), (8.4), \(m_n/n\to\lambda>\nu\), and \(r_n=o(n)\),
\[
-\log A_{n,r_n}
\le b_n+O(1).
\tag{8.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\nu}{Q(L_n)}
=O\left(b_n\sqrt{C_n}\right)
\quad\text{under full no condensation.}
}
\tag{8.8}
\]
Since \(C_n\to0\), this recovers (7.8) and quantifies it by the observable two-ball collision probability.

# 9. New unconditional hierarchy of same-box collision moments

For fixed integer \(s\ge2\), let
\[
C_{n,s}^{\rm same}
:=
E_{Q_n}\left[
\frac{\sum_i(Y_i)_s}{(m_n)_s}
\right].
\tag{9.1}
\]
This is exactly the probability that \(s\) uniformly sampled labeled balls, sampled without replacement, all lie in one box.

## Theorem 9.1 — amplified-cloud factorial-moment explosion

For every fixed \(s\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
C_{n,s}^{\rm same}
\ge
c_s\left(\frac{R_n}{b_n}\right)^{s-1}(1-o(1)),
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
with \(c_s>0\) depending only on \(s,\lambda,\nu\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
b_n^{s-1}C_{n,s}^{\rm same}\to\infty.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

### Proof
On the high-probability event from (3.2), take
\[
T_n\ge\frac\delta2n,
\qquad
N_n\le C\frac{b_n}{R_n}.
\]
Since \(L_n\to\infty\), every tail occupancy is eventually at least \(2s\), so
\[
(y)_s\ge (y/2)^s
\quad\text{for }y>L_n.
\]
Hence, by power-mean/Hölder,
\[
\sum_{i:Y_i>L_n}(Y_i)_s
\ge
2^{-s}\sum_{i:Y_i>L_n}Y_i^s
\ge
2^{-s}\frac{T_n^s}{N_n^{s-1}}.
\]
The right side is at least
\[
c_s n^s\left(\frac{R_n}{b_n}\right)^{s-1}.
\]
Divide by \((m_n)_s\sim\lambda^sn^s\), then average over the high-probability event. \(\square\)

Exchangeability gives
\[
E_{Q_n}(Y_1)_s
=
\frac{(m_n)_s}{n}C_{n,s}^{\rm same},
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
E_{Q_n}(Y_1)_s
\gg
\left(\frac{n}{b_n}\right)^{s-1}.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
For \(s=2\), in particular,
\[
\boxed{
b_nC_n\to\infty,}
\tag{9.5}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_{Q_n}(Y_1)
\gg
\frac{n}{b_n}.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

Under a hypothetical full no-condensation counterexample, \(Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0\) implies for every fixed \(s\)
\[
C_{n,s}^{\rm same}\to0.
\tag{9.7}
\]
Indeed
\[
\sum_i(Y_i)_s
\le
\sum_iY_i^s
\le
m_nY_{(1)}^{s-1},
\]
and bounded convergence applies after division by \((m_n)_s\).

Thus if one defines the order-\(s\) effective condensate support
\[
N_{n,s}^{\rm eff}
:=
\left(C_{n,s}^{\rm same}\right)^{-1/(s-1)},
\tag{9.8}
\]
then any full counterexample must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
1\ll N_{n,s}^{\rm eff}\ll b_n
\qquad\text{for every fixed }s\ge2.
}
\tag{9.9}
\]
For \(s=2\), the conditional one-box variance is trapped in the strict intermediate window
\[
\boxed{
\frac{n}{b_n}\ll
\operatorname{Var}(Y_1\mid S_n=m_n)
\ll n
}
\tag{9.10}
\]
under full no condensation. The upper bound follows from \(C_n\to0\).

# 10. Updated structural picture of any possible counterexample

Combining the newest three checkpoints, a full-sequence counterexample would have to satisfy all of the following simultaneously:

1. **Amplified cloud.** Essentially all missing mass \((\lambda-\nu)n\) lies above
   \[
   L_n=R_n n/b_n,
   \qquad R_n\to\infty,
   \]
   but below a sublinear maximum.
2. **Few but diverging boxes.** The cloud uses a number of boxes tending to infinity but \(o(b_n)\).
3. **Early birthday transition.** Distinct sampled boxes collapse by order
   \[
   O\!\left(\sqrt{b_n/R_n}\right).
   \]
4. **All fixed same-box moments are intermediate.** For every fixed \(s\ge2\), the effective support \(N_{n,s}^{\rm eff}\) diverges but is \(o(b_n)\).
5. **Amplified boundary tail must remain anomalously thick.** Despite \(L_n/(n/b_n)\to\infty\),
   \[
   \log\frac{\nu}{Q(L_n)}=o(b_n),
   \]
   and more quantitatively it is \(O(b_n\sqrt{C_n})\).
6. **Fixed-order size-biased saturation but growing-order collapse.** For each fixed \(r\), the exact size-biased convolution coefficient is asymptotically \((\lambda/\nu)^rp_n\), while the birthday checkpoint shows a collapse by a growing order far below \(\sqrt{b_n}\).

This is substantially narrower than the original statement “the excess might spread among mesoscopic boxes.” A counterexample now has to realize a very specific scale of Palm entropy, collision moments, and fixed-law size-biased tail mass at every sufficiently large admissible \(n\).

# 13. Next attack

The next target should combine the Palm-KL identity with the deterministic strong-record geometry of
\[
p_k^*:=\pi_k^*=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu}.
\]
At a multiplicative rate record \(M\), if
\[
r(x)=\min_{k\le x}\frac{-\log p_k^*}{k},
\]
then every \(k\le\theta M\) obeys
\[
p_k^*\le e^{-kr(\theta M)}.
\]
Hence the complete size-biased boundary mass in a band \((L,\theta M]\) has the deterministic upper bound
\[
\sum_{L<k\le\theta M}p_k^*
\le
\frac{e^{-Lr(\theta M)}}{1-e^{-r(\theta M)}}.
\]
The new necessary condition says that, for a counterexample, the corresponding amplified tail at the physical scale \(L_n\) cannot be exponentially small on the \(b_n\)-scale. The next concrete task is therefore to align strong rate records \(M\asymp(\lambda-\nu)n\) with the amplified cutoff and prove that the record upper bound is incompatible with
\[
\log\frac{\nu}{Q(L_n)}=O(b_n\sqrt{C_n})
\]
on some subsequence. If the alignment fails because of exact arithmetic/local-conditioning cost, the failure should be recorded as an explicit quantitative condition on \(b_n\) and used to design a fixed-law counterexample rather than hidden behind a local-limit assumption.
