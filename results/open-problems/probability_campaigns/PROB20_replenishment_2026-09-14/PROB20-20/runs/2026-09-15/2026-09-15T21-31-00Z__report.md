## 2. Normalized iid representation and the remaining obstruction from the previous checkpoint

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu.
\]
The normalized generating radius is one, and
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\). Write
\[
p_n:=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=-\log p_n.
\]
The weak law gives \(b_n\to\infty\), and Theorem 17.1 gives
\[
\boxed{b_n=o(n).}
\]

The immediately preceding checkpoint reduced one remaining route to the boundary residual atom
\[
\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-M)
\]
when \(M\asymp(\lambda-\nu)n\) is a favorable fixed-law record. The source only gives an \(e^{-o(n)}\) lower scale at such boundary targets, while the universal fixed-law record advantage can diverge much more slowly. Therefore an unrestricted pointwise local-limit assertion cannot be inserted.

This run proves that the pointwise arithmetic obstruction disappears **uniformly on a deterministic growing window of macroscopic giant sizes**. The remaining issue is then geometric alignment of cheap/record weights with that window, not a missing local CLT inside the window.

Throughout the proof below I first divide the lattice by its span, so the boundary law has span one. The statement for general span \(d\) is obtained by applying the argument on the rescaled integer lattice; all windows then consist of admissible lattice points.

# 3. Universal smoothing of convolution powers

The key observation is that every fixed span-one boundary law contains a fixed finite aperiodic smoothing component.

Because \(\operatorname{span}(\pi)=1\), choose a finite set
\[
F\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi)
\]
whose differences generate \(\mathbb Z\). Choose a probability law \(\rho_F\) supported on \(F\), positive at every point of \(F\). There exists a fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) such that
\[
\varepsilon\rho_F(k)\le\pi_k
\qquad\text{for every }k.
\]
Thus
\[
\pi=\varepsilon\rho_F+(1-\varepsilon)\eta
\tag{3.1}
\]
for another probability law \(\eta\).

Let
\[
g_N(s):=\Pr(Z_1+\cdots+Z_N=s),
\qquad Z_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\rho_F.
\]

## Lemma 3.1 — fixed-template anti-concentration and discrete gradient

There is a constant \(C_F<\infty\), depending only on the finite template \(\rho_F\), such that for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\|g_N\|_\infty\le \frac{C_F}{\sqrt N},
\qquad
\|\Delta g_N\|_\infty
:=\sup_s|g_N(s+1)-g_N(s)|
\le\frac{C_F}{N}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
Let
\[
\varphi(\theta)=E e^{i\theta Z_1},\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi.
\]
Since \(\rho_F\) has finite support, positive variance, and span one, there are constants \(c>0\), \(q<1\), and \(\theta_0>0\) such that
\[
|\varphi(\theta)|\le e^{-c\theta^2}
\quad(|\theta|\le\theta_0),
\qquad
|\varphi(\theta)|\le q
\quad(\theta_0\le|\theta|\le\pi).
\]
Fourier inversion gives
\[
g_N(s)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
e^{-is\theta}\varphi(\theta)^N\,d\theta,
\]
so
\[
\|g_N\|_\infty
\le C\int_0^{\theta_0}e^{-cN\theta^2}\,d\theta+Cq^N
\le C_FN^{-1/2}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
g_N(s+1)-g_N(s)
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
e^{-is\theta}(e^{-i\theta}-1)\varphi(\theta)^N\,d\theta.
\]
Using \(|e^{-i\theta}-1|\le|\theta|\),
\[
\|\Delta g_N\|_\infty
\le C\int_0^{\theta_0}\theta e^{-cN\theta^2}\,d\theta+Cq^N
\le C_FN^{-1}.
\]
\(\square\)

Let
\[
p^{(n)}(s):=\Pr(S_n=s)=\pi^{*n}(s).
\]
Use the mixture representation (3.1) independently for each summand. If
\[
N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,\varepsilon),
\]
then, conditional on \(N\), \(S_n\) is the convolution of \(g_N\) with the sum of \(n-N\) iid \(\eta\)-variables. Convolution with a probability measure cannot increase either the \(\ell^\infty\) norm or the \(\ell^\infty\) norm of a discrete first difference.

Chernoff's inequality gives
\[
\Pr(N<\varepsilon n/2)\le e^{-c_\varepsilon n}.
\]
Hence Lemma 3.1 yields the following universal smoothing statement.

## Theorem 3.2 — universal convolution smoothing

For the fixed boundary law \(\pi\), there is \(C_\pi<\infty\) such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_s p^{(n)}(s)\le\frac{C_\pi}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
\sup_s|p^{(n)}(s+1)-p^{(n)}(s)|\le\frac{C_\pi}{n}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof
For the first estimate,
\[
\|p^{(n)}\|_\infty
\le
E\!\left[
\frac{C_F}{\sqrt N};\,N\ge\varepsilon n/2
\right]
+\Pr(N<\varepsilon n/2)
\le \frac{C_\pi}{\sqrt n}.
\]
For the second,
\[
\|\Delta p^{(n)}\|_\infty
\le
E\!\left[
\frac{C_F}{N};\,N\ge\varepsilon n/2
\right]
+2\Pr(N<\varepsilon n/2)
\le \frac{C_\pi}{n}.
\]
\(\square\)

This theorem needs only a fixed lattice law of span one. It uses no variance assumption on \(\pi\), no domain of attraction, and no moving local CLT.

# 4. A growing interval of polynomially large boundary local coefficients

The weak law gives
\[
S_n/n\to_p\nu.
\]
Choose any deterministic integer sequence \(W_n\) satisfying
\[
W_n\to\infty,
\qquad
W_n=o(n),
\tag{4.1}
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\Pr(|S_n-\nu n|\le W_n)\ge\frac34.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Such a sequence exists by convergence in probability; for definiteness one may take a sufficiently slow diagonal sequence of relative LLN windows and then enlarge it to at least \(\sqrt n\) if desired.

Inside
\[
J_n:=\{s\in\mathbb Z:|s-\nu n|\le W_n\},
\]
choose \(s_n^*\) maximizing \(p^{(n)}(s)\). Then
\[
p^{(n)}(s_n^*)
\ge
\frac{3/4}{2W_n+2}
\ge\frac{c}{W_n}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
In particular,
\[
np^{(n)}(s_n^*)\to\infty
\tag{4.4}
\]
because \(W_n=o(n)\).

Define
\[
H_n
:=
\left\lfloor
\frac{n\,p^{(n)}(s_n^*)}{4C_\pi}
\right\rfloor.
\tag{4.5}
\]
By (4.3),
\[
H_n\ge c_1\frac{n}{W_n}\to\infty.
\tag{4.6}
\]
By the anti-concentration half of Theorem 3.2,
\[
H_n=O(\sqrt n)=o(n).
\tag{4.7}
\]
Finally, the discrete-gradient half of Theorem 3.2 gives, uniformly for \(|h|\le H_n\),
\[
p^{(n)}(s_n^*+h)
\ge
p^{(n)}(s_n^*)-\frac{C_\pi|h|}{n}
\ge\frac34p^{(n)}(s_n^*)
\ge\frac{c_2}{W_n}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

We have proved:

## Theorem 4.1 — polynomial local plateau at the boundary mean

There are deterministic sequences
\[
s_n^*=\nu n+o(n),
\qquad
H_n\to\infty,
\qquad
H_n=o(n),
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{|h|\le H_n}
\Pr(S_n=s_n^*+h)
\ge\frac{c}{W_n},
\qquad
H_n\ge c'\frac{n}{W_n},
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
where \(W_n=o(n)\). In particular, every exact local coefficient in this **growing consecutive interval** has only logarithmic cost,
\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr(S_n=s_n^*+h)
\le \log W_n+O(1)
\le\log n+O(1).
}
\tag{4.10}
\]

This is the main new resolution of the previous run's local-arithmetic obstruction: an arbitrary boundary target can still be bad, but a deterministic interval of diverging width around an actual typical center is always locally cheap.

# 5. Transfer to macroscopic candidate giant sizes

Apply Theorem 4.1 to \(S_{n-1}\). Write its chosen center, LLN width, and plateau half-width as
\[
s_{n-1}^*,\qquad W_{n-1},\qquad H_{n-1}.
\]
Define the corresponding **macroscopic giant window**
\[
K_n^*:=m_n-s_{n-1}^*,
\qquad
I_n:=\{k\in\mathbb Z:|k-K_n^*|\le H_{n-1}\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Since
\[
s_{n-1}^*/n\to\nu,
\qquad
m_n/n\to\lambda,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
K_n^*/n\to\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0,
\qquad
H_{n-1}/n\to0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus for all large \(n\), every \(k\in I_n\) satisfies
\[
k\ge\delta n/2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For every \(k\in I_n\),
\[
|m_n-k-s_{n-1}^*|\le H_{n-1},
\]
so Theorem 4.1 gives the **uniform boundary residual lower bound**
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k)
\ge\frac{c}{W_{n-1}}
\qquad(k\in I_n).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This conclusion absorbs both sources of troublesome drift automatically:

1. the arbitrary admissible centering error \(m_n-\lambda n=o(n)\), and
2. any skew or infinite-variance displacement of the most likely boundary sum away from the literal number \(\nu(n-1)\).

No local limit theorem is used.

# 6. Exact macroscopic-window lower bound in the conditioned allocation

Let
\[
N_n(I_n):=\#\{i:Y_i\in I_n\}.
\]
By the iid conditioning representation,
\[
E[N_n(I_n)\mid S_n=m_n]
=
\frac{n}{p_n}
\sum_{k\in I_n}
\pi_k\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Using (5.4),
\[
\boxed{
E[N_n(I_n)\mid S_n=m_n]
\ge
\frac{c n e^{b_n}}{W_{n-1}}
\Pi(I_n),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
where
\[
\Pi(I_n):=\sum_{k\in I_n}\pi_k.
\]

Because every box counted by \(N_n(I_n)\) has at least \(\delta n/2\) balls, deterministically
\[
N_n(I_n)
\le
\frac{2m_n}{\delta n}
\le B_{\lambda,\nu}
\tag{6.3}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), with a fixed finite constant \(B_{\lambda,\nu}\). Therefore
\[
\Pr(N_n(I_n)>0\mid S_n=m_n)
\ge
\frac{1}{B_{\lambda,\nu}}
E[N_n(I_n)\mid S_n=m_n].
\]
Combining with (6.2),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left(Y_{(1)}\ge\frac\delta2n\mid S_n=m_n\right)
\ge
c_{\lambda,\nu}
\frac{n e^{b_n}}{W_{n-1}}
\Pi(I_n).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
(The right side is automatically at most one because it is bounded above by the actual probability through the preceding exact expectation inequalities.)

## Corollary 6.1 — window-mass closure criterion

If along some subsequence
\[
\boxed{
\frac{n e^{b_n}}{W_{n-1}}\Pi(I_n)
\not\longrightarrow0,
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
then \(Y_{(1)}/n\not\to_p0\) along the full sequence. In particular, Janson's full-sequence no-condensation alternative is impossible for that law/centering sequence.

This criterion is exact apart from the explicitly constructed LLN/smoothing window; it has no local-limit hypothesis.

# 7. A necessary moving macroscopic cost trench for every hypothetical counterexample

Now suppose, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\tag{7.1}
\]
along the full sequence. Then the left side of (6.4) tends to zero. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\Pi(I_n)
=
o\!\left(
\frac{W_{n-1}}{n}e^{-b_n}
\right).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Since every \(k\in I_n\) is asymptotic to \(\delta n\), put
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k.
\]
From (7.2), uniformly for support points \(k\in I_n\),
\[
\frac{n e^{b_n}}{W_{n-1}}\pi_k\to0.
\]
Because \(n/k\to1/\delta\) uniformly across \(I_n\), this is equivalent to the **cost trench**
\[
\boxed{
-\log q_k
\ge
b_n-\log W_{n-1}+\omega(1),
\qquad
k\in I_n\cap\operatorname{supp}(\pi),
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
uniformly across the whole growing interval.

Thus any genuine counterexample must exhibit, for every large \(n\), a macroscopic interval
\[
I_n
=
(\lambda-\nu)n+o(n)+[-H_{n-1},H_{n-1}],
\qquad H_{n-1}\to\infty,
\]
on which the fixed boundary size-biased weights are all extraordinarily expensive relative to the exact conditioning cost.

This is a new structural necessity. The previous checkpoint left open the possibility that a favorable record weight was being defeated solely by an anomalously tiny **single** residual lattice atom. That explanation is no longer available whenever the candidate giant lies in \(I_n\): every residual coefficient there is polynomially large.

# 8. Record-alignment consequence and exact remaining gap

Let
\[
r(x)=
\min_{1\le k\le x,\ q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(cq_k)}{k}
\]
for a harmless fixed constant \(c>0\). Earlier checked campaign work established universal strong multiplicative record gaps: for each fixed \(\theta\in(0,1)\), there are infinitely many record indices \(M\) for which
\[
M\bigl(r(\theta M)-r(M)\bigr)\to\infty.
\]
The old F0059 Lean artifact certifies only its narrower geometric-sparsity telescope and is **not** used as a formal certificate for that stronger probabilistic campaign theorem.

The new window theorem gives the following clean sufficient condition without any boundary local-limit assumption. If infinitely often a favorable macroscopic support point \(M_n\) satisfies
\[
M_n\in I_n
\tag{8.1}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
b_n+\log(n\pi_{M_n})-\log W_{n-1}
\ge -O(1),
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
then (6.4) bounds the probability of a macroscopic box away from zero along that subsequence, contradicting (7.1).

Equivalently, a full counterexample must ensure that **every** support point in the whole locally-cheap giant interval obeys the trench (7.3). In particular, if a strong record lands inside \(I_n\), then its absolute log-cost must be at least
\[
b_n-\log W_{n-1}+\omega(1).
\]

The exact remaining obstruction has therefore changed form:

- **removed:** an uncontrolled exact local coefficient \(\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-M)\) at a candidate giant size;
- **remaining:** geometric alignment between favorable fixed-law record/support points and the deterministic growing intervals \(I_n\), plus comparison of their absolute record cost with \(b_n\).

The width is genuinely diverging,
\[
H_n\ge c n/W_n\to\infty,
\]
but under only a finite first moment it need not be a fixed fraction or power of \(n\). An admissible centering sequence can also have sublinear but large oscillations, so this run does **not** claim that every strong record must enter one of these windows.

# 9. Why this does not already prove the full conjecture

The smoothing theorem does not imply that the good residual plateau reaches an arbitrarily preassigned target \(m_n-M\). It constructs a consecutive interval of locally cheap residual targets of width \(H_n\to\infty\), but a fixed-law record sequence may be much sparser, and the centers
\[
K_n^*=m_n-s_{n-1}^*=(\lambda-\nu)n+o(n)
\]
may have sublinear irregular motion because both the allowed centering \(m_n-\lambda n\) and the location of high atoms of \(S_{n-1}\) can fluctuate on sublinear scales.

Thus there is still no justified step saying a strong record falls inside \(I_n\) infinitely often. That is now the principal record-alignment gap for this route.

A raw appeal to a local CLT would overstate what is known. The only local estimates used above come from the fixed finite smoothing component and are proved directly by Fourier inversion.

# 12. Next concrete attack

The next attack should use the new trench geometry rather than return to a pointwise local-limit estimate.

Define the actual locally-cheap giant centers
\[
K_n^*=m_n-s_{n-1}^*
\]
and widths \(H_{n-1}\). There are two concrete directions.

**A. Record-window intersection theorem.** Study whether a nonincreasing record-rate envelope
\[
r(x)=\min_{k\le x}-\log(cq_k)/k
\]
with radius-one finite-mean \(q_k=k\pi_k\) can avoid every interval
\[
[K_n^*-H_{n-1},K_n^*+H_{n-1}]
\]
while the trench lower bounds \(-\log q_k\gtrsim b_n-\log W_n\) hold inside every interval. The centers are asymptotically linear and the widths diverge, but only sublinearly; the interaction with possible sublinear oscillations of the admissible centering must be handled explicitly.

**B. Multi-window averaging.** The proof above selected one maximal local atom in the LLN window. The fixed-template gradient estimate can be combined with the total \(3/4\) probability in the LLN window to produce many locally-smooth clusters rather than one. A quantitative lower bound on the total length/coverage of all clusters with height at least a chosen threshold may make record avoidance impossible even when a single plateau can be skipped.

Direction B is especially attractive because it attacks the alignment problem while retaining the exact polynomial residual bounds proved here.
