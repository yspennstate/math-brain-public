\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k,

\[
0<\nu<\lambda,

m_n/n\to\lambda,
\]

\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\]

Primary source: S. Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, arXiv:1112.0510, Problem 18.39.

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid,
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad \sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad \sum_k k\pi_k=\nu,
\]
and the allocation is the iid boundary law conditioned on its sum:
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\}.
\]
The normalized generating radius is one.

# 4. Sampling balls detects the amplified condensate cloud

For a deterministic allocation vector \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\) with total mass \(m\), sample \(r\) distinct labeled balls uniformly without replacement from the \(m\) balls. Let
\[
D_r(y)
\]
denote the probability that the sampled balls lie in \(r\) distinct boxes. For the random conditioned allocation, write
\[
D_{n,r}:=\mathbb E[D_r(Y)].
\]

The next result says that the universal amplified cloud from (3.3)–(3.4) forces a birthday collision well before \(r\) reaches any macroscopic scale.

## Lemma 4.1 — distinct tail-box bound for a fixed configuration

Let a subset of boxes have occupancies
\[
z_1,\ldots,z_N,
\qquad
T:=z_1+\cdots+z_N.
\]
Condition on exactly \(h\) of the \(r\) sampled balls belonging to this subset. Then the probability that these \(h\) balls lie in distinct boxes is at most
\[
\boxed{
\frac{(N)_h}{N^h}\frac{T^h}{(T)_h}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
If \(h\le N\) and \(h\le T/2\), then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{(N)_h}{N^h}\frac{T^h}{(T)_h}
\le
\exp\left(
-\frac{h(h-1)}{2N}
+\frac{h(h-1)}{T}
\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
If \(h>N\), the distinct-box probability is zero.

### Proof
The exact probability of distinct subset boxes is
\[
\frac{h!\,e_h(z_1,\ldots,z_N)}{(T)_h},
\]
where \(e_h\) is the elementary symmetric polynomial. Maclaurin's inequality gives
\[
\frac{e_h(z_1,\ldots,z_N)}{\binom Nh}
\le
\left(\frac TN\right)^h,
\]
which proves (4.1).

For (4.2),
\[
\frac{(N)_h}{N^h}
=
\prod_{j=0}^{h-1}\left(1-\frac jN\right)
\le
\exp\left(-\frac{h(h-1)}{2N}\right).
\]
Also, when \(h\le T/2\),
\[
\log\frac{T^h}{(T)_h}
=
-\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\log\left(1-\frac jT\right)
\le
\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\frac{2j}{T}
=
\frac{h(h-1)}{T}.
\]
\(\square\)

# 5. New theorem: universal birthday collapse at order \(\sqrt{b_n}\)

## Theorem 5.1 — amplified-cloud birthday collapse

Let
\[
r_n:=\lfloor\sqrt{b_n}\rfloor.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,r_n}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
For the archived block choice satisfying (3.5), there are constants \(c_1,c_2>0\), depending only on \(\lambda\) and \(\nu\), such that
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,r_n}
\le
o(1)
+
\frac{c_1}{\sqrt{b_n}}
+
\exp(-c_2R_n+o(1)).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof
By (3.3), with probability tending to one,
\[
T_n\ge\frac\delta2 n.
\tag{5.3}
\]
For large \(n\), also \(m_n\le2\lambda n\). Hence, on the event (5.3), a uniformly sampled ball belongs to a box above \(L_n\) with probability at least
\[
p_0:=\frac{\delta}{4\lambda}>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Condition on the allocation and let \(H_n\) be the number of the \(r_n\) sampled balls that belong to boxes above \(L_n\). Then \(H_n\) is hypergeometric with mean at least \(p_0r_n\). Its variance is at most \(r_n/4\), so Chebyshev gives
\[
\Pr\left(H_n<\frac{p_0}{2}r_n\mid Y\right)
\le
\frac{C}{r_n}
=
O(b_n^{-1/2}).
\tag{5.5}
\]
This elementary bound is enough; no hypergeometric local limit is used.

On the complementary event put
\[
h\ge\frac{p_0}{2}r_n.
\]
The number \(N_n\) of boxes above \(L_n\) satisfies, for large \(n\),
\[
N_n\le C_0\frac{b_n}{R_n}
\tag{5.6}
\]
by (3.4). If \(h>N_n\), the sampled tail balls cannot be in distinct boxes. Otherwise Lemma 4.1 gives
\[
\Pr(\text{the }h\text{ tail balls are in distinct boxes}\mid H_n=h,Y)
\le
\exp\left(
-\frac{h(h-1)}{2N_n}
+rac{h(h-1)}{T_n}
\right).
\]
Now
\[
\frac{h^2}{N_n}
\ge
c\,
\frac{b_n}{b_n/R_n}
=cR_n,
\tag{5.7}
\]
while
\[
\frac{h^2}{T_n}
\le
C\frac{b_n}{n}
=o(1)
\tag{5.8}
\]
by (3.1). Therefore the conditional distinct-tail probability is at most
\[
\exp(-c_2R_n+o(1)).
\]
If all \(r_n\) sampled balls occupy distinct boxes, in particular their tail subset does. Combining the high-probability cloud event, (5.5), and the previous display gives (5.2), hence (5.1). \(\square\)

### Interpretation

The conclusion is universal in the supercritical boundary regime: even if the largest box were hypothetically sublinear, by the time one samples only \(\sqrt{b_n}=o(\sqrt n)\) balls, collisions become overwhelmingly likely because a positive fraction of all mass is already compressed into only \(O(b_n/R_n)\) amplified-cloud boxes.

# 6. Sharper transition scale

The same proof yields a more precise birthday threshold.

Define
\[
\widehat r_n:=A\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{R_n}},
\]
where \(A>0\) is fixed. Since \(b_n/R_n\to\infty\), we have \(\widehat r_n\to\infty\). Also
\[
\widehat r_n^2/n
=A^2\frac{b_n}{R_nn}	o0.
\]
The argument above gives, for sufficiently large fixed \(A\),
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_{n,\lfloor\widehat r_n\rfloor}<\frac12.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
r_n^\dagger:=\min\{r\ge2:D_{n,r}\le1/2\},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
r_n^\dagger
=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{b_n}{R_n}}\right)
=o(\sqrt{b_n}).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Under a hypothetical full no-condensation law, every fixed \(r\) has \(D_{n,r}\to1\), so necessarily
\[
\boxed{
r_n^\dagger\to\infty.}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Thus any full counterexample has a genuine intermediate birthday scale
\[
1\ll r_n^\dagger\ll\sqrt{b_n}.
\]
This is a new quantitative description of the only possible mesoscopic-cloud mechanism.

# 7. Exact size-biased convolution identity

Let
\[
\pi_k^*:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},
\qquad k\ge1,
\]
and let \(\xi_1^*,\xi_2^*,\ldots\) be iid with this size-biased law, independent of the ordinary iid sum \(S_{n-r}\).

## Lemma 7.1 — all-orders distinct-ball identity
For every integer \(r\le n\),
\[
\boxed{
D_{n,r}
=
\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_r}
\frac{
\Pr\left(
S_{n-r}+\xi_1^*+\cdots+\xi_r^*=m_n
\right)
}{p_n}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Proof
For a deterministic allocation \(y\), the probability that \(r\) ordered sampled balls lie in distinct boxes is
\[
\frac{1}{(m_n)_r}
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r\ \mathrm{distinct}}
\prod_{j=1}^r y_{i_j}.
\]
Take expectation under the iid law with the indicator \(S_n=m_n\). By symmetry, this equals
\[
\frac{(n)_r}{(m_n)_r p_n}
\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_r}
\left(\prod_j k_j\pi_{k_j}\right)
\Pr\left(S_{n-r}=m_n-\sum_jk_j\right).
\]
Since \(k\pi_k=\nu\pi_k^*\), this is (7.1). \(\square\)

# 8. New consequence: universal collapse of growing-order size-biased saturation

Take
\[
r_n=\lfloor\sqrt{b_n}\rfloor.
\]
Because \(b_n=o(n)\),
\[
r_n^2/n\to0.
\]
Also \(m_n/n\to\lambda\), so
\[
\frac{(m_n)_{r_n}}{(n)_{r_n}\nu^{r_n}}
=
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\nu}\right)^{r_n}e^{o(r_n)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Combining Theorem 5.1, Lemma 7.1, and (8.1) gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\Pr\left(
S_{n-r_n}+\xi_1^*+\cdots+\xi_{r_n}^*=m_n
\right)
}{p_n}
=
\left(\frac{\lambda}{\nu}\right)^{r_n}
 e^{o(r_n)}D_{n,r_n},
\qquad
D_{n,r_n}\to0.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
Equivalently, after division by the formal saturation factor,
\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac{\nu}{\lambda}\right)^{r_n}
 e^{-o(r_n)}
\frac{
\Pr\left(
S_{n-r_n}+\xi_1^*+\cdots+\xi_{r_n}^*=m_n
\right)
}{p_n}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This complements an earlier route in the campaign: under hypothetical no condensation, fixed-order size-biased convolution ratios saturate because finitely many sampled balls asymptotically occupy distinct boxes. The present theorem proves that saturation must nevertheless **collapse** by order \(\sqrt{b_n}\), and in fact its median collision transition is no later than order \(\sqrt{b_n/R_n}\).

The unresolved question can therefore be attacked as a transition problem for one explicit family of size-biased convolution coefficients, rather than only as a maximum problem.

# 9. What this says under hypothetical full no condensation

Assume only for contradiction analysis that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\]
Then for each fixed \(r\), a union bound on pair collisions gives
\[
D_{n,r}\to1.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Thus by a diagonal choice there is some deterministic \(s_n\to\infty\) with
\[
D_{n,s_n}\to1.
\]
Theorem 5.1 gives at the same time
\[
D_{n,\lfloor\sqrt{b_n}\rfloor}\to0.
\]
Hence a counterexample must exhibit an increasingly sharp crossover from size-biased saturation to birthday collapse somewhere in the interval
\[
1\ll r\ll\sqrt{b_n},
\]
and (6.1) places a concrete upper bound on the half-collision scale:
\[
r_n^\dagger=O\left(\sqrt{b_n/R_n}\right).
\]

This is compatible with, but substantially sharper than, the previously archived structural cloud
\[
1\ll_pN_n\ll b_n.
\]
The new result packages the cloud into an exact observable that already has the convolution representation (7.1).

The new exact target is the transition function
\[
D_{n,r}
=
\frac{(n)_r\nu^r}{(m_n)_r}
\frac{
\Pr(S_{n-r}+\xi_1^*+\cdots+\xi_r^*=m_n)
}{p_n}.

Under a hypothetical counterexample,

The next attack is to obtain a **lower** bound on this convolution ratio from the fixed size-biased law \(q_k/\nu\). Two promising nonlocal routes avoid a local CLT:

1. use record scales of \(q_k\) to prove that replacing a slowly growing number of ordinary summands by size-biased summands cannot reduce the exact coefficient by the birthday factor required in (5.2);

   then combine the exact \(r=1\) identity, fixed-\(r\) saturation under no condensation, and the new forced collapse scale.

A sufficiently strong interpolation inequality would contradict the existence of the transition window and close Problem 18.39 without any local-limit assumption.
