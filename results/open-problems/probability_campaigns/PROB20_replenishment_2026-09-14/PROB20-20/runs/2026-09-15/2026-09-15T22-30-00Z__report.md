## 2. Normalized iid form and previous checkpoint

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu.
\]
The normalized generating radius is one and
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\). Write
\[
p_n:=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=-\log p_n.
\]
The weak law gives \(b_n\to\infty\), while Janson's Theorem 17.1 gives \(b_n=o(n)\) in this normalized boundary regime.

The immediately preceding checkpoint proved two universal smoothing bounds for the fixed span-one law,
\[
\sup_s\Pr(S_n=s)=O(n^{-1/2}),
\qquad
\sup_s|\Pr(S_n=s+1)-\Pr(S_n=s)|=O(n^{-1}),
\]
and from them a growing interval of residual sums carrying only polynomial local cost. Its remaining obstruction was that a favorable fixed-law record need not literally fall in that interval.

This run removes that *literal intersection* obstruction. The new result is a Gaussian Harnack lower bound extending from a suitable high local mode over every sublinear displacement. Consequently every macroscopic record scale can be paired with an admissible \(n\) at sublinear displacement and receives an explicit residual local lower bound.

Throughout, I divide the lattice by its span first. The general span-\(d\) version is the same argument on the rescaled admissible lattice.

# 3. A fixed Bernoulli smoothing block exists for every span-one law

## Lemma 3.1 — consecutive finite convolution atoms

There exist an integer \(L\ge1\) and an integer \(a\) such that
\[
\pi^{*L}(a)>0,
\qquad
\pi^{*L}(a+1)>0.
\]

### Proof
Because the lattice span is one, the gcd of support differences is one. Hence Bezout gives finitely many support pairs \((x_j,y_j)\) and integer coefficients whose signed combination of differences equals one. Reverse a pair whenever its coefficient is negative and repeat pairs according to absolute coefficient. We obtain
\[
\sum_{j=1}^L x_j-\sum_{j=1}^L y_j=1,
\]
with every \(x_j,y_j\in\operatorname{supp}(\pi)\). Both sums therefore occur with positive probability in the \(L\)-fold convolution and are consecutive. \(\square\)

Put \(\mu=\pi^{*L}\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that
\[
\mu\ge\varepsilon\rho
\]
pointwise, where \(\rho\) is the uniform law on \(\{a,a+1\}\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mu=\varepsilon\rho+(1-\varepsilon)\eta
}
\tag{3.1}
\]
for a probability law \(\eta\).

Partition the first \(LB\) variables into
\[
B=\lfloor n/L\rfloor
\]
blocks of length \(L\). Realize every block using (3.1), with independent indicators \(I_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)\). Let
\[
K=\sum_{j=1}^B I_j,
\qquad
N=\lfloor\varepsilon B/2\rfloor.
\]
Chernoff gives
\[
\beta_n:=\Pr(K<N)\le e^{-c n}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
On the good event \(G_n=\{K\ge N\}\), select the first \(N\) successful blocks. Their random increments above \(a\) are iid \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), independent of the unselected remainder. Hence the conditional sum law
\[
f_n(s):=\Pr(S_n=s\mid G_n)
\]
is a convolution of a translate of \(\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2)\) with an arbitrary residual law. The unconditional mass function has the mixture form
\[
p^{(n)}(s):=\Pr(S_n=s)
=\alpha_nf_n(s)+\beta_ng_n(s),
\qquad
\alpha_n=1-\beta_n,
\tag{3.3}
\]
for another probability mass function \(g_n\).

This deterministic extraction is the basis of the stronger local estimate below.

# 4. Exact binomial ratio convexity

Let
\[
b_N(x)=2^{-N}\binom Nx,
\qquad x=0,\dots,N,
\]
and for an integer \(d\ge1\) define
\[
R_{N,d}(x)
:=
\frac{b_N(x+d)}{b_N(x)}
=
\frac{(N-x)(N-x-1)\cdots(N-x-d+1)}{(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+d)}
\]
for \(0\le x\le N-d\), and set \(R_{N,d}(x)=0\) for \(x>N-d\).

## Lemma 4.1 — decreasing discrete convexity

For each \(N,d\), \(R_{N,d}\) is decreasing and discretely convex on \(\{0,\ldots,N\}\). Its piecewise-linear interpolation to \([0,N]\) is convex.

### Proof
For interior \(x\le N-d-1\),
\[
\frac{R_{N,d}(x+1)}{R_{N,d}(x)}
=:
a_x
=
\frac{(N-x-d)(x+1)}{(N-x)(x+d+1)}
\le1,
\]
so the sequence decreases. A direct simplification gives
\[
1-2a_x+a_xa_{x+1}
=
\frac{d(N+1)(N(d+1)-2x-2)}{(N-x)(N-x-1)(x+d+1)(x+d+2)}
\ge0
\]
whenever both ratios are interior. Multiplying by \(R_{N,d}(x)>0\) yields
\[
R_{N,d}(x)-2R_{N,d}(x+1)+R_{N,d}(x+2)\ge0.
\]
At the terminal transition to zero the same second difference is nonnegative directly, and after that it is zero. \(\square\)

Now let
\[
f(s)=\Pr(R+B=s),
\qquad B\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,1/2),
\]
where \(R\) is arbitrary and independent of \(B\). Under the posterior law conditional on \(R+B=s\), write the binomial coordinate again as \(B\). Then exactly
\[
\frac{f(s+d)}{f(s)}
=E[R_{N,d}(B)\mid R+B=s].
\tag{4.1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{f(s+1)}{f(s)}
=E\left[\frac{N-B}{B+1}\middle|R+B=s\right],
\]
\[
\frac{f(s-1)}{f(s)}
=E\left[\frac{B}{N-B+1}\middle|R+B=s\right].
\tag{4.2}
\]

## Lemma 4.2 — Harnack lower bound at an approximate local mode

Suppose
\[
f(s\pm1)\le(1+\zeta)f(s)
\]
with \(\zeta N\le1\). Then
\[
|E[B\mid R+B=s]-N/2|\le C
\]
for a universal constant \(C\). Consequently there are universal constants \(c_0,C_0>0\) such that, for every integer \(|d|\le c_0N\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{f(s+d)}{f(s)}
\ge
\exp\left[-C_0\frac{d^2+|d|+1}{N}\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
From (4.2),
\[
(N+1)E\frac1{B+1}\le2+\zeta,
\]
and Jensen gives
\[
E B+1\ge\frac{N+1}{2+\zeta}.
\]
The second identity gives the symmetric upper bound. Hence \(EB=N/2+O(1+\zeta N)=N/2+O(1)\).

For \(d>0\), (4.1), Lemma 4.1 and Jensen yield
\[
\frac{f(s+d)}{f(s)}
\ge R_{N,d}(EB).
\]
When \(EB=N/2+O(1)\) and \(d\le N/8\), taking logarithms in the product defining \(R_{N,d}\) and using \(\log(1-u)\ge-u-Cu^2\) gives
\[
\log R_{N,d}(EB)
\ge-C\frac{d^2+d+1}{N}.
\]
Negative \(d\) follows by the symmetry \(B\mapsto N-B\). \(\square\)

No local central limit theorem occurs in this argument.

# 5. Gaussian Harnack lower bound for arbitrary fixed lattice convolution powers

Choose a deterministic LLN width
\[
W_n\to\infty,
\qquad
W_n=o(n),
\]
so that
\[
\Pr(|S_n-\nu n|\le W_n)\ge3/4
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). Choose \(u_n\) in this window with maximal mass. Then
\[
p^{(n)}(u_n)\ge c/W_n.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Starting from \(u_n\), while the current site is not a local maximum, move to a neighboring site of strictly larger mass. The path cannot visit more than \(1/p^{(n)}(u_n)=O(W_n)\) distinct sites, because every visited site has mass at least the starting mass and the masses sum to one. Thus the path terminates at a local maximum \(s_n^\dagger\) with
\[
\boxed{
s_n^\dagger=\nu n+o(n),
\qquad
p^{(n)}(s_n^\dagger)\ge c/W_n.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Use the decomposition (3.3). Since \(p^{(n)}(s_n^\dagger\pm1)\le p^{(n)}(s_n^\dagger)\),
\[
f_n(s_n^\dagger\pm1)
\le f_n(s_n^\dagger)+\beta_n/\alpha_n.
\]
Also
\[
f_n(s_n^\dagger)
\ge(p^{(n)}(s_n^\dagger)-\beta_n)/\alpha_n.
\]
Hence the approximate-mode error satisfies
\[
\zeta_n
\le
\frac{\beta_n}{p^{(n)}(s_n^\dagger)-\beta_n}
\le C W_ne^{-cn},
\]
so \(\zeta_nN\to0\). Lemma 4.2 applies. Since \(N=\Theta(n)\), and \(p^{(n)}\ge\alpha_nf_n\), we obtain:

## Theorem 5.1 — universal Gaussian Harnack window

There are constants \(\gamma,c,C>0\), depending only on the fixed law \(\pi\), and deterministic sequences
\[
W_n\to\infty,
\qquad W_n=o(n),
\qquad s_n^\dagger=\nu n+o(n),
\]
such that uniformly for every integer \(|d|\le\gamma n\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_n=s_n^\dagger+d)
\ge
\frac{c}{W_n}
\exp\left[-C\frac{d^2+|d|+1}{n}\right].
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In particular, for **every** deterministic \(d_n=o(n)\),
\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr(S_n=s_n^\dagger+d_n)
\le
\log W_n+C\frac{d_n^2}{n}+o(1)
=o(n).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This strictly extends the previous checkpoint's flat plateau: literal intersection with a width-\(H_n\) interval is no longer required.

# 6. Every macroscopic record can be paired with an admissible n at sublinear displacement

Put
\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0,
\qquad
K_n^\dagger:=m_n-s_{n-1}^\dagger.
\]
By (5.2),
\[
\boxed{K_n^\dagger/n\to\delta.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let \(R_j\to\infty\) be any increasing sequence of integer scales; later these will be record points in the support of the size-biased boundary law. Define the first crossing
\[
n_j:=\min\{n\ge n_0:K_n^\dagger\ge R_j\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(j\), this exists. Minimality gives
\[
K_{n_j-1}^\dagger<R_j\le K_{n_j}^\dagger.
\]
Using (6.1) on both neighboring indices,
\[
\boxed{R_j/n_j\to\delta.}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Define the crossing overshoot
\[
D_j:=K_{n_j}^\dagger-R_j\ge0.
\]
Because
\[
K_n^\dagger=\delta n+e_n,
\qquad e_n=o(n),
\]
we have
\[
0\le D_j
\le K_{n_j}^\dagger-K_{n_j-1}^\dagger
=\delta+e_{n_j}-e_{n_j-1}
=o(n_j).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus Theorem 5.1 gives the explicit residual local lower bound
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_{n_j-1}=m_{n_j}-R_j)
\ge
\frac{c}{W_{n_j-1}}
\exp\left[-C\frac{D_j^2+D_j+1}{n_j}
\right].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is the first key conclusion of the run: **every** record/support scale, not merely one that lands inside a preselected plateau, can be synchronized with the admissible full sequence at only a sublinear displacement penalty.

# 7. Exact one-giant lower bound at record crossings

Let
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,
\qquad
A_k:=-\log q_k
\]
for positive support points. Let \(N_R\) be the number of boxes containing exactly \(R\) balls. Exact conditioning gives
\[
E[N_R\mid S_n=m_n]
=
\frac{n\pi_R\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-R)}{p_n}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
When \(R/n\to\delta\), deterministically
\[
N_R\le m_n/R\le C_{\lambda,\nu}
\]
for all large \(n\). Hence
\[
\Pr(N_R\ge1\mid S_n=m_n)
\ge c_{\lambda,\nu}E[N_R\mid S_n=m_n].
\tag{7.2}
\]
At the crossings from Section 6,
\[
n_j\pi_{R_j}
=\frac{n_j}{R_j}q_{R_j}
=(\delta^{-1}+o(1))e^{-A_{R_j}}.
\]
Combining (6.5), (7.1), (7.2), and \(p_{n_j}=e^{-b_{n_j}}\) yields
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
Y_{(1)}\ge\frac\delta2n_j
\middle|S_{n_j}=m_{n_j}
\right)
\ge
c
\exp\left[
 b_{n_j}-A_{R_j}-\log W_{n_j-1}
-C\frac{D_j^2+D_j+1}{n_j}
\right].
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore a hypothetical full-sequence counterexample \(Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0\) must satisfy, at every such record crossing,
\[
\boxed{
A_{R_j}-b_{n_j}
+\log W_{n_j-1}
+C\frac{D_j^2+D_j+1}{n_j}
\longrightarrow+\infty.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Conversely, if the expression on the left of (7.4) fails to tend to \(+\infty\) along any record subsequence, persistent macroscopic condensation follows on that subsequence.

This is a quantitative replacement for the previous informal “record must hit the cheap residual window” requirement.

# 8. A generalized record-gap theorem against every sublinear cap

The following deterministic statement is new and independent of local-limit input.

Because \(q_k=k\pi_k\) is summable, choose a fixed \(c_0>0\) so small that
\[
0<c_0q_k<1
\]
for every positive support point. Define the lower rate envelope
\[
r(x)
:=
\min_{1\le k\le x,\ q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(c_0q_k)}{k}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Let
\[
R_1<R_2<\cdots
\]
be the strict record points where this envelope decreases, and write
\[
h_j:=r(R_j),
\qquad
A_j:=R_jh_j=-\log(c_0q_{R_j}).
\]
Since the normalized generating radius is one,
\[
h_j\downarrow0.
\]
Since \(q\) is summable and \(R_j\to\infty\),
\[
A_j\to\infty.
\tag{8.2}
\]

## Theorem 8.1 — every sublinear cap has unbounded record advantage

Let \(U_j\to\infty\) be any deterministic sequence satisfying
\[
U_j/R_j\to0.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{j\to\infty}
R_j\bigl(r(U_j)-r(R_j)\bigr)=+\infty.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

### Proof
Let \(i(j)\) be the last record index with \(R_{i(j)}\le U_j\). Then
\[
r(U_j)=h_{i(j)},
\qquad
i(j)\to\infty,
\]
and, for all large \(j\),
\[
R_{i(j)}\le U_j\le R_j/2.
\tag{8.4}
\]
Assume contrariwise that (8.3) fails. Then for some \(C<\infty\) and all large \(j\),
\[
0\le h_{i(j)}-h_j\le C/R_j.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Fix a large starting record \(j_0=j\), and recursively put
\[
j_{r+1}=i(j_r).
\]
Let
\[
X_j:=h_j^{-1/2}\to\infty.
\]
By (8.2),
\[
R_j/X_j=R_j\sqrt{h_j}=A_j/\sqrt{h_j}\to\infty,
\]
so initially \(R_j>X_j\). Stop at the first \(L\) with
\[
R_{j_L}\le X_j.
\]
For \(r<L\), all involved indices tend to infinity with the starting \(j\), and (8.4) gives
\[
R_{j_{r+1}}\le R_{j_r}/2.
\]
Therefore
\[
\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}\frac1{R_{j_r}}
\le\frac2{R_{j_{L-1}}}
<\frac2{X_j}.
\]
Summing (8.5),
\[
h_{j_L}
\le h_j+\frac{2C}{X_j}.
\]
Consequently
\[
A_{j_L}=R_{j_L}h_{j_L}
\le X_jh_j+2C
=\sqrt{h_j}+2C,
\]
which is bounded. But \(R_{j_{L-1}}>X_j\to\infty\), and \(i(k)\to\infty\), so \(j_L=i(j_{L-1})\to\infty\). This contradicts \(A_i\to\infty\). \(\square\)

This theorem is stronger than a fixed-multiplicative-gap record statement: the comparison scale \(U_j\) may be **any** diverging \(o(R_j)\) sequence.

# 9. Consequence under the hypothetical full no-condensation scenario

Assume for contradiction analysis that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\]
Then there exists a deterministic high-probability cap
\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\qquad
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{9.1}
\]
At the record crossings \(n_j\) of Section 6, (6.3) gives \(R_j\asymp n_j\), hence
\[
U_{n_j}/R_j\to0.
\]
Apply Theorem 8.1 with \(U_j=U_{n_j}\). There is a subsequence along which the deterministic record-over-cap advantage
\[
\boxed{
G_j
:=
R_j\bigl(r(U_{n_j})-r(R_j)\bigr)
\to\infty.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
(after passing to a subsequence along the unbounded limsup).

Thus a hypothetical full counterexample must satisfy **both** of the following:

1. the macroscopic record is increasingly cheaper, on the size-biased exponential-rate scale, than every allowed capped size, by the unbounded amount \(G_j\);
2. nevertheless its one-giant contribution must still be suppressed by the quantitative deficit (7.4).

This isolates the surviving obstruction considerably more sharply than the prior checkpoint.

# 10. Why this still does not close Problem 18.39

The new results eliminate two previous technical escape routes:

- a record no longer has to *land* in the earlier narrow polynomial plateau; every record crossing has the Gaussian Harnack residual lower bound (6.5);
- the record advantage is not merely a fixed-\(\theta\) phenomenon; against the **actual sublinear high-probability cap** of any proposed counterexample it is unbounded along a subsequence by Theorem 8.1.

The remaining quantitative gap is real. The universal theorem (9.2) gives only an **unbounded** record advantage. It does not yet prove that \(G_j\) dominates either
\[
\frac{D_j^2}{n_j}
\]
from the arbitrary admissible centering/crossing motion, or the collective combinatorial/entropy cost of replacing a capped condensate cloud by one record-sized box. Both quantities may be sublinear and can, in principle, grow faster than an arbitrarily slowly diverging \(G_j\).

Therefore it would be circular to declare a solution at this point.

A particularly clean sufficient closure criterion extracted from this run is:
\[
\boxed{
A_{R_j}
\le
b_{n_j}-\log W_{n_j-1}
-C\frac{D_j^2}{n_j}+O(1)
}
\tag{10.1}
\]
along some record subsequence. By (7.3), this immediately yields a fixed positive probability of a macroscopic box along that subsequence, contradicting full no-condensation.

# 11. Failed / insufficient routes retained

1. **Unrestricted local CLT.** Still not assumed. The Gaussian Harnack theorem is proved directly from a fixed Bernoulli smoothing component and is only the lower bound actually needed.
2. **Literal record-window intersection.** No longer needed; Section 6 replaces it by first-crossing plus a sublinear Gaussian penalty.
3. **Generic one-big-jump theorem.** Standard theorems found in the bounded literature search impose subexponential/local-subexponential, regular-variation, stretched-exponential, or comparable hypotheses absent from the arbitrary fixed law here.
4. **Raw composition counting.** It introduces an entropy term that can swamp an arbitrarily slowly diverging record advantage.
5. **Sparse-record Lean artifact.** It certifies only its stated deterministic geometric-sparsity telescope and is not a certificate for Theorem 8.1 or for the probabilistic conjecture.
6. **Problems 18.40--18.42.** Neighboring questions remain outside the target and are not used as substitutes for 18.39.

# 13. Next concrete attacks

The strongest next route is a **direct merge comparison** in size-biased weights. For a capped condensate cloud with occupancies \(k_i\le U_n\), the record envelope gives
\[
q_{k_i}\le e^{-r(U_n)k_i}
\]
(up to the fixed harmless normalization in Section 8), whereas the record giant has
\[
q_{R_j}=e^{-r(R_j)R_j}.
\]
If one can mark boxes/balls so that the label entropy and the factors \(k_i\) are canceled exactly, the weight gain is governed by
\[
\exp\{G_j\}.
\]
The goal is to make the comparison injective or bounded-to-one, avoiding an \(e^{O(r\log n)}\) composition-count loss that would destroy an arbitrarily slow \(G_j\).

A second route is to use the earlier exact capped-canonical and Palm entropy identities to prove
\[
b_{n_j}-A_{R_j}
\gtrsim
G_j-o(G_j)
\]
along a carefully selected record subsequence. The new Harnack theorem means that the residual local coefficient can then be paid explicitly rather than hidden behind an unjustified local CLT.

A third route is to sharpen first-crossing synchronization. A deterministic argument showing
\[

\]
along some record subsequence would combine directly with (7.3) once the conditioning-cost comparison is controlled.
