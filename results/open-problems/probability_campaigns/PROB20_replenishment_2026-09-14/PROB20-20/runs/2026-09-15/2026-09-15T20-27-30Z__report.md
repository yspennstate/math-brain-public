# 3. Exact distinct-ball Palm measure relative to the moving canonical law

For a configuration \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), and an ordered tuple of distinct box indices
\[
\mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_r),
\]
put
\[
w_{\mathbf i}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^r x_{i_j}.
\]

Under the canonical product law \(P_n\),
\[
E_{P_n}\sum_{\mathbf i\,\mathrm{distinct}}w_{\mathbf i}(X)
=(n)_r\lambda_n^r.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Define the marked canonical Palm measure
\[
\widetilde P_{n,r}(x,\mathbf i)
:=
P_n(x)
\frac{w_{\mathbf i}(x)}{(n)_r\lambda_n^r}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Because \(P_n\) is a product law, under \(\widetilde P_{n,r}\) the marked occupancies
\[
X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_r}
\]
are iid with the **canonical size-biased law**
\[
p_n^*(k)
:=
\frac{k p_n^{\rm can}(k)}{\lambda_n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Under \(Q_n^U\), let \(D_{n,r}^U\) be the probability that \(r\) uniformly sampled labeled balls without replacement occupy distinct boxes. Since \(S_n=m_n\) exactly,
\[
E_{Q_n^U}\sum_{\mathbf i\,\mathrm{distinct}}w_{\mathbf i}(Y)
=(m_n)_rD_{n,r}^U.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Define
\[
\widetilde Q_{n,r}(y,\mathbf i)
:=
Q_n^U(y)
\frac{w_{\mathbf i}(y)}{(m_n)_rD_{n,r}^U}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — exact canonical Palm KL identity

For every \(r\le n\) for which \(D_{n,r}^U>0\),
\[
\boxed{
D(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\Vert\widetilde P_{n,r})
=
\ell_n
+
\log\frac{(n)_r\lambda_n^r}{(m_n)_rD_{n,r}^U}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof
By (2.7), on the hyperplane \(S_n=m_n\),
\[
\frac{Q_n^U(y)}{P_n(y)}=\frac1{a_n}=e^{\ell_n}.
\]
Therefore, on the support of \(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\),
\[
\frac{d\widetilde Q_{n,r}}{d\widetilde P_{n,r}}
=
\frac1{a_n}
\frac{(n)_r\lambda_n^r}{(m_n)_rD_{n,r}^U},
\]
which is constant. Taking the logarithm gives (3.6). \(\square\)

Since \(m_n=n\lambda_n\), for \(r=o(\sqrt n)\),
\[
\log\frac{(n)_r\lambda_n^r}{(m_n)_r}
=O(r^2/n).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Thus
\[
D(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\Vert\widetilde P_{n,r})
=
\ell_n-\log D_{n,r}^U+O(r^2/n).
\tag{3.8}
\]

# 4. Tensorization: per-mark entropy is \(\ell_n/r\), not \(b_n/r\)

Let \(V_{n,r}\) be the joint law of the \(r\) marked occupancies under \(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\). Under \(\widetilde P_{n,r}\), their law is exactly
\[
(p_n^*)^{\otimes r}.
\]
Data processing gives
\[
D(V_{n,r}\Vert(p_n^*)^{\otimes r})
\le
D(\widetilde Q_{n,r}\Vert\widetilde P_{n,r}).
\tag{4.1}
\]
All one-coordinate marginals of \(V_{n,r}\) agree by symmetry; call the common marginal \(\nu_{n,r}\). The product-reference KL decomposition gives
\[
D(V_{n,r}\Vert(p_n^*)^{\otimes r})
=
\mathrm{TC}(V_{n,r})
+rD(\nu_{n,r}\Vert p_n^*),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
D(\nu_{n,r}\Vert p_n^*)
\le
\frac1r
\left(
\ell_n-\log D_{n,r}^U+O(r^2/n)
\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

This is the key improvement over the earlier Palm comparison to the original boundary size-biased law, where the entropy budget was \(b_n\). Here the reference law is the moving canonical size-biased law and the budget is only the **central local cost** \(\ell_n\).

# 5. Collision-controlled canonical tail thickness

Use the amplified cutoff \(L_n\) from (2.11). Set
\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
c_*:=\frac{\delta}{\lambda}>0.
\]
Under \(Q_n^U\), a uniformly sampled ball lies in a box larger than \(L_n\) with probability
\[
\frac{1}{m_n}
E_{Q_n^U}\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=c_*+o(1),
\tag{5.1}
\]
because the cap event itself has probability \(1-o(1)\) in the original microcanonical law.

Let
\[
C_n^U:=1-D_{n,2}^U.
\]
The cap changes fixed-ball collision probabilities by only \(o(1)\), so under no condensation
\[
C_n^U\to0.
\tag{5.2}
\]
For every \(r\), a union bound gives
\[
1-D_{n,r}^U
\le \binom r2 C_n^U.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Choose a fixed sufficiently small \(a>0\) and
\[
r_n:=\left\lfloor\frac{a}{\sqrt{C_n^U}}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Then \(r_n\to\infty\), and by choosing \(a\) small enough,
\[
D_{n,r_n}^U\ge 3/4
\tag{5.5}
\]
for all large \(n\).

Conditioning the sampled balls on distinct boxes changes the one-mark tail probability by at most
\[
\frac{1-D_{n,r_n}^U}{D_{n,r_n}^U},
\]
which is a small fixed constant when \(a\) is small. Thus, for all large \(n\),
\[
\nu_{n,r_n}(X>L_n)\ge c_0
\tag{5.6}
\]
for some fixed \(c_0=c_0(\lambda,\nu)>0\).

Define the canonical size-biased tail
\[
q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)
:=
p_n^*(X>L_n)
=
\frac{1}{\lambda_n\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)}
\sum_{L_n<k\le U_n} k\pi_k e^{t_nk}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Binary data processing and the elementary inequality
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p\Vert q)
\ge p\log(1/q)-\log2
\]
then give, from (4.2),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)}
\le
C\left(
\frac{\ell_n}{r_n}+1+\frac{r_n}{n}
\right).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
Since \(r_n=a/\sqrt{C_n^U}+O(1)\),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)}
=
O\!\left(\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+1\right).
}
\tag{5.9}
\]

This is a genuinely stronger necessary condition than the earlier boundary-Palm estimate
\[
\log\frac1{Q(L_n)}=O(b_n\sqrt{C_n}).
\]
The exact conditioning cost \(b_n\) has been replaced by the central local cost \(\ell_n\le b_n+o(1)\).

## Consequence
If along a subsequence
\[
\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}\to0,
\tag{5.10}
\]
then the canonical size-biased law has a tail bounded away from zero above the amplified physical cutoff:
\[
\boxed{
q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)\ge c_1>0.
}
\tag{5.11}
\]
Consequently
\[
\sigma_n^2
\ge
c_1\lambda_nL_n-O(1),
\tag{5.12}
\]
because the canonical size-biased mean is \(E_{p_n^*}X=E_{p_n^{\rm can}}X^2/\lambda_n\).
This is a quantitative canonical version of the mesoscopic-cloud lower bound.

# 6. A one-coordinate entropy inequality for tail first moments

There is a complementary bound that does not use ball sampling. Let \(\mu_n\) be the one-coordinate marginal of \(Q_n^U\). From the exact Pythagorean decomposition in the previous checkpoint,
\[
D(\mu_n\Vert p_n^{\rm can})\le\ell_n/n.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For any nonnegative function \(f\le U_n\), put
\[
A=E_{\mu_n}f,
\qquad B=E_{p_n^{\rm can}}f.
\]
For \(s\ge0\), convexity gives
\[
e^{sf}-1\le\frac f{U_n}(e^{sU_n}-1),
\]
so
\[
\log E_{p_n^{\rm can}}e^{sf}
\le\frac{B}{U_n}(e^{sU_n}-1).
\]
The Gibbs variational inequality yields
\[
D(\mu_n\Vert p_n^{\rm can})
\ge
sA-\frac{B}{U_n}(e^{sU_n}-1).
\]
Optimizing over \(z=sU_n\ge0\) gives
\[
\boxed{
U_nD(\mu_n\Vert p_n^{\rm can})
\ge
\Phi(A,B),
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
where
\[
\Phi(A,B)=
\begin{cases}
A\log(A/B)-A+B,&A>B,\\
0,&A\le B.
\end{cases}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\Phi(A,B)\le\frac{U_n\ell_n}{n}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Apply this to
\[
f(k)=k\mathbf1_{\{k>L_n\}}.
\]
By (2.11),
\[
A=\delta+o(1).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Therefore, whenever
\[
\frac{U_n\ell_n}{n}\to0,
\tag{6.5}
\]
we obtain the stronger first-moment equivalence
\[
\boxed{
E_{p_n^{\rm can}}[X;X>L_n]
=\delta+o(1).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Thus in the branch \(U_n\ell_n=o(n)\), not merely some canonical size-biased tail but essentially the **entire missing first moment** is already present beyond the amplified cutoff in the moving product ensemble.

This gives a clean dichotomy for every putative counterexample:

1. either
   \[
   U_n\ell_n\not=o(n),
   \tag{6.7}
   \]
   so the central local cost is at least of order \(n/U_n\); or
2. the moving canonical product law itself carries \((\lambda-\nu)+o(1)\) first moment above \(L_n\).

# 7. Direct connection to strong records of the fixed boundary law

Define
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k.
\]
Choose a fixed harmless constant \(c>0\) such that \(cq_k<1\) whenever \(q_k>0\), and define
\[
r(x):=\min_{1\le k\le x,\ q_k>0}
\frac{-\log(cq_k)}{k}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Earlier work in this campaign proved that, for every fixed \(\theta\in(0,1)\), there are infinitely many record indices \(M\) such that
\[
G_\theta(M)
:=
M\bigl(r(\theta M)-r(M)\bigr)
\to\infty.
\tag{7.2}
\]
This record theorem does **not** assume geometrically sparse support.

Fix such a record \(M\), and suppose that for the associated allocation scale one has
\[
U_n\le\theta M.
\tag{7.3}
\]
This is automatic if \(M\asymp n\), because \(U_n=o(n)\).

Put
\[
r_\theta:=r(\theta M).
\]
For every \(k\le U_n\),
\[
q_k\le c^{-1}e^{-r_\theta k}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
If \(t_n<r_\theta\), then for every \(L\le U_n\),
\[
\sum_{L<k\le U_n}q_ke^{t_nk}
\le
c^{-1}
\frac{e^{-(r_\theta-t_n)L}}{1-e^{-(r_\theta-t_n)}}.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Since \(\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)\to1\) in the boundary-layer regime, (5.9) and (7.5) imply the following deterministic-probabilistic alignment inequality.

## Theorem 7.1 — record/Palm alignment inequality
Along a no-condensation subsequence satisfying (7.3),
\[
\boxed{
(r_\theta-t_n)_+L_n
\le
C\bigl(\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+\log L_n+1\bigr).
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
t_n
\ge
r(\theta M)
-
C\frac{\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+\log L_n+1}{L_n}.
}
\tag{7.7}
\]

### Check of the logarithmic denominator term
Let \(d=r_\theta-t_n>0\). From (7.5),
\[
q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)
\le
C\frac{e^{-dL_n}}{1-e^{-d}}.
\]
If \(dL_n\le1\), (7.6) is immediate after increasing the constant. If \(dL_n>1\), then \(1-e^{-d}\ge c\min\{d,1\}\), so
\[
\log\frac1{q_n^{\rm can}(L_n)}
\ge dL_n-O(\log L_n+1),
\]
because in the only difficult case \(d<1\), \(-\log d\le\log L_n+O(1)\) once \(dL_n>1\). Combining with (5.9) gives (7.6).

This is the first point in the campaign where the central local cost \(\ell_n\), the collision scale \(C_n\), and the fixed-law multiplicative record rate appear in a single checked inequality.

# 8. What record alignment now gives, and the exact gap that remains

At a strong record \(M\),
\[
r(\theta M)-r(M)=G_\theta(M)/M.
\]
Thus (7.7) gives
\[
t_n-r(M)
\ge
\frac{G_\theta(M)}{M}
-
C\frac{\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+\log L_n+1}{L_n}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Hence a sufficient quantitative condition for the moving saddle to lie strictly above the record rate is
\[
\boxed{
G_\theta(M)
\gg
\frac{M}{L_n}
\bigl(\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+\log L_n\bigr).
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
Under (8.2),
\[
M(t_n-r(M))\to+\infty,
\tag{8.3}
\]
and therefore the record atom satisfies
\[
q_Me^{t_nM}	o\infty
\tag{8.4}
\]
(up to the fixed normalization constant \(c\)). In words: relative to the capped canonical saddle, the excluded macroscopic record scale becomes exponentially favorable.

This is **not yet a proof of Problem 18.39**. To turn (8.4) into a lower bound for the actual microcanonical probability of a giant record box, one must compare the capped partition contribution against
\[
n\pi_M\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-M).
\tag{8.5}
\]
For \(M\sim(\lambda-\nu)n\), the residual target satisfies
\[
\frac{m_n-M}{n-1}\to\nu,
\]
so Theorem 17.1 gives only
\[
-\log\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-M)=o(n).
\tag{8.6}
\]
The universal strong-record theorem supplies only \(G_\theta(M)\to\infty\), which can be much smaller than an arbitrary \(o(n)\) residual local cost. Thus the remaining obstacle is now isolated exactly:

> **Residual boundary-local gap.** Compare the boundary local cost
> \[
> R(M,n):=-\log\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-M)
> \]
> against the record/Palm advantage
> \[
> M(t_n-r(M)).
> \]
> The current universal inputs give \(R=o(n)\) and a positive record advantage under (8.2), but no universal inequality \(R=o(M(t_n-r(M)))\).

This is the same arithmetic obstruction that invalidates any silent use of a local CLT at the boundary law. It is now separated from the canonical central-local factor \(\ell_n\), which this run controls through the Palm identities.

# 9. A clean conditional closure criterion

The previous discussion yields a rigorous conditional theorem that records the exact final missing comparison rather than hiding it.

## Proposition 9.1
Fix \(\theta\in(0,1)\). Suppose there is a sequence of strong record indices \(M_j\to\infty\) and corresponding integers \(n_j\) with
\[
M_j/n_j\to\lambda-\nu,
\qquad
m_{n_j}/n_j\to\lambda,
\]
such that, under a hypothetical no-condensation cap,
\[
G_\theta(M_j)
-
C\frac{M_j}{L_{n_j}}
\bigl(\ell_{n_j}\sqrt{C_{n_j}^U}+\log L_{n_j}\bigr)
\to+\infty,
\tag{9.1}
\]
and
\[
-\log\Pr(S_{n_j-1}=m_{n_j}-M_j)
=o\!\left(M_j(t_{n_j}-r(M_j))\right).
\tag{9.2}
\]
Then the no-condensation hypothesis is false; in particular \(Y_{(1)}/n\not\to_p0\) along the full sequence.

The proof is the direct partition comparison between the capped contribution and the one-record-box contribution, using (8.3) and (9.2). This proposition is **conditional**, not claimed as a solution.

# 10. New structural dichotomy for any possible counterexample

Combining Sections 5–7, every genuine full-sequence counterexample must satisfy, along every amplified-cloud subsequence, at least one of the following obstructions:

1. **central-local obstruction:**
   \[
   \ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}\not\to0;
   \tag{10.1}
   \]
2. **large cap/local-cost obstruction:**
   \[
   U_n\ell_n\not=o(n);
   \tag{10.2}
   \]
3. **record-alignment obstruction:** for strong records \(M\asymp n\),
   \[
   G_\theta(M)
   \lesssim
   \frac{M}{L_n}
   \bigl(\ell_n\sqrt{C_n^U}+\log L_n\bigr);
   \tag{10.3}
   \]
4. **boundary-local obstruction:** even when the saddle beats the record rate, the residual atom at mean \(\nu+o(1)\) has logarithmic cost comparable to or larger than the record advantage.

A proposed counterexample must therefore simultaneously realize a nontrivial central local cost, a mesoscopic collision profile, enough near-record mass to suppress (7.7), and a badly behaved boundary local coefficient. This is substantially narrower than the original unconstrained 'many mesoscopic boxes' picture.

# 12. Failed / insufficient routes retained

1. **Generic one-big-jump theorem:** not valid for arbitrary irregular radius-one laws without subexponential/local-subexponential hypotheses.
2. **Unrestricted moving local CLT:** not assumed; the prior variance-controlled lower bound and exact entropy decomposition replace only what has actually been proved.
3. **Raw partition counting:** too coarse when record advantages diverge arbitrarily slowly.
4. **Lemma 18.31(ii) at an arbitrary cutoff:** invalid; equality is only guaranteed below an existentially slow \(\Omega_n\).
5. **Record gaps alone:** even a favorable record must still pay the boundary residual exact-local cost in (8.5).
6. **Neighboring Problems 18.40–18.42:** not this target and not used as closure.

# 13. Next attack

The most useful next step is now highly specific. For
\[
s_n=m_n-M,
\qquad s_n/(n-1)\to\nu,
\]
study the boundary local atom
\[
\Pr(S_{n-1}=s_n)
\]
**relative to the same fixed record geometry** of \(q_k=k\pi_k\). Two routes are promising:

1. **Boundary finite-template decomposition with a movable correction sector.** The prior finite-template argument controls a local atom when the moving tilted mean is interior. At the boundary mean \(\nu\), isolate a finite smoothing block and allow one additional \(o(n)\) correction variable/sector. The target is a lower bound whose logarithmic cost is expressed in the correction size rather than an arbitrary \(o(n)\).
2. **Record-local correction.** Instead of demanding exactly one box of size \(M\), sum the giant-box contribution over a short support window near \(M\) and pair each support point with a residual target. A successful averaging inequality could force at least one product
   \[
   \pi_k\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k)
   \]
   to retain the record advantage without a pointwise boundary local theorem.

Either route attacks the only step that Section 8 leaves genuinely uncontrolled.
