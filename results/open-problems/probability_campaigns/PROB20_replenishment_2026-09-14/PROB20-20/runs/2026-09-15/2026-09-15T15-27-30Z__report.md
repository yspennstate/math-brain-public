## 1. Exact target and source quantifiers

For one fixed weight sequence \(w=(w_k)\), with
\[
\Phi(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}w_kt^k,
\qquad
\nu=\lim_{t\uparrow\rho}\frac{t\Phi'(t)}{\Phi(t)},
\]
Janson's Problem 18.39 asks whether, when
\[
0<\nu<\lambda,
\qquad
m_n/n\to\lambda,
\]
the largest box can satisfy
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0
\]
along the **full admissible sequence**. Janson explicitly states that Example 18.36 gives this only on a subsequence and conjectures that it is impossible for the full sequence; more strongly, he conjectures some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and some subsequence on which
\[
Y_{(1)}\ge\varepsilon n
\]
w.h.p.

Primary source: S. Janson, arXiv:1112.0510, Problem 18.39, current arXiv HTML lines 3539–3541.

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, write
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu.
\]
The normalized radius is one, and
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
with iid \(\xi_i\sim\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\).

Two source facts are used with their exact hypotheses:

1. **Theorem 17.1:** in the normalized boundary case,
   \[
   \frac1n\log\Pr(S_n=m_n)\to0.
   \]
2. **Lemma 18.31:** for every \(K_n\to\infty\), the mass above \(K_n\) is at most
   \[
   (\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
   \]
   Equality is guaranteed only for cutoffs below an existentially slow \(\Omega_n\); the argument below does not assume equality at an arbitrary cutoff.

A fresh bounded search this run found no exact later resolution of Problem 18.39. Standard one-big-jump results such as Denisov–Dieker–Shneer require subexponential/local-subexponential-type hypotheses and therefore do not cover the full arbitrary irregular fixed-law problem.

# 4. New exact multiband entropy inequality

The previous report proved a one-band KL lower bound. The following additive version controls arbitrarily many disjoint scales simultaneously.

Let \(\mu\) and \(\pi\) be probability laws on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Let
\[
I_1,\ldots,I_s
\]
be pairwise disjoint finite subsets, and choose integers \(L_j\) such that
\[
k\le L_j\qquad(k\in I_j).
\]
Define the mean mass in each band under \(\mu\) and \(\pi\):
\[
a_j:=\sum_{k\in I_j}k\mu_k,
\qquad
Q_j:=\sum_{k\in I_j}k\pi_k.
\]
Put
\[
\Phi(a,Q):=
\begin{cases}
a\log(a/Q)-a+Q,&a>Q,\\0,&a\le Q.
\end{cases}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — additive multiband KL cost
\[
\boxed{
D(\mu\Vert\pi)
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac1{L_j}\Phi(a_j,Q_j).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof
If \(D(\mu\Vert\pi)=\infty\), there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, for arbitrary \(h_j\ge0\), Gibbs' variational inequality with
\[
f(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}h_jk\mathbf1_{\{k\in I_j\}}
\]
gives
\[
D(\mu\Vert\pi)
\ge
\sum_jh_ja_j
-
\log\mathbb E_\pi e^{f(X)}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Because the bands are disjoint,
\[
\mathbb E_\pi e^{f(X)}
=
1+
\sum_j\sum_{k\in I_j}\pi_k(e^{h_jk}-1).
\]
For \(k\le L_j\), convexity gives the chord bound
\[
e^{h_jk}-1
\le
\frac{k}{L_j}(e^{h_jL_j}-1),
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E_\pi e^{f(X)}
\le
1+
\sum_j\frac{Q_j}{L_j}(e^{h_jL_j}-1)
\le
\exp\left\{
\sum_j\frac{Q_j}{L_j}(e^{h_jL_j}-1)
\right\}.
\]
Hence
\[
D(\mu\Vert\pi)
\ge
\sum_j
\left[
h_ja_j-
\frac{Q_j}{L_j}(e^{h_jL_j}-1)
\right].
\]
The variables \(h_j\) now separate. Setting \(z_j=h_jL_j\) and optimizing each term over \(z_j\ge0\) gives exactly \(L_j^{-1}\Phi(a_j,Q_j)\). \(\square\)

### Check
A deterministic finite-model checker was run for 5,000 pseudorandom pairs \((\pi,\mu)\) on \(\{0,\ldots,40\}\), with five disjoint bands. Every trial satisfied (4.1). The smallest observed numerical margin was
\[
D(\mu\Vert\pi)-\sum_jL_j^{-1}\Phi(a_j,Q_j)
=0.16058137427877145>0.
\]
The checker is archived separately under `artifacts/` after this report.

The theorem is exact; the computation is only a bookkeeping check.

# 5. New diagonal amplification lemma

The key idea is that the truncated-overload rate (3.3) diverges after multiplication by its cutoff, while the exact conditioning cost only fixes the much smaller base scale \(n/b_n\). One can therefore enlarge the cutoff by a deterministic factor tending to infinity and still make the truncated-overload event exponentially negligible relative to the conditioning event.

Write
\[
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]
For \(j\ge1\), set
\[
\varepsilon_j:=\frac{\delta}{4j},
\qquad
\mu_j:=\nu+\varepsilon_j.
\]
For each fixed \(j\), define
\[
g_j(L):=LJ_L(\mu_j).
\]
By (3.3),
\[
g_j(L)\to\infty
\qquad(L\to\infty).
\]
Also put
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,
\qquad
Q_{>}(K):=\sum_{k>K}q_k.
\]
Finite mean gives \(Q_{>}(K)\to0\).

## Lemma 5.1 — simultaneous diagonal choice
There exist deterministic integers \(R_n\to\infty\) such that, with
\[
L_n:=\lfloor R_nK_n\rfloor,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
R_n=o(b_n),
\qquad
\frac{b_n}{R_n}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{g_n(L_n)}{R_n}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{-\log Q_{>}(K_n)}{R_n}\to\infty,
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
where \(g_n=g_j\) on the block on which \(R_n=j\), and the corresponding \(\varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_j\to0\).

In fact the construction can ensure, on the block \(R_n=j\),
\[
b_n\ge j^4,
\qquad
g_j(L_n)\ge j^3,
\qquad
Q_{>}(K_n)\le e^{-j^3}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof
Fix \(j\). Because
\[
b_n\to\infty,
\qquad K_n\to\infty,
\qquad g_j(L)\to\infty,
\qquad Q_{>}(K)\to0,
\]
there is \(N_j\) such that all three inequalities in (5.2) hold for every \(n\ge N_j\), after increasing \(N_j\) if needed. Choose \(N_1<N_2<\cdots\), and for
\[
N_j\le n<N_{j+1}
\]
set
\[
R_n=j,
\qquad
\varepsilon_n=\varepsilon_j,
\qquad
L_n=\lfloor jK_n\rfloor.
\]
Then \(R_n\to\infty\), and
\[
\frac{R_n}{b_n}\le j^{-3}\to0,
\qquad
\frac{b_n}{R_n}\ge j^3\to\infty.
\]
Moreover
\[
\frac{g_n(L_n)}{R_n}\ge j^2\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{-\log Q_{>}(K_n)}{R_n}\ge j^2\to\infty.
\]
\(\square\)

A useful geometric consequence is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{L_n}{n}
\sim\frac{R_n}{b_n}	o0,
\qquad
\frac{L_n}{K_n}=R_n\to\infty.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Thus \(L_n\) is still sublinear, but is an unbounded multiplicative factor above the original \(n/b_n\) scale.

# 6. New universal amplified-condensate theorem

Define the total mass in boxes above \(L_n\):
\[
T_n^{>L}:=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}.
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — essentially all missing mass lies above an amplified sublinear scale
For the diagonal sequence \(L_n\) from Lemma 5.1,
\[
\boxed{
T_n^{>L}
=(\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Furthermore, if
\[
N_n^{>L}:=\#\{i:Y_i>L_n\},
\]
then deterministically
\[
\boxed{
N_n^{>L}
\le
\frac{m_n}{L_n}
=(\lambda+o(1))\frac{b_n}{R_n}
=o(b_n).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof
First prove the lower bound in (6.1). On the event
\[
T_n^{>L}
<
(\delta-2\varepsilon_n)n,
\]
the contribution from boxes at most \(L_n\) satisfies
\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le L_n\}}
>
(\nu+\varepsilon_n)n
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), because \(m_n/n\to\lambda=\nu+\delta\).

Under the unconditioned iid law, Chernoff's bound gives
\[
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{L_n,i}
\ge
(\nu+\varepsilon_n)n
\right)
\le
\exp\{-nJ_{L_n}(\nu+\varepsilon_n)\}.
\]
Divide by the conditioning probability \(p_n=e^{-b_n}\). Since
\[
\frac{nJ_{L_n}(\nu+\varepsilon_n)}{b_n}
=
\frac{n}{L_n}\frac{g_n(L_n)}{b_n}
=
(1+o(1))\frac{g_n(L_n)}{R_n}
\to\infty,
\]
the conditional probability of the displayed low-tail-mass event tends to zero. Therefore
\[
T_n^{>L}
\ge
(\delta-2\varepsilon_n)n
\qquad\text{w.h.p.}
\]
Since \(\varepsilon_n\to0\), this is
\[
T_n^{>L}\ge\delta n-o_p(n).
\]

For the opposite inequality, \(L_n\to\infty\), so Janson's Lemma 18.31(i), applied directly to this deterministic sequence, gives
\[
T_n^{>L}\le\delta n+o_p(n).
\]
Combining the two bounds proves (6.1).

Finally, every box counted by \(N_n^{>L}\) contains more than \(L_n\) balls, so
\[
N_n^{>L}L_n<m_n,
\]
which yields (6.2). \(\square\)

### Interpretation
This is stronger than the previously archived \(n/b_n\) statement. There is always a deterministic sublinear cutoff satisfying
\[
L_n\gg n/b_n
\]
for which **all** of the missing \((\lambda-\nu)n\) mass is already above the cutoff, yet it is carried by only
\[
o(b_n)
\]
boxes.

This theorem is universal: no no-condensation assumption has been made.

# 7. New scarce-bridge theorem

The diagonal construction simultaneously makes the fixed boundary law extremely sparse between the old and new condensate scales.

Define the bridge
\[
B_n:=(K_n,L_n]
\]
and its size-biased boundary mass
\[
Q_n^{\rm bridge}
:=
\sum_{K_n<k\le L_n}k\pi_k.
\]

## Theorem 7.1 — the condensate jumps across a boundary-law desert
Along the same deterministic sequence,
\[
\boxed{
Q_n^{\rm bridge}
\le Q_{>}(K_n)
\le e^{-R_n^3}
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\) on the diagonal blocks, while under the conditioned law
\[
\boxed{
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{K_n<Y_i\le L_n\}}
=o_p(n).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof
The boundary-law statement is exactly the third condition in (5.2).

For the conditioned bridge mass, let
\[
T_n^{>K}:=
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>K_n\}}.
\]
Janson's Lemma 18.31(i) gives
\[
T_n^{>K}\le\delta n+o_p(n).
\]
Theorem 6.1 gives
\[
T_n^{>L}=\delta n+o_p(n).
\]
Since the bridge mass is
\[
T_n^{>K}-T_n^{>L}\ge0,
\]
it is \(o_p(n)\). \(\square\)

Thus the conditional allocation does not gradually spread the missing mass through all scales above \(n/b_n\): after a suitable deterministic amplification, essentially no linear mass remains in the entire bridge \((K_n,L_n]\), even though \(L_n/K_n\to\infty\).

# 8. Consequence for any hypothetical full-sequence counterexample

Assume now, only for this section, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Then Theorem 6.1 forces a sharply constrained intermediate cloud.

## Corollary 8.1 — necessary intermediate cloud
Under (8.1),
\[
\boxed{
N_n^{>L}\to_p\infty,
\qquad
\frac{N_n^{>L}}{b_n}\to_p0,
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
and these boxes carry
\[
(\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n)
\]
balls.

### Proof
The upper relation follows from (6.2). For the lower relation, Theorem 6.1 gives, w.h.p.,
\[
T_n^{>L}\ge\frac\delta2n.
\]
But
\[
T_n^{>L}
\le
N_n^{>L}Y_{(1)},
\]
so
\[
N_n^{>L}
\ge
\frac{\delta n/2}{Y_{(1)}}.
\]
Under (8.1), \(Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0\), hence the right side diverges in probability. \(\square\)

This is a substantially narrower necessary mechanism than merely saying "many mesoscopic boxes": any full counterexample must organize the entire missing mass into a cloud whose multiplicity diverges but is asymptotically negligible relative to the exact conditioning cost \(b_n\):
\[
\boxed{1\ll_p N_n\ll b_n.}
\]

The boxes in that cloud are all larger than
\[
L_n=R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),
\qquad R_n\to\infty,
\]
yet the maximum remains \(o_p(n)\).

# 9. Multiband profile constraint for the remaining cloud

Let \(\mu_n\) denote the one-box marginal under the conditioned law. The exact KL budget from the previous verified report is
\[
D(\mu_n\Vert\pi)\le\frac{b_n}{n}.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Partition a high-probability no-condensation capture interval into dyadic bands
\[
I_{j,n}:=(2^{j-1}L_n,2^jL_n],
\]
truncated at some deterministic \(U_n=o(n)\) for which
\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n)\to1.
\]
Define
\[
a_{j,n}:=\sum_{k\in I_{j,n}}k\mu_n(k),
\qquad
Q_{j,n}:=\sum_{k\in I_{j,n}}k\pi_k.
\]
By Corollary 8.1 and the cap, the total conditioned mean mass in these bands is
\[
\sum_j a_{j,n}=\delta+o(1).
\tag{9.2}
\]
Theorem 4.1 and (9.1) yield the exact necessary profile constraint
\[
\boxed{
\sum_j
\frac{n}{2^jL_n}
\Phi(a_{j,n},Q_{j,n})
\le b_n.
}
\tag{9.3}
\]
Equivalently, since \(L_n\sim R_nn/b_n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_j2^{-j}\Phi(a_{j,n},Q_{j,n})
\le (1+o(1))R_n.
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

This is the current cleanest deterministic target. A counterexample must allocate a fixed total mean mass \(\delta\) across a diverging sequence of scales, while the additive Poisson-KL costs in (9.4) remain only \(O(R_n)\), the number of exceptional boxes is \(o(b_n)\), and the base size-biased law is already essentially empty below \(L_n\).

# 12. Next concrete attack

The next attack should work directly with the cloud identified in Corollary 8.1 rather than with all partitions of the full excess.

For a cutoff \(L_n\) as above, define constrained size-biased convolution coefficients
\[
C_r(s;L,U)
:=
\sum_{\substack{k_1+\cdots+k_r=s\\L<k_i\le U}}
\prod_{i=1}^{r}q_{k_i},
\qquad q_k=k\pi_k.
\]
A hypothetical counterexample needs only
\[
r=N_n=o(b_n)
\]
large coordinates, rather than \(O(b_n)\), and the entire bridge below \(L_n\) has already been removed at linear scale. This should reduce the combinatorial entropy enough to revisit the universal strong-record inequality without the old \(e^{O(\sqrt M)}\) loss.

The most promising specific goal is:

> Find a record subsequence \(M\) and a fixed \(\theta<1\) for which the total constrained convolution weight of all \(r=o(b_n)\) decompositions of a mass \(M+o(M)\) into parts \(\le\theta M\) is \(o(q_M)\), uniformly over the allowed correction mass.

The correction must be retained rather than replaced by an unproved local limit. If this estimate is true, the remaining cloud collapses to a macroscopic record box. If false, an optimizer gives a concrete multiscale counterexample template.
