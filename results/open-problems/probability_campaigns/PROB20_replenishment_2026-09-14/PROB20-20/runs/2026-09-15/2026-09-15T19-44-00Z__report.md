# 3. High-probability cap and exact canonical representation

Assume, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then there is a deterministic sequence
\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad U_n=o(n),
\tag{3.2}
\]
such that
\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Let
\[
\lambda_n=m_n/n,
\qquad
\Phi_{U_n}(t)=\sum_{k\le U_n}\pi_ke^{tk},
\]
and let \(t_n>0\) solve
\[
\frac{\sum_{k\le U_n}k\pi_ke^{t_nk}}{\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)}
=
\lambda_n.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Define the moving canonical law
\[
p_n^{\rm can}(k)
=
\frac{\pi_ke^{t_nk}}{\Phi_{U_n}(t_n)}
\mathbf1_{\{k\le U_n\}}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Let \(P_n^{\rm can}=(p_n^{\rm can})^{\otimes n}\), and define
\[
a_n:=P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Let \(Q_n^U\) denote the original iid law conditioned on both
\[
S_n=m_n,\qquad \max_i\xi_i\le U_n.
\]
Because the likelihood ratio in (3.5) is constant on the hyperplane \(S_n=m_n\), one has the **exact identity of probability laws**
\[
\boxed{
Q_n^U
=
P_n^{\rm can}(\,\cdot\mid S_n=m_n).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
No asymptotics or local theorem is used here.

Moreover, if
\[
I_n:=\lambda_nt_n-\log\Phi_{U_n}(t_n),
\]
then
\[
\Pr_\pi(S_n=m_n,\max_i\xi_i\le U_n)
=e^{-nI_n}a_n.
\tag{3.8}
\]
By (3.3),
\[
\boxed{
b_n=nI_n-\log a_n+o(1).}
\tag{3.9}
\]

# 4. Main new theorem: exact Pythagorean decomposition of the local cost

Let \(\mu_n\) be the one-coordinate marginal of \(Q_n^U\). Because \(Q_n^U\) is exchangeable and supported on \(S_n=m_n\),
\[
E_{\mu_n}X=\lambda_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — exact local-cost decomposition

Define the total correlation
\[
\mathrm{TC}(Q_n^U)
:=
D\!\left(Q_n^U\middle\|\mu_n^{\otimes n}\right).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
-\log a_n
=
\mathrm{TC}(Q_n^U)
+nD\!\left(\mu_n\middle\|p_n^{\rm can}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(\mu_n\middle\|p_n^{\rm can}\right)
\le\frac{-\log a_n}{n}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

By (3.7), conditioning the product canonical law on \(S_n=m_n\) gives
\[
D(Q_n^U\|P_n^{\rm can})=-\log a_n.
\tag{4.4}
\]
For any exchangeable \(Q\) with one-coordinate marginal \(\mu\) and any product reference \(p^{\otimes n}\), the chain-rule identity
\[
D(Q\|p^{\otimes n})
=
D(Q\|\mu^{\otimes n})
+nD(\mu\|p)
\tag{4.5}
\]
is exact: expand
\[
\log\frac{dQ}{dp^{\otimes n}}
=
\log\frac{dQ}{d\mu^{\otimes n}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^n\log\frac{d\mu}{dp}(X_i)
\]
and average. Apply (4.5) with \(Q=Q_n^U\) and \(p=p_n^{\rm can}\). \(\square\)

### Equivalent boundary-law Pythagorean identity

Because \(\mu_n\) is supported on \([0,U_n]\) and has mean exactly \(\lambda_n\),
\[
\log\frac{p_n^{\rm can}(k)}{\pi_k}
=t_nk-\log\Phi_{U_n}(t_n).
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_n\|\pi)
=
I_n+D(\mu_n\|p_n^{\rm can}).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Hence the canonical exponential tilt is the exact information projection of the capped microcanonical marginal onto the mean constraint. The entire noncanonical one-box error is literally one summand of the exact central local cost.

# 5. Consequence: universal total-variation equivalence of one-box ensembles

From the preceding checkpoint (2.2),
\[
-\log a_n\le C\sigma_n^2+C\log n+C.
\]
Because the canonical variable lies in \([0,U_n]\) and has bounded mean \(\lambda_n\),
\[
\sigma_n^2\le E X^2\le U_nEX=O(U_n)=o(n).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{-\log a_n}{n}\to0.
\tag{5.1}
\]
By (4.3) and Pinsker,
\[
\boxed{
\|\mu_n-p_n^{\rm can}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{-\log a_n}{2n}}
\le
C\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_n^2+\log n}{n}}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a genuinely moving-support equivalence-of-ensembles statement: the **entire** one-box marginal, not merely every fixed occupancy, is asymptotically close in total variation to the changing truncated canonical law. It is obtained without a triangular-array local CLT.

This does not by itself transfer first moments on the whole support, because the support diameter \(U_n\) also grows. For any bounded observable \(f_n\), however,
\[
|E_{Q_n^U}f_n(Y_1)-E_{p_n^{\rm can}}f_n(X)|
\le
2\|f_n\|_\infty
\|\mu_n-p_n^{\rm can}\|_{\rm TV}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
In particular all moving tail **probabilities** transfer uniformly, even when their thresholds depend on \(n\).

# 6. Growing-block Gibbs conditioning without a local CLT

The previous result extends to blocks whose size tends to infinity.

Let \(Q_{n,r}^U\) denote the marginal of \(Q_n^U\) on any fixed \(r\) coordinates. Exchangeability makes the choice irrelevant.

## Theorem 6.1 — growing-block relative-entropy bound
For every \(1\le r\le n\),
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(Q_{n,r}^U\middle\|(p_n^{\rm can})^{\otimes r}\right)
\le
\frac rn(-\log a_n).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Consequently, whenever
\[
r_n\frac{-\log a_n}{n}\to0,
\tag{6.2}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
\left\|Q_{n,r_n}^U-(p_n^{\rm can})^{\otimes r_n}\right\|_{\rm TV}
\to0.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Using (2.2), it is sufficient that
\[
\boxed{
r_n(\sigma_n^2+\log n)=o(n).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

All variables are supported on the finite set \(\{0,\dots,U_n\}\), so Shannon entropies are finite. Let \(H_r\) be the entropy of an \(r\)-coordinate marginal of \(Q_n^U\), and \(H_n\) the full entropy. Shearer's entropy inequality, applied to all \(r\)-subsets, gives
\[
H_r\ge\frac rn H_n.
\tag{6.5}
\]
The cross-entropy term against the product reference scales exactly by \(r/n\):
\[
E_{Q_{n,r}^U}\sum_{j=1}^r[-\log p_n^{\rm can}(Y_j)]
=
\frac rn
E_{Q_n^U}\sum_{j=1}^n[-\log p_n^{\rm can}(Y_j)].
\]
Subtracting the entropy terms yields
\[
D(Q_{n,r}^U\|(p_n^{\rm can})^{\otimes r})
\le
\frac rn D(Q_n^U\|P_n^{\rm can})
=
\frac rn(-\log a_n).
\]
Pinsker gives (6.3). \(\square\)

### Interpretation

A hypothetical full-sequence counterexample cannot rely on strong finite-dimensional dependence at the mesoscopic saddle. Up to any block size
\[
r_n=o\!\left(\frac{n}{-\log a_n}\right),
\]
the capped microcanonical model is asymptotically indistinguishable in total variation from independent samples from the moving canonical tilt.

The obstruction to Problem 18.39 is therefore genuinely a **larger-scale collective phenomenon**: it must occur beyond the canonical block-equivalence window.

# 7. New universal lower bound on the central local cost

The finite smoothing template used in the preceding checkpoint also gives the opposite, anti-concentration side.

## Lemma 7.1
There is a constant \(C<\infty\), depending only on the fixed original boundary law, such that for every sufficiently large moving truncated saddle,
\[
\boxed{
a_n=P_n^{\rm can}(S_n=m_n)\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}.}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
-\log a_n\ge\frac12\log n-O(1).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

As in the previous checkpoint, every sufficiently large canonical law contains a fixed nondegenerate finite lattice law \(\rho\) with a fixed mixture weight \(\varepsilon>0\):
\[
X\overset d=
\begin{cases}
Z,&I=1,\\
W,&I=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad I\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon),\quad Z\sim\rho.
\]
For \(n\) iid copies let \(N=\sum I_i\). Chernoff's elementary binomial bound gives
\[
\Pr(N<\varepsilon n/2)\le e^{-cn}.
\]
Condition on \(N\ge\varepsilon n/2\) and on the sum of all residual \(W\)-variables. Convolution cannot increase maximal point mass, and the maximum atom of an \(N\)-fold sum of the fixed nondegenerate finite lattice law \(\rho\) is at most \(C/\sqrt N\). This last bound follows, for example, by the elementary Fourier estimate for a fixed aperiodic finite-support law (or directly from Stirling after a finite-support decomposition). Thus every point of the full sum has probability at most
\[
e^{-cn}+C/\sqrt n.
\]
Apply this to the central point \(m_n\). \(\square\)

Combining with the preceding checkpoint,
\[
\boxed{
\frac12\log n-O(1)
\le
-\log a_n
\le
C\sigma_n^2+C\log n+C.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Together with (3.9), every hypothetical no-condensation sequence therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{
b_n
=
nI_n+\ell_n+o(1),
\qquad
\frac12\log n-O(1)\le\ell_n\le C\sigma_n^2+C\log n+C,
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
where
\[
\ell_n:=-\log a_n.
\]

So the exact conditioning cost has two rigorously separated nonnegative pieces:

1. the canonical large-deviation cost \(nI_n\);
2. the exact fixed-sum dependence/local cost \(\ell_n\), which is simultaneously total correlation plus one-box projection error.

# 8. Optional Gaussian branch under a genuinely checked local hypothesis

The full problem does **not** justify a moving local CLT. There is, however, one clean regime where it can be proved directly and safely.

## Proposition 8.1 — small-cap triangular local CLT
Assume in addition that the high-probability cap can be chosen so that
\[
\boxed{U_n^2\log n=o(n).}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Then the fixed smoothing component makes the moving lattice laws uniformly strongly aperiodic, and a direct Fourier argument gives
\[
\boxed{
a_n
=
\frac{d}{\sqrt{2\pi n\sigma_n^2}}(1+o(1)),}
\tag{8.2}
\]
where \(d\) is the common lattice span.

### Proof sketch with the hypotheses exposed

Let \(\psi_n(\theta)=E\exp(i\theta(X-\lambda_n))\) under the moving canonical law.

For \(|\theta|\le c/U_n\), Taylor expansion and
\[
E|X-\lambda_n|^3
\le C U_n\sigma_n^2
\]
give
\[
\log\psi_n(\theta)
=-\frac12\sigma_n^2\theta^2
+O(U_n\sigma_n^2|\theta|^3).
\]
At the central Fourier scale \(\theta=u/(\sqrt n\sigma_n)\), the error is
\[
O\left(\frac{U_n|u|^3}{\sqrt n\sigma_n}\right)=o(1)
\]
because the fixed smoothing template gives \(\inf_n\sigma_n>0\) and (8.1) implies \(U_n=o(\sqrt n)\).

For \(c/U_n\le|\theta|\le\theta_0\), the fixed finite smoothing component gives
\[
|\psi_n(\theta)|\le e^{-c_1\theta^2}.
\]
Condition (8.1) makes
\[
n/U_n^2\gg\log n,
\]
so this entire Fourier region is negligible even relative to the Gaussian main term. For \(\theta_0\le|\theta|\le\pi/d\), the fixed template and its full span give a uniform bound \(|\psi_n(\theta)|\le1-c_2\). Fourier inversion then yields (8.2).

This proposition is deliberately **not** invoked outside (8.1).

Under this additional branch,
\[
\boxed{
\ell_n
=
\frac12\log(2\pi n\sigma_n^2)-\log d+o(1),
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
so the no-condensation cost decomposition becomes
\[
\boxed{
b_n
=
nI_n+rac12\log(2\pi n\sigma_n^2)-\log d+o(1).}
\tag{8.4}
\]
This cleanly isolates the local factor whenever the cap is small enough for a triangular local CLT to be actually proved rather than assumed.

# 9. What this changes about the remaining problem

The previous reports treated the local coefficient as a dangerous moving arithmetic factor. The current results show that, under a hypothetical full counterexample:

- after a high-probability sublinear cap, the model is **exactly** a canonical product tilt conditioned on its mean;
- the complete central local cost \(\ell_n\) is exactly
  \[
  \mathrm{TC}(Q_n^U)+nD(\mu_n\|p_n^{\rm can});
  \]
- the full moving one-box marginal is asymptotically canonical in total variation;
- blocks of size \(r_n=o(n/\ell_n)\) are asymptotically iid canonical;
- \(\ell_n\) is never smaller than \(\tfrac12\log n-O(1)\) and never larger than the previous variance-controlled bound;
- when the cap satisfies the explicit small-cap condition (8.1), \(\ell_n\) has the exact Gaussian asymptotic (8.3).

Thus an eventual counterexample cannot be blamed on uncontrolled finite-dimensional conditioning or on an arbitrary local coefficient. It would have to emerge only after a growing block exceeds the entropy scale
\[
\boxed{n/\ell_n.}
\]
This is a sharper structural target for the next record-scale attack.
