## 2. Normalized iid representation and notation

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu.
\]
The normalized generating radius is one, and
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\).

Write
\[
p_n:=\Pr(S_n=m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=-\log p_n,
\qquad
\lambda_n:=m_n/n,
\qquad
\delta:=\lambda-\nu>0.
\]
The weak law gives \(b_n\to\infty\), while Janson's Theorem 17.1 gives
\[
\boxed{b_n=o(n).}
\]

The preceding checkpoint additionally proved the fixed-template convolution smoothing estimate
\[
\boxed{
\sup_s\Pr(S_n=s)\le C_\pi n^{-1/2},
\qquad
\sup_s|\Pr(S_n=s+1)-\Pr(S_n=s)|\le C_\pi n^{-1}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{b_n\ge\tfrac12\log n-O(1).}
\tag{2.2}
\]
This elementary consequence of (2.1) is recorded explicitly here because it is useful when comparing all later \(b_n\)-scale estimates.

# 3. A many-site version of the locally cheap macroscopic window

The preceding checkpoint selected one high local atom in a deterministic LLN window and thickened it into one interval. The same smoothing input actually forces **at least order \(\sqrt n\) distinct locally cheap residual sites**, even if the LLN window is much wider than \(\sqrt n\).

Choose deterministic \(W_n\to\infty\), \(W_n=o(n)\), with
\[
\Pr(|S_n-\nu n|\le W_n)\ge\frac34
\tag{3.1}
\]
for all large \(n\). Let
\[
J_n:=\{s\in\mathbb Z:|s-\nu n|\le W_n\}
\]
and define the level set
\[
A_n:=\left\{s\in J_n:
\Pr(S_n=s)\ge\frac1{16W_n}
\right\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — at least \(c\sqrt n\) locally cheap central sites

There is \(c_\pi>0\) such that
\[
\boxed{|A_n|\ge c_\pi\sqrt n}
\tag{3.3}
\]
for all large \(n\).

### Proof
The total probability in \(J_n\setminus A_n\) is at most
\[
\frac{|J_n|}{16W_n}
\le\frac18+o(1).
\]
By (3.1),
\[
\Pr(S_n\in A_n)\ge\frac58-o(1).
\]
By the anti-concentration half of (2.1), every single atom has mass at most \(C_\pi/\sqrt n\). Hence
\[
|A_n|\frac{C_\pi}{\sqrt n}
\ge\frac58-o(1),
\]
which proves (3.3). \(\square\)

Apply this to \(S_{n-1}\), and define the corresponding macroscopic candidate set
\[
G_n:=\{m_n-s:s\in A_{n-1}\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Because \(s=\nu n+o(n)\) uniformly on \(A_{n-1}\),
\[
\boxed{
|G_n|\ge c_\pi\sqrt n,
\qquad
k=(\lambda-\nu)n+o(n)
\quad\text{uniformly for }k\in G_n.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k)\ge\frac1{16W_{n-1}}
\quad(k\in G_n).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Let
\[
N_n(G):=\#\{i:Y_i\in G_n\}.
\]
The exact one-coordinate conditioning identity gives
\[
E[N_n(G)\mid S_n=m_n]
=
\frac{n}{p_n}
\sum_{k\in G_n}
\pi_k\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k).
\tag{3.7}
\]
Therefore
\[
E[N_n(G)\mid S_n=m_n]
\ge
\frac{n e^{b_n}}{16W_{n-1}}
\sum_{k\in G_n}\pi_k.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Under a hypothetical full-sequence counterexample \(Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0\), every \(k\in G_n\) is eventually at least \(\delta n/2\), so
\[
P(N_n(G)>0\mid S_n=m_n)\to0.
\]
At the same time \(N_n(G)\) is deterministically bounded by \(2\lambda/\delta+O(1)\). Hence its expectation tends to zero, and (3.8) yields the new necessary condition
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in G_n}\pi_k
=
o\!\left(\frac{W_{n-1}}{n}e^{-b_n}\right).
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
Writing
\[
q_k:=k\pi_k,
\]
and using \(k\asymp n\) uniformly on \(G_n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\in G_n}q_k
=o\!\left(W_{n-1}e^{-b_n}\right).
}
\tag{3.10}
\]
Thus a full counterexample must create a moving **many-site macroscopic cost trench**: at least \(c\sqrt n\) distinct candidate giant sizes near \(\delta n\) all lie in a set whose *total size-biased boundary mass* is at most \(o(W_ne^{-b_n})\).

This strengthens the preceding single-interval trench when \(W_n\gg\sqrt n\): the locally cheap residual sites need not form one long interval, but there are universally at least order \(\sqrt n\) of them.

# 4. Exact Palm transport decomposition of the conditioning cost

The next identity is independent of the level-set construction and is exact for every \(n\).

Normalize the size-biased boundary law
\[
\bar q_k:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},
\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Since \(\nu>0\), \(\bar q=(\bar q_k)\) is a probability law (with \(\bar q_0=0\)).

Let
\[
r_n(k):=\Pr(S_{n-1}=m_n-k),
\qquad k\ge0,
\]
and
\[
c_n:=\sum_{k\ge0}r_n(k)=\Pr(S_{n-1}\le m_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]
Because \(m_n/n\to\lambda>\nu\), the weak law gives
\[
c_n\to1.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Define the **residual-candidate law**
\[
R_n(k):=\frac{r_n(k)}{c_n},
\qquad k\ge0.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Thus if an unconditioned \((n-1)\)-box sum is drawn and conditioned only to lie below \(m_n\), \(R_n\) is the law of the amount \(m_n-S_{n-1}\) that would have to be placed in one additional box.

Now let \(B_n\) be the occupancy of the box containing a uniformly sampled ball in the **conditioned** allocation. Its law is
\[
\mu_n(k)
=
\frac{n k}{m_n}\Pr(Y_1=k\mid S_n=m_n)
=
\frac{q_k r_n(k)}{\lambda_n p_n}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\mu_n(k)
=
\frac{\bar q_k R_n(k)}{Z_n},
\qquad
Z_n:=\frac{\lambda_n p_n}{\nu c_n}.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus the conditioned random-ball occupancy is obtained from the residual-candidate law by an exact multiplicative tilt by the fixed size-biased boundary masses \(\bar q_k\).

## Theorem 4.1 — exact transport/coding split

Define
\[
H_n^{(q)}:=E_{\mu_n}\!\left[-\log\bar q_{B_n}\right].
\tag{4.7}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 b_n
=
D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)
+
H_n^{(q)}
+
\log\frac{\lambda_n}{\nu c_n}.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

### Proof
From (4.6),
\[
\log\frac{\mu_n(k)}{R_n(k)}
=
\log\bar q_k-\log Z_n.
\]
Taking \(\mu_n\)-expectations,
\[
D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)
=
-H_n^{(q)}-\log Z_n.
\]
Since
\[
-\log Z_n
=b_n+\log\frac{\nu c_n}{\lambda_n},
\]
rearrangement gives (4.8). \(\square\)

No asymptotic theorem, local limit estimate, or tail regularity is used in (4.8).

Interpretation:

- \(D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)\) is the **transport cost** required to move the natural residual candidate \(m_n-S_{n-1}\), which is macroscopically near \(\delta n\), to the actual size of the box seen by a random ball under the conditioned allocation.
- \(H_n^{(q)}\) is the **fixed-law coding cost** of the occupied scale under the size-biased boundary law.

The exact point-conditioning cost \(b_n\) is their sum, up to the bounded normalization term in (4.8).

# 5. Under full no-condensation, both parts of the split must diverge

Assume now, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

## Theorem 5.1 — transport cost diverges

For every fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)\),
\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)\to\infty.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
More quantitatively,
\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)
\ge
(1-o(1))
\log\frac{1}{\Pr(S_{n-1}\ge m_n-\varepsilon n)}
-O(1).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof
Let
\[
E_n:=\{k\le\varepsilon n\}.
\]
Since \(B_n\le Y_{(1)}\), (5.1) gives
\[
\mu_n(E_n)\to1.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
R_n(E_n)
=
\frac{\Pr(m_n-\varepsilon n\le S_{n-1}\le m_n)}{c_n}
\le
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}\ge m_n-\varepsilon n)}{c_n}
\to0,
\]
because
\[
\frac{m_n-\varepsilon n}{n-1}\to\lambda-\varepsilon>\nu.
\]
Binary data processing for relative entropy gives (5.2). The standard binary bound
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p\Vert q)\ge p\log(1/q)-\log2
\]
with \(p=1-o(1)\), together with \(c_n\to1\), gives (5.3). \(\square\)

## Theorem 5.2 — fixed-law coding cost also diverges

The previously archived amplified-cloud theorem gives a deterministic scale \(L_n\to\infty\), \(L_n=o(n)\), such that
\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=(\lambda-\nu)n+o_p(n).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Consequently
\[
\mu_n(B_n>L_n)	o\frac{\lambda-\nu}{\lambda}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Since \(\bar q\) is a fixed probability mass function,
\[
u_n:=\sup_{k>L_n}\bar q_k\to0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
H_n^{(q)}
\ge
\left(\frac{\lambda-\nu}{\lambda}+o(1)\right)
\log\frac1{u_n}
\to\infty.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence a hypothetical full counterexample forces the exact conditioning cost to split into **two simultaneously divergent mechanisms**:
\[
\boxed{
 b_n
=
\underbrace{D(\mu_n\Vert R_n)}_{\longrightarrow\infty}
+
\underbrace{H_n^{(q)}}_{\longrightarrow\infty}
+O(1).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
This rules out any route in which the full \(b_n\)-cost is asymptotically attributed only to a bad residual local coefficient or only to sparse fixed-law weights.

Combining (5.3), (5.6), and (4.8) gives the quantitative necessary condition
\[
\boxed{
 b_n
\ge
(1-o(1))
\log\frac{1}{\Pr(S_{n-1}\ge m_n-\varepsilon n)}
+
\left(\frac{\lambda-\nu}{\lambda}+o(1)\right)
\log\frac1{u_n}
-O(1).
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
for every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\).

# 6. A useful subsequential dichotomy

Define
\[
T_n:=D(\mu_n\Vert R_n),
\qquad
C_n:=H_n^{(q)}.
\]
Then under full no-condensation,
\[
T_n\to\infty,
\qquad C_n\to\infty,
\qquad
T_n+C_n=b_n+O(1).
\tag{6.1}
\]
After passing to a subsequence, the ratio \(T_n/b_n\) has a limit \(\theta\in[0,1]\).

This isolates three mathematically different possible counterexample mechanisms:

1. **Transport-dominant** \((\theta=1)\): the residual candidate law puts vanishing mass near the mesoscopic conditioned occupancies, while the occupied fixed-law scales have only \(o(b_n)\) coding cost.
2. **Weight-dominant** \((\theta=0)\): residual transport costs only \(o(b_n)\), while the selected mesoscopic scales themselves consume essentially all of the point-conditioning cost.
3. **Mixed** \((0<\theta<1)\): both effects consume fixed fractions of \(b_n\).

This dichotomy is exact enough to guide the next attack and does not depend on the discredited old F0059 record-gap necessity.

# 9. Next concrete attack

The most concrete next calculation is an **iterated Palm transport decomposition**.

After selecting one random ball and exposing the size \(B_n\) of its box, the remaining \(n-1\) boxes are again an iid boundary allocation conditioned on the residual sum \(m_n-B_n\). Under no condensation, \(B_n=o_p(n)\), so the residual ratio remains supercritical. Iterating this identity over distinct boxes should produce a telescoping decomposition of \(b_n\) into successive transport costs plus successive fixed-law code lengths. The previously proved birthday/collision estimates determine how far distinct-box iteration remains valid.

A useful target theorem would be: before the distinct-box process reaches its birthday scale, the accumulated transport cost cannot consume a fixed fraction of \(b_n\) unless the residual exact-sum probabilities decrease at a rate incompatible with the sublinear amplified-cloud count. Conversely, if accumulated fixed-law code lengths dominate, the same iteration should force many low-entropy size-biased support points in mutually separated mesoscopic bands.

This route uses exact identities and avoids the invalid old record-gap equivalence.
