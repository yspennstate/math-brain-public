## 2. Normalized iid form

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu>0.
\]
Then
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\).

For an admissible state with \(N\) boxes and total \(M\), write
\[
p_N(M):=\Pr(S_N=M),
\qquad
b(N,M):=-\log p_N(M).
\]
For the target sequence,
\[
p_n:=p_n(m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=b(n,m_n).
\]
The weak law gives \(b_n\to\infty\), while Janson's Theorem 17.1 gives
\[
\boxed{b_n=o(n).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Normalize the fixed size-biased boundary law
\[
\bar q_k:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},
\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Then \(\bar q\) is a probability law on the positive support of \(\pi\).

# 3. Exact sequential Palm-peeling identity

Consider \(N\) boxes conditioned on total \(M>0\). Choose one ball uniformly from the \(M\) balls and remove the **entire box** containing it. If the selected box contains \(k\) balls, exact conditioning gives
\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{N k\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{M p_N(M)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The normalization is exact because
\[
\sum_k k\pi_k p_{N-1}(M-k)
=
\frac{M}{N}p_N(M).
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — exact one-step decrement

Let \(B\sim K_{N,M}\) and \(\lambda_{N,M}=M/N\). Then
\[
\boxed{
 b(N,M)
-
\mathbb E\,b(N-1,M-B)
=
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
+
\log\frac{\lambda_{N,M}}{\nu}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
From (3.1) and (2.2),
\[
\log\frac{K_{N,M}(k)}{\bar q_k}
=
\log\frac{N\nu}{M}
+
\log p_{N-1}(M-k)
-
\log p_N(M).
\]
Taking \(K_{N,M}\)-expectations and using \(b=-\log p\) gives
\[
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
=
\log\frac{\nu}{\lambda_{N,M}}
-
\mathbb E b(N-1,M-B)
+
b(N,M),
\]
which is equivalent to (3.2). \(\square\)

There is no local limit theorem, record hypothesis, variance assumption, or tail regularity in this identity.

# 4. Exact path entropy telescope and stopped KL budget

Starting from \((N_0,M_0)=(n,m_n)\), recursively sample
\[
B_{j+1}\sim K_{N_j,M_j},
\qquad
N_{j+1}=N_j-1,
\qquad
M_{j+1}=M_j-B_{j+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
This is the ordinary size-biased permutation of boxes: choose a uniformly random remaining ball, remove its entire box, and repeat.

For a deterministic number of valid steps \(r\), the KL chain rule and (3.2) give the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(P_{n,r}^{\rm peel}\middle\|\bar q^{\otimes r}\right)
=
b_n
-
\mathbb E b(N_r,M_r)
-
\mathbb E\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}
\log\frac{M_j/N_j}{\nu}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Fix
\[
0<c<\lambda-\nu,
\tag{4.3}
\]
and let \(r=o(n)\). Stop as soon as the cumulative removed mass reaches \(cn\), or after \(r\) selections if it has not yet done so. Record both the stopped path and a hit flag. Before the hit,
\[
M_j\ge m_n-cn,
\qquad N_j=n-o(n),
\]
so
\[
M_j/N_j\ge\lambda-c+o(1)>\nu
\tag{4.4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Let \(P_{n,r}^{\rm stop}\) denote the actual stopped path law, and let \(Q_{n,r}^{*,\rm stop}\) be the analogous stopped process driven by iid increments with law \(\bar q\). Summing (3.2) only until stopping gives:

## Theorem 4.1 — stopped Palm entropy budget
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(P_{n,r}^{\rm stop}\middle\|Q_{n,r}^{*,\rm stop}\right)
\le b_n.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof
The conditional KL terms sum to the stopped path KL. The exact costs telescope to
\[
b_n-\mathbb E b(N_\tau,M_\tau),
\]
where \(\tau\) is the number of selections made. The density-premium terms are nonnegative by (4.4), and \(b(N_\tau,M_\tau)\ge0\). Dropping both nonnegative terms yields (4.5). \(\square\)

No collision correction is required: boxes are removed whole and cannot be selected twice.

# 5. The amplified cloud is captured in o(b_n) peeling steps

The checked amplified-cloud theorem supplies deterministic
\[
L_n
=
R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
R_n\to\infty,
\qquad
R_n=o(b_n),
\tag{5.1}
\]
such that, with \(\delta=\lambda-\nu\),
\[
\sum_iY_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i>L_n\}}
=
\delta n+o_p(n).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Set
\[
a_n:=\sqrt{R_n},
\qquad
r_n:=
\left\lceil a_n\frac{m_n}{L_n}\right\rceil.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 r_n
=(\lambda+o(1))\frac{b_n}{\sqrt{R_n}}
=o(b_n)
=o(n).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

For a deterministic allocation \(y\), any cloud box \(i\) with \(y_i>L_n\) survives the first \(r_n\) size-biased removals with probability at most
\[
\left(1-\frac{y_i}{m_n}\right)^{r_n}
\le
\exp\left(-\frac{r_ny_i}{m_n}\right)
\le e^{-a_n}.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Hence the expected cloud mass still unselected after \(r_n\) removals is at most
\[
m_ne^{-a_n}=o(n).
\tag{5.6}
\]
Combining (5.2), (5.6), and Markov's inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
\sum_{j=1}^{r_n}B_j\ge\frac{\delta}{4}n
\right)
\to1.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
If all positive mass is exhausted before \(r_n\) selections, the displayed threshold has necessarily already been hit, so no undefined post-exhaustion transition is needed.

# 6. Unconditional iid size-biased reference-tail theorem

Take
\[
c:=\delta/4
\]
in Theorem 4.1, and use \(r=r_n\) from (5.3). Let \(H_n\) be the hit event that the cumulative selected mass reaches \(cn\) by the \(r_n\)-th selection.

By (5.7),
\[
P_{n,r_n}^{\rm stop}(H_n)\to1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Under the iid reference stopped process, because the increments are nonnegative,
\[
Q_{n,r_n}^{*,\rm stop}(H_n)
=
\Pr_{\bar q}
\left(
X_1+\cdots+X_{r_n}\ge cn
\right),
\tag{6.2}
\]
where \(X_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\bar q\).

Binary data processing applied to (4.5) yields:

## Theorem 6.1
\[
\boxed{
-
\log
\Pr_{\bar q}
\left(
X_1+\cdots+X_{r_n}\ge cn
\right)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
with \(r_n=o(b_n)\).

### Proof
Let \(P_n=P(H_n)\) and \(Q_n=Q(H_n)\). Then \(P_n\to1\), and
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(P_n\Vert Q_n)
\le b_n.
\]
Since
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p\Vert q)
\ge p\log(1/q)-h(p),
\]
we get
\[
\log(1/Q_n)
\le
\frac{b_n+h(P_n)}{P_n}
=(1+o(1))b_n.
\]
\(\square\)

This theorem is unconditional within the supercritical finite-mean boundary regime.

# 7. Corrected truncated obstruction under a hypothetical full counterexample

Assume now, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Choose a deterministic cap
\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\qquad
U_n\ge L_n,
\]
such that
\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)
\to1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
This is possible by (7.1), after replacing any high-probability cap by its maximum with \(L_n\).

On the actual stopped path, (5.7) and (7.2) imply with probability \(1-o(1)\) that \(H_n\) occurs and every selected box before the hit is at most \(U_n\).

For iid \(X_j\sim\bar q\), define the truncated exponential polynomial
\[
M_U(t)
:=
\sum_{1\le k\le U}\bar q_k e^{tk}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The corresponding reference event is contained in the union over \(1\le s\le r_n\) of
\[
\left\{
X_1+\cdots+X_s\ge cn,
\quad
\max_{1\le j\le s}X_j\le U_n
\right\}.
\]
For each \(s\), Chernoff gives
\[
\Pr(\cdots)
\le
\exp(-tcn)M_{U_n}(t)^s.
\]
Therefore, for every \(t\ge0\),
\[
Q_n^{\rm ref}
\le
r_n
\exp\left\{
-tcn
+r_n[\log M_{U_n}(t)]_+
\right\}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
The stopped KL budget and binary data processing give
\[
-\log Q_n^{\rm ref}
\le(1+o(1))b_n.
\]
Because \(r_n=o(b_n)\), we have \(\log r_n=o(b_n)\). Hence:

## Theorem 7.1 — corrected truncated size-biased Cramér obstruction
Under full no-condensation,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left\{
cn t
-r_n[\log M_{U_n}(t)]_+
\right\}
\le
(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is the correct all-\(t\) form. The earlier same-iteration report omitted the positive part on \(\log M_U(t)\); that omission is why this corrected report was archived separately rather than overwriting the earlier file.

# 8. Corrected Renyi and support-entropy corollaries

Define the lower exponential-rate envelope
\[
a(U)
:=
\min_{1\le k\le U:\,\bar q_k>0}
\frac{-\log\bar q_k}{k},
\tag{8.1}
\]
and, for fixed \(0\le\theta<1\),
\[
H_\theta(U)
:=
\sum_{1\le k\le U}\bar q_k^\theta.
\tag{8.2}
\]
For every \(k\le U\),
\[
\bar q_k\le e^{-a(U)k}.
\]
Taking
\[
t=(1-\theta)a(U)
\]
gives
\[
\bar q_ke^{tk}
\le\bar q_k^\theta,
\]
so
\[
M_U(t)\le H_\theta(U).
\]
Theorem 7.1 implies:

## Corollary 8.1
Under full no-condensation, for every fixed \(0\le\theta<1\),
\[
\boxed{
cn(1-\theta)a(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+
r_n[\log H_\theta(U_n)]_+.
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For \(\theta=0\),
\[
H_0(U)=N(U):=\#\{1\le k\le U:\bar q_k>0\}\ge1,
\]
so the positive part is unnecessary and
\[
\boxed{
cn\,a(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+
r_n\log N(U_n).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
Using
\[
r_n=(\lambda+o(1))b_n/\sqrt{R_n},
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{
\frac{n a(U_n)}{b_n}
\le
C_{\lambda,\nu}
\left(
1+
\frac{\log N(U_n)}{\sqrt{R_n}}
\right)
+o(1).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Thus a hypothetical counterexample still splits into two quantitative branches:

1. **sparse-support cap:** if \(\log N(U_n)=o(\sqrt{R_n})\), then
   \[
   \boxed{a(U_n)=O(b_n/n);}
   \]
2. **entropically rich cap:** otherwise the sublinear cap must contain enough admissible support sites to contribute order at least \(\sqrt{R_n}\) logarithmic support entropy.

The Renyi version (8.3) improves support counting when the support is dense but its mass profile is very nonuniform.

# 9. Relation to the checked Gaussian Harnack / record-crossing theorem

The new sequential Palm result says that a full no-condensation counterexample must nevertheless make an \(o(b_n)\)-length iid \(\bar q\) path below \(U_n\) capable of transporting a fixed positive fraction of the linear excess at total exponential cost at most \(b_n\).

The remaining closure problem can therefore be stated deterministically: compare the record-over-cap rate advantage with the truncated path rate in (7.5), including the exact Renyi/composition correction. The old F0059 record-gap necessity is not needed.

# 10. Checks and failure modes

1. **No hidden collision assumption.** Whole-box peeling makes selected boxes distinct by construction.
2. **Reference law is fixed.** \(\bar q_k=k\pi_k/\nu\) is independent of \(n\), \(m_n\), and the evolving residual state.
3. **Stopping is essential for KL <= b_n.** Before the threshold \(cn\), residual density stays above \(\nu\), making the density-premium terms nonnegative. No claim is made after the density may cross \(\nu\).
4. **Cloud capture is conditional-deterministic.** Equation (5.5) uses only the size-biased selection rule and the fact that remaining total mass never exceeds \(m_n\).
5. **No-condensation is used only from Section 7 onward.** The reference-tail theorem (6.3) is unconditional.
6. **No unrestricted local CLT.** None is used in the new peeling proof.
7. **Self-detected correction preserved.** The earlier same-iteration report remains append-only in the repository; this report explicitly records and repairs its Section 7 union-bound slip rather than overwriting history.

# 12. Next concrete attack

For a fixed probability law \(\bar q\), cap \(U\), length \(r\), and target \(x\), define
\[
\Psi(r,U,x)
:=
-\log\Pr_{\bar q}
\left(
X_1+\cdots+X_r\ge x,
\ \max_jX_j\le U
\right).
\]
Any full counterexample now forces
\[
\Psi(r_n,U_n,cn)\le(1+o(1))b_n
\]
with \(r_n=o(b_n)\), while the checked record theorem supplies macroscopic scales at which the per-unit fixed-law cost below \(U_n\) is increasingly worse than at one record-sized jump.

The next goal is a support-sensitive deterministic lower bound
\[
\Psi(r,U,x)
\ge
x a(U)-\mathcal E(r,U),
\]
where \(\mathcal E\) is controlled by the actual Renyi entropy profile rather than the crude \(r\log N(U)\). The corrected Chernoff formula (7.5) is already the Legendre version of that target. A universal record telescope that makes the record advantage dominate this Renyi correction would close the conjectured subsequence statement without any local CLT.
