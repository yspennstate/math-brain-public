## 2. Normalized iid representation

When finite-mean boundary normalization is valid, put
\[
\pi_k=\frac{w_k\rho^k}{\Phi(\rho)},
\qquad
\sum_k\pi_k=1,
\qquad
\sum_k k\pi_k=\nu>0.
\]
Then
\[
(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)
\overset d=
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid\{S_n=m_n\},
\]
where \(\xi_i\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\pi\) and \(S_n=\sum_i\xi_i\).

For arbitrary admissible \((N,M)\), write
\[
p_N(M):=\Pr(S_N=M),
\qquad
b(N,M):=-\log p_N(M).
\]
For the target sequence,
\[
p_n:=p_n(m_n),
\qquad
b_n:=b(n,m_n).
\]
The weak law gives \(b_n\to\infty\), and Janson's Theorem 17.1 gives
\[
\boxed{b_n=o(n).}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Normalize the fixed size-biased boundary law
\[
\bar q_k:=\frac{k\pi_k}{\nu},
\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{2.2}
\]
Then \(\bar q\) is a probability law on the positive support of \(\pi\).

# 3. New exact sequential Palm-peeling identity

Consider an allocation with \(N\) boxes conditioned on total \(M>0\). Choose one ball uniformly from the \(M\) balls and remove the **entire box** containing it. If the selected box contains \(k\) balls, symmetry and exact conditioning give the transition kernel
\[
K_{N,M}(k)
=
\frac{N k\pi_k\,p_{N-1}(M-k)}{M p_N(M)}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The normalization is exact because
\[
\sum_k k\pi_k p_{N-1}(M-k)
=
\frac{M}{N}p_N(M).
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — one-step Palm decrement of the exact conditioning cost

Let \(B\sim K_{N,M}\), and put \(\lambda_{N,M}=M/N\). Then
\[
\boxed{
 b(N,M)
-
\mathbb E\,b(N-1,M-B)
=
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
+
\log\frac{\lambda_{N,M}}{\nu}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
From (3.1) and (2.2),
\[
\log\frac{K_{N,M}(k)}{\bar q_k}
=
\log\frac{N\nu}{M}
+
\log p_{N-1}(M-k)
-
\log p_N(M).
\]
Take expectation under \(K_{N,M}\). Since \(b=-\log p\),
\[
D(K_{N,M}\Vert\bar q)
=
\log\frac{\nu}{\lambda_{N,M}}
-
\mathbb E b(N-1,M-B)
+
b(N,M),
\]
which rearranges to (3.2). \(\square\)

This identity is exact. It uses no local limit theorem, record hypothesis, variance assumption, or tail regularity.

### Interpretation
Each time a box is removed by size-biased sampling, the point-conditioning cost decreases by two nonnegative pieces as long as the residual density remains above \(\nu\):

1. a KL cost measuring how far the actual selected-box law is from the fixed size-biased boundary law \(\bar q\);
2. the deterministic supercritical density premium \(\log(\lambda_{N,M}/\nu)\).

# 4. Exact pathwise entropy telescope

Starting from \((N_0,M_0)=(n,m_n)\), recursively sample
\[
B_{j+1}\sim K_{N_j,M_j},
\qquad
N_{j+1}=N_j-1,
\qquad
M_{j+1}=M_j-B_{j+1}.
\]
Thus \(B_1,B_2,\ldots\) are the occupancies in the ordinary **size-biased permutation of boxes**: select a ball uniformly from the remaining balls, remove its whole box, and repeat.

Let \(P_{n,r}^{\rm peel}\) be the joint law of \((B_1,\ldots,B_r)\), whenever the process is run for \(r\) steps before exhausting all balls.

The chain rule for relative entropy and Theorem 3.1 give the exact formula
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(P_{n,r}^{\rm peel}\middle\|\bar q^{\otimes r}\right)
=
b_n
-
\mathbb E b(N_r,M_r)
-
\mathbb E\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}
\log\frac{M_j/N_j}{\nu}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The formula is valid pathwise through the Markov kernels; no exchangeability approximation is required.

The main benefit appears after stopping before the density can drop to \(\nu\).

Fix once and for all
\[
0<c<\lambda-\nu,
\tag{4.2}
\]
for example \(c=(\lambda-\nu)/4\). For deterministic \(r=o(n)\), define
\[
\tau
:=
\min\left\{
 r,
\inf\left\{j\ge0:\sum_{i=1}^{j}B_i\ge cn\right\}
\right\}.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Before \(\tau\),
\[
M_j\ge m_n-cn,
\qquad
N_j=n-o(n),
\]
so
\[
\frac{M_j}{N_j}\ge\lambda-c+o(1)>\nu
\tag{4.4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Let \(P_{n,\tau}^{\rm peel}\) denote the stopped path, with a deterministic cemetery symbol after stopping, and let \(Q_{n,\tau}^{*}\) be the analogous process driven by iid \(\bar q\) increments and stopped by the same rule. Optional truncation of the finite chain-rule sum yields:

## Theorem 4.1 — stopped Palm entropy budget
\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(P_{n,\tau}^{\rm peel}\middle\|Q_{n,\tau}^{*}\right)
\le b_n
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

### Proof
Sum (3.2) only over steps \(j<\tau\), then take expectations. The KL terms sum to the path KL by the chain rule. The exact costs telescope to
\[
b_n-\mathbb E b(N_\tau,M_\tau),
\]
and the remaining density-premium sum is nonnegative by (4.4). Since \(b(N_\tau,M_\tau)\ge0\), dropping both nonnegative terms gives (4.5). \(\square\)

This is a genuine growing-order identity: there is no collision restriction and no need to condition sampled balls to occupy distinct boxes. The whole price of a long size-biased peeling path, relative to iid draws from the fixed law \(\bar q\), is at most the single exact point-conditioning cost \(b_n\), as long as the path is stopped before removing a fixed fraction smaller than the excess.

# 5. The amplified cloud is captured by only o(b_n) peeling steps

Use the universal amplified cutoff \(L_n\) from (1.1):
\[
L_n
=
R_n\frac{n}{b_n}(1+o(1)),
\qquad
R_n\to\infty,
\qquad
R_n=o(b_n).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Set
\[
a_n:=\sqrt{R_n},
\qquad
r_n:=
\left\lceil
 a_n\frac{m_n}{L_n}
\right\rceil.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
 r_n
=(\lambda+o(1))\frac{b_n}{\sqrt{R_n}}
=o(b_n)
=o(n).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

For a deterministic allocation \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\), call
\[
C_n(y):=\{i:y_i>L_n\}
\]
the amplified cloud and
\[
T_n(y):=\sum_{i\in C_n(y)}y_i
\]
its mass.

## Lemma 5.1 — deterministic capture bound for size-biased peeling
For any fixed cloud box \(i\in C_n(y)\), the probability that it survives the first \(r_n\) size-biased removals is at most
\[
\exp\left(-\frac{r_n y_i}{m_n}\right)
\le e^{-a_n}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Consequently the expected cloud mass left unselected after \(r_n\) removals is at most
\[
T_n(y)e^{-a_n}\le m_ne^{-a_n}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

### Proof
As long as box \(i\) has not been selected, the current total number of remaining balls is at most \(m_n\). Therefore its conditional probability of being selected at the next step is at least \(y_i/m_n\). Hence its survival probability through \(r_n\) steps is at most
\[
(1-y_i/m_n)^{r_n}
\le e^{-r_ny_i/m_n}.
\]
Since \(y_i>L_n\) and \(r_nL_n/m_n\ge a_n\), (5.4) follows. Sum \(y_i\) times this survival bound over the cloud boxes to obtain (5.5). \(\square\)

Now average over the conditioned allocation. By (1.1),
\[
T_n(Y)=\delta n+o_p(n),
\qquad
\delta:=\lambda-\nu.
\]
Since \(a_n\to\infty\), Markov's inequality applied to (5.5) gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
\sum_{j=1}^{r_n}B_j\ge\frac{\delta}{4}n
\right)
\to1.
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
Indeed with high probability the initial cloud contains at least \(3\delta n/4\), while the unselected cloud mass after \(r_n\) steps is at most \(\delta n/4\); the selected cloud mass alone then exceeds \(\delta n/2\).

The constants are deliberately loose; only a fixed positive fraction of the excess is needed.

# 6. New universal reference-tail obstruction

Take
\[
c:=\delta/4
\]
in the stopping rule (4.3), and use \(r=r_n\) from (5.2). By (5.6), under the actual conditioned size-biased peeling process,
\[
P_{n,\tau}^{\rm peel}(\tau<r_n)\to1.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Under the iid reference process \(Q_{n,\tau}^{*}\), the same hitting event is exactly
\[
\left\{
X_1+\cdots+X_{r_n}\ge cn
\right\},
\qquad X_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\bar q,
\tag{6.2}
\]
because the increments are nonnegative.

Binary data processing applied to (4.5) yields:

## Theorem 6.1 — o(b_n) iid size-biased variables must reproduce a macroscopic deviation
\[
\boxed{
-
\log
\Pr_{\bar q}
\left(
X_1+\cdots+X_{r_n}\ge cn
\right)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
where
\[
r_n=(\lambda+o(1))b_n/\sqrt{R_n}=o(b_n).
\]

### Proof
Let \(P_n\) and \(Q_n\) be the probabilities of the hitting event under the target and reference stopped paths. Then \(P_n\to1\), while
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(P_n\Vert Q_n)
\le
D(P_{n,\tau}^{\rm peel}\Vert Q_{n,\tau}^{*})
\le b_n.
\]
Using
\[
d_{\rm Ber}(p\Vert q)
\ge
p\log(1/q)-h(p),
\]
we obtain
\[
\log(1/Q_n)
\le
\frac{b_n+h(P_n)}{P_n}
=(1+o(1))b_n,
\]
because \(b_n\to\infty\). \(\square\)

This theorem is **unconditional** within the supercritical finite-mean boundary regime: it does not assume \(Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0\).

It says that the exact conditioning atom already forces a nontrivial growing-order large-deviation event for the fixed size-biased boundary law. The number of iid reference variables needed is negligible compared with \(b_n\).

# 7. Stronger truncated obstruction under a hypothetical full counterexample

Assume now, only for contradiction analysis, that
\[
Y_{(1)}/n\to_p0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Choose a deterministic cap
\[
U_n\to\infty,
\qquad
U_n=o(n),
\qquad
U_n\ge L_n,
\]
such that
\[
\Pr(Y_{(1)}\le U_n\mid S_n=m_n)\to1.
\tag{7.2}
\]
This is possible by (7.1), after replacing any high-probability cap by its maximum with \(L_n\).

On the target stopped path, (5.6) and (7.2) imply with probability \(1-o(1)\) that the threshold \(cn\) is hit by time \(r_n\) and every pre-hitting selected box is at most \(U_n\).

For iid \(X_j\sim\bar q\), define the truncated moment generating polynomial
\[
M_U(t):=
\sum_{1\le k\le U}\bar q_k e^{tk}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
The corresponding reference stopped event is contained in the union, over \(1\le s\le r_n\), of
\[
\left\{
X_1+\cdots+X_s\ge cn,
\quad
\max_{1\le j\le s}X_j\le U_n
\right\}.
\]
Thus for every \(t\ge0\), Chernoff's bound gives
\[
Q_n^{\rm ref}
\le
r_n
\exp(-tcn)
M_{U_n}(t)^{r_n}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
The stopped KL budget and binary data processing give the matching lower exponential scale
\[
-\log Q_n^{\rm ref}
\le(1+o(1))b_n.
\]
Since \(\log r_n=o(b_n)\) (because \(r_n=o(b_n)\) and \(b_n\to\infty\)), we obtain:

## Theorem 7.1 — truncated size-biased Cramér obstruction to full no-condensation
Under (7.1),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left\{
cn t
-r_n\log M_{U_n}(t)
\right\}
\le
(1+o(1))b_n.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This is a new necessary condition on any fixed-law counterexample. It is not a standard local-limit statement: it is an exact information-theoretic consequence of sequential size-biased peeling plus the previously proved amplified cloud.

# 8. Deterministic rate and Renyi corollaries

Define the lower exponential-rate envelope of the fixed size-biased law by
\[
a(U)
:=
\min_{1\le k\le U:\,\bar q_k>0}
\frac{-\log\bar q_k}{k}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
For \(0\le\theta<1\), define the finite Renyi sum
\[
H_\theta(U)
:=
\sum_{1\le k\le U}\bar q_k^\theta.
\tag{8.2}
\]
For every \(k\le U\),
\[
\bar q_k\le e^{-a(U)k}.
\]
Taking
\[
t=(1-\theta)a(U)
\]
gives
\[
\bar q_ke^{tk}
\le \bar q_k^\theta,
\]
and hence
\[
M_U(t)\le H_\theta(U).
\]
Theorem 7.1 therefore implies:

## Corollary 8.1 — entropy-corrected record envelope constraint
Under full no-condensation, for every fixed \(0\le\theta<1\),
\[
\boxed{
cn(1-\theta)a(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+
r_n\log H_\theta(U_n).
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For \(\theta=0\), let
\[
N(U)=\#\{1\le k\le U:\bar q_k>0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
cn\,a(U_n)
\le
(1+o(1))b_n
+
r_n\log N(U_n).
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
Since
\[
r_n=(\lambda+o(1))\frac{b_n}{\sqrt{R_n}},
\]
this becomes
\[
\boxed{
\frac{n a(U_n)}{b_n}
\le
C_{\lambda,\nu}
\left(
1+
\frac{\log N(U_n)}{\sqrt{R_n}}
\right)
+o(1).
}
\tag{8.5}
\]

This separates two surviving counterexample mechanisms:

1. **sparse-support cap:** \(\log N(U_n)=o(\sqrt{R_n})\), in which case the fixed-law exponential rate at the entire high-probability cap is forced down to the exact conditioning scale \(a(U_n)=O(b_n/n)\);
2. **entropically rich cap:** \(\log N(U_n)\) is at least order \(\sqrt{R_n}\), so the counterexample needs sufficiently many admissible support sites below its sublinear maximum cap to pay the composition entropy in (7.5).

The Renyi form (8.3) is sharper than support counting when the support is dense but its masses are highly nonuniform.

# 9. Relation to the Gaussian Harnack / record-crossing checkpoint

The peer report at commit `4fad48236ed47ffde5c812527fbe5c9a617b3dde` proves that every fixed span-one boundary law contains a Bernoulli smoothing block and hence admits a Gaussian Harnack lower bound for residual coefficients over sublinear displacements. It also proves an unbounded record-over-cap advantage against any diverging sublinear cap.

The new inequality (7.5) attacks the complementary obstruction left in that report. Instead of comparing one macroscopic record directly with the exact boundary coefficient, it asks whether **an entire path of sublinear boxes** can reproduce a fixed positive fraction of the excess under the iid size-biased boundary law. The exact conditioning cost gives this path only a budget \(b_n\), while the number of reference boxes is just \(o(b_n)\).

Combining the two viewpoints suggests a sharper closure route:

- use the record-crossing theorem to choose a macroscopic record \(M_j\asymp n_j\) with a growing exponential-rate advantage over the no-condensation cap \(U_{n_j}\);
- use (7.5) or (8.3) to show that any \(o(b_{n_j})\)-box truncated path below \(U_{n_j}\) paying for a linear amount of excess must spend more than \(b_{n_j}\) once that record advantage is large enough.

What is still missing is a universal inequality converting the unbounded record-over-cap advantage into a lower bound for the truncated rate in (7.5) that beats the Renyi/composition term. The old F0059 record-gap necessity is not used for this step.

# 10. Checks and failure modes

The following points were checked explicitly in this run.

1. **No hidden collision assumption.** Sequential peeling removes whole boxes. Once a box is removed it cannot be selected again, so there is no birthday correction and no requirement that \(r_n\) remain below a distinct-ball collision scale.
2. **Correct reference law.** The fixed reference is exactly \(\bar q_k=k\pi_k/\nu\); it does not depend on \(n\), \(m_n\), the current residual sum, or a truncated tilt.
3. **Stopping is essential.** Formula (4.1) is exact without stopping, but the easy inequality \(D\le b_n\) uses that \(M_j/N_j>\nu\) before the stopping threshold. No claim is made after the residual density may fall below \(\nu\).
4. **Amplified cloud capture is deterministic conditional on the allocation.** The survival estimate (5.4) uses only the fact that the remaining total mass never exceeds \(m_n\). It does not assume independence between boxes after conditioning.
5. **The full-counterexample cap is used only in Section 7 onward.** The path tail theorem (6.3) is unconditional.
6. **No unrestricted local CLT.** The new proof uses no local CLT. The Gaussian Harnack result is a separate checked peer input, not an implicit assumption here.
7. **No promotion of numerics.** Any finite checker added after this report is only a bookkeeping certificate for exact finite identities, not evidence for the universal conjecture.

# 12. Next concrete attack

The next target is now a deterministic convolution inequality for the fixed probability law \(\bar q\).

For a cap \(U\), a number of terms \(r\), and a target \(x\), define
\[
\Psi(r,U,x)
:=
-\log\Pr_{\bar q}
\left(
X_1+\cdots+X_r\ge x,
\ \max_jX_j\le U
\right).
\]
Theorem 7.1 says that any full counterexample must have
\[
\Psi(r_n,U_n,cn)\le(1+o(1))b_n
\]
with \(r_n=o(b_n)\), while the checked record theorem supplies macroscopic scales at which the per-unit fixed-law cost below \(U_n\) is increasingly worse than at one record-sized jump.

The most promising next step is to prove a support-sensitive lower bound of the form
\[
\Psi(r,U,x)
\ge
x\,a(U)
-
\mathcal E(r,U),
\]
where \(\mathcal E\) is controlled by a Renyi entropy of \(\bar q|_{[1,U]}\), and then show by a deterministic record telescope that along some record crossing
\[
x\,[a(U)-a(M)]
\gg
\mathcal E(r,U)+b_n-Ma(M).
\]
The crude bound \(\mathcal E=r\log N(U)\) is exactly (8.4); improving this to the actual Renyi entropy profile, rather than raw support count, is the clearest path for the next iteration.
