## 1. Exact target and scope

Let \(X\) be uniform on \(\{0,1\}^n\), let
\[
Y=X\oplus Z,\qquad Z_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\alpha),\qquad 0\le \alpha\le \tfrac12,
\]
and let \(b:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}\) be arbitrary. Courtade–Kumar Conjecture 1 asks whether
\[
\boxed{I(b(X);Y)\le 1-h_2(\alpha)}
\tag{1.1}
\]
for every \(n,b,\alpha\). A dictator attains equality.

This run does **not** solve the universal conjecture. It proves a finite, source-matching milestone:

> **Theorem N4.** Equation (1.1) holds for every Boolean function on every \(n\le4\) and for the entire continuous interval \(0\le\alpha\le1/2\).

The proof is exhaustive over all four-bit truth tables modulo an explicitly frozen symmetry group, and covers the full noise interval by analytic end regimes plus exact Bernstein subdivision with outward-rounded logarithms in the middle. A finite noise grid is used only for counterexample-first search, never as interval certification.

## 3. Frozen finite convention and counterexample-first phase

For \(n=4\), truth tables are encoded by a 16-bit integer `mask`: bit \(x\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\) is \(b(x)\), with the binary digits of \(x\) representing the four input coordinates.

The frozen symmetry group is generated by:

- all \(4!\) coordinate permutations;
- all \(2^4\) input-bit translations \(x\mapsto x\oplus s\);
- output complementation \(b\mapsto1-b\).

Its size is
\[
4!\,2^4\,2=768.
\]
Every generator preserves \(I(b(X);Y)\) for a uniform cube and a coordinatewise BSC, so it preserves the target gap for every \(\alpha\). Exact orbit marking partitions all 65,536 truth tables into 222 orbits, whose orbit sizes sum exactly to 65,536. There is one constant orbit and one dictator orbit, leaving 220 nontrivial orbits: 57 balanced nondictator orbits and 163 biased nontrivial orbits.

Before interval proof, the checker searched for counterexamples at the exact rational grid
\[
\alpha=j/128,\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,64,
\]
for every nontrivial orbit representative. Posterior probabilities were evaluated from exact rational transition polynomials, and every entropy evaluation used 256-bit MPFR directed rounding. There were zero certified negative gaps. This grid search is **not** credited as interval coverage. Its smallest interior approximate gap was
\[
2.1312135619968728\times10^{-5}\ \text{nats}
\]
at `mask=127`, \(\alpha=63/128\).

## 4. Exact transition polynomials and the target gap

Use natural logarithms for the certificate; multiplying by \(1/\ln2\) converts to bits without changing signs. Let
\[
p=\Pr(b(X)=1)=\frac{|b^{-1}(1)|}{16}.
\]
For \(y\in\{0,1\}^4\), Bayes symmetry of the BSC and the uniform input give
\[
q_y(\alpha):=\Pr(b(X)=1\mid Y=y)
=\sum_{x:b(x)=1}\alpha^{d(x,y)}(1-\alpha)^{4-d(x,y)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Let
\[
c_{y,d}=|\{x:b(x)=1:d(x,y)=d\}|.
\]
Writing \(B_{d,4}(\alpha)=\binom4d\alpha^d(1-\alpha)^{4-d}\), equation (4.1) becomes the exact degree-four Bernstein polynomial
\[
\boxed{
q_y(\alpha)=\sum_{d=0}^4 \frac{c_{y,d}}{\binom4d} B_{d,4}(\alpha).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
All coefficients are rational.

Let
\[
h(t)=-t\ln t-(1-t)\ln(1-t).
\]
The mutual information is
\[
I_{\rm nat}(b(X);Y)=h(p)-\frac1{16}\sum_y h(q_y(\alpha)).
\]
Define the nonnegative target gap
\[
\boxed{
D_b(\alpha)=\ln2-h(\alpha)-h(p)+\frac1{16}\sum_y h(q_y(\alpha)).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
The conjecture for this \(b,\alpha\) is exactly \(D_b(\alpha)\ge0\).

The endpoints are immediate: at \(\alpha=0\), \(I(b(X);X)=h(p)\le\ln2\); at \(\alpha=1/2\), both sides are zero. Constants are trivial and dictators have equality for every \(\alpha\).

## 5. Low-noise interval \([0,1/1024]\)

The proof splits biased and balanced nondictator functions.

### 5.1 Biased functions

After output complementation, every nonconstant biased four-bit function has
\[
p\le7/16.
\]
Using only \(I(b(X);Y)\le H(b(X))=h(p)\),
\[
I_{\rm nat}(b(X);Y)\le h(7/16).
\]
Capacity decreases as \(\alpha\) increases on \([0,1/2]\), so for \(0\le\alpha\le1/1024\),
\[
\ln2-h(\alpha)\ge \ln2-h(1/1024).
\]
The checker evaluates the exact rational arguments with directed MPFR bounds and certifies
\[
\boxed{
\ln2-h(1/1024)-h(7/16)
>8.7872340785797519\times10^{-5}>0.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Thus all biased functions satisfy the conjecture on this interval.

### 5.2 Balanced nondictators

For a balanced \(b\), \(h(p)=\ln2\), so
\[
D_b(\alpha)=H_{\rm nat}(b(X)\mid Y)-h(\alpha).
\tag{5.2}
\]
For each vertex \(y\), let \(s_y\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) be the local sensitivity, i.e. the number of Hamming-neighbors on which the output differs. Let
\[
t=\alpha(1-\alpha)^3.
\]
If \(e_y\) denotes the posterior error probability relative to the value \(b(y)\), every opposite-valued neighbor contributes \(\alpha(1-\alpha)^3\), so
\[
e_y\ge s_y t.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Also
\[
e_y\le1-(1-\alpha)^4<1/2
\]
for \(\alpha\le1/1024\). Binary entropy is increasing on \([0,1/2]\), and \(h(x)\ge x\ln(1/x)\), hence
\[
H_{\rm nat}(b(X)\mid Y)
=\frac1{16}\sum_y h(e_y)
\ge \frac1{16}\sum_y h(s_yt)
\ge t\left[\bar s\ln\frac1t-\overline{s\ln s}\right],
\tag{5.4}
\]
where bars denote vertex averages. Since \(s_y\le4\),
\[
\overline{s\ln s}\le\bar s\ln4.
\]
The exhaustive exact orbit computation proves that among balanced four-bit nondictators,
\[
\boxed{\bar s\ge3/2,}
\tag{5.5}
\]
with equality attained by representative `mask=383`. Therefore
\[
H_{\rm nat}(b(X)\mid Y)
\ge\frac32\,t\ln\frac1{4t}.
\tag{5.6}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
h(\alpha)\le\alpha\left(\ln\frac1\alpha+1\right).
\tag{5.7}
\]
Put \(c=(1-\alpha)^3\), so \(t=\alpha c\), and let \(c_0=(1023/1024)^3\). Dividing the difference between (5.6) and (5.7) by \(\alpha>0\) gives
\[
\frac32 c\left(\ln\frac1\alpha-\ln(4c)\right)-\left(\ln\frac1\alpha+1\right).
\]
For \(0<\alpha\le1/1024\), \(c\ge c_0\), \(\ln(1/\alpha)\ge\ln1024\), and on \([c_0,1]\), \(c\ln(4c)\le\ln4\). Hence this is at least
\[
\left(\frac32c_0-1\right)\ln1024-\frac32\ln4-1.
\tag{5.8}
\]
Directed MPFR rounding certifies the lower bound
\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac32c_0-1\right)\ln1024-\frac32\ln4-1
>0.35586352862839715>0.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]
Thus all balanced nondictators satisfy the conjecture throughout the low-noise interval.

## 6. High-noise interval \([63/128,1/2]\)

Switch to the \(\{-1,1\}\)-valued representation \(f=1-2b\). Put
\[
\rho=1-2\alpha,
\]
so this interval is \(0\le\rho\le1/64\). Write
\[
f(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq[4]}\widehat f(S)\chi_S(x),\qquad \mu=\widehat f(\varnothing)=\mathbb Ef.
\]
Let
\[
W_1=\sum_{|S|=1}\widehat f(S)^2,\qquad
\Delta=\operatorname{Var}(f)-W_1.
\]
The exact orbit scan proves that for every nonconstant nondictator four-bit Boolean function,
\[
\boxed{\Delta\ge11/64,}
\tag{6.1}
\]
with minimum represented by `mask=1`. Also any nonconstant four-bit Boolean function has \(|\mu|\le7/8\).

Let \(g=T_\rho f\), and define
\[
\psi(x)=\ln2-h\!\left(\frac{1+x}{2}\right).
\]
Then
\[
I_{\rm nat}(f(X);Y)=\mathbb E\psi(g(Y))-\psi(\mu),
\tag{6.2}
\]
and
\[
\psi''(x)=\frac1{1-x^2}.
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval,
\[
\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|\widehat f(S)|
\le\sqrt{15\operatorname{Var}(f)}<4,
\]
so pointwise
\[
|g|\le|\mu|+4\rho=:R<1.
\]
Taylor's theorem around \(\mu\), using \(\mathbb E(g-\mu)=0\), gives
\[
I_{\rm nat}(f(X);Y)\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(g)}{2(1-R^2)}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Moreover
\[
\operatorname{Var}(g)
=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\rho^{2|S|}\widehat f(S)^2
\le\rho^2W_1+\rho^4\Delta.
\tag{6.4}
\]
The BSC capacity satisfies
\[
\psi(\rho)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\rho^{2m}}{2m(2m-1)}\ge\frac{\rho^2}{2}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Equations (6.3)–(6.5) show it suffices that
\[
W_1+\rho^2\Delta\le1-(|\mu|+4\rho)^2.
\]
Using \(W_1=1-\mu^2-\Delta\), this is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
(1-\rho^2)\Delta\ge8|\mu|\rho+16\rho^2.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
The left side decreases and the right side increases as \(\rho\) moves from 0 to \(1/64\). Thus the worst case follows from \(\Delta\ge11/64\), \(|\mu|\le7/8\), and \(\rho=1/64\). The checker verifies exactly
\[
\boxed{
\frac{11}{64}\left(1-\frac1{64^2}\right)
=\frac{45045}{262144}
>\frac{29}{256}
=8\frac78\frac1{64}+16\frac1{64^2}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
Hence every nondictator satisfies the conjecture on the high-noise interval; constants and dictators are already trivial/equality cases.

## 7. Middle interval by exact Bernstein subdivision and outward-rounded logs

It remains to cover
\[
\frac1{1024}\le\alpha\le\frac{63}{128}.
\]
For each nontrivial orbit representative and each posterior polynomial (4.2), the checker stores its exact rational Bernstein coefficients. On a rational subinterval \([a,b]\), exact de Casteljau subdivision produces the Bernstein coefficients of the restricted polynomial. The convex-hull property therefore gives an exact range
\[
q_y(\alpha)\in[L_y,U_y]\qquad(\alpha\in[a,b]).
\]
Because \(h\) is concave and symmetric about \(1/2\), its minimum on \([L_y,U_y]\) occurs at the endpoint farther from \(1/2\). Therefore
\[
D_b(\alpha)
\ge \underline{\ln2}-\overline{h(b)}-\overline{h(p)}
+\frac1{16}\sum_y \underline{\min\{h(L_y),h(U_y)\}}
\tag{7.1}
\]
throughout the interval. Bars/underlines here are rigorous MPFR upper/lower bounds, evaluated at 256-bit precision from exact rational inputs with directed rounding.

If the lower bound (7.1) is positive, the interval is certified. Otherwise it is bisected exactly at its rational midpoint. No interval was allowed past depth 24 without certification.

The completed cover contains
\[
\boxed{6631\text{ certified leaf intervals},}
\]
with maximum bisection depth 12. The smallest outward-rounded lower bound among all certified leaves was still strictly positive:
\[
\boxed{3.3175978090317782\times10^{-9}\ \text{nats},}
\tag{7.2}
\]
for `mask=24` on
\[
\left[\frac{257545}{524288},\frac{515593}{1048576}\right]
\]
at depth 10.

Thus the middle interval is rigorously covered continuously, not by a grid.

## 10. Failed route and next concrete attack

The most promising next step is not brute force over all \(2^{32}\) five-bit truth tables. The original Courtade–Kumar Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 show that finite maximization can be reduced to the pairwise-compressed family \(S_n\), and the source reports only 119 candidates at \(n=5\).

I attempted a naive generator based on shifted ideals / repeated pair compressions, but it produced 27 four-bit candidates instead of the source's \(|S_4|=25\). That mismatch means the generator does **not** yet implement the source definition exactly and is not used for any claim. This is the main failed route of the run.

The next concrete obligation is therefore:

1. implement the source's exact \(I\)-section / lexicographic pair-compression definition;
2. verify the implementation reproduces \(|S_2|=5, |S_3|=10, |S_4|=25\) before trusting it;
3. generate all 119 source-valid \(S_5\) candidates;
4. apply the same counterexample-first and full-interval Bernstein/MPFR certification machinery to \(S_5\);
5. if all 119 pass, use Courtade–Kumar Theorem 2/Corollary 1 to move the fixed metric from 4 to 5.

The source's reported numerical validation through \(n=7\) is strong guidance, but it is not substituted for the required continuous exact certificate.
