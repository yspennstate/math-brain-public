# 2. B25 — count the same information defect in two exact ways

Work in natural logarithms. Write

\[
a=\alpha,\qquad b=1-a,\qquad \rho=1-2a,
\qquad m=6,\qquad \varepsilon=2^{-m}=\frac1{64}.
\]

Let `U=X_1` and let `V=(X_2,...,X_7)`. Put

\[
E=\mathbf 1_{\{V=\mathbf 1\}}.
\]

Then the special function is

\[
f=U(1-E).
\]

Let `Y'=(Y_2,...,Y_7)` and define the posterior rare-event probability

\[
S=S(Y'):=\Pr(E=1\mid Y').
\]

Because the prior is uniform and the channel is a product BSC, if `K` is the number of ones among `Y_2,...,Y_7`, then

\[
S=b^K a^{m-K}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
A:=a^6\le S\le B:=b^6,
\qquad
\mathbb ES=\Pr(E=1)=\varepsilon.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Conditioned on `Y_1`, the posterior probability that `U=1` is `b` when `Y_1=1` and `a` when `Y_1=0`. Independence of the two coordinate blocks therefore gives

\[
\Pr(f=1\mid Y)=r(Y_1)(1-S),
\qquad r(Y_1)\in\{a,b\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since `Y_1` is uniform and independent of `S`, the conditional entropy is

\[
H(f\mid Y)
=
\mathbb E\frac{h(a(1-S))+h(b(1-S))}{2}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Also

\[
\Pr(f=1)=\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Define

\[
\psi(t):=\log2-h\!\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right),
\]

and

\[
F_a(s):=rac{h(a(1-s))+h(b(1-s))}{2}-h(a).
\]

Then the Courtade–Kumar defect for this function is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_f(a)
:=\log2-h(a)-I(f(X);Y)
=\psi(\varepsilon)+\mathbb E F_a(S).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The second count is more useful. Let

\[
C(a):=\log2-h(a),
\]

\[
J_a(\theta):=h(a+\rho\theta)-h(a),
\tag{2.6}
\]

which is the mutual information in natural units when a `Bernoulli(theta)` input is sent through `BSC(a)`, and let

\[
K_\varepsilon(s)
:=D\!\left(
\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)
\middle\|
\operatorname{Bern}\!\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}\right)
\right).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
G_a(s)
:=
\frac{1+s}{2}
J_a\!\left(\frac{s}{1+s}\right)
-K_\varepsilon(s).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

A direct entropy expansion shows that

\[
G_a(s)-F_a(s)
=
\ell_\varepsilon(s),
\tag{2.9}
\]

where

\[
\ell_\varepsilon(s)
=
\frac{1+s}{2}\log(1+\varepsilon)
+
\frac{1-s}{2}\log(1-\varepsilon).
\tag{2.10}
\]

The key feature is that `ell_epsilon(s)` is affine in `s`. By (2.2),

\[
\mathbb E\ell_\varepsilon(S)
=\ell_\varepsilon(\varepsilon)
=\psi(\varepsilon).
\tag{2.11}
\]

Combining (2.5), (2.9), and (2.11) gives the exact scalar representation

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_f(a)=\mathbb E G_a(S).
}
\tag{2.12}
\]

This is the B25 gain: the same full-vector information defect has been counted in a form depending only on a scalar posterior `S`, whose support and mean are exactly known.

# 3. B13 — a convex-order monotonicity: the support endpoints are worst

The affine correction in (2.9) does not affect second derivatives, so

\[
G_a''(s)=F_a''(s).
\]

Using `h''(x)=-1/[x(1-x)]`, a direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
G_a''(s)
=-\frac1{2(1-s)}
\left[
\frac{a}{b+as}
+
\frac{b}{a+bs}
\right]
<0
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

for `0<a<1/2` and `0<=s<1`. Thus `G_a` is strictly concave.

For any `s in [A,B]`, concavity puts the graph above its endpoint chord:

\[
G_a(s)
\ge
\frac{B-s}{B-A}G_a(A)
+
\frac{s-A}{B-A}G_a(B).
\]

Taking expectations and using `E S=epsilon` yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_f(a)
\ge
(1-w)G_a(A)+wG_a(B),
\qquad
w:=\frac{\varepsilon-A}{B-A}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is a genuine monovariant/convex-order statement: among all laws supported in `[A,B]` with mean `epsilon`, the endpoint Bernoulli law is at least as adverse for the concave integrand as the actual posterior law. The six-dimensional posterior distribution has collapsed to two endpoint evaluations.
