# New theorem: every generic single-unit-gap depth-six stratum is strictly concave

## Statement

1. the five-weight subsystem `Z=sum_{i=1}^5 u_i B_i` has **no subset-sum collisions**;
2. there is exactly one primitive unit-gap relation up to the translated copies forced by unused coordinates,
   `sum_{i in A} u_i - sum_{j in B}u_j = 1`,
   with `A,B` disjoint;
3. there are no additional unit-gap relations.

Let `B_0~Bernoulli(p)` be independent and put `Y=B_0+Z`. Then

`H(Y)` is strictly concave for every `0<p<1`.

This proves the entire **generic single-unit-gap** part of source-valid depth six. It remains PARTIAL because nested intersections with inherited subset-sum collisions and/or multiple unit-gap relations are not covered.

## Exact reduction to one merge-loss function

Write

`a=|A|`, `b=|B|`, `c=5-a-b`, and `s=a+b+1`.

The primitive relation says that the two configurations

- anchor bit 0, `A` switched on and `B` switched off;
- anchor bit 1, `A` switched off and `B` switched on

have the same output. For each common configuration `T` of the `c` unused coordinates, the corresponding two atom probabilities factor as

`r_T x` and `r_T y`,

where

`x=p^a(1-p)^(b+1)`,
`y=p^(b+1)(1-p)^a`,

and `sum_T r_T=1`.

Merging two masses `rx,ry` loses entropy

`r[(x+y)log(x+y)-x log x-y log y]`.

Summing over all common translates therefore gives the exact total entropy loss

`M_{a,b}(p)=(x+y)log(x+y)-x log x-y log y`,

with no multiplicity factor. Hence

`H_Y(p)=6h(p)-M_{a,b}(p)`,

where `h` is binary entropy.

Positivity leaves exactly eleven primitive generic types:

`(1,0)` and all `(a,b)` with `a,b>=1` and `a+b<=5`.

The finite type count and rational constants below were independently self-checked by the exact integer/rational artifact created this run; that artifact is a self-check only, not the proof.

# B5 — log-odds differentiation

Put

`t=log(p/(1-p))`.

Then

`x/y = exp((a-b-1)t)`.

Thus the only asymmetry in a primitive merge is a one-dimensional logistic ratio. For direct curvature estimates it is convenient to use the following exact identity. If

`M=(x+y)log(x+y)-x log x-y log y`,

then two differentiations give

`M'' = x'' log((x+y)/x) + y'' log((x+y)/y) - [xy/(x+y)](x'/x-y'/y)^2`.      (1)

The final term is nonpositive and is exactly the two-parent Fisher merge loss.
