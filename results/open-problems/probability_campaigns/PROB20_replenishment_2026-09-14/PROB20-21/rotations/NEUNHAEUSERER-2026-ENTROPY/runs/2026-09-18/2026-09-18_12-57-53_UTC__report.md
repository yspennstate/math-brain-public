## 3. B1 — change category: from one-dimensional support calculus to transport on the Boolean-quotient graph

The key useful port is not to force a weighted sum onto the ordinary count lattice. Instead, keep the Boolean-cube coordinate directions and quotient only by the positive linear form.

Let

\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^n w_i B_i,\qquad B_i\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}{\rm Bernoulli}(p),\qquad 0<p<1,
\]

with arbitrary positive weights for the derivation. Put

\[
q_y(p)=\Pr(S=y).
\]

For one omitted coordinate write

\[
q^{(i)}_y(p)=\Pr\!\left(\sum_{\ell\ne i}w_\ell B_\ell=y\right),
\]

and for two omitted coordinates

\[
q^{(ij)}_y(p)=\Pr\!\left(\sum_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}w_\ell B_\ell=y\right).
\]

Every unachievable subscript is interpreted as zero. For `0<p<1`, every achievable full-sum atom has positive probability.

### 3.1 Exact first-derivative continuity equation

For one Bernoulli coordinate, differentiation of its law replaces `(1-p) delta_0 + p delta_{w_i}` by `delta_{w_i}-delta_0`. Applying the product rule over all coordinates gives

\[
\boxed{
q_y'(p)=\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(q^{(i)}_{y-w_i}(p)-q^{(i)}_y(p)\bigr).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This is a genuine continuity equation on the directed quotient of the Boolean cube: coordinate `i` carries probability flow along the edge displacement `w_i`. It is a multi-jump graph transport, not a nearest-neighbor transport on the numerical support.

### 3.2 Exact second derivative

Differentiating two distinct Bernoulli coordinates gives

\[
\boxed{
q_y''(p)=\sum_{i\ne j}
\left(
q^{(ij)}_{y-w_i-w_j}
-q^{(ij)}_{y-w_i}
-q^{(ij)}_{y-w_j}
+q^{(ij)}_y
\right).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

The sum is over ordered pairs; equivalently each unordered Boolean two-face appears twice.

### 3.3 Exact rhombus/Fisher entropy-curvature identity

For

\[
H(p)=-\sum_y q_y(p)\log q_y(p),
\]

normalization gives `sum_y q_y''=0`, hence

\[
H''(p)
=-\sum_y q_y''\log q_y
-\sum_y\frac{(q_y')^2}{q_y}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Insert (3.2). For one ordered pair `(i,j)`, translate the four summation variables back to the common omitted-coordinate value `y`. The logarithmic contribution becomes

\[
\sum_y q^{(ij)}_y
\log\!\left(
\frac{q_{y+w_i}q_{y+w_j}}
     {q_y q_{y+w_i+w_j}}
\right).
\]

Therefore, writing

\[
I_{\rm out}(p)=\sum_y\frac{(q_y')^2}{q_y},
\]

we obtain the exact all-depth identity

\[
\boxed{
H''(p)
=
2\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}\sum_y q^{(ij)}_y(p)
\log\!\left(
\frac{q_{y+w_i}(p)q_{y+w_j}(p)}
     {q_y(p)q_{y+w_i+w_j}(p)}
\right)
-I_{\rm out}(p).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is a complete derivation, not a numerical observation. It is equivalent to the source curvature question. It localizes the previously identified signed logarithmic obstruction onto the images of Boolean two-faces (weighted `rhombi`) while retaining the global output Fisher term.

The previous conditional-rank score formula is consistent with this representation:

\[
\frac{q_y'}{q_y}
=\frac{\mathbb E[K\mid S=y]-np}{p(1-p)},
\qquad K=\sum_i B_i.
\]

Thus (3.4) is a spatial/transport localization of the same Fisher-versus-log-curvature obstruction isolated before the stuck report.

### 3.4 Why this is a real change of route

For equal weights, every Boolean square projects to one numerical step size and the log ratio in (3.4) becomes the usual consecutive log-concavity ratio

\[
\log\frac{q_{y+1}^2}{q_yq_{y+2}}.
\]

That is exactly the one-dimensional geometry in which the Shepp–Olkin machinery has its strongest inequalities. For unequal weights, the natural geometry is instead a quotient DAG with many edge lengths and four-corner ratios. The port target is therefore a **graph transport inequality paying the positive weighted-rhombus curvature by the output Fisher energy**, not a literal copy of the count-lattice proof.

**B1 outcome: SUCCESSFUL REFORMULATION, NOT SOURCE CLOSURE.** Equation (3.4) gives a clean arbitrary-depth target and tells us what a weighted Shepp–Olkin analogue must control. It does not by itself prove the required inequality.

## 5. B22 — read the failures, not the successes

which is their decisive replacement for the earlier `Condition 4` obstacle. Its proof ultimately uses special cubic inequalities satisfied by ordinary Poisson-binomial/count distributions.
