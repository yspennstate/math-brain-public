## 3. Standing negation-first attack

The first objective was to make the exact two-dominant reduction from the previous report fail in the dangerous regime, rather than trying to prove it by optimism.

Let `Z` be any positive-weight common-`p` Bernoulli sum with deterministic total weight `S`. Add two weights `M` and `M+r`, with `M>S`. Let `A,B~Bernoulli(p)` be the corresponding new bits and put

`Y = Z + M A + (M+r) B`, `D=p(1-p)`.

Because `M>S`, the output determines `T=A+B`: the `T=0`, `T=1`, and `T=2` bands are disjoint. Conditional on `T=1`, the two possibilities `(A,B)=(1,0),(0,1)` are equiprobable, independently of `p`. If `J~Bernoulli(1/2)` is independent of `Z`, this gives the exact identity

`H(Y)=H(Bin(2,p))+H(Z)+2D [H(Z+rJ)-H(Z)]`.

Define

`Delta_r(p)=H(Z+rJ)-H(Z)`

and

`C_r(p)=H(J | Z+rJ)=log 2-Delta_r(p)`.

Then

`Delta_r = JS(P_Z, T_r P_Z) = I(J;Z+rJ)`,

where `T_r P_Z` denotes translation of the law of `Z` by `r`; hence the exact reduction can be rewritten as

`H(Y)=H(Z)+2h(p)-2D C_r(p)`.

Differentiating twice gives

`H_Y''(p)=H_Z''(p)-2/D-(2D C_r(p))''`.     (TD)

Thus a strong two-dominant insertion theorem would follow from

`(2D C_r)'' >= -2/D`                        (TD-safe)

throughout `0<p<1` for every source-relevant pair `(Z,r)`.

This is deliberately recorded as a **candidate inequality, not a theorem**. The negation-first work did not yield a rigorous common-`p` source witness with `H_Y''>0`, and I am not promoting bounded numerical exploration or search misses into evidence of truth. The important output of B7 is instead that the entire large-weight scale `M` disappears from the exact curvature problem once `M>S`: only the smaller law `Z` and the represented shift `r` survive.

Two endpoint regimes are still exactly safe:

- If `r>S`, the two translates of `Z` in the `T=1` band are disjoint, so `C_r=0`, `H(Y)=H(Z)+2h(p)`, and concavity follows from concavity of `H(Z)`.
- If `r=0`, the two large weights are equal, `C_r=log 2`, and `H(Y)=H(Z)+H(Bin(2,p))`, again concave whenever `H(Z)` is.

Therefore the only two-dominant danger is exactly `0<r<=S`.

## 6. B29 — ADD THE POINTS AT INFINITY: ordinary compactness is false; stratified blow-up is the right object

A tempting depth-six strategy is to normalize weights to the simplex `sum_i w_i=1`, take its compact closure, prove all boundary faces safe, and certify the remaining compact interior. That strategy is invalid if it assumes ordinary continuity of discrete Shannon entropy in the weights.

The failure is exact already at depth two. For weights `(1,1+epsilon)` with every `epsilon>0`, the four subset sums are distinct, so

`H_epsilon(p)=2h(p)`.

At `epsilon=0`, the middle two atoms collide and

`H_0(p)=H(Bin(2,p))=2h(p)-2p(1-p) log 2`.

Thus for every interior `p`,

`lim_{epsilon downarrow 0} H_epsilon(p)-H_0(p)=2p(1-p) log 2 > 0`.

The entropy is constant on each collision stratum and jumps when strata merge. Ordinary topological compactness in weight coordinates therefore cannot carry curvature estimates through collision walls.

The useful replacement is **stratified projective compactification** by the finite collision arrangement, with blow-ups at incidences of boundary faces and collision flats.

A second exact lemma explains why most ordinary boundary faces are nevertheless safe.

**Small-new-weight recoverability lemma.** Fix an old finite positive-weight law with distinct output support values `S_old`, and let

`delta = min{|x-y| : x,y in S_old, x != y} > 0`.

If a new positive weight `epsilon` satisfies `0<epsilon<delta`, then `S_old` and `S_old+epsilon` are disjoint. Hence the new Bernoulli bit is recoverable from the output and

`H_new(p)=H_old(p)+h(p)`.

Proof. If `x=y+epsilon` with `x,y in S_old`, then `epsilon=|x-y|>=delta`, contradiction. Therefore the translated copies are disjoint, and the output identifies the new bit. QED.

Consequently a genuinely hard family cannot approach a zero-weight boundary while the old support gaps stay fixed. To keep a collision obstruction alive as one coordinate tends to zero, **an old support gap must tend to zero at a comparable scale**. In arrangement language, a hard sequence must approach a boundary face and a collision flat simultaneously. Iterating this observation produces nested collision flags/blow-up charts rather than arbitrary Euclidean boundary points.

The two-dominant family of Section 4 is precisely one such nontrivial blow-up: ordinary projectivization sends the smaller subsystem to zero, while the tangent ratio data `(Z,r)` retains the full collision ambiguity.
