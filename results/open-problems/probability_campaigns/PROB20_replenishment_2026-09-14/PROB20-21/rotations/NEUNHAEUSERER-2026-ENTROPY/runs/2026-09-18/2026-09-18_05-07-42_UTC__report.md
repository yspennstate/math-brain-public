## B11 — dualize to a collision-class curvature functional

For a fixed weighted sum with `m` Bernoulli coordinates, let a collision class `C` consist of all subsets producing the same weighted sum. If

`Q_C(p) = sum_{A in C} p^{|A|}(1-p)^{m-|A|}`,

then the output entropy is

`H(p) = - sum_C Q_C(p) log Q_C(p)`.

Because `sum_C Q_C''(p)=0`, direct differentiation gives the exact collision-pattern functional

`H''(p) = - sum_C [ Q_C''(p) log Q_C(p) + (Q_C'(p))^2 / Q_C(p) ].`   (1)

Thus a counterexample is exactly a realizable positive-weight collision partition and an interior `p` for which the right side of (1) is positive. This isolates the analytic obligation from the weight-search obligation.

There is also a useful midpoint specialization. Put

`M1(C)=sum_{A in C} (2|A|-m)`,

`M2(C)=sum_{A in C} ((2|A|-m)^2-m)`.

At `p=1/2`, elementary differentiation gives

`Q_C=|C| 2^{-m}`,

`Q_C'=2^{1-m} M1(C)`,

`Q_C''=2^{2-m} M2(C)`.

Since `sum_C M2(C)=0`, the common `-m log 2` term cancels and

`H''(1/2) = -2^{2-m} sum_C [ M2(C) log |C| + M1(C)^2/|C| ].`   (2)

Formula (2) is a finite collision-pattern certificate target for the next depth. It is not by itself a universal proof, but it removes all probabilities from the midpoint test except integer class data and logarithms of class sizes.

**Outcome B11: SUCCESS.** It produced exact finite functionals (1) and (2) and, at depth 3, reduces the problem to five explicit entropy-loss formulas.

## B14 — quotient by coordinate permutations and complements

For three positive weights `x,y,z`, the empty subset has sum `0` and the full subset has sum `T=x+y+z`; positivity makes both unique.

Any collision among the remaining six subsets is one of the following:

1. **singleton = singleton**, e.g. `{1}~{2}`, iff `x=y`. Taking complements simultaneously gives `{1,3}~{2,3}`;
2. **two-set = two-set**, which after taking complements is again equality of the omitted singleton weights;
3. **singleton = two-set**. If the singleton lies inside the two-set, positivity would force the other weight to be zero, impossible. Therefore the sets must be disjoint complements, giving exactly one additive relation such as `z=x+y`.

There cannot be two distinct additive relations among positive `x,y,z` unless an impossible zero weight occurs. Combining the equality and additive possibilities, and quotienting by the `S_3` coordinate action, gives exactly five cases:

- **A (`k=8`)**: no equality and no additive relation;
- **B (`k=7`)**: one additive relation, say `z=x+y`, with `x!=y`;
- **C (`k=6`)**: one equality, say `x=y=a`, with `z!=a,2a`;
- **D (`k=5`)**: `x=y=a` and `z=2a`;
- **E (`k=4`)**: `x=y=z`.

This is exhaustive for all positive real triples, hence for the source's positive-integer triples.

Complementing every subset sends output value `s` to `T-s` and simultaneously sends `p` to `1-p`, so `H(p)=H(1-p)`. This symmetry will also cut future searches to `p in (0,1/2]`.

**Outcome B14: SUCCESS.** The group/complement action collapses the full depth-3 frontier to five realizable collision types, with a short completeness proof rather than a bounded enumeration.

## B30 — solve depth 3 completely

Let `q=1-p` and `h(p)=-p log p-q log q`. Before collisions, the three-bit microstate entropy is `3h(p)`. Coalescing microstates with the same weighted sum subtracts their conditional entropy inside each collision class.

We will repeatedly use

`h''(p) = -1/(pq)`

and

`G(p)=pq h(p)`.

A direct differentiation gives

`G''(p) = (6p-2) log p + (4-6p) log q - 1`.   (3)

Define `F(p)=G''(p)+1`. Then

`F'(p) = 6 log(p/q) + 2(1/q-1/p)`.

For `p>1/2` both terms are positive; for `p<1/2` both are negative. Therefore `F` has its global minimum at `p=1/2`, where `F(1/2)=-log 4`. Hence

`G''(p) >= -1-log 4` for every `0<p<1`.   (4)

We now check the five exhaustive collision types.

### A. No collisions (`k=8`)

`H=3h`, so

`H''=-3/(pq)<0`.

### B. One additive collision (`k=7`)

Take `z=x+y`. The colliding complementary configurations have masses `p q^2` and `p^2 q`; their total mass is `pq` and their normalized split is `(q,p)`. The entropy loss is therefore `pq h(p)=G(p)`. Thus

`H=3h-G`,

so by (4) and `pq<=1/4`,

`H'' = -3/(pq)-G'' <= -12 + 1 + log 4 < 0`.

(The final strict inequality is elementary, e.g. `log 4<2`.)

### C. One equality, no additive relation (`k=6`)

Take `x=y=a` and `z!=a,2a`. There are two complementary collision classes, each merging two equal-probability atoms. Their losses are

`2 p q^2 log 2` and `2 p^2 q log 2`,

whose sum is `2pq log 2`. Hence

`H=3h-2pq log 2`,

and

`H''=-3/(pq)+4 log 2 <= -12+4 log 2<0`.

### D. Equality plus additive relation (`k=5`)

Take `(x,y,z)=(a,a,2a)`. The equal-weight pair and its complement contribute the same total loss `2pq log 2` as in Case C. The additional complementary collision `{z}~{x,y}` contributes `pq h(p)=G(p)`. Therefore the **correct** formula is

`H=3h-2pq log 2-G`.   (5)

Consequently, using (4),

`H''=-3/(pq)+4 log 2-G''`

`    <= -12+4 log 2+1+log 4`

`    = -11+6 log 2 <0`.

The live MathOverflow answer currently lists the three individual losses correctly but then writes `-4pq log 2` in this case. Summing its own two equal-pair losses gives `-2pq log 2`, as in (5). This factor-of-two slip is harmless to the sign conclusion: the corrected curvature is still strictly negative.

### E. All weights equal (`k=4`)

Then the output is a rescaled `Binomial(3,p)`. Relative to the three labeled Bernoulli bits, the level-1 and level-2 multiplicities each equal `3`, so the total entropy loss is `3pq log 3`. Hence

`H=3h-3pq log 3`,

and

`H''=-3/(pq)+6 log 3 <= -12+6 log 3<0`

(for example `log 3<2`).

### Endpoint and universal conclusion

Every depth-3 case has `H''(p)<0` on `(0,1)`. The entropy extends continuously to `[0,1]`; therefore it is concave on the closed interval. Because the five cases exhaust every positive real weight triple, they exhaust in particular every source-valid positive-integer triple `(1,d_1,d_2)`.
