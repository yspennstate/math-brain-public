# New theorem of this run: every depth-six relation-rank <= 2 law is concave

Let `w=(w_1,...,w_6)` be arbitrary positive real weights and let

`S_w(p)=sum_{i=1}^6 w_i B_i`,  with iid `B_i~Bernoulli(p)`.

For two subsets `A,B subset [6]`, equality of their subset sums, after cancelling common coordinates, is equivalent to

`r·w=0`

for a primitive vector `r in {-1,0,1}^6` containing both signs, considered modulo `r ~ -r`. Define the **active collision-relation space**

`L(w) = span_R { r : r·w=0, r in {-1,0,1}^6, r has both signs }`

and `rho(w)=dim L(w)`.

**Theorem proved/certified in this run.** If `rho(w) <= 2`, then the Shannon entropy `H(S_w(p))` is strictly concave for every `0<p<1`.

## B18 — encode the first nested flags as relation planes / quotient fibers

The primitive subset-difference normals are exactly the sign-normalized vectors in `{-1,0,1}^6` containing both signs. Their number is

`(3^6 - 2*2^6 + 1)/2 = 301`.

For rank two, I represented a relation plane by the normalized Plücker coordinates of a generating pair `u,v`, namely the fifteen minors

`p_ij = u_i v_j - u_j v_i`.

Two independent generating pairs produce the same normalized Plücker vector exactly when they span the same two-plane. Exact enumeration of all pairs of the 301 primitive normals gives **34,250 distinct rank-two relation planes**.

Not all such planes can annihilate a strictly positive weight vector. By Gordan's alternative, a plane `L` has a strictly positive vector in `L^perp` iff `L` contains no nonzero componentwise-nonnegative vector. Because `dim L=2`, write a general row-space vector as `s u + t v` with `s in {+1,-1}` after normalization (plus the `s=0` direction). Each coordinate inequality is an exact rational half-line constraint on `t`; intersecting these constraints decides positivity with no floating point. Exactly **26,900** of the 34,250 planes pass this positive-kernel test.

For each positive plane `L`, the active primitive relations are exactly `L intersect R`, where `R` is the 301-normal set. For every active primitive relation, I joined every translated Boolean-cube pair realizing that relation, and took transitive closure. This gives the exact subset-sum fibers. `c(C)=(c_0,...,c_6)`,  `c_k = #{A in C: |A|=k}`,

`Q_C(p)=sum_{k=0}^6 c_k p^k (1-p)^(6-k)`.

## B25 — second-order allocation attempt, failure, and the global cancellation that replaces it here

I first tried to continue the requested edge-budget program. The obstruction from the previous stuck report persists: overlapping relations can create a fiber with several Boolean-cube vertices, and writing that fiber as a sequence of binary merges is not canonical. The earlier `(2,2,2,3,6)` diagnostic already shows that first-order Fisher score separation can vanish while second-order acceleration differs, so a purely first-order edge budget cannot be the universal theorem.

For relation rank at most two, however, a fiber-level cancellation removes the dangerous common logarithm and makes an exact global certificate feasible. Let

`F(p) := -H''(p) = sum_C [ Q_C''(p) log Q_C(p) + (Q_C'(p))^2/Q_C(p) ]`.

Since `sum_C Q_C(p)=1`, we have `sum_C Q_C''(p)=0`. Therefore, writing `q=1-p`,

`F(p) = sum_C [ Q_C''(p) log( Q_C(p)/q^6 ) + (Q_C'(p))^2/Q_C(p) ]`.

Now set `t=p/q`. For every fiber,

`Q_C(p)/q^6 = sum_k c_k t^k`,

which is a positive polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients on `0<p<=1/2`. This is the exact global no-double-spending cancellation used by the certificate. It does not yet solve arbitrary relation rank; it does remove the numerically disastrous common `6 log q` term and keeps the finite rank-two proof genuinely exact.
