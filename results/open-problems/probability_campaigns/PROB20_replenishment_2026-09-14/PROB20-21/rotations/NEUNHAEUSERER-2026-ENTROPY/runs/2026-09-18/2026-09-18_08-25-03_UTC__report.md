## 3. Setup retained from the depth-four theorem

Let

`Y = sum_{i=1}^n w_i B_i`,

where every `w_i>0` and `B_i` are iid `Bernoulli(p)`. The source is the positive-integer subfamily `(w_1,...,w_n)=(1,d_1,...,d_{n-1})` with positive integer increments.

Partition the Boolean cube into collision classes `C` according to the value of `Y`. For a class `C`, define

`Q_C(p) = P(Y=C) = sum_{A in C} p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n-|A|}`.

Then

`H(p) = - sum_C Q_C(p) log Q_C(p)`

and, because `sum_C Q_C''=0`,

`H''(p) = -F(p)`,

where

`F(p) = sum_C [ Q_C''(p) log Q_C(p) + (Q_C'(p))^2/Q_C(p) ].`   (3.1)

Complementing subsets gives `H(p)=H(1-p)`. Hence endpoint control near zero automatically gives the symmetric endpoint near one.

# 4. B12 — exact conditional-rank / Fisher identities

This is the cleanest structural reduction found this run.

Put

`K = sum_i B_i`, `D=p(1-p)`.

Within a collision class `C`, let

`mu_C = E[K | Y=C]`,
`v_C  = Var(K | Y=C)`,
`a_C  = mu_C - n p`.

The Bernoulli product measure is an exponential family in the logit

`t = log(p/(1-p))`.

For a microstate of rank `k`, its probability is proportional to `exp(k t)`. Therefore conditioning on a class and differentiating its class partition function gives exactly

`d/dt log Q_C = a_C`,                                      (4.1)

`d^2/dt^2 log Q_C = v_C - nD`.                            (4.2)

Since `dp/dt=D`, conversion back to `p` yields

`Q_C'/Q_C = a_C/D`,                                       (4.3)

and

`Q_C''/Q_C = [v_C + a_C^2 - nD - (1-2p)a_C]/D^2`.       (4.4)

The derivation of (4.4) is explicit: `Q_t/Q=a_C`, so `Q_tt/Q=v_C-nD+a_C^2`, while

`Q_pp = [Q_tt-(1-2p)Q_t]/D^2`.

Two conserved identities now become transparent. By total expectation,

`E[a_C]=0`,                                                (4.5)

and by the law of total variance for `K`,

`E[v_C] + E[a_C^2] = Var(K) = nD`.                        (4.6)

The Fisher term in (3.1) is therefore exactly

`J(p) := sum_C (Q_C')^2/Q_C = E[a_C^2]/D^2`.             (4.7)

Thus collision coarse-graining keeps precisely the amount of rank variance explained by the observed weighted sum. In particular,

`0 <= E[a_C^2] <= nD`,                                    (4.8)

which is the Fisher data-processing inequality here in elementary conditional-variance form.

Substituting (4.4) into (3.1) gives the exact all-depth curvature reduction

`D^2 F(p)
 = E[a_C^2]
 + E[(v_C+a_C^2-nD-(1-2p)a_C) log Q_C].`                 (4.9)

The random coefficient multiplying `log Q_C` has mean zero by (4.5)-(4.6). Therefore the whole conjecture is equivalent to controlling one covariance-type logarithmic term against the explained-rank variance `E[a_C^2]`.

**B12 outcome: SUCCESS.** This does not prove the global sign, but it compresses every depth to conditional first and second moments of the Boolean rank inside collision fibers. It also explains why bare Fisher data processing is insufficient: (4.8) is an upper bound on the positive Fisher contribution, whereas (4.9) needs a lower comparison against a signed logarithmic covariance.

# 5. B3 — prove something stronger: a uniform endpoint theorem at every depth

The depth-four report needed a separate small-`p` argument. The same mechanism in fact works at **every** depth with an explicit source-independent constant.

## Theorem 5.1 — arbitrary-depth endpoint concavity

Let `n>=3` and let `w_1,...,w_n` be arbitrary positive real numbers. Define

`epsilon_n := 1/(128 n^4 2^n)`.

Then the entropy of `Y=sum_i w_i B_i` satisfies

`H''(p)<0`

for every

`0<p<=epsilon_n`

and, by complement symmetry, for every

`1-epsilon_n<=p<1`.

Consequently, at every depth any counterexample to Neunhäuserer's equal-parameter conjecture must lie in the explicit compact central interval

`[epsilon_n, 1-epsilon_n]`.

### Proof

Write `F=J+T` with

`J = sum_C (Q_C')^2/Q_C >=0`,

`T = sum_C Q_C'' log Q_C`.

Since `H''=-F`, it is enough to prove `F>0`.

### Step 1. A universal `1/p` Fisher contribution

Let

`w_* = min_i w_i`

and let `r>=1` be the number of indices attaining this minimum. Because all weights are strictly positive, the smallest positive value of `Y` is exactly `w_*`, and the only subsets producing it are those `r` singletons. No subset of size at least two can sum to `w_*`, since it would have weight at least `2w_*>w_*`.

Hence this collision class has probability

`Q_*(p)=r p(1-p)^{n-1}`.

Differentiating,

`Q_*'(p)=r(1-p)^{n-2}(1-np)`.

Its single contribution to `J` is

`(Q_*')^2/Q_*
 = r (1-p)^{n-3}(1-np)^2/p`.                            (5.1)

Assume `p<=1/(2n)`. Then `(1-np)^2>=1/4`. Also, for `n>=3`, Bernoulli's inequality gives

`(1-p)^{n-3} >= 1-(n-3)p
              >= 1-(n-3)/(2n)
              > 1/2`.

Using `r>=1` in (5.1),

`J >= 1/(8p)`.                                            (5.2)

### Step 2. A crude but uniform bound on the logarithmic term

For a microstate `A` with `k=|A|`, put

`f_k(p)=p^k(1-p)^{n-k}`.

Direct differentiation gives

`f_k'' = k(k-1)p^{k-2}(1-p)^{n-k}
       -2k(n-k)p^{k-1}(1-p)^{n-k-1}
       +(n-k)(n-k-1)p^k(1-p)^{n-k-2}`.                  (5.3)

Whenever a coefficient in (5.3) is nonzero, the corresponding exponents are nonnegative. On `0<p<=1/2`, every power factor is at most one. Therefore

`|f_k''(p)| <= n^2+2n^2+n^2 = 4n^2`.                    (5.4)

Each class derivative is a sum of its microstate derivatives, hence

`sum_C |Q_C''|
 <= sum_{A subset [n]} |f_{|A|}''|
 <= 4n^2 2^n`.                                           (5.5)

For every nonempty class `C`, choose one microstate `A in C`. Since `p<=1/2`, we have `1-p>=p`, and therefore

`Q_C >= p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n-|A|} >= p^n`.                   (5.6)

As also `Q_C<=1`, (5.6) gives

`|log Q_C| <= n log(1/p)`.                               (5.7)

Combining (5.5)-(5.7),

`|T|
 <= 4 n^3 2^n log(1/p)`.                                 (5.8)

### Step 3. Choose an explicit endpoint radius

From (5.2) and (5.8), whenever `p<=1/(2n)`,

`F(p) >= 1/(8p) - 4 n^3 2^n log(1/p)`.                  (5.9)

It remains to show the right side is positive for `p<=epsilon_n`.

The function `p log(1/p)` is increasing on `(0,e^{-1})`. Our `epsilon_n` is below both `e^{-1}` and `1/(2n)` for `n>=3`. Thus

`32 n^3 2^n p log(1/p)
 <= 32 n^3 2^n epsilon_n log(1/epsilon_n)
 = [log(128 n^4 2^n)]/(4n)`.                             (5.10)

For `n>=3`,

`log(128 n^4 2^n) < 4n`.                                (5.11)

To verify (5.11) without numerics, define

`g(x)=4x-[7 log 2 + 4 log x + x log 2]`.

At `x=3`, `g(3)>0`, equivalently `128*3^4*2^3 < e^{12}`; and

`g'(x)=4-log2-4/x > 0`

for every `x>=3`. Hence (5.11) follows for all integers `n>=3`.

Equations (5.10)-(5.11) give

`32 n^3 2^n p log(1/p)<1`,

which is exactly the strict positivity of the right-hand side of (5.9). Therefore `F(p)>0` and

`H''(p)=-F(p)<0`

for `0<p<=epsilon_n`.

Finally, complementing all Bernoulli coordinates reflects `Y` to `sum_i w_i-Y` and replaces `p` by `1-p`, preserving entropy. Thus `H(p)=H(1-p)` and the same result holds on the symmetric interval near one. QED.

**B3 outcome: SUCCESS.** The theorem is stronger than a depth-five calculation: it removes explicit endpoint neighborhoods from the search at **all** depths and for all positive real weights. It does not move `m_all`, because the central interval is still open from depth five onward.

# 6. B21 — what neighboring entropy theory transfers, and what does not

## 6.1 Shepp–Olkin is relevant but not directly composable

Hillion–Johnson prove joint entropy concavity for the ordinary sum of independent Bernoulli variables as their individual parameters vary. If all weights are equal, the present problem is exactly that count law along the diagonal.

But the current MathOverflow discussion gives an exact weighted heterogeneous counterexample at `(w_1,w_2,w_3)=(1,1,2)`. Therefore a route that first proves joint concavity in `(p_1,...,p_n)` for a weighted sum is dead. The equal-parameter line is genuinely more rigid than the heterogeneous cube.

This is a useful failure, because it says a successful transfer from Shepp–Olkin must preserve the **common logit / common thinning direction**, rather than ask for a full multivariate Hessian sign.

## 6.2 A new exact random-prefix representation

There is, however, a sharper common-parameter representation that did not appear in the previous reports.

Let

`K ~ Binomial(n,p)`

and let `Pi` be a uniform random permutation of `[n]`, independent of `K`. Define the random prefix sum

`Y_tilde = sum_{j=1}^K w_{Pi(j)}`.                        (6.1)

Conditional on `K=k`, the first `k` elements of a uniform permutation form a uniform `k`-subset. Hence `Y_tilde` has exactly the same law as the weighted iid Bernoulli sum `Y`.

For every fixed permutation `Pi`, the prefix sums

`0, w_{Pi(1)}, w_{Pi(1)}+w_{Pi(2)}, ..., sum_i w_i`

are strictly increasing because every weight is positive. Therefore, given `Pi`, the value of `Y_tilde` determines `K` uniquely. Consequently

`H(Y_tilde | Pi)=H(K | Pi)=H(K)`,                         (6.2)

and therefore

`H(Y)-H(K) = I(Y_tilde; Pi) >=0`.                         (6.3)

Equation (6.3) gives a conceptual explanation of the entropy increase from the binomial count law to a positive weighted sum. It also identifies a stronger route:

> **Prefix-information route.** Prove that `p -> I(Y_tilde;Pi)` is concave for every positive weight vector.

If that statement is true, then the source conjecture follows immediately because `H(K)` is concave by the equal-parameter Shepp–Olkin theorem and

`H(Y)=H(K)+I(Y_tilde;Pi)`.

I do **not** claim this mutual-information concavity here. Standard generic facts about mutual information do not supply it: `I(Pi;Y_tilde)` is mutual information between two outputs of the latent count/permutation construction, and generic channels need not preserve the required curvature. The special nested-prefix geometry from positive weights must be used.

## 6.3 Relation to the class channel

Writing

`N_{y,k} = #{A subset [n]: |A|=k, sum_{i in A}w_i=y}`

and `b_k=P(K=k)`, one has the fixed channel

`q_y = sum_k T_{y,k} b_k`,

`T_{y,k}=N_{y,k}/binom(n,k)`.                             (6.4)

Every fiber at fixed `y` is an antichain in the Boolean lattice, because strict inclusion strictly increases a positive weighted sum. Thus LYM gives

`sum_k T_{y,k} <=1`                                       (6.5)

for every row, while every column of `T` sums to one. Equations (6.4)-(6.5) are valuable zero-order structure, but this run did not find a theorem turning row-substochasticity alone into the required second-derivative sign. The random-prefix representation is more informative because it retains the nested ordering which generic stochastic matrices forget.
