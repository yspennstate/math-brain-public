# 3. B6 — DEFORM TO A POINT YOU CAN SOLVE: one-shot edge-responsibility selectors cannot even grow

The first attempted deformation removes adaptivity completely and asks whether one can make clique consistency automatic by assigning each possible pair to a responsible endpoint.

For deterministic sets `R_i subset [n]\{i}`, define the degree-one coordinate polynomial

\[
f_i(Y)=1-\sum_{j\in R_i}(1-Y_{ij}).
\]

Then `f_i>=1` iff every edge from `i` to `R_i` is present; otherwise `f_i<=0`, so this family has no intermediate `(1/2,1)` outputs.

Call the responsibility scheme **universally clique-safe** if the threshold-selected set

\[
A_R(Y)=\{i:f_i(Y)\ge1\}
\]

is a clique for every Boolean graph `Y`.

### Theorem 3.1 — sharp constant bound for universally clique-safe one-shot selectors

For every universally clique-safe responsibility scheme,

\[
\mathbb E_{G(n,1/2)}|A_R|<2.
\]

The same bound holds after averaging over an arbitrary graph-independent random choice of the responsibility scheme. Consequently, for every fixed `c>0`,

\[
\Pr\{|A_R|\ge c\log_2 n\}\le \frac{2}{c\log_2 n}\to0.
\]

#### Proof

Universal clique-safety forces a covering condition: for every unordered pair `{i,j}`, at least one of

`j in R_i` or `i in R_j`

must hold. Otherwise set `Y_{ij}=0` and every responsibility edge appearing in `R_i` and `R_j` to `1`; then both `i` and `j` are selected although their mutual edge is absent.

Choose, for every pair, one responsible endpoint guaranteed by this covering condition. This orients the complete graph and produces a tournament `T`. Let `d_i` be its outdegree. Since every oriented responsibility assigned to `i` belongs to `R_i`,

\[
d_i\le |R_i|.
\]

Independence of distinct edge indicators gives

\[
\mathbb E|A_R|=\sum_i2^{-|R_i|}\le\sum_i2^{-d_i}.
\]

Sort the tournament outdegrees increasingly as

`d_(1)<=...<=d_(n)`.

For the `k` vertices of smallest outdegree, their total outdegree in the full tournament is at least the number of internal oriented edges among those `k` vertices, namely

\[
\sum_{i=1}^k d_{(i)}\ge {k\choose2}\qquad (1\le k\le n),
\]

with equality of total sums at `k=n`, since every tournament has total outdegree `{n\choose2}`. Thus the transitive tournament score vector

\[
0,1,\ldots,n-1
\]

majorizes every tournament score vector. Because `x -> 2^{-x}` is convex, Karamata's inequality yields

\[
\sum_i2^{-d_i}
\le
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}2^{-r}
=2(1-2^{-n})<2.
\]

The random-seed version follows by conditioning on the graph-independent seed, and the probability bound is Markov's inequality. ∎

### B6 outcome

This deformation is completely solved and fails decisively: **nonadaptive one-shot pairwise responsibility can make clique validity free, but then it selects fewer than two vertices in expectation.** The transitive-order rule is extremal for this architecture. Hence the half-scale target cannot be reached by replacing the adaptive prefix with a single graph-independent responsibility orientation.

This is an architecture theorem, not a source impossibility theorem.

# 4. B10 — DERANDOMIZE: graph-independent hash sparsification cannot leave only polynomially many candidate paths

The next attempted recovery was to pre-hash or randomly thin the enormous prefix tree before seeing the graph, hoping to leave a small set of candidate half-scale paths and then fix a successful seed.

The first-moment obstruction is exact.

### Theorem 4.1 — candidate-load lower bound for graph-independent sparsification

Let `omega` be any random seed independent of `G~G(n,1/2)`. Given `omega`, let `F_omega` be a family of `k`-vertex subsets. Put

\[
m={k\choose2}.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr_{G,\omega}\{\exists S\in F_\omega:\ S\text{ is a clique}\}
\le 2^{-m}\,\mathbb E_\omega |F_\omega|.
\]

In particular, success probability `1-o(1)` requires

\[
\mathbb E|F_\omega|\ge (1-o(1))2^m.
\]

If, in addition, every seed satisfies the coordinate-load bound

\[
\max_{v\in[n]} |\{S\in F_\omega:v\in S\}|\le \Lambda_n,
\]

then

\[
\Pr(\text{success})
\le
\frac{n\Lambda_n}{k}\,2^{-m}.
\]

Therefore for

\[
k=\left(\frac12-o(1)\right)\log_2 n
\]

and every fixed constant `C`, any graph-independent hashing scheme with `Lambda_n<=n^C` has success probability tending to zero superpolynomially in `n` on the logarithmic scale:

\[
\log_2 \Pr(\text{success})
\le
(C+1)\log_2 n-\left(\frac18-o(1)\right)(\log_2 n)^2.
\]

#### Proof

Conditional on `omega`, every fixed `S` is a clique with probability exactly `2^{-m}`. The union bound gives

\[
\Pr_G(\exists S\in F_\omega\text{ clique}\mid\omega)
\le |F_\omega|2^{-m}.
\]

Average over the seed. For the coordinate-load statement, double-count incidences:

\[
k|F_\omega|
=
\sum_{v=1}^n |\{S\in F_\omega:v\in S\}|
\le n\Lambda_n.
\]

Substitute into the first bound. For half scale, `m=(1/8-o(1))(log_2 n)^2`. ∎

### B10 outcome

This rules out the most tempting form of random prefix masking: **one cannot pre-sparsify the candidate tree, independently of the graph, down to only polynomially many candidate paths per output coordinate and then derandomize the seed.** A half-scale clique requires `2^{Theta((log n)^2)}` graph-independent candidates before graph-dependent adaptivity is used.

Again this is not a lower bound for arbitrary low-degree polynomials. It says that any successful derandomization must retain graph-dependent routing or exploit cancellations/structure beyond a preselected candidate family.

# 5. B30 — ONE SCALE AT A TIME: exact chunking does not reduce the quadratic edge-degree bill

The third attempt was to split the half-depth greedy path into chunks, hoping that a constant or slowly growing chunk size would let exact finite routing inside each chunk compose to total degree `O(log n)`.

For a fixed ordered `k`-tuple `P=(v_1,...,v_k)`, divide the indices into arbitrary consecutive chunks. The exact Boolean transition gate for one chunk must require every newly introduced clique edge — both edges internal to the chunk and edges from the chunk to all earlier vertices of `P` — to be present. These edge sets are disjoint across chunks and their union is precisely all

\[
{k\choose2}
\]

edges of the candidate clique.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact multiplicative chunk propagation is chunk-size invariant

Any architecture that represents each chunk by the exact Boolean product of its newly introduced required edge indicators and propagates a path by multiplying the chunk gates has final degree exactly

\[
{k\choose2},
\]

independently of the chosen chunk sizes.

#### Proof

The degree of a chunk gate is the number of new required edges in that chunk. Multiplying the gates adds degrees because the edge-variable sets are disjoint. Summing over all chunks counts each pair of path vertices exactly once, hence total degree `{k\choose2}`. ∎

At half scale this is

\[
\left(\frac18-o(1)\right)(\log_2 n)^2,
\]

not `O(log n)`.

The previous reports already showed the complementary fact: replacing exact Boolean gates by signed/additive fixed-path margins can keep degree `O(log n)`, but then unrealized-prefix contributions are not zero and the unsolved one-sided masking/compiler problem returns. Thus chunking exposes a genuine dichotomy for this route:

- exact Boolean state propagation pays the full quadratic clique-edge degree;
- additive signed propagation preserves logarithmic degree but does not route one coherent witness.

### B30 outcome

No chunk scale closes the half-scale case inside the exact-gate architecture. The failure is invariant under constant, logarithmic, or nonuniform chunk widths.
