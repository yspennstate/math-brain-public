## (a) Exact blocking statement

The first frozen clause is blocked exactly at the zero-time-constant regime:

\[

\tag{2.1}

Here `mu_F(e_1)=0`, so `a` is the source excess itself. Even if (2.1) is established, the second frozen clause still requires convexity of the resulting positive rate on the full feasible interval for every bounded non-point-mass law.

The immediate missing inequality behind (2.1) is a matching **liminf** / localization statement. Previous runs now have a constructive recovery / limsup side, but not a theorem of the schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\text{global slow passage}
\Longrightarrow
\text{coarse quotient-pseudometric profile with local entropy cost}
-o(n^d),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

uniformly enough to concatenate scales and identify a single limiting cost.

Equation (2.2) is an obligation, not a proved claim.

## (b) Routes tried, and the precise reason each stops

### Route 1 — infinitesimal exponential tilting

The handoff established an atom-safe exponential tilt with exact product relative-entropy cost. This route fails as an infinitesimal strict-uplift mechanism: for the source-valid law

`F = p delta_0 + (1-p) delta_1` with `p>p_c`,

small positive tilts still leave the zero atom above `p_c`, hence the time constant stays zero on a nontrivial parameter interval. Therefore a derivative-at-zero or "every positive tilt strictly increases mu" argument cannot cover the source class.

### Route 2 — finite exponential tilting

The first successor run replaced the failed infinitesimal route with a finite-tilt theorem: for any target macroscopic slope below the support ceiling, one can choose a finite atom-safe tilt for which fast crossings of a box are exponentially unlikely, with `O(n^d)` entropy cost.

This route supplies a valid **recovery / upper-bound mechanism for the rate cost**, but it does not prove rate-limit existence. It does not give the reverse entropy inequality saying every slow environment must pay the same asymptotic local cost.

### Route 3 — direct scalar block subadditivity

This fails structurally because point-to-point FPP is a minimum over all paths. Slow endpoint crossings in adjacent blocks do not glue: a minimizing path can change portals, leave the intended corridor, and exploit cheap short hops. A scalar passage-time statistic discards exactly the boundary information needed for composition.

### Route 4 — complete block boundary metric profiles

The third successor report proves that the complete boundary-to-boundary block metric is an exact sufficient statistic for deterministic gluing. This fixes the information-loss problem.

It stops on probability: no `n^d`-speed LDP / entropy-localization theorem for these complete profiles has been proved in the zero-pseudometric regime. Requiring all microscopic pair distances to be large degenerates into forcing essentially every edge high and overpays entropy; keeping only sparse/macroscopic portals is too weak because of portal hopping.

### Route 5 — pseudometric compactification

The second successor report shows that the relevant quotient-geodesic pseudometric class is compact in the uniform topology. This removes a topological obstacle.

It does not identify the rate. Compactness alone supplies neither local entropy lower bounds nor approximate additivity of those lower bounds across cells when zero clusters percolate.

### Route 6 — positive-edge regularization

Replacing weights below `eta` by `eta` yields deterministic finite-box metric approximation if `eta=eta_n` is sent to zero fast enough. The obstacle is triangular: fixed-`eta` LDPs do not automatically control a sequence `eta_n -> 0`. An interchange

\[
\lim_n a_{n,\eta_n}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\lim_n a_{n,\eta}
\]

has not been justified. The needed estimates would themselves amount to a uniform pseudometric LDP.

### Route 7 — entropy duality

For an event `A`, `-log P(A)` is the minimum relative entropy over laws supported on `A`, and block entropy is superadditive up to a nonnegative dependence term. This correctly identifies the candidate accounting principle.

It stops because the conditioning on the global slow event can create long-range correlations among the many interfaces. There is no proof that the dependence contribution is `o(n^d)` after coarse graining, or that it can be captured by a local integral functional.

### Route 8 — binary cutset packing / coarea

For `0/1` weights, slow passage can be encoded by stacked costly cutsets, and a linear delay requires volume-order total cutset material. This explains the `n^d` scale geometrically.

It stops because a count of necessary costly edges is not an asymptotically sharp probability calculation. The cutsets may share geometry statistically, and a theorem pricing an entire nested stack at matching entropy cost is missing.

### Route 9 — radial multi-shell recovery

The third successor report builds a constructive radial multi-tilt environment and obtains a finite-dimensional variational **limsup** bound. Nested shells avoid the portal-hopping problem because every path must make radial progress.

It stops because no proof shows that an arbitrary optimal slow environment can be replaced, with only `o(n^d)` cost, by such a radial or locally product structure. The reverse inequality is precisely the missing content of (2.2).

### Route 10 — supercritical infinite-zero-cluster access reduction

When `F({0})>p_c`, the unique infinite zero cluster suggests localizing delay into the endpoint access costs. Prior work in this track records inequalities reducing point-to-point delay to access-cost events.

It stops because no `n^d` rate-limit theorem for that weighted access cost, together with the needed endpoint decorrelation/localization at exponential precision, has been established. At the critical boundary `F({0})=p_c`, the infinite-cluster reduction is unavailable entirely.

### Route 11 — import Verges directly

Verges's current point-to-point theorem is the nearest same-model, same-speed result. It proves the full upper-tail limit when `F({0})<p_c`.

It stops exactly where the deterministic limit ceases to be a norm. The positive-metric framework uses a state space bounded below by a positive norm (or, in the extended corollary, remains in the regime where the time constant is nondegenerate). In the residual source class, the macroscopic metric collapses to a pseudometric and zero clusters can connect macroscopic regions for free. The current theorem does not state an LDP for that quotient-pseudometric regime.

### Route 12 — import chemical-distance cut-point or maximal-flow cutset LDPs

Dembin–Nakajima show that space-time cut-points can characterize chemical-distance upper tails and establish a rate in a restricted regime. Dembin–Théret provide rigorous cutset/max-flow large-deviation technology.

These do not transfer as black boxes. Chemical distance has positive graph-distance cost along the infinite open cluster and its studied upper-tail speed is `n`, while the source event here has speed `n^d` and zero-cost percolating clusters. The cutset/max-flow results price one macroscopic interface or flow field; the missing FPP theorem needs control of a **nested stack / full metric profile** whose number of relevant interfaces grows with scale. No theorem found in the checked sources states the required `o(n^d)` interaction estimate.

## (c) What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A successful proof of the first frozen clause must contain all of the following in one compatible argument:

1. **A source-faithful state space in the degenerate regime.** It must retain zero-cost identifications instead of artificially charging percolating zero clusters. The natural object is a quotient pseudometric or an equivalent coarse object.

2. **A local cost with a matching lower bound.** It is not enough to construct slow environments cheaply. One needs a theorem that any environment producing a prescribed coarse slow profile pays at least the integral / sum of the corresponding local entropy costs, up to `o(n^d)`.

3. **Control of dependence between blocks/interfaces.** The conditioning that creates a slow point-to-point metric can correlate `Theta(n)` nested layers. A proof must show that the dependence correction is negligible at volume scale, or absorb it into a finite-dimensional variational object.

4. **Scale gluing without positive ellipticity.** Verges/BGS-type multidimensional subadditivity has to survive when the deterministic metric has zero directions after quotienting, and when blocks can communicate through percolating zero clusters.

5. **A contraction step that also addresses convexity.** To settle the second frozen clause, the identified profile rate or direct point-to-point rate must carry a convexity mechanism on the feasible threshold variable. Neither monotonicity nor continuity is enough.

No previous I0061 route contains Item 2 plus Item 3. That is the central obstruction.
