# 2. B1 — change category: compactify the state space by quotient-geodesic pseudometrics

Verges's compact metric space uses the strict lower ellipticity

\[
a\|x-y\|_1\le D(x,y)\le b\|x-y\|_1,
\qquad a>0.
\]

In the residual regime (1.1), macroscopic zero-cost identifications are not an accident: the correct limit object can be a **pseudometric**. The first question is whether losing the lower bound destroys the compact state-space architecture. It does not.

## Theorem 2.1 — compact quotient-geodesic pseudometric space

Let `X` be a compact convex subset of `R^d` and let `b<infinity`. Let `P_b(X)` be the collection of pseudometrics `D` on `X` such that

1. for every `x,y in X`,
   \[
   0\le D(x,y)\le b\|x-y\|_1;
   \tag{2.1}
   \]
2. the metric quotient
   \[
   X_D:=X/\{x\sim_D y\iff D(x,y)=0\},
   \]
   endowed with the induced distance, is geodesic.

Endow `P_b(X)` with the uniform distance

\[
d_\infty(D_1,D_2)=\sup_{x,y\in X}|D_1(x,y)-D_2(x,y)|.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{(P_b(X),d_\infty)\text{ is compact}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

For every `D in P_b(X)`, the triangle inequality and (2.1) imply

\[
|D(x,y)-D(x',y')|
\le D(x,x')+D(y,y')
\le b(\|x-x'\|_1+\|y-y'\|_1).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the family is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous on compact `X^2`. By Arzela–Ascoli, every sequence `(D_n)` has a uniformly convergent subsequence, say `D_n -> D`.

Symmetry, nonnegativity, `D(x,x)=0`, the triangle inequality and the upper bound (2.1) all pass to the uniform limit. Hence `D` is a continuous pseudometric obeying (2.1). It remains only to prove that its quotient is geodesic.

Fix `x,y in X`. Since each quotient `X_{D_n}` is geodesic, choose a representative `z_n in X` of a midpoint between the classes of `x` and `y`, so

\[
D_n(x,z_n)=D_n(z_n,y)=\frac12D_n(x,y).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Compactness of `X` gives a subsequence `z_n -> z in X`. By uniform convergence and (2.1),

\[
|D_n(x,z_n)-D(x,z)|
\le \|D_n-D\|_\infty+D(z_n,z)
\le \|D_n-D\|_\infty+b\|z_n-z\|_1\to0.
\]

The same holds for the other half, and therefore

\[
D(x,z)=D(z,y)=\frac12D(x,y).
\tag{2.5}
\]

So every pair in the quotient metric space `X_D` has a midpoint. The quotient map `X -> X_D` is continuous because of (2.1); hence `X_D` is compact and therefore complete. A complete metric space in which every pair has a midpoint is geodesic: recursively choose midpoints to define an isometric map on dyadic times and extend it by completeness. Thus `X_D` is geodesic, so `D in P_b(X)`.

Every sequence therefore has a convergent subsequence inside `P_b(X)`. QED.

### Why this matters

This removes one genuine topological obstruction to extending the current random-metric LDP into the zero-time-constant regime. The positive lower metric bound is **not needed for compactness** if one changes category from metrics to quotient-geodesic pseudometrics.

It does **not** prove the LDP: the local rate identification, exponential approximation and gluing arguments still have to be rebuilt in this larger state space. I make no novelty claim for this compactness mechanism; it is elementary once the correct category is chosen.

**B1 outcome:** SUCCESS as a route theorem; no source-clause movement.

# 3. B6 — deform from zero weights to positive weights, and isolate the exact noncommutation obstruction

A natural attempt is to regularize zero weights, use the positive-lower-bound LDP, then send the regularization to zero. The finite-volume comparison is much stronger than it first appears, but it also pinpoints exactly why a fixed-regularization proof does not close the source problem.

Let `G_n=(V_n,E_n)` be any finite induced nearest-neighbour graph contained in a dilate `nX` of a fixed compact box/convex set. Let the original edge weights satisfy

\[
0\le\tau_e\le b.
\]

For `eta>0`, define

\[
\tau_e^{(\eta)}:=\max\{\tau_e,\eta\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Write `T_n` and `T_n^{(eta)}` for the **restricted** passage metrics on `V_n`.

## Theorem 3.1 — deterministic uniform finite-box regularization bound

For every `x,y in V_n`,

\[
0\le T_n^{(\eta)}(x,y)-T_n(x,y)\le \eta(|V_n|-1).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently, for the rescaled lattice metrics

\[
\widetilde T_n(u,v)=n^{-1}T_n(nu,nv),
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde T_n^{(\eta)}-\widetilde T_n\|_\infty
\le \eta\frac{|V_n|-1}{n}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Monotonicity of the edge weights gives the lower inequality. Because `G_n` is finite and all weights are nonnegative, a minimizing path for `T_n(x,y)` can be chosen simple: every repeated-vertex cycle can be erased without increasing passage time. Such a path uses at most `|V_n|-1` edges. Along that path, every edge weight increases by at most `eta`, proving (3.2). Divide by `n` and take the supremum over pairs. QED.

Since `|V_n|=O_X(n^d)`, choosing

\[
\eta_n=n^{-d}
\]

gives the deterministic estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|\widetilde T_n^{(\eta_n)}-\widetilde T_n\|_\infty=O_X(n^{-1}).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular, on every fixed restricted macroscopic box the zero-weight pseudometric sequence is **deterministically exponentially equivalent at speed `n^d`** to a triangular sequence of genuine positive metrics whose lower ellipticity is `eta_n`.

This is a concrete bridge between Theorem 2.1's pseudometric category and the existing positive-metric machinery.

## Why the obvious limit exchange still fails

For fixed `eta>0`, the right side of (3.3) is of order

\[
\eta n^{d-1},
\]

so fixed-`eta` regularization is not uniformly close after rescaling. Conversely, the choice `eta_n=n^{-d}` makes the metrics uniformly close but changes the passage-time law with `n`. Verges's currently proved LDP is for a **fixed** law with a fixed positive lower bound; it does not supply a triangular-array LDP uniform as that lower bound tends to zero.

Thus the exact missing interchange is not vague. Schematically, if `a_{n,eta}` denotes an `n^d`-normalized log probability under regularization, fixed-`eta` theory controls

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n,\eta},
\]

whereas the source problem requires control of a diagonal sequence `a_{n,eta_n}` with `eta_n=O(n^{-d})`. There is no general theorem permitting

\[
\lim_n a_{n,\eta_n}
\stackrel{?}{=}
\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\lim_n a_{n,\eta}
\tag{3.5}
\]

without a uniform/Γ-convergence estimate.

The obstruction is especially real in (1.1): for a Bernoulli law with supercritical zero atom, the ordinary time constant stays exactly zero throughout an open interval of small exponential tilts (proved in the predecessor report), while any fixed positive lower-edge regularization destroys zero-cost travel across long zero clusters. The two operations `n -> infinity` and `eta -> 0` therefore interact nontrivially.

A better deformation for later work is to preserve the zero clusters and only lift **positive** weights,

\[
\tau_e^{[\eta]}=
\begin{cases}
0,&\tau_e=0,\\
\max\{\tau_e,\eta\},&\tau_e>0,
\end{cases}
\tag{3.6}
\]

then work on the quotient by zero clusters. This keeps the degeneracy the source actually has while removing arbitrarily small positive costs. It still requires a pseudometric LDP rather than Verges's present strictly elliptic state space.

**B6 outcome:** SUCCESS as an exact reduction. The finite-box approximation is solved; the remaining missing theorem is a **uniform/triangular pseudometric LDP or Γ-convergence result**, not pointwise regularization. No source-clause movement.

# 4. B25 — count the same rare environment twice

This method produced two exact dual representations. The first is measure-theoretic and applies to every bounded law; the second is a particularly clean combinatorial duality for the atomic `0/1` stress class inside the unresolved regime.

## 4.1 Exact entropy variational identity for any rare event

Let `P` be any probability law and let `A` have `0<P(A)<1`. Then

\[
\boxed{
-\log P(A)
=
\inf\{D(Q\|P):Q\ll P,\ Q(A)=1\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The unique minimizer modulo null sets is `Q=P(.|A)`.

### Proof

For any `Q` supported on `A`,

\[
D(Q\|P)
=
D(Q\|P(\cdot|A))-\log P(A)
\ge -\log P(A),
\]

with equality for the conditional law. QED.

If the edge set of a finite box is partitioned into disjoint blocks `E_1,...,E_m` and `P=\otimes_i P_i` is the product environment, then every `Q` satisfies the exact entropy decomposition

\[
D(Q\|P)
=
D\!\left(Q\middle\|\bigotimes_i Q_i\right)
+
\sum_i D(Q_i\|P_i)
\ge
\sum_iD(Q_i\|P_i),
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `Q_i` is the block marginal.

Equations (4.1)–(4.2) identify the missing gluing theorem sharply. The upper-bound construction from the predecessor report supplies explicit near-product alternatives `Q` whose entropy cost is `Theta(n^d)` and whose coarse metric is slow. For the reverse direction, the event-conditioned optimizer in (4.1) is highly dependent. To obtain a rate limit, one needs to prove that after coarse graining its entropy cannot beat the integral/sum of **local pseudometric block costs** by more than `o(n^d)`. That is the exact entropy-localization/Γ-liminf obligation.

This is the promised “same environment twice”: globally it is the conditional environment realizing `-log P(A)`; locally it pays the sum of block relative entropies plus a nonnegative multi-information term.

## 4.2 Exact cutset-packing duality for Bernoulli `0/1` passage times

Now let `G=(V,E)` be any finite connected graph, let `s,t in V`, and let every edge cost be

\[
c_e\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Write

\[
T_c(s,t)=\min_{\pi:s\to t}\sum_{e\in\pi}c_e.
\]

### Theorem 4.2 — binary FPP distance equals maximum disjoint costly-cut packing

\[
\boxed{
T_c(s,t)
=
\max\{k:\text{there are }k\text{ pairwise edge-disjoint }s\!-\!t\text{ cutsets, all of whose edges have cost }1\}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

If `C_1,...,C_k` are pairwise edge-disjoint `s-t` cutsets consisting entirely of cost-one edges, every `s-t` path crosses each cutset. Since the cutsets are edge-disjoint, the path contains at least `k` distinct cost-one edges. Hence

\[
T_c(s,t)\ge k.
\tag{4.4}
\]

For the reverse inequality, put `m=T_c(s,t)` and define the integer-valued shortest-path potential

\[
\phi(v)=\min\{T_c(s,v),m\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Then `phi(s)=0`, `phi(t)=m`, and for every edge `uv`,

\[
|\phi(u)-\phi(v)|\le c_{uv}\le1.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For each `j=0,...,m-1`, let

\[
S_j=\{v:\phi(v)\le j\}.
\]

Then `s in S_j` and `t notin S_j`, so `delta S_j` is an `s-t` cutset. If an edge `uv` crosses this cut with `phi(u)<=j<phi(v)`, integrality plus (4.6) forces

\[
\phi(u)=j,\qquad \phi(v)=j+1,\qquad c_{uv}=1.
\]

Therefore every edge of `delta S_j` has cost one. Moreover an edge can cross at most one level `j`, so these `m` cutsets are pairwise edge-disjoint. Thus the maximum in (4.3) is at least `m`. Combined with (4.4), equality follows. QED.

This is a shortest-path LP/coarea duality; it is likely folklore in equivalent forms and **no novelty is claimed**. Its value here is that it says exactly what the unresolved Bernoulli member of Question 18 asks us to price: a linear passage delay is equivalent to packing a linear number of disjoint positive-edge cutsets.

### Volume-order geometric certificate

The same potential gives a clean explanation of the `n^d` scale. Consider Bernoulli `0/1` costs in the finite `l1` ball `B_1(r)` and suppose

\[
T_c(0,\partial B_1(r))\ge m,
\qquad 1\le m\le r.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Define

\[
\phi(v)=\min\{T_c(0,v),m\},
\qquad
u(v)=m-\phi(v)
\]

inside the ball and extend `u=0` outside. Then `u(0)=m`, `u=0` on the boundary, `u` is integer-valued and nearest-neighbour 1-Lipschitz, and every edge on which `u` changes has cost one.

For `k=0,...,m-1`, set `A_k={u>k}`. Since a coordinate path to `v` costs at most `||v||_1`,

\[
B_1(0,m-k-1)\subseteq A_k.
\tag{4.8}
\]

For any finite `A subset Z^d`, the edge-isoperimetric bound

\[
|\partial_E A|\ge2d|A|^{(d-1)/d}
\tag{4.9}
\]

follows by counting the two boundary edges contributed by every nonempty line in each coordinate direction, then applying Loomis–Whitney and AM–GM. Since integer 1-Lipschitz increments are `0` or `1`, discrete coarea gives

\[
|\{e:u\text{ changes across }e\}|
=
\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|\partial_E A_k|.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Combining (4.8)–(4.10), and using the cube

\[
[-\lfloor j/d\rfloor,\lfloor j/d\rfloor]^d
\subseteq B_1(0,j),
\]

gives the explicit certificate

\[
\boxed{
|\{e:c_e=1\text{ and }u\text{ changes across }e\}|
\ge
2d\sum_{j=1}^{m}
\left(2\Big\lfloor\frac{j-1}{d}\Big\rfloor+1\right)^{d-1}
=\Omega_d(m^d).
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

Thus a linear radial delay forces a **volume-order cutset-stack certificate**, not merely one surface-order obstruction. This matches the source's `n^d` large-deviation speed and makes the block-pseudometric/entropy route geometrically natural.

Equation (4.11) by itself is not a probability estimate: a typical large box already contains `Theta(n^d)` positive edges. The missing content is the entropy/count of the *organized nested cutset profile*, not just its cardinality.

**B25 outcome:** SUCCESS as an exact dual reformulation and volume-scale certificate; no source-clause movement.
