# 2. B23 — exact atomic obstruction to infinitesimal tilt uplift

The source-valid stress class is the two-point bounded law

\[
F=p\delta_0+(1-p)\delta_1,
\qquad p>p_c(\mathbb Z^d).
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is inside Question 18: bounded and non-point-mass.

Under exponential tilt,

\[
F_\theta(\{0\})
=
\frac{p}{p+(1-p)e^\theta}
=:p_\theta.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The threshold where the zero atom drops to `p_c` is

\[
\boxed{
\theta_c
=
\log\frac{p(1-p_c)}{p_c(1-p)}
>0.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For every `0 <= theta < theta_c`, one has `p_theta>p_c`. In ordinary FPP on `Z^d`, the time constant is zero whenever the zero atom is at least `p_c`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mu(F_\theta,e_1)=\mu(F,e_1)=0,
\qquad 0\le\theta<\theta_c.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

So the hoped-for statement

> every positive exponential tilt gives a strict time-constant uplift

is false even for the simplest bounded atomic law. There is an **open interval of tilt parameters on which the time constant is exactly flat**.

This is not a counterexample to Question 18. It is a rigorous counterexample to an overstrong proof architecture. In particular, differentiating `mu(F_theta)` at `theta=0` cannot solve the full bounded-law problem.

# 3. B27 — swap the quantifiers: a finite tilt always gives arbitrarily large metric uplift

The failed statement in Section 2 asks for strict uplift for **every sufficiently small** `theta`. The owner-specified B27 alternative asks instead:

> for each target upper-tail level, does there exist one finite `theta` that produces a uniform finite-box metric uplift?

The answer is yes for the full bounded source class. This is the main new theorem of this run.

## Theorem 3.1 — atom-safe macroscopic finite-box uplift for every bounded law

Let `F` be any non-point-mass probability law with bounded support contained in `[0,b]`, where

\[
b=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}F<\infty.
\]

Let `F_theta` be its exponential tilt. Fix any

\[
0<c<b.
\]

Then there exists a finite `theta>0` and constants `kappa>0`, `C<infinity`, depending only on `F,c,d`, with the following properties.

### (i) Point-to-sphere / unrestricted point-to-point uplift

For every `n`, under iid `F_theta` edge weights,

\[
\Pr_\theta\!\left(
\exists\text{ a path from }0\text{ to }\partial B_1(n)
\text{ with passage time }\le cn
\right)
\le Ce^{-\kappa n},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `B_1(n)={x:||x||_1<=n}`. Consequently

\[
\Pr_\theta(T(0,ne_1)\le cn)\le Ce^{-\kappa n}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### (ii) Uniform restricted metric-profile uplift

For every fixed `eta>0`, let `T^{(n)}_theta` be the passage metric using only paths contained in `B_1(n)`. Then

\[
\Pr_\theta\!\left(
\exists x,y\in B_1(n):
\|x-y\|_1\ge\eta n,
\quad
T^{(n)}_\theta(x,y)\le c\|x-y\|_1
\right)
\le
C(2n+1)^{2d}e^{-\kappa\eta n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence with probability tending to one exponentially fast, the entire macroscopic boundary-to-boundary restricted metric profile dominates `c ||x-y||_1` simultaneously.

### (iii) Time constant reaches the support ceiling under finite tilts

For every `c<b`, some finite `theta` satisfies

\[
\mu(F_\theta,e_1)\ge c.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Since `theta -> F_theta` is stochastically increasing and every time constant is at most `b`,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\theta\to\infty}\mu(F_\theta,e_1)=b.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus for every feasible Question-18 threshold

\[
c=\mu(F,e_1)+\epsilon<b,
\]
there exists a finite exponential tilt giving at least that macroscopic uplift, **even when `F({0})>p_c` and infinitesimal uplift is impossible**.

### Proof

Choose `delta>0` so that

\[
t:=b-\delta>c,
\qquad
r:=\frac{c}{t}<1.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Call an edge `high` if its weight is at least `t`. Under `F_theta`, let

\[
q_\theta:=\Pr_\theta(\tau<t).
\]

Because `b` is the essential supremum, the number

\[
m_\delta:=F([b-\delta/2,b])
\]

is strictly positive. Therefore

\[
q_\theta
\le
\frac{e^{\theta(b-\delta)}}
{m_\delta e^{\theta(b-\delta/2)}}
=
\frac1{m_\delta}e^{-\theta\delta/2}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Choose `lambda>0` so large that

\[
\lambda(1-r)>\log(2d).
\tag{3.8}
\]

Define

\[
A_\theta
:=
2d\,[q_\theta+(1-q_\theta)e^{-\lambda}].
\tag{3.9}
\]

As `theta -> infinity`,

\[
A_\theta\to2de^{-\lambda}<1,
\]

and

\[
\lambda r+\log A_\theta
\longrightarrow
\log(2d)-\lambda(1-r)<0.
\]

Hence a finite `theta` can be fixed so that, for some `kappa>0`,

\[
A_\theta<1,
\qquad
\lambda r+\log A_\theta\le-\kappa.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Now fix a self-avoiding path `gamma` of length `ell`. Let `H_gamma` be the number of high edges on it. Since its edges are independent under the tilted product measure,

\[
H_\gamma\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(\ell,1-q_\theta).
\]

If `gamma` joins vertices at `l1` distance `m` and has total passage time at most `cm`, then every high edge contributes at least `t`, so

\[
H_\gamma t\le cm,
\qquad
H_\gamma\le rm.
\tag{3.11}
\]

For the `lambda` fixed above, exponential Markov gives

\[
\Pr_\theta(H_\gamma\le rm)
\le
\exp(\lambda r m)
[q_\theta+(1-q_\theta)e^{-\lambda}]^\ell.
\tag{3.12}
\]

There are at most `(2d)^ell` self-avoiding paths of length `ell` starting at a prescribed vertex. Summing over all `ell>=m`,

\[
\Pr_\theta\bigl(\exists\text{ such a fast path}\bigr)
\le
\frac{\exp(\lambda r m)A_\theta^m}{1-A_\theta}
\le
\frac{e^{-\kappa m}}{1-A_\theta}.
\tag{3.13}
\]

For (i), any path from `0` to `ne_1` must first hit the `l1` sphere of radius `n`. If the full path has passage time at most `cn`, then its prefix to that first hit also has passage time at most `cn`; erasing loops cannot increase passage time. Apply (3.13) with `m=n` to obtain (3.1)–(3.2).

For (ii), fix `x,y` with `m=||x-y||_1>=eta n`. A minimizing path for the restricted metric can be loop-erased, and all of its edges lie in the tilted box. Equation (3.13) applies. There are at most `(2n+1)^{2d}` ordered pairs `x,y` in `B_1(n)`, so a union bound gives (3.3).

For (iii), standard subadditivity gives

\[
\frac{T_\theta(0,ne_1)}n\to\mu(F_\theta,e_1)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

If this limit were strictly below `c`, then `Pr_theta(T(0,ne_1)<=cn)` would tend to one, contradicting (3.2). Therefore `mu(F_theta,e_1)>=c`. Since this holds for every `c<b`, while trivially `mu(F_theta,e_1)<=b`, and stochastic ordering makes `mu(F_theta,e_1)` nondecreasing in `theta`, (3.5) follows. QED.

### Why this theorem matters

This closes the precise route-level obligation left by the predecessor report: a quantitative strict **finite-box metric-profile uplift** under an atom-safe change of measure exists for every bounded non-point-mass law. It survives the percolating-minimum regime where infinitesimal time-constant comparison fails.

The theorem does **not** prove the rate limit. The missing issue has moved again: one needs a reverse/scale-comparison argument showing that near-optimal costly box metric profiles can be glued or coarse-grained with asymptotically additive cost, including when the zero cluster makes the unperturbed limiting metric degenerate.

# 4. Entropy corollary: a full-class volume-cost change of measure

The predecessor handoff proved the exact tensorized relative entropy identity

\[
D(F_\theta^{\otimes N}\|F^{\otimes N})
=N\,[\theta\Lambda'(\theta)-\Lambda(\theta)].
\tag{4.1}
\]

Theorem 3.1 can now be combined with it without any density hypothesis.

Fix feasible

\[
c=\mu(F,e_1)+\epsilon<b.
\]

Let `E_n` be the edges with both endpoints in `B_1(n)`, and define a mixed product law `Q_n` by using `F_theta` on `E_n` and the original `F` outside. Set

\[
A_n=\{T(0,ne_1)>cn\}.
\]

The proof of Theorem 3.1(i) only uses the prefix of a putative fast path up to its first hit of `partial B_1(n)`. Every edge of that prefix lies in `E_n`. Consequently

\[
q_n:=Q_n(A_n)\ge1-Ce^{-\kappa n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
D(Q_n\|P)
=|E_n|D(F_\theta\|F)
\le d(2n+1)^dD(F_\theta\|F).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Apply data processing for the binary observation `1_{A_n}`:

\[
D(Q_n\|P)
\ge
D(\operatorname{Bern}(q_n)\|\operatorname{Bern}(P(A_n)))
\ge
q_n\log\frac1{P(A_n)}-h(q_n).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
-\log P(A_n)
\le
\frac{D(Q_n\|P)+h(q_n)}{q_n}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Dividing by `n^d` and using `q_n->1` yields the explicit finite upper bound

\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
-\frac1{n^d}\log P(A_n)
\le
 d2^d\,D(F_\theta\|F)<\infty.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

This is compatible with Kesten's classical volume-speed bounds, but its mechanism is useful here: it gives a completely atom-safe **constructive environmental cost** whose tilted box metric is uniformly slow with overwhelming probability.

Again, (4.6) is not rate-limit existence. It controls one side of the scale comparison and gives a candidate variational upper bound on the eventual positive rate.
