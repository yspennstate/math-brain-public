## 4. Current B1 — change category: down-closure holes as routing polymers

This subsection is diagnostic algebra used to identify the missing object; it is **not promoted as a new theorem claim in this stuck run**.

Start from P13's preserved exact dual. For an arbitrary source-side vertex set `A`, let `D` be its coordinate down-closure, put `H=D\A`, and let `O=V_n\D`. Because `D` is a downset, no directed grid edge can run from `O` into `D`. Hence the only incoming edges of `A` come from `H`, while outgoing edges of `A` split into `A->H` and `A->O`.

Comparing the arbitrary-cut objective with the downset `D` gives the exact bookkeeping identity

\[
\phi_C(A)-|C\cap\delta^+(D)|
=|C\cap E(A,H)|-|E(H,A)|-|C\cap E(H,O)|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Decompose `H` into connected components `H_i` in the underlying undirected grid. Equation (4.1) then separates into component scores

\[
g_i=c_i^{\rm in}-r_i-c_i^{\rm out},
\tag{4.2}
\]

where `c_i^in` is the number of closed `A->H_i` edges, `r_i` is the number of `H_i->A` re-entry edges, and `c_i^out` is the number of closed `H_i->O` edges.

Every nonempty `H_i` has `r_i>=1`: a vertex of `H_i` belongs to the down-closure because it lies southwest of some vertex of `A`; a monotone path to that vertex stays inside `D`, and the first step that leaves `H_i` must enter `A`. Thus a positive component has to satisfy at least

`c_i^in >= r_i + c_i^out + 1`.

The preceding exact four-defect witness is the smallest concrete instance of this picture: its arbitrary cut is a downset with one connected hole, one re-entry, and a positive component advantage.

What this category change **does not** supply is the probability estimate. A hole can have arbitrarily large longitudinal span. Positive score is controlled by a boundary *imbalance*, not by a number of required closed defects proportional to the hole perimeter. Therefore summing `q^k` over bounded local gadgets cannot justify (3.1).

**B1 outcome:** the natural adaptive object is a downset skeleton decorated by connected hole/routing polymers. This is a sharper description of the obstacle, but it gives no proved positive `q` exponent and therefore no metric movement.

## 5. Current B13 — find what only increases: overloaded re-entry, not raw repeat count

Again this is a stuck-run diagnosis, not a promoted new theorem.

The preceding B12 identity says that for a minimum `W`-chain cover of the closed edges, if an arbitrary contour has routing-tax excess `t`, then its selected forward closed exits have chain-repeat surplus `R` satisfying

`R >= r+t`,

where `r=|delta^-(A)|` is the number of incoming/backward cut edges.

For each chain, place one demand between every two consecutive selected exits. A directed connector between those exits must cross at least one incoming edge of `A`; assign the demand to one such edge. If `m_e` is the number of assigned demands at incoming edge `e`, then `sum_e m_e=R`, and consequently

`R-r <= sum_e (m_e-1)_+`.

Thus the tax cannot be explained merely by repeated hits on chains. What matters is **overloaded re-entry**: at least two distinct chain-repeat demands must be paid by the same incoming edge (or by a noncanonical equivalent bottleneck). The minimal P13 witness has exactly this character: one incoming edge coordinates repeats from two chains.

This diagnosis explains both earlier failures. Counting closed defects alone ignores the shared bottleneck, while deleting a backward excursion destroys the coordination that lets several chain repeats use one re-entry. To turn this into a proof one would need a canonical assignment/decomposition for an optimal random cut and a stochastic estimate on the density of overloaded re-entries along a one-dimensional skeleton. Neither is currently available.

**B13 outcome:** a useful candidate monovariant is the overload `sum_e(m_e-1)_+`; the unresolved step is to make the assignment canonical and prove an adaptive-entropy bound. No metric movement.

## 6. Current B26 — hunt the sporadic case: long one-reentry ribbons defeat a naive Peierls energy

The most dangerous architecture is not another fixed four-edge gadget. It is a **single connected hole with one re-entry and arbitrarily large span**.

For an interior hole component `H_i`, the deterministic directed boundary balance gives

`|E(A,H_i)| = |E(H_i,A)| + |E(H_i,O)|`.

Substituting open/closed counts into (4.2) shows that the component score can be viewed as a passage-time imbalance between its two boundary arcs. In particular there is no automatic energy proportional to the longitudinal span: a long one-neck ribbon can in principle have positive score because of a fluctuation in boundary open counts while paying only one re-entry.

This kills the simplest hoped-for Peierls statement, namely “a polymer of span L needs cL prescribed closed edges.” The true four-defect minimality theorem still says a **genuine routing-tax configuration** needs at least four closed edges globally, but it does not force those four edges into a bounded-diameter pattern. Hence the previous `O(q^4)` predetermined-gadget estimate cannot be upgraded by multiplying a local gadget density by area or by contour length.

I tried to push this architecture toward a source-valid counterexample. I did not obtain one. The unresolved competition is exactly between (i) the entropy of choosing a long hole after seeing the environment and (ii) the probability cost of obtaining enough boundary imbalance *and* enough baseline Hammersley structure for the arbitrary cut to beat every downset. A positive component relative to its own down-closure is not by itself positive routing tax because `W_n(C)` may be achieved by a different downset.

**B26 outcome:** no refutation; the smallest plausible obstruction to the current proof strategy is an arbitrarily long, one-reentry routing polymer. Any successful proof must control this family before multi-hole polymers matter.

## 8. What a proof must contain that no route so far contains

A successful proof needs a **uniform adaptive-entropy theorem**. Concretely, it must take an optimizing arbitrary cut after the environment is revealed and decompose it relative to a monotone/Hammersley skeleton into irreducible holes in such a way that:

- the number of admissible anchors is longitudinal (`O(n)` after the right rescaling), not area (`O(n^2)`);
- each irreducible hole is charged by a passage/imbalance quantity whose tail beats the entropy of its possible span and shape;
- overloaded re-entry is not double-counted between polymers;
- the estimate is uniform enough to survive `n->infinity` first and then `q->0`;
- its total expected contribution is `o(n sqrt(q))`.

BGT 2022 has the first-order rare-zero passage-line analysis for ordinary Bernoulli FPP. Krishnan--Rassoul-Agha--Seppäläinen 2023 has convex shift/optimizer-length duality and modification arguments. What P0219 lacks is the corresponding uniform theorem when only **open backward** crossings receive the shift and the optimizer may create free-backstep holes.
