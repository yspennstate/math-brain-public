## 3. B2 — attack the negation first: the obvious microscopic-witness architecture is too sparse

A natural attempt to refute Aldous's coefficient is to replicate the exact four-defect P13 routing-tax witness many times and hope its one-unit excess accumulates on square-root scale.

That simplest architecture fails before any simulation is needed.

Fix any finite deterministic defect gadget `P` consisting of `m` specified edges, and fix a predetermined family of `M_n=O(n)` pairwise disjoint translates of `P` along a macroscopic transversal. In Bernoulli closed-edge density `q`, the event that all `m` prescribed gadget edges are closed has probability exactly `q^m`. Hence, even under the unrealistically favorable assumption that each occurring gadget contributes one full unit of routing tax and all contributions add independently,

\[
\mathbb E[\text{gadget tax}]\le M_n q^m=O(nq^m).
\]

For the first possible routing-tax gadget `m=4`, this is `O(nq^4)`, so after division by `n` it is `O(q^4)=o(\sqrt q)`.

**Outcome B2.** The most literal negation architecture — predetermined microscopic replication of the known four-defect obstruction — cannot alter the source coefficient. This does **not** prove that all routing tax is microscopic. The thermodynamic optimizer is adaptive and can choose among order-`n^2` defect opportunities, while the limit `n->infinity` is taken before `q->0`. Any genuine counterexample architecture would therefore have to exploit either a growing spatial scale, an adaptive extreme-value selection among many candidate clusters, or collective interaction among defects that are not fixed-size independent replicas. No source-valid counterexample was found.

This is exactly why a small-grid counterexample search would be low value here: the finite obstruction is already known and the issue is its thermodynamic selection entropy.

## 4. B5 — add a backward-crossing penalty and differentiate

This method produced the main exact reduction of the run.

### 4.1 The interpolation

Use the planar-dual/free-backstep formulation already established in the preserved R01 work. For a separating dual contour `gamma`, classify its crossings of primal edges relative to the positive source-to-sink orientation:

- `F(gamma)`: forward crossings;
- `B(gamma)`: backward crossings;
- `C_f(gamma)`: forward crossings of closed primal edges;
- `O_b(gamma)`: backward crossings of open primal edges.

For fixed endpoints the net displacement satisfies

\[
F(\gamma)-B(\gamma)=K_n,
\]

where `K_n=2n+O(1)` under Aldous's boundary convention.

For `lambda in [0,1]`, charge an open backward crossing `lambda` and leave a closed backward crossing free in the passage cost. Equivalently define the deficit score

\[
S_\lambda(\gamma)
:=C_f(\gamma)-B(\gamma)-\lambda O_b(\gamma).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
G_{n,q}(\lambda):=\max_\gamma S_\lambda(\gamma).
\tag{4.2}
\]

At `lambda=0`, (4.1) is the Aldous/P13 free-backstep deficit: backward crossings are charged only through the unavoidable net-displacement term `B`, with no extra charge for being open. Under the same wired convention, `G_{n,q}(0)` is the finite flow-deficit/path-cover quantity; Aldous's exact corner convention differs only by `O(1)` and disappears after division by `n`.

At `lambda=1`, the score equals the deficit of ordinary Bernoulli passage cost. Indeed an ordinary contour pays `1` for each open forward crossing and `1` for each open backward crossing, so

\[
K_n-(O_f+O_b)
=(F-B)-O_f-O_b
=C_f-B-O_b=S_1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus this interpolation changes precisely the accounting rule that separates P0219 from ordinary FPP.

### 4.2 Exact finite envelope theorem

For every fixed environment and finite `n`, `G_{n,q}` is the maximum of finitely many affine functions

\[
\lambda\mapsto a_\gamma-\lambda b_\gamma,
\qquad b_\gamma=O_b(\gamma)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]

Therefore:

1. `G_{n,q}(lambda)` is continuous, convex, nonincreasing and piecewise linear on `[0,1]`.
2. If `lambda_1<lambda_2`, and `gamma_i` is any optimizer at `lambda_i`, then
   \[
   O_b(\gamma_1)\ge O_b(\gamma_2).
   \tag{4.4}
   \]
3. At every differentiability point,
   \[
   G'_{n,q}(\lambda)=-O_b(\gamma_\lambda)
   \tag{4.5}
   \]
   for every optimizer `gamma_lambda` having the active slope there.
4. Consequently
   \[
   \boxed{
   G_{n,q}(0)-G_{n,q}(1)
   =\int_0^1 B_{n,q}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,
   }
   \tag{4.6}
   \]
   where `B_{n,q}(lambda)` is the open-backward-crossing count of an optimizer at almost every `lambda` (values at the finitely many breakpoints are immaterial).

#### Proof of the monotonicity (4.4)

Write `a_i=S_0(gamma_i)` and `b_i=O_b(gamma_i)`. Optimality at the two parameter values gives

\[
a_1-\lambda_1b_1\ge a_2-\lambda_1b_2,
\]

\[
a_2-\lambda_2b_2\ge a_1-\lambda_2b_1.
\]

Adding yields

\[
(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)(b_1-b_2)\ge0,
\]

hence `b_1>=b_2`. The derivative and integral identities are the standard finite maximum-of-affine-functions envelope calculation; no regularity assumption on the random environment is needed.

Because `B_{n,q}(lambda)` is finite at finite `n`, Fubini gives the exact expectation identity

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E[G_{n,q}(0)-G_{n,q}(1)]}{n}
=\int_0^1\frac{\mathbb E B_{n,q}(\lambda)}{n}\,d\lambda.
}
\tag{4.7}
\]

This is a theorem-sized exact finite reduction, with the complete proof above; it is not a source closure theorem and it does not by itself move `alpha_tax`.

### 4.3 Why this is useful

Basdevant--Gouéré--Théret already prove that ordinary Bernoulli FPP and its oriented/Hammersley comparator have the same `sqrt(q)` first-order term. Therefore the genuinely new P0219 correction can be isolated as the `lambda`-integrated density of open backward crossings of optimal interpolating contours. A sufficient missing estimate is now

\[
\lim_{q\downarrow0}\frac1{\sqrt q}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\int_0^1\frac{\mathbb E B_{n,q}(\lambda)}{n}\,d\lambda=0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Equation (4.8) is not proved here. Its advantage over the raw `U-W` statement is that the integrand has the exact monotonicity (4.4) and is an optimizer observable, which opens modification/convex-duality methods.

## 5. B30 — localize by scale: what the sparse-defect theorem really does and does not prove

R06's global theorem says the first deterministic routing-tax obstruction needs four defects. It is tempting to turn that immediately into a `q^4` stochastic estimate. That inference is invalid in the sequential source limit.

For any *fixed predetermined* bounded gadget family the B2 calculation is legitimate and gives a high power of `q`. But the maximizing contour/cut is chosen after observing all defects in an `n x n` region. There are order `n^2` possible microscopic locations and vastly many candidate contours. Since `n->infinity` occurs first, a union bound over all local defect quadruples destroys the naive `q^4` estimate.

So the scale decomposition has to count **routing-relevant defects along a near-optimal random contour**, not defect clusters in the entire area.

The interpolation supplies a cleaner scale variable. Because `B_{n,q}(lambda)` is nonincreasing in `lambda`, split

\[
\int_0^1 B_{n,q}(\lambda)d\lambda
=\int_0^\eta B_{n,q}(\lambda)d\lambda
 +\int_\eta^1 B_{n,q}(\lambda)d\lambda.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For `lambda>=eta>0`, every open backward crossing has a definite energetic price. This is the regime in which the path-counting/modification arguments of Basdevant--Gouéré--Théret and the shifted-weight convex-duality machinery of Krishnan--Rassoul-Agha--Seppäläinen are plausibly portable. The singular layer `0<=lambda<eta`, where backsteps are almost free, is the genuinely P0219-specific obstruction.

**Outcome B30.** The right scale attack is not “four defects imply `q^4`.” It is a two-parameter estimate controlling the entropy of adaptive nonmonotone contours against the `lambda`-penalty, uniformly enough to let `eta=eta(q)` tend to zero while keeping both terms in (5.1) `o(n sqrt(q))`. I do not yet have that bound. This identifies precisely why the current cluster-counting idea stops and what a successful replacement must contain.

A natural sharp probability target for a later run is a modification bound of the form: for `lambda>=eta(q)`, contours achieving a fixed positive density of open backward crossings pay an exponentially small probability cost strong enough to survive the count of admissible contours; then separately control the vanishing-`lambda` layer by an extremal deterministic argument. This is a target, not a theorem.
