## 3. Dual-contour notation for the recovery class

Use the planar-dual formulation already derived in R01 and preserved by this track. A separating dual contour `gamma` from the source-side corner to the sink-side corner crosses primal directed edges. Traverse the contour in its canonical source-to-sink separating orientation.

A dual step is called **forward** when it crosses a primal edge in the outgoing direction of the source-side cut and **backward** when it crosses a primal edge in the incoming direction. At `lambda=0` the exact score is

\[
S_0(\gamma)=C_f(\gamma)-B(\gamma),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where `C_f` is the number of closed primal edges crossed forward and `B` is the number of backward crossings, regardless of whether those backward primal edges are open or closed.

A monotone separator has `B=0`; maximizing its closed-forward count is exactly the downset/Hammersley quantity `W_n(C)`.

Let `Gamma_n^(1)` be the class of simple separating contours with **exactly one backward step**. This class contains the dual boundaries of the Section-I recovery geometry: a single connected hole/re-entry component whose relevant cut boundary has one incoming primal edge. Under the one-reentry assumption there is no additional nontrivial closed boundary component: any separate closed dual loop needs at least two non-forward steps to return to its starting coordinate, while the entire cut has only one incoming/backward crossing.

Define

\[
G^{(1)}_n(C)=\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma_n^{(1)}}S_0(\gamma).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The restricted routing-tax excess is

\[
R^{(1)}_n(C)=\bigl(G^{(1)}_n(C)-W_n(C)\bigr)_+.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The chosen recovery target is to prove

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E R_n^{(1)}(C_q)}n=0
\quad\text{for every fixed }q,
\tag{3.4}
\]

which is stronger than the requested `o(sqrt(q))` thermodynamic contribution because the thermodynamic density is then exactly zero before `q` is sent to zero.

## 4. B3 — prove something stronger: exact one-backstep shortcut lemma

The key point is that the apparently two-dimensional one-hole geometry becomes one-dimensional after passing to the dual contour.

### Lemma 4.1 — a single backward step is a width-one ribbon excursion

Rotate/refelect dual coordinates so that forward steps are `N` and `E`. Let `gamma` be a simple separator with exactly one backward step. If that step is `W`, then the path has a unique decomposition

\[
\gamma=\alpha\,W\,N^L\,E\,\beta
\tag{4.1}
\]

for some `L>=0`, where `alpha` and `beta` use only `N,E`. If the backward step is `S`, the symmetric form is

\[
\gamma=\alpha\,S\,E^L\,N\,\beta.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The path obtained by replacing the displayed excursion by the straight parallel segment,

\[
\pi=\alpha\,N^L\,\beta
\quad\text{or}\quad
\pi=\alpha\,E^L\,\beta,
\tag{4.3}
\]

is a valid monotone separating contour.

#### Proof

Suppose the unique backward step is `W`. After that step, no `S` or further `W` step can occur. Since the endpoint displacement is the same as for a monotone separator, at least one subsequent `E` step must occur to compensate the single unit lost in the horizontal coordinate. Before the **first** such `E`, the only possible steps are `N`; hence the segment is exactly `W N^L E`. The prefix before the unique backward step and the suffix after the compensating `E` contain only `N,E`.

The shortcut `N^L` lies on the column immediately to the east of the detour segment. Its vertices are coordinatewise between the endpoint of `alpha` and the starting point of `beta`. Since `alpha` is monotone and ends at the lower shortcut endpoint while `beta` is monotone and starts at the upper shortcut endpoint, the shortcut cannot meet either except at its endpoints. Thus `pi` is a simple monotone separator. The `S` case is the rotated version. ∎

So the straight/ribbon geometry is not merely an extremizer inside the one-reentry class: **it is the only primitive dual geometry produced by one backward step.** The more complicated two-dimensional set picture was hiding this exact one-dimensional normal form.

### Lemma 4.2 — score gain is bounded by a centered strip sum

For the `W N^L E` case, let

- `xi_1,...,xi_L` be the closed-edge indicators on the `L` forward steps of the detour side;
- `eta_1,...,eta_L` be the closed-edge indicators on the `L` shortcut steps;
- `zeta` be the closed-edge indicator on the compensating final `E` step.

Then

\[
S_0(\gamma)-S_0(\pi)
=\sum_{j=1}^L(\xi_j-\eta_j)+(\zeta-1)
\le \sum_{j=1}^L(\xi_j-\eta_j).
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

The prefix and suffix scores cancel. The detour contributes the `L` closed-forward indicators `xi_j`, the final forward indicator `zeta`, and exactly one backward penalty `-1`. The shortcut contributes the parallel closed-forward indicators `eta_j`. Since `zeta<=1`, (4.4) follows. ∎

Because `pi` is monotone,

\[
S_0(\pi)\le W_n(C).
\]

Therefore every one-backstep contour obeys

\[
S_0(\gamma)-W_n(C)
\le
\sum_{j=1}^L(\xi_j-\eta_j).
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is the sought shape-independent reduction: the complete one-reentry gain is bounded by a **centered difference of the two sides of a width-one strip**. It does not require a local four-defect enumeration and it includes arbitrarily long ribbons.

## 5. B15 — break symmetry: the feared long ribbon is harmless in thermodynamic density

For Bernoulli closed-edge density `q`, the pairs `(xi_j,eta_j)` on a fixed strip use disjoint primal edges. Hence

\[
Z_j=\xi_j-\eta_j
\]

are independent, mean zero, and lie in `[-1,1]`. Their variance is `2q(1-q)`, but the proof below does not even need the small variance.

Let `M_n` be the maximum of

\[
\sum_{j=1}^L Z_j
\]

over **all** vertical or horizontal width-one strips that can arise from Lemma 4.1. There are at most `4 n^3` such oriented strip intervals: two orientations, at most `O(n)` adjacent line pairs, at most `O(n^2)` choices of interval endpoints, with a harmless factor two for side orientation.

For a fixed strip of length `L<=n`, Hoeffding's inequality gives, for `t>=0`,

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{j=1}^L Z_j\ge t\right)
\le \exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2L}\right)
\le \exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2n}\right).
\tag{5.1}
\]

A union bound therefore yields

\[
\mathbb P(M_n\ge t)
\le \min\left\{1,4n^3\exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2n}\right)\right\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Take

\[
a_n=\sqrt{8n\log n}\qquad(n\ge2).
\]

Then `exp(-a_n^2/(2n))=n^{-4}`. Integrating the tail and using

\[
\int_a^\infty e^{-t^2/(2n)}dt\le \frac{n}{a}e^{-a^2/(2n)}
\]

gives the explicit uniform estimate

\[
\mathbb E M_n^+
\le a_n+4n^3\frac{n}{a_n}n^{-4}
=\sqrt{8n\log n}+\frac4{\sqrt{8n\log n}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Combining (4.5) with the maximum over all one-backstep contours proves the main restricted theorem.

### Theorem 5.1 — one-backstep routing tax has zero thermodynamic density

For every `q in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E R_n^{(1)}(C_q)
\le
\sqrt{8n\log n}+\frac4{\sqrt{8n\log n}},
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E R_n^{(1)}(C_q)}n=0
\quad\text{uniformly in }q.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\lim_{q\downarrow0}\frac1{\sqrt q}
\left[\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\mathbb E R_n^{(1)}(C_q)}n\right]=0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This completely settles the Section-I recovery target whenever the connected hole/re-entry configuration corresponds to one incoming cut edge / one backward dual step. The exact finite `n=4` witness is not contradicted: it has positive `O(1)` routing tax, while (5.4) only says the **maximum** one-backstep tax over the entire `n x n` environment is sublinear in `n`.

No finite checker was created because this is a theorem-sized analytic argument and no finite enumeration is used. The proof is on the page.

## 6. Why this does not move `alpha_tax`

The frozen metric concerns the full routing tax

\[
U_n(C_q)-W_n(C_q),
\]

where an optimizing arbitrary cut may have an unbounded number of incoming edges/backward crossings as `n->infinity`. Theorem 5.1 controls only the one-backstep class. Therefore it is **PARTIAL** and does not imply `T(q)=O(q^alpha)` for any `alpha>0`.

What it does rule out is important: the dangerous architecture identified in the stuck report — an arbitrarily long one-reentry ribbon — cannot by itself carry positive thermodynamic routing-tax density. Its adaptive choice among `O(n^3)` possible spans/locations only increases the score by `O(sqrt(n log n))`, which vanishes after division by `n`.

Thus any source-scale routing-tax correction must use **a number of backward crossings that diverges with the box size**. A fixed finite number of such primitive one-backstep ribbons cannot produce a nonzero thermodynamic density unless their interactions defeat the separate-strip shortcut argument. This is the next structural frontier.

## 7. B21 — nearest solved shape and what can actually be ported

The preceding R01 bridge already identified Basdevant--Gouéré--Théret's Section 3 comparison between oriented and ordinary Bernoulli FPP: their proof extracts monotone structure and shows that non-monotone detours do not alter the first-order `sqrt(q)` coefficient in ordinary Bernoulli FPP. The present proof is the exact one-backstep analogue in the orientation-sensitive free-backstep model.

The useful common principle is now explicit:

1. extract a monotone skeleton;
2. encode each non-monotone excursion by a reward difference against a monotone shortcut;
3. center that difference by pairing the two sides;
4. pay only the entropy of admissible excursion intervals.

For one backward step the admissible family has only polynomial (`O(n^3)`) entropy, and Hoeffding immediately makes the gain sublinear. The unresolved many-backstep case is where this port stops: primitive ribbons may overlap, nest, share shortcut edges, and interact with the globally optimized monotone skeleton. Treating them as independent candidate strips would double-count rewards and can generate exponentially many decompositions.

Krishnan--Rassoul-Agha--Seppäläinen's shift/length convex duality remains relevant to the lambda envelope, but it does not supply the required noncrossing multi-excursion decomposition for an orientation-selective shift. No literature theorem located in the bounded search directly supplies that missing step.

**B21 outcome:** the one-backstep special case is completely closed by a monotone-shortcut / strip-concentration argument; the exact point where the published ordinary-FPP machinery still fails is multi-backstep interaction and adaptive decomposition, not the single-ribbon probability tail.
