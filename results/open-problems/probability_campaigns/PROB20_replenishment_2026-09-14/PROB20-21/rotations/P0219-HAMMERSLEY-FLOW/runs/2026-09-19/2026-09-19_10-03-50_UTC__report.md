## 4. B7 TAKE THE LIMIT — a rigorous high-density backstep-density bound

The handoff requested a continuum/BV limit of a hypothetical dense-backstep optimizer. A direct limit initially looks impossible because a simple lattice separator can in principle have order `n^2` steps. The first task is therefore to show that **an optimizer with nonnegative score cannot have macroscopic backward variation larger than a quantity tending to zero with `q`.** This can be done by a very crude but fully rigorous self-avoiding-path count.

### 4.1 Setup

For a simple separating dual contour `gamma`, let

- `b=B(gamma)` be its number of backward (`S` or `W`) steps;
- `K_n` be its fixed net forward displacement, with `K_n<=2n` under the standing box convention;
- `L=K_n+2b` be its total number of dual steps;
- `C_f(gamma)` be its number of closed primal edges crossed by forward (`N` or `E`) steps;
- `S_0(gamma)=C_f(gamma)-b` be its free-backstep score.

If `S_0(gamma)>=0`, then necessarily

\[
C_f(\gamma)\ge b.
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular the simple contour crosses at least `b` closed primal edges in total.

A simple dual contour crosses distinct primal edges. For a **fixed** length-`L` simple contour, the number of closed crossed edges is therefore binomial `Bin(L,q)`. The number of self-avoiding step words of length `L` from any fixed initial dual vertex is at most `4*3^{L-1}`: after the first step an immediate reversal is forbidden, and ignoring all other self-avoidance constraints only overcounts. The number of admissible boundary starting points is `O(n)`; use the safe factor `8n` below.

For `q<=1/2`, a fixed contour satisfies

\[
\Pr\{\text{at least }b\text{ crossed edges are closed}\}
\le
\sum_{j=b}^{L}\binom Lj q^j
\le 2^L q^b.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus, for a fixed `b`, unioning over all simple contours of length `K_n+2b` gives

\[
\Pr\{\exists\gamma:B(\gamma)=b,\ S_0(\gamma)\ge0\}
\le
8n\,6^{K_n+2b}q^b
\le
8n\,6^{2n}(36q)^b.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.2 The logarithmic backstep-density theorem

Assume `q<1/36` and put

\[
A(q)=\log(1/q)-2\log6>0,
\qquad
\boxed{\beta_*(q)=\frac{4\log6}{A(q)}.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Summing (4.3) over all integers `b>=beta_*(q)n` gives

\[
\Pr\{\exists\gamma:S_0(\gamma)\ge0,\ B(\gamma)\ge\beta_*(q)n\}
\le
\frac{8n\,6^{2n}(36q)^{\lceil\beta_*(q)n\rceil}}{1-36q}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because

\[
2\log6+\beta_*(q)\log(36q)
=
2\log6-4\log6
=-2\log6,
\tag{4.6}
\]

the right side is polynomial in `n` times `6^{-2n}`. In particular it is summable in `n`.

Hence, by Borel--Cantelli, for each fixed `q<1/36`, almost surely for all sufficiently large `n`, **every simple separator with nonnegative free-backstep score has**

\[
\boxed{\frac{B(\gamma)}n<\beta_*(q).}
\tag{4.7}
\]

The maximizing contour for `U_n` has nonnegative score because the empty/downset baseline is nonnegative, so (4.7) applies to an optimizer. Moreover

\[
\beta_*(q)
=
\frac{4\log6}{\log(1/q)-2\log6}
=O\!\left(\frac1{\log(1/q)}\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The exponentially small exceptional event also makes the same bound valid at the level of normalized expected backward count up to an `o_n(1)` term, since a simple contour has at most order `n^2` edges.

### 4.3 Consequence for B7: the ordinary continuum limit is too coarse

After scaling the square by `1/n`, the total negative coordinate variation of an optimizing contour is at most `B(gamma)/n`. Equation (4.7) therefore says that for high density the macroscopic negative variation is `O(1/log(1/q))`, and tends to zero as `q->0`.

Thus any two-stage `n->infinity` then `q->0` BV/occupation-measure subsequential limit of optimizing contours is **coordinatewise monotone at leading macroscopic order**. The free-backstep correction, if it survives on the source `sqrt(q)` scale, is invisible in that ordinary macroscopic curve.

This is the main B7 outcome:

> the remaining obstruction is not a nonmonotone continuum shape. It is a **tangent-scale oscillation/defect measure around a monotone Hammersley skeleton**.

This is useful progress, but it does not imply (1.1): `1/log(1/q)` is much larger than `sqrt(q)` as `q->0`.

**B7 outcome:** rigorous new logarithmic upper bound on optimizer backstep density; the naive continuum/BV limit collapses to the monotone class and therefore cannot by itself decide the routing tax.

## 5. B11 DUALIZE — exact occupation-measure first moment and why it is the wrong dual

The natural next attempt is to dualize the dense correction into local occupation frequencies and relative entropy. This produces a clean variational inequality, but it also exposes exactly what information such a local dual throws away.

### 5.1 A local step-frequency large-deviation bound

Fix a simple contour with

\[
K_n=\kappa n+o(n),\qquad B(\gamma)=\beta n+o(n),
\]

so its forward-step count is

\[
F=K_n+B=(\kappa+\beta)n+o(n),
\]

and total length is

\[
L=K_n+2B=(\kappa+2\beta)n+o(n).
\]

If the free-backstep score satisfies `S_0(gamma)>=s n`, then

\[
C_f(\gamma)\ge (\beta+s)n+o(n).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For a fixed path the forward crossed primal edges are distinct and iid Bernoulli-closed with density `q`, hence the Chernoff bound gives, provided

\[
r:=\frac{\beta+s}{\kappa+\beta}>q,
\]

\[
\Pr\{S_0(\gamma)\ge sn\}
\le
\exp\left[-(\kappa+\beta)n\,D(r\|q)+o(n)\right],
\tag{5.2}
\]

where

\[
D(r\|q)=r\log\frac rq+(1-r)\log\frac{1-r}{1-q}.
\]

The number of simple step words of length `L` from the boundary is at most a polynomial factor times `3^L`, so the purely local occupation-measure/word-entropy first moment is controlled by

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_q(\kappa,\beta,s)
=(\kappa+2\beta)\log3
-(\kappa+\beta)
D\!\left(\frac{\beta+s}{\kappa+\beta}\middle\|q\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Whenever `Phi_q<0` with uniform margin over a compact parameter range, contours in that range are exponentially absent.

At `s=0`, (5.3) recovers the same qualitative conclusion as B7: for `q` small, sufficiently large backward density is impossible, with the threshold only logarithmically small in `q` under this coarse entropy count.

### 5.2 Exact failure of this dual for the routing **tax**

The crucial diagnostic is what happens at `beta=0`. Then (5.3) still contains the strictly positive entropy term

\[
\kappa\log3.
\tag{5.4}
\]

But `beta=0` is exactly the monotone class whose optimum is **already subtracted off in `U_n-W_n`**. The routing-tax complexity must therefore vanish at `beta=0`. Any dual/entropy functional with an order-one entropy at `beta=0` is measuring the whole monotone Hammersley optimization again rather than the incremental cost of allowing backsteps.

This is why a standard empirical occupation measure, a raw self-avoiding-walk count, or an LP using only directional step frequencies cannot close P0219: all of them retain the exponential entropy of the monotone backbone. That entropy is responsible for the known `sqrt(q)` Hammersley/FPP term and must be quotiented out before the routing correction is estimated.

A successful B11 object would need a **relative feasible-measure entropy**

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm rel}(\beta)
\quad\text{with}\quad
\mathcal H_{\rm rel}(0)=0,
\tag{5.5}
\]

whose states encode only compatible deformations of an already-optimized monotone/Hammersley skeleton. The canonical strip-record language from B10/B19 is a candidate coordinate system, but the previous `(4n^3)^b` count is far too crude: it does not capture the compatibility/nesting quotient needed to produce a finite rate function at `b=Theta(n)` and a vanishing rate as `beta downarrow0`.

This is a precise obstruction, not merely "the union bound is loose": the **wrong entropy has a nonzero value at zero backstep density**.

**B11 outcome:** derived the local large-deviation variational functional (5.3), and proved diagnostically that any successful dual must quotient out all monotone-backbone entropy so that its relative complexity vanishes at `beta=0`. The presently available occupation-measure dual does not do this.
