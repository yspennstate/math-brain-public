## 3. Setup: score words and the quantity to control

Use the same planar-dual orientation as the preceding reports. Forward dual steps are `N,E`; backward steps are `S,W`. For a simple separating contour `gamma`, let

- `B(gamma)=b` be its number of backward steps;
- `C_f(gamma)` be its number of closed primal edges crossed forward;
- `S_0(gamma)=C_f(gamma)-B(gamma)` be the free-backstep score.

Let `K_n` be the fixed net forward displacement of every source-to-sink separator, so

\[
F(\gamma)-B(\gamma)=K_n,
\qquad K_n\le2n
\tag{3.1}
\]

under the current box convention. Thus a contour with `b` backward steps has `K_n+b` forward steps and total word length `K_n+2b`.

For an integer `k>=0`, define the restricted excess

\[
R_n^{(\le k)}(C)
=
\left(
\max_{\gamma:\,B(\gamma)\le k}S_0(\gamma)-W_n(C)
\right)_+.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The new goal is to control (3.2) when `k=k_n` is allowed to diverge.

## 4. B10 — derandomize: a canonical environment-independent reduction record

The essential point is to quotient out the exponentially many monotone skeletons. The reduction below depends only on the **geometry/word of the contour**, never on which edges are open or closed.

### Lemma 4.1 — last-backstep elimination

Let `w` be any lattice-walk word in `N,E,S,W` that stays in the box and has the source-to-sink endpoint displacement. If `w` contains a backward letter, choose its **last** backward letter.

- If that letter is `W`, then because no backward letter occurs later and the endpoint still requires recovery of the lost horizontal unit, the word contains a factor
  \[
  W\,N^L\,E
  \tag{4.1}
  \]
  where the displayed `E` is the first `E` after that `W`.
- If the last backward letter is `S`, symmetrically the word contains
  \[
  S\,E^L\,N.
  \tag{4.2}
  \]

Replace (4.1) by `N^L`, or (4.2) by `E^L`. This removes exactly one backward step and preserves the endpoints and the box. Repeat until no backward letters remain.

The algorithm terminates after exactly `b=B(gamma)` reductions and produces a monotone `N/E` word `pi(gamma)` with the same endpoints.

#### Proof

For the last backward letter, the suffix contains only `N,E`. In the `W` case at least one `E` must occur later to restore the horizontal displacement. Before the first such `E`, only `N` can occur, giving (4.1). The replacement shifts the vertical segment one lattice unit east to the pre-`W` column, which is still in the box. The `S` case is the rotation. Each replacement lowers the backward count by one, hence termination. A final `N/E` word is automatically vertex-simple because `x+y` increases by one at every step, so it is a valid monotone separator. ∎

Intermediate words need not be simple. This causes no problem: use the auxiliary score that counts forward closed crossings with multiplicity and subtracts the number of backward letters. The initial word is the original simple contour, and the final monotone word is again simple, so the initial and final auxiliary scores equal the genuine contour scores.

### Lemma 4.2 — each reduction has a width-one strip record

For a reduction `W N^L E -> N^L`, let `xi_1,...,xi_L` be the closed indicators on the old vertical side, `eta_1,...,eta_L` those on the new shortcut side, and `zeta` the closed indicator of the compensating `E`. Then the exact auxiliary-score decrement is

\[
\Delta
=
\sum_{j=1}^L(\xi_j-\eta_j)+(\zeta-1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The `S E^L N` case is identical after rotation. A reduction record is therefore specified by orientation, the adjacent pair of lattice lines, and the interval endpoints. By the count already used in the one-backstep theorem, the entire `n x n` box has at most

\[
M_n:=4n^3
\tag{4.4}
\]

possible oriented strip records.

Consequently the canonical reduction of a `b`-backstep contour is encoded by a sequence of at most `M_n^b` possible record strings. Crucially, **the score difference between the original contour and its reduced monotone contour is determined by this record string alone**: all untouched prefix/suffix edges cancel at each reduction. This removes the exponential entropy of the monotone backbone.

**B10 outcome:** the many-backstep geometry has an environment-independent canonical coding by `b` width-one ribbon records; no checker was needed because the argument is symbolic and exact.

## 5. B19 — make it an algorithm: telescoping removes overlap/double counting

The concern handed off from the previous run was that shortcut ribbons can overlap or nest, so summing separate one-strip estimates might count the same random edge repeatedly. The canonical elimination resolves this algebraically.

Fix a valid reduction record string of length `b`, and choose any original simple contour `gamma` realizing it. Let `pi=pi(gamma)` be the final monotone contour. Telescoping the exact local increments gives

\[
D:=S_0(\gamma)-S_0(\pi).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Although an intermediate shortcut edge may later participate in another reduction, all such internal occurrences cancel in the telescoping sum. Since both **endpoints of the algorithm** are simple paths, write

\[
A=F(\gamma)\setminus F(\pi),
\qquad
B=F(\pi)\setminus F(\gamma),
\tag{5.2}
\]

where `F` denotes forward primal edges crossed by the contour. Then

\[
D=\sum_{e\in A}X_e-\sum_{e\in B}X_e-b,
\tag{5.3}
\]

with `X_e=1` iff the primal edge is closed. If `a=|A|` and `c=|B|`, then the forward-step counts imply

\[
a-c=b.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence, under Bernoulli closed-edge density `q` and `p=1-q`,

\[
\mathbb E D=q(a-c)-b=-pb.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Moreover the centered random part of `D` has coefficients exactly `+1` or `-1` on distinct primal edges, never multiplicity larger than one, and

\[
a+c\le |F(\gamma)|+|F(\pi)|
=(K_n+b)+K_n
\le4n+b.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus Hoeffding gives the **q-sensitive fixed-record tail**

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(D\ge t)
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{2(t+pb)^2}{4n+b}\right]
\le
\exp\left[-\frac{2t^2}{4n+b}\right].
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is the no-double-counting invariant sought by B19. Nesting and overlap can make intermediate ribbon descriptions ugly, but they cannot increase the final coefficient magnitude: telescoping collapses everything to the symmetric difference between the original forward edge set and the final monotone edge set.

Finally `S_0(pi)<=W_n(C)`, so every source-tax excess from `gamma` is at most `D_+`.

**B19 outcome:** the stack/last-backstep algorithm gives an exact telescoping invariant. Shared shortcut edges do not create a multiplicity loss in the final random variable.

## 6. B28 — typical rather than all: sub-`n/log n` backstep counts have zero density

We now pay only the entropy of canonical reduction records.

For each fixed `b`, there are at most `M_n^b` possible valid record strings. Therefore, for `k>=1`, a union bound over `1<=b<=k` and (5.7) gives

\[
\mathbb P\bigl(R_n^{(\le k)}\ge t\bigr)
\le
\sum_{b=1}^k M_n^b
\exp\left[-\frac{2t^2}{4n+k}\right]
\le
2M_n^k\exp\left[-\frac{2t^2}{4n+k}\right]
\tag{6.1}
\]

for `n>=2` (the harmless factor `2` bounds the geometric sum).

Put

\[
L_{n,k}=k\log M_n+\log2,
\qquad
A_{n,k}=\sqrt{\frac{4n+k}{2}L_{n,k}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

At `t=A_{n,k}` the right side of (6.1) equals `1`. Integrating the Gaussian tail and using

\[
\int_A^\infty e^{-2t^2/(4n+k)}dt
\le
\frac{4n+k}{4A}
 e^{-2A^2/(4n+k)}
\tag{6.3}
\]

proves the explicit expectation bound

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E R_n^{(\le k)}(C_q)
\le
A_{n,k}+\frac{4n+k}{4A_{n,k}}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

**uniformly for every `q in [0,1]`.**

Since `M_n=4n^3`, if a deterministic sequence `k_n` satisfies

\[
\boxed{k_n=o\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right),}
\tag{6.5}
\]

then

\[
\frac{A_{n,k_n}}n
=O\left(\sqrt{\frac{k_n\log n}{n}}\right)\to0,
\tag{6.6}
\]

and the second term in (6.4), divided by `n`, also tends to zero. We have therefore proved:

### Theorem 6.1 — sparse-backstep contours have zero thermodynamic routing-tax density

For every deterministic `k_n=o(n/log n)`, uniformly in `q in [0,1]`,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac1n\,
\mathbb E R_n^{(\le k_n)}(C_q)=0.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Equivalently, any family of contours that contributes a positive thermodynamic routing-tax density must, along some subsequence, use at least order `n/log n` backward crossings. In particular every fixed number of backsteps, every `n^beta` number with `beta<1`, and every `n/(log n)^{1+epsilon}` number of backsteps is harmless at thermodynamic density.

This strictly strengthens the preceding one-backstep result and resolves a substantial portion of the many-backstep frontier. It still does **not** control contours with `Theta(n/log n)` or denser backward-step counts, so it does not imply any positive `q`-exponent for the full `T(q)`.

No finite checker was created. The result is theorem-sized and the complete combinatorial/probabilistic derivation is on the page.

**B28 outcome:** successful restricted theorem; the remaining source-scale obstruction is now forced into near-linear/dense backstep complexity rather than merely a diverging number of excursions.
