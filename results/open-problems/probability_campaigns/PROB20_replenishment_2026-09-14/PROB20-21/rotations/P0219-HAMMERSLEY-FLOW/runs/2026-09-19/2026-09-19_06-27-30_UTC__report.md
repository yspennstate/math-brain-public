## 3. B12 — FIND WHAT IS CONSERVED: exact reward/backstep balance

The lambda interpolation has a stronger elementary conservation law than the previous report used.

For any admissible dual contour `gamma`, split its crossings into forward/backward and open/closed:

\[
F=C_f+O_f,\qquad B=C_b+O_b.
\]

The fixed endpoint displacement gives

\[
F-B=K_n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Starting from the interpolated score,

\[
S_\lambda=C_f-B-\lambda O_b,
\]

substitute `B=F-K_n=C_f+O_f-K_n` to obtain the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
S_\lambda(\gamma)=K_n-O_f(\gamma)-\lambda O_b(\gamma).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the interpolation is literally a passage problem in which open forward crossings cost one and open backward crossings cost `lambda`; all closed crossings cost zero. At `lambda=0` the only passage cost is `O_f`, while at `lambda=1` this is ordinary Bernoulli passage cost along the same contour class.

Rearranging the original score gives a second exact form:

\[
\boxed{
C_f(\gamma)=B(\gamma)+\lambda O_b(\gamma)+S_\lambda(\gamma).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently any contour with nonnegative score satisfies

\[
C_f\ge B+\lambda O_b.
\tag{3.4}
\]

In particular at `lambda=0`, every backward crossing must be paid for by at least one forward closed crossing before the contour obtains even one unit of positive deficit score. This is deterministic; it does not invoke typicality or independence.

### 3.1 Chain-repeat strengthening relative to the Hammersley baseline

There is a more source-specific consequence that uses the preserved exact width `W_n(C)`.

Fix a minimum chain cover of all closed edges in the reachability poset:

\[
C=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_W,
\qquad W=W_n(C).
\]

Let `F_gamma` be the set of forward closed exits selected by a contour and put `k_j=|F_gamma cap C_j|`. Then

\[
\sum_{j=1}^W (k_j-1)_+
=|F_\gamma|-\#\{j:k_j>0\}
\ge |F_\gamma|-W.
\tag{3.5}
\]

If the contour beats the Hammersley/downset baseline by `t>=0`, i.e.

\[
S_\lambda(\gamma)\ge W+t,
\]

then (3.3)--(3.5) give

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=1}^W (k_j-1)_+
\ge B+\lambda O_b+t.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

For a free-backstep optimizer, `t=U-W` and `lambda=0`, so

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j(k_j-1)_+\ge B+(U-W).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Equation (3.7) is the useful invariant from B12: **every backstep and every unit of routing tax requires a repeated hit on a Hammersley chain, beyond the first selected edge on that chain.** The free-backstep advantage is therefore not created by isolated rare defects; it is created by repeated chain hits coupled through a non-downset contour.

This is a complete deterministic derivation, but it does not yet imply a stochastic exponent. The missing quantitative statement is a uniform probability/entropy bound on these repeated-chain hits along an adaptively selected contour after `n->infinity`.

**Outcome B12:** success as an exact structural reduction; no metric movement because no `q`-power bound on `T(q)` is yet proved.

## 4. B20 — FIND WHY THE ALGORITHM FAILS: monotone pruning is impossible pointwise

The obvious algorithm after (3.2) is: take a nonmonotone/free-backstep optimizer, remove each backward excursion, and replace it by a coordinate-monotone/downset boundary. If this could be done without losing score, P0219 would collapse to the solved Hammersley comparator.

The preserved four-defect witness proves that **no such pointwise domination algorithm can exist**.

For that exact `n=4` environment the closed edges are the preserved P13 indices

`{0,2,11,12}`.

P13's maximizing arbitrary set has:

\[
C_f=4,\qquad B=1,
\]

and its unique incoming/backward edge is not closed, so

\[
O_b=1.
\]

Therefore its interpolated affine score is

\[
S_\lambda=4-1-\lambda=3-\lambda.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every coordinate downset has score at most the exact width

\[
W=2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Hence for every `0<=lambda<1`, this single-backstep contour is strictly better than **every** monotone/downset contour by at least

\[
1-\lambda.
\tag{4.3}
\]

At `lambda=0`, forcing monotonicity saves one backward crossing but necessarily loses at least two of the four selected closed exits, so the net score drops by one. The issue is not a removable loop with nonnegative cost. The incoming edge changes which closed exits can coexist on the same source-side boundary, and the reward is global across two reachability chains.

This identifies the precise algorithmic obstruction: the optimizer can use a non-downset indentation to **coordinate repeated hits on several chains at once**. A local rule that deletes one incoming edge has no way to know which two or more closed rewards must be sacrificed to restore a downset.

### 4.1 Why the adaptive-entropy problem remains

The preceding report already ruled out a predetermined `O(n)` family of copies of a fixed four-defect gadget: its expected normalized contribution is `O(q^4)`. But the thermodynamic optimizer is chosen after observing an `n x n` environment. Counting all possible defect quadruples in area gives order `n^2` candidate anchors and cannot be divided by `n` uniformly after the `n->infinity` limit.

The missing algorithm must therefore be **contour-relative**, not area-relative: one needs a canonical decomposition of an optimal nonmonotone boundary into irreducible deviations anchored along a monotone/Hammersley skeleton, so that the number of possible anchors is longitudinal (`O(n)`) and the combinatorial multiplicity is paid by the size of each deviation rather than by the whole area.

That is the exact place where the naive cluster count fails. The sparse four-defect theorem is useful only after such a decomposition exists.

**Outcome B20:** the failure mechanism is now exact: pointwise monotone pruning is refuted by the minimal witness, and area-wise cluster counting fails because selection happens after seeing the environment. The missing object is a canonical skeleton-relative irreducible routing polymer.

## 6. What a proof now has to contain

Combining B12/B20/B24 narrows the missing direction to a concrete three-part mechanism.

1. **Canonical decomposition.** Map every non-downset optimizer to a monotone/Hammersley skeleton plus irreducible routing polymers, without double counting closed rewards.
2. **Reward accounting.** Use (3.6) or a strengthening of it to force each polymer to carry enough repeated-chain closed hits to pay its backward-crossing cost and its tax gain.
3. **Entropy bound.** Count polymers relative to the skeleton, so adaptive selection costs exponential complexity in polymer size rather than an uncontrolled `n^2` area factor.

If a polymer of size `m` requires at least `c m` specified rare closed edges while the number of shapes grows at most `K^m`, then for small `q` a Peierls sum of the schematic form

\[
\sum_m K^m q^{cm}
\]

would be summable and would give a positive power of `q`. This is the intended route; the canonical decomposition and the needed uniform density inequality are **not** proved in this report.

Conversely, the clearest counterexample architecture would be an irreducible family whose longitudinal span tends to infinity while the number of required closed edges per unit routing-tax gain tends to zero. Finding such a family would invalidate the polymer/Peierls route and could threaten the source asymptotic. No such family was found here.
