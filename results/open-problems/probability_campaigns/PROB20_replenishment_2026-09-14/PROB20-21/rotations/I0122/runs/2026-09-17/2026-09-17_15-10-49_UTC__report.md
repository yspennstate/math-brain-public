## 2. Mathematical idea before code

The previous report made critical pairs analytically useful, but a source proof needs a reason that a useful pair can be made critical without destroying the quantitative information that made it useful.

This run attacks exactly that transfer step.

# B13 — FIND WHAT ONLY INCREASES / DECREASES

## 3. Quantitative criticalization lemma

For `x` in a finite poset write

\[
D(x)=\{z:z<_P x\},\qquad U(x)=\{z:x<_P z\}.
\]

An ordered incomparable pair `(x,y)` is critical when

\[
D(x)\subseteq D(y),\qquad U(y)\subseteq U(x).
\]

Let

\[
p(x,y)=\Pr(x<_L y),\qquad d(x,y)=\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(y)-\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(x).
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — one criticalization step

Suppose `x||y`.

If `D(x)` is not contained in `D(y)`, choose

\[
z\in D(x)\setminus D(y).
\]

Then `z||y`, and

\[
\boxed{p(z,y)\ge p(x,y)}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{d(z,y)\ge d(x,y)+1.}
\]

Likewise, if `U(y)` is not contained in `U(x)`, choose

\[
z\in U(y)\setminus U(x).
\]

Then `x||z`, and

\[
\boxed{p(x,z)\ge p(x,y)},
\qquad
\boxed{d(x,z)\ge d(x,y)+1.}
\]

### Proof

In the first case `z<x`. If also `z<y`, then `z` would belong to `D(y)`, contradicting the choice of `z`; and `y<z<x` would imply `y<x`, contradicting `x||y`. Hence `z||y`.

Every linear extension with `x<y` also has `z<x<y`, so

\[
\Pr(z<y)\ge\Pr(x<y).
\]

Also every linear extension has

\[
\operatorname{rank}(x)\ge\operatorname{rank}(z)+1,
\]

hence `E rank(x)-E rank(z)>=1`; adding this to `d(x,y)` gives the rank-gap inequality.

The second case is symmetric: `y<z`, `x||z`, the event `x<y` implies `x<z`, and `E rank(z)-E rank(y)>=1`. QED.

### Lemma 3.2 — termination by a finite monovariant

Define

\[
\Psi(x,y)=|D(x)|+|U(y)|.
\]

In a first-type step `x -> z<x`, transitivity gives

\[
D(z)\subsetneq D(x),
\]

so `Psi` strictly decreases. In a second-type step `y -> z>y`, transitivity gives

\[
U(z)\subsetneq U(y),
\]

so again `Psi` strictly decreases.

Therefore repeated repair terminates at a critical pair `(x*,y*)`. Along the entire route,

\[
\boxed{p(x^*,y^*)\ge p(x,y)}
\]

while after `t` repair steps

\[
\boxed{d(x^*,y^*)\ge d(x,y)+t.}
\]

This is a useful exact structural statement: strong majority survives criticalization, but expected-rank proximity does not.

I do not claim novelty for the existence of a criticalizing descent/ascent; critical-pair reductions are classical. The explicit simultaneous monotonicity statements above are recorded as current working lemmas, with novelty **UNVERIFIED**.

# B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

## 4. A hypothetical counterexample has a canonical strong-majority order

Assume for contradiction-style structural analysis that `P` is a source counterexample: `P` is not a chain and has no 1/3-balanced incomparable pair.

For every distinct pair `x,y`, orient `x -> y` as follows:

- if `x<_P y`, orient `x -> y`; its probability is `1`;
- if `x||y`, exactly one of `Pr(x<y),Pr(y<x)` exceeds `2/3`; orient toward that direction.

This is a tournament on the vertex set of `P`.

### Lemma 4.1 — no directed triangle

The tournament has no directed 3-cycle.

### Proof

Suppose `a->b->c->a`. In any total order of `a,b,c`, at most two of the three cyclic comparisons

\[
a<b,\qquad b<c,\qquad c<a
\]

can hold simultaneously. Hence for a uniformly random linear extension,

\[
\Pr(a<b)+\Pr(b<c)+\Pr(c<a)\le2.
\]

But every oriented edge above has probability strictly greater than `2/3` (or probability `1` if it is a forced poset comparison), making the left side strictly greater than `2`, contradiction. QED.

A tournament is transitive iff it has no directed triangle. Therefore there is a unique order

\[
\boxed{v_1\prec_*v_2\prec_*\cdots\prec_*v_n}
\]

whose direction agrees with every forced comparison and every `>2/3` incomparable comparison. In particular `L*=(v_1,...,v_n)` is a linear extension of `P`.

This `2/3` triangle argument is **not promoted as novel**. De Loof–De Baets–De Meyer (2014) study cycle-free cuts of mutual-rank-probability relations and give a stronger theoretical 3-cycle cut threshold around `0.5556`.

### Corollary 4.2 — an adjacent strong-majority incomparable pair exists

If every adjacent pair `v_i,v_{i+1}` in `L*` were comparable in `P`, then necessarily

\[
v_i<_P v_{i+1}
\]

for every `i`, and transitivity would make `P` a chain. Therefore every source counterexample has at least one index `i` with

\[
\boxed{v_i||v_{i+1},\qquad \Pr(v_i<v_{i+1})>2/3.}
\]

### Corollary 4.3 — criticalization is outward interval expansion in `L*`

Start from such an adjacent pair `(v_i,v_{i+1})` and apply Lemma 3.1.

- If the left endpoint is replaced by `z<v_i` in the poset, then `z` lies strictly to the left of `v_i` in `L*`.
- If the right endpoint is replaced by `z>v_{i+1}` in the poset, then `z` lies strictly to the right of `v_{i+1}` in `L*`.

Hence criticalization produces a nested sequence of intervals in the canonical majority order, expanding only outward, while the orientation probability never drops below its initial value `>2/3`.

This gives a concrete picture of what a counterexample would have to contain: at least one adjacent strong-majority incomparable pair whose outward repair chain ends at a strongly biased critical pair.

### Corollary 4.4 — at least three chain blocks

Cut `L*` at every adjacent incomparable pair. Every maximal run between cuts is a chain in `P`, because all adjacent pairs inside the run are comparable in the `L*` direction and poset transitivity closes the run.

If there were only one cut, `P` would be the union of two chains and therefore have width at most two. The 1/3–2/3 conjecture is known for width-two posets. Hence a source counterexample must have at least two cuts, i.e. at least three such chain blocks.

This is a necessary structural condition only; it does not move the source metric.

# B26 — HUNT THE SPORADIC CASE / ATTACK THE TRANSFER LEMMA

## 5. The criticalization bridge fails maximally on a simple infinite family

The previous run's strongest source-compatible numerical certificate was based on expected-rank gaps of critical pairs. A tempting next theorem would say that if some incomparable pair has a small expected-rank gap, criticalizing it preserves a controlled gap.

This is false in the strongest possible elementary way.

Let

\[
P_m=C_m\,+\,\{y\}
\]

be the disjoint union of a chain

\[
c_1<c_2<\cdots<c_m
\]

and one isolated element `y`. A uniformly random linear extension is obtained by inserting `y` uniformly into one of the `m+1` slots of the chain.

For every `i`,

\[
\Pr(c_i<y)=\frac{m+1-i}{m+1}.
\]

The expected ranks are

\[
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(y)=\frac{m+2}{2},
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(c_i)
=i+\frac{i}{m+1}
=\frac{i(m+2)}{m+1}.
\]

Take `m=2r-1` odd and the median chain element `c_r`. Then

\[
\boxed{\Pr(c_r<y)=1/2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(c_r)=\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(y)},
\]

so `(c_r,y)` is exactly balanced with expected-rank gap `0`.

However `(c_1,y)` is a critical pair, and

\[
\Pr(c_1<y)=\frac{m}{m+1},
\]

while

\[
\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(y)-\mathbb E\,\operatorname{rank}(c_1)
=
\frac{(m+2)(m-1)}{2(m+1)}
=\Theta(m).
\]

Likewise `(y,c_m)` is critical with the symmetric values.

If one insists on cover-by-cover criticalization from the median, each step changes `c_i` to `c_{i-1}` and increases the expected-rank gap by

\[
\frac{m+2}{m+1}>1
\]

while changing the comparison probability by only

\[
\frac1{m+1}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{criticalization can send gap }0\text{ to gap }\Theta(m)\text{ even from an exactly balanced pair}.}
\]

There can be no universal `O(1)` expected-rank-gap preservation theorem for blind criticalization.

This kills the most direct way of combining the first-run shifted-Grünbaum small-gap argument with the second-run critical-pair `d<=2/3` certificate.
