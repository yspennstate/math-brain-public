# 3. Setup: fixed-point influence matrices

Let

\[
V_k(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^3P_{ij,k}x_ix_j,
\qquad P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}.
\]

At a fixed point `x`, define the `3x3` one-parent influence matrix

\[
T_{jk}:=\sum_i x_iP_{ij,k}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Each row of `T` is stochastic. Since `x` is fixed,

\[
xT=x.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The tangent derivative of the QSO is twice the nontrivial action of `T`; equivalently, the two tangent eigenvalues of `DV(x)` are twice the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T`.

If all coefficients are at least `a<1/3`, write

\[
P_{ij,\bullet}=a(1,1,1)+bQ_{ij,\bullet},
\qquad b=1-3a,
\tag{3.3}
\]

where every residual row `Q_{ij,bullet}` is a probability vector and remains symmetric in `i,j`.

Define

\[
R_{jk}:=\sum_i x_iQ_{ij,k}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then `R` is row stochastic and

\[
T=aJ+bR,
\tag{3.5}
\]

where `J` is the all-ones `3x3` matrix. On the quotient by the constant vector, `J` vanishes, so the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` are `b` times the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `R`. In particular

\[
\boxed{\det T=b^2\det R.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

# 4. New theorem A: sharp complex stability at the barycentre

## Theorem A

Assume all heredity coefficients satisfy

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge\frac1{12}
\]

and the barycentre

\[
u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)
\]

is fixed. If the tangent multipliers of `DV(u)` are nonreal conjugates, then

\[
\boxed{|\mu|\le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}<1.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The bound is attained at the weak endpoint `1/12` by an explicit residual deterministic kernel.

## Proof

Use the endpoint decomposition

\[
a=\frac1{12},\qquad b=\frac34.
\]

Because `u` is fixed,

\[
u=a(1,1,1)+bW(u)
\]

forces

\[
W(u)=u.
\]

Thus the residual influence matrix

\[
R_{jk}=\frac13\sum_iQ_{ij,k}
\tag{4.2}
\]

is **doubly stochastic**.

Fix a parent label `j` and child label `k`. Put

\[
C_{jk}:=\sum_iQ_{ij,k}=3R_{jk}.
\]

Residual fixedness of the barycentre gives

\[
\sum_{i,l}Q_{il,k}=3.
\tag{4.3}
\]

By parent symmetry `Q_{il,k}=Q_{li,k}`, the total in (4.3) contains the diagonal term `Q_{jj,k}`, twice every off-diagonal term incident to `j`, and additional nonnegative terms. Hence

\[
3
\ge
Q_{jj,k}+2\sum_{i\ne j}Q_{ij,k}
=2C_{jk}-Q_{jj,k}
\ge
2C_{jk}-1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{R_{jk}\le\frac23\quad\forall j,k.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every row of `R` is a probability vector with all coordinates at most `2/3`. Convexity of the squared Euclidean norm therefore gives

\[
\sum_kR_{jk}^2\le
\left(\frac23\right)^2+\left(\frac13\right)^2
=\frac59.
\]

Summing over the three rows,

\[
\|R\|_F^2\le\frac53.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since `R` is doubly stochastic, `1` is a singular value with normalized singular vector `(1,1,1)`. Let the other singular values be `s_1,s_2`. Then

\[
s_1^2+s_2^2
=\|R\|_F^2-1
\le\frac23.
\]

Because the product of all singular values is `|det R|`,

\[
|\det R|=s_1s_2
\le\frac{s_1^2+s_2^2}{2}
\le\boxed{\frac13}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

If the nontrivial eigenvalues of `R` are a conjugate pair `nu,conj(nu)`, then

\[
|\nu|^2=\det R\le\frac13.
\]

The QSO tangent multipliers are

\[
\mu=2b\nu=\frac32\nu,
\]

therefore

\[
|\mu|^2
\le
\frac94\cdot\frac13
=\frac34,
\]

which is (4.1).

### Sharpness at the weak endpoint

Take the deterministic residual rule on unordered parent pairs

\[
11,12\mapsto1,
\qquad
22,23\mapsto2,
\qquad
13,33\mapsto3.
\]

At the barycentre this has residual influence matrix

\[
R=
\begin{pmatrix}
2/3&0&1/3\\
1/3&2/3&0\\
0&1/3&2/3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its characteristic polynomial is

\[
(\lambda-1)(3\lambda^2-3\lambda+1)/3,
\]

so the nontrivial eigenvalues have modulus `1/sqrt(3)` and

\[
\det R=\frac13.
\]

Restoring the endpoint floor gives tangent modulus exactly

\[
\frac32\cdot\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]

Thus the complex bound is sharp inside the weak endpoint class, while remaining strictly below instability.

# 5. New theorem B: an arbitrary-fixed-point exterior-variance bound

The barycentric theorem uses exact uniform symmetry. The following determinant inequality holds at an arbitrary fixed point and gives a sharp localization of any remaining complex instability.

Let `H` be a `3x2` matrix whose columns form any tangent basis, and write

\[
H_{k,\bullet}=x_kF_k,
\]

where each `F_k` is a row vector in `R^2`. Tangency means

\[
\sum_kx_kF_k=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `M` be the `2x2` tangent representation defined by

\[
DV(x)H=HM.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let `I,J` be iid with law `x` and let `K` be the residual child generated by `Q`. Define the vector score sum

\[
S:=F_I+F_J.
\]

At a fixed point, the residual child marginal is

\[
q_k=\frac{x_k-a}{b}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The vector version of the exact conditional-mean identity from the earlier scalar convex-order work is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[S\mid K=k]
=
\frac{x_k}{x_k-a}F_kM.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

Put

\[
G:=\sum_kx_kF_k^TF_k,
\]

and

\[
L:=\sum_k\frac{x_k^2}{b(x_k-a)}F_k^TF_k.
\]

Because the two parents are independent and the score has mean zero,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(S)=2G.
\]

The covariance of the conditional mean in (5.4) is

\[
M^TLM.
\]

The law of total covariance therefore gives the PSD inequality

\[
\boxed{M^TLM\preceq2G.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Taking determinants,

\[
(\det M)^2\det L\le4\det G.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The determinant ratio is independent of the chosen tangent basis. Indeed, by the two-dimensional Cauchy–Binet identity,

\[
\det\left(\sum_ic_iF_i^TF_i\right)
=
\sum_{i<j}c_ic_j\det(F_i,F_j)^2.
\]

Writing `Delta=det(F_1,F_2)`, the mean relation (5.1) gives

\[
\det(F_1,F_3)=-\frac{x_2}{x_3}\Delta,
\qquad
\det(F_2,F_3)=\frac{x_1}{x_3}\Delta.
\]

Substitution yields the exact ratio

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\det L}{\det G}
=
\frac{x_1x_2x_3}{b(x_1-a)(x_2-a)(x_3-a)}.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Combining (5.6) and (5.7),

\[
\boxed{
(\det M)^2
\le
4b\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{x_i-a}{x_i}.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

This is a new basis-free necessary condition for arbitrary fixed-point instability.

# 6. Consequences for a nonreal unstable pair at the endpoint

Suppose the tangent eigenvalues are a nonreal conjugate pair `mu,conj(mu)`. Then

\[
\det M=|\mu|^2.
\]

At the weak endpoint

\[
a=\frac1{12},\qquad b=\frac34,
\]

(5.8) becomes

\[
\boxed{
|\mu|^4
\le
3\prod_{i=1}^3\left(1-\frac1{12x_i}\right).
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Hence `|mu|>=1` forces

\[
\prod_{i=1}^3\left(1-\frac1{12x_i}\right)\ge\frac13.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Every factor lies in `(0,1)`, so each factor must itself be at least `1/3`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{x_i\ge\frac18\quad\forall i.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

A stronger coordinate localization follows. For a fixed coordinate `t=x_1`, the function

\[
\phi(x)=\log\left(1-\frac1{12x}\right)
\]

is strictly concave on `(1/12,\infty)`. Thus, among `x_2+x_3=1-t`, the product in (6.2) is maximized at

\[
x_2=x_3=\frac{1-t}{2}.
\]

Consequently a necessary condition for (6.2) is

\[
\left(1-\frac1{12t}\right)
\left(1-\frac1{6(1-t)}\right)^2
\ge\frac13.
\tag{6.4}
\]

After clearing the positive denominator, (6.4) is exactly

\[
288t^3-468t^2+216t-25\ge0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The two relevant roots in the simplex coordinate range are

\[
r_-\approx0.174860236322416,
\]

\[
r_+\approx0.553902959429950.
\]

(The third real root is approximately `0.896236804247634`, outside the fixed-point coordinate range allowed by the endpoint floor.) Therefore every coordinate of any putative complex-unstable fixed point must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
0.1748602363\ldots\le x_i\le0.5539029594\ldots
\quad\forall i.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Thus all remaining complex instability is forced into a compact balanced central region; it cannot occur near any face or vertex.

# 7. New theorem C: an explicit central neighborhood is fully complex-stable

The following simple consequence of parent symmetry gives a uniform open neighborhood around the barycentre.

At the endpoint residual decomposition, for arbitrary fixed `x`, let

\[
q_k=\frac{x_k-a}{b}.
\]

For fixed parent state `j` and child state `k`, residual symmetry implies

\[
q_k
=\sum_{i,l}x_ix_lQ_{il,k}
\ge
x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
+2x_j\sum_{i\ne j}x_iQ_{ij,k}.
\]

Since

\[
R_{jk}=x_jQ_{jj,k}+\sum_{i\ne j}x_iQ_{ij,k},
\]

we obtain

\[
q_k\ge2x_jR_{jk}-x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
\ge2x_jR_{jk}-x_j^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
R_{jk}\le\frac{q_k+x_j^2}{2x_j}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Assume now

\[
\left|x_i-\frac13\right|\le\frac1{120}
\qquad\forall i.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Then

\[
\frac{13}{40}\le x_i\le\frac{41}{120}
\]

and, at `a=1/12`, `b=3/4`,

\[
q_i\le\frac{31}{90}.
\]

Using these worst-case bounds in (7.1),

\[
R_{jk}
\le
\frac{41}{240}
+
\frac{31/90}{2(13/40)}
=
\boxed{c:=\frac{6559}{9360}}
\approx0.7007478632.
\tag{7.3}
\]

A probability vector with all coordinates at most `c>1/2` has squared Euclidean norm at most

\[
c^2+(1-c)^2.
\]

Hadamard's determinant inequality therefore gives

\[
|\det R|
\le
\left(c^2+(1-c)^2\right)^{3/2}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Exact rational arithmetic verifies

\[
\left(c^2+(1-c)^2\right)^3
<
\frac{16}{81},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|\det R|<\frac49.}
\tag{7.5}
\]

By (3.6), with `b=3/4`,

\[
|\det T|<\frac14.
\]

If the tangent pair is nonreal, its squared modulus is

\[
|\mu|^2=4\det T,
\]

therefore

\[
\boxed{|\mu|<1.}
\tag{7.6}
\]

So the entire cube (7.2) is uniformly free of complex local instability under the weak endpoint floor.

Combining this with the preceding strict-floor real-spectrum theorems yields:

> **Corollary.** If every heredity coefficient is strictly greater than `1/12`, then every fixed point satisfying `|x_i-1/3|<=1/120` is locally attracting in all tangent directions.

   \[
   |\det R|\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{q_1q_2q_3}{x_1x_2x_3}},

4. The successful barycentric proof works because residual fixedness gives both double stochasticity and the exact entry cap `R_{jk}<=2/3`; away from the barycentre one needs a replacement for double stochasticity or a genuinely two-dimensional convex-order argument.
