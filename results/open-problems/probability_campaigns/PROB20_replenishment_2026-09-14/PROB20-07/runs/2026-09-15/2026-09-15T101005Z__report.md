# I. Reverification of the exact `1/12` lower bound

Fix `0<s<1/3` and put

\[
t(s)=\frac{1-9s^2}{12-27s^2},
\qquad
A(s)=\frac{20-99s^2}{6(4-9s^2)},
\qquad
B(s)=\frac{63s^2+2}{6(4-9s^2)}.
\]

For the six unordered parent pairs define

\[
P_{11}=(t,1-2t,t),\qquad
P_{12}=(t,t,1-2t),
\]
\[
P_{13}=(t,A,B),\qquad
P_{22}=(1-2t,t,t),
\]
\[
P_{23}=(A,t,B),\qquad
P_{33}=(t,t,1-2t),
\]

with `P_{ji}=P_{ij}`.

Exact algebra gives

\[
A+B+t=1,
\]

\[
\frac1{12}-t=\frac{9s^2}{4(4-9s^2)}>0,
\]

\[
A-t=\frac{3(2-9s^2)}{2(4-9s^2)}>0,
\qquad
B-t=\frac{27s^2}{2(4-9s^2)}>0,
\]

and

\[
1-3t=\frac3{4-9s^2}>0.
\]

Hence every coefficient is positive and

\[
\min_{i,j,k}P_{ij,k}=t(s).
\]

Define

\[
x^+=\left(\frac13+s,\frac13-s,\frac13\right),
\qquad
x^-=\left(\frac13-s,\frac13+s,\frac13\right).
\]

Direct substitution gives

\[
\boxed{V(x^+)=x^-}.
\]

The heredity tensor is equivariant under swapping types `1` and `2`, hence

\[
\boxed{V(x^-)=x^+}.
\]

Thus the operator has a genuine nontrivial 2-cycle for every `s>0`. Also

\[
t(s)\uparrow\frac1{12}\qquad(s\downarrow0).
\]

Therefore for every `alpha<1/12`, sufficiently small `s` has `t(s)>alpha`, so **all** coefficients satisfy the strict hypothesis `P_{ij,k}>alpha` while the QSO is not regular. Hence

\[
\boxed{\alpha_3\ge\frac1{12}}.
\]

An exact rational control is obtained at `s=1/30`:

\[
t=\frac{11}{133},\quad
A=\frac{221}{266},\quad
B=\frac{23}{266},\quad
1-2t=\frac{111}{133},
\]

with

\[
x^+=\left(\frac{11}{30},\frac3{10},\frac13\right),
\quad
x^-=\left(\frac3{10},\frac{11}{30},\frac13\right).
\]

This is a fully rational two-cycle certificate and directly verifies the strict-floor lower bound.

# II. Reverification of the global `1/9` upper bound

Assume

\[
\min_{i,j,k}P_{ij,k}>\frac19.
\]

Choose

\[
\frac19<a<\min P,
\qquad
b=1-3a,
\]

and write

\[
P_{ij,k}=a+bQ_{ij,k}.
\]

Then `Q` is again a symmetric stochastic heredity tensor. Let `W` be its QSO, `u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`, and `q=W(x)`. On the simplex,

\[
V(x)=a(1,1,1)+bW(x)=bq+(1-b)u.
\]

Therefore every coordinate of `V(x)` is at least `a`, so every boundary trajectory enters after one iterate the compact interior set

\[
D_a=\{x:x_i\ge a\ \forall i\}.
\]

For an interior point `x` and tangent vector `h` use the Fisher metric

\[
\|h\|_x^2=\sum_i\frac{h_i^2}{x_i}.
\]

Write `f_i=h_i/x_i`. Then `E_x f=0` and `Var_x(f)=||h||_x^2`.

Let `I,J` be iid with law `x`, and conditional on `(I,J)` let the residual child `K` have law `Q_{IJ,\bullet}`. Then `K\sim q`. For every test function `g`,

\[
\sum_k(DV(x)h)_kg_k
=
2b\,\mathbb E[f(J)g(K)].
\]

Set

\[
H(I,J)=\mathbb E[g(K)\mid I,J],
\qquad
U(J)=\mathbb E[g(K)\mid J].
\]

Because `Q_{ij,k}=Q_{ji,k}`, the Hoeffding decomposition of the symmetric function `H` gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H)=2\operatorname{Var}(U)+\operatorname{Var}(H_{12}).
\]

Since conditioning cannot increase variance,

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H)\le\operatorname{Var}_q(g),
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{Var}_x(U)\le\frac12\operatorname{Var}_q(g).
\]

Therefore

\[
|\mathbb E[f(J)g(K)]|^2
\le
\frac12\operatorname{Var}_x(f)\operatorname{Var}_q(g).
\]

For every probability vector `q` on three states,

\[
\operatorname{Var}_q(g)\le\frac32\operatorname{Var}_u(g).
\]

Indeed, after subtracting the uniform mean, the covariance matrix `diag(q)-qq^T` has largest eigenvalue at most `1/2` by Gershgorin, while `Var_u(g)=||g||_2^2/3`.

Now `y=V(x)=bq+(1-b)u`. The variance-mixture identity gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}_y(g)
\ge
b\operatorname{Var}_q(g)+(1-b)\operatorname{Var}_u(g)
\ge
(1-a)\operatorname{Var}_q(g).
\]

Fisher duality yields

\[
\boxed{
\|DV(x)h\|_{V(x)}^2
\le
\frac{2(1-3a)^2}{1-a}\|h\|_x^2
}.
\]

The factor is `<1` exactly when

\[
18a^2-11a+1<0,
\]

whose relevant root is `a=1/9`. Thus `V` is a uniform strict contraction in intrinsic Fisher length on `D_a`. Since `D_a` is compact and stays away from the coordinate hyperplanes, this length metric is complete. Banach gives a unique fixed point and convergence from every point of `D_a`; all original boundary starts enter `D_a` after one iterate.

Hence

\[
\boxed{\alpha_3\le\frac19}.
\]

The strict input hypothesis is used correctly: `min P>1/9` permits an intermediate `a>1/9`.

# III. New theorem this run: sharp barycentric flip rigidity at the conjectural endpoint

This section isolates the exact local mechanism producing the `1/12` threshold at the barycentre and characterizes equality.

Let

\[
u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)
\]

be a fixed point. Define the `3x3` stochastic matrix

\[
T_{jk}=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^3P_{ij,k}.
\]

Since `u` is fixed, `T` is doubly stochastic. Moreover the tangent spectrum of `DV(u)` is twice the nontrivial spectrum of `T`.

For two distinct parent states, say `1,2`,

\[
T_1-T_2
=
\frac13(P_{11}+P_{13}-P_{22}-P_{23}).
\]

Put

\[
A=\frac12(P_{11}+P_{13}),
\qquad
B=\frac12(P_{22}+P_{23}).
\]

If all heredity coefficients are at least `a`, then `A` and `B` are probability vectors with every coordinate at least `a`, hence

\[
\operatorname{TV}(A,B)\le1-3a.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{TV}(T_1,T_2)
\le
\frac23(1-3a).
\]

The same holds for every row pair, so the Dobrushin coefficient satisfies

\[
\delta(T)\le\frac23(1-3a).
\]

Every nontrivial eigenvalue `lambda` of a stochastic matrix obeys `|lambda|<=delta(T)`. Consequently every tangent eigenvalue `mu` of `DV(u)` satisfies

\[
\boxed{|\mu|\le\frac43(1-3a)}.
\]

Thus the barycentre is strictly locally attracting whenever `a>1/12`, and the constant is sharp at the endpoint.

### Endpoint equality characterization for a flip eigenvalue

Assume now

- `a=1/12`,
- `P_{ij,k}>=a`,
- `u` is fixed,
- `DV(u)` has tangent eigenvalue `-1`.

Then `T` has nontrivial eigenvalue `-1/2`. Let `f` be a real right eigenfunction, `Tf=-(1/2)f`, normalized only by nonconstancy. Since `T` is doubly stochastic and the eigenvalue is not `1`, `f_1+f_2+f_3=0`.

Relabel states so that `f_1=M` is maximal and `f_2=m` is minimal. The oscillation contraction

\[
\operatorname{osc}(Tf)\le\delta(T)\operatorname{osc}(f)
\]

is an equality, because both sides equal `(M-m)/2`. Therefore every step in the row-TV estimate above is sharp. In particular, with `b=1-3a=3/4`, the two averaged heredity rows must be opposite extreme floor-constrained distributions:

\[
A=a(1,1,1)+b e_2=(a,1-2a,a),
\]

\[
B=a(1,1,1)+b e_1=(1-2a,a,a).
\]

Because `A` is the average of `P_{11}` and `P_{13}` and both constituent rows have coordinate floor `a`, extremality forces

\[
P_{11}=P_{13}=A.
\]

Similarly,

\[
P_{22}=P_{23}=B.
\]

The fixed-point condition `V(u)=u` is

\[
P_{11}+P_{22}+P_{33}+2(P_{12}+P_{13}+P_{23})=3(1,1,1).
\]

Substituting the four rows already fixed gives

\[
2P_{12}+P_{33}=(1/4,1/4,5/2).
\]

Every coordinate of `P_{12}` and `P_{33}` is at least `a=1/12`. The first coordinate equation has the exact lower bound

\[
2a+a=1/4,
\]

so equality forces

\[
(P_{12})_1=(P_{33})_1=a.
\]

The second coordinate forces the same, hence

\[
P_{12}=P_{33}=(a,a,1-2a).
\]

Write the third value of the eigenfunction as `c=f_3`. Since `f_1+f_2+f_3=0`, `M+m+c=0`. The row-1 eigenvalue equation now reads

\[
\frac13\bigl(2A\cdot f+P_{12}\cdot f\bigr)=-\frac M2.
\]

Using `A\cdot f=bm` and `P_{12}\cdot f=bc` gives

\[
\frac b3(2m+c)=-\frac M2.
\]

With `b=3/4`, this is `2m+c=-2M`. Combined with `M+m+c=0`, it forces

\[
c=0,\qquad m=-M.
\]

Thus, up to a simultaneous permutation of the three labels and scaling of the eigenfunction, the unique endpoint flip configuration is

\[
f=(1,-1,0)
\]

with heredity rows

\[
P_{11}=P_{13}=(1/12,5/6,1/12),
\]

\[
P_{22}=P_{23}=(5/6,1/12,1/12),
\]

\[
P_{12}=P_{33}=(1/12,1/12,5/6).
\]

This is exactly the `s\downarrow0` endpoint of the explicit 2-cycle family from Part I.

**Interpretation.** The conjectural `1/12` endpoint is not an accidental coefficient from one family: at the barycentre, a period-doubling eigenvalue can reach `-1` only through the same extremal heredity tensor (up to relabeling). Any sharper proof must therefore either control fixed points away from the barycentre or show that near-endpoint dynamics are forced into this rigid normal form.

# IV. New route exclusion: even naive two-step Fisher contraction fails above `1/12`

The one-step Fisher method already fails below about `0.0914`. A natural next hope is that two iterates might contract even when one iterate does not. The following exact rational certificate rules out a universal two-step contraction theorem in the **same** Fisher metric, already at a strict floor barely above `1/12`.

Take

\[
a=\frac{21}{250}=0.084>\frac1{12},
\qquad
b=1-3a=\frac{187}{250}.
\]

Choose the deterministic residual kernel `Q` on unordered parent pairs by

- `11,12,13 -> child 1`,
- `22 -> child 2`,
- `23,33 -> child 3`.

The full heredity rows are `P=a(1,1,1)+bQ`; hence every coefficient is either `a` or `a+b=1-2a`, so

\[
\min P=a=21/250>1/12.
\]

On the simplex, writing `x_3=1-x_1-x_2`, the residual map is

\[
W_1=2x_1-x_1^2,
\qquad
W_2=x_2^2,
\qquad
W_3=1-W_1-W_2.
\]

Therefore the full map in the first two coordinates is

\[
V_1=a+b(2x_1-x_1^2),
\qquad
V_2=a+bx_2^2.
\]

Its reduced Jacobian is diagonal:

\[
J(x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2b(1-x_1)&0\\
0&2bx_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Take the exact interior state

\[
x=\left(\frac3{20},\frac1{10},\frac34\right)
\]

and tangent vector

\[
h=(-10,1,9).
\]

The state lies in `D_a`, since every coordinate exceeds `21/250`. Let `z=V^2(x)` and let

\[
k=D(V^2)(x)h.
\]

Using the Fisher norm

\[
\|r\|_p^2=\sum_i\frac{r_i^2}{p_i},
\]

exact symbolic simplification gives

\[
\frac{\|k\|_z^2}{\|h\|_x^2}
=
\frac{43032428053871146674182064894745224411789503}
{42348093240878655419656833460688026367187500}
\]

and hence

\[
\frac{\|k\|_z^2}{\|h\|_x^2}-1
=
\frac{684334812992491254525231434057198044602003}
{42348093240878655419656833460688026367187500}
>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\|D(V^2)(x)h\|_{V^2(x)}>\|h\|_x}
\]

for a QSO with coefficient floor **strictly larger** than `1/12`.

This does **not** refute regularity. It proves that simply replacing the one-step Fisher contraction argument by the same metric applied to two steps cannot settle the conjecture.

## CONDITIONAL / STRUCTURAL

At an arbitrary fixed point `x`, write `P=a\mathbf1+bQ`, `b=1-3a`, and let `q=(x-a\mathbf1)/b` be the residual child marginal. If `h_i=x_if_i` is a tangent eigenvector with `DV(x)h=\mu h`, then with iid parents `I,J\sim x` and residual child `K`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[f(I)+f(J)\mid K=k]
=
\frac{\mu}{b}\frac{x_k}{q_k}f_k
}.
\]

The right-hand three-point variable is a conditional expectation of `f(I)+f(J)` and is therefore dominated by that parent-sum variable in convex order. This contains strictly more information than the second-moment Fisher argument, but a complete arbitrary-fixed-point sharp inequality has not yet been proved.

# VIII. Next concrete attack

The two-step Fisher obstruction changes the priority. The next serious route should not be another fixed iterate in the same metric.

The most precise remaining obligation is the **arbitrary-fixed-point sharp spectral problem**: prove or refute that every fixed point of every three-type QSO with coefficient floor `a>1/12` has tangent spectral radius `<1`. The exact conditional-expectation identity above reduces this to a three-state finite convex-order problem with weights `x`, `q=(x-a)/b`, and the transformed values `(x_k/q_k)f_k`.

A useful next derivation is to sort the three `f_k` values, split according to the order of `(x_k/q_k)f_k`, and write the finite stop-loss inequalities chamber by chamber. The barycentric equality theorem shows what the endpoint equality pattern must collapse to. If this sharp local theorem succeeds, a separate global step will still be required: either an invariant-region/Lyapunov argument or a period-exclusion theorem for positive three-type QSOs.
