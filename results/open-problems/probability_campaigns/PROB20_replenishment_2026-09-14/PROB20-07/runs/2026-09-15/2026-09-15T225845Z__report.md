For a state `z=(z_1,z_2,z_3)`, define the unordered iid-parent law

\[
U(z)=

# 14. Next attack after period-two closure

Period two is now closed, so the next obligation should move rather than continue subdividing an empty chamber.

The highest-value next target is **fixed-point uniqueness plus full local stability**, because these can feed a global index / Lyapunov argument. Two precise routes are available:

1. **Finish the off-center complex spectrum.**  Earlier work reduced every possible complex-unstable fixed point to a strongly balanced compact region and derived the basis-free determinant inequality
   \[
   |\mu|^4
   \le
   3\prod_{i=1}^3\left(1-\frac1{12x_i}\right).
   \]
   The remaining task is to exploit parent symmetry beyond second moments, likely through the full vector conditional-expectation identity or a sharp determinant bound on the residual influence matrix.

2. **Attack uniqueness by relative majorization for two fixed points.**  If `x!=y` are fixed points, the same residual channel satisfies
   \[
   U(x)Q=\frac{x-a\mathbf1}{b},
   \qquad
   U(y)Q=\frac{y-a\mathbf1}{b}.
   \]
   This gives a finite stop-loss system analogous to the two-cycle system but with unswapped targets. The just-completed chamber machinery suggests that a sharp endpoint uniqueness theorem may be approachable by likelihood-ratio ordering rather than a generic norm.

Even if both uniqueness and local stability are obtained, a separate global convergence theorem is still required. The most promising global directions remain an adapted projective/Fisher metric on the invariant interior region, a strict Lyapunov function, or a two-step contraction after exploiting the now-proved absence of period two.
