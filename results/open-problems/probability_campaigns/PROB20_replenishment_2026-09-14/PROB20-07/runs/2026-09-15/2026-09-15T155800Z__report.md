# 3. Fixed-point residual representation and convex-order lemma

For this theorem it is enough to work at the conjectural endpoint floor

\[
a_0:=\frac1{12},\qquad b_0:=1-3a_0=\frac34.
\]

Indeed any QSO satisfying the strict hypothesis `P_{ij,k}>1/12` also satisfies `P_{ij,k}>=a_0`, so an endpoint rigidity theorem automatically rules out strict-floor counterexamples.

Write

\[
P_{ij,\bullet}=a_0(1,1,1)+b_0Q_{ij,\bullet},
\]

where every residual row `Q_{ij,bullet}` is a probability vector.

Let `x` be a fixed point and let `h` be a nonzero tangent eigenvector:

\[
V(x)=x,\qquad DV(x)h=\mu h,\qquad \sum_i h_i=0.
\]

Use score coordinates

\[
h_i=x_if_i,\qquad \sum_i x_if_i=0.
\]

Every fixed-point coordinate satisfies `x_i>=a_0`, because `V_i(z)>=a_0` for every simplex point `z`. Moreover, if `x_k=a_0`, then the equality `V_k(x)=a_0` together with positivity of all parent-pair weights `x_ix_j` forces `P_{ij,k}=a_0` for every `i,j`; hence the tangent derivative in coordinate `k` is zero. Therefore for an eigenvalue `mu!=0`, every nonzero score coordinate has `x_k>a_0`. This removes all apparent denominator singularities below.

Let `I,J` be iid with law `x`, let `K` be the residual child generated by `Q`, and set

\[
S:=f(I)+f(J).
\]

At the fixed point, the residual child marginal is

\[
q_k=\frac{x_k-a_0}{b_0}.
\]

The derivative eigenvalue equation gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[S\mid K=k]
=
\frac{\mu x_k}{x_k-a_0}f_k.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus, if `R:=E[S|K]`, then

\[
\boxed{R\preceq_{cx}S}
\tag{3.2}
\]

by conditional Jensen. Equivalently, every convex call function satisfies

\[
\mathbb E(R-t)_+\le\mathbb E(S-t)_+
\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb R.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The previous checkpoint used this only in the special case one score coordinate was zero. We now use two simple call inequalities to handle **all** score directions.

# 4. Canonical score normal form

Assume

\[
\mu\le-1.
\]

Write

\[
\mu=-\rho,\qquad \rho\ge1.
\]

A nonzero mean-zero three-point score has both signs. Up to multiplying the eigenvector by `-1`, relabeling states, and scaling, we may write

\[
\boxed{f=(-1,v,w),\qquad 0\le v\le w.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Write

\[
x=(p,q,r),\qquad p+q+r=1.
\]

The mean-zero condition is

\[
\boxed{p=qv+rw.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since the negative score is nonzero, `p>a_0`. If `w>0`, then `r>a_0`; if `v>0`, then `q>a_0`.

By (3.1), `R` has residual probabilities

\[
\frac{p-a_0}{b_0},\qquad
\frac{q-a_0}{b_0},\qquad
\frac{r-a_0}{b_0}
\]

and values

\[
R_1=\rho\frac{p}{p-a_0}>0,
\]

\[
R_2=-\rho\frac{qv}{q-a_0}\le0,
\qquad
R_3=-\rho\frac{rw}{r-a_0}\le0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

We split only according to whether the largest positive score `w` is at most one or greater than one.

# 5. Case I: `0<=v<=w<=1`

Because every positive score is at most the magnitude of the unique negative score, the mixed parent sums are nonpositive:

\[
-1+v\le0,\qquad -1+w\le0.
\]

Hence the positive part of `S` comes entirely from positive-positive parent pairs. Its expectation is exactly

\[
\mathbb E S_+
=2(q+r)(qv+rw)
=\boxed{2p(1-p)}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The positive part of `R` comes only from state `1`, so

\[
\mathbb E R_+
=\frac{p-a_0}{b_0}\rho\frac p{p-a_0}
=\boxed{\frac{\rho p}{b_0}}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Applying convex order at threshold `t=0` and cancelling `p>0`,

\[
\frac\rho{b_0}\le2(1-p).
\]

Since `rho>=1` and `b_0=3/4`,

\[
\boxed{p\le\frac13.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Now use the call function with threshold `t=1`. The target positive atom satisfies `R_1>1`, so

\[
\mathbb E(R-1)_+
=\frac{\rho p-(p-a_0)}{b_0}
=\frac{(\rho-1)p+a_0}{b_0}
\ge\frac{a_0}{b_0}
=\boxed{\frac19}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For the source, any pair involving the negative score `-1` has sum at most zero and contributes nothing at threshold `1`. For two positive scores `u,z in [0,1]`, the elementary pointwise inequality

\[
\boxed{(u+z-1)_+\le uz}
\tag{5.5}
\]

holds: if `u+z<=1` it is trivial, while if `u+z>1` it is equivalent to `(1-u)(1-z)>=0`.

Therefore

\[
\mathbb E(S-1)_+
\le(qv+rw)^2
=\boxed{p^2}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Combining (5.4), (5.6), and convex order gives

\[
p^2\ge\frac19,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{p\ge\frac13.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Together with (5.3),

\[
\boxed{p=\frac13.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

All inequalities above must therefore be equalities. Equation (5.4) then forces

\[
\boxed{\rho=1.}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equality in (5.6) must hold termwise for the positive-positive self-pairs because `q,r>0`. For a score `u in [0,1]`, equality in

\[
(2u-1)_+\le u^2
\]

occurs only at

\[
u=0\quad\text{or}\quad u=1.
\]

Hence

\[
v,w\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The possibilities `v=w=0` and `v=w=1` contradict the mean equation (4.2) with `p=1/3`, so necessarily

\[
\boxed{v=0,\qquad w=1.}
\tag{5.10}
\]

Then (4.2) gives `r=p=1/3`, hence also `q=1/3`.

Thus Case I already forces

\[
\boxed{
x=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad \mu=-1,\qquad f=(-1,0,1)}
\]

up to permutation and scale.

# 6. Case II: `w>1` is impossible

Define the positive-score excess above one

\[
\boxed{D:=q(v-1)_+ + r(w-1)_+.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Because `w>1` and `r>0`,

\[
D>0.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## 6.1 The call at zero gives a lower bound on `D`

The positive-positive parent pairs contribute total positive expectation

\[
2(q+r)(qv+rw)=2p(1-p).
\]

A mixed pair consisting of the negative-score state and a positive-score state of value `u` contributes `(u-1)_+`; summing both parent orders gives the additional amount `2pD`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E S_+
=2p(1-p+D).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

As before,

\[
\mathbb E R_+=\frac{\rho p}{b_0}.
\]

Convex order at `t=0` gives

\[
\frac\rho{b_0}\le2(1-p+D).
\]

Using `rho>=1` and `b_0=3/4`,

\[
\boxed{D\ge p-\frac13.}
\tag{6.4}
\]

## 6.2 A lower-tail call gives an upper bound on `D`

Apply convex order to the convex function

\[
\phi(z)=(-z-1)_+.
\]

For the source `S`, the only value below `-1` is the parent pair `(1,1)`, where `S=-2`. Mixed sums equal `-1+v` or `-1+w`, both at least `-1`, and positive-positive sums are nonnegative. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(-S-1)_+=p^2.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For the target `R`, consider any positive-score state `j in {2,3}` with `f_j>1`. Its contribution to `(-R-1)_+` is

\[
\frac{x_j-a_0}{b_0}
\left(ho\frac{x_jf_j}{x_j-a_0}-1\right)
=
\frac{\rho x_jf_j-(x_j-a_0)}{b_0}
\]

and, since `rho>=1`, this is at least

\[
\frac{x_j(f_j-1)+a_0}{b_0}.
\]

Let `m` be the number of scores among `v,w` that exceed one. Since `w>1`, `m>=1`. Summing only these certainly-positive contributions gives

\[
\mathbb E(-R-1)_+
\ge
\frac{D+ma_0}{b_0}
\ge
\boxed{\frac{D+a_0}{b_0}}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Convex order, (6.5), and (6.6) therefore imply

\[
\boxed{D+a_0\le b_0p^2.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

Because `D>0`, (6.7) immediately gives

\[
p^2>\frac{a_0}{b_0}=\frac19,
\qquad
\boxed{p>\frac13.}
\tag{6.8}
\]

On the other hand, combining (6.4) and (6.7) yields

\[
p-\frac13
\le
D
\le
\frac34p^2-\frac1{12}.
\]

Thus

\[
0\le
\left(\frac34p^2-\frac1{12}\right)-\left(p-\frac13\right)
=
\boxed{\frac{(3p-1)(p-1)}4}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

But `1/3<p<1`: the lower inequality is (6.8), while `p<1` because `q+r>0`. Therefore

\[
(3p-1)(p-1)<0,
\]

contradicting (6.9).

Hence

\[
\boxed{w>1\text{ is impossible}.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

# 7. Arbitrary-direction endpoint rigidity theorem

The two cases exhaust every real tangent eigenvector after the canonical normalization (4.1). We have proved:

## Theorem 7.1

Let `V` be any three-type QSO with

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge\frac1{12}
\qquad\forall i,j,k.
\]

Let `x` be a fixed point and let `mu` be a real tangent eigenvalue. If

\[
\mu\le-1,
\]

then necessarily, up to a simultaneous permutation of labels and scaling of the eigenvector,

\[
\boxed{
x=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad
\mu=-1,\qquad
f=(-1,0,1).
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

The prior barycentric equality theorem from commit `41fb0d80c95074ae25e257a7439d264368efc548` then forces the heredity tensor itself to be exactly, up to relabeling,

\[
P_{11}=P_{13}=\left(\frac1{12},\frac56,\frac1{12}\right),
\]

\[
P_{22}=P_{23}=\left(\frac56,\frac1{12},\frac1{12}\right),
\]

\[
P_{12}=P_{33}=\left(\frac1{12},\frac1{12},\frac56\right).
\tag{7.2}
\]

In particular this tensor has coefficients equal to `1/12`. Therefore:

## Corollary 7.2

If

\[
P_{ij,k}>\frac1{12}
\qquad\forall i,j,k,
\]

then **no fixed point has a real tangent eigenvalue `mu<=-1`**.

This closes the arbitrary-direction local period-doubling route at the conjectural threshold.
