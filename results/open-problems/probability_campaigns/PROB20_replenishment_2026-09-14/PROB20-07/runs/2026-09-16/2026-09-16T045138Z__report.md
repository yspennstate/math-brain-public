## 2. A sharp interior tensor-product TV lemma

The key new ingredient is independent of QSOs.

### Lemma 2.1 — interior product-overlap inequality

Let `p,q` be probability vectors on `m>=2` states and assume

\[
p_i\ge c,\qquad q_i\ge c
\quad\text{for every }i,
\]

for some `c>0`. Put

\[
d=d_{TV}(p,q).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
d_{TV}(p\otimes p,q\otimes q)
\le
d(2-2c-d).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The bound is sharp whenever `0<=d<=1-mc`.

### Proof

If `d=0` there is nothing to prove. Assume `d>0` and put

\[
r_i=\min(p_i,q_i).
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i r_i=1-d,
\qquad
r_i\ge c.
\]

Define the positive and negative residual distributions

\[
\alpha_i=\frac{p_i-r_i}{d},
\qquad
\beta_i=\frac{q_i-r_i}{d}.
\]

Then `alpha,beta` are probability vectors and have disjoint coordinate supports:

\[
\alpha_i\beta_i=0
\quad\text{for every }i.
\]

Thus

\[
p=r+d\alpha,
\qquad
q=r+d\beta.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal O
=
\sum_{i,j}\min(p_ip_j,q_iq_j)
\]

be the overlap coefficient of the two product laws. Since both products dominate `r_i r_j` coordinatewise,

\[
\mathcal O\ge (1-d)^2.
\]

There is an additional overlap on cross indices with `alpha_i>0` and `beta_j>0`. For such `(i,j)`,

\[
p_ip_j=(r_i+d\alpha_i)r_j,
\]

while

\[
q_iq_j=r_i(r_j+d\beta_j).
\]

Therefore

\[
\min(p_ip_j,q_iq_j)
\ge
r_ir_j+d\min(\alpha_i r_j,r_i\beta_j).
\]

The reversed ordered pair `(j,i)` contributes the same lower bound. Summing the extra overlap over the positive and negative residual supports gives

\[
\mathcal O
\ge
(1-d)^2
+
2d
\sum_{i,j}
\min(\alpha_i r_j,r_i\beta_j),
\]

where the sum may be restricted to `alpha_i>0`, `beta_j>0`.

Because `r_i,r_j>=c`,

\[
\min(\alpha_i r_j,r_i\beta_j)
\ge
c\min(\alpha_i,\beta_j).
\]

For numbers in `[0,1]`,

\[
\min(u,v)\ge uv.
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_{i,j}\min(\alpha_i r_j,r_i\beta_j)
\ge
c\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i\beta_j
=c.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathcal O
\ge
(1-d)^2+2cd.
\]

Since total variation of two probability vectors equals one minus their overlap coefficient,

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{TV}(p\otimes p,q\otimes q)
&=1-\mathcal O\\
&\le
1-(1-d)^2-2cd\\
&=
d(2-2c-d),
\end{aligned}
\]

which proves (2.1).

### Sharpness

For `m=3`, choose

\[
p=(c+d,c,1-2c-d),
\qquad
q=(c,c+d,1-2c-d).
\]

This is feasible exactly when `0<=d<=1-3c`. Direct expansion gives

\[
d_{TV}(p,q)=d
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
d_{TV}(p\otimes p,q\otimes q)
=
d(2-2c-d).
}
\]

For general `m`, place the positive residual mass on one coordinate and the negative residual mass on another, set both common coordinates there equal to `c`, and distribute the remaining common mass among the other `m-2` coordinates, each at least `c`. Hence the constant in (2.1) cannot be improved using only the coordinate floor.

## 3. Improved QSO secant inequality on the image simplex

Assume a three-type QSO satisfies

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge a,
\qquad
0<a<\frac13,
\]

and put

\[
b=1-3a.
\]

Write

\[
P_{ij,k}=a+bQ_{ij,k},
\]

with residual symmetric stochastic rows `Q`.

Every image point obeys

\[
V_k(x)\ge a,
\]

so

\[
V(\Delta_2)\subseteq \Delta_a
:=
\{x\in\Delta_2:x_i\ge a\}.
\]

For `x,y in Delta_a`, residual-channel data processing gives

\[
d_{TV}(Vx,Vy)
=
b\,d_{TV}(W(x),W(y))
\le
b\,d_{TV}(x\otimes x,y\otimes y).
\]

Applying Lemma 2.1 with coordinate floor `c=a` yields

\[
\boxed{
d_{TV}(Vx,Vy)
\le
b\,d(2-2a-d),
\qquad
d=d_{TV}(x,y).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This strictly improves the previous bound

\[
b\,d(2-d)
\]

by the exact term

\[
2abd.
\]

The improvement is unavailable for arbitrary simplex inputs because they may have zero coordinates; it becomes available precisely after one iterate, and therefore on every invariant or omega-limit set.

## 4. New invariant-set diameter theorem

Let `K` be nonempty, compact, and satisfy

\[
V(K)=K.
\]

Then `K subset Delta_a`. Define

\[
D=\max_{x,y\in K}d_{TV}(x,y).
\]

Choose a diameter pair `x,y` and preimages `x',y' in K` with

\[
V(x')=x,
\qquad
V(y')=y.
\]

Set

\[
d'=d_{TV}(x',y')\le D.
\]

By (3.1),

\[
D
\le
b\,d'(2-2a-d').
\]

On `Delta_a`, the largest possible TV distance is `1-3a=b`. The scalar function

\[
g(t)=t(2-2a-t)
\]

is increasing on `[0,b]`, because

\[
g'(t)=2-2a-2t
\ge
2-2a-2(1-3a)
=
4a>0.
\]

Hence

\[
D\le bD(2-2a-D).
\]

If `D>0`,

\[
1\le b(2-2a-D),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
D
\le
2-2a-\frac1b
=
2-2a-\frac1{1-3a}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

If the right side is nonpositive, `D>0` is impossible and `K` is a singleton.

### Endpoint specialization

At

\[
a=\frac1{12},
\qquad
b=\frac34,
\]

(4.1) gives

\[
\boxed{
D\le
2-\frac16-\frac43
=
\frac12.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus every compact invariant set, every periodic orbit of every prime period, and every omega-limit set at the weak endpoint has TV diameter at most `1/2`.

This replaces the preceding checkpoint's `2/3` bound.

### Strict endpoint hypothesis

If the actual tensor satisfies

\[
\min_{i,j,k}P_{ij,k}>\frac1{12},
\]

choose

\[
\frac1{12}<a<\min P.
\]

The function

\[
\Phi(a)
=
2-2a-\frac1{1-3a}
\]

is strictly decreasing, since

\[
\Phi'(a)
=
-2-\frac3{(1-3a)^2}<0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{diam}_{TV}(K)
<
\frac12
}
\]

for every nonempty compact invariant set under the strict conjectural floor.

This preserves the strict `>` quantifier required by Problem 12.

## 5. Weak-endpoint boundary rigidity for omega-limit sets

The weak endpoint permits tensor coefficients equal to `1/12`, so (4.2) alone is non-strict. However actual non-singleton omega-limit sets cannot touch the coordinate floor.

Let

\[
a=\frac1{12}
\]

and let `Omega=omega(z)` be an omega-limit set. As proved in the preceding checkpoint,

\[
V(\Omega)=\Omega.
\]

Suppose some `y in Omega` has

\[
y_k=a.
\]

Choose `x in Omega` with `V(x)=y`. Every coordinate of `x` is at least `a>0`. Hence all weights `x_i x_j` in

\[
y_k=\sum_{i,j}P_{ij,k}x_ix_j
\]

are strictly positive. Since every `P_{ij,k}>=a` and the weighted average equals exactly `a`, equality forces

\[
\boxed{
P_{ij,k}=a
\quad\text{for every }i,j.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
V_k(w)=a
\quad\text{for every simplex point }w.
\]

After one iterate the whole dynamics lies on the closed interval

\[
I_k=\{w\in\Delta_2:w_k=a\}.
\]

The restriction `V|_{I_k}` is a continuous interval self-map. The already archived endpoint period-two theorem says the full QSO has no nontrivial 2-cycle, hence the interval restriction has no 2-cycle. By Coppel's theorem for continuous interval maps, every orbit on `I_k` converges to a fixed point. The already archived endpoint uniqueness theorem gives exactly one fixed point. Therefore every orbit on `I_k` converges to that unique fixed point, and in particular

\[
\Omega
\]

must be a singleton.

We have proved:

\[
\boxed{
\Omega\text{ non-singleton}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_i>\frac1{12}
\text{ for every }x\in\Omega,\ i=1,2,3.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Because a non-singleton `Omega` is compact, there is then some

\[
c_\Omega>\frac1{12}
\]

such that every coordinate of every point of `Omega` is at least `c_Omega`.

Now repeat Lemma 2.1 using the **state-space** floor `c_Omega`, while retaining the coefficient residual factor `b=3/4`. For a diameter `D>0`,

\[
D
\le
\frac34 D(2-2c_\Omega-D),
\]

so

\[
D
\le
\frac23-2c_\Omega
<
\frac12.
\]

Therefore the weak endpoint actually satisfies the strict omega-limit conclusion

\[
\boxed{
\omega(z)\text{ non-singleton}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{diam}_{TV}\omega(z)<\frac12.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

The margin is orbit-dependent and is not yet uniform over all QSOs or all initial conditions.

Every nontrivial finite periodic orbit is its own omega-limit set, so (5.3) also applies to every hypothetical prime period `>=3`.

## 6. All-dimension form of the TV lemma and a secondary regularity checkpoint

The product-overlap Lemma 2.1 is dimension-independent. For an `m`-type QSO with coefficient floor `a`, put

\[
b=1-ma.
\]

Exactly the same proof gives, on the image simplex,

\[
d_{TV}(Vx,Vy)
\le
b\,d(2-2a-d).
\]

Therefore every compact invariant set has

\[
\boxed{
D\le
2-2a-\frac1{1-ma}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

whenever `D>0`.

The right side becomes nonpositive when

\[
2(1-a)(1-ma)\le1.
\]

The smaller equality root is

\[
\boxed{
a_m^{TV}
=
\frac{m+1-\sqrt{m^2+1}}{2m}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Above this floor, the TV argument alone forces every compact invariant set to be a singleton, hence forces a unique fixed point and global convergence.

For `m=3` this gives

\[
a_3^{TV}
=
\frac{4-\sqrt{10}}6
\approx0.13962,
\]

which improves the old generic `1/6` coefficient estimate but is weaker than the already archived sharp `1/9` Fisher checkpoint. It is therefore recorded as an all-dimension byproduct, not as the best three-type upper bound.

This still leaves two genuine global possibilities:

- a prime periodic orbit of period at least three contained strictly inside the simplex and having diameter `<1/2`;
- an aperiodic compact recurrent set of diameter `<1/2`.

Numerical evidence or local stability does not exclude either.

The new overlap argument suggests two next attacks.

The missing step is to make that gain self-improving or uniform from the invariant geometry, rather than merely orbit-dependent.
