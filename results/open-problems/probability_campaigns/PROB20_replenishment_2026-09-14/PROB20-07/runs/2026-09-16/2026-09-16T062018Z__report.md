## 1. Global invariant-measure information inequality

The preceding diameter theorem controls compact invariant sets geometrically. There is also an exact information constraint on every invariant probability measure.

Let the coefficient floor be

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge a>0,
\qquad
b=1-3a,
\]

and write

\[
P_{ij,k}=a+bQ_{ij,k},
\]

where `Q` is again a symmetric stochastic heredity tensor. Equivalently, conditional on the two parent types, the child is generated as follows:

- with probability `1-b=3a`, ignore the parents and choose the child uniformly from the three types;
- with probability `b`, use the residual heredity law `Q`.

Let `nu` be any Borel probability measure on the simplex satisfying

\[
V_*\nu=\nu.
\]

Draw a random state `X~nu`. Conditional on `X=x`, draw two iid parent types `I,J~x`. Then draw the child `K` with law `P_{IJ,\bullet}`.

Define

\[
\mathcal I:=I(X;I).
\]

If

\[
m=\mathbb E X,
\]

then

\[
\mathcal I
=H(m)-\mathbb E H(X).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Because `K|X=x` has law `V(x)`, because `V(X)` has the same law as `X` under an invariant measure, and because `K` has marginal `m`,

\[
I(X;K)
=H(m)-\mathbb E H(V(X))
=H(m)-\mathbb E H(X)
=\mathcal I.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Now expose the Bernoulli mixture variable `B` deciding whether the uniform-noise branch or the residual branch is used. `B` is independent of `(X,I,J)`. On `B=0`, the child is independent of everything; on `B=1`, the residual child is downstream of `(I,J)`. Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal I
=I(X;K)
&\le I(X;K,B)\\
&=b\,I(X;K\mid B=1)\\
&\le b\,I(X;I,J).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Because `I` and `J` are conditionally independent given `X`, while both have marginal `m`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(X;I,J)
&=H(I,J)-H(I,J\mid X)\\
&=2H(m)-I(I;J)-2\mathbb E H(X)\\
&=2\mathcal I-\mathcal R,
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.4}
\]

where

\[
\mathcal R:=I(I;J)\ge0
\]

is the redundancy/correlation between two iid draws from the same random population state.

Combining (1.3)--(1.4), every invariant measure obeys

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal I
\le
b(2\mathcal I-\mathcal R).
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Hence, whenever `b>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R
\le
\left(2-\frac1b\right)\mathcal I.
}
\tag{1.6}
\]

At the conjectural endpoint

\[
a=\frac1{12},
\qquad
b=\frac34,
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R\le\frac23\mathcal I.
}
\tag{1.7}
\]

For the original strict hypothesis `p_*:=min P>1/12`, one may take `a=p_*`, so `b_*=1-3p_*<3/4` and therefore every non-Dirac invariant measure must satisfy the strictly stronger operator-dependent inequality

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal R}{\mathcal I}
\le
2-\frac1{1-3p_*}
<\frac23.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]

For a non-Dirac invariant measure, `mathcal I>0` by strict concavity of entropy. Also `mathcal R>0`: if `I` and `J` were independent, then

\[
\mathbb E[X\otimes X]
=(\mathbb EX)\otimes(\mathbb EX),
\]

so the covariance matrix of `X` would vanish and `X` would be almost surely constant.

Thus (1.7) and (1.8) are genuine restrictions, not vacuous zero-information statements.

### Periodic-orbit interpretation

If `x_0,...,x_{n-1}` is a prime periodic orbit and `nu` is the uniform measure on that orbit, then (1.6) applies directly. In this language, one sampled parent type carries `mathcal I` nats of information about the phase/state; two conditionally iid parent samples carry exactly

\[
2\mathcal I-\mathcal R,
\]

and the coefficient-floor noise allows the child to retain at most the fraction `b` of that two-parent information. Periodicity/invariance requires the child to recover exactly the same one-sample information `mathcal I`.

This converts the higher-period problem into a precise information redundancy obstruction. It does **not** yet close higher periods: weakly separated invariant states can have `mathcal R/mathcal I` arbitrarily small, so a universal lower bound of `2/3` is false near a singleton.

The next useful form of this route would be an operator-specific lower redundancy bound on a minimal recurrent set, possibly combined with the already proved TV-diameter `<1/2` localization and local attraction around the unique fixed point.

## 2. Fixed-point stationary flow and a new universal circulation cap

The remaining local spectral issue is an off-centre nonreal tangent pair. The earlier stationary-flow checkpoint introduced

\[
T_{jk}=\sum_i x_iP_{ij,k}
\]

at a fixed point `x`, and

\[
M_{jk}=x_jT_{jk}.
\]

`T` is stochastic and `xT=x`, so `M` has both row and column marginals equal to `x`.

For three states, conservation forces one circulation scalar:

\[
\boxed{
M_{12}-M_{21}
=M_{23}-M_{32}
=M_{31}-M_{13}
=:c.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

This run proves the endpoint cap

\[
\boxed{|c|\le\frac16.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Moreover, equality in (2.2) forces

\[
x=(1/3,1/3,1/3).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since the barycentric complex spectrum was already closed sharply in an earlier checkpoint, any hypothetical complex-unstable fixed point must therefore satisfy the strict inequality

\[
|c|<\frac16.
\tag{2.4}
\]

### 2.1 Parent-symmetry edge cap

Work at

\[
a=\frac1{12},
\qquad
b=\frac34,
\]

and write

\[
P_{ij,\bullet}=a(1,1,1)+bQ_{ij,\bullet}.
\]

At a fixed point, define

\[
R_{jk}:=\sum_i x_iQ_{ij,k},
\qquad
q_k:=\frac{x_k-a}{b}.
\]

Then

\[
T_{jk}=a+bR_{jk}.
\]

Using parent symmetry,

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_k
={}&x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
+2x_j\sum_{i\ne j}x_iQ_{ij,k}\\
&+\sum_{i,\ell\ne j}x_ix_\ell Q_{i\ell,k},
\end{aligned}
\]

while

\[
2x_jR_{jk}
=
2x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
+2x_j\sum_{i\ne j}x_iQ_{ij,k}.
\]

Therefore

\[
2x_jR_{jk}-q_k
=x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
-
\sum_{i,\ell\ne j}x_ix_\ell Q_{i\ell,k}
\le x_j^2.
\]

Hence

\[
R_{jk}
\le
\frac{q_k+x_j^2}{2x_j}.
\]

Multiplying by `x_j` and restoring the floor gives

\[
\boxed{
M_{jk}
\le
U_{jk}:=
a x_j+
\frac{x_k-a}{2}
+
\frac b2x_j^2.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The coefficient floor also gives

\[
\boxed{M_{jk}\ge L_{jk}:=a x_j.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

### 2.2 Reduction to a scalar cyclic gap lemma

Assume first `c<=0` and put `d=-c>=0`. From (2.1),

\[
d=M_{21}-M_{12}=M_{32}-M_{23}=M_{13}-M_{31}.
\]

Using (2.5)--(2.6),

\[
d\le U_{21}-L_{12},
\]

\[
d\le U_{32}-L_{23},
\]

\[
d\le U_{13}-L_{31}.
\]

Define

\[
F(u,v)
:=
\left(\frac12-a\right)v
+a u
+\frac b2u^2
-\frac a2.
\]

At `a=1/12,b=3/4`,

\[
\boxed{
F(u,v)-\frac16
=
\frac{9u^2+2u+10v-5}{24}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Thus

\[
d\le
\min\{F(x_2,x_1),F(x_3,x_2),F(x_1,x_3)\}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

We show that the minimum in (2.8) is always at most `1/6`.

Suppose contrariwise that all three terms are strictly larger than `1/6`. Put

\[
y_i=x_i-\frac13,
\qquad
\sum_i y_i=0.
\]

After a cyclic relabelling let

\[
y_1=-r=\min_i y_i<0.
\]

Because every fixed-point coordinate is at least `1/12`,

\[
0<r\le\frac14.
\]

Write

\[
y_2=s,
\qquad
y_3=r-s.
\]

The inequalities

\[
F(x_2,x_1)>\frac16,
\qquad
F(x_1,x_3)>\frac16
\]

become

\[
9s^2+8s-10r>0,
\tag{2.9}
\]

and

\[
9r^2+2r-10s>0.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Equation (2.9) forces `s>0`, while (2.10) gives

\[
0<s<U(r):=\frac{r(9r+2)}{10}.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Since `9s^2+8s` is increasing for `s>=0`, (2.9)--(2.11) imply

\[
9U(r)^2+8U(r)-10r>0.
\]

But exact factorization gives

\[
9U(r)^2+8U(r)-10r
=
\frac{3r}{100}
\left(
243r^3+108r^2+252r-280
\right).
\tag{2.12}
\]

The cubic in parentheses is strictly increasing for `r>=0`, and at `r=1/4` equals

\[
-\frac{13213}{64}<0.
\]

Hence the right side of (2.12) is negative for every `0<r<=1/4`, contradiction.

Therefore

\[
\min\{F(x_2,x_1),F(x_3,x_2),F(x_1,x_3)\}
\le\frac16,
\]

and consequently `d<=1/6`.

The case `c>=0` is identical with the cyclic orientation reversed, proving (2.2).

For equality `|c|=1/6`, all three corresponding edge gaps must be at least `1/6`. The same non-strict version of the preceding argument rules out every `r>0`; hence `r=0` and `x=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`, proving (2.3).

## 3. Consequences for the remaining complex tangent spectrum

Let `D=diag(x)` and set

\[
S=D^{1/2}TD^{-1/2}.
\]

Since `T1=1` and `xT=x`, both `S` and `S^T` fix `sqrt(x)`. Thus the orthogonal complement of `sqrt(x)` is a two-dimensional invariant space.

For the skew part

\[
A=\frac{S-S^T}{2},
\]

we have

\[
A_{jk}
=
\frac{M_{jk}-M_{kj}}{2\sqrt{x_jx_k}}.
\]

Using (2.1), the squared skew magnitude on the two-dimensional tangent plane is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa^2
&=
\frac{c^2}{4}
\left(
\frac1{x_1x_2}
+
\frac1{x_2x_3}
+
\frac1{x_3x_1}
\right)\\
&=
\boxed{\frac{c^2}{4x_1x_2x_3}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

If the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` are a nonreal pair `lambda,bar(lambda)`, then in a real orthonormal tangent basis `S=H+A` with `H` symmetric and `A` the two-dimensional skew rotation of magnitude `kappa`. Therefore

\[
|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|\le\kappa.
\]

The QSO tangent multipliers are

\[
\mu=2\lambda,
\qquad
\bar\mu=2\bar\lambda,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
|\operatorname{Im}\mu|
\le
\frac{|c|}{\sqrt{x_1x_2x_3}}
\le
\frac1{6\sqrt{x_1x_2x_3}}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

There is also a clean trace bound. From the floor,

\[
T_{jj}\ge a,
\]

so

\[
\operatorname{tr}T\ge\frac14.
\]

From (2.5),

\[
T_{jj}
\le
a+\frac12-\frac{a}{2x_j}+\frac b2x_j.
\]

Summing and using `sum x_j=1` gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}T
\le
\frac{17}{8}
-
\frac1{24}
\sum_j\frac1{x_j}.
\]

Since `sum 1/x_j>=9`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac14
\le
\operatorname{tr}T
\le
\frac74.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

For a complex QSO tangent pair,

\[
\operatorname{Re}\mu
=\operatorname{tr}T-1,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Re}\mu|\le\frac34.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Combine this with the earlier harmonic localization from `2026-09-16T030649Z`: if a complex endpoint multiplier satisfies `|mu|>=1`, then every fixed-point coordinate lies in

\[
\frac16\le x_i\le\frac59.
\tag{3.5}
\]

On this box with `sum x_i=1`, the product has the elementary lower bound

\[
x_1x_2x_3\ge
\frac16\cdot\frac5{18}\cdot\frac59
=
\frac{25}{972}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

If `|mu|>=1`, then (3.4) forces

\[
|\operatorname{Im}\mu|
\ge
\frac{\sqrt7}{4}.
\]

Together with (3.2),

\[
|c|
\ge
\frac{\sqrt{7x_1x_2x_3}}4
\ge
\boxed{
\frac{5\sqrt{21}}{216}
}
\approx0.1061.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus every still-hypothetical complex-unstable fixed point must live in the narrow circulation chamber

\[
\boxed{
\frac{5\sqrt{21}}{216}
\le
|c|
<
\frac16,
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

in addition to all previously archived harmonic/product constraints. The strict upper inequality follows because `|c|=1/6` forces the barycentre, where the already proved sharp barycentric theorem gives `|mu|<=sqrt(3)/2<1`.

This does not yet prove full local stability, but it removes all small-circulation candidates and converts the remaining complex problem into a quantitatively narrow, explicitly parameterized flow chamber.

### Failed / insufficient routes retained

- The information inequality alone does not force an invariant measure to be Dirac at the endpoint: for a family of states collapsing to one point, `I(I;J)/I(X;I)` can tend to zero.
- The circulation cap alone does not prove `|mu|<1`; it is a localization theorem, not a spectral closure.
- Raw one-step and raw two-step Fisher contraction remain known to fail above `1/12` from earlier exact counterexamples.
- Unique fixed point plus no period two is not, by itself, a valid global-convergence theorem for a general continuous map of a two-dimensional simplex.
