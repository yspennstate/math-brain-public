# 2. Residual decomposition and a nonlinear total-variation inequality

Assume

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge a,
\qquad 0<a<\frac13,
\]

and set

\[
b:=1-3a.
\]

Write

\[
P_{ij,k}=a+bQ_{ij,k},
\]

where each residual row `Q_{ij,bullet}` is stochastic and symmetric in the parents. Let `W` be the corresponding residual QSO. Then

\[
V(x)=a(1,1,1)+bW(x).
\tag{2.1}
\]

For probability vectors `p,q`, write

\[
d_{TV}(p,q)=\frac12\|p-q\|_1.
\]

Let `U(x)` denote the law of two iid parents with common distribution `x`. One can use the ordered law `x\otimes x`; passing to unordered parent pairs is a measurable pushforward and can only decrease total variation. The residual child is obtained from the parent-pair law through the stochastic channel `Q`. Therefore data processing and (2.1) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{TV}(Vx,Vy)
&=b\,d_{TV}(W(x),W(y))\\
&\le b\,d_{TV}(x\otimes x,y\otimes y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Put

\[
d=d_{TV}(x,y).
\]

Take a maximal coupling `(X,Y)` of `x,y`, so

\[
\mathbb P(X=Y)=1-d.
\]

Take two independent copies of this coupling. The ordered parent pairs then agree with probability at least

\[
(1-d)^2.
\]

By the coupling characterization of total variation,

\[
d_{TV}(x\otimes x,y\otimes y)
\le 1-(1-d)^2
=2d-d^2.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Combining (2.2) and (2.3) yields the global nonlinear estimate

\[
\boxed{
d_{TV}(Vx,Vy)
\le b\bigl(2d_{TV}(x,y)-d_{TV}(x,y)^2\bigr).
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

No fixed-point, local-stability, interior-start, or three-type geometry beyond the common floor is used in (2.4). The dimension enters only through `b=1-3a` for the present three-type problem.

# 3. New theorem: diameter of every compact invariant set

## Theorem 3.1

Let `K` be a nonempty compact subset of the simplex satisfying

\[
V(K)=K.
\]

Let

\[
D:=\max_{x,y\in K}d_{TV}(x,y).
\]

Then:

1. if `b<=1/2`, `D=0`;
2. if `b>1/2`,
   \[
   \boxed{D\le 2-\frac1b.}
   \tag{3.1}
   \]

### Proof

Compactness makes the diameter attainable. Choose `x,y in K` with

\[
d_{TV}(x,y)=D.
\]

Because `V(K)=K`, choose preimages `x',y' in K` such that

\[
V(x')=x,
\qquad
V(y')=y.
\]

By (2.4),

\[
D
\le
b\left(2d_{TV}(x',y')-d_{TV}(x',y')^2\right).
\]

The scalar function

\[
f(t)=b(2t-t^2)
\]

is increasing on `[0,1]`, while `d_{TV}(x',y')<=D`. Hence

\[
D\le b(2D-D^2).
\tag{3.2}
\]

If `D>0`, division by `D` gives

\[
1\le b(2-D),
\]

or

\[
D\le 2-\frac1b.
\]

If `b<=1/2`, the right side is nonpositive, so `D>0` is impossible. This proves the theorem. `square`

### Endpoint specialization

At

\[
a=\frac1{12},
\qquad b=\frac34,
\]

(3.1) gives

\[
\boxed{D\le\frac23.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

If the original coefficients satisfy the strict endpoint floor

\[
\min_{i,j,k}P_{ij,k}>\frac1{12},
\]

choose

\[
\frac1{12}<a<\min P.
\]

Then `b<3/4`, and (3.1) gives the strict improvement

\[
\boxed{D<\frac23.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The strict quantifier is therefore preserved correctly.

# 4. Omega-limit sets and periodic orbits

Let `z` be any initial state. Its omega-limit set `omega(z)` is nonempty and compact because the simplex is compact. Continuity gives

\[
V(\omega(z))\subseteq\omega(z).
\]

For the reverse inclusion, if `w in omega(z)`, choose `n_r -> infinity` with

\[
V^{n_r}(z)\to w.
\]

A subsequence of the predecessors `V^{n_r-1}(z)` converges to some `u`. Then `u in omega(z)` and continuity gives

\[
V(u)=w.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{V(\omega(z))=\omega(z).}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Theorem 3.1 applies directly.

## Corollary 4.1

At weak floor `P>=1/12`, every omega-limit set satisfies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{diam}_{TV}\omega(z)\le\frac23.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Under strict floor `P>1/12`, every omega-limit set has diameter strictly less than `2/3`.

Every finite periodic orbit is itself a compact invariant set, so the same statement applies to periodic orbits of **every prime period**, not only period two.

This is genuinely global information. It is nevertheless insufficient for regularity: a compact recurrent set of positive diameter less than `2/3` has not been excluded.

# 5. Secondary affinity bound and why it is weaker

For completeness, the same residual decomposition gives a parallel Hellinger/Bhattacharyya statement. Let

\[
A(p,q)=\sum_i\sqrt{p_iq_i}
\]

be the affinity. Product laws satisfy

\[
A(x\otimes x,y\otimes y)=A(x,y)^2,
\]

stochastic channels increase affinity, and joint concavity under the common mixture in (2.1) gives

\[
A(Vx,Vy)
\ge
1-b+bA(x,y)^2.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `m` is the minimum pairwise affinity on a compact invariant set, then

\[
m\ge1-b+bm^2.
\]

For `b>1/2`, this yields

\[
\boxed{m\ge\frac{1-b}{b}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At `b=3/4`, `m>=1/3`. This does not improve the total-variation diameter theorem enough to force convergence, so it is recorded as a secondary route rather than promoted as the main result.

# 6. Fixed-point influence matrix as a stationary three-state flow

The remaining local obstruction is a nonreal tangent pair at the unique endpoint fixed point. This section gives an exact low-dimensional representation for that obstruction.

Let `x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)` be a fixed point and define the one-parent influence matrix

\[
T_{jk}=\sum_i x_iP_{ij,k}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then `T` is row stochastic and

\[
xT=x.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Define the stationary edge-flow matrix

\[
M_{jk}:=x_jT_{jk}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Both row and column marginals of `M` are `x`.

For a stationary flow on three states, conservation implies that the antisymmetric flow has one circulation parameter. Choose orientation so

\[
M_{12}-M_{21}
=M_{23}-M_{32}
=M_{31}-M_{13}
=:c.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Define symmetric edge totals

\[
s_{12}=M_{12}+M_{21},
\quad
s_{23}=M_{23}+M_{32},
\quad
s_{31}=M_{31}+M_{13}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Then

\[
M_{12}=\frac{s_{12}+c}{2},
\quad
M_{21}=\frac{s_{12}-c}{2},
\]
\[
M_{23}=\frac{s_{23}+c}{2},
\quad
M_{32}=\frac{s_{23}-c}{2},
\]
\[
M_{31}=\frac{s_{31}+c}{2},
\quad
M_{13}=\frac{s_{31}-c}{2},
\tag{6.6}
\]

and the diagonal entries are

\[
M_{11}=x_1-\frac{s_{12}+s_{31}}2,
\]
\[
M_{22}=x_2-\frac{s_{12}+s_{23}}2,
\]
\[
M_{33}=x_3-\frac{s_{23}+s_{31}}2.
\tag{6.7}
\]

A direct determinant expansion, using `x_1+x_2+x_3=1`, gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\det M={}&x_1x_2x_3+\frac{c^2}{4}
-\frac12\Bigl[
 s_{12}x_3(x_1+x_2)
+s_{23}x_1(x_2+x_3)\\
&\hspace{38mm}
+s_{31}x_2(x_1+x_3)
\Bigr]
+\frac14\left(s_{12}s_{23}+s_{12}s_{31}+s_{23}s_{31}\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.8}
\]

Since

\[
M=\operatorname{diag}(x)T,
\]

we have

\[
\boxed{
\det T=\frac{\det M}{x_1x_2x_3}.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

If the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` form a nonreal conjugate pair, then the tangent multipliers of the QSO are twice those eigenvalues, and hence

\[
\boxed{|\mu|^2=4\det T.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Thus the sharp-looking numerical statement

\[
\boxed{\det T\le\frac3{16}}
\tag{6.11}
\]

would imply the global complex bound

\[
|\mu|\le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}<1
\]

at every fixed point, exactly matching the already proved sharp barycentric theorem.

Equation (6.11) is **not proved here**.

# 7. Parent-symmetry constraints available for the determinant attack

At the endpoint write

\[
P_{ij,\bullet}=\frac1{12}(1,1,1)+\frac34Q_{ij,\bullet}
\]

and define the residual influence matrix

\[
R_{jk}=\sum_i x_iQ_{ij,k}.
\]

The residual child marginal is

\[
q_k=\frac{x_k-1/12}{3/4}.
\]

Parent symmetry gives the pointwise inequality already derived in the earlier complex-spectrum work:

\[
\boxed{
R_{jk}\le\frac{q_k+x_j^2}{2x_j}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Indeed, in the residual child marginal `q_k`, isolate all parent pairs incident to `j` and use `Q_{jj,k}<=1`.

Since

\[
T=\frac1{12}J+\frac34R,
\]

(7.1) becomes an explicit upper bound on each stationary flow entry:

\[
\boxed{
M_{jk}
\le
\frac{x_k-1/12}{2}
+\frac{x_j}{12}
+\frac{3x_j^2}{8}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

The lower coefficient floor gives simultaneously

\[
\boxed{M_{jk}\ge\frac{x_j}{12}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Therefore the next local proof obligation is now a concrete semialgebraic problem: combine (6.6)–(6.9) with (7.2)–(7.3), the nonnegative diagonal constraints, and `x_i>=1/12`, `sum x_i=1`, to prove

\[
\det M\le\frac3{16}x_1x_2x_3.
\tag{7.4}
\]

A stronger row-coupled consequence of symmetry is also available. From

\[
q_k
\ge
2x_jR_{jk}-x_j^2Q_{jj,k}
\]

we obtain

\[
Q_{jj,k}
\ge
\frac{(2x_jR_{jk}-q_k)_+}{x_j^2}.
\]

Summing over `k` and using `sum_k Q_{jj,k}=1` yields

\[
\boxed{
\sum_k(2x_jR_{jk}-q_k)_+\le x_j^2.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

This keeps the shared diagonal residual row that the pointwise caps discard and is a promising source of the missing determinant gain.
