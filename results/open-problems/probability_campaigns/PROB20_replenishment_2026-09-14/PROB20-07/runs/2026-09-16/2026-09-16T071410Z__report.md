## 1. Endpoint fixed-point setup

Work at

\[
a=\frac1{12},\qquad b=1-3a=\frac34.
\]

Let `x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)` be a fixed point and define

\[
T_{jk}=\sum_i x_iP_{ij,k}.
\]

Then `T` is stochastic and

\[
xT=x.
\]

The QSO tangent derivative satisfies

\[
DV(x)|_{T\Delta}=2T|_{\text{nontrivial two-dimensional quotient}},
\]

so if the nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` are `lambda, conjugate(lambda)`, the QSO tangent pair is

\[
\mu=2\lambda,\qquad \bar\mu=2\bar\lambda.
\]

Put

\[
M_{jk}=x_jT_{jk}.
\]

`M` has both row and column marginals equal to `x`. Conservation in three states gives one circulation scalar

\[
M_{12}-M_{21}=M_{23}-M_{32}=M_{31}-M_{13}=:c.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
d:=|c|,\qquad p:=x_1x_2x_3,\qquad H:=\sum_i\frac1{x_i}.
\]

The preceding flow checkpoint proved the skew estimate

\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Im}\mu|\le\frac{d}{\sqrt p}.}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The preceding harmonic checkpoint proved

\[
\boxed{
|\mu|^2\le
\frac74-\frac5{72}H.
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Hence any hypothetical complex instability `|mu|>=1` necessarily satisfies

\[
\boxed{H\le\frac{54}{5}.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

## 2. Two exact upper bounds on the real part

Since the nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` are `lambda,bar(lambda)`,

\[
\operatorname{Re}\mu
=2\operatorname{Re}\lambda
=\operatorname{tr}T-1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### 2.1 Diagonal parent-symmetry cap

The archived parent-symmetry edge estimate is

\[
M_{jk}\le
U_{jk}:=
a x_j+\frac{x_k-a}{2}+\frac b2x_j^2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Taking `j=k`, dividing by `x_j`, and summing gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}T
&\le
\sum_j\left(a+\frac12-\frac{a}{2x_j}+\frac b2x_j\right)\\
&=2+\frac{3a}{2}-\frac a2H.
\end{aligned}
\]

At `a=1/12`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}T\le\frac{17}{8}-\frac{H}{24},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}\mu\le
A(H):=\frac98-\frac{H}{24}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

### 2.2 Circulation consumes off-diagonal mass

Assume first `c=-d<=0` and set

\[
u=M_{12},\qquad v=M_{23},\qquad w=M_{31}.
\]

Then

\[
M_{21}=u+d,\quad M_{32}=v+d,\quad M_{13}=w+d.
\]

The coefficient floor gives

\[
u\ge ax_1,\qquad v\ge ax_2,\qquad w\ge ax_3.
\]

Therefore the total normalized off-diagonal mass satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
3-\operatorname{tr}T
={}&u\left(\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}\right)
+v\left(\frac1{x_2}+\frac1{x_3}\right)\\
&+w\left(\frac1{x_3}+\frac1{x_1}\right)+dH\\
\ge{}&a\left(3+\frac{x_1}{x_2}+\frac{x_2}{x_3}+\frac{x_3}{x_1}\right)+dH\\
\ge{}&6a+dH,
\end{aligned}
\]

because the cyclic ratio sum is at least `3` by AM--GM. Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Re}\mu\le\frac32-dH.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

For `c=+d`, use the reverse cyclic orientation; the same estimate follows.

Combining (2.3)--(2.4), whenever `Re(mu)>=0`,

\[
0\le\operatorname{Re}\mu
\le
\min\left\{A(H),\frac32-dH\right\}.
\tag{2.5}

## 3. Quantitative circulation drop away from the barycentre

## 5. Exclusion of every complex instability with nonnegative real part

Assume for contradiction that

\[
|\mu|\ge1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Re}\mu\ge0.
\]

By (1.4) and (4.6),

\[
9\le H\le\frac{54}{5},
\qquad
0\le\rho\le\frac{\sqrt6}{9}.
\]

Let

\[
A(H)=\frac98-\frac{H}{24},
\qquad
B_H(d)=\frac32-dH.
\]

Equations (1.2) and (2.5) imply

\[
|\mu|^2
\le
\min\{A(H),B_H(d)\}_+^2+\frac{d^2}{p}
=:\Phi(d).
\tag{5.1}
\]

The crossover is

\[
d_0=\frac{H+9}{24H}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

On `[0,d_0]`, the first term in (5.1) is constant and `Phi` increases with `d`. On the interval where `B_H(d)` is active and nonnegative, `Phi` is a convex quadratic in `d`. After `B_H(d)` becomes nonpositive, `Phi=d^2/p` again increases. Hence on the allowed interval

\[
0\le d\le C(\rho)
\]

its maximum occurs at the crossover `d_0` (when present) or at the endpoint `C(rho)`.

### 5.1 Crossover value

At `d=d_0`, using `p=s/H`,

\[
\Phi(d_0)
=
\frac1{576}
\left[
(27-H)^2+
\frac{(H+9)^2}{Hs}
\right].
\tag{5.3}
\]

Maclaurin's inequality gives

\[
p\le\left(\frac{s}{3}\right)^{3/2}.
\]

Since `H=s/p`, this implies

\[
s\ge\frac{27}{H^2}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
576\Phi(d_0)
\le
(27-H)^2+\frac{H(H+9)^2}{27}.
\]

Subtracting `432` factors exactly as

\[
(27-H)^2+\frac{H(H+9)^2}{27}-432
=
\frac{(H-9)(H^2+54H-891)}{27}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

For

\[
9\le H\le\frac{54}{5},
\]

the second factor in (5.5) is negative. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Phi(d_0)\le\frac34<1.}
\tag{5.6}

### 5.2 Quantitative-circulation endpoint

From (4.2)--(4.3),

\[
p\ge p_-(\rho):=
\frac1{27}-\frac{\rho^2}{6}
-\frac{\rho^3}{3\sqrt6}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

This is positive on the interval (4.7). Since `H>=9`,

\[
B_H(C(\rho))
\le
\frac32-9C(\rho).

Therefore

\[
\Phi(C(\rho))
\le
\left(\frac32-9C(\rho)\right)^2

\frac{C(\rho)^2}{p_-(\rho)}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

It remains to prove that the right side is strictly below `1` for

\[
0\le\rho\le\frac{\sqrt6}{9}.
\]

Set

\[
u=\frac{9\rho}{\sqrt6}\in[0,1].
\]

Define

\[
E:=

-p_-(\rho)
\left[1-\left(\frac32-9C(\rho)\right)^2\right].
\]

Exact symbolic simplification gives

\[
E=-\frac{Q(u)}{104976},
\tag{5.9}

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
