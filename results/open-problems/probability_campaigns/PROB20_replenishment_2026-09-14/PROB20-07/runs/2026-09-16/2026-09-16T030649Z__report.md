# 2. Setup at an arbitrary fixed point

Let `a>0` be any coefficient floor and assume

\[
P_{ij,k}\ge a\qquad\forall i,j,k.
\]

Necessarily `a<=1/3`; write

\[
U:=1-2a.
\]

Every heredity coordinate lies in the interval

\[
a\le P_{ij,k}\le U,
\tag{2.1}
\]

because the other two coordinates in the same stochastic row are at least `a`.

Let

\[
x=(x_1,x_2,x_3)
\]

be a fixed point. Since `V_k(z)>=a` for every simplex point `z`, every fixed-point coordinate obeys

\[
x_k\ge a.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Define the one-parent influence matrix

\[
T_{jk}:=\sum_{i=1}^3 x_i P_{ij,k}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Each row of `T` is stochastic. Parent symmetry gives

\[
xT=V(x)=x,
\tag{2.4}
\]

so `x` is a stationary law for `T`.

The derivative of the QSO is

\[
(DV(x)h)_k
=2\sum_j h_j T_{jk}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus on the tangent plane `sum h_j=0`, the two tangent eigenvalues of `DV(x)` are exactly twice the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T`.

# 3. Symmetric Hoeffding inequality for the one-parent channel

Let `I,J` be iid with law `x`. Given `(I,J)`, let `K` have conditional law

\[
\mathbb P(K=k\mid I=i,J=j)=P_{ij,k}.
\]

Since `x` is fixed, `K` also has law `x`.

Take any real function `g` on the three child states with

\[
\mathbb E_x g=0.
\]

Define

\[
H(I,J):=\mathbb E[g(K)\mid I,J]
       =\sum_k P_{IJ,k}g_k,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and

\[
U_g(J):=\mathbb E[H(I,J)\mid J]
       =\sum_k T_{Jk}g_k.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Because `P_{ij,k}=P_{ji,k}`, the random function `H(I,J)` is symmetric in the iid parents. Its two-variable Hoeffding decomposition is

\[
H(I,J)=U_g(I)+U_g(J)+H_{12}(I,J),
\]

where the three summands are pairwise orthogonal in `L^2(x\otimes x)`. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{Var}(H)
=2\operatorname{Var}_x(U_g)+\operatorname{Var}(H_{12}),
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_x(Tg)
\le\frac12\operatorname{Var}(H).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

This is the same symmetry gain that underlies the earlier Fisher estimates, but here it will be summed over a complete tangent basis instead of bounded one direction at a time.

# 4. Sum over a complete tangent basis

Choose an orthonormal basis `g^(1),g^(2)` of the two-dimensional mean-zero subspace of `L^2(x)`. Thus

\[
\sum_k x_k g^{(r)}_k g^{(s)}_k=\delta_{rs},
\qquad
\sum_k x_k g^{(r)}_k=0.
\]

For any probability vector `p=(p_1,p_2,p_3)`, completeness of this basis gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=1}^2
\left(\sum_k p_k g^{(r)}_k\right)^2
=
\sum_k\frac{p_k^2}{x_k}-1
=:\chi^2(p\|x).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, the kernel of the orthogonal projection onto the mean-zero subspace is

\[
\sum_{r=1}^2 g^{(r)}_k g^{(r)}_\ell
=\frac{\delta_{k\ell}}{x_k}-1.
\]

Summing (3.3) for the two basis functions and using (4.1) yields

\[
\sum_{r=1}^2\operatorname{Var}_x(Tg^{(r)})
\le
\frac12\,
\mathbb E_{I,J}\chi^2(P_{IJ,\bullet}\|x).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The left side is exactly the squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the restriction of the stationary Markov operator `T` to the mean-zero `L^2(x)` plane. If its two nontrivial singular values are `s_1,s_2`, then

\[
\boxed{
s_1^2+s_2^2
\le
\frac12\,
\mathbb E\chi^2(P_{IJ,\bullet}\|x).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

# 5. Sharp scalar second-moment bound from the coefficient floor

For every scalar

\[
z\in[a,U],\qquad U=1-2a,
\]

the graph of the convex function `z^2` lies below the chord through the endpoints. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
z^2\le(a+U)z-aU
=(1-a)z-a(1-2a).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Apply this to `z=P_{IJ,k}` and average over the iid parents. Since `x` is fixed,

\[
\mathbb E P_{IJ,k}=V_k(x)=x_k.
\]

Thus

\[
\mathbb E P_{IJ,k}^2
\le
(1-a)x_k-a(1-2a).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\chi^2(P_{IJ,\bullet}\|x)
&=\sum_k\frac{\mathbb E P_{IJ,k}^2}{x_k}-1\\
&\le
3(1-a)-a(1-2a)\sum_k\frac1{x_k}-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
C_a(x):=
2-3a-a(1-2a)\sum_{k=1}^3\frac1{x_k}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then (4.3) becomes the global arbitrary-fixed-point trace bound

\[
\boxed{
s_1^2+s_2^2\le\frac12 C_a(x).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This estimate uses only the exact QSO symmetry, the fixed-point identity, and the coefficient floor. No barycentric assumption is present.

# 6. New complex-multiplier theorem

Suppose the two tangent eigenvalues of `DV(x)` are nonreal:

\[
\mu,\overline\mu.
\]

Then the two nontrivial eigenvalues of `T` are

\[
\lambda=\mu/2,
\qquad
\overline\lambda=\overline\mu/2.
\]

Because `T` has the trivial eigenvalue `1`,

\[
|\det(T|_{\mathbf 1^\perp})|
=|\lambda|^2
=\frac{|\mu|^2}{4}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For any two-dimensional operator, absolute determinant is bounded by the product of the two singular values, and AM-GM gives

\[
|\lambda|^2
\le s_1s_2
\le\frac{s_1^2+s_2^2}{2}.
\]

Using (5.4),

\[
\frac{|\mu|^2}{4}
\le
\frac14 C_a(x).
\]

Therefore:

## Theorem 6.1 — global harmonic complex-spectrum bound

At every fixed point `x` of every three-type QSO satisfying `P_{ij,k}>=a>0`, any nonreal tangent eigenvalue obeys

\[
\boxed{
|\mu|^2
\le
2-3a-a(1-2a)\sum_{k=1}^3\frac1{x_k}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

In particular, if

\[
2-3a-a(1-2a)\sum_k\frac1{x_k}<1,
\]

then the complex pair is strictly inside the unit circle.

This is a new pointwise arbitrary-fixed-point restriction. It complements, rather than replaces, the earlier determinant/product bound archived in the `181044Z` report.

# 7. Exact endpoint localization

At the conjectural endpoint

\[
a=\frac1{12},
\]

formula (6.2) becomes

\[
\boxed{
|\mu|^2
\le
\frac74-\frac5{72}
\sum_{k=1}^3\frac1{x_k}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore any hypothetical complex instability

\[
|\mu|\ge1
\]

must satisfy

\[
\frac74-\frac5{72}\sum_k\frac1{x_k}\ge1,
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=1}^3\frac1{x_k}\le\frac{54}{5}.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

This is a joint harmonic-mean localization of the only remaining local spectral obstruction.

It immediately yields clean coordinate bounds. Fix one coordinate `t=x_i`. By Cauchy-Schwarz on the other two coordinates,

\[
\frac1{x_j}+\frac1{x_k}
\ge\frac4{x_j+x_k}
=\frac4{1-t}.
\]

Hence (7.2) implies

\[
\frac1t+\frac4{1-t}\le\frac{54}{5}.
\]

After clearing denominators,

\[
54t^2-39t+5\le0,
\]

and the polynomial factors as

\[
54t^2-39t+5=(6t-1)(9t-5).
\]

Thus every coordinate of a hypothetical complex-unstable fixed point must obey

\[
\boxed{
\frac16\le x_i\le\frac59
\qquad(i=1,2,3).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

The earlier determinant/product theorem gives a different and in some directions slightly tighter localization; both conditions are now available simultaneously for the remaining complex chamber.

# 9. Why this does not yet close the complex case

\[

The remaining issue is therefore an annular off-centre chamber: the point is sufficiently balanced to satisfy (7.2), but not necessarily close enough to the barycentre for the earlier elementary entry-cap/Hadamard argument.

6. barycentric complex instability is excluded, and arbitrary complex instability is now constrained simultaneously by the earlier determinant/product condition and the new harmonic inequality (7.2).

Thus the full target remains open. Even complete local attraction of the unique fixed point would not by itself prove global convergence, but it would remove the final local obstruction and make the higher-period/aperiodic part of the problem substantially cleaner.

The highest-value next exact proof obligation is therefore to sharpen Section 5 by retaining the full symmetric two-parent structure instead of only the coordinatewise chord. A promising formulation is the vector Hoeffding identity

\[

=T_{i,\bullet}+T_{j,\bullet}-x+W_{ij,\bullet},

where the interaction term has zero `x`-weighted row means. The floor constraints couple the size of the additive part `T_i+T_j-x` to the necessary interaction. Quantifying that tradeoff may lower the `9/16` Hilbert-Schmidt ceiling below `1/2`, which would prove arbitrary complex local stability at the endpoint.

After that, the task must return to genuinely global dynamics: higher periods, aperiodic recurrence, or an adapted Lyapunov/invariant-region argument. The automation remains open because the original regularity threshold has not been closed.
