## 4. Radial monotonicity of the endpoint Lyapunov function

The key new observation is that `L` is monotone under radial contraction throughout the relevant strip.

Let

\[
y=(U,V),\qquad -\frac14\le V\le\frac12.
\]

For `0<=t<=1`, direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}L(tU,tV)
=t\left[2V^2+U^2\left(2+\frac92tV\right)\right].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

If `V>=0`, the bracket is obviously positive unless `U=V=0`. If `V<0`, then `t<=1` and `V>=-1/4` give

\[
2+\frac92tV
\ge
2+\frac92V
\ge
2-\frac98
=\frac78>0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0\le s<t\le1,\quad y\ne0
\Longrightarrow
L(sy)<L(ty).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
L(\lambda y)\le L(y)
\tag{4.4}
\]

for all `0<=lambda<=1`, with strict inequality for `lambda<1` and `y!=0`.

This already combines with the endpoint theorem to prove global convergence of every homotopy member. A sharper estimate gives an explicit geometric factor.

## 5. Explicit radial contraction factor

Write

\[
A=U^2+V^2,
\qquad
B=\frac32U^2V,
\]

so that

\[
L(U,V)=A+B,
\qquad
L(\lambda U,\lambda V)=\lambda^2A+\lambda^3B.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If `V>=0`, then `B>=0` and

\[
L(\lambda U,\lambda V)
\le
\lambda^2L(U,V).
\tag{5.2}
\]

If `V<0`, put

\[
r=-\frac BA.
\]

Because `V>=-1/4`,

\[
0\le r
=\frac{-\frac32U^2V}{U^2+V^2}
\le -\frac32V
\le\frac38.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{L(\lambda U,\lambda V)}{L(U,V)}
=\lambda^2\frac{1-\lambda r}{1-r}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For `0<=lambda<=1`, the right side is increasing in `r`, so (5.3) gives

\[
\frac{L(\lambda U,\lambda V)}{L(U,V)}
\le
\lambda^2\frac{1-3\lambda/8}{1-3/8}.
\]

Therefore throughout the strip,

\[
\boxed{
L(\lambda U,\lambda V)
\le q(\lambda)L(U,V),
\qquad
q(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^2(8-3\lambda)}5.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

The same bound covers `V>=0` because

\[
q(\lambda)\ge\lambda^2.
\]

Moreover

\[
q'(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda(16-9\lambda)}5>0
\quad(0<\lambda\le1),
\tag{5.6}
\]

and `q(1)=1`. Thus

\[
\boxed{q(\lambda)<1\quad\text{for every }0\le\lambda<1.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

## 7. What this does and does not establish

This theorem is useful because the two rays emanate from precisely the two tensors that earlier rigidity arguments identified as the only weak-endpoint real-unit tangent equality cases. It proves that the most obvious inward deformation—adding uniform barycentric heredity noise—cannot create hidden global recurrence. Instead it converts the endpoint quartic damping into explicit global geometric damping.

It also shows that the lower-bound period-doubling family below `1/12` is genuinely one-sided: on the canonical inward side of the `-1` equality tensor, every trajectory converges globally and geometrically.

However, this is **not** an open-neighborhood theorem in the full heredity-tensor parameter space. A general admissible perturbation changes the direction of the centered nonlinear map rather than merely multiplying it by one scalar `lambda`; the radial argument above no longer applies. Therefore this result cannot be promoted to unrestricted regularity at floor `>1/12`.

The cumulative sharp-threshold status remains

\[
\boxed{\frac1{12}\le\alpha_3\le\frac19}.
\]

Already established at or above the endpoint are: the exact periodic lower obstruction from below, global `1/9` regularity including boundary starts, weak-endpoint fixed-point uniqueness, complete weak-endpoint period-two exclusion, full fixed-point tangent spectral closure, `TV<1/2` localization of every non-singleton weak-endpoint omega-limit set, global regularity of the two real-neutral equality tensors, and now global geometric regularity along their full barycentric inward rays.

The unresolved obstruction is still genuinely global for arbitrary tensors: prime periods `>=3` or aperiodic recurrent sets have not been excluded.

Reusing the endpoint polynomial `L` for arbitrary non-radial perturbations, without shifting/adapting it to the perturbed fixed point and nonlinear terms, is not justified. Therefore no claim of robust neighborhood regularity is made here.
