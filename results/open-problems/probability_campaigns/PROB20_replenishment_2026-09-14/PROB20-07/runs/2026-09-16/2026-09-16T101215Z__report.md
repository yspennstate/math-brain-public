## 3. Exact common normal form

The key observation is that the two maps reduce to the same two-dimensional nonlinear recurrence except for the sign of one coordinate.

### 3.1 `+1` tensor coordinates

For the tensor (2.1), use

\[
u=x_1-x_3,
\qquad
v=x_2-\frac13.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Because `x_1+x_2+x_3=1`,

\[
x_1=\frac13-\frac v2+\frac u2,
\qquad
x_2=\frac13+v,
\qquad
x_3=\frac13-\frac v2-\frac u2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Direct substitution of (3.2) into (2.2)--(2.4) gives the exact recurrence

\[
\boxed{
 u'=u\left(1+\frac{3v}{4}\right)
 =\frac{u(4+3v)}4,
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
 v'=-\frac38\left(u^2-3v^2\right)
 =\frac38(3v^2-u^2).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

At `(u,v)=(0,0)` the linearization is `diag(1,0)`, exhibiting the neutral `+1` direction exactly.

### 3.2 `-1` tensor coordinates

For the tensor (2.6), use instead

\[
u=x_1-x_2,
\qquad
v=x_3-\frac13.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Then

\[
x_1=\frac13-\frac v2+\frac u2,
\qquad
x_2=\frac13-\frac v2-\frac u2,
\qquad
x_3=\frac13+v.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Exact substitution gives

\[
\boxed{
 u'=-u\left(1+\frac{3v}{4}\right)
 =-\frac{u(4+3v)}4,
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

and **the identical second recurrence**

\[
\boxed{
 v'=-\frac38\left(u^2-3v^2\right).
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus both endpoint maps have the unified form

\[
\boxed{
 u'=\sigma\frac{u(4+3v)}4,
 \qquad
 v'=-\frac38(u^2-3v^2),
 \qquad
 \sigma\in\{+1,-1\}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]

The sign `sigma` is the only distinction relevant here.

## 4. Forward image strip, including boundary starts

For **any** QSO with coefficient floor at least `1/12`, every output coordinate satisfies

\[
V_k(x)\ge\frac1{12},
\tag{4.1}
\]

because the parent-pair weights sum to one. Since the three output coordinates sum to one, also

\[
V_k(x)\le1-2\cdot\frac1{12}=\frac56.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore after a single iterate, and forever afterwards,

\[
\frac1{12}\le x_k\le\frac56
\qquad(k=1,2,3).
\tag{4.3}
\]

In either coordinate system above the distinguished coordinate defining `v` is one of the simplex coordinates minus `1/3`. Hence every state after the first iterate obeys

\[
\boxed{-\frac14\le v\le\frac12.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is the only strip restriction needed in the Lyapunov proof. No assumption on the initial point is made, so all boundary trajectories are covered after their first image.

## 5. A common polynomial Lyapunov function

Define

\[
\boxed{
L(u,v)=u^2+\frac32u^2v+v^2
      =\left(1+\frac32v\right)u^2+v^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

On the forward strip (4.4),

\[
1+\frac32v
\ge
1-\frac38
=\frac58>0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
L(u,v)\ge\frac58u^2+v^2,
\tag{5.3}
\]

so `L>=0` there and

\[
L(u,v)=0
\iff
(u,v)=(0,0).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because `L` depends on `u` only through `u^2`, the sign `sigma` in (3.9) disappears from `L(u',v')`. Hence both endpoint tensors have exactly the same one-step Lyapunov decrement.

Exact expansion gives

\[
\boxed{
L(u',v')-L(u,v)=-\frac{N(u,v)}{256},
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
N(u,v)={}&
81u^4v^2+216u^4v+108u^4\\
&-243u^2v^4-648u^2v^3-360u^2v^2\\
&-324v^4+256v^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
\]

It remains to prove

\[
N(u,v)>0
\quad\text{for}\quad
-\frac14\le v\le\frac12,
\quad (u,v)\ne(0,0).
\tag{5.7}
\]

## 6. Exact positivity of the decrement polynomial

Put

\[
r=u^2\ge0.
\]

Then `N` is quadratic in `r`:

\[
N=A(v)r^2+B(v)r+C(v),
\tag{6.1}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{A(v)=27(v+2)(3v+2),}
\tag{6.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{B(v)=-9v^2(27v^2+72v+40),}
\tag{6.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{C(v)=-4v^2(9v-8)(9v+8).}
\tag{6.4}
\]

On `[-1/4,1/2]`, both factors in (6.2) are positive, so

\[
A(v)>0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

The discriminant of the quadratic (6.1) factors exactly as

\[
B(v)^2-4A(v)C(v)=27v^2D(v),
\tag{6.6}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
D(v)={}&2187v^6+11664v^5+25920v^4+27648v^3\\
&+6912v^2-8192v-4096.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

We now certify

\[
D(v)<0
\quad\text{on}\quad
\left[-\frac14,\frac12\right].
\tag{6.8}
\]

Map the interval affinely to `t in [0,1]` by

\[
v=-\frac14+\frac34t.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Express the degree-six polynomial `D(-1/4+3t/4)` in the degree-six Bernstein basis on `[0,1]`. Its seven Bernstein coefficients are exactly

\[
-\frac{8018357}{4096},
\qquad
-\frac{6023643}{2048},
\qquad
-\frac{21208553}{5120},
\tag{6.10}
\]

\[
-\frac{2757115}{512},
\qquad
-\frac{7950153}{1280},
\qquad
-\frac{710171}{128},
\qquad
-\frac{63317}{64}.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Every coefficient is strictly negative. Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative on `[0,1]` and sum to one, so the polynomial is a convex combination of these coefficients at each `t`. Therefore (6.8) holds strictly.

For `v!=0`, equations (6.6)--(6.8) give a **strictly negative discriminant**, while (6.5) gives a positive leading coefficient. Thus

\[
N(u,v)>0
\qquad(v\ne0)
\tag{6.12}
\]

for every real `r=u^2`, in particular for every feasible `r>=0`.

At `v=0`, the polynomial reduces exactly to

\[
N(u,0)=108u^4,
\tag{6.13}
\]

which is positive whenever `u!=0`.

Combining (6.12)--(6.13),

\[
\boxed{
N(u,v)>0
\quad\text{throughout the forward strip except at }(0,0).
}
\tag{6.14}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
L(u',v')<L(u,v)
\quad\text{for every forward-strip state }(u,v)\ne(0,0).
}
\tag{6.15}
\]

This strict inequality is exact and applies to both `sigma=+1` and `sigma=-1` normal forms.

## 7. Global convergence from strict Lyapunov descent

Take an arbitrary initial simplex point, including a vertex or edge point. After one step its adapted coordinates lie in the compact feasible subset of the strip (4.4). Every subsequent iterate remains there.

Let

\[
L_n=L(u_n,v_n).
\]

By (5.3) and (6.15), `L_n` is nonnegative and strictly decreasing unless the orbit is already at the barycentre. Hence it has a limit

\[
L_n\downarrow\ell\ge0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Suppose for contradiction that `ell>0`. Compactness provides an accumulation point `(u_*,v_*)` of the orbit. Continuity of `L` gives

\[
L(u_*,v_*)=\ell>0,
\]

so `(u_*,v_*)!=(0,0)`. By the strict decrement theorem,

\[
\Delta_*:=L(F(u_*,v_*))-L(u_*,v_*)<0,
\tag{7.2}
\]

where `F` is either endpoint normal-form map. Continuity then gives a neighborhood of `(u_*,v_*)` on which the one-step decrement is bounded above by (say) `Delta_*/2<0`. Infinitely many iterates enter that neighborhood, contradicting convergence of the decreasing scalar sequence `L_n` to `ell` (equivalently, contradicting `L_{n+1}-L_n -> 0`).

Therefore

\[
\ell=0.
\tag{7.3}
\]

By (5.3),

\[
\frac58u_n^2+v_n^2\le L_n\to0,
\]

so

\[
(u_n,v_n)\to(0,0).
\tag{7.4}
\]

In either coordinate chart this is exactly

\[
\boxed{
V^n(x)\longrightarrow\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13\right)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\Delta_2.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]

Thus both rigid endpoint real-neutral tensors are globally regular.

## 8. What this says about the `1/12` threshold

The result is especially informative because these two tensors are not arbitrary examples. Earlier exact spectral-rigidity theorems identify them as the **only** weak-endpoint tensors, up to simultaneous relabelling, that can realize real unit tangent multipliers:

- the `+1` tensor is the unique positive-real equality case;
- the `-1` tensor is the unique negative-real equality / period-doubling case.

The new theorem therefore removes a natural feared failure mode at the conjectural threshold: the endpoint itself does not support a nonlinear center, invariant curve, or persistent period-two branch inside either real-neutral normal form.

For the `-1` tensor the connection to the lower-bound family is particularly sharp. For floors strictly below `1/12`, the archived explicit family has a genuine two-cycle and its minimum coefficient tends upward to `1/12`. At the limiting tensor, the two-cycle collapses to the barycentre and the exact Lyapunov decrement has a quartic leading loss along the neutral direction. Indeed at `v=0`,

\[
L(u',v')-L(u,v)
=-\frac{108}{256}u^4
=-\frac{27}{64}u^4.
\tag{8.1}
\]

For the `+1` tensor the same quartic loss occurs despite the opposite linear sign. This is strong evidence that endpoint spectral equality is dynamically tame rather than a source of a new counterexample.

It is **not**, however, a proof that every QSO with coefficient floor `>=1/12` is regular, and it is not a robustness theorem for perturbations of the two normal forms. Nonhyperbolic global asymptotic stability does not automatically persist under arbitrary perturbation. The strict-floor theorem still requires a global mechanism that captures all trajectories for every admissible tensor.

## 11. Next attack

The new Lyapunov function suggests a more precise continuation than another local spectral search.

### Route A: perturbative normal-form barrier

Write a general endpoint-floor tensor near either rigid real-neutral tensor as

\[
P=P^{(\pm)}+E.
\]

In the corresponding adapted coordinates, expand

\[
L(V_P(u,v))-L(u,v)
=
-\frac{N(u,v)}{256}+\mathcal E_E(u,v).
\]

The exact base loss is quartic along the neutral direction and strictly negative elsewhere in the image strip. The next proof obligation is to bound the perturbation error `mathcal E_E` in terms of coefficient distance and exploit the **one-sided coefficient-floor constraints**, rather than arbitrary signed perturbations. If the floor geometry forces the dangerous low-order error terms to have a favorable sign, one may obtain a trapping / basin theorem in neighborhoods of both endpoint equality tensors.

### Route B: compactness away from equality tensors

The cumulative equality-rigidity theorems now classify all real unit-multiplier endpoint cases, while all complex endpoint multipliers are strictly inside the unit disk. A useful compactness strategy is therefore:

1. isolate small parameter neighborhoods of the two real-neutral tensors and control them by a perturbative version of the new `L`;
2. on the compact complement, seek a **uniform local spectral gap** at the unique fixed point;
3. combine that gap with the global `TV<1/2` localization of every non-singleton omega-limit set to prove eventual basin entry.

This still needs a nonlocal bridge; a local spectral gap by itself is insufficient. But after the present theorem, the only loss of a uniform endpoint spectral gap is explicitly understood and globally tame.

### Route C: higher-period finite-amplitude obstruction

Independently, the relative-majorization / stochastic-channel machinery that completely removed period two may be adapted to a hypothetical `n`-cycle by coupling successive unordered parent laws. A successful cycle-level inequality that contradicts the already proved `TV<1/2` diameter localization could remove all finite periods before addressing aperiodic recurrence.

The highest-value next step is Route A plus the compactness decomposition in Route B.
