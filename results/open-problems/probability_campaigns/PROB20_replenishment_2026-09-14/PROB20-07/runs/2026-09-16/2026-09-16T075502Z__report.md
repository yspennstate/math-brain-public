## 4. New diagonal lower bounds

Put

\[
u:=M_{12},\qquad v:=M_{23},\qquad w:=M_{31}.
\]

By (2.7),

\[
M_{21}=u+d,\qquad
M_{32}=v+d,\qquad
M_{13}=w+d.
\]

The reverse-edge caps in (3.1) give

\[
u\le U_{21}-d,\qquad
v\le U_{32}-d,\qquad
w\le U_{13}-d.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Use the row marginals of `M`:

\[
M_{11}=x_1-u-(w+d),
\]

\[
M_{22}=x_2-(u+d)-v,

\[
M_{33}=x_3-w-(v+d).

Substitution of (4.1) yields the three exact lower bounds

\[
\boxed{

\tag{4.2}

\boxed{
M_{33}\ge D_3:=x_3-U_{13}-U_{32}+d.

Independently, the coefficient floor itself gives

\[
M_{jj}=x_jT_{jj}\ge\frac{x_j}{12}.
\tag{4.5}

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{tr}T
=\sum_j\frac{M_{jj}}{x_j}
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^3

\tag{4.6}

\[
\boxed{
L(x,d)

\tag{4.7}

Because

\[
\operatorname{Re}\mu=\operatorname{tr}T-1,

we have

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Re}\mu\ge L(x,d).}
\tag{4.8}
\]

This lower bound is the key new analytic ingredient.

## 5. Convert instability into one explicit scalar inequality

The archived skew estimate is

\[
|\operatorname{Im}\mu|
\le\frac d{\sqrt p}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

We are in the remaining chamber `Re(mu)<0`. If `L(x,d)>=0`, (4.8) already contradicts that chamber. Otherwise `L(x,d)<0`, and

\[
|\operatorname{Re}\mu|
\le -L(x,d).

Hence every remaining candidate obeys

\[
\boxed{

\le
L(x,d)^2+\frac{d^2}{x_1x_2x_3}.

\tag{5.2}

Thus it is enough to prove, throughout the exact necessary domain (2.3)--(2.4), `d<=1/6`, and the three polynomial caps (3.4), that

\tag{5.3}

whenever `L(x,d)<0`.

The max-functions in `L` make a short hand factorization unattractive, but the domain is only three-dimensional after `x_3=1-x_1-x_2`. This is where the exact rational certificate enters.

This substantially changes the best next attack. More fixed-point spectral optimization is no longer needed. The main target should now be a **basin-capture theorem** using the already proved recurrent-set diameter localization.

- obtain an explicit neighborhood `N(x_*)` on which an adapted norm contracts, uniformly under a strict floor `1/12+epsilon`;
