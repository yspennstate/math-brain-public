## Current/later literature check — decisive prior art

### 1. Thom 2013: explicit answer to Question 6.13

Andreas Thom, *The expected degree of minimal spanning forests*, arXiv:1306.0303v1 (3 Jun 2013), later published in *Combinatorica* 36 (2016), 591–600:

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0303
- peer-reviewed journal metadata: https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/the-expected-degree-of-minimal-spanning-forests%2804c5e98f-0698-4b4f-b412-b2e59ce2f324%29.html

Thom's Theorem 1 proves, for a vertex-transitive graph of spectral radius `lambda`,

```text
1/(4 lambda) - 1/4 <= delta_FMSF(G) <= degree(G).
```

His Corollary 2 then proves that for every finitely generated nonamenable group the expected FMSF degree depends on the generating **multi-set** and is unbounded over such multi-sets. Immediately afterward the paper explicitly states that this answers Lyons–Peres–Schramm Question 6.13.

The multi-set wording creates a possible scope question if `Cayley graph` is interpreted as arising from an ordinary symmetric generating set with no repeated generators. A second Thom paper removes that ambiguity completely.

### 2. Thom 2013: ordinary finite symmetric generating sets

Andreas Thom, *A remark about the spectral radius*, arXiv:1306.1767v1 (7 Jun 2013):

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.1767

Theorem 1 proves that for every finitely generated nonamenable group `Gamma` and every `epsilon>0`, there is a **finite symmetric generating set** `S subset Gamma` with spectral radius `rho(S)<epsilon`.

Corollary 9 then states that for every finitely generated nonamenable group the expected degree of `FMSF(Gamma,S)` is unbounded as `S` varies over finite symmetric generating sets; in particular it depends on `S`. The paper explicitly notes just before this application that it is getting around the implicit use of multi-sets.

This is a source-exact YES for ordinary Cayley graphs.

### 3. Short complete deduction specialized to `F_2`

For completeness, the two Thom results also yield an immediate witness-existence proof in the concrete group proposed by the admission record.

Let `Gamma=F_2=<a,b>` and let

```text
S0={a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}}.
```

`Cay(F_2,S0)` is the 4-regular tree. It has no cycles, so no edge can be deleted by the FMSF cycle rule. Hence

```text
delta_FMSF(F_2,S0)=4.
```

By Thom's spectral-radius theorem, choose a finite symmetric generating set `S` of `F_2` with

```text
rho(S) < 1/17.
```

Thom's expected-degree lower bound gives

```text
delta_FMSF(F_2,S)
 >= 1/(4 rho(S)) - 1/4
 > 17/4 - 1/4
 = 4.
```

Therefore

```text
delta_FMSF(F_2,S) != delta_FMSF(F_2,S0),
```

which rigorously answers Question 6.13 YES using two Cayley graphs of the same fixed finitely generated group. No simulation, `p_c/p_u` comparison, or alternative spanning-forest model is involved.

This deduction is not claimed as new: it is already subsumed by Thom's Corollary 9.
