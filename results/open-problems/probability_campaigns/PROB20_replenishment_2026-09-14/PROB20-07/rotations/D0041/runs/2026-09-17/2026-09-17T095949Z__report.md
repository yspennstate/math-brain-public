# B15 — BREAK THE SYMMETRY ON PURPOSE

## Outcome: the previous diagonal proof is inadmissible, but the theorem survives on `M=N+2`

The previous report deliberately chose `M=N` to make every scaling identity exact. That choice violates Mo's standing assumption `M-N>0`. This is a genuine defect, not a cosmetic convention issue.

The minimal repair is to move one allowed direction off the diagonal while keeping the discrepancy much smaller than the soft-edge scale:
\[
N\to\infty\text{ through even integers},\qquad M_N=N+2.
\tag{1}
\]
Then `M_N>N`, `N` is even, and `M_N/N -> 1`, so Mo's hypotheses are satisfied. The price is that the Mo and Bloemendal–Virág coordinates are no longer literally equal at finite `N`; the rest of this section proves that their difference vanishes at the relevant scales.

Put
\[
q_N:=\sqrt{M_N/N}=\sqrt{1+2/N},
\qquad
b_N:=\left(\frac N4\right)^{1/3},
\tag{2}
\]
and for fixed finite real `w` choose Mo's spike exactly by
\[
\tau_N:=1-\frac{w}{b_N}.
\tag{3}
\]
For all sufficiently large `N`, `1+tau_N>0`.

In Bloemendal–Virág notation take
\[
p=N,\qquad n=M_N,\qquad \ell_N=1+\tau_N.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the same Gaussian data matrix and the same population covariance. If
\[
Y_N=\frac1{M_N}XX^T
\]
is Mo's sample covariance and
\[
S_N=XX^T
\]
is the unnormalized Wishart matrix used by Bloemendal–Virág, then
\[
S_N=M_NY_N.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the Bloemendal–Virág scale
\[
a_N:=m_{M_N,N}
=(M_N^{-1/2}+N^{-1/2})^{-2/3}.
\tag{6}
\]
Since `M_N=q_N^2N`,
\[
a_N=N^{1/3}(1+q_N^{-1})^{-2/3},
\]
so
\[
\frac{a_N}{b_N}
=\left(\frac{2}{1+q_N^{-1}}\right)^{2/3}
\longrightarrow1.
\tag{7}
\]

### Spike-coordinate check

Bloemendal–Virág's boundary parameter for (4) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_N^{BV}
&=a_N\left(1-\sqrt{M_N/N}(\ell_N-1)\right)\\
&=a_N(1-q_N\tau_N)\\
&=a_N(1-q_N)+\frac{a_N}{b_N}q_N w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Now
\[
q_N-1=\frac{q_N^2-1}{q_N+1}
=\frac{2}{N(q_N+1)},
\tag{9}
\]
and `a_N=O(N^{1/3})`. Hence
\[
a_N(1-q_N)=O(N^{-2/3})\to0.
\tag{10}
\]
By (7) and `q_N->1`, the second term in (8) tends to `w`. Thus
\[
\boxed{w_N^{BV}\to w.}
\tag{11}
\]
This is the exact check needed by Bloemendal–Virág Theorem 1.1; no sign or `n/p` convention is being guessed.

### Edge-coordinate check

Let
\[
Z_N:=\frac{a_N^2}{\sqrt{M_NN}}
\left(\lambda_{\max}(S_N)-(\sqrt{M_N}+\sqrt N)^2\right).
\tag{12}
\]
Mo's CDF event at coordinate `zeta` is
\[
E_N(\zeta):=
\left\{b_N^2(\lambda_{\max}(Y_N)-4)\le\zeta\right\}.
\tag{13}
\]
Using (5), event (13) is exactly
\[
\{Z_N\le x_N(\zeta)\},
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_N(\zeta)
&=\frac{a_N^2}{\sqrt{M_NN}}
\left[M_N\left(4+\frac{\zeta}{b_N^2}\right)
-(\sqrt{M_N}+\sqrt N)^2\right]\\
&=\frac{a_N^2}{q_N}\left[4q_N^2-(q_N+1)^2\right]
+\frac{a_N^2}{b_N^2}q_N\zeta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The bracket factors exactly:
\[
4q_N^2-(q_N+1)^2
=3q_N^2-2q_N-1
=(q_N-1)(3q_N+1).
\tag{16}
\]
Using (9), the first term in (15) is
\[
\frac{a_N^2}{q_N}
\frac{2(3q_N+1)}{N(q_N+1)}
=O(N^{-1/3})\to0.
\tag{17}
\]
The coefficient of `zeta` tends to one by (7) and `q_N->1`. Therefore
\[
\boxed{x_N(\zeta)\to\zeta.}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus the fact that Mo centers at `4` while the exact finite-`N` Bloemendal–Virág edge is `(sqrt(M_N)+sqrt N)^2/M_N` creates only a vanishing `N^{-1/3}` error in the standardized coordinate on this admissible near-diagonal sequence.

# B4 — PROVE SOMETHING WEAKER, COMPLETELY

## Minimal audit-ready proposition

The direct contour-integrand PDE identity is stronger than needed for the installed target. The following proposition is the shortest complete source-level verification of the **normalized** Mo formula.

### Proposition

Let `G_Mo(zeta,w)` be the normalized limiting distribution represented by Mo's Theorem 2.4 in the `M/N -> 1` critical regime, i.e. the complete right-hand side with the constant chosen as in Mo's theorem so that it equals the limiting probability on the left. Then for every finite real `w` and every real `zeta`,
\[
\boxed{G_{Mo}(\zeta,w)=F_{1,w}(\zeta),}
\tag{19}
\]
where `F_{1,w}` is the Bloemendal–Virág beta=1 critical soft-edge distribution. Consequently `G_Mo` is the unique bounded solution of
\[
\boxed{
\partial_\zeta G_{Mo}
+2\partial_w^2G_{Mo}
+(\zeta-w^2)\partial_wG_{Mo}=0
}
\tag{20}
\]
with
\[
G_{Mo}(\zeta,w)\to1
\quad\text{as }\zeta,w\to+\infty\text{ together},
\tag{21}
\]
\[
G_{Mo}(\zeta,w)\to0
\quad\text{as }w\to-\infty\text{ with }\zeta\text{ bounded above}.
\tag{22}
\]

### Proof

Use the admissible sequence (1), spike (3), and common Gaussian matrix in (4)–(5). Define
\[
P_N(\zeta,w):=\Pr(E_N(\zeta)).
\tag{23}
\]
Mo's Theorem 2.4 applies because `N` is even, `M_N-N=2>0`, `M_N/N->1`, and (3) is exactly Mo's critical coordinate `w=b_N(1-tau_N)`. Hence
\[
P_N(\zeta,w)\longrightarrow G_{Mo}(\zeta,w).
\tag{24}
\]

On the other hand, (11) verifies the spike hypothesis of Bloemendal–Virág Theorem 1.1, so
\[
Z_N\Rightarrow -\Lambda_0
\tag{25}
\]
with beta=1 and boundary parameter `w`. By (14),
\[
P_N(\zeta,w)=\Pr(Z_N\le x_N(\zeta)).
\tag{26}
\]
Equation (18) gives `x_N(zeta)->zeta`. The limiting CDF `F_{1,w}` is continuous; in particular it is represented by the bounded PDE solution of Bloemendal–Virág Theorem 1.7. The standard moving-threshold consequence of weak convergence therefore yields
\[
P_N(\zeta,w)\longrightarrow F_{1,w}(\zeta).
\tag{27}
\]
Comparing (24) and (27), the same sequence of real probabilities has both limits, hence (19).

Bloemendal–Virág Theorem 1.7(ii) states that `F_{beta,w}` is the unique bounded solution of
\[
F_x+\frac{2}{\beta}F_{ww}+(x-w^2)F_w=0
\]
with exactly the boundary conditions (21)–(22). Set `beta=1`, `x=zeta`, and use (19). This gives (20)–(22).

This completes the corrected verification. QED.
