# Main theorem

## Theorem (source-valid verification on an exact diagonal subsequence)

Let `G_Mo(zeta,w)` denote the **normalized limiting distribution** represented by Mo's Theorem 2.4, i.e. the complete right-hand side with the normalization chosen so that it equals the limiting probability on the theorem's left-hand side. Then for every finite real `w` and every real `zeta`,
\[
\boxed{G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)=F_{1,w}(\zeta),}
\]
where `F_{1,w}` is the Bloemendal–Virág beta=1 critical-spike distribution. Consequently `G_Mo` is the unique bounded solution of
\[
\boxed{
\partial_\zeta G_{\rm Mo}
+2\partial_w^2G_{\rm Mo}
+(\zeta-w^2)\partial_wG_{\rm Mo}=0
}
\]
with
\[
G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)\to1
\quad\text{as }\zeta,w\to+\infty\text{ together},
\]
\[
G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)\to0
\quad\text{as }w\to-\infty\text{ with }\zeta\text{ bounded above}.
\]
Thus Virág's Problem 3 is answered affirmatively for the exact normalized beta=1 Mo formula under the source normalization.

## Proof

### Step 1. Put both papers on literally the same finite random matrix

Mo starts with an `N x M` real Gaussian data matrix `X` whose columns have covariance `Sigma`, with all but one eigenvalue of `Sigma` equal to `1` and the remaining eigenvalue equal to
\[
1+\tau.
\]
His sample covariance matrix is
\[
Y=\frac1MXX^T.
\]

Restrict Mo's asymptotic theorem to the perfectly admissible diagonal subsequence
\[
M=N,\qquad N\to\infty\text{ through even integers}.
\]
This obeys Mo's hypotheses `N` even and `M/N -> 1`.

For a fixed finite `w`, define
\[
m_N:=\left(\frac N4\right)^{1/3},
\qquad
\tau_N:=1-\frac{w}{m_N}.
\tag{1}
\]
For all sufficiently large `N`, `tau_N>-1`, so the covariance is positive definite.

Now view the same Gaussian data matrix in Bloemendal–Virág's notation. Their real spiked Wishart matrix is the unnormalized
\[
S=XX^T
\]
for a `p`-variate sample with `n` degrees of freedom and one population spike `ell`. On the diagonal subsequence set
\[
p=n=N,
\qquad
\ell_N=1+\tau_N.
\tag{2}
\]
This is not a distributional analogy: it is literally the same `X`, the same covariance, and
\[
S=NY.
\tag{3}
\]

### Step 2. The spike parameter is exactly the same `w`, not merely asymptotically the same

Bloemendal–Virág define
\[
m_{n,p}=(n^{-1/2}+p^{-1/2})^{-2/3}.
\]
At `n=p=N`,
\[
m_{N,N}
=(2N^{-1/2})^{-2/3}
=\frac{N^{1/3}}{2^{2/3}}
=\left(\frac N4\right)^{1/3}
=m_N.
\tag{4}
\]
Their critical boundary parameter is determined by
\[
m_{n,p}\left(1-\sqrt{n/p}\,(\ell_{n,p}-1)\right)\to w.
\]
Using (1)–(4), on every `N` in the chosen subsequence the expression is already exactly
\[
m_N\bigl(1-(\ell_N-1)\bigr)
=m_N(1-\tau_N)
=w.
\tag{5}
\]
There is therefore no sign, factor-of-two, aspect-ratio, or limiting-coordinate ambiguity in the spike variable.

### Step 3. The soft-edge eigenvalue coordinate is also exactly Mo's `zeta`

Let `lambda_max(S)` be the top eigenvalue of Bloemendal–Virág's unnormalized matrix and `lambda_max(Y)` the top eigenvalue in Mo's normalization. By (3),
\[
\lambda_{\max}(S)=N\lambda_{\max}(Y).
\]
Bloemendal–Virág's Theorem 1.1 scales the top eigenvalue by
\[
\frac{m_{n,p}^2}{\sqrt{np}}
\left(\lambda_{\max}(S)-(\sqrt n+\sqrt p)^2\right).
\]
For `n=p=N`, this becomes exactly
\[
\frac{m_N^2}{N}
\left(N\lambda_{\max}(Y)-4N\right)
=m_N^2(\lambda_{\max}(Y)-4).
\tag{6}
\]
But Mo's Theorem 2.4 defines precisely
\[
\zeta=m_N^2(z-4).
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the event appearing in Mo's limiting CDF,
\[
\left\{m_N^2(\lambda_{\max}(Y)-4)\le\zeta\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
is exactly the event obtained from Bloemendal–Virág's scaled largest eigenvalue at the same finite `N`.

### Step 4. Apply the two convergence theorems to the same CDF sequence

For fixed finite `(zeta,w)`, let
\[
P_N(\zeta,w)
:=\Pr\left(m_N^2(\lambda_{\max}(Y)-4)\le\zeta\right)
\]
with the spike `tau_N` in (1), along even `N` and `M=N`.

Mo's Theorem 2.4, applied to this allowed subsequence, gives
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty,\ N\ \mathrm{even}}P_N(\zeta,w)
=G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w),
\tag{9}
\]
where `G_Mo` is exactly the normalized contour/Painlevé expression printed in that theorem.

Bloemendal–Virág's Theorem 1.1, using the exact identities (5) and (6), gives for the same finite-N random variables
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty,\ N\ \mathrm{even}}P_N(\zeta,w)
=\Pr_{1,w}(-\Lambda_0\le\zeta)
=:F_{1,w}(\zeta).
\tag{10}
\]
A sequence of real numbers cannot have two different limits. Hence (9) and (10) imply
\[
\boxed{G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)=F_{1,w}(\zeta)}
\tag{11}
\]
for every finite real `w` and real `zeta` covered by the distribution-function statement. Bloemendal–Virág's diffusion characterization gives a continuous (indeed PDE-regular) distribution function, so there is no continuity-point ambiguity left in extending the CDF equality over all real `zeta`.

### Step 5. Transfer the PDE and the source boundary conditions

Bloemendal–Virág Theorem 1.7(ii) states that, for general beta, `F_{beta,w}(x)` is the unique bounded solution of
\[
\partial_xF+\frac{2}{\beta}\partial_w^2F+(x-w^2)\partial_wF=0
\tag{12}
\]
with
\[
F(x,w)\to1\quad\text{as }x,w\to+\infty\text{ together},
\tag{13}
\]
\[
F(x,w)\to0\quad\text{as }w\to-\infty\text{ with }x\text{ bounded above}.
\tag{14}
\]
Set `beta=1` and identify `x=zeta`. Equation (12) becomes exactly Virág's PDE (10), and (13)–(14) are exactly the source boundary conditions. Combining with (11) proves the theorem.

QED.

The invariant object is the finite-N probability `P_N(zeta,w)`. This distinction is important:
