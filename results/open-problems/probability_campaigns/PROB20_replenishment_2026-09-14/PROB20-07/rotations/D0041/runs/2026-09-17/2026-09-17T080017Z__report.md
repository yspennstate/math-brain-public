## Notation extracted from Mo's Theorem 2.4

Write the upper-contour form as
\[
F_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)=C\,P(\zeta)\,\operatorname{Im}\int_{\Xi_+}e^{-wT/2}A(\zeta,T)\,dT,
\]
where
\[
P(\zeta)=\sqrt{TW_2(\zeta)},\qquad
A(\zeta,T)=e^{-S(T)/2}I_0(T)^{1/2}Q(\zeta,T)^{1/2},
\]
\[
I_0(T)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_0(u,T)\,du,
\qquad
Q(\zeta,T)=\det\bigl(\delta_{jk}-(\alpha_j,\beta_k)\bigr)_{1\le j,k\le3}.
\]
Mo defines `H_j(u,T)=Ai^(j)(u)(T-u)^(-1/2)`, `S=S(T)` by (2.14), and the 3-by-3 determinant by Section 7. Consequently **all w-dependence in the displayed integrand is in `exp(-wT/2)`**, while all zeta-dependence outside `P` is in `Q`.

Let
\[
\sigma(\zeta)=\int_\zeta^\infty\phi_0(y)^2\,dy.
\]
From Mo's definition of `TW2`,
\[
\frac{P'(\zeta)}{P(\zeta)}=\frac{\sigma(\zeta)}2.
\]

# B5 — ADD A PARAMETER AND DIFFERENTIATE

Define, temporarily suppressing `Im`,
\[
J(\zeta,w)=\int_{\Xi_+}e^{-wT/2}A(\zeta,T)\,dT.
\]
Whenever differentiation under the contour is justified,
\[
J_w=-\frac12\int_{\Xi_+}T e^{-wT/2}A\,dT,
\qquad
J_{ww}=\frac14\int_{\Xi_+}T^2e^{-wT/2}A\,dT.
\]
Therefore, **if C is a genuine numerical constant independent of `(zeta,w)`**, the beta=1 PDE for `P J` is equivalent to
\[
\operatorname{Im}\int_{\Xi_+}e^{-wT/2}
\left[
A_\zeta+\frac\sigma2 A+\frac{T^2-\zeta T+w^2T}{2}A
\right]dT=0. \tag{1}
\]
This is the first exact reduction of this run.

### The normalization obstruction is now explicit

Suppose instead that the untracked prefactor in Mo's theorem is `C=C(w)`. Put
\[
c_1=C'/C,\qquad c_2=C''/C.
\]
After dividing the PDE by `CP`, the extra terms are exactly
\[
4c_1J_w+\bigl(2c_2+(\zeta-w^2)c_1\bigr)J. \tag{2}
\]
If `C` also depended on zeta, there would additionally be `(C_zeta/C)J`.

Thus the “some constant C” issue is not cosmetic: a w-dependent normalization changes the PDE. Mo's finite-N distribution of course has a fixed normalization, but the displayed asymptotic theorem does not, on the page inspected, track enough of that normalization to justify setting `c_1=c_2=0`. This remains a real closure obligation.

# B11 — DUALIZE: move the w^2 term to a T-adjoint operator

The exponential obeys
\[
\partial_T^2 e^{-wT/2}=\frac{w^2}{4}e^{-wT/2}.
\]
Set `f(T)=T A(zeta,T)`. Twice integrating by parts gives, on each smooth contour piece,
\[
\frac{w^2}{2}\int e^{-wT/2}TA\,dT
=2\int e^{-wT/2}\partial_T^2(TA)\,dT
+2\Bigl[f\,\partial_T(e^{-wT/2})-f_Te^{-wT/2}\Bigr]_{\partial\Xi_+}. \tag{3}
\]
The second term is deliberately retained. `Xi_+` has an infinity end and a real-axis meeting point with the lower branch; Mo's Remark 6.4 prescribes upper/lower boundary values there. It is **not legitimate** to silently discard that finite-end/jump term. At infinity the steepest-descent estimates are designed to kill the endpoint; the real-axis matching still has to be proved.

Define the interior adjoint residual
\[
R(\zeta,T)
=A_\zeta+\frac\sigma2A+\frac{T^2-\zeta T}{2}A
+2\partial_T^2(TA). \tag{4}
\]
Then, for constant `C`, Mo's PDE reduces to the exact pair of obligations:

1. the contour interior and real-axis jump satisfy
   \[
   \operatorname{Im}\int_{\Xi_+}e^{-wT/2}R(\zeta,T)\,dT
   +\mathcal B(\zeta,w)=0, \tag{5}
   \]
   where `mathcal B` is the explicit bracket in (3), including the upper/lower matching at the real-axis crossing;
2. differentiation under the contour and endpoint decay are uniform enough for the operations above.

A **sufficient**, substantially stronger route is to prove `R(zeta,T)=0` pointwise on the analytic contour pieces and separately prove `mathcal B=0`. I do **not** claim pointwise vanishing is necessary: contour integrals can vanish by analytic/jump cancellation.

This is the precise analogue of moving Virág's w-differential operator onto the spectral/contour variable.

## Scalar residual identity

Put
\[
\ell(\zeta,T)=\log A(\zeta,T)
=-\frac12S(T)+\frac12\log I_0(T)+\frac12\log Q(\zeta,T).
\]
Because `S` and `I0` are zeta-independent,
\[
\frac{A_\zeta}{A}=\frac12\frac{Q_\zeta}{Q}.
\]
Also
\[
\frac{\partial_T^2(TA)}{A}
=2\ell_T+T(\ell_{TT}+\ell_T^2).
\]
Hence the sufficient interior identity `R=0` is equivalent to the single scalar equation
\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_\zeta}{Q}+\sigma+T^2-\zeta T
+8\ell_T+4T(\ell_{TT}+\ell_T^2)=0.
} \tag{6}
\]
Equation (6), together with the contour-jump cancellation and a proof that `C` is independent of `(zeta,w)`, is now the smallest concrete formula-level target found in D0041.

# B19 — MAKE IT AN ALGORITHM: close the needed derivatives by variational ODEs

A direct differentiation of all Fredholm/resolvent objects is unnecessary. Mo already gives a finite ODE representation.

In (2.15) / Proposition 7.1, for `j=0,...,4`, vectors `v_j(zeta,T)` satisfy
\[
\partial_\zeta v_j=U(\zeta,T)v_j+h_j(\zeta,T) \tag{7}
\]
with explicit boundary data. Every entry of the 3-by-3 matrix defining `Q` is an algebraic expression in these `v_j`, the Airy integrals, `psi=(T-zeta)^{1/2}`, `sigma`, and Wronskians. Section 7 also derives a closed zeta-ODE system for the determinant ingredients.

The missing T-flow can be generated directly, without guessing a new Lax pair. Differentiate (7) in T. Writing
\[
y_j=\partial_Tv_j,\qquad z_j=\partial_T^2v_j,
\]
one obtains the exact linear variational systems
\[
\partial_\zeta y_j=Uy_j+U_Tv_j+(h_j)_T, \tag{8}
\]
\[
\partial_\zeta z_j=Uz_j+2U_Ty_j+U_{TT}v_j+(h_j)_{TT}. \tag{9}
\]
The differentiated boundary data are explicit because
\[
H_j(u,T)=Ai^{(j)}(u)(T-u)^{-1/2},
\]
so, for example,
\[
\partial_TH_j=-\frac{H_j}{2(T-u)},
\qquad
\partial_T^2H_j=\frac{3H_j}{4(T-u)^2}. \tag{10}
\]
The entries of `U` involve only explicit powers of `psi`, Painlevé-II functions of zeta, and `partial_zeta log(phi0/psi)`, so `U_T,U_TT` are explicit as well.

Therefore the determinant derivatives in (6) are finite-dimensional quantities. If
\[
M(\zeta,T)=\bigl(\delta_{jk}-(\alpha_j,\beta_k)\bigr)_{1\le j,k\le3},
\qquad Q=\det M,
\]
then wherever `M` is invertible,
\[
(\log Q)_\zeta=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}M_\zeta),\qquad
(\log Q)_T=\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}M_T), \tag{11}
\]
\[
(\log Q)_{TT}=\operatorname{tr}
\bigl(M^{-1}M_{TT}-M^{-1}M_TM^{-1}M_T\bigr). \tag{12}
\]
`M_zeta` is already controlled by Mo's Section 7 ODEs; (8)-(10) control `M_T,M_TT`.

The remaining non-determinant pieces are equally explicit:
\[
I_0'(T)=-\frac12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{H_0(u,T)}{T-u}\,du,
\qquad
I_0''(T)=\frac34\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{H_0(u,T)}{(T-u)^2}\,du, \tag{13}
\]
and `S_T,S_TT` follow by differentiating Mo's explicit (2.14), `S10`, and `S11` formulas.

**Outcome:** checking the beta=1 PDE has been reduced from differentiating Mo's full Fredholm/Painlevé expression to checking one scalar identity (6) in a finite augmented ODE system `(v_j,y_j,z_j)`, plus the normalization and contour-jump conditions. This is a genuine method reduction, but not yet a proof.

## Failed / blocked routes this run

Repackaging common weak convergence would not satisfy Virág's Problem 3.
2. **Assume C is constant in w because the theorem says “constant”:** not safe enough for source closure. Equation (2) shows exactly why this matters.

5. **Appendix-B Lax pair alone:** promising but not yet connected to the T-derivatives of the 3-by-3 determinant. Equations (8)-(12) give a more direct route using Mo's own ODE system.
