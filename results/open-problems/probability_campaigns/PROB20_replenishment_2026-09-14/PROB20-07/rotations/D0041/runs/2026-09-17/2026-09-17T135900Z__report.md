# 5. B1 — CHANGE THE CATEGORY: the Section-7 `T` deformation is rank-one boundary data, not a second local Lax flow

The recovery instruction was to port the beta=2/4 architecture: start from Mo's finite Section-7 `zeta` system and seek a second `T` or `w` connection whose zero curvature forces `(BLOCK)`. Reading Proposition 7.1 carefully changes the category of this search.

## 5.1 What Proposition 7.1 actually says

For `j=0,1,2`, Mo gives
\[
\partial_\zeta v_j(\zeta,T)=U_{\rm Mo}(\zeta)v_j(\zeta,T)+h_j(\zeta),
\tag{5.1}
\]
with
\[
v_j(\zeta,T)\sim I_j(T)e_4,\qquad \zeta\to+\infty,
\tag{5.2}
\]
where
\[
I_j(T):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_j(u,T)\,du,
\qquad
H_j(u,T)=\frac{\operatorname{Ai}^{(j)}(u)}{(T-u)^{1/2}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
The key structural fact visible in Proposition 7.1 is
\[
\boxed{\partial_T U_{\rm Mo}=0,\qquad \partial_T h_j=0.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
The matrix `U_Mo` is built from the Hastings–McLeod/Airy-resolvent quantities `phi_0, psi, sigma`; the displayed `h_j=(0,0,0,phi_j/psi)^T` depends on `zeta`, not on `T`. Thus `T` enters (5.1) only through boundary data (5.2).

## 5.2 Exact rank-one boundary-data factorization

Work on one analytic `T` component away from the real-axis branch meeting point, and use the same uniqueness of the normalized linear boundary-value solution that underlies Proposition 7.1.

Let `s(zeta)` be the normalized homogeneous solution
\[
s_\zeta=U_{\rm Mo}(\zeta)s,\qquad s(\zeta)\sim e_4\quad(\zeta\to+\infty).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Define
\[
p_j(\zeta,T):=v_j(\zeta,T)-I_j(T)s(\zeta).
\tag{5.6}
\]
Using (5.1), (5.4), and (5.5),
\[
\partial_\zeta p_j
=U_{\rm Mo}v_j+h_j-I_jU_{\rm Mo}s
=U_{\rm Mo}p_j+h_j.
\tag{5.7}
\]
By (5.2) and (5.5),
\[
p_j(\zeta,T)\to0\qquad(\zeta\to+\infty).
\tag{5.8}
\]
Both the equation (5.7) and the boundary normalization (5.8) are independent of `T`. Uniqueness therefore makes `p_j` independent of `T`. Write it simply as `p_j(zeta)`. Hence
\[
\boxed{
v_j(\zeta,T)=p_j(\zeta)+I_j(T)s(\zeta),\qquad j=0,1,2.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]
If
\[
V=(v_0\ v_1\ v_2),\quad P=(p_0\ p_1\ p_2),\quad I=(I_0,I_1,I_2)^T,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{V(\zeta,T)=P(\zeta)+s(\zeta)I(T)^T.}
\tag{5.10}
\]
Thus **all `T` dependence of Mo's three Section-7 inhomogeneous columns is rank one**.

This is not another derivative recurrence. It is an exact factorization of the entire family of boundary-value solutions.

## 5.3 The full-line boundary vector has its own closed `3 x 3` connection

Previous runs already proved the full-line Airy identities. Here I use them only to close the boundary-data deformation.

On the same analytic `T` component, differentiation under the integral and integration by parts give, with vanishing Airy boundary terms,
\[
I_j'(T)=I_{j+1}(T),\qquad j=0,1.
\tag{5.11}
\]
For `j=2`, Airy's equation `Ai''(u)=u Ai(u)` implies `Ai'''=Ai+uAi'`; therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_2'&=I_3
=\int \frac{Ai(u)+uAi'(u)}{(T-u)^{1/2}}\,du\\
&=I_0+T I_1-\int Ai'(u)(T-u)^{1/2}\,du\\
&=I_0+T I_1-\frac12 I_0
=T I_1+\frac12 I_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.12}
\]
Hence the boundary vector obeys the exact companion system
\[
\boxed{
\frac{dI}{dT}=K(T)I,
\qquad
K(T)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac12&T&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{5.13}
\]
This is the finite second flow one gets **for the boundary coefficients**, not for the complete Mo determinant amplitude.

## 5.4 What the attempted Lax port becomes
