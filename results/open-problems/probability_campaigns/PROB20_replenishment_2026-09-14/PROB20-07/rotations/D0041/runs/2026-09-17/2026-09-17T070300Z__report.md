## 4. B2 — attack the tempting shortcut first

The first possible shortcut is: Mo and Bloemendal–Virág prove limits for the same finite real rank-one Wishart model, so uniqueness of weak limits should identify Mo's Painlevé representation with the PDE solution automatically.

This shortcut is **substantially more real than the old intake attempt recognized**, because Mo's own paper explicitly quotes the Bloemendal–Virág theorem before stating his main formula.

Mo defines

\[
Y=\frac1MXX^T,
\]

with covariance eigenvalues all `1` except one `1+tau`, and `M/N -> gamma^2`. In his quoted Bloemendal–Virág theorem the critical coordinate is

\[
w=\left(\frac{N}{(1+\gamma^{-1})^2}\right)^{1/3}(1-\gamma\tau).
\]

For `gamma=1` this becomes exactly

\[
w=(N/4)^{1/3}(1-\tau),
\]

which is **the identical `w` used in Mo's Theorem 2.4**.

The eigenvalue normalizations also agree asymptotically at `gamma=1`: the quoted Bloemendal–Virág scaling becomes

\[
(\lambda_{\max}-4)\,\frac{M^{2/3}}{2^{4/3}},
\]

whereas Mo uses

\[
(\lambda_{\max}-4)(N/4)^{2/3}.
\]

Since `M/N -> 1`, their ratio tends to one. Thus Slutsky's theorem puts the two limit statements on exactly the same `(zeta,w)` scale.

This kills the most obvious possible obstruction: **there is no hidden factor-of-two, aspect-ratio, spike, or edge-scaling mismatch at `gamma=1`.**

## 5. B18 — reformulate the source as an identification theorem

Let `G_Mo(zeta,w)` denote the limiting distribution asserted by Mo's Theorem 2.4 (the long contour/Painlevé expression, including its normalization constant). Let `F_1(zeta,w)` denote the unique bounded Bloemendal–Virág solution of

\[
\partial_\zeta F+2\partial_w^2F+(\zeta-w^2)\partial_wF=0
\]

with the prescribed boundary data.

For any fixed `w`, choose a sequence `tau_N` satisfying

\[
(N/4)^{1/3}(1-\tau_N)\to w,
\qquad M/N\to1.
\]

Mo's Theorem 2.4 says the corresponding scaled largest-eigenvalue CDF converges to `G_Mo(zeta,w)`. The Bloemendal–Virág theorem, quoted by Mo himself with the same critical parameter, says the same CDF converges to `F_1(zeta,w)`. Uniqueness of the limit in distribution therefore yields

\[
\boxed{G_{\rm Mo}(\zeta,w)=F_1(\zeta,w)}
\]

at every continuity point in `zeta`; since the right side is the smooth PDE distribution, the identification extends to all `zeta`.

This is a rigorous **probabilistic bridge** between the two descriptions, conditional only on taking the two published limit theorems at face value. It explains why the source problem is not a model/scaling problem.

## 6. Why `V_Mo` is still 0, not 1

I am **not** closing Problem 3 from the common-limit bridge alone.

Virág's wording is specifically to *check Mo's formulas satisfy the PDE*, and the preceding paragraph explains the beta=2 check by differentiating a fully specified Lax-pair expression. Mo's Theorem 2.4 presents the beta=1 expression with `for some constant C`. The paper passage inspected here does not state in the theorem whether that constant is independent of the deformation coordinate `w`. If an omitted normalization is allowed to depend on `w`, then the displayed expression cannot be differentiated in `w` until that normalization is fixed; a factor `C(w)` contributes extra first/second derivative terms to PDE (10).

Therefore the common-limit argument gives a strong identification of the **normalized limiting distribution**, but it does not yet constitute the source-requested explicit formula-level check unless one of the following is supplied rigorously:

1. prove from Mo's derivation that `C` is independent of `w`, then show the displayed right-hand side is regular enough and use the identification above; or
2. determine `C(w)` explicitly from the probability normalization/boundary condition and verify the resulting normalized expression; or
3. bypass `C` by differentiating the logarithmic derivative/Riemann–Hilbert representation and deriving PDE (10) directly.

So the honest first value is

\[
\boxed{V_{\rm Mo}:\ \mathrm{UNMEASURED}\to0.}
\]

This is genuine narrowing, not a failed reset: the scale/parameter mismatch has been eliminated, and the remaining obstruction is now the **w-dependent normalization / direct differential identity**.

## 7. B22 — what the printed history says the real failure was

Virág explicitly says rank-one perturbation theory was the guide that made the beta=2 Lax-pair check possible. For beta=1, Mo's representation is much more elaborate: a contour integral, Airy integrals, a 4x4 linear ODE system whose coefficients involve the Hastings–McLeod solution, and a square root of a 3x3 determinant. Mo remarks that the formula can alternatively be expressed using a Riemann–Hilbert solution.

That makes the next attack concrete. Do **not** differentiate the full formula naively. First isolate the dependence on `w`: in Theorem 2.4 it enters explicitly through `exp(-wT/2)` and implicitly, if at all, through the normalization `C`. The next useful question is whether the remaining `T,zeta` objects are independent of `w`. If yes, then the entire `w` dependence is a Laplace transform plus normalization, and PDE (10) may be converted into an integration-by-parts identity in `T` rather than a huge Painlevé calculation.
