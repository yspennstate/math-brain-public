## 3. Closure action 1 — source-to-transform derivation stress test

I replayed the exact algebra from the source formula rather than trusting the preceding reduction. The result is a PASS: no missing factor, quadrant sign, or rate reciprocal occurs in the reduction used by the proof.

On the source quadrant `theta in (-pi/2,0)`, put

```text
t=-tan(theta)>0,
phi(t)=t-1/t,
u_r(t)=r H(theta;r).
```

The source has `cot(2 theta)=phi(t)/2` and `sec^2(theta)dtheta=-dt`, hence Theorem 4.1 gives exactly

```text
H(t;r)=exp(-r phi(t)) int_0^t exp(r phi(s)) ds,
u_r'(t)=r[1-(1+t^(-2))u_r(t)].
```

The source density symmetry was also checked against the quadrant orientation. If

```text
J(r)=int_0^infinity [1+(t^(-2)-1)u_r(t)]dt,
K(r)=int_0^infinity [t/(1+t^2)-u_r(t)/t]dt,
```

then one quadrant contributes `C(r)J(r)` to total probability and `C(r)K(r)` to the radial-growth integral. The quarter-turn and half-turn symmetries make all four quadrant contributions equal, so

```text
C(r)=1/(4J(r)),
G(r)=4C(r)K(r)=K(r)/J(r).
```

Now put `x=phi(t)` and

```text
a(x)=t(x)^2/(1+t(x)^2)=(1+x/sqrt(x^2+4))/2.
```

Since `dx/dt=1+t^(-2)`, the ODE becomes `du/dx=r(a-u)` with the correct incoming boundary at `x=-infinity`, yielding

```text
u_r(x)=int_0^infinity exp(-q)a(x-q/r)dq.
```

Pairing `x` with `-x`, using `a(-x)=1-a(x)`, and then setting `q=r delta` gives exactly

```text
K(r)=r L[k](r),
J(r)=r L[j](r),
G(r)=L[k](r)/L[j](r).
```

Thus the transform ratio used by the proof is source-exact. In particular, the common factor `r` cancels and there is no hidden reciprocal-rate substitution in the proof itself.

## 4. Closure action 2 — kernel/Wronskian step, including the non-L1 `w` issue

The proof uses

```text
b(x)=2/(x^2+4)^(3/2),
w(x)=1/sqrt(x^2+4),
k'=b*w,
j''=2(b*b)>0.
```

The only delicate point is that `w` is bounded but not integrable. The monotonicity argument remains valid without pretending `w in L1`.

For `N>0`, define

```text
w_N(x)=w(x) 1_{[-N,N]}(x).
```

Each `w_N` and `b` is nonnegative, even, integrable, and nonincreasing in `|x|`. The standard centered-interval layer-cake/overlap argument therefore makes `b*w_N` even and nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`. Also `w_N(x)` increases pointwise to `w(x)`, and `b*w` is finite because `b in L1` and `w` is bounded. Monotone convergence gives

```text
(b*w_N)(delta) -> (b*w)(delta)=k'(delta),
```

so `k'` itself is nonincreasing. Moreover `b' in L1` and `w` is bounded, so `b*w` is continuously differentiable; hence

```text
k''(delta)<=0  for delta>0
```

pointwise, not merely distributionally.

For

```text
q(delta)=k'(delta)j(delta)-k(delta)j'(delta)=j(delta)^2 h'(delta),
h=k/j,
```

we therefore have

```text
q'=k''j-kj''<0
```

because `j>0`, `k>0` and `j''>0` on `(0,infinity)`. With `q(0)=k'(0)j(0)>0`, positivity of `h` for `delta>0`, and `h(delta)->0`, `q` must become negative and can do so only once. The unique-turn conclusion for `h` is therefore robust to the nonintegrability of `w`.

## 5. Closure action 2 continued — exact Karp–Vishnyakova–Zhang hypotheses and a simpler orientation dependency

The current version of Dmitrii Karp, Anna Vishnyakova and Yi Zhang, *Unimodality Preservation by Ratios of Functional Series and Integral Transforms*, Results in Mathematics 80 (2025), article 112, DOI `10.1007/s00025-025-02431-4` (arXiv:2408.01755), was rechecked.

Their Laplace example identifies `e^{-r delta}` as sign-regular of order three with signature `(+,-,-)`.

```text

A=k,
B=j>0,
weight=1.
```

The hypotheses hold. Compact-uniform convergence follows from `k(delta)=O(log(2+delta))` and `j(delta)=O(delta+log(2+delta))`: on every `r in [r_min,r_max]` with `r_min>0`, a single exponentially decaying majorant controls both transforms.

There is also a useful dependency simplification relative to the previous report: **the proof does not need to rely on the signature product to orient the output pattern.** Theorem 2.6 gives unimodality of `G`. Since `G(r)>0` for every finite `r>0` while both endpoint limits are zero, neither a globally monotone function nor a decreasing-then-increasing valley is possible. Therefore the only possible nonconstant unimodal pattern is increasing then decreasing. Real analyticity rules out a plateau, so the order-unimodality is strict on both branches. The published `(+,-,-)` signature is fully consistent with this orientation, but the endpoint/positivity argument makes the orientation independent of that extra clause.

This removes one avoidable theorem-orientation dependency from the closure proof.

## 6. Closure action 2 continued — strict derivative upgrade rechecked

The multiplicity/sign-matcher argument in the proof report also survives adversarial checking.

At an off-mode point `r0`, set `c=G(r0)`. Because `G` is a strictly monotone analytic function through `r0`, if `G'(r0)=0`, then the first nonzero Taylor term of `G-G(r0)` has odd order at least three. Thus

```text
F_c(r)=L[k-cj](r)=L[j](r)(G(r)-c)
```

has a zero of multiplicity at least three at `r0`, so the exponentially weighted moments of

```text
phi_c(delta)=k(delta)-c j(delta)=j(delta)(h(delta)-c)
```

of degrees `0,1,2` vanish.

Strict one-peak behavior of `h`, plus `0<G(r0)<max h`, gives exactly two zeros `alpha<beta` of `phi_c` and sign pattern `-,+,-`. Therefore

```text
P(delta)=-(delta-alpha)(delta-beta)
```

has the same sign as `phi_c`, making

```text
int_0^infinity P(delta)e^{-r0 delta}phi_c(delta) ddelta > 0.
```

But `P` has degree two, so the three moment equations force the same integral to be zero. Contradiction.

All required moments are finite: `k=O(log delta)` and `j=O(delta+log delta)`, while the exponential weight is `e^{-r0 delta}` with `r0>0`. Hence this is not hiding an integrability assumption.
