# 3. B12 — find what is conserved: a strictly decreasing Wronskian

The key object is the Wronskian-type numerator

```text
q(delta) := k'(delta) j(delta) - k(delta) j'(delta)
          = j(delta)^2 h'(delta).
```

The new point is that `q` is **strictly decreasing on `(0,infinity)`**.

## 3.1 `k'` is decreasing

We have

```text
k' = b*w,
```

where both `b` and `w` are positive, even, and strictly decreasing as functions of `|x|`.

The convolution of two even radially nonincreasing functions on the real line is even and nonincreasing on `[0,infinity)`. This standard one-dimensional rearrangement fact applies here even though `w` is not integrable: truncate `w` by `w_N=min(w,N) 1_{[-N,N]}` (or use the layer-cake representation), apply the interval-overlap proof to each truncation, and pass by monotone/dominated convergence. Concretely, the superlevel sets of `b` and `w` are centered intervals, and the length of the intersection of one centered interval with a translate of another decreases with the translation distance. Integrating those overlap lengths over the two layer parameters gives the claim.

Therefore

```text
k''(delta) <= 0    for delta>0.
```

Smoothness of the explicit kernels makes this pointwise; no distributional qualification is needed.

## 3.2 The Wronskian derivative has a strict sign

Differentiate `q`:

```text
q'(delta)
 = k'' j + k' j' - k' j' - k j''
 = k''(delta) j(delta) - k(delta) j''(delta).
```

For `delta>0`,

```text
k''(delta) <= 0,
j(delta) > 0,
k(delta) > 0,
j''(delta)=2(b*b)(delta) > 0.
```

Hence

```text
q'(delta) < 0                         (1)
```

for every `delta>0`.

At the left endpoint,

```text
q(0) = k'(0) j(0) = (pi/8) pi > 0.  (2)
```

If `q` stayed nonnegative forever, then `h'=q/j^2` would be nonnegative forever and `h`, starting from `h(0)=0`, would be nondecreasing. But the preserved tail asymptotic gives `h(delta)->0` as `delta->infinity`, while `h(delta)>0` for every `delta>0`; that is impossible. Therefore `q` is negative somewhere. Combining this with strict decrease (1) and (2), there is exactly one number

```text
delta_* in (0,infinity)
```

such that

```text
q(delta_*)=0,
q(delta)>0  for 0<delta<delta_*,
q(delta)<0  for delta>delta_*.
```

Since `j>0`, this is exactly

```text
h'(delta)>0  for 0<delta<delta_*,
h'(delta_*)=0,
h'(delta)<0  for delta>delta_*.
```

Thus we have proved, without numerics:

**Lemma 1. `h=k/j` is strictly unimodal and has exactly one critical point.**

This is the middle-regime structure that the first run was missing.

# 4. B13 — find what only increases: transfer the one-turn shape through the Laplace kernel

A 2024/2025 general result supplies exactly the right variation-diminishing bridge.

D. Karp, A. Vishnyakova and Y. Zhang, *Unimodality preservation by ratios of functional series and integral transforms*, arXiv:2408.01755v2; Results in Mathematics 80 (2025), 112, Theorem 2.6, proves that for an `SR_3` kernel, a unimodal input quotient `A/B` produces a unimodal ratio of integral transforms. Their Example 2 states explicitly that the one-sided Laplace kernel

```text
K(r,delta)=exp(-r delta)
```

has sign-regular signature `(+,-,-)` and therefore **preserves** the increasing-then-decreasing pattern when the output is not monotone.

We apply that theorem with

```text
I=J=(0,infinity),
A(delta)=k(delta),
B(delta)=j(delta)>0,
w(delta)=1,
K(r,delta)=exp(-r delta).
```

The hypotheses are met:

1. `h=A/B=k/j` is strictly increasing then strictly decreasing by Lemma 1.
2. `B=j` is positive.
3. `K=e^{-r delta}` is the explicit Laplace `SR_3` kernel with signature `(+,-,-)` identified in Karp–Vishnyakova–Zhang.
4. The two transforms converge uniformly on each compact subset of `r>0` by the kernel-growth estimates above.

Therefore

```text
G(r)=L[k](r)/L[j](r)
```

is unimodal. It cannot be globally monotone: `G(r)>0` for every `r>0`, while the already-proved source endpoint limits are both zero. Consequently it is in the nonmonotone alternative of Theorem 2.6, and because the signature product is positive, it inherits the same pattern as `h`:

```text
G is nondecreasing and then nonincreasing.           (3)
```

The output is real analytic on `(0,infinity)`. It is not constant. Hence (3) is actually strict in the order sense away from the mode: if two distinct points on the increasing branch had equal values, monotonicity would make `G` constant on the interval between them, and real analyticity would force `G` to be constant everywhere, contradicting the endpoint/positivity facts. The same applies to the decreasing branch. Likewise a plateau of maxima is impossible.

Thus there is a **unique global maximizer** `r_*`, with

```text
G(r1)<G(r2) for 0<r1<r2<=r_*,
G(r1)>G(r2) for r_*<=r1<r2<infinity.                (4)
```

At this point the only subtlety left is the source's stronger derivative formulation: a strictly increasing analytic function can in principle have an isolated stationary inflection point (for example `x^3`). B13 alone therefore does not yet prove that `G'` has no extra zeros. B25 below removes exactly that loophole.

# 5. B25 — count the same thing twice: level-set sign changes versus moment multiplicity

This step upgrades strict order monotonicity (4) to the exact source assertion

```text
G'(r)>0 before r_*,   G'(r)<0 after r_*.
```

## 5.1 Every interior level of `G` lies strictly below the unique maximum of `h`

For each `r>0`, define the probability measure

```text
dmu_r(delta)
 = e^{-r delta} j(delta) ddelta / L[j](r).
```

Then

```text
G(r) = integral h(delta) dmu_r(delta).              (5)
```

The density of `mu_r` is strictly positive for every `delta>0`. By Lemma 1, `h` has a unique maximum `h_*:=h(delta_*)`, and `h(delta)<h_*` except at one point. Hence (5) gives the strict bounds

```text
0 < G(r) < h_*                                      (6)
```

for every finite `r>0`.

Fix any `c` with `0<c<h_*`. Since `h(0)=0`, `h(infinity)=0`, and `h` is strictly increasing then strictly decreasing, the function `h-c` has exactly two zeros

```text
alpha(c) < delta_* < beta(c)
```

and has the strict sign pattern

```text
h-c :   negative, positive, negative.               (7)
```

Multiplication by the positive `j` preserves this pattern for

```text
phi_c(delta) := k(delta)-c j(delta) = j(delta)(h(delta)-c).
```

## 5.2 A zero of multiplicity three is impossible

Consider

```text
F_c(r) := L[k-cj](r)
        = L[j](r)(G(r)-c).                           (8)
```

Suppose, for contradiction, that at some `r0 != r_*` we have

```text
G'(r0)=0.
```

Set `c=G(r0)`. By (6), `0<c<h_*`.

By (4), `G` is strictly monotone through `r0`: increasing if `r0<r_*`, decreasing if `r0>r_*`. Because `G` is real analytic and `G'(r0)=0`, the first nonzero term in the Taylor expansion of `G(r)-G(r0)` must then have **odd order at least 3**. (An even leading order would not change sign across `r0`; order 1 is excluded by `G'(r0)=0`.) Therefore `G-c` has a zero of multiplicity at least three at `r0`. Since `L[j](r0)>0`, equation (8) implies the same for `F_c`:

```text
F_c(r0)=F_c'(r0)=F_c''(r0)=0.                       (9)
```

Differentiating under the integral, (9) is precisely

```text
integral_0^infinity delta^m e^{-r0 delta} phi_c(delta) ddelta = 0,
                                                     m=0,1,2.    (10)
```

Now let `alpha=alpha(c)`, `beta=beta(c)` be the two zeros from (7), and define the quadratic

```text
P(delta) := -(delta-alpha)(delta-beta).
```

Its sign is exactly the sign in (7): negative on `(0,alpha)`, positive on `(alpha,beta)`, negative on `(beta,infinity)`. Therefore

```text
P(delta) phi_c(delta) > 0
```

for every `delta>0` except the two zero points. Since the weight `e^{-r0 delta}` is strictly positive,

```text
integral_0^infinity P(delta) e^{-r0 delta} phi_c(delta) ddelta > 0.   (11)
```

But `P` is a polynomial of degree two, so (10) says the very same integral is zero. Contradiction.

Therefore

```text
G'(r) != 0 for every r != r_*.                       (12)
```

From the monotonicity orientation (4), differentiability gives `G'(r)>=0` on the left and `G'(r)<=0` on the right. Combining with (12),

```text
G'(r)>0   for 0<r<r_*,
G'(r)<0   for r>r_*.
```

At the unique global maximum, ordinary differentiability gives `G'(r_*)=0`.

This proves the source's **exact one-critical-point assertion**.

### Why the multiplicity argument is robust

The final argument can be viewed as an elementary strict version of total positivity. The level function `phi_c=k-cj` has exactly two sign changes. If its Laplace transform had an order-three zero, it would be orthogonal (under a positive exponential weight) to every polynomial of degree at most two. But the degree-two polynomial whose roots are the two sign-change locations has exactly the same sign as `phi_c`, making such orthogonality impossible. This is why isolated stationary inflections cannot survive the Laplace transform in this explicit two-sign-change situation.

No finite numerical checker is needed for this theorem-sized step: the closure mechanism is analytic and written on the page.
