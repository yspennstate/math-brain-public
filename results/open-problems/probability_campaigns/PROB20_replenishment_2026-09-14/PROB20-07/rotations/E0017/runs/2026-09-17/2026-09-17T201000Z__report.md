## B18 — say it in another language: exact scalar and Laplace-kernel reductions

The source formulas become substantially simpler after two changes of variables.

### 1. From the angular density to a scalar first-order equation

On the source quadrant `theta in (-pi/2,0)`, set

` t = -tan(theta) in (0,infinity),    phi(t)=t-1/t,    u_r(t)=r H(theta;r).`

Since

`cot(2 theta)=(t-1/t)/2`,  and  `sec^2(theta) dtheta = -dt`,

the source definition of `H` becomes exactly

`H(t;r)=exp(-r phi(t)) integral_0^t exp(r phi(s)) ds`,

hence

`u_r'(t)=r[1-(1+t^{-2})u_r(t)]`,

with endpoint behaviour `u_r(0)=0`, `u_r(infinity)=1`.

The source densities on this quadrant are

`p_0 = C(r)(1+t^2)u_r/t^2`,

`p_1 = C(r)(1+t^2)(1-u_r)`.

The source symmetry `p_i(theta+pi/2)=p_{1-i}(theta)` and `p_i(theta+pi)=p_i(theta)` makes all four quadrant contributions to `G` equal. Normalization and the radial-growth integral therefore give

`J(r)=integral_0^infinity [1+(t^{-2}-1)u_r(t)] dt`,

`K(r)=integral_0^infinity [t/(1+t^2)-u_r(t)/t] dt`,

`C(r)=1/(4J(r))`,

and the exact quotient

**`G(r)=K(r)/J(r)`.**

This already removes the two coupled angular densities from the problem.

### 2. Exponential-moving-average representation

Now set

`x = phi(t)=t-1/t`, so `t(x)=(x+sqrt(x^2+4))/2`,

and define

`a(x)=t(x)^2/(1+t(x)^2)=(1+x/sqrt(x^2+4))/2`.

Because `dx/dt=1+t^{-2}`, the scalar solution can be written

**`u_r(x)=integral_0^infinity exp(-q) a(x-q/r) dq`.**

Here

`a(-x)=1-a(x)`,

`a'(x)=2/(x^2+4)^(3/2)>0`.

Thus, for every fixed finite `x`, `u_r(x)` is strictly increasing in the source jump rate `r`: differentiating under the `q` integral gives

`partial_r u_r(x)=integral_0^infinity exp(-q) (q/r^2) a'(x-q/r) dq >0`.

This monotone-kernel fact was not used in the old attempt.

### 3. Ratio of two Laplace transforms

Pair the `x` and `-x` contributions. Put `s(x)=sqrt(x^2+4)`. Direct substitution gives

`K(r)=integral_0^infinity [1-u_r(x)-u_r(-x)]/s(x) dx`,

`J(r)=integral_0^infinity [1+(x/s(x))(u_r(-x)-u_r(x))] dx`.

Using `a(-y)=1-a(y)` and the moving-average formula, define for `delta>=0`

`k(delta)=integral_0^infinity [a(x+delta)-a(x-delta)]/s(x) dx`,

`j(delta)=integral_0^infinity [1-(x/s(x))(a(x+delta)+a(x-delta)-1)] dx`.

Then, with `q=r delta`, one obtains exactly

`K(r)=r integral_0^infinity exp(-r delta) k(delta) ddelta`,

`J(r)=r integral_0^infinity exp(-r delta) j(delta) ddelta`.

The common factor cancels:

**`G(r) = L[k](r) / L[j](r)`,**

where `L[f](r)=integral_0^infinity exp(-r delta) f(delta) ddelta`.

This is the strongest reduction of the run: the original PDMP question is now a one-parameter ratio of Laplace transforms of two deterministic explicit kernels.

### 4. Structural properties of the kernels

Let

`b(x)=a'(x)=2/(x^2+4)^(3/2)`, `w(x)=1/sqrt(x^2+4)`, and `g(x)=x/sqrt(x^2+4)`.

Differentiating `k` gives

`k'(delta)=integral_0^infinity [b(x+delta)+b(x-delta)]w(x) dx`.

Because `b,w` are even, this is the full convolution `(b*w)(delta)`, hence

**`k'(delta)>0` for every `delta>=0`, and `k(0)=0`.**

Likewise

`j'(delta)=integral_0^infinity g(x)[b(x-delta)-b(x+delta)] dx = (g*b)(delta)`.

Since `g'(x)=4/(x^2+4)^(3/2)=2b(x)`, convolution differentiation gives

**`j''(delta)=2(b*b)(delta)>0`.**

Moreover `j(0)=pi` and `j'(0)=0`. Therefore `j` is strictly convex and strictly increasing for `delta>0`.

The source shape problem has consequently been reduced to a ratio of Laplace transforms of a strictly increasing kernel `k` and a strictly convex increasing kernel `j`, both built from the same completely explicit Cauchy-type kernel `b`.

### Low-rate flank: reduced to elementary kernel tails

The explicit formula for `a` gives, for `y>0`,

`0 < 1-a(y) <= min(1/2,1/y^2)`,

and by symmetry the same bound controls `a(y)` for `y<0`.

Consequently `a(x+delta)-a(x-delta)` differs from the indicator `1_{0<x<delta}` only in an integrable transition layer, which yields

`k(delta)=log(delta)+O(1)` as `delta->infinity`.

Similarly the integrand defining `j(delta)` differs from the step profile `1_{0<x<delta}` by integrable tails, giving

`j(delta)=delta+O(log delta)`.

Laplace scaling then gives

`L[k](r)=(1/r)[log(1/r)+O(1)]`,

`L[j](r)=1/r^2 + O((log(1/r))/r)`,

and hence

**`G(r)=r log(1/r)+O(r)` as `r->0+`.**

Differentiating the same Laplace estimates gives `G'(r)=log(1/r)+O(1)`, so **`G'(r)>0` for all sufficiently small `r`**.

Therefore both asymptotic derivative signs required by the conjecture are now structurally accounted for. The only unresolved mathematical obligation is the number of sign changes of `G'` on a compact middle interval.
