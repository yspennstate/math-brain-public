## 3. Mathematical handoff: the source-level positive proof candidate

The strongest D0007 result is the fixed-point-free proof in the preceding report. The audit-ready skeleton is as follows.

Let `F` be any absolutely continuous probability law on `[a,b]`, with mean `mu` and `B=b-a`.

### 3.1 Source-dominated bounded control density

Extract a bounded, nondegenerate probability density `h_0` with the same mean `mu` and

\[
 h_0\le C_0 f
\quad\text{a.e.}
\]

by taking positive-density pieces strictly below and above `mu` and mixing them to restore the barycenter.

### 3.2 One fixed correction scale

For `p_j=h_0^{*j}`, the centered characteristic function is in `L^2`, has modulus strictly below one away from zero, and has the finite-variance quadratic expansion at zero. Fourier inversion gives the fixed-window local limit

\[
\sup_{|t|\le B}
\left|
\sqrt{k}\,p_k(k\mu+t)-\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right|\to0.
\]

Thus for some fixed `k`,

\[
\delta_k:=\min_{|t|\le B}p_k(k\mu+t)>0,
\qquad
M_{k-1}:=\|p_{k-1}\|_\infty<\infty.
\]

Conditioning `k` iid `h_0` variables on any correction sum `s` in that window gives a control marginal `H_s` with

\[
 H_s\le C_kF,
\qquad
C_k=\frac{C_0M_{k-1}}{\delta_k}.
\]

### 3.3 Dimension-free residual error

For any residual law `G` of mean `mu`, let `m=n-k`, let `U` be uniform on `[0,1/m)`, and set

\[
Y_j=Q_G(U+j/m),\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.
\]

The average marginal of these `m` coordinates is exactly `G`, while their total `R_G` obeys

\[
\boxed{|R_G-m\mu|\le B\quad\text{a.s.}}
\]

because the monotone stratified quantile sum has essential oscillation at most one support diameter. Therefore the fixed `k`-coordinate control block can correct the residual total exactly.

### 3.4 Dual residual argument, with no fixed point

Let

\[
\mathcal C_{n,\mu}
=
\left\{
\text{average marginals of probability laws supported on }
\{x\in[a,b]^n:\sum_i x_i=n\mu\}
\right\}.
\]

This is compact and convex. A law is `n`-CM iff it lies in `\mathcal C_{n,\mu}`; uniform coordinate permutation converts an average marginal into identical coordinate marginals.

Define

\[
\mathcal D_n=\left\{G:\ E_GX=\mu,\ G\le\frac n{n-k}F\right\}.
\]

For each `G in D_n`, the residual-plus-control construction gives an exact-sum vector with average marginal

\[
M(G)=\frac{n-k}{n}G+\frac{k}{n}H_G\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}.
\]

For `n>=kC_k`, the algebraic residual update

\[
T(G)=\frac{nF-kH_G}{n-k}
\]

also lies in `D_n`.

Fix any continuous test function `phi` and choose `G_phi` maximizing `int phi dG` over compact `D_n`. Since `T(G_phi)` is admissible,

\[
\int\phi\,dG_\phi\ge\int\phi\,dT(G_\phi).
\]

Rearranging gives

\[
\int\phi\,dM(G_\phi)\ge\int\phi\,dF.
\]

Thus for every continuous `phi`, the support function of `C_{n,mu}` dominates `F`. Hahn–Banach separation then forces

\[
F\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}.
\]

Hence `F` is `n`-CM for every

\[
 n\ge N(F):=\max\{k+1,\lceil kC_k\rceil\}.
\]

This proves the source statement **if the argument survives independent audit**.

## 4. B10 — derandomize: exact finite support is impossible for a non-atomic target

The first final-route question was whether the dual proof could be reduced to a finite convex certificate, eliminating all infinite-dimensional compactness.

It cannot, for a simple exact reason.

### Proposition 4.1 — no finite deterministic-configuration representation of a non-atomic marginal

Let `F` be a non-atomic probability law and fix `n`. Suppose

\[
\Pi=\sum_{r=1}^R\lambda_r\delta_{x^{(r)}}
\]

is a probability law on the constant-sum configuration set with only finitely many deterministic configurations. Its average marginal is

\[
A(\Pi)
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{r=1}^R
\lambda_r\delta_{x_i^{(r)}}.
\]

This measure is supported on at most `nR` points. Therefore

\[
A(\Pi)\ne F
\]

for every non-atomic `F`.

So an exact representation of an absolutely continuous `F` inside `C_{n,mu}` necessarily uses an infinite/non-atomic probability measure on configuration space. There is no finite Carathéodory-style exact certificate in the full measure space.

**Outcome B10:** useful negative result. It does **not** damage the proof. It explains why weak compactness/separation is the natural exact language and why a finite deterministic configuration list cannot replace it for a continuous target law.
