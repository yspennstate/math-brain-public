# 3. Mathematical idea before validation

The previous run's defect-extraction lemma suggests changing the quantifiers. To make a large odd complete mix of a symmetric law `F`, it is unnecessary to make all `n` coordinates from one complicated odd construction. It is enough to extract a **small odd complete-mix submeasure** `H` from `F`; all remaining marginal mass stays symmetric and can be consumed by ordinary 2-coordinate pairs.

For an absolutely continuous symmetric law, a positive-density level set has positive Lebesgue measure. A tiny multiplicative dilation of that level set overlaps itself. That overlap supplies two nearby magnitudes `t` and `((r+1)/r)t` in exactly the ratio needed for a deterministic zero-sum vector with `r+1` positive copies and `r` negative copies. This produces an odd-CM sublaw of `F` at positive weight. Once its weight is diluted by the factor `m/n`, it fits inside `F` for every sufficiently large odd `n`.

This is B3 (prove a stronger source-relevant theorem), B6 (deform the shell ratio to a dilation arbitrarily close to one), and B25 (count the same marginal mass once as one odd block and once as symmetric pairs).

# 4. Imported lemma with hypotheses rechecked: defect extraction

I use the preceding run's lemma, but restate the complete argument because it is the only imported mechanism needed below.

**Lemma 4.1 (defect extraction).** Let `F` be a probability law symmetric about zero. Let odd integers `n>=m`. Suppose `H` is a symmetric `m`-CM law with center zero and

\[
\frac mn H\le F
\]

as measures. Then `F` is `n`-CM.

**Proof.** Define

\[
G:=\frac{F-(m/n)H}{1-m/n}.
\]

The measure domination makes `G` a probability law, and symmetry of `F,H` makes `G` symmetric. Take one `m`-complete mix of `H`, whose sum is zero. Since `n-m` is even, take `(n-m)/2` pairs `(Y,-Y)` with `Y\sim G`. Concatenating the odd block and all pairs gives `n` coordinates with deterministic sum zero. Finally apply an independent uniformly random permutation of the `n` coordinates. Each output coordinate then has marginal

\[
\frac mnH+\frac{n-m}{n}G=F.
\]

Hence `F` is `n`-CM. `QED`

# 5. B3/B6: eventual complete mixability of every symmetric absolutely continuous law

## Theorem 5.1

Let `F` be any absolutely continuous probability distribution on `R` symmetric about some point `mu`. Then there exists an integer `N(F)` such that

\[
\boxed{F\text{ is }n\text{-completely mixable for every integer }n\ge N(F).}
\]

In particular, bounded support is not needed for this symmetric subclass.

## Proof

Complete mixability is translation invariant, so translate by `-mu` and assume the center of symmetry is zero. Let `f` be a symmetric density of `F`.

### Step 1: find a positive-density level set away from zero

Absolute continuity and symmetry imply

\[
\int_0^\infty f(x)\,dx=\frac12.
\]

Hence there exist finite `0<a<b<infinity` for which

\[
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx>0.
\]

Therefore for some `eta>0` the measurable set

\[
S:=\{x\in[a,b]: f(x)\ge\eta\}
\]

has positive finite Lebesgue measure, denoted `|S|>0`.

### Step 2: a small rational dilation of `S` overlaps `S`

For `c>0`, write

\[
c^{-1}S:=\{x:cx\in S\}.
\]

I use the standard strong continuity of dilations in `L^1(R)`:

\[
\mathbf 1_S(c\,\cdot)\longrightarrow \mathbf 1_S
\quad\text{in }L^1(R)\qquad(c\to1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

For completeness, (5.1) follows by approximating the integrable indicator `1_S` in `L^1` by a compactly supported continuous function `phi`; for such `phi`, `phi(c x)->phi(x)` uniformly on one common compact support as `c->1`, while the dilation operator has `L^1` norm factor `1/c`, so the approximation errors remain controlled. Thus

\[
|S\triangle c^{-1}S|\to0,
\]

and consequently

\[
|S\cap c^{-1}S|\to |S|>0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Choose an integer `r>=1` sufficiently large that

\[
c:=\frac{r+1}{r}=1+\frac1r
\]

satisfies

\[
G:=S\cap c^{-1}S,
\qquad |G|>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

By construction,

\[
G\subset S,
\qquad cG\subset S.
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Step 3: build one explicit odd complete-mix law from `G`

Set

\[
m:=2r+1.
\]

Let `T` have the uniform probability density on the measurable set `G`:

\[
k(t)=\frac{\mathbf 1_G(t)}{|G|}.
\]

Conditional on `T=t`, start with the deterministic `m`-vector

\[
(\underbrace{t,\ldots,t}_{r+1\text{ copies}},
 \underbrace{-ct,\ldots,-ct}_{r\text{ copies}}).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Its sum is exactly zero because

\[
(r+1)t-rct=(r+1)t-r\frac{r+1}{r}t=0.
\]

Independently flip the sign of the whole vector with probability `1/2`, then uniformly randomly permute all `m` coordinates. The resulting vector still sums to zero and all its coordinates have the same symmetric law `H`. Therefore `H` is `m`-CM.

For `x>0`, its density is

\[
h(x)=
\frac{r+1}{2m|G|}\mathbf 1_G(x)
+
\frac{r}{2mc|G|}\mathbf 1_{cG}(x),
\tag{5.6}
\]

and `h(-x)=h(x)`. The second coefficient contains the Jacobian `1/c` from the law of `cT`.

Even if `G` and `cG` overlap, (5.6) gives the pointwise bound

\[
h(x)\le
\frac1{|G|}
\left(
\frac{r+1}{2m}+
\frac{r}{2mc}
\right).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Because `c=(r+1)/r`,

\[
(r+1)+\frac rc
=(r+1)+\frac{r^2}{r+1}
<2r+1=m;
\]
indeed after multiplication by `r+1`, the right-minus-left numerator is exactly `r>0`. Hence

\[
\boxed{h(x)<\frac1{2|G|}}
\quad\text{for a.e. }x.
\tag{5.8}
\]

The support of the positive half of `H` lies in `G\cup cG`, and (5.4) says this is contained in `S`; symmetry gives the same conclusion on the negative half. Thus on the support of `H`,

\[
f(x)\ge\eta.
\tag{5.9}
\]

### Step 4: dilute the odd block into `F`

Let `n>=m` be odd and also satisfy

\[
n\ge \frac{m}{2\eta|G|}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Then (5.8)--(5.9) imply on the support of `H`

\[
\frac mn h(x)
\le
\frac{m}{2n|G|}
\le \eta
\le f(x).
\]

Outside the support of `H` the left side is zero. Therefore

\[
\frac mn H\le F.
\]

Lemma 4.1 now proves that `F` is `n`-CM for every odd `n` satisfying (5.10).

Finally, because `F` is symmetric, every even `n` is `n`-CM by concatenating `n/2` pairs `(X,-X)`. Thus all sufficiently large integers are covered. Translating back restores center `mu` and constant sum `n mu`. `QED`

## What is stronger than the source-known sufficient theorem

Wang's source immediately before Open Problem 7 recalls a theorem requiring a density on the whole bounded interval to be bounded away from zero. Theorem 5.1 does **not** require a density lower bound on the support, connected support, unimodality, or even bounded support. It uses only absolute continuity plus symmetry.

It remains only a restricted subclass result relative to Open Problem 7, because an arbitrary absolutely continuous law need not be symmetric.

# 6. B25: exact block-count interpretation

The proof exposes the old `D0007-A001` common-center obstruction in a different way. The special odd law `H` and the residual symmetric law `G` do **not** have to be separately mixed in the same dimension and do not need a common componentwise decomposition. Instead:

- exactly `m` coordinates are consumed by one `m`-CM block of `H`;
- exactly `n-m` coordinates are consumed by symmetric 2-blocks of `G`;
- a uniform permutation makes each coordinate see the mixture weights `m/n` and `(n-m)/n`.

The total center automatically matches because every block in the symmetric proof has center zero. This is the count-twice mechanism of B25 and is the reason a positive amount of odd-CM submeasure suffices asymptotically.

# 7. Consequence for the claimed Yin--Zhu counterexample

The current arXiv version `1706.05499v4` (11 May 2023), Yin and Zhu, *Joint mixability of elliptical distributions and related families*, says in its abstract that Wang's open problem is solved. Example 2.4 defines

\[
f_m(x)=C_m\frac{x^{2m}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\mathbf 1_{(-1,1)}(x),
\qquad m\ge0,
\]

and then, for arbitrary positive weights `alpha_m` with `sum_{m>=1} alpha_m=1`, defines the fixed density

\[
f(x)=\sum_{m=1}^\infty \alpha_m f_m(x).
\tag{7.1}
\]

The text then states that this fixed distribution is not `(2n+1)`-CM for any `n>=1` and presents it as a counterexample to Wang's Open Problem 7.

Theorem 5.1 applies immediately to (7.1): it is an absolutely continuous symmetric probability law. Therefore it is `n`-CM for every sufficiently large `n`. Thus the current Example 2.4 all-odd nonmixability assertion cannot be correct.

The following explicit quantitative corollary removes any dependence on the abstract dilation step.

## Corollary 7.1: explicit eventual-CM bound for every Yin--Zhu mixture

Because every `alpha_m` is positive, `alpha_1>0`. For `m=1`, normalization is exact:

\[
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=\frac\pi2,
\qquad C_1=\frac2\pi.
\]

Choose

\[
a=\frac14,
\qquad
\delta=\frac1{16}.
\]

Use the deterministic zero-sum triple `(a,a,-2a)`, its global sign flip and permutation, then convolve coordinatewise with an independent 3-complete mix of the uniform law on `[-delta,delta]`. This gives a symmetric 3-CM law `H` whose density is

\[
\frac1{6\delta}
\]

on each interval around `+/-a`, and

\[
\frac1{12\delta}
\]

on each interval around `+/-2a`. The four intervals are disjoint.

The smallest positive absolute value appearing is

\[
a-\delta=\frac3{16}.
\]

The function `x^2/sqrt(1-x^2)` is increasing for `x in (0,1)`, so on all four shell intervals the target mixture density obeys

\[
f(x)\ge\alpha_1 f_1(x)
\ge
\alpha_1\frac2\pi
\frac{(3/16)^2}{\sqrt{1-(3/16)^2}}
=
\frac{9\alpha_1}{8\pi\sqrt{247}}
=:c.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The largest density of `H` is `1/(6 delta)`. Therefore

\[
\frac3n h(x)\le f(x)
\]

whenever

\[
\frac{3}{n}\frac1{6\delta}\le c,
\]
that is whenever

\[
\boxed{
n\ge\frac{64\pi\sqrt{247}}{9\alpha_1}.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

For every **odd** integer `n` satisfying (7.3), Lemma 4.1 proves that the mixture (7.1) is `n`-CM. Every even dimension is already CM by symmetry. Hence the fixed Yin--Zhu mixture is eventually CM for every choice of their positive weights.

This is a direct contradiction of the sentence in current Example 2.4 asserting non-CM for every odd dimension. It does **not** solve Wang Open Problem 7, because the source asks about arbitrary absolutely continuous distributions, including asymmetric ones.
