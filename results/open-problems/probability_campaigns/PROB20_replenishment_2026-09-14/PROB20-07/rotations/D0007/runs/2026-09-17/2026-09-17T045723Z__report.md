## 4. Mathematical idea before bookkeeping

The previous proof used a continuous self-map

\[
T_n(G)=\frac{nF-kH_G}{m},\qquad m=n-k,
\]

on a compact convex residual-law domain and invoked Schauder. The vulnerable-looking part was therefore the topology and continuity of the nonlinear map `G -> H_G`.

The new idea is to **dualize the entire existence problem**.

For a fixed dimension `n`, the set of all marginals that admit an exact constant-sum `n`-coupling with center `mu` is a compact convex subset of probability measures. By Hahn–Banach, to prove that `F` belongs to that set it is enough to show that no continuous linear functional separates `F` from it.

For one test function `phi`, choose a residual law `G_phi` maximizing `int phi dG` over the compact residual domain. The algebraic residual update `T_n(G_phi)` is another admissible residual law. Maximality gives

\[
\int\phi\,dG_\phi\ge \int\phi\,dT_n(G_\phi).
\]

After rearrangement this is **exactly** the desired support-function inequality saying that the complete-mixable marginal produced from `G_phi` has `phi`-value at least the `phi`-value of `F`. Doing this separately for every `phi` rules out separation and forces `F` itself to be `n`-CM.

The key point is that **no fixed point and no continuity of `T_n` are needed**.

## 5. B11 — dual characterization of `n`-complete mixability

Fix a compact interval `[a,b]`, an integer `n>=2`, and a center `mu in [a,b]`. Define the compact configuration set

\[
K_{n,\mu}:=\left\{x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in[a,b]^n:\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i=n\mu\right\}.
\]

For a probability measure `Pi` on `K_{n,mu}`, define its average marginal

\[
A(\Pi):=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n (\operatorname{pr}_i)_\#\Pi.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal C_{n,\mu}:=\{A(\Pi):\Pi\in\mathcal P(K_{n,\mu})\}.
\]

### Lemma 5.1 — configuration-space characterization

A probability law `F` on `[a,b]` with mean `mu` is `n`-completely mixable if and only if

\[
F\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}.
\]

**Proof.** If `F` is `n`-CM, take the joint law `Pi` of a complete mix. It is supported on `K_{n,mu}` and all its coordinate marginals are `F`, so `A(Pi)=F`.

Conversely, suppose `A(Pi)=F`. Sample `x` from `Pi`, then independently sample a uniformly random permutation `sigma` of `{1,...,n}` and output

\[
X_i=x_{\sigma(i)}.
\]

The sum stays `n mu`. For each fixed output position, random permutation averages the pre-permutation coordinate marginals, hence the marginal is exactly `A(Pi)=F`. Therefore `F` is `n`-CM. `QED`

### Lemma 5.2 — compact convexity and exact dual criterion

The set `C_{n,mu}` is compact and convex in the weak topology, and

\[
\boxed{
F\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}
\iff
\int\phi\,dF
\le
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}}\int\phi\,d\nu
\quad\text{for every }\phi\in C([a,b]).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, since a linear functional reaches its supremum over `C_{n,mu}` on a deterministic configuration,

\[
\boxed{
F\text{ is }n\text{-CM}
\iff
\int\phi\,dF
\le
\max_{x\in K_{n,\mu}}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\phi(x_i)
\quad\forall\phi\in C([a,b]).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

**Proof.** `K_{n,mu}` is compact. Hence `P(K_{n,mu})` is compact in the weak topology, and `A` is continuous affine. Its image `C_{n,mu}` is therefore compact and convex. If `F` is outside this compact convex set, strict Hahn–Banach separation in the locally convex weak measure space gives a continuous linear functional separating `F`; such functionals are integrations against continuous functions `phi`. This is precisely the negation of (5.1). Conversely, (5.1) rules out every separating functional, hence `F` belongs to the set. Formula (5.2) follows because

\[
\int\phi\,dA(\Pi)
=
\int_{K_{n,\mu}}\left(\frac1n\sum_i\phi(x_i)\right)\Pi(dx),
\]

whose supremum over probability measures on the compact configuration set is the maximum of the integrand. `QED`

This dual criterion is useful beyond this run: it says complete mixability is exactly a support-function membership problem for the compact convex hull of constant-sum empirical measures.

## 6. B4 + B30 — a fixed-`n`, fixed-`k` corrector theorem

The previous report already proved two source-general ingredients. I reuse them rather than restart their derivations.

Let `F` be absolutely continuous on `[a,b]` with density `f`, mean `mu`, and `B=b-a`. Suppose there is a bounded probability density `h_0` with the same mean `mu` and

\[
 h_0\le C_0 f
\quad\text{a.e.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

For an integer `k>=3`, let `p_j=h_0^{*j}` and assume

\[
\delta_k:=\min_{|t|\le B}p_k(k\mu+t)>0,
\qquad
M_{k-1}:=\|p_{k-1}\|_\infty<\infty.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Set

\[
C_k:=\frac{C_0M_{k-1}}{\delta_k}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — finite-`n` dual corrector criterion

If

\[
\boxed{n>k\quad\text{and}\quad n\ge kC_k,}
\tag{6.4}
\]

then `F` is `n`-completely mixable.

**Proof.** Put `m=n-k` and define the compact convex residual-law domain

\[
\mathcal D_n
=
\left\{
G:\ G([a,b])=1,\ \int x\,G(dx)=\mu,\ G\le\frac nmF
\right\}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

It is nonempty because `F in D_n`.

For every `G in D_n`, reuse the exact construction already proved in the prior report:

1. stratified quantiles produce `m` residual coordinates with **average marginal exactly `G`** and total sum `R_G` satisfying

\[
|R_G-m\mu|\le B\quad\text{a.s.;}
\tag{6.6}
\]

2. the required correction sum

\[
S_G=n\mu-R_G
\]

therefore lies in the fixed interval `[k mu-B,k mu+B]`;

3. conditioning `k` iid `h_0` variables on having sum `S_G` gives a control block whose average marginal `H_G` has mean `mu` and satisfies the uniform domination

\[
H_G\le C_kF;
\tag{6.7}
\]

4. concatenating the two blocks gives an exact-sum `n`-vector with average marginal

\[
M(G)=\frac mnG+\frac knH_G.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Thus `M(G) in C_{n,mu}` by Lemma 5.1.

Because of (6.4) and (6.7), the algebraic residual update

\[
T(G):=\frac{nF-kH_G}{m}
\tag{6.9}
\]

is a nonnegative probability measure with mean `mu` and

\[
T(G)\le\frac nmF.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{T(G)\in\mathcal D_n\quad\text{for every }G\in\mathcal D_n.}
\tag{6.10}
\]

Now fix an arbitrary `phi in C([a,b])`. Compactness of `D_n` gives a maximizer

\[
G_\phi\in\operatorname*{argmax}_{G\in\mathcal D_n}\int\phi\,dG.
\tag{6.11}
\]

Since `T(G_phi) in D_n`, maximality gives

\[
\int\phi\,dG_\phi
\ge
\int\phi\,dT(G_\phi)
=
\frac{n\int\phi\,dF-k\int\phi\,dH_{G_\phi}}{m}.
\tag{6.12}
\]

Rearrange (6.12):

\[
\frac mn\int\phi\,dG_\phi
+
\frac kn\int\phi\,dH_{G_\phi}
\ge
\int\phi\,dF.
\tag{6.13}
\]

The left side is exactly

\[
\int\phi\,dM(G_\phi),
\]

and `M(G_phi) in C_{n,mu}`. Therefore, for every continuous `phi`,

\[
\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal C_{n,\mu}}\int\phi\,d\nu
\ge
\int\phi\,dF.
\tag{6.14}
\]

By the exact dual criterion (5.1), `F in C_{n,mu}`. Lemma 5.1 then says `F` is `n`-CM. `QED`

### What changed relative to the previous proof

The previous route sought one residual `G_*` satisfying the simultaneous vector equation

\[
G_*=T(G_*).
\]

The dual route never needs such a `G_*`. For each test function `phi`, it is allowed to choose a different maximizer `G_phi`; Hahn–Banach converts the collection of one-functional inequalities into membership of `F` in the complete-mixability set. The nonlinear map `G -> H_G` need not be continuous at all for Theorem 6.1.

This removes the single most audit-sensitive step of the previous proof.

## 7. Recovering the full source answer from the one-scale criterion

The previous report already proved the two facts needed to apply Theorem 6.1 to **every** absolutely continuous bounded-support law `F`:

1. one can extract a bounded nondegenerate probability density `h_0` with the same mean as `F` and `h_0<=C_0 f` by taking bounded positive-density pieces strictly below and strictly above the mean and mixing them to restore the barycenter;
2. because that `h_0` is bounded and compactly supported, its centered characteristic function is in `L^2`, is strictly smaller than one away from zero, and has the finite-variance quadratic expansion at zero. The fixed-window Fourier local-limit argument then gives

\[
\sup_{|t|\le B}
\left|
\sqrt{k}\,p_k(k\mu+t)-\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right|\to0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Thus for some finite `k`, `delta_k>0`; also `M_{k-1}<infinity`. Freeze that one `k` and define `C_k` by (6.3). Theorem 6.1 then applies for every

\[
 n\ge N(F):=\max\{k+1,\lceil kC_k\rceil\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Therefore the source-level implication follows again:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Every absolutely continuous probability distribution on a bounded interval is }n\text{-CM for all sufficiently large }n.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

This proof uses the same quantitative corrector lemmas as the previous report but replaces the infinite-dimensional fixed point and its continuity proof by the elementary maximization/separation argument above.
