## 4. B2 — attack the full theorem's negation first

I deliberately tried to find an absolutely continuous bounded law for which one of the previous proof's mechanisms fails. The stress tests were chosen to attack assumptions the proof does *not* explicitly impose.

### 4.1 Disconnected support with a gap through the mean

Take an asymmetric two-island density, for example a positive density on two separated intervals with zero density on a neighborhood of its mean. This defeats the old `D0007-A001` common-center decomposition and defeats any argument requiring a global lower bound on `f`.

It does **not** defeat the fixed-point proof. The proof only needs to extract two positive-density sets, one strictly below `mu` and one strictly above `mu`, and mix bounded truncations of those pieces to obtain a bounded nondegenerate `h_0` with the same mean. A gap around `mu` causes no problem. Its convolution powers spread across the fixed correction window once the order `k` is large, by the local-limit step.

**Outcome:** no counterexample; the old gap obstruction is bypassed rather than assumed away.

### 4.2 Density that approaches zero arbitrarily fast and has no positive global lower bound

This is the regime Wang's Open Problem 7 is designed to test. The prior theorem never divides by the target density globally. It constructs a small control density `h_0` satisfying

\[
h_0\le C_0f,
\]

then conditions only the fixed `k`-fold control law. The conditional one-coordinate marginal satisfies

\[
h_s(x)=\frac{h_0(x)p_{k-1}(s-x)}{p_k(s)}
\le
\frac{C_0\|p_{k-1}\|_\infty}{\delta}f(x)=C_1f(x)
\]

uniformly over the correction window. Thus zeros or arbitrarily deep valleys of `f` outside the chosen control support do not produce an illegal residual density.

**Outcome:** no counterexample; source domination is local-to-the-control component, not a global lower-density assumption.

### 4.3 Wildly oscillatory or unbounded density

The proof first replaces the relevant below/above-mean pieces by `min(f,M)`, normalized, before taking the mean-correcting mixture. Hence `h_0` is bounded even when `f` is not. Bounded support then gives finite variance automatically. The quantile residual construction uses only the residual law's support and mean; it does not require a smooth density.

**Outcome:** no counterexample; truncation neutralizes this pathology.

### 4.4 Fat-Cantor-type positive-measure support / infinitely many gaps

A support with infinitely many gaps was the most serious conceptual stress test because the earlier negative search tried to exploit gaps at all scales. But the residual error bound

\[
\operatorname{ess\,diam}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}Q_G(U+j/m)\right)\le b-a
\]

is purely order-theoretic. It does not see how many gaps `G` has. The control block only has to correct a sum error in one fixed compact interval of width `2(b-a)`, not reproduce the geometry of each gap.

**Outcome:** no counterexample; the stratified-quantile step is insensitive to disconnected support geometry.

### 4.5 Artificially enlarged containing interval

If `[a,b]` is not the essential support interval, then `B=b-a` may be unnecessarily large. This merely forces choosing a larger convolution order `k`; the fixed-window local limit is valid for every fixed finite `B`. It does not invalidate the proof.

**Outcome:** no counterexample.

## 6. B27 — swap and audit every important quantifier

The most plausible hidden flaw in an asymptotic existence proof is an unnoticed dependence of `k`, `C_1`, or the fixed-point domain on `n` or on the unknown `G`. I checked the order explicitly.

### 6.1 Correct required quantifiers

The source asks

\[
\forall F\ \exists N(F)\ \forall n\ge N(F)\ \exists\Pi_n
\]

where `Pi_n` is a coupling with all marginals `F` and constant sum.

It does **not** require one coupling valid simultaneously for all `n`, one explicit algorithm uniform over all `F`, or a universal threshold independent of `F`.

### 6.2 Order in the candidate proof

For a fixed `F`:

1. choose `h_0`, hence `C_0` and variance `sigma^2`;
2. choose one fixed convolution order `k` so that the `k`-fold sum density is positive on the whole fixed correction window;
3. define one fixed `C_1=C_0||p_{k-1}||_infty/delta`;
4. set `N(F)=max{k+1,ceil(kC_1)}`;
5. **then**, for each `n>=N(F)`, set `m=n-k`, define `D_n`, define the `n`-dependent map `T_n`, and take a fixed point `G_{*,n}`.

Thus the proof establishes exactly

\[
\forall F\ \exists(k,C_1,N)\ \forall n\ge N\ \exists G_{*,n}\ \exists\Pi_n.
\]

No step needs

\[
\exists G_*\ \forall n\ge N,
\]

which would be a false strengthening.

### 6.3 Continuity has an `m` factor, but `m` is fixed during each fixed-point problem

The estimate

\[
E|R_{G_r}-R_G|\le mW_1(G_r,G)
\]

might look non-uniform as `n` grows. Schauder is applied **separately for each fixed n**. For that application, `m` is one finite constant, so `W_1(G_r,G)->0` still implies convergence of residual sums. Uniform continuity in `n` is unnecessary.

### 6.4 The control lower bound is uniform in `G` and `n`

The correction always lies in

\[
J=[k\mu-B,k\mu+B]
\]

because the residual sum error is at most `B`, independently of `m`, `G`, and `n`. The lower bound

\[
\delta=\min_{s\in J}p_k(s)>0
\]

therefore depends only on the fixed `F` through the fixed control law and chosen `k`.

### 6.5 The final permutation fixes the non-identical pre-permutation residual marginals

Before permutation, residual coordinates generally have different laws. What is exact is their average marginal `G_*`, and the control coordinates have average marginal `H_{G_*}`. The fixed-point identity says the average over all `n` positions is `F`. An independent uniform random permutation therefore makes every final coordinate marginal `F` exactly. This uses only exchange of labels; it does not alter the constant sum.

I found no swapped-quantifier defect. The theorem uses precisely the weaker quantifier order demanded by Wang's source.

## 7. Targeted re-checks of the four technically vulnerable proof steps

This section does not assert new results; it records why the current self-audit did not locate a gap.

### 7.1 Local-limit positivity of a fixed convolution order

Because the extracted `h_0` is bounded and compactly supported, `h_0 in L^2`; hence its characteristic function is in `L^2`. Absolute continuity and nondegeneracy imply `|phi(u)|<1` for `u != 0`, while Riemann–Lebesgue gives decay at infinity. The previous report splits Fourier inversion into a small-frequency Gaussian region and a complement bounded by `sqrt(k) r^{k-2} ||phi||_2^2`. This gives uniform local convergence on every fixed `O(1)` window about `k mu` and therefore a positive minimum there for sufficiently large fixed `k`.

I found no lattice or span issue: the control law is absolutely continuous.

### 7.2 Quantile telescoping

For `0<=u<v<1/m`, monotonicity yields

\[
Q(v+j/m)-Q(u+j/m)
\le Q(u+(j+1)/m)-Q(u+j/m)
\]

for the first `m-1` terms, and the last term is bounded by the remaining jump to `Q(1-)`. Summation telescopes to at most `Q(1-)-Q(u)<=B`. Since the residual sum's expectation is `m mu`, its entire range lies within length `B`, hence every realization differs from its mean by at most `B`.

No continuity of `Q` is needed.

### 7.3 Weak compactness and continuity of the fixed-point map

`D_n` is a weakly closed subset of probabilities on compact `[a,b]`: mean is a continuous linear functional, and domination by `(n/m)F` is preserved under weak limits via nonnegative continuous test functions. Thus `D_n` is compact and convex.

For a fixed `n`, weak convergence on compact support implies `W_1` convergence. The quantile identity gives `L^1` convergence of the residual sums. The conditional one-coordinate expectation is a bounded continuous function of the correction sum because `p_{k-1}` is bounded continuous and `p_k` is bounded below on `J`. Hence `H_G` and then `T_n(G)` depend continuously on `G`.

### 7.4 Positivity and normalization of the residual update

If `n>=kC_1`, then

\[
kH_G\le kC_1F\le nF,
\]

so `nF-kH_G` is a nonnegative measure. Its mass is `n-k=m`; dividing by `m` gives a probability. Its mean is `(n mu-k mu)/m=mu`. Subtracting a nonnegative measure also gives `T_n(G)<= (n/m)F`. So it remains inside `D_n`.
