## 3. B18: rewrite the odd complete-mix problem as signed deficiencies

Write generally

\[
E=20001,
\qquad
f_E(x)=\frac E2|x|^{E-1}\mathbf 1_{[-1,1]}(x).
\]

If \(X\sim F_E\), set

\[
M=|X|,
\qquad
Z=1-M.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb P(M\le m)=m^E,
\qquad 0\le m\le1,
\]
so

\[
\mathbb P(Z\le z)=1-(1-z)^E,
\qquad
0\le z\le1.
\]

Thus \(Z\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,E)\) with

\[
g(z)=E(1-z)^{E-1},
\qquad
\mu_Z=\mathbb EZ=\frac1{E+1},
\]
and quantile

\[
\boxed{Q(u)=1-(1-u)^{1/E}.}
\]

The literature interpretation is useful: `Z` itself has a decreasing density. The monotone-density complete-mixability theorem of Wang–Wang says its ordinary complete mixability is governed by the moderate-mean condition. For support `[0,1]`, this condition holds for `Z` once `n >= E+1 = 20002`. Consequently the obstruction for `F_*` beyond 20001 cannot be merely “the deficiencies do not mix”; the hard part is the **alignment of deficiency mass with the sign minority**.

## 4. B11: a strengthened necessary condition from a bathtub/transport dual

I now prove a necessary condition for **any symmetric continuous law on `[-1,1]`**, specialized afterward to `F_E`.

Let \(n=2q+1\) be odd. Suppose \(X_1,\dots,X_n\) are identically distributed according to a continuous symmetric law on `[-1,1]` and

\[
X_1+\cdots+X_n=0\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

Because the marginals are continuous, with probability one no coordinate is zero. Randomly permuting the coordinates preserves the complete mix and makes it exchangeable; independently applying a global sign flip with probability `1/2` preserves the same properties. These symmetrizations do not change the marginal law.

For a realized vector, orient it so that the majority sign is positive. Let

* \(R\) be the number of minority-sign coordinates, so \(0\le R\le q\);
* \(Z_i=1-|X_i|\);
* \(D=\sum_i Z_i\);
* \(I_i\) indicate that coordinate `i` is in the minority after orientation;
* \(B=\sum_i Z_i I_i\), the total minority deficiency.

The zero-sum identity is exact. Since a majority coordinate contributes \(1-Z_i\) and a minority coordinate contributes \(-(1-Z_i)\),

\[
0=(n-R)-R-\left((D-B)-B\right),
\]
therefore

\[
\boxed{D=n-2R+2B.}\tag{4.1}
\]

Take expectations and put

\[
r=\mathbb ER,
\qquad
b=\mathbb EB,
\qquad
\mu_Z=\mathbb EZ.
\]

Then

\[
n\mu_Z=n-2r+2b.
\]
Equivalently, with \(q=(n-1)/2\),

\[
q-r+b=\frac{n\mu_Z-1}{2}=:c.\tag{4.2}
\]

Set \(t=q-r\ge0\). Equation (4.2) becomes

\[
r=q-t,
\qquad
b=c-t.\tag{4.3}
\]

### 4.1 Exact lower-tail selection lemma

Let `Z` be any continuous random variable in `[0,1]` with quantile `Q`. If `I` is an indicator, possibly dependent on `Z`, with \(\mathbb EI=p\), then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[ZI]\ge\int_0^pQ(u)\,du.
}\tag{4.4}
\]

Proof. Let \(\lambda=Q(p)\). Pointwise,

\[
(Z-\lambda)I\ge (Z-\lambda)\mathbf 1_{\{Z<\lambda\}},
\]
because on `Z<lambda` the left multiplier `I<=1` makes a negative number no smaller, while on `Z>=lambda` the left side is nonnegative. Hence

\[
\mathbb E[ZI]
\ge \lambda p+
\mathbb E[(Z-\lambda)\mathbf 1_{\{Z<\lambda\}}].
\]
Continuity gives \(P(Z<\lambda)=p\), and the right side is exactly the lower-tail quantile integral \(\int_0^pQ(u)du\). This proves (4.4). \(\square\)

By exchangeability,

\[
\mathbb E I_i=\frac rn
\]
for every coordinate. Applying (4.4) coordinate by coordinate and summing gives

\[
\boxed{
 b\ge nJ(r/n),
\qquad
J(p):=\int_0^pQ(u)\,du.
}\tag{4.5}

Combining (4.3) and (4.5), any complete mix must admit some \(t\ge0\) with

\[
c-t\ge nJ\!\left(\frac{q-t}{n}\right).
\]
Define

\[
\Phi(t)=c-t-nJ\!\left(\frac{q-t}{n}\right).
\]
Because \(J'=Q\),

\[
\Phi'(t)=-1+Q\!\left(\frac{q-t}{n}\right)<0.
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) is strictly decreasing. Feasibility for any \(t\ge0\) therefore forces \(\Phi(0)\ge0\). We obtain the general necessary condition

\[
\boxed{
\frac{n\mu_Z-1}{2}
\ge
n\int_0^{(n-1)/(2n)}Q(u)\,du.
}\tag{4.6}

This is strictly stronger for the frozen candidate than the preceding first-moment deficiency test.

## 5. Exact specialization to the frozen beta-power law

For \(Z\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,E)\),

\[
Q(u)=1-(1-u)^{1/E}.
\]
Hence

\[
J(p)
=p-\frac{E}{E+1}
\left[1-(1-p)^{(E+1)/E}\right].\tag{5.1}
\]

Put

\[
p=\frac{n-1}{2n},
\qquad
a=1-p=\frac{n+1}{2n}.
\]
Since \(\mu_Z=1/(E+1)\), straightforward exact algebra gives

\[
(E+1)\left(
J(p)-\frac1n\frac{n\mu_Z-1}{2}
\right)
=E\left(a^{(E+1)/E}-\frac12\right).\tag{5.2}
\]

Condition (4.6) is therefore equivalent to

\[
a^{(E+1)/E}\le\frac12.
\]
Substituting \(a=(n+1)/(2n)\) and raising positive quantities to exact positive powers yields

\[
\boxed{
1+\frac1n\le2^{1/(E+1)}.
}\tag{5.3}
\]
Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
(n+1)^{E+1}\le2n^{E+1}.
}\tag{5.4}

Therefore we have proved:

> **Signed-deficiency obstruction theorem.** For odd `n`, the frozen law `F_E` cannot be `n`-completely mixable whenever
> \[
> (n+1)^{E+1}>2n^{E+1}.
> \]

This is a theorem-sized analytic statement; the proof is the complete derivation above, not a numerical inference.

## 8. What B11/B18/B30 changed conceptually

### B11 — dualize

The old pathwise estimate used only the fact that an odd zero-sum vector cannot have every magnitude too close to `1`. The new argument treats the **minority sign locations as a transport-selection variable** and asks how cheaply the law's deficiencies can be assigned to those locations. Hardy–Littlewood/bathtub minimization gives the exact lower-tail cost, which produces the sharper phase boundary (5.4).

### B18 — say it in the deficiency language

The variable `Z=1-|X|` has a simple decreasing beta density. This separates two issues that were conflated before:

1. ordinary complete mixing of deficiencies, which is already available for large dimensions by the monotone-density theorem;
2. signed partitioning of those deficiencies, which is the actual obstruction for the U-shaped symmetric law.

The latter is where a source-level counterexample would have to live.

### B30 — one dimension at a time

Instead of another asymptotic heuristic, the run attacked the first boundary left by the old theorem. The exact integer criterion moves the first undecided odd dimension from `20003` to `28857` and raises the frozen metric by `8854`.

The present frozen density vanishes at the origin, so that theorem does not apply.

### Current author page

Ruodu Wang's current Waterloo working-paper page for the complete-mixability series explicitly notes that Open Problem #1 is solved and #12 partially solved; it does not mark #7 solved. This is bounded current-status evidence only, not proof that no solution exists elsewhere.

### Yin–Zhu claim

The current arXiv version `1706.05499v4` (11 May 2023) says in its abstract that a Wang open problem is solved. Its Example 2.4 explicitly asserts that a fixed positive mixture of the densities

\[
f_m(x)=C_m\frac{x^{2m}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}
\]

is not `(2n+1)`-CM for every `n`, and identifies Wang's Open Problem 7. A 2019 seminar abstract by Yin repeats the claim of solving Open Problem 7 with a bimodal-symmetric distribution.

However, in the visible v4 proof text, Example 2.1 gives a dimension-dependent shell-mass obstruction and Example 2.2 gives another dimension-dependent shell condition, while Example 2.4 moves from the statement that each fixed component is non-CM only for “not very large” odd dimensions directly to the infinite-mixture all-odd assertion. In this run I did **not** locate an intervening proof that the fixed mixture satisfies an all-large-odd obstruction. Therefore the responsible label remains: **published/preprint claim located; source-level proof not yet independently verified here**. I do not call the Yin–Zhu assertion false.
