## 3. Mathematical idea before bookkeeping

The old common-center strategy tries to decompose the target law `F` directly into pieces which are themselves complete mixes with the same center. That is unnecessarily rigid.

The new construction splits the `n` coordinates by **roles**, not by a common-center mixture of laws:

- `m=n-k` **residual coordinates** are made from a stratified quantile coupling of an unknown residual law `G`. Their *average marginal* is exactly `G`, while their total sum misses `m mu` by at most one support diameter, uniformly in `m`.
- A fixed number `k` of **control coordinates** comes from one bounded density `H_0` dominated by `F`. A local central limit argument makes the `k`-fold sum density uniformly positive on the whole correction window. Conditioning those `k` variables on the exact missing sum produces a control marginal `H_G` which is uniformly dominated by a constant multiple of `F`, independently of `G` and `n`.
- We then solve the exact marginal balance equation

\[
F=\frac{m}{n}G+\frac{k}{n}H_G
\]

by a Schauder fixed point on a compact convex set of residual laws.

Finally, a uniform permutation of all `n` coordinates turns the exact **average marginal** `F` into `n` identical marginals `F`, while preserving the deterministic total sum.

This is the promised B1/B12/B19 change of category:

- **B1:** move from direct mix decomposition to a fixed-point problem on residual probability measures;
- **B12:** exploit the conserved barycenter together with a new bounded-sum-error invariant of stratified quantiles;
- **B19:** turn the existence proof into an explicit operator/coupling construction, with the only nonconstructive step being the fixed-point existence theorem.

## 4. Main theorem

### Theorem 4.1 — asymptotic complete mixability for every absolutely continuous bounded law

Let `F` be an absolutely continuous probability distribution supported on a bounded interval `[a,b]`, and let

\[
\mu=\int x\,F(dx),\qquad B=b-a.
\]

Then there exists an integer `N(F)` such that for every integer `n>=N(F)` there are random variables

\[
X_1,\ldots,X_n\sim F
\]

with

\[
\boxed{X_1+\cdots+X_n=n\mu\quad\text{almost surely}.}
\]

Equivalently, `F` is `n`-completely mixable for every sufficiently large integer `n`.

The rest of Sections 5--11 is a complete proof.

## 5. A bounded control density dominated by `F` and having the same mean

Let `f` be a density of `F`. An absolutely continuous probability law is nondegenerate, so

\[
P(X<\mu)>0,
\qquad
P(X>\mu)>0.
\]

Hence there exists `eps>0` such that the sets

\[
A:=\{x\in[a,b]:x\le\mu-\varepsilon,\ f(x)>0\},
\qquad
C:=\{x\in[a,b]:x\ge\mu+\varepsilon,\ f(x)>0\}
\]

both have positive `F`-mass and positive Lebesgue measure after intersecting with a suitable positive-density level if necessary.

Fix any finite `M>0` for which

\[
q_A:=\int_A \min\{f(x),M\}\,dx>0,
\qquad
q_C:=\int_C \min\{f(x),M\}\,dx>0.
\]

Define bounded probability densities

\[
h_A(x)=\frac{\min\{f(x),M\}\mathbf 1_A(x)}{q_A},
\qquad
h_C(x)=\frac{\min\{f(x),M\}\mathbf 1_C(x)}{q_C}.
\]

Let their means be `mu_A` and `mu_C`. By construction,

\[
\mu_A\le\mu-\varepsilon<\mu<\mu+\varepsilon\le\mu_C.
\]

Set

\[
\lambda:=\frac{\mu_C-\mu}{\mu_C-\mu_A}\in(0,1)
\]

and

\[
h_0:=\lambda h_A+(1-\lambda)h_C.
\]

Then `h_0` is a bounded probability density supported in `[a,b]`, it is nondegenerate, and

\[
\int xh_0(x)\,dx
=
\lambda\mu_A+(1-\lambda)\mu_C
=\mu.
\]

Moreover `min(f,M)<=f`, so with

\[
C_0:=\frac{\lambda}{q_A}+\frac{1-\lambda}{q_C}
\]

we have the pointwise domination

\[
\boxed{h_0(x)\le C_0 f(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let `H_0` denote the law with density `h_0`, and let

\[
\sigma^2:=\int (x-\mu)^2h_0(x)\,dx>0.
\]

The entire later control block will be built from this single source-dominated density.

## 6. A fixed convolution order with a uniformly positive correction window

Let `p_j=h_0^{*j}` be the density of the sum of `j` iid `H_0` variables. We need one fixed integer `k` for which

\[
\inf_{|t|\le B}p_k(k\mu+t)>0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

I prove this directly from Fourier inversion rather than importing a local CLT without hypotheses.

Center `H_0` and write

\[
\phi(u)=\int e^{iu(x-\mu)}h_0(x)\,dx.
\]

Because `h_0` is bounded and compactly supported, `h_0 in L^2`, so Plancherel gives `phi in L^2`. Since `H_0` is nondegenerate and absolutely continuous,

\[
|\phi(u)|<1\qquad(u\ne0),
\]

and the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma gives `phi(u)->0` as `|u|->infinity`. Finite nonzero variance gives

\[
\phi(u)=1-\frac{\sigma^2u^2}{2}+o(u^2)
\qquad(u\to0).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus for some `delta_0,c>0`,

\[
|\phi(u)|\le e^{-cu^2}
\qquad(|u|\le\delta_0).
\tag{6.3}
\]

For every integer `j>=2`, `|phi|^j<=|phi|^2 in L^1`, so Fourier inversion yields a bounded continuous density `p_j`.

For `j>=2`, after the change of variable `v=u sqrt(j)`,

\[
\sqrt j\,p_j(j\mu+t)
=
\frac1{2\pi}
\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ivt/\sqrt j}
 \phi(v/\sqrt j)^j\,dv.
\tag{6.4}
\]

On every fixed bounded `v`-set, (6.2) implies

\[
\phi(v/\sqrt j)^j\to e^{-\sigma^2v^2/2},
\]

while the phase `e^{-ivt/sqrt(j)}` tends to `1` uniformly for `|t|<=B`. On `|v|<=delta_0 sqrt(j)`, (6.3) supplies the integrable Gaussian domination `e^{-cv^2}`.

For the complementary region, continuity, strict inequality `|phi(u)|<1` away from zero, and `phi(u)->0` give

\[
r:=\sup_{|u|\ge\delta_0}|\phi(u)|<1.
\]

Therefore

\[
\sqrt j\int_{|u|\ge\delta_0}|\phi(u)|^j\,du
\le
\sqrt j\,r^{j-2}\int_{\mathbb R}|\phi(u)|^2\,du
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Dominated convergence in (6.4), together with (6.5), proves the uniform fixed-window local limit

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{|t|\le B}
\left|
\sqrt j\,p_j(j\mu+t)-\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
\right|
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Choose once and for all an integer `k>=3` so large that

\[
\delta:=\min_{|t|\le B}p_k(k\mu+t)>0.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Also set

\[
M_{k-1}:=\|p_{k-1}\|_\infty<\infty.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Both `k` and the positive constants `delta,M_{k-1}` now depend only on `F` through the fixed control density `h_0`, never on the eventual dimension `n` or on the residual law introduced below.

## 7. Exact conditional control kernels

Define the correction window

\[
J:=[k\mu-B,k\mu+B].
\]

By (6.7), `p_k(s)>=delta` for every `s in J`.

For each `s in J`, consider `k` iid `H_0` variables conditioned on their sum being `s`. A concrete regular conditional kernel can be written on the first `k-1` coordinates as

\[
K_s(dx_1\cdots dx_{k-1})
=
\frac{
  \left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}h_0(x_i)\right)
  h_0\!\left(s-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_i\right)
}{p_k(s)}
\,dx_1\cdots dx_{k-1},
\tag{7.1}
\]

with

\[
x_k=s-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_i.
\]

The normalizing identity is exactly the definition of `p_k(s)`. Symmetrizing this kernel over coordinate permutations if desired makes exchangeability explicit without changing the sum or one-coordinate average.

Its one-coordinate marginal has density

\[
h_s(x)=
\frac{h_0(x)p_{k-1}(s-x)}{p_k(s)}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Using (5.1), (6.7), and (6.8), for every `s in J`,

\[
h_s(x)
\le
\frac{C_0M_{k-1}}{\delta}f(x).
\]

Set

\[
\boxed{C_1:=\frac{C_0M_{k-1}}{\delta}.}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Then the uniform source domination is

\[
\boxed{h_s\le C_1f\quad\text{for every }s\in J.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This uniform estimate is the mechanism replacing Wang's global lower bound on the target density: only a small bounded control density dominated by `F` needs to be conditioned.

## 8. B12 — the bounded-error stratified quantile invariant

Fix an integer `n>k` and write

\[
m:=n-k.
\]

Let `G` be any probability law supported on `[a,b]` with mean `mu`, and let `Q_G` be a generalized quantile function. Let

\[
U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1/m)
\]

and define `m` residual coordinates

\[
Y_j=Q_G(U+j/m),
\qquad j=0,1,\ldots,m-1.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### 8.1 Exact average marginal

For every bounded Borel `psi`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}E\,\psi(Y_j)
&=
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
\int_0^{1/m}\psi(Q_G(u+j/m))\,du\\
&=
\int_0^1\psi(Q_G(v))\,dv\\
&=
\int\psi\,dG.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus the residual coordinates need not individually have law `G`, but their average marginal is **exactly** `G`.

### 8.2 Exact average sum

Let

\[
R_G(U):=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}Q_G(U+j/m).
\tag{8.3}
\]

Taking `psi(x)=x` in the same partition calculation gives

\[
E R_G(U)=m\mu.
\tag{8.4}
\]

### 8.3 Uniform diameter bound independent of `m`

The crucial invariant is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ess\,diam}R_G(U)\le B.}
\tag{8.5}
\]

Indeed, take `0<=u<v<1/m`. Since `Q_G` is nondecreasing and

\[
v+j/m<u+(j+1)/m
\qquad(j=0,\ldots,m-2),
\]

we have

\[
Q_G(v+j/m)-Q_G(u+j/m)
\le
Q_G(u+(j+1)/m)-Q_G(u+j/m)
\]

for `j<=m-2`, while the last increment is at most

\[
Q_G(1-)-Q_G(u+(m-1)/m).
\]

Summing telescopes:

\[
0\le R_G(v)-R_G(u)
\le Q_G(1-)-Q_G(u)
\le b-a=B.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Because the mean `m mu` lies between the essential infimum and supremum of `R_G`, (8.4)--(8.6) imply

\[
\boxed{|R_G(U)-m\mu|\le B\quad\text{a.s.}}
\tag{8.7}
\]

This is the source-general replacement for the old common-center decomposition: no matter how disconnected or asymmetric `G` is, all but a fixed number of coordinates can be arranged so that their total misses the required barycenter by at most one support diameter.

Define the required control sum

\[
S_G:=n\mu-R_G(U).
\tag{8.8}
\]

Then (8.7) gives

\[
S_G\in[k\mu-B,k\mu+B]=J
\quad\text{a.s.},
\tag{8.9}
\]

and (8.4) gives

\[
E S_G=k\mu.
\tag{8.10}
\]

Let `Lambda_G` denote the law of `S_G`.

## 9. The control marginal associated to `G`

Conditionally on `S_G=s`, sample a `k`-vector from the kernel `K_s` of Section 7. It has deterministic coordinate sum `s`. Let `H_G` be the average one-coordinate marginal of this control block; with the symmetrized kernel it is the common control-coordinate marginal.

From (7.2), its density is

\[
h_G(x)=\int_J h_s(x)\,\Lambda_G(ds).
\tag{9.1}
\]

The uniform bound (7.4) immediately gives

\[
\boxed{H_G\le C_1F\quad\text{as measures}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

Also the control block has total sum `S_G`, so by (8.10) its average coordinate mean is

\[
\int x\,H_G(dx)=\frac{E S_G}{k}=\mu.
\tag{9.3}
\]

Thus `H_G` has exactly the same barycenter as `F`, even though individual conditional blocks have centers `s/k` varying with `s`.

This is the conserved quantity B12 that the earlier common-center route was missing: only the **averaged control marginal** needs center `mu`; the conditional correction blocks are allowed to carry different centers because their total sums are matched pointwise to the residual error.

## 10. B1/B19 — fixed point on residual laws

For the fixed `n>k`, define

\[
\mathcal D_n
:=
\left\{
G:\ G\text{ is a probability law on }[a,b],\
\int x\,G(dx)=\mu,\
G\le\frac{n}{m}F
\right\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

This set is nonempty because `F in D_n`. It is convex. It is weakly compact: probability measures on the compact interval `[a,b]` form a weakly compact set; the mean constraint is weakly closed; and domination by `(n/m)F` is weakly closed because for every nonnegative continuous `psi`,

\[
\int\psi\,dG_r\le\frac nm\int\psi\,dF
\]

passes to weak limits and characterizes measure domination.

For `G in D_n`, define

\[
\boxed{
T_n(G):=\frac{nF-kH_G}{m}.
}
\tag{10.2}
\]

Choose `n` so large that

\[
\boxed{n\ge kC_1.}
\tag{10.3}
\]

Then (9.2) gives

\[
kH_G\le kC_1F\le nF,
\]

so `T_n(G)` is a nonnegative measure. Its total mass is

\[
\frac{n-k}{m}=1,
\]

and by (9.3) its mean is

\[
\frac{n\mu-k\mu}{m}=\mu.
\]

Finally, because the subtracted measure is nonnegative,

\[
T_n(G)\le\frac nmF.
\]

Therefore

\[
T_n(\mathcal D_n)\subseteq\mathcal D_n.
\tag{10.4}
\]

### 10.1 Continuity of `T_n`

Let `G_r -> G` weakly in `D_n`. Since all laws are supported on the common compact interval `[a,b]`, weak convergence is equivalent here to convergence in the 1-Wasserstein metric. In quantile form,

\[
W_1(G_r,G)
=
\int_0^1|Q_{G_r}(v)-Q_G(v)|\,dv
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10.5}
\]

Couple the residual sums with the same `U`. From (8.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
E|R_{G_r}(U)-R_G(U)|
&\le
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
E|Q_{G_r}(U+j/m)-Q_G(U+j/m)|\\
&=
m\int_0^1|Q_{G_r}(v)-Q_G(v)|\,dv\\
&=
mW_1(G_r,G)
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.6}
\]

Hence `S_{G_r}->S_G` in `L^1`, so

\[
\Lambda_{G_r}\Rightarrow\Lambda_G.
\tag{10.7}
\]

For any bounded continuous test function `psi`, define

\[
A_\psi(s)
:=
\int \psi(x)
\frac{h_0(x)p_{k-1}(s-x)}{p_k(s)}\,dx,
\qquad s\in J.
\tag{10.8}
\]

The density `p_{k-1}` is bounded and continuous, `p_k(s)>=delta` on `J`, and `h_0` is integrable. Dominated convergence therefore shows that `A_psi` is continuous on `J`. Using (9.1),

\[
\int\psi\,dH_{G_r}
=
\int_J A_\psi(s)\,\Lambda_{G_r}(ds)
\longrightarrow
\int_J A_\psi(s)\,\Lambda_G(ds)
=
\int\psi\,dH_G.
\tag{10.9}
\]

Thus `H_{G_r}=>H_G`, and (10.2) gives

\[
T_n(G_r)\Rightarrow T_n(G).
\tag{10.10}
\]

So `T_n` is continuous on the compact convex set `D_n` in the weak topology.

### 10.2 Fixed point

The space of finite signed Borel measures on `[a,b]`, endowed with the weak topology induced by `C([a,b])`, is locally convex. The Schauder--Tychonoff fixed-point theorem therefore applies to the nonempty compact convex set `D_n` and continuous self-map `T_n`.

Hence there exists

\[
G_*\in\mathcal D_n
\]

such that

\[
G_*=T_n(G_*).
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
F=\frac mnG_*+\frac knH_{G_*}.
}
\tag{10.11}
\]

This is the exact marginal balance equation needed for a complete mix.

## 11. Construct the `n`-complete mix from the fixed point

Take the fixed point `G_*` from Section 10.

1. Sample `U~Unif[0,1/m)` and form the residual vector

\[
Y_j=Q_{G_*}(U+j/m),
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m-1.
\]

Its sum is `R_{G_*}(U)` and its average marginal is `G_*` by (8.2).

2. Set

\[
s=n\mu-R_{G_*}(U)\in J.
\]

Conditionally on this `s`, sample a `k`-coordinate control vector from `K_s`. Its sum is exactly `s`, and its average marginal is `H_{G_*}`.

3. Concatenate the residual and control coordinates. Their total sum is pointwise

\[
R_{G_*}(U)+s=n\mu.
\tag{11.1}
\]

4. Before symmetrizing positions, the **average marginal over all `n` slots** is

\[
\frac mnG_*+\frac knH_{G_*}=F
\]

by (10.11).

5. Apply an independent uniformly random permutation to all `n` coordinates. The deterministic sum (11.1) is unchanged. After this permutation, every coordinate marginal equals the pre-permutation average marginal, namely `F`.

Therefore there exist

\[
X_1,\ldots,X_n\sim F
\]

such that

\[
X_1+\cdots+X_n=n\mu
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]

whenever

\[
n>k
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n\ge kC_1.
\]

Thus one may take, for example,

\[
\boxed{
N(F)=\max\{k+1,\lceil kC_1\rceil\}.
}
\tag{11.2}
\]

This completes the proof of Theorem 4.1. `QED`

## 12. What the proof does and does not use

The proof uses exactly the source assumptions:

- `F` is absolutely continuous, to extract a bounded nondegenerate control density `h_0` dominated by `F` and to obtain a nonlattice smoothing kernel;
- the support is bounded, to make the stratified residual sum error uniformly bounded by one fixed diameter `B`.

It does **not** assume:

- symmetry;
- unimodality;
- connected support;
- that `f` is bounded;
- that `f` is bounded away from zero;
- any parity restriction on `n`.

The proof therefore answers the literal source question positively if correct.
