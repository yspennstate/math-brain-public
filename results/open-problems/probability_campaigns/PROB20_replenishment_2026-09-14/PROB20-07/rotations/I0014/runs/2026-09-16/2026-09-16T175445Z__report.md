## 6. B18 — SAY IT IN ANOTHER LANGUAGE

### Idea written before code: an Orlicz-energy / operator-correlation / variance-mixture design problem

Instead of describing I0014 as “find a rho-mixing counterexample,” rewrite it as a three-resource allocation problem over scales `j`.

At a candidate scale `m_j`, imagine a component carrying:

- a dependence range/strength budget `r_j` that must fit the maximal-correlation envelope;
- a marginal energy contribution whose cost is measured by the Orlicz gauge `Phi` above;
- a partial-sum variance contribution large enough to remain visible after normalization at `n_j`.

A successful scale must be inexpensive at a **single time** but expensive in the **sum over a long block**. That is exactly the leverage stationarity can provide: a component may have tiny one-time variance but a long correlation length, so its partial-sum variance at a matching scale is magnified.

The catch is that maximal correlation is an operator norm, not a probability of a rare event. A construction cannot simply let a rare latent bit persist for `m_j` steps, because if the same nontrivial bit is visible in both separated sigma-fields then the maximal correlation is one on that event algebra. The scale component must leak information gradually rather than share a perfectly recoverable rare state.

This language points to a more specific counterexample architecture:

```text
many scale-local weak channels
    + scale-dependent heavy value assignment
    + subsequential random variance
    -> mixture-of-normal subsequential limits.
```

This is closer to the Bradley-1983 shape than the previously tested rare-block picture.

**B18 outcome:** no metric movement, but the construction target is narrowed from “heavy-tail + slow mixing” to a synchronized *variance-mixture through weak scale channels*.
