## 3. Why the previous `d=2` reduction must be corrected

The source declares `log=log_2`, but exponentials are ordinary natural exponentials. Put

`beta in (0,1/2)`, `gamma=1-beta in (1/2,1)`, `c=ln 2`, and `L=log_2 n`.

For the source-normalized mixing envelope

`tau(n)=alpha (log_2 n)^(-beta)`, 

the harmonic sum entering Bradley’s displayed condition is

`S_n := sum_{k<=n} tau(k)/k`.

The preceding I0014 report effectively used `S_n ~ (alpha/gamma)L^gamma`, matching Matsui’s displayed inequality. That misses a factor `ln 2`.

Indeed, since `log_2 x = ln x / c`,

`tau(x)/x = alpha c^beta / [x (ln x)^beta]`.

The function is eventually positive decreasing, so sum-integral comparison has bounded error. Since

`int dx/[x(ln x)^beta] = (ln x)^gamma/gamma`, 

and `ln n = cL`, we obtain

`S_n = (alpha c^beta/gamma)(cL)^gamma + O(1)`

`    = (alpha c/gamma)L^gamma + O(1)`,                 (3.1)

because `beta+gamma=1`.

This agrees with the independent neighboring I0013 base-2 audit. It also pinpoints the source-display issue: in Matsui Remark 2.5(ii), the negative `y_n^2/n` exponent should acquire `ln 2` when `d` is interpreted as the literal Bradley coefficient, while the base-2 logarithm inside `q` cancels that factor in a different place. One cannot simply carry the same literal `d` through Matsui’s printed algebra.

## 4. Correct exact-I0014 Bradley-test threshold

This section is a complete analytic derivation for **Matsui’s displayed Bradley condition (1.5) test**. It is not yet a Bradley counterexample, because the literal construction constant and all of Theorem 1’s hypotheses have not been recovered from the primary paper.

Let `d_B>0` denote the literal coefficient multiplying the harmonic sum in Bradley’s argument and define

`y_n = n^(1/2) exp[-(d_B/2) S_n]`.                    (4.1)

The exact I0014 moment condition corresponds to the gauge

`q_C(x) = x^2 exp[C (log_2^+ x)^gamma]`,              (4.2)

where

`C = (2 alpha/gamma) 2^gamma`.                        (4.3)

The displayed Bradley test requires `q_C(y_n)=o(n)`.

### 4.1 Expansion of the quadratic factor

From (3.1),

`ln(y_n^2/n) = -d_B S_n`

` = -(d_B alpha c/gamma)L^gamma + O(1)`.              (4.4)

### 4.2 Expansion of the moment factor

Divide `ln y_n` by `c` to convert back to the source logarithm:

`log_2 y_n`

` = L/2 - (d_B/(2c)) S_n`

` = L/2 - (d_B alpha/(2 gamma))L^gamma + O(1)`

` = (L/2)[1 - (d_B alpha/gamma)L^(gamma-1) + O(L^(-1))]`.    (4.5)

Put

`z_L=(d_B alpha/gamma)L^(gamma-1)`.

Then `z_L->0` because `gamma<1`. Taylor expansion of `(1-z)^gamma` at zero, with the `O(L^(-1))` term included, yields

`(log_2 y_n)^gamma`

` = 2^(-gamma)L^gamma [1 - gamma z_L + O(z_L^2)+O(L^(-1))]`.  (4.6)

Multiplying by (4.3),

`C(log_2 y_n)^gamma`

` = (2 alpha/gamma)L^gamma`

`   - (2 d_B alpha^2/gamma)L^(2gamma-1)`

`   + O(L^(3gamma-2)) + O(L^(gamma-1))`.              (4.7)

Because `gamma<1`, both remainder powers are `o(L^(2gamma-1))`:

`(2gamma-1)-(3gamma-2)=1-gamma>0`,

`(2gamma-1)-(gamma-1)=gamma>0`.

Therefore

`C(log_2 y_n)^gamma`

` = (2 alpha/gamma)L^gamma`

`   - (2 d_B alpha^2/gamma)L^(2gamma-1)`

`   + o(L^(2gamma-1))`.                               (4.8)

### 4.3 Combine the two factors

Combining (4.4) and (4.8),

`ln(q_C(y_n)/n)`

` = (alpha/gamma)(2-d_B c)L^gamma`

`   - (2 d_B alpha^2/gamma)L^(2gamma-1)`

`   + o(L^(2gamma-1))`.                               (4.9)

This gives a complete trichotomy.

**Case 1: `d_B ln2 > 2`.** The first term in (4.9) is negative of order `L^gamma`, dominates the lower-order terms, and tends to `-infinity`. Hence `q_C(y_n)=o(n)`.

**Case 2: `d_B ln2 < 2`.** The first term is positive of order `L^gamma`. Since `gamma>(2gamma-1)` for `gamma<1`, it dominates the negative second term, so the logarithm tends to `+infinity`. Hence the displayed condition fails.

**Case 3: `d_B ln2 = 2`.** The leading term cancels exactly. Since I0014 assumes `beta<1/2`,

`2gamma-1 = 1-2beta > 0`.

Thus

`ln(q_C(y_n)/n)`

` = -(2 d_B alpha^2/gamma)L^(2gamma-1)(1+o(1)) -> -infinity`.

Therefore equality is included.

We have proved:

> **I0014-C02 (source-normalized Bradley displayed-test threshold).** For the exact I0014 exponential moment gauge, Matsui’s displayed Bradley-(1.5) test with a literal Bradley coefficient `d_B` satisfies `q_C(y_n)=o(n)` for `beta in (0,1/2)` exactly when
