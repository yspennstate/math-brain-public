## 5. Method B5 — add a parameter and differentiate: exact phase line for the Bradley displayed test

Fix

\[
0<\beta<\frac12,\qquad \gamma:=1-\beta\in\left(\frac12,1\right),\qquad \alpha>0.
\]

Define the source-rate profile

\[
\tau(k):=\alpha(\log_2(k\vee2))^{-\beta}
\]

and

\[
H(n):=\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{\tau(k)}{k}.
\]

For a strength parameter `lambda>0`, define

\[
q_\lambda(x)
:=x^2\exp\!\left[
\lambda\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2^+x)^\gamma
\right].
\tag{5.1}
\]

`lambda=1` is exactly the I0014 source moment gauge.

For a Bradley-style coefficient `d>0`, set

\[
y_n(d):=n^{1/2}\exp\!\left[-\frac d2 H(n)\right].
\tag{5.2}
\]

The first claim below is only an asymptotic analysis of the displayed Bradley test. It does **not** assert that Bradley's construction permits any particular `d`.

### Lemma 5.1 — parametric phase line

For every fixed `lambda,d>0`, as `n->infinity`, with `L=log_2 n`,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{q_\lambda(y_n(d))}{n}
=
\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}(2\lambda-d\ln2)L^\gamma
-
\frac{2\lambda d\alpha^2}{\gamma}L^{2\gamma-1}
+o(L^{2\gamma-1}).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, because `2gamma-1=1-2beta>0`,

\[
q_\lambda(y_n(d))=o(n)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{d\ge d_c(\lambda):=\frac{2\lambda}{\ln2}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

At equality,

\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{q_\lambda(y_n(d_c))}{n}
=
-rac{4\lambda^2\alpha^2}{\gamma\ln2}L^{1-2\beta}
+o(L^{1-2\beta}).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

In particular,

\[
\frac{\partial d_c}{\partial\lambda}=\frac2{\ln2}.
\]

#### Proof

For

\[
f(x)=\frac{\alpha}{x(\log_2 x)^\beta},\qquad x\ge2,
\]

`f` is positive and decreasing. The integral test therefore gives a bounded sum-integral error:

\[
H(n)=\int_2^n\frac{\alpha\,dx}{x(\log_2x)^\beta}+O(1).
\]

With `u=log_2 x`, `dx/x=(ln2)du`, hence

\[
H(n)
=
\frac{\alpha\ln2}{\gamma}L^\gamma+O(1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

From (5.2),

\[
2\log_2 y_n
=L-\frac d{\ln2}H(n)
=
L-\frac{d\alpha}{\gamma}L^\gamma+O(1).
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since `gamma<1`, `y_n->infinity`. Factor `L` in (5.7):

\[
2\log_2 y_n
=L\left(1-\frac{d\alpha}{\gamma}L^{\gamma-1}+O(L^{-1})\right).
\]

Using `(1-z)^gamma=1-gamma z+O(z^2)` and noting that the `O(z^2)` contribution is

\[
L^\gamma O(L^{2\gamma-2})=O(L^{3\gamma-2})=o(L^{2\gamma-1})
\]

because `gamma<1`, while `L^{gamma-1}=o(L^{2gamma-1})` because `gamma>0`, we obtain

\[
(2\log_2 y_n)^\gamma
=L^\gamma-d\alpha L^{2\gamma-1}+o(L^{2\gamma-1}).
\tag{5.8}
\]

Now

\[
2\log y_n-\log n=-dH(n),
\]

where this line uses the natural logarithm. Combining (5.1), (5.6), and (5.8),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{q_\lambda(y_n)}n
&=-dH(n)+\lambda\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2y_n)^\gamma\\
&= -\frac{d\alpha\ln2}{\gamma}L^\gamma
+\frac{2\lambda\alpha}{\gamma}L^\gamma
-\frac{2\lambda d\alpha^2}{\gamma}L^{2\gamma-1}
+o(L^{2\gamma-1}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Because `2gamma-1>0`, the bounded error in (5.6) is also absorbed into the final `o` term. This proves (5.3). If `d` is below the phase line, the first term tends to `+infinity`; if above, it tends to `-infinity`. At equality, the first term cancels and the displayed second-order term is strictly negative and divergent. This proves (5.4) and (5.5). ∎

### Interpretation

The previous checkpoint had found only the `lambda=1` correction

\[
d_c=\frac2{\ln2}=2.885390\ldots.
\]

The parameter calculation shows that this is not a mysterious one-off coefficient: it is the `lambda=1` point on a linear phase line. The source frontier is exactly critical with respect to the Bradley-style amplitude damping. Increasing the Orlicz coefficient by a factor `lambda` raises the required damping coefficient by the same factor.

This does not move `beta_bad`, because the still-missing question is whether a source-authentic Bradley construction admits a literal coefficient at or above the critical line.

## 6. Method B24 — find the extremal object: one-event-per-block atoms

For the exact source gauge (`lambda=1`) write

\[
Q(x)=x^2\exp\!\left[
\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2^+x)^\gamma
\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]

If a centered marginal obeys `E Q(|X|)<=M`, then for every `T>=1`, monotonicity of the exponential factor gives the elementary but sharp tail-energy inequality

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[X^2\mathbf 1_{\{|X|>T\}}]
\le
M\exp\!\left[-\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2T)^\gamma\right].
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed, on `|X|>T`,

\[
X^2
\le
Q(|X|)\exp\!\left[-\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2T)^\gamma\right],
\]

and expectation proves the claim.

The marginal configuration that spends the moment budget most directly at one scale is therefore a symmetric pair of atoms `+A,-A` with probability of order `1/n`, where `n` is the block scale. Such an atom appears order one times in an iid block of length `n`, so it is the natural extremal candidate if dependence is later used to convert rare scale events into a non-Gaussian variance mixture.

Take

\[
A_n(d)=n^{1/2}e^{-dH(n)/2}.
\]

The B5 phase calculation shows exactly what happens to a one-event-per-block choice `p_n=1/n`:

\[
p_n Q(A_n(d))=\frac{Q(A_n(d))}{n}.
\]

For `d<2/ln2`, this term grows exponentially on the stretched-log scale and cannot even tend to zero. For `d>2/ln2`, it decays on the `L^gamma` scale. At the critical value

\[
\boxed{d_*=2/\ln2,}
\]

it still decays, but only through the second-order margin

\[
\exp\{-cL^{1-2\beta}+o(L^{1-2\beta})\}.
\]

Thus `d_*` is also the exact marginal-feasibility boundary for this natural one-event-per-block atom table. This is independent of whether Bradley's dependence construction realizes that coefficient.

## 7. Method B8 — discretize: an explicit source-frontier marginal for every beta<1/2

The preceding extremal calculation can be converted into an actual probability law satisfying the **exact I0014 marginal moment** but no `2+delta` moment whatsoever.

This is a restricted marginal result only; it is not a stationary-process counterexample and does not move `beta_bad`.

### Theorem 7.1 — exact source-moment marginal at the higher-moment boundary

Fix arbitrary

\[
0<\beta<\frac12,\qquad \alpha>0,\qquad \gamma=1-\beta.
\]

There exists a centered symmetric real random variable `Y` such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[Y^2\exp\!\left(
\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}(2\log_2^+|Y|)^\gamma
\right)\right]<\infty,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E|Y|^{2+\delta}=\infty
\quad\text{for every }\delta>0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Moreover, `Y` can be chosen on a countable symmetric support whose nonzero atom at scale `m` has probability exactly `1/n_m`, with `n_m` a dyadic integer.

#### Construction

Let

\[
L_m:=m^{1/(1-2\beta)},\qquad
j_m:=\lceil L_m\rceil,\qquad
n_m:=2^{j_m}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Let `H(n)` be as in Section 5 and put

\[
d_*:=\frac2{\ln2},
\qquad
A_m:=n_m^{1/2}\exp\!\left[-\frac{d_*}{2}H(n_m)\right].
\tag{7.4}
\]

For all sufficiently large `m`, `A_m>1`. Start the construction at such an index `M`, increased if necessary so that

\[
\sum_{m\ge M}n_m^{-1}<1.
\]

Define

\[
\mathbb P(Y=A_m)=\mathbb P(Y=-A_m)=\frac1{2n_m},\qquad m\ge M,
\tag{7.5}
\]

and put all remaining probability mass at `0`.

This law is symmetric, hence centered whenever its first absolute moment is finite; (7.1) below implies finite second moment and therefore finite first moment.

#### Proof of the source moment

Apply Lemma 5.1 with `lambda=1` and `d=d_*`. Writing `J_m=j_m=log_2 n_m`,

\[
\log\frac{Q(A_m)}{n_m}
=
-rac{4\alpha^2}{\gamma\ln2}J_m^{1-2\beta}
+o(J_m^{1-2\beta}).
\tag{7.6}
\]

By (7.3),

\[
J_m\sim m^{1/(1-2\beta)},
\qquad
J_m^{1-2\beta}\sim m.
\]

Hence there exist constants `c>0` and `m_0` such that

\[
\frac{Q(A_m)}{n_m}\le e^{-cm},\qquad m\ge m_0.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Using (7.5),

\[
\mathbb E Q(|Y|)
=
\sum_{m\ge M}\frac{Q(A_m)}{n_m}
<\infty.
\]

This is exactly (7.1).

#### Proof that every higher polynomial moment diverges

Fix arbitrary `delta>0`. The contribution of the `m`th pair of atoms to `E|Y|^(2+delta)` is

\[
\frac{A_m^{2+\delta}}{n_m}.
\]

From (7.4),

\[
\log\left(\frac{A_m^{2+\delta}}{n_m}\right)
=
\frac\delta2\log n_m
-
\left(1+\frac\delta2\right)d_*H(n_m).
\tag{7.8}
\]

By (5.6), with `J_m=log_2 n_m`,

\[
H(n_m)=\frac{\alpha\ln2}{\gamma}J_m^\gamma+O(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\log\left(\frac{A_m^{2+\delta}}{n_m}\right)
=
\frac{\delta\ln2}{2}J_m-O(J_m^\gamma).
\tag{7.9}
\]

Since `gamma<1`, the linear term in `J_m` dominates and the right-hand side tends to `+infinity`. In particular the summands `A_m^(2+delta)/n_m` do not even tend to zero. Thus the moment series diverges, proving (7.2). ∎

### The beta=1/3, alpha=1 cubic-dyadic specialization

The simplest target frozen by the preceding report becomes especially transparent. Here

\[
\beta=\frac13,\qquad \gamma=\frac23,\qquad \alpha=1.
\]

Take exactly

\[
n_m=2^{m^3}.
\]

The integral estimate is

\[
H(n_m)=\frac{3\ln2}{2}m^2+O(1).
\]

At `d_*=2/ln2`,

\[
\log\frac{Q(A_m)}{n_m}
=
-\frac6{\ln2}m+O(1),
\tag{7.10}
\]

where

\[
Q(x)=x^2\exp\!\left(3(2\log_2^+x)^{2/3}\right).
\]

Thus the exact source moment has **ordinary geometric slack in the scale index `m`** even at the critical damping coefficient. Yet for every `delta>0`,

\[
\log\left(\frac{A_m^{2+\delta}}{n_m}\right)
=
\frac{\delta\ln2}{2}m^3-O(m^2)\to+\infty.
\]

This is the cleanest explicit marginal on which to attempt the missing dependence coupling next.
