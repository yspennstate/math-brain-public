## 4. B22 — read the failure, not the success: the exact beta=1/2 barrier in the source proof

The decisive source definitions are Theorem 2.2 and its proof.

For fixed `delta in (0,1)`, Matsui defines

```text
h_delta(n) = exp( 5 sum_{i<=log n} rho(2^i)^(2/(2+delta)) )
```

and

```text
h_d(n) = exp( -2 sum_{i=k0}^{[log n]} log(1-rho(2_*^i)) ).
```

Theorem 2.2 asks for

```text
g( n^(1/2) (h_delta(n) h_d(n)^((2+delta)/4))^(-1/delta) ) >> h_d(n).      (T2.2)
```

In the proof of Corollary 2.3(b), the exact boundary gauge is chosen as

```text
g_*(x) = exp(2 alpha sum_{i=1}^{[log x^2]} i^(-beta)),                  (G)
```

which has precisely the asymptotic growth corresponding to the displayed Corollary 2.3(b) exponential-log moment.

### 4.1 First obstruction: the fixed-p typical-block tax

Lemma 3.3 uses `p=2+delta>2` and produces dependence through the power

```text
rho^(2/p) = rho^(2/(2+delta)).
```

For a saturating dyadic envelope

```text
rho(2^i) ~ alpha i^(-beta),
```

this gives

```text
log h_delta(n) = O( L^(1-2 beta/p) ),       L=log_2 n.
```

The source's shortened logarithmic argument loses a window of size

```text
e_n = (2/delta) log h_delta(n)
      + ((2+delta)/(2delta)) log h_d(n).
```

Even ignoring the `h_d` contribution, the `h_delta` piece alone is of order

```text
L^(1-2 beta/p).
```

At the boundary gauge `(G)`, removing a terminal window of length `e_n` from

```text
sum_{i<=L} i^(-beta)
```

costs order

```text
e_n L^(-beta)
  = L^(1-beta-2beta/p).
```

For this to tend to zero one needs

```text
1 - beta - 2 beta/p < 0
```

or equivalently

```text
beta > p/(p+2).                                                    (4.1)
```

Since `p>2`, the right side is strictly bigger than `1/2`, and its infimum as `p downarrow 2` is exactly `1/2`.

This reproduces, in structural form, Matsui's explicit end-of-proof condition

```text
1 - beta(4+delta)/(2+delta) < 0,
```

because `p=2+delta`.

**Conclusion of this subroute.** No fixed-`p>2` implementation of Lemma 3.3 can by itself cross below `beta=1/2` at the exact boundary gauge. Taking `p` arbitrarily close to 2 only approaches the barrier; it does not cross it. Allowing `p=p_n downarrow 2` does not repair the exponent: the limiting power is `1-2beta>0` for every `beta<1/2`, while the constants in the moment bound also deteriorate as `delta=p-2` shrinks.

This is a proof-method obstruction, not a theorem that the CLT fails.

### 4.2 Sharper obstruction: the rho^2 term in h_d already defeats the canonical boundary gauge

There is an even cleaner obstruction that does not use `h_delta` at all.

Consider the scalar profile

```text
r(n) = min(r0, alpha (log_2(n vee 2))^(-beta))
```

with a fixed `r0<1`, so that eventually `r(n)=alpha(log_2 n)^(-beta)` and `r(n)->0`.

Let `L` be an integer and take `n=2^L`. In Matsui's definition of `h_d`, put

```text
r_i = r(2_*^i).
```

The auxiliary function `underline ell` used in `2_*^i=[2^i underline ell(2^i)]` is positive and nonincreasing. Hence it is bounded above by a constant. Therefore for some fixed integer/real shift `C` and every sufficiently large `i`,

```text
log_2(2_*^i) <= i+C,
```

and monotonicity of the exact logarithmic profile gives

```text
r_i >= alpha (i+C)^(-beta).                                      (4.2)
```

For `0<r<1`,

```text
-log(1-r) >= r + r^2/2.
```

Consequently

```text
log h_d(2^L)
  = -2 sum_{i=k0}^{L} log(1-r_i)
  >= 2 sum r_i + sum r_i^2.
```

Using (4.2), and the fact that a fixed shift changes `sum i^(-beta)` by only `O(1)` for every `beta>0`, there is a constant `C_0` such that

```text
log h_d(2^L)
 >= 2 alpha sum_{i=1}^{L} i^(-beta)
    + alpha^2 sum_{i=k0}^{L} (i+C)^(-2beta)
    - C_0.                                                       (4.3)
```

Now let

```text
A_n = n^(1/2) (h_delta(n) h_d(n)^((2+delta)/4))^(-1/delta).
```

Both `h_delta` and `h_d` are at least 1, so `A_n <= n^(1/2)`. Since `g_*` is nondecreasing,

```text
g_*(A_n) <= g_*(n^(1/2))
          = exp(2 alpha sum_{i=1}^{L} i^(-beta))
```

for `n=2^L`, up to only the harmless integer convention already built into `(G)`.

Combining this with (4.3),

```text
g_*(A_n)/h_d(n)
 <= C exp( -alpha^2 sum_{i=k0}^{L}(i+C)^(-2beta) ).               (4.4)
```

If `beta <= 1/2`, the sum on the right diverges. Hence

```text
g_*(A_n)/h_d(n) -> 0.                                            (4.5)
```

But Theorem 2.2 requires this ratio to tend to infinity.

Therefore:

> **Proposition (canonical-criterion barrier).** For an eventually exact saturating scalar profile `rho(n)=alpha(log_2 n)^(-beta)`, `beta<=1/2`, the canonical boundary gauge used in Corollary 2.3(b) cannot satisfy Matsui's Theorem 2.2 condition (2.7), for any fixed `delta>0`. In fact the ratio demanded to diverge tends to zero because of the accumulated quadratic term in `-log(1-rho)`.

This proposition is purely analytic. It does **not** by itself produce a counterexample to the CLT, because Theorem 2.2 is sufficient rather than necessary.

It also explains exactly why the line in Matsui's proof that discards `sum i^(-2beta)` is not bookkeeping noise. For `beta>1/2` that correction is summable; for `beta<=1/2` it is a genuinely diverging second-order dependence cost.

## 5. B2 — counterexample construction: the saturating rho profile is realizable, but the hard part is the sum law

The preceding proposition would be much less informative if an exact logarithmic rho profile were merely a formal sequence. Bradley's later prescribed-rate construction removes that concern.

Choose

```text
r_n = min(r0, alpha (log_2(n vee 2))^(-beta))
```

with `0<r0<1`, and define, for example,

```text
q_n=r_n/4,   s_n=r_n,
```

with any positive `t_n`. Bradley's one-dimensional existence statement quoted in Remark 1.7(e) of the 2011 paper then gives a strictly stationary process `Y` with purely nonatomic marginal and

```text
rho_Y(n)=r_n
```

for every `n`.

Thus eventual equality with the I0014 upper envelope is attainable by an actual stationary process.

### 5.1 A control construction satisfying the entire I0014 baseline

The same prescribed-rate result can be combined with an independent iid coordinate to show that the exact saturating rho profile is compatible with all of Matsui's baseline conditions and even bounded marginals.

1. Start with Bradley's `Y` above.
2. Apply a one-to-one bimeasurable coordinate map to put `Y_t` in a bounded interval, say `(0,1/4)`. Because the map is invertible coordinatewise, all sigma-fields generated by the process are unchanged, so every rho coefficient is unchanged.
3. Let `B_t` be iid Bernoulli(1/2), independent of the entire `Y` process.
4. Put `Z_t=Y_t+B_t`, and center it by subtracting its mean.

The two supports `(0,1/4)` and `(1,5/4)` are disjoint, so `Z_t` determines both `Y_t` and `B_t`; conversely `(Y_t,B_t)` determines `Z_t`. Hence the coordinate sigma-fields agree exactly.

For independent joins, maximal correlation is the maximum of the component maximal correlations (the Csaki-Fischer product rule used repeatedly in Bradley's constructions). Since the iid Bernoulli process has zero inter-time dependence,

```text
rho_Z(n)=rho_Y(n)=r_n.
```

The marginal `Z_0` is bounded, so it satisfies the exact I0014 exponential-log moment for every `alpha,beta`.

Moreover, independence of the two component processes gives

```text
Var(sum_{t=1}^n Z_t)
 = Var(sum Y_t) + Var(sum B_t)
 >= n/4,
```

so `sigma_n -> infinity`.

Thus there are genuine source-baseline processes with eventual equality

```text
rho(n)=alpha(log_2 n)^(-beta)
```

and much stronger than the required moment condition. By Ibragimov's classical `2+delta`-moment theorem, these bounded examples satisfy the CLT.

**Why this matters.** The `rho^2` obstruction in Section 4 is therefore a limitation of the canonical exact-frontier criterion, not evidence that the logarithmic rho profile itself forces nonnormality. Any successful below-half theorem must exploit distributional information in a way that (2.7) does not, while any counterexample must exploit a genuinely heavy value assignment rather than dependence-rate saturation alone.

### 5.2 What remains missing for B2

The prescribed-rate process has a purely nonatomic marginal, so invertible transformations give enormous freedom to prescribe marginal tails while preserving every rho coefficient. That suggests a new counterexample platform:

```text
prescribed rho skeleton  +  heavy invertible value map  +  independent variance floor.
```

The unresolved step is decisive: an arbitrary marginal transformation does not preserve the partial-sum law. One must choose the value map so that normalized sums have a certified non-Gaussian subsequential limit (or fail tightness/CLT) **and** the exact exponential-log moment remains finite.

The inaccessible Bradley-1980 theorem is precisely valuable because it solves this synchronization problem by construction. Recreating that synchronization from the prescribed-rate skeleton is a legitimate alternative if the 1980 constant cannot be recovered.

### 5.3 Failed direct rare-block attempt

I also tested the obvious regenerative/rare-long-block construction conceptually. It is not source-valid without a new idea. For alpha- or beta-mixing, a rare long block can often be made cheap because its occurrence probability is small. For rho-mixing, maximal correlation is an operator norm: if both separated sigma-fields can detect the same rare latent block, its rarity does not automatically make the maximal correlation small. A naive renewal-tail estimate therefore does not imply

```text
rho(n) <= alpha(log n)^(-beta).
```

This is why a Bradley/Csaki-Fischer style sigma-field construction, not a plain renewal construction, remains the more credible counterexample route.

No candidate from this route is counted toward `beta_bad`.

## 6. B28 — isolate the typical-block theorem that a successful proof would need

Matsui's proof itself cleanly separates the exceptional tail from the typical bounded part.

With `T=g^{-1}(h_d(n))`, it writes

```text
X_t = X_{t,1} + X_{t,2}
```

where `X_{t,1}` is the centered truncation at `T` and `X_{t,2}` is the centered tail. Equations (3.21)–(3.22) show

```text
sigma_{n,2}=o(sigma_n)
```

from the exact `E[X_0^2 g(|X_0|)]<infinity` tail moment. The `beta>1/2` barrier appears later, when Lemma 3.3 is applied to the **typical truncated block** `X_{t,1}` through a `p=2+delta` moment estimate and the dependence tax `rho^(2/p)`.

So the exceptional tail is not the main obstacle in the current proof architecture. The missing theorem is a stronger typical-block estimate.

### 6.1 Design equation for a replacement typical-block theorem

Suppose a replacement estimate had an effective dependence tax of the schematic form

```text
log H_theta(n) = O( sum_{i<=L} rho(2^i)^theta ),
```

with `L=log_2 n`, and suppose this tax enters the logarithmic argument of the boundary gauge by a window of the same order. Under the saturation model `rho(2^i) ~ i^(-beta)`,

```text
log H_theta(n)=O(L^(1-beta theta))
```

when `beta theta<1`. Multiplying the lost window by the derivative scale `L^(-beta)` of the boundary sum gives

```text
boundary loss = O(L^(1-beta(theta+1))).
```

For this loss to vanish, one needs

```text
beta(theta+1)>1,
```

or

```text
theta > 1/beta - 1.                                             (6.1)
```

This is a design requirement, not a claimed universal necessity theorem.

For Matsui's fixed-`p` lemma, `theta=2/p<1`, so (6.1) reproduces `beta>p/(p+2)>1/2`.

A hypothetical squared-dependence tax (`theta=2`) would make the typical-block window compatible with `beta>1/3`; a cubic tax would reach `beta>1/4`. Reaching every positive beta by this route would require arbitrarily high effective powers, or a qualitatively different cancellation that does not accumulate a power sum at all.

### 6.2 The h_d correction must also be removed or renormalized

Even `theta>1` is not enough if the proof keeps the present `h_d` on the right side unchanged. Section 4.2 shows that the `rho^2` term in

```text
-log(1-rho)
```

already beats the exact boundary gauge when `beta<=1/2`.

Therefore a genuine below-half proof has to do at least one of the following:

1. replace `h_d` by a variance comparison that cancels/renormalizes the quadratic accumulation;
2. prove that the actual source assumptions force an improvement over the worst-case saturation profile at the relevant `2_*^i` scales;
3. bypass the `g(A_n) >> h_d(n)` criterion entirely, e.g. by a martingale/orthogonal-projection approximation whose error uses multi-step products of maximal-correlation operators rather than the scalar product bound encoded in `h_d`.

This is the most concrete proof-design conclusion of the run.
