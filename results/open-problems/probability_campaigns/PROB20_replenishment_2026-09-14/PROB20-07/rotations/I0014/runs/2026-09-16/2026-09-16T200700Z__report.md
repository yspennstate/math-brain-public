## 4. Mathematical idea before code

The previous run showed that the exact source Orlicz moment itself permits a sparse heavy-tail marginal on the critical Bradley amplitude scale. The unresolved part is dependence.

This run therefore tried to separate the problem into two independent design modules:

1. synthesize the exact logarithmic maximal-correlation envelope using finite weak channels whose HGR/maximal-correlation coefficient can be computed exactly;
2. attach a scalar observable with the already-feasible heavy marginal and try to make its partial sums non-Gaussian.

The hope was that maximal correlation would **tensorize by a supremum**, so infinitely many scales could coexist without summing their rho-costs, while their partial-sum effects accumulated.

That hope is half correct: the rho-envelope can indeed be synthesized cleanly. What fails is the naive additive non-CLT mechanism. Weak channels do not provide enough variance cancellation one scale at a time, and independent scale superposition adds variances rather than producing the multiplicative variance suppression that Bradley-type constructions exploit.

## 5. B11 — dualize rho to a conditional-expectation operator

For a pair `(A,B)` with square-integrable nonconstant coordinates, the Hirschfeld–Gebelein–Rényi maximal correlation is

\[
\rho_*(A,B)=\sup\{|E[f(A)g(B)]|:\ Ef=Eg=0,\ Ef^2=Eg^2=1\}.
\]

Equivalently it is the operator norm on centered `L^2` of the conditional-expectation map

\[
T:g(B)\mapsto E[g(B)\mid A].
\]

The product theorem says that if `(A_j,B_j)` are independent pairs, then the maximal correlation between the vectors `(A_j)_j` and `(B_j)_j` equals

\[
\sup_j\rho_*(A_j,B_j).
\]

This is exactly the algebraic behavior desired for a multiscale rho construction: independent channels do not pay an additive correlation cost.

## 6. B19 — an exact one-scale finite weak channel

Fix an integer `N>=1` and an iid sequence of pairs

\[
(A_j,B_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z},
\]

independent across `j`, with

\[
\rho_*(A_j,B_j)=\varepsilon\in[0,1).
\]

Define the stationary vector process

\[
Z_k=(A_k,B_{k-N}).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal F^-_0=\sigma(Z_k:k\le0),\qquad
\mathcal F^+_r=\sigma(Z_k:k\ge r).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\rho_Z(r)=
\begin{cases}
\varepsilon,&1\le r\le N,\\
0,&r>N.
\end{cases}}
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

The past sigma-field is generated by

\[
\{A_j:j\le0\}\cup\{B_j:j\le-N\},
\]

while the future at separation `r` is generated by

\[
\{A_j:j\ge r\}\cup\{B_j:j\ge r-N\}.
\]

If `r>N`, no pair index `j` has one coordinate represented on each side, so the two sigma-fields are independent and `rho_Z(r)=0`.

If `1<=r<=N`, the only cross-linked pair factors are exactly

\[
(A_j,B_j),\qquad r-N\le j\le0.
\]

All these factors are independent across `j`; all remaining variables belong wholly to one side and are independent of the other side. By maximal-correlation tensorization, the correlation between the two product sigma-fields is the maximum of the cross-factor coefficients, namely `epsilon`. This proves (6.2). ∎

A finite-alphabet pair can be encoded injectively into one scalar coordinate without changing either sigma-field, so this is not merely a vector-valued artifact.

## 7. B1 — multiscale product synthesis of the logarithmic rho envelope

Take independent copies of the construction (6.1), indexed by scales `m`, with lengths `N_m` increasing and channel coefficients `epsilon_m` decreasing. For the product process `Z=(Z^{(m)})_m`, tensorization gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho_Z(r)=\sup_{m:N_m\ge r}\varepsilon_m.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore, choosing

\[
\varepsilon_m\le \alpha(\log_2N_m)^{-\beta}
\]

automatically yields

\[
\rho_Z(r)\le \alpha(\log_2r)^{-\beta}
\]

for every sufficiently large `r`: if `N_m>=r`, monotonicity of the target envelope gives

\[
\varepsilon_m\le \alpha(\log_2N_m)^{-\beta}
\le \alpha(\log_2r)^{-\beta}.
\]

Thus the logarithmic rho-rate itself is not the hard part. There is a very explicit independent-channel skeleton with the exact supremum tensorization needed by the source bound.

A bounded injective scalar encoding of all scale coordinates preserves the full time-coordinate sigma-fields and therefore preserves the rho coefficient. But such a bounded stationary sequence, once `sigma_n->infinity`, falls under Ibragimov's finite-`(2+delta)` moment CLT. Hence a bounded encoding cannot be the desired counterexample.

An unbounded injective scalar encoding can preserve the same rho skeleton while escaping all higher moments, but this alone says nothing about the normalized partial-sum law. The hard problem has simply moved into the observable.

## 8. Why the naive heavy observable fails

For any centered square-integrable functions `U=f(A)` and `V=g(B)`, maximal correlation gives

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|
\le
\varepsilon\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(U)\operatorname{Var}(V)}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

In the equal-variance case `Var(U)=Var(V)=v`, even the most favorable anticorrelation can only give

\[
\operatorname{Var}(U+V)
\ge 2v(1-\varepsilon).
\tag{8.2}
\]

So one weak long-range channel cannot create the strong cancellation needed to make a rare critical atom dominate a block sum. Its variance suppression is only a relative `O(epsilon)` effect.

This kills the simplest B19 implementation: put a rare heavy atom into each scale, use one weak channel at distance `N_m`, and add independent scales. Independent scale variances add, so the individual factors `(1-epsilon_m)` do **not** accumulate multiplicatively. At the critical rare-atom intensity `N_mp_m=O(1)`, the dependence correction is itself only `O(epsilon_m)` and vanishes as the required long-scale `epsilon_m->0`.

The opposite extreme also fails. Repeating a single rare latent variable through a whole long block would create the desired non-Gaussian block fluctuation, but if that latent becomes measurable from both sides of a cut then maximal correlation is `1` regardless of how rare the event is. Maximal correlation detects shared information, not merely covariance frequency.

Therefore the required architecture must be **nested/cascaded rather than an independent additive superposition**: many small channel effects have to accumulate into the slowly varying variance factor without exposing any common measurable latent variable across a distant cut. This is precisely the kind of hidden multiscale mechanism for which the unrecovered formula-bearing Bradley constructions remain relevant.

## 9. The exact blocker at retirement

The smallest frozen test remains `beta=1/3`, `alpha=1`. The missing source-level construction is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
X\text{ strictly stationary and centered},\qquad E X_0^2<\infty,\\
\sigma_n^2=\operatorname{Var}(S_n),\quad \sigma_n\to\infty,\\
\rho(n)\le(\log_2n)^{-1/3}\quad\text{eventually},\\
E\!\left[X_0^2\exp\!\left(3(2\log_2^+|X_0|)^{2/3}\right)\right]<\infty,\\
S_n/\sigma_n\not\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\end{gathered}}
\tag{9.1}
\]

No construction satisfying all five lines has been certified.

### Routes tried and why they did not close the target

1. **Bradley displayed-condition asymptotics.** The exact base-2 normalization was recovered and corrected: the source-gauge phase threshold for the literal Bradley damping coefficient is `d>=2/ln 2`, equality included below `1/2`. But the source-authentic admissible value of Bradley's construction constant was not recovered from the inaccessible theorem body.
2. **Matsui's own Theorem 2.2 route.** Earlier I0014 work isolated both the `rho^{2/(2+delta)}` boundary loss and the sharper `h_d` quadratic accumulation. For a saturating logarithmic profile the latter makes the current criterion fail below `1/2`. Criterion failure is not a CLT counterexample.
3. **Prescribed-rho / bounded coordinate transformations.** They can realize the rate cleanly, but bounded marginals fall under Ibragimov's stronger-moment theorem and hence converge normally when the baseline variance condition holds.
4. **Bradley 1983 variance-mixture idea.** It has exactly the right qualitative non-CLT shape, but the formula-bearing construction needed to test the source Orlicz cost was not recovered.
5. **Critical marginal design.** The preceding run proved an explicit marginal satisfying the exact source stretched-log moment while every `(2+delta)` moment fails. This settles marginal feasibility but not temporal dependence.
6. **This run's independent weak-channel tensorization.** It gives an exact logarithmic rho skeleton, but additive independent channels cannot create the necessary multiplicative variance suppression; a shared block latent would instead force rho close to or equal to one.

### What a successful negative solution must contain that these routes lack

A successful source-valid counterexample needs a **hierarchical hidden-state cascade** with all three properties simultaneously:

- conditional-expectation/operator norms across distant cuts bounded by the prescribed logarithmic rho envelope;
- multiplicative or recursively compounded suppression of the ordinary `sqrt(n)` bulk variance, on the scale suggested by Bradley/Matsui's products involving `1-rho`;
- a heavy observable on the already-feasible critical marginal scale whose selected block sums retain a non-Gaussian component, without making a persistent latent variable measurable from both halves.

An independent product of scale channels has the right first bullet but not the second. A deterministic/shared-block construction has the second/third shape but violates the first. The missing mechanism is the nontrivial gluing between them.

### Nearest solved/known problem and missing ingredient

Bradley 1980 proves existence of a finite-variance stationary rho-mixing non-CLT sequence with `rho(n)->0`; Bradley 1983 gives a maximal-correlation-mixing example with partial attraction to centered-normal mixtures. Matsui's Remark 2.5(ii) records Bradley counterexamples under weaker logarithmic moments. None of the accessible statements certifies the exact I0014 stretched-log moment together with the exact eventual logarithmic rho bound. The missing ingredient is the quantitative source-authentic construction linking Bradley's internal scale constants to both the strong marginal moment and Matsui's rate normalization.
