## 2. Exact random-prefix reduction retained

Let
\[
X:=|T|,\qquad \nu_n:=\Pr(X=n),
\]
and let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be iid copies of \(X\). For fixed \(k\), under the ordinary unconditioned tree put
\[
Z_j:=\ell_j(T),\qquad
N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L_k:=Z_k.
\]
The branching property gives exactly
\[
|T|=N_k+\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Put
\[
m_{k,r}:=\mathbb E L_k^r
\]
and define the \(r\)-size-biased prefix pair \((A_k,B_k)\) by
\[
\Pr(A_k=a,B_k=b)
=\frac{b^r}{m_{k,r}}\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b).
\tag{2.2}
\]
The previous checkpoint proved
\[
\boxed{\mathbb EA_k<\infty,\qquad \mathbb EB_k<\infty}
\tag{2.3}
\]
using \(\mathbb EZ_j^q<\infty\) for every fixed generation, and the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\ell_k(T_n)^r
=m_{k,r}\,
\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
Consequently fixed-level UI is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}
\longrightarrow \mathbb EB_k.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

The previous checkpoint reduced this to an averaged tail in \(A_k+B_k\). The new result below shows that the **large-prefix-shift part is automatically harmless**: any failure can only come from prefixes in which \(A_k\) is at most a constant multiple of \(1+B_k\). Thus the true unbounded endpoint variable is the random boundary count \(B_k\), not an independent heavy random shift.

# 3. NEW: deterministic fixed-depth collapse lemma

Fix \(k\ge1\). For a deterministic nonnegative integer sequence
\[
z_0=1,z_1,\ldots,z_k,
\qquad
N:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z_j,\quad L:=z_k,
\]
let
\[
C_k:=k2^k.
\]

### Lemma 3.1
If
\[
N>C_kL,
\tag{3.1}
\]
then there exists \(j<k\) such that
\[
 z_j\ge \frac{N}{C_k}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
 z_{j+1}\le \frac{z_j}{2}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof
Let
\[
M:=\max_{0\le j<k}z_j\ge N/k,
\]
and choose an index \(j_*<k\) attaining \(M\). If there were no factor-two downward jump from generation \(j_*\) through generation \(k\), then
\[
z_{j+1}>z_j/2
\]
for every \(j=j_*,\ldots,k-1\), hence
\[
L=z_k>\frac{M}{2^{k-j_*}}
\ge \frac{N}{k2^k}=\frac{N}{C_k},
\]
contradicting (3.1). Let \(j\ge j_*\) be the first factor-two downward jump. Before this jump every previous step loses a factor strictly smaller than 2, so
\[
z_j\ge M/2^k\ge N/(k2^k)=N/C_k.
\]
This is (3.2). \(\square\)

The additive `+1` used below is only to make the good region convenient at small values; the deterministic lemma itself needs no asymptotics.

# 4. NEW: bad prefix geometry is exponentially light under the \(r\)-size-biased law

Choose a small \(t>0\). Since \(\mathbb E\xi=1\),
\[
\log\mathbb E e^{-t\xi}+t/2=-t/2+o(t)<0.
\]
Therefore there is \(\gamma>0\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_m\le m/2\right)
\le e^{-\gamma m}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Define the ordinary prefix-collapse event
\[
H_k:=\{N_k>C_k(1+L_k)\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
On \(H_k\), Lemma 3.1 supplies a generation \(j<k\) with
\[
Z_j\ge N_k/C_k,
\qquad Z_{j+1}\le Z_j/2.
\]
Conditioning on \(Z_j\) and using (4.1), a union bound over the fixed \(k\) generations gives constants \(c_k,C_k'<\infty\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(H_k,\ N_k\ge x)\le C_k'e^{-c_kx},
\qquad x\ge1.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
No upper-tail assumption on \(\xi\) is used here; this is a one-sided lower-deviation fact.

Now pass to the \(r\)-size-biased law (2.2). On \(H_k\),
\[
L_k\le N_k/C_k,
\]
so \(L_k^r\le N_k^r\). Multiplying the exponentially small tail in (4.3) by this polynomial weight still leaves an exponential tail. Thus for some \(\delta_k>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[e^{\delta_k(A_k+B_k)};\ H_k\right]<\infty
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
under the tilted prefix law. Here `\(H_k\)` is identified with the corresponding event \(\{A_k>C_k(1+B_k)\}\) under (2.2).

This is stronger than the previously known first-moment statement (2.3): **the part of the tilted prefix law where the prefix shift is much larger than the boundary count has an exponential moment.**

# 5. Lattice recoding by excess total progeny

Every ordinary tree size is \(1\pmod h\). Define the excess total progeny
\[
U:=\frac{X-1}{h}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
\qquad
u_{1+hM}=u_M:=\Pr(U=M).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Earlier verified PROB20 work established that \(u\) is locally subexponential on its eventual span-one support:
\[
\frac{u_{M+1}}{u_M}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{u_M^{*b}}{u_M}\to b
\quad\text{for each fixed }b\ge1.
\tag{5.2}
\]
This property is recorded in the preceding checkpoint

commit `fb8cee83b293cf5d8ce8dcfcc49867f9fadeb8de`, Section 7, and in earlier PROB20 reports cited there.

For every feasible prefix pair \((A_k,B_k)=(a,b)\),
\[
a+b-1\equiv0\pmod h.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Indeed, the \(a\) vertices strictly above level \(k\) have exactly \(a+b-1\) children in the exposed prefix, and the sum of their offspring counts is divisible by \(h\).

Thus define the nonnegative integer shift
\[
C:=\frac{A_k+B_k-1}{h}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
If \(n=1+hM\), then exactly
\[
A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n
\iff
\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}U_i=M-C.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Consequently the ratio in (2.5) is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}\right],
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
with the convention \(u^{*b}_m=0\) for \(m<0\).

# 6. NEW: exponential domination for shifted convolution ratios

A local Kesten bound for a locally subexponential law says that for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is \(K_\varepsilon<\infty\) such that
\[
u^{*b}_m\le K_\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)^b u_m
\tag{6.1}
\]
for every \(b\ge1\) and all sufficiently large \(m\). This is the lattice form of Asmussen–Foss–Korshunov, Proposition 4; their proof gives the exponential-in-count domination used in random-sum dominated convergence:

<https://arxiv.org/html/1303.4709> (Proposition 4 and Theorem 2).

The long-tail relation \(u_{M+1}/u_M\to1\) also yields, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le K_\varepsilon'(1+\varepsilon)^c,
\qquad 0\le c\le M,
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
for all large \(M\).

For completeness, when \(M-c\) stays above a fixed large index, (6.2) is obtained by iterating the adjacent ratio bound. When \(M-c\) lies in a fixed initial set, \(c=M-O(1)\); Dwass gives \(\nu_n=a_n/n\), while the already proved \(-\log a_n=o(n)\) implies \(-\log u_M=o(M)\). Hence \((1+\varepsilon)^M u_M\to\infty\), which controls the finite initial set as well.

Combining (6.1) and (6.2), and enlarging the constant to cover bounded \(M-c\), gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le K_\varepsilon''(1+\varepsilon)^{b+c},
\qquad b\ge1,\ 0\le c\le M.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
For every fixed \((b,c)\), (5.2) gives the pointwise limit
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}\to b.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

# 7. NEW theorem: the catastrophic-prefix part always has the correct limit

Let
\[
H:=\{A_k>C_k(1+B_k)\},
\qquad
G:=H^c.
\tag{7.1}
\]
By (4.4), the tilted law restricted to \(H\) has an exponential moment in \(A_k+B_k\), hence also in \(B_k+C\), because from (5.4)
\[
C\le A_k+B_k.
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) so small that the right side of (6.3) is integrable on \(H\). Then dominated convergence with (6.4) gives:

### Theorem 7.1 — automatic bad-geometry convergence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_H\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E[B_k;H].
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Thus **no counterexample can be caused by a prefix that is large because an earlier generation collapses to a much smaller boundary generation.** Such prefixes are exponentially rare even after the \(r\)-size bias, and the standard local Kesten domination is fully adequate for them.

This conclusion uses the exponential Kesten factor only on the exponentially light piece of the prefix law, where it is legitimate. It does **not** assume an exponential moment of the full \(B_k\) law.

# 8. NEW: exact reduction to balanced prefixes and a count-only tail obstruction

On the good geometry event \(G\),
\[
A_k\le C_k(1+B_k),
\tag{8.1}
\]
so from (5.4) there is a fixed constant \(D_k<\infty\) with
\[
\boxed{
0\le C\le D_k(1+B_k)
\qquad\text{on }G.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

For any fixed \(J\), the set of possible integer pairs \((A_k,B_k)\) on
\[
G\cap\{B_k\le J\}
\]
is finite, because (8.1) bounds \(A_k\). Therefore fixed-pair convergence (6.4) gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_G\mathbf1_{\{B_k\le J\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E[B_k;G,B_k\le J].
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

Combining (7.2) and (8.3) yields the sharpest current all-level endpoint reduction.

### Theorem 8.1 — balanced-prefix equivalence
For every fixed \(k\), the desired UI is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{J\to\infty}\limsup_{M\to\infty}
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_G\mathbf1_{\{B_k>J\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]
=0,
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
where on the remaining region
\[
0\le C\le D_k(1+B_k),
\qquad \mathbb EB_k<\infty.
\tag{8.5}
\]

This improves the previous checkpoint's tail condition in \(A_k+B_k\). The separate heavy random shift has disappeared from the unresolved part: **after an exponentially light piece is removed, the shift is deterministically only linear in the random count.**

Equivalently, the universal every-fixed-level conjecture will follow from a branching-specific, shifted finite-mean random-sum theorem for the excess-total-progeny law \(u\): for the explicit \(B_k\) laws arising from (2.2), an averaged local Kesten estimate must hold uniformly for shifts \(c=O(1+b)\).

At the root, \(A_1=1\), so the balanced condition is automatic. Thus (8.4) specializes exactly to the previously isolated weighted root mixture rather than introducing any new root assumption.

# 9. Why this is useful for the all-level problem

Before the random-prefix reduction, a higher-level failure could in principle have been blamed on two different unbounded objects:

1. a very large exposed prefix size \(N_k\), and
2. a large boundary width \(L_k\).

Theorem 7.1 removes the first mechanism whenever it is disproportionate to the second. If \(N_k\gg L_k\), a fixed-depth tree must contain a macroscopic factor-two downward generation jump; that has exponential lower-deviation cost. After the \(r\)-size bias, this part still has an exponential moment and is covered by ordinary local-subexponential Kesten domination.

Therefore a genuine every-level obstruction must come from **balanced large prefixes** in which
\[
N_k=O(1+L_k).
\]
In excess-total-progeny coordinates, this is precisely the shifted random-convolution window
\[
 u^{*b}_{M-c}/u_M,
\qquad c=O(1+b),
\]
averaged against a finite-mean count law.

This justifies, rather than merely heuristically suggests, the earlier branching-specific target
\[
 u^{*L}_{m-O_k(L)}\lesssim L u_m
\]
on the only prefix region that can still matter. A global such pointwise inequality remains stronger than necessary; the exact obligation is the averaged tail (8.4).
