## 1. Exact target, source scope, and recovery

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,
\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree conditioned on total size \(n\), along every admissible \(n\). For every fixed \(k\ge1\), the target is whether
\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]
is uniformly integrable.

Write
\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad p:=\frac q{q-1},\qquad
\pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d),\qquad
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Critical nondegeneracy gives \(0<\pi_0<1\), positive mass above 1, and all sufficiently large admissible tree sizes lie in the residue class \(1\pmod h\).

Primary source: S. The source explicitly conjectures the moment statement for noninteger \(r>0\), and identifies \(\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty\) as the finite-limit regime of interest.

## 2. Cumulative exact identities retained

Let
\[
D_n:=\ell_1(T_n),\qquad
S_m:=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_m,
\qquad
a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\qquad \lambda_n:=-\log a_n.
\]
The exact root formula is
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)
=\frac n{n-1}d\pi_dR_{n,d},
\qquad
R_{n,d}:=\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently, if \(Q_{n,1}\) is the law of one offspring coordinate under \(S_n=n-1\), then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}\mathbb E_{Q_{n,1}}[X^q;X>K].
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For fixed level \(k\), under the ordinary tree write
\[
N:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L:=Z_k,\qquad m:=n-N.
\]
The exact prefix density is
\[
\boxed{
W_n=\frac{nL}{m}
\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L)}{a_n}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Retained denominator and escape results from earlier verified reports:
\[
\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right),
\tag{2.4}
\]
and for every fixed \(k\) there exists a deterministic \(c_n=o(n)\) such that
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
\tag{2.5}
\]
The earlier Palm/renewal machinery also established an exact degree-\(d\) Palm law, renewal localization near renewal length \(s\asymp d\), and the self-normalized inequality used again below.

# 3. NEW: a one-sided entropy lemma without the bounded-function \(KD\) loss

The previous root entropy estimate used a two-sided Bernstein-type entropy bound, which paid a term proportional to the truncation level times the relative entropy. At this endpoint that is unnecessarily expensive. The mean constraint allows a strictly one-sided estimate.

### Lemma 3.1 — one-sided entropy deficit

Let \(P,Q\) be probability laws and let
\[
D:=D(Q\|P)<\infty.
\]
If \(f\ge0\) and \(\mathbb E_Pf^2<\infty\), then
\[
\boxed{
(\mathbb E_Pf-\mathbb E_Qf)_+
\le \sqrt{2D\,\mathbb E_Pf^2}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof
For \(t>0\), the variational formula for relative entropy gives
\[
D\ge -t\,\mathbb E_Q(f-\mathbb E_Pf)
-\log\mathbb E_Pe^{-t(f-\mathbb E_Pf)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
For \(u\ge0\),
\[
e^{-u}\le1-u+u^2/2.
\]
Hence, writing \(m=\mathbb E_Pf\) and \(s_2=\mathbb E_Pf^2\),
\[
\mathbb E_Pe^{-tf}
\le1-tm+t^2s_2/2,
\]
and therefore
\[
\log\mathbb E_Pe^{-t(f-m)}
\le tm+\log(1-tm+t^2s_2/2)
\le t^2s_2/2.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb E_Pf-\mathbb E_Qf
\le D/t+ts_2/2.
\]
Optimizing at \(t=\sqrt{2D/s_2}\) proves (3.1). \(\square\)

The point is that there is **no term of order \(\|f\|_\infty D\)**.

# 4. NEW: a sharper root low-window theorem at the full entropy scale

The full conditioned allocation law
\[
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1)
\]
has
\[
D(Q_n\|\pi^{\otimes n})=\lambda_n.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Relative-entropy tensorization gives
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_{n,1}\|\pi)\le\lambda_n/n.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Moreover
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,1}}X=1-1/n,
\qquad \mathbb E_\pi X=1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

For \(K\ge1\), put
\[
f_K(x):=x\mathbf1_{\{x\le K\}},
\qquad
V(K):=\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le K].
\]
Since
\[
\mathbb E_Q[X;X>K]-\mathbb E_\pi[X;X>K]
=\mathbb E_\pi f_K-\mathbb E_Qf_K-1/n,
\]
Lemma 3.1 yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Q[X;X>K]
\le
\mathbb E_\pi[\xi;\xi>K]
+\sqrt{2D(Q\|\pi)V(K)}.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Define the truncated-variance modulus
\[
v(x):=x^{q-2}V(x).
\tag{4.5}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\) and \(q<2\),
\[
\boxed{v(x)\to0.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Indeed, for fixed \(M\), the contribution of \(\xi\le M\) vanishes after multiplication by \(x^{q-2}\), while on \(M<\xi\le x\),
\[
x^{q-2}\xi^2\le \xi^q.
\]
Let
\[
v^*(K):=\sup_{x\ge K}v(x)\downarrow0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

For \(1\le K<B\), the elementary pointwise inequality
\[
x^q\mathbf1_{\{K<x\le B\}}
\le
K^{q-1}x\mathbf1_{\{x>K\}}
+(q-1)\int_K^B t^{q-2}x\mathbf1_{\{x>t\}}\,dt
\tag{4.8}
\]
combined with (4.4) gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,1}}[X^q;K<X\le B]
\le
\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]
+C\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n}{n}\,B^qv^*(K)}.
}
\tag{4.9}
\]
The integral estimate is
\[
K^{q-1}\sqrt{V(K)}
+\int_K^B t^{q-2}\sqrt{V(t)}dt
\le C B^{q/2}\sqrt{v^*(K)}.
\]

### Theorem 4.2 — entropy-scale root UI

Let \(B_n\to\infty\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\sup_n B_n^q\frac{\lambda_n}{n}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.10}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[D_n^r;K<D_n\le B_n]=0.
}
\tag{4.11}
\]

In particular the **maximal canonical choice**
\[
\boxed{
B_n:=\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q}
}
\tag{4.12}
\]
is valid. This improves the preceding entropy theorem, which required the stronger little-o condition \(B_n^q(\lambda_n+1)/n\to0\).

Using only (2.4), the worst-case polynomial exponent is unchanged,
\[
B_n=n^{2r/(1+r)^2-o(1)},
\]
but the endpoint scale is now available with no spare factor. This is important for matching to the Palm estimate.

# 5. NEW: Palm truncation extends below the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale under an exact tail criterion

The preceding correction addendum identified the key missing step: extend the truncated-second-moment Palm argument to
\[
d=b_n n^{1/q},\qquad b_n\downarrow0.
\]
The proof does extend, with a precise condition determined by the \(q\)-moment tail.

Set
\[
Y:=(\xi-1)_+,
\qquad
A_Y(x):=\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>x],
\]
\[
V_Y(x):=\mathbb E[Y^2;Y\le x],
\qquad
v_Y(x):=x^{q-2}V_Y(x).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
\bar v_Y(x):=\sup_{y\ge x}v_Y(y)\downarrow0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Under the degree-\(d\) Palm law the remaining \(N=n-1\) offspring variables are iid \(\pi\) conditioned on
\[
S_N=N-d.
\]
The exact identity from the preceding report is
\[
N_0=d+\sum_{i=1}^NY_i.
\tag{5.3}
\]
The renewal-switching argument gives
\[
R_{n,d}\,\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]\le C.
\tag{5.4}
\]
On the event
\[
\sum_{i=1}^NY_i\ge N\mathbb EY-\eta d,
\tag{5.5}
\]
renewal localization at lengths \(s\asymp d\) and the extra \(d\) zeros in (5.3) give
\[
nH_{n,d}(N_0)\ge c\exp(c d^2/n)
\tag{5.6}
\]
for supported \(d\to\infty\) with \(d\le c_0n\), where \(c_0>0\) is fixed sufficiently small.

The only question is the unconditional probability of the lower-deviation complement of (5.5). Truncate at \(cd\). Since
\[
\mathbb E[Y;Y>cd]
\le C d^{1-q}A_Y(cd),
\]
we have
\[
\frac{N\mathbb E[Y;Y>cd]}d
\le
C\frac{nA_Y(cd)}{d^q}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Thus whenever
\[
\boxed{
\frac{nA_Y(cd)}{d^q}\le \rho_0
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
for a sufficiently small constant \(\rho_0\), truncation loses at most a fixed small fraction of the desired deviation. For the truncated nonnegative variable, the elementary negative-mgf estimate gives
\[
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^NY_i-N\mathbb EY\le-\eta d
\right)
\le
\exp\left[-c\frac{d^2}{nV_Y(cd)}\right]
=
\exp\left[-c\frac{d^q}{n v_Y(cd)}\right].
\tag{5.9}
\]
No local limit theorem is used.

Multiplying the Palm bad-event probability by \(R_{n,d}\) cancels its conditional denominator, at cost \(a_n^{-1}=e^{\lambda_n}\). Equations (5.4)–(5.9) therefore give:

### Theorem 5.1 — below-natural Palm bound

There are constants \(c,C,c_0,\rho_0>0\), depending only on \(\pi\), such that for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\) and every supported \(d\to\infty\) with \(d\le c_0n\) satisfying (5.8),
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C e^{-c d^2/n}
+
\exp\left\{
\lambda_n-c\frac{d^q}{n v_Y(cd)}
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.10}
\]

The novelty relative to the preceding checkpoint is the range: **(5.10) is not restricted to fixed multiples of \(n^{1/q}\)**. It is valid below the natural scale whenever the actual \(q\)-tail satisfies (5.8). This is exactly the corrected report's stated bridge obligation.

# 6. NEW: exact overlap criterion closing the root case for a broad arbitrary-law subclass

Take the maximal entropy cutoff from (4.12):
\[
B_n:=\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
\frac{n}{B_n^q}=\lambda_n+1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Define
\[
A_n^*:=A_Y(cB_n),
\qquad
v_n^*:=\bar v_Y(cB_n).
\tag{6.3}
\]

For every \(d\ge B_n\), monotonicity of \(A_Y\) and \(d^q\) gives
\[
\frac{nA_Y(cd)}{d^q}
\le
(\lambda_n+1)A_n^*.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Similarly,
\[
\frac{d^q}{n v_Y(cd)}
\ge
\frac{1}{(\lambda_n+1)v_n^*}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Hence the Palm theorem overlaps exactly with the entropy theorem provided
\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_n+1)A_Y(cB_n)\to0
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n(\lambda_n+1)\bar v_Y(cB_n)\to0.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
Under (6.6), the tail-mean hypothesis (5.8) holds uniformly for all \(d\ge B_n\). Under (6.7), the exponent in (5.10) dominates \(\lambda_n\), so
\[
\sup_{B_n\le d\le c_n}R_{n,d}=O(1),
\tag{6.8}
\]
where \(c_n=o(n)\) is the existing large-width escape cutoff from (2.5).

Combining Theorem 4.2 below \(B_n\), (6.8) above \(B_n\), and the large-width escape theorem gives:

### Corollary 6.1 — rigorous conditional root closure

If (6.6)–(6.7) hold, then
\[
\boxed{
\{D_n^r\}_n\text{ is uniformly integrable.}
}
\tag{6.9}
\]

Indeed, on \(B_n<d<c_n\),
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;B_n<D_n<c_n]
\le C\sum_{d>B_n}d^q\pi_d\to0,
\]
and the two complementary ranges were already handled.

This is **not** the universal theorem: finite \(q\)-moment alone gives \(A_Y(x),\bar v_Y(x)\to0\), but does not presently force the products with \(\lambda_n\) in (6.6)–(6.7) to vanish.

However it sharply characterizes the surviving obstruction. If root UI fails, then along some subsequence at least one of
\[
(\lambda_n+1)A_Y\!\left(c(n/(\lambda_n+1))^{1/q}\right)
\tag{6.10}
\]
or
\[
\lambda_n(\lambda_n+1)
\bar v_Y\!\left(c(n/(\lambda_n+1))^{1/q}\right)
\tag{6.11}
\]
fails to tend to zero. A counterexample must synchronize a very small central atom with either an exceptionally slow \(q\)-tail decay or an exceptionally slow truncated-variance decay at the precise entropy scale.

This is substantially narrower than the earlier statement that the obstruction merely lies somewhere below \(n^{1/q}\).

## 7. Relation to the every-fixed-level problem

No root-only result is promoted to full closure. The exact prefix density remains (2.3), and the corrected stopping-prefix theorem from the preceding checkpoint remains the valid all-level low-window input:
\[
N\le C_k(1+L)+A_k(\lambda_n+\log n+1)
\tag{7.1}
\]
outside a contribution negligible for the target fractional moment.

The earlier all-level entropy criterion therefore remains valid. The new one-sided entropy lemma suggests that its \(KD\)-type loss can potentially be removed as well, but a direct application requires a suitable one-sided first-moment identity for the random boundary count \(L\) under the stopped conditioned allocation. That identity has not yet been proved in a noncircular form in this run.

The alternate all-level sufficient target remains the branching-specific shifted linear convolution inequality
\[
\boxed{
 u^{*L}_{m-O_k(L)}\lesssim L u_m,
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
for the excess-total-progeny law \(u\), on the non-negligible prefix region. Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds are still exponentially too expensive in \(L\).

Thus Corollary 6.1 is a genuine root theorem for a broad subclass, but **the universal root case and the universal every-fixed-level case remain open**.

## 8. Failed / insufficient routes retained

1. Bounded expectations do not imply UI.
2. Fixed-shift strong ratios do not control the moving window.
3. Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds cost an exponential factor in the convolution count.
4. Total variation or relative entropy without the exact mean constraint cannot control the endpoint \(q\)-moment.
5. The previous two-sided entropy estimate loses a \(KD\) term; Lemma 3.1 removes it only because the exact conditioned mean converts the needed tail bound into a **downward** deficit of a nonnegative truncation.
6. The Palm truncation cannot simply be pushed below \(n^{1/q}\) without checking the tail-mean condition (5.8). The new theorem records the exact condition rather than assuming it.
7. Conditions (6.6)–(6.7) are sufficient but are not known to follow universally from \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\).
8. No lacunary counterexample satisfying the finite-\(q\) moment condition has been certified. The F0058 missing-upper-tail-mean mechanism remains blocked at this endpoint by the moment budget and canonical recentering.

## 11. Next attack

The highest-value next step is now a **weighted bridge theorem** designed to remove the pointwise products in (6.6)–(6.7). Instead of requiring \(R_{n,d}=O(1)\) for every \(d\ge B_n\), use the Palm identity only after summing with the target weights \(d^q\pi_d\). The bad Palm event is a lower deviation of
\[
Y_1+\cdots+Y_{n-1},
\]
and the same \(q\)-tail \(A_Y\) which obstructs the pointwise truncation is also the quantity available to pay for the weighted sum. A successful estimate of the form
\[
\sum_{d\ge B_n}d^q\pi_dR_{n,d}
\lesssim
\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>cB_n]+o(1)
\]
would close the root problem without assuming (6.6)–(6.7). Only after obtaining such a weighted root inequality should the same argument be transplanted behind the fixed-level prefix.
