## 2. Exact all-level random-prefix reduction retained

Let
\[
X:=|T|,\qquad \nu_n:=\Pr(X=n),
\]
and let \(X_1,X_2,\dots\) be iid copies of \(X\). For fixed \(k\), under the ordinary unconditioned tree put
\[
Z_j:=\ell_j(T),\qquad
N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L_k:=Z_k.
\]
The branching property gives
\[
X=N_k+\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Let
\[
m_{k,r}:=\mathbb E L_k^r
\]
and define the \(r\)-size-biased prefix pair by
\[
\Pr(A_k=a,B_k=b)
=\frac{b^r}{m_{k,r}}\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b).
\tag{2.2}
\]
The preceding verified checkpoint proves
\[
\mathbb EA_k<\infty,\qquad \mathbb EB_k<\infty,
\tag{2.3}
\]
and the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E\ell_k(T_n)^r
=m_{k,r}\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Thus, because \(\ell_k(T_n)\Rightarrow\ell_k(\widehat T)\) and the limiting \(r\)-moment is finite under \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\), fixed-level UI is equivalent to
\[
\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}
\longrightarrow \mathbb EB_k.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The same checkpoint proves that the catastrophic prefix geometry
\[
H_k:=\{A_k>C_k(1+B_k)\},\qquad C_k=k2^k,
\]
has an exponential moment under the tilted prefix law, so its contribution already has the correct limit. On the balanced complement \(G_k=H_k^c\), after recoding the total-progeny lattice as below, the shift is bounded by a constant multiple of \(1+B_k\).

## 3. Excess-total-progeny law and the previous remaining endpoint

Set
\[
U:=\frac{X-1}{h}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad
u_{1+hM}=u_M:=\Pr(U=M).
\tag{3.1}
\]
For every feasible prefix pair,
\[
C:=\frac{A_k+B_k-1}{h}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
\]
and for \(n=1+hM\),
\[
A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n
\iff
\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}U_i=M-C.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Hence the ratio in (2.5) is exactly
\[
\mathbb E\left[\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}\right].
\tag{3.3}
\]
Earlier verified work gives the fixed-count local-subexponential relations
\[
\frac{u_{M+1}}{u_M}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{u_M^{*b}}{u_M}\to b
\quad\text{for every fixed }b,
\tag{3.4}
\]
and
\[
-\log u_M=o(M).
\tag{3.5}
\]
The 22:58 addendum also proved by a Laplace/Chernoff diagonal argument that there exists some unspecified \(\beta_M\to\infty\), \(\beta_M=o(M)\), for which
\[
\sup_{b\ge\beta_M}\sup_{c\ge0}
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}\to0.
\tag{3.6}
\]
The unresolved contribution was therefore mesoscopic: \(b\to\infty\), \(b=o(M)\), with \(c=O_k(b)\).

This run makes that cutoff intrinsic and quantitative, and derives its scale directly from the offspring \(q\)-moment and infinite variance.

# 4. NEW: offspring pgf remainder lies strictly between the \(q\)- and quadratic scales

Let
\[
f(s):=\mathbb E s^\xi,
\qquad
R(y):=f(1-y)-(1-y),\qquad 0<y<1.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Criticality gives \(f(1)=f'(1)=1\), hence
\[
R(y)=\mathbb E\big[(1-y)^\xi-1+\xi y\big]\ge0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — finite \(q\)-moment upper scale
\[
\boxed{R(y)=o(y^q)\qquad(y\downarrow0).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
For integer \(m\ge0\), put
\[
r_m(y):=(1-y)^m-1+my.
\]
Taylor's theorem and the trivial bound \((1-y)^m\le1\) give
\[
0\le r_m(y)\le \min\{\tfrac12m^2y^2,my\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
If \(my\le1\), then
\[
\frac{r_m(y)}{y^q}\le C m^q(my)^{2-q}\le C m^q.
\]
If \(my>1\), then
\[
\frac{r_m(y)}{y^q}\le m^q(my)^{1-q}\le m^q.
\]
For each fixed \(m\), \(r_m(y)/y^q\to0\), because \(2-q>0\). Dominated convergence with \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\) proves (4.3). \(\square\)

### Lemma 4.2 — infinite-variance lower scale
\[
\boxed{\frac{R(y)}{y^2}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad(y\downarrow0).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof
For each fixed cutoff \(K\), Fatou/Taylor on \(\{\xi\le K\}\) gives
\[
\liminf_{y\downarrow0}\frac{R(y)}{y^2}
\ge \frac12\mathbb E[\xi(\xi-1);\xi\le K].
\]
Since \(\mathbb E\xi=1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty\), the truncated factorial second moment tends to infinity as \(K\to\infty\). \(\square\)

Thus the critical pgf defect satisfies the genuinely two-sided non-regular-variation information
\[
y^2\ll R(y)\ll y^q.
\tag{4.6}
\]
No regular variation or domain-of-attraction hypothesis is used.

# 5. NEW: exact Laplace exponent window for critical total progeny

Let \(T(z)=\mathbb E z^X\) be the total-progeny pgf. It satisfies the standard branching equation
\[
T(z)=z f(T(z)).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Let
\[
\phi(t):=\mathbb E e^{-tU},\qquad
K(t):=-\log\phi(t).
\tag{5.2}
\]
Set \(s=t/h\) and
\[
y:=1-T(e^{-s}).
\]
From (5.1),
\[
1-y=e^{-s}f(1-y),
\]
so
\[
e^s=\frac{f(1-y)}{1-y}
=1+\frac{R(y)}{1-y}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Hence, as \(t\downarrow0\) (equivalently \(y\downarrow0\)),
\[
s\sim R(y).
\tag{5.4}
\]
There is also an exact transform identity:
\[
\phi(t)
=\mathbb E e^{-s(X-1)}
=e^sT(e^{-s})
=f(1-y).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Because \(R(y)=o(y^q)=o(y)\),
\[
1-\phi(t)=1-f(1-y)=y-R(y)\sim y,
\]
and thus
\[
K(t)\sim y.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Combining (4.3)–(4.5) with (5.4)–(5.6) yields the new intrinsic window:
\[
\boxed{
\frac{K(t)}{t^{1/q}}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{K(t)}{\sqrt t}\longrightarrow0
\qquad(t\downarrow0).
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Equivalently, for
\[
\psi(t):=\frac{K(t)}t,
\]
\[
\boxed{
 t^{(q-1)/q}\psi(t)\to\infty,
\qquad
 \sqrt t\,\psi(t)\to0.
}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Interpretation: without assuming any stable law or regular variation, the Laplace exponent of the critical total-progeny excess lies strictly between the finite-variance \(t^{1/2}\) scale and the weakest scale permitted by the offspring \(q\)-moment, \(t^{1/q}\). This gives a distribution-specific natural forest-count scale \(1/K(1/M)\), but does not assert that \(K\) is regularly varying.

# 6. NEW: denominator-adapted large-forest cutoff

Put
\[
L_M:=-\log u_M=o(M)
\tag{6.1}
\]
and choose the explicit sequence
\[
t_M:=\sqrt{\frac{L_M+1}{M}}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Then \(t_M\downarrow0\) and
\[
L_M=o(Mt_M),\qquad Mt_M\to\infty.
\tag{6.3}
\]
For every \(b\ge1\) and every \(c\ge0\), if \(c>M\) then \(u^{*b}_{M-c}=0\). Otherwise
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^{*b}_{M-c}
&\le \Pr(U_1+\cdots+U_b\le M)\\
&\le e^{t_M M}\phi(t_M)^b,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the first `nu` in this display is to be read as \(u\): the intended quantity is \(u^{*b}_{M-c}\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le
\exp\{L_M+t_M M-bK(t_M)\}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
\beta_M:=
\left\lceil\frac{2Mt_M}{K(t_M)}\right\rceil
=\left\lceil\frac{2M}{\psi(t_M)}\right\rceil.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
For \(b\ge\beta_M\), (6.4) gives
\[
\sup_{c\ge0}
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le \exp\{L_M-Mt_M+O(K(t_M))\}\to0.
\tag{6.6}
\]
(The ceiling only changes the exponent by at most one \(K(t_M)=o(Mt_M)\).)

The new Laplace window (5.8) gives quantitative information on \(\beta_M\). Since \(\sqrt{t_M}\,\psi(t_M)\to0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\beta_M}{M\sqrt{t_M}}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
In particular \(\beta_M\to\infty\). Since \(t_M^{(q-1)/q}\psi(t_M)\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
\beta_M=o\!\left(Mt_M^{(q-1)/q}\right)=o(M).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
With the explicit choice (6.2), this reads
\[
M^{3/4}(L_M+1)^{1/4}\ll \beta_M
\ll_{\!o}
M^{(q+1)/(2q)}(L_M+1)^{(q-1)/(2q)}.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Here the right-hand relation means the ratio tends to zero; no power-law asymptotic is claimed.

Thus the earlier abstract diagonal cutoff (3.6) is replaced by an **intrinsic denominator-adapted cutoff** calculated from the actual total-progeny Laplace exponent and the actual local denominator \(u_M\).

# 7. NEW: uniform linear ratio below a growing diagonal cutoff

Fix the balanced-prefix constant \(D_k\) such that on \(G_k\),
\[
0\le C\le D_k(1+B_k).
\tag{7.1}
\]
For each fixed integer \(J\), (3.4) and the long-tail ratio imply
\[
\max_{1\le b\le J}
\max_{0\le c\le D_k(1+b)}
\left|
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{b u_M}-1
\right|\to0.
\tag{7.2}
\]
There are only finitely many \((b,c)\) in (7.2). Since \(\beta_M\to\infty\), a standard diagonal selection therefore gives a deterministic sequence
\[
\alpha_M\to\infty,
\qquad
\alpha_M<\beta_M/2,
\tag{7.3}
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{1\le b\le\alpha_M}
\sup_{0\le c\le D_k(1+b)}
\left|
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{b u_M}-1
\right|\to0.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(J\),
\[
\limsup_{M\to\infty}
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{G_k}\mathbf1_{\{J<B_k\le\alpha_M\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]
\le (1+o(1))\mathbb E[B_k;B_k>J],
\tag{7.5}
\]
and the right side tends to zero as \(J\to\infty\) because \(\mathbb EB_k<\infty\).

At the other end, (6.6) gives directly
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{G_k}\mathbf1_{\{B_k\ge\beta_M\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]\to0.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
No moment of \(B_k\) is needed for (7.6).

## 9. Why this still does not prove UI

The tempting next step would be a global bound
\[
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}\le Cb
\quad\text{uniformly for }b=o(M),\ c=O(b),
\tag{9.1}
\]
but neither the current proof nor the generic local-subexponential theory establishes (9.1) under only the present hypotheses. Generic local Kesten bounds are exponential in \(b\), which is too costly for a random count known only to have finite mean.

The classical big-jump-domain literature (for example Denisov–Dieker–Shneer, *Annals of Probability* 2008, arXiv:math/0703265) develops uniform local one-big-jump asymptotics once distribution-specific big-jump-domain hypotheses/natural scales are verified. Their abstract explicitly treats local analogues of \(\Pr(S_n>x)\sim n\Pr(X>x)\), but those hypotheses are not being assumed or silently imported here:

<https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703265>

Similarly, standard local randomly stopped-sum results use a light-tailed/exponential-moment counting law to dominate the exponential Kesten factor. Our branching-derived \(B_k\) is guaranteed only a finite first moment at this endpoint. Thus no literature theorem checked in this run supplies (8.2) automatically.

The new Laplace theorem (5.7) is useful precisely because it identifies the count scale where a bespoke critical-GW argument must replace generic Kesten domination.

## 10. Counterexample implications

A lacunary counterexample, if it exists, must now satisfy all of the following simultaneously:

- it cannot rely on inaccessible future atoms merely shifting the mean; earlier finite-\(q\) tail-mean estimates rule out that F0058-style mechanism at the needed moment scale;
- it cannot rely on a pure lattice obstruction; a fixed finite core and the span reduction remove that;
- it cannot rely on catastrophic prefix collapse for \(k>1\); that region has an exponential moment under the \(r\)-size-biased prefix law;
- it cannot place the offending boundary count above \(\beta_M\); the Laplace bound (6.6) kills that region pointwise;
- it cannot use a bounded or sufficiently slowly growing count below \(\alpha_M\); the fixed-count local ratios are uniformly linear there.

Therefore any genuine failure must synchronize a lacunary local-probability valley with boundary counts in the intrinsic corridor (8.1). No certified example achieving this was found in this run.

## 12. Next attack

The strongest next proof attack is to work directly at the Laplace natural scale rather than continue enlarging generic Kesten windows. Two complementary routes are concrete:

1. **Weighted local one-big-jump estimate.** Prove, only after averaging against the actual \(r\)-size-biased boundary-count law, an inequality of the schematic form
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{\alpha_M<B_k<\beta_M\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}
\right]
\le C\,\mathbb E[B_k;B_k>\alpha_M]+o(1).
\]
This is strictly weaker than a pointwise \(Cb\) theorem and uses the exact finite-\(q\) tail budget.

2. **Critical-GW saddle/cyclic estimate.** Use the exact forest Dwass identity
\[
\Pr(X_1+\cdots+X_b=N)=\frac{b}{N}\Pr(S_N=N-b)
\]
with \(N=Mh+O(b)\), and the pgf defect information \(y^2\ll R(y)\ll y^q\), to prove a uniform central local-ratio bound specifically for \(b\) in the corridor determined by \(K\). This would exploit the skip-free branching structure that a generic locally subexponential law does not have.

On the adversarial side, a lacunary construction should now be tested against the same \(K(t)\)-scale: it must force anomalous local mass for a sequence \(b_M\) with \(\alpha_M<b_M<2Mt_M/K(t_M)\), while preserving \(\sum_j j^q\pi_j<\infty\). The earlier missing-tail-mean design does not meet these constraints.
