## 2. Cumulative exact identities retained

For the ordinary critical tree write \(Z_j=\ell_j(T)\). Fix \(k\) and put
\[
N=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L=Z_k,\qquad m=n-N.
\]
Kemperman–Dwass gives the exact prefix change of measure
\[
\boxed{
W_n=\frac{nL}{m}\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
At the root, with \(D_n=\ell_1(T_n)\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac{n}{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
The cyclic lemma gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}\mathbb E\!\left[
\xi_1^q\mathbf1_{\{\xi_1>K\}}\mid S_n=n-1
\right].
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Thus the root case is exactly an endpoint \(q\)-uniform-integrability problem for one coordinate under exact central microcanonical conditioning.

Earlier arbitrary-law work also established, without a stable-domain hypothesis,
\[
x^{q-1}\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>x]\to0,
\qquad
x^{q-2}\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le x]\to0,
\tag{2.4}
\]
\[
(S_n-n)/n^{1/q}\to0
\quad\text{in probability},
\tag{2.5}
\]
and, for every fixed \(k\), some deterministic \(c_n\to\infty\), \(c_n=o(n)\), such that
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
The newly backfilled finite-core theorem improves the central denominator to
\[
\boxed{
-\log\Pr(S_n=n-1)
=o\!\left(n^{2/q-1}\right)
=o\!\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right),
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
and consequently permits an escape cutoff
\[
\boxed{c_n=o(n^{E_r})},
\qquad
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2},
\tag{2.8}
\]
for every fixed level.

The latest verified report proves a further structural theorem: for every residual constraint \(S_m=m-L\) with \(L=o(m)\), exact truncation followed by exponential recentering produces a finite-support law \(\widetilde\pi_{m,L}\) such that the conditional product law is unchanged and
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde\pi_{m,L}\xrightarrow[W_q]{}\pi.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]
Hence all remaining dangerous prefixes can be studied as **exact mean conditioning of a \(W_q\)-stable triangular family**. This eliminates inaccessible future-tail mean mismatch as an explanation of a counterexample.

# 3. NEW theorem: a fixed finite core forces uniform Bernoulli local smoothing

The main new result this run is an additive local regularity theorem for the canonical triangular arrays in (2.9). It requires neither finite variance nor a local limit theorem.

## 3.1 Two adjacent block sums from a finite support core

Choose a fixed finite set
\[
F\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi)
\]
containing 0 and positive support points with gcd \(h\). Divide all values by \(h\). The additive semigroup generated by the positive scaled values has gcd one and is therefore cofinite. Hence there are adjacent sufficiently large integers \(s,s+1\) belonging to this semigroup.

Choose representations
\[
hs=f_1+\cdots+f_{b_0},
\qquad
h(s+1)=g_1+\cdots+g_{b_1},
\qquad f_i,g_i\in F.
\]
Since \(0\in F\), pad the shorter representation with zeros. Thus for some fixed block length \(b\), **both** \(hs\) and \(h(s+1)\) are sums of exactly \(b\) elements of \(F\).

Let \(\mu_j\) be any sequence of canonical laws arising from sublinear constraints in (2.9). Since \(\mu_j\to\pi\) in \(W_q\), in particular \(\mu_j(f)\to\pi_f>0\) for every fixed \(f\in F\). Therefore, if \(Z_j\) is the sum of \(b\) iid \(\mu_j\)-variables, there is a constant \(\eta>0\), independent of large \(j\), such that
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(Z_j=hs)\ge\eta,
\qquad
\Pr(Z_j=h(s+1))\ge\eta.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

## 3.2 Exact Bernoulli-part extraction on each block

Put
\[
\alpha_j:=\min\{\Pr(Z_j=hs),\Pr(Z_j=h(s+1))\}\ge\eta.
\]
The block law has the exact mixture decomposition
\[
\mathcal L(Z_j)
=2\alpha_j\,\mathcal L(hs+hB)
+(1-2\alpha_j)R_j,
\tag{3.2}
\]
where \(B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and \(R_j\) is a residual probability law. This is simply the extraction of equal mass \(\alpha_j\) from the two adjacent lattice atoms.

Now let \(X_{j,1},\ldots,X_{j,N}\) be iid with law \(\mu_j\). Split the first \(b\lfloor N/b\rfloor\) variables into blocks of size \(b\). For each block independently use (3.2), and let \(M\) be the number of activated Bernoulli blocks. Then
\[
M\sim\mathrm{Bin}\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac Nb\right\rfloor,2\alpha_j\right),
\tag{3.3}
\]
with success probability uniformly bounded below. Conditional on the activations, all residual block values, and the at most \(b-1\) leftover variables, the total sum has the form
\[
C+h\,\mathrm{Bin}(M,1/2)
\tag{3.4}
\]
for a conditionally fixed integer \(C\).

Chernoff gives constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) such that
\[
\Pr(M<c_1N)\le e^{-c_2N}
\tag{3.5}
\]
uniformly in large \(j\).

## 3.3 Two elementary binomial estimates

Let
\[
b_M(k)=2^{-M}{M\choose k}.
\]
Uniformly in \(M\ge1\),
\[
\sup_k b_M(k)\le \frac C{\sqrt{M+1}},
\tag{3.6}
\]
and
\[
\sup_k|b_M(k+1)-b_M(k)|\le \frac C{M+1}.
\tag{3.7}
\]
The first is the standard maximal central-binomial bound. For the second, use
\[
\frac{b_M(k+1)}{b_M(k)}=\frac{M-k}{k+1}
\]
and split into the central region \(|k-M/2|\le C\sqrt M\), where the ratio differs from one by \(O(M^{-1/2})\) and (3.6) gives \(O(M^{-1})\), and the tails, where the pmf is exponentially below its maximum; increasing the absolute constant covers small \(M\).

## 3.4 Uniform additive local smoothing

Let
\[
p_{j,N}(x):=\Pr(X_{j,1}+\cdots+X_{j,N}=x).
\]
Condition on the Bernoulli extraction. On \(M\ge c_1N\), (3.6) gives \(C/\sqrt N\) for every point mass; the bad event is exponentially small by (3.5). Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\sup_x p_{j,N}(x)
\le C N^{-1/2}+e^{-cN}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Similarly, because an \(h\)-shift changes the binomial index by one, (3.7) gives
\[
\boxed{
\sup_x|p_{j,N}(x+h)-p_{j,N}(x)|
\le \frac C N+e^{-cN}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
The constants depend only on the fixed finite core and are uniform over every sufficiently advanced canonical law in a \(W_q\)-convergent sequence.

Telescoping yields, whenever \(u\in h\mathbb Z\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_x|p_{j,N}(x+u)-p_{j,N}(x)|
\le C\frac{|u|}{hN}
+\frac{|u|}{h}e^{-cN}.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]
For \(|u|\le N\), the last term may be absorbed into another \(e^{-c'N}\) after changing constants.

### Interpretation

This theorem proves that every canonical triangular array left by the reduction (2.9) has a **uniform Bernoulli smoothing component of linear size**. The local mass function cannot have lattice-scale spikes or troughs: adjacent admissible point probabilities differ by at most \(O(N^{-1})\), even though the variance may be infinite and no stable local limit theorem is assumed.

This is the precise form of the “finite aperiodic core smooths local probabilities” heuristic that earlier reports had only identified as a next route.

## 4. Corollary: additive comparison between the shifted numerator and the central denominator

Fix one support atom \(a\in F\) with \(\pi_a>0\). Along any canonical sequence \(\mu_j\to\pi\), for all large \(j\),
\[
\mu_j(a)\ge c_0>0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Let \(M\) be the exact mean total for \(N+1\) iid \(\mu_j\)-variables, assumed to be an admissible lattice point. Convolution gives
\[
p_{j,N+1}(M)
=\sum_z\mu_j(z)p_{j,N}(M-z)
\ge c_0p_{j,N}(M-a).
\tag{4.2}
\]
For any supported \(d\), \(d-a\in h\mathbb Z\). By (3.10),
\[
p_{j,N}(M-d)
\le p_{j,N}(M-a)
+C\frac{|d-a|}{N}+e^{-cN}(|d-a|+1).
\]
Combining with (4.2),
\[
\boxed{
 p_{j,N}(M-d)
\le C_0p_{j,N+1}(M)
+C_1\frac{d+1}{N}
+C_2(d+1)e^{-cN}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Thus the numerator local atom is always at most a constant multiple of the central denominator **plus an explicit additive smoothing error**.

Dividing by the central atom gives
\[
\frac{p_{j,N}(M-d)}{p_{j,N+1}(M)}
\le C_0
+C_1\frac{d+1}{N\,p_{j,N+1}(M)}
+\text{exponentially small term}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Why this is still not the desired multiplicative ratio theorem

If (4.4) could be replaced by a uniform constant bound, root UI would follow immediately from the \(q\)-tail of the canonical law. But the additive error in (4.3), when inserted into the weighted root sum, produces an extra factor comparable to \(d\): schematically
\[
\sum_{d>K}d^q\mu_j(d)\frac d{N p_{j,N+1}(M)},
\]
which requires a \((q+1)\)-moment not assumed in the problem. Moreover no universal estimate currently forces \(Np_{j,N+1}(M)\to\infty\). The backfilled denominator theorem gives only a stretched-subexponential lower bound, not enough to absorb this error at the exact \(q\)-moment endpoint.

Therefore (3.9)–(4.4) are genuine progress but **not** a proof of UI.

# 5. NEW exact switching identity for conditioned degree counts

There is a second algebraic route which may eventually upgrade additive smoothing to a moment-compatible multiplicative estimate.

Let \(N_j\) be the number of offspring coordinates equal to \(j\) under the conditioned allocation
\[
(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1.
\]
For a finitely supported vector \(a=(a_j)\) of nonnegative integers, put
\[
A(a):=\sum_j a_j,
\qquad
W(a):=\sum_j j a_j.
\]
Exchangeability gives the exact factorial-moment formula
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_j(N_j)_{a_j}\mid S_n=n-1\right]
=(n)_{A(a)}\prod_j\pi_j^{a_j}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-A(a)}=n-1-W(a))}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Hence if two count patterns \(a,b\) have
\[
A(a)=A(b),\qquad W(a)=W(b),
\]
the local-probability factors cancel **exactly**:
\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\mathbb E\prod_j(N_j)_{a_j}
}{
\mathbb E\prod_j(N_j)_{b_j}
}
=
\frac{\prod_j\pi_j^{a_j}}
{\prod_j\pi_j^{b_j}}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

For every sufficiently large supported \(d\), the scaled value \(d/h\) belongs to the semigroup generated by a fixed finite core. Choose a representation
\[
d=f_1+\cdots+f_s,
\qquad f_i\in F\setminus\{0\},
\]
and pad the one-large-degree pattern with \(s-1\) zeros. The patterns

- one \(d\) and \(s-1\) zeros;
- the \(s\) bounded core values \(f_1,\ldots,f_s\),

have the same number of selected coordinates \(s\) and the same total offspring sum \(d\). Therefore (5.2) gives the exact switching identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[N_d(N_0)_{s-1}\mid S_n=n-1\right]
=
\frac{\pi_d\pi_0^{s-1}}
{\prod_{f\in F}\pi_f^{b_f}}
\mathbb E\!\left[
\prod_{f\in F}(N_f)_{b_f}\mid S_n=n-1
\right],
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
where \(b_f\) is the number of times \(f\) occurs in the chosen representation.

Equation (5.3) eliminates the problematic moving local ratio exactly. It is therefore an attractive route to a linear Kesten/size-bias estimate.

### Obstruction in the naive use of (5.3)

The representation length \(s\) grows proportionally to \(d\). To extract \(\mathbb E N_d\) from the left-hand side one needs good uniform control of
\[
(N_0)_{s-1}
\]
and the analogous bounded-core factorial products when \(s\to\infty\). Fixed-core counts are linearly concentrated under conditioning, but ordinary concentration only turns these factorials into deterministic powers when \(s\) is much smaller than the fluctuation scale (naively \(s=o(\sqrt n)\), and potentially worse if one pays for the conditioning denominator directly). The remaining root window can extend well beyond this.

Thus the exact switching identity is not yet a full bound, but it removes the local-probability ratio algebraically and identifies a different concrete missing lemma: **moderate-order factorial control of fixed core counts under central conditioning**.

## 6. Literature check relevant to the new smoothing route

A bounded current search found classical and modern work using exactly this Bernoulli-part philosophy:

- A. R. Dabrowski and D. McDonald, *An application of the Bernoulli part to local limit theorems for moving averages on stationary sequences*, Canadian Journal of Statistics 24 (1996), 293–305, DOI 10.2307/3315740. Their abstract explicitly says the amount of Bernoulli part determines how relatively flat the local mass function is; a global CLT plus this flatness yields an LLT.
- R. Giuliano and M. Weber, *Approximate Local Limit Theorems with Effective Rate and Application to Random Walks in Random Scenery*, Bernoulli 23 (2017), 3268–3310, arXiv:1412.3980, develops effective local estimates by Bernoulli-part extraction.

The theorem in Section 3 is much more elementary than those full LLTs: it uses only a fixed pair of adjacent block atoms and binomial smoothing, so it remains valid without finite variance or a global CLT.

A potentially relevant earlier paper is S. Janson, *Moment convergence in conditional limit theorems*, Journal of Applied Probability 38 (2001), 421–437, DOI 10.1239/jap/996986753. It treats triangular arrays and conditional moment convergence with applications including branching processes. The inspected full-text theorem has stronger normal-regime hypotheses, including third-moment/variance controls of the form
\[
\|X_n-EX_n\|_3=o(N_n^{1/6}\sigma_{X_n})
\]
(and analogous conditions for the statistic). Those assumptions are not guaranteed at the present \(q<2\), infinite-variance endpoint, so this paper is **not** used as a black-box resolution.

A fresh bounded search through 15 September 2026 still found no full resolution of Janson's fractional infinite-variance fixed-level problem. Absence of a hit is not novelty or priority certification.

## 9. Next concrete attack

The two new routes should now be combined rather than pursued separately.

1. Use the Bernoulli-part decomposition to obtain **conditional concentration of fixed core counts at mesoscopic factorial orders**, not merely ordinary additive concentration. Because the Bernoulli part is extracted from blocks with a uniform activation probability, it may yield exponential control of downward factorial moments even after exact central conditioning.
2. Insert such bounds into the exact switching identity (5.3). The goal is a direct estimate
\[
\mathbb E N_d(T_n)\le Cn\pi_d
\]
(or the precisely needed weighted variant) uniformly through the remaining sublinear degree window. Combined with
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac d{n-1}\mathbb E N_d(T_n),
\]
this would give
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)\le C d\pi_d
\]
and root UI immediately from \(\sum d^q\pi_d<\infty\).
3. If that root bound is achieved, redo the switching at a fixed exposed prefix, where the descendant forest conditioning has the same canonical/Bernoulli structure. Only then would the every-fixed-level target be closed.

The counterexample route should be kept adversarially active: search for a fixed lacunary law whose canonical central pmf varies over a width much larger than one lattice step but still creates a weighted local ratio violating the desired \(Cd\pi_d\) bound. Any such construction must evade all four structural barriers listed above.
