## 1. Exact target, scope, and recovered checkpoint

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,
\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree with offspring law \(\xi\), conditioned on total size \(n\), along every admissible \(n\). The target is to decide, for **every fixed** \(k\ge1\), whether
\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]
is uniformly integrable.

Write
\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad \pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d),\qquad
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Critical nondegeneracy implies \(0<\pi_0<1\) and positive mass above 1. Every finite tree size is \(1\pmod h\); because a finite subset of the positive support already has gcd \(h\), every sufficiently large integer in that residue class is admissible. “Admissible” always means \(\Pr(|T|=n)>0\).

Primary source: S. Janson, *Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton–Watson trees, random allocations and condensation*, arXiv:1112.0510v1, Section 20.1 / Conjecture 20.1 (published numbering 21.1). The source conjectures fixed-level moment convergence for every integer \(r\ge1\), every fixed \(k\ge1\), explicitly extends the conjecture to noninteger \(r>0\), and identifies \(\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty\) as the finite-limit regime of interest.

## 2. Cumulative exact inputs retained

Let
\[
D_n:=\ell_1(T_n),\qquad S_m:=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_m,
\qquad a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad \lambda_n:=-\log a_n.
\]
At the root,
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac n{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently, if \(N_d\) is the number of degree-\(d\) vertices in \(T_n\), cyclic rerooting gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac d{n-1}\,\mathbb E N_d.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac1{n-1}\mathbb E\sum_{v\in T_n}
\deg(v)^q\mathbf1_{\{\deg(v)>K\}}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a fixed level \(k\), under the ordinary unconditioned tree write
\[
Z_j:=\ell_j(T),\qquad
N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L_k:=Z_k.
\tag{2.4}
\]
If \(m=n-N_k\), Kemperman–Dwass gives the exact prefix density
\[
\boxed{
W_n=\frac{nL_k}{m}
\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L_k)}{a_n}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Earlier verified PROB20-14 runs established
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right)=o(n^{2/q-1}),
}
\tag{2.6}
\]
and, for every fixed \(k\), a deterministic \(c_n=o(n)\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
They also established canonical \(W_q\)-recentring, finite-core Bernoulli smoothing, renewal switching/localization, Palm self-normalization, and the entropy-scale root window
\[
B_n=\left(\frac n{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q},
\qquad
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[D_n^r;K<D_n\le B_n]=0.
\tag{2.8}
\]
The latest checkpoint additionally gives the adaptive Palm criterion \(\Gamma_n\to0\) as a sufficient condition for root closure. It is not known whether \(\Gamma_n\to0\) follows from the bare finite-\(q\)-moment assumption.

# 3. NEW: exact finite-mean random-prefix forest reduction for every fixed level

The main new structural result of this run is an exact reduction of the **entire fixed-level fractional-moment problem**, not just \(k=1\), to one local ratio for a forest started from a random finite-mean prefix law.

Let
\[
X:=|T|,
\qquad
\nu_n:=\Pr(X=n)=\Pr(|T|=n).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be iid copies of \(X\), independent of all prefix variables.

For fixed \(k\ge1\), the branching property gives the exact decomposition
\[
\boxed{
|T|=N_k+\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Here \((N_k,L_k)\) is measurable from offspring variables strictly above level \(k\), while the \(L_k\) descendant trees rooted at level \(k\) are iid ordinary Galton–Watson trees and are independent of that prefix.

Define
\[
m_{k,r}:=\mathbb E L_k^r.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Since \(0<r<1\) and \(\mathbb E L_k=1\), Jensen gives \(0<m_{k,r}\le1\).

Define a new random pair \((A_k,B_k)\) by the **\(r\)-size-biased prefix law**
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(A_k=a,B_k=b)
:=
\frac{b^r}{m_{k,r}}
\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
The pair \((A_k,B_k)\) is chosen independently of the iid total-progeny variables \((X_i)\).

### Theorem 3.1 — exact random-prefix identity

For every admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,\ell_k(T_n)^r
=
m_{k,r}\,
\frac{
\Pr\!\left(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n\right)
}{\nu_n}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof
By (3.2), conditioning on the prefix gives
\[
\Pr(|T|=n\mid N_k,L_k)
=
\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i=n-N_k\right).
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_n\,\mathbb E\ell_k(T_n)^r
&=\mathbb E\left[
L_k^r\mathbf1_{\{|T|=n\}}
\right]\\
&=\mathbb E\left[
L_k^r
\Pr\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i=n-N_k\right)
\right]\\
&=m_{k,r}\,
\Pr\!\left(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (3.5). \(\square\)

This identity packages all prefix-size randomness and all boundary-width randomness into a **single fixed random pair** \((A_k,B_k)\), independent of \(n\).

# 4. NEW: the tilted prefix pair has exactly the finite means needed at the endpoint

The reduction above becomes useful because the moment hypothesis \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\) implies that both coordinates of \((A_k,B_k)\) have finite first moment — with no spare \(q+\varepsilon\) moment.

### Lemma 4.1 — propagation of the \(q\)-moment

For every fixed \(j\),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E Z_j^q<\infty.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof
Conditional on \(Z_j=m\),
\[
Z_{j+1}=\sum_{i=1}^m\xi_i
=m+\sum_{i=1}^m(\xi_i-1).
\]
For \(q\in(1,2)\), von Bahr–Esseen gives
\[
\mathbb E\left|\sum_{i=1}^m(\xi_i-1)\right|^q
\le C_q m\,\mathbb E|\xi-1|^q.
\]
Together with \(|x+y|^q\le C_q(|x|^q+|y|^q)\),
\[
\mathbb E[Z_{j+1}^q\mid Z_j=m]
\le C_q(m^q+m).
\]
Induction from \(Z_0=1\) proves (4.1). \(\square\)

### Lemma 4.2 — finite mean of the random number of boundary trees

Under (3.4),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E B_k
=
\frac{\mathbb E L_k^{1+r}}{\mathbb E L_k^r}
=
\frac{\mathbb E Z_k^q}{m_{k,r}}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.3 — finite mean of the random prefix shift

Under (3.4),
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E A_k<\infty.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
It is enough to show \(\mathbb E[N_kL_k^r]<\infty\). For each \(0\le j<k\), condition on generation \(j\). Criticality gives
\[
\mathbb E[L_k\mid\mathcal F_j]=Z_j.
\]
Because \(x\mapsto x^r\) is concave,
\[
\mathbb E[L_k^r\mid\mathcal F_j]
\le
\left(\mathbb E[L_k\mid\mathcal F_j]\right)^r
=Z_j^r.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E[Z_jL_k^r]
\le
\mathbb E Z_j^{1+r}
=
\mathbb E Z_j^q<\infty
\]
by Lemma 4.1. Summing over \(j<k\) and dividing by \(m_{k,r}\) proves (4.3). \(\square\)

Thus the exact residual problem lands at a genuine endpoint: the random shift and random number of descendant trees have finite first moments, but no higher moment is guaranteed by the hypothesis.

# 5. NEW: exact equivalence between fixed-level UI and a finite-mean local random-sum ratio

Janson's fixed-level local convergence gives
\[
\ell_k(T_n)\ \xrightarrow{d}\ \ell_k(\widehat T),
\tag{5.1}
\]
where \(\widehat T\) is the Kesten tree. The first \(k\) levels of the Kesten tree are \(L_k\)-size-biased relative to the ordinary tree, so
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\ell_k(\widehat T)^r
=
\mathbb E L_k^{r+1}
=
\mathbb E Z_k^q<\infty.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
For nonnegative variables converging in distribution to an integrable limit, uniform integrability is equivalent to convergence of the expectations. Combining (3.5), (4.2), and (5.2) gives the following exact equivalence.

### Theorem 5.1 — local-ratio equivalence

For each fixed \(k\), the desired UI is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\Pr\!\left(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n\right)
}{\nu_n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E B_k
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
along all admissible \(n\).

Indeed, by (3.5), moment convergence is equivalent to
\[
m_{k,r}\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}
\to\mathbb E Z_k^q,
\]
and (4.2) says \(\mathbb E Z_k^q=m_{k,r}\mathbb E B_k\).

This replaces the random **growing** prefix under size conditioning by one fixed-in-law pair \((A_k,B_k)\) with
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E A_k<\infty,\qquad \mathbb E B_k<\infty.}
\tag{5.4}
\]
No local persistence or unverified LLT is used.

# 6. Root specialization and exact generating-function form

For \(k=1\),
\[
N_1=1,\qquad L_1=\xi.
\]
Let
\[
m_r:=\mathbb E\xi^r
\]
and define \(B\) by
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(B=d)=\frac{d^r\pi_d}{m_r},\qquad d\ge1.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E B=\frac{\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}}{m_r}<\infty,
\]
and Theorem 5.1 becomes
\[
\boxed{
\{D_n^r\}\text{ is UI}
\iff
\frac{\Pr(X_1+\cdots+X_B=n-1)}{\nu_n}
\to\mathbb E B.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

There is also an exact analytic version. Let
\[
T(z):=\sum_{n\ge1}\nu_n z^n,
\qquad
\phi(s):=\mathbb E s^\xi,
\]
so \(T=z\phi(T)\). For a nonnegative toll \(f\), put
\[
F_f(t):=\sum_{d\ge0}f(d)\pi_d t^d
\]
and
\[
A_f(z):=\sum_{n\ge1}\nu_n
\mathbb E\left[\sum_{v\in T_n}f(\deg v)\right]z^n.
\]
The root-plus-subtrees recursion gives
\[
A_f(z)=zF_f(T(z))+z\phi'(T(z))A_f(z),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
A_f(z)=\frac{zF_f(T(z))}{1-z\phi'(T(z))}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Differentiating \(T=z\phi(T)\), and choosing \(H_f'(t)=F_f(t)/t\), yields
\[
\boxed{
A_f(z)=z^2\frac d{dz}H_f(T(z)).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
For \(f_K(d)=d^q\mathbf1_{\{d>K\}}\), take
\[
H_K(t)=\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_d t^d.
\]
Then the cyclic identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=
\frac{[z^{n-1}]H_K(T(z))}{\nu_n}
=
\frac1{\nu_n}\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_d\,\nu^{*d}_{n-1}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
This is the tailwise form of the random-initial-population reduction.

# 7. Existing local-subexponential random-sum theory does not close the endpoint

Earlier PROB20 work established, after reducing by the lattice span, the fixed-count total-progeny relations
\[
\frac{u_{m+1}}{u_m}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{u_m^{*d}}{u_m}\to d
\quad(d\text{ fixed}).
\tag{7.1}
\]
Thus the total-progeny law has the fixed-count local-subexponential behavior suggested by (5.3).

A fresh literature check found a directly relevant comparison: Ng and Tang, *Asymptotic behavior of tail and local probabilities for sums of subexponential random variables*, J. Appl. Probab. 41 (2004), Theorem 4.2. Their local randomly-stopped-sum conclusion is obtained from a Kesten-type bound growing like \((1+\varepsilon)^d\) and assumes an **exponential moment** of the random count. The proof explicitly uses that exponential moment to dominate the sum over convolution counts.

That theorem does not settle (5.3): here \(B_k\) is guaranteed only a finite first moment. For \(k=1\), an exponential moment of the \(r\)-weighted offspring count would be far stronger than the target assumptions and would exclude the genuinely heavy infinite-variance setting.

Macci–Pacchiarotti (2017) treats total progeny with random initial population at the level of Cramér-type large deviations, not the exact local central ratio (5.3). Abraham–Delmas–Guo supplies the fixed-shift/fixed-count strong ratio used in (7.1), not the random finite-mean mixture needed here.

So Theorem 5.1 identifies a genuine endpoint theorem rather than hiding a known black-box solution.

# 8. NEW: precise averaged Kesten target, weaker than a global pointwise bound

Fix \(M\). On \(A_k+B_k\le M\), only finitely many shifts and convolution counts occur. The fixed-shift strong ratio and (7.1) therefore yield
\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\Pr\!\left(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n,\ A_k+B_k\le M\right)
}{\nu_n}
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E[B_k;A_k+B_k\le M].
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Hence the full theorem is reduced to
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{\{A_k+B_k>M\}}
\Pr(X_1+\cdots+X_{B_k}=n-A_k\mid A_k,B_k)
\right]
}{\nu_n}
=0.
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

This averaged tail estimate is strictly weaker than requiring a pointwise bound
\[
\nu^{*b}_{n-a}\le Cb\nu_n
\]
for every \((a,b,n)\). The latter remains sufficient on the relevant range, but it is stronger than the exact problem. Future work should exploit the explicit averaging law (3.4) and its finite-mean budget instead of unnecessarily taking a supremum over all prefixes.

# 9. Interaction with the adaptive Palm criterion

For the root, (6.5) shows that the desired tail estimate is already a weighted mixture:
\[
\frac1{\nu_n}\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_d\nu^{*d}_{n-1}.
\tag{9.1}
\]
The adaptive Palm theorem controls individual \(d\)-terms after optimizing a tail-mean threshold. The new formulation suggests a stronger route: sum the Palm-bad lower-deviation probabilities against \(d^r\pi_d\) **before** taking a worst-case supremum or forming the modulus \(\Gamma_n\). The finite-\(q\) assumption is then available in its exact summable form
\[
\sum_d d^q\pi_d<\infty.
\]

A target strong enough to close \(k=1\) is
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\frac1{\nu_n}\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_d\nu^{*d}_{n-1}=0.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
For fixed \(k\), (8.2) is the corresponding prefix-averaged target.

# 11. Failed / insufficient routes retained

1. Bounded expectations of \(\ell_k(T_n)^r\) do not imply UI.
2. Fixed-shift strong ratios cannot justify exchanging an infinite random count with the limit.
3. Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds grow exponentially in the convolution count; a first moment of \(B_k\) cannot absorb that.
4. Ng–Tang's local random-sum theorem assumes an exponential moment of the stopping count, unavailable here.
5. Total variation or entropy control of bounded observables does not control the endpoint \(q\)-moment.
6. F0058-style missing-upper-tail-mean compensation is constrained by the finite-\(q\) budget and canonical recentring, but this is not yet a universal proof.
7. The adaptive condition \(\Gamma_n\to0\) is sufficient but not known automatic.
8. A global pointwise linear convolution estimate is stronger than the exact requirement; the averaged target (8.2) is the sharper objective.

# 13. Next attack

The priority is now an **averaged random-prefix Kesten estimate**.

At the root, start from the exact mixture (9.1). Split at the entropy/Palm scale, but sum the Palm-bad lower-deviation probability against \(d^r\pi_d\) before optimizing in \(d\). A useful target is
\[
\sum_{d>M}d^r\pi_d
\frac{\nu^{*d}_{n-1}}{\nu_n}
\le
C\sum_{d>cM}d^q\pi_d+o_M(1)
\]
uniformly in large admissible \(n\). This would close the root case immediately.

For general fixed \(k\), repeat the weighted argument under the explicit law (3.4). Since \(\mathbb E(A_k+B_k)<\infty\), it is enough to establish (8.2); the random shift \(A_k\) should be truncated using its finite mean and handled by fixed-shift ratios, while the random count \(B_k\) is handled by the weighted Palm/renewal mechanism.

The adversarial route is equally concrete: any lacunary counterexample must make the random-prefix ratio (5.3) fail despite \(\mathbb E(A_k+B_k)<\infty\), canonical recentring, finite-core smoothing, and fixed-count local-subexponentiality. Proposed constructions should now be tested directly against (5.3), not only against pointwise local-ratio heuristics.
