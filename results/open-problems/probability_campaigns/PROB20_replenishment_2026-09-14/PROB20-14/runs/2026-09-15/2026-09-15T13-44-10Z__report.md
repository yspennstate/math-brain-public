## 1. Exact target and verified source quantifiers

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}\xi=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,
\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree with offspring law \(\xi\), conditioned on total size \(n\), along every admissible \(n\). For every fixed level \(k\ge1\), determine whether
\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]
is uniformly integrable.

Put
\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad \pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d),\qquad
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Critical nondegeneracy implies \(\pi_0>0\) and positive mass above 1. Every finite tree size is \(1\pmod h\); a finite subset of the positive support already has gcd \(h\), hence all sufficiently large integers in that residue class are admissible.

W_n=\frac{nL}{m}\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.

\tag{2.1}

\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac{n}{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{2.2}

The cyclic lemma yields
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]

\xi_1^q\mathbf1_{\{\xi_1>K\}}\mid S_n=n-1

}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Hence \(k=1\) is exactly an endpoint \(q\)-uniform-integrability problem for one coordinate under exact central microcanonical conditioning.

# 4. NEW: canonical recentering works for every sublinear prefix shift

The main new theorem strengthens the earlier root-only canonical-tilt observation and removes an unnecessary \(L=o(m^{1-r})\) restriction. The correct range is the full **sublinear** range \(L=o(m)\).

### Theorem 4.1 — sublinear canonical recentering
Let \(m\to\infty\) and let integers \(L=L_m\) satisfy
\[
0\le L<m,\qquad L=o(m),
\]
with \(m-L\) admissible for the corresponding lattice sum. Consider \(m\) iid copies of \(\xi\) conditioned on
\[
S_m=m-L.
\]
There is a finite-support exponential tilt \(\widetilde\pi_{m,L}\) on \(\{0,1,\ldots,m-L\}\) such that

1. \(\mathbb E_{\widetilde\pi_{m,L}}X=1-L/m\);
2. the exact conditional product law is unchanged:
   \[
   \mathcal L_\pi((\xi_i)_{i\le m}\mid S_m=m-L)
   =
   \mathcal L_{\widetilde\pi_{m,L}}((X_i)_{i\le m}\mid \sum X_i=m-L);
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
3. as \(m\to\infty\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   \widetilde\pi_{m,L}\xrightarrow[W_q]{}\pi,
   }
   \tag{4.2}
   \]
   uniformly along every deterministic sublinear sequence \(L=o(m)\).

In particular the canonical one-site laws are uniformly \(q\)-integrable throughout the entire sublinear prefix regime.

### Proof
Set
\[
t=m-L,
\qquad
\tau_t=\Pr(\xi>t),
\qquad
M_t=\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>t].
\]
On the event \(S_m=t\), every coordinate is at most \(t\). Therefore conditioning the base law first on \(\{\xi\le t\}\) changes the product weight only by a constant. Define
\[
\beta_t(d)=\frac{\pi_d}{1-\tau_t},\qquad 0\le d\le t.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Its mean is
\[
\mu_t=\frac{1-M_t}{1-\tau_t}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
The target average is
\[
a_{m,L}=1-L/m.
\tag{4.5}
\]
For all large \(m\), this lies strictly between the minimum and maximum support points of \(\beta_t\), because 0 and a fixed support value at least 2 remain available and \(a_{m,L}\to1\). Hence there is a unique exponential tilt
\[
\widetilde\pi_{m,L}(d)
=
\frac{e^{\theta_{m,L}d}\beta_t(d)}
{\sum_{j\le t}e^{\theta_{m,L}j}\beta_t(j)}
\tag{4.6}
\]
with mean \(a_{m,L}\). Since exponential tilting multiplies a product by a factor depending only on the total sum, (4.1) is exact.

Now put
\[
\delta_{m,L}:=a_{m,L}-\mu_t
=\frac{M_t-\tau_t-(L/m)(1-\tau_t)}{1-\tau_t}.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Because \(t\to\infty\), the truncated laws \(\beta_t\) converge to \(\pi\) in \(W_q\) and are uniformly \(q\)-integrable.

The likelihood ratio \(\widetilde\pi_{m,L}/\beta_t\) is monotone in \(d\). Thus if \(\delta_{m,L}<0\), the tilted law is stochastically dominated by \(\beta_t\). Consequently for every \(K\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\widetilde\pi_{m,L}}[X^q;X>K]
\le
\mathbb E_{\beta_t}[X^q;X>K].
\tag{4.8}
\]
So the downward-tilted subfamily is automatically uniformly \(q\)-integrable; no Lipschitz factor \(t^{q-1}\) is needed. Moreover from (4.7), on the negative-sign branch
\[
|\delta_{m,L}|\le L/m\to0.
\tag{4.9}
\]
Monotone coupling therefore gives
\[
W_1(\widetilde\pi_{m,L},\beta_t)=|\delta_{m,L}|\to0.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Thus the downward-tilted laws converge weakly to \(\pi\), and uniform \(q\)-integrability upgrades this to \(W_q\)-convergence.

If \(\delta_{m,L}\ge0\), then \(\widetilde\pi_{m,L}\) stochastically dominates \(\beta_t\), and
\[
0\le\delta_{m,L}\le \frac{M_t}{1-\tau_t}.
\tag{4.11}
\]
Both laws are supported on \([0,t]\), so \(x\mapsto x^q\) has Lipschitz constant \(qt^{q-1}\). Monotone coupling yields
\[
0\le
\mathbb E_{\widetilde\pi_{m,L}}X^q-\mathbb E_{\beta_t}X^q
\le qt^{q-1}\delta_{m,L}
\le
\frac{q}{1-\tau_t}\,t^{q-1}M_t.
\tag{4.12}
\]
But
\[
t^{q-1}M_t
\le \mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>t]\to0.
\tag{4.13}
\]
Also \(W_1=\delta_{m,L}\to0\). Hence the positive-tilt branch also converges weakly to \(\pi\) with convergent \(q\)-moments, i.e. in \(W_q\).

This proves (4.2). \(\square\)

## 5. Why Theorem 4.1 is a real strengthening

A crude bound on the downward tilt would give
\[
|\mathbb E_{\widetilde\pi}X^q-\mathbb E_{\beta}X^q|
\lesssim t^{q-1}L/m,
\]
which only vanishes under the much stronger condition \(L=o(m^{2-q})=o(m^{1-r})\). The stochastic-domination argument (4.8) shows this loss is artificial: downward tilting can only improve the \(q\)-tail. Therefore **every sublinear shift \(L=o(m)\)** admits a \(W_q\)-stable canonical recentering.

This exactly matches the regime left after the universal escape theorem (2.6): all potentially dangerous fixed-level prefixes have \(L=o(n)\), so there is no remaining one-site moment defect after canonical recentering.

## 6. Root consequence: the entire unresolved mesoscopic window is exactly mean-centered

For the root, fixing \(D_n=d\) leaves \(m=n-1\) residual offspring variables with target sum
\[
m-d.
\]
Every unresolved root degree from the earlier mesoscopic reduction satisfies \(d=o(n)\). Theorem 4.1 therefore constructs a law \(\widetilde\pi_{n,d}\) with
\[
\mathbb E_{\widetilde\pi_{n,d}}X=1-d/(n-1),
\qquad
\widetilde\pi_{n,d}\xrightarrow[W_q]{}\pi,
\tag{6.1}
\]
and the exact residual conditional law is unchanged after replacing \(\pi\) by \(\widetilde\pi_{n,d}\).

Thus every root counterexample would have to arise from **exact conditioning at the mean of a \(W_q\)-stable triangular family**. It cannot be caused by:

- offspring values larger than the residual total, which are removed exactly before tilting;
- the mean contributed by such inaccessible values;
- loss of \(q\)-uniform integrability in the canonical one-site law;
- a pure lattice-span obstruction, because a fixed finite support core already has gcd \(h\).

This substantially narrows the F0058-type lacunary search. The F0058 mechanism can create a mean-versus-centre mismatch for the original law, but after the exact truncation/tilt it survives only if central conditioning itself amplifies a vanishing \(q\)-tail inside a \(W_q\)-convergent family.

## 7. Fixed-level consequence: same reduction for every non-negligible prefix

For a fixed prefix with \(N\) interior vertices and \(L=Z_k\) boundary vertices, the descendant forest contains \(m=n-N\) iid offspring variables constrained to total \(m-L\). On the non-negligible region from the earlier escape analysis, \(N=o(n)\) and \(L=o(n)\), hence \(L=o(m)\). Theorem 4.1 applies pathwise to this residual constraint.

Therefore the **all-level** problem is reduced to the following triangular central-conditioning principle:

> For every \(W_q\)-convergent family of nonnegative lattice laws \(\mu_j\to\pi\), with means equal to the exact conditioned average and a fixed finite aperiodic support core inherited from \(\pi\), does exact conditioning of an iid sample at its mean preserve the required \(q\)-tail uniformly enough to control the prefix factor (2.1)?

A proof of this principle, with constants uniform over the sublinear prefix parameters, would settle every fixed level at once.

## 8. New adversarial test: any counterexample must be a central local valley

Theorem 4.1 makes the counterexample target much more rigid. A valid fixed-law counterexample would induce a sequence of finite-support laws \(\mu_j\) such that
\[
\mu_j\xrightarrow[W_q]{}\pi,
\tag{8.1}
\]
with exact conditioning at their own means, but the conditional one-site \(q\)-tails fail uniform integrability.

The familiar single rare-atom toy explains why finite \(q\)-moment resists this. If a row has an atom of size \(d_j\asymp n_j^{1/q}\) with expected count \(\lambda_j=n_jp_j\), then its unconditioned \(q\)-mass is
\[
p_jd_j^q\asymp \lambda_j.
\]
For a fixed underlying law with finite \(q\)-moment, the stage contributions must tend to zero, so \(\lambda_j\to0\). Exact mean conditioning of the bounded core then favors zero such atoms rather than forcing one. To force one compensator using still larger atoms, their missing mean must be of order \(d_j\); but
\[
d^{q-1}\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>d]\to0
\tag{8.2}
\]
shows that at the natural scale the required missing mean is unavailable without spending nonvanishing \(q\)-moment. This does not prove the theorem, but it rules out the two most direct lacunary constructions in the now-canonical formulation.
