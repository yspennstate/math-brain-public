## 2. Cumulative root identities and denominator scale

Let
\[
D_n:=\ell_1(T_n),\qquad
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1),
\qquad
a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\]
and put
\[
\lambda_n:=-\log a_n.
\]
The exact root formula is
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)
=\frac{n}{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
If \(N_d\) is the number of degree-\(d\) coordinates under \(Q_n\), the cyclic lemma gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac d{n-1}\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_d.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac1{n-1}\sum_{d>K}d^q\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_d
=\frac n{n-1}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q\mathbf1_{\{\xi_1>K\}}\mid S_n=n-1].
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The earlier finite-core denominator theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n=o(n^\beta),\qquad
\beta=\frac2q-1=\frac{1-r}{1+r}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
The all-level large-width theorem gives, for each fixed \(k\), a deterministic \(c_n=o(n^{E_r})\),
\[
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2},
\]
such that
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The previous report also established the renewal-switching functional. Let
\[
u_s(d)=\Pr(S_s=d),
\]
and
\[
H_{n,d}(z)
:=\sum_{s=1}^n
u_s(d)\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}.
\tag{2.6}
\]
Then there is a law-dependent constant \(C<\infty\) such that, uniformly in admissible \(n\) and \(d\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_n}[N_dH_{n,d}(N_0)]\le C\pi_d.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]
The proof sums every equal-total factorial switch and identifies the other side with the occupation count of level \(d\) by a random permutation of the conditioned degree multiset.

Finally, the ordinary renewal measure
\[
U(d):=\sum_{s\ge1}\nu_s(d)
\]
satisfies \(U(d)\to h\) along \(d\in h\mathbb Z\). More quantitatively, there are constants \(C_0,c_0,d_0>0\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le s\le C_0d}\nu_s(d)\ge c_0
}
\tag{2.8}
\]
for all sufficiently large admissible \(d\).

# 3. NEW: Palm form of the switching identity

Define the exact moving local ratio
\[
R_{n,d}:=
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_d=n\pi_dR_{n,d}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
For \(d>0\), define the degree-\(d\) Palm law by
\[
\mathbb P^*_{n,d}(A)
:=
\frac{\mathbb E_{Q_n}[N_d\mathbf1_A]}
{\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_d}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Under this Palm law, after selecting one degree-\(d\) coordinate, the remaining \(n-1\) coordinates are iid with law \(\pi\), conditioned on
\[
S_{n-1}=n-1-d.
\tag{3.4}
\]
In particular the selected coordinate is nonzero, so \(N_0\) under the Palm law is simply the zero count among those \(n-1\) remaining coordinates.

Dividing (2.7) by (3.2) gives the self-normalized inequality
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}\,\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]\le C.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
This is the key starting point of the new result: if the local ratio \(R_{n,d}\) is large, then the Palm conditioning event is itself correspondingly less rare, so concentration under the Palm law improves automatically. The previous proof used concentration only under \(Q_n\) and therefore discarded this feedback.

# 4. NEW: a tunable pointwise moving-shift bound

### Theorem 4.1
There are constants \(c,C,C_1>0\), depending only on the offspring law, such that for every sufficiently large admissible \(n\), every supported \(d\) with
\[
d_0\le d\le c n,
\]
and every \(0<\delta\le c\),
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C\exp\!\left(C_1(d\delta+d^2/n)\right)
+
\exp\!\left(\lambda_n-c n\delta^2\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C\exp\!\left[
C_1\left(
 d\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n+1}{n}}
 +\frac{d^2}{n}
\right)
\right]
+C.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
\log^+R_{n,d}
\le
C\left(
1+d\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n+1}{n}}+\frac{d^2}{n}
\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
No domain of attraction or local limit theorem is assumed.

### Proof

Set \(N=n-1\). Under the Palm law, let
\[
G_\delta
:=
\{N_0\ge \pi_0N-n\delta\}.
\]
Unconditionally, before imposing the shifted sum, the zero count among the \(N\) remaining variables is \(\mathrm{Bin}(N,\pi_0)\). Therefore Hoeffding gives
\[
\Pr(G_\delta^c)\le e^{-c n\delta^2}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
The Palm conditioning denominator is
\[
\Pr(S_N=N-d)
=a_nR_{n,d}.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P^*_{n,d}(G_\delta^c)
\le
\frac{\exp(\lambda_n-c n\delta^2)}{R_{n,d}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
This is where the self-normalization enters.

Now take \(z\ge\pi_0N-n\delta\). Since
\[
n\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}
=
\frac{(z)_{s-1}}
{\pi_0^{s-1}(n-1)_{s-1}},
\tag{4.6}
\]
for \(s\le C_0d\le c'n\), every logarithmic factor in the product satisfies
\[
\log\frac{z-j}{\pi_0(N-j)}
\ge
-C\left(\delta+\frac jn\right).
\]
Summing over \(0\le j\le s-2\) yields
\[
 n\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}
\ge
\exp\!\left[-C(s\delta+s^2/n)\right].
\tag{4.7}
\]
Using the renewal lower bound (2.8),
\[
\boxed{
 nH_{n,d}(z)
\ge
c_0\exp\!\left[-C_1(d\delta+d^2/n)\right]
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
on \(G_\delta\).

Write
\[
A=C_1(d\delta+d^2/n),
\qquad
E=\exp(\lambda_n-c n\delta^2).
\]
From (3.5), (4.5), and (4.8),
\[
C
\ge
R_{n,d}\,\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]
\ge
c_0e^{-A}R_{n,d}
\left(1-\min\{1,E/R_{n,d}\}\right).
\]
If \(R_{n,d}\le E\), then (4.1) is immediate. If \(R_{n,d}>E\), the last display gives
\[
R_{n,d}-E\le C'e^A.
\]
Thus in all cases
\[
R_{n,d}\le E+C'e^A,
\]
which is (4.1).

Finally choose \(\delta\) so that \(c n\delta^2=\lambda_n+1\). Since \(\lambda_n=o(n)\), this lies in the permitted small-\(\delta\) range for large \(n\), and (4.2)–(4.3) follow. \(\square\)

## 5. New adaptive UI window and necessary condition for a counterexample

Theorem 4.1 is pointwise, unlike the previous polynomial-window statement.

Let \(b_n\to\infty\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
 b_n=o\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}}\right).
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Then also \(b_n=o(\sqrt n)\), and (4.2) gives
\[
\sup_{d\le b_n}R_{n,d}\le C.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Consequently, from (2.1),
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[D_n^r;K<D_n\le b_n]
=0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Indeed the tail is at most a constant multiple of
\[
\sum_{d>K}d^q\pi_d,
\]
which tends to zero.

Since (2.4) gives
\[
\sqrt{\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}}
=n^{r/(1+r)-o(1)}
\]
in the worst universal case, (5.3) contains the earlier result for every fixed \(\gamma<r/(1+r)\), but is stronger when the actual central atom decays more slowly.

A necessary condition for any pointwise root amplification is now explicit. If a sequence \((n_j,d_j)\) with \(d_j=o(n_j)\) has
\[
R_{n_j,d_j}\to\infty,
\]
then necessarily
\[
\boxed{
 d_j\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{n_j}+1}{n_j}}
+\frac{d_j^2}{n_j}\to\infty.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
Thus any counterexample must live beyond the self-normalized zero-count smoothing scale, not merely beyond the fixed-shift ratio scale.

# 6. NEW: growing-block equivalence of ensembles by entropy tensorization

This run also records a general block estimate which may be useful for the all-level prefix problem.

Because \(Q_n\) is simply \(\pi^{\otimes n}\) conditioned on an event of probability \(a_n\),
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_n\|\pi^{\otimes n})=\lambda_n.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Let \(Q_n^{(s)}\) be the marginal of the first \(s\) coordinates. For \(s\le n/2\), partition the first \(\lfloor n/s\rfloor s\) coordinates into \(\lfloor n/s\rfloor\) disjoint blocks of length \(s\). Relative entropy with respect to a product reference is superadditive over disjoint marginals, and exchangeability makes every length-\(s\) block marginal equal to \(Q_n^{(s)}\). Hence
\[
\lambda_n
\ge
\left\lfloor\frac ns\right\rfloor
D(Q_n^{(s)}\|\pi^{\otimes s}),
\]
so
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_n^{(s)}\|\pi^{\otimes s})
\le
\frac{\lambda_n}{\lfloor n/s\rfloor}
\le
\frac{2s\lambda_n}{n}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Pinsker therefore yields
\[
\boxed{
\|Q_n^{(s)}-\pi^{\otimes s}\|_{\rm TV}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{s\lambda_n}{n}}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
In particular, for every deterministic
\[
 s_n=o\!\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right),
\tag{6.4}
\]
the **entire growing block** of the first \(s_n\) conditioned coordinates is asymptotically iid in total variation.

Using (2.4), the universal exponent supplied by the current denominator theorem is
\[
\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}=n^{2r/(1+r)-o(1)}.
\tag{6.5}
\]
This does not by itself imply endpoint \(q\)-moment UI: a single exceptional coordinate can carry an order-one \(q\)-moment while being invisible in total variation on sublinear blocks. It does, however, rule out any proposed counterexample mechanism requiring a macroscopic distortion of a block of size \(o(n/(\lambda_n+1))\).

## 7. All-level prefix representation and the remaining obstruction

For a fixed \(k\), let \(t\) be a valid first-\(k\)-generation prefix with
\[
N=1+Z_1+\cdots+Z_{k-1},
\qquad L=Z_k,
\qquad m=n-N.
\]
The interior offspring list of \(t\) has total
\[
N-1+L.
\]
Let \(A_t\) be the event that the first \(N\) coordinates of the conditioned allocation \(Q_n\) equal this exact interior offspring list. Then
\[
Q_n(A_t)
=
\Pr_{\rm GW}(T^{(k)}=t)
\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L)}{a_n}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The exact conditioned-tree prefix law is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(T_n^{(k)}=t)
=\frac{nL}{m}\,Q_n(A_t).
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
For different valid prefixes \(t\), the events \(A_t\) are disjoint: from an offspring list, breadth-first exploration determines the successive generation boundaries and hence determines \(N\) and the unique first-\(k\)-generation prefix whenever it exists.

Thus the conditioned tree is an \(L\)-size-bias of a **stopping-prefix subprobability** inside the conditioned iid allocation. This reformulation is exact and exposes the all-level difficulty cleanly. On the non-negligible region where \(m\asymp n\),
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r]
\asymp
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\!\left[
L^q\mathbf1_{\{\text{first }k\text{ generations form a valid prefix}\}}
\right].
\tag{7.3}
\]
The entropy lemma (6.3) makes every deterministic short block asymptotically iid, but the stopping length \(N\) can grow with \(L\), and endpoint \(q\)-moments are not controlled by total variation alone. This is the exact point where a root-only argument remains insufficient.

The earlier exponential-drop lemma remains useful: outside a conditioned-negligible event, a prefix with sublinear \(L\) has
\[
N\le C_k(1+L).
\tag{7.4}
\]
Hence (6.3) can control the **law** of such prefixes whenever \(L=o(n/(\lambda_n+1))\), but a further moment-compatible estimate is required to control their \(L^q\) weight.

## 8. Failed / insufficient routes checked this run

1. **Using Palm concentration without self-normalization.** This gives only the earlier window \(d\sqrt{\lambda_n/n}\to0\). The Palm normalization in Section 4 is essential to obtain the pointwise inequality rather than merely a good-event statement.
2. **Optimizing (4.1) with degree-dependent tail weights.** Choosing \(\delta\) using \(\log(1/(d^q\pi_d))\) makes the bad-Palm term smaller but increases the factorial term. Under only \(\sum d^q\pi_d<\infty\), no summable improvement beyond (4.2) follows uniformly; taking fractional powers of \(d^q\pi_d\) is not justified because those powers need not be summable.
3. **Block total variation alone.** Equation (6.3) is strong for bounded block observables but does not control an endpoint \(q\)-moment; a single sparse exceptional coordinate can remain invisible to total variation on sublinear blocks.
4. **A fixed-core fragmentation of one large degree.** Replacing degree \(d\) by \(O(d)\) bounded degrees incurs a finite-population/falling-factorial correction of order \(\exp[-c d^2/n]\). This is precisely non-negligible near and above the natural \(\sqrt n\) scale, so the method cannot simply be extrapolated to the full mesoscopic window.
5. **Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds.** Their exponential dependence on the convolution count still exceeds the available \(q\)-moment budget.

## 11. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to exploit the Palm law in a way that uses **all low-degree counts simultaneously**, not only \(N_0\). Under the Palm event \(S_{n-1}=n-1-d\), a negative exponential tilt makes that shifted total the exact mean and increases the relative weight of low offspring values. The zero-only renewal switch gains from this tilt but loses \(\exp[-O(d^2/n)]\) because it asks for \(O(d)\) distinct zero fillers. A canonical finite-population switch using an entire low-degree filler block may cancel this collision entropy. A successful construction should replace the exponent in (4.2) by a moment-compatible bound independent of \(d\), which would prove \(\mathbb E N_d\le Cn\pi_d\) and close the root case. Only after that should the same Palm/stopping-prefix mechanism be pushed through (7.2) to settle every fixed level.
