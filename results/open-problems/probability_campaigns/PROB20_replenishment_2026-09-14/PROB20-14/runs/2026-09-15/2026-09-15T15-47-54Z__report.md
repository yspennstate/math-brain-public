## 2. Exact root identities retained

Let \(D_n:=\ell_1(T_n)\), and let \(N_d(T_n)\) be the number of vertices of outdegree \(d\). The exact root formula is
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac{n}{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The cyclic lemma gives
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac d{n-1}\,\mathbb E N_d(T_n),
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac1{n-1}
\sum_{d>K}d^q\,\mathbb E N_d(T_n).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q\mathbf1_{\{\xi_1>K\}}\mid S_n=n-1].
\tag{2.4}
\]
Thus \(k=1\) is exactly an endpoint \(q\)-uniform-integrability problem for one coordinate under exact central conditioning.

Let
\[
a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad \lambda_n:=-\log a_n.
\]
The recovered finite-core denominator theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n=o(n^\beta),\qquad
\beta:=\frac2q-1=\frac{1-r}{1+r}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
The earlier all-level escape theorem gives, for every fixed \(k\), a deterministic \(c_n=o(n^{E_r})\),
\[
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2},
\]
such that
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
\tag{2.6}
\]
The latest checkpoint also proves exact canonical recentring into \(W_q\)-stable triangular arrays and an \(O(N^{-1})\) adjacent-lattice additive smoothing estimate, but not a multiplicative local-ratio bound.

# 3. NEW: a renewal-switching identity that sums all equal-sum factorial switches

The previous report compared one large degree with one fixed core representation of the same total. That single representation incurs growing-order factorial errors. This run removes the choice of representation entirely by summing over **all lengths and all degree patterns having total \(d\)**. The sum is governed by the ordinary renewal measure of the offspring law.

Fix an admissible conditioned size \(n\), and work under the conditioned allocation law
\[
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1),
\]
which has the same degree-count law as \(T_n\) by the cyclic lemma.

For \(s\ge1\) and \(d\ge1\), define
\[
T_{s,d}:=
\#\{(v_1,\ldots,v_s):
\text{distinct ordered vertices and }
 d_{v_1}+\cdots+d_{v_s}=d\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Let
\[
u_s(d):=\Pr(S_s=d).
\]
Exchangeability gives two exact formulas with the **same residual local probability**:
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}T_{s,d}
=(n)_s\,u_s(d)
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-s}=n-1-d)}{a_n},
\tag{3.2}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\big[N_d(N_0)_{s-1}\big]
=(n)_s\,\pi_d\pi_0^{s-1}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-s}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\frac{T_{s,d}}{(n)_s}
=
\frac{u_s(d)}{\pi_d\pi_0^{s-1}}
\mathbb E_{Q_n}
\frac{N_d(N_0)_{s-1}}{(n)_s}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Summing over \(1\le s\le n\), define
\[
\mathcal O_{n,d}
:=\sum_{s=1}^n\frac{T_{s,d}}{(n)_s},
\tag{3.5}
\]
and
\[
H_{n,d}(z)
:=\sum_{s=1}^n
u_s(d)\,
\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Then the exact summed switching identity is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\mathcal O_{n,d}
=
\frac1{\pi_d}
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\big[N_dH_{n,d}(N_0)\big].
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
No local-probability ratio remains in (3.7).

# 4. NEW: the random-permutation occupation side is uniformly bounded

Conditional on a fixed degree multiset, take a uniformly random permutation of the \(n\) vertex degrees and let
\[
R_s=d_{V_1}+\cdots+d_{V_s}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{T_{s,d}}{(n)_s}=\Pr(R_s=d\mid\text{degree multiset}),
\]
so \(\mathcal O_{n,d}\) is the conditional expected number of visits of the nondecreasing partial sums \((R_s)\) to level \(d\).

Let \(N_+=n-N_0\) be the number of positive degrees. Delete the zero degrees from the random permutation. The positive partial sums are strictly increasing, hence they can hit \(d>0\) at most once. If they hit \(d\) after the \(j\)-th positive degree, the full partial sum remains equal to \(d\) during the zero gap immediately following that positive degree.

Conditional on the order of the positive degrees, the \(N_0\) zeros are distributed exchangeably among the \(N_++1\) gaps before, between, and after the positive degrees. Each gap has mean
\[
\frac{N_0}{N_++1}.
\]
Therefore, for every fixed degree multiset and every \(d>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{n,d}
\le_{m expectation}
1+\frac{N_0}{N_++1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
More explicitly, the conditional expectation of \(\mathcal O_{n,d}\) given the degree multiset is at most the right-hand side.

Choose a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(\pi_0+\varepsilon<1\). Unconditionally, \(N_0\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\pi_0)\), so Hoeffding gives
\[
\Pr(N_0>(\pi_0+\varepsilon)n)\le e^{-c_\varepsilon n}.
\]
After conditioning on \(S_n=n-1\), division by \(a_n=e^{-o(n)}\) still gives
\[
Q_n(N_0>(\pi_0+\varepsilon)n)\le e^{-c'_\varepsilon n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
On the complementary event, the right side of (4.1) is bounded by a fixed constant; on the bad event, trivially \(\mathcal O_{n,d}\le n\). Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{n\ {m admissible}}\sup_{d\ge1}
\mathbb E_{Q_n}\mathcal O_{n,d}<\infty.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
Combining (3.7) and (4.3),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_n}[N_dH_{n,d}(N_0)]
\le C\pi_d
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
uniformly in \(n,d\).

This is the principal exact estimate of the run.

# 5. Renewal mass supplies the missing lower bound on the switch functional

Define the renewal mass of the offspring walk
\[
U(d):=\sum_{s\ge1}\Pr(S_s=d).
\tag{5.1}
\]
After scaling by the span \(h\), the increment law is an arithmetic nonnegative law of span one and finite positive mean \(1/h\). The arithmetic renewal theorem therefore gives, along \(d\in h\mathbb Z\),
\[
\boxed{U(d)\longrightarrow h.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
The atom at zero causes repeated visits but does not invalidate the renewal theorem because the mean is positive.

We also need that a linear range of sample sizes captures a positive fraction of this renewal mass. Let
\[
p_+:=\Pr(\xi>0)>0,
\qquad a_*:=\min\{j\ge1:\pi_j>0\}.
\]
If \(S_s=d\), the number of positive increments is at most \(d/a_*\). But the number of positive increments in \(s\) iid trials is \(\mathrm{Bin}(s,p_+)\). Choose \(C_0>2/(a_*p_+)\). For \(s>C_0d\),
\[
\Pr(S_s=d)
\le
\Pr(\mathrm{Bin}(s,p_+)\le d/a_*)
\le e^{-c s}
\tag{5.3}
\]
by a fixed binomial lower-tail Chernoff bound. Hence
\[
\sum_{s>C_0d}\Pr(S_s=d)\le e^{-c'd}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Combining with (5.2), there are constants \(d_0,c_0>0\) such that for every sufficiently large supported/admissible \(d\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le s\le C_0d}\Pr(S_s=d)\ge c_0.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

# 6. NEW theorem: an explicit polynomial root window has the sharp size-biased bound

We now lower-bound \(H_{n,d}(N_0)\) on a high-probability event.

For \(z=N_0\), set
\[
R_{n,s}(z)
:=
n\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}.
\]
Because \((n)_s=n(n-1)\cdots(n-s+1)\),
\[
R_{n,s}(z)
=
\prod_{j=0}^{s-2}
\frac{z-j}{\pi_0(n-1-j)}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
If
\[
\left|\frac zn-\pi_0\right|\le\delta_n,
\qquad s\le C_0d,
\]
then, uniformly while \(d=o(n)\),
\[
\log R_{n,s}(z)
=O(s\delta_n+s^2/n).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
d\delta_n+d^2/n\to0,
\tag{6.3}
\]
then for all sufficiently large \(n\), all \(s\le C_0d\), and every \(z\) in that event,
\[
R_{n,s}(z)\ge\frac12.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Using (5.5),
\[
\boxed{
H_{n,d}(N_0)\ge \frac{c}{n}
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
on the same event, uniformly for large supported \(d\) satisfying (6.3).

To choose \(\delta_n\), use Hoeffding before conditioning:
\[
\Pr(|N_0-n\pi_0|>n\delta)
\le2e^{-2n\delta^2}.
\]
After conditioning,
\[
Q_n\left(\left|N_0/n-\pi_0\right|>\delta\right)
\le2\exp(\lambda_n-2n\delta^2).
\tag{6.6}
\]
Fix any \(A>1\) and take
\[
\delta_n
:=
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n+A\log n}{n}}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Increasing the harmless constant in (6.7) if needed gives
\[
Q_n(G_n^c)\le n^{-A},
\qquad
G_n:=\{|N_0/n-\pi_0|\le\delta_n\}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

From (4.4) and (6.5), for every \(d\) in the range (6.3),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_n}[N_d;G_n]
\le Cn\pi_d.
}
\tag{6.9}
\]
This is the sharp size-biased degree-count bound on the good event.

Now use the quantitative denominator exponent (2.5). Since
\[
\lambda_n=o(n^\beta),
\qquad
\beta=\frac{1-r}{1+r},
\]
we have
\[
\delta_n=o\!\left(n^{-r/(1+r)}\right)
\tag{6.10}
\]
(up to the smaller \(\sqrt{\log n/n}\) term). Therefore, for every fixed
\[
\boxed{0<\gamma<\frac{r}{1+r},}
\tag{6.11}
\]
all degrees \(d\le n^\gamma\) satisfy
\[
d\delta_n+d^2/n\to0.
\tag{6.12}
\]
The second term is valid because \(r/(1+r)<1/2\).

Take \(A>q\gamma+2\) in (6.8). By (2.3), on \(G_n\),
\[
\frac1{n-1}
\sum_{K<d\le n^\gamma}d^q\mathbb E[N_d;G_n]
\le
C\sum_{d>K}d^q\pi_d,
\tag{6.13}
\]
which tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\). On \(G_n^c\), deterministically
\[
\frac1{n-1}\sum_{d\le n^\gamma}d^qN_d
\le Cn^{q\gamma},
\]
so its expectation is at most
\[
Cn^{q\gamma}Q_n(G_n^c)=o(1).
\tag{6.14}
\]
We have proved:

### Theorem 6.1 — polynomial low-degree root UI
For every fixed \(\gamma<r/(1+r)\),
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}
\limsup_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\ {m admissible}}}
\mathbb E\!\left[
D_n^r;
K<D_n\le n^\gamma
\right]
=0.
}
\tag{6.15}
\]

Equivalently, the root conjecture is now proved uniformly through every polynomial degree scale strictly below
\[
n^{r/(1+r)}.
\]
No infinite-variance local limit theorem is used.

This theorem is strictly stronger than the earlier unspecified diagonal fixed-shift window: it supplies a universal polynomial window depending only on \(r\), using the quantitative denominator theorem plus the renewal-switching identity.

# 8. Why the renewal-switching argument does not yet extend automatically to every level

For a fixed deeper level, the exact prefix density is
\[
W_n
=
\frac{nL}{m}
\frac{\Pr(S_m=m-L)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)},
\qquad
L=Z_k,\quad m=n-\sum_{j<k}Z_j.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The new argument uses a global degree-count identity specific to the size-biased root: \(\Pr(D_n=d)=d\,\mathbb EN_d/(n-1)\). There is no immediate analogous identity expressing \(Z_k\) as a single global empirical degree count. Thus Theorem 6.1 does **not** settle the all-level target.

The canonical \(W_q\)-recentring and Bernoulli smoothing from the previous checkpoint do apply after every non-negligible fixed-level prefix, so the natural next task is to construct a **prefix renewal-switching identity** for the \(L\) boundary trees, or prove the previously isolated shifted linear convolution estimate
\[
u^{*L}_{m-O_k(L)}\lesssim L u_m
\tag{8.2}
\]
for the total-progeny excess law \(u\). Either would transfer the moment-compatible linear factor from the root to arbitrary fixed level.

# 9. Failed/insufficient routes recorded this run

1. **Single core representation switching.** The previous factorial identity comparing one large degree to one fixed representation incurs a \(s^{-1/2}\) local-probability factor and growing-order core-count errors. It does not by itself give a moment-compatible bound beyond a very small range.
2. **Summing fixed-core representations only.** A defective renewal mass appears because requiring every replacement degree to lie in a finite core inserts a factor \(p_F^s\); this decays exponentially with \(d\). The successful identity instead sums over **all offspring patterns** with total \(d\), producing the full renewal mass \(U(d)\to h\).
3. **Additive Bernoulli smoothing alone.** The prior \(O(N^{-1})\) adjacent-pmf bound remains additive. Dividing it by a potentially very small central atom costs too much at the endpoint.
4. **Generic conditional moment theorems.** Janson's 2001 paper, *Moment convergence in conditional limit theorems*, treats triangular arrays and applications to branching processes, but its main framework is a normal conditional limit theorem with additional hypotheses. It is not used here as a black-box solution to this infinite-variance \(q<2\) endpoint.

# 12. Next concrete attack

The strongest next route is to **randomize the switch size around the renewal hitting time instead of controlling only \(N_0\)**. In (3.7), the only reason the proof stops at \(d\delta_n=o(1)\) is that the factor
\[
(N_0)_{s-1}/\{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s\}
\]
is sensitive to zero-count fluctuations when \(s\asymp d\). A more symmetric switch that replaces the \(s-1\) zeros by an iid-like background pattern of the same total should cancel this dependence. The all-pattern renewal formulation now makes that precise: seek a second renewal identity in which both sides use empirical background counts and the likelihood ratio is controlled by the canonical \(W_q\) law rather than by one atom count. If successful, the same device may admit a forest/prefix version and attack all fixed levels rather than only \(k=1\).

In parallel, a counterexample must now evade all of the following simultaneously: finite-q missing-mean budget, canonical \(W_q\) recentring, a fixed Bernoulli smoothing component, the full renewal switching identity, and polynomial root control through every exponent \(\gamma<r/(1+r)\).
