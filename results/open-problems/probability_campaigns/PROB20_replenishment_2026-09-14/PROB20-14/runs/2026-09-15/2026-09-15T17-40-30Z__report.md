\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]

\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad p:=\frac q{q-1},\qquad

## 2. Cumulative exact identities retained

Let
\[
D_n:=\ell_1(T_n),\qquad
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1),
\qquad
a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\]
and put
\[
\lambda_n:=-\log a_n.
\]
The exact root formula and cyclic-lemma degree-count formula are
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)
=\frac{n}{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n},
\tag{2.1}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac d{n-1}\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_d.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q\mathbf1_{\{\xi_1>K\}}\mid S_n=n-1].
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The finite-core denominator theorem proved in an earlier archived run gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n=o(n^\beta),\qquad
\beta=\frac2q-1=\frac{1-r}{1+r}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]
The arbitrary-law large-width theorem gives, for every fixed \(k\), a deterministic \(c_n\to\infty\) with
\[
c_n=o(n^{E_r}),
\qquad
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2},
\tag{2.5}
\]
such that
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;\ell_k(T_n)\ge c_n]\to0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The previous report defined the moving ratio
\[
R_{n,d}:=
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}
\tag{2.7}
\]
and the renewal-switching functional
\[
H_{n,d}(z)
:=\sum_{s=1}^n
\Pr(S_s=d)\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}.
\tag{2.8}
\]
It proved the exact self-normalized Palm inequality
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}\,\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]\le C,
}
\tag{2.9}
\]
where under \(\mathbb P^*_{n,d}\), after selecting one degree-\(d\) coordinate, the remaining \(N=n-1\) coordinates are iid \(\pi\) conditioned on
\[
S_N=N-d.
\tag{2.10}
\]

# 3. NEW renewal localization: the useful splitting lengths satisfy \(s\sim d\)

Let
\[
u_s(d):=\Pr(S_s=d),\qquad
U(d):=\sum_{s\ge1}\nu_s(d).
\]
The arithmetic renewal theorem gives
\[
U(d)\to h
\quad\text{as }d\to\infty,\ d\in h\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The previous run used only a coarse linear window \(s\le C_0d\). Here the renewal mass is localized around the natural index \(s=d\).

### Lemma 3.1
For every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|s-d|\le\varepsilon d}\Pr(S_s=d)\longrightarrow h
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
along \(d\in h\mathbb Z\).

### Proof
Because \(\xi\ge0\), \(S_s\) is nondecreasing in \(s\). Put \(m_-:=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)d\rfloor\). If the renewal process visits \(d\) by time \(m_-\), then \(S_{m_-}\ge d\). Once \(d\) is first hit, the number of consecutive visits to \(d\) before the next positive increment is geometric with mean \((1-\pi_0)^{-1}\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{s\le m_-}\Pr(S_s=d)
\le \frac1{1-\pi_0}\Pr(S_{m_-}\ge d)
\to0
\tag{3.3}
\]
by the ordinary law of large numbers, because \(d/m_->1\).

For the late side put \(m_+:=\lceil(1+\varepsilon)d\rceil\). The renewal sequence \(U(y)\) is uniformly bounded in \(y\): it converges along the span by (3.1), and only finitely many values remain. Conditional on \(S_{m_+}=x\le d\), the expected number of future visits to \(d\) is at most \(\sup_yU(y)\). Hence
\[
\sum_{s\ge m_+}\Pr(S_s=d)
\le C\Pr(S_{m_+}\le d)
\to0
\tag{3.4}
\]
by the law of large numbers, because \(d/m_+<1\). Subtracting (3.3)–(3.4) from (3.1) proves (3.2). \(\square\)

This localization is important because the finite-population penalty in the previous switching proof disappears once the exact zero/excess identity under the degree Palm law is used.

# 4. NEW exact zero/excess identity under the degree Palm law

Define
\[
Y:=(\xi-1)_+.
\]
Criticality gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EY=\pi_0.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed \(\xi-1=Y-\mathbf1_{\{\xi=0\}}\) and \(\mathbb E(\xi-1)=0\).

Under \(\mathbb P^*_{n,d}\), let \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N\) correspond to the remaining \(N=n-1\) coordinates. Since their sum is \(S_N=N-d\),
\[
-d=S_N-N
=\sum_{i=1}^N(Y_i-\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i=0\}}).
\]
The selected degree-\(d\) coordinate is nonzero. Thus its Palm zero count satisfies the **deterministic** identity
\[
\boxed{
N_0=d+\sum_{i=1}^NY_i.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
This is substantially stronger than merely knowing that \(N_0\) is close to \(\pi_0N\): a high selected degree automatically shifts the zero count upward by \(d\), except to the extent that the positive-excess sum \(\sum Y_i\) itself falls below its mean.

Because \(Y\) has finite \(q\)-moment, the same one-sided Laplace argument used earlier gives constants \(c,C>0\) such that, for \(0<\ell\le cN\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i-\pi_0N\le-\ell\right)
\le
\exp\!\left[-c\ell^pN^{-(p-1)}\right].
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
No variance or stable-domain assumption is used.

# 5. NEW complementary Palm theorem: large moving shifts have bounded local ratio

### Theorem 5.1
There exist constants \(c,C,d_0>0\) such that for every sufficiently large admissible \(n\) and every supported
\[
d_0\le d\le c n,
\]
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C+
\exp\!\left[
\lambda_n-c\frac{d^p}{n^{p-1}}
\right].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Consequently, for a sufficiently large law-dependent constant \(A\),
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}\le C
\quad\text{whenever}\quad
 d\ge A n^{1/q}(\lambda_n+1)^{(q-1)/q},\ d\le cn.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof
Choose fixed \(\eta,\varepsilon>0\) so small that
\[
\pi_0-\eta-(1-\pi_0)\varepsilon>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]
For example, take \(\eta=\pi_0/4\) and then \(\varepsilon>0\) with \((1-\pi_0)\varepsilon\le\pi_0/4\).

Under the Palm law define
\[
G:=\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^NY_i\ge\pi_0N-\eta d
\right\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
By (4.2), on \(G\),
\[
N_0\ge\pi_0N+(1-\eta)d.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Now take a splitting length \(s\) with
\[
|s-d|\le\varepsilon d.
\]
For every \(0\le j\le s-2\), (5.5) gives
\[
N_0-j-\pi_0(N-j)
\ge
(1-\eta)d-(1-\pi_0)j
\ge
[\pi_0-\eta-(1-\pi_0)\varepsilon]d>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]
Therefore every factor is at least one:
\[
\frac{(N_0)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(N)_{s-1}}
\ge1.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Since
\[
n\frac{(N_0)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}
=
\frac{(N_0)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(N)_{s-1}},
\]
Lemma 3.1 yields, for all sufficiently large supported \(d\),
\[
\boxed{
 nH_{n,d}(N_0)\ge c_0
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
on \(G\), with \(c_0>0\) independent of \(n,d\).

Before Palm conditioning, the residual variables are iid \(\pi\). By (4.3),
\[
\Pr(G^c)\le
\exp\!\left[-c d^p n^{-(p-1)}\right].
\tag{5.9}
\]
The Palm conditioning event has probability
\[
a_nR_{n,d}=e^{-\lambda_n}R_{n,d}.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb P^*_{n,d}(G^c)
\le
\frac{
\exp[\lambda_n-cd^p/n^{p-1}]
}{R_{n,d}}.
\tag{5.10}
\]
Insert (5.8)–(5.10) into the self-normalized inequality (2.9):
\[
C
\ge
R_{n,d}\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]
\ge
c_0R_{n,d}\left(1-\min\left\{1,
\frac{e^{\lambda_n-cd^p/n^{p-1}}}{R_{n,d}}
\right\}\right).
\]
Exactly as in the previous Palm argument, this gives
\[
R_{n,d}\le C'+e^{\lambda_n-cd^p/n^{p-1}},
\]
which is (5.1). Choosing \(d\) as in (5.2) makes the exponential term uniformly bounded. \(\square\)

### Interpretation
The previous Palm theorem controlled **small** moving shifts by concentration of the zero count. The new theorem controls **large** moving shifts by the exact identity \(N_0=d+\sum Y_i\). The only remaining root obstruction is genuinely intermediate.

# 6. NEW entropy–truncated-variance estimate for the one-coordinate marginal

The previous entropy tensorization gives
\[
D(Q_n\|\pi^{\otimes n})=\lambda_n.
\]
Since the reference is a product and \(Q_n\) is exchangeable,
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_{n,1}\|\pi)\le\frac{\lambda_n}{n},
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
where \(Q_{n,1}\) is the one-coordinate marginal.

We now exploit the exact mean constraint, rather than Pinsker alone.

For \(K\ge2\), define
\[
f_K(x):=(x-1)_+\mathbf1_{\{x\le K\}},
\qquad
V_K:=\mathbb E_\pi[f_K(\xi)^2],
\qquad
m_K:=\mathbb E_\pi[(\xi-1)_+;\xi>K].
\tag{6.2}
\]
Finite \(q\)-moment implies
\[
\boxed{
K^{q-2}V_K\to0,
\qquad
K^{q-1}m_K\to0.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

We use the elementary entropy–Bernstein inequality: if \(P,Q\) are probability laws, \(D(Q\|P)=D\), and \(0\le f\le K\), then
\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb E_Qf-\mathbb E_Pf|
\le
C\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_P(f)D}+KD\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
This follows directly from the variational formula for relative entropy and the Bernstein mgf bound for the bounded centered variable \(f-\mathbb E_Pf\).

Let \(X\sim Q_{n,1}\), and set \(D_n^{(1)}:=D(Q_{n,1}\|\pi)\). Since
\[
X-1=(X-1)_+-\mathbf1_{\{X=0\}}
\]
and \(\mathbb E_QX=1-1/n\),
\[
\mathbb E_Q(X-1)_+
=Q(X=0)-1/n.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Under \(\pi\), criticality gives \(\mathbb E(\xi-1)_+=\pi_0\). Pinsker for the zero indicator and (6.4) for \(f_K\) therefore yield
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Q[(X-1)_+;X>K]
\le
m_K+C\left(
\sqrt{D_n^{(1)}}+\sqrt{V_KD_n^{(1)}}+K D_n^{(1)}
\right).
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

For the dyadic shell \(K<X\le2K\), \(X\le2(X-1)_+\) and thus
\[
X^q\le C K^{q-1}(X-1)_+.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Q[X^q;K<X\le2K]
\le
C K^{q-1}
\left(
 m_K+\sqrt{D_n^{(1)}}+\sqrt{V_KD_n^{(1)}}+K D_n^{(1)}
\right).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
This is the key new medium-scale estimate.

# 7. NEW root UI window up to \((n/(\lambda_n+1))^{1/q}\)

Sum (6.7) over dyadic shells \(K_j=2^jK\) up to a cutoff \(B\). The \(\pi\)-tail terms satisfy
\[
\sum_{K_j\le B}K_j^{q-1}m_{K_j}
\le
C\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K],
\tag{7.1}
\]
by exchanging the order of summation. Since \(V_K\) is increasing,
\[
\sum_{K_j\le B}K_j^{q-1}\sqrt{V_{K_j}D}
\le
C B^{q-1}\sqrt{V_BD}.
\]
Using (6.1), for every \(B\le n\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{Q_{n,1}}[X^q;K<X\le B]
\le{}&
C\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]\\
&+C\left[
B^{q-1}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n}{n}}
+B^{q-1}\sqrt{V_B\frac{\lambda_n}{n}}
+B^q\frac{\lambda_n}{n}
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Theorem 7.1
Let \(B_n\to\infty\) satisfy
\[
\boxed{
B_n^q\frac{\lambda_n+1}{n}\to0.
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[D_n^r;K<D_n\le B_n]=0.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof
By (2.3), the desired quantity differs only by a factor \(n/(n-1)\) from the left side of (7.2). Under (7.3),
\[
B_n^{q-1}\sqrt{\lambda_n/n}\to0
\]
because its square is
\[
(B_n^q\lambda_n/n)B_n^{q-2}\to0.
\]
Likewise
\[
B_n^{q-1}\sqrt{V_{B_n}\lambda_n/n}\to0
\]
because the square equals
\[
(B_n^q\lambda_n/n)(B_n^{q-2}V_{B_n})\to0
\]
by (6.3). The final error in (7.2) tends to zero directly from (7.3). Finally let \(K\to\infty\) and use \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\). \(\square\)

### Universal polynomial consequence
Using (2.4), any fixed exponent
\[
\boxed{
\gamma<\frac{2(q-1)}{q^2}
=\frac{2r}{(1+r)^2}
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
may be used in \(B_n=n^\gamma\). Thus the arbitrary-law root UI region improves from the previous
\[
\gamma<\frac r{1+r}
\]
to
\[
\boxed{
\gamma<\frac{2r}{(1+r)^2}.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
The improvement is strict for every \(0<r<1\).

# 8. The root obstruction is now confined to one self-normalized middle window

Let
\[
L_n:=\lambda_n+1.
\]
Theorem 7.1 controls every cutoff
\[
B_n=o\left((n/L_n)^{1/q}\right).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Theorem 5.1 gives a uniformly bounded moving ratio once
\[
d\ge A n^{1/q}L_n^{(q-1)/q}
\tag{8.2}
\]
for sufficiently large fixed \(A\); summing \(d^q\pi_d\) then kills that upper region. The already proved large-width theorem handles the far end uniformly as well.

Therefore any root-level failure must be concentrated in the intermediate window
\[
\boxed{
(n/L_n)^{1/q}\ \lesssim\ d\ \lesssim\
 n^{1/q}L_n^{(q-1)/q},
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
up to arbitrarily slowly varying margins. The ratio of the two natural endpoints is exactly of order
\[
L_n=\lambda_n+1.
\tag{8.4}
\]
This is substantially sharper than the previous polynomial-window description. A counterexample must now create an endpoint \(q\)-moment anomaly inside a multiplicative \(\lambda_n\)-window around the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale.

In the worst universal denominator regime (2.4), the lower endpoint has exponent
\[
\frac{2r}{(1+r)^2},
\]
while the upper endpoint has exponent
\[
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}.
\]
The gap remains nonempty; this run does not close \(k=1\).

# 9. NEW all-fixed-level low-window theorem from block entropy

The entropy idea also yields a genuine every-level result, although at a smaller scale than the root theorem.

For a fixed level \(k\), let \(t\) be a valid truncated prefix, with
\[
N(t)=1+\ell_1(t)+\cdots+\ell_{k-1}(t),
\qquad
L(t)=\ell_k(t).
\]
If \(A_t\) is the corresponding event for the first \(N(t)\) coordinates of the conditioned allocation, the exact prefix identity is
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(T_n^{(k)}=t)
=
\frac{nL(t)}{n-N(t)}Q_n(A_t).
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
Earlier archived work proves that, outside a contribution which is \(o(1)\) in the target fractional tail, one may restrict to prefixes satisfying
\[
n-N(t)\ge\delta_k n,
\qquad
N(t)\le C_k(1+L(t)).
\tag{9.2}
\]
The second condition follows from the factor-two downward-jump argument; the first excludes the exponentially negligible small-remainder region.

Fix a cutoff \(B\), and on (9.2) with \(L\le B\) put
\[
M:=\lceil C_k(1+B)\rceil.
\]
The valid prefix is then a measurable function of the first \(M\) allocation coordinates. Let \(F_{K,B}\) be \(L^q\mathbf1_{\{K<L\le B\}}\) on these valid good prefixes and zero otherwise. From (9.1),
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;K<\ell_k(T_n)\le B,\text{good prefix}]
\le C_k\mathbb E_{Q_n^{(M)}}F_{K,B}.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Under the product law \(\pi^{\otimes M}\), this exploration is just the ordinary Galton–Watson prefix, so
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi^{\otimes M}}F_{K,B}
\le
\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k>K]	o0
\quad(K\to\infty),
\tag{9.4}
\]
and \(\mathbb E Z_k^q<\infty\) by von Bahr–Esseen.

The previous block-entropy theorem gives, for \(M\le n/2\),
\[
D(Q_n^{(M)}\|\pi^{\otimes M})
\le\frac{2M\lambda_n}{n}.
\tag{9.5}
\]
Moreover
\[
0\le F_{K,B}\le B^q,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi^{\otimes M}}(F_{K,B})
\le B^q\mathbb E Z_k^q.
\tag{9.6}
\]
Applying the entropy–Bernstein inequality (6.4) to (9.3) gives an error bounded by
\[
C_k\left[
B^{q/2}\sqrt{\frac{M\lambda_n}{n}}
+B^q\frac{M\lambda_n}{n}
\right]
\le
C_k\left[
B^{(q+1)/2}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_n}{n}}
+B^{q+1}\frac{\lambda_n}{n}
\right].
\tag{9.7}
\]

### Theorem 9.1
For every fixed level \(k\), if \(B_n\to\infty\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
B_n^{q+1}\frac{\lambda_n+1}{n}\to0,
}
\tag{9.8}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[\ell_k(T_n)^r;
K<\ell_k(T_n)\le B_n]=0.
}
\tag{9.9}
\]
The negligible bad-prefix contribution from (9.2) is included when passing to the full expectation.

Using (2.4), this gives the explicit arbitrary-law every-level polynomial window
\[
\boxed{
B_n=n^\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma<\frac{2(q-1)}{q(q+1)}
=\frac{2r}{(1+r)(2+r)}.
}
\tag{9.10}
\]
This does **not** close the all-level problem, but it is the first explicit growing polynomial low-width region obtained here simultaneously for every fixed \(k\) from the entropy/prefix method.

## 13. Next attack

The remaining root interval has a clean interpretation. For
\[
d\asymp n^{1/q}\times\text{a factor between }L_n^{-1/q}\text{ and }L_n^{(q-1)/q},
\qquad L_n=\lambda_n+1,
\]
the deficit is on the natural finite-\(q\) fluctuation scale: neither entropy perturbation nor a one-sided large-deviation estimate alone is sharp.

The next proof attack should therefore condition on the positive-excess sum
\[
Y_N=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\xi_i-1)_+
\]
and use the exact identity
\[
N_0=Y_N+d
\]
to derive a **weighted**, rather than pointwise, Palm renewal inequality across the whole middle window. The target is to show directly
\[
\sum_{d\in\text{middle}}d^q\pi_dR_{n,d}\to0
\]
by charging the part with \(Y_N\) far below its mean to the finite-\(q\) tail of \(Y\), while the complementary part has \(nH_{n,d}(N_0)\gtrsim1\) from renewal localization. This avoids trying to make every single \(R_{n,d}\) uniformly bounded.

For every fixed \(k\), the analogous goal is a stopping-prefix version of the same weighted Palm inequality. The block-entropy Theorem 9.1 supplies a nontrivial low-window foothold for that extension.
