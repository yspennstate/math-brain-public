## 1. Exact target, source scope, recovery status

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,
\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree with offspring law \(\xi\), conditioned on total size \(n\), along every admissible \(n\). For every fixed \(k\ge1\), the full target is to decide whether
\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]
is uniformly integrable.

Write
\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad p:=\frac q{q-1},\qquad
\pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d),\qquad
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Critical nondegeneracy gives \(0<\pi_0<1\) and positive mass above 1. Every finite tree size is \(1\pmod h\). A finite subset of the positive support already has gcd \(h\), hence all sufficiently large integers in that residue class are admissible.

## 3. Palm setup retained

For supported \(d>0\), let \(\mathbb P^*_{n,d}\) denote the degree-\(d\) Palm law obtained by selecting one coordinate of the conditioned allocation with value \(d\). The remaining
\[
N:=n-1
\]
coordinates are iid \(\pi\), conditioned on
\[
S_N=N-d.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Put
\[
Y:=(\xi-1)_+.
\]
Criticality gives
\[
\boxed{\mathbb EY=\pi_0.}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Under the Palm conditioning there is the deterministic identity
\[
\boxed{
N_0=d+\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_i,
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
where \(N_0\) is the number of zeros among the remaining coordinates.

The renewal-switching functional is
\[
H_{n,d}(z)
:=
\sum_{s=1}^{n}
\Pr(S_s=d)
\frac{(z)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
An exact switching/occupation argument gives the self-normalized Palm inequality
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}\,
\mathbb E^*_{n,d}[nH_{n,d}(N_0)]
\le C.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
The ordinary arithmetic renewal theorem plus the monotonicity of nonnegative partial sums gives, for every fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{|s-d|\le\varepsilon d}
\Pr(S_s=d)\longrightarrow h
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
along \(d\to\infty\), \(d\in h\mathbb Z\).

The preceding checkpoint used a generic finite-\(q\) lower-deviation estimate to obtain
\[
R_{n,d}
\le C+
\exp\!\left[
\lambda_n-c\frac{d^p}{n^{p-1}}
\right].
\tag{3.7}
\]
The next section strengthens exactly the lower-deviation ingredient at the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale.

# 4. NEW: one-sided truncated-second-moment lower-tail lemma

The finite \(q\)-moment contains more information than the fixed-coefficient bound (3.7) uses. Near the natural scale, a truncation at a fixed fraction of the deviation gains a factor tending to infinity in the exponent.

For a nonnegative random variable \(Y\) with \(\mathbb EY=\mu<\infty\), \(\mathbb EY^q<\infty\), define
\[
V_Y(x):=\mathbb E[Y^2;Y\le x],
\qquad
v_Y(x):=x^{q-2}V_Y(x).
\tag{4.1}
\]
Since \(q<2\) and \(\mathbb EY^q<\infty\),
\[
\boxed{v_Y(x)\to0.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Also
\[
x^{q-1}\mathbb E[Y;Y>x]\to0.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Lemma 4.1
Fix \(\eta>0\) and \(C_*>0\). There are constants \(c>0\) and \(N_0\) such that whenever
\[
N\ge N_0,
\qquad
C_*N^{1/q}\le d=o(N),
\]
then, for any fixed sufficiently small \(\kappa>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_i-N\mu\le-\eta d
\right)
\le
\exp\!\left[
-c\frac{d^q}{N\,v_Y(\kappa d)}
\right].
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
Here the right side is interpreted only once \(v_Y(\kappa d)>0\); if the truncated second moment is zero, the event is eventually impossible.

### Proof
Set
\[
Z:=Y\mathbf1_{\{Y\le\kappa d\}},
\qquad m_d:=\mathbb E[Y;Y>\kappa d].
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb EZ=\mu-m_d.
\]
Because \(d\ge C_*N^{1/q}\),
\[
\frac{Nm_d}{d}
\le
C\frac{N}{d^q}
\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>\kappa d]
\longrightarrow0
\tag{4.5}
\]
uniformly over this range. Hence eventually
\[
Nm_d\le\eta d/2.
\tag{4.6}
\]
The event in (4.4) implies
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_i-\mathbb EZ)\le-\eta d/2.
\tag{4.7}
\]
For any nonnegative \(Z\) and \(t\ge0\),
\[
e^{-tZ}\le1-tZ+\frac{t^2Z^2}{2},
\]
so
\[
\mathbb E e^{-t(Z-\mathbb EZ)}
\le
\exp\!\left(\frac{t^2\mathbb EZ^2}{2}\right).
\tag{4.8}
\]
Chernoff optimization gives
\[
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{N}(Z_i-\mathbb EZ)\le-x
\right)
\le
\exp\!\left[-\frac{x^2}{2N\mathbb EZ^2}\right].
\tag{4.9}
\]
With \(x=\eta d/2\),
\[
\mathbb EZ^2
\le V_Y(\kappa d)
=(\kappa d)^{2-q}v_Y(\kappa d).
\]
Absorbing \(\eta,\kappa\) into the constant yields (4.4). \(\square\)

### Comment
At the natural scale \(d\asymp N^{1/q}\), the older bound (3.7) has an exponent of constant order. Lemma 4.1 instead gives exponent
\[
\asymp \frac1{v_Y(\kappa d)}\to\infty.
\]
This is the first arbitrary-law estimate in the campaign that becomes increasingly strong exactly at the natural finite-\(q\) fluctuation scale.

# 5. NEW: strengthened Palm local-ratio theorem with factorial boost

The Palm identity (3.3) does more than produce a lower-tail exceptional event: on its complement, it increases the zero count by order \(d\). For renewal lengths \(s\sim d\), this makes the exact falling-factorial switching factor exponentially favorable on the scale \(d^2/n\).

### Theorem 5.1
There exist law-dependent constants \(C_*,c_0,c_1,C<\infty\) such that, for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\) and every supported
\[
C_*n^{1/q}\le d\le c_0n,
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C\exp\!\left[-c_1\frac{d^2}{n}\right]
+
\exp\!\left[
\lambda_n-
 c_1\frac{d^q}{n\,v_Y(c_1d)}
\right].
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Combining with the earlier fractional-Chernoff rate, one may replace the second exponent by
\[
\lambda_n-c
\max\left\{
\frac{d^p}{n^{p-1}},
\frac{d^q}{n\,v_Y(cd)}
\right\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof
Fix small \(\eta,\varepsilon>0\) satisfying
\[
\pi_0-\eta-(1-\pi_0)\varepsilon>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Under the degree-\(d\) Palm law define
\[
G:=\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_i\ge\pi_0N-\eta d
\right\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
By (3.3), on \(G\),
\[
N_0-\pi_0N\ge(1-\eta)d.
\tag{5.5}
\]
For \(s\ge1\), the exact coefficient in (3.4) satisfies, with \(N=n-1\),
\[
 n\frac{(N_0)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}
=
\prod_{j=0}^{s-2}
\frac{N_0-j}{\pi_0(N-j)}
\tag{5.6}
\]
and each factor equals
\[
1+
\frac{(N_0-\pi_0N)-(1-\pi_0)j}
{\pi_0(N-j)}.
\tag{5.7}
\]
For
\[
(1-\varepsilon)d\le s\le(1+\varepsilon)d,
\]
(5.3) and (5.5) imply
\[
(N_0-\pi_0N)-(1-\pi_0)j\ge c d.
\tag{5.8}
\]
Choose \(c_0\) sufficiently small. Then \(j\le2c_0N\), and every factor in (5.6) is at least \(1+c'd/N\). Since there are \(\asymp d\) factors,
\[
 n\frac{(N_0)_{s-1}}{\pi_0^{s-1}(n)_s}
\ge
\exp\!\left(c''\frac{d^2}{n}\right)
\tag{5.9}
\]
uniformly over the renewal window.

By renewal localization (3.6), for all sufficiently large supported \(d\),
\[
\sum_{|s-d|\le\varepsilon d}\Pr(S_s=d)\ge c>0.
\tag{5.10}
\]
Hence on \(G\),
\[
\boxed{
 nH_{n,d}(N_0)
\ge c\exp\!\left(c\frac{d^2}{n}\right).
}
\tag{5.11}
\]

For the exceptional event, the Palm denominator is
\[
\Pr(S_N=N-d)=a_nR_{n,d}.
\]
Therefore Lemma 4.1 gives
\[
\mathbb P^*_{n,d}(G^c)
\le
\frac{
\exp[-c d^q/(n v_Y(cd))]
}{a_nR_{n,d}}
=
\frac{
\exp[\lambda_n-c d^q/(n v_Y(cd))]
}{R_{n,d}}.
\tag{5.12}
\]
Use (5.11) in the self-normalized inequality (3.5):
\[
R_{n,d}\,c e^{c d^2/n}
\left(1-
\frac{e^{\lambda_n-c d^q/(n v_Y(cd))}}
{R_{n,d}}
\right)
\le C.
\]
Rearranging gives (5.1). The older lower-deviation estimate yields (5.2). \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2: necessary obstruction for a root counterexample
If along supported \((n_j,d_j)\) with
\[
d_j\ge C_*n_j^{1/q},\qquad d_j=o(n_j),
\]
the ratios \(R_{n_j,d_j}\) fail to remain bounded, then necessarily
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{n_j}\,v_Y(c d_j)
\gtrsim
\frac{d_j^q}{n_j}
}
\tag{5.13}
\]
along a further subsequence, modulo fixed constants. In particular, at the natural scale \(d_j\asymp n_j^{1/q}\), failure requires
\[
\lambda_{n_j}v_Y(c d_j)\not\to0.
\tag{5.14}
\]
Thus a counterexample must coordinate an anomalously small central atom with an anomalously slow decay of the truncated-second-moment modulus. Neither ingredient alone is sufficient.

# 6. NEW: explicit central-atom modulus bound

The previous denominator theorem gave the exponent \(\lambda_n=o(n^\beta)\). The same finite-core decomposition yields a more informative non-asymptotic modulus that ties \(\lambda_n\) to the actual truncated second moment and tail mean of the offspring law.

Define for the original offspring law
\[
V_\xi(t):=\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le t],
\qquad
M_\xi(t):=\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>t],
\qquad
P_\xi(t):=\Pr(\xi>t).
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1
There are law-dependent constants \(C,c>0\) such that, for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\), with
\[
t_n:=c n^{1/q},
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\le
C\left[
\log n
+V_\xi(Ct_n)
+nM_\xi(Ct_n)^2
+nP_\xi(Ct_n)
+1
\right].
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The constants in the truncation argument may change the fixed multiple of \(t_n\).

### Proof
Choose once and for all a finite set \(F\subset\operatorname{supp}\pi\) containing 0 and finitely many positive values with gcd \(h\). Let \(\nu=\pi(\cdot\mid F)\). Choose fixed \(0<\varepsilon<\pi(F)\) and write
\[
\pi=\varepsilon\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\rho.
\tag{6.3}
\]
The residual law \(\rho\) is pointwise dominated by a constant multiple of \(\pi\), and the finite-support law \(\nu\) has span \(h\).

Represent the \(n\) offspring variables using iid Bernoulli mixture indicators. Let \(M\) be the number of core variables and choose an integer \(m_n\) nearest \(\varepsilon n\). The binomial local estimate gives
\[
\Pr(M=m_n)\ge c n^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Put \(N_n=n-m_n\) and let \(R_1,\ldots,R_{N_n}\) have law \(\rho\). Let
\[
t=t_n.
\]
For all large \(n\),
\[
N_n\Pr_\rho(R>t)=o(1)
\tag{6.5}
\]
because \(n t^{-q}=O(1)\) and the \(q\)-moment tail tends to zero. Thus the event
\[
E_t:=\{R_i\le t\text{ for all }i\}
\]
has probability at least
\[
\exp[-C nP_\rho(t)].
\tag{6.6}
\]
Conditional on \(E_t\), the residual variables are iid with the truncated law \(\rho(\cdot\mid R\le t)\). Let its mean be \(\mu_t\). Then
\[
|\mu_\rho-\mu_t|
\le C(M_\rho(t)+P_\rho(t)),
\tag{6.7}
\]
and its variance is at most
\[
C V_\rho(t).
\tag{6.8}
\]
By Chebyshev, conditional on \(E_t\), with probability bounded below by a positive constant,
\[
\left|
\sum_{i=1}^{N_n}R_i-N_n\mu_t
\right|
\le C\sqrt{nV_\rho(t)}.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Therefore on an event whose probability is at least a constant times (6.6),
\[
\left|
\sum_{i=1}^{N_n}R_i-N_n\mu_\rho
\right|
\le
D_n,
\tag{6.10}
\]
where
\[
D_n
:=C\left[
\sqrt{nV_\rho(t)}
+nM_\rho(t)
+nP_\rho(t)
+1
\right].
\tag{6.11}
\]
Because
\[
\varepsilon\mathbb E\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb E\rho=1
\]
and \(m_n-\varepsilon n=O(1)\), the bounded core sum needed to make the total exactly \(n-1\) differs from its own mean by at most \(D_n+O(1)\).

The finite-support span-\(h\) law \(\nu\) satisfies a uniform exponential-tilt local lower bound in an \(o(n)\) neighborhood of its mean:
\[
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}Y_i=x
\right)
\ge
c n^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left[-C\frac{(x-m_n\mathbb E\nu)^2}{n}\right]
\tag{6.12}
\]
for admissible lattice points in that neighborhood. Under \(t\asymp n^{1/q}\), finite \(q\)-moment gives \(D_n=o(n^{1/q})=o(n)\), so (6.12) applies.

Multiplying (6.4), the residual event probability, and (6.12) gives
\[
 a_n
\ge
c n^{-1}
\exp\!\left[
-CnP_\rho(t)-C\frac{D_n^2}{n}
\right].
\tag{6.13}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{D_n^2}{n}
\le
C\left[
V_\rho(t)
+nM_\rho(t)^2
+nP_\rho(t)^2
+1
\right],
\]
and \(nP_\rho(t)=o(1)\), the \(P^2\) term is absorbed. Finally \(\rho\le C\pi\) outside the finite core, so its truncated/tail quantities are dominated by fixed multiples of those of \(\xi\), with a harmless fixed rescaling of \(t\). Taking minus logarithms yields (6.2). \(\square\)

### Check against the earlier exponent
At \(t_n\asymp n^{1/q}\), finite \(q\)-moment implies
\[
V_\xi(t_n)=o(t_n^{2-q})=o(n^{2/q-1}),
\]
\[
M_\xi(t_n)=o(t_n^{1-q}),
\qquad
nM_\xi(t_n)^2=o(n^{2/q-1}),
\]
and
\[
nP_\xi(t_n)=o(1).
\]
Thus (6.2) recovers
\[
\lambda_n=o(n^{2/q-1})
\]
but now records the actual modulus responsible for a potentially small central atom. This is useful for stress-testing lacunary laws.

# 7. Conditional root closure criterion

The strengthened Palm theorem almost bridges the root middle window. A concise sufficient condition can be stated in terms of the truncated-second-moment modulus.

Define a monotone envelope
\[
\bar v_Y(x)
:=
\sup_{y\ge x}
 y^{q-2}\mathbb E[Y^2;Y\le y].
\tag{7.1}
\]
Then \(\bar v_Y(x)\downarrow0\).

The root low-window theorem handles any \(B_n\to\infty\) with
\[
B_n^q(\lambda_n+1)/n\to0.
\tag{7.2}
\]
Choose a slowly diverging \(L_n\) and set heuristically
\[
B_n
:=
\left(
\frac{n}{(\lambda_n+1)L_n}
\right)^{1/q}.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Then (7.2) holds. For every \(d\ge B_n\), the second exponent in (5.1) is at least, up to constants,
\[
\frac{B_n^q}{n\bar v_Y(cB_n)}
\asymp
\frac1{(\lambda_n+1)L_n\bar v_Y(cB_n)}.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Thus if one can choose \(L_n\to\infty\) so slowly that
\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_n+1)^2 L_n\,
\bar v_Y(cB_n)\to0,
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
then (5.1) makes \(R_{n,d}\) uniformly bounded throughout the remaining root window up to the already established large-width escape scale, and (2.1) plus \(\sum d^q\pi_d<\infty\) closes root UI.

A cleaner, slightly stronger sufficient condition is
\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_n+1)^2
\bar v_Y\left(
 c\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q}
\right)
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
Under (7.6), \(\{D_n^r\}\) is uniformly integrable.

This is **conditional**, not the full theorem. It is nevertheless broader than stable/regularly-varying assumptions: it asks only for compatibility between the actual central-atom exponent and the actual truncated-second-moment modulus.

## 8. What remains open for the root

The new estimates show that any root counterexample must coordinate three features simultaneously:

1. a central atom \(a_n=e^{-\lambda_n}\) anomalously small enough that \(\lambda_n\) matters;
2. a truncated-second-moment modulus \(v_Y(n^{1/q})\) decaying unusually slowly;
3. a moving shift \(d\) in the narrow region not already covered by the low-window entropy theorem or the strengthened Palm bound.

The F0058 missing-upper-tail-mean mechanism cannot by itself achieve this: finite \(q\)-moment already forces
\[
d^{q-1}\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>d]\to0,
\]
and the canonical \(W_q\) recentering absorbs inaccessible future atoms. A new counterexample would need a genuine central local-probability valley whose scale is synchronized with the slow truncated-variance modulus.

No such fixed-law construction was found in this run.

## 9. Every-fixed-level status

The new Palm theorem is root-specific. It must **not** be counted as closure of the requested every-level problem.

For fixed \(k\), the exact prefix formula remains (2.3). The corrected stopping-prefix reduction from blob `c960e8736dbfc810ed4b077be97edb1bc9394914` states that outside a contribution negligible for the target fractional moment,
\[
N\le C_k(1+L)+A_k(\lambda_n+\log n+1).
\tag{9.1}
\]
Together with block relative entropy, this yields the sufficient condition (2.9), but it does not bridge the full middle window for arbitrary \(r\).

Two currently viable full-scope routes remain:

- prove a prefix/Palm analogue of the weighted root argument, conditioning on the exposed first \(k\) levels and using the exact residual constraint \(S_m=m-L\);
- prove the branching-specific shifted linear total-progeny bound
  \[
  \boxed{
  u^{*L}_{m-O_k(L)}\lesssim L u_m
  }
  \tag{9.2}
  \]
  on the non-negligible prefix region. This would consume exactly the available \(\mathbb EZ_k^{1+r}<\infty\) and settle every fixed \(k\).

Neither is proved here.

## 10. Failed routes / adversarial checks this run

1. **Pointwise ratio alone remains too strong a target.** Even (5.1) does not universally prove bounded \(R_{n,d}\) at every natural-scale \(d\); the unresolved obstruction is precisely \(\lambda_n v_Y(cd)\).
2. **Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds remain too expensive.** Exponential-in-count bounds cannot be integrated against the count law available only with one finite moment.
3. **A direct F0058 transplant still fails the moment budget.** Missing-tail-mean compensators contribute vanishing normalized \(q\)-moment at the finite-\(q\) threshold.
4. **Total variation / relative entropy by themselves do not give endpoint \(q\)-UI.** Sparse mass can be invisible in TV while retaining order-one \(q\)-moment.
5. **The all-level stopping-prefix shortcut remains corrected, not restored.** The valid length bound includes the \(O(\lambda_n+\log n)\) term in (9.1).
6. **No unverified infinite-variance local limit theorem is used.** Lemma 4.1 is a direct one-sided truncation/Chernoff estimate; Theorem 5.1 uses exact switching and the ordinary renewal theorem only.

## 13. Next attack

The highest-value next step is no longer another pointwise local-ratio estimate. It is a **weighted Palm middle-window theorem**.

Use the exact identity
\[
N_0=d+\sum Y_i
\]
and split, for each \(d\), according to whether \(\sum Y_i\) lies substantially below its mean. On the bad part, Lemma 4.1 supplies the factor
\[
\exp[-c d^q/(n v_Y(cd))].
\]
On the good part, renewal localization and the falling-factorial boost make \(nH_{n,d}(N_0)\) large. Instead of demanding a uniform bound for every individual \(R_{n,d}\), sum the self-normalized inequality directly with weights \(d^q\pi_d\). The finite \(q\)-moment may then absorb isolated degrees where \(\lambda_n v_Y(cd)\) is large.

In parallel, try to transplant this weighted argument behind a fixed exposed prefix, where the residual sum constraint is \(S_m=m-L\). This is the most direct path from the new root mathematics to the required every-fixed-level theorem.
