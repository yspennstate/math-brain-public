## 1. Exact target, scope, and recovery

Let \(\xi\) be a nondegenerate nonnegative-integer offspring variable with
\[
\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,
\qquad 0<r<1.
\]
Let \(T_n\) be the Galton–Watson tree conditioned on total size \(n\), along every admissible \(n\). For every fixed level \(k\ge1\), determine whether
\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}
\]
is uniformly integrable.

Write
\[
q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad p:=\frac q{q-1},\qquad
\pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d),\qquad
h:=\gcd\{d\ge1:\pi_d>0\}.
\]
Critical nondegeneracy gives \(0<\pi_0<1\) and positive mass above 1. Every finite tree size satisfies \(n\equiv1\pmod h\); because a finite subset of the positive support already has gcd \(h\), all sufficiently large integers in that residue class are admissible. “Admissible \(n\)” means \(\Pr(|T|=n)>0\).

It identifies \(\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty\) as the finite-limit regime of interest.

D_n:=\ell_1(T_n),\qquad S_m:=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_m,
\qquad a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad \lambda_n:=-\log a_n.

The exact root identity is
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D_n=d)
=\frac n{n-1}d\pi_dR_{n,d},
\qquad
R_{n,d}:=\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]

}
\tag{2.2}
\]

\[
N:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L:=Z_k,\qquad m:=n-N.

\[
\boxed{
W_n=\frac{nL}{m}

\tag{2.3}

# 3. NEW: adaptive tail-mean truncation for Palm lower deviations

The previous Palm theorem truncated \(Y\) at a fixed multiple of \(d\) and required a condition involving the \(q\)-moment tail \(\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>cd]\). That is unnecessarily rigid for a lacunary law: a far-future atom may contribute to the \(q\)-tail while contributing very little to the **mean**, which is the actual quantity relevant when truncation shifts the center.

The correct truncation criterion is expressed directly through the tail mean.

Define
\[
M_Y(T):=\mathbb E[Y;Y>T],
\qquad
V_Y(T):=\mathbb E[Y^2;Y\le T].
\tag{3.1}
\]
Both are finite for finite \(T\); \(M_Y(T)\downarrow0\) as \(T\to\infty\).

### Lemma 3.1 — adaptive lower deviation

Fix \(0<\eta<1\). Let \(Y_1,\dots,Y_N\) be iid copies of \(Y\). If \(d>0\) and a threshold \(T\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
N M_Y(T)\le \frac{\eta d}{2},
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(
\sum_{i=1}^NY_i-N\mu_Y\le-\eta d
\right)
\le
\exp\left[-c_\eta\frac{d^2}{N V_Y(T)}\right],
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
with the natural convention that the right side is 0 when the truncated variance term vanishes and the event is impossible.

### Proof
Let
\[
Z_i:=Y_i\mathbf1_{\{Y_i\le T\}},
\qquad \mu_T:=\mathbb EZ_i=\mu_Y-M_Y(T).
\]
If
\[
\sum_iY_i-N\mu_Y\le-\eta d,
\]
then, since \(Z_i\le Y_i\),
\[
\sum_iZ_i-N\mu_T
\le
-\eta d+N M_Y(T)
\le-\eta d/2.
\tag{3.4}
\]
For every \(t>0\) and every nonnegative \(Z\),
\[
e^{-tZ}\le1-tZ+\frac{t^2Z^2}{2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\log\mathbb E e^{-t(Z-\mathbb EZ)}
\le \frac{t^2}{2}\mathbb EZ^2.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Chernoff therefore gives
\[
\Pr\left(\sum_i(Z_i-\mu_T)\le-\eta d/2\right)
\le
\exp\left[
-t\eta d/2+rac{Nt^2V_Y(T)}2
\right].
\]
Optimizing at \(t=\eta d/(2NV_Y(T))\) yields (3.3), with for example \(c_\eta=\eta^2/8\). \(\square\)

No local limit theorem, finite variance, stable domain assumption, or regular variation is used.

# 4. NEW: adaptive Palm local-ratio theorem

Use the fixed \(\eta\) from the archived good-event boost (2.10). Define the **adaptive truncated-variance functional**
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_n(d)
:=
\inf\left\{
V_Y(T):T>0,\ (n-1)M_Y(T)\le\frac{\eta d}{2}
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
The set is nonempty because \(M_Y(T)\to0\). Since the admissibility constraint weakens as \(d\) increases,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_n(d)\text{ is nonincreasing in }d.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — adaptive Palm bound

There are fixed \(c,C,c_0>0\), depending only on \(\pi\), such that for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\) and all supported \(d\to\infty\) with \(d\le c_0n\),
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,d}
\le
C e^{-c d^2/n}
+
\exp\left\{
\lambda_n
-c\frac{d^2}{n\mathcal V_n(d)}
\right\}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
If the infimum in (4.1) is not attained, interpret (4.3) through an arbitrary \(\varepsilon\)-minimizing threshold and then let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

### Proof
Split the Palm expectation in (2.8) into the good event \(G_{n,d}\) from (2.9) and its complement. On \(G_{n,d}\), (2.10) gives
\[
nH_{n,d}(N_0)\ge c e^{c d^2/n}.
\]
Thus (2.8) implies a contribution bounded by \(Ce^{-cd^2/n}\).

For the Palm-bad event, remove the conditioning denominator. Precisely,
\[
R_{n,d}\Pr_{n,d}^*(G_{n,d}^c)
=
\frac{
\Pr\left(
S_{n-1}=n-1-d,\ G_{n,d}^c
\right)
}{a_n}
\le e^{\lambda_n}\Pr(G_{n,d}^c),
\tag{4.4}
\]
where the probability on the far right is under \(n-1\) ordinary iid offspring variables. For any threshold admissible in (4.1), Lemma 3.1 gives
\[
\Pr(G_{n,d}^c)
\le
\exp\left[-c\frac{d^2}{nV_Y(T)}\right].
\]
Optimize over such \(T\), combine with the good contribution, and obtain (4.3). \(\square\)

### Why this is stronger than the previous Palm criterion

The earlier theorem used \(T\asymp d\) and checked the sufficient condition
\[
n\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>cd]/d^q\ll1.
\]
Theorem 4.1 instead allows **any threshold \(T\)** for which the omitted tail contributes only \(O(d/n)\) to the mean. This is strictly better suited to lacunary support: if a huge future atom has substantial \(q\)-moment but negligible mean mass, one may truncate before it without paying for its \(q\)-moment.

# 5. NEW: a single adaptive condition closing the root problem

Take the entropy cutoff (2.11):
\[
B_n=\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q}.
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_n
:=(\lambda_n+1)
\frac{n\mathcal V_n(B_n)}{B_n^2}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Corollary 5.1 — adaptive root closure criterion

If
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_n\to0,}
\tag{5.2}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\{D_n^r\}_n\text{ is uniformly integrable.}
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof
For \(d\ge B_n\), monotonicity (4.2) gives
\[
\mathcal V_n(d)\le\mathcal V_n(B_n),
\]
so
\[
\frac{d^2}{n\mathcal V_n(d)}
\ge
\frac{B_n^2}{n\mathcal V_n(B_n)}
=
\frac{\lambda_n+1}{\Gamma_n}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
If \(\Gamma_n\to0\), the right side dominates \(\lambda_n\). Theorem 4.1 therefore yields
\[
\sup_{B_n\le d\le c_n}R_{n,d}=O(1),
\tag{5.5}
\]
where \(c_n=o(n)\) is the previously proved escape cutoff; for large \(n\), \(c_n<c_0n\).

On the intermediate range, (2.1) gives
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;B_n<D_n<c_n]
\le C\sum_{d>B_n}d^q\pi_d\to0.
\tag{5.6}
\]
The entropy theorem (2.12) handles \(D_n\le B_n\), and the escape theorem (2.5), specialized to \(k=1\), handles \(D_n\ge c_n\). This proves UI. \(\square\)

The criterion has a particularly useful threshold form.

### Corollary 5.2 — movable threshold criterion

If there is any deterministic sequence \(T_n\to\infty\) such that
\[
\boxed{
(n-1)M_Y(T_n)\le \frac{\eta B_n}{2}
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
(\lambda_n+1)\frac{nV_Y(T_n)}{B_n^2}\to0,
}
\tag{5.8}
\]
then root UI holds.

This follows directly from \(\mathcal V_n(B_n)\le V_Y(T_n)\).

### Comparison with the previous two-condition criterion

The preceding report fixed \(T\asymp B_n\) and required separate \(q\)-tail and truncated-variance products to vanish. Corollary 5.2 allows \(T_n\) to be much larger or smaller than \(B_n\), provided the tail **mean** beyond \(T_n\) is already small enough. It packages the obstruction into one optimization problem.

Consequently, if root UI fails, then by contrapositive there is a subsequence on which
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_n\not\to0.
}
\tag{5.9}
\]
Equivalently, at the point where the omitted tail mean has finally fallen to order \(B_n/n\), the accumulated truncated second moment cannot be \(o(B_n^2/((\lambda_n+1)n))\). A counterexample must synchronize **two different tail scales**: tail-mean depletion and accumulated variance.

This is a sharper obstruction than the previous fixed-multiple-of-\(B_n\) conditions.

# 6. NEW supporting result: an adaptive ordinary lower-deviation split

The same identity behind the Palm argument gives a useful nonlocal estimate for an ordinary lower deviation.

For \(m\) iid offspring variables and \(L>0\), on
\[
S_m=m-L
\]
one has exactly
\[
\boxed{
N_0=L+\sum_{i=1}^mY_i.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
Fix the same \(0<\eta<1\). If a threshold \(T\) satisfies
\[
mM_Y(T)\le\eta L/2,
\tag{6.2}
\]
then split according to
\[
\sum_iY_i<m\mu_Y-\eta L
\]
or its complement. On the first event, Lemma 3.1 gives
\[
\Pr(\text{first event})
\le
\exp\left[-c\frac{L^2}{mV_Y(T)}\right].
\tag{6.3}
\]
On the complement, (6.1) forces
\[
N_0\ge m\pi_0+(1-\eta)L.
\]
Unconditionally \(N_0\sim\mathrm{Bin}(m,\pi_0)\), hence Hoeffding gives
\[
\Pr(\text{second event and }S_m=m-L)
\le
\exp(-cL^2/m)
\tag{6.4}
\]
through the sublinear range, with a fixed exponential estimate for linear deviations. Thus
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(S_m=m-L)
\le
\exp(-cL^2/m)
+
\exp\left[-c\frac{L^2}{mV_Y(T)}\right].
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

This is a genuine every-prefix-compatible probability estimate, but it does **not** close the fixed-level problem: substituting (6.5) into (2.3) still divides by \(a_n=e^{-\lambda_n}\). At the root, renewal/Palm self-normalization cancels this denominator on the good event; a corresponding prefix self-normalization has not yet been proved.

## 7. What this says about lacunary counterexamples

The prior fixed \(q\)-tail condition was potentially too pessimistic for lacunary laws. Suppose, for example, there is a large gap in the offspring support. A future atom beyond the gap may contribute noticeably to
\[
\mathbb E[Y^q;Y>x]
\]
while contributing very little to
\[
M_Y(x)=\mathbb E[Y;Y>x].
\]
The adaptive theorem deliberately uses the latter to decide where truncation can occur.

Therefore a simple “future rare atom creates a large \(q\)-tail” construction is not enough. A genuine counterexample must arrange that **for every threshold** at which the future tail mean is small enough to permit recentering, the variance already accumulated below that threshold is still large enough to make \(\Gamma_n\) non-negligible. This is a substantially tighter lacunary design constraint.

This does **not** rule out all lacunary counterexamples. It identifies the precise two-scale synchronization they must achieve.

## 8. Relation to the every-fixed-level target

No root theorem is promoted to an all-level result without a proof.

For fixed \(k\), the exact prefix density is (2.3). The corrected stopping-prefix estimate from the earlier report remains
\[
N\le C_k(1+L)+A_k(\lambda_n+\log n+1)
\tag{8.1}
\]
outside a contribution negligible for the target fractional moment.

The new ordinary lower-deviation estimate (6.5) is compatible with the random prefix because it applies to arbitrary \((m,L)\). But after division by \(a_n\), it still pays \(e^{\lambda_n}\). The missing structural lemma is therefore now very explicit:

> Construct a prefix analogue of the root renewal/Palm switching identity that self-normalizes the denominator while preserving the target \(L^q\) weight.

An alternate sufficient route remains the branching-specific shifted linear convolution estimate for the excess-total-progeny law \(u\):
\[
\boxed{
u^{*L}_{m-O_k(L)}\lesssim L\,u_m
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
on the non-negligible prefix region. Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds remain exponentially too expensive in \(L\).

Thus the new results are a genuine root advance and a new all-level-compatible deviation lemma, but the universal root and universal every-fixed-level statements remain open.

## 9. Failed / insufficient routes retained explicitly

1. Bounded expectations do not imply UI.
2. Fixed-shift local ratios do not control the growing mesoscopic window.
3. Generic local-subexponential Kesten bounds cost an exponential factor in the convolution count.
4. Total variation or relative entropy alone cannot control the endpoint \(q\)-moment without exploiting exact mean information.

8. No certified counterexample has been constructed. The known F0058 missing-upper-tail-mean mechanism remains insufficient at this endpoint because of the finite-\(q\) moment budget and canonical recentering.

## 12. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to decide whether the adaptive obstruction \(\Gamma_n\) can actually stay large under a finite \(q\)-moment law.

Two complementary attacks are now sharply formulated:

1. **Automatic-overlap proof.** Use the explicit central-atom modulus from the preceding report together with the optimization defining \(\mathcal V_n(B_n)\). The hope is to show that if the tail mean remains large until a threshold with large truncated variance, then the same tail mass necessarily improves the central denominator enough to reduce \(\lambda_n\), forcing \(\Gamma_n\to0\). This would close the universal root case.

2. **Lacunary adversary.** Choose atoms \(x_j\) and probabilities \(p_j\) with \(\sum p_jx_j^q<\infty\), and try to make, near a subsequence \(n_j\), every threshold satisfying \(n_jM_Y(T)\lesssim B_{n_j}\) already include enough past mass that \(V_Y(T)\gtrsim B_{n_j}^2/((\lambda_{n_j}+1)n_j)\). The construction must simultaneously make the exact central atom small enough and survive the canonical recentering / finite-core smoothing constraints. Failure of this design would itself suggest the automatic-overlap theorem.

Only after the root obstruction is removed should the same optimized truncation be transplanted behind a fixed exposed prefix and combined with a new prefix switching identity.
