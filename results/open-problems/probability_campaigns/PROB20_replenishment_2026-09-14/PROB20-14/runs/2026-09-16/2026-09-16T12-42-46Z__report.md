## 2. Canonical mean-matched reference

For \(\theta\ge0\), write
\[
Z(\theta):=\mathbb Ee^{-\theta\xi},\qquad P_\theta(d):=\frac{\pi_de^{-\theta d}}{Z(\theta)},\qquad \mu(\theta):=\mathbb E_\theta\xi.
\tag{2.1}
\]
For every admissible \(m>b\ge1\), let \(\theta_{m,b}\) satisfy
\[
\mu(\theta_{m,b})=1-\frac bm,
\]
and set \(P_{m,b}:=P_{\theta_{m,b}}\). Exact conditioning is tilt-invariant:
\[
\mathcal L_\pi((\xi_i)_{i\le m}\mid S_m=m-b)=\mathcal L_{P_{m,b}}((\xi_i)_{i\le m}\mid S_m=m-b).
\tag{2.2}
\]
Let \(Q_{m,b}\) denote this conditioned law and define
\[
c_{m,b}:=-\log P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b).
\tag{2.3}
\]
Write \(P_0:=P_{m,b}\) and \(\mu_0:=1-b/m\).

## 3. NEW exact multi-coordinate canonical deletion identity

Fix \((m,b)\) and expose an ordered vector \(\mathbf d=(d_1,\ldots,d_\ell)\), \(1\le\ell<m\), with positive conditioned probability. Put
\[
D_\ell:=\sum_{i=1}^\ell d_i,\qquad N:=m-\ell,
\]
\[
b_\ell:=b+\sum_{i=1}^\ell(d_i-1).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Then the residual exact sum is
\[
S_N=m-b-D_\ell=N-b_\ell.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Let \(P_\ell:=P_{N,b_\ell}\) and \(c_\ell:=c_{N,b_\ell}\), using the already verified natural endpoint conventions when the residual mean is zero or one. Define
\[
J^{(\ell)}_{m,b}(\mathbf d):=N D(P_\ell\|P_0).
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — direct multi-deletion formula

\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q_{m,b}(\xi_1=d_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell=d_\ell)}{\prod_{i=1}^\ell P_0(d_i)}
=
\exp\{c_{m,b}-c_{N,b_\ell}-J^{(\ell)}_{m,b}(\mathbf d)\}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

**Proof.** Under \(P_0^{\otimes m}\),
\[
Q_{m,b}(\mathbf d)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^\ell P_0(d_i)\right)
\frac{P_0^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b_\ell)}{P_0^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b)}.
\]
The denominator equals \(e^{-c_{m,b}}\). On the residual exact-sum slice, the product likelihood ratio between \(P_0^{\otimes N}\) and the mean-matched law \(P_\ell^{\otimes N}\) is constant, hence
\[
P_0^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b_\ell)=e^{-ND(P_\ell\|P_0)}P_\ell^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b_\ell)=e^{-J^{(\ell)}-c_{N,b_\ell}}.
\]
Substitution proves (3.4). \(\square\)

Define the centered downward log-mgf under \(P_0\)
\[
\Lambda_{P_0}(u):=\log\mathbb E_{P_0}e^{-u(\xi-\mu_0)},\qquad
H_{P_0}(y):=\sup_{u\ge0}\{uy-\Lambda_{P_0}(u)\}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Since the residual mean is \((m\mu_0-D_\ell)/N\),
\[
N(\mu_0-\mu_\ell)=D_\ell-\ell\mu_0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
J^{(\ell)}_{m,b}(\mathbf d)=N H_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{D_\ell-\ell\mu_0}{N}\right).
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
If every \(d_i\ge d\ge1\), then
\[
\boxed{
J^{(\ell)}_{m,b}(\mathbf d)\ge N H_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{\ell(d-\mu_0)}N\right).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
No LLT is used; this is the exponential-family variational identity.

## 4. NEW mean-matched factorial replication theorem retaining residual central cost

Let \(A\subseteq\{d,d+1,\ldots\}\), \(d\ge1\), and write
\[
\alpha:=Q_{m,b}(\xi_1\in A),\qquad p:=P_{m,b}(A),\qquad N_A:=\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i\in A\}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
For an integer-valued nonnegative \(N\) with mean \(\lambda\), discrete convexity of falling factorials gives, whenever \(\lambda\ge2\ell\),
\[
\mathbb E(N)_\ell\ge(\lambda/2)^\ell.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Indeed the second finite difference of \((x)_\ell\) is \(\ell(\ell-1)(x)_{\ell-2}\ge0\); the convex interpolation is minimized at fixed mean on adjacent integers, and \((\lfloor\lambda\rfloor)_\ell\ge(\lambda/2)^\ell\) if \(\lambda\ge2\ell\).

Exchangeability yields
\[
Q_{m,b}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell\in A)=\frac{\mathbb E(N_A)_\ell}{(m)_\ell}\ge(\alpha/2)^\ell
\tag{4.3}
\]
when \(m\alpha\ge2\ell\).

For vectors in \(A^\ell\), define
\[
\underline c_N(A,\ell):=\inf_{\mathbf d\in A^\ell:\,Q(\mathbf d)>0}c_{N,b_\ell}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Theorem 3.1 and (3.7), summed over \(A^\ell\), give
\[
Q_{m,b}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell\in A)
\le p^\ell\exp\left\{c_{m,b}-\underline c_N(A,\ell)-NH_{P_0}\!\left(\frac{\ell(d-\mu_0)}N\right)\right\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Combining (4.3) and (4.5):

### Theorem 4.1 — reduced-budget factorial inequality

If \(m\alpha\ge2\ell\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
c_{m,b}-\underline c_{m-\ell}(A,\ell)
\ge{}&\ell\log\frac{\alpha}{2p}\\
&+(m-\ell)H_{P_{m,b}}\!\left(\frac{\ell(d-\mu_0)}{m-\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.6}
\]
The budget is the **central-exponent drop after deleting the marked coordinates**, not the full central exponent.

## 5. NEW universal subtraction of the residual square-root anti-concentration tax

After lattice reduction, choose a fixed finite subset of the offspring support generating span one. For every \(\delta<1\), the canonical tilt parameters corresponding to deficits \(0\le b'/N\le\delta\) stay in a compact interval, so the probabilities of this finite core are uniformly bounded below. Bernoulli/core extraction gives a uniform concentration bound
\[
\sup_x P_{N,b'}^{\otimes N}(S_N=x)\le\frac{C_\delta}{\sqrt N},
\tag{5.1}
\]
hence in particular
\[
\boxed{c_{N,b'}\ge\frac12\log N-C_\delta.}
\tag{5.2}
\]
This uses only the fixed finite core; no full-variance or infinite-variance LLT is used.

Therefore, whenever every marked vector retained in (4.4) has \(b_\ell/(m-\ell)\le\delta\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
c_{m,b}-\frac12\log(m-\ell)+C_\delta
\ge{}&\ell\log\frac{\alpha}{2p}\\
&+(m-\ell)H_{P_{m,b}}\!\left(\frac{\ell(d-\mu_0)}{m-\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Define the reduced free energy
\[
e_{m,b}:=c_{m,b}-\frac12\log m.
\tag{5.4}
\]
For \(\ell=o(m)\), (5.3) says, up to \(O(1)+o(1)\),
\[
\boxed{e_{m,b}\gtrsim \ell\log\frac{\alpha}{2p}+mH_{P_{m,b}}\!\left(\frac{\ell d}{m}\right).}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Thus the unavoidable \(\frac12\log m\) exact-sum cost is inherited by the residual system and cannot be spent on amplifying repeated tail marks.

## 6. Consequence for an amplified dyadic shell

Let \(A=[d,2d)\cap\mathbb Z\) and define
\[
t:=\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1\in A],\qquad w:=\mathbb E_{P_{m,b}}[\xi^q;\xi\in A].
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then
\[
\alpha\ge\frac{t}{(2d)^q},\qquad p\le\frac{w}{d^q},\qquad \frac{\alpha}{2p}\ge\frac{t}{2^{q+1}w}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
If \(m\alpha\ge8\), choose \(\ell=\lfloor m\alpha/4\rfloor\). Then
\[
\ell\ge c_q\frac{mt}{d^q}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Because selected values are \(<2d\),
\[
\frac{b_\ell}{m-\ell}\le\frac{b+2d\ell}{m-\ell}.
\]
In the unresolved regime \(b=o(m)\), if \(d\to\infty\) and \(t=O(1)\), then \(d\ell/m=O(t/d^{q-1})=o(1)\), so the bounded-deficit condition of Section 5 holds eventually. Hence any shell with \(t\) bounded below by a positive constant and many conditioned copies must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
e_{m,b}+O(1)
\ge{}&c_q\frac{mt}{d^q}\log\frac{t}{2^{q+1}w}\\
&+mH_{P_{m,b}}\!\left(c_q\frac{t}{d^{q-1}}\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Uniformly over downward tilts,
\[
w\le\pi_0^{-1}\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi\ge d]\to0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus a many-copy counterexample shell must consume an unbounded amount of **reduced**, not total, central free energy.

## 7. NEW adaptive path identity

For successive exposed values \(d_1,\ldots,d_\ell\), define
\[
(m_0,b_0)=(m,b),\qquad m_j=m-j,\qquad b_j=b+\sum_{i=1}^j(d_i-1),
\]
and \(P_j=P_{m_j,b_j}\). Iterating the verified one-step identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{Q(d_1,\ldots,d_\ell)}{\prod_{j=1}^\ell P_{j-1}(d_j)}
=
\exp\left\{c_{m,b}-c_{m_\ell,b_\ell}-\sum_{j=1}^\ell m_jD(P_j\|P_{j-1})\right\}.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_\ell\) be the adaptive Markov reference whose step-\(j\) transition is \(P_{j-1}\). Taking the conditioned expectation of the logarithm yields the exact path free-energy decomposition
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
c_{m,b}={}&D(\mathcal L_Q(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_\ell)\|\mathcal R_\ell)\\
&+\mathbb E_Q c_{m_\ell,b_\ell}+\mathbb E_Q\sum_{j=1}^\ell m_jD(P_j\|P_{j-1}).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Every term on the right is nonnegative. This formally explains why the large sparse central exponents constructed in the preceding run do not automatically create a UI counterexample: a valley can be almost completely inherited by the residual exact-conditioning problem and cancel from the actual likelihood ratio.

## 8. Refined counterexample certificate

Suppose a failure produces \(m_j\to\infty\), \(b_j=o(m_j)\), \(K_j\to\infty\) and
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{m_j,b_j}}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K_j]\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The preceding run's one-step cliff theorem already forces some supported \(d_j>K_j\) with
\[
c_{m_j,b_j}-c_{m_j-1,b_j+d_j-1}-J_{m_j,b_j}(d_j)\ge\log\frac{\varepsilon\pi_0}{R_q(K_j)},
\tag{8.2}
\]
where \(R_q(K)=\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]\downarrow0\).

The new many-coordinate theorem adds: if a positive fraction of the anomalous \(q\)-mass lies in a shell whose conditioned expected multiplicity tends to infinity, then (6.4) applies and the anomaly must consume an unbounded amount of the reduced free energy \(e_{m,b}\), while also paying the explicit retuning Cramér cost. Hence a surviving counterexample has only two mechanisms:

1. **Few-copy mechanism:** dangerous mass is carried on degrees with conditioned expected multiplicity \(O(1)\); then the divergent one-step deletion cliff (8.2) must occur.
2. **Many-copy mechanism:** an amplified shell has multiplicity \(\to\infty\); then its amplification is paid from the inherited-free-energy drop in (4.6), not from the full denominator, and at minimum the residual \(\frac12\log m\) tax cancels.

This is stronger than the previous global envelope criterion because it localizes exactly what a counterexample has to defeat.

## 9. What remains open and next attack

The universal theorem is still open. The unresolved analytic object is the local oscillation
\[
c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1}
\]
(or the multi-deletion analogue), after subtracting the positive retuning cost. Arbitrary finite-\(q\) laws can have irregular local probabilities, so no unproved local-limit ratio is being inserted.

The highest-value next lemma is a **shared finite-core deletion comparison**. Construct the Bernoulli/core representation of the initial mean-matched central atom and the retuned residual central atom on the same non-core realization. A valley generated by sparse large atoms should be inherited by the residual system unless those atoms themselves spend a quantitatively nonnegligible amount of the \(q\)-moment budget. Such a result would quantify the cancellation already exact in (3.4) and (7.1).

A parallel route remains the moving-tilt Palm/zero-surplus identity from earlier checkpoints, where the macroscopic deficit \(b\) cancels after selecting a large degree. A renewal-switching lower bound uniform as \(\theta_{m,b}\downarrow0\) should control the same deletion defect.

Fresh literature checking did not reveal a black-box resolution. Janson still states the noninteger extension as a conjecture. Effective Bernoulli-part local limit work such as Giuliano–Weber, *Bernoulli* 23 (2017), arXiv:1412.3980, is relevant to the shared-core route, but is not being treated as an arbitrary infinite-variance moving-window ratio theorem.
