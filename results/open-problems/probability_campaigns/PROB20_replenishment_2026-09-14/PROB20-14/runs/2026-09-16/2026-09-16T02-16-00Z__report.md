\mathbb E\xi=1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty,\qquad 0<r<1,

q:=1+r\in(1,2),\qquad \pi_d:=\Pr(\xi=d).

\[
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r:n\text{ admissible}\}

## 2. Retained all-level endpoint reduction

The preceding checkpoint gives, for each fixed depth \(k\), the ordinary prefix law \(\mathsf P_k\) of
\[
(A,B)=\left(\sum_{j<k}Z_j,Z_k\right)
\]
and the corresponding conditioned-prefix law \(\mathsf Q_{M,k}\), after removal of the tree-size lattice span. If
\[
U=(|T|-1)/h,\qquad u_M=\Pr(U=M),\qquad
C=(A+B-1)/h,
\]
then the exact likelihood ratio is
\[
R_M(A,B)=\frac{u^{*B}_{M-C}}{u_M},
\qquad
\frac{d\mathsf Q_{M,k}}{d\mathsf P_k}=R_M.
\]
For every fixed feasible prefix, \(R_M(A,B)\to B\). Since both \(R_M\) and \(B\) are probability densities with respect to \(\mathsf P_k\), Scheffé gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}|R_M-B|\to0,
\qquad
\|\mathsf Q_{M,k}-\widehat{\mathsf P}_k\|_{\rm TV}\to0.
\]
The remaining endpoint obstruction is the weighted upgrade
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}[B^r(R_M/B-1)]\to0,
\]
where \(\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}B^r=\mathbb EZ_k^q<\infty\).

The same checkpoint also proves the all-level weighted Cramér estimate, with
\[
\tau_k(j)=\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k\ge j],
\]
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
\le \frac{\tau_k(j)}{j}\exp\{L_M-I_M(j)\},
\]
and a moving safe cutoff \(\gamma_{M,k}\) above which the conditioned fractional moment is negligible. Its addendum gives the three-term error estimate
\[
\left|\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}B^r-
\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}B^r\right|
\le 2j^r\delta_{M,k}+\tau_k(j)
+\frac{\tau_k(j)}j e^{L_M-I_M(j)},
\]
where \(\delta_{M,k}\) is the prefix total-variation error.

The present run attacks a different part of the obstruction: can a rare root-degree or degree-shell likelihood spike be amplified without forcing an even more expensive repeated lower-deviation event?

# 3. NEW: a factorial-replication inequality under exact central conditioning

Let
\[
S_n=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_n,
\qquad
a_n=\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\qquad
\lambda_n=-\log a_n,
\]
and let \(Q_n\) denote the law of \((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\) conditioned on \(S_n=n-1\).

For a degree set
\[
A\subseteq\{d,d+1,d+2,\ldots\},\qquad d\ge2,
\]
write
\[
p_A=\Pr(\xi\in A),\qquad
N_A=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i\in A\}},
\qquad
\mu_A=\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_A.
\]
Define the negative-mgf log transform
\[
\Lambda(t)=\log\mathbb Ee^{-t(\xi-1)},\qquad t\ge0,
\]
and its one-sided Legendre transform
\[
\boxed{
H(y)=\sup_{t\ge0}\{ty-\Lambda(t)\},\qquad y\ge0.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — factorial replication / lower-deviation inequality

Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer such that \(\mu_A\ge2m\). Put
\[
N=n-m,
\qquad
x=1+m(d-1).
\]
If \(x\le N\), then
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{\mu_A}{2np_A}
\le
\frac{\lambda_n}{m}
-
\frac{n-m}{m}
H\!\left(\frac{1+m(d-1)}{n-m}\right).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
If \(x>N\), the repeated configuration is impossible and no such \(m\) can coexist with the positive lower factorial bound below.

### Proof

For an integer \(z\ge0\),
\[
(z)_m=z(z-1)\cdots(z-m+1)
\ge (z-m+1)_+^m.
\]
The function \(z\mapsto(z-m+1)_+^m\) is convex, so Jensen yields
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}(N_A)_m
\ge(\mu_A-m+1)_+^m
\ge(\mu_A/2)^m.
\tag{3.3}
\]

By exchangeability,
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}(N_A)_m
=
\frac{(n)_m}{a_n}
\Pr(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m\in A,\ S_n=n-1).
\tag{3.4}
\]
Whenever the first \(m\) variables belong to \(A\), their sum is at least \(md\). Therefore the remaining \(N=n-m\) variables must satisfy
\[
S_N\le n-1-md
=N-x.
\]
Independence before conditioning gives
\[
\Pr(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m\in A,S_n=n-1)
\le p_A^m\Pr(S_N-N\le-x).
\tag{3.5}
\]
Chernoff's bound with (3.1) gives
\[
\Pr(S_N-N\le-x)
\le\exp\{-NH(x/N)\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Combining (3.3)--(3.6), using \((n)_m\le n^m\) and \(a_n=e^{-\lambda_n}\), gives
\[
(\mu_A/2)^m
\le (np_A)^m
\exp\{\lambda_n-NH(x/N)\},
\]
which is exactly (3.2). \(\square\)

### Singleton form

For \(A=\{d\}\), let
\[
R_{n,d}=
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
\]
Then \(\mu_A=n\pi_dR_{n,d}\), and whenever \(m\le\mu_A/2\),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{R_{n,d}}2
\le
\frac{\lambda_n}{m}
-
\frac{n-m}{m}
H\!\left(\frac{1+m(d-1)}{n-m}\right).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
This is a self-amplification obstruction: a large one-coordinate likelihood ratio forces an \(m\)-fold occurrence, but the latter has to pay a genuine lower-deviation rate.

# 4. NEW: the lower-deviation rate is super-\(q\) at the origin

The finite \(q\)-moment assumption gives more than an ordinary power Chernoff bound.

### Lemma 4.1

As \(t\downarrow0\),
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(t)=o(t^q).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
p=\frac q{q-1}=\frac q r,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{H(y)}{y^p}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad(y\downarrow0).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

For \(u\ge0\) and \(1<q<2\),
\[
0\le e^{-u}-1+u\le C_q u^q.
\]
For every fixed \(u\), the left side is \(O(u^2)\), so after setting \(u=t\xi\), dividing by \(t^q\), and using dominated convergence against \(\xi^q\),
\[
\mathbb Ee^{-t\xi}=1-t+o(t^q),
\]
because \(\mathbb E\xi=1\). Hence
\[
\mathbb Ee^{-t(\xi-1)}
=e^t\mathbb Ee^{-t\xi}
=1+o(t^q),
\]
since \(t^2=o(t^q)\). Taking logarithms proves (4.1).

For (4.2), fix any \(c>0\) and set \(t=c y^{1/(q-1)}\). Then
\[
ty=c y^p,
\qquad
\Lambda(t)=o(t^q)=o(c^q y^p),
\]
so
\[
\liminf_{y\downarrow0}\frac{H(y)}{y^p}\ge c.
\]
Since \(c\) is arbitrary, the quotient tends to infinity. \(\square\)

This gain is distribution-dependent and can be arbitrarily slow, but it is strict under only \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\). No finite variance, regular variation, or local limit theorem is used.

# 5. NEW: dyadic-shell amplification theorem

The singleton bound is useful, but a putative non-UI tail may spread mass over neighboring degrees. The factorial argument works for an entire multiplicative shell.

Fix \(L>1\), and for an integer \(d\ge2\) define
\[
A_d^{(L)}=\{j\in\mathbb Z:d\le j<Ld\}.
\]
Let
\[
w_d^{(L)}
=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi\in A_d^{(L)}],
\]
be its ordinary \(q\)-mass, and
\[
t_{n,d}^{(L)}
=\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1\in A_d^{(L)}]
\]
its conditioned one-coordinate \(q\)-mass.

Since every value in the shell lies between \(d\) and \(Ld\),
\[
d^q p_A\le w_d^{(L)}\le(Ld)^q p_A,
\tag{5.1}
\]
and, writing \(\mu_A=nQ_n(\xi_1\in A)\),
\[
\frac{d^q\mu_A}{n}
\le t_{n,d}^{(L)}
\le\frac{(Ld)^q\mu_A}{n}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — quantitative shell replication bound

There are constants \(c_L,C_L>0\) such that the following holds. Let
\[
t=t_{n,d}^{(L)}>0,
\]
and suppose
\[
\frac{nt}{d^q}\longrightarrow\infty
\]
along the sequence under consideration. Then, for all sufficiently large terms,
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{t}{C_L w_d^{(L)}}
\le
C_L\frac{\lambda_n d^q}{nt}
-
c_L\frac{d^q}{t}
H\!\left(c_L t d^{-r}\right).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

By (5.2),
\[
\mu_A\ge\frac{nt}{(Ld)^q}.
\]
Choose
\[
m=\left\lfloor\frac{nt}{4L^q d^q}\right\rfloor.
\]
The assumption \(nt/d^q\to\infty\) makes \(m\to\infty\), and for large terms \(\mu_A\ge2m\). Also (5.1) gives
\[
p_A\le\frac{w_d^{(L)}}{d^q},
\]
so
\[
\frac{\mu_A}{2np_A}
\ge
\frac{t}{2L^q w_d^{(L)}}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Moreover
\[
m\asymp_L\frac{nt}{d^q},
\qquad
\frac{n-m}{m}\gtrsim_L\frac{d^q}{t},
\]
and, because the exact conditioning itself forces \(Q_n(\xi_1\in A)\le 1/d\), the relevant chosen \(m\) is sublinear and
\[
\frac{1+m(d-1)}{n-m}
\gtrsim_L t d^{1-q}=t d^{-r}.
\]
The function \(H\) is nondecreasing because it is a supremum of affine functions with nonnegative slopes. Insert these estimates and (5.4) into Theorem 3.1. \(\square\)

The statement is useful even when \(t\to0\): it records exactly how the conditioned shell amplification, the central denominator cost, the shell \(q\)-mass, and the genuine lower-deviation rate must balance.

# 6. NEW: a strict entropy-scale gap for any macroscopic shell anomaly

Take \(L>1\) and a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). Suppose along a sequence of admissible pairs \((n,d)\),
\[
d\to\infty,
\qquad
d=o(n^{1/q}),
\qquad
t_{n,d}^{(L)}\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{6.1}
\]
Then \(nt/d^q\to\infty\), and Theorem 5.1 gives constants depending only on \(L,\varepsilon\) such that
\[
\log\frac{\varepsilon}{C w_d^{(L)}}
\le
C\frac{\lambda_n d^q}{n}
-cd^qH(c d^{-r}).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\),
\[
w_d^{(L)}\to0.
\tag{6.3}
\]
By (4.2),
\[
\boxed{
d^qH(c d^{-r})\to\infty.}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Indeed \((d^{-r})^p=d^{-q}\).

Rearranging (6.2) gives the necessary central-denominator cost
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda_n}{n}
\ge
c_1H(c_2d^{-r})
+
\frac{c_1}{d^q}
\log\frac{\varepsilon}{Cw_d^{(L)}}
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
for all sufficiently large terms of any sequence satisfying (6.1).

In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lambda_n d^q}{n}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Thus, writing the entropy scale from the previous campaign as
\[
B_n=\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n+1}\right)^{1/q},
\]
any multiplicative shell carrying a fixed positive amount of the conditioned root \(q\)-moment must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
\frac d{B_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]

This is strictly stronger than the earlier statement that the low window through \(B_n\) is harmless: a *macroscopic shell anomaly cannot sit just above the entropy scale*. It must be separated from that scale by a diverging factor determined by the offspring lower-deviation modulus \(H\).

Equivalently, since \(H\) is nondecreasing and (4.2) implies
\[
H^{-1}(z)=o(z^{1/p})\quad(z\downarrow0)
\]
in the generalized-inverse sense, (6.5) forces a shell location at least on the scale
\[
d\gtrsim
\left[H^{-1}(C\lambda_n/n)\right]^{-1/r},
\]
which is asymptotically larger than \((n/\lambda_n)^{1/q}\).

### What this does *not* prove

A failure of root uniform integrability need not put a fixed \(\varepsilon\) of conditioned \(q\)-moment into one dyadic shell. It could spread a nonvanishing total tail over a growing number of shells, each carrying \(o(1)\). Therefore (6.7) is a genuine structural restriction, not a root proof.

The quantitative form (5.3) is intended for exactly that remaining diffuse possibility: if shell masses \(t=t_{n,d}\) tend to zero, it retains their actual value rather than replacing them by a fixed \(\varepsilon\).

## 7. Consequences for counterexample design

The preceding checkpoint already showed that any all-level counterexample must create a tail-amplified likelihood-ratio spike in a balanced mesoscopic prefix corridor. At the root, the new factorial theorem adds another independent requirement.

A candidate lacunary construction cannot simply create a single moving degree, or a bounded number of multiplicative degree bands, each carrying order-one conditioned \(q\)-moment near the entropy scale. Repeating the same anomalous band \(m\) times under the exact conditioning would force a lower deviation of order \(md\), and (4.2) makes that repeated event super-\(q\)-expensive.

Hence any root counterexample in the subnatural range must become increasingly *scale-diffuse*: its non-UI moment mass has to be distributed over a growing collection of degree bands with individual conditioned \(q\)-mass tending to zero, while simultaneously satisfying the shell inequality (5.3) in every active band.

This is compatible with neither the simple one-atom missing-tail-mean mechanism nor a finite collection of lacunary spikes. It does not yet rule out a carefully engineered hierarchy with many simultaneously active bands.

For higher fixed levels, the newest all-level Scheffé/Cramér reduction remains the controlling framework. The factorial replication argument above uses exchangeable offspring-coordinate counts and is therefore currently a root-level tool; it is **not** being promoted to an every-level theorem. A useful next question is whether the same replication principle can be applied to boundary vertices inside the exact finite-depth prefix likelihood ratio without losing the finite \(q\)-budget.

## 8. Literature check and non-use of unverified local limits

The primary Janson source was rechecked. The published form states the same conjecture as Conjecture 21.1 and explicitly says that the statement is further conjectured for noninteger \(r>0\); it also identifies \(\mathbb E\xi^{r+1}<\infty\) as the only interesting finite-limit regime.

A bounded fresh literature search again found the standard local subexponential / big-jump machinery (Asmussen--Foss--Korshunov; Ng--Tang; Denisov--Dieker--Shneer), but no theorem that can be inserted here without additional hypotheses. The available generic random-sum local results use stronger domination/light-count assumptions, while the Denisov--Dieker--Shneer uniform local theorem requires explicit natural-scale, insensitivity, truncation, and small-step sequences. Verifying those assumptions for this arbitrary critical total-progeny law would amount to solving a substantial part of the present mesoscopic local problem. None is assumed here.

The new factorial-replication theorem uses only exact exchangeability, convexity of falling factorials, and a one-sided Chernoff transform whose small-argument behavior follows directly from \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\). No infinite-variance local limit theorem is used.

## 10. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to combine the shell theorem with the exact tail budget instead of treating shells separately.

Let dyadic bands be \(A_j=[2^j,2^{j+1})\), with ordinary and conditioned \(q\)-masses \(w_j,t_{n,j}\). Then
\[
\sum_{j:J\le j}w_j\to0
\]
as \(J\to\infty\), while a root UI failure would require a subsequence for which the corresponding conditioned tail sum stays bounded away from zero. The quantitative inequality (5.3) now constrains every band by its *own* \(t_{n,j}/w_j\), not by a worst-case tail modulus.

The concrete proof target is a summable consequence of (5.3), for example an estimate of the schematic form
\[
\sum_{j\ge J}t_{n,j}
\le C\sum_{j\ge J}w_j+o_J(1)
\]
uniformly in admissible large \(n\). A successful derivation would close the root case without any unverified local limit theorem. The all-level analogue would then be attacked through the exact finite-depth likelihood ratio and its weighted Scheffé formulation.

On the counterexample side, (5.3) supplies a precise certificate to test: a lacunary construction must create a growing family of simultaneously active shells whose amplification factors overcome both the central denominator cost and the super-\(q\) replication penalty, while their ordinary \(q\)-masses remain summable. No such construction is currently certified.
