## 2. Exact all-level reduction retained

Let
\[
X:=|T|,\qquad \nu_n:=\Pr(X=n),
\]
and for fixed \(k\) under the ordinary unconditioned tree set
\[
Z_j:=\ell_j(T),\qquad
N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L_k:=Z_k.
\]
The branching property gives
\[
X=N_k+\sum_{i=1}^{L_k}X_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]
Let
\[
m_{k,r}:=\mathbb E L_k^r
\]
and define the \(r\)-size-biased prefix pair
\[
\Pr(A_k=a,B_k=b)
=\frac{b^r}{m_{k,r}}\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b).
\tag{2.2}
\]
Earlier verified work proves
\[
\mathbb EA_k<\infty,\qquad \mathbb EB_k<\infty,
\tag{2.3}
\]
and exactly
\[
\mathbb E\ell_k(T_n)^r
=m_{k,r}\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Since the fixed-level distributional limit has finite \(r\)-moment, UI is equivalent to
\[
\frac{\Pr(A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n)}{\nu_n}
\longrightarrow \mathbb EB_k.
\tag{2.5}
\]

After removing the lattice span, put
\[
U:=\frac{X-1}{h},\qquad u_M:=\Pr(U=M)=\nu_{1+hM}.
\tag{2.6}

\[
C:=\frac{A_k+B_k-1}{h}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{2.7}

For \(n=1+hM\),
\[
A_k+\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}X_i=n
\iff
\sum_{i=1}^{B_k}U_i=M-C,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\left[\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M-C}}{u_M}\right]

\tag{2.8}

## 3. Retained intrinsic Laplace window

Let
\[
\phi(t):=\mathbb Ee^{-tU},\qquad
K(t):=-\log\phi(t),\qquad
\psi(t):=K(t)/t.
\tag{3.1}
\]
The immediately preceding report proved directly from the offspring pgf equation, without regular variation or an infinite-variance LLT,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{K(t)}{t^{1/q}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{K(t)}{\sqrt t}\to0
\qquad(t\downarrow0).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{
t^{(q-1)/q}\psi(t)\to\infty,
\qquad
\sqrt t\,\psi(t)\to0.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
The proof uses the offspring pgf remainder
\[
R(y)=f(1-y)-(1-y)
\]
and the strict scale window
\[
y^2\ll R(y)\ll y^q.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The preceding addendum used an arbitrarily slowly diverging inflation factor \(a_M\) to pay the denominator \(u_M\) in a Chernoff bound. The main new result below removes that artificial inflation completely by optimizing the Chernoff exponent before choosing the forest-count cutoff.

# 4. NEW: exact Cramér penalty for the forest count

For \(M\ge1\) and integer \(b\ge1\), define
\[
\boxed{
I_M(b):=\sup_{t>0}\{bK(t)-Mt\}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
This is the one-sided Cramér–Chernoff penalty for forcing a sum of \(b\) copies of the infinite-mean variable \(U\) to remain at most \(M\).

### Lemma 4.1 — uniform shifted convolution bound
For every \(b\ge1\) and every \(c\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le \exp\{L_M-I_M(b)\}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof
If \(c>M\), the left side is zero. Otherwise, for any \(t>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^{*b}_{M-c}
&\le \Pr(U_1+\cdots+U_b\le M)\\
&\le e^{tM}\phi(t)^b
=\exp\{tM-bK(t)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first symbol in the display is \(u^{*b}_{M-c}\). Divide by \(u_M=e^{-L_M}\) and optimize over \(t\). \(\square\)

The function \(b\mapsto I_M(b)\) is nondecreasing, since it is the supremum of affine functions of \(b\) with nonnegative slope \(K(t)\).

# 5. NEW: optimal denominator-paying cutoff, with no slow inflation

Because \((u_M)\) is a probability mass function on the eventually full span-reduced support, \(u_M\to0\) and hence
\[
L_M\to\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Choose, for definiteness,
\[
\omega_M:=\sqrt{L_M},\qquad
H_M:=L_M+\omega_M.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus \(\omega_M\to\infty\), \(H_M\sim L_M\), and \(H_M=o(M)\).

Define the **optimal Chernoff safe cutoff**
\[
\boxed{
\beta_M^*:=\min\{b\ge1:I_M(b)\ge H_M\}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
The set is nonempty for every sufficiently large \(M\): for example, fix any \(t>0\) with \(K(t)>0\), then \(bK(t)-Mt\to\infty\) as \(b\to\infty\).

### Theorem 5.1 — all counts above \(\beta_M^*\) are negligible pointwise
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{b\ge\beta_M^*}\sup_{c\ge0}
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le e^{-\omega_M}\to0.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof
For \(b\ge\beta_M^*\), monotonicity gives \(I_M(b)\ge H_M=L_M+\omega_M\). Apply (4.2). \(\square\)

This is stronger than the previous cutoff formulation because it is the exact Cramér threshold needed to beat the actual denominator by a diverging amount. No auxiliary \(a_M\to\infty\) is needed.

# 6. NEW: sharp universal scale sandwich for the optimal cutoff

### Theorem 6.1
With \(H_M\) as in (5.2),
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{M H_M}\ll \beta_M^*
\ll_o
M^{1/q}H_M^{(q-1)/q}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\beta_M^*\to\infty,
\qquad
\beta_M^*=o(M).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Since \(H_M\sim L_M\), this may be written
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{M L_M}\ll \beta_M^*
\ll_o M^{1/q}L_M^{r/q}.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Proof: upper side
Set
\[
t_M:=H_M/M\to0.
\]
Let
\[
b_M:=\left\lceil\frac{2M}{\psi(t_M)}\right\rceil.
\]
Since \(K(t_M)=t_M\psi(t_M)\),
\[
b_MK(t_M)-Mt_M\ge2H_M-H_M=H_M.
\]
Hence
\[
\beta_M^*\le b_M.
\tag{6.4}
\]
By (3.3),
\[
\psi(t_M)t_M^{(q-1)/q}\to\infty,
\]
so
\[
\frac{M}{\psi(t_M)}
=o\left(Mt_M^{(q-1)/q}\right)
=o\left(M^{1/q}H_M^{(q-1)/q}\right).
\]
This proves the upper side of (6.1).

### Proof: lower side
Fix \(C<\infty\). We prove
\[
\sup_{1\le b\le C\sqrt{MH_M}}
\frac{I_M(b)}{H_M}\to0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Let \(\varepsilon>0\). From \(K(t)/\sqrt t\to0\), choose \(t_0>0\) such that
\[
K(t)\le\varepsilon\sqrt t,
\qquad 0<t\le t_0.
\]
For \(b\le C\sqrt{MH_M}\) and \(t\le t_0\),
\[
bK(t)-Mt
\le C\varepsilon\sqrt{MH_Mt}-Mt.
\]
Writing \(x=\sqrt{Mt/H_M}\), the right side divided by \(H_M\) is at most
\[
C\varepsilon x-x^2
\le \frac{C^2\varepsilon^2}{4}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
For \(t\ge t_0\), note that \(\phi(t)\ge\Pr(U=0)=\Pr(X=1)=\pi_0>0\), so
\[
K(t)\le -\log\pi_0=:K_\infty<\infty.
\]
Therefore
\[
bK(t)-Mt
\le CK_\infty\sqrt{MH_M}-Mt_0\to-\infty,
\]
because \(H_M=o(M)\). Thus
\[
\limsup_M\sup_{b\le C\sqrt{MH_M}}
\frac{I_M(b)}{H_M}
\le C^2\varepsilon^2/4.
\]
Let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). This proves (6.5), so no fixed multiple of \(\sqrt{MH_M}\) can reach the threshold \(H_M\), proving the lower side of (6.1). \(\square\)

### Universal exponent consequence
For \(n=1+hM\), (2.11)–(2.12) give
\[
L_M=o\left(M^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right).
\]
Thus the upper side of (6.3) yields
\[
\boxed{
\beta_M^*=o(M^{E_r}),
\qquad
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
Indeed
\[
\frac1q+\frac rq\frac{1-r}{1+r}
=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}.
\]
This is the same universal exponent that arose independently in the earlier arbitrary-law large-width escape theorem. Here it comes from the exact total-progeny Laplace/Cramér geometry.

# 7. NEW: superlinear-spike certificate for any counterexample

The exact all-level reduction and the finite first moment of \(B_k\) force a much stronger necessary condition on any failure than merely “the uniform linear convolution bound is not proved.”

Let the catastrophic prefix contribution be removed as in the preceding checkpoint, and work on the balanced event \(G_k\), where
\[
0\le C\le D_k(1+B_k).
\]
For every fixed \(J\), fixed-count/fixed-shift local subexponentiality handles \(B_k\le J\). Theorem 5.1 handles \(B_k\ge\beta_M^*\) with an error at most \(e^{-\omega_M}\). Therefore failure of UI can only occur through
\[
J<B_k<\beta_M^*.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\delta_k(J):=\mathbb E[B_k;B_k>J]\longrightarrow0
\tag{7.2}
\]
because \(\mathbb EB_k<\infty\).

### Proposition 7.1 — failure forces arbitrarily large superlinear local ratios
If UI fails at fixed level \(k\), then there exist sequences
\[
M_j\to\infty,\qquad b_j\to\infty,\qquad
0\le c_j\le D_k(1+b_j),
\]
with
\[
b_j<\beta_{M_j}^*=o(M_j),
\tag{7.3}
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b_j}_{M_j-c_j}}
{b_j u_{M_j}}
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Proof
If UI fails, after removing the already controlled catastrophic, fixed-count, and \(b\ge\beta_M^*\) pieces, there are \(\varepsilon>0\), a sequence \(J_j\to\infty\), and \(M_j\to\infty\) such that
\[
\mathbb E\left[
\mathbf1_{G_k}\mathbf1_{\{J_j<B_k<\beta_{M_j}^*\}}
\frac{u^{*B_k}_{M_j-C}}{u_{M_j}}
\right]\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{7.5}
\]
If every feasible pair in this expectation satisfied
\[
\frac{u^{*b}_{M_j-c}}{b u_{M_j}}
<\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta_k(J_j)},
\]
then (7.5) would be strictly less than
\[
\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta_k(J_j)}
\mathbb E[B_k;B_k>J_j]
=\varepsilon,
\]
a contradiction. Hence some feasible \((b_j,c_j)\) satisfies
\[
\frac{u^{*b_j}_{M_j-c_j}}{b_j u_{M_j}}
\ge\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta_k(J_j)}\to\infty.
\]
The count bounds follow from its location in (7.5). \(\square\)

So a counterexample cannot arise from a merely nonuniform constant in a Kesten-type estimate. It must create **superlinear spikes of unbounded height** in a mesoscopic shifted convolution ratio.

# 8. NEW: equivalent nearby-central-valley certificate via Dwass

The spike certificate has a direct offspring-sum interpretation.

For a feasible pair \((b,c)\), put
\[
n:=1+hM,
\qquad
s:=b+h(M-c).
\tag{8.1}
\]
Then
\[
\sum_{i=1}^bX_i=s
\iff
\sum_{i=1}^bU_i=M-c.
\]
The forest Dwass identity gives
\[
u^{*b}_{M-c}
=\frac bs\Pr(S_s=s-b),
\tag{8.2}
\]
while ordinary Dwass gives
\[
u_M=\frac1n\Pr(S_n=n-1).
\tag{8.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{b u_M}
=
\frac ns
\frac{\Pr(S_s=s-b)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
On the unresolved balanced corridor, \(b=o(M)\) and \(c=O_k(b)\), so
\[
\frac sn\to1.
\tag{8.5}
\]
Combining (7.4) and (8.4), **any counterexample must therefore exhibit a genuine nearby central local-probability valley**: there exist
\[
|s_j-n_j|=O_k(b_j)=o(n_j),
\qquad b_j\to\infty,
\]
with
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Pr(S_{s_j}=s_j-b_j)}
{\Pr(S_{n_j}=n_j-1)}
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{8.6}
\]
The numerator and denominator concern nearby sample sizes and both are near their means; the numerator is shifted downward by only the mesoscopic amount \(b_j\). Thus a successful lacunary counterexample must manufacture arbitrarily deep **local central valleys**, not merely a sparse support, a small denominator in isolation, or a missing upper-tail mean.

This certificate is compatible with the earlier Bernoulli smoothing theorem: that theorem controls adjacent pmf differences additively by \(O(1/n)\), but an additive estimate alone does not rule out a very deep multiplicative valley when the central atom is much smaller than \(1/n\). The new proposition makes that remaining obstruction explicit.

## 9. Tail budget of the tilted boundary count

The tilted count has an exact integrable tail budget. From (2.2),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[B_k;B_k>J]
=\frac{\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k>J]}{\mathbb E Z_k^r}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
For \(k=1\),
\[
\Pr(B_1=d)=\frac{d^r\pi_d}{\mathbb E\xi^r},
\qquad
\mathbb E[B_1;B_1>J]
=\frac{\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>J]}{\mathbb E\xi^r}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
This makes Proposition 7.1 especially sharp at the root: if root UI fails, the superlinear spike in (7.4) must grow faster than the reciprocal of the vanishing offspring \(q\)-moment tail along a suitable sequence.

A related harmless fact is that finite mean implies
\[
x\Pr(B_k>x)\to0.
\tag{9.3}
\]
At the intrinsic Laplace count
\[
a_M:=1/K(1/M),
\]
(3.2) gives
\[
\sqrt M\ll a_M\ll M^{1/q},
\tag{9.4}
\]
and hence
\[
\Pr(B_k>a_M)=o(K(1/M)).
\tag{9.5}
\]
This is not by itself enough for UI because \(u_M\) can be far smaller than \(K(1/M)\); it is recorded only as a quantitative restriction on where prefix probability mass can sit.

## 10. Literature audit for the random-count endpoint

A fresh check of Ng–Tang, *Asymptotic behavior of tail and local probabilities for sums of subexponential random variables*, J. Appl. Probab. 41 (2004), 108–116, DOI `10.1239/jap/1077134671`, confirms that its abstract allows a finite-mean random count in the general setup, but the **local randomized theorem actually used for sums** is Theorem 4.2 and explicitly assumes
\[
\mathbb E e^{\delta\tau}<\infty
\quad\text{for some }\delta>0.
\]
Their proof first obtains an exponential Kesten-type bound (their (4.4)–(4.5)) and then invokes dominated convergence using this exponential moment. Full-text source checked in this run:

- <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38350634_Asymptotic_behavior_of_tail_and_local_probabilities_for_sums_of_subexponential_random_variables>
- journal page: <https://doi.org/10.1239/jap/1077134671>

Thus the finite-mean hypothesis stated in the abstract does **not** provide the missing local theorem under only \(\mathbb EB_k<\infty\).

Asmussen–Foss–Korshunov, *Asymptotics for sums of random variables with local subexponential behaviour*, J. Theoret. Probab. 16 (2003), 489–518, DOI `10.1023/A:1023535030388`, remains the source of generic local-subexponential/Kesten machinery, but the exponential-in-count domination is exactly too expensive at this endpoint. The power-law-lattice random-sum results of Bloznelis (arXiv:1801.01035) impose power-law structure and do not settle the arbitrary-law problem here.

No fresh source found in this bounded check supplies the finite-mean, arbitrary locally subexponential random-count asymptotic required by (2.8), and no such theorem is assumed.
