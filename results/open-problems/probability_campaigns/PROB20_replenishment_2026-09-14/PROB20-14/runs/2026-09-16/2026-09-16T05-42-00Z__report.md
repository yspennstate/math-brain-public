## 2. Retained notation

Write
\[
S_n=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_n,\qquad
a_n=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad
\lambda_n=-\log a_n,
\]
and let
\[
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1).
\]
Let \(\mu_n\) denote the one-coordinate marginal of \(Q_n\):
\[
\mu_n(d)=Q_n(\xi_1=d).
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}\xi=1-\frac1n.
\tag{2.1}
\]
The cyclic lemma gives for the root degree \(D_n=\ell_1(T_n)\)
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;\xi>K].
}
\tag{2.2}
\]
Earlier verified work gives
\[
\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(2-q)/q}\right),
\tag{2.3}
\]
fixed-shift strong ratios, fixed-level distributional convergence, finite-core smoothing, the root entropy-window theorem, Palm/renewal bounds, and an all-level random-prefix reduction.

Define the one-sided negative transform
\[
\Lambda(\theta):=\log\mathbb E e^{-\theta(\xi-1)},\qquad
H(y):=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E(\xi-1)=0\), Jensen gives \(\Lambda(\theta)\ge0\). The finite-\(q\), infinite-variance assumptions imply in the existing dependency chain
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=o(\theta^q),\qquad
\frac{H(y)}{y^{q/(q-1)}}\to\infty
\quad(y\downarrow0).
\tag{2.5}
\]

# 3. NEW: exact correlation-budget decomposition

The previous entropy argument only used the elementary marginal bound
\[
nD(\mu_n\|\pi)\le\lambda_n.
\]
There is an exact decomposition which shows that a nontrivial part of \(\lambda_n\) is unavoidably spent merely to correlate the coordinates enough to enforce the exact sum.

### Theorem 3.1 — entropy splits into marginal distortion plus total correlation

For every admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
=
D(Q_n\|\pi^{\otimes n})
=
nD(\mu_n\|\pi)
+
D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n}).
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof
Since \(Q_n=\pi^{\otimes n}(\cdot\mid S_n=n-1)\),
\[
D(Q_n\|\pi^{\otimes n})=-\log a_n=\lambda_n.
\]
Also
\[
\log\frac{dQ_n}{d\pi^{\otimes n}}
=
\log\frac{dQ_n}{d\mu_n^{\otimes n}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^n\log\frac{d\mu_n}{d\pi}(\xi_i).
\]
Taking \(Q_n\)-expectations and using exchangeability gives (3.1). \(\square\)

The second term is not optional: \(Q_n\) is supported on one exact-sum hyperplane. Data processing through the indicator of
\[
A_n:=\{x_1+\cdots+x_n=n-1\}
\]
gives
\[
D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})
\ge
-\log \mu_n^{\otimes n}(A_n).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma 3.2 — a universal \(\frac12\log n\) correlation tax

There is a law-dependent finite constant \(C_0\) such that, for every sufficiently large admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_m\mu_n^{*n}(m)\le \frac{C_0}{\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})
\ge \frac12\log n-C_1
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
nD(\mu_n\|\pi)
\le
\lambda_n-\frac12\log n+C_1.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Proof of (3.3)
Choose two distinct offspring values \(a<b\) with \(\pi_a,\pi_b>0\); critical nondegeneracy permits, for example, \(a=0\) and any positive support point. Fixed-coordinate convergence \(\mu_n(d)\to\pi_d\) (already proved independently from the target endpoint) yields an \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
\mu_n(a),\mu_n(b)\ge\eta
\]
for all large admissible \(n\).

Write
\[
\mu_n=2\eta\,\nu+(1-2\eta)\rho_n,
\qquad
\nu=\tfrac12(\delta_a+\delta_b),
\]
a valid mixture after reducing \(\eta\) if necessary. Generate \(n\) iid \(\mu_n\)-variables by first choosing independent core indicators with success probability \(2\eta\). Let \(M\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,2\eta)\) be the number of core variables. Conditional on all non-core values, on the core locations, and on \(M=m\), the remaining randomness is a sum of \(m\) iid fair \(\{a,b\}\)-valued variables. Its largest atom is at most \(C/\sqrt m\). Therefore
\[
\sup_x\mu_n^{*n}(x)
\le
\Pr(M<\eta n)+\frac{C}{\sqrt{\eta n}}
\le e^{-cn}+\frac{C'}{\sqrt n}.
\]
This proves (3.3), and (3.4) follows from (3.2). \(\square\)

Define the **residual marginal-entropy budget**
\[
E_n:=\max\left\{1,\lambda_n-\frac12\log n+C_1\right\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{D(\mu_n\|\pi)\le E_n/n.}
\tag{3.7}
\]
This strictly improves the previous \(\lambda_n/n\) marginal entropy bound whenever a substantial part of the central-atom exponent is merely the unavoidable exact-sum correlation cost.

# 4. NEW: enlarged root entropy window

The preceding one-sided entropy argument can now be rerun with \(E_n\) in place of \(\lambda_n\).

Put
\[
V(x):=\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le x],
\qquad
v(x):=x^{q-2}V(x),
\qquad
v^*(K):=\sup_{x\ge K}v(x).
\]
Finite \(q\)-moment implies \(v^*(K)\downarrow0\).

For \(t>0\), the one-sided entropy lemma applied to
\(f_t(x)=x\mathbf1_{\{x\le t\}}\) and (3.7) gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi;\xi>t]
\le
\mathbb E_{\pi}[\xi;\xi>t]
+
\sqrt{\frac{2E_n}{n}V(t)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Using the layer-cake inequality from the preceding entropy checkpoint,
\[
x^q\mathbf1_{\{K<x\le B\}}
\le
K^{q-1}x\mathbf1_{\{x>K\}}
+(q-1)\int_K^B t^{q-2}x\mathbf1_{\{x>t\}}\,dt,
\]
one obtains
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;K<\xi\le B]
\le
\mathbb E_{\pi}[\xi^q;\xi>K]
+C_q\sqrt{\frac{E_n}{n}B^qv^*(K)}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Therefore, for any deterministic \(B_n\to\infty\) satisfying
\[
\sup_n B_n^qE_n/n<\infty,
\tag{4.3}
\]
we have
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E[D_n^r;K<D_n\le B_n]=0.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]
In particular the natural correlation-corrected entropy scale is
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde B_n:=\left(\frac{n}{E_n}\right)^{1/q},
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
which is never smaller, up to constants, than the earlier \((n/(\lambda_n+1))^{1/q}\) scale and may be substantially larger.

This is a genuine strengthening but does not by itself close the root problem because \(E_n\) need not be bounded.

# 5. NEW: multiplicity-free Bernoulli-KL mark theorem

The preceding factorial-replication theorem used an integer number of replicated marks. The newest single-mark theorem covered the opposite order-one-multiplicity endpoint. The next result gives a continuous interpolation with **no multiplicity threshold** and, crucially, charges marginal distortion against the reduced budget \(E_n\), not the full \(\lambda_n\).

Let
\[
g:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to[0,1].
\]
Pass \(\xi\) through the randomized Bernoulli channel \(B\mid\xi\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(g(\xi))\). Define
\[
\alpha:=\mathbb E_{\mu_n}g(\xi),
\qquad
p:=\mathbb E_{\pi}g(\xi).
\]
For \(\theta\ge0\), let
\[
\pi_\theta(d):=\pi_d e^{-\theta(d-1)-\Lambda(\theta)},
\qquad
\rho_\theta:=\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}g(\xi).
\tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — tilted mark relative-entropy inequality

For every \(\theta\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
nD(\mathrm{Ber}(\alpha)\|\mathrm{Ber}(\rho_\theta))
\le
E_n-\theta+n\Lambda(\theta).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Proof
Data processing through the randomized mark channel gives
\[
D(\mathrm{Ber}(\alpha)\|\mathrm{Ber}(\rho_\theta))
\le D(\mu_n\|\pi_\theta).
\]
By (2.1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\mu_n\|\pi_\theta)
&=D(\mu_n\|\pi)
+\theta\mathbb E_{\mu_n}(\xi-1)+\Lambda(\theta)\\
&=D(\mu_n\|\pi)-\frac\theta n+\Lambda(\theta).
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiply by \(n\) and use (3.7). \(\square\)

Suppose now that \(g\) is supported on \(\{d,d+1,\ldots\}\), \(d\ge1\). Then
\[
\rho_\theta
\le
p\,e^{-\theta(d-1)-\Lambda(\theta)}
\le p.
\tag{5.3}
\]
If \(\alpha\ge p\), binary KL decreases as its second argument increases below \(\alpha\), and
\[
D(\mathrm{Ber}(\alpha)\|\mathrm{Ber}(u))
\ge
\alpha\log(\alpha/u)-\alpha
\qquad(0<u\le\alpha).
\tag{5.4}
\]
Substitute (5.3) into (5.2) and optimize over \(\theta\). For \(0\le\alpha<1\), this yields
\[
\boxed{
E_n
\ge
n\alpha\left(\log\frac\alpha p-1\right)
+
n(1-\alpha)
H\!\left(
\frac{1+n\alpha(d-1)}{n(1-\alpha)}
\right).
}
\tag{5.5}
\]

This is the continuous, multiplicity-free analogue of the earlier factorial certificate. It remains valid when \(n\alpha<1\), and for \(n\alpha\gg1\) it has the same entropy and lower-deviation structure as the replicated-mark theorem, but with no floor/threshold losses.

It is complementary to the previous single-mark exact-conditioning inequality
\[
\alpha
\le
p\exp\!\left\{
\lambda_n-(n-1)H\!\left(\frac d{n-1}\right)
\right\},
\tag{5.6}
\]
which is often stronger when \(d^q\gg n\). The two mechanisms cross naturally when a constant \(q\)-moment contribution is carried at \(d^q\asymp n\).

# 6. NEW: shell form and removal of the shell-spreading loophole up to a logarithmic loss

Fix \(L>1\), and consider a multiplicative shell
\[
A=[d,Ld)\cap\mathbb Z.
\]
Define its conditioned and ordinary \(q\)-masses
\[
t:=\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;\xi\in A],
\qquad
w:=\mathbb E_{\pi}[\xi^q;\xi\in A].
\tag{6.1}
\]
For the hard mark \(g=\mathbf1_A\),
\[
\frac{t}{L^qd^q}\le\alpha\le\frac{t}{d^q},
\qquad
\frac{w}{L^qd^q}\le p\le\frac{w}{d^q}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\frac\alpha p\ge\frac{t}{L^qw}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Whenever \(t/(L^qw)\) is large enough and \(d\) is large enough that \(\alpha\le1/2\), (5.5) yields constants depending only on \(L\) and the law such that
\[
\boxed{
E_n
\ge
c_L\frac{nt}{d^q}
\left(\log\frac{t}{C_Lw}-1\right)
+
c_L n
H\!\left(c_L\frac{t}{d^{q-1}}\right).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
The single-mark theorem simultaneously gives
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\ge
(n-1)H\!\left(\frac d{n-1}\right)
+
\log\frac{t}{L^qw}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus the two lower-deviation arguments act at the dual deficit fractions
\[
\frac{t}{d^{q-1}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac dn.
\tag{6.6}
\]
For \(t\asymp1\), they meet exactly at \(d^q\asymp n\).

The important point is that shell spreading can no longer hide all amplification in shells of vanishing mass without leaving a quantitative trace.

### Proposition 6.1 — amplified-shell localization

Suppose for some \(\varepsilon>0\), some integer \(K\ge1\), and an admissible \(n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;\xi>K]\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Let
\[
R_q(K):=\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K].
\]
Since \(\mu_n\) is supported on \([0,n-1]\), partition \((K,n)\) into dyadic shells
\[
A_j=[2^jK,2^{j+1}K),\qquad j=0,\ldots,J-1,
\]
where
\[
J\le 2+\log_2(n/K).
\tag{6.8}
\]
Write \(t_j,w_j\) for the conditioned and ordinary \(q\)-masses of these shells. Then there exists a shell \(A_j=[d,2d)\) such that
\[
\boxed{
t_j\ge\frac{\varepsilon}{2J}}
\tag{6.9}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\frac{t_j}{w_j}\ge\frac{\varepsilon}{2R_q(K)}.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]

### Proof
Call a shell `large` if \(t_j\ge\varepsilon/(2J)\). The total conditioned mass of non-large shells is at most \(\varepsilon/2\), so the large shells carry at least \(\varepsilon/2\). If every large shell satisfied \(t_j/w_j<\varepsilon/(2R_q(K))\), then
\[
\sum_{j\,\mathrm{large}}w_j
>
\frac{2R_q(K)}\varepsilon
\sum_{j\,\mathrm{large}}t_j
\ge R_q(K),
\]
contradicting \(\sum_jw_j\le R_q(K)\). \(\square\)

For the shell supplied by Proposition 6.1, (6.5) gives the sharpened necessary condition
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\ge
(n-1)H\!\left(\frac d{n-1}\right)
+
\log\frac{\varepsilon}{C_qR_q(K)}.
}
\tag{6.11}
\]
This is strictly stronger than the preceding whole-tail certificate, whose Cramér term only saw the original threshold \(K\): the new shell location \(d\) is where the actual conditional/ordinary amplification occurs.

Moreover, (6.4), (6.9), and (6.10) imply
\[
\boxed{
E_n
\ge
c\frac{n}{Jd^q}
\left(
\log\frac{\varepsilon}{C_qR_q(K)}-C
\right)
+
c n
H\!\left(\frac{c}{Jd^{q-1}}\right).
}
\tag{6.12}
\]
Thus any root counterexample must fit inside a simultaneous **two-budget corridor**: the full central exponent \(\lambda_n\) must pay the single-mark shell Cramér cost, while the residual marginal-distortion budget \(E_n\) must pay the replicated/entropy cost after the unavoidable \(\frac12\log n\) exact-sum correlation tax has already been removed.

Equivalently, if root UI fails, then along some \(K_j\to\infty\), admissible \(n_j\to\infty\), there are dyadic scales \(d_j\in[K_j,n_j]\), with
\[
J_j\le2+\log_2(n_j/K_j),
\]
such that, up to fixed constants,
\[
(n_j-1)H(d_j/(n_j-1))
\lesssim
\lambda_{n_j}-\log(1/R_q(K_j)),
\tag{6.13}
\]
and
\[
\frac{n_j}{J_jd_j^q}\log\frac1{R_q(K_j)}
+n_jH\!\left(\frac{c}{J_jd_j^{q-1}}\right)
\lesssim E_{n_j}.
\tag{6.14}
\]
This is the sharpest anti-spreading certificate currently in the campaign. A lacunary counterexample must manufacture a shell that simultaneously survives both inequalities.

# 7. Relation to the all-level problem

Nothing in Sections 3–6 is promoted from the root to a full every-level proof. The exact all-level random-prefix reduction from earlier verified work remains the correct framework. For fixed \(k\), with ordinary generation size \(B=Z_k\), the preceding campaign established
\[
\tau_k(j):=\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k\ge j]\downarrow0
\]
and a weighted Cramér estimate of the form
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
\le
\frac{\tau_k(j)}{j}
\exp\{L_M-I_M(j)\},
\]
with the exact duality
\[
I_M(j)=(hM+j)H\!\left(\frac{j}{hM+j}\right).
\]
The unresolved all-level endpoint is still the weighted mesoscopic random-prefix corridor below the Cramér-safe cutoff.

The new root analysis suggests a specific transplant rather than a generic pointwise Kesten bound: one should seek an all-level analogue of the entropy decomposition in which the Kesten-prefix law is the reference marginal and the exact-size forest conditioning pays an unavoidable correlation/concentration tax before any endpoint \(B^r\)-distortion is charged. No such theorem is claimed here.

# 10. Next attack

The highest-value next proof step is now a variational one. Assume a root tail anomaly of fixed size \(\varepsilon\). Proposition 6.1 localizes an amplified shell with mass at least \(\varepsilon/O(\log n)\). The task is to optimize the simultaneous constraints
\[
(n-1)H(d/(n-1)) + \log(1/R_q(K))\lesssim\lambda_n
\]
and
\[
\frac{n}{d^q\log n}\log(1/R_q(K))
+nH\!\left(\frac{c}{d^{q-1}\log n}\right)
\lesssim E_n.
\]
One route is to combine these with the finite-core central-atom construction to upper-bound \(E_n\), rather than \(\lambda_n\), in terms of the same truncated-tail moduli. This is the natural place where the new correlation subtraction may matter.

On the adversarial side, a lacunary candidate now has to produce a shell whose ordinary \(q\)-mass is smaller than its conditioned shell mass by at least the global factor \(\varepsilon/R_q(K)\), while paying both the exact-shell Cramér penalty and the residual marginal entropy budget. A numerical construction that does not verify these two inequalities is not a viable counterexample.

For fixed \(k>1\), the analogous research target is a correlation-budget decomposition for the conditioned random-prefix/forest law relative to its Kesten-prefix limit, followed by the same dyadic amplified-shell localization for the boundary count \(Z_k\).
