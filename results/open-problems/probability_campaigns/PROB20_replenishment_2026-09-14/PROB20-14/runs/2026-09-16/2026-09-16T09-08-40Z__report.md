# 3. NEW: an adaptive essentially maximal anti-concentration radius

The preceding checkpoint selected
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{old}}
=\frac{\sqrt{n/(x_n+1)}}{\log(e+n/(x_n+1))}.
\]
The logarithm was used only to make the outer Fourier integral negligible. It is not intrinsic. The correct radius is determined by two transparent constraints: enough truncated variance must survive the marginal entropy perturbation, and the finite support core must suppress the Fourier integral outside the small-frequency window.

Because the span of \(\bar\pi\) is one and \(\bar\pi_0>0\), choose once and for all a finite subset of its support containing \(0\) whose positive elements have gcd one. Since \(d_n\to0\), Pinsker implies that the corresponding masses under \(\bar\mu_n\) are eventually bounded below. Therefore there is a fixed \(c_* >0\) such that, for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\),
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2\ge c_*t^2,
\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi,
\tag{3.1}
\]
where \(\varphi_n(t)=\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}e^{it\bar\xi}\).

Fix a constant \(A>0\) sufficiently large in terms of \(c_*\). For integer \(R\ge1\), call \(R\) **admissible for the Fourier truncation** if
\[
d_nR^2\le \frac{c_0}{2}V(R)
\tag{3.2}
\]
and
\[
\frac n{R^2}\ge A\log(e+V(R)).
\tag{3.3}
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{
\rho_n:=\max\{R\in\mathbb N:R\text{ satisfies (3.2) and (3.3)}\}.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
For large \(n\) this set is nonempty and finite. Indeed, every fixed \(R\) eventually satisfies (3.2) because \(d_n\to0\), and satisfies (3.3) because \(n\to\infty\); while (3.3) fails once \(R\) is larger than a constant multiple of \(\sqrt n\). It follows by diagonalization over fixed \(R\) that
\[
\boxed{\rho_n\to\infty.}
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — adaptive anti-concentration

There is a constant \(C<\infty\), depending only on the offspring law, such that for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_m\bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le
\frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(\rho_n)}}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Proof

By (2.8) and (3.2),
\[
V_n(\rho_n)\ge\frac{c_0}{2}V(\rho_n).
\tag{3.7}
\]
For \(|t|\le1/\rho_n\), take iid \(X,X'\sim\bar\mu_n\). Since \(\bar\mu_n(0)\) is bounded below and \(1-\cos u\ge cu^2\) for \(|u|\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
&=\mathbb E[1-\cos(t(X-X'))]\\
&\ge c t^2V_n(\rho_n)\\
&\ge c' t^2V(\rho_n).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Using \(u^n\le\exp[-n(1-u^2)/2]\) and Fourier inversion,
\[
\sup_m\bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt.
\tag{3.9}
\]
The small-frequency part is therefore
\[
\int_{|t|\le1/\rho_n}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt
\le\frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(\rho_n)}}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
For the complement, the fixed-core estimate (3.1) gives
\[
\int_{1/\rho_n<|t|\le\pi}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt
\le\frac{C}{\sqrt n}
\exp\!\left(-c\frac n{\rho_n^2}\right).
\tag{3.11}
\]
By (3.3), after choosing \(A\) so that \(cA>1/2\),
\[
\sqrt{V(\rho_n)}
\exp\!\left(-c\frac n{\rho_n^2}\right)
\le
(e+V(\rho_n))^{1/2-cA}=o(1).
\tag{3.12}
\]
Thus (3.11) is negligible compared with the right side of (3.10), proving (3.6). \(\square\)

The point is that \(\rho_n\) is chosen from the actual truncated variance profile of the law. No artificial logarithmic loss is imposed unless the outer Fourier integral genuinely requires it.

## 4. NEW explicit lower bound on the adaptive radius

The implicit radius above always dominates a much simpler explicit radius. Choose \(B>0\) sufficiently large, depending only on the fixed-core Fourier constant, and define
\[
\boxed{
R_n^{\sharp}
:=
\left\lfloor
\sqrt{\frac{n}{x_n+B\log n}}
\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Because \(x_n=o(n)\),
\[
R_n^{\sharp}\to\infty.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Moreover
\[
d_n(R_n^{\sharp})^2
\le
\frac{x_n}{x_n+B\log n}
\le1.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Since \(V(R_n^{\sharp})\to\infty\), (3.2) holds eventually. Also
\[
\frac n{(R_n^{\sharp})^2}
\ge x_n+B\log n,
\tag{4.4}
\]
while \(V(R)\le R^2\le n\), so
\[
\log(e+V(R_n^{\sharp}))\le 2\log n
\]
for large \(n\). Taking \(B\ge2A\) makes (3.3) hold. Hence
\[
\boxed{\rho_n\ge R_n^{\sharp}}
\tag{4.5}
\]
eventually.

This is a strict asymptotic improvement over the previous
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{old}}
=\frac{\sqrt{n/(x_n+1)}}{\log(e+n/(x_n+1))}.
\]
Indeed, since \(x_n=o(n^a)\) for some fixed \(a<1\) by (2.3), one checks directly that
\[
\frac{R_n^{\sharp}}{R_n^{\mathrm{old}}}\to\infty.
\tag{4.6}
\]
If \(x_n\gg\log n\), the improvement is essentially a full factor \(\log(n/x_n)\); if \(x_n\lesssim\log n\), it is at least of order \(\sqrt{\log n}\).

# 6. NEW all-level change of reference: use the Kesten prefix, not the ordinary prefix

The preceding report used the ordinary fixed-prefix law \(p_k\). For endpoint moments this wastes the natural size-bias which is already present in the limiting Kesten tree. Removing it gives a cleaner exact identity.

Fix \(k\ge1\). Under the ordinary unconditioned tree write
\[
A:=N_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,
\qquad
B:=L_k=Z_k,
\]
and let
\[
p_k(a,b):=\Pr(A=a,B=b).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Criticality gives
\[
\mathbb E_{p_k}B=\mathbb EZ_k=1.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\widehat p_k(a,b):=b\,p_k(a,b),\qquad b\ge1,
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
is a probability law. It is exactly the law of the first \(k\) levels of the Kesten tree, expressed through \((N_k,Z_k)\).

Under \(T_n\), let
\[
q_{n,k}(a,b):=\Pr(A=a,B=b\mid |T|=n).
\tag{6.4}
\]
Let
\[
c_{n,k}:=q_{n,k}(B\ge1).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Fixed-level local convergence gives
\[
c_{n,k}\to1.
\tag{6.6}
\]
On \(\{b\ge1\}\), normalize
\[
\widetilde q_{n,k}(a,b)
:=\frac{q_{n,k}(a,b)}{c_{n,k}}.
\tag{6.7}
\]

Let \(\nu_m:=\Pr(|T|=m)\). The branching property gives
\[
q_{n,k}(a,b)
=p_k(a,b)\frac{\nu^{*b}_{n-a}}{\nu_n}.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Put \(m=n-a\). Forest Dwass and one-tree Dwass give
\[
\nu_m^{*b}
=\frac bm\Pr(S_m=m-b),
\qquad
\nu_n=\frac1n\Pr(S_n=n-1)=\frac{a_n}{n}.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Dividing (6.8) by the Kesten prefix law (6.3) produces the exact likelihood ratio
\[
\boxed{
\frac{q_{n,k}(a,b)}{\widehat p_k(a,b)}
=
\frac n{n-a}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-a}=n-a-b)}{a_n},
\qquad b\ge1.
}
\tag{6.10}
\]
This is the all-level analogue of the root degree ratio after the natural size-bias has been factored out. There is **no remaining factor \(b\)**.

# 7. NEW exact Kesten-reference entropy/local-cost decomposition

Define the exact local lower-deviation surprise
\[
I_m(b):=-\log\Pr(S_m=m-b),
\qquad 1\le b\le m.
\tag{7.1}
\]
Taking logarithms in (6.10), normalizing by \(c_{n,k}\), and averaging under \(\widetilde q_{n,k}\) yields the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&D(\widetilde q_{n,k}\|\widehat p_k)
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}} I_{n-A}(B)\\
&\qquad=
\lambda_n-\log c_{n,k}
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}}
\log\frac n{n-A}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proof

From (6.10),
\[
\log\frac{\widetilde q_{n,k}(a,b)}{\widehat p_k(a,b)}
=
\lambda_n-\log c_{n,k}
+\log\frac n{n-a}
-I_{n-a}(b).
\]
Average. \(\square\)

This is sharper than the ordinary-prefix decomposition from the preceding report. The natural Kesten size-bias has disappeared exactly, and the central denominator exponent \(\lambda_n\) is split into two nonnegative costs:

1. **prefix distortion from the Kesten law**, \(D(\widetilde q_{n,k}\|\widehat p_k)\);
2. **the exact local price paid by the residual forest**, \(\mathbb E I_{n-A}(B)\);

plus only the geometric shift term \(\mathbb E\log(n/(n-A))\) and the vanishing normalization \(-\log c_{n,k}\).

For \(k=1\), \(A=1\), \(B=D_n\), and \(c_{n,1}=1\) for \(n>1\). Therefore (7.2) specializes to the exact root identity
\[
\boxed{
D(\mathcal L(D_n)\|\widehat\pi)
+\mathbb E I_{n-1}(D_n)
=
\lambda_n+\log\frac n{n-1},
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
where \(\widehat\pi_d=d\pi_d\) is the size-biased offspring law. This exact budget identity appears to be a more natural starting point for the fractional endpoint than relative entropy against \(\pi\) itself.

## 8. Cramér-energy corollary of the exact all-level identity

Retain
\[
\Lambda(\theta):=\log\mathbb Ee^{-\theta(\xi-1)},
\qquad
H(y):=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]
Chernoff gives
\[
\Pr(S_m=m-b)
\le e^{-mH(b/m)},
\]
so
\[
I_m(b)\ge mH(b/m).
\tag{8.2}
\]
Hence (7.2) immediately implies the every-fixed-level budget inequality
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&D(\widetilde q_{n,k}\|\widehat p_k)
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}}
\left[(n-A)H\!\left(\frac{B}{n-A}\right)\right]\\
&\qquad\le
\lambda_n-\log c_{n,k}
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}}
\log\frac n{n-A}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

This inequality is valid without a local limit theorem and without regular variation. It is also non-circular: it is obtained only from the exact branching factorization, the two Dwass identities, and Chernoff’s inequality.

The prior campaign had already proved that the unresolved contribution may be confined to **balanced mesoscopic prefixes**: after removing an exponentially controlled catastrophic-prefix component and a pointwise Cramér-safe large-count component, one is left with
\[
A\le C_k(1+B),\qquad B=o(n).
\tag{8.4}
\]
On this region,
\[
\log\frac n{n-A}=o(1)
\tag{8.5}
\]
pointwise. Thus (7.2)–(8.3) identify the exact two quantities that can consume the denominator budget on the surviving region: distortion away from the Kesten prefix and residual-forest lower-deviation energy.

A global \(o(1)\) bound on the shift expectation is **not** claimed here; local convergence alone does not provide an arbitrary rate. The exact identity (7.2) is retained with the shift term when no prior localization is invoked.

# 9. NEW formulation of the all-level endpoint as weighted Scheffé under a finite-moment reference law

The Kesten reference makes the missing endpoint especially transparent. Under \(\widehat p_k\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}B^r
=\mathbb E_{p_k}B^{1+r}
=\mathbb EZ_k^q
<\infty,
\tag{9.1}
\]
where finiteness was established in earlier verified work by the von Bahr–Esseen recursion for every fixed generation.

Let
\[
R_{n,k}(a,b)
:=
\frac{q_{n,k}(a,b)}{\widehat p_k(a,b)},
\qquad b\ge1.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Then (6.10) gives
\[
R_{n,k}(a,b)
=
\frac n{n-a}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-a}=n-a-b)}{a_n}.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Fixed-level local convergence is exactly ordinary Scheffé convergence of this density to one after the negligible \(B=0\) mass is removed:
\[
R_{n,k}\to1
\quad\text{in }L^1(\widehat p_k).
\tag{9.4}
\]
The desired fractional moment convergence/UI is the **weighted** upgrade
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}
\left[B^r|R_{n,k}-1|\right]\to0.
}
\tag{9.5}
\]
Indeed (9.5) implies
\[
\mathbb E\ell_k(T_n)^r
\to
\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}B^r
=
\mathbb EZ_k^q,
\]
and, together with distributional convergence and integrability of the limit, is equivalent to UI.

Thus the every-fixed-level problem is now pinned to a precise analytic endpoint:

> upgrade unweighted Scheffé convergence of the Kesten-prefix likelihood ratios to \(B^r\)-weighted Scheffé convergence, under the exact entropy/local-cost budget (7.2) and with \(\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}B^r<\infty\).

This formulation automatically includes the root as \(k=1\). It also explains why generic bounded-expectation arguments are insufficient: ordinary \(L^1\) convergence of densities does not imply convergence against an unbounded integrable weight.

## 10. A many-category consequence relative to the Kesten law

Although it does not yet close the endpoint, (7.2) gives a clean all-level anti-spreading mechanism.

Let \(E_1,\ldots,E_J\subseteq\{B\ge1\}\) be disjoint prefix events, and under \(\widetilde q_{n,k}\) and \(\widehat p_k\) write
\[
\alpha_j:=\widetilde q_{n,k}(E_j),
\qquad
\beta_j:=\widehat p_k(E_j),
\qquad
\alpha:=\sum_j\alpha_j.
\]
Data processing of relative entropy to the categorical partition gives
\[
D(\widetilde q_{n,k}\|\widehat p_k)
\ge
\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{\beta_j}-\alpha.
\tag{10.1}
\]
Combining with (7.2),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{\beta_j}-\alpha
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}} I_{n-A}(B)\\
&\qquad\le
\lambda_n-\log c_{n,k}
+\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}}
\log\frac n{n-A}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{10.2}
\]
This is the direct every-level analogue of the root categorical argument, but now the comparison measure is already the Kesten law and hence already carries the correct size bias.

For dyadic boundary-count shells \(E_j=\{d_j\le B<2d_j\}\), define
\[
t_j:=\mathbb E_{\widetilde q_{n,k}}[B^r;E_j],
\qquad
w_j:=\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}[B^r;E_j].
\]
Then
\[
\alpha_j\ge\frac{t_j}{(2d_j)^r},
\qquad
\beta_j\le\frac{w_j}{d_j^r}.
\tag{10.3}
\]
Thus any collection of shells with
\[
t_j\ge 2^re^2 w_j
\]
has \(\alpha_j/\beta_j\ge e^2\), so its entropy contribution in (10.2) is positive and additive. Since
\[
\sum_{d_j>K}w_j
=\mathbb E_{\widehat p_k}[B^r;B>K]	o0,
\tag{10.4}
\]
a hypothetical non-UI subsequence must create a genuinely amplified family of boundary-count shells, and all of those shells must share the **single** budget (10.2). This prevents an all-level counterexample from evading the argument merely by spreading its excess fractional moment over arbitrarily many boundary-count shells.

Unlike the root coordinate argument, the forest deficit generated by one prefix is only \(B\), rather than a sum of \(n\) repeated marked coordinates. Therefore (10.2) does not by itself reproduce the stronger root replication penalty. This is a real remaining gap, not a missing algebraic step.

## 11. Consequences for counterexample design

The cumulative reductions now force a prospective counterexample into a very narrow form.

### Root

A root counterexample must simultaneously survive:

- the aggregate single-mark Cramér-plus-ordinary-\(q\)-tail inequality from the 05:00 checkpoint;
- the many-shell categorical root penalty from the 07:43 checkpoint;
- the stronger marginal/correlation split (5.3), where the variance tax is now evaluated at the adaptive radius \(\rho_n\), and at least at \(R_n^{\sharp}\);
- canonical \(W_q\)-recentering and finite-core smoothing from earlier checkpoints.

The old near-square-root truncation was not the limiting factor: the variance tax can now be pushed to the maximal scale allowed simultaneously by entropy retention and outer Fourier suppression.

### Every fixed level

For \(k>1\), the correct baseline is no longer the ordinary prefix law but the Kesten prefix \(\widehat p_k=b p_k\). Any non-UI sequence must violate the weighted Scheffé upgrade (9.5) while ordinary Scheffé holds. The exact amount of distortion and local forest lower-deviation cost is constrained by (7.2), and shell amplification is constrained collectively by (10.2).

On the already isolated balanced mesoscopic region, the likelihood ratio is
\[
R_{n,k}(A,B)
=\frac n{n-A}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-A}=n-A-B)}{a_n},
\]
so a counterexample must create large weighted likelihood spikes from **nearby central local-probability valleys**, with \(A=O_k(B)=o(n)\), while still paying the single entropy/local-cost budget (7.2).

No such certified construction is currently known in this campaign.
