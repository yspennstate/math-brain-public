## 2. Retained root notation

\[

a_n:=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad
\lambda_n:=-\log a_n,

\[
Q_n:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1).

d_n:=D(\mu_n\|\pi),\qquad x_n:=n d_n.
\tag{2.1}

\[
\boxed{\lambda_n=x_n+D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})}
\tag{2.2}

\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(2-q)/q}\right)=o(n),\qquad x_n\le\lambda_n=o(n).

# 3. NEW: one-sided entropy retains truncated variance with an \(O(dR^2)\), not \(O(\sqrt d\,R^2)\), loss

### Lemma 3.1 — entropy retention

Let \(P,Q\) be probability laws and \(D(Q\|P)=d<\infty\). If \(0\le f\le1\), then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_Q f\ge (1-e^{-1})\,\mathbb E_P f-d.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

The variational inequality for relative entropy with test function \(-f\) gives
\[
d\ge -\mathbb E_Q f-\log\mathbb E_P e^{-f}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
For \(0\le u\le1\), convexity of \(e^{-u}\) gives the secant bound
\[
e^{-u}\le 1-(1-e^{-1})u.
\tag{3.3}
\]
Hence, with \(c_0:=1-e^{-1}\),
\[
-\log\mathbb E_Pe^{-f}
\ge -\log(1-c_0\mathbb E_Pf)
\ge c_0\mathbb E_Pf.
\tag{3.4}
\]
Insert this into (3.2). \(\square\)

Apply the lemma with
\[
f_R(x)=\frac{x^2}{R^2}\mathbf1_{\{x\le R\}}.
\]
Writing
\[
V_n(R):=\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}[\bar\xi^2;\bar\xi\le R],
\]
we obtain the stronger transfer inequality
\[
\boxed{
V_n(R)\ge c_0V(R)-d_nR^2.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
The previous checkpoint used Pinsker and lost \(R^2\sqrt{d_n}\). The linear-in-entropy loss in (3.5) is substantially smaller.

# 4. NEW: near-square-root variance-sensitive anti-concentration

Put
\[
y_n:=\frac{n}{x_n+1}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
L_n:=\log(e+y_n),
\tag{4.1}
\]
and define the almost-square-root truncation radius
\[
\boxed{
R_n:=\frac{\sqrt{y_n}}{L_n}
=\frac{1}{\log(e+n/(x_n+1))}
\sqrt{\frac{n}{x_n+1}}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Then \(R_n\to\infty\) and
\[
d_nR_n^2
=\frac{x_n}{x_n+1}\frac1{L_n^2}
\le \frac1{L_n^2}\to0.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Since \(V(R_n)\to\infty\), (3.5) implies, eventually,
\[
\boxed{V_n(R_n)\ge c_1V(R_n)}
\tag{4.4}
\]
for a fixed \(c_1>0\).

### Theorem 4.1 — improved concentration bound

There is \(C<\infty\), depending only on the offspring law, such that for all sufficiently large admissible \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_m\bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(R_n)}}.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
\varphi_n(t)=\mathbb E_{\bar\mu_n}e^{it\bar\xi},\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi.
\]
Since \(d_n\to0\), Pinsker is used only to retain fixed support masses: \(\bar\mu_n(0)\ge\bar\pi(0)/2>0\) for large \(n\), and a fixed finite span-one support core of \(\bar\pi\) has all its masses bounded below under \(\bar\mu_n\).

For \(|t|\le1/R_n\), if \(X,X'\) are iid \(\bar\mu_n\),
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
=\mathbb E[1-\cos(t(X-X'))].
\]
Restricting to pairs with exactly one coordinate equal to zero and then to \(X\le R_n\) gives
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2
\ge c t^2V_n(R_n)
\ge c't^2V(R_n).
\tag{4.6}
\]
The fixed finite span-one core gives globally
\[
1-|\varphi_n(t)|^2\ge c t^2,
\qquad -\pi\le t\le\pi.
\tag{4.7}
\]
Using \(u^n\le\exp[-n(1-u^2)/2]\) for \(0\le u\le1\), Fourier inversion yields
\[
\sup_m\bar\mu_n^{*n}(m)
\le \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt.
\tag{4.8}
\]
On \(|t|\le1/R_n\), (4.6) gives
\[
\int_{|t|\le1/R_n}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt{nV(R_n)}}.
\tag{4.9}
\]
On the complement, (4.7) gives the Gaussian tail bound
\[
\int_{1/R_n<|t|\le\pi}|\varphi_n(t)|^n\,dt
\le \frac{C}{\sqrt n}
\exp\!\left(-c\frac{n}{R_n^2}\right).
\tag{4.10}
\]
But
\[
\frac n{R_n^2}=(x_n+1)L_n^2\ge L_n^2,
\tag{4.11}
\]
and \(V(R_n)\le R_n^2=y_n/L_n^2\). Therefore
\[
\sqrt{V(R_n)}\,
\exp[-c n/R_n^2]
\le
\frac{\sqrt{y_n}}{L_n}e^{-cL_n^2}	o0.
\tag{4.12}
\]
Thus (4.10) is \(o((nV(R_n))^{-1/2})\), proving (4.5). \(\square\)

This pushes the earlier power radius \((n/(x_n+1))^\gamma\), \(\gamma<1/4\), essentially to
\[
\sqrt{\frac{n}{x_n+1}}
\]
up to a logarithm. No LLT, regular variation, stable-domain assumption, or finite variance is used.

# 5. NEW: stronger correlation tax and self-consistency inequality

Because \(Q_n\) is supported on the exact-sum event,
\[
D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})
\ge -\log\mu_n^{*n}(n-1).
\]
Theorem 4.1 therefore gives
\[
\boxed{
D(Q_n\|\mu_n^{\otimes n})
\ge
\frac12\log n
+\frac12\log V(R_n)-C.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Combining with (2.2),
\[
\boxed{
x_n
+\frac12\log
V\!\left(
\frac{\sqrt{n/(x_n+1)}}{\log(e+n/(x_n+1))}
\right)
\le
\lambda_n-\frac12\log n+C.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
This strictly strengthens the preceding checkpoint’s \(\gamma<1/4\) self-consistency inequality.

Since \(x_n\le\lambda_n\), define
\[
\mathcal R_n
:=
\frac{\sqrt{n/(\lambda_n+1)}}
{\log(e+n/(\lambda_n+1))}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
The function \(y\mapsto \sqrt y/\log(e+y)\) is eventually increasing, so (5.2) also implies
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\ge
\frac12\log n
+\frac12\log V(\mathcal R_n)-C.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
In particular the extra correlation tax above \(\frac12\log n\) diverges, now measured at a substantially larger truncation radius than before.

Any upper bound \(U_n\ge x_n\) may be fed back into (5.2): if
\[
\mathcal R(U_n)
:=
\frac{\sqrt{n/(U_n+1)}}
{\log(e+n/(U_n+1))},
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
x_n
\le
\lambda_n-\frac12\log n
-\frac12\log V(\mathcal R(U_n))+C.
}
\tag{5.5}
\]
This gives a stronger bootstrap for the root marginal-distortion budget and therefore enlarges every previous entropy-controlled low-degree window.

# 6. NEW: categorical tilted-entropy inequality for many degree shells at once

The previous shell-spreading argument isolated one dyadic shell and paid an unavoidable factor of order \(\log n\) by pigeonhole. That loss can be removed by coarse-graining **all amplified shells simultaneously**.

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_J\) be disjoint subsets of the offspring support with
\[
A_j\subseteq\{d_j,d_j+1,\ldots\},\qquad d_j\ge1.
\]
Set
\[
\alpha_j:=\mu_n(A_j),\qquad p_j:=\pi(A_j),\qquad
\alpha:=\sum_j\alpha_j,
\tag{6.1}
\]
and
\[
M:=\sum_j\alpha_j(d_j-1).
\tag{6.2}
\]
Define
\[
\Lambda(\theta):=\log\mathbb E e^{-\theta(\xi-1)},
\qquad
H(y):=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 — categorical shell inequality

With the usual convention \(0\log0=0\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
x_n\ge{}&
 n\sum_{j=1}^J\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{p_j}
-n\alpha\\
&+n(1-\alpha)
H\!\left(
\frac{1+nM}{n(1-\alpha)}
\right).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

### Proof

Let the exponentially tilted offspring law be
\[
\pi_\theta(d)
=\pi_d e^{-\theta(d-1)-\Lambda(\theta)}.
\]
Because \(\mathbb E_{\mu_n}(\xi-1)=-1/n\),
\[
D(\mu_n\|\pi_\theta)
=d_n-\frac\theta n+\Lambda(\theta).
\tag{6.5}
\]
Let
\[
\rho_j:=\pi_\theta(A_j),\qquad \rho:=\sum_j\rho_j.
\]
Data processing to the categorical partition \(A_1,\ldots,A_J,A_0\) gives
\[
D(\mu_n\|\pi_\theta)
\ge
\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{\rho_j}
+(1-\alpha)\log\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\rho}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
The complement term satisfies
\[
(1-\alpha)\log\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\rho}
\ge (1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha)
\ge-\alpha.
\tag{6.7}
\]
Also
\[
\rho_j
\le p_j e^{-\theta(d_j-1)-\Lambda(\theta)}.
\tag{6.8}
\]
Hence
\[
D(\mu_n\|\pi_\theta)
\ge
\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{p_j}
+\theta M+\alpha\Lambda(\theta)-\alpha.
\tag{6.9}
\]
Insert (6.5), multiply by \(n\), rearrange, and optimize in \(\theta\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_n
&\ge n\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{p_j}-n\alpha\\
&\quad+
\sup_{\theta\ge0}
\{\theta(1+nM)-n(1-\alpha)\Lambda(\theta)\},
\end{aligned}
\]
which is (6.4). \(\square\)

For \(J=1\) this recovers the earlier multiplicity-free Bernoulli-KL mechanism up to harmless constants, but (6.4) is additive across many disjoint shells and therefore does not reuse the same entropy budget separately for every shell.

# 7. NEW: no-log-loss dichotomy for a non-UI root tail

Let
\[
T_n(K):=\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;\xi>K],
\qquad
R_q(K):=\mathbb E_{\pi}[\xi^q;\xi>K].
\tag{7.1}
\]
Assume for a candidate counterexample subsequence that
\[
T_n(K)\ge\varepsilon>0
\tag{7.2}
\]
with \(K\to\infty\).

Partition \((K,n)\) into dyadic shells
\[
A_j=[d_j,2d_j),\qquad d_j=2^jK,
\]
intersected with the support and with \(d_j<n\). Put
\[
t_j:=\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[\xi^q;\xi\in A_j],
\qquad
w_j:=\mathbb E_{\pi}[\xi^q;\xi\in A_j].
\tag{7.3}
\]
Fix
\[
C_q:=2^q e^2.
\]
Call a shell **amplified** if \(t_j\ge C_q w_j\). Since the non-amplified shells carry at most \(C_qR_q(K)\), for all large \(K\) with
\[
C_qR_q(K)\le\varepsilon/2,
\]
the amplified shells carry total conditioned \(q\)-mass at least \(\varepsilon/2\).

For an amplified shell,
\[
\alpha_j:=\mu_n(A_j)
\ge \frac{t_j}{(2d_j)^q},
\qquad
p_j:=\pi(A_j)\le\frac{w_j}{d_j^q},
\]
so
\[
\frac{\alpha_j}{p_j}
\ge\frac{t_j}{2^qw_j}\ge e^2.
\tag{7.4}
\]
Therefore on the amplified collection
\[
\sum_j\alpha_j\log\frac{\alpha_j}{p_j}-\alpha\ge\alpha\ge0.
\tag{7.5}
\]
Also, because \(\mathbb E_{\mu_n}\xi=1-1/n\),
\[
\alpha\le\Pr_{\mu_n}(\xi>K)\le\frac1K=o(1).
\tag{7.6}
\]

Fix any scale \(D\) with \(K<D<n/2\). There are two cases.

### Case A: at least \(\varepsilon/4\) amplified \(q\)-mass lies in shells with \(d_j\le D\)

For \(K\ge2\),
\[
M
=\sum_j\alpha_j(d_j-1)
\ge c_q\sum_{d_j\le D}\frac{t_j}{d_j^{q-1}}
\ge c_q\frac{\varepsilon}{D^{q-1}}.
\tag{7.7}
\]
Using (7.5)–(7.6) in Theorem 6.1 and monotonicity of \(H\),
\[
\boxed{
x_n
\ge c n
H\!\left(c_q\frac{\varepsilon}{D^{q-1}}\right)
}
\tag{7.8}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(K,n\).

### Case B: at least \(\varepsilon/4\) amplified \(q\)-mass lies in shells with \(d_j>D\)

Then
\[
T_n(D)\ge\varepsilon/4.
\tag{7.9}
\]
The previously verified aggregate single-mark Cramér inequality gives
\[
T_n(D)
\le R_q(D)
\exp\left\{
\lambda_n-(n-1)H\!\left(\frac{D+1}{n-1}\right)
\right\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_n
\ge
(n-1)H\!\left(\frac{D+1}{n-1}\right)
+\log\frac{\varepsilon}{4R_q(D)}.
}
\tag{7.10}
\]

### Corollary 7.1 — two-mechanism certificate without a dyadic \(\log n\) loss

Any root non-UI subsequence satisfying (7.2) must, for every \(D\in(K,n/2)\) and all sufficiently large \(K\), satisfy at least one of (7.8) or (7.10).

Taking the natural crossover
\[
D\asymp n^{1/q},
\]
both Cramér arguments are of order
\[
nH\!\left(c n^{-(q-1)/q}\right).
\tag{7.11}
\]
Since finite \(q\)-moment gives \(\Lambda(\theta)=o(\theta^q)\), convex duality implies
\[
\frac{H(y)}{y^{q/(q-1)}}\to\infty
\qquad(y\downarrow0),
\tag{7.12}
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
nH(cn^{-(q-1)/q})\to\infty.}
\tag{7.13}
\]
The previous shell argument had an additional logarithmic dilution because it first selected one shell. The categorical inequality removes that loss completely.

This still does **not** close the root case: \(x_n\) and \(\lambda_n\) may themselves diverge faster than the universal rate in (7.13) for an irregular lacunary law. But any counterexample now has to finance one of the two explicit Cramér costs without hiding its anomalous moment over many shells.

# 8. NEW all-level identity: prefix distortion plus forest exact-conditioning cost

The root entropy decomposition has an exact fixed-level analogue that was not recorded in the earlier random-prefix reduction.

Let \(X=|T|\) under the ordinary critical Galton–Watson law and
\[
\nu_m:=\Pr(X=m).
\]
For fixed \(k\), put
\[
N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j,\qquad L_k:=Z_k,
\]
and let
\[
p_k(a,b):=\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b)
\]
under the ordinary tree. Under the conditioned tree \(T_n\), let
\[
q_{n,k}(a,b):=\Pr(N_k=a,L_k=b\mid X=n).
\]
By the branching property,
\[
\boxed{
q_{n,k}(a,b)
=
 p_k(a,b)\frac{\nu^{*b}_{n-a}}{\nu_n},
}
\tag{8.1}
\]
with the convention \(\nu^{*0}_0=1\), \(\nu^{*0}_m=0\) for \(m\ne0\).

### Proposition 8.1 — exact prefix/forest entropy decomposition

\[
\boxed{
-\log\nu_n
=
D(q_{n,k}\|p_k)
+
\mathbb E_{q_{n,k}}
[-\log\nu^{*L_k}_{n-N_k}].
}
\tag{8.2}
\]

### Proof

Take logarithms in (8.1), average under \(q_{n,k}\), and use the definition of relative entropy. \(\square\)

For \(b\ge1\) and \(m\ge b\), the forest Dwass identity gives
\[
\nu^{*b}_m
=
\frac bm\Pr(S_m=m-b)
\le\frac bm.
\tag{8.3}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
D(q_{n,k}\|p_k)
\le
-\log\nu_n
-
\mathbb E_{q_{n,k}}
\left[
\mathbf1_{\{L_k\ge1\}}
\log\frac{n-N_k}{L_k}
\right].
}
\tag{8.4}
\]
Since the one-tree Dwass identity gives
\[
\nu_n=\frac1n\Pr(S_n=n-1)=\frac{a_n}{n},
\]
we have
\[
-\log\nu_n=\lambda_n+\log n.
\tag{8.5}
\]

Fixed-prefix local convergence to the Kesten tree implies tightness of \((N_k,L_k)\) under \(q_{n,k}\), and \(\Pr_{q_{n,k}}(L_k=0)\to0\). Hence one may choose a deterministic \(M_n\to\infty\) so slowly that
\[
M_n=o(n),\qquad \log M_n=o(\log n),
\]
and
\[
\Pr_{q_{n,k}}(N_k+L_k\le M_n,\ L_k\ge1)\to1.
\tag{8.6}
\]
On this event,
\[
\log\frac{n-N_k}{L_k}
\ge
\log\frac{n-M_n}{M_n}
=
\log n-o(\log n).
\]
Because the integrand in (8.4) is nonnegative on feasible forests, (8.4)–(8.6) yield the all-level entropy estimate
\[
\boxed{
D(q_{n,k}\|p_k)
\le
\lambda_n+o(\log n).
}
\tag{8.7}
\]
Using the already proved \(\lambda_n\ge\frac12\log n+\omega(1)\), this may also be written
\[
D(q_{n,k}\|p_k)\le\lambda_n+o(\lambda_n).
\tag{8.8}
\]

This does **not** by itself give endpoint UI: a diverging relative-entropy budget can still hide a small amount of large \(L_k^r\)-mass. Its significance is structural. The extra \(+\log n\) in the total-progeny atom \(-\log\nu_n\) is almost entirely an unavoidable forest exact-conditioning cost, exactly paralleling the root marginal/correlation decomposition. Thus higher-level tail distortion should be charged against \(\lambda_n\), not against \(\lambda_n+\log n\), on the tight prefix region.

## 10. Literature check and non-applicable black boxes

A fresh bounded literature check found no theorem resolving the exact arbitrary infinite-variance, finite-\((1+r)\)-moment, noninteger-\(r\), every-fixed-level endpoint. Janson’s published survey still states the noninteger extension as a conjecture (Section 21.1; arXiv v1 Section 20.1).

General strong-renewal/local-large-deviation results such as Caravenna–Doney, *Local large deviations and the strong renewal theorem*, Electron. J. Probab. 24 (2019), arXiv:1612.07635, work under stable-domain/regular-variation-type structure and cannot be imported as an arbitrary-law LLT/SRT here. The present arguments deliberately avoid those unverified hypotheses.
