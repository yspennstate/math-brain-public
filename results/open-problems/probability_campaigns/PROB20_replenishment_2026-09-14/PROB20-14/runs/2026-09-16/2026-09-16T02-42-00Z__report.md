## 2. Retained endpoint reductions

Write
\[
X:=|T|,\qquad U:=\frac{X-1}{h},\qquad u_M:=\Pr(U=M),\qquad L_M:=-\log u_M.
\]
For fixed depth \(k\), expose the ordinary prefix
\[
A:=\sum_{j<k}Z_j,\qquad B:=Z_k,
\]
and put
\[
C:=\frac{A+B-1}{h}.
\]
The exact conditioned-prefix likelihood ratio at size \(n=1+hM\) is
\[
R_M(A,B)=\frac{u^{*B}_{M-C}}{u_M}.
\]
Earlier verified work proves fixed-prefix convergence \(R_M(A,B)\to B\), Scheffé convergence of the whole fixed-depth prefix to the Kesten prefix, and the weighted Cramér estimate
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
\le \frac{\tau_k(j)}j\exp\{L_M-I_M(j)\},
\tag{2.1}
\]
where
\[
\tau_k(j):=\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k\ge j]\downarrow0
\]
and
\[
I_M(b):=\sup_{t\ge0}\{bK(t)-Mt\},
\qquad
K(t):=-\log\mathbb E e^{-tU}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The immediately preceding checkpoint additionally proves a factorial-replication inequality for offspring degree sets and, with
\[
\Lambda(\theta):=\log\mathbb E e^{-\theta(\xi-1)},\qquad
H(y):=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\},
\tag{2.3}
\]
shows
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=o(\theta^q),\qquad
\frac{H(y)}{y^{q/(q-1)}}\to\infty.
\tag{2.4}
\]
For a multiplicative degree shell \(A_d^{(L)}=[d,Ld)\), with ordinary and conditioned one-coordinate \(q\)-masses
\[
w_d^{(L)}=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi\in A_d^{(L)}],
\qquad
t_{n,d}^{(L)}=\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1\in A_d^{(L)}],
\]
it proves, when \(nt/d^q\to\infty\),
\[
\log\frac{t}{C_Lw_d^{(L)}}
\le C_L\frac{\lambda_nd^q}{nt}
-c_L\frac{d^q}{t}H(c_Ltd^{-r}),
\tag{2.5}
\]
where
\[
\lambda_n=-\log\Pr(S_n=n-1).
\]
The new work below identifies an exact duality between the two rate functions \(K\) and \(H\). This removes a layer of approximation from the all-level Cramér side and places the preceding root shell obstruction and the all-level forest obstruction in one common rate geometry.

# 3. NEW: exact offspring / total-progeny Laplace conjugacy

Let
\[
f(s)=\mathbb Es^\xi,
\qquad
T(z)=\mathbb Ez^X.
\]
The total-progeny pgf satisfies the standard branching equation
\[
T(z)=z f(T(z)).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Recall
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=\log\mathbb Ee^{-\theta(\xi-1)}
=\theta+\log f(e^{-\theta}).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact transform parametrization

For every \(\theta\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
T(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=e^{-\theta}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Consequently, for the span-reduced excess total progeny
\[
U=(X-1)/h,
\qquad
\phi(t)=\mathbb Ee^{-tU},
\qquad
K(t)=-\log\phi(t),
\]
one has the exact parametrization
\[
\boxed{
K(h\Lambda(\theta))=\theta-\Lambda(\theta).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

By (3.2),
\[
e^{-\Lambda(\theta)}f(e^{-\theta})
=e^{-\theta}.
\]
Thus \(e^{-\theta}\) is a solution of (3.1) with \(z=e^{-\Lambda(\theta)}\). For \(z<1\), the solution in \([0,1)\) is unique: the map \(s\mapsto s/f(s)\) is strictly increasing because
\[
\frac{d}{ds}\frac{s}{f(s)}
=\frac{f(s)-sf'(s)}{f(s)^2},
\]
and \(f(s)-sf'(s)>0\) for \(0\le s<1\) in the nondegenerate critical case (its derivative is \(-sf''(s)\le0\) and its value at \(1\) is \(0\)). The case \(\theta=0\) is immediate. Hence (3.3).

Now set \(t=h\Lambda(\theta)\). Since \(X=1+hU\),
\[
\phi(t)
=e^{t/h}T(e^{-t/h})
=e^{\Lambda(\theta)}T(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})
=e^{\Lambda(\theta)-\theta}.
\]
Taking minus logarithms gives (3.4). \(\square\)

### Range of the parametrization

The function \(\Lambda\) is finite on \([0,\infty)\) because \(\xi-1\ge-1\). It is strictly convex for a nondegenerate law, satisfies \(\Lambda(0)=\Lambda'(0)=0\), and hence is strictly increasing for \(\theta>0\). Also \(\pi_0>0\) implies
\[
\Lambda(\theta)\sim \theta+\log\pi_0\to\infty.
\]
Therefore \(\theta\mapsto h\Lambda(\theta)\) is a bijection of \([0,\infty)\) onto itself. No asymptotic inversion or local limit theorem is hidden in (3.4).

# 4. NEW: exact identity for the forest Cramér penalty

Recall
\[
H(y)=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\},
\qquad
I_M(b)=\sup_{t\ge0}\{bK(t)-Mt\}.
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — exact Cramér duality

For every \(M\ge0\) and every real \(b\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
I_M(b)
=(hM+b)\,H\!\left(\frac{b}{hM+b}\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Use the bijective parametrization \(t=h\Lambda(\theta)\) and (3.4):
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_M(b)
&=\sup_{\theta\ge0}
\{b(\theta-\Lambda(\theta))-Mh\Lambda(\theta)\}\\
&=\sup_{\theta\ge0}
\{b\theta-(hM+b)\Lambda(\theta)\}\\
&=(hM+b)
\sup_{\theta\ge0}
\left\{\frac{b}{hM+b}\theta-\Lambda(\theta)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is (4.1). \(\square\)

This identity is exact, including the lattice span. It shows that the total-progeny forest lower-tail free energy is not merely analogous to the offspring lower-deviation transform used in the factorial-replication theorem: it is exactly the same transform evaluated at the corresponding deficit fraction.

For \(n=1+hM\), define
\[
\boxed{
J_n(b):=(n-1+b)
H\!\left(\frac{b}{n-1+b}\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Then simply
\[
I_M(b)=J_n(b).
\tag{4.3}
\]

# 5. NEW: explicit all-level Cramér bounds with no total-progeny transform left

Substituting (4.1) into the previously proved pointwise forest bound gives, uniformly in every shift \(c\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le
\exp\left\{
L_M-(hM+b)H\!\left(\frac{b}{hM+b}\right)
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]
Likewise the all-level weighted tail estimate (2.1) becomes
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
\le
\frac{\tau_k(j)}j
\exp\left\{
L_M-(hM+j)H\!\left(\frac{j}{hM+j}\right)
\right\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Thus the weighted safe cutoff may be defined entirely at the offspring level. For any \(\omega_M\to\infty\), let
\[
\Gamma_{M,k}:=\min\left\{j\ge1:
\tau_k(j)=0\text{ or }
(hM+j)H\!\left(\frac{j}{hM+j}\right)
+\log j-\log\tau_k(j)
\ge L_M+\omega_M
\right\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge\Gamma_{M,k}]
\le e^{-\omega_M}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
This is the preceding weighted Cramér theorem rewritten with an exact, explicit rate. The value is conceptual as well as notational: the all-level forest tail and the root factorial-replication obstruction now use one and the same function \(H\).

### Recovery of the intrinsic Laplace window

The exact parametrization also gives a shorter derivation of the previous Laplace-scale theorem. Finite \(q\)-moment gives
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=o(\theta^q).
\]
Infinite variance gives
\[
\frac{\Lambda(\theta)}{\theta^2}\to\infty.
\tag{5.5}
\]
To see (5.5), expand the centered negative exponential remainder and first truncate \((\xi-1)^2\) at a fixed level; after division by \(\theta^2\), the liminf dominates one half of every fixed truncated second moment, and those truncated moments diverge. Since \(\Lambda(\theta)\to0\), taking the logarithm does not change the divergence.

With \(t=h\Lambda(\theta)\) and \(K(t)=\theta-\Lambda(\theta)\sim\theta\), these two relations immediately yield
\[
\frac{K(t)}{t^{1/q}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{K(t)}{\sqrt t}\to0.
\]
So the earlier pgf-defect argument is consistent with, and can be compressed into, the exact conjugacy above.

# 6. NEW: two-sided law-adaptive certificate for a macroscopic root shell anomaly

This section combines the preceding checkpoint's factorial shell theorem with the exact Cramér identity.

Let \(Q_n\) be the conditioned allocation law given \(S_n=n-1\). Fix \(L>1\), and let
\[
t=t_{n,d}^{(L)}
=\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\xi_1^q;d\le\xi_1<Ld].
\]
Suppose along a sequence
\[
d\to\infty,
\qquad d=o(n),
\qquad t\ge\varepsilon>0.
\tag{6.1}
\]
The previous factorial-replication theorem gives, after discarding its additional favorable shell-tail logarithm,
\[
\boxed{
H(c_1d^{-r})
\le C_1\frac{\lambda_n}{n}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
The earlier report stated this in the subnatural range; the present use is only on sequences for which that theorem's replication hypothesis \(nt/d^q\to\infty\) holds. If it does not, then \(d^q/n\) itself is bounded below at the shell's fixed mass scale, placing the shell at or above the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale, where the Cramér side below is the relevant mechanism.

For the Cramér side, the root degree satisfies the exact cyclic relation
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n\ge d]
=\frac{n}{n-1}
\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1\ge d],
\]
so (6.1) implies a fixed positive lower bound for the root fractional tail above \(d\). The all-level bound (5.2) with \(k=1\), together with
\[
L_M=\lambda_n+\log n,
\qquad hM=n-1,
\]
gives
\[
\varepsilon
\le
C\frac{\tau_1(d)}d
\exp\{L_M-J_n(d)\},
\tag{6.3}
\]
where \(\tau_1(d)=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi\ge d]\). Since \(\tau_1(d)\le\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\),
\[
\boxed{
J_n(d)\le L_M-\log d+O(1)\le L_M+O(1).
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
Because \(d=o(n)\), monotonicity of \(H\) yields from (4.2)
\[
\boxed{
H(c_2d/n)
\le C_2\frac{L_M+1}{n}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]

Equations (6.2) and (6.5) are a new two-sided certificate: every multiplicative degree shell carrying a fixed positive amount of conditioned root \(q\)-moment must simultaneously satisfy an offspring-replication lower-tail constraint at scale \(d^{-r}\) and a forest-Cramér lower-tail constraint at scale \(d/n\).

Let the generalized upper inverse be
\[
H^{\leftarrow}(s):=\sup\{y\ge0:H(y)\le s\}.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Then, up to fixed law- and shell-dependent constants, every such shell is forced into the law-adaptive corridor
\[
\boxed{
\left[H^{\leftarrow}\!\left(C\lambda_n/n\right)\right]^{-1/r}
\ \lesssim\ d\ \lesssim\
n\,H^{\leftarrow}\!\left(C(L_M+1)/n\right).
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
This is sharper than recording only polynomial exponents. It retains the actual small-deviation modulus of the offspring law.

The two arguments \(d^{-r}\) and \(d/n\) meet exactly at
\[
d=n^{1/q},
\]
because \(d^{-r}=d/n=n^{-r/q}\) there. Thus the natural \(n^{1/q}\) scale is not an artifact of either proof: it is the exact crossover at which the replication and forest-Cramér deficit fractions coincide.

### Important limitation

Equation (6.7) excludes large classes of concentrated shell anomalies but does **not** prove root UI. A non-UI tail can in principle be spread across a growing number of shells, each with \(t_{n,d}^{(L)}\to0\). The preceding report's quantitative shell inequality (2.5), not only the fixed-\(\varepsilon\) corollary, is required for that diffuse case.

The classical local-subexponential work of Asmussen–Foss–Korshunov studies local convolution asymptotics and Kesten-type domination (Journal of Theoretical Probability 16 (2003), 489–518; DOI 10.1023/A:1023535030388). Generic randomly stopped local-sum results that use exponential-in-count Kesten domination require correspondingly stronger control of the stopping count than the finite first moment available here. No such theorem is imported as a black box.

Janson's `Moment convergence in conditional limit theorems` (Journal of Applied Probability 38 (2001), DOI 10.1239/jap/996986753) concerns conditional normal-limit / moment-convergence frameworks with additional hypotheses and is not assumed to settle this arbitrary infinite-variance endpoint.

The exact identities in Sections 3–5 above are derived directly from the branching functional equation and elementary convex duality; they use no unverified infinite-variance local limit theorem.

## 9. Next attack

The most promising proof target is now a **single-rate weighted shell summation**. On dyadic shells \(A_j=[2^j,2^{j+1})\), let \(w_j\) and \(t_{n,j}\) be the ordinary and conditioned \(q\)-masses. The factorial theorem gives a penalty involving
\[
\frac{2^{jq}}{t_{n,j}}
H(c\,t_{n,j}2^{-jr}),
\]
while the exact Cramér identity gives a high-shell penalty
\[
(n-1+2^j)
H\!\left(\frac{2^j}{n-1+2^j}\right).
\]
Both are now evaluations of the same convex function \(H\). A root proof would follow from a uniform estimate of the schematic form
\[
\sum_{j\ge J}t_{n,j}
\le C\sum_{j\ge J}w_j+o_J(1),
\]
and the exact duality suggests splitting shells according to which of the two \(H\)-arguments, \(t_{n,j}2^{-jr}\) or \(2^j/n\), is larger rather than according to a predetermined polynomial degree scale.

For every fixed level \(k\), the analogous high-count penalty is already explicit through (5.2). The remaining all-level step is to obtain a quantitative weighted Scheffé rate below \(\Gamma_{M,k}\), ideally by exposing enough disjoint descendant blocks to import a replication penalty without requiring moments beyond \(q=1+r\).

On the counterexample side, a lacunary construction must now simultaneously defeat two manifestations of the same rate transform: repeated high-degree occurrences pay \(H(td^{-r})\), while large boundary forests pay \(H(d/n)\). Any proposed construction should be tested against (2.5), (5.2), and the corridor (6.7), not merely against central-atom smallness.
