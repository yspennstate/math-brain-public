## 2. Retained canonical-tilt framework

For \(\theta\ge0\), set
\[
Z(\theta):=\mathbb Ee^{-\theta\xi},\qquad
P_\theta(d):=\frac{\pi_d e^{-\theta d}}{Z(\theta)},\qquad
\mu(\theta):=\mathbb E_\theta\xi.
\tag{2.1}
\]
For every \(m>b\ge1\), let \(\theta_{m,b}\) be the unique parameter satisfying
\[
\mu(\theta_{m,b})=1-b/m,
\]
and write
\[
P_{m,b}:=P_{\theta_{m,b}}.
\]
The preceding checkpoint proved the exact tilt invariance
\[
\mathcal L_\pi((\xi_i)_{i\le m}\mid S_m=m-b)
=
\mathcal L_{P_{m,b}}((\xi_i)_{i\le m}\mid S_m=m-b).
\tag{2.2}
\]
Thus the conditioning point is exactly the mean under the reference product law.

Define its central exponent
\[
c_{m,b}:=-\log P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b),
\tag{2.3}
\]
and
\[
C_m:=1+\sup_{\substack{1\le b\le m/2\\S_m=m-b\text{ admissible}}}c_{m,b}.
\tag{2.4}
\]
Retained proved input:
\[
C_m=o(m^{2/q-1}).
\tag{2.5}
\]
If \(Q_{m,b}\) is the exact conditioned product law and \(\mu_{m,b}\) its one-coordinate marginal, then
\[
c_{m,b}
=mD(\mu_{m,b}\|P_{m,b})
+D(Q_{m,b}\|\mu_{m,b}^{\otimes m}),
\tag{2.6}
\]
and both \(\mu_{m,b}\) and \(P_{m,b}\) have mean \(1-b/m\).

The previous run also defined
\[
V(x):=\mathbb E[\xi^2;\xi\le x],\qquad
v^*(K):=\sup_{x\ge K}x^{q-2}V(x),
\tag{2.7}
\]
so that \(v^*(K)\downarrow0\), and obtained the sufficient condition
\[
C_m^qv^*\!\left((m/C_m)^{1/q}\right)\to0
\tag{2.8}
\]
for the full one-coordinate endpoint, hence for every fixed tree level by the already archived forest reduction.

A major purpose of the present run is to test whether (2.8) can actually follow from the target hypotheses.  It cannot: Section 5 below constructs an explicit class of target laws for which the left side diverges.  This does **not** refute the original UI conjecture; it refutes only this global-rate closure route.

# 3. NEW exact one-step canonical deletion identity

The canonical tilt has a stronger recursive structure than was used in the preceding report.

Fix an admissible \((m,b)\), put
\[
s:=m-b,\qquad N:=m-1,\qquad
\theta_0:=\theta_{m,b},\qquad P_0:=P_{m,b},\qquad c_0:=c_{m,b}.
\]
Let \(d\) satisfy
\[
\mu_{m,b}(d)>0.
\]
After exposing the first coordinate \(\xi_1=d\), the residual exact sum is
\[
S_N=s-d=N-b',\qquad b':=b+d-1.
\tag{3.1}
\]
If \(0<N-b'<N\), let
\[
\theta_1:=\theta_{N,b'},\qquad P_1:=P_{N,b'},\qquad c_1:=c_{N,b'}.
\]
The endpoint cases have their natural continuous conventions: if \(N-b'=N\), use \(P_1=\pi=P_0\) at zero tilt when appropriate; if \(N-b'=0\), use the limiting tilt \(P_1=\delta_0\) and \(c_1=0\).  In the unresolved bridge \(b=o(m)\), \(d=o(m)\), only the interior case occurs eventually.

Define the **one-step canonical retuning cost**
\[
\boxed{
J_{m,b}(d):=N D(P_1\|P_0)\ge0.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — exact deletion/retuning formula

For every supported \(d\) with \(\mu_{m,b}(d)>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}
=
\exp\{c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1}-J_{m,b}(d)\}.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof
The conditioned marginal is
\[
\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_0(d)}
=
\frac{P_0^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b')}
{P_0^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b)}.
\tag{3.4}
\]
The denominator is \(e^{-c_0}\).  On the residual sum slice \(S_N=N-b'=N\mathbb E_{P_1}\xi\), the likelihood ratio between the two product tilts is constant.  More precisely,
\[
P_0^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b')
=
\exp[-ND(P_1\|P_0)]
P_1^{\otimes N}(S_N=N-b')
=
e^{-J_{m,b}(d)-c_1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Substitution into (3.4) gives (3.3). \(\square\)

This identity retains a positive term that all previous one-sided Cramér estimates discarded: the **new residual central exponent** \(c_{m-1,b+d-1}\).

If \(\psi(\theta)=\log Z(\theta)\), then for the tail degrees \(d\ge1\), one has \(\theta_1\ge\theta_0\) and
\[
D(P_1\|P_0)
=
\int_{\theta_0}^{\theta_1}
[\mu(u)-\mu(\theta_1)]\,du,
\tag{3.6}
\]
while the mean displacement is exactly
\[
\boxed{
N[\mu(\theta_0)-\mu(\theta_1)]
=d-\mu(\theta_0).
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Thus \(J_{m,b}(d)\) is the exact Bregman/Cramér cost of retuning the remaining \(m-1\) coordinates after deleting one value \(d\).

### Corollary 3.2 — one-step free-energy decomposition

Averaging the logarithm of (3.3) under the conditioned marginal gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
c_{m,b}
={}&D(\mu_{m,b}\|P_{m,b})\\
&+\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}
 c_{m-1,b+\xi-1}\\
&+\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}J_{m,b}(\xi).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Every term on the right is nonnegative.

This is a different decomposition from (2.6).  Equation (2.6) splits the exact-conditioning cost into marginal distortion and dependence.  Equation (3.8) instead splits it into marginal distortion, the **remaining exact-conditioning free energy after one deletion**, and the exact retuning cost caused by the deleted value.

A particularly useful consequence is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}
\left[c_{m-1,b+\xi-1}+J_{m,b}(\xi)\right]
\le c_{m,b}.
}
\tag{3.9}
\]
The global size of \(c_{m,b}\) is therefore less informative than its local drop under the deletion \((m,b)\mapsto(m-1,b+d-1)\).

# 4. NEW necessary “central-exponent cliff” certificate for any counterexample

The exact deletion identity turns a hypothetical failure into a stronger certificate than the preceding global condition (2.8).

Let
\[
R_q(K):=\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]\downarrow0.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Because every downward exponential tilt satisfies
\[
P_{m,b}(d)\le \pi_d/\pi_0,
\tag{4.2}
\]
its \(q\)-tail obeys
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{m,b}}[\xi^q;\xi>K]
\le \pi_0^{-1}R_q(K).
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — cliff certificate

Suppose for some admissible \((m,b)\), threshold \(K\), and \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}[\xi^q;\xi>K]\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Then there exists a supported \(d>K\) with \(\mu_{m,b}(d)>0\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1}-J_{m,b}(d)\\
&\qquad\ge
\log\frac{\varepsilon\pi_0}{R_q(K)}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
In particular, as \(K\to\infty\), the required one-step central-exponent drop diverges.

### Proof
Put
\[
L(d):=\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}.
\]
The left side of (4.4) is
\[
\sum_{d>K}d^qP_{m,b}(d)L(d),
\]
whereas (4.3) bounds the same \(q\)-weighted base measure without \(L\) by \(\pi_0^{-1}R_q(K)\).  If every supported \(d>K\) had
\[
L(d)<\frac{\varepsilon\pi_0}{R_q(K)},
\]
the conditioned tail in (4.4) would be strictly smaller than \(\varepsilon\), a contradiction.  Hence some \(d\) has at least this likelihood ratio.  Taking logarithms and using (3.3) proves (4.5). \(\square\)

For an actual counterexample sequence from (1.1), the previously proved low/high windows allow the selected \(d_j\) to be taken in the remaining bridge, hence
\[
d_j=o(m_j),\qquad b_j+d_j-1=o(m_j).
\tag{4.6}
\]
Thus a failure would require an **unbounded downward cliff between two neighboring sample sizes whose canonical tilts both tend to the original critical law**:
\[
(m_j,b_j)\longrightarrow(m_j-1,b_j+d_j-1).
\]
This is materially stronger than merely requiring \(c_{m_j,b_j}\to\infty\), or requiring a large pointwise local ratio with the residual denominator discarded.

The right object for the next attack is therefore the local oscillation
\[
\Delta_{m,b}(d)
:=c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1},
\tag{4.7}
\]
paired with the positive Bregman penalty \(J_{m,b}(d)\), rather than the global envelope \(C_m\).

# 5. NEW: the previous global rate-coupling criterion is not forced by the hypotheses

The preceding report left open whether finite \(q\)-moment might automatically force
\[
C_m^qv^*((m/C_m)^{1/q})\to0.
\]
The answer is **no**.  The following construction is a methodological counterexample to that closure criterion, not a counterexample to Janson’s UI conjecture.

### Theorem 5.1 — a target law for which the global coupling criterion diverges

For every \(q\in(1,2)\), there exists a critical, nondegenerate, span-one integer law \(\pi\) such that
\[
\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\xi)=\infty,
\tag{5.1}
\]
but, with \(C_m\) from (2.4) and \(v^*\) from (2.7), there is a sequence \(m_j\to\infty\) for which
\[
\boxed{
C_{m_j}^q
v^*\!\left((m_j/C_{m_j})^{1/q}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
Hence (2.8) cannot be deduced from the target assumptions alone.

### Construction
Choose a summable positive sequence, for definiteness
\[
a_j:=2^{-j-6}.
\tag{5.3}
\]
We recursively choose rapidly increasing integers \(L_j\ge10\).  Once \(L_1,\ldots,L_{j-1}\) are chosen, put
\[
W_{j-1}:=1+\sum_{i<j}a_iL_i^{2-q}
\tag{5.4}
\]
and, for a candidate \(L_j\),
\[
G_j:=\frac{a_jL_j^{2-q}}{W_{j-1}},
\qquad
p_j:=\frac{a_j}{L_j^q},
\qquad
m_j:=\left\lfloor\frac1{2p_j}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.5}
\]
Because both \(2-q\) and \(q-1\) are positive, \(L_j\) can be made large enough that all of the following hold:
\[
G_j\ge a_j^{-2},
\tag{5.6}
\]
\[
L_j\ge100G_jL_{j-1}
\quad(L_0:=2),
\tag{5.7}
\]
\[
a_jL_j^{1-q}\le2^{-j-8},
\tag{5.8}
\]
and, simultaneously for every earlier \(i<j\),
\[
m_i p_j\le2^{-j}e^{-10G_i},
\tag{5.9}
\]
\[
m_i a_jL_j^{1-q}
\le2^{-j}L_i e^{-10G_i}.
\tag{5.10}
\]
Indeed, as \(L_j\to\infty\), \(G_j\to\infty\), while
\[
\frac{L_j}{G_jL_{j-1}}
=
\frac{W_{j-1}}{a_jL_{j-1}}L_j^{q-1}	o\infty,
\]
and both \(p_j\) and \(a_jL_j^{1-q}\) tend to zero.

After the entire sequence is fixed, define the heavy atoms by
\[
\pi_{L_j}=p_j.
\tag{5.11}
\]
Let
\[
H_0:=\sum_jp_j,\qquad
H_1:=\sum_jp_jL_j=\sum_j a_jL_j^{1-q}.
\]
By (5.8), both are small.  Complete the law with
\[
\pi_1:=\frac14,
\qquad
\pi_2:=\frac{3/4-H_1}{2},
\qquad
\pi_0:=1-\pi_1-\pi_2-H_0.
\tag{5.12}
\]
For the chosen bounds, \(\pi_0,\pi_2>0\), and
\[
\pi_1+2\pi_2+H_1=1.
\]
Thus the law is critical and nondegenerate; \(\pi_1>0\) makes its span one.

Moreover
\[
\sum_jp_jL_j^q=\sum_ja_j<\infty,
\tag{5.13}
\]
so \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\).  On the other hand,
\[
\sum_jp_jL_j^2
=
\sum_ja_jL_j^{2-q}
=
\sum_jG_jW_{j-1}
=\infty,
\tag{5.14}
\]
so the variance is infinite.

### Step 1: the canonical central atom at \((m_j,b=1)\) is exponentially small in \(G_j\)

Fix \(j\), abbreviate
\[
L=L_j,\quad p=p_j,\quad m=m_j,\quad G=G_j,\quad W=W_{j-1},
\]
and let \(P=P_{m,1}=P_{\theta}\) be the canonical tilt with mean \(1-1/m\).

The identity
\[
1-\mu(\theta)
=
\frac{\mathbb E[(\xi-1)(1-e^{-\theta\xi})]}{Z(\theta)}
=\frac1m
\tag{5.15}
\]
has only nonnegative contributions from \(\xi\ge2\).  Keeping only the atom at \(L\) gives
\[
mp(L-1)(1-e^{-\theta L})\le1.
\tag{5.16}
\]
Since \(mp\to1/2\),
\[
1-e^{-\theta L}\le\frac3L
\]
for large \(j\), and hence
\[
\theta L=O(L^{-1}),\qquad \theta=O(L^{-2}).
\tag{5.17}
\]
Also
\[
1-Z(\theta)\le\theta\mathbb E\xi=\theta,
\]
so the tilted probability \(\widehat p:=P(\xi=L)\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_j:=m\widehat p\longrightarrow\frac12.
\tag{5.18}
\]
In particular, eventually
\[
0.45\le\lambda_j\le0.55.
\tag{5.19}
\]

Split the tilted law into three categories: lower values \(<L\), the current atom \(L\), and future atoms \(>L\).  By the recursive constraints (5.9)–(5.10), summed over future indices and using \(P_\theta(d)\le\pi_d/\pi_0\),
\[
mP(\xi>L)\le Ce^{-10G},
\tag{5.20}
\]
\[
m\mathbb E_P[\xi;\xi>L]
\le CL e^{-10G}.
\tag{5.21}
\]
Let \(R\) be the tilted law conditioned on the lower category.  Its support is bounded by \(L_{j-1}\), and because the lower category has probability bounded below by the fixed atom at zero,
\[
\mathbb E_R\xi^2\le CW.
\tag{5.22}
\]

Condition on the event that no future atom occurs and on exactly \(K=k\) occurrences of \(L\).  The remaining \(m-k\) variables are iid \(R\).  Let \(\mu_R=\mathbb E_R\xi\), and denote by \(r_F\) and \(\mu_F\) the tilted future-category probability and conditional mean.  Since the total tilted mean is exactly \((m-1)/m\), the displacement required of the lower sum in order that the total equal \(m-1\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_k
&:=(m-1)-kL-(m-k)\mu_R\\
&=(m\widehat p-k)(L-\mu_R)
+mr_F(\mu_F-\mu_R).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.23}
\]
By (5.7), \(L-\mu_R\ge(1-o(1))L\); by (5.19), the distance from \(m\widehat p\) to every integer is at least \(0.45\); and (5.21) makes the final term negligible.  Consequently, uniformly in every integer \(k\), either the exact target is impossible or
\[
|\Delta_k|\ge L/3
\tag{5.24}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(j\).

Bernstein’s inequality for the bounded lower variables now gives
\[
\Pr_R\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{m-k}\xi_i=(m-1)-kL
\right)
\le
2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{
\frac{L^2}{mW},\frac{L}{L_{j-1}}
\right\}\right].
\tag{5.25}
\]
By (5.5) and (5.7),
\[
\frac{L^2}{mW}\ge2G,
\qquad
\frac{L}{L_{j-1}}\ge100G.
\tag{5.26}
\]
Thus the right side of (5.25) is at most \(2e^{-cG}\).  Summing over \(k\) costs nothing because their conditional probabilities add to at most one, while (5.20) handles the event of at least one future atom.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{
P_{m_j,1}^{\otimes m_j}(S_{m_j}=m_j-1)
\le3e^{-cG_j}.
}
\tag{5.27}
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
c_{m_j,1}\ge c_1G_j}
\tag{5.28}
\]
for a fixed \(c_1>0\) and all large \(j\).

### Step 2: the global coupling quantity diverges

By definition,
\[
C_{m_j}\ge c_{m_j,1}\ge c_1G_j.
\tag{5.29}
\]
Set
\[
B_j:=(m_j/C_{m_j})^{1/q}.
\]
Since \(m_j\le L_j^q/(2a_j)\),
\[
B_j
\le
L_j(2c_1a_jG_j)^{-1/q}.
\tag{5.30}
\]
Condition (5.6) implies \(a_jG_j\ge a_j^{-1}\to\infty\), so eventually
\[
B_j<L_j.
\tag{5.31}
\]
The current atom alone contributes
\[
V(L_j)\ge p_jL_j^2=a_jL_j^{2-q},
\]
and therefore
\[
v^*(B_j)\ge L_j^{q-2}V(L_j)\ge a_j.
\tag{5.32}
\]
Combining (5.29), (5.32), and \(G_j\ge a_j^{-2}\),
\[
\boxed{
C_{m_j}^qv^*(B_j)
\ge c_1^qG_j^qa_j
\ge c_1^q a_j^{1-2q}
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{5.33}
\]
This proves Theorem 5.1. \(\square\)

### What Theorem 5.1 does and does not say

It proves that the **global** central exponent \(C_m\) can be made huge by a sparse, half-expected-count atom while the total \(q\)-moment remains finite.  Therefore a proof strategy that treats \(C_m\) and \(v^*\) only through their separate global envelopes cannot settle the conjecture in full generality.

It does **not** prove a failure of uniform integrability.  No positive lower bound on the conditioned \(q\)-mass of the atom \(L_j\) has been established.  The construction is a certified failure mode of the sufficient criterion (2.8), not a counterexample to Janson’s conjecture.

The exact deletion identity from Section 3 explains the overpayment: a large \(c_{m,b}\) may be inherited almost entirely by the residual exponent \(c_{m-1,b+d-1}\), in which case it cancels from the actual one-coordinate likelihood ratio.  The relevant quantity is the **drop** \(c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1}\), not \(c_{m,b}\) itself.

## 6. Consequences for the proof strategy

The previous run’s global bridge estimate
\[
C_m^qv^*((m/C_m)^{1/q})
\]
is now known not to be a universal route.  The new exact formula gives a replacement target.

For tail degrees define
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal D_{m,b}(d)
:=c_{m,b}-c_{m-1,b+d-1}-J_{m,b}(d)
=\log\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]
The desired one-coordinate endpoint is equivalent to controlling the \(q\)-weighted exponential of this **local deletion defect** under the base tilted family:
\[
\sum_{d>K}d^qP_{m,b}(d)e^{\mathcal D_{m,b}(d)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Theorem 4.1 says that any true counterexample must force
\[
\mathcal D_{m_j,b_j}(d_j)
\ge
\log\frac{\varepsilon\pi_0}{R_q(K_j)}	o\infty
\tag{6.3}
\]
for some \(d_j\to\infty\) in the already isolated sublinear bridge.  Since \(J\ge0\), it in particular requires
\[
c_{m_j,b_j}-c_{m_j-1,b_j+d_j-1}\to\infty.
\tag{6.4}
\]
Thus the remaining conjecture can be attacked as a **no-deep-one-step-cliff theorem for canonical central atoms**, averaged with \(d^qP_{m,b}(d)\), rather than as a uniform local limit theorem.

The most promising next derivation is to couple two finite-core representations, one for \((m,b)\) and one for \((m-1,b+d-1)\), using the same finite-core/residual decomposition.  A shared-core coupling may cancel the large lacunary valley exhibited in Theorem 5.1 before the endpoint \(q\)-weight is applied.  The moving-tilt Palm identity from the preceding report supplies an independent route: after selecting \(d\), the macroscopic deficit cancels from the centered zero-surplus identity, so a uniform renewal-switching bound would also act on precisely the local defect (6.1).
