# 3. NEW: canonical exponential tilting removes the deficit exactly

For \(\theta\ge0\), define
\[
Z(\theta):=\mathbb Ee^{-\theta\xi},
\qquad
\pi_\theta(d):=\frac{\pi_de^{-\theta d}}{Z(\theta)},
\qquad
\mu(\theta):=\mathbb E_\theta\xi.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Because \(\xi\) is nondegenerate, \(\mu(\theta)\) is continuous and strictly decreasing, with
\[
\mu(0)=1,\qquad \mu(\theta)\downarrow0.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Hence, for every \(m>b\ge1\), there is a unique \(\theta_{m,b}>0\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{\mu(\theta_{m,b})=1-b/m.}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Write \(P_{m,b}:=\pi_{\theta_{m,b}}\).

### Proposition 3.1 — exact tilt invariance

For every admissible \((m,b)\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_\pi((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m)\mid S_m=m-b)
=
\mathcal L_{P_{m,b}}((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m)\mid S_m=m-b).
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Moreover \(m-b\) is exactly the mean total under \(P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}\).

### Proof
For every vector \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\) with \(\sum_i x_i=m-b\),
\[
\prod_{i=1}^mP_{m,b}(x_i)
=Z(\theta)^{-m}e^{-\theta(m-b)}\prod_{i=1}^m\pi_{x_i},
\]
and the prefactor is constant on the conditioning slice.  Thus the conditional laws are identical.  Equation (3.3) gives the exact mean statement. \(\square\)

If \(b=o(m)\), then \(\theta_{m,b}\to0\).  In fact
\[
P_{m,b}(d)\to\pi_d
\]
pointwise and
\[
\sum_d d^q|P_{m,b}(d)-\pi_d|\to0
\tag{3.5}
\]
by dominated convergence, because
\[
P_{m,b}(d)\le \pi_d/\pi_0.
\tag{3.6}
\]
Thus the sublinear-deficit family is automatically \(W_q\)-stable.  No truncation is needed in this one-sided lower-sum problem.

This is the first useful simplification of the run: **the parameter \(b\) can be moved completely into the reference law; the exact conditioning itself is always conditioning at the reference mean.**

# 4. NEW: a uniform central-atom exponent for the entire tilted family

Let \(\theta_*\) be the unique tilt with \(\mu(\theta_*)=1/2\).  Then every pair \(1\le b\le m/2\) has
\[
0\le\theta_{m,b}\le\theta_*.
\]
For such a pair define the tilted central exponent
\[
\boxed{
c_{m,b}:=-\log P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — uniform tilted finite-core denominator

Uniformly over all admissible \(1\le b\le m/2\),
\[
\boxed{
c_{m,b}=o(m^\beta),\qquad \beta=2/q-1.}
\tag{4.2}
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
C_m:=1+\sup_{\substack{1\le b\le m/2\\\Pr(S_m=m-b)>0}}c_{m,b},
\tag{4.3}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{C_m=o(m^\beta).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

### Proof
The proof is the finite-core argument from the recovered denominator theorem, with all constants made uniform over \(\theta\in[0,\theta_*]\).

Choose a finite support set \(F\subset\operatorname{supp}(\pi)\) containing 0 and finitely many positive values whose gcd is the span \(h\).  Let \(\nu\) be a fixed nondegenerate probability law supported on \(F\), of span \(h\).  Since \([0,\theta_*]\) and \(F\) are finite/compact, there is a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
\boxed{P_\theta=\varepsilon\nu+(1-\varepsilon)\rho_\theta}
\tag{4.5}
\]
for every \(0\le\theta\le\theta_*\).  Also
\[
\rho_\theta(d)\le C\pi_d+C\mathbf1_{\{d\in F\}},
\tag{4.6}
\]
so \(\{\rho_\theta\}\) is uniformly \(q\)-integrable.

We need a uniform Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund weak law.  If \(X_{\theta,i}\) are iid \(\rho_\theta\), then there exists a deterministic \(\eta_m\downarrow0\) such that, uniformly in \(\theta\in[0,\theta_*]\) and \(N\asymp m\),
\[
\Pr\left(
\left|\sum_{i=1}^N(X_{\theta,i}-\mathbb EX_{\theta,i})\right|
>\eta_m m^{1/q}
\right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4.7}
\]
For completeness, this follows by first truncating the centered variables at a fixed \(L\).  The bounded part is \(o(m^{1/q})\) uniformly by its \(O(\sqrt m)\) \(L^2\)-scale, because \(q<2\).  For the centered tail, the von Bahr–Esseen inequality bounds the normalized \(q\)-moment by a constant times
\[
\sup_{\theta}\mathbb E_{\rho_\theta}
[|X-\mathbb EX|^q;|X-\mathbb EX|>L],
\]
which tends to zero by (4.6).  First let \(m\to\infty\) at fixed \(L\), then \(L\to\infty\), and diagonalize to obtain \(\eta_m\downarrow0\).

Use the mixture (4.5) independently for the \(m\) coordinates, and let \(M\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,\varepsilon)\) count the finite-core coordinates.  Choose an integer \(m_0=\varepsilon m+O(1)\).  Then
\[
\Pr(M=m_0)\ge c m^{-1/2}.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Conditional on \(M=m_0\), write the total as
\[
A_{m_0}+B_{m-m_0},
\]
where \(A\) is a sum of iid \(\nu\)-variables and \(B\) a sum of iid \(\rho_\theta\)-variables.  Since the mixture mean is \(1-b/m\), on the uniform good event from (4.7) the exact core value required to make the total equal \(m-b\) differs from \(m_0\mathbb E\nu\) by at most
\[
O(1)+\eta_m m^{1/q}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

For the fixed finite-support span-\(h\) law \(\nu\), the standard finite-support local Cramér lower bound gives constants \(c,C,\delta>0\) such that for every sufficiently large \(s\) and every attainable lattice point \(x\) with \(|x-s\mathbb E\nu|\le\delta s\),
\[
\Pr(A_s=x)
\ge c s^{-1/2}
\exp\left[-C\frac{(x-s\mathbb E\nu)^2}{s}\right].
\tag{4.10}
\]
The required values in (4.9) lie in the common lattice and are central; because the finite core has span \(h\), they are attainable for all sufficiently large \(m\) whenever the original exact sum is admissible.

Combining (4.7)–(4.10) gives, uniformly over \(0\le\theta\le\theta_*\),
\[
P_\theta^{\otimes m}(S_m=m\mu(\theta))
\ge c m^{-1}
\exp[-C\eta_m^2m^{2/q-1}].
\tag{4.11}
\]
Therefore
\[
c_{m,b}\le C\log m+C\eta_m^2m^\beta=o(m^\beta),
\]
uniformly in \(b\le m/2\). \(\square\)

This extends the earlier fixed-law central-atom theorem to the **full moving tilt family**, including the exact sublinear deficits forced by the all-level reduction.

# 5. NEW: exact entropy budget after canonical tilting

Let
\[
Q_{m,b}:=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m)\mid S_m=m-b)
\]
(the same law whether the reference is \(\pi\) or \(P_{m,b}\)), and let \(\mu_{m,b}\) be its one-coordinate marginal.

Because conditioning restricts \(P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}\) to one event,
\[
D(Q_{m,b}\|P_{m,b}^{\otimes m})=c_{m,b}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Exchangeability gives the exact entropy decomposition
\[
\boxed{
c_{m,b}
=mD(\mu_{m,b}\|P_{m,b})
+D(Q_{m,b}\|\mu_{m,b}^{\otimes m}).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
In particular
\[
\boxed{
D(\mu_{m,b}\|P_{m,b})\le \frac{c_{m,b}}m.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
Both laws have **exactly the same mean**:
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}\xi
=
\mathbb E_{P_{m,b}}\xi
=1-b/m.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
By (4.2), uniformly over \(b\le m/2\),
\[
D(\mu_{m,b}\|P_{m,b})
=o(m^{\beta-1})
=o(m^{-2r/q}).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Thus Pinsker already gives
\[
\|\mu_{m,b}-P_{m,b}\|_{\rm TV}=o(m^{-r/q}),
\tag{5.6}
\]
but endpoint \(q\)-UI does not follow from total variation alone.

The base family itself has uniformly vanishing \(q\)-tails:
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{b\le m/2}
\mathbb E_{P_{m,b}}[\xi^q;\xi>K]
\le \pi_0^{-1}\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]
Consequently, a counterexample is now forced into a very specific phenomenon: an exact-mean lattice slice must create a non-UI \(q\)-tail from a \(W_q\)-stable reference family while spending only \(o(m^\beta)\) total entropy and preserving the one-coordinate mean exactly.

# 6. NEW: the entropy-scale low window holds uniformly for every deficit

Define the target-law truncated-variance modulus
\[
V(x):=\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^2;\xi\le x],
\qquad
v(x):=x^{q-2}V(x),
\qquad
v^*(K):=\sup_{x\ge K}v(x).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Finite \(q\)-moment and \(q<2\) imply
\[
\boxed{v^*(K)\downarrow0.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The one-sided entropy lemma from the verified report

`runs/2026-09-15/2026-09-15T19-42-00Z__report.md`

applies verbatim with \(P=P_{m,b}\) and \(Q=\mu_{m,b}\).  Because the means in (5.4) are equal, there is not even the \(1/n\) correction present in the original root application.  Moreover
\[
V_{P_{m,b}}(x)\le \pi_0^{-1}V(x).
\]
Repeating the layer-cake integration from that report gives, uniformly over admissible \(b\le m/2\), for \(1\le K<B\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mu_{m,b}}[\xi^q;K<\xi\le B]
\le
C\,R_q(K)
+C\sqrt{\frac{c_{m,b}}m\,B^qv^*(K)},
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
where
\[
R_q(K):=\mathbb E_\pi[\xi^q;\xi>K]\downarrow0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Put
\[
\boxed{
B_m:=\left(\frac{m}{C_m}\right)^{1/q},
\qquad C_m=1+\sup_{b\le m/2}c_{m,b}.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Then \(B_m\to\infty\), and (6.3) yields
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}
\sup_{1\le b\le m/2}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;K<\xi_1\le B_m\mid S_m=m-b]
=0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
Since \(C_m=o(m^\beta)\),
\[
\boxed{
B_m\gg m^{(1-\beta)/q}=m^{2r/q^2}.
}
\tag{6.7}
\]
Thus the low-window endpoint previously proved only around the root is now available **uniformly for all exact deficits \(b\le m/2\)** after canonical tilting.

# 7. NEW: a uniform high window from the same tilted denominator

The tilted family has a uniform one-sided lower-deviation bound.  Because \(P_\theta\) for \(0\le\theta\le\theta_*\) has uniformly bounded \(q\)-moment and means in \([1/2,1]\), there are constants \(c,c_0>0\) such that, with \(p=q/(q-1)\),
\[
\Pr_\theta(S_N-N\mu(\theta)\le-\ell)
\le
\exp\left[-c\ell^pN^{-(p-1)}\right]
\tag{7.1}
\]
for \(0\le\ell\le c_0N\), uniformly in \(\theta\); for \(\ell\ge c_0N\) there is a uniform \(e^{-cN}\) bound.  This follows from the uniform small-\(t\) estimate
\[
\log\mathbb E_\theta e^{-t(\xi-\mu(\theta))}\le Ct^q
\]
and Chernoff optimization; no variance assumption is used.

The exact conditioned marginal ratio is
\[
\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}
=
\frac{
P_{m,b}^{\otimes(m-1)}(S_{m-1}=m-b-d)
}{
P_{m,b}^{\otimes m}(S_m=m-b)
}.
\tag{7.2}
\]
The numerator is a lower deviation of size
\[
d-\mu(\theta_{m,b}).
\]
Using (7.1) and the denominator exponent \(c_{m,b}\), for \(d\le c_0m\),
\[
\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}
\le
\exp\left[
c_{m,b}-c\frac{d^p}{m^{p-1}}
\right]
\tag{7.3}
\]
after changing constants for bounded \(d\).  Linear \(d\) is even easier.

Choose a sufficiently large fixed \(A\) and define
\[
\boxed{
D_m:=A\,m^{1/q}C_m^{(q-1)/q}.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]
Then (7.3), (4.4), and the linear-deviation bound give, for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{1\le b\le m/2}\sup_{d\ge D_m}
\frac{\mu_{m,b}(d)}{P_{m,b}(d)}\le C.
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{1\le b\le m/2}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1\ge D_m\mid S_m=m-b]
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]
Indeed, use (5.7) and \(D_m\to\infty\).

The two deterministic scales satisfy the exact relation
\[
\boxed{D_m=A\,C_mB_m.}
\tag{7.7}
\]
Also \(C_m=o(m^\beta)\) implies \(D_m=o(m)\); its worst-case polynomial exponent is the previously encountered
\[
E_r=\frac{1+2r-r^2}{(1+r)^2}.
\]

Thus the entire sublinear-deficit endpoint is now trapped in one explicit annulus:
\[
\boxed{
B_m\lesssim \xi_1\lesssim C_mB_m.
}
\tag{7.8}
\]
Everything below is uniformly UI by entropy; everything above is uniformly UI by one-sided Cramér suppression.

# 8. NEW: a precise coupling certificate for every possible counterexample

The same entropy estimate (6.3) can be applied to the bridge itself, with lower cutoff \(B_m\) and upper cutoff \(D_m=A C_mB_m\).  Since
\[
D_m^q=A^q C_m^q B_m^q=A^q m C_m^{q-1},
\]
we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{b\le m/2}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;B_m<\xi_1\le D_m\mid S_m=m-b]\\
&\qquad\le
C R_q(B_m)
+C\sqrt{C_m^qv^*(B_m)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Therefore:

### Theorem 8.1 — rate-coupling criterion

If
\[
\boxed{
C_m^qv^*(B_m)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad B_m=(m/C_m)^{1/q},
}
\tag{8.2}
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}
\sup_{\substack{m>b\ge1\\b\le m/2}}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=m-b]=0,
}
\tag{8.3}
\]
and, by the exact forest reduction in the preceding verified checkpoint,
\[
\boxed{
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r\}_n\text{ is UI for every fixed }k.
}
\tag{8.4}
\]

### Proof
Use (6.6) below \(B_m\), (8.1) on \((B_m,D_m]\), and (7.6) above \(D_m\).  Equation (8.2) and \(R_q(B_m)\to0\) make the bridge vanish.  The previous forest block theorem then transfers the conditioned-coordinate endpoint to every fixed tree level. \(\square\)

Hence **any counterexample must violate the explicit coupling condition**.  More precisely, along any counterexample sequence forced by (1.1), after passing to a subsequence,
\[
\boxed{
C_{m_j}^q
v^*\!\left((m_j/C_{m_j})^{1/q}\right)
\not\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{8.5}
\]
This is a substantially sharper lacunary certificate than “the local ratio may spike.”  A bad law must synchronize:

1. an unusually small exact central atom for a **mean-matched exponential tilt**, quantified by \(C_m\); and
2. sufficiently slow decay of the truncated-variance modulus \(v^*\) precisely at the entropy scale \((m/C_m)^{1/q}\).

Finite \(q\)-moment gives \(v^*(x)\to0\), but it allows arbitrarily slow decay, so (8.2) is not automatic.  This is the exact remaining rate obstruction exposed in this run.

# 9. NEW exact zero-surplus identity under the moving tilt

The earlier degree-Palm switching argument also becomes cleaner after canonical tilting.

Fix \((m,b)\), put \(\alpha=b/m\), \(\theta=\theta_{m,b}\), and condition one selected coordinate to equal \(d\).  The remaining \(N=m-1\) coordinates are iid \(P_{m,b}\) before the residual exact conditioning
\[
S_N=m-b-d.
\tag{9.1}
\]
Let
\[
Y=(\xi-1)_+,
\qquad \pi_{\theta,0}=P_{m,b}(0).
\]
For every residual configuration satisfying (9.1),
\[
\boxed{
N_0=b+d-1+\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i.
}
\tag{9.2}
\]
Because the tilted mean is \(1-\alpha\),
\[
\mathbb E_\theta Y=\pi_{\theta,0}-\alpha.
\tag{9.3}
\]
Subtracting the tilted expected zero count gives the exact cancellation
\[
\boxed{
N_0-N\pi_{\theta,0}
=d-1+\alpha
+\sum_{i=1}^N(Y_i-\mathbb E_\theta Y).
}
\tag{9.4}
\]
The deficit \(b\) has disappeared except for the bounded term \(\alpha\).  Thus a selected large degree \(d\) creates the same order-\(d\) zero surplus under **every** sublinear deficit as in the root case.

Moreover the family of \(Y\)-laws is uniformly \(q\)-integrable by (3.6), so the one-sided lower-deviation estimates used in the earlier renewal/Palm proof are uniform over \(b=o(m)\).  What is still missing for a complete Palm transplant is a fully quantified renewal-switching lower bound uniform in the moving tilt as \(\theta\downarrow0\).  This is now a sharply isolated technical task rather than a new probabilistic mechanism.

## 11. Failure analysis and next attack

The main failed shortcut remains the same in a sharper form.  Finite \(q\)-moment forces
\[
v^*(x)\to0,
\]
but gives no universal rate.  The tilted central exponent satisfies
\[
C_m=o(m^\beta),
\]
but can also diverge without a known universal rate.  The product in (8.2) is therefore not settled by separate little-\(o\) statements.

The highest-value next attack is to couple these two moduli **inside the finite-core proof of the tilted central atom**, rather than bounding them independently.  A successful self-consistent estimate of the form
\[
C_m^q
