## 2. Retained one-step forest setup

For integers \(m>b\ge1\), let \(F_b\) be a forest of \(b\) iid ordinary Galton–Watson trees conditioned to have total size \(m\). Let
\[
C=\text{the total number of children of the }b\text{ roots}.
\]
The preceding checkpoint proved
\[
\eta_{m,b}(c)
:=\Pr(C=c\mid |F_b|=m)
=\Pr(S_b=c)\frac{\nu_{m-b}^{*c}}{\nu_m^{*b}},
\tag{2.1}
\]
where \(S_s=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_s\) and \(\nu_t=\Pr(|T|=t)\).

Under the Kesten reference transition from a boundary of size \(b\),
\[
\kappa_b(c)=\frac cb\Pr(S_b=c),
\tag{2.2}
\]
and the exact likelihood ratio is
\[
L_{m,b}(c)
=\frac{\eta_{m,b}(c)}{\kappa_b(c)}
=\frac{m}{m-b}
\frac{\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c)}{\Pr(S_m=m-b)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The previous run isolated the sufficient uniform forest endpoint
\[
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}
[C^r;C^r>M(1+b^r)]
\le \varepsilon(M)(1+b^r),
\qquad \varepsilon(M)\downarrow0,
\tag{2.4}
\]
uniformly in admissible \(m>b\). Proving (2.4) would propagate UI inductively from level zero to every fixed level.

The new work below reduces (2.4) to a one-coordinate endpoint under an exact conditioned allocation.

# 3. NEW exact block-size-bias representation

For \(m>b\ge1\), define the exact conditioned allocation law
\[
Q_{m,b}
:=\mathcal L\big((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_m)\mid S_m=m-b\big).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Let
\[
X_b:=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_b.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proposition 3.1 — the forest first generation is a size-biased conditioned block sum

For every admissible \((m,b)\) and every \(c\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\eta_{m,b}(c)
=\frac{mc}{b(m-b)}\,Q_{m,b}(X_b=c).
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Equivalently, for every nonnegative function \(F\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}F(C)
=\frac{m}{b(m-b)}
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}\big[X_bF(X_b)\big].
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof
Under \(Q_{m,b}\), block independence before conditioning gives
\[
Q_{m,b}(X_b=c)
=\Pr(S_b=c)
\frac{\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c)}{\Pr(S_m=m-b)}.
\tag{3.5}
\]
Forest Dwass gives
\[
\nu_m^{*b}=\frac bm\Pr(S_m=m-b),
\qquad
\nu_{m-b}^{*c}=\frac c{m-b}\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c).
\tag{3.6}
\]
Insert (3.6) into (2.1) and compare with (3.5):
\[
\eta_{m,b}(c)
=\frac{mc}{b(m-b)}Q_{m,b}(X_b=c).
\]
Summing against \(F(c)\) proves (3.4). \(\square\)

A useful check is obtained by setting \(F\equiv1\):
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}X_b
=\frac{b(m-b)}m,
\tag{3.7}
\]
which also follows immediately from exchangeability because the conditioned total is \(m-b\).

For the endpoint exponent \(q=1+r\), (3.4) becomes
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}[C^r;C>T]
=\frac{m}{b(m-b)}
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}[X_b^q;X_b>T].
}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Thus the next-width fractional tail is exactly a \(q\)-moment tail of a block sum under central/lower exact conditioning.

# 4. NEW high-average block reduction to one conditioned coordinate

The block in (3.8) can be reduced to one coordinate without any independence assumption under \(Q_{m,b}\); exchangeability is enough.

### Lemma 4.1 — deterministic high-average inequality

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_b\ge0\), \(X=\sum_i x_i\), \(q>1\), and \(A>0\). On the event
\[
X>2Ab,
\tag{4.1}
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{
X^q
\le
2^q b^{q-1}
\sum_{i=1}^b x_i^q\mathbf1_{\{x_i>A\}}.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof
The contribution of coordinates at most \(A\) is at most \(Ab<X/2\). Therefore
\[
\sum_i x_i\mathbf1_{\{x_i>A\}}>X/2.
\]
Hence
\[
X^q
<2^q\left(\sum_i x_i\mathbf1_{\{x_i>A\}}\right)^q.
\]
Hölder's inequality gives
\[
\left(\sum_i y_i\right)^q
\le b^{q-1}\sum_i y_i^q,
\]
which yields (4.2). \(\square\)

Apply this under the exchangeable law \(Q_{m,b}\). From (4.2),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}[X_b^q;X_b>2Ab]
\le
2^q b^q
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>A].
\tag{4.3}
\]
Combining (3.8) and (4.3) yields the exact forest-to-coordinate tail estimate
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}[C^r;C>2Ab]
\le
2^q\frac{m}{m-b}b^r
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>A].
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This estimate spends exactly the available endpoint moment \(q=1+r\). There is no exponential-in-\(b\) Kesten factor.

# 5. NEW relative-jump endpoint is controlled by one conditioned-coordinate q-tail

Fix \(M\ge1\) and put
\[
A_M:=\frac12M^{1/r}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Since
\[
[M(1+b^r)]^{1/r}\ge M^{1/r}b=2A_Mb,
\tag{5.2}
\]
the relative-jump event from the preceding checkpoint obeys
\[
\{C^r>M(1+b^r)\}\subseteq\{C>2A_Mb\}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

If \(b\le m/2\), then \(m/(m-b)\le2\), and (4.4) gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}[C^r;C^r>M(1+b^r)]
}{1+b^r}
\le
2^{q+1}
\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}
[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>\tfrac12M^{1/r}].
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

If \(b>m/2\), the relative-jump event is empty. Indeed a forest of total size \(m\) with \(b\) roots has at most \(m-b<b\) children of the roots, so
\[
C\le m-b<b<[M(1+b^r)]^{1/r}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Therefore (5.4), together with the trivial zero case (5.5), proves:

### Theorem 5.1 — conditional-coordinate endpoint criterion

If
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}
\sup_{\substack{m>b\ge1\\m-b\text{ admissible}}}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=m-b]
=0,
}
\tag{5.6}
\]
then the uniform forest endpoint (2.4) holds, and consequently
\[
\boxed{
\{\ell_k(T_n)^r\}_n\text{ is UI for every fixed }k.
}
\tag{5.7}
\]

Criterion (5.6) is stronger than necessary, but the reduction is exact and reveals that the higher-level problem introduces no new many-root endpoint moment: the entire first-failure forest tail is controlled by the endpoint \(q\)-tail of a **single iid offspring coordinate under an exact lower conditioning**.

The root problem is the special case \(b=1\).

# 6. NEW linear-deficit regimes are harmless by exponential tilting

Criterion (5.6) need not be attacked for all \(b/m\). Deficits that are a fixed positive fraction of the sample size are uniformly harmless.

### Proposition 6.1 — uniform q-UI for fixed fractional deficits

For every fixed \(\delta\in(0,1/2]\),
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{K\to\infty}
\sup_{\substack{m>b\ge1\\
\delta\le b/m\le1/2\\
\Pr(S_m=m-b)>0}}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=m-b]
=0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof
For \(\theta>0\), define the exponentially tilted offspring law
\[
\pi_\theta(d)
=\frac{\pi_d e^{-\theta d}}{Z(\theta)},
\qquad
Z(\theta):=\mathbb Ee^{-\theta\xi}.
\tag{6.2}
\]
Its mean
\[
\mu(\theta):=\mathbb E_\theta\xi
\tag{6.3}
\]
is continuous and strictly decreasing from \(1\) at \(\theta=0\) to \(0\) as \(\theta\to\infty\). Strict decrease follows from
\[
\mu'(\theta)=-\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\xi)<0
\]
for the nondegenerate law.

For \(\alpha=b/m\in[\delta,1/2]\), choose the unique \(\theta=\theta(\alpha)>0\) such that
\[
\mu(\theta)=1-\alpha.
\tag{6.4}
\]
As \(\alpha\) ranges over \([\delta,1/2]\), \(\theta\) ranges over a compact interval
\[
0<\theta_-\le\theta\le\theta_+<\infty.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Conditioning on a fixed sum is invariant under exponential tilting. Thus, writing \(s=m-b=m\mu(\theta)\),
\[
\Pr(\xi_1=d\mid S_m=s)
=
\pi_\theta(d)
\frac{\Pr_\theta(S_{m-1}=s-d)}{\Pr_\theta(S_m=s)}.
\tag{6.6}
\]

Because \(\theta\) remains in the compact positive interval (6.5), the tilted family has uniformly bounded exponential moments, uniformly positive variance, and the same fixed lattice span \(h\). A standard Fourier proof on this compact family gives constants \(c,C>0\), independent of \(m\), \(\alpha\), and the admissible lattice point, such that for all sufficiently large \(m\),
\[
\Pr_\theta(S_m=s)\ge c m^{-1/2},
\qquad
\sup_x\Pr_\theta(S_{m-1}=x)\le C m^{-1/2}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
For completeness, the uniformity uses only: a fixed finite support subset of full span to obtain a uniform characteristic-function gap away from lattice frequencies; a uniform second-order expansion near zero since the tilted variance is bounded above and below on (6.5); and uniform third moments, available because \(\theta\ge\theta_->0\). Thus no infinite-variance local limit theorem is being assumed.

Equations (6.6)–(6.7) give
\[
\Pr(\xi_1=d\mid S_m=s)\le C'\pi_\theta(d).
\tag{6.8}
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=s]
\le C'\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_-,\theta_+]}
\mathbb E_\theta[\xi^q;\xi>K].
\tag{6.9}
\]
The right side tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\), uniformly in \(\theta\), because
\[
\pi_\theta(d)\le C''\pi_d e^{-\theta_-d}.
\tag{6.10}
\]
Finitely many small \(m\) cause no problem because the conditioned coordinate is bounded by the fixed total \(m-b\). This proves (6.1). \(\square\)

# 7. NEW localization of any counterexample: only sublinear deficits can remain

The combination of Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 6.1 sharply localizes the unresolved endpoint.

Define
\[
T_{m,b}(K)
:=\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=m-b].
\tag{7.1}
\]

Suppose the full fixed-level tree theorem fails. By the first-failing-level certificate from the preceding checkpoint, there is a first generation at which a positive amount of fractional tail mass is created by conditioned-forest relative jumps. If
\[
\sup_{m>b}T_{m,b}(K)\to0,
\]
then (5.4) would rule that out. Therefore failure forces a sequence
\[
K_\nu\to\infty,
\qquad
m_\nu>b_\nu\ge1,
\tag{7.2}
\]
of admissible exact-condition states for which
\[
T_{m_\nu,b_\nu}(K_\nu)
\not\to0.
\tag{7.3}
\]
Necessarily \(m_\nu\to\infty\), because otherwise \(\xi_1\le m_\nu-b_\nu\) under the conditioning and the tail would eventually be zero.

By Proposition 6.1, no subsequence can have
\[
\liminf_\nu b_\nu/m_\nu>0.
\]
After passing to a subsequence,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{b_\nu}{m_\nu}\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

Thus:

### Counterexample transfer certificate

If the original every-fixed-level theorem is false, then there exists a **sublinear-deficit conditional-allocation endpoint failure**:
\[
\boxed{
\exists\ m_\nu\to\infty,
\quad 1\le b_\nu=o(m_\nu),
\quad K_\nu\to\infty,
}
\tag{7.5}
\]
with \(\Pr(S_{m_\nu}=m_\nu-b_\nu)>0\) and
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{\nu\to\infty}
\mathbb E[
\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K_\nu
\mid S_{m_\nu}=m_\nu-b_\nu]
>0.
}
\tag{7.6}
\]

Conversely, proving that no sequence (7.5)–(7.6) exists is sufficient, through (5.4) and the previous first-failure induction, to prove UI at **every fixed level**.

This is the strongest reduction of the all-level endpoint obtained so far. The remaining object is no longer a random forest, a Kesten prefix, or a random convolution count. It is one coordinate of an iid sample under an exact sum whose deficit is sublinear in the sample size.

The root conjecture itself is the special sequence \(b_\nu\equiv1\). Hence (7.5)–(7.6) does not solve the problem; it shows that all possible higher-level failures are of the same analytic type as the root endpoint.

# 8. Relation to the existing sublinear-deficit machinery

Earlier verified runs already built tools precisely in the regime (7.5):

1. **Canonical \(W_q\)-recentering for every sublinear residual deficit.** The conditioned sum can be recentered by the inaccessible upper-tail mean without changing the exact target, so a naive one-missing-large-atom construction does not work.
2. **Finite-core/Bernoulli additive smoothing.** A fixed support core gives uniform additive adjacent-lattice smoothing, but additive error is not enough when the central local atom is very small.
3. **Finite-\(q\) denominator control.** At the root, \(-\log\Pr(S_n=n-1)=o(n^{(2-q)/q})\); analogous sublinear-deficit constructions give subexponential denominators but not the multiplicative local-ratio control required at the endpoint.
4. **Renewal/Palm switching.** For \(b=1\), this yields sharp size-biased root control over large low-degree ranges and a complementary bound for sufficiently large moving shifts. The unresolved root window is genuinely mesoscopic.
5. **Missing-upper-tail-mean obstruction.** If a proposed lacunary counterexample makes one large atom recenter the omitted future mean, then the normalized contribution is proportional to \(d^{q-1}\mathbb E[\xi;\xi>d]\to0\), so that mechanism cannot maintain an endpoint defect.

The new reduction shows exactly where these tools should be generalized: not to the whole forest likelihood, but to the single-coordinate conditional law
\[
\mathcal L(\xi_1\mid S_m=m-b),
\qquad b=o(m).
\tag{8.1}
\]

In particular, a generalized renewal-switching identity can be written under \(Q_{m,b}\) with the same equal-sum cancellation used in the root case; the remaining difficulty is to make its lower switch functional uniform over arbitrary sublinear \(b\), especially on the natural finite-\(q\) fluctuation scale.

# 9. Why the reduction is moment-sharp

It is important that (4.4) did not use \(\mathbb E\xi^2\). Under the Kesten transition,
\[
C\stackrel d=\widehat\xi+\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_{b-1},
\]
so the natural reference endpoint uses
\[
\mathbb E\widehat\xi^r=\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}=\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty.
\]
In the conditioned transition, the block-size-bias identity increases the moment of \(X_b\) from \(r\) to exactly \(q=r+1\). Lemma 4.1 then converts a high block average into one coordinate's \(q\)-tail using only convexity/Hölder. No moment above \(q\) appears.

This is why the reduction is compatible with the precise hypothesis of Janson's conjecture rather than smuggling in finite variance or a \((q+\varepsilon)\)-moment.

# 10. Failed shortcuts / cautions recorded this run

1. **Do not replace Proposition 6.1 by an infinite-variance LLT.** The proof only uses local Fourier bounds after an exponential tilt bounded away from zero. That tilted family has uniform exponential moments, so it is a finite-moment compact-family calculation. The unresolved \(b=o(m)\) regime has \(\theta\downarrow0\), where this uniform argument genuinely degenerates.
2. **A bound on \(\mathbb E_{Q_{m,b}}\xi_1\) is insufficient.** Exchangeability gives the exact first moment \(1-b/m\), but endpoint \(q\)-UI does not follow from bounded first moments.
3. **Generic Kesten convolution bounds remain too expensive.** They are exponential in a random count and do not exploit the exact block-size-bias cancellation (3.3).
4. **The criterion (5.6) is stronger than the original theorem.** It is used as a sufficient analytic endpoint and a counterexample-localization device; no equivalence is claimed for arbitrary conditioned states that never arise with appreciable prefix mass.

# 12. Next attack

The highest-value next step is now a **generalized renewal/Palm switching theorem for \(Q_{m,b}\) with \(b=o(m)\)**.

The equal-sum factorial switching identity used at the root survives verbatim under conditioning on \(S_m=m-b\): when a degree-\(d\) coordinate is switched against an arbitrary block whose offspring sum is also \(d\), the same residual local probability cancels. The exact deterministic degree-count identity becomes
\[
N_0=b+\sum_{i=1}^m(\xi_i-1)_+,
\tag{12.1}
\]
and, after selecting a degree \(d\), the residual Palm deficit is \(b+d-1\). Thus the previous root zero/excess Palm method should admit a two-parameter form in \((b,d)\).

The target is to prove, uniformly for \(b=o(m)\), a weighted degree-count estimate strong enough to imply
\[
\lim_{K\to\infty}
\sup_{b=o(m)}
\mathbb E[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K\mid S_m=m-b]=0.
\tag{12.2}
\]
One should split according to the comparison between \(d\), \(b\), and the natural finite-\(q\) fluctuation scale, using the exact upward zero shift \(b+d\) under the degree Palm law for large \(d\), and self-normalized renewal switching for smaller \(d\).

On the adversarial side, any genuine counterexample must now produce a sublinear-deficit exact-conditioning sequence (7.5)–(7.6). It is no longer enough to exhibit a bad random forest transition: the block-size-bias theorem transfers that failure to a single conditioned offspring coordinate.
