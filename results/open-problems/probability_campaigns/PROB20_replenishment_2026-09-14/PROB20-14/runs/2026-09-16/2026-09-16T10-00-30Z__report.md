## 2. Retained fixed-level Kesten likelihood ratio

Let \(T\) be the ordinary unconditioned critical Galton–Watson tree. At level \(j\), write
\[
A_j:=N_j:=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}Z_i,\qquad B_j:=Z_j.
\]
Let \(p_j\) be the ordinary law of the truncated prefix through level \(j\). Criticality gives \(\mathbb E Z_j=1\), so the Kesten-prefix law is
\[
\widehat p_j(dt)=B_j(t)\,p_j(dt)
\tag{2.1}
\]
on prefixes with \(B_j\ge1\).

For the conditioned tree \(T_n\), the branching property and forest Dwass identity give, for a prefix with \((A_j,B_j)=(a,b)\),
\[
q_{n,j}(dt)
=p_j(dt)\frac{\nu_{n-a}^{*b}}{\nu_n},
\qquad
\nu_m:=\Pr(|T|=m),
\tag{2.2}
\]
and therefore, relative to the Kesten prefix,
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,j}(t)
:=\frac{dq_{n,j}}{d\widehat p_j}(t)
=\frac{n}{n-a}
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-a}=n-a-b)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]
The preceding checkpoint showed ordinary Scheffé convergence
\[
R_{n,j}\to1\quad\text{in }L^1(\widehat p_j)
\tag{2.4}
\]
for every fixed \(j\), after the negligible extinction component is separated, while the desired fractional endpoint is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\widehat p_j}\big[B_j^r|R_{n,j}-1|\big]\to0.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Also
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat p_j}B_j^r=\mathbb E Z_j^{1+r}=\mathbb E Z_j^q<\infty.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The new work below factors (2.3) into **one-generation conditioned-forest likelihoods** and identifies exactly what a first failing level would have to do.

# 3. NEW exact one-step conditioned-forest factorization

Fix a level \(j\ge1\). Suppose a prefix through level \(j-1\) has
\[
A_{j-1}=a,\qquad B_{j-1}=b\ge1,
\]
and put the residual forest size
\[
m:=n-a.
\tag{3.1}
\]
Thus there are \(b\) boundary vertices at level \(j-1\), and the part of \(T_n\) rooted at these vertices is a forest of \(b\) independent Galton–Watson trees conditioned to have total size \(m\).

Let
\[
C:=B_j
\]
be the total number of children of these \(b\) roots.

### Proposition 3.1 — conditioned-forest first-generation law

For \(m>b\),
\[
\boxed{
\eta_{m,b}(c)
:=\Pr(C=c\mid |F_b|=m)
=\Pr(S_b=c)\frac{\nu_{m-b}^{*c}}{\nu_m^{*b}},
\qquad c\ge1.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

**Proof.** First expose the \(b\) root offspring counts. Their sum is \(c\) with probability \(\Pr(S_b=c)\). The \(c\) child subtrees are independent ordinary Galton–Watson trees, and after the \(b\) roots are removed their total size must be \(m-b\), which has probability \(\nu_{m-b}^{*c}\). Divide by \(\nu_m^{*b}\), the probability that the original \(b\)-root forest has total size \(m\). \(\square\)

Under the Kesten tree, conditional on \(B_{j-1}=b\), exactly one of the \(b\) level-\((j-1)\) vertices is the spine vertex, uniformly chosen. Its offspring law is size-biased,
\[
\widehat\pi_d=d\pi_d,
\]
while the other \(b-1\) offspring variables have law \(\pi\). Hence the Kesten transition law is
\[
\boxed{
\kappa_b(c)
=\frac cb\Pr(S_b=c),
\qquad c\ge1.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Indeed \(\sum_c c\Pr(S_b=c)=\mathbb ES_b=b\).

Using the forest Dwass identities
\[
\nu_m^{*b}=\frac bm\Pr(S_m=m-b),
\qquad
\nu_{m-b}^{*c}=\frac c{m-b}\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c),
\tag{3.4}
\]
we obtain the exact one-step likelihood ratio
\[
\boxed{
L_{m,b}(c)
:=\frac{\eta_{m,b}(c)}{\kappa_b(c)}
=\frac{m}{m-b}
\frac{\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c)}{\Pr(S_m=m-b)}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]
There is no remaining size-bias factor: it has canceled exactly.

Now compare (3.5) with (2.3). Since
\[
A_j=A_{j-1}+B_{j-1}=a+b,
\]
we have
\[
\frac{R_{n,j}}{R_{n,j-1}}
=\frac{m}{m-b}
\frac{\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-C)}{\Pr(S_m=m-b)}
=L_{m,b}(C)
\tag{3.6}
\]
whenever \(m>b\). Therefore, up to the terminal event on which the residual forest consists only of its roots,
\[
\boxed{
R_{n,k}
=\prod_{j=1}^k L_{m_j,B_{j-1}}(B_j),
\qquad
m_j:=n-A_{j-1}.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]
For \(j=1\), \(A_0=0\), \(B_0=1\), and (3.5) is exactly the familiar root likelihood ratio.

**Interpretation.** The every-fixed-level problem is a finite product of the same object: the first-generation likelihood of a critical \(b\)-root Galton–Watson forest conditioned on its total size. Higher levels do not introduce a fundamentally new combinatorial factor; they introduce a **random predecessor root count \(b\)** and a random residual size \(m\).

# 4. NEW Kesten density is a finite-level supermartingale; transition TV defects vanish on average

Let \(\mathcal F_j\) be the prefix sigma-field through level \(j\) under the Kesten tree \(T^*\). Extend \(R_{n,j}\) by zero after the conditioned tree would already have terminated.

### Proposition 4.1 — density supermartingale

For every fixed \(n\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E^*[R_{n,j}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}]
\le R_{n,j-1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
On a prefix state with residual size \(m>b\), equality holds.

**Proof.** On \(m>b\), (3.6) and the fact that \(L_{m,b}=d\eta_{m,b}/d\kappa_b\) give
\[
\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}L_{m,b}=1.
\]
If \(m=b\), the conditioned residual forest consists of the \(b\) roots and has next width zero, whereas the Kesten transition has positive next width almost surely; thus all positive-prefix density is killed at the next step, giving the inequality. \(\square\)

In particular
\[
\mathbb E^*R_{n,j}=\Pr(H(T_n)\ge j),
\tag{4.2}
\]
which tends to one for every fixed \(j\) by local convergence to the infinite Kesten tree.

The factorization gives more than ordinary prefix Scheffé convergence. On interior states \(m>b\), define the conditional total-variation defect
\[
\delta_{m,b}
:=\frac12\sum_{c\ge1}\kappa_b(c)|L_{m,b}(c)-1|
=d_{\rm TV}(\eta_{m,b},\kappa_b).
\tag{4.3}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\,\mathbb E^*\!\left[R_{n,j-1}\delta_{m_j,B_{j-1}};\ m_j>B_{j-1}\right]
&=\mathbb E^*\!\left[|R_{n,j}-R_{n,j-1}|;\ m_j>B_{j-1}\right]\\
&\le \|R_{n,j}-1\|_{L^1(\widehat p_j)}
+\|R_{n,j-1}-1\|_{L^1(\widehat p_{j-1})}
+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]
The \(o(1)\) is only the terminal-prefix mass, which tends to zero for fixed \(j\). By ordinary Scheffé convergence at both levels,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E^*\!\left[R_{n,j-1}\delta_{m_j,B_{j-1}}ight]
\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]
Equivalently: **under the actual conditioned distribution of the previous prefix, the next-generation conditioned-forest transition converges to the Kesten transition in average total variation.**

This is stronger and more local than merely saying that the complete depth-\(j\) prefix converges in total variation.

# 5. NEW uniform fractional-tail lemma for the Kesten one-step transition

The reference transition \(\kappa_b\) has an especially favorable fractional structure.

Let \(\widehat\xi\) have the size-biased law
\[
\Pr(\widehat\xi=d)=d\pi_d.
\]
Since \(q=1+r\),
\[
\mathbb E\widehat\xi^r=\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}=\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Conditional on \(B_{j-1}=b\) under the Kesten tree,
\[
\boxed{
C\ \stackrel d=\ \widehat\xi+\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_{b-1},
}
\tag{5.2}
\]
with all variables on the right independent.

Because \(0<r<1\), subadditivity and Jensen give
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}C^r
\le \mathbb E\widehat\xi^r+(b-1)^r
\le M_q+b^r,
\qquad M_q:=\mathbb E\xi^q.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]
The more useful fact is a uniform endpoint tail estimate after normalizing by the natural predecessor weight.

### Lemma 5.1 — normalized Kesten-transition UI

Put
\[
W_b:=\frac{C^r}{1+b^r}.
\]
Then \(\{W_b:b\ge1\}\) is uniformly integrable under \(\kappa_b\). Quantitatively, for every \(M\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{b\ge1}
\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}[W_b;W_b>2M]
\le
\mathbb E[\widehat\xi^r;\widehat\xi^r>M]
+M^{-(1-r)/r}.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

**Proof.** Write
\[
X:=\widehat\xi,\qquad Y:=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_{b-1}.
\]
Then \(C^r\le X^r+Y^r\). If \(W_b>2M\), at least one of
\[
\frac{X^r}{1+b^r}>M,
\qquad
\frac{Y^r}{1+b^r}>M
\]
holds. The first contribution is bounded by
\[
\mathbb E[X^r;X^r>M].
\]
For the second, define
\[
T_b:=[M(1+b^r)]^{1/r}.
\]
Since \(r-1<0\), on \(\{Y>T_b\}\),
\[
Y^r\le T_b^{r-1}Y.
\]
As \(\mathbb EY=b-1\),
\[
\frac{\mathbb E[Y^r;Y>T_b]}{1+b^r}
\le
M^{-(1-r)/r}
(b-1)(1+b^r)^{-1/r}
\le M^{-(1-r)/r}.
\]
This proves (5.4). \(\square\)

This lemma is specific to the fractional regime \(0<r<1\): the ordinary \(b-1\) critical offspring variables are controlled by their **first moment**, while only the single spine variable spends the extra \(r\)-moment, exactly matching \(\mathbb E\xi^{1+r}<\infty\).

# 6. NEW induction identity: a first failing level must fail inside one conditioned-forest transition

Define the weighted prefix Scheffé error
\[
E_{n,j}^{(r)}
:=\mathbb E^*\!\left[B_j^r|R_{n,j}-1|\right].
\tag{6.1}
\]
For an interior transition state \((m,b)\), define the one-step weighted likelihood defect
\[
\Delta_r(m,b)
:=\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}
\left[C^r|L_{m,b}(C)-1|\right].
\tag{6.2}
\]

From
\[
R_{n,j}-1
=(R_{n,j-1}-1)+R_{n,j-1}(L_{m,b}(C)-1)
\tag{6.3}
\]
and (5.3), conditioning on \(\mathcal F_{j-1}\) gives
\[
\boxed{
E_{n,j}^{(r)}
\le
M_q\|R_{n,j-1}-1\|_1
+E_{n,j-1}^{(r)}
+\mathbb E^*\!\left[R_{n,j-1}\Delta_r(m_j,B_{j-1})\right]
+o(1),
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
where the \(o(1)\) is the fixed-level terminal-prefix contribution.

Conversely, by the reverse triangle inequality applied to (6.3), if both \(E_{n,j-1}^{(r)}\to0\) and \(E_{n,j}^{(r)}\to0\), then
\[
\mathbb E^*\!\left[R_{n,j-1}\Delta_r(m_j,B_{j-1})\right]\to0.
\tag{6.5}
\]
Thus, assuming the already-known ordinary Scheffé convergence,

\[
\boxed{
\text{if level }j-1\text{ has the weighted endpoint but level }j\text{ does not,}
}
\]
then necessarily
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_n
\mathbb E^*\!\left[R_{n,j-1}\Delta_r(m_j,B_{j-1})\right]>0.
}
\tag{6.6}
\]

In words: **at the first failing level, the obstruction is entirely a one-generation conditioned-forest likelihood defect.** It cannot be blamed on accumulated harmless distortions from earlier levels.

For \(j=1\), \(b=1\), this is exactly the unresolved root endpoint. For \(j>1\), the new content is that the only additional ingredient is a random root count \(b=B_{j-1}\) and residual size \(m=n-A_{j-1}\).

# 7. NEW endpoint-spike certificate: failure requires a supermultiplicative one-generation width jump

The average unweighted transition defect vanishes by (4.5). The normalized Kesten transition has uniformly integrable fractional tails by Lemma 5.1. Therefore a persistent defect in (6.6) cannot live in a bounded fractional-weight region.

There is also a direct formulation that does not mention likelihood ratios.

### Proposition 7.1 — first-failure relative-jump certificate

Suppose that for some fixed \(j\ge1\),
\[
\{B_{j-1}(T_n)^r\}_n
\]
is uniformly integrable but
\[
\{B_j(T_n)^r\}_n
\]
is not. Then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\mathbb E\left[
B_j(T_n)^r;
B_j(T_n)^r>M\bigl(1+B_{j-1}(T_n)^r\bigr)
\right]>0.
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

**Proof.** If (7.1) were zero, fix \(M\). On the complementary event
\[
B_j^r\le M(1+B_{j-1}^r).
\]
Hence for every \(K\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E[B_j^r;B_j^r>K,\ B_j^r\le M(1+B_{j-1}^r)]\\
&\qquad\le
M\,\mathbb E[(1+B_{j-1}^r);\ B_{j-1}^r>K/M-1],
\end{aligned}
\]
which tends to zero as \(K\to\infty\), uniformly in \(n\), by UI of \(B_{j-1}^r\). The relative-jump part can then be made uniformly small by taking \(M\to\infty\), proving UI of \(B_j^r\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

Thus if the full theorem is false, there is a **first failing generation**. At that generation the conditioning must create a child population whose fractional width is arbitrarily large compared with the natural predecessor scale \(1+B_{j-1}^r\).

Under the Kesten reference this mechanism is impossible: (5.4) says precisely that
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{b\ge1}
\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}
\left[
\frac{C^r}{1+b^r};
\frac{C^r}{1+b^r}>M
\right]
\to0.
}
\tag{7.2}
\]
Therefore a counterexample must create such jumps **through the exact-conditioning likelihood \(L_{m,b}\)**.

Combining (4.5), (5.4), and the predecessor UI gives a sharper likelihood version. At a first failing level, for arbitrarily large \(M\), a positive amount of the weighted defect in (6.6) must come from
\[
\boxed{
C^r>M(1+b^r).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]
The contribution with \(C^r\le M(1+b^r)\) vanishes: for bounded \(b\), this follows from the average TV defect (4.5), while the contribution of large \(b\) is killed by predecessor UI and the elementary bound \(\mathbb E_{\kappa_b}|L-1|\le2\). The reference part of the tail in (7.3) vanishes uniformly by (5.4). What can remain is only
\[
R_{n,j-1}\,
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}
[C^r;C^r>M(1+b^r)],
\tag{7.4}
\]
i.e. actual conditioned-forest endpoint mass on a supermultiplicative width jump.

This is a strictly narrower obstruction than the previous generic statement “weighted Scheffé may fail.”

## 8. Relation to the earlier balanced-mesoscopic obstruction

Earlier verified runs had already eliminated:

- catastrophic prefixes with \(A_j\gg B_j\) by an exponential branching-geometry estimate;
- sufficiently large forest counts by the exact Cramér transform;
- every fixed forest count by fixed-count local subexponentiality;
- direct F0058-type missing-tail-mean compensation at the finite-\(q\) scale;
- pure span/residue obstructions via a fixed finite support core;
- generic use of an exponential Kesten bound, because it spends more than the available finite-mean random-count budget.

The unresolved region was a balanced mesoscopic prefix with
\[
A_j=O_j(B_j),\qquad B_j=o(n).
\]
The new one-step factorization refines that region further. A first failure cannot be a gradual accumulation across levels. It must appear in a single conditioned forest transition
\[
(b,m)\longmapsto C
\]
with exact density (3.5), and the offending mass must satisfy the relative amplification (7.3).

The denominator in that transition is
\[
\Pr(S_m=m-b),
\]
while the numerator is
\[
\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-C).
\]
Thus the exact remaining local-probability pathology is now:

> a residual forest whose current root count is \(b\), but whose next width \(C\) is much larger than \(b\), is made disproportionately likely by a nearby central/local valley in \(\Pr(S_m=m-b)\), strongly enough to defeat the finite \(q\)-moment tail budget.

For \(b=1\) this is exactly the root problem. For higher levels it is the same local-ratio problem with a moving baseline deficit \(b\), not a new tree-combinatorics problem.

## 9. A clean sufficient forest endpoint that would close every fixed level

The new reduction suggests the following stronger one-step statement. It is **not proved** here, but its role is now exact.

If one could prove a function \(\varepsilon(M)\downarrow0\) such that for all admissible \(m>b\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}
\left[
C^r;\ C^r>M(1+b^r)
\right]
\le
\varepsilon(M)(1+b^r),
}
\tag{9.1}
\]
then UI would propagate inductively from \(B_0=1\) to every fixed level. Equation (9.1) includes the root endpoint as \(b=1\), so it is stronger than the original theorem, but it is a natural forest form of exactly the remaining obstruction.

A weaker averaged version is enough. At level \(j\), it suffices that
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_n
\mathbb E^*\left[
R_{n,j-1}
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m_j,B_{j-1}}}
\left[C^r;C^r>M(1+B_{j-1}^r)\right]
\right]=0.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Proposition 7.1 shows that (9.2), successively for \(j=1,\dots,k\), is essentially the missing endpoint itself, but (3.5) exposes the exact arithmetic object on which it must be proved.

The next useful attack should therefore be directed at (9.2), not at another global prefix estimate.

## 10. Failed shortcut recorded this run

A tempting generating-function shortcut was checked and rejected rather than promoted.

For the root-tail generating function
\[
R_K(z)=z\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_dT(z)^d,
\]
one has for \(d>K\)
\[
d^{1+r}\le C K^{r-1}d(d-1),
\]
so differentiating \(R_K\) and comparing with derivatives of \(zT(z)f'(T(z))\) produces a coefficient bound of the schematic form
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
\lesssim K^{r-1}\mathbb E D_n.
\]
This does **not** help: in the infinite-variance Kesten regime the conditioned root mean need not be uniformly bounded, and the inequality is essentially the deterministic estimate \(D^r\mathbf1_{D>K}\le K^{r-1}D\). No claim based on this route is retained.

This failed route is recorded to avoid recycling it as a putative renewal solution.

## 12. Next attack

The highest-value next step is to combine the exact transition likelihood
\[
L_{m,b}(c)
=\frac{m}{m-b}
\frac{\Pr(S_{m-b}=m-b-c)}{\Pr(S_m=m-b)}
\]
with the earlier Cramér/entropy budget **at a relative jump \(c\gg b\)**.

There are two promising subcases:

1. **Moderate denominator cost.** If the local surprise
   \[
   I_m(b):=-\log\Pr(S_m=m-b)
   \]
   is substantially smaller than the numerator's lower-deviation cost \((m-b)H(c/(m-b))\), then \(L_{m,b}(c)\) is exponentially suppressed and (7.4) is harmless.

2. **Large denominator cost.** If \(I_m(b)\) is itself large enough to finance \(c\gg b\), then the exact Kesten-prefix entropy/local-cost identity from the preceding checkpoint limits how much conditioned-prefix mass can occupy such states. The remaining task is to make this tradeoff **weighted by \(c^r\)** without taking an exponential-in-\(b\) supremum.

A successful averaged inequality of the form
\[
\mathbb E^*\left[
R_{n,j-1}
\mathbb E_{\eta_{m,b}}
[C^r;C^r>M(1+b^r)]
\right]
\le \varepsilon(M)
\]
with \(\varepsilon(M)\to0\) would close the first-failure corridor and, by induction, the full every-fixed-level theorem.
