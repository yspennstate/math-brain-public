## 2. Retained all-level forest reduction

Let
\[
X:=|T|,\qquad \nu_n:=\Pr(X=n),
\]
and under the ordinary unconditioned tree, for fixed \(k\), set
\[
B:=Z_k=\ell_k(T),\qquad A:=N_k:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}Z_j.
\]
The branching property gives
\[
X=A+\sum_{i=1}^{B}X_i,
\tag{2.1}
\]
where the descendant total progenies \(X_i\) are iid copies of \(X\), independent of the exposed prefix \((A,B)\).

Remove the lattice span by writing
\[
U:=\frac{X-1}{h},\qquad u_M:=\Pr(U=M)=\nu_{1+hM}.
\tag{2.2}
\]
For each feasible ordinary prefix pair \((A,B)=(a,b)\), define
\[
C:=\frac{A+B-1}{h}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
For \(n=1+hM\), (2.1) yields
\[
A+\sum_{i=1}^{B}X_i=n
\iff
\sum_{i=1}^{B}U_i=M-C.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Earlier verified PROB20-14 checkpoints establish
\[
\frac{u_{M+1}}{u_M}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{u_M^{*b}}{u_M}\to b\quad\text{for each fixed }b,
\tag{2.5}
\]
and hence, for each fixed feasible \((a,b)\),
\[
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-C}}{u_M}\to b.
\tag{2.6}
\]
They also establish the intrinsic Laplace window. With
\[
\phi(t):=\mathbb Ee^{-tU},\qquad K(t):=-\log\phi(t),
\tag{2.7}
\]
one has
\[
\frac{K(t)}{t^{1/q}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\frac{K(t)}{\sqrt t}\to0
\qquad(t\downarrow0),
\tag{2.8}
\]
and, with
\[
L_M:=-\log u_M,
\tag{2.9}
\]
\[
L_M=o(M).
\tag{2.10}
\]
By Dwass, when \(n=1+hM\),
\[
L_M=\lambda_n+\log n,
\qquad
\lambda_n:=-\log\Pr(S_n=n-1),
\tag{2.11}
\]
and earlier work proves
\[
\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right).
\tag{2.12}
\]

The immediately preceding checkpoint defines the exact one-sided Cramér penalty
\[
I_M(b):=\sup_{t>0}\{bK(t)-Mt\}
\tag{2.13}
\]
and proves, uniformly for every integer \(b\ge1\) and every \(c\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-c}}{u_M}
\le \exp\{L_M-I_M(b)\}.
}
\tag{2.14}
\]
It also constructs a pointwise safe cutoff \(\beta_M^*\to\infty\), \(\beta_M^*=o(M)\), above which every shifted convolution ratio is negligible.

# 3. NEW: exact Scheffé theorem for the entire fixed-depth prefix

Let \(\mathsf P_k\) be the ordinary law of the finite-depth prefix pair \((A,B)=(N_k,Z_k)\). Let \(\mathsf Q_{M,k}\) be its law under the conditioned tree \(T_{1+hM}\). Define
\[
R_M(a,b):=
\frac{u^{*b}_{M-(a+b-1)/h}}{u_M}
\tag{3.1}
\]
for feasible pairs, with the usual convention that an impossible convolution coefficient is zero.

### Theorem 3.1 — exact prefix likelihood ratio
For every admissible \(M\),
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\mathsf Q_{M,k}}{d\mathsf P_k}(a,b)=R_M(a,b).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}R_M(A,B)=1.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof
By (2.1) and the branching property,
\[
\Pr((A,B)=(a,b),X=1+hM)
=\mathsf P_k(a,b)\,u^{*b}_{M-(a+b-1)/h}.
\]
Divide by \(u_M=\Pr(X=1+hM)\). Summing (3.2) over all prefix pairs gives (3.3). \(\square\)

Let \(\widehat{\mathsf P}_k\) denote the depth-\(k\) Kesten-prefix law. Criticality gives \(\mathbb EB=1\), and the standard spine size bias is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}{d\mathsf P_k}(a,b)=b.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]
By (2.6),
\[
R_M(a,b)\to b
\tag{3.5}
\]
for every fixed feasible pair. Both sides of (3.5) are nonnegative densities of total mass one with respect to \(\mathsf P_k\). Scheffé's lemma therefore gives:

### Theorem 3.2 — full finite-depth total-variation convergence
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}|R_M(A,B)-B|\to0,
}
\tag{3.6}
\]
and equivalently
\[
\boxed{
\|\mathsf Q_{M,k}-\widehat{\mathsf P}_k\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\to0.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

This is stronger than merely checking finitely many prefix configurations one at a time. It uses only the already proved fixed-count/fixed-shift local ratios and the exact normalization (3.3); no local limit theorem or regular variation is inserted.

For \(B>0\), write
\[
W_M:=R_M/B.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Then (3.6) also says
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}|W_M-1|\to0
\tag{3.9}
\]
(up to the vanishing conditioned mass of the \(B=0\) part).

The desired fractional moment is exactly
\[
\mathbb E\ell_k(T_{1+hM})^r
=\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}B^r
=\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}[B^rW_M].
\tag{3.10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}B^r
=\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}B^{1+r}
=\mathbb E Z_k^q<\infty,
\tag{3.11}
\]
where the fixed-generation \(q\)-moment finiteness was proved in an earlier checkpoint by von Bahr–Esseen induction.

Thus the entire unresolved issue is now transparent: (3.9) is ordinary \(L^1\) convergence of the likelihood ratio, while (3.10) asks whether it remains valid after multiplication by the endpoint-integrable weight \(B^r\). Abstractly this upgrade is false without additional structure; a rare large-\(B\) likelihood-ratio spike is the only possible obstruction.

# 4. NEW: weighted Cramér tail inequality

Define the fixed-generation \(q\)-tail modulus
\[
\boxed{
\tau_k(j):=\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}[B^q;B\ge j]
=\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k\ge j].
}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Then
\[
\tau_k(j)\downarrow0
\qquad(j\to\infty).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The pointwise Cramér estimate (2.14) can be combined with the *actual* \(q\)-tail budget before taking a supremum.

### Theorem 4.1 — all-level weighted Cramér bound
For every fixed \(k\), every admissible \(M\), and every integer \(j\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
\le
\frac{\tau_k(j)}{j}
\exp\{L_M-I_M(j)\}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof
By (3.2),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge j]
=\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}[B^rR_M(A,B);B\ge j].
\]
For \(B\ge j\), monotonicity of \(I_M\) and (2.14) give
\[
R_M(A,B)\le e^{L_M-I_M(B)}\le e^{L_M-I_M(j)}.
\]
Also, since \(q-r=1\),
\[
B^r\mathbf1_{\{B\ge j\}}
\le j^{-1}B^q\mathbf1_{\{B\ge j\}}.
\]
Taking expectations yields (4.3). \(\square\)

This estimate is uniform in the prefix shift \(A\): no balanced-prefix restriction is needed. The previous pointwise cutoff paid only the denominator \(u_M\); (4.3) additionally spends the exact \(q\)-moment tail that is available in the theorem's hypotheses.

# 5. NEW: a level-dependent weighted safe cutoff strictly below the pointwise one

Fix any deterministic \(\omega_M\to\infty\) with \(\omega_M=o(L_M+1)\) when convenient; for definiteness one may take \(\omega_M=\sqrt{L_M}\) for large \(M\). For \(\tau_k(j)>0\), define the weighted free-energy score
\[
\mathcal F_{M,k}(j)
:=I_M(j)+\log j-\log\tau_k(j).
\tag{5.1}
\]
If \(\tau_k(j)=0\), the \(B\ge j\) tail is already identically zero.

Define
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{M,k}
:=\min\{j\ge1:\tau_k(j)=0\text{ or }\mathcal F_{M,k}(j)\ge L_M+\omega_M\}.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — weighted moving-tail escape
For every fixed \(k\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge\gamma_{M,k}]
\le e^{-\omega_M}\to0.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof
If \(\tau_k(\gamma)=0\), the claim is trivial. Otherwise (4.3) and (5.2) give
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q}[B^r;B\ge\gamma]
\le
\exp\{L_M-I_M(\gamma)+\log\tau_k(\gamma)-\log\gamma\}
\le e^{-\omega_M}.
\]
\(\square\)

The score \(\mathcal F_{M,k}(j)\) is nondecreasing in \(j\): \(I_M(j)\) and \(\log j\) are nondecreasing, while \(-\log\tau_k(j)\) is nondecreasing. Thus all counts above \(\gamma_{M,k}\) are safe in the *weighted moment itself*, even if their pointwise convolution ratios are not uniformly tiny.

Let \(\beta_M^*\) be the pointwise safe cutoff from the preceding checkpoint, defined using the same \(\omega_M\) by
\[
I_M(\beta_M^*)\ge L_M+\omega_M.
\]
The previous report proves \(\beta_M^*\to\infty\). Since \(\tau_k(j)\to0\), for all large \(M\), \(\tau_k(\beta_M^*)<1<\beta_M^*\), so
\[
\mathcal F_{M,k}(\beta_M^*)\ge I_M(\beta_M^*)\ge L_M+\omega_M.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{M,k}\le\beta_M^*
}
\tag{5.4}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(M\). Thus (5.2) is never worse asymptotically than the prior pointwise cutoff and can be much smaller for thin \(q\)-tails.

If \(B=Z_k\) has unbounded support, then
\[
\boxed{\gamma_{M,k}\to\infty.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Indeed, for every fixed \(j\), \(I_M(j)\to0\): choose \(t_0\) so that \(jK(t)<\varepsilon\) for \(t<t_0\), while for \(t\ge t_0\), \(jK(t)-Mt\to-\infty\). Hence \(\mathcal F_{M,k}(j)\) stays bounded for fixed \(j\), whereas \(L_M+\omega_M\to\infty\). If \(B\) has bounded support instead, the fixed-level moment problem is already finite-count and therefore closed.

Combining (5.4) with the previous intrinsic scale bound gives, without any new regular-variation assumption,
\[
\gamma_{M,k}
\le\beta_M^*
=o\!\left(M^{1/q}L_M^{r/q}\right)
\tag{5.6}
\]
with the more precise preceding-report scale sandwich available for \(\beta_M^*\).

# 6. NEW: quantitative necessary certificate for any failure

The earlier checkpoint proved that disproportional/catastrophic prefixes are harmless and that, on the unresolved balanced component, the shift obeys
\[
0\le C\le D_k(1+B)
\tag{6.1}
\]
for a fixed \(D_k\). Fixed-count ratios also give a diagonal lower window \(\alpha_M\to\infty\) on which the normalized ratio tends to one uniformly. The new weighted escape theorem removes every \(B\ge\gamma_{M,k}\).

Therefore a failure can only live in the narrower moving corridor
\[
\boxed{
\alpha_M<B<\gamma_{M,k}\le\beta_M^*=o(M),
\qquad C=O_k(B).
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

There is also a quantitative spike certificate. Suppose that for some \(\varepsilon>0\), arbitrarily large \(J\), and a subsequence \(M\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf Q_{M,k}}[B^r;B\ge J]\ge\varepsilon.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Since
\[
B^rR_M=B^q\frac{R_M}{B}\qquad(B>0),
\]
and the \(\mathsf P_k\)-mass of the measure \(B^q\mathbf1_{\{B\ge J\}}d\mathsf P_k\) is exactly \(\tau_k(J)\), (6.3) implies that some feasible prefix pair with \(b\ge J\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R_M(a,b)}{b}
\ge\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau_k(J)}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
After subtracting the already negligible catastrophic and weighted-safe pieces, the pair may be chosen in the corridor (6.2), up to changing \(\varepsilon\) by a fixed factor.

Since \(\tau_k(J)\to0\), the required normalized spike height is not merely unbounded: it must dominate the reciprocal of the actual fixed-generation \(q\)-tail.

Combining (6.4) with (2.14) yields the free-energy restriction
\[
\boxed{
I_M(b)+\log b
\le
L_M+\log\tau_k(J)-\log\varepsilon.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
L_M-I_M(b)-\log b
\ge\log\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau_k(J)}\to\infty.
\tag{6.6}
\]
Thus a counterexample spike must lie **strictly below** the denominator-paying Cramér level by a diverging, tail-controlled gap. It cannot be a near-threshold artifact at \(I_M(b)\approx L_M\).

Finally, Dwass turns (6.4) into the nearby-local-valley certificate from the previous checkpoint, now with an explicit required height. If \(n=1+hM\) and \(s=n-a=b+h(M-c)\), then
\[
\frac{R_M(a,b)}{b}
=
\frac ns
\frac{\Pr(S_s=s-b)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}.
\tag{6.7}
\]
On the balanced corridor, \(a=O_k(b)=o(n)\), so \(s/n\to1\). Therefore failure forces
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Pr(S_s=s-b)}{\Pr(S_n=n-1)}
\gtrsim
\frac{\varepsilon}{\tau_k(J)},
\qquad |s-n|=O_k(b)=o(n).
}
\tag{6.8}
\]
A successful lacunary counterexample must therefore engineer a nearby central local-probability valley whose depth beats the reciprocal \(q\)-moment tail budget, while simultaneously staying below the weighted Cramér cutoff.

For the root, \(B=\xi\), so
\[
\boxed{
\tau_1(J)=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi\ge J].
}
\tag{6.9}
\]
The certificate is particularly explicit there.

## 7. Why generic local big-jump literature still does not close the corridor

A fresh literature check included Denisov–Dieker–Shneer, *Large deviations for random walks under subexponentiality: the big-jump domain* (Ann. Probab. 2008; arXiv:math/0703265). Their local Theorem 2.1 is a powerful uniform one-big-jump theorem, but it assumes a natural-scale sequence, a corresponding insensitivity sequence, a truncation sequence, and an \(h\)-small-steps sequence, with additional relations such as \(h_n=O(b_n)\) and \(h_n\le J_n\). See https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703265 and the statement of Theorem 2.1 in the paper. These hypotheses encode precisely the uniform local regularity/small-step control that is not yet proved for the arbitrary critical total-progeny law in the mesoscopic corridor (6.2). Thus invoking that theorem here without verifying its sequences would be circular or incomplete.

The earlier literature audit of randomly stopped local subexponential sums likewise found exponential/light-tail assumptions on the stopping count in the available generic dominated-convergence theorems. Our \(r\)-size-biased / Kesten boundary count has exactly the endpoint finite moment supplied by \(\mathbb E Z_k^q<\infty\), not an exponential moment.

No fresh source found in this bounded search resolves the exact arbitrary infinite-variance fractional fixed-level problem.

## 9. Next attack

The highest-value proof target is now a weighted local-valley inequality that spends the exact tail modulus rather than a generic exponential Kesten factor. In the notation above, seek
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathsf P_k}\left[
B^q\frac{R_M(A,B)}{B};

uniformly in large \(M\).

Two concrete subroutes remain:

On the adversarial side, a lacunary construction must now beat both mechanisms at once: it must create a nearby exact local valley of depth at least reciprocal to its own \(q\)-tail budget, yet do so at a count below \(\gamma_{M,k}\). This is substantially more restrictive than merely making the central atom unusually small.
