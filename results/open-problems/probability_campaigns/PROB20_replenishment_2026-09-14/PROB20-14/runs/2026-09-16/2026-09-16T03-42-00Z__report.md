## 2. Retained endpoint structure

Write
\[
S_n=\xi_1+\cdots+\xi_n,\qquad
a_n=\Pr(S_n=n-1),\qquad \lambda_n=-\log a_n,
\]
and let \(Q_n\) denote the conditioned allocation law
\[
Q_n=\mathcal L((\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)\mid S_n=n-1).
\]
The cyclic lemma identifies the degree-count law under \(Q_n\) with that of the conditioned tree. In particular, for the root degree \(D_n=\ell_1(T_n)\),
\[
\Pr(D_n=d)=\frac n{n-1}d\pi_d
\frac{\Pr(S_{n-1}=n-1-d)}{a_n},
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E[D_n^r;D_n>K]
=\frac n{n-1}\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\xi_1^q;\xi_1>K].
\tag{2.1}
\]

Earlier verified work proves the arbitrary-law denominator estimate
\[
\lambda_n=o\!\left(n^{(1-r)/(1+r)}\right),
\tag{2.2}
\]
root UI through the entropy scale \((n/(\lambda_n+1))^{1/q}\), a distribution-dependent large-width escape theorem for every fixed level, canonical \(W_q\)-recentering for every sublinear residual shift, renewal/Palm switching, and fixed-count/fixed-shift local ratios.

For a fixed level \(k\), expose the ordinary prefix
\[
A=N_k=\sum_{j<k}Z_j,\qquad B=Z_k.
\]
With \(X=|T|\), \(U=(X-1)/h\), \(u_M=\Pr(U=M)\), and
\[
C=\frac{A+B-1}{h},\qquad n=1+hM,
\]
the exact prefix likelihood ratio is
\[
R_M(A,B)=\frac{u^{*B}_{M-C}}{u_M}.
\tag{2.3}
\]
For every fixed feasible prefix, \(R_M(A,B)\to B\). Since \(R_M\) and \(B\) are both probability densities relative to the ordinary prefix law, Scheffé gives full finite-depth total-variation convergence to the Kesten prefix. The endpoint problem is the weighted upgrade by \(B^r\), where
\[
\mathbb E_{\widehat{\mathsf P}_k}B^r=\mathbb E Z_k^q<\infty.
\tag{2.4}
\]

The latest checkpoint introduced
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=\log\mathbb E e^{-\theta(\xi-1)},\qquad
H(y)=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta y-\Lambda(\theta)\},
\tag{2.5}
\]
and proved the exact offspring/total-progeny transform duality
\[
K(h\Lambda(\theta))=\theta-\Lambda(\theta),
\qquad
I_M(b)=(hM+b)H\!\left(\frac{b}{hM+b}\right),
\tag{2.6}
\]
where \(K(t)=-\log\mathbb E e^{-tU}\) and \(I_M(b)=\sup_t\{bK(t)-Mt\}\). It also proved
\[
\Lambda(\theta)=o(\theta^q),\qquad
\frac{H(y)}{y^{q/(q-1)}}\to\infty
\quad(y\downarrow0).
\tag{2.7}
\]
The preceding factorial-replication theorem controlled a **set** of large degrees by replacing every selected degree with its minimum. The new result below removes that loss and works with arbitrary soft marks.

# 3. NEW: soft-mark factorial replication under exact conditioning

Let
\[
g:\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\to[0,1]
\]
be any measurable mark function with
\[
p_g:=\mathbb E g(\xi)>0,
\qquad
q_{n,g}:=\mathbb E_{Q_n}g(\xi_1).
\tag{3.1}
\]
Introduce auxiliary independent uniforms, conditionally on the offspring values, and mark coordinate \(i\) with probability \(g(\xi_i)\). Let \(N_g\) be the number of marked coordinates. Conditional on the offspring vector,
\[
\mathbb E[N_g\mid \xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n]=\sum_i g(\xi_i),
\]
so under \(Q_n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}N_g=nq_{n,g}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
Define the marked negative transform
\[
M_g(\theta):=
\mathbb E\big[g(\xi)e^{-\theta(\xi-1)}\big].
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 — marked factorial-replication inequality

For every integer \(m\ge1\) satisfying
\[
nq_{n,g}\ge2m
\tag{3.4}
\]
and every \(\theta\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{q_{n,g}}{2p_g}
\le
\frac{\lambda_n-\theta}{m}
+\frac{n-m}{m}\Lambda(\theta)
+\log\frac{M_g(\theta)}{p_g}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

This is exact and does not require that \(g\) be an indicator or that all selected values have a common lower bound.

### Proof

For every integer \(z\ge0\),
\[
(z)_m\ge(z-m+1)_+^m.
\]
The right side is convex in \(z\), hence Jensen and (3.4) give
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}(N_g)_m
\ge(nq_{n,g}-m+1)^m
\ge(nq_{n,g}/2)^m.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Exchangeability and the auxiliary marks give
\[
\mathbb E_{Q_n}(N_g)_m
=
\frac{(n)_m}{a_n}
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{i=1}^m g(\xi_i);\ S_n=n-1
\right].
\tag{3.7}
\]
On \(S_n=n-1\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(\xi_i-1)=-1,
\]
so for every \(\theta\ge0\), identically on the conditioning event,
\[
1=e^{-\theta}\exp\left[-\theta\sum_{i=1}^n(\xi_i-1)\right].
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{i=1}^m g(\xi_i);\ S_n=n-1
\right]\\
&\qquad\le
e^{-\theta}
\left(\mathbb E[g(\xi)e^{-\theta(\xi-1)}]\right)^m
\left(\mathbb E e^{-\theta(\xi-1)}\right)^{n-m}\\
&\qquad=
e^{-\theta}M_g(\theta)^m e^{(n-m)\Lambda(\theta)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
\]
Combining (3.6)–(3.8), using \((n)_m\le n^m\) and \(a_n=e^{-\lambda_n}\), gives
\[
(nq_{n,g}/2)^m
\le
n^m e^{\lambda_n-\theta}
M_g(\theta)^m e^{(n-m)\Lambda(\theta)}.
\]
Take logarithms, divide by \(m\), and subtract \(\log p_g\). This is (3.5). \(\square\)

### Exact recovery of the previous set theorem

If \(g=\mathbf1_A\) and \(A\subseteq\{d,d+1,\ldots\}\), then
\[
\frac{M_g(\theta)}{p_g}\le e^{-\theta(d-1)}.
\]
Hence (3.5) becomes
\[
\log\frac{q_{n,g}}{2p_g}
\le
\frac{\lambda_n}{m}
+\frac{n-m}{m}\left[
\Lambda(\theta)
-\theta\frac{1+m(d-1)}{n-m}
\right].
\]
Optimizing over \(\theta\) recovers exactly the earlier factorial-replication inequality with
\[
H\!\left(\frac{1+m(d-1)}{n-m}\right).
\]
Thus Theorem 3.1 genuinely extends rather than replaces the previous argument.

# 4. NEW: one inequality for an entire winsorized q-tail

The main reason for introducing soft marks is to avoid paying the denominator separately on every dyadic shell.

Fix integers \(1\le K<D\) and define
\[
g_{K,D}(x)
:=\mathbf1_{\{x>K\}}
\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{x}{D}\right)^q\right\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Define the ordinary and conditioned winsorized \(q\)-tail masses
\[
W_P(K,D)
:=\mathbb E[\min(\xi^q,D^q);\xi>K],
\tag{4.2}
\]
\[
W_Q(n;K,D)
:=\mathbb E_{Q_n}[\min(\xi_1^q,D^q);\xi_1>K].
\tag{4.3}
\]
Then exactly
\[
p_g=\frac{W_P(K,D)}{D^q},
\qquad
q_{n,g}=\frac{W_Q(n;K,D)}{D^q}.
\tag{4.4}
\]
Define the marked-tail Laplace penalty
\[
\Psi_{K,D}(\theta)
:=-\log
\frac{
\mathbb E[\min(\xi^q,D^q)\mathbf1_{\{\xi>K\}}
 e^{-\theta(\xi-1)}]
}{W_P(K,D)}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — global winsorized-tail replication bound

If
\[
\frac{nW_Q(n;K,D)}{D^q}\ge8,
\tag{4.6}
\]
then, with
\[
m=\left\lfloor\frac{nW_Q(n;K,D)}{4D^q}\right\rfloor,
\tag{4.7}
\]
for every \(\theta\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{W_Q(n;K,D)}{2W_P(K,D)}
\le
\frac{\lambda_n-\theta}{m}
+\frac{n-m}{m}\Lambda(\theta)
-\Psi_{K,D}(\theta).
}
\tag{4.8}
\]
In particular, for absolute numerical constants \(c,C>0\),
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{W_Q(n;K,D)}{2W_P(K,D)}
\le
C\frac{\lambda_nD^q}{nW_Q(n;K,D)}
-
c\frac{D^q}{W_Q(n;K,D)}
H\!\left(
 c\frac{K W_Q(n;K,D)}{D^q}
\right).
}
\tag{4.9}
\]

### Proof

Equation (4.8) is Theorem 3.1 plus (4.4)–(4.5). Under (4.6),
\[
\frac{nW_Q}{8D^q}\le m\le\frac{nW_Q}{4D^q},
\qquad m\le n/4.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Every degree contributing to the mark satisfies \(\xi>K\), hence
\[
\Psi_{K,D}(\theta)\ge \theta(K-1).
\tag{4.11}
\]
Drop the favorable term \(-\theta/m\). Using (4.10), the remaining \(\theta\)-dependent part is bounded by
\[
C\frac{D^q}{W_Q}\Lambda(\theta)-\theta(K-1).
\]
Factor the first coefficient and optimize over \(\theta\). Since \(H\) is nondecreasing and \(K-1\asymp K\) away from the harmless bounded degrees, this gives (4.9), after adjusting constants. \(\square\)

### Why this is a real strengthening

The previous dyadic-shell theorem controls one multiplicative shell at a time. A hypothetical failure could evade any fixed-shell positive-mass certificate by spreading a nonvanishing conditioned \(q\)-tail over a growing number of shells. Theorem 4.1 charges **the whole truncated tail in one conditioning-denominator payment**.

Moreover (4.8), unlike its crude corollary (4.9), retains the full weighted degree distribution through \(\Psi_{K,D}\). A very remote atom is penalized according to its actual soft-mark contribution rather than merely by the minimum \(K\). This is the natural form for further lacunary analysis.

The obstruction is also clear: when \(D^q\asymp n\) and \(W_Q\) is only order one, the expected number of marks is only order one, so factorial replication cannot by itself dispose of the natural one-big-jump scale. The new theorem removes the **shell-spreading** weakness below that scale, but it does not by itself solve the natural-scale local-valley problem.

# 5. NEW: exact derivative duality for the spine-renewal kernel

The previous checkpoint proved the exact transform identity (2.6). Differentiating it reveals another exact structure behind the renewal route.

Let
\[
f(s)=\mathbb Es^\xi,
\qquad
T(z)=\mathbb Ez^{|T|},
\qquad
T(z)=z f(T(z)).
\tag{5.1}
\]
Define
\[
G(z):=z f'(T(z)).
\tag{5.2}
\]
This has nonnegative coefficients and \(G(1)=f'(1)=1\); it is the natural spine/renewal pgf appearing after differentiation of the tree equation.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact derivative conjugacy

For every \(\theta\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{
G(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=1-\Lambda'(\theta),
\qquad
1-G(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=\Lambda'(\theta).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

The exact transform theorem gives
\[
T(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=e^{-\theta}.
\]
Also
\[
\Lambda'(\theta)
=1-
\frac{e^{-\theta}f'(e^{-\theta})}{f(e^{-\theta})}.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Since
\[
e^{-\Lambda(\theta)}
=\frac{e^{-\theta}}{f(e^{-\theta})},
\]
substitution into (5.2) yields
\[
G(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})
=\frac{e^{-\theta}f'(e^{-\theta})}{f(e^{-\theta})}
=1-\Lambda'(\theta).
\]
This is (5.3). \(\square\)

Thus the same offspring transform controls three objects exactly:

1. the factorial lower-deviation penalty \(H\);
2. the total-progeny forest Cramér penalty \(I_M\);
3. the spine-renewal denominator \(1-G\).

This is useful because the remaining problem has repeatedly appeared in seemingly different local-ratio, renewal, and Cramér forms. They are not independent mechanisms.

# 6. NEW: renewal-reward generating identity for the root fractional tail

For \(K\ge0\), define
\[
H_K(t):=\sum_{d>K}d^r\pi_d t^d,
\qquad
F_K(z):=H_K(T(z)).
\tag{6.1}
\]
Earlier exact cyclic/generating-function work gives the root tail numerator as the corresponding coefficient of \(F_K\). Differentiate (5.1):
\[
T'(z)=f(T(z))+zf'(T(z))T'(z).
\]
With
\[
A(z):=\frac{T(z)}z=f(T(z)),
\]
this is
\[
\boxed{
T'(z)=\frac{A(z)}{1-G(z)}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]
Now define the tail reward
\[
M_K(z):=zH_K'(T(z))
=z\sum_{d>K}d^q\pi_d T(z)^{d-1}.
\tag{6.3}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
zF_K'(z)
=
M_K(z)\frac{A(z)}{1-G(z)}.
}
\tag{6.4}
\]
The forcing term has vanishing total mass:
\[
\boxed{
M_K(1)=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>K]\downarrow0.
}
\tag{6.5}
\]
At the exact transform parameter \(z=e^{-\Lambda(\theta)}\),
\[
\boxed{
M_K(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})
=
\frac{\mathbb E[\xi^q e^{-\theta\xi};\xi>K]}
{f(e^{-\theta})},
}
\tag{6.6}
\]
while by (5.3)
\[
1-G(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=\Lambda'(\theta).
\tag{6.7}
\]
Hence the root fractional-tail problem is a **renewal-reward coefficient problem whose forcing mass tends to zero**, and whose renewal denominator is explicitly the derivative of the same \(\Lambda\) that drives the factorial and Cramér penalties.

This does not justify an abstract strong-renewal theorem. In fact, the infinite-mean strong-renewal literature shows that sharp local renewal asymptotics require genuine local hypotheses in difficult regimes; one cannot silently infer them from tail information. See F. Caravenna and R. Doney, *Local large deviations and the strong renewal theorem*, Electron. J. Probab. 24 (2019), arXiv:1612.07635. The present branching-specific identities are therefore valuable precisely because they may supply the additional structure needed without importing an unverified LLT/SRT assumption.

# 7. What the new results do and do not settle

### 7.1 Root

The winsorized marked theorem removes a concrete weakness in the previous root strategy. If a nonvanishing conditioned \(q\)-tail below a cutoff \(D\) is spread over many shells, it is still seen as a single positive \(W_Q(n;K,D)\), and (4.8)–(4.9) force one common lower-deviation payment.

The exact renewal-reward identity (6.4) gives a second aggregate formulation: instead of controlling every moving local ratio individually, it is enough to show that the coefficient amplification of a forcing term whose total mass is \(\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>K]\to0\) cannot remain macroscopic.

The unresolved natural-scale obstruction remains genuine. If a constant conditioned \(q\)-mass is carried by degrees \(d\asymp n^{1/q}\), then the expected number of such degrees in the conditioned allocation can be only order one. Factorial replication cannot manufacture a diverging number of copies. The previously proved Palm/truncated-variance and Cramér estimates constrain this regime, but the currently available moduli do not yet force the required uniform bound under only \(\mathbb E\xi^q<\infty\).

### 7.2 Every fixed level

For fixed \(k\), the exact prefix likelihood (2.3), Scheffé convergence, the weighted Cramér bound, and the exact duality (2.6) remain valid. The new derivative identity shows that the renewal route and the Cramér route are governed by the same \(\Lambda\), but Theorem 3.1 is an iid-coordinate statement and is not by itself a weighted Scheffé theorem for the random boundary count \(B=Z_k\).

Thus no root-only partial result is promoted to the required all-level conclusion.

# 8. Counterexample implications

Any fixed-law counterexample must now survive all of the following verified constraints:

- finite-\(q\) missing-tail-mean budget and canonical \(W_q\)-recentering;
- fixed finite-support span smoothing;
- entropy-scale root control;
- renewal/Palm self-normalization;
- factorial replication for hard sets;

- exact forest Cramér penalty expressed through the same \(H\);
- **new:** exact spine-renewal denominator \(1-G(e^{-\Lambda(\theta)})=\Lambda'(\theta)\).

A lacunary counterexample can no longer rely merely on distributing its anomalous moment across many separated degree shells below the natural scale: the winsorized mark sees them simultaneously. What remains plausible only in principle is a synchronized natural-scale local valley carrying order-one conditioned \(q\)-mass with order-one expected multiplicity, or a higher-level analogue in the balanced mesoscopic prefix corridor.

No such certified construction was found in this run.

1. Bounded expectations do not imply UI.
2. Fixed-shift strong ratios do not control a diverging mesoscopic shift.
3. Generic Kesten bounds are exponential in the convolution count and exceed the endpoint first-moment budget.

5. Pure lattice obstructions are removed by a fixed finite support core of full span.

8. The new marked theorem still needs expected marked multiplicity bounded away from zero strongly enough to take a factorial moment. It does not automatically kill an order-one-multiplicity anomaly at \(d\asymp n^{1/q}\).
9. Generic strong-renewal theorems cannot be imported without their local hypotheses.

# 12. Next attack

The next proof attempt should combine the two new aggregate forms rather than return to pointwise local ratios.

**Root route.** Choose a moving truncation \(D=D(n,K)\). Use Theorem 4.1 to control the entire winsorized tail \(K<\xi\le D\) in one denominator payment. Use the exact Cramér/Palm machinery above \(D\). The only remaining interface is where \(D^q\asymp n\) and expected marked multiplicity is order one. At that interface, use (6.4)–(6.7) to seek a branching-specific weighted renewal inequality of the form
\[
[z^{n-1}]\left(M_K(z)\frac{A(z)}{1-G(z)}\right)
\le o_K(1)\,[z^{n-1}]T'(z)
\]
uniformly in admissible \(n\), with the \(o_K(1)\) supplied by \(M_K(1)=\mathbb E[\xi^q;\xi>K]\to0\). Any proof must exploit the exact relation \(1-G(e^{-\Lambda})=\Lambda'\); an abstract SRT is not enough.

**Counterexample route.** Try to make the renewal reward in (6.4) concentrate on a lacunary coefficient despite \(M_K(1)\to0\). The construction must simultaneously respect the exact derivative conjugacy, finite-core span smoothing, canonical recentering, and the winsorized marked inequality. Failure to build such a renewal-reward spike would be evidence toward the universal theorem but is not itself a proof.

**All-level route.** If the weighted root renewal lemma is obtained, transplant it through the exact random-prefix forest representation, retaining the actual \(q\)-tail \(\tau_k(j)=\mathbb E[Z_k^q;Z_k\ge j]\) before taking any supremum in the boundary count.
